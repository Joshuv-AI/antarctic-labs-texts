Trade-off Invariance principle for minimizers of regularized functionals
Massimo Fornasier 1,2,3, Jona Klemenc 1,2, Alessandro Scagliotti 1,2

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider functionals of the form H α ​ ( u ) = F ⁡ ( u ) + α ​ G ​ ( u ) H_{\alpha}(u)=F(u)+\alpha G(u) with α ∈ [ 0 , + ∞ ) \alpha\in[0,+\infty) , where u u varies in a set U ≠ ∅ U\neq\emptyset (without further structure). We first revisit a result stating that, excluding at most countably many values of α \alpha , we have inf H α ⋆ G = sup H α ⋆ G \inf_{H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G=\sup_{H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G , where H α ⋆ ≔ arg ⁡ min U ⁡ H α H_{\alpha}^{\star}\coloneqq\arg\min_{U}H_{\alpha} , which is assumed to be non-empty. Then, we prove a stronger result that concerns the invariance of the limiting value of the functional G G along minimizing sequences for H α H_{\alpha} , which extends the above Principle to the case H α ⋆ = ∅ H_{\alpha}^{\star}=\emptyset . Moreover, we show to what extent these findings generalize to multi-regularized functionals and—in the presence of an underlying differentiable structure—to critical points. Finally, the main result implies an unexpected consequence for functionals regularized with uniformly convex norms: excluding again at most countably many values of α \alpha , it turns out that for a minimizing sequence, convergence to a minimizer in the weak or strong sense is equivalent.



1.
Introduction

Minimization problems for functionals
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
of the form

(1.1)

H_{\alpha}(u)\coloneqq F(u)+\alpha G(u),

where
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
,
U\neq\emptyset
,
F,G\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
F+G\not\equiv+\infty
,
are ubiquitous in Inverse Problems
[
4
,
10
]
and have enormous relevance for applications.
Without further assumptions on the involved objects—such as convexity of
H_{\alpha}
, which is rather difficult to have in practice—it is common wisdom that there is little we can say about the structure of the set of minimizers.
In particular, there might exist distinct minimizers
u^{\star}_{1}\neq u^{\star}_{2}
.
In this case, despite the fact that both
u^{\star}_{1}
and
u^{\star}_{2}
achieve the infimum of
H_{\alpha}
,
we could expect them to attain distinct trade-offs between the competing terms
F
and
G
.
In other words, we could expect that
F(u^{\star}_{1})\neq F(u^{\star}_{2})
and that
G(u^{\star}_{1})\neq G(u^{\star}_{2})
.
However, in this paper, we shall show that such a variance of trade-offs between minimizers is exceptional, in the sense that it can only occur at a countable values of
\alpha
. We start with the simplest result, which applies to the situation described above.

Theorem 1.1

(Trade-off Invariance Principle I)
.

Let
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be defined as in (
1.1
) and let us define

(1.2)

U\supset H_{\alpha}^{\star}\coloneqq\arg\min_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u).

If there exist
a<b\in[0,+\infty)
such that
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\neq\emptyset
for every
\alpha\in[a,b]
, then
for all but countable
\alpha
in
[a,b]

we have that

(1.3)

\inf_{u\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G(u)=\sup_{u\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G(u).

Remark 1
.

As detailed in the proof, the values of
\alpha
for which the conclusion of
Theorem

1.1
does not hold are included in the set of discontinuities of a monotone non-increasing function. Hence, it follows that they are at most countably many.
Moreover, it is possible to construct examples where there are actually infinitely many instances of such values of
\alpha
.
Namely, let us consider
U=[0,+\infty)
,
F(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-i}(u-i+1)_{+}
and
G(u)=-u
, where
(z)_{+}
denotes the positive part of
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, setting
H_{\alpha}\coloneqq F+\alpha G
with
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
, it turns out that

H^{\star}_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\{0\}&\alpha=\alpha^{0},\\
[k-1,k]&\alpha=\alpha^{k},\\
\{k\}&\alpha\in(\alpha^{k},\alpha^{k+1}),\\
\end{cases}

where
\alpha^{0}\coloneqq 0
and
\alpha^{k}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{-i}
for every
k\geq 1
. In particular, when
\alpha=\alpha^{k}
for
k\geq 1
we obtain
-k=\inf_{u\in H^{\star}_{\alpha}}G(u)<\sup_{u\in H^{\star}_{\alpha}}G(u)=-k+1
. Finally, we observe that for
\alpha\in[1,+\infty)
we have that
H_{\alpha}^{\star}=\emptyset
.

Results in the vein of
Theorem

1.1
have been already observed in the Inverse Problem literature (see
[
10
, Lemma 2.6.3]
).
For instance, in
[
6
]
the authors were interested in tuning the Tikhonov regularization parameter
\alpha
according to noise level to obtain rates of convergence to true solutions. In the same paper, they related the identity in (
1.3
) to the points
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
where the concave ‘value function’
\mathcal{H}\colon[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is differentiable, having set
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\coloneqq\inf_{U}H_{\alpha}
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.1]
).
Because their initial motivation came from inverse problems, in
[
6
,
5
]
the authors assumed Banach space structure for the domain
U
and non-negativity for the regularizer
G
(for the sake of the precision, in
[
5
]
convexity and weak lower semi-continuity of the regularizer are further required). Yet, all these hypotheses are in fact not necessary, as made explicit in our formulation of Theorem
1.1
.

Besides the immediate generalizations just mentioned, the main advancement of the present paper
concerns the extension of
Theorem

1.1
to the limiting values of
F
and

G
along minimizing sequences.
In particular, the following extension of our argument allows us to avoid
the assumption that
H^{\star}_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
, which is present in
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
and
Theorem

1.1
:

Theorem 1.2

(Trade-off Invariance Principle II)
.

For all but countable
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
,
if
\inf_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
, then there exists
some
G_{\alpha}\in[-\infty,+\infty]
such that
G(u_{i})\rightarrow G_{\alpha}

for every minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

of
H_{\alpha}
.

As a relevant and, perhaps, unexpected example of applying the Trade-off Invariance Principle, we propose the next corollary. Let us first introduce the following notion:

Definition 1
.

Let
U
be a Banach space. A functional
G:U\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
weak-to-strong
functional if for every sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
weakly converging to some
u^{\star}
such that
\lim_{i\to+\infty}G(u_{i})=G(u^{\star})
, then the sequence converges also strongly.

Widely used instances of weak-to-strong functionals are norms in uniformly convex Banach spaces
[
3
, Proposition 3.32]
, or more general strictly convex functionals as in
[
11
]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
U
be a Banach space, and let us consider
F\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
F\not\equiv+\infty
and
G\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
a
weak-to-strong
functional.
Then, for all but countable
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, the following holds:

If
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimizing sequence of the functional
H_{\alpha}\coloneqq F+\alpha G
,
which converges
weakly
to some minimizer
u^{\star}\in U
of
H_{\alpha}
,
then
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
strongly
to
u^{\star}
.

Proof.

Since
F\not\equiv+\infty
, we have that
F+G\not\equiv+\infty

Let
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
be such that the conclusion of
Theorem

1.2
holds — we recall that the complement of this set in
(0,+\infty)
is at most countable.
Then, let us consider a minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
for
H_{\alpha}
that is converging weakly to
u^{\star}
, as in the statement.
Since by hypothesis
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)=H_{\alpha}(u^{\star})
, we can apply
Theorem

1.2
to the constant sequence
(u_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with
u^{\prime}_{i}=u^{\star}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and to
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

to deduce that
\lim_{i\to\infty}G(u_{i})=G_{\alpha}=G(u^{\star})
.
Recalling that
G
is assumed to be a weak-to-strong functional (see
Definition

1
), the thesis follows.
∎

Remark 2
.

If we further assume that
H_{\alpha}
is sequentially weakly lower semi-continuous, then
Corollary

1.3
implies that every weakly pre-compact minimizing sequence is actually strongly pre-compact for every but countably many
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
.
In certain applications, obtaining strong convergence is beneficial in order to lift properties of the minimizing sequence to its limiting minimizer.

Remark 3
.

Usually strongly convergent minimizing sequences can be obtained when the functionals are convex by means of Mazur’s Lemma
[
3
, Corollary 3.8]
. The previous result is significantly stronger as it applies to more general functionals
F
(also nonconvex), and it holds for any weakly convergent minimizing sequence. It is worth noticing that the sum
F+\alpha G
is not required to be convex either.

Remark 4
.

In some very special cases, the conclusion of
Corollary

1.3
was already accessible.
Namely, for
F
being the indicator function of a closed convex set
C
in a Hilbert space
U
and
G(u)=\|u-u_{0}\|_{U}
being the distance from a given
u_{0}
,
then the minimizer of
H_{\alpha}
is the orthogonal projection of
u_{0}
onto
C
.
In this case
any minimizing sequence
of
H_{\alpha}
is actually a Cauchy sequence as in the proof presented in
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Remark 5
.

Our initial interest in
Theorem

1.1
was sparked by the minimizing movement scheme (see
[
2
]
). Namely, in
[
2
, Lemma 3.1.2]
, for a metric space
U
equipped with a distance
d
, for a functional
\phi\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and for a fixed
u\in U
, the authors consider the minimizing set

J_{\tau}[u]\coloneqq\argmin_{v\in U}\left\{\phi(v)+\frac{1}{2\tau}d^{2}(v,u)\right\}.

Under the assumption that
J_{\tau}[u]\neq\emptyset
for
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\circ}]
for some
\tau_{\circ}
,
they show that, for all but countable
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\circ}]
,

\inf_{u_{\tau}\in J_{\tau}[u]}d(u_{\tau},u)=\sup_{u_{\tau}\in J_{\tau}[u]}d(u_{\tau},u).

Theorem

1.1
in

In

2.
Derivation of
Theorem

1.1

We first establish a key-lemma.
We report that it can be found in
[
10
, Lemma 2.6.1]
, but we present its proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.1

(Monotonicity Lemma I)
.

Let
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
H_{\alpha}^{\star}
be defined as in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively, and let us consider
\alpha_{2}>\alpha_{1}
such that
H_{\alpha_{i}}^{\star}\neq\emptyset
for
i=1,2
.
Then, we have that

(2.1)

\inf_{u\in H_{\alpha_{1}}^{\star}}G(u)\geq\sup_{u\in H_{\alpha_{2}}^{\star}}G(u).

Proof.

Let us consider
u_{\alpha_{i}}\in H_{\alpha_{i}}^{\star}
for
i=1,2
.
Then, observing that
H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{\alpha_{2}})\leq H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{\alpha_{1}})
, we deduce that

F(u_{\alpha_{2}})+\alpha_{2}G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\leq F(u_{\alpha_{1}})+\alpha_{2}G(u_{\alpha_{1}}),

which yields

(2.2)

F(u_{\alpha_{2}})-F(u_{\alpha_{1}})\leq\alpha_{2}\big(G(u_{\alpha_{1}})-G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\big).

Let us assume by contradiction that
G(u_{\alpha_{2}})>G(u_{\alpha_{1}})
, resulting in
G(u_{\alpha_{1}})-G(u_{\alpha_{2}})<0
.
In this case, we would get
H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{\alpha_{2}})<H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{\alpha_{1}})
, which contradicts the minimality of
u_{\alpha_{1}}
for
H_{\alpha_{1}}
.
Namely, using (
2.2
), it follows that

\begin{split}H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{\alpha_{2}})&=F(u_{\alpha_{2}})+\alpha_{1}G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\\
&\leq F(u_{\alpha_{1}})+\alpha_{1}G(u_{\alpha_{2}})+\alpha_{2}\big(G(u_{\alpha_{1}})-G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\big)\\
&=F(u_{\alpha_{1}})+\alpha_{1}G(u_{\alpha_{1}})+(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})\big(G(u_{\alpha_{1}})-G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\big)\\
&=H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{\alpha_{1}})+(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})\big(G(u_{\alpha_{1}})-G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\big)\\
&<H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{\alpha_{1}}),\end{split}

which is impossible. Therefore, we deduce that

G(u_{\alpha_{2}})\leq G(u_{\alpha_{1}})\quad\forall\,u_{\alpha_{1}}\in H^{\star}_{\alpha_{1}},\forall\,u_{\alpha_{2}}\in H^{\star}_{\alpha_{2}},

and hence (
2.1
) follows.
∎

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us define
R\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
as follows:

(2.3)

R(\alpha)\coloneqq\inf_{u\in H^{\star}_{\alpha}}G(u).

Given
\alpha_{2}\geq\alpha_{1}
, we have that
R(\alpha_{2})\leq R(\alpha_{1})
. Indeed, in the interesting case
\alpha_{2}>\alpha_{1}
, we can take advantage of Lemma
2.1
to deduce that

(2.4)

R(\alpha_{1})=\inf_{u\in H^{\star}_{\alpha_{1}}}G(u)\geq\sup_{u\in H^{\star}_{\alpha_{2}}}G(u)\geq\inf_{u\in H^{\star}_{\alpha_{2}}}G(u)=R(\alpha_{2}).

Let
\bar{\alpha}\in[a,b]
be such that
\sup_{u\in H^{\star}_{\bar{\alpha}}}G(u)>\inf_{u\in H^{\star}_{\bar{\alpha}}}G(u)
.
Then, from (
2.4
) it follows that
\alpha
must be a discontinuity point for the monotone decreasing function
R
. Indeed, we have that

\lim_{\alpha\to\bar{\alpha}^{-}}R(\alpha)\geq\sup_{u\in H^{\star}_{\bar{\alpha}}}G(u)>\inf_{u\in H^{\star}_{\bar{\alpha}}}G(u)=R(\bar{\alpha}).

Since any monotone function admits at most countably many discontinuity points, we deduce that the identity (
1.3
) is violated for at most countably many values of
\alpha
in
[a,b]
.
∎

3.
Derivation of
Theorem

1.2

This section contains the first main contribution of the present paper. Indeed, we provide an extension of the results presented in
[
10
, Section 2.6]
, which required
\arg\min_{U}H_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
and, in some statements, the sequential coercivity and lower semi-continuity of the functionals
F,G
.

Before proving the theorem, we first establish the cornerstone of our argument.

Lemma 3.1

(Monotonicity Lemma II)
.

For
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, we define

S_{\alpha}\coloneqq\{(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ \emph{minimizing sequence of} }H_{\alpha}\},

and we set

(3.1)

\begin{split}G_{\alpha}^{+}&\coloneqq\sup_{(u_{i})\in S_{\alpha}}\limsup_{i\to\infty}G(u_{i})\in[-\infty,\infty],\\
G_{\alpha}^{-}&\coloneqq\inf_{(u_{i})\in S_{\alpha}}\liminf_{i\to\infty}G(u_{i})\in[-\infty,\infty].\end{split}

Then, for all
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\infty
such that
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha_{1}}(u)>-\infty

and
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha_{2}}(u)>-\infty
,
we have that

(3.2)

G_{\alpha_{1}}^{+}\geq G_{\alpha_{1}}^{-}\geq G_{\alpha_{2}}^{+}\geq G_{\alpha_{2}}^{-}.

Proof.

The left and the right inequality are immediate from the definitions, so we only show the middle inequality.
We assume, by contradiction, that for some
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
we have that
G_{\alpha_{1}}^{-}<G_{\alpha_{2}}^{+}
,
and we define

\epsilon\coloneqq\frac{G_{\alpha_{2}}^{+}-G_{\alpha_{1}}^{-}}{5}>0.

By the definition of
G_{\alpha_{1}}^{-}
and
G_{\alpha_{2}}^{+}
,
we can choose sequences
(u_{j}^{1})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in S_{\alpha_{1}}
and
(u_{k}^{2})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in S_{\alpha_{2}}
such that

(3.3)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}G(u_{k}^{2})-\liminf_{j\to\infty}G(u_{j}^{1})\geq 4\epsilon.

Since
F+G\not\equiv+\infty
, we have that
\inf_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)<+\infty
for all

\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
, and by assumption we also have that
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha_{1}}(u)>-\infty
and
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha_{2}}(u)>-\infty
.
Thus, the minimizing sequences
(H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{j}^{1}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{k}^{2}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H_{\alpha_{1}}
and
H_{\alpha_{2}}
, respectively, have finite limits.
Setting
\Delta_{\alpha}\coloneqq\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}>0
, for
j,k
large enough we hence have that
H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{j}^{1})\leq\inf_{U}H_{\alpha_{1}}+\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon
and
H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{k}^{2})\leq\inf_{U}H_{\alpha_{2}}+\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon
, which in particular implies

(3.4)

\displaystyle-H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{j}^{1})

\displaystyle\geq-

\displaystyle H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{k}^{2})-\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon,\qquad\text{and}

(3.5)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{j}^{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{k}^{2})-\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon.

Furthermore, by
eq.

3.3
, we can find arbitrarily large
j,k
such that

(3.6)

G(u_{k}^{2})-G(u_{j}^{1})\geq 3\epsilon.

In particular, we can find
j,k
such that
eqs.

3.4
,
3.5
and
3.6
hold simultaneously.
Fixing such
j,k
, we add
eqs.

3.4
and
3.5
to get

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}G(u_{j}^{1})

\displaystyle=(H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{j}^{1})-H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{j}^{1}))

\displaystyle\geq(H_{\alpha_{2}}(u_{k}^{2})-H_{\alpha_{1}}(u_{k}^{2}))-2\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon=\Delta_{\alpha}G(u_{k}^{2})-2\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon.

Rearranging and using
eq.

3.6
, we have

2\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon\geq\Delta_{\alpha}(G(u_{k}^{2})-G(u_{j}^{1}))\geq 3\Delta_{\alpha}\epsilon,

which is a contradiction and finishes the proof.
∎

We now prove an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Those
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
for which
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
form an interval in
[0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

We observe that this fact descends from the concavity of the value function
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\coloneqq\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)
(see
Lemma

4.3
). However, we give here a direct proof.
To prove the claim, it suffices to show that for
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
,

\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)=-\infty
implies one of those two cases:

(1)

For all
\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha
,
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha^{\prime}}(u)=-\infty
.

(2)

For all
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
,
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha^{\prime}}(u)=-\infty
.

Let us assume that
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)=-\infty
for some
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
,
and let us take a sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
H_{\alpha}(u_{i})\rightarrow-\infty
.
In addition, there must exist one of following two subsequences:

(a)

A subsequence
(u_{i_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
G(u_{i_{j}})\leq 0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

(b)

A subsequence
(u_{i_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
G(u_{i_{j}})\geq 0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

For
\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha
,
(a)
implies that

H_{\alpha^{\prime}}(u_{i_{j}})\leq H_{\alpha}(u_{i_{j}})\rightarrow-\infty
, and thus
(a)
implies
(1)
.
Likewise,
(b)
implies
(2)
, and the claim follows.
∎

Finally, we proceed with the proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

We must show that
G_{\alpha}
exists for all
\alpha
for which
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
, with at most
countably many exceptions. Using
Lemma

3.2
and possibly neglecting the two endpoints
—as we allow for countable exceptions—, it suffices to prove the existence of such
G_{\alpha}

for all but countably many
\alpha
in some open interval
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
, where
\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\in[0,\infty)
,
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\in(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\infty]
and

\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
for all
\alpha
in
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
.

By
eq.

3.2
, the quantity
G_{\alpha}^{+}
defined in
eq.

3.1
is monotone decreasing on
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
and for
each
\alpha\in(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
,
G_{\alpha}^{-}\neq G_{\alpha}^{+}
implies that
\alpha
is a discontinuity point of
G_{\alpha}^{+}
.
Indeed, assume that
G_{\alpha}^{-}<G_{\alpha}^{+}
for some
\alpha\in(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
, and let

(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
converging to
\alpha
from above.
By
eq.

3.2
, we have that

\limsup_{i\to\infty}G_{\alpha_{i}}^{+}\leq G_{\alpha}^{-}<G_{\alpha}^{+},

and thus
\alpha
is a discontinuity point of
G_{\alpha}^{+}
.
Since a monotone function admits at most countably many discontinuity points, we deduce that
G_{\alpha}^{-}\neq G_{\alpha}^{+}
for at most countably many values of
\alpha\in(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
.
Setting
G_{\alpha}\coloneqq G_{\alpha}^{-}=G_{\alpha}^{+}
for all other
\alpha
finishes the proof.
∎

We lastly prove that
Theorem

1.2
implies a refined version of

,
which also shows that

.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
H_{\alpha}:U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\subset U
be defined as in
eqs.

1.1
and
1.2
, respectively.
Then, for all but countable
\alpha
in
[0,+\infty)
,
we have: if
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\neq\emptyset
, then

\inf_{u\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G(u)=\sup_{u\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G(u).

Proof.

If
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\neq\emptyset
, then for any
u^{\star}\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}
, we have
\inf_{u}H_{\alpha}(u)=H_{\alpha}(u^{\star})>-\infty
,
and we can apply
Theorem

1.2
to find
G_{\alpha}
for all but countable many values of
\alpha
.
Using the constant minimizing sequence
(u_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with
u^{\prime}_{i}=u^{\star}
, we deduce that

G(u^{\star})=G_{\alpha}
for every
u^{\star}\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}
. Hence, for all
\alpha
where such a
G_{\alpha}
exists, we have

\inf_{u^{\star}\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G(u^{\star})=G_{\alpha}=\sup_{u^{\star}\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G(u^{\star}).\qed

4.
Trade-off Invariance Principle for multi-regularization

In this section, we consider multi-regularized minimization problems.
To this end, we consider
a set
U\neq\emptyset
and mappings
F,G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with
m\geq 1
.
For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
, we define
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
by

(4.1)

H_{\alpha}(u)\coloneqq F(u)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}G_{j}(u)

for every
u\in U
.
As before, we assume the existence of some
\bar{u}\in U

and some
\bar{\alpha}\in(0,+\infty)^{m}
such that

(4.2)

H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u})<+\infty,

so that
F+\sum_{j=1}^{m}G_{j}\not\equiv+\infty
, and
\inf_{U}H_{\alpha}<+\infty
for every
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
.
In this framework, we strive to formulate a version of the Trade-off Invariance Principle.
In other words, we try to establish a statement of the following form:

Conjecture 1
.

For all but some exceptional
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,
if
\inf_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
, then there exist

G_{\alpha}^{1},\ldots,G_{\alpha}^{m}\in[-\infty,+\infty]
such that
G_{j}(u_{i})\rightarrow G_{\alpha}^{j}

for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and
for every minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

of
H_{\alpha}
.

The main obstacle is to describe the size of the set of exceptional values
\alpha
.
We note right away that, unlike in the single-regularization case, we cannot hope for the set
of exceptional pairs to be countable: Indeed, considering
m=2
and adding the trivial regularizer
G_{2}(u)=0
to
the example in
Remark

1
, all pairs of the form

(\alpha^{k},a)
for
a\in[0,+\infty)
are exceptional—and there are uncountably many of them.

On the other hand, constraining the set of exceptional pairs to be Lebesgue negligible seems
to be in reach: Let us denote by
B_{1}
the set of those values
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
for which

\inf_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
, but for which we cannot find a
G_{\alpha}^{1}
as above.
Then let us define
B_{j}
analogously for
G_{j}
with
j=2,\ldots,m
, i.e.,

(4.3)

B_{j}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}\mid G_{\alpha}^{j,+}>G_{\alpha}^{j,-}\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\mathrm{inf}_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty\}.

If we assume for a moment that
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
are measurable,
then we can use Fubini-Tonelli’s Theorem to estimate the size of the set of exceptional pairs:

\displaystyle|B_{1}|=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Bigg(\underbrace{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbbb{1}_{B_{1}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\alpha_{1}}_{=\,0\text{ by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:principle_relaxed}}}\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha_{2}\cdots\mathrm{d}\alpha_{m}=0.

The same argument applies to
B_{j}
with
j=2,\ldots,m
, and thus
|\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}B_{j}|=0
.
However, it is a priori not obvious why the sets
B_{j}
should be measurable. Indeed, it is worth recalling that we are not making any measurability assumption on the functions
F,G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
.

The aim of the rest of this section is to show that this is actually the case (see
Corollary

4.6
). Therefore, using Fubini-Tonelli as mentioned above, we prove the following version of
Conjecture

1
:

Theorem 4.1
.

For Lebesgue-almost all
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,
if
\inf_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)>-\infty
, then there exist

G_{\alpha}^{1},\ldots,G_{\alpha}^{m}\in[-\infty,+\infty]
such that
G_{j}(u_{i})\rightarrow G_{\alpha}^{j}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and for every minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

of
H_{\alpha}
.

In analogy with the case of a single regularizer, we can deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
H_{\alpha}:U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be defined as in
eq.

4.1
, and let us introduce
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\coloneqq\arg\min_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)
.
Then, for Lebesgue-almost all
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,
we have: if
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\neq\emptyset
, then

\inf_{u\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G_{j}(u)=\sup_{u\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}}G_{j}(u)\qquad\mbox{for every}\qquad j=1,\ldots,m.

Proof.

The argument follows the line of the proof of
Corollary

3.3
, i.e., when
H_{\alpha}^{\star}\neq\emptyset
, we consider
u^{\star}\in H_{\alpha}^{\star}
and we apply
Theorem

4.1
to the minimizing sequence that is constantly equal to
u^{\star}
.
∎

Before we prove the measurability of
B_{j}
, we need to show an ancillary lemma:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
H_{\alpha}:U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be defined as in
eq.

4.1
.
Then the function
\mathcal{H}\colon[0,+\infty)^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
defined by

(4.4)

\alpha\mapsto\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\coloneqq\inf_{u\in U}H_{\alpha}(u)

is concave. In particular, it is measurable.

Proof.

Let us consider
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
. Then, we compute:

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}\big(\theta\alpha+(1-\theta)\alpha^{\prime}\big)&=\inf_{u\in U}\left(F(u)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\theta\alpha_{j}+(1-\theta)\alpha^{\prime}_{j}\big)G_{j}(u)\right)\\
&\geq\theta\mathcal{H}(\alpha)+(1-\theta)\mathcal{H}(\alpha^{\prime}).\qed\end{split}

We show the the measurability of
B_{j}
as part of a stronger result.
We now introduce below some functions that will play a crucial role in our analysis.

Definition 2
.

Let
H_{\alpha}:U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be defined as in
eq.

4.1
.
For every
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
, we define

S_{\alpha}\coloneqq\{(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ minimizing sequence of }H_{\alpha}\},

and we set for every
j=1,\ldots,m

(4.5)

G_{\alpha}^{j,+}\coloneqq\sup_{(u_{i})\in S_{\alpha}}\limsup_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i}),\qquad G_{\alpha}^{j,-}\coloneqq\inf_{(u_{i})\in S_{\alpha}}\liminf_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i}).

We now investigate the properties of the functions
\alpha\mapsto G_{\alpha}^{j,\pm}
.
In what follows, we adopt the following conventions for the
\liminf
and
\limsup
of a function
g:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
:

\begin{split}&\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}g(\alpha^{\prime})\coloneqq\inf\Big\{\liminf_{i\to\infty}g(\alpha^{\prime}_{i})\mid\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha^{\prime}_{i}=\alpha\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\alpha^{\prime}_{i}\neq\alpha\,\,\forall i\Big\},\\
&\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}g(\alpha^{\prime})\coloneqq\sup\Big\{\limsup_{i\to\infty}g(\alpha^{\prime}_{i})\mid\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha^{\prime}_{i}=\alpha\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\alpha^{\prime}_{i}\neq\alpha\,\,\forall i\Big\}.\end{split}

It is convenient to recall that every concave function
f
is continuous in the relative interior of its effective domain, i.e., in the relative interior of the set
\{f>-\infty\}
. See, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 10.1]
for the convex setting.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\colon[0,+\infty)^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be the value function associated to the parametric regularized functionals
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with the form as in
eq.

4.1
.
Let
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,+\infty)^{m}
be an open set such that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>-\infty
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
. Then, for every
j=1,\ldots,m
we have:

-\infty<\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}\leq\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}<+\infty.

In particular, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\ni\alpha
with
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{O}
such that the functions
\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{i,\pm}
are bounded, for every
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

The inequality
\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}\leq\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}
follows directly from the fact that, by definition,
G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{i,-}\leq G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{i,+}
for every
\alpha^{\prime}\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
. Hence, we only have to establish the first and the last bound.

We start by showing that
\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}<+\infty
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
. Let us assume by contradiction that this is not the case for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Then, we can find sequences
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U
such that
\alpha_{i}\to\alpha
as
i\to\infty
and
H_{\alpha_{i}}(u_{i})=\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}
with
0\leq\varepsilon_{i}\leq 1/i
, and such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i})=+\infty
. Hence, we choose
h>0
such that
\overline{B_{2h}(\alpha)}\subset\mathcal{O}
, and we observe that

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(\alpha-he_{j})&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i}-he_{j})\\
&\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{\alpha_{i}-he_{j}}(u_{i})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(H_{\alpha_{i}}(u_{i})-hG_{j}(u_{i})\right)\\
&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}-hG_{j}(u_{i})\right)=\mathcal{H}(\alpha)-h\lim_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i})=-\infty,\end{split}

contradicting the fact that
\mathcal{H}>-\infty
in
\mathcal{O}
. We notice that above we used the continuity of the concave function
\mathcal{H}
at
\alpha,\alpha-he_{j}\in\mathcal{O}
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 10.1]
).

The proof of the fact that
\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}>-\infty
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
follows the lines of the argument presented above. The only difference is that we consider the auxiliary sequence
(\alpha_{i}+he_{j})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
convergent to
\alpha+he_{j}\in\mathcal{O}
.
Finally, the last part of the thesis follows automatically.
∎

The next proposition establishes a regularity result for the mappings
\alpha\mapsto G_{\alpha}^{j,\pm}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\colon[0,+\infty)^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be the value function associated to the parametric regularized functionals
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with the form as in
eq.

4.1
.
If
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,+\infty)^{m}
is an open set such that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>-\infty
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
, then

(4.6)

G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}=\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad G_{\alpha}^{j,-}=\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}=\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}

for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
. Moreover, we have:

(4.7)

G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=\lim_{h\searrow 0}G_{\alpha-he_{j}}^{j,+}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad G_{\alpha}^{i,-}=\lim_{h\searrow 0}G_{\alpha+he_{j}}^{j,-}

for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

Let us take
\hat{j}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
. We detail the identity for
G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},+}
.
In virtue of the Monotonicity Lemma for a single regularizer (
Lemma

3.1
), we observe that

(4.8)

G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},+}\leq\lim_{h\searrow 0}G_{\alpha-he_{\hat{j}}}^{\hat{j},-}\leq\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\hat{j},-}\leq\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\hat{j},+},

where we applied
Lemma

3.1
to the regularization parameter
h>0
.
We are left to prove that
G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},+}\geq\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\hat{j},+}
.
Let us consider
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\alpha_{i}\to\alpha
as
i\to\infty
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}G^{\hat{j},+}_{\alpha_{i}}=\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\hat{j},+}
.
Invoking
Lemma

4.4
, we can assume that
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, so that there exists
C>0
satisfying
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|G_{\alpha_{i}}^{j,\pm}|\leq C
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Moreover, we construct
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U
such that
H_{\alpha_{i}}(u_{i})=\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}
with
0\leq\varepsilon_{i}\leq 1/i
, and such that
|G_{\hat{j}}(u_{i})-G^{\hat{j},+}_{\alpha_{i}}|\leq 1/i
and
|G_{j}(u_{i})|\leq 2C
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.
We claim that
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimizing sequence for
H_{\alpha}
.
Indeed, we compute

\begin{split}\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{\alpha}(u_{i})&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(H_{\alpha_{i}}(u_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\alpha^{j}-\alpha^{j}_{i})G_{j}(u_{i})\right)\\
&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\alpha^{j}-\alpha^{j}_{i})G_{j}(u_{i})\right)=\mathcal{H}(\alpha),\end{split}

showing that the claim is true, where we used the continuity of the concave value function
\mathcal{H}
at
\alpha
and the boundedness of the functions
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
along
(u_{i})_{i\geq 1}
.
Hence,
(u_{i})_{i\geq 1}
being a minimizing sequence for
H_{\alpha}
, the definition of
G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},+}
yields

G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},+}\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}G_{\hat{j}}(u_{i})=\lim_{i\to\infty}G^{\hat{j},+}_{\alpha_{i}}=\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\hat{j},+},

which is the desired inequality. For the identity in
eq.

4.7
, it is sufficient to apply (
4.6
) to the chain of inequalities
G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},+}\leq\lim_{h\searrow 0}G_{\alpha-he_{\hat{j}}}^{\hat{j},+}\leq\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\hat{j},+}
, which follows from
Lemma

3.1
.

The arguments for the identities concerning
G_{\alpha}^{\hat{j},-}

are analogous.
∎

Finally, we are in position for establishing the measurability of the sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

The sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
defined as in
eq.

4.3
are Lebesgue-measurable.

Proof.

Recalling that by definition
B_{j}\subset\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}
(see
eq.

4.3
), we can partition
B_{j}
as follows:

B_{j}=\Big(B_{j}\cap\partial\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}\Big)\cup\Big(B_{j}\cap\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}\Big),

where
\partial\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\},\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}
denote, respectively, the boundary and the interior of the convex set
\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}
. Since the boundary of a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is closed and its Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
is null, we deduce that
B_{j}\cap\partial\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}
is Lebesgue measurable (and, in particular, its measure is zero as well).
On the one hand, in the case
\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}=\emptyset
, the thesis follows immediately.
On the other hand, if
\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}\neq\emptyset
,
Proposition

4.5
guarantees that
\alpha\mapsto G_{\alpha}^{j,+}
and
\alpha\mapsto G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
are, respectively, upper and lower semi-continuous on
\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}
. In particular, this implies that the set
B_{j}\cap\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}=\{\alpha\mid G_{\alpha}^{j,+}>G_{\alpha}^{j,-}\}\cap\mathrm{int}\{\mathcal{H}>-\infty\}
is measurable.
∎

We conclude this section with another immediate consequence of
Proposition

4.5
.

5.
Trade-off Invariance Principle for critical points

In view of
Theorem

1.1
, a natural question is whether the Principle holds true for local minimizers as well.
In this section, we provide a positive answer, and we extend the Trade-off Invariance Principle to the critical points of a regularized functional
H_{\alpha}
having the structure as in
eq.

1.1
.
To this end, we shall assume that
H_{\alpha}
satisfies the celebrated Łojasiewicz inequality (see
[
7
]
). In the present section, we shall equip the Banach space
U
exclusively with the
strong topology
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
U
be a Banach space, and let us consider two functionals
F,G\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
that are of class
C^{1}
, i.e., that are everywhere Fréchet-differentiable, and whose differentials
\nabla F,\nabla G\colon U\to U^{\prime}
are continuous.
Defining
H_{\alpha}\coloneqq F+\alpha G
for
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
, we assume that
H_{\alpha}
satisfies the local Łojasiewicz inequality, i.e., for every
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
for every
u\in U
there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}_{u}\ni u
and constants
C_{u}>0
and
\theta_{u}\in(1,2]
such that

(5.1)

|H_{\alpha}(v)-H_{\alpha}(u)|\leq C_{u}\|\nabla H_{\alpha}(v)\|^{\theta_{u}}

for every
v\in\mathcal{O}_{u}
.
Finally, let
K\subset U
be a strongly compact set.
Then, for all but countable
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
, if
u_{1},u_{2}\in K
are such that
\nabla H_{\alpha}(u_{1})=\nabla H_{\alpha}(u_{2})=0
and
H_{\alpha}(u_{1})=H_{\alpha}(u_{2})
, then
G_{\alpha}(u_{1})=G_{\alpha}(u_{2})
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction, and we assume that the thesis is not true.
In this case,
the set

(5.2)

\begin{split}E\coloneqq\big\{\alpha\in[0,+\infty)\mid&\exists u_{1},u_{2}\in K:\nabla H_{\alpha}(u_{1})=\nabla H_{\alpha}(u_{2})=0,\\
&\qquad H_{\alpha}(u_{1})=H_{\alpha}(u_{2}),\,G(u_{1})-G(u_{2})>0\big\}\end{split}

consists of uncountably many elements.
Similarly, if we define

\begin{split}E^{k}\coloneqq\big\{\alpha\in[0,+\infty)\mid&\exists u_{1},u_{2}\in K:\nabla H_{\alpha}(u_{1})=\nabla H_{\alpha}(u_{2})=0,\\
&\qquad H_{\alpha}(u_{1})=H_{\alpha}(u_{2}),\,G(u_{1})-G(u_{2})>1/k\big\},\end{split}

we observe that

E=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}E^{k},

and we conclude that there exists
\bar{k}
such that
E^{k}
contains uncountably many elements.
Setting
\varepsilon\coloneqq 1/\bar{k}
, we have that there exists a monotone decreasing sequence
(\alpha^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset E^{k}
and two sequences
(u_{1}^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(u_{2}^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that:

(1)

A_{0}\leq\alpha^{i+1}<\alpha^{i}\leq A_{1}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha^{i}\to\bar{\alpha}
as
i\to\infty
;

(2)

\nabla H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})=\nabla H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{2}^{i})=0
;

(3)

H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})=H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{2}^{i})
;

(4)

G_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})\geq G_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{2}^{i})+\varepsilon
.

Moreover, leveraging on the compactness of
K
, up to the extraction of a non-relabelled subsequence, we can further assume that there exists
\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2}\in K
such that
u_{1}^{i}\to\bar{u}_{1}
and
u_{2}^{i}\to\bar{u}_{2}
as
i\to\infty
.
Passing to the limit in (4), we get that
G(\bar{u}_{1})\geq G(\bar{u}_{2})+\varepsilon
, which can be rephrased by saying that

G(\bar{u}_{1})\geq\bar{G}+\varepsilon/2\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad G(\bar{u}_{2})\leq\bar{G}-\varepsilon/2,

where we set
\bar{G}\coloneqq\frac{G(\bar{u}_{1})+G(\bar{u}_{2})}{2}
.
In view of finding a contradiction, we aim at showing that for
i
large enough we have

(5.3)

H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})-\alpha^{i}\bar{G}>H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})-\bar{\alpha}\bar{G}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{2})-\bar{\alpha}\bar{G}>H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{2}^{i})-\alpha^{i}\bar{G},

which, combined with the fact that
H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})=H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{2})
, would result in
H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})>H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{2}^{i})
, that contradicts with the definition itself of
u_{1}^{i},u_{2}^{i}
.
In order to establish the first relation in (
5.3
),we begin by applying the local Łojasiewicz inequality to the functional
H_{\bar{\alpha}}
at the point
\bar{u}_{1}
, and we denote with
\mathcal{O}\ni\bar{u}_{1}
, with
C_{1}>0
, and with
\theta_{1}\in(1,2]
, respectively, the neighborhood of
\bar{u}_{1}
, the constant, and the exponent prescribed by this assumption.
Recalling that
u^{i}_{1}\to\bar{u}_{1}
as
i\to\infty
, we deduce that there exists
\bar{i}\geq 1
such that we have

(a)

G(u_{1}^{i})\geq\bar{G}+\varepsilon/4
,

(b)

|H_{\bar{\alpha}}(u_{1}^{i})-H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})|\leq C_{1}\|\nabla H_{\bar{\alpha}}(v)\|^{\theta_{1}}
,

for every
i\geq\bar{i}
. Moreover, we recall that, since
K\subset U
is compact and
\nabla G
is continuous, there exists
\kappa>0
such that
\sup_{u\in K}\|\nabla G(u)\|\leq\kappa
.
Finally, we compute:

\begin{split}H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})-\alpha^{i}\bar{G}-\left(H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})-\bar{\alpha}\bar{G}\right)&=H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})-H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})-(\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha})\bar{G}\\
&\geq H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})-H_{\bar{\alpha}}(u_{1}^{i})-(\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha})\bar{G}-\left|H_{\bar{\alpha}}(u_{1}^{i})-H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})\right|\\
&=(\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha})(G(u_{1}^{i})-\bar{G})-\left|H_{\bar{\alpha}}(u_{1}^{i})-H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{1})\right|\\
&\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}(\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha})-C_{1}\left\|\nabla F(u_{1}^{i})+\bar{\alpha}\nabla G(u_{1}^{i})\right\|^{\theta_{1}}\\
&=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}(\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha})-C_{1}\left\|(\bar{\alpha}-\alpha^{i})\nabla G(u_{1}^{i})\right\|^{\theta_{1}}\\
&\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}(\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha})-C_{1}\kappa^{\theta_{1}}|\bar{\alpha}-\alpha^{i}|^{\theta_{1}},\end{split}

where we used that, by construction,
\nabla H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{1}^{i})=\nabla F(u_{1}^{i})+\alpha^{i}\nabla G(u_{1}^{i})=0
. Recalling that
\theta_{1}\in(1,2]
and that
\alpha^{i}-\bar{\alpha}\searrow 0
as
i\to\infty
, we obtain the first inequality of (
5.3
). The second relation follows by repeating
verbatim
the same argument to the difference
H_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{u}_{2})-\bar{\alpha}\bar{G}-\left(H_{\alpha^{i}}(u_{2}^{i})-\alpha^{i}\bar{G}\right)
, and by noting that
G(u_{2}^{i})\leq\bar{G}-\varepsilon/4
for
i
sufficiently large.
∎

Remark 6
.

In the case
U
is
\sigma
-compact, i.e., if
U
is finite-dimensional, the statement of
Theorem

5.1
is true even if we allow the critical points
u_{1},u_{2}
to be chosen in the whole domain
U
.
More in general, we can avoid the restriction to a compact set
K\subset U
if there exists a
\sigma
-compact subset of
U
that contains every critical point of
H_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
.

6.
Consequences and applications

In order to exemplify and highlight the relevance of the results stated in
Section

1
, we collect below some of their more immediate implications.

6.1.
Differentiability points of the value function

In this paragraph, we relate the Trade-off Invariance Principle to the points where the value function
\mathcal{H}
(see
eq.

4.4
) is differentiable.
This provides a complete generalization of the results obtained in
[
5
, Section 2]
. Namely, in their work, the authors equipped the domain with a topological structure, and they further assume
F,G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
to be coercive and lower semi-continuous. In this way, their arguments could rely on the existence of minimizers of
H_{\alpha}=F+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}G_{j}
for every
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
. In addition, in
[
5
]
the regularizers
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
are required to be non-negative.
As we are going to show below, the intertwining between the differentiability of the value function
\mathcal{H}
and the Trade-off Invariance Principle holds more generally, and it does not require at all the existence of minimizers for the underlying regularized problems.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,+\infty)^{m}
be an open set such that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>-\infty
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
.
For every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
we set the following notations:

\partial^{+}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\coloneqq\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}(\alpha+he_{j})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)}{h},\qquad\partial^{-}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\coloneqq\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}(\alpha-he_{j})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)}{-h}

for every
j=1,\ldots,m
, where
e_{j}
is the
j
-th element of the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
We recall that the existence of the limits follows directly from the concavity of
\mathcal{H}
(see
Lemma

4.3
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\colon[0,+\infty)^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be the value function associated to the parametric regularized functionals
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with the form as in
eq.

4.1
.
Moreover, for every
i=1,\ldots,m
let
G_{\alpha}^{j,+},G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
be as in
Definition

2
.
Finally, let
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,+\infty)^{m}
be an open set such that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>-\infty
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
.
Then, for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
, we have

\partial^{+}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=G_{\alpha}^{j,-}\leq G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=\partial^{-}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha).

Proof.

Let us start by showing that
\partial^{-}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\geq G_{\alpha}^{j,+}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Let us take
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
such that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>-\infty
.
For every
u\in U
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)-\mathcal{H}(\alpha-he_{j})&\geq H_{\alpha}(u)+(\mathcal{H}(\alpha)-H_{\alpha}(u))-H_{\alpha-he_{j}}(u)\\
&=hG_{j}(u)+(\mathcal{H}(\alpha)-H_{\alpha}(u)).\end{split}

Let us consider a monotonically decreasing sequence
(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
h_{i}\searrow 0
, and let us take a minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
for
H_{\alpha}
such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i})=G^{j,+}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, up to the extraction of a subsequence, we can assume that
H_{\alpha}(u_{i})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\leq h_{i}^{2}
.
If we compute
\partial_{j}^{-}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
along
(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, from the previous estimate we deduce that

\partial_{j}^{-}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}(\alpha-h_{i}e_{j})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)}{-h_{i}}\geq\lim_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i})=G^{j,+}_{\alpha}.

We now address
\partial^{-}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\leq G_{\alpha}^{j,+}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.
As before, let us consider a monotonically decreasing sequence
(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
h_{i}\searrow 0
, and let us introduce the notation
\alpha_{i}\coloneqq\alpha-h_{i}e_{j}
. Moreover, we construct two sequences
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(u_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U
such that

H_{\alpha}(u_{i})=\mathcal{H}(\alpha)+\varepsilon_{i},\quad|G_{j}(u_{i})-G_{\alpha}^{j,+}|\leq\frac{1}{i},

and

H_{\alpha_{i}}(u_{i}^{\prime})=\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime},\quad|G_{j}(u_{i}^{\prime})-G_{\alpha_{i}}^{j,+}|\leq\frac{1}{i},

with
0\leq\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\leq h_{i}^{2}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
From the fact that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\leq H_{\alpha}(u^{\prime}_{i})
, we deduce that

(6.1)

H_{\alpha}(u_{i})-H_{\alpha}(u^{\prime}_{i})\leq\varepsilon_{i}

for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, we compute

(6.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)-\mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i})&=H_{\alpha}(u_{i})-H_{\alpha_{i}}(u_{i}^{\prime})-\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\\
&=h_{i}G_{j}(u_{i})+h_{i}\big(G_{j}(u^{\prime}_{i})-G_{j}(u_{i})\big)+H_{\alpha}(u_{i})-H_{\alpha}(u_{i}^{\prime})-\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\\
&\leq h_{i}G_{j}(u_{i})+h_{i}\big(G_{j}(u^{\prime}_{i})-G_{j}(u_{i})\big)+\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\\
&=h_{i}G_{j}(u_{i})\\
&\qquad+h_{i}\left[\big(G_{\alpha_{i}}^{j,+}-G_{\alpha}^{j,+}\big)+\big(G_{j}(u^{\prime}_{i})-G_{\alpha_{i}}^{j,+}\big)+\big(G_{\alpha}^{j,+}-G_{j}(u_{i})\big)\right],\end{split}

where we used
eq.

6.1
to get the inequality.
Recalling that
\alpha_{i}=\alpha-h_{i}e_{j}
,
eq.

4.7
implies that
G_{\alpha_{i}}^{j,+}-G_{\alpha}^{j,+}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. Therefore, if we divide by
h_{i}
and we pass to the limit in
eq.

6.2
, we obtain that

\partial_{j}^{-}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}(\alpha-h_{i}e_{j})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)}{-h_{i}}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}G_{j}(u_{i})=G^{j,+}_{\alpha}.

The identity
\partial^{+}_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
follows from the same arguments.
∎

We illustrate in the next result the relation between the differentiability of
\mathcal{H}
and the Trade-off Invariance Principle.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\colon[0,+\infty)^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be the value function associated to the parametric regularized functionals
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with the form as in
eq.

4.1
.
Moreover, for every
i=1,\ldots,m
let
G_{\alpha}^{j,+},G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
be as in
Definition

2
.
Finally, let
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,+\infty)^{m}
be an open set such that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>-\infty
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
.
Then, for every
j=1,\ldots,m
and every
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
the following are equivalent:

(1)

The partial derivative
\partial_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
exists, i.e.,
\partial_{j}^{+}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=\partial_{j}^{-}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
;

(2)

The identity
G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
holds;

(3)

The function
\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}
is continuous at
\alpha
;

(4)

The function
\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}
is continuous at
\alpha
.

Finally, the value function
\mathcal{H}
is differentiable at
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
if and only if
G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

The equivalence
(1)
\iff
(2)
follows directly from
Lemma

6.1
.
To establish
(2)
\implies
(3)
, we use
Proposition

4.5
to see that

G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=\limsup_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}\geq\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}\geq\liminf_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,-}=G_{\alpha}^{j,-}.

Therefore, from the identity in
(2)
, it follows that
G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha}G_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,+}
.
Concerning the inverse implication
(3)
\implies
(2)
, the continuity of
\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto G^{j,+}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
in
(3)
implies in particular
G^{j,+}_{\alpha}=\lim_{h\searrow 0}G^{j,+}_{\alpha+he_{j}}
. Moreover, the Monotonicity Lemma (see
Lemma

3.1
) applied to the one-dimensional regularization parameter
h>0
yields

G^{j,+}_{\alpha}\geq G^{j,-}_{\alpha}\geq G^{j,+}_{\alpha+he_{j}},

so that, when letting
h\searrow 0
, we get
G^{j,+}_{\alpha}=G^{j,-}_{\alpha}
. This concludes the equivalence
(2)
\iff
(3)
.
The bi-implication
(2)
\iff
(4)
follows from similar arguments.

Now we address the last part of the thesis.
Since the differentiability of
\mathcal{H}
at
\alpha
implies the existence of the partial derivatives
\partial_{j}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
, we immediately deduce that
G_{\alpha}^{j,+}=G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.
For the inverse implication, let us assume that
\mathcal{H}
is not differentiable at
\alpha
.
Denoting with
\nabla\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
the super-gradient of the concave function
\mathcal{H}
at
\alpha
, since
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
, it follows that
\nabla\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset
.
Moreover, we recall that the differentiability of
\mathcal{H}
at
\alpha
holds if and only if
\nabla\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
contains a single element (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 25.1]
for the convex setting).
Hence, if
\mathcal{H}
is not differentiable at
\alpha
, we can find
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}),(p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p_{m}^{\prime})\in\nabla\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
such that there exists
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
for which
p_{j}<p_{j}^{\prime}
. Recalling the definition of super-gradient, this implies that

\frac{\mathcal{H}(\alpha+he_{j})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)}{h}\leq p_{j}<p_{j}^{\prime}\leq\frac{\mathcal{H}(\alpha-he_{j})-\mathcal{H}(\alpha)}{-h}

for every
h>0
. Passing to the limit as
h\to 0
in the previous expression, we get that
\partial_{j}^{-}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)>\partial_{j}^{+}\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
, which in turn implies that
G_{\alpha}^{j,+}>G_{\alpha}^{j,-}
(see
Lemma

6.1
).
∎

6.2.
Penalty functions in equality constrained optimization

Let us consider the following constrained minimization problem:

(6.3)

\min_{u\in U}F(u)\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad G(u)=0,

where
F,G\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. Up to substituting
G
with
G^{2}
, it is not restrictive to assume that
G\geq 0
. In many situations, it is convenient to relax the hard-constrained problem reported in
eq.

6.3
by incorporating
G
in the objective function as a penalty term for the violation of the constraint:

(6.4)

\min_{u\in U}F(u)+\alpha G(u),

with
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
.
Owing to
Corollary

3.3
, we obtain that, excluding at most countably many values of the penalization parameter
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, whenever different minimizers
u^{\star}_{1}\neq u^{\star}_{2}
of the relaxed reformulation (
6.4
) exist, they are ‘equivalent’ in terms of the constraint, i.e.
G(u^{\star}_{1})=G(u^{\star}_{2})
.

As a relevant example, let us consider a control system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the form

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{u}(t)=h(x_{u}(t),u(t))&\mbox{for a.e. }t\in[0,T],\\
x_{u}(0)=x_{0},\end{cases}

where
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth map such that
|h(x,v)|\leq C(1+|x|)(1+|v|^{p})
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
(
1\leq p<\infty
), and
u\in U\coloneqq L^{p}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Moreover, let us consider the endpoint-constrained problem

(6.5)

\min_{u\in U}\int_{0}^{T}f(x_{u}(t),u(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad x_{u}(T)=x_{T}

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous and bounded below function that prescribes the running cost, and
F(u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}f(x_{u}(t),u(t))\,dt
.
Let us relax the hard terminal-state constraint in
eq.

6.5
by formulating the free-endpoint problem:

(6.6)

\min_{u\in U}\int_{0}^{T}f(x_{u}(t),u(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha G(x_{u}(T))

where
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
G\geq 0
and
\{G=0\}=\{x_{T}\}
, and
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
tunes the penalty term.
Excluding at most countably many exceptional values of
\alpha
, if
u^{\star}_{1},u^{\star}_{2}
are optimal controls for the relaxed free-end point problem (
6.6
), it surprisingly turns out that
G(x_{u_{1}^{\star}}(T))=G(x_{u_{2}^{\star}}(T))
.

6.3.
Regularized functionals in Sobolev Spaces

Corollary

1.3
finds application to functionals that are defined on Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
1<p<\infty
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open, and that are regularized with Sobolev semi-norms.
Namely, let us set
U\coloneqq W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and let us consider a functional
H_{\alpha}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
of the form

H_{\alpha}(u)\coloneqq F(u)+\alpha g(\|Du\|_{L^{p}}),

where
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
,
Du\in L^{p}
denotes the weak derivative of
u
, and
g\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a strictly monotone increasing and continuous function. Finally, we assume that
F\not\equiv+\infty
.
We recall that the weak convergence in
W^{1,p}
(
1<p<\infty
) is characterized as follows: a sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{1,p}
is
W^{1,p}
-weakly convergent to
u_{\infty}
if and only if
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is
L^{p}
-strongly convergent to
u_{\infty}
and
(Du_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is
L^{p}
-weakly convergent to
Du_{\infty}
.
Setting
G(u)\coloneqq g(\|Du\|_{L^{p}})
, it turns out that
G
is a weakly-to-strong functional for
W^{1,p}
-weakly convergent sequences (see
Definition

1
).
Then, by virtue of
Corollary

1.3
, we deduce that, with the exception of at most countably many
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, for a minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{1,p}
, the properties of being weakly and strongly convergent to a minimizer are equivalent.

Remark 7
.

The argument reported above can also be adapted to functionals of the form
H_{\alpha}(u)=F(u)+\alpha g\big(|Du|(\Omega)\big)
defined over
BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open,
g\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a strictly monotone increasing and continuous function, and
|Du|(\Omega)
denotes the total variation of
u
. In this case, excluding at most countably many values of
\alpha
, if
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimizing sequence for
H_{\alpha}
that is weakly
∗
converging to a minimizer
u^{\star}
, then
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is actually
strictly
converging to
u^{\star}
(see
[
1
, Section 3.1]
for these notions of convergence).

6.4.
Approximability through sequentially weakly pre-compact subsets

We present here a negative result that is implied by
Corollary

1.3
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
U
be a Banach space and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq U
be any subset. Let us consider
F\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
F\not\equiv+\infty
and
G\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
a
weak-to-strong
functional, and let
H_{\alpha}\coloneqq F+\alpha G
be the regularized functional, with
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
.
Then, for all but countable
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, the following holds:

If
u^{\star}\in H^{\star}_{\alpha}
is such that
\inf_{v\in\mathcal{D}}\|u^{\star}-v\|_{U}>0
, then it is not possible to find a sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}
that is weakly converging to
u^{\star}
and that is a minimizing sequence for
H_{\alpha}
.
Moreover, if
H_{\alpha}
is sequentially weakly lower semi-continuous, if

H^{\star}_{\alpha}
is contained in the sequentially weak closure of
\mathcal{D}

and

(6.7)

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{D}}\|u^{\star}-v\|_{U}>0\quad\forall u^{\star}\in H_{\alpha}^{\star},

then it is not possible to find a sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}
that is weakly pre-compact and that is a minimizing sequence for
H_{\alpha}
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{D}
is sequentially weakly pre-compact, there is no minimizing sequence for
H_{\alpha}
in
\mathcal{D}
.

As an example of where the above situation occurs, let us consider
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval,
U\coloneqq L^{p}(I)
with
1<p<\infty
,
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
\#V<\infty
, and let us denote with
\mathcal{D}\subseteq U
the set of
functions on
I
taking values in
V
. Then
\mathcal{D}
is sequentially weakly pre-compact, and it turns out that its
L^{p}
-weak closure is
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{w}=\{u\in L^{p}(I):\min V\leq u\leq\max V\,\mbox{a.e.}\}
.
Furthermore, when dealing with a functional
H_{\alpha}=F+\alpha G
, one in general expects its minimizers to take infinitely many values, so that
eq.

6.7
holds true, since

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{D}}\|u-v\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=\int_{I}\inf_{v\in V}|u(x)-v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
u\in U
.
By
Proposition

6.3
, in such a scenario there cannot be a minimizing sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}
for
H_{\alpha}
, for all but countable
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
.

6.5.
\Gamma
-convergence with respect to the weak topology

We shall show how
Corollary

1.3
affects the sequences obtained by considering quasi-minimizers of
\Gamma
-convergent functionals that contain a weak-to-strong regularization term.
For a thorough introduction to
\Gamma
-convergence, we refer to the textbook
[
8
]
.
Here, we assume that
U
is a reflexive Banach space with a separable dual
U^{\prime}
.
In this framework, following
[
8
, Chapter 8]
, given
H\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
H^{i}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we can characterize the
\Gamma
-convergence with respect to the weak topology as follows: The sequence
(H^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\Gamma
-converging to
H
if the following two conditions hold:

•

(
\liminf
inequality) For every
u\in U
and for every
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
that is weakly convergent to
u
we have

H(u)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}H^{i}(u_{i}).

•

(
\limsup
inequality) For every
u\in U
, there exists a sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
that is weakly convergent to
u
and such that

H(u)\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}H^{i}(u_{i}).

Finally, we recall that the functionals
(H^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are
weakly equi-coercive
if, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
K\subset U
weakly compact such that
\{u\in U:H^{i}(u)\leq t\}\subset K
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
U
be a reflexive Banach space with separable dual
U^{\prime}
.
Let us consider
F\colon U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
F\not\equiv+\infty
and
G\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
a
weak-to-strong
functional, and let
H_{\alpha}\coloneqq F+\alpha G
be the regularized functional, with
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
.
Then, for all but countable
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, the following holds:

Let
(H_{\alpha}^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of weakly equi-coercive functionals that are
\Gamma
-convergent to
H_{\alpha}
with respect to the weak convergence of
U
, and let
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
U
that satisfies

(6.8)

The latter is a well-established result (see

Since
H_{\alpha}\not\equiv+\infty

+\infty>H_{\alpha}(\hat{u})\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}H^{i}_{\alpha}(\hat{u}_{i})\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}\inf_{U}H^{i}_{\alpha},

H_{\alpha}(\hat{u})\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}H_{\alpha}^{i_{j}}(\hat{u}_{i_{j}})\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\inf_{U}H_{\alpha}^{i_{j}}=\limsup_{j\to\infty}H_{\alpha}^{i_{j}}(u_{i_{j}})\geq\liminf_{j\to\infty}H_{\alpha}^{i_{j}}(u_{i_{j}})\geq H_{\alpha}(u^{\star}),

Algebraic properties of tensor product of modules over a field
Ahad Rahimi

Abstract
. Let A A and B B be commutative Noetherian algebras over an arbitrary field 𝕜 \Bbbk such that A ⊗ 𝕜 B A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B is Noetherian. We consider ideals I I and J J of A A and B B , respectively, as well as nonzero finitely generated modules L L and N N over A A and B B , respectively. In this paper, we investigate certain algebraic properties of the A ⊗ 𝕜 B A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B -module L ⊗ 𝕜 N L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N , which are often inherited from the properties of the A A -module L L and the B B -module N N . Specifically, we provide characterizations for the Cohen-Macaulayness, generalized Cohen-Macaulayness, and sequentially Cohen-Macaulayness of L ⊗ 𝕜 N L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N with respect to the ideal I ⊗ 𝕜 B + A ⊗ 𝕜 J I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J , in terms of the corresponding properties for L L and N N with respect to I I and J J , respectively.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
and
B
be commutative Noetherian algebras over a field
\Bbbk
, with
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
also being Noetherian. Let
I
and
J
be ideals of
A
and
B
, respectively. Then,
I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
and
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
are also ideals of
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
. We define
\mathcal{I}=I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
.
We can also consider
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
as a local ring with the unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{n}
, where
(A,\mathfrak{m})
and
(B,\mathfrak{n})
are local algebras over the same field
\Bbbk
. In this case,
A
and
B
share
\Bbbk
as the common residue field, and either
A
or
B
is algebraic over
\Bbbk
. This fact is mentioned in Remark
2.8
.
Moreover, let
L
and
N
be nonzero finitely generated modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. In this paper, we investigate the algebraic properties of the
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-module
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, which are connected to those of
L
and
N.
In fact, we provide characterizations for the Cohen-Macaulayness, generalized Cohen-Macaulayness, and sequentially Cohen-Macaulayness of
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
, in terms of the corresponding properties for
L
and
N
with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively. It is important to note that in order to study these algebraic properties for
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, we require the assumption that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian. In general, if
A
and
B
are Noetherian algebras, then
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is not necessarily Noetherian, even when
A
and
B
are field extensions of
\Bbbk
, as shown in
[
11
]
.

Let us begin by discussing Cohen-Macaulayness.
Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
a proper ideal of
R
, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. We say that
M
is Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if either
M=\mathfrak{a}M
or
M\neq\mathfrak{a}M
and
\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{a},M)=\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)
(see
[
8
]
). Here,
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)
denotes the cohomological dimension of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, which is the supremum of all integers
i
for which
\text{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\neq 0
.
We observe that if
(R,\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
is a local ring, then
M
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
M
is Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.

The purpose of Section 2 is to explain how
\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
and
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
can be expressed in terms of the
corresponding quantities of
L
and
N
with respect to
I
and
J
. Indeed, in Theorem
2.6
, we demonstrate that

\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{grade}(J,N)
and
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{cd}(I,L)+\operatorname{cd}(J,N).

Thus, we establish that
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
if and only if
L

and
N
are Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively. Consequently, we obtain
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
(b) and
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
as corollaries.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. We say
M
is
generalized Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)\leq 0
or
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)
(see
[
4
]
). Here,
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
denotes the
finite dimension of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, which is defined as the infimum of non-negative integers
i
for which
\text{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is not finitely generated. Note that if
(R,\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
is a local ring, then
M
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
M
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.

In Section 3, we provide a description of
\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
in terms of the finite dimension and grade of
L
and
N
with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively. Theorem
3.2
states that

\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\min\{\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N),\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{grade}(J,N)\}.

In particular, in the case of an ordinary local ring, we have

\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\min\{0pt_{A}L+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N),\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+0pt_{B}N\}.

Let
\operatorname{cd}(I,L)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{cd}(J,N)\geq 1
. Using this result, we can demonstrate that
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
if and only if it is Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
. This, in turn, is equivalent to
L
and
N
being Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively. Moreover, if one of these equivalent conditions holds, then we have
\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)
, see Proposition
3.4
.
In particular, for the local case: Let
\dim_{A}L
and
\dim_{B}N
be positive. Then,
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay if and only if it is Cohen–Macaulay, which is further equivalent to
L
and
N
both being Cohen–Macaulay. Moreover, we have
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
, see a graded version of this result in
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
. Example
3.7
provides clarity on this result.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. We say that
M
has a
Cohen-Macaulay filtration with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if there exists a finite filtration
\mathcal{F}
:
0=M_{0}\varsubsetneq M_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq M_{r}=M
of
M
by submodules
M_{i}
, such that each quotient
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, and
0\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{1}/M_{0})<\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{2}/M_{1})<\dots<\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{r}/M_{r-1})
. If
M
admits a Cohen-Macaulay filtration with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, then we say that
M
is
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. Note that if
(R,\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
is a local ring, then
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field. Theorem
4.6
describes the sequentially Cohen-Macaulayness of
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
in terms of the sequentially Cohen-Macaulayness of
L
and
N
with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively. Moreover, if
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
, then we have
\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)
, provided that
\operatorname{grade}(I,L)>0
and
\operatorname{grade}(J,N)>0
, as shown in Proposition

.

In particular, in the local case,
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay as an
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-module if and only if
L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay over
A
and
B
, respectively. A graded version of this result can be seen in
[
10
, Theorem 2.11]
. Furthermore, if
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with

and

\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
.

2.
Cohen–Macaulayness

We recall a consequence of the Künneth formula.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
A
and
B
be
\Bbbk
-algebras. Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
are
A
-modules such that
L
is finitely generated, and
let
N
and
N^{\prime}
are
B
-modules such that
N
is finitely generated. For each
i\geq 0
, there are
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-module isomorphisms

(a)

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N,L^{\prime}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N^{\prime})\cong\underset{s+t=i}{\bigoplus}\operatorname{Ext}^{s}_{A}(L,L^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Ext}^{t}_{B}(N,N^{\prime})
.

(b)

\operatorname{Tor}^{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}_{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N,L^{\prime}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N^{\prime})\cong\underset{s+t=i}{\bigoplus}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{s}(L,L^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{t}(N,N^{\prime})
.

See for example
[
12
, Proposition A.1.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
A
and
B
be Noetherian
\Bbbk
-algebras such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian.
Let
I
be an ideal of
A
and
J
an ideal of
B
, and assume that
L
and
N
are modules over
A
and
B
, respectively.
Then, for each
i\geq 0
, there are
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-module isomorphisms

(a)

\text{H}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\cong\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
for
i\geq 0
.

(b)

\text{H}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\cong L\otimes_{\Bbbk}\text{H}_{J}^{i}(N)
for
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

(a): Consider the canonical flat homomorphism of Noetherian rings
f:A\to A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
. For every non-negative integer
i
, the independent theorem yields a natural
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-isomorphism

\text{H}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\cong\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N),

via the canonical ring homomorphism
f
.
For each
i\geq 0
, using Fact
2.1
(a), we have the following isomorphisms of
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-modules.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)&\cong\varinjlim\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Ext}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}\big((A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)/I^{n},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N\big)\\
&\cong\varinjlim\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Ext}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}(A/I^{n}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\\
&\cong\underset{s+t=i}{\bigoplus}\varinjlim\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Ext}^{s}_{A}(A/I^{n},L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Ext}^{t}_{B}(B,N)\\
&\cong\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}N.\\
\end{array}

Consequently,

\text{H}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\cong\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}N,

for
i\geq 0
, as desired. Part (b) is proved in the same way.
∎

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
, and
M
an
R
-module. The cohomological dimension of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)
,
is defined as the supremum of
all integers
i
for which
\text{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\neq 0
. It is important to note that if
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module, then
M=\mathfrak{a}M
if and only if
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)=-\infty
.

Now, let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\mathfrak{a}M\neq M
. The grade of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{a},M)
,
is defined as the infimum of
all integers
i
such that
\text{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\neq 0
. It is worth mentioning that
M=\mathfrak{a}M
if and only if
\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{a},M)=+\infty
.
(Recall that, by convension,
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
and
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
).

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
A
and
B
be
\Bbbk
-algebras. Let
I
be an ideal of
A
and
J
an ideal of
B
, and assume that
L
and
N
are finitely generated
modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. We set
\mathcal{I}=I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
.
Observe that,
\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
if and only if
IL\neq L
and
JN\neq N
.
In fact,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N&\Longleftrightarrow(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)/\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow((A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)/\mathcal{I})\otimes_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow(A/I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B/J)\otimes_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow(A/I\otimes_{A}L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}(B/J\otimes_{B}N)=0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow(L/IL)\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N/JN)=0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow L=IL\;or\;N=JN.\\
\end{array}

The third step follows from the following fact that

(2.3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J}\cong A/I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B/J.

The fourth step follows from Fact
2.1
(b), and the other steps are standard.

From Lemma
2.2
, one obtaines

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
A
and
B
be two Noetherian
\Bbbk
-algebras such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian.
Let
I
be an ideal of
A
and
J
an ideal of
B
, and assume that
L
and
N
are nonzero finitely generated
modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. Then

(a)

\operatorname{grade}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{grade}(I,L)
and
\operatorname{cd}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{cd}(I,L)
.

(b)

\operatorname{grade}(A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{grade}(J,N)
and
\operatorname{cd}(A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{cd}(J,N)
.

Proof.

(a): We first assume that
(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
. This is equivalent to saying that
L=IL
by Remark
2.3
.
Thus,
\operatorname{grade}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{grade}(I,L)=+\infty
, and so the desired equality holds in this case. Thus, we may assume that
(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
. This is equivalent to saying that
L\neq IL
by Remark
2.3
. Thus, in view of Lemma
2.2
(a), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{grade}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)&=\inf\{i:\text{H}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq 0\}\\
&=\inf\{i:\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}N\neq 0\}\\
&=\inf\{i:\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\neq 0\}\\
&=\operatorname{grade}(I,L).\end{array}

Similarly, if
(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, then
\operatorname{cd}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{cd}(I,L)=-\infty
, and so the desired equality holds. If

(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, then in view of Lemma
2.2
(b), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)&=\sup\{i:\text{H}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}^{i}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq 0\}\\
&=\sup\{i:\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}N\neq 0\}\\
&=\sup\{i:\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\neq 0\}\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L).\end{array}

Part (b) is proved similarly.
∎

E_{2}^{s,t}=E_{\infty}^{s,t}\cong U^{s}/U^{s-1}\cong U^{s}=U^{n}=\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N).

This yields the first isomorphism in (a). The second corner isomorphism (b) can be proven using the same method.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
A
and

\Bbbk

.

(b)

\operatorname{cd}(I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J,L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{cd}(I,L)+\operatorname{cd}(J,N)

(a): We define
\mathcal{I}=I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
. If
\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, then

. According to Remark

L=IL
or

or

\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N

and

)

\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\geq t
.

for

.

\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, and so

and

.
Let

. We claim that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=t
. First, we show that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\leq t
. In order to show this,
we claim that
\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=0

0=U^{-1}\subseteq U^{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq U^{n}=\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)

of submodules
\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
such that

for

.

E_{2}^{p,n-p}=0
for

. Moreover,

is a subquotient of

3.
The finiteness dimension

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. The
finiteness dimension of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
, is defined as the infimum of non-negative integers
i
for which
\text{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is not finitely generated. Note that
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
can be either a positive integer or
+\infty
.
If
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)\geq 1
, then
\text{H}^{\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is not finitely generated, see for instance
[
3
, Corollary 3.3(i)]
.
Thus, in this case, one has
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say
M
is
generalized Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)\leq 0
or
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)
, as defined in
[
4
]
.

We observe that if
(R,\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
is a local ring, then
M
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
M
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a filed, and let
A
and
B
be two Noetherian local
\Bbbk
-algebras such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian.
Let
I
be an ideal of
A
and
J
an ideal of
B
, and assume that
L
and
N
are nonzero finitely generated
modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. From Lemma
2.2
one obtaines

\operatorname{f}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\operatorname{f}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{J}(N).

In fact,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{f}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)&=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\text{H}^{i}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\;\text{is not finitely generated}\;\}\\
&=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}N\;\text{is not finitely generated}\;\}\\
&=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\text{H}_{I}^{i}(L)\;\text{is not finitely generated}\;\}\\
&=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L).\\
\end{array}

The second part can be seen similarly.
As the main result of this section, we extend this fact as follows:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a filed, and let
A
and
B
be two Noetherian local
\Bbbk
-algebras such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian.
Let
I
be an ideal of
A
and
J
an ideal of
B
, and assume that
L
and
N
are nonzero finitely generated
modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. Then

\operatorname{f}_{I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\min\{\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N),\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{grade}(J,N)\}.

Proof.

We define
\mathcal{I}=I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
and initially assume that
\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
. Thus,
\text{H}^{i}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=0
for all
i
, implying that
\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=+\infty
. As per Remark
2.3
, we have
IL=L
or
JN=N
. If
IL=L
and
JN=N
, then
\operatorname{grade}(I,L)=\operatorname{grade}(J,N)=+\infty
and
\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)=\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)=+\infty
. Thus the desired equality holds in this case. If
IL=L
and
JN\neq N
, then
\operatorname{grade}(I,L)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)=+\infty
,
\operatorname{grade}(J,N)<\infty
, and
\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)
is a positive integer or
+\infty
. Thus, the required equality holds in this case as well.
Therefore, we can assume that
\mathcal{I}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
, and so
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\geq 0
. If
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=0
, then by Theorem
2.6
(b), we have
\operatorname{cd}(I,L)=\operatorname{cd}(J,N)=0
. Consequently,
\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)=\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)=+\infty
, and therefore the claimed equality holds in this case as well. Thus, we may assume that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\geq 1
.
Consider the spectral sequence of (
2.5.1
)

E_{2}^{i,j}\cong\text{H}^{i}_{I}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\text{H}_{J}^{j}(N)\underset{i}{\Longrightarrow}\text{H}^{i+j}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N),

and set
t=\min\{i+j:E_{2}^{i,j}\;\text{is not finitely generated}\}
. We claim that
f_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=t
.
First, we show that
f_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\geq t
.
In order to show this, we claim that
\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
is finitely generated for
n<t
. In light of (
2.5.1
), a chain

0=U^{-1}\subseteq U^{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq U^{n}=\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)

of submodules
\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
exists such that
U^{p}/U^{p-1}\cong E_{\infty}^{p,n-p}
for
p=0,\dots,n
.
Since
n<t
, it follows that
E_{2}^{p,n-p}
is finitely generated for
p=0,\dots,n
.
Moreover,
E_{\infty}^{p,n-p}
is a subquotient of
E_{2}^{p,n-p}
, so it follows that
E_{\infty}^{p,n-p}
is also finitely generated for
p=0,\dots,n
.
Let
p=0
. Thus,
U^{0}\cong U^{0}/U^{-1}=E_{\infty}^{0,n}
is finitely generated. For
p=1
, we have
U^{1}/U^{0}\cong E_{\infty}^{1,n-1}
is finitely generated. So,
the exact sequence
0\to U^{0}\to U^{1}\to U^{1}/U^{0}\to 0
yields
U^{1}
is finitely generated. Continuing in this way, we obtain that
U^{n}=\text{H}^{n}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
is
finitely generated for
n<t
. Therefore,
\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\geq t
.

In order to show the reverse inequality,
f_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\leq t
, it suffices to show that

\text{H}^{t}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
is not finitely generated. According to (
2.5.1
), there exists a chain

0=U^{-1}\subseteq U^{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq U^{t}=\text{H}^{t}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)

of submodules
\text{H}^{t}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
such that
U^{p}/U^{p-1}\cong E_{\infty}^{p,t-p}
for
p=0,\dots,t
. For
p=t
, we have the following exact sequence,

0\to U^{t-1}\to\text{H}^{t}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\to E_{\infty}^{t,0}\to 0.

To complete our proof, we only need to show that
E_{\infty}^{t,0}
is not finitely generated.
Let’s consider
r\geq 2
and the following differentials

\dots\to E_{r}^{t-2r,2r-2}\xrightarrow{d_{r}^{t-2r,2r-2}}E_{r}^{t-r,r-1}\xrightarrow{d_{r}^{t-r,r-1}}E_{r}^{t,0}\xrightarrow{d_{r}^{t,0}}E_{r}^{t+r,1-r}=0.

Here,
E_{r}^{t+r,1-r}=0
for
r\geq 2
because
E_{r}^{t+r,1-r}
is a subquotient of
E_{2}^{t+r,1-r}
, and
E_{2}^{t+r,1-r}=0
for
r\geq 2
.
As

E_{r+1}^{t,0}=\ker d_{r}^{t,0}/\operatorname{im}d_{r}^{t-r,r-1}=E_{r}^{t,0}/\operatorname{im}d_{r}^{t-r,r-1},

we have the following exact sequence

(3.2.1)

\displaystyle 0\to\operatorname{im}d_{r}^{t-r,r-1}\to E_{r}^{t,0}\to E_{r+1}^{t,0}\to 0.

Note that if
i+j<t
, then
E_{2}^{i,j}
is finitely generated. Hence
E_{r}^{i,j}
is finitely generated for
i+j<t
and
r\geq 2
.
In particular,
E_{r}^{t-r,r-1}
is finitely generated, and so
\operatorname{im}d_{r}^{t-r,r-1}
is finitely generated.
Now, on the contrary suppose
E_{\infty}^{t,0}
is finitely generated. Since
E_{r}^{t,0}=E_{\infty}^{t,0}
for
r
sufficiently large,
we have that
E_{r}^{t,0}
is finitely generated. Fix
r
and suppose
E_{r+1}^{t,0}
is finitely generated. From the exact sequence (
3.2.1
), we obtain that
E_{r}^{t,0}
is finitely generated.
Continuing in this fashion, we see that
E_{r}^{t,0}
is finitely generated for all
r\geq 2
. In particular,
E_{2}^{t,0}
is finitely generated, a contradiction. Consequently,

\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\leq t
. Notice that

\begin{array}[]{ll}t&=\min\{i+j:E_{2}^{i,j}\;\text{is not finitely generated}\}\\
&=\min\{i+j:\text{H}^{i}_{I}(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\text{H}_{J}^{j}(N)\;\text{is not finitely generated}\}\\
&=\min\big\{\min\{i:\text{H}^{i}_{I}(L)\neq 0\}+\min\{j:\text{H}_{J}^{j}(N)\;\text{is not finitely generated}\},\\
&\min\{i:\text{H}^{i}_{I}(L)\;\text{is not finitely generated}\}+\min\{j:\text{H}_{J}^{j}(N)\neq 0\}\big\}\\
&=\min\{\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N),\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{grade}(J,N)\}.\\
\end{array}

Therefore, the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field , and let
A
and
B
be
\Bbbk
-algebras. Let
I
be an ideal of
A
and
J
an ideal of
B
, and assume that
L
and
N
are nonzero finitely generated
modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. Suppose
\operatorname{cd}(I,L)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{cd}(J,N)\geq 1
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
;

(b)

L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
;

(c)

L
and
N
are Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively.

Moreover, if one of these equivalent conditions holds, then we have

\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N),

where
\mathcal{I}=I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
.

Proof.

(a)\Rightarrow(c)
: Suppose
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is generalized
Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
. As
\operatorname{cd}(I,L)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{cd}(J,N)\geq 1
, we have

\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\geq 2
. Observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(I,L)+\operatorname{cd}(J,N)&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\\
&=\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\\
&=\min\{\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N),\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{grade}(J,N)\}\\
&\leq\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)\\
&\leq\operatorname{cd}(I,L)+\operatorname{cd}(J,N).\\
\end{array}

The first step is by Theorem
2.6
(b). Our assumption yields the second step. Since,
\operatorname{cd}(I,L)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{cd}(J,N)\geq 1
, it follows that

\operatorname{grade}(I,L),\operatorname{grade}(J,N),\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)
are finite numbers. Hence the third step is by Theorem
3.2
, and the remaining steps are standard.
Consequently,

\operatorname{cd}(I,L)+\operatorname{cd}(J,N)=\operatorname{grade}(I,L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N).

Similarly, one obtains

\operatorname{cd}(I,L)+\operatorname{cd}(J,N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{grade}(J,N).

Therefore,
\operatorname{grade}(I,L)=\operatorname{cd}(I,L)
and
\operatorname{grade}(J,N)=\operatorname{cd}(J,N)
; so that
L
and
N
are Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively, as desired.

(c)\Longleftrightarrow(b)
: follows from Corollary
2.7
.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)
: is clear.
∎

A graded version of the following result can be seen in
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
and
(B,\mathfrak{n})
be two Noetherian local algebras over a field
\Bbbk
such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian. We also suppose
that
\Bbbk
is the common residue field of both
A
and
B
, and either
A
or
B
is algebraic over
\Bbbk
. Let
L
and
N
be nonzero finitely generated
modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. Assume that both
\dim_{A}L
and
\dim_{B}N
are positive. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay;

(b)

L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is Cohen–Macaulay;

(c)

L
and
N
are Cohen–Macaulay.

Moreover, if one of these equivalent conditions holds, then we have

\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N),

where
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{n}
.

Proof.

The assertions follow from Remark
2.8
and Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We observed that, if one of the equivalent conditions of Proposition
3.4
holds, then we have

\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N).

However, the converse is not true in general. In fact, this equality holds for sequentially Cohen–Macaulay modules which is a generalization of Cohen–Macaulay modules, as shown in Proposition
4.10
.

The following example shows that if any of the equivalent conditions stated in Corollary
3.5
does not hold, then
the equality
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
is no longer valid.

Example 3.7
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field. We set
L=\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2}]/\langle x_{1}^{2},x_{1}x_{2}\rangle
and consider it as an
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2}]
-module. We also set
N=\Bbbk[y_{1},y_{2}]/\langle y_{1}\rangle
and consider it as an
\Bbbk[y_{1},y_{2}]
-module. It can be observed that
L
has depth
0
and dimension
1
. Thus
L
is not Cohen–Macaulay, and
f_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)=1
, where
\mathfrak{m}=\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle
is the unique graded maximal ideal of
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2}]
. Moreover,
N
is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension
1
, so
f_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)=1
, where
\mathfrak{n}=\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle
is the unique graded maximal ideal of
\Bbbk[y_{1},y_{2}]
. Now, we consider
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N=\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]/\langle x_{1}^{2},x_{1}x_{2},y_{1}\rangle
as an
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]
-module. According to Corollary
3.3
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)&=\min\{0pt_{A}L+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N),\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+0pt_{B}N\}\\
&=\min\{0+1,1+1\}\\
&=1,\\
\end{array}

where
\mathfrak{M}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\rangle=\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{n}
is the unique graded maximal ideal of
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]
. Here,
A=\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2}]
and
B=\Bbbk[y_{1},y_{2}]
. Consequently,

1=f_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq f_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+f_{\mathfrak{n}}(L)=2.

Moreover,
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is not generalized Cohen–Macaulay, because
1=f_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)\neq\dim_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N=2
.

4.
Sequentially Cohen–Macaulayness

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module.

Definition 4.1
.

A filtration
\mathscr{D}
:
0=D_{0}\varsubsetneq D_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq D_{r}=M
of
M
by submodules
M_{i}
is called the
dimension filtration of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if
D_{i-1}
is the largest submodule of
D_{i}
for which
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},D_{i-1})<\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},D_{i})

for all
i=1,\dots,r
.

A finite filtration
\mathcal{F}:0=M_{0}\varsubsetneq M_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq M_{r}=M
of
M
by submodules
M_{i}
is called a

Cohen-Macaulay filtration with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if each quotient
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
and

0\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{1}/M_{0})<\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{2}/M_{1})<\dots<\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{r}/M_{r-1})
. If
M
admits a Cohen-Macaulay filtration with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
,
then we say
M
is
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. Note that if
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
,
then the filtration
\mathscr{F}
is uniquely determined and it is just the dimension filtration of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
,
i.e.,
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{D}
. The proof of this fact is based on the same method as in the proof of
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]

by replacing the ring
R
and the general ideal
\mathfrak{a}
with the polynomial ring
S
and the ideal
Q
, respectively.
Notice that every Cohen–Macaulay module with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
is sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore, we may consider
the zero module to be sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
.
We observe that if
(R,\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
is a local ring, then
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. We recall the following fact which will be used in the sequel.

Fact 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be the dimension filtration of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. Then

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}D_{i}/D_{i-1}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M:\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},D_{i})\}.

This fact is also proved in the same way as the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 1.5]
by replacing the ring
R
and the general ideal
\mathfrak{a}
with the polynomial ring
S
and the ideal
Q
, respectively.

Fact 4.3
.

The exact sequence
0\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
of finitely generated
R
-modules yields
\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M)=\max\{\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M^{\prime}),\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M^{\prime\prime})\}
,
see
[
2
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Going forward, we will assume that
\Bbbk
is a field, and that
A
and
B
are two Noetherian
\Bbbk
-algebras such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian. Let
I
and
J
be two ideals of
A
and
B
, respectively. As before, we define
\mathcal{I}
to be
I\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}J
. We also assume that
(A,\mathfrak{m})
,
(B,\mathfrak{n})
and
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{n}
are as described in Remark
2.8
. Additionally, let
L
and
N
be nonzero finitely generated modules over
A
and
B
, respectively. We shall need the following fact.

Fact 4.4
.

Continue with the notation and assumptions as above. Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field. Then

\operatorname{Ass}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{p}_{2}:\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}L\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{p}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{B}N\},

see
[
5
, Corollary 2.8]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Continue with the notation and assumptions as above. Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field. Let
\mathscr{F}
:
0=L_{0}\varsubsetneq L_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r}=L

be the dimension filtration of
L
with respect to
I
, and let

\mathscr{G}
:
0=N_{0}\varsubsetneq N_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq N_{s}=N
be the dimension filtration of
N
with respect to
J
.
Then
\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{F}
is the dimension filtration of
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Let
s\leq r
. We claim that

\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{F}:0=L_{0}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{0}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{s-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{s-1}\varsubsetneq L_{s}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N

is the dimension filtration of
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
. The argument for
r\leq s
is similar.
For
i=1,\dots,s
, the strict inclusions follow from the fact that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i-1})\\
&<\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i}).\\
\end{array}

Theorem
2.6
(b) yields the first and third steps, and the second step follows from our assumptions.
For
i=s+1,\dots,r
, we have the following display

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N)\\
&<\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N)\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N).\\
\end{array}

To complete our proof, for
i=1,\dots,s
, we need to show that
L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}
is the largest submodule of
L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i}

for which
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})<\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})
. Moreover, for
i=s+1,\dots,r
, a similar argument shows that
L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is the largest submodule of
L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
for which
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)<\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)
.
Let
K
be the largest submodule of
L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i}
such that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K)<\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})
. We want to show that
L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}=K
.
By Fact
4.3
, we have

\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K\oplus(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}))=\max\{\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K),\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})\}<\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i}).

Thus, the maximality of
K
yields
K=K\oplus(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})
. Hence,
L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}\subseteq K\varsubsetneq L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i}
.
On the contrary, suppose that
L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}\neq K
. Observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\emptyset\neq\operatorname{Ass}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})&\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})\\
&=\operatorname{Ass}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N_{i}/N_{i-1})\\
&=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{p}_{2}:\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}L_{i}/L_{i-1}\;\;\text{and}\\
&\mathfrak{p}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{B}N_{i}/N_{i-1}\}.\\
\end{array}

The exact sequence

0\to K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})\to(L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})

yields the first step in this sequence. The second step is standard. Fact
4.4
implies the third step.
Thus, there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B}K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})
such that
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B+A\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
where
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}L_{i}/L_{i-1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{B}N_{i}/N_{i-1}
.
Observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(I,A/\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,B/\mathfrak{p}_{2})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},A/\mathfrak{p}_{1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}B/\mathfrak{p}_{2})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},(A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)/\mathfrak{q})\\
&\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}))\\
&\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K).\\
\end{array}

The first and third steps follow from Theorem
2.6
(b). As
\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i-1})<\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i})
, the exact sequence
0\to L_{i-1}\to L_{i}\to L_{i}/L_{i-1}\to 0
yields

\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i})=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})
by Fact
4.3
. Thus, Fact
4.2
implies the second step. The fourth step is by (
2.3.1
). The exact sequence

0\to(A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)/\mathfrak{q}\to K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})

yields
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},(A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B)/\mathfrak{q})\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})
by Fact
4.3
. Thus, the fifth step follows. The exact sequence

K\to K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1})\to 0

yields
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}))\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K)
again by Fact
4.3
. So, the sixth step follows.
Consequently,
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},K)
, a contradiction.
Therefore,
L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}=K
and so the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Continue with the notation and assumptions as above. Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
;

(b)

L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively.

Proof.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
: Assume that
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathcal{I}
.
Let
\mathscr{F}:0=L_{0}\varsubsetneq L_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r}=L
be the dimension filtration of
L
with respect to
I
,
and let
\mathscr{G}:0=N_{0}\varsubsetneq N_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq N_{s}=N
be the dimension filtration of
N
with respect to
J
.
By Lemma
4.5
, we may assume that
r=s
and

\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{F}:0=L_{0}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{0}\varsubsetneq L_{1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{r}=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N

is the dimension filtration of
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
.
Our assumption implies that
\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{F}
is the Cohen-Macaulay filtration of
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
.
Observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{grade}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{grade}(J,N_{i}/N_{i-1})&=\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N_{i}/N_{i-1}))\\
&=\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}))\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i})/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{i-1}))\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N_{i}/N_{i-1}))\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i}/N_{i-1}).\\
\end{array}

The first step follows from Theorem
2.6
(a). The second and fourth steps are standard. Our assumption yields the third step and the final step follows from Theorem
2.6
(b).
Consequently,
\operatorname{grade}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})
and
\operatorname{grade}(J,N_{i}/N_{i-1})=\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i}/N_{i-1})
; so that
L_{i}/L_{i-1}
is Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and

N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
J
for
i=1,\dots,r
.

On the other hand, our assumptions yield

\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i})<\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i+1})=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i+1}/L_{i}),

and

\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i}/N_{i-1})=\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i})<\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i+1})=\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{i+1}/N_{i}),

for
i=1,\dots,r
.
Therefore,
L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)
:
Suppose
L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively.
Thus, there exists a Cohen-Macaulay filtration for
L
with respect to
I
, given by
\mathscr{F}:0=L_{0}\varsubsetneq L_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r}=L
.
Similarly, there exists a Cohen-Macaulay filtration for
N
with respect to
J
, given by
\mathscr{G}:0=N_{0}\varsubsetneq N_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq N_{s}=N
.
Assuming
s\leq r
, we assert that the filtration given by

\displaystyle 0=L_{0}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1}\varsubsetneq L_{1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1}\varsubsetneq L_{2}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r-s+1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1}\varsubsetneq

\displaystyle L_{r-s+2}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{2}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{s-1}\varsubsetneq L_{r}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{s}=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N,

is a Cohen-Macaulay filtration for
L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
. A similar argument can be made for
r\leq s
.
For
0\leq i\leq r-s
, we set

M_{i,1}=(L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1})/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1})\cong(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1}.

By using Theorem
2.6
, we observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},M_{i,1})&=\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1})\\
&=\operatorname{grade}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{grade}(J,N_{1})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{1})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{1})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},M_{i,1}).\\
\end{array}

On the other hand,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},M_{i,1})&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{1})\\
&<\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i+1}/L_{i})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{1})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},M_{i+1,1}).\\
\end{array}

For
r-s+1\leq i\leq r
and
1\leq j\leq s
, we set

T_{i,j}=(L_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{j})/(L_{i-1}\otimes_{\Bbbk}N_{j-1})\cong(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N_{j}/N_{j-1}).

Again, by using Theorem
2.6
, we have the following display

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},T_{i,j})&=\operatorname{grade}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N_{j}/N_{j-1}))\\
&=\operatorname{grade}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{grade}(J,N_{i}/N_{i-1})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{j}/N_{j-1})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}(N_{j}/N_{j-1}))\\
.&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},T_{i,j}).\\
\end{array}

On the other hand,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},T_{i,j})&=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i}/L_{i-1})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{j}/N_{j-1})\\
&<\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{i+1}/L_{i})+\operatorname{cd}(J,N_{j+1}/N_{j})\\
&=\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{I},T_{i+1,j+1}).\\
\end{array}

Therefore, the proof is complete.
∎

As a further implication of Theorem

.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field. Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
and

be two Noetherian local algebras over a field
\Bbbk
such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian. We also assume

, and either

.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
-module;

(b)

L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay over
A
and

, respectively.

We recall the following fact:

Fact 4.9
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of

, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. Assume that
M
is sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
with the Cohen–Macaulay filtration
\mathcal{F}
:
0=M_{0}\varsubsetneq M_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq M_{r}=M
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. Then

(a)

\text{H}^{c_{i}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\cong\text{H}^{c_{i}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M_{i})\cong\text{H}^{c_{i}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M_{i}/M_{i-1})
where
c_{i}=\operatorname{cd}(\mathfrak{a},M_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,r
and
\text{H}^{k}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=0
for
k\not\in\{c_{1},\dots,c_{r}\}
, see
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
.

(b)

\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{a},M_{i})=\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{a},M)
for
i=1,\dots,r
, see
[
9
, Fact 3.3]
.

We remark that the proof of these facts is based on the same method as in the proofs of
[
9
, Lemma 3.2 and Fact 3.3]
by replacing the ring
R
and the general ideal
\mathfrak{a}
with the polynomial ring
S
and the ideal
Q
, respectively.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
and
J
, respectively.
Assume that
\operatorname{grade}(I,L)>0
and
\operatorname{grade}(J,N)>0
. Then

\operatorname{f}_{\mathcal{I}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{J}(N).

Proof.

Based on our assumptions, there is a Cohen-Macaulay filtration for
L
with respect to
I
, represented as
\mathscr{F}:0=L_{0}\varsubsetneq L_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq L_{r}=L
, and a Cohen-Macaulay filtration for
N
with respect to
J
, represented as
\mathscr{G}:0=N_{0}\varsubsetneq N_{1}\varsubsetneq\dots\varsubsetneq N_{s}=N
.
It can be observed that

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}_{I}(L)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cd}(I,L_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{grade}(I,L_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{grade}(I,L).

The first step in this sequence follows from Fact
4.9
(a). Since
L_{1}
is Cohen–Macaulay with respect to
I
, the second step follows. The third step follows from Fact
4.9
(b). Similarly,
\operatorname{f}_{J}(N)=\operatorname{grade}(J,N)
.
The assertion now follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
and
(B,\mathfrak{n})
be two Noetherian local algebras over a field
\Bbbk
such that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}B
is Noetherian. We also assume
that
\Bbbk
is the common residue field of both
A
and
B
, and either
A
or
B
is algebraic over
\Bbbk
. Let
L
and
N
be sequentially Cohen–Macaulay modules with
0pt_{A}L>0
and
0pt_{B}N>0
. Then

\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N).

Remark 4.12
.

Example
3.7
illustrates that the conditions
0pt_{A}L>0
and
0pt_{B}N>0
in Corollary
4.11
are necessary. In fact, since any
R
-module
M
of dimension less than or equal to one is sequentially Cohen–Macaulay, we have that
L
and
N
are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay. However, it should be noted that
0pt_{A}L=0
, so the equality
\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L\otimes_{\Bbbk}N)=\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)+\operatorname{f}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
does not hold.

Complex to Rational Fast Matrix Multiplication
Yoav Moran, Oded Schwartz, Shuncheng Yuan

Abstract
Fast matrix multiplication algorithms are asymptotically faster than the classical cubic-time algorithm, but they are often slower in practice. One important obstacle is the use of complex coefficients, which increases arithmetic overhead and limits practical efficiency. This paper focuses on transforming complex-coefficient matrix multiplication schemes into equivalent real- or rational-coefficient ones. We present a systematic method that, given a complex-coefficient scheme, either constructs a family of equivalent rational algorithms or proves that no equivalent rational scheme exists. Our approach relies only on basic linear-algebraic properties of similarity transformations of complex matrices. This method recovers the previously known ad hoc results of Dumas, Pernet, and Sedoglavic (2025) and extends them to more general settings, including algorithms involving rational coefficients and square roots, with i = − 1 i=\sqrt{-1} as a special case. Using this framework, we show that no rational scheme is equivalent to the Smirnov’s ⟨ 4 , 4 , 9 , 104 ⟩ \langle 4,4,9,104\rangle ℚ ⁡ [ 161 ] \mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{161}] algorithm (2022) and that no real scheme is equivalent to the ⟨ 4 , 4 , 4 , 48 ⟩ \langle 4,4,4,48\rangle complex algorithm of Kaporin (2024). More generally, our approach can also be used to prove the non-existence of integer-coefficient schemes.

De-Groote Action

Given a matrix multiplication tensor
\sum_{j=1}^{r}O_{j}\otimes P_{j}\otimes Q_{j}

[

]

For a tensor, the following is true

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}O_{j}\otimes P_{j}\otimes Q_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}O_{j}\otimes\alpha_{j}P_{j}\otimes Q_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}O_{j}\otimes P_{j}\otimes\alpha_{j}Q_{j}

M_{j}=O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}
.

2. Solve
SM_{j}=\overline{M_{j}}S
,
\overline{S}S=I

j=1,\dots,r
simultaneously.

3. If
S
exists

Go to 4.

Else

Output Null

4. Compute the basis
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
for the fixed point space of
x\to\overline{x}S
.

5. Form
X=[x_{1};\dots;x_{n}]
.

6. Repeat 1-5 with
M_{j}=P_{j}Q_{j}O_{j}
and
M_{j}=Q_{j}O_{j}P_{j}
to form
Y,Z
.

At first glance, this algorithm only ensures that the matrices
UO_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}U^{-1}
,
VP_{j}Q_{j}O_{j}V^{-1}
,
WQ_{j}O_{j}P_{j}W^{-1}
are all in
\mathbb{F}
. In principle, it might happen that for some
j
,
UO_{j}V^{-1}
or
VP_{j}W^{-1}
or
WQ_{j}U^{-1}
are not in the form of
\alpha R
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}]
and
R\in M(\mathbb{F})
. For example,

\displaystyle XO_{1}Y^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},YP_{1}Z^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}-i&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},ZQ_{1}X^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

satisfy that
XO_{1}P_{1}Q_{1}X^{-1}

YP_{1}Q_{1}O_{1}Y^{-1},ZQ_{1}O_{1}P_{1}Z^{-1}
are all identity matrices, but it is not in
\mathbb{F}
up to a scalar.

However, this cannot happen if the
S
in the algorithm is unique up to a scalar
\alpha
with
\alpha\overline{\alpha}=1
, i.e. if
S,S^{\prime}
are both such that
SO_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}=\overline{O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}}S
and
S\overline{S}=I
, then
S=\alpha S^{\prime}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}]
with
\alpha\overline{\alpha}=1
. To show this, we first prove a lemma.

Lemma 4
.

For a matrix multiplication tensor
\sum_{j=1}^{r}O_{j}\otimes P_{j}\otimes Q_{j}
over
\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}]
, suppose that there exists one triple
X,Y,Z
that is derived from the algorithm in STEP 4, such that
XO_{j}Y^{-1}=\alpha_{1}R_{1}
,
YP_{j}Z^{-1}=\alpha_{2}R_{2}
,
ZQ_{j}X^{-1}=\alpha_{3}R_{3}
, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}]
with
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{F}
, and
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
. Then, for every possible
\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z}
derived from the algorithm,
\widetilde{X}O_{j}\widetilde{Y}^{-1}
,
\widetilde{Y}P_{j}\widetilde{Z}^{-1}
and
\widetilde{Z}Q_{j}\widetilde{X}^{-1}
have the same form.

Proof.

Both
X
and
\widetilde{X}
satisfy

\displaystyle\overline{X}S=X\qquad\overline{\widetilde{X}}S=\widetilde{X}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\overline{\widetilde{X}X^{-1}}=\overline{\widetilde{X}}\overline{X^{-1}}=\overline{\widetilde{X}}(\overline{\overline{X}S)^{-1}}=\overline{\widetilde{X}}\overline{S^{-1}\overline{X}^{-1}}=(\overline{\widetilde{X}}S)X^{-1}=\widetilde{X}X^{-1}.

Thus,
\widetilde{X}X^{-1}
is in
\mathbb{F}
. Similar arguments show that
\widetilde{Y}Y^{-1}
and
\widetilde{Z}Z^{-1}
are real. Now

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}O_{j}\widetilde{Y}^{-1}=\widetilde{X}X^{-1}XO_{j}Y^{-1}(\widetilde{Y}Y^{-1})^{-1}=\alpha_{1}\widetilde{X}X^{-1}R_{1}(\widetilde{Y}Y^{-1})^{-1}.

Repeating this with
\widetilde{Y}P_{j}\widetilde{Z}^{-1}
and
\widetilde{Z}Q_{j}\widetilde{X}^{-1}
completes the proof.
∎

This lemma shows that the choice of basis in STEP 4 of the algorithm does not matter. If one choice of basis works, then all bases work. The following theorem shows that the previous potential problem does not occur.

Theorem 3
.

For a matrix multiplication tensor
\sum_{j=1}^{r}O_{j}\otimes P_{j}\otimes Q_{j}
over
\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}]
, suppose there exist
X,Y,Z
such that
XO_{j}Y^{-1}\otimes VP_{j}W^{-1}\otimes WQ_{j}U^{-1}
are all in
\mathbb{F}
up to a scalar. If
S
in the algorithm exists and is unique up to a scalar
\alpha
with
\alpha\overline{\alpha}=1
, then
\widetilde{X}O_{j}\widetilde{Y}^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{Y}P_{j}\widetilde{Z}^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{Z}Q_{j}\widetilde{X}^{-1}
are all in
\mathbb{F}
for any
\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z}
computed by the algorithm.

Proof.

Suppose
X,Y,Z
are such that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}XO_{j}Y^{-1}\otimes YP_{j}Z^{-1}\otimes ZQ_{j}X^{-1}
is in
\mathbb{F}
. Then
S=\overline{X^{-1}}X
satisfies
SO_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}=\overline{O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}}S
and
S\overline{S}=I
. If we assume
S
is unique in the above sense, then
\widetilde{S}
and
\widetilde{X}
computed by the algorithm satisfy
\widetilde{S}=\alpha S
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}]
, and
\overline{\widetilde{X}}\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{X}
.
By Theorem
2
, there exists
T\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}[\sqrt{d}])
, such that
\overline{T}^{-1}T=\alpha I
. It follows that

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}=\alpha S=(\overline{TX})^{-1}TX

(14)

This means the rows of
X
and
\widetilde{X}
form bases for the fixed point space of
x\to\overline{x}S
.
Together with Proposition
4
, this gives the result.
∎

3.2
Integer algorithms

Once a
\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{d}]
tensor is transformed to a
\mathbb{Q}
tensor, one may ask further if there exist
X,Y,Z
such
\sum_{j=1}^{r}XO_{j}Y^{-1}\otimes YP_{j}Z^{-1}\otimes ZQ_{j}X^{-1}
is an integer tensor for even faster computation. In this section, we show a way to check existence by examining the trace of
O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}
.

If in a matrix multiplication tensor there exists
j
such that trace of
O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}
is not an integer, then by Proposition
3
below, there do not exist
X,Y,Z
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}XO_{j}Y^{-1}\otimes YP_{j}Z^{-1}\otimes ZQ_{j}X^{-1}
is a tensor with only integer matrices (up to scalar). This actually shows that the
\langle 3,4,7,63\rangle
complex algorithm found by the Google DeepMind team and the
\langle 3,3,6,40\rangle

\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
algorithm found by Smirnov cannot be transformed into integer algorithms, because in both cases
\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j})\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for some
j
.

If all
O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}
have integer trace, for example, all
O_{j}P_{j}Q_{j}
’s in
\langle 4,4,4,48\rangle
tensor over
\mathbb{Q}[i]
found by Google DeepMind team have trace 1 or 2, then we can next examine the
\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{1}}P_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{1}}\cdot O_{j_{2}}P_{j_{2}}Q_{j_{2}})
for all
j_{1},j_{2}=1,\dots,r
. As it turns out, there exist
j_{1},j_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{1}}P_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{1}})=1

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{2}}P_{j_{2}}Q_{j_{2}})=1

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{1}}P_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{1}}\cdot O_{j_{2}}P_{j_{2}}Q_{j_{2}})=1/2

This implies that the
\mathbb{Z}
equivalent form of this algorithm does not exist. Similarly, the
\langle 2,4,5,32\rangle
algorithm over
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
by Hopcroft and Kerr does not have a
\mathbb{Z}
equivalent form for the same reason.

Finally, the
\langle 2,4,4,26\rangle
algorithm over
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
also does not have a
\mathbb{Z}
equivalent. This algorithm satisfies that for all
j=1,\dots,r
and for all pairs
j_{1},j_{2}=1,\dots,r

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{1}}P_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{1}})\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{1}}P_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{1}}\cdot O_{j_{2}}P_{j_{2}}Q_{j_{2}})\in\mathbb{Z}.

However, there exist
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trace}(O_{j_{1}}P_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{1}}\cdot O_{j_{2}}P_{j_{2}}Q_{j_{2}}\cdot O_{j_{3}}P_{j_{3}}Q_{j_{3}})\not\in\mathbb{Z},

which shows the non-existence of a
\mathbb{Z}
equivalent form.

In general, we can use the following Proposition.

Proposition 3
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Suppose that there exist
j_{1},\dots,j_{k}
such that
\operatorname{Trace}(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}A_{j_{\ell}})\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, then there does not exist
X
invertible, such that
XA_{j}X^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
for every
j
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is an
X
such that
XA_{j}X^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
for every
j
. Then for every indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{k}
,

\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(XA_{j_{\ell}}X^{-1})=X(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}A_{j_{\ell}})X^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}).

Hence
\operatorname{Trace(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}A_{j_{\ell}})}\in\mathbb{Z}
. This proves the contrapositive of the proposition.
∎

5
Open Problems

1.

In this paper, we find a sufficient condition for a
\mathbb{Z}
equivalent algorithm not to exist. It is useful to find a necessary and sufficient condition for an integer algorithm to be achieved using De-Groote actions.

2.

We argue that a problem like
3.1
will not occur if
S
is unique up to a scalar. Although
S
is unique in all cases we see, it is interesting to argue whether the above problem can be avoided.

3.

Many fast matrix algorithms do not have an equivalent form over a reduced ring.
Another future direction is using the alternative basis method to bridge this gap.
The alternative basis method was introduced by Karstadt and Schwartz to reduce the leading coefficient of complexity of fast matrix multiplication algorithms by sparsifying the encoding matrices
[
28
]
.

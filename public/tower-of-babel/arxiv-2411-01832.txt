Construction of Curves with a Controlled First Slope using p p -Symmetric Numbers
Robert Moore, Hui June Zhu

Abstract
. This paper establishes a constructive link between the first slope of the Artin-Schreier curve X f : y p − y = f ⁡ ( x ) X_{f}:y^{p}-y=f(x) along with its length and the p p -adic weight of the support of f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) . If the maximal p p -adic weight element ν \nu in Supp ⁡ ( f ) \Supp(f) is unique, we show that the first slope’s lower bound of 1 / s p ​ ( ν ) 1/s_{p}(\nu) is achieved if and only if ν \nu satisfies an elementary combinatorial p p -adic condition, which we define as p p -symmetry . In this case, we give the length of the first slope segment explicitly. As an application, we construct explicit families of curves in every characteristic p p with first slope equal to 1 / n 1/n for every n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 .

1.
Introduction

A curve in this paper is a smooth projective and
geometrically integral algebraic variety of dimension
1
.
Let
p
be a rational prime.
Let
q
be a
p
-power and let
v_{q}(\alpha)

be the additive
q
-adic valuation
of
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with
v_{q}(q)=1
.
Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus
g
.
Write
Z(X/\mathbb{F}_{q},s)
for the zeta function of
X
.
Then by Weil (
[
We48
]
),
Z(X/\mathbb{F}_{q},s)(1-s)(1-qs)=\prod_{i=1}^{2g}(1-\alpha_{i}s)

where
\alpha_{i}
are Weil
q
-numbers.
We shall order these reciprocal zeros such that

v_{q}(\alpha_{1})\leq v_{q}(\alpha_{2})\leq\dots\leq v_{q}(\alpha_{2g})
.
It is known that a generic curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

is
ordinary
, where

v_{q}(\alpha_{1})=\dots=v_{q}(\alpha_{g})=0
and

v_{q}(\alpha_{g+1})=\dots=v_{q}(\alpha_{2g})=1
.
On the other hand, a curve
X
is
supersingular

if and only if
v_{q}(\alpha_{1})=\dots=v_{q}(\alpha_{2g})=\frac{1}{2}
.
The Jacobian variety of a supersingular curve
is isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
to a product of
g
supersingular elliptic curves.
The
Newton polygon
of
X
is the piecewise linear function
on the real plane on the interval
[0,2g]
with slopes

v_{q}(\alpha_{1}),\ldots,v_{q}(\alpha_{2g})
, each of multiplicity
1
,
denoted by
\NP(X)
.
Each Newton polygon is
a lower convex hull with only integral vertices.
Every possible Newton polygon of
X
lies between the supersingular
and the ordinary ones.
The first slope of the Newton polygon of
X
(called simply the
first
slope
of
X
) has been studied by
Ax
[
Ax64
]
, Katz
[
Ka71
]
, Moreno-Moreno
[
MM95
]
, Adolphson-Sperber

[
AS87
]
, and Wan
[
Wan95
]
.

Much is known about the Newton polygon stratification of the moduli space of
principally polarized abelian varieties (see
[
Oo91b
]
).
By contrast, it is still open in many cases
whether a given Newton polygon can be realized by the Jacobian of a curve;
see
[
Oo91a
]
.
There has been a focus on the supersingular strata in the past decades:
Li and Oort
[
LO98
]
proved that the supersingular locus has dimension

[\frac{g^{2}}{4}]
in the moduli of abelian varieties.
On the other hand, the Torelli locus (which parameterizes Jacobians of curves)
is of dimension
3g-3
(for
g>1
).
It is not clear whether the supersingular locus and the Torelli locus
have a non-empty intersection.
van der Geer and van der Vlugt (
[
GV92
]
,
[
GV95
]
)
explicitly constructed supersingular curves of
every genus
g
when
p=2
, built exclusively upon
Artin-Schreier curves.
For more recent progress on the existence of supersingular curves
over fields of arbitrary odd characteristic, see
[
KHS20
]
for genus 4 for example.

By comparison, the Torelli locus
of non-supersingular and non-ordinary curves remains poorly understood, and
very little is known or even conjectured.
Zarhin (see
[
Zar04
]
) identified a sufficient criterion
for non-supersingularity of hyperelliptic curves in characteristic
p>2
,
but it remains an open question whether,
for instance,
one can construct a family of curves
with a prescribed non-supersingular, non-ordinary Newton polygon.

The main object of study in this paper will be Artin-Schreier curves

X_{f}:y^{p}-y=f

for some
f=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]

of degree
d\geq 3
.
For the rest of the paper, we assume
p\nmid i
for all
i\in\Supp(f)
.
Notice that for an arbitrary polynomial
h
one can
replace it by
h-h(0)
without altering the Newton polygon
since
X_{h}
and
X_{h-h(0)}
share the same one.
If there are nonzero multiples of
p
in
\Supp(h)
,
one can find a polynomial
f
satisfying
p\nmid i
for all
i\in\Supp(f)
,
where
X_{f}
is isomorphic to
X_{h}
, hence
X_{f}
and
X_{h}
have the
same Newton polygon.

For a nonnegative integer
N
, let
s_{p}(N)
denote the

p
-adic weight of
N
, i.e., the sum of
N
’s
p
-adic digits.
It is well-known that the first slope of

X_{f}
is bounded below by
\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}

(see a proof in
[
Wan95
, Theorem 4.1]
).
In
[
MSCK04
]
, the authors describe an open condition on the coefficients
of
f
which, when achieved, guarantees that the first slope
of
X_{f}
is equal to their lower bound.
In
[
Bl12
]
Blache introduces the
p
-density of
f
,
which can be used to compute the first slope.
However, neither the
p
-density nor this open condition is easy to compute
for a family of
X_{f}
with fixed support set for
f
.

In this paper, we identify a class of sets
S

where the lower bound of
\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}

is achieved for every
f(x)
with
\Supp(f)=S
.
In fact, if
\Supp(f)
contains a unique element
\nu
of maximal
p
-adic
weight, then we are able to completely characterize when the lower bound of

\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}
is achieved.
We do this
by establishing a sufficient condition on elements in
\Supp(f)
,
which we define as
p
-symmetry.
A positive integer
\nu
coprime to
p
is

p
-symmetric
if
there exists a
3
-tuple of positive integers
(k,\ell,w)

with
\ell<p^{k}
such that

\nu w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
where the multiplication
\nu w
in
p
-adic digit form is
carry-free (see Section
2
).
For example,
5
-symmetric numbers of precisely
3
digits are
(exhaustive in
5
-adic form):

\displaystyle(111)_{5},(222)_{5},(444)_{5},(101)_{5},(202)_{5},(404)_{5},(121)_{5},(242)_{5},(143)_{5},(341)_{5},(301)_{5},

\displaystyle(103)_{5},(112)_{5},(211)_{5},

which counts
14\%
of all 3-digit numbers
coprime to
5
.
In decimal representation and in increasing order, they are

26,28,31,32,36,48,52,56,62,72,76,96,104,124.

See Section
4.1

for an exploration of
p
-symmetric numbers.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d,p\nmid i}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Then the first slope of the curve

X_{f}:y^{p}-y=f
is
\geq\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}
.

(1)

Suppose
\Supp(f)
contains a unique element
\nu
such that

s_{p}(\nu)=\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i).

Then the first slope is equal to
\frac{1}{s_{p}(\nu)}

if and only if
\nu
is a
p
-symmetric number.

(2)

If
\nu=p^{k}-1
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight
element in
\Supp(f)
, then
the first slope is equal to
\frac{1}{k(p-1)}
and has multiplicity
k(p-1)
.

To highlight the effectiveness of Theorem
1.1
,
we will examine some of its applications.

Firstly, suppose
f
is of degree
p^{k}-1
for some
k\geq 1
.
Then
p^{k}-1
must be the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
.
Moreover,
p^{k}-1
is
p
-symmetric, with
(k,\ell,w)=(k,1,1)
.
By Theorem
1.1
,
the first slope of
X_{f}
is equal to
\frac{1}{k(p-1)}

and has multiplicity
k(p-1)
.
Therefore, we see that the first slope of
X_{f}
and its multiplicity
are completely determined when
f
is of degree
p^{k}-1
.
We actually give an estimate of the multiplicity for many general cases in
Theorem
6.2
.

Our next application of Theorem
1.1
is
for the case when
p>d
, where
d=\deg(f)
.
In this case,
d
is the element in
\Supp(f)

of maximal
p
-adic weight.
If
p\equiv 1\bmod{d}
,
then
d
is clearly
p
-symmetric.
In fact, since
d<p
, we can show

d
is
p
-symmetric if and only if
p\equiv 1\bmod d

(see a proof of this in Example
4.5
(4)).
Theorem
1.1
then implies that the first slope is

1/d
precisely when this occurs.
This is already known (see
[
AS89
]
[
Wan93
]
[
SZ03
]
),
however our result provides a more satisfying
explanation for this fact.

Theorem
1.1
actually allows for a strong generalization
of this result.
One immediate extension stems from the fact that
d
need not be the
element in
\Supp(f)
of maximal
p
-adic weight.
If
\nu\in\Supp(f)
is the unique element of maximal
p
-adic weight
and
\nu<p
, then the first slope of
X_{f}
is equal to
1/\nu

if and only if
p\equiv 1\bmod{\nu}
.
This is true
regardless of the degree of
f
.
This generalization extends further in the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose
\Supp(f)
has a unique maximal
p
-adic weight element
\nu
.
If
p\equiv 1\bmod s_{p}(\nu)
,
then the first slope of
X_{f}
is equal to
1/s_{p}(\nu)
.

Proving this corollary requires showing that

p\equiv 1\bmod{s_{p}(\nu)}
implies that
\nu

is
p
-symmetric.
This is done in Section
6
,
Corollary
6.7
.

A third application of Theorem
1.1
involves building on the
supersingular curves due to van der Geer and van der Vlugt
(see
[
GV92
]
), whose construction used a coding theory–inspired method.
The van der Geer–van der Vlugt supersingular curves are fiber products of
Artin-Schreier curves
of the form
X:y^{p}-y=x\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

If
c_{\nu}\neq 0
for some
p
-symmetric number
\nu>1

and
\nu\neq p^{i}+1
for any

, then any curve of the form

X:y^{p}-y=c_{\nu}x^{\nu}+x\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}
has first slope equal to

and is non-supersingular.

Proof.

The
p
-symmetry of
\nu
implies that it is coprime to

,
and the fact that
\nu>1
and
\nu\neq p^{i}+1
for any

implies that

.
Since
s_{p}(p^{i}+1)=2
for all

,
we see that
\Supp(f)
has

as a unique element of maximal

-adic weight.
Since it is also

-symmetric,
we have by Theorem

that the first slope of

is
1/s_{p}(\nu)
.
∎

2.
p
-adic weight under addition and multiplication

The
p
-adic weight of a nonnegative integer
N
,
which we henceforth denote as
s_{p}(N)
,
gives the sum of the
p
-adic digits of
N
.
That is, if
N=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}n_{i}p^{i}
,
n_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
,
then

s_{p}(N)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}n_{i}
.
Frequently in this paper, for specific
N
, we will write

N=(n_{\ell}\;\cdots\;n_{1}\;n_{0})_{p}

for the
p
-adic (digit) form
of
N
,
and we call
n_{i}
the
i
-th
p
-adic digit of
N
.

Addition of two nonnegative integers
N
and
M
can be done

p
-adically, where the arithmetic addition algorithm is done
using the
p
-adic digit forms of
N
and
M
.
For example, the
2
-adic addition of
3+10
equals

(11)_{2}+(1010)_{2}=(1101)_{2}
.
Notice that there is
a
(
p
-adic) carry
in the
2
-nd digit.
Given any nonnegative integers
N_{1},\ldots,N_{\ell}
,
we say the summation
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}N_{i}

is
p
-adically
carry-free
if their
p
-adic addition
has no carry at each digit.
We simply say ‘carry-free’ when
p
is clear from its context.

Multiplication of two nonnegative integers
N
and
M

can also be done
p
-adically,
using standard arithmetic rules in base
p
.
In the rare case where the
p
-adic multiplication
of
N
and
M
has no carrying, the
k
-th
p
-adic digit
of
NM
is equal to the convolution
n_{0}m_{k}+n_{1}m_{k-1}+\dots+n_{k}m_{0}
.
Thus, we can say that multiplication is
p
-adically
carry-free

if
\sum_{i+j=k}m_{i}n_{j}\leq p-1
for every
k\geq 0
.

The notion of ‘
p
-adically carry-free’ is given a more precise treatment
in the following proposition — which we will use
to swiftly replace the notion of “carry-free addition or multiplication”
by a more convenient characterization in terms of
p
-adic weights.

Proposition 2.1

(Triangle inequality)
.

Let
N,M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then

s_{p}(N)+s_{p}(M)\geq s_{p}(N+M),\quad s_{p}(N)s_{p}(M)\geq s_{p}(NM).

The first equality holds if and only if the

p
-adic addition
N+M
is carry-free;
the second equality holds if and only if the
p
-adic product

NM
is carry-free.

Proof.

The first statement is classical; see
[
HLS11
, Proposition 2.2]
for example.
The remaining assertions follow directly from the base-
p
carrying algorithm.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
Let
i,j
be positive integers with
p^{a}i-j\geq 0
. Then

s_{p}(p^{a}i-j)\geq a(p-1)+s_{p}(i-1)-s_{p}(j-1).

Equality holds if
j\leq p^{a}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
above,
we have

\displaystyle s_{p}(p^{a}i-j)+s_{p}(j-1)

\displaystyle\geq s_{p}(p^{a}i-1)

\displaystyle=s_{p}(p^{a}(i-1)+(p^{a}-1))

\displaystyle=s_{p}(i-1)+s_{p}(p^{a}-1)=s_{p}(i-1)+a(p-1).

If
j\leq p^{a}
, then the summation of
(p^{a}i-j)+(j-1)
is carry-free, hence
the equality holds by Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We take a moment to highlight a particular case of Proposition

2.2
: when
i=j
and
i\leq p^{a}
, we have

s_{p}(i(p^{a}-1))=a(p-1)
.

When we wish to emphasize when the product
NM
is carry-free,
we will often typeset it as
N*M
.
For example,

(110011)_{2}*(101)_{2}
is carry-free, but
(110011)_{2}\times(11)_{2}

and
(242)_{5}\times(12)_{5}
are not.

3.
A change-making problem and its
solution value

This section is a technical preparation
surrounding a particular

p
-adic change-making problem.
In general, a “change-making problem” involves
finding the minimal number of “coins” in a given coin set
needed to achieve
a particular fixed value.
More explicitly, given a set
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\}
of positive integers
and a fixed integer
N
, the change-making problem involves finding
a tuple
(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
of nonnegative integers such that

t_{1}c_{1}+\dots+t_{m}c_{m}=N
, and that minimizes
t_{1}+\dots+t_{m}
.
See
[
CLRS
]
for a broader analysis of change-making problems.

Let
\bf{i}
be a finite set of positive integers coprime to
p
.
For any integer
a\geq 1
, consider the index set of pairs

I\coloneqq\mathbf{i}\times\{0,1,\dots,a-1\}.

We will fix a
system of coins
or
coin set

(3.1)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a):=\left\{ip^{j}\mid i\in\mathbf{i},j=0,1,\ldots,a-1\right\}.

Each integer
c_{ij}\coloneqq ip^{j}
in
\mathcal{C}
is called a
coin
.
Since every
i\in\mathbf{i}
is coprime to
p
, the indexing map
(i,j)\mapsto c_{ij}

is a bijection between
I
and
\mathcal{C}
.
For every integer
N\geq 0
, let

(3.2)

\mathcal{T}(N)=\left\{(t_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{I}\mid\sum_{(i,j)\in I}t_{ij}(ip^{j})=N\right\}.

Each element in
\mathcal{T}(N)
is called a
representation of
N
(under
\mathcal{C}
)
.

If a representation
\mathbf{t}=(t_{ij})\in\mathcal{T}(N)
has the minimal
|\mathbf{t}|\coloneqq\sum_{(i,j)\in I}t_{ij}
, among all elements in
\mathcal{T}(N)
, then it is
called a
solution to the
p
-adic change-making problem for
N
(under

\mathcal{C}
)
.
Then we call

M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)\coloneqq|\mathbf{t}|=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{ij}

the
solution value
to the
p
-adic change-making problem.
In other words,
M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)
is the minimal number of
coins one needs to sum up to
N
.

The following lemma establishes a lower bound for solution values to the
change-making problem, and identifies certain cases where that
lower bound is both tight and achieved at a unique solution.

Key Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{ij}=ip^{j}|i\in\mathbf{i},j=0,1,\ldots,a-1\}

where
\mathbf{i}
is a finite set consisting of positive integers
coprime to
p
and
\nu\in\mathbf{i}
such that
s_{p}(\nu)=\max_{i\in\mathbf{i}}s_{p}(i)
.
Then:

(1)

For every nonnegative integer
N\geq 0
,

M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)\geq\frac{s_{p}(N)}{s_{p}(\nu)}.

(2)

If there is a carry-free factorization
N=\nu*w

for some
w<p^{a}
, then
M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)=\frac{s_{p}(N)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
.
If
\nu
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\mathbf{i}
,
then the converse is also true.
In this case, there is a unique solution
(t_{ij})

to the change-making problem for
N
under
\mathcal{C}
, with

w=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{\nu j}p^{j}
and
t_{ij}=0
for all
i\neq\nu
.
This expression gives the
p
-adic form of
w
; i.e.,
t_{\nu j}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
for all
j
.

Proof.

(1) Let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{ij})

be a solution to the change-making problem for
N
under
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
N=\sum_{(i,j)\in I}t_{ij}c_{ij}
.
Note that
s_{p}(c_{ij})=s_{p}(ip^{j})=s_{p}(i)\leq s_{p}(\nu)

by hypothesis.
Now by Proposition
2.1
,
we have

(3.3)

s_{p}(N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{(i,j)}t_{ij}s_{p}(ip^{j})=\sum_{(i,j)}t_{ij}s_{p}(i)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{\leq}}s_{p}(\nu)\sum_{(i,j)}t_{ij}=s_{p}(\nu)M_{\mathcal{C}}(N).

This immediately proves the desired inequality

M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)\geq\frac{s_{p}(N)}{s_{p}(\nu)}.

(2)
Suppose
N=\nu w
is carry-free. By Proposition
2.1

s_{p}(N)=s_{p}(\nu)s_{p}(w)
.
Write
w=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}t_{i}p^{i}
in
p
-adic form.
Then
N=\nu w=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}t_{i}\nu p^{i}
.
But
\nu p^{i}\in\mathcal{C}
, so
M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}t_{i}=s_{p}(w)=\frac{s_{p}(N)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
. Combined with the inequality of part (1),
the equality must hold.

Now suppose that
\nu
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\mathbf{i}
.
Let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{ij})
be a solution to the change-making problem of
N
,

M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)=\sum_{(i,j)}t_{ij}
and

N=\sum_{(i,j)}t_{ij}ip^{j}
.
By hypothesis, we suppose
s_{p}(N)=s_{p}(\nu)M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)
. Then all the equalities of (
3.3
) hold.
The (
**
) equality holds if and only if
s_{p}(i)=s_{p}(\nu)
for every
t_{ij}\neq 0
and hence

t_{ij}=0
for all
i\neq\nu
.
The (
*
) equality holds if and only if the sum is carry-free. Combined with the above,
the sum
\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{\nu j}\nu p^{j}
is carry-free, and this immediately implies

t_{\nu j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
.
Now
w\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{\nu j}p^{j}
is in its
p
-adic form.
Hence
w<p^{a}
. Notice that
\nu w=\nu\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{\nu,j}p^{j}=N
.
On the other hand,
s_{p}(\nu)s_{p}(w)=s_{p}(\nu)\sum_{(i,j)}t_{ij}=s_{p}(\nu)M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)=s_{p}(N)
.
By Proposition
2.1
, the product
\nu*w=N
is carry-free.

In this case, we have
N=\nu*w
where
w=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{\nu j}\ p^{j}
in
p
-adic form.
Thus the solution
(t_{ij})
is clearly unique since the
p
-adic form of
w
is unique.
∎

We have observed in the Key Lemma that if the minimum

M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)=\frac{s_{p}(N)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
is achieved, then
\nu\mid N
. This depends on the given coin set
\mathcal{C}
.
If the coin set
\mathcal{C}
contains

\nu,\nu p,\ldots,\nu p^{a-1}
whose
p
-adic weight

s_{p}(\nu)
is the unique maximal, then we have the necessary and sufficient condition for the equality.

4.
p
-symmetric numbers and minimizers

Key Lemma
3.1
illustrates the utility of carry-free products
when solving change-making problems for coin sets
\mathcal{C}
of Section
3
.
The Dwork-theoretic approach we will employ
in Section
5
will involve solving change-making problems
for values
N
of the form
(q-1)\ell
;
therefore we dedicate an entire section here to describing carry-free
products for numbers of this form.

4.1.
p
-symmetric numbers

Fix any prime
p
and a positive integer
n
.
We know the
n
-digit number with the highest
p
-adic weight is

p^{n}-1=(p-1,p-1,\ldots,p-1)_{p}
with
s_{p}(p^{n}-1)=n(p-1)
.
More generally, consider the numbers
N=(p^{k}-1)\ell
where
\ell<p^{k}
.
Then
s_{p}(N)=k(p-1)
by Proposition
2.2
.
For example,
N=(3311)_{5}=(5^{2}-1)\cdot 19
has
s_{5}(N)=8=2(5-1)
,
and
N=(11111111)_{2}=2^{8}-1
has
s_{2}(N)=8=8(2-1)
.
Here, we focus on the numbers
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
which factor
numbers of the form
N=(p^{k}-1)\ell
in a carry-free manner.

Definition 4.1
.

An integer
\nu>1
coprime to
p

is
p
-symmetric

if there exists a positive integer
w
such that
the product
\nu w
is carry-free
and
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell

for some positive integers
k
and
\ell<p^{k}
.

Remark 4.2
.

The carry-free aspect of
p
-symmetric numbers has a combinatorial
interpretation to it, similar to the
r
-tiling sequences described
in
[
SZ02c
]
.
The number
N=(p^{k}-1)\ell
may be expressed
p
-adically as

N=(N_{r}\;N_{r-1}\;\cdots\;N_{0})_{p}
,
and the number
\nu
may be expressed
p
-adically as

\nu=(\nu_{s}\;\nu_{s-1}\;\cdots\;\nu_{0})_{p}
.
When viewed as finite sequences of length
r+1
and
s+1

respectively,
N
having a
p
-adic carry-free factorization with
\nu

amounts to whether
(N_{r},\dots,N_{0})_{p}
is equal to the

p
-adic carry-free sum
of some shifts
(\nu_{s},\dots,\nu_{0},0,\dots,0)_{p}
of
(\nu_{s},\dots,\nu_{0})_{p}
.
Families of
p
-symmetric numbers are demonstrated in
Example
4.5
below.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\nu
be
p
-symmetric and suppose

\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
with
1\leq\ell<p^{k}
.
Then
k
and
\ell
are uniquely determined by
w
.

Proof.

Suppose also
\nu*w=(p^{k^{\prime}}-1)\ell^{\prime}
with
1\leq\ell^{\prime}<p^{k^{\prime}}
.
Suppose
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, without loss of generality, and let
k<k^{\prime}
. Since

(p^{k}-1)\ell\equiv(p^{k^{\prime}}-1)\ell^{\prime}\bmod p^{k}
, we can write

\ell=\ell^{\prime}+cp^{k}
for some
c\geq 1
. Then
\ell\geq p^{k}
, in contradiction to our hypothesis; hence
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

If
\nu
is
p
-symmetric, we pick the smallest such
w

such that
\nu*w
is a carry-free product and
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell

with
\ell<p^{k}
. Then such
k,\ell
are unique
by Proposition
4.3
.
This equation is called the
minimal factorization
for
\nu
.
If
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
is the minimal factorization
and
w=(w_{e}\;\dots\;w_{0})_{p}
with
w_{e}\neq 0
, then the nonnegative integer
e

is called the
shift factor
of
\nu
.

Example 4.5

(
p
-symmetric numbers and their properties)
.

(1)

Any integer in
\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
of the form
(p^{k}-1)\ell
is
p
-symmetric
if
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq p^{k}}
and
\ell
is coprime to
p
.
In particular,
p^{k}-1
is always
p
-symmetric.

(2)

As previously stated, the integer
w
in Definition

4.1
is not unique.
If
\nu
is
p
-symmetric with
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
,
then for every integer
b\geq 1
,
we have a carry-free factorization

\nu*w^{\prime}=(p^{kb}-1)\ell
,
where
w^{\prime}=w(1+p^{k}+\dots+p^{(b-1)k})
.

(3)

If
\nu
is a coprime-to-
p
integer in
\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
with
s_{p}(\nu)\mid(p-1)

then one can show
\nu
has to be
p
-symmetric (see the proof of
Corollary
6.7
for an argument).
For example, if
p=5
then
the following are
p

(101)_{5}
,

(202)_{5}
,
(103)_{5}
,
(301)_{5}
,
(112)_{5}
,
(121)_{5}
,
(211)_{5}
.

(4)

Any positive integer
1<\nu<p
is
p
-symmetric if and only if

\nu\mid(p-1)
. If
\nu|(p-1)

then
\nu*\frac{p-1}{\nu}=p-1
, hence
\nu
is clearly
p
-symmetric;
conversely, if
\nu
is
p
-symmetric, then
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
for some
\ell<p^{k}
.
Write
w=(w_{e}\cdots w_{0})_{p}
in standard
p
-adic digit form.
Since
\nu<p
and
\nu*w
is carry-free, we have
\nu w_{i}\leq p-1
,
hence
\nu*w=(\nu w_{e},\ldots,\nu w_{0})_{p}
in the standard
p
-adic digit form.
We may write
\ell=(\ell_{k-1},\ldots,\ell_{0})_{p}
where
\ell_{0}>0
and
0\leq\ell_{i}\leq p-1
.
Then

\;\;\;\;p^{k}\ell-\ell=(\ell_{k-1},\ldots,\ell_{1},\ell_{0}-1,p-1-\ell_{k-1},\ldots,p-1-\ell_{1},p-\ell_{0})_{p}.

Considering the standard
p
-adic digit form of the equation

\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
, we sum up
the
k
-th and
0
-th digits and get

\nu w_{k}+\nu w_{0}=(\ell_{0}-1)+(p-\ell_{0})
.
That is,
\nu(w_{k}+w_{0})=p-1
. Thus we have
\nu\mid(p-1)
.

(5)

If
w\mid p-1
,
then
\nu=(p^{b}-1)/w
is
p
-symmetric for every
b\geq 1
unless
\nu=1
.
This follows from the obvious carry-free product

\nu w=p^{b}-1.

Its shift factor is
0
.
For example, when
p=7
, the following are
7
-symmetric:
(666)_{7}
,

(333)_{7}
,
(222)_{7}
,
(111)_{7}
.

(6)

As a dual to (2),
if
\nu
is
p
-symmetric with factorization
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
,
then

\nu^{\prime}=\nu(1+p^{k}+p^{2k}+\dots+p^{(b-1)k})

is
p
-symmetric for every
b\geq 1
.
Part (5) is the special case
k=1
,
\ell=1
.

(7)

If
\nu
is
p
-symmetric,
then its

-adic digit reversal is also
p
-symmetric.
In other words, if
\nu=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\nu_{i}p^{i}
is

-symmetric,
so is its digital reverse
\nu^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\nu_{s-i}p^{i}
. Indeed, if
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
is the minimal factorization, one can check that

\nu^{\prime}*w^{\prime}=(p^{k}-1)\ell^{\prime}
, hence
\nu^{\prime}
is

-symmetric too.
For example,

(341)_{5}
and
(143)_{5}
,
(301)_{5}
and
(103)_{5}
,
(112)_{5}
and
(211)_{5}

are such pairs of

-symmetric numbers.

(8)

If
\nu
is
p
-symmetric with a minimal factorization
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
and

,
then
w
is also
p
-symmetric.

(9)

For any
n\geq 2
,
m\geq 0
,
there exists at least one
p
-symmetric number
\nu

with
s_{p}(\nu)=n
and shift factor

.
Namely, let
\nu=1+p^{m+1}+p^{2(m+1)}+\dots+p^{(n-1)(m+1)}

Then

is

-symmetric with

\nu*(p^{m+1}-1)=p^{n(m+1)}-1
.

Example 4.6

(Shift factor of a
p
-symmetric number)
.

(1)

The
p
-symmetric number
p^{k}-1
has shift factor

.

(2)

The
p
-symmetric number

has shift factor

.
E.g.,
(10001)_{p}
has shift factor

.

(3)

The

-symmetric number

has shift factor

.
The

-symmetric number

.

(4)

The

-symmetric number

.

Proposition 4.7
.

For any
p
-symmetric number
\nu
with minimal factorization

\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
with
shift factor
e
, we have
k\geq e+1
and
\ell\leq\ell p^{k-e-1}<\nu
.

Proof.

Write
w=p^{k}w_{+}+w_{-}
with
0\leq w_{-}<p^{k},w_{+}\geq 0
, and let
w^{\prime}=w_{+}+w_{-}
.
Then

\nu w^{\prime}=\nu(w_{+}+w_{-})=(p^{k}-1)(\ell-\nu w_{+}).

Since
s_{p}(w^{\prime})=s_{p}(w_{-}+w_{+})\leq s_{p}(w_{+})+s_{p}(w_{-})=s_{p}(w)
,
we have

s_{p}(\nu w^{\prime})=k(p-1)\leq s_{p}(\nu)s_{p}(w^{\prime})\leq s_{p}(\nu)s_{p}(w)=s_{p}(\nu w)=k(p-1).

Hence equality holds throughout. In particular,
s_{p}(\nu w^{\prime})=s_{p}(\nu)s_{p}(w^{\prime})
.
By Proposition
2.1
, the product
\nu w^{\prime}
is
p
-adic carry-free.
By the minimality of
w
, we must have
w=w^{\prime}
. It follows that
w_{+}=0
. Hence

w=w_{-}<p^{k}
.
Now
w=(w_{e}\cdots w_{0})_{p}
with
w_{e}\neq 0
, so
p^{k}>w\geq p^{e}
and hence
e\leq k-1
.
That is
k\geq e+1
.

Finally, we have
\nu=\frac{p^{k}-1}{w}\ell>p^{k-e-1}\ell\geq\ell
.
∎

4.2.
Solution value to
p
-adic change-making problems

For the rest of this subsection
we assume our coin set
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
(as introduced in (
3.1
))
satisfies the following condition:

\nu\in\mathbf{i}
is
p
-symmetric whose minimal factorization is

\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
,
s_{p}(\nu)=\max_{i\in\mathbf{i}}s_{p}(i)
, and
k|a
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
with
a=k
.
Then the
p
-adic form
w=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}w_{j}p^{j}
of
w

encodes a solution to the change-making problem for
(p^{k}-1)\ell
.
Let
t_{ij}=w_{j}
for
i=\nu,j=0,\ldots,k-1
, and
t_{ij}=0
otherwise.
By Key Lemma
3.1
, we have

M_{\mathcal{C}}((p^{k}-1)\ell)=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}t_{ij}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}w_{j}=s_{p}(w)=\frac{k(p-1)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
.
Moreover, by considering
\nu*(wp^{r})
, we also have

M_{\mathcal{C}}(p^{r}(p^{k}-1)\ell)=\frac{k(p-1)}{s_{p}(\nu)}

so long as

r+e\leq a-1
, where
e
is the shift factor of
\nu
.
Should
r
exceed
a-1-e
,
then the coins in the representation encoded by
wp^{r}

become too large, and fall out of the scope of
\mathcal{C}
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
with
a=bk
for some
b\geq 1
.
The minimal factorization
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
gives

\nu*w^{\prime}=(p^{a}-1)\ell

where
w^{\prime}=w(1+p^{k}+\dots+p^{(b-1)k})

(recall Example
4.5
(2)).
The
p
-adic digits of
w^{\prime}
encode a solution to the change-making problem of
(p^{a}-1)\ell
in the same manner as that above for
w
.
Moreover, so long as
r\leq k-e-1
, we will have
p^{r}w^{\prime}<p^{a}
,
and so Key Lemma
3.1
guarantees
that
M_{\mathcal{C}}(p^{r}(p^{a}-1)\ell)=\frac{s_{p}(p^{r}(p^{a}-1)\ell)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
.

We summarize these observations in the following proposition:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
be as above.
Let
e
be the shift factor of
\nu
.
Then for
r\in\{0,1,\dots,k-e-1\}
,

M_{\mathcal{C}}(p^{r}(p^{a}-1)\ell)=\frac{s_{p}(p^{r}(p^{a}-1)\ell)}{s_{p}(\nu)}=\frac{a(p-1)}{s_{p}(\nu)}.

∎

The following proposition pays special attention to the case where

\nu=p^{k}-1
, offering a characterization of
when the lower bound is achieved.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
be as above, where

\nu=p^{k}-1\in\mathbf{i}
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element.
Then we have

M_{\mathcal{C}}((p^{a}-1)\ell)=\frac{a(p-1)}{s_{p}(\nu)}

if and only if
\ell\in\{1,p,\ldots,p^{k-1}\}
.

Proof.

Since
k|a
we write
a=bk
for some
b\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Notice that
\nu
is
p
-symmetric with shift factor
0
.
The sufficient direction thus follows from Proposition
4.8
.

Conversely, suppose
M_{\mathcal{C}}((p^{a}-1)\ell)=\frac{a(p-1)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
.
Then

M_{\mathcal{C}}((p^{a}-1)\ell)=\frac{s_{p}((p^{a}-1)\ell)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
and
\ell<p^{a}
.
Applying Key Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique solution

\mathbf{t}=(t_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I}
to the change-making problem of
(p^{a}-1)\ell
,
and we have a carry-free factorization

(4.1)

(p^{a}-1)\ell=(p^{k}-1)w

where
w=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{p^{k}-1,j}p^{j}
is in its
p
-adic form and
w<p^{a}
.
Then

w=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{p^{k}-1,j}p^{j}=\frac{(p^{a}-1)\ell}{p^{k}-1}

and we have

(4.2)

s_{p}\left(\frac{(p^{a}-1)\ell}{p^{k}-1}\right)=s_{p}(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}t_{p^{k}-1,j}=M_{\mathcal{C}}((p^{a}-1)\ell)=\frac{a(p-1)}{k(p-1)}=b.

Since
w<p^{a}
, (
4.1
) implies
\ell<p^{k}
,
and so the product
\ell(1+p^{k}+\ldots+p^{(b-1)k})
must be carry-free.
Applying Proposition
2.1
we now have

s_{p}(\ell)s_{p}(1+p^{k}+\ldots+p^{(b-1)k})=s_{p}(\ell(1+p^{k}+\ldots+p^{(b-1)k}))
.
Combined with (
4.2
), we see that

s_{p}(\ell)=\frac{s_{p}(\frac{\ell(p^{a}-1)}{p^{k}-1})}{s_{p}(1+p^{k}+\ldots+p^{(b-1)k})}=\frac{b}{b}=1.

Thus
\ell
is a
p
-power.
Since
\ell<p^{k}
, we finish by observing that

\ell\in\{1,p,\ldots,p^{k-1}\}
.
∎

4.3.
Minimizers and heights

This final technical preparation aims to handle the
permutations that arise from a characteristic power series
that lies at the heart of our Dwork-theoretic approach.

For the remainder of this section, we fix a coin set

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
for some
a\geq 1
and
\mathbf{i}
such that

\mathbf{i}
contains a unique element
\nu

with maximal
p
-adic weight.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\mathbf{\Sigma}
denote the set of all pairs
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)

where
\mathcal{I}
is a finite nonempty set of positive integers
and
\sigma
is a permutation of
\mathcal{I}
.
A pair
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
in
\mathbf{\Sigma}
is a
minimizer
if

M_{\mathcal{C}}(p^{a}\ell-\sigma\ell)=\frac{s_{p}(p^{a}\ell-\sigma\ell)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}
.
Let

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\{(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}\mid(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\mbox{ is a minimizer}\}.

For each positive integer
m
,
let

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{m}\coloneqq\{(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}\mid\#\mathcal{I}=m\}.

The following proposition is a straightforward consequence of
Definition
4.10
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}
.
Suppose
\sigma=\prod_{i}\sigma_{i}
where
\sigma_{i}
’s are (disjoint) permutations on sets
\mathcal{I}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{I}_{i}
’s.
Then
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is a minimizer if and only if all
(\mathcal{I}_{i},\sigma_{i})
are minimizers.
In particular, if
\sigma_{i}
’s are the decomposition cycle factors of
\sigma
, and
\mathcal{I}_{i}
is the underlying integers in the cycle
\sigma_{i}
,
then
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is a minimizer if and only if
all
(\mathcal{I}_{i},\sigma_{i})
are cyclic minimizers. ∎

Proposition 4.12
.

(1)

If
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
then
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,\nu\}
.

(2)

Define a relation on
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
as follows:
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\leq(\mathcal{I}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
if

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
and
\sigma
is a decomposition factor of
\sigma^{\prime}
.
Then
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
is partially ordered under
\leq
, and if it is nonempty,
then there is a unique maximum
(\mathcal{I}_{\bullet},\sigma_{\bullet})
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose there exists
\ell\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\ell>\nu
.
Then we observe that there exists
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
such that

\ell^{\prime}>\nu
and
\sigma(\ell^{\prime})\leq\ell^{\prime}
.
Thus
p^{a}\ell^{\prime}-\sigma\ell^{\prime}>\nu(p^{a}-1)
.
But by Key Lemma
3.1
,
there is a carry-free factorization

p^{a}\ell^{\prime}-\sigma\ell^{\prime}=\nu*w
, with
w<p^{a}
.
This implies
p^{a}\ell^{\prime}-\sigma\ell^{\prime}\leq\nu(p^{a}-1)
,
which is a clear contradiction.

(2) It is easy to see that ‘
\leq
’ defines a partial ordering on
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
;
we omit the routine verification and continue to showing the existence
of a unique maximal element.
By Proposition
4.11
, it suffices to show that
any two distinct cyclic minimizers must have disjoint underlying sets
\mathcal{I}
.

If
\#\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}\in\{0,1\}
then there is nothing to prove,
so suppose
\#\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}\geq 2
, and let
(\mathcal{I}_{1},\sigma_{1}),(\mathcal{I}_{2},\sigma_{2})\in\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}

be distinct cyclic minimizers.
We wish to prove that
\mathcal{I}_{1}\cap\mathcal{I}_{2}=\varnothing
, so
suppose for contradiction that
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\cap\mathcal{I}_{2}
.
Let
m\geq 1
be the smallest
such that
\sigma_{1}^{m}(\ell)\neq\sigma_{2}^{m}(\ell)
.
(If no such
m
exists, then
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}
and then
\mathcal{I}_{1}=\mathcal{I}_{2}
, which contradicts our hypothesis.)
Write
\ell^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}^{m-1}(\ell)
— with
\ell^{\prime}=\ell
if
m=1
.
Our hypothesis implies by the Key Lemma
that
\nu|(p^{a}\ell^{\prime}-\sigma_{i}\ell^{\prime})
for
i=1,2
hence
\nu|(\sigma_{1}\ell^{\prime}-\sigma_{2}\ell^{\prime})
.
On the other hand, we have

|\sigma_{1}\ell^{\prime}-\sigma_{2}\ell^{\prime}|\leq\nu-1
since

\sigma_{i}\ell^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\nu\}
by part (1) above.
Thus it must follow that
\sigma_{1}\ell^{\prime}=\sigma_{2}\ell^{\prime}
,
contrary to assumption.
Thus
\mathcal{I}_{1}\cap\mathcal{I}_{2}=\varnothing
,
and the proof is complete.
∎

Definition 4.13
.

Suppose
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
is non-empty.
Then by Proposition
4.12
there is a unique maximal
minimizer
(\mathcal{I}_{\bullet},\sigma_{\bullet})
,
and we call the positive integer

t(\mathcal{C}):=\#\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
the
minimizer height
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i},a)
where
\mathbf{i}
contains a unique element
\nu
with maximal
p
-adic weight.

(1)

If
\nu
is
p
-symmetric with minimal factorization
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell

and
k|a
, then
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}\neq\varnothing
.

(2)

Suppose
\nu<p^{a}
. If
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}\neq\varnothing
,
then
\nu
is
p
-symmetric.

Proof.

(1)
By Proposition
4.8
, the pair
(\{\ell\},\id)

is a minimizer (where
\id
is the identity permutation).
That is
(\{\ell\},\id)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
.

(2)
For any given minimizer in
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
,
by Proposition
4.11
, all of its cycle decomposition factors
are also in
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
.
So we may let
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
be a cyclic minimizer in
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}
,
with
\#\mathcal{I}=m
and
\sigma
being an
m
-cycle.
We then have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}p^{a}\ell-\sigma\ell&=&\nu*w_{m-1}\\
p^{a}\sigma\ell-\sigma^{2}\ell&=&\nu*w_{m-2}\\
&\vdots&\\
p^{a}\sigma^{m-1}\ell-\ell&=&\nu*w_{0}\end{array}\right.

for some
0\leq w_{0},\ldots,w_{m-1}<p^{a}
such that

\nu*w_{m-1},\ldots,\nu*w_{1},\nu*w_{0}
are all carry-free products by Key Lemma
3.1
.
Write
w\coloneqq p^{a(m-1)}w_{m-1}+\cdots+p^{a}w_{1}+w_{0}
.
Since
w_{i}<p^{a}
for all
i
,
the product
\nu w
is also carry-free.
Now,

\displaystyle\nu*w

\displaystyle=\nu(p^{a(m-1)}w_{m-1}+\cdots+p^{a}w_{1}+w_{0})

\displaystyle=p^{a(m-1)}(\nu*w_{m-1})+\cdots+p^{a}(\nu*w_{1})+(\nu*w_{0})

\displaystyle=p^{a(m-1)}(p^{a}\ell-\sigma\ell)+\cdots+p^{a}(p^{a}\sigma^{m-2}\ell-\sigma^{m-1}\ell)+(p^{a}\sigma^{m-1}\ell-\ell)

\displaystyle=p^{am}\ell-\ell.

That is, we have a carry-free factorization
\nu*w=(p^{am}-1)\ell
, where

\ell<p^{am}
under the hypothesis,
so
\nu
is a
p
-symmetric number.
∎

Proposition 4.15
.

Let

(1)

(2)

First of all, we recall Dwork theory (
[

[

.
Let

for some

.
Let
\mathbb{Q}_{q}
be the degree
a
unramified extension over the
p
-adic

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be its ring of integers.
Let
\Omega_{1}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})

\zeta_{p}
is any fixed primitive
p
-th root of unity,
and let
\Omega_{a}=\mathbb{Q}_{q}(\zeta_{p})
. For each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}

\hat{z}

to be the Teichmüller lifting in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
of

.
Additionally, let
\tau:\Omega_{a}\to\Omega_{a}
be the
lift of the Frobenius endomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\Omega_{1}
.
In particular, we have
\tau(\hat{z})=\hat{z}^{p}
.
Let

E(x)=\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p^{i}}}{p^{i}}\right)
be the

p
-adic Artin-Hasse exponential function, which lies in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x]]
.
Pick and fix a root
\gamma\in\Omega_{1}
of
\log E(x)

satisfying
v_{p}(\gamma)=1/(p-1)
.
Since
\gamma
is a uniformizer, and
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\zeta_{p}]=\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\gamma]

our
p
-adic estimate is conveniently reduced to a
\gamma
-adic estimate.

Fix a polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d,p\nmid i}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Let

F(x)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}E(\gamma\hat{a}_{i}x^{i})
.
Let

F_{0}(x)\coloneqq\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\tau^{j}(F(x^{p^{j}}))
.
Notice that
F_{0}(x)
lies in the following
\Omega_{a}
-Banach algebra:

\mathscr{L}=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}x^{n}\in\Omega_{a}[[x]]\;|\;v_{p}(A_{n})\geq\frac{pn}{dq(p-1)}+O(1)\right\}.

Set
I\coloneqq\Supp(f)\times\{0,1,\ldots,a-1\}.

We consider the
p
-adic change-making problem, introduced in Section
3
,
with the following coin set:

(5.1)

\framebox{$\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\Supp(f),a)=\{ip^{j}\mid(i,j)\in I\}=\{ip^{j}\mid i\in\Supp(f),j=0,\ldots,a-1\}.$}

For the readers’ convenience, we recall some notation from
Section
3
:
For every integer
N\geq 0
,

\mathcal{T}(N)
is the representation set defined in (
3.2
),
and
M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)
is the solution value to the
p
-adic change-making
problem.
Because
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the same structure provided in
Section
3
,
Key Lemma
3.1
applies.
We will immediately make use of this in the lemma that follows.

Lemma 5.1
.

Write

E(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}x^{n}
and

F_{0}(x)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}G_{N}x^{N}
.

(1)

Then

G_{N}=\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{T}(N)}\kappa(\mathbf{t})\gamma^{|\mathbf{t}|},

where

\kappa(\mathbf{t})=\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\beta_{t_{ij}}\hat{a}_{i}^{p^{j}t_{ij}}.

(2)

Suppose
\nu
is a maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
.
Then

v_{p}(G_{N})\geq\frac{M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)}{p-1}\geq\frac{s_{p}(N)}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}.

If
\nu
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element and the second inequality is an equality, then

v_{p}(G_{N})=\frac{s_{p}(N)}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}
.

Proof.

(1) By definition,

F_{0}(x)=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\prod_{i\in\Supp(f)}E(\gamma\hat{a}_{i}x^{ip^{j}})^{\tau^{j}}=\prod_{(i,j)\in I}E(\gamma\hat{a}_{i}^{p^{j}}x^{ip^{j}}).

Since each factor has expansion

E(\gamma\hat{a}_{i}^{p^{j}}x^{ip^{j}})=\sum_{t_{ij}=0}^{\infty}(\beta_{t_{ij}}\hat{a}_{i}^{p^{j}t_{ij}}\gamma^{t_{ij}})x^{ip^{j}t_{ij}},

the
x^{N}
-coefficient
G_{N}
of the product is

\sum\left(\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\beta_{t_{ij}}\hat{a}_{i}^{p^{j}t_{i,j}}\right)\gamma^{|\mathbf{t}|},

where the sum ranges over all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{T}(N)
.

(2) The second inequality follows from Key Lemma
3.1
.
Since
v_{p}(\kappa(\mathbf{t}))\geq 0
and
v_{p}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{p-1}
,
we have
v_{p}(G_{N})\geq\frac{1}{p-1}\min_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{T}(N)}|\mathbf{t}|=\frac{M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)}{p-1}
. This proves the first inequality.
By Key Lemma
3.1
, if
\nu
is the unique maximal element
and the second equality holds,
then there is a unique solution
\mathbf{t}=(t_{ij})\in\mathcal{T}(N)
with minimal
|\mathbf{t}|=M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)
.
This yields a unique lowest
\gamma
-power term in Part (1)’s expression of
G_{N}
,
that is,
\kappa(\mathbf{t})\gamma^{M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)}
.
Moreover, since
t_{ij}\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
, we have
\beta_{t_{ij}}=\frac{1}{t_{ij}!}
hence

v_{p}(\beta_{t_{ij}})=0
.
This implies
v_{p}(\kappa(\mathbf{t}))=0
. Therefore,

v_{p}(G_{N})=\frac{M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)}{p-1}
.
Combined with the hypothesis that
M_{\mathcal{C}}(N)=\frac{s_{p}(N)}{s_{p}(\nu)}
, we have

v_{p}(G_{N})=\frac{s_{p}(N)}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}
.
∎

For our result below, we recall the following notation from Section
4.3
:
For every
m\geq 1
,
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{m}
is the set of all pairs
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)

where
\mathcal{I}
is a set of
m
prime-to-
p
positive integers and
\sigma

is a permutation of
\mathcal{I}
. A pair
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is a minimizer if
the change-making solution value

M_{\mathcal{C}}(p^{a}\ell-\sigma\ell)
achieves its minimum

s_{p}(p^{a}\ell-\sigma\ell)/s_{p}(\nu)

for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}
at the same time.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G_{N}
be as in Lemma
5.1
.
Suppose
\nu
is a maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
.
For any
m\geq 1
, if
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}_{m}
, then

v_{p}\left(\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell}\right)\geq\frac{am}{s_{p}(\nu)}.

If
\nu
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
and

\nu<q
, then

(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is a minimizer if and only if

v_{p}\left(\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell}\right)=\frac{am}{s_{p}(\nu)}
.

Proof.

If the product contains
G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell}
with
q\ell-\sigma\ell<0

then the product equals
0
, and the first statement always holds;
since such a
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is not a minimizer, the second statement holds vacuously.
From now on we assume all sub-indices in the product we consider
are positive.

By Lemma
5.1
(2) we have

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}v_{p}(G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell})\geq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{M_{\mathcal{C}}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)}{p-1}\geq\frac{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}s_{p}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}.

By Proposition
2.2
,

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}s_{p}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)\geq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}(a(p-1)+s_{p}(\ell-1)-s_{p}(\sigma(\ell)-1))=am(p-1).

Combining the above two inequalities, we obtain the desired inequality.

It remains to prove the second statement.
First we prove the ‘if’ direction.
By Lemma
5.1
(2) again,
the equality

v_{p}(\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell})=\frac{am}{s_{p}(\nu)}

implies that

v_{p}(G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell})=\frac{M_{\mathcal{C}}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)}{p-1}=\frac{s_{p}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}

for all
\ell\in\mathcal{I}
.
Therefore

M_{\mathcal{C}}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)=\frac{s_{p}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)}{s_{p}(\nu)}

for all
\ell\in\mathcal{I}
; i.e.,
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is a minimizer.

To show the ‘only if’ direction:
Suppose
(\mathcal{I},\sigma)
is a minimizer. This implies by Lemma
5.1
(2)
that
v_{p}(G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell})=\frac{s_{p}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}

for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}
. Since
\ell,\sigma\ell\leq\nu<q
, then by Proposition
2.2

we have
s_{p}(q\ell-\sigma\ell)=a(p-1)+s_{p}(\ell-1)-s_{p}(\sigma\ell-1)
.
Summing these up over
\ell\in\mathcal{I}

we have

v_{p}\left(\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell}\right)=\frac{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}(a(p-1)+s_{p}(\ell-1)-s_{p}(\sigma\ell-1))}{(p-1)s_{p}(\nu)}=\frac{am}{s_{p}(\nu)}.

∎

5.2.
Fredholm determinant of Dwork operator

Let the Dwork operator
\alpha:\mathscr{L}\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}
be defined by
\alpha\coloneqq\Phi_{q}\circ F_{0}(x)
where

\Phi_{q}\left(\sum_{i}A_{i}x^{i}\right)=\sum_{i}A_{qi}x^{i}

and
\alpha(\sum_{i}A_{i}x^{i})=\Phi_{q}(F_{0}(x)\cdot\sum_{i}A_{i}x^{i})

for any
\sum_{i}A_{i}x^{i}\in\mathscr{L}
. Then
\alpha
is a

\Omega_{a}
-linear completely continuous operator on
\mathscr{L}
.

Let
\alpha^{\triangle}
denote the restriction of
\alpha
to the Banach subspace

, and write
\mathcal{B}=\{1,x,x^{2},\dots\}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\triangle}=\{x,x^{2},\dots\}
as
formal bases for
\mathscr{L}
and
x\mathscr{L}
, respectively.
Let
\Mat(\alpha^{\triangle})
be the matrix of
\alpha^{\triangle}

with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}^{\triangle}
;
that is,
\Mat(\alpha^{\triangle})=(G_{qi-j})_{i,j\geq 1}
,
where
G_{qi-j}
’s
are coefficients of

in Lemma
5.1
.
We have the following Fredholm determinant:

(5.2)

C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s)\coloneqq\det(1-s\Mat(\alpha^{\triangle}))=1+C_{1}s+C_{2}s^{2}+\cdots

where each coefficient is a sum over all
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{m}

(see Definition
4.10
) as follows:

(5.3)

C_{m}=\sum_{(\mathcal{I},\sigma)\in\mathbf{\Sigma}_{m}}(-1)^{m}\sgn(\sigma)\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell}.

Below we apply our result from Section
4
(in particular, Propositions
4.14

and
4.15
) to explicit
p
-adic estimates.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\Supp(f),a)
be the coin set as in (
5.1
).

(1)

Let
\nu
be a maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
. Then

for all

.

(2)

Suppose
\nu
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in

and

(a)

If
\nu
is

-symmetric with minimal factorization

,
then
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\bullet}\neq\varnothing
.

be the minimizer height of

,
and let

.
Then we have

,

(b)

Conversely, if

is

-symmetric.

(3)

Suppose

is the unique maximal

-adic weight element in

.
Then
v_{p}(C_{m})\geq\frac{am}{k(p-1)}

,
and
v_{p}(C_{m})>\frac{am}{k(p-1)}

.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
5.2
,

v_{p}(\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}G_{q\ell-\sigma\ell})\geq\frac{am}{s_{p}(\nu)}
for every

(1),

Corollary 5.4
.

Let

-adic Newton polygon of the power series

C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s)
in (

).

(1)

Then the first slope of
\NP_{q}(C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s))

is
\geq\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}
.

(2)

Suppose
\Supp(f)
contains
\nu
which is the unique element achieving the maximal
p
-adic weight and
\nu<q
.

(a)

If
\nu
is a
p
-symmetric number with minimal factorization
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell

and
k|a
, then the first slope of
\NP_{q}(C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s))

equals
\frac{1}{s_{p}(\nu)}
.

.

, where

.

(b)

is

-symmetric.

\frac{1}{k(p-1)}

The first slope of

f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
as

S_{m}(f)=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\zeta_{p}^{\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(f(c))}.

Then the
L
-function of the exponential sum of
f/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

L(f,s)\coloneqq\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}S_{m}(f)\frac{s^{m}}{m}\right).

Following Dwork theory (see
[
Bom66
]
),
there is a relationship between
L(f,s)
and the Fredholm determinant
\det(1-s\alpha)
.
In particular,

L(f,s)=\frac{\det(1-s\alpha)}{(1-s)\det(1-qs\alpha)}.

Let
\NP_{q}
denote the
q
-adic Newton polygon of a power series
and let
\NP_{q}^{<1}
denote the section of the
q
-adic Newton polygon

\NP_{q}
that has slope
<1
.
By Weil’s theorem,

L(f,s)
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\zeta_{p}][s]
of degree
d-1

with Newton slopes
<1
.
An elementary computation shows that

\NP_{q}(L(f,s))=\NP_{q}^{<1}(\det(1-s\Mat(\alpha^{\triangle})))=\NP_{q}^{<1}(C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s)).

By (
5.2
), we obtain the following:

(6.1)

\NP_{q}(L(f,s))=\NP_{q}^{<1}(C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s))=\NP_{q}^{<1}(1+C_{1}s+C_{2}s^{2}+\cdots).

Equivalently,
\NP_{q}(L(f,s))
is equal to
\NP_{q}(1+C_{1}s+C_{2}s^{2}+\cdots)

up to horizontal length
d-1
.

Recall the notion of
p
-symmetric numbers, minimal factorization, and shift factors
from Definitions
4.1
and
4.4
:
if
\nu
is
p
-symmetric, it has unique minimal factorization

\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
, and
the shift factor
e
is equal to the number of
p
-adic digits of
w
minus
1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
f=\sum_{i\geq 1,p\nmid i}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Then
\NP_{q}(L(f,s))

has its first slope
\geq\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}
.

(1)

Suppose
\Supp(f)
contains a
unique element
\nu
achieving the maximal
p
-adic weight. Then
the first slope achieves the minimum
\frac{1}{s_{p}(\nu)}

if and only if
\nu
is a
p
-symmetric number.
In this case, the multiplicity
t_{1}
of this first slope
satisfies
k-e\leq t_{1}\leq\nu
.

(2)

If
\nu=p^{k}-1
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
,
then the first slope of
\NP_{q}(L(f,s))
is

\frac{1}{k(p-1)}
of multiplicity
k
.

Proof.

Since the
q
-adic Newton polygon
\NP_{q}(L(f,s))

of
L(f,s)
is independent of the choice of base field of
f
,
we may extend the base field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
f
such that

q=p^{a}>\nu
and such that
k|a
whenever

\nu\in\Supp(f)
is
p
-symmetric with minimal factorization

\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell
.
By (
6.1
), it is reduced to compute the first slope of

\NP_{q}(C^{\triangle}(f/\mathbb{F}_{q},s))
,
which is done in Corollary
5.4
.
∎

6.2.
Zeta functions of Artin-Schreier curves

The following result will have Theorem

1.1
as an immediate corollary, and will strengthen
the result by offering bounds on the multiplicity of the first slope.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X_{f}:y^{p}-y=f(x)
with
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of
\deg(f)=d\geq 3
.
Then the first slope of
X_{f}
is

\geq\frac{1}{\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)}
.

(1)

If
\nu
is the unique element in
\Supp(f)
with the maximal
p
-adic weight,
then the equality holds if and only if
\nu
is
p
-symmetric.
In this case, if
\nu
has its minimal factorization
\nu*w=(p^{k}-1)\ell

and shift factor
e
, then
the multiplicity
t_{1}
of the first slope satisfies

(k-e)(p-1)\leq t_{1}\leq\nu(p-1)
.

(2)

If
\nu=p^{k}-1
is the unique maximal
p
-adic weight element in
\Supp(f)
, then
the first slope is
\frac{1}{k(p-1)}
of multiplicity
k(p-1)
.

Proof.

Let
g
denote the genus of
X
. We know
g=\frac{(p-1)(d-1)}{2}
.
It is well-known that

Z(X/\mathbb{F}_{q},s)=\frac{\prod_{\sigma}\sigma(L(f,s))}{(1-s)(1-qs)}

where the product ranges over all

\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
The reciprocal zeros
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2g}
of the zeta function are precisely the reciprocal
roots of
\sigma(L(f,s))
for all
\sigma
.
Each
\sigma(L(f,s))
has the same
q
-adic Newton polygon as
L(f,s)

for all
\sigma
. Thus its first slope is equal to the first slope of
\NP_{q}(L(f,s))
,
and our statements about the first slope follow from Theorem
6.1
.
Since the
q
-adic Newton polygon of
the numerator of the zeta function is a dilation of
that of
L(f,s)
by a factor of
p-1
,
our statements about the multiplicities follow
from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If
\Supp(f)
does not have a unique element
\nu
that achieves
the maximal
s_{p}(-)
,
the equality in Theorem
6.2
may or may not hold.
It depends on
the actual
f
, not just the support
\Supp(f)
.
For example, when
p=2
,
the set
\Supp(f)=\{21,19,13,7,3\}
does not have a unique maximum.
We have
\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}(s_{2}(i))=3
.
However, the first slope of
X_{f}
is
1/2

when
f=x^{21}+x^{19}+x^{13}+x^{7}+x^{3}
.

Remark 6.4

(Maximal
p
-adic weight set in
\Supp(f)
is invariant)
.

Since Newton polygons of curves are invariant under isomorphisms, it bears
considering whether a curve
X_{f}
that fails to meet the uniqueness criterion
for
\Supp(f)
could be isomorphic to a curve
X_{g}
that does meet it.

Suppose
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are reduced, i.e., every element of

\Supp(f)\cup\Supp(g)
is coprime to
p
. Suppose

X_{f}
and
X_{g}
are isomorphic as Artin-Schreier covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}

preserving
\infty
.
Then the isomorphism is given by

x\longmapsto\alpha x+\beta,y\longmapsto cy+h,

where

\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
.
Hence

cg(x)=f(\alpha x+\beta)+h(x)-h(x)^{p}.

Let

m:=\max_{i\in\Supp(f)}s_{p}(i)
and

M(f):=\{i\in\Supp(f):s_{p}(i)=m\}.

Fix

N\geq 1
with
s_{p}(N)\geq m
.
A term
a_{w}(\alpha x+\beta)^{w}
in
f(\alpha x+\beta)

can contribute to the coefficient of
x^{N}
only if
w\geq N
, in which case
the binomial factor is
\binom{w}{N}
. By Kummer’s formula,

v_{p}\binom{w}{N}=\frac{s_{p}(N)+s_{p}(w-N)-s_{p}(w)}{p-1}.

Thus
\binom{w}{N}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
only if

s_{p}(w)=s_{p}(N)+s_{p}(w-N).

Since
s_{p}(w)\leq m\leq s_{p}(N)
, this forces

s_{p}(w)=s_{p}(N)=m,s_{p}(w-N)=0.

Therefore
w=N
. Consequently,

[x^{N}]f(\alpha x+\beta)=a_{N}\alpha^{N}
if
N\in M(f)
, and equals
0
otherwise.
On the other hand, if
[x^{N}](h-h^{p})\neq 0
,
then
p\nmid N
since
g(x)
is reduced.
Thus,
[x^{N}]h\neq 0
,
which implies
[x^{p^{r}N}](h-h^{p})\neq 0

for some
r\geq 1
.
This again is impossible because
g(x)
is reduced.
We now have that
[x^{N}](cg)=[x^{N}]f(\alpha x+\beta)=\alpha^{N}[x^{N}]f
.
Therefore the maximal

p
-adic weight subset of
\Supp(g)
is
M(f)
.

It is well-known that the
first slope of an affine curve
X
gives the divisibility of
the number of rational points on
X
(see
[
Ka71
, Proposition 2.2]
).
Write
X_{f}^{\mathrm{aff}}
for the affine Artin-Schreier curve defined by
y^{p}-y=f
.
Then our result above (for example Theorem
1.1
) recovers
the following known divisibility (see
[
Wan95
]
for example).

Corollary 6.5
.

If
\nu\in\Supp(f)
has the maximum
p
-adic weight and
q=p^{a}
then

p^{\lceil\frac{am}{s_{p}(\nu)}\rceil}\;|\;\#X_{f}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})\mbox{ for every $m\geq 1$}.

6.3.
Construction of curves with first slope
1/n

for every
n\geq 2
in every characteristic
p
, and other applications

We conclude this paper by presenting some corollaries of
Theorem
6.2
.
Since for every positive integer
n
there is a
p
-symmetric number
\nu
with
s_{p}(\nu)=n

(see, e.g., Example
4.5
(9) or Remark
6.10
),
the following corollary —
which follows directly from Theorem
6.2
—
enables us to construct many families
of curves with a fixed first slope:

Corollary 6.6
.

Fix an integer
n\geq 2
.
Let
\nu
be a
p
-symmetric number with
s_{p}(\nu)=n
,
and let
g(x)\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
be any polynomial satisfying

\max_{i\in\Supp(g)}s_{p}(i)<n

and
p\nmid i
for all
i\in\Supp(g)
.
Then, setting
f(x)=a_{\nu}x^{\nu}+g(x)

for some
a_{\nu}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
,
the first slope of
X_{f}

is equal to
1/n
. ∎

Recall that it is already known that if
f
is of degree
d
with
d<p
,
then the first slope of
X_{f}
achieves the lower bound of
1/d
if and only if

p\equiv 1\bmod d
.
We provided a generalization of this in the introduction,
and are now ready to prove it here.

Corollary 6.7

(Corollary
1.2
)
.

Suppose
\Supp(f)
has a unique maximal
p
-adic weight element
\nu
.
If
p\equiv 1\bmod s_{p}(\nu)
,
then the first slope of
X_{f}
is equal to
1/s_{p}(\nu)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.2
(1), it remains to show that
if
s_{p}(\nu)\mid(p-1)
, then
\nu
is
p
-symmetric.
If
s_{p}(\nu)=n
,
n|(p-1)
,
then setting
m=(p-1)/n
,
it follows that the (
p
-adic) product
\nu m
is carry-free,
and
s_{p}(\nu m)=p-1
.
If we write
\nu m

p
-adically as
\nu m=d_{0}+d_{1}p+\dots+d_{r}p^{r}
,
then we have
d_{0}+d_{1}+\dots+d_{r}=s_{p}(\nu m)\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}
,
which implies that
(p-1)\mid\nu m
.
Thus
\nu m=(p-1)\ell
for some
\ell>0
.
Since
s_{p}(\nu m)=p-1
,
the product
(\nu m)(1+p+p^{2}+\dots+p^{k-1})
for all
k\geq 1

is carry-free.
Therefore, setting
w=m(1+p+\dots+p^{k-1})

for some
k
such that
p^{k}>\ell
,
we have

\nu*w=(1+p+p^{2}+\dots+p^{k-1})(p-1)\ell=(p^{k}-1)\ell

and so
\nu
is
p
-symmetric.
∎

Our next corollary provides explicit constructions of curves
X_{f}

via the support set of
f
.
Its first part shows that for any
n\geq 2
,
there exist curves of arbitrarily large genus
with first slope equal to
1/n
.
The second part provides a general upper bound on the smallest genus
for which there exists a curve with first slope
1/n
.

Corollary 6.8
.

Fix a prime
p
and an integer
n\geq 2
.
Let
N>0
.

(1)

Let
\nu
be a
p
-symmetric number with
s_{p}(\nu)=n
.
Suppose
\{\nu,1+p^{\lceil\frac{2N}{p-1}\rceil}\}\subseteq\Supp(f)
, and

\Supp(f)\subseteq\left\{\nu,1+p^{\lceil\frac{2N}{p-1}\rceil},1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}p^{i_{k}}\mid 1\leq m\leq n-2,1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{m}\right\}.

Then

X_{f}:y^{p}-y=f
is of genus
\geq N

and first slope
\frac{1}{n}
.

(2)

There is a
p
-symmetric number
\nu
with
s_{p}(\nu)=n
and

\nu\leq\frac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}
.
Suppose
\nu\in\Supp(f)
and

\Supp(f)\subseteq\left\{\nu,1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}p^{i_{k}}\mid 1\leq m\leq n-2,1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n-1\right\}.

Then
X_{f}:y^{p}-y=f
is of genus

\leq\frac{p^{n}-p}{2}
with first slope equal to
\frac{1}{n}
.

Proof.

1) The hypothesis says that the set
\Supp(f)
has a maximal
p
-adic weight element
\nu
, which is unique when
n\geq 3
.
The slope part follows from Theorem
6.2
;
the genus part follows from direct computation: let
d=\deg(f)
,
the genus of
X_{f}
is

\frac{(d-1)(p-1)}{2}>N
.

2)
It is clear that
\frac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}
is
p
-symmetric and
s_{p}(\frac{p^{n}-1}{p-1})=n
.
Following a similar argument as the above, the first slope of
X_{f}
is equal to
\frac{1}{n}

by Theorem
6.2
.
Its genus is
g=\frac{(\nu-1)(p-1)}{2}\leq\frac{p^{n}-p}{2}
.
∎

Our last corollary,
which was known for the case
p=2
in
[
SZ02
]
,
gives a specific family of Artin-Schreier curves
where both the first slope and its multiplicity can be completely determined.

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
f=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d,p\nmid i}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be of degree
d\geq 3
.
Suppose that

p^{k}-1\leq d\leq 2p^{k}-p^{k-1}-2
and
a_{p^{k}-1}\neq 0
for some
k\geq 1
.
Then the first slope of
X_{f}
is equal to
\frac{1}{(p-1)k}

of multiplicity
(p-1)k
, and
X_{f}
is non-supersingular
if and only if
(p,k)\neq(2,2)
.

Proof.

Observe that
s_{p}(p^{k}-1)=k(p-1)
.
The smallest
\nu^{\prime}>p^{k}-1
such that
s_{p}(\nu^{\prime})\geq k(p-1)

is
\nu^{\prime}=2p^{k}-p^{k-1}-1
, so the upper bound on
d

guarantees that

\nu\coloneqq p^{k}-1\in\Supp(f)
is the unique element
with the maximal
s_{p}(\nu)=k(p-1)
in
\Supp(f)
.
The first two statements now follow from Theorem
6.2
.
For the final statement,
notice that
X_{f}
is supersingular if and only if its genus is
\geq 1
and
the first slope is
\frac{1}{(p-1)k}=\frac{1}{2}
.
This is equivalent to
(p,k)=(2,2),

or
(3,1)
. The latter contradicts our hypothesis.
∎

.

(1)

For

Volume estimates for the singular sets of mean curvature flows
Hanbing Fang and Yu Li

Abstract
In this paper, we establish uniform and sharp volume estimates for the singular set and the quantitative singular strata of mean curvature flows starting from a smooth, closed, mean-convex hypersurface in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} .



1
Introduction

A one-parameter family
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in I}
of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
evolves by mean curvature flow if

(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}(x),

where
\mathbf{H}=-H\mathbf{n}
is the mean curvature vector,
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal,
v^{\perp}
denotes the normal component of a vector
v
, and the mean curvature
H
is given by

H=\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{n}).

Mean curvature flow is the gradient flow of the area functional. The simplest example of a non-static mean curvature flow is the family of shrinking spheres
\{S^{n}(\sqrt{2n|t|})\}_{t<0}
. In this case, the flow is smooth except at the origin at time
t=0
, where it becomes extinct. More generally, Huisken
[
Hui84
]
proved that if the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is closed and convex, then
M_{t}
becomes increasingly spherical as it contracts to a point.

Recall that a mean curvature flow
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
from a closed hypersurface
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
must develop singularities in finite time. In studying mean curvature flow, a useful weak solution concept is the level set flow (see
[
ES91
]
and
[
CGG91
]
): Suppose
f
is a function satisfying the equation

\partial_{t}f=|\nabla f|\,\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\right),

(1.1)

then the level sets
M_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mid f(x,t)=0\}
are hypersurfaces evolving under mean curvature flow. In general, Evans-Spruck
[
ES91
]
and Chen-Giga-Goto
[
CGG91
]
established the existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
), providing a weak formulation of mean curvature flow.

A fundamental question is understanding the formation of singularities for the level set flow before its extinction. A special case of interest is the level set flow from a smooth, closed, mean-convex hypersurface, meaning that the mean curvature of the initial hypersurface is nonnegative. The mean-convexity assumption leads to many significant results, as shown in
[
HS99a
]

[
HS99b
]

[
BH16
]

[
Whi00
]

[
Whi03
]

[
Whi15
]

[
HK17a
]

[
HK17b
]

[
CMI16b
]

[
CMI16a
]
, among others. In this context, one can express the level set function
f(x,t)=u(x)-t
(see
[
ES91
, Section 7.3]
), so that (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle-1=|\nabla u|\ \mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right),

(1.2)

where
u
is referred to as the
arrival time
. The level set
M_{t}=\{u(x)=t\}
encloses a mean-convex domain. The family
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
moves inward, sweeping out the domain
\Omega
enclosed by
M_{0}
. In
[
CMI16a
]
, Colding and Minicozzi proved that
u
is twice differentiable, and thus the equation (
1.2
) holds in the classical sense. Furthermore, it is shown in
[
Whi00
, Corollary 3.3]
that the level set flow is nonfattening, meaning the level sets have no interior.

The singular set of the level set flow is given by

\mathcal{S}=\{(x,u(x))\mid\nabla u(x)=0\}.

In
[
Whi00
]
, White established the significant result that
\mathcal{S}
has parabolic Hausdorff dimension of at most
n-1
. Later, in
[
Whi03
]
and
[
Whi15
]
, White demonstrated that all tangent flows are generalized cylinders of the form
\{\mathbb{R}^{k}\times S^{n-k}(\sqrt{2(n-k)|t|})\}_{t\leq 0}
with multiplicity one. Furthermore, in
[
CMI15
]
, Colding and Minicozzi proved that the axis of these generalized cylinders are unique by establishing a discrete Łojasiewicz inequality.

The singular set
\mathcal{S}
admits the following stratification:

\mathcal{S}^{0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{S}^{n-1}=\mathcal{S},

where
\mathcal{S}^{k}
consists of all singularities for which the tangent flow splits off an Euclidean factor of dimension at most
k
. Additionally, White proved in
[
Whi97
]
that the parabolic Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{k}
is at most
k
. Colding and Minicozzi further showed in
[
CMI16b
, Theorem 4.6]
that each stratum
\mathcal{S}^{k}
is contained in countably many of
k
-dimensional
C^{1}
-submanifolds, along with a set of lower dimension.

To further analyze the singular set, we define the following quantitative singular stratum, analogous to those in
[
CN13
]
and
[
CHN13
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

For
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
r\in(0,1)
, the
j
-th quantitative singular stratum of the level set flow
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is define as

\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}:=\{X\in\mathcal{M}\mid X\text{ is not }(j^{\prime},\epsilon,s)\text{-cylindrical}\text{ for any }s\in[r,1)\text{ and any }j^{\prime}>j\}.

Here, a spacetime point
X\in\mathcal{M}
is
(j,\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical if, at scale
r
, the flow in a ball of size
\epsilon^{-1}
can be written as a graph over a cylinder
\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)})
with
C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm smaller than
\epsilon
; see Definition
2.6
for the precise formulation. It can be shown (see Proposition
2.8
) that

\mathcal{S}^{j}=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\bigcap_{r\in(0,1)}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}.

In
[
CHN13
]
, Cheeger, Haslhofer, and Naber introduced a quantitative singular stratum for general Brakke flows of any codimension, establishing a Minkowski-type volume estimate (see
[
CHN13
, Theorem 1.14]
). Our first main result provides a uniform and sharp volume estimate for the
j
-th quantitative singular stratum
\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}
, where the bound depends essentially on the entropy (see equation (
2.2
)) and the noncollapsing constant (see Definition
2.3
) of the initial hypersurface.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a level set flow starting from a closed, smooth, mean-convex,
\alpha
-noncollapsed hypersurface
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\lambda(M_{0})\leq\Lambda
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)>0
such that for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
with
\mathfrak{t}(X)>2
,

\mathrm{Vol}\left(\mathfrak{B}_{r}(\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\right)\leq Cr^{n+3-j},

(1.3)

for
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and any
r\in(0,1)
. In particular, for
\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}:=\bigcap_{r\in(0,1)}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}
, we have

\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}\left(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}^{j}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\right)\leq C.

(1.4)

Here,
\mathrm{Vol}(\cdot)
denotes the parabolic volume, and
\mathfrak{B}(\cdot)
and
\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}
denote balls and the
j
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure with respect to the parabolic distance (see (
2.1
)), respectively. For further notation, readers may refer to subsection
2.1
for precise definitions.

It was proved by Colding and Minicozzi
[
CMI16b
, Theorem 4.6]
that the top stratum
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{n-2}
is contained in finitely many
(n-1)
-dimensional
C^{1}
-submanifolds. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{S}
has locally finite
(n-1)
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure. Since
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{S}
if
\epsilon\leq\epsilon(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
(see Proposition
2.9
), we obtain the following result as a corollary of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a level set flow starting from a closed, smooth, mean-convex,
\alpha
-noncollapsed hypersurface
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\lambda(M_{0})\leq\Lambda
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\alpha,\Lambda)>0
such that for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
with
\mathfrak{t}(X)>2
,

\mathscr{H}_{P}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{S}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\right)\leq C.

If the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is strictly
k
-convex, in the sense that
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}>0
, where
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
are the principal curvatures, then we can assume

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\geq\beta H>0

(1.5)

for some
\beta>0
. It can be shown (see
[
CHN13
]
and
[
HK17a
]
) that condition (
1.5
) is preserved and holds at any smooth point under the level set flow. Furthermore, this condition restricts all tangent flows at a singular point to be of the form of
\{\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)|t|})\}_{t\leq 0}
for
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}
, implying that
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{k-1}
. Similar to Proposition
2.9
, if
\epsilon\leq\epsilon(n,\alpha,\beta,\Lambda)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{k-1}_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{S}
, and we obtain the following result:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a level set flow starting from a closed, smooth,
\alpha
-noncollapsed hypersurface
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying condition (
1.5
) and
\lambda(M_{0})\leq\Lambda
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\alpha,\beta,\Lambda)>0
such that for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
with
\mathfrak{t}(X)>2
,

\mathscr{H}_{P}^{k-1}\left(\mathcal{S}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\right)\leq C.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on several ingredients.

The first is a quantitative version (see Theorem
3.2
) of the uniqueness of cylindrical tangent flows, as established by Colding and Minicozzi
[
CMI15
]
. Roughly speaking, this result states that if the Gaussian density ratio
\Theta(X,\tau)
for a spacetime point
X\in\mathcal{M}
is nearly constant at two different scales
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, then
X
is
(j,\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical for any
r\in[\sqrt{\tau}_{1},\sqrt{\tau}_{2}]
, with the same underlying cylinder
\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)})
.

Next, we define the concept of a neck region
\mathscr{N}
(see Definition
4.1
). The notion of a neck region first appeared in
[
JN21
]
in the proof of the
(n-4)
-finiteness conjecture and was later used to establish the rectifiability of singular sets in noncollapsed limit spaces with Ricci curvature bounded below (see
[
CJN21
]
). With the quantitative uniqueness, we obtain a structural characterization of the center
\mathscr{C}
of the neck region. More precisely, it can be shown (see Theorem
4.4
) that
\mathscr{C}
is a compact Lipschitz graph over a
j
-plane.

A crucial step is to establish the neck decomposition theorem (see Theorem
4.7
), which also holds in the context of Ricci limit spaces (cf.
[
CJN21
, Theorem 2.12]
). In essence, we obtain the decomposition

\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\bigcap\mathcal{M}\subset\bigcup_{a}(\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{a})\bigcup_{b}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\bigcup\mathcal{S}^{j-1}

(1.6)

with the following content estimate:

\sum_{a}r_{a}^{j}+\sum_{b}r_{b}^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{a})\leq Cr^{j}.

(1.7)

Here, each
\mathscr{N}_{a}\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},2r_{a})
is a neck region with center
\mathscr{C}_{a}
, each
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})
approximately splits off an
\mathbb{R}^{j^{\prime}}
for some
j^{\prime}>j
at scale
r_{b}
, and
\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
has lower dimension. We will prove (see Corollary
4.8
) that
\mathcal{S}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
is essentially contained in the union of centers
\mathscr{C}_{a}
, thereby recovering the following important structure theorem, originally proved by Colding and Minicozzi
[
CMI16b
]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a level set flow starting from a closed, smooth, mean-convex hypersurface. Then,

(a)

\mathcal{S}^{n-1}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{n-2}
is contained in finite compact
(n-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz graphs.

(b)

For
j\leq n-2
,
\mathcal{S}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
is contained in countable compact
j
-dimensional Lipschitz graphs.

The proof of the neck decomposition theorem relies on a refined covering argument as in
[
CJN21
]
. By dividing all parabolic balls into different types (see Definition
4.11
), we achieve the decomposition (
1.6
) with the content estimate (
1.7
) via an iterative covering argument (see Proposition
4.12
, Proposition
4.13
and Proposition
4.14
). The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the neck decomposition theorem and the content estimate (
1.7
).

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we review fundamental concepts and results related to mean curvature flows, focusing on level set flows starting from smooth, closed, mean-convex hypersurfaces. In Section 3, we establish the quantitative uniqueness of cylindrical singularities. Section 4, which contains the primary technical details, formulates the definition of a neck region and proves its structural properties. This section also includes the proof of the neck decomposition theorem. Finally, in Section 5, we conclude with the proof of our main theorem.

Preliminaries

In this section, we review some definitions, notations, and prior results in mean curvature flow that will be frequently used throughout the paper.

2.1
Notations and conventions

We define the spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Throughout this paper, uppercase letters such as
X,Y,Z
, etc., denote points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, while lowercase letters
x,y,z
, etc., represent points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The origin in space is denoted by
\vec{0}^{n+1}
, and the origin in spacetime is represented by
\vec{0}^{n+1,1}
. For any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, the space component and time component are denoted by
\mathfrak{e}(X)
and
\mathfrak{t}(X)
, respectively.

The parabolic distance on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is defined as follows: for
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
,

\|X-Y\|:=\max\{|\mathfrak{e}(X)-\mathfrak{e}(Y)|,|\mathfrak{t}(X)-\mathfrak{t}(X)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\},

(2.1)

where
|\cdot|
represents the standard Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Additionally, we denote the parabolic ball centered at
X
with radius
r
by
\mathfrak{B}(X,r)=B(\mathfrak{e}(X),r)\times(\mathfrak{t}(X)-r^{2},\mathfrak{t}(X)+r^{2})
.

The
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is denoted by
\mathscr{H}^{k}
, and the
k
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, denoted by
\mathscr{H}^{k}_{P}
, is defined with respect to the distance given in (
2.1
). In particular, we use
\mathrm{Vol}(\cdot)
to denote
\mathscr{H}^{n+3}_{P}
.

We will also use
\Psi(\epsilon)
to denote a quantity that satisfies
\Psi(\epsilon)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
, assuming all other related quantities are fixed. Note that
\Psi(\epsilon)
may vary from line to line.

2.2
Integral Brakke flows

The Brakke flow is an important notion of the weak mean curvature flow (cf.
[
Bra78
]
[
Ilm94
]
), whose definition we recall.

Definition 2.1

(Integral Brakke flow)
.

An
n
-dimensional integral Brakke flow

\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a one-parameter family of Radon measures
\mathcal{M}=(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
over an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
so that:

(a)

For almost every
t\in I
, there exists an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V(t)
with
\mu_{t}=\mu_{V(t)}
so that
V(t)
has locally bounded first variation, and its mean curvature
\mathbf{H}
is orthogonal to
\mathrm{Tan}(V(t),\cdot)
almost everywhere.

(b)

For any
[a,b]\subset I
and compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{K}(1+|\mathbf{H}|^{2})\,d\mu_{t}dt<\infty.

(c)

If
[a,b]\subset I
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times[a,b],[0,\infty))
, then

\int\varphi(\cdot,b)\,d\mu_{b}-\int\varphi(\cdot,a)\,d\mu_{a}\leq\int_{a}^{b}\int-|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\varphi+\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi\,d\mu_{t}dt.

For an integer
n
-rectifiable Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we set

\displaystyle F(\mu):=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu(x).

Following
[
CMI12
]
, the entropy of
\mu
is defined as

\displaystyle\lambda(\mu):=\sup_{(y,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}F(\mu^{y,\rho})\quad\text{where}\quad\mu^{y,\rho}(U)=\rho^{n}\mu(\rho^{-1}U+y)

denotes the rescaled measure. For an integral Brakke flow
\mathcal{M}=(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we set

\lambda(\mathcal{M})=\sup_{t\geq 0}\lambda(\mu_{t}).

(2.2)

The following celebrated monotonicity formula of Huisken holds (cf.
[
Hui84
]
[
Ilm95
, Lemma 7]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an integral Brakke flow on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\lambda(\mu_{0})<\infty
. Then for any
Y=(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(0,\infty)
and all
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<s
,

\displaystyle\int\Phi_{Y}(x,t_{2})\,d\mu_{t_{2}}(x)-\int\Phi_{Y}(x,t_{2})\,d\mu_{t_{1}}(x)\leq-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int\left|\mathbf{H}(x,t)+\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{2(s-t)}\right|^{2}\Phi_{Y}(x,t)\,d\mu_{t}(x)dt,

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{Y}(x,t)=(4\pi(s-t))^{-n/2}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(s-t)}}.

In particular,
\lambda(\mu_{t})
is non-increasing for
t\geq 0
and hence
\lambda(\mathcal{M})=\lambda(\mu_{0})
.

For
X=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
with
t>0
and
\tau>0
, we recall the
Gaussian density ratio
is defined by

\Theta(\mathcal{M},X,\tau):=\int\Phi_{X}(y,t-\tau)\,d\mu_{t-\tau}(y),

By Proposition
2.2
,
\Theta(\mathcal{M},X,\tau)
is increasing in
\tau
, and thus the limit as
\tau\to 0^{+}
, referred to as the
Gaussian density
, exists and is denoted by

\Theta(\mathcal{M},X):=\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\Theta(\mathcal{M},X,\tau).

It is straightforward from the definition that
\Theta(\mathcal{M},X)
is upper semi-continuous in
X
.

2.3
Level set flow from a mean-convex hypersurface

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the level set flow starting from a closed, smooth, mean-convex hypersurface
M_{0}
. This flow,
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, represents the maximal family of the closed set originating from
M_{0}
that satisfies the avoidance principle; see
[
Ilm94
, Section 10]
. It can be shown, as in
[
Whi00
, Corollary 3.3]
, that the level set flow is nonfattening, meaning the level sets have no interior. Consequently, it follows from
[
Ilm94
, Theorem 11.4]
that

\mathcal{M}=(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}:=(\mathscr{H}^{n}\llcorner M_{t})_{t\geq 0}

defines an integral Brakke flow.

Next, we recall the following definition from
[
SW09
]
and
[
And12
]
.

Definition 2.3

(
\alpha
-noncollapsed)
.

A smooth, mean-convex hypersurface
M
enclosing a domain
\Omega
is
\alpha
-noncollapsed
for a constant
\alpha>0
if for any
x\in M
, there exist closed balls
\bar{B}_{\mathrm{int}}\subset\Omega,\bar{B}_{\mathrm{ext}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathrm{int}(\Omega)
, each with radius at least
\alpha/H(x)
, that are tangent to
M
at
x
from the interior and exterior, respectively.

If
H(x)=0
at some point
x\in M
, then being
\alpha
-noncollapsed implies that
\Omega
is a half-space. It is noteworthy that any compact, smooth, strictly mean-convex hypersurface is
\alpha
-noncollapsed for some
\alpha>0
. An important result from
[
SW09
]
and
[
And12
]
states that the
\alpha
-noncollapsing condition is preserved under mean curvature flow.

By considering the viscosity mean curvature (cf.
[
HK17a
, Definition 1.3]
), the definition of
\alpha
-noncollapsed can be extended to any mean-convex closed set (cf.
[
HK17a
, Definition 1.4]
). We say a level set flow
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
\alpha
-noncollapsed if each
\Omega_{t}
, enclosed by
M_{t}
, is
\alpha
-noncollapsed. In this framework, Haslhofer and Kleiner
[
HK17a
, Theorem 1.5]
established the following result:

Theorem 2.4
.

A level set flow
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
starting from a closed, smooth, mean-convex hypersurface
M_{0}
is
\alpha
-noncollapsed if
M_{0}
is
\alpha
-noncollapsed.

In
[
HK17a
]
, Haslhofer and Kleiner defined a broader class of flows that includes all
\alpha
-noncollapsed compact level set flows with smooth initial conditions.

Definition 2.5

(
\alpha
-Andrews flows)
.

The class of
\alpha
-Andrews flows
is the smallest class of set flows that contains all
\alpha
-noncollapsed compact level set flows with smooth initial condition, which is closed under operations of restriction to smaller time intervals, parabolic rescaling, and passage to Hausdorff limits.

For
r>0
, we denote the spacetime dilation by
\mathcal{D}_{r}:(x,t)\to(rx,r^{2}t)
and define
\mathcal{M}_{X,r}=\mathcal{D}_{1/r}(\mathcal{M}-X)
for any
\alpha
-Andrews flow
\mathcal{M}
. For any
X\in\mathcal{M}
where
\mathfrak{t}(X)
is not the initial time, a subsequential limit of
\mathcal{M}_{X_{i},r_{i}}
with
X_{i}\to X
and
r_{i}\to 0
is called a
limit flow
at
X
. In particular, a subsequential limit of
\mathcal{M}_{X,r_{i}}
with
r_{i}\to 0
is called a
tangent flow
at
X
.

It was shown in
[
HK17a
, Theorem 1.11]
that any limit flow is a smooth, convex, ancient solution to the mean curvature flow. Furthermore, each tangent flow is given by either a static plane or shrinking generalized cylinders.

2.4
Quantitative cylindrical singularities

We define the moduli space
\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
, consisting of all
n
-dimensional level set flows
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
that starts from a closed, smooth, mean-convex,
\alpha
-noncollapsed hypersurface
M_{0}
with
\lambda(M_{0})\leq\Lambda
. Hereafter, all level set flows are assumed to belong to this space unless otherwise specified.

Next, we set
\mathcal{C}^{j}
to be the space consisting of the generalized cylinder

\left(\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)|t|})\right)_{t\leq 0}

of multiplicity one, together with all its rotations. Then, any tangent flow at
X\in\mathcal{S}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{j}
. The entropy of the flow in
\mathcal{C}^{j}
is denoted by
\Theta_{j}
. From
[
Sto94
]
, we have

\sqrt{2}<\Theta_{0}<\Theta_{1}<\ldots<\Theta_{n-1}<2.

(2.3)

Next, we introduce the following definition to describe the local behavior near a given point.

Definition 2.6
.

A point
X\in\mathcal{M}
is said to be
(j,\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical
if for any
s\in[\epsilon r,\epsilon^{-1}r]
,

(\mathcal{M}_{X,s})_{\mathfrak{t}=-1}\bigcap B(\vec{0}^{n+1},\epsilon^{-1})

is a graph over a fixed cylinder
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)})
with
C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm at most
\epsilon
. If the
j
-plane
V
is the
\mathbb{R}^{j}
-factor of
\Sigma
, then we say
X
is
(j,\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
L_{Y}:=(\mathfrak{e}(Y)+V)\times\{\mathfrak{t}(Y)\}
.

We have the following quantitative rigidity.

Proposition 2.7

(Quantitative rigidity)
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)>0
such that for
X\in\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
and
r>0
, if
\mathfrak{t}(X)>\delta^{-1}r^{2}
and

\Theta(\mathcal{M},X,\delta^{-1}r^{2})-\Theta(\mathcal{M},X,\delta r^{2})\leq\delta^{2},

then
X
is
(j,\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical for some
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
r=1
. Suppose that the conclusion fails. Then we can find
\mathcal{M}^{k}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
,
\delta_{k}\leq k^{-1}
and
X_{k}\in\mathcal{M}^{k}
such that

\Theta(\mathcal{M}^{k},X_{k},\delta_{k}^{-1})-\Theta(\mathcal{M}^{k},X_{k},\delta_{k})\leq\delta^{2}_{k},

(2.4)

but
\mathcal{M}^{k}
is not
(j,\epsilon,1)
-cylindrical for any
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
.

By taking a subsequence, we assume
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{X_{k},1}\to\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
in the Hausdorff sense. The limit
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
is an ancient
\alpha
-Andrews flow as defined in Definition
2.5
. By
[
HK17a
, Theorem 1.11]
,
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
is smooth before the extinction time
T_{0}
. Note that
\vec{0}^{n+1,1}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
, we have
T_{0}\geq 0
. By the local regularity theorem of
[
Whi05
]
(see also
[
HK17a
, Appendix C]
), we conclude that
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{X_{k},1}
converges to
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
smoothly on
(-\infty,T_{0})
.

Since
\lambda(\mathcal{M}^{k})\leq\Lambda
, it follows from the convergence of Gaussian densities and (
2.4
) that

\Theta(\mathcal{M}^{\infty},\vec{0}^{n+1,1},\tau)

is constant for any
\tau>0
. Therefore, by Proposition
2.2
, we conclude that
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
is a self-shrinker. In particular,
T_{0}=0
, and by
[
HK17a
, Theorem 1.11]
,
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
must belong to
\mathcal{C}^{j}
for some
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
. This yields a contradiction.
∎

For the quantitative singular stratum
\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}
(see Definition
1.1
), we immediately get that if
j\leq j^{\prime},\epsilon\geq\epsilon^{\prime},r\leq r^{\prime}
, then

\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}\subset\mathcal{S}^{j^{\prime}}_{\epsilon^{\prime},r^{\prime}}.

Moreover, we have the following characterization of
\mathcal{S}^{j}
:

Proposition 2.8
.

With the above definitions, we have

\mathcal{S}^{j}=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\bigcap_{r\in(0,1)}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r}.

(2.5)

Proof.

If
X\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}
, then the tangent flow at
X
is given by shrinking cylinders in
\mathcal{C}^{j^{\prime}}
for
j^{\prime}\geq j+1
. Thus, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a small
r>0
such that
X
is
(j^{\prime},\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical and hence
X\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
. In other words,
X\notin\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
.
Conversely, if
X\notin\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
r_{k}>0
, such that
X
is
(j^{\prime},k^{-1},r_{k})
-cylindrical for some
j^{\prime}>j
. Consequently,
X\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}
.
∎

For the top stratum, we have the following characterization.

Proposition 2.9
.

There exists a constant
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,\alpha,\Lambda)>0
such that

\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{S}.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a sequence
\mathcal{M}^{k}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
, with
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
and
X_{k}\in\mathcal{M}^{k}
such that
X_{k}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k})\setminus\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon_{k}}(\mathcal{M}^{k})
. By Definition
1.1
, there exists a sequence
r_{k}>0
such that
X_{k}
is
(n,\epsilon_{k},r_{k})
-cylindrical. Consequently,
\mathcal{M}_{X_{k},r_{k}}
converges, after taking a subsequence if necessary, to the static plane. By the local regularity theorem of
[
Whi05
]
, it follows that
X_{k}
is a regular point if
k
is sufficiently large, leading to a contradiction.
∎

For later applications, we need the following notion of an independent set, which ensures that a set does not concentrate on a lower-dimensional plane.

Definition 2.10

(Independent set)
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we say a subset
S\subset B(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
(j,\beta,r)
-independent
in
B(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
if for any affine
(j-1)
-plane
V
, there exists some
y\in S
such that

|y-V|\geq\beta r.

For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, we say
S\subset\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is
(j,\beta,r)
-independent in
\mathfrak{B}(X,r)
if the projection of
S
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\{\mathfrak{t}(X)\}
is
(j,\beta,r)
-independent in
B(\mathfrak{e}(X),r)
.

3
Quantitative uniqueness of cylindrical singularities

In this section, we prove a quantitative uniqueness theorem for tangent flows at a point
X\in\mathcal{M}
. The main ideas and techniques are from
[
CMI15
]
and
[
CMI21
]
, see also
[
CMI25
, Theorem 0.5]
for a similar result.

We begin by considering an open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)})
and a family of graphs
\Omega_{u(t)}
over
\Omega
for
t\in I
. Here,

\Omega_{u(t)}:=\{x+u(x,t)\mathbf{n}(x)\mid x\in\Omega\}.

We assume that
\Omega_{u(t)}
evolves by the rescaled MCF for
t\in I
, which is given by the equation:

(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}.

Next, we state the following lemma from
[
CMI15
, Lemma A.48]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

With above assumptions, there exist positive constants
C
and
\epsilon
, both depending only on
n
, such that if
\rVert u(\cdot,t)\rVert_{C^{1}}<\epsilon,\forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset I
, then

\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t_{1})-u(x,t_{2})|e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dx\leq C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega_{u(t)}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxdt.

Now, we prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.2

(Quantitative uniqueness)
.

For any constant
\epsilon>0
, suppose that
\delta\leq\delta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
,
\tau_{1}\leq\delta^{2}\tau_{2}
and

\Theta(X,\tau_{2})-\Theta(X,\tau_{1})\leq\delta^{2},

(3.1)

for a point
X\in\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
. Then, there exists a
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
and a cylinder
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)})
such that for any
r\in[\epsilon(\delta^{-1}\tau_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}},\epsilon^{-1}(\delta\tau_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}]
,

(\mathcal{M}_{X,r})_{\mathfrak{t}=-1}\bigcap B(\vec{0}^{n+1},\epsilon^{-1})

is a graph over
\Sigma
with
C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm at most
\epsilon
.

In particular, there exists a fixed
j
-plane
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
X
is
(j,\epsilon,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
L_{X}:=(\mathfrak{e}(X)+V)\times\{\mathfrak{t}(X)\}
for any
r\in[(\delta^{-1}\tau_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}},(\delta\tau_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}]
.

Proof.

In the proof, we define
B_{i}:=B(\vec{0}^{n+1},i\epsilon^{-1})
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. We set
X=(x_{0},t_{0})
and consider the corresponding rescaled MCF:

M^{\prime}_{s}:=e^{\frac{s}{2}}(M_{t_{0}-e^{-s}}-x_{0}),\quad s=-\log(t_{0}-t).

In addition, we set
s_{2}=-\log(\delta^{-1}\tau_{1})
and
s_{1}=-\log(\delta\tau_{2})
. By our assumption (
3.1
) and Proposition
2.7
, if
\delta
is sufficiently small, then for any
s\in[s_{1},s_{2}]
, there exists a cylinder
\Sigma_{s}
of the form
\mathbb{R}^{j}\times S^{n-j}(\sqrt{2(n-j)})
such that
M^{\prime}_{z}\bigcap B_{4},
for
z\in[s+4\log\epsilon,s-4\log\epsilon]
can be written as a graph over
\Sigma_{s}
with
C^{4[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm smaller than
\epsilon_{0}
, where
\epsilon_{0}
is a constant much smaller than
\epsilon
, to be determined later. Here, since the entropies of the generalized cylinders are discrete (see (
2.3
)), we may assume that
j
is fixed for all
s\in[s_{1},s_{2}]
.

We assume
s^{\prime}\in[s_{1},s_{2}]
is the largest number such that
F(M^{\prime}_{s})\geq\Theta_{j}
for any
s\in[s_{1},s^{\prime}]
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
s^{\prime}=s_{2}
, as the general case can be handled by considering the intervals
[s_{1},s^{\prime}]
and
[s^{\prime},s_{2}]
separately.

It follows from
[
CMI15
]
that the following discrete Łojasiewicz inequality holds: there exists
\eta\in(1/3,1)
such that for any
s\in[s_{1},s_{2}]
,

(F(M^{\prime}_{s})-\Theta_{j})^{1+\eta}\leq K\left(F(M^{\prime}_{s-1})-F(M^{\prime}_{s+1})\right),

(3.2)

where the constant
K
depends only on
n
and
\Lambda
. Next, we set
G(s):=K^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}(F(M^{\prime}_{s+s_{1}})-\Theta_{j})
for
s\in[0,s_{2}-s_{1}]
and define
C_{0}>0
by
C_{0}^{\eta}=8\eta^{-1}
.

Claim 3.3
.

For any
s\in[0,s_{2}-s_{1}]
, we have

G(s)\leq C_{0}[G(0)^{-\eta}+s]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}.

(3.3)

Proof of Claim
3.3
: We first show that (
3.3
) holds for any
s\in[0,\min\{2,s_{2}-s_{1}\}]
. Since
G(s)
is decreasing, this follows from

G(0)\leq C_{0}[G(0)^{-\eta}+2]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}.

(3.4)

By our assumption (
3.1
) and (
3.2
),
G(0)=\Psi(\delta)
, which ensures that (
3.4
) holds.

Next, we show that if the inequality (
3.3
) holds for
s-2
, then it also holds for
s
. Suppose not; then we would have

\displaystyle G(s-2)\leq C_{0}[G(0)^{-\eta}+s-2]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}\quad\text{and}\quad G(s)\geq C_{0}[G(0)^{-\eta}+s]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}.

(3.5)

By (
3.2
), we have

G(s)^{1+\eta}\leq G(s-1)-G(s+1)

and

G(s)^{1+\eta}\leq G(s-1)^{1+\eta}\leq G(s-2)-G(s).

Therefore,

G(s)(1+G(s)^{\eta})\leq G(s-2).

By (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle[G(0)^{-\eta}+s]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}(1+C_{0}^{\eta}[G(0)^{-\eta}+s]^{-1})\leq[G(0)^{-\eta}+s-2]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}.

After simplification, it is equivalent to

\displaystyle(1+C_{0}^{\eta}x)^{-\eta}\geq 1-2x,

(3.6)

where
x=[G(0)^{-\eta}+s]^{-1}
. Since
x
is sufficiently small, we have
(1+C_{0}^{\eta}x)^{-\eta}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}\eta C_{0}^{\eta}x=1-4x
by our definition of
C_{0}
. This contradicts (
3.6
), and the proof of the claim is complete.

It follows from Proposition
2.2
that for any
s\in[0,s_{2}-s_{1}]
,

\int_{s}^{s_{2}-s_{1}}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s_{1}+z}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxdz\leq C[G(0)^{-\eta}+s]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}.

Therefore, one can apply the same argument as in
[
CMI21
, Lemma 2.39]
to conclude that for
\gamma\in(1,\eta^{-1})
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\Lambda,\gamma,\eta)
such that

\int_{1}^{s_{2}-s_{1}}z^{\gamma}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s_{1}+z}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxdz\leq CG(0)^{1-\gamma\eta}.

(3.7)

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{s_{2}-s_{1}}z^{\gamma}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s_{1}+z}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxdz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j}}^{\min\{s_{2}-s_{1},2^{j+1}\}}z^{\gamma}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s_{1}+z}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxdz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j}}^{\min\{s_{2}-s_{1},2^{j+1}\}}2^{\gamma(j+1)}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s_{1}+z}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxdz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\gamma(j+1)}[G(0)^{-\eta}+2^{j}]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}\leq C[1/2+G(0)^{-\eta}]^{\gamma-\eta^{-1}}\leq CG(0)^{1-\eta\gamma}.

Here, the forth inequality holds since (see
[
CMI21
, Lemma 2.37]
):

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\gamma(j+1)}[G(0)^{-\eta}+2^{j}]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\min_{r\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}]}r^{\gamma}[G(0)^{-\eta}+r]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{1-j}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}r^{\gamma}[G(0)^{-\eta}+r]^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 4^{\gamma}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}[G(0)^{-\eta}+r]^{\gamma-1-\frac{1}{\eta}}dr\leq C[G(0)^{-\eta}+1/2]^{\gamma-\frac{1}{\eta}}.

Now, we assume
\bar{s}
is the largest number in
[s_{1},s_{2}]
such that for any
s\in[s_{1}+2\log\epsilon,\bar{s}-2\log\epsilon]
,
M^{\prime}_{s}\bigcap B_{1}
can be written as a graph
u
over
\Sigma_{s_{1}}
with
C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm at most
\epsilon
.

If
\bar{s}<s_{2}
, we apply Lemma
3.1
to get that for any
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[s_{1},\bar{s}]
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s_{1}}\cap B_{1}}|u(x,t_{1})-u(x,t_{2})|e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s}}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(s-s_{1})^{-\gamma}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxds\right)\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s}}(s-s_{1})^{\gamma}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CF(M^{\prime}_{s_{1}})\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M^{\prime}_{s}}(s-s_{1})^{\gamma}\left|\mathbf{H}+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\,dxds\leq CG(0)^{1-\eta\gamma},

(3.8)

where we have used (
3.7
) for the last inequality.

Since
M^{\prime}_{s_{1}}\bigcap B_{4}
is a graph over
\Sigma_{s_{1}}
with
C^{4[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm bounded by
\epsilon_{0}
, it follows from (
3.8
) and interpolation (cf.
[
CMI15
, Lemma B.1]
) that if
\delta
is sufficiently small,
M^{\prime}_{\bar{s}}\bigcap B_{0.5}
is also a graph over
\Sigma_{s_{1}}
with
C^{3[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm bounded by
2\epsilon_{0}
. Additionally,
M^{\prime}_{\bar{s}}\bigcap B_{4}
is a graph over
\Sigma_{\bar{s}}
with
C^{4[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm bounded by
\epsilon_{0}
. Consequently, the Hausdorff distance between
\Sigma_{s_{1}}
and
\Sigma_{\bar{s}}
in
B_{2}
is at most
C_{n}\epsilon_{0}
. Thus, for any
s\in[\bar{s}+4\log\epsilon,\bar{s}-4\log\epsilon]
,
M^{\prime}_{s}\bigcap B_{1}
is a graph over
\Sigma_{s_{1}}
with
C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm bounded by
C_{n}\epsilon_{0}
. Since
\epsilon_{0}
is much smaller than
\epsilon
, this contradicts the definition of
\bar{s}
, leading us to conclude that
\bar{s}=s_{2}
.

In conclusion, we have shown that
M^{\prime}_{s}\bigcap B_{1}
for
s\in[s_{1}+2\log\epsilon,s_{2}-2\log\epsilon]
can be written as a graph over
\Sigma_{s_{1}}
with
C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}
-norm bounded by
\epsilon
. Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.

∎

4
Neck decomposition theorem

In this section, we consider a level set flow
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
. To proceed, we first introduce the following concept of a neck region in our setting, which is similar to those in
[
JN21
]
and
[
CJN21
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Neck region)
.

Fix
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\},\delta>0,r>0
and a universal constant
c_{n}=100^{-10n}
. Given
X\in\mathcal{M}
with
\mathfrak{t}(X)>2\delta^{-1}r^{2}
, we call a subset
\mathscr{N}\subset\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)
a
(j,\delta,r)
-neck region
if
\mathscr{N}=\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{Y}}(\mathscr{C})
, where
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)\bigcap\mathcal{M}
is a closed subset with
r_{Y}:\mathscr{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, satisfies:

(n1)

\{\mathfrak{B}(Y,c_{n}^{3}r_{Y})\}_{Y\in\mathscr{C}}
are pairwisely disjoint;

(n2)
for all
Y\in\mathscr{C}
,

\Theta(Y,\delta^{-1}r^{2})-\Theta(Y,\delta r_{Y}^{2})<\delta^{2};

(n3)
for any
Y\in\mathscr{C}
, there exists a
j
-plane
V_{Y}
such that for all
r_{Y}\leq s\leq c_{n}^{-3}r
,
Y
is
(j,\delta,s)
-cylindrical with respect to
L_{Y}=(V_{Y}+\mathfrak{e}(Y))\times\{\mathfrak{t}(Y)\}
(cf. Definition
2.6
);

(n4)
for all
Y\in\mathscr{C}
and
s\geq r_{Y}
with
\mathfrak{B}(Y,2s)\subset\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)
, we have

L_{Y}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)\subset\mathfrak{B}_{c_{n}s}(\mathscr{C})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)\subset\mathfrak{B}_{c_{n}s}(L_{Y}).

Here,
\mathscr{C}
is called the
center of the neck region
, and
r_{Y}
is referred to as the
radius function
. We decompose
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}_{+}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{0}
, where
r_{Y}>0
on
\mathscr{C}_{+}
and
r_{Y}=0
on
\mathscr{C}_{0}
. In addition, we use the notation

\mathfrak{B}_{r_{Y}}(\mathscr{C}):=\mathscr{C}_{0}\bigcup\bigcup_{Y\in\mathscr{C}_{+}}\mathfrak{B}(Y,r_{Y}).

The following lemma follows directly from the Definition
4.1
(n1).

Lemma 4.2
.

The radius function
r_{Y}
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant at most
c_{n}^{-3}
.

Next, we show that all points in the center have nearly identical time components, and their corresponding affine planes are close.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, let
\mathscr{N}=\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{Y}}(\mathscr{C})
be a
(j,\delta,r)
-neck region with
\delta\leq\delta(n,\epsilon)
. Then, for any
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathscr{C}
with
s=\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|
,

|\mathfrak{t}(Y_{1})-\mathfrak{t}(Y_{2})|\leq\epsilon^{2}s^{2}

(4.1)

and

d_{H}\left((V_{Y_{1}}+\mathfrak{e}(Y_{1}))\bigcap B(\mathfrak{e}(Y_{1}),10s),(V_{Y_{2}}+\mathfrak{e}(Y_{2}))\bigcap B(\mathfrak{e}(Y_{1}),10s)\right)\leq\epsilon s,

(4.2)

where
d_{H}
denotes the Hausdorff distance in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{1},c_{n}^{3}r_{Y_{1}})
and
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{2},c_{n}^{3}r_{Y_{2}})
are disjoint, we have
s\geq c_{n}^{3}\max\{r_{Y_{1}},r_{Y_{2}}\}
. By the definition of the neck region, both
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are
(j,\delta,c_{n}^{-3}s)
-cylindrical with respect to
L_{Y_{1}}
and
L_{Y_{2}}
, respectively. Consequently, it follows from Definition
2.6
that if
\delta
is sufficiently small, the parabolic Hausdorff distance between
L_{Y_{1}}
and
L_{Y_{2}}
in
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{1},10\epsilon)
is smaller than
\epsilon s
. In particular,
|\mathfrak{t}(Y_{1})-\mathfrak{t}(Y_{2})|\leq\epsilon^{2}s^{2}
and that the Hausdorff distance between
(V_{Y_{1}}+\mathfrak{e}(Y_{1}))
and
(V_{Y_{2}}+\mathfrak{e}(Y_{2}))
in the ball
B(\mathfrak{e}(Y_{1}),10s)
is less than
\epsilon s
.
∎

Using Lemma
4.3
, we derive the following structural description of a neck region.

Theorem 4.4

(Neck structure)
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, let
\mathscr{N}=\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{Y}}(\mathscr{C})
be a
(j,\delta,r)
-neck region with
\delta\leq\delta(n,\epsilon)
. Then, the following conclusion holds.

For a fixed
Y_{0}\in\mathscr{C}
, let
V=V_{Y_{0}}
and let
\pi:\mathscr{C}\to V
be the projection onto
V
. Then, for any
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathscr{C}
,

(1-\epsilon)\rVert Y_{1}-Y_{2}\rVert\leq|\pi(Y_{1})-\pi(Y_{2})|\leq\rVert Y_{1}-Y_{2}\rVert.

(4.3)

In particular,
\mathscr{C}
is a Lipschitz graph over
V
.

Proof.

The second inequality in (
4.3
) is straightforward, so we only need to prove the first. Without loss of generality, we assume
r=1
.

For any
Y_{1}=(y_{1},t_{1}),Y_{2}=(y_{2},t_{2})\in\mathscr{C}
, set
s:=\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|
,
V_{1}:=V_{Y_{1}}
and
V_{2}:=V_{Y_{2}}
. First, by (
4.1
), we have
|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq\epsilon^{2}s^{2}
, which implies
|y_{1}-y_{2}|=s
, if
\delta
is sufficiently small. Additionally, from (
4.2
), we obtain

d_{H}\left((y_{1}+V_{1})\bigcap B(y_{1},10s),(y_{2}+V_{2}))\bigcap B(y_{1},10s)\right)\leq\epsilon s,

and consequently,

\displaystyle|\pi_{1}(Y_{1})-\pi_{1}(Y_{2})|\geq\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}s,

(4.4)

where
\pi_{1}
is the projection onto
V_{1}
. Moreover, by (
4.2
) again, we have

\displaystyle d_{H}\left(V_{1}\bigcap B(\vec{0}^{n+1},1),V\bigcap B(\vec{0}^{n+1},1)\right)\leq\epsilon.

(4.5)

Combining (
4.4
) with (
4.5
), we immediately conclude that

\displaystyle|\pi(Y_{1})-\pi(Y_{2})|\geq(1-2\epsilon)s.

∎

For later applications, we define the
packing measure
associated with the neck region
\mathscr{N}=\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{Y}}(\mathscr{C})
by

\mu:=\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{C}_{+}}r_{Y}^{j}\delta_{Y}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}\llcorner\mathscr{C}_{0}.

(4.6)

Next, we establish the following Ahlfors regularity property for the packing measure.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
Y\in\mathscr{C},s\geq r_{Y}
with
\mathfrak{B}(Y,2s)\subset\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)
, we have

C(n)^{-1}s^{j}\leq\mu(\mathfrak{B}(Y,s))\leq C(n)s^{j}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Let
V=V_{Y}
and denote the projection onto
V
by
\pi
.

We first prove

\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{C}_{+}}r_{Y}^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\mathscr{C}_{0})\leq C(n)r^{j}.

(4.8)

Since, by our definition, the sets
\{\mathfrak{B}(Y,c_{n}^{3}r_{Y})\}
are disjoint, it follows from (
4.3
) that
\{B(\pi(Y),c_{n}^{3}r_{Y}/2)\}
are disjoint in
V
. Consequently, we have

\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{C}_{+}}r_{Y}^{j}\leq C(n)\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{C}_{+}}|B(\pi(Y),c_{n}^{3}r_{Y}/2)|_{V}\leq C(n)r^{j},

(4.9)

where
|\cdot|_{V}
denotes the volume on the plane
V
.

Additionally, it follows from (
4.3
) that

\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\mathscr{C}_{0})\leq C(n)\mathscr{H}^{j}(\pi(\mathscr{C}_{0}))\leq C(n)r^{j}.

(4.10)

Combining (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we immediately conclude that (
4.8
) holds.

An important observation here is that
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}=\mathscr{N}\cap\mathfrak{B}(Y,2s)
is also a
(j,\delta,s)
-neck region with center
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}\cap\mathfrak{B}(Y,2s)
and the same radius function. Therefore, by (
4.8
), the upper bound in (
4.7
) holds.

To establish the lower bound of (
4.8
), we consider the containment:

V\bigcap B(\pi(Y),s/2)\subset\bigcup_{Z\in\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)}V\bigcap\bar{B}(\pi(Z),r_{Z}),

(4.11)

where
\bar{B}(\pi(Z),r_{Z})=\pi(Z)
if
Z\in\mathscr{C}_{0}
. By (
4.3
) and (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle C(n)^{-1}s^{j}\leq|V\bigcap\bar{B}(\pi(Y),s/2)|\leq\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{C}_{+}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)}C(n)r_{Z}^{j}+C(n)\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}\left(\mathscr{C}_{0}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)\right)\leq C(n)\mu(\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)).

If (
4.11
) were to fail, there would exist
w\in V\bigcap B(\pi(Y),s/2)
not covered by the union. Define
s_{Z}=|w-\pi(Z)|
for any
Z\in\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)
and set
\bar{s}=\inf_{Z\in\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)}s_{Z}>0
. Assume
\bar{s}=s_{W}>r_{W}
for some
W\in\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y,s)
. Then, by Lemma
4.3
, there exists
W^{\prime}\in L_{W}
such that
|\pi(W^{\prime})-w|\leq c_{n}\bar{s}
. Further, by Definition
4.1
(n4),

\displaystyle L_{W}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(W,2\bar{s})\subset\bigcup_{Z\in\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(W,2\bar{s})}\mathfrak{B}(Z,2c_{n}\bar{s}).

Thus, there exists
W^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{C}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(W,2\bar{s})
such that
\|W^{\prime\prime}-W^{\prime}\|\leq 2c_{n}\bar{s}
. This implies

\displaystyle|w-\pi(W^{\prime\prime})|\leq|w-\pi(W^{\prime})|+\|W^{\prime}-W^{\prime\prime}\|\leq 3c_{n}\bar{s}<\bar{s},

which contradicts the definition of
W
, thereby confirming that (
4.11
) holds.

In conclusion, the proof is complete.
∎

For later applications, we show that any point in the neck region is sufficiently regular.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\chi=\chi(n,\epsilon)>0
such that if
\mathscr{N}=\mathfrak{B}(X,2r)\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{Y}}(\mathscr{C})
is a
(j,\delta,r)
-neck region with
\delta\leq\delta(n,\epsilon)
, then any
Y\in\mathscr{N}\bigcap\mathcal{M}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,r)
is
(n,\epsilon,s)
-cylindrical, provided that
s\leq\chi\|Y-\mathscr{C}\|
.

Proof.

Set
d:=\|Y-\mathscr{C}\|=\rVert Y-Y_{1}\rVert
, where
Y_{1}\in\mathscr{C}
. Then
d\geq r_{Y_{1}}
, and
Y_{1}
is
(j,\delta,d)
-cylindrical. If
\delta
is suffciently small, there exists
\epsilon_{n}>0
depending only on
n
such that
|\mathfrak{t}(Y)-\mathfrak{t}(Y_{1})|\geq\epsilon^{2}_{n}d^{2}
. Indeed, if
|\mathfrak{t}(Y)-\mathfrak{t}(Y_{1})|\leq\epsilon^{2}_{n}d^{2}
, then
\|Y-L_{Y_{1}}\|\leq d/100
. However, by Definition
4.1
(n4), there exists
Y_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\rVert Y-Y_{1}^{\prime}\rVert\leq d/100
, contradicting the assumption that
d=\|Y-\mathscr{C}\|
.

Furthermore, by the clearing-out lemma (see
[
Bra78
, Lemma 6.3]

[
Eck04
, Proposition E.5]
or
[
CMI16b
, Theorem 6.1]
), we have
\mathfrak{t}(Y)\leq\mathfrak{t}(Y_{1})-\epsilon^{2}_{n}d^{2}
, provided
\delta
is small. This implies that
Y
is
(n,\epsilon,\chi d)
-cylindrical, if
\delta
and
\chi
are sufficiently small.
∎

Next, we state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.7

(Neck decomposition theorem)
.

For any
\eta>0
,
r>0
and
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, suppose that
\delta\leq\delta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\eta)
and
X\in\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
with
\mathfrak{t}(X)>4\zeta^{-2}r^{2}
, for some
\zeta=\zeta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta,\eta)
. Then, we have the following decomposition

\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\bigcap\mathcal{M}\subset\bigcup_{a}\left((\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{0,a})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\right)\bigcup_{b}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\bigcup S_{0}

with the following properties:

(a)

For all
a
,
\mathscr{N}_{a}=\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},2r_{a})\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{a,Y}}(\mathscr{C}_{a})
is a
(j,\delta,r_{a})
-neck region.

(b)

For all
b
, there exists point in
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},4r_{b})\bigcap\mathcal{M}
which is
(j^{\prime},\eta,r_{b})
-cylindrical for some
j^{\prime}>j
.

(c)

The following content estimate holds:

\sum_{a}r_{a}^{j}+\sum_{b}r_{b}^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a})\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta,\eta)r^{j}.

(d)

S_{0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
. In particular,
\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(S_{0})=0
.

(e)

If
\eta\leq\eta(n,\epsilon)
and
\delta\leq\delta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
, then

\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\bigcap\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}\subset S_{0}\bigcup\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a}.

Assuming Theorem
4.7
, we derive the following structural description of the singular stratum:

Corollary 4.8
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
4.7
,

\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\bigcap(\mathcal{S}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{j-1})

is contained in countably many compact
j
-dimensional Lipschitz graphs.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
. By Theorem
4.7
parts (d) and (e), we have

\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\bigcap(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon_{k}}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{j-1})\subset\bigcup_{a_{k}}\mathscr{C}_{0,a_{k}},

where each
\mathscr{C}_{0,a_{k}}
is part of the center of a
(j,\delta_{k},r_{a_{k}})
-neck region. Since
\mathcal{S}^{j}=\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon_{k}}
, it follows that

\mathfrak{B}(X,r)\bigcap(\mathcal{S}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{j-1})\subset\bigcup_{k}\bigcup_{a_{k}}\mathscr{C}_{0,a_{k}}.

From Theorem
4.4
, each
\mathscr{C}_{0,a_{k}}
is a compact
j
-dimensional Lipschitz graph. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that for any
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{E}(n,\alpha,\Lambda)
, we have
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathfrak{B}(\vec{0}^{n+1,1},r)
for a sufficiently large
r
depending on the initial hypersurface. Consequently, Theorem
1.5
follows directly from Corollary
4.8
by a covering argument. The only additional point is that
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{n-2}
is compact.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
4.7
, let us provide an example to illustrate the theorem.

Example 4.9
.

We consider the standard shrinking cylinders
\mathcal{M}=\{\mathbb{R}^{k}\times S^{n-k}(\sqrt{2(n-k)|t|})\}_{t\leq 0}
with

. The neck decomposition of
\mathfrak{B}(X,1)
can be obtained as follows with three different cases.

Case

: The decomposition consists of a single parabolic ball
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})=\mathfrak{B}(X,1)
. In particular,
X
is
(k,\eta,r_{b})
-cylindrical for any
\eta>0
.

2.

Case

\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})=\mathfrak{B}(X,1),\mathscr{N}_{a}=\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},2r_{a})\setminus\mathscr{C}_{0}
with

. In particular,
\mathscr{N}_{a}
is a
(j,\delta,r_{a})
-neck region for any
\delta>0
.

3.

Case

: We set
S_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\{\vec{0}^{n+1-k}\}\times\{0\}
. For a fixed
\eta>0
and for each
r_{b}=2^{-b}
, we choose a cover
\{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{r_{b},l},\sigma r_{b})\}_{1\leq l\leq N_{r_{b}}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\bigcap\mathcal{M}\subset\left(\bigcup_{0<r_{b}=2^{-b}\leq 1}\bigcup_{l=1}^{N_{r_{b}}}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{r_{b},l},\sigma r_{b})\right)\bigcup S_{0}.

Additionally, the content estimate holds since

\displaystyle\sum_{0<r_{b}=2^{-b}\leq 1}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{r_{b}}}r_{b}^{j}\leq C(n,\eta)\sum_{0<r_{b}=2^{-b}\leq 1}r_{b}^{-k}r_{b}^{j}=C(n,\eta)\sum_{0\leq b<\infty}2^{-b(j-k)}=C(n,\eta).

The remainder of this section is devoted to proving Theorem
4.7
. Without loss of generality, we assume
r=1
. Throughout, we will use the following notation:

Fix

(Decomposition of

\{\mathfrak{B}(Y^{1}_{e^{\prime\prime}},r_{e^{\prime\prime}}^{1})\}
with
Y^{1}_{e^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathfrak{B}(Y^{1}_{e^{\prime}},r_{e^{\prime}}^{1})\cap\mathcal{M},r_{e^{\prime\prime}}^{1}=\zeta r_{e^{\prime}}^{1}
such that
\mathfrak{B}(Y^{1}_{e^{\prime\prime}},r_{e^{\prime\prime}}^{1}/5)
are disjoint. By (
4.19
), we have

\bar{V}_{Y^{1}_{e^{\prime\prime}},r_{e^{\prime\prime}}^{1}}(1)=\bar{V}_{Y^{1}_{e^{\prime}},r_{e^{\prime}}^{1}}(\zeta^{-1})\leq\bar{V}_{X,1}(1)-\zeta/2.

(4.20)

Combining with the decomposition (
4.18
), we can obtain:

\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\bigcap\mathcal{M}\subset\bigcup_{a}\left((\mathscr{N}_{a}^{1}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{0,a}^{1})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a}^{1},r_{a}^{1})\right)\bigcup\bigcup_{b}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b}^{1},r_{b}^{1})\bigcup\bigcup_{e}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{e}^{1},r_{e}^{1})\bigcup S_{0}^{1},

with
r_{a}^{1},r_{b}^{1},r_{e}^{1}\leq\zeta
,
S_{0}^{1}\subset\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
and the content estimate:

\sum_{a}(r_{a}^{1})^{j}+\sum_{b}(r_{b}^{1})^{j}+\sum_{e}(r_{e}^{1})^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a}^{1})\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta,\eta).

Moreover, for each
e
-ball
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{e}^{1},r_{e}^{1})
, it follows from (
4.20
) that

\bar{V}_{Y^{1}_{e},r_{e}^{1}}(1)\leq\bar{V}_{X,1}(1)-\zeta/2.

Applying Proposition
4.14
to
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{e}^{1},r_{e}^{1})
and repeating the above process, we obtain the following decomposition

\mathfrak{B}(Y_{e}^{1},r_{e}^{1})\bigcap\mathcal{M}\subset\bigcup_{a}\left((\mathscr{N}_{a}^{2}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{0,a}^{2})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a}^{2},r_{a}^{2})\right)\bigcup\bigcup_{b}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b}^{2},r_{b}^{2})\bigcup\bigcup_{e}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{e}^{2},r_{e}^{2})\bigcup S_{0}^{2}

with
S_{0}^{2}\subset\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
and the content estimate:

\sum_{a}(r_{a}^{2})^{j}+\sum_{b}(r_{b}^{2})^{j}+\sum_{e}(r_{e}^{2})^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a}^{2})\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta,\eta)(r_{e}^{1})^{j}.

Moreover, we have
\bar{V}_{Y_{e}^{2},r_{e}^{2}}(1)\leq\bar{V}_{Y_{e}^{1},r_{e}^{1}}(1)-\zeta/2\leq\bar{V}_{X,1}(1)-\zeta
.

We can now apply Proposition
4.14
again to
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{e}^{2},r_{e}^{2})
to continue the decomposition. At each step, the corresponding quantity
\bar{V}_{\ast,\ast}(1)
decreases strictly by
\zeta/2
. Thus, the process must terminate in
\frac{2\Lambda}{\zeta}+1
steps, meaning that, after the last decomposition, no
e
-balls remain. We denote

S_{0}:=\bigcup_{i}S_{0}^{i}.

After reindexing the balls, we obtain the following decomposition:

\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\bigcap\mathcal{M}\subset\bigcup_{a}\left((\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{0,a})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\right)\bigcup_{b}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\bigcup S_{0}

(4.21)

S_{0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{j-1}
and the content estimate:

\sum_{a}r_{a}^{j}+\sum_{b}r_{b}^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a})\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta,\eta);

Combined with Theorem
4.4
, we have shown that (a), (b), (c), and (d) in Theorem
4.7
hold.

With (
4.21
), to prove (e), it suffices to show that if
Y\in\mathcal{M}\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}
or
Y\in\mathcal{M}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})
, then
Y\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
, provided that
\eta
and
\delta
are sufficiently small.

Case 1
: Suppose
Y\in\mathcal{M}\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}
and set
r=\|Y-\mathscr{C}_{a}\|
. It follows from Lemma
4.6
that
Y
is
(n,\epsilon,\chi r)
-cylindrical for
\chi=\chi(n,\epsilon)
, and hence
Y\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
if
\delta\leq\delta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
.

Case 2
: If
Y\in\mathcal{M}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})
, by the definition of
b
-balls, there exists
Y_{b}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},4r_{b})
such that
Y^{\prime}_{b}
is
(j^{\prime},\eta,r_{b})
-cylindrical for
j^{\prime}>j
. For any
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists
\eta\leq\eta(n,\epsilon^{\prime})
such that if
\|Y-L_{Y_{b}^{\prime}}\|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}r_{b}
, then
Y
is
(j^{\prime},\Psi(\epsilon^{\prime}),r_{b})
-cylindrical, which implies
Y\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
if
\epsilon^{\prime}
is sufficiently small. Alternatively, if
\|Y-L_{Y_{b}^{\prime}}\|\geq\epsilon^{\prime}r_{b}
, we conclude that
Y
is
(n,\Psi(\eta),\Psi(\eta)\epsilon^{\prime}r_{b})
-cylindrical, which also means
Y\notin\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon}
.

In sum, we have completed the proof of Theorem
4.7
.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

We first fix all constants. Given
\epsilon>0
,
\alpha>0
and
\Lambda>0
, we choose small positive constants
\eta=\eta(n,\epsilon)
,
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
,
\zeta=\zeta(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
and
\chi=\chi(n,\epsilon)
such that the following properties hold:

(i)

If
\mathscr{N}_{a}=\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},2r_{a})\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{r_{a,Y}}(\mathscr{C}_{a})
is a
(j,\delta,r_{a})
-neck region, then any
Y\in\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathcal{M}
is
(n,\epsilon,s)
-cylindrical for any
s\leq\chi\|Y-\mathscr{C}_{a}\|
.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})
is a
b
-ball, then any
Y\in\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},2r_{b})\bigcap\mathcal{M}
is
(j^{\prime},\epsilon,\chi r_{b})
-cylindrical for some
j^{\prime}>j
.

(iii)

Theorem
4.7
holds for the constants
\eta
,
\delta
and
\zeta
.

Indeed, (i) above follows from Lemma
4.6
, and (ii) can be deduced similarly to Case 2 in the proof of Theorem
4.7
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\mathfrak{t}(X)-16\zeta^{-2}>0
; otherwise, we can cover
\mathfrak{B}(X,1)
by
\{\mathfrak{B}(X_{i},\zeta/4)\}
and prove the conclusion for each ball individually. By Theorem
4.7
, we have

\mathfrak{B}(X,2)\subset\bigcup_{a}\left((\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{0,a})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\right)\bigcup_{b}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\bigcup S_{0}

(5.1)

with the content estimate:

\sum_{a}r_{a}^{j}+\sum_{b}r_{b}^{j}+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a})\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon).

(5.2)

Since
r<1
, it is clear that
\mathfrak{B}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\subset\mathfrak{B}_{r}\left(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,2)\right)
. Based on the decomposition (
5.1
), we divide the control of the volume into two steps.

Step 1
: For any
Y\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})
, it follows from (i) above that
\|Y-\mathscr{C}_{a}\|<2\chi^{-1}r
; otherwise,
Y
would be
(n,\epsilon,2r)
-cylindrical, which contradicts
Y\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}
.

If
r_{a}\geq 2\chi^{-1}r
, we set
W=\{Z\in\mathscr{C}_{a}\mid\|Y-Z\|=\|Y-\mathscr{C}_{a}\|\text{ for some }Y\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\}
. Define
s=10\chi^{-1}r
. Then it follows from Vitali’s covering theorem that there exist
Z_{i}\in W
for
1\leq i\leq K_{a}
such that
\{\mathfrak{B}(Z_{i},s)\}_{1\leq i\leq K_{a}}
covers
W
and
\{\mathfrak{B}(Z_{i},s/5)\}_{1\leq i\leq K_{a}}
are pairwise disjoint.

Denote the corresponding packing measure on
\mathscr{N}_{a}
by
\mu_{a}
(cf. (
4.6
)). Then, by Proposition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle K_{a}C^{-1}(n)s^{j}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K_{a}}\mu_{a}(\mathfrak{B}(Z_{i},s/5))\leq\mu_{a}(\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},2r_{a}))\leq C(n)r_{a}^{j}.

Note that the assumptions of Proposition
4.5
are satisfied, as
r_{a,Z_{i}}\leq s/5\leq r_{a}
. Thus, we conclude that
K_{a}\leq C(n,\epsilon)r_{a}^{j}r^{-j}
. In addition, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\subset\bigcup_{i}\mathfrak{B}(Z_{i},2s)

and hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}\left(\mathfrak{B}_{r}\left(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\right)\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K_{a}}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathfrak{B}(Z_{i},3s))\leq C(n,\epsilon)K_{a}r^{n+3}\leq C(n,\epsilon)r_{a}^{j}r^{n+3-j}.

Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{B}_{r}\left(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\bigcap\bigcup_{r_{a}\geq 2\chi^{-1}r}(\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a}))\right).

In sum, we obtain

\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq C(n,\epsilon)\sum_{a}r_{a}^{j}r^{n+3-j}\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j},

(5.3)

where we have used (
5.2
) for the final inequality.

Step 2
: We take a cover
\{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},r)\}_{1\leq i\leq K}
of
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
such that
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
\{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},r/2)\}
are pairwise disjoint. By our definition, if
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},r/2)\bigcap\mathscr{N}_{a}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\neq\emptyset
, then
r_{a}\leq 2\chi^{-1}r
and hence
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},r/2)\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\neq\emptyset
, then we must have
r_{b}\leq 2\chi^{-1}r
; otherwise,
Y_{i}\in\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},2r_{b})
, and by (ii) above,
Y_{i}
would be
(j^{\prime},\epsilon,\chi r_{b})
-cylindrical for some
j^{\prime}>j
, which contradicts
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}
. Therefore, we also have
\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)
.

Thus, it follows from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},r/2)\subset

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\bigcup\bigcup_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\bigcup(S_{0}\bigcup\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a}).

By comparing volumes, we obtain

\displaystyle C(n)^{-1}r^{n+3}\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},r/2))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a}))+\sum_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b}))

\displaystyle+\mathscr{H}_{P}^{n+3}(S_{0}\bigcup\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n)\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}r_{a}^{n+3}+\sum_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}r_{b}^{n+3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{a},r_{a})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}r_{a}^{j}+\sum_{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{b},r_{b})\subset\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},3\chi^{-1}r)}r_{b}^{j}\right),

where we have used
r_{a}\leq 2\chi^{-1}r,r_{b}\leq 2\chi^{-1}r
and
\mathscr{H}_{P}^{n+3}(S_{0}\bigcup\bigcup_{a}\mathscr{C}_{0,a})=0
.

Taking summation over
i
and using (
5.2
), we get

\displaystyle KC(n)^{-1}r^{n+3}\leq C(n,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j}\left(\sum_{a}r_{a}^{j}+\sum_{b}r_{b}^{j}\right)\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j}.

and hence
K\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)r^{-j}
. Since all
\{\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},2r)\}
cover
\mathfrak{B}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathfrak{B}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathfrak{B}(Y_{i},2r))\leq C(n)Kr^{n+3}\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j}.

(5.4)

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we obtain that for any
r\in(0,1)
,

\mathrm{Vol}\left(\mathfrak{B}_{r}(\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon,r})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\right)\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j}

(5.5)

Furthermore, since
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}^{j}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon,r}^{j}
, it follows from (
5.5
) that

\mathrm{Vol}\left(\mathfrak{B}_{r}(\mathcal{S}^{j}_{\epsilon})\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1)\right)\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)r^{n+3-j}

for any
r\in(0,1)
. From this, it is evident that

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{P}^{j}(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}^{j}\bigcap\mathfrak{B}(X,1))\leq C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon).

In summary, this completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

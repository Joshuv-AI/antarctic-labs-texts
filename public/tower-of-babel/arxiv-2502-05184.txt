Abstract nonautonomous difference inclusions in locally convex spaces
Marko Kostić

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider abstract nonautonomous difference inclusions in locally convex spaces with integer order differences. We particularly analyze the existence and uniqueness of almost periodic type solutions to abstract nonautonomous difference inclusions. Our results seem to be completely new even in the Banach space setting.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|)
is a complex Banach space.
Then we say that
a
Y
-valued sequence
(x(k))_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

is (Bohr)
almost periodic if, for every
\epsilon>0,
there exists
l>0
such that for each
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists
{\bf\tau}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[t,t+l]
such that

\displaystyle\bigl\|x(k+{\bf\tau})-x(k)\bigr\|\leq\epsilon,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Any almost periodic
Y
-valued sequence is bounded and its range is relatively compact in
Y.

We know that a sequence
(x(k))_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
in
Y
is almost periodic if and only if there exists an almost periodic function
F:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow Y

such that
x(k)=F(k)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}};
for more details about almost periodic functions, almost periodic sequences and their applications, we refer to research monographs
[
8
,
14
,
17
,
19
,
23
,
32
]
.

The abstract difference equations with integer order differences
have been considered in the research monograph
[
13
]
by M. I. Gil, where all considered operator coefficients are bounded linear operators between normed spaces. In our recent research monograph
[
20
]
, we have investigated the existence and uniqueness of almost periodic type solutions for various classes of abstract difference inclusions in Banach spaces with multivalued linear operators as operator coefficients.

On the other hand, the almost periodic sequences with values in locally convex spaces and the abstract difference equations in locally convex spaces
have not received so much attention of the authors by now; cf.
[
11
,

,

,

,

]
for some results obtained in this direction.
In
[

The main aim of this paper is to continue the research study raised in

and the research studies
[

-

\displaystyle x(k+1)=A(k)x(k)+b(k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where

Notation and preliminaries.
If
Y
is a Hausdorff locally convex space over the field of complex numbers, then
the abbreviation
\circledast_{Y}
stands for the fundamental system of seminorms which defines the topology of
Y
;
{\rm I}
denotes the identity operator on
Y
. If
Y
is sequentially complete, then we simply write that
Y
is an SCLCS. If
X
is also a Hausdorff locally convex space over the field
{\mathbb{C}}
,
then
L(X,Y)
denotes the space consisting of all continuous linear mappings from
X
into
Y
;
L(X)\equiv L(X,X)
. If
Y_{1},\ ...,\ Y_{k}
are locally convex spaces, then
Y_{1}\times...\times Y_{k}
is a locally convex space and the fundamental system of seminorms which defines the topology on
Y_{1}\times...\times Y_{k}
is given by
\kappa(y_{1},...,y_{k}):=\kappa_{1}(y_{1})+...+\kappa_{k}(y_{k})
,
y_{1}\in Y_{1},...,

y_{k}\in Y_{k}
, where
\kappa_{j}(\cdot)
runs through the set
\circledast_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq k.

For more details about the multivalued linear operators (MLOs) in locally convex spaces, we refer the reader to
[
18
]
; we will use the same notion and notation as in this monograph.
By a trigonometric polynomial
P:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow Y
we mean any linear combination of functions like

e^{i\lambda t}y,

where
\lambda
is a real number and
y\in Y
. By

\Gamma(\cdot)
we denote the Euler Gamma function; finally, if

\gamma>0,
then we define
g_{\gamma}(t):=t^{\gamma-1}/\Gamma(\gamma),

t>0.

1.1.
Generalized almost periodic type sequences in locally convex spaces

In the sequel, we will always assume that
Y
is an SCLCS.
If
F:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
is a given sequence,
\omega\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\},
then we say that
F(\cdot)
is
(\omega,c)
-periodic if
F(k+\omega)=cF(k)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}};
cf.
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
and the references cited therein for more details about
(\omega,c)
-(almost) periodic functions and their applications given in the Banach space setting.
We need the following notion, as well (cf.
[
22
]
for more details):

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
p>0
and
F:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
is a given sequence. Then we say that:

(i)

F(\cdot)
is (Bohr) almost periodic if, for every
\epsilon>0
and
\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
, there exists a finite real number

l>0
such that for each
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists
\tau\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[t,t+l]
such that

\displaystyle\kappa\bigl(F(k+{\bf\tau})-F(k)\bigr)\leq\epsilon,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}};

(ii)

F(\cdot)
belongs to the class
B-e-W_{ap}^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}:Y)
if, for every
\epsilon>0
and
\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
, there exist a trigonometric polynomial
P:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
and an integer
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle l^{-1}\sum_{j=s}^{s+l}\Bigl[\kappa\bigl(F(j)-P(j)\bigr)\Bigr]^{p}\leq\epsilon,\quad s\in{\mathbb{Z}};

(iii)

F(\cdot)
is Besicovitch-
p
-almost periodic if, for every
\epsilon>0
and
\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
, there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\Biggl\{l^{-1}\cdot\sum_{j=-l}^{l}\Bigl[\kappa\bigl(F(j)-P(j)\bigr)\Bigr]^{p}\Biggr\}<\epsilon.

It can be simply proved that, in parts (ii) and (iii), we can use a Bohr almost periodic function
H:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
in place of the trigonometric polynomial
P:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
. Using the discrete Hölder inequality and a simple argumentation (cf. also the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
), we can simply show that a bounded sequence
F:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
belongs to the class
B-e-W_{ap}^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}:Y)
[is Besicovitch-
p
-almost periodic] if and only if
F(\cdot)
belongs to the class
B-e-W_{ap}^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}:Y)
[is Besicovitch-
1
-almost periodic]; cf. also the formulation of Theorem
2.1
(iv)-(v) below, where we have assumed that
p=1.

We need the following results, which can be deduced in the same manner as for Bohr almost periodic functions (cf.
[
21
]
for more details):

Lemma 1.2
.

(i)

Suppose that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
. If
the sequences
F:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
and
G:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
are Bohr almost periodic, then the sequence
(\alpha F+\beta G)(\cdot)
is likewise Bohr almost periodic.

(ii)

Suppose that
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
Y_{i}
is a locally convex space for
1\leq i\leq k
and
the sequence
F_{i}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y_{i}
is Bohr almost periodic for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then the sequence
(F_{1},...,F_{k}):{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y_{1}\times...\times Y_{k}
is Bohr almost periodic.

For the sake of simplicity and better exposition, we will not consider the abstract semilinear nonautonomous difference inclusions henceforth.

2.
Abstract nonautonomous first-order difference inclusions

Let us consider the abstract nonautonomous difference equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle x(k+1)=A(k)x(k)+f(k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where
A(k)\in L(Y),

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
Our first structural result reads as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
there exists a finite real number
c^{\kappa}(k)>0
such that
\kappa(A(k)x)\leq c^{\kappa}(k)\kappa(x)
for all
x\in Y
, and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{+\infty}\Bigl[c^{\kappa}(k-v)\cdot...\cdot c^{\kappa}(k-1)\Bigr]<+\infty,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Suppose, further, that for each
\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
we have
\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c^{\kappa}(k)<+\infty.

Then we have the following:

(i)

If the sequence
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow Y
is bounded, then the sequence

(2.3)

\displaystyle x(k):=f(k-1)+\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}\Biggl[A(k-1)\cdot...\cdot A(j+1)\Biggr]f(j),\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

is well-defined and
x(\cdot)
is a solution of (
2.1
).

(ii)

If the sequence
k\mapsto f(k),

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is Bohr almost periodic and, for every relatively compact set
B\subseteq Y,

\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
and
\epsilon>0,
there exists a finite real number

L>0
such that for each
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists
\tau\in B(t_{0},L)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\kappa\Bigl(A(k+\tau)x-A(k)x\Bigr)\leq\epsilon,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ x\in B,

then a unique almost periodic solution
x(\cdot)
of (
2.1
) is given by (
2.3
).

(iii)

If
\omega\in{\mathbb{N}},

c\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\},
the sequence
k\mapsto f(k),

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is
(\omega,c)
-periodic and
A(k+\omega)=A(k)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

then an
(\omega,c)
-periodic solution
x(\cdot)
of (
2.1
) is given by (
2.3
). The uniqueness of
(\omega,c)
-periodic solutions of (
2.1
) holds if
|c|\geq 1.

(iv)

If the sequence
k\mapsto f(k),

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is bounded and belongs to the class
B-e-W_{ap}^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}:Y)
as well as, for every relatively compact set
B\subseteq Y,

\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
and
\epsilon>0,
there exist a trigonometric polynomial
P:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow L(Y)
and an integer
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle l^{-1}\sum_{j=s}^{s+l}\Bigl[\kappa\bigl(A(j)x-P(j)x\bigr)\Bigr]\leq\epsilon,\quad s\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ x\in B,

then the sequence
x(\cdot)
, given by (
2.3
), is a bounded solution of (
2.1
) and belongs to the class
B-e-W_{ap}^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}:Y).

(v)

If the sequence
k\mapsto f(k),

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is bounded and Besicovitch-
1
-almost periodic as well as, for every relatively compact set
B\subseteq Y,

\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
and
\epsilon>0,
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow L(Y)
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\Biggl\{l^{-1}\cdot\sup_{x\in B}\sum_{j=-l}^{l}\Bigl[\kappa\bigl(A(j)x-P(j)x\bigr)\Bigr]\Biggr\}\leq\epsilon,

, given by (
2.3

1
-almost periodic solution of (
2.1
).

Proof.

It can be simply proved that the series
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}[A(k-1)\cdot...\cdot A(j+1)]f(j)
is absolutely convergent since for each
\kappa\in\circledast_{Y}
we have

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle\Biggl(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}\Bigl[A(k-1)\cdot...\cdot A(j+1)\Bigr]f(j)\Biggr)\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}\Bigl[c^{\kappa}(j+1)\cdot...\cdot c^{\kappa}(k-1)\Bigr]\kappa\bigl(f(j)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=1}^{+\infty}\Bigl[c^{\kappa}(k-+v)\cdot...\cdot c^{\kappa}(k-1)\Bigr]\kappa\bigl(f(k-1-v)\bigr);

cf. also (
2.2

Now it is quite simple to prove that the function

given by (
2.3
), is a solution of (
2.1

then we have

\displaystyle x(k+1)

\displaystyle=f(k)+\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-1}\Bigl[A(k)\cdot...\cdot A(j+1)\Bigr]f(j)

\displaystyle=A(k)f(k-1)+A(k)\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}\Bigl[A(k-1)\cdot...\cdot A(j+1)\Bigr]f(j)+f(k)

\displaystyle=A(k)x(k)+f(k),

which completes the proof of (i). In order to prove (ii), observe first that the range

Since the sequence

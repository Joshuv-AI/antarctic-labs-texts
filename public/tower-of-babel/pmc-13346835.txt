A decision making algorithm for enhancing consumer value in purchasing behavior based on circular spherical fuzzy CRITIC WASPAS method
Authors: Rui Zhang, Xiaoshuo Li, Wenjia Yi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe problems of consumer value in purchasing behavior are usually associated with their high degree of uncertainty, hesitation, and contradicting opinions among the decision-makers. Conventional fuzzy and intuitionistic fuzzy models cannot adequately represent such an achievement and directional uncertainty. Given this limitation, the study offers a novel hybrid multi-attribute group decision-making (MAGDM) model that integrates weighted aggregated sum product assessment (WASPAS) and criteria importance through intercriteria correlation (CRITIC) models into the circular spherical fuzzy set (CSFS) framework. The objective aspect of the proposed method is that it employs the CRITIC method to calculate the weight of the attributes based on the intensity of contrast, and the weighted product model is used to view the summation of the weighted attributes and product models. By doing so, the WASPAS method produces a strong alternative ranking. Representation of membership, abstinence, non-membership, and radius-based hesitation can be more expressive in the CSFS environment, which reflects the uncertainty of decision-makers in a more realistic way. The relevance and suitability of the proposed model are applied to a hypothetical case study of consumer value in buying behavior. The results indicate that “Simplifying the Buying Process” is the optimal strategy across all evaluation scenarios. Sensitivity and comparative analysis also prove the stability, robustness, and high-quality of the proposed CRITIC-WASPAS model in the framework of CSFS as compared to the current fuzzy decision-making methods. Introduction

The reality of uncertainty and indeterminacy is very high in many real-life situations. In this case, binary logic and crisp set theory are not able to conduct effective modeling and decision-making. To correct this, the first theory to enable the basic set membership to take values of 0 to 1 was the fuzzy set (FS) theory of Zadeh1 of 1965. It was the first theory to enable one to treat vague information as being in degrees, as opposed to yes/no, and therefore, much of set theory became applicable in control systems, pattern recognition, and artificial intelligence.

Classical FS theory has been successful, however, as it still integrates, rather clumsily, the concept of membership and non-membership into a single scalar scale, together with the absence and the conflicting existence of evidence. To assist in alleviating this, Atanassov2 in 1986 proposed membership and non-membership degrees, and a generalization of these intuitionistic FS (IFS), but explicitly models the degree of membership and non-membership independently of each other, provided that the sum does not exceed one. This innovation brought the aspect of hesitation, which refers to the remaining uncertainty or the ambiguity brought about by the unfinished information. Therefore, an IFS can give a more detailed and higher-order model of uncertainty, especially when it concerns making decisions and uncertainty is contradictory. Inclusion of abstinence in IFS renders picture FS (PFS) especially handy in decision-making issues where the stakeholders might not entirely disagree or endorse, but rather show indifference or partial reluctance. PFS is a more realistic model of how human opinion can be modeled. The concept of IFS was extended by Cuong3 in 2013 with one more degree, the degree of abstinence. These factors in a PFS are defined by three independent degrees of membership. Subsequently, Mahmood et al.4 in 2019 extended the area of PFS and came up with the concept of spherical FS (SFS).

Although efficient, traditional IFS presuppose that the membership and non-membership levels of elements are accurate point-based values. In practice, however, in real-world contexts of making decisions, like medical diagnosis, risk assessment, or strategic planning, the estimation made by decision-makers (𝒟) is frequently somewhat hesitant or variable, and cannot be well represented by a pair of fixed membership and non-membership degrees. In response to this weakness, Atanassov5 proposed in 2020 circular IFS (CIFS) as a novel generalization of IFS. CPFS is an expansion of the classical formulation, which adds a radius of uncertainty surrounding the membership/non-membership pair, hence capturing the potential range of Pear of opinions. Although the example of CPFS as described by Xu and Zhang6 in the year 2025, the three membership levels are still there, but they are no longer expressed as having definite values; instead, they are modeled as a circle in a three-dimensional membership space. This round format provides a possibility to model variability, hesitation, or uncertainty radius around every evaluation point. By expanding the scope of CPFS, Ashraf and Chohan7 presented the concept of CSFS in 2025. Theoretically, it can be said that the real values of the membership, abstinence, and non-membership degrees can take values within a specific range, limited by the circle. Problem statement

Consumer value in purchasing behavior refers to the overall assessment a buyer makes regarding the benefits received from a product or service relative to the costs incurred in obtaining it. These benefits can be tangible, such as product functionality, quality, and price, or intangible, such as emotional satisfaction, brand image, and social acceptance. Consumers constantly balance the benefits of a product or service against what it costs them, not just in money, but also in time, effort, and potential risk. This perception of value plays a key role in shaping their decisions, influencing which brands they prefer and how loyal they remain over time. Importantly, consumer value isn’t a fixed number; it changes depending on personal preferences, market trends, and the situation. For instance, one person might prioritize affordability, while another may care more about brand image or environmental impact. Companies that truly understand what their customers value and tailor their offerings accordingly are in a much better position to drive purchases and build lasting relationships.

However, consumer decision-making is rarely straightforward, and trade-offs are areas where FS theory is especially useful. MAGDM approach offers valuable frameworks for evaluating these kinds of uncertain and sometimes conflicting attributes. In recent years, researchers have made significant strides in this field, introducing new models and approaches that further support decision-making under uncertainty. One we may refer to, Haseli et al.8 extend the best–worst method based on the spherical fuzzy sets, Van Thang et al.9 discussed a fuzzy-based expert system to analyze purchase behavior under an uncertain environment, Arora et al.10 elaborated the factors affecting consumer purchase intentions of organic food through fuzzy AHP, evaluate waste management under spherical fuzzy sets extended by Haseli et al.11. Impact of customer-perceived value on consumer behavior in a shared economy using fuzzy logic introduced by Ma12, an interval-valued intuitionistic fuzzy analytic hierarchy process model for understanding consumer decision-making in non-wood forest product purchases suggested by Singer and Ozsahin13, while a fuzzy logic and machine learning hybrid model for influencing consumer purchasing behavior in E-Commerce developed by Ayyadapu et al.14.

Contribution

This study makes several significant contributions to the field of multi-attribute group decision-making (MAGDM) under uncertainty, particularly in the context of consumer value assessment:

1. The study innovatively extends the CRITIC and WASPAS methods into the CSF environment. This is the first known integration of these methods in this framework, allowing for a more nuanced representation of uncertainty.

2. The additive model and the multiplicative model within the CSFS allow for a dual-perspective evaluation, improving the accuracy and flexibility of decision outcomes in MAGDM problems.

3. The model is applied to the case study focused on consumer value in purchasing behavior, providing actionable insights for businesses aiming to align their strategies.

4. The integration of CSFS in MAGDM is a relatively unexplored area. This study fills a clear gap in the literature and opens pathways for further applications in complex decision-making scenarios across domains like healthcare, strategic planning, and marketing.

The CRITIC–WASPAS model has been applied in various decision-making contexts. The present study extends its application by integrating it with CSFS. This integration provides a more comprehensive representation of uncertainty, capturing not only membership and non-membership degrees but also abstinence and the geometric radius of fuzziness. As a result, the proposed approach can more accurately model decision-makers’ subjective perceptions and improve the stability of alternative rankings under uncertain or incomplete information. This distinctive combination addresses limitations of the traditional CRITIC–WASPAS method, which may overlook subtle nuances in preference data, thereby offering a more robust tool for MAGDM.

Literature study

The technique of selecting the best option from a range of options by assessing their possible outcomes is known as multi-attribute decision-making (MADM). Wang et al.15 created MADM picture fuzzy Muirhead mean operators, whereas Hussain et al.16 introduced the Aczel-Alsina aggregation operator in MADM. By incorporating a group of 𝒟 who jointly assess options across several qualities, MAGDM expands upon MADM. It works well in situations with a variety of parties and characteristics and is helpful in complex evaluations, such as consumer value in purchasing behavior assessments. The TOPSIS method for MAGDM is further developed by Hussain et al.17. MAGDM was proposed in the MAIRCA technique by Abbas et al.18. Wang19 used the enhanced ARAS approach as the basis for the MAGDM model.

The paper brings WASPAS methodology to the CSF framework, making it more applicable in MAGDM. To obtain a comprehensive evaluation, Zavadskas et al.20 formulated the WASPAS technique that considers a combination of the weighted sum model (WSM) and weighted product model (WPM). It is a popular decision-making strategy that considers both the quantitative and qualitative factors. In Table 2, we provide a summary of the WASPAS technique.

However, as illustrated in Table 2, traditional WASPAS frameworks have a significant limitation: they are not designed to handle data expressed using CSFS. This is a considerable shortcoming, especially given that CSFS offers a more nuanced way to represent uncertainty. Recent studies, such as the HECON model introduced by Haseli et al.26, have shown the effectiveness of advanced MADM techniques in analyzing consumer value and loyalty by accounting for complex decision behaviors like the halo effect. Unlike data-driven neural approaches, this study adopts a fuzzy-based MAGDM framework to explicitly handle uncertainty and hesitation in consumer evaluations. The proposed CRITIC–WASPAS model within the CSFS framework thus offers a complementary and interpretable approach for assessing consumer value strategies.

Although most of the studies conducted on the integration of subjective and objective weighting techniques in various fuzzy decision-making contexts are those of the classical fuzzy framework and even the SFS theory, such strategies are not totally absent in the literature. As an example, previous research has been able to integrate subjective opinions and objective weighting methods and hybrid aggregation mechanisms to enhance the accuracy of the decisions made. Wang and Lee27, Sahin28, and Chang29 have made meaningful contributions by combining subjective and objective weighting approaches, as well as incorporating hybrid decision-making mechanisms in various fuzzy environments. However, the developments are still mostly relegated to a classic fuzzy environment and are not as extensive as the extended uncertainty modeling abilities provided by the CSFS framework. Particularly the simultaneous integration of an objective weighting method such as CRITIC with a dual aggregation approach like WASPAS remains limited in the CSFS context. Therefore, rather than suggesting a complete absence of such hybrid approaches, this study specifically highlights the need for their extension into the CSFS environment, where hesitation, abstinence, and directional uncertainty can be more effectively captured in complex MAGDM issues, such as consumer value assessment.

Research gap

Despite the growing interest in multi-attribute group decision-making (MAGDM) under uncertainty, several critical gaps remain unaddressed in the existing literature:

1. While fuzzy, intuitionistic, picture, and spherical fuzzy environments have been widely used in MAGDM problems. The proposed CSF framework, which offers enhanced capability in modeling hesitation and directional uncertainty, has received minimal attention.

2. Few studies have integrated multiple decision-making tools in the CSFS context. Specifically, no prior work has combined the CRITIC method for objective weighting with the WASPAS method for alternative ranking under CSFS, limiting the scope of accurate and comprehensive decision support.

3. Most MAGDM models in fuzzy environments depend heavily on 𝒟 judgment for assigning weights to attributes, which introduces bias. There is a lack of research applying objective weighting methods, like CRITIC, in the CSFS framework.

4. Although CSFS has theoretical advantages, there is limited empirical application of CSFS-based decision-making models to practical problems, such as consumer value evaluation, sustainability, or supply chain management.

Research questions

Based on the identified gaps, the study seeks to answer the following key research questions:

1. How can the strengths of the CRITIC and WASPAS methods be effectively combined within the CSFS environment to improve the MAGDM approach?

2. Can an objective, correlation-based weighting method (CRITIC) enhance the reliability of decision-making outcomes under high uncertainty in the CSFS context?

3. How does the proposed CRITIC-WASPAS model perform in a real-world consumer value decision-making scenario compared to existing fuzzy MAGDM models?

4. What are the benefits and limitations of using CSFS over traditional fuzzy environments in terms of representing 𝒟 hesitation and uncertainty in group decision-making?

To explore these questions, this study proposes a novel extension of the CRITIC-WASPAS model within the CSFS and outlines an algorithm for applying it to decision-making scenarios involving consumer behavior.

CRITIC-WASPAS method extension in the framework of circular spherical fuzzy set

The methodological necessity and not newness are what have prompted the inclusion of the CRITIC and WASPAS methodologies in the CSFS framework. The perceived value of the consumer is a set of various, conflicting features (e.g., quality, price, convenience, and brand name), the importance of which in relation to each other is seldom certain. The CRITIC technique has been utilized in order to objectively measure the weights of attributes by taking into consideration the contrast intensity and inter-criteria correlation, which reduces the subjectivity and the bias that is usually inherent in the weight of attributes assigned by experts. This is of great essence when analyzing consumer behavior, as personalization and lack of total market knowledge can skew weight allocation. Nonetheless, CRITIC does not have a mechanism of alternative ranking, and it fails to measure compensatory and non-compensatory trade-offs in decision outcomes.

The WASPAS technique makes CRITIC stronger by maintaining the merits of the WSM and the WPM. WSM represents compensatory decision behavior, in which good performance in one attribute can offset poor performance in another, whereas WPM represents non-compensatory behavior in which a poor performance cannot be readily compensated. As real-life consumer choices can tend to be both simultaneous, the hybrid WASPAS aggregation offers a more consistent and realistic ranking compared with either of the models applied singly. Including CRITIC-generated objective weights into WASPAS, the framework obtained provides data-informed and behavior-reliable rankings, which render the suggested model more credible and understandable to business decision-makers.

More so, this hybrid CRITIC-WASPAS structure, when implemented in the Circular Spherical Fuzzy Set environment, greatly improves the uncertainty modeling. The judgments of decision-makers represented by CSFS are not fixed-point but circular areas characterized by membership, abstinence, non-membership, as well as a geometric radius. This framework directly represents ambivalence, changeability, and directional ambiguity in expert judgments that prevail in consumer value estimation. Even though the use of a score function eventually results in acquiring scalar values to weight and rank, it does not deny the fuzzy information. Rather, the score function combines all the CSFS elements, such as the radius of uncertainty, and is used following the fuzzy aggregation, such that the uncertainty information is incorporated in the decision process at all times. As a result, the suggested CRITICWASPAS model, under the CSFS, offers a stronger, dependable, and more practical decision-support framework than the traditional fuzzy or crisp MAGDM solutions. The score function of CSFS presented in Eq. (12) is based on the work of Ashraf and Chohan7, which extends the CSFS framework from picture and spherical fuzzy sets. This formulation was specifically designed to satisfy the key properties required for MAGDM applications:

• Order Ability: The function maps each CSFS to a unique scalar real value within , allowing alternatives to be ranked reliably.

• Monotonicity with Respect to Membership Components: Increasing increases the score, whereas increasing or decreases the score, reflecting their respective positive or negative contribution to the evaluation.

• Incorporation of Radius: The term captures the directional uncertainty associated with the radius, allowing the function to reflect hesitation in decision-makers’ opinions.

While Eq. (12) is commonly used in recent CSFS framework studies, we acknowledge that alternative formulations of the score function are possible. 3. Methodological Justification.

The step of defuzzification is implemented by using the CSFS score function, whose main aim is not only to make decision-makers’ judgments aggregate but also to compute objective weights based on the CRITIC method. This move is not a compromise of uncertainty, since the CSFS framework already coded various dimensions in it, such as membership, abstinence, non-membership, in addition to the radius of uncertainty. This stage of defuzzification is just a way of letting the computations conveniently carry on without interfering with the directional and the hesitancy information in the fuzzy data.

4. Role of Fuzziness.

The fuzziness is actively maintained and made use of during analysis:

• CSFWG is an operator that synthesizes judgment of decision-makers without eliminating uncertainty and hesitation.

• The component of radius reflects variability so that the model can be used to explain the ambiguity in group evaluations.

• The WSM and WPM views are also taken into account as final rankings within the CSFS framework, which guarantees the subtle decision-making based on the initial fuzzy data.

Overall, the initial defuzzification process does not result in the elimination of the advantages of the fuzzy framework, and, on the contrary, it contributes to the computational tractability and to the fact that the natural uncertainty, hesitancy, and ambiguity of CSFS are preserved and mirrored in the outcomes.

Case study

This case study is designed to demonstrate the practical applicability and effectiveness of the proposed CRITIC–WASPAS decision-making hybrid model within the CSFS environment. The main objective is to identify the most effective strategy for enhancing consumer value in purchasing behavior when decision information is uncertain, imprecise, and influenced by multiple evaluation attributes.

Consumer value is subjective and does not exist in all persons and market environments. Quality of products, convenience, customer experience, brand reputation, and price are some of the factors that interact in some complex ways, hence making the decision-making process complex for businesses. To solve this complexity, the problem is stated as a MAGDM task, in which various pairs of a number of strategic options are assessed in the presence of uncertainty.

In this experiment, the three decision-makers (,,) was used to simulate a group environment in decision-making. These decision-makers were chosen to embody various views which can be used as pertinent to consumer value in purchasing behavior, which includes strategic analysis, market cognition, and operational decision-making. Instead of paying attention to the individual identities, the study highlights their evaluative functions in the context of MAGDM, which are in line with the methodological nature of the research.

The decision-makers gave their judgments in preset terminologies of language developed in the CSFS framework. This was a strategy to express their evaluations when there was uncertainty, indecisiveness, and partial concurrence. The data was collected by requesting each decision-maker to personally compare all the options with each attribute using the linguistic scale presented in Table 3. These qualitative measurements were subsequently converted into CSF values to analyze them in detail. Objectively, to derive the score function to calculate the weights of the decision-makers based on influence and reliability, the weights were determined using the score function as shown in Eq. (10). This prevented subjective bias and also guaranteed that the group opinion of aggregate DM was accurate as far as the group is concerned.

Description of alternatives and attributes

In this study, five strategic alternatives are considered, each representing a distinct approach to improving consumer value:

• : Communicate Clear Value Propositions.

• : Simplify the Buying Process.

• : Offer Flexible Pricing and Payment Options.

• : Quality.

Among them, quality, convenience, and brand reputation are the attributes that are considered as benefit-type attributes, whereas customer experience and price are considered as cost-type attributes on the basis of their assessment nature in the decision environment.

Conclusion

This paper hypothesized a hybrid CRITIC-WASPAS decision-making model in the CSFS setting to test the strategies to improve consumer value in the buying decision within an uncertain setting. The effectiveness and strength of the suggested approach are clearly illustrated by the empirical findings received as a result of the case study. Of the 5 options considered, the second alternative, which is called “Simplify the Buying Process,” was the best in the weighted sum model, weighted product model, and the combined WASPAS analysis. In addition, sensitivity analysis using different values of the aggregation parameter established the stability of the results obtained in ranking, which further supports the validity of the proposed model. In practice, the suggested framework can also be a good guide to businesses and decision-makers who want to align their strategies with the expectation of consumer values in volatile environments. This model can be used to serve strategic planning in marketing, management of customer experience, and making decisions that are value-based.

The results also indicate that the convenience and quality attributes are more dominant in determining the consumer value, since they had more objective weights calculated based on the CRITIC method. The weighting process makes the subjectivity more objective and the outcomes of the evaluation more credible by using both contrast intensity and inter-attribute correlations. These findings underscore the relevance of the practicality of simplifying the purchasing procedures with the aim of enhancing consumer impressions, satisfaction, and confidence in the making of decisions. Methodologically speaking, this work contributes to the field in an important way by applying the CRITIC and WASPAS techniques to the CSFS model so as to be able to represent the uncertainty, hesitation, and directional information in group decision-making in a more expressive way. The combination of CSFS, objective weighting, and dual aggregation procedures gives an adaptable and complete instrument to tackle complex problems of MAGDM, which cannot be adequately handled by conventional fuzzy models.

Demonstrative and hypothetical nature for the future validation

It is pointed out that the case presented in this article is in the form of a hypothetical case study, which is only assumed to demonstrate the applicability, effectiveness, and computational flow of the proposed CRITIC-WASPAS approach within the CSFS framework. This is because the hypothetical data allows a clear presentation of the aggregation process, objective attribute weighting, and ranking mechanism without any loss of generality. Even though the CRITIC technique is statistical, and the standard deviation and correlation coefficients can be affected by a few alternatives, the results attained are sufficient evidence to show the viability and internal consistency of the developed technique. The proposed model will be checked in further research and applied to real-life decision-making issues, using bigger datasets, practical limitations, and real expert evaluations, to bring a deeper understanding of its strength, scalability, and efficiency in the real decision-making context.

Constraints and limitations

The created fuzzy aggregation models are beneficial in real life. Nonetheless, they also have their restrictions. The information presented in the form of the form provided in Definition 1 is the only one that can be handled by the proposed work. It does not accommodate complicated fuzzy information and bipolar fuzzy information. Nevertheless, the suggested model is a worthy addition to the sphere of fuzzy decision-making in spite of all its constraints.

It should be mentioned that the case study under consideration is hypothetical and is designed mainly to illustrate how the suggested CRITIC-WASPAS model can be applicable within the scope of CSFS. Although the results demonstrate how the method works, they are not a mirror of the actual consumer information. In future studies, the approach would be implemented on actual marketing data, including consumer surveys or CRM statistics, to check both its usefulness in practice and its ability to work effectively in the environment of uncertainty and to improve the process of decision-making based on the results of monitoring consumer behavior.

Future direction

We will concentrate on the new methods derived using the proposed fuzzy framework, say, Aczel-Alsina hamy, Dombi hamy, Frank and Maclaurin symmetric operators, to make the decision-making outcomes more flexible and more accurate in the future. Once more, one can go further by considering variants such as Dombi hamy mean operators36, Aczel Alsina power aggregation operator37, Aczel-Alsina hamy mean operators38, and TOPSIS method of MAGDM39.

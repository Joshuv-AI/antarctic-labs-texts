On the factorization of twisted L L -values and 11 11 -descents over C 5 C_{5} -number fields
Celine Maistret, Himanshu Shukla

Abstract
. We investigate the Galois module structure of the Tate-Shafarevich group of elliptic curves. For a Dirichlet character χ \chi , we give an explicit conjecture relating the ideal factorization of L ⁡ ( E , χ , 1 ) L(E,\chi,1) to the Galois module structure of X ⁡ ( E / K ) \Sha(E/K) , where χ \chi factors through the Galois group of K / ℚ K/\mathbb{Q} . We provide numerical evidence for this conjecture using the methods of visualization and p p -descent. For the latter, we present a procedure that makes performing an 11 11 -descent over a C 5 C_{5} number field practical for an elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} with complex multiplication. We also expect that our method can be pushed to perform higher descents (e.g. 31 31 -descent) over a C 5 C_{5} number field given more computational power.

1.
Introduction

For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
factoring through a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
we investigate the connection between the Galois module structure of
\Sha(E/K)
and the algebraic special
L
-value of the twisted
L
-function
L(E/\mathbb{Q},\chi,1)
. We start by recalling the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture (BSD), which links the size of Tate-Shafarevich groups of elliptic curves to the special value of their
L
-functions.

1.1.
Conjectural order of
\Sha(E/K)

Computing
\Sha(E/K)
is a fundamental but hard problem.
Assuming its finiteness, the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture provides a formula for its size in terms of arithmetic invariants of the curve.
Let
L(E/K,s)
be the Hasse-Weil
L
-function associated to
E/K
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture)
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve. Then

(1)

{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)={\mathrm{rk}}(E/K)
,

(2)

the leading term of the Taylor series at
s=1
of L(E/K,s) is given by

(1.1)

\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{L(E/K,s)}{(s-1)^{r}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|}}{\Omega_{+}(E)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}|\Omega_{-}(E)|^{r_{2}}}=\frac{{\mathrm{Reg}}_{E/K}|\Sha_{E/K}|C_{E/K}}{|E(K)_{{\mathrm{tors}}}|^{2}},

where
{\mathrm{Reg}}_{E/K}
is the regulator of
E/K
,
\Delta_{K}
is the discriminant of the field,
r
is the order of the zero,
(r_{1},r_{2})
is the signature of
K
,
\Omega_{\pm}
are the periods of
E
and
C_{E/K}
is the product of Tamagawa numbers and other local fudge factors from finite places.

Following
[
3
]
, we will denote the right-hand side of (
1.1
) by
{\mathrm{BSD}}(E/K)
. In practice,
L(E/K,s)
is computable up to a “reasonable” approximation (see
[
2
]
). Therefore Conjecture
1.1
(2) can be used to reasonably approximate
|\Sha(E/K)|
by computing the other quantities in
{\mathrm{BSD}}(E/K)
. This computation is referred to as computing the order of the
analytic
Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sha_{\text{an}}(E/K)
(see the L-function and Modular form database LMFDB
[
6
]
for examples of such computations).

1.2.
Conjectural Galois module structure of
\Sha(E/K)

If
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension and
E/\mathbb{Q}
is an elliptic curve, then the
n
-descent sequence

(1.2)

0\to\frac{E(K)}{nE(K)}\to{\mathrm{Sel}}^{(n)}(E/K)\to\Sha(E/K)[n]\to 0

is an exact sequence of Galois modules. One can then ask if a BSD-like formula can be used to link the Galois module structure of
\Sha(E/K)
to
L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s)
, where
\rho
is an Artin representation associated to
K/\mathbb{Q}
. This is related to conjectures such as the Equivariant Tamagawa Number Conjecture, and can be formalized as follows.

Following Notation 15 in
[
3
]
we define the Euler factor at a prime number
p
of
L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s)
to be

(1.3)

P_{\ell}(E,\rho,s)\coloneqq\det(\text{Id}-\text{Frob}_{\ell}^{-1}\ell^{-s}|(H_{p}^{1}(E)\otimes\rho)^{I_{\ell}}),

where
I_{\ell}
denotes the inertia group at
\ell
and
H_{p}^{1}(E)=H^{1}_{et}(E,\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{C}
for any embedding
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and any prime
p\neq\ell
.

The following definition presents the “twisted” analogue of the left-hand side of (
1.1
).

Definition 1.2

(Definition 12 in
[
3
]
)
.

For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and an Artin representation
\rho
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we write

\mathscr{L}(E,\rho)=\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{L(E,\rho,s)}{(s-1)^{r}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{f}_{\rho}}}{\Omega_{+}(E)^{d^{+}(\rho)}|\Omega_{-}(E)|^{d^{-}(\rho)}w_{\rho}},

where
r={\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,\rho,s)
is the order of the zero at
s=1
,
\mathfrak{f}_{\rho}
and
w_{\rho}
are the conductor and root number of
\rho
respectively, and
d^{\pm}(\rho)
are the dimensions of the
\pm
-eigenspaces of complex conjugation in its action on
\rho
.

On the other hand, for
p
an odd prime such that
(p,[K:\mathbb{Q}])=1
,
\Sha(E/K)[p]
decomposes
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-representation of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/\mathbb{Q})
:

\Sha(E/K)[p]\simeq\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\theta}(\Sha(E/K)[p])_{\theta},

where
(\Sha(E/K)[p])_{\theta}
is the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace corresponding to the
non-trivial irreducible sub-representation
\theta
.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
\chi
a primitive Dirichlet character of square free order
d
, conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}
, factoring through an extension
K^{\chi}/\mathbb{Q}
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K^{\chi}/\mathbb{Q})=\langle\tau\rangle
, and let
\mu_{d}
denote the group of roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then there is an embedding
\iota:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})\to\mathbb{C}
so that for every elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{E}
coprime to
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}
satisfying

\Sha(E/K^{\chi})[p^{\infty}]=\Sha(E/K^{\chi})[p]
,

E(K)[p^{\infty}]=E(\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]
,

E(K)[p^{\infty}]=E(\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]
,

v_{p}(C_{E/K})=v_{p}(C_{E/\mathbb{Q}})
, and

L(E,\chi,1)\neq 0
,

we have

(\iota^{-1}(\mathscr{L}(E,\chi)))_{p}=\prod\limits_{\theta}(h_{\theta}(\chi(\tau)^{d/d_{\theta}}),p),

where

(\mathscr{L}(E,\chi))_{p}
is the part above
p
in the
factorization of the principal ideal

(\iota^{-1}(\mathscr{L}(E,\chi)))\subset\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{d}]
, and
for an irreducible representation
\theta
,
h_{\theta}
is the minimal polynomial of the matrix associated to
\theta(\tau)
in a chosen basis of
(\Sha(E/K)[p])_{\theta}
,
d_{\theta}
is the order of the roots of

, and

\chi

\chi:{\mathrm{Gal}}(K^{\chi}/\mathbb{Q})\to\mu_{d}

We note that in the context of the above conjecture, the first non-trivial case to examine is that of a character of order 5 and 11-torsion in
\Sha(E/K)

\Sha(E/K)

1.3.
Related work

Our work originates from Remark 43 in
[
3
]
, where predicting the factorization of
L(E/\mathbb{Q},\chi,1)
is mentioned in the following context.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
K
be a number field. Artin’s formalism gives

L(E/K,s)=\prod\limits_{\rho}L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s),

where
\rho
runs through the Artin representations of
K
.
Conjecturally, one expects that

{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s)=\langle\rho,E(K)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\rangle,

where the right hand side is the multiplicity of
\rho
in
E(K)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
as a representation of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/\mathbb{Q})
(
[
9
]
). This suggests that the conjectural BSD-type formula for
L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s)
must take into account the Galois module structure on the objects appearing in
{\mathrm{BSD}}(E/K)
.
The following theorem provides a partial answer.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem 38 in
[
3
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{E}
. Suppose Steven’s Manin constant conjecture holds for
E/\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\chi
be a non-trivial primitive Dirichlet character of order
d
and conductor coprime to
\mathfrak{f}_{E}
. Then
\mathscr{L}(E,\chi)\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{d}]
and, if
L(E,\chi,1)\neq 0
, then furthermore

\zeta\cdot\mathscr{L}(E,\chi)\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\text{for }\zeta=\chi(\mathfrak{f}_{E})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\sqrt{\chi(-1)w_{E}}.

If
{\mathrm{rk}}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and the BSD conjecture holds for
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
and
K^{\chi}
with
d
squarefree, then

(1.4)

N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})^{+}/\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta\cdot\mathscr{L}(E,\chi))=\pm\frac{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{{\mathrm{tors}}}|}{|E(K^{\chi})_{{\mathrm{tors}}}|}\sqrt{\frac{|\Sha_{E/K^{\chi}}|\prod_{v}c_{v}(E/K^{\chi})}{|\Sha_{E/\mathbb{Q}}|\prod_{p}c_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})}}.

If moreover
d
is odd and
{\mathrm{BSD}}(E/K^{\chi})={\mathrm{BSD}}(E/\mathbb{Q})
then
\mathscr{L}(E,\chi)=\zeta^{-1}u
for some unit
u\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})^{+}}
.

Remark 1.8
.

We note that if one assumes the conductor of
\chi
to be coprime to that of
E
, the integrality of
\mathscr{L}(E,\chi)
above follows from Theorem 2 in
[
12
]
, hence does not depend on the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

The following example (Example 42 in
[
3
]
) exhibits the limitations of the above theorem regarding the Galois module structure of
\Sha(E/K)
.

Consider the elliptic curves

E_{1}/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x^{2}-1\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{2}/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}+xy=x^{3}+x^{2}-3x-4,

with conductors 291 and 139 respectively. Let
\chi
be the primitive character of order 5 and conductor 31 defined by
\chi(3)=\zeta_{5}^{3}
. For both curves, we have

|E_{i}(\mathbb{Q})|=|E_{i}(K^{\chi})|=|\Sha_{E_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}|=\prod_{p}c_{p}(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q})=\prod_{v}c_{v}(E_{i}/K^{\chi})=1

and
|\Sha_{E_{i}/K^{\chi}}|=11^{2}.

One computes the following ideal factorization

(\mathscr{L}(E_{1},\chi))=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\quad\text{ and }(\mathscr{L}(E_{2},\chi))=\mathfrak{p}_{2}\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{2}},

where
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{1}},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
are distinct ideals above 11, with
\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
the image of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
under complex conjugation.

Theorem
1.7
predicts that the ideals
\mathscr{L}(E_{i},\chi)
must be either
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
or
\mathfrak{p}_{2}\overline{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
, but cannot be used to decide which factorization it is. This prompted Conjecture
1.3
.

N_{F_{i}/F}:\frac{U_{S_{i}}(F_{i})}{U_{S_{i}}(F_{i})^{p}}\to\frac{U_{S_{F}}(F)}{U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}}

be the map
induced by the usual norm map
F_{i}\to F
. Since
p\ \nmid\ [F_{i}:F]=q
, the composition
U_{S_{F}}(F)/U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\to}}U_{S_{F}}(F_{i})/U_{S_{F}}(F_{i})^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{F_{i}/F}}}{{\to}}U_{S_{F}}(F)/U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}
is an isomorphism (where
\iota
is induced by the natural inclusion of
F\subset F_{i}
). In particular,

\iota
splits the exact sequence

1\to\ker(N_{F_{i}/F})\to U_{F_{i}}/U_{S_{i}}(F_{i})^{p}\to U_{S_{F}}(F)/U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}\to 1.

Therefore

U_{S_{i}}(F_{i})/U_{S_{i}}(F_{i})^{p}\simeq U_{S_{F}}(F)/U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}\oplus\ker(N_{F_{i}/F}).

Since the imaginary quadratic number field
K^{\prime}
satisfies the containement
K^{\prime}\subset F\subset F_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq q
,
using Dirichlet’s unit theorem one computes

{\mathrm{dim}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\ker(N_{F_{i}/F}))=\frac{p-1}{2}(1-1/q)+\sum\limits_{j\in\{1,2\}}r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}-r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F},

where
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},-}

is the number of prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{-}
above the ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
of
\mathcal{O}
above
p
.
The following proposition shows that elements of
U_{S}(LK)/U_{S}(LK)^{p}
can be represented by elements of
U_{S_{F}}(F)
and
\ker(N_{F_{i}/F})
for
0\leq i\leq q
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
L
,
K
,
F_{i}
and
F
be as above. Then

U_{S}(LK)/U_{S}(LK)^{p}\simeq U_{S_{F}}(F)/U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{q}\ker(N_{F_{i}/F}).

Proof.

We first check that the groups on both sides have the right
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension. The total dimension on the right hand side is

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2q}-1+\frac{p-1}{2}(1-1/q)(q+1)+r_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},F}+r_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},F}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q}\left(r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}-r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q(p-1)}{2}-1+r_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},F}+r_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},F}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q}\left(r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}-r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}\right).

If a prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
splits in
\mathcal{O}_{F_{i}}
, one has
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}=qr_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
. If it does not, one has

r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}=r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
. Furthermore, if
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}=qr_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
for two distinct
i
, then
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}=qr_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
for all
i
and
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},LK}=q^{2}r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
. In this case one obtains

r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q}\left(r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}-r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}\right)=r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}+(q^{2}-1)r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}=q^{2}r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}=r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},LK}.

Similarly, one checks that the equality
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q}\left(r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}-r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}\right)=r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},LK}
holds when
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}=qr_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
for exactly one
i
, and when
r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F_{i}}=r_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},F}
. Therefore, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension matches on both sides.

If each of the
groups in the summand on the right-hand side intersects the other trivially, we are
done. To see this, note that if
u\in\ker(N_{F_{i}/F}\cap N_{F_{j}/F})
with

i\neq j
, then
u
can be represented by an element in
U_{S_{F}}(F)
but then
u\neq\ker(N_{F_{i}/F})
. Lastly,
U_{S_{F}}(F)/U_{S_{F}}(F)^{p}
intersects trivially with each

\ker(N_{F_{i}/F})
.
∎

Now we look at the class group contribution. The arguments are similar as
for the unit groups. We once again have the following exact sequence

0\to{\mathrm{Cl}}(F_{i})[p]\to{\mathrm{Cl}}(LK)[p]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{LK/F_{i}}}}{{\to}}{\mathrm{Cl}}(F_{i})[p]\to 0,

where
N_{LK/F_{i}}
is the usual norm map on ideal classes.
We obtain a similar sequence when
F_{i}
is replaced by
F
. The following
proposition shows
that
{\mathrm{Cl}}(LK)[p]
is generated by
{\mathrm{Cl}}(F_{i})[p]
and
{\mathrm{Cl}}(F)[p]
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an ideal in the maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{LK}
of
LK
representing a non-trivial
element in

{\mathrm{Cl}}(LK)[p]
. Then there is a field
F_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq q
such that
N_{LK/F_{i}}([\mathfrak{p}])\neq 0
or
N_{LK/F}([\mathfrak{p}])\neq 0.

In particular, we obtain
that
[\mathfrak{p}]
is in the image of
{\mathrm{Cl}}(F_{i})[p]\to{\mathrm{Cl}}(LK)[p]
or
{\mathrm{Cl}}(F)[p]\to{\mathrm{Cl}}(LK)[p]

Proof.

Assume that for every field
F_{i}
and
F
,
N_{LK/F_{i}}([\mathfrak{p}])
and

N_{LK/F}([\mathfrak{p}])
are trivial. This implies that

[\mathfrak{p}]\in\bigcap\limits_{i=0}^{q}\ker(N_{LK/F_{i}}),

and hence that

\prod\limits_{i=0}^{q}\prod\limits_{g\in G_{i}}\mathfrak{p}^{g}

is a principal ideal. But

\prod\limits_{i=0}^{q}\prod\limits_{g\in G_{i}}\mathfrak{p}^{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{q}\prod\limits_{g\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(LK/F)}\mathfrak{p}^{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{q}N_{LK/F}(\mathfrak{p})

implies that

is a principal ideal since

is principal. However, the

[\mathfrak{p}]
is

, hence
\mathfrak{p}
is a principal ideal.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

Our idea to perform an 11-descent on CM elliptic curves in order to produce numerical evidence towards Conjecture
1.3
is optimal in the sense that finding elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
that admit an 11-isogeny seems to be a hard problem. Given an order
5
Dirichlet character
\chi
, this is equivalent to finding
K^{\chi}
-rational points on the elliptic curve
X_{0}(11)
such that the

-invariant is rational. A simple search through
C_{5}
number fields
K^{\chi}
on LMFDB with “low” discriminant yields no field where

even acquires a
K^{\chi}
-rational point.

Another idea could have been to use
\mathbb{Q}
-rational points on

, which is a rational curve, in order to create examples for

and
q=7
. However, not much can be said about the class group and the unit group of the degree 84 étale algebra corresponding to 13-isogeny descent in this case.

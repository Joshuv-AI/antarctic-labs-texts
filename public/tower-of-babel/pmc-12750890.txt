Adverse events following measles, mumps, rubella and varicella virus vaccine live (PROQUAD®) reported to the vaccine adverse event reporting system (VAERS), 2015–2025
Authors: Guojun Liang, Qiong Liu, Yang Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPROQUAD®, a quadrivalent live-attenuated vaccine targeting measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella, is widely used in pediatric immunization programs. While clinical trials and post-marketing studies have established its general safety, real-world evidence from large-scale passive surveillance systems remains limited.MethodsWe performed a disproportionality analysis of PROQUAD-related adverse events (AEs) reported to the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) between 2015 and 2025. Multiple statistical algorithms were applied to detect safety signals, supplemented by stratified analyses across age, sex, severity, and fatal outcomes.ResultsAmong 17,234 reports, the most frequently reported AEs included injection site erythema, swelling, and fever, consistent with established reactogenicity. Several statistically significant signals not listed in the current FDA label were identified, such as febrile convulsion, vaccination failure, cyanosis, and influenza-like illness. These unlabeled events exhibited distinct patterns across age and sex subgroups, with serious and fatal outcomes occurring more frequently in children under three years of age and in medically vulnerable individuals. Most AEs occurred within three days of vaccination, although serious events showed delayed onset profiles.ConclusionThis real-world pharmacovigilance analysis confirms the expected safety profile of PROQUAD while revealing additional adverse event signals that merit further clinical investigation and potential regulatory attention. Continued monitoring is essential to inform vaccine safety practices, particularly in pediatric populations with heightened susceptibility.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12985-025-03022-z.

Introduction

Measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella are highly transmissible viral infections that historically imposed a profound burden of morbidity and mortality on children prior to the widespread implementation of vaccination programs. Before the measles vaccine rollout in the United States, more than 530,000 cases were reported annually, leading to approximately 48,000 hospitalizations and nearly 500 deaths each year. Similarly, congenital rubella syndrome occurred in 0.1 to 0.2 per 1,000 live births, with major outbreaks every 5 to 9 years [1].

Global immunization initiatives have markedly reduced the incidence of these diseases. Between 2000 and 2018, measles mortality decreased by 73%, mumps incidence declined to fewer than 1 case per 100,000 population in countries with national vaccination programs, and rubella elimination was certified in 81 countries by 2019. Nevertheless, in recent years, a resurgence of these infections has been documented, driven by declining vaccine uptake, disruption of immunization services during the COVID-19 pandemic, and waning immunity. For instance, in 2024, Europe recorded over 127,000 measles cases—the highest annual total in 25 years—with more than 40% affecting children under five years of age. Measles remains one of the most contagious human pathogens, associated with a higher case-fatality rate and greater risk of long-term complications such as subacute sclerosing panencephalitis than many other viral infections [2–4].

Varicella, while often mild, historically caused approximately 4 million cases annually in the United States before vaccine introduction in 1995, including 10,600 to 13,000 hospitalizations and up to 150 deaths. The introduction of universal childhood varicella vaccination led to a decline of over 90% in varicella cases and a 71% reduction in hospitalizations [5, 6].Given the severity and potential re-emergence of these vaccine-preventable diseases, sustaining high population coverage with measles-mumps-rubella-varicella (MMRV) combination vaccines remains critical to preserving public health gains.

PROQUAD (Measles, Mumps, Rubella and Varicella Virus Vaccine Live), developed by Merck & Co., is a live-attenuated quadrivalent vaccine that simultaneously immunizes against measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella in a single formulation. It was licensed in the United States in 2005 for use in children aged 12 months through 12 years. Phase III clinical trials demonstrated immunogenic equivalence to separately administered M-M-R II and VARIVAX vaccines, with the added advantage of reducing the number of injections and healthcare visits.

According to current prescribing information, PROQUAD is generally well tolerated, with fever, injection-site erythema, swelling, and rash being the most frequently reported adverse events. Large-scale observational studies involving more than 69,000 vaccinated children have further substantiated this favorable safety profile [7, 8]. Nevertheless, epidemiological analyses have consistently indicated an increased risk of febrile seizures when PROQUAD is administered as the first dose between 12 and 23 months of age, with an estimated excess risk of approximately one additional case per 2,300 to 2,600 doses compared with separate administration of MMR and varicella vaccines. In one pivotal study, 8 of 3,019 children (0.26%) experienced febrile seizures after the first dose, compared to 2 of 2,695 (0.07%) after the second dose [7, 9]. On this basis, the CDC and ACIP recommend shared decision-making regarding the use of PROQUAD for the first dose in children aged 12 to 47 months, underscoring that although febrile seizures may be alarming, they are usually transient and not associated with long-term neurological sequelae [10, 11].

Beyond pre-licensure trials, the ongoing assessment of vaccine safety depends critically on post-marketing pharmacovigilance systems, which are designed to identify rare or unexpected adverse events such as febrile seizures that may not be detected in randomized studies. Passive surveillance programs like the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) provide an essential platform for early signal detection, while active surveillance mechanisms are required to validate such signals and address the inherent limitations of spontaneous reporting, including underreporting and variable data quality. Recent literature has highlighted the role of these complementary approaches in strengthening vaccine safety monitoring, guiding regulatory action, and reinforcing public confidence in immunization programs [12–14].

While randomized controlled trials and post-marketing surveillance have established a generally favorable safety profile for PROQUAD, passive reporting systems such as VAERS offer an opportunity to detect unanticipated patterns in real-world settings that may not be captured during licensure studies. To date, no comprehensive evaluation of adverse event reports specifically related to PROQUAD has been performed using VAERS data from the past decade. This study was therefore designed to explore reporting trends across age and sex subgroups, identify frequently observed or emerging clinical events, and examine their temporal characteristics in order to refine the current understanding of PROQUAD’s safety in the post-approval landscape.

Method

Data source, MedDRA coding and processing

This study utilized individual case safety reports associated with PROQUAD (measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella vaccine) collected through the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS), a passive surveillance database jointly maintained by the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Reports submitted between January 1, 2015, and May 30, 2025, were extracted from the official VAERS website (https://vaers.hhs.gov).

Only domestic reports that explicitly listed PROQUAD® as the administered vaccine were included. Reports were excluded if they lacked any coded adverse event term, originated outside the United States, were identified as duplicates by VAERS ID and vaccination date, or failed to specify the vaccine brand name. To ensure methodological rigor, only reports with at least one valid adverse event code were retained.

Adverse events were coded using version 27.0 of the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA). Preferred Terms (PTs) were mapped to their respective System Organ Classes (SOCs) following the standardized MedDRA hierarchy, without manual recoding to preserve data consistency. Analyses were conducted at the PT and SOC levels; Lower-level terms (LLTs) were not considered, as they map directly to PTs and their inclusion may result in data sparsity and unstable risk estimates, thereby reducing reproducibility [15, 16].

Variable definitions

Patient-level demographic and clinical variables were extracted from the structured fields of the VAERS database. Age at vaccination was categorized into six discrete intervals: 0 to 2 years, 2 to 3 years, 3 to 6 years, 6 to 12 years, 12 to 18 years, and over 18 years. This stratification was designed to capture key developmental and immunological stages relevant to the post-marketing safety evaluation of PROQUAD. The 0–2-year subgroup represents infants with the highest vulnerability to febrile seizures following MMRV immunization, as consistently described in clinical trials and post-marketing surveillance reports. The 2–3-year and 3–6-year strata encompass the standard second-dose window and early childhood period, while the 6–12-year and 12–18-year ranges reflect older pediatric populations occasionally receiving delayed or catch-up immunizations. Reports involving individuals aged 18 years or older were retained to capture potential off-label use and adult exposures. This age classification was consistently applied across subgroup analyses to enhance interpretability while preserving clinical relevance. Records with missing, implausible, or extreme age values were excluded from stratified evaluations but retained for aggregate signal detection.

Sex was recorded as a binary variable and used without further transformation. Vaccination date and symptom onset date were used to compute the post-vaccination onset interval, defined as the number of days between vaccine administration and the appearance of the first reported adverse event. Records with missing or non-positive onset intervals were excluded from time-to-onset evaluation but retained for disproportionality analyses.

All date variables were harmonized to a uniform format to support consistency across derived calculations. Variable definitions and data transformation procedures were applied uniformly throughout the dataset to maintain internal validity and reproducibility of subgroup analyses.

Clinical review of detected signals

All Preferred Terms (PTs) identified through disproportionality analysis underwent structured clinical evaluation to determine their alignment with established safety information and medical relevance. This process aimed to interpret statistical signals within pharmacovigilance frameworks, distinguishing expected vaccine reactions from those requiring further review.

Each statistically significant PT was compared with adverse events documented in the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA) product information for PROQUAD. Events listed in either label were regarded as expected, while unlabeled PTs were flagged as potential new signals, particularly when associated with high disproportionality or vulnerable subgroups.

Medical importance was assessed according to the FDA definition of serious adverse events, including death, life-threatening illness, hospitalization, disability, and congenital anomalies. Causality was considered based on temporal association with vaccination, biological plausibility, and consistency with existing pharmacovigilance data, with serious events analyzed separately. Classification followed International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E2A guidelines, and overlapping PTs were resolved by prioritizing those with the highest reporting frequency and clearer attribution [17].

Statistical analysis

Disproportionality analysis was applied to detect adverse events disproportionately reported after PROQUAD® vaccination. For each adverse event, the number of reports following PROQUAD® was compared against the number of reports for all other vaccines in VAERS during the same study period. This reference group of non-PROQUAD vaccine reports served as the background reporting frequency and was incorporated into two-by-two contingency tables, thereby providing the denominator for the calculation of disproportionality measures [18, 19].

Four established signal detection algorithms were employed: the Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN, expressed as the Information Component, IC), and Multi-Item Gamma Poisson Shrinker (MGPS, expressed as the Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean, EBGM). ROR and PRR were derived from two-by-two contingency tables, with signals considered present when the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval for the ROR exceeded 1 (≥ 3 cases), or when PRR ≥ 2 with χ² ≥ 4 and ≥ 3 cases. For the Bayesian methods, signals were identified when IC025 >0 or EB05 >2. These thresholds were selected in accordance with established pharmacovigilance practice [20–23].

The contingency table framework, mathematical formulas, and detailed signal detection criteria for each algorithm are presented in the Supplementary Table 1.

All statistical modeling was performed in R version 4.3.2 using the “PhViD” and “openEBGM” packages. Given the exploratory nature of disproportionality signal screening, no multiplicity adjustments were applied. Each analysis was independently verified for consistency and reproducibility.

Time-to-onset analysis demonstrated that the vast majority of AEs occurred within the first 30 days post-vaccination across all age groups (Fig. 1B). Although later-onset events beyond 30 days were uncommon overall, their relative contribution modestly increased in older children and adolescents. This finding suggests that acute, short-latency reactions dominate the reporting profile, while delayed events occur less frequently.

Analysis of vaccine exposure indicated that PROQUAD was administered alone in 43.4% of reports, whereas 56.6% involved co-administration with other vaccines. Among those with available dose information, the first dose accounted for 53.6% of reports, followed by the second dose (41.0%) and subsequent doses (2.6%).

The annual distribution of reports showed substantial variability over the 11-year study period. As depicted in Fig. 2, reporting peaked in 2017 and 2019, declined during the COVID-19 pandemic years, and subsequently stabilized before a sharp fall in 2025, which likely reflects incomplete data accrual for the most recent year.

Age-stratified disproportionality analysis

Disproportionality analyses across six age groups revealed distinct safety patterns (Fig. 5 and Supplementary Table 3). In the 0–2-year group, febrile convulsions showed the strongest signal (ROR 6.13), with additional reports of hyperpyrexia and generalized rash, reflecting systemic responses to early vaccination.

Fig. 5Age-specific adverse events with significantly elevated RORs following PROQUAD vaccination, highlighting variations in AE profiles across different age groups

Age-specific adverse events with significantly elevated RORs following PROQUAD vaccination, highlighting variations in AE profiles across different age groups

Children aged 3–6 years predominantly exhibited local injection-site reactions such as erythema, swelling, and warmth, with moderate signal strength (RORs 1.9–2.2). In the 2–3-year stratum, “Measles post vaccine” (ROR 36.01) and “Measles” (ROR 25.8) accounted for the highest signals, likely influenced by coding practices and the narrow vaccination window.

Adolescents showed few events, limited to cellulitis and injection-site erythema, while in adults (≥ 18 years), “Varicella post vaccine” (ROR 165.77) dominated the profile, probably reflecting rare off-label use or reporting bias.

Some nonspecific events, including pyrexia and rash, appeared across multiple groups, indicating background vaccine reactogenicity rather than age-specific vulnerability.

Subgroup analysis of serious and fatal events

Serious reports were mainly driven by febrile convulsion (ROR 20.13, 95% CI: 15.59–25.99), irritability (ROR 11.97, 95% CI: 8.27–17.32), and petechiae (ROR 11.96, 95% CI: 8.70–16.45), with nervous system and psychiatric disorders as the predominant SOCs (Supplementary Fig. 1). By contrast, non-serious cases (Supplementary Fig. 2) largely reflected injection-site reactions such as erythema, rash, and swelling, consistent with expected vaccine reactogenicity. Sex-specific analyses showed overlapping patterns, though some variation was noted. Pyrexia, rash, and febrile convulsion occurred in both sexes, while hyperpyrexia and immune thrombocytopenia were more frequent in females, and vomiting and petechiae in males (Supplementary Fig. 3). These differences may indicate biological variation or reporting bias.

Fatal cases demonstrated a distinct distribution compared with serious but non-fatal events. The strongest fatal signals were influenza-like illness (ROR 25.74, 95% CI: 9.47–69.98), cyanosis (ROR 12.97, 95% CI: 4.12–40.80), and diarrhoea (ROR 5.13, 95% CI: 1.90–13.83), spanning general disorders, vascular, and gastrointestinal systems (Fig. 8). Unlike serious non-fatal outcomes, neurologic syndromes were not prominent among fatal reports, suggesting that mortality may follow a separate pathophysiological trajectory in rare post-vaccination cases.

Time-to-onset analysis of adverse events

Time-to-onset assessment demonstrated that adverse events were overwhelmingly concentrated in the early post-vaccination period, with 89.1% reported on the day of administration and 97.6% within the first three days (Fig. 6). This temporal clustering underscores the predominance of acute, short-latency reactions following PROQUAD.

Discussion

This study offers a decade-long pharmacovigilance evaluation of the PROQUAD vaccine using VAERS database, yielding findings that reinforce several well-established safety characteristics while also revealing previously unrecognized adverse event patterns that warrant additional evaluation. Local injection site reactions, such as erythema, swelling, and warmth, emerged as the most frequently reported adverse events, consistent with immunologic expectations for a live attenuated combination vaccine. These observations align closely with prelicensure clinical trial data and the approved prescribing information, supporting the reproducibility and reliability of these reactions in post-marketing surveillance.

Fatal adverse events most disproportionately reported following PROQUAD vaccination

At a broader system organ class level, the preponderance of events categorized under general disorders and administration site conditions underscores the continued predominance of acute immunoreactivity in PROQUAD recipients. Systemic manifestations such as pyrexia and rash, which featured prominently in the disproportionality analysis, further substantiate the characterization of the vaccine’s reactogenic profile as transient and clinically mild, consistent with previous observational studies. This consistency across data sources affirms the validity of current clinical expectations and risk communication strategies related to these common post-vaccination reactions.

While passive reporting systems such as VAERS remain indispensable for early signal generation, they are inherently constrained by underreporting, variability in data quality, and the absence of reliable denominator information, leading to underestimation of true incidence rates. Therefore, active and enhanced surveillance systems are increasingly recognized as essential complements to spontaneous reporting, as they allow for more precise quantification of event rates and faster detection of rare or unexpected safety signals. Evidence from comparative pharmacovigilance programs demonstrates that active surveillance consistently identifies higher adverse event rates and facilitates earlier signal validation, thereby improving the responsiveness and completeness of post-marketing safety monitoring [24, 25].

Nonetheless, several statistically significant signals were identified that are not currently included in the U.S. FDA or EMA product labeling. Of particular concern is the strong signal for febrile convulsion, predominantly reported in children younger than three years of age. This event yielded one of the highest reporting odds ratios among serious outcomes and showed a distinct temporal aggregation between 5 and 12 days after vaccination, a time window previously correlated with seizure occurrence in controlled investigations [26, 27]. Although febrile seizures following MMRV vaccination are well recognized in prior clinical and epidemiologic studies, their omission from the current PROQUAD label raises important regulatory considerations, especially given the reproducibility of this signal across both severity and age-specific strata. The temporal concentration of these events strengthens biological plausibility and highlights the need to re-examine whether the existing product information accurately represents the risk spectrum observed in real-world pediatric practice.

Beyond convulsions, other unlisted but clinically significant adverse events, including hyperpyrexia, petechiae, and cyanosis, were observed with elevated disproportionality estimates. The distinction of hyperpyrexia from general pyrexia lies in its association with more severe clinical outcomes, as evidenced by its predominance among serious reports and its sex-specific concentration among female recipients [28]. Similarly, petechiae, although reported infrequently, exhibited a disproportionately higher signal in male children and were classified among serious adverse events, indicating potential hematologic or immunologic involvement. The appearance of cyanosis in fatal reports, although infrequent, merits particular attention because of its potential association with cardiorespiratory dysfunction or immune-mediated hypoxia [29, 30]. The absence of concurrent signals for anaphylaxis or bronchospasm suggests that these cases may involve alternate pathophysiologic pathways, possibly related to post-immunization immune dysregulation or inflammatory cascades in predisposed individuals. While these findings cannot establish causality, their repetition in fatal clusters underscores the importance of systematic causality assessment and multidisciplinary review in post-marketing safety evaluation.

Also noteworthy is the recurrent reporting of “influenza-like illness” among death-associated cases. Although this term is inherently nonspecific, its consistent documentation in mortality-linked entries suggests an emerging pattern that warrants targeted clinical investigation and enhanced follow-up through active surveillance frameworks. Whether such presentations reflect vaccine-related immune suppression, viral co-infection, or diagnostic miscoding remains uncertain; however, the convergence of such reports across fatal outcomes points to a gap in current understanding of rare but severe post-vaccination trajectories. This further illustrates the limitations of passive reporting systems such as VAERS, in which spontaneous submissions often fail to capture complete clinical evolution or confirmatory diagnostic data. Integration of active pharmacovigilance platforms, including sentinel site monitoring and electronic health record linkage, could improve early detection of atypical clinical syndromes and strengthen real-time signal validation.

The detection of “measles post vaccine” and “vaccination failure,” particularly in the 2–3-year age group, introduces additional complexity to the safety interpretation. Although infrequent, these signals may indicate potential cold chain deviations or incomplete seroconversion, particularly in resource-limited settings where strict temperature control may be difficult to maintain. The markedly elevated reporting odds ratios in this narrow age interval could reflect suboptimal immune priming or interference from residual maternal antibodies, although reporting bias and diagnostic miscoding remain plausible explanations. Their identification underscores the value of immunologic monitoring and serologic follow-up as complementary tools to confirm vaccine effectiveness and safety in transitional pediatric populations. Moreover, the inclusion of such real-world immunogenicity data within pharmacovigilance systems may facilitate earlier recognition of potential programmatic or operational gaps that affect vaccine performance.

The age-stratified signal landscape observed in this study further illustrates the biological diversity of vaccine responses across developmental stages. Infants under two years of age exhibited significantly higher frequencies of serious adverse events, particularly febrile convulsion and hyperpyrexia, suggesting greater vulnerability to systemic inflammatory responses during early neurodevelopment. In contrast, children aged three to six years demonstrated a predominance of local injection-site reactions, which is consistent with secondary immune activation typical of booster dosing. This differentiation highlights the importance of age-adapted post-vaccination observation and counseling, particularly for infants in whom short-lived systemic symptoms may cause clinical uncertainty or parental anxiety. Integrating such stratified analyses into routine safety monitoring may enable more personalized communication and risk mitigation in pediatric immunization programs.

Among adolescents and adults, adverse event reports were less frequent but displayed distinctive features. The elevated signal for “varicella post vaccine” in individuals over 18 years is especially noteworthy given that PROQUAD is not approved for adult administration. This finding likely reflects off-label administration or incomplete immunization histories, although inadvertent administration in immunocompromised adults cannot be excluded. Regardless of causation, these observations reinforce the importance of adherence to age-appropriate recommendations and emphasize that pharmacovigilance activities should extend beyond routine pediatric cohorts to capture off-label or exceptional use. Active post-marketing surveillance and transparent communication between healthcare providers and regulatory authorities are essential to maintain vaccine confidence and to strengthen adherence to complete vaccination schedules and booster doses [25, 31].

In addition to age-related variation, sex-specific differences in adverse event profiles warrant further scrutiny. Immune thrombocytopenia and hyperpyrexia were found to occur more frequently in female recipients, whereas petechiae and vomiting appeared more often in males. These sex-linked variations likely arise from both biological dimorphism in immune activation and behavioral differences in reporting, as women and caregivers of female children have been shown to engage more frequently with post-vaccination monitoring systems. Incorporating sex-stratified analyses into post-marketing surveillance therefore enables regulatory authorities and public health agencies to interpret these findings with greater nuance and to design gender-sensitive communication strategies that enhance transparency and vaccine confidence.

The temporal pattern of adverse event onset provides additional insight into the biological dynamics of PROQUAD-associated events. Most cases were reported within the first three days after vaccination, with a median onset of zero days. While serious outcomes displayed a longer latency, particularly among infants. This divergence implies that acute events largely reflect expected reactogenic responses, whereas delayed-onset manifestations may result from complex immune or inflammatory cascades. Such delayed reactions should not be dismissed simply because they fall outside standard observation windows. This observation underscores the need for extended pharmacovigilance follow-up and structured causality assessment frameworks, involving multidisciplinary stakeholder participation, to ensure that serious delayed events are accurately attributed.

Our study carries practical implications for clinicians and regulators. For healthcare providers, identifying both labeled and unlabeled adverse events supports more individualized risk communication and informed clinical management. From a public health perspective, transparent post-marketing communication and the visible engagement of stakeholders, including regulators, manufacturers, and community health organizations, are essential to sustain vaccine confidence and encourage adherence to full immunization schedules. Educational and organizational strategies that foster transparent dialogue between healthcare providers and the public, supported by consistent stakeholder feedback mechanisms, have been shown to improve public trust and strengthen adherence to vaccination programs [25, 32].

These findings also highlight a broader limitation inherent to passive surveillance systems such as VAERS. Because reporting is spontaneous and voluntary, incidence and occurrence of vaccine-related events are likely underestimated, while reporting bias and incomplete clinical documentation restrict causal inference. Active or enhanced surveillance models have demonstrated superior capacity to identify rare adverse events and verify causality in near real time [33]. Future integration of passive and active monitoring would allow more precise estimation of true event frequencies and earlier detection of emerging safety concerns.

From a regulatory standpoint, our findings reinforce the importance of continuous, evidence-based label revision and systematic causality evaluation. The recurrence of unlabeled but medically significant events, such as febrile convulsion and hyperpyrexia, should prompt reassessment of current product information. Nevertheless, these signals do not alter the established benefit–risk balance of PROQUAD, which remains favorable in preventing highly contagious and potentially severe viral infections in children. Ongoing integration of real-world evidence, strengthened stakeholder collaboration, and expansion of active surveillance mechanisms will be crucial to ensure that safety profiles remain current, credible, and publicly trusted.

Conclusions

This study provides a comprehensive real-world safety evaluation of the PROQUAD vaccine using VAERS data from 2015 to 2025. Most adverse events were consistent with known reactogenicity, including injection site reactions and fever, which aligns with prelicensure findings and the current product label. However, several previously unlisted but clinically significant signals, such as febrile convulsion, cyanosis, and influenza-like illness, were identified, particularly in specific age groups or outcome categories. These findings underscore the continued need for vigilant post-marketing surveillance and suggest that select adverse events may warrant further investigation or regulatory review. By integrating large-scale passive surveillance with signal detection analytics, this study contributes meaningful evidence to guide risk communication, clinical vigilance, and future vaccine safety assessments.

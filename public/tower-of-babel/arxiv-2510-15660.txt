Depth of powers of integrally closed edge ideals of edge-weighted paths Thanks: * Corresponding author. Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13B22, 13F20; Secondary 05C99, 05E40 Thanks: Keywords: Depth, edge-weighted graph, edge ideal, integrally closed path
Jiaxin Li, Thanh Vu, Guangjun Zhu*

Abstract
. We compute the depth of powers of edge ideals of integrally closed edge-weighted paths.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite, simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the quadratic monomial ideal in
S={\mathbb{K}}[V(G)]
generated by all monomials
xy
for each edge
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
. According to a celebrated theorem by Trung
[
16
]
, if
G
is a connected simple graph, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{depth}(S/I(G)^{t})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }G\text{ is bipartite},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In this work, we show that this phenomenon does not generally hold for edge ideals of edge-weighted paths. In general, it is known from a result of Brodmann
[
1
]
that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
is eventually constant for any homogeneous ideal
I
in a polynomial ring
S
. Nonetheless, computing the depth of the powers of a given ideal
I
remains a challenging problem, and the behavior of the depth function of powers of homogeneous ideals can be wild
[
5
]
. Even when
I
is the edge ideal of a simple graph, exact values are known only for a few specific classes, such as cycles, starlike trees
[
12
]
, and Cohen-Macaulay trees
[
6
]
.

Suppose
{\bf w}:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is a weight function on the edges of
G
, and we write
G_{\bf w}
for the pair
(G,{\bf w})
. We refer to
G_{\bf w}

as an
edge-weighted graph
with underlying graph
G
. The
edge ideal
of
G_{\bf w}
, introduced in
[
14
]
, is defined as

I(G_{\bf w})=\left(x^{{\bf w}(xy)}y^{{\bf w}(xy)}\mid\{x,y\}\in E(G)\right).

In
[
9
]
, Li, Trung, and Zhu characterized the Cohen-Macaulay property for all powers of edge ideals of
G_{\bf w}
when
G
is a tree with a perfect matching of pendant edges. In
[
19
]
, Zhu, Li, Cui, and Yang derived exact formulas for the depth of powers of edge ideals for integrally closed weighted cycles. In
[
18
]
, Zhu, Duan, Cui, and Li established exact formulas for the depth of powers of edge ideals of weighted star graphs and obtained lower bounds for the depth of integrally closed edge-weighted paths. In this work, we prove that these lower bounds from
[
18
]
are sharp.

For simplicity, let
{\mathbf{w}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1})
be a list of
n-1
positive integers. We denote by
I(P({\mathbf{w}}))
the ideal

I(P({\mathbf{w}}))=\left((x_{1}x_{2})^{w_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{w_{n-1}}\right).

By
[
3
,
17
]
,
I(P({\mathbf{w}}))
is integrally closed if and only if
{\mathbf{w}}
has at most two entries greater than one, referred to as non-trivial weights. Furthermore, if
{\mathbf{w}}
has exactly two non-trivial weights, they must occur in positions
a
and
a+2
for some
a
. In this article, we compute the depth of powers of the ideal
I(P({\mathbf{w}}))
when it is integrally closed. Our main results are as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{w}}=(1,\ldots,1,\omega,1,\ldots,1)
, where
\omega>1
appears in the
a
-th position, for some
1\leq a\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Then, for any
t\geq 2
,

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P({\mathbf{w}}))^{t})=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\right\rceil,\,1\right\},&\text{if }a=1,\text{ or }t=2,a\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{3}\text{\ and\ }n\equiv 2\!\!\!\!\pmod{4},\\[5.0pt]
\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\right\rceil,\,1\right\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{w}}=(1,\ldots,1,\gamma,1,\delta,1,\ldots,1)
, where
\gamma>1
and
\delta>1
appear at positions
a
and
a+2
, respectively, for some
1\leq a\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
. Then, for any
t\geq 2
,

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P({\mathbf{w}}))^{t})=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\right\rceil,\,2\right\},&\text{if }a=1,\\[5.0pt]
\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\right\rceil,\,2\right\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular, the limit depth is
2
when
{\mathbf{w}}
has two non-trivial weights. Section
2
provides the basic facts and notation used throughout the paper. Section
3
proves Theorem
1.1
.Section
4
proves Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we let
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, and let
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
S
. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, the depth of
L
is defined as follows:

\operatorname{depth}(L)=\min\{i\mid H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(L)\neq 0\}.

Here
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
. We use the following results throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
7
, Proposition 1.2.2]
)

Let
I
and
J
be two monomial ideals. Then,
I:J
is a monomial ideal, and

I:J=\bigcap_{v\in G(J)}I:(v).

Furthermore, the set
\{u/\gcd(u,v):u\in G(I)\}
is a set of minimal generators of
I:(v)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
15
, Corollary 1.3]
) Let
I
be a monomial ideal and let
f
be a monomial such that
f\notin I
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\leq\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:f)).

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Let
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
S_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{m+1},\dots,x_{n}]
be two polynomial rings over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Let
S=S_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}S_{2}
and
I\subset S_{1}
,

J\subset S_{2}
be two non-zero homogeneous ideals. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I+J))=\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/I)+\operatorname{depth}(S_{2}/J)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
13
, Lemma 2.8]
) Let
P_{n}
be a path with
n
vertices. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{n}))=\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil.

We now state the following simple lemma, which generalizes
[
18
, Lemma 4.10]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph. Assume that
e=\{x_{n-1},x_{n}\}
is a leaf edge of
G
with a trivial weight, that is,
x_{n}
has a unique neighbor in
G
, namely
x_{n-1}
, and
{\mathbf{w}}(e)=1
. Then, for all
t\geq 2
, we have

I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}:(x_{n-1}x_{n})=I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t-1}.

Proof.

Clearly, the left-hand side contains the right-hand side. According to Lemma
2.1
, it is sufficient to prove that if
f
is a minimal generator of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}
, then

g=f/\gcd(f,x_{n-1}x_{n})\in I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t-1}.

Indeed, we can write
f=f_{1}\cdots f_{t}
, where each
f_{i}
is a minimal generator of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
. If
x_{n}
divides
f_{j}
for some
j
, then
f_{j}=x_{n-1}x_{n}
, since
x_{n}
is a leaf of
G
and
x_{n-1}
is its unique neighbor. Hence,

g=f/\gcd(f,x_{n-1}x_{n})=(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{i})(\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}f_{i})\in I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t-1}.

Now, assume that
x_{n}
does not divide
f_{j}
for any
j=1,\ldots,t
. Then
\gcd(f,x_{n-1}x_{n})
is either
1
or
x_{n-1}
. In the first case,
g=f\in I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}
. In the second case, there exists some
j
such that
f_{j}
is divisible by
x_{n-1}
; therefore,

g\text{ is divisible by }(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{i})(\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}f_{i}).

The conclusion follows.

\square

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph. Assume that
G_{\mathbf{w}}
has a leaf with a trivial weight. Then, for all
t\geq 1
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t})\geq\operatorname{depth}(S/I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t+1})
.

Proof.

This conclusion follows from Lemmas
2.5
and
2.2
.

\square

By the results of
[
18
]

n\geq 4

Lemma 2.7

Let

\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\right\rceil,\,1\right\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
{\mathbf{w}}=(1,\ldots,1,\gamma,1,\delta,1,\ldots,1)
be a weight sequence with the only two non-trivial weights
\gamma>1
and
\delta>1
, at the positions

and

I=I(P({\mathbf{w}}))

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\geq\begin{cases}\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\right\rceil,\,2\right\},&\text{if }a=1,\\[5.0pt]
\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\right\rceil,\,2\right\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

A graph
H
is said to be
bipartite
if there exists a decomposition of
V(H)
into two disjoint subsets,
X
and
Y
, such that
E(H)\subseteq X\times Y
.
If
E(H)=X\times Y
, then
H
is called a
complete bipartite graph
and is denoted by
K_{X,Y}
.
We recall the notion of
bipartite completion
introduced in
[
12
]
.

Let
G
be a simple graph, and let
\mathbf{e}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\}
be a collection of edges of
G
, and let
N[\mathbf{e}]

denote the
closed neighborhood
of
\mathbf{e}
in
G
, i.e., the set of vertices that are in
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
or are neighbors of some vertex in
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
.
Let
G[\mathbf{e}]
and
G[\overline{\mathbf{e}}]
denote the induced subgraphs of
G
on the sets
N[\mathbf{e}]
and
V(G)\setminus N[\mathbf{e}]
), respectively.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
G
be a simple graph, and let
\mathbf{e}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\}
a collection of edges of
G
.
Assume that
G[\mathbf{e}]
is bipartite with bipartition
N[\mathbf{e}]=U\cup V
.
The
bipartite completion
of
G
with respect to
\mathbf{e}
, denoted by
\widetilde{G[\mathbf{e}]}
, is the graph obtained from
G
by adding all edges
\{u,v\}
for
u\in U
and
v\in V
.

By the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have the following.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be a simple graph such that
G[\mathbf{e}]
is bipartite and
G[\overline{\mathbf{e}}]
is weakly chordal,
i.e., both
G[\overline{\mathbf{e}}]
and its complement have no induced cycles of length greater than
4
, and
\mathbf{e}
is connected when viewed as a subgraph of
G
. Then

\operatorname{depth}\left(S/I(\widetilde{G[\mathbf{e}]})\right)\leq 1+\operatorname{depth}\left(R/I(G[\overline{\mathbf{e}}])\right),

where
R
is the polynomial ring in the variables corresponding to
G[\overline{\mathbf{e}}]
.

3.
The case of a weight sequence with one non-trivial weight

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
. We establish the following notation: Given a non-zero monomial
f\in S
, its
support
, denoted by
\operatorname{supp}(f)
, is the set of indices
j
for which
x_{j}
divides
f
, and its largest index in
\operatorname{supp}(f)
is denote by
\operatorname{last}(f)
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, let
\deg_{j}(f)
denote the exponent of
x_{j}
in
f
.

In the following, we compute colon ideals of the form
I^{t}:f
. First, we make some general observations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph. Assume that
x_{n}
is a leaf of
G
with its unique neighbor
x_{n-1}
.
Let
f
be a non-zero monomial of
S
such that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\cap\{n-1,n\}=\emptyset
.
Then, for all
t\geq 1
, we have

I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}:f\subseteq(I(H_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}:f)\;+\;I(G_{\mathbf{w}}),

where
H
is the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus\{x_{n}\}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.1
,
we can assume that

h\;=\;\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}g_{i}}{\gcd(\prod_{i=1}^{t}g_{i},f)}

is a minimal generator of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}:f
, where
g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}
are minimal generators of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
.
We can assume that
\operatorname{last}(g_{1})\leq\cdots\leq\operatorname{last}(g_{t})
.

If
\operatorname{last}(g_{t})=n
, then
g_{t}\;=\;(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{{\mathbf{w}}(x_{n-1}x_{n})}
. In particular,
\operatorname{supp}(g_{t})\cap\operatorname{supp}(f)=\emptyset
, and thus
h
is divisible by
g_{t}
. Therefore,
h\in I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
.

If
\operatorname{last}(g_{t})<n
, then
g_{i}\in I(H_{\mathbf{w}})
for all
i=1,\ldots,t
, so
h\in I(H_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}:f
. The conclusion follows.

\square

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph, and let
{\mathbf{e}}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t-1}\}
be a collection of edges of
G
.
Assume that
{\mathbf{e}}
is connected when viewed as a subgraph of
G
, and that
G[{\mathbf{e}}]
is bipartite.
Assume that the weight function
{\mathbf{w}}
on the edges in the closed neighborhood of
{\mathbf{e}}
is trivial.
Then

I(G_{\mathbf{w}})^{t}:\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}e_{i}\;=\;I\bigl(\widetilde{G[{\mathbf{e}}]}_{{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}}\bigr),

where the weight function
{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}
is the extension of
{\mathbf{w}}
to the edges of
\widetilde{G[{\mathbf{e}}]}
and assigns trivial weights to all new edges.

Proof.

According to
[
6
, Lemma 2.7]
, the left-hand side contains the right-hand side.
Denote

\operatorname{supp}({\mathbf{e}})\;=\;\operatorname{supp}(e_{1})\cup\cdots\cup\operatorname{supp}(e_{t-1}).

According to Lemma
2.1
, we can assume that

h\;=\;\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}g_{i}}{\gcd(\prod_{i=1}^{t}g_{i},f)},

where
f=e_{1}\cdots e_{t-1}
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}
are minimal generators of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
.

If
\operatorname{supp}(g_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}({\mathbf{e}})=\emptyset
for some
i
, then
h
is divisible by
g_{i}
and thus
h\in I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
.
Therefore, we can assume that
\operatorname{supp}(g_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}({\mathbf{e}})\neq\emptyset
for all
i=1,\ldots,t
.
In this case,
h
belongs to
J^{t}:f
, where
J
is the restriction of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
to the closed neighborhood of
{\mathbf{e}}
, and the
conclusion follows from
[
6
, Lemma 2.7]
.

\square

Let
u_{j}=x_{j}x_{j+1}
for any
j=1,\ldots,n-1
. Let
{\mathbf{w}}=(1,\ldots,1,\omega,1,\ldots,1)
be a sequence of
n-1
weights, with a unique non-trivial weight
\omega
, at the
a
-th position,
where
1\leq a\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Define

I=I(P({\mathbf{w}}))=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{a-1},u_{a}^{\omega},u_{a+1},\ldots,u_{n-1}).

We now distinguish several classes of monomials and study the corresponding quotients of powers of
I
. By convention, we can assume that
\prod\limits_{i=p}^{q}u_{i}=1
if

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
2\leq t\leq n-a-1
. Let

f_{a,t}\;=\;x_{a+2}\,u_{a}^{\omega}\prod\limits_{i=a+2}^{a+t-1}u_{i}.

Then, for any

, we have

I^{t}:f_{a,t}\;=\begin{cases}I+(\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{t+2}\{x_{i}\}),&\text{if $a=1$,}\\
I\;+\;(x_{a-1})\,+\,(\bigcup\limits_{i=a+1}^{a+t+1}\{x_{i}\}),&\text{if $a\geq 2$.}\\
\end{cases}

In particular,

\operatorname{depth}\!\left(S/(I^{t}:f_{a,t})\right)\;=\;1\;+\;\left\lceil\frac{a-2}{3}\right\rceil\;+\;\left\lceil\frac{n-t-a-1}{3}\right\rceil.

Let
U
and

\displaystyle I^{t}:f_{a,t}

\displaystyle\supseteq\begin{cases}I+(\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{t+2}\{x_{i}\}),&\text{if $a=1$,}\\

It remains to prove the reverse inclusion

Lemma 3.4

Assume that

Remark 4.4

If

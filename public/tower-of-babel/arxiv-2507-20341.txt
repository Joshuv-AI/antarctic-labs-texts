Structure of (Fine) Mordell–Weil Groups
Rusiru Gambheera, Debanjana Kundu

Abstract
. In this article we study the algebraic structure of fine Mordell–Weil groups and plus/minus Mordell–Weil groups in the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of number fields. We provide refinements of previously known results and also prove some equivariant results. In particular, we are able to give stronger results even when the base field is ℚ \mathbb{Q} .

Primary: 11R23

1.
Introduction

In the study of rational points on Abelian varieties, the Selmer group plays an important role.
In analogy with the work of K. Iwasawa on growth of class groups in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions, B. Mazur introduced the study of Selmer groups of elliptic curves in such extensions in
[
Maz72
]
, by exploiting the intimate connection between class groups and Selmer groups.
More recently, in
[
CS05
]
, J. Coates–R. Sujatha initiated the systematic study of a subgroup of the classical Selmer group called the
fine Selmer group
.
They showed that the fine Selmer group has stronger finiteness properties than the classical Selmer group.
In fact, it ‘interpolates’ between the classical Selmer group and the class group.

In this article, we study refinements of the structure of (fine) Mordell–Weil groups over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
and more general number fields.
Throughout we consider elliptic curves
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
with good reduction at an odd prime
p
.

Main Results

We will now highlight some of the main results that we prove in this paper.

Structure of fine Mordell–Weil groups

A key advantage of studying the fine Selmer group is that it approximates the ideal class group better than the classical Selmer group
and
the results can often be stated uniformly independent of the reduction type.
In particular, analogous to the classical Iwasawa
\mu=0
conjecture it is expected (see
[
CS05
, Conjecture A]
) that the dual fine Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{0}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.
The fine Mordell–Weil group introduced by C. Wuthrich
[
Wut07
]
plays a key role in studying the fine Selmer group.
A primary goal in this paper is to understand the algebraic structure of fine Mordell–Weil groups in more detail.
By
[
Lei23
, Theorem 3.3]
, we know that the
\Lambda
-module structure of the fine Mordell–Weil group is

\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle\Phi_{c_{i}}(x)\rangle}\text{ for some integers }c_{i},u.

Here,
\Phi_{k}(x)
denotes the
p^{k}
-cyclotomic polynomial.
On the other hand, our goal is to count the
number of times
each
\Phi_{k}(x)
arises in the structure of
\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}
.

In the following result, we have technical notation whose explicit description will be introduced in Definition
4.7
.
Intuitively,
e_{n}
is an indicator of whether there is rank jump from the
(n-1)
-th layer to the
n
-th layer of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
.
Whereas,

measures the growth of
p
-adic image, due to the rank growth at the
n

Theorem A
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian extension with Galois group
\mathsf{G}
such that
K\cap\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}=\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\#\operatorname{Ann}(\mathsf{G})=m
and
p\geq 3
be a fixed odd prime which does not split in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with good reduction at
p
.
Suppose that the
p
-primary fine Shafarevich–Tate group
\Zha(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for each
n
.
Then the
\Lambda
-structure of the dual fine Selmer group is given by

\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\langle\Phi_{n}(x)\rangle}\right)^{e_{n}-\theta_{n}}.

In particular, the characteristic ideal of the dual fine Mordell–Weil group is given by

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee})=\Bigg\langle\prod_{n\geq 0}(\Phi_{n}(x))^{e_{n}-\theta_{n}}\Bigg\rangle.

Subsequently, we also study an equivariant refinement of this above result in Theorem
4.13

\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}
as a
\Lambda[\mathsf{G}]
-module.

Generalization of a problem of Kurihara–Pollack

When
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
has good
supersingular
reduction at
p
with
a_{p}(\mathsf{E})=0
, M. Kurihara and R. Pollack posed the following problem in
[
KP07a
, Problem 3.2]

(KP)

\gcd(L_{p}^{+},L_{p}^{-})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\left(x^{e_{0}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>0\\
e_{n}>0\end{subarray}}\Phi_{n}(x)^{e_{n}-1}\right).

where
L_{p}^{\pm}
are Pollack’s
p
-adic
L
-functions defined in
[
Pol03
]
.
We remind the reader that the relationship between the problem posed by Greenberg and Kurihara–Pollack have been studied in
[
KP07a
, Section 3]
.
In order to provide an answer to this question, A. Lei introduced the notion of
\pm
-Mordell–Weil groups and proved that the description of
\gcd(L_{p}^{+},L_{p}^{-})
predicted by the Kurihara–Pollack problem is in equal to the greatest common divisor of the characteristic ideals of the Pontryagin duals of the plus and minus Mordell–Weil groups.
In particular, he proved in
[
Lei23
, Theorem D]
that if
\Sha(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all

then

\gcd(\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}),\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}))=\Bigg\langle x^{e_{0}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>0\\
e_{n}>0\end{subarray}}\Phi_{n}(x)^{e_{n}-1}\Bigg\rangle.

Furthermore, he explains that the answer to the Kurihara–Pollack problem is affirmative under the hypotheses that certain
\pm
Shafarevich–Tate groups are finite and that Kobayashi’s plus and minus main conjecture holds.
For the definition of these objects see Section
2.3.2
.
These results were generalized in
[
Ray23
]
to the case of signed Selmer groups and signed Shafarevich–Tate groups of abelian varieties.

Our contribution is that we prove a version of Lei’s theorem when the base field is a more general number field
K
. We note that the main result we prove also provides a refinement of
[
Lei23
, Theorem D]
when
K=\mathbb{Q}
.
In particular, we provide explicit description of the characteristic ideal of of the
\pm
Mordell–Weil group.
More precisely, we prove the following result.

.

Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
p
be a fixed prime of good

Let

be an abelian extension with Galois group
\mathsf{G}
such that
p
is unramified in

Let
\#\operatorname{Ann}(\mathsf{G})=m
such that
p
does not split in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}
.
Suppose that
a_{p}(\mathsf{E})=0
.
Let
\mathcal{M}^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})
denote the
\pm
Mordell–Weil groups over
K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
.
Further assume that

Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee})

\displaystyle=x^{e_{0}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>0\\

In particular,

\gcd\left(\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}),\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee})\right)=x^{e_{0}}\prod_{n>0}\Phi_{n}(x)^{e_{n}-\theta_{n}}.

Refinements of the structure of Selmer groups

The third theme of this article involves studying the ‘cyclotomic’ part of the dual Selmer group over the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}/\mathbb{Q}
.
We study this separately in the good ordinary and the good supersingular reduction case.
J. Lee provided precise structure of the dual Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
when
p
is a primes of good ordinary reduction.
In particular, he showed that under reasonable hypothesis on the finiteness of the Shafarevich–Tate group

\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}\sim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle g_{i}^{d_{i}}(x)\rangle}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
f_{j}\geq 2\end{subarray}}^{s}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle\Phi_{a_{j}}^{f_{j}}(x)\rangle}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{t}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle\Phi_{b_{k}}(x)\rangle}\right),

where the
g_{i}
’s are coprime to the cyclotomic polynomials.
We prove a version of this result in the case when
p
is a prime of good
supersingular
reduction.
We show that when
\pm
Shafarevich–Tate group is finite at each layer of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension (see Theorem
6.7
for the precise statement) then

\operatorname{Sel}^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}\sim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle g_{i}^{d_{i}}(x)\rangle}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
f_{j}\geq 2\end{subarray}}^{s}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle\Phi_{a_{j}}^{f_{j}}(x)\rangle}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{t}\frac{\Lambda}{\langle\Phi_{b_{k}}(x)\rangle}\right).

Then, using the work of T. Kataoka and other results proven in this paper, we are able to provide finer results on the structure of the cyclotomic part of the dual Selmer group and the dual Shafarevich–Tate group.
In particular, we prove the following result.

Theorem C
.

Fix an elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
and an odd prime
p
of good
ordinary
(resp.
supersingular
) reduction.
Suppose that
\Sha(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]

(
resp.
\Zha^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]
)
is finite for all
n
and that
\Zha(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]
is finite.
Then
a_{j}
’s are distinct.

Also, the cyclotomic-part of
\Sha(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]^{\vee}

(
resp.
\Zha^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]^{\vee}
)
is cyclic as a
\Lambda
-module.

The key takeaway of the above result is that knowing how the Mordell–Weil rank grows in
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
along with information on the
\Lambda
-characteristic ideal of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})
– which can be obtained from an
L
-function by the Iwasawa Main Conjecture – completely determines the ‘cyclotomic’ structure of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}
.

As a corollary we can also prove the following result which gives stronger evidence for the Kurihara–Pollack problem.
As compared to
[
Lei23
, Theorem D]
we need milder hypotheses.

Theorem D
.

Fix an elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
and an odd prime
p
such that
a_{p}=0
.
Suppose that
\Zha^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all
n
and that
\Zha(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]
is finite.
Then for
t\geq e_{0}

\gcd\left(\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\operatorname{Sel}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee}),\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\operatorname{Sel}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})^{\vee})\right)=\langle x^{t}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
e_{n}>1\end{subarray}}\Phi^{e_{n}-1}_{n}(x)\rangle.

If
\Zha^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]^{\Gamma}
is finite then
t=e_{0}
.

Organization

Including this introduction, this article has six section.
Section
2
is preliminary in nature where we discuss the basic notions and definitions which are used throughout this paper.
Section
3
is a section on algebraic preliminaries where we prove technical (algebraic) results that are useful in the remainder of the paper.
In Section
4
we first derive arithmetic implications of the technical results proven in the previous section.
We then prove results on the
\Lambda
-structure of fine Selmer groups and also prove equivariant results.
Section
5
focuses on the case when
p
is a prime of supersingular reduction.
In particular, we study a generalization of the Kurihara–Pollack question upon base change and provide refinement of solutions towards the original problem (over
\mathbb{Q}
).
In Section
6
, we use our earlier results to study the ‘cyclotomic’ part of the structure of the Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension when the base field is
\mathbb{Q}
.

Outlook

A possible future direction is to remove the hypotheses that we are forced to impose in the theorems that we prove.
Further understanding of
\Zha(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]
and
\Zha^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]
will be instrumental in formulating a generalized version of the problem posed by Greenberg (resp. Kurihara–Pollack) for number fields.

2.
Notation and Basic Definitions

2.1.
Iwasawa theory

Fix a prime
p
.
We write
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
to denote the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
This is the maximal totally real pro-
p
subfield of
\bigcup_{n}\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n}})
where
\zeta_{p^{n}}
denotes a primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity.
In particular, there is a tower

\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}_{(0)}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{(1)}\subset\ldots\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}

such that each
\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}
is the totally real subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n+1}})
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.
By infinite Galois theory,

\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Now, let
K
be any number field.
Then
K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}=K\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
, i.e., the compositum of
K
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
.
By definition
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, as well.
As before, we write
K_{(n)}
to denote the
n
-th layer of
K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
and write
\Gamma_{n}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{(n)}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda=\Lambda(\Gamma)
is the completed group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
.
Fix a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
; this gives an isomorphism of rings

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto 1+x.

Let
M
be a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module.
The
structure theorem of
\Lambda
-modules

[
Was97
, Theorem 13.12]
asserts that
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to a finite direct sum of cyclic
\Lambda
-modules.
In other words, there is a homomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

M\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(x))\right)

with finite kernel and cokernel.
Such a homomorphism is a
pseudo-isomorphism
.
Here,
m_{i}>0
and
f_{j}(x)
is a distinguished polynomial (i.e. a monic polynomial with non-leading coefficients divisible by
p
).
The characteristic ideal of
M
is (up to a unit) generated by the characteristic element,

f_{M}^{(p)}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p^{\sum_{i}m_{i}}\prod_{j}f_{j}(x).

For ease of notation, we often write
f(x)=f_{M}^{(p)}(x)
.
The
\mu
-invariant of
M
is defined as the power of
p
in
f_{\rm{M}}^{(p)}(x)
.
More explicitly,

\mu(M)=\mu_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }s=0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}&\textrm{ if }s>0.\end{cases}

The
\lambda
-invariant of
M
is the degree of the characteristic element, i.e.

\lambda(M)=\lambda_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\deg f_{j}(x).

2.2.
Selmer group and fine Selmer group of elliptic curves

Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is a subfield of this fixed algebraic closure,
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Throughout,
p
denotes a fixed rational odd prime.
Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve.
Fix a finite set
S
of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
containing
p
, the primes of bad reduction of
\mathsf{E}
, and the unique archimedean prime.
Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
, the maximal algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
unramified outside
S
.
For every (possibly infinite) extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
, write
G_{S}\left({L}\right)=\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/{L}\right)
.
Write
S\left(L\right)
for the set of primes of
L
above
S
.
If
L
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
w
is a place of
L
, we write
L_{w}
for its completion at
w
; when
L/\mathbb{Q}
is infinite, it is the union of completions of all finite sub-extensions of
L
.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix an elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
with good reduction at
p
.
View
\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}]
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module equipped with a continuous
G_{S}(\mathbb{Q})
-action.

(i)

For any finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
set

\displaystyle K^{1}_{v}\left(\mathsf{E}/L\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}\left(L_{w},\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}]\right),

\displaystyle J^{1}_{v}\left(\mathsf{E}/L\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}\left(L_{w},\mathsf{E}\right)[p^{\infty}],

where the direct sum is taken over all primes
w
of
L
lying above a rational prime
v
.

(ii)

For an infinite algebraic extension
\mathcal{L}/\mathbb{Q}
, define
K_{v}^{1}\left(\mathsf{E}/\mathcal{L}\right)
by taking the inductive limit of
K_{v}^{1}\left(\mathsf{E}/L\right)
over all finite extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathcal{L}
, and similarly for
J_{v}^{1}(\mathsf{E}/\mathcal{L})
.

(iii)

Let
L
be an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that is contained inside
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
, define the
p^{\infty}
-fine Selmer group
of
\mathsf{E}
over
L
as

\operatorname{Sel}_{0}(\mathsf{E}/L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L)}K^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/L)\right).

and the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group
as

\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L)}J^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/L)\right).

Since all primes are finitely decomposed in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, we will henceforth simplify notation and write
\bigoplus_{v\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})}H^{1}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}},v},\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}]\right)
in place of
\bigoplus_{v\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})}K^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}))
, and similarly for
\bigoplus_{v\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})}J^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}))
.
Since
p
is fixed, we drop it from the notation of the (fine) Selmer group.
We remind the reader that both the definitions are independent of
S
as long as
S
contains the primes above
p
, the primes of bad reduction of
\mathsf{E}
, and the archimedean prime(s).

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an algebraic extension.
The Selmer group sits inside the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(L)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\Sha(\mathsf{E}/L)[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0.

The left-most object is called the
Mordell–Weil group
of
\mathsf{E}
and the right-most object is called the
Shafarevich–Tate group
.
Analogously, the fine Selmer group also sits inside a short sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{0}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\Zha(\mathsf{E}/L)[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0.

Once again, the left-most object is referred to as the
fine Mordell–Weil group
and the right-most object is called

fine Shafarevich–Tate group
.
An equivalent definition of the fine Selmer group is the following

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{0}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\mid p}\mathsf{E}(L_{w})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

2.3.
Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves

Given a number field
K
, denote by
K_{(n)}
the
n
-th layer in the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.
Let
\mathsf{E}/K
be an elliptic curve, then (as previously explained) the Selmer group over
K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}
can be viewed as the direct limit of the Selmer groups over the number fields
K_{(n)}
in
K_{\operatorname{{cyc}}}

Lemma 5.5

The proof in

Set

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n}}

\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n}

to denote the Jacobson radical of

Analogous to

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\{P\in\hat{\mathsf{E}}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})\mid\operatorname{tr}_{n/m+1}(P)\in\hat{\mathsf{E}}(\mathfrak{m}_{m}),\text{ for }0\leq m<n\text{ and }m\text{ even }\right\},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{E}}^{-}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\{P\in\hat{\mathsf{E}}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})\mid\operatorname{tr}_{n/m+1}(P)\in\hat{\mathsf{E}}(\mathfrak{m}_{m}),\text{ for }-1\leq m<n\text{ and }m\text{ odd }\right\}.

Lemma 5.6
.

For
n,k\geq 0
,

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{n})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{n})=\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})/p^{k}.

Proof.

By
[
Kat21
, Proposition 3.10]
and the observation that
\hat{\mathsf{E}}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})
is precisely the

-part of
\mathsf{E}^{\pm}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n})
we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{E}}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathsf{E}^{\pm}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\text{ for all }n\geq 0

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{E}}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{m}_{-1})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Applying
[
Kat21
, Proposition 4.21(2)]
we see that

0\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}^{+}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus\mathsf{E}^{-}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow 0,

where the map

is given by
(x,y)\mapsto x-y
.
Note that the first map is injective because
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})[p^{\infty}]
is trivial.
It follows from the definition of the map

\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\mathsf{E}^{+}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cap\mathsf{E}^{-}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\subseteq\mathsf{E}^{+}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cap\mathsf{E}^{-}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Lemma 5.7
.

With notation introduced previously.

(i)

\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K)=\mathsf{E}(K)/p^{k}
.

(ii)

V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))[\Phi_{n}(x)]=V_{p}\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})[\Phi_{n}(x)]

Proof.

This is an analogue of
[

, Lemma 6.3]

(i)

By definition of
\mathsf{E}^{\pm}(\mathbb{K}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})
, we observe that

\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\subseteq(\mathsf{E}^{\pm}(\mathbb{K}_{\operatorname{{cyc}}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{M}^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{0}).

Now, we can take

-torsion and use Lemma

Next, we use the definition of the plus/minus Mordell–Weil group,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\ker\left(\mathsf{E}(K)/p^{k}\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{0},\mathsf{E}[p^{k}])}{\mathcal{M}_{p^{k}}^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}(K)/p^{k}.

The reverse inclusion is automatic from the definition.

(ii)

By definition of plus/minus Mordell–Weil groups

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\ker\left(\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})/p^{k}\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{n},\mathsf{E}[p^{k}])}{\mathcal{M}_{p^{k}}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{n})\cap\mathcal{M}_{p^{k}}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{K}_{n})}\right)

Writing this as an exact sequence,

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{+}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})/p^{k}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{n})/p^{k}}{\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})/p^{k}}

and taking projective limit (over

) and tensoring with

yields

0\longrightarrow V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})^{\bullet}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{n})^{\bullet}}{\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{0})^{\bullet}}.

For

, this induces the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))[\Phi_{n}(x)]\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})^{\bullet}[\Phi_{n}(x)]\xrightarrow{\eta_{n}}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{n})^{\bullet}[\Phi_{n}(x)],

where the map
\eta_{n}
is the one described in Lemma
4.6
.
On the other hand, if we reconsider (

) and take

-torsion, we obtain

0\longrightarrow V_{p}\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})[\Phi_{n}(x)]\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})^{\bullet}[\Phi_{n}(x)]\xrightarrow{\eta_{n}}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{n})^{\bullet}[\Phi_{n}(x)].

Comparing the kernel of

gives the desired result.

∎

Before we continue the discussion, we want to introduce a new notation.
We set
r_{n}^{\pm}
to be the exponent of
\frac{\Lambda}{\Phi_{n}(x)}
appearing in the pseudo-isomorphism (

Proposition 5.8
.

Keep the notation introduced previously and the assumption that
\Zha(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]
and
\Zha^{\pm}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})[p^{\infty}]
are finite for each
n
.
Then

r_{0}^{+}=r_{0}^{-}=e_{0}.

Proof.

Using Corollary

we know that for

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma

The claim follows immediately.

5.2.2.

The goal of this section is to study the sum of plus and minus Mordell–Weil groups.

Lemma 5.9
.

Fix
k,n\geq 0
.
Then quotient
\frac{\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})/p^{k}}{\mathcal{M}^{+}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})+\mathcal{M}^{-}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})}
has finite order bounded independently of
k
.

Proof.

The proof is modelled on
[
Lei23
, Lemma 6.5]
but we provide the details for the convenience of the reader.
We begin with the observation that for
\mathsf{Q}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{n})
, we have

\operatorname{tr}_{n/m+1}(\mathsf{Q})=\left(\prod_{i=m+2}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x)\right)\cdot\mathsf{Q}.

Here we are using the identification of
\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\mathcal{G}_{n}]
and
\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\mathsf{G}][x]}{\langle(1+x)^{p^{n}}-1\rangle}
obtained by sending
\gamma_{n}
to
x+1
where
\gamma_{n}
is the image of
\gamma
(the topological generator of
\Gamma
) in
\Lambda_{n}
.
Define

\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x)\text{ and }\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{+}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x).

Claim:

\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}\cdot\mathsf{Q}\in\mathsf{E}^{+}(\mathbb{K}_{n})
.

Justification:
By definition, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{tr}_{n/m+1}(\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}\cdot\mathsf{Q})\in\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{m})
where
m
is even.
We carry out the following computation when
m
is even

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{n/m+1}(\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}\cdot\mathsf{Q})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=m+2}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x)\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x)\right)\cdot\mathsf{Q}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x)\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq m+1\\
i\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{n}\Phi_{i}(x)\right)\mathsf{Q}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}_{n/m}(\mathsf{Q}^{\prime})\in\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{K}_{m}).

This proves the claim.

In exactly the same way we can now show that
\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{+}\cdot\mathsf{Q}\in\mathsf{E}^{-}(\mathbb{K}_{n})
.
Since
\gcd(\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{+},\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-})=1
, we know that there exist polynomials
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
and a positive integer
m
such that

A\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{+}+B\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}=p^{m}.

If
\mathsf{Q}\in\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})/p^{k}
for some
k
then we can further write

\mathsf{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p^{m}\mathsf{Q}=A\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{+}\mathsf{Q}+B\widetilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}\mathsf{Q}=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathsf{P}^{-}+\mathsf{P}^{+}.

Now we take localization of
\mathsf{P}^{\pm}
at
p
and note that

\operatorname{loc}_{p}(\mathsf{P}^{\pm})\in\mathsf{E}^{\pm}(\mathbb{K}_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\pm}(\mathbb{K}_{n}).

This forces that
\mathsf{P}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{p^{k}}(K_{(n)})
.
The quotient is thus bounded by
p^{m}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})
.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

With notation and assumptions introduced previously,

r_{n}^{+}+r_{n}^{-}=2e_{n}-\theta_{n}.

Proof.

This proof is adapted from
[
Lei23
, Corollary 6.7]
.
We consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{+}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{+}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\oplus\mathcal{M}^{-}_{p^{k}}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})/p^{k}

where the first map is given by diagonal embedding and the second map is given by
\mathsf{P}\oplus\mathsf{Q}\mapsto\mathsf{P}-\mathsf{Q}
.
We can take inverse limit and tensor with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for each of the objects appearing in the above exact sequence and combine it with Lemma
5.9
to obtain

0\rightarrow V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})\cap\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))\rightarrow V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))\oplus V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{-}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))\rightarrow\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})^{\bullet}\rightarrow 0.

Now using Theorem
4.9
, Corollary
5.3

r_{n}^{+}+r_{n}^{-}=s_{n}+e_{n}=(e_{n}-\theta_{n})+e_{n}=2e_{n}-\theta_{n}.\qed

and

Proposition 5.11

We prove the case when
n

It follows from the discussion in the proof of Lemma

\tilde{\omega}_{n}^{-}\cdot\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})^{\bullet}\subseteq V_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)}))\subseteq\mathsf{E}(K_{(n)})^{\bullet}.

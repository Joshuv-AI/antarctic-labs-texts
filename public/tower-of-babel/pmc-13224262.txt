Endovascular management of traumatic superficial temporal artery pseudoaneurysms: A case series and literature review
Authors: Aman Kumar Singh, Vikas Chandra Jha, Achintya Ajaya, Vivek Saran Sinha, Nitish Kumar, Sangam Jha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Traumatic pseudoaneurysms of the superficial temporal artery (STA) are uncommon extracranial vascular lesions that often present with delayed, progressive scalp swelling following head injury. Owing to their superficial location, these lesions carry a risk of rupture, rebleeding, and cosmetic deformity. Endovascular therapy has emerged as a minimally invasive alternative to surgical excision, allowing angiography-guided, goal-directed treatment.Case Description: We report two patients, a 47-year-old man and a 16-year-old boy, who presented with delayed, enlarging temporal swellings following blunt head trauma. Diagnosis was established using colour Doppler ultrasonography, computed tomography angiography, and digital subtraction angiography. Both patients underwent superselective endovascular coil embolization with complete exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm while preserving antegrade flow in the STA main trunk and distal branches. Clinical follow-up at 1 month demonstrated complete resolution of the temporal swelling, with no recurrence, neurological deficit, or vascular compromise at 1 year.Conclusion: Endovascular management of traumatic STA pseudoaneurysms allows lesion-specific, angiography-guided strategies, including intrasaccular aneurysm exclusion with preservation of the STA trunk and branches, or selective occlusion of the involved branch while maintaining flow in uninvolved arterial segments, resulting in durable clinical resolution at mid-term follow-up.

INTRODUCTION

Traumatic pseudoaneurysms of the superficial temporal artery (STA) are rare extracranial vascular lesions that typically arise following blunt or penetrating scalp trauma, surgical procedures, or iatrogenic injury.[9-11] Owing to their superficial location and delayed presentation, these lesions are frequently misdiagnosed as post-traumatic hematomas or soft-tissue swellings, particularly when initial scalp lacerations are sutured without vascular imaging.[13]

Clinically, STA pseudoaneurysms often present days to weeks after injury as a progressively enlarging, pulsatile temporal mass and carry a risk of rupture, rebleeding, and cosmetic deformity if left untreated.[7] Conventionally, surgical ligation with excision has been considered definitive management; however, surgery may be associated with visible scarring, alopecia, facial nerve injury, and patient dissatisfaction related to cosmesis.[2]

Advances in endovascular and percutaneous techniques have expanded treatment options, allowing minimally invasive aneurysm exclusion or reconstruction under angiographic guidance.[6,10-12] Endovascular therapy offers the advantage of precise lesion targeting, reduced morbidity, and the ability to tailor treatment objectives based on angiographic anatomy, including selective flow preservation in appropriate cases. Despite these advantages, the existing literature is limited to isolated case reports with heterogeneous techniques and variable follow-up, placing relatively little emphasis on angiographic decision-making or the durability of clinical resolution.[1,3,4]

We report two cases of traumatic STA pseudoaneurysms managed with endovascular coil embolization, highlighting a structured diagnostic pathway, an individualized angiographic treatment strategy, and complete clinical resolution of temporal swelling at 6-month follow-up, demonstrating procedural durability and cosmetic benefit.

CASE 1

A 47-year-old man presented with a progressively enlarging swelling over the left temporal region for 1.5 months. He had sustained a road traffic accident 2 months earlier, resulting in lacerations over the left forehead and temporal scalp that were sutured at a peripheral center. Initial non-contrast head computed tomography (CT) demonstrated an acute-on-chronic right frontoparietal subdural hematoma, which was managed conservatively. No extracranial vascular abnormality was identified at that time.

Approximately 2 weeks after injury, following suture removal, the patient noticed a small swelling over the left temporal region that gradually increased to approximately 2 × 2 cm and was associated with local pressure. On examination, the lesion was soft, mildly pulsatile, and non-tender. Colour Doppler ultrasonography demonstrated a focal outpouching arising from the left STA with turbulent bidirectional “yin–yang” flow, suggestive of a pseudoaneurysm. CT angiography confirmed a saccular pseudoaneurysm with a neck measuring approximately 4 mm and a sac measuring 1.5 × 1.5 cm.

Endovascular treatment was performed under general anesthesia through a right femoral approach. Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) demonstrated a pseudoaneurysm arising at the junction of the frontal and parietal branches of the left STA. Superselective coil embolization using Orbit Galaxy soft coils achieved complete exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm, preserving flow in the STA trunk and distal branches. The post-procedural course was uneventful. The temporal swelling completely resolved by 6 months, with no recurrence, neurological deficit, or vascular compromise. At 1-year follow-up, the STA remained patent on imaging [Figure 1].

CASE 2

A 16-year-old boy presented with a progressively enlarging swelling over the right temporal region for 1 month following blunt trauma caused by impact from a heavy object. There was no loss of consciousness, neurological deficit, or overlying skin injury. The swelling gradually increased in size and raised cosmetic concern.

On examination, the lesion was soft, non-tender, and mildly pulsatile. CT angiography demonstrated a saccular pseudoaneurysm arising from the frontal branch of the right STA, measuring approximately 5 × 9 × 9 mm, with a dome diameter of 9 mm and a neck measuring 5 mm.

Endovascular treatment was performed under general anesthesia through a right femoral approach using an 8 Fr sheath. Diagnostic angiography confirmed a pseudoaneurysm arising from the frontal branch of the STA without arteriovenous shunting. Superselective intrasaccular coil embolization achieved complete exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm.

The post-procedural course was uneventful. The temporal swelling resolved completely by 6 months. At the 1-year follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic, with no recurrence, and preserved STA patency on imaging [Figure 2].

DISCUSSION

Traumatic pseudoaneurysms of the STA represent an uncommon but clinically important sequela of scalp trauma, often characterized by delayed diagnosis and progressive enlargement.[1,13] The superficial location of the STA predisposes these lesions to secondary trauma and rupture, underscoring the importance of early recognition and definitive management.[7]

Our cases reinforce the diagnostic value of a structured imaging algorithm incorporating color Doppler ultrasonography, CT angiography, and DSA. Doppler ultrasound is a useful initial screening tool that demonstrates the characteristic yin–yang flow pattern, whereas CTA provides accurate anatomical localization.[13] DSA remains essential for definitive diagnosis and treatment planning, allowing precise delineation of STA branching anatomy and exclusion of hazardous external-to-internal carotid anastomoses before embolization.[1,7,13]

While surgical excision has historically been favored, multiple reports have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of endovascular and percutaneous techniques, including coil embolization, liquid embolic agents, and ultrasound-guided thrombin injection.[1,6,11,12] While coil embolization may involve higher upfront procedural costs compared with surgical excision, it offers several indirect advantages, including avoidance of surgical incision, reduced operative morbidity, shorter hospital stays, and improved cosmetic outcomes, particularly in cosmetically sensitive regions such as the temporal scalp. In addition, endovascular treatment avoids risks related to facial nerve injury, wound complications, and alopecia, which may carry additional long-term costs and patient dissatisfaction.[2,9]

A key contribution of the present report is the demonstration of angiography-guided, lesion-specific endovascular strategies tailored to the anatomy of the STA branches. In the first patient, the pseudoaneurysm was successfully excluded through intrasaccular coil embolization, achieving complete aneurysm occlusion while preserving antegrade flow in the STA main trunk and both frontal and parietal branches. This approach allowed definitive treatment of the lesion without compromising physiological scalp perfusion.

In contrast, the second patient demonstrated a dissecting pseudoaneurysm arising from the parietal branch of the STA, for which superselective embolization intentionally occluded the involved parietal branch, while preserving flow in the STA main trunk and the frontal branch. This selective branch sacrifice was guided by angiographic anatomy and lesion morphology, avoiding unnecessary compromise of uninvolved arterial segments.

In our cases, a transfemoral approach was intentionally selected due to its familiarity, superior catheter control, and the ability to perform comprehensive diagnostic angiography of the external carotid circulation, including assessment of potentially hazardous external-to-internal carotid anastomoses. This approach also allowed precise superselective catheterization and controlled coil deployment.

Direct STA puncture can be an effective alternative in selected cases, particularly for superficial, straight-segment lesions; however, it has limitations, including limited catheter stability, risk of local hematoma, and difficulty with coil deployment in tortuous or branch-point aneurysms.

In the present cases, dense intrasaccular coil packing was achieved under angiographic guidance until complete flow stagnation was observed, thereby minimizing the risk of recanalization. Given the small size and low-flow nature of STA pseudoaneurysms, adequate packing rather than numerical packing density metrics was prioritized, as is commonly practiced in peripheral and extracranial pseudoaneurysms.

Liquid embolic agents such as n-butyl cyanoacrylate or Onyx offer effective aneurysm occlusion but require meticulous control of polymerization, careful injection technique, and thorough understanding of flow dynamics. In STA pseudoaneurysms, particularly those supplying the scalp, there is a potential risk of non-target embolization leading to skin ischemia or necrosis.

For these reasons, and given the favorable aneurysm morphology in our patients, coil embolization was preferred to allow controlled deployment, precise exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm, and preservation of uninvolved arterial segments.

Although parent vessel or branch sacrifice has traditionally been considered acceptable in STA pseudoaneurysms, deliberate preservation of the STA trunk and uninvolved branches has been infrequently emphasized in the literature.[3,6,10,11] Our cases illustrate that both complete aneurysm exclusion with full arterial preservation and selective branch occlusion with preservation of the main trunk are feasible using modern endovascular techniques. Such tailored strategies may be particularly relevant in younger patients and in cosmetically sensitive regions, where maintaining normal scalp perfusion and avoiding ischemic or cosmetic complications are desirable.[5-11]

Importantly, both patients demonstrated progressive subsidence of the temporal swelling following endovascular treatment, with complete clinical resolution at 6-month follow-up. This resolution reflects durable aneurysm exclusion and effectively mitigates the risk of rebleeding or secondary trauma, an outcome of particular relevance for superficially located pseudoaneurysms. In addition, the absence of residual scalp deformity highlights the cosmetic advantage of endovascular therapy over open surgical approaches, consistent with prior reports[2,6,9-12] [Table 1].

CONCLUSION

Endovascular treatment offers a safe, effective, and anatomically adaptable approach for traumatic STA pseudoaneurysms. Careful angiographic evaluation enables lesion-specific strategies, including intrasaccular aneurysm exclusion while preserving the STA trunk and branches, or selective occlusion of the involved branch while maintaining flow in uninvolved arterial segments. The durable resolution of temporal swelling observed at mid-term follow-up in both patients underscores the clinical effectiveness of this tailored approach. These findings support angiography-guided endovascular management as a preferred first-line strategy for appropriately selected STA pseudoaneurysms, particularly in younger patients and cosmetically sensitive scalp regions.

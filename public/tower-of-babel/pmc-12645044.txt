Application of mobile learning system based on convolutional network technology in students’ open teaching strategies
Authors: Chang Yu, Xue Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study designs and develops a mobile learning system based on a convolutional neural network to support open teaching strategies. By integrating a temporal convolutional network (TCN), dilated causal convolution (DCC), and reinforcement learning (RL), the study proposes a TCN–DCC–RL model for personalized learning resource recommendations. The model is evaluated using the learning analytics dataset from the UK Open University. Experimental results show that the TCN–DCC–RL model achieves high performance, with an accuracy of 96.49%, F1-score of 90.57%, Mean Average Precision of 92.27%, and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@20) of 0.938. These findings demonstrate that the proposed model significantly enhances the personalization and intelligence of learning resource recommendations, offering a novel technical approach for optimizing future intelligent education systems.Keywords: CNN, Deep reinforcement learning, Recommendation system, Mobile learning, Open teachingSubject terms: Psychology, Mathematics and computing, Applied mathematics, Computational science, Computer science, Information technology, Pure mathematics, Scientific data, Software, Statistics

Introduction

Research background and motivations

With the rapid advancement of information technology, mobile learning (M-learning) has become a key innovation in education. Supported by intelligent devices and wireless networks, M-learning overcomes the spatial and temporal limitations of traditional classrooms, providing learners with a more flexible and autonomous learning environment1,2. Additionally, open teaching strategies—an important concept in modern education—emphasize student-centered approaches and promote inquiry-based, self-directed, and collaborative learning3. In practice, however, significant individual differences among students challenge traditional mobile learning systems. Limited content adaptability, rigid interaction modes, and insufficient learning feedback hinder their ability to fully support open teaching strategies.

Recent advances in deep learning have opened new opportunities for intelligent education. Convolutional neural network (CNN), in particular, has been widely applied in educational technology due to their strengths in image processing, pattern recognition, and feature extraction4. CNN-based mobile learning systems can analyze students’ learning behaviors, personalize learning pathways, and provide real-time intelligent feedback, thereby improving both learning experience and outcomes5. Exploring the integration of CNN technology into mobile learning systems and evaluating its effectiveness within open teaching strategies therefore holds substantial theoretical and practical significance.

Research objectives

The primary objective of this study is to design and develop a CNN-based mobile learning system to support open teaching strategies. By leveraging CNN’s deep feature learning capabilities, the system intelligently analyzes learners’ behavioral data and the characteristics of learning resources, enabling accurate personalized recommendations and real-time feedback. The study also evaluates the system’s effectiveness in enhancing learning outcomes, fostering self-directed learning, and improving interactive learning experiences. The findings provide both theoretical insights and practical guidance for advancing intelligent mobile learning systems.

Literature review

Review on the application of mobile learning system in education

The development of M-learning systems has progressed alongside advancements in intelligent devices, wireless communication, and cloud computing technologies, including online course platforms (e.g., MOOCs) and intelligent tutoring systems (ITS). Numerous scholars have contributed research in this area. Dehbi et al.6 investigated the perceptions of Moroccan students and teachers toward E-learning and M-learning through surveys, highlighting M-learning’s potential to enhance educational equity and accessibility. Tatang and Algarni7 integrated M-learning with open data to improve MOOC course exploration, resulting in enhanced personalization and accessibility in online learning environments. Srivastava and Bhati8 identified technology acceptance, user experience, and self-directed learning motivation as key factors influencing the adoption of M-learning, offering a theoretical foundation for mobile education system design. Lin et al.9 emphasized M-learning’s benefits in supporting personalized learning paths, automated feedback, and learning data analysis. Kochmar et al.10 proposed a data-driven personalized teaching intervention system that employed intelligent algorithms to generate adaptive learning content. Wang et al.11 demonstrated that integrating M-learning with ITS improved the quality of personalized education and fosters active learning. In another study, Wang et al.12 introduced an interpretable diagnostic framework for intelligent learning, capable of predicting learner performance within ITS environments. Ramadhan et al.13 conducted a systematic review on the integration of ITS and game-based learning, concluding that gamification elements enhanced learner engagement and motivation. Dutt and Ahuja14 examined the emotional and cognitive effects of intelligent tutoring on students with learning disabilities, finding that personalized feedback effectively reduced anxiety and improved academic performance.

In recent years, the emergence of intelligent educational technologies such as data mining and artificial intelligence (AI) has created new opportunities for advancing M-learning systems. For instance, Ahmed15 explored AI applications in M-learning, focusing on adaptive learning, intelligent recommendation systems, and automated assessment technologies. Alshurideh et al.16 reported that learners’ technology acceptance, system usability, and interactive experience significantly influenced their willingness to adopt M-learning, confirming the effectiveness of data mining and AI in predicting learning behavior. Boudine et al.17 evaluated Morocco’s “Digital Classroom” initiative in science education, noting its positive impact on student autonomy and learning outcomes. Jallad et al.18 found that AI improved learning performance and decision-making capabilities in medical M-learning systems by optimizing resource allocation and learning assessment processes.

Review on the application of deep learning technology in education

The application of deep learning technologies—particularly CNN—has steadily expanded in the field of education, encompassing areas such as learning resource recommendation and learning behavior analysis. For instance, Alshmrany19 proposed a CNN model based on Levy flight distribution, and demonstrated its effectiveness in identifying learner preferences and enhancing the accuracy of personalized teaching recommendations. Alatrash et al.20 integrated an enhanced language model with deep learning for sentiment analysis, thereby optimizing online learning recommendation systems. Poudyal et al.21 developed a hybrid two-dimensional CNN model that provided robust support for personalized learning interventions and educational resource optimization. Liu et al.22 employed neural networks to improve the adaptability of online learning resources in higher education, and showed that this approach enhanced learning efficiency and supported the development of intelligent education systems. Similarly, Li23 demonstrated that learning resource recommendation systems improved the relevance of recommended content and provided more effective resource allocation strategies for intelligent learning platforms.

In intelligent classroom environments, CNN has also been widely used in combination with computer vision technologies. Li et al.24 utilized computer vision and CNN models to effectively identify classroom interactions, improve the accuracy of instructional feedback, and support individualized education practices. Li25 applied deep learning to optimize speech and image recognition technologies, thereby improving the adaptability of teaching resources and the efficiency of classroom interactions, and contributing to the advancement of smart education. Du26 designed an AI-driven intelligent classroom capable of accurately capturing learner behavior, thereby enhancing classroom interaction quality and overall learning outcomes. Wang et al.27 introduced SLBDetection-Net, a model that integrated CNN and computer vision to perform both closed-set and open-set detection of student learning behaviors in K-12 intelligent classrooms.

Research gap and innovation of this study

Analysis of the existing literature reveals several research gaps in personalized recommendation, learning behavior analysis, and intelligent feedback within M-learning systems. Traditional approaches primarily rely on rule-based recommendation algorithms or shallow machine learning models, which struggle to capture the deep features embedded in learners’ behavioral data. As a result, these systems often suffer from limited accuracy and adaptability in delivering personalized recommendations. Furthermore, most current studies are centered on static learning environments and lack mechanisms for optimizing open and dynamic learning paths, making it difficult to support learners’ individualized exploration needs. Although CNN has been applied in educational contexts such as image processing, handwriting recognition, and voice analysis, their potential remains underexplored in interactive learning systems and context-aware learning environments. In particular, areas such as learning motivation prediction, knowledge mastery assessment, and analysis of long-term learning patterns have not been adequately addressed. To bridge these gaps, this study proposes an intelligent mobile learning system based on CNN. The system enhances the recommendation of learning resources through deep feature extraction and adaptive learning strategies. It also introduces a multimodal learning behavior analysis framework that integrates computer vision and natural language processing (NLP) to improve the intelligence and responsiveness of learning interactions. This study aims to provide a new theoretical foundation and practical direction for future educational technology research based on deep learning.

Deep learning technologies—such as CNN—enable the modeling and prediction of learners’ behavioral data, offering technical support for optimizing personalized learning pathways.

Construction and analysis of mobile learning resource recommendation model based on CNN

The primary objective of a personalized learning resource recommendation system is to deliver optimal resource matching based on students’ learning behavior data. Traditional recommendation systems often struggle with time series data; however, temporal convolutional network (TCN), combined with dilated causal convolution (DCC) in CNN architectures, can effectively capture long-term dependencies in sequential data, thereby enhancing personalized recommendations in mobile learning environments. To further improve the precision of resource matching, Reinforcement Learning (RL) is incorporated into the model.

In terms of model selection, Transformers and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown strong performance in related fields, but they exhibit specific limitations in mobile learning recommendation scenarios. Transformer models incur high computational complexity when processing long sequential data due to their self-attention mechanisms, making it difficult to meet the real-time requirements of mobile devices. GNNs, by contrast, focus on capturing structural relationships in data, which may weaken the critical temporal dependencies inherent in learning behaviors. The TCN–DCC–RL integrated architecture proposed in this study offers clear advantages. TCNs combined with DCCs efficiently capture long-term temporal features of learning sequences at a lower computational cost. The inclusion of RL allows the model to dynamically optimize recommendation strategies based on real-time student feedback. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional supervised learning models in adapting to individualized and dynamic learning paths. As a result, the integrated architecture is well-suited to the study’s application scenario, achieving a balanced combination of computational efficiency, temporal modeling accuracy, and strategy adaptability.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

This study utilizes the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/anlgrbz/student-demographics-online-education-dataoulad) as the experimental data source. The dataset contains detailed records of students’ course registrations, learning behaviors, test scores, and final exam results, providing a rich foundation for personalized learning resource recommendation. Covering multiple semesters and various courses, the dataset includes diverse learning activities (such as video watching, reading materials, and homework submission) along with corresponding timestamps, making it well-suited for time series modeling. During data preprocessing, missing values and abnormal entries were removed, including samples with incomplete courses or missing grades. Subsequently, students’ learning behavior data were organized into time-ordered sequences to meet the input requirements of the temporal convolutional network (TCN–DCC). To maintain data consistency and ensure compatibility with the N-hot encoding method used in the model, discrete variables—such as course type and learning activity category—were processed using One-Hot Encoding. Additionally, time series data were normalized using Min–Max scaling, enabling different students’ learning behaviors to be modeled on a common scale. Finally, the processed data were partitioned into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets for evaluating the model’s recommendation performance.

Experimental Environment and Parameters Setting

The experiments were conducted on a computer equipped with an Intel Core i9-13900K CPU, 32 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, running the Windows 11 operating system. Python 3.8 served as the primary programming language, while TensorFlow 2.9 and PyTorch 1.13 were employed to implement the deep learning models. To enhance computational performance, CUDA 12.1 was enabled for GPU acceleration. During hyperparameter tuning, multiple key parameters were iteratively optimized and validated to ensure the model’s convergence, stability, and generalization capability on mobile learning behavior data. The convolution kernel size was set to 3, based on the TCN’s requirement for an effective receptive field to capture short- and mid-term dependencies. A smaller kernel size would limit feature extraction, while a larger size could introduce feature overlap and instability. Empirical results indicated that a kernel size of 3 provided the optimal balance between convergence speed and predictive accuracy. The dilation rates were configured as [1, 2, 4, 8], allowing the DCC module to expand the receptive field without increasing network depth. This enabled the model to effectively capture long-range dependencies in sequential learning behavior, improving the modeling of cross-temporal relationships. Experimental results demonstrated that this configuration outperformed both linear and fixed dilation strategies. The dropout rate was set to 0.3, determined to be optimal after extensive testing within the range of 0.1 to 0.5. Lower dropout rates increased the risk of overfitting, while higher rates negatively affected model stability and representational power. The selected value of 0.3 provided consistent improvements in generalization and effectively stabilized F1-score and MAP metrics, particularly in later stages of training. The learning rate was set at 0.001, which, in conjunction with the Adam optimizer’s adaptive update mechanism and favorable gradient convergence behavior, achieved a good balance between convergence speed and prediction accuracy. Higher learning rates led to significant loss fluctuations, while lower rates slowed convergence. Comparative experiments showed that a learning rate of 0.001 enabled stable performance gains after approximately 50 epochs, with MAP and NDCG scores outperforming baseline models from the mid-training stage onward. Therefore, the hyperparameter configuration used in this study was the result of extensive cross-validation and iterative refinement, ensuring robust performance across sequential modeling, personalized recommendation, and dynamic policy optimization tasks. Performance evaluation

To more clearly demonstrate the performance advantages of the proposed TCN–DCC–RL model, four representative baseline models were selected for comparative experiments: TCN28, DCC29, DenseNet30, and the educational recommendation model proposed by Wang et al. (2025). The TCN model is a time series modeling approach based on one-dimensional convolution, characterized by residual connections and causal convolution. It can effectively capture long-term dependencies in time series data but has limited capacity for modeling nonlinear behavioral patterns. The DCC model extends the receptive field by introducing dilation rates into traditional convolutional networks, making it suitable for long-sequence modeling. However, it lacks a feedback mechanism, making it difficult to dynamically optimize recommendation strategies based on students’ evolving behaviors. DenseNet is a densely connected deep neural network originally designed for image processing tasks. While it has strong feature extraction capabilities in recommendation scenarios, it lacks advantages in sequential modeling and comes with higher computational complexity. The model proposed by Wang et al. (2025) focused on graph-based modeling in the educational domain. It primarily leveraged GNN to capture relationships between students and learning resources, emphasizing the representation of structured data. However, it did not adequately address the sequential nature of learning behaviors. In contrast, the proposed TCN–DCC–RL model integrates the sequential modeling capabilities of TCN with the long-range dependency modeling of DCC and further incorporates a RL mechanism to dynamically adjust resource recommendation strategies in order to maximize students’ learning gains. This hybrid architecture not only ensures sufficient modeling depth and expressive power but also enhances the model’s adaptability in learning path optimization and behavioral prediction. As a result, in subsequent performance evaluations, the TCN–DCC–RL model demonstrated significant advantages across multiple metrics, including accuracy, F1-score, Mean Average Precision (MAP), and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG).

Discussion

Although neural network models such as Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM), Transformer, and GNNs have been widely applied in time series modeling and recommendation systems, the integration of TCN, DCC, and RL in this study is not arbitrary. It reflects a deliberate choice based on careful consideration of model architecture, task suitability, and computational efficiency. TCNs offer greater parallelism than recurrent neural networks (RNNs), enabling efficient training while capturing long-range dependencies. The addition of DCCs further expands the receptive field, allowing the model to detect behavioral changes over longer sequences with shallower network depth. This design is particularly robust against the periodicity and sparsity often seen in student behavior data. In comparison, BiLSTM can model bidirectional dependencies but suffers from long training times and unstable gradient propagation, making it difficult to scale to large educational datasets. Its bidirectional nature also hinders real-time deployment in recommendation scenarios. Transformers excel at global context modeling but require substantial hardware resources. Their self-attention mechanism scales quadratically with sequence length, posing challenges for resource-constrained mobile learning platforms. GNNs are suitable for modeling structured relationships, such as between students and learning resources. However, the behavioral data in this study are primarily temporal rather than structural. GNNs also struggle with non-uniform time intervals and lack the granularity to capture individual learning trajectories. Against this backdrop, incorporating RL allows the model to dynamically adjust recommendation strategies based on students’ historical behavior. RL continuously optimizes the learning path, compensating for the limitations of traditional neural networks that rely solely on static training data. This approach aligns well with the open-ended nature of education, where learners exhibit high autonomy and diverse trajectories. Overall, the TCN–DCC–RL integration provides a synergistic architecture that balances training efficiency with strong capabilities in time-series modeling, long-range dependency capture, and adaptive policy optimization. This design forms a structurally complementary and functionally cooperative framework, making it well-suited for personalized mobile learning resource recommendation.

Analysis of the experimental results shows that the proposed TCN–DCC–RL model consistently achieves the highest performance across key metrics, including Accuracy, F1-score, MAP, and NDCG. Its performance also demonstrates greater stability throughout training. At the 100th training epoch, the model attains an accuracy of 96.49%, an F1-score of 90.57%, a MAP of 92.27%, and an NDCG@20 score of 0.938—substantially outperforming all baseline models, particularly in modeling and optimizing the ranking of long-sequence learning behavior data. The TCN–DCC–RL model offers significant structural advantages for these tasks. By replacing traditional RNNs with one-dimensional convolutions, TCN enhances parallel computation efficiency and mitigates issues such as vanishing gradients in long sequences, improving the model’s ability to stably learn long-term behavioral features. DCC further expand the receptive field exponentially without increasing network depth, enabling the model to capture long-range temporal dependencies, such as changes in students’ mastery across learning phases. This capability is particularly valuable for predicting learning motivation and planning personalized learning paths. For ranking optimization, the model incorporates a RL strategy. Unlike conventional approaches that focus solely on classification accuracy, the RL component optimizes a cumulative reward function designed to maximize recommendation utility. This reward is based on the alignment between recommended resources and student learning outcomes, dynamically guiding the model to learn ranking strategies that match students’ long-term learning goals. Compared to traditional supervised methods, RL allows continual adjustment of ranking logic during training, resulting in superior ranking accuracy and generalization performance on long-range, complex behavioral patterns. Overall, the TCN–DCC–RL model establishes a unified framework that integrates temporal modeling, long-range dependency learning, and dynamic strategy optimization. This structural synergy improves representation, ranking, and recommendation accuracy for long-sequence learning data. These advantages are reflected in metrics such as NDCG@20 and MAP, confirming the effectiveness of its ranking-oriented optimization. Additionally, compared to the model proposed by Nguyen et al.31, TCN–DCC–RL achieves higher scores across multiple evaluation indicators, demonstrating the benefits of incorporating RL for weight distribution and enhanced data learning. These findings align with the research directions highlighted by Huang et al.32 and Lv et al.33, reinforcing the model’s capacity to handle complex data patterns while delivering superior accuracy and stability in recommendation ranking and feature learning.

This study introduces a novel integration of TCN and RL, offering an effective approach for feature learning and optimization. The proposed framework provides methodological support for intelligent recommendation and data analysis, serving as a valuable reference for research in recommendation systems, intelligent analytics, and decision-making optimization. It also lays a foundation for deploying deep learning-based intelligent systems, particularly in enhancing recommendation accuracy and decision-making efficiency. Moreover, the framework offers a practical technical pathway for future applications involving larger and more complex datasets.

From a practical perspective, the TCN–DCC–RL personalized recommendation model demonstrates strong potential for adoption in intelligent education environments. In higher education, the model can be integrated into Learning Management Systems (LMS) to provide students with resource recommendations based on historical learning behaviors and assessment results, supporting instructors in implementing differentiated instruction. In vocational training and adult education platforms, it can dynamically suggest modular learning resources aligned with learners’ skill development goals and task performance, enabling personalized competency development pathways. For online STEM courses, the model’s ability to capture long-term learning trajectories can support exercise recommendations and simulation-based learning guidance. Regarding deployment, the model can operate on mobile devices and edge computing infrastructures in hybrid teaching or mobile learning settings, ensuring real-time data processing, privacy protection, and offline recommendations under varying network conditions. Looking ahead, the framework can be extended to multimodal input environments by incorporating diverse data sources such as speech, video, text, and clickstream behavior, enhancing the detection of learning states and resource preferences. Furthermore, the model holds promise for regional educational governance platforms. It can facilitate intelligent decision-making by enabling large-scale student behavior clustering and personalized recommendations, supporting the integration of teaching, learning, and administration. Such applications contribute to evidence-based educational policymaking and promote educational equity.

Conclusion

Research contribution

The proposed TCN–DCC–RL model, which combines TCN with RL, achieves notable improvements in recommendation ranking, feature extraction, and data optimization. Experimental results demonstrate that it consistently outperforms existing methods across key evaluation metrics, including Accuracy, F1-score, MAP, and NDCG, achieving an accuracy rate above 95%. The model also shows superior training stability and faster convergence. This study contributes to technological advancements in recommendation systems and provides a valuable reference for intelligent analysis and data-driven optimization strategies.

Future works and research limitations

Despite its strong performance in recommendation ranking and data optimization, the proposed method has certain limitations. First, its adaptability to large-scale datasets requires further validation. Future studies could explore self-supervised or federated learning techniques to enhance generalization. Second, parameter tuning within the RL component is relatively complex, highlighting the need for automated optimization methods to reduce manual configuration efforts. Second, the computational cost of the model requires further optimization. Although the model’s inference speed is sufficient for online services, the integrated RL module significantly increases training time and resource consumption. This may pose challenges for rapid model iteration and deployment on highly resource-constrained edge devices. Therefore, future research will focus on model compression techniques, such as knowledge distillation or quantization. The goal is to develop a “lightweight” version of the TCN–DCC–RL model that maintains high performance while reducing computational overhead. This approach would broaden the model’s potential application scenarios.

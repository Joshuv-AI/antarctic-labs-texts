Abstract
. We establish global existence of the pluriclosed flow with arbitrary initial data on Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds, and Gromov-Hausdorff convergence of blowdown limits to a torus under natural conjectural bounds on the flow at infinity. In the case of generalized Kähler-Ricci flow we prove refined a priori estimates in support of these conjectural bounds.



1.
Introduction

In recent years the pluriclosed flow
[
27
,
29
]
and generalized Kähler-Ricci flow
[
3
,
25
,
28
]
have been developed as a tool for understanding the geometry of complex, especially non-Kähler, manifolds
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
32
]
. A natural class of non-Kähler manifolds are the Oeljeklaus-Toma (OT) manifolds
[
22
]
, whose geometry is linked to the structure of number fields, and which are natural higher dimensional generalizations of Inoue surfaces
[
18
]
. In
[
11
]
a complete description of the pluriclosed flow with left-invariant initial data on OT manifolds was obtained, in particular showing that the solution exists for all time and collapses after blowdown to a torus in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. Moreover the blowdown on the universal cover converges in the Cheeger-Gromov sense to a soliton. It is natural to conjecture that these statements hold for arbitrary initial data (cf. Conjecture
3.2
). In this work we confirm some aspects of this conjecture.

The first main result is to establish the global existence of the flow:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
(M^{2n},J)
an OT manifold and
g_{0}
a pluriclosed metric on
M
. The solution to pluriclosed flow with initial data
g_{0}
exists on
[0,\infty)
.

The proof exploits natural class of background metrics arising from the homogeneous structure on OT-manifolds. In the case of Inoue surfaces these are known as Tricerri metrics
[
30
]
. In the next theorem we give some refined estimates in the special case of generalized Kähler-Ricci flow (GKRF)
[
28
]
. Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds admit natural classes of generalized Kähler structures with split tangent bundle, and for such metrics the GKRF reduces to a scalar parabolic flow of Monge-Ampère type
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For generalized Kähler-Ricci flow on an Oeljeklaus-Toma manifold

(1)

The scalar potential
\phi
satisfies

\displaystyle-C\leq\phi\leq Ce^{-t}(1+t).

(2)

Assuming there exists a Tricerri-type metric in
[\omega_{0}]
, we have

\displaystyle-Ce^{-t}(1+t)\leq\phi\leq Ce^{-t}(1+t).

(3)

On Inoue surfaces of type
S_{M}
, the estimate of item (2) holds. In addition:

\displaystyle-C\leq\dot{\phi}\leq C.

(4)

On Inoue surfaces of type
S_{M}
, we have:

\displaystyle\omega(t)\geq C\omega_{h}(t),

where
\omega_{h}(t)
is the model flow with initial data the Tricerri metric
h
.

2.
Geometry of Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds

2.1.
Definition

In this section, we recall the family of compact non-Kähler complex manifolds constructed by Oeljeklaus-Toma
[
22
]
. First, we need some facts from algebraic number theory. Let
K
be an algebraic number field, i.e.
K\simeq\mathbb{Q}[x]/(f)
, where
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
is a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
s+2t=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, where

\displaystyle s=\#\text{ real roots },\quad 2t=\#\text{ complex roots},

which for arbitrary given
s
and
t
exists by (
[
22
]
Remark 1.1).

Now, consider the embedding,
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}
, given by the roots of
f
: Let
a_{1},...,a_{s}\in\mathbb{R}
be the real roots of
f
, and let
a_{s+t+1}=\overline{a_{s+1}},...,a_{s+2t}=\overline{a_{s+t}}\in\mathbb{C}
be the complex roots of
f
. Define the embedding:

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}:K\to\mathbb{C},\quad x\mapsto a_{i}.

Thus,
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{s}:K\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma_{s+1},...,\sigma_{s+2t}:K\to\mathbb{C},
where
\sigma_{s+i}=\overline{\sigma_{s+t+i}}
. There exist
n:=s+2t
different embeddings.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}\subset K
be the ring of algebraic integers. Note
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a finitely generated free abelian group of rank
n=s+2t
, i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{k}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. (The rank of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
as a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module is
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=s+2t
). Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the multiplicative group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. By the Dirichlet unit theorem, for
s\geq 1
we have
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}\cong\{\pm 1\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{t+s-1}
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}:=\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}\ |\ \sigma_{i}(a)>0,\ 1\leq i\leq s\}.

By definition,
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}
is a finite index subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
.

Now, let
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}\}
. We define two kinds of actions on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
:

\displaystyle T:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t})

\displaystyle T(a)=[(w_{1},...,w_{s},z_{s+1},...,z_{s+t})\mapsto(w_{1}+\sigma_{1}(a),...,z_{s+t}+\sigma_{s+t}(a))],

and

\displaystyle R:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t})

\displaystyle R(u)=[(w_{1},...,w_{s},z_{s+1},...,z_{s+t})\mapsto(w_{1}\cdot\sigma_{1}(u),...,z_{s+t}\cdot\sigma_{s+t}(u))].

Let

\displaystyle\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C}^{s+t},\quad

\displaystyle a\mapsto(\sigma_{1}(a),...,\sigma_{s+t}(a)).

Note
\sigma(\mathcal{O}_{K})
is a lattice of rank
s+2t
in
\mathbb{C}^{s+t}
. Thus, we have
\operatorname{rank}(T(\mathcal{O}_{K}))=s+2t
. Note
R(u)\sigma(\mathcal{O}_{K})=\sigma(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, by the property of the algebraic integer ring structure. Then:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}

acts on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
. By
[
22
]
there exists a subgroup
U\leq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}
,
\operatorname{rank}(U)=s
, such that
U\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}
is cocompact and acts properly discontinuously on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
. The compact quotient:

\displaystyle X(K,U)=\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}/U\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}

is called an
Oeljeklaus-Toma manifold
of type
(s,t)
. Note:

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}/\sigma(\mathcal{O}_{K})\cong(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{s}\times(S^{1})^{2t+s}

and
U
acts on
(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{s}
preserves the fiber by the property of the algebraic integer structure. Thus, we may regard
X(K,U)
as a torus
\mathbb{T}^{t}
bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{s}
.

When
s=t=1
, it is the famous
Inoue surface
of type
S_{M}

[
18
]
. The original
S_{M}
is constructed in a more direct way:
consider
M\in\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
,
M=(m_{ik})
. Denote the eigenvalues:
\lambda,\mu,\bar{\mu}
with the relation:
\lambda\cdot|\mu|^{2}=1
. In addition, we require
\mu\neq\bar{\mu}
and
\lambda>1
. The corresponding unit eigenvectors:
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
,
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
and
(\bar{b_{1}},\bar{b_{2}},\bar{b_{3}})
. Define the actions:

\displaystyle g_{0}:(z,w)\mapsto(\mu z,\lambda w)

\displaystyle g_{i}:(z,w)\mapsto(z+b_{i},w+a_{i})

for
i=1,2,3
. Note that
\{\left(\begin{aligned} a_{i}\\
b_{i}\end{aligned}\right)\}
are linearly independent and satisfy:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{aligned} \lambda a_{i}\\
\mu b_{i}\end{aligned}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{3}m_{ik}\left(\begin{aligned} a_{k}\\
b_{k}\end{aligned}\right).

Then, the quotient
M:=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{H}/\langle g_{0},g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
is a compact complex surface, which is the desired Inoue surface
S_{M}
. The relation between two constructions can be seen in this way:
choose
f
as the characteristic polynomial of
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
. Note that when
s=t=1
, then
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Choose a generator
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}
, by Dirichlet unit theorem,
\sigma_{1}(u)\cdot|\sigma_{2}(u)|^{2}=1
. Then, any choice of the basis of
\mathcal{O}_{K}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
will correspond to an element of
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
.

2.2.
Pluriclosed and generalized Kähler structures on Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds

Consider the pluriclosed metrics on OT manifolds. Note that not all OT manifolds admit pluriclosed metrics. In fact:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
23
,
2
]
)
.

Let
X(K,U)
be an OT manifold of type
(s,t)
. It admits pluriclosed metrics if and only if
s=t
and, for any
u\in U
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(u)|\sigma_{s+j}(u)|^{2}=1,\quad\text{for any }i\in\{1,...,s\}.

Note that for Inoue surfaces
S_{M}
, this is automatically true. From now on, we restrict ourselves to the case where
s=t
, i.e. the universal cover of the OT manifolds is
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
.

Some pluriclosed metrics can be constructed explicitly on OT manifolds of type
(s,s)
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\omega_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sqrt{-1}\frac{dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w_{i}}}{(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}+\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}}.

It is easy to verify
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{h}=0
, hence it is pluriclosed. Moreover, this metric is invariant under the group action
U\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and therefore descends to the quotient. When
s=1
, the metric
\omega_{h}
is the pluriclosed metric discovered by Tricerri
[
30
]
on the Inoue Surface.

More generally, for any given sequence
a=\{a_{1},...,a_{s}\}
, and
b=\{b_{1},...,b_{s}\}
, where each
a_{i}
,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. We can define the following metric:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\omega^{a,b}_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sqrt{-1}a_{i}\frac{dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w_{i}}}{(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}+\sqrt{-1}b_{i}\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}}.

Note, the family of metrics
\omega^{a,b}_{h}
are pluriclosed as well. Thus, we have a family of pluriclosed metrics on
X(K,U)
. We shall write
\omega_{h}
for short if
a_{i}
,
b_{i}=1
. We record some curvature facts of these model metrics, the proof of which is a standard computation.

Proposition 2.2
.

The metric
\omega^{a,b}_{h}
satisfies:

(1)

The only nonvanishing components of the Chern curvature are

\displaystyle\Omega^{Ch}_{w_{i}\overline{w_{i}}w_{i}\overline{w_{i}}}=-\frac{a_{i}}{2(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{4}},\qquad\Omega^{Ch}_{w_{i}\overline{w_{i}}z_{i}\overline{z_{i}}}=\frac{b_{i}}{4(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})}.

(2)

The Bismut Ricci form satisfies

\displaystyle\rho_{B}(\omega_{h})

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i}.

2.3.
Generalized Kähler structures on Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds

A
generalized Kähler structure (GK structure)

[
14
,
15
]
on a manifold
M
is a triple
(g,I,J)
, where
g
is a Riemannian metric compatible with two integrable complex structures
I
and
J
, furthermore satisfying

\displaystyle d_{I}^{c}\omega_{I}

\displaystyle=-d_{J}^{c}\omega_{J},\quad dd_{I}^{c}\omega_{I}=-dd_{J}^{c}\omega_{J}=0.

Hitchin
[
17
]
shows that a GK structure has an associated Poisson tensor

\displaystyle\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}g^{-1}[I,J].

We are interested here in the simplest case of GK manifolds, namely when
\sigma
vanishes. This is equivalent to

\displaystyle[I,J]=0,

and then we refer to the GK structure as
commuting type
. See
[
4
]
for further background on such structures. We define

\displaystyle\Pi:=IJ\in\operatorname{End}(TM)

where
\Pi^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
. Then, we can decompose
TM
with respect to the eigenvalue
\pm 1
:

\displaystyle TM=T_{+}M\oplus T_{-}M.

Now, we introduce the GK-structure of commuting type on OT manifolds. On the universal cover,
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
. Consider the invariant complex structures on
\mathbb{H}^{s}
and
\mathbb{C}^{t}
,
J_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}
and
J_{\mathbb{C}^{s}}
. Let:

\displaystyle I=\left(\begin{aligned} J_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}&\\
&J_{\mathbb{C}^{s}}\end{aligned}\right),\qquad J=\left(\begin{aligned} J_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}&\\
&-J_{\mathbb{C}^{s}}\end{aligned}\right).

These are invariant complex structures on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
, hence pass to the quotient. Moreover,
[I,J]=0
. Defining
\Pi=IJ=-\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{H}}\oplus\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
E_{\mathbb{C}}=T\mathbb{C}^{s}
,
E_{\mathbb{H}}=T\mathbb{H}^{s}
. With the associated splitting as above, we see:

\displaystyle TM=E_{\mathbb{H}}\oplus E_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle T_{-}M=E_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle T_{+}M=E_{\mathbb{C}}.

In our case, since we have a specific split of tangent bundle, we will use the precise component notation (
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
) instead of the standard notation
(+/-)
. Throughout this paper, we use coordinates
(w,z):=(w_{1},\dots,w_{s},z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
, with respect to the complex structure
I
.
Note that we obtain differential operators

\displaystyle d=

\displaystyle\ d_{\mathbb{C}}+d_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{C}}=

\displaystyle\ \pi_{\mathbb{C}}\circ d=\partial_{z}+\partial_{\overline{z}},

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}}=

\displaystyle\ \pi_{\mathbb{H}}\circ d=\partial_{w}+\partial_{\overline{w}},

where
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\pi_{\mathbb{H}}
are the projections of
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and
E_{\mathbb{H}}^{*}
, respectively.

Proposition 2.3
.

The triple
(h^{a,b},I,J)
where
h^{a,b}
is the metric determined by (
2.2
), is generalized Kähler of commuting type.

Proof.

We will give the proof for the standard metric
h
in (
2.1
), with the general case being analogous.
First, note the pluriclosed metric
h
is compatible with both complex structures
I
and
J
, and
E_{\mathbb{C}}
and
E_{\mathbb{H}}
are
h
-orthogonal. Second, we let
\omega_{I}=h(\cdot,I\cdot)
, and
\omega_{J}=h(\cdot,J\cdot)
. Let
(w,z)
be the complex coordinate with respect to
I
. Then:

\displaystyle\omega_{I}=\omega_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sqrt{-1}\frac{dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w_{i}}}{(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}+\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}},

\displaystyle\omega_{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sqrt{-1}\frac{dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w_{i}}}{(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}-\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}},

and

\displaystyle d_{I}^{c}\omega_{I}=\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{\overline{w}}+\partial_{\overline{z}}-\partial_{w}-\partial_{z})\omega_{I}=-(\partial_{\overline{w}}-\partial_{w})(\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}}),

\displaystyle d_{J}^{c}\omega_{J}=\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{\overline{w}}+\partial_{z}-\partial_{w}-\partial_{\overline{z}})\omega_{J}=(\partial_{\overline{w}}-\partial_{w})(\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}}).

Thus,
d_{I}^{c}\omega_{I}=-d_{J}^{c}\omega_{J}
. By direct computation,
dd_{I}^{c}\omega_{I}=-dd_{J}^{c}\omega_{J}=0
. Hence,
(h,I,J)
is a GK structure.
∎

We observe that the metric satisfies the leafwise Kähler conditions
d_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\left.\omega_{I}\right|_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}\right)=0
and
d_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\left.\omega_{I}\right|_{E_{\mathbb{H}}}\right)=0
, in line with the general theory of commuting-type GK structures developed by Apostolov-Gualtieri
[
4
]
.

3.
Pluriclosed flow on Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds

3.1.
Pluriclosed flow and its normalized flow

The
pluriclosed flow
is the evolution equation
[
27
]
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega(t)=-\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega(t)),

where
\rho_{B}
is the Bismut-Ricci form. Motivated by the
Type III behavior of the Ricci flow, we let the time parameter
s=e^{t}-1
and consider the following blow-down family of metrics:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}(t)=\frac{\omega(s)}{s+1},

where
\omega(s)
satisfies the pluriclosed flow (
3.1
). It follows that
\widetilde{\omega}(t)
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega(t)=-\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega(t))-\omega(t),

which we call the
normalized pluriclosed flow
.

3.2.
Model solutions

The expected qualitative behavior of the normalized pluriclosed flow on OT manifolds is captured by solutions with initial data the model metrics
\omega_{h}^{a,b}
. Straightforward computations show that the normalized pluriclosed flow with this initial data is

(3.3)

\displaystyle\omega^{a,b}_{h}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sqrt{-1}((1-e^{-t})\frac{3}{4}+e^{-t}a_{i})\frac{1}{(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i}+\sqrt{-1}e^{-t}b_{i}\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{i}.

Later, we shall denote the time-dependent normalized model metric starting with
\omega^{a,b}_{h}
as
\omega^{a,b}_{h}(t)
. We shall write
\omega_{h}(t)
for short if
a_{i}
,
b_{i}=1
, for all
1\leq i\leq s
.
Observe that for these model solutions the Chern torsion
T
is uniformly bounded in time, i.e.,
|T(t)|\leq C
. Moreover, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{h}^{a,b}(t)}{t+1}\to\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{3}{4(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i},

which can be considered as a degenerate metric on
X(K,U)
. As explained in
[
11
]
, the blowdown manifolds converge in Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a torus
\mathbb{T}^{s}
with a canonical flat metric
d(K,U)
depending only on the algebraic field
K
and the rank
s
subgroup
U
. We recall the result here, noting that OT manifolds are compact solvmanifolds and the model metrics are left-invariant:

Theorem 3.1
.

(
[
11
]
) Let
\omega_{0}
be a left-invariant pluriclosed metric on an OT manifold
M
, then the normalized pluriclosed flow starting with
\omega_{0}
converges to
(\mathbb{T}^{s},d(K,U))
in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense.

It is reasonable to conjecture that this behavior holds in the general case:

Conjecture 3.2
.

Let
M=X(K,U)
be an OT manifold of type
(s,s)
, then for any pluriclosed metric
\omega_{0}
, the normalized pluriclosed flow
3.2
with initial metric
\omega_{0}
exists on
[0,\infty)
, and converges to
(\mathbb{T}^{s},d(K,U))
in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense.

We shall prove the first part of the conjecture in Section
3.3
. For the second half of the conjecture, we have the following sufficiency condition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\omega(t)
be the normalized pluriclosed flow solution on the OT manifold
X(K,U)
. Suppose that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

•

C^{-1}\leq\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\omega_{h}(t)\leq C
,

•

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\omega_{h}(t)=1
for all
x\in X(K,U)
.

Then, we have

\displaystyle(X(K,U),\omega(t))\to(\mathbb{T}^{s},d(K,U)),

to the flat metric
d(K,U)
in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense.

Proof.

We give a brief sketch as the result is already essentially contained in e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
33
]
. Note that for OT manifolds, there is a canonical fibration map:

\displaystyle F:X(K,U)\to\mathbb{T}^{s}

where the fiber is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{3s}
. In particular,
E_{\mathbb{C}}
will be in the kernel of
dF
. Since the quotient of
\{z\}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
is dense in the
\mathbb{T}^{3s}
fiber (See Section 2 of
[
31
]
), the degenerate metric
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{3}{4(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i}
will induce a metric on the base
\mathbb{T}^{s}
. Let
d(K,U)
be the metric on
\mathbb{T}^{s}
induced from
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{3}{4(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i}
, which is flat.

Now we choose
G:\mathbb{T}^{s}\to X(K,U)
to be any map so that
F\circ G=\operatorname{Id}
, and show that for
T
large,
F
and
G
are
\epsilon(T)
-Gromov-Hausdorff approximations. By our assumption, the limit of
\omega(t)
exists and is equal to
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{3}{4(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i}
. Since the quotient of
\{z\}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
is dense in the
\mathbb{T}^{3s}
fiber, the distance between two points in
F^{-1}(x)
will converge to
0
and the distance of any two representatives of two fibers will approximate to the distance
d(K,U)
, from which the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence follows.
∎

Thus, to prove the second half of the conjecture, it is enough to prove that
\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\omega_{h}(t)
is uniformly bounded from above and below and converges to
1
. In geometric flow theory, to obtain the long-time behavior, a Type III curvature estimate is usually needed. Then the collapsing behavior can be shown using the monotonicity formula and the Cheeger-Fukaya-Gromov collapsing theory (e.g.,
[
20
,
21
]
). In our case, using the complex structure,
\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\omega_{h}(t)
will satisfy a strictly parabolic equation (See Section
3.3
), and thus it is possible to estimate it directly. The Type III estimate for (
3.1
) starting with an arbitrary initial pluriclosed metric on the OT manifolds remains an interesting open question, even in the Inoue surface case (
s=1
).
See a recent study of the long-time behavior of the a related curvature flow following this route in
[
16
]
.

3.3.
Long-time existence of pluriclosed flow

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
M=X(K,U)=\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}/U\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}
be an OT manifold of type
(s,s)
. Since the universal cover
\widetilde{M}\cong\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
is a product manifold, and the action
U\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}
acts on
\widetilde{M}
diagonally, the tangent bundle
TM=E_{\mathbb{H}}\oplus E_{\mathbb{C}}
, split as a direct sum of two subbundles assocaited with two projection maps,
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb{H}}:TM\to E_{\mathbb{H}}
and
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb{C}}:TM\to E_{\mathbb{C}}
. Now, given any Riemannian metric

, the induced metric on

. Similarly,

. Note that the model metric

splits with respect to the splitting of

, the difference of the Chern connection;
\Omega^{g}
is the Chern curvature of metric

\Delta
is the Chern laplacian. Denote

as the standard coordinate on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
. All other letters in the formulas represent general coordinates. Denote:

\displaystyle Q_{i\overline{j}}=g^{\overline{l}k}g^{\overline{n}m}T_{ik\overline{n}}T_{\overline{j}lm}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Given the setup above,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}=-g^{r\overline{s}}g^{u\overline{q}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w_{j}}}\Upsilon_{ru}^{w_{i}}\Upsilon_{\overline{s}\overline{q}}^{\overline{w}_{j}}+g^{r\overline{s}}g^{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}\Omega^{h}_{r\overline{s}w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}-g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}Q_{p\overline{q}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\operatorname{tr}_{h_{\mathbb{H}}}g_{\mathbb{H}}=-g^{r\overline{s}}h^{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}\Upsilon^{p}_{rw_{i}}\Upsilon^{\overline{q}}_{\overline{s}\overline{w}_{j}}g_{p\overline{q}}+h^{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}Q_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}-g^{r\overline{s}}g_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}h^{w_{i}\overline{q}}h^{p\overline{w}_{j}}\Omega^{h}_{r\overline{s}p\overline{q}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}h_{\mathbb{C}}=-g^{r\overline{s}}g^{u\overline{q}}h_{z_{i}\overline{z_{j}}}\Upsilon_{ru}^{z_{i}}\Upsilon_{\overline{s}\overline{q}}^{\overline{z}_{j}}+g^{r\overline{s}}g^{z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}\Omega^{h}_{r\overline{s}z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}-g^{z_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{z}_{j}}h_{z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}Q_{p\overline{q}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\operatorname{tr}_{h_{\mathbb{C}}}g_{\mathbb{C}}=-g^{r\overline{s}}h^{z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}\Upsilon^{p}_{rz_{i}}\Upsilon^{\overline{q}}_{\overline{s}\overline{z}_{j}}g_{p\overline{q}}+h^{z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}Q_{z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}-g^{r\overline{s}}g_{z_{i}\overline{z}_{j}}h^{z_{i}\overline{q}}h^{p\overline{z}_{j}}\Omega^{h}_{r\overline{s}p\overline{q}}.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

The proof is almost identical to Lemma 4.3 of
[
25
]
, except that the metric

does not necessarily split with respect to

. Here we sketch the computation of

. Note,
\rho_{B}^{1,1}=S-Q
, where

. Thus, using the local formula of the Chern curvature:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}=g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}(-g_{p\overline{q},r\overline{s}}+g^{u\overline{v}}g_{p\overline{v},r}g_{u\overline{q},\overline{s}})g^{r\overline{s}}-g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}Q_{p\overline{q}}.

Also, the Chern Laplacian acts on
\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}
by a standard formula:

\displaystyle\Delta\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}=

\displaystyle g^{r\overline{s}}(g^{w_{i}\overline{v}}g^{u\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}g_{u\overline{v},r}g_{p\overline{q},\overline{s}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}+g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{v}}g^{u\overline{w}_{j}}g_{u\overline{v},r}g_{p\overline{q},\overline{s}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}-g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}g_{p\overline{q},r\overline{s}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}

\displaystyle-g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}g_{p\overline{q},\overline{s}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j},r}-g^{w_{i}\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{w}_{j}}g_{p\overline{q},r}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j},\overline{s}}+g^{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j},r\overline{s}}).

Combining these two formulas, with the cancellation of the first order terms and the completing of squares, the result follows. The other results are analogous.
∎

To show Theorem
1.1
, we need to show that, for any time interval
[0,T]
, with

, the metrics
g(t)
is

-equivalent to

, i.e.,

\displaystyle C(T)^{-1}h\leq g(t)\leq C(T)h

for any

. In other words, we need to show time dependent estimates of
\operatorname{tr}_{g}h
and
\tr_{h}g
. To achieve this, we need to use the Chern curvature of the model metric, moreprecisely the fact that the Chern curvature on

is negative. In particular, using Proposition
2.2
and (

) we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}})^{2}.

By the maximum principle:
\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{1}{C_{0}+\frac{1}{2}t}
, where
C_{0}
only depends on the initial metric
g(0)
. Now, for the full trace, using Lemma
3.4
and the above estimate we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\operatorname{tr}_{g}h

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s}g^{z_{i}\overline{z}_{i}}g^{w_{i}\overline{w}_{i}}\Omega^{h}_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{i}z_{i}\overline{z}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}h_{\mathbb{C}})(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4C_{0}+2t}\operatorname{tr}_{g}h.

By the maximum principle again,

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{g}h\leq C_{0}^{\prime}(4C_{0}+2t)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

again only depends on

. Thus, we have a time-dependent lower bound:

\displaystyle g(t)\geq C_{1}(T)h.

Now, for every fixed

, we can apply Theorem 1.8 of
[

]
, we have a time dependent upper bound,

. Hence:

\displaystyle C_{1}(T)h\leq g(t)\leq C_{2}(T)h.

Using the higher regularity of uniformly parabolic solutions of pluriclosed flow
[

,
19
,

]
, the long-time existence follows.
∎

4.
Generalized Kähler-Ricci flow on Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds

In this section we consider the more restricted setting of GKRF on OT manifolds. As explained in §
2
, OT manifolds come equipped with generalized Kähler metrics of commuting type, and the pluriclosed flow starting with such a metric will preserve this structure
[
28
]
, leading to new estimates.

4.1.
Cohomology constraint and scalar reduction

The key point established in
[
25
]
is that after accounting for a certain cohomological constraint, the flow
3.2
can be reduced to a scalar twisted Monge-Ampère type equation. We recall some aspects of this cohomology group (cf.
[
24
]
Definition 7.3 and 7.4,
[
13
]
)

Definition 4.1

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
(M^{2n},g,I,J)
be a generalized Kähler manifold of commuting type. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(M)=\{\omega\in\Lambda_{I,\mathbb{R}}^{1,1}|\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0\}/\{\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{+}\overline{\partial}_{+}-\partial_{-}\overline{\partial}_{-})u|u\in C^{\infty}(M)\}.

Note
\omega\in[\omega_{0}]
, if and only if there exists a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\omega=\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{+}\overline{\partial}_{+}-\partial_{-}\overline{\partial}_{-})f.

Firstly, the dimension of the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}(M)
(Definition
4.1
) on the Inoue surface
S_{M}
is known.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

For a complex surface with split tangent bundle,
M
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(S_{M})=\left\{[\omega_{h}^{a,b}]\ |\ a,b\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

In particular,
\dim\mathcal{H}(S_{M})=2
.

For more general OT manifolds we prove a conjecturally sharp lower bound on the dimension of this space by exhibiting a space spanned by the model metrics.

Proposition 4.3
.

For positive constants,
a_{i}
,
b_{i}
, and
a_{i}^{\prime}
,
b_{i}^{\prime}
, such that there exists
i
with either
a_{i}\neq a_{i}^{\prime}
or
b_{i}\neq b_{i}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle[\omega^{a,b}_{h}]\neq[\omega^{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}_{h}].

Proof.

It suffices to show that for constants
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
such that
a_{i}
,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, if there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(X(K,U))
such that on the universal cover
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
,

\displaystyle({\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}}-{\partial_{w}\partial_{\overline{w}}})u=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\frac{dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w_{i}}}{(\operatorname{Im}w_{i})^{2}}+b_{i}\operatorname{Im}w_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z_{i}},

then
a_{i}=b_{i}=0
. Tracing the above equation with
\omega^{a,b}_{J}
yields

\displaystyle\Delta^{Ch}_{\omega_{I}^{a,b}}u=\mbox{const}

on
X(K,U)
. By the maximum principle,
u\equiv C
, hence
a_{i}=b_{0}=0
as required.
∎

The relevance of the above cohomology group stems from its relationship to the Bismut Ricci form for commuting-type generalized Kähler metrics. In particular from Proposition 8.20 of
[
13
]
, and specializing to the case of OT manifolds here, for such metrics
\rho_{B}^{1,1}
can be decomposed:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega)=\rho_{\mathbb{C}}(\omega_{\mathbb{C}})-\rho_{\mathbb{H}}(\omega_{\mathbb{C}})-\rho_{\mathbb{C}}(\omega_{\mathbb{H}})+\rho_{\mathbb{H}}(\omega_{\mathbb{H}}).

Moreover this leads to the following transgression formula for commuting-type GK metrics:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega_{1})-\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega_{2})=-\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}-\partial_{w}\partial_{\overline{w}})\log\frac{\det g^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\det g^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\det g^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot\det g^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}}.

Definition 4.4

(Model flow)
.

To simplify notation we set
P=\rho_{B}^{1,1}(h)
, where
h
is the model metric (
2.1
). Let
\widetilde{\omega}(t)=e^{-t}\widetilde{\omega}_{0}-(1-e^{-t})P
, where
\widetilde{\omega}_{0}
is a representative in
[\omega(0)]
, and
P:=\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}-\frac{3\sqrt{-1}}{4}h_{w_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{j}
, the Bismut curvature of the model metric (
2.1
).

Analogous to the Kähler Ricci flow, we can reduce the GKRF to a parabolic fully nonlinear twisted Monge-Ampére equation.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
\omega(t)=\widetilde{\omega}(t)+\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}-\partial_{w}\partial_{\overline{w}})\phi
, where
\phi
satisfies

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(t)+\phi(t)=\operatorname{log}\frac{\operatorname{det}g_{\mathbb{C}}}{\operatorname{det}h_{\mathbb{C}}}-\operatorname{log}\frac{\operatorname{det}g_{\mathbb{H}}}{\operatorname{det}h_{\mathbb{H}}}+c(t),

where
c(t)
is some time-dependent constant. Then,
\omega_{t}
solves
3.2
.

Proof.

Use the transgression formula (
4.3
), we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega(t)

\displaystyle=-e^{-t}\omega_{0}-e^{-t}P+P-P+\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}-\partial_{w}\partial_{\overline{w}})\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(t)

\displaystyle=-\omega(t)+\sqrt{-1}(\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}-\partial_{w}\partial_{\overline{w}})(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi+\phi)-\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega_{h})

\displaystyle=-\omega(t)-\rho_{B}^{1,1}(\omega(t)).

∎

Lemma 4.6

\displaystyle-C_{0}\leq\phi(t)\leq C_{0}e^{-t}(1+Ct),

Proof.

Recall:

At a minimum point, note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(e^{t}\min\phi(t))\geq e^{t}s(\operatorname{log}\frac{C_{3}}{1+C_{1}e^{-t}})\geq-C_{4}e^{t}.

Thus, the lower bound is obtained similarly.

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}-(C_{1}-1)\phi\leq A.

By Lemma
4.6
again, done.
∎

4.3.
Results on the Inoue surface

When
s=1
, then the OT manifolds
X(K,U)
become the well-known Inoue surfaces of type
S_{M}
. On these surfaces, several estimates can be strengthened. First note that by Theorem
4.2
, for any GK initial metric
\omega_{0}
, there will be constants
a
and
b
such that
\omega_{0}\in[\omega^{a,b}_{h}]
.
Thus, we always have the improved
C^{0}
estimate for
\phi
from Corollary
4.8
.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
\omega_{0}
be any GK metrics on the Inoue surface
S_{M}
. Then, if we choose
c(t)=t+\log(\frac{3}{4})
, then:

\displaystyle-C_{0}e^{-t}(1+Ct)\leq\phi(t)\leq C_{0}e^{-t}(1+Ct),

for some
C_{0}
,
C>0
.

Secondly, when
s=1
, then
E_{\mathbb{C}}
and
E_{\mathbb{H}}
are holomorphic line bundles. Thus, the AM-GM inequality becomes equality in this case, and we can have the following lower bound of
\dot{\phi}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

On Inoue surface
S_{M}
, for the potential
\phi
, there exists a constant
C>0
, such that:

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}\geq-C.

Proof.

Note
s=1
. Choose a large constant
\Lambda>0
, consider the quantity
\dot{\phi}+\phi+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\phi
. Then

\displaystyle(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)(\dot{\phi}+\phi+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\phi)=1+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\dot{\phi}+\frac{1}{\Lambda}e^{-t}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}g^{0}_{\mathbb{C}}-\frac{1}{\Lambda}e^{-t}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}g^{0}_{\mathbb{H}}-(\frac{1}{\Lambda}(1-e^{-t})+1)\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}.

Now, for
A>0
, a very large constant, such that
\dot{\phi}+\phi+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\phi<-A
. By (
4.4
), notice that in this case
s=1
, we know:

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}+\phi=\log\frac{\det g_{\mathbb{C}}/c_{1}}{\det e^{-t}h_{\mathbb{C}}}-\log\frac{\det g_{\mathbb{H}}}{\det\frac{3}{4}h_{\mathbb{H}}}=-\log(c_{1}e^{-t}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}h_{\mathbb{C}})+\log(\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}).

By Corollary
4.14
, in particular
|\phi|\leq C_{0}
. Then, there is a large constant
A^{\prime}=A-C_{0}>0
such that
\dot{\phi}\leq-A^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{4}{3}e^{\frac{C_{0}}{\Lambda}-A}e^{-t}c_{1}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}h_{\mathbb{C}}\leq\frac{4}{3}e^{\frac{C_{0}}{\Lambda}-A}c_{0}e^{-t}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}g^{0}_{\mathbb{C}},

for some constant
c_{0}
, only depends on the initial metric.
By choosing
\Lambda
large, such that
1-\frac{C_{0}}{\Lambda}>\frac{2}{3}
, fixed, when
A
is large enough,
c_{0}e^{\frac{C_{0}}{\Lambda}-A}\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda}
. Thus, choose an
A=\frac{\Lambda}{2}
large enough, such that
\dot{\phi}(t)\geq-\frac{1}{2}A
, for
t\in[0,T]
, where
T
is large enough. Then for the first time
\dot{\phi}+\phi+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\phi=-A
, we have:

\displaystyle(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)(\dot{\phi}+\phi+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\phi)

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{1}{\Lambda}-\frac{C_{1}e^{-t}}{\Lambda}-c_{0}e^{\frac{C_{0}}{\Lambda}-A})e^{-t}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}h_{\mathbb{C}}+1+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle>0.

By the maximum principle, the result follows.
∎

Combine with the potential derivative upper bound (Lemma
4.13
), we have the following metric lower bound.

Theorem 4.16
.

On an Inoue surface
S_{M}
, for generalized Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
, there is a constant
C
, such that the normalized pluriclosed flow solution
\omega(t)
with initial data
\omega_{0}
will satisfy

\displaystyle\omega(t)\geq C\omega_{h}(t),

where
\omega_{h}(t)
is the model flow.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.13
and Lemma
4.15
, we have that:

\displaystyle-C\leq\dot{\phi}+\phi\leq C

for some constant
C
. Now, from the equation (
4.4
) and
s=1
, we have:

\displaystyle-C\leq\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h_{\mathbb{H}}/\operatorname{tr}_{g_{\mathbb{C}}}e^{-t}h_{\mathbb{C}}\leq C.

Note that in the normalized pluriclosed flow model case, (
3.3
), the
E_{\mathbb{C}}
part will shrink at the rate of
e^{-t}
, while the
E_{\mathbb{H}}
part will be equivalent to
h_{\mathbb{H}}
. Thus, by Corollary
4.11
, we have the desired lower bound for
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Combine Lemma
4.6
, Corollary
4.8
, Lemma
4.13
, Corollary
4.14
, Lemma
4.15
, and Theorem
4.16
, the results follow.
∎

5.
Closing remarks

As pluriclosed flow is in particular a solution to generalized Ricci flow
[
29
]
, there are scalar curvature monotonicity formulas as explained in
[
26
]
. The key extra input is a solution of the
dilaton flow

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi=\Delta\psi+\tfrac{1}{6}\left|H\right|^{2}.

To state the monotonicity, we recall the notation of weighted Ricci and scalar curvatures:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{H,\psi}=\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{1}{4}H^{2}+\operatorname{Hess}\psi-\frac{1}{2}(d_{g}^{*}H+i_{\nabla\psi}H)

\displaystyle R^{H,\psi}=R-\frac{1}{12}|H|^{2}+2\Delta\psi-|\nabla\psi|^{2}

Along the
normalized
generalized Ricci flow we then obtain

Corollary 5.1
.

(cf.
[
26
]
Prop 1.1) If
(g_{t},H_{t})
is a solution of normalized generalized Ricci flow and
\psi
is a solution of the dilaton flow, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)R^{H,\psi}=2|\operatorname{Ric}^{H,\psi}|^{2}+R^{H,\psi},

and the estimate:

\displaystyle R^{H,\psi}\geq\inf_{M\times\{0\}}R^{H,\psi}e^{t}.

For a solution to pluriclosed flow there is a natural class of solutions to the dilaton flow, namely the induced metrics on flat line bundles (cf.

[
24
]
Lemma 6.1). On Inoue surfaces for instance there is a natural such bundle
\det E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes(\det E_{\mathbb{C}})^{\otimes 2}
. In particular by direct computations it can be shown that

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{1}{9}\left(\operatorname{log}\frac{\det g_{\mathbb{H}}}{\det h_{\mathbb{H}}}+2\log\frac{\det g_{\mathbb{C}}}{\det e^{-t}h_{\mathbb{C}}}\right)

is a solution of the dilaton flow after appropriate normalization and gauge-fixing. In particular, nonnegativity of
R^{H,\psi}
is preserved. Noting that in context now all the data defining
R^{H,\psi}
is canonically determined by a pluriclosed metric alone, it will be interesting to ask whether
X(K,U)
admits a metric such that
R^{H,\psi}\geq 0
, which becomes an elliptic problem.

Conjecture 5.2
.

On an OT manifold
X(K,U)
there exists no pluriclosed metric with
R^{H,\psi}\geq 0
.

This question bears a resemblence to a result of Albanese
[
1
]
showing that the Inoue surface
S_{M}
does not have any positive scalar curvature metric.

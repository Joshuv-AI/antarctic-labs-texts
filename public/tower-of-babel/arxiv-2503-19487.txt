Asymptotic-preserving and positivity-preserving discontinuous Galerkin method for the semiconductor Boltzmann equation in the diffusive scaling Thanks: Submitted to the editors February, 2025.
Huan Ding, Liu Liu, Xinghui Zhong

Abstract
In this paper, we develop an asymptotic-preserving and positivity-preserving discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method for solving the semiconductor Boltzmann equation in the diffusive scaling. We first formulate the diffusive relaxation system based on the even-odd decomposition method, which allows us to split into one relaxation step and one transport step. We adopt a robust implicit scheme that can be explicitly implemented for the relaxation step that involves the stiffness of the collision term, while the third-order strong-stability-preserving Runge-Kutta method is employed for the transport step. We couple this temporal scheme with the DG method for spatial discretization, which provides additional advantages including high-order accuracy, h h - p p adaptivity, and the ability to handle arbitrary unstructured meshes. A positivity-preserving limiter is further applied to preserve physical properties of numerical solutions. The stability analysis using the even-odd decomposition is conducted for the first time. We demonstrate the accuracy and performance of our proposed scheme through several numerical examples.

Introduction

Kinetic equations have been widely used in various areas, including rarefied gas, plasma physics, astrophysics, semiconductor device modeling, social and biological sciences
[
25
]
. These equations describe the non-equilibrium dynamics of a system composed of a large number of particles and bridge atomistic and continuum models in the hierarchy of multiscale modeling. The Boltzmann-type equation, as one of the most representative models in kinetic theory, provides a power tool to describe molecular gas dynamics, radiative transfer, plasma physics, and polymer flow
[
1
]
. They have significant impacts in designing, optimization, control, and inverse problems. For example, it can be used in the design of semiconductor devices, topology optimization of gas flow channel, or risk management in quantitative finance
[
7
]
. The linearized Boltzmann equation, particularly the semiconductor Boltzmann equation, which is the focus of this study, plays a pivotal role in semiconductor device modeling
[
23
]
.

From the computational point of view, the major challenge in simulating kinetic equations arises from the presence of multiple temporal and spatial scales. This variation in scales is typically characterized by the Knudsen number
\varepsilon
, a dimensionless quantity representing the mean free path. The Knudsen number can vary by many orders of magnitude within the computational domain, and drives the system towards drift-diffusion equations as it goes to zero. In the past two decades, asymptotic-preserving (AP) schemes have been a popular and robust computational approach for kinetic and hyperbolic problems. These schemes preserve the asymptotic transition from one scale to another at the discrete level, ensuring accuracy across regimes. For a comprehensive review, see
[
18
]
. The main advantage of AP schemes lies in their efficiency across all regimes, especially in the hydrodynamic or diffusive regimes. Unlike traditional methods, AP schemes do not meed the numerical resolution of small physical parameters yet can still accurately capture the macroscopic behavior. AP schemes were first developed for steady neutron transport problems in
[
19
,
15
]
, later applied to non-stationary transport problems in
[
21
,
22
,
24
]
, and have since been successfully applied to a wide range of other problems.

In this paper, we aim to construct an efficient AP scheme within the even-odd decomposition framework using the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) spatial discretization. DG methods are a class of finite element methods using discontinuous basis functions. They were first proposed in
[
29
]
to solve linear transport equations, and later were extended to hyperbolic conservation laws in
[
12
,
10
,
11
,
9
,
13
]
, where a framework using nonlinearly stable high order Runge-Kutta (RK) time discretizations
[
31
]
and DG discretization in space to easily solve nonlinear time dependent problems, resulting the so-called RKDG method. DG methods offer several advantages including high-order accuracy,
h
-
p
adaptivity, and the ability to handle arbitrary unstructured mesh.
DG methods have gained popularity in a wide range of mathematical models. For kinetic problems, we refer to
[
4
,
3
,
5
,
16
,
17
,
26
,
6
,
2
,
27
,
14
,
34
,
33
,
32
]
and the references therein for an incomplete list.

The goal of this paper is to design high-order asymptotic-preserving DG (APDG) methods for the semiconductor Boltzmann equation. Inspired by the work in
[
20
]
, we first derive the diffusive relaxation system based on the even-odd decomposition method, which can be split into one relaxation step involving the stiffness of the collision term and one transport step. Then we adopt a robust explicitly implementable implicit scheme
[
20
]
for the relaxation step and the third-order strong-stability-preserving RK (SSPRK) method
[
30
]
for the transport step. The main novelty is that we couple this efficient temporal discretization method with high-order DG method for spatial discretization, together with the Hermite quadrature rule for the velocity discretization, rendering a high-order APDG scheme for the model problem. We further apply a positivity-preserving limiter to preserve physical properties of the solution. Moreover, we prove that the proposed fully discrete scheme is stable under a suitable CFL condition.
It is worth emphasizing that within the even-odd decomposition framework, to the authors’ best knowledge, this is the
first time
the stability analysis is conducted.
Numerical examples are provided to demonstrate the performance of the proposed method.

We summarize the features of the proposed APDG scheme in the following:

•

The scheme is high-order accurate on the fully discrete level, and uniformly accurate for different
\varepsilon
;

•

The scheme is positivity-preserving, i.e.,
f^{n}\geq 0
holds for any time level
n
;

•

The scheme is AP;

•

The scheme is stable under a suitable CFL condition.

2
Model equations and temporal discretization

In this section, we introduce the model equations and the AP temporal discretization scheme.

The Boltzmann equation is one of the most fundamental kinetic equations which describes the time evolution of the particle probability density distribution.
Under proper scaling, the dimensionless Boltzmann equation reads as

\varepsilon\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}(f),

(2.1)

where
f(x,v,t)
is the probability density function of particles at position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
at time
t>0
, with
d
representing the dimension of the computational field. The
constant
\varepsilon
is the Knudsen number, and
E
is the electric field.
The Boltzmann collision operator
\mathcal{Q}(f)
is given by

\mathcal{Q}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R^{d}}}\sigma(v,w)(M(v)f(w)-M(w)f(v))\,dw,

(2.2)

where
\sigma
is the anisotropic scattering kernel satisfying
\sigma(v,w)=\sigma(w,v)>0
, and
M
is the normalized Maxwellian distribution defined as

M(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{v^{2}}{2}}.

(2.3)

We further define the collision frequency
\lambda
as

\lambda(x,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R^{d}}}\sigma(v,w)M(w)\,dw,

(2.4)

and assume that it satisfies
\lambda\leq\mu
for some positive constant
\mu
. More details about this model can be found in
[
25
]
.

In the following subsections, we briefly review the process to formulate the diffusive relaxation system based on the even-odd decomposition method
[
21
,
22
,
20
]
and introduce the AP temporal discretization scheme, as a preparation for deriving the AP fully discrete scheme in Section
3
. For simplicity of discussion, we consider one-dimensional velocity variable
v\in\mathbb{R}
as an illustrative example to show the main idea. We refer readers to

[
21
,
22
,
20
]
for more details.

2.1
Even-odd decomposition and diffusive relaxation system

We first split (
2.1
) into two equations for
v
and
-v
, respectively,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}f(v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(v)-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}(f)(v),

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}f(-v)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(-v)+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f(-v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}(f)(-v).

(2.5b)

Without abuse of notations, here and after, we adopt simpler notations such as
f(v)
to represent
f(x,v,t)
.

Then we define the even parity
r
and odd parity
j
as

\displaystyle r(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(f(t,x,v)+f(t,x,-v)\right),

(2.6a)

\displaystyle j(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(f(t,x,v)-f(t,x,-v)),

(2.6b)

which, by adding and subtracting the two equations in (
2.5
), leads to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}r+v\cdot\nabla_{x}j-E\cdot\nabla_{v}j

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{Q}(r),

(2.7a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lambda j.

(2.7b)

The density
\rho
can be expressed in terms of the new variables as

\displaystyle\rho=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(v)dv=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}r(v)dv.

(2.8)

As in
[
20
,
22
]
, (
2.7
) can be rewritten into the following diffusive relaxation system

\begin{split}\partial_{t}r+v\cdot\nabla_{x}j-E\cdot\nabla_{v}j&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{Q}(r),\\
\partial_{t}j+\phi(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r)&=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\lambda j+(1-\varepsilon^{2}\phi)(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r)\right),\end{split}

(2.9)

where
\phi
can be chosen in the simple form of
\phi=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}
.

2.2
Temporal discretization

In this subsection, we present the AP temporal discretization scheme for the diffusive relaxation system (
2.9
).

As in
[
21
,
22
]
, the system (
2.9
) can employ the conventional splitting resulting one relaxation step

\displaystyle\partial_{t}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{Q}(r),

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\lambda j+(1-\varepsilon^{2}\phi)(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r)\right),

(2.10b)

and one transport step

\displaystyle\partial_{t}r+v\cdot\nabla_{x}j-E\cdot\nabla_{v}j

\displaystyle=0,

(2.11a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j+\phi(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r)

\displaystyle=0.

(2.11b)

Let
\Delta t
denote the time step size and
(r^{n},j^{n})
denote the solution at
n
-th time level. The temporal scheme for advancing (
r^{n},j^{n}
) to (
r^{n+1},j^{n+1}
) consists of schemes for updating (
r^{n},j^{n}
) to the intermediate solution (
r^{\ast},j^{\ast}
) by the relaxation step and for updating (
r^{\ast},j^{\ast}
) to (
r^{n+1},j^{n+1}
) by the transport step.

For the relaxation step (
2.10
) which involves the challenge arising from the stiffness of the collision term, we adopt a robust explicitly implementable implicit scheme proposed in
[
20
]
, where the variable
r
is discretized by the following first-order time-relaxed scheme

r^{*}=(1-\tau)r^{n}+\tau(1-\tau)\frac{\mathcal{P}(r^{n})}{\mu}+\tau^{2}\rho^{n}M,

(2.12)

with
\tau=1-\exp(-\mu\,\Delta t/\varepsilon^{2})
and
\mathcal{P}(r)=\mathcal{Q}(r)+\mu r
being a non-negative operator, and the variable
j
is discretized by the backward Euler scheme

j^{\ast}=\alpha j^{n}-\beta(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r^{\ast}-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r^{\ast}),

(2.13)

with the coefficients defined as

\alpha=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}+\lambda\Delta t},\quad\beta=\frac{\Delta t(1-\varepsilon^{2}\phi)}{\varepsilon^{2}+\lambda\Delta t}.

It is worth mentioning that the scheme (
2.12
)-(
2.13
) is asymptotic-preserving. We refer readers to
[
20
]
for a comprehensive discussion of the technical intricacies.

For the transport step (
2.11
), we adopt the third-order SSPRK method
[
30
]
. This time discretization is a convex combination of first order forward Euler steps. Therefore, to simplify the presentation, we use the following first-order forward Euler scheme

\displaystyle\frac{r^{n+1}-r^{*}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle+v\cdot\nabla_{x}j^{*}-E\cdot\partial_{v}j^{*}=0,

(2.14a)

\displaystyle\frac{j^{n+1}-j^{*}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle+\phi(v\cdot\nabla_{x}r^{*}-E\cdot\nabla_{v}r^{*})=0,

(2.14b)

as an example to show the framework of the fully discrete scheme and theoretical results in the following sections. Other explicit solvers can also be used here.

Fully discrete numerical scheme

In this section, we present the fully discrete scheme with velocity and space discretizations for the systems (
2.10
) and (
2.11
). For the space discretization, we employ the DG method with a positive-preserving limiter, which will be discussed in the following subsections. For the velocity discretization, we adopt the same approach in
[
20
]
, and the details are provided in Appendix
A
. For simplicity, we only present the one-dimensional schemes in both spatial and velocity spaces. It is straightforward to generalize to the multi-dimensional cases.

3.1
Discontinuous Galerkin method

In this subsection, we apply the DG method for the spatial discretization of the temporal schemes (
2.12
), (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), while leaving the variable
v
continuous in the discussions.

Let
\Omega_{x}=[x_{L},x_{R}]
be the computational domain. We consider a uniform partition of
\Omega_{x}
into
N_{x}
cells of size
h=(x_{R}-x_{L})/N_{x}
.
Denote the cell as
I_{i}=[x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}]
and the cell center as
x_{i}=(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+x_{i-\frac{1}{2}})/2
, for
i=1,\dots,N
, where
x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=x_{L}+ih,\quad i=0,\dots,N.
The finite element approximation space is defined by

\mathbb{V}^{k}_{h}=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):u|_{I_{i}}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(I_{i}),\;i=1,\dots,N\},

where
\mathbb{P}^{k}(I_{i})
denotes the polynomial space of degree at most
k
defined in the cell
I_{i}
. We also define the jump and average of
u
at the cell interface
x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
as

\displaystyle[u]_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}-u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\qquad\{u\}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}+u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}),

(3.1)

where
u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}
and
u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}
denote the left and right limits of the function
u
at the cell interface
x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
, respectively. We further define the following notations to simplify the presentation:

(u,w)_{i}=\int_{I_{i}}uw\,dx,\qquad(u,w)=\int_{\Omega_{x}}uw\,dx.

With a slight abuse of notations, the DG method with the time integrator (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) is defined as follows: find the unique solutions
j^{\ast},\ r^{n+1},\ j^{n+1}\in\mathbb{V}^{k}_{h}
such that for all test functions
\xi,\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{V}^{k}_{h}
,

\displaystyle(j^{\ast},\eta)_{i}=\alpha(j^{n},\eta)_{i}+\beta\left(v(r^{*},\partial_{x}\eta)_{i}-v\hat{r}^{*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{-}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+v\hat{r}^{*}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{+}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+(E\partial_{v}r^{*},\eta)_{i}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r^{n+1}-r^{*}}{\Delta t},\xi\right)_{i}+v\left(\tilde{j}^{*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\xi^{-}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\tilde{j}^{*}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\xi^{+}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}-(j^{*},\partial_{x}\xi)_{i}\right)-(E\partial_{v}j^{*},\xi)_{i}=0,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{j^{n+1}-j^{*}}{\Delta t},\zeta\right)_{i}+\phi v\left(\tilde{r}^{*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\zeta^{-}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\tilde{r}^{*}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta^{+}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}-(r^{*},\partial_{x}\zeta)_{i}\right)-(\phi E\cdot\partial_{v}r^{*},\zeta)_{i}=0,

holds for
i=1,\dots,N_{x}
, while the solution
r^{*}\in\mathbb{V}^{k}_{h}
is obtained directly from the right hand side of (
2.12
). Here,
\hat{r}^{*},\tilde{j}^{*}
and
\tilde{r}^{*}
are the so-called numerical fluxes, which are single valued functions defined at the cell interfaces and depending on the values of the numerical solutions from both sides of the interface. We choose the following alternating fluxes

\hat{r}^{*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=r^{+}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\tilde{j}^{*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=j^{-}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\tilde{r}^{*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=r^{-}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}.

For the convenience of analysis, by summing up the above DG schemes over all cells, we obtain the DG method with the time integrator (
2.12
), (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) in the global form for the relaxation step

\displaystyle r^{\ast}

\displaystyle=(1-\tau)r^{n}+\tau(1-\tau)\frac{\mathcal{P}(r^{n})}{\mu}+\tau^{2}\rho^{n}M,

(3.3a)

\displaystyle(j^{\ast},\eta)

\displaystyle=\alpha(j^{n},\eta)+\beta\left(v\sum_{i}\hat{r}^{*}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}[\eta]_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+v(r^{*},\partial_{x}\eta)+(E\partial_{v}r^{*},\eta)\right),

(3.3b)

and for the transport step

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r^{n+1}-r^{*}}{\Delta t},\xi\right)

\displaystyle-v\left(\sum_{i}\tilde{j}^{*}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}[\xi]_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+(j^{*},\partial_{x}\xi)\right)-(E\partial_{v}j^{*},\xi)=0,

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{j^{n+1}-j^{*}}{\Delta t},\zeta\right)

\displaystyle-\phi v\left(\sum_{i}\tilde{r}^{*}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}[\zeta]_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+(r^{*},\partial_{x}\zeta)\right)-(\phi E\partial_{v}r^{*},\zeta)=0.

(3.4b)

3.2
Positivity-preserving limiter

In this section, we apply a positivity-preserving limiter to ensure that the obtained numerical solution
f^{n}
at each time step is non-negative, which satisfies the physical property of the distribution function. For more details regarding this type of limiters, see the review paper
[
36
]
.

Starting from the numerical solution
f^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{k}
at time level
n
, which can be obtained by
f^{n}=r^{n}+\varepsilon j^{n}
according to (
2.6
), a positivity-preserving limiter is applied to “limit”
f^{n}
to obtain a new function
f^{n,new}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{k}
, which is then advanced to the next time level. The limiting procedure to construct
f^{n,new}
from
f^{n}
is as follows.

For simplicity, we omit the superscript
n
and denote
f_{i}
as the numerical solution in the cell
I_{i}
and
\bar{f}_{i}
as the cell average of
f_{i}
in the cell
I_{i}
. The “limited” function
f_{i}^{new}
is constructed as

f_{i}^{new}=\theta(f_{i}-\bar{f}_{i})+\bar{f}_{i},

(3.5)

where
\theta
is determined by

\theta=\min\left(\frac{\bar{f}_{i}}{\bar{f}_{i}-f_{\mathrm{min}}},\;1\right),\qquad f_{\mathrm{min}}=\min_{x\in I_{i}}f_{i}.

(3.6)

We can easily conclude from Equation (
3.5
) that the limiter preserves the cell average, i.e.
\bar{f}_{j}=\bar{f}_{j}^{new}
, and guarantee the positivity of
f^{new}_{j}
if
\bar{f}_{j}\geq 0
.

For the parity variables
r
and
j
in the numerical schemes (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), it follows from their definition in (
2.6
) that the limited functions
r_{i}^{new}
and
j_{i}^{new}
in the cell
I_{i}
are constructed as

\displaystyle r_{i}^{new}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(f_{i}^{new}(v)+f_{i}^{new}(-v)),

(3.7a)

\displaystyle j_{i}^{new}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(f_{i}^{new}(v)-f^{new}(-v)).

(3.7b)

3.3
Boundary conditions

In this subsection, we show how to deal with the boundary conditions in the DG framework. We restrict our attention to the case of inflow boundary conditions, which are considered in our numerical examples. Other types of boundary conditions can be handled in a similar way.

The incoming boundary conditions for the model problem (
2.1
) is given by

f(t,x_{L},v)=F_{L}(v),\quad f(t,x_{R},-v)=F_{R}(v),\qquad v>0,

(3.8)

where
F_{L}
and
F_{R}
are assigned nonnegative functions. Similar to
[
20
]
, the approximation of the boundary conditions for
r
up to
O(\varepsilon^{2})
is represented as

\displaystyle\begin{split}r-\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}(v\partial_{x}r-E\partial_{v}F_{L})\Big|_{x=x_{L}}=F_{L},\\
r+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}(v\partial_{x}r-E\partial_{v}F_{R})\Big|_{x=x_{R}}=F_{R},\end{split}

(3.9)

and for
\epsilon\ll 1
, a reasonable approximation for
j
is given by

j(v)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(-v\partial_{x}r+E\partial_{v}r),\quad x=x_{L},x_{R}.

(3.10)

By implementing these boundary conditions in the DG framework, the numerical fluxes
\hat{r}
at the cell interfaces
x_{1/2}
and
x_{N+1/2}
is discretized as

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{h(\lambda F_{L}-\varepsilon E_{1/2}\,\partial_{v}F_{L})+2\varepsilon vr_{1}}{\lambda h+2\varepsilon v},

(3.11a)

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{N+1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{h(\lambda F_{R}+\varepsilon E_{N+1/2}\,\partial_{v}F_{R})+2\varepsilon vr_{N}}{\lambda h+2\varepsilon v},

(3.11b)

where
r_{1}=r(x_{1})
and
r_{N}=r(x_{N})
are the values of the DG solution

at the centers of the cells
I_{1}
and
I_{N}
, respectively.
Similarly, the numerical fluxes
\hat{j}
at the cell interfaces
x_{1/2}
and
x_{N+1/2}
is

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(-v\frac{r_{1}-\hat{r}_{1/2}}{h/2}+E_{1/2}\partial_{v}F_{L}\right),

(3.12a)

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{N+1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(-v\frac{r_{N}-\hat{r}_{N+1/2}}{h/2}+E_{N+1/2}\partial_{v}F_{R}\right).

(3.12b)

\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\psi,u)=-(\psi,u_{x})-\sum_{i}\psi^{\pm}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}[u]_{i-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\psi,\,u\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{k}.

4.1
Stability analysis

In this section, we carry out the stability analysis of the APDG scheme (
4.1
) for the model problem with

4.1.1
Preliminaries

We adopt the weighted
L^{2}
norm for the velocity variable
v
, given by

\|f\|_{L^{2}(dv),\mathrm{weighted}}=\left<f^{2}\right>^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{2}\frac{\lambda}{M}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(4.3)

with the operator

Theorem 3
(Stability).

Suppose
\varepsilon<1
,
E=0
, and
\sigma
is a constant in the model problem with periodic boundary conditions. When the APDG scheme (

) is applied to the system, the numerical solution

|\!|\!|r^{n+1}|\!|\!|^{2}+{\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{n+1}|\!|\!|^{2}\leq|\!|\!|r^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}+{\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2},

(4.8)

under the CFL condition

\Delta t\leq\min\left\{\frac{\lambda h^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon^{2})(C_{i}+4C_{t}^{2})(2N_{v}+1)},\ C_{0}h\right\},

(4.9)

where
C_{i},C_{t},C_{0}
are positive constants independent of
h
and
\Delta t
.

Remark 4.1
.

One can get a uniform stability respect to
\varepsilon
, with the CFL condition tends to be
\Delta t=O(h^{2})
as
\varepsilon\ll 1
.

Lemma 4
.

Assume
\sigma
is a constant. Then the numerical solution
r^{*}
of the scheme (
4.1
) satisfies

|\!|\!|r^{*}|\!|\!|\leq|\!|\!|r^{n}|\!|\!|

(4.10)

Proof.

It follows from the assumption that
\sigma
is a constant that
\lambda=\sigma
, and
\mathcal{P}(r)=\lambda\rho M+(\mu-\lambda)r
. Then the scheme (
4.1a
) can be rewritten as

r^{*}=\delta r^{n}+\left(1-\delta\right)\rho^{n}M,

(4.11)

where
\delta=\left(1-\tau^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\tau(1-\tau)\right).

By taking the norm
|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|
on both sides of Equation (
4.11
),
we obtain

|\!|\!|r^{*}|\!|\!|\leq\delta|\!|\!|r^{n}|\!|\!|+(1-\delta)|\!|\!|\rho^{n}M|\!|\!|.

(4.12)

Thus, the conclusion (
4.10
) can be proved by showing that

|\!|\!|\rho^{n}M|\!|\!|\leq|\!|\!|r^{n}|\!|\!|,

(4.13)

which will be discussed in the following. Without causing any ambiguity, we omit the superscript
n
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}M^{2}\frac{\lambda}{M}\,dv=\lambda\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}r\,dv\right)^{2}\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{r^{2}}{M}\,dv\int_{\mathbb{R}}M\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}}r^{2}\frac{\lambda}{M}\,dv.

(4.14)

Then the proof is complete by integrating both sides of (
4.14
) over
x
.

Lemma 5
.

Under the same assumption as in Theorem
3
, the numerical solution
r^{n+1},j^{*},r^{*},j^{n}
of the APDG scheme (
4.1
) satisfies

|\!|\!|r^{n+1}|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}\leq|\!|\!|r^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}

(4.15)

under the CFL condition

\frac{\Delta t}{h^{2}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{(1-\varepsilon^{2})(C_{i}+4C_{t}^{2})(2N_{v}+1)},

(4.16)

where
C_{i},C_{t}
are positive constants independent of
\Delta t
,
h
and the solution.

Proof.

By taking
\eta=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}j^{*}
in (
4.1b
) and
\xi=r^{n+1}
in (
4.1c
),
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\Delta t(1-\varepsilon^{2})}\left(j^{*}-j^{n},j^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}(j^{*},j^{*})-v\mathcal{L}^{+}(r^{*},j^{*}),

(4.17a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(r^{n+1}-r^{*},r^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle+v\mathcal{L}^{-}(j^{*},r^{n+1})=0.

(4.17b)

By adding (
4.17a
) and (
4.17b
) together, combining with the properties of
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
in Lemma
2
and the fact

\displaystyle\left(j^{*}-j^{n},j^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|j^{*}\|^{2}-\|j^{n}\|^{2}+\|j^{*}-j^{n}\|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\left(r^{n+1}-r^{*},r^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|r^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|r^{*}\|^{2}+\|r^{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}\right),

and applying the operator
\left<\cdot\right>
defined in (
4.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(|\!|\!|r^{n+1}|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-|\!|\!|r^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\Delta t}|\!|\!|r^{n+1}-r^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2\Delta t(1-\varepsilon^{2})}|\!|\!|j^{*}-j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}+\left<v\mathcal{L}^{+}(r^{n+1}-r^{*},j^{*})\right>.

Now we estimate the term
\left<v\mathcal{L}^{+}(r^{n+1}-r^{*},j^{*})\right>
, which, according to the definition (
4.2

\displaystyle|\left<v\mathcal{L}^{+}(r^{n+1}-r^{*},j^{*})\right>|=|\left<v\mathcal{L}^{+}(\Delta r,j^{*})\right>|

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\int_{I_{i}}\left<\left|v\Delta r\partial_{x}j^{*}\right|\right>dx+\sum_{i}\left<\left|v\Delta r_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{+}[j^{*}]_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\right|\right>\triangleq\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2},

where
\Delta r=r^{n+1}-r^{*}
and
\Delta r_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{+}=r^{n+1,+}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}-r^{*,+}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
.

For the term
\Lambda_{1}
, a simple use of Young’s inequality, together with Lemma
1
and the definition of the norm in (
4.5
), yields

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{1}\sum_{i}\int_{I_{i}}\left<\Delta r^{2}dx\right>+\frac{1}{4\theta_{1}}\sum_{i}\int_{I_{i}}\left<(v\partial_{x}j)^{2}\right>dx

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{1}|\!|\!|\Delta r|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{C_{i}}{4\theta_{1}h^{2}}|\!|\!|vj|\!|\!|^{2},

where
\theta_{1}
is an arbitrary positive constant. Note
j=M\psi
for the velocity discretization with
\psi
defined in (
A.1
). Clearly
\psi
is an
N_{v}
-th polynomial in
v
. Thus
\psi^{2}
is a
2N_{v}
-th polynomial in
v
, and can be rewritten as

\psi(v)^{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2N_{v}}\tilde{\psi}_{\ell}v^{\ell},

where the coefficients
\psi_{\ell}
are linear combinations of the coefficients of the Hermite expansion (
A.1
). Then the term
|\!|\!|vj|\!|\!|
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\!|\!|vj|\!|\!|^{2}&=\int_{\Omega_{x}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2N_{v}}\tilde{\psi}_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda Mv^{\ell+2}\,dvdx=\int_{\Omega_{x}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2N_{v}}(\ell+1)\tilde{\psi}_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda Mv^{\ell}\,dvdx\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega_{x}}(2N_{v}+1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{2N_{v}}\tilde{\psi}_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda Mv^{\ell}\,dvdx=(2N_{v}+1)|\!|\!|j|\!|\!|^{2},\end{split}

(4.21)

where we have used the following equality,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}Mv^{\ell+2}\,dv=(\ell+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}Mv^{\ell}\,dv,\quad\ell\geq 0.

By substituting (
4.21
) into (
4.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}\leq\theta_{1}|\!|\!|\Delta r|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{C_{i}(2N_{v}+1)}{4\theta_{1}h^{2}}|\!|\!|j|\!|\!|^{2}.

(4.22)

For the term
\Lambda_{2}
, similar estimate can be performed to obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta_{2}h}{C_{t}}\sum_{i}\left<(\Delta r_{i-1/2}^{+})^{2}\right>+\frac{C_{t}}{4\theta_{2}h}\sum_{i}\left<(v[j]_{i-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\right>

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta_{2}h}{C_{t}}\sum_{i}\left<(\Delta r_{i-1/2}^{+})^{2}\right>+\frac{C_{t}}{2\theta_{2}h}\sum_{i}\left(\left<(vj^{*,+}_{i-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}+(vj^{*,-}_{i-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\right>\right)

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{2}|\!|\!|\Delta r|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{C_{t}^{2}}{\theta_{2}h^{2}}|\!|\!|vj|\!|\!|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{2}|\!|\!|\Delta r|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{C_{t}^{2}(2N_{v}+1)}{\theta_{2}h^{2}}|\!|\!|vj|\!|\!|^{2},

where
\theta_{2}
is an arbitrary positive constant.

It follows from substituting (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) into (
4.18
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(|\!|\!|r^{n+1}|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-|\!|\!|r^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}\right)

(4.24)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}-\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\right)|\!|\!|r^{n+1}-r^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2\Delta t(1-\varepsilon^{2})}|\!|\!|j^{*}-j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{C_{i}(2N_{v}+1)}{4\theta_{1}h^{2}}+\frac{C_{t}^{2}(2N_{v}+1)}{\theta_{2}h^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2},

which, by choosing
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=\frac{1}{4\Delta t}
and dropping the positive term
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2\Delta t(1-\varepsilon^{2})}|\!|\!|j^{*}-j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}
, yileds

\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(|\!|\!|r^{n+1}|\!|\!|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-|\!|\!|r^{*}|\!|\!|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}|\!|\!|j^{n}|\!|\!|^{2}\right)\leq\gamma|\!|\!|j^{*}|\!|\!|^{2},

\frac{\Delta t}{h^{2}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{(1-\varepsilon^{2})(C_{i}+4C_{t}^{2})(2N_{v}+1)}.

4.2
Analysis of asymptotic-preserving property

In this subsection, we discuss the AP property of the proposed scheme.

It is straightforward to find that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\tau\to 1,\alpha\to 0
, and
\beta\to\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Then the scheme (
4.1
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle r^{*}

\displaystyle=\rho^{n}M,

(4.27a)

\displaystyle(j^{*},\eta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{v}{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{+}(r^{*},\eta),

(4.27b)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r^{n+1}-r^{*}}{\Delta t},\xi\right)

\displaystyle+v\mathcal{L^{-}}(j^{*},\xi)=0,

(4.27c)

where we omit the scheme (
4.1d
) for updating from
j^{*}
to

, since

is no longer needed for updating
j^{*}
in (
4.27b
), and consequently there is no need for updating

.

We then introduce a new variable
g^{*}
defined as

j^{*}=-\frac{vM}{\lambda}g^{*}.

(4.28)

By substituting (
4.27a
) and (
4.28
) into (
4.27b
) and
(
4.27c
), and integrating with respect to
v
, we have

\displaystyle(g^{*},\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{+}(\rho^{n},\eta),

(4.29a)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n}}{\Delta t},\xi\right)

\displaystyle=D\mathcal{L^{-}}(g^{*},\xi),

(4.29b)

with the constant

(4.30)

The limiting scheme (
4.29
) is exactly the local DG (LDG) scheme with the forward Euler method for the following limiting diffusion equation

\partial_{t}\rho=D\partial_{xx}\rho,

(4.31)

which demonstrates the AP property of the proposed scheme.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the linear semiconductor Boltzmann equation under the diffusive scaling. An efficient and accurate numerical scheme that owns the property of asymptotic-preserving and positivity-preserving is proposed, with DG discretization in space and even-odd decomposition in time. We show the stability analysis in the even-odd decomposition framework and provide the CFL condition. Extensive numerical experiments have shown the accurate and robust performance of our proposed scheme. In the future work, we will develop higher-order IMEX Runge-Kutta methods and study other nonlinear kinetic equations.

Appendix A
Velocity discretization

In this section, we provide the velocity discretization using the Hermite polynomials, which has been widely used, see e.g.
[
24
,
20
]
.

Let
f(t,x,v)=\psi(t,x,v)M(v)
, with
M(v)
given in (
2.3
)
and

\psi(t,x,v)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\psi_{\ell}(t,x)\tilde{H}_{\ell}(v),

(A.1)

where
\tilde{H}_{\ell}
are the renormalized Hermite polynomials
defined as
\tilde{H}_{-1}=0
,
\tilde{H}_{0}=1/(2\pi)^{1/4}
and

\tilde{H}_{\ell+1}=v\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ell+1}}\tilde{H}_{\ell}-\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}}\tilde{H}_{\ell-1}\quad\text{for }\ell\geq 0,

satisfying
\partial_{v}\tilde{H}_{\ell}=\sqrt{\ell}\,\tilde{H}_{\ell-1}
.
The inverse Hermite expansion is given by

\psi_{i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\psi(v_{\ell})\,\tilde{H}_{i}(v_{\ell})\,w_{\ell},\quad i=0,\dots,N_{v},

(A.2)

where
\big\{v_{\ell}\big\}_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}
are the Gauss-Hermite quadrature points, and
\big\{w_{\ell}\big\}_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}
are the associated quadrature weights. Here and after, we omit the arguments
x,t
and adopt simpler notations such as
\psi_{\ell}
,
\psi(v)
,
f(v)
, etc..

The collision operator
Q
can be computed by

Q(f)(v)=M(v)\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\sigma(v,v_{\ell})\,\psi(v_{\ell})\,w_{\ell}-\lambda(v)f(v),

with
\lambda(v)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\sigma(v,v_{\ell})\,w_{\ell}
. Therefore, we can compute the values of
Q(f)(v)
on the Gauss-Hermite quadrature points as

Q(f)(v_{i})=M(v_{i})\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\sigma(v_{i},v_{\ell})\,\psi(v_{\ell})\,w_{\ell}-\lambda(v_{i})f(v_{i}).

(A.3)

It follows from (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) that the derivative of
\psi
with respect to
v
by

\displaystyle\partial_{v}\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\psi_{\ell}\,\partial_{v}\tilde{H}_{\ell}(v)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\psi_{\ell}\sqrt{\ell}\,\tilde{H}_{\ell-1}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\sum_{i=0}^{N_{v}}\psi(v_{i})\,\tilde{H}_{\ell}(v_{i})w_{i}\sqrt{\ell}\,\tilde{H}_{\ell-1}(v).

Thus, the derivative of
\psi
with respect to
v
at the Gauss-Hermite quadrature points can be further expressed as

\partial_{v}\psi(v_{m})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\sum_{i=0}^{N_{v}}\psi(v_{i})\,\tilde{H}_{\ell}(v_{i})w_{i}\sqrt{\ell}\,\tilde{H}_{\ell-1}(v_{m})=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{v}}\psi(v_{i})\,C_{im},

(A.4)

where
C_{im}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{v}}\sqrt{\ell}\,\tilde{H}_{\ell}(v_{i})\tilde{H}_{\ell-1}(v_{m})w_{i}

, which can be precomputed before the time iteration.

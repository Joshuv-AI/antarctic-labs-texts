Freeze-in gravitational waves and dark matter in warm inflation
Quan Chen, Siyu Jiang, Dayun Qiu, Peilin Chen, Fa Peng Huang

Abstract
Recent study Berghaus et al. 2025 has suggested that warm inflation may be realized with Standard Model gauge interaction alone. Motivated by this framework, we investigate the gravitational wave spectrum and graviton-portal dark matter production through the freeze-in process generated during warm inflation scenarios. We perform a comparative analysis for different dissipation terms, focusing on their distinct gravitational wave signatures in the high-frequency regime. Our findings reveal qualitative and quantitative differences in the spectral behavior, offering a preliminary pathway for discriminating among various inflationary and dark matter models through high-frequency gravitational wave signals.



I
Introduction

Cosmic inflation
Starobinsky 1980
;
Guth 1981
;
Linde 1982
can naturally explain the cosmic microwave background radiation and the large-scale structure observations, since the density perturbation of the inflation field can provide the primordial seeds of our Universe. Warm inflation (WI)
Berera 1995
is one type of inflationary scenario that fundamentally alters the conventional picture of the early universe’s expansion by including dissipative effects and concurrent radiation production during inflation. Unlike the standard cold inflation model, where the universe supercools and reheating occurs from the late stage of inflation
Huang 2025
, WI maintains a thermal bath throughout the inflation process by dissipating the inflaton field’s energy into radiation. This continuous particle production modifies the dynamics of the inflation field and the generation of primordial density perturbations, offering possible solutions to challenges faced by cold inflation such as the “eta problem” and allowing for sub-Planckian field excursions.
The conceptual foundation of WI integrates quantum field theory techniques to model dissipative processes, showing that interactions between the inflation field and other fields produce radiation simultaneously with inflationary expansion.

Although the concept of WI presents a compelling alternative to conventional cold inflationary scenarios, constructing a theoretically consistent WI model has proven challenging. Early formulations of WI relied on scalar couplings between the inflaton field and the particles comprising the thermal bath. However, subsequent analyses revealed that scalar interactions predominantly induce thermal corrections to the inflaton potential, rather than generating significant dissipative friction. These thermal effects tend to hinder inflationary dynamics rather than facilitate them, complicating efforts to build viable WI models based on scalar field interactions
Yokoyama and Linde 1999
.

Recently, K.V. Berghaus, M. Drewes, and S. Zell in Ref.
Berghaus et al. 2025
, demonstrate, for the first time, that WI can be consistently realized through Standard Model gauge interactions. This finding provides an intriguing framework for dissipative dynamics during inflation and establishes a concrete connection between early-universe cosmology and well-motivated particle physics interactions. Motivated by this idea in Ref.
Berghaus et al. 2025
, we investigate the production of gravitational wave (GW) originating from the scattering of thermal plasma constituents and the graviton-portal dark matter (DM) during the inflationary epoch and estimate their relic density. The GW produced during reheating in the standard cold inflation paradigm has been investigated before
Khlebnikov and Tkachev 1997
;
Bassett 1997
;
Nakayama and Tang 2019
;
Huang and Yin 2019
;
Barman et al. 2023
;
Bernal et al. 2025
;
Bernal and Xu 2025
;
Xu 2024
. In WI, the universe naturally transitions from the inflationary epoch to the radiation-dominated era without requiring a reheating phase, and the gravitons can be continuously produced from plasma
Ghiglieri and Laine 2015
;
Ghiglieri et al. 2020
;
Ringwald et al. 2021
;
Montefalcone et al. 2025
. The graviton and DM production mechanism closely resembles the freeze-in DM scenarios
Freese et al. 2024
;
Ghiglieri et al. 2024
. In this work, we mainly investigate the GW spectrum in the high frequency regime and the associated DM production mediated by gravitons, within the framework of WI. Our analysis encompasses both a simplified version of the recently proposed WI scenario based solely on Standard Model gauge interactions
Berghaus et al. 2025
, as well as the typical WI model considered in Ref.
Montefalcone et al. 2025
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we briefly review the mechanism of WI. In Sec.
III
, we calculate the GW spectrum from freeze-in mechanism during WI. In Sec.
IV
, we also estimate DM production solely by gravitational interaction from Standard Model plasma, and study the correlation between the DM mass and the quartic coupling of the inflaton field. Finally, a concise conclusion is given in Sec.
V
.

II
Warm inflation

During the WI, the evolution of the background is governed by the following equations:

\ddot{\phi}+(3H+\Upsilon)\dot{\phi}+V^{\prime}(\phi)=0

(1)

\dot{\rho}_{R}+4H\rho_{R}=\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}

(2)

H^{2}=\frac{8\pi}{3M_{pl}^{2}}\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}+V(\phi)+\rho_{R}\right)

(3)

where
\phi
is the inflaton field,
H
the Hubble parameter,
\rho_{R}
the radiation energy density, and
M_{pl}\simeq 1.221\times 10^{19}\mathrm{GeV}
the Planck mass, respectively.
\Upsilon
is the dissipation coefficient which decides the energy transfer between the inflaton and the thermal bath. The distinction between the warm and cold inflation is that in the former model, the dissipation term causes the radiation energy not to vanish. In this work, we assume the inflation potential

V(\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^{4}

(4)

The radiation energy density with temperature
T
is given by

\rho_{R}=\frac{g_{*}\pi^{2}}{30}T^{4}

(5)

where
g_{*}
is the degrees of freedom of the thermal bath and we set
g_{*}=106.75
.

For convenience, one defines the dimensionless parameter
Q\equiv\frac{\Upsilon}{3H}
. Then, we can classify WI based on the parameter
Q
as follows: for
Q>1
, strong dissipative regime; for
Q<1
, weak dissipative regime.
The dissipation coefficient
\Upsilon
has the general form

\Upsilon\approx\kappa\frac{T^{a}\phi^{b}}{f^{a+b-1}}

(6)

where
\kappa
is a constant.
a
, and
b
could be some numerical powers.
f
could be the symmetry-breaking scale. There are some models that provide the specific form of
\Upsilon
. For example, Refs.
Berghaus et al. 2020
;
Laine and Procacci 2021
;
Berghaus et al. 2025
realize the WI by introducing axion coupling to a pure Yang-Mills gauge group and give
\Upsilon\propto T^{3}
. Ref.
Bastero-Gil et al. 2016
proposes a scenario which is similar to the little Higgs model, where
\Upsilon\propto T
. In this work, we discuss these two cases separately,
\Upsilon\propto T
,
T^{3}
, and compare their corresponding predictions.
In our calculations, we compare different values of
\lambda
and
Q_{\mathrm{ini}}
(the dissipation strength at the start of inflation) and fix the e-folding number to
N_{e}=60
.

III
freeze-in production of gravitons

In the WI model, gravitons are continuously produced from the plasma via particle scattering processes during and after inflation. We only consider gravitons with sufficiently high momenta to ensure that they always remain within the horizon. Their evolution is governed by the Boltzmann equation

\frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}}{dt}+4H\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}=\frac{4T^{7}}{M^{2}_{pl}}\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\hat{\eta}(\hat{k},T)

(7)

with
\ \hat{k}=\frac{k}{T}
. Here
\rho_{\rm GW}
is the GW energy density and
k
represents the momentum of graviton. The
\hat{\eta}
function takes into account all the Standard Model contributions and has been calculated in Ref.
Ghiglieri and Laine 2015
and Ref.
Ghiglieri et al. 2020
using the leading-log approximation. The calculation including hard thermal loop resummation has been done in Ref.
Ringwald et al. 2021
. Here we only consider the results of leading-log approximation
Ghiglieri and Laine 2015
,

\hat{\eta}(\hat{k},T)=\frac{\hat{k}}{8\pi(e^{\hat{k}}-1)}\sum_{i}d_{i}\hat{m}_{i}^{2}(T)\ln\left(\frac{5}{\hat{m}_{i}(T)}\right)

(8)

with

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=1,\quad d_{2}=3,\quad d_{3}=8,

(9)

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{11}{6}g_{1}^{2},\quad\hat{m}_{2}^{2}=\frac{11}{6}g_{2}^{2},\quad\hat{m}_{3}^{2}=g_{3}^{2}

where
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
,
g_{3}
are gauge couplings of the Standard Model
U(1)_{Y}
,
SU(2)_{L}
and
SU(3)_{C}
gauge groups, respectively.

Using
dN_{e}/dt=H
, the left-handed side of Eq. (
7
) can be re-parametrized as

\frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}}{dt}+4H\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}=He^{-4N_{e}}\frac{d(\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}e^{4N_{e}})}{dN_{e}}

(10)

Then the GW energy density can be expressed as

\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}M_{pl}^{2}}e^{-4N_{e}}\int_{N_{e}^{i}}^{N_{e}}dN_{e}^{\prime}\ e^{4N_{e}^{\prime}}\frac{T^{7}}{H}\int d\hat{k}\ \hat{k}^{2}\hat{\eta}(\hat{k},T)

(11)

We can then calculate the GW energy spectrum

\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}=\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}}{d\ln k}

(12)

and use

\rho_{\gamma}=\rho_{\gamma,0}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{4}\big(\frac{g_{*s,0}}{g_{*s}}\big)^{\frac{4}{3}}\quad,\quad\frac{k}{T}=\frac{k_{0}}{T_{0}}\left(\frac{g_{*s,0}}{g_{*s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}

(13)

where
g_{*s,0}=3.931
is the present degrees of freedom of the entropy density.
\rho_{\gamma,0}
is the current photon energy density.

\begin{split}\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(k_{0})h^{2}=&\frac{2}{\pi^{2}M_{pl}^{2}}\frac{\Omega_{\gamma,0}h^{2}}{\rho_{c}(N_{e})}\frac{g_{*}(N_{e})}{g_{\gamma}}\left(\frac{g_{*s,0}}{g_{*s}(N_{e})}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\times\\
&e^{-4N_{e}}\int_{N_{e}^{i}}^{N_{e}}dN_{e}^{\prime}\ e^{4N_{e}^{\prime}}\frac{T^{7}}{H}\left(\frac{k_{0}}{T_{0}}\right)^{3}\hat{\eta}\left[\frac{k_{0}}{T_{0}}\left(\frac{g_{*s}}{g_{*s,0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},T\right]\end{split}

(14)

where
\Omega_{\gamma,0}=5.38\times 10^{-5}
,
g_{\gamma}=2
and the dimensionless Hubble parameter
h=0.68
. We choose
g_{*s}\simeq g_{*}=106.75
.
The present frequency is
f=\frac{k_{0}}{2\pi}
. It is easy to see that the GW spectrum can be parametrized by the form of

\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(f)=\mathcal{A}\frac{(f/f_{\rm peak})^{4}}{e^{f/f_{\rm peak}}-1}

(15)

where the peak frequency can be derived from the maximum point of
\hat{k}^{4}/(e^{\hat{k}}-1)
,

f_{\rm peak}\simeq 3.92\frac{T_{0}}{2\pi}\left(\frac{g_{*s,0}}{g_{*s}}\right)\approx 4.76\times 10^{11}~\mathrm{Hz}

(16)

So different
Q_{\mathrm{ini}}
and different forms of dissipation terms only affect the strength of the GW spectrum.

IV
freeze-in production of purely gravitational dark matter

The DM could be produced during the reheating stage in the standard cold inflation paradigm
Garny et al. 2018
;
Tang and Wu 2017
;
Bernal et al. 2018
;
Clery et al. 2022
;
Xu 2025
. However, it is also expected that the DM can be produced during the WI. This has been considered in Ref.
Freese et al. 2024
where the authors found that the DM relic density could be enhanced by several orders, compared with the cold inflation.
We consider DM produced by purely gravitational interaction from Standard Model plasma.

\frac{dn_{\chi}}{dt}+3Hn_{\chi}=\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi}(n_{\chi}^{eq})^{2}

(17)

where
\ n_{\chi}^{eq}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\zeta_{3}}{\pi^{2}}T^{3}
and the value of Riemann-Zeta function
\zeta_{3}=1.2\
.

According to Ref.
Garny et al. 2018
, the thermal averaged cross sections of all gravitational annihilation channels have been calculated,

\langle\sigma v\rangle_{0\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{\pi m_{\chi}T}{2M_{pl}^{4}}\left[\frac{4}{5}\frac{T}{m_{\chi}}+\frac{1}{5}\frac{m_{\chi}}{T}-\frac{1}{5}\frac{m_{\chi}}{T}\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{K_{2}^{2}}+\frac{2}{5}\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}\right]

(18)

\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\frac{1}{2}\rightarrow\chi}=\langle\sigma v\rangle_{1\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{4\pi m_{\chi}T}{M_{pl}^{4}}\left[\frac{6}{5}\frac{T}{m_{\chi}}+\frac{2}{15}\frac{m_{\chi}}{T}-\frac{2}{15}\frac{m_{\chi}}{T}\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{K_{2}^{2}}+\frac{3}{5}\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}\right]

(19)

where
\ K_{i}=K_{i}(m_{\chi}/T)
are the modified Bessel functions.
In the limit of
m_{\chi}\ll T
, we have

\langle\sigma v\rangle_{0\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{2\pi T^{2}}{5M_{pl}^{4}}\quad,\quad\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\frac{1}{2}\rightarrow\chi}=\langle\sigma v\rangle_{1\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{24\pi T^{2}}{5M_{pl}^{4}}

(20)

Thus the total cross section gives

\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi}=4\langle\sigma v\rangle_{0\rightarrow\chi}+45\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\frac{1}{2}\rightarrow\chi}+12\langle\sigma v\rangle_{1\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{1376\pi T^{2}}{5M_{pl}^{4}}

(21)

Now Eq. (
17
) becomes

He^{-3N_{e}}\frac{dn_{\chi}e^{3N_{e}}}{dN_{e}}=\beta\frac{T^{8}}{M_{pl}^{4}}

(22)

where
\beta=3096\zeta_{3}^{2}/5\pi^{3}
.
Then the solution of DM number density can be expressed as

n_{\chi}(N_{e})=e^{-3N_{e}}\int_{N_{e}^{i}}^{N_{e}}dN_{e}^{\prime}\ \frac{e^{3N_{e}^{\prime}}}{H}\beta\frac{T^{8}}{M_{pl}^{4}}

(23)

and the DM yield
Y_{\chi}\equiv n_{\chi}/s
reads

Y_{\chi}(N_{e})=\frac{45}{2\pi^{2}g_{*}}\frac{e^{-3N_{e}}}{T^{3}(N_{e})}\int_{N_{e}^{i}}^{N_{e}}dN_{e}^{\prime}\frac{e^{3N_{e}^{\prime}}}{H}\beta\frac{T^{8}}{M_{pl}^{4}}

(24)

We show the evolution of
Y_{\chi}
in Fig.
2
. The evolution of the Hubble parameter
H
, the plasma temperature
T
and the parameter
Q
are also given. During the early stage of WI, although the inflaton energy dominates the universe, the thermal bath is still sustained with a nearly constant temperature. DM gradually accumulates. After the inflation stage, the universe transits to a radiation-dominated phase, where
T\propto e^{-N_{e}}
and
H\propto T^{2}
.

Actually, it is easy to see that the WI enhances the DM freeze-in production. From the integrand in Eq. (
24
), we can see that it is an exponentially increasing function during the inflation stage where the inflaton energy dominates the universe. And when the universe transits to radiation domination, the integrand in Eq. (
24
) will be proportional to
e^{-3N_{e}}
. So the DM production should be sharply peaked at some e-fold,
N_{\mathrm{peak}}
, which satisfies,

\left.\left[3+8\frac{dT(N_{e})}{TdN_{e}}-\frac{dH(N_{e})}{HdN_{e}}\right]\right|_{N_{e}=N_{\mathrm{peak}}}=0

(25)

The DM relic abundance can be expressed approximately as
Freese et al. 2024
,

Y_{\chi}(N_{e})\simeq\frac{45}{2\pi^{2}g_{*s}}\frac{e^{3(N_{\mathrm{peak}}-N_{e})}}{M_{pl}^{4}T^{3}(N_{e})}\Delta N_{\mathrm{peak}}\times\frac{\beta T^{8}(N_{\mathrm{peak}})}{H(N_{\mathrm{peak}})}\quad(N_{e}>N_{\mathrm{peak}})

(26)

with
\Delta N_{\mathrm{peak}}\gtrsim 1
being the half-width of the integrand. In the cold inflation paradigm, the DM can be produced during the reheating after inflation, with the reheating temperature
T_{\mathrm{RH}}=T({N_{e}^{0}})
where
N_{e}^{0}
should be some value that is slightly larger than 60. The DM relic abundance then reads

Y_{\chi}^{\mathrm{RH}}\simeq\frac{45\beta}{4\pi^{4}g_{*s}}\sqrt{\frac{5\pi}{g_{*}}}\left(\frac{T_{\mathrm{RH}}}{M_{pl}}\right)^{3}

(27)

We can compare the DM relic abundance from WI and reheating,

\frac{Y_{\chi}(N_{e})}{Y_{\chi}^{\mathrm{RH}}}\sim\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\chi}(N_{\mathrm{peak}})}{\mathcal{I}_{\chi}(N_{e}^{0})}\Delta N_{\mathrm{peak}}

(28)

where
\mathcal{I}_{\chi}(N_{e})=\frac{e^{3N_{e}}}{H}\beta\frac{T^{8}}{M_{pl}^{4}}
is the integrand of Eq. (
24
). It is evident that
\mathcal{I}_{\chi}(N_{\mathrm{peak}})\gg\mathcal{I}_{\chi}(N_{e}^{0})
such that the relic abundance is significantly enhanced in the WI compared with the cold inflation.

V
conclusions

Motivated by the recent WI model with solely Standard Model gauge interactions
Berghaus et al. 2025
, we have investigated the GW spectrum in the high frequency regime generated via the freeze-in mechanism in two types of WI models.
We find that the peak frequencies are almost the same, while the peak amplitude of the GW spectrum for different WI models is significantly different for
Q_{\rm ini}=1
and
Q_{\rm ini}=100
.
We also studied the graviton-portal DM freeze-in scenario within two classes of WI framework.
Under the constraint of reproducing the observed DM relic abundance, we have identified a correlation between the DM mass and the quartic coupling of the inflation field, and determined the corresponding peak amplitude of the GW signal. These findings provide a theoretical basis for probing and distinguishing such WI scenarios and DM production mechanisms through future GW observations.

Score-Based Diffusion Models in Infinite Dimensions: A Malliavin Calculus Perspective
Ehsan Mirafzali, Frank Proske, Daniele Venturi, Razvan Marinescu

Abstract
We develop a new mathematical framework for score-based diffusion modelling in infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert spaces, through Malliavin calculus, extending generative modelling techniques beyond the finite-dimensional setting. The forward diffusion process is formulated as a linear stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) driven by space–time coloured noise with a trace-class covariance operator, ensuring well–posedness in arbitrary spatial dimensions. Building on Malliavin calculus and an infinite-dimensional extension of the Bismut–Elworthy–Li formula, we derive an exact, closed-form expression for the score function (the Fréchet derivative of the log-density) without relying on finite-dimensional projections or discretisation. Our operator–theoretic approach preserves the intrinsic geometry of Hilbert spaces and accommodates general trace-class operators, thereby incorporating spatially correlated noise without assuming semigroup invertibility. The framework also connects score estimation to functional data analysis, enabling computational strategies such as kernel methods and neural operator architectures.

Recent advances in diffusion generative models

Classical score-based diffusion models are defined in finite, yet often very high-dimensional, spaces. Extending these models to infinite dimensions, such as those arising in functional data analysis, presents significant mathematical challenges.
For instance, infinite-dimensional spaces often lack a Lebesgue measure, making traditional density gradients ill-defined. 3
Summary

We developed a new mathematical framework for score-based diffusion models in infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert spaces using Malliavin calculus, addressing a significant gap in the extension of generative modelling techniques beyond finite dimensions. By formulating the forward diffusion process as a linear stochastic partial differential equation driven by space-time coloured noise with a trace-class covariance operator, we ensure mathematical well-posedness across arbitrary spatial dimensions. Our derivation of the score function (the Fréchet derivative of the log-density) uses Malliavin calculus and an infinite-dimensional generalisation of the Bismut–Elworthy–Li formula, yielding an exact expression that avoids any reliance on finite-dimensional projections or approximations. This operator-theoretic approach not only preserves the intrinsic structure of Hilbert spaces but also incorporates general trace-class operators, accommodating spatially correlated noise without assuming semigroup invertibility.

By reinterpreting score estimation as an infinite-dimensional regression problem, we establish a new connection to functional data analysis, facilitating computational strategies such as kernel methods and neural operator representations.
The mathematical framework developed in this paper can be extended to nonlinear SPDEs with state-dependent diffusion coefficients, allowing for multiplicative noise terms that introduce additional stochastic variability. Such generalisations could advance diffusion-based generative modelling in infinite-dimensional settings, potentially delivering transformative impacts on scientific discovery and data synthesis.

H_{W}=L^{2}([0,t];U)

with inner product
\left\langle h,g\right\rangle_{H_{W}}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle h(s),g(s)\right\rangle_{U}\,ds.

For
h\in H_{W}
, the Wiener integral

W(h)=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle h(s),dW_{s}\right\rangle_{U}

is a Gaussian random variable with mean
\mathbb{E}[W(h)]=0
and variance
\mathbb{E}[W(h)^{2}]=\left\|h\right\|_{H_{W}}^{2}
.

A.1
Cylindrical functionals and Malliavin Derivatives

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote smooth cylindrical functionals of the form

F=f(W(h_{1}),\ldots,W(h_{n}))

where
h_{i}\in H_{W}
and
f\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(smooth functions with polynomial growth derivatives).

Definition 3

(Malliavin derivative)
.

For
F\in\mathcal{S}
, the Malliavin derivative is the
H_{W}
-valued random variable

D_{r}F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(W(h_{1}),\ldots,W(h_{n}))h_{i}(r),\quad r\in[0,t].

Lemma 7

(Malliavin derivative of the solution to the SPDE (
1
))
.

Let
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
be the Sobolev space defined as the completion of
\mathcal{S}
under the norm

\left\|F\right\|_{1,2}=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D_{r}F\right\|_{U}^{2}\,dr\right]\right)^{1/2}.

The mild solution of the linear SPDE (
1
), i.e., (
4
), belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(H)
and its Malliavin derivative is given by

D_{r}u(t)=S(t-r)Q^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(r).

Proof.

Consider the perturbation
W^{\varepsilon}(s)=W(s)+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{s}\eta(\tau)\,d\tau
for
\eta\in H_{W}
. The perturbed solution is

u^{\varepsilon}(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)Q^{1/2}\,dW_{s}^{\varepsilon}=u(t)+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)Q^{1/2}\eta(s)\,ds.

Taking the Fréchet derivative

\left\langle Du(t),\eta\right\rangle_{H_{W}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{u^{\varepsilon}(t)-u(t)}{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)Q^{1/2}\eta(s)\,ds.

By the Riesz representation theorem,
D_{r}u(t)=S(t-r)Q^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(r)
.
Finally, we verify
u(t)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(H)
. To this end, we notice that

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-r)Q^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,dr<\infty,

by condition (
3
).

∎

Hereafter, we characterise the Malliavin derivative of a function of the SPDE solution
u(t)
.

Theorem 8

(Chain rule)
.

Let
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H)
with bounded Fréchet derivative. Then for
F=\phi(u(t))

D_{r}F=\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(t-r)^{*}\nabla\phi(u(t))\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(r).

Proof.

By the chain rule for Fréchet derivatives

D_{r}(\phi(u(t)))=\nabla\phi(u(t))[D_{r}u(t)]=\left\langle\nabla\phi(u(t)),D_{r}u(t)\right\rangle_{H}.

Since
D_{r}u(t)=S(t-r)Q^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(r)
takes values in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HS}}(U,H)
, we need the adjoint action. For
v\in U
we have

\left\langle D_{r}F,v\right\rangle_{U}=\left\langle\nabla\phi(u(t)),S(t-r)Q^{1/2}v\right\rangle_{H}=\left\langle\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(t-r)^{*}\nabla\phi(u(t)),v\right\rangle_{U}.

Therefore
D_{r}F=\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(t-r)^{*}\nabla\phi(u(t))\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(r)
.

∎

A.2
Skorokhod Integral

For
\eta\in H_{W}
, let us define the operator

Du(t):H_{W}\to H
as

Du(t)\eta=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)Q^{1/2}\eta(s)\,ds

with adjoint
Du(t)^{*}:H\to H_{W}

Du(t)^{*}h=\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(t-\cdot)^{*}h\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(\cdot).

Definition 4

(Skorokhod integral)
.

The Skorokhod integral
\delta:\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)\subset L^{2}(\Omega;H_{W})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is the adjoint of
D

\mathbb{E}[\left\langle DF,v\right\rangle_{H_{W}}]=\mathbb{E}[F\delta(v)]

(44)

for all
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
v\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
.

It can be shown that for deterministic
v\in H_{W}
, the Skorokhod integral
reduces to the Itô integral

\delta(v)=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle v(s),dW_{s}\right\rangle_{U}=W(v).

Proposition 9

(Integration by parts)
.

Let
\gamma_{u(t)}
be the Malliavin covariance operator (
8
). For
F=\left\langle u(t),h\right\rangle_{H}
with
h\in\mathrm{Ran}(\gamma_{u(t)})
, and
v(r)=(Q^{1/2})^{*}S(t-r)^{*}\gamma_{u(t)}^{\dagger}h\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(r)
we have

\mathbb{E}[F\delta(v)]=\left\langle h,h\right\rangle_{H}.

We have seen that the solution to the SPDE (
1
) is Gaussian with mean
S(t)u_{0}
and
covariance operator (
8
). This covariance operator, satisfies the following recursion.

Lemma 10

(Covariance recursion)
.

For all
s,r\geq 0

\gamma_{u(t+s)}=\gamma_{u(t)}+S(t)\gamma_{u(s)}S(t)^{*}.

Consequently,
S(t)\mathrm{Ran}(\gamma_{u(s)})\subset\mathrm{Ran}(\gamma_{u(t+s)})
for all
t,s\geq 0
.

Proof.

By direct computation,

\displaystyle\gamma_{u(t+s)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t+s}S(r)Q^{1/2}\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(r)^{*}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(r)Q^{1/2}\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(r)^{*}\,dr+\int_{t}^{t+s}S(r)Q^{1/2}\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(r)^{*}\,dr

\displaystyle=\gamma_{u(t)}+S(t)\left(\int_{0}^{s}S(\sigma)Q^{1/2}\left(Q^{1/2}\right)^{*}S(\sigma)^{*}\,d\sigma\right)S(t)^{*}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{u(t)}+S(t)\gamma_{u(s)}S(t)^{*}.

The range inclusion follows immediately.

∎

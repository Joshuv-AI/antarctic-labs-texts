Functional Determinants for False Vacuum Decay
Pietro Baratella, Miha Nemevšek, Yutaro Shoji, Katarina Trailović, Lorenzo Ubaldi

Abstract
We derive simple expressions to regularise functional determinants from fluctuations of fields with spin 0, 1/2, and 1. These are important for the precise dimensionful determination of false vacuum decay rates. We work in D = 4 D=4 Euclidean dimensions and use familiar Feynman diagrammatic techniques with a double expansion in interactions and masses, together with dimensional regularisation in momentum space. We Fourier transform to coordinate space and end up with a simple regularisation prescription in terms of single integrals over the Euclidean radius of field-dependent masses and their derivatives. Our results apply to models with an arbitrary scalar potential and with any number of scalars, fermions, gauge bosons and associated ghosts. We exemplify this approach on the Standard Model with a streamlined calculation of the renormalisation and isolation of divergences in fluctuation determinants.



I
Introduction

The quantitative study of bubble nucleation has applications in many areas of physics.
They range from the study of metastable states in thermal physics, to the computation of
the Standard Model (SM) electroweak vacuum lifetime, statistical and condensed
matter physics.
If the system of interest is described in terms of a weakly coupled Quantum Field Theory
(QFT), the standard procedure
[
1
,
2
]
for computing the
rate of bubble formation applies.
It consists of first computing the bounce
\overline{\phi}
, which is a semi-classical
scalar field configuration that extremises the action
S[\phi]

and connects the false vacuum (FV)
\hat{\phi}
with an escape point.
Then one has to perform the path integral in a Gaussian approximation, expanding
S
around
the bounce and truncating to quadratic order in the fluctuations.
The path integral sums up all the contributions from any field that couples to the bounce:
scalars
\phi
, fermions
\psi
and vectors (gauge bosons)
A_{\mu}
.
This is a one-loop effect
[
2
]
that boils down to computing the functional
determinant of the fluctuation operator
S^{\prime\prime}[\phi,\psi,A_{\mu}]
, which
is the second variational derivative of the action.
The inclusion of one-loop effects and renormalisation is needed for obtaining a finite and
dimensionful nucleation rate, which is usually schematically written in the form

\gamma=Ae^{-S[\bar{\phi}]}
, where the prefactor
A
encodes the functional determinant.

A vast part of the literature on vacuum decay concentrates on the exponent and
estimates the magnitude of
A
based on dimensional analysis.
This is not only because the priority is to get the exponent right, but
also because the functional determinant is a lot harder to compute
[
2
]
.
Nonetheless, there has been some pioneering work on the latter, done in
two
[

,

,

,

,

,

]
, three
[

,

,

,

,

,
15
]
, and
four dimensions
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.
Apart from Ref.
[
15
]
, in which the functional determinant is calculated
numerically for fermion fluctuations around a bounce solution approximated as a step
function, the rest of the studies focused on scalar fluctuations.
Most of them worked in the thin-wall approximation, in which the metastable and true
vacua are almost degenerate.
Still for the scalar case, Refs.
[
19
,
20
,
21
]

refined the study of functional determinants and provided prescriptions which work when
departing from the thin-wall limit as well.
In particular, Refs.
[
20
,
21
]
brought such prescriptions to
a very simple form in two, three and four dimensions.

In this work we focus on
D=4
dimensions and develop a Feynman diagrammatic method,
improving significantly on Ref.
[
19
]
, and arrive at the same simple
results of Refs.
[
20
,
21
]
, obtained with different methods
for scalars.
Moreover, we extend our method to fermion and gauge boson fluctuations and
arrive at analogous simple and elegant prescriptions for the functional determinants
of these species as well.
To our knowledge these are new results.
Fermions and gauge bosons are more involved and require a proper understanding of the group
theoretical structure associated with the rotational invariance of the fluctuation operators.
We work out these non trivial aspects in detail in the main body of the paper.

Even though there might be some tendency to dismiss the calculation of functional
determinants and leave it to guesswork, we believe that times are mature to improve
on the precision of vacuum decay rates’ calculations, including an accurate determination
of the prefactor as well.
Refs.

already took important steps

where
this kind of calculations are implemented.
In light of this, the present work provides state-of-the-art prescriptions for
functional determinants, which are completely generic and can be implemented in a
large variety of models.

The quantitative expression for the nucleation rate per unit volume
\gamma=\Gamma/V

at one-loop is given by

\begin{split}\gamma=e^{-S}\,\left(\frac{S}{2\pi}\right)^{2}{\rm Im}\sqrt{\left.\frac{{\det}{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}{{\det}^{\prime}S^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{\phi}}\,\left.\frac{{\det}S^{\prime\prime}}{{\det}{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{\psi}\,V_{G}\,{J_{G}}\;\sqrt{\left.\frac{{\det}{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}{{\det}^{\prime}S^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{A,a}}\,\left.\frac{{\det}S^{\prime\prime}}{{\det}{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{c\bar{c}}\,.\end{split}

(1)

Here,
S
is the renormalised Euclidean bounce action and
S^{\prime\prime}
is the second variational
derivative of
S
with respect to various fields, evaluated on the bounce, whilst

\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}
is evaluated on the FV.
We use
\hat{O}
to label any operator
O
or a function/constant that is evaluated
on the FV throughout the paper.

The purpose of our work is to revisit and simplify, while keeping general validity,
the regularisation of the determinants in the scalar
|_{\phi}
, fermion
|_{\psi}

and gauge/would-be Nambu-Goldstone boson
|_{A,a}
sectors.

S^{\prime\prime}|_{\phi}
and
S^{\prime\prime}|_{A,a}
are differential operators that may contain eigenmodes
with zero eigenvalue, called zero modes.
These appear in the presence of symmetries and have to be removed from the determinant to
get
\det^{\prime}
.
The removal employs the use of collective coordinates
[
23
,
2
,
24
]
and results in adding of various pre-factors to the rate:
(S/2\pi)^{2}

for translations,
V_{G}
for the volume of the gauge group
G
and
J_{G}
for the Jacobian of
the gauge zero modes.
For the case of the Standard Model (SM) see e.g
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
and
[
28
]
for zero removal subtleties.
To interpret
\gamma
as a tunneling rate, the determinant needs to have an imaginary part.
It comes from
S^{\prime\prime}|_{\phi}
, which has exactly one
[
29
]
negative eigenvalue,
such that the square root in (
1
) becomes imaginary
[
1
]
.
While important, these features are related to the infrared (IR) part of the spectrum.
Instead, the focus of this work is on the ultraviolet (UV).

As usual in QFTs, calculating loop effects means dealing with UV divergences.
The functional determinants in spacetime dimensions
D\geq 2
are no exception:
they are UV divergent and must be regularised.
In this paper we provide simple prescriptions for such a regularisation in
D=4
.
Given the spherical
O(4)
symmetry of the bounce
[
30
]
, the various fluctuation
operators
S^{\prime\prime}
, whose determinant we need to compute, turn out to be rotationally invariant.
The eigenfunctions of
S^{\prime\prime}
can then be decomposed into a radial and an angular part, employing
a generalisation of spherical harmonics, as
\sum_{\nu,m}c_{\nu,m}(\rho){\mathbb{Y}}_{\nu,m}(\hat{\theta})
,
such that
S^{\prime\prime}
acts diagonally in
\nu
and
m
, respectively a total angular momentum quantum number
(whose precise definition for the fields of various spins is given later), and a polarisation index.
The determinant then factorises as
{\det}S^{\prime\prime}=\prod_{\nu}({\det}S^{\prime\prime}_{\nu})^{d_{\nu}}
,
where
d_{\nu}
is the degeneracy factor counting the independent polarisations at given
\nu
,
and
{\det}S^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}
is a partial determinant.
Here,
S^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}
is a differential operator in the radial variable
\rho^{2}=t^{2}+x_{i}^{2}
.
Using the Gelfand-Yaglom theorem
[
31
]
, the product of eigenvalues of
S^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}

is carried out.
It is the singular behaviour of the product

\prod_{\nu}\big(\det S^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}/\det\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}\big)^{d_{\nu}}

at large
\nu
that encodes the UV divergences.

One way of dealing with this divergent product was studied in Ref.
[
19
]
for
the case of scalar fluctuations.
It amounts to isolating a few one-loop Feynman diagrams (on the bounce background), which are
responsible for the UV divergences of the functional determinant.
These diagrams are then evaluated in two manners on two different spaces.
The first uses a basis in coordinate space together with the multipole expansion (similar to

{\det}S^{\prime\prime}
), so the variables at play are
\rho
and
\nu
.
The second is done in momentum space with standard dimensionally-regulated integrals that produce

1/\epsilon
poles in combination with
\ln\mu
, with
\epsilon=4-D
.
The first expression is then subtracted from the determinant to make it convergent, while
the second is added back to compensate for the subtraction.
Schematically,

\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{\nu}\left(d_{\nu}\ln\frac{\det S_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}}{\det\hat{S}_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}}-{\rm subtraction}_{\nu}\right)+\left({\rm addback}\right)_{\rm regularised}\,.

(2)

One complication in these calculations is in the momentum space part, where one has to deal
with integrals of the form

\int{\rm d}^{D}q\,{\tilde{\phi}}^{2}(q)\tilde{\phi}^{2}(-q)\int\frac{\text{d}^{D}k}{(k^{2}-m^{2})((k+q)^{2}-m^{2})}\,.

(3)

Here
\tilde{\phi}(q)
is the Fourier transform of the bounce field configuration
\bar{\phi}(\rho)
.
Typically
\tilde{\phi}(q)
is a complicated function, the integral above cannot be solved
analytically and one has to resort to numerical methods.
A notable exception is the Fubini-Lipatov bounce
[
32
,
33
]
, relevant for the calculation of the electroweak
vacuum lifetime, for which the integral can be done analytically.

Ref.
[
20
]
revisited the problem and provided a simpler recipe using radial WKB
and an angular momentum cutoff regularisation
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
.
They also showed numerically that their result was in agreement with Ref.
[
19
]
.
A year later, Ref.
[
21
]
used the
\zeta
-function regularisation method, with a
derivation in generic
D
dimensions, and arrived at a much simpler prescription, containing only
integrals in the coordinate
\rho
, no integrals in momentum space.
They also showed that in
D=4
their result agreed with those of
Refs.
[
19
,
20
]
in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme.

In this paper we adopt the dimensional regularization scheme, as in Ref.
[
19
]
.
Differently from them, we perform a double expansion of the Feynman diagram series, both in the
coupling
and
in the squared mass parameter
m^{2}
, which appears in (
3
)
and corresponds to the second derivative of the potential evaluated on the false vacuum.
This is the first key to simplification.
On top of it, we perform manipulations on the “subtraction” part to isolate the leading divergent
terms in the
\nu
series from the finite pieces.
Last, in the “addback” part, we perform the inverse Fourier transform to revert back to
coordinate space at the end of the calculation.
To do so we take advantage of two identities which we derive in Appendix
A
.
When the dust settles, we obtain a very simple prescription corresponding to (
2
)
in terms of single integrals only in the coordinate
\rho
.
Our result is identical to that of Ref.
[
21
]
for scalars, but obtained with a
different method.

Our method, which employs dimensional regularisation, not only reproduces the simple result for
scalars, but is now extended to the analogous calculations involving fermions and gauge bosons.
Here, we perform the analysis also for these cases and manage to provide simple prescriptions
corresponding to (
2
) in terms of single
\rho
-integrals only.
Our results are general, in the sense that they work for a generic scalar potential and bounce, for
a generic number of species of spin
0,1/2,1
, and easily generalise to non Abelian gauge bosons.

The final expression for the functional determinant is a regularised quantity, with divergences
regulated by poles, with a residual dependence on the dimensionful parameter
\mu
, plus some
remaining finite parts.
The
1/\epsilon
and
\ln\mu
terms at the end of the day must cancel out against the analogous
terms coming from the factor
e^{-S}
in (
1
), where
S
is the renormalised bounce
action at one-loop, in order to obtain the physical observable decay rate
\gamma
.

II
Summary

In this section we provide the essence of our findings, assuming some knowledge on how to set up
the calculation of functional determinants
[
2
]
.
We distil our results to compact formulas that can readily be used for any concrete problem.
We work in
D=4
Euclidean space-time dimensions
and start with considering a weakly coupled theory for a set of real scalars

\phi^{i}
and Dirac fermions
\psi^{a}
, and we generalize the procedure of dealing with Majorana

We discuss separately the case of minimally coupled gauge bosons

The most general Euclidean action for scalar and fermions can be expressed as

\displaystyle S=\int_{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{i}+V(\phi^{i})+i\bar{\psi}^{a}\not{\partial}\psi^{a}+\bar{\psi}^{a}{\bf{M}}_{ab}(\phi^{i})\psi^{b}\right)\,,

(4)

where
\not{\partial}=\gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}

the

Throughout the paper we use the shorthand notation for integrals,

\displaystyle\int_{x}

\displaystyle\int_{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\,,

\displaystyle\int_{\rho}

The action can be expanded up to second order in the fluctuations around the bounce, with

\phi^{i}(x)=\bar{\phi}^{i}(\rho)+\delta\phi^{i}(x)

This gives

\displaystyle S_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\,\left(\delta\phi^{i}\left(-\delta_{ij}\partial^{2}+m^{2}_{ij}(\rho)\right)\delta\phi^{j}+\bar{\psi}^{a}\left(i\delta_{ab}\not{\partial}+{\bf M}_{ab}(\bar{\phi})\right)\psi^{b}\right)\,.

The operators

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}_{ij}|_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{2}S_{2}}{\delta(\delta\phi^{i})\delta(\delta\phi^{j})}\,,

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}_{ab}|_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{2}S_{2}}{\delta\bar{\psi}^{a}\delta\psi^{b}}\,.

(7)

Given the rotational symmetry of the whole problem, the determinant ratios

\det S^{\prime\prime}/\det\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}

\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}
calculated on the FV, can be split into sectors
with definite

angular momentum
[

, which we label by
\nu

an integer running from 1 to
\infty
.
The determinant ratio is then a product of partial contributions
R_{\nu}
for each

with an additional degeneracy factor

\det S^{\prime\prime}/\det\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}=\prod_{\nu}R_{\nu}^{d_{\nu}}

This infinite product is divergent and must be regularised.
The procedure consists in isolating the UV divergent terms, subtracting them and adding
back their regularised counterparts.
In this work we adopt the dimensional regularisation scheme, which is familiar
to particle physicists.

For scalar fluctuations we find

\begin{split}\left.\ln\frac{{\det}^{\prime}S^{\prime\prime}}{{\det}{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{\phi}=&\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left(d_{\nu}^{\phi}\ln R_{\nu}^{\phi}-\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{\rho}\,\rho\,\tr\delta m^{2}+\frac{1}{8\nu}\int_{\rho}\,\rho^{3}\tr\delta m^{4}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\rho}\,\rho^{3}\tr\delta m^{4}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+1+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}\rho}{2}\right)\,,\end{split}

(8)

where we defined the field-dependent mass matrices and their constant FV values as

\displaystyle m^{2}_{ij}(\rho)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\text{d}^{2}V}{\text{d}\phi^{i}\text{d}\phi^{j}}\bigg|_{\bar{\phi}}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{m}^{2}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\text{d}^{2}V}{\text{d}\phi^{i}\text{d}\phi^{j}}\bigg|_{\hat{\phi}}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\delta m^{2}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv m^{2}_{ij}-\hat{m}^{2}_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle\delta m^{4}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv m^{4}_{ij}-\hat{m}^{4}_{ij}\,.

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{\psi}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv m_{\psi}^{4}-\hat{m}_{\psi}^{4}\,,

The partial determinants split into two sectors: one contains three degrees of

The subtraction series and compensating term are expressed, as before, out

-

Physically, it makes sense to separate the cases in which the FV is

Comparing against scalars (

The object in (

II.1
Standard Model

Our purpose in this section is twofold.
First, we want to exemplify the use of our prescriptions on a concrete and important example,
showing that our results are ready-to-use and simple.
Second, we want to check our formulæ against SM results in previous literature.

Physical Higgs boson.

We start with the contribution to the functional determinant coming from the fluctuations of
the physical Higgs boson
h
, following the notation of
[
42
]
.
The scalar potential is given by
V(h)=\frac{1}{4}\lambda h^{4}

\lambda<0

The metastable FV solution corresponds to
\hat{h}=0

[

,
33

\bar{h}(\rho)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{-\lambda}}\frac{R}{R^{2}+\rho^{2}}\,,

(23)

with

We need a simplified version of (
8

can drop the ‘tr’.
The FV mass is trivial

m_{h}^{2}(\rho)=3\lambda\bar{h}^{2}(\rho)=-24R^{2}/(R^{2}+\rho^{2})^{2}

We only need to compute the two simple integrals

\displaystyle\int_{\rho}\,\rho\,m_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=-12\,,

\displaystyle\int_{\rho}\,\rho^{3}\,m_{h}^{4}\left(a+b\ln\rho\right)

\displaystyle=48\left(a+b\ln R\right)\,,

and plug them into (

\left.\ln\frac{{\det}^{\prime}S^{\prime\prime}}{{\det}{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{h}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left(d_{\nu}^{h}\ln R_{\nu}^{h}+{6}{\nu}+\frac{6}{\nu}\right)-6\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+1+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}R}{2}\right)\,.

(25)

To evaluate the sum we need the partial determinant

R_{\nu}^{h}=(\nu-1)(\nu-2)/(\nu+1)/(\nu+2)
, which can be found for example
in
[
26
,
27
]
.
At large
\nu

\ln R_{\nu}^{h}=-6/\nu-6/\nu^{3}+O(\nu^{-4})

in (

This is already a first non-trivial check of our subtraction prescription.
To carry out the sum explicitly there is another issue to take care of.
For
\nu=1,2

vanishes due to the presence of zero
modes, so it has to be cured.
We defer to previous literature for a discussion of this delicate

[
26
,
27
]

noting again that this problem has to do with the low-
\nu

while the results of the present work deal with the high-
\nu

Following
[
26
]
, one can consider the operator

S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm red}\equiv m_{h}^{-2}(\rho)S^{\prime\prime}

The advantage of this choice is that the usual zero mode removal procedure works

After zero-removal

26

[

and reaffirms the validity

and take the reciprocal of its square root as dictated by (

Multipole decomposition.

Because

is a spherically symmetric function of
\rho

one can take advantage of the

The same holds for

\hat{m}^{2}

To compute the ratio of determinants, the standard route is to use the

\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}}=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(\rho)}{\hat{\varphi}(\rho)}\equiv R_{\nu}\,,

where
\varphi

\hat{\varphi}

and

with boundary conditions

\rho\to 0

\nu

3

Subtraction procedure.
Our goal is not to provide an explicit solution
for (
45
), but rather to give a simple understanding of how to regularise
the infinite sum over
\nu
.
We shall characterise the structure of divergences by turning the log of the determinant
into an infinite sum of traces, which are interpretable in terms of 1-loop Feynman diagrams
with scalar propagators, see Fig.
1
.
We begin with the formal operator expansion, where we use (
41
) to write

\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}=\tr\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}+\hat{m}^{2}}\delta m^{2}\right)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\tr\left(\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}+\hat{m}^{2}}\delta m^{2}\right)^{n}\,,

(52)

where
\delta m^{2}\equiv m^{2}-\hat{m}^{2}
.
From the diagrammatic point of view, each
(-\partial^{2}+\hat{m}^{2})^{-1}
that comes from
expanding Eq. (
52

n
, corresponds to a propagator,
and each
\delta m^{2}
to an insertion of the background field.

A crucial simplification is to organise the entire regularisation procedure in a
power counting scheme with
two
expansion indices.
Along with the number of insertions of
\delta m^{2}
, which correspond to the green circles
in Fig.
1
, we expand over the number of mass insertions
\hat{m}^{2}
, denoted
by the red crosses in Fig.
1
.
In performing this double expansion we get

\begin{split}\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}&=\tr\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m^{2}{-}\tr\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\hat{m}^{2}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m^{2}{-}\frac{1}{2}\tr\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m^{2}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m^{2}+O\big((\delta m^{2})^{3},\hat{m}^{4}\big)\\
&\equiv T_{1,0}-T_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T_{2,0}+O\big((\delta m^{2})^{3},\hat{m}^{4}\big)\,.\end{split}

(53)

We have labelled the individual traces by
T_{i,j}
, where the first index
i
counts the
number of interactions
\delta m^{2}
, and the second index
j
the number of mass insertions

\hat{m}^{2}
, as in Fig.
1
.
The relation to Feynman diagrams makes it easy to determine the degree of divergences in
any
D
spacetime dimensions.
In
D=4
, we have a quadratic divergence carried by
T_{1,0}
and a logarithmic divergence
in
T_{1,1}
and
T_{2,0}
.
In
D=3
, there is only a linear divergence in
T_{1,0}
.
This is obvious if one thinks about the one-loop integral in momentum space and counts
the number of scalar propagators in the loop.
We will see that in the angular momentum basis the same UV divergence structure is evident
at large multipoles
\nu
.

We are going to evaluate these divergent traces via insertions of the appropriate states in
the angular momentum bases.
This will provide a series that matches the large
\nu
behaviour of (
45

Subtracting it from from
\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}d_{\nu}\ln R_{\nu}
will make the series
convergent.
Then we will evaluate the same traces in momentum space using dimensional regularisation
and add them back to obtain our final expression.
In short, we subtract the divergent pieces and add back their regularised counterparts

\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}d_{\nu}\ln R_{\nu}-\left(T_{1,0}-T_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T_{2,0}\right)+\left(T^{\rm DR}_{1,0}-T^{\rm DR}_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T^{\rm DR}_{2,0}\right)\,,

(54)

where DR stands for dimensional regularisation.

Let us start with the evaluation in angular momentum space.

T_{1,0}
consists of a single insertion of the interaction, a massless propagator and
no mass insertions:

\begin{split}T_{1,0}&=\sum_{\nu m}\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\int_{\lambda}\bra{\rho;\nu m}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\ket{\lambda;\nu^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\bra{\lambda;\nu^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\delta m^{2}\ket{\rho;\nu m}\\
&=\sum_{\nu m}\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\int_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\rho}J_{\nu}(\lambda\rho)\bra{\lambda;\nu m}\delta m^{2}\ket{\rho;\nu m}\\
&=\sum_{\nu m}\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\delta m^{2}\int_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\rho}J_{\nu}(\lambda\rho)\right)^{2}=\sum_{\nu}d_{\nu}\frac{1}{2\nu}\int_{\rho}\ \rho\ \delta m^{2}\,.\end{split}

In the first line we used the completeness relations from (
51

5

In the last step we performed the integral over

T^{\rm DR}_{1,1}=\frac{\hat{m}^{2}}{8}\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\delta m^{2}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}+2\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}}{\delta_{\rm IR}}\right)\,.

(68)

The IR regulator

The trace was evaluated in momentum space and we ended up with a simple expression

Let us turn to

\begin{split}T^{\rm DR}_{2,0}&=\int_{x}\int_{p}\int_{y}\int_{q}\bra{x}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\ket{q}\bra{q}\delta m^{2}\ket{y}\bra{y}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\ket{p}\bra{p}\delta m^{2}\ket{x}\\

\begin{split}\int_{k}\widetilde{f}(\left|k\right|)^{2}\ln k^{2}&=-4\pi^{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}f(\rho)\left(f(\rho)\left(\gamma_{E}{-}1{+}\ln\frac{\rho}{2}\right)+2\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\,x^{3}\,\frac{f(x\rho)-f(\rho)}{1-x^{2}}\,\right),\end{split}

\begin{split}T^{\rm DR}_{2,0}&=\frac{1}{8}\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\left(\delta m^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}+2\gamma_{E}+2\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}\rho}{2}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\delta m^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{3}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\delta m^{2}(x\rho)-\delta m^{2}\right)\,.\end{split}

(71)

The second line matches exactly the last term in (

Combining everything in (

\begin{split}\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left(d_{\nu}\ln R_{\nu}-\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{\rho}\,\rho\,\delta m^{2}+\frac{1}{8\nu}\int_{\rho}\,\rho^{3}\,\delta m^{4}\right)\\

which matches (

IV
Fermions

In this section we focus on fermions and work out the UV subtraction and regularisation
procedure.
We start with the simplest Yukawa action in Euclidean signature for a real scalar and a
Dirac fermion

S[\phi,\psi]=S[\phi]+\int_{x}\left(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+\left(m+y\phi\right)\bar{\psi}\psi\right)\,.

(73)

At the end we consider the general case with an arbitrary number of flavours and
include the pseudo-scalar terms of the type
\phi\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\psi

as Majorana fermions.
The scalar action
S[\phi]

\hat{\phi}

We want to compute the ratio of determinants among the fluctuation operators on the bounce

Following the notation of Section

S^{\prime\prime}=\cancel{\partial}+m_{\psi}(\rho)
, while
\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}=\cancel{\partial}+\hat{m}_{\psi}

where
m_{\psi}=m+y\bar{\phi}

\hat{m}_{\psi}=m+y\hat{\phi}

Given that the determinants of

(74)

To work out the product among the differential operators we have used that

(\cancel{\partial})^{\dagger}=-\cancel{\partial}

[

Multipole decomposition.
Like for the scalar field, our study of the
fluctuation operators starts from a choice of basis on the function space

\psi_{\alpha}(x)
of spinor configurations, that is the space where the operators act on.
Given the rotationally symmetric nature of the problem, the fluctuation operators on both
the FV and the bounce commute with the total angular momentum generators,

i.e.

[J_{\mu\nu},S^{\prime\prime}]=0
.
Therefore, we look for a basis of ‘Dirac-spinor spherical harmonics’

{Y}_{\alpha}^{(\nu M)}(\hat{x})
that are
SO(4)
multiplets, and with the property that
any Dirac spinor
\psi_{\alpha}(x)
can be decomposed as

\psi_{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{\nu,M}c_{\nu M}(\rho){Y}_{\alpha}^{(\nu M)}(\hat{x})
.
Here,
\nu
is a quantum number that characterises total angular momentum, while
M

collects all other quantum numbers (polarisations) that are needed to fix the state
at given
\nu
.

To characterise the
{Y}_{\alpha}^{(\nu M)}(\hat{x})
basis, we consider the Dirac spinor

\psi_{\alpha}(\hat{x})
(we suppress the radial variable as it is inert under rotations)
from the point of view of rotations, as a tensor product among a scalar function in
the
\hat{x}
variable and a
(1/2,0)\oplus(0,1/2)
finite-dimensional
representation of
SO(4)
.
A scalar function is an infinite dimensional representation

\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(l/2,l/2\right)
, as in the previous section.
Working out the tensor product among
(1/2,0)
and
(l/2,l/2)
with

tensor product rules familiar from

, we find

\left(\tfrac{1}{2},0\right)\otimes\left(\tfrac{l}{2},\tfrac{l}{2}\right)\simeq\left(\tfrac{l+1}{2},\tfrac{l}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\tfrac{l-1}{2},\tfrac{l}{2}\right)\,,

(75)

and a symmetric result for
(0,1/2)
.
For

, the tensor product is trivial and we end up with only one term.
After summing over

, we can rearrange the infinite sum as

\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{2},0\right)\oplus\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right)\,\otimes\,\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\infty}\ \left(\tfrac{l}{2},\tfrac{l}{2}\right)\ \simeq\ 2\,\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\tfrac{j}{2},\tfrac{j+1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\tfrac{j+1}{2},\tfrac{j}{2}\right)\right)\,,

(76)

where the factor of 2 in front of everything on the RHS means that there are two copies
for each representation.

Action of the fluctuation operator.

Previous arguments imply that the action of the operator

on a state

S^{\prime\prime\dagger}S^{\prime\prime}\left[c(\rho)Y_{\alpha}^{(\nu\sigma m\chi)}(\hat{x})\right]=\sum_{\chi^{\prime}}Y_{\alpha}^{(\nu\sigma m\chi^{\prime})}(\hat{x})\,\left(S^{\prime\prime\dagger}S^{\prime\prime}\right)^{(\nu)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\left[c(\rho)\right]\,,

In particular, an explicit computation similar to

\left(S^{\prime\prime{\dagger}}S^{\prime\prime}\right)^{(\nu)}=\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta_{\nu-1}+m_{\psi}^{2}&-\dot{m}_{\psi}\\

(79)

where we introduced the differential operator

\Delta_{\nu}=\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{3}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}-\frac{\nu(\nu+2)}{\rho^{2}}\,.

(80)

By inspecting the diagonal terms, we see that the chiral states also have definite

and

\nu(\nu+2)

and
(\nu-1)(\nu+1)
.
The off-diagonal terms can be understood after noting that
\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}

\{\gamma_{5},\gamma_{\mu}\hat{x}_{\mu}\}=0

\gamma_{\mu}\hat{x}_{\mu}
flips the

\gamma_{5}
when applied once and returns the initial state when applied twice.

\left\langle\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi|\lambda;\nu^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}m^{\prime}\chi^{\prime}\right\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\rho}J_{\nu+\frac{1}{2}(1+\chi)}(\lambda\rho)\ \delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\delta_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\,,

(81)

and the completeness relations

{\bf 1}=\sum_{\nu,\sigma,m,\chi}\int_{\lambda}\left|\lambda;\nu\sigma m\chi\right\rangle\left\langle\lambda;\nu\sigma m\chi\right|=\sum_{\nu,\sigma,m,\chi}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\left|\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi\right\rangle\left\langle\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi\right|\,,

(82)

where

ranges from 1 to
d^{\psi}_{\nu}=\nu(\nu{+}1)
, while
\sigma,\chi=\pm 1
.

Subtraction procedure.

We proceed like in section
III
and work out

\begin{split}2\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}}&=\ln\frac{\det\left(-\partial^{2}+m_{\psi}^{2}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}\right)}{\det\left(-\partial^{2}+\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}\right)}=\tr\ln\left(1+\frac{\delta m^{2}_{\psi}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}}{-\partial^{2}+\hat{m}^{2}_{\psi}}\right)\\
&=\tr\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}(\delta m^{2}_{\psi}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi})-\tr\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\left(\delta m^{2}_{\psi}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\tr\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\left(\delta m^{2}_{\psi}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}\right)\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\left(\delta m^{2}_{\psi}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}\right)+\ldots\\
&=T_{1,0}-T_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T_{2,0}+\ldots\,.\end{split}

(83)

Here
T_{i,j}
are traces defined like in (
53
), where the index
i
counts
insertions of
\delta m_{\psi}^{2}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}
, while
j
counts insertions of

\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}
.
We have thereby isolated all the three UV divergent traces in
D=4
.
The ellipses stand for terms of higher order in either

\delta m_{\psi}^{2}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}
or
\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}
, which are convergent and
do not need to be regularised.
The rest is the same as in the scalar case; we first subtract the traces evaluated
in angular momentum space and then add back their dimensionally regularised counterparts

\left.\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{\psi}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}d_{\nu}^{\psi}\ln R_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{1,0}-T_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T_{2,0}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(T^{\rm DR}_{1,0}-T^{\rm DR}_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T^{\rm DR}_{2,0}\right)\,.

(84)

Let us start with
T_{1,0}
and use the same formal manipulations as for
scalars to get

\displaystyle T_{1,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\sigma m\chi\chi^{\prime}}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\int_{\lambda}\langle\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi|\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}|\lambda;\nu\sigma m\chi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\lambda;\nu\sigma m\chi^{\prime}|\delta m_{\psi}^{2}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}|\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\sigma m\chi}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\int_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\rho}J_{\nu+\frac{1}{2}(1+\chi)}(\lambda\rho)\langle\lambda;\nu\sigma m\chi|\delta m_{\psi}^{2}|\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\sigma m\chi}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{\psi}^{2}\int_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\rho}J_{\nu+\frac{1}{2}(1+\chi)}(\lambda\rho)\right)^{2}

(85)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\chi}2\,d_{\nu}^{\psi}\,\frac{1}{2\nu+1+\chi}\int_{\rho}\rho\ \delta m_{\psi}^{2}=\sum_{\nu}d^{\psi}_{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu+1}\right)\int_{\rho}\rho\ \delta m_{\psi}^{2}\,.

In the first line we used the completeness relations to write down the trace in angular
momentum space.
To go to the second one, we first let
\partial^{-2}
act on the right to get a bracket
proportional to
\delta_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}
.
This implies that the second bracket has to be evaluated only on terms that are diagonal
in
\chi
space, such that contributions coming from
\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}
are
projected out.

Next we consider
T_{1,1}
, which is IR-divergent (as for scalars), so we use
an IR-cutoff regulator
{\delta_{\rm IR}}
to regularise it; it will cancel out later.
We find

\begin{split}T_{1,1}&=\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}\sum_{\nu\sigma m\chi}\int_{\rho}\rho\int_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda}{\left(\lambda^{2}+{\delta_{\rm IR}}^{2}\right)^{2}}J^{2}_{\nu+\frac{1}{2}(1+\chi)}(\lambda\rho)\,\delta m_{\psi}^{2}\\
&=\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{\psi}^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{12}-\gamma-\ln\frac{\rho{\delta_{\rm IR}}}{2}+\sum_{\nu>1}\frac{d^{\psi}_{\nu}}{2\nu(\nu+1)}\left(\frac{1}{\nu-1}+\frac{1}{\nu+2}\right)\right)\\
&=\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{\psi}^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{12}-\gamma-\ln\frac{\rho{\delta_{\rm IR}}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\nu-1}+\frac{1}{\nu+2}-\frac{2}{\nu}\right)+\sum_{\nu=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu}\right)\\
&=\hat{m}_{\psi}^{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{\psi}^{2}\left(-\gamma-\ln\frac{\rho{\delta_{\rm IR}}}{2}-1+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu}\right)\,,\end{split}

(86)

where the IR-divergent term comes from
\nu=1
and
\chi=-1
.
We used (
57

\int_{\lambda}1/\lambda^{3}J_{\nu}^{2}(\lambda\rho)=\rho^{2}/(4\nu\left(\nu^{2}-1\right))
for
\nu>1
.
The first sum in the third line is equal to
1/12
, and in the last line the sum
starts at
\nu=1
, compensated by the
-1
term.
The very last term in (
86
) with the divergent sum encodes the expected
logarithmic UV divergence.

Finally, we calculate

&\times\langle\lambda;\nu\sigma m\chi|\delta m_{\psi}^{2}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}|\rho^{\prime};\nu\sigma m\chi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\lambda^{\prime};\nu\sigma m\chi^{\prime}|\delta m_{\psi}^{2}-\cancel{\partial}m_{\psi}|\rho;\nu\sigma m\chi\rangle\,.\end{split}

We split

for which the integral

(\delta m_{\psi}^{2}(x\rho)-\delta m_{\psi}^{2})

and
(\dot{m}_{\psi}(x\rho)-\dot{m}_{\psi})
for which the

Before exploring the generalisation to a theory with Majorana-type particles, let us
recall what happens in the scalar analogue, keeping in mind that our goal is to compute

Schematically, in the complex case the determinant comes from a path integral like

Notice that, keeping the number of degrees of freedom fixed, the second case
is more general, since a hermitian

Coming back to fermions, in the Majorana basis (of gamma matrices) a Majorana

]

among an operator and

).
With
\ell_{\alpha,j}
we refer to the orbital angular momentum of a state

\alpha\in\{a,D^{\pm},T^{\pm}\}
, coming from the multipole decomposition (
105
).

Subtraction procedure.

We can now work out the regularisation of the functional determinant for gauge bosons.
Following the same procedure as for scalars and fermions, we isolate the divergent traces
T_{1,0}
,

T_{1,1}
and
T_{2,0}
and compute them in the angular momentum and in the momentum basis using DR.
Using (
107
) and performing the same formal steps as in (
52
),
we get to the following sum over the various sectors

\displaystyle\!\!\!\ln\frac{\det{S^{\prime\prime}}}{\det\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{\sigma,j}d_{j}^{\sigma}\,{\rm tr}\,\ln\left[1+{\left({\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime(\sigma)}_{j}\right)}^{-1}\delta{S^{\prime\prime}}_{j}^{(\sigma)}\right]=\sum_{\sigma,j}d_{j}^{\sigma}\left(T_{1,0}^{\sigma,j}-T_{1,1}^{\sigma,j}-\frac{1}{2}T_{2,0}^{\sigma,j}\right)+\ldots\,,

(115)

where the ellipses stand for terms that are not UV divergent.
We have defined
\delta{S^{\prime\prime}}=S^{\prime\prime}-\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}
and
\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}=(-\partial^{2}+\hat{m}^{2})
.
In the second step we have expanded both the logarithm around
1
and

\hat{S}^{\prime\prime}
in powers of
\hat{m}^{2}
.
As in previous sections we use the notation
T_{I,J}
, with the index
I
counting insertions
of
\delta{S^{\prime\prime}}
and
J
insertions of
\hat{m}^{2}
.

Following the discussion above, we compute the traces in subspaces defined by
j
and sectors

\sigma=T^{+},T^{-},Da
.
As usual
m_{A}
and
m_{B}
are sterile indices, so we do not carry them around, but take into
account their multiplicity in the degeneracy factors

\displaystyle d_{j}^{Da}

\displaystyle\equiv d_{j}^{\phi}=\left(j+1\right)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle d_{j}^{T^{\pm}}

\displaystyle\equiv d_{j}^{T}=j\left(j+2\right)\,.

(116)

By inspection of (
115
) we note that
{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime(\sigma)}_{j}
is diagonal
in each space
{\cal H}_{\alpha}^{jm_{A}m_{B}}
.
The same holds for
\delta{S^{\prime\prime}}_{j}^{(\sigma)}
, except for the off-diagonal effects of

S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}
that we described earlier.
The
S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}
only affects the
T_{2,0}
sector, because a purely off-diagonal matrix
x

has
{\rm tr}[xd]=0
for any diagonal matrix
d
, and one needs at least a second power of
x

in order not to have a vanishing trace.
It is analogous to what we encountered in the fermion case in Sec.
IV
, where
the off-diagonal part of the matrix entered only in the calculation of
T_{2,0}
.
In light of this, it is natural to split

\displaystyle T_{1,J}^{Da,j}

\displaystyle=T_{1,J}^{a,j}+T_{1,J}^{D^{+},j}+T_{1,J}^{D^{-},j}\,,

\displaystyle T_{2,0}^{Da,j}

\displaystyle=T_{2,0}^{a,j}+T_{2,0}^{D^{+},j}+T_{2,0}^{D^{-},j}+T^{j}_{\rm o.d.}\,,

(117)

where
T^{j}_{\rm o.d.}
is the only trace where the off-diagonal element enters.

Let us start with
T_{1,0}
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{1,0}^{\alpha,j}&={\rm tr}\left(\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\right)=-\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\int_{\lambda}\langle\rho|\Delta_{\ell_{\alpha,j}}^{-1}|\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda|\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}|\rho\rangle\\
&=\int_{\rho}\int_{\lambda}\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}(\rho)J_{\ell_{\alpha,j}+1}(\lambda\rho)^{2}=\frac{1}{2(\ell_{\alpha,j}+1)}\int_{\rho}\rho\,\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\,.\end{split}

The index
\alpha
runs over all species
a,D^{\pm},T^{\pm}
, while
\ell_{\alpha,j}
label here is related to orbital angular momentum and can be read off
from (
105
)

\displaystyle\ell_{a,j}

\displaystyle=\ell_{T^{\pm},j}=j\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{D^{\pm},j}

\displaystyle=j\pm 1\,.

(118)

With similar manipulations, for
T_{1,1}
we get

\begin{split}T_{1,1}^{\alpha,j}&={\rm tr}\left(\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\hat{m}_{\alpha}^{2}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\right)\\
&=\hat{m}_{\alpha}^{2}\int_{\rho}\int_{\lambda}\frac{\rho}{\lambda^{3}}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}(\rho)J_{\ell_{\alpha,j}+1}(\lambda\rho)^{2}=\frac{1}{4\,\ell_{\alpha,j}(\ell_{\alpha,j}+1)(\ell_{\alpha,j}+2)}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\hat{m}_{\alpha}^{2}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\,.\end{split}

(119)

Due to the presence of the
\ell_{\alpha,j}^{-1}
factor, we find a singular result when

\alpha=D^{-},j=1
and
\alpha=a,j=0
.
We introduce the IR regulator
\delta_{\rm IR}
, which will cancel out against the
DR term, like in the scalar and fermion cases.
Omitting terms that go to zero with
\delta_{\rm IR}
, we find

\begin{split}{T_{1,1}^{a,0}}\big|_{\rm IR}&=-\frac{1}{4}{\hat{m}_{a}^{2}}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{a}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\delta_{\rm IR}\rho}{2}\right)\,,\\
{T_{1,1}^{D^{-},1}}\big|_{\rm IR}&=-{\hat{m}_{A}^{2}}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{A}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\delta_{\rm IR}\rho}{2}\right)\,.\end{split}

(120)

For the
T_{2,0}
traces without the
S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}
part, we get

\displaystyle T_{2,0}^{\alpha,j}={\rm tr}\left(\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2(\ell_{\alpha,j}+1)^{2}}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\ x^{2\ell_{\alpha,j}+3}\delta m_{\alpha}^{2}(x\rho)\,.

(121)

Finally, we have to calculate the effects of the mixing term
S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}
.
The only non-vanishing trace where it enters yields

\displaystyle T_{\rm o.d.}^{j}={\rm tr}\left(\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}\frac{1}{-\partial^{2}}S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}\right)=\frac{2}{(j+1)^{2}}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\ \dot{m}_{A}(x\rho)\left(x^{2j+4}+x^{2j+2}\right),

(122)

which is taken in the Hilbert subspace

{\cal H}_{a}^{jm_{A}m_{B}}\oplus{\cal H}_{D^{+}}^{jm_{A}m_{B}}\oplus{\cal H}_{D^{-}}^{jm_{A}m_{B}}
.
In order to get to the final form, it is useful to recall (
110
) and the mirror
result
\frac{1}{2\dot{m}_{A}}S^{\prime\prime}_{\rm o.d.}|D^{\pm},j\rangle=c^{\pm}|a,j\rangle
.

Our next step is to sum over
\sigma
and
j
; for the first trace we have

\begin{split}T_{1,0}&=\sum_{j}d_{j}^{\phi}\left(T_{1,0}^{a,j}+T_{1,0}^{D^{+},j}+T_{1,0}^{D^{-},j}\right)+\sum_{j}d_{j}^{T}\left(T_{1,0}^{T^{+},j}+T_{1,0}^{T^{-},j}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho\ \delta m_{a}^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{j+1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho\ \delta m_{A}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{j+2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{j}+2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{T}}{j+1}\right)\,.\end{split}

(123)

Note that for the NGB field
a
and for
D^{+}
the sum starts at
j=0
, while for
D^{-}

and
T^{\pm}
it starts at
j=1
.
This is a consequence of the group theoretical structure and our choice of
j
.
However, we can relabel the index in each sum and bring the result to a form similar to those
of scalars and fermions with the following choice

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=j+1\,,

for

\displaystyle\ a,D^{\pm}\,,

(124)

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=j\,,

for

\displaystyle\ T^{\pm}\,.

(125)

After some manipulations we get

T_{1,0}=\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left(\nu\int_{\rho}\rho\,\delta m_{a}^{2}+2\,(2\nu+1)\int_{\rho}\rho\,\delta m_{A}^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho\,\delta m_{A}^{2}\,.

(126)

The last term compensates for the finite difference between the series in
j

in (
123
) and in
\nu
.
Summing over
\sigma
and
j
for
T_{1,1}
we have

\displaystyle T_{1,1}=\left.T_{1,1}^{a,0}\right|_{\rm IR}+\left.T_{1,1}^{D^{-},1}\right|_{\rm IR}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\hat{m}_{a}^{2}\delta m_{a}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{j(j+1)(j+2)}

(127)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\hat{m}_{A}^{2}\delta m_{A}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{(j+1)(j+2)(j+3)}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{(j-1)j(j+1)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2d_{j}^{T}}{j(j+1)(j+2)}\right)\,.

Switching from
j
to
\nu
using (
124
), after some algebra we get

\begin{split}T_{1,1}&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{8\nu}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}2\hat{m}^{2}_{a}\delta m_{a}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}2\hat{m}_{a}^{2}\delta m_{a}^{2}\left(1+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\delta_{\rm IR}\rho}{2}\right)\\
&+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{8\nu}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}8\hat{m}^{2}_{A}\delta m_{A}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}8\hat{m}_{A}^{2}\delta m_{A}^{2}\left(1+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\delta_{\rm IR}\rho}{2}\right)\,.\end{split}

(128)

The last trace to assemble is

\begin{split}T_{2,0}&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{a}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\ \delta m_{a}^{2}(x\rho)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{(j+1)^{2}}x^{2j+3}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\ \delta m_{A}^{2}(x\rho)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{(j+2)^{2}}x^{2j+5}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{j^{2}}x^{2j+1}\right)\\
&+2\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\ \dot{m}_{A}(x\rho)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{(j+1)^{2}}x^{2j+4}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j}^{\phi}}{(j+1)^{2}}x^{2j+2}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\ \delta m_{A}^{2}(x\rho)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2d_{j}^{T}}{(j+1)^{2}}x^{2j+3}\,.\end{split}

(129)

We switch from
j
to
\nu
and perform manipulations analogous to those we used in
the scalar and fermion cases when dealing with
T_{2,0}
.
We arrive at the form

\displaystyle T_{2,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4\nu}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\left(\delta m_{a}^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\left(\delta m_{a}^{2}\right)^{2}

(130)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{a}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{3}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\delta m_{a}^{2}(x\rho)-\delta m_{a}^{2}\right)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\left(\delta m_{A}^{2}\right)^{2}-\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\left(\delta m_{A}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{3}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\delta m_{A}^{2}(x\rho)-\delta m_{A}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}\left(-4+3\ln 2\right)\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}^{2}+4\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{4}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\dot{m}_{A}(x\rho)-\dot{m}_{A}\right)\,.

This concludes the calculation of the three divergent traces in angular momentum space.

Next, we move on to momentum space using dimensional regularisation.
As usual, we have
T_{1,0}^{\rm DR}=0
.
The rest naturally splits as
T_{1,1}^{\rm DR}=T_{1,1}^{a,{\rm DR}}+T_{1,1}^{A,{\rm DR}}

and
T_{2,0}^{\rm DR}=T_{2,0}^{a,{\rm DR}}+T_{2,0}^{A,{\rm DR}}+T_{\rm o.d.}^{\rm DR}
.
The contribution from the would-be NGB is given by

T_{1,1}^{a,{\rm DR}}={\rm RHS}\eqref{eq:T11Reg2}|_{m\to m_{a}}
and

T_{2,0}^{a,{\rm DR}}={\rm RHS}\eqref{T20regp1}|_{m\to m_{a}}
.
Altogether, we find

\displaystyle T_{1,1}^{\rm DR}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}}{\delta^{2}_{\rm IR}}\right)\int_{\rho}\ \rho^{3}\hat{m}_{a}^{2}\delta m_{a}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}}{\delta^{2}_{\rm IR}}\right)\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\hat{m}_{A}^{2}\delta m_{A}^{2}\,,

(131)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{2,0}^{\rm DR}&=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{k}\left|\widetilde{\delta m_{a}^{2}}(k)\right|^{2}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}+2+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\big(\delta m_{A}^{2}\big)^{2}+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{k}\left|\widetilde{\delta m_{A}^{2}}(k)\right|^{2}\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}}{k^{2}}\\
&+2\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+1\right)\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{k}\left|\tilde{m}_{A}(k)\right|^{2}k^{2}\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}}{k^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(132)

As we did in the previous sections, we wrote the above expressions as single
\rho
integrals
whenever possible, and kept in momentum space the inverse Fourier transforms involving
\ln k^{2}
.
Now we can use the identities (
70
) and (
94
) and obtain

\displaystyle T_{2,0}^{\rm DR}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}(\delta m_{a}^{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}\rho}{2}\right)

(133)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{a}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{3}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\delta m_{a}^{2}(x\rho)-\delta m_{a}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}(\delta m_{A}^{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\gamma_{E}-\frac{1}{4}+\ln\frac{\tilde{\mu}\rho}{2}\right)+2\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\delta m_{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{3}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\delta m_{A}^{2}(x\rho)-\delta m_{A}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\gamma_{E}-\frac{5}{3}+\ln(2\tilde{\mu}\rho)\right)+4\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}\dot{m}_{A}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{4}}{1-x^{2}}\left(\dot{m}_{A}(x\rho)-\dot{m}_{A}\right)\,.

We have all the ingredients needed for the regularisation prescription,

\begin{split}\left.\ln\frac{\det S^{\prime\prime}}{\det{\hat{S}}^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{A,a}^{\xi=1}&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left(d_{\nu}^{\phi}\ln R_{\nu}^{(Da)}+2d_{\nu}^{T}\ln R_{\nu}^{T}\right)\\
&\qquad\quad-\left(T_{1,0}-T_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T_{2,0}\right)+\left(T^{\rm DR}_{1,0}-T^{\rm DR}_{1,1}-\frac{1}{2}T^{\rm DR}_{2,0}\right)\,.\end{split}

(134)

All the double integrals cancel out, the rest combines into the result given
in (
17
).

VI
Outlook

In this work we give concise and precise expressions for the regularised determinants
of different quantum fields, obtained from a well defined double expansion and high
multipole subtraction.
We believe these simple results are useful for a precise determination of meta-stability
conditions for generic BSM models, especially when additional non-bounce quantum fluctuation
have a significant impact on the rate.
Here we focused on
D=4
, but the conceptual steps and the power-counting expansions apply
in any
D
and it would be interesting to derive even more general expressions in generic
D
s,
as in
[
20
]
for scalars.
In particular, the
D=3
case is relevant for computing vacuum decay rates at finite temperature.
One can also go to higher orders in the subtraction
[
47
]
and speed up
the multipole convergence.
In our formalism, this would imply computing traces
T_{i,j}
with a number of insertions

i\geq 1
and
j\geq 2
, that are UV convergent in
D=4
.
Similarly, one can consider using the
Feynman diagrammatic approach with the double expansion
developed here and generalise it to simplify two loop
[
48
,
49
]

corrections.
See also
[
50
]
for a recent work on two-loop corrections to false vacuum decay rates.

Our final prescription bypasses the Fourier transform and may thus be numerically more
stable and easier to implement.
This could be useful for expanding on the existing packages
[
22
]
,
perhaps building on the polygonal bounce
[
51
]
framework and
its
FindBounce

[
52
]
implementation.
Another potential use of these expressions is to derive exact formulæ for quantum
fluctuations in BSM cases
[
53
,
54
,
55
]
, where the bounce and the scalar determinants are known.
These may include the thin-wall
[
38
]
expansion and its thick
limits
[
40
]
, as well as the double quartic
[
39
]

or similar setups
[
56
]
.

Generally speaking our work simplifies the form of quantum corrections around a scalar
instanton.
It might be of interest to consider also more general background objects, such as gauge
instantons
[
57
]
, sphalerons
[
58
,
59
]

or Q-balls
[
60
,
61
]
.
These may find practical applications in QCD
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
62
]
.

Appendix A
Fourier transforms with logarithms

The purpose of this appendix is to prove the key equations (
70
)
and (
94
) using two different methods, as we believe that both
approaches have their virtues.
Both techniques employ a regularisation method, whose aim is to isolate the UV
divergences that eventually cancel out.
The method we employ for (
70
) is more straightforward and uses standard
distribution-theory reasoning, where a finite length scale
a
acts as a regulator,
which is sent to zero in the end.
On the other hand, we prove (
94
) via analytic continuation techniques,
inspired by
\zeta
-function renormalisation.
It is used to make the trace better behaved in
the UV, since its modified propagators make it go to zero faster for
k\to\infty
.

Proof via regulated distributions.

Let us start with Eq. (
70
) and rewrite it in the following form,
using integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{k}\big(\tilde{f}(|k|)\big)^{2}\ln k^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}\nabla_{x}^{2}f(|x|)\int_{y}f(|y|)\int_{k}e^{-i(x-y)k}\frac{\ln k^{2}}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}\nabla_{x}^{2}f(|x|)\int_{y}f(|y|)\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}|x-y|^{2}}\bigg({-}\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4}\bigg)

(135)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{x}f(|x|)\int_{y}f(|y|)\nabla_{x}^{2}\bigg[\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\bigg(\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4}\bigg)\bigg]\,.

The extra factor of
1/k^{2}
in the 4D FT of
\ln k^{2}/k^{2}
makes it
convergent and straightforward to calculate.
To evaluate the last line, we start by looking for a solution
G(x)
, regular
at infinity and with
x\in{\bf R}^{4}
, to the partial differential equation

\nabla_{x}^{2}G(x)=-\frac{1}{(|x|^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}\,.

(136)

Here,
a>0
is a dimensionful regulator that we send to zero at the end.
Since the equation is
O(4)
symmetric around
x=0
,
G
can only depend on
\rho=|x|
.
Integrating (
136
) once from
0
to
\rho
, we get

2\pi^{2}\rho^{3}\frac{{\rm d}G(\rho)}{{\rm d}\rho}=\pi^{2}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}+a^{2}}+\ln\frac{a^{2}}{a^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right),

(137)

and after a second integration we find

G(\rho)=\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\ln\frac{a^{2}+\rho^{2}}{a^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\left(\ln\rho^{2}-\ln a^{2}+\ln\frac{a^{2}+\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)\,.

(138)

Isolating the
\ln\rho^{2}/\rho^{2}
term and taking the
\nabla^{2}
, we can establish

\nabla_{x}^{2}\,\frac{\ln|x-y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}=-\frac{4}{(|x-y|^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}-4\pi^{2}\ln a^{2}\delta^{4}(x-y)-\nabla_{x}^{2}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\ln\frac{|x-y|^{2}+a^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,,

(139)

where we used (
136
) and
\nabla_{x}^{2}{|x-y|^{-2}}=4\pi^{2}\delta^{4}(x-y)
.
The last term is
O(a^{2})
as a distribution, so we drop it.
Applying (
139
) to the last line of (
135
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{k}\big(\tilde{f}(|k|)\big)^{2}\ln k^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\gamma_{E}+\ln\frac{a}{2}\,\right)\int_{x}f^{2}(|x|)-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{x}\int_{y}\frac{f(|x|)f(|y|)}{(|x-y|^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}+O(a^{2})\,.

(140)

Since the LHS does not depend on
a
, the double integral on the RHS has to carry
a
\ln a
term that exactly cancels
-2\ln a\int_{x}f^{2}(|x|)
.
We can write it explicitly as

\displaystyle\!\int_{x}\int_{y}\frac{f(|x|)f(|y|)}{(|x-y|^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}=16\pi^{3}\int_{\rho}\rho^{3}f(\rho)\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}xx^{3}f(\rho x)\int_{\theta}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{(1+x^{2}-2x\cos\theta+a^{2}/\rho^{2})^{2}}\,.

(141)

From here it becomes clear that the divergence comes from the
a\to 0
,
x=1
,

\theta=0
region, where the denominator blows up.
Indeed, setting
f(\rho x)\xrightarrow{x\to 1}f(\rho)
, we can manifestly isolate
the
\ln a
dependence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}x\int_{\theta}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta\,x^{3}}{\left(1+x^{2}-2x\cos\theta+a^{2}/\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}=-\frac{\pi}{4}\left(1+\ln\frac{a}{\rho}\right)+O(a)\,.

(142)

Consequently, we can split
f(\rho x)=f(\rho)+\big(f(\rho x)-f(\rho)\big)

in (
141
), such that the first term with
f(\rho)
brings out the

\ln a
divergence, which cancels against the one in (
140
).
The remaining term with
f(\rho x)-f(\rho)
is finite when

x\to 1
, so we can safely send the regulator
a\to 0

already in the integrand and get (
70
).

Eq. (
94
) can be derived in a similar way by considering the key identity

\displaystyle\int_{k}\big(\tilde{f}(|k|)\big)^{2}k^{2}\,\ln k^{2}

Systematic Analysis of Stromal-Epithelial Crosstalk Reveals the FGF21-FGFR3 Axis as a Key Driver of Corneal Wound Healing
Authors: Zhe Zhu, Hongran Zhao, Qingjun Zhou, Shengqian Dou, Weiyun Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeCorneal epithelial defects are a significant clinical problem and pose a severe threat to vision. However, their underlying mechanisms remain largely elusive. Here, we used single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and constructed cell-cell communication networks between corneal stromal cells (CSCs) and epithelial cells (CEpCs) to elucidate the pathological mechanism underlying corneal epithelial wound repair.MethodsCorneal tissues from normal and corneal epithelium defect mice were subjected to scRNA-seq. Comprehensive bioinformatic analyses, including unsupervised clustering, differential gene expression, functional enrichment, pseudotime, and RNA velocity analysis, were conducted to illustrate CSC heterogeneity. CellChat was used to identify the signaling crosstalk between CEpCs and CSCs. Mechanistic studies using co‑culture, FGFR3 perturbation, FGF21 manipulation, functional assays, and bulk RNA‑seq were performed to dissect the FGF21-FGFR3 axis in CEpCs and CSCs.ResultsUnbiased clustering of the scRNA-seq data revealed eight distinct CSC subpopulations. Cell-cell communication mapping and immunofluorescence validation revealed that CSCs actively engaged CEpCs via the FGF21-FGFR3 axis. CEpCs co-cultured with FGF21‑overexpressing CSCs showed greater wound closure and EdU uptake. FGF21 stimulated ERK/AKT phosphorylation, promoted wound healing and proliferation, affects that were blunted by dabogratinib or FGFR3 knockdown and rescued by FGFR3 re‑expression. Functional experiments demonstrated that Fgf21 knockdown significantly delayed corneal wound healing, whereas topical administration of FGF21 eye drops promoted corneal epithelial wound repair in vivo. Bulk RNA-seq further revealed an FGF21-responsive transcriptional program involving extracellular matrix remodeling, cell adhesion, migration-related processes, and MAPK signaling.ConclusionsOur study revealed a novel interaction between CSCs and CEpCs mediated by the FGF21-FGFR3 axis. Exogenous FGF21 promotes the wound healing of CEpCs, suggesting a promising novel therapeutic strategy for corneal epithelial repair.

The corneal epithelium serves as the first protective barrier of the ocular surface and plays a critical role in maintaining corneal transparency and visual acuity. Corneal epithelial injury remains a common clinical problem caused by trauma, infection, drugs, surgery, or metabolic disorders.1–3 Although corneal wound healing is generally considered a rapid process involving epithelial limbal stem cells,4 impaired epithelial repair can lead to persistent epithelial defects, stromal scarring, and neovascularization.5 More seriously, delayed corneal wound healing increases the susceptibility to severe complications, including corneal ulcers, microbial keratitis, and even perforation.2 Despite advances in the management of corneal epithelial defects, the molecular mechanisms underlying corneal epithelial injury remain incompletely understood, highlighting the need to identify effective therapeutic targets for clinical intervention.

Previous studies have focused mainly on improving corneal epithelial cell (CEpC) function to promote corneal epithelial repair, especially in the case of limbal stem cells.6–9 However, the contributions of other corneal cell types, especially corneal stromal cells (CSCs), which are near CEpCs, to corneal epithelial wound healing remain poorly understood.10,11 Emerging evidence indicates that corneal epithelial wound healing is not solely governed by CEpCs but instead depends on dynamic interactions between epithelial cells and the underlying stromal microenvironment.12,13 Deciphering the intercellular communication networks between CSCs and CEpCs is critical for understanding the regulatory mechanisms of corneal epithelial repair and identifying potential therapeutic targets.14,15 Thus, it is necessary to elucidate how CSCs communicate with CEpCs and which specific ligand-receptor pathways orchestrate epithelial repair.

Fibroblast growth factor (FGF) signaling is an important component of corneal epithelial homeostasis and regeneration. FGF7 can stimulate epithelial proliferation, although sustained or excessive epithelial FGF7 signaling may cause hyperplasia, whereas FGF10-fibroblast growth factor receptor 2b (FGFR2b) supports corneal epithelial proliferation, differentiation, barrier maintenance, and resistance to cellular stress.16–20 Together, these findings underscore the cell context- and receptor-dependent effects of individual FGF ligands in the cornea. However, whether FGF21 acts as a locally derived paracrine signal during corneal epithelial repair remains unclear. Elucidating its cellular source, epithelial receptor dependence, and functional role may therefore provide insight into a previously underappreciated component of stromal-epithelial communication during corneal wound healing.

In this study, we first used single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to systematically analyze cell-cell communication between CSCs and CEpCs during epithelial wound healing. We observed marked functional remodeling and enhanced intercellular signaling in CSCs after injury. Notably, we identified the FGF21-FGFR3 axis as a key paracrine pathway through which CSCs promote corneal epithelial wound healing. Functional validation further demonstrated that modulation of the topical administration of FGF21 eye drops significantly improved epithelial repair. Collectively, these findings reveal the crucial roles of CSCs and stromal-epithelial communication in corneal epithelial wound healing and highlight that the FGF21-FGFR3 axis represents a promising therapeutic target for improving corneal epithelial wound healing and the clinical management of corneal surface diseases.

Methods

Animals

Male C57BL/6J mice (approximately 6–8 weeks old) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. All procedures were performed in accordance with the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research and were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Eye Institute of Shandong First Medical University (SDSYKYJS No. 20250607).

ScRNA-Seq Data Analysis

ScRNA-seq data were analyzed using the Seurat R package (version 4.4.0). Cells with a total UMI count (nCount) less than 500, fewer than 500 detected genes (nFeature), or mitochondrial gene expression exceeding 20% were excluded from the analysis. The “NormalizeData” function was used to standardize the data, and the top 2000 highly variable genes (HVGs) were identified from the normalized expression matrix using the “ScaleData” and “FindVariableFeatures” functions. Dimensionality reduction was performed using canonical correlation analysis (CCA),21,22 and cell similarity was evaluated using shared nearest neighbors (SNNs). DoubletFinder (version 2.0.4) was used to remove doublet cells.23 Finally, cell types were manually annotated based on canonical cell markers and visualized using uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP). Furthermore, marker genes for each cell type were identified using the “FindAllMarkers” function.

Additionally, sample origin identification and distribution analysis of integrated mouse corneal cells were performed using the Souporcell algorithm, as previously described.24 Souporcell identified four source clusters for each condition (Ctrl_0–3 and Wound_0–3). Cells that could not be confidently assigned were labeled as “NA” (Supplementary Fig. S1A).

Differential Expression Analysis

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the “FindMarkers” function with a threshold of P < 0.05 and |logFC| > 0.25 (determined via the “MAST” test).25

High-Dimensional Weighted Gene Co-Expression Network Analysis

The high-dimensional weighted gene co-expression network analysis (hdWGCNA) package (version 0.3.00) was used to characterize the key molecular features of corneal stromal cells.26 Detailed information about hdWGCNA is available online at https://smorabit.github.io/hdWGCNA.

Gene Ontology Enrichment Analysis and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis

For each individual cell type, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of DEGs was performed using the GSEABase software package (version 1.64.0) combined with Gene Ontology (GO) terms (m5.go.v2025.1.Mm.symbols) to identify alterations in cellular function across different groups; significant GO terms were defined as those for which P < 0.05.27

Enrichment analyses of marker genes for each cell cluster were conducted using the “enrichGO” function in the clusterProfiler software package (version 4.10.0) to obtain cluster-specific functions; significant GO terms with P < 0.05 were selected.28

Cell-Cell Communication Analysis

Cell-cell communication analysis based on the scRNA-seq data was performed using the CellChat R software package (version 2.1.2).29 Specifically, CellChat was applied to each group using normalized expression data and cell annotation information, following the standard workflow with default parameters and the mouse ligand-receptor database; intergroup comparisons were subsequently performed.

Gene Set Score Analysis

Gene set score analysis utilized gene sets sourced from the gene list, with details provided in Supplementary Table S1. The signature scores for each gene set within every cell were calculated using the “AddModuleScore” function. Significance testing was performed through a one-tailed Mann–Whitney U test (Wilcoxon rank-sum test).

RNA Velocity Analysis

The Velocyto.R (version 0.6) package was used to estimate the RNA velocities of corneal stromal cells by quantifying unspliced and spliced mRNAs based on output files from the Cellranger R package (version 1.1.0).30

Trajectory Analysis

The Monocle 3 (version 1.3.7) R software package was used to infer the pseudotime trajectories of corneal stromal cells and corneal epithelial cells.31 A principal graph within each partition was fit using the learn graph function, and the results were visualized using the UMAP method. Lineage trajectory plots based on CellDataSet were generated using the “plot_cells” function.

In addition, pseudotime trajectory inference on corneal stromal cells was performed using the slingshot R software package, following methods described previously.32

Culture and Treatment of Human Corneal Epithelial Cell Lines

Human corneal epithelial cells (HCECs) were seeded in 6-well plates at an initial density of 5 × 10⁵ cells per well. The cells were cultured in DMEM-F12 (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco) and 1% penicillin‒streptomycin (Gibco). Upon reaching approximately 60% to 80% confluence, the cells were subjected to serum starvation for 12 hours in serum-free DMEM-F12, followed by treatment with 1 µg/mL FGF21 (HY-P72651, MedChemExpress, USA) for 24 hours; phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) was used as the control. After treatment, cells from each group were harvested for subsequent analyses. All experiments were performed in a humidified incubator at 37°C with 5% CO₂.

Bulk RNA-Seq Analysis

Total RNA was extracted from HCECs using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The libraries were subsequently constructed using a VAHTS Universal V10 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit (Premixed Version). The libraries were subsequently sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq X Plus platform, and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated.

Gene-level count data were analyzed for differential expression using DESeq2 in R software.33 Six independent biological replicates were analyzed per group. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks were generated using STRING, with Homo sapiens specified as the organism.34 Candidate upstream transcription factors were identified using the ChEA_2022 library in Enrichr.35,36

CSCs Transfection and Non-Contact Co-Culture

Human stromal keratocyte cell lines (HTK cells/CSCs) were cultured in DMEM-F12 (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco) and 1% penicillin‒streptomycin (Gibco) and transfected with an FGF21 overexpression plasmid (FGF21-OE) or the corresponding empty-vector plasmid (Con-OE; Genechem, China) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Cells were used 24 hours after transfection, and overexpression efficiency was verified by Western blotting. For non-contact co-culture, transfected CSCs were seeded in the upper chamber of Transwell inserts (Corning, USA) with 0.4 µm pores, and HCECs were seeded in the lower chamber. After 24 hours, HCECs were subjected to scratch-wound and EdU assays.

FGFR3 Inhibition, Knockdown, and Rescue

To inhibit FGFR3, HCECs were pretreated with dabogratinib (TYRA-300; MedChemExpress, USA) at 50 nM for 6 hours,37 followed by FGF21 (1 µg/mL, HY-P72651, MedChemExpress, USA) for 12 hours in the continued presence of dabogratinib. Vehicle controls received PBS alone. For genetic perturbation, HCECs were transduced with an FGFR3-targeting lentiviral shRNA or a negative-Con lentivirus (Genechem, China) at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 50. The target sequence was GCACAACCTCGACTACTACAA, and stable cells were selected using puromycin. For rescue, FGFR3-depleted cells were transfected with an shRNA-resistant FGFR3 expression plasmid (Genechem, China) using Lipofectamine 3000. FGFR3 knockdown and re-expression were verified by Western blotting.

Scratch Wound-Healing Assay

HCECs were seeded in 12-well plates and cultured to a confluent monolayer. A linear wound was generated using a sterile 200 µL pipette tip,38 and detached cells were removed by washing with PBS. Cells were maintained under various experimental conditions, and imaging was performed at 0 and 12 hours post-culture. The wound area was quantified using ImageJ software by an investigator blinded to group allocation.

EdU Proliferation Assay

Cell proliferation was assessed using an EdU cell proliferation kit (CX002, CellorLab, China). HCECs were incubated with EdU (10 µM) for 6 hours, fixed with 4% formaldehyde, permeabilized, and processed according to the manufacturer's protocol. Nuclei were counterstained with 4',6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; Abcam, AB104139).

Intrastromal Injection of AAV

After the mice were anesthetized, a 36-gauge needle was used to make a horizontal stromal incision at the corneal limbus. A volume of 2.5 to 3 µL AAV9-shFgf21 (1 × 1012 vg; Target seq: GCAGAGGTACCTCTACACA) was slowly injected into the corneal stroma. Antibiotic eye ointment was applied to prevent infection. AAV9-shFgf21 and the negative control were purchased from Shanghai Genechem Co., Ltd. (China).

Corneal Epithelial Wound Healing In Vivo

After the mice were anesthetized, proparacaine hydrochloride eye drops (Alcon, UK) were applied as topical anesthesia before the injury was performed. The epithelium in the central 2.5 mm region of the eye was marked with a corneal trephine and then scraped using an Alger Brush II corneal rust ring remover (BR2–5, Alger, USA). The corneal epithelial defects were visualized with fluorescein sodium (Jingming, China). Corneal epithelial wound healing was evaluated using ImageJ software.

Treatment With FGF21

Following corneal epithelial scraping, a 50 µg/mL solution of FGF21 (HY-P72651, MedChemExpress, USA) was administered to mice topically via eye drops (5 µL per dose) three times daily. Control mice received an equivalent volume of PBS. Corneal epithelial defects were visualized by sodium fluorescein (Jingming, China) staining via slit-lamp biomicroscopy. Corneal epithelial wound closure was quantified using ImageJ software.

Immunofluorescence

After the corneas were fixed, permeabilized, and blocked, they were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies, including anti-FGF21 (26272-1-AP, Proteintech, China), anti-FGF10 (29749-1-AP, Proteintech, China), anti-ZO-1 (33–9100, Thermo Fisher, USA), and anti-occludin (ab216327, Abcam, USA). After being washed three times with PBS, the corneas were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Abcam, AB104139). The tissue sections were subsequently imaged using a fluorescence microscope (DMi8, Leica, Germany).

Western Blotting

Protein lysates were prepared using RIPA lysis buffer. The obtained protein lysates were subjected to 10% SDS‒PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Following blocking with 5% BSA for 1.5 hours at room temperature, the membranes were incubated with primary antibody at 4°C overnight. Afterward, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibody for 1 hour at room temperature, after which the proteins were detected with an ECL detection kit. Primary antibodies included those targeting FGF21 (26272-1-AP, Proteintech, China), FGFR3 (30202-1-AP, Proteintech, China), phospho-ERK1/2 (80031-1-RR, Proteintech, China), ERK1/2 (11257-1-AP, Proteintech, China), phospho-AKT (28731-1-AP, Proteintech, China), AKT (10176-2-AP, Proteintech, China), and GAPDH (10494-1-AP, Proteintech, China).

Statistical Analysis

The results of the statistical analysis of the scRNA-seq data were analyzed in R software using the corresponding packages. The experimental data were statistically analyzed using R software (version 4.3.2) and GraphPad Prism (version 10.4.1). Statistical significance was denoted as P < 0.05.

Results

ScRNA-Seq From the Corneas of Control and Wound Healing Mice

To characterize the cellular landscape of the mouse cornea during wound healing, scRNA-seq was performed on corneal tissues from control and wounded mice. After stringent quality control, 16,450 cells from control mouse corneas and 12,417 cells from wound healing mice were generated, with a mean of 3883 genes detected per cell (see Supplementary Fig. S1). Based on the expression of established marker genes, these clusters were annotated as corneal epithelial cells (CEpC, Pax6+), corneal stromal cells (CSC, Kera+), corneal endothelial cells (CEnC, Car3+), myeloid cells (Adgre+), T cells (Cd3d+), and neutrophils (Csf3r+; Figs. 1A, 1B). GO enrichment analysis of marker genes of each cell type revealed that CEpCs were enriched for pathways related to epithelial differentiation and barrier function, CSCs for extracellular matrix organization and collagen fibril assembly, CEnCs for cell-cell junction and transport processes, and immune cell populations for immune activation and inflammatory responses (Fig. 1C).

Comparisons of cell-type composition between control and wounded corneas revealed notable changes following injury. Although CEpCs and CSCs remained the predominant populations in both groups, the wounded corneas exhibited increased proportions of immune cells, particularly myeloid cells and neutrophils (Fig. 1D), which is consistent with an active inflammatory response during corneal wound healing.3 To further investigate wound healing-induced transcriptional alterations, DEGs between the control and wounded groups were identified within each cell type. Heatmap visualization revealed both upregulated and downregulated gene sets specific to each population (Fig. 1E), indicating that corneal epithelial wound healing triggers cell type-dependent transcriptional reprogramming.

Transcriptomic Characterization Reveals Heterogeneous Corneal Stromal Cell Subpopulations

Unsupervised clustering analysis performed with Seurat was subsequently applied to identify and characterize distinct cell subpopulations among CSCs. The CSCs detected were heterogeneous and clustered into eight clusters (Figs. 2A, 2B). Further examination of the proportions of stromal cell subpopulations revealed marked decreases in the numbers of the c0 and c7 subclusters, accompanied by a significant increase in the c4 subcluster (Fig. 2C). Notably, the c4 subcluster predominantly expressed inflammation-related factors, and GO enrichment analysis of marker genes revealed that the c4 subcluster was enriched in inflammatory pathways (e.g., “Cytokine production,” “Inflammatory response,” and “Cell chemotaxis”; Fig. 2D), indicating an inflammation-associated phenotypic shift in CSCs under wound healing conditions.

To explore potential lineage relationships among CSC subclusters, pseudotime trajectory analysis was performed. The trajectory reconstruction revealed a continuous differentiation landscape spanning the eight CSC subpopulations (Fig. 2E). The inferred trajectory suggested a branching pattern, indicating potential lineage divergence among stromal subsets. Consistently, RNA velocity analysis projected onto the UMAP embedding demonstrated coherent directional flows across clusters (Fig. 2F). The velocity vectors indicated dynamic state transitions along the inferred differentiation axis and supported the pseudotime-derived lineage relationships. Further visualization of the pseudotime distribution colored according to the experimental groups confirmed that the CSCs from both the control and wound conditions were distributed along the trajectory, although differences in density were observed at specific trajectory branches (Supplementary Figs. S2A, S2B). These findings suggest that corneal injury is associated with the redistribution of CSC states along the differentiation continuum.

Transcriptional Activation and Enhanced Cell-Cell Communication of CSCs During Corneal Wound Healing

We further investigated the functional alterations of CSCs by performing heterogeneity analysis between the wound healing and control groups. First, differential expression analysis demonstrated that the expression of genes involved in inflammatory signaling, chemokine activity, and immune cell recruitment markedly changed during wound healing (Fig. 3A). Gene set enrichment analysis further confirmed that these upregulated DEGs were significantly enriched in pathways related to cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, leukocyte chemotaxis, and innate immune responses (Fig. 3B), indicating a shift of CSCs toward a proinflammatory and immunomodulatory phenotype. Additionally, based on the enrichment analysis results, we identified key chemokines (including Ccl2, Ccl5, Ccl8, Cxcl1, Cxcl5, and Mif) that were significantly upregulated in CSCs derived from wound healing corneas compared with those derived from control corneas (Fig. 3C). These findings underscore the role of CSCs in orchestrating immune cell infiltration and amplifying local inflammatory signaling during tissue repair.

To further delineate the key molecular signatures of CSCs and validate the findings from the aforementioned differential gene expression analysis, we performed hdWGCNA. By constructing a scale-free network to optimize connectivity, a total of four gene modules were identified (Supplementary Figs. S3A, S3B). Based on gene expression levels and module activity scores, CSC-M1 and CSC-M3 were positively correlated with the wound healing groups (Supplementary Fig. S3C). Consistent with the preceding analytical findings, the CSC–M3 module in CSCs formed a highly interconnected co-expression network enriched in genes involved in the inflammatory response and cell migration (Figs. 3D, 3E). Concurrently, the CSC-M1 module (Supplementary Fig. S3D) revealed a regulatory network potentially associated with ribosomal components and transcriptional-translational processes, indicating that the CSCs in the wound healing group were in a functionally activated state. Consistently, GO enrichment analysis of the DEGs further validated these observations (Supplementary Fig. S3E).

Finally, cell-cell communication analysis revealed substantial remodeling of ligand-receptor interactions across corneal cells during wound healing. Compared with control corneas, wound healing corneas exhibited both an increased number and stronger strength of inferred interactions (Supplementary Fig. S3F). These observations suggest that the intercellular communication network within corneal tissue becomes more complex and displays significantly enhanced communication strength during epithelial wound healing. Furthermore, analysis of the relative changes in information flow intensity across major intercellular signaling pathways in the cornea revealed significantly greater overall communication network activity during wound healing than in the control, notably involving the CXCL, CCL, and MIF pathways (Supplementary Fig. S3G). Interaction network analysis of the CCL and MIF pathways further revealed crosstalk between CSCs and immune cell populations—including myeloid cells and neutrophils—in the wound healing group, with CSCs serving as the predominant secretory source (Fig. 3F). These findings underscore the pivotal role of CSCs as central signaling hubs that integrate inflammatory cues and orchestrate multicellular responses during corneal repair.

In summary, these findings indicate that corneal wound healing involves a tightly coordinated transcriptional and intercellular communication program in which CSCs adopt an activated, immune-interacting state to regulate inflammation, tissue remodeling, and restoration of corneal homeostasis.

Intercellular Communication Between Corneal Epithelial Cells and Stromal Cells

We subsequently conducted a more detailed characterization of the alterations in intercellular crosstalk between CSC and CEpC subclusters, together with their corresponding functional consequences. Unsupervised clustering analysis revealed heterogeneity within the CEpCs, which were partitioned into 14 distinct clusters (Supplementary Fig. S4A). Based on the expression levels of established marker genes, these clusters were annotated as quiescent limbal stem cells (qLSC, Ifitm3⁺, and Gpha2⁺), transitional amplifying cells (TAC, Mki67⁺, Top2a⁺, and Ccna2⁺), activated limbal stem cells (aLSC, Col17a1⁺, Mt1⁺, and Mt2⁺), corneal basal cells (CBC, Lama3⁺, and Itgb4⁺), corneal suprabasal cells (CSbC, Dsc2⁺, Dsg1a⁺, and Dsp⁺), corneal superficial cells (CSfC, Tjp3⁺, Muc20⁺, and Elf3⁺), and conjunctival cells (CjC, Krt4⁺, Krt17⁺, and Krt19⁺; see Supplementary Figs. S4A; S4B).

CellChat was used to construct and analyze the intercellular communication networks in the control and wound healing groups separately. Comparative analysis revealed that the number of inferred interactions was significantly increased in the wound healing corneas (Supplementary Fig. S4C). Although the inferred interaction strength slightly decreased, the relative strength analysis of information flow across major signaling pathways between the two groups demonstrated that the overall activity of the cellular communication network was markedly greater in the wound healing group (see Supplementary Fig. S4C; Fig. 4A). Furthermore, the upregulated signaling pathways in the wound healing group were enriched mainly in growth factor-related pathways as well as inflammatory and chemokine-related pathways, including those involving TGFβ, SPP1, CXCL, and MIF. Previous studies have shown that the FGF pathway plays a critical role in skin wound healing (see Fig. 4A).39 To further investigate this relationship, we assessed the major roles of distinct subclusters in FGF signaling by calculating centrality scores. These analyses revealed that in the wound healing group, CSCs (c0, c2, and c4) exhibited enhanced FGF signal sender function, whereas CEpCs (qLSC, TAC, aLSC, and CBC) acted as the primary signal receivers (Fig. 4B). The relative contributions of ligand-receptor pairs in the FGF pathway in the wound healing group were subsequently investigated. The results demonstrated that Fgf21 and Fgf10 were the primary effector ligands (Fig. 4C). Further analysis of the FGF pathway interaction network in the wound healing group revealed that interactions involving Fgfr1 were restricted to within CSC subpopulations (Supplementary Figs. S4D, S8). Consequently, Fgf10 to Fgfr2 and Fgf21 to Fgfr3 emerged as the key ligand-receptor pairs underlying the FGF communication network between stromal and epithelial cells. Among these, the c0, c2, and c4 CSC subpopulations functioned as the major signal senders, whereas the qLSC, TAC, aLSC, and CBC subpopulations served as the primary signal receivers (Fig. 4D).

Similarly, pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed a continuous differentiation landscape spanning CEpC subpopulations, which aligned with the biological transition from qLSC to CSfC (Fig. 4E). Furthermore, cell differentiation activity scoring revealed that, compared with those in the control group, the differentiation activities of qLSC and TAC in wound healing corneas were reduced, whereas those of aLSC, CBC, CSbC, and CSfC were significantly elevated (Fig. 4F). Consistent with these findings, the proportions of qLSC and TAC cells were increased, whereas the proportions of aLSC, CBC, CSbC, and CSfC were decreased (Fig. 4G). Collectively, these findings indicate that CEpCs in the wound healing group exhibit an activated state of epithelial wound healing function: the proliferative capacity of qLSC and TAC is enhanced, whereas aLSC, CBC, CSbC, and CSfC display a state of heightened differentiation.

To functionally test the predicted stromal-to-epithelial communication, we also established a non-contact Transwell co-culture model. FGF21 overexpression in CSCs was confirmed by Western blotting (Fig. 4H; Supplementary Fig. S4E). HCECs were then co-cultured with Con-OE or FGF21-OE CSCs across a 0.4 µm membrane, allowing exchange of soluble factors without direct cell contact (Fig. 4I). Compared with Con-OE co-culture, FGF21-OE CSCs significantly increased HCEC wound closure at 12 hours (Figs. 4J, 4K) and increased the proportion of EdU-positive HCECs (Fig. 4L). These results provide functional evidence that stromal FGF21 is sufficient to enhance epithelial repair-related behavior through a paracrine co-culture mechanism.

In summary, interactions between CSCs and CEpCs may facilitate corneal epithelial wound healing, among which the FGF pathway is likely to play a key role.

The FGF21-FGFR3 Axis Mediates the Interaction Between Corneal Stromal Cells and Epithelial Cells

Building on the FGF signaling identified by the intercellular communication studies, we next sought to determine which ligand-receptor pair was most likely to mediate signaling from CSCs to CEpCs during corneal epithelial repair. Because Fgf21 and Fgf10 contributed most strongly to the predicted FGF interactions in the wound group (see Fig. 4C), we examined their spatial distribution in injured corneas. Immunofluorescence staining showed an apparent increase in stromal FGF21 signal after injury, whereas the increase in FGF10 signal was less evident (Figs. 5A, 5B). Consistent with a stromal-to-epithelial mode of signaling, FGF ligands, including Fgf21 and Fgf10, were only sparsely detected in CEpCs (Supplementary Fig. S5A). Among the FGF receptors expressed by CEpCs, Fgfr3 was significantly upregulated after injury, whereas Fgfr2 was reduced and Fgfr1 remained scarcely expressed (Supplementary Fig. S5B). Klb (β-Klotho) transcripts were also rarely detected in CEpCs (Supplementary Fig. S5C). These findings led us to focus subsequent experiments on the FGF21-FGFR3 axis.

To elucidate the receptor-level mechanism underlying the effects of FGF21, we investigated whether FGFR3 was required for FGF21-induced epithelial responses. FGF21 treatment significantly increased HCECs wound closure and EdU incorporation, whereas co-treatment with the FGFR3-selective inhibitor dabogratinib attenuated both effects (Figs. 5C, 5D; Supplementary Fig. S6A).37 FGF21 also increased ERK1/2 and AKT phosphorylation, and these responses were reduced by dabogratinib (Fig. 5E; Supplementary Figs. S6B; S6C). To validate receptor dependence genetically, FGFR3 knockdown reduced FGF21-induced wound closure and EdU incorporation, whereas FGFR3 re-expression after knockdown restored both phenotypes (Figs. 5F, 5G; Supplementary Fig. S6F). Western blotting confirmed FGFR3 knockdown and rescue efficiency (Supplementary Figs. S6D, S6E). Together, these pharmacological and genetic data support an FGFR3-dependent mechanism for FGF21-induced responses in HCECs.

FGF21 Promotes Corneal Epithelial Wound Healing

To further investigate the protective role of FGF21 in corneal wound repair, the corneal epithelia of intrastromal AAV9-mediated FGF21 knockdown (Supplementary Figs. S7A, S7B) and negative control mice were debrided and visualized by fluorescein staining (Fig. 6A). Intrastromal AAV9-mediated FGF21 knockdown mice exhibited delayed corneal wound healing (Fig. 6B). We further evaluated the corneal wound healing rates in control and FGF21 eye drop-treated mice following epithelial injury. Compared with the control treatment, the topical administration of FGF21 significantly accelerated epithelial wound healing (Fig. 6C), as evidenced by a reduced residual defect area and faster recovery of epithelial continuity (Fig. 6D). In addition, whole-mount staining of tight junction ZO-1 and occludin revealed improved epithelial cell morphology, indicating that FGF21 enhanced barrier integrity during the repair process (Figs. 6E, 6F).

To further explore the underlying mechanism of the effects of FGF21 on corneal wound healing, we performed RNA-seq on HCECs after treatment with 1 µg/mL FGF21. At the cutoff of P value < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.585, 109 significant DEGs were identified, including 73 upregulated and 36 downregulated genes (Fig. 7A; Supplementary Table S2). Hierarchical clustering of these genes clearly separated FGF21-treated cells from controls (Supplementary Fig. S7C). GO analysis of the upregulated DEGs highlighted several processes relevant to epithelial repair, including extracellular matrix organization and disassembly, epithelial cell proliferation and differentiation, cell junction and adhesion, migration-related processes involving filopodia and small GTPase signaling, p38 MAPK regulation, and fibroblast growth factor receptor binding (Fig. 7B). These terms contained genes involved in matrix remodeling (MMP10, COL2A1, and ADAMTS6), cell junction and cytoskeletal regulation (MIP, CLCA2, IQGAP2, and TIAM2), as well as FGF21 itself (Fig. 7C). PPI analysis yielded a network of 43 proteins and 69 interactions, with FGF21 positioned within the main interaction cluster (Fig. 7D). Transcription factor enrichment highlighted PAX6, SOX2, and SMAD4 as candidate upstream regulators potentially involved in epithelial repair (Supplementary Fig. S7D).40–42 These results link the HCEC response to FGF21 with extracellular matrix remodeling, epithelial proliferation and differentiation, cell migration and adhesion, and MAPK-related signaling.

Discussion

Corneal epithelial wound healing is a coordinated, multicellular process that requires timely epithelial resurfacing, the restoration of barrier function, and controlled inflammatory resolution.43,44 In the present study, we used single-cell transcriptomics to reconstruct the cellular landscape of the mouse cornea under homeostasis and after epithelial debridement injury and further integrated computational and experimental approaches to elucidate the intercellular communication between CSCs and CEpCs. Our data reveal that corneal epithelial injury triggers broad transcriptional reprogramming across multiple cell types and markedly enhances the overall communication strength within the corneal microenvironment. Importantly, we identified a stroma-derived FGF21-FGFR3 axis as a key paracrine module that promotes epithelial wound healing, highlighting a previously underappreciated stromal-epithelial regulatory mechanism during corneal epithelial repair (Fig. 8).

Schematic illustration of the molecular mechanism underlying the FGF21-mediated promotion of corneal epithelial wound healing.

Corneal repair is governed by bidirectional communication among the epithelium, keratocytes, extracellular matrix, resident immune cells, and recruited leukocytes. Epithelial injury exposes and releases signals that activate CSCs, whereas activated stromal cells remodel the extracellular matrix, produce cytokines and chemokines, and secrete growth factors that feed back to epithelial stem/progenitor and basal cells.10–13,43,45 Classical epithelial-stromal mechanisms include EMMPRIN/CD147-dependent matrix metalloproteinase regulation and FGF7/FGF10-FGFR2 signaling.10,16–20 Our scRNA-seq analysis unveiled profound heterogeneity of CSCs and further stratified eight stromal subpopulations, which exhibited a continuous trajectory distribution. Following epithelial injury, the composition of stromal subpopulations shifts significantly, characterized by decreases in selected clusters and a marked expansion of an inflammation-associated subset. Consistent with this phenotypic shift, differential expression and enrichment analyses demonstrated that wound-associated CSCs upregulate genes involved in chemokine activity, cytokine signaling, leukocyte chemotaxis, and innate immune responses.46 The increased expression levels of key chemokines (e.g., Ccl2, Ccl5, Ccl8, Cxcl1, Cxcl5, and Mif) support the notion that CSCs function as not only matrix-producing cells but also immunomodulatory hubs that shape the inflammatory milieu after injury. Such stromal activation likely contributes to the recruitment of myeloid cells and neutrophils, which we indeed observed as increased immune proportions in injured corneas. Furthermore, current evidence has demonstrated that macrophages recruited during the early phase of injury facilitate the rapid healing of corneal epithelial defects.47 Together, these data reinforce a model in which stromal cells orchestrate early inflammatory amplification, thereby indirectly delivering repair-facilitating signals to epithelial cells.

Beyond stromal heterogeneity, our analysis also revealed dynamic epithelial changes during repair. CEpCs remain the dominant corneal population, yet injury induces cell-state transitions suggestive of a coordinated repair program. Trajectory analysis indicated the progression of epithelial states, and gene set scoring suggested altered differentiation patterns in wound healing samples. From a tissue perspective, successful corneal epithelial repair requires not only rapid resurfacing but also the restoration of tight junction integrity and barrier function. Thus, epithelial repair should be viewed as a process that integrates cell-state reprogramming with microenvironmental cues and functional barrier reconstruction.

ScRNA-seq provides a powerful tool for revealing a panoramic view of cell-cell communication across various cell types.29 Recently, scRNA-seq has been applied to explore the cross-talk among all cell types of the cornea in healthy mice.48 However, intercellular communication among distinct corneal cell types under injury conditions has not yet been sufficiently investigated—particularly the functional role of CSCs adjacent to the corneal epithelium. Thus, in the present study, we used CellChat to demonstrate that corneal epithelial defects are accompanied by an increase in inferred ligand-receptor interactions and enhanced information flow across signaling pathways, with particularly strengthened communication between CSCs and CEpCs.49 Notably, the cell-cell interactome highlights FGF-related interactions as prominent candidate pathways during epithelial wound healing. Although FGF signaling is known to regulate epithelial growth and regeneration in multiple tissues,39 the specific stromal ligands and epithelial receptors that drive corneal epithelial repair have remained ambiguous. In our dataset, FGF21 emerged as a stromal-enriched ligand whose expression increased after injury, whereas its receptor, FGFR3, was upregulated in epithelial cells. Because CellChat infers intercellular communication from coordinated ligand and receptor expression, its predictions should be regarded as a framework for prioritizing candidate pathways rather than direct evidence of signal transmission.29,49 We therefore performed Transwell co-culture experiments, which showed that FGF21-overexpressing stromal cells enhanced epithelial wound closure and proliferation through non-contact, soluble-factor exchange. Subsequent pharmacological and genetic perturbation of FGFR3 further demonstrated that this epithelial receptor is required for the response to FGF21. Together, these findings support a functional paracrine model in which injury-activated stromal cells provide FGF21 signals to FGFR3-expressing epithelial cells to promote corneal epithelial wound healing.

FGF family members play essential roles in regulating corneal wound healing. For example, FGF10 treatment protects mouse corneal epithelial proliferation and barrier integrity from oxidative stress, ER stress, and apoptosis through FGFR2 and SLC7A11 signaling.18–20 Although FGF7 can promote epithelial cell proliferation, excessive FGF7 has been shown to induce corneal intraepithelial neoplasia in young mice and epithelial hyperplasia in adult mice.16,17 Here, we found that FGF21 was among the most prominently increased FGF ligands in the CSCs of an epithelial defect mouse model. FGF21 is a peptide hormone synthesized by multiple organs that acts as an autocrine, paracrine, and endocrine factor.50,51 Injury-induced CSC stress was accompanied by increased FGF21 expression, which in turn enhances epithelial repair through FGFR3, thereby accelerating resurfacing and promoting junctional recovery. Immunofluorescence showed increased stromal FGF21 immunoreactivity after injury, supporting the predicted stromal origin. Importantly, functional perturbation using AAV-mediated stromal knockdown of Fgf21 demonstrated that a reduction in stromal FGF21 expression impaired the repair-promoting effect, providing mechanistic evidence that stroma-derived FGF21 contributes causally to epithelial wound healing. In addition, the topical administration of FGF21 accelerated wound closure in vivo and was associated with improved epithelial tight junctions. Moreover, bulk RNA-seq analysis also revealed that FGF21 can promote the reprogramming of the state of CEpCs and the reconstruction of barrier function. Receptor-level perturbation experiments provided complementary mechanistic evidence: pharmacological FGFR3 inhibition with dabogratinib attenuated FGF21-induced wound closure, EdU incorporation, and ERK/AKT phosphorylation, whereas FGFR3 knockdown recapitulated this loss of responsiveness and FGFR3 re-expression restored it. The convergence of pharmacological inhibition, genetic loss of function, and rescue therefore indicates that FGFR3 is functionally required for key epithelial responses to FGF21 rather than merely being co-expressed during repair. Nevertheless, these findings do not suggest that all FGF signaling in the injured cornea is mediated by FGFR3. FGFR1-associated interactions were detected predominantly among stromal subpopulations, whereas FGF10-FGFR2 signaling remains a plausible parallel pathway mediating stromal-epithelial communication. The injured cornea may therefore use multiple spatially organized FGF ligand-receptor modules, with FGF21-FGFR3 representing the pathway mechanistically prioritized in the present study. The potential involvement of β-Klotho also warrants consideration because classical FGF21 signaling generally requires β-Klotho to confer receptor specificity and can activate FGFR1c or FGFR3c in reconstituted systems.52 Although Klb transcripts were only sparsely detected in our epithelial single-cell dataset, their low abundance and the possibility of dropout inherent to scRNA-seq prevent us from concluding that KLB protein is absent.53 Our inhibitor, knockdown, and rescue experiments establish FGFR3 dependence, but KLB itself was not directly perturbed. It therefore remains to be determined whether corneal epithelial responses to FGF21 require a low-abundance FGFR3-KLB complex or another cofactor, and the current findings should not be interpreted as evidence of KLB-independent signaling. Together, these results bridge computational prediction with biological validation and support the conclusion that the FGF21-FGFR3 axis is a key regulatory module in corneal epithelial repair. Thus, by integrating the aforementioned findings, it can be concluded that CSCs facilitate corneal epithelial wound healing via two distinct mechanisms: indirectly by modulating the inflammatory microenvironment and directly by paracrine secretion of growth factors.

There were several limitations that need to be elucidated. First, the corneal epithelial defect mouse model used in our study represents acute epithelial wound healing. Whether stromal-epithelial signaling is conserved in chronic epithelial defects, diabetes, or infectious keratitis-induced corneal epithelial defects requires further investigation. Second, although FGFR3 dependence was demonstrated pharmacologically and genetically in HCECs, the requirement for β-Klotho and the relative contributions of FGFR1- or FGFR2-associated pathways remain unresolved. Third, the bulk RNA-seq networks are computational associations that require gene-specific functional validation. Finally, the pharmacokinetics, ocular tolerability, optimal dose, and long-term safety of topical FGF21 require systematic evaluation before clinical translation.

Conclusions

Our study provides a single-cell atlas of the corneal wound healing microenvironment and reveals that epithelial injury induces marked stromal activation and enhanced epithelial-stromal communication. Notably, we revealed that the FGF21-FGFR3 axis plays a crucial role in promoting epithelial repair. These findings not only deepen the mechanistic understanding of corneal epithelial wound healing but also reveal a tractable paracrine pathway with potential therapeutic relevance.

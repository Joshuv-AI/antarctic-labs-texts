Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices with universal quantifiers
Feng Guo, Jie Wang

Abstract
. This paper investigates Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices subject to universally quantified polynomial matrix inequality constraints. We first establish a matrix-valued Positivstellensatz under the Archimedean condition, incorporating universal quantifiers. For scalar-valued polynomial objectives, we further develop a sparse Positivstellensatz that leverages correlative sparsity patterns within these quantified constraints. Moving beyond the Archimedean framework, we then derive two generalized Positivstellensätze under analogous settings. These results collectively unify and extend foundational theorems in three distinct contexts: classical polynomial Positivstellensätze, their universally quantified counterparts, and matrix polynomial formulations. Applications of the established Positivstellensätze to robust polynomial matrix inequality constrained optimization are also investigated.

Introduction

Positivstellensätze are fundamental results in real algebraic geometry, asserting under which conditions a polynomial is guaranteed to be positive on a given set

[
5
,
26
,
31
,
41
]
.
Most Positivstellensätze achieve this by expressing the polynomial using
sums of squares (SOS).
These powerful results offer constructive methods for certifying whether a polynomial
f
is positive over a basic semialgebraic set defined by polynomial inequalities

\mathbf{K}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid g_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\geq 0,j=1,\ldots,s\},

and have widespread applications in areas such as optimization, algebraic geometry, control theory, and more where polynomial positivity is a key concern
[
4
,
14
,
25
,
26
,
34
]
.

Depending on whether the semialgebraic set
\mathbf{K}
is assumed to be compact,
Positivstellensätze are divided into two categories.
When
\mathbf{K}
is a compact polyhedron with non-empty interior, Handelman’s Positivstellensatz

[
12
]
states
that a polynomial
f
that is positive on
\mathbf{K}
can be expressed as a positive linear combination of cross-products of
g_{j}
’s.
In the case of a more general compact basic semialgebraic set
\mathbf{K}
, Schmüdgen
[
43
]
demonstrated that if a polynomial
f
is positive on
\mathbf{K}
, then it belongs to the preorder generated by
g_{j}
’s, that is,
f
can be represented as an SOS-weighted combination of cross-products of
g_{j}
’s.
Putinar’s Positivstellensatz
[
39
]
provides an alternative representation that avoids the need for cross-products of
g_{j}
’s under the Archimedean condition (slightly stronger than compactness).
Building on Putinar’s Positivstellensatz, Lasserre
[
22
,
24
,
25
]

introduced a moment-SOS hierarchy of semidefinite relaxations
for polynomial optimization. This framework generates a non-decreasing sequence of lower bounds converging
to the optimal value of a given polynomial optimization problem.

There have been several attempts to provide certificates of positivity of a polynomial
over a non-compact semialgebraic set. Without requiring compactness of
\mathbf{K}
,
Krivine-Stengle Positivstellensatz
[
21
,
46
]
states that a polynomial
f
is positive
over
\mathbf{K}
if and only if
\psi f=1+\phi
for some
\psi,\phi
from the preorder generated by
g_{j}
’s. Pólya
[
35
]
demonstrated that if
f
is homogeneous and positive on

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, then
multiplying
f
by some power of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
, one obtains a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients.
Dickinson and Povh
[
7
]
generalized Pólya’s Positivstellensatz for homogeneous polynomials being positive on the intersection

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\cap\mathbf{K}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, where
g_{j}
’s are assumed to be also homogeneous.
Reznick
[
40
]
proved that, after multiplying by some power of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}
, any
positive definite (PD) form is a sum of even
powers of linear forms. Putinar and Vasilescu
[
38
]
extended Reznick’s result to the constrained case where
f
and
g_{j}
’s are homogeneous polynomials of even degree.
When
\mathbf{K}
is non-compact, SOS-structured positivity certificates of
f
over
\mathbf{K}
could be
also established by investigating various geometric objects associated with the data,
e.g., gradient varieties
[
33
]
, principal gradient tentacles
[
45
]
,
truncated tangency varieties
[
48
]
, and the polar varieties
[
10
]
.

Recently, Hu, Klep, and Nie
[
16
]
studied Positivstellensätze concerning semialgebraic sets defined by universal quantifiers (UQ). Specifically, for a given tuple
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{s})
of real polynomials
in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
{\boldsymbol{y}}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
, and a closed set
\mathcal{Y}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, they consider
representations of polynomials that are positive over the semialgebraic set

\mathcal{U}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid g_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\geq 0,\ldots,g_{s}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\geq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\}.

For a fixed measure
\nu
with support exactly on
\mathcal{Y}
, under the Carleman condition on
\nu
and the Archimedean condition,
they proved that if a polynomial
f
is positive on
\mathcal{U}
, then
f
belongs to the quadratic module associated to
(g,\nu)
. In other words,
f
admits a representation

\sigma_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\sigma_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})g_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})+\cdots+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\sigma_{s}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})g_{s}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}),

where each
\sigma_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,s
, are SOS polynomials. They also investigated the
corresponding moment problem on the semialgebraic set
\mathcal{U}
. As an important application,
their results could be used to solve
semi-infinite optimization problems which are highly challenging.

Most Positivstellensätze could be generalized to the matrix setting that both
f
and
g_{j}
’s are polynomial matrices.
Scherer and Hol
[
42
]
developed a matrix-version of Putinar’s Positivstellensatz.
Cimprič
[
6
]
extended the Krivine-Stengle Positivstellensatz to the case of polynomial matrices with polynomial constraints.
A matrix version of Handelman’s Positivstellensatz was proposed in
[
27
]
.
Building on Scherer and Hol’s Positivstellensatz, Dinh et al.
[
8
]

generalized the classical Schmüdgen,
Putinar-Vasilescu, and Dickinson–Povh Positivstellensätze to the polynomial matrix setting.
Given the projections of two semialgebraic sets defined by polynomial matrix inequalities (PMI), Klep and Nie
[
19
]
provided
a matrix Positivstellensatz with lifting polynomials to determine whether one is contained in the other. These generalizations have a wide range of applications, particularly in areas such as optimal control, systems theory
[
13
,
15
,
17
,
37
,
47
]
.

Computing SOS-structured representations involved in Positivstellensätze could be typically cast as
semidefinite programs (SDP).
However, the size of the SDPs grows rapidly with the dimension of the problem.
Hence, from the perspective of computation, it becomes appealing to develop sparse versions of Positivstellensätze for sparse data, e.g., correlative sparsity
[
32
,
23
,
49
]
, term sparsity
[
32
,
29
,
51
,
52
,
50
]
, and matrix (chordal) sparsity
[
54
,
53
]
.

Due to estimation errors or lack of information,
the data from real-world problems often involve uncertainty.
As a result, ensuring the robustness of PMIs over a prescribed set with uncertainty is crucial for some
safety-critical applications with little tolerance for failure
[
3
]
.
Consequently, Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices with UQs will be a powerful
mathematical tool for addressing this issue, which serves as the primary motivation for this work.

\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\},

In the following, we highlight the main results of this paper.
Throughout the paper, let
\nu
be a fixed Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with support
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)=\mathcal{Y}

and satisfying
\int_{\mathcal{Y}}|h({\boldsymbol{y}})|\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})<\infty
for all
h({\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{y}}]
.
For any
H({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{p}
, let us write

H({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\mbox{supp}_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}(H)}H_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}({\boldsymbol{x}}){\boldsymbol{y}}^{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
as a polynomial matrix in
{\boldsymbol{y}}
with

and let

\int_{\mathcal{Y}}H({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\coloneqq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\mbox{supp}_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}(H)}H_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\boldsymbol{y}}^{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}.

The main result of this paper is the following matrix-valued Positivestellensatz incorporating universal quantifiers,
which holds under the Archimedean condition (Assumption
3.1
)
and the Carleman condition on
\nu
(Assumption
3.2
).
See Section
2
for the definition of the product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{p}
.

Theorem A. (Theorem
3.1
)

Suppose that Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
hold.
If
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
is PD on
\mathcal{X}
, then there exist
SOS matrices
S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
,
S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}
such that

F({\boldsymbol{x}})=S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}).

Then we consider the particular case of
p=1
, namely, the objective is a scalar polynomial.
Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is defined by multiple PMIs with UQs (
G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0,j=1,\ldots,s
).
We assume the presence of correlative sparsity in the problem data, which implies that the variables
{\boldsymbol{x}}
decompose as a union of subsets
{\boldsymbol{x}}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{t}{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})
such that
\{G_{j}\}_{j=1}^{s}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{t}\{G_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}

and
G_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]^{q_{j}}
,
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\ldots,t
(see Assumption
3.3
).
Under these conditions, we could give the following sparse Positivstellensatz
that leverages correlative sparsity patterns within these quantified constraints.

Theorem B. (Theorem
3.2
)
Suppose that
\mathcal{Y}
is compact, Assumption
3.3
holds for
f\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
and
\mathcal{X}
,
and Assumption
3.1
holds with respect to each
{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})
.
If
f>0
on
\mathcal{X}
, then there exist SOS polynomials
\sigma_{\ell,0}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]
, and SOS polynomial matrices
S_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}),{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q_{j}}
,
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}
,
such that

f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\left(\sigma_{\ell,0}({\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\langle S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}),{\boldsymbol{y}}),G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}),{\boldsymbol{y}})\rangle\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\right).

Next, building on Theorem A and existing techniques,
we establish two generalized Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices with UQs
and without assuming the Archimedean condition.
The first is a Positivstellensatz for non-compact case.

Theorem C. (Theorem
4.1
)
Suppose that Assumption
3.2
holds,

F\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
and
G\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q}
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
of even degree, and

F({\boldsymbol{x}})\succ 0
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Then, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
SOS matrices
S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
,
S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}
which
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
\deg S_{0}=2N+\deg F
,
\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}S=2N+\deg F-\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}G
,
such that

\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2N}F({\boldsymbol{x}})=S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}).

The second is a Positivstellensatz on the nonnegative orthant.

Theorem D. (Theorem
4.2
)
Suppose that

F\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
and
G\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q}
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
,
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\succ 0
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\cap\mathcal{X}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, and
\mathcal{Y}
is compact.
Then, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
polynomial matrices
P_{0}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}P_{0,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}{\boldsymbol{x}}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
,

P=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}P_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}({\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{x}}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}
which
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and satisfy each

,
P_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}({\boldsymbol{y}})\succ 0

for all

, such that

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{N}F({\boldsymbol{x}})=P_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle P({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}).

The inhomogeneous counterparts of Theorems C and D appear in Corollaries
4.1
and
4.3
, respectively.

As applications, leveraging the SOS-structured certificates provided by the established Positivstellensätze, we propose hierarchies of SDP relaxations for the robust PMI constrained optimization
problem and analyze their convergence.

[{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}
if and only if there exists
Z\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{p|\mathbb{N}^{n}_{d}|}
such that

S({\boldsymbol{x}})=([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{p})^{\intercal}Z([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{p})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
be an SOS matrix and
S_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{k\times k}

be a principal submatrix of
S({\boldsymbol{x}})
whose rows and columns are indexed by
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
with

1\leq p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k}\leq p
, then
S_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})
is an SOS matrix.

Proof.

As
S({\boldsymbol{x}})
is an SOS matrix, there exists an
l\times p
polynomial matrix
T({\boldsymbol{x}})
for some

l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S({\boldsymbol{x}})=T({\boldsymbol{x}})^{\intercal}T({\boldsymbol{x}})
. Denote
by
T_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})
the submatrix of
T({\boldsymbol{x}})
consisting of the columns of

T(x)
indexed by
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
. Then
S_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})=T_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})^{\intercal}T_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})
and hence is an SOS matrix.
∎

We next recall Scherer-Hol’s Positivstellensatz for polynomial matrices obtained in
[
42
]
.
Define the bilinear mapping

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{p}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{pq\times pq}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{q\times q}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p},\quad\left\langle A,B\right\rangle_{p}=\mbox{tr}_{p}\left(A^{\intercal}(I_{p}\otimes B)\right),

with

\mbox{tr}_{p}\left(C\right)\coloneqq\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mbox{tr}\left(C_{11}\right)&\cdots&\mbox{tr}\left(C_{1p}\right)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mbox{tr}\left(C_{p1}\right)&\cdots&\mbox{tr}\left(C_{pp}\right)\end{array}\right]\quad\text{for }C=[C_{ij}]_{i,j\in[p]}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{pq\times pq},C_{ij}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q\times q}.

When
p=1
, the product
\left\langle A,B\right\rangle_{1}
coincides with the matrix
inner product
\langle A,B\rangle=\mbox{tr}\left(A^{\intercal}B\right)
.

Let
\mathbf{H}\coloneqq\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}\}
where each
H_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{r_{j}}
for some
r_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H({\boldsymbol{x}})=\mbox{diag}(H_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}}),\ldots,H_{t}({\boldsymbol{x}}))
be a block diagonal matrix, then for any SOS matrix
S({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{pr}
where
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
r=r_{1}+\cdots+r_{t}
,
there are SOS matrices
S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{r_{j}}
,
j\in[t]
,
such that

\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}}),H({\boldsymbol{x}})\right\rangle_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\left\langle S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}}),H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\right\rangle_{p}.

Proof.

For each
j\in[t]
, let
S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})
be the
pr_{j}\times pr_{j}
principal submatrix of
S({\boldsymbol{x}})
whose rows and columns are indexed by

\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}r_{\ell}+1,\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}r_{\ell},r+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}r_{\ell}+1,r+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}r_{\ell},\ldots,(p-1)r+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}r_{\ell}+1,(p-1)r+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}r_{\ell}\right).

As
H({\boldsymbol{x}})
is block diagonal, by the definition of the mapping
\left\langle.,.\right\rangle_{p}
,
it is easy to see that

\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}}),H({\boldsymbol{x}})\right\rangle_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\left\langle S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}}),H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\right\rangle_{p}.

By Lemma
2.1
, each
S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})
is an SOS matrix.
∎

The
matrix quadratic module

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})
generated by
\mathbf{H}
is defined as the cone

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})\coloneqq\left\{S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\left\langle S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}}),H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\right\rangle_{p}\ \middle|\ S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p},S_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{pr_{j}},j\in[t],\ \text{are SOS}\right\}.

The
k
-th truncated matrix quadratic module
\mathcal{Q}^{p}_{k}(\mathbf{H})
is a subcone of
\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})
in which the degrees of the polynomial matrices
S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})
and
\sum_{j=1}^{t}\left\langle S_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}}),H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\right\rangle_{p}
are bounded by
2k
.

Assumption 2.1
.

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})
is
Archimedean
, i.e.,
there is
C>0
such that
C-\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}^{1}(\mathbf{H})
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Scherer-Hol’s Positivestellensatz)

Let Assumption
2.1

and
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
be PD on

\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\succeq 0,\ j\in[t]\}
. Then
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})
.

Proof.

It was proved in
[
42
, Corollary 1]
for the case
t=1
.
The case
t>1
can be derived from
[
42
, Corollary 1]
and Lemma
2.2
.
∎

3.
Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices with UQs under the Archimedean condition

In this section, assuming the Archimedean condition,
we shall present a matrix-valued Positivstellensatz incorporating universal quantifiers,
along with a sparse version in the presence of correlative sparsity.

3.1.
A Positivstellensatze for polynomial matrices with UQs

Recall the set
\mathcal{X}
in (
1
).
Throughout the paper, let
\nu
be a fixed Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with support
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)=\mathcal{Y}

and satisfying
\int_{\mathcal{Y}}|h({\boldsymbol{y}})|\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})<\infty
for all
h({\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{y}}]
.
Similarly to the scalar case in
[
16
]
, let us define the
matrix quadratic module

associated with
(G,\nu)
as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

The matrix quadratic module
\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
generated by
G
and
\nu
is defined as

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)\coloneqq\left\{S_{0}+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S,G\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\ \middle|\ S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p},S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}\,\,\text{are SOS matrices}\right\}.

Next, we derive a Positivstellensatze for polynomial matrices with UQs, providing SOS-structured
characterizations for polynomial matrices that are PD over
\mathcal{X}
.

Assumption 3.1
.

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
is
Archimedean
, i.e.,
there is
C>0
such that
C-\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}^{1}(G,\nu)
.

Consider the
Carleman condition imposed on
\nu
which is
automatically satisfied when
\mathcal{Y}
is compact.

Assumption 3.2
.

The Borel measure
\nu
satisfies the multivariate Carleman condition

\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathcal{Y}}y_{j}^{2d}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\right)^{-\frac{1}{2d}}=\infty,\quad\forall j\in[m].

Proposition 3.1
.

[
16
, Proposition 3.2]

Suppose that
\nu
satisfies Assumption
3.2
.
Then, SOS polynomials are dense in the cone of nonnegative
functions in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},\nu)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\nu
satisfies Assumption
3.2
. Then

\mathcal{X}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid H({\boldsymbol{x}})\succeq 0,\ \forall H\in\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)\}.

(3)

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
, we only need to prove that

\mathcal{X}
contains the set on the right-hand side of the equation in (
3
).
Fix
{\boldsymbol{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
H({\boldsymbol{u}})\succeq 0
for all
H\in\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
.
Suppose on the contrary that
{\boldsymbol{u}}\not\in\mathcal{X}
,
i.e., there exists
{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\mathcal{Y}
such that
G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{w}})\not\succeq 0
. Then, there is a ball

\mathcal{O}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with a radius
\rho>0
around
{\boldsymbol{w}}
such that

G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\not\succeq 0
on
2\mathcal{O}
.
We may assume that there exists
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
and
\delta>0
such that

{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal}G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{v}}\leq-\delta
on
2\mathcal{O}
.
Define a continuous function
h({\boldsymbol{y}})
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
by
h({\boldsymbol{y}})=2\rho-\|{\boldsymbol{y}}-{\boldsymbol{w}}\|

for
{\boldsymbol{y}}\in 2\mathcal{O}
and
h({\boldsymbol{y}})=0
otherwise.
By Proposition
3.1
, there exists a sequence of SOS polynomials

\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k}
in
{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{y}}]
that converges to
h
in the
L^{2}
-norm.
Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal}G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{v}}\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})=\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal}G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{v}}h({\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Y}\cap 2\mathcal{O}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal}G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{v}}(2\rho-\|{\boldsymbol{y}}-{\boldsymbol{w}}\|)\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\leq\int_{\mathcal{Y}\cap\mathcal{O}}-\delta\rho\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})=-\delta\rho\nu(\mathcal{Y}\cap\mathcal{O})<0,

where the last inequality is due to the fact that
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)=\mathcal{Y}
and thus
\nu(\mathcal{Y}\cap\mathcal{O})>0
.
Note that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal}G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{v}}\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})=\frac{1}{p}\mbox{tr}\left(\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{y}})I_{p}\otimes{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal},G({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\right)\geq 0,

since
\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{y}})I_{p}\otimes{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\intercal}
is an SOS and
H({\boldsymbol{u}})\succeq 0
for all
H\in\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
.
A contradiction follows.
∎

Now, we present our main result concerning a Positivstellensatz for polynomial matrices with UQs.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
hold.
If
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
is PD on
\mathcal{X}
, then
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
.

Proof.

Since the quadratic module
\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)
is Archimedean and the equality in (
3
) holds,
the conclusion follows from the fundamental Positivstellensatz for matrix algebras of polynomials

[
44
, Theorem 10.25]
.
∎

Next, we derive a corollary of Theorem
3.1
,
which will be used in Section
4
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q}
is an SOS matrix in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
{\boldsymbol{y}}
.
Then
\int_{\mathcal{Y}}S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})
is an SOS matrix in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
.

Proof.

As
S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})
is an SOS matrix, there exists an
\ell\times q
polynomial matrix
T({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})

for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})=T({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})^{\intercal}T({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})
.
With
d=\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}T
, we could write
T({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})
as

T({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})=Q({\boldsymbol{y}})([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{q})\text{ with }Q({\boldsymbol{y}})=[Q_{1}({\boldsymbol{y}}),\ldots,Q_{|\mathbb{N}^{n}_{d}|}({\boldsymbol{y}})],\quad Q_{i}({\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{\ell\times q},

where
Q({\boldsymbol{y}})
is the vector of coefficient matrices of
T({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})
(considered as a polynomial
matrix in
{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{\ell\times q}
) with respect to
[{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Y}}S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})=\int_{\mathcal{Y}}([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{q})^{\intercal}(Q({\boldsymbol{y}})^{\intercal}Q({\boldsymbol{y}}))([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{q})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})=([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{q})^{\intercal}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}Q({\boldsymbol{y}})^{\intercal}Q({\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})([{\boldsymbol{x}}]_{d}\otimes I_{q})

As
\int_{\mathcal{Y}}Q({\boldsymbol{y}})^{\intercal}Q({\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})
is PSD,

\int_{\mathcal{Y}}S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})
is an SOS matrix in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{H}\coloneqq\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}\}
where each
H_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{r_{j}}
for some
r_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose that Assumption
3.2
holds and there exists
C>0
such that

C-\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}^{1}(G,\nu)+\mathcal{Q}^{1}(\mathbf{H})
.
If
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
is PD on
\mathcal{X}\cap\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\succeq 0,\ j\in[t]\}
,
then
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)+\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\succ 0
on

\mathcal{X}\cap\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid H_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\succeq 0,\ j\in[t]\}
if and only if

F({\boldsymbol{x}})\succ 0
on

\left\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,\middle|\,\widehat{G}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\coloneqq\mbox{diag}(G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),H_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}}),\ldots,H_{t}({\boldsymbol{x}}))\succeq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\right\}.

By Lemmas
2.2
and
3.1
, it is easy to see that

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\widehat{G},\nu)=\mathcal{Q}^{p}(G,\nu)+\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{H})
.
So, the conclusion follows from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.2.
A sparse Positivstellensatz for polynomial matrices with UQs

There are sparse Positivstellensätze for scalar polynomials being positive
on a basic semialgebraic set in the presence of correlative sparsity; see
[
11
,
23
]
where the set is defined by polynomial inequalities
and
[
20
]
where the set is defined PMIs.
Let
p=1
. Building on the ideas from
[
11
]
and
[
20
]
, we now prove a sparse Positivstellensatz for polynomial matrices with UQs.
Let
f\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
and

\widehat{\mathcal{X}}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0,\ \forall j\in[s],\ {\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\},

(4)

where each
G_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q_{j}}
,
q_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assumption 3.3

(correlative sparsity pattern)
.

Subsets
\{\mathcal{I}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[t]}
of
[n]
and subsets
\{\mathcal{J}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[t]}
of
[s]
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

The
running intersection property
holds for
\{\mathcal{I}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[t]}
, i.e.,

\text{for }\ell=2,\ldots,t,\ \exists k<\ell\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\ell}\cap\bigcup_{j<\ell}\mathcal{I}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k};

(ii)

For every
\ell\in[t]
and
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}
,
G_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}),{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q_{j}}
,
where
{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\ell}}
;

(iii)

f
decomposes as
f=f_{1}+\cdots+f_{t}
with each
f_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]
.

For each
\ell\in[t]
, let
\mathbf{G}^{\ell}\coloneqq\{G_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{G}^{\ell},\nu)

be the quadratic module generated by
\mathbf{G}^{\ell}
and
\nu
in
{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]^{p}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{Q}^{p}(\mathbf{G}^{\ell},\nu)\coloneqq\left\{S_{0}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S_{j},G_{j}\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\ \middle|\ S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]^{p},S_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}),{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq_{j}},j\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell},\text{ are SOS matrices}\right\}.

Using the correlative sparsity pattern and the Archimedean condition,
we are able to derive the following sparse Positivstellensatz.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{Y}
is compact, Assumption
3.3
holds for
f
and
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
,
and Assumption
3.1
holds for each
\mathcal{Q}^{1}(\mathbf{G}^{\ell},\nu)
,
\ell\in[t]
.
If
f>0
on
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
, then
f\in\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(\mathbf{G}^{\ell},\nu)
.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we need the following intermediary results.

Proposition 3.3
.

[
11
, Lemma 3]

Let
\{\mathcal{I}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[t]}
satisfy the running intersection property. For any
C>0
, if
f=f_{1}+\cdots+f_{t}
with
f_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]
satisfies

on

, then

for some

with

on
[-C,C]^{|\mathcal{I}_{\ell}|}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
is PD on
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
.
Then for any
C>0
, there exists
M>0
and
\bar{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

F({\boldsymbol{x}})-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle I_{p}\otimes\left(I_{q_{j}}-G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})/M\right)^{2k},G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\succ 0

(5)

on

for all

.

The proof of Proposition
3.4
is postponed to Section
6.1
.
Note that using Proposition
3.4
, we can provide a constructive proof of Theorem
3.1
assuming that
\mathcal{Y}
is compact (see Section

).

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By assumption, let
C>0
be such that
C-\|{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\|^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}^{1}(\mathbf{G}^{\ell},\nu)

holds for all
\ell\in[t]
. By Proposition
3.4
, there exists
M>0
and
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle\left(I_{q_{j}}-G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})/M\right)^{2k^{\prime}},G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})>0

on

. Note that for each

,

\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle\left(I_{q_{j}}-G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})/M\right)^{2k^{\prime}},G_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]

for some

. By Proposition
3.3
, there exist

,
\ell\in[t]
,

on

.

4.
Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices with UQs and without the Archimedean condition

The results in Section
3
are derived under the Archimedean condition on the quadratic
module generated by
(G,\nu)
, which requires
\mathcal{X}
to be bounded.
Many efforts have been made to provide SOS-structured
representations for polynomials that are PD on
a semialgebraic set without the Archimedean condition.
The goal of this section is to extend some of these well-known results to
the setting of polynomial matrices with UQs.

4.1.
A Positivstellensatz for the non-compact case

Let
\theta\coloneqq 1+\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2}
.
For every nonnegative polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
on a general basic semialgebraic set,
Putinar and Vasilescu
[
38
]
proved that for a given
\varepsilon>0
and
2d\geq\deg f
,
there exists a nonnegative integer
k
such that
\theta^{k}(f+\varepsilon\theta^{d})
belongs to the quadratic module associated with the basic semialgebraic set.
Using Jacobi’s technique
[
18
]
, Mai et al.
[
30
]

provided an alternative proof of Putinar and Vasilescu’s
result with an effective degree bound on polynomials involved in such certificates.

Building on Theorem
3.1
and similar techniques from
[
30
]
,
we next derive matrix-valued Positivstellensätz for the non-compact case,
incorporating universal quantifiers. In the homogeneous case, the result is stated as follows.
See Section
6.2
for its proof.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.2
holds,

F\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
and
G\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q}
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
of even degree, and

F({\boldsymbol{x}})\succ 0
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Then, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
SOS matrices
S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
,
S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}
that
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
\deg S_{0}=2N+\deg F
,
\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}S=2N+\deg F-\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}G
,
such that

\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2N}F({\boldsymbol{x}})=S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}).

For any
H=[H_{ij}]\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{r}
,
let
d_{H}\coloneqq\max\left\{\lfloor\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}(H_{ij})/2\rfloor+1:i,j\in[r]\right\}

and
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\coloneqq({\boldsymbol{x}},x_{n+1})
.
By applying Theorem
4.1
, we obtain its inhomogeneous counterpart, as given below.

Corollary 4.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.2
holds and

F({\boldsymbol{x}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
is PSD on
\mathcal{X}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
,
SOS matrices
S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
, and
S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}
with
\deg S_{0}\leq 2(N_{\varepsilon}+d_{F})
and
\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}S\leq 2(N_{\varepsilon}+d_{F}-d_{G})
, such that

\theta^{N_{\varepsilon}}(F({\boldsymbol{x}})+\varepsilon\theta^{d_{F}}I_{p})=S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}).

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{F}\coloneqq[x_{n+1}^{2d_{F}}F_{ij}({\boldsymbol{x}}/x_{n+1})]_{i,j\in[p]}\in{\mathbb{S}}[\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}]^{p}
,

\widetilde{G}\coloneqq[x_{n+1}^{2d_{G}}G_{ij}({\boldsymbol{x}}/x_{n+1},{\boldsymbol{y}})]_{i,j\in[q]}\in{\mathbb{S}}[\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q}
,
and consider

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\coloneqq\left\{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\middle|\widetilde{G}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\right\}.

We first prove that
\widetilde{F}+\varepsilon\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\|^{2d_{F}}I_{p}\succ 0

on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Fix a point
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}=({\boldsymbol{u}},u_{n+1})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

-

Case 1:
u_{n+1}\neq 0
. As
\widetilde{G}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}},{\boldsymbol{y}})=u_{n+1}^{2d_{G}}G({\boldsymbol{u}}/u_{n+1},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0
,
we have
G({\boldsymbol{u}}/u_{n+1},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0
for all
{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}
, which implies
{\boldsymbol{u}}/u_{n+1}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Hence,
\widetilde{F}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}})=u_{n+1}^{2d_{F}}F({\boldsymbol{u}}/x_{n+1})\succeq 0
. Since

\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\|^{2}\neq 0
,

\widetilde{F}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}})+\varepsilon\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\|^{2d_{F}}I_{p}\succ 0
.

-

Case 2:
u_{n+1}=0
. By the definition of
d_{F}
,
x_{n+1}
divides
\widetilde{F}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})
.
Thus,
\widetilde{F}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}})=0
. Since
\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\|^{2}\neq 0
,

\widetilde{F}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}})+\varepsilon\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\|^{2d_{F}}I_{p}\succ 0
.

Applying Theorem
4.1
to the polynomial matrices
\tilde{F}+\varepsilon\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\|^{2d_{F}}I_{p}

and
\widetilde{G}
, we obtain
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
and SOS matrices
\widetilde{S}_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
,
\widetilde{S}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}
, which
are homogeneous in
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
\deg\widetilde{S}_{0}=2N_{\varepsilon}+2d_{F}
,

\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\widetilde{S}=2N_{\varepsilon}+2d_{F}-2d_{G}
, such that

\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\|^{2N_{\varepsilon}}(\widetilde{F}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\varepsilon\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\|^{2d_{F}}I_{p})=\widetilde{S}_{0}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle\widetilde{S}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),\widetilde{G}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}).

(6)

Then, letting
x_{n+1}=1
in (
6
), we achieve the desired conclusion.
∎

The proof is postponed to Section

5.
Applications to robust PMI constrained optimization

Verifying PMIs over a prescribed set has a wide range of applications in many fields.
For example, many control problems for systems of ordinary differential equations can be formulated as convex optimization problems with PMI constraints that must be satisfied over a specified portion of the state space
[
13
,
15
,
17
,
37
,
47
]
.
These problems are typically formulated as follows:

\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}}\ {\boldsymbol{c}}^{\intercal}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\ \text{ s.t. }\ \Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\coloneqq\Lambda_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Lambda_{i}({\boldsymbol{x}})\gamma_{i}\succeq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid H({\boldsymbol{x}})\succeq 0\},

where
{\boldsymbol{c}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
,
\Lambda_{0},\ldots,\Lambda_{r}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p}
and
H\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{q}
.

However, due to estimation errors or lack of information,
data from real-world problems often involve uncertainty.
Therefore, ensuring the robustness of PMIs over a prescribed set under uncertainty is a critical issue, which could be formulated as the following robust optimization problem:

\tau^{\star}\coloneqq\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}}\ {\boldsymbol{c}}^{\intercal}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\ \text{ s.t. }\ \Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\succeq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\succeq 0,\ \forall{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\},

(8)

where

G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{q}
and
\mathcal{Y}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is closed.

Consequently, Positivstellensätze for polynomial matrices with UQs
developed in this paper serve as a powerful mathematical tool for addressing this issue.
Indeed, by leveraging the SOS-structured certificates provided by Positivstellensätze for the positive definiteness of
\Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})
over
\mathcal{X}
, we are able to establish hierarchies of SDP relaxations for the problem (
8
).

5.1.
The compact case

If
\mathcal{X}
is compact, then one could apply Theorem
3.1
to construct a hierarchy of SDP relaxations for (
8
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tau(k)\coloneqq\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},S_{0},S}&\ {\boldsymbol{c}}^{\intercal}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\\
\text{s.t.}&\ \Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})=S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}),\\
&\ S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p},S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}\,\,\text{are SOS matrices},\\
&\ \deg S_{0}\leq 2k,\ \deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}S\leq 2(k-\lceil\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}G/2\rceil),\ \deg_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}S\leq 2(k-\lceil\deg_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}G/2\rceil).\end{aligned}\right.

(9)

It is clear that
\{\tau(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of non-increasing upper bounds on
\tau^{\star}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions

3.1
and
3.2
hold and there exists a point
\bar{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}

such that
\Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},\bar{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}})\succ 0
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Then we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau(k)=\tau^{\star}
.

Proof.

Note that for any feasible point
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\prime}
of (
8
) and any
\varepsilon>0
, the point
(1-\varepsilon){\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\prime}+\varepsilon\bar{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}
satisfies
\Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},(1-\varepsilon){\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\prime}+\varepsilon\bar{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}})\succ 0
on
\mathcal{X}
. Then, the conclusion follows by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Given that the SDP problem
derived from (
9
) scales rapidly and becomes computationally prohibitive as
k
increases,
we revise it by imposing separate degree constraints on
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
{\boldsymbol{y}}
, leading to the following problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tau(k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}},k_{{\boldsymbol{y}}})\coloneqq\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},S_{0},S}&\ {\boldsymbol{c}}^{\intercal}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\\
\text{s.t.}&\ \Lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})=S_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left\langle S({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}),G({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right\rangle_{p}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}}),\\
&\ S_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]^{p},S\in{\mathbb{S}}[{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}]^{pq}\,\,\text{are SOS matrices},\\
&\ \deg S_{0}\leq 2k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}},\ \deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}S\leq 2(k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\lceil\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}G/2\rceil),\ \deg_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}S\leq 2(k_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}-\lceil\deg_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}G/2\rceil).\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

If
\mathcal{Y}
is low-dimensional, we can maintain a small
k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}
while increasing
k_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}
separately;
this allows us to solve (
10
) in reasonable time and obtain a nontrivial upper bound

\tau(k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}},k_{{\boldsymbol{y}}})
of
\tau^{\star}
.

In practice, the SDP relaxation (
10
) requires an efficient evaluation of the integral

\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\boldsymbol{y}}^{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\mathrm{d}\nu({\boldsymbol{y}})
. If
\nu
is chosen to be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{Y}
(as in the
following illustrating examples), this integral is available in closed form or by numerical methods
whenever
\mathcal{Y}
is a simple domain; examples include hypercubes, spheres
[
9
]
,
balls
[
9
]
, and polytopes
[
2
]
.
All numerical experiments in the sequel were carried out on a PC with
an 8-Core Intel i7 3.8GHz CPU and 8G RAM, using the SDP solver MOSEK

and
a customized version of the MATLAB optimization toolbox YALMIP

(11)

For any
k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\geq\lceil\deg_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}G_{j}/2\rceil

\tau(k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}},k_{{\boldsymbol{y}}})

\{\tau(k,k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

\tau(\infty,\infty)\coloneqq\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau(k,k)

5.1

3.1
and

Provided the presence of the correlative sparsity pattern stated in

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tau^{\mbox{\tiny cs}}(k_{{\boldsymbol{x}}},k_{{\boldsymbol{y}}})\coloneqq\sup_{\gamma,\sigma_{0},S_{j}}&\ \gamma\\

7.
Conclusions

We extend various classical Positivstellensätze to provide SOS-structured characterizations
for polynomial matrices that are positive (semi)definite over a
semialgebraic set defined by a PMI with UQs.
Under the Archimedean condition, we first present a matrix-valued Positivstellensatz
incorporating universal quantifiers,
along with a sparse version for scalar-valued polynomial objectives, leveraging the correlative sparsity patterns.
We also derive two generalized Positivstellensätze without assuming the Archimedean condition.
These results significantly extend the existing work on Positivstellensätze, and some of them remain novel and intriguing even in the absence of UQs.
Our results are valuable in ensuring the robustness of PMIs over a prescribed set with uncertainty, allowing potential applications in areas such as optimal control, systems theory, and certifying stability.

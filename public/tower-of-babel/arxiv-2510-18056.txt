Wiener–Wintner points for topological dynamical systems
Daniel Lenz, Nicolae Strungaru

Abstract
. We consider measurable and topological dynamical systems over locally compact abelian groups. Our main observation relates convergence of Wiener-Wintner type averages to eigenvalues of the dynamical system in question. As a consequence we infer existence of Fourier–Bohr coefficients for all characters for a set of points satisfying a specific genericity condition. In the topological case this leads naturally to the concept of what we call Wiener–Wintner point and we present a thorough study of such points. In particular we show that they have full measure in the ergodic case, and we relate them to Besicovitch almost periodicity. For dynamical systems of translation bounded measures, which are the crucial models in aperiodic order, our results give that the Wiener–Wintner points are exactly the points allowing for a diffraction theory with the consistent phase property.



1.
Introduction

We consider Wiener–Wintner type averages for (continuous) functions on
dynamical systems over locally compact Abelian groups. In the
simplest situation we deal with a compact metric space
X
, together with a
homeomorphism
T:X\longrightarrow X
and a
T
-invariant
probability measure
\mathsf{m}
, and we study points
x\in X
for which the
limits

(1.1)

\lim_{N}\frac{1}{2N+1}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}f(T^{n}x)z^{n}\,,

exist for all continuous
f:X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
and a given fixed

z\in\mathbb{T}
. Here,
\mathbb{T}
denotes the multiplicative group

\mathbb{T}:=\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mbox{ with }|w|=1\}\,.

Our basic observation relates existence of these limits (for all
f\in C(X)
) to existence of an eigenfunction to
z
(see Lemma

3.5
for a precise statement in a substantially more
general situation). This allows us to establish vanishing (and, in
particular, existence) of the limit for compact subsets of
\mathbb{T}
not
meeting any eigenvalue for all generic points
x\in X
(Proposition

3.7
). In the uniquely ergodic case this also
allows us to infer uniform (in
x\in X
) vanishing of the limit for
compact sets in
\mathbb{T}
outside of eigenvalues, thereby generalizing
results of Lenz
[
14
]
, which in turn generalize results of Assani

[
2
]
(see
[
20
,
22
]
as well). All of this is
discussed in Section
4
.

Then, in Section
5
, we study those points
where the above limits exist for all continuous
f\in C(X)
and all
z\in\mathbb{T}
. We call these Fourier–Bohr points. By our basic observation any
Fourier–Bohr point comes with a family of eigenfunctions. It is not
clear that this family is an orthonormal basis of the space of
eigenfunctions. If this is the case, we call the corresponding point a
Wiener–Wintner point. The study of Wiener–Wintner points is the main
thrust of the second half of the article. We show that existence of
such points implies ergodicity and that ergodicity implies that almost
all points are Wiener–Wintner points. Moreover, we show that for dynamical systems with pure
point spectrum, the
Wiener–Wintner points are exactly the generic points that are Besicovitch almost
periodic. This ties in with recent work of Kasjan and Keller

[
11
]
focusing on the generic Besicovitch almost periodic points.

Our interest in generic Besicovitch almost periodic points comes from
aperiodic order also known as mathematical theory of quasicrystals,
see
[
3
]
. A key feature of aperiodic order is pure point
diffraction. In the mathematical modelling this leads to dynamical
systems with discrete spectrum. The Besicovitch almost periodic points
are then exactly the points allowing for a convincing diffraction
theory
[
15
,
16
]
. This is discussed in the last section of the article.

Our results in the topological case are an applications of a more
general approach working for measurable dynamical systems as
developed in Section
3
after the general introduction to
dynamical systems in Section
2
. This approach is
centered around two concepts we introduce. These are the concept of
genericity of a point for a subspace and the concept of core for a
subspace. Our results in the measurable case give the classical Wiener–Wintner
theorem as a by product. The application to the topological case then
comes about by choosing
C(X)
as the subspace in question.

The results of this article are used in Chapter 8 of the treatise
[
15
]
.

2.
Basics on measurable dynamical systems

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-compact, locally compact Abelian
group
G
(LCAG). The composition
on
G
itself is written additively. We fix a Haar measure on
G
. The Haar measure
of a measurable subset
A\subseteq G
is denoted by
|A|
and the
integral of an integrable function
f
on
G
by
\int_{G}f(t)\,\mbox{\rm d}t
.

Whenever the measurable space
(X,\Sigma)
is equipped with a measurable action

\alpha:G\times X\longrightarrow X,\qquad(t,x)\mapsto\alpha_{t}(x)\,,

we call
(X,\Sigma,G)
a
measurable dynamical system (over the
space
X
)
and write
t.x

instead of
\alpha_{t}(x)
for
t\in G
and
x\in X
.
The
orbit
of
x
under the action is given by

\mbox{O}(x):=\{t.x:t\in G\}.

For a function
f:X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in X
we then define the
coding
of
f
on the orbit of
x
to be the function

f_{x}:G\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

f_{x}(t):=f(t.x)\,.

For a function
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
and some
t\in G
, we denote by
\tau_{t}
the translation

(\tau_{t}f)(x):=f(x-t)\,.

Similarly, for
g:X\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
t\in G
we denote by

\tau_{t}g
the translation

(\tau_{t}f)(x):=g(t.x)\,.

It is easy to see that for all
g:X\to{\mathbb{C}}
, all
x\in X
and all
t\in G
we have

\tau_{t}(g_{x})=(\tau_{-t}g)_{x}\,.

A measure
\mathsf{m}
on
(X,\Sigma)
is called
invariant
if
\mathsf{m}(\alpha_{t}A)=\mathsf{m}(A)
holds for all
A\in\sigma
and all
t\in G
.

Given a
G
-invariant measure
\mathsf{m}
on
(X,\Sigma)
, we refer to the triple
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
as measurable dynamical system.
Invariance of
\mathsf{m}
implies

\int_{X}f(t.x)\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(x)=\int_{X}f(y)\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(y)

for all
t\in G
and all measurable positive
f
on
X
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
we denote by
\mathcal{L}^{p}(X,m)
the set of measurable
f:X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
with

\int_{X}|f(x)|^{p}\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(x)<\infty\,.

Identifying functions which agree almost everywhere we then arrive at the Banach spaces
L^{p}(X,\mathsf{m})
. We will mostly deal with
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
, which is a Hilbert space with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{X}f(x)\cdot\overline{g(x)}\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(x)\,,

and associated norm

\|f\|_{2}:=\langle f,f\rangle^{1/2}\,,

where we followed the custom and wrote
f,g
for the classes represented by the functions
f
and
g
.

In our considerations below we will have to be more careful at certain places and restrict attention to elements of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
rather than of
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
as we are interested in specific sets of points, where suitable statements hold (or do not hold).

Any invariant measure
\mathsf{m}
on
X
gives rise to a family of unitary operators
U_{t},t\in G,
on the space
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
via

\displaystyle U_{t}:L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})\longrightarrow L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})

\displaystyle(U_{t}f)(x)=f(t.x)\,,

for
t\in G
. As
\alpha
is an action,
(U_{t})_{t\in G}
form a unitary representation i.e.

\displaystyle U_{0}

\displaystyle=\mbox{Id}\qquad\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle U_{t+s}

\displaystyle=U_{t}U_{s}\qquad\forall t,s\in G\,.

A function
f
on
X
is
G
-invariant
(or simply
invariant
) if for any
t\in G
the functions
\tau_{t}f
and
f
agree almost everywhere. The dynamical system
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
is
ergodic
if any invariant measurable complex valued function is constant almost everywhere.

Recall next that a
Følner sequence

(A_{n})
in
G
, is a sequence of open relatively compact subsets
A_{n}
of
G
with the property that for all
t\in G
we have

\lim_{n}\frac{|(A_{n}\triangle(t+A_{n}))|}{|A_{n}|}=0\,.

A Følner sequence exists in a LCAG
G
if and only if
G
is
\sigma
-compact
[
23
, Prop. B6]
. For this reason, we will always assume that
G
is
\sigma
-compact.
A Følner sequence
{\mathcal{A}}
is
tempered
if there exists a
C>0
with

\left|\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(B_{n}-B_{k})\right|\leq C|B_{n}|\,,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Some basic properties of tempered Følner sequences to be used subsequently are discussed in the Appendix
A
.
We have the following pointwise ergodic theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Pointwise ergodic theorem)
.

[
17
, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 1.4]
Let
G
be any
\sigma
-compact LCAG. If
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
is any ergodic dynamical system and
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{n}\}
is any tempered Følner sequence then the pointwise ergodic theorem holds, i.e. for each
f\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}(X,\mathsf{m})

there exists a set
X_{0}\subseteq X
with
\mathsf{m}(X_{0})=1
such that for all
x\in X_{0}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\,\mbox{\rm d}t=\int_{X}f(y)\,\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(y)\,.

Moreover, any Følner sequence contains a subsequence, which is tempered, and along which accordingly the pointwise ergodic theorem holds.

The dual group of
G
, i.e. the set of all continuous group homomorphisms from
G
to the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
is denoted by
\widehat{G}
.
It is a group with addition defined by pointwise multiplication of characters, and it becomes a LCAG under a certain topology (see
[
21
, Sect 1.2.6]
for details).

Whenever
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
is a dynamical system we call an
f\in L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
with
f\neq 0
an
eigenfunction
to the
eigenvalue

\chi:G\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
if

f(t\cdot)=\chi(t)f

holds for all
t\in G
. It is not hard to see that
\chi
must then belong to
\widehat{G}
. The set of all eigenfunctions to a given eigenvalue
\chi
together with the constant function
0
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
called the
eigenspace
to
\chi
.
We call

\mathbb{S}:=\{\chi\in\widehat{G}:\chi\mbox{ is an eigenvalue for }(X,G,\mathsf{m})\}\ ,

the
point spectrum
of
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
. Moreover, we write
L(X,\mathsf{m})_{\mathsf{pp}}
for the closure in
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
of the linear span of the eigenfunctions.

For a given Følner sequence
(A_{n})
,
\chi\in\widehat{G}
and a measurable function
h:G\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
we define

a_{\chi}^{A_{n}}(h):=\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}h(t)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\mbox{ (provided the integral exists)}

and we define

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(h):=\lim_{n}a_{\chi}^{A_{n}}(h)\mbox{ (provided the limit exists)}\,.

Whenever the limit
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(h)
exists, it is called the
Fourier–Bohr coefficient
of
h
at
\chi
.

For a given
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(X,\mathsf{m})
, a Følner sequence
(A_{n})
, some
\chi\in\widehat{G}
and
x\in X
we can then consider

a_{\chi}^{A_{n}}(f_{x})
and
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(f_{x})
(provided these expressions are defined) and we call

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f_{x})=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

the
Fourier–Bohr coefficient
of
f
at
\chi
in
x\in X
.

The pointwise ergodic theorem can then be understood to deal with existence of Fourier–Bohr coefficients for
\chi=1
. The case of general
\chi
is the topic of this article. Here, we already note that, by von Neumann ergodic theorem,
the averages

x\mapsto\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

converge to
P_{\chi}f
in
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
, where
P_{\chi}
is the projection onto the eigenspace to
\chi
.

3.
The main observation

In this section we present our main observation. We start by introducing the following definitions.

Definition 3.1

(
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic points)
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be a measurable dynamical system and
\mathcal{A}
a Følner sequence on
G
. Let
E
be a subset of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
and
G^{\prime}
a subgroup of
G
. An element
x\in X
is called
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic (with respect to
\mathcal{A}
) if the following holds:

•

\int_{X}f(y)\overline{g(y)}\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(y)=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\overline{g(t.x)}\mbox{\rm d}t

holds for all
f,g\in E
; and

•

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}\triangle(A_{n}+s)}|f(t.x)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t=0
for each
f\in E
and all
s\in G^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If all elements in
E
are bounded functions, then for any subgroup
G^{\prime}\leq G
an element
x\in X
is
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic if and only if

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{g(t.x)}\mbox{\rm d}t\,=\,\int_{X}f(y)\cdot\overline{g(y)}\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(y)\,,

holds for all
f,g\in E
.

In the situation of the definition an
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic point is also generic for the linear span of
E
. Thus, we can always assume without loss of generality that
E
is a subspace when dealing with generic points. We also note that genericity is preserved under uniform limits. Here, the function
f
on
X
is the uniform limit of the sequence of functions
(f_{n})
if

\|f-f_{n}\|_{\infty}\to 0,n\to\infty,

with
\|g\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in X}|g(x)|
.
Thus, we can also always assume that
E
is closed under uniform limits.

Definition 3.3

(Core)
.

Let
E
be a subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
. A subset
F\subseteq E
is called a
core for
E
if any element of
E
is a uniform limit of a sequence in the linear span of
F
.

We will be particularly interested in subspaces with a countable core. The reason is that for such subspaces we can show under an ergodicity assumption that generic points have full measure.
Specifically, the following is true.

Proposition 3.4

(Existence of generic elements)
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be a measurable dynamical system and let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{n}\}
be a Følner sequence.

Let
E
be a subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
with a countable core and let
G^{\prime}
be a countable subgroup of
G
. Then, the following holds:

(a)

If
\mathsf{m}
is ergodic and
{\mathcal{A}}
is tempered then the set of generic elements for
(E,G^{\prime})
has full measure.

(b)

If
\mathsf{m}
is ergodic, then there exists a subsequence
{\mathcal{B}}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that the set of generic elements for
(\mathsf{m},{\mathcal{B}})
has full measure.

Proof.

(a)

Let
D
be a countable core of
E
. By the pointwise ergodic theorem, for each
f,g\in D
there exists some set

X_{f,g}\subseteq X
of full measure such that

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\overline{g(t.x)}\mbox{\rm d}t=\langle f,g\rangle

holds for
f
and
g
and all
x\in X_{f,g}
.

By Lemma
A.2
, for each
s\in G
, the sequences
(B_{n})
and
(C_{n})
with

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=A_{n}\cap(s+A_{n})

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=A_{n}\cup(s+A_{n})

are tempered Følner sequences. Therefore, for each
f\in D
there exists some
X_{f,s}\subseteq X
of full measure such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\int_{B_{n}}|f(t.x)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t=\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|C_{n}|}\int_{C_{n}}|f(t.x)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t=\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,,

holds for
f
and all
x\in X_{f,s}
.
It follows that the set

Y:=\left(\bigcap_{f,g\in D}X_{f,g}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{f\in D,s\in G^{\prime}}X_{f,s}\right)

has full measure. For
f,g\in D,s\in G^{\prime}
and
x\in Y
we then infer

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\overline{g(t.x)}\mbox{\rm d}t=\langle f,g\rangle

and with to
A_{n}\triangle(A_{n}+s)=C_{n}\setminus B_{n}
we also conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}\triangle(A_{n}+s)}|f(t.x)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|C_{n}|}\int_{C_{n}}|f(t.x)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t-\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\int_{B_{n}}|f(t.x)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{2}^{2}-\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=0\,.

This proves (a).

(b)
This follows from (a) and Theorem
2.1
.
∎

The following lemma is the main ingredient for our subsequent considerations.

Lemma 3.5

(Main observation: forcing eigenvalues)
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be a measurable dynamical system,
{\mathcal{A}}
a Følner sequence and
G^{\prime}
a dense subgroup of
G
.

Let
E\subset\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
be a
G^{\prime}
-invariant subspace and let
x\in X
be
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic. Let
\bar{E}
be the closure of
E
in
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
.

Let
(\chi_{n})
be a sequence in
\widehat{G}
converging pointwise to
\chi\in\widehat{G}
such that

F(g):=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

exists for all
g
in
E
. Then, there exists a unique
f_{\chi}\in\bar{E}
satisfying

•

\|f_{\chi}\|_{2}\leq 1
;

•

U_{t}f_{\chi}=\chi(t)f_{\chi}\,
for all
t\in G
.

Specifically,
f_{\chi}=0
if
F(g)=0
for all
g\in E
and
f_{\chi}
is an eigenfunction to
\chi
otherwise.

Proof.

Clearly, the map

F
is linear. Moreover, for
g\in E
and all
n
, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x)\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\left|g(t.x)\right|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t}\sqrt{\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\left|\chi_{n}(t)\right|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\left|g(t.x)\right|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle(x\mbox{ generic })

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\|g\|_{2}.

Therefore, we find

|F(g)|=\lim_{n}\left|\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x)\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\right|\leq\|g\|_{2}\ .

This means that
F
is a linear bounded operator on
E
. Therefore,
F
can be extended to a bounded operator
F:\overline{E}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. By the Riesz- representation theorem, there exists some
f_{\chi}\in\overline{E}
such that, for all
g\in E
we have

F(g)=\langle g,f_{\chi}\rangle\ .

Since
\|F\|\leq 1
we get
\|f_{\chi}\|_{2}\leq 1
.
Moreover, for all
g\in E
and
s\in G^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\langle g,U_{s}f_{\chi}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U_{-s}g,f_{\chi}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(U_{-s}g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\tau_{-s}g(t.x)\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\overline{\chi(s)}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g((t-s).x)\overline{\chi_{n}(t-s)}\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\overline{\chi(s)}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}-s}g(t.x)\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle(\mbox{$x$ generic})\;\>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\overline{\chi(s)}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x)\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\chi(s)}F(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle g,\chi(s)f_{\chi}\rangle.

This easily gives

U_{s}f_{\chi}=\chi(s)f_{\chi}

for all
s\in G^{\prime}
and then also for all
s\in G
(by continuity and denseness). Clearly,
f_{\chi}\neq 0
if and only if there exists
g\in E
with
a_{g}\neq 0
and the last statement follows.
∎

Next, we discuss the vanishing of Fourier–Bohr coefficients. Let us first introduce the following definition:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
K\subset\widehat{G}
and
E\subset\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
be given. We say that
E
is
perpendicular
to
K
, and write
E\perp K
, if any element of
E
is perpendicular to any eigenfunction to an eigenvalue from
K
.

Proposition 3.7

(Vanishing result)
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be a measurable dynamical system, let
{\mathcal{A}}
a Følner sequence and
G^{\prime}
a dense subgroup of
G
. Let
K\subset\widehat{G}
be compact.

Let
E
be
G^{\prime}
invariant subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(X)
with a countable core and
E\perp K
. Then,

\lim_{n}\sup_{\chi\in K}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\left|\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\right|=0\,,

for any
f\in E
and any
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic point
x\in X
.

Proof.

Let us assume that this does not happen. Then, there exists some
f\in E
, a
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic point
x\in X
, a subsequence
(k_{n})
in the natural numbers, some
c>0
and
\chi_{n}\in K
such that

\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}|\int_{A_{k_{n}}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t|\geq c\,.

Since
K
is compact, without loss of generality we can assume
\chi_{n}\to\chi\in K
.

Next, let
D\subseteq E
be a countable core. Then, for each
g\in D\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(X)
the sequence

\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}g(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

is bounded. As
D
is countable, via a standard diagonalisation argument, we can find a subsequence
(l_{n})
such that the following limit exists for all
g\in D

(3.1)

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{k_{l_{n}}}|}\int_{A_{k_{l_{n}}}}g(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{l_{n}}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\,.

Since
D
is a core in
E
, the limit in (
3.1
) then exists for all
g\in E
.

Moreover,

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{k_{l_{n}}}|}\int_{A_{k_{l_{n}}}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{l_{n}}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\neq 0\,.

We can apply the previous lemma to conclude that there exists an eigenfunction to an eigenvalue from
K
in
\overline{E}
. On another hand, by
E\perp K
we immediately get
\overline{E}\perp K
, a contradiction.
∎

Applying Proposition
3.7
with
K=\{\chi\}
we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 3.8

(Vanishing of Fourier–Bohr coefficient of non-eigenvalues for generic points)
.

Let
(X,G,m)
be a measurable dynamical system and
{\mathcal{A}}
a Følner sequence and
G^{\prime}
a dense subgroup of
G
. Let
\chi\in\widehat{G}
not be an eigenvalue. Let
E
be
G^{\prime}
-invariant with a countable core. Then,

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=0

holds for any
(E,G^{\prime})
-generic point
x\in X
and any
f\in E
. ∎

We can now prove the following result:

Corollary 3.9

(Vanishing of Fourier–Bohr coefficients on countable sets)
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be an ergodic dynamical system with second countable
G
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{n}\}
be a tempered Følner sequence.

Let
D\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
be a countable set. Then, there exists a set
X_{0}\subseteq X
of full measure with

\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=0

for all
f\in D
,
x\in X_{0}
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}\setminus\mathbb{S}
.

Proof.

As
G
is second countable it possesses a countable dense subgroup
G^{\prime}
.

Then,

D^{\prime}:=\{\tau_{t}f:f\in D,t\in G^{\prime}\}

is countable. Let
E
be the linear span of
D^{\prime}
. Then,
E
is a subspace with a countable core that is
G^{\prime}
-invariant.

By Proposition
3.4
we infer that there exists a set
X_{0}
of full measure of
(E,G)
-generic points.

Now, the statement follows from the previous corollary.
∎

A consequence of the previous corollary is the following.

Theorem 3.10

(Wiener–Wintner)
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be an ergodic measurable dynamical system with separable
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
and second countable
G
and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a tempered Følner sequence.

Let
E\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
be any subspace with countable core. Then, there exists a set
X_{0}\subseteq X
of full measure so that the following holds:

•

The Fourier–Bohr coefficient
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(f_{x})
exists for all
f\in E,x\in X_{0}
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.

•

The Fourier–Bohr coefficient
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(f_{x})
is zero for all
f\in E,x\in X_{0}
and all
\chi\notin\mathbb{S}
.

Proof.

Let
D\subseteq E
be a countable core.

By Corollary
3.9
, we can find a subset
X_{1}\subseteq X
of full measure so that the second bullet point holds for all
f\in D,x\in X_{1}
and
\chi\notin\mathbb{S}
.

Next, for each
\chi\in\mathbb{S}
we can pick an eigenfunction
f_{\chi}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(X)
. As
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
is separable, the set
\mathbb{S}
is countable. Moreover, as
\mathsf{m}
is ergodic, each eigenspace is 1-dimensional. Therefore,

\{f_{\chi}:\chi\in\mathbb{S}\}

is a basis for
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})_{\mathsf{pp}}
.

Hence, there exists a set
X_{2}\subseteq X
of full measure so that the pointwise ergodic theorem holds for all
x\in X_{2}
and each element from
\{hf_{\chi}:h\in D,\chi\in\mathbb{S}\}
.

The set
X_{0}:=X_{1}\cap X_{2}
satisfies the result.
∎

4.
The topological situation:
E=C(X)

Let us recall that a
topological dynamical system

(X,G)
consists of a compact space
X
together with a continuous action
\alpha:G\times X\longrightarrow X
of
G
on
X
.
Clearly, such an action is measurable with respect to the
\Sigma
-algebra of Borel sets. So, we are within the setting of the previous section.

We denote the set of continuous complex valued functions on a compact topological space
X
by
C(X)
and equip it with the supremum norm

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{y\in Y}|f(y)|\,.

The considerations of the previous section can be applied with
E=C(X)
. Moreover, we can then formulate and prove uniform (in
x\in X
) results.

Note here that
E=C(X)
is a natural
G
-invariant set for which generic points may be considered. As the functions in
C(X)
are bounded the vanishing requirement in the genericity definition is automatically satisfied. Moreover, as the constant function
g=1
belongs to
C(X)
the genericity definition in the previous section agrees with the one usually given in the literature.

Specifically, whenever a topological dynamical system
(X,G,)
, a Følner sequence

(A_{n})
are given, and a
G
-invariant measure
\mathsf{m}
are given, a point
x\in X
is easily be seen to be
(C(X),G)
-generic
with respect to the Følner sequence
(A_{n})
if and only if

(4.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\,\mbox{\rm d}t=\int_{X}f(y)\,\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(y)

holds for all continuous functions
f\in C(X)
. We refer to such points a
generic for
(\mathsf{m},\mathcal{A})
or, if
\mathsf{m}
and
\mathcal{A}
are clear from the context, we just speak about
generic
points.

While the existence of generic points in general is not clear, when
\mathsf{m}
is ergodic the set of generic points along tempered Følner sequence has full measure by Proposition
3.4
. In particular, given any
ergodic measure
\mathsf{m}
and any Følner sequence
{\mathcal{A}}
, there exists a subsequence
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that the set of generic points for
(\mathsf{m},\mathcal{A})
has full measure in
X
.

Existence of a countable core was crucial for certain application in the last section. In the topological situation with
E=C(X)
, this is ensured by metrizability of
X
. Indeed, as metrizability of
X
is equivalent to
C(X)
having a countable dense subset (with respect to the supremum norm) we immediately infer the following statement.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,G)
be a topological dynamical system with metrizable
X
. Then, there exists a countable set
D\subseteq C(X)
such that
D
is a countable core for all
G
-invariant probability measures
\mathsf{m}
on
X
. ∎

Remark 4.2
.

Even if
X
is not metrizable, it is still possible that
C(X)
has a countable core for specific measures
\mathsf{m}
. This would hold for example if
\mathsf{m}
is supported on a finite orbit.

Given this discussion, the results of the previous sections can all be applied with
E=C(X)
and
G^{\prime}=G
(and the additional assumption of metrizability of
X
if necessary). We refrain from spelling this out here but rather only state the following immediate consequence of Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
3.7
.

Corollary 4.3

(Vanishing of Fourier–Bohr in generic points)
.

Let
(X,G)
be a topological dynamical system with metrizable
X
. Let
\mathsf{m}
be a
G
-invariant measure and let
\mathcal{A}
be a Følner sequence.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\widehat{G}\setminus\mathbb{S}
. Then,

\lim_{n}\sup_{\chi\in K}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}|\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t|=0

for any
f\in C(X)
and any generic point
x\in X
. ∎

Remark 4.4
.

In the case of metrizable
X
and an ergodic measure
\mathsf{m}
it is a rather direct consequence of the classical Wiener–Wintner theorem that for a set of full measure we have vanishing of Fourier Bohr coefficients for all
f\in C(X)
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}\backslash\mathbb{S}
. However, to the best of our knowledge it is a new result that this set of points contains the generic points.

We now turn to a strengthening of the results of the previous section. This strengthening concerns a uniform (in
x\in X
) version of the results there. The key is the following topological variant of the basic observation.

Proposition 4.5

(Topological variant of the basic observation)
.

Let
(X,G)
be topological dynamical system and
{\mathcal{A}}
a Følner sequence. Assume that
X
is metrizable.

Let
x_{n}\in X
and
\chi_{n}\in\widehat{G}
, be so that

\lim_{n}\chi_{n}=\chi\in\widehat{G}\,.

Then, there exists a subsequence
{\mathcal{B}}=(B_{n})_{n}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, a
G
-invariant probability measure
\mathsf{m}
on
X
and some
f_{\chi}\in L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
with the following properties:

(a)

For all
t\in G
we have

U_{t}f_{\chi}=\chi(t)f_{\chi}\,.

(b)

For all
f\in C(X)
we have

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\int_{B_{n}}f(t.x_{n})\mbox{\rm d}t=\int_{X}f(y)\mbox{\rm d}\mathsf{m}(y)\,.

(c)

For all
f\in C(X)
we have

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\int_{B_{n}}f(t.x_{n})\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=\langle f,f_{\chi}\rangle\,.

Proof.

Let
D
be a countable dense subset of
C(X)
. Then, for any Følner sequence
F_{n}
and each
g\in D
, both sequences

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x_{n})\mbox{\rm d}t\qquad\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x_{n})\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

are bounded by
\|g\|_{\infty}
. Therefore,
\{A_{n}\}
has subsequences for which these sequences converge.
Via a standard diagonalisation argument, we can then find a subsequence
{\mathcal{B}}=\{A_{k_{n}}\}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that for all
g\in D
we have

•

the sequence
\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}g(t.x_{k_{n}})\mbox{\rm d}t
converges to some number
L(g)
and

•

the sequence
\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}g(t.x_{k_{n}})\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t
converges to some
a(g)
.

Now, clearly the set of
g\in C(X)
for which both these sequences converge is closed in
C(X)
.

Thus, we obtain that the sequence

\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}f(t.x_{k_{n}})\mbox{\rm d}t

converges to some number
L(f)
for all
f\in C(X)
and the sequence

\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}f(t.x_{k_{n}})\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

converges to some number
a(f)
for all
f\in C(X)
.

It is clear that for all
f\in C(X)
with
f\geq 0
we have
L(f)\geq 0
and that

L(1_{X})=1\,.

Thus, by the Riesz- representation theorem, there exists a Radon probability measure
\mathsf{m}
on
X
so that

L(f)=\int_{X}f(y)\mbox{\rm d}y\qquad\forall f\in C(X)\,.

The Følner property gives that
\mathsf{m}
is
G
-invariant. This proves (b).

Next, a standard application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (see proof of the lemma for the basic observation) gives

|a(f)|\leq\|f\|_{2}\,.

It follows that
a
is a linear bounded map on
C(X)\subset L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
, and hence it can be extended to a bounded linear map

a:L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})\to{\mathbb{C}}\,.

Therefore, by Riesz- lemma, we can find an
f_{\chi}\in L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
such that, for all
f\in C(X)
we have

a(f)=\langle f,f_{\chi}\rangle\,.

This gives (c).

A similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.5
gives that
f_{\chi}
satisfies

U_{s}f_{\chi}=\chi(s)f_{\chi}\qquad\forall s\in G\,.

This shows (a) and completes the proof.
∎

The proposition has the following consequence on vanishing of Fourier–Bohr coefficients uniform in
x\in X
:

Lemma 4.6

(Uniform vanishing)
.

Let
(X,G)
be a dynamical system with metrizable
X
and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a Følner sequence. Let
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.
Then, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

For each
G
-invariant measure
\mathsf{m}
on
X
,
\chi
is not an eigenvalue for
\mathsf{m}
.

(ii)

For each ergodic measure
\mathsf{m}
on
X
,
\chi
is not an eigenvalue for
\mathsf{m}
.

(iii)

For each
f\in C(X)
and any sequence
\chi_{n}\in\widehat{G}
converging pointwise to
\chi\in\widehat{G}
we have

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=0

uniformly in
x\in X
.

Proof.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) follows from a standard application of Choquet Theorem
[
19
]
(see
[
9
]
for the details).

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) Assume by contradiction that there exists some
f\in C(X)
so that

\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{n}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

does not converge to
0
uniformly in
x\in X
. Then, we can find some
C>0
and some
x_{k_{n}}
in
X
so that for all
n
we have

\left|\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}f(t.x_{k_{n}})\cdot\overline{\chi_{k_{n}}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\right|\geq C\,.

As
\|f\|_{\infty}<\infty
, we can find a subsequence
(l_{n})
of
(k_{n})
such that

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{l_{n}}|}\int_{A_{l_{n}}}f(s.x_{l_{n}})\cdot\overline{\chi_{l_{n}}(s)}\mbox{\rm d}s=a\neq 0\,.

Proposition
4.5
implies that there exists a
G
-invariant probability measure
\mathsf{m}
so that
\chi
is an eigenvalue of
\mathsf{m}
. This is a contradiction to (i).

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) Assume by contradiction that
\chi
is an eigenvalue for some ergodic measure
\mathsf{m}
. Pick a normalized eigenfunction
f_{\chi}
.
Chose some
f\in C(X)
so that
\langle f,f_{\chi}\rangle\neq 0
.

Next, by Theorem
2.1
, we can pick a subsequence
(A_{k_{n}})
of
{\mathcal{A}}
along which the Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem holds.

Then, there exists some set
X_{0}\subseteq X
with
\mathsf{m}(X_{0})=1
so that for all
x\in X_{0}
we have

\langle f,f_{\chi}\rangle=f_{\chi}(x)\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\,.

Fix some
x\in X_{0}
. Then, (iii) gives

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{k_{n}}|}\int_{A_{k_{n}}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=0\,.

Therefore,

0\neq\langle f,f_{\chi}\rangle=f_{\chi}(x)\cdot 0=0\,,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

To the best of the authors knowledge this is the first result on uniform (in
x\in X
) vanishing of Fourier–Bohr coefficients outside of the uniquely ergodic situation. For the uniquely ergodic situation we recover some previous results as discussed next.

Recall that a dynamical system
(X,G)
is
uniquely ergodic
if it admits just one invariant probability measure.
For such systems, conditions (i) and (ii) of Lemma
3.7
involves just one measure
\mathsf{m}
and we obtain the following consequence that recovers a theorem of
[
14
]
, which in turn generalizes
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
(for
G={\mathbb{Z}}
) as well as a result of Assani
[
2
]
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(X,G)
be a uniquely ergodic metrizable dynamical system. Let
K
be a compact subset of
\widehat{G}\setminus\mathbb{S}
. Then, for all
f\in C(X)
we have

\lim_{n}\sup_{x\in X}\sup_{\chi\in K}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\left|\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t\right|=0\,.

∎

5.
Wiener–Wintner points

In this section we introduce and study the main objects of interest for the remaining part of the article viz Wiener–Wintner points. We show that the Fourier–Bohr coefficients exist for such points and provide various characterizations for them.

Consider a topological dynamical system
(X,G)
. Let a Følner sequence
\mathcal{A}
be given. We call an
x\in X
a
Fourier–Bohr point
if

a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(g_{x})=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}g(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t

exists for all
g
in
C(X)
and all
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.

Given any
G
-invariant measure
\mathsf{m}
, by Lemma
3.5
, any Fourier–Bohr point
x
comes with a unique system of functions
\{f_{\chi}^{x},\chi\in\widehat{G}\}

satisfying

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(g_{x})=\langle g,f_{\chi}^{x}\rangle

for all
g\in C(X)
.

Moreover, the non-vanishing
f_{\chi}^{x},\chi\in\widehat{G}
, form an orthogonal system of eigenfunctions. In general these functions will not be normalized and not form a basis of the space of eigenfunctions as shown in the next example.

Example 5.1
.

Consider
\{0,1\}
with discrete topology. Then,
Y=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, equipped with product topology is a compact space.

G={\mathbb{Z}}
acts continuously on
Y
via

(\alpha(n,y))(k)=y(k-n)

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
y\in Y
. Let

A_{n}:=[-n,n]\,.

Define

u(n):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mbox{ if }n<0\\
1&\mbox{ if }n\geq 0\,.\end{array}\right.

Then,
u\in Y
.

Define

O(u):=\{\alpha(n,u):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\,,

and
X:=\overline{O(u)}
. Then
(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is a topological dynamical system. It is easy to see that

X=\{\alpha(n,u):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cup\{\underline{0},\underline{1}\}

where
\underline{1}(n)=1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and the point
\underline{0}
with
\underline{0}(n)=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Both
\underline{1}
and
\underline{0}
are invariant under the action.

Then, the measure

\mathsf{m}:=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\underline{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\underline{0}}\,,

is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant probability measure, where
\delta_{a}
denotes the Dirac measure supported at
a
.

A short computation shows that every
x\in O(u)
is generic for
\mathsf{m}
and that the Fourier–Bohr coefficients exist for each
x\in X
. Moreover, the functions
f_{\chi}^{x}
can be calculated explicitly and satisfy

f_{\chi}^{x}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mbox{ if }\chi\neq 1\\
\frac{1}{2}1_{\underline{0}}&\mbox{ if }\chi=1\mbox{ and }x=\underline{0}\\
\frac{1}{2}1_{\underline{1}}&\mbox{ if }\chi=1\mbox{ and }x=\underline{1}\\
\frac{1}{2}(1_{\underline{1}}+1_{\underline{0}})&\mbox{ if }\chi=1\mbox{ and }x\in O(u)\\
\end{array}\right.\,.

Therefore,
f_{\chi}^{x}
are not normalized. Moreover, for each
x\in X
, the functions
f_{\chi}^{x}
do not form a basis for the two dimensional space
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
.

The example suggests to single out those Fourier–Bohr points for which the non-zero
f_{\chi}^{x},\chi\in\widehat{G}
do form an orthonormal basis of the space of eigenfunctions. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 5.2

(Wiener-Winter points)
.

Let
(X,G)
be a topological dynamical system,
\mathsf{m}
a
G
-invariant probability measure on
X
and
\mathcal{A}
a Følner sequence.

A point
x\in X
is called a
Wiener–Wintner point of
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
if
there exists a set
B
in
\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
consisting of eigenfunctions with the following two properties:

•

For each
\chi\in\mathbb{S}
the set
\{e\in B:e\mbox{ is an eigenfunction for }\chi\}
is an orthonormal basis in the eigenspace for
\chi
.

•

For all
f\in C(X)
and
e\in B
with corresponding eigenvalue
\chi_{e}
the following limit exists and

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\cdot\overline{\chi_{e}(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=\langle f,e\rangle\,.

In this case
B
is called the
basis associated to
x
.

We note that the Wiener-Wintner points are indeed exactly the Fourier-Bohr points
x\in X

for which the non-zero
f_{\chi}^{x},\chi\in\widehat{G}
, form an orthonormal basis of the space of eigenfunctions.
At this point it is not clear that Wiener–Wintner points exists at all. However, we will show in the subsequent considerations that existence of a Wiener–Wintner point implies ergodicity of the system, which in turn implies that almost every point is a Wiener–Wintner point. So, if such points exist at all there is an ample supply of them.

We start by showing that existence of Wiener–Wintner points implies ergodicity.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be a dynamical system and
{\mathcal{A}}
a Følner sequence. If there exists an Wiener–Wintner point
x\in X
, then
\mathsf{m}
is ergodic.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\mathsf{m}
is not ergodic. Then, there exist distinct
g,h\in B
corresponding to the eigenvalue
1
. Then, for all
f\in C(X)
we get

\langle f,g\rangle=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(t.x)\mbox{\rm d}t=\langle f,h\rangle\,.

Since
C(X)
is dense in
L^{2}(X,\mathsf{m})
this gives the contradiction
g=h
. ∎

By the preceding proposition we can (and will) from now on write the basis
B
appearing in the definition of generic points in the form

\{e_{\chi}:\chi\in\mathbb{S}\},

where
e_{\chi}
is the unique eigenfunction in
B
to
\chi
. Next, we show that any Wiener–Wintner point is generic.

Lemma 5.4

(Wiener–Wintner points are generic)
.

Let
(X,G)
be a topological dynamical system,
\mathsf{m}
a
G
-invariant measure on
X
and
{\mathcal{A}}
a Følner sequence. Then, every Wiener–Wintner point of
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
is generic.

Proof.

Let
x
be a Wiener–Wintner point and let

e_{1}
be the eigenfunction to the eigenvalue
1
in the associated basis. As existence of Wiener–Wintner points implies that the system is ergodic the function
e_{1}
is almost surely constant with value, say,
c\neq 0
. Since
x
is Wiener–Wintner point we then get

c=\langle 1,e_{1}\rangle=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}1\mbox{\rm d}t=1\,.

So, we infer
e_{1}=1
almost everywhere.

The aim of this section is to relate Wiener–Wintner points to Besicovitch almost periodicity.

\|h\|_{b}:=\limsup_{n}\sqrt{\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}|h(t)|^{2}\mbox{\rm d}t}\,.

16

If a dynamical system

Let
(X,G,\mathsf{m})
be an ergodic topological dynamical system with pure point spectrum, let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a Følner sequence and let
x\in X
. Then,
x
is a Wiener–Wintner point if and only if
x
is
\mathsf{m}
-generic and Besicovitch almost periodic.

Proof.

Let
x
be a Wiener-Winter point. Then,
x
is generic and, by Theorem
6.1
, it is Besicovitch almost periodic.

Conversely, if
x
is generic Besicovitch almost periodic then by Theorem
6.1

x
is a Wiener–Wintner point for some ergodic measure
\mathsf{m}^{\prime}
with pure point spectrum.
It follows that
x
is a generic point for both
\mathsf{m}
and
\mathsf{m}^{\prime}
we get that
\mathsf{m}=\mathsf{m}^{\prime}
. As
x
is Wiener–Wintner point for
\mathsf{m}^{\prime}
, the claim follows.
∎

7.
Application to translation bounded measure dynamical systems

In this section we give an application to dynamical systems of translation bounded measures. Such dynamical systems serve as models in aperiodic order. For a brief overview of the field of aperiodic order we recommend the monographs
[
3
,
12
]
, while for an introduction to dynamical systems of translation bounded measures see
[
6
]
.

Starting from a Delone set
\Lambda
, or more generally a translation bounded measure
\mu
, the idea is to construct a dynamical system
\mathbb{X}
consisting of all pointsets/measures which locally look similar to
\Lambda
or
\mu
, respectively. We will make this precise below. Then, via the so called Dworkin argument
[
8
]
, we can study the diffraction of
\Lambda
or
\mu
by studying the spectrum of the induced unitary representation of
G
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X},\mathsf{m})
for some (ergodic) measure
\mathsf{m}
on
\mathbb{X}
.
Let us now recall some of the basic definitions.

Recall that, via the Riesz- representation theorem
[
21
]
, we can identify the set of
Radon measures
on
G
with the functionals on
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
which are continuous in the so called inductive topology of
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.

A measure
\mu
on
G
is called
translation bounded
if the for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
the convolution
\mu\ast\varphi:G\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\mu\ast\varphi(t):=\int_{G}\varphi(t-s)d\mu(s)

is a bounded function. This is equivalent
[
1
]
to

\|\mu\|_{U}:=\sup_{t\in G}\left|\mu\right|(t+U)<\infty\,,

for one (and hence all
[
7
]
) precompact set
U
with non-empty interior. Here,
\left|\mu\right|
denotes the total variation measure of
\mu
, see
[
18
, Theorem 6.5.6]
.

The set of all translation bounded measures is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
.

Given a precompact open set
U
and a constant
C>0
the set

{\mathcal{M}}_{C,U}:=\{\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G):\|\mu\|_{U}\leq C\}

is vaguely compact and
G
-invariant
[
6
]
. Moreover, the action of
G
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{C,U}
via translates is continuous in the
vague topology
(i.e. the topology of convergence on test functions
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
). Therefore, any
G
-invariant vaguely closed set
\mathbb{X}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{C,U}
gives rise to a topological dynamical system
(\mathbb{X},G)
. Such a dynamical system is called a
dynamical system of translation bounded measures
or just
TMDS
for short.

Such systems can be thought of as generalizations of subshifts over finite alphabets. Indeed, for
G={\mathbb{Z}}
the translation bounded measures can be identified with the bounded functions.

In particular, for each
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
, the
hull

\mathbb{X}(\mu):=\overline{\{\alpha(t,\mu):t\in G\}}

is a TMDS.

Over the years much effort has been put into understanding the case of systems with pure point spectrum. The equivalence of pure point dynamical and diffraction spectrum was established in
[
6
,
13
,
10
]
. Recently, the equivalence between pure point spectrum for dynamical systems and mean/Besicovitch almost periodicity of its points has been established in
[
15
,
16
]
.

Finally, we show that for TMDS, we can classify the Wiener–Wintner points in terms of Besicovitch almost periodicity of the point (considered as a translation bounded measure).

First, us recall that a measure
\mu
is called
Besicovitch almost periodic
with respect to the van Hove sequence
(A_{n})
if
\mu\ast\varphi\in Bap_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{2}(G)
for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.

Corollary 7.1

(Characterization of Besicovitch almost periodic measures)
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a van Hove sequence. Then,
\mu
is a Besicovitch almost periodic along
{\mathcal{A}}
if and only if there exists an ergodic measure
\mathsf{m}
on
\mathbb{X}(\mu)
with pure point spectrum such that
\mu
is Wiener–Wintner point for
(\mathsf{m},{\mathcal{A}})
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
6.1
and
[
16
, Prop. 7.1]
.
∎

Appendix A
Tempered Følner sequences

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(A_{n})
be a Følner sequence and let
B_{n}\subseteq G
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be given with

\lim_{n}\frac{|A_{n}\Delta B_{n}|}{|A_{n}|}=0\,.

Then,
(B_{n})
is Følner. Moreover, if
(A_{n})
is tempered and
B_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
(B_{n})
is also tempered.

Proof.

The assumptions on
(A_{n})
and
(B_{n})
easily imply

\lim_{n}\frac{|B_{n}|}{|A_{n}|}=1\,.

Now, for each
t\in G
we clearly have

\displaystyle B_{n}\Delta(t+B_{n})

\displaystyle\subseteq\left(B_{n}\Delta A_{n}\right)\cup\left(A_{n}\Delta(t+A_{n})\right)\cup(t+(A_{n}\Delta B_{n}))\,.

This easily gives that
(B_{n})
is Følner. Next, if
B_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
for all
n
, we then have

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(B_{n}-B_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_{n}-A_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|A_{n}|\leq C^{\prime}|B_{n}|

for some constant
C^{\prime}
, with the last inequality following from the fact that
\frac{|B_{n}|}{|A_{n}|}
converges to
1
and hence is bounded.
∎

Let us note here in passing that if
B_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
and
B_{n}
is tempered, it does not seem that
A_{n}
is tempered. This is the reason why below we need a separate proof for the union.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(A_{n})
be a tempered Følner sequence and let
s\in G
. Then, the sequences
(B_{n})
and
(C_{n})
defined by

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=A_{n}\cap(s+A_{n})

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=A_{n}\cup(s+A_{n})

are tempered van Følner sequences.

Proof.

Lemma
A.1
implies that
(B_{n})
and
(C_{n})
are Følner sequences and that
(B_{n})
is tempered. For
C_{n}
we obviously have

\displaystyle\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(C_{n}-C_{k})

\displaystyle\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_{n}-A_{k})\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_{n}-(s+A_{k}))\right)

\displaystyle\cup\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}((s+A_{n})-A_{k})\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(s+A_{n})-(s+A_{k})\right)\,.

This implies

\left|\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(C_{n}-C_{k})\right|\leq 4\left|\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_{n}-A_{k})\right|\leq 4C|A_{n}|\,.

giving that
(C_{n})
is tempered.
∎

Small Mitochondria-Targeting Fluorophore with Multifunctional Therapeutic Activities against Prostate Cancer via the HIF1α/OATPs Pathway
Authors: Jing Qin, Caiqin Zhang, Yong Zhao, Dengxu Tan, Pengpeng Wu, Xue Shui, Weijun Qin, Xu Ge, Changhong Shi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractProstate cancer (PCa) is considered to be the most prevalent malignancy in males worldwide. Abiraterone is a 17α-hydroxylase/C17, 20-lyase (CYP17) inhibitor that has been approved for use in patients with prostate cancer. However, several negative aspects, such as drug resistance, toxicity, and lack of real-time monitoring of treatment responses, could appear with long-term use. Therefore, the development of anticancer agents with specific targeting to avoid side effects is imperative. Here, we used MHI-148, a type of heptamethine cyanine (HC) near-infrared fluorescence dye (NIRF), as a prototype structure to synthesize two theranostic agents, Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783. The new compound Abi-DZ-1 retained the excellent photophysical characteristics and NIRF imaging property of MHI-148, and it could preferentially accumulate in prostate cancer cells but not in normal prostate epithelial cells via the HIF1α/organic anion-transporting polypeptides axis. NIRF imaging using Abi-DZ-1 selectively identified tumors in mice bearing PCa xenografts. Moreover, Abi-DZ-1 treatment significantly retarded the tumor growth in both a cell-derived xenograft model and a patient-derived tumor xenograft model. This finding demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 may hold promise as a potential multifunctional theranostic agent for future tumor-targeted imaging and precision therapy. Constructing theranostic agents using the NIRF dye platform holds great promise in accurate therapy and intraoperative navigation.Keywords: near-infrared fluorescence, heptamethine cyanine dyes, prostate cancer, theranostic agents

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the most common malignancy affecting men’s health worldwide; it ranks the first among tumors in incidence rate and is second only to lung cancer in mortality, according to the report from the American Cancer Society in 2020.1,2 Radical prostatectomy and radiation therapy can successfully treat PCa in the majority of patients, whereas up to ∼30% will experience local recurrence or develop castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC).2−6 Clinical symptoms of CRPC worsened with the progression of the disease, including bone pain, hematuria, lower urinary tract obstruction, and so on. The patients had poor quality of life, a relatively poor prognosis, and high mortality. Abiraterone (Abi), a 17α-hydroxylase/C17, 20-lyase (CYP17) inhibitor and an antagonist to the androgen receptor, has been approved to treat CRPC in clinical settings.7 However, several negative aspects, such as drug resistance, toxicity, and lack of real-time monitoring of treatment responses, could appear with long-term application.8 For these reasons, developing techniques to locate tumors, visualize their boundaries during surgery, and deliver anticancer drugs selectively into PCa tumors could improve therapeutic outcomes and reduce disease recurrence.9

Recently, near-infrared fluorescence (NIRF)-guided multifunctional theranostic strategies have attracted a huge research hotspot and hold significant promise in cancer treatment.10−12 Due to the extremely low autofluorescence and absorption from organisms, NIRF agents could minimize background interference and improve tissue penetration and therapeutic efficacy and they show great potential in the detection of tissue function, metabolism, biodistribution, and for real-time monitoring of various patho-physiological states.13−15 Nevertheless, the current approaches for treating PCa with available agents are still challenging, which is mainly due to their poor tumor-specific distribution characteristics and short half-life period, such as indocyanine green (ICG).16−18 Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop a new theranostic agent for cancer-targeted imaging and multifunctional therapeutics.15,19

Previously, we reported a series of heptamethine cyanine NIRF dyes. Among them, MHI-148 could selectively accumulate in tumor tissue and display strong fluorescence intensity.20−23 Structure–inherent relationship studies and other reports have characterized the heptamethine core with lipophilic cationic properties as being essential for cancer targeting.16,24,25 Based on these previous studies, we speculated that MHI-148 could act as a drug delivery vehicle into tumor tissue, providing a good strategy for the enhanced permeability and retention effect in cancer sites.16 However, the design and synthesis of high-performance MHI-148-theranostic agents remain a challenge.

Here, we have synthesized and identified two new NIRF dyes, Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783, assembled by the tumor-targeting heptamethine moiety of MHI-148 and the bioactive anticancer abiraterone (Abi). The new compound Abi-DZ-1 retained the excellent photophysical characteristics and NIRF imaging property of MHI-148, which could preferentially accumulate in PCa cells but not in normal prostate epithelial cells via the HIF1α/organic anion-transporting polypeptides (OATPs) signaling axis. NIRF imaging using Abi-DZ-1 selectively identified the tumors in mice bearing PCa xenografts. Moreover, Abi-DZ-1 treatment significantly retarded the tumor growth both in a cell-derived xenograft (CDX) model and a CRPC patient-derived tumor xenograft (PDX) model.26,27 In summary, these findings demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 may hold promise as a potential multifunctional theranostic agent for accurate therapy and intraoperative navigation.

Materials and Methods

Synthesis of Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783

To improve the water solubility, the (CH2)5CO2– side chain of MHI-148 was replaced with (CH2)4SO3–. The modified compounds were named DZ-1 and 783, respectively. The compounds were synthesized by Fanbo Biochemicals Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The method of synthesis was provided by Dr. Leland W.K. Chung (Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, Los Angeles, CA, USA). To obtain Abi, abiraterone acetate tablets were ground into powder, purified by column chromatography, and distilled under reduced pressure to obtain the product. Abiraterone acetate (100 mg, 0.255 mmol, FW = 391.55) was mixed with 15:2 (v/v) water/methanol on ice. A saturated solution of lithium hydroxide (36.64 mg, 1.53 mmol, FW = 23.95) was added in a water bath under nitrogen protection and reacted at room temperature for 24 h. After checking that the reaction was complete, the mixture was distilled under reduced pressure to remove the methanol, 15 mL of water was added, and the pH was adjusted to 2–3 with dilute hydrochloric acid. The solids were separated and the filter cake was collected via filtration and dried to obtain Abi.

To obtain Abi-DZ-1, Abi (30 mg, 0.0858 mmol, FW = 349.51) and DZ-1 (47.47 mg, 0.0687 mmol, FW = 691.32) were dissolved in dry DMSO and mixed with 1-ethyl-3(3-dimethylpropylamine) carbodiimide (19.75 mg, 0.103 mmol, FW = 191.7). Next, 0.1 mL of triethylamine was added, and the reaction was allowed to proceed at room temperature for 8 h. After checking that the reaction was complete, ether was added to precipitate the solid, which was filtered, and the filter cake was purified by column chromatography.

Cells and Cell Cultures

Human PCa cell lines PC3, LNCap, 22RV1, and C42 were purchased from the Shanghai Institute of Life Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences. The GES-1 cell lines were purchased from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Typical Culture Preservation Committee cell bank. RPMI-1640 culture media were obtained from HyClone/Thermo Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA) and supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (HyClone/Thermo Scientific) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). The cells were cultured in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. The following primary antibodies were used: HIF1α (1:100; Abcam, Cambridge, UK) and OATP (1:50; Santa Cruz, Santa Cruz, CA, USA). Chlorpromazine, amiloride, the filipin complex, and rifampicin were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA. A CCK8 assay kit was purchased from Futai Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). A mitochondrial membrane potential assay kit (JC-1) was purchased from ElabScience.

Animals

Six-to-seven-week-old male BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Vital River (Beijing, China), and male NPG mice were purchased from Vital Star (Beijing, China). All mice were fed in a pathogen-free system at the Laboratory Animal Center of the Fourth Military Medical University (FMMU). The mice were anesthetized under isoflurane during the experiment. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of FMMU (Protocol 20210705). The animal experiments followed the 3R principle. The animals were not subjected to any form of abuse during the experiments. Isoflurane was used for gas anesthesia. When the tumor was too large or the animal was obviously unable to survive, the animal was euthanized. The method of euthanasia was carbon dioxide asphyxiation.

Optical Characteristics of Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783

Abi-DZ-1 or Abi-783 was dissolved at a 100 nM concentration in DMSO, PBS, or 5% FBS, and the UV absorption wavelength and fluorescence intensity were measured using an ultraviolet spectrophotometer (Shimadzu Corporation) and a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Shimadzu Corporation), respectively.

Cellular Uptake Study In Vitro

The human PCa lines (PC3, 22RV1) and the humor normal cell line GES-1 were cultured in 35 mm petri dishes (2 × 104 cells/well) for 24 h in a CO2 incubator. The cells were incubated with Abi-DZ-1 (20 μM, 200 μL) for 20 min at 37 °C, and then the plate was washed three times using PBS. Next, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min. After washing with PBS, the cells were incubated with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) (Tiangen, Shanghai, China) for 10 min followed by a simple washing step. Next, the cells were immediately observed on a near-infrared fluorescence microscope equipped with an ICG filter (excitation/emission: 750–800/820–860 nm).

For subcellular localization, LysoTracker green DND-26 and MitoTracker green FM were used to track the lysosomes and cytoplasmic mitochondria. After being co-cultured with Abi-DZ-1, the PC3 cells were cultured with LysoTracker green DND-26 (100 μM) for 60 min at 37 °C or with MitoTracker green FM (200 μM) for 30 min at 37 °C. Next, the cells were fixed, DAPI-stained, and immediately observed on a near-infrared fluorescence microscope. Images were merged to show subcellular localization. Abi-783 operated in a similar manner. The emission/excitation light wavelengths for the MitoTracker and LysoTracker were 490/516 and 554/576 nm, respectively.

The human PCa PC3 cell line was cultured in 35 mm petri dishes (2 × 104 cells/well) for 24 h. After being co-cultured with inhibitor drugs chlorpromazine (clathrin pathway, 13 μg/mL), amiloride (micropinocytosis, 300 μM), filipin complex (caveolae, 3.2 μg/mL), or rifampicin (OATP inhibitor) for 1 h at 37 °C, the cells were washed, fixed, and stained as described above. Next, the cells were observed on a near-infrared fluorescence microscope.

Antitumor Activity Assays In Vitro

For the CCK8 assay, PC3, LNCAP, and C42 cells were separately seeded into 96-well plates (1 × 103 cells/well) for 24 h and incubated with different concentrations (0, 10, 20, 40, 80, and 160 μM) of Abi-DZ-1 for 72 h. Next, the cells were gently washed with fresh medium and cultured with medium containing CCK-8 reagent (10%) for 4 h. Next, the absorbance was determined using an xMark microplate absorbance spectrophotometer at 450 nm (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Abi-783 and Abi were operated in a similar manner.

For determination of CYP17 viability, the human PCa PC3 cell line was cultured in 6-well dishes (1 × 106 cells/well) for 24 h. Abi, Abi-DZ-1, or Abi-783 (20 μM) was added in one well, and an untreated control was set up separately. Cells were collected after 24 h. The CYP17 viability colorimetric assay kit was purchased from GenMed Scientifics Inc., USA, and all experimental steps were carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

For the ROS assay, PC3 cells were cultured in a 96-well plate (1 × 104 cells/well) for 24 h. The DCFH-DA (20 μM) was added, and cells were cultured for 20 min. After washing with PBS three times, Abi, Abi-DZ-1, or Abi-783 (20 μM) was added. The microplate reader was used to determine the absorption value in real time.

For the apoptosis assay by flow cytometry, PC3 and 22RV1 cells were seeded in 6-well plates (1 × 105 cells/well) for 24 h. Next, Abi, Abi-DZ-1, or Abi-783 (20 μM) was added, and the cells were cultured for another 24 h. Untreated cells were used as the control. After digestion using pancreatin and centrifugation, the cells were collected and the proportion of apoptotic cells was evaluated via flow cytometry. The apoptosis rate was defined as the percentage of Annexin V-positive and PI-negative cells.

For the migration/invasion assay, PC3 cells were cultured in a 6-well plate (1 × 105 cells/well) for 24 h. Abi, Abi-DZ-1, or Abi-783 (20 μM) was added, and the cells were cultured for another 48 h. After digestion and centrifugation, the cells were resuspended in fresh medium with 1% FBS and placed into the upper chambers of the transwell plate (1 × 104 cells/well). Next, 500 μL of fresh medium with 20% FBS was placed into a new 24-well plate, and the upper chambers with cells were put in the 24-well plate such that the FBS gradient could promote cell migration. Incubating for 24 h, the upper chamber was taken out, and cells were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min. After washing three times with PBS, the cells were stained for 15 min using 0.1% crystal violet solution (Beyotime). The chamber was then observed under a DP-70 fluorescence microscope.

For the invasion assay, PC3 cells were cultured in a 6-well plate (1 × 105 cells/well) for 24 h. Next, a 100 μL pipet nozzle was used to draw a line in the middle of the well. Next, the plate was washed three times with PBS to remove the dead cells. Abi, Abi-DZ-1, or Abi-783 (20 μM) was added to fresh medium supplemented with 1% FBS and cultured for 24 h. The cells were then observed under an Olympus DP-70 fluorescence microscope, and the invasion rate was calculated.

Prostate Cancer Organoid Model

The fresh PCa specimen was obtained from Xijing Hospital of the FMMU, as described previously.28 The corresponding organoid model was established according the protocol.29 A small percentage of tumors were moved to a 10 cm petri dish; PBS was used to wash the tissue three times. Next, the samples were fully cut into small pieces using scissors and cultured in a 15 mL centrifuge tube containing digestive liquid (2.5 mg/mL type II collagenase and 10 μM Y-27632 basic medium) for 1 h. Then the samples were added to the serum-containing DMEM medium to terminate digestion. The digestive products were filtered through a 70 μm filter. After centrifugation at 1700 rpm for 5 min, the supernatant was removed. The cells were cultured with 30 μL of matrix glue in a 24-well plate (1 × 105 cells/well) for 15 min; after the matrix gel solidified, 500 μL of medium (Advanced DEME/F12) was added. The medium was changed every 3 days. When the diameter of the organoids was about 50–100 μm, Abi, Abi-783, and Abi-DZ-1 with different concentrations (0, 10, 20, 40, and 80 μM) were added to the medium. Then, 72 h later, the organoids were observed under an Olympus DP-70 fluorescence microscope and the survival rate was calculated.

Prostate Cancer Xenograft Model

The CDX model was established as described previously.13 The human PCa PC3 cell line (100 μL, 1 × 107 cells/mL) was inoculated subcutaneously into 27 athymic nude mice (5–6 weeks). The mice were allocated for subsequent studies when the diameter of the tumors reached ∼5 mm, as measured by a caliper. A B45354 PDX model was established in our lab from an androgen-independent CRPC patient, and 1–3 mm3 tumor fragments were subcutaneously implanted into NPG mice.28 The tumor-bearing mice were randomly distributed into four groups when the tumor size reached 100 mm3. The tumor specimen was xenografted subcutaneously into athymic nude mice to obtain the PDX model according to a published protocol.

NIRF Imaging In Vivo

Abi-DZ-1 or Abi-783 (20 μmol/L) was injected intraperitoneally into six tumor-bearing mice. After 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h, NIRF images of the mice were obtained using the Caliper Lumina II small animal optical imaging system (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) equipped with NIRF filter sets (excitation/emission, 783/840 nm), and the fluorescence intensity was measured via the ROI calculation. After 72 h, the mice were sacrificed, and NIRF images of vital organs were captured.

Antitumor Assay In Vivo

Sixteen PCa CDX models were employed for the antitumor assay. They were randomly assigned to the following four groups: control group, Abi group, Abi-783 group, and Abi-DZ-1 group. Each group had four CDX models. The control group was treated with 100 μL of PBS. The Abi group was intraperitoneally injected with 100 μL of Abi (2 mM) every other day, the Abi-783 group was intraperitoneally injected with 100 μL of Abi-783 (2 mM) every other day, and the Abi-DZ-1 group was intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected with 100 μL of Abi-DZ-1 (2 mM) every other day. Subsequently, tumor size was monitored in the following 4 weeks. Tumor volumes were calculated using the following formula: tumor volume = (length × width2)/2. After 4 weeks of therapy, the mice were sacrificed, and the tumors were extracted and weighed. Some of the tumor tissue was fixed with 4% formalin for the apoptosis assay using the TUNEL assay kit (Abcam). The sections were observed under an Olympus DP-70 fluorescence microscope. Twenty prostate cancer PDX models were further employed for antitumor assays, and they were randomly assigned to the following four groups: control group, Abi group, Abi-783 group, and Abi-DZ-1 group. Each group had five PDX models. The administration method was consistent with the CDX model; tumor size was monitored in the following 4 weeks.

Toxicity Studies

Twenty-four BALB/C mice (4–6 weeks old, 20–25 g) were randomly allocated to four groups to evaluate the acute toxicity of Abi-783 and Abi-DZ-1. Abi, Abi-783, or Abi-DZ-1 (100 μL, 20 mM) was i.p. injected into 6 BALB/C mice daily for 30 days. Six mice in the control group were injected with 100 μL of PBS. These mice were observed and weighed daily. Thirty days later, the mice were sacrificed, and the blood was used for routine blood and liver function examination.

Statistical Analysis

All data are presented as the mean ± the standard deviation of at least three independent experiments. GraphPad Prism 8.0 was used for statistical analysis. All other comparisons were analyzed via Student’s t tests. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001.

Results

Synthesis and Characterization of Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783

We developed a new route for the chemical synthesis of Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 (Figure 1A). Hyperchem 7.5 programs under a quantitative structure–activity relationship (QSAR) module was used to calculate the character and structure parameters of the new synthesis molecules, such as the oil–water distribution coefficient (Log D), polarity, and volume. Log D is a key index that was used to determine the ability of dyes to pass through cell membranes. The simulation results showed that the Log D value of Abi-DZ-1 was higher than that of Abi-783 (2.06 vs 1.16, respectively), indicating that Abi-DZ-1 might have the better ability to target tumor cells (Table S1). Solubility is one of the important parameters that directly affects the absorption and bioavailability of the dyes; poorly water-soluble dyes usually require high doses to reach therapeutic concentrations, which could increase the metabolic burden of the human body.30 Both of our synthetic dyes had outstanding solubility. The absorption and emission wavelengths of Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 were investigated in different solvents including PBS, 5% serum, and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). The absorption wavelengths of Abi-DZ-1 were 776, 796, and 795 nm in DMSO, 5% serum, and PBS, respectively. When 760 nm was set as the excitation wavelength of Abi-DZ-1, emission peaks were detected at 774 nm in PBS, 783 nm in serum, and 776 nm in DMSO (Figures 1B,1C, and S1). Interestingly, we found that the excitation and emission wavelengths of Abi-DZ-1 were higher in 10% serum than in PBS (Figure S2), which may be ascribed to the higher human serum albumin binding affinity of Abi-DZ-1. The albumin binding capability (Abc) of Abi-DZ-1 was further determined using specific inhibitors, including ibuprofen, according to the methods previously reported.24 The results suggested that Abi-DZ-1 had high binding affinity to serum proteins (Figure S3). The above experiments were repeated with Abi-783, and the results are shown in Figures S1 and S2. From these we could find that Abi-DZ-1 was superior to Abi-783 both in terms of fluorescence intensity and albumin binding ability. This means Abi-DZ-1 was reliable for biomedical applications.

Cellular Uptake of Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 In Vitro

The PCa cell lines (PC3 and 22RV1) and normal cells (GES-1) were co-cultured with 20 μM Abi-DZ-1 or Abi-783 to evaluate the cellular uptake. As shown in Figures 2A, 2B, and S4, the NIRF dyes specifically accumulated in PCa cells but not in normal cells. Quantification of the fluorescence values (Figure 2D) showed that Abi-DZ-1 had a higher fluorescence signal than Abi-783, indicating that Abi-DZ-1 maintained excellent specificity and the optical property of MHI-148. To determine in which organelles Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 preferentially accumulated, we investigated the subcellular location of the two conjugates in PC3 cells (Figures 2C and S4). Microscopic image analysis showed that the new conjugates mainly accumulated in the mitochondria and lysosomes of tumor cells, indicating that Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 could target mitochondria to deliver the antitumor drug into the cells.

Mechanism of the NIRF Compound Target Tumor

Next, we investigated the mechanisms underlying the uptake of the NIRF compound by co-treating the cells with different inhibitors, including amiloride (micropinocytosis inhibitor), filipin complex (alveolar transport inhibitor), chlorpromazine (clathrin-mediated endocytosis inhibitor), or rifampicin (OATP inhibitor). Compared with the control group (Figure S5), the addition of amiloride, filipin complex, and chlorpromazine had no effect on the absorption of Abi-DZ-1, while we found the fluorescence intensity significantly decreased with the OATP inhibitor (Figure 3A). This indicated that Abi-DZ-1 entered cells via the HIF1α/OATPs signaling axis.18 Immunohistochemical analysis was used to measure the expression levels of OATP1B3 and HIF1α (Figures 3B and S6), and the results showed that those two proteins were positively expressed in cancer cells but negatively expressed in normal cells. We analyzed several human prostatic cancer statistics data deposited in the Oncomine database and further confirmed that the expression of OATP1B3 was significantly higher in PCa tissue samples than in healthy tissue samples (Figure 3C), which makes it easier for Abi-DZ-1 to concentrate on PCa tissue. To characterize whether Abi-DZ-1 could penetrate into the tumor, we intraperitoneally injected the dye into the PC3 tumor-bearing mice model; after a while, frozen sections of harvested tumor tissue were imaged, and the results suggested the accumulation of Abi-DZ-1 was on the invasive edges of the tumor as well as in the regions of the tumor that appeared necrotic, indicating that the dye not only could accumulate on the surface of the tumor but also could penetrate deep into the tumor to selectively deliver anticancer drugs into cancer cells (Figure 3D).

Antitumor Effect In Vitro

The impact of Abi, Abi-DZ-1, and Abi-783 on the viability of PCa cells (C42, PC3, and LNCAP) was evaluated using the CCK-8 assay kit (Figure 4A). Abi-DZ-1 showed concentration-dependent cytotoxicity on cancer cells but had no obvious effects on normal cells, suggesting that Abi-DZ-1 had significant antitumor effects for cancer cells. Meanwhile, we explored the toxicity of Abi, Abi-DZ-1, and Abi-783 in the organoids model. As shown in Figure S7, organoids maintain complete structure and good morphology in the low-dose group. When the concentration of Abi-DZ-1 was 80 μM, the organoids lost their basic structure and showed a large number of cell fragments. The results demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 has no obvious cytotoxicity at low concentrations and could eliminate nearly all organoids at the concentration of 80 μM, suggesting the potential tumoricidal ability of Abi-DZ-1 in PCa cells. In addition, invasion/migration assays (Figures S8 and S9) showed that Abi-DZ-1 apparently reduced the migration and invasion potential of cancer cells compared with other groups. As revealed in apoptotic assays, Abi-DZ-1 increased the number of apoptotic cells in both 22RV1 and PC3 cells (Figures 4B and S10). The function of Abi was to inhibit the viability of the cytochrome CYP17 enzyme, which was vital in the growth of tumor cells. We measured the expression of CYP17 in prostate cancer cells, PC3, 22RV1, and C42, by PCR and explored the expression of CYP17 in tumor tissues by the TGCA database. The results showed that the expression of CYP17 in tumor tissues was much higher than that in paracancer tissues (Figure S11). Afterward, we measured the expression of CYP17 in PC3, 22RV1, and C42 cells after Abi, Abi-783, and Abi-DZ-1 treatment by PCR (Figure S11). The results demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 could reduce the level of expression of CYP17 in PC3 and 22RV1 cells. We next investigated whether the two synthesized dyes could enhance the inhibitory effect of Abi on CYP17. The CYP17 activity in the Abi-, Abi-DZ-1-, and Abi-783-treated cells was measured (Figure 4C). The results showed that CYP17 had the lowest activity in Abi-DZ-1-treated cells. To further determine whether the NIRF compound could target mitochondria, mitochondrial membrane potential experiments were performed. The PCa cell lines PC3 were co-cultured with 20 μM Abi, Abi-DZ-1, or Abi-783 for 72h, and the kit JC-1 was used to evaluate the mitochondrial membrane potential. As shown in Figure S12, the ratio of green and red fluorescence intensity was significantly increased in the Abi-DZ-1 group compared with the Abi and Abi-783 groups, indicating that Abi-DZ-1 caused the most severe damage to the mitochondrial membrane. Meanwhile, we measured the expression levels of ROS in cells after 3 days treatment. The results showed that Abi-DZ-1 could cause more ROS than other groups (Figure 4D); the reason may be that the new synthesized dyes could target and disrupt the function of mitochondria, which could explain the stronger antitumor ability of Abi-DZ-1 than the other compounds.

Optical Imaging

To determine whether Abi-DZ-1 could be used in NIFR imaging, Abi-DZ-1 or Abi-783 (20 μmol/L) was i.p. injected into six athymic nude mice bearing PC3-derived tumor xenografts, and the preferential accumulation of the NIFR dyes was investigated in the next 72 h. As shown in Figure 5A, Abi-DZ-1 gradually accumulated in tumor tissues, but Abi-783 deviated from tumor sites, confirming the preferential accumulation of Abi-DZ-1 in PCa tissues. Moreover, not only did the fluorescence intensity of the two NIRF dyes at the tumor site increase with time but also Abi-DZ-1 was nearly an order of magnitude higher than that of Abi-783. After the mice were sacrificed, vital organs were harvested for imaging. In the Abi-DZ-1 group, only the tumor site showed significant fluorescence, while in the Abi-783 group almost all vital organs had significant fluorescent signals. The result demonstrated Abi-DZ-1 more easily accumulated in tumor tissues, which further confirmed the selective accumulation of Abi-DZ-1 in PCa tissues (Figure 5B). In addition, we found a strong correlation between the NIRF intensity and tumor volume (Figure S13), demonstrating the feasibility of using Abi-DZ-1 for PCa imaging.

In parallel, we constructed an orthotopic PCa model by injecting fluorescent-labeled PC3 into the prostate of nude mice. H&E staining of the orthotopic PCa model revealed the presence of cancer tissue (Figure 5C). Abi-DZ-1 (20 μM) was intraperitoneally injected into the orthotopic PCa model. The strong NIRF signal detected only in cancer tissues demonstrated the feasibility of Abi-DZ-1 for PCa imaging.

Antitumor Effect In Vivo

The antitumor effects were investigated using the PC3-derived tumor xenograft model. The tumor-bearing mice were randomly separated into four groups; each included four nude mice. The mice received intraperitoneal injections of PBS, Abi, Abi-783, or Abi-DZ-1 every 2 days, and the tumor volumes were recorded in the next 4 weeks of continuous treatment. The results demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 prominently inhibited tumor growth compared to other treatment groups (Figure 6A). The mice were then sacrificed, their tumor tissues were harvested, and Ki67 immunohistochemistry (Figures S14a–S14d) and TUNEL assays (Figures 6B and S14e) clearly revealed that Abi-DZ-1 treatment inhibited tumor growth and exhibited more obvious cell apoptosis. To further study the therapeutic effect of Abi-DZ-1, we used the PCa tissue B45354 to construct 20 CRPC PDX models. Compared with Abi and Abi-783, Abi-DZ-1 showed a more pronounced tumor inhibition effect, and a statistically significant difference in tumor weight between the Abi-783 and Abi-DZ-1 treatment groups was observed (Figure 6C). These results confirmed the dual function of Abi-DZ-1 for cancer imaging and therapy. In addition, blood routine and liver function examination showed that even at a 10-fold higher dose, neither Abi-783 nor Abi-DZ-1 caused death or significant weight loss (Table S2). The pharmacokinetic behavior of Abi-783 and Abi-DZ-1 in vivo was evaluated after intraperitoneal injection. As shown in Figure S15, the dyes were significantly cleared from some vital organs after 48 h, and the results showed that Abi-DZ-1 could be safely used as an excellent theranostic agent for application in clinical trials.

Discussion

Androgen deprivation therapy was the standard treatment for PCa,31 but most patients eventually might progress to CRPC after an average of 1–3 years.32 One mechanism leading to castrate-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) is the upregulation of CYP17. Abiraterone, the inhibitor of CYP17A, is an FDA-approved drug for metastatic CRPC and could block androgens from testes, adrenal glands, and tumor cells and inhibit tumor growth. However, there exist concerns about the toxic effects from long-term treatment.33,34 Therefore, the development of anticancer agents with specific targeting to avoid side effects is imperative. In recent years, near-infrared fluorescence (NIR)-guided multifunctional theranostic strategies have attracted extensive attention, which could provide good methods to monitor responses to treatments in real time as well as optimize the efficacy and safety of cancer therapy.9 Previously, our group synthesized a class of heptamethine carbocyanine NIR dyes, which have been demonstrated to preferentially localize in tumor tissues and have been employed as imaging probes.14,18,35,36 Among these dyes, MHI-148 exhibited predominant fluorescence properties and cancer-targeting abilities; thus, it would be a promising probe to combine MHI-148 with Abi for clinical applications.19 However, the design and synthesis of high-performance MHI-148 theranostic agents remains a challenge.

In this study, inspired by the structure-inherent targeting strategy, we synthesized the compounds and evaluated them in different models.25 The results revealed that both Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 retained the tumor-homing properties of MHI-148, but the fluorescence intensity of Abi-DZ-1 was clearly stronger than that of Abi-783, indicating that Abi-DZ-1 had excellent fluorescence properties. Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 could specifically accumulate in PCa cells but not in normal cells, and microscopic image analysis showed that the new conjugates mainly accumulated in the mitochondria and lysosomes of tumor cells, meaning that Abi-DZ-1 could target mitochondria. The uptake of NIRF dye by tumor cells was not affected by micropinocytosis, alveolar transport, or clathrin-mediated endocytosis (Figure 3A), but a significant decrease in fluorescence intensity was observed in cancer cells treated with an OATP inhibitor, indicating that OATPs were involved in the regulation of Abi-DZ-1 uptake. According to the Oncomine database, PCa cells express OATPs at higher levels than normal cells. Recent studies have implicated, under hypoxic conditions, that the active HIF1α protein interacts with hypoxia response elements in the OATP1B3 promoter, resulting in the increased transcription of OATP1B3.20,37 HIF1α is destabilized under normoxia conditions due to the rapid degradation initiated by the PHD/VHL pathway; this may be the reason why expression of OATP was relatively low in normal tissues. Inactivation of HIF1α/OATPs signaling in normal cells and tissues blunts their sensitivity to recruit NIRF dyes.38 Thus, the Abi-DZ-1 dyes exhibit preferential accumulation in tumors mediated by tumor hypoxia and the HIF1α/OATPs signaling axis.

Furthermore, the multimodal cancer therapeutic activity of Abi-DZ-1 was extensively investigated. In the cytotoxicity test, Abi-DZ-1 was demonstrated to efficiently decrease the viability of PCa cells, while it had no obvious effects on normal cells (RWPE-1 and GES-1). Organoids are 3D primary cultures that are derived from patient tissue and can be grown over a long period of time while better maintaining genomic stability.38 We constructed the CRPC organoid models according to our previous reports to explore the toxicity of new compound dyes;28 the result of the organoids was meaningful because they “behaved” just like the patient’s tumor and could accurately replicate and predict treatment outcomes. The results demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 has no obvious cytotoxicity at low concentrations and could eliminate nearly all organoids at the concentration of 80 μM, suggesting the potential tumoricidal ability of Abi-DZ-1 in PCa cells.39

In addition, Abi-DZ-1 could promote cell apoptosis and inhibit cell migration, indicating that it had a strong ability to kill tumors. These effects might be related to the asymmetrical and amphipathic structure of Abi-DZ-1. Abiraterone is a CYP17 inhibitor, and this structure of Abi-DZ-1 could closely bind to enzyme proteins, which facilitates the enhancement of the inhibition effect of Abi on CYP17 and leads to more PCa cell death compared to Abi-783 alone. Mitochondria are considered the major source of cellular ROS production. Under normal conditions, intracellular ROS is balanced. However, once mitochondria are damaged, the balance is broken, and the excessive ROS produced was a key factor to induce cell death.16,40 In subcellular localization studies, we characterized the dyes more easily targeted in mitochondria (Figure 2C) and damaged mitochondrial membrane potential (Figure S12). Next, we further explored whether excessive ROS can be produced after dye treatment. The results demonstrated that Abi-DZ-1 rapidly induces the increase of ROS levels and persisted for more than 2 h, suggesting a type of apoptosis was induced. In all, the above results indicated that Abi-DZ-1 had mitochondria-targeting abilities and possessed effective anticancer activity.

Another distinct advantage of Abi-DZ-1 was its dual function for cancer imaging and therapy. In in vivo imaging, Abi-DZ-1 gradually accumulated in tumor tissues, but Abi-783 deviated from tumor sites, confirming the preferential accumulation of Abi-DZ-1 in PCa tissues. Moreover, we showed that Abi-DZ-1 accurately targeted orthotopic PCa tissue, which had been proven by the existence of PCa by H&E staining, demonstrating the potential of Abi-DZ-1 for the real-time monitoring of therapeutic effects and the visualization of tumor boundaries during surgery.

Although the excellent anticancer ability of Abi-DZ-1 was demonstrated in both PCa cells and the CDX model, the treatment of CRPC is a major challenge in the management of PCa. We further established the PDX model B45354 to accurately mimic the main clinical manifestations of CRPC, which have been characterized by hormone dependence and castration resistance.33,41 In this model, Abi-DZ-1 more significantly restricted tumor volumes within a specific range and induced more tumor cell apoptosis compared with Abi-783 or Abi. Thus, Abi-DZ-1 displayed great potential as an ideal theranostic agent to realize cancer imaging and therapy simultaneously. In addition, Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 did not show systemic toxicity at doses appropriate for imaging and treatment purposes by blood routine and liver function tests. Although both Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783 are built based on MHI-148, Abi-DZ-1 has the asymmetrical and amphipathic structure. According to the structure-inherent theory,25 the structure may help it bind closely to albumin proteins, forming dye–protein complexes by self-assembly in the plasma and facilitating its tumor-preferential accumulation through the permeability and retention effects. Therefore, Abi-DZ-1 may hold promise as a candidate multifunctional theranostic agent for future tumor-targeted imaging and precision therapy.

Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a mitochondria-targeting near-infrared fluorophore with multifunctional therapeutic activities against PCa. The multifunctional small molecules were assembled by the tumor-targeting heptamethine moiety MHI-148 and the bioactive anticancer abiraterone (Abi) and named Abi-DZ-1 and Abi-783. These modifications allow small molecules to target cancer cells easily and enhance therapeutic effects via the OATP-mediated active transport. Because of the higher preferential tumor accumulation and antitumor capability of Abi-DZ-1, it could be applied as a potent theranostic agent for simultaneous NIRF imaging and therapy of PCa. Abi-DZ-1 retarded tumor growth in both a CDX model and a CRPC model. Our findings may present a practicable strategy to develop small-molecule-based theranostic agents to improve therapeutic outcomes and reduce disease recurrence.

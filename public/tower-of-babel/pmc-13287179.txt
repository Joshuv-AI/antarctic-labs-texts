Clinical Outcomes in Concurrent Cohorts of Elexacaftor/Tezacaftor/Ivacaftor (ELX/TEZ/IVA)-Treated versus ELX/TEZ/IVA-Ineligible Individuals with Cystic Fibrosis in the US during COVID-19
Authors: Christian A Merlo, Hela Romdhani, Maral DerSarkissian, Aruna Muthukumar, Lisa J McGarry, Yiyue Lou, Kate Van Brunt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe initial US launch of elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ELX/TEZ/IVA) coincided with the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, altering healthcare-seeking behaviors and possibly resulting in decreased respiratory infection transmission. We evaluated real-world effectiveness of ELX/TEZ/IVA, accounting for potential impact of COVID-19 by comparing outcomes in people with cystic fibrosis (CF) treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA with ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible people with CF.MethodsThis retrospective, observational study used US CF Foundation Patient Registry data (2017–2021) of people with CF aged ≥ 12 years initiating ELX/TEZ/IVA between Q4 2019 and Q3 2021 and a concurrent comparator cohort ineligible for ELX/TEZ/IVA. Primary outcomes (follow-up through 2021 or death) included change in percent predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 s (ppFEV1) from baseline over follow-up, annualized rate of ppFEV1 decline, rate of pulmonary exacerbations (PEx), time to lung transplantation, and overall survival.ResultsppFEV1 increased in ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated individuals through follow-up but decreased in the comparator cohort (+6.20 versus −3.13 percentage points, respectively; mean difference 9.34, 95% confidence interval [CI] 8.84 to 9.85). The annualized rate of ppFEV1 change was −0.03 (95% CI −0.18 to 0.07) in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort and −2.24 (95% CI −2.66 to −1.80) in the comparator cohort (relative reduction in rate of decline of 99%; 95% CI 92–103%). Rates of PEx requiring intravenous antibiotics or hospitalization were 0.17 and 0.77 among ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts, respectively (77% reduction, 95% CI 74–81%). Proportion undergoing lung transplant was 0.22% and 2.94% in ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts, respectively (90% reduction, 95% CI 29–98%). All-cause mortality was 0.76% and 3.45% in ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts, respectively (79% reduction; 95% CI 56–88%).ConclusionsDuring COVID-19, ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible people with CF experienced lung function decline and higher rates of PEx, lung transplant, and death versus the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort, despite infection prevention measures, suggesting ELX/TEZ/IVA’s benefits remain despite the potential bias from protective effects of the pandemic.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s41030-026-00363-0.Keywords: COVID-19, Cystic fibrosis, Elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Efficacy and safety of elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ELX/TEZ/IVA) in people with cystic fibrosis (CF) have been demonstrated in multiple phase 3 trials and real-world evidence studies.The initial launch of ELX/TEZ/IVA in the US coincided with the COVID-19 pandemic, which altered health-seeking behaviors and possibly resulted in reduced respiratory infection transmission.Questions have been raised as to whether the infection prevention measures put in place during the COVID-19 pandemic may have biased results observed in real-world studies of ELX/TEZ/IVA; this study examined the clinical benefits of ELX/TEZ/IVA during the pandemic by comparing outcomes with a concurrent comparator cohort of people with CF ineligible for ELX/TEZ/IVA.What was learned from the study?Despite possible reduced respiratory infections during COVID-19, the ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible and untreated comparator group showed greater lung function decline, and higher rates of pulmonary exacerbations, lung transplant, and death compared with ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated individuals.This study used a concurrent comparator cohort to address concerns associated with the use of historical controls in previous ELX/TEZ/IVA research studies.Overall, this study suggests that the benefits of ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment on pulmonary, transplant, and mortality outcomes may be robust to potential bias from protective effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Introduction

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a rare inherited genetic disorder caused by mutations in the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) gene [1, 2]. These mutations lead to the production of abnormally thick mucus, which obstructs airways and glands and results in dysfunction across multiple organ systems, including pulmonary, gastrointestinal, and genitourinary systems [3]. Pulmonary complications are the primary cause of death among people with CF [4].

Therapeutic agents developed to treat the underlying cause of CF include CFTR modulators (CFTRm) ivacaftor (IVA), lumacaftor (LUM), tezacaftor (TEZ), and elexacaftor (ELX) [5, 6]. These have been approved for use in combination to treat specific subsets of people with CF with the F508del-CFTR mutation or a mutation in the CFTR gene that is responsive based on in vitro data [7]. Efficacy and safety of the triple combination ELX/TEZ/IVA in people with CF were demonstrated in several phase 3 trials [8–11]. Moreover, real-world evidence has demonstrated the benefit of ELX/TEZ/IVA in improving lung function, reducing pulmonary exacerbations (PEx) and hospitalizations, and lowering rates of lung transplant and death relative to historical data [12]. Open-label extension studies show, on average, no decline in percent predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 s (ppFEV1) over up to a 4-year follow-up period in patients treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA; this is in contrast to a 1–3 percentage point annual decline seen in natural history studies and in an ELX/TEZ/IVA-untreated historical control group from the US Cystic Fibrosis Foundation Patient Registry (CFFPR) [8, 12, 13]. The initial availability of ELX/TEZ/IVA in the US (October 2019) [7, 14] coincided with the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, which altered healthcare-seeking behaviors and possibly resulted in decreased transmission of respiratory infections [15, 16]. To account for the potential impact of COVID-19 on the real-world effectiveness of ELX/TEZ/IVA, this study assessed the clinical benefit of ELX/TEZ/IVA during the COVID-19 pandemic versus a concurrent comparator cohort of people with CF ineligible for ELX/TEZ/IVA, such that both cohorts would be similarly impacted by the pandemic.

Methods

Study Design

This retrospective, noninterventional, longitudinal study using US CFFPR data [17] (2017–2021) compared clinical outcomes in people with CF aged ≥ 12 years who were eligible for and treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA with a concurrent comparator cohort ineligible for and not treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA. The study protocol and other necessary documents were reviewed and approved by the Advarra Institutional Review Board (IRB) and the US CFFPR Protocol Review Committee before initiation of the analyses. Determination of exempt status was received in June 2022 by the IRB, and the study was approved by the US CFFPR Committee in January 2023. The study was conducted in accordance with legal and regulatory requirements, as well as with scientific purpose, value, and rigor, and followed generally accepted research practices described in Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices (GPP) issued by the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology (ISPE) and Good Research Practices for Outcomes Research issued by the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR).

Index dates were defined as ELX/TEZ/IVA initiation date (Q4: 2019 to Q3: 2021) in the treated cohort; ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible comparator cohort index dates were selected from available healthcare encounters to mirror treated cohort index distribution by year/quarter to balance timing relative to COVID-19. Study design, including cohort mean follow-up and index date distribution for ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and concurrent comparator cohorts, is shown in Fig. 1. The study period included 2-year baseline pre-index (inclusive) and post-index (exclusive) follow-up through 2021 (end of data availability) or death. People with CF in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort were followed from index date until the end of data availability or evidence of ELX/TEZ/IVA discontinuation; other censoring events are described below. Discontinuation was based on encounter and drug exposure data and determined from two sequential encounters at least 90 days apart where ELX/TEZ/IVA use was not recorded. The second date on which ELX/TEZ/IVA use was not recorded confirmed discontinuation and defined the ELX/TEZ/IVA discontinuation date.

Study Population

A flow diagram of cohort selection is shown in Fig. 2. The treated cohort included individuals with a clinical diagnosis of CF who had ≥ 1 copy of F508del-CFTR mutation and initiated ELX/TEZ/IVA from 21 October 2019 through November 2020 or ≥ 1 copy of the F508del-CFTR mutation or a mutation in the CFTR gene that was responsive based on in vitro data and initiated ELX/TEZ/IVA from December 2020 through September 2021 to correspond with US regulatory approvals. For the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort, the index date was defined as the ELX/TEZ/IVA start date recorded in the annual form, if it fell on an encounter date. If the ELX/TEZ/IVA start date did not fall on an encounter date, then the date of the closest prior encounter was used as the index date. If the ELX/TEZ/IVA start date was not available, then the index date was defined as the date of the encounter prior to the encounter in which ELX/TEZ/IVA use was first recorded in the encounter form, to minimize misclassification of treated time during the baseline period. In addition, people with CF in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort were required to have no evidence of continued use of other CFTRm from the index date to two encounters after the index date. The comparator cohort included people with a clinical diagnosis of CF without the presence of F508del-CFTR mutation or a mutation in the CFTR gene that was responsive based on in vitro data at the time of the study, and who never used ELX/TEZ/IVA. In addition, people with CF in the comparator cohort were required to have an index date between October 2019 and September 2021, inclusive. The index dates for people in the comparator cohort were randomly selected from available encounter dates such that the distribution index quarters in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort and the comparator cohort were similar. Following assignment of index dates, baseline characteristics were defined relative to the index date. For both the treated and comparator cohorts, people with CF were aged ≥ 12 years as of index date and had data available for ≥ 24 months prior to the index date.

Study Outcomes and Statistical Analysis

Primary outcomes included change from baseline in ppFEV1 over follow-up, the annualized rate of ppFEV1 decline, rate of PEx, time to lung transplantation, and overall survival. Mean ppFEV1 during baseline and follow-up were calculated as the average of the best available (i.e., highest value) quarterly ppFEV1 values over year 2 of the baseline period and follow-up period (excluding month 1 post-index date). Mean differences in change from baseline in ppFEV1 were estimated through linear regression. ELX/TEZ/IVA’s relative effect on the annualized rate of change in ppFEV1 was calculated by computing the relative difference of the estimated annualized rate of change in ppFEV1 between the treated and comparator cohorts and then dividing the difference by the annualized rate of change in the comparator cohort [8, 13]. For the rate of change analysis, all valid ppFEV1 measures during follow-up period were used except for ppFEV1 measures that occurred in the first month following the index date to avoid values related to the initial acute increase in ppFEV1 associated with ELX/TEZ/IVA. All ppFEV1 measurements were included if they met registry validity criteria, including those that may have occurred during periods of pulmonary exacerbation or hospitalization. The annualized rate of change in ppFEV1 for each cohort was estimated using mixed models with unstructured covariance structure. The models included a random intercept and a random slope for each individual, and fixed effects for ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment, follow-up time, and ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment by follow-up time interaction term. PEx were defined as episodes requiring home intravenous (IV) antibiotics or hospitalizations for PEx during the follow-up period. PEx rate ratio (RR) was estimated using generalized linear models (GLM) with Poisson distribution. Cox proportional hazard models were utilized to estimate hazard ratios (HR) for lung transplantation and death. Treatment-by-time interaction was included, if necessary, on the basis of inspection of Schoenfeld residuals to account for deviations from the proportional hazard assumption. Exploratory outcomes included the rate of hospitalizations and prevalence of microorganisms. The rate of hospitalizations was also estimated using GLM with Poisson distribution. Prevalence of microorganisms, defined as the proportion of people with CF with ≥ 1 positive culture among those with ≥ 1 culture, was assessed at 12 months of follow-up in both cohorts and reported descriptively using frequencies and percentages.

For all analyses, 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using a nonparametric bootstrap procedure with 999 replications, to account for the standardized mortality ratio (SMR) weighting. All analyses were performed using Statistical Analysis Software (SAS) Enterprise Guide version 7.1 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Standardized Mortality Ratio Weighting

Baseline characteristics and disease severity may differ between the treated cohort and the ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible cohort, as people with CF have different CFTR mutations that determine treatment eligibility and may lead to different clinical phenotypes. In addition, because > 90% of the people with CF aged ≥ 12 years were eligible for ELX/TEZ/IVA during the study period, it was important to preserve sampling among the ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible cohort. To adjust for potential confounding factors between the two study cohorts, SMR weighting [18] was used to reweight the ELX/TEZ/IVA-ineligible comparator cohort to be representative of ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort’s demographic and baseline clinical characteristics. These baseline characteristics were defined relative to the assigned index dates prior to SMR weighting.

SMR weighting reweights the comparator cohort to be representative of the treated cohort and thereby estimates the average treatment effect among the treated population (i.e., the average difference that would be found if all people with CF in the treated cohort received treatment versus if none received treatment). SMR weights were generated on the basis of propensity score (PS) values [19]. The PS was defined as the probability of receiving ELX/TEZ/IVA versus not receiving ELX/TEZ/IVA, conditional on observed baseline characteristics selected on the basis of clinical significance. The baseline characteristics included age at index date; sex; race; ethnicity; average of best-available ppFEV1 in each quarter over the second year of the baseline period; change in ppFEV1 over the baseline period; number of PEx in each baseline year; year and quarter of index date; health insurance type; annual number of healthcare utilization visits; average body mass index (BMI)/BMI z-scores; prevalence of microorganisms; and medication use and region of residence. Genotype and prior CFTRm exposure were not considered as these were different in the two cohorts by design. Baseline data covering 2 years were used to calculate lung function variables (ppFEV1 and PEx) to better account for disease trajectory, while all other variables were evaluated over the year prior to and including the index date. For variables with missing values, missing data categories were created and incorporated into the PS model.

Individuals in the treated cohort were assigned a weight of 1, while individuals in the comparator cohort were assigned a weight equal to the ratio of the estimated PS/(1 − PS). Standardized differences were calculated to evaluate the comparability of baseline characteristics, with a difference < 10% indicating that characteristics were well-balanced by SMR weighting [20]. Unbalanced characteristics after SMR weighting were included in the regression analysis to control for residual confounding. The SMR-weighted comparator cohort was used in all analyses [19].

Results

Patient Demographics and Baseline Disease Characteristics

The study included 13,414 and 1600 people with CF (pre-SMR weighting) in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts, respectively (mean follow-up: 1.83 [SD: 0.46] years in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort and 1.76 [SD: 0.54] years in the comparator cohort) (Supplementary Table S1). SMR weighting reweighted people with CF in the comparator cohort (weighted N = 13,176) to be similar to those in the treated cohort (N = 13,414). After SMR weighting, baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were well balanced between cohorts, including age at index date, race, ethnicity, year and quarter of index date, region of residence, and decline in ppFEV1 during baseline (Table 1). Furthermore, final distributions of index dates by quarter indicated that cohorts were concurrent (standardized differences < 10%) (Table 1 and Fig. 1) to ensure that any impact of COVID-19 on clinical outcomes would be similar between the two cohorts.

Change from Baseline and Annualized Rate of Change in ppFEV1

ppFEV1 improved from baseline over follow-up among the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort but worsened in the comparator cohort (mean +6.20 percentage points versus −3.13; mean difference 9.34 [95% CI 8.84 to 9.85]). The annualized rate of ppFEV1 change was −0.03 (95% CI −0.18 to 0.07) in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort (estimated on the basis of a mean of 5.68 measurements per patient) and −2.24 (95% CI −2.66 to −1.80) in the comparator cohort (estimated on the basis of a mean of 7.92 measurements per patient), resulting in a 99% reduction in the rate of ppFEV1 decline compared with the comparator cohort over follow-up (95% CI 92–103%) (Fig. 3).

All encounter dates (and associated ppFEV1 measures) were available as month/year and assumed to occur on 15th of the month. Rate of PEx and Hospitalizations

Rates (per person-year) of PEx requiring IV antibiotics or hospitalization were 0.17 and 0.77 among the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts, respectively, resulting in a 77% lower rate among the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort (RR: 0.23 [95% CI 0.19 to 0.26]; Table 2). Similarly, the rate was 82% and 78% lower in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort for PEx-related home IV antibiotic use (RR: 0.18 [95% CI 0.14 to 0.24]) and PEx-related hospitalizations (RR: 0.22 [95% CI 0.19 to 0.26]).

Prevalence of Respiratory Microorganisms

Among individuals having ≥ 1 culture collected during the 12 months post-index date (82.73% and 83.09% in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts, respectively), people with CF in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort had numerically lower prevalence of all assessed respiratory microorganisms versus the comparator cohort (Pseudomonas aeruginosa [34% versus 50%], methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus [MSSA] [41% versus 47%], methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus [MRSA] [21% versus 27%], and Burkholderia cepacia complex [2% versus 4%]; Fig. 5).

Discussion

ELX/TEZ/IVA was first approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in October 2019 [7], just months before the World Health Organization declared the COVID-19 pandemic. The widespread infection prevention and social distancing measures implemented during the pandemic may have contributed to improved clinical outcomes in real-world studies of ELX/TEZ/IVA in people with CF independent of therapeutic effects [12, 16, 21]. By comparing outcomes among people with CF treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA with a concurrent comparator cohort of those ineligible for ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment, this study ensured comparison of people with CF who were similarly impacted by the pandemic, allowing for an unbiased assessment of the clinical benefit of ELX/TEZ/IVA during the COVID-19 pandemic in a real-world setting.

Patients treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA demonstrated significant improvements in ppFEV1 from baseline relative to a worsening in ppFEV1 seen in the comparator cohort (mean difference of 9.34 percentage points). Additionally, a significant decline in lung function was observed in the concurrent comparator cohort while the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort, on average, did not show any decline in lung function. These findings are consistent with prior analyses of ELX/TEZ/IVA’s impact on rate of lung function change, including a prior analysis comparing people with CF from two ELX/TEZ/IVA phase 3 trials with a historical (pre-COVID-19) matched group of people with CF from the US CFFPR who had the same genotypes as the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohorts but were ineligible for treatment with a CFTRm during the time period examined. This prior study also found, on average, no decline in lung function among those treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA, while the historical matched controls had a significant decline in lung function [13]. The decline in lung function seen in the concurrent cohort of this current study and the historical cohort of the prior study is similar in magnitude (−2.24 versus −2.32), thus reinforcing the findings of this prior study and supporting its methodology, despite their use of historical controls that could not account for pandemic-related effects. Of note, findings from this concurrent comparator study contrast with an Australian registry analysis, which demonstrated improved lung function during the COVID-19 pandemic among people with CF, most of whom were CFTRm-naïve [22]. The Australian analysis suggests that public health measures implemented for COVID-19 may have been more effective in Australia than in the US, potentially conferring benefits to the Australian CF population [23]. This may be due to differences in how public health measures were enforced or how the public adhered to these measures, suggesting that findings from this Australian analysis may not be generalizable to other countries.

Compared with the concurrent comparator cohort, the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort in this study showed a 77% lower rate of PEx requiring IV antibiotic use or hospitalization. Furthermore, ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment was associated with a 90% lower rate of lung transplantation and a 79% lower rate of death relative to the concurrent comparator cohort. These findings are consistent with a prior real-world, registry-based study evaluating clinical outcomes among people with CF before and after treatment with ELX/TEZ/IVA, which reported a 79% reduction in the mean number of PEx in year 2 of treatment compared with the pretreatment period [12]. The prior registry study also reported 85% and 72% reductions in lung transplantation and risk of death, respectively, among people with CF compared with a historical cohort of people with CF aged ≥ 12 years with ≥ 1 F508del-CFTR allele from 2019, who would not have been impacted by the pandemic [12]. The results seen in this current study are consistent with findings from prior studies using alternative comparator groups and methodologies.

To address potential survival bias related to censoring the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort at treatment discontinuation, we conducted a sensitivity analysis for the lung transplantation outcome in which censoring at ELX/TEZ/IVA discontinuation was removed. Treatment discontinuation occurred in a small proportion of treated patients (~5%). In this sensitivity analysis, the number of lung transplantation events in the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort increased by one patient; however, the overall event proportion and estimated HR remained unchanged (0.22% and 0.10, respectively). These findings suggest that censoring at treatment discontinuation did not influence the lung transplantation outcomes.

In addition to these improvements, people with CF treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA in this current study had numerically lower rates of hospitalization and a lower prevalence of respiratory microorganisms compared with the concurrent comparator cohort. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating a reduction in hospitalization rates, use of IV antibiotics, and prevalence of respiratory bacterial pathogens following ELX/TEZ/IVA initiation [12, 24, 25]. This consistency across studies confirms that the observed benefits of ELX/TEZ/IVA are not artifacts of COVID-19 or limited to the brief post-ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment or pre-pandemic period.

This study had several strengths. First, the use of the US CFFPR registry, which includes > 80% of people with CF in the US [17], enhances the generalizability of the findings to the nationwide CF population. Second, SMR weighting was applied for differences in baseline characteristics between treated and comparator cohorts, minimizing confounding bias. Third, the use of a concurrent comparator cohort addresses concerns raised about the use of historical control groups from previous ELX/TEZ/IVA research studies [5, 7, 16]. Moreover, the use of concurrent controls is an intuitive method that provided the ability to distinguish the impact of ELX/TEZ/IVA from any potential impact attributable to the COVID-19 pandemic control measures. Limitations of this study include the imbalanced sample size between ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated and comparator cohorts before SMR weighting and the potential for residual confounding due to unobserved differences, particularly genotype-driven biological differences, despite SMR weighting yielding cohorts with balanced baseline characteristics. Distinct CFTR mutations may influence underlying disease trajectory, susceptibility to respiratory infections, or responsiveness to pandemic-related protective measures, which could affect outcomes independently following treatments. To account for this potential limitation, our analytic approach focused on adjustment for phenotype, rather than genotype alone, through detailed baseline characterization and SMR weighting. Further, there is evidence available demonstrating that individuals with the heterozygous F508del/minimal function (F/MF) mutation have similar annual rates of ppFEV1 decline and pulmonary exacerbations as those with minimal function/minimal function (MF/MF) genotypes in the absence of CFTRm, suggesting comparable disease trajectories between the two groups in this present study [26]. While these considerations may provide further support for this study’s methodology and interpretation, the potential for residual confounding cannot be fully excluded and should be considered when interpreting the magnitude of treatment outcomes.

Conclusions

Despite potential reduction in respiratory infection transmissions during COVID-19, concurrent comparators had significant lung function decline and several-fold higher rates of PEx, lung transplant, and death compared with the ELX/TEZ/IVA-treated cohort. This study used a concurrent comparator cohort to address concerns associated with the use of historical controls in prior ELX/TEZ/IVA research studies. These findings suggest that the benefits of ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment on pulmonary, lung transplant, and mortality outcomes may be robust to potential bias from protective effects of the COVID-19 pandemic response.

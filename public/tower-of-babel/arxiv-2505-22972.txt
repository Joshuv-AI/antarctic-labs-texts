Abstract
. Recently, Bansah and Sehba studied in [ 3 ] the boundedness of a family of Hilbert-type integral operators, in which they characterized the L p − L q L^{p}-L^{q} boundedness of the operators for 1 ≤ p ≤ q ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty . In this paper, we deal with the corresponding discrete Hilbert-type operators acting on the weighted sequence spaces. We establish some sufficient and necessary conditions for the l p − l q l^{p}-l^{q} boundedness of the operators for 1 ≤ p ≤ q ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty . We find out that the conditions of the boundedness of discrete Hilbert-type operators are different from those of the boundedness of Hilbert-type integral operators. Also, for some special cases, we obtain sharp norm estimates for discrete Hilbert-type operators.

Let
p\geq 1
, we denote the conjugate of
p
by
p^{\prime}
, i.e.
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. In particular,
p^{\prime}=\infty

p=1

\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)

\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the class of all real-valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}.
Let
L^{p}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the usual Lebesgue space on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, i.e.,

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\|f\|_{p}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}dx)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

When
p>1
. If
f\in L^{p},g\in L^{p^{\prime}}
, then we have the following famous Hilbert inequality

(1.1)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{x+y}dxdy\Big|\leq\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},

where the constant
\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})
in (
1.1
) is the best possible, see
[
19

) has the following equivalent form

(1.2)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(y)}{x+y}dy\right|^{p}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})\|f\|_{p}.

(
1.2
) is also called Hilbert inequality and the constant
\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})
in (
1.2
) is still the best possible.

The Hilbert inequality (

H

Hf(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(x)}{x+y}dx,\>f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\>y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
. Let
H
be as above. Then
H
is bounded on
L^{p}
and the norm
\|H\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}
of

is
\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})

\|H\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}=\sup_{f\in L^{p}}\frac{\|Hf\|_{p}}{\|f\|_{p}}.

There is a discrete version of this result. For a sequence
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\in l^{q}
, we define the discrete Hilbert operator
\mathcal{H}
as

{\mathcal{H}}a(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{m+n},\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Here

is the space of sequences of real numbers, i.e.,

l^{p}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{p}=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

We have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>1
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be as above. Then
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
l^{p}
and the norm

is
\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{l^{p}\rightarrow l^{p}}=\sup_{a\in l^{p}}\frac{\|\mathcal{H}a\|_{p}}{\|a\|_{p}}.

Several lemmas

In this section, to prove our main results of this paper, we recall some known lemmas and establish some new ones.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
Let
\tau,\lambda
be real numbers and
y>0
be fixed. Then

I(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\tau}}{(x+y)^{\lambda}}dx<\infty

if and only if
\tau>-1
and
\lambda-\tau-1>0
. Moreover, when
\tau>-1
and
\lambda-\tau-1>0
, we have

I(y)=y^{\tau+1-\lambda}B(\tau+1,\lambda-\tau-1).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tau,\lambda
be real numbers and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Then

S(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}<\infty

if and only if
\lambda-\tau-1>0
. Moreover, when
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
-1<\tau\leq 0
, we have

S(n)\leq n^{\tau+1-\lambda}B(\tau+1,\lambda-\tau-1),

when
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
\tau>0
, we have

S(n)\leq Cn^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

Here,
C
is a positive constant which is independent of
n
.

Proof.

First, we note that, for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{m^{\tau-\lambda}}\cdot\frac{m^{\tau}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}\rightarrow 1,\,\,{\text{as}}\,\,m\rightarrow\infty.

Then we conclude that
S(n)<\infty
if and only if
\lambda-\tau>1
.

On the other hand, when
\lambda-\tau>1
, for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the function

k_{n}(x):=\frac{x^{\tau}}{(x+n)^{\lambda}},\,x>0.

We see from the fact

\displaystyle[k_{n}(x)]^{\prime}=\frac{d}{dx}\Big[\frac{x^{\tau}}{(x+n)^{\lambda}}\Big]=\frac{x^{\tau-1}}{(x+n)^{\lambda+1}}[\tau n+(\tau-\lambda)x],

that, if
-1<\tau\leq 0
, then
[k_{n}(x)]^{\prime}\leq 0
for all
x\in(0,\infty)
. Consequently, we obtain from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\tau}}{(x+n)^{\lambda}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{\tau+1-\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\tau}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}dt=n^{\tau+1-\lambda}B(\tau+1,\lambda-\tau-1).

If
\tau>0
. We let
\tau=\omega+\tau^{\prime}
with
\tau^{\prime}\in(-1,0]
, then
0<\tau\leq\omega
and
\lambda-\omega>0
. It follows from

\begin{cases}[k_{n}(x)]^{\prime}\leq 0,\,\,\,{\text{when}}\,\,\,x\in[\frac{\tau}{\lambda-\tau}n,+\infty),\\
[k_{n}(x)]^{\prime}\geq 0,\,\,\,{\text{when}}\,\,\,x\in(0,\frac{\tau}{\lambda-\tau}n],\end{cases}

that

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{T}\frac{m^{\tau}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}+\sum_{m=T+1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T\frac{(\frac{\tau n}{\lambda-\tau})^{\tau}}{(\frac{\tau n}{\lambda-\tau}+n)^{\lambda}}+\sum_{m=T+1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}n^{\tau+1-\lambda}+\sum_{m=T+1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}.

Here
T=[\frac{a}{\lambda-a}n]+1
and

C_{1}=\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-\tau}\Big)^{\tau}\Big(\frac{\tau}{\lambda-\tau}\Big)^{1-\lambda}.

Consequently, we obtain that

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}n^{\tau+1-\lambda}+\sum_{m=T+1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau^{\prime}}}{(m+n)^{\lambda-\omega}}\Big(\frac{m}{m+n}\Big)^{\omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}n^{\tau+1-\lambda}+\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\tau^{\prime}}}{(x+n)^{\lambda-\omega}}dx.

Then we see from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}n^{\tau+1-\lambda}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\tau^{\prime}}}{(x+n)^{\lambda-\omega}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}n^{\tau+1-\lambda}+n^{\tau^{\prime}+1-\lambda+\omega}B(\tau^{\prime}+1,\lambda-\omega-\tau^{\prime}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[C_{1}+B(\tau^{\prime}+1,\lambda-\tau-1)]n^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

When
\lambda-\tau-1>0
, we note that
[k_{n}(x)]^{\prime}\leq 0
for all
x\in(|\frac{\tau}{\lambda-\tau}|n,\infty).

Then it is not hard to see that, for fixed

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\lambda,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\alpha}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}\leq Cn^{\beta},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\lambda\geq\alpha-\beta
,
\lambda\geq\alpha
and
\lambda\geq-\beta.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that, if
\lambda\geq\alpha-\beta
,
\lambda\geq\alpha
and
\lambda\geq-\beta
, then

(2.1)

\sup_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\alpha}n^{-\beta}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}<\infty.

{\bf Case\,\,1.}
If
\alpha\geq 0
and
-\beta\geq 0
. We let
\Delta=\lambda-\alpha+\beta
, then
\Delta\geq 0
and

\sup_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\alpha}n^{-\beta}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}=\sup_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{m}{m+n}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{n}{m+n}\Big)^{-\beta}\frac{1}{(m+n)^{\Delta}}\leq 2^{-\Delta}.

{\bf Case\,\,2.}
If
\alpha<0
, we let
m=kn
, then

\displaystyle\frac{m^{\alpha}n^{-\beta}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}=\frac{k^{\alpha}}{(k+1)^{\lambda}}\cdot n^{\alpha-\beta-\lambda}.

We consider the function

g(x):=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{(x+1)^{\lambda}},\,x>0.

We see from the fact

\displaystyle[g(x)]^{\prime}=\frac{d}{dx}\Big[\frac{x^{\alpha}}{(x+1)^{\lambda}}\Big]=\frac{x^{\alpha-1}}{(x+1)^{\lambda+1}}[\alpha+(\alpha-\lambda)x],

that, when
\alpha<0
, we have
[g(x)]^{\prime}\leq 0
for all
x\in(0,\infty)
. On the other hand, since
k\geq\frac{1}{n}
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{k^{\alpha}}{(k+1)^{\lambda}}\cdot n^{\alpha-\beta-\lambda}\leq\frac{n^{-\alpha}}{(n^{-1}+1)^{\lambda}}\cdot n^{\alpha-\beta-\lambda}=\frac{n^{-\beta}}{(n+1)^{\lambda}}<\infty.

{\bf Case\,\,3.}
If
-\beta<0
, we let
n=km
, by the similar way as in
{\bf Case\,\,2}
, we can prove that (
2.1
) still holds.
The lemma is proved.
∎

The following lemma provides the necessary conditions for the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers and
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as in (
1.4
). If
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from

l_{\alpha}^{p}
to
l_{\beta}^{q}
, then we have

\begin{cases}\lambda\geq\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{q}-\frac{\alpha+1}{p},\\
\beta+1<q(\lambda-\nu),\\
\alpha+1>p(\mu+1-\lambda).\end{cases}

Proof.

We suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
l_{\alpha}^{p}
to
l_{\beta}^{q}
. Take
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
with
a_{1}=1
and
a_{m}=0
for
m\geq 2.
Then we see that
\|a\|_{p,\alpha}=1,
and

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a(n)=\frac{n^{\nu}}{(1+n)^{\lambda}},

so that

\|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a\|_{q,\beta}^{q}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{q\nu+\beta}}{(1+n)^{q\lambda}}<\infty.

This implies that
q\lambda-q\nu-\beta>1.
That is

\beta+1<q(\lambda-\nu).

On the other hand, we know from the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
that its adjoint operator
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
l_{\beta}^{q^{\prime}}
to
l_{\alpha}^{p^{\prime}}
. A simple computation shows that the adjoint operator
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is given by

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\nu+\beta}n^{\mu-\alpha}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}a_{m},\,\,a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Take
a
as above, we obtain that

\|\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a\|_{p^{\prime},\alpha}^{p^{\prime}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{p^{\prime}(\mu-\alpha)+\alpha}}{(1+n)^{p^{\prime}\lambda}}<\infty.

Hence we have

p^{\prime}\lambda-p^{\prime}(\mu-\alpha)-\alpha>1.
This is equivalent to

\alpha+1>p(\mu+1-\lambda).

Now, we let

\rho:=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{q}-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\lambda.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we take
a_{\varepsilon}:=\{a_{n}^{\varepsilon}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with

(2.2)

a_{n}^{\varepsilon}=n^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}},n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\alpha}^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\varepsilon}<1+\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\varepsilon}dx=1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

It follows from the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a_{\varepsilon}(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\mu}n^{\nu}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}n^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}<\infty.

Consequently, from Lemma
2.2
, we know that

\displaystyle\lambda-\mu+\frac{\alpha+1}{p}+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}>1,

for any
\varepsilon>0
. On the other hand, we take

{\bf M}:=\Big|\frac{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1+\varepsilon}{p}}{\lambda-\mu+\frac{\alpha+1+\varepsilon}{p}}n\Big|+1.

Then, in view of Remark
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\beta}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\beta}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\mu}n^{\nu}}{(m+n)^{\lambda}}m^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\right]^{q}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\beta}\left[\int_{\bf M}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\mu}n^{\nu}}{(x+n)^{\lambda}}x^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}dx\right]^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\frac{q}{p}\varepsilon+q\rho}\left[\int_{\frac{\bf M}{n}}\frac{t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{q}.

Meanwhile, for all
n\geq 1
, we have

\frac{\bf M}{n}\leq\Big|\frac{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1+\varepsilon}{p}}{\lambda-\mu+\frac{\alpha+1+\varepsilon}{p}}\Big|+\frac{1}{n}\leq\Big|\frac{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1+\varepsilon}{p}}{\lambda-\mu+\frac{\alpha+1+\varepsilon}{p}}\Big|+1:={\bf M}_{1}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\beta}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\frac{q}{p}\varepsilon+q\rho}\left[\int_{{\bf M}_{1}}\frac{t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{q}.

Thus, if
\rho>0
, when
\varepsilon<q\rho
, we obtain that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\frac{q}{p}\varepsilon+q\rho}=\infty,

so that
\|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\beta}^{q}=\infty
, which contradicts to the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
. This implies that
\rho\leq 0
. That is

\lambda\geq\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{q}-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}.

The lemma is now proved.
∎

3.1.
Proof of Theorem

For the sake of simplicity, we will write

Note that the necessity of the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}

Then
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded
l_{\alpha}^{p}
to
l_{\beta}^{q}

), we see that

(3.3)

s>1

\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}=1

3.2

A

(3.4)

(3.6)

\begin{cases}\frac{\mu}{s}p^{\prime}+p^{\prime}A>-1,\\

(3.7)

\begin{cases}\frac{\nu}{t}q+B+\beta>-1,\\

with
a_{m}\geq 0
for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a(n)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[k(m,n)]^{\frac{1}{s}}m^{A}\cdot[k(m,n)]^{\frac{1}{t}}m^{-A}a_{m}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a(n)

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[k(m,n)]^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{s}}m^{p^{\prime}A}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big\{[k(m,n)]^{\frac{p}{t}}m^{-pA}a_{m}^{p}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{B+\beta}\Big\{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[k(m,n)]^{\frac{p}{t}}m^{-pA}a_{m}^{p}\Big\}^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

) and again Lemma

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{3}\leq{\bf C}_{2}m^{\frac{\nu}{t}q+B+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}q+\frac{\mu}{t}q}.

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{2}=B\Big(\frac{\nu}{t}q+B+\beta+1,\frac{\lambda}{t}q-\frac{\nu}{t}q-B-\beta-1\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\beta}}{\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\varepsilon^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{p}}[1+o(1)]\int_{\frac{1}{{\bf N}}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{\bf N}p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}dt,\,\,{\text{a}s}\,\,\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

We let

\mathcal{S}_{{\bf N}}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:t\geq\frac{1}{{\bf N}}\}.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{{\bf N}}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{\bf N}p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{S}_{\bf N}}(t)t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{\bf N}p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}dt.

Note that, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{S}_{\bf N}}(t)t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{\bf N}p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\rightarrow\frac{t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}},\,\,{\text{a}s}\,\,{\bf N}\rightarrow\infty,

and
(\varepsilon^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{p}}[1+o(1)]\rightarrow 1
as
{\bf N}\rightarrow\infty.
Consequently, by Fatou’s lemma, we see from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\|_{l_{\alpha}^{p}\rightarrow l_{\beta}^{p}}\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\,dt=B\Big(\mu+1-\frac{1}{p}(\alpha+1),\nu+\frac{1}{p}(\beta+1)\Big).

This proves Theorem
1.10
.

Remark 6.1

Theorem 6.2

Let

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{q}-\frac{\alpha+1}{p},\\

in the proof of
Case
{\bf I}

Also, Theorem
1.4

Theorem 6.3
.

. Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu

.

.

. Let

Proof of Theorem

.

We only need to prove that, if

6.2
.

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\|_{L_{\alpha}^{1}\rightarrow L_{\beta}^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\mu-\alpha}y^{\nu+\beta}}{(x+y)^{\lambda}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\Big(\nu+\beta+1,\lambda-\nu-\beta\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\Big(\mu-\alpha,\nu+\beta+1\Big).

. To continue the proof, we have to use some similar arguments in the proof of

{\bf I}

.

, we use

We next prove that

For

Circular RNAs in tumorigenesis: an insight into recent research in biogenesis and targeted tumor therapy
Authors: Zahra Shafaghat, Pezhman Golshanrad, Saber Saharkhiz, Anna Leśniak, David Mikeladze, Nelly Datukishvili, Marzieh Ramezani Farani, Iraj Alipourfard
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCircular RNAs (circRNAs) are eukaryotic RNA molecules that are covalently closed and have the characteristics of high stability, tissue-specific expression, and cell-specific expression. The majority of circRNAs are produced from pre-mRNAs that also result in linear RNA, and they have expression patterns that are similar to those of their host mRNAs. Despite their abundance, circRNAs are typically expressed at lower levels than mRNAs. These molecules are often dysregulated in Cancer and play important roles during the beginning and progression of tumors, according to mounting data. Dysregulated circRNAs unique to Cancer may be used as biomarkers for cancer diagnosis and prognosis since certain circRNAs from cancer tissues have been shown to go through the bloodstream and be persistently present in sera. It has been discovered that circRNAs not only influence tumor immunity and play a major role in tumor indicators, tumor development, and prognosis, but they may also be expressed improperly in various tumors.

Introduction

About 75% of the human genome can be translated into RNA, according to extensive RNA profiling. Approximately 9% of the expressed genes in the heart create circRNAs, compared to 20% of the genes in the human brain [1]. Furthermore, the quantity of circRNAs is cell type-specific, as evidenced by the fact that they tend to express themselves at higher levels in low-proliferating cells like cardiomyocytes than in high-proliferating cells like liver cells [2]. Notably, circRNAs are found in most organisms, including archaea, plants, yeast, and the majority of metazoans, and they have undergone evolutionary conservation across species [3]. Only a small percentage of the mouse circRNAs were orthologous to the human ones, according to a comparison of circRNAs in different human and mouse data sets. This finding suggests that circRNA expression is species-specific. However, the precise nature of this species-specificity of circRNA expression and its underlying mechanism is still completely unknown [4]. Certain circular RNAs can be expressed differently at different stages of development. An experiment revealed that as people aged, the abundance of some circular RNAs varied [5].

CircRNAs either move through nuclear export or are released into the cytoplasm during cell division, where they are incredibly stable, most likely because of their resistance to RNA exonucleases and debranching enzymes [6]. They are capable of resisting the destruction caused by the RNA exonuclease because of the special covalent connection between the 5′ and 3′ ends. Moreover, the circRNAs are resistant to RNA debranching enzymes due to the lack of a 2′-5′ connection of an RNA lariat [7]. As a result, circRNAs are more stable than their linear RNA counterparts, and they have an average half-life of more than 48 hours, whereas mRNAs only survive 10 hours on average [1].

CircRNAs have the potential to be employed as predictive, diagnostic, and therapeutic biomarkers since they are easily found in the blood and are expressed differently in Cancer [8]. A significant number of circRNAs are expressed in blood at comparatively higher levels than their linear mRNAs, making circRNAs attractive as diagnostic tools for tracking the mechanisms of encoded genes that would otherwise be unavailable in cell-dependent assays [9] (Table 1). CircRnas serve as a miRNA sponge

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a broad category of noncoding small (21 nt) RNAs that inhibit the translation of mRNAs involved in a wide range of biological activities [6]. CircRNAs may “sponge up” miRNAs of a certain family, acting as competitive inhibitors that block the ability of the miRNA to bind its mRNA targets. This is possible because a sponge’s binding sites are unique to the miRNA seed region [16]. This mechanism allows circRNAs to modulate critical cancer-related processes, such as proliferation, apoptosis, invasion, migration, and drug resistance, positioning them as either oncogenic or tumor-suppressive molecules [17]. For instance, Hansen et al. demonstrated that CDR1as (ciRS-7) sponges miR-7, significantly inhibiting its activity in the mouse brain, particularly in neocortical and hippocampal neurons, suggesting tissue-specific regulatory roles [18]. However, conflicting findings exist regarding CDR1as expression levels across cancer types. While Xu et al. reported CDR1as upregulation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) promoting microvascular invasion [10], other studies note its variable expression in colorectal cancer, potentially due to differences in tumor microenvironment or detection methods [19].

CircRnas modulate the transcription of linear RNA and protein production

In addition to controlling miRNAs, circRNAs can base-pair with RNAs other than miRNAs or bind and sequester RNA-binding proteins (RBPs), leading to the creation of massive RNA-protein complexes (RPCs). These RPCs have the ability to control the variety of short RNAs, or RBPs, that can interact with their canonical linear RNA counterparts. Different from the roles played by their canonical counterparts, other circRNAs may encode proteins [6].

A distinct classification of circular RNAs, known as nuclear exon-intron circular RNAs (EIciRNAs), possesses the ability to engage with the machinery responsible for transcription. This particular form of circRNAs, which conserves certain intronic sequences derived from their linear gene counterparts, can establish interactions with the U1 component of the spliceosomal machinery, thereby enlisting RNA polymerase II to the promoter region of genes and subsequently heightening the expression of its designated target genes [20]. Since circRNAs do not include poly(A) tail structures or a 5′ cap, they were thought not to be translated via cap-dependent processes. CircRNAs are, therefore, often regarded as noncoding RNAs. However, according to a bioinformatics study, some circRNAs containing open reading frames (ORFs) or internal ribosome entry sites (IRES) may be able to facilitate translation [21,22].

IRESs are cis-acting RNA elements that enable cap-independent translation by directly recruiting ribosomes [23]. Functional IRESs have been identified within circRNAs, allowing their translation initiation [24]. Canonical IRESs serve as structural scaffolds recognized by eIF4G2 and the eIF3 complex, cooperating with eIF4A to recruit the 40S subunit and assemble the 43S pre-initiation complex [25,26]. Early studies showed that m6A methylation can promote cap-independent translation of linear mRNAs under stress [27]. Later work demonstrated that circRNAs are similarly methylated by METTL3/METTL14, with m6A influencing their translation, stability, and roles in innate immunity [28]. Recent studies show that the exon junction complex (EJC) can directly promote internal initiation of circRNA translation [29]. Once deposited on circRNAs, the EJC recruits eIF3 through interactions between eIF4A3 and eIF3G, acting as a scaffold that bridges the 40S ribosomal subunit to circRNAs and facilitates cap-independent translation initiation [24].

CircRnas in the regulation of cellular stress

To maintain the viability of living organisms, it is necessary to maintain homeostasis at the cellular level. CircRNAs regulate aspects of cell growth, apoptosis, immune response, and drug resistance, impacting cellular homeostasis both positively and negatively. Studies have shown that CDR1s potentially regulate p21-activated kinase 1 (PAK1), promote DNA repair, and prevent apoptosis [2,20].

Leung & Sharp [30] looked at how important miRNAs are in how cells respond to stress. This suggests that some circRNAs may control different stress response pathways by stopping miRNA activity. Two groundbreaking investigations in 2013 demonstrated that a circRNA known as CDR1as/ciRS-7, with over 70 predicted miR-7 binding sites, suppresses the function of miR-7 in the brain by acting as a miRNA sponge [18,31]. These consecutive investigations of CDR1as revealed the first biological role (i.e. miRNA sponge) for circRNAs [8].

CircRnas and tumor microenvironment maintenance

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a complicated structure consisting of stromal cells, extracellular matrix (ECM) elements, and exosomes. A vast network of interactions between tumor and stromal cells, particularly immune cells, is dynamically regulated by the TME. There are different types of immune cells in the stroma, such as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), endothelial cells, pericytes, and lymphocytes; natural killer (NK) cells; regulatory T cells (Tregs); tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs); and myeloid-derived suppressor cells. Additionally, chemokines, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPS), integrins, and other secreted molecules are present. Emerging evidence highlights circRNAs as key regulators of TME dynamics, often via the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA axis, despite earlier assumptions of limited interplay with lncRNAs and mRNAs [32,33] (Table 2). For example, Zhang et al. demonstrated that circRNAs like hsa_circ_0020397 in colorectal cancer enhance immune evasion by upregulating PD-L1 via miR-138 sponging, promoting tumor cell survival [34]. Conversely, CircARSP91 in hepatocellular carcinoma enhances NK cell cytotoxicity by targeting ULBP1, suggesting a tumor-suppressive role [38]. However, contradictory findings exist; for instance, some studies report inconsistent circRNA expression across tumor types, potentially due to TME heterogeneity or sequencing variability [38]. These discrepancies underscore the need for integrative analyses, as proposed by Song et al., to elucidate circRNA-TME interactions [52,53]. CircRnas mediate tumor immune surveillance

The relationship between miRNAs and tumor immunity is clearer than the relationship between lncRNAs and circRNAs. The close relationship between circRNAs and miRNAs can help circRNAs participate in antitumor immunity through the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA axis. circRNAs interact with miRNAs involved in tumor immunity [54]. Moreover, the plasma expression levels of circRNAs are closely related to the levels of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in the TME. Therefore, circRNAs have a high potential to regulate tumor immunity [55].

In recent years, circRNAs have been found to play a potential role in regulating tumor immunity. Highly produced circular RNAs in tumors can bind to proteins and miRNAs to control immune cell activity, or they can be packed in exosomes and delivered to immunocytes as tumor antigens to stimulate antitumor immunity [56]. 1. Exogenously purified circRNAs, acting as immune system antigens, have the potential to stimulate the activation of innate immunity through the RIG-I-mediated pathway in vitro. CircRNA-induced nucleic acid sensor RIG-I is a well-known innate immunity regulator, and RIG-I agonists have been shown to activate anticancer immune responses to fight tumors. Therefore, exogenous circRNAs entering tumor cells have the potential to affect RIG-I and activate antitumor immunity [57–59]. 1. Beyond their role as tumor antigens, abnormal circular RNAs (circRNAs) may also facilitate the translation of proteins or polypeptides by functioning as tumor antigens. Additionally, these abnormal circRNAs could engage in intercellular communication and interact with immune factors to modulate immune responses. For instance, the immune factors NF90 and NF110 can connect with intronic RNA pairs to generate circRNAs; however, during viral infections, these factors detach from circRNA-binding proteins and instead associate with viral mRNAs to influence antiviral immunity [60].

CircRnas and tumor angiogenesis

Angiogenesis influences tumor growth and development and provides a way for tumor cells to detach from the original lesion and metastasize distantly. Angiogenesis, a characteristic of Cancer, is essential for the growth and spread of tumors. Compared to veins in normal tissue, the tumor vasculature has an anomalous leaky shape and function, which creates a unique “hypoxia and malnutrition island” that promotes the growth of Cancer [56,61].

Tumors mainly occur due to mechanistic disorders, leading to the stimulation of original blood vessels by carcinogenic factors, including external carcinogenic factors and their oncogenes [62]. Tumor cells can secrete carcinogens in the tumor microenvironment. An important factor in tumor angiogenesis is vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). CircRNAs can target VEGF molecules to affect tumor angiogenesis [63]. Tumor formation is induced by overexpression of VEGFA, which also stimulates angiogenesis, EMT, and the Ras/ERK signaling cascade [64]. Circ-CSPP1 promotes VEGFA expression and promotes angiogenesis by targeting VEGFA [47]. The poor clinical prognosis of glioma patients was discovered to be strongly associated with the overexpression of circSCAF11 in glioma tissue specimens and cell lines. The molecular mechanism of circSCAF11 in the pathophysiological process of glioma formation was revealed by a study on the protein. Sponging miR-421, the upregulated molecule circSCAF11 increased SP1 expression, which in turn activated the transcription of VEGFA [39].

CircRnas regulate endothelial monolayer permeability

The circRNAs are potentially multipurpose small molecules because of their unique structures, which enable them to interact with proteins, RNA, and even DNA. Upon binding to these molecules, circRNAs combine to create the circRNA-DNA, circRNA-RNA, and circRNA-protein interaction networks. Recent investigations have discovered the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory networks linked to the pathophysiology of endothelial dysfunction [65]. A molecular sponge, circ-DENND4C, was found to be abundant in glioma endothelial cells. It bound miR-577 and prevented it from negatively regulating the target genes ZO-1, Occludin, and Claudin-1, hence favorably regulating blood – tumor barrier (BTB) permeability [45].

In addition to stimulating angiogenesis, pancreatic cancer cells release circRNA, such as circ-IARS, into exosomes, increasing the permeability of the vessel wall and speeding up the spread of Cancer. Circ-IARS, which was overexpressed, sucked down miR-122 and stimulated the activity of the Ras homolog gene family, member A (RhoA). This prevented Zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) from acting as a tight junction ligand and increased the permeability of the endothelium monolayer, which in turn promoted cancer growth [44].

CircRNA and remodeling of the extracellular matrix

Cells produce ECM to meet their structural and metabolic needs. It also plays an important role in cell division, proliferation, and tissue homeostasis maintenance. The MMP protein family regulates collagen and fibronectin, the two most significant ECM proteins. According to recent research, circRNA misregulates the MMP family, causing the ECM to remodel. This promotes tumor cell invasion of the stroma and basement membrane, as well as vascular infiltration and metastasis [66,67].

Zou et al. ‘s functional enrichment study says that circRNA CDR1, which is also called ciRS-7, helps with binding integrins and collagen and changing the ECM. Additionally, they discovered that CDR1as mediated TME by acting as a regulator in the ECM-receptor interaction [68]. MMPs are essential for the abnormal degradation of the ECM and the malignant activity of cancer cells throughout the development of Cancer. Several tumor types exhibit elevated levels of MMPs [69]. In addition to hsa_circ_0008433, hsa_circ_0000096 was also discovered to control the expression of MMP-2 and MMP-9 in gastric Cancer. The clinical diagnostic relevance of hsa_circ_0000096 levels in gastric Cancer was demonstrated by their Correlation with many clinicopathological variables, such as TNM stage, invasion, and gender [47].

CircRnas and immune cells in TME

Over the past ten years, a novel therapeutic strategy called tumor immunotherapy has emerged, significantly altering the available cancer treatment options [70]. By impacting T, NK, macrophage, and other immune cell activity, circRNAs have significant effects on tumor immunotherapy. The key molecule involved in vascular mimicry (VM) is vascular endothelial (VE)-cadherin, which co-localizes with erythropoietin-producing hepatocellular receptor A2 (EphA2) at cell – cell junctions. This interaction activates focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and drives the cleavage of Laminin5γ2 into γ2′ and γ2x subunits [71,72]. CircRNAs that target VE-cadherin may modulate VM formation. For example, in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), androgen receptors directly bind the promoter of circRNA7, suppressing its expression and consequently inhibiting VM formation via the circRNA7–miR-7-5p – VE-cadherin signaling axis [73] (Table 3, Figure 2). The role of circRNAs in tumor immunity and immunotherapy is becoming clearer. They impair NK cells’ ability to eradicate tumors, hinder macrophages’ ability to eradicate tumors, and cause normal fibroblasts (NFs) to change into inflammatory cancer-associated fibroblasts (iCAFs). Additionally, circRNAs are crucial in influencing T cell activation, preventing T cell invasion, and encouraging T cell depletion. CircRnas impact natural killer cells’ capacity to kill tumors

CircRNAs can regulate two different aspects of NK cell antitumor activity: the expression of NK cell receptors on tumor cells and the production of cytokines by NK cells [83]. According to a study by Yuanming Ma et al. (2019), CircARSP91 can make hepatocellular carcinoma cells (HCC) more susceptible to NK cell cytotoxicity. These circRNAs can improve innate immune surveillance [38].

Tumor-produced circRNAs control the activity of NK cells. Through its action on RNA enzyme 1, androgen receptor (AR) upregulated adenosine to inosine, thereby suppressing circRNA expression in HCC in a differentiated manner (ADAR1). ADAR1 directly reduces RNA circularization, as observed with circARSP91. In HCC, CircARSP91 increased the cytotoxicity of NK cells, which improved innate immune surveillance. Increasing the activity of circARSP91, a repressor of HCC, was a powerful new therapeutic approach [84].

However, contrasting findings suggest that some circRNAs, such as circUHRF1, promote NK cell exhaustion in HCC, reducing anti-PD-1 therapy efficacy [85]. These divergent roles may stem from tumor-specific contexts or differences in circRNA detection methods, as noted by Kristensen et al. (2019) [15].

CircRnas prevent macrophages from destroying tumors

M1 and M2 macrophages displayed distinct circRNA expression patterns, as demonstrated by circRNA expression profiling, which provided new information on the role of circRNAs in macrophage polarization and differentiation [86].

Researchers are currently studying the effects of circRNAs on macrophages, primarily in HCC. If circRNAs contribute to M2 macrophage polarization, they promote tumor immune escape and, conversely, increase the antitumor effect. In HCC tissue, when hsa_circ_0074854 levels are lowered, HCC cells are stopped from migrating and invading by stopping exosomes from activating M2 macrophages [87]. Under hypoxic conditions in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), tumor cells significantly upregulate and enrich hsa-circ-0048117 in exosomes, contributing to M2 macrophage polarization [88].

CircRnas and myeloid-derived immune suppressor cells

TME includes myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSC), which have significant roles. Apart from their immunosuppressive properties, MDSC can also stimulate angiogenesis, invasion, and metastasis, all of which can lead to tumor growth. These are immature bone marrow-derived cells that repress T cells and become activated and deployed in diseased environments like Cancer. Their primary roles are to stimulate the growth of tumors and TAM transformation associated with tumors [89,90]. CircPTK2 and circHIPK3 are crucial for the differentiation of monocytic MDSCs into CD163+ M2 macrophages in lung cancer [91].

According to an experiment, circSLC8A1 may function as a sponge for miR-494, which is essential for MDSC migration into the tumor site and control of ARG1 and iNOS synthesis, boosting the tumor immune response. This data suggests that by regulating the immunological response mediated by MDSCs, circRNAs may be useful targets [92].

CircRnas convert fibroblasts to inflammatory cancer-associated fibroblasts

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are produced or differentiated from a variety of cell subpopulations in tumor tissues, including normal fibroblasts, mesothelial, endothelial, and bone marrow cells, to influence the incidence and progression of Cancer in a context-dependent manner. CAFs release various regulatory factors, generate and change the extracellular matrix, and control other cell types in TME through bidirectional cell contact. They function as a double agent in malignancies because of the intricate and subtle interactions that can have either stimulatory (such as increasing metastasis) or inhibitory effects on CAF cells and related cells [93–96].

Shangyou Zheng et al.‘s study revealed that circCUL2 enrichment in tumor tissues was significantly correlated with a poor prognosis for Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) patients. Mechanistically, circCUL2 acted as a ceRNA and modulated the miR-203a-3p/MyD88/NF-κB/IL6 axis, activating the STAT3 signaling pathway in pancreatic cancer cells to induce PDAC progression [97].

CircRnA and T cells in the tumor microenvironment

CD8+ Tumor-associated antigen (TAA) can activate T lymphocytes, which then attack tumor cells exclusively. Despite this, human malignancies continue to grow because T cells are unable to completely destroy the tumor due to many molecular and cellular pathways that promote tumor growth, such as T cell fatigue, anergy, senescence, and stemness. CircRNAs contribute to these networks of immunosuppression by preventing tumor escape and compromising regular T-cell function [56].

CircRNAs control the release of particular cytokines from CD4+ T cells, which influences the immune system’s response to malignancies. As an example, hsa_circ_0005519 stimulated the immune response by controlling hsa-let-7a-5p in CD4+ T cells, which may lead to the production of the cytokines IL-13 and IL-6 [98]. By sponging DNA methylation-related miRNAs in CD4+ T cells, CircRNA hsa_circ_0012919 aided in the DNA methylation of CD70, a member of the TNF family. The connection between CD27 and CD70 is crucial in mediating the immune response since it stimulates T and B cell activation, proliferation, and differentiation [99]. By targeting miR-448, circRNA circ-NT5C2 functioned as an oncogene in tumor proliferation and metastasis, ultimately suppressing the immune response. These findings imply that circ-NT5C2 suppression may enhance the immune system’s defenses against malignancies [100].

CircRnA and B cells in the tumor microenvironment

CircRNA can enhance the antibody response directly or indirectly. Zheng et al. analyzed the circRNA expression profile of chickens inoculated with Salmonella enterica serovar enteritidis (SE). The results revealed that a specific circRNA, of which NFATC2 was the parental gene, was related to B cell proliferation. CircRNA NC_006099.4:1 6,132,825|16236906 and circRNA NC_006099.4:15993284|16006290 mediated B cell proliferation through the Foxp1 pathway [101]. Weng et al. compared and analyzed the expression profile of circRNAs between the plasma of HCC patients with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and low TILs and identified that hsa_circ_0064428, which was significantly downregulated in HCC patients with high TILs, was negatively correlated with cancer prognosis. Given the evidence above, hsa_circ_0064428 might be a key regulator of TIL formation with the potential to be utilized in B cell-related therapy [102].

Circular RNAs in tumor immunotherapy

Cancer immunotherapy seeks to change the suppressive tumor microenvironment, boost immune system function, and stimulate the host’s antitumor immunological response to cure cancer [103]. CircRNAs can be used as tumor antigens, adjuvants for vaccines, or in conjunction with other molecules, such as proteins or miRNAs, in immunocytes to support tumor immunotherapy (Table 4). One strategy is to introduce circRNA, which functions as an antisense to reduce onco-miRNAs, into the targeted adversary, as some miRNAs are important regulators of carcinogenesis [114]. CircRNAs can enter cells using a variety of delivery methods, including viroids or exosomes. Current circular RNA in cancer therapy.

Although circRNAs have demonstrated considerable therapeutic potential in basic research, their translation into clinical applications remains challenging [116]. The current development of circRNA-based therapeutics is primarily constrained by three factors: the design and optimization of circRNA molecules, achieving efficient cyclization, and establishing robust chemical manufacturing and control (CMC) processes [117]. These technical limitations have hindered the progression of circRNA from experimental studies to clinical use. Nevertheless, ongoing advancements in molecular biology and production technologies are helping to overcome these barriers [116]. By refining circRNA design strategies, enhancing cyclization efficiency, and standardizing production protocols, circRNA therapeutics are expected to address current limitations and establish a strong foundation for clinical application. Such efforts will facilitate the broader implementation of circRNAs in disease treatment [118].

Immune checkpoint blockade therapy

Dysregulated lncRNAs and circRNAs, which function as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors, are becoming more and more evidently linked to the advancement of many malignancies. Although most circRNAs function as “miRNA sponges” to modify immunological checkpoints indirectly, they can also operate directly on them [119]. CircRNAs have been shown to control immunological checkpoint molecule production in tumor cells, which may allow circRNAs to reduce the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) as a treatment [70]. Furthermore, an increasing amount of research has demonstrated that several immune-related ceRNAs are found in the TME and that they have a major impact on the infiltration of immune cells and the response of cancer cells to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in many types of Cancer [120,121]. Checkpoint-blocking antibodies, such as anti-CTLA-4 antibodies, when used in combination, can help T cells identify tumor antigens and lessen tumors [103].

Under hypoxic circumstances, CircPRDM4 promotes the recruitment of HIF-1α onto the CD274 promoter and elevates PD-L1 expression, which leads to CD8+ T cell-mediated immune evasion. According to this, circPRDM4 is a potential onco-immune target for HCC [122]. Additionally, CircFGFR1 may increase NSCLC cells’ susceptibility to anti-PD-1 medications. Overexpression of circFGFR1 in xenograft mice has the potential to dramatically lower PD-1 in Lewis lung cancer (LLC) cells. When compared to the sham cell group, the tumor development in LLC-circFGFR1 cell recipient xenograft mice clearly showed phenotypic resistance to anti-PD-1 treatment, and the xenograft mice also had a reduced life duration [119].

NK cells overexpressing PD-1 can encourage the functional Dysregulation of activated NK cells in HCC patients. HCC cells’ exosomal circUHRF1 caused NK-cell fatigue and facilitated the anti-PD-1 treatment resistance [123,124]. In a different research, it was demonstrated that the proportion of NKG2D-positive cells in HCC patients resistant to anti-PD-1 therapy was much lower than in patients sensitive to the treatment. Furthermore, the outcomes of a subcutaneous implantation xenograft model demonstrated that circUHRF1 knockdown cells increased susceptibility to anti-PD-1 therapy and the overall survival rate [123].

In intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), circHMGCS1-016 and miR-1236-3p target CD73 and GAL-8. According to research, CD73 and GAL-8 proteins were shown to be strongly expressed in ICC tissues and to be negatively correlated with CD8+ T cells. Moreover, circHMGCS1-016 served as a sponge for miR-1236-3p and facilitated ICC development via miR-1236-3p/CD73 and GAL-8 [125].

CricRnas-based cancer vaccines

By expressing tumor antigens in vivo, the cancer vaccine triggers antitumor responses by presenting antigen-presenting cells and activating T cells that are specific to tumor antigens. The most promising kind of cancer vaccine currently is thought to be RNA-based, as it can quickly produce antigens in the cytoplasm for a strong immune response while lowering the danger of genome integration or T-cell tolerance [126]. Research has also shown how adaptable circRNA vaccines are, showing promise as an immune adjuvant to presently prescribed cancer medicines or as a stand-alone immune therapy intervention to impede the course of advanced malignancies (Figure 3). A tumor vaccination introduces tumor-specific antigens into the human body, which either stimulates the immune system or produces additional CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTL). Within the cell, the proteasome complex degrades the intracellular antigens into smaller peptide fragments, which are then presented to cytotoxic T cells (CD8+ T cells) by binding to MHC class I molecules located on the surface of nucleated cells. Conclusions and future perspective

Worldwide, researchers have become more interested in ncRNAs, particularly circRNAs. CircRNAs and linear ncRNAs have a high potential to act as tumor antigens or interact with mRNAs and proteins in immunocytes, yet their role in tumor immunity is unknown. CircRNAs may be able to control or regulate the amount of immune cell infiltration in tumors, according to mounting data that links them to immune cells inside the immunological milieu of the tumor. It is important to investigate the regulatory functions of circRNAs in cancer biology and treatment, particularly in tumor immunology and immunotherapy. In this review, we covered the interaction of circRNAs in immune cells and their function in immunotherapy. We suggest that circRNA-based therapeutics can aid in detecting and managing Cancer in light of all the available data.

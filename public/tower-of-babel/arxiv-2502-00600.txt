Abstract

To illustrate our main result, we show that any subalgebra of L 0 ​ ( μ ) L^{0}\left({\mu}\right) with μ {\mu} σ \sigma -finite (respectively, C ∞ ​ ( K ) C^{\infty}\left(K\right) with K K Stonean) containing L ∞ ​ ( μ ) L^{\infty}\left({\mu}\right) (respectively, C ⁡ ( K ) C\left(K\right) ) is automatically a Dedekind complete f f -algebra with unit.



1
Introduction

Let
X
be a topological space. The lattice-ordered algebra of all
real-valued continuous functions on
X\
is denoted by
C\left(X\right)

and its subalgebra of all bounded functions by
C_{b}\left(X\right)
. In
their interesting paper
[
5
]
, Domínguez, Gómez-Pérez,
and Mulero define an intermediate algebra in
C\left(X\right)
to be any
subalgebra of
C\left(X\right)
containing
C_{b}\left(X\right)
. Among
other facts, they proved that an intermediate algebra in
C\left(X\right)

is an order ideal (i.e., a solid vector subspace) of
C\left(X\right)
.
Recently, this remarkable result has been extended by Domínguez in
[
4
]
to the more general setting of Archimedean unital
f
-algebras (
\Phi
-algebras in the terminology of Henriksen and Johnson in
[
6
]
) as
follows.

Let
A
be an Archimedean
f
-algebra with a multiplicative unit
e
and
assume that
A
is bounded inversion closed, i.e.,
a\geq e
in
A
implies
a
has an inverse in
A
(this concept is due again to Henriksen and Johnson
in
[
6
]
). The set of all bounded elements in
A
is denoted by
A_{b}
, i.e.,

A_{b}=\left\{a\in A:\left|a\right|\leq{\mu}e\text{ for some }{\mu}\in\left(0,\infty\right)\right\}

(notice that
A_{b}
is just the principal order ideal of
A\
generated by
e
). Then any intermediate algebra in
A
(i.e., a subalgebra of
A\
containing
A_{b}
) is an order ideal of
A
(for more details, see
[
4
, Proposition 4]
).

The main purpose of this paper is to extend Domínguez’s theorem to the
wider class of Archimedean semiprime
f
-algebras then to provide some
applications mainly in concrete situations. In order to get around the lack
of multiplicative units, we thought about using the notion of
quasi-invertible elements (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
), which is valid in the
non unital case. We then introduced a “new” concept, namely, bounded quasi-inversion closedness,
to replace the notion of bounded inversion closedness. Further details seem
to be in order.

Let
A
be an Archimedean
f
-algebra and assume that
A\
is semiprime,
i.e., with no nonzero nilpotent elements. Then
A\
is said to be bounded
quasi-inversion closed if, for every
a\in A
, the inequality
\left|a\right|\leq\left|a^{2}-a\right|
implies that
a
has a
quasi-inverse in
A
(see
[
3
]
for quasi-invertibility in an
arbitrary associative real algebra). We first prove that this the two
closedness notions coincide in the unital case. Also, we define the set
A_{b}
of all bounded elements in
A
by
A_{b}=\left\{a\in A:a^{2}\leq{\mu}\left|a\right|\text{ for some }{\mu}\in\left(0,\infty\right)\right\}
. We prove that
A_{b}
is an order
ideal and a subalgebra of
A
. We can thus extend the notion of intermediate
algebras in
A
in an obvious way. We prove that if
A
is bounded
quasi-inverison closed then any intermediate algebra in
A
is an order
ideal. We show also that if
A
is relatively uniformly complete then
A\
bounded quasi-inversion closed and so any intermediate algebra in
A
is
again an order ideal of
A
. As application, we show that if
L
is a
Dedekind complete vector lattice with a distinguished weak order unit
e
,
and if its universally complete
L^{u}
is equipped with its unique
structure of an
f
-algebra with
e
as multiplicative unit, then any
subalgebra of
L^{u}
containing the principal order ideal of
L
generated
by
e
is a Dedekind complete
f
-algebra with
e
as multiplicative unit.
This result can be illustrated as follows. Any subalgebra of
L^{0}\left({\mu}\right)
with
{\mu}

\sigma
-finite (respectively,
C^{\infty}\left(K\right)
with
K

Stonean) containing
L^{\infty}\left({\mu}\right)
(respectively,
C\left(K\right)
) is automatically a Dedekind
complete
f
-algebra with unit.

We take it for granted that the reader is familiar with the Theory of Vector
Lattices. In this regard, we refer to the classical references
[

,
9
]
for either unexplained terminology and notation or unproved

2
Bounded quasi-inversion closedness

The real associative algebra
A
is called a
lattice-ordered algebra

if
A
is simultaneously a vector lattice whose positive cone
A_{+}=\left\{a\in A:0\leq a\right\}
is closed under multiplication, i.e.,

ab\in A_{+}\text{ for all }a,b\in A_{+}.

The lattice-ordered algebra
A
is called an
f
-algebra
if
A

satisfies the extra condition that

a\wedge b=0\text{ in }A\text{ implies }ac\wedge b=ca\wedge b=0\text{ for all
}c\in A_{+}.

An
f
-algebra
A
is said to be
semiprime
if
0
is the only
nilpotent element in
A
. Most of the classical real algebras of functions
are examples of semiprime
f
-algebras. We now record some elementary
properties of
f
-algebras (for proofs, see
[
7
,
11
,
12
]
).

1.

Any Archimedean
f
-algebra is commutative.

2.

Multiplications by positive elements in any
f
-algebra
A
are
lattice homomorphisms. Hence, if
a,b\in A
with
b\in A_{+}
then
a^{+}b=\left(ab\right)^{+}
and
ba^{+}=\left(ba^{+}\right)
.

3.

The equality
\left|ab\right|=\left|a\right|\left|b\right|
holds in any
f
-algebra.

4.

If
a
is an element in an
f
-algebra then
a^{+}a^{-}=a^{-}a^{+}=0
.

5.

Squares in an
f
-algebra
A
are positive. More precisely,
a^{2}=\left|a\right|^{2}
for all
a\in A
.

6.

If
A
is a semiprime
f
-algebra and
a,b\in A
, then
ab=0
if and
only if
\left|a\right|\wedge\left|b\right|=0
.

7.

If
A
is semiprime and
a,b\in A
then
a^{2}\leq b^{2}
if and only
if
\left|a\right|\leq\left|b\right|
.

8.

If an
f
-algebra has a multiplicative unit
e
then
0\leq e
and if
A
is, in addition, Archimedean the
A
is semiprime.

Throughout the paper,
A
is an Archimedean
semiprime
f
-algebra.
An element
a\in A
is said to be
quasi-invertible
if the equality

a+a^{\ast}=aa^{\ast}

holds for some
a^{\ast}\in A
. It is easily checked that such an element
a^{\ast}
—if one exists— is unique, referred to as the
quasi-inverse
of
a
. The set of all quasi-invertible elements in
A
is
denoted by
Q\left(A\right)
. Now,
A
is said to be
bounded
quasi-inversion closed
if, for every
a\in A
, we have

\left|a\right|\leq\left|a^{2}-a\right|\text{ implies }a\in Q\left(A\right).

The following lemma will be very useful later.

Lemma 2.1

Assume that
A
is bounded quasi-inversion closed and let
a\in A
. If
a\leq 0
then
a\in Q\left(A\right)
and
0\leq a^{\ast}
.

Proof.
Let
a\in A_{+}
and observe that

\left|\left(-a\right)^{2}-\left(-a\right)\right|=a^{2}+a\geq a=\left|-a\right|.

It follows that
-a\in Q\left(A\right)
because
A
is bounded
quasi-inversion closed. For brevity, put
b=\left(-a\right)^{\ast}
.
Multiplying the equality
ba=a-b
by
b^{-}
, we get

-\left(b^{-}\right)^{2}a=b^{-}a+\left(b^{-}\right)^{2}.

Therefore,

0\leq\left(b^{-}\right)^{2}\leq b^{-}a+\left(b^{-}\right)^{2}=-\left(b^{-}\right)^{2}a\leq 0.

Consequently,
\left(b^{-}\right)^{2}=0
and so
b^{-}=0
because
A
is
semiprime. This shows that
b\in A_{+}
and completes the proof of the lemma.

Recall that if
A
has a multiplicative unit
e
, then
A\
is said to be

bounded inversion closed
if
a\in A
has an inverse in
A
whenever
e\leq a
. Next, we shall see that bounded quasi-inversion closedness and
bounded inversion closedness coincide in the unital case.

Theorem 2.2

If
A
is unital then
A
is bounded quasi-inversion closed if and
only if
A
is bounded inversion closed.

Proof.
Let
e
denote the multiplicative unit of
A
. Assume that
A
is bounded
quasi-inversion closed and pick
a\in A
with
a\geq e
. Since
a-e\geq 0
,
Lemma
2.1
tells us that
e-a\in Q\left(A\right)
and so the
equality

\left(e-a\right)c=e-a+c

holds for some
c\in A
. Therefore,
a\left(e-c\right)=e
and so
a
has
an inverse in
A
. This means that
A
is bounded inversion closed.

Conversely, assume that
A
is bounded inversion closed and choose
a\in A

such that
\left|a\right|\leq\left|a^{2}-a\right|
.
Hence,

\left(\left|e-a\right|-e\right)\left|a\right|\geq 0

from which it follows that

\left(\left|e-a\right|-e\right)^{-}\left|a\right|=0.

Moreover,

\left(\left|e-a\right|-e\right)^{-}\leq\left|\left|e-a\right|-e\right|\leq\left|a\right|.

Accordingly,

0\leq\left(\left(\left|e-a\right|-e\right)^{-}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\left|e-a\right|-e\right)^{-}\left|a\right|=0

and thus
\left(\left|e-a\right|-e\right)^{-}=0
. We derive that

\left|e-a\right|\geq e
. But then
\left|e-a\right|
is
invertible in
A
(and so is
e-a
) as
A
is bounded inversion closed.
There exists therefore
b\in A
such that
\left(e-a\right)b=e
.
Consequently,

a\left(e-b\right)=a-ab=a+\left(e-b\right),

which yields that
a\in Q\left(A\right)
and then
A
is bounded
quasi-inversion closed.

For every
a\in A
, an operator
\pi_{a}
can be defined on
A
by

\pi_{a}\left(x\right)=ax\text{ for all }x\in A.

It follows directly from the definition of an
f
-algebra that

\pi_{a}\in\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)\text{ for all }a\in A,

where
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
denotes the unital Archimedean
f
-algebra of all orthomorphisms on
A
. Moreover, the operator
\pi
from
A

into
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
given by

\pi\left(a\right)=\pi_{a}\text{ for all }a\in A

is an injective algebra and lattice homomorphism. Accordingly, identifying
any
a\in A
with the orthomorphism
\pi_{a}
,
A
can be regarded as a
subalgebra and a vector sublattice of
A
. Furthermore, it is not hard to
see that, for every
a\in A
, the equality
\pi\pi_{a}=\pi_{\pi\left(a\right)}
holds and so
A
is even a ring ideal of
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
. Lastly, if
A
is relatively uniformly complete, then so is
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
(see
[
9
]
for relatively uniform
completeness). The reader can consult the books
[
2
,
12
]
to have
more detailed information about orthomorphisms. However, we do believe that
the manuscript
[
11
]
remains the most complete reference on the subject.

Theorem 2.3

If
A
is relatively uniformly complete then
A
is bounded
quasi-inversion closed.

Proof.
Assume that
A
is uniformly complete. Choose
a\in A
such that
\left|a\right|\leq\left|a^{2}-a\right|
in
A
. Hence, the
inequality

0\leq\left(\left|I-a\right|-I\right)\left|a\right|

holds in
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
, where
I
denotes the identity
operator on
A
(which is the multiplicative unit of
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
). It follows that

\left(\left|I-a\right|-I\right)^{-}\left|a\right|=0.

Moreover,

\left(\left|I-a\right|-I\right)^{-}\leq\left|\left|I-a\right|-I\right|\leq\left|a\right|.

Therefore,

0\leq\left(\left(\left|I-a\right|-I\right)^{-}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\left|I-a\right|-I\right)^{-}\left|a\right|=0

and thus
\left(\left|I-a\right|-I\right)^{-}=0
. In other
words,
I\leq\left|I-a\right|
in
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
. But then
\left|I-a\right|
has an inverse in
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
because, being relatively uniformly complete,
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
is bounded inversion closed (Theorem 3.4 in
[
7
]
).
Consequently,
I-a
has an inverse in
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
, so
there exists
\pi\in\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
such that
\left(I-a\right)\pi=I
. This yields that
I-\pi=-a\pi
and thus
I-\pi\in A

because
A
is a ring ideal in
\mathrm{Orth}\left(A\right)
. Put
b=I-\pi
and observe that

ab=a\left(I-\pi\right)=a-a\pi=a+I-\pi=a+b.

This shows that
A
is bounded quasi-inversion closed and completes the
proof of the theorem.

On closer examination of the proof of Theorem
2.3
, it can be deduced
that if
A
is unital and bounded inversion closed, then any vector
sublattice of
A
which is simultaneously a ring ideal of
A
is bounded
quasi-inversion closed as a subalgebra of
A
. This fact extends Sufficiency
in Theorem
2.2
.

3
Intermediate algebras and applications

Yet again,
A

stands for an Archimedean semiprime
f
-algebra.

Our investigation starts with the following elementary properties
which will come in handy later on.

Lemma 3.1

The following hold for all
a,b,c\in A
.

(i)

If
ax\leq bx
for all
x\in A_{+}
then
a\leq b
.

(ii)

If
a^{2}\left|b\right|\leq\left|ac\right|
then
\left|ab\right|\leq\left|c\right|
.

Proof.

\mathrm{(i)}
If
a\leq b
and
x\in A_{+}
then, of course,
ax\leq bx
.
Conversely, assume that
ax\leq bx
for all
x\in A_{+}
. Hence, of every
x\in A_{+}
, we have

\left(a-b\right)^{+}x=\left(ax-bx\right)^{+}=0.

In particular,
\left(\left(a-b\right)^{+}\right)^{2}=0
and so
\left(a-b\right)^{+}=0
as
A
is semiprime This yields that
a\leq b
and
completes the proof of
\mathrm{(i)}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}
Assume that
a^{2}\left|b\right|\leq\left|ac\right|
and observe that

0\leq\left(\left(\left|ab\right|-\left|c\right|\right)^{+}\right)^{2}\leq\left|ab\right|\left(\left|ab\right|-\left|c\right|\right)^{+}=\left|b\right|\left(a^{2}\left|b\right|-\left|ac\right|\right)^{+}=0.

But then
\left(\left|ab\right|-\left|c\right|\right)^{+}=0
because
A
is semiprime and so
\left|ab\right|\leq\left|c\right|
, which is the required inequality.

The principal order ideal of
A
generated by an element
a\in A
is denoted
by
A\left(a\right)
, that is,

A\left(a\right)=\left\{x\in A:\left|x\right|\leq{\mu}\left|a\right|\text{ for some }{\mu}\in\left(0,\infty\right)\right\}

An element
a\in A
is said to be
bounded
if
a^{2}\in A\left(a\right)
. The set of all bounded elements in
A
is denoted by
A_{b}
. An
alternative characterization of bounded elements in
A
is given next.

Lemma 3.2

If
a
is an element in
A
then
a\in A_{b}
if and only if

ax\in A\left(x\right)\text{ for all }x\in A.

Proof.
The “if” part being trivial, we prove the
“only if” part. So, suppose that
a\in A_{b}
and pick
{\mu}\in\left(0,\infty\right)
such that
a^{2}\leq{\mu}\left|a\right|
. Hence, if
x\in A_{+}
then
a^{2}x\leq{\mu}\left|a\right|x
. Using conveniently Lemma
3.1

\mathrm{(ii)}
, we get
\left|a\right|x\leq{\mu}x
and we are done.

The structure of
A_{b}
is given in what follows.

Theorem 3.3

The set
A_{b}
is simultaneously a subalgebra and an order
ideal of
A
.

Proof.
Clearly,
0\in A_{b}
and so
A_{b}
is nonempty. Let
a,b\in A_{b}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly, from Lemma
3.2
it follows that
there exists
{\mu}\in\left(0,\infty\right)
such that

\left|a\right|x\leq{\mu}x\ \text{and }\left|b\right|x\leq{\mu}x\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{+}.

Hence, if
x\in A_{+}
then

0\leq\left|a+\delta b\right|x\leq\left|a\right|x+\left|\delta\right|\left|b\right|x\leq\left(1+\left|\delta\right|\right){\mu}x.

We derive that
a+\delta b\in A_{b}
and so
A_{b}
is a vector subspace of
A
. Now, if
x\in X
then

\left|ab\right|x=\left|a\right|\left|b\right|x\leq{\mu}\left|b\right|x\leq{\mu}^{2}x.

This means that
ab\in A_{b}
from which it follows that
A_{b}
is a
subalgebra of
A
. Finally, assume that
\left|c\right|\leq\left|a\right|
for some
c\in A
. Hence,

\left|c\right|x\leq\left|a\right|x\leq{\mu}x\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{+}.

and so
c\in A_{b}
. Accordingly,
A_{b}
is an order ideal of
A
and the
proof is complete.

It should be pointed out that a look at bounded elements in an Archimedean
semiprime
f
-algebra from a different point of view can be found in
[
10
]
by Triki.

In spite of its simplicity, the following fact which plays a key role in the
proof of the main theorem of this paper.

Lemma 3.4

If
a\in A
then
a\in A_{b}
if and only if
a^{2}\in A_{b}
.

Proof.
Theorem
3.3
yields that if
a\in A_{b}
then
a^{2}\in A_{b}
.
Conversely, suppose that
a^{2}\in A_{b}
, that is,
a^{4}\in A\left(a^{2}\right)
. Thus, there exists
{\mu}\in\left(0,\infty\right)
such that
a^{4}\leq{\mu}a^{2}
. Whence,

\left(a^{2}\right)^{2}=a^{4}\leq{\mu}a^{2}=\left(\sqrt{{\mu}}\left|a\right|\right)^{2}.

It follows
a^{2}\leq\sqrt{{\mu}}\left|a\right|
and the lemma follows.

Following Rodriguez in
[
4
]
, we call a subalgebra of
A
containing
A_{b}
an
intermediate algebra
in
A
. We are in position at this
point to state and prove the central theorem of this study.

Theorem 3.5

Assume that
A
is bounded quasi-inversion closed. Then
any intermediate algebra in
A
is an order ideal in
A
.

Proof.
Let
a\in A
and
b\in B
such that
\left|a\right|\leq\left|b\right|
. By Lemma
2.1
,
-a^{2}\in Q\left(A\right)
and
so, to simplify matters, we can put
c=\left(-a^{2}\right)^{\ast}
.
First, we claim that
b-bc\in A_{b}
. To this end, observe that

b^{2}c\leq b^{2}c+c=b^{2}

because
c\geq 0
(see Lemma
2.1
). Using Lemma
3.1

\mathrm{(ii)}
, we obtain
\left|b\right|c\leq\left|b\right|
and so
0\leq\left|b\right|-\left|b\right|c
. Hence, if
x\in A_{+}
then

\displaystyle\left|b-bc\right|x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|bx-bxc\right|=\left|b\right|\left|x-xc\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left|b\right|x-\left|b\right|xc\right|=\left|\left|b\right|-\left|b\right|c\right|x=\left(\left|b\right|-\left|b\right|c\right)x

As
x
is arbitrary in
A_{+}
, we derive from Lemma
3.1

\mathrm{(i)}
that

\left|b-bc\right|=\left|b\right|-\left|b\right|c.

Moreover,

b^{2}\left|b-bc\right|=\left|b^{3}-b^{3}c\right|=\left|b\left(b^{2}-b^{2}c\right)\right|=\left|bc\right|=\left|b\right|c\leq\left|b\right|.

Thus,

b^{2}\left|b-bc\right|x\leq\left|b\right|x\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{+}.

This together with Lemma
3.1

\mathrm{(ii)}
yields that

\left|b-bc\right|^{2}x\leq\left|b\right|\left|b-bc\right|x\leq x\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{+}.

Combining Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
, we infer that
b-bc\in A_{b}
, as required.

Now, let
x\in A_{+}
and observe that

\displaystyle\left|a-ac\right|x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|ax-acx\right|=\left|a\right|\left|x-cx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|b\right|\left|x-cx\right|=\left|bx-bcx\right|=\left|b-bc\right|x.

It follows that
\left|a-ac\right|\leq\left|b-bc\right|
, where we use Lemma
3.1

\mathrm{(i)}
. As
b-bc\in A_{b}
and
A_{b}
is an order ideal of
A
(see Theorem
3.3
), we derive that

a-ac\in A_{b}\subset B.

Finally,

a=a+\left(b^{2}-b^{2}c-c\right)a=\left(a-ac\right)+b^{2}\left(a-ac\right)\in B

and the proof is complete.

Theorem
3.5
together with Theorem
2.3
leads straightforwardly to
the following result.

Corollary 3.6

If
A\
relatively uniformly complete then any
intermediate algebra in
A
is an order ideal in
A
.

Taking into account that any Dedekind complete vector lattice is relatively
uniformly complete, the following can be directly derived from Corollary
3.6
.

Corollary 3.7

If
A\
Dedekind complete then any intermediate algebra in
A
is
an order ideal in
A
. In particular, any intermediate algebra in
A
is a
Dedekind complete semiprime
f
-algebra.

Now, let
L
be a Dedekind complete vector lattice with a distinguished weak
order unit
e
. It is well-known that that there exists a unique
multiplication on the universal completion
L^{u}
of
L
which turns
L^{u}

into a universal complete
f
-algebra with
e
as multiplicative unit (see

[
1
]
or
[
8
]
). We obtain the following.

Corollary 3.8

Let
L
be a Dedekind complete vector lattice with a
distinguished weak order unit
e
. Any subalgebra of
L^{u}
containing the
principal order ideal
L\left(e\right)
of
L
generated by
e
is a
Dedekind complete
f
-algebra with
e
as a multiplicative unit.

Proof.
Let
B
be a subalgebra of
L^{u}
and suppose that
B
contains
L\left(e\right)
. Clearly,
L\left(e\right)
is contained in
L^{u}\left(e\right)
, the principal order ideal of
L^{u}
generated by
e
.
Conversely, if
x\in L^{u}\left(e\right)
then
x\in L\left(e\right)

because
L
is an order ideal in
L^{u}
. This means that
B
is an
intermediate algebra in
L^{u}
and the result follows from the previous
corollary.

We end by discussing two (equivalent) concrete illustrations.

Example 3.9

(i)

Let
K
be an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff
space. Hence,
C\left(K\right)
is a Dedekind complete vector lattice with
the constant function
1
as a strong
(
and so weak
)
order
unit. The universal completion of
C\left(K\right)
is the universally
complete unital
f
-agebra
C^{\infty}\left(K\right)
of all
almost-finite extended-real valued continuous functions

on on
K
.
According to
Corollary
3.8
, any subalgebra of
C^{\infty}\left(K\right)
containing
C\left(K\right)
is a Dedekind complete
f
-algebra with the constant function
1
as multiplicative unit.

(ii)

Let
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,{\mu}\right)
be a measure space such that the measure
{\mu}
is
\sigma
-finite and let
L^{0}\left({\mu}\right)
denote the universally complete unital
f
-algebra of all
{\mu}
-measurable real-valued
(
classes of
)
functions on
\Omega
.
It is know that
L^{0}\left({\mu}\right)
is the universal completion of the Dedekind complete vector
lattice
L^{\infty}\left({\mu}\right)
of all bounded
(
classes of
)
functions on
\Omega
.
Moreover, the constant function
1
is a weak order unit in
L^{\infty}\left({\mu}\right)
and, in the same time, the multiplicative identity of
L^{0}\left({\mu}\right)
. In view of
Corollary
3.8
, any subalgebra of
L^{0}\left({\mu}\right)
containing
L^{\infty}\left({\mu}\right)
is a Dedekind complete
f
-algebra with the constant function
1

as multiplicative unit.

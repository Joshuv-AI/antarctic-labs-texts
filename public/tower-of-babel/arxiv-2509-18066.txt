On the de Almeida–Thouless Transition Surface in the Multi-Species SK Model with Centered Gaussian External Field
Heejune Kim

Abstract
We study the phase transition of the Parisi formula for the free energy in the multi-species Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model with a centered Gaussian external field and a positive-semidefinite variance profile matrix. We show that in terms of the strength of the external field and the variance profile, the de Almeida–Thouless surface delineates the boundary between replica symmetric solutions and replica symmetry breaking solutions.

Overview of the proof

Under the assumption of positive-definite
\Delta^{2}
and nonzero external field, RSB outside the AT surface has already been achieved by Toninelli’s perturbative argument

We supply a necessary adjustment to extend the result to irreducible positive-semidefinite

The main task of this paper is to establish RS solutions inside the AT surface. We now outline the key steps of our approach, focusing on the case when
\Delta^{2}
is positive-definite and
\rho(\Gamma\Delta^{2}\Lambda)<1/2

Step 1. Uniqueness of the minimizer
q^{*}
of the RS functional.

A first routine but highly nontrivial step, as in the case of the SK model, is to analyze the RS functional, in particular the nagging question of uniqueness of its minimizer.
Partial results in tackling this technical issue have appeared in
[
2
,
11
,
27
]
; see Remark
2.3
below. They are usually based on the Latala–Guerra argument or on the contraction argument that do not appear to work in the entire regime we consider here.
We resolve this problem by using two ingredients: the irreducibility of
\Delta^{2}
and the concavity of the fixed-point map in (
1.10
), which critically relies on the assumption of the external field being centered Gaussian.
5
5

5

Unfortunately, concavity does not seem to hold for nonzero deterministic external fields.
In Lemma
2.6
below, we show that the irreducibility of
\Delta^{2}
rules out the possibility that the minimizers of the RS functional occur on the boundary of
[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}
, except possibly at
\mymathbb{0}
. Consequently, for irreducible positive-definite
\Delta^{2}
, any minimizer satisfies the fixed-point equation, which together with the concavity of the fixed-point map ensures the uniqueness of minimizers (see Proposition
2.1
below).

Step 2. Setting up the interpolation.
The next major step is motivated by Talagrand’s treatment

55

A standard computation shows that

\frac{d}{dt}(\mathsf{RS}^{*}(t)-\mathbb{E}F_{N}(t))\approx 2^{-1}\mathbb{E}\langle\!\langle\|R_{12}-q^{*}\|^{2}\rangle\!\rangle_{N,t}

Minimizers of the replica symmetric functional

This section is devoted to establishing various properties of the minimizers of the RS functional.
The following main result of this section is a description of the minimizers of the RS functional for irreducible positive-definite
\Delta^{2}.

Proposition 2.1

Assume
\Delta^{2}

The infimum

If

and
\rho(\Delta^{2}\Lambda)\leq 1/2
, then
\mymathbb{0}
is the minimizer.
Otherwise, the minimizer is in the interior

To achieve this, we first show that any minimizer must be a solution of the fixed-point equation; see Lemma

.
In turn, we can study the number and locations of the fixed-points as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

For an irreducible
\Delta^{2}
, the following holds.

(i)

If
\tau^{2}\neq\mymathbb{0}
, then there is a unique fixed-point of (

), and it is in
(0,1)^{\mathscr{S}}
.

(ii)

Suppose
\tau^{2}=\mymathbb{0}
.
If

\mymathbb{0}
and the other is in

.
If
\rho(\Delta^{2}\Lambda)\leq 1/2

\mymathbb{0}
is the only fixed-point.

Remark 2.3
.

The uniqueness of the fixed-point in the high temperature regime

and nonzero deterministic external field was established in

readily imply

∎

The following rules out

as a minimizer of the RS functional at zero external field and low temperature.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume
\tau^{2}=\mymathbb{0}
and irreducible

.
If

does not attain the infimum of the RS functional.

Proof.

Suppose

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
q\in[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}

By the same calculation yielding (

), for any

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathsf{RS}(\varepsilon v)

\displaystyle=-v^{\intercal}\Lambda\Delta^{2}\Lambda\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\sqrt{2\Delta^{2}\Lambda\varepsilon v})+\varepsilon v^{\intercal}\Lambda\Delta^{2}\Lambda v,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}\mathsf{RS}(\varepsilon v)

\displaystyle=-v^{\intercal}\Lambda\Delta^{2}\Lambda D\Delta^{2}\Lambda v+v^{\intercal}\Lambda\Delta^{2}\Lambda v,

For later use, we establish a variant of Proposition

under the weaker assumption of positive-semidefinite and irreducible

.

).

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume
\Delta^{2}
is irreducible and positive-semidefinite.

is a minimizer of the RS functional and a fixed-point of (

).

Proof.

For

, consider

\Delta^{2}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Delta^{2}+n^{-1}I.

(2.28)

,
\Delta^{2}_{n}
is positive-definite and irreducible.
Hence, for each

, Proposition

guarantees unique minimizers
q^{*}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q^{*}(\Delta^{2}_{n},\tau^{2})

3
Support of Parisi measures

This section is dedicated to establishing an important property of a Parisi measure, which was the key ingredient in Step 4 of the proof sketch.
Denote the space of probability measures on
[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}
with totally ordered supports by

\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}=\bigl\{\mu:\forall x,y\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu),\;\text{either }x\preceq y\text{ or }y\preceq x\bigr\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
be the discrete measures with totally ordered supports.
For an arbitrary
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
, we define
q_{\text{min}}^{\mu}
and
q_{\text{max}}^{\mu}
to be the unique minimizer and maximizer of the
L^{1}
norm on
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
, respectively.
These are well-defined since the support of a measure is totally ordered and compact in
[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume irreducible and positive-definite
\Delta^{2}
.
For any Parisi measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
, it holds that either
q^{\mu}_{\mathrm{min}}=q^{*}
or
q^{\mu}_{\mathrm{min}}\prec q^{*}
.
Similarly, it holds either
q^{\mu}_{\mathrm{max}}=q^{*}
or
q^{\mu}_{\mathrm{max}}\succ q^{*}
.

The idea to achieve this is to show that the fixed-point map (
2.1
) evaluated at either
q^{\mu}_{\mathrm{min}}
or
q^{\mu}_{\mathrm{max}}
is simultaneously monotone in all species in accordance with Lemma
2.12
.
We will first derive a certain critical point equation for Parisi measures in Proposition
3.7
, and we analyze this equation by discretizing the Parisi measure as in (
3.6
).
Hence, most of the effort in this section is to examine this discretization, which is the content of the next subsection.

3.1

Define the random variables
Y^{s}_{0}=h_{s}

\displaystyle Y^{s}_{l}

\displaystyle=h_{s}+\sum_{1\leq l^{\prime}\leq l}z_{l^{\prime}}(Q_{l^{\prime}}^{s}-Q_{l^{\prime}-1}^{s})^{1/2}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq l\leq r,\,\,s\in\mathscr{S},

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
1\leq l\leq r
and

W^{s}_{l}=\frac{\widetilde{T}_{l}\cosh Y^{s}_{r}}{\mathbb{E}_{l}\widetilde{T}_{l}\cosh Y^{s}_{r}}.

Proof sketch.

We compute the last three terms and leave the formal inductive proof to the reader.
From (

),
\exp(\zeta_{r-1}X_{r-1}^{s})=\mathbb{E}_{r}\cosh^{\zeta_{r-1}}(Y^{s}_{r})

One more step yields
\exp(\zeta_{r-2}X_{r-2}^{s})=\mathbb{E}_{r-1}(\mathbb{E}_{r}\cosh^{\zeta_{r-1}}(Y^{s}_{r}))^{\zeta_{r-2}/\zeta_{r-1}}

We present three auxiliary lemmas about symmetric random variables.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a random variable with a symmetric non-increasing density and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an odd measurable function such that
f\geq 0
on
[0,\infty)
.
Then, for any
a\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}f(a+X)\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
g
be the density of
X
.
By some change of variables,

\mathbb{E}f(a+X)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(a+x)g(x)dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)g(x-a)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)(g(x-a)-g(x+a))dx,

where the last inequality holds since
f
is odd and
g
is even.
As a result, it suffices to show

g(x+a)\leq g(x-a),\quad\forall x\geq 0.

(3.1)

The triangle inequality implies
|x-a|\leq|x+a|
, and since
g
is even and decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, we have
g(x+a)=g(|x+a|)\leq g(|x-a|)=g(x-a)
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an odd measurable function such that
f\geq 0
on
[0,\infty)
and
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an even measurable function which is non-increasing on
[0,\infty)
.
Let
X
be a symmetric random variable.
Suppose that
\mathbb{E}f(X)^{2}<\infty
and
\sup_{a\geq 0}\mathbb{E}g(a+X)^{2}<\infty.

Then, for any
a\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{E}f(X)g(a+X)\leq 0.

Proof.

Since
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S|X|
for some independent Rademacher random variable
S
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X)g(a+X)

\displaystyle=2^{-1}\mathbb{E}[f(|X|)g(a+|X|)+f(-|X|)g(a-|X|)]

\displaystyle=2^{-1}\mathbb{E}[f(|X|)(g(a+|X|)-g(a-|X|))],

where we used the fact that
f
is odd in the second equality.
From the preceding display, since
f\geq 0
on
[0,\infty)
, the asserted inequality follows from (
3.1
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a random variable with symmetric non-increasing density.
Let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth, positive, and even function, which is non-decreasing on
[0,\infty)
and satisfies a mild integrability condition, e.g.,
\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}[|g^{\prime}(t+X)|+g(t+X)]<\infty.

Suppose
t\mapsto g(t)(\cosh t)^{-1}
is non-increasing on
[0,\infty)
.
Then, for any
0\leq m\leq 1
, the function

t\mapsto(\mathbb{E}g(t+X))^{m}(\cosh t)^{-1}

is non-increasing on
[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Denote
h(t)=(\mathbb{E}g(t+X))^{m}(\cosh t)^{-1}
and
j(t)=g(t)(\cosh t)^{-1}
.
We claim that for any
t\geq 0
, we have

0\leq\mathbb{E}g(t+X)\tanh(t+X)\leq\mathbb{E}g(t+X)\tanh t.

(3.2)

The first inequality in (
3.2
) is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.3
.
The second inequality follows from

\displaystyle\cosh(t)\mathbb{E}[g(t+X)(\tanh(t+X)-\tanh t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[j(t+X)(\sinh(t+X)\cosh(t)-\cosh(t+X)\sinh(t))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[j(t+X)\sinh(X)]\leq 0,

where the last inequality is due to Lemma
3.4
.
The claim is now established.

A differentiation gives
j^{\prime}(t)=(\cosh t)^{-1}(g^{\prime}(t)-g(t)\tanh t)
.
Since
j
is even and non-increasing on
[0,\infty)
, we have that
k(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}g^{\prime}(x)-g(x)\tanh(x)
is an odd function such that

on
[0,\infty)
.
On the other hand, (
3.2
) implies that for

,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(\cosh t)^{-1}(\mathbb{E}g(t+X))^{m-1}(m\mathbb{E}g^{\prime}(t+X)-\mathbb{E}g(t+X)\tanh t)

\displaystyle\leq(\cosh t)^{-1}(\mathbb{E}g(t+X))^{m-1}(m\mathbb{E}g^{\prime}(t+X)-\mathbb{E}g(t+X)\tanh(t+X))

\displaystyle=(\cosh t)^{-1}(\mathbb{E}g(t+X))^{m-1}(\mathbb{E}k(t+X)-(1-m)\mathbb{E}g^{\prime}(t+X)).

By Lemma

, it holds that
\mathbb{E}k(t+X)\leq 0\leq\mathbb{E}g^{\prime}(t+X)

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For all

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{l}[(\cosh Y^{s}_{l})^{-1}\widetilde{T}_{l}(\cosh Y^{s}_{r})\sinh(Y^{s}_{l}-Y^{s}_{l-1})]\leq 0.

Since

-measurable, there exists some some measurable function

For positive-semidefinite

,

.

Let

Suppose there exists some constant

,

4.1
The ASS scheme along the interpolation

We carry out two straightforward calculations about the interpolated free energies.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any

is uniform in

by uniform

Proof.

In the absence of the perturbing Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}F_{N}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{\sigma}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}}((2\Delta^{2}\Lambda q^{*})_{s})^{1/2}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}z_{i}\sigma_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}h_{i}\sigma_{i}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\log 2+\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma}\prod_{1\leq i\leq N}\exp\Bigl(\sigma_{i}\bigl(z_{i}((2\Delta^{2}\Lambda q^{*})_{s(i)})^{1/2}+h_{i}\bigr)\Bigr)

Combining the preceding display with Lemma

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For

and

by uniform

Proof.

A direct differentiation yields

Lemma 5.4

In this appendix, we extend the Parisi functional to a larger domain that contains continuous measures as well, and validate Proposition

The results are presented for centered Gaussian external field, but it should be noted that they all hold for deterministic external fields with necessary modifications.
The proofs of this appendix are straightforward extensions of those of the single-species SK model.

The first result of this appendix is the extension of the Parisi functional to arbitrary measures on
[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}
with totally ordered supports.
Before stating the result, let us first provide relevant definitions.
Denote the
L^{p}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}
by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty

For any probability measures
\mu
and
\nu

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{X\sim\mu,\;Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}\|X-Y\|_{1}
, which metrizes the weak topology.
Recall
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}_{d}.

We start with confirming the Lipshitz continuity of the Parisi functional on

Proposition A.1
.

For any
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}_{d}
,

|\mathscr{P}(\mu_{1})-\mathscr{P}(\mu_{2})|\leq\|\Lambda\Delta^{2}\Lambda\|_{\infty}W_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

To extend the Parisi functional to all of
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
, it remains to observe the following.

Lemma A.2
.

Under the weak topology, the closure of
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}_{d}
is equal to
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
is compact.

Proof.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
, we can find a sequence of discrete probability measures
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
that weakly converges to
\mu
, and such that
\cup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mu).

In particular,
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{n})
is totally ordered for any
n\geq 1.

This proves half of the lemma.

Conversely, suppose there exists a sequence of discrete measures
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with totally ordered support that converges weakly to some
\mu.

We need to show that
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
is totally ordered.
Suppose otherwise.
We can find two points

such that, without loss of generality,
x_{1}>y_{1}
and
x_{2}<y_{2}.

There exist open sets

and
B_{y}\ni y
such that
B_{x}\cap B_{y}=\emptyset
, and
z_{1}>w_{1}
and
z_{2}<w_{2}

By the Portmanteau theorem, since

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(B_{x})\geq\mu(B_{x})>0\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(B_{y})\geq\mu(B_{y})>0,

\mu_{m}(B_{x})\wedge\mu_{m}(B_{y})>0

This is a contradiction to

being totally ordered.

This extension was initially carried out by Guerra

]

.
Then,
a\notin K
and
\{y,z\}
is not totally ordered, which is a contradiction to
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
.
To establish existence, note
0<\nu(\mathsf{B}_{1/n}(x))=\mu(S^{-1}(\mathsf{B}_{1/n}(x)))\leq\mu(S^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}(\mathsf{B}_{1/n}(x))))
for every
n\geq 1.

In particular,
(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\cap S^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}(\mathsf{B}_{1/n}(x))))_{n\geq 1}
is a decreasing family of nonempty closed sets.
By the compactness of
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
, we must have
\emptyset\neq\cap_{n\geq 1}\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\cap S^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}(\mathsf{B}_{1/n}(x)))\bigr)=\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\cap S^{-1}(x)
.

Next, to check
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\subseteq S^{-1}\operatorname{supp}(\nu),
suppose
y\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
.
Then,
\nu(\mathsf{B}_{\varepsilon}(Sy))=\mu(S^{-1}\mathsf{B}_{\varepsilon}(Sy))\\
>0,
where the inequality follows from the fact that
S^{-1}(\mathsf{B}_{\varepsilon}(Sy))
is an open set containing
y
and
y\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu).

This means that
Sy\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)
, as desired.

∎

Appendix D
Simultaneous replica symmetry breaking

As a simple byproduct of our proof, we can address whether all species are simultaneously RSB; that is, the projections of a Parisi measure into each species have supports of the same cardinality.

Proposition D.1
.

Consider MSK with deterministic external field.
Suppose
\Delta^{2}
is irreducible and positive-definite.
Let
(\pi_{s})_{*}
denote the push-forward by the projection map
\pi_{s}
into the
s
-species.
For every Parisi measure
\mu
on
[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}
, we have
|\operatorname{supp}((\pi_{s})_{*}\mu)|=|\operatorname{supp}((\pi_{s^{\prime}})_{*}\mu)|
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathscr{S}
.

Remark D.2
.

This result is not new and was obtained in Chen–Mourrat
[
18
, Theorem 6.2]
for possibly indefinite
\Delta^{2}
by analyzing a critical point equation of a certain functional, which is conceptually similar to Lemma
3.7
.
Simultaneous RSB in the setting of multi-species spherical mixed
p
-spin models with convex interaction was also established in Bates–Sohn
[
12
]
.
We note that in all of these results, the support cardinality of a Parisi measure is not determined, but we expect full-RSB at low temperatures, meaning a nontrivial continuous component of the support; see Zhou
[
58
]
for recent progress on full-RSB in the single-species SK model without external field at low temperature just below the criticality.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\uparrow}
be any Parisi measure and suppose
p,q\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
satisfy
p_{s}<q_{s}
for some species
s\in\mathscr{S}.

We aim to show
p_{k}<q_{k}
for any
k\in\mathscr{S}
such that
\Delta^{2}_{ks}>0
.
In spirit of
[
13
]
, we will achieve this by examining an identity satisfied by the support of a Parisi measure;
by Lemma
3.7
which also holds for deterministic external fields, it suffices to establish

\Psi_{\mu}(h,p)_{k}<\Psi_{\mu}(h,q)_{k},

which is equivalent to, in view of definition (
3.5
),
f_{k}(p)<f_{k}(q)
where

f_{k}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gamma_{\mu\circ(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda)^{-1}\circ\pi_{k}^{-1}\circ S_{k},\;\xi_{k}}\Bigl(h_{k},\frac{(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda x)_{k}}{(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda\mymathbb{1})_{k}}\Bigr),\quad x\in[0,1]^{\mathscr{S}}.

By the chain rule, we have

\partial_{x_{s}}f_{k}(x)=\frac{(\Delta^{2}\Lambda)_{ks}}{(\Delta^{2}\Lambda\mymathbb{1})_{k}}\partial_{2}\Gamma_{\mu\circ(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda)^{-1}\circ\pi_{k}^{-1}\circ S_{k},\;\xi_{k}}\Bigl(h_{k},\frac{(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda x)_{k}}{(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda\mymathbb{1})_{k}}\Bigr)\quad\text{for any }s\in\mathscr{S},

where we denote the derivative in the second coordinate by
\partial_{2}
.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

f_{k}(q)-f_{k}(p)=\sum_{l\in\mathscr{S}}(q_{l}-p_{l})\frac{(\Delta^{2}\Lambda)_{kl}}{(\Delta^{2}\Lambda\mymathbb{1})_{k}}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{2}\Gamma_{\mu\circ(2\Delta^{2}\Lambda)^{-1}\circ\pi_{k}^{-1}\circ S_{k},\;\xi_{k}}\Bigl(h_{k},\frac{(\Delta^{2}\Lambda(tq+(1-t)p))_{k}}{(\Delta^{2}\Lambda\mymathbb{1})_{k}}\Bigr)dt.

By item (ii) in the proof of Lemma
A.7
, the integrand hence the integral on the right-hand side of the preceding display is positive.
As a result,
f_{k}(q)>f_{k}(p)
for all
k\in\mathscr{S}
such that
(\Delta^{2}\Lambda)_{ks}>0
, i.e.,
\Delta^{2}_{ks}>0
.
Together with the irreducibility of
\Delta^{2}
, this establishes bijections between the support of all the species.

∎

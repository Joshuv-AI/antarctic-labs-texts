Power-logconcavity of the Laplacian ground state
Graziano Crasta, Ilaria Fragalà

Abstract
. Let u u be the first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunction of a bounded convex set Ω \Omega in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . 1.
Introduction

A seminal result by Brascamp-Lieb states that logconcavity is preserved by the heat flow
v_{t}=\Delta v
with Dirichlet boundary conditions
on an open bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
6
]
: as a consequence of the logconcavity of the heat kernel,
if the initial datum
v_{0}(x)=v(x,0)
is a bounded, positive, logconcave function in
\Omega
, then
v(\cdot,t)
remains logconcave for every
t>0

(in fact, Lee-Vázquez later proved
that logconcavity arises spontaneously along the Dirichlet heat flow even when the initial datum is not logconcave, see
[
19
]
).

The logconcavity of the ground state, namely the first Dirichlet eigenfunction
u
, follows by a limiting argument, since
log-concavity is stable under scalar multiplication and, for some
c>0
,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}e^{\lambda_{1}t}v(t,x)=cu(x)\quad\text{ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}\,.

This result has several extensions (see e.g.
[
21
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
10
]
) and plenty of important implications, for instance in the study of the fundamental gap, concentration phenomena, Poincaré or Brunn-Minkowski type inequalities

[
2
,
3
,
5
,
9
,
18
]
.

In a series of recent papers
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, Ishige-Salani-Takatsu raised and investigated the question whether the above properties remain valid for some stronger kind of concavity beyond logconcavity.
In particular, they introduced the following notion of power-logconcavity (see
[
14
, Definition 2.1]
):
given
\alpha\in(0,1]
, a nonnegative bounded function
u
is said to be
\alpha
-logconcave
in
\Omega
if, letting

(1)

L_{\alpha}(s):=-(-\log s)^{\alpha}\,,

for every sufficiently small
\kappa>0
one has

(2)

L_{\alpha}(\kappa u((1-t)x+ty)\geq(1-t)L_{\alpha}(\kappa u(x))+tL_{\alpha}(\kappa u(y))\qquad\forall x,y\in\Omega,\ \forall t\in[0,1]\,.

If
u
takes values into
[0,1]
, the above definition amounts to requiring that
(
2
) be satisfied for some
\kappa\in(0,1]
, as it then automatically remains valid for all smaller
\kappa
, see
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
).
Moreover, it is straightforward to see that, if
u
is
\alpha
-logconcave, it is also
\beta
-logconcave for every
\beta>\alpha
.
Thus, for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\alpha
-logconcavity is stronger than logconcavity (which corresponds to
\alpha=1
).

The choice of the function
L_{\alpha}
in (
1
) was motivated by the concavity properties of the heat kernel. In this respect,
the value
\alpha=1/2
appears to be a critical threshold, because the heat kernel turns out to be
\alpha
-logconcave if and only if

\alpha\geq 1/2
.

It is then natural to ask whether
(1/2)
-log concavity is preserved – or even arises spontaneously – along the Dirichlet heat flow, and whether the
Laplacian ground state is
(1/2)
-logconcave.
An affirmative answer to both the the preservation and the spontaneous appearance of
(1/2)
-log concavity along the Dirichlet heat flow was given in
[
14
, Corollary 3.1 and Theorem 3.3]
. The authors also showed that
(1/2)
-logconcavity is, in a suitable sense, the strongest concavity property preserved along this flow
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Yet the question about the ground state of the Laplacian remained open, as it was recently pointed out also in
[
13
]
.
Aim of this paper is to answer affirmatively such question.

We emphasize that the limiting argument used by Brascamp-Lieb to deduce the logconcavity of the ground state from the preservation of logconcavity breaks down when one attempts to reproduce it for
(1/2)
-logconcavity. Indeed, the largest parameter
\kappa_{t}
for which
(
2
) holds, with
\alpha=1/2
, for the solution to the heat equation at time
t
might, in principle, degenerate to
0
as
t\to+\infty
,
(see
[
14
, Remark 4.2]
and
[
15
, Section 5]
).
Our main result establishes that such degeneracy does not occur, since we obtain that
the ground state is
(1/2)
-logconcave:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
u
be its first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunction, normalized so that

\max_{\overline{\Omega}}u=1
.
Then
u
is
(1/2)
-logconcave. More precisely, if
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
and
D_{\Omega}
denote respectively the first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue of
\Omega
and its
diameter,
letting

(3)

\overline{\kappa}(\Omega):=\exp\Big[-\frac{3}{2}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)D_{\Omega}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}-1\Big)\Big]\,,

for every
\kappa\in(0,\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)]
it holds that

L_{1/2}(\kappa u((1-t)x+ty)\geq(1-t)L_{1/2}(\kappa u(x))+tL_{1/2}(\kappa u(y))\qquad\forall x,y\in\Omega,\ \forall t\in[0,1]\,.

Remark 2
.

In order to estimate the size of the constant
\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)
in (
3
), observe that it is a monotone decreasing function of the product
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)D_{\Omega}^{2}
.
By the Faber-Krahn inequality and the isodiametric inequality, this product is minimized by balls,
while it diverges to
+\infty
along sequences of thinning convex domains. Consequently, the value of
\overline{k}(\Omega)
is maximized by balls, whereas it degenerates to
0
along sequences of thinning convex domains. Thus, denoting by
B_{n}
a ball of radius
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
j_{\nu,1}
the first zero of the Bessel function
J_{\nu}
, with
\nu=\frac{n}{2}-1
, for any
open bounded convex subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

we have:

(4)

\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)\leq\overline{\kappa}(B_{n})=\exp\Big[-\frac{3}{2}\Big(\frac{4\lambda_{1}(B_{n})}{\pi^{2}}-1\Big)\Big]=\exp\Big[-\frac{3}{2}\Big(\frac{4j_{\nu,1}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}-1\Big)\Big]\,.

Notice that the map
n\mapsto\overline{\kappa}(B_{n})
is monotone decreasing. For
n=2
,
we have
j_{0,1}\simeq 2.405
, so that

\overline{\kappa}(B_{2})\simeq 0.133
.

Clearly, for certain convex domains such as balls or parallelepipeds, the threshold
\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)
given by (
3
) is
not optimal. Indeed,
for these domains a direct computation of the eigenfunction shows that the threshold can be increased up to
1
; namely,

(-\log u)^{1/2}
is convex.
At present, we are unable to determine whether such property holds true for every convex domain.

The proof of Theorem
1
is obtained via a nonstandard application of the
classical convex envelope method by Alvarez–Lasry–Lions
[
1
]
.
Actually, the equation
satisfied by
w_{\kappa}:=(-\log(\kappa u))^{1/2}
is of the kind

\Delta w_{\kappa}=f(w_{\kappa},\nabla w_{\kappa})
(see (
11
)), for a function
f
which does not guarantee the validity of
the comparison principle, and does not fit the structure conditions required to apply convex envelope method (nor other classical methods such as the concavity maximum principle by Korevaar
[
17
,
8
]
,
or the continuity method by Caffarelli-Friedman
[
7
]
).

The novelty of our approach consists in invoking
the improved log-concavity inequality for the ground state established by Andrews-Clutterbuck in
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
,
in order to restore the applicability of the convex envelope method.
Such inequality asserts that the tangent function serves as a modulus of concavity for
v=-\log u
, namely

(5)

\langle\nabla v(z)-\nabla v(y),\tfrac{z-y}{|z-y|}\rangle\geq\frac{2\pi}{D_{\Omega}}\tan\!\Big(\frac{\pi|z-y|}{2D_{\Omega}}\Big)\qquad\forall z,y\in\Omega\,,\ z\neq y\,.

The key idea in our proof is that the above inequality can be used to derive
a gradient estimate for the convex envelope
w_{\kappa}^{**}
of

w_{\kappa}
(see Proposition
7
). This estimate leads us to show that
w_{\kappa}^{**}
is a supersolution to the equation satisfied by
w_{\kappa}
,
for
\kappa
below the threshold
\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)
introduced in (
3
).
Once proved this fact, the conclusion is reached by circumventing the lack of comparison principle, thanks to a variational argument and to the simplicity of the first eigenfunction.

As pointed out in Remark
2
, the threshold
\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)
in Theorem
1
is suboptimal, at least for some domains.
On the other hand we are able to establish that, even beyond this threshold, the ground state enjoys
the following
local

(1/2)
-logconcavity property
in a convex neighbourhood of its maximum point:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Omega
and
u
be as in Theorem
1
.
For every
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
let
w_{\kappa}:=(-\log(\kappa u))^{1/2}

and let
(w_{\kappa})^{**}
denote its convex envelope in
\Omega
.
Then there exists
\overline{u}_{\kappa}\in(0,1)
, with

\lim\overline{u}_{\kappa}=1
as
\kappa\to 1^{-}
, such that

w_{\kappa}=(w_{\kappa})^{**}

on the nonempty convex subset of
\Omega
given by

\Omega_{\kappa}:=\big\{x\in\Omega\ :\ u(x)>\overline{u}_{\kappa}\big\}\,.

In particular, it holds that

L_{1/2}(\kappa u((1-t)x+ty)\geq(1-t)L_{1/2}(\kappa u(x))+tL_{1/2}(\kappa u(y))\qquad\forall x,y\in\Omega_{\kappa}\,,\ \forall t\in[0,1]\,.

The proof of Theorem
3
again relies on the improved concavity inequality (
5
), this time combined with a classical gradient estimate for the ground state.

At present, it is unclear to us whether the local result given in Theorem
3

might be helpful as a starting point for improving the global result given in Theorem
1
.

The paper is organized as follows:
after providing some preliminaries in Section
2
, the proofs of Theorems
1
and
3
are given respectively in Sections

3
and
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we provide some lemmas needed in the proof of Theorem
1
.

We begin by quoting, without proof, a classical Li-Yau-type gradient estimate for the first Dirichlet eigenfunction,
which can be found, for instance,
in
[
22
, Corollary 5.1]
(see also
[
20
,
23
]
).

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
u
be its first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunction, normalized so that
\max_{\overline{\Omega}}u=1
, and let

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
denote the first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue of
\Omega
. Then it holds that

|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+\lambda_{1}(\Omega)|u(x)|^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\qquad\forall x\in\Omega\,.

Next, we provide
two basic statements concerning
the convex envelope.
Recall that, if
\Omega
is an open bounded convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
for any
w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, its convex envelope in
\Omega
is the function

w^{**}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(6)

w^{**}(x):=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}w(x_{i})\ :\ m\in\mathbb{N},\ t_{i}\geq 0,\ x_{i}\in\Omega\,,\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}=1,\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}x_{i}=x\Big\}.

The first statement (Lemma
5
) concerns some features of
w^{**}
, holding when
w
is lower semicontinuous and diverges to
+\infty
at the boundary of
\Omega
.
Although the proof relies on standard arguments, we include it for the sake of self-containedness.

The second statement (Lemma
6
) is essentially a less technical version of Proposition 1 in
[
1
]
,
holding when
w
is sufficiently smooth. It
gathers some information about the second order subjet of
w^{**}
at
x
,
that we denote by the usual notation
J^{2,-}w^{**}(x)
. (Recall that, by definition, it consists of all pairs

(p,A)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{{\rm sym}}
for which there exists a smooth function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
u-\varphi
has a local minimum at
x
and
(p,A)=(\nabla\varphi(x),\nabla^{2}\varphi(x))
.)

Lemma 5
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
let
w\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a lower semicontinuous function
satisfying

w(x)\to+\infty
as
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)\to 0
.

Then
w^{**}(x)\to+\infty

as
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)\to 0
.

Moreover, for every
x\in\Omega
, there exist

t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}>0
,
m\leq n+1
, with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}=1
,
and points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\Omega
, such that

(7)

w^{**}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}w(x_{i}),

i.e., the infimum which defines
w^{**}
according to (
6
) is attained.

Proof.

Let us prove first that
w^{**}(x)\to+\infty

as
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)\to 0
.

Since, under our assumptions,
w
is bounded from below,
by possibly adding a constant to
w
it is not restrictive to
assume that
w\geq 1
in
\Omega
.
Assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence
\{x_{h}\}\subset\Omega
and a constant
C>1
such that

\lim_{h\to+\infty}\dist(x_{h},\partial\Omega)=0,\qquad w^{**}(x_{h})\leq C\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{N}.

For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist points

x_{h}^{1},\ldots,x_{h}^{m_{h}}\in\Omega

and positive numbers
t_{h}^{1},\ldots,t_{h}^{m_{h}}>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m_{h}}t_{h}^{i}=1,\qquad x_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{h}}t_{h}^{i}x_{h}^{i},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{m_{h}}t_{h}^{i}w(x_{h}^{i})\leq w^{**}(x_{h})+1\,.

Let
K:=\{x\in\Omega\colon w(x)\leq C+2\}
.
By assumption,
K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
,
so that
\dist(K,\partial\Omega)>0
.
For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, let

I_{h}:=\big\{i\in\{1,\ldots,m_{h}\}\colon x_{h}^{i}\in K\big\},\qquad J_{h}:=\big\{i\in\{1,\ldots,m_{h}\}\colon x_{h}^{i}\not\in K\big\},\qquad\alpha_{h}:=\sum_{i\in I_{h}}t_{h}^{i}\,.

We have that

\begin{split}C&\geq w^{**}(x_{h})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m_{h}}t_{h}^{i}w(x_{h}^{i})-1=\sum_{i\in I_{h}}t_{h}^{i}w(x_{h}^{i})+\sum_{i\in J_{h}}t_{h}^{i}w(x_{h}^{i})-1\\
&\geq\sum_{i\in I_{h}}t_{h}^{i}w(x_{h}^{i})+(C+2)(1-\alpha_{h})-1\,.\end{split}

Taking into account that
w\geq 1
, from this estimate
we obtain that

\alpha_{h}\leq\sum_{i\in I_{h}}t_{h}^{i}w(x_{h}^{i})\leq(C+2)\alpha_{h}-1,

i.e.,

\alpha_{h}\geq 1/(C+1)
.
Finally, by the concavity of the distance function,

\dist(x_{h},\partial\Omega)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m_{h}}t_{h}^{i}\dist(x_{h}^{i},\partial\Omega)\geq\sum_{i\in I_{h}}t_{h}^{i}\dist(x_{h}^{i},\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{C+1}\,\dist(K,\partial\Omega),

in contradiction with the assumption

\dist(x_{h},\partial\Omega)\to 0
.

Let us prove now the second part of the lemma.
Let
x\in\Omega
.
By Carathéodory’s theorem, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
there exist points

x^{h}_{1},\ldots,x^{h}_{n+1}\in\Omega

and non-negative numbers
t^{h}_{1},\ldots,t^{h}_{n+1}
, such that

(8)

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}t^{h}_{i}=1,\qquad w^{**}(x)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}t^{h}_{i}w(x^{h}_{i})-\frac{1}{h}\,.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, we can extract a subsequence (not relabeled) such that

\lim_{h\to+\infty}x^{h}_{i}=x_{i}\in\overline{\Omega},\qquad\lim_{h\to+\infty}t^{h}_{i}=t_{i}\in[0,1]\,.

Clearly,
t_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}t_{i}=1
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
there exists
m\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
such that

t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}>0
and
t_{i}=0
for
i>m
,
i\leq n+1
.

Since our assumptions imply in particular that
w
is nonnegative,
by (
8
)
we have that, for every
i=1,\ldots,n+1
,

w^{**}(x)\geq t^{h}_{i}w(x^{h}_{i})-\frac{1}{h}\,.

Now, if
x_{i}\in\partial\Omega
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
,
by passing to the limit as
h\to+\infty
in the above inequality
and taking into account that
w(x^{h}_{i})\to+\infty
,
we deduce that
t_{i}=0
, so that
i\geq m+1
.
As a consequence, the points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
belong to
\Omega
,
and
x=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}x_{i}
.
Finally, by using the definition of
w^{**}
and the inequality in (
8
), we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}w(x_{i})\geq w^{**}(x)\geq\liminf_{h\to+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}t^{h}_{i}w(x^{h}_{i})\geq\liminf_{h\to+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}t^{h}_{i}w(x^{h}_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}w(x_{i}),

so that (
7
) follows.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
let
w\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
w(x)\to+\infty
as
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)\to 0
.

Given
x\in\Omega
, let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}>0
,
m\leq n+1
, with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}=1
,
and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\Omega
be optimal for the infimum defining
w^{**}(x)
according to (
6
).

If
(p,A)\in J^{2,-}w^{**}(x)
, then
the following properties hold:

(i)

\nabla w(x_{i})=p

for every
i=1,\ldots,m
;

(ii)

the matrices
A_{i}:=\nabla^{2}w(x_{i})
are positive semidefinite for every
i=1,\ldots,m
;

(iii)

if all the matrices
A_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, are positive definite,
then

A\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}A_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1},\qquad\Tr(A)\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\Tr(A_{i}^{-1})\right)^{-1};

(iv)

if at least one of the matrices
A_{i}
is degenerate, then

\Tr(A)\leq 0
.

Proof.

The properties (i)–(iv) were already discussed in
[
1
, p. 272]
.
However, for the sake of clarity, we provide some additional details concerning items (iii) and (iv).
In
[
1
]
the authors, after establishing (i) and (ii), went on to show that

(9)

\left\langle Ah,\,h\right\rangle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\left\langle A_{i}h_{i},\,h_{i}\right\rangle

for every
h,h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

h=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}h_{i}
.
If all the matrices
A_{i}
are positive definite, then for every fixed
h

we can minimize the right-hand side of (
9
)
under the constraint
h=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}h_{i}
,
obtaining

h_{i}=A_{i}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}A_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1}\!\!\!\!\!h,\qquad i=1,\ldots m\,.

Replacing these expressions for
h_{i}
into (
9
) yields
the first inequality in (iii).

The second inequality then follows from the concavity
of the map
Q\mapsto[\Tr(Q^{-1})]^{-1}
on the class of
positive definite symmetric matrices, proved in the Appendix of
[
1
]
.

To prove (iv), let
A_{i,N}:=A_{i}+\frac{I}{N}
, where
I
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix.
From (
9
) we have

\left\langle Ah,\,h\right\rangle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\left\langle A_{i,N}h_{i},\,h_{i}\right\rangle\,.

By performing the same minimization of the right-hand side as above,
we deduce that (iii) holds with the matrices
A_{i,N}
in place of
A_{i}
.
If
A_{k}
is degenerate for some
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
then
\Tr(A_{k,N}^{-1})\to+\infty
as
N\to+\infty
. Therefore,
from the second inequality in (iii), we conclude that

\Tr(A)\leq 0
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

Throughout this section, we assume without any further mention that

u
is the first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunction of an open bounded convex subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
normalized so that
\max_{\overline{\Omega}}u=1
. For any
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
we set

(10)

w_{\kappa}:=(-\log(\kappa u))^{1/2}\qquad\text{ in }\Omega\,.

Observe that
\min_{\Omega}w_{\kappa}=(-\log\kappa)^{1/2}>0
.
Moreover, some straightforward computations show that
w_{\kappa}
is a (classical) solution to

(11)

-\Delta w+\frac{1}{w}\big[(2w^{2}-1)|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{2}\big]=0\qquad\text{ in }\Omega\,.

We shall denote by
w_{\kappa}^{**}
the convex envelope of
w_{\kappa}
defined according to (
6
),
and by
u_{k}
the function defined in
\Omega
by

(12)

u_{\kappa}:=\exp(-\log\kappa-(w_{\kappa}^{**})^{2})\,,\qquad\text{i.e.},\ w_{\kappa}^{**}=(-\log(\kappa u_{\kappa}))^{1/2}\,.

We now outline of the main steps of our proof:

–

In Proposition
7
we establish a gradient estimate for
w_{\kappa}^{**}
, valid for every
\kappa\in(0,1)
, as a consequence of the improved logconcavity inequality (
5
).

–

In Proposition
8
we prove that, provided
\kappa\in(0,\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)]
,
w_{\kappa}^{**}
is a viscosity supersolution of equation (
11
) in
\Omega
.

–

In Proposition
9
we show that, for every
\kappa\in(0,\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)]
,
u_{\kappa}
belongs to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
attains the same maximum value
1
as
u
, and satisfies
-\Delta u_{\kappa}\leq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)u_{\kappa}
a.e. in
\Omega
.

We then deduce Theorem
1
by exploiting the simplicity of the first eigenfunction.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,1)
and assume that, at some point
x\in\Omega
, one has
w_{\kappa}^{**}(x)<w_{\kappa}(x)
.
Let
x_{i}\in\Omega
,
i=1,\dots,m
(with
m\geq 2
) be points where the infimum which defines
w_{\kappa}^{**}(x)
according to
(
6
) is attained, and let
p_{\kappa}

denote the common value of
\nabla w_{\kappa}
at any of the points
x_{i}
, as given by Lemma
6
(i).
Then

|p_{\kappa}|^{2}\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{2D_{\Omega}^{2}}\,.

Proof.

Since the function
w_{\kappa}^{2}
satisfies the improved log-concavity estimate (

), for every pair of distinct points

in

, we have

\langle w_{\kappa}(z)\nabla w_{\kappa}(z)-w_{\kappa}(y)\nabla w_{\kappa}(y),\frac{z-y}{|z-y|}\rangle\geq\frac{\pi}{D_{\Omega}}\tan\Big(\frac{\pi|z-y|}{2D_{\Omega}}\Big)\,.

Pick two distinct points

and
x_{j}
among those at which the infimum which defines

according to
(

.
Applying the above estimate, we obtain

\langle(w_{\kappa}(x_{i})-w_{\kappa}(x_{j}))p_{\kappa},\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|}\rangle\geq\frac{\pi}{D_{\Omega}}\tan\Big(\frac{\pi|x_{i}-x_{j}|}{2D_{\Omega}}\Big)\,.

Since

is affine on the segment

Proposition 8

For any

Proof.

Let

F(w_{\kappa}^{**}(x),p,A)\geq 0\qquad\forall x\in\Omega\,,\quad\forall(p,A)\in J^{2,-}w_{\kappa}^{**}(x)\,.

We first observe that (

(p,A)\in J^{2,-}w_{\kappa}(x)

) holds because

is a classical solution, hence a viscosity solution, to equation (

Observe that this case happens, in particular, when

u
.

Thus, in the remaining of the proof we are going to assume that
w_{\kappa}^{**}(x)<w_{\kappa}(x)
and
p\neq 0

By Lemma
5
, there exist
t^{\kappa}_{i}\in(0,1)
and
x^{\kappa}_{i}\in\Omega
,
i=1,\dots,m_{\kappa}
(with
m_{\kappa}\geq 2
),
such that

(14)

\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\kappa}}t^{\kappa}_{i}=1\,,\qquad x=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\kappa}}t^{\kappa}_{i}x^{\kappa}_{i}\,,\qquad w^{**}_{\kappa}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\kappa}}t^{\kappa}_{i}w_{\kappa}(x^{\kappa}_{i})\,.

Throughout the proof, the parameter

, the point

, and accordingly the

we are going to write for brevity

(with

Moreover, we set

(15)

so that

\displaystyle F(w^{**},p,A)=-\Tr(A)+2|p|^{2}\frac{(w^{**})^{2}+a}{w^{**}}\,,

\displaystyle F(w^{i},p,A_{i})=-\Tr(A_{i})+2|p|^{2}\frac{(w^{i})^{2}+a}{w^{i}}\,.

By Lemma

(ii), we know that all the matrices

are positive semi-definite.
Let us first prove the inequality (

In this case, by Lemma

(iii), it holds that

Hence,

\displaystyle F(w^{**},p,A)=-{\rm Tr}(A)+2|p|^{2}\frac{(w^{**})^{2}+a}{w^{**}}

\displaystyle\geq-\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\big[{\rm Tr}(A_{i})\big]^{-1}\Big\}^{-1}+2|p|^{2}\frac{(w^{**})^{2}+a}{w^{**}}

\displaystyle=-\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\Big[2|p|^{2}\frac{(w^{i})^{2}+a}{w^{i}}\Big]^{-1}\Big\}^{-1}+2|p|^{2}\frac{(w^{**})^{2}+a}{w^{**}}\,,

where in the last equality we have used the identity

(16)

F(w^{i},p,A_{i})=0\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m,

holding since
w_{\kappa}
satisfies the pde (
11
)
at the points
x_{i}
’s.

We infer that the target inequality (
13
) is fulfilled provided

(17)

\frac{w^{**}}{(w^{**})^{2}+a}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\frac{w^{i}}{(w^{i})^{2}+a}\,.

We now observe that, since we are assuming that the matrices
A_{i}
are positive definite, and since
the equalities (
16
) hold, we have

0<{\rm Tr}(A_{i})=2|p|^{2}\,\frac{(w^{i})^{2}+a}{w^{i}}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m.

We deduce that

(18)

w^{i}\in\mathcal{R}:=\big\{s\in(0,+\infty)\text{ such that }s^{2}>-a\big\}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m\,.

Hence, we see that
the inequality (
17
) is satisfied provided the values
\{w^{1},\dots,w^{m},w^{**}\}

\mathcal{R}_{0}
of
\mathcal{R}
where the function

is convex.

we have

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}=\mathcal{R}\cap\{s^{2}\geq 3a\}\,.

So we distinguish two cases:

Case 1
:

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}=\mathcal{R}=\{s>\sqrt{-a\}}\,.

By (

), we have that, for every

w^{i}
lie in
\mathcal{R}_{0}

\mathcal{R}_{0}
, also
w^{**}
lie in
\mathcal{R}_{0}

the required inequality (

) is satisfied, and our proof is achieved.

Case 2
:
a>0
. In this case, imposing that

amounts to ask that

(19)

|p|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{4}\frac{1}{\frac{(w^{i})^{2}}{3}+\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m\,.

We claim that these inequalities are fulfilled thanks to the assumption that the constant

10

\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)
.
Indeed, since

, we are in a position to apply Proposition

\frac{\pi^{2}}{2D_{\Omega}^{2}}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{4}\frac{1}{\frac{(w^{i})^{2}}{3}+\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m\,,

or equivalently

(iv), we know that

To conclude we observe that: if

Proposition 9
.

\kappa\in(0,\overline{\kappa}(\Omega)]

Circular RNA profiling uncovers regulators of early body color transition in juvenile leopard coral grouper (Plectropomus leopardus)
Authors: Xianwu Deng, Chenxian Huang, Qiuyan Wu, Silvana Rahayu, Xi Liu, Lei Pang, Fen Hu, Huapu Chen, Chunhua Zhu, Mouyan Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe leopard coral grouper is a valued aquaculture species whose market value is influenced by its body color. Current research on its coloration focuses on adults, leaving early developmental mechanisms unclear. Circular RNAs are important regulators in various processes, including pigmentation. This study aimed to explore the role of circRNAs in early color formation of leopard coral grouper.ResultsUsing skin tissues from transparent (26 dph) and newly pigmented red (30 dph) juveniles, we identified 42 differentially expressed circRNAs. Their source genes were enriched in pathways like Hedgehog signaling, cAMP signaling, and carotenoid metabolism. Four key circRNAs were identified, and a circRNA-miRNA network analysis suggested their involvement in melanin synthesis, pigment cell development, and carotenoid metabolism. The qPCR verification results were consistent with the trends in the sequencing results.ConclusionsThis study provides the first circRNA profile during early color transition in leopard coral grouper, revealing a potential post-transcriptional regulatory network. Introduction

Fish body coloration, as a complex and diverse phenotypic trait, exhibits significant variations across different taxa, species, and even developmental stages [2]. This characteristic not only plays a pivotal role in social behaviors of fish (such as interspecific communication, predation, and camouflage) but also directly determines their economic value [3–6], making it a research hotspot in life sciences. Recent studies have revealed that non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) constitute the largest proportion of the transcriptome [7] and have been demonstrated to play crucial roles in regulating fish body coloration [8, 9].

Non-coding RNAs are mainly divided into microRNA (miRNA), long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), and circular RNA (circRNA), while a subset of circRNAs have been reported to encode functional peptides via internal ribosome entry site (IRES)-mediated translation [10–13]. CircRNAs are a class of covalently closed single-stranded RNA molecules, which are distinguished from linear RNAs by the absence of 5' cap and 3' poly(A) tail structures, and have higher stability in eukaryotic cells [14–16]. Based on genomic origins, circRNAs can be categorized into various types including exonic, antisense, exon-intronic, intergenic, intronic, and single-exon circRNAs, with exonic and intronic circRNAs being the most abundant [17]. Additionally, circRNAs can regulate the expression of their source genes by competitively binding miRNAs through shared or similar target sites, thereby acting as miRNA sponges to modulate miRNA activity. They participate in diverse biological processes including body coloration, development, immunity, and reproduction [8, 18–20].

In the regulation of fish body coloration, multiple studies suggest that circRNAs may play a crucial role. For instance, Hao et al. [8] identified 24 differentially expressed circRNAs in red and black color morphs of adult Plectropomus leopardus, and constructed 19 corresponding competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory networks. Functional enrichment analysis further revealed that these circRNAs participate in melanin metabolism and immune responses. Similarly, Sun et al. [21] detected 73 differentially expressed circRNAs in the skin of Cynoglossus semilaevis, with their source genes significantly enriched in pigmentation-related pathways. Furthermore, studies in goats have also indicated that circ_000353 influences cashmere color by targeting miR-143—3p [22]. These results collectively suggest that circRNAs play a conserved regulatory role in animal body color formation.

The leopard coral grouper (Plectropomus leopardus) is a marine aquaculture species with extremely high economic value, whose market price is significantly affected by body color. Red individuals command nearly twice the price of brown-black individuals of the same size. At present, studies on its body coloration mainly focus on the adult stage, whereas the role of circular RNAs (circRNAs) in body color formation during the juvenile stage remains unclear. Our preliminary observations revealed that juvenile P. leopardus maintain a transparent body surface until 26 days post hatching (dph), and complete early red pigmentation by 30 dph. Within this short developmental window, rapid differentiation of skin pigment cells and a critical transition in body color phenotype occur, making this stage an ideal model for investigating the regulatory mechanisms underlying early pigmentation in fish. Accordingly, we performed circRNA sequencing on juvenile P. leopardus at 26 dph (PT ‑ 26 dph) and 30 dph (PR—30 dph), aiming to identify key circRNAs involved in early skin pigmentation and construct the relevant circRNA—miRNA regulatory networks. This study is the first to reveal the molecular basis of body color formation in juvenile P. leopardus from the perspective of circRNAs, providing new insights for further elucidating its body color regulatory mechanism.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All animal experimental procedures performed in this study strictly complied with the guidelines set forth by the Animal Management and Use Committee of Guangdong Ocean University. Informed consent for the use of the animals in this study was obtained from the owner.

Experimental animals and skin sampling

The juvenile leopard coral grouper (Plectropomus leopardus) used in the experiment were collected from outdoor culture ponds of Hainan Oriental Star Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Plectropomus leopardus fertilized eggs were obtained from sexually mature broodstock in floating net cages of Lingshui, Hainan, China, and the hatched larvae and juveniles were reared in the outdoor ponds of the aforementioned company. Rearing conditions included a water temperature of 27—30℃, salinity of 30—33 ppt, natural photoperiod (shaded during incubation), and a feeding regime: pre-cultured starter feeds and live rotifers/copepods were provided for larvae at 3—28 days post hatching (dph), and juveniles at 28 dph were gradually weaned to formulated feed in flow-through tanks. Prior to sampling, all juveniles were observed and graded under a stereomicroscope, with only individuals presenting uniform and typical phenotypes selected for the study: 26 days post hatching transparent (PT—26 dph) juveniles with fully transparent bodies and no visible pigment deposition were assigned to the PT group, 30 days post hatching red (PR—30 dph) juveniles with fully red bodies and stable whole-body pigment deposition were assigned to the PR group, and individuals with abnormal or intermediate phenotypes were excluded. Three independent biological replicates were set for each group, with each replicate consisting of 50 randomly selected juveniles of the same phenotypic grade from the same culture pond (totaling 150 juveniles per group).

Prior to tissue sampling, experimental fish were subjected to euthanasia using a 300 mg/L aqueous solution of ethyl 3-methylsulfonylbenzoate (MS-222, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). The euthanasia process was deemed complete when three sequential endpoints were observed: first, the termination of gill cover movement; second, the loss of postural equilibrium; and third, the absence of any behavioral response to external sensory stimuli. This protocol guaranteed both rapidity and humaneness of euthanasia before tissue harvest. During sampling, the fry were anesthetized and fixed on a dissection board, with their skin surfaces cleaned using ultrapure water and dried with sterile gauze. Subsequently, alcohol-sterilized forceps and scissors were used to excise skin tissues from both sides of the fish body, which were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and transferred to a −80 °C ultra-low temperature freezer for long-term storage. A total of 150 PT and PR skin samples were respectively pooled into three biological replicates, labeled as PT1, PT2, PT3 and PR1, PR2, PR3. Total RNA extraction was performed following the protocol described by Xi Liu et al. [23].

CircRNA-seq data analysis

The circRNA sequencing was commissioned to Guangzhou Gidio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The brief procedure is as follows: After total RNA extraction, total RNA samples were treated with RNase R to remove linear RNA and enrich circular RNA (circRNA) prior to library construction. RNase R treatment (Vazyme JE2303KA, 20 U/μL): 1—4 U per 1 μg total RNA, 37 °C 30 min incubation, 70 °C 10 min inactivation; linear RNA depleted for circRNA enrichment. This was followed by library construction and sequencing using the Illumina HiSeq™ 4000 platform, for which paired-end sequencing was adopted with a read length of 150 bp. After obtaining raw data, high-quality sequencing reads (Clean Data) were aligned to the reference genome of P. leopardus (NC_056468.1) using HISAT2 (v2.1.0). circRNA identification was accomplished through the find_circ (v1.0) software [24]. We conducted basic characteristic analysis of the identified circRNAs, including classification, chromosomal location distribution, and length distribution. By comparing and annotating circRNAs against the circBank (www.circbank.cn) [25] and circBase (http://www.circbase.org/) [26] databases, those already existing in the databases were defined as known circRNAs, while those without matches were defined as newly predicted circRNAs.

Differential analysis of circRNA

Differential expression analysis was performed using the edgeR [27] package. circRNAs meeting the screening criteria of log2|fold change|> 1 (|Fold Change|> 2) and p-value < 0.05 were identified as significantly differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs). The source genes generating circRNAs were defined as their source genes. We conducted Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses on both the circRNA source genes and DEC source genes to infer their potential biological functions.

Construction and analysis of circRNA-miRNA network

To explore the circRNA-miRNA regulatory mechanism, we first obtained circRNA-miRNA targeting relationships from the StarBase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) database [28], and predicted unreported interactions between identified circRNAs and P. leopardus miRNAs via miRanda (v1.2; http://mirtoolsgallery.tech/mirtoolsgallery/node/1055) [29] and TargetScan (v7.0; https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/) [30]. Next, we integrated 42 differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs) from this study with 106 previously reported differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) in early juvenile P. leopardus [23], the two sets of sequencing data were derived from the same batch of juvenile fish individuals with completely consistent rearing conditions and sampling time points, ensuring reliable biological comparability. And performed stepwise screening for circRNA-miRNA pairs: first, pairs with Spearman's rank correlation coefficient ≤ −0.5 were retained; second, pairs with correlation coefficient ≥ 0.7 were defined as potential interaction pairs; finally, pairs with P < 0.05 in the hypergeometric cumulative distribution function test [31] were identified as the final valid interaction pairs. On this basis, we constructed and visualized the circRNA-miRNA regulatory network using Cytoscape (v3.7.1), and screened core circRNAs related to body color formation.

Quantitative validation of circRNA

To further validate the authenticity of our circRNA data, we randomly selected eight DECs for verification. Total RNA samples were treated with RNase R to remove linear RNA. RNase R treatment (Vazyme JE2303KA, 20 U/μL): 1–4 U of enzyme per 1 μg total RNA, incubated at 37 °C for 30 min and inactivated at 70 °C for 10 min. Using Primer Premier 5 software, sequences spanning the backsplice junction were designed from circRNA-Seq data (Supplementary Table S1). qRT-PCR was then performed on the LightCycler® 480 II instrument (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) using 5 μl SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, Japan), 0.3 μl each of Forward primer and Reverse primer, 1 μl cDNA, and 3.4 μl ddH2O. The thermal cycling program was set as follows: initial pre-denaturation at 95℃ for 5 min; 40 cycles of denaturation at 95℃ for 30 s, annealing at 60℃ for 20 s, and extension at 72℃ for 20 s; a final full extension at 72℃ for 10 min; and a final hold at 4℃ for sample storage. Ribosomal protein L13 (Rpl13) [32–34] was used as the internal reference gene. Gene expression analysis and statistical data processing were conducted using the comparative Ct method (2−ΔΔCt) [35] and SPSS statistical software package (version 22.0), respectively.

Discussion

As a valuable marine fish species, the body color of P. leopardus (particularly the red phenotype) is a crucial factor influencing market acceptance. Fish skin coloration serves not only as an important phenotypic characteristic but also as the first physiological barrier against pathogen invasion. Recent studies have demonstrated that non-coding RNAs play key roles in regulating skin pigmentation and immune processes in fish [8, 36]. With advancements in high-throughput sequencing technologies, several body color-related genes have been identified in multiple fish species. However, research on the role of circRNAs in fish body color formation, particularly during the juvenile stage, remains relatively limited. In this study, circRNA sequencing analysis was performed on the skin of early juvenile P. leopardus—26 days post hatching transparent and 30 days post hatching red. A total of 42 differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs) were identified, and the corresponding circRNA-miRNA regulatory network was constructed. These findings provide new insights and a data foundation for elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying body color transition in early juvenile P. leopardus at the post-transcriptional regulatory level.

Interaction between CircRNA and its source genes in pigmentation

Exon-derived circRNAs are often co-expressed with their linear transcripts. Mechanistically, they can compete with linear splicing machinery for the same splice sites, potentially leading to decreased linear mRNA expression [37, 38], or interact with RNA polymerase II to enhance host gene transcription [39–41]. To evaluate this regulatory relationship in P. leopardus, expression correlation analysis was performed between the identified DECs and their corresponding linear mRNA transcripts across the transparent and red sample groups. Our analysis revealed novel_circ_000018 originates from the pax7 gene, which has been reported to be associated with carotenoid metabolism in betta fish (Betta splendens), its deficiency leads to a reduction in the number of xanthophores in zebrafish embryos and adult stages [42]. The source gene of novel_circ_000144 is sox6. Research indicates that miR-380—3p in alpacas (Vicugna pacos) can regulate the expression of key genes involved in melanin synthesis (TYR, TYRP1, DCT) by directly targeting SOX6 via the β-catenin/MITF pathway [43]. Furthermore, the key gene mc1r (the source of novel_circ_000496) in the melanin synthesis pathway has the potential to participate in melanin synthesis regulation across various fish species [44–46]. Knockout experiments in white-black Oujiang color common carp (Cyprinus carpio var. color) revealed that its deficiency leads to skin albinism phenotype [47]. Another noteworthy gene, ap3s1 (the source of novel_circ_001181), exhibits significantly increased sensitivity to hypopigmentation in developing melanocytes under low-copper conditions when partially knocked out in zebrafish, either for Ap3s1 or Ap1s1 [48]. Based on these expression dynamics, we performed functional enrichment analysis on the source genes of circular RNAs and found that they were significantly enriched in multiple biological processes and signaling pathways related to body color regulation, including melanin metabolic process (GO:0006582), pigment metabolic process (GO:0042440), regulation of metabolic process (GO:0019222), regulation of Wnt signaling pathway (GO:0030111), pigment metabolic process involved in developmental pigmentation (GO:0043324), pigment metabolic process involved in pigmentation (GO:0043474), pigmentation (GO:0043473), Melanogenesis (ko04916), Tyrosine metabolism (ko00350), and Thyroid hormone signaling pathway (ko04919). It is noteworthy that the vast majority of pigment cells are derived from neural crest cells [49]. In this study, genes including Dxb_GLEAN_10004759/Dxb_GLEAN_10012439 (nrp1a), Dxb_GLEAN_10015476 (sema3ab), and Dxb_GLEAN_10015495 (sema3b) were significantly enriched in GO terms such as neural crest cell migration (GO:0001755), neural crest cell development (GO:0014032), and neural crest cell differentiation (GO:0014033), implying the fundamental roles of early developmental events of pigment cells during body color formation. These results collectively suggest that the source genes of these circRNAs may affect skin pigmentation in Plectropomus leopardus by regulating key processes including pigment cell fate determination, pigment synthesis and metabolism.

To further screen key DECs associated with skin pigmentation in juvenile P. leopardus, we focused on seven significantly enriched pigment-related pathways through KEGG pathway analysis (Fig. 6), and identified four critical circRNAs along with their source genes. Among these, novel_circ_000598 originates from the gli3 gene, which is enriched in the Hedgehog signaling pathway (ko04340) and cAMP signaling pathway (ko04024). This gene has been reported to be involved in pigment formation across various animal species, studies have demonstrated that gli3 is involved in regulating neural crest cell differentiation and melanin synthesis in multiple animal models [50–52]. Zhang et al. found that in Gangba sheep, gli3 inhibits melanin synthesis by interfering with the differentiation of neural crest cells into melanocytes [50]. Guan et al. mapped gli3 to chromosome 4 in the Murciano-Granadina goat genome [51]. Loss of its function can lead to decreased expression of early melanocyte marker genes (mitf, sox10, dct, and si) and hypopigmentation during embryonic development [52]. Comparative analysis of skin samples from Thai flag betta, blue betta, and white betta revealed upregulated expression of Bsgli3 in Thai flag betta [53]. Furthermore, gli3 as a key regulator of the Hedgehog signaling pathway, is involved in cell differentiation during various embryonic developmental processes, such as being essential for the normal expression of ath5 and the differentiation of retinal ganglion cells in the eye [54]. Therefore, we speculate that novel_circ_000598 may participate in regulating melanin synthesis in the P. leopardus through gli3.

The source gene of novel_circ_001397 is mme, which is enriched in the Protein digestion and absorption pathway (ko04974). This pathway has been shown to function in the body color transition from gray to red in red crucian carp [55], identified as a pathway related to pigmentation and structural colors in discus fish [56]. Additionally, mme has been reported as a pigmentation-related gene in pigs [57], suggesting that this circRNA may participate in the regulation of pigmentation metabolism in P. leopardus. novel_circ_000453 is derived from the plpp2 gene and is significantly enriched in lipid-related pathways such as Fat digestion and absorption (ko04975), Sphingolipid metabolism (ko00600), Glycerophospholipid metabolism (ko00564), and Metabolic pathways (ko01100). Given that the transport and deposition of carotenoids (the primary pigments for red and yellow coloration) are closely associated with lipid metabolism [58], and considering that plpp2 has been reported to correlate with meat color traits [59], it is hypothesized that novel_circ_000453 may participate in the formation of red body coloration by influencing lipid metabolism and carotenoid deposition. Furthermore, the source gene ptch1 of novel_circ_001494 is also enriched in the Hedgehog signaling pathway (ko04340) and cAMP signaling pathway (ko04024). In common carp, this gene has been associated with scale development [1], suggesting its potential indirect influence on body color presentation through affecting scale structure or pigment distribution. These findings provide important candidate molecules for subsequent functional validation, though the specific regulatory relationship between circRNA and its source gene requires further experimental elucidation.

The potential role of circRNA-miRNA network in pigmentation

CircRNA can function as a molecular sponge to adsorb miRNA, thereby relieving the inhibitory effect of miRNA on its target genes [60, 61], playing a crucial role in fish body color regulation. In this study, by integrating the analysis of 42 DECs with 106 differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) obtained from previous research [23], a circRNA-miRNA regulatory network comprising 144 potential targeting relationships was constructed (Fig. 7). The study further focused on core interaction pairs related to melanin synthesis, pigment cell development, and carotenoid metabolism. Regarding melanin synthesis, the findings revealed that the upregulated novel_circ_001207 may interact with the downregulated miR-137—y. A previous study [62] has suggested that miR-137 can inhibit melanogenesis by suppressing the core receptor c-Kit of the SCF/c-Kit signaling pathway and its downstream target gene Tyrp2. In the Manila clam, the expression of miR-137 is significantly lower in orange individuals compared to white and zebra-striped individuals [63]. In the red P. leopardus, the downregulation of miR-137—y may inhibit eumelanin synthesis by targeting epha2 and slc7a11, thereby promoting the formation of the red phenotype [23]. Therefore, the upregulated novel_circ_001207 may act as an endogenous molecular sponge for the downregulated miR-137—y, thereby relieving the inhibitory effect of miR-137—y on its target genes related to melanin synthesis, and ultimately participating in the regulation of red body color formation in P. leopardus. Similarly, this study found that the upregulated miR-141—z in red P. leopardus [23] had potential binding sites with multiple downregulated circRNAs (including novel_circ_000141, novel_circ_001071, novel_circ_001377, novel_circ_001461, and novel_circ_001496). It is speculated that the downregulation of these circRNAs weakens their competitive adsorption of miR-141—z, thereby enhancing the inhibitory effect of miR-141—z on its target genes related to melanin formation, and ultimately promoting the development of red body coloration. miR-27a-3p exhibits melanin-inhibiting effects in mammals, with its expression level in alpaca white skin being six times higher than that in brown skin [64, 65]. In this study, miR-27—z, a homolog of miR-27a-3p, was upregulated in red individuals, and was predicted to have potential binding sites with multiple downregulated circRNAs including novel_circ_000141 and novel_circ_000827. We speculate that these downregulated circRNAs may act as miRNA sponges, and their reduced expression would weaken the competitive adsorption of miR-27—z, thereby enhancing the melanin-inhibiting effect of miR-27—z and participating in the regulation of melanin synthesis in P. leopardus. miR-455—x and miR-455—y were upregulated in red individuals, with their known target genes oca2 and ednrb being key positive regulators of melanogenesis [66]. In this study, the circRNAs predicted to be targeted by miR-455—x/y, such as novel_circ_000444 and novel_circ_000827, were downregulated in red individuals. It is inferred that these downregulated circRNAs may reduce the sponge adsorption of miR-455—x/y, thus enhancing the inhibitory effect of miR-455—x/y on melanin synthesis-related target genes, and participating in the regulation of melanin synthesis in P. leopardus. Slc7a11 is a key regulator of pheomelanin synthesis in mammalian skin [67]. Previous studies have suggested that novel-m0226—5p can inhibit eumelanin synthesis and promote red coloration by targeting epha2 and slc7a11 [23]. In this study, novel-m0226—5p was significantly upregulated in red individuals, and was predicted to have potential binding sites with the downregulated novel_circ_001496. We speculate that the downregulation of novel_circ_001496 may weaken its sponge adsorption of novel-m0226—5p, thereby enhancing the inhibitory effect of novel-m0226—5p on eumelanin synthesis, and participating in the regulation of red body color formation in P. leopardus.

The development of chromatophores is crucial for fish body coloration, originating from neural crest progenitor cells that migrate and differentiate into melanophores, xanthophores, etc. [68–70]. The mechanism underlying erythrophore development remains unclear, with some studies suggesting they may share a common progenitor with xanthophores [71]. In this study, the downregulated miR-423—x had potential binding sites with the upregulated novel_circ_000531 and novel_circ_000598. Previous research has shown that miR-423—x can target sox6, which is a key gene involved in the regulation of melanophore development [23]. Therefore, we speculate that these upregulated circRNAs may act as miRNA sponges to adsorb miR-423—x, relieve the inhibitory effect of miR-423—x on sox6, and thus participate in the regulation of chromatophore development processes. In addition, we found that novel-m0025—5p and miR-23—x had potential targeting relationships with a series of differentially expressed circRNAs. Relevant studies have shown that these two miRNAs are involved in the regulation of hair follicle growth and development [72, 73], suggesting that their targeted circRNAs may act as molecular sponges to regulate the activity of the two miRNAs, and thus participate in the proliferation, migration, or differentiation processes of pigment progenitor cells. However, their specific biological functions still require further experimental verification.

Carotenoids are the key pigments responsible for the red and yellow coloration in fish, which must be obtained through dietary intake. This study identified several potential circRNA-miRNA pairs involved in carotenoid metabolism, such as miR-204—x/y targeting multiple downregulated circRNAs (e.g., novel_circ_000827, novel_circ_001071). Previous studies have shown that miR-204—x/y can target bco1 (involved in carotenoid degradation), thereby promoting red phenotype formation [23]. However, other research has found that upregulated miR-204 may inhibit melanin synthesis by targeting slc18a3, as members of the SLC family have been reported to be associated with melanin production [74]. Therefore, the aforementioned downregulated circRNAs may weaken the competitive adsorption of the upregulated miR-204—x/y, enhance the inhibitory effect of miR-204—x/y on its target gene bco1 related to carotenoid degradation [23], and thus regulate the carotenoid metabolism pathway to affect the red body color formation of P. leopardus. Meanwhile, these circRNAs may also participate in the regulation of melanin synthesis through the miR-204-slc18a3 axis [74]. However, the specific molecular regulatory mechanisms still require further in-depth investigation.

In summary, this study preliminarily revealed the potential important roles of circRNAs and their involved ceRNA networks during the transition from transparent to red body color in juvenile P. leopardus through circRNA sequencing and integrated analysis. The results indicated that multiple differentially expressed circRNAs might collectively influence the final body color phenotype by regulating the expression of genes related to melanin synthesis, pigment cell development, and carotenoid metabolism. These findings provide new clues and theoretical basis for in-depth understanding of post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms in fish body color formation. Future studies should further validate the specific biological functions and regulatory pathways of key circRNAs through gain-of-function/loss-of-function experiments.

Conclusion

We inferred that circRNAs are potentially involved in body color development of early juvenile P. leopardus by performing circRNA sequencing analysis on the skin tissues of transparent individuals at 26 days post hatching (dph) and red individuals at 30 dph, identifying a total of 42 differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs). Functional enrichment analysis revealed that the source genes of these DECs were significantly enriched in multiple pigmentation-related pathways. Further screening yielded seven key pigmentation-associated KEGG pathways, from which four core circRNAs and their source genes potentially implicated in body color transition were identified. Notably, we constructed a regulatory network consisting of 18 circRNAs and 14 miRNAs, suggesting that multiple DECs may collectively contribute to body color phenotype formation by regulating genes involved in melanin synthesis, pigment cell development, and carotenoid metabolism. Collectively, these findings indicate that circRNAs play a potentially crucial regulatory role in the body color development of juvenile P. leopardus, not only providing valuable data for deciphering the post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms underlying pigmentation but also offering new insights into the molecular basis of fish body color formation.

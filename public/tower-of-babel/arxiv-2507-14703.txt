Bounded compactness from G(E)UP
Roberto Casadio, Ilim Irfan Çimdiker, Octavian Micu, Jonas Mureika

Abstract
We analyse how different Generalised Uncertainty Principles could place bounds on the compactness of self-gravitating systems. By considering existing experimental bounds on the relevant parameters, we conclude that the compactness of large astrophysical objects is bounded above by the inverse of the GUP parameter, which would naturally be of order one. Conversely, the existence of black holes imposes stronger bounds on those parameters.



1
Uncertainty Principles and Quantum Gravity

The nature of gravity and quantum physics seem to imply that physical measurements cannot probe arbitrarily
short length scales
[
1
,
2
]
.
Typically, a minimum scale is assumed of the order of the Planck length on dimensional grounds and
considering that black hole geometries are supposed to emerge in scattering processes at Planckian
energies
[
3
]
.
However, semiclassical studies of collapsing compact objects (possibly of astrophysical size), generically
predict a bounce at a scale that remains largely undetermined
[
4
]
, and corresponds
to a quantum transition from the formation of a black hole to a white hole geometry
[
5
]
.
Moreover, a quantum description of the (very large number of) particles forming such objects
shows that the ground state at the bounce could be a (significant) fraction of the horizon radius,
hence many orders of magnitude larger than the Planck length
[
6
]
.
These results suggest that quantum physics might not just imply the existence of a minimum length
but also of a maximum compactness for self-gravitating objects
[
7
]
.

A rather straightforward way of implementing a minimum length in quantum mechanics is to
consider a Generalised Uncertainty Principle (GUP) for the position
x
and momentum
p

of a particle, which can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta x\,\Delta p\geq\hbar\left(1+\alpha\,\frac{\Delta p^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\ ,

(1.1)

where the dimensionless parameter
\alpha
denotes the additional uncertainty introduced
by the formalism and
m_{\rm p}
is the Planck mass.
The minimum length is then given by
\ell_{\rm min}\sim\sqrt{\alpha}\,\ell_{\rm p}
, where
\ell_{\rm p}
is the
Planck length.
The literature is replete with studies devoted to the GUP, mostly as they relate to the quantum
gravity regime.
The GUP itself is a common feature of a variety of quantum gravity theories, including
string theory
[
8
,
9
,
3
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
,
loop quantum gravity
[
13
,
14
]
, ultraviolet self-complete gravity
[
15
]
,
non-commutative quantum mechanics
[
16
]
, and generic minimum length
models
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
A full summary of GUP black hole related works would be exhaustive, but the interested reader
is referred to
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
and references therein.
A recent work
[
28
]
provided a comprehensive study of quantum and classical constraints
on the GUP parameter as it relates to the “
M+1/M
” model presented in
[
27
]
.
Such GUP effects are strictly relegated to the domain of Planck-scale black holes and their thermodynamics,
so this work will provide a new perspective on the GUP as it relates to astrophysical scale black holes.

Compared to the GUP, the literature on the Extended Uncertainty Principle (EUP) is sparse,
but has been growing steadily over the past few years.
As a complement to the GUP, the EUP introduces a minimum momentum scale, or alternatively
a maximum length scale, at which quantum gravity effects will manifest themselves.
This is written as

\Delta x\,\Delta p\geq\hbar\left(1+\frac{\bar{\beta}}{L_{*}^{2}}\,\Delta x^{2}\right)\ ,

(1.2)

where
L_{*}
is some fundamental large length, possibly, but not necessarily, of the Hubble scale.
A number of studies have considered large scale EUP effects on black
holes
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
The weak field limit of an EUP-modified Schwarzschild black hole metric was also derived
in
[
33
]
, where it was suggested that the length scale
L_{*}
can fix the flattening
radius of galactic rotation curves, and thus provide an alternative to dark matter.
Additional related studies include the effect of the EUP on strong lensing effects in Sgr A*
and M87
[
34
]
, the Shapiro time delay, Mercury’s perihelion shift, and S2 star orbit
precessions
[
35
]
.
Ref.
[
36
]
also explored lensing effects, in addition to solar spin precession
and the motion of binary pulsar systems.
Lastly, the EUP’s influence on cosmology has been addressed, namely through connections
with the Hubble tension
[
37
]
and the cosmological constant been
addressed
[
38
]
.
Most recently, the authors of
[
39
]
have shown the EUP to be emergent
from non-local gravity effects.

The combination of the GUP and the EUP is known as the Generalised Extended Uncertainty
Principle (GEUP), and assumes the form

\Delta x\,\Delta p\geq\hbar\left(1+\alpha\,\Delta p^{2}+\frac{\bar{\beta}}{L_{*}^{2}}\,\Delta x^{2}\right)\ ,

(1.3)

The main aim of the present work is to see if the existence of a minimum length of the order
of the Planck scale generically implies bounds on the compactness of self-gravitating
systems.
To investigate this question, we will consider the GUP
[
3
]

and the GEUP
[
29
]
as effective ways to incorporate
the existence of a minimum length in quantum mechanics by modifying the Heisenberg
Uncertainty Principle (HUP).

2
Compactness and Quantum Uncertainties

We describe a compact object by means of a quantum wavefunction
\psi=\psi(x_{i})

with Cartesian coordinates
x_{i}=(x,y,z)
in its centre-of-mass reference frame, so that

\displaystyle\bra{\psi}\hat{x}_{i}\ket{\psi}\equiv\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{x}_{i}\,\rangle$}=0=\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{p}_{i}\,\rangle$}\ .

(2.1)

The restriction to the centre-of-mass reference frame should remove unphysical deviations
from well-established classical dynamics
[
40
]
, an issue known as the
“soccer ball problem”
[
1
]
.
We then have the uncertainties

\displaystyle\Delta x_{i}^{2}\equiv\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{x}_{i}^{2}\,\rangle$}-\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{x}^{i}\,\rangle$}^{2}=\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{x}_{i}^{2}\,\rangle$}

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\Delta p_{i}^{2}\equiv\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{p}_{i}^{2}\,\rangle$}-\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{p}_{i}\,\rangle$}^{2}=\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{p}_{i}^{2}\,\rangle$}\ .

(2.3)

Furthermore, we assume spherical symmetry, which implies

\displaystyle\Delta x_{i}^{2}=\frac{R^{2}}{3}\ ,

(2.4)

and the distribution of momentum uncertainties is isotropic,

\displaystyle\Delta p_{x}^{2}=\Delta p_{y}^{2}=\Delta p_{z}^{2}=\frac{M^{2}}{3}\ ,

(2.5)

where the mass of the object is given by the dispersion relation
M^{2}\simeq p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}^{2}+p_{z}^{2}
.

The (dimensionless) compactness of a star or black hole is defined as the
ratio
1
1

1

From now on, we mostly use units with
\hbar=\ell_{\rm p}m_{\rm p}
, and
G_{\rm N}=\ell_{\rm p}/m_{\rm p}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{R}=\frac{\ell_{\rm p}\,M}{m_{\rm p}\,R}\ ,

(2.6)

where now
M
and
R
are obtained from Eqs. (
2.5
) and (
2.4
).
It is important to remark that we employed Cartesian coordinates in the above argument
because all of the operators
\hat{x}_{i}
and
\hat{p}_{i}=-i\,\hbar\,\partial_{i}
are Hermitian
[on square integrable functions
\psi=\psi(x_{i})
of the real numbers].
Although spherical coordinates would seem a more natural choice to describe spherically
symmetric systems, the operator
\hat{p}_{r}=-i\,\hbar\,\partial_{r}
is not Hermitian,
and it would not be clear how to implement uncertainty relations to bound the compactness
in that frame.

3
Upper bound from GUP

The simple GUP is given by Eq. (
1.1
), which we rewrite as

\displaystyle\Delta x\,\Delta p\gtrsim\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}\left(1+\alpha\,\frac{\Delta p^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.1)

and assume that
\alpha
is positive.
In the extremal case, in which the equality holds, there are two solutions for
\Delta p
,
to wit

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\pm}=\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\Delta x}{2\,\alpha\,\ell_{\rm p}}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\Delta x^{2}}}\right)\ .

(3.2)

Note that
\Delta p_{-}
reproduces the HUP in the limit
\alpha\to 0^{+}
(or for
\Delta x\gg\ell_{\rm p}
).
This restriction will be taken into account to describe objects with size
R\sim\Delta x\gg\ell_{\rm p}
.

The above Eq. (
3.2
) for
\alpha>0
yields complex momenta for
\Delta x
smaller than a minimum length
determined by

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\Delta x^{2}}}=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\Delta x=2\,\sqrt{\alpha}\,\ell_{\rm p}\equiv\ell_{\rm GUP}\ ,

(3.3)

which is also called the remnant limit in the thermodynamic community
[
43
,
44
]
.
This limit for the physical solution
\Delta p=\Delta p_{-}
from Eq. (
3.2
) is shown in Fig.
1

for positive values of
\alpha
.
We recall that negative values of
\alpha
do not imply the existence a minimum length scale.
However, this case still results in objects with exotic behaviour such as an infinite lifetime
[
45
]
.

\Delta p=\Delta p_{-}
from Eq. (
3.2
), with
\Delta x
in units of
\ell_{\rm p}
and
\Delta p
in units of
m_{\rm p}
.

The inequality in Eq. (
3.1
) can be solved in terms of
\Delta x
,

\displaystyle\Delta x\gtrsim\frac{\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}}{\Delta p}\left(1+\alpha\frac{\Delta p^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.4)

and replaced in the definition of the compactness from Eq. (
2.6
), to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\lesssim\frac{M^{2}}{3\,m_{\rm p}^{2}+\alpha\,M^{2}}\simeq\frac{1}{\alpha}\ ,

(3.5)

where the approximate expression is for objects with mass
M\gg m_{\rm p}
.
Assuming
\alpha\sim 1
, the conclusion is that the GUP in Eq. (
3.1
) implies an upper
bound for the compactness of order one.

It is important to recall that black holes are characterised by
\mathcal{C}>1/2
, and Eq. (
3.5
)
therefore allows for their formation only provided
\alpha\lesssim 2
,
otherwise the gravitational collapse would lead to horizonless exotic compact objects
[
41
]
.
On the other hand, the limit
\alpha\to 0
, for fixed
M
, yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\lesssim\frac{M^{2}}{3\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\ ,

(3.6)

which implies that the usual HUP does not place any significant bound on the compactness
of astrophysical compact objects and formation of black holes with
M\gg m_{\rm p}
.

) bares a qualitative resemblance with

4
Bounds from GEUP

We rewrite the GEUP in Eq. (
1.3
) as

\displaystyle\Delta x\,\Delta p\gtrsim\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}\left(1+\alpha\,\frac{\Delta p^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}+\beta\,\frac{\Delta x^{2}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\ .

(4.1)

The additional term proportional to the dimensionless constant
\beta

is phenomenologically linked to a non-zero cosmological constant
[
29
]
,
which suggests the existence of a Hawking-Page-like phase transition.
From a thermodynamical perspective, it is also noteworthy that the limit
\alpha\to 0
of the GEUP
[that is, the EUP in Eq. (
1.2
)] exhibits a functional form identical to that
of the Rényi entropy
[
46
,
47
]
.

Again, in the extremal case there are two solutions

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\pm}=\frac{\Delta x\,m_{\rm p}}{2\,\alpha\,\ell_{\rm p}}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta-4\,\alpha\,\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\Delta x^{2}}}\right)\ ,

(4.2)

where the solution
\Delta p_{-}
again reduces to the HUP for
\alpha\to 0^{+}
and any finite value of
\beta
.
Like previously, for positive
\alpha
, we obtain a minimum measurable length determined by

\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta-4\,\alpha\,\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\Delta x^{2}}}=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\Delta x=\frac{2\,\ell_{\rm p}\,\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta}}\equiv\ell_{\rm GEUP}\ ,

(4.3)

which is real for
4\,\alpha\,\beta<1
.
This is the GEUP remnant limit, and the GUP case (
3.3
) is recovered for
\beta\to 0
.

Both solutions
\Delta p=\Delta p_{\pm}
in Eq. (
4.2
) display a sort of
phase transition
,
where the slope changes sign at

\displaystyle\Delta x_{\rm pt}=\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{\sqrt{\beta-4\,\alpha\,\beta^{2}}}\ ,

(4.4)

again provided
\beta>0
and
4\,\alpha\,\beta<1
.
In the limit
\alpha\to 0
, we obtain
\Delta x_{\rm pt}=\ell_{\rm p}/\sqrt{\beta}
, while
\Delta x_{\rm pt}

diverges for
\beta\to 0
, which is consistent with the GUP case of Section
3
.

In Fig.
2
, the minimum length values are highlighted for the solution
\Delta p=\Delta p_{-}
.
We see that positive values of
\alpha
lead to a minimum length, whereas positive values of
\beta

yield a phase transition.
Negative values of
\beta
, on the other hand, lead to unphysical negative momentum uncertainties.

Solving the inequality in Eq. (

) for
\Delta x
, we obtain the two constraints

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{\rm p}\,\Delta p}{2\,\beta\,m_{\rm p}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta-4\,\beta\,\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}}{\Delta p^{2}}}\right)\lesssim\Delta x\lesssim\frac{\ell_{\rm p}\,\Delta p}{2\,\beta\,m_{\rm p}}\left(1+\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta-4\,\beta\,\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}}{\Delta p^{2}}}\right)\ ,

(4.5)

with the solution
\Delta p_{-}
yielding the correct HUP limit this time for
\beta\to 0
.
The corresponding compactness range is then given by

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\beta}{1+\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta-12\,\beta\,{m_{\rm p}^{2}}/{M^{2}}}}\lesssim\mathcal{C}\lesssim\frac{2\,\beta}{1-\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta-12\,\beta\,{m_{\rm p}^{2}}/{M^{2}}}}\ ,

(4.6)

which, in the large
M
limit, reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta}}{2\,\alpha}\lesssim\mathcal{C}\lesssim\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\,\alpha\,\beta}}{2\,\alpha}\ ,

(4.7)

where we again assumed
\beta>0
and
0\lesssim 4\,\alpha\,\beta<1
.
Note that the GUP bound (
3.5
) is recovered for
\beta\to 0
, whereas the limit

\alpha\to 0
for finite
\beta>0
yields a lower bound
\mathcal{C}\gtrsim\beta
.

In Fig.
3

and above by the values corresponding to inequality (
3.5
).
Negative values of
\beta
also yield a valid upper bound displayed in Fig.
4

\mathcal{C}
).

5
Discussion and conclusions

In this work we have obtained bounds on the compactness of self-gravitating objects
depending on the GUP and GEUP parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Such parameters are in turn bounded by their effects on experimental observations.
For the GUP, one the strongest constraint
\alpha\lesssim 10^{7}
comes from
the study of harmonic oscillators
[
48
]
.

For the EUP,
bounds are typically placed on the associated length scale
L_{*}
.
To our knowledge, the strongest of such bounds is obtained from the precession
of S2’s orbit and is given by
L_{*}\gtrsim 4\,\sqrt{\bar{\beta}}\,10^{10}\,
m
[
35
]
,
which results in

\beta\lesssim 10^{-90}\ .

(5.1)

For such small values of
\beta
, the upper bound from the GEUP in Eq. (
4.7
)
essentially reduces to the GUP bound (
3.5
).

However, the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration observed the shadow resulting
from the phenomena taking place near the event horizon of the supermassive black
hole candidates in the centre of M87
[
49
]
and in the
centre of our Galaxy, Sgr A*
[
50
]
.
Requiring that black holes do indeed form, one finds that
\mathcal{C}\gtrsim 1/2

must be allowed, which then implies
\alpha\lesssim 2
according to the GUP bound (
3.5
).
Moreover, using the same argument, we can place independent bounds on the GEUP
paramater
\beta
.
Starting from the inequality on the right of Eq. (
4.7
) one can constrain

\beta
in terms of the value of
\alpha
.
When both parameters are positive and all the factors contained in the GEUP are real,
the constraint which ensures that the maximum compactness is large enough to allow
for black hole (horizon) formation yields
0\lesssim\beta\lesssim{1}/{4\,\alpha}

for
0\lesssim\alpha\lesssim 1
, or
0\lesssim\beta\lesssim(2-\alpha)/4
for

1\lesssim\alpha\lesssim 2
, with values of
\alpha
outside this range being
excluded.

Constraints using the minimum compactness can be imposed in the GEUP case,
but we found those not to be relevant since they depend on an arbitrarily low defined value of this parameter.

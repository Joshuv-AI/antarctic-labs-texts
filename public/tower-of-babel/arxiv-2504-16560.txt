On Existence of Spatially Regular Strong Solutions for a Class of Transport Equations
Jouko Tervo, Petri Kokkonen 1

Abstract
. The paper considers existence of spatially regular solutions for a class of linear Boltzmann transport equations. The related transport problem is an (initial) inflow boundary value problem. This problem is characteristic with variable multiplicity, that is, the rank of the boundary matrix (here a scalar) is not constant on the boundary. It is known that for these types of (initial) boundary value problems the full higher order Sobolev regularity cannot generally be established. In this paper we present Sobolev regularity results for solutions of linear Boltzmann transport problems when the data belongs to appropriate anisotropic Sobolev spaces whose elements are zero on the inflow and characteristic parts of the boundary.



1.
Introduction

Various linear and non-linear transport equations have been widely used as basic models e.g. in fluid mechanics and particle physics. Usually the solution must obey certain initial and boundary value conditions.
It is known that the solutions of these types of problems have poor or limited (Sobolev) regularity properties.
These regularity issues may be subtle even for solutions
of a pure time-dependent advection equation
{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}}+c\cdot\nabla_{x}u=f
.

We consider regularity of solutions for certain classes of
linear
continuous slowing down Boltzmann transport equations
of the form

(1)

\displaystyle a(x,E){\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial E}}(x,\omega,E)+\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(x,\omega,E)+\Sigma(x,\omega,E)\psi(x,\omega,E)-(K_{r}\psi)(x,\omega,E)=f(x,\omega,E)

for
(x,\omega,E)\in G\times S\times I
.
The cases
a=0
and
a\neq 0
will be studied separately.

Here
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the spatial domain,
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the unit sphere (velocity direction domain) and
I=[E_{0},E_{m}]
is the energy interval.
In addition, the solution
\psi
satisfies the inflow boundary condition

(2)

\displaystyle{\psi}_{|\Gamma_{-}}=g,

where
\Gamma_{-}
is the
inflow boundary

\{(y,\omega,E)\in(\partial G)\times S\times I\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)<0\}
(see Section
2
below)
and the initial condition (if
a\neq 0
)

(3)

\displaystyle\psi(\cdot,\cdot,E_{m})=0,

where
E_{m}
is the so-called
cut-off energy
.
The conditions (
2
) and (
3
) guarantee that the overall initial inflow boundary value problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) is (under relevant assumptions) well-posed.

The
restricted collision operator

K_{r}
is a partial integral type operator
(for some details we refer to
[
35
, section 5]
) of the form

(4)

K_{r}\psi=\int_{S^{\prime}}\int_{I^{\prime}}\sigma^{1}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega,E^{\prime},E)\psi(x,\omega^{\prime},E^{\prime})dE^{\prime}d\omega^{\prime}+\int_{S^{\prime}}\sigma^{2}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega,E)\psi(x,\omega^{\prime},E)d\omega^{\prime}\\
+\int_{I^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sigma^{3}(x,E^{\prime},E)\psi(x,\gamma(E^{\prime},E,\omega)(s),E^{\prime})dsdE^{\prime}=:K_{r}^{1}\psi+K_{r}^{2}\psi+K_{r}^{3}\psi.\quad

For clarity we denote
S=S^{\prime}
and
I=I^{\prime}
when the integration variables
are
\omega^{\prime}
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively.
In this paper, we confine ourselves mainly to the case where
K_{r}=K_{r}^{2}
.
The function
f=f(x,\omega,E)
in (
1
) represents the internal source
and
g=g(y,\omega,E)
in (
2
) represents the inflow boundary source.
The solution
\psi
of the problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
)
describes the fluence of the considered particle type.

We assume throughout that the spatial dimension
n=3
(
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
)
even though this is by no means essential.
In addition, we restrict to the case where the Lebesgue index
p=2

in
L^{p}
-based spaces.
The regularity results are formulated by using certain anisotropic Sobolev spaces

H^{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}(G\times S\times I)
(section
2.1
) which are relevant in formulations of such results for transport equations
because they allow for different degrees of regularity
with respect to the variables
x
,
\omega
and
E
.
In this paper, we consider only the regularity with respect to the spatial
variable
x
and so
m_{2}=m_{3}=0
.

The equation (
1
) is a special case of the equation

(5)

\displaystyle a(x,E){\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial E}}+c(x,E)\Delta_{S}\psi+d(x,\omega,E)\cdot\nabla_{S}\psi+\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi+\Sigma(x,\omega,E)\psi-K_{r}\psi=f,

where
\Delta_{S}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator and
\nabla_{S}
is the gradient operator on
S
.
In
[
38
]
we have given some reasons to use an equations like (
5
) for modelling charged particle transport
(for example for the propagation of electrons and positrons) in dose calculation for radiation therapy.
The starting point is that the differential cross sections for charged particles may contain hyper-singularities with respect to energy
variable and hence the corresponding exact (original) collision operator is a partial
hyper-singular integral operator
.
This singular integral operator can be reasonably approximated by partial integro-differential operator which leads to an equation of the form (
5
).
In practice the realistic dose calculation model consists of the coupled system of equations like (
5
) since the simultaneous evolution
of several species of particles (electrons, positrons, photons) must be modelled.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions for various classes of (initial) inflow boundary value problems like (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) is well understood
(e.g.
[
10
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
35
]
,

[
37
]
). The regularity issues of solutions are much more poorly understood.
A well-known difficulty in the initial boundary value theory of first-order PDEs is that the equation may be characteristic on the boundary (the so-called boundary matrix has a non-zero kernel).
Especially the problems in which the rank of the boundary matrix is not constant on the boundary
(the so-called problems with
variable multiplicity
) are challenging
(e.g.
[
29
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
32
]
).
The problem considered in this paper is with variable multiplicity. The other challenge in the present case is the inclusion of the restricted collision operator
K_{r}
which is a (non-local) partial integral operator.
The equation in the problem is not a pure PDE.
The full higher order Sobolev regularity can not be expected for the characteristic initial boundary value problems.
However, partial (more precisely, tangential) regularity can be proved for problems with constant multiplicity (
[
29
]
,
[
25
]
). For problems with variable multiplicity even this partial (tangential) regularity need not be valid.

The application of general theory of initial boundary value problems related to the systems of first-order PDEs yields some foundations and results for the regularity of solutions of transport problems.
For certain problems with variable multiplicity higher order regularity can be shown when the data is appropriately restricted.
One such result can be found in
[
26
]
, the idea of which we shall utilize in the section
3
below.
In certain weighted co-normal Sobolev spaces the higher order (tangential) regularity can be achieved with less restrictive data (see e.g.
[
27
]
).

There are also some regularity results specific to initial inflow boundary value transport problems. We mention some of them.
In
[
2
, Chapter 4]
regularity results for solutions
are presented for certain mono-kinetic Boltzmann transport equations (BTEs).
Some collision operators obey the so-called
smoothing property
(
[
15
]
).
In
[
7
]
one has applied the smoothing property to derive regularity results.
An iteration formula together with the smoothing property of the collision operator has been used there to prove the regularity of solutions
with respect to the spatial variables up to order
1-\epsilon
for any
0<\epsilon<1
for certain transport problems.
In
[
18
]
one has obtained the first order (
W^{p,1}
or weighted
W^{p,1}
) regularity results for some non-linear time-dependent BTEs.
A similar kind of non-linear equation along with its linearized equation (section 4.2 therein)
have been studied in
[
3
]
in the global case
G=\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
There one has obtained, under certain assumptions, weighted regularity of solutions with respect to all variables.
In
[
8
]
one has applied Neumann series to find spatially
H^{1}
-regular solutions when the diameter of the domain
G
is small enough.
In
[
39
]
we obtained some regularity results
for the BTEs like considered here in the global case
G=\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Therein we applied
explicit formulas for solutions of certain types of equations
and the difference method for more general equations.
We remark that in the global case
G=\mathbb{R}^{3}
the regularity issues are less challenging and
the ”regularity of solutions may increase according to the data”.
When
G\neq\mathbb{R}^{3}

the corresponding initial inflow boundary value problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) is characteristic with variable multiplicity (as mentioned above) which leads to limited regularity of solutions.
In fact, it is known that the regularity of the solutions of the transport equations
with respect to (the spatial)
x
-variable, for example,
is in general limited up to the space

H^{(s_{1},0,0)}(G\times S\times I)
with
s_{1}<3/2
regardless of the smoothness of the data
(see the counterexample given in
[
34
]
, section 7.1).
In
[
40
]
we studied regularity of solutions in bounded domains by using various methods. At first we applied the well-known explicit formulas of solutions for equations without the restricted collision operator
K_{r}
.
After that,
in section 5 we considered regularity of solutions for an equation involving
the integral operator
K_{r}
.
The techniques were based on Neumann series.
Furthermore, the treatments therein were confined to special cases of transport equations (
1
) and the data (
f,\ g
) was appropriately restricted.

This paper is organized as follows. After preliminaries (section
2
),
we show in section
3
results of existence of solutions in spaces
H_{0}^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})
which are completions of

\displaystyle\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\{\psi\in C_{0}^{k}(\overline{G},C(S\times I))\ |\ {\rm supp}(\psi)\cap(\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{0})=\emptyset\}

with respect to
H^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I)
-norms.
The formulations are related to those in
[
26
]
.
At first, the existence of
H_{0}^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})
-solutions is shown in the case
a(x,E)=0
.
The proof is based on the
m
-dissipativity of the smallest closed extension (in
the spaces
H_{0}^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})
) of the transport operator.
Then the existence of
C^{1}(I,H_{0}^{(m,0)}(G\times S,\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}))
-solutions
is deduced for the complete equation (
1
) by applying the theory of
evolution equations
(the space
H_{0}^{(m,0)}(G\times S,\Gamma^{\prime}_{-})
is defined similarly
to
H_{0}^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})
).
We remark that in these formulations the
data
f
must
be restricted to the space

H_{0}^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})
. Moreover, the
inflow boundary data
g=0
due to the definition of the space
H_{0}^{(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})
.
Finally, the appendix (section 4) contains some additional proofs.

We assume that

(see e.g.
[

,
[

).
Moreover, we assume that the closure

of

The unit outward pointing normal on

is denoted by

. The surface measure on

.
Let

be the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
equipped with the usual surface measure

.
Furthermore, let

be an interval

of

where

. The interior

.
We also denote

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\partial G\times S\times I,\quad\Gamma^{\prime}:=\partial G\times S,

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}:=\{(y,\omega,E)\in\partial G\times S\times I\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)<0\},\quad\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}:=\{(y,\omega)\in\partial G\times S\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)<0\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}:=\{(y,\omega,E)\in\partial G\times S\times I\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)>0\},\quad\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}:=\{(y,\omega)\in\partial G\times S\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)>0\}.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}:=\{(y,\omega,E)\in\partial G\times S\times I\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)=0\},\quad\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}:=\{(y,\omega)\in\partial G\times S\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)=0\}.

For
(x,\omega)\in G\times S
the

is defined by

(6)

\displaystyle t(x,\omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\{s>0\ |\ x-s\omega\not\in G\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{t>0\ |\ x-s\omega\in G\ {\rm for\ all}\ 0<s<t\}.

Furthermore, for

(from boundary to boundary)
is defined by

(7)

\displaystyle\tau_{-}(y,\omega):=\inf\{s>0\ |\ y+s\omega\not\in G\},\quad(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}

\displaystyle\tau_{+}(y,\omega):=\inf\{s>0\ |\ y-s\omega\not\in G\},\quad(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}.

We find that (see e.g. Lemma
3.4
below for why

)

\displaystyle 0<t(x,\omega)\leq d:=\mathrm{diam}(G),\quad 0<\tau_{\pm}(y,\omega)\leq d,

where

.

2.1.
Basic Function Spaces

All function spaces below are real valued.
Define the space
W^{2}(G\times S\times I)
by

(8)

\displaystyle W^{2}(G\times S\times I)=\{\psi\in L^{2}(G\times S\times I)\ |\ \omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi\in L^{2}(G\times S\times I)\}.

The space
W^{2}(G\times S\times I)
is equipped with the inner product

(9)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\psi},v\right\rangle_{W^{2}(G\times S\times I)}=\left\langle{\psi},v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G\times S\times I)}+\left\langle\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi,\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G\times S\times I)}.

Then
W^{2}(G\times S\times I)
is a Hilbert space.
Let for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

(10)

\displaystyle C^{(k,0,0)}(G\times S\times I^{\circ}):=\{{}

\displaystyle f:G\times S\times I^{\circ}\to\mathbb{R}\ |\ \textrm{all partial derivatives}

(x,\omega,E)\mapsto\partial^{\alpha}_{x}f(x,\omega,E)
,
|\alpha|\leq k
, exist and

\displaystyle\textrm{are continuous on $G\times S\times I^{\circ}$}\}

\displaystyle C^{(k,0,0)}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S\times\mathbb{R}):=\{{}

\displaystyle f\in C^{(k,0,0)}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S\times\mathbb{R})\ |\ \textrm{support of $f$ is compact in $\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S\times\mathbb{R}$}\}

and

(11)

\displaystyle C^{k}(\overline{G}\times S\times I):={}

\displaystyle\{f_{|G\times S\times I^{\circ}}\ |\ f\in C_{0}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S\times\mathbb{R})\}

\displaystyle C^{(k,0,0)}(\overline{G}\times S\times I):={}

\displaystyle\{f_{|G\times S\times I^{\circ}}\ |\ f\in C_{0}^{(k,0,0)}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S\times\mathbb{R})\}

The space
C^{1}(\overline{G}\times S\times I)
is a dense subspace of
W^{2}(G\times S\times I)
.
Clearly,
C^{k}(\overline{G}\times S\times I)\subset C^{(k,0,0)}(\overline{G}\times S\times I)
for all
k
.

The space of
L^{2}
-functions on
\Gamma_{-}
with respect to the measure

|\omega\cdot\nu|\,d\sigma d\omega dE
is denoted by
T^{2}(\Gamma_{-})

that is,
T^{2}(\Gamma_{-})=L^{2}(\Gamma_{-},|\omega\cdot\nu|\,d\sigma d\omega dE)
.

T^{2}(\Gamma_{-})
is a Hilbert space and its natural inner product is

(12)

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle_{T^{2}(\Gamma_{-})}=\int_{\Gamma_{-}}g_{1}(y,\omega,E)g_{2}(y,\omega,E)|\omega\cdot\nu|\,d\sigma(y)d\omega dE.

In patches
\Gamma_{-}^{i}=\{(y,\omega,E)\in(\partial G)_{i}\times S\times I\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)<0\}
the inner product (
12
) is computed by

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle_{T^{2}(\Gamma_{-}^{i})}=\int_{S\times I}\int_{U_{i,-,\omega}}g_{1}(h_{i}(z),\omega,E)g_{2}(h_{i}(z),\omega,E)|\omega\cdot\nu(h_{i}(z))|\left\|(\partial_{z_{1}}h_{i}\times\partial_{z_{2}}h_{i})(z)\right\|dzd\omega dE

where
U_{i,-,\omega}:=\{z\in U_{i}|\ \omega\cdot\nu(h_{i}(z))<0\}
and
h_{i}:=\varphi_{i}^{-1}
(a local parametrization)
when
(U_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a chart of
(\partial G)_{i}
.

The spaces
T^{2}(\Gamma_{+})
and
T^{2}(\Gamma)
and their inner products are similarly defined.
In addition, we define the (Hilbert) space

\displaystyle T_{\tau_{\pm}}^{2}(\Gamma_{\pm})=L^{2}(\Gamma_{\pm},\tau_{\pm}(\cdot,\cdot)|\omega\cdot\nu|\ d\sigma d\omega dE)

where the canonical inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle_{T_{\tau_{\pm}}^{2}(\Gamma_{\pm})}=\int_{\Gamma_{\pm}}g_{1}(y,\omega,E)g_{2}(y,\omega,E)\tau_{\pm}(y,\omega)|\omega\cdot\nu|\ d\sigma(y)d\omega dE

is used.

Since
\tau_{\pm}(y,\omega)\leq\mathrm{diam}(G)=d<\infty
we obtain
from
[
10
]
, p. 252
or
[
35
]
, Theorem 2.16

Theorem 2.1

The inflow trace operators

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}:W^{2}(G\times S\times I)\to T_{\tau_{\pm}}^{2}(\Gamma_{\pm})\ {\rm defined\ by}\ \gamma_{\pm}(\psi):=\psi_{|\Gamma_{\pm}}

are (well-defined) bounded surjective operators and they have bounded right inverses
L_{\pm}:T^{2}_{\tau_{\pm}}(\Gamma_{\pm})\to{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
that is,
\gamma_{\pm}\circ L_{\pm}=\mathcal{I}
(the identity map). The operators
L_{\pm}
are called
lifts
.

Remark 2.2

The lift
L_{-}
can be given explicitly, for instance by

\displaystyle(L_{-}g)(x,\omega,E)=e^{-\lambda t(x,\omega)}g(x-t(x,\omega)\omega,\omega,E)

where
\lambda\geq 0
. For
\lambda=0
the lift
L_{-}
is an isometrics
L_{-}:T^{2}_{\tau_{-}}(\Gamma_{-})\to{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
. Note that the lift
L_{-}
is not unique. Analogous observations are valid for
L_{+}
.

From Theorem
2.1
it follows that
the trace operators
\gamma_{\pm}:W^{2}(G\times S\times I)\to\ L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Gamma_{\pm},|\omega\cdot\nu|\ d\sigma d\omega dE)
are continuous.
However, the trace
\gamma(\psi),\ \psi\in{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
is not necessarily in the space
T^{2}(\Gamma)
. Hence we define the spaces

(13)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)=\{\psi\in{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)\ |\ \gamma(\psi)\in T^{2}(\Gamma)\}.

The space
\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
is equipped with the inner product

(14)

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi,v\right\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)}=\left\langle\psi,v\right\rangle_{{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)}+\left\langle\gamma(\psi),\gamma(v)\right\rangle_{T^{2}(\Gamma)}.

Then
\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
is a Hilbert space (
[
34
]
, Proposition 2.5).
For convex domains
G

the space
\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
is the completion of
C^{1}(\overline{G}\times S\times I)
with respect to the inner product (
14
) and then
C^{1}(\overline{G}\times S\times I)
is dense in

\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
(
[
35
]
, Corollary 2.22).

For
v\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
and
\psi\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
it holds the Green’s formula (Stokes’s Theorem)

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{G\times S\times I}(\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi)v\ dxd\omega dE+\int_{G\times S\times I}(\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}v)\psi\ dxd\omega dE=\int_{\partial G\times S\times I}(\omega\cdot\nu)v\ \psi\ d\sigma d\omega dE

which is obtained by classical Stokes Theorem for

and then by the density arguments for general

and
\psi\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(G\times S\times I)
.

2.2.
Anisotropic Sobolev Spaces

Let
m=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
be a multi-index (for simplicity we restrict ourselves here to integer multi-indexes but the definitions below can be generalized for fractional indexes
s=(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\in[0,\infty[^{3}
in the standard way).
Define an
anisotropic (or mixed-norm) Sobolev spaces

H^{m}(G\times S\times I):=H^{m,2}(G\times S\times I)
by

\displaystyle H^{m}(G\times S\times I):=\Big\{\psi\in L^{2}(G\times S\times I)\ |\ \partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{{\omega}}^{\beta}\partial_{E}^{l}\psi\in L^{2}(G\times S\times I),

(16)

\displaystyle{\rm for\ all}\ |\alpha|\leq m_{1},\ |\beta|\leq m_{2},\ l\leq m_{3}\Big\}.

Here we denote more shortly
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}:={{\partial^{\alpha}}\over{\partial x^{\alpha}}}

and similarly
\partial_{\omega}^{\beta}:={{\partial^{\beta}}\over{\partial\omega^{\beta}}}={{\partial^{\beta_{1}}}\over{\partial\omega^{\beta_{1}}}}{{\partial^{\beta_{2}}}\over{\partial\omega^{\beta_{2}}}}

where
\{\partial_{\omega_{j}},\ j=1,2\}
is a local basis of the tangent space
T(S)
.
We recall that for sufficiently smooth functions
f:S\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial f}{\partial\omega_{j}}}_{\Big|\omega}={\partial\over{\partial w_{j}}}(f\circ h)_{\Big|z=h^{-1}(\omega)},\ j=1,2

where
h:W\to S\setminus S_{0}
is a parametrization of
S\setminus S_{0}
(
S_{0}
has the surface measure zero).
Moreover, recall that
f\in L^{2}(S)
if and only if
f\circ h\in L^{2}(W,\left\|\partial_{1}h\times\partial_{2}h\right\|dz)
.
For
f\in L^{2}(S)
we define the inner product

(17)

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=\int_{S}f_{1}f_{2}d\omega=\int_{W}(f_{1}\circ h)(f_{2}\circ h)\left\|\partial_{1}h\times\partial_{2}h\right\|du.

The space
H^{m}(G\times S\times I)
is a Hilbert space when equipped with the inner product

(18)

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi,v\right\rangle_{H^{m}(G\times S\times I)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m_{1},|\beta|\leq m_{2},l\leq m_{3}}\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\omega}^{\beta}\partial_{E}^{l}\psi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\omega}^{\beta}\partial_{E}^{l}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G\times S\times I)}.

The corresponding norm is

\displaystyle\left\|\psi\right\|_{H^{m}(G\times S\times I)}=\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m_{1}}\sum_{|\beta|\leq m_{2}}\sum_{l\leq m_{3}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\omega}^{\beta}\partial_{E}^{l}\psi\right\|_{L^{2}(G\times S\times I)}^{2}\Big)^{{1\over 2}}.

Note that for
m^{\prime}\geq m
(this means that
m_{j}^{\prime}\geq m_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
)

\displaystyle H^{m^{\prime}}(G\times S\times I)\subset H^{m}(G\times S\times I)

and that (since
G\times S\times I
is bounded)

\displaystyle W^{\infty,m}(G\times S\times I)\subset H^{m}(G\times S\times I),

where for
m=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}

(19)

\displaystyle W^{\infty,m}(G\times S\times I):=\Big\{\psi\in L^{\infty}(G\times S\times I)\ \big|\

Theorem 3.11

A.

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\infty,(m,0,0)}(G\times S^{\prime}\times S\times I,\Gamma_{-})\subset W^{\infty,(m,0,0)}(G\times S\times I,L^{1}(S^{\prime}))\cap W^{\infty,(m,0,0)}(G\times S^{\prime}\times I,L^{1}(S)),

Furthermore, by the Leibniz’s rule and Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality

(47)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(K_{r}\psi)(x,\omega,E)|=\big|\int_{S^{\prime}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\big(\sigma^{2}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega,E)\psi(x,\omega^{\prime},E)\big)d\omega^{\prime}\big|

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}{\alpha\choose\beta}\int_{S^{\prime}}|(\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\beta}\sigma^{2})(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega,E)|^{1/2+1/2}|(\partial_{x}^{\beta}\psi)(x,\omega^{\prime},E)|d\omega^{\prime}

(y_{0},\omega_{0})\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}
,
that is,
\omega_{0}\cdot\nu(y_{0})<0

and therefore by Proposition 4.7 in

the escape time map

is continuously differentiable on a neighbourhood of

.
This proves that

.
In particular,

is continuous on
G\times S

(see also Lemma 2.5 in
[
35
]
).

Since
\tilde{t}=0

\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}
(see (

)),
it follows from (ii) of Lemma
4.2
(see below)
that
\tilde{t}
is continuous at points of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}
.
Because also
\tilde{t}|_{G\times S}=t
is continuous at points of
G\times S
,
we conclude that

\tilde{t}
is continuous on
(G\times S)\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}
.

Let
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
for the remainder of the proof.
By (iv) of Lemma
3.4
,
it holds
(x_{0}-\tau_{+}(x_{0},\omega_{0})\omega_{0},\omega_{0})=(x_{0}-\tilde{t}(x_{0},\omega_{0})\omega_{0},\omega_{0})\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}
.
Hence by Lemma 2.5 in
[
35
]
, the map
t
has the limit

(106)

\displaystyle\lim_{G\times S\ni(x,\omega)\to(x_{0},\omega_{0})}t(x,\omega)=\tau_{+}(x_{0},\omega_{0})

that is
\tilde{t}(x,\omega)\to\tilde{t}(x_{0},\omega_{0})

when
(x,\omega)
tends to
(x_{0},\omega_{0})
from
G\times S
.

Thus, in order to conclude that
\tilde{t}
is continuous on all of
\overline{G}\times S
,
it remains to show that
\tau_{+}
is continuous on
\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
.

Notice that if
(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}

then from (i) of Lemma
3.4
,
we have
\tau_{+}(y,\omega)>0

and hence the definitions of

and
\tau_{+}

(see (

))
imply that
y-s\omega\in G
and

\tau_{+}(y,\omega)=s+t(y-s\omega,\omega)

for all
0<s<\tau_{+}(y,\omega)
.

Let
(x_{k},\omega_{k})
,
k=1,2,\dots
, be a sequence in
\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}

converging to
(x_{0},\omega_{0})
.
If the sequence
\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})
,
k=1,2,\dots
,
were
not
bounded from below by a constant
c>0
,
then it would have a subsequence
\tilde{t}(x_{k_{i}},\omega_{k_{i}})
,
i=1,2,\dots
,
converging to
0

and hence (ii) of Lemma
4.2

would imply that
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}

which is absurd.

This shows that there must exist a constant
c>0

such that
\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})\geq c
for all
k=1,2,\dots

Since
\tau_{+}(x_{0},\omega_{0})>0
by (i) of Lemma
3.4
, we may assume that
0<c<\tau_{+}(x_{0},\omega_{0})

Then for all
0<s<c
and all

we have

and

\displaystyle\tau_{+}(x_{k},\omega_{k})=s+t(x_{k}-s\omega_{k},\omega_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{k\to\infty}s+t(x_{0}-s\omega_{0},\omega_{0})=\tau_{+}(x_{0},\omega_{0}),

where the limit exists and equals the right-hand side because of (

)
and
(x_{k},\omega_{k})\to(x_{0},\omega_{0})
as

(note that

The above argument shows that
if

(x_{k},\omega_{k})
,

\tau_{+}(x_{k},\omega_{k})=\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})\to\tilde{t}(x_{0},\omega_{0})=\tau_{+}(x_{0},\omega_{0})

In other words,

is also continuous on points of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
.

As mentioned above, this completes the proof that

is continuous on
\overline{G}\times S

∎

Lemma 4.2

Suppose that
(y,\omega)\in\partial G\times S

and
(x_{k},\omega_{k})
,
k=1,2,\dots
,
is a sequence in
\overline{G}\times S
converging to
(y,\omega)
.

(i)

If the sequence
\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})
,
k=1,2,\dots
,
is bounded from below by some constant

then
(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
and
(x_{k},\omega_{k})\in(G\times S)\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}

k=1,2,\dots
.

(ii)

We have
(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}

if and only if
\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

(i):

If
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in\overline{G}\times S

is such that
\tilde{t}(x_{0},\omega_{0})=0
,
it follows from the definition (
29
) of
\tilde{t}

that
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}
.
That is, if
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in\overline{G}\times S

and
\tilde{t}(x_{0},\omega_{0})>0
then
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in(G\times S)\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
.

Let
c>0
be such that
\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})\geq c
for all
k
.
Then by what we have just discussed, it holds

(x_{k},\omega_{k})\in(G\times S)\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
for all
k
.

The rest of the proof of (i) is divided into three parts.

Part I:

If
0<s\leq c
, it then follows from the definition
of
t
and
\tau_{+}
(see (
6
) and (
7
))
that
x_{k}-s\omega_{k}\in G
for all
k
.
In the limit
k\to\infty
, we have
y-s\omega\in\overline{G}

for all
0<s\leq c
.

Part II:

We claim that there exists
s_{1}>0

such that

for all

First, if

On the other hand, if

,

Since also

.

.

Part III:

By

s_{1}>0

.
Therefore, (iii) of Lemma

-s\omega\cdot\nu(y)<0

.
This means that
\omega\cdot\nu(y)>0
, i.e.
(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
,
and hence the proof of (i) is complete.

(ii):

Write
\tilde{t}_{k}:=\tilde{t}(x_{k},\omega_{k})\geq 0
,

,

(y,\omega)\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}

Let

cannot

5.
Discussion

In the case where the spatial domain
G\not=\mathbb{R}^{3}
only solutions with limited Sobolev regularity can be achieved (without essential restrictions on data
(f,g)
) for transport problems and the regularity of solutions does not increase according to data
f,\ g
.
The corresponding boundary matrix (here a scalar) has a variable rank on
\Gamma
and the boundary matrix changes the definiteness on
\Gamma
.
One knows from the general theory of first-order PDE systems that solutions of these kinds of initial boundary value problems possess only limited regularity.
In the case of initial inflow boundary value transport problems, the limit of Sobolev regularity with respect to
x
-variable seems to be
{3\over 2}
.
If the transport equation does not contain the slowing down term
a{\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial E}}
the regularity with respect to
E
-variable is not necessarily limited (see
[
40
, section 4.1.3]
). When the term
c\Delta_{S}\psi
is included (not considered in this paper) the basic existence results (
[
38
, section 6]
) guarantee that

\psi\in L^{2}(G\times I,H^{1}(S))=H^{(0,1,0)}(G\times S\times I)
. The partially elliptic term

c\Delta_{S}\psi
(that is, elliptic with respect to
\omega
-variable only) actually smooths the solution and higher order regularity with respect to
\omega
-variable is possible (this issue needs further research). When the term
c\Delta_{S}\psi
is not included the regularity with respect to
\omega
-variable is limited as well.

Nevertheless, when one appropriately restrict the data
f,\ g
the existence of higher order regular solutions is possible. This paper considered one such case where
g=0
and
f_{|\Gamma_{-}}=0
in the Sobolev sense. Our results in section
3
can be considered as a variant of the results given in
[
26
]
where certain general symmetric first-order partial differential systems
\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}u+B(x)u=f
and the corresponding time-dependent systems are considered in an open bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In
[
26
]
the boundary matrix changes its definiteness only
when crossing an
(n-2)
-dimensional submanifold of the boundary. This is the situation in our case since the boundary matrix (here a scalar) is

\sum_{j=1}^{3}\nu_{j}A_{j}=\omega\cdot\nu
and
\omega\cdot\nu
changes its sign only on
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}=(\partial G)\times S=\partial(G\times S)
,
where
\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}=\{(y,\omega)\in(\partial G)\times S\ |\ \omega\cdot\nu(y)=0\}
is a
3
-dimensional submanifold of the
5
-dimensional
G\times S
.
Yet the problem we consider and the techniques we use are different.
Note that our model contains the partial integral operator
K_{r}
. Moreover, our basic domain is of the form
G\times S
, where
S
is a manifold without boundary.
We apply explicit solution formulas and the smallest closed extensions of transport operators to deduce existence results in higher order Sobolev spaces. In

[
26
]
one has applied cutting/weight function techniques and the basic assumption of definiteness (1.2) therein is needed to obtain relevant apriori estimates for the relevant operators.
Moreover, the existence of solutions is based on the Hahn-Banach theorem.

The regularity of solutions is crucial in the convergence analysis of numerical schemes. Perhaps the most effective methods for transport problems are based on the finite element methods (FEM) in spatial variables. The error estimates for various (discontinuous) FEM schemes are also known.
The transport equation (
1
) is hyperbolic in nature. So discontinuous FEM schemes are favourable.

There exist some special FEM convergence results for transport problems. For example, in
[
19
]
one has considered mono-kinetic (mono-energetic) transport equation
\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi+\Sigma\psi-K_{r}\psi=f
. Firstly one applies the so-called discrete-ordinate scheme to discretize the angular variable
\omega
. The collision term
K_{r}\psi
is discretized by using an appropriate numerical quadrature.
As a result one obtains a semi-discretized approximation, say
\psi_{do}
, which obeys a system of first order partial differential equations with respect to spatial variable
x
.
The system is further discretized with respect to
x
by applying the discontinuous Galerkin method where the (fully discretized) approximation, say
P_{h}(\psi_{do})
, is the elementwise
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of
\psi_{do}
onto the finite dimensional element space (that is,

P_{h}(\psi_{do})_{|K}=P_{k}({\psi_{do}}_{|K})
where
P_{k}:L^{2}(K)\to P_{k}(K)
is the orthogonal projection).
The element space
P_{k}(K)
is the set of piecewise polynomials whose degree is less than or equal to
k
in each element
K
.
The convergence is under control if one assumes the regularity
\psi\in H^{(r,0)}(G\times S),\ r>{1\over 2}
. Actually, one gets the convergence rate
\sim h^{\min\{r,k+1\}-{1\over 2}}
, where
h
is the maximal diameter of elements
K
.
For example, if
k=1
and
r=1
one gets the convergence rate
\sim h^{{1\over 2}}
and if
k=1
and
r=2
one gets the convergence rate
\sim h^{{3\over 2}}
. Hence we find that regularity of solutions strongly affects the convergence speed.
Many other similar results can be found in the literature.
The above procedure can be applied to more general equations (
5
). However, the presence of terms
a{\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial E}}
,

c\Delta_{S}\psi
and

d\cdot\nabla_{S}\psi
requires additional treatments.

Coupled systems of equations like (
5
) can be used to model simultaneous propagation of several types of particles (photons, electrons, positrons, etc.)
in radiotherapy dose calculation (e.g.
[
20
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,

[
36
]
,
[
35
]
).
In practice the corresponding initial inflow boundary value problem is solved numerically. The discrete (matrix) equation is high dimensional and so only numerical methods whose rigorous convergence is under control can be used successfully.
When the solution
\psi
is regular enough the discontinuous FEM methods offer one such approach as part of numerical schemes. Some of today’s commercial dose calculation codes already use FEM approaches. So the regularity analysis of solutions is important also from that point of view.

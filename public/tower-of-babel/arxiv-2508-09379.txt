Picard group action on the category of twisted sheaves
Ting Gong, Yeqin Liu, and Yu Shen

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the category of twisted sheaves over a scheme X X . Let ℳ \mathcal{M} be a quasi-coherent sheaf on X X , and α \alpha in Br ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{Br}(X) . We show that the functor − ⊗ 𝒪 X ℳ : QCoh ( X , α ) → QCoh ( X , α ) -\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}:\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha) is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor if and only if ℳ ≅ 𝒪 X \mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X} . 1.
Introduction

For a scheme
X
, let
\mathrm{Br}(X)
denote the group of Brauer equivalence classes of Azumaya algebras over
X
.
Given
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(X)
, let
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
(resp.
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
) denote the abelian category of coherent (resp. quasi-coherent) sheaves, and let
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
(resp.
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)
) denote the abelian category of
\alpha
-twisted coherent (resp. quasi-coherent) sheaves.
The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.0.1
.

We have the following results:

(i)

(Theorem
2.0.11
) Let
X
be any scheme and
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
. Then the functor

-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}:\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)

is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor
\operatorname{id}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

(ii)

(Theorem
2.0.12
) Assume further that
X
is Noetherian and
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Coh}(X)
. Then the functor

-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}:\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)\to\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)

is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor
\operatorname{id}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Note that Theorem
1.0.1
(i)
hold for any scheme

X
and any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(X)
.
We will prove this result using non‑commutative ring theory.

Theorem
1.0.1
leads to many interesting and important consequences. For example, let
D^{b}(X,\alpha):=D^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X,\alpha))
denote the bounded derived category of
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
.
Then the Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
has a natural action on
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
via tensoring with a line bundle. As an application of Theorem
1.0.1
, we know that this action is faithful when
X
is Noetherian.

Corollary 1.0.2

(Theorem
2.0.12
)
.

Assume that
X
is a Noetherian scheme. Then the map
\Psi
is injective, where
\Psi
is defined as follows:

\Psi:\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Aut}(D^{b}(X,\alpha)),\quad\mathcal{L}\mapsto\Psi(\mathcal{L}):\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}.

Remark 1.0.3
.

In
[
CS07
, Theorem 1.1]
, Canonaco and Stellari show that for a smooth projective variety
X
over a field
k
, if the Fourier–Mukai transform

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon D^{b}(X,\alpha)\longrightarrow D^{b}(X,\alpha)

is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor, then
\mathcal{P}\cong\Delta_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
, where
\Delta\colon X\to X\times X
is the diagonal embedding. Therefore, Corollary
1.0.2
holds when
X
is smooth and projective over
k
. However, it is not clear whether their method can be generalized to arbitrary schemes.

Now assume that
X
is a smooth projective variety over a field
k
. Let
\mathscr{X}\to X
be the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe associated to the Brauer class
\alpha
. Let
\mathscr{A}ut_{\mathscr{X}}
be the fibered category which to any
k
-scheme
S
associates the groupoid of isomorphism
\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}
inducing the identity on the stabilizer group schemes
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Then
\mathscr{A}ut_{\mathscr{X}}
acts on
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
. As a Corollary of Theorem
1.0.1
, we show that this action is faithful, which answers a question of Olsson in
[
Ols25
, Chapter 6.4]
.

Corollary 1.0.4

(Corollary
2.0.13
)
.

Assume that
k
is algebraically closed. Let
\sigma\in\mathscr{A}ut_{\mathscr{X}}(k)
be an automorphism of the stack inducing the identity functor on
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
. Then we have
\sigma=\operatorname{id}
.

It follows from our results that taking Brauer twists of varieties does not yield new Calabi–Yau categories. More precisely, we show that
D^{b}(X)
is Calabi–Yau if and only if
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
is Calabi–Yau.

Theorem 1.0.5

(Theorem
2.0.15
)
.

Assume
\operatorname{dim}X=n
. Then
D^{b}(X)
is a Calabi-Yau
[n]
-category if and only if
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
is a Calabi-Yau
[n]
-category.

In
[
Ols25
, Example 6.6]
, Olsson gives an example showing that Corollary
1.0.2
does not hold for Deligne–Mumford stacks. We provide a new example (see Example
2.0.16
) demonstrating that all of the theorems above—Theorems
1.0.1
,
1.0.2
, and
1.0.5
—fail for Deligne–Mumford stacks. However, the examples in
[
Ols25
]
and this paper are both certain gerbes over varieties. It would be interesting to know if such examples exist for Deligne–Mumford stacks with generically trivial stabilizers.

(Conjecture
2.0.18
)

-modules.

Lemma 2.0.1

(
[

, Theorem 1.3.7]
)
.

We have an equivalence of categories

i:\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)\cong\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}).

Moreover, let
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
be a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
, we have the following commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}}

By Lemma
2.0.1
, from now on we work in the category
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A})
.
To prove Theorem
1.0.1
, we need some algebraic lemmas.
In what follows, we assume that
R
is a commutative ring and that
A
is an Azumaya algebra over
R
.
Let
M
be an
R
-module. Then
A\otimes_{R}M
naturally inherits an
A
-bimodule structure, induced by the standard
A
-bimodule structure on
A
.

Lemma 2.0.2
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module, and define

Z(A\otimes_{R}M):=\{b\in A\otimes_{R}M\mid ab=ba\text{ for all }a\in A\}.

Then
Z(A\otimes_{R}M)\cong M
, via the inclusion
m\mapsto 1\otimes m
.

Proof.

We may assume
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is connected, and that
\operatorname{rank}_{R}(A)=n^{2}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that we have the following short exact sequence of
R
-modules, where each term is a projective
R
-module:

0\to R\to A\to A/R\to 0.

Tensoring with
M
, we obtain the short exact sequence

0\to M\xrightarrow{}A\otimes_{R}M\to(A/R)\otimes_{R}M\to 0.

Therefore, we have
M\subseteq Z(A\otimes_{R}M)
, which induces the following short exact sequence:

(2.1)

0\to M\xrightarrow{}Z(A\otimes_{R}M)\to Z(A\otimes_{R}M)/M\to 0.

By
[
CTS21
, Theorem 3.1.1]
, there exists a finite surjective étale morphism
\operatorname{Spec}S\to\operatorname{Spec}R
such that
A_{S}:=A\otimes_{R}S\cong\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(S)
.
Let
M_{S}:=M\otimes_{R}S
. Then we have

Z(A_{S}\otimes_{S}M_{S})=Z(\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(M_{S}))=M_{S}.

Note that there is a natural inclusion

Z(A\otimes_{R}M)_{S}\subseteq Z(A_{S}\otimes_{S}M_{S}).
By pulling back the short exact sequence (
2.1
) to
S
, we obtain the following exact sequence:

0\to M_{S}\xrightarrow{}Z(A\otimes_{R}M)_{S}\to\left(Z(A\otimes_{R}M)/M\right)_{S}\to 0.

Therefore, we have

Z(A_{S}\otimes_{S}M_{S})=M_{S}\subseteq Z(A\otimes_{R}M)_{S}\subseteq Z(A_{S}\otimes_{S}M_{S}),

so all three terms are equal. In particular,

Z(A\otimes_{R}M)_{S}=M_{S}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(Z(A\otimes_{R}M)/M\right)_{S}=0.

Since
S
is faithfully flat over
R
, it follows that
Z(A\otimes_{R}M)/M=0
, and hence

Z(A\otimes_{R}M)=M.

∎

Let
A^{\operatorname{op}}
denote the opposite algebra of
A
, and set
A^{e}:=A\otimes_{R}A^{\operatorname{op}}
. Note that

\operatorname{Hom}_{A\text{-bimod}}(A,A\otimes_{R}M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(A,A\otimes_{R}M),

where both
A
and
A\otimes_{R}M
are regarded as left
A^{e}
-modules via the standard structure induced by the
A
-bimodule actions.

Lemma 2.0.3
.

There is a natural isomorphism of
R
-modules

\eta\colon M\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(A,A\otimes_{R}M),\qquad m\longmapsto\bigl(a\longmapsto a\otimes m\bigr).

Proof.

Since
1\otimes m\in Z(A\otimes_{R}M)
, the map
\eta(m)
is
A
-bilinear; hence

\eta(m)\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(A,A\otimes_{R}M)
. Thus
\eta
is well-defined.

If
m\neq m^{\prime}
, then

\eta(m)(1)=1\otimes m\neq 1\otimes m^{\prime}=\eta(m^{\prime})(1),

so
\eta
is injective.

For surjectivity, let
\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(A,A\otimes_{R}M)
and set
u:=\psi(1)
. For any
a\in A
,

\psi(a)=\psi\bigl((a\otimes 1)\cdot 1\bigr)=(a\otimes 1)\psi(1)=a\,u,\qquad\psi(a)=\psi\bigl((1\otimes a)\cdot 1\bigr)=(1\otimes a)\psi(1)=u\,a,

hence
au=ua
for all
a\in A
. By Lemma
2.0.2
, we conclude
u\in Z(A\otimes_{R}M)=M
, and therefore

\psi=\eta(u)
. This shows
\eta
is surjective.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.0.4
.

By Lemma
2.0.3
, we know for any
\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(A,A\otimes_{R}M)
,
\psi=\operatorname{id}_{A}\otimes\phi,
where
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R,M)
.

We are now ready to begin the proof of the main theorem. Let
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{QCoh}
(X). We first observe the following proposition.

Proposition 2.0.5
.

The functor

-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}:\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A})

is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor
\operatorname{id}
if and only if we have the
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

\Longleftarrow
: If we have
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
as
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules, then for any
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A})
, we have

\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})\cong\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}.

\Longrightarrow
: Suppose that
-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\cong\operatorname{id}
as functors.
Then
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
as right
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
Let
\psi\colon\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
be this isomorphism of right
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
We want to show that
\psi
is an
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule isomorphism. For any open subset
U\subseteq X
, we have the map

\psi|_{U}\colon\mathcal{A}|_{U}\longrightarrow\bigl(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\bigr)|_{U}.

Since
\psi
is right
\mathcal{A}
-linear, we have
\psi|_{U}(a)=\psi|_{U}(1)\,a
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}(U)
.
To show that
\psi
is an
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule morphism, it suffices to prove that

\psi|_{U}(1)\,a\;=\;a\,\psi|_{U}(1)\qquad\text{for all }a\in\mathcal{A}(U).

Let
i\colon U\hookrightarrow X
be the open immersion. For any
a\in\mathcal{A}(U)
, the left multiplication by
a

L_{a}\colon i^{*}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}|_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}|_{U},\qquad a_{0}\longmapsto a\,a_{0},

is a homomorphism of right
i^{*}\mathcal{A}
-modules. It induces a homomorphism of right
\mathcal{A}
-modules

i_{*}L_{a}\colon i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{A}.

Thus we have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}L_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,i_{*}L_{a}\otimes\operatorname{id}\,}

Applying
i^{*}
and using
i^{*}i_{*}\cong\operatorname{id}
, we obtain the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi|_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi|_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,L_{a}\otimes\operatorname{id}\,}

For any

, we have

\psi|_{U}\circ L_{a}(1)=\psi|_{U}(a)=\psi|_{U}(1)a,\quad\text{and}\quad(L_{a}\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\psi|_{U}(1)=a\psi|_{U}(1).

So we have
\psi|_{U}(1)a=a\psi|_{U}(1)
for all

, and therefore

is an

∎

Remark 2.0.6
.

In Proposition
2.0.5
, the “only if” direction requires that
X
be a scheme.
We cannot combine Proposition
2.0.5
with descent to treat Deligne–Mumford stacks.
The reason is that for an étale morphism
p\colon U\to X
, there is in general no natural isomorphism

p^{*}p_{*}\cong\operatorname{id}
on quasi-coherent sheaves.

We want to determine the
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule homomorphisms from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
. This will be accomplished by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.0.7
.

We have the isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{M})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})

Proof.

Let
\{U_{i}\}
be an affine open cover of
X
, where each
U_{i}=\operatorname{Spec}R_{i}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}}\cong\widetilde{M_{i}}
for some
R_{i}
-module
M_{i}
. By Lemma
2.0.3
, we have isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}
-modules:

\eta_{i}:\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})|_{U_{i}}.

By Lemma
2.0.3
again, the maps
\eta_{i}
agree on the intersections
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
. Therefore, by gluing
\eta_{i}
’s together, we obtain a global isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules:

\eta:\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}).

Thus we have

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{M})\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}).

By Lemma
2.0.3
, the isomorphism is given by

\eta_{X}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{M})\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\bigr),\qquad m\longmapsto\bigl(a\longmapsto a\otimes m\bigr).

∎

Remark 2.0.8
.

By Proposition
2.0.7
, we know for any
\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})
,
\psi=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\phi,
where
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{M})
.

Remark 2.0.9
.

The proof of Proposition
2.0.7
cannot, in general, be extended to Deligne–Mumford stacks via descent. For instance, let

p\colon Y\longrightarrow[Y/G]=X,

where
Y
is a scheme and
G
is a finite group. Over
Y
we obtain an isomorphism

\varphi\colon p^{*}\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}p^{*}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\bigr).

However, it is not clear whether
\varphi
is
G
-equivariant. Consequently, we cannot conclude that

\mathcal{M}\;\cong\;\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\bigr)

over
X
.

Proposition 2.0.10
.

We have the isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
as
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{X}
i.e.
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Proof.

One direction is clear. For the other direction, suppose
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
as
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules. Let
\psi:\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
denote the isomorphism. By Remark
2.0.8
, we have
\psi=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\phi
for some
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{M})
. Note that we have the following exact sequence:

0\to\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathcal{M}\to\operatorname{Coker}(\phi)\to 0.

After tensoring with
\mathcal{A}
, we obtain the following exact sequence:

0\to\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)\to\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\operatorname{Coker}(\phi)\to 0.

Since
\psi
is an isomorphism, we have
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\operatorname{Coker}(\phi)=0
.
Note that
\mathcal{A}
is locally free of finite rank as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, so we have
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=\operatorname{Coker}(\phi)=0
.
Thus,
\phi
is an isomorphism, and hence
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
∎

\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Then
\mathscr{A}ut_{\mathscr{X}}
acts on
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
. We prove that the action is faithful.

Corollary 2.0.13
.

Assume that
k
is algebraically closed. That is let
\sigma\in\mathscr{A}ut_{\mathscr{X}}(k)
be an automorphism of the stack inducing the identity funtor on
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
, then
\sigma=\operatorname{id}.

Proof.

By
[
Ols25
, Proposition 6.5]
, we know that
\sigma
is given by tensoring with a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
. By Theorem
2.0.12
, we know
\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We complete the proof.
∎

Now we want to show that
D^{b}(X)
is Calabi–Yau if and only if
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
is Calabi–Yau. To do so, we first need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.0.14

(Serre duality)
.

Let
n=\operatorname{dim}(X)
. Then
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}[n]
is the dualizing object for
D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})
, where the
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}
is defined as
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}
. Moreover, we have the following version of Serre duality:

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\omega_{\mathcal{A}}[n])^{*}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}[n])^{*}

for any
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
YZ06
, Example 6.4]
.
∎

Theorem 2.0.15
.

Let
n=\dim(X)
. Then

D^{b}(X)
is a Calabi–Yau
[n]
-category if and if
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
is a Calabi–Yau
[n]
-category.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}
is the Azumaya algebra on
X
such that
[\mathcal{A}]=\alpha.

\Longrightarrow
: If
D^{b}(X)
is a Calabi–Yau
[n]
-category, then
\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Then the result follows from Lemma
2.0.14
.

\Longleftarrow
: If
D^{b}(X,\alpha)=D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})
is a Calabi–Yau
[n]
-category. Then for any
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})
we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}[n])^{*}.

By Lemma
2.0.14
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}[n])\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(X,\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}[n]).

So
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\cong\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}
for all
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
. Thus by Theorem
2.0.12
,
\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Thus,
D^{b}(X)
is a Calabi–Yau
[n]
-category.

∎

In
[
Ols25
, Example 6.6]
, Olsson gave an example showing that Theorem
2.0.12
does not hold for Deligne–Mumford stacks. We provide a new example demonstrating that Theorems
2.0.11
,
2.0.12
, and
2.0.15
may fail in the setting of Deligne–Mumford stacks.

Example 2.0.16
.

Let
X
be an Enriques surface over
\mathbb{C}
. By
[
BHPVdV04
, Chapter VIII]
, we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mu_{2})\cong\operatorname{Pic}(X)[2]\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

which classifies 2-torsion line bundles on
X
. The nontrivial class corresponds to the canonical bundle
\omega_{X}
, satisfying
\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. This
\mu_{2}
-torsor determines an étale double cover

p:Y\to X,

where
Y
is a K3 surface. By
[
Bea09
]
, we know that
\mathrm{Br}(X)=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Let

\pi:\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}=[X/\mu_{2}]\to X

be the trivial
\mu_{2}
-gerbe over
X
. By
[
LS25
, Lemma 3.0.1]
, we have

\mathrm{Br}(\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X})=\mathrm{Br}(X)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mu_{2})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Note that by
[
IU15
, Theorem 1.5]
, we have

D^{b}(\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X})=D^{b}(X)\oplus D^{b}(X),

which is not a Calabi–Yau category.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}_{\acute{e}t}^{1}(X,\mu_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Br}(\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X})
be the Brauer class of the
\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}
corresponding to the double cover
p:Y\to X
. By
[
LS25
, Lemma 5.1.7]
, we have

D^{b}(\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X},\alpha)\cong D^{b}(Y),

which is a Calabi–Yau
[2]
-category. This shows that Theorem
2.0.15
does not hold for Deligne–Mumford stacks.

At the same time, since
D^{b}(\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X},\alpha)
is a Calabi–Yau
[2]
-category, the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.0.15
shows that

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}}}\omega_{\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}}\cong\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}

for all
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X},\alpha)
, where
\omega_{\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}}
is the canonical bundle. Note that
\omega_{\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}}=p^{*}\omega_{X}
, which is not trivial. This shows that Theorems
2.0.11
and
2.0.12
does not hold for Deligne–Mumford stacks.

Note that in Example
2.0.16
, every (geometric) point of the Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathbf{B}\mu_{2,X}
has stabilizer group
\mu_{2}
, which is nontrivial.
Now assume that
X
is a quasi-separated Deligne–Mumford stack with generically trivial stabilizer.
Then there exists a dense open substack
V\hookrightarrow X
such that
V
is an algebraic space.
By
[
Alp25
, Theorem 4.5.1]
, there exists a dense open subspace
U\hookrightarrow V
such that
U
is a scheme.

Proposition 2.0.17
.

Let
X
be a quasi-separated Deligne–Mumford stack with generically trivial stabilizer, and let
U\hookrightarrow X
be a dense open substack which is a scheme.
Assume
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(X)
.
If the functor

-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}:\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)

is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor
\operatorname{id}
, then
\mathcal{M}|_{U}\cong\mathcal{O}_{U}
.

Proof.

Let
i\colon U\hookrightarrow X
be the open immersion.
For any
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(U,i^{*}\alpha)
, we have
i_{*}\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\alpha)
and, by assumption,

i_{*}\mathcal{F}\;\cong\;i_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}.

Since
i^{*}i_{*}\cong\operatorname{id}
, we obtain

\mathcal{F}\;\cong\;i^{*}\!\bigl(i_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\bigr)\;\cong\;\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}i^{*}\mathcal{M}.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
be a morphism in
\operatorname{QCoh}(U,i^{*}\alpha)
. We obtain the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

yields the commutative diagram

∎

Landau–Siegel zeros of Rankin–Selberg L L -functions
Jesse Thorner, Shifan Zhao

Abstract
. We establish standard zero-free regions with no exceptional Landau–Siegel zeros for Rankin–Selberg L L -functions and triple product L L -functions in several new families for which modularity is not yet known.



1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

Let
F
be a number field,
\mathbb{A}_{F}
the ring of adeles over
F
, and
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
the set of cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Let
\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}
be the analytic conductor of
\pi
(see (
2.1
)), which captures the arithmetic and spectral complexity of
\pi
. Let
L(s,\pi)
be the standard
L
-function,
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
the contragredient, and
\omega_{\pi}
the central character of
\pi
(which we normalize to be unitary). The generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) asserts that if
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2}
, then
L(s,\pi)\neq 0
. Jacquet and Shalika
[
15
]
proved that if
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
, then
L(s,\pi)\neq 0
, extending classical work on the Riemann zeta function. Let
|\cdot|
denote the idelic norm. Replacing
\pi
with
\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{it}
and varying
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we find that it is equivalent to prove that if
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\sigma\geq 1
, then
L(\sigma,\pi)\neq 0
.

In the absence of GRH, it is important for arithmetic applications that the zero-free region of
L(s,\pi)
to have strong uniform dependence on
\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}
. Classical techniques (e.g.,
[
35
]
) show that there is an absolute constant
>0
such that
L(\sigma,\pi)
has at most one real exceptional zero in the interval
\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR1}/(n\log\mathfrak{C}_{\pi})
. This exceptional zero, which can only exist when
\pi=\widetilde{\pi}
(i.e.,
\pi
is
self-dual
), might be very close to
s=1
as a function of
\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}
. Many important problems depend on the elimination of this exceptional zero, which is sometimes called a Landau–Siegel zero.

Let
v
be a place of
F
, and let
F_{v}
be the completion of
F
relative to
v
. Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, we express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth, admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
. There is a finite
S_{\pi}
(possibly empty) set of places
v
at which
\pi_{v}
is ramified. If
v\notin S_{\pi}
is non-archimedean, then the Satake isomorphism attaches to
\pi_{v}
a semisimple conjugacy class of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with representative
A(\pi_{v})=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{1,\pi}(v),\ldots,\alpha_{n,\pi}(v))
. The Langlands principle of functoriality predicts that if
r\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is an algebraic representation, then there should be a map
\rho
from automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
to automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, with compatible local maps, such that if
v\notin S_{\pi}
is non-archimedean, then
r(A(\pi_{v}))=A(\rho(\pi)_{v})
. In order to establish the principle of functoriality for all representations
r
, it suffices to establish it for irreducible
r
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
. For
m\geq 0
, let
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
be the
(m+1)
-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on symmetric tensors of rank
m
. If
P(x,y)
is a homogeneous degree
m
polynomial in two variables and
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, then
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(g)\in\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
is the matrix giving the change in coefficients of
P
under the change of variables by
g
. Let
\varphi_{v}
be the two-dimensional representation of the Deligne–Weil group attached to
\pi_{v}
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi_{v})
be the smooth admissible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(F_{v})
attached to the representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\circ\varphi_{v}
. By the local Langlands correspondence,
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi_{v})
is well-defined for every place
v
, so we can define the Euler product associated to the
m
-th symmetric power lift of
\pi
: If
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then

L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\otimes\chi)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi_{v})\otimes\chi_{v}).

If
r_{0}
is the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with determinant
L
, then for each irreducible representation
r
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, there exist integers
n\geq 0
and
k
such that
r=\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(r_{0})\otimes L^{\otimes k}
. The principle of functoriality now predicts that
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi)=\bigotimes_{v}\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi_{v})
is an automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. This is known for
m\leq 4

[
5
,
16
,
18
]
. If
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi)
is known to be automorphic, then we write
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi)\otimes\chi)
instead of
L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\otimes\chi)
.

Let
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
be the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function associated to
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
(see Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
14
]
). Shahidi
[
32
]
proved that
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0
when
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Equivalently, replacing
\pi
with
\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{it}
and varying
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that if
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
and
\sigma\geq 1
, then
L(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0
. Brumley
[
3
,
19
]
proved that there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{Brumley}(n,n^{\prime},F)>0
such that
L(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0
when
\sigma\geq 1-1/(\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi^{\prime}})^{\Cr{Brumley}}
. See also the related recent work of Harcos and Thorner
[
7
,
8
]
.

The principle of functoriality also asserts that
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
factors as a product of standard
L
-functions (i.e., it is
modular
). Hoffstein and Ramakrishnan
[
10
]
proved that if all Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions are modular and
\pi\in\cup_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, then
L(s,\pi)
has no exceptional zero (see Section
5.1
for a more detailed discussion). Modularity for
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is known only in special cases, most notably when
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}\cup\mathfrak{F}_{3}

[
18
,
27
]
. Therefore, the unconditional elimination of exceptional zeros remains a difficult and fruitful problem. We say that
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
has
no exceptional zero
, or
no Landau–Siegel zero
, if there exists an absolute and effectively computable constant
>0
such that

L(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR11}/(nn^{\prime}\log(\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi^{\prime}})).

If
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
and
r,r^{\prime}
are algebraic representations such that
r(\pi)
and
r^{\prime}(\pi^{\prime})
are automorphic, then we say that
L(s,r(\pi)\times r^{\prime}(\pi^{\prime}))
has
no exceptional zero
, or
no Landau–Siegel zero
, if there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{ZFR_{r}}(r,r^{\prime})>0
such that

L(\sigma,r(\pi)\times r^{\prime}(\pi^{\prime}))\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR_{r}}/\log(\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi^{\prime}}).

We call
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}

twist-equivalent
, denoted
\pi\sim\pi^{\prime}
, if there exists
\psi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
such that
\pi^{\prime}=\pi\otimes\psi
. Otherwise, we write
\pi\not\sim\pi^{\prime}
. Let
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
be the trivial representation, whose
L
-function is the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{F}(s)
. If
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
,
\chi=\overline{\chi}
, and
L(s,\chi)
appears in as a factor of another
L
-function, then we call
L(s,\chi)
a
self-dual abelian factor
. If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
and there exists a nontrivial quadratic
\eta\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
such that
\pi=\pi\otimes\eta
, then
\pi
is
dihedral
.

Proposition 1.1
.

(1)

[
10
,
35
]
If
\pi\in\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi\neq\widetilde{\pi}
, then
L(s,\pi)
has no exceptional zero.

(2)

[
2
,
10
]
If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}\cup\mathfrak{F}_{3}
, then
L(s,\pi)
has no exceptional zero.

(3)

[
20
]
If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi\otimes\psi=\pi
for some
\psi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}-\{\mathbbm{1}\}
, then
L(s,\pi)
has no exceptional zero.

(4)

[
29
]
If
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
, then any exceptional zero of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is a zero of a self-dual abelian factor. No such factor exists when
\pi\not\sim\pi^{\prime}
and at least one of
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
is non-dihedral.

(5)

[
29
]
If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
is self-dual, then any exceptional zero of
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi)\times\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi))/\zeta_{F}(s)=L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi)\otimes\omega_{\pi})L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\pi))
is a zero of a self-dual abelian factor.

(6)

[
20
]
Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}\times\mathfrak{F}_{3}
. If
\pi^{\prime}\not\sim\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi)
or
\pi
is dihedral, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
has no exceptional zero.

(7)

[
12
,
35
]
Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. Suppose that
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=L(s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
. If
t\neq 0
, then
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes|\cdot|^{it}))
has no exceptional zero.

(8)

[
11
,
35
]
If
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
,
\pi\neq\widetilde{\pi}
, and
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}
, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
has no exceptional zero.

When
F
is totally real and
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
are non-dihedral and regular algebraic (so that they correspond with holomorphic primitive Hilbert cusp forms), Newton and Thorne
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
proved that if
n\geq 1
, then
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi),\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n+1}
. Using this, Thorner proved the following result.

Proposition 1.2

(
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
F
be totally real. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
be non-dihedral and regular algebraic. If
m,n\geq 0
,
m+n\geq 1
, and
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
corresponds with a ray class character over
F
, then any exceptional zero of
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi)\times(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\chi))
is a zero of a self-dual abelian factor. No such factor exists when
m\neq n
or
\pi\not\sim\pi^{\prime}
.

In this paper, we eliminate exceptional zeros for new families of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions and triple product
L
-functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime},\pi^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
be non-dihedral and pairwise twist-inequivalent. Let
\pi_{0}\in\mathfrak{F}_{3}
satisfy
\pi_{0}\not\sim\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi)
and
\pi_{0}\not\sim\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi^{\prime})
.

(1)

The following
L
-functions have no exceptional zeros:

(a)

L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi)\times\pi_{0})
,

(b)

L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\times(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\chi))
,

(c)

L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\pi)\times\pi^{\prime})
,

(d)

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}\times\pi^{\prime\prime})
,

(e)

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}\times\pi_{0})
, and

(f)

L(s,\pi\times\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi^{\prime})\times\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi^{\prime\prime}))
.

(2)

If
\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\neq\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\overline{\omega}_{\pi^{\prime}}^{2}
, then
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\times\pi^{\prime})
has no exceptional zero.

(3)

If
\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\pi)\otimes\overline{\omega}_{\pi}^{2}\neq\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\overline{\omega}_{\pi^{\prime}}^{2}
or
\chi^{2}\omega_{\pi}^{4}\omega_{\pi^{\prime}}^{2}\neq\mathbbm{1}
, then
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\pi)\times(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\chi))
has no exceptional zero.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
accounts for more possibilities than we have stated (e.g., at least one of

Theorem 1.5
.

If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
and
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then any exceptional zero of
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\pi)\otimes\chi)
is a zero of a self-dual abelian factor. No such factor exists when

is octahedral or not of solvable polyhedral type.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}

such that
\pi=\pi\otimes\psi

\pi^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\psi

has no exceptional zero.

An application

In each of our theorems, we can replace
\pi
with
\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{it}
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
vary. So doing, our theorems produce
standard zero-free regions
with no exceptional zero. This makes the results described above useful in the theory of primes. Let
F
be a totally real number field, and let
\pi,\pi^{\prime},\pi^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
be non-dihedral, pairwise twist-inequivalent, and regular algebraic. The motivation of Thorner in
[
34
]
for proving Proposition
1.2
was to obtain a highly uniform rate of convergence in the Sato–Tate law for the Hecke eigenvalues of
\pi
and the joint Sato–Tate law for the Hecke eigenvalues of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
. One possible application of Theorem
1.3
is the existence of an absolute constant
>0
, and nontrivial proper subintervals
I,I^{\prime},I^{\prime\prime}\subseteq[-2,2]
, and a non-archimedean place
v
at which
\pi_{v}
,
\pi_{v}^{\prime}
, and
\pi_{v}^{\prime\prime}
are unramified such that
q_{v}\leq(\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi^{\prime}}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi^{\prime\prime}})^{\Cr{ST3}}
and the Hecke eigenvalues at
v
satisfy
(a_{\pi}(v),a_{\pi^{\prime}}(v),a_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}(v))\in I\times I^{\prime}\times I^{\prime\prime}
. The authors plan to explore this in another paper.

2.
Analytic properties of
L
-functions

Let
F
be a number field with absolute discriminant
D_{F}
. For a place
v
of
F
, let
F_{v}
be the associated completion. For each place
v
of
F
, we write
v\mid\infty
(resp.
v\nmid\infty
) if
v
is archimedean (resp. non-archimedean). For
v\nmid\infty
,
q_{v}
is the cardinality of the residue field of the local ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{v}\subseteq F_{v}
, and
\varpi_{v}
is the uniformizer. The properties of
L
-functions given here rely on
[
6
,
14
,
22
]
.

2.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient, and let
\omega_{\pi}
the central character. We express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
. Let
\delta_{\pi}=1
if
\pi=\mathbbm{1}
and
\delta_{\pi}=0
otherwise. Define the sets
S_{\pi}=\{v\nmid\infty\colon\textup{$\pi_{v}$ ramified}\}
and
S_{\pi}^{\infty}=S_{\pi}\cup\{v\mid\infty\}
. Let
N_{\pi}
be the norm of the conductor of
\pi
. If
v\nmid\infty
, then there are
n
Satake parameters
(\alpha_{j,\pi}(v))_{j=1}^{n}
such that

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}),\qquad L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)q_{v}^{-s}}

converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. If
v\in S_{\pi}
, then at least one of the
\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)
equals zero.

If
v\mid\infty
, then
(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))_{j=1}^{n}
are the Langlands parameters at
v
, from which we define

\Gamma_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$,}\end{cases}\qquad L(s,\pi_{\infty})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)).

The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(1-s))^{\delta_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}N_{\pi})^{\frac{s}{2}}L(s,\pi)L(s,\pi_{\infty})

is entire of order
1
, and there exists a complex number
W(\pi)
of modulus
1
such that

\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}).

Since
\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
,
N_{\widetilde{\pi}}=N_{\pi}
, and
\{\mu_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\mu_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
, we have that
L(s,\widetilde{\pi})=\overline{L(\overline{s},\pi)}
. The analytic conductor is

(2.1)

\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}=D_{F}^{n}N_{\pi}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(|\mu_{j,\pi}(v)|+3)^{[F_{v}:\mathbb{R}]}.

By
[
21
,
23
]
there exists
\theta_{n}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}]
such that

(2.2)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)|\leq q_{v}^{\theta_{n}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))\geq-\theta_{n}.

We define
a_{\pi}(v^{\ell})
by the Dirichlet series identity

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)=\sum_{v\nmid\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)^{\ell}\log q_{v}}{q_{v}^{\ell s}}=\sum_{v\nmid\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi}(v^{\ell})\log q_{v}}{q_{v}^{\ell s}}.

2.2.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. Let

\delta_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For each
v\notin S_{\pi}^{\infty}\cup S_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty}
, define

L(s,\pi_{v}\times\pi_{v}^{\prime})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(v)q_{v}^{-s}}.

Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
14
]

If
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, then there exist

On the Casimir number and formal codegree of Haagerup-Izumi fusion rings
Ying Zheng, Jiacheng Bao, Zhiqiang Yu

Abstract
For any cyclic group ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} , we first determine the Casimir number and determinant of the Haagerup-Izumi fusion ring ℋ ​ ℐ ℤ n \mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}} , it turns out that they do not share the same set of prime factors. Then we show that all finite-dimensional irreducible representations of ℋ ​ ℐ ℤ n \mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}} are defined over certain cyclotomic fields. As a direct result, we obtain the formal codegrees of ℋ ​ ℐ ℤ n \mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}} , which satisfy the pseudo-unitary inequality. Keywords: Haagerup-Izumi fusion ring; Casimir number; formal codegree Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 18M20



1
Introduction

The discovery of the Haagerup fusion category is one of the results in the classification of subfactors of small index
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
. Explicitly, the rank (i.e., the number of isomorphism classes of simple objects) of the Haagerup fusion category is
6
, and its fusion rule is as follows:

\displaystyle g\otimes X=X\otimes g^{2},g^{3}=\mathbf{1},X\otimes X=\mathbf{1}\oplus X\oplus gX\oplus g^{2}X.

Surprisingly, currently we still do not know whether the Drinfeld center of the Haagerup fusion category can be obtained from the semisimplification of tensor categories of representations of quantum groups at root of unity. Meanwhile, with the help of the Cuntz algebra, M. Izumi generalized the Haagerup fusion rules to a family of fusion rings determined by an arbitrary finite abelian group
[
5
]
, which is called the Haagerup-Izumi fusion rings now.

Generally, let
G
be a finite group. The Haagerup-Izumi fusion ring
\mathcal{HI}_{G}
is a fusion ring with
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-basis
\{g,gX|g\in G\}
and they satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle g(hX)=ghX=(hX)g^{-1},\quad(gX)(hX)=gh^{-1}+\sum_{l\in G}lX,\quad\forall g,h\in G.

M. Izumi characterized the existence of unitary fusion categories with Haagerup-Izumi fusion rules using a family of nonlinear equations
[
5
]
, and solutions were found for some groups of odd small orders. For example, if
G
is trivial, then the corresponding fusion ring is Yang-Lee fusion ring; if
G=\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, then we get the Haagerup fusion ring (or category). As far as we know, by solving Izumi’s equations, there exist fusion categories with Haagerup-Izumi fusion rules for cyclic groups
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of odd integers less than or equal to
29
, see
[
2
,
5
]
and the references therein.

On the one hand, in
[
8
]
, Ostrik defined the formal codegrees of fusion categories (or, generally fusion rings), which must be cyclotomic
d
-numbers (see
[
8
, Definition]
for details). It was further proved that the formal codegrees of fusion categories satisfy the pseudo-unitary equation (Equation
1
), and they are useful in determining the structure of Drinfeld centers of fusion categories, see
[
9
]
. However, these properties fail for that of fusion rings, in general. Hence, we can use the formal codegrees to detect whether there is a fusion categories with specific fusion rules.

On the other hand, the Casimir operator for Frobenius algebras was introduced in
[
6
]
, and the author studied the Casimir operator for both
H
and the Grothendieck ring of
\text{Rep}(H)
, where
H
is a Hopf algebra and
\text{Rep}(H)
is the category of finite-dimensional representations of
H
. In
[
12
]
, the authors defined the Casimir number and determinant of fusion rings, and they investigated relations between these concepts and related concepts, such as the global dimension and Frobenius-Schur exponent of fusion categories, see
[
3
]
for specific definition. It was asserted in
[
12
]
that the Casimir number and the determinant of the Grothendieck ring of a fusion category share the same set of prime factors. By determining the Casimir number and determinant of Haagerup-Izumi fusion rings
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, we find that their statement is incorrect, see Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.3
. Indeed, in the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 2.2]
, the authors used the commutativity of fusion rules inattentively.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some definitions of fusion categories and fusion rings, we refer the reader to
[
3
]
. In Section
3
, we calculate the Casimir number and determinant of the Haagerup-Izumi fusion rings
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, and we determine all the irreducible representations and formal codegrees of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
specifically (Lemma
3.5
and Proposition
3.7
), which then shows that
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
satisfies the the pseudo-unitary inequality (Theorem
3.8
).

Preliminary

A
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is called a
fusion category
if
\mathcal{C}
is a finite semisimple tensor category
[
3
]
. In the following, we use
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
and
\otimes
to denote the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
and the monoidal product on
\mathcal{C}
, respectively.

Let
R
be a fusion ring with
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-basis
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{s}\}
. It is well-known
[
3

that the Frobenius-Perron homomorphism
\text{FPdim}(-):R\to\mathbb{C}
is the unique ring homomorphism such that
\text{FPdim}(x_{j})\geq 1
for all
x_{j}
. Moreover,
\text{FPdim}(x_{j})
is an algebraic integer and is defined as the Frobenius-Perron dimension of
x_{j}
. The sum

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(R):=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\text{FPdim}(x_{j})^{2}

is called the Frobenius-Perron dimension of fusion ring
R
. For example, let
R=\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, then
\text{FPdim}(gX)=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}
,
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}})=n\sqrt{n^{2}+4}(\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2})
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pivotal fusion category with a pivotal structure
j
, then the categorical dimensions
\dim(-)
determined by
j
induces a homomorphism from
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathbb{C}
. If
\dim_{j}(X)=\dim_{j}(X^{*})
is true for all objects
X
of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{C}
said to be spherical
[
3
]
. And the global dimension of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\dim(X)^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. The
Casimir operator
of the Grothendieck ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
(or, generally a fusion ring)
[
6
, Section 3.1]
is the map
c
from
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to its center

given by

\displaystyle c(a)=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}XaX^{*},~\text{for}~a\in\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C}).

The element
c(\mathbf{1})=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}XX^{*}
is called the
Casimir element
of
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the unit object of
\mathcal{C}
. The intersection
\mathbb{Z}\cap\text{Im}(c)
is a nonzero principal ideal of
\mathbb{Z}
, and the positive generator of
\mathbb{Z}\cap\text{Im}(c)
is called the
Casimir number
of
\mathcal{C}

[
12
]
.
It is well-known that the matrix
[c(\mathbf{1})]
of left multiplication by
c(\mathbf{1})
with respect to the basis of
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
is a positive definite integer matrix, and its determinant
\text{det}[c(\mathbf{1})]
is called the
determinant
of
\mathcal{C}

[
12
]
.

Given a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, let
\text{Irr}(\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C}))
be the set of equivalence classes of irreducible representations of the associative algebra
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. For irreducible representations
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in\text{Irr}(\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C}))
, let
\text{Tr}_{\varphi}(-)
be the ordinary trace function on the representation
\varphi
. Then there exists a central element

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varphi}:=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\text{Tr}_{\varphi}(X)X^{*}\in\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}

such that
\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha_{\varphi})=0
if
\varphi\ncong\varphi^{\prime}
, and
\alpha_{\varphi}
acts on
\varphi
as a positive algebraic integer
f_{\varphi}

[
7
,
8
]
, called the
formal codegree
of
\mathcal{C}

[
8
]
.

It was shown in
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
that
c(\mathbf{1})
acts on
\varphi
as
f_{\varphi}\varphi(\mathbf{1})\cdot\text{id}
. For example, given a spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, the formal codegree determined by the Frobenius-Perron homomorphism and dimension homomorphism
\dim(-)
are the Frobenius-Perron dimension
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
and global dimension
\dim(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
, respectively. Moreover, the formal codegrees of a spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
satisfy the
pseudo-unitary equation
[
9
, Theorem 2.21]

\displaystyle\sum_{\varphi\in\text{Irr}(\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C}))}\frac{1}{f_{\varphi}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{C})}\right).

The Casimir number and formal codegrees of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}

In this section, we will first compute the Casimir number and determinant of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, then we give a complete equivalence classes of irreducible representations and the the corresponding formal codegrees of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
.

3.1
The Casimir number and determinant

In the following, we denote

\displaystyle Y_{0}=\mathbf{1},Y_{1}=g,...\quad Y_{n-1}=g^{n-1},Y_{n}=X,Y_{n+1}=gX,...Y_{2n-1}=g^{n-1}X.

Theorem 3.1
.

If n is odd, then the Casimir number of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
is
n(n^{2}+4)
;

Proof.

Let
x=\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}\lambda_{i}Y_{i}\in\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle c(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}Y_{i}xY_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\big[2n\lambda_{0}+n(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n\lambda_{1}+n\lambda_{n-1}+n(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n\lambda_{n-1}+n\lambda_{1}+n(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1})+(n^{2}+2)(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1})+(n^{2}+2)(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{2n-1}.

It follows from the definition of the Casimir number that

\displaystyle n\lambda_{1}+n\lambda_{n-1}

\displaystyle+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n\lambda_{2}+n\lambda_{n-2}

\displaystyle+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n\lambda_{n-1}+n\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots

\displaystyle+\lambda_{n-1})+(n^{2}+2)(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{1}+...+\lambda_{n-1})+(n-1)(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle 2n\lambda_{0}+(n^{2}+n+4)(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0.

Therefore,
c(x)=[2n\lambda_{0}+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})]\mathbf{1}=\frac{2n(n^{2}+4)}{n^{2}+n+4}\lambda_{0}\mathbf{1}
. Notice that both
n
and
(n^{2}+4)
are relatively prime to
n^{2}+n+4
, and
(n^{2}+n+4)
is even, so
\lambda_{0}=\frac{n^{2}+n+4}{2}
is the smallest positive integer such that the ratio
\frac{2n(n^{2}+4)}{n^{2}+n+4}\lambda_{0}
is an integer. Then we have
c(x)=n(n^{2}+4)\mathbf{1}
, and the Casimir number is
n(n^{2}+4)
by definition.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

If
n
is even, then the Casimir number of the fusion ring
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
is
\frac{n(n^{2}+4)}{2}
if
4\mid n
, otherwise it is
n(n^{2}+4)
.

Proof.

For any
x=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n-1}\lambda_{i}Y_{i}\in\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, similarly,
we have:

\displaystyle c(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}Y_{i}xY_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\big[2n\lambda_{0}+n(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n\lambda_{1}+n\lambda_{n-1}+n(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n\lambda_{n-1}+n\lambda_{1}+n(\lambda_{n}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1})+4(\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{n+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-2})+n^{2}(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1})+4(\lambda_{n+1}+\lambda_{n+3}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})+n^{2}(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{n+1}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1})+4(\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{n+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-2})+n^{2}(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{2n-2}

\displaystyle\quad+\big[n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1})+4(\lambda_{n+1}+\lambda_{n+3}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-1})+n^{2}(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})\big]Y_{2n-1}

From the definition of Casimir number,

\displaystyle n\lambda_{1}+n\lambda_{n-1}

\displaystyle+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n\lambda_{2}+n\lambda_{n-2}

\displaystyle+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n\lambda_{n-1}+n\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle+n(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n(\lambda_{0}+...+\lambda_{n-1})

\displaystyle+4(\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{n+2}+...+\lambda_{2n-2})+n^{2}(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n(\lambda_{0}+...+\lambda_{n-1})

\displaystyle+4(\lambda_{n+1}+\lambda_{n+3}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})+n^{2}(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0.

These equations imply

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{1}+...+\lambda_{n-1})+(n-1)(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle n(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+...+\lambda_{n-1})+(n^{2}+2)(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0,

\displaystyle 2n\lambda_{0}+(n^{2}+n+4)(\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1})=0.

Therefore,
\lambda_{n}+...+\lambda_{2n-1}=-\frac{2n}{n^{2}+n+4}\lambda_{0}
, it follows that
c(x)=\frac{2n(n^{2}+4)}{n^{2}+n+4}\lambda_{0}\mathbf{1}
. Since
n
is even, the largest common divisor
(n^{2}+n+4,n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}4,&\hbox{if $4\mid n$}\\
2,&\hbox{if $4\nmid n$.}\end{array}\right.
. By using a similar argument as Proposition
3.1
, the Casimir number of Haagerup-Izumi fusion ring
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
is
\frac{n(n^{2}+4)}{2}
if
4\mid n
, otherwise, it is
(n^{2}+4)n
.∎

Next we compute the determinant of the Haagerup-Izumi fusion ring
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The determinant of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
is
n^{2n}2^{2n-2}(n^{2}+4)
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
c(\mathbf{1})=2n\mathbf{1}+n(Y_{n}+Y_{n+1}+...+Y_{2n-1})
. Then we have

\displaystyle c(\mathbf{1})(Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2},...,Y_{2n-1})=(Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2},...,Y_{2n-1})\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}2n\cdot I_{n}&B\\
B&D\end{array}}\right),

where
B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}n&n&\cdots&n\\
n&n&\cdots&n\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
n&n&\cdots&n\\
\end{array}\right)
and
D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}n^{2}+2n&n^{2}&\cdots&n^{2}\\
n^{2}&n^{2}+2n&\cdots&n^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
n^{2}&n^{2}&\cdots&n^{2}+2n\\
\end{array}\right)
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\det\bigg(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}2n\cdot I_{n}&B\\
B&D\end{array}}\right)\bigg)=\det\bigg(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}2n\cdot I_{n}&0\\
B&D-\frac{1}{2}B^{2}\end{array}}\right)\bigg)=n^{2n}2^{2n-2}(n^{2}+4).

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

.

Notice that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=2n=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{i})^{2},&\hbox{if $n$ is odd,}\\
4+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{i})^{2},&\hbox{if $n$ is even,}\end{array}\right.

hence, together with Lemma
3.5
, the Wedderburn’ s Theorem shows that any irreducible representation with dimension being
larger than
1
must be isomorphic to one of these
V_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq[\frac{n-1}{2}])
. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

[
11
, Proposition 2.16]
.

Recall that the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a non-degenerate braided fusion category, and there is a surjective tensor functor
F
(called the forgetful functor) from
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

If
n
is odd, then the formal codegrees of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
are

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}),\sigma(\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}})),\overbrace{n,\cdots,n}^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

and if
n
is even, then the formal codegrees of the fusion ring
R
are

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}),\sigma(\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}})),2n,2n,\overbrace{n,\cdots,n}^{\frac{n-2}{2}},

where
\sigma
is a generator of
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n^{2}+4})/\mathbb{Q})
. Therefore, the formal codegrees of the fusion ring
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
satisfy the pseudo-unitary inequality.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 3
be an odd integer, the other case is similar. A direct computation shows

\displaystyle c(\mathbf{1})=2n\mathbf{1}+n\sum_{j=n}^{2n-1}Y_{j}.

Then Lemma
3.5
shows that
c(\mathbf{1})
acts on one-dimensional representations as scalar
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}})
and
\sigma(\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}))
, which are formal codegrees of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
. And
c(\mathbf{1})
acts on all two-dimensional irreducible representations as
2n
by Proposition
3.7
, so all the corresponding formal codegrees of
\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
are
n
by definition. Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi}\frac{1}{f_{\chi}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{2n^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sigma(\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}))^{2}}+\frac{1}{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}+n+4}{2n(n^{2}+4)}<\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{C})}\right).

This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{C})\neq n,2n
. In particular,
\dim(\mathcal{C})
is a Galois conjugate of
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{HI}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}})
.

Proof.

Notice that the rank of
\mathcal{C}
is
2n
,
[
10
, Theorem 4.2.2]
states that there is a Galois conjugate of
\dim(\mathcal{C})
that is no less than
2n
, so
\dim(\mathcal{C})\neq n
. Moreover,
\dim(\mathcal{C})=2n
if and only if all simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
has Frobenius-Perron dimension
1

by
[
10
, Remark 4.2.3]
, which is impossible for Haagerup-Izumi fusion rings.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let

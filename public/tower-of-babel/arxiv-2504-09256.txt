1.
Introduction

The braid group on
n
strands,
B_{n}
, is a fundamental algebraic structure that has connections to many areas of Mathematics such as Representation Theory, Knot Theory and Quantum Computing
[
24
,
14
]
. It was introduced by E. Artin in 1925
[
1
]
, where it was defined by its generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}
with the following defining relations.

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},\hskip 18.49411pti=1,2,\ldots,n-2,

\displaystyle\ \ \hskip 1.42271pt\ \ \ \sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i},\hskip 46.94687pt|i-j|\geq 2.

The pure braid group,
P_{n}
, is a normal subgroup of
B_{n}
, which is defined as the kernel of the homomorphism
B_{n}\rightarrow S_{n}
defined by
\sigma_{i}\rightarrow(i\hskip 5.69046pti+1)
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
, where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group of
n
elements (see
[
2
]
). It admits a presentation with the following generators.

A_{ij}=\sigma_{j-1}\sigma_{j-2}\ldots\sigma_{i+1}\sigma^{2}_{i}\sigma^{-1}_{i+1}\ldots\sigma^{-1}_{j-2}\sigma^{-1}_{j-1},\hskip 14.22636pt1\leq i<j\leq n.

There are a lot of generalizations of
B_{n}
, both from algebraic and geometric point of view. One of these generalizations is the singular braid monoid,
SM_{n}
, which was introduced by J. Baez in 1992
[
3
]
and J. Birman in 1993
[
9
]
independently. It is a monoid generated by the generators
\sigma_{1}^{\pm 1},\sigma_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}^{\pm 1}
of
B_{n}
and a family of singular generators
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}
. In 1998, R. Fenn, E. Keyman and C. Rourke proved that
SM_{n}
embeds into a group, denoted by
SB_{n}
, and called the singular braid group
[
15
]
, which has the same generators and defining relations as
SM_{n}
.

On the other hand, one of the monoid extensions of both
B_{n}
and
SM_{n}
is the virtual singular braid monoid,
VSM_{n}
, which was introduced by C. Caprau, A. Pena and S. McGahan in 2016
[
12
]
.
VSM_{n}
is a monoid generated by the generators
\sigma_{1}^{\pm 1},\sigma_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}^{\pm 1}
,
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}
of
SM_{n}
in addition to a family of non-singular generators
\nu_{1}^{\pm 1},\nu_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,\nu_{n-1}^{\pm 1}
. It was shown in 2022 by C. Caprau and A. Yeung that
VSM_{n}
embeds into a group, denoted by
VSB_{n}
, and called the virtual singular braid group
[
13
]
, which has the same generators and defining relations as
VSM_{n}
.

One of the most important questions to be examined in this field is to study the irreducibility of representations of many algebraic structures. A representation
\rho
of a group
G
on a vector space
V
is said to be irreducible if there is no non-trivial subspace of
V
which is invariant under
\rho
. Another important question to be examined for representations is to study their faithfulness. The importance of finding a faithful representation of a group
G
is to prove that
G
is linear.

There are many famous representations of the braid group
B_{n}
where the question of irreducibility and faithfulness is answered. One of these representations is Burau representation
[
11
]
and the other is Lawrence-Krammer-Bigelow representation
[
18
]
. Regarding the irreducibility of these two representations, it has been proven by E. Formanek in
[
16
]
that Burau representation is reducible, and by C. Levaillant in
[
19
]
that Lawrence-Krammer-Bigelow representation is irreducible. On the other hand, regarding the faithfulness, Burau representation was proved to be faithful for
n\leq 3
in
[
8
]
and unfaithful for
n\geq 5
in
[
23
,
20
,
6
]
; whereas the case
n=4
remains open. In contrast, it was shown in
[
7
]
and
[
17
]
that Lawrence-Krammer-Bigelow representation is faithful for all
n\geq 2
; thus,
B_{n}
is shown to be linear.

Lately, it was interested in extending the known representations of
B_{n}
to its group and monoid extensions, and to examine the irreducibility and the faithfulness of these extended representations. An important task to do here is to study the ways of extending representations of
B_{n}
to its extensions. One of these ways, for example, is the
k
-local extensions way, which is defined by M. Nasser in
[
28
]
. A lot of work has been done in classifying this type of extensions to many group and monoid extensions of
B_{n}
(see
[

,
27
]
). More specifically, another way for extending representations of

to

and

was found by V. Bardakov, N. Chbili, and T. Kozlovskaya in
[

; we call it the

-type extensions way. One of the extensions in the

-type way is the Birman representation of

defined in
[
9
]
, that was shown to be faithful by L. Paris in
[

]
, which implies that

is linear. M. Nasser studied the faithfulness of this type of extensions of representations of

to

in some cases
[
26
]
. Also, M. Prabhakar and N. Komal extended the concept of

-type extensions to one of the monoid extensions of both
B_{n}
and
SM_{n}
, namely the singular twisted virtual braid monoid
[
30
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in another important representation of
B_{n}
, which is called the standard representation (or Tong-Yang-Ma representation), that introduced first by D. Tong, S. Yang and Z. Ma in 1996
[

Recently, in

to extend the standard representation

to the string links and welded string links. We aim, in our article, to extend the standard representation

: the singular braid group

and the virtual singular braid group

. Moreover, we examine the irreducibility and the faithfulness of these extensions. The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give the main definitions and relations for the targeted groups and monoids in this paper:

4.
On the Irreducibility and the Faithfulness of the Representations of the Form
\rho^{\prime}_{s}
of
SB_{n}

4.1.
On the Irreducibility of
\rho^{\prime}_{s}

In the first part of this section, we specify
t
to be a non-zero complex number. Recall that, according to Theorem
9
, the standard representation
\rho_{S}:B_{n}\to GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is irreducible if and only if
t\neq 1
. In this subsection, we find necessary and sufficient conditions for the irreducibility of the representations of the form
\rho^{\prime}_{S}:SB_{n}\to GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
determined in Section 3. First, we give two lemmas that helps us in our proof.

Lemma 14
.

Consider
n\geq 2
and let
\rho:G\to GL_{n}(V)
be a representation of a group
G
on a vector space
V
. Let
H
be any subgroup of
G
. If
\rho:H\to GL_{n}(V)
is irreducible, then,
\rho:G\to GL_{n}(V)
is also irreducible.

Proof.

Suppose that
\rho:G\to GL_{n}(V)
is reducible, then, there exists an invariant subspace
S
of
V
such that
\rho(g)s\in S
for every
s\in S
and
g\in G
. This implies that
\rho(h)s\in S
for every
s\in S
and
h\in H\subset G
. Hence,
S
is an invariant subspace of
V
with respect to
\rho:H\to GL_{n}(V)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
\rho^{\prime}_{S}:SB_{n}\to GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an extension of the standard representation of
B_{n}
determined in Section 3 evaluated at
t=1
. Let
S
be a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that is invariant under
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
. If there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
e_{i}\in S
, where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

Recall that the representation
\rho^{\prime}_{S}:SB_{n}\to GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
evaluated at
t=1
becomes as follows.

\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}I_{i-1}\end{matrix}&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip 5.69046pt\begin{matrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{matrix}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&I_{n-i-1}\end{array}\right)

and

\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\tau_{i})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}I_{i-1}\end{matrix}&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip 5.69046pt\begin{matrix}a&c\\
c&a\\
\end{matrix}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&I_{n-i-1}\end{array}\right),

where
a,c\in\mathbb{C}
with
a^{2}-c^{2}\neq 0
. We consider the following cases.

(1)

Case
i=1
: If
e_{1}\in S
, then
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{1})(e_{1})=e_{2}\in S
, and so,
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{2})(e_{2})=e_{3}\in S
. Continue in this way, we get that
e_{i}\in S
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and so,
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, as required.

(2)

Case
i=n
: If
e_{n}\in S
, then
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{n-1})(e_{n})=e_{n-1}\in S
, and so,
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{n-2})(e_{n-1})=e_{n-2}\in S
. Continue in this way, we get again that
e_{i}\in S
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and so,
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, as required.

(3)

Case
1<i<n
: If
e_{i}\in S
in this case, then
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i})(e_{i})=e_{i+1}\in S
, and so,
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i+1})(e_{i+1})=e_{i+2}\in S
. Continue in this way we get that
e_{j}\in S
for all
i\leq j\leq n
. On the other hand, we have
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i-1})(e_{i})=e_{i-1}\in S
, and so,
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i-2})(e_{i-1})=e_{i-2}\in S
. Continue in this way, we get that
e_{i}\in S
for all
1\leq j\leq i
. Therefore,
e_{i}\in S
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and so,
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, as required.

∎

Theorem 16
.

Let
\rho^{\prime}_{S}:SB_{n}\to GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an extension of the standard representation of
B_{n}
determined in Section 3. Recall that

\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}I_{i-1}\end{matrix}&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip 5.69046pt\begin{matrix}0&t\\
1&0\\
\end{matrix}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&I_{n-i-1}\end{array}\right)

and

\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\tau_{i})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}I_{i-1}\end{matrix}&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip 5.69046pt\begin{matrix}a&ct\\
c&a\\
\end{matrix}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&I_{n-i-1}\end{array}\right),

where
a,c\in\mathbb{C}
with
a^{2}-tc^{2}\neq 0
. Then, the following holds true.

(1)

If
t\neq 1
, then
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
is irreducible.

(2)

If
t=1
, then
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
is irreducible if and only if
a+c\neq 1.

Proof.

We consider each case separately.

(1)

First, if
t\neq 1
, then, the restriction of
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
to
B_{n}
, which is the standard representation
\rho_{S}
, is irreducible by Theorem
9
. So,
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
is irreducible by Lemma
14
.

(2)

Now, set
t=1
.

\bullet

For the necessary condition, suppose that
a+c=1
, then, we see that the column vector
(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}
is invariant under
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
. So, the representation
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
is reducible, as required.

\bullet

For the sufficient condition, suppose that
a+c\neq 1
and suppose to get a contradiction that
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
is reducible. Let
S
be a non-trivial subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that is invariant under
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i})
and
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\tau_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
and pick
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}\in S
to be a non-zero vector. For all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have,

\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{i})(x)-x=(0,0,\ldots,0,\underbrace{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}_{i},\underbrace{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}_{i+1},0,0,\ldots,0)\in S.

So, if
x_{i}=x_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then,
x
becomes the vector
(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}
multiplied by a constant, which is invariant under
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\tau_{1})
by our assumption, and so, we get that
a+c=1
, a contradiction.

Then, there exists
1\leq j\leq n-1
such that
x_{j}\neq x_{j+1}
. Hence, as
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{j})(x)-x=(x_{j+1}-x_{j})e_{j}+(x_{j}-x_{j+1})e_{j+1}\in S
, we get that the column vector

v_{1}=e_{j}-e_{j+1}\in S.

And so, we get that the column vector

v_{2}=\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{j+1})(v_{1})=e_{j}-e_{j+2}\in S,

which gives that the column vector

v_{3}=\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\sigma_{j})(v_{2})=e_{j+1}-e_{j+2}\in S
.

Now, we can see that

v_{4}=\rho^{\prime}_{S}(\tau_{j})(v_{3})=ce_{j}+ae_{j+1}-e_{j+2}
,

and so,

v_{5}=v_{4}-v_{3}-cv_{1}=(a+c-1)e_{j+1}\in S
.

Hence, as
a+c\neq 1
, we get that
e_{j+1}\in S
. Therefore, by Lemma
15
, we get
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, a contradiction. Thus,
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
is irreducible in this case and the proof is completed.

∎

Reply to “Comment on ‘QCD factorization with multihadron fragmentation functions’ ”
T. C. Rogers, M. Radici, A. Courtoy, T. Rainaldi

Abstract
We respond to comments in Ref. Pitonyak et al. 2025 about our article, Ref. Rogers et al. 2025 . We stand by our conclusions and defend them against the criticisms.



I
Introduction

An important feature of factorization theorems like Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (3))
is that all dependence on the observed small-mass nonperturbative external states is isolated within the states themselves. When considering different external states, one simply switches
|h\rangle\to|h^{\prime}\rangle
in the relevant parton correlation function and nothing else in the formula changes. Our example, collinear factorization for the semi-inclusive annihilation (SIA) process, is among the most firmly established of all the factorization formulas. No new derivations are necessary when changing the identity of the observed final state, as long as the kinematics remain consistent with collinear SIA as described by a single fragmenting parton.

Historically, dihadron fragmentation functions have been very attractive for studying nonperturbative structures because the above means the factorization theorem applies automatically to nonperturbative hadronic final states made of multiple hadrons, so long as one remains within the kinematical domain of factorization.
That is, it is valid for the
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}
in Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (3))
to be the general phase space in Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (4))
. All results can be taken over directly from existing knowledge about the treatment of single hadron factorization.
See, for example, the textbook account in Sec. 12.13.3 of Ref.
Collins 2011
. There one reads

“
In the final state in a definition like (12.35), we replace

|p,X,\text{out}\rangle\langle\text{out},X,p|

by

|p_{1},p_{2},X,\text{out}\rangle\langle\text{out},X,p_{1},p_{2}|
.
At the partonic end of the fragmentation function, nothing changes. So all the issues about renormalization, DGLAP evolution, and the construction of a hard-scattering coefficient function are unchanged.
”

We see this as central when the interpretation of measurements involves intrinsic partonic structures. It is why, in past work, single and multihadron fragmentation functions have always been taken to have identical definitions, up to the identity of the nonperturbative final state – see for example Ref.
Collins and Ladinsky 1994
.

However,
Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024

makes strong claims that call into question this overall view, arguing that the standard fragmentation function definition has no number density interpretation at all when extended to multihadrons, thus bringing into question the reliability of past work.
Instead, it argues that the basic operator definition
must be modified with nontrivial external-state-dependent prefactors. This is the origin of the current debate.

We disagree with these conclusions, and in our Ref.
Rogers et al. 2025
we defended the standard form of the definition and its origins in factorization.
We identified the contradiction between it and the assertions of Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
as originating from a mixing up of partonic and hadronic kinematics combined with the imposition of an invalid sum rule.
We also explained that the ordinary fragmentation function does indeed retain a solid number density interpretation.
In their comment
Pitonyak et al. 2025
, the authors further promote their arguments from Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
.

The foundation of the argument in Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
is to replace the regular factorization theorem for hadron multiplicities by a specific type of conjectured multiplicity sum rule
(
Pitonyak et al. 2024
, Eq. (6))
.
Then, by demanding that this sum rule be satisfied, it is argued that the normal definition of the fragmentation function must be modified by a nontrivial prefactor when the final state is a small-mass multihadron system.

However, the sum rule in Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2024
, Eq. (6))
is not valid for multiple reasons. These were explained in Refs.
Collins and Rogers 2024
;
Rogers and Courtoy 2025
and Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Section VIII)
. Moreover, it implies for the SIA cross section a factorized expression that contradicts the above principle, as recalled from Ref.
Collins 2011
(and stressed also in Ref.
Rogers et al. 2025
).
Our perspective is that
the sum rule is not a valid basis for either derivations or their interpretations. It is the root of the discrepancy between the standard definition and the one recommended in Refs.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
. We will continue to expand on this here.

II
Parton Model

For notation and an explanation of reference frames, see Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Sec.III)
.

In Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2025
it is written that in Ref.
Rogers et al. 2025
we must “
define a “reduced” DiFF
in the middle of
a parton model calculation
” to ensure “
the expected
expression for the cross section is achieved.
”
That this is not the case can be seen from
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (86))
. By definition, the parton model is the result of dropping
O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)
errors (and integrating over angles for the unpolarized case) to get

\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{parton model}}}{{=}}d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})\,.

(1)

The
d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
is just the standard fragmentation function definition for a multi-hadron system with small invariant mass, collective 4-momentum variables
\{p_{h}\}
, and carrying a fraction
\xi=z
of the fragmenting parton momentum. It is completely determined, up to a choice of renormalization scheme, by its operator definition in
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eqs. (79-81))
. There is no need to invoke a concept of a reduced fragmentation function to get to the parton model expression here.
For the transverse-momentum dependent (TMD) case, each combination of
\xi
,
-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}}
,
and set of 4-vectors
\left\{p_{h}\right\}
maps onto a definite value for the fragmentation function, and the collinear function is obtained by integrating upon
-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}}
. In the parton model,
d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
is a density in
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits{}
.

\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits
is simply
the result of transforming variables in the full hadronic phase space
as (see Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (4))
)

\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits=\frac{1}{2E_{p_{h}}(2\pi)^{3}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits\,.

(2)

We could leave Eq. (
1
) differential in
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits
, but separating out the total momentum
\bm{p}_{h}
is convenient and allows us to immediately integrate over
\bm{p}_{h}
angles.
If writing a generic
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits{}
is unsatisfying, one may use the specific variables in
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (106))
:
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits{}=\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits\,M_{h}/32\pi^{3}
.

The above expressions
are unambiguous.
The fragmentation function is not a density with respect to an unspecified variable choice. Rather, it is a density with respect to the physical hadronic phase space from asymptotic hadronic states, the same states that appear when defining an
S
-matrix.
In terms of partons, it is a function of one partonic momentum fraction
\xi
, as expected because there is only one fragmenting parton leaving the hard part.
The hard part (which here is
\sigma_{0}
) is independent of the arbitrary choice of external kinematical variables that describe hadrons. If in Eq. (
1
) one instead used the fragmentation function from Refs.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
;
Pitonyak et al. 2025
with its hadron-number-dependent prefactor, then the hard part would
also become hadron-number-dependent, thus breaking factorization. We explain this around our
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (137))
.

The number density interpretation of the standard
d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
follows immediately from Eq. (
1
). We hope this definitively clarifies that the standard definition does not lack a number density interpretation. In particular, the statement from Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024

If one instead were to use a prefactor of
1/(4z)
, to be in full analogy with single-hadron fragmentation [1, 59–61], the quark uDiFFs would not retain a
number density interpretation

is not correct.

When making variable transformations to the left side of
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (3))
, there is no reference to partonic momenta. So, in deciding on a set of variables to use for external physical momenta, Jacobian factors should not involve internal partonic degrees of freedom in any way. The parton momentum is an internal integration variable.
Therefore, we do not agree that the various
partonic
variable transformations in Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
, Sec. III )
are necessary or helpful.

If the relative internal momenta in an
n
-hadron system cannot be measured (but are restricted to the factorization region) then it is useful to rewrite Eq. (
1
) as

\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{parton model}}}{{=}}d(z,\mathcal{V})\,,

(3)

with

d(z,\mathcal{V})=\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits d(z,\{p_{h}\})\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{V}
is some fixed region of the internal
n
-hadron phase space.
The
d(z,\mathcal{V})
and
d(z,\{p_{h}\})
are the same fragmentation function definitions except that with
d(z,\mathcal{V})
the final states include integrals
upon relative internal momenta over some fixed region of phase space.
Most importantly, no factors get shifted between the hard part and the fragmentation function just because a subset of the final states includes integrals upon
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits{}
. As always, changing the identity of the measured final state changes nothing in the definition but the state itself.

Leaving subsets of the variables in
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits{}
unintegrated gives versions of Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
1
) differential in
these subsets.
For example, leaving the integral over
M_{h}
in Eq. (
4
) undone immediately makes Eq. (
3
) into
Eq. (9) from Ref.
Courtoy et al. 2012
.

III
Factorization

In our
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (137))
and surrounding text, we observed that one may force the fragmentation function preferred by Refs.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
;
Pitonyak et al. 2025
into an expression that matches the cross section, but only if
at least an additional extra power of
\xi
is allowed to be absorbed into the hard part for each extra hadron that appears in the final state. We wrote,

…one is forced to modify the hard partonic
part with extra powers of
\xi
for each additional hadron

But having an external-state-dependent hard part destroys factorization, and then
the question of which prefactor for
d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
is correct becomes ill-defined. It means one may simply shift external-state-dependent factors arbitrarily between the hard part and the fragmentation function
without invalidating the expression for the cross section.
That is, one may always break
factorization in the formula
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq.(3))
just by rewriting it as

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}}=\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(X_{h}(\xi)2E_{\hat{k}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{\sigma}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{\hat{k}}}\nolimits{}}\right)\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{X_{h}(\xi)}d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})\right)}_{d^{\prime}(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})}\,,

(5)

where
X_{h}(\xi)
is some function of
\xi
made to depend on the observed nonperturbative external state
|h\rangle
. The
d^{\prime}(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
is a modified definition of the fragmentation function. The
X_{h}(\xi)
could depend on any details of the nonperturbative state including its species, mass or, most relevantly for the current discussion, the number of hadrons in the state.

The
absolute
normalization of the hard part can be fixed by convention, but once a choice is made it must be kept fixed for all types of external states. Factors should not move in and out of the hard part in a way that depends on the external nonperturbative states.
If they do, then the hard part does not describe properties that are independent of the nonperturbative states.
By arbitrarily choosing different
X_{h}(\xi)
’s for each type of nonperturbative final state, one may always force the fragmentation functions
d^{\prime}(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
to be any functions one desires. Then, the question of which definition is correct loses any meaning.

We believe that this is what the authors of Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2025
do to arrive at their
Eq.(7)

.
The manipulations there are expressed in the language of Jacobians, but the steps in going between Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
,
Eq.(7)

)
and Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
,
Eq.(9)

)
amount to redefining the fragmentation function within the factorization formula by shuffling factors back and forth between the fragmentation function and the hard part as in Eq. (
5
) above.
Moreover, the authors of Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2025
claim to have written, with their
Eq.(7)

, a factorized formula for the cross section employing the standard parton model expression for the hard part.
But that is not the case.
The extra hadron-number-dependent powers of
\xi
have just been grouped
into
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{z}}\nolimits{}/\hat{z}^{n-1}
with the factors outside the parentheses.
However, there is only one fragmenting parton, hence the integration upon its variables must not depend on the number
n
of hadrons in the final state.
The crucial point, in other words, is that the information about the dependence on the number of hadrons in the observed nonperturbative final state has been moved outside of the fragmentation function, thereby spoiling factorization. The presence of these spurious factors cannot be avoided by grouping them with factors labeled as being part of the integration measure.

Finally, the authors of Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2025
mention that when
n=2
the dihadron fragmentation needs to be matched onto two single-hadron fragmentation functions at large
M_{h}
. We just point out here that nothing prevents one from expanding the
d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
from
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Eq. (3))
in single-hadron collinear fragmentation functions at large
M_{h}
and performing the matching consistently. There is no difficulty with consistently matching single parton fragmentation at small
M_{h}
with a large
M_{h}
treatment.

If the alternative definition from Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
is used, the problems with the factorization discussed above extend also to the treatment of evolution kernels. Renormalization factors are independent of the identity of nonperturbative external states, and this is central to their partonic interpretation, but the modified definition in Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
forces them to acquire external state dependence. The definition in the last set of parentheses of
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
, Eq. (7))
, when treated as the fundamental definition, suffers from the problems outlined in
Ref.
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Sec. VIII & App. C)
.

IV
Number Densities

To compare the standard definition with the one promoted in Refs.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2024
;
Pitonyak et al. 2025
, Sec. IV)
, we retrace here the steps for writing down a quark fragmentation function. One starts with the general number density operator for hadron
h
made from completely generic asymptotic out-states,

a_{p_{h},\text{out}}^{\dagger}a_{p_{h},\text{out}}=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}}=\SumInt_{X}|p_{h},X\rangle\langle p_{h},X|=(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h}}\nolimits}=(2\pi)^{3}2\xi\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h,p\text{T}}}\nolimits}\,.

(6)

It only involves the hadron momentum
p_{h}
, it has no knowledge about the existence of partons, and it makes no reference to a specific process.
The far right is expressed using the parton momentum fraction
\xi
, but that is only possible because we specify the components of
p_{h}
in a parton frame (
\bm{p}_{h,p}
) where there is a fixed external
k_{p}^{+}
. The full phase space is Lorentz invariant and independent of coordinates.

To construct a fragmentation function, one takes the
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits{}/\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}
expectation value between quark states and sets that equal to a function identified with the fragmentation function, up to a quark state normalization,

\langle\bm{k}|\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\bm{k}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle\mathcal{H}\,d(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},p_{h}).

(7)

The left side of the equality
has two separate parts:
i)
the hadron number density operator from Eq. (
6
), which only depends on external hadron states and makes no reference to quarks;
ii)
the quark states
which bring in the dependence on a quark momentum.
On the right side of Eq. (
7
), one isolates all the dependence on the external states inside a function labeled the fragmentation function
d(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},p_{h})
. Having all the dependence on nonperturbative external states isolated in a universal factor
is central to what it means that
d
is
a fragmentation function. The rest of the factors on the right side are any leftover parts that are
independent
of the external nonperturbative states. That includes the
distribution
\langle\bm{k}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle
and a possible normalization factor
\mathcal{H}
that fixes the integrals over
\bm{k^{\prime}}
that determines the normalization convention for the light-cone quark states.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is unrelated to the external nonperturbative states, and is not inside the fragmentation function, it ends up being absorbed into the hard parts of whatever factorization formulas involve the fragmentation function.
Its practical effect is to fix the
overall normalizations of the hard parts.
1
1

1

The overall normalization is fixed not only at the parton model level but for any perturbative order.

Since it goes into the hard part,
\mathcal{H}
must be completely independent of the states in Eq. (
6
) if factorization is to be preserved, and it must be completely universal. Normally, one automatically just uses the most natural normalization,

\mathcal{H}=(2\pi)^{3}2\xi\,,

(8)

because then hard parts in factorization have exactly the normalizations of partonic cross sections.
With this choice, the

\mathcal{H}
brings the factorization formula as close as possible to the intuitive parton model.
Other values for
\mathcal{H}
are acceptable in principle
so long as it is kept totally universal and independent of the external states that make up the hadron number density operator.

Using Eq. (
8
), we can write

\langle\bm{k}|(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h}}\nolimits}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\bm{k}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle(2\pi)^{3}2\xi\,d(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},p_{h})\,.

(9)

This is the normal way to build up the definition of a fragmentation function.
Equation (
9
) is equivalent, for example, to Ref.
(
Collins and Soper 1982
, Eq.(4.5))
in the parton frame.
2
2

2

Note that, similarly to Eq. (
6
) above, it is the number density operator
a_{A}^{\dagger}a_{A}
that appears in the quark matrix element in Ref.
(
Collins and Soper 1982
, Eq.(4.5))
, and the factor
1/2\,P^{+}(2\pi)^{d-1}
on the right-hand side just gives the
(2\pi)^{3}2\xi
in Eq. (
9
) when using parton frame variables.

It is also equivalent to Ref.
(
Collins 2011
, Eq. (12.35))
.
The definition is very intuitive and natural: One just takes the quark expectation value of the standard hadron number density operator.
Using Eq. (
6
) we can rewrite
Eq. (
9
) as

\langle\bm{k}|\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h,p\text{T}}}\nolimits}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\bm{k}|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle\,d(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},p_{h})\,.

(10)

Nothing in the discussion between Eq. (
6
) and Eq. (
10
) depends on the number density operator in Eq. (
6
) being just for
single
hadrons.
Therefore, generalizing to multiple observed hadrons simply amounts to including the possibility that
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}}\nolimits/\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits

has more than one hadron.
The
n
-hadron generalization of Eq. (
6
) is

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N_{n}}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}}

\displaystyle=a_{p_{h_{1}},\text{out}}^{\dagger}a_{p_{h_{2}},\text{out}}^{\dagger}\cdots a_{p_{h_{n}},\text{out}}^{\dagger}a_{p_{h_{n}},\text{out}}\cdots a_{p_{h_{2}},\text{out}}a_{p_{h_{1}},\text{out}}

\displaystyle=\SumInt_{X}|p_{h_{1}},\dots,p_{h_{n}}X\rangle\langle p_{h_{1}},\dots,p_{h_{n}},X|=(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h_{1}}\times\cdots\times(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h_{n}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}_{n}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits\cdots\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{n}}}\nolimits}\,.

It is important that this operator is differential in multiple
hadron
momenta, not parton momenta.
There are no other changes at all in the definition of the fragmentation function. In particular, nothing on the right side of Eq. (
7
) changes. All dependence on nonperturbative external states remains isolated within the fragmentation function
d(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},\{p_{h}\})
. (See also Eq.(2.14) of Ref.
Collins and Ladinsky 1994
.)

The number density interpretation here is also very direct and intuitive. The ordinary fragmentation function applies in exactly the same familiar way, even if the observed final state is made of
n
hadrons.
All aspects related to the partonic end of the fragmentation function are unchanged.
This is the basic form that has been used most in past applications and is what we used in
(
Rogers et al. 2025
, Sec. V)
.

By comparison, to recover the definition promoted in
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
,
Eq.(14)

)
, one is forced to replace the standard quark normalization factor
\mathcal{H}
in Eq. (
7
) by the external-state-dependent factor

\mathcal{H}\to(2\pi)^{3}2\xi_{1}\times\cdots\times(2\pi)^{3}2\xi_{n}\propto(\xi)^{n}\,.

(19)

However, this has a nontrivial dependence on the identity of the external nonperturbative state
through the dependence on the number of hadrons
n
in the state. The dependence on the external state is no longer isolated inside of
d(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},\{p_{h}\})
. This dependence gets placed inside the hard part when used in a factorization formula, and so it breaks factorization.

We disagree with Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2025
that their
Eq.(14)

, which is written as a density in
n
separate
parton
momenta, is standard or expected. While there are
n
final state hadrons, there is only one fragmenting quark, so the appearance of
n
copies of differentials in parton momentum fractions
is very unexpected. In reality, in the standard definition the dependence on external
hadron
momenta enters through the hadron number operator in Eq. (
IV
), which makes no reference to partons. To obtain the definition for an
n
-hadron system,
one does not first divide out the
(2\pi)^{3}2\xi
factor on both sides of Eq. (
9
) for the special case of the single hadron and then simply append extra partonic differentials
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi_{i}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{i},p\text{T}}}\nolimits
to the left side to extend it to the case of multiple hadrons, but this appears to be what has happened with Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
,
Eq.(14)

)
.

To try to better understand how to interpret the operator in Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2025
,
Eq.(14)

)
, we
rewrite it using Eq. (
IV
) and
the parton frame relation
\xi_{i}k_{p}^{+}=p_{h_{i}}^{+}
, namely

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}_{n}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi_{1}}\nolimits\cdots\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi_{n}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1},p\text{T}}}\nolimits\cdots\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{n},p\text{T}}}\nolimits}\sim\frac{1}{\xi^{n}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\widehat{N}_{n}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}}\,,

(20)

where the “
\sim
” means that we do not write simple numerical factors that do not depend on
\xi
. Because it incorporates information about a
parton
through the
1/\xi^{n}
, the operator in Eq. (
20
) is not a regular hadron number density operator like Eq. (
IV
). Nor is it related to Eq. (
IV
) through a universal and state-independent normalization factor, like the
\mathcal{H}
in Eq. (
8

V
Conclusion

Moving forward, it is important to have clarity about which aspects of factorization, operator definitions,
etc
are reasonably well established and which might need to be changed or modified. Our concern regarding Ref.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
is the likelihood that the sum rule there, due to its depiction as rigorous and indispensable for interpretations, will get incorporated into future constraints on theoretical treatments of fragmentation functions in studies of nonperturbative QCD.
Nothing guarantees that Ref.
(
Pitonyak et al. 2024
, Eq. (6))
relates fragmentation functions to actual hadron multiplicities, however, even to a rough approximation. Thus,
using it to constrain models or nonperturbative treatments in this way would be problematic.
Meanwhile, the standard fragmentation function definition that has long been used in the past for nonperturbative modeling, for example in Ref.
(
Matevosyan et al. 2017
, Eq. (11))
, is valid both technically and in the sense of interpretations.
Past efforts like these do not need to be discarded on the basis of the claims made in Refs.
Pitonyak et al. 2024
;
Pitonyak et al. 2025
.

Such considerations become increasingly important as prospects for addressing fragmentation from first principles in nonperturbative QCD grow more feasible (see, e.g., the recent work in Ref.
Gálvez-Viruet
et al. 2025a
;
Gálvez-Viruet
et al. 2025b

Lee and Stewart 2025
;
Kang et al. 2025
). The independence of process-specific hard parts from universal, external-state-dependent fragmentation functions is central to the partonic interpretations that inspire this work.

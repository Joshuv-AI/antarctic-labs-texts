Construction and clinical validation of a multidimensional stratified management path based on dynamic patient-reported outcomes (PROs) in cancer pain management: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Jie Huang, Chong Zhang, YangJun Yue, XiaoLin Yu, FuRong Zhang, YingNan Zhang, WanXia Lu, Yue Wang, XiaoYun Wang, YiXin Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPain is one of the most prevalent symptoms among patients with cancer, and its presence and severity provide important prognostic information regarding survival. Patients with cancer pain experience a decline in their overall quality of life, along with a corresponding reduction in physical, cognitive, emotional and social functioning. Symptom management based on dynamic patient-reported outcomes (PROs) is recognised as the optimal ‘patient - centered’ healthcare model. However, clinical trial evidence on symptom management for patients with cancer pain remains insufficient. Therefore, we plan to conduct a randomised controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a PRO-based symptom monitoring response programme on pain intervention in patients with cancer in pain.Research methods and analysisThis study will recruit 116 patients with cancer in pain from our hospital. They will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention group or the control group. When a patient in the intervention group reports target symptoms (pain Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) >3, emotional screening Patient Health Questionnaire-4 Score >5 or nutritional screening NRS2002 Score ≥3), they will receive stratified symptom management based on PROs. A specialist physician will make adjustments according to symptom severity. Patients in the control group will not trigger alerts and will continue to follow the standard symptom management process. All patients will undergo a baseline assessment on the day of enrolment. The control group will receive routine outpatient management, and follow-ups will be conducted via telephone or online at 1 week, 2 weeks and 1 month after discharge. All patients will be followed up until 3 months after enrolment, disease progression, achievement of a pain-free state or death, whichever occurs first. The primary outcome measure—the effective rate of pain control—will be statistically analysed to compare differences between the intervention and control groups.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chengdu BOE Hospital on 14 August 2025 (approval No.: 2025052) and subsequently amended on 14 January 2026 (approval No.: 2026006). The manuscript is based on V.2.0 of the study protocol dated 14 January 2026. The study results will be disseminated in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at academic conferences.Trials registration numberChiCTR2500114591Keywords: Cancer Pain, Case-Control Studies, Chronic Disease, ONCOLOGY

Introduction

According to the Global Cancer Report,1 the number of newly diagnosed cancer cases worldwide reached 19.96 million in 2022, and it is projected to exceed 35 million by 2050. Among these cases, China reported approximately 4.8 million new cancer cases in 2022 (accounting for 24% of the global total), with about 2.6 million cancer-related deaths (representing 26.7% of the global total). Pain is one of the most prevalent symptoms among patients with cancer and has been recognised by the WHO and the International Association for the Study of Pain as the fifth vital sign, alongside respiration, pulse, temperature and blood pressure.2

A study3 reports that 64% of patients with locally advanced or metastatic cancer will experience pain. Among patients currently undergoing anticancer therapy, 59% report pain, and a third of patients continue to experience pain even after completing curative treatment. Among all patients with cancer in pain, it is estimated that pain in 68% of them is directly caused by the disease, 18% is treatment-related and the remaining 14% is due to other non-cancer-related reasons.4 The presence and severity of pain provide significant information regarding survival prognosis. Research5 indicates that patients with cancer pain experience a decline in overall quality of life, along with corresponding impairments in physical, cognitive, emotional and social functioning. Furthermore, inadequate pain control is associated with greater psychological distress, reduced social activities and diminished social support.6 Conversely, enhanced symptom monitoring and patient self-reporting of pain have been shown to improve health-related quality of life, reduce unexpected medical costs and increase adherence to antitumour therapy.7

Despite the long-standing promotion of the WHO analgesic ladder and various clinical guidelines, real-world cancer pain management remains suboptimal, characterised by widespread issues such as inadequate assessment, insufficient treatment and unsystematic follow-up.6 A systematic review revealed a 25% reduction in the neglect rate of cancer pain between 2007 and 2013.8 However, approximately 50% of patients with cancer pain still receive inadequate treatment.9 From 2007 to 2013, the undertreatment rate for cancer pain declined from 43.4% to 31.8% in European and American countries, while in Asian populations, it decreased from 59.1% to 45.2%.10 Studies report that 41.3%–52.9% of patients with cancer in China express dissatisfaction with analgesic efficacy or pain management, with undertreatment being particularly prominent among outpatient and home-based patients.11 A 2022 study12 investigated the prevalence of cancer pain among 533 patients with primary lung cancer across 18 hospitals in northern China, along with the pain management practices of 197 physicians. The results showed cancer pain prevalence of 45.4%, and among patients reporting pain in the past 24 hours, only 74.4% were taking analgesics. Another study10 surveyed 411 healthcare professionals from seven provincial cancer hospitals in China, revealing that cancer pain is highly prevalent among patients, with severe pain accounting for the majority, and that pain management remains inadequate. These findings highlight persistent shortcomings in standardised pain assessment, rational medication administration, adverse effect monitoring and healthcare professionals’ knowledge of cancer pain management. Consequently, effective pain management is of paramount importance. The traditional pain management model, which relies on intermittent outpatient visits, fails to capture the dynamic fluctuations of pain and treatment-related side effects, leading to delayed adjustments in analgesic regimens and prolonged periods of uncontrolled pain for patients.

Against this background, a multidimensional stratified management pathway based on dynamic patient-reported outcomes (PRO) has emerged. Currently, a growing body of research identifies PRO-based symptom management as the optimal ‘patient-centered’ healthcare model.13 PRO refers to reports directly provided by patients regarding their own health status, functional status and treatment experiences, without interpretation by healthcare professionals or other parties.14 Studies indicate15 that patients’ self-assessment of their health status may be more accurate than assessments conducted by healthcare providers. The application of professional symptom monitoring and alerts, followed by real-time symptom management by healthcare teams, can improve patient quality of life, prolong survival and enhance patient satisfaction.13 16 7 17

A randomised controlled trial (RCT) reported by Basch et al16 demonstrated that proactive symptom monitoring based on PROs improved symptom management in patients undergoing chemotherapy, resulting in survival benefits. Compared with the traditional reactive monitoring group, patients in the proactive monitoring group exhibited a median survival extension of 5.2 months (31.2 months vs 26.0 months, p=0.03). Dai et al17 evaluated PRO-based symptom management following lung cancer surgery and found lower symptom burden and fewer complications within 4 weeks after discharge compared with usual care. Long-term follow-up results further indicated that at 12 months postdischarge, the PRO group reported significantly fewer symptom threshold events than the usual care group. Additionally, between 1 month and 12 months after discharge, the PRO group consistently reported significantly lower composite scores for physical interference.18

However, there is currently limited research on symptom management specifically for patients with cancer pain. Furthermore, most previous PRO intervention studies have employed a ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach, applying uniform monitoring frequencies and alert thresholds to all patients without adequately accounting for the significant heterogeneity within the patient population with cancer pain. This model may result in overintervention for low-risk patients or insufficient management for high-risk patients, leading to inefficient utilisation of healthcare resources. Consequently, constructing a precise, stratified management pathway based on individual patient characteristics and dynamic PRO data—prioritising limited healthcare resources for those most in need and implementing differentiated intervention strategies—has emerged as a key scientific question in enhancing the effectiveness of cancer pain management.

We now propose an RCT aimed at evaluating the effectiveness of a PRO-based symptom monitoring, alert and response system in improving the quality of life of patients with cancer pain. This study seeks to reduce the pain burden among patients with cancer, enable real-time and convenient reporting of pain status in home settings, and provide professional health guidance from medical personnel. Consequently, patients will receive more personalised and precise treatment plans, leading to significant improvements in their quality of life. Additionally, the study aims to reduce the frequency of medical visits due to pain or pain-related complications, decrease the incidence of anxiety/depression and malnutrition, lower healthcare resource consumption, and enhance the utilisation efficiency of medical resources.

We will employ the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) for pain assessment, the Patient Health Questionnaire-4 (PHQ-4) for emotional screening, the Nutritional Risk Screening 2002 (NRS2002) for nutritional evaluation, and the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire-Core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30) to frequently monitor pain and its impact on the functioning of patients with cancer in pain. Monitoring will continue from enrolment until 3 months postenrolment, disease progression, achievement of a pain-free state or death, whichever occurs first.

Our research hypothesis is that patients in pain receiving PRO-based symptom management will achieve higher rates of effective pain control and improved quality of life compared with those receiving standard symptom management.

Methods and analysis

Research design

This study was designed as a single-centre, randomised, parallel-group, controlled and superiority trial. This protocol adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials.19 The outcomes of this trial will be reported in accordance with the guidelines of the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials.20 The flow chart of this experiment is shown in figure 1.

Setting

Participants will be recruited from Chengdu BOE Hospital, an institution that admits over 2000 patients with cancer annually.

Participant recruitment

Subject recruitment will be conducted by participating clinicians among outpatients or inpatients. Eligible patients must meet all inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria for subjects include: (1) A confirmed diagnosis of malignant tumour via histopathological examination; (2) Age between 18 years and 75 years; (3) A history of cancer pain with regular use of analgesic medications; (4) The ability and willingness to complete repetitive questionnaires via smartphone, tablet or telephone follow-up. Exclusion criteria include: (1) Visual, auditory or expressive impairments that prevent comprehension and cooperation with study requirements. To enhance patient retention and follow-up compliance, participants in the intervention group will receive free long-term medical consultations via WeChat, provided by the research team. This incentive was designed to offset the burden of daily PRO reporting and was approved by the institutional ethics committee. In contrast, patients in routine care typically pay out of pocket for follow-up consultations. The study is expected to run from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2027. Patient recruitment has not yet begun.

Sample size and statistical methods

This study uses a superiority trial design to examine the influence of PRO-based management on the efficacy rate of pain control in patients with cancer pain. The primary outcome is the efficacy rate of pain control after the intervention. Based on prior literature and expert insights, the projected efficacy rate in the control group is 45%. The non-inferiority margin (δ) is 10%, and the anticipated efficacy rate in the intervention group is 80%. With a one-sided α of 0.025, 80% power and a 1:1 allocation ratio, the sample size calculated using PASS 2025 software for a superiority design comparing two proportions is 52 participants per group. Considering an estimated 10% dropout rate, at least 58 participants are needed per group, leading to a total sample size of 116 participants.

All analyses shall adhere to the intention-to-treat principle. Statistical analyses will be conducted using SPSS V.31.0, and statistical significance will be determined based on p values and 97.5% CIs. For outcomes where higher values signify superior results (high-better outcomes), a statistically significant difference is deemed to exist if the lower limit of the CI for the difference is greater than 0. For outcomes where lower values denote better results (low-better outcomes), a statistically significant difference is considered to be present if the upper limit of the CI is <0 and the value of p is <0.025.

Continuous data conforming to a normal distribution will be presented as mean±SD (x±s), non-normally distributed continuous data as median and IQR (P25, P50, P75), and categorical data as frequency and percentage.

The Shapiro-Wilk test will be used to evaluate the normality of continuous variables. Baseline characteristics among groups will be compared by appropriately applying the χ2 test, Fisher’s exact test, independent samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U test.

For binary outcome measures, a generalised linear mixed model (GLMM) with a binomial distribution and an exchangeable correlation structure will be employed, with covariates (eg, baseline pain level) being adjusted. Effect estimates will be presented as risk difference along with a 95% CI. For count outcomes (eg, number of breakthrough pain episodes), GLMMs based on Poisson or negative binomial regression will be implemented, and the final model will be selected according to dispersion parameters and the Akaike Information Criterion. For continuous outcomes (eg, quality-of-life scores), linear mixed models will be used. Subgroup analyses will adopt the same analytical methods as the primary analysis.

Missing data will be managed using the full information maximum likelihood method. In the event that the missing rate surpasses 5%, multiple imputation will be applied as part of sensitivity analyses.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

Following the recruitment of subjects and their signing of the informed consent form, the randomisation procedure will be accomplished online via the central randomisation module of the WeChat mini-program. The data administrator will upload the randomisation allocation table to the platform and store it independently. Researchers will execute the allocation by clicking the randomisation button at the top of the WeChat mini-program, subsequently assigning subjects to either the intervention group or the control group in a 1:1 ratio. Each group will consist of 58 subjects.

Owing to the characteristics of the intervention, participants and the experts implementing the intervention cannot be blinded. Nevertheless, the data collectors assisting in the collection of PROs will be kept unaware of the group allocation to minimise measurement bias. The statisticians analysing the results will also be blinded to the group allocation.

Intervention

On enrolment, all patients will establish a connection with the WeChat accounts of participating specialists through a WeChat mini-program. Subsequently, patients will be instructed on the utilisation of the program. The ePRO questionnaire will be automatically transmitted to patients’ WeChat accounts subsequent to randomisation. Patients are obliged to complete the ePRO questionnaire on the day of enrolment (baseline period), on a daily basis thereafter (during hospitalisation or outpatient visits) and postdischarge in accordance with group requirements using smartphones or tablets. In a hospital environment, if patients fail to complete the ePRO questionnaire within the specified time frame, the system will dispatch an electronic reminder (e-reminder) and a maximum of two bedside reminders on the same day. Postdischarge, if patients still fail to complete the questionnaire within the specified time, the system will send an electronic reminder and a maximum of two telephone reminders within 24 hours.

Comparison

Intervention group

As part of the consent procedure, patients will be comprehensively informed that they are participating in the PRO-based management pathway. Nevertheless, to mitigate the Hawthorne effect, they will not be apprised of the specific symptom score thresholds that prompt different levels of intervention (eg, the numerical values for yellow vs red alerts). When a patient’s PRO score attains a preset threshold, the participating specialist will receive an alert via WeChat and will initiate appropriate symptom management strategies (eg, consultation, medication adjustment or outpatient follow-up). During hospitalisation, specialists will intervene proactively; postdischarge, they will contact the patient within 24 hours via the WeChat mini-program or telephone to implement symptom alleviation measures.

The symptom alleviation measures for patients in the intervention group will adhere to the latest guidelines and be standardised across all specialists, presented in the form of a standard operating procedure manual. Patients will be followed up after each intervention, primarily to inquire about their compliance. Patients who do not adhere to the specialists’ advice will be monitored, and the number of non-compliances will be recorded. If a patient refuses to adhere to the specialists’ advice more than three times, it will be regarded as a serious violation of the study protocol, and the patient will be excluded. Patients will be guided and permitted to seek medical assistance through regular channels to address severe symptoms.

Control group

Conventional outpatient management (on-demand follow-up+medication adjustment): All patients will sign informed consent forms and complete either paper-based or electronic questionnaires on the enrolment day to establish baseline data. These data will not trigger any alerts or responses associated with their symptoms. Symptom management for patients in the control group will adhere to the current standard pain management protocols. During hospitalisation, physicians will manage symptoms based on their clinical judgement rather than PRO scores. After discharge, standardised pain management follow-ups will be carried out through routine outpatient visits and telephone or online follow-ups at 1 week, 2 weeks and 1 month (or 1 week and 3 weeks) postdischarge. Patients are advised to seek medical assistance if severe symptoms are reported.

Withdrawal criteria

Participants meeting the following criteria shall be withdrawn from the study, and no further data collection shall be conducted: (1) The patient’s condition deteriorates further, manifested by symptoms such as confusion and impaired consciousness, rendering the completion of the questionnaire unfeasible; (2) The patient experiences refractory pain. After multidisciplinary team discussion and multiple courses of treatment, the analgesic effect is unsatisfactory, necessitating long-term hospitalisation (since patients with long-term hospitalisation (exceeding 14 days) generally have severe complications, and their compliance will gradually decrease, which may affect the accuracy of PRO data); (3) Participants seriously violate the study protocol (persistently failing to follow expert advice, deliberately having agents complete the PRO survey and deliberately providing false PRO data); (4) Participants request to withdraw from the study.

Outcomes and measurement

Primary outcome

The primary endpoint was the efficacy rate of pain management. For patients suffering from cancer-related pain, pain management serves as the most direct indicator influencing their quality of life.21 The calculation formula is as follows: efficacy rate of cancer pain therapy = (number of patients with effective pain alleviation after treatment / total number of patients receiving concurrent treatment) × 100%. In this metric, effective alleviation refers to meeting any of the following criteria after treatment: (1) Dynamic PROs indicate that the NRS scores for persistent pain are ≤3; (2) Dynamic PROs show a reduction of over 50% in persistent NRS scores; (3) A reduction of more than 50% in the analgesic dosage; (4) The frequency of breakthrough pain is reduced to fewer than three episodes per day; (5) Functional improvement (including enhanced motor and digestive functions).6 22 23 The evaluation time points were 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks and 12 weeks after the intervention.

Secondary endpoint: Frequency of breakthrough pain. NRS Score: 0 point indicates the absence of pain; 1–3 points represent mild pain, which does not interfere with sleep; 4–6 points signify moderate pain, exerting a certain influence on sleep; 7–10 points denote severe pain, significantly affecting the quality of sleep. In accordance with the recommendations of the National Comprehensive Cancer Network and published literature,24 25 when a patient’s symptom score is ≥4 points, it is classified as moderate-to-severe pain. A pain score of 4 or higher is reported as breakthrough pain.

The intervention rate of breakthrough pain refers to the proportion of breakthrough pain episodes that undergo corresponding analgesic management, which is calculated by dividing the number of interventions for breakthrough pain by the total number of breakthrough pain episodes.

The emergency department visit rate refers to the proportion of patients who visit the emergency department due to the exacerbation of pain or pain-related complications, calculated as the number of patients with emergency visits divided by the total number of patients.

The psychological symptom event rate is defined based on the PHQ-4 Scale, with a score >5 regarded as a positive event. It is calculated by dividing the number of patients experiencing psychological symptom events by the total number of patients.

The incidence of nutritional risk events is defined according to the NRS2002 screening tool, with a score of ≥3 regarded as a positive event. It is computed as the ratio of the number of patients experiencing nutritional risk events to the total number of patients.

The quality-of-life score is evaluated using the EORTC QLQ-C30, a multidimensional health-related quality-of-life questionnaire consisting of 30 items categorised into 15 domains. These domains encompass five functional subscales (physical, role, cognitive, emotional and social functioning), three symptom subscales (fatigue, pain, nausea and vomiting), six single-item measures (dyspnoea, insomnia, appetite loss, constipation, diarrhoea and financial difficulties) and one global health status subscale. The raw score (RS) for each domain is calculated by summing the scores of the included items and then dividing by the number of items: RS = (Q1+Q2 + … + Qx) / n. Standardised scores (SS) are subsequently derived as follows: For functional domains and global health domains: SS = (1 − (RS − 1) / R) × 100. For symptom domains: SS = ((RS − 1) / R) × 100. In functional domains and global health domains, higher scores signify better functioning, whereas in symptom domains, higher scores indicate greater symptom severity or a poorer health status. The EORTC QLQ-C30 has been translated and validated for application in Chinese populations.

Data collection, management and quality control

In this study, the WeChat mini-program serves as a specialised platform for the collection and management of research data. PRO data will be gathered via electronic questionnaires and documented within the mini-program. Participants are required to independently complete the electronic questionnaires. In the event that participants encounter challenges during the completion of the electronic questionnaire, data collectors or their family members can offer assistance by verbally reading each question and recording the participants’ responses. Specialist doctors will not have access to the PRO data of patients in the control group; they can only access the PRO data of patients in the intervention group. Other data, encompassing demographic characteristics, clinicopathological features and follow-up information, will also be inputted into the WeChat mini-program database.

Quality controllers will conduct regular data checks. Participants’ private information will not be recorded in the WeChat mini-program. Prior to patient enrolment, all researchers will undergo training on standard operating procedures.

Data analysis

To be incorporated into the analysis, participants are required to furnish baseline data and follow-up data from a minimum of two additional time points. In the event that a participant satisfies the withdrawal criteria, their data will not be included in the analysis.

Data monitoring and interim analysis

A Data Monitoring Committee shall be established, composed of one clinician, one statistician and members of the Ethics Committee of Chengdu BOE Hospital. The members of the Data Monitoring Committee will independently carry out regular study monitoring, separate from the researchers. Given that the content of this study presents a low-risk nature and has a short research duration, an interim analysis will not be performed.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the general public will not be engaged in the design, recruitment or implementation of this study. We will notify the applicants of the study findings. Given that providing study results to research participants is not a common practice in China, there are no arrangements to disseminate the results to the research participants. Participants will be informed that they can acquire the final results of this study via our future published articles.

Ethics and dissemination

This research was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chengdu BOE Hospital. All recruited patients are required to sign a written informed consent form. Any subsequent amendments to the protocol necessitate further review and approval. The research findings will be disseminated via peer-reviewed journals and academic conferences.

Discussion

This trial centres on the efficacy of pain management in patients with cancer. Adopting a prospective, RCT design, it directly tackles the fundamental challenges in contemporary cancer pain management, specifically, ‘discontinuous assessment, passive intervention, and a one - size - fits - all strategy’. The research innovatively combines dynamic PRO monitoring, a multidimensional risk prediction model and a stratified clinical management pathway. This integrated ‘monitoring - assessment - intervention’ proactive management model signifies a notable paradigm transformation from traditional reactive care to precise, preventive management, exhibiting considerable clinical and scientific innovation.

Potential implications of the study findings encompass the following aspects: (1) Ascertaining whether PRO-based symptom management exhibits superiority over conventional symptom management; (2) Assessing whether PRO-based symptom management can alleviate the pain burden and enhance the quality of life among patients with cancer; (3) Evaluating whether PRO-based symptom management can curtail healthcare resource consumption and augment resource utilisation efficiency; (4) Establishing a foundation for future research on whether symptom management in patients with chronic diseases can improve survival outcomes; (5) Offering valuable data resources for pain management research, thus promoting scientific development and advancement in the field of pain management.

This trial presents several limitations. First, as a single-centre study, despite its facilitation of quality control and implementation homogeneity, the distinctive characteristics of the study population, local medical culture and standard care practices may restrict the generalisability and external validity of the findings to healthcare institutions of varying levels and regions. Our institution being a tertiary hospital may confine the applicability of the results to non-tertiary settings, especially primary care or community hospitals. Future multicentre studies are necessary for validation.

Second, the inclusion and exclusion criteria for participant enrolment are relatively rigorous. For instance, the protocol stipulates that patients must be capable and willing to complete repeated questionnaires via smartphones, tablets or telephone follow-ups. This considerably restricts the study’s applicability to populations such as the elderly, individuals with lower educational attainment or those uncomfortable with technology, potentially introducing selection bias and diminishing the representativeness of the study sample for all patients with cancer pain.

Third, while strict screening criteria contribute to ensuring study adherence, withdrawal criteria may introduce selection bias and limit external validity. Future pragmatic clinical trials (PCTs) will be carried out to assess the effectiveness of the monitoring system in more diverse populations, thereby enhancing generalisability.

Fourth, the absence of blinding for both participants and the specialist physicians implementing the interventions represents a limitation, as it may heighten the risk of measurement bias.

Fifth, although patient involvement in the trial design and development was limited, which may impact feasibility assessment, prior research and our ongoing observational studies have furnished preliminary data supporting the feasibility and acceptability of the trial design. This RCT aims to validate the effectiveness of the PRO monitoring system, while subsequent PCTs will incorporate patient engagement in study design, implementation and interpretation to further evaluate its effectiveness.

Sixth, the follow-up duration is relatively brief. The long-term effects of the intervention on sustained pain control stability, quality of life, healthcare resource utilisation, as well as the sustainability and cost-effectiveness of this management model in routine clinical practice, are outside the scope of this study and require further investigation in future research.

Seventh, while formal patient and public involvement (PPI) was not required in this study, we recognise its potential value. Future research should contemplate integrating structured PPI from the design phase to further improve patient-centred outcomes.

In summary, as an RCT, this study endeavours to establish and verify the multidimensional stratified management pathway of the PRO monitoring system in the management of patients with cancer pain. Simultaneously, it offers feasible data support for subsequent implementation in real-world scenarios. This study can furnish more medical access avenues for the subsequent enrolment of patients from community hospitals and those with low socioeconomic status.

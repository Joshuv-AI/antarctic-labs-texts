Quadratic Embedding Constants of Cartesian Products and Joins of Graphs
Projesh Nath Choudhury, Raju Nandi

Abstract
. The quadratic embedding constant (QEC) of a finite, simple, connected graph originated from the classical work of Schoenberg [ Ann. of Math. , 1935] and [ Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. , 1938] on Euclidean distance geometry. In this article, we study the QEC of graphs in terms of two graph operations: the Cartesian product and the join of graphs. We derive a general formula for the QEC of the join of an arbitrary graph with a regular graph and with a complete multipartite graph. We then provide quadratic embedding constants for the Cartesian product of an arbitrary graph G G with a complete graph and with a complete bipartite graph in terms of QEC ⁡ ( G ) \qec(G) .

Introduction

All graphs in this paper are assumed to be finite, simple, and unweighted. Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, we define
C(V):
the space of all
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions on
V
,
e:
the constant function in
C(V)
taking value
1
,
J:
matrix with all entries one and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:
the canonical inner product on
C(V)\cong\mathbb{R}^{|V|}
,
A_{G}
: the adjacency matrix of

whose
(v,w)

is adjacent to
w
, and 0 otherwise, for all
v,w\in V
. If
G
is connected, we define
D_{G}
: the distance matrix of

(v,w)

, and
(D_{G})_{vv}=0\ \forall v\in V

.

The goal of this article is to study the quadratic embedding constant of a connected graph
G
using the graph operations, the Cartesian product and the join of graphs. A connected graph
G=(V,E)
is quadratically embeddable into a Euclidean space
\mathcal{H}
if there exists a map
\psi:V\to\mathcal{H}
such that

for all
i,j\in V
, where
\parallel\cdot\parallel
denotes the norm of
\mathcal{H}
. Such a map

from

is called a

of
G
and if

admits a quadratic embedding, we say that
G
is of
QE class
. Graphs of QE class have numerous applications in several branches of mathematics, including quantum probability and non-commutative harmonic analysis
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
15
,
16
]
. In 1935, Schoenberg
[

gave a striking characterization of the QE class in terms of the conditional negative definiteness. A symmetric matrix

is said to be conditionally negative definite if

for all

is of QE class if and only if

is conditionally negative definite. Inspired by this result, Obata–Zakiyyah

17

introduced the notion of

of

, denoted and defined as follows.

\qec(G):=\max\{\langle f,D_{G}f\rangle;\ f\in C(V),\ \langle f,f\rangle=1,\ \langle e,f\rangle=0\}.

In particular, Schoenberg’s result states that a connected graph

. Since then, the QEC of graphs has been studied by several authors
[

,

. For instance, the quadratic embedding constants of some well-known classes of graphs, including the complete graph, cycle, path, and complete multipartite graph are studied in

,
17
]
. In
[
4
]
, we provided tight lower and upper bounds for the QEC of a tree, and showed that it lies between a path and a star for all trees.

Determining the quadratic embedding constant for a more general class of graphs is a challenging problem. A well-studied question involving the QEC asks to study the QEC of a connected graph
G
by factorizing it into smaller graphs using a graph operation. In the literature, QEC of graphs is explored using several graph operations, including Cartesian product
[
17
]
, star product
[

(u_{i},v_{p})
and
(u_{j},v_{q})
in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
gives us a path between
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
in
G_{1}
, and a path between
v_{p}
and
v_{q}
in
G_{2}
. This implies

(3.1)

d_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}((u_{i},v_{p}),(u_{j},v_{q}))=d_{G_{1}}(u_{i},u_{j})+d_{G_{2}}(v_{p},v_{q}).

Thus the
ij
-th block of
D_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}
formed by rows
M_{i}
and columns
M_{j}
is
d_{G_{1}}(u_{i},u_{j})\ J+D_{G_{2}}
, and

D_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{G_{2}}&D_{G_{2}}+d_{G_{1}}(u_{1},u_{2})J&\ldots&D_{G_{2}}+d_{G_{1}}(u_{1},u_{m})J\\
D_{G_{2}}+d_{G_{1}}(u_{2},u_{1})J&D_{G_{2}}&\ldots&D_{G_{2}}+d_{G_{1}}(u_{2},u_{m})J\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
D_{G_{2}}+d_{G_{1}}(u_{m},u_{1})J&D_{G_{2}}+d_{G_{1}}(u_{m},u_{2})J&\ldots&D_{G_{2}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The first result of this section gives us a relation between
\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})
and
\qec(G_{1}),\qec(G_{2})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two connected graphs. Then
\qec(G_{1})\leq\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})
and
\qec(G_{2})\leq\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})
.

Proof.

Note that the subgraph induced by the vertices
\{(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{1},v_{2}),\ldots,(u_{1},v_{n})\}
is the graph
G_{2}
and using
(
3.1
),
d_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}((u_{1},v_{p}),(u_{1},v_{q}))=d_{G_{2}}(v_{p},v_{q})
. Thus
G_{2}
is isometrically embedded in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
. By
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
,
\qec(G_{2})\leq\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})
. Similarly one can show that
G_{1}
is isometrically embedded in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
and
\qec(G_{1})\leq\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})
.
∎

We now recall an interesting result that gives us the quadratic embedding constant of the Cartesian product of two arbitrary graphs of QE class.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
17
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two non-trivial graphs of QE class. Then,
\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})=0
.

As an immediate consequence of the above two results, we conclude the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two connected graphs. Then
G_{1}\times G_{2}
is of QE class if and only if both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are of QE class.

Proof.

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are of QE class, then by Theorem
3.4
,
G_{1}\times G_{2}
is of QE class. To prove the converse, let either
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
be of the non-QE class. By Proposition
3.3
,
0<\qec(G_{1}\times G_{2})
, a contradiction since
G_{1}\times G_{2}
is a graph of the QE class. Thus
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are of QE class.
∎

For a graph
G
of QE class, by Theorem
3.4
,
\qec(K_{m}\times G)=0
. We now obtain a formula of
\qec(K_{m}\times G)
for an arbitrary connected graph
G
of non-QE class.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
V
be the vertex set of
K_{m}\times G
. Note that

D_{K_{m}\times G}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{G}&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J\\
D_{G}+J&D_{G}&\ldots&D_{G}+J\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{S}(D_{K_{m}\times G})
be the set of all
(f,\lambda,\mu)\in(C(V)\cong\mathbb{R}^{mn})\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle(D_{K_{m}\times G}-\lambda I)f

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{2}\ e,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=1,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle e,f\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

By Proposition
2.2
,
\qec(K_{m}\times G)=\max\{\lambda:(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}(D_{K_{m}\times G})\}
. Suppose
f=({x^{1}}^{T},{x^{2}}^{T},\ldots,{x^{m}}^{T})^{T}
, where
x^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
i\in[m]
. Then, the equation (
3.2
) gives us a system of
m
equations. Subtracting the
(i+1)
-th equation from the
i
-th equation, we get

(3.5)

(J+\lambda I)(x^{i}-x^{i+1})=0,\ \ \forall\ \ i\in[m-1].

Since
G
is a connected graph of non-QE class, by Proposition
3.3
, it is enough to consider
0<\lambda
. From (
3.5
), we have
x^{i}=x^{i+1}
for all
i\in[m-1]
and equation (
3.4
) gives us
\langle e,x^{1}\rangle=0
. Thus the equations (
3.2
)-(
3.4
) reduces to

(3.6)

\displaystyle(mD_{G}-\lambda I)x^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{2}\ e,

(3.7)

\displaystyle m\langle x^{1},x^{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=1,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\langle e,x^{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Notice that
\qec(G)=\max\{a:(x,a,\eta)\in\mathcal{S}(D_{G})\}
, where
\mathcal{S}(D_{G})
is the set of all stationary points
(x,a,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(3.9)

(D_{G}-aI)x=\frac{\eta}{2}\ e,\ \ \ \langle x,x\rangle=1,\ \ \ \langle e,x\rangle=0.

From equations (
3.6
)-(
3.9
), the following relations

\lambda=ma,\ \ \ x^{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\ x,\ \ \ \mu=\sqrt{m}\ \eta,

gives us a one to one correspondence between
\mathcal{S}(D_{K_{m}\times G})
and
\mathcal{S}(D_{G})
. Thus

\displaystyle\qec(K_{m}\times G)

\displaystyle=\max\{\lambda:(({x^{1}}^{T},{x^{2}}^{T},\ldots,{x^{m}}^{T})^{T},\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}(D_{K_{m}\times G})\}

\displaystyle=\max\{ma:(x,a,\eta)\in\mathcal{S}(D_{G})\}

\displaystyle=m\qec(G).

∎

We next derive the quadratic embedding constant of the Cartesian product of
K_{m,n}
and an arbitrary connected graph
G
. To proceed, we require a preliminary result for the quadratic embedding constant of a complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

[
17
, Theorem 2.8]

Let
K_{m,n}
be a complete bipartite graph on
(m+n)
vertices. Then

\qec(K_{m,n})=\frac{m(n-2)+n(m-2)}{m+n}
for all
m\geq 1
,
n\geq 1
.

Moreover,
K_{m,n}
is of QE class if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

Either
m=1
or
n=1
.

(ii)

m=n=2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
l
vertices and
K_{m,n}
be a complete bipartite graph on
m+n
vertices. Without loss of generality, assume that
m\geq n
. For
m=n=1
,
K_{1,1}=K_{2}
and by Theorem
1.3
,
\qec(K_{1,1}\times G)=2\qec(G)
for any connected graph
G
of non-QE class. Let
m>1
. Then, all possibilities of
m
and
n
can be expressed in three disjoint sets:
(a)\ m>1,n=1
,
(b)\ m=n=2
and
(c)\ m>2,n\geq 2
. Note that the distance matrix of
K_{m,n}\times G
is

D_{K_{m,n}\times G}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{G}&D_{G}+2J&\ldots&D_{G}+2J&D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J\\
D_{G}+2J&D_{G}&\ldots&D_{G}+2J&D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots\\
D_{G}+2J&D_{G}+2J&\ldots&D_{G}&D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J\\
D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J&D_{G}&D_{G}+2J&\ldots&D_{G}+2J\\
D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J&D_{G}+2J&D_{G}&\ldots&D_{G}+2J\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
D_{G}+J&D_{G}+J&\ldots&D_{G}+J&D_{G}+2J&D_{G}+2J&\ldots&D_{G}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Now,
\qec(K_{m,n}\times G)=\max\{\lambda:(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}(D_{K_{m,n}\times G})\}
, where
\mathcal{S}(D_{K_{m,n}\times G})
is the set of all stationary points
(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+n)l}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(3.10)

\displaystyle(D_{K_{m,n}\times G}-\lambda I)f

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{2}\ e,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\langle f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=1,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\langle e,f\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Notice that the equation (
3.10
) gives us a system of
m+n
equations. Suppose that
f=({x^{1}}^{T},{x^{2}}^{T},\ldots,{x^{m+n}}^{T})^{T}
, where
x^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
for all
i\in[m+n]
. Then, subtracting the
(i+1)
-th equation from the
i
-th equation, we get the following relations for different cases.

Case I.
For
m=n=2
and
m>2,n\geq 2
,

(3.13)

(J+\frac{\lambda}{2}I)(x^{i}-x^{i+1})=0,\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\ \&\ \ m+1\leq i\leq m+n-1

and subtracting the
(m+1)
-th equation from the
m
-th equation, we have

(3.14)

\sum_{p=1}^{m-1}Jx^{p}-Jx^{m}+Jx^{m+1}-\sum_{q=m+2}^{m+n}Jx^{q}+\lambda(x^{m+1}-x^{m})=0.

Case II.
For
m>1,n=1
,

(3.15)

(J+\frac{\lambda}{2}I)(x^{i}-x^{i+1})=0,\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq m-1

and subtracting the
(m+1)
-th equation from the
m
-th equation, we have

(3.16)

\sum_{p=1}^{m-1}Jx^{p}-Jx^{m}+Jx^{m+1}+\lambda(x^{m+1}-x^{m})=0.

We are interested in the Cartesian product
K_{m,n}\times G
where either
K_{m,n}
or
G
is of non-QE class. Otherwise, the quadratic embedding constant is zero by Theorem
3.4
. Thus, it suffices to consider
\lambda>0
, and from (
3.13
), (
3.15
),

(3.17)

x^{i}=x^{i+1},\forall\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\ \&\ \ m+1\leq i\leq m+n-1

and

(3.18)

x^{i}=x^{i+1},\forall\ 1\leq i\leq m-1,

respectively. Also, recall that
\qec(G)=\max\{a:(x,a,\eta)\in\mathcal{S}(D_{G})\}
, where
\mathcal{S}(D_{G})
is the set of all stationary points
(x,a,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(3.19)

(D_{G}-aI)x=\frac{\eta}{2}\ e,\ \ \ \langle x,x\rangle=1,\ \ \ \langle e,x\rangle=0.

Keeping these relations in hand, we now prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
(i).

Without loss of generality assume that
m\geq n
. By Theorem
3.6
,

m=n=2
or
m\geq 1,n=1

Case I.

: From (

) and (

(3.20)

(4D_{G}-\lambda I)x^{1}=\frac{\mu}{2}\ e,\ \ \ 4\langle x^{1},x^{1}\rangle=1,\ \ \ \langle e,x^{1}\rangle=0.

Using (
3.19

\displaystyle\qec(K_{2,2}\times G)

(m-2)Jx^{m}+Jx^{m+1}+\lambda(x^{m+1}-x^{m})=0.

\displaystyle\qec(K_{m,1}\times G)

(ii).

By (

x^{i}=x^{i+1}

(m-2)Jx^{m}-(n-2)Jx^{m+1}+\lambda(x^{m+1}-x^{m})=0.

Case I.

\displaystyle\qec(K_{m,n}\times G)

∎

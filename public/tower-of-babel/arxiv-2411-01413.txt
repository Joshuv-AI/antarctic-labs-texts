Kupershmidt operators on 3 3 -BiHom-Poisson color algebras and 3 3 -BiHom-pre-Poisson color algebras structures
Othmen Ncib, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to introduce the class of noncommutative 3 3 -BiHom-Poisson color algebras, which is a combination of 3 3 -BiHom-Lie color algebras and BiHom-associative color algebras under a compatibility condition, called BiHom-Leibniz color identity, and then study their representations and associated Kupershmidt operators. In addition, we introduce the notion of noncommutative 3 3 -BiHom-pre-Poisson color algebras and investigate the relationship between noncommutative 3 3 -BiHom-Poisson color algebras and 3 3 -BiHom-pre-Poisson color algebras via Kupershmidt operators.

3
-BiHom-pre-Poisson color algebra, representation, Kupershmidt operator.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abelian group.

1.

A linear space
V
is said to be
\Gamma
-graded if, there exists a family
(V_{a})_{a\in\Gamma}
of linear subspaces of
V
such that

V=\bigoplus\limits_{a\in\Gamma}V_{a}.

2.

An element
x\in V
is considered homogeneous of degree
a\in\Gamma
if
x\in V_{a}
. We denote
\mathcal{H}(V)
the set of all homogeneous elements in
V
.

3.

Let
V=\bigoplus\limits_{a\in\Gamma}V_{a}
and
V^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{a\in\Gamma}V^{\prime}_{a}
be two
\Gamma
-graded linear spaces. A Linear mapping
f:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is said to be homogeneous of degree
b\in\Gamma
if for all
a\in\Gamma
,

f(V_{a})\subseteq V^{\prime}_{a+b}.

If a linear mapping
f
is homogeneous of degree zero, that is
f(V_{a})\subseteq V^{\prime}_{a}
for any
a\in\Gamma
, then it is sometimes called an even mapping (as for
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading case).

4.

An algebra
(A,\cdot)
is said to be
\Gamma
-graded if its underlying linear space is
\Gamma
-graded,
A=\bigoplus\limits_{a\in\Gamma}A_{a}
, and if furthermore for all
a,b\in\Gamma
,

A_{a}\cdot A_{b}\subseteq A_{a+b}.

(2.1)

A morphism
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}

of
\Gamma
-graded algebras
A
and
A^{\prime}

is by definition a morphism of algebras which is even, that is homogeneous mapping of degree zero.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abelian group. A map
\epsilon:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\bf\mathbb{K}^{*}=\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}}
is called commutation factor (or a skew-symmetric bicharacter) on
\Gamma
if, for all
a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\epsilon(a,a^{\prime})\epsilon(a^{\prime},a)=1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\epsilon(a,a^{\prime}+a^{\prime\prime})=\epsilon(a,a^{\prime})\epsilon(a,a^{\prime\prime}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\epsilon(a+a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})=\epsilon(a,a^{\prime\prime})\epsilon(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}).

(2.4)

In particular, we can observe that
\epsilon(a,0)=\epsilon(0,a)=1
and
\epsilon(a,a)=\pm 1
for all
a\in\Gamma
. If
x
and
y
are two homogeneous elements of degree
a
and
b
respectively and
\epsilon
is a skew-symmetric bicharacter, then the notation
\epsilon(x,y)
is often used instead of
\epsilon(a,b)
.

Example 2.1
.

Standard examples of skew-symmetric bicharacters are

1)

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2},\quad\varepsilon(i,j)=(-1)^{ij}
,

2)

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}=\{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\mid\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}
,

\varepsilon((\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}))=(-1)^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}
,

3)

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\quad\varepsilon((i_{1},i_{2}),(j_{1},j_{2}))=(-1)^{i_{1}j_{2}-i_{2}j_{1}}
,

4)

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
,
\epsilon(m,n)=\cos(mn\pi)=(-1)^{mn}
,

5)

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\quad\varepsilon((i_{1},i_{2}),(j_{1},j_{2}))=(-1)^{(i_{1}+i_{2})(j_{1}+j_{2})}
,

6)

\Gamma=\{-1,+1\},\quad\varepsilon(i,j)=(-1)^{(i-1)(j-1)/{4}}
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
9
]
)
.

(Multiplicative)
BiHom-associative color algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
consist of a
\Gamma
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
, two even commuting linear maps
\alpha,\beta:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, an even bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and a skew-symmetric bicharacter
\epsilon:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\bf\mathbb{K}^{*}}
such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
,

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\alpha(\mu(x,y)),\quad\mu(\beta(x),\beta(y))=\beta(\mu(x,y))

\displaystyle\quad\text{multiplicativity},

(2.5)

\displaystyle ass_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(y,z))-\mu(\mu(x,y),\beta(z))=0

\displaystyle\quad\epsilon\text{-BiHom-associativity}.

(2.6)

Definition 2.4
.

If the multiplicativity conditions (
2.5
) in
Definition
2.3
are not required, that is, if
\alpha
and
\beta
are just commuting linear maps, then we call this algebraic structure
possibly non-multiplicative BiHom-associative color algebra
, and if the multiplicativity (
2.5
) is not satisfied (or satisfied), we refer to it as non-multiplicative (or multiplicative, respectively). If linear mappings

\alpha
and
\beta
are not commuting, then we call it a BiHom-associative color algebra with non-commuting twisting maps.

A morphism
f:(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mu_{1},\epsilon,\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\to(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mu_{2},\epsilon,\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})
of the possibly non-multiplicative BiHom-associative color algebras is an even linear map
f:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
such that
\alpha_{2}\circ f=f\circ\alpha_{1},\;\beta_{2}\circ f=f\circ\beta_{1}
and
f\circ\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}\circ(f\otimes f)
.

A possibly non-multiplicative BiHom-associative color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
is said to be BiHom-commutative if, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
,

\mu(\beta(x),\alpha(y))=\epsilon(x,y)\mu(\beta(y),\alpha(x)).

(2.7)

Remark 2.1
.

If
\alpha=\beta
, possibly non-multiplicative BiHom-associative color algebras reduce to general Hom-associative color algebras and multiplicative Hom-associative color algebras when
\alpha=\beta
is moreover multiplicative. For
\alpha=\beta=Id_{\mathfrak{g}}
, one recovers the associative color algebras.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-associative color algebra. A left representation of
\mathfrak{g}
(also called left
\mathfrak{g}
-module) is a quadruple
(V,\mathfrak{l},\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})

\Gamma

V=\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}

\mathfrak{l}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes V\to V,\;x\otimes u\mapsto x\cdot u=\mathfrak{l}(x)u

\alpha_{V},\beta_{V}:V\to V

\displaystyle\alpha_{V}\circ\beta_{V}

\displaystyle=\beta_{V}\circ\alpha_{V},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\alpha_{V}(\mathfrak{l}(x\otimes u))

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\cdot\alpha_{V}(u),

(2.10)

\displaystyle\beta_{V}(\mathfrak{l}(x\otimes u))

\displaystyle=\beta(x)\cdot\beta_{V}(u).

(2.11)

A right representation
(V,\mathfrak{r},\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})
can be defined in a similar manner.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(\alpha(x))\mathfrak{l}(y)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(\mu(x,y))(\beta_{V}(v))

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(\alpha(x))\mathfrak{r}(y)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(x,y)\mathfrak{r}(\beta(y))\mathfrak{l}(x)(v)

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(\mu(x,y))\alpha_{V}(v)

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r},\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})
be a bimodule of a BiHom-associative color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
. Then, the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
of vector spaces
\mathfrak{g}
and
V
is turned into a BiHom-associative color algebra by defining the multiplications in
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V

and

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}((x+u),(y+v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)+\mathfrak{l}(x)v+\epsilon(y,u)\mathfrak{r}(y)u,

\displaystyle(\alpha\oplus\alpha_{V})(x+u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(x)+\alpha_{V}(u),

(2.16)

\displaystyle(\beta\oplus\beta_{V})(x+u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(x)+\beta_{V}(u).

This BiHom-associative color algebra, denoted by

Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(\alpha(x))\mathfrak{l}(y)w,

\displaystyle\epsilon(x+y,z)\mathfrak{r}(\beta(z))\mathfrak{l}(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(z,v)\mathfrak{l}(\alpha(x))\mathfrak{r}(z)v,

\displaystyle=

(2.20)

Definition 2.7
.

A
Kupershmidt operator
on a BiHom-associative color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
with respect to a bimodule
(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r},\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})
is defined as an even linear map
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying, for all
u,v\in\mathcal{H}(V)
,

\displaystyle T\circ\alpha_{V}=\alpha\circ T,\quad T\circ\beta_{V}=\beta\circ T,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mu(T(u),T(v))=T\Big(\mathfrak{l}(T(u))v+\epsilon(u,v)\mathfrak{r}(T(v))u\Big).

(2.22)

Remark 2.2
.

A
Rota-Baxter operator

\mathcal{R}
of weight zero on a BiHom-associative color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
is just a Kupershmidt operator associated with the bimodule
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{R},\alpha,\beta)
where
\mathfrak{L}
and
\mathfrak{R}
are the left and right multiplication operators corresponding to the multiplication
\mu
, that is
\mathcal{R}
commuting with
\alpha
and
\beta
and satisfying, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
,

\mu(\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y))=\mathcal{R}\Big(\mu(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+\mu(x,\mathcal{R}(y)\Big).

(2.23)

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a BiHom associative color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
. Then, there exists a BiHom associative color algebra structure
\mu_{\mathcal{R}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
given for any
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\mu_{\mathcal{R}}(x,y)=\mu(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+\mu(x,\mathcal{R}(y)).

(2.24)

Proof.

For
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
,

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha(x),\mu_{\mathcal{R}}(y,z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{R}}\big(\alpha(x),\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),z\big)+\mu\big(y,\mathcal{R}(z))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{R}}\big(\alpha(x),\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),z)\big)+\mu_{\mathcal{R}}\big(\alpha(x),\mu(y,\mathcal{R}(z))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\big(\mathcal{R}(\alpha(x)),\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),z)\big)+\underbrace{\mu\big(\alpha(x),\mathcal{R}(\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),z))\big)}_{a_{1}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\big(\mathcal{R}(\alpha(x)),\mu(y,\mathcal{R}(z))\big)+\underbrace{\mu\big(\alpha(x),\mathcal{R}(\mu(y,\mathcal{R}(z)))\big)}_{a_{2}}.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on
\mathfrak{g}
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{1}+a_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),\mathcal{R}(z))),\text{which implies that}

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{R}}\Big(\alpha(x),\mu_{\mathcal{R}}(y,z)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\mathcal{R}(\alpha(x)),\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),z)\Big)+\mu\Big(\mathcal{R}(\alpha(x)),\mu(y,\mathcal{R}(z))\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\alpha(x),\mu(\mathcal{R}(y),\mathcal{R}(z))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\mu(\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y)),\beta(z)\Big)+\mu\Big(\mu(\mathcal{R}(x),y),\beta(\mathcal{R}(z))\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\mu(x,\mathcal{R}(y)),\beta(\mathcal{R}(z))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\mathcal{R}(\mu_{\mathcal{R}}(x,y)),\beta(z)\Big)+\mu\Big(\mu_{\mathcal{R}}(x,y),\mathcal{R}(\beta(z))\Big)

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\mu,\epsilon,\alpha,\beta)
be a noncommutative
3
-BiHom-Poisson color algebra and
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be a Kupershmidt operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to representation
(V,\rho,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r},\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})
. Then, the operations

\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{V},\;\prec_{V}
and
\succ_{V}
define a noncommutative
3
-BiHom-pre-Poisson color algebra structure on
V
, where
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{V}
is given by Theorem
4.3
and
\prec_{V},\;\succ_{V}
are given by Theorem
4.7
.

Proof.

By using Theorems
4.3
and
4.7
, we deduce that
(V,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{V},\epsilon,\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})
is a
3
-BiHom-pre-Lie color algebra and
(V,\prec_{V},\succ_{V},\epsilon,\alpha_{V},\beta_{V})
is a BiHom-dendriform color algebra. It remains to show that conditions (
4.12
)-(
4.14
) are satisfied.
For any
x,y,z,t\in\mathcal{H}(V)
, by using the definitions of

\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{V}
and
\prec_{V}
and the fact that
T
is Kupershmidt of the
3
-BiHom-Poisson color algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\{\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y),z\prec_{V}t\}_{V}-\{\beta_{V}(x),\beta_{V}(y),z\}_{V}\prec_{V}\beta_{V}(t)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(z,t)\{\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y),\mathfrak{r}(T(t))z\}_{V}-\epsilon(t,x+y+z)\mathfrak{r}(T(\beta_{V}(t)))\{\beta_{V}(x),\beta_{V}(y),z\}_{V}

\displaystyle=\epsilon(z,t)\rho(\alpha\beta(T(x)),\alpha\beta(T(y)))\mathfrak{r}(T(t))z-\epsilon(t,x+y+z)\mathfrak{r}(\beta(T(t)))\rho(\beta(T(x)),\beta(T(y)))z

\displaystyle=\epsilon(t,x+y+z)\mathfrak{r}(\beta(T(t)))\rho(\beta(T(x)),\beta(T(y)))z

\displaystyle\quad-\epsilon(t,x+y+z)\mathfrak{r}(\beta(T(t)))\rho(\beta(T(x)),\beta(T(y)))z+\epsilon(z,t)\mathfrak{r}(\{\alpha(T(x)),\alpha(T(y)),T(t)\})\beta_{V}(z)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(z,t)\mathfrak{r}(\{T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}(y)),T(t)\})\beta_{V}(z)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(z,t)\mathfrak{r}\Big(T\Big(\rho(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}(y)))t-\epsilon(y,t)\rho(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}^{-1}\beta_{V}(t)))\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}^{-1}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x,y+t)\rho(T(\alpha_{V}(y)),T(\alpha_{V}^{-1}\beta_{V}(t)))\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}^{-1}(x)\Big)\Big)\beta_{V}(z)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(x+y,z)\beta_{V}(z)\prec_{V}\rho(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}(y)))t

\displaystyle\quad-\epsilon(x+y,z)\epsilon(y,t)\beta_{V}(z)\prec_{V}\rho(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}^{-1}\beta_{V}(t)))\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}^{-1}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x,y+z+t)\epsilon(x,t)\epsilon(z,t)\beta_{V}(z)\prec_{V}\rho(T(\alpha_{V}(y)),T(\alpha_{V}^{-1}\beta_{V}(t)))\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(z,x+y)\beta_{V}(z)\prec_{V}\Big(\{\alpha_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}(y),t\}_{V}-\epsilon(y,t)\{\alpha_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}^{-1}\beta_{V}(t),\alpha^{2}\beta_{V}^{-1}(y)\}_{V}

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x,y+t)\{\alpha_{V}(y),\alpha_{V}^{-1}\beta_{V}(t),\alpha^{2}\beta_{V}^{-1}(x)\}_{V}\Big),

then, the condition (
4.12
) is satisfied. We have also

\displaystyle\{\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}(y),\alpha_{V}(z)\succ_{V}t\}_{V}-\epsilon(x+y,z)\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(z)\succ_{V}\{\alpha_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}^{2}(y),t\}_{V}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}(y),\mathfrak{l}(T\alpha_{V}(z))t\}_{V}\epsilon(x+y,z)\mathfrak{l}(T\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(z))\{\alpha_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}^{2}(y),t\}_{V}

\displaystyle=\rho\big(T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}^{2}\beta_{V}(y))\big)\mathfrak{l}(T(\alpha_{V}(z)))t

\displaystyle\quad-\epsilon(x+y,z)\mathfrak{l}(T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(z)))\rho\big(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}^{2}(y))\big)t

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}\big(\{T(\beta_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y)),T(\alpha_{V}(z))\}\big)\beta_{V}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x+y,z)\mathfrak{l}(T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(z)))\rho\big(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}^{2}(y))\big)t

\displaystyle\quad-\epsilon(x+y,z)\mathfrak{l}(T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(z)))\rho\big(T(\alpha_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}^{2}(y))\big)t

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}\big(\{T(\beta_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y)),T(\alpha_{V}(z))\}\big)\beta_{V}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}\Big(T\big(\rho(T(\beta_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y)))\alpha_{V}(z)-\epsilon(y,z)\rho(T(\beta_{V}(x)),T(\beta_{V}(z)))\alpha_{V}^{2}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x,y+z)\rho(T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y)),T(\beta_{V}(z)))\alpha_{V}(x)\big)\Big)\beta_{V}(t)

\displaystyle=\Big(\rho(T(\beta_{V}(x)),T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y)))\alpha_{V}(z)-\epsilon(y,z)\rho(T(\beta_{V}(x)),T(\beta_{V}(z)))\alpha_{V}^{2}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x,y+z)\rho(T(\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y)),T(\beta_{V}(z)))\alpha_{V}(x)\Big)\succ_{V}\beta_{V}(t)

\displaystyle=\Big(\{\beta_{V}(x),\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y),\alpha_{V}(z)\}_{V}-\epsilon(y,z)\{\beta_{V}(x),\beta_{V}(z),\alpha_{V}^{2}(y)\}

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon(x,y+z)\{\alpha_{V}\beta_{V}(y),\beta_{V}(z),\alpha_{V}(x)\}\Big)\succ_{V}\beta_{V}(t),

which shows that the condition (
4.13
) is satisfied. The same direct computation gives the condition (
4.14
). The Theorem is proved.
∎

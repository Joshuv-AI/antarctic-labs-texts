Finiteness of heights in isogeny classes of motives with semistable reduction
Alice Lin

Abstract
. Using integral p-adic Hodge theory, Kato and Koshikawa define a generalization of the Faltings height of an abelian variety to motives defined over a number field. Assuming the adelic Mumford-Tate conjecture, we prove a finiteness property for heights in the isogeny class of a motive, where the isogenous motives are not required to be defined over the same number field. This expands on a result of Kisin and Mocz for the Faltings height in isogeny classes of abelian varieties.



1.
Introduction

Heights are a useful tool to prove finiteness theorems in arithmetic geometry over global fields, as they provide a method of measuring the size of a given arithmetic object defined over a global field in terms of the various absolute values of its defining coordinates. For example, in proving the Mordell conjecture, Faltings introduces and utilizes a height
h_{\Fal}(A)
for any abelian variety
A
defined over a number field
[
Fal83
,
Fal86
]
. One major input is the Shafarevich conjecture for abelian varieties over number fields: for any number field
F
and finite set
S
of finite places of
F
, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties of a fixed dimension defined over
F
with good reduction outside of
S
. In particular, Faltings shows that the number of abelian varieties defined over
K
in a fixed isogeny class is finite
[
Fal86
, proof of Theorem 6]
.

We use the following to denote the isogeny class over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of an abelian variety
A
defined over a number field.

\mathscr{I}(A)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}:=\{B_{/F^{\prime}}\mid\text{$\exists$ an isogeny $A\to B$ defined over some field $F^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$}\}.

This set contains infinitely many isomorphism classes.
However, explicit computations in the case of elliptic curves suggest the following conjecture, formulated by Mocz: For any
c>0
the geometric isogeny class of a given abelian variety contains only finitely many isomorphism classes of Faltings height less than
c
. This is true in the case of CM elliptic curves, where one has a formula for
h_{\Fal}(E)
due to Deligne
[
Del85
]
and extended by Nakkajima–Taguchi
[
NT91
]
. Szpiro and Ullmo prove the case for non-CM elliptic curves using Arakelov intersection theory
[
SU99
]
. In
[
Moc18
]
, Mocz considers the case of CM abelian varieties using integral
p
-adic Hodge theory.

Kisin and Mocz prove the following result for abelian varieties satisfying the Mumford–Tate conjecture.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
KM
]
)
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field. Assume that the Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for
A
. Then for any
c>0
, the set

\{B\in\mathscr{I}(A)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid h_{\Fal}(B)<c\}

contains finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The main goal of this paper is to prove an analogous result for the Kato–Koshikawa height, which is a generalization of the Faltings height to motives. The setup is somewhat technical, so let us first mention an application to Shimura varieties.

As a result of
1.1
, Kisin and Mocz prove a finiteness result for the Faltings height in the Hecke orbits of Galois-generic points on Hodge type Shimura varieties. Namely, Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hecke orbit of an algebraic point
x_{0}
on a Shimura variety of Hodge type
\Sh(G,X)
. If the image of the Galois representation attached to
x_{0}
is Zariski dense in
G
, then for any
c>0
, the following set is finite:

\{x\in\mathcal{H}\mid h_{\Fal}(x)<c\}.

The Faltings height of a point
x\in\Sh(G,X)
is well-defined because Hodge type Shimura varieties can be viewed as moduli spaces of abelian varieties.
As a corollary of our main theorem, we prove the following for any Shimura variety.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hecke orbit of an algebraic point
x_{0}
on a Shimura variety
\Sh(G,X)
. Suppose that the image of the Galois representation attached to
x_{0}
is open in the finite adelic points
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
. Then for any

c>0
, the following set is finite:

\{x\in\mathcal{H}\mid h(x)<c\}.

This extends the corollary of Kisin–Mocz to Shimura varieties without a moduli interpretation in terms of abelian varieties, namely the exceptional Shimura varieties.

To prove
A
, we use the height defined by Kato
[
Kat14
]
and Koshikawa
[
Kos15
]
for a
Koshikawa motive
, by which we mean any system of realizations coming from a Grothendieck motive over a number field with an integral structure on the adelic étale realization. This is referred to as a “pure
\mathbb{Z}
-motive” in
[
Kos15
]
. See §
4
for a precise definition. Such families of Koshikawa motives exist for all Shimura varieties, including exceptional ones.

The Kato–Koshikawa height is a direct generalization of the Faltings height, in that both are defined using an integral structure on the associated graded of the de Rham realization together with a norm at each infinite place of
F
defined using the Hodge polarization.
A
is an application of the following theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
M
be a Koshikawa motive
defined over a number field
F
with semistable reduction everywhere.
Assume the adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture for
M
and that the Hodge classes of
M
are absolute Hodge and de Rham (see
5.2.8
,
5.4.7
,
5.4.2
). Then the set

\{M^{\prime}\in\mathscr{I}(M)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid\;h(M^{\prime})<c\}

attains only finitely many height values.

Remark
.

If we consider an isogeny class of Koshikawa motives coming from fibers of a finite type family
\mathcal{X}\to S
of smooth projective varieties over an
F
-variety
S
satisfying some mild conditions
[
Kos15
, §6]
and a quasifinite Hodge-theoretic period map, then one can upgrade the statement of the theorem to finiteness of isomorphism classes of Koshikawa motives rather than finiteness of height values.

Here, an isogeny
M^{\prime}\to M
defined over
F^{\prime}
refers to a finite-index inclusion of the integral structure of
M^{\prime}
into that of
M
which is
\Gal(\overline{F^{\prime}}/F^{\prime})
-equivariant. The underlying vector spaces for
M
and
M^{\prime}
are otherwise identical. This mirrors the situation of abelian varieties: one can define an isogeny
A\to B
defined over
F^{\prime}
as a
\Gal(\overline{F^{\prime}}/F^{\prime})
-equivariant finite-index inclusion of
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules
\prod_{p}T_{p}(A)\to\prod_{p}T_{p}(B)
.

We remark that the adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture is a stronger version of the Mumford–Tate conjecture, but the two are equivalent in the case of abelian varieties
[
CM20
]
. The conditions on the Hodge classes of
M
are known to be true for abelian varieties due to Deligne
[
Del82
]
and Blasius
[
Bla94
]
, respectively. These conditions on the Hodge classes are also satisfied for the Hodge structures coming from algebraic points on a Shimura variety.

1.2.
Proof outline

The proof of
B
centers around a formula due to Koshikawa for the change of height
h(M^{\prime})-h(M)
when
M^{\prime}\to M
is an isogeny of motives. The framework of this argument follows
[
KM
]
, which studies the analogous isogeny formula for the Faltings height.
Any isogeny factors into a sequence of prime-power isogenies, so without loss of generality, we may assume that the isogeny is determined by the finite-index inclusion
T^{\prime}\subsetneq T\subset M_{\et,p}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattices in the
p
-adic étale realization
M_{\et,p}
. The formula for
h(M^{\prime})-h(M)
is in terms of the torsion Galois representation
T/T^{\prime}

[
Kos15
, 8.1]
.
Suppose that the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
T^{\prime}
is only stable under
\Gal(\overline{F}/F^{\prime})
for a finite extension
F^{\prime}/F
. If
T
was
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
-stable, then such a finite extension
F^{\prime}
exists for any finite-index sublattice
T^{\prime}
. We can then adapt the isogeny formula of Koshikawa to show

(1.1)

\displaystyle h({M}^{\prime})-h({{M}})

\displaystyle=\log\#(T/T^{\prime})\left(\frac{w}{2}-\nu(T,T^{\prime})\right),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\nu(T,T^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\mid p\\
\text{of $F$}\end{subarray}}\frac{[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{\sigma\in\Gal_{F}}\mu\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T)}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T^{\prime})}\right)d\sigma.

Here,
w
is the weight of the motive
M
, and
\nu(T,T^{\prime})
is a weighted average of invariants (slopes) attached to torsion Breuil–Kisin–Fargues modules
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T)/\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T^{\prime})
. As the notation suggests,
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T)
and
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T^{\prime})
are functorially attached to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattices in semistable
p
-adic Galois representations after choosing an embedding
\overline{F}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
extending a place
v\mid p
of
F

[
Kis06
]
. The cokernel of
\varphi:\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T)\to\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T)
contains the data of the Hodge–Tate decomposition of the Galois representation
T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
For a torsion quotient of finite free Breuil–Kisin–Fargues modules
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
, the slope
\mu(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
measures the relative size of the cokernels of Frobenius on
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.

In the formula (
1.2
) for
\nu
, observe that there is an integral over the Galois orbit, and there is a sum over different places
v\mid p
. If there are infinitely many
M^{\prime}
in the isogeny class of
M
with
h(M^{\prime})<c
, one of the following two cases must occur: infinitude of
p
-power isogenies for a fixed prime, and infinitude of
p
-torsion isogenies for infinitely many primes
p
.

1.2.1.
Fixed prime case

In the first case, it is always possible to produce an infinite subset
\{T^{\prime}_{i}\to T\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of isogenies which are of the form
T^{\prime}_{i}=L+p^{n_{i}}T
for some lower-rank
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-submodule
L\subset T
with torsion-free quotient, and
n_{i}\to\infty
. A priori,
L
does not have to satisfy any Galois-stability condition. For simplicity we can just consider
n_{i}=i
for all
i
. We prove the following.

Proposition 1.3

(§
6.5
)
.

Let
M
be as in
B
, and let
L
and
T
be as above. Then

\lim_{i\to\infty}\nu(T,L+p^{i}T)\leq\frac{w}{2},

where
w
is the weight of the Koshikawa motive
M
. Moreover, if equality is achieved, then
L
is stable by the action of
\Gal(\overline{F}/F^{\prime})
for some finite extension
F^{\prime}/F
.

If the inequality is strict, it follows that the height
h(M_{i}^{\prime})
increases without bound as
i\to\infty
. If the inequality is an equality, then all the
M_{i}^{\prime}
are motives defined over some number field
E
. We can thus apply
[
Kos15
, Theorem 9.8]
to conclude that the
h(M_{i}^{\prime})
for all
i
attain only a finite set of values.

To prove the Proposition, we first construct
\mathfrak{M}(L)
as a finite free Breuil–Kisin–Fargues module, and show that
\mathfrak{M}(L+p^{i}T)=\mathfrak{M}(L)+p^{i}\mathfrak{M}(T)
. For any
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
, we can bound

\lim_{i}\mu(\mathfrak{M}(T)/\mathfrak{M}(\sigma L)\bmod{p^{i}})

in terms of the length of the Frobenius cokernel
\mathfrak{M}(L)/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}(L)\otimes B_{\dR}^{+}
. However, as remarked earlier, the Frobenius cokernel of
\mathfrak{M}(L)
contains the data of the location of
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C
in the Hodge–Tate decomposition on
T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C
.

What’s more, the Mumford–Tate assumption implies
\rho(\Gal_{F})\subset G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is open, so we can replace the integral over
\sigma\in\Gal_{F}
in (
1.2
) with one over
g\in G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. The integral will equal the “generic” slope value, i.e. the one attained by a measure one (or Zariski open) set of
g\in G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

The absolute Hodge class and étale-de Rham compatibility assumptions imply a Hodge symmetry on the Hodge–Tate decompositions for conjugate pairs of places
v\mid p
, which allows us to bound the sum of generic slope values for conjugate pairs of
v\mid p
in terms of the weight
w
. Because
\nu
is a sum over all places
v\mid p
, this Hodge symmetry relation implies the Proposition.

1.3.1.
Varying prime case

Suppose there exist
p
-power isogenies
M^{\prime}\to M
with
h(M^{\prime})<c
for infinitely many primes
p
. Unlike the fixed-prime case, the proof in the varying-prime case of
[
KM
]
still applies in our situation if we assume the adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture.

Proposition 1.4

(
Corollary

7.4.3
)
.

Assume
M
is as in
B
. For any prime
p
, let
T_{p}
denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-integral structure chosen on the
p
-adic étale realization
M_{p}
for
M
. After replacing
F
with a finite extension, there exists a constant
c_{M}>0
such that for
p\gg 0
, and for any proper sublattice
T_{p}^{\prime}\subset T_{p}
such that the
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
-orbit of
T_{p}^{\prime}
generates
T_{p}
, the following inequality holds.

\frac{w}{2}-\nu(T_{p},T_{p}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2[F:\mathbb{Q}](\rk(T_{p})-1)}\left(1-\frac{c_{M}}{p}\right).

The result of Koshikawa
[
Kos15
, Theorem 9.8]
implies that there must be infinitely many prime-power isogenies of the form specified in the Proposition. However, for
p\gg 0
, the Proposition shows that any such
p
-power isogeny
M^{\prime}\to M
must have
h(M^{\prime})>c
, where
c
is as in
B
.

We prove the Proposition by reducing to the situation of
p
-torsion isogenies, i.e.
p
-power isogenies defined by a finite-index inclusion of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules
T_{p}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow T_{p}
such that
T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space. The proof in the
p
-torsion case is due to
[
KM
, Proposition 3.6.3]
, and we briefly summarize it here. The adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture for
M
implies that for
p\gg 0
, we can replace the integral over
\Gal_{F}
in (
1.2
) with an average over the
G_{MT}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
-orbit acting on the subspace
T^{\prime}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
in the vector space
T_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Allowing
p\gg 0
also ensures that the associated
p
-torsion BKF modules are Fontaine–Laffaille, which obey a good structure theory. This, combined with a similar Hodge-symmetry inequality used in the fixed-prime case, also gives a lower-bound for
\nu(T_{p},T^{\prime}_{p})
whenever
T_{p}\supset T_{p}^{\prime}\supset pT_{p}
.

3.
Breuil–Kisin–Fargues modules

Breuil–Kisin–Fargues modules generalize Breuil–Kisin modules from “classical” integral

Remark 3.2.6

3.3.1.

Definition 3.3.2

(

\displaystyle\deg\mathfrak{M}

Here, we list some properties of degree and slope which follow from the definitions and are proved in

Lemma 3.3.3

(1)

Suppose

3.4.
Nygaard filtration and
\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}

In order to study the change in height under an isogeny of motives, we will use a Nygaard filtration on torsion BKF modules of the form
\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\in\Mod^{\varphi,t}_{/A_{\inf}}
, where
\mathfrak{M},\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\in\Mod^{\varphi}_{/A_{\inf}}
are finite free BKF modules.

Definition 3.4.1

(Nygaard filtration on finite free BKF modules)
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}\in\Mod^{\varphi,\leq w}_{/A_{\inf}}
. We can define a filtration on
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}
by

\Fil_{\mathcal{N}}^{r}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}=\{x\in\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\;|\;\varphi(x)\in\varphi(\xi)^{r}\mathfrak{M}\}.

Remark 3.4.2
.

When
\mathfrak{M}\in\Mod^{\varphi,\leq w}_{/A_{\inf}}
, i.e. when the cokernel of
\varphi
on
\mathfrak{M}
is
\varphi(\xi)^{w}
-torsion, observe that
\varphi\left(\Fil_{\mathcal{N}}^{w}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\right)=\varphi(\xi)^{w}\mathfrak{M}\subset\mathfrak{M}
.

This is the base change of the Nygaard filtration defined for Breuil-Kisin modules in
[
Kos15
, §7.1]
if we base change under the composed map
\mathfrak{S}=W(k)[[u]]\xrightarrow{f}A_{\inf}\xrightarrow{\varphi}A_{\inf}
sending
u\mapsto[\pi^{\flat}]^{p}
, which is the map used to relate Breuil–Kisin to Breuil–Kisin–Fargues modules in
3.2.7
. The identification of the two Nygaard filtrations is due to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4.3
.

Let
E(u)
be an Eisenstein polynomial in
W(k)[u]
, and let
f:W(k)[[u]]\to A_{\inf}
be the ring map determined by
u\mapsto[\pi^{\flat}]
, where
\pi
a root of
E(u)
. Then
f(E(u))
is a generator of the principal ideal
\ker\theta\subset A_{\inf}
.

Proof.

Since
E(u)
is Eisenstein, it can be written in the form
E(u)=u^{n}+p^{2}(a_{n-1}u^{n-1}+a_{n-2}u^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}u)+pv
for some
n
, where
a_{i}\in W(k)
and
v\in W(k)^{\times}
. If we write

(3.5)

\displaystyle f(E(u))=\sum_{i\geq 0}[b_{i}]p^{i},\qquad b_{i}\in O_{C}^{\flat},

then we see that
b_{0}=(\pi^{\flat})^{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\flat}
and
b_{1}=v\in(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\flat})^{\times}
. In particular, we can use
[
BC09
, Prop. 4.4.3(2)]
to conclude that
f(E(u))
is a generator of
\ker\theta
.
∎

3.5.
Filtered lattice in de Rham cohomology

The Nygaard filtration above induces a filtered lattice which will be useful in analyzing the change in Kato–Koshikawa height under isogenies.

Definition 3.5.1
.

For an effective BK module
\mathfrak{M}\in\Mod^{\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}}
coming from a
\Gal_{K}
-stable

One has the containment

3.5.3.

Continuing the notation from §

\Fil^{i}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{BK}

Since the map

To get to the

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{M}_{BK})_{\dR}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\mathcal{O}_{C}

Remark 3.5.4
.

After the present author noticed that the definition of the Nygaard filtration on torsion BK modules in
[
Kos15
, §7.1]
does not behave will in short exact sequences, T. Koshikawa suggested to use the following quotient filtration instead.

Definition 3.5.5

When

\Fil^{\prime}
on

(3.9)

\displaystyle 0\to\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{N}}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\to\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{N}}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\Fil^{\prime i}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})\to 0

Moreover, the same is true for the graded pieces
\gr^{i}_{\mathcal{N}}
and

3.5.6.

For free Breuil-Kisin-Fargues modules

\displaystyle 0\to\gr_{F}^{j}\gr^{r}_{\mathcal{N}}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\to\gr_{F}^{j}\gr^{r}_{\mathcal{N}}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\gr_{F^{\prime}}^{j}\gr^{\prime r}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})\to 0.

Also, by definition of

\gr_{F}^{j}\gr^{r}_{\mathcal{N}}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\cong\gr^{r-j}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR},

\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}:=\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{A_{\inf},\tilde{\theta}}\mathcal{O}_{C}

Because (

4.
Heights on motives

4.1.
Motives as systems of realizations

In this section, we will use the same definitions as used by
[
Kos15
]
. Roughly, a motive is the data of the
w
th Betti, de Rham, and étale cohomologies of a smooth projective variety
X
over a number field
F
, all of which are compatible under various comparison isomorphisms with their various structures (e.g. polarized Hodge structure, filtration, or Galois representation).

Rather than directly working with the category of motives when studying heights in isogeny classes, we instead work with varying choices of integral structures on a fixed system of realizations
[
Del89
, §7]
coming from a single Grothendieck motive. A system of realizations is a cohomological avatar of a motive, remembering the various cohomological realizations of a motive and the extra data and relations among them.

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
F
be a number field. A
system of realizations
of weight
w
defined over
F
is the tuple
{\bf M}=(M_{B,\sigma},M_{\dR},M_{\et},i_{\sigma,\dR},i_{\sigma,B,\et},\{i_{v,\et,\dR}\}_{v})
, where

•

for each
\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the Betti realization
M_{B,\sigma}
, which is a polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure of weight
w
,

•

the de Rham realization
M_{\dR}
, which is a filtered
F
-vector space,

•

the adelic étale realization
M_{\et}
, which is an
\mathbb{A}^{f}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-module with a continuous
\Gal_{F}
-action,

•

i_{\sigma,\dR}:M_{B,\sigma}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C}
,

•

i_{\sigma,B,\et}:M_{B,\sigma}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}^{f}_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{\et}

•

For every embedding
v:\overline{F}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
extending a place
v\mid p
of
F
, an isomorphism
i_{v,\et,\dR}:M_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}B_{\dR}\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,v}B_{\dR}
respecting filtrations and
\Gal(\overline{F}_{v}/F_{v})
-actions. Here,
M_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic projection of
M_{\et}
.

Remark 4.1.2
.

One might consider the
\mathbb{A}_{f}
-topology to be dependent on some choice of integral structure on
M_{\et}
. However, any choice of complex embedding
\sigma
and any choice of
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice inside the
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure

will define a
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
-integral structure on

under the comparison isomorphism

, which will allow one to define the restricted product structure on
\prod_{p}M_{p}
to make
M_{\et}
an
\mathbb{A}_{f}
-module. Regardless of the choice of
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice, the topology on
M_{\et}
will be the same.

Definition 4.1.3

(
[
Kos15
]
, 4.2)
.

Fix a finite place
v
of
F
above
p
, and let
{\bf M}
be a system of realizations defined over
F
.

(1)

{\bf M}
has
good reduction
at

if the
\ell

is unramified at
v

\ell\neq p
, and the
p
-adic étale realization

is a crystalline representation at
v
.

(2)

{\bf M}
has
semistable reduction
at

if the inertia
I_{v}
acts unipotently on

for any
\ell\neq p
, and the
p

is a semistable representation at

.

Remark 4.1.4
.

Suppose we have smooth proper variety
X/F
.
Faltings showed that at places
v\mid p
of
F
, the Galois representation on
p
-adic étale cohomology of

is de Rham, and when
X
has good reduction at
v
, the representation is crystalline
[

.
Any smooth proper

will have good reduction at almost all places of

[

]
and separately by André, Mebkhout, and Kedlaya. Therefore we may replace

with a finite extension so that the system of realizations corresponding to the

th cohomology of

Definition 4.1.5
.

A
Koshikawa motive

M=({\bf M},\hat{T})
of weight
w
defined over a number field
F
is a system of realizations
{\bf M}
over
F
associated to a Grothendieck motive over
F
, together with the choice of a
\Gal_{F}
-stable
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
-submodule

which is open in the adelic topology. We will always assume that
{\bf M}
has semistable reduction everywhere.

Remark 4.1.6
.

In what follows, one might ask whether the Kato–Koshikawa height could be defined for only the data of a system of realizations with a choice of integral structure, i.e. discarding the assumption that the data arises as the image of some actual correspondence of smooth projective varieties defined over
F
. However, the construction of the Kato–Koshikawa height requires the various
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-lattices in

produced via the choice of

and integral
p
-adic Hodge theory to be coherent. That is, for almost all
v
of
F
, we need the
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-lattice to be the base-change of a single
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice in

. In
[

]
, Koshikawa uses some results that follow from having integral models of the varieties involved in producing the motive to guarantee coherence of the

-lattices. Without putting some unnatural extra conditions on the system of realizations, we do not know how to replace this use of geometry in ensuring the height is well-defined. Therefore we will require that a Koshikawa motive must have an underlying system of realizations coming from geometry.

4.2.

-adic Hodge theory to produce a filtered
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-lattice

M_{\dR}(T_{p})_{v}\subset M_{\dR}\otimes_{F}F_{v}

from the
\Gal_{F}
-action on the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
T_{p}
restricted to
\Gal_{F_{v}}
for all places
v\mid p
of
F
and all
p
.
He then uses a comparison to log de Rham cohomology to show that these various filtered

-lattices will combine to give a filtered

-lattice in

. He provides an alternate justification in

using the new technique of

-cohomology developed in

]

4.2.2.

We have a smooth integral model
\mathcal{X}\to\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
of
X
defined over the
S

F
. We can enlarge

so that the following hold:

(1)

the finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-modules

and
H^{w+1}_{\dR}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{F,S})
are torsion-free,

(2)

the finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-modules
H^{w-i}(\mathcal{X},\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{F,S}})
are torsion-free for all
i
, and

(3)

for all

such that some

satisfies
v\mid p
, the choice of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
lattice in
M_{p}
coming from
\hat{T}
is the natural one:
T_{p}=H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset M_{p}
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a proper smooth formal scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
with generic fiber
X_{C}
.
There is a perfect complex of
A_{\inf}
-modules
R\Gamma_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that all cohomology groups are finitely presented BKF modules. This

-cohomology complex is a way to geometrically construct the BKF modules associated to the étale cohomology of

, and satisfies various comparison isomorphisms to de Rham, étale, and crystalline cohomology.
The construction and comparison theorems are originally proved by Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze in
[
BMS18
]
. Since then, Bhatt and Scholze have shown that these results can be proven using the formalism of the prismatic site
[
BS22
]
,
[
GR24
, Theorem 9.1]
.
One may then combine the
A_{\inf}
-cohomology comparison theorems with Fargues’ classification of finite free BKF modules to prove the following, under a torsion-free assumption.

Theorem 4.2.3

(
[
BMS18
]
, 14.5(iii))
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be the formal scheme as above, and fix
i\geq 0
. Assume that
H^{i}_{\dR}(\mathfrak{X})
is torsion-free. Then
H^{i}_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X}):=H^{i}(R\Gamma_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X}))
is a finite free BKF module. In particular, it is the BKF module associated to
H^{i}_{\et}(X_{C},\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

The
A_{\inf}
-de Rham comparison theorem is the following form
[
BMS18
, Theorem 1.8(ii)]
:

R\Gamma_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X})\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{\inf}}\mathcal{O}_{C}\simeq R\Gamma_{\dR}(\mathfrak{X}).

With the choice of
S
satisfying the torsion-free conditions above, for all
v\not\in S
, we can thus conclude that

H^{w}_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X})\otimes_{A_{\inf}}\mathcal{O}_{C}\cong H^{w}_{\dR}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{F,S})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F,S},v}\mathcal{O}_{C}.

The tensor product on both sides are non-derived. In other words, we have found a
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-lattice inside de Rham cohomology
M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,v}C
. Moreover, the above theorem shows that
H^{w}_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X})
depends only on
X_{C}
and not on the choice of integral model.

Let’s see how this produces at least a choice of
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-lattice inside
M_{\dR}
that is independent of choice of integral model of
X
. For all
v\not\in S
, we get the
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-module structure on
M_{\dR}\otimes_{F}F_{v}
as

(H^{w}_{A_{\inf}}(\mathfrak{X})\otimes_{A_{\inf}}\mathcal{O}_{C})\cap(H^{w}_{\dR}(X/F)\otimes F_{v})=H^{w}_{\dR}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{F,S})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F,S}}\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}.

So we have found an
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-lattice in
M_{\dR}=H^{w}_{\dR}(X/F)
such that for all
v\not\in S
, the base-change to
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
of the lattice is independent of the choice of integral model, and therefore so is this
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-lattice.

4.2.4.

Now that we have at least a good choice of an
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-lattice in
M_{\dR}
. Suppose that for each
v\in S
, we could construct an
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-lattice
M_{\dR}(T_{p})_{v}
in
M_{\dR}\otimes_{F}F_{v}
. Then we can combine these data to get a global
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice
M_{\dR}(\hat{T})\subset M_{\dR}
locally of the correct rank.

With the choice of
\Gal_{F}
-stable
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice
T_{p}\subset M_{p}
in the semistable representation
M_{p}
, we can associate a finite free BK module
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})
as follows. Choose a place
v\mid p
of
F
. Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{F}
, i.e. an embedding
v:\overline{F}\to C
extending
v:F\hookrightarrow C
. Choose a uniformizer
\pi
of
F_{v}
and compatible
p^{n}
th roots
\pi^{1/p^{n}}
in
\overline{F_{v}}
for all
n
. Write
K:=F_{v}
, and let
K_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{n}F_{v}(\pi^{1/p^{n}})
. This defines inclusions of absolute Galois groups
\Gal_{K_{\infty}}\subset\Gal_{K}\subset\Gal_{F}
. One can then use the equivalence between in
[
Kis06
, Lemma 2.1.15]
to define
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})
.

One may be concerned that different choices of
\pi^{1/p^{n}}
defining

may result in different BK modules
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})
, and thus not having a well-defined way to construct the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice in

used to define the height. However, the resulting
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
lattice in

will be independent of the choice of
\pi^{1/p^{n}}

[
Liu18
, Theorem 1.0.1]

Given a BK module
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})
, one has a natural map
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathcal{O}_{K}\to D_{\dR}(M_{p})=M_{\dR}\otimes_{F}F_{v}

[
Liu18
, §2.3, §4.2]
. Here,
\mathfrak{S}\to\mathcal{O}_{K}
is given by

, where
\pi
is a root of the Eisenstein polynomial
E(u)\in\mathfrak{S}
.
The image of the Nygaard filtration on
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}
defines a filtration on
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Define the filtered
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-lattice

M_{\dR}(T_{p})_{v}:=\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}.

We can then intersect the filtered
\mathcal{O}_{F,S}
-lattice
H^{w}_{\dR}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{F,S})
in
M_{\dR}
and the filtered
\mathcal{O}_{F,v}
-lattices
M_{\dR}(T_{p})_{v}
for the finitely many

to get a filtered
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice
M_{\dR}(\hat{T})
in
M_{\dR}
.

Now we have a filtered
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice
M_{\dR}(\hat{T})
in
M_{\dR}
. For the
v\not\in S
, the torsion-free assumption (2) from
4.2.2
on the relative Hodge cohomology means that the relative Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence computing

degenerates at the

page, because the
E_{1}
differentials are 0 after tensoring with

.

Therefore, for
v\not\in S
,
(\gr^{r}M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
is already torsion-free. Define
\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))_{\fr}
to be the unique
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module such that

\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))_{\fr}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}=\begin{cases}\gr^{r}M_{\dR}(\hat{T})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}&v\not\in S\\
(\gr^{r}M_{\dR}(\hat{T})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})/\text{torsion}&v\in S.\end{cases}

This defines an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice
\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))_{\fr}
in
\gr^{r}(M_{\dR})
.

Remark 4.2.5
.

In fact, the process outlined in §2.6 of
[
Kos15
]
to produce

would also work for the places

[

, Theorem 3.5(3)], but a priori it would not be clear that the intersection of all these lattices with

would produce an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-submodule of the correct rank. Some further argument is needed, either using

-cohomology as above or log de Rham cohomology as is used in
[

]
, to show that these

-lattices are compatible.

4.3.
Kato–Koshikawa height of a Koshikawa motive

Using the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattices
\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))_{\fr}
for all
r
from above, we can now define an

\mathcal{O}_{F}
-integral version of the Griffiths line bundle, which Koshikawa then uses to define the height in a way analogous to the Faltings height.

Definition 4.3.1

(
[
Kos15
]
, §4.2)
.

Define the locally free rank 1
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module

\mathcal{L}({M}):=\bigotimes_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}(\det(\gr^{r}M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))_{\fr})^{\otimes r}.

The tensor products and determinants are all taken over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

We actually do need to keep track of the torsion of the
\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))
in the definition of the height, to ensure that the height change is well-behaved under isogenies.

Definition 4.3.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{F}^{\text{fin}}
denote all finite places of
F
. For a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
-module
M
, let
M_{\tor}
denote the torsion submodule of
M
. Define

\#\mathcal{L}({M})^{\tor}:=\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{F}^{\text{fin}}}\prod_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\#\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}(\hat{T})_{v})_{\tor}

We use the superscript
\tor
on the left-hand side so as not to suggest that we are taking the torsion submodule of the torsion-free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module
\mathcal{L}({M})
.

4.3.3.

Fix an embedding
\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and let
\mathcal{L}({M})_{\sigma}:=\mathcal{L}({M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F},\sigma}\mathbb{C}
. Let
c\in\Aut(\mathbb{C})
denote complex conjugation. Using the Hodge symmetry on the Hodge decomposition, there is a natural isomorphism

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({M})_{\sigma}\otimes\mathcal{L}({M})_{c\circ\sigma}

\displaystyle\cong\left(\bigotimes_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\det\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes r}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\det\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,c\circ\sigma}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes r}\right)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\cong\left(\bigotimes_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\det\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes r}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\det\gr^{w-r}(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes r}\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\cong\bigotimes_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\det\gr^{r}(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C})\right)^{\otimes w}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\cong\det(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes w}.

The Betti-étale comparison isomorphism, together with the integral structure given by
\hat{T}
, defines a
\mathbb{Z}
-module inside
\det(M_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes w}
. Choosing a generator
s_{B,\sigma}
of this free rank 1
\mathbb{Z}
-module and using the Betti-de Rham comparison isomorphism
\det(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes w}\cong\det(M_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes w}
then allows us to define an isomorphism
of 1-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces

\displaystyle\det(M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))^{\otimes w}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}\mathbb{C}\cong\det(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes w}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle zs_{B,\sigma}

\displaystyle\mapsto z.

For a section
s\in\mathcal{L}({M})
, the image of
s\otimes\overline{s}
in
\det(M_{\dR}\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes w}
will be some multiple
zs_{B,\sigma}
of the generator
s_{B,\sigma}
, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Then define

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left\|s\right\|_{\sigma}:=|z|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{C}},

where
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}
is the usual absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 4.3.4
.

Let
{M}:=({\bf M},\hat{T})
be a Koshikawa motive over
F
such that
{\bf M}={H}^{w}(X)
for
X
a smooth projective variety defined over
F
.
Choose any
s\in\mathcal{L}({M})
.
The
logarithmic Kato–Koshikawa height
is the sum

h({M}):=\frac{1}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\left(\log\#\mathcal{L}({M})^{\tor}+\#(\mathcal{L}({M})/\mathcal{O}_{F}\cdot s)-\sum_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}\log\left\|s\right\|_{\sigma}\right).

Remark 4.3.5
.

The height is independent of the choice of
s
. Indeed, any two choices
s,s^{\prime}
will satisfy
s=\alpha s^{\prime}
for
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, and the product formula for
\alpha
will ensure the heights computed using
s
and
s^{\prime}
are the same.

Remark 4.3.6

(
[
Kos15
]
, Proposition 4.8)
.

The height of a Koshikawa motive
{M}
defined over
F
is independent of base change to a finite field extension of
F
.

4.4.
Comparison to other heights

4.4.1.
Arakelov height

Consider smooth projective scheme
\mathcal{X}\to\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Consider the Koshikawa motive
{M}=({\bf M},\hat{T})
, where
{\bf M}={H}^{w}(\mathcal{X}_{F})
with integral structure
\hat{T}:=\prod_{p}H^{w}_{\et}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})_{\fr}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}({M})
is a metrized line bundle on
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F}
with Hermitian metric at the infinite place
\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by (
4.5
), which is invariant under complex conjugation.
Let
\widehat{\deg}(\mathcal{L}({M}))
denote the normalized Arakelov degree of this metrized line bundle
[
BGS94
, (2.1.15), §3.1.4]
.

Proposition 4.4.2

(
[
Kos15
]
, Proposition 4.10)
.

The difference
\lvert h({M})-\widehat{\deg}(\mathcal{L}({M}))\rvert
is bounded by a constant that depends only on
\dim X
and the Hodge numbers of
{\bf M}
.

The Kato—Koshikawa height also recovers the Faltings height when applied to
M={H}^{1}(A)
when
A/F
is an abelian variety.

Proposition 4.4.3

(
[
Kos15
]
)
.

Let
h_{\Fal}(A)
denote the stable Faltings height of the abelian variety
A
. Then
|h_{\Fal}(A)-h(M)|
is bounded by a constant depending only on
\dim A
.

4.5.
Formula for height change under isogeny

Let
{M}=({\bf M},\hat{T})
and
{M}^{\prime}=({\bf M},\hat{T}^{\prime})
be two pure Koshikawa motives over
F
defined by putting two different
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
-structures on the same rational Grothendieck motive
{\bf M}
.

Definition 4.5.1
.

The data of
{M}
,
{M}^{\prime}
as above together with a finite-index inclusion
f:\hat{T}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\hat{T}
of
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules is called an
isogeny
. If the inclusion
f
is
\Gal_{F^{\prime}}
-equivariant for some extension
F^{\prime}/F
, then the isogeny is said to be defined over
F^{\prime}
. We will sometimes abuse notation and write
f:{M}^{\prime}\to{M}
for the isogeny.

Remark 4.5.2
.

If an isogeny
f:{M}^{\prime}\to{M}
is such that
\hat{T}/f(\hat{T}^{\prime})
is a
p
-group, then we say that
f
is a
p
-power isogeny
. Any isogeny defined over
F^{\prime}
can be factored into a finite composition of
p_{i}
-power isogenies, all defined over the same
F^{\prime}
. The
p_{i}
are the primes which divide
\#\hat{T}/f(\hat{T}^{\prime})
.

4.5.3.

Let
f:{M}^{\prime}\to{M}
be an isogeny of pure Koshikawa motives as above which is defined over
F
. It suffices to write down a formula for
h({M^{\prime}})-h({M})
in the case that
f
is a
p
-power isogeny for some prime
p
.
We may twist the motive such that the Hodge–Tate weights of the étale realization of
{\bf M}
are nonpositive, which allows us to assume that any BK modules associated to lattices in
M_{p}
are effective.

Let
v\mid p
be a finite place of
F
. Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{F}
, i.e. an embedding
v:\overline{F}\to C
extending
v:F\hookrightarrow C
. Choose a uniformizer
\pi
of
F_{v}
and compatible
p^{n}
th roots
\pi^{1/p^{n}}
in
\overline{F_{v}}
for all
n
. Write
K:=F_{v}
, and let
K_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{n}F_{v}(\pi^{1/p^{n}})
. This defines inclusions of absolute Galois groups
\Gal_{K_{\infty}}\subset\Gal_{K}\subset\Gal_{F}
.

Using
Theorem

3.1.3
, a
\Gal_{K_{\infty}}
-stable
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattices
T^{\prime}_{p}\subset T_{p}
inside the semistable rational representation
M_{p}
corresponds to an inclusion of finite free effective BK modules
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T^{\prime}_{p})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})
, where
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T^{\prime}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}=\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Remark 4.5.4
.

[
Kos15
]
uses a contravariant functor
\mathfrak{M}(-):\Rep^{free}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Gal_{K})\to\Mod^{\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}}
. We will use the covariant one, but the two approaches differ only by taking duals on both sides
[
BC09
, Remark 11.2.5]
.

4.5.5.

The following isogeny formula is due to
[
Kos15
, Proposition 8.1]
. The original proof uses a slightly different definition of the Nygaard filtration on the torsion BK module
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})/\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p}^{\prime})
. See
3.5.4
. For the sake of completeness, we rewrite the proof of Koshikawa’s isogeny formula using the quotient Nygaard filtration defined in §
3.4
, but the idea of the proof is otherwise entirely the same as the original one given by Koshikawa.

Proposition 4.5.6
.

Use the setup of a
p
-power isogeny
{M}^{\prime}\to{M}
as in
4.5.3
. Recall from
Definition

3.3.2
the definition of the slope
\mu(\mathfrak{N})
of an effective torsion BK module
\mathfrak{N}\in\Mod^{\varphi,t}_{/\mathfrak{S}}
. Then

\displaystyle h({M}^{\prime})-h({{M}})

\displaystyle=\log\#(T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime})\left(\frac{w}{2}-\nu(T_{p},T_{p}^{\prime})\right),

where we define

\displaystyle\nu(T_{p},T_{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v\mid p}[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\>\mu\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p}^{\prime})}\right).

Proof.

Suppose that for each place
v\mid p
, the inclusion
T_{p}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow T_{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gal_{F_{v}}]
-modules gives rise to inclusions
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p}^{\prime})\to\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})
.
Recall that

\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})_{\dR}:=\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}=M_{\dR}(T_{p})_{v}

and the analogously defined
\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p}^{\prime})_{\dR}

are filtered
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules.
By definition of
\mathcal{L}({M})
, one has that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\#\mathcal{L}({M})_{\tor}}{\#\mathcal{L}({M}^{\prime})_{\tor}}\#\frac{\mathcal{L}({M})}{\mathcal{L}({M}^{\prime})}=\prod_{v\mid p}\prod_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\#\left(\frac{\gr^{r}(\mathfrak{M}_{v})_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{v})_{\dR}}\right)^{r}.

The inclusion
{M}^{\prime}\to{M}
induces an inclusion
\mathcal{L}({M}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{L}({M})
. The
\mathbb{Z}
-structures
\mathbb{Z}\cdot s^{\prime}_{B,\sigma}\subset(\det M_{\dR}(\hat{T}^{\prime}))^{\otimes w}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F},\sigma}\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{Z}\cdot s_{B,\sigma}\subset(\det M_{\dR}(\hat{T}))^{\otimes w}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F},\sigma}\mathbb{C}
from the respective Betti-étale comparisons satisfy

\#(\mathbb{Z}s_{B,\sigma})/(\mathbb{Z}s^{\prime}_{B,\sigma})=\#(\hat{T}/\hat{T}^{\prime})^{w}.

These generators were used to define the norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\sigma}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\sigma}^{\prime}
which show up in the definition of
h({M})
and
h({M}^{\prime})
, respectively.
For
s\in\mathcal{L}({M^{\prime}})\subset\mathcal{L}({M})
, the relation above implies that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\|s\right\|_{\sigma}=\#(\hat{T}/\hat{T}^{\prime})^{w/2}\left\|s\right\|_{\sigma}^{\prime}

Together, (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) for a
p
-power isogeny show that

h({M}^{\prime})-h({{M}})=-\frac{1}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v\mid p}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}r\cdot\log\#\left(\frac{\gr^{r}(\mathfrak{M}_{v})_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{v})_{\dR}}\right)+\frac{w}{2}\log\#(T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime}).

In order to use the filtrations in
3.5.6
to rewrite the change in height in terms of slope, we need to convert these BK modules to BKF modules, which involves a twist by Frobenius. As stated after (
3.4
), we have that for an effective torsion BK module
\mathfrak{N}_{BK}
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\log_{p}\#\left(\mathfrak{N}_{BK}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{N}_{BK}\right)=[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\lambda\left(\mathfrak{N}_{BKF}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{N}_{BKF}\right),

where
\mathfrak{N}_{BKF}:=\mathfrak{N}_{BK}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S},\varphi\circ f}A_{\inf}
, where
\varphi\circ f(u):=[\pi^{\flat}]^{p}
as in
3.2.7
.

From now on, for a fixed (and omitted)
v\mid p
, we will write

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}

\displaystyle:=(\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p}))_{BKF}=\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S},\varphi\circ f}A_{\inf}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}

\displaystyle:=M_{\dR}(T_{p})_{v}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}}\mathcal{O}_{C}=(\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{v}(T_{p})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{A_{\inf}}\frac{A_{\inf}}{\varphi(\xi)},

and we define
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}
analogously.
From now on in the proof, we will be working with BKF modules. We hope this does not cause the reader too much confusion.

Recall that
\Fil^{\prime w}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})=\varphi(\xi)^{w}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
, and there are inclusions
\Fil^{\prime w}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})\subset\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})\subset\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}

\varphi
is injective, since the quotient
\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is
\varphi(\xi)
-torsion free. By taking quotients, we then have the relation

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}{\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}{\varphi(\xi)^{w}\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}\right)-\sum_{r=0}^{w-1}\lambda(\gr^{\prime r}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}))

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}{\varphi(\xi)^{w}\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}\right)-\sum_{r=0}^{w-1}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\lambda(\gr^{j}_{F^{\prime}}\gr^{\prime r}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}))

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}{\varphi(\xi)^{w}\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}\right)-\sum_{r=0}^{w-1}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\lambda(\gr^{\prime r-j}\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}))

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}{\varphi(\xi)^{w}\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}\right)-\sum_{r=0}^{w}(w-r)\lambda\left(\frac{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}}\right)

(4.15)

\displaystyle=w\cdot\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}}{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}}\right)-\sum_{r=0}^{w}(w-r)\lambda\left(\frac{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}}\right)

(4.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{w}r\lambda\left(\frac{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}}\right).

The second-to-last equality comes from the fact that
\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is
\varphi(\xi)
-torsion free, and the last equality follows from the fact that
(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})_{\dR}=\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}

\lambda(\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR})=\sum_{r=0}^{w}\lambda(\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}/\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR})

\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}/\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}
comes from a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module which is
p

4.8
) also holds for this module, and so
[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\lambda\left(\frac{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}}\right)=\log_{p}\#\frac{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}_{\dR}}{\gr^{r}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{\dR}}
.
Then we get

h({M}^{\prime})-h({{M}})=\log\#(T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime})\left(\frac{w}{2}-\frac{1}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v\mid p}[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\frac{\lambda\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}{\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}\right)}{\log_{p}\#(T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime})}\right),

which becomes the desired formula after using the definition of slope.

Analogously to the isogeny formula given by Faltings in

5.
Adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture and other assumptions

5.1.
Mumford–Tate conjecture

The following setup and statement of the Mumford–Tate conjecture is adapted from the versions given in
[
CM20
]
and in

Fix a subfield
k
of
\mathbb{C}
. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
. Fix an integer
w
. Let
M
be the Koshikawa motive of weight
w
corresponding to the
w
th cohomology of
X
. Using the inclusion
k\subset\mathbb{C}
, we can take the Betti realization
M_{B}:=H^{w}_{\Betti}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})/\text{torsion}
, which is a polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure. Let
G_{MT}\subset\GL(M_{B})
denote the associated Mumford–Tate group over
\mathbb{Z}
.
This is the Zariski closure of the usual definition of the Mumford–Tate group
G_{MT}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
as an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.

For every prime
\ell
, let
M_{\ell}:=H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})/\text{torsion}
be the
\ell
-adic étale realization. There is a continuous Galois representation

\rho_{{M},\ell}:\Gal(\overline{k}/k)\to\GL(M_{\ell}).

Definition 5.1.1
.

Fix
M
as above. The
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group

G_{\ell}
for
M
is the algebraic group over
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
which is the Zariski closure of the image
\rho_{{M},\ell}(\Gal(\overline{k}/k))
in
\GL(M_{\ell})
.

Remark 5.1.2
.

Usually, the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group is an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, but we want an integral version so that we can state an adelic version of the Mumford–Tate conjecture.

Remark 5.1.3
.

The groups
G_{MT}
and
G_{\ell}
depend on the choice of complex embedding of
k
and on
\ell
, respectively.

Despite not knowing whether the groups
G_{\ell}
are independent of
\ell
, one can replace
k
with a finite extension so that the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy groups
G_{\ell}
are connected for all
\ell
. The following is originally due to Serre.

Proposition 5.1.4

(6.14,
[
LP92
]
)
.

The finite index open subgroup
\rho_{{M},\ell}^{-1}(G_{\ell}^{\circ}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))\subset\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
is independent of
\ell
, and therefore the groups

G_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})/G^{\circ}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong\Gal(\overline{k}/k)/\rho_{{M},\ell}^{-1}(G_{\ell}^{\circ}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))

are independent of
\ell
.

Without loss of generality, we will assume from now on that the
G_{\ell}
are connected.
By
Remark

4.1.4
, if the field of definition
k
is a number field
F
, then for every
v\mid p
of
F
, the
p
-adic Galois representation
\Gal(\overline{F_{v}}/F_{v})
acting on
M_{p}
is de Rham, hence Hodge–Tate. The following is due to Bogomolov.

Theorem 5.1.5

(Théorème 1,
[
Bog80
]
)
.

Let
F
be a number field, and let
\rho:\Gal_{F}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be a continuous
p
-adic representation of
\Gal_{F}
. Let
G
be the algebraic subgroup over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which is the Zariski closure of
\rho(\Gal_{F})
in
\GL_{n}
. For each place
\overline{v}
of
\overline{F}
extending
v\mid p
of
F
, suppose that the restriction of
\rho
to
\Gal(\overline{F}_{\overline{v}}/F_{v})
is a Hodge–Tate representation. Then the image
\rho(\Gal_{F})
is an open subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
in the
p
-adic topology.

Given an embedding
\tilde{\sigma}:\overline{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, there is a canonical étale-Betti comparison isomorphism
M_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{A}_{f}\to\prod_{\ell}M_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
of
\mathbb{A}_{f}
-modules
[
AGV73
, Exposé XI]
.
Therefore we may view both
G_{MT}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and

as subgroup schemes of
\GL(M_{\ell})
.

Conjecture 5.1.6

(Mumford–Tate conjecture)
.

Using the comparison isomorphism as above for some
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\ell
,
G_{MT}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}=G_{\ell}
as connected subgroup schemes of
\GL(M_{\ell})
.

As mentioned in
Remark

5.1.3
, the Mumford–Tate conjecture a priori depends on the choice of
F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and choice of
\ell
.

5.2.
The adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture

The conjecture may be stated when working over subfield of
\mathbb{C}
of finite type, but let us work with a motive
{M}={\bf H}^{w}(X)
for smooth proper
X
over a number field
F
, and fix a complex embedding
\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

Suppose that the Mumford–Tate conjecture (
5.1.6
) is true for some choice of
\ell
. By
Theorem

5.1.5
, the map
\rho_{\ell}:\Gal_{F}\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
has open image in the
\ell
-adic topology.

If we consider all
\ell
-adic representations together, and we assume that the Mumford–Tate conjecture is true for
M
for every
\ell
, we get a map

\hat{\rho}:\Gal_{F}\to G_{MT}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}).

Serre suggested the following extension of the Mumford–Tate Conjecture.

Conjecture 5.2.1

(Adelic Mumford–Tate conjecture, 11.4,
[
Ser94
]
)
.

Suppose that the Betti realization
M_{B}
is Hodge-maximal. Then the image of
\hat{\rho}
is an open subgroup of
G_{MT}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
in the adelic topology. In particular, for large enough
\ell
,
\rho_{\ell}:\Gal_{F}\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is surjective.

Definition 5.2.2
.

Suppose that
M_{B}
is a polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure, with the Hodge structure given by the map
h:\mathbb{S}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{R}}
. We say that
M_{B}
is
Hodge-maximal
if there does not exist a nontrivial isogeny of connected
\mathbb{Q}
-groups
G^{\prime}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that the Hodge structure lifts along the isogeny to a map
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{S}\to G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Remark 5.2.3
.

By
[
CM20
, Remark 2.6]
, it is a necessary condition that
M_{B}
is Hodge-maximal in order for
5.2.1
to hold. Otherwise, the fact that
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
does not have open image is an obstruction to
\hat{\rho}(\Gal_{F})
having open image in
G_{MT}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\subset G_{MT}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
.

The following remark closely follows
[
CM20
, §2.2]
, which in turn closely follows
[
Win02
, §0.2]
.

Remark 5.2.4
.

Once again, let the Hodge structure on
M_{B}
be given by the map

h:\mathbb{S}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{R}}\subset\GL(M_{B})_{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
h_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the base change to
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}:(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the Hodge cocharacter, given by
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}(z):=h_{\mathbb{C}}(z,1)
.
By definition of the Mumford–Tate group as the smallest group defined over
\mathbb{Q}
containing the image of
h
,
the images of
\sigma^{*}h_{\mathbb{C}}
for
\sigma\in\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C})
must generate
G_{MT}
as an algebraic group. One can check that the images of
\sigma^{*}\bm{\mu^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{C}}
also generate
G_{MT}
as an algebraic group, which is defined in
[
Bor98
, §1]
as
\pi_{1}(G_{MT})=X_{*}/\langle R^{\vee}\rangle
, the quotient of the cocharacter lattice
X_{*}
by the coroot lattice
\langle R^{\vee}\rangle
. Each cocharacter
\sigma^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}
defines a
G_{MT}
-conjugacy class of cocharacters and equivalently a Weyl group orbit of cocharacters landing in a specific maximal torus. The Weyl group acts trivially on the quotient
\pi_{1}(G_{MT})
, so the
\sigma^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}
define elements of
\pi_{1}(G_{MT})
. The fact that the images of these cocharacters generate
G_{MT}
as a group means that they generate a full-rank sublattice of
X_{*}
, and therefore the images
(\sigma^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime})_{*}(\pi_{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
generate a finite-index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(G_{MT})
.

One can check that the condition that
h
is Hodge-maximal is equivalent to the condition that the subgroups
\sigma^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}(\pi_{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
for all
\sigma\in\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C})
generate the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G_{MT})
.

5.2.5.

We do not necessarily know that our motive
M
is Hodge-maximal. There exists a
G^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}
and a central isogeny
G^{\prime}\to G_{MT}
such that the lift

is itself Hodge-maximal. Indeed, the group

corresponds to the finite cover of

defined by the finite-index subgroup of

generated by the

for all

(see

One may lift the Galois representations to

. We also fix a finite place

of

5.3.1.
Hodge cocharacter

As in
Remark

5.2.4
, let the Hodge structure on
M_{B,\tilde{v}}=H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})/\text{torsion}
be given by the map
h:\mathbb{S}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{R}}\subset\GL(M_{B,\tilde{v}})_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
h_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the base change to
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}:(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}\to(G_{MT})_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the Hodge cocharacter. In particular, for an element
x\in(M_{B,\tilde{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{p,q}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}(z)(x)=z^{-p}x

[
Mil05
, §2, p. 26]
.
This gives the Hodge decomposition

(5.1)

\displaystyle H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\cong H^{w}_{HT}(X/F)\otimes_{F}\mathbb{C}.

Here, we define the Hodge cohomology

H^{w}_{HT}(X/F):=\gr^{*}H^{w}_{\dR}(X/F)

as the graded
F
-vector space associated to the filtered de Rham cohomology.

Because we have fixed the embedding
\tilde{v}:\overline{F}\to\mathbb{C}
, we get a canonical isomorphism
[
AGV73
, Exposé XI]

H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{C}\cong H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}.

So we may view
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}:(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}\to\GL(H^{w}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes\mathbb{C})
.

Let
c\in\Aut(\mathbb{C})\cong\Aut(C)
denote complex conjugation.
The
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\displaystyle(H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{C},c}\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{c}H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle(x\otimes z)\otimes z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mapsto x\otimes\overline{z}z.

puts a new grading on the left-hand side using the Hodge decomposition on the right-hand side.

We can identify the left-hand side
(H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{C},c}\mathbb{C}
with
H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}
as
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces in a set-theoretic way, where
(x\otimes z)\otimes z^{\prime}
corresponds to
x\otimes z\overline{z}^{\prime}
. This new grading on the left-hand side, now viewed as
H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}
, defines a new cocharacter which we denote

(5.2)

\displaystyle c^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}:(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}\to\GL(H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}).

Hodge symmetry dictates that the
p
th graded piece on
H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}
with respect to the grading from
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}
corresponds to the
(w-p)
th graded piece on
H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}
with respect to the grading from
c^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}
. In other words, we have the relation

(5.3)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}\cdot c^{*}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}_{v}=\bm{w}^{-1},

where
\bm{w}^{-1}:(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}\to\GL(H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C})
is the inverse of the weight cocharacter and acts on
H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}
via
\bm{w}^{-1}(z):v\mapsto z^{-w}v
.

5.3.2.
Hodge–Tate cocharacter

By
[
Fal89
, Theorem 8.1]
(and ignoring the Galois action), there is a canonical isomorphism of graded
C
-vector spaces

(5.4)

\displaystyle H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C\cong H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F_{v}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C\cong H^{w}_{HT}(X/F)\otimes_{F,v}C,

which gives rise to what is known as the Hodge–Tate decomposition on the left-hand side.
The first isomorphism follows from smooth base change.
Let

(5.5)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{v}:(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{C}\to\GL(H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C)

denote the cocharacter corresponding to the Hodge–Tate decomposition.

5.4.
Absolute Hodge conjecture and compatibility with
D_{\dR}

Another assumption we need to make is to ensure that we can compare the Hodge cocharacter with respect to
\tilde{v}
and Hodge–Tate cocharacter with respect to
v
. In particular, we want these two characters to be conjugate by an element in
G_{MT}(C)
. Moreover, we want to translate the relation of Hodge cocharacters from (
5.3
) to a relation on Hodge–Tate cocharacters. This relation is what allows us to lower-bound the change in height in both the fixed prime and large prime cases of the proof of the main theorem.

5.4.1.

Fix a choice
v:\overline{F}\hookrightarrow C
and an isomorphism
C\cong\mathbb{C}
, which yields
\tilde{v}:\overline{F}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
The choice of
\tilde{v}|_{F}:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
defines a comparison isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\cong H^{w}_{\dR}(X/F)\otimes_{F}\mathbb{C}.

The choice of
\tilde{v}:\overline{F}\to\mathbb{C}
defines a comparison isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}_{f}\cong\left(\prod_{p}H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.

For convenience, we will write

H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{A}_{f}):=\left(\prod_{p}H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.

These together define the isomorphism

(5.6)

\displaystyle H^{w}_{B}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{A}_{f}\times\mathbb{C})\cong H^{w}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{A}_{f})\times\left(H^{w}_{\dR}(X/F)\otimes_{F}\mathbb{C}\right).

Conjecture 5.4.2

(Hodge classes are absolute Hodge, cf. Theorem 2.11,
[
Del82
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a algebraically closed field

of finite transcendence degree over

. Suppose that a pair

comes from a Hodge class under the comparison isomorphism with Betti cohomology.

Remark 5.4.3
.

We say that such pair

satisfying the statement of the conjecture is an

. The conjecture is saying that all Hodge classes for any embedding

are absolute Hodge classes.

The absolute Hodge conjecture was proved by Deligne for abelian varieties
[
Del82
]
.

Lemma 6.1.7

By

Lemma 6.2.6

(
[

,
Tag 0ASP

Every finitely presented

Note that

Then, for each of those finitely many primes

shows that there can only be finitely many height values of elements in

The rest of this section is dedicated to proving

to make sure

7.1.
Switching to subobjects instead of quotients of BK modules

Suppose that
f:{M}^{\prime}\to{M}
is a
p
-power isogeny such that
T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime}
is
p
-torsion.
There is a map of Koshikawa motives
g:{M}\to{M}^{\prime}
such that
f\circ g:{M}\to{M}
is the
p
-power isogeny defined by the injection
T_{p}\xrightarrow{\times p}T_{p}
. We can thus compute

\displaystyle h({M}^{\prime})-h({{M}})

\displaystyle=\log\#(T_{p}/T_{p}^{\prime})\left(\frac{w}{2}-\nu(T_{p},T_{p}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle h({M})-h({{M}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\log\#(T_{p}^{\prime}/pT_{p})\left(\frac{w}{2}-\nu(T_{p}^{\prime},pT_{p})\right).

For a given prime
p
, there is a finite Galois extension
F^{\prime}/F
such that all of the finitely many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-submodules
T_{0}
such that
pT_{p}\subset T_{0}\subset T_{p}
are stable under the action of
\Gal_{F^{\prime}}
. In other words, the action of
\Gal_{F}
on such sublattices
T_{0}
factors through the finite quotient
\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)
.
From
Lemma

4.6.1
, we have

(7.1)

\displaystyle\nu(T_{p}^{\prime},pT_{p})=\sum_{v\mid p}\frac{[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\cdot\frac{1}{|\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)|}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)}\mu\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T_{p}^{\prime})}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(pT_{p})}\right)

The above expression involves slopes of various submodules of
\mathfrak{M}(T_{p}/pT_{p})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S},\varphi}A_{\inf}=\mathfrak{M}(T_{p})
. Given the data of an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace
L
of
T_{p}/pT_{p}
, one can associate the submodule

(7.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}(L):=(L\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}C^{\flat})\cap(\mathfrak{M}(T)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C^{\flat}}).

Observe that this is analogous to the definition of the BKF module
\mathfrak{M}(L)
in
6.1
, as both come from the canonical identification of
\mathfrak{M}(T)\otimes_{A_{\inf}}W(C^{\flat})\cong T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}W(C^{\flat})
. In particular,
\mathfrak{N}(\sigma T_{p}^{\prime}/pT_{p})=\frac{\mathfrak{M}(\sigma T_{p}^{\prime})}{\mathfrak{M}(pT_{p})}
, where the right-hand side is viewed as a torsion BKF module. To summarize, we have the following.

(7.3)

\displaystyle\nu(T_{p}^{\prime},pT_{p})=\sum_{v\mid p}\frac{[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\cdot\frac{1}{|\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)|}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)}\mu\left(\mathfrak{N}(\sigma\cdot L)\right),

where
L:=T_{p}^{\prime}/pT_{p}\subset T_{p}/pT_{p}
.

7.2.
Replacing Galois group action with an algebraic group action

The adelic Mumford–Tate assumption (
5.2.8
) ensures that there is a finite extension
E/F
such that for
p\gg 0
,

\imag(\Gal_{E}\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))=\imag(G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\pi}G_{MT}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))

In particular, the degree
[E:F]
provides a bound independent of
p
on the index of the subgroup

\imag(G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\xrightarrow{\pi}G_{MT}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))=\imag(\Gal_{E}\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))\subseteq\imag(\Gal_{F}\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{F}_{p})).

Therefore we can replace the summation over a Galois orbit in (
7.3
) with summation over a
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
-orbit: choose
t\leq[E:F]
coset representatives
h_{1},\ldots,h_{t}
for
\pi(G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))
in
\imag(\Gal_{F}\to G_{MT}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))
. We may also assume without loss of generality that
F/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, so that
[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]/[F:\mathbb{Q}]=1/s(p)
, where
s(p)
is the number of places of
F
above
p
. Then

(7.4)

\displaystyle\nu(T_{p}^{\prime},pT_{p})=\frac{1}{s(p)t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\mid p\\
j=1,\ldots,t\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\mu\left(\mathfrak{N}(g^{\prime}\cdot h_{j}L)\right).

The problem is then reduced to studying the variation of slopes of torsion BK submodules
\mathfrak{N}(g^{\prime}\cdot h_{j}L)
in a given BK module
\mathfrak{M}(T_{p})\bmod p
for all
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, where
h_{j}L\subset T_{p}/p
is a fixed
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector subspace.

In order to obtain lower bounds on such slopes which depend on
p
, Kisin–Mocz work with
p
-torsion BK modules of Fontaine–Laffaille type, whose structure and slopes are more tractable to study.
In particular, Fontaine–Laffaille BK modules have a good structure theory as extensions of irreducible FL modules, and moreover, the Hodge–Tate grading on
\mathbb{V}(\mathfrak{M})
can be matched with the structure of the cokernel of
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}}
. This allows us to link the slope of a torsion BK module of FL type to the Hodge filtration, which ultimately provides the bounds on the slope to prove the main result.

In addition, replacing the Galois group action with the action of an algebraic group allows us to use results from algebraic geometry and representations of algebraic groups to study the average slope
\mu(\mathfrak{N}(g^{\prime}L))
as
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
varies.

7.3.
Fontaine–Laffaille modules

Let
K
be an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with a discrete valuation. In this subsection, we will assume that
k
, the residue field of
K
, is algebraically closed, and that
K
is totally unramified, i.e.
K=K_{0}
, and we let
E(u)=u-p
.

Definition 7.3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}\in\Mod^{\varphi}_{/k[[u]]}
, i.e. a torsion Breuil-Kisin module which is a finite free
k[[u]]
-module. We say that
\mathfrak{M}
is of
Fontaine–Laffaille (FL)
type if
\mathfrak{M}
is effective and satisfies the following condition: there exist bases of
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M}
such that the map
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}}
is given by a diagonal matrix with entries
u^{i_{1}},\ldots,u^{i_{r}}
with
i_{j}\in[0,p-1]
.

We write
\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}\subset\Mod^{\varphi}_{/k[[u]]}
for the full subcategory of modules of FL type.

Remark 7.3.2
.

Note that the definition implies that
\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}\subset\Mod^{\varphi,\leq p-1}_{/k[[u]]}
. We will denote by
\Mod^{\varphi,\leq h,FL}_{/k[[u]]}\subset\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}
the full subcategory of objects in
\Mod^{\varphi,\leq h}_{/k[[u]]}
.

The following lemma is due to Kisin–Mocz. Recall the definition of the multiset
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{M})
from
3.3.4
.

Lemma 7.3.3

(
[
KM
]
, Lemma 3.3.5)
.

The subcategory
\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}\subset\Mod^{\varphi,\leq p-1}_{/k[[u]]}
is closed under quotients and saturated subobjects. Moreover, for extensions in
\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}
of the form

0\to\mathfrak{M}_{1}\to\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}_{2}\to 0,

we have that
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{M})=\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{M}_{1})\sqcup\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{M}_{2})
as multisets.

This property of
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{M})
allows one to reduce studying the valuations of irreducible modules of FL type.
All modules in
\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}
, being Noetherian, can be written as a finite number of extensions of irreducible modules of FL type. These irreducible modules
\mathfrak{M}_{irr}
all take an explicit form.
Using this, Kisin–Mocz provide a lower bound of the slope of any saturated rank 1
\varphi
-stable submodule
\mathfrak{N}\subset\mathfrak{M}_{irr}
in terms of
\mu_{\max}(\mathfrak{M}_{irr})
.
They then combine this with
Lemma

7.3.3
to give the following lower bound on the slope of a generic saturated rank 1
\varphi
-stable submodule
\mathfrak{N}(L)\subset\mathfrak{M}
coming from an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-line
L\subset\mathbb{V}(\mathfrak{M})
for any
\mathfrak{M}
of FL type.

Corollary 7.3.4

(
[
KM
]
, Corollary 3.4.13)
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{M}\in\Mod^{\varphi,FL}_{/k[[u]]}
. Then there exists a proper subspace
W\subset\mathbb{V}(\mathfrak{M})
such that for any
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-line
L\subset\mathbb{V}(\mathfrak{M})
with
L\not\subset W
, we have

\mu(\mathfrak{N}(L))\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\mu_{\max}(\mathfrak{M}).

Kisin and Mocz are able to work with the relatively well-behaved FL modules for the varying prime case due to the following result of
[
GLS15
]
(see
[
KM
, Proposition 3.3.8]
): For sufficiently large primes

, if an effective BK module
\mathfrak{M}
comes from the mod

, then
\mathfrak{M}
will be of FL type and the valuations of

are
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{M})=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
, matching the Hodge–Tate weights with multiplicity, up to flipping the sign of the weights.

7.3.5.

This section closely follows
[
KM
, §3.5]
.
Recall that we assume that our Galois representation takes a certain form, namely

. In our situation,

We can then view this process as a functor

The same can be repeated in the

Within the category

Suppose that we are consider a

of

7.3.6.

The upshot of the above paragraph is that for

7.4.
Bound on slopes

We once again use the adelic MT conjecture, absolute Hodge conjecture and compatibility with

assumption (

,

,
5.4.2

Lemma 7.4.1

(
[
KM
]
, Lemma 3.6.5)
.

Fix a place

extending

of

.
Let
\bm{\mu}_{v}
be the Hodge–Tate cocharacter for the

Remark 7.4.2

(1)

(2)

By applying

Corollary 7.4.3

,

, and

for

Assume that

does not preserve

Then there is a constant

\displaystyle\frac{w}{2}-\nu(T_{p},T^{\prime}_{p})\geq\frac{1}{2[F:\mathbb{Q}](\rk(T_{p})-1)}\left(1-\frac{c_{M}}{p}\right).

Observe that the condition on

Proof.

Since we are only working with

to denote

and

, respectively.
Let’s first assume that

.
Suppose that for every

and all

Therefore, for at least one

\displaystyle\nu(T^{\prime},pT)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{V}}{p}\right)\left(w+\frac{1}{s(p)}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{V}}{p}\right)\left(w+\frac{1}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\right).

Now, assume that

is as in the statement. Let

. For

This defines a fiiltration

\frac{T^{i}}{T^{i+1}}\xhookrightarrow{\times p^{n-1-i}}\frac{T^{n-1}}{T^{n}}=\frac{p^{n-1}T\cap T^{\prime}}{p^{n}T}\subseteq\frac{p^{n-1}T}{p^{n}T}\xrightarrow[\sim]{\times p^{-(n-1)}}\frac{T}{pT},

One can check that this yields inclusions

Because of the natural isomorphisms

where we use the notions of degree and rank of an effective torsion BKF module from

By construction,
\mathfrak{M}(T^{\prime})/\mathfrak{M}(p^{n}T)

for

Let

(7.8)

\displaystyle\nu(T^{\prime},p^{n}T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s(p)d^{\prime}}\sum_{v\mid p}\int_{\Gal_{F}}\deg\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T^{\prime})}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(p^{n}T)}\right)d\sigma.

The useful step is that this quantity is additive in short exact sequences. In particular,

(7.10)

\displaystyle\nu(T^{\prime},p^{n}T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s(p)d^{\prime}}\sum_{v\mid p}\int_{\Gal_{F}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\deg\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T^{i})}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T^{i+1})}\right)d\sigma.

for any
\sigma\in\Gal_{F}

(7.11)

\displaystyle\nu(T^{\prime},p^{n}T)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{s(p)d^{\prime}}\sum_{v\mid p}\int_{\Gal_{F}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\deg\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T_{i}^{\prime\prime})}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(pT)}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\log_{p}\#(T_{i}^{\prime\prime}/pT)\left(\frac{1}{s(p)}\sum_{v\mid p}\int_{\Gal_{F}}\mu\left(\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(\sigma T_{i}^{\prime\prime})}{\mathfrak{M}_{v}(pT)}\right)d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\log_{p}\#(T_{i}^{\prime\prime}/pT)\;\nu(T_{i}^{\prime\prime},pT)

p

\displaystyle\nu(T^{\prime},p^{n}T)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{V}}{p}\right)\left(w+\frac{1}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\right)\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\log_{p}\#(T_{i}^{\prime\prime}/pT).

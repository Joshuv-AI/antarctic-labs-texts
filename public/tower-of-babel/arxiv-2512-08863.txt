Segre classes and integral dependence
Yairon Cid-Ruiz

Abstract
. A fundamental property of Segre classes is their birational invariance. This invariance implies that the Segre class of a closed subscheme only depends on the integral closure of the defining ideal sheaf. In this paper, we show that, conversely, the Segre class of a closed subscheme encodes an integral dependence criterion for its defining ideal sheaf. As an application, we prove that Aluffi’s Segre zeta function provides an integral dependence criterion for homogeneous ideals in polynomial rings.

1.
Introduction

The
Segre class
associated with an embedding of schemes plays a central role in Fulton–MacPherson’s intersection theory and underlies numerous applications.
A detailed account of Segre classes and their applications can be found in
[
15
,
3
]
.

A central and defining property of Segre classes is their
birational invariance
.
Indeed, if
Z
is a proper closed subvariety of a variety
X
over a field
\mathbb{k}
, this invariance yields

s(Z,X)\;=\;\eta_{*}\left(s(E,\mathcal{B})\right)\;=\;\eta_{*}\left(c\left(N_{E}\mathcal{B}\right)^{-1}\smallfrown[E]\right)\;=\;\eta_{*}\left(\frac{[E]}{1+E}\right)\;\in\;A_{*}(Z),

where
\pi:\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)\rightarrow X
is the blow-up of
X
along
Z
,
\eta:E=E_{Z}X\rightarrow Z
is the exceptional divisor, and
N_{E}\mathcal{B}
is the normal bundle to
E
in
\mathcal{B}
(see
[
15
, §4.2]
).
Thus, by birational invariance, computing Segre classes reduces to the case of effective Cartier divisors, whose Segre classes admit an explicit formula since they are regularly embedded.

The birational invariance of Segre classes also implies that they depend only on the
integral closure
of the underlying ideal sheaf.
More precisely, if we let
Z^{\prime}=V\left(\overline{\mathscr{I}_{Z}}\right)\subset X
where
\overline{\mathscr{I}_{Z}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
is the integral closure of the ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
of
Z
, then the Segre class
s(Z,X)\in A_{*}(Z)
of
Z
equals the Segre class
s(Z^{\prime},X)\in A_{*}(Z^{\prime})
of
Z^{\prime}
(since
Z^{\prime}_{\rm red}=Z_{\rm red}
, we can identify their Chow groups).

Our main result demonstrates that Segre classes, in fact, determine integral dependence, leading to the following criterion:

Theorem A

(
Theorem 4.3
)
.

Let
X
be an equidimensional projective scheme over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
\mathscr{L}
be an ample line bundle on
X
and
W\subseteq Z
be two closed subschemes of
X
.
Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathscr{I}_{W}
is integral over
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
.

(b)

s(Z,X)=s(W,X)
viewed in
A_{*}(X)
.

(c)

\deg_{\mathscr{L}}\left(s^{i}(Z,X)\right)=\deg_{\mathscr{L}}\left(s^{i}(W,X)\right)
for all
i\geq 0
.

In
Theorem A
, even if one is not interested in integral dependence, the equivalence (b)
\Leftrightarrow
(c) seems interesting in its own right.
We note that
Theorem A
is sharp in the sense that the ampleness hypothesis appears to be indispensable.

Section 4
illustrates this by exhibiting a big and nef line bundle for which the conclusion of
Theorem A
does not hold.

The proof of
Theorem A
relies on van Gastel’s foundational result
[
39
]
, which establishes the connection between Fulton–MacPherson’s intersection theory
[
15
]
and Stückrad–Vogel’s intersection theory
[
41
,
14
]
.
Indeed, our starting point is van Gastel’s result expressing Segre classes in terms of Vogel cycles, and conversely expressing Vogel cycles in terms of Segre classes.
An advantage of considering the Vogel cycle is that it opens the door to applying technical positivity results, including the one established in
Section 4
.

As an application of
Theorem A
, we show that Aluffi’s Segre zeta function
[
2
]
furnishes a criterion for the integral dependence of homogeneous ideals in a polynomial ring.
This power series records information about the Segre classes that arise when the ideal is extended to projective spaces of arbitrarily large dimension (see
Section 5
for further details).
We obtain the following corollary:

Corollary B

(
Section 5
)
.

Let
I\subseteq J\subset R
be two homogeneous ideals in a polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(a)

J
is integral over
I
.

(b)

\zeta_{I}(t)=\zeta_{J}(t)
.

The idea of detecting integral dependence through numerical invariants originates in Rees’ seminal work
[
30
]
.
Rees proved that in an equidimensional and universally catenary Noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
, two
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideals
I\subseteq J
have the same integral closure if and only if they share the same Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity.
Since then, the search for numerical criteria to characterize integral dependence has been an important research topic in algebraic geometry, commutative algebra and singularity
theory.
Much effort has gone into extending Rees’ theorem to arbitrary ideals, modules, and more broadly, to algebras (see
[
6
,
35
,
36
,
23
,
24
,
16
,
13
,
31
,
17
,
11
,
37
,
38
,
29
,
8
,
9
,
7
,
5
,
12
]
).
In this direction, two notable results are:

–

Teissier’s Principle of Specialization of Integral Dependence (PSID)
[
35
]
,
[
36
, Appendice I]
.
Given a map of germs of analytic spaces, the PSID asserts that
for a family of zero-dimensional ideals with constant Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity, a section is integrally
dependent on the total family if and only if it is integrally dependent along the fibers corresponding to a dense open subset of the base.

–

An extension of Rees’ theorem to arbitrary ideals (in an equidimensional and universally catenary Noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
) was established by Polini, Trung, Ulrich, and Validashti
[
29
]
, using the multiplicity sequence introduced by Achilles and Manaresi
[
1
]
. (In the analytic setting, an analogous result had been obtained earlier by Gaffney and Gassler
[
16
]
.)

Note that these criteria are local in nature, whereas
Theorem A
furnishes a global criterion.

We recall some useful notation from

.
Let

be a subscheme of

We define the

if

2.5
.

Let

be an ideal in a commutative ring

.
The

\overline{I}\subset A
of

the ideal given by

\overline{\,I\,}\;:=\;\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}f\in A&f^{n}+a_{1}f^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}=0\;\text{ where $n\geq 1$ and $a_{i}\in I^{i}$ for $1\leq i\leq n$}\end{array}\Big\}.

2.6
.

Let
X
be a scheme and
\mathscr{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
be an ideal sheaf in
X
.
The
integral closure

\overline{\mathscr{I}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
of
\mathscr{I}
is the sheaf associated to the presheaf

U\subset X\;\mapsto\;\overline{\mathscr{I}(U)}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}(U).

2.7
.

Let
X
be a scheme and
\mathscr{I}\subset\mathcal{O}
be an ideal sheaf in
X
.
The
Rees algebra
of
\mathscr{I}
is given by

\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{I})\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{I}^{n}.

The
blow-up
of
X
along a closed subscheme
Z\subset X
is given by
{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X):=\fProj_{X}\left(\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\right)
.

2.8
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme and
\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathscr{J}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
be two ideal sheaves in
X
.
We say that
\mathscr{J}
is integral over
\mathscr{I}
if one the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(a)

\overline{\mathscr{I}}=\overline{\mathscr{J}}
.

(b)

\mathscr{I}\cdot\mathscr{J}^{n}=\mathscr{J}^{n+1}
for all
n\gg 0
.

(c)

\mathscr{R}\left(\mathscr{J}\right)
is of finite type as a module over
\mathscr{R}\left(\mathscr{I}\right)
.

(d)

The inclusion
\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{I})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{J})
induces finite morphism
{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{W}(X)\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)
of blow-ups, where
Z=V(\mathscr{I})\subset X
and
W=V(\mathscr{J})\subset X
are the corresponding closed subschemes of
X
.

3.
Segre classes, Vogel cycles, and polar cycles

This section establishes the connection between Segre classes, Vogel cycles, and polar cycles.
Our main tool will be the fundamental result of van Gastel
[
39
]
expressing Segre classes in terms of Vogel cycles and vice versa (also, see
[
14
, Chapter 2]
).
For any unexplained notation regarding intersection theory, the reader is referred to
[
15
]
.

Setup 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
X
be an equidimensional proper scheme over
\mathbb{k}
, and
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on
X
.
Let
d:=\dim(X)
be the dimension of
X
.
Let
r\geq 1
and
\underline{\sigma}=\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\in\text{H}^{0}(X,\mathscr{L})
be global sections of
\mathscr{L}
.
Let
Z:=V(\sigma_{1})\cap\cdots\cap V(\sigma_{r})\subset X
be the closed subscheme given by intersecting the sets of zeros of the sections
\underline{\sigma}
.

Consider the purely transcendental field extension

\mathbb{L}\;:=\;\mathbb{k}\left(u_{i,j}\,\mid\,1\leq i,j\leq r\right)

where
u_{i,j}
are new indeterminates.
We write
X_{\mathbb{L}}:=X\times_{\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})}\text{Spec}(\mathbb{L})
and
Z_{\mathbb{L}}:=Z\times_{\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})}\text{Spec}(\mathbb{L})
.
We now discuss the intersection algorithm that leads to the construction of the Vogel cycle.

Definition 3.2

(Intersection Algorithm; see
[
39
]
,
[
14
, Chapter 2]
)
.

Let
D_{i}:=V(s_{i})
be the set of zeros of the generic linear combination

s_{i}\;:=\;\sum_{j=1}^{r}u_{i,j}\sigma_{j}.

Inductively, we define two sequences
\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)
and
\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)
of cycles for
0\leq i\leq r
.

– (Initial Step):

Decompose
[X_{\mathbb{L}}]=\nu^{0}(\underline{\sigma},X)+\beta^{0}(\underline{\sigma},X)
, where
\nu^{0}(\underline{\sigma},X):=\left[X_{\mathbb{L}}\right]^{Z_{\mathbb{L}}}
is the part of the fundamental cycle
[X_{\mathbb{L}}]
supported by
Z_{\mathbb{L}}
, and
\beta^{0}(\underline{\sigma},X):=\left[X_{\mathbb{L}}\right]^{X_{\mathbb{L}}\setminus Z_{\mathbb{L}}}
is the rest.

– (Inductive Step):

Assume that
\nu^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)
and
\beta^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)
have been computed.
We obtain that
D_{i}
restricts to an effective Cartier divisor on
\beta^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)
.
Consider the intersection of
\beta^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)
with the divisor
D_{i}
.
Decompose this intersection as

(1)

D_{i}\,\cdot\,\beta^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;=\;\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)+\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X),

where

\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;:=\;\left[D_{i}\,\cdot\,\beta^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)\right]^{Z_{\mathbb{L}}}

is the part of the intersection supported by
Z_{\mathbb{L}}
, and

\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;:=\;\left[D_{i}\,\cdot\,\beta^{i-1}(\underline{\sigma},X)\right]^{X_{\mathbb{L}}\setminus Z_{\mathbb{L}}}

is the rest.

We have that
\beta^{r}(\underline{\sigma},X)=0
, so we set
\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X):=0
and
\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X):=0
for
i>r
.
The cycles
\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)
and
\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)
(if nonzero) are effective cycles of pure dimension
d-i
on
Z_{\mathbb{L}}
and on
X_{\mathbb{L}}
, respectively.

Definition 3.3

(Vogel cycle; see
[
39
]
,
[
14
, Chapter 2]
)
.

The
Vogel cycle
of
\underline{\sigma}
on
X
is the cycle

\nu(\underline{\sigma},X)\;:=\;\sum_{i\geq 0}\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;\;\in\;\;Z_{*}\left(Z_{\mathbb{L}}\right).

Definition 3.4

(Polar cycle)
.

The
polar cycle
of
\underline{\sigma}
on
X
is the cycle

\beta(\underline{\sigma},X)\;:=\;\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;\in\;Z_{*}\left(X_{\mathbb{L}}\right).

The reason for calling
\beta(\underline{\sigma},X)
the polar cycle will become clear after
Section 3
.

Remark 3.5
.

By a result of Fulton (see
[
39
, Lemma 1.4]
,
[
14
, Proposition 2.1.8]
), we have a natural isomorphism

A_{*}(Y)\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;A_{*}\left(Y\times_{\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})}\text{Spec}(\mathbb{L})\right),\qquad\left[V\right]\mapsto\left[V\times_{\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})}\text{Spec}(\mathbb{L})\right],

for any
\mathbb{k}
-scheme
Y
of finite type.

Hence, under this natural isomorphism, we can make the identifications

\nu(\underline{\sigma},X)\;\in\;A_{*}(Z)\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta(\underline{\sigma},X)\;\in\;A_{*}(X)

(i.e., we can consider rational equivalence classes over
\mathbb{k}
instead of
\mathbb{L}
).

Let
C:=C_{Z}X:=\fSpec_{Z}({\text{gr}}_{\mathscr{I}_{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))
be the normal cone to
Z
in
X
, where

{\text{gr}}_{\mathscr{I}_{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n+1}

is the associated graded ring of
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
q:{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)\rightarrow Z
be the projective completion of the cone
C
.
The
i
-th Segre class
of
Z
in
X
is given by

s^{i}(Z,X)\;:=\;s^{i}(C)\;:=\;q_{*}\Big(c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)}(1))^{i}\smallfrown\left[{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)\right]\Big)

where
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)}(1)
is the canonical line bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)
.
Then

s(Z,X)\;:=\;\sum_{i\geq 0}s^{i}(Z,X)

is the (total)
Segre class
of
Z
in
X
.

Remark 3.7
.

Since
X
is equidimensional of dimension
d
, we get that
{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)
is also equidimensional of dimension
d
(see, e.g.,
[
15
, §B.5]
), and so it follows that
s^{i}(Z,X)\in A_{d-i}(Z)
.

We also consider the following twisted version of the normal cone

C^{\mathscr{L}}\;:=\;C_{Z}X\otimes\mathscr{L}\;:=\;\fSpec_{Z}\left(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n+1}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n}\right).

An advantage of
C^{\mathscr{L}}
over
C
is the following: the first graded part
\mathscr{I}_{Z}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{L}
of
C^{\mathscr{L}}
is generated by global sections.
Indeed, by construction
\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathscr{L}
is generated by the sections
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}
, and so the coherent sheaf
\mathscr{I}_{Z}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{L}
is generated by the initial forms
\sigma_{1}^{*},\ldots,\sigma_{r}^{*}\in\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathscr{I}_{Z}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{L}\big)
.

We now present van Gastel’s fundamental result relating Segre classes and Vogel cycles.

Theorem 3.8

(van Gastel; see
[
39
, Corollary 3.7]
,
[
14
, Corollary 2.4.7]
)
.

Modulo rational equivalence on
Z
, for all
i\geq 0
, we have the equalities

\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;=\;s^{i}\big(C_{Z}X\otimes\mathscr{L}\big)\;=\;\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i-1}{j-1}c_{1}(\mathscr{L})^{i-j}\smallfrown s^{j}(Z,X)\;\in\;A_{d-i}(Z)

and

s^{i}(Z,X)\;=\;\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i-1}{j-1}{(-1)}^{i-j}c_{1}(\mathscr{L})^{i-j}\smallfrown\nu^{j}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;\in\;A_{d-i}(Z).

In particular, the Vogel cycle
\nu(\underline{\sigma},X)
and the Segre class
s(Z,X)
determine each other modulo rational equivalence on
Z
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)=\fProj_{X}\left(\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\right)
be the blow-up of
X
along
Z
, where
\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}
is the Rees algebra of the ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}_{Z}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Let
\pi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow X
be the natural projection.
Consider the twisted Rees algebra

\mathscr{R}^{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n}

and the twisted blow-up
\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}:={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}^{\mathscr{L}}(X):=\fProj_{X}\left(\mathscr{R}^{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\right)
.
We have a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}\cong\mathcal{B}
with the identification

(2)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}}(1)\;\cong\;\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(1)\otimes\pi^{*}(\mathscr{L});

see, e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma II.7.9]
.

Remark 3.9
.

Since
\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathscr{L}
is generated by the global sections
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}
, we obtain a natural surjection

\text{Sym}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r}\right)\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\mathscr{R}^{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})

that yields the closed immersion

\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}=\fProj_{X}\left(\mathscr{R}^{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\right)\;\subset\;\fProj_{X}\left(\text{Sym}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r}\right)\right)\;=\;X\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r-1}.

Denoting by
p:\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}\subset X\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r-1}
the natural projection, we get
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}}(1)\cong p^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r-1}}(1)\big)
.

Remark 3.10
.

The irreducible components of
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)
are mapped (via the projection
\pi
) birationally onto the irreducible components of
X
not contained in
Z
.
Consequently, since
X
is equidimensional of dimension
d
, it follows that either
\mathcal{B}
is equidimensional of dimension
d
or
\mathcal{B}=\varnothing
(the latter case happens when
Z_{\rm red}=X_{\rm red}
).

The following proposition gives a blow-up formula for the polar cycle
\beta(\underline{\sigma},X)
(cf.,
[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
,
[
7
, Theorem C(i)]
).
This blow-up formula is a consequence of
Theorem 3.8
.

Proposition 3.11

(Blow-up formula)
.

Modulo rational equivalence on
X
, for all
i\geq 0
, we have the equality

\beta^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;=\;\pi_{*}\left(c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}}(1)\right)^{i}\smallfrown\left[{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)\right]\right)\;\in\;A_{d-i}(X).

Proof.

We proceed by induction of
i
.
By
Section 3
, it follows that
\pi_{*}\left(\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)=[X]^{X\setminus Z}=\beta^{0}(\underline{\sigma},X)
.
Thus the initial case
i=0
is clear.

By utilizing
[
15
, Lemma 1.7.2]
,
[
15
, §B.5]
and the fact that
{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}}\oplus 1)
is equidimensional, we obtain
c_{1}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}}\oplus 1)}(1)\big)\smallfrown[{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}}\oplus 1)]=[{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}})]
.
Notice that
\eta:{\mathbb{P}}(C)\cong{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}})\rightarrow Z
is the exceptional divisor of the blow-up
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)
;
again, we have the similar identification
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}})}(1)\cong\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(C)}(1)\otimes\eta^{*}(\mathscr{L})
.
Thus, for
i\geq 1
,
Theorem 3.8
yields

(3)

\nu^{i}(\underline{\sigma},X)\;=\;\eta_{*}\left(c_{1}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(C^{\mathscr{L}})}(1)\big)^{i-1}\smallfrown[{\mathbb{P}}(C)]\right).

As
{\mathbb{P}}(C)
is the exceptional divisor of
\mathcal{B}
, we have

(4)

c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(-1))\smallfrown\left[\mathcal{B}\right]=\left[{\mathbb{P}}(C)\right].

We can now compute

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\left(c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}}(1)\right)^{i}\smallfrown\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}\left(\big(c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(1)\right)+c_{1}\left(\pi^{*}(\mathscr{L})\right)\big)c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}^{\mathscr{L}}}(1)\right)^{i-1}\smallfrown\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)\text{ \;\quad(by \autoref{eq_can_line_O(1)})}

.

Let

Then, by applying

\displaystyle s\left(Z,X\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\eta_{*}\big(s\left(E_{Z}X,\,{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z}(X)\right)\big)

\displaystyle\;=\;\eta_{*}\left(\psi_{*}\big(s\left(E_{W}X,\,{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{W}(X)\right)\big)\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\overline{\eta}_{*}\big(s\left(E_{W}X,\,{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{W}(X)\right)\big)

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c): This implication is clear by definition.

(c)
\Leftrightarrow
(d)

(e)

\text{H}^{0}\left(\mathscr{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{Y}}/\mathscr{I}_{y}^{n_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{Y}}(v+n_{0}\delta)\otimes\pi^{*}(\mathscr{L})^{\otimes m}\right)\;\cong\;\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{Y},y}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{y}^{n_{0}}

The function

[

It then follows that

Finally, we have that

because
\mathscr{Y}
is equidimensional of dimension

is a closed point.

∎

Example 4.7

(Line bundles that are big and nef do not suffice in

)
.

Assume

is algebraically closed.
Consider the following two successive blow-ups:

(i)

Let
\pi_{1}:Y={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{p}\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}\right)\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
be the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
along a closed point

Notice that the exceptional divisor
E_{p}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
of

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
.

(ii)

Choose a line
C

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\cong E_{p}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
.
Let
\pi_{2}:X={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{C}(Y)\rightarrow Y
be the blow-up of
Y
along
C
.
Notice that

is a smooth threefold.
Let
E=E_{C}Y
be the exceptional divisor of

.

Let
\pi=\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{2}:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
be the natural projection.
Let
\mathcal{L}=\pi^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(1)\big)
be the line bundle on
X
given as the pullback of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(1)

Notice that

Let

As closed subschemes, we have

(although they have the same support).
We also obtain that their Segre classes are different

here

denotes the self-intersection of

(see
[

However, we can compute the vanishings

\deg_{\mathcal{L}}\left(E\right)\;=\;\deg_{\mathcal{L}}\left(E^{2}\right)\;=\;\deg_{\mathcal{L}}\left(E^{3}\right)\;=\;0.

This shows that the big and nef line bundle

cannot be used in

.

Proof.

We only need to show the claimed vanishings.
The projection formula yields

\deg_{\mathcal{L}}(E)\;=\;\int c_{1}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(1)\big)^{2}\smallfrown\pi_{*}\left([E]\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\deg_{\mathcal{L}}(E^{2})\;=\;\int c_{1}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(1)\big)\smallfrown\pi_{*}\left(E\cdot[E]\right).

By construction,

is mapped onto the point

via
\pi

Since

and

(see
[
15
, §1.4]

It then follows that
\deg_{\mathcal{L}}\left(E\right)=\deg_{\mathcal{L}}\left(E^{2}\right)=0
.

It remains to show that
\deg_{\mathcal{L}}(E^{3})=\int E^{3}\cdot[X]=0
.
Let

be the normal bundle to
C
in
Y
.
We have the isomorphism

(see
[
15
, B.7.1]
).
Let
\eta:E\cong{\mathbb{P}}(N)\rightarrow C
be the natural projection and
\xi=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(N)}(1))

From

, Example 8.3.4]
, we have

Then we can compute

\displaystyle\int E^{3}\cdot[X]

5.
Segre zeta functions and integral dependence

In this section, we show that Aluffi’s Segre zeta function
[
2
]
yields an integral dependence criterion for homogeneous ideals in a polynomial ring.
This power series encodes information about the Segre classes that arise when the ideal is extended to projective spaces of arbitrarily large dimension.
Further developments regarding Segre zeta functions were made in
[
20
,
4
,
10
]
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
and
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal of
R
.
Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dotsc,f_{r}\in R
be homogeneous generators of
I
, with the degree of
f_{i}
equal to
d_{i}=\deg\left(f_{i}\right)
.
Denote by
\iota:Z\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
the closed subscheme defined by
I\subset R
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}=\text{Proj}(R)
.
For each integer
N\geq n
, we consider the following objects:

–

R^{N}=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{N}]
a polynomial ring containing
R
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}=\text{Proj}(R^{N})
.

–

\iota_{N}:Z^{N}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
the closed subscheme defined by the extension of
I
to
R^{N}
.

Aluffi
[
2
]
introduced and studied the
Segre zeta function

\zeta_{I}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!]
of the homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
.
This power series is characterized by the fact that, for all
N\geq n
, the class

\big(a_{0}+a_{1}H+\cdots+a_{N}H^{N}\big)\smallfrown\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right]\;\in\;A^{*}\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right)

equals the pushforward
{\iota_{N}}_{*}\left(s(Z^{N},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})\right)
to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
of the Segre class
s(Z^{N},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})
; here
H
denotes the class of a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
.
An important result concerning Segre zeta functions is their
rationality
.

Theorem 5.1

(Aluffi
[
2
]
)
.

We can write

\zeta_{I}(t)\;=\;\frac{P(t)}{(1+d_{1}t)(1+d_{2}t)\cdots(1+d_{r}t)}

where
P(t)\in\mathbb{N}[t]
is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.

By utilizing the birational invariance of Segre classes, Aluffi
[
2
]
showed that the Segre zeta function only depends on the integral closure of the ideal.
As a consequence of
Theorem A
, we obtain that, in fact, the Segre zeta function yields an integral dependence criterion.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
J\subset R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a homogeneous ideal such that
J\supseteq I
.
Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(a)

J
is integral over
I
.

(b)

\zeta_{I}(t)=\zeta_{J}(t)
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): This implication is a direct consequence of the birational invariance of Segre classes (see
[
2
, Proposition 5.12]
or the proof of the implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) in
Theorem 4.3
).

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a):
Assume that
\zeta_{I}(t)=\zeta_{J}(t)
.
Write
J=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{s})
, where the
g_{i}
’s are homogeneous generators.
With a similar notation as before, set
W=V(J)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
and
W^{N}=V(JR^{N})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
for all
N\geq n
.
Fix
N\geq\max\{r,s\}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{N})
be the graded irrelevant ideal in
R^{N}
.
By assumption, we have

\deg_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}}(1)}\left(s^{i}(Z^{N},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})\right)\;=\;\deg_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}}(1)}\left(s^{i}(W^{N},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})\right)\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\geq 0.

Then
Theorem A
yields

\overline{\mathscr{I}_{Z^{N}}}\;=\;\overline{\mathscr{I}_{W^{N}}}\;\subset\;\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}}.

This, in turn, leads to the following equality of saturated homogeneous ideals

\left(\overline{IR^{N}}:{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\;=\;\bigoplus_{v\geq 0}\,\text{H}^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N},\overline{\mathscr{I}_{Z^{N}}}(v)\big)\;=\;\bigoplus_{v\geq 0}\,\text{H}^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N},\overline{\mathscr{I}_{W^{N}}}(v)\big)\;=\;\left(\overline{JR^{N}}:{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right).

Since
N+1>\max\{r,s\}
, the irrelevant ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{N})
cannot be associated prime of
\overline{IR^{N}}
or
\overline{JR^{N}}
. Indeed, every associated prime of the integral closure of an ideal has height at most the minimal number of generators of the ideal (see, e.g.,
[
28
, Proposition 3.9 and Proposition 4.1]
,
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
).
It then follows that

\overline{IR^{N}}\;=\;\left(\overline{IR^{N}}:{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\;=\;\left(\overline{JR^{N}}:{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\;=\;\overline{JR^{N}}.

Finally, this implies that
\overline{\,I\,}=\overline{\,J\,}
, as required.
∎

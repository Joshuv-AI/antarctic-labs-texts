Acceleration for Polyak-Łojasiewicz Functions with a Gradient Aiming Condition
Julien Hermant

Abstract
It is known that when minimizing smooth Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) functions, momentum algorithms cannot significantly improve the convergence bound of gradient descent, contrasting with the acceleration phenomenon occurring in the strongly convex case. To bridge this gap, the literature has proposed strongly quasar-convex functions as an intermediate non-convex class, for which accelerated bounds have been suggested to persist. We show that this is not true in general: the additional structure of strong quasar-convexity does not suffice to guaranty better worst-case bounds for momentum compared to gradient descent. As an alternative, we study PL functions under an aiming condition that measures how well the descent direction points toward a minimizer. This perspective clarifies the geometric ingredient enabling provable acceleration by momentum when minimizing PL functions.

1.1
Contributions

Our work addresses what additional structure beyond the PL condition is required to obtain improved theoretical convergence bounds over gradient descent when using momentum. We summarize our contributions as follows:

(1.)

We show in Section
3
that, in contrast with existing interpretations, minimizing strongly quasar-convex functions with momentum does not necessarily yield better theoretical convergence bounds than gradient descent. This is because, in some cases, the PL condition admits tighter bounds that cannot be improved by momentum.

(2.)
We propose in Section
4
a different viewpoint by studying the convergence of PL functions under an additional
aiming condition

\left<\nabla f(x),x-x^{\ast}\right>\geq a\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert,

(1)

for
1\geq a>0
, which quantifies how well the descent direction aims toward a minimizer
x^{\ast}
. Using a stochastic parameterization of Nesterov momentum’s algorithm, we show that we obtain accelerated bounds when this alignment constant
a
is sufficiently large (Section
4.2
).

(3.)

We relax the aiming condition by requiring it to hold only on average along the optimization trajectory, recovering similar convergence rates (Section
5
).
It shows that acceleration can be achieved as long as (
1
) holds over a sufficiently large fraction of the optimization path,
better reflecting the behavior of practical optimization algorithms.

(4.)
We exhibit a two-dimensional PL function with a unique minimizer that fails to
satisfy (
1
). When minimizing this function, we empirically observe that the momentum mechanism initially drives the iterates away from the minimizer, causing gradient descent to outperform momentum methods during the early stages of optimization.

Gradient Descent and Gradient Flow

Among the most classical first-order algorithms is gradient descent (

\tilde{x}_{k+1}=\tilde{x}_{k}-\gamma\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k}).

It is the building block of many important algorithms used in modern applications

(

Writing gradient descent as
\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{\gamma}=-\nabla f(x_{k})

\tilde{x}_{k}\approx x_{k\gamma}

\dot{x}_{t}=-\nabla f(x_{t}).

Nesterov’s Momentum: Ode and Algorithm

If (

\tilde{x}_{k+1}=\tilde{x}_{k}+\underbrace{\alpha(\tilde{x}_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k-1})}_{\text{momentum}}-\underbrace{\eta\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})}_{\text{gradient step}}.

(HB)

In this work, we focus on the Nesterov momentum (
2.1
) version
(
66
)
.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}_{k}&=\tilde{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}(\tilde{z}_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k}),\\
\tilde{x}_{k+1}&=\tilde{y}_{k}-\gamma\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k}),\\
\tilde{z}_{k+1}&=\tilde{z}_{k}+\beta_{k}(\tilde{y}_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k})-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k}).\end{array}\right.

Although its formulation is slightly more involved than Polyak’s Heavy Ball,
the latter exhibits worse convergence guarantees in some settings
(
33
;
35
)

As for gradient descent, taking the limit of (
2.1
) as stepsize
\gamma

2.1

(

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}_{t}&=\eta_{t}(z_{t}-x_{t})-\gamma\nabla f(x_{t}),\\
\dot{z}_{t}&=\eta^{\prime}_{t}(x_{t}-z_{t})-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(x_{t}).\end{array}\right.

Since the strong connection between these ODEs and the corresponding algorithm has been established
(

, their study has gained a rising interest both in the convex

(

;

;

;

;

Continuized Parameterization

We use a specific parameterization of (
2.1
) with stochastic parameters that arises from the

method
(
26
)
. Precisely, for some
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\frac{\eta}{\eta+\eta^{\prime}}\left(1-e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}\right),

\displaystyle\beta_{k}=\eta^{\prime}\frac{\left(1-e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}\right)}{\eta^{\prime}+\eta e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}}.

With this parameterization, the algorithm is inherently stochastic, yielding convergence guarantees holding with high probability.
This viewpoint can be particularly well suited to non-convex optimization,
where deterministic parameterization may lead to weaker results
(
86
;
43
)
. Its analysis involves a Poisson-driven SDE; see the latter reference for a methodological introduction to this continuized viewpoint.

We first introduce a geometrical setting in which momentum-based acceleration is long established.

Definition 2.1
.

For
L>0
, we call
\text{LS}^{L}
the set of
L
-smooth functions, namely
f
such that
\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~f(x)-f(y)-\left<\nabla f(y),x-y\right>\leq\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\mu>0
, we call
\text{SC}^{\mu}
the set of
\mu
-strongly convex functions, namely
f
such that
\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~f(x)+\left<\nabla f(x),y-x\right>+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}\leq f(y).

Intuitively,

-smoothness and

-strong convexity provide global upper and lower bounds on the curvature of

.
For a

implies that any eigenvalue
\lambda(x)

is such that
\mu\leq\lambda(x)\leq L
, for any

. The ratio

, and characterizes how ill-conditioned the minimization problem is—a higher condition number typically reflecting a more difficult problem.
\text{SC}^{\mu}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
is a favorable setting for minimization: there exists a unique critical point

, satisfying

,
and linear convergence of function values is ensured by first order methods, namely

\log(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast})\leq-\Lambda t+\log(K(x_{0})),

being the

, replacing

by

and

by

.
In this work, we specifically focus on the convergence rate value

.
In the
\text{SC}^{\mu}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
regime, the improvement of (
2.1
) over (

) is well established

2.3
Relaxed setting

A first relaxation of
\text{SC}^{\mu}
and
\text{LS}^{L}
consists in lower and upper
global quadratic bounds of the function.

Definition 2.3
.

For
\mu_{0},L_{0}>0

\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu_{0}}
the set of functions

\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu_{0}}\cap\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}

A key relaxation of strong convexity that does not preclude this objective is the class of Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) functions
(

;

)
.

Definition 2.4
.

For
\mu>0
, we call
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
the set of functions

that satisfies
\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert^{2}\geq 2\mu(f(x)-f^{\ast}).

It immediately follows that for

, any critical point is a global minimizer of

. From an optimization perspective, two key results hold for the class
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{LS}^{L}

)
, and (ii) this rate is optimal among first-order algorithms

)
. As a consequence, (

) cannot yield any significant theoretical improvement over this class, see Table

. This motivates studying more structured non-convex relaxations of

. The class of strongly quasar-convex functions (SQC) is expected to fulfill the demand.

Definition 2.5
.

For
(\tau,\mu)\in(0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{\ast}_{+}
, we call
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}
the set of functions
f
that verify
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~f^{\ast}\geq f(x)+\frac{1}{\tau}\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}
.

\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}
is a relaxation of
\text{SC}^{\mu}
, while being more structured than
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}

(

)
. One possible application of these functions is the training of generalized linear models
(
86
, Section 3)
. Functions in
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}
keep the property of
\text{SC}^{\mu}
to having a unique minimizer, but can exhibit strong oscillations, see Figure
4
.
Unlike PL functions, this class admits distinct convergence rates for (
GD
) and (
2.1
), resembling the strongly convex case up to a factor
\tau
, see Table
1
.
This apparent similarity should be treated with care, as it partly results from a notational convention that reuses the parameter

\mu
for both the classes
\text{SC}^{\mu}
and
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}
, as discussed in Section

.

Relation between classes

The following inclusions hold
(

\text{SC}^{\mu}\subset\text{SQC}_{1}^{\mu}\subset{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\subset\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu},\quad\text{LS}^{L}\subset\text{QG}_{+}^{L}.

As observed by

In particular, it is possible to have

Acceleration under Strong Quasar-Convexity: Hidden Pitfalls

Using momentum to optimize functions in
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
is often described as accelerated optimization
(
45
;
29
)
. This interpretation is
motivated by an analogy with the complexity bounds obtained for functions in
\text{SC}^{\mu}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
, see Table

. Precisely, following this line of reasoning, one might expect that

Weaker Assumption, Sharper Rate

To compare the convergence rates of (

However, any function belonging to

Strikingly, in some cases, this latter rate

can be sharper
than those given for the class

.
In Figure

The previous discussion shows that
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
does not guarantee improved convergence rates in general. This naturally raises the question of when acceleration can actually be expected. An important obstacle is that the convergence rates for
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{LS}^{L}

3.1
Specific Problems of Strong Quasar-Convexity

The intuition that a similar acceleration phenomenon occurs for

Parameter Interpretation

Acceleration for PL Functions with an Aiming Condition

{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
is not sufficient to guarantee accelerated bounds with momentum. Rather than introducing a different intermediate function class such as
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}
, we augment the
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
framework with an additional geometric ingredient and study its effect on acceleration.
We initially assume the existence
of a unique minimizer
x^{\ast}
, which can be relaxed as discussed in
Section
5
.

Definition 4.1
.

For
a\in(0,1]
, we call
\text{AC}^{a}
the set of functions satisfying the
aiming condition
,
i.e.

\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~\left<\nabla f(x),x-x^{\ast}\right>\geq a\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert
.

The parameter
a
measures the alignment between the descent direction
-\nabla f
and the
direction toward the minimizer. Values of
a
close to
1
indicate a strong alignment. In one dimension, any function in
\text{AC}^{a}
has perfect alignment, yielding
a=1
.
Such strong alignment is particularly favorable to momentum methods, as it ensures that
the accumulated momentum reinforces progress toward
x^{\ast}
.
In contrast, if
a
is small, momentum may amplify steps taken in poorly aligned directions.
For example, the rightmost function in Figure
1
exhibits
a small aiming condition value,
a\approx 3\cdot 10^{-4}
, which helps to explain why the convergence rate of
(
GD
) is sharper than that of (
2.1
) in this case. The connection between
\text{AC}^{a}
and
\text{SQC}_{\tau}^{\mu}
is discussed in Appendix
G
.

In Section
4.1
, we observe the impact of the aiming condition when studying standard gradient methods (
GF
) and (
GD
). We consider in Section
4.2
the convergence of
(
2.1
) and (
2.1
), and the question of acceleration.

Theorem 4.2

See the proof in Appendix

Comparison of Convergence Rates
In addition to the PL constant

in (ii) depends on a quadratic lower bound parameter

\mu_{0}

The Aiming Condition Exploits the Local Information at
x^{\ast}

For functions in
\text{SC}^{\mu}\cap\text{LS}^{L}

\mu

For functions in

Bound (i) of Theorem

5
Relaxed Setting: Aiming Condition on Average

There is a gap between the convergence observed in practice and the theoretical bounds established for globally defined classes such as PL functions.
The reason is intuitive: if the global landscape of non-convex functions can be highly irregular, nevertheless, the optimization path may remain in a favorable region. Importantly, the performance of first-order algorithms such as (
GD
) and (
2.1
) only depends on the geometry surrounding their optimization path.

Naive Relaxation
A first direct idea to take this intuition into account is to consider an algorithm
\{\tilde{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and a minimizer
\hat{x}
such that
\tilde{x}_{k}\to_{k\to+\infty}\hat{x}
, and assuming

\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}},\left<\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k}),\tilde{x}_{k}-\hat{x}\right>\geq a\left\lVert\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right\rVert\left\lVert\tilde{x}_{k}-\hat{x}\right\rVert.

(7)

With words,
f
is assumed to satisfy the
a
-aiming condition along the trajectory
\{\tilde{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
, with respect to its convergence point
\hat{x}
. For a function in
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
, if
\{\tilde{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\sim\eqref{eq:gd}
or (
2.1
) verify (
7
), we would deduce for this sequence the same result as for Section
4
.
The relaxation is twofold: (i) we get rid of the uniqueness minimizer assumption, and (ii) the convergence rate is not affected by potential pathological regions of
f
, as long as the sequence does not cross it. However, it suffices for (
7
) to not be verified for a single iteration to make this reasoning fail. We thus derive a relaxation that, in the case where pathological regions remain a negligible section of the optimization path, still allows to keep accelerated results.

Averaging Relaxation
We relax the viewpoint (
7
). We address the continuous case; the discrete case follows similarly, see Appendix
E
.

Assumption 5.1
.

For some minimizer
x^{\ast}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\theta>0
,
a_{avg}>0
, and
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\Delta_{s}ds\geq 0,

\Delta_{s}:=\left<\nabla f(x_{s}),x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right>-a_{avg}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s})\right\rVert\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert
.

A function
f
satisfies Assumption
5.1
as long as the aiming condition holds along a specific average of the trajectory.
If
f\in\text{AC}^{a}
, Assumption
5.1
holds with
a_{avg}=a
for any initialization. However, we could have
a\ll a_{avg}
, or having that the path crosses some regions where the aiming condition is not verified, while still having
a_{avg}>0
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
,
\mu_{0}:=\sup\{\mu^{\prime}\geq\mu:f\in\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu^{\prime}}\}
and
a_{avg}>0
. If:

(i)
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}\sim\eqref{eq:gf}
for a given initialization
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is such that Assumption
5.1
holds with
\theta=a_{avg}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}
. , then

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq K_{0}e^{-a_{avg}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}t}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

(ii)
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}\sim\eqref{eq:nmo}
for
\eta^{\prime}=a_{avg}\left(\mu_{0}/L_{0}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{\mu},\gamma^{\prime}=(\mu_{0}L_{0})^{-1/4},~\eta=(\mu_{0}L_{0})^{1/4},~\gamma\geq 0
and such that
Assumption
5.1
holds with
\theta=a_{avg}\left(\mu_{0}/L_{0}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{\mu}
, then

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq K_{0}e^{-a_{avg}\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{L_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\mu}}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

In both statements,
K_{0}:=\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\right).

The proof is in Appendix
E.1
. We obtain the same convergence rate as for Theorem
4.2
(ii) and Theorem
4.4
(i), replacing
a
with
a_{avg}
. However, the rates of Theorem
5.2
could be either significantly sharper, or allow for obtaining such a rate while the algorithm crosses regions with negative aiming condition values. This reasoning can be extended to other geometrical conditions, such as
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
. This perspective might explain why, in practice, grid-search selection of parameters often leads to relatively large constants, compared to what would be allowed by geometrical constants that parameterize globally the associated landscape.

In this section, we first design an example that shows how negative aiming condition values can hurt the convergence of momentum algorithms, and then discuss the aiming condition in neural networks. For our experiment, we consider an alternative formulation of (

\begin{array}[]{c}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{y}_{k}=\tilde{x}_{k}+\alpha(\tilde{x}_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k-1})\\

6.2
Aiming Condition and Neural Networks

Because of the curse of dimensionality and their high level of non-convexity, we could expect that large-scale models such as those involved in deep learning do not verify any aiming condition. This intuition is challenged by the fact that well-designed neural networks are known to have favorable optimization properties. It was observed in
(
53
)
that around 90
\%
of the variations of the path of SGD with large batch size lie in a dimension 2 space, despite the high dimensionality of the models. Of special interest to our work,
(
38
)
performs an extensive empirical evaluation of the aiming condition, which they name the
cosine similarity
. They observe that for various tasks and architectures,
a
remains between
10^{-1}
and
10^{-3}
, despite the high dimensionality of the model. This observation was consistent across several stochastic first-order algorithms, including SGD with momentum. Although our theoretical results do not apply to general deep learning models, these experiments tend to highlight the crucial role of the aiming condition as a key ingredient in explaining the effectiveness of momentum.

Appendix C
Technical Lemmas

The following lemmas serve as technical tools for our convergence proofs.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{AC}^{a}\cap\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
. Let
\mu_{0}:=\sup\{\mu^{\prime}\geq\mu:f\in\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu^{\prime}}\}
.
Then, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast})-\frac{a}{2}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

If
x=x^{\ast}
, the inequality holds trivially. Assume
x\neq x^{\ast}
.

Step 1 (AC + PL).

Since
f\in\text{AC}^{a}
, we have

\left<\nabla f(x),x-x^{\ast}\right>\geq a\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert,\quad\text{hence}\quad\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-a\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert.

Since
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
, we also have
\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{2\mu}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}
, so

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-a\sqrt{2\mu}\,\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert.

(20)

Step 2 (use
\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
).

Since
f\in\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
,

f(x)-f^{\ast}\leq\frac{L_{0}}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{0}}}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}.

Plugging this into (
20
) yields

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-a\sqrt{2\mu}\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast})=-2a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast}).

(21)

Step 3 (use
\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu_{0}}
).

By definition of
\mu_{0}
, we have

f(x)-f^{\ast}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert.

Plugging this into (
20
) yields

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-a\sqrt{2\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}=-a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

(22)

Step 4 (combine the two bounds).

From (
21
) and (
22
), we have

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-\max\Bigl\{2a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast}),\;a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr\}.

Since
\max\{u,v\}\geq\frac{u+v}{2}
for
u,v\geq 0
, we obtain

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>\leq-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(2a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast})+a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr),

which is the claim.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Let
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
. Noting
\mu_{0}:=\sup\{\mu^{\prime}\geq\mu:f\in\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu^{\prime}}\}
,
we have for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}(f(x)-f^{\ast})+\frac{\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

If
x=x^{\ast}
, the statement is trivial. Assume
x\neq x^{\ast}
and write

\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert=\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}\,\frac{\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert}{\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}}.

(1) Step 1 (
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
and
\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
).

Since
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
,
\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{2\mu}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}
, and since
f\in\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}
,

f(x)-f^{\ast}\leq\frac{L_{0}}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{0}}}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}.

Therefore,

\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{0}}}(f(x)-f^{\ast})\sqrt{2\mu}=2\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}(f(x)-f^{\ast}).

(23)

Step 2 (
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
and
\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu_{0}}
).

By definition of
\mu_{0}

f(x)-f^{\ast}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert.

Combining with
\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{2\mu}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}
gives

\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

(24)

Combine
the bounds

From (
23
) and the previous bound,

\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\max\Bigl\{2\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}(f(x)-f^{\ast}),\ \sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr\}.

Using
\max\{u,v\}\geq\frac{u+v}{2}
for
u,v\geq 0
, we obtain

\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}(f(x)-f^{\ast})+\frac{\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma C.3
.

Assume
x\neq x^{\ast}
and
f(x)\neq f^{\ast}
. Define the point-wise quantities

\mu(x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert^{2}}{f(x)-f^{\ast}},\qquad a(x)=\frac{\left<\nabla f(x),x-x^{\ast}\right>}{\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert},\qquad\mu_{0}(x)=2\frac{f(x)-f^{\ast}}{\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}}.

Then,

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>=-a(x)\sqrt{\frac{\mu(x)}{\mu_{0}(x)}}(f(x)-f^{\ast})-\frac{a(x)}{2}\sqrt{\mu(x)\mu_{0}(x)}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

If
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
, it follows

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>=-a(x)\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}}(f(x)-f^{\ast})-\frac{a(x)}{2}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2},

with
\mu_{0}:=\sup\{\mu^{\prime}\geq\mu:f\in\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu^{\prime}}\}
.

Proof.

By definition of
a(x)
,

\left<\nabla f(x),x^{\ast}-x\right>=-a(x)\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert.

Moreover, by the definitions of
\mu(x)
and
\mu_{0}(x)
,

\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert=\sqrt{2\mu(x)}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}},\qquad\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu_{0}(x)}}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}.

Hence

\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert=2\sqrt{\frac{\mu(x)}{\mu_{0}(x)}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast}).

Also, using
\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert=\sqrt{2\mu(x)}\sqrt{f(x)-f^{\ast}}=\sqrt{2\mu(x)}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}(x)}{2}}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert
,

\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert=\sqrt{\mu(x)\mu_{0}(x)}\,\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

Combining the two equal expressions and using
\xi=\frac{1}{2}\xi+\frac{1}{2}\xi
with

\xi=\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert
gives

\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert=\sqrt{\frac{\mu(x)}{\mu_{0}(x)}}\,(f(x)-f^{\ast})+\sqrt{\mu(x)\mu_{0}(x)}\,\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2},

and multiplying by
-a(x)
yields the claim.
∎

Assumption D.1

(Strong growth condition)
.

We assume we have access to stochastic approximations
\nabla f(x,\xi)
of the real gradient
\nabla f(x)
, where
\xi\sim\mathcal{P}(\Xi)
, such that

(i)

(Unbiased estimator)
\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla f(x,\xi)\right]=\nabla f(x)
.

(ii)

(Strong Growth Condition)
\exists\rho\geq 1,\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(x,\xi)\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\rho\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert^{2}
.

To our knowledge, this assumption was introduced in
(
72
)
, and further used by
(
19
)
and
(
83
)
as a relaxation of the maximal strong growth condition
(
82
;
79
)
.
We avoid this more general setting in the main text, to discard adding a layer of complexity. The deterministic case is a special instance of Assumption
D.1
, corresponding to
\rho=1
.
For functions in
\text{LS}^{L}
that verify Assumption
D.1
, we have a stochastic descent lemma, see
(
40
, Lemma 16)
.

Lemma D.2
.

If
f\in\text{LS}^{L}
and Assumption
D.1

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[f\Big(x-\frac{1}{\rho L}\nabla f(x,\xi)\Big)-f(x)\right]\leq-\frac{1}{2\rho L}\lVert\nabla f(x)\rVert^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
4.2

(i)
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}

The following proof is classical
(

)
. We provide a proof the sake of completeness.
Let

E_{t}=f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}.

By definition of (
GF
), we have

\dot{E}_{t}=\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),\dot{x}_{t}\right>=-\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq-2\mu\,(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast})=-2\mu\,E_{t},

where we used
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
. By integration,

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq e^{-2\mu t}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

(ii)
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{AC}^{a}\cap\text{QG}_{+}^{L_{0}}

Let

E_{t}=f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),\dot{x}_{t}\right>+\beta\left<x_{t}-x^{\ast},\dot{x}_{t}\right>

\displaystyle=-\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}-\beta\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right>

\displaystyle\leq-\beta\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right>=\beta\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),x^{\ast}-x_{t}\right>.

By Lemma
C.1
, for all

,

\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),x^{\ast}-x_{t}\right>\leq-a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast})-\frac{a}{2}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2},

hence

\dot{E}_{t}\leq-\beta a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\,(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast})-\frac{\beta a}{2}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,\left\lVert x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

Choose
\beta
so that the two coefficients match:

\beta a\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}=a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\beta=\sqrt{\mu_{0}L_{0}}.

Then

\dot{E}_{t}\leq-a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\Bigl(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr)=-a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,E_{t},

and by integration,

E_{t}\leq e^{-a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,t}E_{0}.

Since
f\in\text{QG}_{-}^{\mu_{0}}
, we have

\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\beta\,\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast})=\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}),

so
E_{0}\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\right)\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast})
.
Finally, since
f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq E_{t}
, we deduce

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\right)\,e^{-a\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,t}\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

We prove the following more general result.

Theorem D.3
.

Proof of Theorem
D.3
.

(i)
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}

This result and proof is well known, see by instance
(

)
in the deterministic setting,
(

)
for the stochastic case. We provide a proof for completeness.
Define

E_{k}=f(\tilde{x}_{k})-f^{\ast}.

By Lemma

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[E_{k+1}-E_{k}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[f(\tilde{x}_{k+1})-f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right]\leq-\frac{1}{2\rho L}\left\lVert\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right\rVert^{2}.

Since

(ii)

Set

Using the update

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2\rho L}\left\lVert\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\Bigl(-2\gamma\left<\tilde{x}_{k}-x^{\ast},\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right>+\gamma^{2}\rho\left\lVert\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2\rho L}+\frac{\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}\rho\Bigr)\left\lVert\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right\rVert^{2}-\beta\gamma\left<\tilde{x}_{k}-x^{\ast},\nabla f(\tilde{x}_{k})\right>.

C.1

and using

(since

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq e^{-\theta t}E_{0}=e^{-a_{avg}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,t}\Bigl(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr).

Finally, using
f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}
and
\beta=\sqrt{\mu_{0}L_{0}}
,

\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}),

hence

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq\Bigl(1+\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\Bigr)e^{-a_{avg}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\,t}\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

Proof with Nesterov ODE momentum (
2.1
)

Let

E_{t}=e^{\theta t}\Bigl(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\Bigr)+\frac{\beta}{2}e^{\theta t}\left\lVert z_{t}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.4
(i), choose
\eta^{\prime}=\theta
and
\eta=\beta\gamma^{\prime}
.
Then we get (see (
29
))

\dot{E}_{t}\leq e^{\theta t}\Bigl(\theta(f(x_{t})-f^{\ast})+\eta\left<\nabla f(x_{t}),x^{\ast}-x_{t}\right>+\frac{\beta\theta}{2}\left\lVert x_{t}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr).

(45)

Integrating,

\displaystyle E_{t}

\displaystyle\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\Bigl(\theta(f(x_{s})-f^{\ast})+\frac{\beta\theta}{2}\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr)\,ds+\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\left<\nabla f(x_{s}),x^{\ast}-x_{s}\right>\,ds

\displaystyle=E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\Bigl(\theta(f(x_{s})-f^{\ast})+\frac{\beta\theta}{2}\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr)\,ds-\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\left<\nabla f(x_{s}),x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right>\,ds.

(46)

By Assumption
5.1
,

\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\left<\nabla f(x_{s}),x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right>\,ds\geq a_{avg}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s})\right\rVert\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\,ds,

hence

E_{t}\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\Bigl(\theta(f(x_{s})-f^{\ast})+\frac{\beta\theta}{2}\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr)\,ds-\eta a_{avg}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s})\right\rVert\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\,ds.

(47)

Using Lemma
C.2
,

-\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s})\right\rVert\leq-\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}(f(x_{s})-f^{\ast})-\frac{\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}}{2}\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2},

and inserting into (

E_{t}\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta s}\left[\Bigl(\theta-\eta a_{avg}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\Bigr)(f(x_{s})-f^{\ast})+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\beta\theta-\eta a_{avg}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}\Bigr)\left\lVert x_{s}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\right]ds.

(48)

Choose
\beta=\sqrt{\mu_{0}L_{0}}
and impose the cancellations

\theta=\eta a_{avg}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L_{0}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta\theta=\eta a_{avg}\sqrt{\mu\mu_{0}}.

These two equalities are equivalent to
\beta=\sqrt{\mu_{0}L_{0}}
and

\theta=a_{avg}\Bigl(\frac{\mu_{0}}{L_{0}}\Bigr)^{1/4}\sqrt{\mu},\qquad\eta=(\mu_{0}L_{0})^{1/4}.

With
\eta^{\prime}=\theta
and
\eta=\beta\gamma^{\prime}
, we can take

\gamma^{\prime}=\frac{\eta}{\beta}=(\mu_{0}L_{0})^{-1/4},\qquad\eta^{\prime}=\theta=a_{avg}\Bigl(\frac{\mu_{0}}{L_{0}}\Bigr)^{1/4}\sqrt{\mu},\qquad\gamma\geq 0.

Then the integrand in (

) is identically zero and we get
E_{t}\leq E_{0}
.
Since
f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq e^{-\theta t}E_{t}
, we deduce

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq e^{-\theta t}E_{0}=e^{-\theta t}\Bigl(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert z_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr).

As
z_{0}=x_{0}
, then

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq e^{-\theta t}\Bigl(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{0}L_{0}}}{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\Bigr).

Using
f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}
, we obtain

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq\Bigl(1+\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}}{\mu_{0}}}\Bigr)e^{-\theta t}\,(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}),\qquad\theta=a_{avg}\Bigl(\frac{\mu_{0}}{L_{0}}\Bigr)^{1/4}\sqrt{\mu}.

\displaystyle\left<\nabla\varphi(\overline{x}_{s}),b(\overline{x}_{s})\right>+\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\!\bigl(\varphi(\overline{x}_{s}+G(\overline{x}_{s},\xi))-\varphi(\overline{x}_{s})\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\rho B_{s}\gamma^{\prime 2}-\frac{A_{s}}{\rho L}\Bigr)\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s})\right\rVert^{2}.

For any predictable process

F.4

E.3
Possible Extensions: Adapt to other Conditions and Exact Rates

We could consider other extensions of the proposed viewpoint. First, we could consider other averaged condition, such as an
averaged PL
condition:

\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta t}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s})\right\rVert^{2}ds\geq 2\mu_{avg}\int_{0}^{t}(f(x_{s})-f^{\ast})ds,

for some
\mu_{avg}>0
.
This inequality is automatically verified if
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
, with
\mu_{avg}\geq\mu
. Interestingly,
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
could cross a saddle point
\hat{x}
, such that
f\notin{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
, while having that this inequality is verified. In particular, (
2.1
) could cross and escape such saddle point, and then converging to a global minima. This saddle point could be ”averaged out”.

Alternatively, we also could consider point-wise parameter, as illustrated next.

Proposition E.3
.

Define the point-wise Polyak-Lojasiewicz constant
\mu(x):=2\frac{\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert^{2}}{f(x)-f^{\ast}}
. If
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}\sim\eqref{eq:gf}
, we have

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}=e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\mu(x_{s})ds}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

We obtain an exact rate for the decrease of
f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}
. If
f\in{\text{PL}^{\mu}}
, we have in particular
2\int_{0}^{t}\mu(x_{s})ds\geq 2\mu t
. We can carry similar analysis with (
2.1
), under a point-wise aiming condition.

Proposition E.4
.

Define the point-wise: Polyak-Lojasiewicz constant
\mu(x):=2\frac{\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert^{2}}{f(x)-f^{\ast}}
, aiming condition constant
a(x):=\frac{\left<\nabla f(x),x-x^{\ast}\right>}{\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert}
and quadratic growth constant
\mu_{0}(x)=0.5\cdot\frac{f(x)-f^{\ast}}{\left\lVert x-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}}
.
If
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}\sim\eqref{eq:nmo}
with
\eta^{\prime}_{t}=a(x_{t})\sqrt{\mu(x_{t})},\quad\eta_{t}=\frac{L_{0}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}(x_{t})}},\quad\gamma^{\prime}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}(x_{t})}},\quad\gamma_{t}\geq 0
, we have

f(x_{t})-f^{\ast}\leq e^{-\int_{0}^{t}a(x_{s})\sqrt{\mu(x_{s})}ds}L_{0}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2},

with
L_{0}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mu_{0}(x)
.

This rate is however purely conceptual, as it involves exact values of parameters we cannot compute. The averaged conditions such as Assumption
5.1
are more practical, as it is sufficient to use a small enough value of
a_{avg}
, and not an exact one.

Let

Appendix F
Continuized Toolbox

The continuized framework
(
26
)
offers an alternative momentum method compared to the more classic ones. In practice, it results in a stochastic parameterization of (
2.1
). It can lead to interesting results in some non-convex setting, see
(
86
;
43
)
. We refer to
(
43
, Section 3)
for an methodological introduction of this concept, on the class of function
{\text{PL}^{\mu}}\cap\text{LS}^{L}
. In this section, we will simply state the fundamental objects and tools. The fundamental object are the continuized Nesterov equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dx_{t}&=\eta(z_{t}-x_{t})dt-\gamma\nabla f(x_{t})dN_{t}\\
dz_{t}&=\eta^{\prime}(x_{t}-z_{t})dt-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(x_{t})dN_{t}\end{array}\right.

where
dN(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\delta_{T_{k}}(dt)
is a Poisson point measure with intensity
dt
, with
T_{1},T_{2},\dots
random times such that for all
i
,
T_{i+1}-T_{i}\overset{i.i.d}{\sim}\mathcal{E}(1)
. It mixes a continuous component, the
dt
factor, with a discrete component that acts at random times, the
dN_{t}
factor.
(x_{t},z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
cannot be differentiated, but we can still use Lyapunov-approaches through Itô calculus.

Proposition F.1

(
(
26
)
, Proposition 2)
.

Let
x_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a solution of

dx_{t}=b(x_{t})dt+\int_{\Xi}G(x_{t},\xi)dN(t,\xi)

and
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth function. Then,

\varphi(x_{t})=\varphi(x_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\left<\nabla\varphi(x_{s}),b(x_{s})\right>ds+\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}{\varphi(x_{s}+G(x_{s},\xi))-\varphi(x_{s})}ds+M_{t},

(62)

where
M_{t}
is a martingale such that
\mathbb{E}\left[M_{t}\right]=0
,
\forall t\geq 0
.

Importantly, the following evaluations
\tilde{y}_{k}:=x_{T_{k+1}^{-}}
,
\tilde{x}_{k}:=x_{T_{k}}
and
\tilde{z}_{k}:=z_{T_{k}}
of (
F
) can be written as an algorithmic form.

Proposition F.2

(
(
43
)
, Proposition 5)
.

Let
(x_{t},z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
follow (
F
). Define
\tilde{y}_{k}:=x_{T_{k+1}^{-}}
,
\tilde{x}_{k}:=x_{T_{k}}
and
\tilde{z}_{k}:=z_{T_{k}}
as evaluations of this process. Then,
(\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
writes as a Nesterov algorithm in the form of (
2.1
) with stochastic parameters

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}_{k}&=(\tilde{z}_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k})\frac{\eta}{\eta+\eta^{\prime}}\left(1-e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}\right)+\tilde{x}_{k}\\
\tilde{x}_{k+1}&=\tilde{y}_{k}-\gamma\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k})\\
\tilde{z}_{k+1}&=\tilde{z}_{k}+\eta^{\prime}\frac{\left(1-e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}\right)}{\eta^{\prime}+\eta e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}}(\tilde{y}_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k})-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k})\end{array}\right.

In order to transfer a result involving
(x_{t},z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to
(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, we use need a stopping theorem, such as the following.

Theorem F.3

(Stopping theorem,
(
43
)
, Theorem 6)
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
be a nonnegative process with cadlag trajectories, such that it verifies

\varphi_{t}\leq K_{0}+M_{t},

for some positive random variable
K_{0}
, some martingale
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
M_{0}=0
. Then, for an almost surely finite stopping time

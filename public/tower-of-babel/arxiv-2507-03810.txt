Introduction

In this work, we study free boundary Allen-Cahn equation, derived from the energy functional

J_{\epsilon}(v):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\epsilon|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(v)}{\epsilon},

(1.1)

where
\epsilon>0
is a small parameter, and
W(t)
is an indicator potential
\chi_{(-1,1)}(t)
(see Figure
1
). This functional arises naturally in models of phase transitions—such as those describing the concentration in a binary alloy—where
v
represents a physical state and
W(v)
represents its associated potential energy.

Figure 1:
Description of potential
W
. The left side depicts the potential for the traditional Allen-Cahn model, given by
W(t):=(1-t^{2})^{2}/4
. The right illustrates free boundary version, defined as
W(t)=\chi_{(-1,1)}(t)
.

Since
W(v)
attains its minimum at
v=\pm 1
, the system energetically favors these states. In the absence of the gradient term
|\nabla u|^{2}
, any function attaining
\pm 1
almost everywhere (for example
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E}^{c}
for any measurable set
E
) would minimize the energy functional. However, the gradient term penalizes discontinuities and forces the transition regions to behave like interfaces with a nontrivial surface energy in the minimization of
J_{\epsilon}
.

In this sense, the minimization problem of (
1.1
) is closely related to the theory of minimal surfaces. Consider a minimizer
u_{\epsilon}
of
J_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon>0
. Modica’s classical result
[
22
]
shows that, in the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, converging subsequences of
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
have their limit

u=\chi_{A}-\chi_{A^{c}},

where
A
is a minimal set in
\Omega
(i.e.,
\partial A\cap\Omega
is a minimal surface).

This connection between phase transition models and minimal surfaces is epitomized by the celebrated Allen-Cahn equation. Namely, the Euler-Lagrange equation of the classical Allen-Cahn energy

J(v)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}+\frac{(1-v^{2})^{2}}{4},

\Delta u=u^{3}-u,\,|u|\leq 1,\quad\text{ in }\quad\Omega.

(1.2)

De Giorgi
[
10
]
proposed in 1978 the following conjecture, known as De Giorgi’s conjecture, or
\epsilon
-Bernstein problem: for
n\leq 8
, any global monotone (in one direction) solution of (
1.2
) has level sets that are hyperplanes. De Giorgi’s conjecture is stated only for lower dimensions
n\leq 8
, because for
n\geq 9
, there exists a non-planar minimal surface based on the Simons cone

\{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=x_{5}^{2}+x_{6}^{2}+x_{7}^{2}+x_{8}^{2}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{8},

due to Del Pino, Kowalczyk, and Wei
[
13
]
.

De Giorgi’s conjecture has attracted considerable attention, and numerous partial results have been established. In dimension two and three, the problem is very well understood. In this setting, it has been shown that global monotone solutions are indeed one-dimensional (see
[
15
]
for

[

]
for

[

showed that for

, the conjecture holds true under the additional limit condition

u(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\rightarrow\pm 1,\quad\textrm{as}\quad x_{n}\rightarrow\pm\infty.

For more comprehensive introduction on Allen-Cahn equation and De Giorgi’s conjecture, interested readers may visit the survey of Savin
[
25

24

, and Chan and Wei
[

\chi_{(-1,1)}(t)
. By perturbing the energy functional

J_{\epsilon}(v):=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\frac{\epsilon|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\chi_{(-1,1)(v)}}{\epsilon},

any critical point

satisfies the following free boundary problem in the viscosity sense (see

\begin{cases}\Delta u_{\epsilon}=0&\text{ in }\{|u_{\epsilon}|<1\}\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{\epsilon}=1/\epsilon&\text{ on }\partial\{|u_{\epsilon}|<1\},\end{cases}

where

\partial\{|u_{\epsilon}|<1\}

and outward on

. To provide a geometric illustration of the problem, consider a band with a width comparable to

\{|u_{\epsilon}|<1\}

Mathematically speaking, the free boundary Allen-Cahn, (
1.3
), enjoys a simpler structure than that of classical Allen-Cahn. The ambient function
u_{\epsilon}
is harmonic in the transition layers, and all the nonlinearities are “concentrated” as the free boundary condition
\partial_{\nu}u_{\epsilon}=1/\epsilon
. It also enjoys the property that separate interfaces (that is to say, the interfaces that are separated by free boundaries) do not interact with each other, unlike the classical Allen-Cahn equation. Therefore, a free boundary Allen-Cahn model can also serve as a natural framework for approximating minimal surfaces, that may be more tractable, in some cases, than the classical Allen-Cahn equation. This motivates the study of properties of free boundary Allen-Cahn equation.

Almost all of the progress made for the classical Allen-Cahn equation has also been made for its free boundary version. In the context of Alt-Caffarelli free boundary problem (introduced by Alt-Caffarelli in 1981
[
4
]
), defined by

\begin{cases}u\geq 0&\quad\textrm{in}\quad\{u>0\}\\
\Delta u=0&\quad\textrm{in}\quad\{u>0\}\\
\partial_{\nu}u=1&\quad\textrm{on}\quad\partial\{u>0\},\end{cases}

Caffarelli, Jerison, and Kenig
[
7
]
showed that, in three dimensions, global minimizers of corresponding energy are planar. Jerison and Savin
[
17
]
further established that stable cones are hyperplanes in four dimensions. De Silva and Jerison
[
12
]
provided a counterexample to the free boundary analogue of De Giorgi’s conjecture in
n=7
, while Kamburov
[
18
]
did so for
n=9
. Recently, Basulto and Kamburov
[
2
]
offered a complete classification of global solutions with finite Morse index in the one-dimensional case.

Most notably, Chan, Fernandez-Real, Figalli, and Serra
[
9
]
have made a significant breakthrough by classifying all stable global solutions to the one-phase free-boundary problem in dimension 3. This achievement has led to the resolution of the free boundary version of De Giorgi’s conjecture for global stable 3D solutions.

Despite all these combined efforts and groundbreaking works, the regularity theory of interfaces remains somewhat incomplete. In 2006, Caffarelli and Córdoba
[
6
]
demonstrated, that if the level surfaces are uniformly Lipschitz with respect to
\epsilon
, then the solution possesses a uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
bound for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, for both classical and free boundary version of Allen-Cahn equations. Subsequently, Wang and Wei
[
29
]
resolved the uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity problem for the classical Allen-Cahn equation. However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, the extension of this result to the free-boundary Allen-Cahn equation remains an open question.

Precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and let
u
satisfy (
1.3
). Suppose that the level sets of
u
,
\{u=\tau\}
for
\tau\in(-1,1)
are given by the graph of a function defined on
B_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<1\}
. Moreover, assume uniform
C^{1,1}
regularity for each level surfaces,

\|h_{\tau}\|_{{L^{\infty}}(B_{1})}\leq\eta<1/2,\quad\forall\tau\in[-1,1],

where
h_{\tau}
is the second fundamental form of the
\tau
-level surface of
u
.

Then, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
C=C(n,\alpha)>0
, such that

\|h_{\tau}\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\eta,\quad\forall\tau\in[-1,1],

(1.4)

and

\|H_{\tau}\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\epsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{2},\quad\forall\tau\in[-1,1],

(1.5)

where
H_{\tau}
denotes the mean curvature of the
\tau
-level surface of
u
.

Remark 1.2
.

The theorem provides an improved interior estimate for the mean curvature
H_{\tau}
as a byproduct. In particular,

\|H_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\lesssim_{\alpha}\epsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{2}<\eta,

even though we did not assume any better initial estimates than
\eta
for
H_{\tau}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The proof generalizes very naturally to a general manifold setting. The only difference will be the presence of the ambient Ricci curvature term. In particular, if the ambient curvature is smaller than
\eta
, this does not affect the conclusion.

The proof of the uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity of free boundary Allen-Cahn equation is much more elementary than that of classical Allen-Cahn by Wang and Wei
[
29
]
, which involves many technical details, due to the presence of interactions between interfaces.

Naturally, to prove such a regularity theorem with free boundaries, the first tool one may come up with would be the hodograph transform. However, the nature of the problem gives thin domain
\{|u|<1\}
with its thickness comparable to
\epsilon
, and classical hodograph transform arguments that can be applied to Alt-Caffarelli free boundary problem cannot be applied to obtain uniform estimates in
\epsilon
.

We present a novel framework to view interfaces as geometric flow, where the notion of time corresponds to the level of
u
. In this framework, we show that the surface velocity
\sigma:=1/|\nabla u|
evolves by the mean curvature
H
and
\Delta u
:

\frac{d}{d\tau}\sigma=-\sigma^{2}(H+\sigma(-\Delta)u),

(1.6)

where the notion of time
\tau
is the level of the ambient function
u
. Using this ODE and the apriori bound on the mean curvature
H
, one can approximate
\sigma
from its one dimensional profile
\epsilon
,

|\sigma-\epsilon|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\eta.

(1.7)

Using standard geometric flow structure equations, we show that
\sigma-\epsilon
(deviation of surface velocity from one dimensional model profile) also solves an elliptic equation involving the mean curvature
H
and the second fundamental form
h
of the surface:

\Delta(\sigma-\epsilon)=\sigma(2H^{2}-|h|^{2}).

(1.8)

From this elliptic equation and constructing explicit barrier on
\sigma-\epsilon
, in the interior (away from the boundary of the cylinder), up to the free boundary, if a priori
C^{1,1}
bounds
\eta>0
of the level surfaces are given, we can improve (
1.7
) to

|\sigma-\epsilon|\lesssim\epsilon^{3}\eta^{2}.

(1.9)

Combining the elliptic equation (
1.8
) and the improved estimate of
\sigma-\epsilon
(
1.9
), we directly apply the elliptic boundary regularity estimate by Lian and Zhang
[
20
]
, to obtain the uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity of the free boundaries. Together with a uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate of the free boundary and a standard global estimate up to the free boundary, we obtain a uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
bound for all interfaces.

The line of proof we suggest here generalizes very naturally to the manifold setting, that is to say, when
u
is defined on a Riemannian manifold. Also this new framework can be applied to various scenarios with elliptic operators where the geometry of the interface matters, including parabolic Allen-Cahn type equations and several free boundary problems. Also, the framework can generalize to many different local elliptic operators such as
p
-Laplacians.

It is possible to study the classical Allen-Cahn equation or other nonlinear elliptic equations

\Delta u=\varphi(u)

using this framework. The resulting equation is

\Delta\sigma=\sigma\left(2(H+\sigma\varphi)^{2}-|h|^{2}-\frac{d}{d\tau}\varphi\right),

and in particular, in case of classical Allen-Cahn nonlinearity
\varphi(\tau):=-\epsilon^{-2}\tau(1-\tau^{2})
, we have

\Delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1D})=\sigma(2H^{2}-|h|^{2})+O(\eta),

where
\sigma_{1D}=\sqrt{2}\epsilon/(1-u^{2})
corresponds to the one-dimensional model profile for the classical Allen-Cahn equation, and
\eta>0
is the
C^{1,1}
bound of the level surfaces. If we can find a more accurate approximation of
\sigma
(e.g. construction in Wang-Wei
[
29
]
) and reduce the error estimate from
O(\eta)
, we will obtain a straightforward proof of a uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate for classical Allen-Cahn and similar double-well potential models, and moreover can hope for the interior improvement of the mean curvature as in the free boundary case (
1.5
). We leave this as our future project.

Level Sets as a Geometric Flow

Figure 3:
Level sets as a geometric flow

In this section, we provide an overview of how the level sets of functions can be interpreted as a hyperbolic type geometric flow, using elliptic operators.

This relation can have further implications, particularly in free boundary problems and overdetermined problems. Following the proof in
[
19
]
, the local-in-time well-posedness of this geometric flow can also be established for sufficiently regular initial conditions.

Let
(M,G)
be
n+1

G

be a smooth function on open connected domain
\Omega\subset M
without critical points, i.e.,
\nabla v\neq 0
. Denote
\Gamma=\{v=0\}
as the zero level surfaces of

. Let
F:\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\Omega\subset M
be an immersion of
\tau
-level surface of
v
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

3

x\in\Gamma
,

F(x,0)=x,\textrm{ and }\frac{dF}{d\tau}(x,\tau)=\frac{\nabla_{G}v\circ F(x,\tau)}{|\nabla_{G}v\circ F(x,\tau)|^{2}},\quad\forall t\in[-1,1],

(2.1)

where
\nabla_{G}v
is the gradient of
v
under ambient metric
G
. Then

\frac{d}{d\tau}v\circ F(x,\tau)=\frac{\nabla_{G}v\circ F(x,\tau)\cdot\nabla_{G}v\circ F(x,\tau)}{|\nabla_{G}v\circ F(x,\tau)|^{2}}=1,

and thus,

v\circ F(x,\tau)=\tau.

Now, consider level surfaces
\Gamma_{t}:=F(\Gamma,\tau)
of
v
, as a geometric flow in
M
, with the immersion metric

. We denote
\Delta_{\Gamma}
as the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the immersed manifold
(\Gamma,g)
(note that the metric depends on
\tau

\sigma:=1/|\nabla_{G}v\circ F|
is the normal velocity, and
\nu:=\frac{\nabla_{G}v\circ F}{|\nabla_{G}v\circ F|}
is the unit normal vector of
\tau

is considered “outward”). Finally, we use the abuse of notations

and

\frac{d}{d\tau}F=\sigma\nu.

Decomposition of the ambient Laplacian into Laplace-Beltrami operator of the submanifold and its normal derivatives is standard.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)

(\Delta_{G}\varphi)\circ F=\Delta_{\Gamma}(\varphi\circ F)+(\partial_{\nu\nu}\varphi)\circ F-H(\partial_{\nu}\varphi)\circ F,

(2.2)

\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{d}{d\tau}\varphi\circ F=(\partial_{\nu}\varphi)\circ F.

(2.3)

Proof.

By applying the Laplace-Beltrami operator to
\varphi\circ F
, with chain-rule, we have

\Delta_{\Gamma}(\varphi\circ F)=\textrm{tr}_{G}((\nabla_{G}F)^{T}((\nabla_{G}^{2}\varphi)\circ F)\nabla_{G}F)+((\nabla_{G}\varphi)\circ F)\cdot\Delta_{\Gamma}F.

Since
F(\cdot,\tau)
is an immersion map of
\Gamma

\Delta_{\Gamma}F=H\nu
. Moreover, it follows that

\textrm{tr}_{G}((\nabla_{G}F)^{T}((\nabla_{G}^{2}\varphi)\circ F)\nabla_{G}F)+(\partial_{\nu\nu}\varphi)\circ F=(\Delta_{G}\varphi)\circ F.

This shows (
2.2

2.1

Lemma 3.1

Let

\left|\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}-\epsilon\right|=|\sigma-\epsilon|\leq\epsilon^{2}\eta,\quad\textup{in}\quad\{|u|<1\}.

\Phi=C_{x}|x|^{2}\geq 0,\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\{|u|<1\},

and also on the boundary of the cylinder

\Phi\geq C_{x}=4\epsilon\eta^{2}\geq\sigma-\epsilon,\quad\textrm{on}\quad\partial\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\overline{\{|u|<1\}}.

Therefore, we deduce that
\Phi
is a supersolution to
\sigma-\epsilon
. The construction of a subsolution follows similarly, and by applying the maximum principle, we conclude that

\left|\sigma-\epsilon\right|\leq C_{\tau}\epsilon^{2}(1-\tau^{2})+C_{x}|x|^{2}\circ F(\cdot,\tau)\lesssim\epsilon^{3}\eta^{2},\quad\textrm{on}\quad F(0,[-1,1]).

This gives the estimate on
F(0,[-1,1])
.

By repeating the similar argument for all other points in
\mathcal{C}_{3/4}
, we arrive at the conclusion.
∎

Using the improved estimate and elliptic equation (
3.3

C^{2,\alpha}

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
H_{\tau}
and
h_{\tau}
as in Theorem
1.1
. There exists
C=C(n,\alpha)>0
such that

\|H_{\pm 1}\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\epsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{2},

and

\|h_{\pm 1}\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\eta.

(3.4)

Proof.

Recall that
\sigma=1/|\nabla u|
satisfies (
3.3
). Also recall (
3.2
). Applying pointwise
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary estimate (e.g. see

) to
\sigma-\epsilon

\|\nabla\sigma\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{3/4}\cap\Gamma_{\pm 1})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\epsilon^{2-\alpha}\eta^{2}.

Then recall (
2.3
) and
\frac{d}{d\tau}\sigma=-\sigma^{2}H

H=-\frac{\partial_{\nu}\sigma}{\sigma}=-\epsilon^{-1}\partial_{\nu}\sigma\quad\textrm{on}\quad\partial\{|u|<1\}.

Therefore, we have
C^{\alpha}

\|H_{\pm 1}\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{3/4})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\epsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{2}.

By standard interior estimate, we have

\|h_{\pm 1}\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\eta.

This concludes the proof.
∎

A condition equivalent to the Hölder continuity of harmonic functions on unbounded Lipschitz domains
Marijan Marković

Abstract
. Our main result concerns the behavior of bounded harmonic functions on a domain in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} which may be represented as a strict epigraph of a Lipschitz function on ℝ N − 1 \mathbb{R}^{N-1} . Generally speaking, the result says that the Hölder continuity of a harmonic function on such a domain is equivalent to the uniform Hölder continuity along the straight lines determined by the vector 𝐞 N \mathbf{e}_{N} , where 𝐞 1 , 𝐞 2 , … , 𝐞 N \mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{N} is the base of standard vectors in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . More precisely, let Ψ \Psi be a Lipschitz function on ℝ N − 1 \mathbb{R}^{N-1} , and U U be a real-valued bounded harmonic function on E Ψ = { ( x ′ , x N ) : x ′ ∈ ℝ N − 1 , x N > Ψ ( x ′ ) } E_{\Psi}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N}):x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},x_{N}>\Psi(x^{\prime})\} . We show that for α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) the following two conditions on U U are equivalent: (a) There exists a constant C C such that | U ( x ′ , x N ) − U ( x ′ , y N ) | ≤ C | x N − y N | α , x ′ ∈ ℝ N − 1 , x N , y N > Ψ ( x ′ ) ; |U(x^{\prime},x_{N})-U(x^{\prime},y_{N})|\leq C|x_{N}-y_{N}|^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},x_{N},y_{N}>\Psi(x^{\prime}); (b) There exists a constant C ~ \tilde{C} such that | U ⁡ ( x ) − U ⁡ ( y ) | ≤ C ~ ​ | x − y | α , x , y ∈ E Ψ . |U(x)-U(y)|\leq\tilde{C}|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in E_{\Psi}. Moreover, the constant C ~ \tilde{C} depends linearly on C C . The result holds as well for vector-valued harmonic functions and, therefore, for analytic mappings.

1.
Introduction and the main result

In this paper
\alpha
is a real number in
(0,1)
. We denote by
\Lambda^{\alpha}(E)
the Lipschitz class of real-valued
functions on a non-empty set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. A function
f
belongs to this class if it is

\alpha
-Hölder continuous, i.e., there exists a constant
C
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in E.

In 1997, Dyakonov
[
3
]
proved that an analytic function on the unit disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
, continuous
on the closed unit disk, is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{B}^{2}
if and only if its modulus is

\alpha
-Hölder continuous on the unit circle, i.e.,
|f|\in\Lambda^{\alpha}(\partial\mathbb{B}^{2})
, and the
following condition is satisfied

(1.1)

||f(r\zeta)|-|f(\zeta)||\leq c(1-r)^{\alpha},\quad r\in(0,1),\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{2},

where
c
is a constant. Pavlović
[
8
]
gave a new proof of this result.

In 2007, Pavlović
[
9
]
establish a counterpart of the Dyakonov theorem for real-valued harmonic
functions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, continuous on the closure of the domain. He showed that
it suffices to assume that the modulus of a harmonic function belongs to the class

\Lambda^{\alpha}(\partial\mathbb{B}^{N})
, or that the Lipschitz type condition (
1.1
) is
satisfied for
r\in(0,1)
and
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{N}
, to conclude that the function is

\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

It is natural to ask what can be said for harmonic functions on domains in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
different from the unit
ball. It seems that one cannot give an easy answer even in the case of bounded domains with a smooth boundary.
Ravisankar considers this in his work
[
12
]
, as well as in his Ph.D. thesis
[
11
]
.
Here, the author considers the behavior of a function along transverse curves with respect to the boundary of the
domain.

This work is devoted to the study of the Lipschitz-type condition posed on bounded harmonic functions defined on an
unbounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which boundary is the graph of a Lipschitz function on
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
. A
domain of the such type is called the strict epigraph of a function. Our main theorem states that if a harmonic
function is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous only along straight lines parallel to the
x_{N}
-axis, then it is

\alpha
-Hölder continuous globally.

The main theorem of this paper is stated in the following. Its proof is given in the next section.

Theorem 1.1

(The main theorem)
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L
-Lipschitz, i.e., assume that

|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)|\leq L|x-y|,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

If for a bounded real-valued harmonic function
U
on

E_{\Psi}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},x_{N}>\Psi(x^{\prime})\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N},

there exists a constant
C
such that

|U(x^{\prime},x_{N})-U(x^{\prime},y_{N})|\leq C|x_{N}-y_{N}|^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},y_{N},x_{N}>\Psi(x^{\prime}),

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then there are constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U(x)\right|\leq C_{1}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1},\quad|\nabla U(x)|\leq C_{2}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1},\quad x\in E_{\Psi}.\end{split}

Moreover,
U\in\Lambda^{\alpha}(E_{\Psi})
, i.e., there exists a constant
C_{3}
for which we have

|U(x)-U(y)|\leq C_{3}|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in E_{\Psi}.

In particular,
U
has a continuous extension on the epigraph of
\Psi
. The constants
C_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
linearly
depend on
C
.

Similar statements may be found in
[
1
]
, and in the papers mentioned in the references there.
In particular, it is known that if a harmonic function satisfies the
\alpha
-Hölder condition on the
boundary of a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then the function is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on the
domain, provided that
\alpha<\gamma
where the constant
\gamma\leq 1
depends on the domain. This result is well
known in the case of the unit ball or the upper half-space
[
13
]
.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

We shall start this section with some preliminaries and auxiliary results. For the theory of harmonic functions on
domains in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we refer to
[
2
]
.

We need the gradient estimate for bounded harmonic functions on a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which includes the distance
function. Here we also mention the estimate of the derivative in an arbitrary direction. The derivative of a function

U
in the direction
l\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|l|=1
, is denoted by

\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{U(x+t\ell)-U(x)}{t}.

This auxiliary result is a consequence of the Schwarz lemma for harmonic functions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. It
will be used several times in the proof of the main theorem. The lemma that follows after it concerns the estimate from
below of the distance of
x+\lambda\mathbf{e}_{N}
,
\lambda>0
,
x\in E_{\Psi}
, from the boundary of the epigraph

E_{\Psi}
of the Lipschitz function
\Psi
. Here, we use some geometric considerations.

The content of the following proposition can be found in
[
2
, Theorem 6.26]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Schwarz lemma for harmonic functions)
.

Let
U
be a real-valued harmonic function on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
,
|U(x)|\leq 1
,
x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Then

|\nabla U(0)|\leq\frac{2m_{N-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N-1})}{m_{N}(\mathbb{B}^{N})},

where
m_{N}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
U
be a bounded harmonic function with real value on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

|\nabla U(x)|,\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|\leq\frac{K_{N}}{d(x,\partial D)}\sup_{y\in D}|U(y)|,\quad x\in D,\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|l|=1,

where

\begin{split}K_{N}&=\frac{2m_{N-1}(\mathbb{B}_{N-1})}{m_{N}(\mathbb{B}_{N})}.\end{split}

In particular, this estimate holds for the
i
-th partial derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}U(x)
,

1\leq i\leq N
.

In case of an unbounded domain we have

\lim_{d(x,\partial D)\to\infty}|\nabla U(x)|=0.

Proof.

Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)=\left<\nabla U(x),\ell\right>
, we have

\left|\nabla U(x)\right|=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|\ell|=1}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|,

so it is enough to prove the gradient estimate.

Assume that
U
is not a constant function, and denote
M=\sup_{y\in D}|U(y)|
. We shall consider

V(z)=M^{-1}U(x+d(x,\partial D)z),\quad z\in\mathbb{B}^{N}.

It is a real-valued harmonic function on the unit ball. Since
|V(z)|<1
,
z\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
, we may apply
the gradient estimate given in Proposition
2.1
. By this theorem, we have

M^{-1}d(x,\partial D)|\nabla U(x)|=|\nabla V(0)|\leq K_{N}.

From this inequality, we derive the gradient estimate for
U
at
x\in D
.
∎

In the sequel, we denote by
B(a,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-a|<r\}
the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the center at

a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the radius
r>0
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
L
-Lipschitz function. For
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}
and
\lambda\geq 0

let us denote

x_{\lambda}=x+\lambda\mathbf{e}_{N}=(x^{\prime},x_{N}+\lambda)\in E_{\Psi}.

Then we have

d(x_{\lambda},\partial E_{\Psi})\geq d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})\cos\gamma+{\lambda\cos\gamma},

where
\gamma=\arctan L
.

Proof.

Denote
d=d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})
and
d^{\prime}=d(x_{\lambda},\partial E_{\Psi})
. There exists
P\in\partial E_{\Psi}

such that
d=|x-P|
(
P
is any point where the distance is achieved).

Assume first that
P=(x^{\prime},\Psi(x^{\prime}))
. Let
C_{\gamma}
be the cone with the vertex at
P
, with the axis parallel to

\mathbf{e}_{N}
and the opening
\frac{\pi}{2}-\gamma
. Denote
R=d(x,\partial C_{\gamma})
and

R^{\prime}=d(x_{\lambda},\partial C_{\gamma})
. Since
\Psi
is Lipschitz, we have
d^{\prime}\geq R^{\prime}
. From the similarity of
triangles, we obtain the following proportion

R^{\prime}:R=(d+\lambda):d,

from which we find
R^{\prime}=\frac{R}{d}(d+\lambda)
. Since
\frac{R}{d}=\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}-\gamma)=\cos\gamma
, it
follows

d^{\prime}\geq R^{\prime}\geq(d+\lambda)\cos\gamma,

which we aimed to prove.

Let us now consider the case where
P
is not of the preceding form, that is,
P=(y^{\prime},\Psi(y^{\prime}))
,
y^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime}
. Then we
can obtain a better estimate from below of
d(x_{\lambda},\partial E_{\Psi})
. We consider the intersection of
E_{\Psi}

with the plane
\Pi
, which is determined by the vectors
\vec{xP}
and
\mathbf{e}_{N}
. The intersection of
\Pi
and
the boundary of the cone
C_{\gamma}
is made up of two straight lines that form the angle
\frac{\pi}{2}-\gamma
with

\mathbf{e}_{N}
. In this case one of them, let it be denoted by
l_{\gamma}
, is the tangent to the circle

\partial B(x,d)\cap\Pi
at
P
. Denote
R^{\prime}=d(x_{\lambda},l_{\gamma})
. From the similarity of triangles, this
time we obtain

R^{\prime}:d=\left(\frac{d}{\cos\gamma}+\lambda\right):\frac{d}{\cos\gamma}.

From this proportion we have

R^{\prime}=d+\lambda\cos\gamma\geq d\cos\gamma+\lambda\cos\gamma,

which implies the inequality in this lemma, since
d^{\prime}\geq R^{\prime}
.
∎

Proof of the main theorem.

This proof is separated into three parts. In
i
-th part of the proof we establish the existence of the constant

C_{i}=C_{i}(C)
,
i=1,2,3
, where
C
is the constant from the assumed inequality

(2.1)

|U(x^{\prime},x_{N})-U(x^{\prime},y_{N})|\leq C|x_{N}-y_{N}|^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},y_{N},x_{N}>\Psi(x^{\prime}).

During the proof we shall see that each
C_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, depends linearly on
C
.

I part
.
Here we show the existence of the constant
C_{1}
in the estimate of the
N
-th partial derivative of
U
.

For
\lambda>0
we shall consider the following (harmonic) function

U_{\lambda}(x)=U(x_{\lambda})=U(x^{\prime},x_{N}+\lambda),\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}.

In other words,
U_{\lambda}
is the translation of
U
for the vector
(-\lambda)\mathbf{e}_{N}
.

Attach to
U_{\lambda}
the number

(2.2)

A_{\lambda}=\sup_{x\in E_{\Psi}}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|.

We show below that
A_{\lambda}
is indeed finite, then we shall find an upper bound for
A_{\lambda}
,
\lambda>0
, which
does not depend on
\lambda
. This is enough for the existence proof of the constant
C_{1}
. Note that
A_{0}
should
correspond to the function
U
. However, it seems that its finiteness cannot be shown in the way which follows.
This is the reason for introducing
U_{\lambda}
.

By applying Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\begin{split}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|&=d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U(x_{\lambda})\right|\\
&\leq\frac{d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{1-\alpha}}{d(x_{\lambda},\partial E_{\Psi})}K_{N}\sup_{y\in E_{\Psi}}|U(y)|\\
&\leq\frac{d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{1-\alpha}}{d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})+\lambda}\frac{K_{N}}{\cos\gamma}\sup_{y\in E_{\Psi}}|U(y)|.\end{split}

Considering separately the cases
d(x,\partial E)<1
and
d(x,\partial E)\geq 1
, it is not hard to derive

d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|\leq\max\{1,\lambda^{-1}\}\frac{K_{N}}{\cos\gamma}\sup_{y\in E_{\Psi}}|U(y)|.

From the last inequality we conclude that
A_{\lambda}
is the supremum of a bounded set, which implies that it is
a finite number. However, the right side in the above estimate is not bounded in
\lambda
, so we shall proceed to
obtain one which is independent of
\lambda
.

For
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}
, for the sake of simplicity, let us denote
R=d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})
. Applying the
Taylor expansion on the one variable function
s\to U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},s)
around
x_{N}
, assuming that

s\in(\Psi(x^{\prime}),x_{N})
, we obtain that there exists
t\in(s,x_{N})
such that

\begin{split}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},s)&=U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})(s-x_{N})\\
&+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\frac{(s-x_{N})^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

In the above expansion let us take
s=x_{N}-\gamma R
, where the number
\gamma\in(0,1)
will be chosen letter. We
obtain

\begin{split}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N}-\gamma R)&=U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})(-\gamma R)\\
&+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\frac{(-\gamma R)^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

From the last equation we have

\begin{split}\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})R&=U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})-U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N}-\gamma R)\\
&+\gamma^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\frac{R^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

Taking the absolute values on both sides above, we derive

\begin{split}\gamma\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\right|R&\leq|U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})-U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N}-\gamma R)|\\
&+\gamma^{2}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\right|\frac{R^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

Applying now (
2.1
), since
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
t\in(x_{N}-\gamma R,x_{N})
, we obtain

(2.3)

\begin{split}\gamma\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|R&\leq CR^{\alpha}+\gamma^{2}\sup_{y\in{B}(x,\gamma R)}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}U_{\lambda}(y)\right|\frac{R^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

We shall use Lemma
2.2
for (the harmonic function)
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}

on the domain
B(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}R)\subseteq E_{\Psi}
in order to estimate the the second term on the right
side of (
2.3
). Since for
y\in{B}(x,\gamma R)
we have

d(y,\partial B(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}R))\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{2}R
, it follows

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}U_{\lambda}(y)\right|\leq\frac{2K_{N}}{(1-\gamma)R}\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}R)}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(z)\right|.

Applying the last estimate in (
2.3
), we arrive at the following one

\gamma\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|R\leq CR^{\alpha}+\gamma^{2}\frac{2K_{N}}{(1-\gamma)R}\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}R)}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(z)\right|\frac{R^{2}}{2}.

After dividing it by
\gamma R
, we obtain

(2.4)

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}R^{\alpha-1}+{\gamma}\frac{K_{N}}{{1-\gamma}}\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}R)}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(z)\right|.

Note that for
z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}R)
we have
d(z,\partial E_{\Psi})\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{2}R
. Having in mind
the relation (
2.2
), since

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(z)\right|\leq A_{\lambda}d(z,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1}\leq A_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}R\right)^{\alpha-1},

from (
2.4
) we obtain

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}R^{\alpha-1}+\gamma\frac{K_{N}}{1-\gamma}A_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}R\right)^{\alpha-1},

from which follows

(2.5)

R^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}+\gamma\frac{K_{N}}{1-\gamma}A_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\right)^{-1}.

Taking the supremum on the left side of (
2.5
) with respect to
x\in E_{\Psi}
we have

\begin{split}A_{\lambda}\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}+\frac{2\gamma K_{N}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}A_{\lambda}.\end{split}

At this moment we take
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{2\gamma K_{N}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
; it is easy to
check that this quadratic equation in
\gamma
has an unique solution in
(0,1)
. From the last estimate of

A_{\lambda}
, since we are sure that
A_{\lambda}
is finite, we obtain
A_{\lambda}\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}C
. A simple
computation gives the value of
\gamma
, and the estimate
\frac{2}{\gamma}\leq 7K_{N}
. Therefore, for the
constant
C_{1}
we set

C_{1}=7K_{N}C.

Since the estimate of
A_{\lambda}
,
\lambda>0
, we have just obtained, does not depend on
\lambda
, in the inequality

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U(x^{\prime},x_{N}+\lambda)\right|=\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\right|\leq C_{1}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1},

we may let
\lambda\to 0
. In this way we derive the following estimate of the
N
-th partial derivative

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U(x^{\prime},x_{N})\right|\leq C_{1}d(x,\partial D)^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi},

which we aimed in this part of the proof. As one may expected, the constant
C_{1}
does not depend on
L
.

II part
. We start now proving the existence of the constant
C_{2}
for the gradient estimate of
U
.

For
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}
, as before, denote
R=d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})
. Let us chose arbitrary a direction
vector
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|\ell|=1
. Applying Lemma
2.2
on (the harmonic function)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U
on the ball
B(x,\frac{R}{2})
as a domain, then the just obtained estimate of the

N
-th partial derivative, we derive

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\ell\partial x_{N}}U(x)\right|&\leq\frac{K_{N}}{\frac{R}{2}}\sup_{y\in{B}(x,\frac{R}{2})}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}U(y)\right|\\
&\leq\frac{2K_{N}}{R}C_{1}\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{\alpha-1}\\
&=2^{2-\alpha}K_{N}C_{1}R^{\alpha-2},\end{split}

since for
y\in{B}(x,\frac{R}{2})
we have
d(y,\partial E_{\Psi})\geq\frac{R}{2}
.

After we permute the
N
-th partial derivative and the derivative in the direction
\ell
, we obtain

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}\partial\ell}U(x)\right|\leq 4K_{N}C_{1}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-2},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}.

By applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we derive (note that
x_{0}=x
)

(2.6)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial U}{\partial{\ell}}(x_{\lambda})-\frac{\partial U}{\partial{\ell}}(x_{0})\right|&=\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}U(x^{\prime},x_{N}+\lambda)-\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}U(x^{\prime},x_{N})\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial x_{N}\partial\ell}(x_{t})dt\right|\\
&\leq 4K_{N}C_{1}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d(x_{t},\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-2}dt.\end{split}

We estimate the last integral by applying Lemma
2.3
. Since by this lemma

d(x_{t},\partial E_{\Psi})\geq(d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})+t)\cos\gamma,\quad 0\leq t\leq\lambda,

we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d(x_{t},\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-2}dt&\leq(\cos\gamma)^{\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})+t)^{\alpha-2}dt\\
&=\frac{(\cos\gamma)^{\alpha-2}}{1-\alpha}\left(d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1}-(d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})+\lambda)^{\alpha-1}\right).\end{split}

Let us denote

C_{2}=\frac{4K_{N}C_{1}}{(1-\alpha)(\cos\gamma)^{2-\alpha}}.

Now, from (
2.6
), we derive

(2.7)

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}U(x_{\lambda})-\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}U(x)\right|\leq C_{2}\left(d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1}-(d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})+\lambda)^{\alpha-1}\right).

Since,
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}{d(x_{\lambda},\partial E_{\Psi})}=\infty
(which follows from the distance estimate
from below given in the Lemma
2.3
), according to the second conclusion given in the
Lemma
2.2
, we have

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}|\nabla U(x_{\lambda})|=0.

The same holds for derivative in direction
\ell
instead of the gradient. Therefore, if we let
\lambda\to\infty

in (
2.7
), we obtain

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|\leq C_{2}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1}.

Since this estimate holds for every direction
\ell
, we derive the gradient estimate

\begin{split}|\nabla U(x)|&=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|\ell|=1}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|\leq C_{2}d(x,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}.\end{split}

III part
.
We shall proceed now to prove that
U
belongs to the Lipschitz class
\Lambda_{\alpha}(E_{\Psi})
, i.e., that there
exists the constant
C_{3}
.

Let
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{\Psi}
,
y=(y^{\prime},y_{N})\in E_{\Psi}
be arbitrary. Chose
\lambda>0
and
\lambda^{\prime}>0
such that

N
-th coordinates of
x_{\lambda}
and
y_{\lambda^{\prime}}
are equal, i.e., such that
x_{N}+\lambda=y_{N}+\lambda^{\prime}
, and

\min\{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\}=(1+L)|x-y|.

Since
\Psi
is
L
-Lipschitz, the segment

[x_{\lambda},y_{\lambda^{\prime}}]=\{tx_{\lambda}+(1-t)y_{\lambda^{\prime}}:t\in[0,1]\}
belongs to the domain
E_{\Psi}
.
For the same reason, we have
d(z,\partial E_{\Psi})\geq|x-y|
,
z\in[x_{\lambda},y_{\lambda^{\prime}}]
, so having in mind
the proved gradient estimate, it follows

\begin{split}\int_{[x_{\lambda},y_{\lambda^{\prime}}]}|\nabla U(z)||dz|&\leq C_{2}\int_{[x_{\lambda},y_{\lambda^{\prime}}]}d(z,\partial E_{\Psi})^{\alpha-1}|dz|\\
&\leq C_{2}|y_{\lambda^{\prime}}-x_{\lambda}||x-y|^{\alpha-1}\\
&\leq C_{2}|x-y|^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Now, we have

\begin{split}|U(x)-U(y)|&\leq|U(x)-U(x_{\lambda})|+|U(x_{\lambda})-U(y_{\lambda^{\prime}})|+|U(y_{\lambda^{\prime}})-U(y)|\\
&\leq C|x-x_{\lambda}|^{\alpha}+\int_{[x_{\lambda},y_{\lambda^{\prime}}]}|\nabla U(z)||dz|+C|y-y_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{\alpha}\\
&\leq C(\lambda^{\alpha}+{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\alpha})+C_{2}|x-y|^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Assume that
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
. Then
\lambda=(1+L)|x-y|
. Since
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+x_{N}-y_{N}
, we have

\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda+|x-y|
. It follows

\begin{split}|U(x)-U(y)|&\leq C(2\lambda^{\alpha}+|x-y|^{\alpha})+C_{2}|x-y|^{\alpha}\\
&=(2C(1+L)^{\alpha}+C+C_{2})|x-y|^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Therefore, for the constant
C_{3}
we may take
C_{3}=(2L+3)C+C_{2}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

A version of the our main result probably holds for harmonic functions on more general Lipschitz domains than domains
which are strict epigraphs of Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
. This question will be considered
elsewhere. We give here some remarks on the proof of the main theorem which concern possible extensions of the result.

It seems that the existence of the constant
C_{1}
may be proved in a very general setting, even when we do not have
any strict conditions on the boundary of a domain.

As may be readly seen, the main problem in the course of a potential extension of the result is the existence of
the constant
C_{2}
for the gradient estimate. In our proof the crucial role plays the behavior of the gradient of a
bounded harmonic function on an unbounded domain. At this moment it is not clear how to overcome this difficulty in
case of bounded domains.

If one establishes the existence of the constant
C_{2}
, the existence of
C_{3}
is straightforward in case of
Lip
α
-extension domains. For the exposition on this type of domain we refer to Lappalainen dissertation

[
6
]
. Below we briefly mention some facts.

On the Lipschitz class
\Lambda^{\alpha}(E)
the norm may be introduced by

\|f\|_{\alpha}=\sup_{x,y\in E,\,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

On the other hand, the local Lipschitz class
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)
contains functions
f
on
E
for which

\|f\|_{\mathrm{loc},\alpha}=\sup_{x,y\in E,\,x\neq y,\,d(x,y)<\frac{d(x,\partial E)}{2}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

is finite. This number is defined to be the norm in
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)
.

By Gehring and Martio
[
5
]
, a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is called the

\mathrm{Lip}_{\alpha}
-extension domain if
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)=\Lambda^{\alpha}(D)
along with the norm
equivalence. They proved the following criterion: A domain
D
is the
\mathrm{Lip}_{\alpha}
-extension domain if and
only if there exists a constant
C_{D}
such that for every
x,y\in D
may be connected by a rectifiable curve

\gamma\subset D
such that

(2.8)

\int_{\gamma}d(z,\partial D)^{\alpha-1}|dz|\leq C_{D}|x-y|^{\alpha}.

For example, uniform, or John domains in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
are Lip
α
-extension domains. It is known that the
class of uniform domains contains the class of Lipshitz domains. Since the function
\Psi
in the main theorem
is Lipschitz,
E_{\Psi}
is the Lip
α
-extension domain. By modifying of the last part of the proof of the
main theorem one shows that
E_{\Psi}
is the Lip
α
-extension domain. Therefore, the existence of the constant

C_{3}
may be alternatively derived from the following result proved by the author quite recently in a very general
setting – for regularly oscillating mappings between metric spaces
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a Lip
α
-extension domain. If a function
f
is differentiable on
D
, and if
it satisfies

|\nabla f(z)|\leq C_{f}d(z,\partial D)^{\alpha-1},\quad z\in D,

where
C_{f}
is a constant, then
f
belongs to the class
\Lambda^{\alpha}(D)
, and we have

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C_{D}C_{f}|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in D,

where
C_{D}
is the constant in (
2.8
).

High-performance FPGA implementation of HEVC with dynamic programming for optimized intra-frame prediction
Authors: Neeru Rathee, Tripti Sharma, Jyoti Yadav, Babita Tiwari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
High-efficiency video coding (HEVC) is renowned for achieving efficient video compression without compromising quality; however, it introduces significant computational complexity, particularly in intra-frame prediction. This paper proposes a dynamic programming optimization technique for HEVC encoders, implemented on an FPGA platform to improve both performance and resource efficiency. The architecture, which includes a sample extractor, correlation analyser, and sample predictor, uses dynamic programming to compute optimal pixel correlations and generate precise directional vectors, enhancing prediction accuracy. Hardware validation was performed on the Virtex-6 ML605 FPGA platform, achieving an operating frequency of 838 MHz and enabling real-time encoding of Ultra High Definition (UHD) 4K video frames. Benchmark results show that the proposed design achieves a 43% improvement in throughput, processing 50 frames per second at UHD resolution, compared with existing HEVC implementations. The architecture demonstrates high resource efficiency, utilising only 12% of available logic, 7% of block RAM, and 4% of DSP resources, while maintaining low power consumption (2.4 W). PSNR comparisons against traditional discrete cosine transform (DCT)-based prediction methods show consistent improvements in video quality, ranging from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$+2.91$$\end{document} dB to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$+3.49$$\end{document} dB across various block sizes (4 × 4, 8 × 8, 16 × 16, and 32 × 32). The proposed architecture minimises encoding time and achieves low latency, making it well suited to real-time applications. The proposed method can facilitate efficient, high-quality video compression in next-generation video processing systems deployed on FPGA platforms.

Introduction

High-efficiency video coding (HEVC) is an advanced video compression standard developed by the Joint Collaborative Team on Video Coding (JCT-VC), a joint effort between the ITU-T Video Coding Experts Group and the ISO/IEC Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG)1. One of the most notable characteristics of HEVC is that it provides similar visual quality to its predecessor, H.264/AVC, with up to a 50% bit-rate reduction; it has therefore become the codec of choice in high-definition and ultra-high-definition video applications2.

HEVC utilises an advanced hybrid coding scheme that represents a departure from previous video encoding standards. It combines block-based prediction with transform coding to achieve a high compression ratio. Unlike previous codecs, which used fixed macroblock structures, HEVC enables pictures to be partitioned into coding tree units (CTUs) of adjustable size. The CTUs are subdivided into coding units (CUs), which are further divided into prediction units (PUs)3. The PUs form a transform quadtree structure with transform units (TUs) as leaf nodes. A TU can be partitioned into two to four smaller units to improve rate-distortion (RD) efficiency in blocks with varying edge content, which is one of the key advantages of HEVC over previous standards that supported only fixed partition sizes of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4\times 4$$\end{document} or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$8\times 8$$\end{document}4.

One particularly notable change in HEVC is the replacement of macroblocks by coding units (CUs), which may be large coding units (LCUs) or small coding units (SCUs) depending on the video content. Each CU is predicted using prediction units (PUs) that apply either inter-frame or intra-frame prediction. The intra-frame prediction method uses information from the current frame to estimate pixel values, enabling high-quality compression at low bit rates5. Inter-frame prediction exploits redundancy between consecutive frames to minimise data size, as used in most video codecs6.

Although HEVC achieves better compression efficiency than its predecessors, it introduces greater computational complexity, largely because of the increased variety of intra-frame prediction modes. This complexity arises from the requirement that each PU must evaluate numerous prediction modes. Given these challenges, hardware-based solutions, particularly FPGA implementations, have attracted considerable interest owing to their capacity to accelerate HEVC encoding. FPGAs offer the parallel processing power needed to handle the high computational complexity associated with HEVC7,8, making them an attractive platform for optimising encoding procedures.

In this paper, an FPGA-based architecture for HEVC intra-frame prediction is presented, optimised using a dynamic programming approach. The objective is to enhance both the performance and resource efficiency of HEVC encoders by reducing computational complexity and improving prediction accuracy. The design employs dynamic programming to identify optimal prediction paths, thus increasing prediction accuracy with low resource usage. This technique is implemented on an FPGA platform to encode UHD video content in real time, enabling high-performance video processing for next-generation video standards. Literature survey

A prominent focus of HEVC research is intra-frame prediction. The goal is to minimise prediction complexity, computation time8, and memory requirements. Two general classes of methodology have been developed.

The first category comprises techniques that exploit texture features to reduce spatial redundancy. A fast mode decision algorithm based on pixel gradients achieves time savings of up to 20%9. An adaptive discretisation-based approach for CU size selection reduces computational complexity by varying CU sizes according to spatial image characteristics10. Edge-based mapping further improves prediction efficiency11. A three-stage pipelined architecture for direction-strength computation was developed to improve the processing of directional modes12. Recent texture-based advances include deep learning techniques for CU partitioning: Zhang et al.5 proposed a convolutional neural network (CNN)-based approach to predict CU split decisions, achieving a 35% reduction in encoding time while maintaining video quality. Similarly, Jiang et al.9 introduced a gradient-based fast intra-prediction algorithm leveraging machine learning to classify texture complexity, reducing mode decision time by 25%.

The second category comprises approaches that exploit the relationships between CUs. Li et al.13 proposed a memory-efficient architecture for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4\times 4$$\end{document} intra directional modes. A VLSI architecture combining data reuse and pixel equality models further accelerates processing and reduces energy dissipation14. A fuzzy rule-based PID controller for visual quality control was also proposed12. Luo et al.36 proposed a fusion-based versatile video coding (VVC) intra-prediction algorithm combining template matching and linear prediction, demonstrating reduced complexity for VVC – the successor to HEVC – and providing a useful baseline for next-generation comparisons. Most recently, Menasri et al.37 introduced a reconfigurable implementation of a parallel intra prediction algorithm for VVC, further motivating the extensibility of hardware-based approaches to next-generation standards.

Methods

Intra-frame prediction

In intra-picture prediction, a prediction block (PB) is generated using boundary samples decoded from neighbouring spatial blocks. This is the rationale behind intra-picture prediction when encoding the first frame of a video sequence39. This approach is also applied at each random-access point within the video sequence.

The intra-prediction module of HEVC has been refined over time to include more directional orientations, larger coding block sizes, and a planar mode for smooth cross-block continuity. Novel coding techniques have been incorporated to streamline intra-mode encoding; however, these enhancements increase computational complexity by approximately 502% compared with the Advanced Video Coding (AVC) standard3,39.

Reference samples

The prediction modes rely on reference samples from the periphery of neighbouring blocks, which may be positioned above and to the left, or below and to the right, of the current PU. For diagonal directions, top-left corner samples may be used. When predicting a current PU of size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N\times N$$\end{document}, a total of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4N+1$$\end{document} spatial reference samples are used.

DP–Bellman-Ford formulation

Dynamic programming (DP) is an optimisation methodology that leverages the Bellman-Ford algorithm, originally developed for shortest-path determination in graph theory. The fundamental idea is to decompose a complex problem into smaller sub-problems, solve each once, and store the results to avoid redundant computation, thus reducing overall time complexity23.

For a problem of size N, each sub-problem of size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N-1$$\end{document} is further subdivided to size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N-2$$\end{document}, and so on. Proposed DP correlation analysis

The RDO algorithm selects the optimal prediction mode from among angular (horizontal and vertical), DC, and planar modes. The proposed architecture supports all 35 HEVC intra-prediction modes, handled by distinct hardware paths.

The DC mode (Mode 1) computes the prediction block as the mean of available top and left reference samples and is implemented as a fixed, low-latency computation in the arithmetic module. The planar mode (Mode 0) applies bilinear gradient interpolation using fixed weights derived from the reference sample boundaries, also implemented in the arithmetic module. The angular modes (Modes 2–34) require evaluation of 17 candidate directional vectors to identify the optimal displacement \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d^*$$\end{document}; the DP network is therefore applied exclusively to these modes, where it replaces exhaustive sequential direction search with parallel Bellman-Ford cost minimisation. The cost coefficients from all three mode types are forwarded to the RDO comparator for final mode selection25.

The cost coefficient for each candidate angular direction is computed using RMSE between the projected reference samples and the actual PU pixel values, implemented via the 30 DSP48E slices of the Virtex-6 FPGA. While Hadamard transform-based cost coefficients have been proposed as an alternative distortion measure22, RMSE was selected in the present work for its direct hardware mapping onto DSP blocks and compatibility with the Bellman-Ford DP minimisation framework.

Conclusion

This paper presents a high-performance, resource-efficient FPGA implementation of HEVC intra-frame prediction using dynamic programming optimisation. Validated on the Virtex-6 ML605 platform, the proposed architecture achieves an operating frequency of 838 MHz and processes 50 fps for 4K UHD video, delivering a 43% throughput improvement over existing implementations. Using dynamic programming to compute optimal pixel correlations and directional vectors, the design achieves PSNR gains of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$+2.91$$\end{document} dB to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$+3.49$$\end{document} dB over traditional DCT-based methods, while maintaining ultra-low resource utilisation (12% logic, 7% BRAM, 4% DSP) and 2.4 W power consumption. The architecture supports all block sizes (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4\times 4$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$32\times 32$$\end{document}) with minimal latency and reduced encoding time, making it well suited to real-time applications. These results demonstrate that algorithmic optimisations such as dynamic programming can overcome HEVC’s computational complexity, enabling high-quality, energy-efficient UHD video encoding on FPGA platforms without reliance on cutting-edge hardware.

Future work will extend the Bellman-Ford DP framework to inter-frame motion vector prediction to form a complete HEVC encoder core. Integration with transform coding, entropy coding, and deblocking filter modules is feasible within the remaining resource headroom. The quadtree-scalable pipeline supports extension to 8K UHD resolution, and porting to newer FPGA platforms (UltraScale+, Versal ACAP) is expected to push frequency beyond 1.5 GHz and power below 1 W. The DP engine is directly extensible to the 67 angular modes of VVC/H.266, establishing the proposed design as a foundation for next-generation video coding hardware.

Random batch list method for metallic system with embedded atom potential
Jieqiong Zhang, Jizu Huang, Zihao Yang

Abstract
The embedded atom method (EAM) is one of the most widely used many-body, short-range potentials in molecular dynamics simulations, particularly for metallic systems. To enhance the efficiency of calculating these short-range interactions, we extend the random batch list (RBL) concept to the EAM potential, resulting in the RBL-EAM algorithm. The newly presented method introduces two “core-shell” lists for approximately computing the host electron densities and the force terms, respectively. Direct interactions are computed in the core regions, while in the shell zones a random batch list is used to reduce the number of interaction pairs, leading to significant reductions in both computational complexity and storage requirements. We provide a theoretical, unbiased estimate of the host electron densities and the force terms. Since metallic systems are Newton-pair systems, we extend the RBL-EAM algorithm to exploit this property, thereby halving the computational cost. Numerical examples, including the lattice constant, the radial distribution function, and the elastic constants, demonstrate that the RBL-EAM method significantly accelerates simulations several times without compromising accuracy.

Introduction

In recent decades, the molecular dynamics (MD) method
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
has achieved remarkable success in various fields such as chemical physics, materials science, and biophysics
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, emerging as a widely used computational simulation technique to analyze the physical movements of atoms and molecules.
By numerically integrating Newton’s equations of motion, MD predicts the time evolution of atomic trajectories within a molecular system and enables the measurement of the equilibrium and dynamical properties of physical systems based on the resulting atomic configurations
[
7
]
.
The force terms in Newton’s equations are determined by the interactions between particles, which are often calculated using interatomic potentials or molecular mechanical force fields.
For nonbonded interactions, such as Lennard-Jones (LJ)
[
8
]
, the embedded atom method (EAM)
[
9
,
10
]
, and Coulomb potentials
[
11
]
, the computational cost associated with force calculations typically accounts for 90% or more of the total computational effort
[
3
]
. This substantial computational burden significantly limits both the efficiency and the scalability of the MD method.

Coulomb interactions, a typical example of long-range interactions, are commonly computed using Ewald-type summation methods
[
12
]
. These methods handle the long-range smooth component in a uniform mesh using fast Fourier transforms, while the short-range contribution is computed in real space
[
13
,
14
]
.
Ewald-type methods reduce computational complexity from
{\cal O}(N^{2})
to
{\cal O}(N\log N)
or even
{\cal O}(N)
and have been successfully applied in various contexts
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
Inspired by the “random mini-batch” idea
[
17
]
, the random batch Ewald (RBE) method was proposed to further enhance Ewald-type approaches, enabling an
{\cal O}(N)
Ewald method for efficient molecular dynamics simulations
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.
To improve the efficiency of force calculations in short-range interactions, extensive researches has been conducted
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
.
A common strategy for reducing computational cost is to introduce a cutoff radius, approximating the interaction force as zero when the interatomic distance exceeds this threshold.
Neighbor lists, such as the Verlet-style neighbor list
[
2
,
30
]
, are widely used to store neighboring particles within this radius. Due to its advantages in parallel efficiency and memory usage, the Verlet-style neighbor list method has become a standard technique in widely used MD software, such as LAMMPS
[
31
]
and GROMACS
[
32
]
.
The prefactor of the linear-scaling neighbor list algorithm depends on the average number of particles within the cutoff radius, which can be large in heterogeneous systems due to the need for a larger radius.

To further enhance the Verlet-style neighbor list method, inspired by the random batch method (RBM) introduced by Jin et al.
[
17
]
, Xu and Liang et al.
[
33
,
22
,
34
,
35
]
proposed the random batch list (RBL) method for calculating short-range interactions in MD simulations. This method decomposes the Verlet neighbor list into two separate neighbor lists based on the “core-shell” structure surrounding each particle. The random batch concept is then applied to construct a mini-batch of particles randomly selected from the shell zone, thereby reducing the computational cost of calculating interactions in the this zone. Current research efforts are primarily focused on applying the RBL method to the liquid systems with LJ potential.
To the best of our knowledge, no studies have explored the application of the RBL method to other long-range potentials, such as the EAM potential.

The EAM potential is a semi-empirical, many-body potential used to compute the total energy of a metallic system and has been successfully applied to bulk and interface problems
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
.
It is derived from the second-moment approximation to tight-binding theory, which models the energy of the metal as the energy obtained by embedding an atom into the host electron density provided by the surrounding atoms. In the EAM potential, interactions between atoms are decomposed into pairwise interactions and atom-host interaction. The pairwise interactions share a structure similar to that of the LJ potential, while the atom-host interaction is inherently more complex than the simple pair-bond model. As a result, the embedding function accounts for important many-atom interactions
[
9
]
. In contrast to the pairwise LJ potential, the many-body EAM potential requires computing the host electron density, which involves a summation over neighboring atoms. This summation makes it challenging to directly generalize the RBL method to the EAM potential.

In this work, we extend the RBL method to metallic systems with the EAM potential, resulting in the Random Batch List for EAM potential (RBL-EAM) method. Similar to the RBL method, the proposed RBL-EAM constructs a "core-shell" list to efficiently approximate the host electron density. Both pairwise interactions and the embedding force within the EAM potential are then approximately computed using a separate "core-shell" list. Drawing inspiration from the “random mini-batch” idea
[
17
]
, the batch size can be much smaller than the number of particles in the shell zone, leading to significant reductions in computational complexity and storage requirements by several folds.
Under the assumption that the embedding energy is a quadratic function of the host electron density, we provide a theoretical, unbiased estimate of the host electron density and the force acting on each particle. Since the particle system with the EAM potential is a Newton-pair system, the computational complexity of force terms is halved by utilizing a half neighbor list. To further enhance efficiency, we combine the RBL-EAM with the half neighbor list and present a theoretical, unbiased estimate of the force in this case. The accuracy and efficiency of the RBL-EAM method are rigorously validated through several benchmark simulations.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In
section 2
, we briefly introduce the MD simulation for metallic systems with the EAM potential.
section 3
presents a detailed description of the newly proposed RBL-EAM method and the theoretical unbiased estimate of the force terms. Numerical examples, including the lattice constant, radial distribution function, and elastic constants, are presented in
section 4
to validate the accuracy and effectiveness of the RBL-EAM method. Finally, we draw our conclusions in
section 5
.

2
Molecular dynamic simulation for metallic system with EAM potential

We consider a metallic system composed of
N
atoms. The
i
-th atom, located at position
\bm{q}_{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots
,
N
, is confined within a rectangular box with side length
L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}
.
The total energy of this system can be defined as a summation of the following form:

\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots\bm{q}_{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{U}_{i},

(1)

where
\mathcal{U}_{i}
represents the energy associated with the
i
-th atom, determined by its interactions with the surrounding atoms.
For metallic systems, the Embedded Atom Method (EAM) potential
[
10
]
is a widely adopted interaction potential to construct
\mathcal{U}_{i}
, which is a second moment approximation to the tight binding theory.
According to the EAM potential,
the energy
\mathcal{U}_{i}
of the
i
-th atom can be uniformly expressed as follows:

\mathcal{U}_{i}=\mathcal{F}(\bar{\rho}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{V}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|),

(2)

where
|\bm{r}_{ij}|
with
\bm{r}_{ij}=\bm{q}_{i}-\bm{q}_{j}
denotes the distance between the
i
-th and
j
-th atoms,

\mathcal{V}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)
is a pairwise potential energy as a function of
|\bm{r}_{ij}|
, and
r_{s}
is the cutoff radius, respectively.
Atoms within the cutoff radius
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}
are denoted as neighbors of the
i
-th atom.
We represent the set of all neighbors of atom
i
as
\mathfrak{R}_{i}=\bigl\{j\big|\,|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\bigr\}
.
In (
2
),
\mathcal{F}(\bar{\rho}_{i})
is the embedding energy that represents the energy required to place the
i
-th atom into the electron cloud.
The host electron density
\bar{\rho}_{i}
for atom
i
is closely approximated by a sum of the atomic density
\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)
contributed by its neighbor atoms
j
:

\bar{\rho}_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|).

(3)

The EAM potential defined in (
2
) requires the specification of three scalar functions:
the embedding function
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
, a pair-wise interaction
\mathcal{V}(\cdot)
, and an electron cloud contribution function
\rho(\cdot)
.
These functions are typically treated as fitting functions with undetermined parameters. According to
[
10
]
, the function
{\cal F}
should be fitted under several constrains, i.e.,
{\cal F}
has a single minimum and is linear at higher densities.
The pairwise function
{\cal V}
usually takes the following particular form:

{\cal V}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)=\frac{{\cal Z}_{i}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|){\cal Z}_{j}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)}{|\bm{r}_{ij}|},

(4)

where
{\cal Z}_{i}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)
is the effective charge of the
i
-th atom.
To ensure the zero of energy correspond to neutral atoms separated to infinity,
{\cal Z}_{i}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)
should be monotonic and vanish continuously beyond a certain distance
[
10
]
.
The electron density function
\rho(|\bm{r}|)

[
39
]
is defined as

\rho(|\bm{r}|)=\rho_{e}\exp(-a(|\bm{r}|-r_{e})),

(5)

where
\rho_{e}>0
is a scaling constant,
r_{e}
is the equilibrium nearest distance, and
a>0
is an adjustable parameter. For systems without external electric fields, the equilibrium nearest distance
r_{e}
is typically set to zero.
For further details on the EAM potential, including interatomic potentials for various metals, we refer readers to
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
.

For a system with
N
atoms, the Lagrangian is defined as
{\cal L}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}-\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots\bm{q}_{N})
,
where
\bm{p}_{i}=m_{i}\dot{\bm{q}}_{i}
represents the momenta of the
i
-th atom and
m_{i}
is the mass. The Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
of this system is given by
{\cal H}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{p}_{i}\dot{\bm{q}}_{i}-{\cal L}
. In this work, we assume that the system is in the canonical ensemble (NVT)
[
43
]
.
By introducing the Langevin thermostat, the motion of the
N
atoms is modeled as

\dot{\bm{q}}_{i}:=\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}=\frac{\bm{p}_{i}}{m_{i}},\quad\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}:=-\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots,\bm{q}_{N})}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}-\gamma\bm{p}_{i}+\xi W_{i},\quad i=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N.

(6)

To ensure the system recovers the canonical ensemble distribution, the parameters
\gamma
and
\xi
are connected by a fluctuation-dissipation relation
\xi^{2}=2\gamma m_{i}k_{B}T
with
k_{B}
and
T
being the Boltzmann constant and temperature, respectively.

The MD simulation is performed by numerically solving the motion equations (
6
) with a numerical time integration such as Verlet algorithm
[
30
]
.
During the time integration, the primary computational cost arises from calculating the potential
\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots,\bm{q}_{N})
and the force
\bm{\sigma}_{i}:=-{\partial\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots,\bm{q}_{N})}/{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
.
In EAM potential, the potential is the summation of the embedding term and the pairwise term. Therefore, the force
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\sigma}_{i}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots,\bm{q}_{N})}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}:=\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij},\end{split}

(7)

where
\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{i}
is the embedding force and
\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}
is the pairwise force between the
i
-th atom and the
j
-th atom. According to (
1
) and (
2
), we derive

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial{\cal F}(\bar{\rho}_{j})}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}+\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}\right),

(8)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)}\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}+{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)}\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}\Bigr]={\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)}\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}.

(9)

For the pairwise force
\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}
, we have
\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}=-\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ji}
.
Let
\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,1}=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
and
\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,2}=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
be the partial embedding force contributed by host electron density
\bar{\rho}_{i}
and
\bar{\rho}_{j}
, respectively.
As there exist
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j})
in (
8
) and
\bar{\rho}_{i}=\bar{\rho}_{j}
is usually unavailable, the embedding force cannot be taken as a pairwise force.
However, we can still obtain the following force balance

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,1}+\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,2}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}

(10)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{j}}+\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{j}}=-\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ji,2}-\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ji,1}.

According to (
5
), we observe that
\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,1}
and
\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,2}
decay exponentially as
|\bm{r}_{ij}|
approaches infinity. Due to the form of
{\cal V}
given in (
4
), the pairwise force
\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}
also decays as
|\bm{r}_{ij}|
goes to infinity, with the decay rate depending on the effective charge
{\cal Z}
.
This is why a cutoff radius is introduced in the EAM potential. It is well known that the computational cost of potential and force for EAM increase rapidly as the cutoff radius
r_{s}
increases.The cutoff radius

depends on the lattice structure of metals.
In this paper, we consider three types of lattice structures (see

The EAM potential as a function of

Due to the lattice structure, the neighbor set

can be decomposed into the

-th nearest neighbors. By assuming that all atoms are in the equilibrium positions, we count the number of atoms in the

3
The random batch list method for EAM potential

Inspired by
[
33
]
, we extend the random batch method to the metallic systems with EAM potential, resulting the RBL-EAM method.
This newly presented method consist of two main components: the calculation of host electron density
\bar{\rho}_{i}
and the computation of force terms.

3.1
The random batch list method for host electron density

Following the idea of RBL method
[
33
]
, we introduce a core-shell structured neighbor list for each atom, which is derived from the stenciled cell list and implements on the top of LAMMPS.
As shown in
Figure 3
, a small spherical core with radius
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}
and a shell with
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}+\Delta r
are constructed around each atom. Here
\Delta r
is a “skin” distance that allows the neighbor list to be reused for multiple steps.
Atoms within the core radius are treated using direct summation, while atoms in the shell are handled using a random batch approach. This strategy reduces the number of interacting pairs while maintaining computational accuracy.
In the neighbor list of atom
i
, atoms are classified into two types:
(1) I-type atoms within the small core defined as
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}=\bigl\{j\big||\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\bigr\}
;
and (2) II-type atoms outside the core but within the shell, defined as
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}=\bigl\{j\big|r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}+\Delta r\bigr\}
.
For further details on the core-shell structured neighbor list, readers are referred to
[
33
]
.

2-dimensional case

(b)
3-dimensional case

Figure 3:
The core-shell structured neighbor list for the
i
-th atom. (a) 2-dimensional case and (b) 3-dimensional case. Here
r_{c}
is the cutoff radius of core region,
r_{s}
is the cutoff radius for the shell region, and
\Delta r
is a “skin” distance that set according to the format of neighbor list in LAMMPS.

We then develop a random method to efficiently calculate the host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}:=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}+\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
, which is expressed as the summation of two parts from I-type atoms and II-type atoms. The contribution to the summation of atomic electron density
\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)
from I-type atoms is calculated directly as

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|).

(11)

For the II-type atoms, we employ a random batch approach.
Given a batch size
p
, we randomly select
p
atoms from all II-type atoms. Let
N_{i}^{s}
denote the number of II-type atoms.
If
N_{i}^{s}\leq p
, the host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
is calculated by considering all II-type atoms, resulting in
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}={\bar{\rho}}_{i}
.
Otherwise, we select
p
atoms from II-type atoms
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
to form the batch
{\mathfrak{B}}_{i}
, and approximate the host electron density contributed by the II-type atoms as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|),

(12)

where
\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)=0
with
|\bm{r}_{ij}|>r_{s}
.
The calculation process for the host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
using the RBL-EAM method is outlined in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

RBL-EAM: the calculation of electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
.

1:

Divide the neighboring list of atom
i
into
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
.

2:

For all
j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}
, calculate the electron density
\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}
using (
11
).

3:

Select
p
atoms randomly from
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
into a batch
\mathfrak{B}_{i}
.

4:

For all
j\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}
, compute the electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
according to (
12
).

Output:

The host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}+\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}-\bar{\rho}_{i}
.
The following
Theorem 1
demonstrates that the expectation of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is zero and its variance is bounded, validating the unbiased nature of our approximation for the host electron density.

Theorem 1
.

The expectation of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is zero and its variance is bounded.

Proof.

We rewrite the host electron density
{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
as the summation of contributions from the core and shell regions, i.e.,
{\bar{\rho}}_{i}=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}+{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
with

\displaystyle{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|).

(13)

To facilitate the analysis, let us introduce an indicative function
H_{ij}
, which is equal to
1
for
j\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}
and
0
otherwise.
The expectation of
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
is calculated as

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}]=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\mathbb{E}[H_{ij}]=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{s},

(14)

which implies:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{i}]=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}]-{\bar{\rho}}_{i}=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}+\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}]-(\bar{\rho}_{i}^{c}+\bar{\rho}_{i}^{s})=0.

(15)

The variance of the quantity
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is given by

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}]=\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}^{s}_{i}-{\bar{\rho}}^{s}_{i}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}\bigr]-({\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}.\end{split}

(16)

Using the indicative function
H_{ij}
, we can derive

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}\bigr]&=\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}{p^{2}}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\Bigl(\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)H_{ij}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]\\
&=\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}{p^{2}}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\rho^{2}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)H_{ij}+\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k(k\neq j)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ik}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\rho(|\bm{r}_{ik}|)H_{ij}H_{ik}\biggr]\\
&=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\biggl[\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\rho^{2}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)+\frac{p-1}{N_{i}^{s}-1}\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k(k\neq j)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ik}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\rho(|\bm{r}_{ik}|)\biggr]\\
&\leq\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\biggl[\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\rho^{2}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)+\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k(k\neq j)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ik}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\rho(|\bm{r}_{ik}|)\biggr]=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}({\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2},\end{split}

(17)

where the last inequality holds due to
\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\geq 0
.
Therefore, the variance of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}]\leq\frac{N_{i}^{s}-p}{p}({\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}.

(18)

For a homogeneous system with atomic density
\varrho
, the leading term of
\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}]
has the following asymptotic bound:

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{Q}_{i}]&\leq\frac{N_{i}^{s}-p}{p}\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{R}_{i}}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\frac{N_{i}^{s}-p}{p}\left(\int_{r_{c}}^{r_{s}}4\pi\varrho r^{2}\cdot\rho_{e}\exp(-ar)\textnormal{d}r\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{N_{i}^{s}-p}{p}\left[\frac{4\pi\varrho\rho_{e}}{a^{3}}(a^{2}r_{c}^{2}+2ar_{c}+2)\exp(-ar_{c})\right]^{2}.\end{split}

(19)

This shows that the variance of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
decays rapidly as
r_{c}
increase.
∎

Remark 1
.

As suggested by

]
, the electron density function

decays exponentially. In

, we plot the curve of electron density function
\rho(r)
for metal Cu, which clearly shows that
\int_{r_{c}}^{r_{s}}\rho(r)\textnormal{d}r

1

3.2
The random batch list method for the force terms

Next, we consider the RBL approximation of the interaction force defined in Eq.(
7
).
Similar to the host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
, the approximate interaction force is expressed as:

\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}:=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps},

(20)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es},

(21a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{ij}^{e}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\left(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}\right),

(21b)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}}\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{ij}^{e}=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}}\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}}\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}\right),

(21c)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}_{i}^{pc}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij},

(21d)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}.

(21e)

Here
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
and
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
.
In (
20
),
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}
and
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}
are the embedding force and pairwise force in the core region, computed directly. Since the host electron density
\bar{\rho}_{i}
and
\bar{\rho}_{j}
are approximately by the RBL-EAM method in
subsection 3.1
,
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,k}
(with
k=1,2
) differ from
{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,k}
.
For a given batch size
p
, we randomly select
p
atoms from all II-type atoms, independent of the random selection used in the calculation of
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
.
This yields a new random batch set
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}
, which is used to construct
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}
as defined in (
21c
).
Algorithm
2
outlines the calculation process for the interaction force
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
using the newly proposed RBL-EAM method.
After calculating the net force for all atoms, we subtract the average net force
\bm{\sigma}_{ave}
on each particle to ensure zero net force,
thereby conserving total momentum.

Algorithm 2

RBL-EAM: the calculation of force terms
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
.

Input:

\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
obtained by Algorithm
1
and neighbor lists
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
.

1:

For all
j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}
, calculate the embedding force
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}
and pairwise force
\bm{\sigma}_{i}^{pc}
using (
21b
) and (
21d
).

2:

Select
p
atoms randomly from
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
into a batch
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}
.

3:

For all
j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}
, compute the embedding force
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}
and pairwise force
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}
using (
21c
) and (
21e
).

Output:

The interaction force
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}-\bm{\sigma}_{i}+\bm{\sigma}_{ave}
, where
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
is the interacting force defined in (
7
) with cutoff radius
r_{s}
. To demonstrate the accuracy of the approximation for interaction force in (
20
), we needs to prove that expectation of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is zero and its variance is bounded.
Following the idea of RBL
[
33
]
, we can estimate the contribution of pairwise force to expectation and variance of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
.
However, as an approximate host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
is used to construct the embedding force
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}
and
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}
, the calculation of
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}]
and its variance are more challenging than that for the pairwise force.
For a linear function
{\cal F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
,
Theorem 2
proves that the force calculated by the RBL-EAM is unbiased (i.e., the expectation of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is zero) and its variance is bounded.

Theorem 2
.

The expectation of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is zero for linear function
{\cal F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
and close to zero otherwise. Moreover, the variance of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is bounded.

Proof.

We decompose
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
similarly to (
20
), i.e.,
\bm{\sigma}_{i}={\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}
. According to the definition of
\bm{\sigma}_{ave}
, we have

\bm{\sigma}_{ave}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}\bigr).

(22)

The expectation of
\bm{\sigma}_{ave}
is zero due to the force balance for pairwise force and (
10
).
Thus, the expectation of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]&=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}-\bm{\sigma}_{i}]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\bm{\sigma}}_{ave}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}\bigr].\end{split}

(23)

Similar to the analysis of electron density, by introducing the indicative function
H^{\prime}_{ij}
for
j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}]

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}\mathbb{E}[H^{\prime}_{ij}]-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}=0.

(24)

If
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\rho)}
is a linear function of
\rho
, due to
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}]=\bar{\rho}_{i}
, we can further obtain

\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})}\bigr]=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}]\bigr)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\bigl(\bar{\rho}_{i}\bigr).

For
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}
, the expectation of
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}
is

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}\bigr]=-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})\bigr]{\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}={\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}.

(25)

Similarly, we get

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}\bigr]={\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}
. For
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}

\displaystyle-{\bar{\rho}}_{i})H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ik}|\leq r_{s}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ik}|)H^{\prime}_{ij}H_{ik}\biggr]-\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}{\bar{\rho}}^{s}_{i}

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}\sum_{r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ik}|\leq r_{s}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ik}|)-\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}{\bar{\rho}}^{s}_{i}=0,

where
\mathbb{E}\left[H^{\prime}_{ij}H_{ik}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[H^{\prime}_{ij}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[H_{ik}\right]=\frac{p^{2}}{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}
due to the independence between
H^{\prime}_{ij}
and
H_{ik}
. Since
\cal{F}^{\prime}(\rho)
is a linear function, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]{\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}

(27)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})H^{\prime}_{ij}+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})\cdot(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}-{\bar{\rho}}_{i})H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]{\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}=\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{j})H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]{\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}

(28)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j})H^{\prime}_{ij}+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j})\cdot(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{j}-{\bar{\rho}}_{j})H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]{\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}=\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}.

By combining (
24
), (
25
), (
27
) and (
28
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]&=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}\bigr]\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{es}_{ij,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{es}_{ij,2})\right]+\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{es}_{ij,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{es}_{ij,2})H^{\prime}_{ij}\right]-{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{e}=0,\end{split}

(29)

which completes the proof of
\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]=0
for a linear function
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\rho)}
.

If
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\rho)}
is not a linear function, then we can rewrite
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})}
as

{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})\cdot(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}-{\bar{\rho}}_{i})+{\cal O}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}-{\bar{\rho}}_{i})^{2},

(30)

which implies that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})}\bigr]=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\bigl(\bar{\rho}_{i}\bigr)+{\cal O}(\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}])
.
Repeating the proof process for a linear function
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})}
, we have
\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]={\cal O}(\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}])
.
According to Remark
1
,
\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}]
is close to zero for
r_{c}>r_{\rm{2NN}}
. Therefore, our approximation for force
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
is close to unbiased.

Next, we estimate the variance of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
. At the beginning, we still assume that
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i})}
is a linear function. Due to
\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]=0
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]&=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{2}_{i}+{\bm{\sigma}}^{2}_{ave}-2\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}{\bm{\sigma}}_{ave}-{\bm{\sigma}}^{2}_{i}\bigr].\end{split}

(31)

Let
\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}
.
According to the force balance condition in (
10
),
\sigma_{ij}^{p}=-\sigma_{ji}^{p}
, and the mutual independence of forces on atoms, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\sigma_{ave}]&=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\hat{\sigma}_{k}+{\sigma}_{k}^{pc})\right]=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k\neq i}^{N}(\hat{\sigma}_{k}+{\sigma}_{k}^{pc})+{\sigma}_{i}^{pc}\right]+\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\hat{\sigma}_{i}]\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\left\{\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{i}]-(\sigma_{i}-{\sigma}_{i}^{pc})^{2}\right\}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{V}[\hat{\sigma}_{i}].\end{split}

(32)

Since
\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{ave}]=0
, the variance of
\sigma_{ave}
is

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\sigma_{ave}]=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i,j}^{N}(\hat{\sigma}_{i}+{\sigma}_{i}^{pc})(\hat{\sigma}_{j}+{\sigma}_{j}^{pc})\right]=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i}^{N}(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{i}]-(\sigma_{i}-{\sigma}_{i}^{pc})^{2})=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i}^{N}\mathbb{V}[\hat{\sigma}_{i}].\end{split}

(33)

Similarly, the expectation of
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}_{i}
is

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\sigma}^{2}_{i}]-{\sigma}^{2}_{i}&=\mathbb{E}\left[(\hat{\sigma}_{i}+{\sigma}_{i}^{pc})^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{i}]-(\sigma_{i}-{\sigma}_{i}^{pc})^{2}=\mathbb{V}[\hat{\sigma}_{i}].\end{split}

(34)

Combining (
32
), (
33
), and (
34
), we can obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]&=\frac{N-2}{N}\mathbb{V}[\hat{\sigma}_{i}]+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{V}[\hat{\sigma}_{j}].\end{split}

(35)

Using the covariance inequality and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the variance of
\hat{\sigma}_{i}
is

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\hat{\sigma}_{i}]&=\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ec}_{i}+\tilde{\sigma}^{es}_{i}+\tilde{\sigma}^{ps}_{i}]\leq 3\left(\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ec}_{i}]+\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{es}_{i}]+\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ps}_{i}]\right).\end{split}

(36)

We now analyze the three variances separately. Following the proof for
\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ps}_{i}]
in
[
33
]
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ps}_{i}]&\lesssim\frac{N_{i}^{s}-p}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}({\sigma}^{p}_{ij})^{2}\leq\frac{(N_{i}^{s}-p)N_{i}^{s}}{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}({\sigma}^{p}_{ij})^{2}.\end{split}

(37)

Due to the form of pairwise function
{\cal V}
,
\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ps}_{i}]
is bounded and decays rapidly as
r_{c}
increase.
Similarly, the variance of
\tilde{\sigma}^{ec}_{i}
is

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\sigma}^{ec}_{i}]&=\mathbb{V}\Bigl[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\left(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{ec}_{ij,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{ec}_{ij,2}\right)\Bigr]\leq 2N_{i}^{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{V}\left[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{ec}_{ij,1}\right]+\mathbb{V}\left[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{ec}_{ij,2}\right]\right)\\
&\leq 2N_{i}^{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\left(\big[\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\big]^{2}\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{i}\right]+\big[\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\big]^{2}\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{j}\right]\right)\\
&\lesssim 4(N_{i}^{c})^{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{j}\right],\end{split}

(38)

which is bounded and decays exponentially with the increasing
r_{c}
. Here
N_{i}^{c}
is the number of atoms in the core of
i
-th atom.

If the
j
-th atom is in the shell of the
i
-th atom, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij})^{2}H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}H^{\prime}_{ij}\bigr]^{2}=\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij})^{2}\bigr]-\frac{p^{2}}{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}\bigr]^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{N_{i}^{s}}\mathbb{V}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}\bigr]+\frac{p(N_{i}^{s}-p)}{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}({\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij})^{2}.

Similar to (
38
),
we can prove
\mathbb{V}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}\bigr]\lesssim 2(\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{i}\right]+\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{j}\right])
.
Then we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{es}_{i}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}{p^{2}}\mathbb{V}\Bigl[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}H^{\prime}_{ij}\Bigr]\leq\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{3}}{p^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{V}\Bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}H^{\prime}_{ij}\Bigr]

(40)

\displaystyle=\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{V}\Bigl[\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij}\Bigr]+\frac{N_{i}^{s}(N_{i}^{s}-p)}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}({\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{2(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{i}\right]+\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{j}\right]\Bigr)+\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}(N_{i}^{s}-p)}{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}(\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{4(N_{i}^{s})^{3}}{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{V}\left[{\cal Q}_{j}\right]+\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}(N_{i}^{s}-p)}{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(j\neq i)\\
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}\end{subarray}}(\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij})^{2},

which is also bounded and decays exponentially with the increasing
r_{c}
.
Finally, substituting (
36
-
40
) into (
35
), we conclude that
\mathbb{V}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]
is bounded and decays rapidly as
r_{c}
increase.

If
{\cal F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
is not a linear function, the calculation of
\mathbb{V}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]
is quite complicated. Let us denote
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}=\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}^{1}+\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}^{2}
, where
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}^{1}
denotes the contribution of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{i})\cdot(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}-{\bar{\rho}}_{i})
to
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
.
Repeating the proof for a linear linear function
{\cal F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
, we find that
\mathbb{V}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}^{1}]
is bounded and decays rapidly with the increase in
r_{c}
.
From (
30
) and the relationship between
{\cal F}
and
\bm{\sigma}^{e}
, we observe that
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}^{1}
is a random quantity with exponentially decaying variance.
Therefore, we conclude that
\mathbb{V}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}]
is bounded and decays rapidly with the increasing
r_{c}
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.3
RBL-EAM method for Newton-Pair system

In MD simulations, the computational cost of force calculations can be reduced by half when the interaction forces adhere to Newton’s third law. Such a system is typically referred as a Newton-pair system.
For metallic systems described by the EAM potential, as demonstrated by (
9
) and (
10
), we observe that these systems exhibit Newton-pair behavior.
Consequently, the computational cost of force calculations can be further minimized by integrating the Newton-pair property into the RBL-EAM method.
To achieve this, we first follow the rule used in LAMMPS to construct a half neighbor list. As illustrated in
Figure 6
, let us consider a system with 14 atoms. The full neighbor list for the 6-th atom includes all other atoms in the system except itself.
Furthermore, the 6-th atom also appear in the neighbor list of other atoms.
However, the half neighbor list for the 6-th atom includes only atoms with indices
j>6
, but the 6-th atom would not appear in the neighbor lists of atoms with indices
j>6
. This reduces redundancy in force calculations.

(a)
Full neighbor list

(b)
Half neighbor list

Sketch of the (a) full and (b) half neighbor list for the 6th atom. Here an arrow pointing to atom
i
means that atom
j
belongs to the neighbor list of atom
i
.

For a Newton-pair system, we further categorize the atoms in the half neighbor list into two types: (1) I-type atoms within the small core region, defined as
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}=\bigl\{j\big||\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}\bigr\}
; and (2) II-type atoms outside of the core but within the shell region, defined as
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}=\bigl\{j\big|r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}+\Delta r\bigr\}
.
In the case of half neighbor list, if
|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{c}
, then exactly one of the following conditions holds:
j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}
and
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}^{c}
.
Similarly, if
r_{c}<|\bm{r}_{ij}|\leq r_{s}+\Delta r
, then exactly one of the following conditions holds:
j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
and
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}^{s}
.
Using the half neighbor list, the host electron density is calculated as

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{i}=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}\cup\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{c}\cup\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{s}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|).

(41)

The second term on the right-hand side of (
41
) can be computed by copying the results from the
j^{\prime}
-th atom, thereby reducing the computational cost. Similarly, the interaction force
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
is obtained as following

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\sigma}_{i}=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}\cup\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}}\left(\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,1}+\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{ij,2}+\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{c}\cup\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{s}\end{subarray}}\left(\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{j^{\prime}i,2}+\bm{\sigma}^{e}_{j^{\prime}i,1}+\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{j^{\prime}i}\right).\end{split}

(42)

The second term on the right-hand side of (
42
) can be computed by copying the results from the
j^{\prime}
-th atom and then change the sign.

Next, we apply the RBL-EAM method to the Newton-pair system. For the
i
-th atom, we randomly select
p
atoms from
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
to form the batch
\mathfrak{B}_{i}
. The host electron density is approximated as

\displaystyle\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}:={\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{c}+\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{c}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|)+\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{B}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}{p}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|),

(43)

where
N_{i}^{s}=|\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}|
and
N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}=|\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{s}|
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}-\bar{\rho}_{i}
, we can further prove in
Theorem 3
that this approximation for the host electron density is unbiased, namely, the expectation of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is zero and its variance is bounded.

Theorem 3
.

The expectation of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is zero and its variance is bounded.

Proof.

By introducing the indicative functions
H_{ij}
and
H_{j^{\prime}i}
with
\mathbb{E}[H_{ij}]=p/N_{i}^{s}
and
\mathbb{E}[H_{j^{\prime}i}]=p/N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}
, the expectation of
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}
is calculated as

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s}]=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\mathbb{E}[H_{ij}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}{p}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|)\mathbb{E}[H_{j^{\prime}i}]=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{s}.

(44)

According to the derivation in (
15
), it is clear that
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{i}]=0
. Similar to (
17
), we can further calculate the variance of the quantity
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
as
\mathbb{V}[{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}]=\mathbb{V}[\tilde{\bar{\rho}}^{s}_{i}-{\bar{\rho}}^{s}_{i}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}\bigr]-({\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}\bigr]&=\frac{(N_{i}^{s})^{2}}{p^{2}}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)H_{ij}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]+\mathbb{E}\biggl[\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}{p}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|)H_{j^{\prime}i}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]\\
&\quad+2\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p^{2}}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)H_{ij}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|)H_{j^{\prime}i}\Bigr)\biggr]\\
&\leq\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{\max_{j^{\prime}}N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}{p}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|)\Bigr)^{2}\\
&\quad+2\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{i}}\rho(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}^{s}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}\rho(|\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}i}|)\Bigr)\leq\frac{\max_{j}N_{j}^{s}}{p}({\bar{\rho}}_{i}^{s})^{2}.\end{split}

(45)

Here we use the fact
\mathbb{E}[H_{ij}H_{j^{\prime}i}]=\frac{p^{2}}{N_{i}^{s}N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}
and
\mathbb{E}[H_{j^{\prime\prime}i}H_{j^{\prime}i}]=\frac{p^{2}}{N_{j^{\prime\prime}}^{s}N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}
, which hold due to the independence of the random processes.
Following the proof of
Theorem 1
, we complete the proof of this theorem.
∎

Similar to the host electron density
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
, the approximate interaction force is given by

\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}+{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{pc}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps},

(46)

where each component is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ec}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}}\left(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{c}\end{subarray}}\left(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{j^{\prime}i,2}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{j^{\prime}i,1}\right),

(47a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}}\left(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}{p}\left(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{j^{\prime}i,1}+\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{j^{\prime}i,2}\right)\right),

(47b)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}_{i}^{pc}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{c}}{\bm{\sigma}}^{p}_{ij}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{R}_{j^{\prime}}^{c}\end{subarray}}{\bm{\sigma}}^{p}_{j^{\prime}i},

(47c)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{ps}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{i}^{s}}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{i}}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{ij}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}\neq i)\\
i\in\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{j^{\prime}}^{s}}{p}\bm{\sigma}^{p}_{j^{\prime}i}.

(47d)

Here
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,1}=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ij}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
and
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}^{e}_{ij,2}=-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{j}){\rho}^{\prime}(|\bm{r}_{ji}|)\frac{\partial|\bm{r}_{ji}|}{\partial\bm{q}_{i}}
.
For a given batch size
p
, we randomly select
p
atoms from
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{s}
, resulting in
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}
.
It should be noted that this random processor is independent of the one involved in the calculation of
\tilde{\bar{\rho}}_{i}
.
Due to the half-list structure, we observe that the net force
\bm{\sigma}_{ave}
is always equals to zero.
Let
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}-\bm{\sigma}_{i}
, where
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
is the interacting force defined in (
7
) or (
42
) with cutoff radius
r_{s}
.
We establish the following theorem by following the ideas of
Theorem 2
and
Theorem 3
.
The proof of this theorem is omitted here for brevity.

Theorem 4
.

The expectation of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is zero for linear function
{\cal F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
and close to zero otherwise. Moreover, the variance of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
is bounded.

Remark 3
.

In (
21c
) and (
47b
), a new random batch set
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}
is introduced to compute
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}^{es}
, which incurs additional computational cost.
A natural approach to avoid this cost is to set
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{B}_{i}
.
However, as shown in
A
, this choice prevents us from rigorously proving that the force calculated by the RBL-EMA method is unbiased, even when
{\cal F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
is a linear function.
Nonetheless, numerical simulations in
4
indicate that the accuracy of RBL-EMA method remains acceptable even when
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{B}_{i}
.
Therefore, to enhance the efficiency of the proposed approach, we will consistently set
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{B}_{i}
throughout the remainder of this paper.

Let us define the Wasserstein-2 distance
[
44
]
as:

{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\varphi,\phi)=\left(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\varphi,\phi)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{2}\textnormal{d}\pi(\bm{x},\bm{y})\right)^{1/2},

(48)

where
\Pi(\varphi,\phi)
denotes the set of all joint distributions with marginal distributions
\varphi
and
\phi
.
Assume that
\tau
represents the time step of the velocity-Verlet algorithm for the underdamped Langevin dynamics.
The following theorem demonstrates that our method is effective in capturing finite-time dynamics.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\bm{Q}_{i},\bm{P}_{i})
be the solution to the following dynamic system:

\displaystyle m_{i}\mathrm{d}{\bm{Q}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bm{P}_{i}\mathrm{d}t,

(49a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{\bm{P}}_{i}

\displaystyle=(\bm{\sigma}_{i}-\gamma\bm{P}_{i})\mathrm{d}t+\xi\mathrm{d}W_{i},

(49b)

where
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are i.i.d. Wiener processes,
\bm{Q}_{i}
and
\bm{P}_{i}
denote the coordinate and momentum of the
i
-th atom, respectively. The processes generated by the RBL-EAM method satisfy the following stochastic differential equations (SDEs):

\displaystyle m_{i}\mathrm{d}{\tilde{\bm{Q}}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{P}}_{i}\mathrm{d}t,

(50a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{\tilde{\bm{P}}}_{i}

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}-\gamma\tilde{\bm{P}}_{i})\mathrm{d}t+\xi\mathrm{d}W_{i}.

(50b)

Suppose that both the above equations share the same initial data, and let
\bm{R}
denote the initial configuration of the system.
Assume that the mass of each atom is bounded, the force
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous, and the variances
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
are bounded by a constant
C_{1}

For any time
t^{*}>0

C(t^{*})
independent of
N
such that the Wasserstein distance satisfies

\sup_{\bm{R}}{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(Q(\bm{R},\cdot),\tilde{Q}(\bm{R},\cdot))\leq C(t^{*})\sqrt{C_{2}\tau+(1+\xi/2+C_{1})\tau^{2}},

(51)

where
C_{2}=\|\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mathcal{X}}^{2}_{i}]\|_{\infty}
,
Q(\bm{R},\cdot)
and
\tilde{Q}(\bm{R},\cdot)
represent the transition probabilities of the SDEs of the direct truncation method (
49

50

4.1
Lattice constant

The lattice constant (or lattice parameter) refers to the physical dimensions and angles that define the unit cell geometry in a crystal lattice. This parameter is critical for describing atomic arrangements in crystals and is primarily determined by the balance of interatomic forces.
As a measurable physical property, the accuracy of the lattice constant serves as an essential benchmark for evaluating how well a potential function models the real atomic interactions. Therefore, we use it to validate the accuracy of the RBL-EAM method.

By treating each atom as a hard sphere with a radius of
r/2

r_{0}=\arg\min_{r}~\bar{\mathcal{U}}(r),

The radial distribution function (RDF),
g(r)

For example, peaks in the RDF correspond to characteristic atomic distances, revealing underlying crystal lattice structures,
Then features of the RDF curves, including the height and shape of these peaks, are influenced by interatomic interactions.
Thus, it indicate that the RDF is a valuable tool for assessing the performance of the RBL-EAM method by comparing its RDF curves with reference results.

4.3
Elastic constants

Elastic constants are fundamental parameters that characterize a material’s stiffness in response to applied stresses. They play a crucial role in defining the elastic behavior of materials and are essential for solving linear elasticity equations.
When experimental measurements of elastic constants are unavailable, MD simulations offer a reliable approach for their computation, serving as a bridge between atomic-scale interactions and continuum-level mechanical properties.
Elastic constants are determined by the interatomic forces acting on atoms when they are displaced from their equilibrium positions, thereby quantifying the material’s stiffness. The accuracy of these calculations is often used as a benchmark to assess the reliability of an interatomic potential. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed RBL-EAM method in practical applications, we employ it to compute the elastic constants of several metals.

For an atoms system in the NVT ensemble, the components
C_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
(
\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=1,2,3
) of the elastic stiffness tensor
\bm{C}
are calculated as follows
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
:

C_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=\langle C_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}^{B}\rangle-\frac{V}{k_{B}T}\Big[\langle\varsigma_{\alpha\beta}^{B}\varsigma_{\mu\nu}^{B}\rangle-\langle\varsigma_{\alpha\beta}^{B}\rangle\langle\varsigma_{\mu\nu}^{B}\rangle\Big]+\frac{Nk_{B}T}{V}(\delta_{\alpha\mu}\delta_{\beta\nu}+\delta_{\alpha\nu}\delta_{\beta\mu}),

(53)

where
\bm{C}^{B}=(C_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}^{B})
is the Born contribution to the elastic stiffness tensor,
\varsigma_{\alpha\beta}^{B}
is the Born contribution to the stress tensor
\bm{\varsigma}
,
\delta_{\alpha\mu}
is the Kronecker delta, and
V
is the volume of the system.
The components of two tensors are given by:

C_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}^{B}=\frac{1}{V}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots\bm{q}_{N})}{\partial\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\partial\epsilon_{\mu\nu}},\quad\varsigma_{\alpha\beta}^{B}=\frac{1}{V}\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\cdots\bm{q}_{N})}{\partial\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}},

where
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
are the components of the strain tensor
\bm{\epsilon}
.
Due to the symetry of
\bm{\varsigma}
and
\bm{\epsilon}

[
51
]
can be written as
\varsigma_{i}=C_{ij}\epsilon_{j}
,
i,j=1,2,3,4,5,6
.

4.4
Efficiency of the RBL-EAM method

Finally, we investigate the speedup of the proposed RBL-EAM method compared with the EAM-DT method.
For solid materials with lattices under small deformations, atoms undergo random perturbations around their equilibrium positions. In this case, we can estimate the computational costs of the RBL-EAM and EAM-DT methods by considering the number of neighboring atoms in the core and shell regions.
The ratio of computational costs for these two methods should be close to
\frac{<N_{i}^{c}>+p}{N_{i}^{c}+N_{i}^{s}}
. Thus, the theoretical speedup of the RBL-EAM method is expected to be slightly less than
\frac{N_{i}^{c}+N_{i}^{s}}{<N_{i}^{c}>+p}
.
To verify the speedup, we simulate six systems with varying numbers of atoms
N=500

,

,
16,384,000
using both RBL-EAM and EAM-DT methods, respectively.
For each simulation, we run 20 steps in the NVT ensemble and measure the computational time for force and potential calculations to compute the speedup.
We consider two batch sizes
p=5
and
10
.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have extended the RBL method to metallic systems with EAM potential. Leveraging the random mini-batch idea, both pairwise forces and embedding forces are approximately computed using two “core-shell” lists. The accuracy and efficiency of the proposed RBL-EAM method are validated through comprehensive numerical tests, including the calculation of lattice constants, radial distribution functions, and elastic constants.
The RBL-EAM method achieves several times of the speedup compared to the traditional EAM-DT method, without compromising accuracy.
This method shows great potential for large-scale simulations of metallic systems, with further improvements possible in batch size optimization and broader applications in material science, particularly in modeling complex atomic interactions and higher-order elastic constants.

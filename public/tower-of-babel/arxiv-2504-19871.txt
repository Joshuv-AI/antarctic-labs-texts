Degree-1 maps and rank inequalities in Heegaard Floer homology Thanks: FB was supported by the Simons Grant New structures in low-dimensional topology
Fraser Binns, Sudipta Ghosh

Abstract
. Ghosh-Sivek-Zentner constructed degree-1 maps from certain rational homology solid tori to the twisted I I -bundle over the Klein bottle. We show that these maps yield rank inequalities for Heegaard Floer homology. To do so, we use Hanselman-Rasmussen-Watson’s immersed curve interpretation of bordered Floer homology, extending their proof of a similar rank inequality corresponding to degree-1 maps to the solid torus. Our result provides further evidence for Kronheimer-Mowka’s conjectured relationship between Heegaard Floer homology and instanton Floer homology.

2.
Background

We begin with some generalities. A
rational homology solid torus
is a
3
-manifold with torus boundary,
M
, such that
H_{*}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_{*}(S^{1};{\mathbb{Q}})
. If
M
is a rational homology solid torus, then
H_{1}(M)
is a rank one
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module. Here, and throughout this paper unless explicitly stated otherwise, we take the singular (co)-homology groups to have coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Examples of rational homology solid tori include the exteriors of knots in
S^{3}
as well as the twisted
I
-bundle over the Klein bottle, as well as the manifolds
E_{n}
discussed in the introduction more generally. Consider the map induced by the inclusion
i:\partial M\hookrightarrow M
. The half-lives half-dies principle implies that the kernel of
i_{*}:H_{1}(\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
has rank one.
\ker(i_{*})\subset H_{1}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by one of two element
h_{M}^{\pm}
each of which we call
a homological longitude of
M
. These can alternately be viewed as a single closed curve in
\partial M
with a choice of one of two orientations.
h_{M}^{\pm}
is a positive multiple of some primitive class
\lambda_{M}^{\pm}
called
a rational longitude of
M
.
\lambda_{M}^{\pm}
can be viewed as a
simple
closed curve in
\partial M
equipped with one of two orientations.

A
rational homology sphere
is a
3
-manifold
Y
with
H_{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_{*}(S^{3};{\mathbb{Q}})
. One way of producing rational homology spheres is to glue a pair of rational homology solid tori
M,N
via a diffeomorphism
f:\partial M\to\partial N
such that
f(\lambda_{M}^{\pm})
is
not
isotopic to
\lambda_{N}^{\pm}
. This can be verified by an application of the Mayer-Veitoris exact sequence. As a partial converse, the exterior of any rationally null-homologous knot in a rational homology sphere is a rational homology solid torus.

Of course, there is a version of the algorithm presented in the proof of Lemma

First-Order Logic and Twin-Width for Some Geometric Graphs
Colin Geniet, Gunwoo Kim, Lucas Meijer

Abstract
For some geometric graph classes, tractability of testing first-order formulas is precisely characterised by the graph parameter twin-width . This was first proved for interval graphs among others in [BCKKLT, IPEC ’22], where the equivalence is called delineation , and more generally holds for circle graphs, rooted directed path graphs, and H H -graphs when H H is a forest. Delineation is based on the key idea that geometric graphs often admit natural vertex orderings, allowing to use the very rich theory of twin-width for ordered graphs. Answering two questions raised in their work, we prove that delineation holds for intersection graphs of non-degenerate axis-parallel unit segment graphs, but fails for visibility graphs of 1.5D terrains. We also prove delineation for intersection graphs of circular arcs.

Introduction

The
first-order model checking
problem asks, given a graph
G
and a first-order (FO) formula
\phi
, to test whether
G
satisfies
\phi
.
In general, this is a difficult problem, known to be
\mathsf{AW}[*]
-hard
[
7
]
,
and subsumes problems such as finding an independent set or dominating set of a specified size, or any given induced subgraph in
G
.
For a graph class
\mathcal{C}
, one may ask whether this FO model checking problem admits a
fixed parameter tractable
(FPT) algorithm,
i.e. one running in time
f(|\phi|)\cdot|G|^{O(1)}
for some (computable) function
f
.

Classically, this problem has been studied in sparse graph classes, such as bounded degree graphs
[
17
]
and more generally nowhere dense classes
[
12
]
, which admit such FPT algorithms.
A more recent and incomparable development has come from the graph parameter
twin-width
introduced by Bonnet, Kim, Thomassé, and Watrigant
[
5
]
:
if
\mathcal{C}
is a class of bounded twin-width, and witnesses of this (so-called
contraction sequences
) can be efficiently computed,
then FO model checking in
\mathcal{C}
is FPT.

Since bounded twin-width and bounded degree are incomparable, a class with FPT FO model checking need not have bounded twin-width.
Yet a number of remarkable cases exist where twin-width is exactly the boundary of efficient FO model checking:
a fundamental result of Bonnet, Giocanti, Ossona de Mendez, Simon, Thomassé, and Toruńczyk
[
4
]
is that
for any hereditary (i.e. closed under induced subgraphs) class
\mathcal{C}
of
ordered graphs
(i.e. graphs given with a total ordering of vertices, which formulas can query), model checking in
\mathcal{C}
is FPT if and only if
\mathcal{C}
has bounded twin-width

\mathsf{FPT}\neq\mathsf{AW}[*]

.

Overview of the proof of
\cref
thm:main

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the graph class we are interested in, e.g. the class of all circular-arc graphs.
Our goal is the following:
for any hereditary
2

2

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}

if and only if
\mathcal{C}
has bounded twin-width.
Inspired by

2

14
]
, we obtain such an equivalence by proving a dichotomy of the following form, for any subclass
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
:

•

Either we can compute in polynomial time a contraction sequence for any

has bounded twin-width.
Then
[
5
]
gives FPT model checking in
\mathcal{C}
.

•

Or
\mathcal{C}
is
monadically independent
, which informally means that graphs in
\mathcal{C}
can be used to encode arbitrary graphs through FO logic (see

In that case, and if
\mathcal{C}
additionally is hereditary, then FO model checking on the class of all graphs reduces to FO model checking on

[
8

, proving that the latter is hard for the parametrised complexity class
\mathsf{AW}[*]

[
7
]
.

Let us point out that if
\mathcal{C}
has bounded twin-width, then it is monadically dependent (the opposite of monadically independent).
Thus a subclass
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
has bounded twin-width if and only if it is monadically dependent;
this is the precise definition of

[
2
]
.
The above dichotomy is a slight strengthening of delineation, additionally requiring to be able to efficiently find contraction sequences.
When
\mathcal{C}
is hereditary, and assuming
\mathsf{FPT}\neq\mathsf{AW}[*]
, this strengthening gives the equivalence of
\mathcal{C}
having bounded twin-width,
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}

Let us point out that it is a major conjecture in finite model theory that the last two points are always equivalent:

Conjecture 5

(
[
10
, Conjecture 8.2]
)
.

For any hereditary class
\mathcal{C}
, there is an FPT FO model checking algorithm for graphs in
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}

Our results confirm special cases of this, where twin-width explains the equivalence.

Coming back to the proof overview, let us explain how the aforedescribed dichotomy is achieved.
The starting point is the characterisation of twin-width through obstructions in matrices
[
5
]
:
given any 0–1 matrix
M
, one can efficiently find either a contraction sequence for
M
(witness of small twin-width), or a
grid
.
Given the geometric representation of the graph
G
, we construct some auxiliary matrix
M_{G}
.
For instance, for circular arc graphs, we identify the circle with
\{1,\dots,n\}
modulo
n
, and the matrix
M
has a 1 at position
(i,j)
whenever there is an arc clockwise from
i
to
j
.
We then prove two facts:

•

First,
G
can be reconstructed from
M_{G}
by a sequence of operations that preserve twin-width.
Thus if
M_{G}
has bounded twin-width, then so does
G
. This is efficient: a contraction sequence for
G
can quickly be computed from one for
M_{G}
.

•

Second, if
M_{G}
contains a sufficiently large grid, then
G
contains a complex structure called
transversal pair
.
It is known that if graphs in
\mathcal{C}
contain arbitrarily large transversal pairs, then
\mathcal{C}
is monadically independent
[
2
]
.
To prove this, we assume the geometric representation of
G
we use is
minimised
in an appropriate sense.

Thus either one can find contraction sequences for the auxiliary matrices
\{M_{G}:G\in\mathcal{C}\}
, and thus also for
\mathcal{C}
,
or these matrices have arbitrarily large grids, hence
\mathcal{C}
contains arbitrarily large transversal pairs and is monadically independent.
The case of APUS graphs requires one additional step: we construct
local
auxiliary matrices, corresponding to 1-by-1 squares in the segment representation.
If any such matrix has a grid, then we find a transversal pair; if all local matrices have bounded twin-width, then so does the full graph.

Open problems

Continuing on the theme of intersection graphs of objects in the plane, we ask whether unit-disk graphs are delineated.
The difficulty in adapting our technique to this case may be to define and use the right notion of minimisation.
Disks with a fixed, finite set of allowed radii should behave similar to unit disks,
but we expect that allowing two radii with unbounded ratio, or arbitrary radii in
[1,1+\epsilon]
causes delineation to fail with constructions similar to that of
\cref
thm:seg-non-delineated.
One may also ask about intersection graphs of squares of rectangles.
We expect our techniques to easily apply to axis-aligned unit squares, but wonder whether delineation still holds without the axis-aligned constraint.

One may also consider other parameters than twin-width.
Since our non-delineated constructions in
\cref
thm:seg-non-delineated,thm:terrain-not-delin use merge-width to obtain FPT model checking,
it is sensible to look for geometric graph classes in which merge-width characterises monadic dependence and FPT model checking.
Tackling this question would likely require characterising merge-width through some obstructions, a difficult open problem.
One can observe that this equivalence does
not
hold for axis-parallel segments:
the construction of
[
2
, Figure 8]
shows that for any bipartite graph
H=(V,W,E)
,
one can obtain as axis-parallel segment graph the 2-subdivision of
H
, with a full biclique added between
V
and
W
.
Taking
H
to be graphs with girth and minimum degree going to infinity, this class is monadically dependent, but has unbounded merge-width.

2.1
Graphs and relational structures

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, we write
V(G)=V
and
E(G)=E

The set of neighbours of

N(v)
. For

,

is the subgraph induced by

.
When
<
is a linear ordering of

.
Similarly, if
<_{V}
and
<_{E}

<_{V}

More generally, a

(or simply binary structure)
(V,E_{1},\dots,E_{\ell})

consists of a vertex set

E_{i}\subseteq V^{2}
.
It can be viewed as a directed graph with
\ell

where there may be several edges of different kinds between two vertices (but not multiple edges of the same kind).
For binary structures, each relation has its own adjacency matrix, denoted by
Adj(E_{i},<)
.
An
ordered binary structure
is a binary structure in which one of the relations is a linear ordering of the vertex set, normally denoted by
<
. The structure is thus of the form
(V,E_{1},\dots,E_{\ell},<)
.
A special case is
ordered graphs

(V,E,<)
, where
(V,E)
is a graph and
<
a linear ordering of
V
.

2.2
Grids

The following notion of grids comes from
[
13
]
.
Given a point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we write
\pi_{x}(p),\pi_{y}(p)
for its
x
- and
y
-coordinates.
A
t
-grid
in the plane is a family of points
(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[t]}
satisfying the following for any
i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}\in[t]
:

if
i<i^{\prime}
, then
\pi_{x}(p_{i,j})<\pi_{x}(p_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})

and

if
j<j^{\prime}
, then
\pi_{y}(p_{i,j})<\pi_{y}(p_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})
.

Observe then that there are intervals
X_{1}<\dots<X_{t}
and
Y_{1}<\dots<Y_{t}
such that
p_{i,j}\in X_{i}\times Y_{j}
.
Indeed, take
X_{i}
to be the smallest interval containing
\pi_{x}(p_{i,j})
for all
j
, and similarly with
Y_{i}
.

This extends to 0–1 matrices, interpreting a ‘1’ at position
(i,j)
in the matrix
M
as a point with coordinates
(i,j)
.
We say that
M
contains a
t
-grid if the point set defined by the ‘1’s in
M
contains a
t
-grid in the previous sense.
Then there are partitions into intervals
\mathcal{R}=\{X_{1}<\dots<X_{t}\}
and
\mathcal{C}=\{Y_{1}<\dots<Y_{t}\}
of the rows and columns as above.
The ordering of rows and columns is crucial in this definition, which is why we make it explicit when defining an adjacency matrix
Adj(G,<)
.

The Ramsey property for grids easily follows from the Ramsey theorem on bicliques:

Theorem 6

(Ramsey theorem for grids)
.

There is a function
R(t,k)
such that if
\mathcal{P}
is a point set containing a
R(t,k)
-grid,
and
\lambda:\mathcal{P}\to[k]
is any colouring, then
\mathcal{P}
contains
t
-grid consisting only of points of colour
c
for some
c\in[k]
.

Proof.

Choose
R(t,k)
to be the bipartite Ramsey bound.
For
T=R(t,k)
, consider a grid
(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[T]}
.
Construct a biclique with vertices
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in[T]},\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[T]}
, and assign to the edge
u_{i}v_{j}
the colour
\lambda(p_{i,j})
.
The bipartite Ramsey theorem gives a monochromatic sub-biclique with
t
vertices on each side, say of colour
c
.
The corresponding points form a
t
-grid of colour
c
.
∎

When the

- and

-coordinates of the grid are indexed by the same set (e.g. in an adjacency matrix),
they can be assumed to come from disjoint intervals at a slight cost:

Lemma 7
.

Any
2t
-grid
\mathcal{P}
contains a

-subgrid
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}

.

Let
(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[2t]}
be the points of

.
There exists an

whenever

, and

:

.
Similarly, there is a

The following technical lemma relates grids in incidence and adjacency matrices.

Lemma 8
.

Let
E\subseteq V^{2}
be a binary relation such that
Adj(E,<_{V})
has no
k
-grid.
Let
<_{\mathsf{lex}}
be the lexicographic ordering of

, i.e.
(x,y)<_{\mathsf{lex}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
if either
x<_{V}x^{\prime}
, or
x=x^{\prime}
and
y<_{V}y^{\prime}
.
Then
Inc(E,<_{V},<_{\mathsf{lex}})
has no
4k
-grid.

Proof.

For a directed edge
(x,y)\in E
, we write
s(e)=x
and
t(e)=y
for its source and target.
Assume that the
Inc(E,<_{V},<_{\mathsf{lex}})
contains an
\ell
-grid
(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[\ell]}
.
Each
p_{i,j}
is a ‘1’ in the matrix, corresponding to a vertex
v_{i,j}
and an incident edge

. The definition of
\ell
-grid gives the following inequalities for any
i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle i<i^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\text{implies}\quad v_{i,j}<_{V}v_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}},\text{ and}

(1)

\displaystyle j<j^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\text{implies}\quad e_{i,j}<_{\mathsf{lex}}e_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}.

(2)

By definition of the lexicographic ordering, condition (
2

j<j^{\prime}\quad\text{implies}\quad s(e_{i,j})\leq_{V}s(e_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}).

(3)

Say that the pair
(i,j)
is of

if
v_{i,j}=s(e_{i,j})
, and otherwise of

, meaning

.
Observe that

(i,j+1)

v_{i+1,j}\leq_{V}v_{i,j+1}

By grouping the pairs

into

(2i,2j),(2i,2j-1),(2i-1,2j),(2i-1,2j-1)

-grid in which all pairs

are of target type.
Thus explicitly, we have edges
f_{i,j}
for

that

\displaystyle i<i^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\text{implies}\quad t(f_{i,j})<_{V}t(f_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}),\text{ and}

(4)

\displaystyle j<j^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\text{implies}\quad f_{i,j}<_{\mathsf{lex}}f_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}.

(5)

We claim that for

and

Indeed, we have

which translates into the corresponding non-strict inequalities on the respective source vertices.
But on the other hand, (

Then, for

to hold, it must be that
s(f_{i,j})<_{V}s(f_{1,j+1})
.
Thus, when considering edges

only for

and odd values of

we have that the order of sources

coincides with the ordering of
j
-indices,
and the ordering of targets

with that of

-indices.
This yields an
\lfloor\ell/4\rfloor
-grid in

, a contradiction.
∎

2.3
Twin-width

We recall the definition of the twin-width
\mathsf{tww}(G)
of a graph
G
from
[
5
]
.

In a graph
G
, two sets of vertices
X,Y
are
non-homogeneous
if there exist both an edge and a non-edge between them.
Given a partition
\mathcal{P}
of the vertices of
G
, the
error graph
of
\mathcal{P}
is the graph
whose vertices are parts of
\mathcal{P}
, and in which two parts are adjacent if they are non-homogeneous.
The
error degree
of
\mathcal{P}
is the maximum degree of this error graph.

A
contraction sequence
for
G
is a sequence
\mathcal{P}_{n},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{1}
of partitions of
V(G)
,
where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the partition into singletons,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is the partition in a single part,
and
\mathcal{P}_{i-1}
is obtained by merging two parts of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
Its
width
is the maximum error degree of
\mathcal{P}_{n},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Finally, the

More generally, in a binary relational structure

two sets of vertices

It is easy to observe that the twin-width of a graph is the maximum twin-width of its connected components.
We will use the following variant of this result for ordered graphs.

Lemma 11

Let

Proof.

Call

First-order logic

We present first-order (FO) logic and transductions restricted to

relational structures, as this is sufficient for our purposes.
A

For a signature

2.5
Merge-width

Merge-width
[
9
]
is a major generalisation of twin-width, designed to also capture graphs with bounded degree, or more generally bounded expansion.
The definition also involves sequences of vertex partitions, but the notion of error is finer:
one counts errors for each vertex individually, rather than for each part.
Merge-width really is a family of parameters: one defines
merge-width at radius
r
, denoted
\mathsf{mw}_{r}(G)
, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

A
construction sequence
is a sequence
(\mathcal{P}_{1},E_{1},N_{1}),\ldots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},E_{m},N_{m})
,
where
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are partitions of the vertex set
V
, and
E_{i},N_{i}
are disjoint sets of
resolved
edges and non-edges, obtained as follows.

•

The sequence starts with the partition into singletons
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{\{v\}:v\in V\}
, and with no resolved edge or non-edge, i.e.
E_{1}=N_{1}=\varnothing
.

•

One advances from step
i
to
i+1
with one of the following three operations:

1.

merge two parts of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
to obtain
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
,

2.

positively resolve
two parts

(possibly
X=Y
), i.e. define
E_{i+1}
by adding to
E_{i}
all pairs
x\in X
,

that are not yet in
E_{i}
or
N_{i}
,

3.

or
negatively resolve

X,Y
, adding these pairs to
N_{i+1}
instead.

•

The sequence ends when all vertices are merged into
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{V\}
, and all pairs are resolved in either
E_{m}
or
N_{m}
. The resulting graph is
(V,E_{m})
.

Define
R_{i}\coloneqq E_{i}\cup N_{i}
the set of resolved pairs at time
i
.
Then for

, the
radius-
r
width of
\mathcal{P}
is

\max\limits_{t\in[m]}\,\max\limits_{v\in V}\Big|\bigl\{A\in\mathcal{P}_{t}:A\text{ is at distance at most~$r$ from }v\text{ in }(V,R_{t})\bigr\}\Big|.

The
radius-
r
merge-width of
G
, denoted by
\mathsf{mw}_{r}(G)
, is the least radius-
r
width of a construction sequence creating
G
.
A graph class
\mathcal{C}
is said to have
bounded merge-width
if there is a function
f
such that
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
G\in\mathcal{C}
, it holds that

.

The main motivation for merge-width, and the reason we use it, is the following generalisation of
\cref
thm:model-checking.

Theorem 18

(
[
9
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a class with bounded merge-width, then
\mathcal{C}
is monadically dependent, and FO model checking in
\mathcal{C}
is FPT.
3
3

3

To be accurate, if radius-
r
merge-width of graphs in
\mathcal{C}
is bounded by some
constructible

,
then model checking is truely FPT.
Otherwise, one only obtains a

In our uses of
\cref

Geometric graphs

Axis-parallel unit segment graphs

An axis-parallel unit segment (APUS) is a segment of length 1 in the plane that is horizontal or vertical.
We denote by
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)
the horizontal segment from
(x,y)

(x+1,y)

\mathsf{Ver}(x,y)

to
(x,y+1)
.
An APUS graph is the intersection graph of a family of APUS.
We again work with discrete representations:

has a representation

such that there is some

A family

We say

is a non-degenerate APUS graph if it is the intersection graph of a non-degenerate APUS family.
This is equivalent to

We will use APUS representations with minimized coordinates, which is harder for non-degenerate APUS families.
Thus we prefer the second point of view on non-degenerate APUS graphs:
degenerate intersections can exist, but are ignored in intersection graphs.

This section proves

A natural attempt is to take the lexicographic ordering

of segments,
i.e.
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)<_{\mathsf{lex}}\mathsf{Hor}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
if and only if
x<x^{\prime}

x=x^{\prime}
and
y<y^{\prime}

imagine segments

h_{i},v_{i}
intersect close to

h_{i},v_{j}
do not intersect for

By slightly tweaking their

can be realised as the lexicographic ordering
<_{\mathsf{lex}}

[
4

).

The solution is to have two layers of lexicographic ordering, at local and global scales.
That is, we first order the sets
\mathcal{H}_{s,t}

(s,t)
,
and then inside each
\mathcal{H}_{s,t}
order the segments
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)
lexicographically as in
<_{\mathsf{lex}}
.
The same ordering applies to vertical segments.
Finally, all horizontal segments are placed before all vertical ones.
Crucially, in
<_{\mathsf{seg}}
, and unlike
<_{\mathsf{lex}}
, each set
\mathcal{H}_{s,t}
or
\mathcal{V}_{s,t}
is an interval.

Lemma 25
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of APUS of the form
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)
or
\mathsf{Ver}(x,y)
with
x,y\in[0,1)
.
If the matrix
M_{\mathcal{F}}
has no
k
-grid, then
\mathsf{tww}(G_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathsf{seg}})\leq f(k)
for some function
f
.

Proof.

Firstly, since the segments of
\mathcal{F}
have coordinates in the unit square
[0,1)^{2}
,
the ordering
<_{\mathsf{seg}}
by definition coincides with the lexicographic ordering
<_{\mathsf{lex}}
on
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}
denote the columns and rows of
M_{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. the sets of
x
- and
y
-coordinates of endpoints of segments in
\mathcal{F}
respectively.
We think of
\mathcal{F}
as a binary relation in
X\times Y
, where a segment
h_{x,y}
or
v_{x,y}
is interpreted as the pair
(x,y)
.
We order
X\cup Y
as
X<Y
, with the natural ordering inside each of them.
Then the adjacency matrix
Adj(\mathcal{F},<)
consists of
M_{\mathcal{F}}
, plus some empty zones to extend the columns to
Y
and rows to
X
.
Thus
Adj(\mathcal{F},<)
has no
k
-grid, and by
\cref
lem:incidence-matrix-grid, the incidence matrix
Inc(\mathcal{F},<,<_{\mathsf{lex}})
has no
4k
-grid.

Construct now the following ordered graph
H=(V,E,<)
.
The vertex set is
V\coloneqq X\uplus Y\uplus\mathcal{F}
, ordered as
X<Y<\mathcal{F}
, with the natural ordering inside
X,Y
, and the lexicographic ordering
<_{\mathsf{lex}}
inside
\mathcal{F}
.
The edges in
E
connect each segment
h_{x,y}
or
v_{x,y}
in
\mathcal{F}
to the coordinates
x\in X
and
y\in Y
of its endpoint.
Thus the adjacency matrix of
E
consists of
Inc(\mathcal{F},<,<_{\mathsf{lex}})
and its transpose, arranged as diagonal blocks.
One may check that this matrix has no
(4k+1)
-grid,
hence by
\cref
thm:grid-tww,
H
has twin-width bounded by some
f(k)
.

To conclude, it suffice to show that there is a fixed first-order transduction
\Phi
such that
G_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathsf{lex}}\in\Phi(H)
.
Indeed, this implies by
[
5
, Theorem 8.1]
that
\mathsf{tww}(G_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathsf{lex}})\leq g(\mathsf{tww}(H)\leq g(f(k))
for some function
g
depending only on
\Phi
, proving the result.
The transduction
\Phi
proceeds as follows given
H
:

•

Firstly, non-deterministic colouring is used to distinguish the subsets of vertices
X
,
Y
, and
\mathcal{F}
,
and to indicate whether a vertex
w\in\mathcal{F}
is an horizontal or vertical segment.

•

Next, observe that since the coordinates
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
are restricted to
[0,1)
, the segments
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)
and
\mathsf{Ver}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
intersect if and only
x\leq x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}\leq y
.
To test if two segments
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
intersect,
it thus suffices to check that (1) one is horizontal and the other vertical (using the colours given at step 1),
and (2) their coordinates satisfy the former inequalities.
The later is checked by following edges of
E
from
s,s^{\prime}
to their coordinates, and comparing them with the ordering
<
.

All of the above can be expressed as a first-order formula
\phi(s,s^{\prime})
.

•

Finally, vertices of
X,Y
are deleted, and the edge set is redefined by the formula
\phi(h,v)
above, while keeping the same ordering
<
whose restriction to
\mathcal{F}
is
<_{\mathsf{lex}}
.

Assuming that the correct non-deterministic choices are done in the colouring step,
it is clear that
\phi(s,s^{\prime})
holds if and only if
s,s^{\prime}
intersect,
and the structure obtained is thus
G_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathsf{lex}}=G_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathsf{seg}}
, proving the result.
∎

We now show that when
M_{\mathcal{F}_{s,t,d}}
does contain a grid, then
G_{\mathcal{F}}
contains a transversal pair.
As in
\cref
sec:cag, this requires some minimality hypothesis.

Here, we assume discrete coordinates as in
\cref
sec:prelim:geom,
i.e. in an APUS family
\mathcal{F}
, the coordinates of segments are non-negative multiples of some
\eta(\mathcal{F})>0
.
Subject to this condition, we call
\mathcal{F}

minimized
if its segments cannot be pushed towards the bottom-left while preserving the intersection graph.
Formally, for a fixed choice of
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{F})
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is minimized if there is no different family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with the same
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
and with a bijection
\phi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

that preserves the vertical/horizontal orientation, is an isomorphism of the intersection graphs,
and such that the endpoint of
\phi(s)
is coordinate-wise no larger than that of
s
.

Lemma 26
.

In a minimized APUS family
\mathcal{F}
, for any horizontal
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}
, there are:

1.

a vertical segment
\mathsf{Ver}(x-\eta,y^{\prime})
or
\mathsf{Ver}(x+1,y^{\prime})
with
y^{\prime}\leq y\leq y^{\prime}+1
, unless
x=0
, and

2.

a vertical segment
\mathsf{Ver}(x^{\prime},y-1-\eta)
or
\mathsf{Ver}(x^{\prime},y)
with
x\leq x^{\prime}\leq x+1
, unless
y=0
,

and similarly when flipping the roles of vertical and horizontal, and of
x
- and
y
-coordinates.

Proof.

If that is not the case,
\mathsf{Hor}(x,y)
can be moved to the left (in case 1) or the bottom (in case 2) by
\eta
without changing the intersection graph, contradicting minimality.
∎

4.3
Segments with a fixed set of permissible lengths

We now consider the generalisation of APUS from the last item of
\cref
thm:main:
axis-parallel segments whose lengths belong to a fixed, finite set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
By compactness, these lengths can be assumed to be rational, and thus up to rescaling,
this is equivalent to requiring segments to have integer lengths in
\{1,\dots,L\}
for some fixed
L\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 29
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of non-degenerate, axis-parallel segments graphs, with segment lengths in
[L]
for some fixed
L
.
Then
\mathcal{C}
has bounded twin-width if and only if it is monadically dependent.
Further, when
\mathcal{C}
is hereditary, and assuming
\mathsf{FPT}\neq\mathsf{AW}[*]
, these are also equivalent to FO model checking in
\mathcal{C}
(given by segment representations) being FPT.

Sketch of proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of axis-parallel segments with lengths in
[L]
.
We once again partition the segments according to their direction, the unit square containing their first endpoint, and now also their length.
That is, define
\mathcal{H}_{s,t,\ell}
to contain all horizontal segments in
\mathcal{F}
of length
\ell
with their left endpoint in
[s,s+1)\times[t,t+1)
, and similarly
\mathcal{V}_{s,t,\ell}
for vertical ones.
Clearly segments of
\mathcal{H}_{s,t,\ell}
and
\mathcal{V}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}
can only intersect when
|s-s^{\prime}|
and
|t-t^{\prime}|
are at most L.
We also generalise the global ordering
<_{\mathsf{seg}}
of segments to account for segment lengths.
The ordering
<_{\mathsf{seg}}
sorts segments according to, in that order: (1) direction, (2) segment length, (3) the square containing the endpoint, lexicographically, and finally (4) the endpoint coordinate, lexicographically.

Generalising
\cref
lem:unitgrid-apus, if the ordered intersection graph induced by
\mathcal{H}_{s,t,\ell}\cup\mathcal{V}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}
has twin-width at most
k
for all parameters
s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime},\ell,\ell^{\prime}
,
then the full ordered intersection graph
(\mathcal{F},E,<_{\mathsf{seg}})
has twin-width bounded by a function of
k
and
L
.
The proof is essentially the same:
Each edge of
G_{\mathcal{F}}
joins some sets
\mathcal{H}_{s,t,\ell}
and
\mathcal{V}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}
,
with
i\coloneqq s-s^{\prime}
and
j\coloneqq t-t^{\prime}
both in
\{-L,\dots,L\}
.
Then, we classify all edges of
G_{\mathcal{F}}
according to the offsets
i,j
and the lengths
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
as above.
This is a partition of the edge set into
O(L^{4})
groups, and by
\cref
thm:ordered-tww-ramsey, it suffices to prove that each of these classes of edges gives an ordered subgraph of bounded twin-width.
The latter is obtained by using
\cref
lem:ordered-components exactly as in the proof of
\cref
lem:unitgrid-apus.

Next, with essentially no modifications,
\cref
lem:gridthm-apus,lem:transversal-apus can be adapted to show that:

•

If the endpoints of segments in
\mathcal{H}_{s,t,\ell}\cup\mathcal{V}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime},\ell}
do not form a grid,
then the corresponding induced subgraph, ordered by
<_{\mathsf{seg}}
, has bounded twin-width, and

•

if however the endpoints do form an
f(k)
-grid (for appropriate
f
), and
\mathcal{F}
has been minimized, then
G_{\mathcal{F}}
contains the transversal pair
T_{k}
.

These results combine to prove the theorem in the same way as
\cref
thm:delineation-apus.
∎

5
Obstructions to delineation

In this section, we construct graphs with unbounded twin-width, but for which FO model checking is FPT.
We use them to show that delineation fails for 1.5D-terrain visibility graphs,
and also when relaxing any of the hypotheses of
\cref
thm:delineation-apus.

Precisely, we construct a class
\mathcal{H}
of graphs combining half-graphs and paths of slightly more than constant length,
and prove that is has unbounded twin-width, but bounded
merge-width

[
9
]
.
The latter implies FPT model checking and monadic dependence.
The class
\mathcal{H}
differs significantly from the class of all subcubic graphs used as the basic example of non-delineation in
[
2
]
:
any monadically stable (i.e. that does not transduce all half-graphs) subclass of
\mathcal{H}
has bounded twin-width.
This gives a concrete counter-example to
[
2
, Conjecture 6.6]
.
Samuel Braunfeld previously found a different, non-explicit counter-example to this conjecture (personal communication).

.

Corollary 33
.

The class
\mathcal{H}
has unbounded twin-width, but is monadically dependent, and FO model checking in
\mathcal{H}
is FPT.

Proof.

The class
\mathcal{H}
has unbounded twin-width by
\cref
fact:logn-subdiv-tww, and bounded merge-width by
\cref
fact:subdiv-mw.
The latter implies monadically dependent and FPT model checking by
\cref
thm:mw-model-checking.
∎

Fact 34
.

For
f(n)=o(\log n)
a positive function,
\mathcal{G}_{f}
has unbounded twin-width.

Proof.

We denote by
K_{n}^{\ell}
the clique with
n
vertices, where every edge is replaced by a path of length
\ell
. For a positive function
g
, consider the class
\mathcal{K}_{g}\coloneqq\{K_{n}^{g(n)}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. It is known that
\mathcal{K}_{g}
has unbounded twin-width whenever
g(n)=o(\log n)
and
g(n)\geq 2

[
3
, Theorem 6.2]
. We show that
\mathcal{G}_{f}
transduces
\mathcal{K}_{g}
for
g(n)\coloneqq f(n^{2})+2=o(\log n)
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{f}
also has unbounded twin-width by
[
5
, Theorem 8.1]
.

We construct an FO transduction
\Phi
such that
\Phi(G_{\sigma}^{\ell})\ni K_{n}^{\ell+2}
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\sigma
will be chosen appropriately later on. Let
A,B,C
be the partition of vertices of
G_{\sigma}^{\ell}
as in its description. The vertices in
B
are partitioned into
n
parts
B_{k}=\{b_{k}^{1},\dots,b_{k}^{n}\}
for
k\in[n]
, each of which contains
n
vertices. We choose every
n
-th vertex from
C
to let
C^{\prime}=\{c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{n}^{\prime}\}
, which form a half-graph with the vertices in
B
in such a way that
c_{i}^{\prime}
is connected with all the vertices in
B_{j}
for
j\leq i
.
We want
b_{j}^{i}
and
b_{i}^{j}
to be connected by disjoint paths
P_{i,j}
of length
\ell
whose inner vertices are in
A
, for
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
Since these
\binom{n}{2}
paths are disjoint, we can choose a permutation
\sigma
such that the path induced by
A
in
G_{\sigma}^{\ell}
extends all of them.

Now the transduction
\Phi
is a composition of the following transductions, which is then again a transduction due to
\cref
lem:transduction-comp.
Note that taking an arbitrary induced subgraph is a transductions, and so is removing all edges between two sets.

•

Take the induced subgraph consisting of vertices in
C^{\prime}
,
B_{k}\setminus\{b_{k}^{k}\}
for
k\in[n]
, and
A^{\prime}\coloneqq V(P_{i,j})\cap A
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

•

Remove all edges in the clique
G_{\sigma}^{\ell}[C^{\prime}]
, and in the biclique
G_{\sigma}^{\ell}[C^{\prime}\cup A^{\prime}]
.

•

Remove edges between
c_{i}^{\prime}
and the vertices in
B_{j}
for
j<i
by using an FO interpretation similar to the one used in
\cref
fact:logn-subdiv-tww.

Then the output of
\Phi
is
K_{n}^{\ell+2}

On the other hand, using
\cref

Let

For any bipartite graph

G
once, and taking the complement of this subdivision, except that no edge is added between
U

Corollary 36
.

There is a hereditary class
\mathcal{C}
of
H_{\infty}
-graphs that is monadically dependent and has FPT FO model checking,
but has unbounded merge-width (and thus unbounded twin-width).

Proof.

Take
\mathcal{D}
a class of bipartite graphs that is nowhere dense but has unbounded expansion (see e.g.
[
16
]
for the definitions).
These are typically obtained from graphs with girth and minimum degree both growing to infinity.
By
[
9
, Corollary 1.8]
, the class
\mathcal{D}
also has unbounded merge-width.
Then the class of 1-subdivisions of graphs in
\mathcal{D}
is still nowhere dense (immediate from the definition of nowhere dense),
and still has unbounded merge-width (because one can FO transduce
\mathcal{D}
from the class of 1-subdivisions of
\mathcal{D}
, and FO transductions preserve bounded merge-width
[
9
, Theorem 1.12]
).
Note that nowhere dense implies FPT FO model checking
[
12
]
and monadically dependent
[
1
]
.

Now let
\mathcal{C}=\{G^{\bullet}:G\in\mathcal{D}\}
be obtained by the previous construction of
H_{\infty}
-graphs.
The graph
G^{\bullet}
is obtained from the 1-subdivision of
G
by complementing edges between the appropriate sets of vertices.
In the terminology of e.g.
[
8
]
, this means that
G^{\bullet}
is obtained from the 1-subdivision by a bounded number of
flips
.
Flips are a very simple case of FO transduction, and thus preserve monadic dependence, FPT model checking, bounded twin-width, and bounded merge-width.
It follows that
\mathcal{C}
still is monadically dependent and has FPT model checking, while having unbounded merge-width and twin-width.
∎

Finally, if
H
is any graph with two cycles (not necessarily disjoint) in the same connected component,
then
H
contains
H_{\infty}
as a minor (if we adapt the usual notion of minors to allow double edges and self loops).
It is simple to check that when
H^{\prime}
is a minor of
H
, then any
H^{\prime}
-graph is also an
H
-graph.
Thus the non-delineated class
\mathcal{C}
of
\cref

\cref

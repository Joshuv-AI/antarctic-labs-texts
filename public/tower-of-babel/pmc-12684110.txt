Immune-modulating effects on tumor-draining lymph nodes of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy combined with dual immunotherapy in patients with T3-4N0-2 NSCLC
Authors: Ezgi B Ulas, Sofie J I Koomen, Anne Vrijmoet, Ilias Houda, H Ibrahim Korkmaz, Chris Dickhoff, Idris Bahce, Suresh Senan, Tanja D de Gruijl, Marieke F Fransen, Teodora Radonic, Febe van Maldegem, Famke L Schneiders
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTumor-draining lymph nodes (TDLN) play a key role in inducing and promoting antitumor immunity. TDLN are commonly situated near the primary tumor and are therefore often exposed to therapeutic radiation. The impact of induction therapies comprising concurrent immunotherapy and radiation on TDLN is poorly understood. We studied the immune-modulating effects in TDLN of patients with T3-4N0-2 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) using a combination of spatial transcriptomics and immunohistochemical analyses.MethodsThis observational cohort study collected TDLN from 2 groups: (1) patients from the INCREASE trial who were treated with neoadjuvant ipilimumab/nivolumab (IPI/NIVO) plus chemoradiotherapy (CRT) (n=25) and (2) a matched control cohort of patients with NSCLC who had neoadjuvant CRT only (n=25). TDLN were classified based on the cumulative dose of radiation received, categorized as low (≤5 Gy), intermediate (20–30 Gy), or high dose (50–60 Gy). The TDLN were subjected to duplex immunohistochemistry of CD8/Ki67, PD-1/FOXP3, and CD8/cleaved caspase-3. On a subset of TDLN, additional GeoMx spatial transcriptomics profiling of CD8/Ki67+T cell hotspots was carried out.ResultsAfter addition of IPI/NIVO to CRT in the INCREASE cohort, resected TDLN showed robust type I immune responses and increased levels of CD8 and regulatory T cells in irradiated TDLN when compared with the control cohort. These immune responses were observed across all radiation dose groups, with the most pronounced effects in high dose TDLN. Significant changes in extracellular matrix and macrophage-associated gene signatures, indicating elevated fibrosis and prolonged inflammation, were observed in the TDLN with high radiation exposure, changes which were partially alleviated by immunotherapy.ConclusionIn the INCREASE trial, neoadjuvant IPI/NIVO combined with CRT for patients with T3–4N0–2 NSCLC elicited enhanced immune responses in TDLN despite exposure to high dose radiation. Even though radiation-induced fibrosis was evident in high dose TDLN, the immune responses were not diminished when compared with low dose TDLN. These findings underscore the resilience of TDLN immunological function under intense radiation exposure.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

As expected, fibrosis and prolonged inflammation were observed in TDLN exposed to high doses of radiation.

Background

The standard treatment of locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) aims to optimize both local control and prevent distant metastases. This is typically achieved through a multimodality approach, most commonly consisting of platinum-based doublet chemotherapy combined with concurrent radiotherapy (CRT). In patients with unresectable disease, which makes up the majority of cases, CRT is followed by consolidation durvalumab.1 In a smaller subset of patients with borderline resectable tumors, surgical resection following CRT may be performed to further improve local control.

To build on this strategy, we previously conducted the phase 2 INCREASE trial, which explored whether local and systemic disease control could be improved by adding dual immunotherapy, specifically ipilimumab (IPI, anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4)) and nivolumab (NIVO, anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)), to standard CRT followed by surgery.2 This intensified approach resulted in a doubling of the pathological complete response (pCR) rate compared with historical controls, highlighting the potential synergy between CRT and dual immunotherapy.3

Tumor-draining lymph nodes (TDLN) take a key position in the cancer-immunity cycle and are thought to play a contributing role in immunotherapy efficacy.4 Tumor antigens are captured by dendritic cells and migrated to the TDLN, where (cross-)presentation of epitopes is performed to initiate antitumor T-cell responses, mediated by dual immunotherapy.4 5 During radiotherapy treatment, the TDLN are frequently encompassed within the radiation field, yet the extent to which irradiation affects their essential role in immune priming remains incompletely understood. Unintended radiation to the TDLN may damage lymphatic architecture and proliferating T cells, thereby impairing the normal trafficking and immune cell function. Chronic inflammation in irradiated lymph nodes may lead to tissue remodeling and fibrosis, which could further disrupt lymphatic drainage and hinder the initiation of effective immune responses.68 This hampers lymphoid tissue regeneration and immune memory formation, while persistent inflammation causes terminal immune exhaustion and possibly a reduced efficacy of immunotherapy.5 Previous research in metastatic models treated with combined radio-immunotherapy showed restored local and systemic antitumor responses when nodal irradiation was delayed by 1 week, highlighting the importance of preserving TDLN function for optimal therapeutic outcomes within the immune oncology context.9 Altogether, the interactions between radiotherapy, immunotherapy, and TDLN remain poorly understood, highlighting an important gap in current knowledge.

We hypothesized that adding immune checkpoint inhibitors to standard CRT could boost immune activation in TDLN, but that this effect might be influenced by the radiation dose received by these lymph nodes. To explore this, the present study aimed to investigate the impact of adding dual immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) to standard CRT, as well as the influence of varying radiation doses, on the immune cell subsets within TDLN from patients with T3-4N0-2 NSCLC. Analyses included samples from all resected patients enrolled in the INCREASE trial, as well as a matched cohort of historical controls who underwent CRT without immunotherapy followed by surgery.

Methods

Study population and case–control matching

This single-center study was conducted at the Amsterdam University Medical Centers (Amsterdam UMC) and included two distinct groups of patients. The first group consisted of patients participating in the INCREASE trial (EudraCT-Number: 2019–003454–83) who underwent the per-protocol treatment consisting of neoadjuvant IPI/NIVO+CRT followed by surgery.3 The second group included a historical control cohort matched to the first group, who received standard of care neoadjuvant CRT followed by surgery. Clinical data and pathological responses were extracted retrospectively from medical records and collected in electronic databases. Major pathological response (MPR) was defined as the presence of ≤10% viable tumor cells in the resected material. pCR was defined as the presence of no viable tumor cells (0%) in the resected material. The institutional review board granted ethical approval, and all patients consented to the collection of their data (METc VUmc numbers 2019.710 and 2021.0163).

Between 2020 and 2022, patients with cT3-4N0-2 NSCLC tumors (International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) eighth tumor, node, metastases (TNM) edition10) by radiological imaging and pathological assessment on baseline biopsies were included in the INCREASE trial.2 3 These patients received a treatment regimen of two cycles of chemotherapy (carboplatin+pemetrexed and carboplatin+etoposide for non-squamous and squamous tumors, respectively) alongside IPI/NIVO (1 mg/kg intravenous IPI and 360 mg flat dose NIVO) and a radiation dose of 50 or 60 Gy, as once daily doses of 2 Gy. One cycle of IPI/NIVO and chemotherapy was administered on the first day of radiotherapy and 3 weeks later, the second cycle of NIVO and chemotherapy was given. Surgery was scheduled 6 weeks following the completion of neoadjuvant therapy. The inclusion and exclusion criteria of the INCREASE patients have been reported previously.2

The control patients were matched one-to-one with the INCREASE patients based on baseline characteristics including age, sex, histology and TNM stage (table 1). A maximum age difference of 10 years was allowed for matching. Control patients were eligible for inclusion if they met the same criteria applied to INCREASE patients, except that they received the standard neoadjuvant CRT treatment regimen, without immunotherapy, followed by surgery after a comparable 6-week interval.11 12 These patients were treated between 2012 and 2020. Furthermore, inclusion was dependent on successful matching with an INCREASE patient and only if resected lymph nodes from surgery were available and adequately stored.

TDLN selection and sample processing

All resected TDLN during surgery were collected, formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) and stored as tissue blocks. The TDLN were selected and used for further analysis based on the received cumulative radiation as determined from the isodoses of the treatment plan. Radiation doses were categorized as low (≤5 Gy), intermediate (20–30 Gy) and high dose (50–60 Gy). The aim was to include one TDLN from each radiation category per patient. If multiple TDLNs were available within a single category, the lymph node with the largest long-axis diameter was selected.

For immunohistochemistry (IHC), the FFPE blocks of the selected TDLN were cut into 4 µM thick sections and mounted on glass TOMO slides for duplex staining. From the TDLN selected for IHC, we planned to include a low (≤5 Gy) and high (50–60 Gy) radiation dose-exposed TDLN from each included patient to construct tissue microarray (TMA) blocks for downstream spatial transcriptomic and histochemical analyses. These TDLNs were represented in the TMA blocks by 1.5 mm2 cores and additional cores from tonsil were added to the TMA blocks for orientation. The cores were selected based on the presence of Ki67+CD8 T-cell hotspots observed by duplex IHC.

(Immuno)histochemical analysis

The selected whole TDLN sections were subjected to duplex IHC, with antibody pairs for CD8/Ki67, PD-1/FOXP3, and CD8/cleaved caspase-3, using the Ventana staining system. The slides were evaluated in regions of T-cell hotspots, visually selected based on the high presence of double-positive (CD8/Ki67) cells. Three regions of each 0.347 mm2 were chosen per TDLN in which the (double) positive cells were manually counted. The separate counts were added together to result in one final number per TDLN. Analyses were performed by two separate experienced individuals (EBU and AV) and in case of differences, a consensus on the final number of counted cells was reached. On consecutive 4 µM thick TMA sections, a picrosirius red (PSR) staining was performed using the Ventana staining system, while IHC staining for collagen I was conducted separately in the laboratory. An overview of all used antibodies is presented in online supplemental table 1 and further elaboration on the staining methods is described in the online supplemental methods. All slides were scanned using the Olympus vs200 ASW research slide scanner.

NanoString GeoMx digital spatial profiler

Using the GeoMx digital spatial profiler (DSP) platform through the NanoString Technology Access Program, 4 µM thick TMA sections were cut and stained with a cocktail of four fluorescently labeled antibodies (CD11c-FITC, CD8a-Texas Red, Ki-67-Cy5 and DNA-Cy3) combined with the Whole Transcriptome Atlas oligo-tagged in situ hybridization probe panel. 24 regions of interest (ROIs) were selected based on T-cell hotspots characterized by the abundance of Ki67 and CD8a double-positive cells. On ultraviolet light exposure, oligos from the ROIs were released and collected in microplate wells. The photocleaved oligos were sequenced using an Illumina NGS instrument. An overview of all morphology markers used for NanoString GeoMx DSP is presented in online supplemental table 2.

The Workflow Of Matrix BioLogy Informatics

To determine collagen content and identify matrix patterns, PSR and collagen I-stained TDLN images were pre-processed before running them through The Workflow Of Matrix BioLogy Informatics (TWOMBLI) in Fiji/ImageJ (V.2.14.0).13 A custom macro was used to remove any non-specific tar-related background signal (black), which impaired proper identification of the collagen fibers, stained red and brown for the PSR and collagen I stains, respectively (online supplemental figure 4A,B). Each image was separated into its separate red, green and blue channels, which were selectively subtracted for the PSR and collagen I stain. 8-bit images were created and used for further analysis. Subsequently, TWOMBLI was run using specific parameters for both PSR and collagen I, which are described in the online supplemental methods. The following metrics were calculated from the line masks, as generated by the Ridge Detection Tool: lacunarity, total length of the fibers, average fiber length, number of endpoints, hyphal growth unit (HGU), number of branch points, fractal dimension, curvature and alignment. Average fiber length was calculated by dividing the total fiber length by 0.5 times the number of endpoints plus the number of branch points. High-density matrix (HDM) values were derived from the HDM images generated.

Statistical analysis

Patient characteristics were analyzed by χ2 tests using SPSS software V.28 (SPSS, Chicago, Illinois, USA). GeoMx transcriptome data analysis was performed in R (V.4.3.2). Spatial deconvolution was performed using the SpatialDecon package and the SafeTME reference matrix. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) were performed using the GSVA package (Bioconductor) and clusterProfiler (Bioconductor) package, respectively, to assess pathway activity across samples. The type I immune response signature was constructed according to gene lists reported in previous studies.1416 Plots were visualized using the ggplot2 (tidyverse) and pheatmap (CRAN) libraries. Cell counts from IHC slides and collagen metrics were statistically analyzed using non-parametric tests; Mann-Whitney U for INCREASE versus control and high dose versus low dose radiation comparisons, and one-way analysis of variance (Kruskal-Wallis test) followed by post hoc Dunn’s multiple comparisons test for conditions grouped based on both patient cohort and radiation dose. Paired low and high dose analyses were performed by Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed rank tests. These tests were performed and visually displayed using GraphPad Prism (V.10.2.0). P values<0.05 were considered statistically significant (*p<0.05, **p<0.01, ***p<0.001).

Results

Patient enrollment and sample selection

A total of 32 patients were enrolled in the INCREASE trial, of which 25 completed neoadjuvant treatment and continued to surgery. Historical databases consisting of 598 patients with resected NSCLC were assessed for eligibility. A total of 124 patients were selected for further analysis, from which 25 patients were identified after one-on-one matching to the INCREASE patients, leading to a total study sample size of 50 patients. Online supplemental figure 1 depicts the flow chart of inclusion.

Table 1 displays the baseline characteristics used for matching. The non-significant p values between the two cohorts indicate that the matching process resulted in comparable groups. In the INCREASE cohort, MPR and pCR were achieved in 76% (19/25) and 60% (15/25) of patients, respectively.3 In the control cohort, MPR and pCR were achieved in 92% (23/25) and 36% (9/25) of patients, respectively.

Following selection based on the radiation dose categories, a total of 80 TDLN were included for further analysis: 33 from 25 patients in the control cohort and 47 from 25 patients in the INCREASE cohort. All TDLN were subjected to IHC duplex staining. Additionally, 48 of these TDLN (24 from each cohort, of which 12 low dose and 12 high dose) were incorporated into the TMA, with each TDLN represented by two 1.5 mm² cores. For most of the patients from the INCREASE cohort (n=11), we selected TDLN from both the high dose and low dose radiation category per patient, whereas this was not always possible in the control cohort (n=3) due to a smaller overall availability of TDLN resected during surgery. Eight additional cores from tonsil were added in each TMA block for orientation. For 24 of these TDLNs, an ROI was chosen for GeoMx analysis. Matching between the INCREASE and controls was ensured as much as possible when choosing the 24 TDLN, while taking into account the limited number of control patients with both low and high doses available. Ultimately, the low and high dose TDLN of 6 INCREASE patients, and low and/or high dose of 11 controls were selected for GeoMx analysis. An overview of the TMA design is presented in online supplemental figure 2 and the baseline patient characteristics corresponding to the chosen TDLN for GeoMx analysis are depicted in online supplemental table 3.

IHC analysis reveals increased Ki67+ CD8 T cells and T regulatory cells within TDLN after dual immunotherapy and exposure to high dose radiation therapy

A total of 80 TDLN, 47 from the INCREASE and 33 from the control cohort, were subjected to CD8/Ki67, FOXP3/PD-1 and CD8/cleaved caspase-3 duplex IHC. Figures1 2 show an overview of representative IHC stains from both cohorts with low-high dose differentiation and the quantitative analyses, respectively. Significantly increased numbers of proliferating CD8 T cells and activated T regulatory cells (Tregs) were observed in the INCREASE cohort compared with the control cohort (figure 2A). There was no significant difference in apoptotic CD8 T cells. The increased quantities of activated CD8 T cells in the INCREASE cohort were also consistently measured within the radiation exposure categories separately (figure 2B). Furthermore, a dose-dependent relationship was noted for the INCREASE cohort, with the highest radiation dose resulting in higher numbers of activated CD8 T cells as compared with the lowest dose, though in a smaller set of paired samples, this trend did not reach significance (online supplemental figure 3). Increases in activated Treg numbers in the INCREASE cohort were found significant in the intermediate and high dose levels. Frequencies of Ki67+CD8 T cells, activated Tregs and apoptotic CD8 T cells in the TDLN did not correlate with pathological responses in the tumor (online supplemental figure 4). A comparison between the presence of cN0 and cN+ disease determined at baseline did not lead to significant differences (online supplemental figure 5).

Deconvolution of spatial transcriptomics data reveals differences in cell type distribution patterns in relation to TDLN radiation dose and dual immunotherapy

After staining for CD11c-FITC, CD8a-Texas Red, Ki-67-Cy5 and DNA-Cy3 antibodies, 24 T-cell hotspots (Ki67/CD8) were selected for GeoMx spatial transcriptomics. Cellular deconvolution of the data was performed to estimate the abundance of different immune cell subsets and stromal cells in the TDLN (figure 3A). Deconvolution revealed an unusually high abundance of naive CD4 T cells and plasma cells, respectively, in the low dose and high dose lymph node of INCREASE patient 23. This patient was subsequently found to have received a blood transfusion after developing autoimmune hemolytic anemia and was therefore excluded from all further analysis. Within the control cohort, increased proportions of naive B cells and a trend towards increased fibroblast proportions were observed in the TDLN that received a high radiation dose, compared with the low dose-irradiated TDLN (figure 3B,C). The effect of radiation exposure on the proportion of naive B cells and fibroblasts was abrogated in the INCREASE cohort, with fibroblast proportions significantly lower in the high dose-irradiated TDLN of the INCREASE cohort compared with those of the control cohort (figure 3C). The INCREASE cohort also exhibited a significantly lower proportion of mast cells and higher proportion of conventional dendritic cells (cDCs) and memory CD8 T (CD8 Tm) cells, compared with the control cohort (figure 3D–F). The abundance of mast cells, cDCs and CD8 Tm cells was not affected by high-dose radiation of the TDLN (online supplemental figure 6). In the INCREASE cohort, higher proportions of CD8 Tm cells and cDCs were associated with better pathological response, although this trend did not reach significance (online supplemental figure 7). Together with the increase in the proportion of CD8 Tm cells in the INCREASE cohort, Tregs show a similar trend, but only in the TDLN exposed to a high radiation dose (figure 3G).

Radiation-induced fibrosis and prolonged inflammation of the TDLN is potentially less severe when combined with dual immunotherapy

Radiation-induced fibrosis arises from a dysregulated wound healing response, in which persistent inflammation and fibroblast activation drive excessive collagen deposition, primarily type I and type III collagen, and extracellular matrix remodeling.17 18 With high-dose radiation, irrespective of ICI addition, an increase in type IV collagen (COL4A1 and COL4A2) and basic fibroblast growth factor, and a decrease in matrix metalloproteinases (MMP) 2 and 9 expression was observed in the TDLN (figure 4A). Although transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β; TGFB1) expression was elevated in the high dose irradiated TDLN of INCREASE patients, the expression of type I collagen (COL1A1 and COL1A2), tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMP) 1 and 3 were specifically upregulated in the high dose irradiated TDLN of the control cohort patients (figure 4A). The GO_EXTRACELLULAR_MATRIX and GOCC_COLLAGEN_CONTAINING_EXTRACELLULAR_MATRIX pathways showed elevated activity in the high dose irradiated TDLN of the control cohort, compared with TDLN of the INCREASE cohort (figure 4B,C). Additionally, MYOFIBROBLAST and HP_POOR_WOUND_HEALING pathway activity was highest in the high dose irradiated TDLN of the control cohort, with both signatures being decreased in the INCREASE TDLN, compared with control TDLN (figure 4D,E). Additional evidence of a radiation-induced dysregulated wound healing response comes from the persistent inflammation, characterized by complement activity and presence of macrophages and their pro-inflammatory mediators (interleukin (IL)-1, IL-6, IL-8 and tumor necrosis factor).19 GOBP_COMPLEMENT_ACTIVATION displayed increased activity with high dose radiation in the control cohort, but not in the INCREASE cohort (figure 4F). Pro-inflammatory M1 macrophage genes and inflammatory response cytokines showed elevated pathway activity in TDLN of the control cohort, compared with TDLN of the INCREASE cohort (figure 4G,H).

Dual immunotherapy induces a type I immune response, which is not inhibited by high radiation dose

In line with the observed increase in proportion of CD8 Tm cells in the TDLN from the INCREASE patients compared with the control cohort (figure 3F), we observed an enhanced type I immune response in the INCREASE cohort,1416 as indicated by elevated expression of multiple genes expressed by cytotoxic T cells (CTLs), T helper 1 (Th1) cells and type 1 innate lymphoid cells (ILC1s) (figure 6A,B). However, type I immune response pathway activity did not correlate with pathological responses in the tumor (online supplemental figure 9A,B). Notably, several cytotoxicity-associated genes exhibited their highest expression levels in the high dose TDLN of the INCREASE cohort (figure 6A), with type I immune response pathway activity elevated compared with the low dose TDLN (online supplemental figure 9C), potentially reflecting the anatomical proximity of the high dose TDLN to the tumor site. Despite downregulation of some interferon-related genes (IFNGR1, IFNGR2, IFNA14, IL-27), GSEA revealed significant enrichment of hallmark interferon gamma response and positive but non-significant enrichment of hallmark interferon alpha response in high dose irradiated TDLN of the INCREASE cohort, compared with low dose TDLN (online supplemental figure 10). Pathway activity of gene sets related to T cell-mediated cytotoxicity and Th1 cytokine production was elevated in the INCREASE cohort compared with the control cohort, and pathway activity was not affected by intensified radiation (figure 6C,D).

Discussion

Our study is, to our knowledge, the first to directly compare the immune-modulating effects on TDLN of patients with T3-4N0-2 NSCLC between standard neoadjuvant CRT and its combination with IPI/NIVO. We demonstrate a marked increase in activated CD8 T cells and Tregs following IPI/NIVO addition. Substantial radiation-induced alterations in extracellular matrix and macrophage-associated gene signatures were observed in response to radiation, indicative of elevated fibrosis, prolonged inflammation and tissue repair responses. Furthermore, IPI/NIVO addition elicited strong type I immune responses, which were not abrogated by high dose radiation when compared with low dose radiation, suggesting that high dose nodal irradiation, even when combined with dual immunotherapy, is not detrimental to their immunological function.

With the combination of treatment modalities in a neoadjuvant setting, there was a legitimate concern that the immunological function of the TDLN, essential for an effective immunotherapy response, could be compromised. Localized radiation promotes immunogenic cell death of tumor cells and release of tumor-associated antigens, thereby enhancing systemic immune responses and even tumor regression outside the irradiated field by the so-called abscopal effect.20 21 By combining radiation with dual immunotherapy by anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4, this effect could potentially be enhanced due to the synergy of these two agents.22 Yet, preclinical models showed restrained immune responses and reduced abscopal effects when TDLN were irradiated,23 24 emphasizing the need for in-depth analysis.

Our data suggest that IPI/NIVO combined with CRT led to enhanced immune responses in the TDLN, which were not abrogated by high radiation exposure, as seen by elevated counts of CD8 T cells and Tregs, irrespective of received dose. Increased collagen and extracellular matrix signatures were observed in the high radiation TDLN, driven by increased expression of TIMPs and decreased expression of MMPs, which are known to be indicative of radiation-induced fibrosis.171925 26 This was further supported by the decreased average fiber length and HGU of collagen in the high dose TDLN.19 27 28 Fibrosis-related gene signatures, M1 macrophage and IL-6 signaling gene signatures were reduced in TDLN of the INCREASE cohort compared with the control cohort, suggesting that dual immunotherapy addition is associated with alleviation of radiation-induced dysregulated inflammation and fibrosis. Notably, high-dose radiation was not accompanied by less expression of the genes associated with CTLs, Th1 cells and ILC1s, which are related to type I immune response,1416 and in both the INCREASE and control cohorts, the significant differences between radiation categories showed higher frequencies in the higher dose category rather than the lower dose radiation category. The non-significant differences in apoptotic CD8 T cells between the two cohorts and within radiation categories further strengthen the conclusion that high radiation did not impair the immunological function of the TDLN. It is important to consider that 6 weeks post-CRT may still be an early time point to fully assess fibrotic remodeling. Although fibrosis-related transcriptional programming seems to be active, corresponding changes in collagen deposition may not yet be fully manifested or detectable at this stage. Notably, the addition of dual ICI to CRT even reduced the extent of radiation-induced fibrosis in the high radiation TDLN shown by the decrease in number of endpoints of collagen fibers, thereby supporting the possible synergetic effect of radio-immunotherapy combination.

The results argue against radiation-induced lymph node immunosuppression as a cause for the lack of added clinical benefit in radioimmunotherapy combinations. The PACIFIC trial demonstrated significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival in patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC when durvalumab was given as consolidation therapy following CRT.29 In contrast, the PACIFIC-2 trial, which evaluated concurrent durvalumab with CRT followed by consolidation, failed to demonstrate a survival benefit compared with CRT alone (median PFS 13.8 vs 9.4 months; HR 0.85, p=0.247).30 This divergence is similarly observed in other studies,3133 and highlights a potential window of opportunity for immune activation post-CRT that may be blunted when ICI is given concurrently. Although our study showed favorable TDLN characteristics with dual ICI added to CRT, we cannot rule out the possibility that adjuvant ICI administration could have yielded superior survival outcomes, which was observed in other cancers.34 35 Furthermore, immune responses in the TDLN did not correlate with the pathological response in the tumor, indicating that other factors, such as an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment or radiation-induced lymphopenia,36 may also influence pathological outcomes. Interestingly, while lymph node metastases are generally associated with immunosuppression within the TDLN and reduced efficacy of ICIs, contributing to poorer survival outcomes,37 our analysis did not reveal significant differences in the cellular immune landscape of non-malignant TDLN between patients with clinically node-negative and node-positive disease.

The timing, fractionation and sequencing of radioimmunotherapy are critical in determining the efficacy of the treatment. The limited studies evaluating specifically the effects of radio-immunotherapy on TDLN most often use mouse models and single fractions of high dose radiation combined with either anti-CTLA-4 or anti-PD-1 monotherapy.9 24 38 This differs from clinical protocols using fractionated radiotherapy (25×2 Gy or 30×2 Gy), potentially in combination with dual immunotherapy in the context of a trial. Our study showed substantially increased pCR rates in the INCREASE cohort compared with the control cohort, underlining the efficacy of dual immunotherapy combined with CRT that might be attributed to increased antitumor responses within the TDLN.3

A possible confounding factor in the relationship between the received radiation dose and immune responses could be the precise location of the irradiated TDLN. The radiation dose received by the TDLN is contingent on its location within the radiation field, meaning that TDLN that received high dose radiation (50–60 Gy) were more proximally located to the tumor, whereas low dose irradiated (≤5 Gy) TDLN were positioned further away. Differences in immune responses could have been influenced not only by the radiation dose but also by the proximity of the TDLN to the tumor and the associated tumor microenvironmental factors.

Our findings underscore the importance of recognizing the immunological function of the TDLN when administering systemic treatment and combining them with other modalities such as (chemo)radiotherapy. Further research in this area is warranted to fully harness the immunostimulatory potential of the TDLN, with the broader aim of optimizing neoadjuvant treatment strategies in NSCLC and ultimately improving oncological outcomes.

Conclusion

This study showed enhanced immune responses in TDLN of patients with borderline resectable T3-4N0-2 NSCLC treated with neoadjuvant IPI/NIVO concurrent with CRT as compared with neoadjuvant CRT alone, marked by elevated frequencies of proliferating CD8 T cells alongside increased Treg levels and accompanied by upregulation of transcriptional programs indicative of type I immune responses. Enhanced immune activity in the TDLN persisted despite high-dose radiation exposure, highlighting the resilience of TDLN immunological function under intense radiation. High radiation exposure to the TDLN led to elevated fibrosis and prolonged inflammation, which was less pronounced in TDLN of patients treated with concurrent IPI/NIVO. Additional investigation is essential to fully exploit the TDLN’s immunomodulatory capacity in the neoadjuvant setting for NSCLC.

Cognitive integration of internet of things and feedforward learning models for smart irrigation in sustainable agriculture
Authors: Nalla Sandhya Rani, N. Sharmila Banu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Agriculture is regarded as the backbone of any country’s economy and heavily relies on water resources. To optimize the use of water resources, farmers employ various irrigation systems, such as drip, sprinkler, and linear motion models, to achieve better cultivation and improved crop yields. However, uncertain water distribution and the lack of adoption of intelligent techniques often result in the inefficient utilization of water resources, leading to excessive absorption that hampers agricultural sustainability. To address this challenge, Automated Irrigation Systems (AIS) have been introduced, significantly enhancing water conservation and promoting crop yields. In recent advancements, AIS has been further improved through the incorporation of the Internet of Things (IoT) and Artificial Intelligence (AI). While existing AIS models often suffer from high computational latency due to complex data processing and algorithmic demands, our proposed model tackles this challenge through streamlined data handling and a more efficient learning framework. This research article introduces an innovative framework incorporating IoT and a Feed Forward Learning Model (FFLM) to maximize efficiency in water conservation and provide farmers with actionable insights into sustainable water usage. The framework consists of four key components: IoT-based data collection using NodeMCU and soil sensors, data cleaning and preprocessing, predictive analysis using FFLM, and a control system for motor activation based on sensor input. Data are stored in the ThingSpeak cloud for further diagnosis. Extensive experimentation showed that the proposed model achieved 99% accuracy, 98.6% precision and recall, 99% F1-score, and a 2.6-second response time. These results demonstrate that AIS, powered by IoT and FFLM platforms, enables farmers to remotely monitor and control irrigation systems while gaining valuable knowledge about water conservation techniques.

Introduction

Agriculture, which encompasses the cultivation of crops and livestock, is a fundamental practice for sustaining and expanding the human population. Beyond providing essential nourishment, agriculture holds an important part in eradicating extreme poverty and significantly contributes to a nation’s economy. Globally, it accounts for approximately 4% of GDP and is projected to support the nourishment of an estimated 9.7 billion people by 20501,2.

Throughout history, it has been intricately linked with technological advancements. More than 10,000 years ago, rudimentary tools crafted from wood or animal bones marked the beginning of agricultural practices3–5. These tools have since evolved significantly; by the second agricultural revolution in the United States, tractors had become a typical feature on farmlands6–8.

Water, a vital component for plant growth, facilitates the distribution of essential nutrients within the soil. In regions with insufficient rainfall, irrigation—the artificial application of water through pumps, pipes, and sprinklers—has become an essential practice4–11. Among various irrigation techniques, drip irrigation stands out as an efficient method for sustainable agriculture. By delivering water gradually near plant roots in the form of droplets, it conserves a substantial amount of water compared to other techniques12.

In recent decades, advancements in technology have further revolutionized agricultural practices. The incorporation of IoT and AI has introduced a new era of precision farming13. These innovations have enhanced the capability of smart irrigation systems, empowering farmers to check and regulate their resources efficiently. Through dynamic sensing of soil quality, weather behavior, and crop growth factors IoT-enabled systems maximize water efficiency and minimize wastage. Meanwhile, AI algorithms assess this data to uncover valuable insights, allowing farmers to make knowledgeable decisions and improve crop yields14.

These smart systems serve as a bridge between traditional farming techniques and modern technological solutions. They facilitate the acquisition and transmission of data to centralized data centers, often hosted in cloud environments, ensuring accessibility and scalability15–17. Moreover, the flexibility and adaptability of these systems allow for tailored solutions that meet the specific needs of diverse agricultural settings. With AI’s growing role, smart agriculture has become increasingly user-friendly, fostering a future of more productive and environmentally-conscious farming practices18–20.

Related works

Naveena et al. (2024)21 introduced an IoT and ML-based system to address water scarcity in agriculture. Their system uses environmental parameters and weather forecasting to predict irrigation requirements, optimized by an ensemble ML method. The low-cost prototype achieves over 90% accuracy, reducing water, labour, and plant nutrient usage. The flexible IoT platform allows easy integration of IoT and ML components for customized analytical methods in precision irrigation, benefiting both IoT specialists and farmers. However, the study lacks detailed analysis of the system’s performance across different crop types and diverse climatic conditions, which could limit its broad applicability.

Et-Taibi et al. (2024)22 introduced a cloud and IoT-based smart irrigation framework aimed at enhancing water management in agriculture. The system connects various small-scale smart farms, centralizing data collection and analysis. Real-time environmental data, including soil moisture and weather conditions, are collected utilizing IoT sensors and processed through a sophisticated big data management system. Solar energy powers the testbed, integrating Wireless Sensor Networks and Cloud Computing to optimize irrigation schedules. However, scalability and cost-effective deployment for large-scale farming remain areas for further investigation.

Mujawar et al. (2024)23 examined IoT-enabled intelligent irrigation for rice agriculture using sensors to retrieve cognitive data on temperature, water level, and humidity. Machine learning models - ANN, SVM, DT, and RF were applied to predict water demand, with ANN achieving the highest accuracy (95.6%). Confusion matrices validated model performance, highlighting the system’s precision.

Esmail et al. (2023)24 introduces a smart irrigation platform leveraging IoT, data analysis, and ML for efficient water application and scheduling. The system uses the “Crop Irrigation Scheduling” dataset from Kaggle. Data is acquired using IoT sensors and processed by a machine learning model, with the decision tree model showing the best performance.

Tace et al. (2023)25 presents an intelligent and flexible irrigation approach using IoT and ML algorithms. Various ML models were tested. Although, the research does not consider the potential challenges of scaling this system to larger agricultural operations and its performance under extreme weather conditions.

Gor et al. (2023)26 develop an IoT and ML-driven crop recommendation framework integrated with automated irrigation. The system uses NodeMCU ESP8266, soil moisture sensors, submersible DC motors, DTH11 sensors, and relay modules for hardware. ThingSpeak and Google Sites are used for software implementation. They compared various ML algorithms, with SVM attaining the maximum accuracy of 0.82. A key limitation is the lack of long-term field evaluating the system’s functionality durability and reliability in authentic agricultural settings.

Pandey and Agarwal (2023)27 propose a low-cost irrigation planning system aimed at reducing installation and maintenance costs while automating water supply control. Their approach uses soil moisture sensors to collect crop water prerequisites and implements an algorithm for water supply decisions. Experimental results show that The recommended system has the potential to cut water demand in comparison to standard irrigation practices and decrease water wastage by up to 22% compared to popular valve automation systems. Meanwhile, the research does not thoroughly explore its compatibility with existing irrigation infrastructure.

Bharath et al. (2022)28 present a smart irrigation system utilizing ML and IoT for optimal water consumption with minimal farmer interaction. The system employs IoT sensors to capture ground and environmental data, that is subsequently examined using ML to equip irrigation recommendations. They compared various ML techniques, encompassing C4.5, Naïve Bayes (NB), SVM, Neural Networks, and Random Forest, with SVM attaining the highest accuracy of 0.82. A significant drawback is the absence of an in-depth examination of the system’s energy consumption and potential environmental impact.

Iorliam et al. (2022)29 focus on classifying data yielded by smart IoT-enabled irrigation devices. Their experiments show that random forest attained the highest accuracy at 99.98%, making it the most promising for assessing smart irrigation conditions. However, the study does not examine the model’s performance across different soil types and crop varieties, which may contribute to overfitting issues.

Rahul et al. (2022)30 develop an automatic irrigation system based on Artificial Intelligence and IoT. The system uses soil moisture data and prediction algorithms that analyse historical meteorological data to forecast rainfall patterns and climatic changes. In a controlled setting, the technology demonstrated an accuracy rate of 80%, effectively addressing the issue of water waste in irrigation. A notable limitation is the absence of a comparative analysis with traditional irrigation strategies concerning of crop yield and water conservation efficacy.

Sumarudin et al. (2021)31 introduced an IoT-driven precision agriculture system that incorporates Support Vector Machine (SVM) technology for smart irrigation management. The system analyses various environmental factors to estimate crop water needs. Utilizing the MQTT protocol, the system ensures efficient real-time communication between nodes for irrigation control. Their research builds on earlier efforts, including SVM-based moisture prediction, AI methods for cucumber detection, and fuzzy-neural systems for determining water quantities. Despite its strong accuracy and practical applications in precision farming, the study lacks testing across diverse crops and environmental settings.

Ramya et al. (2020)32 propose an IoT framework for smart irrigation by utilizing ML techniques. Their system predicts irrigation requirements using environmental parameters and weather forecasting, optimized by an ensemble ML method. The low-cost prototype achieves over 90% accuracy. The research lacks a comprehensive analysis of the long-term economic viability of implementing such a system across different farm sizes and resource availability contexts.

Although previous studies have explored IoT and machine learning-based smart irrigation systems, many face limitations such as high computational latency, poor scalability, and limited adaptability to diverse agricultural conditions. These gaps highlight the need for an efficient, flexible, and real-time solution, which this proposed approach aims to address.

Proposed framework

System overview

The recommended cognitive IoT-based irrigation system is designed to efficiently automate irrigation by integrating hardware and cloud-based technologies. At the core of the system is the NodeMCU microcontroller, interfaced with the MCP3008 ADC, which processes data from varied sensors, including soil moisture, pH, humidity, and temperature sensors. These sensors monitor essential soil parameters in real time. The system utilizes 5 V relay boards to trigger the solenoid valve for watering plants relied on predictions. The NodeMCU communicates with the cloud server via Wi-Fi, while the ThingSpeak Cloud platform stores the collected data and hosts the deployed learning model, which predicts water levels and ensures optimal control of the solenoid valve. Data acquisition

The IoT based Data Collection Unit consist of the NodeMCU with the sensors as the main components. The thorough breakdown of every component in this system is explained as follows.

NodeMCU controller unit (NCC)

The smart irrigation system comprises of various components in which the NodeMCU interfaced with the MCP3008 is considered as the major control unit. NodeMCU is an open-source development platform that integrates both hardware and software. It is centred around a cost-effective System-on-a-Chip (SoC) referred as the ESP8266. Manufactured by Express, the ESP8266 incorporates significant aspects of a contemporary computer, comprising of a CPU, RAM, Wi-Fi networking abilities. Due to the Low cost development, NodeMCU is utilized in this research article. But the major problem in this microcontroller, lack of analog pins which is utilized to interface the sensors. To overcome this bottleneck, MCP3008 10-Bit ADC is used for interfacing the sensors with the NodeMCU controllers. MCP3008 is utilized to transform the analog signals to digital data and transfers the data to NODEMCU CPU using SPI protocol.

Data labelling and pre-processing technique

Nearly 3,037 data samples collected over 30 days resulted in 9,291 entries retained in the cloud. To ascertain the values for diverse elements, the data received from the IoT devices required preprocessing. Due to considerable asymmetry, the sensor values were normalized, and Studentized Residual methods36– 37 were employed to detect and remove outliers caused by environmental noise or faulty readings, thereby enhancing dataset quality and supporting accurate model performance. This pre-processed information was then utilized during the training and testing phases to inform decisions made by the water control system. Approximately 70% of the data was allocated for training and 30% for testing. Feed forward training layers

The recommended research employs the deep feed forward networks for classification of CVD The deep feed forward networks was constructed with the principle of Extreme Learning Machines (ELM). It exploits kernel functions to attain enhanced performance. The primary benefits of ELM comprise minimised training error and improved approximation capabilities. By utilizing auto-tuning for weight biases and non-zero activation functions, ELM is effectively applied in both classification tasks and the recognition of classification outcomes. A comprehensive explanation of the operational functioning of ELM is presented in38.

In this architectural approach, the ‘L’ neurons within the hidden layer are expected to utilize a smooth and highly differentiable activation function—commonly the sigmoid function—due to its efficiency in learning non-linear mappings. Conversely, the output layer typically adopts a linear activation function to ensure accurate output prediction. In the framework of ELM, the hidden layer does not necessitate iterative tuning or training. The internal parameters, including neuron weights and bias values, can be set randomly and remain fixed throughout the training process without degrading the model’s performance, which simplifies the learning mechanism significantly. This does not imply that hidden nodes are unimportant; rather they merely require no fine-tuning, and the parameters for the hidden neurons can be pre-generated at random.

Conclusion and future direction

In this study, a Cognitive IoT-enabled smart irrigation system is proposed. The framework is deployed using a hardware test bed consisting of a NodeMCU interfaced with various sensors, including moisture sensors, humidity sensors, and pH sensors. It features an upgraded automation capability where soil parameters are measured and transferred to the cloud, aiding in decision-making for controlling irrigation motors. The ThingSpeak cloud is utilized to store soil condition data from the IoT nodes, and the ThingSpeak dashboard enables monitoring of soil moisture, temperature, and air humidity. A novel feed-forward network is employed to predict soil levels, which in turn controls motor operation. Nearly 10,000 datasets were collected and used for training the network to predict soil moisture levels, aiding in the efficient control of irrigation motors. Comprehensive experimentation was conducted, with performance measures like accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, and F1-score examined and evaluated against existing ML algorithms. The experimental findings reveal that the recommended scheme achieves an accuracy of 99.3%, precision of 99%, recall of 98.5%, and an F1-score of 99%, outperforming existing learning models. Additionally, MBT was calculated for both the existing and proposed models. The lower MBT of the proposed model indicates reduced computational overhead, resulting in faster response times. In terms of future prospects, further improvements in the learning scheme are essential to handle real-time, larger datasets with multi-class soil moisture levels (e.g., dry, moderate, wet), enabling more precise irrigation decisions. Moreover, the integration of Fog and Edge gateways will enhance security and reduce response times, providing significant benefits for farmers.

A novel delay-affected two degree of freedom PID controller using physics-inspired optimization for robust control applications
Authors: Gökhan Yüksek
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a delay-affected two-degree-of-freedom proportional-integral-derivative (DE-2DOFPID) controller for accurate speed regulation of brushless direct-current (BLDC) motors operating under time-delay conditions. In the proposed architecture, the delay is explicitly embedded into the control law through a weighted delayed-error feedback term. At the same time, two reference-weighting coefficients reshape the proportional and derivative paths. This structure extends the classical 2DOFPID controller by enriching the instantaneous error response with memory-based information and tunable reference coupling, which strengthens both transient behavior and robustness in delay-dominated regimes. The controller gains are tuned using the physics-inspired Rime-inspired metaheuristic (RIME) algorithm, which is well-suited to the highly multimodal, nonconvex search landscape induced by the composite performance index. The objective function combines multiple criteria, including integral absolute error, integral time-weighted absolute error, integral squared error, steady-state error, rise time, settling time and overshoot. For a BLDC speed-control benchmark, comparative optimization studies are carried out against recent metaheuristics, including the educational competition optimizer, Fata morgana algorithm, Slime mould algorithm, Moss growth optimizer, and Ruppell’s Fox optimizer, as well as RIME-based tuning of PI-FF, PID, PID with anti-windup, and conventional 2DOFPID controllers. The proposed RIME-tuned DE-2DOFPID reduces the settling time by 8.03% and the lower cost by 6.99% relative to PID-based methods, while maintaining overshoot below 3%. Real-time experiments on a laboratory BLDC drive confirm that the delay-affected 2DOFPID architecture preserves its advantages under measurement noise, parameter uncertainty and external disturbances, demonstrating its practical relevance for robust motion-control applications.Keywords: Two-degree-of-freedom PID controller, Physics-based optimization, Delay compensation, Robust control, Brushless DC motorSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Brushless direct current motors (BLDC) are used in a wide range of applications, from electric vehicles to industrial automation lines, robotic systems, and medical devices, thanks to their advantages of high efficiency, compact size, and low maintenance requirements1,2. In these motors, unlike brush-type motors, electronic commutation is applied, and the stator windings are sequentially switched based on the rotor position. This reduces friction losses and increases motor life. However, the trapezoidal back electromotive force (EMF) and six-step drive technique in BLDC motors can cause torque fluctuations, which complicates precise speed and torque control. Under sudden load changes and reference speed deviations, weak angular speed control can lead to reduced system efficiency and vibration problems3–5.

The most commonly used method for angular speed control of BLDC motors is the classic proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controller. Due to their simple structure and ease of gain adjustment, controllers are still preferred in industry6,7. However, classic PID achieves limited success under variable load conditions and in time-delayed systems. Measurement delays, computation times, or actuator response delays cause phase shifts in the control signal and exacerbate problems such as oscillation, overshoot, and steady-state error in the transient regime8,9.

Various compensation methods have been developed in the literature to mitigate the adverse effects of time delay. The Smith predictor and Padé approximation are among the most frequently used techniques in this field10. In the Smith predictor, a predictive structure is designed by estimating the system’s delay-free model11,12, while in the Padé approximation, the delay is approximated using a rational transfer function13. However, both approaches are highly sensitive to model uncertainties. Stability degradation under parameter variations, measurement noise, and external disturbances limits the use of these structures in real-time applications. Furthermore, the fact that these methods are not integrated into the control law but operate as an external additional layer reduces application flexibility. This problem indicates the need for more direct and robust structures.

Due to the limitations of classical compensation methods, research has focused on integrating time delay directly into the control equation. In such approaches, both instantaneous error information and a delayed error component are included in the control law, thereby attempting to preempt deviations based on the system’s past behavior14. However, the excessive historical dependence on the control signal introduced by these structures can weaken stability. Leaving the delayed error contribution uncontrolled results in sudden jumps or slow responses. This problem highlights the need to balance delayed information with an appropriate gain parameter. In this context, methods such as adaptive gain scheduling15,16 and model-referenced adaptive structures17,18 have been developed in the literature. However, these methods have disadvantages, including high parameter sensitivity, susceptibility to measurement noise, and high computational load. Especially in embedded systems, complex adaptive structures create application difficulties and complicate real-time operation. This problem highlights the need for a more straightforward yet powerful control structure.

Such limitations have encouraged researchers to explore alternative controller formulations and metaheuristic-based tuning approaches. In recent years, the limitations of classical PID and conventional delay-compensation approaches have motivated a large body of research that attempts to enhance controller flexibility through fractional calculus, multi-stage structures, and metaheuristic tuning frameworks. Fractional-order PID families, for instance, have been widely explored for BLDC and DC motor speed regulation, where additional fractional degrees of freedom aim to improve transient shaping under nonlinear and uncertain operating conditions. BA-optimized FOPID controllers have been shown to enhance rise time, overshoot and steady-state performance in sensorless BLDC motors19. Another related study demonstrated that WOA-tuned FOPIλ controllers maintain robustness under fluctuating PV inputs, load variations, and parameter uncertainty in PV-fed PMBLDC drives20. Beyond fractional dynamics, several works have proposed higher-order or filtered PID variants to refine memory effects and enrich frequency-domain behaviour. A high-order FPID approximation optimized via GWO yielded improved disturbance rejection and transient performance for DC motor control21, and the PIDα structure tuned with the HHO algorithm outperformed several metaheuristic-tuned PID formulations in nonlinear furnace systems22. More elaborate multi-stage control structures have also been introduced, such as the TDn(1 + PIDn) controller optimized through the diligent crow search algorithm, which achieved reduced overshoot, faster settling and improved ITAE in nonlinear steam condenser pressure regulation23. Similarly, the PIDN controller tuned via the artificial-rabbits optimization algorithm demonstrated robustness to noise, saturation, and external disturbances in electric-furnace applications24.

In related work, combining a 2DOF-PIDA structure with the starfish optimization algorithm has been reported to improve tracking accuracy and disturbance rejection in nonlinear CSTH thermal processes25. Other studies have applied modified atom-search-based optimization methods to the design of stable IIR filters and to parameter identification problems, showing strong performance across several dynamic systems and further highlighting the usefulness of metaheuristic techniques for modelling high-order systems26. Taken together, these contributions point to a growing interest in enhanced PID architectures and metaheuristic tuning strategies. At the same time, they also show that most existing solutions either do not embed time delay directly into the control law or rely on fractional or high-order structures that add computational burden and make real-time use more difficult. This situation strengthens the motivation for a controller that is both structurally simple and explicitly delay-aware, while still retaining the flexibility of the 2DOF design and maintaining robustness in the presence of parameter variations, measurement noise, and rapid transients.

A growing body of work has pointed to the usefulness of two-degree-of-freedom PID (2DOFPID) designs in situations where tracking and disturbance rejection need to be shaped independently. This flexibility helps reduce overshoot and unwanted oscillations, so the approach has become fairly common in recent studies. Yet, a recurring issue in the standard 2DOFPID formulation is that the control law does not explicitly handle time delay. When the system experiences rapid transitions, this missing element becomes a real limitation27–29. As a result, there is still a need for a controller that keeps the benefits of the 2DOF structure but also accounts for delay in a direct and transparent way.

To fill this gap, the present work introduces a Delay-Affected 2DOFPID (DE-2DOFPID) controller. The model includes three additions to the classical structure. First, a delay term is inserted into the reference path through the Kτ parameter. Second, the proportional action is scaled with the coefficient. Third, the derivative path is redefined using the weight. These changes allow the controller to use both the immediate and delayed components of the error signal, which helps to damp overshoot and limit oscillations during transient behavior while still keeping steady-state tracking accurate. Even so, selecting the parameters for this extended structure becomes a multi-objective tuning problem that is not easy to solve. Traditional tuning rules struggle when several criteria must be optimized at the same time, integral measures such as IAE, ITAE, ISE and ITSE30, as well as overshoot, settling time and the final steady-state error. In such cases, metaheuristic optimization tends to work better. Among these, the Rime-Inspired Metaheuristic (RIME)31 has shown that it can search broadly while still focusing on promising regions, which helps it move toward a global optimum more reliably than classical methods.

In this study, the DE-2DOFPID controller was tuned using the RIME algorithm—a physics-driven optimization method, and its performance was assessed both in MATLAB/Simulink simulations and through real-time experiments on a DC motor test setup. The results were compared with several recent metaheuristic approaches, including the Educational Competition Optimizer (ECO)32, Fata Morgana Optimizer (FATA)33, Slime Mould Algorithm (SMA)34, Moss Growth Optimization (TMGO)35, and Ruppel’s Fox Optimizer (RFO)36. Across these comparisons, the DE-2DOFPID consistently delivered shorter rise times, lower settling times and more decisive disturbance rejection, showing that the method performs well not only in simulation but also on physical hardware.

To better explain the structural contribution of the proposed controller, it is helpful to contrast the DE-2DOFPID with the classical PID and the familiar 2DOF-PID structures. A standard PID acts purely on the instantaneous error . The 2DOF-PID extends this idea by adjusting the influence of the reference signal in the proportional and derivative paths. However, neither formulation accounts for delayed system behaviour in the control law. The DE-2DOFPID, on the other hand, incorporates a weighted delayed-error signal and modifies both the proportional and derivative branches through the and parameters. These additions create a memory-enhanced 2DOF framework that cannot be achieved by simple gain adjustments and that directly influences the closed-loop behaviour when delays are present.

The delayed-error component serves as a meaningful corrective mechanism: it counters the phase lag introduced by sensor delays, computation time and actuation dynamics. By using a short window of past error information, the controller partially restores the temporal detail lost due to delay and reduces the oscillatory response typically observed in delayed feedback systems. Unlike Smith predictor or Padé approximation techniques, which require accurate plant models and operate as external modules, the DE-2DOFPID embeds delay handling directly into the control law. This intrinsic structure improves robustness to parameter uncertainty and measurement noise. Existing delay-oriented PID variants often rely on filtering or fractional-order operators. Yet, they do not include an explicit delayed-error pathway and therefore cannot shape transient behaviour based on the system’s recent history.

Compared to these prior approaches, the DE-2DOFPID introduces a unified and computationally lightweight structure that simultaneously (i) embeds a tunable delay memory, (ii) employs dual reference-weighting coefficients to influence both proportional and derivative behaviour, and (iii) preserves compatibility with real-time implementations on embedded hardware. To the best of our knowledge, no existing study provides such an integrated formulation that jointly addresses delay compensation, transient shaping and robustness within a single 2DOFPID architecture.

In summary, the existing literature shows that most delay-compensation techniques rely on external predictor layers or model-dependent approximations rather than integrating delay information directly into the control law. These approaches often exhibit sensitivity to modelling uncertainties, measurement noise and parameter drift, and classical 2DOFPID structures also do not explicitly address time delay in their formulation. Moreover, many optimization-based PID tuning studies focus solely on tracking performance and do not evaluate computational cost, energy usage or robustness against disturbances and parameter variations. Motivated by these gaps, this study makes three primary contributions. First, the DE-2DOFPID architecture is formulated in which a weighted delayed-error term and dual reference-weighting coefficients are explicitly embedded into the proportional and derivative channels, enabling improved transient shaping and robustness under time-delay conditions. Second, a RIME-based tuning framework is developed to minimize a composite performance index that includes multiple integral error measures, transient-response specifications and robustness-oriented criteria. Third, the proposed controller is validated both in simulation and in real-time BLDC experiments, and its behaviour is quantitatively compared against state-of-the-art PID-based controllers and recent metaheuristic optimizers, demonstrating clear improvements in settling time, ISE, disturbance rejection and practical implementability.

Justification of RIME selection, optimization settings, and statistical evaluation

The optimization of the DE-2DOFPID controller constitutes a multimodal, nonconvex search problem resulting from the combined effects of proportional, integral, derivative, delay-related, and reference weighting parameters. The composite cost function defined in Sect. 2.3 yields a rough error landscape with numerous shallow minima and narrow basins of attraction, due to the inherent conflict between minimizing integral-type indices and improving transient metrics. Under such rough, delay-induced oscillatory conditions, gradient-based or neighborhood-constrained search methods tend to converge prematurely, necessitating a robust heuristic metaheuristic.

The RIME algorithm was chosen for DE-2DOFPID tuning because its internal operators are structurally well-matched to the nature of this optimization environment. The soft rime mechanism provides long-range stochastic exploration and enables reliable escape from local minima induced by competing error terms. In contrast, the hard-rime drilling operator performs focused exploitation around promising candidates. This is crucial when tuning parameters such as the delay error gain and the reference weighting coefficients and , as small changes to these parameters can significantly reshape the closed-loop dynamics. Finally, the cohesion coefficient-driven transition scheme ensures a smooth transition from exploration to exploitation and guarantees convergence even when the objective function becomes flatter near the optimum, a behavior frequently observed in delay-affected PID environments.

All algorithms were tested under identical computational constraints to ensure fair comparisons. Each method used a population of 30, a maximum of 100 iterations, and 3000 evaluations, with the same parameter bounds and fixed constraint handling. Results and discussions

This section investigates how the DE-2DOFPID controller performs when its parameters are tuned with the RIME algorithm. The evaluation includes rise time, settling time, overshoot, steady-state error and the overall cost. To understand the benefit of RIME-based tuning, its results are compared with those of several recent metaheuristic optimizers that exhibit different exploration and exploitation behaviours. Limitations and practical considerations

Although the DE-2DOFPID controller performs well under changes in delay, parameter variations, and noise, several practical limitations remain. The delay–memory gain is the most sensitive part of the tuning; very large values can slow the transient response or reduce the phase margin. Parameter bounds are also necessary, because an unrestricted search may produce unstable solutions in the early stages of optimization. The controller remained stable only within a delay range of 0.10 to 0.30 s, and values outside this window may require retuning to maintain the desired phase behaviour. As with other metaheuristic tuning schemes, the ability to approach a near-global solution depends on the available computational budget and the definition of the search space. These points describe the main practical limitations of the method and indicate where further improvements may be needed.

Conclusion

This study introduced a DE-2DOFPID controller tuned by the RIME algorithm. The design extends the classical PID structure by incorporating a delayed-error feedback component and two weighting factors in the derivative path, giving the controller more flexibility when dealing with systems that operate under time delays.

The simulation and optimization studies showed that the RIME-tuned DE-2DOFPID performs better than both the standard PID-based designs and the other optimization methods tested. In the benchmark cases, it produced the shortest rise and settling times while still keeping overshoot and steady-state error at very low levels. Its minimum cost function value and lowest ISE score support the conclusion that it handles both transient and steady-state behavior effectively.

A Wilcoxon pairwise statistical test confirmed that RIME consistently outperformed the competing optimizers, with p-values far below 0.05. The robustness tests, covering disturbances, perturbations in the control signal, and measurement noise, also showed that the RIME-based DE-2DOFPID remained stable, recovered quickly, and avoided excessive oscillations across all scenarios examined. From the computational perspective, RIME provided a good balance between solution accuracy and processing effort. Although ECO was the fastest and most energy-efficient option, RIME delivered a more rounded performance when robustness, response quality and resource usage were considered together, making it the more practical choice for real-time use.

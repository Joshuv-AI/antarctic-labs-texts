Sustained effect of a multifaceted VAP prevention program over more than seven years including the COVID-19 pandemic
Authors: Andrea C Büchler, Holly Jackson, Niccolò Buetti, Filippo Boroli, Christophe Juge, Aurélie Perret, Zilfi Koyluk Tomsuk, Caroline Landelle, Stephan Harbarth, Jérôme Pugin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the long-term impact of a multifaceted intervention bundle containing nine measures to reduce the incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in mechanically ventilated patients admitted to the intensive care division at Geneva University Hospitals over a prolonged follow-up period of more than seven years, including the COVID-19 pandemic. The original study included a follow-up of 11 months and showed a marked decrease in VAP incidence. We compared the five pre-defined time periods (pre-intervention, intervention, post-intervention, prolonged follow-up, and COVID-19 pandemic period) using a Poisson model. The incidence of VAP per 1000 ventilator days reduced from 24.3 (95% confidence interval [CI] 18.8–30.9) in the pre-intervention period to 3.9 (95%CI 2.0–6.8) in the post-intervention period. During the prolonged follow-up and the COVID-19 pandemic periods, VAP incidence per 1000 ventilator-days remained similar as in the initial post-intervention period with 4.0 (95%CI 2.9–5.4) and 3.7 (95%CI 2.8–4.7), respectively. Adherence to the bundle measures was assessed using the monthly percentage of correct observations and shown overall for each of the five time periods. Adherence to all but two bundle measures (oral care and hand hygiene) remained high or even improved during the prolonged follow-up period. In conclusion, after implementing a VAP prevention bundle in 2014, there was a sustained effect on VAP incidence during a 7-year follow-up, including the COVID-19 pandemic period.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13756-025-01646-y.Keywords: Ventilator-associated pneumonia, Multimodal strategy, Bundles, Intensive care unit, Prevention and control, Implementation

Introduction

Multifaceted intervention bundles have been shown to decrease the incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) [1, 2]. However, the sustainability of these bundles is often questioned due to the limited follow-up time after implementation in many prevention trials. In addition, the Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic challenged existing infection prevention and control (IPC) programs leading to an overall increase in healthcare-associated infections (HAI) and VAPs in intensive care units (ICUs) during the pandemic years [3, 4], despite evidence supporting the effectiveness of these measures in critically ill COVID-19 patients [5, 6].

At Geneva University Hospital, a multifaceted intervention bundle containing nine measures to reduce the incidence of VAP was introduced in 2014. In a quasi-experimental study, a sustained reduction in VAP incidence and an additive effect of selective oral decontamination (SOD) could be shown in the post-intervention period of 11 months [1]. The aim of the present study was to describe the long-term effect of this multifaceted intervention bundle to reduce VAP incidence over a prolonged follow-up period (including the COVID-19 pandemic) after the initial study period ended.

Methods

Setting and study design

Geneva University Hospitals is a 2100-bed primary and tertiary care facility in Geneva, Switzerland. The mixed medical-surgical adult ICU has around 30 beds and admits between 1800 and 2100 patients per year on average. This surveillance was conducted as part of the routine quality improvement activities of the ICU infection control program. Thus, institutional review board approval from the Commission Cantonale d’Éthique de la Recherche de Genève, as well as individual consent, was not required, according to the definition of research in the Swiss Human Research Act. Only aggregated data were used.

Data collection

After the end of the initial follow-up period in July 2016, absolute number of VAPs per month, total ventilation days per month, and the adherence to the nine bundle measures were continuously collected as part of a quality improvement program. Data extraction was carried out electronically leading to a slight difference in the number of ventilator-days compared to the initial study periods (pre-intervention, intervention, and post-intervention). During the prolonged follow-up period, months with fewer than 20 observations assessing hand hygiene compliance were removed from the analysis.

Definitions

For the initial study and the current analysis, the overall incidence of probable and definite VAP was used. Probable VAP was defined as the presence of (1) radiologic changes with systemic inflammation (temperature ≥ 38°C, or leukocyte count > 12,000 or < 4000 cells/mL) with clinical pulmonary signs (i.e., purulent tracheal secretions), and (2) a Clinical Pulmonary Infection Score (CPIS) > 6 [7]. Definite VAP was defined by the addition of positive quantitative cultures of distal pulmonary sampling obtained by Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL; significant threshold ≥ 104 colony-forming units (CFU)/mL) or mini-BAL (significant threshold ≥ 103 CFU/mL) [1]. Only first VAPs were considered.

The time periods were defined as follows:

Pre-intervention: January 2014 through August 2014 (8 months)

Pre-intervention: January 2014 through August 2014 (8 months)

Intervention: September 2014 through August 2015 (12 months)

Intervention: September 2014 through August 2015 (12 months)

Post-intervention (initial follow-up): September 2015 through July 2016 (11 months)

Post-intervention (initial follow-up): September 2015 through July 2016 (11 months)

Prolonged follow-up: August 2016 through February 2020 (43 months)

Prolonged follow-up: August 2016 through February 2020 (43 months)

COVID-19 pandemic: March 2020 through February 2023 (36 months)

Intervention

A detailed description of the initial intervention has been previously published [1]. In short, a multifaceted VAP prevention program was introduced in 2014, and the study used a quasi-experimental, uncontrolled, before-and-after design to analyse the effects of the bundle with a follow-up period of 11 months until July 2016. The intervention bundle consisted of nine preventive measures: (1) adherence with hand hygiene, (2) keeping patients in a semi recumbent position (> 30°), (3) oral hygiene three times daily with 0.2% chlorhexidine-gluconate aqueous solution, (4) maintaining an endotracheal tube cuff pressure ≥ 25 cm H2O, (5) daily sedation hold and (6) daily trial of ventilation weaning when appropriate, (7) daily active or passive mobilization, (8) aspiration of subglottic secretions using a EVAC endotracheal tube and, (9) SOD with a suspension of colistin, tobramycin, and nystatin administered in the subglottic space three times per day in patients likely to be ventilated ≥ 48 h [1].

The surveillance of the number of VAP episodes per month, the total number of ventilator days per month, as well as the adherence to the nine different bundle components were continued after the initial follow-up period until February 2023, resulting in an additional follow-up time of 79 months and a total follow-up time of 90 months. VAP surveillance included all patients at risk of VAP, hence with mechanical ventilation (MV) of > 48 h. We reviewed monthly the VAP incidence, and a 0.4 full-time equivalent of an ICU nurse was dedicated to the measurement of adherence to the elements of the bundle during the whole follow-up.

Statistical analysis

All VAP episodes (definite and probable) were included in the analysis and incidence rates of VAP were graphically illustrated per 1000 ventilator-days per month. The total incidence of VAP per 1000 ventilator days was calculated with their 95% exact confidence intervals (CIs) per time period [8]. Complete pairwise comparisons of the five pre-defined time periods were performed by calculating incidence rate ratios (IRRs) and their 95% CIs, applying the “sidak” adjustment for multiple comparisons, using a Poisson regression model on aggregated monthly data with ventilator days as an offset. Overdispersion was tested using the dispersion ratio via the ‘performance’ package within R [9] with subsequent fitting of a negative binomial model, if appropriate. As the dispersion ratio was 0.851, producing a Pearson’s chi-squared statistic = 89.378 with a p-value = 0.862, no overdispersion was detected. Hence, we only fit a Poisson regression model and did not use the negative binomial model. Adherence to the bundle measures was assessed using monthly percentage of total observations and shown for the pre-defined time periods. All statistical tests were two-tailed and a p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed with R Version 4.3.1 [10].

Results

We observed a total of 226 VAP episodes and 36,405 ventilator-days from August 2016 to February 2023 (Table 1). The incidence of VAP per 1000 ventilator days reduced from 24.3 (95%CI 18.8–30.87) in the pre-intervention period to 3.9 (95%CI 2.01–6.81) in the post-intervention period. During the additional follow-up period of 79 months, the VAP incidence per 1000 ventilator days remained low, at 4.04 (95%CI 2.92–5.44) in the period of prolonged follow-up and 3.66 (95%CI 2.78–4.72) during the COVID-19 pandemic period (Fig. 1). Comparing each of the four later time periods (intervention, post-intervention, prolonged follow-up, and COVID-19 pandemic) with the pre-intervention period showed a significant decrease in the incidence of VAP with IRR (and 95% CIs) of 0.490 (0.286–0.840), 0.160 (0.067–0.386), 0.166 (0.096–0.287) and 0.150 (0.091–0.248), respectively. In addition, comparing the post-intervention, prolonged follow-up and COVID-19 pandemic time periods produced IRR very close to 1. Discussion

After the implementation of a VAP prevention bundle containing nine IPC measures in 2014 with an initial post-intervention period of 11 months, a significant reduction in VAP incidence was observed [1]. By analysing this incidence as well as the adherence to the bundle measures over an additional 79 months, we demonstrated that the effect was sustained; VAP incidence remained similarly low during the post-intervention and prolonged follow-up periods despite the COVID-19 pandemic.

While individual measures and prevention bundles have been shown to be effective for VAP prevention, their successful long-term implementation is not regularly reported as there is no post-intervention period or only a short post-intervention period of less than 30 months [2, 11–15]. In addition, adherence to individual prevention measures and bundles often varies or is not reported. However, an adherence of more than 70% seems to be associated with a reduction of VAP [11], which is in line with our results showing an adherence of more than 70% for six of the nine bundle measures over all three post-intervention periods. For subglottic suctioning and SOD, the adherence still increased from 50 to 70% to above 80% over the three follow-up periods, and only hand hygiene adherence remained lower, at around 65%, over the whole study period. The COVID-19 pandemic additionally challenged IPC programs, since an increase in the incidence of HAI, and specifically VAP, during this period has been described [4]. One of the reasons for the increase in VAP during the pandemic might have been the severity of disease, the high number of patients in need of prolonged MV, the reduction of compliance to bundles due to overwhelmed staff and related IPC challenges [16–18]. However, by accounting for the number of ventilation days in our study, we could show a stable VAP incidence while the adherence to the bundle measures remained stable or even increased for subglottic suctioning and SOD during the pandemic period. This underlines the importance of well-established IPC programmes to manage exceptional situations such as pandemics.

This study has several limitations. First, the data during the prolonged follow-up period was continuously collected as part of a quality improvement program and not in a specified study setting. This has led to a less dense surveillance of the adherence to the individual bundle measures possibly overestimating the adherence. In addition, the analysis performed in the initial study could not be fully replicated as individual patient data was missing. We, therefore, cannot exclude the possibility that the patient population may have slightly changed during the entire study period. Second, as our analysis was based on aggregated data, neither the bundle adherence across the different time periods, nor the impact of individual bundle measures, nor the COVID 19-status of individual patients during the COVID 19-pandemic could be taken into account. Third, due to the timing of the initial study period, our definition of VAP differs from the surveillance definitions currently in use by the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) which might impact interfacility comparability of our results.

Conclusion

After the implementation of a VAP prevention bundle containing nine prevention measures in 2014, we showed a sustainable effect on the VAP incidence over a prolonged follow-up of more than seven years including the COVID-19 pandemic period.

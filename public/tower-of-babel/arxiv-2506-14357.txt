C C -self-adjoint contractive extensions of C C -symmetric non-densely defined contractions
Yury Arlinskiĭ, Konrad Schmüdgen

Abstract
. Given a conjugation (involution) C C on a Hilbert space, C C -self-adjoint contractive extensions of a non-densely defined C C -symmetric contraction are studied and parameterizations of all such extensions are obtained. As an application, a proof of an announced result of Glazman [ 14 ] on the existence of maximal dissipative C C -self-adjoint extensions of a densely defined C C -symmetric dissipative operator is given.



AMS Subject Classification (2020)
.
47A20, 47B44, 47B02.

Key words:
contraction, conjugation, dissipative operator, complex symmetric operator

1.
Introduction

A conjugation (or involution) on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is an anti-linear isometric operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
C^{2}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, see
[
14
,
8
,
9
,
25
,
20
]
.
Then it follows that

(1.1)

(Cf,g)=(Cg,f)\;\;\forall f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}.

If
C
be a conjugation on
{\mathcal{H}}
, a linear operator
B
on
{\mathcal{H}}
is called
C
-symmetric if

(1.2)

(Bx,Cy)=(x,CBy)\;\;\forall x,y\in{\rm dom\,}B.

If
B
is densely defined, then (
1.2
)
is equivalent to
B\subseteq CB^{*}C
. If
B=CB^{*}C
, then the operator
B
is called
C
-self-adjoint.
These notions were introduced by Glazman
[
15
]
.

The present paper deals with norm preserving
C
-self-adjoint extensions of non-densely defined bounded
C
-symmetric operators.

The study of norm preserving extensions of linear operators and their interplay with other problems has a long history in operator theory,
see e.g.
[
6
,
5
,
7
,
11
,
13
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
. A sample is von Neumann’s theory of the Cayley
transform which connects self-adjoint extensions of a symmetric operator with unitary extensions of a non-densely defined isometric operator
[
1
,
25
]
. The Cayley transform reduces also the problem of maximal dissipative (accretive) extensions of a dissipative (accretive) operator to
contractive extensions of a non-densely defined contraction.

The starting point and first motivation for this paper was the following result which was announced by I.M. Glazman in
[
14
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Each
C
-symmetric (
J
-symmetric in Glazman’s terminology) dissipative operator with dense domain admits a
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative
extension.

According to our knowledge, a proof of this theorem never appeared. In
[
14
]
it is argued as follows: Suppose that
T
is a closed densely
defined dissipative operator and let
V=(T-iI)(T+iI)^{-1}
be its Cayley transform.
Then
V
is a contraction defined on the subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{V}:={\rm ran\,}(T+iI)
. If
T
is
C
-symmetric, then
V
is
C
-symmetric as well.
Further, it is noted in
[
14
]
that a modification of Krein’s approach in
[
18
]
gives the existence of contractive
C
-self-adjoint extension

\widetilde{V}
of
V
. Having
\widetilde{V}
the inverse Cayley transform
\widetilde{T}=i(I+\widetilde{V})(I-\widetilde{V})^{-1}
,
{\rm dom\,}\widetilde{T}={\rm ran\,}(I-\widetilde{V}),

yields a
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative extension of
T
.

A proof of the existence of such an extension
\widetilde{V}
was missing.
In Theorem
2.6
of this paper we prove the existence of
C
-self-adjoint contractive extensions of non-densely defined
C
-symmetric contractions.
In particular, combined with the reasoning of the preceding paragraph, this completes the proof of Glazman’s Theorem
1.1
.

Let us sketch the main results of this paper. In Theorem
2.6
we describe all
C
-self-adjoint bounded extensions of a non-densely defined

C
-symmetric bounded operator.
A description of these extensions has been already established in
[
24
, Theorem 1]
. Our Theorem
2.6
gives a new form of such
extensions.

Theorem
3.3
contains a complete description of all contractive
C
-self-adjoint extensions of a non-densely defined
C
-symmetric contraction. For
this we use Crandall’s parameterization
[
10
]
of contractive extensions of non-densely defined contractions.

A particular emphasis in this paper is on the uniqueness of the corresponding extensions. In Theorem
3.7
we characterize the uniqueness of

C
-self-adjoint contractive extensions by various equivalent conditions and Theorem
4.4
characterizes the uniqueness of
C
-self-adjoint maximal
dissipative extensions.

Let us fix some notation. The Banach space of all bounded operators acting between Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is denoted by
{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{2})
and

{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}):={\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}})
.
The symbols
{\rm dom\,}T
,
{\rm ran\,}T
,
\ker T
denote
the domain, range and kernel of a linear operator
T
, respectively,
and
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T
denotes the closure of the range of
T
. If
Z\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{2})
is a contraction, we abbreviate
{\mathcal{D}}_{Z}:=(I-Z^{*}Z)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}_{Z}:={\rm\overline{ran}\,}{\mathcal{D}}_{Z}.

2.
Bounded
C
-self-adjoint extensions of a non-densely defined
C
-symmetric bounded operator

Let us begin with two simple facts which are of interest in itself.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C
be a conjugation in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then the map
J\to U:=CJ
is a bijection of the set of conjugations
J
on the set of
C
-self-adjoint unitary operators
U
.

Proof.

If
J
is a conjugation, then
U=CJ
is obviously a unitary and
C=UJ
, so
CUC=CCJC=JC=U^{*}
, that is,
U
is
C
-self-adjoint.

Conversely, let
U
be a
C
-self-adjoint unitary. Set
J:=CU
. Then
J
is anti-linear, isometric,
U=CJ
and
J^{2}=CUCU=U^{*}U=I
, that is,
J
is a conjugation.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C
be a conjugation in
{\mathcal{H}}
and let
A\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}).
Then the operator

(2.1)

{\mathcal{A}}:={\frac{1}{2}}(A+CA^{*}C)

is
C
-self-adjoint and, moreover,
||{\mathcal{A}}||\leq||A||.
If
A
is
C
-self-adjoint, then
{\mathcal{A}}=A=CA^{*}C
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}={\frac{1}{2}}(A^{*}+CAC)
we get
C{\mathcal{A}}^{*}C={\frac{1}{2}}(CA^{*}C+A)={\mathcal{A}}
, i.e., the operator
{\mathcal{A}}
is
C
-self-adjoint. Since
||CA^{*}C||\leq||A^{*}||=||A||
, we obtain
||{\mathcal{A}}||\leq||A||
.
∎

Let
V
be a bounded linear operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
defined on a subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{V}
. Suppose
V
is a
C
-symmetric w.r.t.
C
, i.e.,

(2.2)

(Vf,Cg)=(f,CVg),\;\forall f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}.

We are interested in bounded
C
-self-adjoint extensions
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, i.e., bounded linear operators
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
that

{\rm dom\,}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{H}},\;\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}{\upharpoonright\,}{\mathcal{H}}_{V}=V,\;\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}C.

Clearly, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
is a bounded and
C
-self-adjoint extension of
V
;

(ii)

C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}C
is a bounded
C
-self-adjoint extension of
V
.

If
{\mathcal{U}}
is the bounded operator defined by

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}}:={\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{U}}=C{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{V}}=C{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{V}}},\;{\mathcal{U}}h=C{\mathcal{V}}Ch,\;h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}},

then the equality (
1.2
) implies that the operator
{\mathcal{U}}
is
C
-symmetric and the pair
\{{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{U}}\}
forms the
adjoint (dual) pair
of operators, i.e. we have

({\mathcal{V}}f,h)=(f,{\mathcal{U}}h)\;\;\forall f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{V}}},\;\forall h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}}.

The adjoint
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}
of any
C
-self-adjoint bounded extension
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
of a
C
-symmetric operator
{\mathcal{V}}
is a
C
-self-adjoint extension of
{\mathcal{U}}
.

Let
V^{*}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V})
be the adjoint to
V\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V},{\mathcal{H}})
. Then (
2.2
) is equivalent to the equality

(2.3)

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}CVh=V^{*}Ch\;\;\forall h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}.

Set
{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}:={\mathcal{H}}\ominus{\mathcal{H}}_{V}
. Note that the formula

(2.4)

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}:=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}

gives is a one-to-one correspondence between all operators
\widetilde{L}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}})
and all bounded extensions
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
of
V
.
The following result has been established by Raikh in
[
24
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The formula

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+(CV^{*}C+S)P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}

gives a
bijective correspondence between the set of all
C
-self-adjoint extensions of a bounded non-densely defined
C
-symmetric operator

V
and the set of all
C
-self-adjoint operators
S\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},CH_{V}^{\perp})
.

We will develop a new description of
C
-self-adjoint bounded extensions.
For this purpose we define the following linear operator

(2.5)

Z_{0}:Ch\mapsto P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVh,\;h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}

Clearly,
Z_{0}\in{\mathbf{B}}(C{\mathcal{H}}_{V},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
,
||Z_{0}||\leq||V||,
and for
Z_{0}^{*}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},C{\mathcal{H}}_{V})
we have

(2.6)

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}CZ^{*}_{0}g=V^{*}Cg,\;\;g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}.

If
{\mathfrak{M}}
is a subspace then, see
[
24
]
, the equality

(2.7)

P_{C{\mathfrak{M}}}=CP_{\mathfrak{M}}C

holds. Hence from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) it follows that

(2.8)

CV^{*}Cg=Z_{0}^{*}g\;\;\forall g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
V
be a
C
-symmetric bounded operator defined on a subspace
{\rm dom\,}V={\mathcal{H}}_{V}
and let
Z_{0}
be the operator defined by (
2.5
).
Then a bounded extension
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
of
V
is
C
-self-adjoint if and only if the operator
\widetilde{L}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}})
in
(
2.4
) satisfies the following conditions:

(2.9)

\widetilde{L}^{*}{\upharpoonright\,}C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}=Z_{0}.

(2.10)

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}C\widetilde{L}f=\widetilde{L}^{*}Cf\;\;\forall f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},

Proof.

Assume
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{L}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}
is a bounded
C
-self-adjoint extension of
V
, i.e.,

C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{L}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{L}}C\Longleftrightarrow C\left(VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\right)=\left(V^{*}+\widetilde{L}^{*}\right)C.

The latter is equivalent to the equations

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}CVP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}C\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=V^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+V^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}},\\[5.69054pt]
P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}C\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=\widetilde{L}^{*}C.\end{array}\right.

From (
2.3
) it follows the equality

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}C\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=V^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}.
Passing to the adjoint we obtain (
2.9
). Then the second equality in (
2.11
) yields (
2.10
).
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

If
{\rm codim\,}{\mathcal{H}}_{V}=1
and
\widetilde{L}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}})
satisfies (
2.9
), then the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
of the form (
2.4
) is a
C
-self-adjoint
extension of
V
.

Proof.

Using (
1.1
) we check the equality (
2.10
) for any
\widetilde{L}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}})
. Because of
{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}=\{\lambda e_{0}\}_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}}
,

e_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}
,
||e_{0}||=1
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{L}^{*}Ce_{0}=(\widetilde{L}^{*}Ce_{0},e_{0})e_{0}=(Ce_{0},\widetilde{L}e_{0})e_{0}=(C\widetilde{L}e_{0},e_{0})e_{0}\\
=(P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}C\widetilde{L}e_{0},e_{0})e_{0}=P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}C\widetilde{L}e_{0}.\end{array},

that is, (
2.10
) is valid. If, in addition,
\widetilde{L}^{*}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
is an extension of
Z_{0}
, then the equality (
2.10
) holds as
well. Therefore
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{L}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}
is a
C
-self-adjoint extension of
V
.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

(1)

Each bounded
C
-slfadjoint extension
\widetilde{T}
of
V
has the form

(2.12)

\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}C\right),

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}
is given by (
2.4
) and
\widetilde{Z}=\widetilde{L}^{*}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
is an extension on
{\mathcal{H}}
of the operator
Z_{0}
defined in
(
2.5
). Conversely, each extension
\widetilde{Z}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
of the operator
Z_{0}
determines a
C
-self-adjoint bounded extension

\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}
of
V
of the form (
2.12
).

(2)

The equalities

(2.13)

VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+CV^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}_{0}}={\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}_{0}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}_{0}}C),

hold, where

(2.14)

\widetilde{Z}_{0}=Z_{0}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}};

(3)

If
\widetilde{Z}=Z_{0}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Y}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}
, where
\widetilde{Y}\in{\mathbf{B}}(C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
, then

(2.15)

\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}=\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}_{0}}+{\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{Y}^{*}+C\widetilde{Y}C)P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}.

Proof.

(1): By Lemma
2.2
the operator
\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}
is
C
self-adjoint. If
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
is a
C
-self-adjoint extension of
V
, then the equality

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}C
implies that
C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}C{\upharpoonright\,}{\mathcal{H}}_{V}=V
. Using (
2.4
), for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}Vh=C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}Ch=C(V^{*}+\widetilde{L}^{*})Ch=CV^{*}Ch+C\widetilde{L}^{*}Ch\\
=CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}CVh+C\widetilde{L}^{*}Ch=CCVh-CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVh+C\widetilde{L}^{*}Ch=Vh-CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVh+C\widetilde{L}^{*}Ch.\end{array}

Hence
CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVh+C\widetilde{L}^{*}Ch
, i.e.,
\widetilde{L}^{*}Ch=P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVh,

\forall h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}
. Thus
\widetilde{L}^{*}{\upharpoonright\,}C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}=Z_{0}.

Set

\widetilde{Z}:=\widetilde{L}^{*}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}),\;\widetilde{Y}:=\widetilde{L}^{*}{\upharpoonright\,}C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}\in{\mathbf{B}}(C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}).

Then

(2.16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{Z}=\widetilde{L}^{*}=Z_{0}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Y}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\\
\widetilde{Z}^{*}=Z_{0}^{*}+\widetilde{Y}^{*}\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Z}^{*}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+(Z_{0}^{*}+\widetilde{Y}^{*})P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}=V^{*}+\widetilde{Z}=V^{*}+Z_{0}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Y}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\end{array}\right..

Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}C
, we get
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}C\right).

Now suppose that
\widetilde{Z}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
is an extension of
Z_{0}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Z}^{*}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}
and

\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}C\right)
. Then
C\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}C=\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}

and due to (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) we have
T_{\widetilde{Z}}{\upharpoonright\,}{\mathcal{H}}_{V}=V
.

(2) and (3): Using equalities (
2.8
), (
2.16
), and since
C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}
,
P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=0
, we
derive

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}C=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Z}^{*}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\\
=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+(Z_{0}^{*}+\widetilde{Y}^{*})P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}+VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+C\left(V^{*}+Z_{0}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Y}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\right)CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\\
=2VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+2CV^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}+(\widetilde{Y}^{*}+C\widetilde{Y}C)P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}.\end{array}

Thus,

\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\widetilde{Z}}+C\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}}C\right)=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+CV^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}+{\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{Y}^{*}+C\widetilde{Y}C)P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}.

If
\widetilde{Y}=0\in{\mathbf{B}}(C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp})
, i.e.,
\widetilde{Z}=\widetilde{Z}_{0}
, see (
2.14
), then
equality (
2.13
) holds. Therefore, for
\widetilde{Z}=Z_{0}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+\widetilde{Y}P_{C{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}
the representation (
2.15
) of
\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{Z}}

is valid.
∎

3.
Contractive
C
-self-adjoint extensions of a non-densely
C
-symmetric contraction

A special case is that
V
is a
C
symmetric non-densely defined contraction and
\widetilde{V}
is a
C
-self-adjoint contractive extension of
V
.

The following result belongs to M.G. Crandall
[
10
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The formula

(3.1)

\widetilde{V}_{\widetilde{K}}:=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}

gives a bijective correspondence between all contractive extensions
\widetilde{V}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
of a non-densely defined contraction
V
and all contractions

\widetilde{K}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathfrak{D}}_{V^{*}})
, where
V^{*}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{V})
is the adjoint to
V\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V},{\mathcal{H}})
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}:=(I-VV^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

{\mathfrak{D}}_{V^{*}}:={\rm\overline{ran}\,}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}.

Note that the adjoint
\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\widetilde{K}}
of
\widetilde{V}_{\widetilde{K}}
is of the form

\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\widetilde{K}}=V^{*}+\widetilde{K}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}

and

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}||{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{V}_{\widetilde{K}}}f||^{2}=||{\mathcal{D}}_{V}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}f-V^{*}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}f||^{2}+||{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{K}}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}f||^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
||{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\widetilde{K}}}f||^{2}=||{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{K}^{*}}D_{V^{*}}f||^{2},\;f\in{\mathcal{H}}.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 3.2
.

A contractive extension
\widetilde{V}
of a
C
-symmetric contraction
V
is
C
-self-adjoint if and only if the contraction
\widetilde{K}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp},{\mathfrak{D}}_{V^{*}})
in
(
3.1
) satisfies the following conditions

(3.3)

\widetilde{K}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}Ch=P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVh\;\;\forall h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V},

(3.4)

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}C{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}f=\widetilde{K}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}Cf\;\;\forall f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}.

Proof.

Assume
\widetilde{V}_{\widetilde{K}}=VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}
is
C
-self-adjoint contractive extension of
V
, i.e.,

C\widetilde{V}_{\widetilde{K}}=\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\widetilde{K}}C\Longleftrightarrow C\left(VP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}\right)=\left(V^{*}+\widetilde{K}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\right)C.

The latter is equivalent to the equalities

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}CVP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}C{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=V^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+V^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}},\\[5.69054pt]
P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}CVP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}+P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}C{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=\widetilde{K}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}C.\end{array}\right.

From (
2.3
) it follows that

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}C{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\widetilde{K}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}=V^{*}CP_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}.

Passing to the adjoint we obtain (
3.3
). Then the second equality in (

)

Therefore

Since
Y^{*}{\mathfrak{D}}_{Y^{*}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{D}}_{Y}
for an arbitrary contraction
Y

[
28
]
, from (

\inf\limits_{h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{V}}||{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{V}_{X^{*}_{0}}}f-{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{V}_{X^{*}_{0}}}h||^{2}=||{\mathcal{D}}_{X^{*}_{0}}P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{V}^{\perp}}f||^{2}\;\;\forall f\in{\mathcal{H}},

Hence we conclude that
{\mathcal{D}}_{X^{*}_{0}}g=0
for all

. Thus,

is an isometry.

Let us prove that
\widetilde{W}_{0}=V_{X^{*}_{0}}
is the unique contractive extension of the adjoint pair of contractions

.
Suppose that there is a contractive extension

of the adjoint pair

C
-symmetric contraction

Hence,
\widetilde{W}=\widetilde{W}_{0}=\widetilde{V}_{X^{*}_{0}}
. By Crandall’s Theorem

the operator

4.
Dissipative
C
-symmetric operators and their
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative extensions

First we develop some basics on the Cayley transform of a dissipative operator.

4.1.
Dissipative operators and their Cayley transforms

Recall that
a linear operator
T
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is called
dissipative
if the quadratic form

\gamma_{T}[f]:={\rm Im\,}(Tf,f),\;{\rm dom\,}\gamma={\rm dom\,}T

is nonnegative.
The operator
T
is called
maximal dissipative
if it is dissipative and has no dissipative extensions on
{\mathcal{H}}
. It is well known (see e.g.

[
15
,
16
,
23
,
27
]
) that
a closed densely defined operator
T
is maximal dissipative if and only if
-T^{*}
is maximal dissipative (
T^{*}
is maximal accumulative).
If
T
is a maximal dissipative (
accumulative
) operator, then the resolvent set of
T
contains the open lower half-plane
{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
and the
resolvent satisfies

||(T-zI)^{-1}||\leq(|{\rm Im\,}z|)^{-1}\;\;\forall z\;\;{\rm Im\,}z<0.

The Cayley transform
V_{\lambda}
of a dissipative operator
T
is defined as follows:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\psi=(T-\bar{\lambda}I)f\\
V_{\lambda}\psi=(T-\lambda I)f\end{array}\right.f\in{\rm dom\,}T,\;{\rm Im\,}\lambda>0

Then
{\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda}={\rm ran\,}(T-\bar{\lambda}I)
,
\ker(I-V_{\lambda})=\{0\}
.
The operator
V_{\lambda}
is a contraction. Further,
{\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda}
is a closed subspace if and only if the operator
T
is closed, and
{\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda}={\mathcal{H}}

if and only if the operator
T
is maximal dissipative.

The inverse transform is of the form:

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f=(I-V_{\lambda})h\\
Tf=(\lambda I-\bar{\lambda}V_{\lambda})h\end{array}\right.,\;h\in{\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda}.\end{array}

If
T
is a maximal dissipative operator, then

V_{\lambda}^{*}=(T^{*}-\bar{\lambda}I)(T^{*}-\lambda I)^{-1}.

Let
T
be a closed dissipative operator. Set

{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}:={\rm ran\,}(T-\bar{\lambda}I),\;\;{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}={\mathcal{H}}\ominus{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}.

Then
{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}
is a closed subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
for any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. If
{\rm dom\,}T
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, then
{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}=\ker(T^{*}-\lambda I).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T
be a maximal dissipative operator and let
V_{\lambda}=(T-\lambda I)(T-\bar{\lambda}I)^{-1}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+},
be its Cayley transform (see
(
4.1
)).
Then

\begin{array}[]{l}||{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\lambda}}\psi||^{2}=4~({\rm Im\,}\lambda)\,{\rm Im\,}(T(T-\bar{\lambda}I)^{-1}\psi,(T-\bar{\lambda}I)^{-1}\psi)\quad\forall\psi\in{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
||{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\lambda}}\varphi||^{2}=-4~({\rm Im\,}\lambda)\,{\rm Im\,}(T^{*}(T^{*}-\lambda I)^{-1}\varphi,(T^{*}-\lambda I)^{-1}\varphi)\quad\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}.\end{array}

Proposition 4.2
.

If
T
is a closed densely defined dissipative operator, then

(1)

{\rm dom\,}T\cap{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}=\{0\}\;\;\forall\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+};

(2)

the operator

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm dom\,}\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}={\rm dom\,}T\dot{+}{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}\\
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}(f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda})=Tf_{T}+\lambda\varphi_{\lambda},\;f_{T}\in{\rm dom\,}T,\varphi_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}\end{array}\right.

is a maximal dissipative extension of
T
for each
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and

(4.3)

(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}-\lambda I)(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}-\bar{\lambda}I)^{-1}=V_{\lambda}P_{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}}.

Proof.

Let
V_{\lambda}
be the Cayley transform given by (
4.1
). Then
{\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda}={\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}
,
({\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda})^{\perp}={\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}
.

We show that
\ker(I-P_{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}}V_{\lambda})=\{0\}
.
Suppose that
P_{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}}V_{\lambda}h=h
. Since
V_{\lambda}
is a contraction,
P_{{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}}V_{\lambda}h=0
, i.e.,
V_{\lambda}h=h
. Since

\ker(I-V_{\lambda})=\{0\}
, we get
h=0
.

If
(I-V_{\lambda})h\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}
, then
(I-P_{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}}V_{\lambda})h=0
. Hence
h=0
. Because
{\rm ran\,}(I-V_{\lambda})={\rm dom\,}T
, we obtain

{\rm dom\,}T\cap{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}=\{0\}.

Let
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
be the operator defined in (
4.2
). Then

\begin{array}[]{l}(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}(f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda}),f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda})=(Tf_{T}+\lambda\varphi_{\lambda},f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda})=(Tf_{T},f_{T})+\lambda(\varphi_{\lambda},f_{T})\\[5.69054pt]
+(f_{T},\lambda\varphi_{\lambda})+\lambda||\varphi_{\lambda}||^{2}.\end{array}

Hence

{\rm Im\,}(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}(f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda}),f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda})={\rm Im\,}(Tf_{T},f_{T})+{\rm Im\,}\lambda\,||\varphi_{\lambda}||^{2}\geq 0,\;\;f_{T}\in{\rm dom\,}T,\varphi_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}.

Thus we have shown that the operator
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
is dissipative. Moreover, the equality

(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}-\bar{\lambda}I)(f_{T}+\varphi_{\lambda})=(T-\bar{\lambda}I)f_{T}+(\lambda-\bar{\lambda})\varphi_{\lambda}

implies that
{\rm ran\,}(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}-\bar{\lambda}I)={\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}\oplus{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}={\mathcal{H}}
.
This means that
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
is maximal dissipative.

The equality (
4.3
) follows from (
4.2
) and (
4.1
).
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
T
be a closed densely defined dissipative operator. Then the domain
{\rm dom\,}T^{*}
for each
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
admits the direct decomposition

(4.4)

{\rm dom\,}T^{*}={\rm dom\,}\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}\dot{+}{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda},

where
\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}
is the adjoint to the maximal dissipative extension
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
, defined in (
4.2
).

Proof.

Since
{\rm ran\,}(\widetilde{T}^{*}-\lambda I)={\mathcal{H}}
, for each
f\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}
there exists a vector
g\in{\rm dom\,}\widetilde{T}^{*}
such that
(T^{*}-\lambda I)f=(\widetilde{T}^{*}-\lambda I)g
. So,

f=g+\varphi_{\lambda}
, where
\varphi_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}=\ker(T^{*}-\lambda I)
, and (
4.4
) is satisfied.
∎

4.2.
C-symmetric dissipative operators and the uniqueness of
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative extensions

Let
T
be a closed densely defined
C
-symmetric dissipative operator, i.e.,
{\rm Im\,}(Tf,f)\geq 0
for all
f\in{\rm dom\,}T
and
T\subseteq CT^{*}C
.

According to
[
14
, Theorem 1]
,
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
, a closed
C
-symmetric operator
T
is maximal dissipative if and only if
T
is
C-self-adjoint and dissipative.

Define an operator
S
by

{\rm dom\,}S=C{\rm dom\,}T,\;\;S:=CTC.

Then
S
is a closed densely defined and anti-dissipative (accumulative)
C
-symmetric operator. Moreover,
(Tf,g)=(f,Sg)
for all
f\in{\rm dom\,}T
and all
g\in{\rm dom\,}S.
Thus
\{T,S\}
is an adjoint pair. Moreover,
\widetilde{T}
is a
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative extension of
T
if and only if
\widetilde{S}:=\widetilde{T}^{*}
is a
C
-self-adjoint and maximal anti-dissipative extension of
S
.

From (
4.1
) it follows that if
T
is
C
-symmetric (
C
-self-adjoint), then its Cayley transform
V_{\lambda}
is
C
-symmetric (
C
-self-adjoint) for
each
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
.
By Theorem
3.1
, the Caley transform
V_{\lambda}
admits a
C
-self-adjoint contractive extension
\widetilde{V}
. Then
\widetilde{T}=(\lambda I-\bar{\lambda}\widetilde{V})(I-\widetilde{V})^{-1}
is a
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative extension of
T
.
This proves that
Glazman’s Theorem
1.1
holds.

Our next aim it to supplement this result by some uniqueness criteria.
Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and let
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
, see (
4.2
), be a maximal dissipative extension of the operator
T
.
If
V_{\lambda}
is the Cayley transform of
T
given by (
4.1
), then
{\rm dom\,}V_{\lambda}={\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}
,
V_{\lambda}
is a contraction and the Cayley
transform of
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
is the operator
\widetilde{V}_{\lambda}:=V_{\lambda}P_{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}}
, see Proposition
4.2
and the equality
(
4.3
).

Let us define the following linear space:

(4.5)

{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda}:=(\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}-\lambda I)^{-1}C{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
T
is a closed
C
-symmetric dissipative operator with dense domain.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The operator
T
admits a unique
C
-self-adjoint maximal dissipative extension.

(ii)

The operator
T

\widetilde{T}_{0}
such that
\widetilde{T}_{0}^{*}
is a maximal anti-dissipative extension of

(iii)

For at least one
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
(and then for all such
\lambda

{\rm ran\,}{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}_{\lambda}}\cap C{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}=\{0\}

holds.

(iv)

For at least one
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
(and then for all such
\lambda
) the equality

\inf\limits_{h\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}}||{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}_{\lambda}}C\varphi_{\lambda}-{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}_{\lambda}}Ch||^{2}=0

is satisfied for each
\varphi_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}\setminus\{0\}
.

(v)

For at least one
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
(and then for all such
\lambda
) the equality

\sup\limits_{f\in{\mathcal{H}}}\cfrac{|(Cf,\varphi_{\lambda})|^{2}}{||{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}_{\lambda}}f||^{2}}=+\infty

holds for each
\varphi_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}\setminus\{0\}
.

(vi)

For at least one
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
(and then for all such
\lambda
) the equality

\inf\limits_{\psi\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda}}{\rm Im\,}\left(-\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}(f-\psi),f-\psi\right)=0\;\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}\setminus\{0\}

is valid, where
{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda}
is defined in (
4.5
).

(vii)

For at least one
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
(and then for all such
\lambda
) the equality

\sup\limits_{g\in{\rm dom\,}\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}}\cfrac{|(C(\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}-\bar{\lambda}I)g,\varphi_{\lambda})|^{2}}{-{\rm Im\,}(\widetilde{T}^{*}_{\lambda}g,g)}=+\infty

holds for each
\varphi_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Set
{\mathcal{H}}_{V_{\lambda}}:={\mathfrak{M}}_{\bar{\lambda}}
. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{V_{\lambda}}^{\perp}={\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}
. The non-densely defined contraction
V_{\lambda}
is a
C
-symmetric.
By Theorem
3.3
:
(
3.20
)
\Longleftrightarrow
the operator
V:=V_{\lambda}
admits a unique
C
-self-adjoint contractive extension
\widetilde{V}_{0}

\Longleftrightarrow

\widetilde{V}_{0}
is a unique contractive extension of the adjoint pair of contractions

. But (
3.20
)
is the same as
{\rm ran\,}{\mathcal{D}}_{V_{\lambda}^{*}}\cap C{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}=\{0\}
.
This proves the equivalences of (i), (ii) and (iii).

The equivalence of the other statements can be shown by applying Remark
3.9
with
B:={\mathcal{D}}^{2}_{V_{\lambda}^{*}}
and

Note that

,

.

4.1

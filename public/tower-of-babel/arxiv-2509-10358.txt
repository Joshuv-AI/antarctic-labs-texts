The Single Ring Theorem and a Question of Shub
Joshua Paik

Abstract
. Given an orthogonally invariant probability measure on G ​ L ​ ( d , ℝ ) GL(d,\mathbb{R}) , Mike Shub asked whether the average product of the k k top eigenvalues in the ensemble can be lower bounded by the average distortion along k k dimensional Grassmanians. Recently, [ 1 ] provided partial progress, however they attach a constant c d , k → 0 c_{d,k}\to 0 as d → ∞ d\to\infty . In this paper, by invoking the Single Ring Theorem [ 6 ] [ 7 ] , we show the conjecture asymptotically for the spectral radius, in particular, c d , k → 1 c_{d,k}\to 1 as d → ∞ d\to\infty , and k = 1 k=1 .

2.1.
The Single Ring Theorem

Given a matrix
A
, the
empirical eigenvalue distribution
of
A
is the probability measure

\Lambda_{A}=\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}\delta_{\lambda_{i}(A)},

and the
empirical singular value distribution
of
A
is the probability measure

\Sigma_{A}=\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}\delta_{\sigma_{i}(A)}.

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C})
denote the space of probability measures on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C})).
Denote the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
C\subset\mathbb{C}
as
C^{\varepsilon}
. Recall that the
Lévy–Prokhorov distance
is

d_{LP}(\alpha,\beta)=\inf\left\{\varepsilon>0:\forall C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}),\text{ we have }\begin{cases}\beta(C)\leq\alpha(C^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\\
\alpha(C)\leq\beta(C^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon.\end{cases}\right\}.

A useful fact about the
d_{LP}(\cdot,\cdot)
is that it induces the weak topology
[
3
]
. This means,
\nu_{d}\to\mu
weakly if and only if
\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}d_{LP}(\nu_{d},\mu)=0.

The Single Ring Theorem of Guionnet–Krishnapur–Zeitouni is as follows.

Proof of Theorem

The proof of part
(2)
of Theorem
1.3
uses Levy’s inequality for concentration near the mean. Assume
A_{d}=\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},...,a_{d})
. The assumption of the theorem states that each
a_{i}
is bounded above and below by some fixed
C,1/C
, for every
d

f_{d}:v\in S^{2d-1}\to\|A_{d}v\|^{2}.

The maps
v\to f_{d}(v)
is
C

v\to\|Av\|
is
2C^{2}
Lipschitz, for all
d.

The symmetry of the sphere implies
\mathbb{E}(v_{1}^{2})=\mathbb{E}(v_{2}^{2})=...=\mathbb{E}(v_{d}^{2})
. Because expectation is linear, we have
\mathbb{E}(v_{1}^{2})=1/d
. Therefore, the expectation of
f_{d}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{d-1}}\|Av\|^{2}\ dv=\int_{S^{d-1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{2}\ dv=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}^{2}\int_{S^{d-1}}v_{i}^{2}\ dv=\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}^{2}.

In particular,

\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{E}f_{d}=R_{+}^{2},

where
R_{+}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\ d\mu_{\sigma}(x)}.

Lévy’s inequality asserts that

\mathbb{P}_{d}[|f_{d}(v)-\mathbb{E}f_{d}\geq\varepsilon]=\mathbb{P}_{d}[|\|A_{d}v\|^{2}-R_{+}^{2}|\geq\varepsilon]\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{cd\varepsilon^{2}}{L^{2}}\right).

In the limit, we have then that for all
\varepsilon>0
, then

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{d}[|\|A_{d}v\|^{2}-R_{+}^{2}|\geq\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{d}[|\|A_{d}v\|-R_{+}|\cdot|\|A_{d}v\|+R_{+}|\geq\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\ \mathbb{P}_{d}\left[|\|A_{d}v-R_{+}\|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{C+R_{+}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}2\exp\left(-\frac{cd\varepsilon^{2}}{L^{2}(C+R_{+})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

This implies that

\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{d}[|\|A_{d}v\|-R_{+}\|\geq\varepsilon]=0.

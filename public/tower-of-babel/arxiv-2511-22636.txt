Quantitative Stability for the Brascamp-Lieb inequality and moment measures
João Miguel Machado, João P. G. Ramos

Abstract
. By employing the recently obtained sharp stability versions of the Prékopa–Leindler inequality, we are able to obtain a sharp quantitative stability version for the Brascamp–Lieb inequality, as well as several different results on the stability of moment measures. As main features of our results, we highlight the independence of the stability constant for the Brascamp–Lieb inequality on the convex function considered, a completely novel feature. In the realm of moment measures, we highlight in the same vein that the stability results obtained are uniform , which we expect to be particularly valuable not only from a purely mathematical point of view, but also for applications. Keywords. Brascamp-Lieb inequality, moment measures, optimal transport, stability, log-concave measures 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 39B82, 26D10, 49Q22

Introduction

In recent years, the fields of calculus of variations and functional inequalities have become deeply interwined. If from one hand, many of the inequalities used in analysis and probability have deep variational interpretations, functional and geometric inequalities are fundamental in the study of many variational problems. To name a few exemples we can cite the use of Sobolev type inequalities in the stability of matter
[
44
,
43
]
, the use of isoperimetric type inequalities in the study of shape optimization problems
[
39
]
, and more recently the use of Brascamp-Lieb
[
12
]
and Prékopa-Leindler inequalities
[
47
,
48
,
40
]
has become fundamental on the quantitative stability theory of optimal transport
[
22
,
41
,
42
]
.

In the present work, we study the quantitative stability of the
Brascamp-Lieb inequality
and of
moment measures
– as in, for instance,
[
12
,
19
,
50
]
– and their deep connections. Indeed, that connection has not only been made rigorous several times over (see for instance
[
38
]
where a regularity theory for moment measures is combined with a suitable Brascamp-Lieb inequality to obtain bounds on a Poincaré constant
[
37
]
), but they also have a common denominator in
optimal transport theory
; see
[
49
,
54
,
1
]
.

More specifically, the Brascamp-Lieb inequality is not only ubiquitous in the modern stability theory of optimal transport, but it can also be derived with linearization arguments of transportation type inequalities, as done for example in
[
20
]
. On the other hand, in
[
50
]
it has been showcased how to obtain moment measures with the minimization of functionals coming from optimal transport.

By relying on the recent advances in the quantitative stability of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality in a series of recent works
[

,
11
,

]
, we are able to obtain both a
sharp

In spite of the fact that both objects can be studied from many different perspectives, a common theme between them is that both can be approached from the viewpoint of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality.

Indeed, we mention, for instance, the classical work
[

[

1.1.
Stability of the Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality

In order to describe our main contributions, let us first recall the Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality
[
12
,
7
]
: given a convex function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\displaystyle\varrho\propto e^{-\varphi}
be its associated Gibbs measure. Then for every sufficiently smooth function
f
, the following quantity

(1.1)

\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle(D^{2}\varphi)^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho-\Var_{\varrho}(f)\geq 0

is non-negative. It is well known that the optimal functions for such inequality – which make
\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)
vanish – are the affine functions on the geometry induced by the log-concave measure
\varrho
,
i.e.

f(x)=a\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)+b
; see for instance
[
20
]
or the derivation at the start of Section
3
.

Given the characterization of the optimizers of a functional inequality, the question of stability consists of: under which topology can we estimate the distance of
f
to the manifold of optimizers?

Results of this type have increasingly gained importance over the past two decades. Indeed, the question of stability has been extensively studied in the case of the isoperimetric inequality
[
34
,
28
]
, as well as in other geometric inequalities such as the Faber-Krahn inequality
[
13
]
, and, more recently, in
[
28
,
27
,
30
,
31
,
51
,
52
]
the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, in
[
24
,
23
,
5
]
for Sobolev and log-Sobolev inequalities and relations with non-linear evolution equations, in
[
10
,
32
,
29
]
for the Prékopa-Leindler and Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequalities.

In that vein, our first main contribution is a
sharp
L^{1}-
based stability estimate
for the Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality. In order to state it, we define the finite dimensional manifold of optimal functions

(1.2)

\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}:f(x)=a\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)+b,\text{ for some }a\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ b\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Clearly, if
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}}
, then the parameter
b
must be given by
\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f
since

\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=a\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\varphi(x)e^{-\varphi}{\rm d}x+b=a\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla(e^{-\varphi}){\rm d}x+b=b.

Our first main result must then be an estimate of the distance of a function
f
to the manifold of optimizers

(1.3)

\text{dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm inf}_{g\in\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}}}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}=\mathop{\rm inf}_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert f-(a\cdot\nabla\varphi+\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})},

in terms of the deficit of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be an essentially continuous and convex function, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi}<+\infty
so that the Gibbs mesuare
\varrho_{\varphi}
is well defined as in (
1.4
).

Then, there exists a universal constant
C_{d}
depending only on the ambient dimension such that

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}})\leq C_{d}{\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)}^{1/2}

for all locally Lipschitz functions
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
.

As the classical Brascamp-Lieb is independent of the dimension, the first efforts in the literature into improving it were focused on next order terms that would exploit the ambient dimension
[
36
,
8
]
. These results are equivalent with bounding the deficit from below with a dimensional term which vanishes in the manifold of optimizers for this inequality. A similar improvement was also obtained in
[
20
]
, by measuring the distance to the manifold of optimal functions with a
L^{2}
term in a geometry that compensates for the curvature induced by the potential
V
. This is very natural to expect for the Brascamp-Lieb inequality as it is closely related to the spectral gap of the diffusion operator induced by
\nabla V
that is central to the
carré du champ method

[
3
,
4
]
, see also Section
3
. We refer the reader to
[
39
]
for a particular version for the Gaussian Poincaré inequality with relations to uncertainty principles.

It is worth pointing out that not only the present work is the first that proposes a quantitative stability result for the Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality in full generality, but that the result is
sharp
in terms of the dependence on
\delta_{\textrm{BL}}
on the right-hand side, with constant
independent
of
\varphi
. In particular, the independence of our stability results on the convex function provides us with the perfect platform to apply Theorem
1.10
to deduce several novel results about the stability of moment measures, as we shall see below.

Our method of proof will be based on the approach of Bobkov and Ledoux
[
7
]
. A main new twist in our proof, however, is the usage of the newly developed
sharp stability estimates
for the Prékopa-Leindler inequality stemming from the work of A. Figalli, P. van Hintum and M. Tiba
[
32
]
, together with a careful use of the geometry of the functionals involved in order to conclude. This explains our result being stated in terms of the
L^{1}
-topology, which is natural viewing the Brascamp-Lieb inequality as a geometric inequality steaming from Brunn-Minkowski’s.

1.2.
Stabiliy for moment measures

Our next few results use the previous machinery to obtain sharp quantitative stability results for moment measures. Using Prékopa-Leindler, we obtain stability of the Gibbs measures in the moment measures representation, while the quantitative version of Brascamp-Lieb yields stability of the potentials.

Given a convex function
\varphi
, we recall the notation of its associated Gibbs probability measure

(1.4)

\varrho_{\varphi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{e^{-\varphi}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi}}.

On the other hand, given
\psi
also convex, which we will view informally as in the dual space of variables of
\varphi
by frequently considering
\psi=\varphi^{*}
, we define the
moment measure
associated with
\psi
as

(1.5)

\mu_{\psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}{(\nabla\psi)}_{\sharp}\varrho_{\psi}.

In
[
19
,
50
]
, it was proven that for any measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R^{d}})
, that is a Radon probability measure over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite first moments, admits a moment measure representation, there exists a convex and essentially continuous (see the definition in Section
2.2
) potential
\psi_{\mu}
such that (
1.5
) holds. In addition, it is also unique up to translations. Using the Prékopa-Leindler inequality, it was shown in
[
19
]
that the moment measure of
\psi
is
\mu
if and only if its Legendre transform
\varphi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\psi^{*}
is a maximizer of

(1.6)

\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi^{*}}{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi{\rm d}\mu.

The same result was recovered in
[
50
]
using optimal transport, but this time for a dual perspective: a measure
\varrho
is the Gibbs measure in (
1.5
) if and only if it minimizes

(1.7)

\mathscr{E}_{\mu}(\varrho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\varrho(x){\rm d}\varrho(x)+\mathcal{T}(\varrho,\mu),

where
\mathcal{T}
is the maximal correlation formulation of the quadratic optimal transport problem. The reader is referred to Sections
2.1
and
2.2
for more details on optimal transport theory and for general results on moment measures.

The quantitative stability question that we wish to answer then becomes: given two measures
\mu,\nu
can we quantify the distance between either
\psi_{\mu},\psi_{\nu}
or between
\varrho_{\mu},\varrho_{\nu}
. Since the variational characterization described above is necessary and sufficient, one can only expect to answer this question as an estimate on the distance to the optimal sets defined as

(1.8)

\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\mu},\quad\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\mu},

which can be characterized as follows: fixed some reference
\varrho_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
and
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mu}
, the manifolds of optimizers can be expressed as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\{\varrho_{0}(\cdot-x_{0}):x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\{\varphi_{0}+(a\cdot x+b):a\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ b\in\mathbb{R}\}.

We start with the following Theorem, which is a backbone to the stability results that will follow. We hope it can be used to other purposes, such as deriving explicit convergence rates for numerical algorithms for computing moment measures.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given a Radon measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose barycenter lies that the origin and
\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\mu=d
, let
\bar{\varphi}
be a maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
. Then for any convex function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
it holds that

(1.10)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\varrho_{\varphi^{*}},\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d}{\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)}^{1/2}.

In addition, there exists
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
such that, setting
v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\bar{\varphi}-\varphi
and
\varphi_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\bar{\varphi}+\lambda v
, it holds that

(1.11)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mu_{\lambda})}\left(\varphi,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)^{1/2},

where
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{*}}
is the moment measure associated with the interpolation
(\varphi_{\lambda})^{*}
.

We note that this result may be regarded as a Polyak-Lojasiewicz type inequality for the functional
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
. More precisely, estimate (
1.11
) gives a way of using this functional to measure the distance of any
\varphi
to the class of maximizers of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
. This is done by establishing a connection between the equality case of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality and the invariance of the functional
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
with respect to the addition of affine functions. Estimate (
4.1
) relates the invariance of the moment measure representation with respect to translations to the equality cases in the Prékopa-Leindler inequality, estimating the distance of
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}
to the set of minimizers of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
.

In spite of the fact that Theorem
1.2
provides us, by itself, with a novel quantitative way to show that almost maximizers of the functionals
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
and
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
must indeed converge to the original potentials, we highlight that its true scope lies in the
deeper applications
to the theory of moment measures it unveils, as pointed below.

Stability in a compact domain

Using Theorem
1.2
, we are able to obtain several quantitative stability results for moment measures in different contexts. When the moment measure and the associated Gibbs measure in (
1.5
) are restricted to a compact domain we obtain a strong stability result controlling the
L^{1}
distances between
\varrho_{\mu},\varrho_{\nu}
and
\varphi_{\mu},\varphi_{\nu}
with the
1
-Wasserstein distance of
\mu,\nu
; this is the content of Theorem
4.2
.

In spite of the fact that compactness allows for a fairly simpler mathematical structure, diverging from the objects described by the existence theorem by
[
19
,
50
]
, we remark that such a result means a remarkable control
especially for numerical applications
, as we are able to control a strong norm with a distance which metrizes a fairly weak convergence of Radon measures, see Section
2.1
for more details on these topologies.

Rates of convergence for quadratic regularization

The classical definition of moment measures from (
1.5
) has strong motivations from convex and toric geometry
[
6
]
, but variations thereof have recently found several applications to sampling and generative models
[
53
]
. For such applications, the essential property of moment measures is their capacity of representing a given measure via the pushforward of a log-concave distribution through the gradient of the same convex convex potential.

This can also be achieved with the
quadratically regularized moment measures
recently proposed in
[
21
]
. That is, given
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there is a convex potential
\psi_{\alpha}
such that

(1.12)

\mu={(\nabla\psi_{\alpha})}_{\sharp}\varrho_{\alpha},\text{ where }\varrho_{\alpha}\propto e^{-(\varphi_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\cdot|^{2})};

see also Section
2.2
.

By introducing the quadratic regularization, the quantitative stability investigated in
[
21
]
turns out to become a bit simplified for a few reasons: first, we mention that the uniqueness modulo translations is replaced by simply uniqueness. This way, the quantitative stability can be understood without the distance to a certain manifold of optimizers. Secondly, the regularization makes the functional corresponding to (
1.7
) strongly geodesically convex in Wasserstein space. On the other hand, the stability is formulated with the
2
-Wasserstein distance.

For these reasons, both of theoretical and of applied nature, obtaining explicit rates of convergence with respect to the regularization parameter is of great interest. This is achieved once again by using Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix a Radon
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose barycenter lies at the origin and such that
\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\mu=d
. Then there exists a constant
C_{d,\mu}
depending on the ambient dimension and on
\mu
such that

(1.13)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\varrho_{\alpha},\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d,\mu}\alpha^{1/2},

for
\varrho_{\alpha}
given in (
1.12
).

In addition, set
\varphi_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\psi_{\alpha}
, where
\psi_{\alpha}
is given in (
1.12
). Then, for each
\alpha
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
such that, setting
v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi-\varphi_{\alpha}
and
\varphi_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi+\lambda v
, it holds that

(1.14)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mu_{\lambda})}\left(\varphi_{\alpha},\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d,\mu}\alpha^{1/2},

where
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{*}}
is the moment measure associated with the interpolation
(\varphi_{\lambda})^{*}
.

Stability in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

To tackle the stability question in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we can in principle combine the quantitative stability of regularized moment measures arguments based on strong geodesic convexity from
[
21
]
with our explicit rates of convergence
1.3
. But, in order to achieve this, we have to make quantitative the assumption that
\mu
is not supported on a hyperplane, otherwise such a result would allow the construction of a
\psi
yielding (
1.5
) for such a singular
\mu
, contradicting the existence theory of
[
19
,
50
]
. See Proposition
4.8
for more details on this construction.

Our approach is then to either impose a
uniform lower bound
on the geometric quantity

\Theta(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm inf}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\theta\cdot y|{\rm d}\mu(y)\geq\vartheta,

or to control the Hessian of the log-density of
\mu
. This is done by defining the two classes

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\left\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega):\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\text{ is centered, }\\
\displaystyle\Theta(\mu)\geq\vartheta\end{array}\right\}\text{ for a compact $\Omega$,}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\left\{\mu\in\Pac(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\propto e^{-V}\text{ is centered, }\\
\displaystyle D^{2}V\leq\Lambda{\mathop{\rm id}}\end{array}\right\}.

The results we obtain can be summarized as follows, where the reader is referred to Section
4.3
for more information.

Theorem 1.4
.

Take
\mathcal{K}
to be either
\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
or
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
. Then for each
p>2
there exists a constant
C
such that, for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{K}
it holds thats

(1.15)

{\rm dist}_{W_{2}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mu},\mathcal{M}_{\nu}\right)\leq C{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}^{\frac{p-2}{6p-4}},

where
C
grows linearly in
p
.

In both cases, our approach is to control the moments of order
p
of the Gibbs measures
\varrho_{\mu}
, uniformly for all elements
\mu
of the classes
\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
. With this finer information we can come back to the strong geodesic convexity arguments for regularized moment measures from
[
21
]
and obtain an optimal dependence on
\alpha
in the quantitative stability results in this case. For the first class
\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
, this can be done with a Lemma
4.6
due to Klartag. For the class
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
, similar controls on the
p
-moments can be obtained with a regularity result of moment measures, namely

(1.16)

\mu\propto e^{-V}={\left(\nabla\psi\right)}_{\sharp}e^{-\psi},\text{ such that }D^{2}V\leq\Lambda{\mathop{\rm id}},\text{ then $\psi$ is $\Lambda^{-1/3}$-strongly convex.}

The proof of (
1.16
) is done in Theorem
4.11
, and is based on a bootstrap argument using Caffarelli’s contraction theorem
[
14
,
15
]
. It states that if
\mu
is as in (
1.16
) and
\nu\propto e^{-W}
with
D^{2}W\geq\lambda{\mathop{\rm id}}
, then the optimal transportation map
T
from
\mu
to
\nu
is globally Lipschitz with
\mathop{\rm Lip}T\leq\sqrt{\Lambda/\lambda}
. Since we cannot know in principle that
\psi
is strongly convex, what we do instead is consider the quadratic regularized moment measure presentation of
\mu
. In this case, the Gibbs measure has indeed a strongly convex potential, with a modulus of convexity
\alpha
. This modulus of convexity can be iteratively increased with successive applications of the contraction theorem, which yields
\Lambda^{-1/3}
at the end. Besides the application to the stability of moment measures, we believe that this result might have important applications in sampling, see the discussion after the proof of Theorem
4.11
.

2.
Some tools on optimal transport and functional inequalities

2.1.
A primer on optimal transport and Wasserstein distances

The Wasserstein distances are defined via the value function of the optimal transport problem as

(2.1)

W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm min}_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{p}{\rm d}\gamma(x,y),

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
the set of probability measures over
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose marginals are respectively
\mu
and
\nu
, the so called set o transportation plans. This quantity is finite if and only if
\mu
and
\nu
have finite
p
-moments,
M_{p}(\mu),M_{p}(\nu)<+\infty
, where

(2.2)

M_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}{\rm d}\mu.

The reader is referred to
[
49
,
54
]
for a complete introduction to optimal transport and Wasserstein distances, in what follows we introduce its properties that will be useful in the sequel.

We are particularly interested in the case
p=2
, where the optimal transport problem
2.1
has a close relation with convex analysis. This can be easily seen by introducing the
maximal correlation formulation

(2.3)

\mathcal{T}(\mu,\nu)=\mathop{\rm sup}_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle{\rm d}\gamma=\mathop{\rm inf}_{\varphi\text{ convex }}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi{\rm d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi^{*}{\rm d}\nu,

in such a way that

\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}M_{2}(\mu)+\frac{1}{2}M_{2}(\nu)-\mathcal{T}(\mu,\nu).

The infimum on the RHS is attained by a convex function
\varphi
, and Brenier’s Theorem states that the optimal transportation plan is unique and given by
\gamma=(\mathrm{id},\nabla\varphi)_{\sharp}\mu
, whenever
\mu
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure.

Topological properties of Wasserstein distances

A very important property of Wasserstein distances is that they metrize the narrow topology of probability measures. A sequence of Radon probability measures
{\left(\mu_{n}\right)}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges narrowly to
\mu
if for all
f\in\mathscr{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, continuous and bounded function, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\mu_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\mu,

and we write
\mu_{n}\xrightharpoonup[n\to\infty]{}\mu
. A very important property is that

(2.4)

W_{p}(\mu_{n},\mu)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0\text{ if and only if }\begin{cases}\mu_{n}\xrightharpoonup[n\to\infty]{}\mu,&\\
M_{p}(\mu_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}M_{p}(\mu).\end{cases}

In addition, the space of Radon probability measures with finite
p
-moments
\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
becomes itself complete and separable when endowed with the topology of
W_{p}
.

This is conceptually very important for the contributions of this work since many of the quantitative stability results regarding moment measure representations from Section
4
are stated with respect to the Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
. This allows to compare very singular objects with a weak topology. On the other hand, if we know that two probability measures are also in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and have finite
p
-moments, then we can interpolate the Wasserstein distance with the
L^{1}
distance via the following inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two probability densities in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with finite
p
-moments, for some
p>1
. Then, for all
1\leq q<p
there exists a constant
C_{p,q}
such that

(2.5)

W_{q}(\mu,\nu)\leq C_{p,q}{(M_{p}(\mu)+M_{p}(\nu))}^{1/p}\left\lVert\mu-\nu\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{1/q-1/p}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu,\nu
have densities
f,g
, set
\varepsilon\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and define the common density and the residuals
f_{r},g_{r}
as

h(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm min}\{f(x),g(x)\}\ f_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}f-h,\ g_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}g-h.

As a result, we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{r}(x){\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{r}(x){\rm d}x=\varepsilon,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{r}(x){\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{r}(x){\rm d}x=0.

so that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{r}(x){\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{r}(x){\rm d}x=\varepsilon/2
.

Now, we can construct a transportation plan
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
as follows: we first transport the common part of both densities via the identity map, and to transport the residuals we use the product measure. More precisely, we set
\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varepsilon/2
and

\gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}{(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})}_{\sharp}h+\frac{1}{\alpha}f_{r}(x)g_{r}(y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y.

We then have

\displaystyle W_{q}^{q}(\mu,\nu)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{q}{\rm d}\gamma(x,y)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{q}f_{r}(x)g_{r}(y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}I}.

To estimate the RHS, we split the integral into two parts as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2},

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}|x-y|^{q}f_{r}(x)g_{r}(y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y+\int_{(B_{R}\times B_{R})^{c}}|x-y|^{q}f_{r}(x)g_{r}(y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y.

The first term can be easily estimated as

I_{1}\leq(2R)^{q}\alpha^{2},

while the second can be estimated as with the
p
-moments, for any
p>q
by noticing that for
|x|>R
we have
|x|^{q}\leq R^{q-p}|x|^{p}
, so that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}\alpha\int_{|x|\geq R}|x|^{q}f_{r}(x){\rm d}x+2^{q-1}\alpha\int_{|y|\geq R}|y|^{q}g_{r}(y){\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}R^{q-p}\alpha\left(M_{p}(\mu)+M_{p}(\nu)\right).

Combining both estimates, we obtain

W_{q}^{q}(\mu,\nu)\leq(2R)^{q}\alpha+2^{q-1}R^{q-p}\left(M_{p}(\mu)+M_{p}(\nu)\right).

Optimizing in
R>0
gives the desired result.
∎

Geodesic convexity in Wasserstein spaces

Given two probability measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, any optimal transportation plan
\gamma
yields a natural interpolation between then given by

\mu_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}{\pi_{t}}_{\sharp}\gamma,\text{ where }\pi_{t}(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty,\quad t\in[0,1].

It turns out that this interpolation is a constant speed geodesic in the metric space
(\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{p})
, see
[

This allows to define the notion of geodesic convexity for functionals defined over Wasserstein spaces. A functional
\mathscr{F}:\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to be geodesically convex if for every
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any geodesic interpolation
\mu_{t}
between them the function
t\mapsto\mathscr{F}(\mu_{t})
is convex in the classical sense. It is strictly geodesically convex if the same function is strictly convex.

The
2
-Wasserstein distance is not itself geodesically convex, this is a feature of the geometry of the Wasserstein space, see
[
2
, Chapter 9]
. In fact, it satisfies the opposite inequality. In
[

, Santambrogio exploited this fact to show the geodesic convexity of the maximal correlation functional
\varrho\mapsto\mathcal{T}(\varrho,\mu)
. This fact is at the heart of the study of moment measures via optimal transport, as well as the geodesic convexity of the entropy functional defined as

(2.6)

H(\varrho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\varrho{\rm d}\varrho,&\text{ if }\varrho\ll\mathscr{L}^{d},\\
+\infty,&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

which follows from McCann’s criterion
[
46
]

And seems to be equivalent ot the Prékopa-Leindler inequality

]
.

.

Another important example of geodesically convex functionals are the
p
-moments,
\mu\mapsto M_{p}(\mu)
, this follows directly from the convexity of
x\mapsto|x|^{p}
, see
[
49
, Chapter 7]
. In fact, it is easy to see that the second moment
M_{2}
is even
strongly
geodesically convex, that is, for any geodesic interpolation
\mu_{t}
between

and

it holds that

(2.7)

M_{2}(\mu_{t})\leq(1-t)M_{2}(\mu_{0})+tM_{2}(\mu_{1})-\frac{1}{2}t(1-t)W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

This property is particularly useful to derive quantitative stability results for minimizers, as done in

2.2.
General facts on moment measures

In
[
19
]
, Cordero-Eurasquin and Klartag gave a complete characterization of the measures
\mu
which admit a meaningful moment measure representation. Their analysis goes through the functionals defined as in (
1.6
)

They identified that if the potential
\psi
in (
1.5
) does not satisfy certain regularity properties, its moment measure can loose a lot of information. They shed some light on this issue with the following example: take
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider

\psi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}x\cdot y,&x\in C\\
+\infty,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then one can readily check that
\mu_{\psi}=\delta_{y}
, which does not give much information on the measure
\delta_{y}
, and certainly is not amenable to a quantitative stability result since one can drastically change the convex set
C
and obtain the same moment measure. For this reason they focus on characterizing measures that admit a moment measure representation with an
essentially continuous
and convex function.

Definition 2.2
.

A convex function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to be essentially continuous if it is lower semi-continuous and the set of points where it is discontinuous has zero
\mathscr{H}^{d-1}
measure.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\psi
be a convex and essentially continuous function such that
0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\psi}<+\infty
, and such that
0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\psi}<+\infty
. Then, its moment measure
\mu_{\psi}
is centered and its support spans
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular,

\mathop{\rm inf}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x\cdot\theta|{\rm d}\mu_{\psi}(x)>0.

Alternatively, Santambrogio proposed an optimal transport proof of the same result by exploring the
geodesic convexity
of the following functional

(2.8)

\mathop{\rm min}_{\varrho\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathscr{E}_{\mu}(\varrho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}H(\varrho)+\mathcal{T}(\varrho,\mu),

where
H
corresponds to the negative entropy functional (
2.6
) and
\mathcal{T}
denotes Brenier’s formulation of the quadratic optimal transport problem, or maximimal correlation problem (
2.3
). Santambrogio relates both problems and recovers the characterization of moment measures with minimizers of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
, which are log-concave probability densities whose potential is essentially continuous, thus recovering the results of Cordero-Eurasquin and Klartag.

More recently, Delalande and Farineli
[
21
]
studied the quantitative stability of
regularized moment measures
, which they define via the unique minimizer of the following functional, which consists of a second moment regularized version of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
:

(2.9)

\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}H(\varrho)+\mathcal{T}(\varrho,\mu)+\frac{\alpha}{2}M_{2}(\varrho).

Minimizers of the regularized version are now unique and of the following form: there exists
\psi_{\alpha}
convex such that

\varrho_{\alpha}=e^{-(\psi_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}\text{ and }{(\nabla\psi_{\alpha})}_{\sharp}e^{-(\psi_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}=\mu.

A major interest of introducing regularized moment measures is computational; indeed regularizing the functional
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
with the second moment term
M_{2}(\varrho)
makes it
\alpha
-strongly geodesicaly convex in the Wasserstein geometry while preserving the most important feature of the representation via moment measures, that is being the push-forward of a Gibbs measure through (a perturbation of) its potential’s gradient. For these reasons, its not surprising that the arguments in
[
21
]
are based on strong geodesic convexity and actually do not require the dual formulation of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
.

To fully exploit the sharp stability version of Prékopa-Leindler’s inequality, we shall require the connection of both primal and dual formulations. For this reason we introduce the regularized dual functional defined for any
\alpha\geq 0
as

(2.10)

\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(\varphi^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi{\rm d}\mu.

In the following theorem, we summarize the contributions from the literature and show a strong-duality type result with a small adaptation of a remark from Santambrogio in
[
50
]
, but that will be useful in the sequel. More importantly, we identify a way to map minimizers of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
into maximizers of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
and vice-versa.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
19
,
50
,
21
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon probability measure in
\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose barycenter lies at the origin and such that
\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\mu=d
. The following assertions hold:

•

For every
\alpha\geq 0
there is strong duality:

(2.11)

\mathop{\rm sup}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}=-\mathop{\rm inf}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}.

•

\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
admits a minimizer of the form

(2.12)

\varrho_{\alpha}=e^{-(\psi_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}\text{ and }{(\nabla\psi_{\alpha})}_{\sharp}\varrho_{\alpha}=\mu,

where
\psi_{\alpha}
is an essentially continuous and convex function, so that
\nabla\psi_{\alpha}
is the unique Brenier map from
\varrho_{\alpha}
to
\mu
.

If
\alpha>0
this minimizer is unique; and if
\alpha=0
it is unique up to translations. In any case, if
\varrho_{\alpha}
is a minimizer, defining

(2.13)

\varphi_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\text{ for }\psi_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}-\log\varrho_{\alpha}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2},

it follows that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is a maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
.

•

\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
admits a maximizer
\varphi_{\alpha}
, whose Legendre transform
\psi_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}
is an essentially continuous and convex function, and gives the regularized moment measure representation for
\mu

(2.14)

{(\nabla\psi_{\alpha})}_{\sharp}e^{-(\psi_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}=\mu

If
\alpha>0
, the maximizer is unique; whereas if
\alpha=0
it is unique up to the addition of an affine function.

In any case, if
\varphi_{\alpha}
is a maximizer, defining
\psi_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}
it follows that

(2.15)

\varrho_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}e^{-(\psi_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}

is a minimizer of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
.

Proof.

First let us prove the strong duality type-result. It was already proven in the case
\alpha=0
in
[
50
]
, we prove it here for general
\alpha
for completeness and for the reader’s convenience.

First, we compute the Legendre transform of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
. It is defined as a functional over the space of convex functions as

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}^{*}(f)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm sup}_{\rho\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\langle f,\rho\right\rangle-H(\rho)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}{\rm d}\rho-\mathop{\rm inf}_{\varphi\text{ convex }}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi^{*}{\rm d}\rho+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi{\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm sup}_{\varphi\text{ convex }}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi{\rm d}\mu+\mathop{\rm sup}_{\rho\in\Pac(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\langle f-\varphi^{*}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2},\rho\right\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\rho{\rm d}\rho

The supremum on
\rho
is attained, and the maximizer is characterized by the following Euler-Lagrange equations; see
[
49
]
:
\rho>0
a.e. over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\log\rho+\varphi^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2}-f\equiv\text{cte},

so that
\rho\propto e^{-(\varphi^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2}-f)}
. A straightforward computation then gives

\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}^{*}(f)=\mathop{\rm sup}_{\varphi\text{ convex }}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(\varphi^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2}-f)}{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi{\rm d}\mu.

As a result, taking
f\equiv 0
we get that

\mathop{\rm sup}_{\varphi\text{ convex }}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi)=-\mathop{\rm inf}_{\varrho\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho).

The existence of minimizers for
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
and maximizers for
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
is completely equivalent, as we will see in the sequel. However, the proof of existence for
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
and general
\alpha\geq 0
has been done in
[
21
]
, along with the claimed characterization of minimizers and the relation with moment measures. Therefore, to be economic the reader referred to this work for a proof of the second assertion, we only comment that the uniqueness in the case
\alpha>0
stems from the strong geodesic convexity of the energy, while the functional
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
is invariant with respect to translations,
[
50
, Prop. 3.1]
.

Now given a minimizer
\varrho_{\alpha}
, for any
\alpha\geq 0
, consider
\varrho_{\alpha}
as defined above and let us compute
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\alpha})
as follows

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\alpha}{\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\log\varrho_{\alpha}}{\rm d}x}_{=0}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\alpha}{\rm d}\mu.

Now using the regularized moment measure representation for
\mu
in terms of
\psi_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}
and Fenchel’s identity
\varphi_{\alpha}(\nabla\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}(x))=\left\langle x,\nabla\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}(x)\right\rangle-\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}(x)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\nabla\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}(x))e^{-(\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle x,\nabla\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}(x)\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\alpha}}_{=\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{\alpha},\mu)}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2}\right){\rm d}\varrho_{\alpha}}_{=-H(\varrho_{\alpha})}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\alpha}).

As a result, we finally obtain that

\mathop{\rm sup}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}\geq\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\psi_{\alpha})=-\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\alpha})=-\mathop{\rm inf}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}=\mathop{\rm sup}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha},

so that
\varphi_{\alpha}
must be a maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
.

Doing the same reasoning, but on the inverse, starting an arbitrary maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
, we can construct a log-concave probability measure which minimizes
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
, but since those are unique thanks to the strong geodesic convexity, or at least unique up to a translation in the case
\alpha=0
, we obtain that the maximizers of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
must also be unique, or unique up to adding an affine function in the case
\alpha=0
.
∎

It can also be shown that the Gibbs measure associated with the moment measure (resp. regularized moment measure) representation of a given
\mu
is a minimizer for
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
(resp.
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
), see
[
50
, Prop. 5.1]
for classical moment measures,
[
21
, Prop. 4.2]
and
[
19
, Thm. 8]
for the corresponding statement for the dual functional
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
.

The moment measure representation can be seen as a stationarity condition for
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
and its maximazation can be studied through its concavity, which is equivalent to the Brascamp-Lieb inequality. Indeed, its first and second variations are computed as follows:

Lemma 2.5
.

The first and second variations of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
are computed as follows:
given two convex functions
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}
, setting
v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{0}
,
\varphi_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi_{0}+tv
, and the Gibbs probaility measure
\displaystyle\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}\propto e^{-(\varphi^{*}_{t}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}
and its regularized moment measure
\mu_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}={(\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*})}_{\sharp}\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{t})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v{\rm d}(\mu_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}-\mu).

In addition, defining
w(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}v(\nabla\varphi^{*}(x))
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{t})=\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi^{*}_{t},\alpha}}(w)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi_{t}^{*})}^{-1}\nabla w,\nabla w\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi^{*}_{t},\alpha}.

Proof.

For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we start by computing the first and second time derivatives of
\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)
with an application of the envelope theorem. This quantity can be shown to be
\mathscr{C}^{2}
with an implicit function argument as done rigorously in
[
22
]
.

Let
y_{t}
be optimal for the supremum defining
\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)
, that is

(2.16)

y_{t}=\mathop{\rm argmax}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle y,x\right\rangle-\varphi_{t}(y),\text{ so that }y_{t}={\nabla\varphi_{t}}^{-1}(x)=\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*}(x).

From the envelope theorem we have that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)=\left.\partial_{t}\left(\left\langle y,x\right\rangle-\varphi_{t}(y)\right)\right|_{y=y_{t}}=-v(\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)).

To compute the second time derivative, we then have

\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)=-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}v(\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*}(x))=-\nabla v(\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*}(x))\cdot y^{\prime}_{t}.

Taking derivatives w.r.t.
t
on the optimality condition defining
y_{t}
, that is
x=\nabla\varphi_{t}(y_{t})
, we get

y_{t}^{\prime}=-D^{2}\varphi_{t}(y_{t})^{-1}\nabla v(y_{t}).

In addition, for the gradient of the quantity
w
we have that

\nabla w(x)=\nabla(v(y_{t}))=D^{2}\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)(\nabla v)\circ y_{t}.

Therefore, using that
D^{2}\varphi_{t}(y_{t})^{-1}=D^{2}\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)
, the previous computation gives

\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\varphi_{t}^{*}(x)=\left\langle{\left(D^{2}\varphi_{t}^{*}\right)}^{-1}\nabla w,\nabla w\right\rangle.

We can then compute the first and second time derivatives of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi+tv)
via derivation under the integral sign as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(\varphi^{*}_{t}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}\left(-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\varphi_{t}^{*}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v{\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v{\rm d}(\mu_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}-\mu),

for
Z_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(\varphi^{*}_{t}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}
.

For the second time derivative, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{t})

\displaystyle=-\frac{Z_{t}^{\prime}}{Z_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*}){\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(v(\nabla\varphi_{t}^{*})^{2}-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\varphi_{t}^{*}\right){\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}}(w)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{\left(D^{2}\varphi_{t}^{*}\right)}^{-1}\nabla w,\nabla w\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*},\alpha}.

The result follows.
∎

For
\alpha=0
, the above computation for the second variation of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
gives precisely minus the deficit of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality. In the case that
\alpha>0
we need to be a bit careful to perform a Taylor expansion on the Hessian and get the same deficit plus lower order terms in
\alpha
. Either way, these computations give a clear intuition on why Cordero-Eurasquin and Klartag’s characterization should hold, but it does not provide the essential continuity of the potential, which is at the heart of their statement.

Remark 2.6
.

More importantly to our purposes, it becomes clear that having access to a sharp quantitative stability version of Brascamp-Lieb’s inequality shall give stability results for the potentials of moment measure representations. As discussed in the introduction, there are a few next order expansions of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality in the literature
[
20
,
8
,
36
]
. However, not only our Theorem
4.13
is more adapted to our purposes as we show that the deficit
\delta_{\textrm{BL}}
controls a distance, but also we do it with a multiplicative constant
C_{d}
that is independent of the measure
\varrho_{\varphi}
. This last feature is crucial for the applications to moment measures, as we shall see below.

2.3.
State of the art on the Prékopa-Leindler inequality

The original version of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality was initially proposed independently by Prékopa in
[
47
,
48
]
and Leindler
[
40
]
and can be stated as follows: given a triplet of measurable functions
f,g,h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for a given parameter
s\in(0,1)
the so-called
Prékopa condition
holds

(2.17)

f(x)^{s}g(y)^{1-s}\leq h(sx+(1-s)y)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

then we have the following bound

(2.18)

{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x){\rm d}x\right)}^{s}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x){\rm d}x\right)}^{1-s}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(x){\rm d}x.

This inequality is a functional generalization of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, for which many stability results have been derived, see for instance the seminal work
[
28
]
, where stability version of such geometric inequalities have been derived.

A stability version for the Prékopa-Leindler inequality quantifies how functions nearly satisfying the equality case must be close to an optimal log-concave profile. The result takes the following form: if
h,f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfy Prékopa condition (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) is almost satisfied in the sense that

(2.19)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(x){\rm d}x\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x){\rm d}x\right)^{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x){\rm d}x\right)^{1-s}.

for some
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a log-concave function
\tilde{h}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and parameters
a>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following proximity estimates hold:

(2.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)-a^{-s}\tilde{h}(x-sx_{0})|dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\tau)\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}(\tau)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(x)-a^{1-s}\tilde{h}(x+(1-s)x_{0})|dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\tau)\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}(\tau)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(x)-\tilde{h}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\tau)\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}(\tau)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(x)dx,

where

\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm min}(s,1-s),\quad a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g}

C(\tau)
is a constant that depends on
\tau
, and
\alpha_{n}(\tau)>0
is a computable exponent that typically depends on both the dimension
n
and the parameter
\tau
.

Obtaining sharper versions of this inequality is currently a very active area of research. On one side, the sharp exponent
\alpha
is expected to be
1/2
, as is the case, for instance, for the isoperimetric and Brunn-Minkowski inequalities
[
28
]
. Indeed, this exponent has been obtained in
[
32
]
, but at the cost of a sub-obtimal constant
C(\tau)
that does not behave properly as
\tau\to 0^{+}
. On the other hand, in
[
29
]
, a quantitative stability version has been shown with
\alpha=1/2
and
C\propto\tau^{-1/2}
in the 1D or radial in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, when the involved functions are log-concave.

In the present work, we will only need a stability version of Prékopa-Leindler for the case
\tau=1/2
. Therefore, the
\tau-
dependence of the constant
C(\tau)
is not particularly important for us, and we may hence use some of the results from
[
32
]
directly, which we collect in the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f,g,h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be measurable functions satisfying Prékopa’s condition (
2.17
) with
s=1/2
. If there is
\varepsilon>0
such that (
2.19
) holds, there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a dimensional constant
C_{d}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{f(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f}-\frac{g(x+x_{0})}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g}\right|{\rm d}x\leq C_{d}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Proof.

Recall
a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g}
, so from
[
32
]
, estimates (
2.20
) hold with
\alpha=1/2
and a constant
C(\tau)=C(1/2)=C_{d}
, that depends only on the dimension. So there is a log-concave function
\tilde{h}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{f(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f}-\frac{\tilde{h}(x-\frac{1}{2}x_{0})}{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\right)}^{1/2}}\right|{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\varepsilon^{1/2},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{g(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g}-\frac{\tilde{h}(x+\frac{1}{2}x_{0})}{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\right)}^{1/2}}\right|{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

A change of variables on the inequality concerning
g
and an easy triangular inequelity give the desired bound.
∎

3.
Sharp Quantitative Stability for Brascamp-Lieb inequality

In this section we are focused on the quantitative stability of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality, as stated in Theorem
1.1
. Due to its importance to derive quantity stability results, and for the reader’s convenience, we recall how this can be obtained with the
carré du champ
method, which is based on the geometry of the following operator

Lu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\Delta u-\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u.

This method is based on the so called
\Gamma
-calculus, which revolves around the following commutator operators

\displaystyle\Gamma(f,g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\left(L(fg)-fLg-gLf\right)=\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f,g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\left(L\Gamma(f,g)-\Gamma(f,Lg)-\Gamma(g,Lf)\right)=D^{2}f\colon D^{2}g+\left\langle D^{2}\varphi\nabla f,\nabla g\right\rangle.

It is then a straight-forward computation to check the integration by parts formula

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma(f,g){\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle f,g\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fLg{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}gLf{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Inequality (
1.1
) holds, and we have equality if, and only if, there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

f(x)=a\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)+\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f.

Proof.

Given
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, set
\bar{f}=f-\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f
and let
u
be the unique weak solution of

Lu=\bar{f}.

From the classical regularity theory of uniformly elliptic equations,
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. So we can use the
\Gamma
calculus tools to write the variance of
f
as

\displaystyle\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{f}^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{f}Lu{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1/2}\nabla f,{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{1/2}\nabla u\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1/2}\nabla f\right\rVert^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{1/2}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{1/2}\nabla u\right\rVert^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle={\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{1/2}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{D^{2}\varphi}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{1/2}.

Now we notice that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma_{2}(u){\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla Lu\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla Lu\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

since the integrand of the first integral is a divergence. In addition, by definition of
\Gamma_{2}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{D^{2}\varphi}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma_{2}(u){\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert^{2}_{F}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla Lu\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert^{2}_{F}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert^{2}_{F}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}.

Plugging it back into the original estimations of
\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f)
, we notice that

\displaystyle\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{1/2}{\left(\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert^{2}_{F}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{1/2}

which gives inequality (
1.1
). This proof is also enlightening on the equality cases; indeed notice that the chain of inequalities are all equalities, except for the application of a Cauchy-Scharwz and the non-positive term on
-\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
.

As a result, we have equality if, and only if,

D^{2}u\equiv 0\text{ and }{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1/2}\nabla f\parallel{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{1/2}\nabla u.

The first condition implies that
u=a\cdot x+b
for constant
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
u
is a classical solution of the equation
Lu=\bar{f}
, it follows that, to have equality
f
must satisfy

f=a\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)+\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f.

The result follows.
∎

With the knowledge of the equality case for (
1.1
), we now aim at a quantitative stability version of this inequality in terms of the
L^{1}
distance to the optimal set (see Remark
2.6
), as highlighted in Theorem
1.1
. We state that result below once more for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be an essentially continuous and convex function, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi}<+\infty
so that the Gibbs mesuare
\varrho_{\varphi}
is well defined as in (
1.4
).

Then, there exists a universal constant
C_{d}
depending only on the ambient dimension such that

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}})\leq C_{d}{\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)}^{1/2}

for all locally Lipschitz functions
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
.

Our proof of Theorem
3.2
will be based, as mentioned in the introduction, on the linealization argument from Bobkov-Ledoux
[
7
]
, while employing the main results from
[
32
]
in order to control the deficit, and carefully using the geometry induced by the potential in order to control the several stability parameters involved.

Proof.

We first prove the result of the case that
\varphi
is strongly convex and essentially continuous function. That is, we assume that there exists
\alpha>0
such that
D^{2}\varphi\geq\alpha{\mathop{\rm id}}
, where the gradients and hessians
\nabla\varphi,D^{2}\varphi
are understood in the sense of Alexandrov (see for instance
[
26
, Chapter 6]
). Therefore
\mathscr{L}^{d}
and
\varrho_{\varphi}
are a.e. well defined.

Given
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
, it can be arbitrarily approximated in
L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
by a sequence of smooth functions, so for the moment we assume
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As a result, from the
\alpha
-strong convexity of
\varphi
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
the function
2\delta f-\varphi
is concave.

Our goal is to apply the quantitative stability version of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality with convexity parameter
s=1/2
. In order to do so, we consider the functions

u_{\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}e^{2\delta f-\varphi},\quad v_{\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}e^{-\varphi},\quad w_{\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}e^{f_{\delta}-\varphi},

where
f_{\delta}(z)
is pointwise defined as

\displaystyle f_{\delta}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm sup}_{z=\frac{x+y}{2}}\delta f(x)-\left[\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y)-\varphi\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm sup}_{h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta f(z+h)-\left[\frac{1}{2}\varphi(z+h)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi(z-h)-\varphi(z)\right].

It is not too hard to check that the triple of functions
u_{\delta},v_{\delta},w_{\delta}
satisfies the condition in Prékopa–Leindler’s inequality. Hence, as the total mass of
v_{\delta}
is
1
by assumption, the standard Prékopa-Leindler inequality holds and we can define the non-negative quantity
\varepsilon_{\delta}
such that

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w_{\delta}{\rm d}x=(1+\varepsilon_{\delta}){\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2},\quad\varepsilon_{\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w_{\delta}{\rm d}x-{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}}{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}}.

Analogously as in Bobkov-Ledoux
[
7
]
, our argument to obtain the sharp quantity version of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality will consist of studying the dependence on
\delta
of the above quantities. Starting with the integral of
u_{\delta}
, we get the following Taylor expansion on
\delta
:

(3.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x=1+2\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+2\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+o(\delta^{2}).

For the expansion on the integral of
w_{\delta}
, recall the classical result from log-concave measures that

\text{ if }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi}<+\infty,\text{ then $\varphi$ is coercive.}

Therefore, the quantity being maximized in the definition of
f_{\delta}
is strongly concave and goes to
-\infty
as
h\to+\infty
. Hence for
\varrho_{\varphi}
-a.e.
z
let
h_{\delta}=h_{\delta}(z)
be optimal for the supremum in
f_{\delta}(z)
. The optimality conditions for
h_{\delta}
give, for
\varrho_{\varphi}
-a.e. point where the gradients and hessian of
\varphi
are uniquely defined, that

\displaystyle\delta\nabla f(z+h_{\delta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\nabla\varphi(z+h_{\delta})-\nabla\varphi(z-h_{\delta})\right]=D^{2}\varphi(z)h_{\delta}+o(\delta).

Since
D^{2}\varphi\geq\alpha{\mathop{\rm id}}
, the hessian is invertible and
{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\leq\alpha^{-1}{\mathop{\rm id}}
, and developing the Taylor expansion for
\nabla f
in the identity above we obtain that

h_{\delta}=\delta{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\nabla f(z)+o(\delta).

In particular
h_{\delta}=O(\delta)
, uniformly in
z
. As a result, we can expand the value of
f_{\delta}(z)
as

f_{\delta}(z)=\delta f(z)+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle+o(\delta^{2}).

Therefore, with a similar argument to the expansion of the integral of
u_{\delta}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w_{\delta}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{f_{\delta}}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=1+\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+o(\delta^{2}).

In addition, using the Taylor expansion
{(1+s)}^{1/2}=1+\frac{1}{2}s-\frac{1}{8}s^{2}+o(\delta^{2})
and the expansion for the integral of
u_{\delta}
we obtain that

{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}=1+\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}\right)}^{2}+o(\delta^{2}).

Joining these estimates, we obtain the following expansion for
\varepsilon_{\delta}
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\delta}

\displaystyle={\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x\right)}^{-1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w_{\delta}{\rm d}x-{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+O(\delta))\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{(D^{2}\varphi)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}-\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f)+\frac{o(\delta^{2})}{\delta^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=(1+O(\delta))\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\left[\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)+\frac{o(\delta^{2})}{\delta^{2}}\right]

From the quantitative stability of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality we obtain that there exists a universal constant
C_{d}
, depending only on the dimension and on the convexity parameter which is fixed
s=1/2
, and some vector
x_{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{u_{\delta}(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}}-e^{-\varphi(x+x_{\delta})}\right|{\rm d}x\leq C_{d}\varepsilon_{\delta}^{1/2}.

Taking into account the scaling of
\varepsilon_{\delta}
, which behaves as
\delta^{2}
, we see that the RHS of the above inequality is of the order
\delta
. Developing the LHS depends strongly on the behavior of
x_{\delta}
when
\delta\to 0
. Clearly it must hold that
x_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
, as a result we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\left|\frac{u_{\delta}(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}}-e^{-\varphi(x+x_{\delta})}\right|{\rm d}x={\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}\right)}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|e^{2\delta f}-e^{-(\varphi(x+x_{\delta})-\varphi(x))}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\delta}\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=(1+O(\delta))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|1+2\delta f+o(\delta)-(1+\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot x_{\delta}+o(x_{\delta}))\left(1+2\delta\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f+o(\delta)\right)\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=(1+O(\delta))2\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f-\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot\frac{x_{\delta}}{2\delta}-\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+o(\delta+x_{\delta}).

Therefore, in order to obtain a meaningful estimate in terms of
{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}})
, we must show that
x_{\delta}/2\delta
has a cluster point as
\delta\to 0^{+}
. For this, define the sets

\Omega_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\left\{x\in\mathop{{\rm dom}}\varphi:D^{2}\varphi(x)\leq n{\mathop{\rm id}}\right\}.

From the convexity of
\varphi
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose
\delta
sufficiently small so that
\varphi(x+x_{\delta})-\varphi(x)=O(x_{\delta})
. More precisely, there is
\delta_{n}
such that for
\delta\leq\delta_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert v_{\delta}-v_{\delta}(\cdot+x_{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert v_{\delta}-v_{\delta}(\cdot+x_{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left|x_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D^{2}\varphi(x)x_{\delta},x_{\delta}\right\rangle+o(x_{\delta}^{2})\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|x_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}.

Using a suitable triangle inequality, we get for
\delta\leq\delta_{n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|x_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v_{\delta}-v_{\delta}(\cdot+x_{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v_{\delta}-u_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\lVert u_{\delta}-v_{\delta}(\cdot+x_{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\delta,

where the bound
\left\lVert v_{\delta}-u_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=O(\delta)
follows directly from the definition of
u_{\delta}
as a perturbation of
v_{\delta}
and
\left\lVert u_{\delta}-v_{\delta}(\cdot+x_{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=O(\delta)
is a consequence of the previous estimations on the deficit of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality. But then we conclude that the quantity

\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left|\frac{x_{\delta}}{2\delta}\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

must remain uniformly bounded for
\delta\leq\delta_{n}
. Now, we wish to exploit the fact that, since
\varphi
is essentially continuous, its moment measure
\mu_{\varphi}
has full-dimensional support, as highlighted in Lemma
2.3
. Since
x_{\delta}/\left\lVert x_{\delta}\right\rVert\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we can find a subsequence of
\delta_{n}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0
such that
x_{\delta_{n}}/\left\lVert x_{\delta_{n}}\right\rVert\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\bar{\theta}
, for some
\bar{\theta}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. But then using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}
converges strongly in
L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})
to
1
, we get that

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left|\frac{x_{\delta_{n}}}{2{\delta_{n}}}\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert x_{\delta_{n}}\right\rVert}{2{\delta_{n}}}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left|\frac{x_{\delta_{n}}}{\left\lVert x_{\delta_{n}}\right\rVert}\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bar{\theta}\cdot y|{\rm d}\mu_{\varphi}(y)\right)\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert x_{\delta_{n}}\right\rVert}{2{\delta_{n}}}

Since the moment measure
\mu_{\varphi}
has full-dimensional support,
x_{\delta_{n}}/\delta_{n}
remains bounded as
n\to\infty
.

Therefore, we can extract a subsequence
\delta_{n}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0
such that
\frac{x_{\delta_{n}}}{2\delta_{n}}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}a
, for some vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Coming back to the estimate on
\varepsilon_{\delta}
from (
3.4
), dividing both sides by
\delta
and passing to the limit of these estimates as
\delta_{n}\to 0
we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL}})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left(f-\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot a-\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f\right)\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}(1+O(\delta_{n}))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left(f-\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot\frac{x_{\delta_{n}}}{2\delta_{n}}-\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f\right)\right|{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi}+o(\delta_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}C_{d}{\left(\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)+\frac{o(\delta_{n}^{2})}{\delta_{n}^{2}}\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=C_{d}{\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f)}^{1/2},

and the result follows when
\varphi
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})\cap\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By approximation, it follows also for all
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
.

For an arbitrary
\varphi
convex and essentially continuous, we finish the proof with an approximation argument. In order to make it clearer, we modify the notation used previously in order for it to emphasize the dependence on the potential
\varphi
. Hence, we shall write

\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f;\varphi)\text{ and }{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL},\varphi}),

in order to denote the deficit of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality and the distance to optimal functions with respect to the potential
\varphi
, respectively. Our strategy is to add a little quadratic regularization by defining
\varphi_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\cdot|^{2}
. The result will follow if we prove that the deficit if upper semi-continuous

(3.5)

\limsup_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f;\varphi_{\alpha})\leq\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f;\varphi),

and that the distance to optimal functions is lower semi-continuous

(3.6)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL},\varphi})\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL},\varphi_{\alpha}}).

For the first identity (
3.5
), first notice that the Gibbs measures
\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}}
converge pointwise, even uniformly, to
\varrho_{\varphi}
as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
. Then, for any
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
, convergence of the variance follows from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem:

\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}}}(f)\xrightarrow[\alpha\to 0^{+}]{}\Var_{\varrho_{\varphi}}(f).

For the positive term in the deficit, notice that
D^{2}\varphi_{\alpha}=D^{2}\varphi+\alpha{\mathop{\rm id}}\geq D^{2}\varphi
, hence
D^{2}\varphi_{\alpha}
is invertible and it follows that
{\left(D^{2}\varphi_{\alpha}\right)}^{-1}\leq{\left(D^{2}\varphi\right)}^{-1}
in the partial ordering of positive semi-definite matrices, even if
D^{2}\varphi
becomes singular. As a result, assuming that
\delta_{\textrm{BL}}(f;\varphi)<+\infty
, we have for all
\alpha>0
and
f\in L^{2}(\varrho_{\varphi})
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{\left(D^{2}\varphi_{\alpha}\right)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{\left(D^{2}\varphi\right)}^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle{\rm d}\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}}

and passing to the limit as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
yields (
3.5
).

Moving on the sencond identity (
3.6
), notice that the finite dimensional function

(a,b)\mapsto\left\lVert f-(a\cdot\nabla\varphi_{\alpha}+b)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}})}

is convex, due to the triangle inequality, and coercive. So the infimum is attained at some
(a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha})
and, in particular, we know that it must be the case that
b_{\alpha}=\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}}}f\xrightarrow[\alpha\to 0^{+}]{}\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{\varphi}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\bar{b}
. In addition, the optimal
a_{\alpha}
must be all contained in the same compact ball since
{\left(\varrho_{\varphi_{\alpha}}\right)}_{\alpha>0}
have equibounded moments. So we can assume w.l.o.g. that
a_{\alpha}\xrightarrow[\alpha\to 0^{+}]{}\bar{a}
. It then follows from Fatou’s Lemma that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL},\varphi})

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f-(\bar{a}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\bar{b})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\varrho_{\varphi})}(f,\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BL},\varphi_{\alpha}}).

The result follows.
∎

4.
Quantitative Stability of Moment Measures

In this section we use the previous machinery to obtain sharp quantitative stability results for moment measures. We start by proving Theorem
1.2
. As usual, we shall state it again below for the reader’s convenience:

Theorem 4.1
.

Given a Radon measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose barycenter lies that the origin and
\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\mu=d
, let
\bar{\varphi}
be a maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
. Then for any convex function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
it holds that

(4.1)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\varrho_{\varphi^{*}},\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d}{\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)}^{1/2}.

In addition, there exists
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
such that, setting
v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\bar{\varphi}-\varphi
and
\varphi_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\bar{\varphi}+\lambda v
, it holds that

(4.2)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mu_{\lambda})}\left(\varphi,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)^{1/2},

where
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{*}}
is the moment measure associated with the interpolation
(\varphi_{\lambda})^{*}
.

Proof.

To prove the first estimate (
4.1
), we let
\bar{\varphi}
be a maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
, while
\varphi
is a general convex function. We then employ the quantitative version of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality with the functions

f=e^{-\bar{\varphi}^{*}},\quad g=e^{-\varphi^{*}}\text{ and }h=e^{-\varphi_{1/2}^{*}}\text{ with }\varphi_{1/2}=\frac{\bar{\varphi}+\varphi}{2}.

Therefore
f,g,h
satisfy the Prékopa condition with convexity parameter
s=1/2
. In addition, due to concavity of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
and the optimality of
\bar{\varphi}
we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1/2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})+\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})+\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varepsilon.

The quantity
\delta
can be precisely related to the deficit of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality. Indeed:

\displaystyle\delta=

\displaystyle\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi_{1/2}^{*}}{\rm d}x\right)-\log{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\bar{\varphi}^{*}}{\rm d}x\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi^{*}}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\underbrace{\varphi_{1/2}-\frac{1}{2}(\bar{\varphi}+\varphi)}_{=0}){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi_{1/2}^{*}}{\rm d}x}{\displaystyle{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\bar{\varphi}^{*}}{\rm d}x\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi^{*}}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Now, notice that since
\varrho_{\bar{\varphi}^{*}}
and all elements in
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
are probability measures, their distance in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is at most
2
. As a result, we can assume that
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)<1
otherwise the inequality is trivial. From the previous estimates we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi_{1/2}^{*}}{\rm d}x<(1+\varepsilon){\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\bar{\varphi}^{*}}{\rm d}x\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi^{*}}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/2}.

Then, the quantitative version of Prékopa-Leindler inequality in the form of Lemma
2.7
implies that there exists a universal constant
C_{d}
depending only on the dimension and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\varrho_{\bar{\varphi}^{*}}(x)-\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(x+x_{0})\right|{\rm d}x\leq C_{d}{\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)}^{1/2}.

Estimate (
4.1
) follows.

For the second estimate recall the interpolation
\varphi_{t}=\bar{\varphi}+tv
, so that
\varphi_{0}=\bar{\varphi}
, and define the one-dimensional function

t\mapsto J(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{t}).

Hence a second order Taylor expansion and the characterization of the second variation of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
from Lemma
2.5
we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})

\displaystyle=J(1)-J(0)=J^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)J^{\prime\prime}(t){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\langle\nabla\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi}),v\right\rangle}_{=0}+\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{t}){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\delta_{\textrm{BL}}\left(v(\nabla\varphi^{*}_{t}),\varrho_{\varphi_{t}^{*}}\right){\rm d}t,

where we have used the fact that if
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
then the moment measure
\mu_{\varphi^{*}}
coincides with
\mu
so that the first variation vanishes. In the sequel, using the quantitative stability version of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality provided by Theorem
3.2
, there exists a dimensional constant
C_{d}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\mathop{\rm inf}_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{d},b\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lVert(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})-(a\cdot x+b)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{1}(\mu_{\varphi^{*}_{t}})}{\rm d}t\leq C_{d}^{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right).

In particular, there must exist some
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
such that the integrand above evaluated at the interpolation
\mu_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{*}}
yields

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm inf}_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{d},b\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lVert(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})-(a\cdot x+b)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{1}(\mu_{\varphi^{*}_{\lambda}})}\leq 2C_{d}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)^{1/2}.

This finishes our proof.
∎

4.1.
Stability in a compact domain

The simplest case of stability is when both
\varrho
and
\mu
are restricted to be supported on a convex and compact domain
\Omega
, that is
\varrho,\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
. The attentive reader might have hesitated if this is even possible without contradicting the uniqueness result of Cordero-Eurasquin and Klartag for moment measure representations. Here the question is to find
\psi\in\mathscr{C}(\Omega)
such that

(4.3)

\mu={(\nabla\psi)}_{\sharp}e^{-\psi}\mathscr{L}^{d}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Omega.

The existence question follows with simplified versions of the arguments from
[
19
,
50
]
by considering the functionals

\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\Omega}(\varrho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\int_{\Omega}\log\varrho{\rm d}\varrho+\mathcal{T}(\varrho,\mu),\quad\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\Omega}(\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\log\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi^{*}}{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi{\rm d}\mu.

Uniqueness then follows, now without modulo translations, due to the strict geodesic convexity of the entropy since
\Omega
is convex.

One could embed
\mu
in
\mathscr{P}(\Omega^{\prime})
for any
\Omega^{\prime}
containing its support and obtain another moment measure representation. What is happening here? For any representation in a bounded domain the potential
\psi_{\Omega}
is necessarily Lipschitz continuous, for instance due to
\nabla\psi_{\Omega}
being the Brenier map with the compactly supported target
\mu
, and hence bounded in
\Omega
. Therefore, if one wishes to use
\psi_{\Omega}
for a moment measure representation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we must extend it with
+\infty
outside of
\Omega
so that
e^{-\varphi_{\Omega}^{*}}
remains a probability measure. But this means that this extention is not continuous in all of
\partial\Omega
, and hence it is not essentially continuous. Therefore have no contradiction with the results of
[
19
]
, since their result states uniqueness up to translations of a moment measure representation with an essentially continuous potential.

From one hand, this case is much less interesting from a strictly mathematical point of view, since the compactness of the domain makes many arguments easier. However, it is particularly relevant for numerical applications where one often restricts the problem to a bounded domain. Furthermore, in this case we obtain stronger stability results as a direct corollary of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex and compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given two Radon measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
whose barycenters lie at the origin and
\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\mu=\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\nu=d
, let
\varphi_{\mu},\varphi_{\nu}
be maximizers of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\Omega},\mathscr{J}_{\nu,\Omega}
respectively. Then, there exists a universal constant
C_{d,\Omega}
depending only on the dimension and on the diameter of
\Omega
such that

(4.4)

\mathop{\rm inf}_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert\varrho_{\varphi_{\mu}^{*}}(\cdot)-\varrho_{\varphi_{\nu}^{*}}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,\Omega}{W_{1}(\mu,\nu)}^{1/2}

and

(4.5)

\mathop{\rm inf}_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ b\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lVert(\varphi_{\mu}-\varphi_{\nu})-(a\cdot x+b)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mu_{\lambda})}\leq C_{d,\Omega}{W_{1}(\mu,\nu)}^{1/2},

where
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{\psi_{\lambda}}
is the moment measure of
\psi_{\lambda}=((1-\lambda)\varphi_{\mu}+\lambda\varphi_{\nu})^{*}
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Consider
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
, and consider two convex functions
\varphi_{\mu},\varphi_{\nu}
such that

\varphi_{\mu}\in\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\mu},\quad\varphi_{\nu}\in\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\nu}.

Since
\varphi_{\mu},\varphi_{\nu}
are optimal Kantorovitch potentials for the
L^{2}
-optimal transportation problem in a compact domain, they are both Lipschitz with constant
\mathop{\rm diam}\Omega
. Then by optimality of
\varphi_{\mu}
, for the maximization of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
, and concavity of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
we have that

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{\mu})-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{\nu}),\varphi_{\mu}-\varphi_{\nu}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nu-\mu,\varphi_{\mu}-\varphi_{\nu}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\rm Lip}(\varphi_{\mu}-\varphi_{\nu})W_{1}(\mu,\nu),

which can then be easily combined with Lemma
4.1
to obtain bounds on the distance between the sets
\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\mu},\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\nu}
, and between
\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\mu},\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\nu}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

A similar argument can be employed to estimate the distance between the sets
\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha},\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\nu,\alpha}
in the
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
topology. Indeed, for all
\alpha\geq 0
the functional
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
remains concave, with an application of the classical Prékopa-Leindler inequality. Hence taking
\varphi_{\mu,\alpha},\varphi_{\nu,\alpha}
such that

\varphi_{\mu,\alpha}\in\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha},\quad\varphi_{\nu,\alpha}\in\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\nu,\alpha},

these are also Lipschitz continuous, and we have the same bound

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\mu,\alpha})-\mathscr{J}_{\nu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\nu,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\nu,\alpha}),\varphi_{\mu}-\varphi_{\nu}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nu-\mu,\varphi_{\mu,\alpha}-\varphi_{\nu,\alpha}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\rm diam}\Omega W_{1}(\mu,\nu).

Which can also be used as in Lemma
4.1
to bound the distance
\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha},\mathop{\rm argmin}\mathscr{E}_{\nu,\alpha}
, but not of
\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha},\mathop{\rm argmax}\mathscr{J}_{\nu,\alpha}
, since for
\alpha>0
the argument via quantitative estability of Brascamp-Lieb does not apply.

4.2.
Convergence rates w.r.t. regularization parameter

We are also able to prove results on the sharp rates of convergence of regularized moment measures introduced in
[
21
]
to the classical ones from
[
19
]
.

Recall that for each
\alpha>0
, the regularization present in the energies
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
and
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
induces a unique minimizer/maximizer pair
(\varrho_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})
associated with the moment regularized measure representation of
\mu
. Therefore, it makes sense to study the convergence of these pairs as
\alpha\to 0
by estimating the distance of
\varrho_{\alpha}
to
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
and of
\varphi_{\alpha}
to
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Fix a Radon measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose barycenter lies at the origin and such that
\dim\mathop{\rm supp}\mu=d
. Then there exists a constant
C_{d,\mu}
depending on the ambient dimension and on
\mu
such that

(4.6)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\varrho_{\alpha},\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d,\mu}\alpha^{1/2},

where the Gibbs probability measures

\varrho_{\alpha}\propto e^{-(\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})},

give the regularized moment measure representation for
\mu
.

In addition, let
{\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\right)}_{\alpha>0}
be the family of uniquer maximizers of
{\left(\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}\right)}_{\alpha>0}
. Then, for each
\alpha
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
such that, setting
v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi-\varphi_{\alpha}
and
\varphi_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varphi+\lambda v
, it holds that

(4.7)

{\rm dist}_{L^{1}(\mu_{\lambda})}\left(\varphi_{\alpha},\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\right)\leq C_{d,\mu}\alpha^{1/2},

where
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{*}}
is the moment measure associated with the interpolation
(\varphi_{\lambda})^{*}
.

Example 4.5
.

Set
S_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda x
, and consider the scallings

\mu_{\lambda}={(S_{\lambda})}_{\sharp}\mu,\text{ and }\varrho_{\lambda}=e^{\psi_{\lambda}}\text{ with }{(\varphi_{\lambda})}_{\sharp}\varrho_{\lambda}=\mu.

Then one can check that
\varphi_{\lambda}(\cdot)=\lambda^{d}\varphi(\lambda\cdot)
, where
\varphi
is the moment map. As a result we have that

M_{2}(\mu_{\lambda})=\lambda^{2}M_{2}(\mu),\quad M_{2}(\varrho_{\lambda})=\lambda^{-2}M_{2}(\varrho),

so it is perfectly possible that
M_{2}(\varrho)
explodes while
M_{2}(\mu)
remains bounded.

For the purposes of Theorem
4.4
, the only important fact is that
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu})
is finite, which is always the case for log-concave measures, but in the next section we identify conditions on
\mu
which ensure that
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu})
can be controlled uniformly.

As we will see in the proof below, the constant
C_{d,\mu}=C_{d}M_{2}(\varrho)
, where
\varrho=\varrho_{\mu}
, the Gibbs measure associated with the moment measure representation of
\mu
. Therefore, the constant depends on
\mu
only via the second moment of
\varrho_{\mu}
, which is finite since
\varrho_{\mu}
is log-concave, but cannot be controlled in general only by bounding the second moment of
\mu
as the following example shows.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

The goal is to compare the
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
energies of
\varphi_{\alpha}
, the unique maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
, and the maximizer
\varphi
of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
such that
\varrho=e^{-\varphi^{*}}
is a centered probability measure.

From Theorem
2.4
, we know that the unique minimizer of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
can be obtained from
\varphi_{\alpha}
as

\varrho_{\alpha}\propto e^{-(\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})},

and similarly for the case
\alpha=0
, we can map
\varphi
into a minimizer of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu}
defined as the Gibbs probability measure

\varrho\propto e^{-\varphi^{*}}.

To compare the
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
energies, we start by recalling the regularized energy
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
and noticing the following trivial bound

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\alpha}{\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}}{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\alpha}{\rm d}\mu=\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{\alpha}).

Therefore we have that

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)-\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(\varphi)-\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\alpha})-\mathscr{E}_{\mu}(\varrho)

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho)-\mathscr{E}_{\mu}(\varrho)=\alpha M_{2}(\varrho)=C_{\mu}\alpha,

where the first equality comes from the strong duality formula between the minimization of
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
and the maximization of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu,\alpha}
proved in Theorem
2.4
. Since
\varrho
is a log-concave measure, its second moment is finite and hence a constant
C_{\mu}
depending only on
\mu
.

Combining this estimate with Lemma
4.1
, the result follows.
∎

4.3.
Stability over
\mathbb{R}^{d}

The stability result in a compact setting proven in Section
4.1
covers most cases of applications, since for computational purposes one usually needs to truncate the measures to a compact domain. Although the restriction of
\mu
to a compact domain is not problematic due to many interesting cases already being in this setting, the Gibbs measure
\varrho_{\mu}
is in general supported over the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore, it is interesting to obtain stability results also for moment measure representations over the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

As mentionned above, this was achieved for regularized moment measures in
[
21
]
, using the strong geodesic convexity of the functional
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
. The convergence results from Section
4.2
depending on
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu})
give a hint on why the stability for regularized moment measures appears to be a more direct issue, as in this case one can explect to control the second moment of the Gibbs measure
\varrho_{\mu,\alpha}
independently of
\mu
, thanks to the regularization term. Without such a uniform control, we cannot know if the constant multiplying any quantitative stability inequality will remain bounded. Example
4.5
above shows that we cannot in general bound
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu})
in terms of
M_{2}(\mu)
.

In this section, we identify two very natural classes of measures
\mu
, for which
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu})
remains uniformly bounded. We can then leverage the stability proof via geodesic convexity from
[
22
]
and the convergence rates in Theorem
4.4
to obtain quantitative stability results for moment measure representations over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in terms of the
W_{2}
-distance.

The first approach is to impose a lower bound on the quantity

(4.8)

\Theta(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathop{\rm inf}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y\cdot\theta|{\rm d}\mu(y),

that measures the minimal spread of the measure
\mu
in all directions, beging therefore very natural for the study of moment measures. We shall also require that
\mu
is supported on a convex and compact set
\Omega
, so that we can control the Lipschitz constant of the potential
\varphi_{\mu}^{*}
. Therefore, we consider the class of measures defined as

(4.9)

\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\left\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega):\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\text{ is centered, }\\
\displaystyle\Theta(\mu)\geq\vartheta\end{array}\right\}.

The fact that
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
implies that the potential
\varphi_{\mu}^{*}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L=\mathop{\rm diam}(\Omega)
. This is not sufficient to control
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu})
, but enforcing the lower bound on
\Theta(\mu)
we have

(4.10)

\Theta(\mu)=\mathop{\rm inf}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\varphi_{\mu}^{*}(x)\cdot\theta|e^{-\varphi_{\mu}^{*}(x)}{\rm d}x\geq\vartheta.

Combining this with the Lipschitz continuity of
\varphi_{\mu}^{*}
, we can employ the following growth estimate for convex functions due to Klartag
[
38
]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

[
38
, Lemma 2]

Suppose that
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
L
-Lipschitz, convex function such that
\varrho\propto e^{-\psi}
has barycenter at the origin. If there exists
c>0
such that

\mathop{\rm inf}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\theta|e^{-\psi(x)}{\rm d}x\geq c,

then there exists
\ell,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
depending only on
d,L,c
such that

\ell|x|-\beta_{1}\leq\psi(x)\leq L|x|+\beta_{2},\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular, the second moment of
\varrho
is bounded by a constant depending only on
d,L,c
.

We can now state our quantitative stability result for measures in
\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Given
\Omega
a convex and compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\vartheta>0
, then for every
p>2
there exists a constant
C
such thats for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}

(4.11)

{\rm dist}_{W_{2}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mu},\mathcal{M}_{\nu}\right)\leq C{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}^{\frac{p-2}{6p-4}},

where
C
depends only on the
d
,
\mathop{\rm diam}\Omega
,
\vartheta
, and grows linearly in
p
.

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

The proof consists of combining the convergence rates of regularized moment measures from Theorem
4.4
with strong geodesic convexity argument from
[
22
]
and the uniform bounds on the second moment that we obtain for
\varrho_{\mu}
whenever
\mu\in\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
through Lemma
4.6
.

First, notice that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
and
\alpha\geq 0
, letting
\varrho_{\mu,\alpha}
denote the Gibbs measure associated with the (regularized if
\alpha>0
) moment measure representation of
\mu
, we have that
M_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha})\leq C_{\Omega,\vartheta,d}
, where the constant depends only on
\Omega,c
and
d
. This is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.6
, since the potentials
\varphi_{\mu,\alpha}^{*}
are all
\mathop{\rm diam}(\Omega)
-Lipschitz continuous.

For the reader’s convenience, we detail the part of
[
21
]
’s strong geodesic convexity argument that is relevant to us. Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
and some
\alpha>0
, let
{\left(\varrho_{\alpha,t}\right)}_{t\in[0,1]}
be the constant speed geodesic with endpoints

\varrho_{\alpha,0}=\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\quad\varrho_{\alpha,1}=\varrho_{\nu,\alpha}.

Since both
\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}
and
\mathscr{E}_{\nu,\alpha}
are
\alpha
-strongly geodesically convex functionals over
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha})\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\alpha,1/2})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})-\frac{\alpha}{8}W_{2}^{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu,\alpha}),

and similarly for
\mathscr{E}_{\nu,\alpha}
. Rearranging we get

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}W_{2}^{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})-\mathscr{E}_{\mu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha}),

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}W_{2}^{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{E}_{\nu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha})-\mathscr{E}_{\nu,\alpha}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha}).

Adding these inequalities, and recalling that
\mathcal{T}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=-W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})+M_{2}(\mu_{0})+M_{2}(\mu_{1})
and
W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\leq 2M_{2}(\mu_{0})+2M_{2}(\mu_{1})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}W_{2}^{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\mu)-\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\nu)+\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\nu)-\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\mu)+W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\nu))(W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\mu)-W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\nu))

\displaystyle+(W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\nu)+W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\mu))(W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\nu)-W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\mu))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\mu)+W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\nu)+W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\nu)+W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\mu))W_{2}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\Omega,\vartheta,d}W_{2}(\mu,\nu),

where we have used that for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
, all second moments above are uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on
\Omega,\vartheta
and
d
. In particular, this gives an explicit behaviour on
\alpha
for the bound proven in
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
for the particular case of
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{K}_{\vartheta}
, i.e.

(4.12)

W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})\leq{\alpha}^{-1/2}C_{\Omega,\vartheta,d}{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}^{1/2}.

To finish the proof we need to exploit the explicit rates of convergence of regularized moment measures from Theorem
4.4
. The issue is that these convergence rates concern the
L^{1}
distance between
\varrho_{\mu,\alpha}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
. But for absolutely continuous measures with bounded
p
-moments, the
L^{1}
norm can be used to control Wasserstein distances. There exists a constant
C_{p,q}
such that

(4.13)

W_{q}(\varrho_{0},\varrho_{1})\leq C_{p,q}{(M_{p}(\varrho_{0})+M_{p}(\varrho_{1}))}^{1/p}\left\lVert\varrho_{0}-\varrho_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{1/q-1/p},

see Lemma
2.1
in Section
2
.

As a result, combining (
4.13
), with the stability of regularized moment measures from (
4.12
), and the explicit rates of convegence from
4.4
, we let
\varrho_{\mu}=\varrho_{\mu}(\cdot+x_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
be a Gibbs measure associated with the moment measure representation of
\mu
such that

\left\lVert\varrho_{\mu,\alpha}-\varrho_{\mu}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2{\rm dist}_{L^{1}}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\mathcal{M}_{\mu}),\leq C_{\Omega,d,\vartheta}\alpha^{1/2},

and similarly for
\varrho_{\nu}
. Then, for every
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mu},\mathcal{M}_{\nu})\leq W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu},\varrho_{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu},\varrho_{\mu,\alpha})+W_{2}(\varrho_{\mu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu,\alpha})+W_{2}(\varrho_{\nu,\alpha},\varrho_{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\alpha^{1/4-1/2p}+\alpha^{-1/2}{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}^{1/2}\right),

where the constant
C
above depends on
\Omega,\vartheta,d,p
, more specifically it depends on
M_{p}(\varrho_{\mu})^{1/p}
, which behaves linearly in
p
, so that the constant explodes as
p\to\infty
.

But for every finite
p>2
, we can optimize the above estimate in
\alpha
to obtain the best possible bound, which is achieved for

\alpha={W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}^{\frac{2p}{3p-2}},

which gives

{\rm dist}_{W_{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mu},\mathcal{M}_{\nu})\leq C{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}^{\frac{p-2}{6p-4}},

for all
p>2
, with a constant

depending on
\Omega,\vartheta,d,p
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

The exponent
\frac{p-2}{6p-4}
in Theorem
4.7
can be made arbitrarily close to
1/6
by taking
p
large enough, at the cost of getting a worse constant
C
, which behaves linearly at
p
. It is somewhat natural to expect a worse exponent that
1/2
obtained in
[
21
]
for regularized moment measures, since the regularization term improves the second moment of solutions. However, it is not clear whether the limiting exponent
1/6
is optimal or not.

The second class of measures for which we can derive quantitative stability results is obtained by bounding the curvature they induce. Given
\Lambda>0
we define

(4.14)

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\left\{\mu\in\Pac(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\mu\propto e^{-V},\text{ where }D^{2}V\leq\Lambda{\mathop{\rm id}}\right\}.

In (
4.14
), the condition
D^{2}V\leq\Lambda{\mathop{\rm id}}
is understood only on
\mathop{{\rm dom}}V
and we do not make any convexity assumption. In this case that
\mu
has bounded curvature, we can expect that the Gibbs measure
\varrho_{\mu}
has a stronger geometry than the log-concavity of the orginal measure. Indeed we shall show that the optimal potential
\varphi_{\mu}^{*}
that yields the moment measure representation of
\mu
is
\Lambda^{-1/3}
-strongly convex.

This improved strong-convexity result will be obtained by bootstraping the suplementary regularity enjoyed by the regularized moment measure representation in conjunction with
Caffarelli’s contraction thereom
, a global Lipschitz regularity result for the optimal transport between log-concave measures. See for instance the original works of Caffarelli
[
14
,
15
]
, or
[
35
]
for a modern approach via entropic regularization and the references therein. For the reader’s convenience, we recall that it is stated as follows:

Theorem 4.10

(
[
14
,
15
]
Caffarelli’s contraction theorem)
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\Pac(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\mathop{{\rm dom}}\mu=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathop{{\rm dom}}\nu
is convex, and satisfying

(4.15)

\mu\propto e^{-V},\ \nu\propto e^{-W},\text{ where }D^{2}V\leq\Lambda{\mathop{\rm id}}\text{ and }D^{2}W\geq\lambda{\mathop{\rm id}},

where
\Lambda,\lambda>0
. Then the
L^{2}
-optimal transport map
T_{\sharp}\mu=\nu
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
\sqrt{\Lambda/\lambda}
.

With this result we prove the following regularity result for moment measures associated with elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
.

Theorem 4.11
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
, let
\varphi_{\mu}^{*}
be a potential inducing the moment measure representation
\mu={\left(\nabla\varphi_{\mu}^{*}\right)}_{\sharp}e^{-\varphi_{\mu}^{*}}
. Then
\varphi_{\mu}^{*}
is
\Lambda^{-1/3}
-convex.

Proof.

Fix some
\alpha>0
and consider the regularized moment measure representation of
\mu
as

\mu={(\nabla\varphi^{*}_{\alpha})}_{\sharp}e^{-(\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2})}={(\nabla\varphi^{*}_{\alpha})}_{\sharp}\varrho_{\alpha},

so that
\varrho_{\alpha}\propto e^{-V_{\alpha}}
where
D^{2}V_{\alpha}\geq\alpha{\mathop{\rm id}}
. In addition, from Brenier’s Theorem
\nabla\varphi_{\alpha}
is the
L^{2}
optimal transportation map from
\mu
to
\varrho_{\alpha}
.

From Caffarelli’s contraction theorem we have that

\mathop{\rm Lip}(\nabla\varphi_{\alpha})\leq\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{\alpha}},

which in turns implies further regularity for the Legendre transform
\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}
, being therefore
\sqrt{\alpha/\Lambda}
-strongly convex. This implies a better bound for strong convexity of
V_{\alpha}
and can be exploited once again in Caffarelli’s contraction theorem, implying that

\mathop{\rm Lip}(\nabla\varphi_{\alpha})\leq\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{\alpha/\Lambda}}}=\frac{\Lambda^{1/4}}{\alpha^{1/4}}.

This argument can be bootstrapped indefinably, and one can check by induction that at the
k
-th time it yields

\mathop{\rm Lip}(\nabla\varphi_{\alpha})\leq\frac{\Lambda^{S_{k}}}{\alpha^{1/2^{k}}}\text{ and }\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\text{ is $\frac{\alpha^{1/2^{k}}}{\Lambda^{S_{k}}}$--strongly convex,}

with
S_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{2^{i}}
. Passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
, we obtain that for all
\alpha>0

\mathop{\rm Lip}(\nabla\varphi_{\alpha})\leq\Lambda^{1/3}\text{ and }\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\text{ is $\Lambda^{-1/3}$--strongly convex.}

In particular, this implies that
\nabla\varphi_{\alpha}
converges locally uniformly to
\nabla\varphi_{\mu}
a maximizer of
\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
, so that
\varphi
is also
\Lambda^{1/3}
-Lipschitz, so that the optimal potential
\varphi^{*}
is
\Lambda^{-1/3}
–strongly convex, as we wanted to prove.
∎

In Lemma
4.6
we have exploited the lower bound on
\Theta(\mu)
aligned with the Lipschitz continuity of the moment map induced by

. This can even more easily be achieved for

by exploiting the strong convexity result from Theorem
4.11
and the following bound on the
p
-moments.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\psi
be a centered,
\lambda
-strongly convex function. Then for every
p>2
the
p
-moment of its associated Gibbs measure can be bounded as

{M_{p}(e^{-\psi})}^{1/p}={\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}e^{-\psi(x)}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/p}\leq Cp,

where
C
is a constant depending only on
\lambda
and the dimension
d
.

Proof.

Since the
\lambda
-strongly convex function
\psi
is centered, it is a known fact in convex analysis that:

(4.16)

\psi(0)\leq d+\mathop{\rm inf}\psi=d+\psi(x_{*})

where
x^{*}
denotes its minimizer. This is proven for instance in
[
33
]
. As a result of the
\lambda
-strong convexity of
\psi
, and the fact that
\nabla\psi(x_{*})=0
that

(4.17)

\left\langle\nabla\psi(x),x-x_{*}\right\rangle\geq\lambda|x-x_{*}|^{2}.

On the other hand, the divergence theorem gives that

(4.18)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathop{\rm div}}\left(|x-x_{*}|^{p-2}(x-x_{*})\right)e^{-\psi}{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-x_{*}|^{p-2}(x-x_{*})\cdot\nabla\psi(x)e^{-\psi}{\rm d}x.

Now define the following quantity

V(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-x_{*}|^{p}e^{-\psi(x)}{\rm d}x.

Combining identities (
4.17
), (
4.17
), and developing the divergence, we obtain that

V(p)\leq\frac{(d+p-2)}{\lambda}V(p-2).

Noticing that
V(0)=1
, by induction we obtain that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

V(2k)\leq\lambda^{-k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(d+2i)=\lambda^{-k}2^{k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+k\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)},

where
\Gamma
corresponds to the gamma function.

Using Hölder’s inequality, in the form
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p_{0}}^{(1-t)p_{0}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p_{1}}^{tp_{1}}
, for a convex combination
p=(1-t)p_{0}+tp_{1}
, we can interpolate the above inequality for any
p>2
, by choosing
p_{0}=2k
and
p_{1}=2(k+1)
for a convenient
k
such that
p\in(2k,2(k+1))
. Due to Stirling’s formula, this yields an estimate such that

{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-x_{*}|^{p}e^{-\psi(x)}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/p}\leq Cp,

for a constant
C
depending on
d
and
\lambda
.

To conclude, we need only to bound
|x_{*}|
, this can be done with (
4.16
) and the strongl convexity. Indeed, using once again that
\nabla\psi(x_{*})=0
, we have

\lambda|x-x_{*}|^{2}\leq\psi(0)-\psi(x_{*})\leq d.

Hence, combing all these estimates we get that

{M_{p}(e^{-\psi})}^{1/p}\leq{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-x_{*}|^{p}e^{-\psi(x)}{\rm d}x\right)}^{1/p}+|x_{*}|\leq Cp+\sqrt{\frac{d}{\lambda}},

and the result follows.
∎

Evaluation of the AEON Powered Stapler System in Primary Sleeve Gastrectomy: Results of a Prospective Clinical Trial
Authors: Antanas Mickevicius, Agne Sikarske, Mindaugas Kiudelis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeStaple-line complications such as bleeding and leaks remain clinically relevant in sleeve gastrectomy despite advances in surgical technique and device technology. This prospective trial was conducted to investigate the hypothesis that the AEON Powered Stapling System could improve consistency and stability of firing intraoperatively, which could lead to less tissue trauma, improved hemostasis, and fewer intra- and post-operative complications.Materials and MethodsThis was a prospective, single-center, single-arm study including 50 consecutive patients undergoing primary sleeve gastrectomy using the AEON Powered Stapling System. The primary endpoint was postoperative staple-line complications requiring intervention (bleeding or leak). Intraoperative hemostasis was assessed using a visual analogue scale (VAS). Postoperative outcomes and early quality-of-life (QOL) changes were evaluated.ResultsFifty patients (49 female; mean age 38.5±7.4 years; mean body mass index 35.8±3.7 kg/m2) underwent sleeve gastrectomy. Mean intraoperative VAS bleeding score was 1.04±0.19. No intraoperative bleeding requiring intervention occurred. There were no postoperative staple-line bleeds, leaks, reoperations, or device-related adverse events. Mean hemoglobin decrease was clinically insignificant, and no blood transfusions were required. Early postoperative pain scores were low and decreased progressively until discharge. In an exploratory analysis, improvement in five 36-Item Short Form Health Survey QOL domains was observed at 3 weeks.ConclusionThe AEON Powered Stapling System was safe and effective and demonstrated low intraoperative staple-line bleeding scores and a favorable short-term safety profile in primary sleeve gastrectomy. Because this was a single-arm study without a comparator group, larger comparative studies with longer follow-up are warranted.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06474637

INTRODUCTION

Obesity is a chronic disease which is increasing on a global level and is associated with significant morbidity, mortality, societal, and healthcare costs worldwide [123]. At the World Health Assembly in 2022, the World Health Organization (WHO) adopted new recommendations for the prevention and management of obesity and endorsed the WHO acceleration plan to stop obesity [4]. Prevention and treatment of obesity can be achieved through public health initiatives focusing on dietary changes, behavioral, and lifestyle modications, progressing to medical management through supervised diets and obesity management medications such as glucagon-like peptide-1 agonists [5]. The effectiveness of these methods may be limited, especially for individuals with severe obesity, and may be accompanied by signicant morbidity [567].

Bariatric surgery has been established as the most effective method for treatment of severe obesity or when conservative measures fail [5]. It carries an acceptable risk of morbidity and mortality, durability of weight loss, reduction of obesity related diseases and rapid improvement of quality of life (QOL) [89]. Sleeve gastrectomy is the most common bariatric procedure due to its low morbidity and mortality [6]. Although there is some regional variability, the number of bariatric surgery procedures performed is increasing year-to-year [6].

Surgical stapling devices are a mainstay in virtually all bariatric surgical procedures. Although these devices are generally safe, complications related to the use of staplers can occur. Major stapler related complications include staple line bleeding (both intra-operatively and post-operatively), leaks, and anastomotic stenosis, and occur in approximately 5% of cases [1011]. The etiology of these complications varies; abnormal tissue compression (either too much or not enough) can contribute to leaks, bleeding can occur from poorly formed staples or improper staple height selection, or from patient factors such as hypertension or anticoagulation [1012]. The use of staple line reinforcement has been shown in some studies to decrease the risks of leaks and/or bleeding, but this adds cost and time to the procedure [1314]. Surgeon judgment and experience in selection of proper staple height and appropriate firing technique also play a major role in complication rates [1011]. It is clear, however, that the stapling device itself can be a major factor in the etiology of complications [12].

Technologies that improve staple line formation, safety, ergonomics, and minimize tissue trauma are essential requirements for the next generation of staplers and will reduce complication rates related to the device itself [12].

The AEON endoscopic manual stapler (Lexington Medical, Bedford, MA, USA) was introduced to the market in 2018 with an AEON Powered Stapling System developed more recently. These devices have been shown to be safe and effective in bariatric, hepatic, and pancreatic procedures as demonstrated by internal device testing and published data in animal models [15] and clinical evaluations [16171819]. Independent peer-reviewed data have also compared the AEON stapler favorably with other staplers in terms of staple line bleeding [1720] and pancreatic fistula rates [21].

This prospective trial was conducted to investigate the hypothesis that the AEON Powered Stapling System could improve consistency and stability of firing intraoperatively, which could lead to less tissue trauma, improved hemostasis, and fewer intra- and post-operative complications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This is a prospective, single-arm, single-center study designed to evaluate the clinical outcomes of patients undergoing primary sleeve gastrectomy using the AEON Powered Stapling System.

The primary endpoint of the trial was to assess the percentage of patients experiencing postoperative staple line complications (i.e., bleeding or leaks) requiring blood transfusion or revision surgery.

The secondary endpoint was to compare clinical outcomes for patients without staple line complications versus those with such complications. Because no staple-line complications were observed in this cohort, this planned comparison could not be performed (see RESULTS).

In addition to the planned endpoints, the hemostatic appearance of the sleeve gastrectomy staple line was analyzed intraoperatively. A visual analogue scale (VAS), graded from 1 to 5 based on bleeding observed at the staple line was used as described by Redmann et al [20].

A single laparoscopic image capturing the entire gastric staple line was obtained 10 seconds after the last firing of the sleeve gastrectomy.

Each image was scored by an independent third-party evaluator who was not involved in the surgical procedure and was blinded to patient and procedural details. The VAS score assigned to each patient reflected bleeding across the entire visible staple line.

QOL before surgery and 3 weeks after sleeve gastrectomy procedure were evaluated using MOS 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) [22].

Safety endpoints included evaluating device-related adverse events, serious adverse events, unanticipated adverse device effects, and revision surgeries. The study was planned for 50 patients.

Prospectively collected preoperative data for statistical analysis included demographics, body mass index (BMI), and preoperative comorbidities and QOL scores.

Sleeve gastrectomies were performed according to our institutional standard-of-care and all subjects underwent standard pre-operative evaluation as well as postoperative care. Sutures, cautery, or clips were applied to the staple line at the surgeon’s discretion.

All firings with the AEON Endoscopic Powered Handle Stapler and reloads conformed to the AEON Instructions For Use.

AEON reloads used were Purple (4.0 mm open staple height, 1.8 mm closed staple height) and Orange (3.25 mm, 1.5 mm). Purple reloads were 60 mm in length, and Orange reloads were 45 mm and 60 mm in length.

First firings were performed using AEON Purple. Subsequent firings were performed using AEON Orange.

No staple line reinforcement was used in any of the cases.

The staple line bleeding evaluator analyzed staple line bleeding for each image according to the VAS scale [20] (Fig. 1) as soon as all images became available, and scores were tabulated.

Visual analogue score Bleeding Severity Scale for hemostasis evaluation [20]. (A) No bleeding at staple line; (B) Minimal bleeding at staple line; (C) Moderate bleeding at staple line; (D) Excessive bleeding at staple line: (E) Profuse bleeding at staple line.

Postoperatively the patient’s pain level on a 0 to 10 scale (per standard protocol) 4 hours after procedure, on postoperative day 1 and postoperative day 2 were recorded. Postoperative pain management followed the institution’s standard multimodal analgesic protocol. Patients received intravenous paracetamol 1 g four times daily and intravenous dexketoprofen twice daily, unless contraindicated. A single 50 mg dose of pethidine was routinely administered immediately after surgery. Additional analgesia was provided when clinically indicated (VAS score >4). Antiemetic therapy consisted of intravenous metoclopramide three times daily and intravenous ondansetron four times a day. Hemoglobin (Hb) levels were collected in the recovery room, on postoperative day 1 and postoperative day 2 and compared with preoperative Hb.

Three weeks after the surgery all the patients had a follow-up appointment to assess for complications, wound healing, and device-related adverse events. Additionally, postoperative outcomes on their weight-loss, duration of analgesic use, and QOL were analyzed. Staple-line bleeding, staple-line leak, and device-related adverse events were monitored intraoperatively, during the index hospitalization, and at the 3-week follow-up visit; readmissions and reoperations were captured through 30 days postoperatively; and QOL was assessed at the 3-week visit. No longer-term follow-up was performed.

DISCUSSION

Obesity is a chronic relapsing disease which is recognized as a serious public health issue due to its widespread prevalence, increase in morbidity and mortality in affected individuals, and increased societal and healthcare costs. Treatment modalities continue to evolve, with surgical intervention currently playing a major role.

Requirements for any therapeutic approach to obesity treatment are no different from any other disease, and include safety, efficacy, sustainability of results, acceptable adverse effects, and improvement of QOL. Surgical treatment of obesity is widely recognized as the most effective both in terms of weight loss and sustainability of results, and surgical procedures for obesity continue to increase year-over-year [56]. Due to standardized surgical techniques and advances in medical devices, intraoperative and postoperative complications continue to decrease [71120].

Despite improvements in technique and devices, complications still occur, and it is incumbent upon surgeons to strive to minimize complications. One factor that can play a major role in decreasing the most serious complications of bleeding and leakage is the improvement of endoscopic stapling devices. There is growing evidence that powered staplers produce superior results to manual staplers in gastrointestinal and thoracic surgery, with lower rates of bleeding and leakage [2324]. Despite the increase in cost of powered staplers, the overall cost of hospitalization is lowered by using them, due to improved complication rates and shorter operative times [23].

Lexington Medical has developed both a powered and a manual linear stapler, (AEON Endostapler, AEON Powered Stapler System) for a variety of indications including general, thoracic, and bariatric surgery. Studies comparing the AEON to other commonly used surgical staplers have shown that the AEON staple line exhibits better hemostasis even without staple line reinforcement [161720], and decreased rates of pancreatic fistula in distal pancreatectomy [21]. One feature of the AEON stapler is the use of a uniform closed staple height across all 6 rows of staples, in contrast to devices such as Medtronic Tri-Staple technology, which uses graduated staple heights across rows. Uniform staple height may contribute to improved hemostasis but has not been definitively established and would require dedicated comparative study. In addition, an integrated microprocessor assesses tissue thickness and resistance 1,000 times per second and adjusts its stapling and cutting to one of seven speeds based on this feedback. These features likely play a role in the AEON’s improved results. Internal ex vivo studies have shown a higher rate of proper staple formation using the AEON stapler across a variety of tissue thicknesses compared with other staplers.

In this study, the AEON Powered Stapler System was used for the first time in sleeve gastrectomy. The study was focused on hemostasis of the staple line intraoperatively, postoperative outcomes and complications, and changes in the QOL of patients after surgery.

During the study, we did not experience any device malfunctions or misfiring, and our patients did not experience any stapler-related complications. Specifically, there was no staple line bleeding requiring intraoperative or postoperative intervention or transfusion, there were no obvious excessive tissue compression signs, staple-line leaks. This is likely related to tissue chickness assessment and adapting the stapler compression and firing speed to the individual circumstances.

No strictures in the follow-up period were observed. No staple line reinforcement was used.

The AEON powered stapler demonstrated excellent hemostasis, with a mean of 1.04±0.19 on a 1–5 VAS by an independent observer. In our experience, hemostasis of the AEON staple line was favorable even in the absence of buttressing material or other reinforcement; however, because this study did not include a comparator group, no direct comparison with other devices can be made. This was not only reflected in the visual appearance of the staple line, but in the clinically insignificant drop in Hb (1.624±0.835 g/dL) and no blood transfusion requirement.

Most studies find a positive impact of metabolic and bariatric surgery on patients’ QOL [8925]. We found it interesting that we saw statistically significant changes in 5 scales of the SF-36 QOL questionnaire at only 3 weeks postoperatively. Improvement occurred in the physical functioning, energy, general health and 2 emotion scales. These findings should be regarded as exploratory: the 3-week time point reflects very early postoperative recovery, the analysis was uncontrolled, and the changes cannot be interpreted as evidence of durable quality-of-life improvement.

Limitations of our study include a relatively small number of patients, a short follow-up period, and the absence of major complications, which precluded full evaluation of the initially planned outcomes. As a single-center, single-arm study without a comparator group, it cannot support conclusions of superiority over other devices. The cohort was also highly skewed by sex, with 49 of 50 patients being female, which limits the generalizability of the findings. These limitations did allow us to achieve 100% follow-up of our cohort, however.

CONCLUSIONS

In this single-arm study, the AEON Powered Stapler System was feasible in primary laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy and demonstrated a favorable short-term safety profile, with low intraoperative staple-line bleeding scores. The hemostatic potential advantages could be related to the feature of powered handle to recognize thicker tissues and change gears to facilitate appropriate firing speed, minimizing trauma of the tissue and allowing for better staple formation. Comparative studies with a control group and longer follow-up are warranted to confirm these preliminary findings.

Abstract
. In this paper, we define the multiple Eisenstein series in positive characteristic, with Thakur’s multiple zeta values appearing as the “constant terms” of their expansions in terms of “multiple Goss sums”. We show that the multiple Eisenstein series satisfy the same q q -shuffle relations as the multiple zeta values do, thereby lifting the relations from “values” to “functions”.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Classical Results

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers.
Recall that for any (non-empty) index
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
s_{1}>1
, the classical multiple zeta value is defined by the infinite series

\zeta(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\in\mathbb{R}.

The quantities
\operatorname{wt}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}
and
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r
are called the weight and depth of the presentation
\zeta(\underline{s})
, respectively.
Via the integral (resp. series) representations of the multiple zeta values, one may express the product of two multiple zeta values as
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of multiple zeta values of the same weight, called the shuffle (resp. stuffle) relations.
In particular, this implies that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by all multiple zeta values admits a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra structure.
Furthermore, the above two product relations, together with some regularized process, lead to the so-called extended double shuffle relations of multiple zeta values.
Conjecturally, these relations provide all
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations among multiple zeta values
[
IKZ06
]
.

On the other hand, the notion of double Eisenstein series was introduced by Gangl, Kaneko, and Zagier
[
GKZ06
]
, and the double zeta value in question appears as the constant term of the
q
-expansion of the corresponding double Eisenstein series.
Bachmann
[
Bac12
]
later generalized their idea and defined the
multiple Eisenstein series
as follows.

Definition 1.1

(
[
GKZ06
]
)
.

Let
\tau
be an element in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}\tau>0\}
.
For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}
, we define the notation
\succ
as follows:

(1)

m=a\tau+b\succ 0
if
a>0
or
(a,b)\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

m\succ n
if
m-n\succ 0
.

Definition 1.2

(
[
GKZ06
]
for
r\leq 2
,
[
Bac12
]
for general
r
)
.

For
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
s_{1}\geq 3
and
s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}\geq 2
, the
multiple Eisenstein series
is defined by

G_{\underline{s}}(\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\tau+n_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\tau+n_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}\\
m_{1}\tau+n_{1}\succ\cdots\succ m_{r}\tau+n_{r}\succ 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}\tau+n_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(m_{r}\tau+n_{r})^{s_{r}}}.

For
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
with
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\geq 2
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
, we consider the
multitangent function

[
Bou14
]
defined by

\Psi_{\underline{s}}(\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(\tau+n_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(\tau+n_{r})^{s_{r}}}.

By splitting up the summation in Definition
1.2
, one has the following expansion in terms of multitangent functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
s_{1}\geq 3
and
s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}\geq 2
, we have

G_{\underline{s}}(\tau)=\zeta(\underline{s})+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\zeta(s_{j+1},\ldots,s_{r})g(s_{1},\ldots,s_{j})

where
g
is given by

g(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
0=r_{0}<r_{1}<\cdots<r_{j-1}<r_{j}=r\\
m_{1}>\cdots>m_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\prod_{{}_{i=1}}^{j}\Psi_{s_{r_{i-1}+1},\ldots,s_{r_{i}}}(m_{i}\tau).

Bachmann
[
BB23
]
(see also
[
Bac20
]
) further expanded these multitangent functions and obtained the
q
-expansion of the multiple Eisenstein series
G_{\underline{s}}(\tau)
with the corresponding multiple zeta value
\zeta(\underline{s})
appearing as the constant term, generalizing the result of Gangl-Kaneko-Zagier
[
GKZ06
]
.

The aforementioned
q
-expansion result leads to the questions of “lifting” relations from multiple zeta values to multiple Eisenstein series.
However, some shuffle/stuffle relations cannot be lifted directly since the corresponding multiple Eisenstein series may not be defined due to the convergence issues.
For example, in the shuffle relation
\zeta(3)\zeta(3)=12\zeta(5,1)+6\zeta(4,2)+2\zeta(3,3)
, the Eisenstein series is not defined for
\underline{s}=(5,1)
.
This obstruction was overcome in
[
BT18
]
and
[
Bac19
]
by introducing the notions of
shuffle
and
stuffle

regularized multiple Eisenstein series
, respectively.
Their results can be described as the following.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
BT18
]
for shuffle relations,
[
Bac19
]
for stuffle relations)
.

The shuffle (resp. stuffle) regularized multiple Eisenstein series satisfy the same shuffle (resp. stuffle) relations as the corresponding multiple zeta values do.

1.2.
Multiple Zeta Values in Positive Characteristic

In this paper, we consider an analog of Theorem
1.4
in positive characteristic.
Let
p
be a prime number and
q
be a power of
p
.
Let
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
be the polynomial ring in the variable
\theta
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
q
elements (with the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
), and
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
be its field of fractions.
We let
K_{\infty}
be the completion of
K
with respect to the absolute value
|\cdot|
at the infinite place normalized so that
|\theta|=q
, and
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
be the completion of a fixed algebraic closure of
K_{\infty}
.
For a positive integer
d
, we let
A_{+},A_{+,d},A_{<d}
be the subsets of
A
consisting of all monic polynomials, monic polynomials of degree
d
, and polynomials of degree less than
d
, respectively.
Moreover, we define
A_{+,0}=\{1\}
.

In
[
Tha04
]
, Thakur introduced the multiple zeta values over
A
, which generalized the notions of Carlitz single zeta values
[
Car35
]
as follows.
For any
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and (non-empty) index
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we define the
d
-th power sum

(1)

S_{d}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{s_{r}}}\in K

and Thakur’s
multiple zeta value

\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}S_{d}(\underline{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A_{+}\\
\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{s_{r}}}\in K_{\infty}.

Similar to the classical setting, the quantities
\operatorname{wt}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}
and
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r
are called the weight and depth of the presentation
\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})
, respectively.
It is known by
[
Tha09
]
that all these multiple zeta values are non-vanishing.
Moreover, Thakur
[
Tha10
]
showed the existence that the product of two multiple zeta values can be expressed as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear combination of multiple zeta values of the same weight, called the
q
-shuffle relations
for multiple zeta values.
In particular, this implies that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space spanned by all multiple zeta values admits an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure.

In contrast to the classical situation mentioned in Section
1.1
, Thakur
[
Tha09
]
conjectured that the multiple zeta values over
A
admit only one “shuffle relation” and gave some heuristic reasons and observations based on the “motivic interpretations” of multiple zeta values
[
AT09
]
.

Regarding the existence of the
q
-shuffle product mentioned above, the first concrete example was due to Chen
[
Che15
]
, who proved the following explicit formula:

\zeta_{A}(r)\zeta_{A}(s)=\zeta_{A}(r,s)+\zeta_{A}(s,r)+\zeta_{A}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\zeta_{A}(i,j),

where
\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{r-1}\binom{j-1}{r-1}+(-1)^{s-1}\binom{j-1}{s-1}
.
Later on, based on Chen’s formula, Yamamoto gave the following (conjectural) explicit
q
-shuffle relations for the multiple zeta values, which were verified in Shi’s PhD thesis
[
Shi18
]
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the free monoid generated by the set
\{x_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and let
\mathcal{R}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space generated by
\mathcal{S}
. For
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we define
x_{\underline{s}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{s_{1}}\cdots x_{s_{r}}
, and for
\underline{s}=\varnothing
we define
x_{\varnothing}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
. An element
x_{\underline{s}}
is called a
word
of
depth
r
.
We define the
q
-shuffle product

\ast
on
\mathcal{R}
inductively on the sum of depths as follows:

(1)

For the empty word
x_{\varnothing}=1
and any
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{R}
, define

1*\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}*1=\mathfrak{a}.

(2)

For
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, define

x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{s}}=x_{r_{1}}(x_{\underline{r}^{-}}*x_{\underline{s}})+x_{s_{1}}(x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{s}^{-}})+x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\underline{r}^{-}}*x_{\underline{s}^{-}})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r_{1},s_{1}}x_{i}((x_{\underline{r}^{-}}*x_{\underline{s}^{-}})*x_{j})

where

(a)

\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{r-1}\binom{j-1}{r-1}+(-1)^{s-1}\binom{j-1}{s-1}
.

(b)

x\sum_{i}f_{i}x_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i}f_{i}(xx_{i})
for
f_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
x,x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
.

(c)

x_{\underline{r}^{-}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{r_{2}}\cdots x_{r_{m}}
and
x_{\underline{s}^{-}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{s_{2}}\cdots x_{s_{n}}
.

(3)

Expand the product
\ast
to the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathcal{R}
by the distributive law.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
Shi18
]
)
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{S}_{<d},\bm{\zeta}_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to K_{\infty}
be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear maps satisfying

\mathbf{S}_{<d}(1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1,\quad\mathbf{S}_{<d}(x_{\underline{s}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S_{<d}(\underline{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}S_{i}(\underline{s}),

and

\bm{\zeta}_{A}(1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1,\quad\bm{\zeta}_{A}(x_{\underline{s}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbf{S}_{<d}(x_{\underline{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\underline{s}).

Then
\mathbf{S}_{<d}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.
Consequently,
\bm{\zeta}_{A}
is also an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism, i.e.,

\bm{\zeta}_{A}(x_{\underline{r}}\ast x_{\underline{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\underline{r})\zeta_{A}(\underline{s}).

1.3.
Multiple Eisenstein Series

We now define the multiple Eisenstein series and state the main result of this paper.
Let
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus K_{\infty}
be the Drinfeld upper half-plane, which has a rigid analytic structure
[
Dri74
, Proposition 6.2]
.
The following definition is due to
[
Che17
]
, which is analogous to Definition
1.1
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
z\in\Omega
and
m,n,a,b\in A
. We define the notation
\succ
as follows:

(1)

mz+a\succ 0
if
m\in A_{+}
or
(m,a)\in\{0\}\times A_{+}
.

(2)

For
mz+a,nz+b\succ 0
, we write
mz+a\succ nz+b
if one of the following holds:

(a)

m,n\in A_{+}
with
\deg m>\deg n
.

(b)

m\in A_{+}
and
n=0
.

(c)

m=n=0
,
a,b\in A_{+}
, and
\deg a>\deg b
.

We now define the multiple Eisenstein series, which generalize the depth two case defined by Chen
[
Che17
]
.

Definition 1.8

(Multiple Eisenstein series)
.

For
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we define the
multiple Eisenstein series
on
\Omega
by

E_{\underline{s}}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}z+a_{1},\ldots,m_{r}z+a_{r}\in Az+A\\
m_{1}z+a_{1}\succ\cdots\succ m_{r}z+a_{r}\succ 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}z+a_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(m_{r}z+a_{r})^{s_{r}}}.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be the algebra of rigid analytic functions on
\Omega
.
We will see in Proposition
2.1
that
the multiple Eisenstein series are rigid analytic functions on
\Omega
, i.e.,
E_{\underline{s}}(z)\in\mathcal{X}
.
Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.9

(Main Result)
.

Let
\mathbf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{X}
be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map satisfying

\mathbf{E}(1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{E}(x_{\underline{s}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{\underline{s}}(z).

Then
\mathbf{E}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism, i.e.,

\mathbf{E}(x_{\underline{r}}\ast x_{\underline{s}})=E_{\underline{r}}(z)E_{\underline{s}}(z).

In Proposition
2.3
, we prove that for any
\underline{s}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the multiple zeta value
\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})
appears as the “constant term” of the expansion of the multiple Eisenstein series
E_{\underline{s}}(z)
in terms of “multiple Goss sums” (see Definition
2.4
).
Hence, our main result illustrates the phenomenon that Thakur’s
q
-shuffle relations for the multiple zeta values (cf. Theorem
1.6
) can be “lifted” to the corresponding multiple Eisenstein series, thereby generalizing Chen’s result
[
Che17
]
to arbitrary depth.

1.4.
Strategy of Proof

We begin the proof by establishing the expansion of the multiple Eisenstein series in terms of multiple zeta values and multiple Goss sums (Proposition
2.3
).
Inspired by Chen’s calculation in the depth one case
[
Che17
]
, we formulate the explicit
q
-shuffle relations for multiple Goss sums.
Furthermore, we define the
q
-shuffle algebra
\mathcal{E}
, which contains
\mathcal{R}
(Definition
1.5
) as a subalgebra, to give a formalism of these
q
-shuffle relations.
This enables us to decompose the map
\mathbf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{X}
into
\mathbf{G}\circ\mathbf{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{E}/\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{X}
, where
\mathfrak{A}
is the ideal generated by the associators of
\mathcal{E}
.
The main result follows from showing that both
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{e}
are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphisms (Propositions
2.10
and
2.13
).

We investigate the map
\mathbf{G}
via the “
d
-th Goss power sums” (
3
), which can be viewed as a rank-two analog of the
d
-th power sums (
1
).
For this reason, we adopt a similar approach to Shi’s proof of Theorem
1.6
to establish the result for
\mathbf{G}
.
The difficulty arises in the map
\mathbf{e}
since the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\mathcal{E}
involves complicated calculations of the
q
-shuffle products.
For our purpose, we may circumvent this obstacle by passing to the quotient algebra
\mathcal{E}/\mathfrak{A}
.
The result then follows from careful calculations on
q
-shuffle products of words.

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem

2.1.
Basic Properties of the Multiple Eisenstein Series

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, let
|z|_{\Im}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{|z-x|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x\in K_{\infty}\}
be the “imaginary part” of

.
Recall that

\Omega=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{n}\quad\text{where}\quad\Omega_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\mid q^{-n}\leq|z|_{\Im}\leq|z|\leq q^{n}\}

is an admissible cover of
\Omega
.
Also recall that a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is called rigid analytic if its restriction to each
\Omega_{n}
is the uniform limit of a sequence of rational functions without poles on
\Omega_{n}
.
(See
[
Gos80
, Proposition 2.4]
,
[
Pel21
, Section 4.5]
, or
[
FP04

Proposition 2.1
.

The multiple Eisenstein series
E_{\underline{s}}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is a rigid analytic function on
\Omega
.

Proof.

For any
z\in\Omega_{n}
, we consider
m_{1}z+a_{1},\ldots,m_{r}z+a_{r}\in Az+A
with
m_{1}z+a_{1}\succ\cdots\succ m_{r}z+a_{r}\succ 0
.
We choose
0\leq i\leq r
such that

(1)

m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}\in A_{+}
with
\deg m_{1}>\cdots>\deg m_{i}
.

(2)

m_{i+1}=\cdots=m_{r}=0
.

(3)

a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A_{+}
with
\deg a_{i+1}>\cdots>\deg a_{r}
.

Then we see that

\displaystyle\left|(m_{1}z+a_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(m_{r}z+a_{r})^{s_{r}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|m_{1}z\right|_{\Im}^{s_{1}}\cdots\left|m_{i}z\right|_{\Im}^{s_{i}}|a_{i+1}|^{s_{i+1}}\cdots|a_{r}|^{s_{r}}

\displaystyle=\left|m_{1}\right|^{s_{1}}\left|z\right|_{\Im}^{s_{1}}\cdots\left|m_{i}\right|^{s_{i}}\left|z\right|_{\Im}^{s_{i}}|a_{i+1}|^{s_{i+1}}\cdots|a_{r}|^{s_{r}}

\displaystyle\geq q^{-n(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i})}\left|m_{1}\right|^{s_{1}}\cdots\left|m_{i}\right|^{s_{i}}|a_{i+1}|^{s_{i+1}}\cdots|a_{r}|^{s_{r}}.

Hence,
E_{\underline{s}}(z)
is the uniform limit of rational functions with poles outside
\Omega_{n}
.
∎

Fix a

Recall that in

, which is a sequence of polynomials over

(2)

\tilde{\pi}^{k}G_{k}(t(z))=\sum_{b\in A}\frac{1}{(z+b)^{k}}.

In what follows, we define two types of notations:

i

\underline{s}

\underline{s}_{(i)}

\underline{s}

Definition 2.2

For any (non-empty) index

For any

For

Summing over elements in
S_{i}
, we see that

\sum_{(\underline{m},\underline{a})\in S_{i}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}z+a_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(m_{r}z+a_{r})^{s_{r}}}=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A_{+}\\
\deg a_{i+1}>\cdots>\deg a_{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{i+1}^{s_{i+1}}\cdots a_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right)\times\\
\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}\in A_{+}\\
\deg m_{1}>\cdots>\deg m_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{a_{1}\in A}\frac{1}{(m_{1}z+a_{1})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{a_{i}\in A}\frac{1}{(m_{i}z+a_{i})^{s_{i}}}\right)\right).

By definition, the first term is
\zeta_{A}(\underline{s}^{(i)})
.
And by (
2
), the second term is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}\in A_{+}\\
\deg m_{1}>\cdots>\deg m_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\tilde{\pi}^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}}G_{s_{1}}(t(m_{1}z))\cdots G_{s_{i}}(t(m_{i}z)).

Since the set

\{(\underline{m},\underline{a})=(m_{1}z+a_{1},\ldots,m_{r}z+a_{r})\in(Az+A)^{r}\mid m_{1}z+a_{1}\succ\cdots\succ m_{r}z+a_{r}\succ 0\}

is the disjoint union of
S_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq r
, it follows that

\displaystyle E_{\underline{s}}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{(\underline{m},\underline{a})\in S_{i}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}z+a_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(m_{r}z+a_{r})^{s_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\zeta_{A}(\underline{s}^{(i)})\tilde{\pi}^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}\in A_{+}\\
\deg m_{1}>\cdots>\deg m_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}G_{s_{1}}(t(m_{1}z))\cdots G_{s_{i}}(t(m_{i}z)).

∎

2.2.
The Multiple Goss Sums

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We define the
d-th Goss power sum
by

(3)

G_{d}(\underline{s})=\tilde{\pi}^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg m_{1}>\cdots>\deg m_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}G_{s_{1}}(t(m_{1}z))\cdots G_{s_{r}}(t(m_{r}z)).

Moreover, we define for
d\in\mathbb{N}

G_{<d}(\underline{s})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}G_{i}(\underline{s})\quad\text{and}\quad G(\underline{s})=\lim_{d\to\infty}G_{<d}(\underline{s}).

The latter is called the
multiple Goss sum
.
We conventionally put
G_{d}(\varnothing)=\delta_{d,0}
and
G_{<d}(\varnothing)=1
, where
\delta_{i,j}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

As a partial sum of the multiple Eisenstein series,
G_{d}(\underline{s})
and
G(\underline{s})
are rigid analytic functions on
\Omega
.
In what follows, we drop the variable
z
to simplify the notations.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that under this definition, Proposition
2.3
can be restated as follows:
For any
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
z\in\Omega
, we have

(4)

E_{\underline{s}}(z)=\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\zeta_{A}(\underline{s}^{(i)})G(\underline{s}_{(i)}).

We also note that for all
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we have

(5)

G_{d}(\underline{s})=G_{d}(s_{1})G_{<d}(\underline{s}^{(1)}).

Recall that for
i,j,r,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{r-1}\binom{j-1}{r-1}+(-1)^{s-1}\binom{j-1}{s-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
r,s
be positive integers. Then for any
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\displaystyle G_{d}(r)G_{d}(s)=G_{d}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta_{r,s}^{i,j}\zeta_{A}(j)G_{d}(i)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}G_{d}(i,j).

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of
[
Che17
, Theorem 4.1]
.
Recall for any two distinct elements
a,b
in
A[z]
(or any integral domain), we have the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{a^{r}b^{s}}=\sum_{i+j=r+s}\frac{1}{(a-b)^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{a^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{b^{i}}\right).

Consider

\displaystyle G_{d}(r)G_{d}(s)=\sum_{m,n\in A_{+,d}}\tilde{\pi}^{r+s}G_{r}(t(mz))G_{s}(t(nz))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in A_{+,d}}\tilde{\pi}^{r+s}G_{r}(t(mz))G_{s}(t(mz))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in A_{+,d}\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\tilde{\pi}^{r+s}G_{r}(t(mz))G_{s}(t(nz)).

For the first summand, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in A_{+,d}}\tilde{\pi}^{r+s}G_{r}(t(mz))G_{s}(t(mz))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a,b\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mz+a)^{r}(mz+b)^{s}}\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq-Goss-polynomials})}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mz+a)^{r}(mz+a)^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a,b\in A,a\neq b\end{subarray}}\sum_{i+j=r+s}\frac{1}{(a-b)^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+b)^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle G_{d}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a,f\in A,f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{i+j=r+s}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+a-f)^{i}}\right).

For a fixed pair
(i,j)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a,f\in A,f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a\in A,f\in A_{+}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(\varepsilon f)^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }q-1\nmid j,\\
\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a\in A,f\in A_{+}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}},&\text{if }q-1\mid j.\end{cases}

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a,f\in A,f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+a-f)^{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
b,f\in A,f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+b)^{i}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }q-1\nmid j,\\
\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
b\in A,f\in A_{+}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+b)^{i}},&\text{if }q-1\mid j.\end{cases}

Therefore, the first summand becomes

\displaystyle G_{d}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a,f\in A,f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{i+j=r+s}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+a-f)^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle G_{d}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
a\in A,f\in A_{+}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{f^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}\binom{j-1}{s-1}+(-1)^{j-r-1}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle G_{d}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\zeta_{A}(j)G_{d}(i).

For the second summand, note that the summand vanishes if
d=0
. For
d>0
, a similar argument shows

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in A_{+,d}\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\tilde{\pi}^{r+s}G_{r}(t(mz))G_{s}(t(nz))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in A_{+,d}\\
m\neq n\\
a,b\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mz+a)^{r}(nz+b)^{s}}\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq-Goss-polynomials})}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in A_{+,d}\\
m\neq n\\
a,b\in A\end{subarray}}~\sum_{i+j=r+s}\frac{1}{((m-n)z+(a-b))^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(mz+a)^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(nz+b)^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=r+s}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A_{+,d}\\
n_{1}\in A_{<d}\\
a,b_{1}\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}}{(n_{1}z+b_{1})^{j}(mz+a)^{i}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in A_{+,d}\\
m_{1}\in A_{<d}\\
a_{1},b\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(m_{1}z+a_{1})^{j}(nz+b)^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=r+s}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2},n_{2}\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg m_{2}>\deg n_{2}\\
a_{2},b_{2}\in A\end{subarray}}~\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{j}}\frac{(-1)^{s}\binom{j-1}{s-1}+(-1)^{j-r}\binom{j-1}{r-1}}{(m_{2}z+a_{2})^{i}(n_{2}z+b_{2})^{j}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}G_{d}(i,j).

This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
The
q
-shuffle Algebra
\mathcal{E}
and the Map
\mathbf{G}

In view of Proposition
2.3
, to establish the
q
-shuffle relations for the multiple Eisenstein series, we extend the
q
-shuffle algebra
\mathcal{R}
to deal with the
q
-shuffle relations for both multiple zeta values and multiple Goss sums.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the free monoid generated by the set
\{x_{n},y_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, subject to the commutativity relations
x_{i}y_{j}=y_{j}x_{i}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
,
and let
\mathcal{E}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space generated by
\mathcal{T}
.
For
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we define
x_{\underline{s}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{s_{1}}\cdots x_{s_{r}}
and
y_{\underline{s}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=y_{s_{1}}\cdots y_{s_{r}}
. For
\underline{s}=\varnothing
, we define
x_{\varnothing}=y_{\varnothing}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
.
An element
x_{\underline{s}}
or
y_{\underline{s}}
is called a
word
of
depth
r
.
We define the
q
-shuffle product

\ast
on
\mathcal{E}
inductively on the sum of depths as follows:

(1)

For the empty word
x_{\varnothing}=y_{\varnothing}=1
and any
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{E}
, define

1*\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}*1=\mathfrak{a}.

(2)

For any indices
\underline{r}
and
\underline{s}
, define

x_{\underline{r}}*y_{\underline{s}}=x_{\underline{r}}y_{\underline{s}}=y_{\underline{s}}x_{\underline{r}}=y_{\underline{s}}\ast x_{\underline{r}}.

(3)

For
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, define

x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{s}}=x_{r_{1}}(x_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*x_{\underline{s}})+x_{s_{1}}(x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})+x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*x_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r_{1},s_{1}}x_{i}((x_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*x_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})*x_{j}).

(4)

For all
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, define

y_{\underline{r}}*y_{\underline{s}}=y_{r_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}})+y_{s_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}}*y_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})+y_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r_{1},s_{1}}y_{i}((y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})*x_{j})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r_{1},s_{1}}y_{i}((y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}^{(1)}})*y_{j}).

(5)

For indices
\underline{r},\underline{r}^{\prime},\underline{s},\underline{s}^{\prime}
, define

(x_{\underline{r}}y_{\underline{s}})*(x_{\underline{r}^{\prime}}y_{\underline{s}^{\prime}})=(x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{r}^{\prime}})*(y_{\underline{s}}*y_{\underline{s}^{\prime}}).

(6)

Expand the product
\ast
to the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathcal{E}
by the distributive law.

One sees that
\mathcal{E}
is a commutative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra, which contains
\mathcal{R}
as a subalgebra (recall Definition
1.5
).

Definition 2.8
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define
\mathbf{G}_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{X}
to be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map such that for any indices
\underline{r}
and
\underline{s}
,

\mathbf{G}_{d}(x_{\underline{r}}y_{\underline{s}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\zeta_{A}(\underline{r})G_{d}(\underline{s}),

where we put
\zeta_{A}(\varnothing)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
by convention.
Also, we define for
d\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{E}
,

\mathbf{G}_{<d}(\mathfrak{a})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\mathbf{G}_{i}(\mathfrak{a})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{G}(\mathfrak{a})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbf{G}_{<d}(\mathfrak{a}).

Remark 2.9
.

Note that for any index
\underline{s}
, we have (recall the convention in Definition
2.4
)

\mathbf{G}_{d}(x_{\underline{s}})=\mathbf{G}(x_{\underline{s}}y_{\varnothing})=\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})G_{d}(\varnothing)=\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})\delta_{d,0}

and

\mathbf{G}_{d}(y_{\underline{s}})=\mathbf{G}(x_{\varnothing}y_{\underline{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\varnothing)G_{d}(\underline{s})=G_{d}(\underline{s}).

In particular, for
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(6)

\mathbf{G}_{<d}(x_{\underline{s}})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})\delta_{d,0}=\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{s}})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}G_{i}(\underline{s})=G_{<d}(\underline{s}).

Moreover, it follows from (
5
) that for any index
\underline{s}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(7)

\mathbf{G}_{d}(y_{r}y_{\underline{s}})=\mathbf{G}_{d}(y_{r})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{s}}).

Proposition 2.10
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{G}_{<d}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.
Consequently,
\mathbf{G}
is also an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to check
\mathbf{G}_{<d}
is compatible with the
q
-shuffle relations in Definition
2.7
.

(1)

For the first relation, we have

\mathbf{G}_{<d}(1*\mathfrak{a})=\mathbf{G}_{<d}(\mathfrak{a}*1)=\mathbf{G}_{<d}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathbf{G}_{<d}(1)\mathbf{G}_{<d}(\mathfrak{a}).

(2)

For the second relation, we have by (
6
) that

\mathbf{G}_{<d}(x_{\underline{r}}*y_{\underline{s}})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\mathbf{G}_{d}(x_{\underline{r}}y_{\underline{s}})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\zeta_{A}(\underline{r})G_{i}(\underline{s})=\zeta_{A}(\underline{r})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{s}})=\mathbf{G}_{<d}(x_{\underline{r}})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{s}}).

(3)

For the third relation, note that by (
6
), we have
\mathbf{G}_{<d}|_{\mathcal{R}}=\bm{\zeta}_{A}
.
Thus, by Theorem
1.6
,

\mathbf{G}_{<d}(x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{s}})=\bm{\zeta}_{A}(x_{\underline{r}}*x_{\underline{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\underline{r})\zeta_{A}(\underline{s})=\mathbf{G}_{<d}(x_{\underline{r}})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(x_{\underline{s}}).

(4)

For the fourth relation, namely,
\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{r}}*y_{\underline{s}})=\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{r}})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{s}})
, note that the result is reduced to (1) when either
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{r})
or
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{s})
is zero. Assuming both
\underline{r}
and
\underline{s}
are non-empty, we proceed by induction on the total depth
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{r})+\operatorname{dep}(\underline{s})
.
For the base case
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{r})=\operatorname{dep}(\underline{s})=1
, we write
\underline{r}=r
and
\underline{s}=s
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{r})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{s})=\left(\sum_{d>d_{1}}\mathbf{G}_{d_{1}}(y_{r})\right)\left(\sum_{d>d_{2}}\mathbf{G}_{d_{2}}(y_{s})\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{1}>d_{2}}G_{d_{1}}(r)G_{d_{2}}(s)+\sum_{d>d_{2}>d_{1}}G_{d_{1}}(r)G_{d_{2}}(s)+\sum_{d>d_{1}=d_{2}}G_{d_{1}}(r)G_{d_{2}}(s)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{1}}G_{d_{1}}(r)G_{<d_{1}}(s)+\sum_{d>d_{2}}G_{d_{2}}(s)G_{<d_{2}}(r)+\sum_{d>d_{3}}G_{d_{3}}(r)G_{d_{3}}(s)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\sum_{d>d_{1}}G_{d_{1}}(r,s)+\sum_{d>d_{2}}G_{d_{2}}(s,r)\\
+\sum_{d>d_{3}}\Bigg(G_{d_{3}}(r+s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}G_{d_{3}}(i)\zeta_{A}(j)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}G_{d_{3}}(i,j)\Bigg)\\
\text{(by \eqref{eq-G_d=G_dG_<d} and Proposition \ref{prop-G_d(r)G_d(s)})}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{r}y_{s})+\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{s}y_{r})+\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{r+s})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{i}x_{j})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r+s\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r,s}\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{i}y_{j})\end{multlined}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{r}*y_{s})\quad\text{(by Definition \ref{df-Ecal} (3))}.

Thus, the base case is proved.

Suppose now the claim holds for any indices with total depth less than
N
for some
N>2
.
Let
\underline{r}
and
\underline{s}
be non-empty indices with
\operatorname{dep}(\underline{r})+\operatorname{dep}(\underline{s})=N
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{r}})\mathbf{G}_{<d}(y_{\underline{s}})=\left(\sum_{d>d_{1}}\mathbf{G}_{d_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}})\right)\left(\sum_{d>d_{2}}\mathbf{G}_{d_{2}}(y_{\underline{s}})\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{1}>d_{2}}G_{d_{1}}(\underline{r})G_{d_{2}}(\underline{s})+\sum_{d>d_{2}>d_{1}}G_{d_{1}}(\underline{r})G_{d_{2}}(\underline{s})+\sum_{d>d_{1}=d_{2}}G_{d_{1}}(\underline{r})G_{d_{2}}(\underline{s})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\sum_{d>d_{1}}G_{d_{1}}(r_{1})G_{<d_{1}}(\underline{r}^{(1)})G_{<d_{1}}(\underline{s})+\sum_{d>d_{2}}G_{d_{2}}(s_{1})G_{<d_{2}}(\underline{r})G_{<d_{2}}(\underline{s}^{(1)})\\
+\sum_{d>d_{3}}G_{d_{3}}(r_{1})G_{d_{3}}(s_{1})G_{<d_{3}}(\underline{r}^{(1)})G_{<d_{3}}(\underline{s}^{(1)})\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq-G_d=G_dG_<d})}.\end{multlined}

For the first and the second summands, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{1}}G_{d_{1}}(r_{1})G_{<d_{1}}(\underline{r}^{(1)})G_{<d_{1}}(\underline{s})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{1}}\mathbf{G}_{d_{1}}(y_{r_{1}})\mathbf{G}_{<d_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}})\quad

(by induction hypothesis)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{1}}\mathbf{G}_{d_{1}}\left(y_{r_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}})\right)\quad

(by (
7
))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{<d}\left(y_{r_{1}}(y_{\underline{r}^{(1)}}*y_{\underline{s}})\right).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d>d_{2}}G_{d_{2}}(s_{1})G_{<d_{2}}(\underline{r})G_{<d_{2}}(\underline{s}^{(1)})

2.4.
The Map
\mathbf{e}

We let
\mathfrak{A}
be the ideal of
\mathcal{E}
generated by the set

Remark 2.11

At the present time, we are unable to prove whether

itself is associative.
For small values of

-algebra homomorphism.

and

immediately.

-algebra

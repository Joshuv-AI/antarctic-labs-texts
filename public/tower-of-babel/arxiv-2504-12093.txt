The Tripod High-Speed Quantum Memory As a Beam Splitter with Arbitrary Splitting Ratios
A. S. Losev

Abstract
Аннотация The utilisation of a quantum memory cell as a beam splitter with arbitrary coefficients is demonstrated theoretically. For such a beam splitter, an input-output matrix is derived. We investigate the high-speed quantum memory based on the tripod-type atomic levels and transitions.



I
Introduction

Over the past two decades, considerable attention has been paid to the realisation of light beam routers on atomic media in the tripod configuration. Such media allow a signal pulse to be split into two, separated in time
Paspalakis-2002
;
Paspalakis-2002PRA
;
Wang-2004

Unanyan-2004
;
Raczynski-2006

Moller-2007
;

and possessing new quantum-statistical properties
Wang-2004

Yang-2015

Raczynski-2007
;
Raczynski-2007PRA

Losev-2016
;
Losev-2017
;
Losev-2020

In this Letter, in continuation of the works
Losev-2017
;
Losev-2020

II
Mapping and readout procedures

We consider the interaction of a cold atomic ensemble in the one-dimensional, paraxial approximation with the signal and driving pulses of the electromagnetic field. The atoms are regarded as quasi-immobile, representing four-level systems of tripod configuration (see Fig.
1
). The interaction is considered to be resonant.

The following description will outline the procedure of mapping (MP) and readout (RP). At the point of initiation of the first mapping process, all atoms are in state
|3\rangle

The high-speed quantum memory regime is considered, wherein the times of mapping and readout are much less than the lifetime of atoms in the excited state
|e\rangle

The use of the interaction Hamiltonian in the dipole approximation and the rotating wave approximation results in the derivation of a concise system of equations that delineate the field
\hat{a}
and spin wave
\hat{b}
canonical amplitudes of interest

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\hat{a}_{1,2}(z,t)=-g\sqrt{N}\hat{c}(z,t),

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{c}(z,t)=g\sqrt{N}\hat{a}_{1,2}(z,t)+\Omega\,\hat{b}_{1,2}(z,t),

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{b}_{1,2}(z,t)=-\Omega\,\hat{c}(z,t).

(3)

\hat{b}_{1,2}

Solutions to this system for spin wave operators

\hat{b}_{1,2}(z)=-g\sqrt{N}\Omega\!\int^{T\!\!\!\!\!\!}_{0}dt^{\prime}\,\hat{a}_{1,2}(T-t^{\prime})\>G_{ab}(t^{\prime},z)+\hat{v},

(4)

\hat{v}

\hat{b}_{1,2}

It is not difficult to see that the system of equations at the readout stage, in which two driving fields interact with the medium simultaneously, will take the following form

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\hat{a}_{\pm}(z,t)=-g\sqrt{N}\hat{c}(z,t),

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{c}(z,t)=g\sqrt{N}\hat{a}_{\pm}(z,t)+\Omega\,\hat{b}_{\pm}(z,t),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{b}_{\pm}(z,t)=-\Omega\,\hat{c}(z,t).

(7)

Here

\hat{b}_{+}=(\Omega_{1}^{r}\,\hat{b}_{1}+\Omega_{2}^{r}\,\hat{b}_{2})/\Omega,

III
The full memory cycle eigenmodes

The process can be analysed by examining the eigenmodes of the integral operator of the full memory cycle
Tikhonov-2014
:

\int^{T\!\!\!\!\!\!}_{0}dt^{\prime}G(t,t^{\prime})\varphi_{i}(t^{\prime})=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\,\varphi_{i}(t).

(15)

Here
\varphi_{i}(t)
are eigenfunctions and
\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}
are eigenvalues. The functions form the complete orthonormal set:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T\!\!\!\!\!\!}dt\,\varphi_{i}^{*}(t)\varphi_{j}(t)=\delta_{ij},

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}^{*}(t)\varphi_{i}(t^{\prime})=\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(17)

then

G(t,t^{\prime})=\sum_{i}\!\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\,\varphi_{i}(t)\,\varphi_{i}^{*}(t^{\prime}).

(18)

It is also possible to decompose amplitudes
\hat{a}(t)
over the complete set of functions
\varphi_{i}(t)
:

\hat{a}(t)=\sum_{i}\hat{e}_{i}\,\varphi_{i}(t),

(19)

wherein
\hat{e}_{i}
are discrete canonical operators

\hat{e}_{i}=\int_{0}^{T\!\!\!\!\!\!}dt\,\psi_{i}^{\ast}(t)\hat{a}(t),\quad\left[\hat{e}_{i},\hat{e}_{j}^{\dagger}\right]=\delta_{ij}.

(20)

Substituting (
18
), (
19
) into (
13
), (
14
), and utilising conditions (16), (17), results in the following:

\hat{e}_{+,i}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}{\Omega}\left(\Omega_{1}^{r}\hat{e}_{1,i}+\Omega_{2}^{r}\hat{e}_{2,i}\right)+\mathcal{N}_{i}\hat{e}_{v,i},

(21)

\hat{e}_{-,i}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}{\Omega}\left(\Omega_{3}^{r}\hat{e}_{1,i}-\Omega_{4}^{r}\hat{e}_{2,i}\right)+\mathcal{N}_{i}\hat{e}_{v,i}.

(22)

Here
\mathcal{N}_{i}=\sqrt{1-\lambda_{i}}
is the normalisation constant.
It is possible to combine the obtained equations by means of input-output matrix transformation
\mathcal{M}_{i}

\begin{pmatrix}\hat{e}_{+,i}\\
\hat{e}_{-,i}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{M}_{i}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{e}_{1,i}\\
\hat{e}_{2,i}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{N}_{i}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{e}_{v,i}\!\\
\hat{e}_{v,i}\!\end{pmatrix},

(23)

\mathcal{M}_{i}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}{\Omega}\begin{pmatrix}\Omega_{1}^{r}&\Omega_{2}^{r}\\
\Omega_{3}^{r}&-\Omega_{4}^{r}\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

It is assumed that the transformation
\mathcal{M}_{i}
should be unitary (
\Omega_{4}^{r}=\Omega_{1}^{r}
,
\Omega_{3}^{r}=\Omega_{2}^{r}
). The matrix is parametrized with the angle
\theta_{i}
, which leads to a form similar to a usual beam splitter
Campos-1989
for fixed phase values

\mathcal{M}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{i}&\ \sin\theta_{i}\\
\sin\theta_{i}&-\cos\theta_{i}\end{pmatrix},

(25)

wherein

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{i}=\frac{\Omega_{1}^{r}}{\Omega}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}},

(26)

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{i}=\frac{\Omega_{2}^{r}}{\Omega}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}},

(27)

and

\Omega^{2}=(\Omega_{1}^{w})^{2}=(\Omega_{2}^{w})^{2}=(\Omega_{1}^{r})^{2}+(\Omega_{2}^{r})^{2}=(\Omega_{3}^{r})^{2}+(\Omega_{4}^{r})^{2}.

(28)

It can be concluded that mapping in only one mode with eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}=1
provides an exact analogue to a beam splitter with arbitrary values of the splitting ratios:

\begin{pmatrix}\hat{e}_{+}\\
\hat{e}_{-}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\ \sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&-\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{e}_{1}\\
\hat{e}_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(29)

IV
Conclusion

It has been demonstrated that analysing input field mixing processes in the language of memory eigenmodes
\hat{e}_{i}
yields the equation (
23
) similar to the equation for the beam splitter with accuracy to vacuum losses
\hat{e}_{v,i}
. Operating with only one memory eigenmode, for eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
equal to 1, makes the tripod memory analogous to a beam splitter with arbitrary values of the splitting ratios.
As shown in
Tikhonov-2014
, the selection of defined values for cell length
L
and mapping time
T
can reduce the number of eigenmodes in which the mapping and readout operations are most efficient. So in
Tikhonov-2014
the eigenvalues of only the first two modes are close to unity
\lambda_{1}=1
,
\lambda_{2}=0.9
. The eigenvalues of the other modes
\lambda_{i}
(
i=3,4,...
) are close to zero. Furthermore, an additional feature of high speed memory is that the first two modes are associated with different time intervals of readout
Tikhonov-2014
. Thus, selecting the necessary experiment parameters can allow working with specific eigenmodes of memory.

The use of quantum memory as a beam splitter has an obvious feature. The mixed fields must reach the memory cell at different times. Concurrently, the emitted fields are also separated in time. These conditions can offer a significant advantage over a usual beam splitter in quantum information protocols.

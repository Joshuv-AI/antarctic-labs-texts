Functions of bounded Musielak-Orlicz-type deformation and anisotropic Total Generalized Variation for image-denoising problems
Giacomo Bertazzoni, Elisa Davoli, Samuele Riccò, Elvira Zappale

Abstract
In the first part of this paper we introduce the space of bounded deformation fields with generalized Orlicz growth. We establish their main properties, provide a modular representation, and characterize a decomposition of the modular into an absolutely continuous part and a singular part weighted via a recession function. A further analysis in the variable exponent case is also provided. The second part of the paper contains a notion of Musielak-Orlicz anisotropic Total Generalized Variation. We establish a duality representation, and show well-posedness of the corresponding image reconstruction problem. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 26A45, 26B30, 46E35, 46E99, 49J27, 49J45 Keywords and phrases: Functions of bounded deformation, generalized Orlicz spaces, modular, Total Generalized Variation, imaging.

Introduction

Ever since the early 90s, the variational approach has been a cornerstone of the mathematical image reconstruction. Generally speaking, in its simpler formulation, this methodology can be summarized as follows: for a given bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, grayscale images are identified with functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. Starting from a datum
f
, possibly corrupted by noise, a clean image is obtained through the minimization of an energy functional of the form

\mathcal{I}(u):=\alpha\|Ku-f\|_{H}+\beta\,\mathcal{R}(u),

for
\alpha,\beta>0
. The structure of
\mathcal{I}
relies on the competition between the
fidelity term

\|Ku-f\|_{H}
, for a given normed space
H
and a forward operator
K
, intended to keep track of the original datum
f
, and a
regularizing term

\mathcal{R}(u)
, whose task is to filter out the noise and return a clean image. The strength of both effects is quantified by the two
tuning parameters

\alpha
and
\beta
.

In this paper we propose a novel regularizer based on a Musielak-Orlicz generalization of classical Total Generalized Variation functionals, and provide a first study of the associated space of functions with bounded deformation with generalized Orlicz growth.

Depending on specific applications, various types of variational regularizers have been proposed in the literature. A classical example is the ROF model, where
\mathcal{R}(u)
is the total variation of
u
, cf.
[
43
]
. A known drawback of such functional is the so-called staircasing effect, namely the tendency of producing artificially piecewise-affine artifacts in the image denoising, see
[
16
,
35
]
. In order to counteract such problems, several higher-order models have been introduced. A particularly successful one is the second-order Total Generalized Variation, defined as

TGV^{2}_{\alpha}(u):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\,dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{1},\|\mathrm{div}\,\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{2}\right\},

for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, with
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. We refer to the seminal paper
[
11
]
for a general introduction and higher-order generalizations, and to
[
12
]
for an in-depth study of the second-order formulation.

In the last few years, due to an increasing interest in accurate and adaptive image reconstruction, an emerging trend has been considering anisotropic models, allowing to tailor the denoising effects to specific features of different parts of the images. First steps in this direction have already been taken, e.g., in
[
9
,
16
,
44
]
. In particular, in
[
17
]
the authors have proposed an anisotropic ROF-type functional with
\mathcal{R}
of the form
\mathcal{R}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\phi(x,|Du|)\,dx
with

\phi(x,t):=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{p(x)}\,t^{p(x)}&\textnormal{ if }t\in[0,1],\\
t-1+\tfrac{1}{p(x)}&\textnormal{ if }t>1,\end{cases}

and with the variable exponent
p:\Omega\to[c,2]
, with
c>1
.

In parallel, the study of variational problems in variable, Orlicz, and generalized Orlicz spaces has boomed in the past ten years. Among the vast literature, we single out the contributions
[
19
,
24
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
42
]
and the references therein.

The extension of the model in
[
17
]
to incorporate the case
c=1
, as well as an Orlicz generalization for regularizers
\mathcal{R}(u)\simeq\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|Du|)
for
\Phi
-functions
\varphi
possibly having linear growth, is the subject of
[
26
]
, where a thorough mathematical foundation of the space of anisotropic functions with bounded variation with generalized Orlicz growth has also been provided. A further step in this direction has been taken in
[
28
]
, where the associated Euler-Lagrange equations and gradient flows have also been analyzed. The effect of an anisotropic norm in the Chan-Vese (or anisotropic Mumford-Shah) is the subject of
[
38
]
. Anisotropic models, at least in the ROF setting, have indeed proven to allow for more accurate computations, as well as for a staircasing reduction and a sharper texture recognition. We refer to
[
18
,
27
,
36
]
for an overview and further references.

Our contribution is twofold. First, we introduce a novel anisotropic functional setting of functions with bounded deformation, namely the space
BD^{\varphi}
of bounded deformation fields with generalized Orlicz growth. After analyzing its basic properties, we establish a link to a modular representation and provide a further study in the special case of variable Lebesgue integrability.

Second, we analyze a Musielak-Orlicz anisotropic Total Generalized Variation class of regularizers. Let
\Omega
be a bounded, connected and open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
. Inspired by
[
11
]
, for a fixed generalized weak
\Phi-
function
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
(see Section
2.3
for the detailed assumptions), we define our anisotropic Total Generalized Variation of order
2
with weight
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

\displaystyle TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u):=\sup\bigg\{

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\,dx\ :\

(1.1)

\displaystyle\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{1},\|\mathrm{div}\,\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{2}\bigg\},

for every
u\in L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi^{*}}
is the norm associated to the Fenchel conjugate function of
\varphi
, cf. Definition
2.11
, and where the divergence of order
2
of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
is defined as

\mathrm{div}^{2}\xi:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{ii}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+2\sum_{i<j}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{ij}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},

(see also
[
11
]
for higher-order notions of divergence). Note that, in principle,
TGV^{\varphi,2}(u)
might take infinite values.

A prototypical example of admissible functions
\varphi
is given by
\varphi(t):=\tfrac{1}{p(x)}t^{p(x)}
(see also
[
26
, Example 4.3]
), with
p:\Omega\to[1,+\infty]
, thus possibly describing linear, superlinear, or even infinite growth in different portions of the domain.

After studying the basic properties of
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
, we identify a dual representation, and show how classical existence and stability for classical Total Generalized Variation carry over to our Musielak-Orlicz setting.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect some definitions and technical results on
\Phi
-functions and generalized Orlicz spaces. Section
3
contains the definition and main properties of the novel space of bounded deformation fields with generalized Orlicz growth
BD^{\varphi}
. In particular, in Subsection
3.1
we establish a connection between
BD^{\varphi}
and an associated weighted-modular representation, while in Subsection
3.2
we further establish a decomposition of the modular function in terms of the modular of the absolutely continuous part of the symmetric gradient and a singular part weighted by the recession function, cf. Theorem
3.14
. Eventually, in Subsection
3.3
we specify our setting to the variable exponent case. The focus of Section
4
is on our anisotropic Total Generalized Variation. In Subsection
4.1
we establish its main properties, and in Subsection
4.2
we identify its dual reformulation in terms of a notion of anisotropic measure variation. Finally, in Subsection
4.3
we show stability with respect to the data and well-posedness of the minimization problem. In Appendices
A
and
B
we give the proofs of some auxiliary results we need for the proof of Theorem
3.14
.

2
Preliminary results

In this section, after giving some notation in Subsection
2.1
, we collect some definitions and technical results on modulars and norms in Subsection
2.2
and on generalized
\Phi
-functions and Orlicz spaces in Subsection
2.3
.

2.1
Notation

The notation
f\approx g
means that there exist two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}f\leq g\leq c_{2}f
. Given an exponent
p\in(1,+\infty)
, we denote by
p^{\prime}
its Hölder conjugate exponent, namely
p^{\prime}:=\frac{p}{p-1}
. If
p=1
, then its Hölder conjugate exponent is
p^{\prime}=+\infty
and viceversa. In the sequel
\Omega
denotes an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote by
L^{0}(\Omega)
the set of measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and we use the notation
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\ :\ {\rm supp}(\sigma)\textnormal{ is compact in }\Omega\}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We denote with
|\cdot|
the Frobenius norm for matrices, i.e.
|M|^{2}:={\rm tr}(MM^{\textnormal{T}})
where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and with
:
the scalar product associated with the Frobenius norm, i.e.
A:B:={\rm tr}(AB^{\textnormal{T}})
for
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. With the same notation, namely
|\cdot|
, we also indicate the standard vector Euclidean norm, and with
\cdot
the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We say that a function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
is a
modulus of continuity
if it is increasing and such that
\omega(0)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=0
. Note that we do not require concavity and we allow extended real values.

We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
the space of real-valued
n\times n
symmetric matrices. By
\mathcal{M}(\Omega;X)
we denote the space of all finite Radon measures in
\Omega
with values in
X
, which can be
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
depending on the situation. Given a set
O\subset X
, we denote by
I_{O}
the indicator function of the set
O
, namely

I_{O}(x):=\begin{cases}0&\textnormal{ if }x\in O,\\
+\infty&\textnormal{ if }x\notin O,\end{cases}

and with
\chi_{O}
the characteristic function of the set
O
, namely

\chi_{O}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{ if }x\in O,\\
0&\textnormal{ if }x\notin O.\end{cases}

Given a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote by
Du
its distributional gradient. We indicate by
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
the space of functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
Du\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. If
m=1
we denote the latter space simply by
BV(\Omega)
. When
n=m
, for any function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
we use the notation
Eu
for the distributional symmetric gradient of
u
, namely
Eu=\frac{1}{2}(Du+Du^{\textnormal{T}})
, where
Du^{\textnormal{T}}
stands for the transpose of
Du
. We write

BD(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\ :\ Eu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\}.

We will often indicate by
c
a generic constant, whose value may change from line to line.

2.2
Modulars and norms

In order to define the spaces we use in our work, we need to give different notions of norms and modulars that generate our spaces. Note that our terminology differs from the original one in
[
39
]
, but follows the lines of
[
26
,
33
]
in order to get a clearer connection between the modulars and their associated norms.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a real vector space and let
\|\cdot\|:X\to[0,+\infty]
be a function on
X
. Consider the following conditions:

(N1)

\|f\|=0
if and only if
f=0
,

(N2)

\|af\|=|a|\,\|f\|
for all
f\in X
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

(N3)

\|f+g\|\leq\|f\|+\|g\|
for all
f,g\in X
,

(N3’)

there exists
c>0
such that
\|f+g\|\leq c\,(\|f\|+\|g\|)
for all
f,g\in X
.

We say that
\|\cdot\|:X\to[0,+\infty]
is

•

a quasi-seminorm if and only if it satisfies (N2) and (N3’),

•

a seminorm if and only if it satisfies (N2) and (N3),

•

a quasinorm if and only if it satisfies (N1), (N2) and (N3’),

•

a norm if and only if it satisfies (N1), (N2) and (N3).

Note that conditions (N2) and (N3), equivalently (N2) and (N3’), already imply that if
f=0_{X}
then
\|f\|=0
, namely condition (N1) only adds one of the two implications.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a real vector space. A function
\varrho:X\to[0,+\infty]
is called a quasi-semimodular on
X
if and only if

(i)

\varrho(0_{X})=0
,

(ii)

the function
\lambda\mapsto\varrho(\lambda x)
is non-decreasing on
[0,+\infty)
for every
x\in X
,

(iii)

\varrho(-x)=\varrho(x)
for every
x\in X
,

(iv)

there exists
\beta\in(0,1]
such that for every
x,y\in X
and every
\theta\in[0,1]

\varrho\left(\beta(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)\right)\leq\theta\varrho(x)+(1-\theta)\varrho(y).

If (iv) holds with
\beta=1
, then
\varrho
is called semimodular. A quasi-semimodular is called a quasimodular when
\varrho(x)=0
if and only if
x=0_{X}
. A modular is defined analogously from a semimodular.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a real vector space. If
\varrho
is a quasi-semimodular in
X
, then the modular space

X_{\varrho}:=\{x\in X\ :\ \|x\|_{\varrho}<+\infty\}

is defined by the quasi-seminorm

\|x\|_{\varrho}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\ :\ \varrho\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

2.3
Generalized
\Phi
-functions and Orlicz spaces

In this subsection we give some definitions regarding
\Phi
-functions and we then define generalized Orlicz (or Musielak-Orlicz) spaces, following the lines of
[
29
]
. Since we are also going to make use of singular measures, the assumptions always need to get adjusted to hold for every point, as in
[
33
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is called
L
-almost increasing if there exists
L\geq 1
such that
f(s)\leq Lf(t)
for every
s\leq t
. If this inequality holds for
L=1
, the function is called non-decreasing. We define analogously
L
-almost decreasing and non-increasing functions.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a function
\varphi:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
is a weak
\Phi
-function, and we write
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, if the following conditions hold:

•

the function
x\mapsto\varphi(x,|f(x)|)
is measurable for every function
f\in L^{0}(\Omega)
,

•

the function
t\mapsto\varphi(x,t)
is non-decreasing for every
x\in\Omega
,

•

\varphi(x,0)=\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\varphi(x,t)=0
and
\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\varphi(x,t)=+\infty
for every
x\in\Omega
,

•

the function
t\mapsto\tfrac{\varphi(x,t)}{t}
is
L
-almost increasing on
(0,+\infty)
for every
x\in\Omega
with a constant
L
independent of
x
.

If, moreover,
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
is convex and left-continuous with respect to
t
for every
x\in\Omega
, then
\varphi
is a convex
\Phi
-function, and we write
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
. If
\varphi
does not depend on
x
, then we omit the set and write
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
or
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}
.

Note that since we deal with conjugates of functions possibly with linear growth at infinity, it is needed to allow for extended real-valued
\Phi
-functions. Now we are in the position of defining generalized Orlicz spaces.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
and define

\varrho_{\varphi}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|f|)\,dx\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{for every }f\in L^{0}(\Omega).

Then, the set

L^{\varphi}(\Omega):=(L^{0}(\Omega))_{\varrho_{\varphi}}=\left\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega)\ :\ \varrho_{\varphi}(\lambda f)<+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{ for some }\lambda>0\right\}

is called a generalized Orlicz space with quasinorm given by
\|f\|_{\varphi}:=\|f\|_{\varrho_{\varphi}}
. We use the abbreviation
\|f\|_{\varphi}:=\left\||f|\right\|_{\varphi}
for vector-valued functions.

Thanks to
[
29
, Lemma 3.2.2]
, it is known that
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
is a quasinorm in
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
if
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, and a norm if
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
.

Now we need to define some standard conditions on
\Phi
-functions that grant them additional properties, which sometimes are inherited by their conjugate functions as well.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\varphi:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
and let
p,q>0
. Then we define the following conditions:

(A0)

There exists
\beta\in(0,1]
such that
\varphi(x,\beta)\leq 1\leq\varphi(x,\tfrac{1}{\beta})
for every
x\in\Omega
,

(A1)

For every
K>0
there exists
\beta\in(0,1]
such that for every
x,y\in\Omega

\varphi(x,\beta t)\leq\varphi(y,t)+1\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{when }\varphi(y,t)\in\left[0,\tfrac{K}{|x-y|^{n}}\right],

(VA1)

For every
K>0
there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega
such that for every
x,y\in\Omega

\varphi\left(x,\frac{t}{1+\omega(|x-y|)}\right)\leq\varphi(y,t)+\omega(|x-y|)\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{when }\varphi(y,t)\in\left[0,\frac{K}{|x-y|^{n}}\right],

(aInc)
p

There exists
L_{p}\geq 1
such that
t\mapsto\tfrac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{p}}
is
L_{p}
-almost increasing on
(0,+\infty)
for every
x\in\Omega
,

(aDec)
q

There exists
L_{q}\geq 1
such that
t\mapsto\tfrac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{q}}
is
L_{q}
-almost decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
for every
x\in\Omega
.

We say that (aInc), respectively (aDec), holds if (aInc)
p
, respectively (aDec)
q
, holds for some
p>1
, respectively
q>1
.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that, by Definition
2.5
, every function
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfies (aInc)
1
.

Note that if
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, then
\varphi(\,\cdot\,,1)\approx 1
implies (A0). Moreover, if
\varphi
satisfies (aDec), then (A0) and
\varphi(\,\cdot\,,1)\approx 1
are equivalent. Assumption (A1) is an almost continuity condition and (aInc) and (aDec) are quantitative versions of the
\nabla_{2}
and
\Delta_{2}
conditions and measure lower and upper growth rates. We define the condition (A1) as in
[
26
]
. We point out that it slightly differs from
[
29
,
32
]
, where it is assumed that

\varphi(x,\beta t)\leq\varphi(y,t)\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{when }\varphi(y,t)\in\left[1,\tfrac{1}{|B|}\right]

and
x
and
y
belong to the ball
B
. Lastly, the “vanishing (A1)” condition (VA1) is a continuity condition for
\varphi
, introduced in
[
34
]
to prove maximal regularity of minimizers.

In order to state the last property which we need for our analysis, we first need to define the recession function of
\varphi
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. We call recession function of
\varphi
the function
\varphi^{\infty}:\Omega\to[0,+\infty]
defined by

\varphi^{\infty}(x):=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t}\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{for all }x\in\Omega.

Note that, since the continuity of
\varphi
is not necessarily inherited by
\varphi^{\infty}
, this makes the non-autonomous case not trivial. For our analysis, we also need the following weaker version of (VA1) where at least one of the points has to belong to the set
\{\varphi^{\infty}<+\infty\}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. We say that
\varphi
satisfies restricted (VA1) if and only if it satisfies (A1) and for every
K>0
there exists
\omega
modulus of continuity such that

\varphi\left(x,\frac{t}{1+\omega(|x-y|)}\right)\leq\varphi(y,t)+\omega(|x-y|)\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{when }\varphi(y,t)\in\left[0,\frac{K}{|x-y|^{n}}\right],

for every
x,y\in\Omega
such that
\varphi^{\infty}(x)<+\infty
or
\varphi^{\infty}(y)<+\infty
.

We now need to define the associate space of the generalized Orlicz space
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
. In general, the associate space is a variant of the dual function space which works better at the end-points
p=1
and
p=\infty
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. We define the associate space
(L^{\varphi})^{\prime}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{0}(\Omega):\|u\|_{(L^{\varphi})^{\prime}}<+\infty\}
with

\|u\|_{(L^{\varphi})^{\prime}}:=\sup_{\|v\|_{\varphi}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx.

Thanks to
[
29
, Theorem 3.4.6]
, it is well know that for weak
\Phi
-functions the associate space corresponds to the generalized Orlicz space
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
, where

\varphi^{*}(x,t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}[st-\varphi(x,s)]

is the (Fenchel) conjugate function of
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it can be shown that (see
[
26
, Theorem 3.4.6]
) for all
u\in L^{0}(\Omega)
,
\|u\|_{L^{\varphi}}
is equivalent to
\sup_{\|v\|_{L^{\varphi^{*}}}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx.

Let us state some well-known properties of the conjugate function.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Then,

(i)

\varphi^{*}\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
, meaning that
\varphi^{*}
is always convex and left-continuous,

(ii)

If
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
, then
\varphi
satisfies (aInc
p
) or (aDec
q
) if and only if
\varphi^{*}
satisfies respectively (aDec
{}_{p^{\prime}}
) or (aInc
{}_{q^{\prime}}
),

(iii)

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
, then

\varphi^{*}\left(x,\tfrac{\varphi(x,t)}{t}\right)\leq\varphi(x,t)\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{for every }x\in\Omega\textnormal{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

and
\varphi^{**}=\varphi
,

(iv)

If
\varphi
satisfies (A0) or (A1), then so does
\varphi^{*}
.

∎

Conditions (i), (ii) and (iv) have been proven in
[
29
]
, respectively in Lemma 2.4.1, Proposition 2.4.9 and Lemmas 3.7.6 and 4.1.7, while condition (iii) has been shown in
[
24
, Corollary 2.6.3]
, see also
[
29
, Proposition 2.4.5]
.

Lastly, we report the following result regarding some immersions of generalized Orlicz spaces into Lebesgue spaces.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
|\Omega|<+\infty
and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. If
\varphi
satisfies (A0), then

L^{\infty}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega).

Moreover, if
\varphi
satisfies (aInc)
p
, then
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
.
∎

Note that the second continuous inclusion comes from the fact that every weak
\Phi
-function satisfies (aInc)
1
, see Remark
2.8
. This Lemma was shown in
[
29
, Corollary 3.7.9 & Corollary 3.7.10]
. We point out that condition (A0) is essential both for establishing the above embeddings and to ensure that
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
is a Banach space, cf.
[
29
, Theorem 3.7.13]
. In particular, of (A0) is violated, the first inclusion in Lemma
2.13
might fail to hold. We refer to
[
29
, Example 3.7.11]
for a counterexample.

3
The space of bounded deformations fields with generalized Orlicz growth

In
[
26
, Definition 4.1]
, given a function
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, an anisotropic total variation (there called
dual norm
) is introduced exploiting the conjugate function of
\varphi
, namely for any
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
we have

V_{\varphi}(u):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}.

(3.1)

Moreover, the space of functions of bounded variation with generalized Orlicz growth is defined as

BV^{\varphi}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega)\ :\ \|u\|_{BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)}<+\infty\},

(3.2)

where

\|u\|_{BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{\varphi}+V_{\varphi}(u).

(3.3)

Equivalently, given
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
in
[
8
]
a vectorial anisotropic total variation is introduced, namely for any
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
we have

V^{m}_{\varphi}(u):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\,\mathrm{div}\,w_{i}\,dx\ :\ w\in\left[C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\right]^{m},\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}.

(3.4)

One can equivalently write

V^{m}_{\varphi}(u)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\mathrm{div}\,w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}),\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\},

where the divergence
\mathrm{div}\,w
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, namely taken a matrix-valued field
\xi
, it is computed row-wise, i.e.

(\mathrm{div}\,\xi)_{i}:=\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\xi_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}},

(3.5)

for any
i\in[1,m]\cap\mathbb{N}
and any
\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. Moreover, we say that
u
belongs to
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
if

\|u\|_{BV^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}:=\|u\|_{\varphi}+V_{\varphi}^{m}(u)<+\infty.

In the case
m=1
, (
3.4
) reduces to (
3.1
) and we denote
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
simply by
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
.

Note that in general Harjulehto & Hästö in their works, see e.g.
[
29
]
, use the symbol
C^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to denote the space
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For this reason, we gave the definition of the total variation with our notation instead of the one used in
[
26
]
.

Remark 3.1
.

Clearly, when
\varphi(x,t)=|t|
,
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})=BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. A priori, without assuming any other property on
\varphi
except for it to be in the space
\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, the spaces
BV(\Omega)
and
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
are not related to each other. Considering
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded, one gets the continuous inclusion
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow BV(\Omega)
assuming (A0) on the function
\varphi
, see Lemma
2.13
. The fact that
\varphi
satisfies (aInc)
1
is already included in the definition of weak
\Phi
-function, see Remark
2.8
.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
2
]
and
[
37
]
, in the simple anisotropic case, (i.e.
\varphi(x,t)
with linear behavior in

) the dual function
\varphi^{0}
is used instead of the conjugate function
\varphi^{*}
in order to define a weighted total variation (they call it generalized total variation). But under the assumptions considered in
[

and

\varphi:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a continuous function which is convex in the second variable, satisfies (A0) and such that

\varphi(x,t)=t\,\varphi(x,1)\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{ for any }x\in\Omega\textnormal{ and }t\in[0,+\infty).

Assume that there exist
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<+\infty
such that

\lambda t\leq\varphi(x,t)\leq\Lambda t\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ for any }x\in\Omega\textnormal{ and for any }t\in[0,+\infty).

Moreover, let us consider
\psi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
defined as
\psi(x,\xi):=\varphi(x,|\xi|)
for any

\psi

\psi(x,t\xi)=\varphi(x,|t\xi|)=|t|\varphi(x,|\xi|)=|t|\psi(x,\xi)

,

and every

.

In
[
2
]

and under these assumptions on

with respect to
\psi
in
\Omega

\overline{V_{\psi}}(u):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\psi^{0}(x,w(x))\leq 1\textnormal{ for all }x\in\Omega\right\},

(3.6)

where
\psi^{0}
is the dual function of
\psi

\psi^{0}(x,\xi^{*}):=\sup\{(\xi^{*},\xi)\ :\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\psi(x,\xi)\leq 1\}.

Note that in
[
2
]
they use the notation
C^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to indicate the space that we denote with
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, given a function
u\in BV(\Omega)
, the anisotropic total variation of
u
with respect to
\varphi
in
\Omega
introduced in
[
26
]
as (
3.1
), coincides with the notion given by
[

]
, namely (
3.6
).

Indeed, by
[

the right-hand side of (
3.6
) coincides with

\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\int_{\Omega}\psi^{*}(x,w(x))\,dx<+\infty\right\}.

(3.7)

Considering the even extension of
\varphi(x,\cdot)
, for any
x\in\Omega
, and since
\varphi(x,\cdot)
is convex, in view of our definition of
\psi
and Lemma

\psi^{*}(x,\cdot)=\varphi^{*}(x,|\cdot|)\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(3.8)

we have that (

\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|w(x)|)\,dx<+\infty\right\}.

(3.9)

Taking into account

\psi^{*}(x,\xi^{*})=\begin{cases}0&\textnormal{ if }\psi^{0}(x,\xi^{*})\leq 1,\\
+\infty&\textnormal{ if }\psi^{0}(x,\xi^{*})>1,\end{cases}

for every

and every

\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|w(x)|)\,dx\leq 1\right\},

(3.10)

where (
3.8

\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\ :\ \int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}\left(x,\left|\frac{w(x)}{\lambda}\right|\right)\,dx<1\right\},

we get that (

3.1
).

In order to treat the minimization problem using
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
as a regularizer, see (

defined in (

). For every

with

, we define

\widetilde{V}^{m}_{\varphi}(v):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\psi\cdot dv(x)\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}.

(3.11)

If we are working on a set
A\subsetneq\Omega
, then we will denote it by
\widetilde{V}^{m}_{\varphi}(v;A)
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that for any
u\in BV(\Omega)
, in order to know that at least that
Du\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, an integration by parts yields

\widetilde{V}^{n}_{\varphi}(Du)=V_{\varphi}(u),

where the second term is defined as in (

), namely the anisotropic total variation introduced in
[

]
.

In the following, we will either use the above notation for

or

. Note that in the latter case the scalar product coincides with the one underlying the Frobenius norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, namely reading for any

as

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(v)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\psi:dv(x)\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}.

Considering test functions in

or

is equivalent when

, since the scalar product only takes into account the symmetric part of the test function. In general the notation

could be used also for measures in

without any symmetry property, but that is not the case in this work. We avoid specifying the symmetry in order to lighten the notation.

Remark 3.4
.

Considering an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if

\widetilde{V}^{n}_{\varphi}(Du-w)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,\psi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi\cdot dw(x)\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\},

see also
[

]
. Note that it is possible to rewrite it like this by
[

, indeed having

and

.

In the same way, if

is also bounded and

, by

. Namely, in the special case

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}E^{*}\psi\cdot u\,dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\},

where

denotes the matrix divergence of order

Remark 3.5
.

We point out that, here and in the following, the set

Thanks to the definition of generalized variation associated to

in (

BD^{\varphi}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\ :\ \|w\|_{BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)}<+\infty\right\},

(3.13)

where

\|w\|_{BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)}:=\|w\|_{\varphi}+\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Ew).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded and open set and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Then
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
is a seminorm.

Proof.

We need to check that
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
satisfies conditions (N2) and (N3) in Definition
2.1
. The homogeneity property (N2) is obvious, in fact
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(av)=|a|\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(v)
for every
v\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and any
a\in\mathbb{R}
. In order to check (N3), namely the triangle inequality, let us consider
v,w\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. We have

\int_{\Omega}\psi:d(v+w)=\int_{\Omega}\psi:dv+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dw\leq\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(v)+\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(w)

for any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that
\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1
. By taking the supremum over such
\psi
we have the thesis.
∎

This result entails that if
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, then
\|\cdot\|_{BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)}
is a quasinorm. In addition, if
\varphi
verifies (A0), or if
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
, then

is a norm, see
[

, Lemma 3.2.2 & Theorem 3.7.13]
.

Remark 3.7
.

As for
BV(\Omega)
and
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, see Remark

, without assuming any other property on
\varphi
except for it to be in the space
\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, the spaces

and

are not related to each other. Considering a set
\Omega
bounded, one gets the continuous immersion

assuming (A0) on the function

, see Lemma
2.13
.

Remark 3.8
.

Since a priori we define the space starting from a function
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
, one may ask whether the space defined starting from

, always a convex

[

, Theorem 2.5.10]
or
[
29
, Proposition 3.2.4]
, are already stated with the

-dependence.

With the next example we want to show that, at least considering some

Example 3.9
.

We first remark that (

3.1
Weighted modular and associated norms

Analogously to
[
26
, Definition 4.1]
, we define the anisotropic modular, or
dual modular
, associated to the anisotropic total variation
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
, defined as in (
3.11
). In particular, we define for any
v\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(v):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\psi:dv(x)-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\,dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\right\}.

(3.14)

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded and open set and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Then
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
is a semimodular.

Proof.

We need to check that
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
satisfies the conditions of Definition
2.2
. Conditions (i) and (iii) are obvious, respectively thanks to the properties of
\varphi^{*}
and that if
\psi
is an admissible test function, then also
-\psi
is. Condition (iv) is also easy to prove using the linearity of the first integral in the definition of
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
and the fact that the supremum of a sum is smaller than the sum of the suprema.

We are only left to prove condition (ii), namely that the function
\lambda\mapsto\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\lambda v)
on
[0,+\infty)
is non-decreasing for any
v\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Indeed, if
\lambda=0
, then
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\lambda v)=0
, but we know already that for any other
\mu>\lambda=0
then
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\mu v)\geq 0
, since we can always choose
\psi=0
as a test function. Let us instead fix
0<\lambda<\mu<+\infty
. We point out that since both
\psi
and
-\psi
are possible test functions, we can also only consider without loss of generality functions in the space

\overline{T}^{+}:=\left\{\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\ :\ \int_{\Omega}\psi:dv(x)\geq 0\right\},

since the second integral in the definition of the modular is always zero or negative. Then we have

\displaystyle\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\lambda v)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\lambda\psi:dv(x)-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\,dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\lambda\int_{\Omega}\psi:dv(x)-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\,dx\ :\ \psi\in\overline{T}^{+}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\left\{\mu\int_{\Omega}\psi:dv(x)-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\,dx\ :\ \psi\in\overline{T}^{+}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\mu\psi:dv(x)-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\,dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\right\}=\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\mu v),

for any
v\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, proving condition (ii).

Since condition (iv) holds with the constant
\beta=1
, then
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
is a semimodular.
∎

Note that, analogously to the anisotropic total variation, see Remark
3.4
, considering a bounded,
C^{1}
and open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in the special case
v=Eu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
this rewrites as

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left[u\cdot E^{*}\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\right]dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\right\},

(3.15)

where
E^{*}\psi=-\mathrm{div}\,\psi
.

Remark 3.11
.

On the one hand, note that testing (
3.15
) with
\psi=0
, we get that the supremum in
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
is always non-negative. On the other hand, if
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi|)=+\infty
, then the integral in (
3.15
) is equal to
-\infty
, since the first term is finite. Here we used again
[
45
, Chapter II, Theorem 2.3]
yielding that
Eu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
implies
u\in BD(\Omega)
.

This means that test functions
\psi
with
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi|)=+\infty
can be omitted and
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
can be equivalently rewritten as

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left[u\cdot E^{*}\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\right]dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi|)<+\infty\right\}.

The analogous reasoning can be repeated for the general expression of
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
in (
3.14
).

In Lemma
3.10
we showed that
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
is a semimodular, implying that, thanks to the Luxemburg method, we obtain a seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}
. The following result relates the anisotropic variation we introduced in (
3.11
) with the seminorm associated to the modular defined in (
3.14
), similarly to
[
26
, Lemma 4.7]
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected,
C^{1}
and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
and let
u\in BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)
. Then

\|Eu\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}\leq\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)\leq 2\,\|Eu\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}.

Proof.

Since
u\in BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, the case
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)=+\infty
is excluded a priori. Moreover, if
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)=0
, then
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\frac{Eu}{\lambda})=0
for every
\lambda>0
, yielding
\|Eu\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}=0
. Lastly, since the claim is homogeneous, the case
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)\in(0,+\infty)
can always be reduced to
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)=1
.

By the definition of
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
, see (
3.12
), for any
w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that
\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1
it follows that

\int_{\Omega}u\cdot E^{*}w\,dx\leq\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)\,\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}=\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1+\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|w|),

where the last inequality comes from a general property of the Luxemburg norms, see
[
24
, Corollary 2.1.15]
. Thus, by the definition of
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
given in (
3.14
),

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)\leq\sup_{w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})}[1+\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|w|)-\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|w|)]=1,

implying
\|Eu\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}\leq 1
. This concludes the proof of the first inequality.

We next establish the opposite inequality
2\,\|Eu\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}\geq 1
, which is equivalent to

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{2Eu}{\lambda}\right)>1\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ for every }\lambda<1.

(3.16)

Indeed, on the one hand if
\|v\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}\geq 1
, by Definition
2.3
we have that

\inf\left\{k>0:\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{v}{k}\right)\leq 1\right\}\geq 1,

entailing that for any
\lambda<1
it holds
\|v\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}>\lambda
. By
[
29
, Lemma 3.2.3]
then this implies that
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\tfrac{v}{\lambda})>1
for any
\lambda<1
. On the other hand, having
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(\tfrac{v}{\lambda})>1
for any
\lambda<1
entails that, by Definition
2.3
,

\|v\|_{\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\ :\ \varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{v}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}\geq 1

directly from the definition of
\inf
. Now, by
[
29
, Lemma 3.2.3]
and thanks to the fact that
\varphi^{*}\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
, see Lemma
2.12
(iii),
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|w|)\leq 1
if and only if
\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1
, and we can immediately conclude that

\displaystyle\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{2Eu}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{2u}{\lambda}\cdot E^{*}w-\varphi^{*}(x,|w|)\right]dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\lambda}\,\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)-1>1.

This proves (
3.16
) and yields the thesis.
∎

The following result, instead, relates the seminorm
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
, see Lemma
3.6
, with the norm of the symmetrized gradient, in particular considering the norm associated to the associate space
(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}
. Obviously, in the associate space norm we test with functions in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
, while in
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
the test functions are smooth. This means that some approximation is needed, but a priori we are not able to use density in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
since
\varphi^{*}
is in general not doubling.

In order to state the result, we need the classical decomposition of the symmetrized gradient, see for instance
[
7
]
,

Eu=\mathcal{E}u\,\mathcal{L}^{n}+E^{s}u.

Moreover, let us remark the definition of the space
LD_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, namely

LD_{\rm loc}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\ :\ Eu\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\}.

The result, analogous to
[
26
, Theorem 5.2]
, reads as follows.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
and let
u\in LD_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Then

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)\leq\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}.

Moreover, if additionally
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
is dense in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
, then

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)=\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Since
u\in LD_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, it follows from the definition of
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}
that

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Eu)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:w\,dx\ :\ w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}.

(3.17)

The definition of the associate space norm (extended in a standard way to the vector valued setting), see Definition
2.11
, implies that, chosen
w\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that
\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1
,

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:w\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{E}u||w|\,dx\leq\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}.

Taking the supremum over those
w
, we conclude that
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{E}u)\leq\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}
.

Under the density assumption, namely
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
being dense in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
, we next show the opposite inequality, namely
\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}\leq\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{E}u)
. Indeed, let
w\in L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
with
\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}=1
and let
(w_{j})_{j}\subset C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
be a sequence such that
w_{j}\to w
in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and pointwise almost everywhere. Since we know that also
w_{j}/\|w_{j}\|_{\varphi^{*}}\to w
in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, we may assume that
\|w_{j}\|_{\varphi^{*}}=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. By Fatou’s Lemma, we have

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:w_{j}\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:w\,dx.

So, from (
3.17
) follows that

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{E}u)\geq\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:w\,dx\ :\ w\in L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|w\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}.

Let
h\in L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
. We can set

w:=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathcal{E}u}{|\mathcal{E}u|}\,h&\textnormal{ if }|\mathcal{E}u|\neq 0,\\
0&\textnormal{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

This gives

\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{E}u)\geq\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{E}u|\,h\,dx\ :\ h\in L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega),\|h\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\}=\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}.

Hence
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{E}u)=\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{(L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}))^{\prime}}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In the second part of Lemma
3.13
we assume that
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
is dense in
L^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. If
\varphi^{*}
satisfies (A0) and (aDec), then this holds automatically by
[
29
, Theorem 3.7.15]
(note that this theorem is stated for scalar functions, but the vectorial case can be deduced working components-wise thanks to the equivalence of euclidean norms in spaces of finite dimension). We know already that, by Lemma
2.12
(ii) & (iv),
\varphi^{*}
satisfies these conditions if and only if
\varphi
satisfies (A0) and (aInc), meaning that it is not clear whether this density is there or not in the subsets where the function
\varphi
is linear.

3.2
Explicit expression of the anisotropic modular

In this subsection we derive an explicit formula for the dual modular
\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}
in terms of the modular
\varrho_{\varphi}
of the absolutely continuous part of the symmetric gradient and its singular part with weight given by the recession function, see Definition
2.9
. In particular, analogously to
[
26
, Theorem 6.4]
, the main result of the section reads as follows.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega\times[0,+\infty))
satisfy (A0), (aDec) and restricted (VA1). If
u\in BD(\Omega)
, then

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)=\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|)+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|.

∎

The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.14
, when the
\Phi
-function
\varphi
does not depend on
x
, i.e. when it is a classical Orlicz
\Phi
-function.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}
be independent of
x
and satisfy
(aDec)
. If
u\in BD(\Omega)
, then

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)=\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|)+\varphi^{\infty}|E^{s}u|

in any Borel subset of
\Omega
and so

(1)

BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)=BD(\Omega)
if
\varphi^{\infty}<\infty
,

(2)

BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)=LD^{\varphi}(\Omega)
if
\varphi^{\infty}=\infty
.

∎

We observe that in (2) we cannot say that
BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)=W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega)
, see e.g.
[
6
, Definition 3]
, since the latter equality would follow from a Korn’s inequality. But, as proven in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
, the latter, for classical convex
\Phi
-functions holds if and only if
\varphi
satisfies both
\nabla_{2}
and
\Delta_{2}
conditions. Now, recall first that
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}
implies that
\varphi=\varphi^{**}
and that the
\nabla_{2}
condition for
\varphi
is equivalent, by
[
24
, Definition 2.4.10]
, to
\varphi^{*}
satisfying
\Delta_{2}
. Furthermore,
[
24
, Corollary 2.4.11]
states that
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
satisfies
\nabla_{2}
if and only if it satisfies
(aInc)
. By assumption
\varphi
satisfies
(aDec)
but not
(aInc)
, thus
\varphi
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
but not
\nabla_{2}
. As a particular case, one can consider
\varphi(t)=t\log(1+t)
, see
[
24
, Remark 1.3]
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.14
we need the following lemma, proven in
[
26
, Lemma 6.1]
, which shows the importance of the recession function
\varphi^{\infty}
, see Definition
2.9
.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
satisfy restricted (VA1). If
\psi\in C(\Omega)
with
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(\psi)<+\infty
, then
|\psi|\leq\varphi^{\infty}
.
∎

In
[
30
, Section 3 & Example A.1]
it was shown that log-Hölder continuity is not sufficient when working in
BV^{p(\cdot)}
and here, similarly, the (A1) condition (corresponding to log-Hölder continuity in the general case of convex
\Phi
-functions) is not sufficient in the previous result, as in the following ones, in view of
[
26
, Example 4.3]
. That is why we make use of the restricted (VA1) condition, which corresponds to strong log-Hölder continuity in the case of the variable exponent function, see
[
26
, Proposition 3.2]
.

In the next two results we consider the singular and absolutely continuous parts of the symmetric gradient separately. Then we combine them to handle the whole function in Theorem
3.14
. Since the proofs are similar to the ones of
[
26
, Propositions 6.2 & 6.3]
, they can be found in our setting in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0), (aDec) and restricted (VA1). If
u\in BD(\Omega)
, then

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u=\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|,

where

T^{\varphi}:=\left\{\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\ :\ \varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi|)<+\infty\right\}.

(3.18)

∎

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega\times[0,+\infty))
satisfy (A0) and (aDec).
If
u\in BD(\Omega)
, then

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\right]dx=\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|).

∎

Note that removing the assumption that
\varphi
is continuous in both variables is not trivial.

Having the two previous propositions at hands, we can now prove Theorem
3.14
, exploiting the ideas used in
[
26
, Theorem 6.4]
.

Proof of Theorem
3.14
.

Since
Eu=\mathcal{E}u+E^{s}u
and since
u\in BD(\Omega)
, an integration by parts implies that

\int_{\Omega}u\cdot E^{*}\psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\psi:dEu=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:\psi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u

for any
\psi\in T^{\varphi}
. Hence, the claim follows from Propositions
3.17
and
3.18
once we prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx+\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u.

The first inequality, namely “
\leq
”, is obvious, so we only need to prove the opposite one.

Let us first assume that
\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|)+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|<+\infty
and let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. We can always choose
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in T^{\varphi}
such that

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi_{1}-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{1}|)]\,dx+\varepsilon<+\infty

(3.19)

and

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\leq\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2}:dE^{s}u+\varepsilon<+\infty.

(3.20)

Since
u\in BD(\Omega)
and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in T^{\varphi}
, we have
|\mathcal{E}u|\,|\psi_{i}|\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi_{i}|)<+\infty
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Thus, by the absolute continuity of the integral, we can find
\delta>0
such that

\left|\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi_{i}-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{i}|)]\,dx\right|\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}}[|\mathcal{E}u|\,|\psi_{i}|+\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{i}|)]\,dx\leq\varepsilon

(3.21)

for
i\in\{1,2\}
and any
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
with
|\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}|<\delta
and

\left|\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2}}\psi_{2}:dE^{s}u\,\right|\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2}}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|\leq\varepsilon

for any
\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega
with
|E^{s}u|(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2})<\delta
.

Since
{\rm supp}\,E^{s}u
is a set with zero Lebesgue measure, we can find a finite collection of open rectangles
Q_{i}\subset\Omega
such that
|E^{s}u|(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{i})>|E^{s}u|(\Omega)-\delta
and
|\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}2Q_{i}|<\delta
. Let us now choose
\theta\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
\theta=1
in
\Omega_{2}:=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{i}
and
\theta=0
in
\Omega_{1}:=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}2Q_{i}
, and let us fix
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=\theta\,\psi_{2}+(1-\theta)\psi_{1}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Since
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is a pointwise convex combination, we get

\varphi^{*}(\cdot,|\psi_{\varepsilon}|)\leq\varphi^{*}(\cdot,\max\{|\psi_{2}|,|\psi_{1}|\})\leq\varphi^{*}(\cdot,|\psi_{2}|)+\varphi^{*}(\cdot,|\psi_{1}|).

This yields that
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi_{\varepsilon}|)\leq\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi_{2}|)+\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi_{1}|)<+\infty
, yielding
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in T^{\varphi}
. We then get that

\displaystyle\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi_{\varepsilon}-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{\varepsilon}|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\varepsilon}:dE^{s}u

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega_{1}}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi_{1}-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{1}|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\psi_{2}:dE^{s}u-c_{\theta}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi_{1}-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{1}|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2}:dE^{s}u-5\varepsilon,

where, thanks to (
3.21
),

c_{\theta}:=\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}}[|\mathcal{E}u|\,|\psi_{\varepsilon}|+\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{\varepsilon}|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2}}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|\,d|E^{s}u|\leq 3\varepsilon

by the absolute integrability assumptions and by Lemma
3.16
. By the choices of
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, namely (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we then get

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)\geq\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx+\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u-7\varepsilon.

The lower bound follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. This concludes the proof in the case
\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|)+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|<+\infty
.

If, instead,
\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|)=+\infty
and
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|<+\infty
, we simply estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx-\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|)-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|=+\infty.

We are only left to consider the case
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|=+\infty
. By the proof of Proposition
3.17
, see Appendix
A
, there exists
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that

\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\varepsilon}:dE^{s}u>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}

and
|\psi_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{\varphi(\cdot,k)}{k}
for some
k=k_{\varepsilon}
. For any function
\theta:\Omega\to[0,1]
, we have that

\mathcal{E}u:(\theta\psi_{\varepsilon})-\varphi^{*}(\cdot,|\theta\psi_{\varepsilon}|)\geq\mathcal{E}u:(\theta\psi_{\varepsilon})-\varphi^{*}\left(\cdot,\frac{\varphi(\cdot,k)}{k}\right)\geq-\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}(k)}{k}\,|\mathcal{E}u|+\varphi^{+}(k)\right),

see Lemma
2.12
(iii). Since the function on the right-hand side is integrable, we can choose
\delta_{k}>0
such that its integral over any measurable
A
with
|A|<\delta_{k}
is at least
-1
. Furthermore, since
{\rm supp}\,E^{s}u
has measure zero, we can choose
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
as before to have support with Lebesgue measure at most
\delta_{k}
and such that satisfies

\int_{\Omega}(\theta\psi_{\varepsilon}):dE^{s}u>\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\varepsilon}:dE^{s}u>\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}.

Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\theta\psi_{\varepsilon}):dE^{s}u+\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:(\theta\psi_{\varepsilon})-\varphi^{*}(x,|\theta\psi_{\varepsilon}|)]\,dx\geq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}-1.

Letting
\varepsilon\to+\infty
, the claim follows. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.3
The variable exponent case

In this section we focus on the special
\Phi
-function

\varphi(x,t):=\frac{1}{p(x)}\,t^{p(x)},

(3.22)

with
p:\Omega\to[1,+\infty)
a measurable function. Let us recall that, choosing
\varphi
as in (
3.22
) and considering the set

Y:=\{x\in\Omega\ :\ p(x)=1\},

(3.23)

we have that the conjugate
\varphi^{*}
is of the form

\varphi^{*}(x,t)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{q(x)}\,t^{q(x)}&\textnormal{ if }x\in\Omega\setminus Y,\\
\infty\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)&\textnormal{ if }x\in Y,\end{cases}

(3.24)

see
[
26
, Example 4.3]
, where
q
is the function defined pointwise in
\Omega\setminus Y
by
\tfrac{1}{q(x)}+\tfrac{1}{p(x)}=1
and with the convention that
\infty\cdot 0=0
. From now, given a set
A\subset\Omega
, we write

p^{+}_{A}:=\underset{x\in A}{\textnormal{ess-sup}\ }p(x)\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 20.00003ptp^{-}_{A}:=\underset{x\in A}{\textnormal{ess-inf}\ }p(x),

respectively
q^{\pm}_{A}
for the conjugate exponent function
q
. In the following, we will assume
p^{+}_{\Omega}<+\infty
.

Remark 3.19
.

The explicit definition of the function
\varphi
in (
3.22
) entails some additional properties other than the explicit form of its conjugate function given by (
3.24
). Indeed, it is immediate to see that
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
, see Definition
2.5
and
[
29
, Section 7.1]
. Moreover, arguing as in
[
29
, Lemma 7.1.1]
, it is easy to see that
\varphi
satisfies (A0), (aInc)
{}_{p^{-}_{\Omega}}
and (aDec)
{}_{p^{+}_{\Omega}}
, see Definition
2.7
.

The first result we prove in this subsection is the equivalent of Theorem
3.14
in this specific setting. In particular, we show that the same conclusion of Theorem
3.14
holds under less restrictive hypotheses on our function
\varphi
. Before doing so, we need to recall the definition of the modular associated to the variable exponent space when
p^{+}_{\Omega}<+\infty
, given in an equivalent way to the one in
[
30
, Subsection 2.2]
, namely

\varrho_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x)}|u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx

for any
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Now, the theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. Let
\varphi
be defined as in (
3.22
) and let
p:\Omega\to[1,+\infty)
be a lower semicontinuos function with
p^{+}_{\Omega}<+\infty
and such that
Y
is closed, see (
3.23
). If
u\in BD(\Omega)
, then

\varrho_{\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}}(Eu)=\varrho_{\textnormal{old}}(Eu):=|Eu|(Y)+\varrho_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega\setminus Y;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})}(\mathcal{E}u).

(3.25)

Proof.

First, let us observe that the claim of Lemma
3.16
is automatically satisfied in the case of
\varphi
given by (
3.22
) with no continuity required on
p
, but only under the assumption that
p^{+}_{\Omega}
is finite. Indeed, on the one hand, in view of (
3.24
) and Definition
2.9
(which is a limit in this specific case), it suffices to prove the inequality in the set
Y
given by (
3.23
), since in
\Omega\setminus Y
the recession function is
+\infty
. On the other hand though, whenever
\psi\in C(\Omega)
with
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi|)<+\infty
, then by (
3.24
) we have that
\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)=0
for almost every
x\in Y
, implying that
|\psi|\leq 1
almost everywhere in
Y
. This means that
|\psi(x)|\leq 1=\varphi^{\infty}(x)
in
Y
, which
proves our claim.

As a second step, let us remark that choosing
\varphi
as in (
3.22
), a careful inspection of the proof of Proposition
3.18
guarantees that the result is still true if we remove the assumptions therein and, again, we only require that
p^{+}_{\Omega}<+\infty
.
In fact, by Remark
3.19
, we know that (A0) and (aDec) are satisfied and this implies that we can apply
[
29
, Theorem 3.7.15]
, meaning the required continuity on
\varphi
can be bypassed exploiting the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
. Hence, we can conclude that

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)\right]\,dx=\frac{1}{p(x)}\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{E}u|^{p(x)}\,dx

for any
u\in BD(\Omega)
, where
T^{\varphi}
is defined as in (
3.18
).

We are left to prove that Proposition
3.17
holds true under these hypotheses and choosing
\varphi
as in (
3.22
), namely that

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u=\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|.

Note that the inequality "
\leq
" is immediately verified thanks to the fact that Lemma
3.16
holds under our assumptions. So it suffices to prove that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}d|E^{s}u|\leq\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u.

Thanks to the lower semicontinuity assumption on
p
on
\Omega
, we obtain that (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) hold. In particular, the second one holds for any
\beta\in[0,1]
, due to the non decreasing monotonicity of
(\cdot)^{p(x)}
. Observe that
h_{k}
is lower semicontinuous by definition (it is a lower semicontinuous envelope, see
[
21
, Chapter 1]
) and increasing in
k
. Consequently the same arguments as in the proof of Proposition
3.17
lead to the desired conclusion. Moreover, let us remark that for every
u\in BD(\Omega)
it holds

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u=\int_{Y}d|E^{s}u|=|E^{s}u|(Y),

since
\varphi^{\infty}(x)=1
in
Y
as observed before, which indeed says that
E^{s}u
is concentrated on
Y
.

Finally, following the same steps of the proof of Theorem
3.14
, since under our hypotheses the equivalent formulations of Lemma
3.16
, Proposition
3.17
and Proposition
3.18
hold, we get the thesis.
∎

Remark 3.21
.

As a corollary of Theorem
3.20
, under the same hypotheses we get the coincidence of the spaces
BD^{\varphi}
and
BD\cap LD^{p(\cdot)}
, with

LD^{p(\cdot)}(U):=\{u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(U;\mathbb{R}^{n})\ :\ Eu\in L^{p(\cdot)}(U;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\},

when

.

4
The imaging problem

In this section we finally deal with the novel regularizer based on a Musielak-Orlicz generalization of classical Total Generalized Variation functionals that we defined in (
1.1
), namely the anisotropic Total Generalized Variation of order
2
, with weight
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, given by

\displaystyle TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u):=\sup\bigg\{

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\,dx\ :\

\displaystyle\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{1},\|\mathrm{div}\,\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{2}\bigg\},

where we consider
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

In Subsection
4.1
we prove some properties of this functional, while in Subsection
4.2
we give a dual characterization of this functional exploiting the space
BD^{\varphi}
we defined in Section
3
. Finally, in Subsection
4.3
we show how the anisotropic Total Generalized Variation inherits classical existence and stability properties.

4.1
Basic properties of
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)

Following the lines of
[
11
]
, we define the space of functions of bounded variation of order
2
with Orlicz growth and weight
\alpha
as

BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega)\ :\ TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)<+\infty\},

(4.1)

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}}:=\|u\|_{\varphi}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u),

where the functional
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is defined as in (
1.1
).

Remark 4.1
.

Note that it would also be possible to define the functionals
TGV^{\varphi,k}_{\alpha}
and the spaces
BGV^{\varphi,k}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
for general
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
following the lines of
[
11
]
. Moreover, for
k=1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and chosen
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, we would have

TGV^{\varphi,1}_{\alpha}(u)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\psi\,dx\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha\right\}=\alpha V_{\varphi}(u),

where the right-hand side is defined as in (
3.1
). Hence, we can indeed speak of a generalization of an anisotropic total variation. Contrary to Remark
3.3
, which was true for
u\in BV(\Omega)
in order to know at least that
Du\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, here it is enough to take
u\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
.

In the following result we prove some basic properties of the anisotropic Total Generalized Variation.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, connected and open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0) and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is a seminorm on the normed space
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

For
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, there holds
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=0
if and only if
u
is a polynomial of degree
0
or
1
.

(iii)

For eevery
\tilde{\alpha}=(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the seminorms
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
and
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\tilde{\alpha}}
are equivalent.

(iv)

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is rotationally invariant, namely for any matrix
R\in SO(n)
and
u\in BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
we can define
\tilde{u}(x):=u(Rx)
and we have that
\tilde{u}\in BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(R^{\textnormal{T}}\Omega)
and
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\tilde{u})=TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)
.

(v)

For
r>0
and
u\in BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
, define
\bar{u}(x):=u(rx)
. Then
\bar{u}\in BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(r^{-1}\Omega)
and

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\bar{u})=r^{-n}\,TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\bar{\alpha}}(u)\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ with }\bar{\alpha}=(\bar{\alpha}_{1},\bar{\alpha}_{2}):=(\alpha_{1}r^{2},\alpha_{2}r).

Proof.

(i).
Thanks to the definition of the functional
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
given in (
1.1
), it is obvious to see that the two conditions (N2) and (N3) in Definition
2.1
hold, implying that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is a seminorm. Note that
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
is a normed space because
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
and satisfies (A0), so
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
is a norm, see
[
29
, Theorem 3.7.13]
.

(ii).
Let us denote the set of the test functions for the functional
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
by

K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega):=\{\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\ :\ \|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{1},\|\mathrm{div}\,\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{2}\}.

Let us first assume that
u
is a polynomial of degree
0
or
1
, implying that
\nabla^{2}u=0
. Then, since the test function
\psi
is in
K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
and thanks to two integration by parts, it is obvious that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=0
.

Suppose now that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=0
for some
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. For any
\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, one can find a
\lambda>0
such that

\|\lambda\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{1}\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\|\mathrm{div}(\lambda\psi)\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{2}.

Since
\varphi
satisfies (A0), meaning that
\varphi^{*}
satisfies (A0) as well, see Lemma
2.12
(iv), and since
\Omega
is bounded, thanks to Lemma
2.13
we have that
\lambda\psi\in K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
. Testing in (
1.1
) with
\lambda\psi
, we then get

\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\,dx=0\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ for any }\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),

implying that
\nabla^{2}u=0
in the weak sense. Since
\Omega
is connected, this implies that
u
has to be a polynomial of degree less than
2
.

(iii).
The equivalence of the seminorms
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
and
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\tilde{\alpha}}
can be proven noticing that, chosen

c:=\frac{\max\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}}{\min\{\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\}},

we have that
c\,K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\subset K^{\varphi,2}_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\Omega)
, meaning that
c\,TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)\leq TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\tilde{\alpha}}(u)
for any
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Interchanging the roles of
\alpha
and
\tilde{\alpha}
gives us the desired equivalence.

(iv).
Thanks to
[
11
, (A.4)]
, we know that given a matrix
R\in SO(n)
, then

\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(R^{\textnormal{T}}\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\hskip 10.00002pt\iff\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\psi}:=(\psi\circ R^{\textnormal{T}})R^{\textnormal{T}}\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),

with
\mathrm{div}^{l}\,\widetilde{\psi}=((\mathrm{div}^{l}\,\psi)\circ R^{\textnormal{T}})R^{\textnormal{T}}
, with
l=0,1
where
\mathrm{div}^{0}=id
. Hence, for
l=0,1
,

\|\mathrm{div}^{l}\,\widetilde{\psi}\|_{\varphi^{*}}=\|((\mathrm{div}^{l}\,\psi)\circ R^{\textnormal{T}})R^{\textnormal{T}}\|_{\varphi^{*}}=\|(\mathrm{div}^{l}\,\psi)\circ R^{\textnormal{T}}\|_{\varphi^{*}}=\|\mathrm{div}^{l}\,\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}},

implying that
\psi\in K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(R^{\textnormal{T}}\Omega)
if and only if
\widetilde{\psi}\in K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
. Lastly, for any
\psi\in K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(R^{\textnormal{T}}\Omega)
we have

\int_{R^{\textnormal{T}}\Omega}u(Rx)\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi(R^{\textnormal{T}}x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}(x)\,dx,

yielding
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u\circ R)=TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)
.

(v).
Note that

\mathrm{div}(v\circ r^{-1}\mathbb{I}_{n})=r^{-1}(\mathrm{div}\,v)\circ r^{-1}\mathbb{I}_{n},

implying that, chosen
\psi\in C^{l}_{c}(r^{-1}\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\rm sym})
and
\bar{\psi}:=r^{2}\,\psi\circ r^{-1}\mathbb{I}_{n}
, we have

\|\mathrm{div}^{l}\bar{\psi}\|_{\varphi^{*}}=r^{2-l}\|\mathrm{div}^{l}\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}},

for
l=0,1
. As a consequence,
\psi\in K^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(r^{-1}\Omega)
if and only if
\bar{\psi}\in K^{\varphi,2}_{\bar{\alpha}}(\Omega)
with
\bar{\alpha}=(\bar{\alpha}_{1},\bar{\alpha}_{2}):=(\alpha_{1}r^{2},\alpha_{2}r)
. Lastly, this yields

\int_{r^{-1}\Omega}u(rx)\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi(x)\,dx=r^{-n}\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi(r^{-1}x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\bar{\psi}(x)\,dx,

implying
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u\circ r\,\mathbb{I}_{n})=r^{-n}\,TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\bar{\alpha}}(u)
.
∎

Note that it is possible to prove that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is weakly lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak
L^{p}
convergence, as well as to the weak* convergence in
L^{\infty}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Then,

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is weakly lower semicontinuous in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p<+\infty
, as well as weak* lower semicontinuous in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Proof.

We only prove the lemma for
p\in[1,+\infty)
, as the case
p=+\infty
is analogous.

Chosen
\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
with
\|\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{1}
and
\|\mathrm{div}\,\psi\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{2}
as in (
1.1
), then
\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then, chosen
(u_{k})_{k}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
we get

\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\,dx=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\,\mathrm{div}^{2}\psi\,dx\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u_{k}).

The thesis follows by taking the supremum over all such
\psi
.
∎

The next proposition is the equivalent of
[
11
, Proposition 3.5]
in our setting, proving that
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
is a Banach space.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, connected and open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0). Then, for any weight
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
the space
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
is a Banach space if endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition
4.2
(iii), we know that all the seminorms
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
, respectively norms
\|\cdot\|_{BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}}
, are equivalent for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, so we only need to prove that
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
is complete for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Note that, since
\Omega
is bounded and
\varphi
satisfies (A0), then by Lemma
2.13
we have
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
. This, together with Lemma
4.3
, implies that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is a lower semicontinuous functional with respect to
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
for any
\alpha
and
\varphi
chosen as in the hypotheses.

Now let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a Cauchy sequence in
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
. This yields that
(u_{n})_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence also in
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, implying the existence of a limit
u\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
such that

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u_{n}),

thanks to the lower semicontinuity of the functional. As a consequence,
u\in BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
and the only thing left to be shown is that
u
is the limit in the corresponding norm as well. In order to do so, let us choose
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
\bar{n}\geq n
it holds that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u_{n}-u_{\bar{n}})\leq\varepsilon
. Thanks again to the lower semicontinuity of the functional (with respect to the
L^{1}
weak convergence), letting
\bar{n}\to+\infty
we then get

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u_{n}-u)\leq\liminf_{\bar{n}\to+\infty}TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u_{n}-u_{\bar{n}})\leq\varepsilon,

implying that
u_{n}\to u
in
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
.
∎

4.2
Dual formulation

The first step in order to treat minimization problems in image denoising with this new regularizer is to prove an equivalent result to
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
, namely proving that (
1.1
) can be rewritten equivalently as a minimization problem which can be interpreted as an optimal balancing between the first and second derivative of
u
in terms of “sparse” penalization, see
[
12
, Remark 3.2]
. In
[
12
]
this was done via the Radon norm (i.e. the norm in
\mathcal{M}
associated to the Radon measure
Du
), while here we need to use the variant of the generalized variation associated to
\varphi
defined in (
3.11
). Given the anisotropic nature of such regularizers, we expect them to better capture fine texture details in the images. The result, providing a dual characterization of
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
, reads as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected,
C^{1}
and open set. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0) and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then for any
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
we have

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=\min_{w\in BD(\Omega)}\left\{\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}^{n}_{\varphi}(Du-w)+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Ew)\right\},

(4.2)

where
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is defined as in (
1.1
) and where we have adopted the convention that the above right-hand side is infinite if
\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Ew)=+\infty
. If, additionally,
u\in BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, defined as in (
3.2
), then

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=\min_{w\in BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)}\left\{\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}^{n}_{\varphi}(Du-w)+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{V}^{n\times n}_{\varphi}(Ew)\right\},

(4.3)

where the space
BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)
is defined as in (
3.13
).

Proof.

Let us define
X_{2}:=C^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
,
X_{1}:=C^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\Lambda:=\mathrm{div}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{2},X_{1})
. We stress that

X_{2}=\overline{C^{2}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})}^{\|\cdot\|_{C^{2}}}\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 20.00003ptX_{1}=\overline{C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\|\cdot\|_{C^{1}}}.

(4.4)

Chosen
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we define

F_{1}(M):=I_{\{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi*}\leq\alpha_{2}\}}(M),\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{ for any }M\in X_{2}

and

F_{2}^{u}(v):=I_{\{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq\alpha_{1}\}}(v)-\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}v\,dx,\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{ for any }v\in X_{1}.

Since
\varphi
satisfies (A0), then by Lemma
2.12
(iv) also
\varphi^{*}
satisfies (A0) and, by Lemma
2.13
, we have that
F_{1}
and
F^{u}_{2}
are continuous in the
C^{2}
- and
C^{1}
-topologies, respectively. Then, thanks to a density argument and to (
4.4
), we have that

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=-\inf_{M\in X_{2}}\left[F_{1}(M)+F_{2}^{u}(\Lambda M)\right].

Let us remark that we can write the space
X_{1}
equivalently as

X_{1}=\bigcup_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\left(\mathrm{dom}F_{2}^{u}-\Lambda\,\mathrm{dom}F_{1}\right).

(4.5)

Indeed, if
z\in\mathrm{dom}F_{2}^{u}
then, trivially,
z\in X_{1}
implies
z\in\bigcup_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\left(\mathrm{dom}F_{2}^{u}-\Lambda\,\mathrm{dom}F_{1}\right)
and for some
\lambda\geq 0
it is always verified that
\tfrac{z}{\lambda}\in\mathrm{dom}F^{u}_{2}
. This implies that
X_{1}\subset\bigcup_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\left(\mathrm{dom}F_{2}^{u}-\Lambda\,\mathrm{dom}F_{1}\right)
. On the other hand, the converse is true since
X_{1}
is a closed vector space (and
\mathrm{dom}F^{u}_{2}
and
\mathrm{dom}F_{1}
are subsets of
X_{1}
).

Now, since
X_{1}
is a closed set, it means that also the right-hand side of (
4.5
) is closed, which allows us to use
[
5
, Corollary 2.3]
, a Fenchel-Rockafellar duality result, getting

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=-\inf_{M\in X_{2}}\left[F_{1}(M)+F_{2}^{u}(\Lambda M)\right]=\min_{w\in X_{1}^{*}}\left[(F_{2}^{u})^{*}(w)+F_{1}^{*}(-\Lambda^{*}w)\right].

Now, exploiting the definition of Fenchel conjugate, we get

(F_{2}^{u})^{*}(w)=\alpha_{2}\sup_{v\in X_{1}}\left\{\langle w,v\rangle_{Y}+\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}v\,dx\ :\ \|v\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\},

for every
w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

F_{1}^{*}(-\Lambda^{*}w)=\alpha_{1}\sup_{M\in X_{2}}\left\{\langle-\Lambda^{*}w,M\rangle_{X}\ :\ \|M\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\},

for every
w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
-\Lambda^{*}w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X_{1}}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X_{2}}
denote, respectively, the duality products between
X_{1}
and
X_{1}^{*}
and between
X_{2}
and
X_{2}^{*}
. Now, we observe that if
F_{1}^{*}(-\Lambda^{*}w)=+\infty
, then by Lemma
2.13
we find
\|-\Lambda^{*}w\|_{\mathcal{M}}=+\infty
. Moreover, since
-\Lambda^{*}=E
, we infer that for every
w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
-\Lambda^{*}w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
we have

F_{1}^{*}(-\Lambda^{*}w)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n\times n}(Ew)&\textnormal{if }Ew\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\\
+\infty&\textnormal{if }Ew\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\setminus\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}).\end{cases}

Analogously, by observing that, since
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,

(F_{2}^{u})^{*}(w)=\alpha_{2}\sup_{v\in X_{1}}\left\{\langle Du-w,v\rangle_{X_{1}}\ :\ \|v\|_{\varphi^{*}}\leq 1\right\},

for every
w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the same argument as for
F_{1}^{*}
yields

(F_{2}^{u})^{*}(w)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du-w)&\textnormal{if }Du-w\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\\
+\infty&\textnormal{if }Du-w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\end{cases}

again for every
w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus, to summarize, by classical properties of
BD
and
BV
, see
[
45
, Chapter II, Theorem 2.3]
and
[
4
]
, we have that the following characterization holds:

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)=\begin{cases}\min_{w\in BD(\Omega)}\left\{\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du-w)+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n\times n}(Ew)\right\}&\textnormal{if }u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\\
+\infty&\textnormal{otherwise in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).\end{cases}

(4.6)

To conclude the proof we observe that if
u\in BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, then by
[
29
, Theorem 3.4.6]
the above infimum problem can be considered on the subspace of
BD(\Omega)
where additionally
\|w\|_{\varphi^{**}}<+\infty
, namely
BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, see Remark
3.8
.
∎

Note that in the proof we exploited the condition (A0) on the function
\varphi
in order to have the inclusions
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow BV(\Omega)
and
BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow BD(\Omega)
, see Remarks
3.1
and
3.7
.

The following result reduces the functional-analytic setting to the space
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, see (
3.2
), by establishing equivalence of the two spaces
BGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
and
BV^{\varphi}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
4.5
, we have that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)<+\infty
if and only if
u\in BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Remark
3.3
, choosing
w=0
as a test function in the characterization of
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)
, namely (
4.2
), we directly infer

TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)\leq\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du)=\alpha_{2}V_{\varphi}(u),

(see (
3.1
)),
which, in turn, yields

\|u\|_{\varphi}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)\leq\max\{1,\alpha_{2}\}\|u\|_{BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)}.

Conversely, since
\widetilde{V}^{n}_{\varphi}
satisfies the triangle inequality, letting
w_{u}
be the minimum in (
4.2
), we deduce

\alpha_{2}V_{\varphi}(u)=\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du)\leq\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du-w_{u})+\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(w_{u})\leq TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)+\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(w_{u}).

∎

Remark 4.7
.

Thanks to Proposition
4.2
, in particular thesis (i), and to Corollary
4.6
, we have that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is a seminorm also on the space
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
.

Remark 4.8
.

Differently from the classical
TGV
-setting, it is not clear whether it is possible to show that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\|u\|_{BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{\varphi}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)

or not. Roughly speaking, this is due to the absence of a Musielak-Orlicz equivalent of Sobolev-Korn inequality. We refer the interested reader to the discussion and the references in Section
3
.

We also stress that, as it follows from the analysis in Proposition
4.9
,

\frac{1}{C}(\|u\|_{\varphi}+|Du|(\Omega))\leq\|u\|_{\varphi}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)

for every
u\in BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
.

Thanks to what we have proven in Subsections
3.2
and
3.3
, we expect also the variation
\widetilde{V}^{m}_{\varphi}
and, as a consequence, the total generalized variation
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
to have a decomposition depending on the absolutely continuous part and the singular part of the gradient of the functions
u\in BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)
. We leave this open question for future works.

4.3
Existence and stability of solutions to the minimization problem

In this section we show how our notion of total generalized variation inherits classical existence and stability properties. Throughout this section we will always tacitly assume that
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is extended to
+\infty
outside of its domain. We first prove that a Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality also holds in our setting, entailing the coercivity needed to prove the existence of solutions to minimum problems in image denoising.

To this aim, from now on we denote by
\mathcal{P}^{1}(\Omega)
the space of affine functions, as well as by
\Pi:L^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{P}^{1}(\Omega)
a linear projection.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected, open set with
C^{1}
boundary. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0) and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Let
1<p\leq\tfrac{n}{n-1}
. Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\,TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u),

for every
u\in{\rm ker}\,\Pi\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Because
\varphi
satisfies (A0), by Remark
3.1
we have
BV^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow BV(\Omega)
and so

|Du-w|(\Omega)\leq\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du-w).

Analogously, thanks to Remark
3.7
we get
BD^{\varphi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow BD(\Omega)
, entailing

|Ew|(\Omega)\leq\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n\times n}(Ew),

for every
w\in BD(\Omega)
. In view of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1 & Proposition 4.1]
, we infer the existence of a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\,TGV^{2}_{\alpha}(u)\leq c\left[\alpha_{2}|Du-w|(\Omega)+\alpha_{1}|Ew|(\Omega)\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\alpha_{2}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n}(Du-w)+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{V}_{\varphi}^{n\times n}(Ew)\right],

for every
w\in BD(\Omega)
. The thesis follows then by Theorem
4.5
.
∎

In view of the Lemma
4.3
, we deduce existence of minimizers whenever
TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}
is augmented by suitable fidelity terms. In what follows, given two Hilbert spaces
X
and
H
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(X;H)
the set of linear and continuous operators between
X
and
H
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected, open set with
C^{1}
boundary. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0) and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Let
1<p\leq\tfrac{n}{n-1}
. Let further
H
be an Hilbert space, and let
K\in\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\Omega);H)
be injective on
\mathcal{P}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, for every
f\in H
, the minimum problem

\min_{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{2}\|Ku-f\|_{H}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u)

(4.7)

has a solution.

Proof.

Arguing exactly as in the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
, in view of Propositions
4.2
and
4.9
, we find that any minimizing sequence for (
4.7
) is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p\leq\tfrac{n}{n-1}
. The thesis follows then by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

We conclude this section by showing stability of minimizers of (
4.7
) with respect to the fidelity datum
f
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected, open set with
C^{1}
boundary. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy (A0) and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Let
1<p\leq\tfrac{n}{n-1}
. Let
H
be an Hilbert space and let
K\in\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\Omega);H)
be injective on
\mathcal{P}^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
(f_{k})_{k}\subset H
be such that
f_{k}\to f
strongly in
H
. Then, setting

\mathcal{F}_{k}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|Ku-f_{k}\|_{H}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{F}_{0}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|Ku-f\|_{H}+TGV^{\varphi,2}_{\alpha}(u),

for every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we have that
(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k}

\Gamma
-converges to
\mathcal{F}_{0}
with respect to the weak
L^{p}
-topology.

Proof.

The limsup inequality is immediately verified by considering for each
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
the constant recovery sequence
\bar{u}_{k}=u
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. To show the liminf inequality, let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and let
(u_{k})_{k}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
be such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. The fact that

\mathcal{F}_{0}(u)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(u_{k})

is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.11
we infer convergence of the associated minimizers.

Corollary 4.12
.

Under the same hypotheses of Theorem
4.11
, we have

(i)

\min\mathcal{F}_{k}\to\min\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

(ii)

Letting
u_{k}\in\textnormal{argmin }\mathcal{F}_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
(u_{k})_{k}
is precompact in
L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(\Omega)
, and that every limiting point
u
satisfies
u\in\textnormal{argmin }\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

Proof.

The result follows from the Fundamental Theorem of Gamma-convergence, cf.
[
10
]
, by combining Theorem
4.11
and Proposition
4.9
.
∎

Appendix A
Proofs of the results in Subsection
3.2

In Proposition
3.17
and Proposition
3.18
we consider respectively the singular and absolutely continuous parts of the symmetric gradient separately and we then combine them to handle the whole function in Theorem
3.14
.

In order to prove Proposition
3.17
, we need the following result, analogous to
[
26
, Lemma 3.5]
, which holds a characterization of the singular part of the symmetric gradient of our function.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, connected and open set. If
u\in BD(\Omega)
, then

|E^{s}u|(\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\ :\ \psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),\,|\psi|\leq 1\right\}.

Proof.

By the definitions of total variation of a measure and of weak derivative, together with the fact that
Eu=\mathcal{E}u+E^{s}u
, we see that

\displaystyle|Eu|(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),|\psi|\leq 1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\psi:d\mathcal{E}u+\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),|\psi|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\psi:d\mathcal{E}u+\sup_{\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}),|\psi|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{E}u|(\Omega)+|E^{s}u|(\Omega).

Since
|\mathcal{E}u|(\Omega)+|E^{s}u|(\Omega)=|Eu|(\Omega)
as
\mathcal{E}
and
E^{s}
are mutually singular, each inequality has to be an equality, and so the claim follows.
∎

We can now give the proof of Proposition
3.17
, following the lines of
[
26
, Proposition 6.2]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.17
.

By the definition of the total variation of a measure, Lemma
3.16
and the definition of
T^{\varphi}
, see (
3.18
), we have

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u\leq\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}|\psi|\,d|E^{s}u|\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\prime}_{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|.

In order to tackle the opposite inequality, let us define
h_{k}:\Omega\to[0,+\infty]
by

h_{k}(x):=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\inf_{y\in B(x,r)}\frac{\varphi(y,k)}{k}\hskip 20.00003pt\textnormal{ for any }x\in\Omega.

Notice that
h_{k}
is lower semicontinuous with

h_{k}\leq\tfrac{\varphi(\cdot,k)}{k}\leq\varphi^{\infty}.

(A.1)

From the first inequality it follows that
\varphi^{*}(\cdot,h_{k})\leq\varphi(\cdot,k)
, implying that
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(h_{k})\leq\varrho_{\varphi}(k)<+\infty
since
\varphi
satisfies (A0) and (aDec), and
\Omega
is bounded.

Let us now remark that
h_{k}\to\varphi^{\infty}
as
k\to+\infty
. Indeed, if
\varphi^{\infty}(x)=+\infty
, then we can use (A1) in all sufficiently small balls, since
\varphi^{+}(k)<+\infty
, to conclude that

h_{k}(x)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\inf_{y\in B(x,r)}\frac{\varphi(y,k)}{k}\geq\frac{\varphi(x,\beta k)-1}{k}\to\beta\varphi^{\infty}(x)=+\infty

(A.2)

as
k\to+\infty
. If instead
\varphi^{\infty}(x)<+\infty
, then we use the same inequality but now with
\beta:=\tfrac{1}{1+\omega(r)}
, where
\omega
is the modulus of continuity coming from the restricted (VA1) condition, and we obtain the desired convergence as
\beta\to 1^{-}
.

Note that
h_{k}
is increasing in
k
since
\varphi
is convex. Then, by monotone convergence,

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}h_{k}\,d|E^{s}u|.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
and assume that
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|<+\infty
. Then, we can find
h=h_{k}
and
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}h\,d|E^{s}u|+\varepsilon\leq\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{K}\,\chi_{\{h>\tfrac{j}{K}\}}\,d|E^{s}u|+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}|E^{s}u|\left(\left\{h>\frac{j}{K}\right\}\right)+2\varepsilon.

Since
h
is lower semicontinuous, then
\{h>\tfrac{j}{K}\}
is open, and hence by Lemma
A.1
we can choose
\psi_{j}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
with
|\psi_{j}|\leq 1
such that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}\int_{\{h>\tfrac{j}{K}\}}\psi_{j}:dE^{s}u+3\varepsilon=\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}\frac{1}{K}\psi_{j}\right):dE^{s}u+3\varepsilon.

Defining
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}\tfrac{1}{K}\psi_{j}
, we can notice that
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and

|\psi_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}|\psi_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K^{2}}\chi_{\{h>\frac{j}{K}\}}\leq h,

giving
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(|\psi_{\varepsilon}|)<+\infty
. Therefore
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in T^{\varphi}
and

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|\leq\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\varepsilon}:dE^{s}u+3\varepsilon\leq\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u+3\varepsilon.

The upper bound then follows letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. If instead
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\infty}\,d|E^{s}u|=+\infty
, then a similar argument gives
\tfrac{1}{3\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}\psi:dE^{s}u
and the claim again follows.
∎

Lastly, we now give the proof of Proposition
3.18
, following the lines of
[
26
, Proposition 6.3]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.18
.

The upper bound follows directly from Young’s inequality, indeed

\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}u:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\mathcal{E}u|)\,dx=\varrho_{\varphi}(|\mathcal{E}u|).

In order to prove the lower bound let us choose
g_{i}\in C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\cap L^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that
g_{i}\to\mathcal{E}u
pointwise a.e and in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Then Fatou’s Lemma and
L^{1}
-convergence yield

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\mathcal{E}u|)\,dx\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|g_{i}|)\,dx\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}g_{i}:\psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}u:\psi\,dx

for any
\psi\in T^{\varphi}
. Thus, it suffices to show that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|g|)\,dx\leq\sup_{\psi\in T^{\varphi}}\int_{\Omega}[g:\psi-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx

for any
g\in C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\cap L^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Moreover, replacing
\psi
by
\frac{g}{\varepsilon+|g|}|\psi|
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we see that
g:\frac{g}{\varepsilon+|g|}|\psi|\to|g|\,|w|
pointwise. Thus, by monotone convergence, the vector-values of
g
and
\psi
are irrelevant and we only need to show that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|g|)\,dx\leq\sup_{\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}[|g\psi|-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx

(A.3)

for any
g\in C(\Omega)\cap L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
. We can also exclude test functions such that
\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(\psi)=+\infty
, see Remark
3.11
.

Now let us denote with
\varphi^{\prime}
the left-derivative of
\varphi
with respect to the second variable. Then
\varphi^{\prime}
is left-continuous and
\varphi(x,s)\geq\varphi(x,s_{0})+\varphi^{\prime}(x,s_{0})(s-s_{0})
by convexity. For any
\varepsilon>0
, let us define

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,\max\{\tau,0\})\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}(t-\tau)\,d\tau=(\varphi*_{t}\zeta_{\varepsilon})(x,t),

(A.4)

where
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
is a mollifier in
\mathbb{R}
with support in
[0,\varepsilon]
and where
*_{t}
denotes the convolution only in the second variable. Since
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
are increasing in the second variable and left-continuous, we have that
\psi_{\varepsilon}\nearrow\varphi
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\nearrow\varphi^{\prime}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Moreover,
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=\varphi*_{t}\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
is continuous in both
x
, since
\varphi
is, and
t
, as a convolution with a smooth function.

Let
v_{i}\in C_{c}(\Omega)
with
0\leq v_{i}\leq 1
and
v_{i}\nearrow 1
as

. By uniform continuity in
{\rm supp}\,v_{i}
, we can choose
\delta=\delta_{\varepsilon,i}>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x,|g(x)|\,v_{i}(x))-\varepsilon\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y,|g(y)|\,v_{i}(y))

for all
x\in B(y,\delta)
and
y\in\Omega
. Then

\psi_{\varepsilon,i}:=\max\{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\,\cdot\,,|g|\,v_{i})-\varepsilon,0\}*_{x}\eta_{\delta}\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\cdot,|g|)\leq\varphi^{\prime}(\cdot,|g|),

where
\eta_{\delta}
is a mollifier in
\mathbb{R}
with support in
[0,\delta]

\psi_{\varepsilon,i}\to\varphi^{\prime}(\cdot,|g|)
, so we conclude by Fatou’s Lemma that

\int_{\Omega}|g|\,\varphi^{\prime}(x,|g|)\,dx\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty,\,\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|g\,\psi_{\varepsilon,i}|\,dx.

\varphi
satisfies (A0) and (aDec), we see that

\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{\varepsilon,i}|)\leq\varphi^{*}(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x,|g|))\leq|g|\,\varphi^{\prime}(x,|g|)\leq c\,\varphi(x,|g|),

where

is a constant. Since

and
\varphi
satisfies (aDec), then
\varrho_{\varphi}(g)<+\infty
. Thus, dominated convergence with upper bound

yields

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x,|g|))\,dx=\lim_{i\to+\infty,\,\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{\varepsilon,i}|)\,dx.

Since
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is a valid test function and
\varphi^{\prime}(\cdot,|g|)<+\infty

, the last equality, together with “Young’s equality”, see
[
40

, implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),\,\varrho_{\varphi^{*}}(\psi)<+\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[|g\psi|-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi|)]\,dx\geq\liminf_{i\to+\infty,\,\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}[|g|\,|\psi_{\varepsilon,i}|-\varphi^{*}(x,|\psi_{\varepsilon,i}|)]\,dx

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[|g|\,\varphi^{\prime}(x,|g|)-\varphi^{*}(x,\varphi^{\prime}(x,|g|))]\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|g|)\,dx,

A.3
) and completing the proof of the lower bound.
∎

Remark A.2
.

The natural idea would be to choose

Appendix B
A Convex Analysis lemma

The following result is presented for readers convenience and it is due to
[
1
]
, see Lemma 2.33 therein. It has been used in Section
3
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an even and convex function, and define
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
as
f(x):=\phi(\|x\|_{X})
. Then

f^{*}(x^{*})=\phi^{*}(\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}})

where
X^{*}
denotes the dual of
X
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X^{*}}
.

Proof.

By definition

f^{*}(x^{*})=\sup_{x\in X}[\langle x^{*},x\rangle-f(x)]=\sup_{x\in X}[\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\phi(\|x\|_{X})],

which can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle f^{*}(x^{*})

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=t}[\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\phi(t)]

(B.1)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{\left[\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=t}\langle x^{*},x\rangle\right]-\phi(t)\right\}=\sup_{t\geq 0}[t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(t)].

On the other hand, by definition

\phi^{*}(\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}})=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}[t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(t)],

(B.2)

hence it suffices to prove that (
B.1
) coincides with (
B.2
), i.e. that it suffices to take the supremum on nonnegative real numbers. Using the fact that
\phi
is even, for any
t>0
we have

-t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(-t)=-t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(t)\leq t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(t),

hence

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}[t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(t)]=\sup_{t\geq 0}[t\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}-\phi(t)],

which concludes the proof.
∎

Updated data of CLL1 CAR-T cell therapy in adult patients with relapsed/refractory acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Xiaomei Zhang, Wenyi Lu, Wenjun Zhang, Xiaoyuan He, Yu Zhang, Xin Jin, Meng Zhang, Yingshuai Wang, Xuwen Guan, Rui Zhang, Bing Wang, Hairong Lyu, Xia Xiao, Xue Bai, Yedi Pu, Juanxia Meng, Haibo Zhu, Zhao Wang, Huan Zhang, Cuicui Lyu, Hongkai Zhang, Aibin Liang, Mingfeng Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
CLL1-targeted chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cell therapy has shown clinically meaningful activity in relapsed/refractory acute myeloid leukemia (R/R AML). This updated phase I study enrolled 38 adults with R/R AML to evaluate the safety and efficacy of this treatment according to the prespecified protocol. Treatment-related adverse events included grade 3/4 cytokine release syndrome (CRS) in 17 patients (44.74%) and grade 4 immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) in 1 patient (2.63%). Persistent cytopenia (all grades) was observed in all patients, with a median time to neutrophil recovery of 45 days. At a median follow-up of 24.87 months, the objective response rate (ORR) was 73.68% (28/38), and the minimal residual disease-negative complete response rate (MRD−CR) was 42.11%. Median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were 9 months (95%CI 3.03–14.47) and 12.17 months (95%CI 3.03–24.87), respectively. The two-year PFS and OS rates were 47.94% (95%CI: 32.00%-63.83%) and 51.43% (95%CI 35.51%-67.32%), respectively. These results indicate a clinically relevant antitumor efficacy and a manageable safety profile of CLL1-targeted CAR-T cell therapy in adults with R/R AML.Registry: www.chictr.org.cn, TRN: ChiCTR2000041054, Registration date: 17 December 2020.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13045-025-01764-5.

To the editor

The prognosis for individuals with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (R/R AML) remains dismal. Chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cell targeting AML-associated antigens demonstrate suboptimal efficacy in AML. CLL1 (C-type lectin-like molecule 1), a type II transmembrane glycoprotein, is highly expressed on AML blasts but absent from normal hematopoietic stem cells [1]. We previously reported outcomes of 10 adult R/R AML patients who were treated with CLL1 CAR-T cells, where seven achieved complete remission (CR) [2]. In this expanded cohort study with prolonged follow-up, we evaluated long-term outcomes of CLL1-targeted CAR-T therapy in R/R AML. The study design was detailed in Additional file 1.

Patient characteristics

From January 2021 to May 2023, 38 R/R AML patients were treated with CLL-1 CAR-T cells. The median age was 38 (range,18–73; 52.6% male and 47.4% female), patients had received a median of 5 prior lines of therapy. 27 (71.05%) patients relapsed after multiple therapies, and 12 (31.58%) of them have received hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). 17 (44.74%) patients had FLT3-ITD mutation, four (10.53%) patients had IDH1/IDH2 mutation, two (5.26%) patients had TP53 mutation, 3 (7.89%) patients had MLL gene rearrangement and 6 (15.79%) patients had MDS-related genetic mutations. 13 (34.21%) patients had complex karyotype. 15 patients manifesting extramedullary disease (EMD). Baseline characteristics were summarized in Additional file 2 and Additional file 8. All participants who received CLL1 CAR-T cell were followed up until May 15, 2025, with a median follow-up time of 24.87 months.

Safety

Treatment-related adverse events were detailed in Additional file 3. Fever occurred in 35 patients (92.10%) following CLL1 CAR-T cell infusion, with onset at 4–14 days post-infusion (Fig. 1a). All 38 patients developed cytokine release syndrome (CRS), with 15 experiencing grade 3 CRS. Two patients experienced grade 4 CRS, which were resolved following additional interventions including rituximab and plasma exchange, alongside standard therapy (Fig. 1b). Notably, the incidence of severe CRS was higher in patients who achieved a response. Additionally, seven patients (18.42%) developed immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) (Fig. 1c). Among them, one patient developed grade 4 ICANS, he achieved CR 14 days post CAR-T cell infusion but ultimately died due to severe ICANS. The most common ≥ 3 grade AEs were hematological abnormalities, including neutropenia (n = 37, 97.37%), leukopenia (n = 36, 94.74%), lymphopenia (n = 37, 97.37%), thrombocytopenia (n = 38, 100%) and anemia (n = 36, 94.74%). (Fig. 1d & Additional file 3). In the non-HSCT group, four patients developed infections due to severe granulocytopenia. Three died of fatal sepsis, while one, despite persistent low neutrophil counts (Additional file 4 A), recovered after active anti-infective therapy. By contrast, patients receiving bridging HSCT showed a marked increase in post-transplant neutrophil peaks compared to pre-transplant levels (Additional file 4B).

Efficacy

The objective response rate (ORR) was 73.68% (95%CI 59.00%−88.41%), comprising 16 cases with MRD− CR (42.11%, 95%CI 25.74%−58.60%), and 12 cases with MRD positivity (MRD+) CR (31.57%, 95%CI 16.13%−47.06%) (Fig. 2a). The ORR of patients with extramedullary infiltration was 60% (9/15), with five of them achieved MRD− CR. As for the data cut-off, the median progression free survival (PFS) was 9 (95%CI 3.03–14.47) months, while median overall survival (OS) was 12.17 (95%CI 3.03–24.87) months (Fig. 2b). The rates of two-year PFS and OS were 47.94% (95%CI: 32.00%−63.83%) and 51.43% (95%CI 35.51%−67.32%), respectively. In subgroup analysis of dose levels (DL1-DL4), no statistically significant differences were observed in two-year OS or PFS rates (Additional file 5 A).

Of the 19 patients (7 with MRD+ CR and 12 with MRD− CR) who proceeded to allogeneic HSCT, 18 (94.73%) achieved MRD− CR following transplantation. Compared with non-transplanted patients, those receiving HSCT had significantly longer median OS (26.25 vs. 6.66 months; p < 0.0001) and PFS (25.16 vs. 6.66 months; p < 0.0001). Seven patients experienced relapses after transplantation, including two patients exhibiting extramedullary relapses (spleen, CNS). The median time from transplantation to recurrence was 6.53 months (range: 3.07–23.29 months) (Additional file 5B).

Biomarker analysis

Serum biomarkers including IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ increased in most patients during CAR-T cells infusion (Additional file 6 A). The maximum secretion of IL-6 (p = 0.0003) and IFN-γ (p = 0.01) was significantly higher in responders versus non-responders. The expansion of CLL1 CAR-T cells is shown in Additional file 6B. Responders exhibited a significantly greater peak CAR-T cell expansion proportion than non-responders (Additional file 6 C). Moreover, serum levels of IL-6 and IFN-γ correlated positively with the peak expansion of CLL1 CAR-T cells in vivo (Additional file 6D). Single cell transcriptome sequencing revealed higher proportions of proliferating and naïve T cells in responders, particularly within the CD8+T cell subset, suggesting that effective CAR-T cell infusion is associated with robust expansion and enhanced activation capacity (Additional file 7).

This pioneering study demonstrates that CLL1 CAR-T therapy confers promising long-term efficacy, with superior PFS and OS over historical benchmarks in R/R AML [3, 4]. For all 38 patients had CRS, the safety profile suggests that the therapy is not superior in safety to other targeted approaches [5, 6]. It further validates consolidative HSCT as a critical strategy to mitigate cytopenias and enhance survival. While the single-center design and limited cohort preclude subgroup analyses and long-term safety assessment due to rapid post-infusion transplantation, these findings lay the groundwork for future multi-center studies aimed at confirming these results and evaluating long-term outcomes.

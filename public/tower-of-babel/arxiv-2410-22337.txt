Approximation by matrix transform means with respect to the Walsh system in Lebesgue spaces
István Blahota, Dóra Nagy

Abstract
. In this paper, we improve, complement and generalize (from Nörlund to matrix transform means) a result of Móricz and Siddiqi [ 26 ] and some statements of Areshidze and Tephnadze [ 3 ] , and (from T T (weighted) to matrix transform means) Anakidze, Areshidze, Persson and Tephnadze [ 2 ] .

1.
Notation and definitions

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of positive natural numbers and
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{P}\cup\{0\}
.
Let denote the discrete cyclic group of order

2
by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The group operation is the modulo
2
addition. Let every subset
be open. The normalized Haar measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is given
in the way that
\mu(\{0\})=\mu(\{1\})=1/2
. That is, the measure of a singleton is
1/2
.
G:=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{{\times}}}\mathbb{Z}_{2},

G

is called the dyadic group. The elements of dyadic group
G
are the
0,1
sequences. That is,

x=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots)
with
x_{k}\in\{0,1\}\ (k\in\mathbb{N}).

The group operation on
G
is the coordinate-wise addition (denoted by
+
),
the normalized Haar measure
\mu
is the product measure and the topology is the product topology. For an other topology on the dyadic group see e.g.
[
14
]
.

Dyadic intervals are defined in the usual way

I_{0}(x):=G,\ \ I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G:y=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1},y_{n},y_{n+1},\dots)\}

for
x\in G,n\in\mathbb{P}
and denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}(0)
. Intervals form a base for the neighbourhoods of
G
.

Let
L_{p}(G)
denote the usual Lebesgue spaces on
G
(with the
corresponding norm
\|.\|_{p}
).

For the sake of brevity in notation, we agree to write
L_{\infty}(G)
instead of
C(G)
(the space of continuous functions on
G
) and set

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup\{|f(x)|:x\in G\}.
Of course, it is clear that the space
L_{\infty}
is not the same as the space of continuous functions, i.e. it is a proper subspace of it. But since in the case of continuous functions the supremum norm and the
L_{\infty}
norm are the same, for convenience we hope the reader will be able to tolerate this simplification in notation.

Now, we introduce some concepts of Walsh-Fourier analysis.
The Rademacher functions are defined as

r_{n}(x):=(-1)^{x_{n}}\quad(x\in G,n\in\mathbb{N}).

The sequence of the Walsh-Paley functions is the product system of the Rademacher functions. Namely,
every natural number
n
can uniquely be expressed in the number system based
2
, in the form

n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{k}2^{k},\quad n_{k}\in\{0,1\}\ (k\in\mathbb{N}),

where only a finite number of
n_{k}
’s different from zero.

Let the order of
n\in\mathbb{P}
be denoted by
|n|:=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}:n_{j}\neq 0\}.
It means
2^{|n|}\leq n<2^{|n|+1}
. The Walsh-Paley functions are
w_{0}(x):=1
and for
n\in\mathbb{P}

w_{n}(x):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{|n|}n_{k}x_{k}}.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
be the collection of Walsh polynomials of order less than
n
, that is, functions of the form

P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}w_{k}(x),

where
n\in\mathbb{P}
and
\{a_{k}\}
is a sequence of complex numbers.

It is known
[
19
]
that the Walsh-Paley system
(w_{n},n\in\mathbb{N})
is the character system of
(G,+)
.

The
L_{p}(G)
modulus of continuity are defined by

\omega_{p}(f,\delta):=\sup_{|t|<\delta}\left\|f(.+t)-f(.)\right\|_{p},

for
f\in L_{p}(G)
, where
\delta>0
with the notation

\lvert x\rvert:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{x_{i}}{2^{i+1}}\quad\textrm{for all }x\in G.

In the case
f\in C(G)
we change
p
by
\infty
.

The
j
th Fourier-coefficient, the
k
th partial sum of the Fourier series and the
n
th Dirichlet kernel is defined by

\hat{f}(j):=\int_{G}f(x)w_{j}(x)d\mu(x),

S_{k}(f):=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\hat{f}(j)w_{j},

D_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}w_{k},\ D_{0}:=0.

Fejér kernels are defined as the arithmetical means of Dirichlet kernels and Fejér means are defined as the arithmetical means of partial sums of the Fourier series, that is,

K_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_{k}

and

\sigma_{n}(f):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}(f).

Let
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers. The
n
th Nörlund mean of the Walsh-Fourier series is defined by

t_{n}(f):=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}S_{k}(f),

where
Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}

(n\in\mathbb{P})
. It is always assumed that
q_{0}>0
and

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}Q_{n}=\infty.

In this case, the summability method generated by
\{q_{k}\}
is regular if and only if

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=0.

As to this notion and result, we refer the reader to
[
25
]
, pp. 37-38.

If
q_{k}=1/(k+1)
, we get the so-called Nörlund logarithmic mean of the function
f
.

Let
\{p_{k}:k\in\mathbb{P}\}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers. The
n
th
T
(weighted) mean of Vilenkin-Fourier series is defined by

T_{n}(f):=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}S_{k}(f),

where
P_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\ (n\in\mathbb{P}).
It is always assumed that
p_{1}>0
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{n}=\infty,

which is the condition for regularity.

In particular case Nörlund and
T
(weighted) means are Fejér ones (for all
k
set
p_{k}:=1
and
q_{k}:=1
).

Let
T:=\left(t_{i,j}\right)_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
be a doubly infinite matrix of numbers. It is always supposed that matrix
T
is triangular.
Let us define the
n
th decreasing diagonal matrix transform mean determined by the matrix
T

\sigma_{n}^{T}(f):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k,n}S_{k}(f),

where
\{t_{k,n}:1\leq k\leq n,\ k\in\mathbb{P}\}
be a finite sequence of non-negative numbers for each
n\in\mathbb{P}
.

With assumption
\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k,n}=1
, Nörlund means are special matrix transform means, choosing

(3)

t_{k,n}:=\frac{q_{n-k}}{Q_{n}}.

The
n
th matrix transform kernel is defined by

K_{n}^{T}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k,n}D_{k}(x).

It is easily seen that

\sigma_{n}^{T}(f;x)=\int_{G}f(u+x)K_{n}^{T}(u)d\mu(u),

where
x,u\in G
. This equality (and its analogous versions for special means) shows us the necessity of observing kernel functions.

We introduce the notation

\Delta t_{k,n}:=t_{k,n}-t_{k+1,n},
where
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ n\in\mathbb{P}
and
t_{n+1,n}:=0
.

Theorem 1

(Móricz and Siddiqi

Let

(4)

If
{q_{k}}

\left\|t_{n}(f)-f\right\|_{p}\leq\frac{5}{2Q_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{|n|-1}(Q_{n-2^{j}-1}-Q_{n-2^{j+1}-1})\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{j}}\right)+c\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{|n|}}\right).

Theorem 4
.

. For every

Theorem

Theorem 5
.

Let the finite sequence
\ \{t_{k,2^{n}}:1\leq k\leq 2^{n}\}
of non-negative numbers be non-decreasing for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

(9)

\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}t_{k,2^{n}}=1.

Then for any
f\in L_{p}(G)
for some
1\leq p<\infty
, we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\sigma^{T}_{2^{n}}(f)-f\right\|_{p}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{s}}{2^{n}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)+3\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}(n-s)2^{s}t_{2^{n}-2^{s}+1,n}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(2+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right).

Proof.

Using Corollary
1
, condition (
9
) and equality

\left(2^{n}-1\right)K_{2^{n}-1}=2^{n}K_{2^{n}}-D_{2^{n}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma^{T}_{2^{n}}(f;x)-

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}D_{2^{n}}(u)(f(x+u)-f(x))d\mu(u)

\displaystyle-\int_{G}w_{2^{n}-1}(u)t_{1,2^{n}}2^{n}K_{2^{n}}(u)(f(x+u)-f(x))d\mu(u)

\displaystyle+\int_{G}w_{2^{n}-1}(u)t_{1,2^{n}}D_{2^{n}}(u)(f(x+u)-f(x))d\mu(u)

\displaystyle+\int_{G}w_{2^{n}-1}(u)\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}-2}\Delta t_{2^{n}-k-1,2^{n}}kK_{k}(u)(f(x+u)-f(x))d\mu(u)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I+II+III+IV.

Based on the generalized Minkowski’s inequality, Lemma
1
, condition (
9
) and the monotonicity of the sequence
\{t_{k,2^{n}}:1\leq k\leq 2^{n}\}
, we obtain inequalities

\displaystyle\left\|I\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I_{n}}D_{2^{n}}(u)\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}d\mu(u)

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\left\|III\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq t_{1,2^{n}}\int_{I_{n}}D_{2^{n}}(u)\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}d\mu(u)

(11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right).

Combining the generalized Minkowski’s inequality and Lemma
3
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|II\right\|_{p}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}K_{2^{n}}(u)d\mu(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{n}}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}K_{2^{n}}(u)d\mu(u)

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\int_{I_{n}(e_{s})}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}K_{2^{s}}(u)d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}+1}{2}\int_{I_{n}}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}d\mu(u)

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}2^{s-1}\int_{I_{n}(e_{s})}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}+1}{2}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{s-1}}{2^{n}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{s}}{2^{n}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right).

On the other side, it means that inequality

(13)

\displaystyle 2^{n}\int_{G}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}K_{2^{n}}(u)d\mu(u)\leq\sum_{s=0}^{n}2^{s}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

is true for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

From the generalized Minkowski’s inequality, Lemma
7
and inequality (
13
), we get that

\displaystyle\left\|IV\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-2}\left|\Delta t_{2^{n}-k-1,2^{n}}\right|\int_{G}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}k|K_{k}(u)|d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=2^{j}-1}^{2^{j+1}-2}\left|\Delta t_{2^{n}-k-1,2^{n}}\right|\sum_{l=0}^{j}2^{l}\int_{G}\left\|f(.+u)-f(.)\right\|_{p}K_{2^{l}}(u)d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=2^{j}-1}^{2^{j+1}-2}\left|\Delta t_{2^{n}-k-1,2^{n}}\right|\sum_{l=0}^{j}\sum_{s=0}^{l}2^{s}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=3\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(t_{2^{n}-2^{j}+1,2^{n}}-t_{2^{n}-2^{j+1}+1,2^{n}})\sum_{l=0}^{j}\sum_{s=0}^{l}2^{s}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=3\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=l}^{n-1}(t_{2^{n}-2^{j}+1,2^{n}}-t_{2^{n}-2^{j+1}+1,2^{n}})\sum_{s=0}^{l}2^{s}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}t_{2^{n}-2^{l}+1,2^{n}}\sum_{s=0}^{l}2^{s}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=3\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}2^{s}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)\sum_{l=s}^{n-1}t_{2^{n}-2^{l}+1,2^{n}}

(14)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}(n-s)2^{s}t_{2^{n}-2^{s}+1,2^{n}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right).

Taking inequalities (
10
), (
11
), (

Theorem 6
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{P}
, let the finite sequence
\ \{t_{k,n}:1\leq k\leq n\}
of non-negative numbers be non-decreasing for all
n
and we suppose that

(15)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k,n}=1

and

(16)

t_{n,n}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Then for any
f\in L_{p}(G)
for some
1\leq p<\infty
, we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\sigma^{T}_{n}(f)-f\right\|_{p}\leq c\sum_{k=0}^{|n|}\frac{2^{k}}{2^{|n|}}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{2^{k}},f\right).

Proof.

From Abel transformation it is easy to get equality

(17)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Delta t_{k,n}k+t_{n,n}n

and a connection between
T
and Fejér means

\sigma^{T}_{n}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Delta t_{k,n}k\sigma_{k}(f)+t_{n,n}n\sigma_{n}(f).

Using (
15
), (
17
) and (
18
) we can conclude equality

\sigma^{T}_{n}(f)-f=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Delta t_{k,n}k(\sigma_{k}(f)-f)+t_{n,n}n(\sigma_{n}(f)-f)

and

\displaystyle\left\|\sigma^{T}_{n}(f)-f\right\|_{p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|\Delta t_{k,n}|k\left\|\sigma_{k}(f)-f\right\|_{p}+t_{n,n}n\left\|\sigma_{n}(f)-f\right\|_{p}=:I+II.

Then

I=\sum_{k=1}^{2^{|n|}-1}|\Delta t_{k,n}|k\left\|\sigma_{k}(f)-f\right\|_{p}+\sum_{k=2^{|n|}}^{n-1}|\Delta t_{k,n}|k\left\|\sigma_{k}(f)-f\right\|_{p}=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Lemma
9
, condition (

) and
|\Delta t_{k,n}|=-\Delta t_{k,n}=t_{k+1,n}-t_{k,n}

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{|n|-1}\sum_{k=2^{l}}^{2^{l+1}-1}|\Delta t_{k,n}|k\left\|\sigma_{k}(f)-f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=6\sum_{l=0}^{|n|-1}(t_{2^{l+1},n}-t_{2^{l},n})\sum_{s=0}^{l}{2^{s}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=6\sum_{s=0}^{|n|-1}{2^{s}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)\sum_{l=s}^{|n|-1}(t_{2^{l+1},n}-t_{2^{l},n}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{s=0}^{|n|-1}\frac{2^{s}}{2^{|n|}}\omega_{p}\left(f,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right).

The epidemiological characteristics of notifiable infectious diseases in a Yunnan hospital, China, 2014–2023
Authors: Yuanyuan Zhang, Yiwen Zhang, Fang Zhou, Shangmei Chen, Dingmei Dai, Chungang Li, Yan Duan, Min Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundYunnan, particularly its border area, has consistently been identified with a high incidence of infectious diseases in China. We aimed to determine the incidence of notifiable infectious diseases (NIDs) by analyzing their distribution in a tertiary hospital located in Yunnan border.MethodsData on NIDs from January 2014 to December 2023 were extracted from the national reporting system. Descriptive analyses of the demographic, seasonal, and geographical distributions were conducted after categorizing the diseases into four groups: respiratory, blood-borne or sexually transmitted, digestive, and natural foci/vector-borne diseases.ResultsA total of 5,760 NIDs have been reported over the past ten years. Respiratory infectious diseases (51.88%) and blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases (45.28%) were the mainly categories. The male-to-female incidence ratio was 1.84 (3,729 vs. 2,031 cases). Most patients with blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases were 40–54 years old, while most patients with respiratory infections were over 55 years old. Following the COVID-19 pandemic, the proportion of respiratory diseases exceeded others. Cases showed a clear seasonal pattern and a pronounced geographic concentration, with Honghe Prefecture alone accounting for 76.11% of all reported cases.ConclusionsOver the past decade, respiratory and blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases were the predominant categories of NIDs in this Yunnan border hospital, with a significant burden on adult males and a marked geographic concentration in Honghe Prefecture. The observed dominance of respiratory diseases following the COVID-19 pandemic underscores the need to prioritize the prevention and control of respiratory infections, particularly among the elderly.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25970-5.

Introduction

Infectious diseases continue to pose serious threats to public health and societal well-being [1, 2]. Particularly in recent years, the emergence and outbreaks of respiratory infectious diseases, such as influenza and COVID-19, have presented formidable challenges to global public health systems [3]. These diseases, characterized by rapid transmission and extensive spread, necessitate that governments and relevant agencies efficiently and accurately acquire epidemic information. In China, this capability is supported by a long-established notifiable infectious disease surveillance system. This system operates through a five-tiered network from township to national levels, mandating that hospitals report cases in real-time to a central database. This database is accessible to authorized health institutions nationwide, enabling evidence-based decision-making and timely interventions to effectively curb clustered cases and outbreaks [4, 5]. Infectious disease reporting and data analysis serve as the core elements of disease surveillance and control. These components facilitate the dynamic monitoring of epidemic trends, the early identification of infected individuals or pathogen carriers, and provide the foundation for implementing targeted containment measures [6]. With the rapid advancement of information technology, the integration of emerging technologies, including big data and artificial intelligence, has strengthened statutory infectious disease reporting systems, significantly improving the efficiency and accuracy of epidemic data collection, analysis, and processing [7, 8]. This system, operational since 2004, maintained its core structure during the COVID-19 pandemic, demonstrating its robustness without requiring fundamental changes.

To comprehensively clarify the epidemiological characteristics of notifiable infectious diseases (NIDs) in a tertiary hospital located in the southern border region of Yunnan Province over recent years, and to enhance the regional capacity for infectious disease surveillance, early warning, and control, this study made use of data from the infectious disease reporting system. Specifically, reported cases from this hospital spanning from 2014 to 2023 were collected. Employing descriptive epidemiological approaches, an in-depth analysis of the reporting patterns and epidemiological characteristics of infectious diseases during this decade-long period was carried out. The aim was to provide scientific evidence that could guide the formulation of targeted prevention and control strategies for high-risk populations and areas in the broader Yunnan border region.

Methods

Study setting

This study was conducted in Yunnan Province, a southwestern border region of China with a diverse population of approximately 46.7 million people. The province is served by a comprehensive healthcare system comprising primary health centers, county-level hospitals, and tertiary medical institutions. The study site, the No. 926 Hospital of the Joint Logistics Support Force of PLA, is a major tertiary hospital located in Honghe Prefecture. It serves as a key healthcare provider for both the local civilian population and military personnel in this border region, with its catchment area extending throughout Honghe Prefecture and receiving referrals from neighboring prefectures.

Data preparation

Cases of NIDs reported by the hospital via the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention from January 2014 to December 2023 were initially screened. The inclusion criteria were: newly infected cases that were jointly confirmed by the attending clinician, the hospital’s full-time infectious disease report management staff, and the superior institution for disease control and prevention. Only cases that met these criteria and were reported online within the time limit stipulated by the Law of the People’s Republic of China on Prevention and Treatment of Infectious Diseases were included in our study. Cases that were duplicate reports, not newly infected, or lacked key information (e.g., confirmed diagnosis date) were excluded.

Patient information including age, gender, residence area, diagnosis, and reporting time was collected, with all personal identifiers removed to protect privacy. The diagnosis of NIDs was made and reported in accordance with the national unified diagnostic criteria, which integrate both clinical manifestations and laboratory confirmation. Infectious diseases were categorized into four groups based on mode of transmission: respiratory infectious diseases, blood-borne or sexually transmitted diseases, digestive infectious diseases and natural foci or vector-borne diseases (which are maintained in wildlife reservoirs and transmitted to humans through vectors or environmental exposure).

Data analysis

Demographic characteristics (age, gender), temporal trends (by year and month), and geographical distribution (across provincial, prefectural, and county levels) were summarized. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (n) and percentages (%). The male-to-female sex ratio was calculated as the number of male cases divided by the number of female cases. The proportion of each disease category for a specific year was calculated as (number of cases in that category/total number of cases in that year) × 100%. The overall proportion of cases for a specific year across the entire decade was calculated as (total number of cases in that year/total number of cases from 2014 to 2023) × 100%. The annual case reporting rate was calculated as (number of NIDs cases/total annual hospital visits) × 100%. All numerical results in the tables reflect these calculations.

Data visualization was performed using R software (version 4.3.1) with the “ggplot2” package. Specifically, bar charts were used to illustrate the distribution of cases by demographic groups and years, and a map was created to visualize the geographical distribution.

We used Excel 2019 for initial data processing and cleansing. All original data, including disease classifications, are provided in Supplementary Data.

Results

A total of 8,371 NIDs cases have been reported by the hospital from 2014 to 2023. Among these, 2,611 (31.19%) were excluded according to the exclusion criteria, resulting in a final dataset of 5,760 (68.81%) cases for our study (Supplementary Data). For the cases of infectious diseases included, there was a total reporting rate of 180.24 per 100,000 patients. Specifically, respiratory infectious diseases accounted for 2988 cases (51.88%, with a reporting rate of 93.50/100,000), followed by blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases with 2608 cases (45.28%, 81.61/100,000), digestive infectious diseases with 110 cases (1.91%, 3.44/100,000), and natural foci/vector-borne diseases associated with 54 cases (0.94%, 1.69/100,000) (Table 1). Before 2019, the year 2015 reported the highest number of 1111 cases (19.29%, reporting rate of 336.39/100,000), while the lowest number was recorded in 2016 with only 312 cases (5.42%, 94.61/100,000). There was a notable decline attributed to the COVID-19 pandemic, dropping to 204 cases in 2020 (3.54%, 77.89/100,000), followed by an increasing trend leading to a peak of 1028 cases in 2023 (17.85%, 285.21/100,000). To delineate the specific impact on the composition of respiratory pathogens, we analyzed the distribution of all respiratory diseases (Supplementary Figure). The data reveal a notable shift in 2023, where influenza cases surged to 563, concurrently with the emergence of 170 COVID-19 cases, making them the dominant pathogens.

Demographic characteristics

Most reported infections were among males than females, with an overall ratio of approximately 1.84 (3,729 male cases versus 2,031 female cases; Fig. 1, Supplementary Data). Prior to the age of 10, case numbers were similar between males and females, predominantly involving respiratory infectious diseases. Subsequently, both the number of cases and the male-to-female ratio increased after the age of 10, reaching their highest point in the 40–54 age group before declining. Furthermore, as age increased, there was a corresponding change in disease spectrum: blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases were most common among young and middle-aged adults, while respiratory diseases were most frequent among those over 55 years old (Fig. 1A and B). Before 2020, most cases were among men, with blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases comprising the majority of cases in the general population. However, post-2020, while respiratory infectious diseases surpassed other categories, the male-to-female ratio showed a clear declining trend, decreasing from 2.34 in 2020 to 1.24 in 2023 (Fig. 1C and D).

Seasonal characteristics

Seasonal patterns were evident in respiratory infectious diseases, digestive infectious diseases, and natural foci/vector-borne diseases, with the exception of blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases (Fig. 2). Among the major respiratory diseases prevalent, tuberculosis exhibited the highest incidence in August (326 cases) and May (292), while influenza peaked in April (259) and March (133) during spring, as well as November (93) and December (94) in autumn and winter. COVID-19 infections were predominantly reported in May (48) and June (77) (Fig. 2A). Hepatitis B and C were the most common blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases and were most reported in August, with 171 cases reported in August for Hepatitis B and 94 cases for Hepatitis C. Syphilis had the highest frequency of reports in September with 83 cases, while other diseases manifested sporadically throughout the year (Fig. 2B). Digestive infectious diseases showed prevalence in spring and summer, with the highest number of cases occurring in August (23) followed by April (14). Hepatitis A virus infection was the most commonly reported disease from February to May and July to October, with 4–7 cases reported each month. Typhoid fever mainly appeared in August with nine cases, while paratyphoid fever was prevalent from August to November with two to four cases each month (Fig. 2C). Each natural foci/vector-borne disease displayed different seasonal characteristics. Brucellosis demonstrated widespread occurrence, particularly in July (8) and August (5), whereas scrub typhus epidemic months were more sporadic. Dengue fever primarily occurred during the summer months of June (1) and August (2) (Fig. 2D).

Geographical distribution

Most of the NIDs cases were concentrated in the vicinity of the hospital, with fewer instances reported at a greater distance (Fig. 3). Yunnan Province had the highest number of cases, totaling 5710, followed by Guizhou with 13 cases and Chongqing with 10 cases (Fig. 3A). The distribution within Yunnan Province revealed that Honghe Prefecture had reported the most infectious cases at 4384, followed by Wenshan with 1202 and Yuxi with 48 (Fig. 3B). Amongst the counties in Honghe Prefecture, Kaiyuan had the highest number of cases reported at 1530, followed by Yuanyang (596), Mile (461), and Jianshui (302) (Fig. 3C). Brucellosis, accounting for 37 reported cases in all the 54 identified natural foci infections, was observed to be widely distributed across Kaiyuan (9) and its surrounding areas such as Gejiu (7), Luxi (4) and Jianshui (4). Additionally, scrub typhus was primarily located in Mile (4), Jianshui (1), Yuanyang (1), Shiping (1) and Honghe (1) counties. Dengue fever was reported in both Kaiyuan (2) and Pingbian (1). Typhus fever has been founded in Kaiyuan (1) and Yanshan (1). Similarly, Japanese encephalitis was distributed over counties of Lvchun (1) and Jinping (1). A case of paragonimiasis was documented in Maguan County, whereas an imported malaria case from Cambodia was also recorded in Kaiyuan (Fig. 3D).

Discussion

This study conducted a descriptive analysis of the epidemiological characteristics of 5,760 NIDs cases recorded at a tertiary hospital in the border region of Yunnan Province from 2014 to 2023. The results indicated that respiratory infectious diseases (51.88%) and blood-borne or sexually transmitted diseases (45.28%) accounted for the majority of the disease burden, which aligns with the general epidemiological trends of infectious diseases in China [9, 10]. Notably, after 2020, respiratory infectious diseases became the leading category, surpassing blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases. This shift may be attributed to heightened surveillance of respiratory symptoms and improved diagnostic capacities during the COVID-19 pandemic [11, 12]. Although digestive infectious diseases and natural foci/vector-borne diseases collectively accounted for less than 3%, their potential for seasonal outbreaks, such as typhoid fever in August [13] and brucellosis in July and August [14], remains a public health concern, particularly in local areas with significant agricultural and animal husbandry activities.

In 2020, against the backdrop of widespread implementation of non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) in response to COVID-19[15], the number of cases sharply decreased to 204, highlighting the broad preventive effects of NPIs on the infectious diseases [16, 17]. However, in 2023, a significant rebound occurred, with reported cases rising to 1,028, of which respiratory diseases accounted for as much as 89.40% (919 cases). This resurgence may be explained by several factors: (1) the relaxation of COVID-19 NPIs, which increased interpersonal contact and transmission opportunities; (2) potentially heightened population susceptibility to seasonal respiratory pathogens following periods of reduced exposure during the pandemic (“immunity debt”); and (3) the restoration of more robust disease surveillance systems during the post-pandemic recovery phase. Consequently, the post-pandemic era is confronted with dual challenges: the potential release of a backlog of previously unreported cases and the continued resurgence risk of traditional infectious diseases amid evolving public health priorities. Our detailed analysis of respiratory pathogen shifts provides further evidence of the pandemic’s profound impact (Supplementary Figure). The pre-pandemic dominance of tuberculosis was disrupted, followed by a dramatic resurgence of influenza and the emergence of COVID-19 in 2023, highlighting an altered epidemiological landscape.

Analysis of the population distribution characteristics indicates that the overall infection rate among males is significantly higher than that among females, with a total gender ratio of 1.84. This gender disparity may be attributed to several factors. While our study lacked direct occupational data, the pattern is consistent with broader regional socioeconomic profiles where males are more frequently employed in high-exposure occupations such as animal husbandry and manual labor, potentially increasing their contact with infectious agents [18]. Thus, the observed disparity is likely primarily driven by behavioral and occupational factors leading to differential exposure, rather than biological differences in susceptibility. Among individuals aged over 15 years, blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases accounted for a larger proportion of cases in males (54.37%, 1,772/3,259) than in females (49.48%, 805/1,627). This difference may be associated with generally higher levels of sexual activity among males[19], particularly the elevated risk of HIV and other sexually transmitted diseases among men who have sex with men [20]. The age distribution showed high proportions of cases in the 0–1 year and 1–10 years age groups. The high number of cases in infants under 1 year may reflect their developing immune systems and potential exposure to pathogens in early life [21]. In the 1–10 years group, while routine vaccination provides protection against many childhood diseases, the observed cases may represent infections from non-vaccine preventable diseases, vaccine breakthrough infections, or pathogens for which immunity wanes over time. Notably, respiratory infections accounted for the majority of cases among individuals over 55 years old, which is closely linked to age-related immune decline [22] and the higher prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases (e.g., Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), asthma) in this cohort [23]. This vulnerable group has a higher risk of severe illness and death. Therefore, it is necessary to systematically integrate evidence-based response measures into chronic disease management programs, including targeted influenza/pneumococcal vaccination programs and screening for multiple respiratory pathogens, in order to intervene as early as possible [24].

Seasonal patterns exhibit notable heterogeneity. Tuberculosis, a respiratory disease, demonstrates bimodal peaks in May and August, which is consistent with the seasonal patterns observed in other studies [25]. Gastrointestinal diseases are most prevalent during the summer months, particularly in August, which is directly associated with enhanced pathogen proliferation and increased consumption of raw or undercooked food. Zoonotic diseases, such as brucellosis, tend to concentrate between July and August, coinciding with the livestock breeding season. Blood-borne/sexually transmitted diseases do not exhibit significant seasonal variation, suggesting that their transmission is primarily influenced by relatively consistent risk factors, including behavioral patterns and medical procedures such as blood transfusions, which typically show limited seasonal fluctuation. Analysis of the geographical distribution reveals that 76.11% (4384/5760) of reported cases are concentrated in Honghe Prefecture, with Kaiyuan City accounting for 26.56% (1530/5760) of all cases, where the hospital is located, underscoring the influence of healthcare accessibility on case reporting rates [26]. The spatial clustering of zoonotic diseases, such as Yunnan is the province in southern China with the highest incidence rate of brucellosis[27], indicates the presence of active animal reservoirs, occupational exposure risks, and potential cross-border transmission (e.g., malaria cases imported from Cambodia), highlighting the need for targeted public health interventions, including livestock vaccination programs and strengthened border quarantine measures [28].

Conclusions

This study represents the first effort to comprehensively elucidate the incidence of infectious diseases in the border regions of Yunnan over a ten-year period, offering vital empirical evidence to inform regional prevention and control strategies. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, the use of single-center data may lead to an underestimation of the incidence of mild cases. Secondly, the absence of pathogen molecular typing data hinders the accurate tracing of transmission chains. In the end, social, economic, and environmental confounding factors were not subjected to quantitative analysis. Future research should integrate multi-center surveillance with genomic technologies to further explore the underlying mechanisms.

Temporal Trends and Projected Burden of Bone and Articular Cartilage Malignancies in East Asia: A Systematic Analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023
Authors: Karlen Ka Pui Law, Alex Kwan Ho Yau, Henry Chung Ngai Chan, Angela On Kei Lai, Agnes King Yan Lam, Kenney Ki Lee Lau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMalignant neoplasm of bone and articular cartilage (MNBAC) is a rare malignancy associated with substantial premature mortality and disability, particularly among younger populations. Comprehensive, region‑specific assessments in East Asia remain limited despite evolving demographic and epidemiologic patterns. Over the same period, age-standardized incidence rates per 100,000 population increased modestly, death rates declined from 3.60 to 3.09, and DALY rates decreased from 138.61 to 112.04. Introduction

Malignant neoplasm of bone and articular cartilage (MNBAC) constitutes a diverse group of primary bone tumors,1 among which osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and chondrosarcoma are the most prominent subtypes encountered in clinical practice.2 These entities differ in age at onset, anatomical distribution, histopathological characteristics, prognosis, and treatment strategies.3 Despite their relative rarity compared to other malignancies, these tumors frequently require intensive multimodal treatment modalities, including limb-sparing surgery, systemic chemotherapy, and radiotherapy.4 Such interventions pose substantial risks of premature mortality, long-term disability, and diminished health-related quality of life.5 Etiologically, the development of primary MNBAC is influenced by a combination of genetic susceptibility and environmental or treatment-related exposures.6 Recognized high-risk conditions include hereditary cancer syndromes such as Li-Fraumeni syndrome and hereditary retinoblastoma, as well as bone disorders like Paget’s disease, which notably predispose individuals to MNBAC.7 Prior exposure to therapeutic or environmental ionizing radiation and, in some cases, contact with environmental pollutants have also been identified as potential risk factors.8 Given the clinical significance and complexity of MNBAC, it is essential to develop a comprehensive understanding of demographic trends and shifts within healthcare systems to interpret its burden across different regions and over time.

Among the global regions, East Asia is of critical interest to MNBAC. First, it encompasses more than one-fifth of the global population and is experiencing rapid demographic shifts, including population aging in countries such as Japan, alongside large and still-evolving populations in China.9 China alone is estimated to have one of the largest absolute numbers of patients with osteosarcoma and other primary bone malignancies, attributable to its large population.10 Meanwhile, South Korea is among the fastest aging societies globally, potentially increasing the total burden of adult-onset entities such as chondrosarcoma.11 Second, there is considerable heterogeneity in cancer registry coverage and data quality across East Asian countries and territories. Some regions possess well-established, high-quality registries, while others face more limited or uneven case ascertainment.12 These disparities highlight the necessity for standardized burden estimates that can accommodate data gaps and yield internally consistent metrics across different settings.

Previous studies employing the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) framework have produced comparable metrics for a broad spectrum of cancers at global, regional, and national levels, thereby facilitating the evaluation of temporal trends and geographic disparities.13 The GBD 2023 reports and associated analyses provide updated estimates using standardized methodologies and publicly accessible data.14–16 Nevertheless, detailed, region-specific analyses of MNBAC in East Asia remain scarce. Specifically, there is a notable absence of comprehensive evaluations that concurrently examine temporal trends, age-standardized patterns, and future projections under varying epidemiological scenarios for East Asian countries and territories.

To address these deficiencies, the present study reports GBD 2023 estimates for MNBAC in five East Asian countries and territories from 1990 to 2023 and projections to 2050 under reference and alternative foresight scenarios. By presenting data on incidence, prevalence, deaths, years of life lost (YLLs), years lived with disability (YLDs), and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), both in absolute and age-standardized terms, this study offers a comprehensive and comparable assessment of the current and future burden of MNBAC in East Asia. The findings seek to support regional public health planning and to guide targeted cancer control efforts. This manuscript was produced as part of the GBD Collaborator Network and in accordance with the GBD Protocol.17

Methods

Study Overview

This study utilized estimates from the GBD 2023 to characterize the past burden of MNBAC across five East Asian countries and territories, namely China, Japan, the Republic of Korea, the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea, and Taiwan, from 1990 to 2023. Additionally, projections were made to estimate the future burden up to 2050 under multiple foresight scenarios. The primary objectives encompassed (1) delineating the incidence, prevalence, deaths, YLLs, YLDs, and DALYs associated with MNBAC in East Asia in 2023, (2) examining temporal trends and age-standardized patterns within the region from 1990 to 2023, and (3) summarizing projected deaths and DALYs through 2050. The estimates were originally produced by the GBD 2023 Causes of Death Collaborators,14 GBD 2023 Disease and Injury and Risk Factor Collaborators,15 and GBD 2023 Cancer Collaborators,16 and shared publicly in the GBD Results Tool (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results). An international network of collaborators provided, reviewed, and assessed the available data used to generate GBD 2023 estimates. Across all metrics, GBD 2023 drew on the expertise of more than 14,000 collaborators from more than 160 countries and territories. This study used de-identified data and was deemed exempt from requiring ethics approval from the Joint Chinese University of Hong Kong and New Territories East Cluster Clinical Research Ethics Committee.

Data Sources

Input data for MNBAC in GBD 2023 were obtained from population-based cancer registries, vital registration systems, and other pertinent sources, as documented in the GBD 2023 Sources Tool (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2023/sources). Metadata and citations for the input data sources specific to this study are available by selecting “causes of death” and “non-fatal health outcomes” for components, “malignant neoplasm of bone and articular cartilage” for causes, and “China”, “Japan”, “Republic of Korea”, “Democratic People’s Republic of Korea”, and “Taiwan” for locations. All results are reported in both absolute counts and age-standardized rates. When applicable, percentage changes between 1990 and 2023 were calculated for absolute numbers and rates by dividing the difference between the endpoint and baseline values by the baseline, expressed as a percentage. See GBD 2023 Causes of Death Collaborators,14 GBD 2023 Disease and Injury and Risk Factor Collaborators,15 and GBD 2023 Cancer Collaborators,16 for details on how input data were processed.

Outcome Measures

Incidence was defined as the number of new MNBAC cases per year divided by the mid-year population, whereas prevalence was characterized as the point prevalence of existing MNBAC cases during that year. Deaths referred to the number of MNBAC-attributable fatalities within a specified year and population. YLLs were calculated by multiplying age-specific death counts by the standard life expectancy at each age, based on the GBD 2023 reference life table. YLDs were derived by multiplying cause-specific prevalence by disability weights from population surveys, and DALYs were defined as the sum of YLLs and YLDs. Age-standardized rates for incidence, prevalence, deaths, YLLs, YLDs, and DALYs were computed using the GBD 2023 world standard population. For each metric, age-specific rates were multiplied by the corresponding population weights for each age group, and the resulting products were summed to produce an age-standardized rate per 100,000 population.

Regional Aggregation

Measures for East Asia were derived by aggregating country- and territory-specific GBD 2023 estimates. For each model draw, the number of cases or deaths within each jurisdiction was summed and then divided by the total population to compute regional measures, thereby weighting each country or territory proportionally to its population size. This methodology was similarly applied to YLLs, YLDs, and DALYs. The GBD 2023 framework accounts for variations in data availability and quality across countries by assigning source-specific weights and employing statistical models that borrow strength across different locations and time periods, especially when local data are limited or absent. In regions with scarce or no cancer registry data, such as the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea, the GBD ensemble modeling framework and covariate-based predictions were utilized to estimate measures. This manuscript presents regional aggregates to illustrate the overall trends within East Asia.

Forecasting Framework

GBD 2023 projections employed a forecasting framework that concurrently models future trends in risk factors, demographic drivers, and cause-specific outcomes. For cause-specific mortality, including MNBAC, future death estimates are obtained using a combination of cause-of-death ensemble models, autoregressive integrated moving-average components, and covariate-based adjustments that incorporate trajectories of risk factors and sociodemographic indicators. DALYs were projected by integrating mortality forecasts with estimates of nonfatal outcomes within a consistent modeling structure. This study extracted projections of deaths and DALYs attributable to MNBAC from 2024 to 2050 under the GBD foresight scenarios, including the reference, safer environment, improved behavioral and metabolic risk, improved childhood nutrition and vaccination, and combined scenarios. The reference scenario assumes a continuation of historical trends in risk factors, health system performance, and sociodemographic determinants, without the influence of additional policy interventions. The safer environment scenario envisions accelerated reductions in environmental and occupational risks, including household air pollution, unsafe water, sanitation, and hygiene, as well as ambient particulate matter, aligned with a rapid decarbonization pathway. The improved behavioral and metabolic risk scenario posits significant declines in tobacco use, harmful alcohol consumption, unhealthy diets, and cardiometabolic risk factors, including elevated body mass index, fasting plasma glucose, and systolic blood pressure. The improved childhood nutrition and vaccination scenario anticipates the eradication of childhood growth failure and micronutrient deficiencies, accompanied by universal coverage of essential childhood immunizations. The combined scenario synthesizes all these risk factor trajectories to project maximal potential health benefits. Although MNBAC is a relatively rare neoplasm with a complex etiology, these scenarios influence its future burden primarily through indirect pathways, including changes in population size and age distribution, overall survival, and competing risks, which are collectively modeled within the GBD forecasting framework. In this analysis, scenario projections are interpreted as illustrative of how broader risk factors and demographic trajectories shape future MNBAC burden at the population level, rather than as direct causal attributions of specific risk factors to this tumor group.

Smoothing Procedures

GBD 2023 employs a combination of statistical smoothing and regularization techniques to stabilize estimates across age, time, and geographic regions. For nonfatal outcomes, cause-severity models and spatiotemporal Gaussian process regression were utilized to smooth prevalence estimates across adjacent ages and years, while ensuring non-negativity and plausible temporal trends. Regarding causes of death, ensemble models integrated multiple component models with various functional forms and covariates, optimizing out-of-sample predictive performance and mitigating overfitting.

Calculating Uncertainty

Uncertainty was introduced throughout the estimation process. Mean estimates for all other metrics reported represent the mean across 250 draws from the estimate’s distribution, with 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) calculated as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentile values across the draws. To reduce computing power and time across the estimation process, the number of draws was reduced from 500 in previous GBD iterations to 250 for GBD 2023. Simulations confirmed that estimates and uncertainties were not affected by this reduction. See the GBD 2023 Disease and Injury and Risk Factors Collaborators for more details.15

GBD Researching and Reporting Practices

GBD 2023 complies with the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER) guidelines.18 The original GBD 2023 analyses were completed using Python versions 3.8.17 and 3.10.4, Stata version 15.1, and R versions 3.5 and 4.2. The statistical code used in GBD 2023 is publicly available online (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2023/code). No statistical modeling beyond the GBD 2023 estimation and forecasting framework was employed in this study.

Results

From 1990 to 2023, the absolute disease burden of MNBAC in East Asia increased significantly (Figure 1A). Incident cases rose from 9,054 (UI: 6,083–14,339) in 1990 to 29,877 (UI: 19,225–41,263) in 2023, representing a 230% escalation. Prevalent cases expanded from 59,169 (UI: 39,662–93,723) to 192,196 (UI: 123,417–272,649), indicating a 225% increase. The number of deaths increased from 6,300 (UI: 4,644–9,492) in 1990 to 21,627 (UI: 14,478–26,729) in 2023, reflecting a 243% rise. Conversely, age-standardized rates per 100,000 inhabitants exhibited a different trend (Figure 1B). The incidence rate rose from 5.64 (UI: 3.58–9.46) in 1990 to 6.28 (UI: 3.41–11.28) in 2023, an increase of 11%. The prevalence rate increased slightly from 37.39 (UI: 23.29–63.75) to 41.56 (UI: 22.68–74.60), representing an 11% increase. In contrast, the death rate declined from 3.60 (UI: 2.69–5.16) to 3.09 (UI: 2.07–4.58), representing a 14% reduction.

The aggregate burden of health loss followed a similar pattern in absolute terms (Figure 2A). YLLs increased from 249,744 (UI: 183,643–367,752) in 1990 to 594,029 (UI: 405,076–731,732) in 2023, a 138% increase. YLDs also rose from 6,505 (UI: 3,705–11,033) to 21,405 (UI: 12,767–32,744), a 229% increase. DALYs increased from 256,249 (UI: 189,186–378,200) to 615,434 (UI: 420,067–755,724), reflecting a 140% rise. When standardized per 100,000 population (Figure 2B), YLLs decreased from 134.48 (UI: 97.94–192.45) in 1990 to 107.61 (UI: 72.00–155.56) in 2023, a 20% reduction. YLDs increased slightly from 4.13 (UI: 2.26–7.49) to 4.43 (UI: 2.22–8.08), a 7% increase. Similarly, DALYs declined from 138.61 (UI: 100.87–198.69) to 112.04 (UI: 75.22–161.80), a 19% decrease.

Regarding projections of mortality attributable to MNBAC in East Asia, all estimates continue to indicate an increasing trend (Figure 3A). Under the baseline projection, the number of deaths is anticipated to reach 38,467 (UI: 19,414–66,899) by 2050, representing a 78% increase from 2023. Scenario analyses suggest that, under a more favorable socioeconomic environment, deaths could reach 38,975 (UI: 19,673–67,683), a 180% increase. The scenario involving improved behavioral and metabolic risk factors estimates 48,184 deaths (UI: 24,496–82,653), a 123% increase. The scenario focusing on improved childhood nutrition and vaccination predicts 38,460 deaths (UI: 19,410–66,885), a 78% rise. The combined scenario results in the highest projected death toll, at 48,578 (UI: 24,693–83,366), a 125% increase. The death rate per 100,000 individuals is also projected to rise (Figure 3B). Under the baseline projection, it is expected to reach 3.15 (UI: 1.68–5.36) by 2050, a 2% increase from 2023. Scenario analyses indicate that, under a safer environment scenario, the rate will be 3.16 (UI: 1.68–5.38), reflecting a 2% increase, while the scenario involving improved behavioral and metabolic risks projects a rate of 3.34 (UI: 1.77–5.71), a 108% increase. The combined scenario predicts a rate of 3.34 (UI: 1.77–5.71), an 8% increase.

The projection of DALYs attributable to MNBAC in East Asia shows a continued increase across all scenarios (Figure 4A). Under the baseline projection, DALYs are expected to reach 804,272 (UI: 424,826–1,363,167) by 2050, a 31% increase from 2023. Scenario analyses indicate DALYs of 810,549 (UI: 428,166–1,374,539), a 32% increase, under a more favorable environment. The scenario with improved behavioral and metabolic risk forecasts 940,423 DALYs (UI: 503,108–1,588,158), a 123% increase. The scenario focusing on enhanced childhood nutrition and vaccination projects DALYs of 803,939 (UI: 424,646–1,362,676), a 31% increase. The combined scenario yields the highest DALY burden at 943,950 (UI: 505,297–1,593,094), a 53% rise. Conversely, estimates of the DALY rate per 100,000 population continue to decrease in all scenarios (Figure 4B). The baseline projection indicates a reduction to 104.66 (UI: 57.02–173.92) by 2050, a 7% decrease from 2023. Scenario analyses suggest rates of 104.91 (UI: 57.11–174.45), 109.73 (UI: 59.38–183.35), and 104.64 (UI: 57.01–173.88) for the respective scenarios, corresponding to decreases of 6%, 2%, and 7%. The combined scenario projects a rate of 109.76 (UI: 59.38–183.44), a 2% reduction.

Discussion

This study presents the first East Asia-specific, comprehensive analysis of MNBAC based on GBD 2023 data from 1990 to 2023, including projections to 2050. The primary finding indicates a rapidly increasing absolute burden driven by rising incidence and prevalence. This trend occurs alongside a decline in age-standardized death and DALY rates, reflecting genuine improvements in survival and healthcare. Nonetheless, the absolute number of deaths and healthy life years lost continues to rise. The results highlight that, despite favorable age-standardized trends, East Asia faces a growing and increasingly apparent burden of MNBAC that is likely to strain healthcare systems through 2050, especially under optimistic socioeconomic and environmental scenarios that do not fully mitigate demographic and epidemiological pressures.

From 1990 to 2023, all absolute measures of disease burden increased in East Asia, reflecting the interplay between a large, aging population and modest changes in age-standardized incidence and prevalence of MNBAC. Similar patterns have been documented in global cancer burden studies, where regions with high population density and middle- to high socio-demographic index exhibited rapid growth in absolute case numbers despite declines in age-standardized mortality.13 The observed increase in age-standardized incident and prevalent cases should be interpreted cautiously, as it likely results from a combination of factors rather than a straightforward rise in underlying risk.19 Additionally, shifts in health-seeking behaviors and referral patterns may have increased the likelihood of evaluating and diagnosing patients with suspicious bone lesions.20 These diagnostic and registration improvements, along with potential epidemiological changes related to increased survival with comorbid conditions and evolving environmental exposures,21,22 may all partly explain the observed rise in incidence and prevalence. Conversely, age-standardized death and DALY rates declined over the same period, indicating that, on average, patients were living longer post-diagnosis and experiencing fewer healthy life-years lost per capita.23

Consistent with other registry-based studies, particularly those from SEER (Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results Program) and EUROCARE (European Cancer Registry),24,25 rising numbers of patients with MNBAC may also result from improvements in survival rates and more comprehensive documentation of long-term survivors. The expanded use of sophisticated imaging techniques, enhanced histopathological characterization, and improved classification of primary bone tumors over time have likely contributed to increased identification and registration of MNBAC cases,26 especially in earlier years when diagnostic infrastructure was less advanced in parts of East Asia.5,16 These factors may have artificially inflated apparent increases in incidence and prevalence over time, particularly when comparing earlier periods to more recent ones, thereby complicating the interpretation of long-term trends. Consequently, the increase in MNBAC cases should be viewed as the combined effect of genuine epidemiological changes and the evolution of diagnostic, reporting, and registration practices, rather than a purely biological rise in disease frequency.

Projections through 2050 indicate that the absolute numbers of deaths and DALYs attributable to MNBAC in East Asia are anticipated to increase across all foresight scenarios. In contrast, age-standardized rates are expected to remain relatively stable or decline slightly. This discrepancy highlights the significant influence of demographic dynamics and the assumptions embedded within the GBD forecasting framework on these projections. Since these estimates depend on historical correlations among risk factors, demographic variables, and health outcomes, any future changes in diagnostic practices, registration completeness, or coding systems that diverge from past trends could alter the projected occurrence and mortality.27 Contrariwise, modifications in administrative coding, registry consolidation, or changes in reporting priorities could lead to an underestimation of incidence independent of true biological risk.28 These uncertainties emphasize the need to treat such projections as scenario-based estimates grounded in historical trends and current data, rather than definitive forecasts.

Given that MNBAC includes biologically distinct entities such as osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and chondrosarcoma, each characterized by differing natural histories and responses to treatment, it is important to avoid overgeneralizations when referring to the group as a whole. While aggregated GBD estimates for ICD-10 codes of C40–C41 are valuable for public health planning, they do not elucidate subtype-specific trends. Instead, the current findings are more accurately interpreted as indicating that, at a population level, survival has improved and the balance between YLLs and YLDs has shifted, resulting in a higher proportion of patients living with long-term sequelae.

To improve the translational relevance of this work, it is essential to contextualize these burden estimates within the framework of emerging strategies for precision diagnosis and personalized cancer management. Recent research has underscored the potential of artificial intelligence–assisted imaging and decision-support tools to enhance early detection and risk stratification for bone tumors.29 Additionally, liquid biopsy approaches, including circulating tumor deoxyribonucleic acid and extracellular vesicle-based biomarkers, show promise in monitoring disease activity and guiding targeted therapies.30 Experimental investigations into bacterial extracellular vesicles and other innovative delivery platforms suggest future directions for precision oncology that could, in principle, be applicable to rare cancers such as osteosarcoma and related entities.31 Although these technologies are at varying stages of development, their integration into clinical practice may, over time, influence incidence by enabling earlier detection and survival through more tailored treatment approaches, potentially altering the future burden trajectories outlined in this study. While the current analysis cannot account for such breakthroughs, their consideration accentuates the importance of adaptable cancer control strategies that can incorporate new diagnostic and therapeutic modalities as they emerge.

Long-term management and rehabilitation constitute essential components of care for patients with MNBAC, especially considering the high prevalence of limb-sparing surgeries, reconstructive procedures, and enduring functional impairments.32 Recent advancements in bone and joint tissue engineering, biomaterials, and functional reconstruction present promising opportunities to enhance post-treatment quality of life and restore mobility for patients with MNBAC.33 Research on advanced biomaterial scaffolds, bioactive implants, and comprehensive orthopedic rehabilitation programs demonstrates how innovations in tissue repair and reconstruction may reduce the YLD component of MNBAC’s burden in future populations.34 Incorporating such reconstructive and rehabilitative strategies into national cancer plans would help ensure that improvements in survival are complemented by gains in functional ability and social participation, rather than being diminished by chronic disability.

The interpretation of these findings is subject to several limitations inherent in the GBD 2023 framework and data environment in East Asia. Firstly, GBD 2023 relies on model-based estimates to address data gaps, particularly in settings with sparse or incomplete vital registration and cancer registry data, which introduces uncertainty in the precise and age-standardized burden of MNBAC in certain East Asian contexts. Second, aggregating all malignant neoplasms coded as C40-C41 without distinguishing subtypes, thereby restraining insights into differential trends in osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, or Ewing sarcoma. Third, projections are based on extrapolations of past relationships between risk factors, demographic drivers, and outcomes. Thus, unforeseen changes in diagnostic practices, coding systems, or treatment standards could alter future trajectories in ways not captured by the foresight scenarios. Fourth, disability weights for MNBAC are derived from general population surveys and may not entirely reflect the lived experiences associated with long-term physical and psychological sequelae, potentially leading to an underestimation of the true YLD component of the overall burden.

Future research could build on this analysis by triangulating GBD estimates with detailed data from SEER, EUROCARE, and Asian registries to better contextualize East Asian trends within the global landscape of MNBAC burden and outcomes. Linking population‑based registries with treatment and outcome data would enable more precise evaluation of survival patterns, subtype‑specific trends, and the impact of health system factors, such as access to specialized centers. Incorporating information on diagnostic technologies, imaging utilization, and pathology capabilities would help disentangle the effects of detection and registration from underlying epidemiological changes. Finally, integrating scenario‑based modeling of cancer‑specific interventions, such as scaling up artificial intelligence‑assisted early detection, implementing precision medicine approaches, expanding reconstructive and rehabilitation services, and enhancing survivorship care, could provide more nuanced forecasts of future deaths and DALYs and inform strategic planning for rare but high‑impact cancers like MNBAC in East Asia.

Conclusions

This study shows that the burden of MNBAC in East Asia has increased significantly in absolute numbers from 1990 to 2023 and is projected to continue rising through 2050, according to modeled estimates. Although these increases are largely consistent with demographic changes, they occur alongside decreases in age-standardized death and DALY rates, which align with improvements in survival and healthcare delivery. The discrepancy between the rising absolute burden and the declining age-standardized rates indicates a growing number of patients surviving longer with disease-related morbidity, with implications for long-term treatment, rehabilitation, and survivorship support. Within the limitations of the available data and the GBD 2023 modeling framework, these results suggest that broad improvements in environmental, behavioral, and metabolic risk factors, as outlined in the foresight scenarios, are unlikely to fully counterbalance the projected increase in the absolute burden of MNBAC at the population level. These findings emphasize the necessity for cancer-specific strategies in East Asia, including enhanced orthopedic oncology services, earlier diagnosis and referral, and more comprehensive survivorship and rehabilitation programs. Given the incomplete understanding of the etiology of primary malignant bone tumors and the fact that these estimates are derived from modeled GBD data rather than primary registry or clinical datasets, caution should be exercised in interpretation. Continued development of national cancer registries, detailed clinical research, and etiological studies will be crucial to refining these estimates, validating observed trends, and informing targeted interventions for patients with MNBAC in the region.

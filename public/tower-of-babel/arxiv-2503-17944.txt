Relating elliptic curve point-counting and solutions of quadratic forms with congruence conditions
Koustav Mondal

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze the theta series associated to the quadratic form Q ⁡ ( x → ) ≔ x 1 2 + x 2 2 + x 3 2 + x 4 2 Q(\vec{x})\coloneqq x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2} with congruence conditions on x i x_{i} modulo 2 , 3 , 4 2,3,4 and 6 6 . By employing special operators on modular, non-holomorphic Eisenstein series of weight 2, we construct a basis for Eisenstein space for levels 2 k , k ≤ 7 2^{k},k\leq 7 , 3 ℓ , ℓ ≤ 3 3^{\ell},\ell\leq 3 and p p , for odd prime p p . Using the relation between the trace of Frobenius on an elliptic curve and the Fourier coefficients of the cusp form part of theta series corresponding to Q Q , we establish relation between the number of integer solutions to the equation Q ⁡ ( x → ) = p Q(\vec{x})=p and the number of 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -rational points on the associated elliptic curve under certain congruence conditions on p p .

Introduction

Analyzing representations of natural numbers by quadratic forms has a long history in mathematics.
Bhargava (2000)
;
Bhargava and Hanke (2011)
;
Beli (2022)
;
Cox (1989)
;

. In this paper, we prove that counts of representations by quadratic forms with various congruence conditions are linearly related to counting points on elliptic curves over finite fields.

We begin by setting the notation and terminology for quadratic forms. A quadratic form
Q(\vec{x})
, for
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, is degree 2 homogeneous polynomial in
n
variables. We will only focus on quadratic forms with integer coefficients. Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be a positive-definite,
symmetric matrix that satisfies

A_{ii}\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 2).

Such matrices are called
even
.
We say that
A
is the associated matrix to the positive-definite quadratic form
Q(\vec{x})
if

Q(\vec{x})=\frac{1}{2}\vec{x}^{t}A\vec{x}.

(1.1)

A quadratic form
Q

represents
a number
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
if there is exists some
\vec{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
Q(\vec{x}_{0})=\ell

universal quadratic forms

Ramanujan (1917)
gave a list of all universal quaternary forms . Later, Bhargava and Hanke
Bhargava (2000)
;
Bhargava and Hanke (2011)
proved all the cases for universal quaternary quadratic forms.

For a positive-definite quadratic form

\theta_{Q}

\theta_{Q}(\tau)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\end{subarray}}q^{Q(\vec{x})},\hskip 20.0ptq\coloneqq e^{2\pi i\tau},

(1.2)

for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}

Employing associated theta series is a conventional approach to analyze representation of numbers by the quadratic form. Recall that modular forms are holomorphic functions on upper-half complex plane
\mathbb{H}
that satisfy certain transformation properties with respect to the action of a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

Consequently, a modular form

has a Fourier expansion

Theta series, which are modular forms, provide insights into the representation of numbers by the associated quadratic forms by virtue of analysis of the coefficients of the Fourier series.

Background

In this section, we recall the necessary background for modular forms and congruent theta series. Additionally, we define and mention some necessary properties of the
S
and
V

2.1
Modular forms

We will adopt the notations and details from Chapter 3 of
Koblitz (1993)
.
A modular form of weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}
for a finite index subgroup
\Gamma\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a function

f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying the following three conditions.

•

f
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}
,

•

f(\gamma(\tau))=f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{k}f(\tau)
for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
, where
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
,

•

f|_{k}\gamma(\tau)\coloneqq(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma(\tau))
is bounded as
\operatorname{Im}(\gamma(\tau))\rightarrow\infty,\text{ for all }\gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We denote the space of modular forms of weight
k
on
\Gamma
by
\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
. For modular form
f\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
, if
(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma(\tau))\rightarrow 0
as
\operatorname{Im}(\gamma(\tau))\rightarrow\infty\text{ for all }\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we call
f
a cusp form. We will use

\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Gamma)
to denote the cuspidal forms subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
.

Next, we recall the Petersson inner product that will help us to decompose a modular forms into cuspidal parts and non-cuspidal parts to analyze them separately.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be a congruence subgroup and,
F
be a fundamental domain for
\Gamma
. Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
with at least one of
f,g
is a cusp form. The
Petersson inner product
of
f
and
g
is defined as

\langle f,g\rangle\coloneqq\frac{1}{[\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\bar{\Gamma}]}\int_{F}f(z)\overline{g(z)}y^{k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}},

(2.1)

where

\bar{\Gamma}=\begin{cases}\Gamma/\{\pm I\},&\text{if }-I\in\Gamma,\\
\Gamma,&\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
I
is the identity matrix.

In addition, we denote the Eisenstein space, which is the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Gamma)
with respect to the Petersson inner product in
\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
, by
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\Gamma)
. That is, we have

\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{E}_{k}(\Gamma)\oplus\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Gamma).

(2.3)

Assume that the translation matrix
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
. If
f\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
, then
f(\tau+1)=f(\tau)
for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
; that is,
f

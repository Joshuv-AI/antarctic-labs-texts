Relative 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -Contractibility of Koras-Russell prototypes and Exotic Motivic Spheres
Krishna Kumar Madhavan Vijayalakshmi

Abstract
. The Koras-Russell threefolds are a certain family of smooth, affine contractible threefolds exhibiting "exotic" behavior in the algebro-geometric context. Our goal in this note is to extend its 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractibility from a field to a general base scheme. As a consequence, we also give a general strategy to extend the 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractibility of Koras-Russell prototypes in higher dimensions over a general base scheme. As a major consequence, we establish the existence of "exotic" motivic spheres in all dimensions at least 4 over infinite perfect fields.



1.
Introduction

One of the emerging problems in algebraic geometry is to uniquely characterize the affine
n
-space
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
among smooth affine
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible
n
-schemes over a field
k
. The uniqueness of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
holds up to dimensions
n\leq 2
over

(cf.
[

,
CR24
]
) and fails from dimensions

. When char

\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
. Indeed, recall that an
m

k

is said to be

. This is the algebro-geometric version of the famously known

(ZCP), which began its formulation in the setting of commutative algebra (cf.
[
Gup22

[
AHE72
,
EH06
]
) and 2 (cf.
[

,
Rus81
]
). It is proven in negative for all dimensions

(see

[

]

[

]

[
Gup22
]
for comprehensive survey). Over a field of characteristic zero, it is now known that the uniqueness of
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{k}

-homotopy category

[

2.
Historical Outset

The characterization of the affine
n
-space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
is a classical problem that traces back to the 1950s, aimed to classify the real Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
up to topological contractibility. In dimensions
n\leq 2
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unique smooth manifold that is contractible; the story threw a curve ball in dimension 3 already. In an attempt to solve the Poincaré Conjecture in dimension 3, Whitehead put forth a proof
[
Whi34
]
where, in essence, he argued that an open contractible real 3-manifold that is homotopic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is necessarily homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. However, this argument collapsed owing to his own counter-example - now called the
Whitehead manifold

[
Whi35
]
. Soon after this discovery, several higher-dimensional prototypes of Whitehead manifolds emerged (see
[
AØ21
, §1.1]
for a beautiful overview and chronological contributions), which apparently did not facilitate
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to be characterized by its contractibility alone. Nevertheless, it was later observed that
\mathbb{R}^{3}
stayed simply connected in the complement of any larger compact subset, while the Whitehead manifold did not, revealing that
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is
simply connected at infinity
. Thus, invariants coming from topology at infinity found a home in the heart of such a characterization. Building on several mathematical predecessors, the final breakthrough happened in
[
Sie68
]
, proving that
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unique open contractible
n
-manifold that is simply connected at infinity for all
n\geq 3
. The aforementioned homotopical characterization of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is sometimes called the
Open Poincaré Conjecture
.

Recall from topology that a smooth complex
n
-manifold is said to be
exotic
if it is topologically contractible but not isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In analogy, we say that a smooth affine
n
-dimensional variety over a field
k
is
exotic
if it is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible but isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
. As we will witness in this note, the algebro-geometric analog of the Whitehead manifold will be played by a family of smooth affine threefolds called the
Koras-Russell threefolds

\mathcal{K}
(
Section

4
) and it is one of the overriding dreams in motivic homotopy theory to construct "motivic topology at infinity" characterizing
\mathbb{A}^{n}
among spaces similar to
\mathcal{K}
(see
[
AD07
, §6.4]
,
[
DDØ22
]
).

We briefly introduce the following notions from affine algebraic geometry.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space
over a scheme
S
is a smooth morphism of finite presentation
f:X\to S
whose fibers
X_{s}:=X\times_{S}\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)
over all points
s\in S
are isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
over the corresponding residue field
\kappa(s)
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space
f:X\to S
as above is called a
locally trivial
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-bundle
in the Zariski (resp. Nisnevich, resp. étale) topology if every
s\in S
admits a Zariski (resp. Nisnevich, resp. étale) neighborhood
U
in
S
such that
U\times_{S}X\cong U\times\mathbb{A}^{n}
as schemes over
U
.

We adopt the terminology of "
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space" as an alternative to the classical notion of "
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fibration" or "affine fibration", which is traditionally used by the authors from the affine algebraic geometry realm. This is to avoid any possible confusion, either with that of fibrations from the model category or that of topology.

Convention

Unless specified otherwise, all schemes considered are smooth and are of finite type over a base scheme. Throughout,
k
will denote the base field and
S
will denote a Noetherian base scheme of finite Krull dimension. The category of smooth
S
-schemes of finite type is denoted by
\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
. We denote the category of presheaves (resp. sheaves) by
\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{s})
(resp.
\operatorname{Shv}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
) and the category of motivic
S
-spaces which are simplicial presheaves by
\operatorname{Spc}_{S}:=\operatorname{sPsh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
. The unstable (resp. stable)
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category over a scheme
S
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}(S)
(resp.
\mathrm{SH}(S)
). For motivic spaces
X,Y\in\operatorname{Spc}_{S}
, its
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence is denoted by
X\simeq Y
(lives in
\mathcal{H}(S))
, while its isomorphism is denoted by
X\cong Y
(lives in
\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
).

3.
Motivic homotopy theory

3.1.
Preliminaries

Motivic homotopy (also known as the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy) theory is a nascent domain in algebraic geometry, established by Morel and Voevodsky
[
MV99
]
. The conceptual aim was to provide a homotopical framework for algebraic varieties, tackling the then-open Milnor Conjecture (cf.
[
OVV07
]
) and the Bloch-Kato Conjecture (cf.
[
Voe03
]
). Soon after its formulation, it became clearer that the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category provides a natural ground to several of Grothendieck’s visions towards the universal theory of motives. The underpinning of the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category is facilitated by the homotopy theory of the simplicial presheaves, equivalently, the category of
motivic
S
-spaces

\operatorname{Spc}_{S}:=\operatorname{sPsh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
equipped with the discrete simplicial structure. The category
\operatorname{Spc}_{S}
is then subjected to two formal Bousfield localizations, thereby producing the desired
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathcal{H}(S)
. Roughly speaking, the first localization inverts the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
by a process of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localization
, thereby producing motivic spaces that are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant; in other words, a space
X\to S
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant
if the induced morphism

\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}:\Hom_{\operatorname{Spc}_{S}}(U,X)\to\Hom_{\operatorname{Spc}_{S}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U},X)

is a bijection for the canonical projection
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
, for every
U\in\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
. The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localization functor
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}:\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})\to\operatorname{Psh}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
is the left adjoint to the inclusion functor
\iota:\operatorname{Psh}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
of presheaves that are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant. The second localization inverts the descent maps into weak equivalences; that is, a simplicial presheaf
X\in\operatorname{Spc}_{S}
satisfies
Nisnevich descent
if for every Čech covers
\check{U}_{\bullet}\to X
, we have that the
{\mathrm{hocolim}_{n}\ U_{n}\xrightarrow{\simeq}X}
is a weak equivalence of simplicial presheaves. The Nisnevich localization inverts these maps in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category via the functor
\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}:\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})\to\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
which is the left derived functor of the identity functor. Although in a general context, one replaces the Čech covers by hypercovers and studies the Nisnevich hyperdescent (see
[
AE17
, §3.4]
). The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
is then defined as the subcategory of
\operatorname{sPsh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
consisting of simplicial presheaves those that are both
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant
\operatorname{sPsh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
and satisfying the Nisnevich descent
\operatorname{Shv}_{\Nis}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
,

\mathcal{H}(S):=\operatorname{sPsh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\cap\operatorname{Shv}_{\Nis}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})\subset\operatorname{sPsh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})=:\operatorname{Spc}_{S}.

However, note that for a simplicial presheaf
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}(\mathcal{F}))
need not be a Nisnevich sheaf and
\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F}))
need not be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant (cf.
[
MV99
, §3, Example 2.7]
). This issue is circumvented by
considering the
motivic localization functor

\mathbb{L}_{mot}:\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})\to\mathcal{H}(S)
which is the left adjoint of the inclusion
\iota:\mathcal{H}(S)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Psh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
defined as

\mathbb{L}_{mot}:=\textrm{colim}(\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{L}_{\Nis}\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\to\dots..)=(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\circ\mathbb{L}_{\Nis})^{\circ\mathbb{N}}.

Being a left adjoint, it preserves colimits. Furthermore, it is locally cartesian and preserves finite products (
[
Hoy17
, Proposition 3.15 & 4.1]
). For a detailed background on the construction of the motivic homotopy category, we redirect the reader to
[
Hoy17
,
AE17
,
AØ21
]
. Let us now recollect some basic jargon in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category.

Definition 3.1
.

The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected component sheaf
of a scheme
X\to S
is the Nisnevich sheaf associated to the presheaf

U\in\mathrm{Sm}_{S}\mapsto\Hom_{\mathcal{H}(S)}(U,X)

for all
U\in\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
. The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected component sheaf of
X
is denoted by
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)
and is a Nisnevich sheaf of sets on
\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
. We say that a scheme
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected
if
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)
is isomorphic to the trivial sheaf
S
.

Example 3.2
.

Every
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected smooth scheme
X
over a locally Henselian scheme
S
admits an
S
-point (
[
MV99
]
, Remark 2.5). In other words, any variety that does not possess a rational point cannot be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected as a variety.

Definition 3.3
.

A

-scheme

is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigid
if the induced morphism

\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}:\Hom_{\mathrm{Sm}_{S}}(U,X)\to\Hom_{\mathrm{Sm}_{S}}(U\times\mathbb{A}^{1},X)

is a bijection, for the canonical projection morphism
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
, for every

.

Example 3.4
.

Any 0-dimensional smooth scheme over a field is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigid (
[
AØ21
, Example 2.1.10]
). The affine variety
\mathbb{G}_{m}

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigid (
[

). Any smooth projective curve of positive genus and any Abelian variety are

-rigid varieties. In the view of

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigid variety

.

For more examples, one can refer to
[
CR24
, §2]
,
[
AØ21
, §4.1]
. In parallel to topology, one defines the analogous notion of path-connectedness in algebraic geometry via images of the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

Definition 3.5
.

A
k
-scheme
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected
if for any finitely generated separable field extension
L/k
and for all rational points
x
and

, there exists rational points
x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}
,

and an

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence

f_{i}:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{L}\to X
between
x_{i-1}
and

, that is,
f_{i}(0)=x_{i-1}
and

for

.

Example 3.6
.

Affine
n
-spaces or more generally, any
n
-dimensional smooth
k
-variety covered by affine spaces in the sense of
[
AM11
, Definition 2.2.10]
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected. Any
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected variety is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected (
[
AM11
, Proposition 2.2.7]
) but the converse is not true (
[
BHS15
, §4.1]
). The smooth exotic surfaces such as Ramanujam surface and tom Dieck-Petrie surfaces cannot be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected as they are not even
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected (this is a consequence of the characterization of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
(
[
CR24
, Theorem 1.1]
) over a field of characteristic zero.

Definition 3.7
.

A motivic
S
-space
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible
if the canonical map
\mathcal{X}\to S
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in
\operatorname{Spc}_{S}
or equivalently, an isomorphism in
\mathcal{H}(S)
.

Example 3.8
.

Here are some of the basic examples of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible spaces in nature.

»

By construction, the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible. And so, by induction on the dimension
n\geq 1
, the affine
n
-spaces form the prototypical examples of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibles. In fact, the map

\mathbb{A}^{n}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}\quad(x_{1}\dots,x_{n},t)\mapsto(tx_{1},\dots,tx_{n})

corresponds to the usual radial rescaling morphism, which is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence.

»

The singular cuspidal curves
C_{r,s}
defined by the equation
\{y^{r}-t^{s}=0\}
, for coprime integers

are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible (
[
AD07
, Example 2.1]
). In fact, the normalization map

\mathbb{A}^{1}\to C_{r,s}\quad w\mapsto(w^{s},w^{r})

is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence of motivic spaces.

»

Any vector bundle morphism is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy equivalence (
[
MV99
, Example 2.2]
). More generally, any Zariski (resp. Nisnevich) locally trivial bundles with
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible fibers are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalences.

»

Any
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible variety
X
is necessarily
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. Hence,
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigid if and only if
X
is trivial.

3.2.
Functoriality in Motivic Homotopy Theory

We shall discuss the functoriality in the motivic homotopy theory with a special emphasis on the base change property that is crucial for our context. For a versatile background on functoriality, we refer to
[
MV99
, §3]
,
[
Ayo07
,
CD19
]
. For other recent accounts, see also e.g.,
[
Hoy17
, §4]
,
[
ELSØ22
, §2]
, and
[
Rön25
, §3]
. We shall now outsource several results from the articles mentioned above, wherein our contribution is the style of the presentation and the explicit illustration
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility in the relative setting (§
3.3
).

The Base Change

Let
f:T\to S
be a morphism of base schemes
2
2

2

By a base scheme, we mean a Noetherian separated scheme of finite Krull dimension
. Then the pullback along
f
defines a functor

\displaystyle f^{\bullet}:\mathrm{Sm}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sm}_{T}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptU\hskip 5.69054pt\longmapsto U_{T}:=U\times_{S}T.

Precomposition with
f^{\bullet}
, we obtain another colimit preserving functor at the level of motivic spaces defined as

\displaystyle f_{*}:\operatorname{Spc}_{T}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spc}_{S}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91692pt\mathcal{F}\hskip 14.22636pt\longmapsto\mathcal{F}\circ f^{\bullet}:=U\mapsto\mathcal{F}(U_{T}).

By the left Kan extension, we see that
f_{*}
admits a left adjoint
f^{*}:\operatorname{Spc}_{S}\to\operatorname{Spc}_{T}
on the level of motivic spaces, which is strict symmetric monoidal and preserves limits. Since every (pointed) motivic space is a colimit of representable motivic spaces, we can characterize
f^{*}
by the following formula

(3.9)

\displaystyle f^{*}(X_{+})=(X\times_{S}T)_{+}\quad\text{for all}\ X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{S}.

Smooth Base Change

If furthermore,
f:T\to S
is a smooth morphism of base schemes, then composition with
f
defines a functor
f_{\bullet}:\mathrm{Sm}_{T}\to\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
. Precomposition with this functor defines another functor
f^{\bigstar}:\operatorname{Spc}_{S}\to\operatorname{Spc}_{T}
which itself admits a left adjoint
f_{\sharp}:\operatorname{Spc}_{T}\to\operatorname{Spc}_{S}
via (enriched) Kan extension, that is
f_{\sharp}\dashv f^{\bigstar}\dashv f_{*}
. Again, using the fact that any motivic space is a colimit of representable ones,
f_{\sharp}
can be characterized by the formula

f_{\sharp}(X\xrightarrow{g}T)_{+}=(X\xrightarrow{g}T\xrightarrow{f}S)

for every
X\in\operatorname{Spc}_{T}
. In other words,
f_{\sharp}
is simply induced by the forgetful functor
\mathcal{U}:\mathrm{Sm}_{S}\to\mathrm{Sm}_{T}
. On the other hand, if
g:Z\to S
is a morphism, then the canonical
S
-morphism
Z\times_{S}T
defines a map
B(Z)\to f_{*}f^{\bigstar}B(Z)
which is natural in
Z
and
B\in\operatorname{Spc}_{T}
, and consequently, a natural transformation
\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{Spc}_{S}}\to f_{*}\circ f^{\bigstar}
. Moreover, for a smooth morphism
\phi:X\to Y
, the adjoint
f^{*}\to f^{\bigstar}
of the natural transformation
\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{Spc}_{S}}\to f_{*}\circ f^{\bigstar}
is a natural transformation (
[
Rön25
, Lemma 3.7]
). Denoting by
Lf^{*}
(resp.
RF_{*}
) the left (resp. right) derived Quillen functor on the level of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy categories, the following provides us with the concise base change functoriality relating the category of motivic spaces and the associated
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
f:T\to S
be a morphism of schemes, let
\operatorname{Spc}_{S}
and
\operatorname{Spc}_{T}
be the category of motivic spaces endowed with their respective local projective model structures, and let
\mathcal{H}(S)
and
\mathcal{H}(T)
be their respective localizations with their
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-local projective model structure. Then the following holds:

(1)

The adjoint pair
(f^{*},f_{*})
induces Quillen adjunctions

\operatorname{Spc}_{S}\mathrel{{\mathop{\underset{f^{*}}{\longrightarrow}}\limits}}\operatorname{Spc}_{T}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{H}(S)\mathrel{{\mathop{\underset{Lf^{*}}{\longrightarrow}}\limits}}\mathcal{H}(T).

(2)

If
f:T\to S
is smooth, then the adjoint pair
(f_{\sharp},f^{\bigstar})
induces Quillen adjunctions

\operatorname{Spc}_{T}\mathrel{{\mathop{\underset{f_{\sharp}}{\longrightarrow}}\limits}}\operatorname{Spc}_{S}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{H}(T)\mathrel{{\mathop{\underset{Rf_{\sharp}}{\longrightarrow}}\limits}}\mathcal{H}(S).

Proof.

The fact that
(f^{*},f_{*})
(resp.
(f_{\sharp},f^{\bigstar})
when
f
is smooth) induces a Quillen adjunction between the corresponding model categories is a consequence of using the projective model structure, and this follows from
[
Lur09
, Proposition A.2.8.7]
. Indeed, the first assertion follows from the observation that if
U_{\bullet}\to X
is a Nisnevich hypercover of a smooth
S
-scheme
X
, then
(U_{T})_{\bullet}\to X_{T}
is a Nisnevich hypercover of
X_{T}
and that for every smooth
S
-scheme
X
,
(X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S})\cong X_{T}\times_{T}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{T}
which implies by
[
Lur09
, Proposition A.3.7.9]
that the Quillen adjunction

\operatorname{Spc}_{S}\mathrel{{\mathop{\underset{\mathrm{id}\circ f^{*}}{\longrightarrow}}\limits}}\operatorname{Spc}_{T}

induces a Quillen adjunction

\mathcal{H}(S)\mathrel{{\mathop{\underset{\mathrm{id}\circ f_{*}\circ\mathrm{id}}{\longrightarrow}}\limits}}\mathcal{H}(T)

The second assertion follows for the same reasons after observing that when
f:T\to S
is smooth, a Nisnevich hypercover
U_{\bullet}\to X
of a smooth
T
-scheme
X
is again a Nisnevich hypercover of
X
considered as a smooth
S
-scheme via the composition with
u
and that for every smooth
T
-scheme
X
,
X\times_{T}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{T}\cong X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
as schemes over
S
.
∎

3.4.
Milnor-Witt
K
-Theory

To prove the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of Koras-Russell threefolds over an arbitrary field, we will need a description of the endomorphism ring of the motivic spheres
\mathbb{A}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
that is intertwined with the Milnor-Witt
K
-theory. The Milnor Witt
K
-theory is essentially an extension of Milnor’s
K
-theory, which cleverly mixes the generators and relations of Milnor’s
K
-theory and Grothendieck Witt ring. Interested readers can refer to
[
Mor12
,
Fas20
,
Dég23
,
BCDFØ25
]
for an extensive background.

Definition 3.20
.

Let
F
be any arbitrary field and
F^{\times}
be its units. The
Milnor-Witt
K
-theory
or
Milnor-Witt ring
, denoted as
K^{\operatorname{MW}}_{*}(F)
, is defined as the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative unital ring freely generated by the formal symbols
[a]
of degree +1, for
a\in F^{\times}
and a symbol
\eta
of degree -1 (called the
Hopf element
) subjected to the following relations:

(1)

[a][1-a]=0
, for any
a\neq 0,1
(Steinberg relations)

(2)

[ab]=[a]+[b]+\eta[a][b]
, for any
a,b\in F^{\times}

(3)

\eta[a]=[a]\eta
, for any
a\in F^{\times}

(4)

\eta(\eta[-1]+2)=0

It is related to the Milnor
K
-theory
[
Mil70
]
by killing
\eta
, that is,
K^{\operatorname{MW}}_{*}/(\eta)=K_{*}^{\operatorname{M}}(F)
. It is related to the Grothendieck-Witt ring of symmetric bilinear forms by killing the hyperbolic form
h:=\langle-1,1\rangle
, that is,
K^{\operatorname{MW}}_{*}{F}/(h)=\operatorname{GW}(F)
. There are various standard relations that one immediately derives by working with the generator and the relations. One such important element is the
\epsilon
in degree 0 described as follows:

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
\epsilon:=-\langle-1\rangle\in K^{\operatorname{MW}}_{0}(F)
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

n_{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle(-1)^{i-1}\rangle&\text{if}\quad n>0.\\
0&\text{if}\quad n=0.\\
\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{-n}\langle(-1)^{i-1}\rangle&\text{if}\quad n<0\end{cases}

Then the following holds.

(1)

For any
\alpha\in K^{\operatorname{MW}}_{n}(F)
and
\beta\in K^{\operatorname{MW}}_{m}(F)
, we have
\alpha\beta=\epsilon^{mn}\beta\alpha

(2)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in F^{\times}
, we have
[a^{n}]=n_{\epsilon}[a]
.

Residue homomorphism:

One of the fundamental tools to build a Gersten-type (cochain) complex with coefficients in the Milnor-Witt
K
-theory is the
residue homomorphism
. Let

A note on base change for motivic spheres

In this short section, we will comment on the base change properties of motivic spheres. In anticipation of the existence of exotic motivic spheres over fields (
Section

6.2
), it is natural to systematically ask for the verity over a base scheme
S
. The question of interest can be framed as follows:

Question 6.12
.

Let
S
be any "reasonable" base scheme. Then for every
m\geq 0
, are the smooth quasi-affine varieties
\mathcal{X}_{m}\backslash\{p\}
and
\mathbb{A}^{m+3}_{S}\backslash\{0\}
necessarily
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic. If so, are they isomorphic as
S
-schemes?

We intuitively anticipate that they should
not
be isomorphic as
S
-schemes owing to the failure of relative Makar-Limanov invariants as in the case of fields. To show that they are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic, one might hope for the following approach.

Gluing motivic spheres

One may choose to work fiberwise as in the case of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility. For concreteness, fix
S
to be a base scheme with infinite perfect residue fields. Let us now decipher the obstruction to taking up this approach. For every point
s\in S
, let us consider the base change
f_{s}:(\mathcal{X}_{m}\backslash\{p\})_{s}\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa_{s}
, where
\kappa_{s}
is the corresponding residue field at
s\in S
. Thus, by
Section

6.2
, we have that for every
s\in S
, the quasi-affine schemes
(\mathcal{X}_{m}\backslash\{p\})_{s}
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic to
\mathbb{A}^{m+3}_{\kappa_{s}}\backslash\{q\}
in
\operatorname{Spc}_{\kappa_{s}}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{A}_{\kappa_{s}}^{m+3}\backslash\{0\}\simeq(\mathcal{X}_{m}\backslash\{p\})_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{m}\backslash\{p\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}\kappa_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

But since in general the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy classes of motivic spheres are not trivial, owing to the non-triviality the motivic Brouwer degree (cf.
[
Mor12
, Corollary 6.43]
), knowing that the fibers

are
\mathbb{A}^{1}

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic to

for motivic spheres in comparison to that of contractible objects as provided by
Section

3.3
. Anticipating such a gluing lemma for motivic spheres, it is straightforward to verify that there are exotic motivic spheres in relative dimensions
\geq 3
over an appropriate base scheme
S
.

The relative approach

An alternative approach would be to directly show that the varieties in
Section

6
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic over
S
itself. However, this requires one to extend the tools exploited in the proof of

Uniqueness of motivic spheres in relative dimension 2

In anticipation of the analogous gluing lemma, one could attempt to establish that there are no exotic motivic spheres in relative dimensions 2. Here is one possible formulation.

Conjecture 6.13
.

Let
S
be any Noetherian scheme with characteristic zero residue fields. Let

be a smooth scheme of relative dimension 2. Then if
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}\backslash\{0\}

Due to
[

, we have that there are no exotic motivic spheres over a field of characteristic zero. If
S
is any Noetherian scheme with characteristic zero residue fields, then by the "anticipated" gluing lemma, it is verbatim to verify the nonexistence of exotic motivic spheres over

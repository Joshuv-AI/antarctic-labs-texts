The Modified Novikov-Veselov Equation and the Inverse Scattering Transform
Peter Perry

Abstract
. This paper corrects several errors in the author’s previous papers [ 6 ] and [ 7 ] respectively on the modified Novikov-Veselov (mNV) and the defocussing Davey-Stewartson II (DS II) equations. The mNV equation lies in the integrable hiearchy of the DS II equation, so that the same scattering transform can be used to solve both equations. Each paper presents a proof that the solution formula u ⁡ ( t ) = ℐ ⁡ ( e i ​ t ​ φ ​ ℛ ​ ( u 0 ) ) u(t)=\mathcal{I}\left(e^{it\varphi}\mathcal{R}(u_{0})\right) (where ℛ \mathcal{R} (resp. ℐ \mathcal{I} ) is the direct (resp. inverse) scattering map, and φ \varphi is a phase function) gives a solution to the PDE provided u 0 ∈ 𝒮 ⁡ ( ℝ 2 ) u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) . In [ 6 ] , this results in an incorrect form of the mNV equation which has also been used by others who cited this paper. Here we correct errors in proofs and obtain a correct statement of the mNV equation as solved by inverse scattering. This result plays in important role in [ 4 ] .

Inverse Scattering

In what follows, we let

We denote by

\partial f=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y})f,\quad\overline{\partial}f=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}+i\partial y)f.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\overline{z}-\overline{\zeta}}\,dA(z)

The direct scattering transform is based on the following
\overline{\partial}
-problem:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{z}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e_{k}u(\overline{\mu_{2})},

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{z}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e_{k}u(\overline{\mu_{1})},

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

\displaystyle\underset{|z|\to\infty}{\sim}(1,0).

The
direct scattering transform
of
u
is given by

(2.2)

\mathcal{R}(u)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int e_{k}(z)u(z)\mu_{1}(z,k)\,dA(z),

and its linearization at
u=0
is

(2.3)

(\mathcal{F}f)(k)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int e_{k}(z)f(z)\,dA(z).

The
inverse scattering transform
is determined by the
\overline{\partial}
-problem

(2.4)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{k}\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e_{k}\overline{r}(\overline{\nu_{2}}),

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{k}\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e_{k}\overline{r}(\overline{\nu_{1}}),

\displaystyle(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})

\displaystyle\underset{|k|\to\infty}{\sim}(1,0).

The
inverse scattering transform
of
r
is given by

(2.5)

u(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int e_{-k}(z)r(k)\nu_{1}(z,k)\,dA(k)

and its linearization at

is

(2.6)

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}g)(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int e_{-k}(z)g(k)\,dA(k).

The maps

,
\mathcal{I}
and their linearizations are continuous from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to itself.

The DSII and mNV equations take the respective forms

(2.7)

\displaystyle u_{t}+i(\partial^{2}_{z}+\overline{\partial}^{2}_{z})u

\displaystyle=NL_{DS}(u)

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle u_{t}+(\partial_{z}^{3}+\overline{\partial}_{z}^{3})u

\displaystyle=NL_{mNV}(u)

where

is a cubic nonlinearity. To compute the dispersion relations for (

to obtain (setting

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{(DS II)},

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{t}+(-k^{3}+\overline{k}^{3})\widehat{u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{(mNV)}.

We conclude that

\varphi_{DS}(k)=k^{2}+\overline{k}^{2}

and

(2.10)

This implies the solution formulas

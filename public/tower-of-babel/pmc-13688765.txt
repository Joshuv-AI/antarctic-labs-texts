Uncovering research themes in coronary slow flow predictors: a bibliometric and cluster analysis
Authors: Difei Liu, Yan Chen, Tao Zhong, Yawen Zhang
Published: 2026

Background

Coronary slow flow (CSF) is a distinct clinical condition identified through coronary angiography, characterized by epicardial coronary arteries that appear unobstructed, with stenosis of less than 40% [1]. The diagnosis of CSF is determined by the thrombolysis in myocardial infarction frame count; specifically, a count exceeding 27 frames (at a rate of 30 frames per second) in at least one epicardial vessel indicates the presence of CSF [2]. The prevalence of CSF among patients undergoing diagnostic angiography for chest pain or related symptoms ranges from 1% to 7% [3]. Despite the angiographically benign appearance of their coronary arteries, individuals with CSF frequently experience recurrent angina pectoris, which can significantly impair their quality of life and, in severe cases, lead to life-threatening complications such as ventricular tachyarrhythmias or cardiac death [4]. The precise causes and mechanisms underlying CSF remain unclear, and treatment strategies have yet to demonstrate consistent effectiveness [5]. Nevertheless, patients with CSF may encounter recurrent angina and, in extreme cases, serious cardiovascular events, thereby rendering it an emerging global health concern and an area of increasing research interest [4].

CSF has traditionally been considered a relatively neglected entity within the broader spectrum of coronary syndromes, and only recently has systematic attention been paid to its epidemiology, pathophysiology, and clinical implications. Among the efforts to synthesize the evolving literature, Tong et al. [6] conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of CSF research, providing an overview of global publication trends, collaborative networks, and thematic clusters across all CSF-related topics. However, that study was not designed to specifically interrogate the predictive and biomarker-focused dimension of CSF research. In particular, it did not systematically characterize which predictors have been most extensively investigated, how interest in different biomarker categories has evolved over time, or where the major gaps and emerging opportunities lie in the prediction of CSF.

Accurate identification and prediction of CSF risk are critical for enhancing patient outcomes and developing effective interventions. Factors such as endothelial dysfunction, microvascular abnormalities, occult atherosclerosis, and inflammatory processes have been proposed as contributors to the pathogenesis of CSF [7]. Recent studies have investigated parameters such as mean platelet volume (MPV), platelet distribution width (PDW), and red cell distribution width (RDW), which are associated with inflammation and thrombosis, key features of atherosclerosis [8]. Furthermore, Abdulmecit et al. identified that the atherogenic index of plasma and Castelli risk indices II levels are independent predictors of CSF [9]. However, these predictors necessitate further validation through high-quality trials. Identifying potential predictors of CSF would not only enhance understanding of its pathophysiology but also assist clinicians in recognizing high-risk patients earlier, thereby enabling more targeted prevention and management strategies.

While numerous studies on CSF predictors have been conducted, there remains a lack of objective consolidation of these findings and a comprehensive mapping of research focal points. Bibliometric analysis serves as a valuable tool for evaluating research progress, identifying key contributors, and mapping collaboration networks. By systematically analyzing published studies, bibliometric methods can elucidate research categories, keyword trends, co-authorship patterns, and highly cited articles [10]. In this context, a bibliometric analysis offers an appropriate and informative approach because it does not attempt to establish new predictors at the individual-patient level, but rather systematically maps the existing body of evidence on CSF predictors. By quantitatively assessing publication trends, influential contributors, and thematic hotspots related to predictors and biomarkers, bibliometric methods can delineate which markers have been most extensively studied, which remain underexplored, and how research priorities have evolved over time. Such a structured overview is an essential prerequisite for designing future mechanistic and clinical studies that aim to validate specific predictors and translate them into practical tools for risk stratification and management in patients with CSF.

Building upon this foundation, the present study narrows the focus to CSF predictors and biomarkers. By restricting the analysis to studies explicitly addressing predictors of CSF, and by applying bibliometric and visualization techniques tailored to this subset of the literature, the study aimed to (i) map the global research landscape devoted to CSF predictors, (ii) identify the most influential countries, institutions, authors, and journals in this domain, (iii) delineate the evolution and clusters of key predictor-related themes and research frontiers, and (iv) highlight underexplored areas that may guide future mechanistic and clinical investigations. This predictor-focused bibliometric perspective is intended to complement prior general overviews of CSF research and to provide a more clinically oriented view of the evidence base relevant to risk stratification and biomarker development.

Methods

Search strategy and data collection

The literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) to retrieve relevant articles published between 1977 and 2026. The search strategy was performed on June 17, 2026, using the following search terms: (TS= (coronary slow flow)) AND TS= (predict* OR biomarker) [6, 11]. The publication type was restricted to articles, thereby excluding retractions, retracted publications, and book chapters. Only publications in English were included. Bibliographic data were exported in both “Full record and cited references” and “plain text” formats to ensure the comprehensive capture of all pertinent information for analysis.

Statistical analysis

For bibliometric analysis and visualization, three robust tools were employed: VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.4.R1), and R-bibliometrix (version 4.5.2). VOSviewer was instrumental in mapping collaboration networks among institutions, authors, and publications [12]. It enabled the visualization and exploration of complex relationships within the academic field, providing insights into co-authorship, co-citation, and keyword co-occurrence networks. To further identify emerging trends and research hotspots, CiteSpace was utilized for keyword burst detection. This tool facilitated the visualization and identification of keywords exhibiting significant citation bursts in this study [13], employing a time slice parameter spanning from January 1977 to June 2026, with annual intervals and a focus on keywords. R-bibliometrix was utilized to rank countries, institutions, and authors, as well as to generate trend charts that monitor publication volume and citation metrics over time. Various bibliometric indices (including the H-index, G-index, and M-index) were applied to evaluate the academic impact of authors and journals. The H-index quantifies both productivity and citation impact, while the G-index places greater emphasis on highly cited articles, providing a more nuanced assessment [14]. The M-index reflects the progression of the H-index over time. Furthermore, journals were categorized based on their Journal Citation Reports (JCR) impact factors (IF) [15], which serve as a metric of a journal’s influence by averaging the number of citations received by its articles.

Results

An overview of publications

A total of 440 publications were identified from the WoSCC. After excluding 74 records, 366 studies were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1A). The publications were disseminated across 166 peer-reviewed journals, which collectively cited a total of 8,459 references. Annual publication counts have exhibited a consistent upward trajectory from 1977 to 2026, with an average annual growth rate of 5.68% (Fig. 1B). A positive correlation was observed between the number of publications and the year (Fig. 1C). The volume of publications peaked in 2024.

Analysis of journals

Circulation boasts the highest IF (2025), signifying its substantial influence and frequent citations in this research domain. It is followed by the Circulation Journal and American Journal of Physiology-Heart and Circulatory Physiology. Collectively, these journals are situated within the esteemed JCR Q1 quartile, reflecting their preeminent status in the discipline (Supplementary Table 2).

The total link strength, a metric that quantifies the frequency with which two journals are cited together in the same articles or references, revealed that BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, Circulation Journal, and International Journal of General Medicine demonstrated the highest total link strengths within the co-occurrence networks (Fig. 3A). Analysis of authors

Wang Yong emerged as the most prolific author. Ma Chunyan closely followed, with nine publications, securing the second position. Wang Yonghui has eight publications (Supplementary Table 3). Among the 56 authors engaged in international collaborations, Ma Chunyan and Wang Yonghuai distinguished themselves as prominent figures within this network, exhibiting the highest total link strength, followed by Demirbag Recep and Wang Yong. Their substantial total link strength highlights their active involvement in collaborative research projects (Fig. 3C).

Analysis of keywords

A total of 48 keywords, each exhibiting a minimum of ten occurrences, were identified. Cluster 1 emphasized the pathogenesis of CSF, with key terms including “mechanisms”, “endothelial dysfunction”, “inflammation”, “lymphocyte ratio” and “oxidative stress”.

Cluster 2 focused on prediction for CSF following percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), incorporating keywords such as “percutaneous coronary intervention”, “primary angioplasty”, “thrombolysis”, and “intravascular ultrasound”. Cluster 3 centered on prognostic impacts of CSF, featuring keywords such as “outcomes”, “intervention”, and “management” (Fig. 4A and Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

Summary of key findings

This bibliometric analysis systematically examined the research landscape pertaining to CSF predictors from 1977 to 2026 and demonstrated a growing scholarly interest in this area over time. China ranked first in both MCP and TP, aligning with its recent prominence in global cardiovascular disease (CVD) research [16, 17]. Besides, CVD threatens the gains in life expectancy, productivity, and quality of life that have been achieved in low- and middle-income countries, which is the leading cause of death around the world [18, 19]. Since 2006, the prevalence of CVD in the Chinese population has been gaining momentum, contributing to huge resource investment by the government in CVD research [20]. This may be the reason why China has the most publications, including institutions such as China Medical University and Capital Medical University, reflecting their active roles in collaborative research and significant research and development budgets. Furthermore, Wang Yong emerged as the most prolific author, reflecting a substantial and sustained research effort in China. His work primarily focuses on the pathophysiological mechanisms of CSF, particularly the roles of inflammation and microvascular dysfunction. Key predictors investigated include hematological indices such as the neutrophil-to-albumin ratio [21], as well as lipid profiles including the total cholesterol to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio [22], and the atherogenic index of plasma [22]. Represented by JCR Q1 publications like Circulation and the American Journal of Physiology-Heart and Circulatory Physiology, the USA continues to solidify its leadership in CVD research.

The findings should be interpreted in the context of previous bibliometric work on CSF, particularly the study by Tong et al. [6], which provided a broad overview of global CSF research activity. Whereas Tong et al. [6] analyzed the CSF literature as a whole, the present study focused specifically on predictors and biomarkers of CSF. This difference in scope is reflected in several aspects of the results. For example, although the leading countries and institutions identified in this analysis broadly overlap with those reported by Tong et al. [6], the keyword and co-citation maps are dominated by predictor-related terms (e.g., inflammatory markers, hematologic indices, imaging parameters), rather than by general CSF topics. Moreover, the burst analysis highlights emerging predictor themes that were not the primary emphasis of the earlier study. Together, these distinctions suggest that research on CSF predictors has developed its own trajectory within the wider CSF field, with specific hotspots and gaps that warrant targeted attention.

Hotspots and frontiers

Cluster 1 (Red): Pathogenesis of CSF

Cluster 1 aggregates the mechanistic vocabulary of the field, like “mechanisms”, “endothelial dysfunction”, “inflammation”, “lymphocyte ratio” and “oxidative stress”, whose dense co-occurrence reflects a long-standing but still-unresolved debate over the primary driver of CSF. The temporal distribution of these keywords offers critical insights into the conceptual evolution of CSF research. While “C-reactive protein (CRP)” dominated the early and mid-period literature, “endothelial dysfunction” emerged as a prominent burst term around 2023. This trajectory signifies a paradigmatic shift, moving beyond the initial reliance on systemic inflammatory biomarkers toward a more integrative conceptualization of CSF as a pathological interplay between inflammation and microvascular dysfunction. Physiologically, the epicardial arteries contribute negligibly to coronary resistance; consequently, the microvasculature serves as the primary determinant of myocardial perfusion. Coronary vascular tone is governed by the endothelium, which sustains a delicate equilibrium between vasoconstrictors (e.g., endothelin-1) and vasodilators (e.g., nitric oxide) [23]. Disruption of this homeostatic balance underscores the pivotal role of endothelial dysfunction in CSF pathogenesis, potentially exacerbated by secondary phenomena such as platelet activation [24]. Moreover, emerging evidence supports the utility of composite inflammatory-endothelial indices, such as the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and aggregate index of systemic inflammation (AISI), in the context of CSF [25, 26]. Notably, “reserve” has emerged as a recent high-intensity burst term, signifying a paradigm shift wherein microvascular reserve function is increasingly recognized as a key correlate of CSF [27]. While diminished microvascular reserve frequently accompanies CSF and reinforces the integrative inflammatory–endothelial conceptualization of its pathogenesis [28], this bibliometric trajectory underscores the field’s evolution. Specifically, Cluster 1 illustrates a transition from viewing CSF through isolated inflammatory pathways toward embracing integrated inflammatory-endothelial mechanisms, mirroring our increasingly sophisticated understanding of CSF pathophysiology. Addressing these complexities will necessitate large-scale, well-phenotyped cohorts and mechanistic studies designed to explore these emerging frontiers.

Cluster 2 (Green): Prediction of CSF following PCI

The co-occurrence structure of Cluster 2 is dominated by procedure-centered keywords like “percutaneous coronary intervention”, “primary angioplasty”, “thrombolysis”, and “intravascular ultrasound”, reflecting the synchronous expansion of post-PCI CSF research alongside the global uptake of primary PCI for ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) [29]. Consistent with this trajectory, “no reflow” and “management” emerged as strong-burst keywords from 2021 onward, signaling a shift in scholarly attention from describing the phenomenon toward identifying modifiable procedural and patient-level predictors. Post-PCI CSF demands particular scrutiny given its association with microvascular thrombosis and endothelial dysfunction (2023–2026), which collectively drive adverse clinical outcomes [30]. Indeed, endothelial dysfunction has been established as a central mechanism of post-PCI CSF. Preprocedural elevations in endothelium-specific biomarkers—such as endocan and endothelin-1—coupled with impaired flow-mediated dilation, serve as independent predictors of CSF following primary PCI for STEMI [31, 32]. Furthermore, circulating endothelial microparticles and elevated asymmetric dimethylarginine underscore impaired nitric oxide bioavailability and microvascular injury, reinforcing an integrative inflammatory–endothelial paradigm of CSF pathogenesis [33]. Beyond these mechanistic insights, established clinical predictors of post-PCI CSF include advanced age, hyperglycemia, prolonged reperfusion time, higher stent-to-vessel diameter ratios, renal dysfunction, and heavy thrombus burden [34–36]. Collectively, these co-occurrence and burst dynamics underscore the need for integrated periprocedural strategies aimed at preserving endothelial function, improving coronary flow “reserve” (2024–2026), achieving glycemic control, and protecting renal function. Such multifaceted interventions represent a coherent and increasingly prioritized frontier for mitigating CSF.

Cluster 3 (Blue): Prognostic Impacts of CSF

Cluster 3 groups outcome-oriented keywords like “outcomes”, “intervention”, and “management”, along with “prevalence” and “myocardial infarction” emerging as prominent burst terms from 2023 onward. This trajectory signals a definitive paradigm shift: CSF is no longer viewed merely as a benign angiographic curiosity but is increasingly recognized as a condition carrying substantial prognostic weight, establishing itself as a contemporary research hotspot. Accumulating evidence links CSF to considerable cardiovascular morbidity, encompassing silent ischemia, ventricular arrhythmias, myocardial infarction, and chronic refractory angina that markedly impairs quality of life [37, 38]. Crucially, prior studies have consistently demonstrated that the presence of CSF independently predicts adverse clinical events in patients with ischemic heart disease [39]. The coincident emergence of “intervention” and “management” as high-intensity burst keywords highlights the imperative of meticulous pre-procedural risk assessment to identify patients at high risk for CSF and implement targeted prophylactic strategies to mitigate its incidence. Collectively, these bibliometric trends reinforce the clinical imperative of utilizing CSF as a robust risk-stratification tool, facilitating intensified surveillance and potentially optimizing overall survival in affected populations.

Collectively, bibliometric analysis delineates a conceptual evolution in CSF research from a historically isolated focus on systemic inflammation toward a contemporary, integrated paradigm centered on the interplay between inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and impaired microvascular reserve. This shift coincides with the global adoption of primary PCI, catalyzing a transition from mere phenomenological description to the identification of modifiable procedural and patient-level risk factors, such as composite inflammatory-endothelial indices and clinical comorbidities, for post-PCI CSF. Consequently, CSF has transcended its characterization as a benign angiographic curiosity to emerge as a robust, independent predictor of adverse cardiovascular outcomes, thereby underscoring its clinical utility as a tool for risk stratification. Moving forward, the field necessitates the development of multidimensional, mechanism-targeted intervention strategies—specifically those aimed at preserving endothelial function and microvascular reserve—to mitigate CSF and optimize long-term survival in affected populations.

Strengths and Limitations

The strengths of the study are evident in its comprehensive approach to the literature review, utilizing a reputable database and rigorous inclusion criteria, which ensured a focused and relevant analysis. The application of bibliometric techniques facilitated a nuanced understanding of the research landscape, illuminating key trends and influential publications. The examination of keywords, particularly through burst detection, revealed thematic shifts and evolving research priorities over time. A key strength of our study is that it extends prior bibliometric work on CSF by providing a focused analysis of predictors and biomarkers. While Tong et al. [6] delineated the overall structure and growth of CSF research, the present targeted approach allowed the identification of predictor domains that have attracted sustained interest, which ones remain underrepresented, and how the emphasis on different biomarker categories has shifted over time. These insights may help investigators prioritize mechanistic hypotheses, select candidate biomarkers for validation, and design future studies that address the most salient gaps in the CSF predictor literature. At the same time, the present analysis should be viewed as complementary rather than duplicative; integrating the predictor-focused findings with broader overviews of CSF research can offer a more comprehensive understanding of how biomarker development fits within the evolving CSF landscape.

Conversely, this study still has inherent limitations. First, the bibliometric analysis was conducted using the WoSCC database, which, while comprehensive, may have excluded relevant studies indexed in other databases such as PubMed or Scopus. This could introduce a selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the analysis was limited to English-language publications, which may have excluded relevant high-quality studies published in other languages. Third, the reliance on bibliometric indicators such as publication counts and citation metrics does not fully capture the quality or impact of the research, such as self-citation or regional citation practices. Additionally, while the findings of the study are current, they may rapidly become outdated in the fast-paced scientific domain, highlighting the necessity for ongoing analysis to capture emerging trends.

Conclusion

A bibliometric analysis was conducted to elucidate the research trends associated with CSF. Our findings indicate a significant increase in publications within this domain. The analysis revealed key research hotspots, including pathogenesis of CSF, prediction for CSF following PCI, and prognostic impacts of CSF, as well as emerging frontiers such as risk stratification and mechanism-targeted intervention strategies. This synthesis provides a comprehensive overview of the current research landscape and offers insights that may inform future research directions and clinical applications in the field of CSF.

The Seasons of a Career in Physical Chemistry
Authors: Gemma C. Solomon, Joan-Emma Shea, Gregory D. Scholes
Published: 2026

One of the great joys of a scientific career is the remarkable people we have the privilege of working with, be they our mentors, students, colleagues, or collaborators. We are inspired by their example, humbled by their generosity and stimulated by thought-provoking questions. As a community, we have a long tradition of honoring scientists at the end of their careers through conferences and festschrifts. One need only browse through festschrifts that have been published in The Journal of Physical Chemistry to be struck by the remarkable stories behind these researchers’ careers. While there is something inherently satisfying in looking back at a career, the story any of these researchers might have told at an earlier stage would have looked very different.

This editorial is written to mark the launch of a series called “The Seasons of a Career in Physical Chemistry” that will be published across ACS Physical Chemistry Au, The Journal of Physical Chemistry A/B/C and The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters. With this series, we celebrate the people behind the physical chemistry, showcasing the remarkable work being done in all kinds of research environments, and the joys and challenges faced by researchers at all career stages. The series will feature interviews with physical chemists, giving them the opportunity to tell their own stories: how they came to be where they are today and what they imagine as they look to the future.

As we navigate the personal and professional challenges in our own careers, it is very natural to look to role models to provide a vision for what overcoming these challenges might entail. Some researchers are fortunate to have an immediate network of colleagues and friends who can provide this advice and support, but the mobility common to so many research careers means this is not always the case. Moving abroad, starting in a new research institution or moving between academia and industry are all transitions that can lead to a more fragmented network or a small local environment where colleagues may have limited understanding of the challenges these transitions bring. A senior colleague’s career trajectory might seem entirely deliberate and purposeful in retrospect, while one’s own prospects seem completely uncertain. A move abroad, leaving family and friends behind, can feel extremely challenging in the moment, yet seem quite trivial 20 years later, viewed through the lens of a lifetime built in a new country. We hope this series will provide a window into how other researchers have overcome these challenges, highlighting the perspectives of those looking back later in their careers and also those who are grappling with them right now.

When we look around at our research environments and indeed also broader society, we can be struck by the dynamic nature of research culture as technological and social developments change the world around us. While many of these changes do not necessarily strike at the core of physical chemistry, they can have a significant impact on what the experience of working as a physical chemist might be. With this series, we hope you will see glimpses of how research culture has changed and continues to evolve in both environments close to your own and those further away, allowing all of us to reflect on what we would like to prioritize and strengthen in our own research environment.

We invite you to read these interviews and hope you will find something of yourself in the stories they tell. The careers represented here are as varied as the science itself, each shaped by moments of doubt and discovery, by the colleagues who appeared at the right time, and by choices whose significance only became clear in retrospect. Every season of a career in physical chemistry brings its own rewards and difficulties, and there is value in hearing from those who have lived through them all. We hope this series becomes not only a record of the remarkable community physical chemistry has built but also a source of inspiration and companionship for those navigating their own path through it.

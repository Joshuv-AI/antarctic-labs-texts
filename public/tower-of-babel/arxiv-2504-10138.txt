k k -Fibonacci numbers that are palindromic concatenations of two distinct Repdigits
Herbert Batte, Florian Luca

Abstract
Let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and { F n ( k ) } n ≥ 2 − k \{F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geq 2-k} be the sequence of k k –generalized Fibonacci numbers whose first k k terms are 0 , … , 0 , 0 , 1 0,\ldots,0,0,1 and each term afterwards is the sum of the preceding k k terms. In this paper, we determine all k k -Fibonacci numbers that are palindromic concatenations of two distinct repdigits.

Introduction

1.1
Background

Given an integer
k\geq 2
, the
k
-generalized Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, is defined by the recurrence relation

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+F_{n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)},\quad\text{for all}\ n\geq 2,

with initial values
F_{2-k}^{(k)}=\cdots=F_{-1}^{(k)}=F_{0}^{(k)}=0
and
F_{1}^{(k)}=1
. This sequence generalizes the well-known Fibonacci sequence, which corresponds to the case when
k=2
. It has been investigated in numerous algebraic settings.

A
repdigit
in base 10 is a positive integer consisting of repeated occurrences of the same digit. More formally, a number
N
of this form can be expressed as

N=\underbrace{\overline{d\ldots d}}_{\ell\text{ times}}=d\left(\frac{10^{\ell}-1}{9}\right),

where
d
and
\ell
are non negative integers satisfying
0\leq d\leq 9
and
\ell\geq 1
. The study of Diophantine properties of recurrence sequences, particularly in relation to repdigits, has been an active area of research. In particular, previous studies have explored cases where terms in such sequences can be written as sums or concatenations of specific types of numbers.

The work by Banks and Luca,
[
4
]
provided some initial insights into the number of such sequence terms, although the results were limited in scope. Additionally, the case of Fibonacci numbers formed by concatenations of two repdigits was investigated in
[
1
]
, where it was established that the largest such Fibonacci number is
F_{14}=377
. This work was later extended to the sequence of
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers in
[
2
]
.

More recently, researchers have explored the connection between linear recurrence sequences and palindromic repdigits. A number is said to be a
palindrome
if it reads the same backwards as forward, such as 121, 40404, and 77. The occurrence of palindromes in linear recurrence sequences has been studied extensively. For instance, Chalebgwa and Ddamulira, in
[
12
]
, showed that 151 and 616 are the only Padovan numbers that can be written as palindromic concatenations of two distinct repdigits. Similarly, it was shown in
[
13
]
that the only palindromic concatenation of two distinct repdigits appearing in the Narayana’s cows sequence is 595. Further results on repdigits and palindromes in recurrence sequences can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

Motivated by the work in
[
2
]
, we instead investigate a more constrained Diophantine equation involving
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers:

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}=\overline{\underbrace{d_{1}\ldots d_{1}}_{\ell\text{ times}}\underbrace{d_{2}\ldots d_{2}}_{m\text{ times}}\underbrace{d_{1}\ldots d_{1}}_{\ell\text{ times}}},

(1.1)

where
d_{1},d_{2}\in\{0,1,2,\dots,9\}
with
d_{1}>0
,
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
and
\ell
,
m\geq 1
because we are studying
k
-Fibonacci numbers that are palindromic concatenations of two
distinct
repdigits. Also note that for
F_{n}^{(k)}
to be a palindromic number of two distinct repdigits, it must have at least three digits in its decimal representation, implying that
n\geq 9
. So, we also assume
n\geq 9
throughout the paper and prove the following main result of our study.

Theorem 1.1
.

F_{11}^{(5)}=464
is the only
k
-Fibonacci number that is a palindromic concatenation of two distinct repdigits.

2
Some properties of
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers

The following is a well known identity:

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2},\qquad\text{for all}\qquad 2\leq n\leq k+1,

(2.1)

which can be easily proved by induction. Moreover, the first
k
-generalized Fibonacci number that is not a power of two is
F_{k+2}^{(k)}=2^{k}-1
. The characteristic polynomial of
(F_{n}^{(k)})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is given by

\Psi_{k}(x)=x^{k}-x^{k-1}-\cdots-x-1.

This polynomial is irreducible in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
and it possesses a unique real root
\alpha:=\alpha(k)>1
. All the other roots of
\Psi_{k}(x)
are inside the unit circle, see
[
7
]
. The particular root
\alpha
can be found in the interval

\displaystyle 2(1-2^{-k})<\alpha<2,\qquad\text{for}\qquad k\geq 2,

(2.2)

as noted in
[
7
]
. As in the classical case when
k=2
, it was shown in
[
8
]
that

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-2}\leq F_{n}^{(k)}\leq\alpha^{n-1},\qquad\text{holds for all}\qquad n\geq 1,\quad k\geq 2.

(2.3)

Lastly, for
k\geq 2
we define

\displaystyle f_{k}(x):=\dfrac{x-1}{2+(k+1)(x-2)}.

If
2(1-2^{-k})<x<2
, then
\frac{d}{dx}f_{k}(x)<0
and so inequality (
2.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}=f_{k}(2)<f_{k}(\alpha)<f_{k}(2(1-2^{-k}))\leq\dfrac{3}{4},

holds for all
k\geq 3
. The inequality
1/2=f_{k}(2)<f_{k}(\alpha)<3/4
also holds for
k=2
.

In addition, it is easy to verify that
|f_{k}(\alpha_{i})|<1
holds for all
2\leq i\leq k
, where
\alpha_{i}
for
i=2,\ldots,k
are the roots of
\Psi_{k}(x)
inside the unit disk.
Lastly, it was shown in
[
15
]
that

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{k}(\alpha_{i})\alpha_{i}^{n-1}~~\text{and}~~\left|F_{n}^{(k)}-f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}\right|<\dfrac{1}{2},

(2.4)

hold for all
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2-k
. The first expression in (
2.4
) is known as the Binet-like formula for
F_{n}^{(k)}
. Furthermore, the second inequality expression in (
2.4
) shows that the contribution of the zeros that are inside the unit circle to
F_{n}^{(k)}
is small. However, there is a sharper estimate than (
2.4
) and it appears in as Lemma 3 in
[
9
]
. It states that if
n<2^{k/2}
, then

\displaystyle\left|F_{n}^{(k)}-2^{n-2}\right|<\dfrac{2}{2^{k/2}}.

(2.5)

3
Methods

3.1
Linear forms in logarithms

We use four times Baker-type lower bounds for nonzero linear forms in three logarithms of algebraic numbers. There are many such bounds mentioned in the literature like that of Baker and Wüstholz from
[
3
]
or Matveev from
[
10
]
. Before we can formulate such inequalities we need the notion of height of an algebraic number recalled below.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\gamma
be an algebraic number of degree
d
with minimal primitive polynomial over the integers

a_{0}x^{d}+a_{1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{d}=a_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\gamma^{(i)}),

where the leading coefficient
a_{0}
is positive. Then, the logarithmic height of
\gamma
is given by

h(\gamma):=\dfrac{1}{d}\Big(\log a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log\max\{|\gamma^{(i)}|,1\}\Big).

In particular, if
\gamma
is a rational number represented as
\gamma:=p/q
with coprime integers
p
and
q\geq 1
, then
h(\gamma)=\log\max\{|p|,q\}
.
The following properties of the logarithmic height function
h(\cdot)
will be used in the rest of the paper without further reference:

\displaystyle h(\gamma_{1}\pm\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle\leq h(\gamma_{1})+h(\gamma_{2})+\log 2;

\displaystyle h(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}^{\pm 1})

\displaystyle\leq h(\gamma_{1})+h(\gamma_{2});

\displaystyle h(\gamma^{s})

\displaystyle=|s|h(\gamma)\quad{\text{\rm valid for}}\quad s\in\mathbb{Z}.

With these properties, it was easily computed in Section 3 of
[
8
]
that

\displaystyle h\left(f_{k}(\alpha)\right)<3\log k,\qquad\text{for all}\qquad k\geq 2.

(3.1)

A linear form in logarithms is an expression

\Lambda:=b_{1}\log\gamma_{1}+\cdots+b_{t}\log\gamma_{t},

where for us
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t}
are positive real algebraic numbers and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}
are nonzero integers. We assume,
\Lambda\neq 0
. We need lower bounds
for
|\Lambda|
. We write
{\mathbb{K}}:={\mathbb{Q}}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t})
and
D
for the degree of
{\mathbb{K}}
.
We start with the general form due to Matveev, see Theorem 9.4 in
[
10
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Matveev, see Theorem 9.4 in
[
10
]
)
.

Put
\Gamma:=\gamma_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{t}^{b_{t}}-1=e^{\Lambda}-1
. Assume
\Gamma\neq 0
. Then

\log|\Gamma|>-1.4\cdot 30^{t+3}\cdot t^{4.5}\cdot D^{2}(1+\log D)(1+\log B)A_{1}\cdots A_{t},

where
B\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,\ldots,|b_{t}|\}
and
A_{i}\geq\max\{Dh(\gamma_{i}),|\log\gamma_{i}|,0.16\}
for
i=1,\ldots,t
.

3.2
Reduction methods

Typically, the estimates from Matveev’s theorem are excessively large to be practical in computations. To refine these estimates, we use a method based on the LLL–algorithm. We next explain this method.

Let
k
be a positive integer. A subset
\mathcal{L}
of the
k
-dimensional real vector space
{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
is called a lattice if there exists a basis
\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{Z}b_{i}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}b_{i}\mid r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

We say that
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}
form a basis for
\mathcal{L}
, or that they span
\mathcal{L}
. We
call
k
the rank of
\mathcal{L}
. The determinant
\text{det}(\mathcal{L})
, of
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\displaystyle\text{det}(\mathcal{L})=|\det(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})|,

with the
b_{i}
’s being written as column vectors. This is a positive real number that does not depend on the choice of the basis (see
[
11
]
, Section 1.2).

Given linearly independent vectors
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we refer back to the Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization technique. This method allows us to inductively define vectors
b^{*}_{i}
(with
1\leq i\leq k
) and real coefficients
\mu_{i,j}
(for
1\leq j\leq i\leq k
). Specifically,

\displaystyle b^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=b_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\mu_{i,j}b^{*}_{j},~~~\mu_{i,j}=\dfrac{\langle b_{i},b^{*}_{j}\rangle}{\langle b^{*}_{j},b^{*}_{j}\rangle},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the ordinary inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Notice that
b^{*}_{i}
is the orthogonal projection of
b_{i}
on the orthogonal complement of the span of
b_{1},\ldots,b_{i-1}
, and that
\mathbb{R}b_{i}
is orthogonal to the span of
b^{*}_{1},\ldots,b^{*}_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. It follows that
b^{*}_{1},b^{*}_{2},\ldots,b^{*}_{k}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Definition 3.2
.

The basis
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}
for the lattice
\mathcal{L}
is called reduced if

\displaystyle\|\mu_{i,j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq j<i\leq n,~~\text{and}

\displaystyle\|b^{*}_{i}+\mu_{i,i-1}b^{*}_{i-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4}\|b^{*}_{i-1}\|^{2},\quad\text{for}\quad 1<i\leq n,

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the ordinary Euclidean length. The constant
{3}/{4}
above is arbitrarily chosen, and may be replaced by any fixed real number
y
in the interval
{1}/{4}<y<1

(see
[
16
]
, Section 1)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
k-
dimensional lattice with reduced basis
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
and denote by
B
the matrix with columns
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
.
We define

l\left(\mathcal{L},y\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\min_{x\in\mathcal{L}}||x-y||\quad;~~y\not\in\mathcal{L}\\
\min_{0\neq x\in\mathcal{L}}||x||\quad;~~y\in\mathcal{L}\end{array}\right.,

where
||\cdot||
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. It is well known that, by applying the
LLL–algorithm, it is possible to give in polynomial time a lower bound for
l\left(\mathcal{L},y\right)
, namely a positive constant
c_{1}
such that
l\left(\mathcal{L},y\right)\geq c_{1}
holds (see
[
17
]
, Section V.4).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
z=B^{-1}y
with
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})^{T}
. Furthermore,

(i)

if
y\not\in\mathcal{L}
, let
i_{0}
be the largest index such that
z_{i_{0}}\neq 0
and put
\lambda:=\{z_{i_{0}}\}
, where
\{\cdot\}
denotes the distance to the nearest integer.

(ii)

if
y\in\mathcal{L}
, put
\lambda:=1
.

Then, we have

c_{1}:=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\{\dfrac{||b_{1}||}{||b_{j}^{*}||}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta:=\lambda\dfrac{||b_{1}||}{c_{1}}.

In our application, we are given real numbers
\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}
which are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
and two positive constants
c_{3}
and
c_{4}
such that

\displaystyle|\eta_{0}+x_{1}\eta_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}\eta_{k}|\leq c_{3}\exp(-c_{4}H),

(3.2)

where the integers
x_{i}
are bounded as
|x_{i}|\leq X_{i}
with
X_{i}
given upper bounds for
1\leq i\leq k
. We write
X_{0}:=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\{X_{i}\}
. The basic idea in such a situation, from
[
14
]
, is to approximate the linear form (
3.2
) by an approximation lattice. So, we consider the lattice
\mathcal{L}
generated by the columns of the matrix

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&1&0\\
\lfloor C\eta_{1}\rfloor&\lfloor C\eta_{2}\rfloor&\ldots&\lfloor C\eta_{k-1}\rfloor&\lfloor C\eta_{k}\rfloor\end{pmatrix},

where
C
is a large constant usually of the size of about
X_{0}^{k}
. Let us assume that we have an LLL–reduced basis
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
of
\mathcal{L}
and that we have a lower bound
l\left(\mathcal{L},y\right)\geq c_{1}
with
y:=(0,0,\ldots,-\lfloor C\eta_{0}\rfloor)
. Note that
c_{1}
can be computed by using the results of Lemma
3.1
. Then, with these notations the following result is Lemma VI.1 in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma VI.1 in
[
17
]
)
.

Let
S:=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}X_{i}^{2}
and
T:=\dfrac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}}{2}
. If
\delta^{2}\geq T^{2}+S
, then inequality (
3.2
) implies that we either have
x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{k-1}=0
and
x_{k}=-\dfrac{\lfloor C\eta_{0}\rfloor}{\lfloor C\eta_{k}\rfloor}
, or

H\leq\dfrac{1}{c_{4}}\left(\log(Cc_{3})-\log\left(\sqrt{\delta^{2}-S}-T\right)\right).

Python is used to perform all the computations in this work.

def is_power_of_two(n):
"""Returns True if n is a power of 2"""
return n > 0 and (n & (n - 1)) == 0
def find_power_of_two_palindromes(max_l=3, max_m=12):
valid_palindromes = []
for d1 in range(1, 10): # d1 > 0 (no leading zero)
for d2 in range(10):
if d1 == d2:
continue
for l in range(1, max_l + 1):
for m in range(1, max_m + 1):
left = str(d1) * l
middle = str(d2) * m
right = str(d1) * l
pal_str = left + middle + right
pal_num = int(pal_str)
if is_power_of_two(pal_num):
valid_palindromes.append((pal_num, d1, d2, l, m))
return valid_palindromes
# Run it
results = find_power_of_two_palindromes()
# Print result
print(f"Found {len(results)} palindromes that are powers of 2:")
for val, d1, d2, l, m in results:
print(f"{val} = 2^{val.bit_length() - 1}, from d1={d1}, d2={d2}, l={l}, m={m}")

def k_fibonacci_sequence(k, n_max):
"""Generates the k-Fibonacci sequence up to n_max terms."""
F = [0] * (k - 1) + [1]
for _ in range(n_max - len(F)):
F.append(sum(F[-k:]))
return F
def is_palindromic_concatenation(num):
s = str(num)
n = len(s)

part3 = s[l + m:]
if (
part1 == part3
and len(set(part1)) == 1 # all digits same
and len(set(part2)) == 1
and part1 != part2

return False

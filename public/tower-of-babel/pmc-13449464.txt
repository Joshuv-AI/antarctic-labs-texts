Dynamic response of growth and organ nutrient allocation in Ochroma lagopus plantations to water-soluble fertilizer frequency in the rainy season
Authors: Juncheng Han, Wenhao Zhang, Jialan Chen, Weisong Zhu, Gang Chen, Junwen Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As a fast-growing tropical tree species, Ochroma lagopus exhibits rapid growth and high nutrient demand. This study investigated one-year-old Ochroma lagopus trees under four fertilization frequencies: control (CK), high (F1), medium (F2), and low (F3). The research examined how water-soluble fertilizer frequency during the rainy season affects tree growth, nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) uptake, stoichiometric ratios, and nutrient allometric relationships among organs, aiming to identify optimal fertilization strategies. Results showed that F2 treatment (500 g/plant every 30 days) produced the most significant height increase at 60, 90, and 120 days after fertilization, with a maximum increase of 26.53% compared to CK. The stable nutrient supply promoted preferential "height growth." In contrast, F3 treatment (500 g/plant every 45 days) achieved a 36.58% increase in diameter over CK. The lower nitrogen supply likely avoided potential inhibition of cambium cell differentiation and lignification caused by excess nitrogen, thereby enhancing stem diameter growth and improving plant stability under stress. Nutrient contents and stoichiometric ratios in organs showed stage-specific variations: at 60 days, a "source organ competition" pattern dominated, prioritizing leaf allocation for photosynthesis; by 90 days, photosynthetic products were transported from leaves to roots via branches, enhancing organ synergy and shifting to "source-sink-translocation coordination"; by 120 days, nutrients accumulated in "sink" organs. Allometric analysis revealed that high-frequency F1 caused inter-organ imbalance and elemental conflict; medium-frequency F2 maintained synergistic balance; while low-frequency F3 promoted a "root nutrient reserve" stress-response strategy. Therefore, for Ochroma lagopus plantations, medium-frequency fertilization (500 g/plant every 30 days) during the rainy season is recommended.

Introduction

Ecochemometrics refers to the equilibrium of various chemical elements during ecological interactions [9]. This concept is crucial for assessing the internal stability of plant organs, evaluating nutrient utilization efficiency, and identifying limiting elements [8]. In ecochemometrics, each element possesses distinct meanings. Carbon is the most significant component of dry matter in plants and serves a structural role [22, 30]. Nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) are considered the primary elements that limit plant growth. Nitrogen is an essential constituent of plant cells, including proteins, vitamins, and nucleic acids (DNA), and it is a nutrient that plants require in substantial quantities for growth. Nitrogen is actively involved in critical physiological processes such as photosynthesis and respiration [5, 17]. Both nitrogen deficiency and excess can diminish photosynthesis, reduce leaf area, and shorten the lifespan of green leaves, ultimately impacting plant productivity [27]. Studies have demonstrated that low nitrogen levels can enhance the chlorophyll content and net photosynthetic rate of Quercus mongolica seedlings, thereby promoting growth. In contrast, high nitrogen levels lead to a reduction in stomatal conductance, impair the electron transfer capacity of the photosystem, decrease the net photosynthetic rate, and inhibit growth [41]. Phosphorus (P) is a crucial element in intracellular genetic material, including RNA, and in the energy molecule ATP. It is involved in sugar and lipid metabolism and is essential for cell growth, division, and the overall growth rate of organisms [48]. Research has demonstrated that plants detect intracellular phosphorus signaling molecules (IP8) via the vacuolar membrane phosphate transporter VPT1. This mechanism regulates vacuolar phosphorus storage and release, thereby maintaining intracellular phosphorus homeostasis [24]. The rational distribution of nutrients within plants is essential for sustaining stable metabolic capacity and optimizing growth potential. Research indicates that the scaling relationship between nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) varies between lobar and non-lobar organs, exhibiting different growth rates, while remaining consistent among non-lobar organs [56]. Nutrient distribution among functionally similar organs tends to be uniform, whereas it is often heterogeneous among organs with distinct functions [57]. Studies have demonstrated that in chlorosomes, potassium (K) directly contributes to the synthesis of ATP and NADPH during photoreactions by maintaining ionic balance and generating a proton driving force. This process creates a conducive environment for CO₂ fixation and sugar synthesis in the Calvin cycle [38]. Additionally, potassium synergizes with other nutrients to uphold plant metabolic homeostasis. It mitigates the effects of phosphorus deficiency on photosynthesis by facilitating the transport of assimilates, promoting the regeneration of inorganic phosphorus, and enhancing the activity of the Rubisco enzyme [45]. The measurement ratios of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), and phosphorus (P) are intricately linked to the growth and development of individual plants, population dynamics, community interactions, and ecosystem processes [19]. Specifically, the C:N ratio indicates the efficiency with which plants assimilate carbon to convert absorbed nitrogen into essential compounds, such as proteins [21]. Research on the C:N ratios across various plant organs has demonstrated that higher C:N ratios correspond to increased nitrogen utilization efficiency, whereas lower C:N ratios promote rapid growth. This co-variation in carbon–nitrogen ratios illustrates the coordination of overall plant growth and its adaptability to environmental conditions [52]. Additionally, the C:P ratio serves as an indicator of phosphorus utilization efficiency in plants, while the N:P ratio is commonly employed to assess the types of nutrient limitations affecting plant growth [18]. Studying nutrient contents and stoichiometric characteristics of N, P, and K in different plant organs reveals nutrient utilization efficiency, survival strategies, limitations, and environmental adaptability.

Different plant organs, including roots, branches, and leaves, exhibit varying capabilities for nutrient absorption and fulfill distinct physiological functions. In response to environmental changes, plants regulate nutrient distribution among these organs through homeostatic mechanisms. This collaborative interaction results in closely related chemical element measurement characteristics across different organs [46]. Leaves function as "source" organs, primarily responsible for generating and temporarily storing photosynthetic products. Roots serve as absorption "reservoirs," tasked with acquiring nutrients from the soil. Branches act as "conduction" organs, facilitating the transportation and distribution of substances [16]. The nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) contents in leaves and roots are relatively high, supplying essential nutrients for plants to carry out various metabolic activities. Organs characterized by strong metabolic activity exhibit a tendency for prioritized resource distribution [49]. In contrast, branches, which serve primarily as support structures and facilitate material transport, typically possess lower nutrient content than metabolically active leaves. Plants allocate limited resources preferentially to metabolic and absorption organs that yield the highest returns, while striving to minimize resource consumption in structural support components [58]. Currently, an increasing number of studies examine the heterogeneous growth relationships of nutrient content across various plant organs as a crucial approach to understanding the processes of nutrient absorption, distribution, and utilization. Heterotrophic growth serves as a fundamental theory that elucidates the allocation of resources among different plant organs throughout their growth. The heterotrophic growth index for nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) across distinct organs reflects the relative change rates of N, P, and K contents and can serve as a predictor of plant growth. This theory has been extensively utilized in simulating and investigating nutrient distribution strategies among plant organs [57].

Fertilization serves as a crucial strategy for enhancing tree growth and development by effectively improving soil nutrient status and supplying essential nutrients. This process helps regulate the nutritional balance between trees and forest land. Water-soluble fertilizers, recognized for their efficiency and precision, have emerged as vital tools for enhancing the quality and productivity of modern agriculture [11]. These fertilizers offer advantages such as rapid dissolution, high utilization rates, and compatibility with integrated water and fertilizer systems [50]. Consequently, they are well-suited for the "small amount and multiple times" fertilization model in regions with high rainfall, which helps maintain nutrient balance in the rhizosphere and mitigate leaching pollution. Fast-growing tree species exhibit a significant demand for nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium during the rainy season,however, frequent rainfall exacerbates the conflict between nutrient absorption and loss [31]. The exploration of the frequency of water-soluble fertilizer application to optimize nutrient dynamics has emerged as a critical technical challenge. This approach is essential for promoting rapid growth and enhancing carbon sink functionality, which is significant for achieving the objectives of green forestry. The frequency of fertilization not only impacts the nutrient supply rhythm in the soil but also affects non-structural carbohydrates and their components by regulating the balance of carbon and nitrogen metabolism within plants. Previous studies indicate that an optimal fertilization frequency can enhance the effective distribution of non-structural carbohydrates among sources and reservoirs, thereby improving the carbon reserves and stress resistance of trees [10]. Conversely, both excessively high and low fertilization frequencies can disrupt the carbon–nitrogen balance, resulting in limited accumulation of photosynthetic products or nutrient waste. Additionally, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium work synergistically to regulate various physiological metabolic processes within plants [43]. Consequently, the application of water-soluble fertilizers at varying frequencies can enhance the absorption and utilization efficiency of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in plants, thereby influencing their growth quality and adaptability to adverse conditions.

Ochroma lagopus is an evergreen tree that belongs to the genus Ochroma within the Kapok family. This species is characterized as a fast-growing broad-leaved tree. Its rapid growth, lightweight nature, and both economic and ecological advantages suggest significant potential for introduction and promotion. Furthermore, it is well-suited for artificial forest cultivation and the establishment of carbon sink forests [2, 33]. Currently, China has successfully tested Ochroma lagopus cultivation only in the low-altitude regions of Xishuangbanna, Yunnan Province; however, the cultivation and management techniques remain underdeveloped. Existing research primarily addresses seed germination [7], seed viability assessments [25], and seedling growth [42]. Notably, there is a paucity of studies examining the nutrient utilization efficiency of Ochroma lagopus in response to fertilization. Consequently, this study evaluated the relationship between fertilization frequency and the absorption and stoichiometric ratios of elements such as nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K), as well as the heterogeneous nutrient growth among various organs. This evaluation was conducted by applying different frequencies of water-soluble fertilizer during the rainy season. The objective was to elucidate the dynamic nutrient responses and to identify the optimal fertilization strategy.

Materials and method

Overview of the test site

The experimental site is situated within the Ochroma lagopus Artificial Forest Farm of Jinghong Farm, located in Jinghong City, Xishuangbanna Dai Autonomous Prefecture, Yunnan Province (21°98' ~ 21°59'n, 100°75' ~ 100°45'e), at an altitude of approximately 540 m. This region experiences a tropical monsoon climate characterized by high temperatures and abundant rainfall, with long summers and no winters. The climate is divided into dry and wet seasons, featuring an average temperature ranging from 22 °C to 28 °C and an annual rainfall between 1200 and 1700 mm.

Experimental materials and experimental design

This study focused on one-year-old Ochroma lagopus at Jinghong Farm in Jinghong City, Xishuangbanna Prefecture, Yunnan Province, and examined the effects of applying water-soluble fertilizers at varying frequencies. The selection of fertilization frequency and quantity was informed by the properties of water-soluble fertilizers, local climatic conditions, agricultural practices, and fertilization trials conducted on fast-growing tree species, including eucalyptus [55], as well as other tropical tree species. In this experiment, four fertilization frequency gradients were established: CK (control treatment without water-soluble fertilizer), F1 (application of 500 g of water-soluble fertilizer per plant every 15 days), F2 (application of 500 g of water-soluble fertilizer per plant every 30 days), and F3 (application of 500 g of water-soluble fertilizer per plant every 45 days). The water-soluble fertilizer used was produced by Shandong Runkang Fertilizer Industry Co., LTD. Each treatment included 90 Ochroma lagopus plants, with three replicates per treatment, totaling 360 plants. The fertilization method was adapted to local climatic and production conditions, involving direct application followed by soil covering. The fertilization cycle lasted three months, from mid-May to mid-August. Sampling was conducted on the 60th, 90th, and 120th days after fertilization completion.

Index measurement

(1) Measurement of Tree Height and Diameter at Breast Height: Tree height and diameter at breast height were measured once prior to the commencement of the experiment. Following the initiation of the experiment, nine representative Ochroma lagopus were selected from each treatment group each month for the measurement of tree height and diameter at breast height. Tree height was measured using a height gauge with an accuracy of 0.1 cm, while diameter at breast height was measured with a diameter gauge accurate to 0.1 mm. The data were subsequently recorded.

(2) Nine standard trees were randomly selected from each sample plot. Using pruners, branches and leaves from the new shoots on the sunny side of the tree canopy were collected, and the plant roots were excavated. After marking, the samples were transported to the laboratory, where they were thoroughly cleaned. The samples were then placed in an oven at 120℃ for blanching for 30 min, followed by drying at 80℃ until a constant weight was achieved. Subsequently, they were ground using a grinding machine and sieved through a 100-mesh sieve. The processed samples were packed into sealed bags for preservation. The contents of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) were determined using the Nessler colorimetric method [28], the vanadium-molybdenum yellow colorimetric method, and the flame photometric method [1], respectively.

Statistical analysis

Using Excel 2016 for preliminary data processing, one-way ANOVA was performed with SPSS 27.0 to test the significance of differences in nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium contents and their stoichiometric ratios (N/P, N/K, K/P) in various organs (leaves, branches, roots) of Ochroma lagopus under different water-soluble fertilizer treatments. The significance level was set at P < 0.05. To exclude the potential effects of initial tree height and initial diameter at breast height (DBH) on the experimental results, the tree height and DBH of individuals in all treatment groups were measured before the experiment, and one-way ANOVA was conducted. Additionally, one-way ANCOVA was performed for initial tree height and initial DBH with tree height and DBH at 60, 90, and 120 days.

The relationship among the nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) contents in the leaves, branches, and roots of various organs was modeled using the anisotropic growth equation y = ax^b. This equation was logarithmically transformed into log(y) = b log(x) + log(a), where y and x denote the dependent and independent variables, respectively. Here, a represents the anisotropic constant, which serves as the intercept of the linear relationship, while b signifies the anisotropic exponent, corresponding to the slope of the linear relationship. When a = 1, the two elements exhibit isotropic growth, whereas a < 1 or a > 1 indicates anisotropic growth. Specifically, when a = 1, the two elements are deemed isochronous, while a < 1 or a > 1 suggests anisotropic behavior. The isochronous exponent and constant were derived through standardized major axis regression analysis (SMA) utilizing the "smart" package in R version 4.5.1. Graphical representations were created using Oringan 2024 and R version 4.5.1 software.

At 60, 90, and 120 days post-fertilization, tree heights across treatments were significantly greater than in the control (CK). Furthermore, tree height in F2 showed an initial increase followed by decrease relative to fertilizer application frequency. At 90 days post-fertilization, DBH values in F1, F2, and F3 treatments remained significantly higher than CK, with increases ranging from 14.52% to 28.49%. By 120 days after fertilization, DBH in F1, F2, and F3 treatments continued to exceed CK by 17.21%, 23.92%, and 34.63% respectively (P < 0.05). Discussion

Regulatory effects and mechanisms of different water-soluble fertilizer frequencies on the growth of Ochroma lagopus

This study found that water-soluble fertilizer application significantly promoted the growth in tree height and diameter at breast height (DBH) of Ochroma lagopus. However, the response of different growth indicators to fertilization frequency varied significantly, which is closely related to the species' stage-specific developmental demands and nutrient allocation strategies.In terms of tree height, the F2 treatment (application of 500 g/plant of water-soluble fertilizer every 30 days) consistently yielded the best performance at 60, 90, and 120 days after fertilization, with tree heights reaching 7.79 m, 8.42 m, and 8.96 m, respectively. Compared to the control (CK), the maximum increase in tree height reached 26.53% (Fig. 1a). Furthermore, as the fertilization frequency decreased, tree height exhibited a trend of initial increase followed by a decrease. This phenomenon may stem from the high demand for "stability" in nutrient supply during height growth. Although the F1 treatment provided sufficient nutrients in the short term due to its excessively short fertilization intervals, it disrupted the carbon–nitrogen metabolic balance within Ochroma lagopus. The high-frequency nitrogen input stimulated the preferential allocation of leaf photosynthetic products towards nitrogen assimilation (e.g., protein synthesis), rather than the accumulation of structural carbohydrates required for xylem elongation. Consequently, this led to constrained height growth in the later stages [14]. The F3 treatment (application of 500 g/plant of water-soluble fertilizer every 45 days), however, failed to meet the nutrient demands of Ochroma lagopus during its rapid growth phase in the rainy season. The extended fertilization intervals, combined with frequent rainfall that promoted nutrient leaching from the rhizosphere, easily led to interruptions in nutrient supply. Consequently, this negatively impacted tree height growth [20]. The F2 treatment, with its 30-day fertilization interval, effectively maintained stable nutrient concentration in the rhizosphere while providing an optimal window for carbon–nitrogen metabolic balance. This enabled efficient allocation of photosynthetic products to stem elongation, thereby maximizing tree height growth.

The DBH of Ochroma lagopus performed best under the F3 treatment, reaching 11.65 cm, 12.78 cm, and 13.82 cm at 60, 90, and 120 days, respectively. Compared to the CK group, the maximum increase reached 36.58%, demonstrating a "steady upward" trend as fertilization frequency decreased (Fig. 1b). This may be attributed to the dependence of diameter growth on cambial cell division and lignification [44], while the lignification process requires substantial phosphorus to support energy metabolism and cell wall synthesis, along with considerable potassium to facilitate assimilate transport [12]. Although the F3 treatment featured extended fertilization intervals, the nutrients from each application remained available in the soil for a relatively longer duration. Moreover, the root system of Ochroma lagopus could accumulate phosphorus and potassium through a "nutrient storage" strategy. This aligns with findings that fast-growing trees, such as Ochroma lagopus, under discontinuous nutrient supply conditions, prioritize storing absorbed phosphorus in the xylem of roots and stems to provide raw materials for subsequent cambial activity [6]. Furthermore, under the F3 treatment with relatively lower nitrogen supply intensity, the stem diameter growth trend of Ochroma lagopus was more pronounced. This phenomenon may be attributed to the avoidance of potential inhibitory effects caused by nitrogen excess on cambium cell differentiation and lignification processes under moderate nitrogen conditions. This physiological mechanism aligns closely with findings from Raza et al.'s study on the herbaceous plant Phaseolus vulgaris, where high nitrogen treatment promoted cell division and increased the number of root cortex cell layers but significantly suppressed xylem vessel development and lignin accumulation [29]. Therefore, an appropriate nitrogen supply intensity facilitates the "effective growth" of stem diameter, characterized by the accumulation of lignified cells [54].

Dynamic changes and allocation strategies of N, P, K contents and their stoichiometric ratios among organs of ochroma lagopus under different frequencies of water-soluble fertilizer

Different plant organs possess distinct functions and physiological characteristics. The allocation and trade-offs of nutrient contents among organs within the same tree species are linked to their functional traits and physiological processes [40]. As a "source organ," the N, P, K contents, and stoichiometric ratios in leaves directly reflect photosynthetic function and nutrient use efficiency [36] and exhibit a clear dynamic temporal response to fertilization frequency. Branches primarily provide support and facilitate material transport, with their nutrient contents and stoichiometric ratios reflecting the capacity for resource translocation [34]. Roots mainly serve to absorb water and nutrients while also functioning in nutrient storage,their nutrient contents and stoichiometric ratios indicate the efficiency of resource acquisition and reserve capacity [49]. However, under different fertilization treatments, various organs exhibited distinct response patterns across growth stages.

In this study, at 60 days, Ochroma lagopus was in a stage of "rapid nutrient uptake—priority allocation." During this period, the effects of different treatments were mainly reflected in "source organs preempting nutrients," with significant differences in regulatory effects among treatments. Leaves, as "source organs," demonstrated a significant advantage in priority nutrient acquisition. Under the F3 treatment, leaf N content reached 37.43 mg/g, representing a significant increase of 73.81% compared to CK (P < 0.05; Fig. 2a). Under the F1 treatment, leaf K content was significantly increased by 9.73% compared to CK (Fig. 2c). In contrast, nutrient contents in branches and roots were generally lower than those in CK (branch N decreased by 51.14%–53.37%, Fig. 3a; root N decreased by 36.75%–54.66%, Fig. 4a), forming a competitive pattern characterized by "leaf nutrient enrichment—branch and root nutrient scarcity." This result aligns with the "priority allocation to metabolically active organs" theory proposed by Yu et al. [49]. During the initial stage of nutrient supply, absorbed nitrogen and potassium are preferentially allocated to leaves to support photosynthetic system development (e.g., synthesis of Rubisco enzyme and maintenance of ion balance). In contrast, branches (transport organs) and roots (absorption organs) experience a temporary "sacrificial" nutrient status due to their delayed functional demands. However, root P content under the F2 treatment increased significantly by 32.10% compared to CK, while it decreased markedly by 42.94% under the F3 treatment (Fig. 4b). This discrepancy may be attributed to the stable nutrient supply in the F2 treatment alleviating aluminum-induced inhibition of phosphorus uptake in Xishuangbanna soils. Research from the Xishuangbanna Tropical Botanical Garden has demonstrated that aluminum ions suppress root phosphorus transporter activity [3]. Conversely, the single high-concentration phosphorus input in the F3 treatment may have intensified the "aluminum-phosphorus" antagonism, thereby impeding root phosphorus absorption. The stoichiometric ratios of nutrient elements in plant organs reflect both ecological strategies for environmental adaptation [37] and functional differentiation among organs. Leaf N/P ratios under F1 and F3 treatments increased significantly by 57.97% and 58.76% respectively compared to CK (Fig. 2d), yet remained below 14 overall, indicating that Ochroma lagopus remained nitrogen-limited during the early growth stage. This observation aligns with the diagnostic threshold proposed by Koerselman et al. [18]. Additionally, both branch N/P and K/P ratios were significantly lower than CK (N/P decreased by 30.35%−39.46%, Fig. 3d,K/P decreased by 20.89%−51.76%). Concurrently, branch N content showed significant positive correlations with branch P, branch K, branch N/P, branch N/K, and branch K/P ratios (P < 0.05, Fig. 8), suggesting the initial formation of nutrient coordination in branches (transport organs). However, Mantel tests indicated no significant correlation with growth indicators, potentially because branches were still in the "early nutrient translocation phase" without fully developed functionality. Meanwhile, the significant reduction in root N/P ratios under F1 and F2 treatments (51.81%−54.28%, Fig. 4d) reflects the root's strategy of compromising its own N-P balance to meet the nitrogen demands of leaves.

By 90 days after fertilization, the regulatory effects of all treatments transitioned from "unidirectional nutrient competition" to "source-sink-translocation coordination." Organ nutrient content exhibited a "branch-root nutrient recovery coupled with leaf nutrient optimization" pattern. Leaf K content surged by 203.65%−213.03% across all treatments compared to CK (Fig. 2c). As an osmotic regulator, potassium reduces transpirational water loss by maintaining leaf cell osmotic pressure while simultaneously enhancing Rubisco enzyme activity [38]. During this phase, when photosynthetic rates peaked and K demand surged, the fertilizer treatments effectively replenished K consumption, resulting in dramatically elevated K levels. The stable nutrient supply in the F2 treatment maintained leaf K content at 10.2 mg/g, significantly higher than F1 (8.7 mg/g) and F3 (7.9 mg/g), providing critical support for the photosynthetic peak.Branch N and K contents shifted from being significantly lower than CK at 60 days to significantly exceeding CK (F2 treatment increased branch N by 72.02% and branch K by 29.21%). Root N content increased by 45.3% compared to CK, indicating activation of the "leaf-to-branch-root nutrient translocation" cycle. This aligns with the theory proposed by Guan et al. [10] that optimal fertilization frequency promotes source-sink allocation of non-structural carbohydrates. At the stoichiometric level, leaf K/P increased by 165.58%−211.37% over CK (Fig. 2f), and branch K/P ratios significantly recovered (35.27% increase under F2), demonstrating that potassium supplementation effectively alleviated phosphorus limitation, consistent with the mechanism whereby "potassium accelerates inorganic phosphorus regeneration by enhancing assimilate transport" [45].Correlation analysis and Mantel tests in Fig. 9 confirmed that Ochroma lagopus growth had entered a "source-sink-translocation coordination" phase: photosynthetic products from leaves (source) were transported to roots through branches (translocation pathway), enhancing nutrient synergy among organs. Simultaneously, branch N content showed significant positive correlations with root N, root N/P, root N/K, branch N/P, and branch N/K (P < 0.001), while branch K content correlated positively with root K, root K/P, and branch K/P (P < 0.05), indicating substantially improved nutrient exchange efficiency between branches and roots, ultimately establishing an "aboveground-belowground coordinated" nutrient cycle.

At 120 days, the regulatory effects of all treatments shifted from "promoting growth" to "optimizing nutrient reserves," with further differentiation in nutrient allocation strategies among treatments. Organ nutrient content exhibited a characteristic of "sink organ nutrient enrichment." Branch P content under the F2 treatment increased significantly by 67.76% (1.98 mg/g) compared to CK (Fig. 3b). Root N content under the F3 treatment rose significantly by 139.76% (18.72 mg/g) compared to CK (Fig. 4a). Leaf N content under the F1 treatment remained high at 46.68 mg/g (a 63.6% increase over CK; Fig. 2a), indicating distinct nutrient storage strategies formed under different treatments. This divergence relates to the environmental adaptation traits of fast-growing tree species. During Xishuangbanna's dry season, decreased soil moisture reduces phosphorus uptake efficiency. The branch phosphorus storage under F2 treatment directly alleviates dry season phosphorus limitation, consistent with the findings of Lu et al. [23] that under dry season water deficit conditions, soil moisture content affects plant phosphorus uptake and utilization, and plants extensively absorb and store phosphorus. Concurrently, under the F1 treatment, Ochroma lagopus maintained leaf nitrogen content, enabling sustained photosynthetic capacity and extended photosynthetic lifespan under adverse conditions [53]. At the stoichiometric level, leaf N/P ratios recovered significantly across treatments (53.77% increase under F1,Fig. 2d) but remained below 14, indicating nitrogen limitation was alleviated but not fully eliminated. Branch K/P under F2 reached 12.3 (a 53.80% increase over CK; Fig. 3f), ensuring assimilate transport. Root N/P ratios increased significantly by 103.97%−112.08% under F1 and F3 treatments (Fig. 4d), and root K/P increased significantly by 35.53% under F1 (Fig. 4f), reflecting optimized nutrient balance in these "sink" organs. Simultaneously, correlation analysis and Mantel tests in Fig. 9 indicated that Ochroma lagopus growth had entered a "sink organ storage" phase—roots (sink organs) began substantial nutrient storage, with nutrients being translocated from leaves and branches to these sink organs.

Variation characteristics of allometric growth relationships of N, P, and K among organs of ochroma lagopus under different frequencies of water-soluble fertilizer

The nutrient allocation strategies in different plant organs represent a crucial mechanism for environmental adaptation and growth optimization [4]. Nutrient allocation among organs often follows scalable allometric relationships, and variations in allometric growth indices can indicate distinct nutrient allocation patterns between plant organs [47]. In this study, fertilization altered the plant's endogenous nutrient balance by modulating exogenous nutrient supply, resulting in dynamic changes in nutrient allometric growth relationships in response to fertilization frequency.

High-frequency fertilization disrupted inter-organ nutrient allocation balance, leading to allometric growth imbalance. At 60 days under the F1 treatment, Ochroma lagopus exhibited a significant positive allometric relationship between leaf N and branch N (b = 0.615, P < 0.01, Table 1), but significant negative allometric relationships between leaf N and root N, and between branch N and root N (both P < 0.01). This occurred because high-frequency nitrogen input stimulated leaf photosynthetic demand, causing nitrogen translocation from branches to leaves, while simultaneously suppressing root respiration and absorption function due to nitrogen excess [14]. Over time, by 90 days, the allometric index between branch N and root N reached 2.946 (P < 0.01), indicating extreme aboveground bias in nitrogen allocation. The plant directed most nitrogen resources to support rapid branch growth and expansion, gaining advantage in light competition and promoting short-term aboveground biomass accumulation. However, prolonged root system weakening ultimately impaired water and nutrient uptake capacity, failing to sustain the large aboveground canopy and restricting root development [51].Under high-frequency fertilization, P and K allometric relationships also showed biased imbalance. Phosphorus was preferentially allocated to branches (positive allometric growth between leaf P and branch P under F1 at 90 days, b = 0.416, P < 0.001), while potassium was excessively transferred to leaves (negative allometric growth between leaf K and branch K under F1 at 60 days, b = −0.419, P < 0.05).Under high-frequency fertilization stress, plants executed "stress-induced reprogramming," which not only disrupted internal nitrogen budgeting but also induced biased P and K cycling imbalances. This demonstrates that excessive nutrient input exacerbates inter-element allocation conflicts, leading to functional dysregulation in the plant's nutrient coordination network [13].

The stable nutrient supply under medium-frequency fertilization demonstrates synergistic and dynamically balanced allometric growth among organs. At 60 days after fertilization, the F2 treatment in Ochroma lagopus showed near-isometric growth between branch N and root N (b = 0.992, P < 0.05), indicating relatively balanced growth rates between these organs. This allocation strategy facilitates equitable resource utilization during growth, preventing excessive growth or deficiency in any particular organ. Simultaneously, a moderate positive allometric relationship between leaf N and root N (b = 0.201, P < 0.05) demonstrated that as root nitrogen content increased, leaf nitrogen content increased proportionally. This strategy allows the plant to maintain fundamental root structure while prioritizing additional nitrogen resources to leaves [32], thereby ensuring photosynthetic nitrogen demand while avoiding excessive nitrogen accumulation in roots [15].As growth progressed to 90 days, a significant negative allometric relationship between branch N and root N (b = −1.738, P < 0.001) indicated that as root N content increased, branch N content relatively decreased. This mechanism prevents nitrogen oversupply from inhibiting lignification [29], as excess nitrogen may cause over-expansion of plant cells, thereby affecting cell wall lignification and consequently compromising mechanical strength and structural stability. Meanwhile, a significant positive allometric relationship between leaf N and root N (b = 1.841, P < 0.001) showed that leaf N content increased faster than root N content. Since leaves are the primary site of photosynthesis, adequate nitrogen supply promotes chlorophyll synthesis and photosynthetic efficiency, thereby supporting plant growth and development [39], and this allocation strategy helps maintain highly efficient photosynthesis. The negative allometric relationship between leaf P and branch P (b = −0.777, P < 0.05) indicated that as leaf P content increased, branch P content decreased relatively. By allocating more phosphorus to leaves to support efficient photosynthesis and energy metabolism [26], potassium use efficiency is effectively optimized. The negative allometric relationship between branch K and root K (b = −0.625, P < 0.05) showed that Ochroma lagopus allocated more potassium to branches to support growth and water management, while reducing root K accumulation to avoid unnecessary resource expenditure, achieving functional synergy. By 120 days after fertilization cessation, the positive allometric index between branch N and root N reached 3.741 (P < 0.001). This highly significant positive allometric relationship indicates directional and efficient nitrogen translocation from roots to branches, providing nutrient support both for enhanced branch transport function and for leaf photosynthetic product translocation, while simultaneously accumulating nitrogen in branches as reserves for subsequent growth. Concurrently, a significant negative allometric relationship between leaf K and branch K (b = −7.611, P < 0.05) demonstrated preferential potassium retention in leaves to maintain chloroplast structural stability and ensure photosynthetic enzyme activity, thereby sustaining highly efficient photosynthetic function [35].The synergistic action of these two allometric relationships fully reflects the nutrient adaptation strategy of Ochroma lagopus at this stage: ensuring current photosynthetic function while reserving resources for future growth.

Under low-frequency fertilization conditions, Ochroma lagopus exhibited significant dynamic adjustments in the allometric growth relationships of N, P, and K among organs across different growth periods, reflecting its adaptive strategies to fluctuating nutrient supply. At 60 days, a positive allometric relationship between branch N and root N (index = 1.458, P < 0.05) indicated directional translocation of nitrogen from roots to branches for temporary storage, preparing for potential subsequent interruptions in nutrient supply. Simultaneously, a highly significant negative allometric relationship between leaf P and branch P (b = −0.593, P < 0.001) demonstrated preferential phosphorus retention in leaves, consistent with the principle of "P priority for photosynthetic energy metabolism" proposed by Klausmeier et al. [17], thereby ensuring stable photosynthetic function. As nutrient depletion intensified by 90 days, the positive allometric index for branch N versus root N decreased to 0.672 (P < 0.01), showing that the translocation rate of nitrogen from roots to branches slowed significantly due to insufficient supply. By 120 days, branch N and root N showed a highly significant negative allometric relationship (b = −2.299, P < 0.001), indicating massive nitrogen transfer from branches to roots to enhance long-term reserves. Concurrently, a highly significant positive allometric relationship between leaf N and branch N (b = 0.131, P < 0.001), combined with a significant allometric relationship between branch K and root K (P < 0.05), reflected a trend of potassium redistribution toward roots. These responses collectively formed a resilience strategy centered on "root nutrient reserve" to cope with environmental stress from inadequate nutrient supply under low-frequency fertilization.

Conclusion

This study systematically reveals the regulatory mechanisms of different water-soluble fertilizer application frequencies on the growth and nutrient allocation strategies of Ochroma lagopus. The research demonstrates that fertilization frequency, by modulating the dynamics of exogenous nutrient supply, significantly influences the tree height and diameter at breast height (DBH) of Ochroma lagopus, as well as its inter-organ nutrient allocation strategies across different growth periods and the allometric growth relationships among organs under various fertilization treatments. The differential responses of tree height and DBH to fertilization frequency essentially reflect a "growth strategy trade-off" employed by Ochroma lagopus in fluctuating nutrient environments. In the Xishuangbanna region, characterized by high rainfall and significant nutrient leaching risk during the rainy season, Ochroma lagopus adjusts its growth priority to adapt. When nutrient supply is relatively stable (F2 treatment), it preferentially adopts a "height growth" strategy to capture more light resources, aligning with the "competition strategy" of fast-growing tree species. Conversely, under longer fertilization intervals (F3 treatment), it shifts towards "diameter growth" to enhance plant stability. This trade-off mechanism enables Ochroma lagopus to maintain high growth efficiency across different nutrient supply patterns, representing a key ecological adaptation to the tropical monsoon climate. Analyzing changes in N, P, K contents and their stoichiometric ratios across organs reveals stage-specific adaptive characteristics in Ochroma lagopus: at 60 days after fertilization cessation, a competitive pattern of "source organs preempting nutrients" emerged, characterized by leaf nutrient enrichment but branch and root nutrient scarcity; by 90 days, it transitioned to a "source-sink-translocation coordination" cycle, with significant nutrient recovery in all organs and establishment of an aboveground-belowground synergistic nutrient cycle; by 120 days, it further differentiated into distinct "sink organ nutrient reserve" strategies. Regarding allometric growth relationship dynamics, different fertilization frequencies induced distinct nutrient allocation patterns: high-frequency fertilization (F1) caused inter-organ nutrient allocation imbalance and inter-element allocation conflicts; medium-frequency fertilization (F2) maintained synergistic and dynamic balance in inter-organ allometric growth, achieving balanced allocation at 60 days, bidirectional regulation at 90 days, and a nutrient adaptation strategy by 120 days that both secured current photosynthetic function and reserved resources for future growth; whereas low-frequency fertilization (F3) promoted a resilience strategy centered on "root nutrient reserve". The study confirms that medium-frequency fertilization (F2) performs optimally in maintaining inter-organ nutrient balance and promoting efficient nutrient utilization, providing a theoretical basis for fertilizing Ochroma lagopus plantations.

’t Hooft model in the temporal gauge
Paul Hoyer

Abstract
I consider QCD 2 in the N c → ∞ N_{c}\to\infty limit at fixed g 2 ​ N c g^{2}N_{c} . The derivation starts from equal-time q ​ q ¯ q{\bar{q}} bound states in coordinate space and temporal ( A 0 = 0 A^{0}=0 ) gauge, avoiding the use of quark and gluon propagators. The wave function is given analytically by a F 1 1 {}_{1}F_{1} function with an explicit frame dependence. In the infinite momentum frame the Fourier transformed wave function satisfies the ’t Hooft equation, however with contributions also from quarks with negative kinetic energy. Such contributions are present also in the rest frame, and do not vanish under boosts.

III.3

It is helpful to recall an analogous feature of the Dirac equation for an electron in a linear potential. This was noted already in 1932
[

, see also Sec. IV of
[

]
. For a large potential (

) the eigenstates of the Dirac equation include

, the pairs reside in
\left|{0}\right\rangle
. A basis without pairs is obtained by Bogoliubov transforming the free creation and annihilation operators in
\psi^{\dagger}(x)
. Then at small values of the potential (small
|x|

it is mostly a positron. This reveals the physics: A linear potential that confines electrons repels positrons. The Dirac wave function oscillates with phase
\propto x^{2}
at large
|x|
, describing positrons with momenta
\propto x
. In analogy to plane waves, the wave function is not normalizable and the energy levels are continuous.

The

and
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle{(P)}}_{3}(x)

, a “fragmentation” of bound state

. Physically, it is akin to string breaking,
i.e.
, the production/annihilation of a

pair by the bound state potential. Since the potential

(
8

limit, the fragmentation is not suppressed. The bound state overlap qualitatively resembles the dual features of hadron dynamics; initially produced (“parent”) states describe final (“daughter”) states in an average sense
[

VII
Discussion

Color confinement is a central feature of hadron dynamics. Numerical (lattice) calculations have determined that the hadron spectrum of QCD agrees with data
[
28
]
, whereas analytic methods remain elusive. Confinement has not been demonstrated using the formally exact Bethe-Salpeter
[
20
]
approach to bound states. QCD
2
may provide insights since the Coulomb potential is confining in
D=1+1
dimensions. ’t Hooft found
[
1
]
that the Bethe-Salpeter equation of QCD
2
can be solved exactly in the
N_{c}\to\infty
limit, which inspired many further studies.

The present study of the ’t Hooft model does not use the Bethe-Salpeter approach. This avoids quark propagators, which were found to be singular and to lack Lorentz covariance
[
8
]
. Instead, I directly determine the color singlet
\left|{q{\bar{q}}}\right\rangle
eigenstates (
6
) of the QCD
2
Hamiltonian (
14
). Poincaré covariance emerges in an explicit, yet nontrivial way for these equal-time states. The ’t Hooft equation (
88
) is reproduced (
87
) with a surprising twist: The wave function
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle{(\infty)}}({x_{B}})
(
69
) has negative energy components even in the IMF. Hence
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle{(\infty)}}({x_{B}})
does not vanish for
{x_{B}}\to 0,1
as postulated by ’t Hooft (Fig.
1
, right).

Negative energy solutions are familiar from the Dirac equation, but it may seem surprising that they survive in the IMF. Due to Lorentz contraction the potential is of
{\cal O}\left(1/P\right)
while the kinetic energies are of
{\cal O}\left(P\right)
. However, the kinetic energy contributions to the BSE cancel at
{\cal O}\left(P\right)
(
81
), leaving only
{\cal O}\left(1/P\right)
terms in the ’t Hooft equation. A bound state constituent with negative kinetic energy in the rest frame is boosted to a large negative energy in the IMF. This allows the energy and momentum
{x_{B}}P
of a quark to be balanced by that of an antiquark with
(1-{x_{B}})P
even if
{x_{B}}>1
.

The
q{\bar{q}}
wave function (
37
) oscillates
\propto\exp(iV^{\prime}x^{2}/4)
at large values of the variable
\tau_{P}(x)=M^{2}/V^{\prime}-2E_{P}|x|+V^{\prime}x^{2}
(
9
), and is thus not globally normalizable. There is a nonvanishing overlap
\langle{B\,C}|A\rangle
between bound states
A,B
and
C
, due to
q{\bar{q}}
production in the binding potential, with features expected of “string breaking”. When
E_{A}=E_{B}+E_{C}
this allows the decay
A\to B+C
, giving the bound states finite widths of
{\cal O}\left(g^{2}N_{c}\right)
. It has previously been assumed that decays are due to the interaction term of
{\cal O}\left(g\right)
, implying zero widths at leading order in
N_{c}\to\infty

[
6
,
*Witten:1979kh
]
. For
E_{A}\ll E_{B}+E_{C}
on the other hand the rapid oscillations of the wave function should suppress contributions to physical processes. Further study is required to establish whether these features can give an explanation of the duality observed in data on hadron dynamics
[
27
]
.

The present bound state method suggests a mechanism for confinement in
D=3+1
dimensions. In Sec.
VI.2
I noted that adding a homogeneous term (
105
) to the solution of Gauss’ constraint (
5
) implied an isotropic gluon field energy density
E_{\Lambda}
given by a new parameter
\Lambda
(
108
). This does not change the equations of motion and maintains full Poincaré covariance. Instead of taking the
N_{c}\to\infty
limit we may now set
N_{c}=3
and consider
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}g^{2}N_{c}\ll\Lambda^{2}
in (
109
). This allows a perturbative expansion in
g^{2}
, which at lowest order gives all the previous results vith
V^{\prime}=\Lambda^{2}
.

Adding a homogeneous solution similarly introduces a scale
\Lambda_{QCD}
and a confining instantaneous potential in
D=3+1

[
22
]
. Such a change of boundary condition for the gauge-dependent, longitudinal color electric field seems not to violate the basic principles of QCD. At
{\cal O}\left(\alpha_{s}^{0}\right)
the hadronic states and processes given by
\Lambda_{QCD}
qualitatively agree with data. The essential and nontrivial requirement of Poincaré covariance was verified for the electromagnetic form factor in
[
29
]
. The spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry required for a quantitative phenomenology of light hadrons remains to be studied.

Towards precision agriculture for assessing germination rates and density of rice seedling using hierarchical convolutional neural network on drone imagery
Authors: Sultan Almutairi, Eatedal Alabdulkreem, Nuha Alruwais, Hatim Dafaalla, Achraf Ben Miled, Haidar Almubarak, Nouf Atiahallah Alghanmi, Saad A AlOwayyed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRice is a significant food that plays a vital part in delivering nutrition to the world’s population. Hence, approaches for assessing rice yield have received considerable study. The amount of rice seedlings (density) is a main agronomic module. It is related to harvest and also plays a significant part in the survival rate. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are prepared with lightweight sensors, which creates a substantial effect in the field of crop phenotyping. The UAV was effectively used to measure germination rates and density in an accurate and effective method that would otherwise be laborious and expensive to obtain when compared to manual valuation. In image processing, mainly over the applications of deep learning (DL) models, there was a notable academic search for the value of UAV images for varied agricultural monitoring tasks. This work develops a Rice Seedlings for Assessing Germination Rates and Density using Aerial Images with Hierarchical Deep Network (RSAGRD-AIHDN) model. The goal of this paper is to assess germination rates and seedling density in rice fields using remote sensing (RS) or UAV-based imaging techniques for improved crop establishment monitoring. To accomplish that, the image pre-processing stage is initially applied with dual stages, such as image acquisition and pre-processing, to ensure high-quality and consistent inputs. Furthermore, the RSAGRD-AIHDN model employs the ConvNeXt method for the feature extraction process. For rice seed detection and classification, the RSAGRD-AIHDN model implements ensemble models, namely stacked autoencoder (SAE), bidirectional temporal convolution network (BiTCN), and Deep Q-Learning (DQL). The experimental assessment of the RSAGRD-AIHDN method is performed under the aerial dataset of rice seedlings. The experimentation of the RSAGRD-AIHDN method portrayed a superior accuracy value of 98.68% over existing approaches.Keywords: Rice seedlings, Germination rates, Density, Ensemble deep learning, Unmanned aerial vehicles, ConvNeXtSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

Rice is a fundamental staple food that holds a crucial role in supplying nutrition to a large portion of the global population1. Consequently, techniques for predicting rice yield have attracted considerable study focus. The quantity of rice seedlings in a specific region (density) is a significant agronomic factor. It is strongly linked with yield and plays a key part in assessing survival rates. From the breeding standpoint, scholars focus on developing new variations, and the survival rates of rice seedlings act as a standard for choosing breeding materials2. Therefore, precise calculation of rice seedling amounts is vital for predicting rice yield and serves as an essential aspect of field phenotyping. Currently, rice seedling counting mainly relies on manual labor. This model is both time-consuming and physically demanding for scholars managing large-scale field studies3. Hence, there is an urgent requirement for a rapid, non-invasive, and dependable method that can precisely count rice seedlings in agricultural fields. Measuring plant density can still be performed manually using different techniques, like systematic design counting, which helps evaluate various levels of plant density4. The need for crops compels the farming sector to embrace technology to tackle production issues5. Mainly, the rise in population puts continuous strain on the agricultural method to deliver more food to meet worldwide needs, prompting farmers to implement advanced technologies like precision agriculture in food crop cultivation.

Internationally, precision agriculture holds a vital function in enhancing crop quality, maintaining crop production, and making choices through the analysis of vast datasets and crop condition information collected from fields6. Additionally, it is applied for efficient fertilizer management and irrigation, along with minimizing labor. In real-world use, RS is commonly applied to aid precision agriculture in recent times7. With the benefit of technologies in obtaining a high-resolution (HR) image, primarily through the use of UAVs, a significant volume of RS information is effortlessly gathered to study crop yield in precision agriculture8. Presently, UAVs are employed in agricultural observation, including research evaluating potato above-ground biomass and yield9. In the area of land use assessment, UAVs are used for collecting higher-resolution (HR) images for vegetation classifications, along with incorporating UAV-equipped Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) systems to support thorough data acquisition and analysis. With advancements in computer vision (CV), cameras, and sensors combined with DL, there is a growing interest in gathering data and further study in smart agriculture. Smart farming holds a key position in farming processes by modifying different agricultural management practices10. It presents recommendations and visions for more productive and efficient crop production and to address the problems in agricultural systems. Due to improvements in computing power and a large volume of UAV imagery, DL methods are being revived and delivering numerous outcomes in agriculture for use.

Literature review on rice seedling detection

Yang et al.11 presented a non-contact quality evaluation method for rice mechanical transplanting depending upon UAV images. In particular, a semantic segmentation relies on an enhanced HRNet framework, termed RSHRNet. Employing a UAV image as the data resource, this method utilizes HRNet as the backbone network. Next, the coordinate attention mechanism (AM) and object-contextual representations (OCR) technique are consequently presented for meticulously aggregating contextual feature information and enhancing the method’s capability in extracting spatial positional information. Wu et al.12 proposed a new missing seedling recognition technique that depends on UAV image merged with rice transplanter operation information. At first, UAV obtained images of rice seedlings from a rice seedling recognition technique that met the precision necessity. Then, the transplanter’s transplanting trajectory is obtained depending on the navigation data and merged with parameters. Luo et al.13 introduced a CAD-UNet methodology to detect rice seedling rows using RS imagery and utilize assessment indicators like the seedling row’s parallelism and straightness to assess the rice transplanter’s operation qualities. Presented attention gate (AG) and convolutional block AM (CBAM) methods grounded in the novel UNet network, which can combine several information flows and feature maps jointly, assisting the model in better selecting important features or areas of seedling rows in images, thus refining the interpretation of image content and performance of task execution. Li et al.14 introduced RS-P2PNet that autonomously locates and counts rice seedlings using point supervision. Particularly, RS-P2PNet initially applies and presents mixed local channel attention (MLCA) in every phase.

Moreover, a multiscale feature fusion (MSFF) technique is presented by combining diverse feature levels from the backbone. It merges high-order semantic data and shallow information of rice seedlings, advancing the model’s positioning precision. Liu et al.15 proposed an assessment technique that relied on image recognition technologies. Based on the assessment technique, the main operational and structural parameters are enhanced. The authors16 presented an effective method that employs CV for exactly counting rice seedlings from digital images. At first, an UAV with RGB cameras was used to obtain field imagery. Following that, employ a regression network for regressing density maps. Anuar et al.17 focused on exploring numerous deep CNN (DCNN) paradigms to identify which one is effective for identifying defective paddy seedlings utilizing aerial images. The main drawbacks are identifying and counting every paddy seedling to detect planting density. The defective paddy seedlings’ locations aren’t highlighted to assist farmers during the sowing process. Zhao et al.18 addressed issues like low precision and efficacy related to manual observation for the assessment of rice seedling planting state. Hence, to create a seedling insertion state detection method, this investigation proposed a methodology that depends on the integration of DL and image processing. The image processing step is initially used for identifying missing seedlings, employing the centroid detection technique to acquire the missing seedling’s accurate coordinates.

Pre-processing techniques

Initially, the image pre-processing is executed in two phases, such as image acquisition and pre-processing to ensure high-quality inputs20. This model prioritizes high-quality, noise-free inputs that enhances classification reliability and feature learning in dense agricultural scenes. The techniques also utilize a patch-based classification model, making it more robust to occlusion and scale discrepancy in seedling clusters. The detector outputs are transformed into patch-level predictions for ensuring fair comparison, thus averting direct mAP-based evaluation and enabling consistent accuracy, precision, and recall assessment across all methods.

Image acquisition

While rice seedlings are increasing in the field, physical investigations are conducted by specialists. In every field, the technicians sample seedlings and examine the seedlings’ growth rate. If their seedlings revealed a similar phase of growth, it is recorded by the rice seedling period of the area. The images are gathered with the lens shooting, and the height is 3 m with a 0.08 cm/pixel. The neighbouring image and flight direction are met on average of one-third. A new size of an image is 5,472 × 3 642 pixels, and images are distinctly saved.

Image pre-processing

There is redundant data in the RGB image captured by a UAV, like fields and roads. Additionally, the massive size of the image is not well-suited for the application of ML. Thus, image pre-processing is essential. The image pre-processing model is chiefly partitioned into three major phases. Primarily, Gaussian filtering is implemented to decrease the noise after the imaging is converted to a gray scale. Subsequently, an improvement of the image is performed before edge gradient recognition. Thereafter, the seedling increasing areas are eliminated depending upon the edge gradient recognition, which means the RGB images were removed. Afterwards, the images are cropped to various pixel sizes, like 100 × 100, 200 × 200, 224 × 224, 300 × 300, 400 × 400, and 600 × 600. At last, images containing redundant data are extracted.

Deep feature learning via ConvNeXt

Furthermore, the RSAGRD-AIHDN utilizes the ConvNeXt method for the feature extraction process21. This method attains transformer-level analysis while also retraining convolutional efficiency. The model also exhibits robust representation capability and modernized CNN design and is excellent in capturing fine-grained texture and spatial patterns crucial for discriminating single and clustered rice seedlings. Moreover, the model is chosen for its uracy, scalability, and training stability with lower computational overhead than vision transformers. Figure 4 represents the framework of ConvNeXt.

Framework of ConvNeXt.

ConvNeXt depicts a vital development in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), combining the efficacy of classical CNNs with the advantages of Vision Transformer (ViT). The method is advanced to bridge the gap among these frameworks, like ImageNet classification, while preserving computational efficacy. To refine the ResNet-inspired CNN paradigm with recent novelties like simplified blocks, activation of GELU, and larger kernels, ConvNeXt provides a streamlined yet effective solution for intricate visual tasks. ConvNeXt is an innovative DL framework that combines various significant techniques to enhance the CNN performance in tasks of image recognition. It develops the principles of ResNet and presents various structural alterations that improve its capability to eliminate intricate features. Specifically, ConvNeXt highlights the utilization of depth-wise convolution and a huge kernel that enhances feature extraction at numerous scales and levels of abstraction. Depth-wise convolution presented in the structure of MobileNet decreases computation complexity while maintaining spatial data. Furthermore, accepting the massive size of the kernel permits ConvNeXt to collect a broader context, facilitating the method to recognize enhanced spatial relations and increase classifier precision.

ConvNeXt advantage from a CNN-transformer hybrid framework that presents the ability to acquire either global or local features in an image. Although classical CNNs excel at local feature elimination, transformers acquire longer-range dependency as well as context data. Integrating these dual elements permits ConvNeXt to employ the power of either transformer or CNN, making it appropriate for image recognition. Moreover, ConvNeXt’s frameworks integrate various components of the model that aid in decreasing overfitting and increasing generalization.

Ensemble-based detection models

For rice seed detection and classification, the RSAGRD-AIHDN method implements ensemble approaches, namely SAE, BiTCN, and DQL. Among the ensemble model, discriminative feature representation is improved by using SAE, while the contextual and spatial dependencies between neighboring patches are captured by the BiTCN model. The decision-making process is optimized by the DQL model. The integration also presents higher accuracy and adaptability compared to single-model or conventional classifiers. Furthermore, the DQL model is utilized for physical interaction and in the process, the state corresponds to the feature vector from SAE–BiTCN, the action is selecting a class label (single, clustered, bare land), and the reward is defined by correct or incorrect classification. Additionally, the feature learning and contextual modeling is performed by the SAE and BiTCN techniques, while DQL acts as a policy-based classifier and not a voting or averaging scheme.

SAE model

There are three major components with every AE, such as the bottleneck, encoder, and decoder22. Every AE is proficient in preserving vital facets and decreasing the dimension of data concurrently. Every encoder can identify notable features, but each decoder can reconstruct the unique data based on central facets. Although the major aspects are part of a feed-forward framework, every AE aims to decrease the size of the data. Various training processes are similar to traditional feed-forward architectures that focus on creating numerous outputs, depicting their inputs. Each AE makes a precise validation of input by reducing the number of active neurons present in the hidden layer (HL). It provides for employing several AEs for a complicated dataset with interconnected facets rather than relying on a single AE. Every encoder is structured in a vertical manner to contain the transiting input data.

Initially, the input data is added into AE, and the output of AE is the part of the input and the 2nd output of AE. Similarly, the 2nd output of AE has become the input of the initial AEs. Finally, the vector length of the input increases, which is doubled from the 2nd AE to the 3rd AE. The framework allows SAE to capture unique data that is proficient in enhancing their capability in scrutinizing and understanding the link between the features. SAE employs Stack Sparse Layers for every AE, and the output of HL serves as the succeeding input of HL.

Conclusion

In this study, the RSAGRD-AIHDN approach is proposed to evaluate germination rates and seedling density in rice fields utilizing RS-based imaging processes for better-quality crop establishment monitoring. To perform that, the proposed methodology has image preparation, ConvNeXt-based feature representation, and combined learning models. Initially, the image pre-processing stage is applied with two stages, like image acquisition and pre-processing, to ensure high-quality and consistent inputs. Furthermore, the ConvNeXt method is utilized for feature learning to increase the efficiency and precision of the assessment procedure in large-scale rice cultivation. Finally, the RSAGRD-AIHDN method implements ensemble models, namely the SAE, BiTCN, and DQL, for rice seed detection and classification. The experimental assessment of the RSAGRD-AIHDN method is performed under the aerial dataset of rice seedlings. The experimentation of the RSAGRD-AIHDN method portrayed a superior accuracy value of 98.68% over existing approaches. The limitations include generalization to varying illumination, weather, and field heterogeneity. Also, sampling bias can be seen due to restricted dataset size and geographic diversity. Furthermore, the evaluation depends on fixed-resolution image patches, which may overlook multi-scale contextual data. Manual annotation may also introduce labeling inconsistencies. Future work should incorporate massive multi- season and location datasets and real-time field deployment and edge-device optimization. Moreover, integrating agronomic ground-truth data will enable more precise estimation of crop establishment metrics.

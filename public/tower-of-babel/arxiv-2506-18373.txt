Topological Phase Transitions and Edge-State Transfer in Time-Multiplexed Quantum Walks
Huimin Wang, Zhihao Xu, Zhijian Li

Abstract
We investigate the topological phase transitions and edge-state properties of a time-multiplexed nonunitary quantum walk with sublattice symmetry. By constructing a Floquet operator incorporating tunable gain and loss, we systematically analyze both unitary and nonunitary regimes. In the unitary case, the conventional bulk-boundary correspondence (BBC) is preserved, with edge modes localized at opposite boundaries as predicted by topological invariants. In contrast, the nonunitary regime exhibits non-Hermitian skin effects, leading to a breakdown of the conventional BBC. By applying non-Bloch band theory and generalized Brillouin zones, we restore a generalized BBC and reveal a novel transfer phenomenon, where edge modes with different sublattice symmetries can become localized at the same boundary. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the structure of the spectral loops in the complex quasienergy plane provides a clear signature for these transfer behaviors. Our findings deepen the understanding of nonunitary topological phases and offer valuable insights for the experimental realization and control of edge states in non-Hermitian quantum systems.

II
A TIME-MULTIPLEXED NONUNITARY QW

We consider a discrete-time nonunitary QW on a one-dimensional (1D) finite chain, governed by the Floquet operator

U=C_{\theta_{2}/2}G_{\gamma}SC_{\theta_{1}}SG_{\gamma}C_{\theta_{2}/2},

(1)

which acts on the tensor product space
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{site}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{coin}}
, where
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{site}}
and
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{coin}}
denote the spatial and coin subspaces, respectively.
The coin operator
C_{\theta}
performs a rotation of the coin state by an angle
\theta
about the
y
axis, and is defined as

C_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\cos{\theta}&-\sin{\theta}\\
\sin{\theta}&\cos{\theta}\end{pmatrix},

(2)

where
n
labels the lattice sites and
N
denotes the system size. The coin-dependent shift operator
S
moves the walker to neighboring sites depending on its polarized states—horizontal (
\left|H\right\rangle
) or vertical (
\left|V\right\rangle
). Explicitly, it is given by

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left|n+1\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|\otimes\left|H\right\rangle\left\langle H\right|+\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n+1\right|\otimes\left|V\right\rangle\left\langle V\right|

\displaystyle+P\left(\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle N\right|\otimes\left|H\right\rangle\left\langle H\right|+\left|N\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|\otimes\left|V\right\rangle\left\langle V\right|\ \right)

\displaystyle+(1-P)\left(\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|\otimes\left|H\right\rangle\left\langle V\right|+\left|N\right\rangle\left\langle N\right|\otimes\left|V\right\rangle\left\langle H\right|\right),

(3)

where
P=1
corresponds to periodic boundary conditions (PBCs), and
P=0
corresponds to fixed boundary conditions (FBCs), in which case the walker’s polarization is flipped at the two ends of the chain. Nonunitarity is introduced via the gain and loss operator

G_{\gamma}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|\otimes\begin{pmatrix}e^{\gamma}&0\\
0&e^{-\gamma}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where
\gamma
is a tunable gain and loss parameter. The Floquet operator
U
is applied repeatedly to the walker’s state, governing the discrete-time Floquet dynamics. These dynamics can equivalently be described by an effective Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{eff}}
, with the Floquet operator
U=e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}}

Asbóth and Obuse 2013
. The quasienergy
E
of
H_{\mathrm{eff}}
is defined as
E=i\ln\lambda_{E}
, where
\lambda_{E}
denotes the eigenvalue of
U
.

Our system exhibits sublattice symmetry in the the Floquet operator
U
, characterized by the relation
\Gamma U\Gamma^{-1}=U^{-1}
, where the sublattice symmetry operator is defined as
\Gamma=\sum_{n}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|\otimes\sigma_{x}
, with
\sigma_{x}
being the standard Pauli matrix. This symmetry inherently protects the topological properties of the quasienergy spectrum. Furthermore, the Floquet operator
U
also respects time-reversal symmetry,
TU^{*}T^{-1}=U^{-1}
, and particle-hole symmetry,
\Xi U^{*}\Xi^{-1}=U
, where the time-reversal operator is
T=\sum_{n}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|\otimes\sigma_{x}
, and the particle-hole operator is
\Xi=\sum_{n}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|\otimes\sigma_{0}
with
\sigma_{0}
being a
2\times 2
identity matrix. Therefore, in the unitary limit, the system is classified into the
BDI
class within the the Altland-Zirnbauer (AZ) classification
Kawabata et al. 2019
, while for
\gamma\neq 0
, it falls into the
\text{D}^{\dagger}
class in the extended
\text{AZ}^{\dagger}
classification
Kawabata et al. 2019
.

For a QW with sublattice symmetry hosting a time-periodic feature, the quasienergy is
2\pi
-periodic
Rudner et al. 2013
;
Kitagawa 2012
, leading to an additional quasienergy gap at the zone boundary (
E=\pi
), alongside the conventional gap at
E=0
. Consequently, two distinct types of topological edge states may emerge, associated with nontrivial quasienergies at both
E=0
and
E=\pi

Kitagawa et al. 2010
;
Asbóth and Obuse 2013
. To characterize the topological phase transitions in our system, we apply the winding number
\nu
, defined in quasi-momentum
k
space under PBCs via the global Berry phase:
\nu=\varphi_{B}/2\pi
, where
\varphi_{B}=\varphi_{Z+}+\varphi_{Z-}
, and

\varphi_{Z\pm}=-\oint dk\frac{\left\langle\psi_{\pm}^{(L)}\right|i\nabla_{k}\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(R)}\right\rangle}{\left<\psi_{\pm}^{(L)}\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(R)}\right.\right>}.

(5)

Here, the right eigenstates
\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(R)}\right\rangle
and the left eigenstates
\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(L)}\right\rangle
are defined by
U\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(R)}\right\rangle=e^{-iE_{\pm}(k)}\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(R)}\right\rangle
and
U^{\dagger}\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(L)}\right\rangle=e^{iE^{*}_{\pm}(k)}\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(L)}\right\rangle
, with
E_{\pm}(k)=\pm\arccos D(k)
and
D(k)=\cos\left[2\left(k+i\gamma\right)\right]\cos\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}-\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}
. When
\gamma=0
,
\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(R)}\right\rangle=\left|\psi_{\pm}^{(L)}\right\rangle
. Following Ref.
Asbóth and Obuse 2013
, we further introduce an alternative winding number
\nu^{\prime}
, defined from the global Berry phase of the Floquet operator in a different time frame,
U^{\prime}=C_{\theta_{1}/2}SG_{\gamma}C_{\theta_{2}}G_{\gamma}SC_{\theta_{1}/2}
. The bulk topological invariants
\left(\nu_{0},\nu_{\pi}\right)
can then be constructed from winding numbers
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
as

\left(\nu_{0},\nu_{\pi}\right)=\left(\frac{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}{2},\frac{\nu-\nu^{\prime}}{2}\right),

(6)

which correspond to the emergence of quasienergy modes at
E=0
and
E=\pi
, respectively.

In the following, we compare the topological phase diagrams for both unitary and nonunitary cases, and demonstrate the the transfer characteristics of the edge states in the nonunitary QWs.

V
CONCLUSION

In summary, we have systematically studied topological phase transitions and edge-state behaviors in a time-multiplexed nonunitary QW with sublattice symmetry. In the unitary regime, conventional BBC holds, with edge modes localized at opposite boundaries. In the nonunitary regime, non-Hermitian skin effects emerge, and the conventional correspondence breaks down. By employing non-Bloch band theory and generalized Brillouin zones, we restored a generalized BBC and revealed a unique transfer phenomenon: edge modes with different sublattice symmetries can localize at the same boundary. Furthermore, we established that the structure of spectral loops under PBCs offers a clear signature for these transfer behaviors. Our results deepen the understanding of nonunitary topological systems and provide guidance for future experimental and theoretical studies.

\sqrt{\frac{1-\left|e^{2\gamma}Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|^{2}}{2\left(1+Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{2}\right)\left(1-\left|e^{2\gamma}Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|^{N}\right)}}\left|e^{2\gamma}Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|^{m-1}&\text{for}~\left|Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|\neq e^{-2\gamma}\end{cases},

(A17)

and
a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m}=Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m-1}
where
m=\left\{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \ldots,\ \frac{N}{2}\right\}
.

By the same way, for edge states in
\left|-\right\rangle
, we derive

a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m-1}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{1}{N\left(1+Z_{1,E\ }^{2}\right)}}&\text{for}~\left|Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|=e^{-2\gamma}\\
\sqrt{\frac{1-\left|e^{-2\gamma}Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|^{-2}}{2\left(1+Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{-2}\right)\left(1-\left|e^{-2\gamma}Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|^{-N}\right)}}\left|e^{-2\gamma}Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|^{-(m-1)}&\text{for}~\left|Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|\neq e^{-2\gamma}\end{cases},

(A18)

and
a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m}=-Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{-1}a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m-1}
where
m=\left\{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \ldots,\ \frac{N}{2}\right\}
.

In summary, the right eigenstates of the
\tilde{\alpha}
-modes can be written as:

\left|\psi_{\tilde{\alpha},\eta}^{(R)}\right\rangle=\sum_{m=1}^{N/2}{\left[a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m-1}^{(\eta)}\left|2m-1\right\rangle+a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m}^{(\eta)}\left|2m\right\rangle\right]}\otimes\left|C_{\eta}\right\rangle,

(A19)

where
\left|C_{\eta}\right\rangle=\left|H\right\rangle+\eta\left|V\right\rangle
and
\eta=\pm 1
, and

\begin{cases}a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m-1}^{(\eta)}=e^{2(m-1)\tilde{T}_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\eta)}\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{4\tilde{T}_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\eta)}}{2(1+Z^{2\eta}_{\tilde{\alpha}})[1-e^{2N\tilde{T}_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\eta)}]}}\\
a_{\tilde{\alpha},2m}^{(\eta)}=\eta Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{\eta}\alpha_{\tilde{\alpha},2m-1}^{(\eta)}\end{cases},

(A20)

where the inverse of the localization length for the nonunitary QWs
\tilde{T}_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\eta)=\gamma+T_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\eta)
depends on both the gain-loss parameter
\gamma
and
T_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\eta\ln\left|Z_{\tilde{\alpha}}R_{2}\right|
.

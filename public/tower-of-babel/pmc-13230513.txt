Diagnostic performance of antigen test for the detection of SARS-CoV-2 among patients presenting with COVID-19 symptoms at Bugando Medical Centre
Authors: Phares C. Lutema, Helmut Nyawale, Mathias Mlewa, Benson R. Kidenya, Evelyne T. Konje, Betrand Msemwa, Vitus Silago, Nyambura Moremi, Mohamed Mohamed, Mariam M. Mirambo, Stephen E. Mshana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) is a global public health threat causing Coronavirus Disease-2019 (COVID-19). The World Health Organization approved reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) as a gold standard for the detection of SARS-CoV-2. RT-PCR requires high technology and well-trained personnel, hindering its applicability in low-health care facilities. We evaluated the diagnostic performance of an antigen rapid diagnostic test (Ag-RDT) in detecting SARS-CoV-2 infection among patients presenting with COVID-19 symptoms at Bugando Medical Centre in Mwanza, Tanzania. This laboratory-based cross-sectional study involving 503 COVID-19 suspects was conducted in Mwanza between May 2022 and August 2022. The samples were tested by the RT-PCR and RADI COVID-19 Ag-RDT. Of 98 (20%) samples tested positive for both RT-PCR and Ag-RDT, 379 (75%) tested negative for both RT-PCR. The overall diagnostic performance of Ag-RDT showed 79.0% sensitivity and 100% specificity. During the early stage of cough with fever (≤ 4 days), Ag-RDT showed a high sensitivity of 86.1%. Ag-RDT can be used with much confidence in screening for SARS-CoV-2 infection in individuals presenting with a combination of cough and fever within the first four days of infection and cough was significantly associated with a positive Ag-RDT (p = 0.008).

Introduction

In December 2019, a pneumonia outbreak was reported in Wuhan, China, and quickly spread around the world, posing a significant public health threat. On January 9, 2020, it was officially confirmed that the outbreak in Wuhan, China, was caused by the new coronavirus 2019 (nCoV-2019)1–3. The novel coronavirus was later termed Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome-Coronavirus-2 (SARS-CoV-2) by the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses, and the disease was named Coronavirus Disease-2019 or COVID-19 by the World Health Organization (WHO)3.

SARS-CoV-2 genome was sequenced immediately after its discovery and became available online in January 20204. Since then, several methods have been developed for SARS-CoV-2 detection5,6. Among these methods were the real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR)7 and Antigen-detection rapid diagnostic test (Ag-RDT)8. When choosing the right test kit for SARS-CoV-2 detection, several factors have to be considered, including the context in which the test is being used, prevalence of SARS-CoV-2 in the population being tested, the status (signs, symptoms, close contacts) of the person being tested, affordability of the test kit in a given setting, and test performance of test kit in consideration9. The WHO approved RT-PCR to be the gold standard for the detection of SARS-CoV-210. The high accuracy and the possibility of batch testing that can be obtained by SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR testing has made the testing more useful for those requiring screening for travel and mass screening during the pandemic. However, the need of sophisticated facilities for sample processing, expensive laboratory instruments such as thermal cyclers, the need for trained personnel, and a long turnaround time compared with the number of samples needed for testing SARS-CoV-2 associated with SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR testing make the testing less useful in the community and post-pandemic SARS-CoV-2 serologic survey where rapid testing methods are more convenient11. SARS-CoV-2 immunoassay rapid testing methods such as antigen-detection rapid diagnostic tests (Ag-RDTs) are less expensive and can be used in point of care (POC) settings compared to the RT-PCR method. Antigen-detection rapid diagnostic test (Ag-RDT) is a simple and most rapid test method for SARS-CoV-2 detection. This method does not need centralized laboratory processing and can be performed in less than an hour at a lower cost compared to RT-PCR. Thus, Ag-RDTs can facilitate high-volume testing as well as offering rapid results12. The WHO suggests the use of Ag-RTD that meet least performance prerequisites of ≥ 80% sensitivity and ≥ 97% specificity. These kits are suggested to be used for primary case detection, contact tracing, outbreak investigation, and surveillance studies of COVID-19 in the community13. Because of the high global demand for SARS-CoV-2 test kits, the development and production of rapid test kits (Ag-RDT) by various manufacturers in the world have rapidly scaled up with limited performance data from Low- and Middle-Income Countries (LMICs)14. Tanzania is one of the LMICs and had high burden of SARS-CoV-2 infection and disease. In 2022, the SARS-CoV-2 IgM seropositivity was about 7%15. Tanzania therefore requires a simple and rapid validated SARS-CoV-2 diagnostic test that can be used for post-pandemic community surveys, POC settings and preparedness for future SARS-CoV-2 pandemics. The aim of this prospective, laboratory based cross-sectional study was to compare the utilities of Antigen test against RT-PCR as the gold standard and to assess the correlation of concordance results and the symptoms of COVID-19 using sample remnants from SARS-CoV-2 suspects brought to Bugando Medical Centre molecular laboratory for routine SARS-CoV-2 diagnosis.

Results

Sociodemographic and clinical characteristics of the study participants

A total of 503 samples from COVID-19 suspects were used in this cross-sectional study. Of these, half of them, 259 (51.49%), were from female patients with a median age of 52 (interquartile range [IQR: 37–70]) years.

The most frequent symptom reported was fever 282 (56.1%) followed by cough 268 (53.3%), shortness of breath 252 (50.1%) and abnormal chest x-ray 252 (50.1%), while the least frequent symptoms were dysgeusia 50 (9.9%), diarrhea 48 (9.5), irritability 41 (8.2) and vomiting 33 (6.6%) (Fig. 1). The median number of days of symptoms was 4(IQR: 2–7) days (Fig. 2).

Sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive value, positive and negative likelihood ratios, and kappa score of Ag-RDT in detection of SARS-CoV-2 as compared with RT-PCR

The performance of Ag-RDT was assessed against RT-PCR as the reference standard for testing SARS-CoV-2. Out of 503 samples, 98 (20%) tested positive by both RT-PCR and Ag-RDT, 379 (75%) tested negative for both RT-PCR and Ag-RDT, 26 (5%) samples had discordant results of RT-PCR positive and Ag-RDT negative. None of samples tested RT-PCR negative and Ag-RDT positive (Table 1). The Ag-RDT sensitivity and specificity were 79% and 100% respectively. The Ag-RDT negative and positive predictive values, and the negative and positive likelihood ratios for an estimated prevalence of 24.7% were 100% and 93.58%, 0.21 and ∞ respectively. Furthermore, the agreement of Ag-RDT with reference standard RT-PCR showed a perfect agreement with a kappa score of 86% (Table 2). Among the symptoms analyzed, presence of cough was significantly associated with a positive Ag-RDT (p = 0.008).

Sensitivity of Ag-RDT in the detection of SARS-CoV-2 with the duration of symptoms onset

The Ag-RDT sensitivity was 80.7% and 80.0% within the first four and seven days of cough onset respectively. The sensitivity was 86.7% and 82.8% in participants who presented with cough combined with fever within the first four and seven days respectively. The sensitivity was 75.0% and 71.4% among participants who presented without cough within first four and seven days of COVID-19 presumptive diagnosis respectively. Similarly, the sensitivity was 77.3% and 75.8% in participants who presented without cough and fever in combination within the first four and seven days of COVID-19 presumptive diagnosis respectively (Table 3).

Discussion

The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic faces many challenges in the identification of cases, mostly in low- and middle-income countries. This study evaluated the performance of the Ag-RDT test against RT-PCR as a gold standard. The median age of the COVID-19 suspects was 52 (IQR 37–70) years, which is in agreement with a previous report by Polyakova et al., which suggests that COVID-19 does not very much affect young individuals16. Among the COVID-19 suspects enrolled, the most frequent symptoms were fever, cough, general weakness, and shortness of breath in about half of the studied population. This observation is consistent with what has been reported by Dadok et al. among Polish patients17. The symptoms like diarrhea, vomiting, irritability and dysgeusia are the least common symptoms reported in the current study, which is similar to other previous reports among COVID-19 patients elsewhere18,19.

In evaluating the performance of Ag-RDT against RT-PCR, the current study shows sensitivity and specificity of 79.0% and 100%, respectively. Specificity of Ag-RDT in the current study is in agreement with the WHO recommendations requiring a rapid test device to have a specificity of at least 97%20. Although, the current study reports an overall sensitivity of 79.0% which is slightly low compared to the 80% which is recommended by the WHO20, the sensitivity among the symptomatic patients was ≥ 80% within the first four and seven days of presumptive SARS-CoV-2 onset. These findings are of particular interest for the proposed use of Ag-RDT as a reliable alternative to RT-PCR for the rapid detection of individuals with a high risk of infectivity in mass screening among the symptomatic individuals. This will benefit most of LMICs, especially during the outbreak of COVID-19, since most of these countries face socioeconomic insufficiencies and lack of public investment in extensive laboratory structures or highly skilled healthcare professionals to implement molecular techniques21. Ag-RDTs are not difficult to operate, permitting execution in decentralized settings. Moreover, Ag-RDT kits are likewise easy to perform and to read the results, cost-effective and expeditious, augmenting the speed of testing, recognizing and treating individuals with COVID-19 at the point of care.

Despite the fact that sensitivity and specificity are important intrinsic characteristics of a test, positive and negative predictive values are also important parameters for making public health decisions regarding the use of the test. In this report, positive predicting value (PPV) and negative predicting value (NPV) were 93.6% and 100%, respectively, implying that the kit has the probability of 0.936 of detecting true positives and the probability of 1 of confirming true negative cases. These findings are in line with those reported by Berger et al. in a community-based testing center in Geneva, Switzerland22. The Kappa statistics between Ag-RDT and RT-PCR was 94.83% vs. 65.47% indicating near perfect agreement, therefore, Ag-RDT can be used in settings where RT-PCR is unavailable. The results are in line with a previous report by Perez et al.23.

Among the signs and symptoms tested in the current study, only cough show significant association with the positive Ag-RDT results, which is in agreement with Martin et al. in a population of health workers exposed to COVID-19 patients in Spain24. This calls for the need for clinicians to consider coughing as one of the symptoms to be considered in the diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2 among COVID-19 suspects.

Our data show a greater Ag-RDT performance in the first seven days after symptom onset, then afterward the sensitivity decreases, which is consistent with a previous report by Basile et al.25. This could be explained by the high viral load associated with high virus dissemination before the immune system overcomes the virus. Therefore, it is necessary to take into account the duration of symptom onset when using Ag-RDT. One of the limitations of the current study is the use of transport medium, which might cause sample dilution resulting into reduced sensitivity of Ag-RDT26. However, the authors of this index study could not easily confirm if the RADI COVID-19 Ag Rapid test had been validated to be used with VTM samples. Furthermore, the current study did not evaluate the detection limit of the Ag-RDT, which is a very important parameter to consider before choosing the test kits. Based on the findings from this study, we conclude that Ag-RDT can be used with much confidence in screening for SARS-CoV-2 infection in symptomatic individuals particularly those presenting with a combination of cough and fever within the first four days of SARS-CoV-2 infection. We also recommend further studies to evaluate the current and other Ag-RDTs for the diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2, particularly in LMICs.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

Ethical clearance (CREC/571/2022) was sought from CUHAS/BMC Research Ethics and Review Committee (CREC). Informed consent was waived by the CREC as the study did not adversely affect the rights and welfare of the participants. Permission to conduct the study and use samples remnants of COVID-19 suspects was granted by the director of the BMC. Confidentiality was maintained throughout the study period. All research procedures and methods were performed in accordance with the respective guidelines and regulations.

Study design and setting

This prospective, laboratory-based cross-sectional study was conducted at Bugando Medical Centre (BMC) and Catholic University of Health and Allied Sciences (CUHAS) laboratories in Mwanza, Tanzania, from May 2022 to August 2022, involving a minimum sample size 503 which was calculated by Buderer’s formula27 as shown below.

Inclusion criteria

Only samples with a filled COVID-19 investigation form were included in this study.

Experimental protocol

Sample remnants from nasopharyngeal swabs collected from the COVID-19 suspect at the point of health care and brought to the BMC molecular laboratory for routine SARS-CoV-2 detection by real-time reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was used. The samples were collected from May 2022 to August 2022. For routine SARS-CoV-2 detection, nasopharyngeal swabs collected from SARS-CoV-2 suspect at the POC and immersed in 2 ml of viral transport medium (VTM) using an aseptic technique were brought to the BMC molecular laboratory which was the SARS-CoV-2 Lake Zone testing Centre. Nasopharyngeal swabs were collected and immersed in 2 ml of viral transport medium (VTM) using an aseptic technique. At BMC molecular laboratory, each individual specimen was tested for SARS-CoV-2 using the Abbott RealTime SARS-C0V-2 assay as per the manufacturer’s instructions28. Primer sets and TaqMan probes were designed to target RNA-dependent RNA Polymerase (RdRp) and N genes of SARS-CoV-2. Results were reported as positive when Ct ≤ 31.5, with S-shaped amplification curve was obtained and reported as negative when null Ct or Ct > 31.5 was observed. The assay has the detection limit of 100 copies/ml. The remained specimens of the tested samples were brought to CUHAS laboratory and briefly stored at −20 °C while waiting for immunoassay retesting by using rapid antigen (RADI COVID-19 Ag Rapid test, KH Medical Co. Ltd South Korea). RADI COVID-19 Ag-RDT detects nucleo-capsid protein of SARS-CoV-2 with a sensitivity and specificity of 97.3% and 100% respectively. These sensitivity and specificity figures were reported by the manufacture29. Briefly, 350 µl of extraction buffer was mixed in the extraction tube with 350 µl of the sample. Then the filter cap was screwed tightly onto the extraction tube. Then, 3 drops of each extracted specimen were applied to the specimen well of the test device. The results were read within 30 min. Two red colored lines (C and T) appeared in the result window if SARS-CoV-2 antigen was present in the sample, while for negative samples, one red-colored line (C) appeared in the result window. The invalid results were retested with the new test device30.

Data management and analysis

Data were entered into the logbook and then transferred into the computer using Microsoft Excel 2016. Data were cleaned and analyzed using STATA software version 15. Continuous data were summarized using a median with an interquartile range in case of skewed data and a mean with standard deviation for the normally distributed data. A contingency table was used to calculate the sensitivity, specificity, and predictive values of a test. Chi-square was used to determine the association between symptom duration and Ag-RDT results. The kappa statistic was used to calculate the percentage of agreement between tests (Ag-RDT and RT-PCR). In all analyses, a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Stationary Reweighting Yields Local Convergence of Soft Fitted Q-Iteration
Lars van der Laan, Nathan Kallus

Abstract
Fitted Q Q -iteration (FQI) and its entropy-regularized variant, soft FQI, are central tools for value-based model-free offline reinforcement learning, but can behave poorly under function approximation and distribution shift. In the entropy-regularized setting, we show that the soft Bellman operator is locally contractive in the stationary norm of the soft-optimal policy—not in the behavior norm used by standard FQI. This geometric mismatch explains the instability of soft Q Q -iteration with function approximation in the absence of Bellman completeness. To restore contraction, we introduce stationary-reweighted soft FQI , which reweights each regression update using the stationary distribution of the current policy. We prove local linear convergence under function approximation with geometrically damped weight-estimation errors, assuming approximate realizability. Our analysis further suggests that global convergence may be recovered by gradually reducing the softmax temperature, and that this continuation approach can extend to the hardmax limit under a mild margin condition.



1
Introduction

A core objective in reinforcement learning is to compute the optimal value
function, defined as the fixed point of a Bellman optimality equation

(
10
)
. The hard
\max
in this operator introduces
nondifferentiability and can exacerbate instability when combined with function
approximation and bootstrapping. Entropy regularization replaces the hard
maximum with a smooth softmax, yielding a more stable “soft” Bellman operator
that underlies many modern value-based algorithms

(
67
;
68
;
24
;
25
)
.

Fitted
Q
-iteration and its entropy-regularized analogue,
soft
FQI,
approximate this fixed point by iterating a projected Bellman update: each
iteration forms bootstrapped Bellman targets and regresses them onto a function
class. These algorithms are central in offline RL and large-scale approximate
dynamic programming
(
17
;
39
;
31
;
53
)
. When the function class is Bellman complete, classical
analyses establish finite-sample guarantees and contraction-like behavior for
the projected Bellman operator
(
23
;
50
;
37
;
47
;
18
)
.

However, fitted value-iteration methods such as FQI can degrade under function
approximation and distribution shift when Bellman completeness fails. Recent
information-theoretic and hardness results show that offline value-function
approximation can suffer poor time-horizon dependence or even exponential error
amplification without additional structural assumptions

(
16
;
19
;
61
;
4
)
. Classical examples of projected and bootstrapped operators

(
23
;
7
;
50
)
likewise
demonstrate instability under function approximation. These limitations have
motivated a line of minimax and adversarial soft-
Q
methods that relax
completeness via saddle-point formulations

(
51
;
52
;
28
;
63
;
53
)
. While these approaches provide a flexible alternative, they
optimize dual objectives and rely on adversarial losses and auxiliary critic
classes rather than directly iterating the Bellman operator.

Motivated by these challenges, our analysis identifies a more fundamental
source of instability: a
norm mismatch
between the regression update and
the soft Bellman operator. The regression step is performed under the behavior
distribution, whereas the soft Bellman operator is contractive—
locally
around its soft-optimal fixed point
—under the stationary distribution of the
soft-optimal policy. This mismatch breaks the fixed-point geometry inherited
from dynamic programming and explains why standard FQI can perform poorly
without completeness. It also suggests a simple remedy: reweight the regression
step so that it is performed in the stationary norm in which the Bellman
operator contracts.

This perspective is the natural
control analogue
of our companion
paper
56
, which shows that, in the simpler setting of
fitted Q evaluation with a fixed target policy, stationary weighting restores
global linear convergence without requiring Bellman completeness.

1.1
Contributions

Building on this geometric insight, our contributions are:

1.

Stationary-weighted soft
Q
-iteration.

We introduce a population-level projected soft Bellman operator whose projection
is defined in the stationary norm induced by the soft-optimal policy, and show
that this operator is locally contractive around the soft-optimal fixed point
under function approximation.

2.

Practical algorithm: stationary-reweighted soft FQI.

We propose a fitted
Q
-iteration procedure that reweights each regression
update using an estimate of the stationary density ratio of the current policy,
so that regression is performed in the stationary norm under which the soft
Bellman operator contracts.

3.

Local linear convergence under function approximation.

We establish finite-sample guarantees showing that the iterates converge
linearly within the local contraction region, with density-ratio estimation
errors entering only through a geometrically damped term.

4.

Mild structural assumptions.

Our analysis requires only:
(i) an approximate realizability condition on the function class; and
(ii) the existence of an admissible weighting function—such as the stationary
density ratio—which strictly relaxes Bellman completeness.

Scope and limitations.

Our guarantees are inherently
local
: soft FQI is contractive only within
a neighborhood of the fixed-point solution. Determining when practical algorithms enter this region, or whether our local convergence guarantees can be extended globally, remains an open question. A promising
direction is a continuation-style approach based on varying the softmax
temperature (Section
4
, Appendix
A
), which may
enlarge the convergence regime.

Practical implications.

We show that soft FQI can be stabilized via iterative
stationary
reweighting
, where each regression is weighted to approximate the stationary
distribution of the current policy. Convergence is sensitive to the choice of
initialization, with sensitivity increasing at lower temperatures. In practice,
a simple strategy is to warm-start at a high softmax temperature,
run the reweighted iterations to convergence, and then gradually decrease the
temperature. Preliminary theory suggests that this approach remains valid in
low-temperature regimes and extends to the hardmax under mild margin
conditions.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Soft Optimal Control

We consider a discounted MDP with state space
\mathcal{S}
, action space

\mathcal{A}
, transition kernel
P
, reward
r_{0}
, discount
\gamma\in[0,1)
,
and an arbitrary behavior distribution
\nu_{b}
over
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
.
We adopt the entropy-regularized (
soft
) control model used in soft FQI.
For any
Q:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
and temperature
\tau>0
,
the associated softmax policy is

\pi_{Q}(a\mid s)=\frac{\exp\{Q(s,a)/\tau\}}{\sum_{b\in\mathcal{A}}\exp\{Q(s,b)/\tau\}}.

Soft evaluation operator.

Let
\mathsf{H}(\pi_{Q}(\cdot\mid S^{\prime}))
denote the Shannon entropy of
\pi_{Q}
at
S^{\prime}
.
Define the soft reward

\tilde{r}_{Q}(s,a):=r_{0}(s,a)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{S^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a)}\bigl[\tau\,\mathsf{H}(\pi_{Q}(\cdot\mid S^{\prime}))\bigr],

and the evaluation operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{Q}f(s,a)

\displaystyle:=\tilde{r}_{Q}(s,a)+\gamma\,(P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{Q}}f)(s,a),

\displaystyle(P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{Q}}H)(s,a)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{S^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a),\,A^{\prime}\sim\pi_{Q}(\cdot\mid S^{\prime})}\bigl[f(S^{\prime},A^{\prime})\bigr].

Soft Bellman operator.

Setting
f=Q
in
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{Q}f
gives the soft Bellman optimality operator

(\mathcal{T}Q)(s,a)=r_{0}(s,a)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{P(\cdot\mid s,a)}\!\left[\tau\log\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}e^{\,Q(S^{\prime},a^{\prime})/\tau}\right],

where the expectation is over
S^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a)
. There is a unique bounded fixed point
Q^{\star}=\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}
and induced soft optimal policy

\pi_{\star}:=\pi_{Q^{\star}}
.

Stationary measure and norm.

Let
\mu_{\star}:=\mu_{\pi_{\star}}
denote the stationary state–action distribution of the chain
induced by
(\pi_{\star},P)
, i.e.,
\mu_{\star}=\mu_{\star}\pi_{\star}P
.
We measure errors in the stationary
L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
norm,

\|f\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}:=\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\star}}[f(S,A)^{2}]\bigr)^{1/2},

the geometry under which
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{\star}}
is a

\gamma
-contraction
(
12
;
50
;
26
)
.

2.2
Local Geometry of the Soft Bellman Operator

To analyze the local dynamics of fitted
Q
-iteration, we characterize the local
geometry of the soft Bellman operator
\mathcal{T}
in a neighborhood of its
fixed point
Q^{\star}
. Our starting point is that the local behavior of

\mathcal{T}
is governed by the Bellman evaluation operator

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{Q^{\star}}
associated with the soft-optimal
policy. The following lemma formalizes this observation.

Lemma 1

(First derivative of the soft Bellman operator)
.

For any
Q
and perturbation
H
, the Fréchet derivative of
\mathcal{T}
at
Q

is

D\mathcal{T}(Q)[H]\;=\;\gamma\,P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{Q}}H.

In particular, at
Q^{\star}
,
\|D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})[H]\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

Linearizing
\mathcal{T}
around
Q^{\star}
yields the approximation

\mathcal{T}(Q)\;\approx\;Q^{\star}+\gamma\,P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{\star}}(Q-Q^{\star})\;=\;\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{Q^{\star}}(Q),

(1)

where we used the fixed-point identity

Q^{\star}=\tilde{r}_{Q^{\star}}+\gamma\,P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{\star}}Q^{\star}
.
Since
P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{\star}}
is nonexpansive in the stationary

L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
norm, the evaluation operator

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{Q^{\star}}
is a
\gamma
-contraction.
As a result,
\mathcal{T}
inherits a local contraction behavior in a
neighborhood of
Q^{\star}
. This observation identifies the relevant local
geometry for analyzing and stabilizing soft
Q
-iteration, and it motivates
the projection and reweighting schemes developed in subsequent sections.

The validity of this linear approximation hinges on controlling the nonlinear
remainder of
\mathcal{T}
. To this end, we restrict attention to a sufficiently
smooth convex function class
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
and impose
regularity conditions under which the nonlinear remainder of
\mathcal{T}
is
well controlled on
\mathcal{F}
, ensuring that
(
1
) remains locally accurate.

C1)

Stationary action positivity.

There exists
\pi_{\min}\in(0,1)
such that

\pi_{\star}(A\mid S)\geq\pi_{\min}
holds
\mu_{\star}
-almost surely.

C2)

Model convexity.

\mathcal{F}\subseteq L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
is a convex function class.

C3)

Hölder-type
L^{\infty}
–
L^{2}
relation.

There exist
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
C_{\infty}<\infty
such that for all

Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})\cup\{Q^{\star}\}
,

\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{\infty}\,\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{\,\alpha}.

Under these conditions, the following lemma establishes a second-order bound on
the remainder of the linear approximation.

Lemma 2

(Second-order remainder bound)
.

Under Conditions
C1
–
C3
, for all

Q\in\mathcal{F}
,

\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(Q)-\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{eval}}_{Q^{\star}}(Q)\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}}{2}\,\|Q-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{1+\alpha},

where

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}:=\frac{\gamma}{2\tau}C_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\pi_{\min}}}.

Condition
C1
ensures that the soft–optimal policy assigns each
action uniformly positive probability, which in turn guarantees uniform
boundedness of
Q^{\star}
. Many commonly used function classes
satisfy
C2
, including linear models, RKHSs
(
36
)
,
total-variation classes
(
11
)
, Barron-type neural networks

(
6
)
, and classical Hölder and Sobolev smoothness classes

(
1
;
49
, e.g.,)
.

Condition
C3
provides a local Hölder-type relationship between
the stationary
L^{2}
and sup norms. This condition controls the local
curvature of
\mathcal{T}
over
\mathcal{F}
, and requires only mild
smoothness of
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
, and
Q^{\star}
. Condition
C3
is satisfied by many standard smoothness classes.
For finite-dimensional linear models of dimension
p
, it holds with

C_{\infty}\asymp\sqrt{p}
and
\alpha=1
.
For reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces with eigenvalue decay

\lambda_{j}\asymp j^{-2r}
, one may take

\alpha=\tfrac{2r}{2r+1}

(
36

.
It also holds for signed convex hulls of suitable bases

(
54
, Lemma 2)
, and for
d

of order
s>d/2
on bounded domains, where

\alpha=1-\tfrac{d}{2s}

(
15
;

1
;
49

3.1
Soft
Q
-iteration and local contraction

The preceding section established that the soft Bellman operator
\mathcal{T}

is locally contractive around its fixed point
Q^{\star}
in the stationary norm

L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
. We now show how this local contraction geometry governs the
behavior of soft
Q
-iteration when updates are restricted to a function class

\mathcal{F}
, and why projections must be performed in the stationary norm to
preserve this contraction structure
(
56
)
.

We therefore analyze soft
Q
-iteration with projections taken under the
stationary norm. In the population limit, this yields the
stationary-projected update

Q^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(Q^{(k)}\bigr),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q):=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathcal{T}(Q),

defined by iterating the associated fixed-point map. Here,
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}

denotes the orthogonal projection onto the closure
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
of
the convex class
\mathcal{F}
under the stationary norm

\|\cdot\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
. We refer to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
as the

projected soft Bellman operator
. This update differs from the usual
behavior-weighted iteration whenever Bellman completeness fails
(
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})\not\subseteq\mathcal{F}
), and it aligns the update with
the norm in which the soft Bellman operator is locally contractive.

The stationary distribution
\mu_{\star}
is typically unknown, as it depends on
the soft-optimal policy and hence on
Q^{\star}
. The update above should therefore
be viewed as a population-level idealization rather than a directly
implementable algorithm. In Section
3.4
, we show how this
population update can be approximated in practice by performing projections
under the stationary distribution induced by the current iterate
Q^{(k)}
,
leading to a stationary
reweighting
procedure. Building on this, and
accounting for the fact that projections must be approximated via regression,
we present a fitted version of this procedure in Section
4

Having defined the projected update and its algorithmic idealization, we now
formalize the local contraction property of projected soft
Q
-iteration. This
requires introducing several key geometric quantities. The
misspecification gap
of
\mathcal{F}
is

d(\mathcal{F},Q^{\star}):=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

the minimum
L^{2}(\mu_{\star})

Q^{\star}
and
\mathcal{F}
.
For any
r>0
and
Q\in L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
, define the ball

\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q):=\{\,f\in\mathcal{F}:\|f-Q\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\leq r\,\}\cup\{Q^{\star}\}.

We also define the local contraction radius

r_{0}:=\Bigl(\frac{1-\gamma}{\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}}\Bigr)^{1/\alpha},\qquad\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}:=\frac{\gamma}{2\tau}\,C_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\pi_{\min}}}.

We refer to
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r_{0},Q^{\star})

3.2
Local linear convergence of Soft
Q
-Iteration

The local contraction property of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
immediately
implies linear convergence of the population projected soft
Q
-iteration in a
neighborhood of its fixed point. We formalize this result in this subsection.

When
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
exists, we define the approximation error

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}:=\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

which measures how well the projected fixed point approximates the true soft
optimum and, in particular, its displacement from the center of the contraction
region
\mathbb{B}(r_{0},Q^{\star})
.

We define the
basin of attraction
as the ball

\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r_{\max},Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}),\qquad r_{\max}:=r_{0}-\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}.

It is the largest ball centered at
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
on which

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is guaranteed to contract toward
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}

(and on which
\mathcal{T}
contracts toward
Q^{\star}
), as visualized in
Figure
1
. To ensure that this set is nonempty,
we impose a mild relaxation of realizability.

C4)

Approximate realizability.

Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
exists and satisfies

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;\leq\;\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\,(1-\gamma)\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{(\alpha+1)\,\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

By Lemma
3
, a sufficient condition for
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
to exist is that the same bound placed on

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
also holds for
d(\mathcal{F},Q^{\star})
. Thus,
C4
is slightly stronger than mere existence of
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

With these definitions in place, we now state the main consequence of the local
geometry: inside the basin of attraction, the population soft Bellman update
enjoys local linear convergence.

Theorem 2

(Local linear convergence around the projected soft optimum)
.

Assume
C1
–
C4
hold.
Let
r>0
satisfy

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;\leq\;r<r_{\max},\qquad\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r):=\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,(r+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}})^{\alpha}.

Then, for any initialization
Q^{(0)}\!\in\!\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})
and all
k\geq 0
,

\|Q^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)^{k}\,\|Q^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

In words, the theorem shows that soft
Q
-iteration behaves locally around

Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star}
as a contraction mapping: within any admissible ball

\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
with
r<r_{\max}
, each
iterate moves toward
Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star}
at rate

\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)
, converging to
Q^{\star}
up to the irreducible
misspecification error
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
. By the triangle
inequality,
\|Q^{(k)}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
is at most the bound for

\|Q^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
plus the approximation
error
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
. Because
\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}
increases with
|\mathcal{A}|
and
1/\tau
, the convergence
rate deteriorates and the contraction basin shrinks as the action space grows or
the temperature decreases. In particular, our results do not guarantee
convergence in the infinite-action limit
(|\mathcal{A}|\uparrow\infty)
, the
hard-max limit
(\tau\downarrow 0)
, or the undiscounted case
(\gamma=1)
without
additional assumptions.

To build intuition, Figure
1
illustrates a gravitational
analogy.
Consider any
Q\in\mathbb{B}(r_{\max},Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})
lying in the
basin of attraction.
Within the contraction region
\mathbb{B}(r_{0},Q^{\star})
, iteration of

\mathcal{T}
exerts a pull toward
Q^{\star}
at rate
\rho(r^{\star})
, where

r^{\star}=d(Q,Q^{\star})
.
The projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
adds a force
that keeps the trajectory inside
\mathcal{F}
.
The combined dynamics yield an effective attraction toward the projected
optimum
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
with rate
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)
, where

r=d(Q,Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})
, and with strength governed by the local
curvature parameter
(\beta_{\mathrm{loc}},\alpha)
.

3.3
Implications of the Local Contraction Theorem

Effect of misspecification.

Misspecification limits both the attainable contraction rate and the final
accuracy. Since any admissible radius satisfies
r\geq\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
,
the best possible contraction modulus is

\rho_{\min}=\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
, which approaches

\gamma
only when
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
is small. Meanwhile, the iterates
converge to the projected optimum
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
, leaving an
irreducible error of size
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
relative to the true soft
optimum
Q^{\star}
.

Basin of attraction.
The geometry of the basin
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r_{\max},Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})

governs how quickly the iterates contract. Inside this region, the modulus
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)

decreases with
r
and approaches
\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}
at the fixed point, yielding
progressively stronger contraction. The next result quantifies this finite-time tightening along the trajectory when
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}=0
.

Corollary 1

(Finite-time tightening of the contraction rate)
.

Assume
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}=Q^{\star}
and the conditions of
Theorem
2
. Let

e_{k}:=\|Q^{(k)}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
and

\rho:=\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}r^{\alpha}
. Then the induced per-iteration modulus

\rho_{k}:=\frac{e_{k+1}}{e_{k}}\leq\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,e_{k}^{\alpha}

tightens geometrically:

\rho_{k}-\gamma\;\leq\;\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,e_{0}^{\alpha}\,\rho^{\,\alpha k},\qquad k\geq 0.

Role of the Hölder exponent.

By Lemma
2
, the exponent
\alpha
quantifies the local smoothness (curvature) of the Bellman operator
\mathcal{T}
around
Q^{\star}
. As a consequence, by Corollary
1
, it determines how rapidly the contraction modulus tightens as the iterates move through the basin of attraction. When
\alpha=1
, the remainder is quadratic, and the contraction modulus tightens at the fast rate
\rho_{k}-\gamma\;\leq\;\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,e_{0}\,\rho^{\,k}.

Remark (completeness and basin entry).

If
\mathcal{F}
is
soft Bellman complete
(
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{F}
),
the projection is vacuous and the update becomes
Q^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{T}(Q^{(k)})
,
so soft Q-iteration converges
globally
to
Q^{\star}
at rate
\gamma

in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
Under approximate completeness,

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{F}}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}(Q)-\mathcal{T}(Q)\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon

implies the standard perturbation bound

\|Q^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma^{k}\|Q^{(0)}-Q^{\star}\|_{\infty}+\tfrac{3\varepsilon}{1-\gamma}.

Hence the iterates enter the local contraction region whenever

3\varepsilon/(1-\gamma)<r_{\max}
, after which
Theorem
2
ensures linear convergence.

3.4
Stationary-Reweighted Soft
Q
-Iteration

We begin with a population version of stationary-reweighted soft
Q
-iteration,
assuming that the behavior distribution
\nu_{b}
, reward function
r_{0}
, and
transition kernel
P
are known. The update is a simple modification of standard

Q
-iteration: at each iteration, we reweight the regression by the density ratio
of the stationary distribution of the current policy
\pi_{Q^{(k)}}
, thereby
aligning the regression geometry with the stationary norm in which the soft
Bellman operator contracts.

Admissible weighting functions.

Our theory does not require the exact stationary ratio. Instead, it assumes the
existence of a weighting function that induces the correct projection geometry.
This requirement is substantially weaker than Bellman completeness
(
\mathcal{T}=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}
), under which
any
weighting
function is admissible.

C5)

Weighted Bellman projection orthogonality.

There exists
w_{\mathcal{F}}\in L^{2}(\nu_{b})
such that for all
f,Q\in\mathcal{F}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{b}}\!\Big[w_{\mathcal{F}}(S,A)\,f(S,A)\,\bigl(\mathcal{T}Q-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}Q\bigr)(S,A)\Bigr]=0.

Condition
C5
ensures that projecting
\mathcal{T}Q
onto

\mathcal{F}
in the stationary norm
L^{2}(\mu^{\star})
is equivalent to projecting
it in the weighted behavior norm
L^{2}(w_{\mathcal{F}}\nu_{b})
. Consequently, the
projected fixed point
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
is shared by both projected
operators. The condition is satisfied by choosing

w_{\mathcal{F}}=d\mu_{\pi^{\star}}/d\nu_{b}
, in which case it reduces to
a stationary–behavior overlap requirement. It holds trivially under
Bellman completeness since the projection error is then orthogonal to

\mathcal{F}
under
any
weighting.

The existence of a weighting function that achieves this geometric
alignment places our setting outside the minimax hardness regime of

19
and is the key structural requirement for our local convergence guarantee.

In this work, we let
w_{\mathcal{F}}
denote any weight
function satisfying
C5
. In algorithmic and informal
discussions, we use the canonical choice where
w_{\mathcal{F}}
is the
stationary density ratio.

Algorithm.

Initialize
Q^{(0)}\in\mathcal{F}
in the basin of attraction

\mathbb{B}(r_{\max},Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
At iteration
k\in\{0,\dots,K\}
, compute the stationary ratio

w_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}\propto d\mu_{\pi_{Q^{(k)}}}/d\nu_{b}
for the induced
policy
\pi_{Q^{(k)}}
. Then update

Q^{(k+1)}:=\argmin_{Q\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{b}}\!\left[w_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}(S,A)\,\bigl\{(\mathcal{T}Q^{(k)}-Q)(S,A)\bigr\}^{2}\right].

This iteration approximates the fixed-point map described in
Section
3.1
, with the weights
w_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}

aligning the behavior-distribution regression norm with the stationary geometry
of the policies
\{\pi_{Q^{(k)}}\}
.

4
Stationary-Reweighted Soft FQI

We now move from the population operator to a practical, finite-sample
algorithm. In
stationary-reweighted soft FQI
, both the stationary
weights and the Bellman targets are estimated from offline, off-policy data.

Algorithm.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}:=\{(S_{i},A_{i},R_{i},S_{i}^{\prime})\}_{i=1}^{n}
denote a batch of
transitions collected under the behavior distribution
\nu_{b}
, and let

\mathcal{F}
be the chosen function class.
Initialize
\widehat{Q}^{(0)}
, form its softmax policy
\pi_{\widehat{Q}^{(0)}}
,
and estimate initial weights
\widehat{w}_{\mathcal{F}}^{(0)}
by approximating
the stationary ratio
d\mu_{\pi_{\widehat{Q}^{(0)}}}/d\nu_{b}
(or any weight
function satisfying
C5
).

For each
k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
, form the empirical soft Bellman targets

\widehat{y}_{i}^{(k)}=R_{i}+\gamma\tau\log\sum_{a^{\prime}}\exp\!\bigl(\widehat{Q}^{(k)}(S_{i}^{\prime},a^{\prime})/\tau\bigr).

Update the
Q
-estimate by

\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}\in\argmin_{Q\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{w}_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}(S_{i},A_{i})\,\bigl(\widehat{y}_{i}^{(k)}-Q(S_{i},A_{i})\bigr)^{2},

and re-estimate an admissible stationary ratio for the induced policy

\pi_{\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}}
to obtain updated weights

\widehat{w}_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k+1)}
.

Estimating stationary ratios.

The stationary density ratio
d\mu_{\pi}/d\nu_{b}
can be estimated using
stationarity-matching objectives from the DICE family of methods

(
40
;
66
;
51
;
32
;
33
)
.
In our setting, these ratios are used solely to learn a stationary weighting
function
w_{\mathcal{F}}
rather than to construct unbiased value estimators.
Because the weights influence only the regression loss used for projection, even
coarse or misspecified estimates (e.g., simple linear models) can improve the
stability of FQI under violations of Bellman completeness.

Periodic vs. continuous weight updates.

Weights may be updated at every iteration or only periodically. Continuous
updates more closely track the evolving policy and maintain the stationary
geometry, whereas periodic updates reduce computation and remain valid as long
as the iterates stay within the local basin.

Initialization and basin entry.

Our local guarantees require the iterates to enter the contraction basin

\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r_{\max},Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
In practice, a short warm start—for example, a few iterations of unweighted
soft FQI or any preliminary estimator—can move the initialization closer to the
fixed point.

Global convergence via temperature homotopy.

A practical way to ensure that the initialization lies inside the local
contraction region is to begin with a sufficiently large temperature
\tau
,
for which the operator is smoother and the contraction radius scales as

r_{0}\asymp\tau^{1/\alpha}
.
This motivates a simple
temperature–homotopy
strategy: warm-start at a
high temperature, run the stationary-reweighted updates to convergence, then
gradually decrease
\tau
, re-solving at each stage while remaining within each
stage’s contraction region.
For
\tau>\tau^{\star}>1
, the drift between successive soft-optimal value
functions satisfies

Q^{\star}(\tau)-Q^{\star}(\tau^{\star})=O(\tau-\tau^{\star})
,
ensuring stability of the homotopy path under small temperature steps.
This continuation viewpoint parallels classical homotopy methods in nonlinear
optimization
(
2
;
29
;
42
, e.g.,)
.
In the low-temperature regime (
\tau\in(0,1]
), preliminary analysis
(Appendix
A
) suggests that the homotopy remains valid under
additional margin and action-gap conditions on the hard-max value function

(
53
)
, which keep the contraction radius bounded as

\tau\downarrow 0
.

Sources of error.

The algorithm incurs three sources of approximation:
(i) sampling error in the empirical Bellman targets;
(ii) function-approximation error from restricting to
Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}
; and
(iii) estimation error in the weights
\widehat{w}_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}
.
Because the weights are constructed from
\widehat{Q}^{(k)}
, they inherit the
value-estimation error in addition to the stationary-ratio error.
All three components are reflected in the finite-sample analysis below.

5
Finite-Sample Convergence Analysis

5.1
Inexact Updates Inside the Local Basin

Our finite-sample analysis departs from the classical global arguments used for
fitted value iteration
(
37
)
and develops a new

local
analysis tailored to the stationary-reweighted and
entropy-regularized setting. Instead of relying on global (sup-norm)
contractivity, we characterize a neighborhood around the soft optimum in which
the projected soft Bellman update is strictly contractive in the stationary
geometry. We derive an inexact-iteration recursion that quantifies how
approximation errors perturb this contraction and show that these errors remain
small enough to keep the iterates inside the basin with high probability.
Together, these steps yield a finite-sample recursion with geometrically damped
errors and linear convergence up to an explicit error floor.

We begin with an inexact-iteration lemma, a local analogue of the classical
recursion of
37
.

Lemma 4

(Inexact iteration error bound)
.

Assume
C1
-
C5
. Fix any
0<r<r_{\max}
and let
\rho_{\star}:=\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)
be the local
contraction modulus from Theorem
2
.
For
k\geq 0
, consider inexact updates

\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\!\left(\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\right)+\vec{\xi}_{k},\qquad\|\vec{\xi}_{k}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\leq\xi_{k}.

Assume
\|\widehat{Q}^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\leq r
and

\xi_{k}\leq(1-\rho_{\star})\,r
for all
k\geq 0
.
Then, for every
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\star}^{k}\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\rho_{\star}^{\,k-1-j}\,\xi_{j}.

To apply Lemma
4
, we require a high-probability bound on
the inexactness term
\|\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

which must account for sampling noise, approximation error, and weight-estimation error.
The per-iteration inexactness constraint ensures these errors remain small enough to keep
the iterates within the local basin
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
Under these conditions, Lemma
4
yields finite-sample linear
convergence up to the error floor
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\rho^{\,k-1-j}\,\xi_{j}
.

Next we obtain high-probability bounds by characterizing the statistical
complexity of the weighted Bellman regression problems at each iteration.

5.2
Finite-Sample Bounds on the Update Error

We characterize the update error in terms of the statistical complexity of the
functions involved in each regression step. Define the extended class

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}:=\mathcal{F}\;\cup\;\bigl\{\pi_{Q}f:f\in\mathcal{F},\;Q\in\mathcal{F}\bigr\},

which captures both elements of
\mathcal{F}
and their soft–Bellman-evaluated
counterparts under policies induced by
\mathcal{F}
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let

N(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}},L^{2}(Q))

denote the
\varepsilon
–covering number of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}
under

L^{2}(Q)
.
Define the uniform entropy integral
(
57
)

\mathcal{J}(\delta,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}):=\int_{0}^{\delta}\sup_{Q}\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}},L^{2}(Q))}\,d\varepsilon,

and let the associated critical radius
(
8
)
be

\delta_{n}:=\inf\Bigl\{\delta>0:\frac{\mathcal{J}(\delta,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}})}{\sqrt{n}\,\delta^{2}}\leq 1\Bigr\}.

C6)

Stationary–weighted norm equivalence.

There exist
0<c_{0}\leq c_{1}<\infty
such that for all

f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
,

c_{0}\|f\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\|f\|_{2,\,w_{\mathcal{F}}\nu_{b}}\;\leq\;c_{1}\|f\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

C7)

Uniform boundedness.

The functions
w_{\mathcal{F}}
,
1/w_{\mathcal{F}}
, the reward
R
, and the
iterates

\{\widehat{w}_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)},\,\widehat{Q}^{(k)},\,\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})\}

are uniformly bounded by a constant
M<\infty
.

C8)

Sample splitting.

The weight estimators
\{\widehat{w}_{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{K}
are computed
using data independent of the batch
\mathcal{D}_{n}
used for the regression
updates.

C9)

Complexity control.

It holds that

\mathcal{J}(\infty,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}})<\infty
and

\frac{\mathcal{J}(\delta,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ext}})}{\delta\sqrt{\log\!\log(1/\delta)}}\;\to\;\infty\qquad\text{as }\delta\to 0.

Condition
C6
translates weighted–behavior errors into stationary-norm
guarantees and is automatically satisfied when
w_{\mathcal{F}}
is the stationary
density ratio.
C7
is standard and can be relaxed via
truncation.
C8
ensures independence between weight estimation and
regression and can be removed via cross-fitting

(
45
;
20
)
. Finally,
C9
controls localized Rademacher complexities,
following
37
.

Fix a horizon
K\geq 1
and confidence level
\eta\in(0,1)
, and define the
statistical error

\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K):=\delta_{n}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(K/\eta)}{n}},

and the weight error

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k):=\bigl\|\widehat{w}^{(k)}_{\mathcal{F}}/w_{\mathcal{F}}-1\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

We further define the local Bellman approximation error

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)

\displaystyle:=\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\;\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\,\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

This quantity vanishes under Bellman completeness, and is small whenever

\widehat{Q}^{(k)}
and
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
lie close to
Q^{\star}
.

Lemma 5

(Statistical accuracy of one weighted regression)
.

Assume Conditions
C3
,
C5
–
C9
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
and
0\leq k\leq K-1
,
with probability at least
1-\eta
,

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;

\displaystyle C\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)

\displaystyle+\,C\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}\;+\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\right).

The first term is the statistical error
\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)

expected from empirical risk minimization over
\mathcal{F}
. As

\alpha\to 0
, the dominant weight contribution is
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)
,
whereas as
\alpha\to 1
the leading term becomes

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)
. This reveals a
double-robust structure: the error vanishes if either the density-ratio
estimate is accurate or the Bellman approximation error is small. In particular, approximate Bellman completeness can offset weight
misspecification when
\mathcal{F}
is sufficiently smooth (i.e., for larger

\alpha
).

For
s
–sparse linear models in dimension
d
,

\delta_{n}\asymp\sqrt{s\log(d/s)/n}

(
59
)
.
For VC-subgraph classes of dimension
V
,

\delta_{n}\asymp\sqrt{V\log(n/V)/n}

(
57
)
.
For Hölder or Sobolev classes of smoothness
s
with
d\leq 2s
,

\delta_{n}\asymp n^{-s/(2s+d)}

(
44
)
.
For finite Hardy–Krause variation classes,

\delta_{n}\asymp n^{-1/3}(\log n)^{2(d-1)/3}

(
14
)
.

5.3
Finite-Sample Convergence Bound (main result)

With high-probability bounds for the per-iteration errors in hand, we combine
Lemma
4
and Lemma
5
to obtain our
main finite-sample convergence bound.

We define the per–iteration inexactness

\displaystyle\xi_{k}

\displaystyle:=C\Bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\Bigr),

where
C
is the constant in Lemma
5
. We introduce the weight–adjusted contraction factor

\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k):=\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr),

and its worst–case version

\rho_{\star}(r):=\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{wt}}\bigr),\qquad\bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{wt}}:=\max_{0\leq j\leq K-1}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(j).

C10)

Basin stability.

There exists
r\in(0,r_{\max})
such that, for all
0\leq k\leq K-1
,

\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k)<1,\qquad\xi_{k}\;\leq\;\bigl(1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k)\bigr)\,r.

Condition
C10
is a mild local stability requirement: it ensures
that the update remains contractive on a radius-
r
neighborhood around

Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star}
and that the per-iteration inexactness is too small to
push the iterate outside this ball. The inequality
\xi_{k}\leq(1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k))\,r

guarantees that, after the contractive part of the update pulls the iterate
to radius
\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k)\,r
, the remaining additive
error keeps it inside
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
As
\xi_{k}
decreases (e.g., at larger sample sizes), it becomes
increasingly easy to choose an
r\in(0,r_{\max})
for which
this condition holds.

Our main convergence theorem is stated below. To quantify the total
weight–estimation error, we define the discounted
\alpha
-power
cumulative error

\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,\alpha):=(1-\rho_{\star})\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\rho_{\star}^{\,k-1-j}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(j)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}},

a geometrically discounted and
\alpha
-scaled aggregation that
vanishes when the weight errors are small and
\alpha\to 1
.

Theorem 3

(Local convergence with geometrically damped weight error)
.

Assume Conditions
C1
–
C10
hold for some

r\in(0,r_{\max})
with
\rho_{\star}:=\rho_{\star}(r)
, and that

\widehat{Q}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
Then, there exists
C^{\prime}<\infty
, with probability at least
1-\eta
, for all
1\leq k\leq K
,

\displaystyle\bigl\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\rho_{\star}^{\,k}\,\bigl\|\widehat{Q}^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\quad+\;\frac{C^{\prime}}{1-\rho_{\star}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)

\displaystyle\quad+\;\frac{C^{\prime}}{1-\rho_{\star}}\Bigl(\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,\alpha)+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,0)\Bigr).

Structure of the final error bound.

Theorem
3
yields an inexact Picard recursion: the bias
contracts at rate
\rho_{\star}
, while the statistical error

\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)
and the discounted weight errors

\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,\alpha)
enter additively with the usual

(1-\rho_{\star})^{-1}
factor. This mirrors the classical sup-norm analysis of

37
, but now in the locally contractive stationary

L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
geometry. Stationary reweighting restores a genuine fixed
point: weight-induced errors are
geometrically damped
through

\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,\alpha)
, leaving only the approximation term

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,0)
. In contrast with
existing FQI analyses, our guarantees do not require Bellman completeness and
instead rely on the weaker weighted orthogonality condition
C5

together with approximate realizability.

A consequence of Theorem
3
is that the iterate
error depends superlinearly on the weight errors, through

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{1/(1-\alpha)}
, up to the approximation floor

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
. Hence, even without refreshing the weights at every
update, the iterates contract to a neighborhood whose radius is determined by
the statistical error, a power of the weight errors, and
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Geometric error decay under weight refreshing.

When the weights improve over iterations, the effective error floor decreases
and near-geometric convergence is restored. If

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)
decays geometrically as

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)=b^{k}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(0)
with

b\in(0,1)
, then so does
\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k,\alpha)
, at the faster of
the rates
b^{1/(1-\alpha)}
and
\rho_{\star}
(up to a polynomial factor).
Combined with Theorem
3
, this yields geometric decay of
the iterate error up to the irreducible statistical and approximation floors.

The preceding argument assumed
a priori
geometric decay of the weight
errors. In practice, however, these errors depend on the iterates themselves,
creating a circular dependence: contraction of
\widehat{Q}^{(k)}
requires
small weight errors, while reduction of the weight errors requires the iterates
to stay within the contraction region. The next theorem resolves this by
introducing a mild polynomial coupling between iterate and weight errors,
yielding a joint recursion that controls both simultaneously. This provides
convergence guarantees based solely on the initialization error, without any
assumptions on how the weight errors evolve.

C11)

Weight–error coupling.

For any
\eta\in(0,1)
, there exist constants
C^{\prime}<\infty
and

\kappa>0
such that, with probability at least
1-\eta
, the following
holds for all
0\leq k\leq K-1
:

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\;\leq\;C^{\prime}\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{\kappa}\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K).

Condition
C11
decomposes the weight–estimation error at
round
k
into a component controlled by the iterate error and a statistical
remainder
\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)
.

We define the
r
-dependent per–iteration inexactness

\xi_{\star}(r,k):=\Delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n)+\Phi(r),

where the nonlinear error term is

\Phi(r):=r^{1+\kappa}+r^{\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,r^{\kappa},

and the total statistical error is

\Delta_{\mathrm{stat}}:=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K).

For a constant
B<\infty
determined only by the structural constants in our
assumptions, define the contraction factor

\bar{\rho}(r):=\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)+B\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K).

C12)

Basin stability.

There exists
r\in(0,r_{\max})
such that, for all
0\leq k\leq K-1
,

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(r)<1,\qquad B\,\xi_{\star}(r,k)\leq(1-\bar{\rho}(r))\,r.

This condition is akin to
C10
but involves only initialization and statistical components.

Theorem 4

(Local convergence with decoupled errors)
.

Assume Conditions
C1
–
C9
,

C11
, and
C12

hold for some
r\in(0,r_{\max})
with
\bar{\rho}:=\bar{\rho}(r)
, and that

\widehat{Q}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
Then, with probability at least
1-\eta
, the errors
e_{k}:=\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star}\|_{2,\mu^{\star}}
satisfy, for some
constant
C<\infty
and all
1\leq k\leq K
,

\displaystyle e_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\rho}^{\,k}e_{0}\;+\;B^{\prime}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{\rho}^{\,k-1-j}\,\Phi(e_{j})\;+\;B^{\prime}\,\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{stat}}}{1-\bar{\rho}}.

(4)

Theorem
4
shows that the iterates contract at the
geometric rate
\bar{\rho}^{\,k}e_{0}
, up to a discounted sum of the nonlinear errors
\Phi(e_{j})
. When
\kappa>1-\alpha
, all terms in
\Phi(e_{j})
are strictly higher
order near the fixed point, and the remaining
e_{j}^{\kappa}
term is scaled by
the approximation error
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}
. In this regime, the sum

not higher order, so the theorem guarantees convergence only to a radius
determined by

7
Discussion and Conclusion

This work develops a fitted iterative regression procedure with theoretical
guarantees for local contraction and linear convergence, without requiring
realizability or Bellman completeness. A central challenge in fitted methods is
that the soft Bellman operator is contractive in the stationary norm of the
target policy, whereas regression is carried out under the behavior
distribution. We show that stationary reweighting reduces this mismatch by
bringing the regression geometry closer to the operator’s natural norm,
yielding a locally contractive mapping with an explicit basin of attraction and
a contraction modulus approaching
\gamma
. These guarantees are inherently

local
: linear convergence holds only within a neighborhood of the fixed
point.

Several directions remain open. A key challenge is to design reliable
initialization strategies, such as warm starts or temperature–homotopy
procedures as discussed in Section
4
. Our preliminary analysis in
Appendix
A
suggests that a homotopy over the softmax
temperature may expand the region in which local convergence can be certified
and, under suitable margin conditions, may yield analogous guarantees for
hardmax FQI in the low-temperature limit. Finally, while our focus here has
been primarily theoretical, an empirical evaluation of
stationary-reweighted fitted soft
Q
-iteration—including comparisons with
existing minimax and density-ratio–based methods—remains an important next
step.

Appendix A
Towards Global Convergence via Temperature Homotopy

A.1
Overview of challenges

Global convergence via temperature homotopy.

Our local linear convergence guarantees require that the initialization lie
sufficiently inside the contraction region. A natural way to ensure this is to
begin with a large softmax temperature
\tau
, for which the contraction radius

r_{0}(\tau)
grows as
\tau^{1/\alpha}
while the soft–hard bias scales only as

O(\tau)
. Thus, when
\tau>1
, the stationary-reweighted update reliably
converges to
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}(\tau)
from essentially any reasonable
initialization. This suggests a
temperature–homotopy
strategy: warm-start
at a large temperature, solve the projected Bellman equation, then decrease

\tau
gradually while keeping each intermediate iterate inside its associated
local basin. This continuation viewpoint parallels classical homotopy methods
for extending local fixed-point analyses

(
2
;
29
;
42
, e.g.,)
.

The main difficulty is that for
\tau<1
, the baseline curvature bound

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau)=O(1/\tau)
causes the contraction region to shrink faster
than the
O(\tau)
bias, so the continuation path may exit the local basin as

\tau\downarrow 0
. Under a uniform action-gap condition at the hard-max
solution, however, the curvature decays exponentially in
1/\tau
, yielding an
enhanced radius
r_{0}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)
that remains bounded away from zero
as
\tau\downarrow 0
. This restores the viability of the homotopy path down to
small temperatures.

The next subsection formalizes the structural assumptions under which these
refinements hold and sketches how our local theory can, in principle, be
extended toward global behavior. A full theoretical and empirical development
of temperature–homotopy schemes remains an interesting direction for future
work.

Remark.

For fixed
\tau
, one may instead use a
discount–factor homotopy
, slowly
increasing
\gamma
so that the soft-optimal fixed point drifts smoothly and the
iterates remain within each stage’s local contraction region. Since

r_{0}(\gamma,\tau)\asymp\bigl((1-\gamma)/\gamma\bigr)^{1/\alpha}
for fixed
\tau
,
the step sizes required to stay within the basin shrink rapidly as

\gamma\uparrow 1
, reflecting the collapse of the contraction region at long
horizons. In practice, combining temperature and discount–factor homotopies
may further improve stability.

A.2
A Path Toward Global Behavior: Assumptions and Heuristics

In this section we index the softmax temperature by
\tau
, writing

Q^{\star}(\tau)
for the soft-optimal value function and

Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}
for the hard-optimal limit. Under
Conditions
C1
–
C3
, our local theory yields

r_{0}(\tau)\asymp\tau^{1/\alpha}
and

\|Q^{\star}(\tau)-Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}\|_{\infty}=O(\tau)
. As

\tau\downarrow 0
, however, the curvature parameter
\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau)
grows
as
O(1/\tau)
, causing the contraction region to collapse. To extend the
analysis to small temperatures, one must prevent this collapse.

A natural structural requirement is a uniform action gap at the hard-max
optimum. Under this margin condition (together with a local
gap-invariance assumption), the covariance term in the second derivative of the
soft Bellman operator decays exponentially in
1/\tau
. This yields a much
smaller effective curvature
\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau)
and maintains a nonvanishing
contraction radius down to
\tau\approx 0
, suggesting a path toward a global
analysis via a temperature–homotopy argument.

To formalize the improved curvature bound, we introduce two structural
assumptions used only in this appendix:

S1

Uniform action gap.

There exists
\Delta>0
such that,
\mu_{\star}
-almost surely, for all
a\neq A^{\star}(S)
,

Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}(S,A^{\star}(S))\;\geq\;Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}(S,a)+2\Delta,

for a measurable optimal action
A^{\star}(S)
.

S2

Local neighborhood around
Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}
.

Let
r_{\mathrm{gap}}>0
satisfy

C_{\infty}\,r_{\mathrm{gap}}^{\alpha}\;\leq\;\Delta,

and define the localized region

\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}:=\bigl\{\,Q\in\mathcal{F}:\|Q-Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\leq r_{\mathrm{gap}}\bigr\}.

For any
Q\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
, the action
A^{\star}(S)
remains
uniquely optimal with margin at least
\Delta
.

Under Assumptions
S1
–
S2
,
Lemma
8
can be substituted for
Lemma
7
throughout the local analysis,
replacing
\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau)
by

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau):=\frac{\gamma}{\tau}\,C_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\pi_{\min}}}\,C_{\mathrm{gap}}\,e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}.

The admissible contraction radius must then lie inside both the geometric radius
and the margin-stable ball
\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
, giving

r_{0}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau):=\min\!\left\{r_{\mathrm{gap}},\;\Bigl(\frac{1-\gamma}{\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)}\Bigr)^{1/\alpha}\right\}.

All subsequent local contraction and linear-convergence statements hold with

r_{0}(\tau)
replaced by
r_{0}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)
and

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau)
replaced by
\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)

informally extending the same arguments
, provided the contraction ball
around

remains inside
\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
.

A possible route toward global convergence.

By combining the
\tau>1
and
\tau<1
regimes, one obtains a continuous
temperature–homotopy path. One can take
\tau
sufficiently large to ensure
initialization within a contraction region and then decrease
\tau
gradually.
As
\tau\downarrow 0
, the contraction region stabilizes to

\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
. Heuristically, if the fitted soft
Q
-iteration
iterates remain within the appropriate local basin for all intermediate
temperatures, the solution should converge even at temperatures arbitrarily
close to the hard-optimal limit.

Formalizing this continuation argument presents several challenges. Our current
bounds are expressed in the stationary norm induced by the
\tau
-dependent
soft-optimal policy, which varies with temperature. A full temperature–homotopy
analysis would require a common, temperature-independent norm; the stationary
norm associated with the hard-max optimal policy is a natural candidate for
controlling the entire path.

Appendix B
Gradient-Descent Interpretation

A useful perspective on the projected soft Bellman update is obtained by writing
it in residual form. Define

F_{\mathcal{F}}(Q):=Q-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q),

so the population iteration becomes

Q^{(k+1)}=Q^{(k)}-F_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{(k)}).

This is a unit-step projected gradient method, and its behavior near
Q^{\star}

is governed by the local geometry of
F_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Suppose for simplicity that
Q^{\star}
lies in the interior of
\mathcal{F}
, so
that the projection is smooth in a neighborhood of
Q^{\star}
. At the
soft-optimal fixed point
Q^{\star}
, the Jacobian of
F_{\mathcal{F}}
is

DF_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})=I-\gamma\,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\,T_{\pi_{\star}}^{\mathrm{eval}}.

Because
T_{\pi_{\star}}^{\mathrm{eval}}
is a
\gamma
-contraction in the
stationary norm and
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
is nonexpansive in this geometry, the
operator
\gamma\,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}T_{\pi_{\star}}^{\mathrm{eval}}
has norm at
most
\gamma
. Consequently,
DF_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})
is positive definite on
the action-differential subspace, implying that
F_{\mathcal{F}}
is locally

strongly monotone
and smooth in a neighborhood of
Q^{\star}

(
9
)
. In this region, the projected soft Bellman update
behaves like gradient descent on a locally strongly convex, smooth objective.

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}}{2}\,\|Q-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{1+\alpha}.

∎

C.3
Better temperature dependence of curvature under margin conditions.

The next lemma refines the previous curvature bound under additional structural
assumptions. Under a uniform action gap and within a suitably small

L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
-ball around
Q^{\star}
, the second derivative of

\mathcal{T}_{\tau}
admits an improved bound of order

\tfrac{1}{\tau}e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}
. This refinement is strictly local
around
Q^{\star}
and strengthens the curvature bound of
Lemma
7
, which is used throughout the main
text.

Lemma 8

(Local second-derivative bound under an action gap)
.

Assume Conditions
C1
–
C3
and the structural
assumptions
S1
–
S2
. Then there exists a finite constant

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau):=\frac{\gamma}{\tau}\,C_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\pi_{\min}}}\,C_{\mathrm{gap}}\,e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}

for some
C_{\mathrm{gap}}<\infty
such that, for
all
Q\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
and all directions

H_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{diff}}
,
H_{2}\in L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
,

\bigl\|D^{2}\mathcal{T}(Q)[H_{1},H_{2}]\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)\,\|H_{1}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{\alpha}\,\|H_{2}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

(7)

In particular, for fixed
\Delta>0
the local curvature decays as

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)=O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\tau}e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}\right)
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same structure as
Lemma
7
, with an improved bound on the
covariance term under the action-gap condition. By
Lemma
6
,

D^{2}\mathcal{T}(Q)[H_{1},H_{2}]
can be written in terms of

\mathrm{Cov}_{\pi_{Q}}\bigl(H_{1}(S^{\prime},\cdot),H_{2}(S^{\prime},\cdot)\bigr)
. Fix
S^{\prime}
and write

h_{i}:=H_{i}(S^{\prime},\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|}
and

p_{Q}:=\pi_{Q}(\cdot\mid S^{\prime})
. The gap assumption at
Q^{\star}_{\mathrm{hard}}
,
together with Condition
C3
and the choice of
r_{\mathrm{gap}}
,
implies that for all
Q\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
the same action
A^{\star}(S^{\prime})

remains uniquely optimal and

Q(S^{\prime},A^{\star}(S^{\prime}))\;\geq\;Q(S^{\prime},a)+\Delta\quad\text{for all }a\neq A^{\star}(S^{\prime}).

By the definition of the softmax policy,

p_{Q}(a)=\frac{\exp\{Q(S^{\prime},a)/\tau\}}{\sum_{b\in\mathcal{A}}\exp\{Q(S^{\prime},b)/\tau\}},\qquad a\in\mathcal{A}.

For any
a\neq A^{\star}(S^{\prime})
we have

\frac{p_{Q}(a)}{p_{Q}(A^{\star}(S^{\prime}))}=\exp\!\left(\frac{Q(S^{\prime},a)-Q(S^{\prime},A^{\star}(S^{\prime}))}{\tau}\right)\;\leq\;e^{-\Delta/\tau},

so
p_{Q}(a)\leq e^{-\Delta/\tau}
and

\sum_{a\neq A^{\star}(S^{\prime})}p_{Q}(a)\;\leq\;(|\mathcal{A}|-1)e^{-\Delta/\tau}=:\varepsilon.

Thus
p_{Q}(A^{\star}(S^{\prime}))\geq 1-\varepsilon
, and we may take

C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{gap}}:=|\mathcal{A}|-1
so that

p_{Q}(A^{\star}(S^{\prime}))\geq 1-C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{gap}}e^{-\Delta/\tau}
.

Next, consider the covariance matrix

\Sigma_{Q}:=\mathrm{diag}(p_{Q})-p_{Q}p_{Q}^{\top}
. For any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|}
,

h^{\top}\Sigma_{Q}h=\Var_{\pi_{Q}}\bigl(h(A)\bigr).

Let
a^{\star}:=A^{\star}(S^{\prime})
and write
\varepsilon:=\sum_{a\neq a^{\star}}p_{Q}(a)

as above, and set
m:=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{Q}}[h(A)]
. Then

\displaystyle\Var_{\pi_{Q}}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}p_{Q}(a)\bigl(h(a)-m\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{a}p_{Q}(a)\bigl(h(a)-h(a^{\star})\bigr)^{2}+2\bigl(h(a^{\star})-m\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{a\neq a^{\star}}p_{Q}(a)\bigl(h(a)-h(a^{\star})\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{gap}}\,\varepsilon\,\|h\|_{2}^{2},

where we used
(x-y)^{2}\leq 2(x^{2}+y^{2})
and absorbed numerical constants into

C_{\mathrm{gap}}
. Since
\varepsilon\leq C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{gap}}e^{-\Delta/\tau}
, we obtain

\Var_{\pi_{Q}}(h)\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{gap}}e^{-\Delta/\tau}\,\|h\|_{2}^{2}.

Using
e^{-\Delta/\tau}\leq e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}
for
\tau>0
, we can absorb the
resulting change into the constant and write

\Var_{\pi_{Q}}(h)\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{gap}}e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}\,\|h\|_{2}^{2}.

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the covariance,

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_{\pi_{Q}}(h_{1},h_{2})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\Var_{\pi_{Q}}(h_{1})\,\Var_{\pi_{Q}}(h_{2})}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{gap}}e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}\,\|h_{1}\|_{2}\,\|h_{2}\|_{2}.

Substituting this bound into the argument of
Lemma
7
, and repeating the same steps
(Jensen’s inequality, stationarity of
\mu_{\star}
, and the

\pi_{\min}
-lower bound), we obtain

\displaystyle\bigl\|D^{2}\mathcal{T}(Q)[H_{1},H_{2}]\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\pi_{\min}}\,C_{\infty}^{2}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\tau}\,C_{\mathrm{gap}}e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}\right)^{\!2}\|H_{1}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{2\alpha}\,\|H_{2}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{2},

for all
Q\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
, which yields
(
7
) with the stated

\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{gap}}(\tau)
. The bound is uniform over

Q\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{gap}}
and depends on
\tau
only through the
prefactor
\tfrac{1}{\tau}e^{-\Delta/(2\tau)}
.
∎

Proof of local contraction theorem

Proof of Theorem
1
.

First, since
r>d(\mathcal{F},Q^{\star})
, we have that
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star})
is the intersection of a
closed ball (which is convex) and a convex set. It is therefore also convex. It is also
nonempty because
r>d(\mathcal{F},Q^{\star})
and

\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}
with

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}=d(\mathcal{F},Q^{\star})<r
. Fix
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star})
and set
H:=Q_{1}-Q_{2}
. For

t\in[0,1]
, define the path
Q_{t}:=Q_{2}+tH\in\mathcal{F}
. By the
fundamental theorem of calculus in Banach spaces,

\mathcal{T}(Q_{1})-\mathcal{T}(Q_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})[H]\,dt.

Taking
L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
norms and using the triangle inequality,

\|\mathcal{T}(Q_{1})-\mathcal{T}(Q_{2})\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{1}\|D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})[H]\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\,dt.

(8)

We now bound
\|D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})[H]\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
uniformly over

t\in[0,1]
and
Q_{t}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{}}{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star})
.
Add and subtract the derivative at the optimum:

D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})[H]=D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})[H]+\bigl(D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})-D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr)[H].

Using Lemma
1
and
Lemma
7
, we control each term separately.

First, at
Q^{\star}
,

\|D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})[H]\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}=\gamma\,\|P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{\star}}H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

(9)

since
P^{\mathrm{eval}}_{\pi_{\star}}
is nonexpansive in
L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
.

Second, by the mean–value representation of the derivative,

\displaystyle\bigl(D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})-D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr)[H]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}\mathcal{T}\!\left(Q^{\star}+u\,(Q_{t}-Q^{\star})\right)\bigl[\,Q_{t}-Q^{\star},\;H\,\bigr]\,du.

Applying Lemma
7
with

H_{1}=Q_{t}-Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{diff}}
and

H_{2}=H\in L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
gives

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\bigl(D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})-D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr)[H]\Bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{1}\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,\|Q_{t}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}^{\alpha}\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\,du.

where we used that
Q_{t}-Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{diff}}
because

Q_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
Q_{t}
lies on the segment between
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
,
both in
\mathbb{\mathbb{}}{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star})
,

\|Q_{t}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\max\bigl\{\|Q_{1}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},\|Q_{2}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\bigr\}\;\leq\;r.

Thus,

\Bigl\|\bigl(D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})-D\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr)[H]\Bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,r^{\alpha}\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

(10)

Combining the bounds in (
9
) and (
10
),
we obtain, for all
t\in[0,1]
,

\|D\mathcal{T}(Q_{t})[H]\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;+\;\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\,r^{\alpha}\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}=\rho(r)\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

where
\rho(r):=\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}r^{\alpha}
. Substituting this bound into
(
8
) yields

\|\mathcal{T}(Q_{1})-\mathcal{T}(Q_{2})\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{1}\rho(r)\,\|H\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\,dt=\rho(r)\,\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

Finally, by construction

r<r_{0}=\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}}\right)^{1/\alpha}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho(r)=\gamma+\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}r^{\alpha}<1,

so
\mathcal{T}
is a strict contraction on
\mathbb{\mathbb{}}{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star})
in the

L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
norm.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4
.

For any distribution

Lemma 12

(Fixed-point operator as population risk minimizer)
.

Under Condition
C5
,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})=\argmin_{Q\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}}\hat{R}_{0}(Q).

Proof.

Consider the stationary–population objective

\tilde{R}_{0}(Q):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\star}}\!\left[\bigl\{R+\gamma(\widehat{\pi}^{\mathrm{init}}\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})(S^{\prime})-Q(S,A)\bigr\}^{2}\,\bigm|\right],

where

is the stationary state–action distribution used to define
the
L^{2}(\mu_{\star})
norm in the main text. By definition of the projected
Bellman operator,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})=\argmin_{Q\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}}\tilde{R}_{0}(Q),

since this is precisely the

projection of the soft Bellman

onto
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

Condition
C5
guarantees that, for functions in
\mathcal{F}

transfers the

inner product to
the behavior distribution
\nu_{b}
. In particular, for every

and direction

\displaystyle D\tilde{R}_{0}(Q)[h]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\|\Delta_{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\bigl(\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\bigr)^{\alpha}.

Putting the two pieces together, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\tfrac{\widehat{w}^{(k)}}{w}-1\Big)\,\big\{\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q})-\widehat{Q}^{+}\big\}\Big\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

(17)

\displaystyle\;\lesssim\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\bigl(\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\bigr)^{\alpha},

(18)

where the implicit constant depends only on
C_{\infty}
and the bounds in
Condition
C7
.

Step 5: Solve in the behavior norm, then translate to
\mu_{\star}
.

Combining the basic inequality (
15
), the
empirical-process bound (
16
), and the weight–error
bound (
17
), we obtain: with probability at least

1-\eta
,

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)^{2}+\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)\,\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}}\Bigr)

(19)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\,\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\bigl(\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\bigr)^{\alpha}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}},

for some constant
C=C(M)>0
.

By Condition
C6
, we have

c_{1}^{-1}\,\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}}\;\leq\;\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;c_{0}\,\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,w\nu_{b}},

for constants
c_{0},c_{1}\in(0,\infty)
depending only on the bounds on
w
. Applying these
equivalences to (
19
) and absorbing constants into

C
, we can rewrite the inequality in terms of

x_{k}:=\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
as

\displaystyle x_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)^{2}+\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)\,x_{k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\,x_{k}+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\bigl(x_{k}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\bigr)^{\alpha}\,x_{k}.

A standard one-dimensional calculus / Young-inequality argument applied to
this scalar inequality then yields

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\leq

\displaystyle C\Bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)\Bigr)

for a (possibly larger) constant
C=C(c_{0},c_{1},M)>0
, which is the claimed
bound.

∎

Appendix F
Proof of main convergence bounds

F.1
Technical lemma

Lemma 14
.

Assume
C1
–
C3
. Let
r\in(0,r_{\max})
and suppose
\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
. Then,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r))\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}+\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle+\bigl\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\,\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

By Theorem
1
, the unprojected operator
\mathcal{T}
is a
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)
–contraction on

\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
, we have

\bigl\|\mathcal{T}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}(Q^{\star})\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;\leq\;\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

and hence

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

Applying the triangle inequality once more,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;+\;\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\;+\;\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}},

so

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(1+2\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r))\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

∎

F.2
Proof of Theorem
3

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We proceed by induction. We will show that

\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})

for all
0\leq k\leq K
. This holds for
k=0
by assumption
C10
. Throughout the proof, whenever we state that a bound holds with high probability, we mean that it holds uniformly for all
0\leq k\leq K
.

Fix an iteration
0\leq k\leq K-1
and suppose

\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

(20)

\displaystyle+\|\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

By the local contraction property in Theorem
2
and the
fixed-point identity
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})
, the first term satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
. By Lemma
5
, for all

0\leq k\leq K-1
, with probability at least
1-\eta
, the second term satisfies

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k).

Since
\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})

by the induction hypothesis, Lemma
14
yields

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigr)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{Q}^{(k)})-\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}\bigr\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)\;+\;C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\Bigl[\bigl(1+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigr)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,e_{k}\Bigr],

where
e_{k}:=\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
.

Plugging (
21
) and
(
22
) into (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

(23)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\Bigl[\bigl(1+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigr)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,e_{k}\Bigr].

Collecting the terms multiplying

\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}
and the residual
terms, and enlarging
C
if necessary (using
1+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\leq 3
), this becomes

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigl(1+2C\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)\,\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}.

Define

e_{k}:=\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}},

so the recursion reads, for a (possibly larger) constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)\,e_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}.

By assumption
C10
, there exists a radius
r\in(0,r_{\max})
such that for all

0\leq k\leq K-1
,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)<1,

\displaystyle C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\Bigl[1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)\Bigr]\,r.

Hence, whenever
e_{k}\leq r
,

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\;\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)\,e_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl[1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)\bigr]\,r

\displaystyle\leq\;r.

Thus, by induction,

\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}})

for all
k\leq K
.

We now unroll the recursion. Define the effective contraction factor

\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k):=\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr),

so that

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,k)\,e_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}.

Unrolling this recursion yields

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl(\prod_{j=0}^{k}\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,j)\Biggr)e_{0}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sum_{i=0}^{k}\Biggl(\prod_{j=i+1}^{k}\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,j)\Biggr)\Bigl[\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(i)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(i)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\Bigr].

By definition of the worst–case effective contraction factor
\rho_{\star}
, for all
0\leq j\leq k\leq K-1
,

\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,j)\;\leq\;\rho_{\star}.

Using

\prod_{j=i+1}^{k}\rho_{\mathrm{eff},\mathrm{wt}}(r,j)\leq\rho_{\star}^{\,k-i}

and the geometric-series bound

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\rho_{\star}^{\,k-i}\leq(1-\rho_{\star})^{-1},

we finally obtain, for a (possibly larger) constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\;\rho_{\star}^{\,k+1}\,e_{0}\;+\;\frac{C}{1-\rho_{\star}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{1-\rho_{\star}}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k+1,\alpha)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}}{1-\rho_{\star}}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(k+1,0).

Reindexing
k:=k+1
yields the claimed bound.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We use the same induction structure as in the proof of
Theorem
3
.
The base case
\widehat{Q}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})

holds by Condition
C12
.
Now fix
0\leq k\leq K-1
and assume

\widehat{Q}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{F}}(r,Q_{\mathcal{F}}^{\star})
. Throughout the proof, whenever we state that a bound holds with high probability, we mean that it holds uniformly for all
0\leq k\leq K
.

Define the iterate error

e_{k}:=\|\widehat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu_{\star}}.

The proof of Theorem
3
yields the recursion

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigl(1+2C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigr)\,e_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\bigl(1+2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\bigr)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}},

for a constant
C<\infty
, ensuring the bound holds with probability at least

1-\eta/2
.

Condition
C11
implies that, for a (possibly different)
constant
C<\infty
and with probability at least
1-\eta/2
,

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}(k)\;\leq\;C\,e_{k}^{\kappa}\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K).

Combining these inequalities, enlarging
C
if necessary to absorb all fixed
multiplicative factors, and applying a union bound gives, with probability at
least
1-\eta
,

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,e_{k}\;+\;2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,\bigl(Ce_{k}^{\kappa}+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)\bigr)\,e_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+C\Bigl(e_{k}^{\kappa}+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)\Bigr)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\bigl(e_{k}^{\kappa}+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)\bigr)\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\;+\;C\,\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K).

By the inequality
(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(a^{p}+b^{p})
for
p\geq 1
, this yields,
for a constant
C>0
(which may change from line to line),

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\,e_{k}\;+\;C\,e_{k}^{1+\kappa}\;+\;C\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)\,e_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+C\,e_{k}^{\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}}\;+\;C\Bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\Bigl(e_{k}^{\kappa}+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)\Bigr).

Absorbing constants and rearranging terms, there exists
B>0

(depending only on the structural constants) such that

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)+B\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}(n,\eta,K)\bigr)\,e_{k}

(24)

\displaystyle+B\Bigl(e_{k}^{1+\kappa}+e_{k}^{\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,e_{k}^{\kappa}\Bigr)

r\in(0,r_{\max})
and
assume
e_{k}\leq r
. Then

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)+B\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}\bigr)\,r

\displaystyle\quad+B\Bigl(r^{1+\kappa}+r^{\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,r^{\kappa}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+B\Bigl(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}\Bigr).

Since
r
satisfies
C12

e_{k}\leq r
, we have

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)+B\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}\bigr)\,r

\displaystyle\quad+B\Bigl(r^{1+\kappa}+r^{\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,r^{\kappa}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+B\Bigl(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\eta,K)+\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)+B\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}\bigr)\,r+\bigl[1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)-B\,\delta_{\mathrm{wt}}\bigr]\,r

\displaystyle=r.

Consequently, by induction, all iterates remain in the ball of radius
r
, and
the recursion in (
24

least
1-\eta
, uniformly over
0\leq k\leq K-1

24

\Phi(e_{j})
and
\bar{\rho}

\displaystyle e_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\rho}^{\,k+1}e_{0}\;+\;B\sum_{j=0}^{k}\bar{\rho}^{\,k-j}\,\Phi(e_{j})\;+\;B\Delta_{\mathrm{stat}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\bar{\rho}^{\,k-j}

(25)

\displaystyle=\bar{\rho}^{\,k+1}e_{0}\;+\;B\sum_{j=0}^{k}\bar{\rho}^{\,k-j}\,\Phi(e_{j})\;+\;B\Delta_{\mathrm{stat}}\frac{1-\bar{\rho}^{\,k+1}}{1-\bar{\rho}}.

The bound then follows.

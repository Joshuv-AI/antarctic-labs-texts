Abstract
. In a seminal article, Higuchi and Sudbery showed that a pure four-qubit state can not be maximally entangled across every bipartition. Such states are now known as absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states. Here we give a series of old and new proofs of the fact that no four-qubit AME state exists. These are based on invariant theory, methods from coding theory, and basic properties from linear algebra such as the Pauli commutation relations.

Absolutely maximally entangeld states

The existence of absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states (and in particular that of four-qubit AME states) is,
in a sense, one of the simplest instances of the quantum marginal problem
[
Kly06
]
and beautifully illustrates a
key difficulty at the core of all of many-body physics:
It asks whether there exists a pure
n
-qubit state, such that every bipartition is maximally entangled. Naturally, in the case of two and three qubits, the Bell and Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger states satisfy this requirement. But what about four qubits?

In a seminal article (revisited in
Proof 1: Higuchi-Sudbery
), Higuchi and Sudbery found that no such state can possibly exist. This opened up a field of research that dealt with both bounds and constructions on absolutely maximally entangled and
k
-uniform states, the latter having all
k
-body marginals maximally mixed
[
GAL+15
,
HGS17
,
RBB+22
]
. If a whole subspace satisfies such condition, we are dealing with a pure quantum code of distance
k+1

[
HG20
]
.
Indeed it was early recognized by Scott that methods of coding theory are useful in the characterization of quantum entanglement
[
Sco04
]
.
We present here two proofs inspired by coding theory.

Proof 5: Shadow inequalities
relies on Rains’ shadow inequalities
[
Rai99
]
, a monogamy relation constraining the purities in multipartite quantum states.

Proof 7: Lovász bound
rests on the Lovász theta number,
a quantity traditionally used to bound the independence number and thus the capacity of a graph, but which has recently shown to be also applicable to quantum codes
[
MNH24
]
.

A second influential connection is that to invariant theory: clearly the property of being maximally entangled does not depend on the local basis chosen, and thus it should be possible to characterize such states with local unitary polynomial invariants.
Proof 6: Gour-Wallach
, as well as the newly presented proof using the
L+M+N=0
identity in
Proof 4: Polynomial Invariants of Four Qubits

follow this line of thinking.
Perhaps Rains’ article on polynomial invariants of quantum codes is the most complete combination of these two approaches
[
Rai00
]
; see also the subsequent book by Nebe, Rains, and Sloane
[
NRS01
]
.

Finally, we present two proofs that are based on more pedestrian approaches: these proofs chiefly use the Schmidt decomposition and the commutation relations among Pauli matrices. The even-odd correlation approach of
Proof 2: Even and odd correlations
was first used in Ref.
[
HGS17
]
to show the non-existence of a seven-qubit state, but surprisingly the same argument applies to all n-qubit states excluding
n=2,3,5,6
.

Proof 3: Opposite two-body correlations
relies on the fact that all three-qubit GHZ-like states are local unitary invariant, and that the the correlations in a convex combination of two of such states must sufficiently cancel to allow for maximally mixed two-body marginals.

While this collection of proofs shows that a lot is known, it is still difficult to pinpoint the essence of this non-existence fact. Although we are perfectly able to outline the problem and can elucidate its technical solution by connecting it to many different facts and mathematical properties of quantum many-body systems, we lack a simple intuition for how local features of the parts relate to (and constrain) the global structure of the system as a whole.

We now provide a collection of proofs for the following statement:

Observation 1
.

Pure four-qubit states that are maximally entangled across every bipartition do not exist.

We review the following proofs.
To our knowledge,
Proof 3: Opposite two-body correlations
and
Proof 4: Polynomial Invariants of Four Qubits
are presented here for the first time.

Proof 1: Higuchi-Sudbery

In their remarkable work, Higuchi and Sudbery
[
HS00
]
analyzed the possibility of maximum entanglement among four qubits.
While entanglement for two and three qubits was well understood at the time, for
n>3
qubits not much was known. They recognized that the obvious
n
-qubit entangled Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state is probably not the ’most entangled’, where that concept had yet to be specified.
They did the latter by requiring the maximum number of maximally mixed reductions – from then on this has been the common definition for
absolute maximal entanglement (AME)

[
HC13
]
.
They noticed that it is not obvious that such a state would exist: The Bell state (
n=2
) and the GHZ state (
n=3
) correspond to the definition. For four qubits there at least exists the state

\mathinner{|\Psi\rangle}\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(\mathinner{|0000\rangle}+\mathinner{|0011\rangle}+\mathinner{|1001\rangle}+\mathinner{|1110\rangle}\right)\ \ ,

which has four (out of six possible) maximally mixed two-qubit reductions. Hence, Higuchi and Sudbery studied the question whether there can exist a four-qubit state
with six maximally mixed reduced states of two qubits. In the following we briefly sketch how they found their negative answer.

Assuming a four-qubit AME state
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
exists,
we can expand it in the computational basis as

(1)

\displaystyle\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}=\sum a_{jklm}\mathinner{|jklm\rangle}\ \ .

The conditions that all two-qubit reductions be fully mixed translate to the fact that

(2)

\displaystyle a_{jklm}\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(U_{1}\right)_{jl,km}\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(U_{2}\right)_{jk,lm}\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(U_{3}\right)_{jm,kl}\ \ ,

where
U_{1}
,
U_{2}
and
U_{3}
are unitary matrices.
By applying local unitaries to

\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
it can be achieved that

(3)

\displaystyle a_{1000}\ =\ a_{0100}\ =\ a_{0010}\ =\ a_{0001}\ \ .

Subsequently they argue by using the orthogonality
conditions of the columns and rows of the unitary matrices
U_{j}
(
j=1,2,3
) that more coefficients of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
must be zero. With the remaining non-zero elements it is impossible to simultaneously guarantee unitarity of

U_{1}
,
U_{2}
and
U_{3}
, which concludes their proof.

Proof 2: Even and odd correlations

The following strategy was used in Ref.
[
HGS17
]

to disprove the existence of a seven-qubit AME state,
but it also works - in simpler form - for the non-existence of a four-qubit AME.

The proof follows the strategy of:
1) recognizing that all marginals of an AME state are proportional to projectors,
2) expanding the density matrix into correlations of different weights,
and
3) splitting the resulting equation by an even-odd parity rule on the weights of anti-commutators of Pauli matrices.

Proof.

Suppose there exists a a four-qubit AME state
\mathinner{|\phi\rangle}
.
Let
\varrho_{123}=\tr_{4}(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)
be its three-body reduced density matrix on the first three systems. It can be written as

(4)

\varrho_{123}=\frac{1}{8}(\mathds{1}+P_{3})\,,

where
P_{3}
contains three-body terms only. That is, it has the form

(5)

P_{3}=\sum_{\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma\neq 0}\,c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\gamma}\,.

where the sum is over all Pauli matrices
\sigma_{\alpha},\sigma_{\beta},\sigma_{\gamma}\neq\mathds{1}
.

The single-body marginal
\varrho_{4}=\tr_{123}(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)
is maximally mixed and has eigenvalues
(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})
.
By the Schmidt decomposition of pure states,
it can be seen that the complementary reduction
\varrho_{123}
has the same non-zero spectrum.
Consequentially,
\varrho_{123}
is proportional to a projector and satisfies,

(6)

\varrho_{123}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{123}\,.

Expanded in terms of the correlations as in Eq. (
4
), we decompose this projector relation as

(7)

\big(\frac{1}{8}(\mathds{1}+P_{3})\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{8}(\mathds{1}+P_{3})\,.

Simplifying, one obtains

(8)

P_{3}^{2}=3\mathds{1}+2P_{3}\,.

We now recall the even-odd Lemma of Ref.
[
HGS17
]
.
For this, denote the weight of a Pauli operator as even (odd), if it contains a non-identity Pauli matrix in an even (odd) number of positions.
Then in any anti-commutator of Pauli operators, the weights of correlations satisfy

\displaystyle\{\text{even},\text{even}\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{even}\,,

\displaystyle\{\text{odd},\text{odd}\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{even}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\{\text{even},\text{odd}\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{odd}\,.

We use this to split the rhs and lhs of Eq. (
8
) into its even and odd parts,

(10)

\displaystyle P_{3}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\{P_{3},P_{3}\}

\displaystyle=3\mathds{1}\quad\quad\text{(even)}

(11)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=P_{3}\quad\quad\text{(odd)}

Now the second equation yields clearly a contradiction: necessarily
P_{123}\neq 0
due to the fact that
\varrho_{123}
cannot have full rank, which follows from the Schmidt decomposition of a pure state.
As a consequence, a four-qubit AME state does not exist.
∎

Remark 2
.

The proof for the non-existence of the seven-qubit AME state requires only one additional argument:
the Schmidt decomposition shows that the joint state is an eigenvector of every marginal,

(12)

\tr_{S}(\varrho)\otimes\mathds{1}_{S^{c}}\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}\propto\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}\,.

The even-odd Lemma is then used to split two projector relations as in Eq. (
6
)
each into two contributions, for which the eigenvector relations provide a contradiction.

Proof 3: Opposite two-body correlations

For this proof we need a short lemma.

Lemma 3
.

All pure three-qubit states with three maximally mixed 1-RDM are local unitary equivalent to the standard
GHZ state
(\mathinner{|000\rangle}+\mathinner{|111\rangle})/\sqrt{2}
.

The proof can be found in the Appendix
A
.

We return to the non-existence of a four-qubit AME state.

Proof.

A four-qubit AME state corresponds to a pure quantum error-correcting code with parameters
(\!(4,1,3)\!)_{2}
[
CRSS98
,
Sco04
]
.
Any pure code with parameters
(\!(n,K,d)\!)_{D}
can be propagated by a partial trace to a pure
(\!(n-1,DK,d-1)\!)_{D}

code
[
Rai98
, Theorem 19]
.
Propagating the
(\!(4,1,3)\!)_{2}
by
a partial trace on the last party yields a pure code with parameters
(\!(3,2,2)\!)_{2}
,
represented by the associated density matrix
\varrho_{ABC}
.
By the Schmidt decomposition, this reduced state
has eigenvalues
(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})
and thus fulfills the relation
\varrho_{ABC}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{ABC}
.
We can write this state as a convex combination,

(13)

\varrho_{ABC}=\frac{1}{2}\big(|v\rangle\langle v|+|w\rangle\langle w|\big),

where
\mathinner{|v\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|w\rangle}
are orthogonal vectors spanning the code space.
The code is pure with distance two,
and thus
every vector in its span must be 1-uniform, including
the vectors
\mathinner{|v\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|w\rangle}
.
We can thus expand them as

\displaystyle|v\rangle\langle v|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}(\mathds{1}+P_{2}+P_{3})\,,

(14)

\displaystyle|w\rangle\langle w|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}(\mathds{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3})\,,

where
P_{2},Q_{2}
contain only Pauli operators of weight two,
and
P_{3},Q_{3}
contain only Pauli operators of weight three.
In this way,
|v\rangle\langle v|
and
|w\rangle\langle w|

have vanishing one-body correlations.

Lemma
3

shows that any pure state 1-uniform state
must be locally unitary equivalent to the three-qubit Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger state.
We can thus restrict
\mathinner{|v\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|w\rangle}
to be of GHZ type. In particular,
choose a local basis so that
\mathinner{|v\rangle}=\mathinner{|\text{GHZ}\rangle}
.

Let us analyze the spectra of their correlations. The state

\mathinner{|\text{GHZ}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathinner{|000\rangle}+\mathinner{|111\rangle})
decomposes in terms of Pauli operators as

(15)

|\text{GHZ}\rangle\langle\text{GHZ}|=\frac{1}{8}(III+ZZI+ZIZ+IZZ-XYY-YXY-YYX+ZZZ)\,.

Now group the two and three-body correlations,

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=ZZI+ZIZ+IZZ\,,

(16)

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=-XYY-YXY-YYX+ZZZ\,.

It can be checked that the eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\sigma(P_{2})

\displaystyle=(-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,3,3)\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\sigma(P_{3})

\displaystyle=(-4,0,0,0,0,0,0,4)\,.

Due to Lemma
3
, all three-qubit GHZ-type states are local unitary equivalent. As a consequence, also the eigenvalue structures of their corresponding two- and three-body correlations are equal.
That is,
\sigma(Q_{2})=\sigma(P_{2})
and

\sigma(Q_{3})=\sigma(P_{3})
.

Now note that because
\varrho_{ABC}=(|v\rangle\langle v|+|w\rangle\langle w|)/2
is two-uniform (being the reduction of a two-uniform state), we must necessarily have that
Q_{2}=-P_{2}
in Eq. (
Proof.
).
Thus for
|w\rangle\langle w|
, we need to make the Ansatz

(18)

|w\rangle\langle w|=\frac{1}{8}(\mathds{1}-P_{2}+Q_{3})\,,

in the hope that
Q_{3}
renders the matrix

|w\rangle\langle w|
positive semidefinite.
However, the spectrum obtained from the two-body correlations
P_{2}
of
\mathinner{|v\rangle}
alone already has negative eigenvalues,

(19)

\sigma(\mathds{1}-P_{2})=(-2,-2,2,2,2,2,2,2)\,.

By Eq. (
Proof.
),
Q_{3}
has the non-zero eigenvalues
\{4,-4\}
.
Now recall that that

\lambda_{\max}(A+B)\leq\lambda_{\max}(A)+\lambda_{\max}(B)
for all hermitian matrices
A,B
.
Then
\mathinner{|w\rangle}
being pure requires that
\lambda_{\text{max}}(Q_{3})\geq 6
so that

\lambda_{\text{max}}(\mathds{1}-P_{2}+Q_{3})=8
.
This leads to a contradiction, as by Eq. (
Proof.
) one has
\lambda_{\text{max}}(Q_{3})=4
. This ends the proof.

∎

Proof 4: Polynomial Invariants of Four Qubits

Polynomial invariants are relevant for the quantification of entanglement because they allow for a straightforward construction of measures for different classes of genuine multipartite entanglement
[
VDDM03
,
ES14
]
. For states of four qubits it is possible to construct the full generating set of invariants under the group SL
(2,C)^{\otimes 4}
as was demonstrated by Luque and Thibon
[
LT03
]
. It consists of four elements, one degree-2 invariant, two degree-4 invariants and one degree-6 invariant.
By writing a four-qubit state in the computational basis,

\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}=\sum a_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}j_{4}}\mathinner{|j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}j_{4}\rangle}

(where
j_{k}\in\{0,1\}
) one obtains three degree-4 invariants
[
LT03
]

\displaystyle L\ \equiv\ \det{\mathsf{L}}\ =\ \left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0000}&a_{0100}&a_{1000}&a_{1100}\\
a_{0001}&a_{0101}&a_{1001}&a_{1101}\\
a_{0010}&a_{0110}&a_{1010}&a_{1110}\\
a_{0011}&a_{0111}&a_{1011}&a_{1111}\end{array}\right|\ \ ,

\displaystyle M\ \equiv\ \det{\mathsf{M}}\ =\ \left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0000}&a_{1000}&a_{0010}&a_{1010}\\
a_{0001}&a_{1001}&a_{0011}&a_{1011}\\
a_{0100}&a_{1100}&a_{0110}&a_{1110}\\
a_{0101}&a_{1101}&a_{0111}&a_{1111}\end{array}\right|\ \ ,

\displaystyle N\ \equiv\ \det{\mathsf{N}}\ =\ \left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0000}&a_{0001}&a_{1000}&a_{1001}\\
a_{0010}&a_{0011}&a_{1010}&a_{1011}\\
a_{0100}&a_{0101}&a_{1100}&a_{1101}\\
a_{0110}&a_{0111}&a_{1110}&a_{1111}\end{array}\right|\ \ ,

where any two of them are linearly independent.
It can be seen by inspection (i.e. evaluating the determinants) that they fulfill the relation

(21)

\displaystyle L\ +\ M\ +\ N\ =\ 0\ \ .

The properties of these invariants give rise to yet another approach to prove the non-existence of the four-qubit AME state.

Proof.

In Ref.
[
EBOS12
]
it was observed that the matrices
\mathsf{L}
,
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{N}
are closely related to the two-qubit reduced states of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
, namely,

(22a)

\displaystyle\rho_{12}\ =\

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{T}\mathsf{L}^{*}\ \ ,

(22b)

\displaystyle\rho_{24}\ =\

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}\mathsf{M}^{\dagger}\ \ ,

(22c)

\displaystyle\rho_{14}\ =\

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{T}\mathsf{N}^{*}\ \ .

For an AME state we must have

\rho_{12}=\rho_{24}=\rho_{14}=\frac{1}{4}\mathds{1}_{4}
. These equalities imply that the matrices
\mathsf{L}
,
\mathsf{M}
, and
\mathsf{N}
, up to a factor
\frac{1}{2}
, must be unitary. Accordingly, the columns and rows of each matrix form an orthogonal basis, with all vectors normalized to
\frac{1}{2}
.
Moreover, we can choose the global phase of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
so that, because of Eq. (
21
),

(23)

\displaystyle L\ =\ \frac{1}{16}\ ,\ \ M,N\in\Bigg\{\frac{1}{16}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}},\frac{1}{16}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}\Bigg\}\ \ .

Consider now, e.g., the two-qubit column vectors of
\mathsf{L}
. We may apply local unitaries to the qubits of the first column (which amounts to applying local unitaries to the third and fourth qubit of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
) so as to obtain the Schmidt decomposition, i.e.,

a_{0001}\ =\ a_{0010}\ =\ 0\ \ .

Similarly we can apply local unitaries to the first and second qubit of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
and get the Schmidt form also for the first row vector of
\mathsf{L}
,

a_{0100}\ =\ a_{1000}\ =\ 0\ \ .

Assume for the moment that
a_{0011}\neq 0
(we discuss the opposite case below). The orthogonality conditions then require the following structure of
\mathsf{L}
(up to sign changes and column or row permutations),

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}s&0&0&-t\\
0&u&-v^{*}&0\\
0&v&u^{*}&0\\
t&0&0&s\end{array}\right]\ \ ,

where
s,t\in\mathds{R}
and
u,v\in\mathds{C}
. This fixes also the entries of
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{N}
.
We then find,

(28)

\displaystyle M\ =\ (s^{2}-|u|^{2})(t^{2}-|v|^{2})\ ,\ \ \ N\ =\ -(t^{2}+|u|^{2})(s^{2}+|v|^{2})\ \ .

It is evident from Eq. (
28
) that

M,N\in\mathds{R}
, in contradiction with
Eq. (
23
).

If, instead,
a_{0011}=0
it follows from the normalization for all rows and columns in
\mathsf{L}
,
\mathsf{M}
,

\mathsf{N}
that

a_{1100}=a_{0101}=a_{1010}=a_{1001}=a_{0110}=0\ \ .

This, however, leads to
L=M=N=0
which again is a contradiction to Eq. (
23
).
∎

We note that in its spirit, this proof version is rather similar to the original Higuchi-Sudbery idea.
The coefficients of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
may be arranged into three different two-qubit unitaries, which are linked to one another by partial transposition or reshuffling of the matrix elements
[
BŻ17
,
RBB+22
]
. By exploiting the properties of the coefficients of
\mathinner{|\psi\rangle}
under local unitaries it can be shown that not all of the three matrices can simultaneously
be unitary.

Proof 5: Shadow inequalities

This proof uses the shadow inequality by Rains
[
Rai99
,
Rai00
]
:
Denote by
\varrho_{S}=\tr_{S^{c}}(\varrho)
the marginal of
\varrho
on subsystem
S
.
The inequality states that
for all states
\varrho
and subsets
T\subseteq\{1,\dots n\}
,
it holds that
1
1

1

This trace inequality has been promoted to an
operator
inequality in Ref.
[
EHGS18
]
.

(29)

S_{T}=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1\dots n\}}(-1)^{|S\cap T|}\tr_{S}(\varrho_{S}^{2})\geq 0\,.

The non-negativity of this expression can be understood by projecting
\varrho^{\otimes 2}
locally
onto symmetric and anti-symmetric subspaces given by
\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathds{1}+\operatorname{swap})
and
\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathds{1}-\operatorname{swap})
and taking the trace
[
Rai98
]
.

Proof.

The marginals of AME states are maximally mixed, and by the Schmidt decomposition all of its purities are known.
For a four-qubit AME state,

\displaystyle\tr(\varrho_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=\tr(\varrho_{ijk}^{2})=\frac{1}{2}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\tr(\varrho_{ij}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\,.

We evaluate Eq. (
29
) with
T=\{A,B,C,D\}
and get

(31)

S_{ABCD}=1-(4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2})+(6\cdot\tfrac{1}{4})-(4\cdot\tfrac{1}{4})+1=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,.

However, the shadow inequality requires that
S_{ABCD}
be non-negative, leading to a contradiction.
As a consequence, a four-qubit AME state does not exist.
∎

Proof 6: Gour-Wallach

Independently of Rains, Gour and Wallach
[
GW10
]
presented a proof that also relies on an inequality involving invariants, however, without explicit reference to Rains’
shadow inequalities.

Proof.

Define the linear entropy

\tau_{X|\bar{X}}\ =\ 2\left(1-\tr(\rho_{X}^{2})\right)
,
and let

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\tau_{A|BCD}+\tau_{B|ACD}+\tau_{C|ACD}+\tau_{D|ABC}\big)\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\big(\tau_{AB|BC}+\tau_{AC|BD}+\tau_{AD|BC}\big)\,.

Gour and Wallach observed that for a four-qubit state
[
GBS07
]
,

(33)

4\tau_{1}-3\tau_{2}=\tau_{ABCD}\geq 0\,,

where
\tau_{ABCD}=|\!\mathinner{\langle\psi|}\sigma_{y}^{\otimes 4}\mathinner{|\psi^{*}\rangle}\!|^{2}
.
However, a state with maximally mixed two-qubit marginals must have

\tau_{1}=1
and
\tau_{2}=3/2
, which is in contradiction to inequality (
33
).
∎

Remark 4
.

Note that for qubits,

\tau_{ABCD}
is nothing else than
S_{ABCD}
from Eq. (
29
).

Proof 7: Lovász bound

This proof follows the strategy of Ref.
[
MNH24
, Corollary 9]
,
which provides a complete semidefinite programming hierarchy for the existence of qubit quantum codes with given parameters
(\!(n,K,d)\!)
.
Here,
n
denotes the number of qubits the code contains,
K
is the dimension of the code space, and
d
the distance.
Here, we only need a relaxation of this hierarchy, which is given by the Lovász theta number.

Let
G
be a graph of
N
vertices,
where we write
i\sim j
if
\{i,j\}
is an edge in the graph.
The Lovász theta number is given by the following semidefinite program
[
GL17
]
:

\displaystyle\vartheta(G)\quad=\quad\text{maximize}\quad

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}M_{ii}

subject to

\displaystyle M_{ii}=a_{i}\quad\forall i\in V\,,

\displaystyle M_{ij}=0\quad\,\,\text{if}\quad i\sim j\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}1&a^{T}\\
a&M\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0\,.

It is known that
\vartheta
bounds the independence number of a graph,
that is the maximum size of a set in which no two vertices are connected,
as
\alpha(G)\leq\vartheta(G)
.

Proof.

Our aim is to construct a anti-commutativity graph
G
that captures the correlations of the AME state,
and to show that its theta number is too small for it to correspond to a pure state.
Denote by
\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}
the three Pauli matrices,
and by
\sigma_{0}

Discussion

Finally, we should mention a proof that we skipped:
The proof by Grassl, Beth, and Pellizzari shows that no
(\!(3,2,3)\!)
exists. As a consequence of, also no
(\!(4,1,3)\!)
.
This proof is rather intricate
[
GBP97
, Theorem 5]
, relying on a sequence of arguments involving the Knill-Laflamme conditions, and the fact
[
GBP97
, Lemma 3]
that for every two–dimensional subspace of
\mathds{C}^{2}\otimes\mathds{C}^{2}
there exists a basis that contains at least one product state. This leads to the conclusion that a
(\!(3,2,3)\!)
code can be factored into a tensor product,
which contradicts that there does not exist a non-trivial code of length two.

In summary, we have discussed a variety of proofs for the nonexistence
of the four-qubit AME state. Our work highlights that this
nonexistence can be traced back to several well-established results
from diverse areas of quantum information theory. However, none of the
existing methods offers a simple explanation for why three completely
mixed two-qubit marginals cannot arise from a global pure four-qubit state.
Nevertheless, we hope that our findings contribute to a deeper
understanding of this issue by illuminating the interplay
between the various constraints and structural requirements.
This perspective may serve as a foundation for future work
aimed at uncovering more general principles governing multipartite
entanglement and marginal compatibility.

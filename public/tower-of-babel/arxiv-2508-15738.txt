Abstract
. We algebraically characterize free by cyclic groups that have coarse medians, and prove that this is equivalent to the a priori stronger properties of being colourable hierarchically hyperbolic groups and being quasi-isometric to CAT(0) cube complexes. Our algebraic characterization involves a condition on intersections between maximal virtually F n × ℤ F_{n}\times\mathbb{Z} subgroups that we call having unbranched blocks . We also characterise hierarchical hyperbolicity of Γ = F n ⋊ ϕ ℤ \Gamma=F_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z} in terms of a property of completely split relative train track representatives of ϕ ∈ Out ⁡ ( F n ) \phi\in\mathrm{Out}(F_{n}) that we call excessive linearity , a slight refinement of the rich linearity condition for relative train track maps introduced by Munro and Petyt.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a free group of finite rank
\geq 3
and let
\Aut(\mathbb{F})
be the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{F}
. The quotient group

\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F})/\mathrm{Inn}(\mathbb{F})
is the group of outer automorphisms of
\mathbb{F}
. A group
\Gamma
is
free by cyclic
if it fits in a short exact sequence

1\to\mathbb{F}\to\Gamma\to{\langle\phi\rangle}\to 1.

In other words
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}

is the pullback of
\mathbb{Z}=\langle\phi\rangle
to
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
, where
\phi\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.

\Gamma
is the
mapping torus
of the outer automorphism
\phi
, following the terminology of fundamental groups of mapping tori surface homeomorphisms, and has a presentation

\Gamma=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}=\langle\mathbb{F},t\mid\{txt^{-1}=\Phi(x):x\in\mathbb{F}\}\rangle,

where
\Phi\in\Aut(\mathbb{F})
represents
\phi
; the group
\Gamma
depends on
\phi
but not on the choice of
\Phi
.

Given an arbitrary outer automorphism of
\mathbb{F}
, there is no corresponding analog of the Nielsen-Thurston decomposition into invariant subsurfaces; however, there are still three distinguishable behaviors: exponential growth, polynomial growth (generalizing the linear case for surface homeomorphisms), and finite order. An element
\phi\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F})
is called
exponentially growing
if for some conjugacy class
[w]
of an element
w\in\mathbb{F}
, the word length of
\phi^{i}([w])
grows exponentially with
i
for any fixed generating set of
\mathbb{F}
.
It is called
polynomially growing
if the word length grows at most polynomially.

The geometry of free by cyclic groups varies with the dynamical type of outer automorphism
\phi
(the
monodromy
). When
\phi
is exponentially growing and atoroidal, in other words no power of
\phi
fixes a conjugacy class of
\phi
,
\Gamma
is hyperbolic (
[
Bri00
]
). More generally,
\phi
is (non-atoroidal) exponentially growing if and only if
\Gamma
is hyperbolic relative to the polynomially growing parts (
[
Gho23
,
DL22
]
). However, much is unknown about free by cyclic groups where the monodromy is polynomially growing, which we call
polynomial free by cyclic
groups.

Hyperbolic free by cyclic groups are cocompactly cubulated
[
HW16
,
HW15
,
DKMM23
]
. Some polynomial free by cyclic groups also act freely on CAT(0) cube complexes
[
But17
,
HW25
]
, but it is unknown exactly when this is true, and in general such actions cannot be cocompact
[
Ger94
,
MP24
]
. Example
8.4
makes explicit the known fact that there are linear-growth mapping tori (in fact, geometric ones) that are CAT(0) but not virtually cocompactly cubulated, using
[
HP15
]
. As we will see, these examples also rule out a possible shortcut to one of the implications in Theorem A below.

On the coarse geometric side, polynomial free by cyclic groups are
thick

[
Mac02
,
Hag19
]
, and hence are not hyperbolic relative to any collection of proper subgroups, using
[
BDM09
]
. However, they exhibit some coarse nonpositive curvature properties. Famously, they satisfy a quadratic isoperimetric inequality
[
BG10
]
, for example.

In this paper we fill in the picture a bit by proving that, under a straightforward condition related to their
linearly growing components
, polynomial free by cyclic groups, and hence all free by cyclic groups satisfying the condition, are
hierarchically hyperbolic groups (HHGs)
and, moreover, they are
quasicubical
(i.e. quasi-isometric to a CAT(0) cube complex). More precisely: we prove that relatively hyperbolic free by cyclic groups characterized in
[
Gho23
,
DL22
]
are hierarchically hyperbolic and quasicubical provided the peripheral subgroups are, and this in turn can be characterized by examining linearly-growing strata in a completely split relative train track representative. Our main result is:

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite-rank free group (of rank at least
2
), let
\phi\in\Out(\mathbb{F})
, and let
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
be the mapping torus. Then the following are equivalent:

(I)

\Gamma
has
unbranched blocks
(see Definition
3.1
).

(II)

\Gamma
admits a coarse median, in the sense of
[
Bow13a
]
.

(III)

\Gamma
is
virtually
a colourable HHG, in the sense of
[
DMS23
]
.

(IV)

\Gamma
is a colourable HHG.

(V)

\Gamma
has no QI–embedded dimension–
2
richly branching flat (Definition
5.1
).

(VI)

\Gamma
is quasi-isometric to a finite-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex.

Moreover, the coarse median in (
II
) has finite rank if it exists.

The proof of the theorem is in Section
7
. The case
\rank(\mathbb{F})=2
follows from
[
Gho23
,
DL22
]
and
[
HRSS24
]
, and the “unbranched blocks” hypothesis holds automatically in rank
2
. Roughly speaking,
\Gamma
has unbranched blocks if it is impossible for three pairwise-incommensurable maximal free-times-
\mathbb{Z}
subgroups to intersect along a non-cyclic subgroup, i.e. it is an algebraic condition on
\Gamma
. We often say
\Gamma
“is unbranched” to mean it has unbranched blocks.

Hierarchically hyperbolic spaces/groups (HHS/G) were introduced in
[
BHS17b
,
BHS19
]
to study cubical groups, mapping class groups of surfaces of finite type, and other natural examples, using a common axiomatic framework involving a collection of projections to hyperbolic spaces enabling study of the geometry of the space by working in the hyperbolic “coordinate spaces”. This reveals features reminiscent of hyperbolicity inside the space itself. The class of HHSs includes
mapping class groups and many CAT(0) cube complexes (including all universal covers of compact special cube complexes as in
[
HW08
]
), along with Gromov hyperbolic spaces, extensions of finitely generated Veech groups
[
Bon24
]
, Teichmüller space with any of the usual metrics, many Artin groups
[
BHS17b
,
HMS24
]
, and many more. However,
\Out(\mathbb{F})
is not an HHS/HHG for isoperimetric inequality reasons
[
BHS19
]
. Colourability (Definition
2.14
) does not hold for all HHGs
[
Hag23
]
but it (virtually) holds for the best-known examples.

In our proof, we obtain an explicit hierarchically hyperbolic structure in which the hyperbolic spaces are either bounded or of one of the following types: in the relatively hyperbolic case, the
\sqsubseteq
–maximal hyperbolic space is the coned-off Cayley graph; in all cases, the rest of the hyperbolic spaces are quasi-trees (many of them are Bass-Serre trees of some well-known splitting), and the
\sqsubseteq
–minimal hyperbolic spaces are quasi-lines.

Remark
.

Although being an HHS is a quasi-isometry invariant of a space, for a group
G
to be an HHG, one needs not only that any Cayley graph is an HHS, but that the hierarchically hyperbolic structure is
G
–invariant in an appropriate sense. Hence, being an HHG is not only not a quasi-isometry invariant property of a group, it is not even a commensurability invariant
[
PS23
]
, so the implication (
III
)
\implies
(
IV
) in Theorem
A
has actual content.

Corollary B
.

Relatively hyperbolic free by cyclic groups are hierarchically hyperbolic if and only if the peripheral subgroups have unbranched blocks.

Proof.

Use Proposition
4.1
(which is from
[
Gho23
]
), Theorem
A
, and
[
BHS19
, Thm. 9.1]
.
∎

Our results imply that free by cyclic groups given in Theorem
A
satisfy previously unknown properties. For example, there is a
distance formula
(
[
BHS19
, Thm. 4.4, 4.5]
) which measures, in the sense of Masur and Minsky
[
MM00
]
, the distance between points in terms of projection distances into the coordinate sets of HHS structure. Here are consequences of Theorem

that, to our knowledge, have not been proven for free by cyclic groups by other methods:

Corollary C
.

Let
\Gamma
be a free by cyclic group. If
\Gamma
is unbranched, then the following hold:

(i)

\Gamma
acts freely and coboundedly on an injective metric space.

(ii)

\Gamma
is
semihyperbolic
in the sense of
[
AB95
]
.

(iii)

\Gamma
acts geometrically on a metric space whose asymptotic cones are all CAT(0).

(iv)

Every asymptotic cone of
\Gamma
is bilipschitz to a
real cubing
in the sense of
[
CRHK24
]
.

Proof.

Given Theorem
A
, items (
i
) and (
ii
) follow from
[
HHP23
, Cor. H, Prop. 1.1]
, item (
iii
) from
[
DMS25
, Thm. A]
, and item (
iv
) from
[
CRHK24
, Thm. C]
. Alternatively, item (

) also follows from Theorem

and
[
DMS23
, Cor. C]
.
∎

The

mentioned in Corollary
C
are a special class of finite-rank median spaces equipped with isometric embeddings in (infinite) products of real trees whose images are solution sets of a “system of equations” involving pairs of the factors. Roughly, real cubings are to median spaces as HHSs are to coarse median spaces.

We next discuss a version of Theorem
A
phrased in relative train track terms. Outer automorphisms of the free group are topologically represented by their
relative train tracks
on graphs
[
BFH00
,
BH92
]
. Relative train track theory is extremely powerful and has undergone various upgrades. We will work with
completely split improved relative train track maps (CTs)
introduced in
[
FH11
]
(see Section
2.1
). An automorphism that is
rotationless
is represented by a CT map, and hence the existence of a CT representative is guaranteed once we pass to a positive power, depending only on
\mathbb{F}
, of the automorphism. Our next result is stated in terms of such CT representatives:

Theorem D
.

Let
\phi\in\Out(\mathbb{F})
be an infinite order element. Then the following are equivalent, where
k\geq 1
is a constant depending only on
\rank(\mathbb{F})
:

(1)

\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is a colourable hierarchically hyperbolic group.

(2)

Given any
\phi^{k}
–invariant free factor
[F^{1}]
such that
\phi^{k}|_{F^{1}}
has at most linear growth, no CT map representing this restriction has
excessive linearity
.

Excessive linearity
(Definition
4.11
) is a condition on CT representatives of linearly growing automorphisms that is very closely related to the
rich linearity
used in
[
MP24
]
as an obstruction to the existence of coarse median structures on mapping tori; Question 2 of
[
MP24
]
asks whether rich linearity characterises mapping tori without coarse median structures; this is not quite the case (see Example
4.15
) but Theorem

shows that Munro-Petyt’s question does not need to change much to have a positive answer. Excessive linearity can be read from a CT map, but has an interpretation in terms of
principal lifts
of
\phi
to
\Aut(\mathbb{F})
; see Remark
4.12
. In the linear case, we show:

Theorem E
.

Suppose
\phi\in\Out(\mathbb{F})
is a rotationless UPG automorphism of at most linear growth. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The mapping torus
\Gamma
of
\phi
is not unbranched.

(2)

Any CT map

representing
\phi

The theorem is proved by analyzing the tree of cylinders splitting of

from the proof of
[

Further questions

We now propose some questions.

1.1.1.
(Non)-existence of Cannon-Thurston maps

Hierarchically hyperbolic groups are compactified using the hierarchical boundary (
HHS boundary
) from
[
DHS17
]
. One description is as follows. Any two points in an HHS/HHG are connected by a
hierarchy path

[
BHS19
, Thm. 4.4, Thm. 4.5]
, which is
a uniform quasigeodesic projecting to a uniform unparametrized quasigeodesic in the hyperbolic spaces. Imitating
the constructions of the Gromov boundary and the visual boundary of a CAT(0) space, a boundary for HHS/HHGs was constructed in
[
DHS17
,
DHS20
]
by taking as boundary points
asymptotic classes of “hierarchy rays” emanating from a
fixed basepoint and having a “well-defined gradient”. For hyperbolic groups, the HHS and Gromov boundaries coincide
[
DHS17
, Thm. 4.3]
. We suggest exploring HHS boundaries of mapping tori satisfying the conditions in Theorem
A
.

For hyperbolic groups
H\leq G

the Cannon–Thurston map
is a continuous map

\widehat{\iota}:\hat{H}\rightarrow\hat{G}
that extends the inclusion
\iota:\mathcal{C}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(G)
where
\mathcal{C}(H)
and
\mathcal{C}(G)
are Cayley graphs for
H
and
G
, respectively, given by choosing a finite generating set for
H
and extending it to a finite generating set for
G
; such an extension may or may not exist. For hyperbolic free by cyclic groups, Cannon– Thurston maps exist by work of Mitra (
[
Mit98
]
), and by work of Bowditch (
[
Bow12
]
), a Cannon–Thurston map for suitable subgroups
\mathbb{F}\triangleleft G
of a relatively hyperbolic group
G
also exists. However, there are examples of relatively free by cyclic groups for which the Cannon–Thurston map does not exist (
[
BCG+22
]
.) We ask:

Question 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an unbranched mapping torus and let
H<\Gamma
be a HHG subgroup. When does the inclusion map
H\hookrightarrow\Gamma=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
extend to a continuous map
{\partial}_{HH}H\longrightarrow{\partial}_{HH}\Gamma
between HHG boundaries?

When
H<\Gamma
is hierarchically quasiconvex, the answer is positive by
[
DHS17
]
. This case includes those where:
H
is one of the peripheral polynomial mapping tori, in the relatively hyperbolic case;
H
is the centralizer of some element;
H
is a maximal abelian subgroup;
H
is one of the vertex groups in the cyclic hierarchy from Proposition
4.3
, etc.

When
H
is a free subgroup, then
{\partial}_{HH}H
is the Gromov boundary. In many cases,
H
is hierarchically quasiconvex, so the answer is positive. However if
H
is a free
normal
subgroup, e.g.
H=\mathbb{F}
, then we suspect the answer is positive only if
\phi
is atoroidal (the converse follows from
[
Mit98
]
since hierarchical boundaries of hyperbolic groups are just the Gromov boundaries). We suspect that the answer is negative if
H
is free normal and
\Gamma
is not hyperbolic, using arguments similar to
[
BCG+22
]
. In fact we conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
G
be a HHG and and
H<G
an infinite hyperbolic
normal subgroup of infinite index. Then the Cannon–Thurston map does not exist for
(H,G)
unless either
G
is hyperbolic or
H
is hierarchically quasiconvex in some HHG structure on
G
.

1.1.2.
Asymptotic cones

Another question concerns Corollary
C
.(
iv
). Recall that this says that asymptotic cones of unbranched free by cyclic groups are real cubings, since this is true of all HHGs. Real cubings are a special class of median spaces. We ask:

Question 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a free by cyclic group that is not unbranched, and hence contains a
2
–RBF. Can the proof of
[
MP24
, Thm. 4.3]
be adapted to show that no asymptotic cone of
\Gamma
is bilipschitz equivalent to a median metric space?

The answer is likely positive; one should bound the rank of such a median structure (using something like
[
Hae16
, Cor. 3.7]
) and then imitate the argument from
[
MP24
, Thm. 4.3]
.

1.1.3.
Other nonpositive curvature properties

Semihyperbolicity was introduced by Alonso and Bridson in
[
AB95
]
, and is of interest, for instance, because it is a necessary condition for the existence of biautomatic structures, and because semihyperbolicity is sufficient for solving the conjugacy problem. As far as we know, semihyperbolic and biautomatic free by cyclic groups have not been classified, but non-biautomatic examples exist
[
BB98
]
.

Semihyperbolicity is a consequence of other (coarse) nonpositive curvature properties. To begin with, semihyperbolicity follows from the existence of a proper, cocompact action on a CAT(0) space. A classical example due to Gersten
[
Ger94
]
shows that even linear-growth mapping tori need not be CAT(0), and in Example
8.2
, we examine the JSJ decomposition to see that this example fails to be unbranched.

On the other hand, Example
8.3
shows that
\Gamma
can be CAT(0) without being unbranched/an HHG. There are many such examples. This is not surprising: as a general matter, the existence of CAT(0) structures on a group says little about hierarchical hyperbolicity. However, CAT(0)
cubical
structures often give rise to HHG structures
[
BHS17b
,
HS20
]
(and also always give biautomatic structures
[
NR98
]
). But Example
8.4
illustrates that Theorem
A
gives HHGs that are not even virtually cubical. All of this motivates:

Question 1.4
.

Does there exist
\phi\in\Out(\mathbb{F})
such that
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is unbranched (and hence an HHG), but does not act geometrically on a CAT(0) space?

Corollary
C
.(
iii
) says that unbranched mapping tori act geometrically on asymptotically CAT(0) spaces, so the non-HHG CAT(0) examples show that the converse of that statement cannot hold (since CAT(0) spaces are asymptotically CAT(0)), while an answer to Question
1.4
would determine whether or not Corollary
C
can be strengthened by replacing asympotically CAT(0) spaces with CAT(0) spaces.

Recall that Corollary
C
establishes semihyperbolicity for unbranched
\Gamma
in two independent ways, one of which is via the existence of a proper cocompact action on a proper
coarsely injective space
, and hence a metrically proper cobounded action on a (not necessarily proper) injective space, using results of Haettel-Hoda-Petyt
[
HHP23
]
. Harry Petyt has informed us in personal communication that there are free by cyclic groups that are not coarsely injective, which gives a sense in which item (
i
) is sharp.

Stronger than coarse injectivity is the existence of a proper, cocompact action on a locally finite
Helly graph
; the study of groups with this property was initiated in
[
CCG+25
]
, where it is shown that groups admitting such an action enjoy strong forms of some of the properties discussed above: they act properly and cocompactly on proper injective spaces (strengthening coarse injectivity), and they are biautomatic (strengthening semihyperbolicity).

Question 1.5
.

For which free by cyclic
\Gamma
is there a proper cocompact action on a coarsely injective space? On a Helly graph? On a proper injective space? Are there non-CAT(0) examples with such actions?

Interestingly, some of the aforementioned non-coarsely injective examples (which will appear in forthcoming work by Haettel-Hoda-Petyt) are CAT(0). They also have examples that are Helly (hence biautomatic) and CAT(0), but are not coarse median (hence they do not satisfy any of the conditions in Theorem
A
).

2.1.
Relative train track maps and CT maps

We now summarise the parts of the theory of improved relative train track maps that we will use; see
[
BFH00
,
BFH05
]
for more details.

Given a marked graph
\mathbb{G}
, a
filtration
of
\mathbb{G}
is a strictly increasing sequence
\mathbb{G}_{0}\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{G}_{k}=\mathbb{G}
of subgraphs
\mathbb{G}_{r}
with no isolated vertices. The filtration is
f
-invariant
if
f(\mathbb{G}_{r})\subset\mathbb{G}_{r}
for all
r
, where
f:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
is a homotopy equivalence sending vertices to vertices and edges to paths.
The
r^{\text{th}}
stratum
is the subgraph
H_{r}
consisting of
\mathbb{G}_{r}\setminus\mathbb{G}_{r-1}
together with the endpoints of each edge not in
\mathbb{G}_{r-1}
.

If
\lambda=1
then
H_{r}
is a
nonexponentially growing (NEG) stratum
whereas if
\lambda>1
we say that
H_{r}
is an
exponentially growing (EG) stratum
, where
\lambda
is the Perron-Frobenious eigen value associated to the stratum
[
BH92
]
.

An automorphism
\phi\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F})
can be represented by a homotopy equivalence
f:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
that takes vertices to vertices and edges to edge-paths of a marked graph
\mathbb{G}
with marking
\rho:R_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
, called a
topological representative
. Topological representative
f
preserve the marking of
\mathbb{G}
, in other words
\overline{\rho}\circ f\circ\rho:R_{n}\rightarrow R_{n}
represents
R_{n}
. A nontrivial path
\alpha
in G is a
periodic Nielsen path
if
f^{k}_{\#}(\alpha)=\alpha
for some
k
, where the smallest such
k
is called the
period
.
When
k=1
, we simply call
\alpha
a
Nielsen path
; if in addition
\alpha
is a (nontrivial) immersed cycle, we call
\alpha
a
Nielsen cycle
.

2.1.1.
Improved relative train track map (IRTT)

Relative train track maps are topological representatives whose exponentially-growing strata satisfy certain extra conditions
[
BH92
]
. Since our automorphisms will also have non-exponentially-growing (NEG) strata, we will also add conditions on the zero strata and on the NEG strata. This will result in a topological representative
f
, called an
improved relative train track (IRTT)
, satisfying Theorem
2.1
.

If
\phi\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F})
is polynomially growing and has unipotent image in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
(in other words
\phi
induces a unipotent action on
on
H_{1}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{Z})
); we call
\phi

unipotent polynomially growing (UPG)
.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 5.1.5
[
BFH00
]
)
.

For every UPG outer automorphism

\phi\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F})
there is a relative train track map
f:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
with a filtration
\mathbb{G}_{0}\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{G}_{k}=\mathbb{G}
such that
f
represents
\phi
and
f
has the following properties.

•

Filtration gives a
\phi
–invariant factor system.

•

Every periodic Nielsen path has period one.

•

Each
\overline{\mathbb{G}_{i}\setminus\mathbb{G}_{i-1}}
is a single edge
E_{i}
satisfying
f(E_{i})=E_{i}\cdot u_{i}
for some

For every vertex

2.2.
Coarse geometry and coarse median spaces

2.2.1.
Acylindrical action

An acylindrical action on a space is one where the number of elements that simultaneously coarsely stabilize sufficiently distant points is uniformly bounded.

Definition 2.4

(
[
Bow08
]
)
.

The action of a group
G
on a hyperbolic graph
X
is
acylindrical
if for all
r\geq 0
there
exist
R,N>0
such that for all vertices
a,b\in X
with
d(a,b)\geq R
, there are at
most
N
distinct
g\in G
such that
d(a,ga)\leq r
and
d(b,gb)\leq r
.

An action is
k
-acylindrical if, for every pair of vertices that are at distance at
least
k+1
the stabilizer of the pair is finite.
∎

We will use acylindricity in two contexts. First, when
G
is a relatively hyperbolic group and
\widehat{G}
is the coned-off Cayley graph, the left-multiplication action of
G
on
\widehat{G}
is acylindrical. Second, when
X
is a tree, the two notions of acylindricity in Definition
2.4
are equivalent, and we will work with the latter one.

2.2.2.
Coarse median spaces

Defined in
[
Bow13a
]
, coarse medians are motivated by a
centroid construction
capturing certain aspects of the large-scale “cubical”
structure of various naturally occurring spaces.
A coarse median space
is a geodesic metric space equipped with a ternary “coarse median” operation,
defined up to bounded distance, and satisfying some natural axioms. Roughly
speaking, these require that any finite subset of the space can be embedded in
a finite CAT(0) cube complex in such a way that the coarse median operation
agrees, up to bounded distance, with the natural combinatorial median in such
a complex.

Definition 2.5

(Median algebra)
.

Let
M
be a set and
\mu:M^{3}\rightarrow M
a ternary operation.
(M,\mu)
is a median algebra, if, for any
x,y,z,u,t\in M
,

•

\mu(x,y,z)=\mu(y,x,z)=\mu(y,z,x)

•

\mu(x,x,y)=x

•

\mu(x,y,\mu(z,u,t))=\mu(\mu(x,y,z),\mu(x,y,u)),t)
∎

Intuitively, we
think of
\mu
mapping points
x,y,z\in M
in
M
to a point “between
a,b
” for each pair of distinct
a,b\in\{x,y,z\}
. For
x,y\in M
the
interval between
x
and
y
is,
[x,y]_{\mu}=\{s\in M:\mu(x,y,s)=s\}
and we write
[x,y]
if the choice of
\mu
is clear.

Definition 2.6
.

A median metric space is any metric space
(X,d)

such that for all
x,y,z\in X
, there is a unique
m
such that
d(x,y)=d(x,m)+d(m,y)
and the analogous condition is also satisfied for
d(x,z)
and
d(y,z)
. Setting
\mu(x,y,z)=m
makes
(X,\mu)
a median algebra.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,d_{X})

(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces and
\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}
be ternary operators on
X,Y
respectively. Then, a map
\rho:X\rightarrow Y
is called
k
–quasi-median if, for all
x,y,z
in
X
,

d_{Y}(\rho(\mu_{X}(x,y,z)),\mu_{Y}(\rho(x),\rho(y),\rho(z)))\leq k.

∎

Definition 2.8
.

A coarse median space
(X,d)
is a metric space with a
coarse median
, which is a ternary operator
\mu:X\rightarrow X
such that there is a sequence
\{s_{n}\}
satisfying the following:

•

\mu
is
s_{0}
–coarsely Lipschitz in each factor,

•

for each finite
A\subset X
there is a finite median algebra
M
and maps
\iota:M\rightarrow X
,
j:A\rightarrow M
that are
s_{|A|}
–quasi-median, and
d_{X}(ij(a),a)\leq s_{|A|}
for all
a\in A
. ∎

The existence of a coarse median on a geodesic space is a quasi-isometry invariant. Gromov hyperbolic spaces, mapping class groups and Teichmüller
spaces of compact surfaces, right-angled Artin groups and geometrically finite
Kleinian groups in any dimension are coarse median. Hierarchically hyperbolic spaces are also coarse median (
[
BHS17b
,
BHS19
]
). The notion is useful for establishing coarse rank bounds and quasi-isometric rigidity.

Definition 2.9
.

A finitely generated group is
coarse median
if some (hence any)
Cayley graph with respect to a finite generating set admits a coarse median. A
invariant coarse median
on a finitely generated group
G
is a ternary operator
\mu:G^{3}\to G
that is
G
–equivariant for the left-multiplication action, and also a coarse median.
∎

Not every coarse median group admits an equivariant coarse median, but every hierarchically hyperbolic group does. In Theorem
A
.(
II
), we mean the weaker (non-invariant) notion. Hence the theorem says that (for free by cyclic groups), either there is an invariant coarse median, or there is no coarse median at all.

Remark 2.10
.

If a space
X
is quasi-isometric to a CAT(0) cube complex, then, immediately from the definition, it is a coarse median space.
∎

2.3.
Hierarchical hyperbolicity

Heuristically, a hierarchically hyperbolic space structure is a collection of hyperbolic spaces forming a “coordinate system” for a larger space.
The notion originates in
[
BHS17b
]
and was given a simpler equivalent formulation in
[
BHS19
]
.

Definition 2.11

(Hierarchically hyperbolic space structure)
.

Let
E\geq 0
, and let
(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}})
be a
(E,E)
–quasi-geodesic space. A
hierarchically hyperbolic space structure (HHS structure)
on
\mathcal{X}
consists of

•

an index set
\mathfrak{S}
for a collection of
domains

W
;

•

a set of hyperbolic spaces
\left\{(\mathcal{C}(W),d_{W}):W\in\mathfrak{S}\right\}
;

•

a set of projection maps
\left\{\pi_{W}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{C}(W)}\right\}
; and

•

nesting, orthogonality, and transversality relations so that each pair of domains is related by exactly one of these relations

satisfying the following axioms. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
and
U,V,W\in\mathfrak{S}
.

(P)

Projection.

P1

Coarsely Lipschitz:
Each projection
\pi_{W}
is
(E,E)
–coarsely Lipschitz.

P2

Coarsely well defined:
The image under the projection
\pi_{W}(x)
is nonempty and has diameter bounded by
E
.

P3

Coarsely surjective:
The image of
\pi_{W}(\mathcal{X})
is
E
–dense in
\mathcal{C}(W)
.

P4

Uniqueness:
For each
r\geq 0
there is a
\theta
(depending only on
r
) such that if
d_{\mathcal{X}}(x,y)\geq\theta
, then there is some
W\in\mathfrak{S}
for which
d_{W}(\pi_{W}(x),\pi_{W}(y))\geq r
.

(N)

Nesting. The index set
\mathfrak{S}
is partially ordered by the nesting relation
\sqsubseteq
. If
V\sqsubseteq W
, say that
V
is
nested
in
W
. Nesting is reflexive, i.e.,
V\sqsubseteq V
. Denote
\mathfrak{S}_{W}^{\sqsubseteq}:=\left\{V\in\mathfrak{S}:V\sqsubseteq W\right\}
.

N1

Maximum:
The index set
\mathfrak{S}
is either
\emptyset
or contains a unique
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element.

N2

Finite height:
Any set of pairwise–
\sqsubseteq
–comparable elements has size at most
E
.

N3

Relative projection:
For all
V,W\in\mathfrak{S}
with
V\sqsubsetneq W
there is a specified nonempty subset
\rho_{W}^{V}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(W)
with diameter at most
E
and a map
\rho_{V}^{W}:\mathcal{C}(W)\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{C}(V)}
.

N4

Bounded geodesic image:
If
V\sqsubsetneq W
, then for all geodesics
\gamma\subset\mathcal{C}(W)
either
\rho_{V}^{W}(\gamma)
has diameter at most
E
or
\gamma
intersects the
E
-neighborhood of
\rho_{W}^{V}
.

N5

Large links:
Let
N
be the largest integer no greater than
Ed_{W}(\pi_{W}(x),\pi_{W}(y))+E
. There exists
\left\{V_{1},\dots,V_{N}\right\}
with
V_{i}\sqsubsetneq W
such that, for all
U\sqsubsetneq W
, either
U\sqsubseteq V_{i}
for some
i
or
d_{U}(\pi_{U}(x),\pi_{U}(y))\leq E
.

(O)

Orthogonality. The orthogonality relation
\bot
is symmetric and antireflexive. Denote
\mathfrak{S}_{W}^{\bot}:=\left\{V\in\mathfrak{S}:V\bot W\right\}
.

O1

\sqsubseteq
–compatibility:
If
V\sqsubseteq W
and
U\bot W
, then
V\bot U
.

O2

Containers:
For
U\sqsubseteq W
with
\mathfrak{S}_{W}^{\sqsubseteq}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{U}^{\bot}\neq\emptyset
, there exists
Q\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}^{\sqsubseteq}
, called the
container
of
U
in
W
, such that
V\sqsubseteq Q
whenever
V\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}^{\sqsubseteq}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{U}^{\bot}
.

(T)

Transversality. The transversality relation
\pitchfork
is symmetric and antireflexive.

T1

Relative projections:
If
V\pitchfork W
, then there are specified nonempty sets
\rho_{W}^{V}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(W)
and
\rho_{V}^{W}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(V)
each of diameter at most
E
.

(C)

Consistency.

C1

If
V\pitchfork W
, then
\mathrm{min}\left\{d_{W}(\pi_{W}(x),\rho_{W}^{V}),d_{V}(\pi_{V}(x),\rho_{V}^{W})\right\}\leq E
.

C2

If
V\sqsubsetneq W
, then
\mathrm{min}\left\{d_{W}(\pi_{W}(x),\rho_{V}^{W}),\mathrm{diam}(d_{V}(\pi_{V}(x)\cup\rho_{W}^{V}(\pi_{V}(x))\right\}\leq E
.

C3

If
U\sqsubseteq V
and either
V\sqsubsetneq W
or
V\pitchfork W
and
W\not\bot U
, then
d_{W}(\rho_{W}^{V},\rho_{W}^{U})\leq E
.

(R)

Partial realization. For any set of pairwise orthogonal domains
\left\{V_{j}\right\}
and any points
p_{j}\in\mathcal{C}(V_{j})
, there exists
x\in\mathcal{X}
such that
d_{V_{j}}(x,p_{j})\leq E
for all
j
; and for each
j
and each
W\in\mathfrak{S}
with
V_{j}\sqsubsetneq W
or
V_{j}\pitchfork W
,
d_{V}(x,\rho_{V}^{V_{j}})\leq E
.∎

3.
Unbranched blocks

Definition 3.1

(Block, unbranched)
.

Let
G
be a group. A subgroup
B\leq G
is a
block
if both of the following hold:

•

B
has a finite-index subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}\times\mathbb{Z}
, where
\mathbb{F}
is a nonabelian free group.

•

If
B^{\prime}\leq G
has a finite-index subgroup
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{Z}
, where
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
is nonabelian free, and
[B:B^{\prime}\cap B]<\infty
, then
[B^{\prime}:B^{\prime}\cap B]<\infty
.

The group
G

has unbranched blocks
if for all blocks
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
such that
[B_{i}:B_{i}\cap B_{j}]=\infty
whenever
i\neq j
, the subgroup
B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap B_{3}
is virtually cyclic (possibly finite).
∎

If
G
has unbranched blocks, we will sometimes say
G
is unbranched
. Observe:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G^{\prime}\leq G
. If
[G:G^{\prime}]<\infty
, then
G^{\prime}
is unbranched if and only if
G
is.

Proof.

Suppose
G^{\prime}\leq G
has finite index and let
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
be blocks in
G
. For
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
B_{i}^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap B_{i}
. Observe that
B_{i}^{\prime}
is a block in
G
. Indeed, suppose that
B_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq G^{\prime}
is virtually the product of
\mathbb{Z}
with a nonabelian free group, and
B_{i}^{\prime}
is virtually contained in
B_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Then, viewing
B_{i}^{\prime\prime}
as a subgroup of
G
, the fact that
B_{i}^{\prime}
has finite index in
B_{i}
, and
B_{i}
is a block in
G
, together imply (using Definition
3.1
) that
[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}:B_{i}^{\prime}\cap B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]<\infty
.

This shows that
B_{i}
is a block in
G
if and only if
B_{i}^{\prime}=B_{i}\cap G^{\prime}
is a block in
G^{\prime}
. Moreover, each of the intersections
B_{i}\cap B_{j}
is commensurable with
B_{i}^{\prime}\cap B_{j}^{\prime}
, and
B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap B_{3}
is commensurable with
B_{1}^{\prime}\cap B_{2}^{\prime}\cap B_{3}^{\prime}
. Hence
G
is unbranched if and only if
G^{\prime}
is.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group splitting as a graph of groups such that each edge group is virtually abelian and the
G
–action on the Bass-Serre tree is acylindrical. Then every block in
G
fixes a unique vertex in
T
. If every edge group is virtually cyclic, then
G
is unbranched if and only if every vertex group is unbranched.

Proof.

Let
T
be the Bass-Serre tree and let
B
be a block in
G
. Since
G
acts acylindrically on
T
, Definition
3.1
implies that
B
must fix a point. The assumption on edge-stabilizers means
B
cannot fix an edge, so there is a unique vertex
v(B)\in T
with
B\leq\stabilizer_{G}(v(B))
.

Now suppose edge stabilizers are virtually cyclic. If
B_{1},B_{2}
are blocks and
B_{1}\cap B_{2}
is non-(virtually-cyclic), then
v(B_{1})=v(B_{2})
, since
B_{1}\cap B_{2}
fixes the geodesic from
v(B_{1})
to
v(B_{2})
. In particular, if
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
are blocks witnessing that
G
is not unbranched, then
v(B_{1})=v(B_{2})=v(B_{3})
, so the corresponding vertex group
G_{v}
contains
B_{1},B_{2},
and
B_{3}
, and these subgroups are blocks in
G_{v}
whose mutual intersection is not virtually cyclic.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group that is hyperbolic relative to a finite collection
\mathcal{P}
of subgroups. Then
G
is unbranched if and only if each
P\in\mathcal{P}
is unbranched. If each
P\in\mathcal{P}
is coarse median, then so is
G
.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{G}
be a coned-off Cayley graph witnessing that
(G,\mathcal{P})
is a relatively hyperbolic pair. Since
G
acts on
\widehat{G}
acylindrically
[
Osi16
, Prop. 5.2]
, any block
B\leq G
has bounded orbits, and it follows from relative hyperbolicity that it is conjugate into a unique
P\in\mathcal{P}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is almost malnormal, distinct blocks have infinite intersection only if they belong to a common peripheral subgroup, so
G
is unbranched exactly when each
P\in\mathcal{P}
is. If each
P\in\mathcal{P}
is coarse median, then so is
G
, by
[
Bow13b
, Thm. 1.1]
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Proposition
3.3
says that each block lies in a unique vertex stabilizer. The proof of Proposition
3.4
shows that, in a relatively hyperbolic group, any block is conjugate into a unique peripheral subgroup.
∎

Remark 4.5

(Comparison to graph manifolds)

In the 3-manifold case, the blocks are the virtually

4.2.

.

Let

Definition 4.10

Let

be a CT map fixing all vertices of
\mathbb{G}
. For each Nielsen cycle

Remark 4.12
.

A reader seasoned with the train-track theory would have perhaps noticed that what we are doing in Definition

Remark 4.13
.

In light of the above remark and looking back at Definition

is not about “too many linear edges” but rather about too many closed Nielsen paths glued at the vertices of linear edges and their suffixes. Equivalently, excessive linearity is about existence of too many principal lifts (giving large fixed subgroups) with basepoints chosen on the suffixes of linear edges. Hence we chose to formulate the definition in terms of fixed subgroups, but still retained the phrase

Remark 4.14

Excessive linearity is closely related to the

Proof.

Being coarse median is a quasi-isometry invariant property, and hierarchically hyperbolic groups are coarse median of finite rank
[
BHS19
, Thm. 7.3]
, so the first part of the lemma implies the second. Next, we claim that
\Gamma
is virtually a colourable hierarchically hyperbolic group. We divide into cases according to the growth rate of
\phi
.

Linear growth.
If
\phi
has polynomial growth rate at most
1
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}
is virtually a colourable HHG, by Lemma
6.8
, which is an application of
[
HRSS24
]
.

Superlinear polynomial growth.
Proposition
6.7
implies that
\Gamma
is virtually a colourable hierarchically hyperbolic group in the case where
\phi
has polynomial growth rate at least
1
. (This is proved by induction below, with the base case being the linear growth case.)

Exponential growth.
By Proposition
4.1
,
\Gamma
is hyperbolic relative to a finite collection
\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of mapping tori of polynomially-growing automorphisms. By the preceding case, for each
i\in I
there is a finite-index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}\leq\Gamma_{i}
admitting a hierarchically hyperbolic group structure
(\Gamma^{\prime}_{i},\mathfrak{S}_{i})
. By Lemma
4.2
, there is a finite-index normal subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\leq\Gamma
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}\leq\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in I
. For each
i
, let
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}
, and note that
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime\prime}
inherits a hierarchically hyperbolic group structure from
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
. On the other hand,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is hyperbolic relative to a finite collection of subgroups, each conjugate in
\Gamma
to some
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{i}
. So, by
[
BHS19
, Thm. 9.1]
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is hierarchically hyperbolic, as claimed. Finally, since the HHG structure
(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{S})
constructed in the proof of
[
BHS19
, Thm. 9.1]
does not introduce any orthogonality, one verifies easily that colourability of
(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{S})
is inherited from colourability of the
(\Gamma_{i},\mathfrak{S}_{i})
.
∎

We will handle the polynomial case using the virtual cyclic hierarchy from Proposition
4.3
, where the terminal vertex groups are mapping tori of linearly growing automorphisms, which are hierarchically hyperbolic by
[
MP24
]
. The following abstracts the properties that we need to verify at each stage of our induction on the length of the hierarchy.

Definition 6.2

(Acceptable graph of groups)
.

Let
\Omega
be a finite graph and let
\mathcal{G}
be a graph of groups with underlying graph
\Omega
. For each
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(\Omega)
, let
G_{v}
be the corresponding vertex group, and for each (oriented) edge
e
, let
G_{e}
be the corresponding edge group. Let
e^{\pm}
be the endpoints of
e
, and let
\iota_{e}^{\pm}:G_{e}\to G_{e^{\pm}}
be the edge-monomorphisms. We call the graph of groups
(\Omega,\{G_{v}\}_{v},\{G_{e}\}_{e},\{\iota_{e}^{\pm}\})

acceptable
if all of the following hold:

(1)

For each
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(\mathcal{G})
, there is a hierarchically hyperbolic group structure
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
. For each
U\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}
, let
\pi_{U}:G_{v}\to\mathcal{C}U
be the associated projection to a hyperbolic space. Let
S_{v}\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}
be the unique
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element.

(2)

For each edge
e
, the group
G_{e}
is infinite cyclic, generated by an element
g_{e}
.

(3)

For each
e
, the element
\iota^{+}_{e}(g_{e})\in G_{e^{+}}
is loxodromic on the hyperbolic space
\mathcal{C}S_{e^{+}}
.

(4)

For each
e
, consider the element
z_{e}=\iota^{-}_{e}(g_{e})\in G_{e^{-}}
. Then
\langle z_{e}\rangle
is hierarchically quasiconvex in
(G_{e^{-}},\mathfrak{S}_{e^{-}})
and, more strongly, there exists a unique
U_{e}\in\mathfrak{S}_{e^{-}}
such that
z_{e}
is loxodromic on
\mathcal{C}U_{e}
, and
\mathcal{C}U_{e}
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}
and
\sqsubseteq
–minimal in
\mathfrak{S}_{e^{-}}
.

(5)

Each
\iota^{\pm}_{e}(g_{e})
generates a maximal elementary subgroup of
G_{e^{\pm}}
.

(6)

For each
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(\Omega)
, the following holds. Let
U\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}
. Then
\stabilizer_{G_{v}}(U)
acts coboundedly on the
standard product region

P_{U}
in
G_{v}
.

Moreover, letting
T
be the Bass-Serre tree, we require
G
to act on
T

2
–acylindrically.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Standard product regions in hierarchically hyperbolic groups are introduced in
[
BHS19
, Sec. 5]
and a more detailed treatment is given in, for instance,
[
CRHK25
, Sec. 17]
. We just need the following facts. Given an HHG
(G,\mathfrak{F})
, to each
U\in\mathfrak{F}
there is an associated subset
P_{U}\subseteq G
, the
standard product region
. For each
g\in G
, we have
P_{gU}=gP_{U}
, and in particular
\stabilizer_{G}(U)
acts on
P_{U}
. This action need not be cobounded (see e.g.
[
BHS19
, Rem. 8.25]
but in practice it often is. Finally,
P_{U}
is hierarchically quasiconvex (with HQC parameters independent of
U
) and for all
W\in\mathfrak{F}
, the projection
\pi_{W}:G\to\mathcal{C}W
has the following properties with respect to
P_{U}
:

•

\pi_{W}(P_{U})
coarsely coincides with
\pi_{W}(G)\asymp\mathcal{C}W
when
W\sqsubseteq U
or
W\bot U
, and

•

\pi_{W}(P_{U})
has uniformly bounded diameter for all other
W
.

Some other results on product regions are used below; references will be given as needed.
∎

Definition 6.4

(Very HQC element)
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{S})
be a hierarchically hyperbolic group. Recall from
[
DHS17
, Sec. 6]
that for all infinite-order
g\in G
, the set
\mathrm{Big}(g)
of
U\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
\pi_{U}(\langle g\rangle)
is unbounded is nonempty and consists of pairwise orthogonal elements.

An element
g\in G
is
very HQC
if one of the following holds:

•

\mathrm{Big}(g)=\{S\}
, where
S\in\mathfrak{S}
is the unique
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element, or

•

\mathrm{Big}(g)=\{U\}
, where
U\in\mathfrak{S}
is
\sqsubseteq
–minimal and
\mathcal{C}U
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}
.

In the former case, we say
g
is
top-level
and in the latter,
g
is
bottom-level
.
∎

Lemma 6.5

(Combination lemma)
.

Let
(\Omega,\{G_{v}\}_{v},\{G_{e}\}_{e},\{\iota_{e}^{\pm}\})
be an acceptable graph of groups, with total group
G
and Bass-Serre tree
T
. Then
G
admits a hierarchically hyperbolic structure
(G,\mathfrak{S})
with the following properties:

•

g\in G
is a top-level very HQC element if and only if
g
is loxodromic on
T
.

•

For all
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(\Omega)
, the element
g\in G_{v}
is bottom-level very HQC in
(G,\mathfrak{S})
if and only if it is bottom-level very HQC in
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
.

•

For each
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, the group
\stabilizer_{G}(U)
acts on the product region
P_{U}
coboundedly.

Moreover, if each
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
is colourable, then so is
(G,\mathfrak{S})
.

Proof.

For each vertex
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(\Omega)
, by hypothesis we have a hierarchically hyperbolic group
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
. For each
e\in\mathbf{Edges}(\Omega)
, let
g_{e}
generate the infinite cyclic group
G_{e}
, and let
z^{\pm}_{e}=\iota^{\pm}_{e}(g_{e})
. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{v}
be the set of
e\in\mathbf{Edges}(\Omega)
such that
e^{\pm}=v
.

We now modify each
\mathfrak{S}_{v}
as follows. Consider the finite collection
\mathcal{H}=\{\langle z_{e}^{+}\rangle:e\in\mathcal{E}_{v}^{+}\}
of subgroups of
G_{v}
. By Definition
6.2
, each
z_{e}^{+}
is loxodromic on the hyperbolic space
\mathcal{C}S_{v}
corresponding to the
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element
S_{v}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{v}
. Hence we can apply
[
BHS17a
, Prop. 6.14]
— which applies since the edge groups are maximal elementary subgroups that are top-level HQC — to obtain a new HHG structure
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{v})
which is identical to
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
, except that
\mathcal{C}S_{v}
is replaced by the hyperbolic space
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}S_{v}
obtained by coning off each
G
–translate of each
\langle z_{e}^{+}\rangle\cdot\pi_{S_{v}}(1)
, and adding to
\mathfrak{S}_{v}
, for each coset
a\langle z_{e}^{+}\rangle
, a new element that is nested in
S_{v}
and transverse to everything else. In summary:

(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime})
is an HHG structure where
\iota^{\pm}_{e}(g_{e})
is bottom-level very HQC for all
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{v}\cup\mathcal{E}^{-}_{v}
.

(Note that this was already true of
e\in\mathcal{E}^{-}_{v}
for the HHG structure
\mathfrak{S}_{v}
, because of Definition
6.2
, and we did not change the bigset of such
g_{e}
when applying
[
BHS17a
, Prop. 6.14]
.)

Now we will apply the combination theorem for hierarchically hyperbolic groups, namely
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
, whose hypotheses we check as follows.

Hierarchical quasiconvexity.
The hierarchical quasiconvexity of edge groups in vertex groups, which is hypothesis (1) of
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
, holds because each
g_{e}
maps to a bottom-level very HQC element in each of its incident vertex groups, equipped with the HHG structures
\mathfrak{S}_{e^{\pm}}^{\prime}
. (Note that
\iota_{e}^{\pm}
is a uniform-quality quasi-isometric embedding since cyclic subgroups of HHGs are undistorted, by
[
DHS17
, Thm. 7.1]
and
[
DHS20
, Thm. 3.1]
.)

Fullness.
The requirement that each
\iota_{e}^{\pm}
be a
full hieromorphism
is why we passed to
\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
for each
v
. This requirement is hypothesis (2) of
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
. First, we equip each
G_{e}
with the trivial HHG structure
(G_{e},\mathfrak{S}_{e})
, where
\mathfrak{S}_{e}
has a single element
S_{e}
with
\mathcal{C}S_{e}
the obvious Cayley graph of
\langle g_{e}\rangle
. Now, in
\mathfrak{S}_{e^{\pm}}^{\prime}
, the element
\iota^{\pm}_{e}(g_{e})=z_{e}^{\pm}
has a unique element in its bigset, which we denote
U_{e}^{\pm}
, and
U_{e}^{\pm}
is
\sqsubseteq
–minimal, and
\mathcal{C}U_{e}^{\pm}
is a quasiline on which
\langle z_{e}^{\pm}\rangle
acts loxodromically. The action of
\langle z_{e}^{\pm}\rangle
on
\mathcal{C}U^{\pm}_{e}
defines an equivariant map
\mathcal{C}S_{e}\to\mathcal{C}U^{\pm}_{e}
making
\iota_{e}^{\pm}
an equivariant hieromorphism. The
fullness
condition (see
[
BHS19
, Defn. 8.1]
) is satisfied since
\mathcal{C}S_{e}\to\mathcal{C}U^{\pm}_{e}
is a quasi-isometry (the coarse surjectivity uses that
\mathcal{C}U_{e}^{\pm}
is a quasiline by Definition
6.4
) and since
U_{e}^{\pm}
is
\sqsubseteq
–minimal in
\mathfrak{S}_{e^{\pm}}^{\prime}
.

Pairwise-orthogonal sets.
We need to check hypothesis (5) of
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
(see also
[
BHS19
, Rem. 8.25]
), which says that for each
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(\Omega)
, the action of
G_{v}
on the set
\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
has the property that there are finitely many orbits of subsets
\mathcal{U}
that consist of pairwise orthogonal elements. We deduce this from the assumption in Definition
6.2
that stabilizers of elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{v}
act on product regions coboundedly, as follows.

The assumption implies that each
\stabilizer_{G_{v}}(U),\ U\in\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{v}
, acts coboundedly on the associated
P_{U}
. Indeed, this holds for
U\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}
by hypothesis; the new elements
U\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{S}_{v}
are not orthogonal to anything, and they are
\sqsubseteq
–minimal, so
P_{U}
coarsely coincides with the quasi-axis of some
z_{e}^{+}
, so
\langle z_{e}^{+}\rangle
acts on
P_{U}
coboundedly. Now apply Lemma
6.6
to each
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime})
.

Bounded supports.
Hypothesis (3) of
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
, bounded supports, follows from the assumption that the
G
–action on the Bass-Serre tree
T
is acylindrical. Indeed, acylindricity provides
n<\infty
such that if
v,w\in\mathbf{Vertices}(T)
are at distance at least
n
, then
|\stabilizer_{G}(v)\cap\stabilizer_{G}(w)|<\infty
(in our setting, the
2
–acylindricity hypothesis allows us to take
n=3
). On the other hand, from Definition
6.2
and
[
BHS19
, Defn. 8.5]
, the proof of
[
BHS19
, Lem. 8.20]
implies the following: if
v,w\in\mathbf{Vertices}(T)
belong to a common support, then the corresponding vertex spaces (viewed as subsets of
G
) have unbounded coarse intersection. Since these vertex spaces are
G
–cosets of vertex groups, and
\mathbf{Vertices}(\Omega)
is finite, it follows that
\stabilizer_{G}(v)\cap\stabilizer_{G}(w)
is infinite cyclic, and hence
d_{T}(v,w)\leq n
, as required.

Additional hypotheses.
The combination theorem
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
has two additional hypotheses, called (4) and (6) in
[
BHS19
]
. However, as observed by Berlai and Robbio in
[
BR20
, Rem. 4.7]
, hypothesis (4) of
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
is unnecessary (the authors of
[
BR20
]
explain the slight change to the proof needed to eliminate that hypothesis). The remaining hypothesis is (6). Here, again, Berlai and Robbio come to our aid: by
[
BR20
, Thm. 4.9]
, our edge-maps satisfy hypothesis (6) of
[
BHS19
, Cor. 8.24]
(which Berlai and Robbio note as a consequence of their theorem in
[
BR20
, Rem. 5.1]
) once we observe that the trivial HHG structures
(G_{e},\mathfrak{S}_{e}),\ e\in\mathbf{Edges}(\Omega)
, are
concrete
in the sense of
[
BR20
, Defn. 3.10]
, which holds vacuously since
\mathfrak{S}_{e}
has a single element.

The HHG structure.
We now apply
[
BHS19
, 8.24]
to obtain an HHG structure
(G,\mathfrak{S})
.

Very HQC elements.
The proof of
[
BHS19
, 8.24]
(specifically
[
BHS19
, Defn. 8.14]
) says that the unique
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element of
\mathfrak{S}
has associated hyperbolic space equal to the Bass-Serre tree
T
, so
g\in G
is top-level very HQC if and only if
g
is loxodromic on
T
. Now, any
U\in\mathfrak{S}-\{T\}
for which
\mathcal{C}U
is unbounded has the property that
U\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
for at least one
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(T)
, and the hyperbolic space
\mathcal{C}U
is inherited from the HHG structure
\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
, which implies the claim about bottom-level very HQC elements.

Persistence of colourability.

\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{S}_{0}\sqcup\mathfrak{K}
, where
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
consists of
T
, together with a set of equivalence classes
[V]
, where
V\in\bigcup_{v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(T)}\mathfrak{S}_{v}
and the equivalence relation is from
[
BHS19
, Defn. 8.5]
. The set
\mathfrak{K}
comes from Definition 8.11 in
[
BHS19
]
. Now,
\{T\}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{K}
are all
G
–invariant, and by the proof of
[
BHS19
, Lem. 8.13]
, no two elements of
\mathfrak{K}
are orthogonal. Hence it suffices to show that elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
in the same
G
–orbit are non-orthogonal. If
[W],[V]\in\mathfrak{S}_{0}
and
[W]\bot[V]
, then by definition, there exists
v\in\mathbf{Vertices}(T)
and representatives
W_{v},V_{v}\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}
of the two classes, with
W_{v}\bot V_{v}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{v}
. By colourability of
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
, the elements
W_{v},V_{v}
are in different
G_{v}
–orbits. Now, suppose toward a contradiction that
g\in G
has the property that
g[W]=[V]
.

Let
T_{[V]},T_{[W]}\subset T
be the support trees of
[V],[W]
(Definition 8.5 in
[
BHS19
]
). Note that
T_{[W]}=gT_{[V]}
and hence
v\in T_{[V]}\cap gT_{[V]}
.

We will show below that
gv=v
. Now,
V_{v}
is the unique representative of
[V]
in
\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
, and
W_{v}
is the unique representative of
[W]=g[V]
in
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{v}
. So if
gv=v
, then
gV_{v}
is the unique representative of
g[V]
in
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{gv}=\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{v}
, so
gV_{v}=W_{v}
, which contradicts the above conclusion that
V_{v},W_{v}
are in different
G_{v}
–orbits. So it indeed suffices to show
gv=v
.

Suppose that
T_{[V]}=\{v\}
. Then
T_{[W]}=\{gv\}
, so since
v\in T_{[V]}\cap T_{[W]}
, we must have
gv=v
, as required. The other option is that
T_{[V]}
is nontrivial, which is equivalent to the statement that the equivalence class
[V]
has more than one element. By construction, this implies that the representative
V_{v}\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
is
\sqsubseteq
–minimal, and since
V_{v}\bot W_{v}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
, we have the following for all
u\in T_{[V]}
adjacent to
v
: in
\mathfrak{S}_{u}^{\prime}
, the representative
V_{u}
of
[V]
is
\sqsubseteq
–minimal and not in
\mathfrak{S}_{u}
(i.e. it is one of the new quasilines added to make the edge-hieromorphisms full). In particular,
V_{u}
is not orthogonal to any element of
\mathfrak{S}_{u}^{\prime}
. Now, since
\mathcal{C}W_{v}
is unbounded and
V_{v}\bot W_{v}
, there are infinitely many vertices in
T_{[V]}
incident to
v
, so by
2
–acylindricity,
u
is a leaf of
T_{[V]}
. We have shown that
T_{[V]}
is a star whose central vertex
v
is the unique vertex in
T_{[V]}
where the representative
V_{v}
is orthogonal to some other domain in the HHG structure at that vertex. Hence,
T_{[W]}=gT_{[V]}
is also a star, centred at
gv
, and
W_{gv}
is orthogonal to
V_{gv}
. Thus the central vertices of the stars
T_{[V]}
and
T_{[W]}
coincide, i.e.
w=gv=v
.

Thus, in either case,
gv=v
, and this contradicts colourability of
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime})
as noted above. This proves that
(G,\mathfrak{S})
is colourable.

Cobounded stabilizers.
We have to check that
\stabilizer_{G}(U)
acts coboundedly on
P_{U}
for all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, where
P_{U}
is the standard product region in
G
. The argument has three cases, provided by the proof of
[
BHS19
, 8.24]
(specifically, by Definition 8.11 in
[
BHS19
]
):

(1)

If
U\in\mathfrak{S}
is the
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element, then
P_{U}=G
and
\stabilizer_{G}(U)=G
, and the left multiplication action of
G
on itself is cobounded, so we are done.

(2)

If
U
is an equivalence class, i.e.
U=[U_{v}]
where
U_{v}\in\mathfrak{S}_{v}
for some vertex
v
of
T
, then
\stabilizer_{G}(U)
must stabilize the subtree
T_{[U_{v}]}
on which
[U_{v}]
is supported, so by bounded supports,
\stabilizer_{G}(U)\leq\stabilizer_{G}(v)
for some choice of
v\in T_{[U_{v}]}
, namely any vertex
v
in
T_{[U_{v}]}
that is fixed by
\stabilizer_{G}(U)
. Specifically, either
T_{[U_{v}]}=\{v\}
, or
T_{[U_{v}]}
is an infinite star with central vertex
v
. In either case, let
P^{v}_{U}:=P_{U_{v}}
be the standard product region in
G_{v}
associated to
U_{v}
in the HHG
(G_{v},\mathfrak{S}_{v})
.

Note that
\stabilizer_{G}(U)=\stabilizer_{G_{v}}(U_{v})
. We claim that
P_{U}
is contained in a regular neighbourhood in
G
of
P^{v}_{U}
. Since
\stabilizer_{G_{v}}(U_{v})
acts coboundedly on
P_{U}^{v}
by hypothesis, it will follow from this that
\stabilizer_{G}(U)
acts coboundedly on
P_{U}
. For each
u\neq v
in
T_{U}
, the vertices
u,v
are adjacent and the representative
U_{u}
of
U
in
\mathfrak{S}_{u}^{\prime}
is
\sqsubseteq
–minimal and not orthogonal to any element of
\mathfrak{S}_{u}^{\prime}
, so
P_{U}^{u}\subset G_{u}
is a quasiline at uniformly bounded Hausdorff distance from the edge space in which
G_{v},G_{u}
coarsely intersect. On the
G_{v}
side, this quasiline is contained in
P_{U}^{v}
, so we are done. If no such
u
exists, then either
P^{v}_{U}=P_{U}
, or
P_{U}
consists of
P^{v}_{U}
together with some quasilines corresponding to
\sqsubseteq
–minimal elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{v}^{\prime}
that are nested in
U_{v}
and whose support trees are stars centred at
v
. In the latter case, we again conclude that
P_{U}
intersects the adjacent vertex spaces in uniform neighbourhoods of edge spaces, so we are done.

(3)

The final possibility is that
U=K^{\bot}_{m}([U_{1}],\ldots,[U_{m}])
, where the notation is as in
[
BHS19
, Defn. 8.11]
. From that definition, we obtain a set
[U_{1}],\ldots,[U_{m}]\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
[U_{i}]\bot[U_{j}]
whenever
i\neq j
, and each
[U_{i}]
is an equivalence class in
\bigcup_{v}\mathfrak{S}_{v}
. As noted in
[
BHS19
, Defn. 8.11]
, the
\sim
–class
[V]
is nested in
U
if and only if
[V]\bot[U_{i}]
for all
i
, and
[V]\bot U
if and only if
[V]\sqsubseteq[U_{i}]
for some
i
. By the proof of
[
BHS19
, Lem. 8.13]
, no other element of
\mathfrak{S}
is orthogonal to
U
. It is possible that there are

is not unbranched. Using the cyclic hierarchy splitting Proposition
4.3
we see that some terminal vertex group is not unbranched. Proposition
3.3
then gives us that
\Gamma
must not be unbranched. This contradicts our hypothesis that
F\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is a colourable HHG. Therefore we conclude that, for the restriction of
\phi
to any linearly growing free factor, there are no CT maps where we can witness excessive linearity, proving
\eqref{item:col-hhg}\implies\eqref{item:excessive-linearity}
for the higher order polynomial growth case.

Suppose next that (
2
) holds in the quadratic case and call the mapping torus of
\phi
as
\Gamma
. By the cyclic hierarchy decomposition (Proposition
4.3
) we have a graph of groups decompositon of
\Gamma
that satisfies the following conditions:

•

Each vertex group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Each vertex group is a mapping torus corresponding to a restriction of
\phi
to an invariant free factor, and the growth of the restriction is at most linear.

•

The
\Gamma-
action on the Bass-Serre is acylindrical.

Theorem
E
implies that each vertex group is unbranched. Invoking Proposition
3.3
, we get that
\Gamma
is unbranched. Theorem
A
proves
(\ref{item:excessive-linearity})\implies(\ref{item:col-hhg})
for the quadratic growth case.

Case 3:
Assume
\phi
has polynomial growth of order
\geq 3
. The direction
(\ref{item:col-hhg})\implies(\ref{item:excessive-linearity})
has already been handled while proving it for the quadratic case. For the converse direction, proceed by induction replicating the steps of case (2) and using the appropriate version of the cyclic hierarchy decomposition. In summary, we have shown that when
\phi
is UPG and rotationless,
(\ref{item:col-hhg})\Leftrightarrow(\ref{item:excessive-linearity})
.

Case 4:
We finally consider the case where
\phi
has exponential growth. We first show (
1
)
\implies
(
2
). Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
3.4
together imply that all the peripheral subgroups from Proposition
4.1
must be unbranched. Our proof of for the polynomially growing cases completes the proof in this direction.

We now prove (
2
)
\implies
(
1
). Using the polynomially growing case, we get that each peripheral subgroup is unbranched. Using Proposition
3.4
now completes the proof.
∎

Example 8.1

(Quadratic growth HHG)
.

Consider the automorphism

a\mapsto a,\,\,\,b\mapsto ba,\,\,\,\,c\mapsto cb

and the CT

representative
f
on the standard rose with filtration

\emptyset\subset\{a\}\subset\{a,b\}\subset\{a,b,c\}.

Its cyclic hierarchy decomposition consists of a vertex stabilizer
H=\langle a,b\rangle\rtimes\langle t_{v}\rangle
and edge stabilizer
\langle t_{e}\rangle
.
Then the mapping torus

\Gamma=\langle H,c^{-1}\colon c^{-1}t_{e}c=bt_{v},t_{e}=t_{v}\rangle

is virtually an HHG for any of the following reasons.

(1)

The CT representative

does not have excessive linearity (see Definition
4.11
).
The given CT has only one linear stratum,

. Excessive linearity requires more than one linear stratum by definition.
So
\Gamma
is an HHG by Theorem
A
.

(2)

The mapping torus
\Gamma
has unbranched blocks (see Definition
3.1
). There is only one block up to conjugacy, namely

, and one can use this to directly verify that
\Gamma
has unbranched blocks and apply Theorem
A
to get that
\Gamma
is an HHG.

(3)

The mapping torus
\Gamma
is (virtually) an acceptable graph of groups. Because
\Gamma
has quadratic growth and unbranched blocks, Proposition
6.7
gives that
\Gamma
virtually splits as an acceptable graph of groups and is virtually HHG.

Example
8.1
acts geometrically on a CAT(0) space. Indeed,

(Linear growth non-HHG)

(

.

Consider the outer automorphism

of

. It is sufficient to verify this in a finite-index subgroup, by Lemma

, and let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}(H)
, which is an index-
5

\Gamma

associated to the above double HNN extension is shown in Figure

The coloured cylinders correspond to cyclic subgroups whose centralizers are the fundamental groups of the four coloured subspaces intersecting in the middle torus. One way to produce a virtual HHG structure on a group is to show that it is virtually compact special, but the next example shows that one cannot recover all HHG structures provided by Theorem
A
in this way:

Example 8.4

(HHG but not cocompactly cubulated)
.

The group

\langle a,b,t\mid tat^{-1}=a^{-1},tbt^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}\rangle

has the form
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}_{2}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Phi
is linearly growing. The finite-index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle a,b,t^{2}\rangle_{\Gamma}
splits as a single HNN extension of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\mathbb{Z}
edge group, from which one can check (directly or using
[
Wis14
,
Woo18
]
) that
\Gamma
is virtually compact special, and hence a virtual HHG. One can also check directly that the virtual splitting from Proposition
4.4
has one black vertex, so Lemma
4.8
and Theorem
A
imply that
\Gamma
is an HHG. On the other hand, using
[
WY25
, Lem. 7.1]
, one verifies that
\Gamma
does not have the
RFRS
property, so it is not special (compact or otherwise), just
virtually
special.

However, slightly more complicated examples are HHGs but not even virtually cocompactly cubulated. Indeed,
[
HRSS24
]
shows that
\Gamma
is an HHG if
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of the mapping torus of a multitwist on a (punctured) orientable surface. On the other hand, if such a
\Gamma
is virtually cocompactly cubulated, then it is
chargeless

[
HP15
, Thm. B]
, and we can use the latter criterion to choose
\Gamma
with the desired properties.

Here is an explicit example where
\Gamma
is not virtually cocompactly cubulated but it is the fundamental group of a graph manifold with boundary, and hence an HHG. Let
S_{0}
be a torus with one boundary component
b_{0}
, let
S_{1}
be a torus with two boundary components
b_{1}
and
c^{\prime}
, and let

be a once-punctured genus–
2
surface; the image of
b_{0},b_{1}
in
S
is a separating curve we denote
b
. Let
\tau:S\to S
be the Dehn twist about
b
. Note that
\mathbb{F}=\pi_{1}S
is a free group of rank
4
, and the induced homomorphism
\tau_{*}:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}
is a linearly growing automorphism. Let
\Gamma
be its mapping torus, which is the fundamental group of the mapping torus of
\tau
, a graph manifold
M
with base surface
S
. The decomposition of
\Gamma
from Proposition
4.4
corresponds to the JSJ decomposition of
M
, which is obtained by cutting along the torus
b\times f\subset M
, where
f
is a fiber. The vertex spaces are
S_{i}\times f_{i}
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
, where
f_{i}
is a fiber circle in the vertex space. The edge maps
b\times f\to S_{i}\times f_{i}
are given by
s\mapsto s_{1},f\mapsto f_{1}
and

, and

represents the nontrivial homology class represented by

is nonvanishing (see

[

,

is not virtually cocompactly cubulated. (However, since

is a graph manifold with boundary, it has a nonpositively-curved Riemannian metric, and hence

is a (non-cubical) CAT(0) group.) The existence of examples like this was pointed out in
[
HRSS24
]
but explicit ones were not given.

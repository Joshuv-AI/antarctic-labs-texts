Non-strong ergodicity of canonical actions of the Thompson groups
Ryoya Arimoto

Abstract
. We show that the canonical actions of the Thompson group V V and its generalizations on the Cantor set are not strongly ergodic. This implies that the associated crossed product von Neumann algebras are not full. This also yields a non-embedding result for the Thompson groups.

Primary 37A40; Secondary 20F38, 20L05

1.
Introduction

In 1965, R. J. Thompson introduced the groups
F,T,V
in his unpublished note and showed that they are finitely presented, and
T,V
are simple.
Since then, these groups have attracted considerable attention and have been investigated extensively.

In 2017, U. Haagerup and K. K. Olesen showed that
T,V
are not inner amenable (
[
13
]
).
This property, non-inner amenability, is closely related to the fullness of their group von Neumann algebras, which is a strong form of non-amenability.
By a result of Effros (
[
10
]
), if a discrete ICC group is not inner amenable, then its group von Neumann algebra does not have property
\Gamma
.
The absence of property
\Gamma
is known to be equivalent to the fullness of a von Neumann algebra (
[
7
, Corollary 3.8]
).
Thus, non-inner amenability of a group is a sufficient condition for fullness of its group von Neumann algebra, and hence the group von Neumann algebras
L(T),L(V)
of
T,V
are full.
The result of Haagerup and Olesen is extended to the Higman–Thompson groups, one of the generalizations of the Thompson group, by E. Bashwinger and M. C. B. Zaremsky (
[
2
]
).

Since the Thompson groups can be viewed as subgroups of the group of homeomorphisms of the Cantor set
X_{2}:=\{1,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, they have the canonical action on the Cantor set.
With respect to the probability measure
\mu_{2}:=(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2})^{\mathbb{N}}
on
X_{2}
, the action of
V
on the Cantor set is non-singular, that is, for every
s\in V
, a Borel subset
A\subset X_{2}
satisfies
\mu_{2}(A)>0
if and only if
\mu_{2}(s^{-1}A)>0
.
A non-singular action gives rise to a crossed product von Neumann algebra.
Considering that the group von Neumann algebra
L(V)
is full, it is natural to ask whether the crossed product von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(X_{2},\mu_{2})\bar{\rtimes}V
is full or not.
The action
V\curvearrowright(X_{2},\mu_{2})
is ergodic.
Moreover, we will show that this action is non-amenable in the sense of Zimmer, using the fact that the Thompson group
V
contains a non-amenable subgroup whose elements have Radon–Nikodym derivatives equal to
1
except on a small subset of the Cantor set.
Thus,
L^{\infty}(X_{2},\mu_{2})\bar{\rtimes}V
is a non-amenable factor.
Observe that the action
V\curvearrowright(X_{2},\mu_{2})
is not free.
This causes difficulties, for instance, in that the crossed product von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(X_{2})\bar{\rtimes}V
differs from the von Neumann algebra associated with the orbit equivalence relation
L(\mathcal{R}_{V\curvearrowright X_{2}})
.

Regarding the question whether the crossed product
L^{\infty}(X_{2})\bar{\rtimes}V
is full or not, we obtain the following.
The same conclusion also holds for some generalizations of the Thompson group.

Theorem

(see Corollary
3.5
)
.

Let
V
be the Thompson group and
V\curvearrowright X_{2}
be the canonical action.
Then the associated von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(X_{2},\mu_{2})\bar{\rtimes}V
is non-amenable, non-full factor of type
\textrm{III}_{1/2}
.

To prove this, we show that the action
V\curvearrowright X_{2}
is not strongly ergodic.
A non-singular action of a discrete group on a probability space is called strongly ergodic if any almost invariant sequence of measurable sets is trivial (see section
2.3
for a precise definition).
If the crossed product von Neumann algebra is full, then the action is necessarily strongly ergodic.
Consequently, if the action is not strongly ergodic, then the associated crossed product is not full.
As the fullness of von Neumann algebras is a strong form of non-amenability, strong ergodicity also reflects non-amenability.
To see that the action
V\curvearrowright X_{2}
is not strongly ergodic, we focus on its orbit equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{V\curvearrowright X_{2}}
and show that it is amenable.
We can derive the amenability of
\mathcal{R}_{V\curvearrowright X_{2}}
from the groupoid of germs
\mathcal{G}_{2}
of
V\curvearrowright X_{2}
, which is known to give rise to the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{2}
and is known to be amenable.

More generally, we treat topological full groups of amenable groupoids.
Since the Thompson groups have remarkable properties, many generalizations of them have been considered.
One of the broad generalizations of the Thompson group
V
is given by the topological full groups
[[\mathcal{G}]]
of groupoids
\mathcal{G}
.
They act on the unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
of the groupoid.
Topological full groups include the Higman–Thompson groups (
[
14
]
) and the Brin–Thompson groups (
[
3
]
).
For instance, there is a groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{d,k}
such that its topological full group
[[\mathcal{G}_{d,k}]]
is isomorphic to the Higman–Thompson group
V_{d,k}
and its action
[[\mathcal{G}_{d,k}]]\curvearrowright\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{d,k}
is also isomorphic to the canonical action
V_{d,k}\curvearrowright X_{d,k}
of the Higman–Thompson group on the Cantor set (see Example
2.1
).
These groups, the Thompson group
V
, the Higman–Thompson groups, and the Brin–Thompson groups, are known to arise from a topologically principal, amenable, ample groupoid.
From the operator algebraic perspective, groupoids can be considered as a generalization of dynamical systems, and a topological full group of a groupoid is regarded as a realization of the dynamics of the groupoid.
This class is known to provide a rich source of examples of groups and group actions.

We show that not only the canonical action of the Thompson group but also those of topological full groups of topologically principal, amenable, ample groupoids are not strongly ergodic.

Theorem

(see Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topologically principal, amenable, ample groupoid.
Then the canonical action
[[\mathcal{G}]]\curvearrowright\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is not strongly ergodic with respect to any quasi-invariant probability measure on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

Consequently, the canonical actions of the Thompson group
V
, the Higman–Thompson groups, and the Brin–Thompson groups are not strongly ergodic, and their associated crossed product von Neumann algebras are not full.
This theorem also readily implies that if
\mathcal{G}
is a topologically principal, amenable, ample groupoid,
\mu
is a quasi-invariant measure on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, and
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
[[\mathcal{G}]]
, then the restricted action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
is not strongly ergodic either.
For example, this shows that for any embedding
\iota
of the free group
F_{m}

(m\geq 2)
into
[[\mathcal{G}]]
, the restricted action
\iota(F_{m})\curvearrowright(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
is not isomorphic to the Bernoulli action
F_{m}\curvearrowright(\{0,1\},\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1})^{F_{m}}
.

In the proof of the latter theorem, we show that an equivalence relation associated with an amenable groupoid is amenable.
Since our proof is not constructive, we do not know a concrete description of a non-trivial almost invariant sequence, even for the Thompson group
V
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Groupoids and topological full groups

A groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a set with a subset
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\subset\mathcal{G}
, called the unit space of
\mathcal{G}
, source and range maps
s,r\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, and a multiplication

\mathcal{G}^{(2)}:=\{(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}\mid s(g)=r(h)\}\ni(g,h)\mapsto gh\in\mathcal{G}

such that

•

for all
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)},s(x)=x=r(x)
,

•

for all
g\in\mathcal{G},gs(g)=g=r(g)g
,

•

for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)},s(gh)=s(h)\text{ and }r(gh)=r(g)
,

•

for
(g,h),(h,k)\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)},(gh)k=g(hk)
, and

•

for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
, there exists
g^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
g^{-1}g=s(g)
and
gg^{-1}=r(g).

Let
\mathcal{G}_{x}:=s^{-1}(x)
,
\mathcal{G}^{x}:=r^{-1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{G}_{x}^{x}:=\mathcal{G}_{x}\cap\mathcal{G}^{x}=s^{-1}(x)\cap r^{-1}(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

A topological groupoid is a groupoid equipped with a topology for which maps
(g,h)\mapsto gh
and
g\mapsto g^{-1}
are continuous.
In this article, all groupoids are assumed to be locally compact, Hausdorff, and with a compact unit space.
A topological groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be minimal if
\{r(g)\in\mathcal{G}\mid g\in\mathcal{G}_{x}\}\subset\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is dense for all
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.
A topological groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be topologically principal if the set
\{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\mid\mathcal{G}_{x}^{x}=\{x\}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

A topological groupoid is said to be étale if the source and range maps are local homeomorphisms.
An étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be ample if its unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is totally disconnected.
A compact open subset
U
of an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be a full bisection if
s|_{U}\colon U\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and
r|_{U}\colon U\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
are homeomorphisms.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ample groupoid.
The collection
[[\mathcal{G}]]
of full bisections of
\mathcal{G}
forms a group under the operations
UV:=\{uv\in\mathcal{G}\mid u\in U,v\in V\}
and
U^{-1}:=\{u^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}\mid u\in U\}
.
This group is called the topological full group of
\mathcal{G}
.
For a topologically principal, ample groupoid
\mathcal{G}
,
[[\mathcal{G}]]
faithfully acts on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
by
U.x:=r((s|_{U})^{-1}(x))
for
U\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
and
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

The amenability of locally compact groupoids has been introduced by Renault.
We introduce the amenability of étale groupoids as
[
1
]
.

Definition

(
[
1
]
)
.

An étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be amenable if there is a net
((m_{i}^{x})_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}})_{i}
, where
m_{i}^{x}\in\operatorname{Prob}{\mathcal{G}^{x}}
, such that
x\mapsto m_{i}^{x}(f)
is continuous for every
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
and
\|g.m_{i}^{s(g)}-m_{i}^{r(g)}\|_{1}\to 0
uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
.

Note that amenability of étale groupoid is equivalent to nuclearity of its reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}

[
4

).

Example 2.1
.

Let
d,k\geq 2
be integers.
For a positive integer
n
, put
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
X_{d}:=[d]^{\mathbb{N}}
and
X_{d,k}:=[k]\times[d]^{\mathbb{N}}
be the Cantor sets.
For
\mu\in[k]\times[d]^{*}:=[k]\cup\bigcup_{p=1}^{\infty}[k]\times[d]^{p}
and
\omega\in X_{d}
,
|\mu|
denotes a length of
\mu
,
\mu\omega\in X_{d,k}
denotes their concatenation, and
\mu X_{d}:=\{\mu\omega\mid\omega\in X_{d}\}\subset X_{d,k}
.

Let

\mathcal{G}_{d,k}:=\{(\nu\omega,|\nu|-|\mu|,\mu\omega)\in X_{d,k}\times\mathbb{Z}\times X_{d,k}\mid\mu,\nu\in[k]\times[d]^{*},\omega\in X_{d}\}

be a groupoid with structure maps

\displaystyle s(\nu\omega,|\nu|-|\mu|,\mu\omega)=\mu\omega,\quad r(\nu\omega,|\nu|-|\mu|,\mu\omega)=\nu\omega,

\displaystyle(\nu\omega,|\nu|-|\mu|,\mu\omega)^{-1}=(\mu\omega,|\mu|-|\nu|,\nu\omega)

\displaystyle(\mu_{3}\omega,|\mu_{3}|-|\mu_{2}|,\mu_{2}\omega)(\mu_{2}\omega,|\mu_{2}|-|\mu_{1}|,\mu_{1}\omega)=(\mu_{3}\omega,|\mu_{3}|-|\mu_{1}|,\mu_{1}\omega).

This becomes an ample, topologically principal groupoid with a topology generated by
\{(\nu\omega,|\nu|-|\mu|,\mu\omega)\in\mathcal{G}_{d,k}\mid\omega\in X_{d}\}
where
\mu,\nu\in[k]\times[d]^{*}
.
Moreover, this groupoid is amenable since its reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{d,k})
is isomorphic to a nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathbb{M}_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{d}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{d}
is the Cuntz algebra of degree
d
.

The topological full group
[[\mathcal{G}_{d,k}]]
of this groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{d,k}
is known to be isomorphic to the Higman–Thompson group
V_{d,k}
.
Here, the Higman–Thompson group
V_{d,k}
is a collection of homeomorphisms of
X_{d,k}
of the following form:
let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n},\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}\in[k]\times[d]^{*}
be finite words such that
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}X_{d}=X_{d,k}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i}X_{d}
.
Then
X_{d,k}\ni\mu_{i}\omega\mapsto\nu_{i}\omega\in X_{d,k}
defines a homeomorphism of
X_{d,k}
.
This homeomorphism is denoted by

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n}\\
\nu_{1}&\cdots&\nu_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
The isomorphism is given by

V_{d,k}\ni\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n}\\
\nu_{1}&\cdots&\nu_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\{(\nu_{i}\omega,|\nu_{i}|-|\mu_{i}|,\mu_{i}\omega)\mid\omega\in X_{d}\}\in[[\mathcal{G}_{d,k}]].

Example 2.2
.

Using the similar notations as the above example, let

\mathcal{G}_{2}:=\{(\nu\omega,|\nu|-|\mu|,\mu\omega)\in X_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}\times X_{2}\mid\mu,\nu\in[2]^{*},\omega\in X_{2}\}.

Then
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{m}:=\mathcal{G}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{G}_{2}

This groupoid is also amenable since
\mathrm{C}_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{2}^{m})

\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\otimes m}
.
It is well known that its topological full group
[[\mathcal{G}_{2}^{m}]]

mV

2.2.
Equivalence relations

An equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on a set
X
is a principal groupoid whose unit space is
X
, i.e., a groupoid with
\mathcal{R}_{x}\cap\mathcal{R}^{x}=\{x\}
.
Let
(X,\mu)
be the standard probability space.
A Borel equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
X
is an equivalence relation on
X
such that
\mathcal{R}\subset X\times X
is a Borel subset.
Let
[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
denote a equivalence class of
\mathcal{R}
containing
x\in X
.
In this article, we assume that all equivalence classes of an equivalence relation are countable.
The equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
is said to be non-singular for
\mu
if for each
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)=0
, one has
\mu([E]_{\mathcal{R}})=0
where
[E]_{\mathcal{R}}:=\bigcup_{x\in E}[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
.
The full group
[\mathcal{R}]
of
\mathcal{R}
is a collection of Borel automorphisms
\varphi\colon X\to X
such that
\{(\varphi(x),x)\in X\times X\mid x\in X\}\subset\mathcal{R}
.

Example 2.3
.

Assume that a discrete group
\Gamma
acts on the standard Borel space
X
by Borel automorphisms.
Its orbit equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}
is an equivalence relation defined by
\{(sx,x)\in X\times X\mid s\in\Gamma,x\in X\}
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ample groupoid.
The associated equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
defined by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}:=\{(r(g),s(g))\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\mid g\in\mathcal{G}\}
.

The groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{d,k}
in Example
2.1
gives the tail equivalence relation on
[k]\times[d]^{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e., for
x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty},y=(y_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in[k]\times[d]^{\mathbb{N}}
,
(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}_{d,k}}
if there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{p+i}=y_{q+i}
for all
i\geq 1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{x}:=\{(y,x)\in\mathcal{R}\},\mathcal{R}^{y}:=\{(y,x)\in\mathcal{R}\}
for
x,y\in X
.

The measure-theoretic notion of amenability for equivalence relations is introduced by Zimmer (
[
22
]
), and an equivalent condition is given by Connes, Feldman, and Weiss (
[
8
]
).
The Borel-theoretic notion of amenability for equivalence relations is introduced by Jackson, Kechris, and Louveau (
[
16
]
).

Definition

(see
[
22
,
8
,
16
]
)
.

A countable Borel equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on the standard probability space
(X,\mu)
is said to be (Borel) amenable if there is a net
((p_{i}^{x})_{x\in X})_{i}
, where
p_{i}^{x}\in\operatorname{Prob}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}
such that
\mathcal{R}\ni(y,x)\mapsto p_{i}^{y}(y,x)
is measurable and
\|\gamma p_{i}^{x}-p_{i}^{y}\|_{1}\to 0
for all

, where
\gamma p_{i}^{x}(y,z):=p_{i}^{x}(x,z)
.
It is said to be
\mu
-amenable if there is a
\mu
-conull Borel subset
A\subset X
such that
\mathcal{R}|_{A}
is amenable.

Note that a subequivalence relation of a
\mu

\mu
-amenable (see
[

]
and
[

).

With a non-singular equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)

L(\mathcal{R})
.
This associated von Neumann algebra
L(\mathcal{R})
is generated by unitaries
\{u_{\varphi}\mid\varphi\in[\mathcal{R}]\}
and
L^{\infty}(X)
with relations

for
\varphi\in[\mathcal{R}]

.
For an accurate definition of von Neumann algebras associated with equivalence relations, see, for example,
[

.

\mathcal{R}

2.3.
Strongly ergodic actions and full factors

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
be a non-singular action of a discrete group
\Gamma
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
.
The action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is said to be strongly ergodic if for any sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of measurable subsets of
X
such that
\lim_{n}\mu(A_{n}\bigtriangleup sA_{n})=0
for all
s\in\Gamma
, we have
\lim_{n}\mu(A_{n})(1-\mu(A_{n}))=0
.
Note that any amenable action is never strongly ergodic.

Next, we review the fullness of factors.
Fullness of factors has been introduced by Connes.
He defines fullness by several equivalent conditions.
Here, we introduce fullness by using a centralizing sequence.
For a von Neumann algebra
x\in M
and
\varphi\in M_{*}
, define
x\varphi,\varphi x\in M_{*}
by
[x\varphi](y):=\varphi(yx)
and
[\varphi x](y):=\varphi(xy)
.

Definition

(
[
7
]
)
.

A factor
M
with separable predual is called full if for any bounded sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
of elements of
M
such that
\|x_{n}\varphi-\varphi x_{n}\|\to 0
for any
\varphi\in M_{*}
, there exists a bounded sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
of elements of
\mathbb{C}
such that
x_{n}-z_{n}\to 0
in the
*
-strong topology.

If the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is not strongly ergodic, then the crossed product
L^{\infty}(X)\bar{\rtimes}\Gamma
is never full.
Indeed, if
(A_{n})_{n}
is a non-trivial almost invariant sequence, i.e.,
\lim_{n}\mu(A_{n}\bigtriangleup sA_{n})=0
for all
s\in\Gamma
and
\limsup_{n}\mu(A_{n})(1-\mu(A_{n}))\neq 0
, then
(1_{A_{n}})_{n}\subset L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\bar{\rtimes}\Gamma
is a non-trivial centralizing sequence.
In some cases, strong ergodicity is one of the sufficient conditions for fullness of the crossed product, e.g.,
[
5
,
15
]
.

3.
Main result

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an amenable étale groupoid.
Then the associated equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
is Borel amenable.

Proof.

Let
(m_{i}^{x})_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
be a net of a family of maps as in the definition of amenability.
Define
\displaystyle p_{i}^{x}(x,y):=\sum_{s(g)=y,r(g)=x}m_{i}^{x}(g)
for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, then
p_{i}^{x}\in\operatorname{Prob}{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{G}}^{x}
since

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)};(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}}p_{i}^{x}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)};(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{s(g)=y,r(g)=x}m_{i}^{x}(g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}^{x}}m_{i}^{x}(g)

\displaystyle=1.

We will show that
\|\gamma p_{i}^{y}-p_{i}^{x}\|_{1}\to 0
for
\gamma=(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Fix
g\in\mathcal{G}
with
s(g)=y,r(g)=x
.
Then one has

\displaystyle\gamma p_{i}^{y}(x,z)

\displaystyle=p_{i}^{y}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s(h)=z,r(h)=y}m_{i}^{y}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s(k)=z,r(k)=r(g)}gm_{i}^{s(g)}(k).

One also has
\displaystyle p_{i}^{x}(x,z)=\sum_{s(k)=z,r(k)=r(g)}m_{i}^{r(g)}(k)
and we have

\displaystyle\|\gamma p_{i}^{y}-p_{i}^{x}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,z)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}^{x}}\left|\sum_{s(h)=z,r(h)=x}gm_{i}^{s(g)}(h)-m_{i}^{r(g)}(h)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|gm_{i}^{s(g)}-m_{i}^{s(g)}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\to 0

Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
is amenable.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topologically principal, amenable, ample groupoid.
Then the canonical action
[[\mathcal{G}]]\curvearrowright\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is not strongly ergodic with respect to any quasi-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

Proof.

Since the equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
associated to
\mathcal{G}
is
\mu
-amenable, by Rokhlin’s lemma (
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
), there is a sequence
(A_{n})
of measurable subsets of
X
such that
\mu(A_{n}\triangle sA_{n})\to 0
for all
s\in[\mathcal{R}]
and
\mu(A_{n})(1-\mu(A_{n}))\not\to 0
.
Since
[[\mathcal{G}]]<[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}]
, this
(A_{n})
is a non-trivial almost invariant sequence for
[[\mathcal{G}]]\curvearrowright(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
.
Therefore,
[[\mathcal{G}]]\curvearrowright(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
is not strongly ergodic.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain a non-embedding result.
Note that there are many ways to embed free groups into
[[\mathcal{G}]]
.
For instance, it is not hard to see that there is an embedding
\iota\colon F_{2}\hookrightarrow V
such that the restricted action
\iota(F_{2})\curvearrowright\{1,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}
is (topologically) isomorphic to the action
F_{2}\curvearrowright\partial F_{2}
of the free group on its Gromov boundary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topologically principal, amenable, ample groupoid,
\mu
a quasi-invariant measure on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, and
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\nu)
a strongly ergodic action on a standard probability space.
Then there is no embedding
\iota\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow[[\mathcal{G}]]
such that the restricted action
\iota(\Gamma)\curvearrowright(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
is isomorphic to
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\nu)
.

In particular, for any embedding
\iota\colon F_{m}\hookrightarrow[[\mathcal{G}]]
, the restricted action
\iota(F_{m})\curvearrowright(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
is not isomorphic to the Bernoulli action
F_{m}\curvearrowright(\{0,1\},\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1})^{F_{m}}
, which is known to be strongly ergodic.

Finally, we investigate the canonical action
V_{d,k}\curvearrowright X_{d,k}
of the Higman–Thompson group on the Cantor set and the associated crossed product von Neumann algebra.
Let
\mu_{d,k}
be the probability measure on
X_{d,k}=[k]\times[d]^{\mathbb{N}}
defined by
\mu_{d,k}:=\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}\right)\otimes\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{i}\right)^{\mathbb{N}}
.
First, we see that the action
V_{d,k}\curvearrowright(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})
is ergodic.
Let
A\subset X_{d,k}
be a
V_{d,k}
-invariant measurable subset with positive measure.
Then one sees that the value
\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{d,k}(A\cap\nu X_{d,d})}{\mu_{d,k}(\nu X_{d,d})}
is independent of
\nu\in[k]\times[d]^{*}
since for every
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in[k]\times[d]^{*}
, there exists
g\in V_{d,k}
such that
g(\nu_{1}X_{d,d})=\nu_{2}X_{d,d}
.
Let this value be
c
.
Then, for any disjoint union
B
of finitely many cylinder sets,
\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{d,k}(A\cap B)}{\mu_{d,k}(B)}=c
.
Since the set of Borel sets which can be approximated by a disjoint union of finitely many cylinder sets is a
\sigma
-algebra of whole Borel sets,
\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{d,k}(A\cap B)}{\mu_{d,k}(B)}=c
for every Borel subset
B
of
X_{d,k}
.
Hence, we have
\displaystyle\mu_{d,k}(A)=c=\frac{\mu_{d,k}(A)}{\mu_{d,k}(A)}=1
.

We now show that this action is not amenable in the sense of Zimmer.
By
[
21
]
, if a non-singular action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is non-amenable in the sense of Zimmer, then the crossed product
L^{\infty}(X)\bar{\rtimes}\Gamma
is non-amenable.
To see non-amenability, we need the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
9
, Theorem 7]
,
[
20
, Proposition 5.3]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
be its non-singular action on a standard probability space.
Denote by
\omega\colon\Gamma\times X\to(0,+\infty)
the Radon–Nikodym cocycle:
\displaystyle\omega(s,x):=\frac{ds^{-1}\mu}{d\mu}(x)
.
Assume that there exists a finite subset
F\subset\Gamma
such that

\sum_{s\in F}\int_{X}\sqrt{\omega(s,x)}\,d\mu(x)>\left\|\sum_{s\in F}\lambda_{s}\right\|,

where
\lambda\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
denotes the left regular representation.
Then the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is non-amenable in the sense of Zimmer.

Since the Higman–Thompson group
V_{d,d}
is non-amenable, there exists a finite subset
F\subset V_{d,d}
such that
\displaystyle\|\sum_{s\in F}\lambda_{s}\|<|F|
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2.6.8]
).
Take an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such as
\displaystyle|F|\left(1-\frac{1}{kd^{n-1}}\right)>\|\sum_{s\in F}\lambda_{s}\|
.
Let
\nu=1\cdots 1\in[k]\times[d]^{n-1}
be a finite word consisting of
n
consecutive 1s.
Consider an embedding
V_{d,d}
into
V_{d,k}
in such a way that
V_{d,d}
acts canonically on
\nu X_{d}\cong X_{d}
and trivially on
X_{d,k}\setminus\nu X_{d}
.
Note that for each
s\in V_{d,d}\subset V_{d,k}
and
x\in X_{d,k}\setminus\nu X_{d}
,
\omega(s,x)=1
.
Thus we have

\sum_{s\in F}\int_{X}\sqrt{\omega(s,x)}\,d\mu_{d,k}(x)>\sum_{s\in F}\mu_{d,k}(X_{d,k}\setminus\nu X_{d})=|F|\left(1-\frac{1}{kd^{n-1}}\right)>\|\sum_{s\in F}\lambda_{s}\|.

Therefore, the action
V_{d,k}\curvearrowright(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})
is non-amenable by Theorem
3.4
.
Note that this argument also applies to a topological full group of essentially principal, ample, purely infinite, and minimal groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, hence the action
[[\mathcal{G}]]\curvearrowright\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is non-amenable.
For an embedding of
V
into
[[\mathcal{G}]]
, see
[
12
, Proposition 3.7]
.

The associated crossed product
L^{\infty}(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k}
is a factor since
V_{d,k}
is ICC and the action is ergodic.
Its type is computed as follows (cf.
[
18
, Proposition 2.7]
).
Let
(V_{d,k})_{\mu_{d,k}}
be a subgroup of
V_{d,k}
consisting of elements of
V_{d,k}
which fix
\mu_{d,k}
.
Then this group is ICC, and it is not hard to see that the restricted action
(V_{d,k})_{\mu_{d,k}}\curvearrowright(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})
is ergodic, and thus,
L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k})_{\mu_{d,k}}
is a (II
1
) factor.
The inclusion
L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k})_{\mu_{d,k}}\subset L^{\infty}(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k}
is irreducible since
V_{d,k}
is ICC relative to
(V_{d,k})_{\mu_{d,k}}
.
Observe that the modular operator
\Delta_{\varphi}
on
L^{2}(X_{d,k})\otimes\ell^{2}(V_{d,k})
of the canonical faithful state
\varphi(f\lambda_{s}):=\delta_{s,e}\mu_{d,k}(f)
is given by
\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}(\xi\otimes\delta_{s}):=\left(\frac{ds\mu_{d,k}}{d\mu_{d,k}}\right)^{-1}\xi\otimes\delta_{s}
and the modular automorphism group
(\sigma_{t}^{\varphi})_{t}
on
L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k}
of
\varphi
is given by
\displaystyle\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(f\lambda_{s})=\left(\frac{ds\mu_{d,k}}{d\mu_{d,k}}\right)^{-it}f\lambda_{s}
.
Thus, we have

L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k})_{\mu_{d,k}}\subset(L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k}))^{\sigma^{\varphi}}\subset L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k},

where
(L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k}))^{\sigma^{\varphi}}:=\{x\in L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k})\mid\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)=x\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, and
(L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}(V_{d,k}))^{\sigma^{\varphi}}
is a factor.
Hence, by
[
6
, Corollary 3.2.7]
, the Connes’ S-invariant
S(L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k})
is
\sigma(\Delta_{\varphi})=d^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{0\}
and thus
L^{\infty}(X_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k}
is of type III
1/d
.

In conclusion, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
V_{d,k}
be the Higman–Thompson group,
V_{d,k}\curvearrowright(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})
be the canonical action.
Then the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(X_{d,k},\mu_{d,k})\bar{\rtimes}V_{d,k}
is non-amenable, non-full factor of type
\textrm{III}_{1/d}
.

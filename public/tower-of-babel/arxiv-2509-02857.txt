Magnetic double-wells: Absence of Tunneling
Charles L. Fefferman, Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
We present a magnetic double-well Hamiltonian where the tunneling between the two wells vanishes, as recently announced in [ FSW25a ] .



1
Introduction

Quantum tunneling in double-well systems is a cornerstone phenomenon in mathematical physics. In a non-magnetic setting, a particle localized in one of two deep and well-separated potential wells has a nonzero probability of tunneling to the other well. The tunneling time is inversely proportional to the distance between the lowest two eigenvalues of the double-well Hamiltonian, a quantity
which is (typically) exponentially small in the well-depth and well-separation.
This paradigm, developed both heuristically and rigorously, goes back to the classical literature and underlies a great variety of effects in physics and chemistry; see, for example,
[
LL81
]
in physics or
[
Sim84
,
HS84
]
in mathematics. The latter two works derive a lower bound on the eigenvalue splitting assuming the single-well potential has a single non-degenerate minimum. In
[
FLW18
]
lower bounds are derived assuming rather that the single-well is compactly supported.

In contrast, adding a
constant magnetic field
fundamentally alters the picture. Early works on the magnetic case resolved the case of weak magnetic fields, confirming agreement with the non-magnetic case
[
HS87
]
. For strong magnetic fields, magnetic translations endow localized states with nontrivial complex phases, yielding interference among tunneling paths. For example in
[
FSW22
]
we showed that for double-well magnetic systems whose single-well is radial, the magnetic field effect leads to an exponentially smaller tunneling amplitude. However, such systems with radial single-well potential were shown to still possess a strictly positive lower bound on tunneling
[
FSW22
,
HK24
,
Mor24
]
.

Recently we have announced
[
FSW25a
]
that the magnetic setting admits a phenomenon that has no analogue in the non-magnetic case:
exact vanishing of tunneling
in a double-well system built from a suitable non-radial single-well potential. In this paper we present a complete proof. We construct a family of (non-radial) single-well potentials such that, in a strong constant magnetic field and for large well depth, the associated symmetric double-well has zero eigenvalue splitting between its two lowest levels, so that tunneling is entirely eliminated. Moreover, by varying parameters within this family one can flip the parity of the ground state (from even to odd),
via a coalescence and re-emergence of two distinct eigenvalues.

A recent preprint
[
EM25
]
studies diminished tunneling due to asymmetry of the single-well Hamiltonian in a setting with “magnetic wells” and no electronic potential.

Intuitive explanation of the mechanism.

Engineered small asymmetric deviations from radial symmetry in a single-well, as a mechanism for reducing and even completely eliminating
tunneling between wells in quantum magnetic systems, was first explained - in brief - in
[
FSW25a
]
. We start with what we call a "planet": a radially-symmetric single-well potential
v^{\circ}
whose tunneling effect is well-understood (since
[
FSW22
]
). On top of it we add so-called "sophons": perturbations
W
which are placed nearby to
v^{\circ}
; their support is exponentially small and moreover they have a coupling constant which is itself also exponentially small. However, the key is where they are placed: thanks to the oscillations afforded by the magnetic translations, we can position the sophons so that: (1) some of the sophon-planet interaction terms dominate the planet-planet interaction term in a basic formula for
\rho_{0}
and (2) the overall sign of (the real quantity)
\rho_{0}
can be made positive or negative by slightly perturbing the position of the sophons (see
eq.

2.32
below). Since
\rho_{0}
asymptotically controls
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
as well as
\Delta_{0}
, this allows for the vanishing of both.

Consequences and outlook.

Beyond its intrinsic spectral interest, a robust handle on the sign and magnitude of
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
has implications for engineered quantum systems. In periodic settings, suppressed nearest-neighbor hopping leads to dispersionless (flat) bands near the atomic energy, a regime associated with enhanced interaction, nonlinearities, and correlation effects. Our construction suggests a possible route to nearly flat magnetic bands in 2D tight-binding models obtained from continuum magnetic Schrödinger operators, with quite general (not necessarily translation invariant) underlying atomic lattice, by strong-binding reduction; see the discussion in
[
SW22
]
. It is also natural to ask about extensions to three dimensions, other magnetic field orientations, and the interplay with additional (e.g. spin or spin–orbit) couplings.

\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{z\right\}<\inf\sigma(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega})
we have

\displaystyle\left(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,y)=\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{tz}\exp\left(-tH^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}\right)\left(x,y\right)\operatorname{d}{t}\,.

We are only interested in the absolute value of the Green’s function, which is positive if
z\in\mathbb{R}
, so let us (for the sake of brevity) WLOG assume
z\in\mathbb{R}
in the sequel. Since
\sigma(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega})=\omega(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}+1)
we find for all
z<\omega
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Set{0}

\displaystyle\left(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{4\pi}\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(tz-\frac{\omega}{4}\coth\left(\omega t\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)}{\sinh\left(\omega t\right)}\operatorname{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\left(\coth(s)-\frac{4z}{\omega^{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\,s\right)\right)}{\sinh(s)}\,\operatorname{d}{s}\,.

Splitting the integral into
(0,\infty)=(0,1)\cup[1,\infty)
and using
\sinh(\xi)\geq\xi
and
\coth(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{\xi}
for all
\xi>0
we get

\displaystyle I_{s\,\rm small}\leq\frac{\operatorname{e}^{z_{+}/\omega}}{4\pi}\int_{s=0}^{1}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}/s\right)}{s}\operatorname{d}{s}\leq\frac{\operatorname{e}^{z_{+}/\omega}}{\pi\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)\,;

for large
s
, we use
\sinh(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\operatorname{e}^{-2}\right)\operatorname{e}^{\xi}
which holds for all
\xi\geq 1
and
\coth(\xi)\geq 1
, to get

\displaystyle I_{s\,\rm large}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\left(\coth(s)-\frac{4z}{\omega^{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\,s\right)\right)}{\sinh(s)}\,\operatorname{d}{s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{2}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-2}}\int_{s=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-s-\frac{\omega}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\left(1-\frac{4z}{\omega^{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\,s\right)\right)\,\operatorname{d}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{2}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)\int_{s=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\left(1-z/\omega\right)s\right)\operatorname{d}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)\frac{\exp\left(-\left(1-z/\omega\right)\right)}{1-z/\omega}\,.

Together this yields (for
z<\omega
and
x\neq 0
):

\displaystyle\left(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,0)\leq\left[\frac{1}{\,\omega\,\|x\|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{e}-\operatorname{e}^{-1})}\,\frac{\omega}{\omega-z}\right]\frac{\operatorname{e}^{z_{+}/\omega}}{\pi}\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{1}{4}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\,.

For the lower bound, assuming
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we rewrite

\displaystyle\left(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}f(s)\right)}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-2s}}\,\operatorname{d}{s}

with

\displaystyle f(s):=\coth(s)+\left(1-z/\omega\right)\frac{4}{\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\,s\,.

We use
1-\operatorname{e}^{-2s}\leq 1
and
\coth(s)=1+\frac{2}{\operatorname{e}^{2s}-1}
to get

\displaystyle\left(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,0)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{2}\frac{\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}{\operatorname{e}^{2s}-1}-\left(1-z/\omega\right)s\right)\operatorname{d}{s}\,.

Now iff
s\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)
then
\frac{\omega}{2}\frac{\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}{\operatorname{e}^{2s}-1}\leq 1
and so we have

\displaystyle\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega}{2}\frac{\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}{\operatorname{e}^{2s}-1}-\left(1-z/\omega\right)s\right)\operatorname{d}{s}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{e}^{-1}\int_{s=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\left(1-z/\omega\right)s\right)\operatorname{d}{s}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{e}^{-1}\frac{\exp\left(-\left(1-z/\omega\right)\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)\right)}{1-z/\omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\operatorname{e}\left(1-z/\omega\right)}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-z/\omega\right)}\,.

Combining everything together we find

\displaystyle\left(H^{\mathrm{SHO}}_{\omega}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,0)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{e}\left(1-z/\omega\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-z/\omega\right)}

This completes the proof of
A.1
.
∎

Appendix B

Gaussian decay of ground states of magnetic Hamiltonians with compact wells

Proposition B.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded, smooth and compactly supported. Say
\psi,E
is a normalized ground state eigen pair for
H:=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}+\lambda^{2}u(X)
where
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}
is the Landau Hamiltonian. If
E<\lambda
then there is some explicit constant
C_{\lambda}(E,x)<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\left|\psi(x)\right|\leq\lambda^{2}C_{\lambda}(E,x)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\left|\operatorname{supp}(u)\right|}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{4}\mathrm{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}(u))^{2}\right)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Set{0})\,.

Here
|\operatorname{supp}(u)|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\operatorname{supp}(u)
and
C_{\lambda}(E,x)
is given by

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}(E,x):=\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\frac{E_{+}}{\lambda}}}{\pi\left(1-\frac{E}{\lambda}\right)}\left(\frac{1-\frac{E}{\lambda}}{\,\lambda\,\|x\|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{e}-\operatorname{e}^{-1})}\right)\,.

(B.1)

In particular if
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\geq a
for some
a>0
and
E\leq 0
we have

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}(E,x)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\,\lambda a^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{e}-\operatorname{e}^{-1})}\right)\leq 1

for
\lambda
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We rewrite
H\psi\equiv E\psi
as

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)\psi=-\lambda^{2}u(X)\psi\,.

Since
E<\lambda
, we may invert
\left(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)
. We then invoke the estimates above (combining
A.1
and
eq.

A.4
) on the Landau resolvent to obtain

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,y)\right|\lambda^{2}\left|u(y)\right|\left|\psi(y)\right|\operatorname{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\int_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(u)}\left|\left(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,y)\right|^{2}\operatorname{d}{y}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}(E,x)\lambda^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\int_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(u)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}\right)\operatorname{d}{y}}

where
C_{\lambda}(E,x)
is given by
eq.

B.1
(it is the constant appearing on the RHS of
A.1
with
\omega\mapsto\lambda,z\mapsto E
). Now if
\mathrm{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}(u))>0
then for all
y\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
,
\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\geq\mathrm{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}(u))
; otherwise use the trivial bound by zero.
∎

Appendix C

Comparing
\varphi_{\lambda}
with
\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}

Our goal in this section is to compare the ground state eigenpairs of the Hamiltonians
h^{0}_{\lambda}
and
h_{\lambda}
.

Let us first review the setup.
The single-well Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{2}+v^{\circ}(x)+\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W(x)\ =\ h^{\circ}_{\lambda}+\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W(x).

Assumptions:

v^{\circ}
and
W
are smooth

(C.1a)

\displaystyle\|v^{\circ}\|_{\infty},\ \|W\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}

(C.1b)

\displaystyle v^{\circ}(x),\ W(x)\leq 0

(C.1c)

\displaystyle v^{\circ}\textrm{ is radial},\quad{\rm supp}\ v^{\circ}\subset B_{r_{0}}(0)

(C.1d)

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\ W\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{r_{0}+D}(0);\ D\geq C_{2},\ \textrm{dependent on $c_{1}$ and $C_{1}$}

(C.1e)

\displaystyle 0<\tau<\tau_{0},\textrm{a small constant dependent only on $c_{1}$ and $C_{1}$}

(C.1f)

The ground state eigenvalue,
e^{\circ}_{\lambda}
, of
h^{\circ}_{\lambda}
satisfies

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-c_{1}\lambda^{2}\leq e^{\circ}_{\lambda}\leq C_{1}\lambda^{2},

(C.1g)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\textrm{dist}\Big(e^{\circ}_{\lambda},\sigma(h_{\lambda}^{\circ})\setminus\{e^{\circ}_{\lambda}\}\Big)\geq c_{\rm gap}>0

(C.1h)

\displaystyle\lambda\geq C_{1},\ \textrm{a large enough constant dependent on $c_{1}$ and $C_{1}$.}

(C.1i)

We note that
D
appearing above is actually
D-C
from the main text, and
r_{0}=1
was used in the main text.

In this appendix, constants
c,C
etc are determined by the constants in the above assumptions.

Claim C.1
.

Under assumptions
Equation

C.1
, the Hamiltonian
h_{\lambda}
has a ground state eigenpair

(\varphi_{\lambda},e_{\lambda})
:

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}\ \varphi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda},\quad\|\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}=1,

(C.2)

with
e_{\lambda}\sim e_{\lambda}^{\circ}

and
\varphi_{\lambda}\sim\varphi_{\lambda}^{\circ}
.

Bounds on:
|e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda}|
and
\|\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}

Using that
v_{\lambda}
is a small perturbation of
\lambda^{2}v^{\circ}
, we prove the following:

Proposition C.2
.

In the setting of assumptions
Equation

C.1
,

\displaystyle|e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\leq\|W\|_{\infty}\ \tau_{\lambda,M,D},

(C.3)

\displaystyle|\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{2}\ \exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{4}\big[{\rm dist}\big(x,\operatorname{supp}v^{\circ}\big)\big]^{2}\right),\quad\textrm{if $x\in{\rm supp}(W)$}

(C.4)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\ |\tau|\ \lambda^{2}\ \exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{4}D^{2}\right).

(C.5)

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of
C.2
.

We express
\varphi_{\lambda}
in the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}=\beta\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}+u,\quad\textrm{where $\beta\in\mathbb{C}$ and $u\perp\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}$.}

(C.6)

Without any loss of generality, we may assume that
\beta
is real and non-negative.
We next estimate
e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda}
and
u
.

Claim C.3
.

|e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda}|\leq\|W\|_{\infty}\ \tau
.

Proof.

Note that
h_{\lambda}\leq h_{0}^{\lambda}
, since
W\leq 0
. Hence,
e_{\lambda}\leq e^{\circ}_{\lambda}
. On the other hand,

e_{\lambda}=\left\langle h_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}\right\rangle=\left\langle h^{0}_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W\varphi_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}\right\rangle\geq e^{\circ}_{\lambda}-\tau\|W\|_{\infty}.

Therefore,
-\tau\|W\|_{\infty}\leq e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda}\leq 0
. This implies
eq.

C.3
.
∎

We next estimate
u
, defined in
eq.

C.6
. Note
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\mathbb{C}\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\oplus\{\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\}^{\perp}
and introduce the orthogonal projection operators
\Pi^{\circ}=\Pi^{\circ}_{\lambda}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{C}\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}
and
\Pi^{\circ,\perp}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\{\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\}^{\perp}
. Next let

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0}=\Pi^{\circ,\perp}\Big(h^{0}_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda}\mathds{1}\Big)^{-1}\Pi^{\circ,\perp}

(C.7)

By the spectral gap
2.2
we have that

\|\mathcal{G}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2})}\leq C
, where
C
is independent of
\lambda
for
\lambda\geq C_{1}
.

(C.8)

By
Equation

C.6
,
(h_{0}^{\lambda}+\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W-e_{\lambda})(\beta\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}+u)=0
, and since
h_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}=e^{\circ}_{\lambda}\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}
, it follows that

\displaystyle f\equiv(h_{0}^{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda})u=-\beta\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda,M,D}Wu+(e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda})\beta\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}+(e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda})u.

(C.9)

Then, since
u\perp\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}
,
we have

f=\Pi^{\circ,\perp}f\ \textrm{and}\ u=\mathcal{G}_{0}f.

Then,

\displaystyle f=-\beta\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda,M,D}W\mathcal{G}_{0}f+(e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda})\beta\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}+(e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda})\mathcal{G}_{0}f,

and hence by applying
\Pi^{\circ,\perp}
:

\displaystyle f=-\beta\tau_{\lambda,M,D}\Pi^{\circ,\perp}W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda,M,D}\Pi^{\circ,\perp}W\mathcal{G}_{0}f+(e_{\lambda}-e^{\circ}_{\lambda})\Pi^{\circ,\perp}\mathcal{G}_{0}f.

Thus,

\Big(\ I+\tau_{\lambda,M,D}\Pi^{\circ,\perp}W\mathcal{G}_{0}+(e^{\circ}_{\lambda}-e_{\lambda})\Pi^{\circ,\perp}\mathcal{G}_{0}\Big)f=-\beta\tau_{\lambda,M,D}\Pi^{\circ,\perp}W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}.

Since
|e^{\circ}_{\lambda}-e_{\lambda}|\leq C\tau
(
C.3
),
\|\mathcal{G}_{0}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C
(bound
Equation

C.8
) and
\|W\|_{\infty}\leq C
, the operator perturbing the identity has norm bounded by
\lesssim\tau
. Hence, the Neumann series converges in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2})
, and we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\ |\beta|\ |\tau|\ \Big\|W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\ |\tau|\ \Big\|W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L^{2}}.

(C.10)

The latter inequality in
eq.

C.10
follows since

\displaystyle 0\leq\beta\leq 1.

(C.11)

Indeed, since
\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}
and
\varphi_{\lambda}
are normalized, the decomposition

C.6
implies

1=\beta^{2}+\|u\|^{2}
, where we recall that
\beta
could be taken to be real and non-negative. This implies
eq.

C.11
.

To bound
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
, recall from
eq.

C.9
that
u=\mathcal{G}_{0}f
and
\|\mathcal{G}_{0}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C
.
Therefore, by
Equation

C.10

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\ \|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|\tau|\ \Big\|W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L^{2}}.

(C.12)

Hence, we next bound the

norm of
W\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}
. For this we apply
2.5
to obtain a pointwise upper bound on
\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}
on the support of
W
.
Note that

, the constant appearing in

.
Then we have

\displaystyle|\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\lambda^{2}\ \exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{4}[{\rm dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}(v^{\circ}))]^{2}\right),\ \ {\rm dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}(v^{\circ}))\geq D.

(C.13)

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\varphi^{\circ}_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\lambda^{2}\ \exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{4}D^{2}\right),\quad\textrm{if $x\in{\rm supp}(W)$.}

Bounding the

Therefore, substituting

C.15

C.12

Recall that

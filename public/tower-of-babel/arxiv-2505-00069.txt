Superfluid Weight of Inhomogeneous Superconductors
Jonathan Schirmer, Enrico Rossi

Abstract
In this work, we obtain the expression, within the linear response approximation, of the superfluid weight of a superconductor, taking into account the response of the superconductor’s pairing potential to the perturbing vector potential. We find that, in general, the correction is non-zero when the system is spatially non-uniform. We apply the formula to two exemplary cases: the case when strong inhomogeneities in the pairing potential are induced by a periodic potential, and the case when superconducting vortices are induced by an external magnetic field. For both cases we show that the correction to the superfluid weight due to the response of the paring potential to the perturbing vector potential can be significant, it must be included to obtain quantitatively correct results, and that for the case when vortices are present the expression of the superfluid weight that does not include such correction returns qualitatively wrong results.



The Meissner effect is the hallmark signature of superconductivity.
It is described by the London’s equation

J_{\mu}=D_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}A_{\nu}

where
J_{\mu}
is the
\mu
component of the charge current,
A_{\nu}

is the
\nu
component of a, static, transverse, long-wavelength, vector potential,
and
D_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}
is the superfluid weight tensor.
This equation shows that the superfluid weight quantifies the strength of the Meissner effect, and therefore
the “robustness” of the superconducting state.
As a consequence
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
can be seen as the key quantity that characterizes
a superconductor
Scalapino et al. 1992
;
Scalapino et al. 1993
.
In two dimensions (2D)
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
is also the quantity
that fixes the critical temperature,
T_{BKT}
, at which the
Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT)
Berezinsky 1971
;
Kosterlitz and Thouless 1973
transition,
between superconducting and normal phase, takes place.
The essential role
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
plays in determining the crucial properties
of superconductors makes its correct and accurate estimation very important.

For a standard, isotropic, superconductor with an isolated parabolic band crossing the Fermi
energy in the normal phase, at zero temperature, we have the conventional result
{\rm Tr}[D_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}]/d=e^{2}n/m^{*}
,
where
d
is the number of dimensions,
e
is the electron’s charge,
n
is the electrons’ density,
and
m^{*}
is the effective mass.
The realization of superconducting states in magic-angle twisted bilayer
graphene
Cao et al. 2018
;
Lu et al. 2019
;
Yankowitz et al. 2019
;
Stepanov et al. 2020
;
Saito et al. 2020
;
Andrei and MacDonald 2020
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Hao et al. 2021
;
Zhou et al. 2021
;
Cao et al. 2021
;
Zhou et al. 2022
,
for which the bands are extremely flat so that
m^{*}\to\infty
,
has made clear the limitations of the conventional result.
In recent years, more general expressions for
D_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}
accounting for the
effect of quantum geometry in multi-band superconductors have
been obtained
Peotta and Törmä 2015
;
Liang et al. 2017
;
Wu and Das Sarma 2020
;
Bernevig et al. 2021
;
Huhtinen et al. 2022
;
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2022
;
Lau et al. 2022
;
Törmä et al. 2022
;
Törmä 2023
;

;

;

;

;

;
Jiang et al. 2025
;
Penttilä et al. 2025
;

.
These formulations show how, in flat-band systems like twisted-bilayer graphene,
the contribution to

arising from the quantum geometry can be
dominant

;
Julku et al. 2020
;

;
Verma et al. 2021
;

;

,
a result supported by recent experiments

;
Banerjee et al. 2025
;
Tanaka et al. 2025
.
Despite being more general, these expressions
are only valid for the case of spatially uniform superconducting states. The assumption of a homogeneous pairing field
\Delta

is equivalent to the “uniform pairing” condition in which
\Delta
is assumed to be the same for all the bands.
However, in many cases of interest,
\Delta
cannot be assumed to be uniform.
This is the case, for instance, when disorder is present

;
Paramekanti et al. 1998
;
Ghosal et al. 2001
;
Seibold et al. 2012
;
Lau et al. 2022
;
Datta et al. 2023
,
or in the presence of superconducting vortices.

In this work we obtain an expression for the superfluid weight tensor that properly takes into
account spatial inhomogeneities of the order parameter. The presence of spatial inhomogeneities
mixes the system’s response to the longitudinal and transverse components of an external vector field,
a fact that makes the calculation of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
more challenging.
The reason is that whereas
the BCS mean-field treatment, within the linear response approximation, returns
the correct response of a superconducting system to a transverse vector field
Schrieffer 1964
;
Scalapino et al. 1992
;
Scalapino et al. 1993
,
it is known that it returns an incorrect, gauge-dependent, response
to a longitudinal vector field
Schrieffer 1964
.
We find the gauge invariant expression for
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
in the presence of inhomogeneities
by properly including the corrections to
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
due to the response of
\Delta

to the external, perturbing, vector field.
To verify the accuracy of the derived expression we compare its predictions to the results obtained
by calculating, via a numerical self-consistent approach,
the free energy,
F
, as a function of the perturbing field
{\bf A}
and then

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
as the second derivative of
F
with respect to
{\bf A}
:

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=(1/V)d^{2}F/dA_{\mu}dA_{\nu}
,
where
V
is the system’s volume.
We do this for two important exemplary cases: the case when strong inhomogeneities in
\Delta

are induced by a periodic superlattice
potential applied to a 2D superconductor,
and the case when superconducting vortices are induced
by an external magnetic field perpendicular to a 2D superconductor.
In both cases we find that the results obtained by numerically calculating the second
derivative with respect to
{\bf A}
of the free energy are quantitatively indistinguishable
from the ones obtained using the derived expression, but can be significantly
different from the ones obtained using the expressions for
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
available in the
literature that do not take into account the presence of inhomogeneities.
For the case when vortices are present, the expression of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
that
does not include the response of
\Delta
to
{\bf A}
returns a qualitatively
wrong result – it erroneously predicts a finite value of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
even for
an infinite 2D array of vortices, in contrast to the expression that we present,
that correctly returns
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=0
for this situation.

We describe the superconducting state using a Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG)
effective mean field Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm BdG}
. Given that the goal of this work is to obtain
the correct response of inhomogeneous superconductors to an external
vector field, and not the identification of the many-body ground state,
the use of the BdG approach is very pragmatic: it allows the modeling of
any superconducting state taking as inputs from experiments the values
of the parameters entering the model.
For concreteness, we consider a superconductor with s-wave pairing
originating from an on-site attractive interaction of strength
U>0
.
For such a system

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm BdG}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{jl\sigma}t_{j}^{\ell}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}-\sum_{j\sigma}\frac{1}{2}(\mu-V_{j})c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j}\left[\Delta_{j}c^{\dagger}_{j,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j,\downarrow}-\frac{|\Delta_{j}|^{2}}{2U}\right]+\text{H.c.}

(1)

where the subscript
j
is shorthand for the position vector
\bm{r}_{j}
,
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
is the spin index,

t_{j}^{\ell}
is the hopping amplitude at position
\bm{r}_{j}
along the bond
\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}
,

c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}
(
c_{j,\sigma}
) is the creation (annihilation) operator
for an electron at position
\bm{r}_{j}
with spin
\sigma
,

\mu
is the chemical potential,
V_{j}
is an applied potential,
and
\Delta_{j}
is the pairing potential at position
\bm{r}_{j}
obtained
from the self-consistent equation
\Delta_{j}=U\langle c_{j,\downarrow}c_{j,\uparrow}\rangle
,
where the angle brackets denote equilibrium expectation values at temperature
T
.
In general, the lattice can have a basis and so
\bm{r}_{j}={\bf R}_{i}+{\bf b}_{m}
,
where
\{{\bf R}_{i}\}
are the position vectors that identify the lattice and
{\bf b}_{m}
are the vectors
for the positions of the basis elements within the primitive cell.

{\mathbf{\beta}}^{\ell}_{j}
are bond vectors that connect sites within, and between, primitive cells, making the
expression of
\hat{H}_{\rm BdG}
, Eq. (
1
), very general.
In the remainder, the primitive cell is chosen so that
\Delta({\bf r}_{j})=\Delta({\bf r}_{j}+{\bf R}_{i})
,
and therefore, when
\Delta
is inhomogeneous, can be much larger than the crystal’s primitive cell.

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
relates the strength of the charge current
\hat{\bm{J}}
to a static, transverse, vector field
{\bf A}
with zero parallel momentum
{\bf q}_{\parallel}
,
and perpendicular momentum
{\bf q}_{\perp}\to 0

Scalapino et al. 1992
:

\langle\hat{J}_{\mu}\rangle=D^{(s)}_{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}({\bf q}_{\parallel}=0,{\bf q}_{\perp}\to 0,\omega=0)
,
where
\langle\hat{J}_{\mu}\rangle
is the expectation value of the
\mu
component of the current,
and
\omega
the frequency of the field
{\bf A}
.

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
can therefore be obtained by calculating the linear response of
\hat{\bm{J}}

to a transverse vector field
{\bf A}
.
For a tight-binding model, the presence of the field
{\bf A}
can effectively be
taken into account by introducing a Peierls phase for
the hopping parameters:
t_{j}^{\ell}\to t_{j}^{\ell}e^{i{\bf A}\cdot{\bm{\beta}}^{\ell}_{j}}
.
In addition, it can induce a change in the pairing field that, as we will show, cannot be neglected for inhomogeneous superconductors.
Taking this into account, starting from the expression of
\hat{H}_{BdG}
, Eq. (
1
),
for the components,
\hat{J}_{\mu}=-\delta\hat{H}_{BdG}/\delta A_{\mu}
, of the charge current operator, we find:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})=\sum_{\ell,\sigma}\left(i(\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell})_{\mu}~t_{j}^{\ell}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{r}_{j},t)\cdot\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}+H.c.\right)+

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07164pt\sum_{j^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}c^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime},\downarrow}-\frac{1}{2U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}+H.c.\right]

(2)

To first order in
{\bf A}
we have

\hat{J}_{\mu}=\hat{J}^{Kp}_{\mu}+\hat{T}^{K}_{\mu\nu}A^{\nu}+\hat{J}^{\Delta p}_{\mu}+\hat{T}^{\Delta}_{\mu\nu}A^{\nu}

(3)

where
\hat{J}^{Kp}_{\mu}
,
\hat{T}^{K}_{\mu\nu}A^{\nu}
are the paramagnetic and diamagnetic currents, respectively, arising from the kinetic energy part of the BdG Hamiltonian,
and
\hat{J}^{\Delta p}_{\mu}
,
\hat{T}^{\Delta}_{\mu\nu}A^{\nu}
the paramagnetic and diamagnetic currents due to the change of
\Delta_{j}
induced by
{\bf A}
.

\hat{T}^{\Delta}_{\mu\nu}A^{\nu}
does not contribute to
\langle\hat{J}_{\mu}\rangle

and to
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
(see SM) and so we can neglect it.

\hat{J}^{\Delta p}_{\mu}
is given by the second line of Eq. (
2
) by
evaluating the variational derivatives at
{\bf A}=0
.

\hat{J}^{\Delta p}_{\mu}
also does not contribute to
\langle\hat{J}_{\mu}\rangle
,
but it does contribute to
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
. As we show below, its contribution to
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
is critical when
\Delta
is not homogeneous.

To obtain the current response to a vector field with vanishing momentum

,
it is convenient to express the current in momentum space.
By performing the Fourier transform with respect to
{\bf R}_{i}
we can write

c_{{\bf R}_{i}+{\bf b}_{m},\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}c_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})e^{i\bm{k}\cdot{\bf b}_{m}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot{\bf R}_{i}}.

(4)

where
c_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})
(
c^{\dagger}_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})
)
is the creation (annihilation) operator for an electron in the state
|\bm{k}m\sigma\rangle

with momentum
\bm{k}
, orbital
m
, and spin
\sigma
.
The operator
c(\bm{k})_{m,\sigma}
is defined apart from an overall phase factor.
In writing Eq. (
4
) we have chosen this overall phase factor to be

,
given that this choice allows us
to write the full paramagnetic current operator, in momentum space, in the limit

where
N_{c}
is the number of unit cells,

\psi_{m}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})=(c^{\dagger}_{m\uparrow}(\bm{k}),c^{\dagger}_{m\downarrow}(\bm{k}))
,

I_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial H_{mm^{\prime}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\big|_{\bm{k}}&&\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}}\big|_{0}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}}\big|_{0}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}&&-\frac{\partial H_{mm^{\prime}}^{*}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\big|_{-\bm{k}}\end{pmatrix}

For the operator
\hat{T}^{K}_{\mu\nu}A^{\nu}

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}^{K}(\bm{q})\!=\!

\displaystyle-\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{m,\ell,\sigma,\bm{k}}\!\!\!\frac{(\bm{\beta}_{m}^{\ell})_{\mu}(\bm{\beta}_{m}^{\ell})_{\nu}}{N_{c}}

\displaystyle\times\left[t_{m}^{\ell}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell},\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right.

\displaystyle+\left.t_{m}^{\ell*}e^{i(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell},\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right].

The superfluid weight is given by the sum of the paramagnetic current response
\Pi_{\mu\nu}({\bf q}\to 0,\omega=0)

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=\Pi_{\mu\nu}({\bf q}\to 0,\omega=0)+\langle\hat{T}^{K}_{\mu\nu}\rangle.

We have

\Pi_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},0)=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\langle[\hat{J}^{K,p}_{\mu}(\bm{q},t),\hat{J}^{p}_{\nu}(\bm{-q},0)]\rangle

In the limit

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}({\bf q}\to 0,0)=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k},ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt\langle\phi_{a}(\bm{k})|I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k})|\phi_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|I_{\nu}(\bm{k})|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle

is the Fermi-Dirac function, and

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
.
The results show that, indeed, for
V_{0}=0

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
, shown by the blue circles,
coincides with
D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
, shown by the red circles, and with the
value obtained by calculating
d^{2}F/dA_{\mu}dA_{\nu}
, yellow crosses.
However, for
V_{0}\neq 0

\Delta_{j}
is inhomogeneous and so the correction
\delta D^{s}_{\mu\nu}

is non negligible making
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}\neq D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
, as shown in Fig.
1
(a).
We see that for
V_{0}\neq 0
only the value of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
obtained by taking into account the corrections due to

\delta\Delta_{m}/{\delta A_{\mu}}\big|_{0}=0

agrees with the value of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
obtained by calculating

d^{2}F/dA_{\mu}dA_{\nu}
.

The corrections to
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
due to
\delta\Delta_{m}/{\delta A_{\mu}}\big|_{0}
, i.e.,
\hat{J}^{\Delta p}_{\mu}
, become qualitatively very important
when vortices are present.
A lattice of unpinned vortices was found to have vanishing superfluid stiffness
Teitel and Jayaprakash 1983
;
Franz and Teitel 1995
;
Teitel 2013
.
This can be understood considering that for a vortex lattice in the
(x,y)
plane induced by a background magnetic field
B_{z}

in the direction perpendicular to the plane,
for a spatially, perturbing, constant, in-plane, vector potential
{\bf A}
, say along the
x
direction,
the vortices respond by shifting their position along the
y
direction, as shown in the inset of Fig.
1
(b), by an amount

\Delta y=\frac{\hbar}{e}\frac{|{\bf A}|}{B_{z}}
.
Because this translation has no free energy, given that it corresponds to the
{\bf q}\to 0

Goldstone mode associated to the translational symmetry spontaneously broken by the vortex lattice we have
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=0
.

To study the superfluid weight in the presence of vortices, and a pinning periodic potential with period
M
of the form
given by Eq. (
17
), we consider the case of a 2D superconductor on a square lattice in the presence of a perpendicular
background magnetic field
B_{z}
(see SM for details).
In Fig.
1
(b) we show the results for
D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
,

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
obtained taking into account the correction
\delta D^{s}_{\mu\nu}
,
and
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
obtained by taking the second derivative of
F
with respect to
{\bf A}
.
The results show that for
V_{0}=0
, even though an unpinned vortex lattice is present,

D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
is finite and quite large (red circles in the figure), of the same order as for the case
of a superconducting state with no vortices (see Fig.
1
(a)).
This contrasts with the expectation that superfluid weight should vanish.
The inclusion of the correction
\delta D^{(s)}_{\mu\nu}
leads to

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=0
(blue circles), the correct value in the presence of an unpinned vortex lattice,
the same value that we find by calculating
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
as the second derivative of
F
with respect to
{\bf A}
(yellow crosses).
This is one of the key results of the present work: it shows that in the presence
of vortices the correction to
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
due to the response of
\Delta_{j}
to
{\bf A}

is essential to obtain the qualitatively correct value of the superfluid weight.
As
V_{0}
increases, and the vortices start getting pinned,
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
also increases
from zero and starts getting closer to the value of
D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
.
Notice, however, that even for
V_{0}=0.5t
, the value of
D^{(s0)}_{\mu\nu}
is still
about 60% larger than the value given by the full expression of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
, value that coincides
with the one obtained by calculating the second derivative of
F
with respect to
{\bf A}
.

In summary, we have obtained the expression within linear response theory for the superfluid weight
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
that includes
corrections due to the response of the superconducting order parameter to the external
vector potential
{\bf A}
. We find that these corrections play a significant role
for superconducting states for which the pairing is inhomogeneous.
We show that for such states the expression for
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
that does not include such corrections
returns values that are different from the values of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
obtained by directly calculating
the second derivative of the free energy with respect to
{\bf A}
.
For the case of a superconducting vortex lattice with no pinning potential,
we find that only the expression of
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
that includes the corrections returns
the expected result of zero superfluid weight, showing the importance of such corrections
for this case. For two-dimensional systems, the results presented imply that
the response of the superconducting order parameter to the external
vector potential
{\bf A}
should be included when calculating
the critical temperature
T_{BKT}
for the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless phase transition
of an inhomogeneous superconductor, given the connection between
D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}
and
T_{BKT}
.

II
Hamiltonian

Let us suppose our system is defined on a lattice with sites
\{\bm{r}_{j}\}
, where the site/orbital labels are
j
, and with bonds described by a set of vectors
\{\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{j}\}
that start at site
j
, are labelled at each
j
by the label
\ell
, and where
\text{Arg}(\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell})\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2]
in 2D. The latter condition is so that we consider “forward hopping” only. We take care of backward hopping, at the same time as ensuring Hermiticity, by adding the Hermitian conjugate of the forward hopping terms to the Hamiltonian. If two orbitals reside in the same location, one can unambiguously define
\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}
via point-splitting. We include an on-site superconducting pairing, originating from an on-site attractive interaction of magnitude
U>0
. The mean field Hamiltonian is

\hat{H}_{\rm MF}=-\sum_{j,\ell,\sigma}\left(t_{j}^{\ell}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}+\text{H.c.}\right)-\sum_{j,\sigma}\left(\mu-V_{j}\right)c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}-\sum_{j}\Big(\Delta_{j}c^{\dagger}_{j,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j,\downarrow}+\Delta_{j}^{*}c_{j,\downarrow}c_{j,\uparrow}-\frac{|\Delta_{j}|^{2}}{U}\Big)

(18)

where the subscript
j
is shorthand for the position vector
\bm{r}_{j}
, the complex hopping amplitude along bond
\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}
is
t_{j}^{\ell}
, the chemical potential is
\mu
,
V_{j}
is an applied potential, and, at zero temperature,

\Delta_{j}=U\langle c_{j,\downarrow}c_{j,\uparrow}\rangle

with the angle bracket denoting equlibrium expectation values at temperature
T
.
These are the self-consistency equations.

Suppose that the system possesses a translational invariance by vectors
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}
(specializing for 2D here). We may then define unit cells whose locations we specify by
\bm{R}_{i}=m\bm{a}_{1}+n\bm{a}_{2}
with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The position of any orbital can then be given by

\bm{r}_{j}=\bm{R}_{i}+{\bf b}_{m}

(19)

where
{\bf b}_{m}

specifies the location of the orbital
within the unit cell.
The bonds and the hopping amplitudes then possess this translational invariance:

\displaystyle\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{j+\bm{R}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{j}

(20)

\displaystyle t^{\ell}_{j+\bm{R}}

\displaystyle=t^{\ell}_{j}

(21)

where the subscript
j+\bm{R}
is shorthand for
\bm{r}_{j}+\bm{R}_{i}
. We will also suppose that the solution to the self-consistency equations possesses this same periodicity:

\Delta_{j+\bm{R}}=\Delta_{j}

(22)

We thus perform the Fourier transformation

c_{j\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}c_{m,\sigma}(\bm{k})e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}

(23)

where
m
is shorthand for
\bm{\tilde{r}}_{j}
.
Using the basis of Nambu spinors

\hat{\bm{\Psi}}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1,\uparrow}(\bm{k})\\
\vdots\\
c_{N,\uparrow}(\bm{k})\\
c^{\dagger}_{1,\downarrow}(-\bm{k})\\
\vdots\\
c^{\dagger}_{N,\downarrow}(-\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}

(24)

with
N
the number of sites/orbitals per unit cell,
the Hamiltonian (
18
) can then be cast in Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) form:

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm MF}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\hat{\bm{\Psi}}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})H_{\rm BdG}(\bm{k})\hat{\bm{\Psi}}(\bm{k})+\sum_{\bm{k}}\Tr[H(\bm{k})]+\frac{|\Delta_{j}|^{2}}{U}

(25)

where

H_{\rm BdG}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}H(\bm{k})&&-\bm{\Delta}\\
-\bm{\Delta}^{*}&&-H^{*}(-\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}

(26)

is the BdG Hamiltonian,
H(\bm{k})
is the normal state Hamiltonian, and

\bm{\Delta}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\Delta_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\Delta_{3}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\Delta_{N}\end{pmatrix}

(27)

III
Current Operator

In this Supplementary Materials,
\bm{A}
will refer to a probe electromagnetic potential. The orbital effect of any background magnetic field is accommodated in the model by the complex hopping amplitudes
t_{j}^{\ell}
. To determine the current, we must specify how the system couples to an external vector potential
\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)
. We suppose the hopping part of the Hamiltonian couples via a Peierls substitution

t_{j}^{\ell}\rightarrow\exp\left(i\phi_{j}^{\ell}\right)t_{j}^{\ell}

(28)

where

\phi_{j}^{\ell}=\int_{\bm{r}_{j}}^{\bm{r}_{j}+\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}}\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)\cdot d\bm{r}

(29)

Notice that this change in the hopping amplitudes leads to a change in the groundstate
|\rm G\rangle
which in general leads to a change in
\Delta_{j}
.
We will compute this change in later sections. We choose the path of integration to be the straight line connecting
\bm{r}_{j}
to
\bm{r}_{j}+\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}
given by the vector
\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}
.

The system only couples to the average value of
\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)
along the bonds. This is because

\int_{\bm{r}_{j}}^{\bm{r}_{j}+\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}}\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)\cdot d\bm{r}=\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}

(30)

where
\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}(t)
denotes the average value of
\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)
along bond
\beta_{j}^{\ell}
. The current along bond
\beta_{j}^{\ell}
(the current in lattice models is defined on the bonds) is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm MF}}{\delta\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}}=\sum_{\sigma}\left(i\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}~t_{j}^{\ell}e^{i\phi_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}+\text{H.c.}\right)+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{\delta\Delta_{i}}{\delta\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{i,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{i,\downarrow}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{i}^{*}}{\delta\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}}c_{i,\downarrow}c_{i,\uparrow}-\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{i}|^{2}}{\delta\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}}\right)

(31)

\displaystyle=\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}\sum_{\sigma}\left(it_{j}^{\ell}e^{i\phi_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}+\text{H.c.}\right)+\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}\sum_{i}\left(\frac{\delta\Delta_{i}}{\delta\phi_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{i,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{i,\downarrow}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{i}^{*}}{\delta\phi_{j}^{\ell}}c_{i,\downarrow}c_{i,\uparrow}-\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{i}|^{2}}{\delta\phi_{j}^{\ell}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell K}+\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell\Delta}

(33)

The first sum in (
32
) is denoted
\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell K}
and is the contribution of the current coming from the kinetic energy. The second sum, which is the contribution of the current from the pairing potential, is denoted
\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell\Delta}
. The distinction between these two contributions, which we may call
kinetic current
and
pairing current
, respectively, will play an important role in this SM. We see explicitly that the current is directed along the bonds. We have also taken the chemical potential to be fixed.

We make a few additional comments on (
32
):

1.

In general,
{\delta\Delta_{j}}/{\delta\phi_{j^{\prime}}^{\ell}}\neq 0
for
j\neq j^{\prime}
. For example, when varying
\phi_{j}^{\ell}
, we should at the very least expect the pairing potential
\Delta_{i}
to respond at the starting and ending sites of the bond
\beta_{j}^{\ell}
.

2.

The self-consistency equations imply that the average pairing current vanishes
\left\langle\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell\Delta}\right\rangle\equiv 0
. Thus the average current is given by the average kinetic current

\left\langle\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell}\right\rangle=\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}\sum_{\sigma}\left(it_{j}^{\ell}e^{i\phi_{j}^{\ell}}\Big\langle c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}\Big\rangle+\text{c.c.}\right)=\left\langle\hat{\bm{J}}_{j}^{\ell K}\right\rangle

(34)

However, the pairing current does contribute to current correlations in the system, as we shall see.

If we are only interested in the responses of the system to vector potentials which vary slowly in space compared to the length of the bonds, we may replace
\bm{A}_{j}^{\ell}\rightarrow\bm{A}(\bm{r}_{j},t)
, up to negligible error of
\mathcal{O}\big(|\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}|/\lambda\big)
, where
\lambda
is the characteristic length scale of variations in
\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)
. We can then meaningfully define the current at the lattice sites by

\begin{split}\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})=-\frac{\delta\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{MF}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}=&\sum_{\ell,\sigma}\left(i(\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell})_{\mu}~t_{j}^{\ell}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{r}_{j},t)\cdot\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}+\text{H.c.}\right)\\
&+\sum_{j^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}c^{\dagger}_{i,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{i,\downarrow}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}c_{i,\downarrow}c_{i,\uparrow}-\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}\right)\\
&\equiv\hat{J}_{\mu}^{K}(\bm{r}_{j})+\hat{J}_{\mu}^{\Delta}(\bm{r}_{j})\end{split}

(35)

If the system is perturbed by a vector potential of the form
A_{\mu}(\bm{r},t)=A_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}
then the corresponding coupling is

\sum_{j,\mu}\frac{\delta\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{MF}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j},t)}A_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}=-\sum_{\mu}A_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)\sum_{j}\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j},t)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}=-\sum_{\mu}A_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)

(36)

where
\mu
is a spatial index (
\mu=x,y,\ldots
). Thus

\begin{split}\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{q})=&\sum_{j,\ell,\sigma}\left(i(\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell})_{\mu}~t_{j}^{\ell}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{r}_{j},t)\cdot\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell}}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}+\text{H.c.}\right)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}\\
&+\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}c^{\dagger}_{i,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{i,\downarrow}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}c_{i,\downarrow}c_{i,\uparrow}-\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}\right)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}\\
&\equiv\hat{J}^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{q})+\hat{J}^{\Delta}_{\mu}(\bm{q})\end{split}

(37)

III.1
Paramagnetic Current

It is useful to decompose the current into paramagnetic and diamagnetic components

\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{q})=\hat{J}^{p}_{\mu}(\bm{q})+\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}(\bm{q})A_{\nu}(\bm{q})

(38)

where there is an implied sum over
\nu
. Both
\hat{J}^{p}_{\mu}(\bm{q})
and
\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}(\bm{q})
admit further decomposition into kinetic and pairing contributions, as in the previous section. The kinetic component of the paramagnetic current is

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{K,p}_{\mu}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,\ell,\sigma}\left(i(\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell})_{\mu}~t_{j}^{\ell}c^{\dagger}_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}-i(\bm{\beta}_{j}^{\ell})_{\mu}~t_{j}^{\ell*}c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j+\beta_{j}^{\ell},\sigma}\right)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}

(39)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{{N_{c}}}\sum_{m,\ell,\sigma}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\bm{\beta}_{m}^{\ell})_{\mu}\left[t_{m}^{\ell}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell}\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-t_{m}^{\ell*}e^{i(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell}\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right]

(40)

where we have used (
23
).

The pairing contribution to the paramagnetic current is

\begin{split}\hat{J}^{\Delta,p}_{\mu}(\bm{q})&=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\left[\left(\sum_{j}\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}\Bigg|_{A=0}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}\right)c^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime},\downarrow}+\left(\sum_{j}\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}\Bigg|_{A=0}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}\right)c_{j^{\prime},\downarrow}c_{j^{\prime},\uparrow}-\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}\Bigg|_{A=0}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}\right)\right]\\
&=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\Bigg|_{A=0}c^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime},\downarrow}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\Bigg|_{A=0}c_{j^{\prime},\downarrow}c_{j^{\prime},\uparrow}-\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\Bigg|_{A=0}\right]\end{split}

(41)

where the derivatives with respect to
\bm{A}
are evaluated at
\bm{A}=0
. Hereafter, all derivatives with respect to
\bm{A}
will be evaluated at
\bm{A}=0
; thus, we omit the evaluation symbol on the derivatives from now on. We have used that the variation
\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}
with respect to a vector potential of the form
\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r},t)=\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}
is

\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q},t)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}

(42)

so that

\sum_{j}\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{r}_{j})}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}=\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}

(43)

Recall that we have assumed that
\Delta_{j}
at
\bm{A}=0
is periodic with the same periodicity as the hopping amplitudes
t^{\ell}_{j}
, i.e. we can still define a unit cell by the vectors
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}
(in 2D) in the presence of pairing.
More generally we take
\Delta_{i}
to be periodic, and take its period to be commensurate with that of the hopping amplitudes. This situation requires a modification of
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}
, but otherwise no generality is lost.
Thus
\Delta_{j}
is only a function of the intra-unit cell label. In other words,

\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{m}

(44)

From this, it follows that
\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}
can be written as a periodic function times a plane wave:

\frac{\delta\Delta_{j^{\prime}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}=\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot{\bf R}_{i}}

(45)

Thus the pairing contribution to the current, after using (
23
), is

\hat{J}^{\Delta,p}_{\mu}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{m,\bm{k}}\left[\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}c^{\dagger}_{m,\uparrow}(\bm{k})c^{\dagger}_{m,\downarrow}(-\bm{k}-\bm{q})+\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}c_{m,\downarrow}(-\bm{k})c_{m,\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right]-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{j}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}

(46)

The paramagnetic current is then

\hat{J}_{\mu}^{\text{p}}(\bm{q})=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{m,\ell,\sigma}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\bm{\beta}_{m}^{\ell})_{\mu}\left[t_{m}^{\ell}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell}\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-t_{m}^{\ell*}e^{i(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell}\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right]\\
+\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{m,\bm{k}}\left[\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}c^{\dagger}_{m,\uparrow}(\bm{k})c^{\dagger}_{m,\downarrow}(-\bm{k}-\bm{q})+\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}^{*}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}c_{m,\downarrow}(-\bm{k})c_{m,\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right]-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{U}\frac{\delta|\Delta_{i}|^{2}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}

(47)

It is convenient to write the kinetic part of the current operator in the following way (what follows is merely a matter of convenient notation)

\begin{split}\hat{J}^{K,p}_{\mu}(\bm{q})=&\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k},\sigma}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})c^{\dagger}_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m^{\prime}\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\\
=&\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\left[J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})c^{\dagger}_{m\uparrow}(\bm{k})c_{m^{\prime}\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-J_{m^{\prime}m;\mu}(-\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q})c_{m\downarrow}(-\bm{k})c^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}\downarrow}(-\bm{k}-\bm{q})\right]+\text{c-numbers}\end{split}

(48)

where
m
and
m^{\prime}
are site/orbital labels. The matrix elements
J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})
can be read off from (
47
). Now, Hermiticity of the current operator implies
J_{m^{\prime}m;\mu}(-\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q})=J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(-\bm{k},-\bm{q})^{*}
. Thus the total paramagnetic current can be written in the basis

\psi_{m}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}c_{m\uparrow}(\bm{k})\\
c^{\dagger}_{m\downarrow}(-\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}

as

\begin{split}\hat{J}_{\mu}^{p}(\bm{q})&=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{m}(\bm{k})\begin{pmatrix}J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})&&\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\\
\frac{\delta\Delta^{*}_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}&&-J^{*}_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(-\bm{k},-\bm{q})\end{pmatrix}\psi_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})+\text{c-numbers}\\
&\equiv\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\psi_{m}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})I_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\psi_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})+\text{c-numbers}\end{split}

(49)

where

I_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})=\begin{pmatrix}J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})&&\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\\
\frac{\delta\Delta^{*}_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q})}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}&&-J^{*}_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(-\bm{k},-\bm{q})\end{pmatrix}

(50)

It is convenient to do the same for the kinetic current only:

\hat{J}_{\mu}^{K,p}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\psi_{m}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})I^{K}_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})+\text{c-numbers}

(51)

where

I^{K}_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})=\begin{pmatrix}J_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})&&0\\
0&&-J^{*}_{mm^{\prime};\mu}(-\bm{k},-\bm{q})\end{pmatrix}

(52)

III.2
Diamagnetic Current

The diamagnetic current should also be computed in order to calculate the linear response to an electromagnetic field. The diamagnetic current is obtained by carrying out the expansion in
\bm{A}
to linear order in (
37
) and going over to momentum space. The result for the prefactor of
A_{\nu}(\bm{q})
coming from the kinetic current in such an expansion is

\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}^{K}(\bm{q})=-\frac{1}{{N_{c}}}\sum_{m,\ell,\sigma}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\bm{\beta}_{m}^{\ell})_{\mu}(\bm{\beta}_{m}^{\ell})_{\nu}\left[t_{m}^{\ell}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell}\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})+t_{m}^{\ell*}e^{i(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\cdot\bm{\beta}^{\ell}_{m}}c^{\dagger}_{m\sigma}(\bm{k})c_{m+\beta_{m}^{\ell}\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right]

(53)

There is also a pairing contribution
\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}^{\Delta}(\bm{q})
. However, since
\langle\hat{J}^{\Delta}_{\mu}(\bm{q})\rangle\equiv 0

IV
Superfluid Stiffness

The superfluid stiffness may be computed by calculating the response of the current
\langle\hat{J}_{\mu}\rangle\equiv\langle\hat{J}^{K}_{\mu}\rangle
to a static vector potential (
\omega=0
) in the long wavelength limit (
\bm{q}\rightarrow 0
)
Scalapino et al. 1992
;
Scalapino et al. 1993

\delta\langle\hat{J}_{\mu}(\bm{q}\rightarrow 0,\omega=0)\rangle\equiv\delta\langle\hat{J}^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{q}\rightarrow 0,\omega=0)\rangle=D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}(\bm{q}=0,\omega=0)

(54)

where

D^{\rm(s)}_{\mu\nu}=\left\langle\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}^{K}(\bm{q}=0,\omega=0)\right\rangle+\lim_{\bm{q}\rightarrow 0}\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\bm{q},\omega=0)

(55)

We will see that the pairing potential modifies the paramagnetic current-current correlation function

IV.1
Paramagnetic Current-Current Correlation Function

We will compute the paramagnetic current-current correlation function while taking into account the dependence of the pairing potential on

We compute the following response function in imaginary time

\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\bm{q},\tau)=-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}\hat{J}^{K,p}_{\mu}(\bm{q},\tau)\hat{J}^{p}_{\nu}(\bm{-q},0)\right\rangle

The time-ordering symbol in imaginary time is

.
Note that the first factor in the expectation value above is the kinetic current operator

,
whereas the second factor is the full current

The response function may be represented in the Matsubara frequency domain, using (

(57)

The centered dots indicate matrix multiplication in particle/hole space and
\mathcal{G}
can be expressed in the basis

\psi_{m}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}c_{m\uparrow}(\bm{k})\\
c^{\dagger}_{m\downarrow}(-\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}

(58)

\mathcal{G}_{mm^{\prime}}(\bm{k},ik_{n^{\prime}})=-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}\psi_{m}(\bm{k},\tau)\psi^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{k},0)\right\rangle e^{ik_{n^{\prime}}\tau}

which is a
2\times 2
matrix-valued Green’s function;
\alpha
and
\beta

is a bosonic frequency and

in terms of eigenstates

v^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}

and energies

\mathcal{G}_{mm^{\prime}}(\bm{k},ik_{m})=\sum_{a}\frac{|\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{a}^{m^{\prime}}(\bm{k})|}{ik_{n^{\prime}}-E_{a}(\bm{k})}

(61)

Inserting this into (

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\bm{q},i\omega_{n}=0)=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})}\langle\phi_{a}(\bm{k})|I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q})|\phi_{b}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\rangle

(62)

\displaystyle\times\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})|I_{\nu}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},-\bm{q})|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle

Where we have introduced the shorthand notation

\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|M|\phi_{a}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\rangle=\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\langle\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})|M_{mm^{\prime}}|\phi^{m^{\prime}}_{a}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\rangle

We then take the limit
\bm{q}\rightarrow 0
, where
I_{m\beta;\mu}(\bm{k},\bm{q}\rightarrow 0)

\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}^{*}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}&&-\frac{\partial H_{mm^{\prime}}^{*}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\big|_{-\bm{k}}\end{pmatrix}

(63)

where

is the normal state Hamiltonian. We have called

. Note that
\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}\equiv\left.\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}}\right|_{0}
from the main text. We have

\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\bm{q}\rightarrow 0,i\omega_{n}=0)=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi_{a}(\bm{k})|I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k},0)|\phi_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|I_{\nu}(\bm{k},0)|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle

Note that the limit

IV.2
Calculating the Response of
\Delta

In order to complete the calculation, we need to compute
\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}
.
Treating

as a parameter and computing
\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}
by solving for
\Delta_{m}
self-consistently as a function of

is misguided because of the phase ambiguity at different values of
Q_{\mu}
.

The operator
\hat{\Delta}_{m}
can be written in Nambu form by writing

\hat{\Delta}_{m}(\bm{q})=U\sum_{\bm{k}}c_{m,\downarrow}(-\bm{k})c_{m,\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})=U\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{l}(\bm{k})\cdot\mathcal{M}^{m}_{ll^{\prime}}\cdot\psi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})

(65)

where
\mathcal{M}^{m}_{ll^{\prime}}=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{l,m}\tau_{-}
and
\tau_{-}=\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{x}-i\tau_{y})
where
\tau_{x}
and
\tau_{y}
are the Pauli
x
and
y
matrices in particle/hole space.
The centered dots indicate matrix multiplication in particle/hole space. We compute the response function

\begin{split}\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q},\tau)}&=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}\hat{\Delta}_{m}(\bm{q},\tau)J^{p}_{\mu}(\bm{-q},0)\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{pll^{\prime}p^{\prime}}\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}\psi^{\dagger}_{p}(\bm{k},\tau)\cdot\mathcal{M}^{m}_{pl}\cdot\psi_{l}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},\tau)\psi_{l^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\bm{k}^{\prime},0)\cdot I_{l^{\prime}p^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k}^{\prime},-\bm{q})\cdot\psi_{p^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{q},0)\right\rangle\end{split}

(66)

We have assumed that
\left\langle\Delta_{m}(\bm{q})\right\rangle=0
for
\bm{q}\neq 0
and
\left\langle J^{p}_{\mu}(\bm{q}=0)\right\rangle=0
(i.e. the total current vanishes in the groundstate).
The above may be expressed in Matsubara frequency space as

\begin{split}\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}(\bm{q},i\omega_{n})}&=-\frac{U}{\beta N_{c}}\sum_{pp^{\prime}ll^{\prime}}\sum_{\bm{k},ik_{n^{\prime}}}\Tr\bigg[\mathcal{G}_{p^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(\bm{k},ik_{n^{\prime}})\cdot\mathcal{M}^{m}_{l^{\prime}l}\cdot\mathcal{G}_{lp}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},ik_{n^{\prime}}+i\omega_{n})\cdot I_{pp^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},-\bm{q})\bigg]\\
&=-\frac{U}{\beta N_{c}}\sum_{pp^{\prime}}\sum_{\bm{k},ik_{n^{\prime}}}\Tr\bigg[\mathcal{G}_{p^{\prime}m}(\bm{k},ik_{n^{\prime}})\cdot\tau_{-}\cdot\mathcal{G}_{mp}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},ik_{n^{\prime}}+i\omega_{n})\cdot I_{pp^{\prime};\mu}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},-\bm{q})\bigg]\end{split}

(67)

We follow the steps leading to (
64

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi_{a}(\bm{k})|\mathcal{M}^{m}|\phi_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|I_{\mu}(\bm{k},0)|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle

(68)

\displaystyle=-\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|I_{\mu}(\bm{k},0)|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle

(69)

This is an equation in
\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}
since this quantity also appears in the right hand side as

I_{\mu}(\bm{k},0)\equiv I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k},0)+\sum_{m}\left(\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}\mathcal{M}^{mT}+\frac{\delta\Delta^{*}_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}\mathcal{M}^{m}\right)

(70)

where

I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k},0)\equiv I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial H}{\partial k_{\mu}}\big|_{\bm{k}}&&0\\
0&&-\frac{\partial H^{*}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\big|_{-\bm{k}}\end{pmatrix}

(71)

Plugging this in to the right hand side gives

\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}=-\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k})|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\\

(72)

where
\tau_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{x}+i\tau_{y})
. This may be written in the following form

(C_{\mu})_{m}=\sum_{m^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{mm^{\prime}}\frac{\delta\Delta_{m^{\prime}}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}+\mathcal{B}_{mm^{\prime}}\frac{\delta\Delta^{*}_{m^{\prime}}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}\right)

(73)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{mm^{\prime}}&=-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi^{m^{\prime}}_{b}(\bm{k})|\tau_{+}|\phi^{m^{\prime}}_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle-\frac{1}{U}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\\
\mathcal{B}_{mm^{\prime}}&=-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi^{m^{\prime}}_{b}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m^{\prime}}_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\\
(C_{\mu})_{m}&=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k})|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\end{split}

(74)

The vector
C_{\mu}
corresponds to the first term on the right hand side of (
72

\begin{split}C_{\mu}&\rightarrow C_{\mu}e^{i\theta}\\
\mathcal{A}&\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\\
\mathcal{B}&\rightarrow\mathcal{B}e^{i2\theta}\end{split}

(75)

which implies that

\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}\rightarrow\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\mu}}e^{i\theta}

(76)

The equation (
73

\Delta_{j}

\Delta_{j}\rightarrow e^{i\alpha}\Delta_{j}\approx(1+i\alpha)\Delta_{j}

(77)

where
\alpha\ll 1
. We may think of this as perturbing the Hamiltonian (

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}=-i\alpha\sum_{j}\Big(\Delta_{j}c^{\dagger}_{j,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j,\downarrow}-\Delta_{j}^{*}c_{j,\downarrow}c_{j,\uparrow}\Big)

(78)

We then compute the response of the pairing potential
\Delta_{i}
to this static perturbation. On the one hand, the answer is clear, it is

\delta\Delta_{m}=i\alpha\Delta_{m}

(79)

On the other hand, the response
\delta\Delta_{m}
follows from the static linear response formula, which may be written as

\begin{split}\delta\Delta_{m}=&\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi_{a}(\bm{k})|\mathcal{M}^{m}|\phi_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|\left(-i\alpha\mathcal{M}^{mT}\Delta_{m}+i\alpha\mathcal{M}^{m}\Delta^{*}_{m}\right)|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\\

Combining (
79

80

\sum_{m^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{mm^{\prime}}\Delta_{m^{\prime}}-\mathcal{B}_{mm^{\prime}}\Delta^{*}_{m^{\prime}}\right)=0

Thus, for any solution

(82)

since according to (

(86)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta Q_{\nu}}\langle\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})|\tau_{+}|\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle+\frac{\delta\Delta^{*}_{m}}{\delta Q_{\nu}}\langle\phi^{m}_{b}(\bm{k})|\tau_{-}|\phi^{m}_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\right)

where we have used the expression for

IV.3.1

The formula (
87

), meaning that it automatically projects out the response of the Goldstone mode. This is because

\begin{split}&2~\text{Re}\left[\sum_{m}(C_{\mu})_{m}(-i\Delta_{m}^{*})\right]\\

&+\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{ab}\frac{n_{F}\left(E_{a}(\bm{k})\right)-n_{F}\left(E_{b}(\bm{k})\right)}{E_{a}(\bm{k})-E_{b}(\bm{k})}\langle\phi_{a}(\bm{k})|I^{K}_{\mu}(\bm{k})|\phi_{b}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\phi_{b}(\bm{k})|(i\Delta_{m})\mathcal{M}^{mT}|\phi_{a}(\bm{k})\rangle\\

since the current is gauge invariant, and thus its linear response to a small rotation in the angle of

V
Inversion of Equation (
12
)

As stated in the main text the square matrix
K
that appears in Eq. (
12
) is singular, it has rank one less than its dimension,
n
,
reflecting the fact that the superconducting order parameter is defined
apart from an overall phase factor.
To obtain
\delta\Delta_{m}/{\delta A_{\mu}}\big|_{0}
we need to calculate the pseudoinverse,
\tilde{K}
, of
K
.
To do this we first perform a single-value-decomposition (SVD) of
K
:

K=U\Lambda V^{\dagger}

(93)

where
U
and
V
are
n\times n
unitary matrices and
\Lambda
is a
n\times n
diagonal matrix of the form

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&0&\lambda_{3}&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\lambda_{n}=0\end{pmatrix}.

(94)

Let

\tilde{\Lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}^{-1}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}^{-1}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&0&\lambda_{3}^{-1}&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\tilde{\lambda}_{n}=0\end{pmatrix}.

(95)

then, the pseudoinverse of
K
is given by

\tilde{K}=V\tilde{\Lambda}U^{\dagger}

(96)

and

\left.\frac{\delta\Delta_{m}}{\delta A_{\mu}}\right|_{0}=\tilde{K}C_{\mu}+(1-\tilde{K}K)W

(97)

where
C_{\mu}
is the column vector with elements
\left\{(C_{\mu})_{m}\right\}
, and

W
is the vector containing the free parameters. In our case, only the last element
of the diagonal matrix
(1-\tilde{K}K)
is nonzero leaving just one free parameter,
corresponding to the overall gauge phase factor.

VI
Superconductor with vortex lattice

We consider a superconductor with a 2D vortex lattice in the
(x,y)
plane
induced by a background perpendicular magnetic field
B_{z}
.
The presence of this field is taken into account within the tight model Hamiltonian (
18
)
via the introduction of a Peierls phase.
This has the effect of altering the translation group of the underlying square lattice to that
of the magnetic translation group
Hofstadter 1976
.
A magnetic unit cell must be chosen such that an integer number of magnetic flux quanta
\Phi_{0}=h/e

due to
B_{z}
thread the 2D systems. We chose the magnetic unit cells to be
Ma\times Ma
with one flux quantum threading it.

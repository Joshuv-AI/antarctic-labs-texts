Harmonic measures and rigidity for transverse foliations on Seifert 3 3 -manifolds
Adachi, Masanori, Matsuda, Yoshifumi, Nozawa, Hiraku

Abstract
Thurston proposed, in part of an unfinished manuscript, to study surface group actions on S 1 S^{1} by using an S 1 S^{1} -connection on the suspension bundle obtained from a harmonic measure. Following the approach and previous work of the authors, we study the actions of general lattices of PSU ⁡ ( 1 , 1 ) \operatorname{PSU}(1,1) on S 1 S^{1} . We prove the Gauss–Bonnet formula for the S 1 S^{1} -connection associated with a harmonic measure, and show that a harmonic measure on the suspension bundle of the action with maximal Euler number has rigidity, having a form closely related to the Poisson kernel. As an application, we prove a semiconjugacy rigidity for foliations with maximal Euler number, which is analogous to theorems due to Matsumoto, Minakawa and Burger–Iozzi–Wienhard.

Introduction

The Euler number is fundamental in the rigidity property of surface group actions on the circle
S^{1}
. Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
a homomorphism. In the case where
\Gamma
is torsion-free and uniform, the quotient
\Sigma=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
of the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{D}
is a closed hyperbolic surface with
\pi_{1}\Sigma\cong\Gamma
. The Euler number
e(\rho)
satisfies the Milnor–Wood inequality
|e(\rho)|\leq-e(\Sigma)

[
30
,
39
]
, and the equality holds if and only if
\rho
is semi-conjugate to the Fuchsian action
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
or its complex conjugate by Matsumoto’s rigidity theorem
[
28
]
.

If
\Gamma
is torsion-free and non-uniform, then the usual notion of the Euler number does not make sense since
\Sigma=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
is an open surface and the Euler number in the standard sense would be always trivial. Burger–Iozzi–Wienhard
[
8
]
, however, extended the definition of the Euler number
e(\rho)
to this case based on Ghys’ bounded Euler class
[
15
]
and generalized the Milnor–Wood inequality and Matsumoto’s rigidity theorem.
In our previous work
[
3
]
, we obtained rigidity results for foliated harmonic measures of the suspension foliation
\mathcal{F}
of
\rho
based on the approach due to Frankel
[
12
]
and Thurston
[
37
]
to give alternative proofs to these preceding results. We used Thurston’s connection on the suspension bundle associated with a harmonic measure, whose curvature
K
satisfies
|K|\leq 1
. Note that the connection is merely continuous and its curvature is defined in a weak sense (see Definition
3.8
).

In this article we extend these results for the general lattices in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
. First we will extend the definition of the Euler number
e(\rho)
of the
\Gamma
-action on
S^{1}
to this general case following the manner of
[
8
]
, and express
e(\rho)
in terms of the translation numbers of the lifts of certain elements in
\rho(\Gamma)
to the universal cover
\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})
of
\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
(See Proposition
3.7
). A key is the following construction of Thurston’s connection and the Gauss–Bonnet formula in this context.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
with corresponding orbifold
\Sigma:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
a homomorphism. Assume that
\rho(\Gamma)
has no finite orbit in
S^{1}
. Take a harmonic measure
\mu
on the suspension foliation of
\rho
on the suspension
S^{1}
-orbibundle
\Sigma\times_{\rho}S^{1}
. The
S^{1}
-connection form
\overline{\omega}
associated with
\mu
has curvature of the form
K\operatorname{vol}
in a weak sense, where
\operatorname{vol}
is the hyperbolic volume form on
\Sigma
and
K
is a measurable function such that
|K|\leq 1
, and we have

e(\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}K(z)\operatorname{vol}(dz).

The Milnor–Wood inequality is a direct consequence of this result.

Corollary 1.2
.

For a lattice
\Gamma
in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
, we have

|e(\rho)|\leq-e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)

(1.1)

where
e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
is the orbifold Euler characteristic of
\Sigma=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
due to Satake.

Furthermore, with Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following rigidity for harmonic measures for lattice actions with maximal Euler numbers.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
a homomorphism. If we have
e(\rho)=e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
, then the pull back of every harmonic measure on the suspension bundle of
\rho
to the universal cover
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
is of the form

\frac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\mathfrak{m}(e^{\sqrt{-1}t})-z\rvert^{2}}\operatorname{vol}(z)\nu(t)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D},t\in\mathbb{R}),

where
\operatorname{vol}
is the leafwise hyperbolic volume form, for a Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
and a continuous monotone mapping
\mathfrak{m}:S^{1}\to S^{1}
of degree one which is
(\rho,\rho_{0})
-equivariant, namely,
\mathfrak{m}\circ\rho(\gamma)=\rho_{0}(\gamma)\circ\mathfrak{m}
holds for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

By this result, we can see that given lattice actions with maximal Euler numbers is semi-conjugated to the Fuchsian action by the mapping
\mathfrak{m}
, which shows the following generalization of rigidity theorems of Matsumoto and Burger–Iozzi–Wienhard.

Corollary 1.4
.

For a lattice
\Gamma
in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
, we have
e(\rho)=e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)

(
resp.
e(\rho)=-e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
)
if and only if
\rho
is semiconjugate to
\rho_{0}

(
resp.
\overline{\rho}_{0}
)
, where
\rho_{0}
is the Fuchsian action of
\Gamma
induced from the inclusion
\Gamma\to\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
, and
\overline{\rho}_{0}
is its complex conjugate.

We remark that Minakawa
[
31
]
has already proved a generalization of the Milnor–Wood inequality and Matsumoto’s rigidity theorem for uniform lattices of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
.
By Selberg’s lemma
[
36
]
, any lattice
\Gamma
has a torsion-free finite index normal subgroup
\Pi
. Minakawa derived his result for uniform lattice
\Gamma
from Matsumoto’s theorem for uniform torsion-free
\Pi
. Similarly we can prove Corollary
1.4
for general lattice
\Gamma
by using the result for torsion-free
\Pi
. The proof of Corollary
1.4
by Theorem
1.3
, therefore, should read as a direct alternative proof for this fact based on foliated harmonic measure. We note that Mann–Wolff
[
27
]
studied the action of the mapping class groups of surfaces on the circle by using the orbifold fundamental groups of some
2
-orbifolds, which is the main object in this article.

For lattices whose quotient orbifold have nonzero genus, we can refine the Milnor–Wood inequality in terms of the Seifert invariants by using a result of Eisenbud–Hirsch–Neumann
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
for transverse foliations on compact Seifert manifolds with boundary. Note that, for lattices with nontrivial torsion, the suspension bundle of its action on
S^{1}
may not be a
3
-manifold, but only a Seifert
3
-orbifold (see Section
3.2
for the detail and the Seifert invariants).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
with corresponding orbifold
\Sigma:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
a homomorphism. Let
M
be the suspension bundle of
\rho
whose the Seifert invariant of
M
normalized with respect to
\rho
is
(g;(1,\beta_{0}),(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),\dots,(\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{\ell}))
. If the topological genus
g
of
\Sigma
is not
0
, then we have

e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{(\alpha_{i}-1)-\beta_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\leq e(\rho)\leq-e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\beta_{i}-1}{\alpha_{i}}.

(1.2)

There is a clear relation between the equality conditions of this inequality and the Milnor–Wood inequality as follows:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
with corresponding orbifold
\Sigma:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
a homomorphism. We have
e(\rho)=e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
if and only if we have the equality in the left inequality in (
1.2
) and
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}-1
for every
i=1,\dots,\ell
. We have
e(\rho)=-e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
if and only if we have the equality in the right inequality in (
1.2
) and
\beta_{i}=1
for every
i=1,\dots,\ell
.

In the exceptional case where the topological genus of
\Sigma
is
0
, by
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
, we have

e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)-1+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{(\alpha_{i}-1)-\beta_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\leq e(\rho)\leq-e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)+1-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\beta_{i}-1}{\alpha_{i}},

which can be weaker than the Milnor–Wood inequality.

Let us mention an application of Corollaries
1.2
and
1.4
to transverse foliations on Seifert manifolds
M
whose base orbifold
\Sigma
possibly has cusps. There is a well known correspondence between homomorphisms
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}\Sigma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
and foliations on Seifert manifolds transverse to fibers via suspension bundles. Then we can define the Euler number
e(\mathcal{F})
of a transverse foliation
\mathcal{F}
as the Euler number of the corresponding homomorphism
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}\Sigma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
. Note that, if
\Sigma
has no cusp, then
e(\mathcal{F})
coincides with the Euler number of the Seifert manifold
M
. The following is a rigidity result for Seifert manifolds with transverse foliations, which is equivalent to Corollaries
1.2
and
1.4
via the correspondence.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
M
be a Seifert
3
-orbifold whose singular locus is the union of a finite number of
S^{1}
-fibers. Assume that the base orbifold
\Sigma
is an oriented hyperbolic surface of finite volume without boundary that possibly has cusps.
If
M
admits a foliation
\mathcal{F}
transverse to the Seifert fibration, then we have

|e(\mathcal{F})|\leq-e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma),

where
e(\mathcal{F})
is the Euler number of the action of the orbifold fundamental group of
\Sigma
on
S^{1}
given by
\mathcal{F}
.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if there exist a lattice
\Gamma
in
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and a homotopy equivalence
f\colon M\to\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
which monotonely maps each Seifert fiber to a Seifert fiber and whose restriction to each leaf of
\mathcal{F}
is a diffeomorphism to a leaf of the stable or unstable foliation on
\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
.

The stable (resp. unstable) foliation on
\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
is the orbit foliation of the right action of the upper (resp. lower) triangular matrix subgroup of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
. A closed Seifert manifold with hyperbolic base space and nonzero Euler number admits an
\widetilde{SL}(2;\mathbb{R})
-geometry
[
35
]
, but it may not be a homogeneous space of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
in general. Closed Seifert manifolds which are homogeneous spaces were characterized by
[
33
]
.

2
Harmonic measures on the suspension foliation

Transverse invariant measures are a basic tool to study foliations. However, there are many foliations without such good measures. Garnett
[
13
]
introduced a weaker notion, harmonic measures on a foliated manifold, which are measures invariant under the leafwise heat flow. The advantage is that a nontrivial harmonic measure always exists for compact foliated manifolds by Garnett’s theorem. We refer to
[
13
,
9
]
for the basics of harmonic measures and to
[
40
,
10
,
2
]
for their applications.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and
\Sigma:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
the quotient orbifold. For a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
, we can construct its suspension
M:=\Sigma\times_{\rho}S^{1}:=\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb{D}\times S^{1})\to\Sigma
, where
\gamma\cdot(z,t)=(\gamma z,\rho(\gamma)t)
for
\gamma\in\Gamma,z\in\mathbb{D},t\in S^{1}
. We have a canonical map
\pi:M\to\Sigma;[(z,t)]\mapsto[z]
.
Since
\Gamma
may have torsions, the
\Gamma
-actions on
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}
may not be free but only proper. Therefore, in general,
\Sigma
and
M
are not smooth manifolds but orbifolds. Similarly
\pi
may have multiple fibers. A partition
\mathcal{F}
on
M
is induced from the product foliation
\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}=\sqcup_{w\in S^{1}}\mathbb{D}\times\{w\}
. If
\Gamma
is torsion-free, then
\mathcal{F}
is the suspension foliation. In general, the image of
\mathbb{D}\times\{w\}
may be an orbifold, and
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation by orbifolds, which means that
(M,\mathcal{F})
is covered by an orbifold atlas consisting of foliated charts with finite group actions and compatible foliations.
Smooth functions on
\Sigma
and
M
are identified with
\Gamma
-invariant smooth functions on
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}
, respectively.
C^{k}
functions along the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are identified with
\Gamma
-invariant continuous functions on
\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}
such that its restriction to
\mathbb{D}\times\{w\}
is
C^{k}
for each
w\in S^{1}
. Differential forms, Riemannian metrics and Laplacians on these orbifolds are considered in a similar way by using the
\Gamma
-actions on the corresponding spaces. Since
\Gamma
preserves the Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{D}
, the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
admits a natural Riemannian metric
g
of constant curvature
-1
. Let us denote the Laplacian associated with
g
by
\Delta
.

The following is the main tool in this article.

Definition 2.1
.

A Borel measure
\mu
on
M
is called
harmonic
if

\int_{M}\Delta f(x)\mu(dx)=0

for every compactly supported
C^{2}
function
f
along the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
such that
\Delta f
is continuous on
M
.

Alvarez
[
5
]
proved the existence of a harmonic measure for the suspension bundle of a
\Gamma
-action for torsion-free
\Gamma
even if the total space may not be compact. Thanks to the Selberg’s lemma
[
36
]
, a general lattice of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
has a torsion-free normal subgroup of finite index. Therefore, by using a finite covering of
M
, we get the following by the result of Alvarez:

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a harmonic probability measure
\mu
on the suspension foliation
(M,\mathcal{F})
.

By the disintegration formula due to
[
13
]
, the pull back of
\mu
to
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
is of the form

\tilde{\mu}=h(z,t)\operatorname{vol}(z)\nu(t),

(2.1)

where
\operatorname{vol}
is the leafwise volume measure on
\mathbb{D}
, for some Borel measurable function
h(z,t)
whose restriction to
\mathbb{D}\times\{t\}
is a positive harmonic function for
\nu
-a.e.
t
and
\nu
is a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

3
The Euler number of actions of lattices on
S^{1}

3.1
Definition of the Euler number

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
, and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
a homomorphism. In the case where
\Gamma
is uniform, it is classical to define the Euler number of
\rho
by the pairing of the Euler class and the fundamental class of
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
. Burger–Iozzi–Wienhard
[
8
]
defined the Euler number based on bounded cohomology in the case where
\Gamma
is non-uniform and torsion-free, i.e.,
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
is a hyperbolic surface with cusps. Let us extend their definition to the case where
\Gamma
is a general lattice.

By Selberg’s lemma
[
36
]
, there exists a finite index torsion-free normal subgroup
\Pi
of
\Gamma
. Let
\Sigma:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
. Let
H_{b}(\cdot)
denote the bounded cohomology with real coefficient of a topological space or a discrete group. First let us explain that so-called Gromov isomorphism for
\Gamma
follows from that for
\Pi

[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have an isomorphism

H_{b}^{k}(\Gamma)\cong H_{b}^{k}(\Sigma),\quad\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(3.1)

Proof.

Since
\Gamma/\Pi
is a finite group, its bounded cohomology with any coefficient module vanish by a result of Trauber (see
[
16
]
). Then the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence
[
26
,
17
]
for bounded cohomology for the sequence
1\to\Pi\to\Gamma\to\Gamma/\Pi\to 1
,

H_{b}^{p}(\Gamma/\Pi,H^{q}_{b}(\Pi))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{b}(\Gamma)

collapses at the
E^{2}
-level, which implies

H_{b}^{k}(\Gamma)\cong H^{0}_{b}(\Gamma/\Pi,H^{k}_{b}(\Pi))\cong H^{k}_{b}(\Pi)^{\Gamma/\Pi}.

Let
B^{\bullet}(\cdot)
be the complex of bounded singular cochains on a topological space. By a result of Ivanov
[
19
]
, we have
H_{b}^{k}(\Pi)\cong H^{k}(B^{\bullet}(\mathbb{D})^{\Pi})
for every
k\geq 0
, where
B^{\bullet}(\mathbb{D})^{\Pi}
denotes the subspace of
\Pi
-invariant elements. Therefore, since
\Pi
is torsion-free and
\Gamma/\Pi
is a finite group, we have

H^{k}_{b}(\Pi)^{\Gamma/\Pi}\cong H^{k}((B^{\bullet}(\mathbb{D})^{\Pi})^{\Gamma/\Pi})\cong H^{k}(B^{\bullet}(\Pi\backslash\mathbb{D})^{\Gamma/\Pi})\cong H^{k}_{b}(\Sigma),

which implies (
3.1
).
∎

Let us define the Euler number of a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
following
[
8
]
.
We cut off all cusps of
\Sigma
and obtain a compact orbifold
\Sigma^{\prime}
with boundary
\partial\Sigma^{\prime}
. We can pull back the universal Euler class
\operatorname{eu}\in H_{b}^{2}(\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1}))
by
\rho
to have
\rho^{*}\operatorname{eu}\in H_{b}^{2}(\Gamma)
, which we regard as an element of
H_{b}^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime})
via the isomorphism (
3.1
). Since
H_{b}^{1}(\partial\Sigma^{\prime})\cong H_{b}^{2}(\partial\Sigma^{\prime})\cong 0
, by the relative exact sequence, we have an isomorphism

f:H_{b}^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime})\to H_{b}^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime}).

Definition 3.2
.

We define the Euler number
e(\rho)\in\mathbb{R}
of
\rho
by

e(\rho)=\langle f^{-1}\rho^{*}\operatorname{eu},[\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime}]\rangle,

where
[\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime}]
is the fundamental class of
(\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime})
.

By the formula due to Burger–Iozzi–Wienhard
[
8
]
, if
\Gamma
is torsion-free and non-uniform, we may express the Euler number
e(\rho)
in terms of translation numbers of the holonomy along cusps. Let

\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})=\{\,f\in\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{R})\mid f(x+2\pi)=f(x)+2\pi,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\,\}

be the universal cover of
\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
and
\tau:\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the translation number, which is defined by

\tau(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f^{n}(x)-x}{n}

for
f\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})
. Then, by
[
8
]
, we have

e(\rho)=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tau(\tilde{\rho}(c_{j})),

(3.2)

where
c_{j}
is a loop on
\Sigma
that goes around the
j
-th cusp
(j=1,\dots,m)
oriented in a way compatible with the boundary of
\Sigma
when we cut off the cusps, and a homomorphism
\tilde{\rho}:\Gamma\to\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})
is a lift of
\rho
.

3.2
The Seifert invariant and the translation number

In this section, let us quickly recall the classical notion of the Seifert invariants, and introduce a normalized version for Seifert manifolds with transverse foliation. By using this normalized invariant, we will extend this formula (
3.2
) to the case where
\Gamma
has a nontrivial torsion taking in account of multiple fibers of the suspension bundle
\pi:M\to\Sigma
in the next section. Since
\Gamma
is assumed to contain a torsion, the
\Gamma
-actions on
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}
may not be free but only proper in general. Therefore
\Sigma
is not a hyperbolic surface but a hyperbolic
2
-orbifold, and
M
is not a circle bundle but a Seifert fibered
3
-orbifold in general.

Let us recall the Seifert invariant of a multiple fiber of a Seifert
3
-orbifold (see, for example,
[
4
,
6
]
). A multiple fiber has a neighborhood of the form
B^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}/\alpha\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
, where
B^{2}
is a
2
-disk and the
(\mathbb{Z}/\alpha\mathbb{Z})
-action on
B^{2}\times S^{1}
is generated by
(v,t)\mapsto(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/\alpha}v,e^{-2\beta\pi\sqrt{-1}/\alpha}t)
for some positive integer
\alpha
and an integer
\beta
. Note that
\beta
is well defined only up to modulo
\alpha
. Taking
\beta
such that
0\leq\beta<\alpha
, the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is called
the Seifert invariant of the multiple fiber
, which classifies the local normal form of the fiber.

Remark 3.3
.

Let us see that the normal form of multiple fibers in our situation differ from the case of Seifert
3
-manifolds at most only up to orbifold singular loci. Let
\delta=\operatorname{GCD}(\alpha,\beta)
and
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/\delta
,
\beta^{\prime}=\beta/\delta
. Since the action of the subgroup
H<\mathbb{Z}/\alpha\mathbb{Z}
of order
\delta
on
B^{2}\times S^{1}
is generated by
(v,t)\mapsto(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/\delta}v,t)
, it is easy to see that the isotropy subgroup of
\{0\}\times S^{1}
is
H
. By using a homeomorphism
B^{2}/H\cong B^{2}
, we can see that the quotient
(B^{2}/H)\times S^{1}
with the induced
\mathbb{Z}/\alpha^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
-action is equivariantly homeomorphic to
B^{2}\times S^{1}
with the
\mathbb{Z}/\alpha^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
-action generated by
(v,t)\mapsto(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/\alpha^{\prime}}v,e^{-2\beta^{\prime}\pi\sqrt{-1}/\alpha^{\prime}}t)
. The latter is the normal form of the multiple fiber of a Seifert
3
-manifold with Seifert invariant
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
. Thus, ignoring the orbifold singularity on the multiple fiber
\{0\}\times_{\mathbb{Z}/\alpha\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
, the local model is topologically same as the case of multiple fibers of Seifert
3
-manifolds.

Remark 3.4
.

If
M
is a Seifert
3
-manifold, then
\alpha
is coprime to
\beta
. Thus the Seifert invariant
(\alpha,\beta)
is recovered from
\beta/\alpha
. In our case,
\alpha
may not be coprime with
\beta_{i}
, and
\operatorname{GCD}(\alpha,\beta)
is determined by the orbifold singularity on the multiple fiber.

As in the classical case of Seifert
3
-manifolds, Seifert
3

4.1
Construction of the
S^{1}
-connection

We can construct the
S^{1}
-connection
\overline{\omega}
associated with a harmonic measure in a way similar to the torsion-free case
[

\Gamma
be a lattice of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)

\rho
a homomorphism
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
and
\Sigma=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
. We assume that
\rho(\Gamma)
has no finite orbit in
S^{1}
.
Consider the suspension foliation on
M=\Sigma\times_{\rho}S^{1}
equipped with the leafwise Poincaré metric. There exists a harmonic probability measure
\mu
on this foliation by Lemma
2.2
.
Consider the lift
\tilde{\mu}
of
\mu
to
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
. By disintegration formula (
2.1
),
\tilde{\mu}
is of the form

\tilde{\mu}=q(z,t)\operatorname{vol}(z)\nu(t),

where
\operatorname{vol}
is the leafwise volume measure on
\mathbb{D}
, for some Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
and a locally integrable function
q
on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\operatorname{vol}(z)\nu(t)
such that
q(\cdot,t)
is a positive harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
for
\nu
–a.e.
t
.

The first step is to collapse the complement of the support of
\mu
. For each

, consider a measure
\mu_{z}
on
S^{1}
induced by
\tilde{\mu}_{z}:=q(z,t)\nu(t)
on
\mathbb{R}
. Since there is no finite orbit of
\rho(\Gamma)

is non-atomic for every

. We consider a non-decreasing map
\tilde{\psi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\tilde{\psi}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\mu}_{0}(ds)=\int_{0}^{t}q(0,s)\nu(ds).

Since
\mu_{0}
is non-atomic,
\tilde{\psi}
is continuous. It covers a semiconjugacy
\psi:S^{1}\to S^{1}
which collapses the complement of the support of
\mu_{0}
. Thus we obtain a
\Gamma
-action

such that
\rho^{\prime}(\gamma)\circ\psi=\psi\circ\rho(\gamma)
for every

. Let

be the suspension bundle of
\rho^{\prime}
, which is a Seifert
3
-orbifold in general. The suspension foliation of

admits a harmonic measure
\mu^{\prime}
of full support induced by
\mu
, whose lift
\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}
to
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
is expressed as

\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}=h(z,t)\operatorname{vol}(z)\lambda(t),

where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
, for some locally integrable function
h
on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\operatorname{vol}(z)\lambda(t)
such that

is a positive harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
for

–a.e.
t
.

\omega_{j}(z,\theta):=\left.\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(z,t)\right\rvert_{t=\tau(z,\theta)}

for

\omega

For each

.
Then we have

\overline{\omega}=d\theta-\sum_{j=1,2}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\omega_{j}(z,\theta)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\right)dx_{j}

Among the definition of

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{\partial\omega_{1}}{\partial\theta}(z,\theta)\omega_{2}(z,\theta)+\frac{\partial\omega_{2}}{\partial\theta}(z,\theta)\omega_{1}(z,\theta)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\omega_{j}(z,\theta)=\frac{\partial\log h}{\partial x_{j}}(z,\tau(z,\theta))

\lvert K\rvert\leq\frac{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial\log h}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial\log h}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}}d\theta\right)^{2}\\

4.3
Applications to rigidity

A key point of the application of the Gauss–Bonnet formula (Theorem
1.1
) to rigidity is the characterization of harmonic functions on the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{D}
which achieves the equality in Harnack’s inequality: If
h
is a positive harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\|d\log h\|=1
at some point, then
h
is of the form
h(z)=C\frac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{\lvert m-z\rvert^{2}}
for some
m\in S^{1}
and a positive constant
C
(see
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
). We outline a direct proof of the rigidity theorem (Theorem
1.3
) for harmonic measures based on Theorem
1.1
below, which is essentially contained in
[
3
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Recall that
\tilde{\mu}
is of the form

\tilde{\mu}=q(z,t)\operatorname{vol}(z)\nu(t),

where
\operatorname{vol}
is the leafwise volume measure on
\mathbb{D}
, for some Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
and a locally integrable function
q
on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\operatorname{vol}(z)\nu(t)
such that
q(\cdot,t)
is a positive harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
for
\nu
–a.e.
t
. Let
\tilde{\psi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the map that collapses the complement of the support of
\tilde{\mu}_{0}=q(0,t)\nu
, which we considered in Section
4.1
. Then, the pushforward
\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}=(\operatorname{id}\times\psi)_{*}\tilde{\mu}
is of the form

\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}=h(z,t)\operatorname{vol}(z)\lambda(t),

where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure and
h(\cdot,t)
is a positive harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
for
\lambda
–a.e.
t
. Since
\tilde{\psi}_{*}(q(0,t)\nu)=\lambda
, we have

q(z,t)=q(0,t)h(z,\tilde{\psi}(t))

(4.12)

for
\nu
–a.e.
t
in
\mathbb{R}
. By the hypothesis
e(\rho)=e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
, by the Gauss–Bonnet theorem
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}K(z)\operatorname{vol}(dz)=e^{\operatorname{orb}}(\Sigma)
for orbifolds due to Satake
[
34
]
the equalities hold in the isoperimetric inequality for the curve
(\omega_{1}(z,\theta),\omega_{2}(z,\theta))
(see (
4.3
)) and Harnack’s inequality for
h(\cdot,t)
in (
4.5
). Since
h(\cdot,t)
achieves the equality in Harnack’s inequality, by
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
, we have that, for
\lambda
–a.e.
t
in
\mathbb{R}
, we have

h(z,t)=\frac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{\lvert m(e^{\sqrt{-1}t})-z\rvert^{2}}

for some
m(e^{\sqrt{-1}t})\in S^{1}
. Regard

as a map
S^{1}\to S^{1}
. From the equality in the isoperimetric inequality, it follows that
(\omega_{1}(z,\theta),\omega_{2}(z,\theta))
is a round circle for a.e.

, which implies that

is a rotation. Thus we have

h(z,t)=\frac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{\lvert e^{\sqrt{-1}(t+t_{0})}-z\rvert^{2}},

for some

. By (

), we have

q(z,t)=q(0,t)\frac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\mathfrak{m}(e^{\sqrt{-1}t})-z\rvert^{2}},

where

in
\mathbb{R}

∎

5
Eisenbud–Hirsch–Neumann’s inequality

Let us prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
. Recall the hypothesis and the notation. Given a lattice
\Gamma
of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
and a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})
, the suspension bundle is a Seifert
3
-orbifold

(see Section
3.2
). Take a free
S^{1}
-fiber
O_{0}
, and let

be the multiple fibers of
M
. Let
(g;(1,\beta_{0}),(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),\dots,(\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{\ell}))
be the Seifert invariant of

normalized with respect to
\rho
(see Section
3.2
). We assume that the topological genus
g
of the base orbifold
\Sigma:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
is not

. Recall that
\pi:M\to\Sigma
is trivialized on
\Sigma-\cup_{i=0}^{\ell}D_{i}
, where

is a small open disk neighborhood of
\pi(O_{i})
for

.

Note that the orbifold locus of

is contained in the union of multiple fibers. Then after cutting off an
S^{1}
-invariant tubular neighborhood of each component of the singular locus, the remaining part can be regarded as the interior of a compact Seifert
3
-manifold with boundary. Since
g\geq 1
, by a result of Eisenbud–Hirsch–Neumann
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
, we have

2-2g-\ell-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lceil\underline{m}h_{j}\rceil\leq\beta_{0}\leq 2g-2-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lfloor\overline{m}h_{j}\rfloor,

(5.1)

where
m
is the number of cusps of the base orbifold
\Sigma
, the map
h_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the lift of the holonomy along the curve that goes around the
j
-th cusp with respect to the fixed trivialization,
\underline{m}h=\min_{x}(h(x)-x)
and
\overline{m}h=\max_{x}(h(x)-x)
for
h\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})
. We use the following inequality.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
h\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})
, we have

\lceil\overline{m}h\rceil-1\leq\tau(h)\leq\lfloor\underline{m}h\rfloor+1.

Proof.

Take
n\in\mathbb{Z}
so that
n\leq\tau(h)<n+1
. Assume that
n+1\leq\overline{m}h
. Since
\tau(h)<n+1
, we have
h(x)-x<n+1
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}
. By the intermediate value theorem, we have
h(y)-y=n+1
for some
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
h\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Homeo}}_{+}(S^{1})
, we have
h^{s}(y)-y=s(n+1)
, which implies that
\tau(h)=n+1
. It contradicts to
\tau(h)<n+1
. Therefore, by contradiction, we have
\overline{m}h<n+1
, which implies
\lceil\overline{m}h\rceil-1\leq\tau(h)
. The second inequality is proved in a similar way.
∎

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Dispersive perturbations of Burgers’ equation

In this paper we continue our investigation of the well-posedness at low regularity of nonlocal dispersive perturbations of the Burgers’ equation. More specifically, we consider the class of initial value problems (IVP)

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-L_{\alpha+1}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\end{cases}

where
u=u(t,x)
is a real valued function,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}
or
\mathbb{T}(={\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
and
L_{\alpha+1}
is a Fourier multiplier of symbol
i\omega_{\alpha+1}
, with
\omega_{\alpha+1}
behaving in absolute value at high frequency as
|\xi|^{\alpha+1}
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
. We refer to Hypothesis
1
below for more precise assumptions about
\omega_{\alpha+1}
.

This class of equation contains in particular the fractional KdV equation (fKdV), also known as dispersion generalized Benjamin-Ono equation,

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u-D^{\alpha}\partial_{x}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),

where
D^{\alpha}
is the Fourier multiplier of symbol
|\xi|^{\alpha}
.
In the cases where
\alpha=2
, respectively
\alpha=1
, equation (
1.2
) corresponds to the well-known Korteweg-de Vries (KdV), respectively Benjamin-Ono (BO) equations, while in the case
\alpha=0
, it corresponds to the inviscid Burgers equation.

Also of physical interest with connections to fluid mechanics are the cases
\alpha=\pm\frac{1}{2}
: the Whitham equation

(1.3)

\partial_{t}u-w(D)\partial_{x}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),

where
w(D)
is the Fourier multiplier of symbol
w(\xi)=\left(\frac{\tanh(\xi)}{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is included in the class of equations (
1.1
). It was obtained by Whitham
[
56
]
by replacing the dispersion
\partial_{x}^{3}
in the KdV equation by that of the linearized water wave equations around the equilibrium. Here
\tau\geq 0
is a parameter related to surface tension, so that the case of pure gravity waves (
\tau=0
) corresponds to
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
, while the case of capillary-gravity waves (
\tau>0
) corresponds to
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in our setting. We also refer to
[
33
]
for a detailed survey on the Whitham equation.

While both the KdV and the BO equation are completely integrable, no integrable structures are known for the fKdV equations with non-integer values of
\alpha
. However, equation (
1.2
) enjoys an Hamiltonian structure and the quantities

(1.4)

M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{K}}u^{2}(t,x)dx

and

(1.5)

H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{K}}(D^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u)^{2}(t,x)dx+\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{K}}u^{3}(t,x)dx

are conserved by its flow.
Moreover, the solutions of fKdV are preserved under the scaling transformation
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\alpha}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{1+\alpha}t)
,
\lambda>0
, which leaves the
\dot{H}^{s}
invariant for
s=\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
. In particular, equation (
1.1
) is
mass-critical
for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
energy critical
for
\alpha=\frac{1}{3}
. As explained above, the case
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
is of particular interest because of its connection with fluid mechanics.

1.2.
Well-posedness review on fractional KdV equations

We are interested in the local well-posedness of the IVP associated to (
1.1
) in the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
at low regularity. The notion of
local well-posedness
(LWP) is to be understood in the sense of Hadamard and Kato, meaning that for every initial datum
u_{0}\in H^{s}
, there exist a positive time
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
and a solution
u\in C([0,T]:H^{s})
of (
1.1
) satisfying
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}
. This solution is unique, at least in the resolution space in which it is constructed. Moreover, the flow map data-solution which to the initial datum
u_{0}
maps the solution
u
is continuous from
H^{s}
to
C([0,T^{\prime}]:H^{s})
, for any
T^{\prime}<T
. If the solution map is smooth, the flow is said to be
semi-linear
, otherwise, if the solution map is only continuous, the flow is said to be
quasi-linear
. Finally, when
T
can be chosen arbitrarily large in the definition above, the IVP is said to be
globally well-posed
(GWP) in
H^{s}
.

Based on the numerical evidences obtained by Klein and Saut in
[
34
]
, it is conjectured that the IVP associated to (
1.2
) is globally well-posed in the sub-critical case
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, whereas blow-up may occur for
\alpha\leq 1/2
. In the previous work
[
41
]
, we proved that all ”sufficiently smooth” solutions are global for
\alpha>\frac{6}{7}
. Here, we extend that result to
\alpha>\frac{2}{3}
.

We now carry out a review of the well-posedness of the IVP associated with the classes (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in
H^{s}
. Using classical energy estimates and compactness arguments, one obtains local well-posedness in
H^{s}
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
, both on the real line and the torus, for the IVP (
1.1
), under the sole assumption that the operator
L_{\alpha+1}
is skew-adjoint (see, for instance,
[
1
]
). This regularity threshold is optimal in the absence of dispersion: the Burgers’ equation, as noted in Remark 1.6 of
[
36
]
, fails to be well-posed in
H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
.

When
\alpha>0
, the dispersive nature of the equation provides additional smoothing, which can be exploited to lower the regularity requirement. This was achieved for the KdV equation in the 90’s in seminal works by Kenig, Ponce, and Vega
[
29
,
30
]
, and by Bourgain
[
6
]
, by applying a fixed point argument on the associated Duhamel formulation in some suitable resolution spaces. The resolution space of Kenig, Ponce, and Vega is constructed using the linear smoothing effects of the equation, namely Strichartz estimates, local Kato smoothing, and maximal function estimates, while Bourgain’s approach is based on the Fourier restriction norm method. As a consequence of these results, the flow map of KdV is semi-linear in any Sobolev space
H^{s}
, with enough regularity (
s>-\frac{3}{4}
to be precise).

However, this result breaks down, at least on the line, as soon as
\alpha<2
. Indeed it was proved by Molinet, Saut and Tzvetkov in
[
44
]
that the flow map is quasi-linear in any Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, so it is not possible to solve the IVP by using a fixed-point argument on the associated Duhamel formulation. One has then to come back to compactness methods and energy estimates to prove well-posedness below the threshold
s=\frac{3}{2}
.

This was done for the Benjamin-Ono equation by Ponce
[
48
]
who established global well-posedness in
H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
by using the Strichartz estimate
\|e^{tD\partial_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
. Later on, Koch and Tzvetkov
[
35
]
, and thereafter Kenig and Koenig
[
27
]
, pushed down this result up to
s>\frac{5}{4}
and
s>\frac{9}{8}
, respectively. These improvements were achieved by estimating the norm
\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}dt
of a solution localised around a dyadic frequency
N
. The key idea was to partition the time interval
[0,T]
into subintervals of length approximately
N^{-\delta}
, for some suitable
\delta>0
, apply Strichartz estimates on each subinterval, and then sum over the contributions from all intervals. Similar ideas were previously used in the wave equation setting
[
4
,
55
]
and in the Schrödinger equation setting
[
7
]
. We also refer to a short-time Strichartz estimate in the periodic setting, established by Kenig, Ponce, and Vega
[
28
]
, which will be useful in this work.

In a different direction, Tao introduced in
[
53
]
a complex version of the Cole-Hopf transformation, localized in frequency, to cancel the problematic high-low frequency interactions in the nonlinearity of the BO equation. This
gauge
transform, combined with Strichartz estimates, enabled him to prove global well-posedness for BO in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Building on this idea and using the Fourier restriction norm method, Ionescu and Kenig
[
24
]
, and independently Molinet
[
38
,
39
]
, proved global well-posedness in
L^{2}
on the real line and on the torus, respectively (see also Burq and Planchon
[
8
]
for a result in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s>\frac{1}{4}
). We also refer to the works
[
40
,
21
]
for alternative proofs of global well-posedness in
L^{2}
. The approach of Molinet and Pilod
[
40
]
, which still relies on the Fourier restriction norm method, applies both on the real line and the torus, and also establishes unconditional uniqueness (see
[
32
,
47
]
for further results in this direction). In contrast, the proof by Ifrim and Tataru
[
21
]
does not use the Fourier restriction norm method and additionally provides long-time asymptotics for small data.
Recently, by exploiting the complete integrability, the global well-posedness threshold for the BO equation was lowered to
H^{s}
,
s>-\frac{1}{2}
, first on the torus by Gérard, Kappeler and Topalov in
[
14
]
, and then on the line by Killip, Laurens and Visan
[
31
]
.

Although the gauge transform is somewhat related to the integrability of the equation (see
[
15
]
), the method appears more robust and adaptable to dispersive equations beyond the BO equation. We refer to
[
20
,
9
]
for well-posedness results in
L^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{T}
for the Intermediate Long Wave equation relying on the BO gauge, (see also
[
13
]
for an improvement to
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
using the integrable structure of BO). By using a paradifferential version of the gauge Herr, Ionescu, Kenig and Koch
[
17
]
proved global well-posedness in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for the IVP associated with (
1.2
), for
1<\alpha<2
. This result was extended recently to negative regularities by Ai and Liu
[
2
]
who employed a pseudo-differential version of the gauge transform. At present, it remains an open question whether these techniques can be extended to lower dispersions
0<\alpha<1
. Nevertheless, we would like to mention the recent work of Said
[
49
]
in this direction, where a paralinearized version of (
1.2
) is considered.

Other types of energy methods have been employed to study the IVP for (
1.1
) in the case
1<\alpha<2
. In
[
16
]
, Guo used the short-time Fourier restriction norm method, in the framework introduced by Ionescu, Kenig, Tataru for KPI in
[
25
]
, to prove local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s>2-\alpha
. This result was improved by Molinet and Vento
[
46
]
, who established unconditional well-posedness in
H^{s}
,
s>1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
both on the line and on the torus, by introducing a new energy method for strongly resonant dispersive equations. More recently, Schippa
[
50
]
combined the short-time Fourier restriction norm method with modified energies to further lower the threshold in the periodic setting, proving well-posedness in
L^{2}
when
\frac{3}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 2
and in
H^{s}
for
s>\frac{3}{2}-\alpha
when
1\leq\alpha\leq\frac{3}{2}
.

Only few results are currently available in the literature for (
1.2
) in the case
0<\alpha<1
. On the line, Linares, Pilod and Saut
[
36
]
established local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3\alpha}{8}
by using the refined Strichartz estimate as in
[
35
,
27
]
. This result was subsequently improved by the authors of the present paper in
[
41
]
to
s>\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5\alpha}{4}
, which in turn yields global well-posedness in the energy space
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
for
\alpha>\frac{6}{7}
by relying on the conserved quantities in (
1.5
). The proof in
[
41
]
combines the energy method in
[
46
]
with modified energies. On the torus, Jöckel proved recently in
[
26
]
local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}-\alpha
by combining the short time restriction norm method with modified energies. In this work, we further improve these results, both on the line and on the torus.

Finally, we would like to mention some interesting related results concerning the small data, long-time existence problem for fKdV equations. In the case of the Burgers-Hilbert equation (corresponding to
\alpha=-1
in (
1.2
)), Hunter and Ifrim
[
18
]
, as well as Hunter, Ifrim, Tataru, and Wong
[
19
]
, proved that solutions with initial data of size
\epsilon\ll 1
exist on a time interval of length at least
\epsilon^{-2}
. These results were later extended by Ehrnström and Wang to the fKdV equations (
1.2
) for
-1<\alpha<1
,
\alpha\neq 0
in
[
10
]
, and to more general Whitham-type symbols in
[
11
]
. The proofs of all these results rely on modified energies and normal form transformations.

1.3.
Statements of the results

Here, we work on
{\mathbb{K}}\in\{\mathbb{T},{\mathbb{R}}\}
. As explained above, we focus on the case
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Our regularity threshold
s_{\alpha}
is given by

(1.6)

s_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}&\text{for}\quad\frac{2}{3}\leq\alpha\leq 1;\\
\frac{3}{2}(1-\alpha)&\text{for}\quad\frac{1}{3}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{2}{3};\\
\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}&\text{for}\quad 0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}.\end{cases}

We also define the regularity threshold
\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
for the
a priori
estimate by

(1.7)

\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}&\text{for}\quad\frac{2}{3}\leq\alpha\leq 1;\\
1-\alpha&\text{for}\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{2}{3};\\
\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}&\text{for}\quad 0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Observe in particular that
s_{\alpha}\geq\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
.

Moreover, we assume that the symbol
\omega_{\alpha+1}
of the Fourier multiplier
L_{\alpha+1}
satisfies the following assumption.

Hypothesis 1
.

\omega_{\alpha+1}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{0\})
is a real-valued odd function satisfying: there exists
\xi_{0}>0
such that, for all
|\xi|\geq\xi_{0}
and
\beta\in\{0,1,2,3\}
,

(1.8)

\left|\partial^{\beta}\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi)\right|\sim|\xi|^{\alpha+1-\beta}

Remark 1.1
.

It follows from the mean value theorem and the fact that
\omega_{\alpha+1}(0)=0
(
\omega_{\alpha+1}
is odd) that the symbol
\omega_{\alpha+1}
also satisfies, for
|\xi|\leq\xi_{0}
,

(1.9)

\left|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi)\right|\lesssim|\xi|.

Remark 1.2
.

The following symbols satisfy Hypothesis
1
:

(1)

for
0<\alpha\leq 1
: the homogeneous symbol
\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi
corresponding to the dispersive operator
D^{\alpha}\partial_{x}
in the fKdV equation (
1.2
);

(2)

for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
: the Whitham symbol
\omega_{W}(\xi)=\left(\frac{\tanh(\xi)}{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, with
\tau>0
corresponding to the Whitham equation with surface tension (
1.3
);

(3)

for
\alpha=1
: the symbol
\omega_{ILW}(\xi)=\xi^{2}\coth(\delta\xi)
corresponding to the Intermediate Long Wave equation (ILW).

(4)

for
\alpha=1
: the symbol
\omega_{S}(\xi)=\left(\left(1+\xi^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\right)\xi
corresponding to the Smith equation (see
[
51
]
).

Next we state our main result.

Theorem 1.3

(Local well-posedness)
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}\in\{\mathbb{T},{\mathbb{R}}\}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
s>s_{\alpha}
and
s^{\prime}>\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
with
s^{\prime}\leq s
. Assume that
L_{\alpha+1}
satisfies Hypothesis
1
. There exist positive constants
A_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
such that the following is true. For any
u_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{K}})
, let

T:=A_{0}(1+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}})^{-\beta_{0}}.

Then, there exists a unique solution
u
of the IVP (
1.1
) on the time interval
[0,T]
satisfying

(1.10)

u\in C([0,T]:H^{s}({\mathbb{K}}))\cap L^{2}\big((0,T):W^{(s-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}))_{-},\infty}({\mathbb{K}})\big).

Moreover, for any
0<\widetilde{T}<T
, there exists a neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{K})
such that the flow-map data-solution
v_{0}\mapsto v
is continuous from
\mathcal{U}
into
C([0,\widetilde{T}]:H^{s}({\mathbb{K}}))
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the case
\alpha=1
including the Benjamin-Ono and Intermediate Long Wave equations, we obtain local well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{K}})
,
s>\frac{1}{4}
. This result is similar to our earlier work in
[
41
]
on the real line and, to our knowledge, is new on the torus without relying on a gauge transformation. It remains an open question whether the threshold
s=\frac{1}{4}
can be lowered for these equations without using a gauge transform.

Remark 1.5
.

In the case
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{5}
, the result is less sharp than in our previous work
[
41
]
where we worked at the regularity
s>\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5\alpha}{4}
. It is likely that this could be improved at the cost of introducing additional technical complications. However, for the sake of simplicity and clarity of presentation, we choose to not pursue these refinements here.

Corollary 1.1

(Unconditional uniqueness)
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}\in\{\mathbb{T},{\mathbb{R}}\}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
s>\max\{\frac{1}{2},s_{\alpha}\}
. Assume that
L_{\alpha+1}
satisfies Hypothesis
1
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}
. Then there exists a unique solution to (
1.1
) in
C([0,T]:H^{s}(\mathbb{K}))
, where
T
is the existence time in Theorem
1.3
.

The quantities (
1.4
) and

(1.11)

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{K}}(\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u)^{2}(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{K}}u^{3}(t,x)dx,

where
\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is the Fourier multiplier of symbol
\sqrt{\frac{|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi)|}{|\xi|}}
, are conserved by the flow of (
1.1
). Note that the first integral in
E(u)
is well-defined in low frequency thanks to Remark
1.1
. By making use of these conservation laws, we can iterate Theorem
1.3
and thereby obtain global well-posedness in
H^{s}
for
\alpha>\frac{3}{4}
and in the energy space
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
for
\alpha>\frac{4}{5}
.

Theorem 1.6

(Global well-posedness)
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}\in\{\mathbb{T},{\mathbb{R}}\}
. Assume that
L_{\alpha+1}
satisfies Hypothesis
1
.

(i)

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{3},1]
. Then for any
s>s_{\alpha}
, the IVP (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{K})
.

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{4}{5},1]
. Then the IVP (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in the energy space
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{K})
.

Remark 1.7
.

The global well-posedness result in Theorem
1.6

(i)
follows from the fact that the
a priori
estimate holds at a lower regularity threshold
\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
than the local well-posedness threshold
s_{\alpha}
(see Propositions
4.2
). This kind of mechanism, where global control is achieved below the local well-posedness threshold, has been recently exploited by Ifrim and Tataru for general cubic defocusing dispersive equations
[
22
,
23
]
.

Remark 1.8
.

As a consequence of Corollary
1.6
, the IVP associated with (
1.2
) is globally well-posed in
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
for
\alpha\in(\frac{4}{5},1]
. This result completes the proofs of both the orbital stability of solitary waves in
[
37
]
, and the construction of solutions asymptotically behaving as a finite sum of solitary waves in
[
12
]
, for the fKdV equation in the same range of
\alpha
, as both results rely on the global well-posedness in the energy space.

1.4.
Strategy of the proof

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is an improvement of the strategy developed in
[
41
]
, which combines energy methods for strongly resonant symbols introduced by the first and third authors in
[
46
]
, refined Strichartz estimates, and the use of a modified energy. The main new ingredient is that, in this case, we fully symmetrize the symbol of the modified energy, both for the
a priori
estimate and for the difference.

The resolution space
Y^{s}_{T}
consists of functions in
C([0,T]:H^{s})
equipped with both the energy norm
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}
and the Strichartz norm
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}
. Recall that the Strichartz estimate for fKdV equation

\|e^{tD^{\alpha}\partial_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq c\|D^{\frac{1-\alpha}{4}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

involves a loss of
(1-\alpha)/4
derivatives. However, by chopping the time interval, applying this estimate on each subinterval and resumming over the whole interval, as explained above, we can control the Strichartz norm
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}
of a solution at the regularity level
H^{s}
, for any
s>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}
, (see Proposition
2.2
for a precise statement where we even recover
s-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
derivatives when working at the regularity
s>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}
).
Moreover, the space
Y^{s}_{T}
is embedded in the Bourgain space
X^{s-1,1}_{T}
. Indeed, the
X^{s-1,1}_{T}
-norm can be controlled by applying the classical linear
X^{s,b}
estimates on the Duhamel formulation associated to (
1.1
), followed by the fractional Leibniz rule, yielding

\|u\|_{X^{s-1,1}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|J_{x}^{s-1}\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}.

As in
[
41
]
, the resolution space
Z^{\sigma}_{T}
for the difference
w
of two solutions
u
and
v
is more involved. First,
w
can only be controlled at a negative regularity
\sigma
, with

-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}<\sigma<\min\left\{\frac{\alpha-1}{2},s-\frac{3}{2}+\alpha\right\}<0

and with a weight
|\xi|^{-1}
applied on the low frequencies. Accordingly, we use the energy norm
\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\overline{H^{\sigma}_{x}}}
and the Strichartz norm
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}\overline{L^{\infty}_{x}}}
where the overline indicates the addition of the low-frequency weight. On the other hand, since we are working at negative regularity, it is not possible to close the estimate on the Bourgain norm by applying the fractional Leibniz rule as before. For this reason, we replace the Bourgain space
X^{\sigma-1,1}
by the
sum space

F^{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}=X^{\sigma-1,1}+X^{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{F^{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}}=\inf\big\{\|u_{1}\|_{X^{\sigma-1,1}}+\|u_{2}\|_{X^{\sigma,(1/2)_{+}}}\,:\,u=u_{1}+u_{2}\big\}.

The use of a sum space was introduced by Tao
[
54
]
in the study of the quartic gKdV equation, and later applied in
[
46
,
41
]
in this setting.

Therefore, the main part of the paper is devoted to controlling the energy norms, both for a solution
u
and for the difference
w=u-v
of two solutions, at the corresponding levels of regularity. To achieve this, we employ modified energies. The main new idea is to fully symmetrise the symbol of these modified energies. In order to preserve as much symmetry as possible, we choose to perform the dyadic frequency decomposition on the functions only after the symmetrisation and cancellation process.

We illustrate this approach on the energy associated with the difference of two solutions
w=u-v
, which satisfies the equation

(1.12)

\partial_{t}w-L_{\alpha+1}w=\partial_{x}(zw),

where
z=u+v
. This equation is less symmetric than (
1.1
), making the analysis more delicate. The modified energy for
w
is then defined by

\widetilde{E}^{\sigma}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\||\xi|^{-1}\widehat{w}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\||\xi|^{\sigma}\widehat{w}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\geq 1)}^{2}+\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\sigma}(t)

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\sigma}(t)=\int_{\Gamma^{3}}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\widehat{w}(t,\xi_{1})\widehat{w}(t,\xi_{2})\widehat{z}(t,\xi_{3})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Here
\Gamma^{3}
denotes the hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:
\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}=0
,

\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\frac{(\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi_{1})+\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi_{2})){\bf 1}_{|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|>N_{0}}}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})},\ \text{with}\ \widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi)=\xi|\xi|^{2\sigma}{\bf 1}_{|\xi|\geq 1},

and
\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is the quadratic resonant function defined by

\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{2}).

It is not difficult to verify that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\sigma}
is coercive under suitable condition on
s
and
N_{0}
(see Proposition
5.1
). To obtain the energy estimate in Proposition
5.2
, we differentiate
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\sigma}(t)
with respect to time, use the equations (
1.1
) and (
1.12
) and then integrate again from
0
to
t
.

The contribution from the quadratic low-frequency part
\||\xi|^{-1}\widehat{w}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}
is readily controlled under the condition
-\sigma<s
. . Meanwhile, the contribution from the quadratic high-frequency part
\||\xi|^{\sigma}\widehat{w}(t^{\prime},\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\geq 1)}^{2}
cancels out with the term arising from the cubic correction
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\sigma}(t)
when inserting the linear contributions of the equations (
1.1
) and (
1.12
) into the time derivatives. It then remains to control the additional quartic terms produced by substituting the nonlinear terms from (
1.1
) and (
1.12
) into the time derivatives within the cubic correction
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\sigma}(t)
.

To do so, we fully symmetrize this term. The most delicate contribution to control is

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(t)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma^{4}}\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\widehat{w}(\xi_{1})\widehat{w}(\xi_{2})\widehat{z}(\xi_{3})\widehat{z}(\xi_{4})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}d\xi_{3}dt^{\prime},

where the symbol
\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})

\displaystyle=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{2})\right]

\displaystyle\quad-(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

We then perform a spatial dyadic decomposition on all the functions involved and carefully analyze the cancellations of
\widetilde{a}_{4}
in the different frequency regions: non-resonant, mildly resonant, and resonant. This analysis, carried out in detail in Appendix
A
, relies only on elementary calculus arguments. It is worth noting, however, that certain second-order cancellations are exploited in specific regions. The proof of the energy estimate is then completed by combining these symbol estimates with appropriate bounds in the resolution space norms.

A key point in this analysis is the cubic resonance function

\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{2})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{3})

which factorises as
|\Omega_{4}|\sim M_{min}M_{med}N_{max}^{\alpha-1}
, using the notation
|\xi_{i}|\sim N_{i}
,
M_{1}\sim|\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}|
,
M_{2}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|
,
M_{3}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|
. Both the fully resonant case
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N_{4}=N
and
M_{min}\sim M_{med}\ll N
(corresponding to Region A in our analysis), and the mild resonant cases
N_{min}\ll N_{thd}\sim N_{sub}\sim N_{max}
, (corresponding to Region C in our analysis) yield the threshold
s>1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}
(when
\frac{2}{3}\leq\alpha\leq 1
).

Finally, with these estimates in hand, we outline the proof Theorem
1.3
. First, we establish a Lipschitz bound at the negative threshold
\sigma
on the difference of two solutions emanating from initial data at regularity
s>s_{\alpha}
with the same low-frequency part. This directly yields uniqueness. Next, given an initial datum
u_{0}\in H^{s}
,
s>s_{\alpha}
, we consider the sequence of smooth solutions
u_{n}
emanating from the truncated data
\Pi_{\leq 2^{n}}u_{0}
with frequencies lower than
2^{n}
. Using the same estimates for smooth solutions (Proposition
4.2
), we derive
a priori

H^{s}
estimates on
[0,T]
, where
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}})
for any
s\geq s^{\prime}>\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, we also use a smoothing effect for these solutions at large frequency
N_{0}
(see Corollary
4.1
) to conclude that
\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T^{\prime}]:H^{s})
and
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
. This yields the existence of a solution
u\in C([0,T]:H^{s})
by iterating the argument a finite number of times as the
a priori
estimate hold on
[0,T]
. Instead of using the Bona-Smith argument
[
5
]
or the frequency enveloppe method (see
e.g.

[
3
]
and the references therein), we rely again on our smoothing effect described above to prove the continuity of the flow map.

Throughout the paper, we focus on the real line case. A section addressing the periodic case is included at the end of the paper, where the necessary modifications are discussed. The main new difficulty in the periodic setting is to establish a refined Strichartz estimate analogous to the one available on the real line. To this end, we rely on a crucial Strichartz estimate on short time intervals, originally derived by Kenig, Ponce, and Vega for (
1.2
) when
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}
(see Proposition 2.2 in
[
28
]
), which we adapt to our setting. The remainder of the proof proceeds similarly to the real line case.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

X^{s,b}
-estimates established in
[
41
]
. Section
3
is devoted to proving the

-trilinear estimates, which are then applied in Sections
4
and
5
to derive the energy estimates for solutions and for the difference of two solutions, respectively. In Section
6
, we provide the proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
and Corollary
1.1
, and we explain the main changes needed to address the periodic case in Section

3.
L^{2}
-trilinear estimates

We follow the notation in
[
52
]
. For any integer
n\geq 1
and any
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the hyperplane
\Gamma^{n}(\xi)
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

\Gamma^{n}(\xi)=\{(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n}=\xi\}.

When
\xi=0
we simply denote
\Gamma^{n}=\Gamma^{n}(0)
. As usual, if
F
is a measurable function on
\Gamma^{n}(\xi)
we set

\int_{\Gamma^{n}(\xi)}F(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}F(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1},\xi-(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n-1}))d\xi_{1}\ldots d\xi_{n-1}.

Moreover, for fixed
t>0
we introduce the sets
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{t}=[0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\Gamma^{n}_{t}=[0,t]\times\Gamma^{n}
.

Now if
\chi
is a bounded measurable fonction on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we define the multilinear pseudo-product operator
\Pi_{\chi}
by its Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}\left(\Pi_{\chi}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\right)(\xi)=\int_{\Gamma^{n}(\xi)}\chi(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{f_{j}}(\xi_{j}).

Sometimes, when there is no risk of confusion, we also denote for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})\in\Gamma^{n}
,

\chi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1})=\chi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},-\xi_{1}-\cdots-\xi_{n-1}).

We easily check that for any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi_{\chi}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1})f_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}}(f_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,f_{\sigma(n-1)})f_{\sigma(n)}

where
\widetilde{\chi}
is also bounded and satisfies
\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

We start with a basic
L^{2}
-trilinear estimate.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
j=1,...,4
and
M\in\mathbb{D}
. Assume that each
f_{j}
is supported in
\{|\xi|\sim N_{j}\}
for some dyadic numbers
N_{j}\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Then it holds that

(3.2)

\int_{\Gamma^{4}}\phi_{M}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\prod_{j=1}^{4}|f_{j}(\xi_{j})|\lesssim P\prod_{j=1}^{4}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\,,

where
P=\min\{M,(N_{min}N_{thd})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
.

Proof.

Estimate (
3.2
) is proved in
[
42
]
, Lemma 3.1 with a factor
M
in the right hand-side. Otherwise, it is a direct consequence of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that if
\chi
is a bounded symbol on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and

are dyadic numbers, then

(3.3)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})f_{4}\right|\lesssim P\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

where

and
N_{min}
,

are the third-maximum and the minimum of
\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},N_{4}\}
as introduced in the notation.

Below, we recall some important technical lemmas proved in

]
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[

]

Let

,

and

is bounded in

uniformly in

.

For any
T>0
, we consider
1_{T}
the characteristic function of the interval

and use the decomposition

1_{T}=1_{T,R}^{low}+1_{T,R}^{high},\quad\widehat{1_{T,R}^{low}}(\tau)=\eta(\tau/R)\widehat{1_{T}}(\tau)

for some

and where

.

For any

(3.7)

\|1_{T,R}^{high}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim T\wedge R^{-1}\textrm{ and }\|1_{T,R}^{high}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1.

and

. These estimates will be fundamental to derive the energy estimates in the next sections.
In the sequel we set

(3.9)

\Omega=M_{min}M_{med}N_{max}^{\alpha-1}\;.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
t\in]0,1]
and
u_{i}\in Z^{0}
,
i=1,2,3,4
satisfy
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits\widehat{u_{i}}\subset\{|\xi|\sim N_{i}\}
for some dyadic numbers
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},N_{4}
such that
N_{max}\gg 1
. Let
\chi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and define
\widetilde{\chi}
as in (
3.4
) for some dyadic numbers
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}>0
. If
\Omega\gtrsim 1
, then for any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, it holds

(3.10)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})u_{4}dxdt\right|\lesssim P(N_{max}\Omega^{-1})^{\theta}\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}

where
P
is defined in (
3.5
).

Proof.

When
\theta=0
, estimate (
3.10
) is directly obtained from (
3.3
). Thus, by interpolation we may assume
\theta=1
. By (
3.1
), we also may assume by symmetry
N_{1}\leq N_{2}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{4}
, which enforces
N_{3}\sim N_{4}=:N
. We split the integral
G_{t}
in the left hand-side of (
3.10
) as
G_{t}=G_{t}^{low}+G_{t}^{high}
with

G_{t}^{low}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{t,R}^{low}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})u_{4}dxdt

for
R=N^{-1}\Omega\ll\Omega
(recall that
N\gg 1
). The high-part is estimated thanks to (
3.2
) and (
3.7
) by

(3.11)

|G_{t}^{high}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})|\lesssim PR^{-1}\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}},

which is acceptable since
PR^{-1}=PN\Omega^{-1}
.
Next, we decompose
G_{t}^{low}
with respect to the modulation variables as follows:

G_{t}^{low}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})=\sum_{L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{t,R}^{low}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}}(Q_{L_{1}}u_{1},Q_{L_{2}}u_{2},Q_{L_{3}}u_{3})Q_{L_{4}}u_{4}dxdt.

Thanks to Proposition
A.2
, combined with the fact that
R\ll\Omega
, this sum vanishes unless
L_{max}\gtrsim\Omega
where
L_{max}=\max\{L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4}\}
, and we are led to evaluate each contribution
G_{t,j}^{low}
,
j=1,2,3,4
of
G_{t}^{low}
to the case
L_{max}=L_{j}
. By making use of (
3.8
) we bound
G_{t,1}^{low}
by

\displaystyle|G_{t,1}^{low}|

\displaystyle\lesssim P\|1_{t,R}^{low}\|_{L^{2}}\|Q_{\gtrsim\Omega}u_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{tx}}\prod_{i=2}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim P(\langle N_{1}\rangle\Omega^{-1}+\Omega^{-(1/2)_{+}})\|u_{1}\|_{F^{0}}\prod_{i=2}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

Observe that since
\Omega=M_{min}M_{med}N^{\alpha-1}\lesssim N^{2}
for
\alpha\leq 1
, it holds recalling that
\Omega\gtrsim 1
by hypothesis,

(3.12)

\Omega^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{+}}\lesssim N\Omega^{-1}.

Thus, since
\langle N_{1}\rangle\lesssim N
we get

|G_{t,1}^{low}|\lesssim PN\Omega^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

To deal with the contribution
G_{t,2}^{low}
we infer from (
3.3
), Lemma
3.2
, (
3.8
) and (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle|G_{t,2}^{low}|

\displaystyle\lesssim P\|1_{t,R}^{low}\|_{L^{2}}\|Q_{\ll\Omega}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|Q_{\gtrsim\Omega}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{tx}}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim P(\langle N_{2}\rangle\Omega^{-1}+\Omega^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{+}})\|u_{2}\|_{F^{0}}\prod_{i\neq 2}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim PN\Omega^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

Finally, by symmetry if suffices to estimate
G_{t,3}^{low}
. We get similarly

\displaystyle|G_{t,3}^{low}|

\displaystyle\lesssim P\|1_{t,R}^{low}\|_{L^{2}}\|Q_{\ll\Omega}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|Q_{\ll\Omega}u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|Q_{\gtrsim\Omega}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{tx}}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim P(N\Omega^{-1}+\Omega^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{+}})\|u_{2}\|_{F^{0}}\prod_{i\neq 3}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim PN\Omega^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

∎

To deal with a particular region in ou energy estimates, we will also need the following result involving the Strichartz part in the

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
t\in]0,1]
. Let
u_{i}\in Z^{0}
,
i=1,2,3,4
with spatial Fourier support in
\{|\xi_{i}|\sim N_{i}\}
for some

satisfying
N_{min}\ll N_{thd}\ll N_{max}
and
N_{thd}\gg 1
. Then we have

(3.13)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}u_{4}dxdt\right|\lesssim N_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{thd}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{max}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition
3.4
. By symmetry, it suffices to consider the case
N_{1}\ll N_{2}\ll N_{3}\sim N_{4}
, so that
\Omega\sim N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}\gtrsim 1
. First we decompose the integral
G_{t}
in the left hand-side of (
3.13
) as

G_{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{t,R}^{low}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}u_{4}dxdt+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{t,R}^{high}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}u_{4}dxdt=:G_{t}^{low}+G_{t}^{high}

with
R=N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{4}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\ll\Omega
. From Holder inequality and (
3.7
) it holds

|G_{t}^{high}|\lesssim(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{4}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

For the low part of
G_{t}
, we write

G_{t}^{low}=\sum_{L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4}\atop L_{max}\gtrsim N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{t,R}^{low}Q_{L_{1}}u_{1}Q_{L_{2}}u_{2}Q_{L_{3}}u_{3}Q_{L_{4}}u_{4}.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, let us denote by
G_{t,i}^{low}
the contribution of the sum over
L_{max}=L_{i}
to
G_{t}^{low}
. Observe that for any function
v\in Z^{0}
, and for any
N\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
L\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\geq 1}
, we have

\|Q_{L}v_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|Q_{L}u_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(NL^{-1}+L^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{-}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Q_{L}u_{N}\|_{F^{0}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim(NL^{-1}+L^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{-}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{N}\|_{Z^{0}}.

Then, for
G_{t,1}^{low}
, after resuming over
L_{2}
,
L_{3}
,
L_{4}
, Lemma
3.3
provides

\displaystyle|G_{t,1}^{low}|

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|1_{t,R}^{low}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}u_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{tx}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\langle N_{1}\rangle(N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha})^{-1}+(N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha})^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{+}})^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{4}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}},

since we assumed
N_{2}=N_{th}\gg 1
.
In the case
L_{max}=L_{2}
we argue similarly and use Lemma
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle|G_{t,2}^{low}|

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|1_{t,R}^{low}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q_{\ll N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|Q_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{tx}}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{2}(N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha})^{-1}+(N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha})^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{+}})^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{4}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

It remains to consider the contribution of the sum over
L_{max}=L_{3}
since the case
L_{max}=L_{4}
may be evaluated in the same way. We get

(3.14)

|G_{t,3}^{low}|\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{4}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|Q_{\ll N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\|u_{3}\|_{F^{0}}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}.

Since it is not clear wether
Q_{L}
is bounded or not in

, we rewrite

and obtain thanks to Bernstein inequality that

\displaystyle\|Q_{\ll N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}+N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Q_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha}}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{tx}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}+N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{2}(N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha})^{-1}+(N_{2}N_{4}^{\alpha})^{-(\frac{1}{2})_{+}})\right)\|u_{2}\|_{Z^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\|u_{2}\|_{Z^{0}}.

Inserting this into (

) we conclude

|G_{t,3}^{low}|\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{4}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Z^{0}}.

∎

4.
Energy estimates for solutions

We provide a priori estimates for solutions of (
1.1
) in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
where
\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}
is defined in (
1.7
).

Let
0<T\leq 1
,
N_{0}\gg 1
, and
u\in Y^{s}_{T}
be a solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
. For
t\in(0,T)
we define the modified energy

(4.1)

E^{s}(t)=E^{s}(t,N_{0},u)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(t,u)+c\mathcal{E}_{3}(t,N_{0},u)

where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{3}(t,N_{0},u)=\mathcal{E}_{3}(t)
will be defined later, and

\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(t,u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|D_{x}^{s}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right).

Differentiating
\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)
and integrating on
(0,t)
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{2}(0)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}D^{2s}_{x}u\partial_{x}(u^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{2}(0)-i\int_{\Gamma^{3}_{t}}\xi_{1}|\xi_{1}|^{2s}{\bf 1}_{|\xi_{1}|\leq N_{0}}\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3})-i\int_{\Gamma^{3}_{t}}\xi_{1}|\xi_{1}|^{2s}{\bf 1}_{|\xi_{1}|\geq N_{0}}\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3})

(4.2)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{E}_{2}(0)+\mathcal{I}_{0}(t)+\mathcal{I}(t).

We easily bound the low frequencies term
\mathcal{I}_{0}
thanks to Plancherel and Bernstein inequalities by

(4.3)

|\mathcal{I}_{0}(t)|\lesssim TN_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}^{2}.

We symmetrize
\mathcal{I}(t)
to

(4.4)

\mathcal{I}(t)=-\frac{i}{3}\int_{\Gamma^{3}_{t}}a_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3})

with

a_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{i}|\xi_{i}|^{2s}1_{|\xi_{i}|\geq N_{0}}.

To remove the contribution of
\mathcal{I}
, we set
c=1/3
and

(4.5)

\mathcal{E}_{3}(t)=\mathcal{E}_{3}(t,N_{0},u)=\int_{\Gamma^{3}}b_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{u}(\xi_{i})

with a symbol
b_{3}
defined on
\Gamma^{2}
by

b_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=\frac{a_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})}.

Note that
b_{3}
is symmetric and that we have from (
A.1
),
|b_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\sim|\xi_{max}|^{2s-\alpha}
. The following lemma states that our energy is coercive.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
C_{1}>0
such that for any
\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}<s\leq 3
and
0<T\leq 1
, if
u\in C([0,T]:H^{s})
is a solution of (
1.1
), then for any
t\in(0,T)
and
N_{0}\geq C_{1}(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}}})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
, it holds

(4.6)

\left|E^{s}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Proof.

From the definition in (
4.1
) we infer

\left|E^{s}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right|\lesssim|\mathcal{E}_{3}(t)|\lesssim\sum_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\geq N_{0}}N_{max}^{2s-\alpha}\int_{\Gamma^{3}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left|\widehat{u}_{N_{i}}(\xi_{i})\right|.

It follows then applying Bernstein in space that

\left|E^{s}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right|\lesssim(N_{0}^{-\alpha}+N_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha-\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}}_{x}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Thus we complete the proof of lemma since
N_{0}^{-\alpha}+N_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha-\widetilde{s}_{\alpha}}\lesssim N_{0}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists
C_{2}>0
and

,

Remark 4.3

\displaystyle=i\int_{\Gamma^{4}_{t}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})\right]\widehat{w}(\xi_{1})\widehat{w}(\xi_{2})\widehat{z}(\xi_{3})\widehat{z}(\xi_{4})

\xi_{2}+\xi_{4}=-(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})
and
\widetilde{b_{3}}(\mu,\eta)=\widetilde{b_{3}}(-\mu,-\eta)
. A second symmetrization leads to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{4}_{t}}\Big((\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{2})\right]\Big)\widehat{w}(\xi_{1})\widehat{w}(\xi_{2})\widehat{z}(\xi_{3})\widehat{z}(\xi_{4}).

On the other hand, the second term in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
is

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(t)=-\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{4}_{t}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\widehat{w}(\xi_{1})\widehat{w}(\xi_{2})\widehat{z}(\xi_{3})\widehat{z}(\xi_{4})

Combining theses identities we conclude

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(t)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{4}_{t}}\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\widehat{w}(\xi_{1})\widehat{w}(\xi_{2})\widehat{z}(\xi_{3})\widehat{z}(\xi_{4})

with a symbol

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})

\displaystyle=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\left[\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})-\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{2})\right]

\displaystyle\quad-(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

(5.13)

\displaystyle=:(\widetilde{a}_{41}+\widetilde{a}_{42}+\widetilde{a}_{43})(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}).

Note that
\widetilde{a_{4}}
is symmetric in
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
and in
(\xi_{3},\xi_{4})
. We collect all needed estimates on this symbol in Lemma
A.7
.
From the localization properties in the definition of
\widetilde{a}_{3}
, it is clear that
\widetilde{a}_{4}=0
in the region
\max(|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{3}|)\ll N_{0}
.
Thus we may decompose
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
as
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}=\sum_{N\gtrsim N_{0}}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N}
where

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(t)=\sum_{N_{1}\lesssim N_{2},N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}\atop N_{max}=N}\sum_{M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}>0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}}(w_{N_{1}},w_{N_{2}},z_{N_{3}})z_{N_{4}}

with

\widetilde{\chi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=\frac{i}{2}\phi_{M_{1}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\phi_{M_{2}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\phi_{M_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}).

In the sequel, we will split the sum in
N_{i}
into several regions, and we will denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}
the contribution of the region
\mathcal{R}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N}
. Estimate (
5.12
) follows then if we prove that for any
N\gtrsim N_{0}
and all
\mathcal{R}
, it holds

|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}(t)|\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}.

For each region
\mathcal{R}
, we decompose
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}^{low}+\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}^{high}
where
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}^{low}
denotes the contribution of the sum over
\Omega:=M_{min}M_{med}N^{\alpha-1}\ll N^{\alpha}
. This ensures that we may apply Proposition
3.4
for the high modulation term
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,\mathcal{R}}^{high}
.

Region
A
:
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N_{4}\sim N
.

In this region, the bound

\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}
follows from (
A.16
). For the contribution of
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,A}^{low}
, we deduce from (
3.3
) and
M_{min}\ll N^{\frac{1}{2}}
, (which follows from
\Omega\ll N^{\alpha}
), that

|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,A}^{low}|\lesssim\sum_{M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\atop M_{min}M_{med}\ll N}M_{min}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}^{2}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}^{2}\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

since
s>1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\geq\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}
. In the case
\Omega\gtrsim N^{\alpha}
, the above bound on
\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and (
3.10
) with
\theta=1/2
provide

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,A}^{high}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\lesssim M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\lesssim N}M_{min}N^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{min}M_{med}N^{\alpha-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}^{2}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{+}}N^{-2s+2-\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\|w\|_{Z^{s}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

where we used that
s>1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}
.

Region
B
:

N_{min}\ll N_{th}\sim N_{sub}\sim N_{max}=N
.

Note that in this region we have
M_{min}\sim M_{max}\sim N
, and thus
\Omega\sim N^{\alpha+1}\gtrsim N^{\alpha}
, so that
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,B}^{low}=0
.

Subregion
B_{1}
:

N_{min}=N_{1}
.

In this region it holds
\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}
thanks to (
A.17
).
Hence, (
3.10
) with
\theta=1
yields

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,B_{1}}^{high}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}\sum_{M_{i}\sim N}(N_{1}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}NN^{-1-\alpha}N_{1}^{-1}N^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{+}}N^{\sigma-2s+\frac{5}{2}-2\alpha}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

which is acceptable since
\sigma-2s+\frac{5}{2}-2\alpha<-s+1-\alpha<0
.

Subregion
B_{2}
:

N_{min}=N_{3}
.

On the support of
\widetilde{\chi}
we have
|\widetilde{a_{4}}|\lesssim N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}
. By (
3.10
) with
\theta=1
, we get

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,B_{2}}^{high}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}\ll N}\sum_{M_{i}\sim N}(N_{3}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}NN^{-1-\alpha}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}^{2}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}\ll N}N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-s}N^{-s+\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

which is acceptable since
s_{\alpha}>1-\alpha
and
s_{\alpha}>\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha
.

Region
C
:

N_{min}\lesssim N_{th}\ll N_{sub}\sim N_{max}=N
.

In this region, it holds
M_{min}\ll M_{med}\sim M_{max}\sim N
and
\Omega\sim M_{min}N^{\alpha}
, so that

\Omega\ll N^{\alpha}\Longleftrightarrow M_{min}\ll 1.

Subregion C1:

N\sim N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gg N_{4}\geq N_{3}
.

Note that in this region
M_{3}=M_{min}
and
\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{4}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}
by
(
A.19
)
. The term
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{1}}^{low}
is easily estimated thanks to (
3.3
) as well as (
A.19
) by

|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{1}}^{low}|\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}\ll N}\sum_{M_{3}\ll 1}M_{3}N_{4}N^{-\alpha}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{N_{4}}\|_{Y^{0}}\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}.

To deal with the high modulation part, we proceed as in the region
C
of the a priori estimate, and we first suppose
\alpha>1/2
. Recall that we have
N_{4}\gtrsim M_{3}\gtrsim 1
. Then we infer from (
3.10
) with
\theta=1
and (
A.19
) that
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{1}}^{high}(t)|
is bounded by

\sum_{N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}\ll N}\sum_{1\lesssim M_{3}\lesssim N_{4}}\min\{M_{3},(N_{3}N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}M_{3}^{-1}N^{1-\alpha}N_{4}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}^{2}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{N_{4}}\|_{Y^{0}}

Moreover, we observe that

\min\{M_{3},(N_{3}N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}M_{3}^{-1}N_{4}\lesssim(N_{3}N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{1}}^{high}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\sum_{N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}}N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-s}N_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}-s}N^{(1-2\alpha)_{+}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

since
s>\max(1-\alpha,\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha)
. In order to handle the case
\alpha\leq 1/2
we apply estimate (
3.10
) with
\theta=\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)_{-}
as well as (
A.19
) to bound
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{1}}^{high}(t)|
by

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}\ll N}\sum_{1\lesssim M_{3}\lesssim N_{4}}

\displaystyle\min\{M_{3},(N_{3}N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}M_{3}^{-\theta}N^{\theta(1-\alpha)}N_{4}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}^{2}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{N_{4}}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\sum_{N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}}N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-s}N_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}-s}N_{4}^{1-\theta}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

which is acceptable since
s>\frac{3}{2}-\theta
.

Subregion
C_{2}
:

N_{1}\leq N_{2}\ll N_{3}\sim N_{4}=N
.

In this region,
M_{min}=M_{3}
and

\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}
by
(
A.20
)
.
The bound for the low modulation part follows by using (
3.3
) and noticing that if
M_{3}\ll 1
, it holds

\min\{M_{3},(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}N_{1}^{-1}\lesssim M_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence,
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{2}}^{low}(t)|
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\leq N_{2}\ll N}\sum_{M_{3}\ll 1}

\displaystyle\min\{M_{3},(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}N_{1}^{-1}N^{2\sigma-2s+2-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{N_{2}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{\overline{Z^{\sigma}}}\|w_{N_{2}}\|_{\overline{Z^{\sigma}}}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

where we used the conditions
-\frac{1}{2}<\sigma
and
2\sigma-2s+2-\alpha<\alpha-1<0
.

To handle the high modulation term, where
N_{2}\gtrsim M_{3}\gtrsim 1
, we use that

\min\{M_{3},(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}N_{1}^{-1}M_{3}^{-1}\lesssim N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, we infer from (
3.10
) with
\theta=1
that
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{2}}^{high}(t)|
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\leq N_{2}\ll N}\sum_{1\lesssim M_{3}\lesssim N_{2}}\min\{M_{3},(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}M_{3}^{-1}N^{1-\alpha}N_{1}^{-1}N^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{N_{2}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\sum_{N_{1}\leq N_{2}\ll N\atop N_{2}\gtrsim 1}N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-\sigma}N_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}N^{2\sigma-2s+3-2\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|w_{N_{2}}\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

where we used the conditions
-\frac{1}{2}<\sigma
and
\sigma<s-\frac{3}{2}+\alpha
.

Subregion
C_{3}
:

N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{4}\sim N
.

Note that in this region, it holds
M_{min}=M_{2}\sim N_{3}
and
\Omega\sim N_{3}N^{\alpha}
. First, by using (
3.3
) and the basic bound
|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}
(see (
A.21
)), we bound
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{low}(t)|
by

\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll 1}(N_{1}N_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z\|_{Y^{0}}\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

where we used the conditions
2\sigma+\frac{5}{2}-\alpha
and
-\sigma<s
.

Estimate (
A.21
) is not sufficient to get a suitable bound for
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high}
without using the Strichartz norm in
Z^{\sigma}
, which would require a Coifmann-Meyer type estimate on the symbol

(see Theorem 2.2 in
[
41
]
). To avoid this technical difficulty, we use (
A.22
) to decompose

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,j}(t)

with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}_{1}}(w_{N_{1}},w_{\sim N},z_{N_{3}})z_{\sim N}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\chi}_{2}}(w_{N_{1}},w_{\sim N},z_{N_{3}})z_{\sim N}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,3}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{N_{1}}w_{\sim N}\Lambda^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}z_{N_{3}}z_{\sim N}

and where

is the Fourier multiplier by
\frac{\xi\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi)}
and the multipliers
\widetilde{\chi}_{j}
satisfy
\|\widetilde{\chi}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{1}^{2\sigma}N_{3}^{1-\alpha}
and
\|\widetilde{\chi}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}
on the support of the integral in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,j}
. To estimate
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,1}
, we apply (
3.10
) with
\theta=1/2
and deduce that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}(N_{1}N_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}N_{1}^{2\sigma}N_{3}^{1-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}\frac{N_{1}}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}N_{1}^{2\sigma+\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-\sigma}N_{3}^{-s+1-\alpha}N^{-s-\sigma+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|z\|_{Z^{s}}^{2},

which is acceptable since
-1/2<\sigma<s-\frac{3}{2}+\alpha
and
s+\sigma>\frac{1}{2}(1-\alpha)
.

To deal with
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,2}
, we argue as in the region
C
in the proof of Proposition
3.4
and apply (
3.10
) with
\theta=\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)_{-}\right\}\in]0,1]
, to bound
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,2}|
by

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}

\displaystyle(N_{1}N_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{-\theta}N^{\theta(1-\alpha)}N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\lesssim N_{3}\ll N}N_{3}^{-s+\frac{3}{2}-\theta}N^{\sigma-s+(1+\theta)(1-\alpha)}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

where we used that
\sigma<s-\frac{3}{2}+\alpha
and
s>1-\alpha
if
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
and
s>\frac{3}{2}-\theta
in the case
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
.

Finally, the term
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,3}
is bounded with the help of Proposition
3.5
and Bernstein inequality by

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{3}}^{high,3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N\atop N_{3}\gtrsim 1}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}N^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}\|\Lambda^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\lesssim N_{3}\ll N}N_{3}^{2\sigma-s+2-\frac{5\alpha}{4}}N^{-s-\sigma+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

which is acceptable since
s+\sigma>\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
and
\sigma-2s+\frac{5}{2}-\frac{7\alpha}{4}<-s+1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}<0
.

Subregion
C_{4}
:

N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{4}=N
.

In this region
M_{min}=M_{2}
, so that
\Omega=M_{2}N^{\alpha}
and
\|\widetilde{\chi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{1}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}
in view of (
A.23
). By using (
3.3
), we first note that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{4}}^{low}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}\ll N}\sum_{M_{2}\ll 1}M_{2}N_{1}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}N_{1}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}N^{-s-\sigma}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{-}}\|w\|_{\overline{Z}^{\sigma}}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

where we used that
2\sigma+1-\alpha>-1
if
N_{1}\lesssim 1
, and
s>1-\alpha
otherwise.
Finally, we apply estimate (
3.10
) with
\theta=1/2
combined with (
A.23
) to bound
|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{N,C_{4}}^{high}(t)|
by

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{3}\leq N_{1}\ll N}

\displaystyle\sum_{1\lesssim M_{2}\lesssim N_{1}}M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}N_{1}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{0}}\|w_{\sim N}\|_{Z^{0}}\|z_{N_{3}}\|_{Y^{0}}\|z_{\sim N}\|_{Y^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{0_{+}}\sum_{N_{3}\leq N_{1}\ll N\atop N_{1}\gtrsim 1}N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-s}N_{1}^{\sigma+1-\alpha}N^{-s-\sigma+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|w_{N_{1}}\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}.

Since

, this sum is bounded by

N^{0_{+}}\sum_{N_{3}\leq N_{1},\atop N_{1}\gtrsim 1}N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-s}N_{1}^{-s+\frac{3}{2}(1-\alpha)}\|w\|_{Z^{\sigma}}^{2}\|z\|_{Y^{s}}^{2},

which is acceptable for
s>\max\{\frac{3}{2}(1-\alpha),1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\}
. This completes the proof of (
5.11
).

Estimate of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

Thanks to Plancherel identity,
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
may be written as

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{4}_{t}}\widetilde{\widetilde{a}}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{4}\widehat{w}(\xi_{i})

with a symbol

\widetilde{\widetilde{a}}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

This term enjoys more symmetries than
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{1}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
, and the corresponding estimates will be very similar to the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
Performing a dyadic decomposition, we get
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{N\gtrsim N_{0}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
where

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}(t)=\sum_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},N_{4}\atop N_{max}=N}\sum_{M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}>0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}}\Pi_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\chi}}}(w_{N_{1}},w_{N_{2}},w_{N_{3}})w_{N_{4}}

with

\widetilde{\widetilde{\chi}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=\frac{i}{2}\phi_{M_{1}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\phi_{M_{2}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\phi_{M_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{\widetilde{a}}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

Moreover, as a consequence of Lemma
A.5
, we get that

(5.14)

\|\widetilde{\widetilde{\chi}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N_{min}^{-1}N^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}.

Again, we separate the low and high modulation frequencies corresponding respectively to
\Omega:=M_{min}M_{med}N^{\alpha-1}\ll N^{\alpha}
and
\Omega\gtrsim N^{\alpha}
, which leads to the decomposition
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{low}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{high}
.

From estimates (
3.3
) and (
5.14
) we infer

7.
The periodic case

In this section we explain the main changes in the periodic setting. Let us first define our function space in this setting. Let
\mathbb{T}
be the one dimensional torus.
First we define our function spaces in the periodic setting.
For a
2\pi
-periodic function
\varphi
, we define its space Fourier transform on

\mathbb{Z}
by

\hat{\varphi}(k)=\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}}e^{-i\xi x}\,f(x)\,dx,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}\quad.

The Lebesgue spaces
L^{q}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, for
2\pi
-periodic functions, will be defined as usually by

\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}}=\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\varphi(x)|^{q}\,dx\Bigr)^{1/q}

with the obvious modification for
q=\infty
.

The Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
2\pi
-periodic functions are endowed with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}}=\|\langle k\rangle^{s}\widehat{\varphi}(k)\|_{l^{2}_{k}}=\|J^{s}_{x}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\quad,

where
\langle\cdot\rangle=(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{1/2}
and
\widehat{J^{s}_{x}\varphi}(k)=\langle k\rangle^{s}\widehat{\varphi}(k)
.

In the same way, for a function
u(t,x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}
, we define its space-time Fourier transform by

\hat{u}(\tau,k)={\mathcal{F}}_{t,x}(u)(\tau,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-i(\tau t+kx)}\,u(t,x)\,dxdt,\quad\forall(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{Z}\quad.

For any
(s,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we define the Bourgain space
X^{s,b}
, of
2\pi
-periodic (in
x
) functions
as the completion of
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{T}\times{\mathbb{R}})
for the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}=\|\langle\tau-p_{\alpha+1}(k)\rangle^{b}\langle k\rangle^{s}\hat{u}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}l^{2}_{k}}\,.

Finally, we define the function
\phi_{N}
and
\psi_{L}

and the Fourier multipliers
P_{N}
and
Q_{L}
as in Subsection
2.1
. In the periodic setting it holds

(7.1)

u=P_{0}u+\sum_{N>0}P_{N}u

where
\widehat{P_{0}u}(\xi)=\widehat{u}(0)
.

In order to extend our results to the periodic case, the main difficulty is to obtain suitable refined Strichartz estimates, similar to those of Proposition
2.2
in the real line case. Recall that the main tool in the proof of this result is the classical linear Strichartz estimate

(7.2)

\|D_{x}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4}}U_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

which turns out to be false in the periodic settings. However, a careful reading of the proof of Proposition
2.2
in
[
41
]
shows that estimate (
7.2
) is only needed for short time intervals of length
N^{-1}
when the data
u_{0}
have spatial frequencies localized around the dyadic number
N
. Fortunately, such an estimate has been proved in the periodic case by Kenig, Ponce and Vega in Theorem 5.2 of
[
28
]
for the case where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\alpha>1
. We observed however that their proof adapts well to our general setting.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and
N\geq\xi_{0}
be a dyadic number, where
\xi_{0}
is defined in Hypothesis
1
. Then for any time interval
I
satisfying
|I|\lesssim N^{-\alpha}
, it holds

(7.3)

\|P_{N}D_{x}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4}}U_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{4}_{I}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

As mentioned above, the proof of Proposition
7.2
follows closely the one of Theorem 5.2 in
[
28
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we give the details below.
We will need the following lemma due to Zygmund.

Lemma 7.2

(
[
57
]
, p. 198)
.

Let
f
be a real-valued function defined on an interval
(a,b)
such that
f^{\prime}
is monotone and
|f^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then, we have

\left|\sum_{k\in(a,b)}e^{2\pi if(k)}-\int_{a}^{b}e^{2\pi if(\xi)}d\xi\right|\lesssim 1,

where the implicit constant is independent of
a<b
and
f
.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Since
U_{\alpha}(t)
is a unitary group in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}
), we may always assume that
I=]0,T[
with
T\lesssim N^{-\alpha}
. Using the classical duality P. Thomas argument, (
7.3
) will follows from estimate

\|P_{N}D_{x}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4}}U_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

for all
t\in I
. From Young and Bernstein inequalities, it will suffice to prove that

\left|\sum_{k\sim N}e^{i(t\omega_{\alpha+1}(k)+kx)}\right|\lesssim N^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for any
t\in I
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
. Setting
\varphi(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(t\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi)+x\xi)
, we get from Hypothesis
1
that
\varphi^{\prime}
is a continuous monotonic function on
D_{N}:=\{\xi\sim N\}
. Moreover, it holds that
|\varphi^{\prime}(\xi)|\lesssim 1+tN^{\alpha}\lesssim 1
for all
t\in I
, where we use the assumption that
0<t<T\lesssim N^{-\alpha}
. Therefore, there exist a subdivision
D_{N}=\cup_{j=1}^{n}[\xi_{j},\xi_{j+1}]
with
n\lesssim 1
and integers
|p_{1}|,\ldots,|p_{n}|\lesssim 1
such that
|\varphi^{\prime}(\xi)-p_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all
\xi\in[\xi_{j},\xi_{j+1}]
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. It follows then that

\left|\sum_{k\sim N}e^{2\pi i\varphi(k)}\right|=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k\in(\xi_{j},\xi_{j+1})}e^{2\pi i\varphi(k)}\right|\leq n\sup_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{k\in(\xi_{j},\xi_{j+1})}e^{2\pi i(\varphi(k)-kp_{j})}\right|.

By virtue of Lemma
7.2
, we infer

\left|\sum_{k\sim N}e^{2\pi i\varphi(k)}\right|\lesssim 1+\sup_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j+1}}e^{2\pi if(\xi)}d\xi\right|

where
f(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)-p_{j}\xi
. Observing that
|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\sim tN^{\alpha-1}
, we conclude thanks to Van der Corput lemma that

\left|\sum_{k\sim N}e^{2\pi i\varphi(k)}\right|\lesssim 1+(tN^{\alpha-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}},

which finishes the proof of Proposition
7.1
.
∎

With this proposition in hand, the rest of the proof is similar to, and even simpler than, the case of the real line, since we do not need to worry about the low-frequency contributions. Indeed, as usually, we notice that
u
is solution to (
1.1
) then its spatial mean value
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hskip-11.38109pt- \, u
is a conserved quantity. Therefore
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) if and only if
\tilde{u}(t,x)=u(t,x-t\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hskip-11.38109pt- \,u_{0})-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hskip-11.38109pt- \, u_{0}
is a solution to (
1.1
) with
\Lambda_{\alpha+1}
replaced by
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\alpha+1}=\Lambda_{\alpha+1}-(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hskip-11.38109pt- \, u_{0})\partial_{x}
. Since
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\alpha+1}
satisfies Hypothesis
1
as soon as
\Lambda_{\alpha+1}
does (with possibly a different
\xi_{0}
), by this change of unknown, we can always reduce to initial data with mean value zero. We can thus avoid to add a weight on the spatial low frequencies in the function space where we put the difference of two solutions. In this framework we get the same estimates than those on the solutions and on the difference of solutions, having the same mean value, obtained in Section
4
and
5
. Finally the proofs in Section
6
are exactly the same. We only have to care about the fact that we can not assume the sequence
\{u_{0}^{j}\}
to be mean-value zero in Subsection
6.4
since the mean-value of
u_{0}^{j}
may depend on
j
. However, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{0}^{j}
and
\Pi_{\leq 2^{n}}u_{0}^{j}
have the same mean value and thus (
6.16
) still holds. Then (
6.17
) follows as in the real line case since Proposition
6.1
is still valid in the periodic setting (cf
[
1
]
).

Appendix A
Symbol estimates

A.1.
Estimates for the resonance function

For
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Gamma^{n}
, the resonance function of order
n
associated with (
1.1
) is defined by

\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{i})

where
\omega(\xi)
satisfies Hypothesis
1
.
Note that
\Omega_{n}
is symmetric:

(A.1)

\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\Omega_{n}(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\sigma(n)})\quad\textrm{ for any permutation }\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}.

Moreover, it satisfies

(A.2)

\Omega_{n}(-(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}))=-\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}).

A.1.1.
The case
n=3

We recall the well-known result:

Proposition A.1
.

[Lemma 2.1 in
[
46
]
]
Let
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\in\Gamma^{3}
and
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
be dyadic numbers such that
N_{i}\sim|\xi_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3
. Assume moreover
N_{max}\gg\xi_{0}
. Then

(A.3)

|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\sim N_{min}N_{max}^{\alpha}

where
N_{min}=\min_{i=1,2,3}N_{i}
and
N_{max}=\max_{i=1,2,3}N_{i}
.

A.1.2.
The case
n=4

We will prove the following estimate:

Proposition A.2
.

Let
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})\in\Gamma^{4}
and
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},N_{4}
and
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}
be dyadic numbers such that
N_{i}\sim|\xi_{i}|
for

and
M_{1}\sim|\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}|
,
M_{2}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|
,
M_{3}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|
. Assume moreover
N_{max}\gg\xi_{0}
. Then we have

(A.4)

|\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})|\sim M_{min}M_{med}N_{max}^{\alpha-1}

with the usual notation.

Proof.

We use the notation
N_{min}\leq N_{thd}\leq N_{sub}\leq N_{max}
to denote
N_{1},\ldots,N_{4}
and recall that
N_{sub}\sim N_{max}\sim M_{max}
. We split the proof into three parts.

Case
N_{thd}\ll N_{max}
. By (
A.1
), we may assume

N_{1},N_{2}\ll N_{3}\sim N_{4},

which implies
\xi_{3}\xi_{4}<0
. In this case we have
M_{min}=M_{3}
and
M_{med}\sim N_{max}
.

From the mean value theorem, we infer

|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})+\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{2})|=|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(-\xi_{2})|=|(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi^{\ast})|\sim M_{3}|\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi^{\ast})|

with
|\xi^{\ast}|\lesssim N_{1}\vee N_{2}\ll N_{max}
. Therefore,

(A.5)

|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})+\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{2})|\ll M_{3}N_{max}^{\alpha}.

In the same way, since
\xi_{3}\xi_{4}<0
we can find
\xi^{\ast}
with
|\xi^{\ast}|\sim N_{max}
such that

(A.6)

|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{3})+\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{4})|=|(\xi_{3}+\xi_{4})\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi^{\ast})|\sim M_{3}N_{max}^{\alpha}.

Hence, (
A.4
) is deduced by combining (
A.5
) and (
A.6
).

Case
N_{min}\ll N_{thd}\sim N_{max}
. By (
A.1
), we may assume

N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N_{4}.

It follows that
M_{min}\sim M_{max}\sim N_{4}
. Then, rewrite
\Omega_{4}
as

\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})=\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+\Omega_{3}(\xi_{3},\xi_{4}).

From Proposition
A.1
, we get
|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\sim N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha}\ll N_{4}^{\alpha+1}
and
|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{3},\xi_{4})|\sim N_{4}^{\alpha+1}
. Estimate (
A.4
) follows immediately.

Case
N_{min}\sim N_{max}
. We separate 2 subcases.

\bullet
3 of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{4}
are of the same sign
. From (
A.1
)-(
A.2
), we may assume
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}>0
and
\xi_{4}<0
. Then rewrite
\Omega_{4}
as

\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})=\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+\Omega_{3}(\xi_{3},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}).

At this point, we remark that both terms in the above sum have the same sign. Since they both are of size
N_{4}^{\alpha+1}
, we conclude

|\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})|\sim N_{max}^{\alpha+1},

To show that both terms in the RHS of (
A.7
) have the same sign, it suffices to prove that

(A.8)

\forall\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>\xi_{0},\quad\mathop{\rm sign}\nolimits(\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}))=-\mathop{\rm sign}\nolimits(\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})).

Indeed, we rewrite
-\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
as

(A.9)

\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{2}}\int_{0}^{\xi_{1}}\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\theta+\mu)d\mu d\theta+\left(\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{0})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{0})\right).

On the one hand, by the mean value theorem and Hypothesis
1

\left|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{0})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{0})\right|\leq N_{max}^{\alpha}|\xi_{0}|\ll N_{max}^{\alpha+1}.

On the other hand, by Hypothesis
1
,
\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}
is continuous outside of

and
|\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\sim|\xi|^{\alpha-1}
, so that
\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)
does not change sign for
\xi>\xi_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we assume for instance
\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)>0
. Thus,

\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{2}}\int_{0}^{\xi_{1}}\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\theta+\mu)d\mu d\theta\sim\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{2}}\int_{0}^{\xi_{1}}(\theta+\mu)^{\alpha-1}d\mu d\theta\sim N_{max}^{\alpha+1},

which, combined with (
A.9

A.8
).

\bullet
2 of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{4}
are of the same sign, and the 2 others are of the opposite sign
. From (
A.1
)-(
A.2
), we may assume
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>0

\{M_{min},M_{med}\}=\{M_{1},M_{2}\}
. To prove (
A.4
), we notice that

\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})=-\int_{0}^{\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}}\int_{-\xi_{3}}^{\xi_{1}}\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\theta+\mu)d\mu d\theta

and it suffices to show that

.

If

and

If

and

.

The other cases follow by symmetry since
\int_{a}^{b}\int_{b}^{c}f=\int_{b}^{a}\int_{c}^{b}f

∎

\displaystyle a_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})

\displaystyle=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})[b_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})-b_{3}(-\xi_{3},-\xi_{4})]

\displaystyle\quad+(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})[b_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})-b_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})]

\displaystyle\quad+(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})[b_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{3})-b_{3}(-\xi_{2},-\xi_{4})]

\displaystyle=:a_{44}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})+a_{45}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})+a_{46}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4}).

From the symmetry in
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
, it suffices to estimate
a_{44}
and
a_{45}
. Using the definition of
b_{3}
we may decompose

b_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})-b_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})=a_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})}-\frac{1}{\Omega_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})}\right)+\frac{a_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})-a_{3}(-\xi_{1},\xi_{4})}{\Omega_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})}

Then, notice that
\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})-\Omega_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})=\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
, therefore we may take advantage of Lemma
A.2
to infer

\left|a_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})}-\frac{1}{\Omega_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})}\right)\right|\lesssim M_{1}N^{2s}\frac{M_{1}M_{2}N^{\alpha-1}}{(M_{1}N^{\alpha})^{2}}\lesssim M_{2}N^{2s-1-\alpha}.

Similarly, since the bounds for
a_{3}
are the exact same than those for
\Omega_{3}
with
\alpha
replaced with
2s
, we get

\left|\frac{a_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})-a_{3}(-\xi_{1},\xi_{4})}{\Omega_{3}(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{4})}\right|\lesssim\frac{M_{1}M_{2}N^{2s-1}}{M_{1}N^{\alpha}}\lesssim M_{2}N^{2s-1-\alpha}.

Gathering the above estimates we deduce
|a_{45}|\lesssim M_{1}M_{2}N^{2s-1-\alpha}
.
Finally, the bound for
a_{44}
is obtained in the same way:

\displaystyle|a_{44}|

\displaystyle\lesssim N\left(\left|a_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\frac{\Omega_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\Omega_{3}(\xi_{3},\xi_{4})}\right|+\left|\frac{a_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+a_{3}(\xi_{3},\xi_{4})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{3},\xi_{4})}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim N\left(N^{2s+1}\frac{M_{1}M_{2}N^{\alpha-1}}{N^{2(1+\alpha)}}+\frac{M_{1}M_{2}N^{2s-1}}{N^{1+\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{1}M_{2}N^{2s-\alpha-1}.

∎

A.3.
Estimates for
\widetilde{a}_{k}

A.3.1.
Estimates for
\widetilde{b}_{3}

Recall that for
N_{0}\gg 1
,
\widetilde{a}_{3}
is defined by

\widetilde{a_{3}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=(\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi_{1})+\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi_{2})){\bf 1}_{|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|>N_{0}},

where
\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi)=\xi|\xi|^{2\sigma}{\bf 1}_{|\xi|>1}
and that
\widetilde{b}_{3}=\widetilde{a}_{3}/\Omega_{3}
. It is worth noticing that
\widetilde{a}_{3}
vanishes unless
\max(|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|)\gg 1
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\in\Gamma^{3}
and
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
be dyadic numbers such that
N_{i}\sim|\xi_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3
.

(1)

If
N_{1}\sim N_{2}
, one has

(A.13)

|\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\lesssim N_{max}^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

(2)

If
N_{1}\ll N_{2}
or
N_{2}\ll N_{1}
, one has

(A.14)

|\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\lesssim N_{min}^{-1}N_{max}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

Proof.

We may always assume
N_{max}\gg 1
. In the case
N_{3}\sim N_{min}
then this forces
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{max}\gg 1
. Splitting the cases
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}>0
and
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}<0
, and using the mean value theorem in the latter case, we find that
|\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\sim N_{3}N^{2\sigma}
. Estimate (
A.13
) follows from this and (
A.3
). In the same way, for
N_{3}\sim N_{max}
we easily get
|\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\sim N_{max}^{2\sigma+1}
and we may conclude (
A.14
) with (
A.3
).
∎

The following lemma will also be useful.

Lemma A.6
.

With the notations of Lemma
A.5
, if
N_{1}\ll N_{2}
, then

(A.15)

|\partial^{(0,1)}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{2}^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

Proof.

Noticing that
|\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\xi_{2})|\sim N_{2}^{2\sigma}
and
|\partial^{(0,1)}\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\sim N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha-1}
thanks to the mean value theorem, we get using the classical derivative rules that

\displaystyle|\partial^{(0,1)}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\xi_{2})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}-\frac{\left(\partial^{(0,1)}\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\right)\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}{(\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}))^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{N_{2}^{2\sigma}}{N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha}}+\frac{N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha-1}N_{2}^{2\sigma+1}}{(N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha})^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{2}^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

∎

A.3.2.
Estimates for
\widetilde{a}_{4}

In this section we prove the following lemma.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})\in\Gamma^{4}
and
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},N_{4}
and
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}
be dyadic numbers such that
N_{i}\sim|\xi_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3,4
and
M_{1}\sim|\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}|
,
M_{2}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|
,
M_{3}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|
. From the symmetry for
\widetilde{a}_{4}
we assume
|\xi_{1}|\leq|\xi_{2}|
and
|\xi_{3}|\leq|\xi_{4}|
. Then, for any
\sigma\in(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2})
, the following estimates for
\widetilde{a}_{4}
hold true.

(1)

Region
A
: If
N_{min}\sim N_{max}
,

(A.16)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{max}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

(2)

Region
B
:

.

(a)

Sub-region
B_{1}
: If
N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N_{4}
,

(A.17)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{max}^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}.

(b)

Sub-region
B_{2}
: If
N_{3}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{4}
,

(A.18)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{max}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

(3)

Region
C
:
N_{thd}\ll N_{max}
.

(a)

Sub-region
C_{1}
: If
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gg N_{4}\gtrsim N_{3}
,

(A.19)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{4}N_{max}^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

(b)

Sub-region
C_{2}
: If
N_{1}\lesssim N_{2}\ll N_{3}\sim N_{4}
,

(A.20)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{max}^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}.

(c)

Sub-region
C_{3}
: If
N_{1}\ll N_{3}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{4}
,

(A.21)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}.

Moreover, we have

(A.22)

\left|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})+\frac{\xi_{3}\nu_{\sigma}(\xi_{3})}{\xi_{1}\omega^{\prime}(\xi_{3})}\right|\lesssim N_{1}^{2\sigma}N_{3}^{1-\alpha}+N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

(d)

Sub-region
C_{4}
: If
N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{4}
,

(A.23)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{1}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

Remark A.8
.

(i)

For the region
C_{2}
in the particular case
M_{3}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}
, we have the simpler bound

|\widetilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{2}^{-1}M_{3}N_{max}^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}.

However, estimate (
A.20
) is good enough for our purpose.

(ii)

In the resonant region
A
, we have the better bound

(A.24)

|\widetilde{a}_{4}|\lesssim M_{3}N_{max}^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

However, we will not use this bound in our energy estimate.

We will extensively use Lemma
A.5
to bound
\widetilde{a}_{4}
.
Recalling the definition of
\widetilde{a}_{4}
in (
5.13
), we also decompose

(A.25)

\widetilde{a}_{41}=\widetilde{a}_{411}+\widetilde{a}_{412}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{a}_{42}=\widetilde{a}_{421}+\widetilde{a}_{423}

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{411}

\displaystyle=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2});

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{412}

\displaystyle=-(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1});

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{421}

\displaystyle=(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1});

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{422}

\displaystyle=-(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})\widetilde{b_{3}}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{2}).

Region
A
:
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N_{4}=:N

Since
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|\lesssim|\xi_{1}|\sim|\xi_{2}|
Lemma
A.5
provides

\displaystyle|\widetilde{a}_{41}|

\displaystyle\leq|(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})|+|(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

Similarly, we obtain the same bound for
|\widetilde{a}_{42}|+|\widetilde{a}_{43}|\lesssim N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}
.

Region
B_{1}
:
N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N_{4}=:N

In this region, we have

. Therefore we get on one hand

and on the other hand

|\widetilde{a}_{412}|+|\widetilde{a}_{421}|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N^{2\sigma+2-\alpha}.

Then, (
A.17
) follows immediately.

Region
B_{2}
:
N_{3}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{4}=:N

Again, we have

and every term
\widetilde{a}_{4ij}
,

, as well as

.

Region

:
N_{3}\lesssim N_{4}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}=:N

In
C_{1}
it holds that
M_{3}\lesssim N_{4}\ll M_{1}\sim M_{2}\sim N
. First, we deduce from (
A.13
) that

(A.26)

|\widetilde{a}_{43}|=|(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\lesssim M_{3}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

Using the symmetry in
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
it remains to estimate

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{411}+\widetilde{a}_{421}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}[\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})]

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{3}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\xi_{4}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(1)}+\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(2)}+\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(3)}.

From (
A.13
) we get

(A.27)

|\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(2)}|+|\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(3)}|\lesssim(N_{3}+N_{4})N^{2\sigma-\alpha}\lesssim N_{4}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

Then, we may rewrite
\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(1)}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\left(\frac{\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})}-\frac{\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\Big(\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})}-\frac{1}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})}\Big)

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(11)}+\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(12)}.

Using twice Taylor formula we deduce

\displaystyle|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})-\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle=|\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{2})+\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1})-\omega_{\alpha+1}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi_{3}\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi_{2})-\xi_{3}\omega_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi_{1})|+N_{3}^{2}N^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{3}M_{3}N^{\alpha-1}+N_{3}^{2}N^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{4}^{2}N^{\alpha-1}.

It follows that

(A.28)

|\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(11)}|\lesssim NM_{3}N^{2\sigma}\frac{N_{4}^{2}N^{\alpha-1}}{(N_{4}N^{\alpha})^{2}}\lesssim N_{4}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

Similarly, recalling that
\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}(\xi)=\xi_{|}\xi|^{2\sigma}{\bf 1}_{|\xi|\geq 1}
and
2\sigma+1>0
we obtain

\displaystyle|\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},\xi_{2})-\widetilde{a}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi_{4}\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\xi_{1})-\xi_{4}\widetilde{\nu}_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\xi_{2})|+N_{4}^{2}N^{2\sigma-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{4}M_{3}N^{2\sigma-1}+N_{4}^{2}N^{2\sigma-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{4}^{2}N^{2\sigma-1},

from which we conclude

(A.29)

|\widetilde{a}_{411}^{(12)}|\lesssim N\frac{N_{4}^{2}N^{2\sigma-1}}{N_{4}N^{\alpha}}\lesssim N_{4}N^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

Gathering (
A.26
)-(
A.27
)-(
A.28
)-(
A.29

.

Region

:

Thanks to (

:

). Note that in

, we have

. According to Lemma
A.5

|\widetilde{a}_{411}|+|\widetilde{a}_{422}|\lesssim N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}.

For

, we split

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{412}

\displaystyle=-\xi_{3}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})+\mathcal{O}(N_{3}^{2\sigma+1-\alpha})

\displaystyle=-\xi_{3}\frac{\nu_{\sigma}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})-\nu_{\sigma}(\xi_{1})}{\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3},-\xi_{1})}+\mathcal{O}(N_{1}^{2\sigma}N_{3}^{1-\alpha}).

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(N_{1}^{-1}N_{3}N^{2\sigma+1-\alpha}).

C_{4}
:
N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{4}=:N

From Lemma

\displaystyle=\xi_{2}[\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})-\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})]+[\xi_{3}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},\xi_{1})-\xi_{1}\widetilde{b}_{3}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})]

A.6
. This concludes the proof of (
A.23
).

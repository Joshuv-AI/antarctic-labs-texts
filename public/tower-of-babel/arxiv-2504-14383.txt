Exotic periodic phenomena in the cohomology of the moduli stack of 1 1 -dimensional formal group laws Thanks: The first author was supported by NSF grant DMS-2202267.
Dan Isaksen, Hana Jia Kong, Guchuan Li, Yangyang Ruan, Heyi Zhu

Abstract
. We describe some periodic structure in the cohomology of the moduli stack of 1-dimensional formal group laws, also known as the E 2 E_{2} -page of the classical Adams–Novikov spectral sequence. This structure is distinct from the familiar v n v_{n} -periodicities, and it displays interesting number-theoretic properties. Our techniques involve the ℂ \mathbb{C} -motivic Adams spectral sequence, and we obtain analogous periodic structure in ℂ \mathbb{C} -motivic stable homotopy.

1.1.
The cohomology of the moduli stack of 1-dimensional formal group laws

The moduli stack of 1-dimensional formal group laws is an object of interest in number theory. The cohomology of this moduli stack is also known as the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page, which is of interest to homotopy theorists because it is an approximation to stable homotopy via the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence.

The situation is well-illustrated by
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy. In stems congruent to 7 modulo 8,
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy is detected in the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page in filtration
1
. (In stems congruent to 3 modulo 8, Adams–Novikov differentials and hidden extensions occur.) In other words,
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy is detected by the cohomology of the moduli stack of 1-dimensional formal group laws. This observation is intended to “explain” the appearance of number-theoretic structure in
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy. Following this logic, we should not be surprised to find other regular patterns in the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page that also display number-theoretic structure. This manuscript describes such a regular pattern.

Long experience shows that the information in the stable homotopy groups of spheres can be sorted into separate periodic families. It is a deep and central fact of classical stable homotopy theory that the only such
2
-primary periodicities are
v_{n}
for
n\geq 0
. The traditional
v_{n}
-periodicities appear along horizontal lines in the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page. However, the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page possesses additional algebraic periodicities
[
Kra18
]
. We study a particular family of elements in the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page that exhibits an “exotic” periodicity. Our elements lie along lines of slope
\frac{1}{5}
in the Adams–Novikov
E_{2}
-page.

1.2.
Motivation from topological modular forms

One of the many roles of the topological modular forms spectrum
\mathit{tmf}
in stable homotopy theory is that it serves as a testing ground for computational methods and strategies. If a method computes the homotopy of
\mathit{tmf}
efficiently, then it is likely to be of use for the much more difficult sphere spectrum.

The manuscript
[
IKL+24
]
studies
\mathit{tmf}
, and also its
\mathbb{C}
-motivic analogue
\mathit{mmf}
, from a
g
-periodic perspective, where
g
is a particular element in the 20-stem. The
g
-periodic structure of
\mathit{mmf}
reveals certain information that is otherwise difficult to obtain. Therefore, we expect that a
g
-periodic perspective on the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic sphere is also interesting.

In this manuscript, building on previous work of Michael Andrews
[
And18
]
, we investigate
g
-periodic phenomena in the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic sphere spectrum. We find a very rich structure, analogous to the number-theoretically interesting
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy, but quantifiably distinct from
v_{1}
-periodic phenomena. In fact, our results only scratch the surface of a vastly more complicated structure.

However, the

In order to summarize our results, we will refer to the motivic coweight, which is the difference of the stem and the weight. The coweight is relevant because
\eta

\eta

We describe non-zero

-motivic stable homotopy elements in coweights that are congruent to 3 modulo 4. We start with one previously identified element in each coweight that is annihilated by
\eta

\eta

2
-torsion element in a cyclic

-periodic stable homotopy group, and then dividing by
2

In many (but not all) coweights, we determine exactly how many times our
\eta
-torsion elements can be divided by
\eta

\mathbb{Z}[\eta]/(M,2\eta,\eta^{N})
inside the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic stable homotopy groups

\eta

\eta
-exponents, i.e., the smallest value of

\eta^{N}
annihilates the element.

Starting with coweight 3, we obtain the following

\infty,3,2,7,2,6,2,15,2,6,2,12,2,6,2,31,2,6,2,12,2,6,2,?,2,6,2,12,2,6,2,63,\ldots.

1.8.
The Burklund–Xu spectral sequence

Our main algebraic tool is the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence
[
BX25
]
. This spectral sequence was originally intended to compute the
E_{2}
-page of the Cartan–Eilenberg spectral sequence associated to the extension

\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{1}^{2},\zeta_{2}^{2},\ldots]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots]\rightarrow E(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots),

where the middle object is the classical dual Steenrod algebra, and the quotient object on the right is an exterior algebra. In order to obtain information about the Adams
E_{2}
-page, one must additionally study the Cartan–Eilenberg differentials.

Later we will be particularly interested in elements of Chow degree one, i.e., elements in degree
(s,f,w)
such that
s+f-2w=1
. Here
s
,
f
, and
w
are the stem, Adams filtration, and motivic weight respectively.

It turns out that for elementary degree reasons, there are no possible
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg differentials in Chow degree one. Consequently, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence converges all the way to the Adams
E_{2}
-page in Chow degree one.

The Burklund–Xu spectral sequence is a remarkably powerful tool for studying the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Adams
E_{2}
-page in Chow degree one. One may reasonably ask about the significance of computations in Chow degree one. In our case, there is a particular family of products
h_{0}h_{2}\cdot h_{2}g^{k}
in degrees
(20k+6,4k+3,12k+4)
of relevance to our study of
w_{1}
-periodic homotopy. We use the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence to show that all of these products are non-zero.

We draw attention to
Figure

7
and
Figure

8
, which display the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence in Chow degree one in a range, including all differentials and some hidden extensions in its
E_{\infty}
-page. These charts demonstrate that the spectral sequence is computable in practice.

The related article
[
IKL+25
]
offers another demonstration of the power of the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence. It studies another infinite family of elements in Chow degree one.

Background

Regarding gradings,

•

in motivic bigraded contexts, such as in

-motivic stable homotopy, all elements are graded in the form
(s,w)
, where
s
is the topological stem and

is the motivic weight.

•

in motivic trigraded contexts, such as in the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Adams
E_{2}
-page, all elements are graded in the form
(s,f,w)
, where

is the Adams filtration.

•

in classical bigraded contexts, such as the classical Adams
E_{2}
-page, all elements are graded in the form
(s,f)
.

•

the Chow degree of a trigraded element is
s+f-2w
, and the Chow degree of a motivic bigraded element is
s-2w
.

•

we also use the motivic coweight, which is defined to be
s-w

•

for an element in stem

-intercept is the number

-intercept is the

Let

be an element of a (typically graded) commutative

-algebra

An

is

-submodule of

-multiplication. We are primarily interested in cyclic

-submodules that are generated by a single element.

In general, there are many isomorphism types of cyclic

-submodules, such as

However, in practice we will only consider elements

such that

-submodule, but we will generally not study

. We refer to

as the

-exponent

-submodule.

Let

-torsion elements. This ambiguity is no problem for us. In practice, our arguments apply equally well to any choice of

The element

is the unique non-zero

-torsion element inside of a largest cyclic

-submodule

and

. We refer to

of

Lemma 4.2
.

No element of the form
\frac{\overline{w_{1}^{4k}}}{h_{1}^{n}}
or
\frac{\overline{w_{1}^{4k+2}}}{h_{1}^{n}}
is hit by an Adams differential.

Proof.

All of these elements have Chow degree zero. The Adams
d_{r}
differential increases the Chow degree by
r-1
. The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Adams
E_{2}
-page is zero in negative Chow degrees by
2.3
, so all differentials entering Chow degree zero are null.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

No element of the form
\frac{\overline{w_{1}^{4k}}}{h_{1}^{n}}
or
\frac{\overline{w_{1}^{4k+2}}}{h_{1}^{n}}
is the target of a hidden
\eta
-extension.

Proof.

Each element under consideration has Chow degree zero, so it has a degree of the form
\left(s,f,\frac{s+f}{2}\right)
for some
s
and
f
. If that element were the target of a hidden
\eta
extension, then the source of the extension would have degree
\left(s-1,f^{\prime},\frac{s+f}{2}-1\right)
, where
f^{\prime}<f-1
. Then the Chow degree of this source would be
f^{\prime}-f+1
, which is negative. The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Adams
E_{2}
-page is zero in negative Chow degrees by
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

No element of the form
\frac{\overline{w_{1}^{4k}}}{h_{1}^{n}}
or
\frac{\overline{w_{1}^{4k+2}}}{h_{1}^{n}}
is the target of a (hidden or non-hidden)
\tau
-extension.

Proof.

Each element under consideration has Chow degree zero, so it has a degree of the form
\left(s,f,\frac{s+f}{2}\right)
for some
s
and
f
. If that element were the target of a
\tau

\left(s,f^{\prime},\frac{s+f}{2}+1\right)

precisely when the

, which is negative. The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Adams
E_{2}
-page is zero in negative Chow degrees by
2.3
.

[

5.
The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence

Having stated the main theorems of this manuscript, we now start on the technical work required to compute the coexponents that are given in
4.7
and
4.11
.

In this section, we describe the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic version of the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence. The classical version of this spectral sequence is described in detail in
[
BX25
]
and
[
Ben24
]
.

The
E_{1}
-page of the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence takes the form

(5.1)

\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tau][q_{0},q_{1},\ldots]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{***}\mathcal{B}.

Recall from
Section

2
that the dual
\mathcal{B}_{**}
of
\mathcal{B}
is the subalgebra

\mathcal{B}_{**}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots]

of the dual
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{**}
. Also,
2.1
tells us that
H^{***}\mathcal{B}
is isomorphic to
H^{**}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, but beware that this isomorphism shifts degrees as in
Equation

2.2
.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that
\mathcal{B}
is similar to the construction
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{mot}}
of
[
BX25
, Construction 2.7]
. The difference is that we do not include
\tau
in
\mathcal{B}
.

The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence converges to the
E_{2}
-page of the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg spectral sequence, which further converges to
H^{***}\mathcal{A}
. However, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic and classical versions of the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence are essentially identical. After a careful inspection of degrees, one finds that the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu differentials involve no powers of
\tau
. In effect, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence is merely the classical Burklund–Xu spectral sequence tensored over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tau]
.

In this manuscript, we are only interested in a small portion of the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence. We will study only the part of the spectral sequence in Chow degrees zero and one. As observed in
[
Ben24
, Proposition 10]
, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence splits as a direct sum of its subobjects in each fixed Chow degree. In terms of
Equation

5.1
, the Chow degree is determined by the facts that each
q_{i}
has Chow degree
1
;
\tau
has Chow degree
2
; and
H^{***}\mathcal{B}
is concentrated in Chow degree zero.

5.1.
The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence in Chow degree zero

First we study the Chow degree zero part of the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence. This part of the
E_{1}
-page is equal to

1^{BX}\cdot H^{***}\mathcal{B}.

We use the notation
1^{BX}\cdot(-)
to emphasize that we are considering elements of the Burklund–Xu
E_{1}
-page. This helps to disambiguate between multiple interpretations of the same symbol.

Remark 5.3
.

Recall from
[
Ben24
, Proposition 15]
that the Burklund–Xu differentials are
H^{***}\mathcal{B}
-linear, in the sense that

(5.4)

d_{r}(y\cdot x)=d_{r}(y)\cdot x

for all
y
in the
E_{r}
-page and all
x
in
H^{***}\mathcal{B}
.

2.1
was originally proved in
[
Isa19
, Theorem 2.19]
. We provide an alternative proof using the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence. This new proof is arguably more complicated than the simple observation that
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and
\mathcal{B}
are isomorphic as Hopf algebras. We offer this proof for its consistency with the techniques used elsewhere in this manuscript.

Proof of
2.1
.

One consequence of
Equation

5.4
is that elements of the form
1^{BX}\cdot x
are permanent cycles in the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence
[
Ben24
, Proposition 15]
. Therefore, the spectral sequence collapses in Chow degree zero.

Moreover, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg spectral sequence in Chow degree zero also collapses. There are two main points to this observation. First,
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg differentials preserve Chow degree, so we need only consider differentials whose sources and targets are both in Chow degree zero. Second,
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg differentials take values in multiples of
\tau

[
BX25
, Theorem 2.8]
, and there are no multiples of
\tau
in Chow degree zero.

Hence, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu
E_{1}
-page in Chow degree zero is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg
E_{2}
-page in Chow degree zero, which is isomorphic to the part of
H^{***}\mathcal{A}
in Chow degree zero.
∎

5.2.
The
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence in Chow degree one

We now study the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence in Chow degree one in more detail.

Proposition 5.5
.

There is an isomorphism from the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg
E_{2}
-page in Chow degree one to the part of
H^{***}\mathcal{A}
in Chow degree one.

Proof.

As in the proof of
2.1
just above,
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Cartan–Eilenberg differentials take values in multiples of
\tau
. Therefore, the values of such differentials have Chow degree of at least
2
.
∎

After restricting to Chow degree one, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence takes the form

(5.6)

\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}q_{i}\cdot H^{***}\mathcal{B}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H^{***}\mathcal{A}\big|_{s+f-2w=1}.

The
E_{1}
-page consists of a sum of copies of
H^{***}\mathcal{B}
. Because of
5.5
, the spectral sequence converges all the way to the part of
H^{***}\mathcal{A}
that lies in Chow degree one.

Because of
2.1
, we find it useful to adopt classical notation for the cohomology of
\mathcal{B}
. This can be confusing at first, but we find that it helps us carry out the computations and simplifies formulas in the long run. Our spectral sequence now takes the form

(5.7)

\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}q_{i}\cdot H^{**}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H^{***}\mathcal{A}\big|_{s+f-2w=1}.

Moreover, we change the gradings on the spectral sequence to be compatible with this use of classical notation. In the usual motivic grading, our spectral sequence only has elements in degrees of the form
\left(s,f,\frac{s+f-1}{2}\right)
because it is concentrated in Chow degree one. Such elements are now placed in degree
\left(\frac{s-f+1}{2},f\right)
in our modified grading.

This change of grading has the following practical effects. First, the elements
q_{i}
have degree
(2^{i+1}-2,1,2^{i}-1)
in the traditional motivic grading. In our new grading, these elements now have degree
(2^{i}-1,1)
. Second, elements of
H^{**}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}
now have their familiar degrees.

.

.

Proposition 9.1

-motivic

\frac{\mathbb{F}_{2}[h_{1},h_{2},u,g]}{h_{1}h_{2},h_{2}^{3},h_{2}u,u^{2}+h_{1}^{2}g}.

\frac{\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tau][\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}{\tau_{0}^{2}+\tau\xi_{1},\xi_{1}^{4},\tau_{1}^{2}+\tau\xi_{2},\xi_{2}^{2},\tau_{2}^{2}}

Lemma 9.3
.

In the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Burklund–Xu spectral sequence for the cohomology of
A(2)
in Chow degree one, there are no hidden
h_{0}
extensions. The only hidden
h_{1}
extensions are from
q_{2}\cdot P^{k}a
to
q_{0}\cdot P^{k+1}
.

Proof.

For degree reasons, there are no possible hidden
h_{0}
extensions, and the only possible hidden
h_{1}
extensions are from
q_{2}\cdot P^{k}a
to
q_{0}\cdot P^{k+1}
.

Recall the Massey product
\langle a,h_{1},h_{1}^{2}\rangle=Ph_{0}
in
H^{**}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}(1)
. Then there is a similar Massey product
\langle q_{2}\cdot a,h_{1},h_{1}^{2}\rangle=q_{2}\cdot Ph_{0}
in the Burklund–Xu
E_{1}
-page. If this Massey product were well-defined in
H^{***}\mathcal{A}(2)
, then the May convergence theorem
[
May69
]
would imply that
q_{2}\cdot Ph_{0}
is a permanent cycle in the Burklund–Xu spectral sequence. But we already saw in the proof of
9.2
that
q_{2}\cdot Ph_{0}
is not a permanent cycle. Therefore, the Massey product is not well-defined in
H^{***}\mathcal{A}(2)
. The only possible problem is that there is a hidden
h_{1}
extension on
q_{2}\cdot a
, and
q_{0}\cdot P
is the only possible target for this hidden extension.

The rest of the hidden extensions follow from the first one by
P^{k}
multiplication.
∎

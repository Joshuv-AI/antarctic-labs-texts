Error analysis of the space-time interface-fitted finite element method for an inverse source problem for an advection-diffusion equation with moving subdomains
Thi Thanh Mai Ta, Quang Huy Nguyen, Dinh Nho Hào

Abstract
A space-time interface-fitted approximation of an inverse source problem for the advection-diffusion equation with moving subdomains is investigated. The problem is reformulated as an optimization problem using Tikhonov regularization. A space-time interface-fitted method is employed to discretize the advection-diffusion equation, where two second-order a priori error estimates are established with respect to the L 2 \Ls^{2} -norms. Additionally, the regularized source is discretized sequentially using the variational approach, the element-wise constant discretization, and finally, the post-processing strategy. Optimal error estimates are achieved for the first two methods, while superlinear convergence is obtained for the third. Furthermore, a priori choices for the regularization parameter are proposed, depending on the mesh size and noise level. These choices ensure that the discrete and post-processing solutions strongly converge to the exact source as the mesh size and noise level tend to zero.

An alternative is the space-time method, which treats the time variable as an additional spatial variable. In this paper, we discretize the state and adjoint problems using the space-time interface-fitted method
[
23
]

Furthermore, a priori error estimates for this method are provided when the solution exhibits globally low but locally high regularity, differing from the space-time methods presented in

25

26

Notably, this low-order finite element approximation is well-suited to our problem, as the noise data typically exhibit low smoothness, which limits the adjoint regularity and, consequently, the finite element order. Furthermore, we extend the numerical analysis from
[

-norms under appropriate assumptions.

Next, we focus on discretizing the regularized source. Among the numerous methods available in the literature, our focus is on the element-wise constant discretization, the post-processing strategy, and the variational approach. The element-wise constant discretization is the most classical method, first studied for elliptic optimal control problems
[
27
,
28
]
. This method is straightforward to implement and well-suited to a wide range of problems. However, it suffers from its slow convergence, being only of first order with respect to the
\Ls^{2}
-norm. The post-processing strategy constructs an improved approximation by projecting a term involving the discrete adjoint onto an admissible set. This approach achieves quadratic convergence in the
\Ls^{2}
-norm. Meyer and Rösch
[
29
]
originally introduced this core concept for elliptic optimal control problems, and it was subsequently applied in
[
30
,
31
]
to parabolic optimal control problems. The third method, the variational approach, was developed by Hinze
[
32
]
for elliptic optimal control problems and also achieves second-order convergence. Recently, Zhang et al.
[
8
]
employed this strategy to discretize an optimal control problem for a parabolic interface system, resulting in optimal error estimates for the control, state, and adjoint. Unlike the two previous methods, the variational approach implicitly discretizes the control using a projection formula.

In this work, we sequentially combine the space-time interface-fitted method with the variational approach, the element-wise constant discretization, and the post-processing strategy, to discretize the
Inverse problem
. The error analyses of these combinations are presented, yielding optimal error estimates for the first two methods and superlinear convergence for the third with respect to the
\Ls^{2}
-norm. Finally, we derive the overall error from regularization and discretization in terms of the regularization parameter, mesh size, and noise level. Following our approach in
[
33
]
, we suggest a priori choices for the regularization parameter to ensure strong convergence of both the discrete and post-processing solutions to the exact source. Furthermore, we establish the corresponding convergence rates. To the best of our knowledge, such convergence rates have not been previously reported in the literature on inverse source problems for advection-diffusion equations with moving subdomains.

\displaystyle\min_{f\in F_{+}}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right):=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f\right)-z_{d}^{\varepsilon}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{ subject to \eqref{eq: weak state equation}}.

Given a noise level

U_{d}^{\varepsilon}\in\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
the imprecise observation of
U_{d}\in\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
that satisfies

\left\lVert{U_{d}^{\varepsilon}-U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\leq\varepsilon,

(3.2)

and
z_{d}^{\varepsilon}:=U_{d}^{\varepsilon}-u^{\ast}_{\,\mid\,\omega_{T}}\in\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
is the noisy data. As with Problem (
2.9
), the data
z_{d}^{\varepsilon}
is well-defined in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
by interpreting
z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\equiv 0
out side of
\omega_{T}
. Finally,
\lambda>0
denotes the regularization parameter.

3.1
Existence of solutions and optimality conditions

To demonstrate the effectiveness of Tikhonov regularization, we first prove that Problem (
3.1
) admits a unique solution. While the following theorem is based on classical results, we revisit it here for clarity and completeness within the context of regularizing an inverse problem with moving subdomains.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any fixed
\lambda>0
, Problem (
3.1
) has a unique solution
f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\in F_{+}
.

Proof.

Recall that the set
F_{+}
is nonempty. Together with
J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)\geq 0
on
F_{+}
, we deduce that
\inf\limits_{f\in F_{+}}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)
is finite. Hence, there exists a sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F_{+}
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f_{n}\right)=\inf\limits_{f\in F_{+}}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right).

(3.3)

The inequality
\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f_{n}\right)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
implies that the sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, which allows us to extract a (not relabeled) weakly convergent subsequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, with
f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Moreover, there exists a sufficiently large
\rho>0
such that

\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F_{+}\cap\overline{B}\left(\rho\right),

where
\overline{B}\left(\rho\right)
denotes a closed ball with radius
\rho>0
in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Since
F_{+}\cap\overline{B}\left(\rho\right)
is a closed, bounded, and convex subset of
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, it is weakly sequentially compact
[
43
, Theorem 2.10]
. This gives us
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
.

Consider the variational problem: Find
u_{n}:=u\left(f_{n}\right)\in\Xs_{0}
that satisfies

a\left(u_{n},\varphi\right)=\left(\ell f_{n},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ys.

(3.4)

This problem is well-posed. From (
2.4
), we know that

\left\lVert{u_{n}}\right\rVert_{\Xs}\leq C\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

which implies that the sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\Xs
. Therefore, there exists
u_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\in\Xs
and a (not relabeled) weakly convergent subsequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
in
\Xs
. Thus, for all
\varphi\in\Ys
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\left\langle{\partial_{t}u_{n},\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\right)\varphi+\kappa\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\varphi+\kappa\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Taking the limit in (
3.4
), we obtain

a\left(u_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon},\varphi\right)=\left(\ell f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ys.

(3.5)

Next, we will prove that
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs_{0}
. To do this, we choose
\varphi\in\Xs_{T}
in (
3.4
) and apply the integration by parts formula with
u_{n}\in\Xs_{0}
and
\varphi\in\Xs_{T}
. One obtains

-\left\langle{\partial_{t}u_{n},\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\right)\varphi+\kappa\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left(\ell f_{n},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

We take
n\to\infty
to get

-\left\langle{\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\varphi+\kappa\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left(\ell f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

On the other hand, by integrating (
3.5
) by parts with
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs
and
\varphi\in\Xs_{T}
, we deduce that

-\left\langle{\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\varphi+\kappa\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left(\ell f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\int\limits_{\Omega}u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{x},0\right)\varphi\left(\boldsymbol{x},0\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

From the last two identities, we infer that
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(\cdot,0\right)=0
, and hence
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs_{0}
. Combining this with (
3.5
), we conclude that
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}=u\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)
.

Finally, we apply (
3.3
) and the lower semi-continuity of the
\Ls^{2}
-norms to get

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{f\in F_{+}}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\left(f_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=J_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\left(f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\right),

which indicates that
f_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\in F_{+}
is a minimizer. Uniqueness follows from the strict convexity of the functional
J_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we derive the optimality conditions for Problem (
3.1
). To do so, let us introduce the following adjoint problem: Find
p\left(f\right)\in\Xs_{T}
such that

a^{\prime}\left(p\left(f\right),\phi\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(u\left(f\right)-z_{d}^{\varepsilon}\right),\phi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ys,

(3.6)

where the bilinear form
a^{\prime}:\Xs\times\Ys\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

a^{\prime}\left(p,\phi\right):=-\left\langle{\partial_{t}p,\phi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}-\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla p\right)\phi+\kappa\nabla p\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

and
\chi_{\omega_{T}}
is the characteristic function of the subdomain
\omega_{T}
. By changing the time and velocity field directions and applying
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
, we conclude the well-posedness of this problem.

Theorem 3.2
.

The unique solution
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
to Problem (
3.1
), together with the corresponding state
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs_{0}
and adjoint
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs_{T}
, satisfies the following optimality conditions

a\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi\right)=\left(\ell f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ys,

(3.7)

and

a^{\prime}\left(p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\phi\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right),\phi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ys,

(3.8)

and the variational inequality

\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}+\lambda f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},f-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\geq 0\qquad\qquad\forall f\in F_{+}.

(3.9)

This inequality is equivalent to the projection formula

f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Proj}_{F_{+}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right),

(3.10)

where the operator
\operatorname{Proj}_{F_{+}}:\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)\rightarrow F_{+}
is defined for all
v\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
as

\operatorname{Proj}_{F_{+}}\left(v\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right):=\max\left\{0,v\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\right\}\qquad\qquad\text{for almost every }\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\in Q_{T}.

Proof.

Following the classical arguments in
[
43
, Lemma 2.21]
, we prove that the functional
J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
defined in (
3.1
) is Fréchet differentiable and its gradient
\nabla J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)
at
f\in F_{+}
is given by

\nabla J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)=\ell p\left(f\right)+\lambda f,

where
p\left(f\right)\in\Xs_{T}
is the solution to Problem (
3.6
).

To proceed, let us take a small variation
\delta f\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
of
f\in F_{+}
; we have

\displaystyle J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f+\delta f\right)-J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)=

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f+\delta f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f+\delta f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f+\delta f\right)-u\left(f\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left(u\left(f+\delta f\right)-u\left(f\right),u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{\delta f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\lambda\left(f,\delta f\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left(u\left(\delta f\right),u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{\delta f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\lambda\left(f,\delta f\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

Here,
u\left(\delta f\right)\in\Xs_{0}
is the solution to the problem

a\left(u\left(\delta f\right),\varphi\right)=\left(\ell\delta f,\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ys.

(3.11)

Thanks to (
2.1
) and (
2.4
), we have
\left\lVert{u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rVert_{\Xs}\leq C\left\lVert{\delta f}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}
, resulting in

\left\lVert{u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}=o\left(\left\lVert{\delta f}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)\qquad\qquad\text{as }\left\lVert{\delta f}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\rightarrow 0.

Thus, we imply

J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f+\delta f\right)-J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)=\left(u\left(\delta f\right),u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\lambda\left(f,\delta f\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+o\left(\left\lVert{\delta f}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right).

(3.12)

To derive the functional gradient, we rewrite the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.12
) as a scalar product in the solution space. Let
p\left(f\right)\in\Xs_{T}
be the solution to Problem (
3.6
). We choose
\phi=u\left(\delta f\right)\in\Xs_{0}
in (
3.6
) to arrive at

\displaystyle\left(u\left(\delta f\right),u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(u\left(\delta f\right),\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\partial_{t}p\left(f\right),u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}-\left[\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla p\left(f\right)\right]u\left(\delta f\right)+\kappa\nabla p\left(f\right)\cdot\nabla u\left(\delta f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

By integrating by parts with
p\left(f\right)\in\Xs_{T}
and
u\left(\delta f\right)\in\Xs_{0}
, we rewrite
\left\langle{\partial_{t}p\left(f\right),u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rangle
as

\displaystyle\left\langle{\partial_{t}p\left(f\right),u\left(\delta f\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}p\left(f\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)u\left(\delta f\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}-\int\limits_{\Omega}p\left(f\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},0\right)u\left(\delta f\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},0\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}-\left\langle{\partial_{t}u\left(\delta f\right),p\left(f\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\partial_{t}u\left(\delta f\right),p\left(f\right)}\right\rangle.

To handle the advection part on the right-hand side of (
3.13
), we invoke the divergence theorem, the incompressibility condition
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)=0
for all
\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
, and the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. We obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla p\left(f\right)\right]u\left(\delta f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\left[p\left(f\right)u\left(\delta f\right)\mathbf{v}\right]-\left[\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\left(\delta f\right)\right]p\left(f\right)-p\left(f\right)u\left(\delta f\right)\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}p\left(f\right)u\left(\delta f\right)\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\left(\delta f\right)\right]p\left(f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\left(\delta f\right)\right]p\left(f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}
is the outward normal to
\partial\Omega
. Hence, (
3.13
) becomes

\left(u\left(\delta f\right),u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u\left(\delta f\right),p\left(f\right)}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\left(\delta f\right)\right]p\left(f\right)+\kappa\nabla u\left(\delta f\right)\cdot\nabla p\left(f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

which leads to

\left(u\left(\delta f\right),u\left(f\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}=a\left(u\left(\delta f\right),p\left(f\right)\right)=\left(\ell p\left(f\right),\delta f\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

(3.15)

where
\varphi=p\left(f\right)\in\Xs_{T}
was chosen in (
3.11
) for the first equality. By substituting this into (
3.12
), we conclude the Fréchet differentiability of the functional
J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
, together with its gradient.
∎

3.2
Convergence

This subsection investigates some convergence properties of Tikhonov regularization. We begin by showing that the solution
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
to Problem (
3.1
) is stable with respect to the perturbations in the data
z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\in\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
. The following theorem is a constrained variant of the results presented in
[
44
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
45
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any fixed
\lambda>0
, let
\left\{z_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
be a sequence that strongly converges to
z_{d}^{\varepsilon}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
, and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F_{+}
denote the sequence of solutions to the corresponding problems

\displaystyle\min_{f\in F_{+}}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},\qquad\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\text{ subject to \eqref{eq: weak state equation}}.

Then,
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
strongly converges to the solution
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
of Problem (
3.1
) in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique solution
f_{n}\in F_{+}
to Problem (
3.16
). For any
f\in F_{+}
, we have

\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

which implies the boundedness of the sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. By applying the technique in Theorem
3.1
, we conclude the existence of an element
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
and a (not relabed) weakly convergent subsequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
and
u\left(f_{n}\right)\rightharpoonup u\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)
in
\Xs
. This, together with the strong convergence of the sequence
\left\{z_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
z^{\varepsilon}_{d}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
, gives us
u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}\rightharpoonup u\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
, and hence

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\geq\left\lVert{u\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}.

(3.17)

By invoking the lower semi-continuity of the
\Ls^{2}
-norms, we deduce that

\displaystyle J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f\right)-z_{d}^{\varepsilon}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}=J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right),

for all
f\in F_{+}
. Therefore,
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
solves Problem (
3.1
).

Next, we will prove that the sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
strongly converges to
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. By contradiction, suppose that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\neq\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}
, then

\theta:=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}>\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\geq\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

(3.19)

Since there exists a (not relabeled) subsequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}=\theta
, we choose
f=f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
in (
3.18
) to have

J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\theta^{2}.

Combine this with (
3.19
), we arrive at

\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left(\theta^{2}-\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)>\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{n}\right)-z_{n}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)},

which contradicts (
3.17
). The proof is finished.
∎

Regarding the error in regularizing the exact source using Tikhonov regularization, let us recall the following result from
[
40
, Theorems 5.16 and 5.19]
for general linear inverse problems with convex constraints:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f_{+}\in F_{+}
be the exact source and
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
be the solution to Problem (
3.1
). Assume that there exists
\zeta\in\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
with the minimal
\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
-norm such that
f_{+}=\operatorname{Proj}_{F_{+}}\left(A^{\ast}\zeta\right)
, where
A^{\ast}:\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)\to\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
denotes the adjoint of the operator
A
in (
2.9
). Then, the following inequality holds

\left\lVert{f_{+}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\sqrt{\lambda}\left\lVert{\zeta}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Discretization

Assume that
\Omega
is a polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \left(d=1\text{ or }2\right)
. The space-time domain
Q_{T}=\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)
is partitioned into shape-regular simplicial finite elements by a family of interface-fitted meshes
\left\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}_{h\in\left(0,h_{\ast}\right)}
, where
h\in\left(0,h_{\ast}\right)
denotes the mesh size, with
h_{\ast}>0
be given. More precisely, each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a triangle (when
d=1
) or a tetrahedron (when
d=2
), and it can be classified into one of the following cases:

1.

K
lies entirely within
\overline{Q_{1}}
;

2.

K
lies entirely within
\overline{Q_{2}}
;

3.

K
intersects
\Gamma^{\ast}
at
d+1
vertices.

For further details, please refer to
[
23
]
. Additionally, assume that the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is quasi-uniform. Let
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
denote the linear approximation of
\Gamma^{\ast}
, consisting of all edges (or faces) with nodes lying on
\Gamma^{\ast}
. The discrete interface
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
divides
Q_{T}
into two subdomains
Q_{1,h}
and
Q_{2,h}
, which are the approximated counterparts of
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, respectively.

4.1
Space-time interface-fitted method for state and adjoint problems

This subsection recalls the space-time interface-fitted method from
[
23
]
: On the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the following space-time finite element spaces

\Vs_{h,0}:=\left\{\varphi_{h}\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)\mid\varphi_{h\,\mid\,K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\left(K\right)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\text{, and }\varphi_{h}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T)\text{ and }\Omega\times\left\{0\right\}\right\},

and

\Vs_{h,T}:=\left\{\phi_{h}\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)\mid\phi_{h\,\mid\,K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\left(K\right)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\text{, and }\phi_{h}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T)\text{ and }\Omega\times\left\{T\right\}\right\},

where for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the space
\mathbb{P}_{m}\left(K\right)
consists of all polynomials of degree
m
on
K
. Clearly,
\Vs_{h,0}\subset\Xs_{0}
and
\Vs_{h,T}\subset\Xs_{T}
.

We first consider the discrete state problem: For
\ell\in\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
and
f\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, find
u_{h}\left(f\right)\in\Vs_{h,0}
that satisfies

a_{h}\left(u_{h}\left(f\right),\varphi_{h}\right)=\left(\ell f,\varphi_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,0}.

(4.1)

The bilinear form
a_{h}:\Xs_{0}\times\Ys\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

a_{h}\left(u,\varphi\right):=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u,\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\right)\varphi+\kappa_{h}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\kappa_{h}
approximates
\kappa
by means of

\kappa_{h}:=\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}>0&\text{in}\quad Q_{1,h},\\
\kappa_{2}>0&\text{in}\quad Q_{2,h}.\end{cases}

Regarding the numerical analysis, we introduce the seminorm

\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\kappa_{i}\left|{\nabla v}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1,0}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right).

When
v\in\Ys\subset\Hs^{1,0}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
, the right-hand side of this expression simplifies to

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\kappa_{i}\left|{\nabla v}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa_{h}\left|{\nabla v}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

which defines an equivalent norm in
\Ys
, also denoted by
\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}
. This norm, incorporating the coefficient
\kappa_{h}
, provides advantages for analyzing discrete problems compared to the norm
\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{\Ys}
. Moreover, for
v\in\Xs_{0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\langle{\partial_{t}v,v}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla v\right)v+\kappa_{h}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{v\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{v\left(\cdot,0\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla v\right)v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{v\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{v\left(\cdot,0\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2},

owing to the integration by parts formula and the technique in (
3.14
). Therefore, we obtain

a_{h}\left(v,v\right)\geq\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Xs_{0}.

(4.2)

This inequality will be used later. On the space
\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
, we introduce the seminorm

\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h,0}^{2}:=\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\left\lVert{\xi_{h}\left(v\right)}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right),

where
\xi_{h}\left(v\right)\in\Vs_{h,0}
is the unique solution to the problem

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa_{h}\nabla\xi_{h}\left(v\right)\cdot\nabla\psi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}(\partial_{t}v)\psi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\psi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,0}.

By choosing
\psi_{h}=\xi_{h}\left(v\right)
and applying (
2.1
), we end up with

\left\lVert{\xi_{h}\left(v\right)}\right\rVert_{h}\leq C\left\lVert{\partial_{t}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right).

(4.3)

The following discrete stability condition holds

\sup_{\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,0}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{a_{h}\left(u_{h}\left(f\right),\varphi_{h}\right)}{\left\lVert{\varphi_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}}\geq C_{s}\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(f\right)}\right\rVert_{h,0}\qquad\qquad\forall u_{h}\left(f\right)\in\Vs_{h,0},

(4.4)

ensuring that Problem (
4.1
) is well-posed, see
[
23
, Lemma 3.1]
. Here,
C_{s}>0
is a constant that depends only on the space-time domain
Q_{T}
, the norm
\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}
, and the coefficient
\kappa
.

Next, we present the discretization of Problem (
3.6
). Similarly, we determine
p_{h}\left(f\right)\in\Vs_{h,T}
such that

a_{h}^{\prime}\left(p_{h}\left(f\right),\phi_{h}\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(u\left(f\right)-z_{d}^{\varepsilon}\right),\phi_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,T},

(4.5)

where the bilinear form
a_{h}^{\prime}:\Xs_{T}\times\Ys\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

a_{h}^{\prime}\left(p,\phi\right):=-\left\langle{\partial_{t}p,\phi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}-\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla p\right)\phi+\kappa_{h}\nabla p\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

On the space
\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
, we introduce another seminorm

\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h,T}^{2}:=\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\left\lVert{\xi_{h}^{\prime}\left(v\right)}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right),

where
\xi_{h}^{\prime}\left(v\right)\in\Vs_{h,T}
denotes the unique solution to the problem

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa_{h}\nabla\xi_{h}^{\prime}\left(v\right)\cdot\nabla\psi_{h}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}(\partial_{t}v)\psi_{h}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\psi_{h}^{\prime}\in\Vs_{h,T}.

Employing the technique for (
4.4
), we establish the following stability condition

\sup_{\phi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,T}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{a^{\prime}_{h}\left(p_{h}\left(f\right),\phi_{h}\right)}{\left\lVert{\phi_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}}\geq C_{s}\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(f\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\qquad\qquad\forall p_{h}\left(f\right)\in\Vs_{h,T},

(4.6)

and concludes the well-posedness of Problem (
4.5
).

4.2
Discretization of the regularized source

For a subspace
F^{d}\subseteq\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, which may be either finite-dimensional or the entire space
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, we denote by
F_{+}^{h}:=F^{d}\cap F_{+}
the discrete admissible set. We call an element
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\in F^{h}_{+}
the discrete source if, together with the corresponding state
u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\in\Vs_{h,0}
and adjoint
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\in\Vs_{h,T}
, it solves the system

a_{h}\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},\varphi_{h}\right)=\left(\ell f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},\varphi_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,0},

(4.7)

and

a^{\prime}_{h}\left(p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},\phi_{h}\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-z_{d,h}^{\varepsilon}\right),\phi_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,T},

(4.8)

and the variational inequality

\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}+\lambda f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},f_{h}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\geq 0\qquad\qquad\forall f_{h}\in F^{h}_{+},

(4.9)

where
z^{\varepsilon}_{d,h}:=U^{\varepsilon}_{d}-u^{\ast}_{h\,\mid\,\omega_{T}}\in\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)
denotes the discrete data. Similar to Problem (
2.9
),
z^{\varepsilon}_{d,h}
can be interpreted as an element of
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Here,
u^{\ast}_{h}\in\Vs_{h,0}
approximates
u^{\ast}\in\Xs_{0}
in (
2.7
), also using the space-time interface-fitted method. It can be defined analogously to
u_{h}\left(f\right)\in\Vs_{h,0}
in (
4.1
).

This paper will not discuss the existence and uniqueness of solutions to Problem (
4.7
)–(
4.9
). For simplicity, we assume that the discrete source exists uniquely.

The way we choose
F^{d}
leads to the corresponding discretization method. Our work focuses on the following three approaches:

1.

Variational approach: We choose
F^{d}
to be the whole space
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, which means that the two sets
F_{+}
and
F^{h}_{+}
coincide
[
32
]
. By invoking a projection form of (
4.9
), we convert the discretization of the regularized source into the discrete treatment for a term involving the adjoint. We will address the error of this method in Subsection
6.1
.

2.

Element-wise constant discretization: For simplicity, assume that the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is used for discretizing the regularized source. We choose
F^{d}
to be the space of element-wise constant functions
[
27
]
, i.e.,

F^{d}=\left\{f_{h}\in\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)\mid f_{h\,\mid\,K}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(K\right)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}.

(4.10)

The error of this approach will be estimated in Subsection
6.2
.

3.

Postprocessing strategy:
F^{d}
is chosen as in the element-wise constant discretization. After computing the element-wise constant solution
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}
to Problem (
4.7
)–(
4.9
), we project the term
\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}
onto
F_{+}
to obtain a better approximation
f_{h}^{\dagger}

[
29
]
. Here,
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}
is defined in (
4.8
), and
f_{h}^{\dagger}
is called the post-processing solution

f_{h}^{\dagger}:=\operatorname{Proj}_{F_{+}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\right).

(4.11)

The error analysis of this strategy will be discussed in Subsection
6.3
.

5
A priori error estimates for advection-diffusion problems

In this section, we focus on discretization errors of the state and adjoint, along with the stability of the discrete state. First, we present an auxiliary result regarding the mismatch between each space-time subdomain
Q_{i}
and its approximated counterpart
Q_{i,h}\ \left(i=1,2\right)
. Let us define

S_{h}^{1}:=Q_{1,h}\setminus\overline{Q_{1}}=Q_{2}\setminus\overline{Q_{2,h}}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad S_{h}^{2}:=Q_{2,h}\setminus\overline{Q_{2}}=Q_{1}\setminus\overline{Q_{1,h}},

and
S_{h}:=S_{h}^{1}\cup S_{h}^{2}
(see Figure
2
). We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}:=\left\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\mid K\cap\Gamma^{\ast}\neq\varnothing\right\}
the set of all interface elements.

Figure 2:
The region
S_{h}=S_{h}^{1}\cup S_{h}^{2}
lies between the space-time interface
\Gamma^{\ast}
(red) and the discrete one
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
(black), considering
d=1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\Gamma^{\ast}
is a
\Cs^{2}
-continuous hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\ \left(d=1\text{ or }2\right)
. Then, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
, we have

\left|{K\cap S_{h}}\right|\leq Ch^{d+2}.

(5.1)

There holds for the cardinality of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}}1\leq Ch^{-d}.

(5.2)

The area (or volume) of the region formed by all interface elements is bounded by

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}}\left|{K}\right|\leq Ch.

(5.3)

Proof.

When
d=1
, the proof of the first inequality can be found in
[
14
, Section 2]
. The second inequality follows by combining (
5.1
) with
[
46
, Lemma 3.3.4]
. All the arguments can be extended to the case
d=2
without essential changes. As a consequence of (
5.2
) and the inequality
\left|{K}\right|\leq Ch^{d+1}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we obtain (
5.3
).
∎

Let
v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
. By the Sobolev embedding theorem
[
34
, Theorem 2.35]
, if
v\in\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, then
v\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{1}}\right)\cap\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{2}}\right)
. Additionally, if
v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, it follows from
[
34
, Theorem 18.8]
that
\gamma_{1}v-\gamma_{2}v=0
, where
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are the trace operators defined in Subsection
2.1
. Consequently,
v\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
.

We now study the approximability of the Lagrangian interpolant. Let
I_{h}:\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)\rightarrow\Vs_{h,0}
be the nodal interpolation operator
[
34
, Section 19.3]
. For the case
Q_{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Chen and Zou established the interpolation estimate in
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
. However, the order of convergence was only nearly optimal, up to a factor of
\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}
, where
h
denotes the mesh size.

This paper imposes an additional condition on
v
and extends Chen and Zou’s approach to recover an optimal error estimate for both cases
Q_{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
Q_{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We obtain the following result:

Lemma 5.2
.

For all
v\in\Ws
, the interpolation operator
I_{h}
satisfies the following inequality

\left\lVert{v-I_{h}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+h\left\lVert{\Ds\left(v-I_{h}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)},

where
\Ds:=\left(\nabla,\partial_{t}\right)^{\top}
denotes the space-time gradient operator.

Proof.

Let us present the estimate of
\left\lVert{v-I_{h}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}
. The result for
\left\lVert{\Ds\left(v-I_{h}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}
can be proved with the same technique. The idea is to first estimate the interpolation error on an arbitrary element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, then sum over all elements to obtain the desired result.

Under the regularity assumption, we have
v\in\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
on any
K\notin\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
. The classical interpolation theory
[
34
, Theorem 11.13]
gives us

\left\lVert{v-I_{h}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)}.

(5.4)

Next, consider an arbitrary element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
K\cap S_{h}\subset Q_{1}
and
K\setminus\overline{S_{h}}\subset Q_{2}
. For
v\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
with
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
, note that
\Es_{i}v\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{T}\right)\subset\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)\ (i=1,2)

[
34
, Theorem 2.31]
, where
\Es_{1}
and
\Es_{2}
are the extension operators in (
2.3
). By applying (
5.1
) and the classical interpolation theory, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{v-I_{h}v}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{\Es_{1}v-I_{h}\left(\Es_{1}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\cap S_{h}\right)}+\left\lVert{\Es_{2}v-I_{h}\left(\Es_{2}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\setminus\overline{S_{h}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left|{K\cap S_{h}}\right|\left\lVert{\Es_{1}v-I_{h}\left(\Es_{1}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(K\cap S_{h}\right)}+\left\lVert{\Es_{2}v-I_{h}\left(\Es_{2}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\setminus\overline{S_{h}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{d+2}\left\lVert{\Es_{1}v-I_{h}\left(\Es_{1}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(K\right)}+\left\lVert{\Es_{2}v-I_{h}\left(\Es_{2}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{d+4}\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Es_{1}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(K\right)}+Ch^{4}\left\lVert{\Ds^{2}\left(\Es_{2}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(K\right)},

where
\Ds^{2}
refers to the space-time Hessian operator. We then sum over all elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
. By using (
5.2
), the Sobolev embedding
\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\hookrightarrow\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
for
s>\frac{d+3}{2}

[
34
, Theorem 2.31]
, and (
2.3
) again, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}}\left\lVert{v-I_{h}v}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(K\right)}\leq

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{d+4}\max{\left\{\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Es_{1}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2},\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Es_{2}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2}\right\}}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}}1\right)+Ch^{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\lVert{\Ds^{2}\left(\Es_{i}v\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}\max{\left\{\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Es_{1}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2},\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Es_{2}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2}\right\}}+Ch^{4}\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

From (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we obtain
\left\lVert{v-I_{h}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}
. By following the same arguments, we can prove that
\left\lVert{\Ds\left(v-I_{h}v\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}
. The proof is complete.

p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\in\Vs_{h,T}
be the solutions to Problems (
3.8
) and (
6.4
), respectively. Under Assumption

, we have the following estimates

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq Ch\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right),

and

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

Proof.

To prove the first estimate, we begin with the triangle inequality

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}.

(6.13)

Thanks to Lemma
5.8
, there holds the following estimate

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq Ch\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

By using Assumption
5.1
, (
2.4
), (
6.11
), and (
6.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\right)

(6.14)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

Thus, we imply

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq Ch\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

(6.15)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
6.13
), we subtract (
6.4
) from (
6.10
) to obtain

a^{\prime}_{h}\left(\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right),\phi_{h}\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}+z^{\varepsilon}_{d,h}\right),\phi_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,T}.

By invoking the technique in (
6.6
), Lemma
5.6
, and subsequently Remarks
5.1
and
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq C\left(\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{u^{\ast}-u^{\ast}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left(\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+\left\lVert{u^{\ast}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left(\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right).

Together with (
6.12
), we arrive at

\left\lVert{\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq\dfrac{Ch^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)+Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

(6.16)

The first result follows by combining (
6.13
), (
6.15
), and (
6.16
).

Next, we prove the second result. We also start with the triangle inequality

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

On the first hand, Lemma
5.8
and (
6.14
) give us

\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

On the other hand, by applying (

\left\lVert{\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{\widetilde{p_{h}}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq\dfrac{Ch^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)+Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

The proof is complete.

We now arrive at the main result of this subsection by combining Lemmas

,
6.2
, and
6.3
with the triangle inequality.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\in\Xs_{0}\times\Xs_{T}\times F_{+}
and
\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\right)\in\Vs_{h,0}\times\Vs_{h,T}\times F^{h}_{+}
be the solutions to Problems (
3.7
)–(
3.9
) and (
4.7
)–(
4.9
) in case of variational discretization, respectively. Let
C_{2}>0
be the constant defined in Lemma
6.1
. Assume that Assumption
5.1
is satisfied. Then, the following estimates hold

\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\tau}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(h\right),

and

\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\tau}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{2}\right),

where

\tau=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }\lambda\leq 8\left\lVert{\ell}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}C_{2},\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For the first estimate, we use the triangle inequality and apply Lemmas
5.4
,
6.1
, and
6.2
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+C\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+C\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\tau}}\right)\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}\qquad\qquad\quad\text{with }\tau=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{2}&\text{if }\lambda\leq 8\left\lVert{\ell}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}C_{2},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By using Remark
5.2
and (
6.12
), we have

\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\dfrac{C}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

Together with Lemma
6.3
, we hence obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\tau}}+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\tau}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\tau}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)\qquad\qquad\quad\text{with }\tau=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{2}&\text{if }\lambda\leq 8\left\lVert{\ell}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}C_{2},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The conclusion follows. The second inequality is proved similarly.
∎

6.2
Element-wise constant discretization

We choose
F^{d}
as the space of step functions, as described in (
4.10
). First, we discretize the regularized source while leaving the advection-diffusion equations unchanged. Mathematically, we consider a pure state problem: For
f_{h}\in F^{h}_{+}=F^{d}\cap F_{+}

such that

a\left(u\left(f_{h}\right),\varphi\right)=\left(\ell f_{h},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ys.

(6.17)

The well-posedness of this problem is proved in Subsection
2.2
. We then introduce the following problem

\displaystyle\min\limits_{f_{h}\in F^{h}_{+}}J^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(f_{h}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(f_{h}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert{f_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

(6.18)

\displaystyle\text{ subject to \eqref{eq: weak pure state equation constant}}.

Clearly, Problem (
6.18
) admits a unique solution
\widehat{f_{h}}\in F^{h}_{+}
. Furthermore, the following result holds:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\widehat{f}_{h}\in F^{h}_{+}
be the solution to Problem (
6.18
), and let
\widehat{u}\in\Xs_{0}
and
\widehat{p}\in\Xs_{T}
be its corresponding state and adjoint, respectively. Then, the following system is satisfied

a\left(\widehat{u},\varphi\right)=\left(\ell\widehat{f}_{h},\varphi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ys,

(6.19)

and

a^{\prime}\left(\widehat{p},\phi\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(\widehat{u}-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right),\phi\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ys,

(6.20)

and the variational inequality

\left(\ell\widehat{p}+\lambda\widehat{f}_{h},f_{h}-\widehat{f}_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\geq 0\qquad\qquad\forall f_{h}\in F^{h}_{+}.

(6.21)

Regarding an error analysis, we now discuss the regularity of the regularized source
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
in (
3.1
), along with its stability estimate. Specifically, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
be the solution to Problem (
3.1
) and

be its corresponding adjoint. Assume that Assumption
5.1
holds and
\ell\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Then,
\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, and consequently,
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Moreover, the following stability estimate holds

\left\lVert{\Ds f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

Proof.

For
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
with
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
, the Sobolev embeddings
\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{i}\right)\hookrightarrow\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{i}\right)\ (i=1,2)

[
34
, Theorem 2.31]
give us
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
. On the other hand, since
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, we imply
\gamma_{1}p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\gamma_{2}p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}=0
by
[
34
, Theorem 18.8]
. Here,
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are the trace operators introduced in Subsection
2.1
. By invoking
[
34
, Theorem 18.8]
once more, we obtain
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Combine this with
\ell\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, we conclude that
\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
is an element of
\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
.

The regularity of

follows from (
3.10
) and Stampacchia’s lemma
[
49
, Theorem A.1]
. Together with Ziemer’s representation
[
50

∎

Denote by
\Pi_{d}:\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)\rightarrow F^{d}
the
\Ls^{2}
-orthogonal projection operator, defined element-wise as

\left(\Pi_{d}v\right)_{\mid\,K}:=\dfrac{1}{\left|{K}\right|}\int\limits_{K}v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right),

(6.22)

for all
v\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Recall from
[
34
, Section 18.4]
that for all

and for all

(6.24)

Here,
\Lambda
denotes an arbitrary element

or the entire space-time domain

, and the constant

and

.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
be the solution to Problem (
3.1
) and
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs_{T}
be its corresponding adjoint. Let
\widehat{f_{h}}\in F^{h}_{+}
be the solution to Problem (
6.18
). Assume that Assumption
5.1
is satisfied and
\ell\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Then, the following estimate holds

\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\dfrac{Ch}{\lambda}\sqrt{\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

Proof.

By inserting
f=\widehat{f_{h}}\in F_{+}
into (
3.9
), we find

\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}+\lambda f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\geq 0.

On the other hand, note that
\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F^{h}_{+}

\left(\ell\widehat{p}+\lambda\widehat{f_{h}},f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{f_{h}}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left(\ell\widehat{p}+\lambda\widehat{f_{h}},\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\geq 0.

\lambda\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2}\leq\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\ell\widehat{p},\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left(\ell\widehat{p}+\lambda\widehat{f_{h}},\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

(6.25)

6.25
), we invoke (
3.15
) twice. It follows that

\displaystyle\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\ell\widehat{p},\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}-\left(\ell\widehat{p},\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\widehat{u}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}-\left(\widehat{u}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},\widehat{u}-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=-\left\lVert{\widehat{u}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\leq 0.

\widehat{f_{h}}
and

belong to two orthogonal subspaces of

\left(\widehat{f_{h}},\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}=0.

Therefore, (
6.25
) reduces to

\lambda\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2}\leq\left(\ell\widehat{p},\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

6.24

\displaystyle\left(\ell\widehat{p},\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}=\left(\ell\widehat{p}-\Pi_{d}\left(\ell\widehat{p}\right),\Pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

(6.26)

Finally, let us proceed with the first norm on the right-hand side of (

). We invoke
\ell\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, Assumption
5.1
, (
2.4
), and the arguments of (

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\ell\widehat{p}\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{\widehat{p}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert{\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert{\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z^{\varepsilon}_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

We obtain the desired estimate by substituting this into (
6.26
).
∎

Next, we employ the space-time interface-fitted method

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\widehat{f_{h}}\in F^{h}_{+}
and

Proof.

We add these two inequalities to obtain

\lambda\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{2}\leq\left(\ell\widehat{p}-\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},\ f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

(6.30)

where
J_{1},J_{2}
, and

are defined as

J_{1}:=\left(\ell\widehat{p}-\ell p_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right),\ f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad J_{2}:=\left(\ell p_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-\ell\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right),\ f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

(6.31)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

For handling

, we subtract (

6.28

a^{\prime}_{h}\left(p_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right),\phi_{h}\right)=\left(\chi_{\omega_{T}}\left(\widehat{u}-u_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-z^{\varepsilon}_{d}+z^{\varepsilon}_{d,h}\right),\phi_{h}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in\Vs_{h,T}.

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left(\left\lVert{\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+\left\lVert{u^{\ast}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{Ch^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)+Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle J_{3}=\left(\ell\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}\right)_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\ell}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

We insert (

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda},f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\in\Xs_{0}\times\Xs_{T}\times F_{+}
and
\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h},f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}\right)\in\Vs_{h,0}\times\Vs_{h,T}\times F^{h}_{+}
be the solutions to Problems (
3.7
)–(
3.9
) and (
4.7
)–(
4.9
) in case of element-wise constant discretization, respectively. Let

be the constant defined in Lemma

5.1

\ell\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{Ch}{\lambda}\sqrt{\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+Ch\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\tau}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right),

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{Ch}{\lambda}\sqrt{\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+Ch^{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\tau}}\right)\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

In both estimates, the convergence order is linear, i.e., of
\mathcal{O}\left(h\right)
, and the exponent
\tau
is given by

\tau=\begin{cases}\frac{5}{2}&\text{if }\lambda\leq 4\left\lVert{\ell}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}C_{2},\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We begin with the first estimate. For the state error, we apply the triangle inequality to obtain

\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}\leq\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{\widehat{u}-u_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,0}+\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}.

(6.33)

The first term on the right-hand side of (
6.33
) can be bounded by using (
4.3
) and Remark
5.2

u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h,0}=\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\left\lVert{\xi_{h}\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}\right)}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+C\left\lVert{\partial_{t}\left(u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{u}\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

Next, we estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (

\left\lVert{\widehat{u}-u_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,0}\leq Ch\left\lVert{\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\leq Ch\left\lVert{\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\dfrac{Ch}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right).

Finally, we invoke the arguments of (

\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}\leq C\left\lVert{\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

By substituting the last three inequalities into (
6.33

\left\lVert{u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,0}\leq C\left[\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert{U_{d}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\varepsilon\right)+\left\lVert{\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right].

\displaystyle\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{p}}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{\widehat{p}-p_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)}\right\rVert_{h,T}+\left\lVert{\widehat{p_{h}}\left(\widehat{f_{h}}\right)-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-\widehat{f_{h}}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+h\left\lVert{\widehat{p}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+h^{2}\left\lVert{\widehat{u}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+h^{2}\left\lVert{u^{\ast}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}+\left\lVert{\widehat{f_{h}}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)

6.6
and
6.7
, noting that
h^{2}<Ch
for all

with a given

6.3
Post-processing strategy

We first discretize the regularized source
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F_{+}
in (
3.1
) by using element-wise constant functions associated with the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Subsequently, we construct the post-processing solution
f_{h}^{\dagger}\in F^{h}_{+}
, as defined in (
4.11
). Recall that
F^{h}_{+}=F^{d}\cap F_{+}
denotes the discrete admissible set, where
F^{d}
is defined in (
4.10
). This subsection aims to estimate the error
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f_{h}^{\dagger}
.

Let us begin by decomposing the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
into three subsets
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}\cup\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}\cup\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}
, where

\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}:=\left\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\mid f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}=0\text{ at every point in }K\right\}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}:=\left\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\mid f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}>0\text{ at every point in }K\right\},

and
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}=\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\left(\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}\cup\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}\right)
. The sets
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}
are referred to as the active and inactive sets, respectively, while
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}
contains elements intersecting the free boundary between
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}
. Notably, the sets are pairwise disjoint, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{i}\cap\mathcal{M}_{h}^{j}=\varnothing
for all distinct
i,j\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}
.

Next, we deduce from (
3.10
) the regularity of
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
. Clearly, it depends on the regularity of its corresponding adjoint
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Xs_{T}
and the function
\ell
. Assume that
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, where
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
is given (cf. Assumption
5.1
). Additionally, if
\ell\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, Lemma
6.5
asserts that
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Furthermore, if
\ell\in\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, then on certain elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we also have
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
. Indeed, the following three scenarios arise:

1.

K\in\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}
: Since
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\equiv 0
on
K
, it follows directly that
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
.

2.

K\in\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}
: Since
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
on
K
, the regularity of
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
matches that of
\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
. If
K\notin\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
, then
\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
, and hence
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
. Conversely, if
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
, then
\ell p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(K\right)
, but not in
\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
. The same applies to
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
.

3.

K\in\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}
: Due to the kink in (
3.10
), we only have
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(K\right)
.

In general,
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
exhibits lower regularity than
p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
, which consequently complicates the derivation of a high-order estimate for the error
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f_{h}^{\dagger}
. To overcome this difficulty and achieve the desired high-order convergence, an additional condition on the mesh structure is required. In
[
51
, Assumption 2]
and
[
30
, Assumption 1]
, the following assumption was introduced

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}}\left|{K}\right|\leq Ch,

(6.34)

where
C>0
is an
h
-independent constant. This assumption holds if the boundary of the set
\left\{\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\in Q_{T}\mid f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)=0\right\}
consists of a finite number of curves (or surfaces) having a finite length (or area)
[
52
, Section 6]
.

In this paper, we weaken (
6.34
) by exploiting (
5.3
). Based on
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1},\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}
, we define

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{1}:=\left(\mathcal{M}_{h}^{1}\cup\mathcal{M}_{h}^{2}\right)\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\mathcal{T}_{h}^{2}:=\mathcal{M}_{h}^{3}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast},

and form a new partition

\mathcal{T}_{h}=\mathcal{T}_{h}^{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{h}^{2}\cup\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}.

It is evident that
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{1}\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{2}=\mathcal{T}_{h}^{2}\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}=\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{1}=\varnothing
. To address the mesh structure, the following alternative assumption is sufficient:

Assumption 6.1
.

Assume that there exists an
h
-independent constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{2}}\left|{K}\right|\leq Ch.

Due to (
5.3
), we observe that if Assumption
6.1
is satisfied, then (
6.34
) also holds. Denote by
S_{K}
the barycenter of an element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We define the barycentric interpolant
\pi_{d}:\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)\to F^{d}
element-wise as follows

\left(\pi_{d}v\right)_{\mid\,K}:=v\left(S_{K}\right)\qquad\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

(6.35)

for all
v\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
. From Lemma
6.5
and the compact embedding
\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)\hookrightarrow\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)

[
34
, Theorem 2.35]
, it follows that
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
. Consequently, we have
\pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\in F^{h}_{+}
.

Regarding the approximability of
\pi_{d}
, we recall the following lemma:

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
K
be an arbitrary element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\pi_{d}
be the function defined in (
6.35
). For all
v\in\Hs^{2}\left(K\right)
, the following inequality holds

\left|{\int\limits_{K}\left(v-\pi_{d}v\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|\leq Ch^{2}\left|{K}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert{\Ds^{2}v}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(K\right)}.

For all
v\in\Ws^{1,\infty}\left(K\right)

\left\lVert{p_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{p_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{h,T}\leq C\left\lVert{u_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{\pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

Therefore, thanks to the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+C\left\lVert{\pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

and thus

\left\lVert{f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f_{h}^{\dagger}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq\dfrac{C}{\lambda}\left(\left\lVert{p^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{p_{h}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)-p_{h}^{\pi}\left(f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{\pi_{d}f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}-f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right).

By combining this with Lemmas
6.3
,
6.9
, and
6.10
, we obtain the desired result.

Remark 6.1
.

Under appropriate conditions, we have shown that the post-processing solution

, constructed by (

more rapidly than the element-wise constant solution

Nevertheless, the order of

Our situation differs, as the regularity of

7
Conclusion

We present an error analysis for the numerical solution of an inverse source problem associated with the advection-diffusion equation featuring moving subdomains. The numerical scheme integrates a space-time method with multiple source discretization strategies. Specifically, the state and adjoint problems are discretized using the space-time interface-fitted method. Under appropriate conditions, we establish two second-order error estimates in the
\Ls^{2}
-norms. For the regularized source, discretization is performed sequentially using the variational approach, the element-wise constant discretization, and the post-processing strategy. We derive optimal error estimates for the variational approach and the element-wise constant discretization while achieving superlinear convergence for the post-processing strategy. Furthermore, we propose a priori choices for
\lambda
to ensure that
f^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda,h}
and
f_{h}^{\dagger}
strongly converge to
f_{+}
in
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
as
h
and
\varepsilon
approach zero. The convergence rate is established in each case.

For future work, we aim to extend these results to three-dimensional spatial domains and enhance the convergence order of the post-processing strategy. Another direction of interest is to develop additional error estimates where
\lambda
appears in the numerator of the terms on the right-hand side, as demonstrated in
[
54
,
55
]
, to achieve higher-order convergence rates.

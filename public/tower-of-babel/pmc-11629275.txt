Introduction

Syringoma is a common type of benign skin disorder. Young women's eyelids and cheeks are where papules are typically observed. They can also be found in areas like the extremities and vulva, in a small number of patients [1]. Surgery is the main treatment option. Laser and botulinum toxin are used by some physicians to treat smaller syringoma with satisfactory results [2]. Here we report a case of an exophytic syringoma in a male. The tumor has a large diameter and a pedicle, with an uneven surface that resembles a black pitaya. Malignant syringoma was diagnosed as an extremely rare case by the final pathological examination after surgical treatment.

Our work has been reported in line with the SCARE Guidelines 2023 criteria [3].

Presentation of case

A 57-year-old man was admitted to our hospital. A neoplasm on the lower margin of the right eyelid was discovered by the patient accidentally 5 years prior to admission. No additional diagnosis or treatment was necessary due to the absence of discomfort. According to the patient, the mass has grown to the size of an egg over the past year. It had a pedicle length of approximately 3 cm. The right eye was unable to close effectively, and the conjunctiva was red and enlarged (Fig. 1A, B). As a result, the patient came to our hospital for treatment. No significant abnormalities were observed in any of the other physical examinations.

Preoperative and postoperative appearance comparison. (A) Preoperative ocular condition; (B) preoperative tumor appearance; (C) postoperative ocular appearance; (D) postoperative eyelid closure.

After admission, the patient underwent a Dermatoplasty. Through the surgery, the patient's facial skin basically returned (Fig. 1C), and the eyelids were able to close normally (Fig. 1D). Result suggested Syringoma.

The resected specimen was Syringoma, 8 cm × 6 cm × 2.3 cm, malignant (Fig. 2). After 5 days of hospitalization, the patient was finally cured and discharged.

Pathological outcome. (A) Tumor overview; (B) fibrous connective tissue interstitial duct and small sac-like structure (HE, ×100); (C) tumor nuclear heterogeneity (HE, ×200).

Discussion

Syringoma, which is a benign sweat gland tumor that separates toward the terminal sweat ducts, typically expresses small pupils in skin color that range from light yellow to brown, is More common in females than males, preferably on eyelids, and can also occur on the forehead, cheeks, neck, chest, abdomen, and external genitalia [1,4,5]. Malignant syringoma is fairly infrequent in clinical work [6]. In this case, the syringoma is exophytic and pedunculated, which is extremely rare. The patient was unable to close the eyelid completely when standing and had chronic infection in his right eye before the surgery. After receiving surgical treatment, the patient's ocular problem was resolved. The pathology results after surgery indicated syringoma. After HE staining, cystic-like structures formed by basaloid cells were seen (Fig. 2B). At the same time, the cell heterogeneity was obvious (Fig. 2C), demonstrating a very high degree of malignancy. Finally, the patient was discharged without incident.

As everyone is aware, if benign syringoma has an impact on aesthetics, treatment is necessary. The primary methods of treatment include surgical procedures such as surgery and laser. Reports indicate that wide local excision (WLE) is the primary treatment approach for the malignant syringoma. Due to its potential benefits, Mohs Micrographic Surgery (MMS) can also be considered in centers with the appropriate conditions [6]. In this case, the patient underwent wide local resection for the pedicle and a portion of the skin, with negative surgical margins, and the following results were favorable. However, malignant tumors have the potential of postoperative recurrence and metastasis [7,8]. Whether radiotherapy or chemotherapy is necessary after surgery for malignant syringoma and whether tumor cells are sensitive to them remain unknown and require further study based on more clinical cases.

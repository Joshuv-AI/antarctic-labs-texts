Dual realizations of Bergman spaces on strongly convex domains
Agniva Chatterjee

Abstract
. The Fantappiè and Laplace transforms realize isomorphisms between analytic functionals supported on a convex compact set K ⊂ ℂ n K\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}} and certain spaces of holomorphic functions associated with K K . Viewing the Bergman space of a bounded domain in ℂ n {\mathbb{C}^{n}} as a subspace of the space of analytic functionals supported on its closure, the images of the restrictions of these transforms have been studied in the planar setting. For the Fantappiè transform, this was done for simply connected domains (Napalkov Jr–Yulumukhamtov, 1995), and for the Laplace transform, this was done for convex domains (Napalkov Jr–Yulumukhamtov, 2004). In this paper, we study this problem in higher dimensions for strongly convex domains, and establish duality results analogous to the planar case. We also produce examples to show that the planar results cannot be generalized to all convex domains in higher dimensions.

1.
Introduction

For a compact convex subset
K
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the following spaces are known to be isomorphic:

(a)

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(K)
, the space of analytic functionals on
K
,

(b)

\mathcal{O}(K^{*})
, the space of holomorphic functions on
K^{*}
, the dual complement of
K
, given by

K^{*}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\langle\zeta,z\right\rangle\neq 1,\,\forall\zeta\in K\right\},

where
\left\langle\zeta,z\right\rangle=\zeta_{1}z_{1}+\zeta_{2}z_{2}+\cdots\zeta_{n}z_{n}
, and

(c)

\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{\operatorname{exp}}}(K)
, the space of all entire function
F
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
C_{\epsilon}>0
so that

|F(z)|\leq C_{\epsilon}e^{(1+\epsilon)H_{K}(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

where
H_{K}
is the support function of
K
, defined as
H_{K}(z)=\sup\limits_{\zeta\in K}\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,z\rangle,\,z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The duality between
(a)
, and
(b)
was established by Aizenberg and Martineau in the case of convex sets, and by Gindikin and Henkin in the more general case of
\mathbb{C}
-convex sets; see
[
2
,
12
,
7
]
. The
Fantappié transform
on
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(K)
, given by

\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\mu\right)(z)=\mu\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-\left\langle\cdot,z\right\rangle\right)^{k}}\right),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(K),z\in K^{*},

for any fixed integer
k\geq 1
, gives an explicit isomorphism between the two spaces (see Znamenskii
[
18
]
). Since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(K\right)
is a large space, it is of interest to study the restrictions of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
to certain important subclasses of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(K\right)
. For instance, when
K
is the closure of a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, then under different assumptions on
\Omega
, the restriction of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
to the Bergman space of
\Omega
, the restriction of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
to the holomorphic Hardy space of
\Omega
, and the restriction of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
to the space of holomorphic functions with polynomial growth on
\Omega
, has been considered in
[
13
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
1
]
, respectively.

The Paley-Wiener type duality between
(a)
and
(c)
was established by Pólya-Ehrenpreis-Martineau, via the Laplace transform

\mathcal{L}(\mu)(z)=\mu\left(e^{\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\right),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(K),z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

See
[
3
, Theorem 4.1.9]
. As is the case for
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to different subspaces of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(K)
has been considered. For instance, the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to holomorphic Hardy spaces of a bounded domain has been studied for planar domains, strongly convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and some weakly convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
in
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
6
]
, respectively.

In this note, we consider the restriction of the Fantappiè and the Laplace transforms on
p
-Bergman spaces on certain bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
For a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
p\in(1,\infty)
, the
p
-Bergman space on
\Omega
is defined as

A^{p}\left(\Omega\right)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega\right):\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|f(\zeta)\right|^{p}dV(\zeta)\right)^{1/p}<\infty\right\},

where
V
is the Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
. Each
A^{p}(\Omega)
is a Banach space when endowed with the
L^{p}
-norm, and can be viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
via the (possibly non-injective) inclusion

(1.1)

A^{p}(\Omega)\overset{\iota}{\hookrightarrow}L^{p}(\Omega)\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right),

where
\theta:L^{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
is given by

(1.2)

\theta:f\mapsto\left(\mu_{f}:g\mapsto\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\Omega}\overline{f(\zeta)}g(\zeta)dV(\zeta)\right).

The composition
\mathcal{F}_{k}\circ\theta\circ\iota
, denoted simply by
\mathcal{F}_{k}
for convenience, is referred to as the Fantappiè transform on the
p
-Bergman space, and is explicitly given by

\mathcal{F}_{k}(g)(z)=\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\overline{g(\zeta)}}{\left(1-\langle\zeta,z\rangle\right)^{k}}dV(\zeta),\quad g\in A^{p}(\Omega),\,z\in\text{int}\,\Omega^{*}=\overline{\Omega}^{*}.

First, we study the question of when
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{p}(\Omega)
and
A^{p}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)
, where, in an abuse of notation, we denote the open set
\overline{\Omega}^{*}
by
\Omega^{*}
. The range of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
can be sensitive to the choice of
k
, and we focus on the case
k=n+1
. This is motivated by the following observation: if
\mathbb{B}^{n}
denotes the unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, then

\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\left(f\right)(z)=\overline{f(\overline{z})},\quad f\in A^{2}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n}\right),z\in\mathbb{B}^{n},

and thus,
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
is an isometry between
A^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and
A^{2}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n}^{*}\right)
. This follows from the fact that
\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\left(1-\left\langle\overline{\zeta},z\right\rangle\right)^{-(n+1)}
,
\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{B}_{n}
, is the reproducing kernel of
A^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, and
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}}^{*}=\mathbb{B}^{n}
. We prove the following generalization of the above observation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain containing the origin, such that
\Omega^{*}
is strongly convex. Then, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
and
A^{p}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)
.

Theorem
1.1
can now be used to describe the range of the Laplace transform on
A^{2}(\Omega)
, given by

\mathcal{L}(f)(z)=\int_{\Omega}\overline{f(\zeta)}e^{\left\langle\zeta,z\right\rangle}dV(\zeta),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

under an additional assumption of convexity on
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly convex domain containing the origin, such that
\Omega^{*}
is strongly convex. Then the Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}
on
A^{2}(\Omega)

is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\Omega)
, and the weighted Bergman space

A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\omega_{\Omega})=\left\{F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n}):\|F\|_{\omega_{\Omega}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|F(z)|^{2}\omega_{\Omega}(z)<\infty\right\},

with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\omega_{\Omega}}
, where

\displaystyle\omega_{\Omega}(z)=e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}\|z\|^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{n}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

and
H_{\Omega}
is the support function of
\Omega
.

In general,
\Omega^{*}
need not be strongly convex, even if
\Omega
is. However, if
\Omega
is strongly convex
and
circled, i.e., for every
z\in\Omega
,
e^{i\theta}z\in\Omega
for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, then so is the domain
\operatorname{int}\Omega^{*}
. Thus, the above results apply to all circled strongly convex domains.

In the planar case, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are known to hold for
p=2
, under a much less restrictive hypothesis on the domain. In particular, for
every
bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, it is known that
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\Omega)
and
A^{2}(\Omega^{*})
, and
\mathcal{L}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\Omega)
and
A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{\Omega})
; see
[
15
,
14
,
16
]
. We show that these results cannot be extended to higher dimensions in their full generality.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

\Omega=\left\{\left(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|\zeta_{1}|+|\zeta_{2}|<1\right\}
. Then,

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{3}
is a bounded injective operator from
A^{2}(\Omega)
onto a proper subspace of
A^{2}(\Omega^{*})
,

(ii)

\mathcal{L}
is a bounded injective operator from
A^{2}(\Omega)
onto a proper subspace of
A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{\Omega})
.

We provide an outline of the proofs. Let
\Omega
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Then, it admits a strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex neighborhood basis, and hence,
\overline{\Omega}
is
\mathbb{C}
-convex, see
[
3
, page 27]
. Thus, by the duality result stated earlier,
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)
. In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
maps
A^{p}(\Omega)
to
\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)
. Thus, to prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to establish that, for each
p\in(1,\infty)
,

1.

\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
is an
L^{p}(\Omega)
-
L^{p}(\Omega^{*})
-bounded operator,

2.

\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
is injective on
A^{p}(\Omega)
, and

3.

\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
maps
A^{p}(\Omega)
onto
A^{p}(\Omega^{*})
.

For
1
, we use the domain’s strong
\mathbb{C}
-convexity to convert
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
into a singular integral operator on
\Omega^{*}
. This operator is then compared with a projection operator,
\mathcal{B}_{\Omega^{*}}
, on
L^{p}(\Omega^{*})
. This projection operator is a simpler version of an operator used in the literature to study the regularity properties of the Bergman projection on strictly pseudoconvex domains via a Kerzman–Stein-type approach; see
[
9
]
,
[
17
, § 7.1]
, and
[
8
]
. Lemma
3.1
is the key boundedness property of
\mathcal{B}_{\Omega^{*}}
that we need, and this result may be of independent interest. For both
2
and
3
, we use that
A^{p}(\Omega)^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
A^{q}(\Omega)
when
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
; see Lemma
3.4
. This duality allows us to show that
\theta\circ\iota
in (
1.1
) is injective, following which, the injectivity of
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
on
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
gives
2
. For
3
, we show that each function
g
in
A^{p}(\Omega^{*})
induces a bounded functional on
A^{q}(\Omega)
, and thus, can be mapped to a function
\phi_{g}
in
A^{p}(\Omega)=A^{q}(\Omega)^{\prime}
. Using a Cauchy–Fanttapiè-type reproducing formula for
A^{p}(\Omega)
— see Lemma
3.3
— we show that
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}(\phi_{g})
is, in fact,
g
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we relate
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
with
\mathcal{L}
via the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{2}(\Omega)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{2}(\Omega^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\omega_{\Omega})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{n+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{B}_{n}}

where
\mathfrak{B}_{n}:F\mapsto\left(z\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}F(tz)t^{n}e^{-t}dt\right)
is known as the Borel transform. Owing to Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\omega_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}(\Omega^{*})
.

Let
\Omega
be as in Theorem
1.3
. Then, both
\Omega
and
\Omega^{*}
are complete Reinhardt domains (in fact,
\Omega^{*}
is the unit polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
). This allows us to give characterizations of Bergman-space functions on both domains using power-series representations. We produce an explicit element in
A^{2}(\Omega^{*})
that is not in the image of
\mathcal{F}_{3}
, and an element in
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{\Omega})
that is not in the image of
\mathcal{L}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some preparatory results and observations for our main objects of study.

2.1.
Notation

The following notation will be used throughout the paper.

(1)

\mathbb{B}^{n}
denotes the unit Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(2)

d=\partial+\overline{\partial}
denotes the standard exterior derivative.

(3)

d^{c}={i}\left({\overline{\partial}-\partial}\right).

(4)

Given a bounded domain
\Omega
,
\sigma_{\Omega}
denotes the Euclidean surface area measure on
b\Omega
.

(5)

Given two
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions

and
g
on set a

,

–

g\lesssim f
on
X

C_{1}>0

.

–

, for all

.

(6)

\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of all natural numbers, i.e., the set of all positive integers union
\{0\}
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth bounded domain that is star-convex with respect to the origin. The Minkowski functional of
D
is given by

m_{D}(z)=\inf\left\{t>0:t^{-1}z\in D\right\},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Due to the star-convexity of
D
,
m_{D}
is a positively
1
-homogeneous function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, i.e.,
f(tz)=tf(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
t>0
. Moreover,
D=\{z:m_{D}(z)<1\}
, and
bD=\{z:m_{D}(z)=1\}
. We note the following result on the regularity of
m_{D}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth bounded domain that is star-convex with respect to the origin. Further, assume that
D
has non-radial tangents, i.e., for any
\zeta\in bD
,
\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,\eta(\zeta)\rangle\neq 0
, where
\eta(\zeta)
is a normal to
bD
at
\zeta
. Then
m_{D}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since
bD
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, there exists a defining function of
D
,
\rho
, which is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We define
\phi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\left(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\phi\left(z,t\right)=\rho\left(t^{-1}z_{1},t^{-1}z_{2},\cdots,t^{-1}z_{n}\right),\quad\left(z,t\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\left(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\right).

As
\rho\in\mathcal{C}^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\right)
, it follows that
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\left(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\right)\right)
. Also, since
m_{D}
is positively
1
-homogeneous, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
\frac{z}{m_{D}(z)}\in bD
. Consequently,

\phi\left(z,m_{D}(z)\right)=0,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Let us now fix a
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. We claim that
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(\tilde{z},m_{D}(\tilde{z}))\neq 0
. Suppose
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(z_{0},m_{D}(z_{0}))=0
. Then, using the chain rule, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(\tilde{z},m_{D}(\tilde{z}))=\frac{-1}{m_{D}(\tilde{z})}\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial\rho\left(\frac{\tilde{z}}{m_{D}\left(\tilde{z}\right)}\right),\frac{\tilde{z}}{m_{D}\left(\tilde{z}\right)}\right\rangle=0.

As
m_{D}\left(\tilde{z}\right)\neq 0
, it must be that

\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial\rho\left(\frac{\tilde{z}}{m_{D}\left(\tilde{z}\right)}\right),\frac{\tilde{z}}{m_{D}\left(\tilde{z}\right)}\right\rangle=0
. This is a contradiction, since
D
has non-radial tangents by assumption. Thus, we may apply the implicit function theorem to
\phi
and conclude that
m_{D}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

The next result allows us to freely move between
D
and
D^{*}
under the assumption of strong
\mathbb{C}
-convexity.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain that is star-convex with respect to the origin. Further, assume that
D
has non-radial tangents, i.e., for any
\zeta\in bD
,
\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,\eta(\zeta)\rangle\neq 0
, where
\eta(\zeta)
is a normal to
bD
at
\zeta
. Then, the map
T_{D}:D\rightarrow D^{*}
, defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle T_{D}\left(\zeta\right)=\begin{cases}m_{D}^{2}(\zeta)\dfrac{\partial m_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial m_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle},&\quad\zeta\in D\setminus\{0\},\\
0,&\quad\zeta=0,\end{cases}

(a)

is invertible, with
T_{D^{*}}
as the inverse map,

(b)

is a homeomorphism between
D
and
D^{*}
, and

(c)

is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism between
D\setminus\{0\}
and
D^{*}\setminus\{0\}
.

Moreover, if
f
is a measurable function on
D^{*}
, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{D^{*}}f\left(\zeta\right)dV(\zeta)=\int_{D}f\left(T_{D}(\zeta)\right)h_{D}(\zeta)dV(\zeta),

where
h_{D}
is a positive function on
D
, which is bounded above and below on

Proof.

Let
S_{D}:bD\rightarrow bD^{*}
be given by

S_{D}\left(\eta\right)=\frac{\partial m_{D}(\eta)}{\left\langle\partial m_{D}(\eta),\eta\right\rangle},\qquad\eta\in bD.

The following implications follow from
[
10
]
.

(i)

D^{*}
is also a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain that is star-convex with respect to the origin, and

is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth on

; see
[
10

9
]
. Thus, for any
\xi\in bD^{*}
,
\operatorname{Re}\left<\partial m_{D^{*}}(\xi),\xi\right>=\frac{1}{2}
, and hence,

has non-radial tangents.

(ii)

S_{D}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism from

onto

, whose inverse is

; see
[
10
, page

]
.

(iii)

If

is a measurable function with respect to

, then,

\int_{bD^{*}}g\left(\xi\right)d\sigma_{D^{*}}(\xi)=\int_{bD}g\left(S_{D}(\eta)\right)\widetilde{h}(\eta)d\sigma_{D}(\eta),

for some positive continuous function
\widetilde{h}
on

; see
[
10

]
.

(iv)

\displaystyle\int_{D^{*}}f\left(\zeta\right)dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{bD}f\left(rS_{D}(\eta)\right)r^{2n-1}{j_{D}(\eta)}dr\,d\sigma_{D}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{bD^{*}}f\left(r\xi\right)r^{2n-1}{\ell_{D^{*}}(\xi)}dr\,d\sigma_{D^{*}}(\xi),

for some positive continuous functions

and
\ell_{D^{*}}
on
bD
and
bD^{*}
, respectively; see
[
10
, page 308]
.

We write an arbitrary
\zeta\in D
as
\zeta=r\eta
, where

, and
\eta\in bD
. Then. using the

-homogenity of

, it follows that

. From (ii), it is immediate that

is a homeomorphism between

. Furthermore, since

is

-smooth on

is a

-diffeomorphism between

.
Finally, using (iv), we get that

\displaystyle\int_{D^{*}}f\left(\zeta\right)dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{bD}f\left(rS_{D}\left(\eta\right)\right)r^{2n-1}{j_{D}(\eta)}d\sigma_{D}(\eta)\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{bD}f\left(T_{D}(r\eta)\right)r^{2n-1}{j_{D}(\eta)}d\sigma_{D}(\eta)\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{bD}(f\circ T_{D})(r\eta)r^{2n-1}{h_{D}(r\eta)}\ell_{D}(\eta)d\sigma_{D}(\eta)\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D}(f\circ T_{D})(\zeta)h_{D}(\zeta)dV(\zeta),

where
h_{D}(r\eta)=j_{D}(\eta){\ell_{D}(\eta)}^{-1}
, for
r\in(0,1),\eta\in bD
. We obtained the last equality by applying (iv) to
D
, instead of
D^{*}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Note that if the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
, then
\Omega^{*}
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.2
due to strong convexity, and hence
(\Omega^{*})^{*}=\Omega
also satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.2
.

Now, let

(2.4)

\rho_{D}(\zeta)=m_{D}^{2}\left(\zeta\right)-1,\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Since
m_{D}
is a
C^{2}
-smooth function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
\rho_{D}
is a
C^{2}
-smooth defining function of
D
. In fact, the following lemma shows that
\rho_{D}
is also strongly convex on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly convex domain containing the origin. Then
\rho_{D}(\zeta)=m_{D}^{2}(\zeta)-1
is a strongly convex function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Hess}(\rho_{D})(\zeta)
is positive definite, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
\left(\zeta,w\right)\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{2}
. Writing
w
in real co-ordinates as
w=\left(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{2n}\right)
, we get that,

\displaystyle w^{T}\cdot\operatorname{Hess}(\rho_{D})(\zeta)\cdot w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)u_{j}u_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\left(\frac{\partial m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}}(\zeta)\frac{\partial m_{D}}{\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)+m_{D}(\zeta)\frac{\partial^{2}m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)\right)u_{j}u_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}}(\zeta)u_{j}\right)^{2}+2m_{D}(\zeta)\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)u_{j}u_{k}.

Since
m_{D}
is positively
1
-homogeneous, the right-hand side is
0
-homogeneous in
\zeta
, i.e.,
f(t\zeta)=f(\zeta),
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
t>0
.
Thus, it suffices to show that

(2.5)

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}}(\zeta)u_{j}\right)^{2}+2m_{D}(\zeta)\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)u_{j}u_{k}>0,\quad\forall\zeta\in bD\,\text{and}\,\forall w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Now, as
D
is convex,
m_{D}
is a convex function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which implies that

(2.6)

\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)u_{j}u_{k}\geq 0,\quad\text{for all }\zeta\in bD\,\text{and }w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Furthermore, as
\rho_{D}
is a defining function of
D
, it follows that for any
\zeta\in bD
and
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\text{if }\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}}(\zeta)u_{j}=0,\quad\text{then }\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}m_{D}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\zeta)u_{j}u_{k}>0.

Thus, (
2.5
) follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).
∎

3.
Some sngular integral operators

We now inspect some singular integral operators that are closely related to our problem. Given a bounded strongly convex domain
D\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, let

(3.1)

\displaystyle B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{D}(\zeta)\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle+\left(1-m_{D}^{2}(\zeta)\right),\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{D}(f)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right)^{-n-1}dV(\zeta),\quad f\in L^{p}(D),z\in D.

We first establish the
L^{p}
-regularity of
\mathcal{B}_{D}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strongly convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin, and
p\in(1,\infty)
.
The integral operator denoted by
\left|\mathcal{B}_{D}\right|
defined on
L^{p}\left(D\right)
by

\left|\mathcal{B}_{D}\right|(f)(z)=\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left|B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|^{-n-1}dV(\zeta),\quad z\in D,

is a bounded operator on
L^{p}\left(D\right)
.

Proof.

We claim that for
(\zeta,z)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}
,
\operatorname{Re}{B_{D}}(\zeta,z)
vanishes if and only if
\left(\zeta,z\right)\in bD\times bD
and
\zeta=z
.
When
\zeta,z\in bD
, and
\zeta=z
, clearly,
\operatorname{Re}B_{D}
vanishes. For the converse, suppose there is a
(\zeta_{0},z_{0})\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}
such that
\operatorname{Re}B_{D}(\zeta_{0},z_{0})=0
. As
m_{D}
is positively
1
-homogeneous, by Euler’s identity,

(3.3)

\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle=m_{D}(\zeta),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Using this, it follows that
m_{D}^{2}(\zeta_{0})-m_{D}(\zeta_{0})\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta_{0}),z_{0}\right\rangle=m_{D}^{2}(\zeta_{0})-1
. In other words,

(3.4)

m_{D}(\zeta_{0})\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta_{0}),z_{0}\right\rangle=1.

Let us consider the polar set of
D
, denoted as
D^{\circ}
, and defined by

D^{\circ}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:H_{D}(z)<1\},

where
H_{D}
is the support function of
D
.
It is known that
2\partial m_{D}(\zeta)\in bD^{\circ}
for all
\zeta\in bD
; see
[
10
, Section 2]
, and as
\partial m_{D}
is
0
-homogeneous,
2\partial m_{D}(\zeta)\in bD^{\circ}
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.
Hence, by the definition of
D^{\circ}
, we have that
\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta_{0}),z_{0}\right\rangle\leq 1
. Since
m_{D}(\zeta)<1
, whenever
\zeta\in D
, it follows from (
3.4
) that
\left(\zeta_{0},z_{0}\right)\notin{D}\times\overline{D}
. Thus,
\left(\zeta_{0},z_{0}\right)\in bD\times\overline{D}
. However, this means that
\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta_{0}),\zeta_{0}-z_{0}\right\rangle=0
, which implies that the real tangent space to
bD
at
\zeta_{0}
intersects the point
z_{0}
. As
D
is strongly convex, the real tangent space to
bD
at
\zeta_{0}
does not intersect any points in
\overline{D}\setminus\{\zeta_{0}\}
. Hence,
\zeta_{0}=z_{0}
, which proves our claim.

Writing
B_{D}
in terms of
\rho_{D}
, we have that

B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)=\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}\left(\zeta\right).

We claim that
B_{D}
satisfies the following estimate

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left|B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|\approx\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|+\left|\rho_{D}(z)\right|+\left|\operatorname{Im}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle\right|+\|\zeta-z\|^{2},\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}.

To prove this, first choose a small positive number
\delta>0
, and consider the region
G_{\delta}=\{\zeta\in\overline{D}:-\rho_{D}(\zeta)\leq\delta\}
.
Applying Taylor’s theorem for each
\zeta\in G_{\delta}
, we get that

\rho_{D}(z)=\rho_{D}(\zeta)-2\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle+\operatorname{Re}Q_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))+R_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))+E(\zeta,z),\quad\forall z\in\overline{D},

where

\displaystyle Q_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\zeta_{k}}(\zeta)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})(\zeta_{k}-z_{k}),

\displaystyle R_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\overline{\zeta_{k}}}(\zeta)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})\overline{(\zeta_{k}-z_{k})},

and
E(\zeta,z)
is a continuous function on
G_{\delta}\times\overline{D}
, satisfying
\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow z}E(\zeta,z)/\|\zeta-z\|^{2}=0
.
Then

\widetilde{E}\left(\zeta,z\right)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{E(\zeta,z)}{\|\zeta-z\|^{2}},&\quad\text{when}\,\zeta\neq z,\\
0,&\quad\text{when}\,\zeta=z,\end{cases}

is a uniformly continuous function on the compact set
G_{\delta}\times\overline{D}
.
Writing
\zeta
and
z
in real coordinates as

\zeta=(u_{1},u_{2}\cdots,u_{2n})
and
z=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n})
, the real Hessian of
\rho_{D}
at
\zeta
is related to
Q_{\zeta},R_{\zeta}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial u_{j}\partial u_{k}}(\zeta)(u_{j}-x_{j})(u_{k}-x_{k})=\operatorname{Re}Q_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))+R_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z)).

Furthermore, by Lemma
2.3
, as
\rho_{D}
is a strongly convex function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, we get that

C_{1}\|\zeta-z\|^{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}Q_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))+R_{\zeta}(\rho_{D},(\zeta-z))\leq C_{2}\|\zeta-z\|^{2},\quad(\zeta,z)\in G_{\delta}\times\overline{D},

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are positive constants that are independent of both
\zeta
and
z
.
Combining this with the Taylor expansion of
\rho_{D}
above, we get that
for a fixed
\zeta\in G_{\delta}
,

C_{1}\|\zeta-z\|^{2}+E(\zeta,z)\leq 2\operatorname{Re}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right)+\rho_{D}(\zeta)+\rho_{D}(z)\leq C_{2}\|\zeta-z\|^{2}+E(\zeta,z),\quad z\in\overline{D}.

Now, due to the uniform continuity of
\widetilde{E}
, there exists a
\epsilon>0
,
such that for any
\left(\zeta,z\right)\in G_{\delta}\times\overline{D}
satisfying
\|\zeta-z\|<\epsilon
,

\widetilde{C_{1}}\leq\frac{C_{1}\|\zeta-z\|^{2}+E\left(\zeta,z\right)}{\|\zeta-z\|^{2}}\leq C_{2}+\widetilde{E}\left(\zeta,z\right)\leq\widetilde{C_{2}},

where
\widetilde{C_{1}},\widetilde{C_{2}}
are constants that do not depend on
\zeta,z
.
As a result, we have that

(3.6)

2\operatorname{Re}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right)\approx-\rho_{D}(\zeta)-\rho_{D}(z)+\|\zeta-z\|^{2},\quad(\zeta,z)\in G_{\epsilon,\frac{\delta}{2}},

where

G_{\epsilon,\frac{\delta}{2}}=\{\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D};-\rho_{D}(\zeta)<\delta/2,\|\zeta-z\|<\epsilon\}
. Note that both the right-hand side and left-hand side in (
3.6
) vanish if and only if
\zeta
and
z
are in
b\Omega
, and
\zeta=z
. Hence, by the compactness of
\overline{D}\times\overline{D}\setminus G_{\epsilon,\frac{\delta}{2}}
, it follows that

(3.7)

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right)\approx-\rho_{D}(\zeta)-\rho_{D}(z)+\|\zeta-z\|^{2},\quad\forall\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left|B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left|\operatorname{Re}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right)\right|+\left|\operatorname{Im}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle\right|,

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|+\left|\rho_{D}(z)\right|+\left|\operatorname{Im}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle\right|+\|\zeta-z\|^{2},\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}.

This proves (
3.5
).
Now, using (
3.5
), and following the steps presented in
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
, we conclude the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
{\mathcal{B}_{D}}
.
∎

Next, we consider a kernel that appears when one pushes forward the Fantappiè transform on
D^{*}
under the diffeomorphism
T_{D^{*}}
considered in Lemma
2.2
. Let

(3.8)

\displaystyle K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\langle T_{D}(\zeta),z\rangle,\quad(\zeta,z)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\left\langle\partial m_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-m_{D}^{2}(\zeta)z\right\rangle}{\left\langle\partial m_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle},&\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\left(\overline{D}\setminus\{0\}\right)\times\overline{D},\\
1,&\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\{0\}\times\overline{D},\end{cases},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{D}(f)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right)^{-n-1}dV(\zeta),\quad f\in L^{p}(D),z\in D.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
be a bounded strongly convex domain containing the origin. Then,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left|B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|\leq C\left|K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|,\quad\forall\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}.

where
C>0
are constants not depending on
\zeta,z
.

Proof.

Writing
K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)
and
B_{D}(\zeta,z)
, with respect to
\rho_{D}
, we get that

\displaystyle K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\frac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle\right),

\displaystyle B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}\left(\zeta\right).

We write
\zeta\in\overline{D}
as
\zeta=r\xi
, where
r\in[0,1]
and
\xi\in bD
. As
m_{D}
is positively
1
-homogeneous,
\partial m_{D}
is positively
0
-homogeneous. Thus,

(3.12)

\frac{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}=\frac{m_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}=\frac{1}{\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\xi),\xi\right\rangle}.

Now, from (
3.3
), we have that
\operatorname{Re}\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\xi),\xi\right\rangle=1,\,\forall\xi\in bD
. Consequently,
\left|\left\langle 2\partial m_{D}(\xi),\xi\right\rangle\right|\approx 1
on
bD
, which in turn implies that
\left|\dfrac{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}\right|\approx 1
on
\overline{D}
. Thus, if we set

(3.13)

\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)=K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\frac{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)},\quad(\zeta,z)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D},

we have that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left|{K_{D}}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|\approx\left|\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|,\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times\overline{D}.

Now, observe that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{{B_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)}}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\dfrac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\dfrac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle+\rho_{D}(\zeta)\left(\dfrac{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}-1\right)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\dfrac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|\left|\dfrac{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}-1\right|}{\left|\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\dfrac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle\right|}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|}{\left|\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\dfrac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|}{\left|\operatorname{Re}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\dfrac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle\right)\right|}

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{eq:rho m}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|}{\left|\operatorname{Re}\left(\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle-\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right)\right|}

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{eq:real part est}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|}{C_{1}\left(\left|\rho_{D}(\zeta)\right|+\left|\rho_{D}(z)\right|+\|\zeta-z\|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{C_{1}}.

Finally, combining this with (
3.14
), we conclude the lemma.
∎

We now discuss a reproducing kernel on
p
-Bergman spaces of strongly convex domains. Define a
(1,0)
-form as follows. For
\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times D
, consider

(3.15)

G\left(\zeta,z\right)=\dfrac{\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)},

where
\widetilde{K}_{D}
is as in (
3.13
). Since
\rho_{D}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
smooth on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, it follows that for each
z\in D
,
G
is a
C^{1}
-smooth form on
\overline{D}\setminus\{0\}
. We show that the
(n,n)
-form
\left(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}G\right)^{n}(\zeta,z)
reproduces functions in
A^{p}(D)
. For this, we exploit the reproducing property of the Cauchy–Leray operator for functions in the dense subclass
\mathcal{O}\left(D\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\overline{D}\right)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
be a bounded strongly convex domain. Given
p\in(1,\infty)
, let
f\in A^{p}\left(D\right)
. Then, for each
z\in D
,

(3.16)

f(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right).

Proof.

Consider the modified
(1,0)
-form

\displaystyle L\left(\zeta,z\right)=\dfrac{\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)+\frac{\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{1+\rho_{D}(\zeta)}\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}=\dfrac{\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle},\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\overline{D}\times D.

This form is a generating form for a convex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary; see
[
17
, §3]
. Thus the
(n,n-1)
-form,
L\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}L\right)^{n-1}
is a Cauchy–Fanatppiè form on
b\Omega
, and reproduces functions in
\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega\right)\cap\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
from its boundary values. As a consequence, we get that for any
f\in\mathcal{O}\left(D\right)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\overline{D}\right)
,

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{bD}f(\zeta)j^{*}\left(L\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}L\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z)

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{bD}\dfrac{f(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{D}(\zeta),\zeta-z\right\rangle^{n}}j^{*}\left(\partial\rho_{D}\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho_{D}\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta),\quad z\in D,

where
j:bD\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the inclusion map. It is easy to verify that

j^{*}\left(L\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}L\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z)=j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z),\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in bD\times D.

Hence by (
3
),

(3.18)

f(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{bD}f(\zeta)j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z),\quad z\in D.

Now, to conclude (
3.16
) for
f
, we wish to apply Stokes’ theorem to the term on the right-hand side of the above equality. However, since the form
\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}
is not
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth at the origin, we cannot apply Stokes’ theorem directly. To get around this issue, let us first fix a
z\in D
. Consider
\lambda\in(0,1)
, such that
z\in D\setminus\overline{\lambda D}
, where
\lambda D=\{\lambda\zeta:\zeta\in D\}
. Applying Stokes’ theorem on the domain
D\setminus\overline{\lambda D}
, we obtain that

(3.19)

\int_{D\setminus\overline{\lambda D}}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right)=\int_{bD}f(\zeta)j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z)\minus\int_{\lambda bD}f(\zeta)j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z).

By a straightforward computation, we can verify that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}(\zeta,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(\left(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho_{D}\right)^{n-1}\wedge\overline{\partial}\widetilde{K}_{D}\wedge\partial\rho_{D}+\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho_{D}\right)^{n}\right)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathfrak{h}_{D}\left(\zeta\right)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)^{n+1}}dV(\zeta),\quad\left(\zeta,z\right)\in\left(D\setminus\{0\}\right)\times D,

where

\mathfrak{h}_{D}(\zeta)=c_{n}\det\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\rho(\zeta){\left\langle\partial\rho(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}}{1+\rho(\zeta)}&\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}}\\
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\zeta}_{j}}\left(\dfrac{\rho(\zeta)\left\langle\partial\rho(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle}{1+\rho(\zeta)}\right)&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\bar{\zeta}_{k}}\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j,k\leq n},\quad\zeta\in D.

Similarly, using
[
17
, Lemma 3.4.]
, we have that

\displaystyle j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z)=\frac{j^{*}\left(\partial\rho_{D}\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho_{D}\right)^{n-1}\right)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)^{n}}=\dfrac{\mathfrak{h}_{bD}\left(\zeta\right)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)^{n}}\sigma_{D},

where
\sigma_{D}
is the surface area measure on
bD
, and

\mathfrak{h}_{bD}\left(\zeta\right)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\pi^{n}}\det\begin{pmatrix}0&\dfrac{\partial\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}}\\
\dfrac{\partial\rho_{D}}{\partial\bar{\zeta}_{j}}&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\bar{\zeta}_{k}}\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j,k\leq n},\quad\zeta\in bD.

Since
D
is strongly convex,
\mathfrak{h}_{bD}
is bounded above and below by positive constants.
Furthermore, since
m_{D}
is
1
-homogeneous, the second order partial derivatives of
\rho_{D}
are
0
-homogeneous, which implies that
\sup_{\zeta\in\overline{D}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\bar{\zeta}_{k}}(\zeta)=\sup_{\zeta\in bD}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\bar{\zeta}_{k}}(\zeta)
. Consequently, as
\rho_{D}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth on
bD
, it follows that
\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{D}}{\partial\zeta_{j}\partial\bar{\zeta}_{k}}
is bounded above on
\overline{D}
. Hence

(3.21)

\left|\mathfrak{h}_{D}(\zeta)\right|\leq C,\quad\zeta\in\overline{D},

where
C
is a positive constant.
As mentioned earlier, since
z\in D
is fixed
\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)\neq 0,\,\forall\zeta\in\overline{D}
, and hence by continuity
\left|\frac{1}{\widetilde{K}_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)}\right|\leq C_{z}
, where
C_{z}
is a constant depending on
z
. Thus combining this with (
3.20
), (
3.21
), and the fact
f\in\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{D}\right)
,we get that

\left|\int_{D\setminus\overline{\lambda D}}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right)\right|\lesssim\text{vol}(D).

Thus, applying the dominated convergence theorem, we get that, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
,

\int_{D\setminus\overline{\lambda D}}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right)\rightarrow\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right).

Applying the change of variable
\zeta=\lambda\zeta^{\prime}
, where
\zeta^{\prime}\in bD
, we get that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\lambda bD}f(\zeta)j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda bD}j^{*}\left(\partial\rho_{D}\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho_{D}\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta),

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{2n}\int_{bD}j^{*}\left(\partial\rho_{D}\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho_{D}\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{2n}\sigma\left(bD\right).

The right-hand side tends to
0
, as
\lambda
tends to
0
. Hence,
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\int_{\lambda bD}f(\zeta)j^{*}\left(G\wedge\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n-1}\right)(\zeta,z)\rightarrow 0
. Combining these observations with (
3.19
) and (
3.18
), we get that

f(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right),\quad z\in D.

Since
D
is a convex domain, the class
\mathcal{O}\left(D\right)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\overline{D}\right)
is dense in
A^{p}\left(D\right)
. Hence to prove (
3.16
) for
A^{p}(D)
functions, where
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
, it is enough to show that for every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
, the integral operator

L^{p}(D)\ni f\mapsto\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{D}f\left(\zeta\right)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,z\right),

is a bounded operator on
L^{p}\left(D\right)
. To see this, for
f\in L^{p}\left(D\right)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{D}f\left(\zeta\right)\left(\overline{\partial}G\right)^{n}\left(\zeta,\cdot\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(D)}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{D}f\left(\zeta\right)\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{D}(\zeta)}{\widetilde{K}_{D}(\zeta,\cdot)^{n+1}}dV(\zeta)\right\|_{L^{p}(D)}^{p}

\displaystyle(\text{by}\,\eqref{eq:comp vol},\eqref{eq:density bdd abv})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{D}\left|f\left(\zeta\right)\right|\left|K_{D}\left(\zeta,\cdot\right)\right|^{-n-1}dV(\zeta)\right\|_{L^{p}(D)}^{p}

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma~\ref{le:comp of two ker}})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{D}\left|f\left(\zeta\right)\right|\left|B_{D}\left(\zeta,\cdot\right)\right|^{-n-1}dV(\zeta)\right\|_{L^{p}(D)}^{p}

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma~\ref{le:bdd of LS ker}})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(D)}^{p}.

This proves our claim, and hence completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we note the following duality result on strongly convex domains, which is implicitly present in the literature. In fact, the result holds for all
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex domains.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly convex domain. Given
p\in(1,\infty)
, let
q\in(1,\infty)
be the conjugate exponent of
p
, i.e.,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Then, the map

\Phi_{p}:A^{q}\left(D\right)\rightarrow A^{p}(D)^{\prime}
, defined as

\Phi_{p}(f)(h)=\int_{D}h(\zeta)\overline{f(\zeta)}dV(\zeta),\quad f\in A^{q}\left(D\right),\,h\in A^{p}\left(D\right),

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
.
By
[
5
, Theorem 2.15]
, a sufficient condition for the surjectivity of
\Phi_{p}
is the
L^{p}\left(D\right)
-boundedness of the Bergman projection on
D
. This is indeed the case for bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly convex domains, as shown in
[
8
]
. Following the same argument but switching the roles of
p
and
q
, we obtain that
\Phi_{q}
is also surjective. Hence, from
[
5
, Corollary 2.14]
, it follows that
\Phi_{p}
is injective, which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we introduce the Borel transform on

with

.

Let

. The Borel transform on

(3.23)

\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(F\right)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(tz)e^{-t}t^{n}dt,\quad F\in\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{\operatorname{exp}}}(D).

It follows from the definition that
\mathfrak{B}_{n}(F)\in\mathcal{O}(D^{\circ})
, where

is the polar set of

. Furthermore, it is known that
\mathfrak{B}_{n}(F)

; see
[
12
]
, or
[
3
, page 139]
. Next, we show that for planar strongly convex domain,
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is an isomorphism between

\omega_{D}=e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}\|z\|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{D}\right)

.

]

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strongly convex domain with

. Then the identity map is a normed space isomorphism between

and
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mu_{D})

\displaystyle A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mu_{D})=\left\{F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n}):\|F\|_{\mu_{D}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}{|F(z)|^{2}}{\left\|e^{\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\right\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(D)}}\left(dd^{c}H_{D}\right)^{n}(z)<\infty\right\},

\|.\|_{\mu_{D}}

Proof.

Following the same idea as in the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 7.2]
, it is sufficient to prove that for any

, satisfying
\|z\|\geq 1
,

(3.24)

\left\|e^{\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\approx{e^{2H_{D}(z)}}\|z\|^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}.

To show this, by applying (
2.3
) to

(3.25)

\displaystyle{e^{-2H_{D}(z)}}\left\|e^{\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{bD}e^{2r\operatorname{Re}\langle\xi,z\rangle-2H_{D}(z)}}r^{2n-1}d\sigma_{D}(\xi)dr

Also, applying

\displaystyle\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{-2C_{1}\|z\|\left(1-\operatorname{Re}\langle\eta,z\rangle\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}},(\eta)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\|z\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-{(1-r)H_{D}(z)}}r^{2n-1}dr\lesssim\|z\|^{n+\frac{1}{2}}{e^{-2H_{D}(z)}}\|e^{\cdot z}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\lesssim\|z\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-{(1-r)H_{D}(z)}}r^{n-\frac{1}{2}}dr.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded strongly convex domain with
0\in D
. Then
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{D})
and
A^{2}(D^{*})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the following space:

B_{2}^{1}\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus D\right)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus D):f(\infty)=0,\,\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus D}|f^{\prime}(\lambda)|^{2}dV(\lambda)<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{B_{2}^{1}\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus D\right)}^{2}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus D}|f^{\prime}(\lambda)|^{2}dV(\lambda)\right)
.
Since
D^{*}
is biholomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus D
via the map
\tau:\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1}{\lambda}
, the following map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}:

\displaystyle B_{2}^{1}\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus D\right)\rightarrow A^{2}(D^{*}),

\displaystyle F\rightarrow(F\circ\tau)^{\prime},

is a normed space isomorphism between
B_{2}^{1}\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus D\right)
, and
A^{2}(D^{*})
.
From
[
16
, Preliminary theorem]
, it follows that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}:A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mu_{D})\rightarrow B_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus D)
, defined as

\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(F)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t)e^{-tz}dt,

is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mu_{D})
and
B_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus D)
. Note that, in
[
16
]
, the inverse of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}
has been considered as the Borel transform. For
z\in D^{*}
, applying a change of variable
t^{\prime}=\frac{t}{z}
we get that

\displaystyle\left(\mathfrak{T}\circ\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(F)\right)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t)e^{-t/z}tdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t^{\prime}z)e^{-t^{\prime}}t^{\prime}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{1}(F)(z).

Combining all these with Lemma
3.5
, it follows that,
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{D})
and

.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
. As discussed in the introduction, the proof splits into three parts: the
L^{p}
-boundedness, the injectivity, and the surjectivity of
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
.

4.1.
\mathbf{L^{p}}
-boundedness of the Fantappiè transform.

By Lemma
2.2
, applied to
D=\Omega^{*}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\left(f\right)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\Omega^{*}}\dfrac{\overline{\left(f\circ T_{\Omega^{*}}\right)}(\zeta)\left\langle\partial m_{\Omega^{*}}(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle^{n+1}h_{\Omega^{*}}(\zeta)}{\left\langle\partial m_{\Omega^{*}}(\zeta),\zeta-m_{\Omega^{*}}^{2}(\zeta)z\right\rangle^{n+1}}dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\Omega^{*}}\overline{\left(f\circ T_{\Omega^{*}}\right)}(\zeta)h_{\Omega^{*}}(\zeta)K_{\Omega^{*}}\left(\zeta,z\right)^{-n-1}dV(\zeta),\quad f\in A^{P}\left(\Omega\right),z\in\Omega^{*},

where
K_{\Omega^{*}}
is as given by (
3.9
).

Now, for a bounded strongly convex domain
D\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
containing the origin, let

|\mathcal{K}_{D}|(f)(z)=\int_{\Omega}f(\zeta)|K_{D}\left(\zeta,z\right)|^{-n-1}dV(\zeta),\quad z\in D.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have that for any
g\in L^{p}\left(D\right)
,

(4.1)

\left|\mathcal{K}_{D}\right|\left(\left|g\right|\right)(z)\leq\tilde{C}\left|\mathcal{B}_{D}\right|\left(\left|g\right|\right)(z),\quad\forall z\in D,

where
\tilde{C}
is a positive constant, which is independent of
z
and
g
.

Applying (
4.1
) to
D=\Omega^{*}
, we get for
g\in L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{n+1}(g)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{*})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{K}_{\Omega^{*}}\left(\overline{\left(g\circ T_{\Omega^{*}}\right)}h_{\Omega^{*}}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\mathcal{K}_{\Omega^{*}}\right|\left(\left|\overline{\left(g\circ T_{\Omega^{*}}\right)}\right|\left|h_{\Omega^{*}}\right|\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{*})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\mathcal{B}_{\Omega^{*}}\right|\left(\left|\overline{\left(g\circ T_{\Omega^{*}}\right)}\right|\left|h_{\Omega^{*}}\right|\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{*})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{\left(g\circ T_{\Omega^{*}}\right)}h_{\Omega^{*}}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)}

where the last inequality follows due to the boundedness of
h_{\Omega^{*}}
. This shows that
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
is a bounded operator from
A^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
to
L^{p}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)
,
\forall p\in(1,\infty)
.

4.2.
Injectivity of the Fantappiè transform

Recall that
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
on
A^{p}(\Omega)
is, in fact, the composition

A^{p}(\Omega)\overset{\iota}{\hookrightarrow}L^{p}(\Omega)\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(\overline{\Omega})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}_{n+1}}\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{*}),

where
\iota
is the inclusion map, and
\theta
is as in (
1.2
). By the Martineau–Aizenberg duality theorem, the right-most map is already known to be injective. Thus, we must show that, for
g\in A^{p}(\Omega)
, if
(\theta\circ\iota)(g)=\mu_{g}
given by

\mu_{g}:f\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\overline{g(\zeta)}f(\zeta)dV(\zeta),\,\quad f\in\mathcal{O}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)

is the zero map, then
g\equiv 0
. Let
g\in A^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
, such that
\mu_{g}=0
.
For any
f
in the dense subspace
\mathcal{O}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)\subset A^{q}\left(\Omega\right)
,
\mu_{g}(f)=\overline{\Phi_{g}(f)}
, where
\Phi_{g}
is as in Lemma
3.4
. Thus,
\Phi_{g}\equiv 0
, which implies that
g\equiv 0
. This completes the proof of injectivity.

4.3.
Surjectivity of the Fantappiè transform

Let
g\in A^{p}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)
, where
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
. We define a linear functional
F_{g}
on
A^{q}\left(\Omega\right)
, where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
, as follows

F_{g}(\psi)=\int_{\Omega}\psi\left(\zeta\right)\left(g\circ T_{\Omega}\right)\left(\zeta\right)\left(\mathfrak{H}\circ T_{\Omega}\right)\left(\zeta\right)dV(\zeta),\quad\forall\psi\in A^{q}\left(\Omega\right),

where
\mathfrak{H}:\Omega^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, defined as

(4.2)

\mathfrak{H}\left(\eta\right)={\mathfrak{h}_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta)}{h_{\Omega}\left(T_{\Omega^{*}}\left(\eta\right)\right)}\left(\frac{1+\rho_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta),\eta\right\rangle}\right)^{n+1},

where
\mathfrak{h}_{\Omega^{*}}
,
h_{\Omega}
are as in (
3.20
) and (
2.2
), respectively, and
T_{\Omega},T_{\Omega^{*}}
are as in (
2.1
).
Since
h_{\Omega}(T_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta))
,
\left|\mathfrak{h}_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta)\right|
, and
\left|\frac{1+\rho_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta)}{\left\langle\partial\rho_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta),\eta\right\rangle}\right|
are bounded above by positive constants on
\Omega^{*}
,
\left|\mathfrak{H}\right|
is bounded above on
\Omega^{*}
. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|F_{g}\left(\psi\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|g\circ T_{\Omega}\right|^{p}\left(\zeta\right)dV\left(\zeta\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}\left(\Omega\right)},

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}\left(\Omega\right)}.

This shows that
F_{g}
is a bounded linear functional on
A^{q}\left(\Omega\right)
. Thus, by Lemma
3.4
, there exists a
\phi_{g}\in A^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
, such that

F_{g}(\psi)=\int_{\Omega}\overline{\phi_{g}\left(\zeta\right)}\psi\left(\zeta\right)dV\left(\zeta\right).

Now, for a fixed
z\in\Omega^{*}
, taking
\psi(\zeta)=\left(1-\left\langle\zeta,z\right\rangle\right)^{-n-1}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n+1}(\phi_{g})(z)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\overline{\phi_{g}\left(\zeta\right)}}{\left(1-\left\langle\zeta,z\right\rangle\right)^{n+1}}dV\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(g\circ T_{\Omega}\right)\left(\zeta\right)}{\left(1-\left\langle\zeta,z\right\rangle\right)^{n+1}}\left(\mathfrak{H}\circ T_{\Omega}\right)\left(\zeta\right)dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle(\text{since }T_{\Omega}^{-1}=T_{\Omega^{*}})\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(T_{\Omega}(\zeta))\mathfrak{H}(T_{\Omega}(\zeta))}{\left(1-\left\langle T_{\Omega^{*}}(T_{\Omega}(\zeta)),z\right\rangle\right)^{n+1}}\frac{h_{\Omega}(\zeta)}{h_{\Omega}(T_{\Omega^{*}}(T_{\Omega}(\zeta)))}dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{le:cov}})\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{*}}\dfrac{g(\eta)\mathfrak{H}(\eta)}{\left(1-\left\langle T_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta),z\right\rangle\right)^{n+1}}\frac{1}{h_{\Omega}(T_{\Omega^{*}}(\eta))}dV(\eta)

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{eq:def of FLD kera}, \eqref{eq:tildeK}, and \eqref{eq:mathfrak H}})\quad

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\overline{\zeta_{1}^{m_{1}}}\overline{\zeta_{2}^{m_{2}}}\zeta_{1}^{k_{1}}\zeta_{2}^{k_{2}}dV(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+4\right)}\frac{{\left(2m_{1}+1\right)!}{\left(2m_{2}+1\right)!}}{{\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+3\right)!}},&\quad\text{for}\,(k_{1},k_{2})=(m_{1},m_{2}),\\
0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Replacing this in the above equation, the claim follows for
p_{k}
, and hence for
f\in A^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
.
∎

Let

, with power series as in (
6.3
). Due to Lemma

, we get that for

,
\mathcal{F}_{3}\left(f\right)(z)=\sum\limits_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}

Now, using (

), (

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{3}\left(f\right)\|_{A^{2}\left(\Omega^{*}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{|t_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}}{(2m_{1}+2)(2m_{2}+2)}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{\left|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}\right|^{2}}{\left(2m_{1}+1\right)\left(2m_{2}+1\right)}\frac{{\left(m_{1}+m_{2}+1\right)!}^{2}}{{m_{1}!}^{2}{m_{2}!}^{2}}\frac{{\left(2m_{1}+1\right)!}^{2}{\left(2m_{2}+1\right)!}^{2}}{{\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+3\right)!}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}}{(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)}\frac{{\left(2m_{1}+1\right)!}{\left(2m_{2}+1\right)!}}{{\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+3\right)!}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{A^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2},

where the second last inequality follows from Stirling’s approximation applied as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}+2}{\left(2m_{1}+1\right)\left(2m_{2}+1\right)}\frac{{\left(m_{1}+m_{2}+1\right)!}^{2}}{{m_{1}!}^{2}{m_{2}!}^{2}}\frac{{\left(2m_{1}+1\right)!}{\left(2m_{2}+1\right)!}}{{\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+3\right)!}}\approx{m_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+m_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim 1.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{A^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2},

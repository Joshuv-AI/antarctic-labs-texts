1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
K
. The group
E(K)
of
K
-rational points, known as the Mordell–Weil group, is finitely generated, and its rank is a fundamental arithmetic invariant. One fruitful approach to studying the structure of
E(K)
is through Galois cohomology. For a fixed integer
n\geq 1
, the
n
-torsion subgroup
E[n]\subset E(\overline{K})
carries a natural action of the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{G}_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{n}(E/K)\subset H^{1}(K,E[n])
, defined by imposing local conditions at all primes of
K
, sits in a natural short exact sequence,

0\rightarrow E(K)/nE(K)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{n}(E/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E/K)[n]\rightarrow 0

encoding information about both the Mordell–Weil group and the Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/K)
.

A central question in arithmetic geometry is to understand how the Mordell–Weil rank of a fixed elliptic curve varies in field extensions. For instance, given an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, it is natural to ask how often its rank remains the same in a finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
. More specifically, one studies how frequently
\operatorname{rank}E(L)=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})
as
L
ranges over number fields of fixed degree, ordered by discriminant.

In particular, these diophantine stability questions are studied for cyclic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
with prime degree. Mazur and Rubin
[
MR10
]
studied the distribution of 2-Selmer ranks in families of quadratic twists of a fixed elliptic curve. Their techniques yield results on the stability of rank across large sets of quadratic twists, and under certain conditions, also demonstrate that arbitrarily large ranks can arise after base change by quadratic extensions. For any prime
\ell
, Klagsbrun–Mazur–Rubin
[
KMR14
]
, Mazur–Rubin
[
MRL18
]
have studied the behavior of
\ell
-Selmer groups in
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
-extensions, establishing patterns of stability and growth. Smith has recently obtained similar results using arithmetic statistics and the geometry of numbers
[
Smi22
,
Smi23
]
.

The broader phenomenon of rank growth and stability in families of number field extensions has attracted significant recent attention. For example, see the work of Lemke-Oliver and Thorne
[
LOT21
]
, Shnidman and Weiss
[
SW23
]
, Beneish–Kundu–Ray
[
BKR24
]
, Berg–Ryan–Young
[
BRY24
]
, Keliher
[
Kel24
]
, Pathak–Ray
[
PR25
]
and Keliher–Park
[
KP25
]
.

Understanding Galois extensions of a fixed number field with a prescribed Galois group is a fundamental problem in number theory. Conjectures predicting the asymptotic number of such extensions, ordered by their absolute discriminant, were proposed by Malle
[
Mal02a
,
Mal04
]
. These conjectures may be viewed as statistical refinements of the classical inverse Galois problem. Substantial progress towards this has been made in special cases. For example, the conjecture is known for abelian groups, due to the work of Mäki
[
Mäk85
]
and Wright
[
Wri89
]
, and more recently for certain nilpotent groups by Koymans and Pagano
[
KP23
]
. Although the inverse Galois problem for finite solvable groups over number fields was resolved by Shafarevich
[
Š54
]
, Malle’s conjecture remains open for general solvable groups.

1.2.
Main results

In this article, we apply Galois-theoretic methods to study the behavior of the Mordell–Weil rank and Selmer group of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
in certain families of Galois extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with a prescribed Galois group. Fix a prime
\ell\geq 5
, a finite
\ell
-group
G
, and an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
. Assume that
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
, or equivalently,
E(\mathbb{Q})[\ell]=0
,
\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[\ell]=0
. Further, suppose that the Galois representation

\rho_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})

associated to
E[\ell]
is surjective. Under these assumptions, we show that there exist infinitely many Galois extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G
such that
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/L)=0
. Moreover, we obtain an asymptotic lower bound for the number of such extensions ordered by their absolute discriminant.

For each real number
X>0
, let
\mathcal{N}(G;X)
denote the number of Galois extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
G
and discriminant satisfying
|\Delta_{L}|\leq X
. The Hermite–Minkowski theorem ensures that
\mathcal{N}(G;X)
is finite for every
X
, and hence is well-defined.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite group and
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow S_{\#\mathcal{G}}
denote the regular representation of
\mathcal{G}
. For
g\in\mathcal{G}
, define the index of
g
, denoted
\operatorname{ind}(g)
, as

\operatorname{ind}(g)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\mathcal{G}-\#\text{ orbits of }\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(g).

We set
a(\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\min_{g\neq 1}\operatorname{ind}(g)\right)^{-1}
and note that when
\mathcal{G}
is an
\ell
-group with
\ell^{n}
elements,
a(\mathcal{G})=\left(\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)\right)^{-1}
. The weak form of Malle’s conjecture (cf.
[
Mal02b
, p.316]
) predicts that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist constants
c_{1}(\mathcal{G}),c_{2}(\mathcal{G};\epsilon)>0
such that

c_{1}(\mathcal{G})\,X^{a(\mathcal{G})}\leq\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{G};X)\leq c_{2}(\mathcal{G};\epsilon)\,X^{a(\mathcal{G})+\epsilon}

for all sufficiently large
X
. This has been established for all finite nilpotent groups by Klüners and Malle
[
KM04
]
. For finite nilpotent groups satisfying additional hypotheses, Koymans and Pagano
[
KP23
]
have proven a stronger version of Malle’s conjecture, obtaining the exact asymptotic.

On the other hand, let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G},E;X)
denote the number of Galois extensions
F/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathcal{G}
,
|\Delta_{F}|\leq X
, and
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/F)=0
. Note that for such fields
F/\mathbb{Q}
,
\operatorname{rank}E(F)=0
and
\Sh(E/F)[\ell]=0
. For a group
G
with
\#G=\ell^{n}
, our main result establishes asymptotic lower bounds for
\mathcal{M}(G,E;X)
as
x\to\infty
, showing that it grows comparably to
\mathcal{N}(G;X)
. This demonstrates that the vanishing of the
\ell
-Selmer group over such
G
-extensions is not a rare phenomenon.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number, and let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
. Assume further that the representation
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective. Then

\mathcal{M}(G,E;X)\gg X^{\frac{1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)}}(\log X)^{\delta-1}=X^{a(G)}(\log X)^{\delta-1},

where
\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell-1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell^{2}-1)}
.

It follows that the asymptotic growth of
\mathcal{M}(G,E;X)
matches that of
\mathcal{N}(G;X)
up to a power of
\log X
. In particular,

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathcal{M}(G,E;X)}{\log\mathcal{N}(G;X)}=1.

For
\ell=5
, it is proven by Bhargava and Shankar
[
BS13
]
that there is a positive density of elliptic curves (ordered by height)
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
. On the other hand, a result of Duke
[
Duk97
]
shows that
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective for almost all elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus for
\ell=5
, the conditions of Theorem
1.1
hold for a positive density of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
.

1.3.
Methodology

Let
L/K
be an
\ell
-cyclic extension of number fields, and let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
. Suppose in addition that the
\ell
-Selmer group of
E
over
K
vanishes. Analyzing local to global control arguments for Selmer groups, we show that under certain ramification and splitting conditions for the extension
L/K
, the implication

\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/K)=0\Rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/L)=0

holds. The argument then follows via induction on the length of
G
. The inverse Galois problem for finite
\ell
-groups is known from the work of Reichardt
[
Rei37
]
and Scholz
[
Sch37
]
. The Galois cohomological strategy allows us to inductively construct towers of number fields

\mathbb{Q}=L_{0}\subset L_{1}\subset\dots\subset L_{n-1}\subset L_{n}=F,

where
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G
and
L_{i}/L_{i-1}
is an
\ell
-cyclic extension. Furthermore, extending the methods of Klüners and Malle, we are able to construct many such extensions, for which at each stage, the implication

\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/L_{i-1})=0\Rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/L_{i})=0

is satisfied. A synthesis of methods from Galois cohomology, arithmetic statistics and analytic number theory give us an asymptotic lower bound, in terms of
X
, for the number of such extensions
F/\mathbb{Q}
with
|\Delta_{F}|\leq X
.

1.4.
Organization

Including the introduction, the article is organized into four sections. Section
2
revisits the classical strategy of Reichardt
[
Rei37
]
and Scholz
[
Sch37
]
for resolving the inverse Galois problem for finite
\ell
-groups over
\mathbb{Q}
. Our emphasis is on making explicit which primes split or ramify in such extensions. This information is essential for analyzing the behavior of Selmer groups in Galois towers. In Section
3
, we establish sufficient conditions on an
\ell
-cyclic extension
L/K
under which the vanishing of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/K)
implies the vanishing of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/L)
. These build upon previous constructions in
[
PR25
, Section 2]
. We conclude this section by proving the existence of infinitely many
G
-extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/L)=0
. Finally, Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We recall key results of Klüners and Malle concerning the enumeration of
G
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
ordered by absolute discriminant. The problem is reduced to counting global Galois cohomology classes subject to prescribed local conditions. To estimate the size of these cohomology groups, we invoke a formula of Wiles which yields precise asymptotics, allowing us to deduce the desired lower bounds.

2.
The inverse Galois problem for finite
\ell
-groups

Shafarevich proved that given any number field

and a finite solvable group

with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\simeq G
(see
[

]
and
[

\ell
be an odd prime number. Given a natural number

\mu_{k}
denote the set of
k

G
with
\#G=\ell^{n}
. In this special case, the inverse Galois problem over
\mathbb{Q}
for the group

is due to Reichardt
[
Rei37
]
and Scholz
[
Sch37
]
. An exposition of their method can be found in
[

2.1.
The embedding problem

We show that there is a Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G
. We note that
G
has a nontrivial center. Thus by induction on

, there is a filtration of

(2.1)

\{1\}=G_{n}\subset G_{n-1}\subset\dots\subset G_{1}\subset G_{0}=G

such that for all

•

G_{i-1}/G_{i}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
,

•

1\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\to G/G_{i}\to G/G_{i-1}\to 1
is a central extension of

We construct Galois extensions

K=L_{0}\subset L_{1}\subset L_{2}\subset\dots L_{n-1}\subset L_{n}=L

\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}/K)\simeq G/G_{i}
. We assume that

Let

be a finite
\ell

be a central extension of

(2.2)

1\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\iota}\widetilde{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}G\rightarrow 1.

Let
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
and assume that there exists a Galois extension

such that
{\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G}
. This gives rise to a surjection
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow G

can be embedded into a larger Galois extension

along with an isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}&0&\to&\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{L}/L)&\to&\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})&\to&\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})&\to 0\\
&&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&\\
&0&\to&\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}&\to&\widetilde{G}&\to&G&\to 0.\end{array}

In the above diagram, the downward arrows are isomorphisms. Equivalently, the embedding problem is solvable if

can be lifted to a surjective homomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{G}
. Indeed, setting
\widetilde{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\operatorname{ker}\widetilde{\varphi}}
we find that

. Note that if the extension (
2.2
) is non-split then any lift
\widetilde{\varphi}
is surjective. In order to inductively construct the extensions

, it suffices to solve the embedding problem for

1\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow G/G_{i+1}\rightarrow G/G_{i}\rightarrow 1

for each

0\leq i<n
.

We recall how to parametrize group extensions by cohomology classes. Let
A
be a given abelian group and
G

by

1\rightarrow A\rightarrow\widetilde{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}G\rightarrow 1.

Then

has an induced

\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\widetilde{G}
be a set theoretic section of the map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G
. Since
\pi
is surjective, such a map exists. We define the action of
G

as follows: for

\sigma\cdot a\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\eta(\sigma)\,a\,\eta(\sigma)^{-1}.

There are a few things to take note of here. First, since
A
is a normal subgroup of

\eta(\sigma)\,a\,\eta(\sigma)^{-1}
is in

is independent of the choice of set theoretic section

. From here on, when referring to

as a

such that the following diagram commutes:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}A&\longrightarrow&\widetilde{G}_{1}&\longrightarrow&G\\
\|&&\downarrow{f}&&\|\\
A&\longrightarrow&\widetilde{G}_{2}&\longrightarrow&G.\end{array}

Proposition 2.5
.

The embedding problem for (
2.2
) is solvable if and only if
\varphi^{*}(\theta)=0
, where
\varphi^{*}
is given by (
2.3
).

Proof.

This is a well known result, we give a sketch of the argument. Setting
\mathfrak{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widetilde{G}\times_{G}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},
consider the fiber product diagram:

\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}1&\to&\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}&\to&\mathfrak{G}&\rightarrow&\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}&\to 1\\
&&\|&&\downarrow&&\downarrow{\varphi}&\\
1&\to&\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}&\to&\widetilde{G}&\rightarrow&G&\to 1.\end{array}

It is easy to see that the top row is a central extension corresponding to
\varphi^{*}(\theta)
. This sequence splits if and only if
\varphi^{*}(\theta)=0
. If the extension splits, there exists a section
j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{G}
to the projection map
\pi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{G}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p_{1}\circ j
is a lift of
\varphi
. Since it is assumed that (
2.2
) is nonsplit,
\widetilde{\varphi}
is surjective. The proof of the converse follows along similar lines, and is omitted.
∎

In what follows, for ease of notation, set
H^{i}(F,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{F},\cdot)
, where
F
is a field of characteristic
0
. Given a prime number
p
, denote by

\operatorname{res}^{i}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{i}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})

the natural restriction map. This induces a map

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\bigoplus_{p}\operatorname{res}_{p}^{2}}\bigoplus_{p}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}).

Proposition 2.6
.

The map
\alpha
is an injection.

Proof.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic zero that contains the
\ell
-th roots of unity, and let
\operatorname{Br}(F)
denote its Brauer group. By the standard identification
\operatorname{Br}(F)\cong H^{2}(F,\overline{F}^{\times})
. Letting
\operatorname{Br}(F)[\ell]
be the
\ell
-torsion in the Brauer group, there is a natural isomorphism:

H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{Br}(F)[\ell].

Now, let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
be the cyclotomic field generated by the
\ell
-th roots of unity, and let
M_{K}
denote the set of places of
K
. By the Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem, the natural localization map

\alpha_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in M_{K}}H^{2}(K_{v},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})

is injective, meaning that an element of
H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
is determined by its local invariants.

Since
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension with degree prime to
\ell
, the inflation–restriction sequence for group cohomology shows that the restriction map

H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})

is also injective.

These maps fit naturally into the following commutative diagram:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})&\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}}&H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\\
\alpha\Big\downarrow&&\Big\downarrow\alpha_{K}\\
\bigoplus\limits_{v\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})&\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}}&\bigoplus\limits_{v\in M_{K}}H^{2}(K_{v},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}).\end{array}

We deduce from injectivity of the horizontal restriction maps and the injectivity of
\alpha_{K}
that
\alpha
is injective.
∎

Given a prime
p
, let
G_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\subset G
, where
v
is a prime of
L
lying over
p
. A different choice of
v
gives rise to the same subgroup of
G
up to conjugation by an element of
G
. Set
\theta_{p}
to denote the image of
\theta
with respect to the natural restriction map:

\operatorname{res}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(G_{p},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}).

Consider the associated central extension

(2.4)

0\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\to\widetilde{G}_{p}\to G_{p}\to 1

and let
\varphi_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\twoheadrightarrow G_{p}
be the natural quotient map. The
local embedding problem
then asks whether
\varphi_{p}
admits a lift to a homomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}_{p}
. Here we do not insist that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}
is surjective. By arguments similar to those in the proof of Proposition
2.5
, we find that the local embedding problem at
p
has a solution if and only if
\varphi_{p}^{*}(\theta_{p})=0
.

Proposition 2.7
.

With the notation as above, a surjective lift
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{G}
exists if and only if the local embedding problem is solvable for all primes
p
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.5
, a surjective lift
\widetilde{\varphi}
exists if and only if
\varphi^{*}(\theta)=0
. The map
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
maps
\varphi^{*}(\theta)
to the tuple
\alpha(\varphi^{*}(\theta))=\left(\varphi_{p}^{*}(\theta_{p})\right)_{p}
. Proposition
2.6
shows that the map
\alpha
is injective, so it suffices to verify that
\varphi_{p}^{*}(\theta_{p})=0
for all primes
p
. This is equivalent to the existence of a local lift
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\widetilde{G}_{p}
at each prime
p
, which establishes the claim.
∎

We now show that the local embedding problem is solvable. We then deduce from Proposition
2.7
that a surjective lift
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{G}
of
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow G
does exist. Given a finite group
\mathcal{G}
, recall that the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(\mathcal{G})
is the intersection of all proper maximal subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
. If
\mathcal{G}
is an
\ell
-group, then
\Phi(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{G}^{p}[\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}]
, and
\mathcal{G}/\Phi(\mathcal{G})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{d}
for some
d\geq 0
. It is well known that if
\mathcal{G}
is a finite
\ell
-group, then
\mathcal{G}
is cyclic if and only if the Frattini quotient
\mathcal{G}/\Phi(\mathcal{G})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose
L/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G
and canonical surjection
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\twoheadrightarrow G
as before. Then, for every prime
p
, the local embedding problem (
2.4
) is solvable.

Proof.

If the sequence (
2.4
) splits, then
\theta_{p}=0
. Note that the local embedding problem is solvable if and only if
\varphi_{p}^{*}(\theta_{p})=0
. Thus we assume without loss of generality that (
2.4
) is non-split.

First suppose that
p
is unramified in
L
. In this case,
G_{p}
is cyclic, generated by the Frobenius element. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\operatorname{nr}}
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and note that
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\operatorname{nr}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. We seek a lift
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}_{p}
of
\varphi_{p}
. In order to show that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}
exists, it suffices to prove that
\widetilde{G}_{p}
is cyclic. Indeed,
G_{p}
is cyclic, in particular, abelian. Hence
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
contains
[\widetilde{G}_{p},\widetilde{G}_{p}]
. If
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}=[\widetilde{G}_{p},\widetilde{G}_{p}]
, then,
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
is contained in
\Phi(\widetilde{G}_{p})=[\widetilde{G}_{p},\widetilde{G}_{p}]\widetilde{G}_{p}^{\ell}
. Therefore, there is a surjection
\widetilde{G}_{p}/\left(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{p}/\Phi(\widetilde{G}_{p})
. In particular, we find that
\widetilde{G}_{p}/\Phi(\widetilde{G}_{p})
must be cyclic. It follows that
\widetilde{G}_{p}
must be cyclic as well. This contradicts the assumption that
[\widetilde{G}_{p},\widetilde{G}_{p}]=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
\widetilde{G}_{p}
is abelian. Since
G_{p}
is cyclic and the sequence (
2.4
) is non-split, this forces
\widetilde{G}_{p}
to be cyclic. Therefore,
\varphi_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow G_{p}
lifts to a map
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}_{p}
.

Next, we consider the case in which
p
is ramified. Note that
p\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{N}}
by the property
(\mathfrak{S}_{N})
and thus
p\neq\ell
and the ramification of
p
in
L
is tame. Choose a prime
v
of
L
which lies above
p
. We have that
(\mathcal{O}_{L_{v}}/v)^{\times}=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Choose a uniformizer
\pi
of
L_{v}
and consider the homomorphism
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{p}\to(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
defined
by
\sigma\mapsto\overline{\left(\frac{\sigma\pi}{\pi}\right)}
. Since
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is the residue field of
L_{v}
, it is easy to check that
\lambda
is a homomorphism. The kernel of
\lambda
is the wild inertia subgroup of
G_{p}
, which is trivial. Thus,
G_{p}
injects into
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. It follows therefore that
\varphi_{p}
factors through a map
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\operatorname{G}_{p}
, where
F
is the maximal abelian tamely ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with exponent dividing
\ell^{N}
. More explicitly,
F=F_{1}\cdot F_{2}
, where
F_{1}
is the unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of degree
\ell^{N}
and
F_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/\ell^{N}})
. Because
G_{p}
is abelian, it follows (from the same argument as in the unramified case) that
\widetilde{G}_{p}
is abelian. Note that
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{N}\mathbb{Z})\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{N}\mathbb{Z})
. As
G_{p}
is a quotient of
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, we find that
G_{p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, or
G_{p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k_{2}}\mathbb{Z}
for some
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1
. Since
\widetilde{G}_{p}
is abelian and fits into a non-split sequence (
2.4
), we find that
\widetilde{G}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k+1}\mathbb{Z}
or
\widetilde{G}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k_{1}+1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k_{2}}\mathbb{Z}
respectively. In both cases it is clear that
\varphi_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow G_{p}
lifts to a homomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\widetilde{G}_{p}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

There is a surjective lift
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\twoheadrightarrow G
of
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow G
.

Proof.

Proposition
2.8
implies that for each prime
p
, the local embedding problem is solvable, i.e., there is a lift
\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}
of
\varphi_{p}
. Thus it follows from Proposition
2.7
that a lift
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}
of
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow G
exists. Since the extension

1\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1

is non-split,
\widetilde{\varphi}
is surjective.
∎

This completes Step 1. We now move on to Step 2 which involves modifying
\widetilde{L}
so that the set of primes that ramify in
\widetilde{L}
matches the set of ramified primes in
L
.

The action of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
on
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
is trivial, allowing us to identify
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
with
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\widetilde{L}
and
\widetilde{L}^{\prime}
be extensions of
L
that solve the embedding problem, and let
\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\widetilde{G}
be the corresponding lifts of
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to G
respectively. Then for
\sigma\in\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have that

\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma)\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma)^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}=\operatorname{ker}(\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G).

The map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widetilde{\psi}\widetilde{\varphi}^{-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
is a homomorphism. In greater detail,

\begin{split}f(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})=&\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})^{-1}\\
=&\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma_{1})\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma_{2})\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma_{2})^{-1}\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma_{1})^{-1}\\
=&\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma_{1})\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma_{1})^{-1}\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma_{2})\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma_{2})^{-1}=f(\sigma_{1})f(\sigma_{2})\\
\end{split}

wherein the second last equality follows since
\widetilde{\psi}(\sigma_{2})\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma_{2})^{-1}
belongs to the center of
G
. It is clear that
f(1)=1
and
f(\sigma^{-1})=f(\sigma)^{-1}
. Conversely, suppose that
f\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
and
\widetilde{L}/L/\mathbb{Q}
solves the embedding problem, with
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}
the corresponding homomorphism. Then,
\widetilde{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widetilde{\varphi}f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}
is a well defined surjective homomorphism that lifts
\varphi
. This also gives rise to a solution
\widetilde{L}^{\prime}/L/K
of the embedding problem.

Definition 2.10
.

With respect to above notation, we refer to
\widetilde{L}^{\prime}

of

\mathbb{Q}

cut out by
C
. In greater detail,

\mathbb{Q}(C)

\Sigma_{C}

For each prime

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\perp}

In other words,

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},C^{*})\mid\langle w,v\rangle_{p}=0\text{ for all }w\in\mathcal{L}_{p}\}.

Definition 2.11
.

With respect to notation above, the Selmer and dual Selmer groups, denoted

and

\begin{split}&H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in S}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},C)}{\mathcal{L}_{p}}\right\},\\
&H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C^{*})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in S}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},C^{*})}{\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\perp}}\right\}.\\
\end{split}

A well known formula due to Wiles implies that

\begin{split}\dim H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C)-\dim H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C^{*})&=\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},C)-\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},C^{*})\\
+&\sum_{p\in S\cup\{\infty\}}\left(\dim\mathcal{L}_{p}-\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},C)\right),\end{split}

where it is understood that
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}=0
. A variation of the Poitou–Tate sequence gives the following:

\begin{split}0&\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in S}\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},C)}{\mathcal{L}_{p}}\right)\\
&\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},C^{*})^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in S}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},C),\end{split}

see for instance,
[

, p. 555, l.7]
. We set
I_{p}
to be the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and given a
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-module

According to Proposition

\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{r}
be the non-zero elements in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma\cup\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}}/\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi}))
. Let
\psi\in\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{r}\}
and
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q})
. Note that the inflation–restriction sequence gives:

0\rightarrow H^{1}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi})\right)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma\cup\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}}/\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi}))\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma\cup\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}),\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi}))^{\Omega}.

Since
\#\Omega
is coprime to
\ell
,
H^{1}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi})\right)=0
and thus the restriction map is an injection. Thus the homomorphism
\operatorname{res}(\psi)
is non-zero. It gives rise to a
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
extension
L_{\psi}
over
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
, which is a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with

(4.2)

\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\Omega\ltimes\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})).

Here, the action of
\Omega
on
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
is via the character
\overline{\chi}
.

There are two possibilities for the intersection
L_{\psi}\cap L(\mu_{\ell})
, namely either
L_{\psi}\cap L(\mu_{\ell})=L_{\psi}
or
L_{\psi}\cap L(\mu_{\ell})=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
. Recall that
\Omega
acts non-trivially on
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{Gal}(L(\mu_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\Omega\times\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
. Therefore, if
L_{\psi}\subset L(\mu_{\ell})
, then the action of
\Omega
on
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))
would be trivial, which contradicts (
4.2
). Thus we have that
L_{\psi}\cap L(\mu_{\ell})=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
. Lemma
3.8
implies that
L_{\psi}\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
. The Chebotarev conditions determining
\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}
are thus independent of the extension
L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
. There is thus a nonempty Chebotarev set contained in
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\psi}\cdot L(E[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})
determining a positive density set of primes
q\in\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}
which are non-split in the extension
L_{\psi}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
. Pick one such prime
q_{i}
for each
\psi_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r
. Then setting
S_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\}
, we have that:

V_{S_{0}}^{\perp}=\operatorname{ker}\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{0}\cup\Sigma\cup\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}}/\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi}))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{q\in S_{0}}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}(\overline{\chi}))\}.

For each
\psi_{i}
,
\operatorname{res}_{q_{i}}(\psi_{i})\neq 0
and hence
\psi_{i}\notin V_{S_{0}}^{\perp}
. This implies that
V_{S_{0}}^{\perp}=0
, thus proving the result.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

For the rest of this article, we fix a choice of
S_{0}
as in Lemma
4.4
and set
Z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S_{0}\cup\Sigma\cup\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}
.

Let
T\subset\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\setminus Z
be a finite set of primes. Let
W_{T}
be the subset of
V_{{S_{0}}\cup T}
consisting of
f
which are ramified at each of the primes in
T
. We have a natural exact sequence:

(4.3)

0\rightarrow V_{S_{0}}\xrightarrow{\iota}V_{S_{0}\cup T}\xrightarrow{\pi}\bigoplus_{q\in T}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}.

Lemma 4.6
.

The following assertions hold:

(i)

The sequence (
4.3
) above is a short exact sequence, i.e.,
\pi
is surjective;

(ii)

\#W_{T}=(\ell-1)^{\#T}\#V_{S_{0}}
.

Proof.

For the proof of part (i) it suffices to show that

\dim V_{S_{0}\cup T}=\dim V_{S_{0}}+\sum_{q\in T}\dim\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}\right).

There is a unique unramified
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{q}
, and thus,
\dim H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})=1
. Since
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
for all primes
q\in\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}
, a simple application of local class field theory shows that
\dim H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})=2
. It thus follows that

\dim\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}\right)=1.

By the choice of
S_{0}
in Lemma
4.4
one has that
V_{S_{0}}^{\perp}=0
and consequently,
V_{S_{0}\cup T}^{\perp}=0
as well. Thus by Wiles’ formula,

\dim V_{S_{0}\cup T}=\dim V_{S_{0}}+\sum_{q\in T}(\dim H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})-\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}))=\dim V_{S_{0}}+\#T

and part (i) follows.

Part (ii) can be derived from part (i) by noting that
W_{T}=\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{W})
, where

\mathcal{W}\subset\bigoplus_{q\in T}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})}

consists of elements
(h_{q})_{q\in T}
such that
h_{q}\neq 0
for all
q\in T
. We find that

\#W_{T}=\#V_{S_{0}}\times\#\mathcal{W}=\#V_{S_{0}}(\ell-1)^{\#T}.

∎

As
T
ranges over finite subsets of
\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\setminus Z

are mutually disjoint. To see this in detail, let
T
and

be distinct finite subsets of
\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\setminus Z

, whereas every element of
W_{T^{\prime}}
is unramified at
q
. It follows that
W_{T}\cap W_{T^{\prime}}=\emptyset
. Let
W
denote the disjoint union
W=\bigsqcup_{T}W_{T}
as
T
ranges over finite subsets of
\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\backslash Z
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\widetilde{L}/L/\mathbb{Q}
be defined as in the start of Section
4.2
and
Z
be the finite set of primes defined above (see Definition
4.5
). Set

g(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{T}(\ell-1)^{\#T}\left(\prod_{q\in T}q\right)^{-s}=\prod_{q\in\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\backslash Z}\left(1+(\ell-1)q^{-s}\right),

where
T
ranges over finite subsets of
\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\backslash Z
. Write
g(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}n^{-s}
where
a_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\ell-1)^{r}
if
n
is a product of
r
distinct primes in
\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}\backslash Z
, and
a_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
otherwise.

Arguing as in
[
Ser75
, Theorem 2.4, p.5]
, we find that

\log g(s)=(\ell-1)\sum_{q\in\mathfrak{T}_{E,L}}\frac{1}{q^{-s}}+\theta_{1}(s)

where
\theta_{1}
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}s\geq 1
. Moreover,

\log g(s)=(\ell-1)\alpha\,\log\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)+\theta_{2}(s),

where
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathfrak{d}(\mathfrak{T}_{E,L})
. It follows from Lemma
3.7
that

(\ell-1)\alpha=\delta=\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell-1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell^{2}-1)}.

Thus, we find that

g(s)=(s-1)^{-\delta}\,h(s),

where
h(s)
is a non-zero holomorphic function in
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. By Delange’s Tauberian theorem (cf.
[
Ten15
, Theorem 7.28]
),

(4.4)

\sum_{n\leq X}a_{n}\gg X(\log X)^{\delta-1}.

By Lemma
4.3
,
\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell}(E/\widetilde{L}^{f})=0
for all
f\in W
and thus,

(4.5)

\mathcal{M}(G,E,X)\geq\#\{f\in W\mid|\Delta_{\widetilde{L}^{f}}|\leq X\}.

According to Lemma
4.3
, we have that
|\Delta_{\widetilde{L}^{f}}|\leq c_{2}\left(\prod_{q\in S_{0}\cup T}q\right)^{\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)}
, for an absolute constant
c_{2}>0
. Thus, from (
4.5
), we deduce that

(4.6)

\mathcal{M}(G,E,X)\geq\#\left\{f\in W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\prod_{q\in T}q\leq c_{3}X^{\frac{1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)}}\right\},

where
c_{3}>0
is an explicit constant given by

c_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(c_{2}^{\frac{1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)}}\prod_{q\in S_{0}}q\right)^{-1}.

Note that

(4.7)

\#\left\{f\in W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\prod_{q\in T}q\leq c_{3}X^{\frac{1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)}}\right\}=\sum_{n\leq c_{3}X^{\frac{1}{\ell^{n-1}(\ell-1)}}}a_{n}

and thus from (
4.4
) and (

), we deduce that

\mathcal{M}(G,E,X)\gg X^{\frac{1}{(\ell-1)\ell^{n-1}}}(\log X)^{\delta-1}.

∎

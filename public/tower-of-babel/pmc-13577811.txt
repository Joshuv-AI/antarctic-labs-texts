Effects of Dietary Vitamin and Mineral Combinations on Production Performance and Immune Response in Pigeons
Authors: Jesmin Mahmood, Tayeaba Jannat, Maksuda Begum, Md Anowarul Haque Beg, Md Jisan Ahmed, Md Abdul Masum
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundPigeon farming represents an important component of the poultry sector in Bangladesh; however, productivity is frequently constrained by infectious diseases, particularly Newcastle disease (ND).ObjectivesThis study evaluated the effects of dietary supplementation with different vitamin and mineral combinations on production performance and humoral immune response to ND vaccination in adult pigeons.MethodsSixteen pairs of 3‐month‐old Gola pigeons were randomly assigned to four groups: T0 (basal diet, unvaccinated control), T1 (basal diet supplemented with vitamins A, D and E and selenium), T2 (basal diet supplemented with vitamin B‐complex) and T3 (basal diet supplemented with vitamins A, D, E and B‐complex and selenium). Birds in T1, T2 and T3 were vaccinated against ND, whereas the control group remained unvaccinated.ResultsBody weight, feed intake and egg weight were not significantly affected by treatments (p > 0.05). However, feed conversion ratio and egg production differed significantly (p < 0.05), with higher egg production observed in T1. Hemagglutination inhibition antibody titres differed significantly among groups (p < 0.05), with the highest titre observed in the T1 group (272 ± 16). Most immune organ weights were unaffected, although thymus length and width increased significantly following supplementation.ConclusionsDietary supplementation with vitamins and selenium had limited effects on growth performance in adult pigeons. However, supplementation with vitamins A, D and E and selenium was associated with improved egg production and enhanced the humoral immune response to ND vaccination. These findings suggest that targeted vitamin–mineral supplementation may be beneficial for improving reproductive performance and vaccine‐induced immunity in pigeons.

Introduction

In the growing poultry industry, pigeon farming is an important agribusiness in Bangladesh used for meat production and ornamental purposes (Rahman et al. 2021). Pigeon farming offers scope for reducing the unemployment problem in Bangladesh and contributes to increasing Gross Domestic Production (GDP) in the livestock sector (Islam et al. 2020). Agriculture contributes substantially to the national economy, while the livestock subsector remains an important contributor to agricultural production. Although the economic contribution of pigeon farming has not yet been independently estimated within national GDP statistics, Bangladesh presently has an estimated pigeon population of nearly 10 million birds representing more than 68 breeds, showing its growing importance as a low‐input production system that supports animal protein supply, household income generation and rural welfare (Asaduzzaman et al. 2009; Hoque et al. 2021). However, in recent years, pigeons have been affected by diseases such as Newcastle disease (ND), pigeon pox, salmonellosis and cancer (Paul et al. 2016). In addition, pigeons play a vital role in transmitting different types of diseases to other pigeons, domestic animals, poultry and especially to humans, thereby acting as carriers of these diseases (Islam et al. 2020). Approximately 80% of pigeon owners attribute ND to drastically affecting pigeon production (Al‐Garib et al. 2003). Vaccination against ND has been practiced for a long time as a means of protecting birds from the frequent outbreaks of the disease. In Bangladesh, vaccination against ND is commonly implemented as a preventive measure in poultry and pigeon production systems; however, vaccination coverage in pigeons remains limited. A survey conducted in Hakimpur Upazila of Dinajpur District reported that approximately 25% of pigeon farms used Baby Chick Ranikhet Disease Vaccine (BCRDV), suggesting that adoption of ND vaccination in pigeon farming remains limited (Hoque et al. 2021). Despite ND vaccinations, due to poor biosecurity, improper ventilation, extreme temperatures, stress and the use of low‐quality vaccines and medicines, several outbreaks of ND are being reported globally (Shabbir et al. 2008).

Vitamins and minerals play important roles in pigeon production by supporting physiological functions and helping birds cope with environmental and metabolic stress. According to Bourre and Galea (2006), vitamins act as co‐factors in several metabolic functions in an immune reaction and are required for optimum health and normal physiological functions such as growth, development, maintenance or reproduction, whose deficiency in the diet might cause impairment of immunity. Like vitamins, the importance of certain trace minerals in immune function has been increasingly evident. Selenium (Se), zinc, copper, iron and cobalt have been found to affect various components of the immune system (Suttle and Jones 1989). This suggests that poultry feeds should be supplemented with suitable vitamins and minerals for optimal immune response to ND vaccinations, such as Vitamins A, C and E and Se, using the recommended vitamin–mineral allowances (McDowell 2000; McDowell and Ward 2008). Limited information is available regarding the effects of different combinations of vitamin and mineral supplementation on production performance and immunity in pigeons.

Therefore, this study was conducted to determine the effects of supplementation of vitamins A, D, E and B‐complex and Se separately and in combination through drinking water on the production performance and humoral immune response in pigeons against the ND vaccine.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Approval Statement

All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (Approval No.: SAU 536) and conducted in accordance with institutional and international guidelines for animal care and use.

Experimental Location and Duration

The study was conducted at the university poultry research facility during the period of 38 weeks from May 2022 to February 2023.

Experimental Birds and Management

A total of 16 pairs of 3‐month‐old Gola pigeons were purchased from a local farm, and the birds were randomly assigned to 4 dietary treatment groups, namely T0, T1, T2 and T3, with one pair per replication and eight birds in each group. All the birds were raised under similar standard management and intensive housing conditions. Each pigeon pair was considered one experimental replicate (n = 4 replicates per treatment). Feed intake and egg production were recorded at the replicate level, and the feed conversion ratio (FCR) was calculated based on replicate‐level measurements.

Dietary Treatments

The experimental treatments were as follows: T0: Basal diet without vitamin or Se supplementation (control) T1: Basal diet + vitamins A, D and E and Se (0.5 mL/L drinking water) T2: Basal diet + vitamin B‐complex (0.5 mL/L drinking water) T3: Basal diet + vitamins A, D, E and B‐complex and Se (0.5 mL/L drinking water)

Supplements were administered via drinking water for five consecutive days twice monthly throughout the experimental period, including the periods before and after vaccination.

Vitamins and Selenium

The supplemented vitamins and minerals were collected from a veterinary medicine shop. For vitamins A, D and E, Renasol AD3E vet (Renata Ltd.); for the vitamin B‐complex, B‐Com‐Vit Liquid (Square Pharmaceuticals) and for the Se, E‐Sel Solution (Square Pharmaceuticals) was used. These supplementations were given orally in drinking water for five consecutive days twice a month before and after vaccination.

Vaccination Program

In this experiment, birds in T1, T2 and T3 were vaccinated against ND using live Ranikhet disease vaccine (RDV), which was acquired from the Livestock Research Institute (LRI). Primary vaccination was administered intramuscularly at 12 weeks of age (0.5 mL per bird), followed by a booster dose at 38 weeks. The control group (T0) remained unvaccinated.

Production Performance Measurements

Feed Consumption

The daily feed intake in all the groups was monitored. A measured amount of feed per pair was distributed in their feed troughs and the birds allowed feed for 24 h. The difference between the quantity given and the remainder was calculated as the weekly feed intake. The average feed intake per bird per day was calculated for each group.

Body Weight and Body Weight Gain

The initial live weight at 12 weeks of age and the final live weight at 38 weeks of age were recorded per bird. The change in body weight per bird was determined by subtracting the initial weight from the final weight.

Feed Conversion Ratio

A weighed quantity of feed was delivered to each experimental group and the left‐over feed was weighed to determine weekly feed consumption (FC) by each group. At the end of the experiment (38 weeks), FCR of each bird was calculated by the following formula (Singh and Panda 1992).

FCR = total feed intake (g) / total egg mass (g) (Singh and Panda 1992)

Because adult pigeons were evaluated during the reproductive phase, FCR was calculated based on egg mass rather than body weight gain.

Reproductive Performance Measurement

Egg Production and Egg Weight

Regular records were maintained for egg production and the total weight of eggs produced. By 6 months of age, the birds had reached peak production. Almost 7 days after mating, females laid 1–3 (usually 2) white eggs, which hatched after 17–18 days.

Sample Collection and Immune Organ Measurements

Ratio of Immune Organs

The lymphoid organs ratio was defined as the ratio of the weight of the organs to its body weight. It was calculated using the following equation:

Organ ratio = organ weight (g) / live body weight (g) (Lucio and Hitchner 1979)

Blood and Serum Collection

Blood samples were collected from the birds after booster vaccination. Four pigeons were randomly chosen from each group, and blood was collected from the wing vein after their slaughter. Blood was collected into sterile tubes without anticoagulant. The tubes were placed at an inclined position for 8–12 h to allow serum separation. The separated serum was transferred into sterile microcentrifuge tubes and stored at −20°C until analysis. The serum was collected in a test tube using a micropipette. Subsequently, it was centrifuged at 1330 rpm for 2 min and 20 s to separate the clear serum. This clear serum was then transferred to small vials, appropriately labelled, and stored at −20°C until needed.

Serological Test

The sera were used to assess the pigeon's immune response to vaccination using the hemagglutination inhibition (HI) test, following the procedure outlined by the OIE (2000). The test involved employing a constant 4HA unit antigen and a serum dilution method (β‐procedure) as described in National Research Council, Subcommittee on Avian Diseases (1971). Initially, the hemagglutination titre of the HA antigen was determined through the HA test. Then, the antigen was diluted in PBS to achieve 4HA units per 50 µL of suspension. A series of twofold serum dilutions were prepared for conducting the HI test. Antibody titres against NDV in the sera were determined via the HI assay at the ACI Animal Health Diagnostic Laboratory in Dhaka.

Collection of Samples

Samples were collected after the slaughtering of birds. The pigeons were weighed and slaughtered at 38 weeks of age. The thymus, spleen, caecal tonsil and ileum were promptly extracted, with the utmost care given to the removal of adhering connective tissues, and subsequently weighed individually. The relative weights of these organs were determined by computing the ratio of their respective weights to that of the live body. The data about body weight (BW), FC, feed conversion, as well as the weights of lymphoid organs were aggregated for statistical analysis.

Statistical Analysis

The data were recorded and analysed statistically by using statistical software ‘SPSS’ (version 26.0). ANOVA Duncan's Multiple Range Test (DMRT) was performed with p–value and significance were determined when p < 0.05. The Kruskal–Walli's test was used to compare the immune organs ratio. Pearson's correlation coefficient (r) was used to examine the relationships between production performance and immune response parameters. Statistical significance was accepted at p < 0.05.

Discussion

Vaccine failure and disease prevalence directly affect immunosuppression, which causes the disease and recurring economic problems in pigeon farming. Various types of factors affect the quality of feed, cumulatively making the pigeons vulnerable to diseases, but nutrition plays a significant role in the development and function of the immune system, which helps the birds to prevent the occurrence of diseases. Therefore, this study was conducted to evaluate the effects of vitamins A, D, E and B‐complex and Se on the production performance and immune response of ND vaccinated adult pigeons.

We examined the final body weight, body weight gain, FC and FCR to observe the effect of the supplementation and vaccination on the adult pigeons. No significant differences were observed in the final body weight, body weight gain or FC, except for the FCR of pigeons among different treatment groups. Although the highest FC was observed in group T2, the body weight gain did not increase satisfactorily. Moreover, there was a negative correlation between feed intake and body weight gain. Therefore, these findings suggest that vitamin–mineral supplementation did not markedly improve body weight gain in adult pigeons under the conditions of this study. The results of this study are also in correlation with the earlier findings of Yalçınkaya et al. (2010), Adebiyi (2011) and Li et al. (2018). This may be associated with poorer feed utilization efficiency, in which excess feed intake is not effectively converted into body mass but instead is diverted toward maintenance requirements, metabolic heat production, or less efficient nutrient utilization. In contrast, birds with lower FI may have utilized nutrients more efficiently, resulting in comparatively higher BWG and improved feed conversion efficiency. Similar inverse relationships between FI and growth efficiency have been reported in poultry studies evaluating feed utilization and residual feed intake (Chambers and Lin 1988).

For egg production parameters, we examined egg production and egg weight to assess the effects of vitamins and Se in adult pigeons. Significantly (p < 0.05) higher levels of egg production were found in the T1 group. The treatment T1 (Vitamins A, D and E and Se) showed the overall best performance on egg production compared to other vitamin‐mineral supplementation treatments, whereas the treatment T2 (Vitamin B‐complex) showed the least performance. On the other hand, we did not find a statistically significant difference in egg weight parameters. In correlation analysis, we found a positive correlation with body weight gain, but did not find a positive correlation between these parameters. So, we conclude that there was an impact of vitamins and Se on the egg production, but not on the egg weight of adult pigeons. These findings are consistent with the reports (Giri et al. 2012; Khashaba et al. 2009) that egg number, egg weight, fertility and hatchability significantly (p < 0.05) increased as dietary vitamins and minerals premix levels increased in the diet. In the case of layer, FCR is calculated by the egg production, not by the body weight gain. In the study, we observed egg production of the adult pigeons that is similar to layer.

The observed data for FCR showed that birds of the T2 (vitamin B‐complex) exhibited the highest FCR values. Although the egg production and egg weight showed a negative correlation with FCR, the feed intake was positively correlated in the correlation analysis. These findings suggest that there was a significant effect of dietary supplements on the FCR of adult pigeons. However, interpretation of FCR results should be made cautiously because feed intake and egg production were measured at the replicate (pair) level with a limited number of replicates per treatment, which may influence the precision of feed efficiency estimates.

The immune system is composed of the primary and secondary lymphoid organs. The bursa of Fabricius, spleen and thymus are the main lymphoid organs in birds, and their weights are directly related to the immune system and protection. The thymus is a primary lymphoid organ, and it atrophies with age. The spleen is considered the secondary lymphoid organ, and the caecal tonsil consists of mucosa‐associated lymphoid tissue. These organs were evaluated to investigate the effects of dietary supplementation on immune development. The average weight, length and width of the thymus, spleen and caecal tonsil were measured. All the parameters showed significant differences (p < 0.05) except spleen weight, where a numerically improved average weight was found in the T1 (Vitamins A, D and E and Se) treated group. In this study, the results showed that the control group performed better across almost all characteristics, except thymus length and width. Moreover, we found a strong correlation between these two parameters. The findings suggest that vitamin–mineral supplementation was associated with increased thymus length and width. The previous findings of Shaik et al. (2005), Bhatti et al. (2016) and Rostami et al. (2018) showed that supplementation with vitamins A, D, E and B complex and Se did not have a beneficial effect on immune organs.

The HI antibody titres were higher in the treatment groups than in the control group. Different treatment groups showed a significant (p < 0.05) effect on the serum antibody titres against the ND vaccine. Significantly (p < 0.05) higher individual titre was found in vitamins A, D and E and Se supplemented T1 group than in T2 (vitamin B‐complex), T3 (vitamins A, D, E and B‐complex and Se) and T0 (control) groups. These findings suggest that supplementation with vitamins and Se may enhance the humoral immune response to ND vaccination in pigeons. In the correlation analysis, we also found positive correlations with thymus length, width, and spleen weight. The high ND vaccine immunity in birds on vitamin‐supplemented diets is consistent with the findings of Sanda and Oyewole (2015) and Sanda et al. (2015), who observed a positive correlation between antibody titre and vitamin‐mineral supplementation.

Based on the examination of the research results provided above, there were no significant effects of vitamins and Se on growth performance. Vitamins and Se supplementation of adult pigeon diets did not improve thymus weight, spleen length and width, and caecal length, width, and weight; however, supplementation of vitamins A, D and E and Se combination gave a better humoral immune response against NDV, egg production, thymus length and width. Overall, although dietary supplementation did not improve body weight, the present findings suggest that supplementation with vitamins A, D and E and Se may improve egg production and enhance the humoral immune response to ND vaccination in adult pigeons. These findings should be confirmed in larger studies with increased experimental replication.

Some limitations should be considered while interpreting the present findings. The experiment was conducted using a limited number of pigeon pairs per treatment, which may have affected the sensitivity for detecting treatment differences. In addition, feed intake and FCR were estimated based on paired records rather than individual bird measurements. Immune evaluation in the present study mainly focused on antibody response to ND vaccination and selected lymphoid organ characteristics; therefore, other aspects of immunity were not evaluated. Further studies with larger experimental populations and additional immune parameters may provide a broader understanding of the effects of vitamin–mineral supplementation in pigeons.

Conclusions

Dietary vitamin and mineral supplementation had no significant effect on growth performance parameters in adult pigeons. However, supplementation significantly affected FCR and reproductive performance, with the combination of vitamins A, D and E and Se producing superior egg production compared with other treatments. This supplementation strategy significantly enhanced the humoral immune response to ND vaccination, as demonstrated by higher hemagglutination inhibition antibody titres. Correlation analysis revealed that antibody titres were positively associated with thymus length and width and showed significant relationships with several lymphoid organ characteristics, indicating improved immune responsiveness rather than changes in organ mass. Although most immune organ weights and ratios were not significantly altered, the observed enhancement in thymus dimensions and antibody titres suggests a functional improvement in immune competence. Overall, targeted supplementation with vitamins A, D and E and Se appears to be a promising nutritional strategy for improving reproductive performance and vaccine‐induced immunity in adult pigeons without adversely affecting growth performance.

Effects of Ban Lian Zi Jin compound on intestinal barrier and apoptosis of Heat-stressed broilers
Authors: Zongtao Zuo, Yongxin Liao, Yulin He, Yuqing Li, Jiahui Mei, Xiaoxiao Du, Yun Gao, Kailun Xi, Dayou Shi, Cui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Heat stress can damage the integrity of the intestinal barrier in broiler chickens. The aim of this study is to evaluate the protective and reparative effects and related mechanisms of Ban Lian Zi Jin (BLZJ) compound on the intestinal barrier function of heat stressed broiler chickens. Acute heat stress was induced in 216 broilers, which were randomly allocated to six treatment groups. Broilers in the treatment groups received BLZJ daily via dietary supplementation or drinking water, whereas the HS group was fed a basal diet with normal water only. LC-MS results indicate that components such as scopolamine and magnolol in BLZJ may alleviate heat stress-induced intestinal barrier damage through anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and regulatory activities.Moreover, Supplementation with BLZJ significantly increasing the jejunum gene expression of TJPs (Occludin, Claudin-1, Claudin-5), MUCs (MUC2, E-cadherin) and the anti-apoptotic protein(BCL2)(P<0.05), significantly decreased the jejunum gene expression of pro-apoptotic factors (BAX, CYTC, caspase-3, caspase-9)(P < 0.05), enhanced the abundance of beneficial gut microbiota, and improved microbial community stability. In conclusion, BLZJ can repair intestinal barrier damage in heat stressed broiler chickens by enhancing tight junctions and mucin expression, regulating intestinal microbiota homeostasis, and inhibiting excessive intestinal mucosal apoptosis.

Introduction

Heat stress is one of the primary environmental stressors affecting the development of the poultry industry. Compared to other animals, poultry are characterised by a high basal body temperature, a high metabolic rate and the absence of sweat glands in their skin, making them more sensitive to temperature-related environmental threats such as heat stress (DEEB et al., 2002). When ambient temperatures exceed 30°C, it is highly likely to trigger heat stress responses in flocks, severely impairing their normal productive capacity. This results in slow weight gain, reduced feed conversion efficiency and lower egg production rates, causing significant economic losses to modern large-scale poultry farming (HOUSHMAND et al., 2012).

Modern research indicates that traditional Chinese medicine is rich in various bioactive compounds, vitamins, minerals and trace elements. It can mitigate the adverse effects of heat stress through multiple mechanisms, including regulating the body’s thermal balance, modulating hormone secretion, and enhancing feed intake and immune function (Wu et al., 2018). Previous studies have demonstrated that the Ban Lian Zi Jin (BLZJ) compound possesses anti-inflammatory, antipyretic and immunomodulatory properties, and can alleviate heat stress-induced damage. It mitigates the secretion of inflammatory cytokines, recovers the expression of intestinal tight junction proteins (TJPs), and safeguards the intestinal barrier function in the jejunum of heat-stressed broilers (Yang et al., 2024. Zuo et al., 2026). However, its role and mechanism in protecting the intestinal barrier are still unclear Heat stress disrupts the function of these three types of intestinal barriers, leading to increased intestinal permeability and the translocation of bacterial toxins, thereby exacerbating tissue damage (Liu et al., 2021). Studies have shown that the stability of intestinal barrier function is closely related to the apoptosis-autophagy pathway. Heat stress can disrupt the balance of this pathway, exacerbating intestinal barrier damage by promoting excessive apoptosis of intestinal mucosal cells and inhibiting autophagy-mediated repair (Niu et al., 2025). Therefore, it is crucial to study the heat stress mechanism from the stability of intestinal barrier function and the apoptotic autophagy pathway.

Based on previous research, this study aims to provide a theoretical basis for alleviating heat stress-induced intestinal damage in poultry. The active ingredients in BLZJ compound were identified using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) technology, elucidating their role in alleviating heat stress. By establishing a high-temperature heat stress model in broiler chickens, the repairing effect of BLZJ compound on intestinal barrier dysfunction caused by heat stress was explored. This provides a scientific theoretical basis and practical reference for clinical prevention and control of heat stress in broiler chickens and improvement of their intestinal health.

Materials and methods

LC-MS technology analysis

Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography-high-resolution mass spectrometry (UPLC—HRMS). After extraction with methanol-acetonitrile (1:1, v/v), the samples were separated using BEH amide (for polar metabolites) and C18 (for non-polar metabolites) columns, and mass spectrometry data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes using an Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer. After converting the raw data into ProteoWizard format, metabolite identification was performed using a custom-built database, and data quality control and statistical analysis were carried out using R software packages.

Preparation of BLZJ compound

The powder of BLZJ compound is dried and crushed in a ratio of 4:4:3:3:3:2:2, and then mixed through an 80 mesh sieve for use in feed (Yang et al., 2024). Boiled the powder of BLZJ compound for 1 h, decoct twice, and concentrate the resulting liquid using a rotary evaporator to obtain a BLZJ compound solution concentration of 0.9g/ml, which can be added to drinking water.

Experimental design

The experimental animal is yellow feathered broiler chicken, with a feeding period of 28 days, provided by Lianxing Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Company. 216 1-day-old broilers were adaptively raised for 14 days and randomly divided into 6 groups: blank group (CON), heat stress group (HS), low-dose BLZJ powder mixed feed group (BL), high-dose BLZJ powder mixed feed group (BH), low-dose BLZJ solution by drinking water group (SL), and high-dose BLZJ solution by drinking water group (SH) (3 replicates per group, 12 chickens per replicate). During the adaptive feeding stage, all experimental groups were fed a basic diet with an ambient temperature of 24±2 °C and a relative humidity of 50±5%. The basic diet is formulated according to the Chinese Chicken Feeding Standards (NY/T33-2004). The experimental grouping and treatment are shown in Table 1. Western blot analysis

Approximately 50 mg of frozen jejunal tissue was homogenized in 1 mL RIPA lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors. The homogenate was centrifuged at 12,000 g for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatant was collected. Protein concentration was quantified using a BCA protein assay kit.Protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto a PVDF membrane. The membrane was washed four times with TBST (10 min each) and blocked with 5% skimmed milk for 1 h. Primary antibodies against Bax, Cytochrome C (CYTC), Claudin-1 and GAPDH were applied for overnight incubation at 4°C.The membrane was washed with TBST for 10 min and incubated with corresponding secondary antibodies for 2 h. Chemiluminescent substrate was prepared according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Signals were detected with a chemiluminescence imaging system. Images were acquired and analyzed.

16S rRNA gene sequencing and bioinformatics analysis

Fresh broiler feces (150 mg) were collected and analyzed according to a previously published study. The V3-V4 regions of 16S rRNA were amplified via a specific primer (338F 5′-ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCA-3′. 806R 5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′). The amplification product contents were measured via agarose gel electrophoresis during the experiment study. The amplified product was purified using the E.Z.N.A. Kit (Omega, USA). Sequencing libraries were obtained based on the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit. The quality of the library was detected on the Qubit@ 2.0 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Finally, the Illumina Nova6000 platform was used for sequencing, and a paired reading code of 250 bp was generated (Guangdong Magigene Biotechnology Co., Ltd. China). The quality of the original data was controlled by Fastp 0.14.1 to determine the matching end cleaning label that meets the requirements. The R software was applied to identify the species, composition of community, and richness of species. Alpha diversity was applied in analyzing the complexity of species diversity. Beta diversity analysis was conducted to assess differences of samples.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS 22.0 software. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to assess inter-group differences, with Duncan’s multiple range test applied for post-hoc comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. Graphs were plotted using Origin 2021 software.

Unconstrained ranking methods, such as Principal Coordinate Analysis (PCoA) and Non-metric Multidimensional Scaling (NMDS), were employed to perform dimensionality reduction on the microbiome data, thereby providing a visual representation of the overall trends in sample community structure. PCoA1 accounts for 41% of the variance, whilst PCoA2 accounts for 25%. Compared with the CON group, the distribution of sample points in the HS group is more concentrated and the confidence ellipses are smaller, indicating that heat stress can alter the gut microbiota structure of broiler chickens and increase intra-group microbial similarity. Compared with the HS group, samples from the BH group showed distinct spatial separation in the ordination plot (Fig. 6.B).

The diversity of the microbial community was comprehensively assessed using the Chao-1 and Simpson indices from the alpha diversity indices. The results of the richness-related indices indicated that, compared with the CON group, the Chao-1 index was lower in the HS group, suggesting that the richness of the broiler chicken gut microbiota decreased following heat stress. In contrast, the Chao-1 indices in the BL, BH, SL and SH groups were all higher than those in the HS group, approaching normal levels. Results for the composite diversity indices showed that, compared with the CON group, the Simpson’s index was elevated in the HS group, whilst the Simpson’s indices in the BL and SL groups were lower than those in the HS group, approaching normal levels (Fig. 6.C).

At the class, phylum and order levels, there were significant differences in the composition of the gut microbiota of broiler chickens across the different treatment groups. At the class level, the Bacilli class was the dominant group. Its relative abundance was similar and low in the CON, BL and SL groups, whereas it was significantly elevated in the HS, BH and SH groups. The Actinobacteria and Clostridia classes were more abundant in the CON, BL and SL groups, whilst the remaining groups accounted for a lower proportion and had a minor impact on the overall community structure (Fig. 6.D). At the phylum level, the Firmicutes and Actinobacteriota were the dominant phyla. The Firmicutes were less abundant in the CON, BL and SL groups and most abundant in the BH and SH groups, whilst the Actinobacteriota exhibited the opposite trend. the remaining phyla accounted for less than 0.05% and had a negligible impact (Fig. 6.E). At the order level, the Lactobacillales was the most dominant group, with its abundance significantly increased in the CON, BL and SL groups, and relatively lower in the HS, BH and SH groups. The Corynebacteriales exhibited a similar trend in abundance, whilst the Micrococcales, Lachnospirales, Staphylococcales and Bacillales were also distributed to varying degrees across the groups (Fig. 6.F).

Discussion

Heat stress generally refers to a situation in which an organism is exposed to a hot environment, resulting in a series of non-specific systemic responses, including perceptible discomfort and physiological fatigue (Mishra et al., 2003). With global warming, high temperatures have gradually become a major factor affecting poultry growth performance among various environmental factors (Dai et al., 2010). Heat stress not only affects the growth, development, production performance and slaughter performance of poultry, but also impacts the immune function of the organism, causing immunosuppression and inducing various diseases. This experiment measured the rectal temperature and weight gain of broiler chickens at different time points. The results indicated that from the 7th day of heat stress until the end of the stress period, the rectal temperature of the broiler chickens rose significantly and remained consistently above 42°C. Whilst weight gain decreased significantly, falling below normal levels. This indicates that during this period, the broilers were affected by high temperatures and were in a state of heat stress, with their thermoregulatory mechanisms disrupted and unable to maintain normal body temperature (Saeed et al., 2019). Furthermore, the data indicate that different formulations of the BLZJ compound significantly inhibited the rise in rectal temperature and improved weight gain in broilers under heat stress, suggesting that appropriate doses of the traditional Chinese medicine compound have a positive effect on the neuroendocrine system and exert a regulatory effect on certain hormones related to temperature regulation (Onagbesan et al., 2023).This study compared the efficacy of BLZJ compound administered via solution by drinking water and Basic diet mixed. Only subtle changes were detected in a small number of physiological indices across the two delivery routes, with no significant disparity in overall therapeutic effects. It is concluded that both administration routes are viable for alleviating heat stress in broilers.

Using LC-MS analysis has revealed that the BLZJ compound contains a variety of bioactive compounds, such as scopolin, 1-caffeoylquinic acid, magnoloside, among others. These bioactive compounds act synergistically and complementarily, jointly participating in the regulation of physiological functions, thereby providing the material basis for the intestinal protective and anti-stress effects of the BLZJ. Studies have shown that scopolin can influence the strong binding of HSP90 to glucose-6-phosphate isomerase (GPI), glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase, and mitochondria (GPD2), thereby inhibiting tumour growth in a dos e-dependent manner, disrupting the morphology and structure of tumour cells, increasing Bax expression in tumour tissue, suppressing Bcl-2 expression, and promoting tumour necrosis (Wang et al., 2022). Researchers administered Magnoloside to rats with functional dyspepsia (FD) and found that oral administration of Magnoloside improved functional dyspepsia by regulating the brain-gut axis and the gut microbiota (Xue et al., 2019). In light of the findings of this study, the BLZJ formula can upregulate the expression of TJPs and MUCs in the intestines of heat-stressed broiler chickens. It is speculated that the synergistic action of its active components, including scopolin, magnolol and 1-caffeoylquinic acid, through anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and gut function-regulating mechanisms, to repair heat stress-induced intestinal barrier damage in broiler chickens and alleviate the adverse effects of heat stress.

The intestinal mucosa consists of a single layer of columnar epithelial cells, divided into villi and crypts, with tight junctions playing a central role in maintaining barrier integrity. This structural integrity is crucial for the healthy growth of broiler chickens (Wang et al., 2022).Heat stress can synergistically compromise intestinal barrier integrity through multiple pathways. In addition to activating oxidative stress pathways and mediating inflammatory signalling, it significantly disrupts the homeostasis of the gut microbiota, leading to a decline in the abundance of beneficial bacteria within the intestine, and the overproliferation of harmful bacteria (Bortoluzzi et al., 2016). This microbial imbalance further exacerbates damage to the intestinal mucosa. simultaneously, heat stress inhibits the synthesis and secretion of intestinal mcuin proteins (MCUs) . As MCUs constitute the first physical barrier of the intestinal mucosa, a reduction in their secretion directly diminishes the mucosa’s defensive capacity, rendering it more susceptible to external stimuli and harmful bacterial invasion, thereby further compromising intestinal barrier integrity (Gieryńska et al., 2022). Furthermore, heat stress downregulates the expression of TJPs, leading to the atrophy and shedding of intestinal villi and disruption of crypt morphology, which in turn causes an abnormal increase in mucosal permeability and simultaneously inducing endotoxin entry into the bloodstream and low-grade systemic inflammation, reducing the gut’s ability to digest and absorb nutrients, and ultimately leading to growth retardation in broiler chickens, an increased feed-to-weight ratio, and impaired immune function, thereby significantly increasing susceptibility to intestinal diseases (Zeisel et al., 2019). Among these, Occludin and Claudin are key functional proteins that regulate the structure of intestinal tight junctions and maintain the stability of barrier permeability. Heat stress significantly suppresses the expression levels of both, disrupting the structural arrangement of tight junction complexes, inducing intestinal barrier leakage and promoting endotoxin entry into the bloodstream, which further activates intestinal inflammatory cascades and exacerbates mucosal tissue damage (Wang et al., 2020). This represents the core molecular mechanism by which heat stress impairs the physiological function of the broiler chicken intestine. The results of this study indicate that supplementing broiler chickens with the BLZJ significantly upregulates the mRNA transcription and protein expression of Occludin, Claudin-1 and Claudin-5 in the intestines of heat-stressed broilers, effectively repairing the tight junction structure. simultaneously regulating the balance of the gut microbiota, increasing the abundance of beneficial bacteria and inhibiting the proliferation of harmful bacteria, promoting the synthesis and secretion of intestinal MCUs (MUC2, E-cadherin), and enhancing the defensive capacity of the intestinal mucosa. Consequently, this reduces abnormal intestinal mucosal permeability, minimises endotoxin translocation and excessive inflammatory activation, effectively preserves the morphological integrity of the intestinal mucosa, and improves the intestinal barrier function and health status of broiler chickens under heat stress conditions.

The apoptosis-autophagy pathway is a central mechanism regulating the survival and death of intestinal epithelial cells. its homeostatic balance is crucial for maintaining the structural integrity of the intestinal barrier and ensuring normal intestinal physiological function (Tang et al., 2024. Liang et al., 2020). Heat stress can disrupt the balance of the apoptosis-autophagy pathway, inducing abnormal apoptosis in intestinal mucosal cells, thereby compromising the integrity of the intestinal barrier (Kim et al., 2013). This leads to massive apoptosis of intestinal mucosal cells, damage to villous structures, and abnormal crypt proliferation, subsequently resulting in increased mucosal permeability and intestinal dysfunction. simultaneously, abnormally apoptotic mucosal cells release pro-inflammatory factors, further activating the intestinal inflammatory response and exacerbating damage to the intestinal barrier. this is also one of the key molecular mechanisms by which acute heat stress induces intestinal functional damage in broiler chickens. BAX, CYTC, caspase-3 and caspase-9 are key molecules regulating apoptosis, whilst BCL2 is the primary anti-apoptotic molecule. the two work in concert to regulate the apoptosis process and maintain cellular homeostasis (Chen et al., 2024.Nordestgaard et al., 2021. Sandesara et al., 2019. Balling et al., 2023). The results of this study indicate that in heat-stressed broiler chickens, the mRNA expression of BAX, CYTC, caspase-3 and caspase-9 increased significantly, whilst BCL2 expression decreased significantly. This suggests that heat stress can significantly induce apoptosis in the intestinal mucosal cells of broiler chickens, disrupting the homeostasis of the apoptotic pathway and consequently compromising the integrity of the intestinal barrier. Conversely, supplementation with the BLZJ compound can inhibit heat stress-induced abnormal apoptosis of intestinal mucosal cells, restore the homeostasis of the apoptotic pathway, thereby protecting the integrity of intestinal mucosal cells, repairing the intestinal barrier function damaged by heat stress, and improving the intestinal health of broiler chickens under acute heat stress conditions.

Conclusion

BLZJ compound can upregulate the expression of intestinal TJPs and MUCs, restore the homeostasis of gut microbiota, and regulate the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway to suppress excessive apoptosis of intestinal mucosal cells, thereby ameliorating intestinal barrier dysfunction in broiler chickens subjected to heat stress.

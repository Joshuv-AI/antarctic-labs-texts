A meta-modal logic for bisimulations
Alfredo Burrieza

Abstract
We propose a modal study of the notion of bisimulation. Our contribution is twofold. First, we extend the basic modal language with a new modality [ b ] {[\,b\,]} , whose intended meaning is universal quantification over all states that are bisimilar to the current one. We show that bisimulations are definable in this object language. Second, we provide a sound and complete axiomatisation of the class of all pairs of Kripke models linked by bisimulations.

Introduction

Modal logic can be understood in a narrow, classical sense as the logical study of alethic modalities: necessity, possibility, contingency and impossibility. In a broader sense, (propositional) modal logic is the extension of propositional logic with modal operators, which are operators that take other formulas as arguments. This also encompasses the study of how these extended languages are useful for describing certain mathematical structures
[
3
]
(see also
[
5
,
10
,
6
,
17
,
22
]
).

In the latter, broader sense, there is a long-standing tradition in which modal operators are interpreted over a chosen mathematical concept.
1

1

This can be contrasted with one promoting an intuitive or philosophical reading of modal operators, such as knowledge/belief
[
9
]
, time
[
14
]
, or moral obligation
[
21
]
.
Then, such a concept is studied in a modal disguise, and its axiomatisation provides researchers with an informative, fresh look at the idea. Moreover, modalization of mathematical theories enables the importation of modal logic tools beyond proof methods, such as model-theoretical and computational machinery. This tradition can be dated back at least to the work of Tarski and McKinsey on the topological interpretation of the basic modal language
[
12
]
, which demonstrated that a rather intricate mathematical structure (topologies) admits a relatively simple and well-behaved normal modal logic (S4).

A further step in this research direction is to provide a modal approach to notions that belong to the meta-mathematical theory of modal logic. This naturally results in what might be called
meta-modal logic
: the construction of object modal languages to study concepts stemming from classical modal meta-theory. The first milestones in this enterprise have focused, to the best of our knowledge, on the modal approach of filtrations
[
1
,
11
,
19
]
, a well-known method for obtaining the finite model property of modal logics and therefore a direct path to decidability. Here, we propose to focus on another modal logic concept: bisimulations.

Bisimulations are a fundamental formal tool in the model theory of standard modal logic.
2

See
[

and
[
15

for a systematic presentation as well as historical notes. Johan van Benthem was the pioneer of the notion, under the name of p-relations
[

.
Roughly speaking, bisimulations provided a clear answer to a foundational model-theoretical question: Given two (Kripke-style) models, what conditions are sufficient and necessary for them to satisfy the same modal formulas? Hennessy and Miller
[
8
]
showed that bisimulations are the exact answer whenever the models are image-finitary, where the number of accessible worlds for any given world is always finite. Among many other applications, bisimulations are used to yield definability and undefinability results, as well as tight correspondence results between first-order and modal logic. Additionally, they provide techniques for reducing the size of models, which in turn have a direct practical payoff.

Our seminal contribution to the modal study of bisimulations is twofold. First, we show that the main definitional conditions of bisimulations between two arbitrary Kripke models, usually called “atomic harmony”,“forth” (or “zig”) and “back” (or “zag”), can be modally defined with the sole aid of a normal modality (which we will denote by
{[\,b\,]}
). Intuitively,
{[\,b\,]}A
means that
A
holds at every state that is linked to the current state by a given bisimulation. Second, we provide a sound and complete axiomatisation for the class of all pairs of Kripke models that are linked by a bisimulation (“bi-models”, in our terminology). Completeness is proved via a canonical model technique, but it requires some creativity and deviation from the fully standard constructions.

Our methodology is also double-sided. On the one hand, we propose a novel application of standard arguments and methods in the literature on modal definability and completeness. On the other hand, our soundness and completeness proofs have been fully encoded and verified in Isabelle/HOL
[
13
]
, enhancing the reproducibility and applicability of our approach. Indeed, Isabelle identified some flaws and unjustified steps in our original handwritten proofs, which compelled us to reconsider several key points of the completeness argument.

Background

We assume a denumerable set of atoms
\mathsf{At}
as fixed from now on.

Definition 1

(Basic modal language)
.

The language
{\cal L}_{\square}
is the one generated by the following grammar:

A::=p\mid\perp\mid(A\to A)\mid\square A

where
p
ranges over
\mathsf{At}
.

We can introduce new defined operators. The rest of the Boolean connectives
\top,\lnot,\land,\lor,\leftrightarrow
are defined as usual.
The dual of
\square
is
\lozenge
, where
\lozenge A
is shorthand for
\neg\square\neg A
.

Definition 2

(Basic frames, models and truth)
.

A Kripke frame for
{\cal L}_{\square}
is an ordered pair of the form
\mathcal{F}=(W,R)
, where
W\neq\varnothing
and
R\subseteq W\times W
.
We define
{\textsf{Fclass}}=\{\cal F\mid\cal F\hbox{ is a Kripke frame}\}
. A frame is called image-finitary iff for every
w\in W
,
R[w]=\{v\in W\mid wRv\}
is finite.

A Kripke model
{\cal M}
is a tuple of the form
(W,R,V)
, where
(W,R)
is a Kripke frame and
V\colon\mathsf{At}\longrightarrow 2^{W}
is a valuation function that assigns a set of worlds to each atom; intuitively, the set of worlds where the atom is true. The model
(W,R,V)
is said to be based on the frame
(W,R)
. If
w\in W
(that is,
w
is in
{\cal M}
), then
({\cal M},w)
is called a pointed model. We tend to omit brackets when denoting pointed models.

We also define the relation
\models
, so that
{\cal M},w\models A
means ‘the formula
A
is satisfied at (or true at)
w
in the model
{\cal M}
’. Inductively:

{\cal M},w\models p

iff

w\in V(p)
(for any
p\in\mathsf{At}
)

{\cal M},w\not\models\perp

{\cal M},w\models A\to B

iff

either
{\cal M},w\not\models A
or
{\cal M},w\models B

{\cal M},w\models\square A

iff

for all
w^{\prime}\in W
s.t.
wRw^{\prime}
,
{\cal M},w^{\prime}\models A

The defined connective
\lozenge
can be shown to have the following meaning:

{\cal M},w\models\lozenge A

iff

there exists
w^{\prime}\in W
s.t.
wRw^{\prime}
and
{\cal M},w^{\prime}\models A

Definition 3

(Bisimulation for
\mathcal{L}_{\square}
)
.

Let
{\cal M}=(W,R,V)
and
{\cal M^{\prime}}=(W^{\prime},R^{\prime},V^{\prime})
be a pair of Kripke models. We say that a non-empty binary relation
Z\subseteq W\times W^{\prime}
is a bisimulation between
{\cal M}
and
{\cal M^{\prime}}
(in symbols:
Z\cal\colon M\,\underline{\leftrightarrow}\,M^{\prime}
) if the following conditions hold:

(1)

If
wZw^{\prime}
, then
w
and
w^{\prime}
satisfies the same atoms (atomic harmony).

(2)

If
wZw^{\prime}
and
wRv
, then there exists
v^{\prime}
in
{\cal M^{\prime}}
such that
vZv^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}R^{\prime}v^{\prime}

(forth)
.

(3)

If
wZw^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}R^{\prime}v^{\prime}
, then there exists
v
in
{\cal M}
such that
vZv^{\prime}
and
wRv

(back)
.

If
w
(in
{\cal M}
) and
w^{\prime}
(in
{\cal M^{\prime}}
) are a pair of states linked by a bisimulation
Z
, then we say that
w
and
w^{\prime}
are bisimilar (written
Z\colon{\cal M},w\,\underline{\leftrightarrow}\,{\cal M^{\prime}},w^{\prime}
or if the context is clear, simply,
w\,\underline{\leftrightarrow}\,w^{\prime}
).

The following well-known theorem from the literature on modal logic highlights the fundamental importance of bisimulations as a tool for logical inquiry. In words, modal information (true formulas) amounts to bisimilarity, given that the models being considered are image infinitary.

Theorem 1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Given two Kripke pointed models
({\cal M},w)
and
(\mathcal{M^{\prime}},w^{\prime})
which are image-finitary, then
{\cal M},w\,\underline{\leftrightarrow}\,{\cal M^{\prime}},w^{\prime}
iff
({\cal M},w)
and
(\mathcal{M^{\prime}},w^{\prime})
satisfy the exact same
\mathcal{L}_{\square}
-formulas.

3
A simple modal logic for bisimulations

3.1
Syntax

Definition 4
.

The language
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
is the extension of
\mathcal{L}_{\square}
generated by the following grammar:

A::=p\mid\perp\mid(A\to A)\mid\square A\mid{[\,b\,]}A

where
p
ranges over
\mathsf{At}
.

The meaning of
{[\,b\,]}A
is that “
A
is true in all states corresponding to the current one”.
The dual of
{[\,b\,]}
is
{\langle\,b\,\rangle}
, so that
{\langle\,b\,\rangle}A
reads “
A
is true at least one corresponding state of the current state”, and it is defined
\neg{[\,b\,]}\neg A
.
The underlying idea is that
{[\,b\,]}
(and its dual
{\langle\,b\,\rangle}
) refer in the models only to the points of the domain of a
correspondence
relation (as a particular case, the correspondence relation may be a bisimulation).

3.2
Semantics

Definition 5

(Frames for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
)
.

A frame for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
(or simply, a frame) is a tuple
{\mathfrak{F}}=({\cal F},{\cal F^{\prime}},Z)
where:

•

{\cal F},{\cal F^{\prime}}\in{\textsf{Fclass}}
, where
{\cal F}=(W,R)
and
{\cal F}^{\prime}=(W^{\prime},R^{\prime})
.

•

Z\subseteq W\times W^{\prime}
(
Z
is a relation
relation called
correspondence relation
).

A frame where
Z\neq\emptyset
and it satisfies properties (2) (forth) and (3) (back) of Def.
3
is called a bisimulation frame (or simply a bi-frame).

Definition 6

(Models for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
)
.

A model for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
(or simply, a model) is a tuple
{\mathfrak{M}}=({\mathfrak{F}},{\cal M},{\cal M^{\prime}})
where:

•

{\mathfrak{F}}=({\cal F},{\cal F^{\prime}},Z)
is a frame for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
.

•

{\cal M}
is a Kripke model based on
{\cal F}
and
{\cal M^{\prime}}
is a Kripke model based on
{\cal F^{\prime}}
.

We say in this case that the model
{\mathfrak{M}}
is based on
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

A model based on a bi-frame that moreover satisfies condition 1 of Def.
3
is called a
bi-model
.

Given a frame
{\mathfrak{F}}=({\cal F},{\cal F^{\prime}},Z)
, in what follows we simplify the model
{\mathfrak{M}}=({\mathfrak{F}},{\cal M},{\cal M^{\prime}})
by means of
{\mathfrak{M}}=({\cal M},{\cal M^{\prime}},Z)
.

Definition 7

(Truth for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}=({\cal M},{\cal M^{\prime}},Z)
be a model for
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
. Let
w
be in
{\cal M}
, and
w^{\prime}
be in
{\cal M^{\prime}}
. Then for any formulas
A
and
B
, we define the
truth relation
, that relates triples of the form
({\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{(\prime)},w^{(\prime)})
and formulas, as follows:

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\models p
iff
w\in V(p)
(for any
p\in\mathsf{At}
).

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models p
iff
w\in V^{\prime}(p)
(for any
p\in\mathsf{At}
).

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\not\models\perp
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\not\models\perp
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\models A\to B
iff either
{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\not\models A
or
{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\models B
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models A\to B
iff either
{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\not\models A
or
{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models B
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\models\square A
iff for every
v\in W
,

implies
{\mathfrak{M}},\mathcal{M},v\models A
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M^{\prime}},w^{\prime}\models\square A
iff for every
v^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
,
wR^{\prime}v
implies
{\mathfrak{M}},\mathcal{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime}\models A
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M},w\models[\,b\,]A
iff for all
w^{\prime}\in W
s.t.
wZw^{\prime}
,
{\mathfrak{M}},\mathcal{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models A
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models[\,b\,]A
.

When the context is clear, we simplify
{\mathfrak{M}},{\cal M}^{(\prime)},w^{(\prime)}\models A
as
{\cal M}^{(\prime)},w^{(\prime)}\models A
or as
{\mathfrak{M}},w^{(\prime)}\models A
depending on convenience.
A formula
A
is
valid in a model

iff
{\mathfrak{M}},w^{\ast}\models A
for all

. A formula is

iff it is valid in every model

based on

. A formula

\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
(in symbols,

) iff for every pointed model

,
\mathfrak{M},\mathcal{M}^{(\prime)},w^{(\prime)}\models B
for all
B\in\Gamma

\mathfrak{M},\mathcal{M}^{(\prime)},w^{(\prime)}\models A
.

.

Note that the definition of frame does not assume that

The clauses of satisfaction for the defined connective

iff there is

.

•

.

Expressivity

The first logical question we might face is whether we gained anything at the level of expressivity by adding

3.4
(Un)definability

Definition 9

(Definibality)
.

Given a class of frames (models)
\mathfrak{C}
and a language
\mathcal{L}
interpreted over
\mathfrak{C}
, we say that a property P is
\mathcal{L}
-definable (within
\mathfrak{C}
) through a set of formulas

\mathfrak{S}\in\mathfrak{C}

satisfies P iff
\mathfrak{S}\models A
for all

. Moreover, we say that a property P is
\mathcal{L}
-definable (within
\mathfrak{C}
) iff it is

The following two propositions show that the new modality
{[\,b\,]}
is sufficient to talk about the main definitional conditions of bisimulations.

Proposition 2
.

The property (atomic harmony) (see Def.
3
) is
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
-definable in the class of all models.

Proof.

Consider the set

\Gamma_{AH}=\{l\to{[\,b\,]}l\mid\text{ for any literal }l\}

All we have to prove is that all formulas of this set are valid in a model iff this model satisfies the condition of atomic harmony. Let
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime},Z)
be a model where
{\cal M}=({\cal F},V)
and
{\cal M^{\prime}}=({\cal F^{\prime}},V^{\prime})
. Assume first that
\mathfrak{M}
satisfies atomic harmony. Let us show that
l\to{[\,b\,]}l
is valid in
\mathfrak{M}
for any literal
l
. Take a world
w
of
\mathfrak{M}
and suppose
\mathfrak{M},w\not\models{[\,b\,]}l
. This implies, by the semantics of
{[\,b\,]}
, that there is a

such that
wZw^{\prime}
and

. Since
\mathfrak{M}
satisfies atomic harmony, the latter implies that
\mathfrak{M},w\not\models l
, hence
\mathfrak{M},w\models l\to{[\,b\,]}l
. Since

is any world of
\mathfrak{M}
, we conclude that all formulas in
\Gamma_{AH}
are valid in
\mathfrak{M}
. Second, assume

does not satisfy atomic harmony, then there are

and

such that

and there are some

such that

and

or vice versa. In the first case, we have that

. In the second, we have that

. Hence,

is not valid at

∎

.

Properties (forth) and (back) (see Def.

5
Conclusion

Recap.

Let us start our closing with a brief recap. This paper is inserted into a long-standing tradition of approaching mathematical notions through the use of modal languages and semantics. More specifically, it advances a milestone in the modal study of notions that stem from the meta-theory of standard modal logic, an enterprise that we have coined
meta-modal logic
. Our focus has been on a fundamental notion in the model and correspondence theory of modal logic: bisimulations. Our main findings can be summarised as follows. First, the main definitional conditions of bisimulations for the basic modal language (
atomic harmony
,
forth
, and
back
) can be defined in a quite simple extension of such a language that uses one single additional modality,
{[\,b\,]}
, to describe the bisimulation relation among two arbitrary Kripke models. Moreover, this modality is shown to be not only sufficient, but also necessary to capture the three mentioned conditions on bisimulations. Second, the set of all valid formulas in this extended language can be captured in a sound and complete axiomatisation, based on our previous definitional results. Finally, we apply a robust method for the second group of results: our proofs are encoded and verified in Isabelle/HOL, which, in addition, helped us spot some errors in the original, handwritten proof trials.

Discussion.

As mentioned in the introduction, the closest line of work that we are aware of is the modal approach to filtrations developed in
[
1
,
11
,
19
]
. Curiously, in
[
19
]
, the authors claim that “the Lindstrom-style analysis of modal logic in
[
20
]
shows how the crucial notion of a bisimulation is not itself modally definable”. This assertion can be interpreted in at least two different senses. First, bisimulations are not definable in the basic modal language. This seems rather obvious, and we have given formal arguments for this in Section
3.4
. Second, bisimulations are not definable with the aid of any additional modalities. This is false, according to our definability results (also presented in Section
3.4
). There is a subtlety here: the nonemptiness of
Z
, which is also present in the standard definition of bisimulations, is indeed non-definable in
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
. However, this is shown to be harmless in the axiomatisation of validities in bi-models. Moreover, the condition can be easily defined with the aid of a universal modality
[\,U\,]

[
7
]
which, in principle, provokes no special difficulty for axiomatisation purposes either.
[\,U\,]
is interpreted in a pointed model as follows:
\mathfrak{M},w\models[\,U\,]A
iff
\mathfrak{M},u\models A
for all
u\in W\cup W^{\prime}
. Then, it is easy to check that the formula
\langle\,U\,\rangle{\langle\,b\,\rangle}\top
defines precisely the non-emptiness of the relation
Z
.

Open challenges.

One natural open question about our approach is whether the problem of deciding if a formula is valid in the class of all bi-models is computable. Again, previous voices seem sceptical about it: “The general modal logic of bisimulation over universes of relational models seems undecidable, since bisimulation imposes a form of commutation that can encode grid structure”
[
19
]
. On a similar line,
[
18
]
claims: “Axiomatizing complete meta-model-theories in this style may have its surprises. E.g., the modal logic of bisimulation relating modal models may be undecidable: the back-and-forth property of bisimulation defines a grid whose complete modal logic is known to be of high complexity”. Hence, a formal counter-part of these claims seems worthy to be pursued.

Even if undecidable, the modal logic presented here paves the way for a new investigation of automating reasoning tasks related to bisimulations. For instance, deciding whether a given relation between models is a bisimulation amounts to checking frame validity of (FORTH) and (BACK), and model validity of (HARM) in the associated
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
-structure. These problems are clearly decidable for finite frames and models. Hence, one could implement a modal model-checker that benefits from state-of-the-art techniques (e.g, symbolic model checking
[
4
]
) and compare it with tools in the literature that follow different approaches (e.g.,
[
2
]
).

Finally, a third research perspective consists of the study of extensions of
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
in order to tackle further meta-modal notions. Naturally, the next target would be surjective p-morphisms
[
3
]
, which are known to preserve validity between Kripke frames. A surjective p-morphism is a bisimulation among frames that is moreover a total, surjective function. We know from classical frame correspondence results that partial functionality of
Z
is definable in
{\cal L}_{{\square[\,b\,]}}
(it corresponds to
{\langle\,b\,\rangle}p\to{[\,b\,]}p
), but totality and surjectivity need further expressive resources (again, the use of a universal modality
[
7
]
).

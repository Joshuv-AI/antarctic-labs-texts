Application of multimodal ultrasound radiomics in the diagnosis of superficial lymph node tuberculosis
Authors: Dongming Su, Peijun Chen, Ying Zhang, Ying Wang, Gaoyi Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo develop preoperative diagnostic models for superficial lymph node tuberculosis (LNTB) using radiomic features extracted from multimodal ultrasound imaging, including gray-scale ultrasound (US), ultrasound elastography (UE), and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS), in conjunction with various machine learning algorithms.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted on 222 patients with lymphadenopathy. The patients were randomly divided into a training group (n = 156) and a validation group (n = 66) in a 7:3 ratio. 837 radiomics features were extracted from images of each modality (US, UE and CEUS). After initial screening by hypothesis testing, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with five-fold cross-validation was used for feature dimensionality reduction and selection. After feature selection, five machine learning models—logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, support vector machine, and AdaBoost—were used to construct radiomic-based models. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated, and the area under the curve (AUC) was computed to assess the performance of each model in predicting superficial LNTB. Clinical decision curve (DCA) is used to measure the net benefits under various probability thresholds. The diagnostic performance of ultrasound physicians was also compared with that of the best-performing machine learning model.ResultsAmong the models generated by different algorithms, the decision tree model exhibited the best performance, achieving an AUC of 0.909 (95% CI, 0.789–0.949) in the training cohort and 0.866 (95% CI, 0.774–0.958) in the validation cohort. The AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) for ultrasound physicians were 0.693 (95% CI, 0.568–0.818), 0.698, 0.689, 0.722, and 0.664, respectively. The DCA shows that the decision tree model has the best net income in the range of clinical relevance threshold of 0.6–0.8. Delong test showed that decision tree model was superior to ultrasonic doctor’s diagnosis (Z = 2.98, p<0.0029).ConclusionThe radiomic model constructed from US, UE, and CEUS demonstrated robust diagnostic performance for superficial LNTB, with the decision tree model yielding the best results.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02403-2.Keywords: Ultrasound, Radiomics, Lymph node tuberculosis, Diagnostic models, Machine learning

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease primarily transmitted through aerosolized droplets during coughing and caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) [1]. According to the World Health Organization’s 2022 Global TB Report, approximately 10.6 million new TB cases and 1.6 million TB-related deaths occurred in 2021 [2]. Lymph node tuberculosis (LNTB) accounts for 35% of all extrapulmonary TB cases, with cervical LNTB being the most common form [3]. Clinical manifestations of LNTB include lymphadenopathy, tenderness [4]. However, clinical manifestations of LNTB are non-specific, and its ultrasonic features overlap significantly with those of other benign and malignant lymphadenopathies, including nonspecific hyperplasia, nontuberculous mycobacteria, sarcoidosis, metastatic cancer, and malignant lymphoma [5]. Consequently, clinicians may face challenges in accurately diagnosing the condition [6]. This mismanagement may result in increased morbidity and mortality, as well as the risk of TB transmission from undiagnosed pulmonary TB patients with co-infection [7].

The definitive diagnosis of LNTB relies on the detection of MTB in clinical samples from symptomatic patients [8]. Common diagnostic techniques include direct microscopy, pathological culture, nucleic acid amplification tests, and biopsy culture [9], many of which are invasive procedures [10]. Microscopy has limited diagnostic value due to its low sensitivity [11], while histological diagnosis depends on the presence of acid-fast bacilli and granulomatous inflammation. However, histopathology can be easily confused with other diseases, and bacterial load correlates with tissue pathology [12]. The Xpert MTB/RIF test (Cepheid Inc.), an automated polymerase chain reaction-based assay [13], has low sensitivity, and a negative result cannot rule out TB [14]. While culture remains the gold standard for TB diagnosis, its sensitivity ranges from 30% to 80% in extrapulmonary specimens, with the added limitation of a long turnaround time [15].

Ultrasound is a convenient, cost-effective, radiation-free, rapid, and non-invasive diagnostic modality for evaluating lymph nodes, but traditional qualitative assessment of multimodal ultrasound (gray-scale [US], ultrasound elastography [UE], and contrast-enhanced ultrasound [CEUS]) is subjective and has limited diagnostic accuracy for superficial LNTB, with high misdiagnosis rates in clinical practice [16].Radiomics is an emerging field in medical imaging that transforms images into quantitative data, which can then be analyzed to support clinical decision-making. By extracting features that reflect underlying pathophysiological processes from biomedical images, radiomics holds great promise for improving diagnostic accuracy [17].

This study aims to develop a radiomic model using multimodal ultrasound imaging to predict superficial LNTB preoperatively, and further construct single-modal subgroup models to evaluate the diagnostic value of different modal combinations, providing a potential early diagnostic tool adaptable to different clinical practice conditions.

Materials and methods

Ethical consideration

The study protocol was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital (2022KY173). The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was retrospective, and all patients gave up informed consent. This study was designed, implemented, and reported in strict accordance with the CLEAR radiomics framework and the METRICS AI model reporting standard to ensure scientificity, reproducibility, and clinical applicability.

Study subjects

This retrospective study included patients with lymphadenopathy who presented to our hospital between September 2018 and September 2022. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients aged 18 years or older; (2) patients who underwent preoperative US, ultrasound elastography (UE), and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) examinations; (3) all enlarged lymph nodes were confirmed via ultrasound-guided biopsy or surgical excision, with a definitive pathological diagnosis; (4) complete clinical data and original DICOM images available from the hospital’s PACS system; (5) no prior clinical treatment. The exclusion criteria were: (1) poor image quality due to artifacts such as gas or anatomical variations; (2) poor CEUS video quality caused by excessive patient breathing amplitude or probe movement; (3) concurrent malignancies.

Instruments and methods

Ultrasound examinations were performed using Philips IU22 and IU-Elite ultrasound diagnostic machines, equipped with L12-5 and L9-3 broadband linear probes, with a frequency range of 3 to 12 MHz. Two experienced physicians, each with over five years of expertise in superficial organ ultrasound diagnosis, collected gray-scale, UE, and CEUS images of the primary lesions. Patients were examined in the supine position, and images of the largest long-axis and short-axis sections of the lesions were stored. UE was performed on the long-axis section, followed by CEUS, using SonoVue (a second-generation contrast agent). After reconstituting SonoVue lyophilized powder with 5.0 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride solution, 2.0 mL of the contrast agent was intravenously injected through the antecubital vein, and dynamic CEUS images were acquired. Subsequently, the patient underwent tissue biopsy or surgical excision, and etiological examination to confirm his final diagnosis. The interval between multimodal ultrasound examination and ultrasound-guided biopsy/surgical resection was 1–7 days (median, 3 days). All patients received no antibiotics, anti-tuberculosis drugs, radiotherapy, chemotherapy or other treatments before imaging and pathological biopsy.

Ultrasound image feature analysis

A senior ultrasound physician (all with more than 10 years of clinical experience in superficial organ ultrasound diagnosis) were enrolled for comparative diagnostic analysis. The physician independently read the gray-scale, UE, and CEUS images in a blinded manner to pathological results, and the majority voting method was used to determine the final diagnostic result of the physicians. The evaluations included the following parameters: (1) lymph node enlargement, cortical thickening, disordered architecture, absence of the hilum, liquefaction necrosis, coarse calcification, and sinus tract formation on US; (2) stiffness or moderate stiffness on UE; (3) heterogeneous hyperenhancement or ring-like enhancement on CEUS.

Image segmentation and radiomic feature extraction

Two physicians, each with over five years of experience in superficial lymph node ultrasound diagnosis and blinded to pathological results, manually delineated regions of interest (ROI) on the US, UE, and CEUS images using 3D Slicer software to obtain ROI mask files, which were saved in NRRD format. The segmented ROIs were then imported into Pyradiomics software to extract radiomic features. During model development, the synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) was applied to the training cohort to address potential class imbalance. In addition, all extracted features were normalized using Z-scores before feature selection and model construction. For image filtering, in addition to the original images, wavelet-filtered images were included to extract higher-order radiomic features. 30 cases of ROI were independently delineated by each of the two physicians were randomly selected for intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) analysis to evaluate the inter-observer reproducibility of the extracted imaging features. If ICC>0.75 is considered to have high inter-observer consistency, biological range and good repeatability.

In this study, the image omics features we obtained include first-order features, Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix, GLCM) features, and Gray-Level Difference. Co-occurrence matrix (gldm) feature, gray-level run-length matrix (glrlm), Gray-Level Size Zone Matrix, GLSZM) feature, neighborhood gray. Neighborhood gray-tone difference matrix (NGTD M), shape features and wavelet features.

Feature selection and model construction

Patients were randomly divided into a training group and a validation group in a 7:3 ratio. All extracted features were normalized using Z-scores. Feature selection was performed using a two-step procedure. First, univariate hypothesis testing was applied to identify features that differed significantly between the LNTB and non-LNTB groups (P < 0.05). The retained features were then further reduced using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with five-fold cross-validation. LASSO performs variable selection by shrinking the coefficients of less informative features toward zero, with the degree of shrinkage controlled by the regularization parameter λ. As this parameter increases, the constraints on model complexity become stricter, which helps to establish a model architecture with fewer variables. Univariate hypothesis testing (P < 0.05) was first used to screen out differential features related to LNTB, which eliminated noise features with no biological significance, reduced the computational burden of subsequent LASSO regression, and avoided overfitting caused by high-dimensional features. LASSO regression was then used for further feature selection. The optimal features were used to construct predictive models employing logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), and AdaBoost algorithms.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 20.0 and R software. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s) and compared using t-tests. Non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-square test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to evaluate the predictive performance of each model. Clinical decision curve (DCA) is used to measure the net benefits under various probability thresholds. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Feature extraction and dimensionality reduction

From the image of each modality(US, UE, and CEUS images), the image radomics features can be extracted, and 837 features can be extracted from the image of each modality, with a total of 2511 features extracted.

The consistency between the observers of the extracted features is determined by the ICC. ICC>0.75 is considered to be the strong stability of the image characteristics, and the ICC of 837 features in this study is 0.928–0.982, which shows that the consistency between observers is good.

Initial feature screening was conducted using hypothesis testing, followed by dimensionality reduction with the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and five-fold cross-validation. LASSO regression with five-fold cross-validation determined the optimal regularization parameter λ = 0.025, and a total of 7 optimal radiomic features were finally retained (3 from US, 2 from UE, 2 from CEUS). Model construction and validation

Five machine learning models—logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), and AdaBoost—were constructed to classify and predict LNTB status (Figs. 3 and 4). Hyperparameter tuning was conducted in the training cohort using 5-fold cross-validation combined with a grid-search strategy. For each candidate parameter combination, the mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) across the five folds was calculated, and the combination yielding the highest mean AUC was selected as the optimal setting. Specifically, for the decision tree model, the search included the complexity parameter (cp.) and maximum tree depth (maxdepth), and the final values were cp. = 0.001 and maxdepth = 3. For the random forest model, mtry was tuned and the optimal value was 2, while the number of trees was fixed at 500. For the support vector machine model with a radial basis function kernel, cost and gamma were optimized, and the final values were cost = 10 and gamma = 1; probability estimation was enabled. For the AdaBoost model, the number of boosting iterations (iter) and the maximum tree depth of the base learner were tuned, and the final values were iter = 50 and maxdepth = 3; discrete boosting with exponential loss was used. Logistic regression was fitted using the default binomial setting and therefore did not require hyperparameter tuning.

Discussion

This study constructed five radiomic models for superficial LNTB diagnosis based on multimodal ultrasound (US, UE, CEUS) and different machine learning algorithms, and found that the decision tree model had the optimal diagnostic performance (training set AUC = 0.909(95% CI, 0.789–0.949); validation set AUC = 0.866(95% CI, 0.774–0.958). The diagnostic efficacy of the decision tree model was significantly superior to that of experienced senior ultrasound physicians (AUC = 0.693(95% CI, 0.568–0.818), P < 0.0029), indicating that quantitative radiomic features extracted from multimodal ultrasound can effectively capture the pathological characteristics of LNTB and improve diagnostic accuracy. The radiomic model constructed from US, UE, and CEUS demonstrated robust diagnostic performance for superficial LNTB. Multimodal ultrasound-based radiomic models demonstrate high diagnostic accuracy for superficial LNTB. These models outperform traditional ultrasound diagnosis, offering valuable preoperative diagnostic support.

Jones and Campbell described the progression of lymph node tuberculosis (LNTB) in five stages, beginning with enlarged, firm, and mobile lymph nodes, progressing to immobile and suppurative stages, and eventually forming persistent sinus tracts [18]. This progression closely mirrors the clinical presentation of malignant lymphadenopathy, posing significant challenges for differential diagnosis. Moon et al. emphasized the critical importance of accurately diagnosing LNTB, as it requires specific treatment [19]. For drug-sensitive LNTB, a six-month regimen of anti-tuberculosis drugs—including isoniazid, pyrazinamide, rifampin, and ethambutol—is typically sufficient to achieve a cure [20]. However, long-term follow-up is essential, as post-treatment lymph node enlargement, fluctuation, and sinus formation may persist [21].

Machine learning has shown promising results in distinguishing between pathological and normal cervical lymph nodes, with solid performance across a variety of pathological types [22]. The typical histopathology of TB includes granulomatous inflammation, granuloma formation, caseous necrosis, abscess formation, fibrosis, and calcification [23]. These pathologic changes are closely reflected in the internal ultrasound appearance of LNTB [24]. In our study, we employed three ultrasound modalities—US, ultrasound elastography (UE), and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS)—to enhance diagnostic accuracy.

Ultrasound is widely utilized for characterizing superficial lymph nodes [25], offering superior capability compared to CT and MRI for assessing lymph node morphology and internal structure [26]. Previous studies have identified characteristic findings of LNTB on US, such as hyperechoic regions and thin echo layers [27], consistent with the findings in our study. Hyperechoic regions are typically caused by necrotic debris (e.g., caseation, calcification, or air) [28], while the thin echo layer represents granulomatous tissue surrounding areas of caseous necrosis, characterized by proliferation of epithelioid cells, Langerhans giant cells, and capillaries, as well as lymphocyte and plasma cell infiltration [29]. In some cases, tuberculous lymph nodes may appear hypoechoic due to the high incidence of cystic necrosis within the nodule [30].

Ultrasound elastography (UE) is increasingly recognized as a complementary tool to traditional ultrasound, offering virtual palpation and the ability to objectively measure tissue stiffness [31]. Yang et al. developed a diagnostic model for LNTB based on both US and UE features, demonstrating that shear-wave elastography (SE-US) outperformed conventional US in evaluating LNTB in the training set, suggesting that SE-US may offer better differentiation between benign and malignant cervical lymph nodes [32]. Previous studies have shown that superficial LNTB predominantly exhibits moderate to hard tissue stiffness on UE, which aligns with our findings [33]. When lymph nodes predominantly appear blue on UE, the corresponding pathological specimens are typically firm, with granulomas and significant caseous necrosis. Conversely, when lymph nodes appear green or yellow on UE, the tissue tends to be softer, with less caseous necrosis and more liquefaction [4].

CEUS is a novel diagnostic technique that has proven useful for differentiating between normal and abnormal lesions [34]. CEUS demonstrates higher sensitivity in detecting small areas of vascular regression or increased vascularity that Doppler technology may miss, improving the accuracy of lymph node classification [26]. Dan et al. analyzed 203 CEUS cases for diagnosing LNTB and found that combining heterogeneous enhancement with rim enhancement significantly distinguished LNTB cases (74.23%, 72/97) from non-tuberculous cases (5.66%, 6/106) [35]. These findings are consistent with our results. Rim-like enhancement on CEUS is associated with the abnormal proliferation of granulomatous tissue and capillaries surrounding the lymph node, while enhanced internal areas indicate lymphocyte and granulomatous tissue proliferation, and non-enhanced areas are indicative of caseous necrosis [19].

While traditional ultrasound provides useful information, single-modal ultrasound imaging offers limited diagnostic insights. Combining multiple imaging modalities, alongside clinical information, may enhance the accuracy and comprehensiveness of superficial lymph node evaluations. Sun et al. noted that combining UE with conventional ultrasound enhances the evaluation of malignant lymph nodes and improves diagnostic efficiency for enlarged superficial lymph nodes [36]. Similarly, studies have shown that CEUS significantly improves diagnostic accuracy for malignant cervical lymph nodes when used in conjunction with conventional ultrasound [37]. In our study, ultrasound physicians diagnosed LNTB with a sensitivity of 0.698, specificity of 0.689, PPV of 0.722, and NPV of 0.664. The relatively lower diagnostic performance compared to radiomics may be attributed to the classification of LNTB into four types, where diagnostic accuracy is dependent on the presence and extent of granuloma formation, caseous or liquefactive necrosis, fibrosis, and calcification [38]. Thus, while US, UE, and CEUS contribute valuable diagnostic information for LNTB, they have a relatively high rate of misclassification.

In this study, we constructed radiomic models based on US, UE, and CEUS using various machine learning algorithms. All models demonstrated high diagnostic performance for superficial LNTB, with the random forest and AdaBoost models achieving perfect AUC values (1.000) in the training cohort. In the validation cohort, the random forest and AdaBoost models maintained high performance, with AUC values of 0.861 and 0.820, respectively, and sensitivities of 0.972 and 0.917, with PPVs of 0.833 and 0.805. These results highlight the potential of the random forest and AdaBoost models in diagnosing LNTB. The decision tree model, with an AUC of 0.909 in the training set and 0.866 in the validation set, was identified as the most optimal model, demonstrating excellent sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV. Overall, the decision tree model provides valuable support in improving the accurate diagnosis and treatment of LNTB patients. Therefore, the multimodal ultrasound-based radiomics model offers enhanced stability and diagnostic efficiency for diagnosing LNTB.

This study has several limitations: (1) it was a single-center, retrospective study with a limited sample size; (2) all ultrasound images were obtained using the same brand of ultrasound equipment, and it is unclear whether images from other manufacturers would show any biases; (3) a binary classification approach was used, and non-tuberculous lymphadenopathy was not further subdivided. Future research should include subgroup analyses to better differentiate between various non-tuberculous lymph node diseases; (4) all the patients in this study completed three-mode ultrasound, which avoided the disadvantage of lower diagnostic efficiency compared with the single-mode prediction model studied in the past. This may also lead to case selection bias, and prospective research will be carried out in the future to verify the generalization of the model; (5) a prospective targeted validation study will be carried out in the future, including specific clinical populations such as primary hospital patients with only US examination, immunocompromised patients (e.g., diabetes, HIV) with suspected LNTB, and pediatric patients with superficial lymphadenopathy, to further verify the model’s diagnostic efficacy in different clinical scenarios.

Conclusion

Multimodal ultrasound-based radiomic models demonstrate high diagnostic accuracy for superficial LNTB, with the decision tree model exhibiting the best performance. These models outperform traditional ultrasound diagnosis, offering valuable preoperative diagnostic support.

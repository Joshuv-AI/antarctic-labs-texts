Lost and found charge in quantum batteries
Debanjan Dey Sarkar, Mallika Mondal, Preeti Parashar, Tamal Guha

Abstract
Quantum batteries are prone to loosing their stored charge, when interacting with a thermal environment. However, getting a limited assistance from the thermal environment, is it possible to recover the charge back, in a reusable form? Here we answer this question affirmatively, leveraging a non-trivial usage of the seemingly useless thermal environment to recycle the quantum batteries. The framework involves two different kind of assistance from thermal environment - one by accessing only the thermal particle, actively participating in the interaction; and the other, involving assistance from an additional purifying subsystem for the thermal environment, bearing a passive role to the interaction. Interestingly, we report that the difference between the retrieved charge between these two degrees of assistance characterizes the amount of entanglement generated by the thermal operation between the quantum battery and the purifying subsystem for the thermal environment.

In the context of thermodynamic charge reversal, we first model the thermal operations in terms of their isometric extensions, involving two entangled parties which are locally thermal . One of them actively participates in the thermal interaction while the other can be thought of as a purifying reference, which is also a thermal state marginally. The assistance from the thermal environment, therefore, can be characterized in two different ways: the

Isometric extensions for thermal operations
–The resource theory of thermodynamics in presence of a thermal bath undertakes a singleton set of free states with the temperature identical to that of the bath
Brandao et al. 2013
.

To put it more formally, consider a quantum system governed by the system Hamiltonian
H_{s}=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}|\epsilon_{i}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{i}|
, together with a bath of given Hamiltonian
H_{b}=\sum_{k}E_{k}|E_{k}\rangle\langle E_{k}|
. Any thermal state corresponding to the bath Hamiltonian can be written as

\tau_{\beta}^{b}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{b}}}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H_{b}})}=\frac{\sum_{k}e^{-\beta E_{k}}|E_{k}\rangle\langle E_{k}|}{\sum_{k}e^{-\beta E_{k}}}

(1)

where,
\beta=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}
is the inverse temperature;
k_{B}
being the Boltzmann constant and
T
being the temperature of the thermal bath. Accordingly, the free state of the system is identified as

\tau_{\beta}^{s}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{s}}}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H_{s}})}=\frac{\sum_{i}e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}|\epsilon_{i}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{i}|}{\sum_{i}e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}}.

In the rest of the paper, we will often refer to such states as the thermal state of
inverse temperature

\beta
.

At this juncture, any
\beta
-thermal operation
(\Lambda_{\beta})
on the system state (
\rho_{s}
) will have the following Stinespring realization:

\Lambda_{\beta}(\rho_{s})=\operatorname{Tr}_{b^{\prime}}[U_{sb}(\rho_{s}\otimes\tau_{\beta}^{b})U_{sb}^{\dagger}],

(2)

where,
U_{sb}
is the system-bath joint unitary, preserving the total energy, that is,

[U_{sb},H_{s}\otimes I_{b}+I_{s}\otimes H_{b}]=0,

with
I_{x}
a s the identity operator on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{x}
. The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{b^{\prime}}
associated to the traced out system

can be, in general, different from that of the
\mathcal{H}_{b}
, such that
\Lambda_{\beta}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{s})\mapsto\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{s^{\prime}})
and
\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b}=\mathcal{H}_{s^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b^{\prime}}

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{x})
denotes the set of all linear operators on the set
\mathcal{H}_{x}
.

The thermal state for a given Hamiltonian is regarded as the state of minimal energy (maximal entropy) for a given entropy (energy). This, together with the fact that the global unitary
U_{sb}
in Eq. (
2
) is energy preserving directs
Gour 2024
,

\Lambda_{\beta}({\tau_{\beta}^{s}})=\tau_{\beta}^{s^{\prime}}.

Moreover, under such an operation the free energy (with respect to the inverse temperature
\beta

always decreases. That is,

F_{\beta}(\rho_{s})\geq F_{\beta}(\Lambda_{\beta}(\rho_{s})),\quad\forall\rho_{s}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{s}),

where,

is the set of all density operators on

and the free energy
F_{\beta}(\rho_{x})=\operatorname{Tr}(H_{x}\rho_{x})-\frac{1}{\beta}S(\rho_{x})

as the von Neumann entropy of

While the analysis given below holds for most general thermal operations, for the sake of simplicity, we will drop the
primed
-symbol over the output Hilbert spaces, assuming
\mathcal{H}_{s^{\prime}}\simeq\mathcal{H}_{s}
and
\mathcal{H}_{b^{\prime}}\simeq\mathcal{H}_{b}
. Let us now consider the following bipartite purification of the thermal state of the bath, given in Eq. (
1
),

\displaystyle\ket{\phi^{+}_{\beta}}_{bR}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z}}\sum_{k}\exp(-\frac{\beta E_{k}}{2})\ket{E_{k}}_{b}\otimes\ket{E_{k}}_{R}

(3)

where,

is the partition function. Note that, while the basis choice for the reference (

to preserve local thermality. Moreover, among the two quantum systems

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda_{\beta}}\ket{\psi}_{s}=(U_{sb}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{R})(\ket{\psi}_{s}\otimes\ket{\phi^{+}_{\beta}}_{bR})=\sum_{k}a_{k}\ket{\phi_{k}^{\beta}}_{sbR}.

Retrieving the lost charge from thermal environment
– We will now move to our main results, concerning the retrieval of the free energy dissipated to the thermal environment.
Consider a d-level quantum battery
\rho_{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{s}
in its spectral form, where

\ket{\psi_{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}a_{k}^{i}\ket{\epsilon_{k}},\text{ such that }\forall k,\penalty\ a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\text{ and }\sum_{k}|a_{k}|^{2}=1.

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}a^{i}_{k}\ket{\phi_{k}^{\beta}}_{sbR},\text{ as in Eq. (\ref{e4})}.

The resource theory of thermodynamics then immediately implies

Definition 1
.

The optimal charge retrieval under weak assistance is defined as

W_{weak}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})=\max_{\{\Pi^{k}_{b}\}}W(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta},\{\Pi^{k}_{b}\})

In a similar fashion, one can consider the amount of charge retrieval when both the reference and bath qudit can be locally accessed , i.e.,

\displaystyle W(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta},\{\Pi_{b}^{k}\otimes\Pi_{R}^{l}\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}p_{k,l}F_{\beta}(\sigma_{s|k,l})

\displaystyle\text{where, }p_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}[(\mathbb{I}_{s}\otimes\Pi_{b}^{k}\otimes\Pi_{R}^{l})\sigma_{sbR}],

\displaystyle\text{and }\sigma_{s|k,l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{k,l}}\operatorname{Tr}_{bR}[(\mathbb{I}_{s}\otimes\Pi_{b}^{k}\otimes\Pi_{R}^{l})\sigma_{sbR}]

Accordingly, we can define

Definition 2
.

The optimal charge retrieval under strong assistance is defined as

W_{strong}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})=\max_{\{\Pi^{k}_{b}\otimes\Pi^{l}_{R}\}}W(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta},\{\Pi^{k}_{b}\otimes\Pi^{l}_{R}\})

After setting the complete framework for the environment assisted charge retrieval in a quantum battery, we can now give the following proposition, which will be crucial for the subsequent results.

Proposition 1
.

For any thermal operation
\Lambda_{\beta}
and an arbitrary input quantum state
\rho_{s}
, we have the following relation:

F_{\beta}(\sigma_{s})\leq W_{weak}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})\leq W_{strong}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{s}H_{s})

where,
\sigma_{s}=:\Lambda_{\beta}(\rho_{s})
and
H_{s}
is the governing system Hamiltonian, as mentioned earlier.

It may be noted that the true amount of retrieved free-energetic charge, both for the weak and strong assistance, should be accounted in terms of the free energy difference between the average post-measurement states of the QB and the free energy of the thermal bath of inverse temperature

Main Results.
– Before moving on to our main findings, we will first briefly recall a specific entanglement measure for the bipartite quantum systems.

Consider a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
and we would like to compute the minimal number of maximally entangled states required to prepare this
\rho_{AB}
asymptotically. This quantity is generally characterized in terms of
Entanglement of Formation

E_{f}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle E_{f}(\rho_{AB})=\min_{\{p_{i},\psi_{i}\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{f}(\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}_{AB}),

(8)

where,
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}_{AB}
and the minimization is taken over all such decompositions of
\rho_{AB}
.

Coming back to the charge retrieval of a quantum battery under the weak assistance from the environment, we can obtain the following upper-bound in terms of the amount of entanglement generated between the battery and the reference.

For any quantum battery
\rho_{s}
and any nontrivial thermal operation
\Lambda_{\beta}

W_{weak}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{s}H_{s})-\frac{1}{\beta}E_{f}(\sigma_{sR}),

where,
\sigma_{sR}=\operatorname{Tr}_{b}(\sigma_{sbR})
. Moreover, the above inequality saturates whenever

is pure.

Theorem

More precisely, during the thermal operation,the joint state of the system, bath and reference becomes a pure entangled state, when the initial state of QB is pure. Under weak assistance only a part (the bath qudit) of the pure entangled state can be accessed,thereby restricting the optimal retrieval of charge.But in the strong assistance scenario,the access to the complete pure state lifts this bound and reaches to its the optimal value i.e. the total energy of the system.Similarly, for the initially mixed state QB,the joint system-bath-reference state is a mixed entangled state and can be purified with an additional reference system (

For any arbitrary quantum battery
\rho_{s}

\Delta(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta}):=W_{strong}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})-W_{weak}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})\geq\frac{1}{\beta}E_{f}(\sigma_{sR})

is to be a pure quantum battery.

Discussions.
— We have explored the possibilities of recycling an arbitrary dimensional quantum battery by retrieving the charge it lost due to spontaneous thermal interactions. The scenario in it’s weakest variant involved a minimal classical assistance from the bath particle. However this is not the optimal amount, in general. On the other hand, when an additional quantum system that purifies the thermal bath, takes active participation in the retrieving process, the amount increases further and achieves optimality whenever the initial battery is a pure quantum state. While our analysis involved charge as the free energy stored in a quantum state, the assistance of an additional system, to extract ergotropy from the target one has also been encountered in various contexts. For instance, the classical assistance from the ancillary system leads to the notion of daemonic ergotropy
Francica et al. 2017
, which eventually identifies the presence of one-way discord in bipartite pure quantum states. On the other hand, in an implicit form, the assistance of the measurement outcome, performed on the additional correlated system results in successful transportation of ergotropy via controlled configurations of thermal channels
Simonov et al. 2022
;
Simonov et al. 2025
.

Interestingly, here we have shown that the difference between the strong and the weakly assisted retrieved charge quantifies the amount of distant entanglement generated by the thermal operation between the quantum battery and the reference system. In this regard, a recent result is worth mentioning
Biswas et al. 2025
, where incompatible measurements on the ancillary system may give raise to additional free energy extraction from the thermodynamic systems. Moreover, there the steerability of quantum correlations has been identified as a sole reason behind such an advantageous free energy extraction. In our premises, alternatively, the difference between the retrieval of the lost charge, under various degrees of assistance, identifies with a fundamental entity, viz., entanglement of formation for multipartite quantum systems. Remarkably, our results also suggest that the inaccessibility of the reference system and hence the entanglement, lock the potential to retrieve optimal charge in a quantum battery, when the assistance is limited to the bath system only.

\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}
performed on the subsystem
R
we get

W_{strong}(\ket{\psi},\Lambda_{\beta})=E(\tau_{\beta}).

Since each of the outcome of the projective measurement collapses the battery state in a corresponding pure state. In fact, any possible projective measurement here results in the same amount of charge retrieval.

Also note that in this case retrieved charge under both the strong and weak assistance is independent of the initial QB. Therefore, the same of charge can also be retrieved when the QB is mixed in general.

.6.2
Case 2.

For
\alpha=1
, the action of
\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda_{\beta}}
on a pure QB
\ket{\psi}=a\ket{0}+b\ket{1}
, will take the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda_{\beta}}(a\ket{0}+b\ket{1})

\displaystyle\to a\ket{0}_{s}\otimes\ket{\phi^{+}_{\beta}}_{bR}-b\ket{1}_{s}\otimes\ket{\phi^{-}_{\beta}}_{bR}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z}}(\ket{\xi}_{s}\otimes\ket{00}_{bR}+e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\ket{\psi}_{s}\otimes\ket{11}_{bR}).

(29)

where,
\ket{\xi}=a\ket{0}-b\ket{1}
.

Evidently, from Eq. (
.6.2
), it is clear that

\displaystyle W_{weak}(\ket{\psi},\Lambda_{\beta})

\displaystyle=W_{strong}(\ket{\psi},\Lambda_{\beta})=E(\sigma_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}E(|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|)+\frac{e^{-\beta}}{Z}E(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)

\displaystyle=|b|^{2}.

Note that, in particular, the measurement
\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}
on the bath qubit suffices to achieve this value.

For completeness, let us now consider a mixed qubit as the initial QB of the form
\rho_{s}=p|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+(1-p)|\psi^{\perp}\rangle\langle\psi^{\perp}|
. Now, under the action of the isometric extension
\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda_{\beta}}
of the thermal operation
\Lambda_{\beta}
gives the final state, dephased in the computational basis for the bath and the reference qubit jointly, where

\sigma_{sbR}=\frac{1}{Z}\rho^{\xi}_{s}\otimes|00\rangle\langle 00|_{bR}+\frac{e^{-\beta}}{Z}\rho^{\psi}_{s}\otimes|11\rangle\langle 11|_{bR},

and
\rho^{x}=p|x\rangle\langle x|+(1-p)|x^{\perp}\rangle\langle x^{\perp}|
with
x\in\{\xi,\psi\}
. Moreover, the resultant QB state is

\sigma_{s}=\Lambda_{\beta}(\rho_{s})=\frac{1}{Z}\rho^{\xi}_{s}+\frac{e^{-\beta}}{Z}\rho^{\psi}_{s}

.

Therefore, by performing the computational basis measurement
M_{0,1}^{b}=\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}
on the bath qubit, the restored charge on the QB becomes

\displaystyle W_{weak}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta},M_{0,1}^{b})

\displaystyle=E(\sigma_{s})-\frac{1}{\beta}(\frac{1}{Z}S(\rho^{\xi})+\frac{e^{-\beta}}{Z}S(\rho^{\psi}))

\displaystyle=E(\sigma_{s})-\frac{1}{\beta}S(\rho^{\xi})

\displaystyle=E(\sigma_{s})-\frac{1}{\beta}E_{f}(\sigma_{s|R^{\prime}}),

(30)

where,
R^{\prime}
is the purifying reference for the state
\sigma_{sbR}
. Also note that the second equality follows from the fact that
S(\rho^{\xi})=S(\rho^{\psi})=-p\log_{2}p-(1-p)\log_{2}(1-p)
.

Now, with the help of Eq. (
22
) we can conclude that the quantity in Eq. (
.6.2
) is in fact the upper bound of the amount of charge retrieval under strong assistance. Therefore, in this case also

W_{strong}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta})=W_{weak}(\rho_{s},\Lambda_{\beta}),

and the best possible measurement is the computational basis measurement for the bath qubit. Moreover, under strong assistance, any measurement on the reference system
R
is indeed redundant.

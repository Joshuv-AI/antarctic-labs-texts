Expression of mTOR, CD163, α-SMA, FOXp3 as survival predictors and its significance in patients with oral squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Suganya Ramalingam, Sivaramakrishnan Shantha, Chakravarthy Purushothaman Srinivasan, Nagarajan Priyathersini, Susruthan Muralitharan, Uma Sudhakar, Harikrishnan Thamizhchelvan, Venkatachalam Deepa Parvathi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOral cancer; categorised under head and neck cancers (HNC) predominantly originates from squamous cells and is referred as oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). Various factors (internal and external) causes OSCC. PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathways are known to be primarily mutated in HNC. mTOR remains as a key regulator for various physiological and developmental processes in normal and cancer cells. Cancer cells are surrounded by tumor microenvironment, majorly composed of immune cells. Cancer associated fibroblasts, macrophages and regulatory T cells controlled by mTOR, plays an important role in the progression of cancer.MethodsTwo hundred sixty retrospective patient samples were collected along with their demographical and clinic-pathological data. Here, we have analysed expression of mTOR, α-SMA, CD163 and FOXp3 using immunohistochemistry and their survival outcomes were calculated using Kaplan-Meier statistical method.ResultsOverexpression of CD163 and α-SMA was detected in samples of patients compared to mTOR and FOXp3. Their expression was compared with clinico-oncological parameters. We also observed two and three combinations of markers and its association with the prognosis of the cancer. The results suggest, higher the expression of all the four markers in combination correlated to poor prognosis of patients and vice-versa.ConclusionThe study reveals that over expression of CD163 and α-SMA is strongly associated with disease outcome. The combinations of all the four marker expression profile will be emerging strategy towards prognosis and also to determine survival outcomes in patients. This is a pioneering observation of these combinations of markers in OSCC despite certain limitations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-024-05245-y.Keywords: Immunohistochemical Markers, Kaplan-Meier, Prognosis, Head and Neck cancer, Retrospective

Introduction

Oral cancer (OC) is grouped under the category of head and neck cancers (HNC). OCs originating from squamous cells contributes to 90% of diagnosed cases and is generally referred to as squamous cell carcinoma [1]. HNC predominantly occurs in the oral cavity and oropharynx region. The global ranking of cancer in the oral cavity is ranked 16th position based on incidence and 15th position based on mortality rate [2]. Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) classified under HNC; is a type of malignant tumor that primarily targets the epithelium. It’s cellular differentiation takes place in the squamous cells of oral cavity. It’s characteristics are red or white coloured lesions with coarse surface and visible borders. At the initial stage, these lesions appear to be painless which then progresses through several processes like nodularity and attachment with the tissues [3–5]. Although smoking and tobacco chewing are the major causes of OC; in the recent years lifestyle modifications and environmental impact have also contributed to the development of oral cancer. It is reported that Human Papilloma Virus (HPV) is significantly associated with head and neck squamous cell carcinoma [6]. Apart from these external factors, there are certain genes that have prominent roles in the progression of oral cancer. These genes can be categorised into two groups (i) dominant genetic alteration that arise in proto-oncogenes and oncogenes leading to gain of function. These genes are typically regulatory genes which control the normal cellular growth process. A change in its normal activity like overexpression or abnormal activation results in tumor initiation (ii) genetic alterations (recessive) that influence the growth inhibitory pathways resulting in functional loss. These genes often function as controllers and negative regulators. Activation of oncogenes alone is not responsible for tumor formation but inactivation of proto-onco genes is also necessary for the expression of neoplasm [7, 8].

mTOR known as the mammalian/mechanistic target of rapamycin comes under serine/threonine kinase of the PI3K-related kinase family. This serves as the centre of regulation for various process of development namely cellular growth & proliferation, survival, cellular differentiation and motility. It also regulates variety of molecular processes like protein translation of ribosome, cap dependent translation and in synthesis of protein [9]. Several research studies conducted in the past have stated that abnormalities in the mTOR signalling pathway results in resistance to anticancer drugs, tumorigenesis, metastatic progression of tumour and tumour angiogenesis. Currently, there are drugs that inhibit mTOR and are used in the treatment of different cancers [10]. Although, it has a prominent role in tumour growth and survival; significance of mTOR in the survival of patients with squamous cell carcinoma remains unclear. In earlier studies, researchers have analysed the prognostic importance of phosphorylated mTOR (p-mTOR) along with other proteins in oesophageal and OSCC. The results have demonstrated that mTOR remains as self-sufficient protein and contributes to poor prognosis. The expression of p-mTOR and its downregulating pathway proteins have the ability to be a potential prognostic biomarker; it also has capacity to be strong therapeutic target [11–15]. However; although the alterations and overexpression status have been emphasised, the prognostic values have to be explored in detail irrespective of its site of incidence (in squamous cell carcinomas).

Although many biomarkers have been identified over the past decades that helps in the diagnosis and treatment of OSCC; it has now been understood that cancer cells are a complex mix of distinct cell types that interrelate with each other resulting in the progression and development of cancer. This motivated researchers to explore tumor microenvironment (TME) towards therapy [16]. Cancer associated fibroblasts (CAFs) have a hand in majority of the mechanisms shown by the tumor cells of OSCC, it also has an unintended role in the progression of OSCC by immune evasion mechanism [17]. There are many cells present in TME, of which CAFs; a kind of activated fibroblasts and one of the dominant stromal cells accounts for 80% of the tumor [18]. These CAFs are usually identified as α-Smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) in tissue samples, which are also expressed by pericytes [19]. The α-SMA expression in TME have been studied and results have reported that higher expression and density is correlated with tumor stage, metastasis, invasion and their effect on type of treatment [20]. There are many research studies and systematic meta-analysis, which have individually assessed only α-SMA expression to predict survival. The results have proved that higher expression indicates poor survival in OSCC patients [21–24]. Although it has been concluded that it could be a predictive and potential biomarker, its role along with combination of expression of other TME cells has been investigated here.

Tumor associated macrophages (TAMs), one of the major constituents of the cell has been studied and its role in the initiation of tumor, angiogenesis, metastasis, anticancer drug resistance and in the suppression of immune response towards cancer has been identified. TAMs consist of two types of macrophages M1, M2. M2 subtype is represented as CD163 in immunohistochemical analysis [25]. The CD163 expression within the tumor was found to be higher in patients affected with OSCC and is related to the size of the tumor, involvement of node and TNM stages [26]. Several systematic reviews and meta-analyses describing the expression of TAMs along with other proteins in OSCC patients have been reported. It indicates that higher number of CD163 TAMs was related to poor overall survival. It could also be used as a potential prognosticator [27–30]. The expression of CD163 with inflammatory markers investigated in pre-operative stage for its prognostic implications have showed inverse correlation with the survival [31]. However, these results suggested that there is an inverse relation with CD163 expression and survival. Standardised research studies should be conducted to conclude its role as a dominant marker to analyse the prognosis of patients with OSCC. Here we analyse the expression of CD163 along with other cells of TME to determine the survival rate of the patients.

FOXp3 (Forkhead Box) is one of the proteins represented by certain cell types present in the TME. It acts as a transcription factor that controls regulatory T cell development and maturation. It is elevated in case of tumors but because of the progressive and immune suppressive nature of the tumor, its function is not clear [32]. A study concluded that expression of FOXp3 by regulatory T cells in patients with lip squamous cell carcinoma is associated with the clinicopathological parameters. They have elevated levels in early stages compared to advanced stages [33]. A study also revealed that it helps in cancer progression in patients with OSCC by several immune suppression and self-tolerance [34]. A study of immune cell marker investigation, revealed that FOXp3 expression increased up to 58% in patients than in normal individuals [35]. A research study indicated FOXp3 & CTLA-4 and its relationship with the survival of the patient. The results revealed that FOXp3 could serve as independent prognosticating factor and its association with stages of tumor has remarkably improved prognosis [36, 37]. There have not been sufficient studies to prove that it could be a significant immune marker to be used for the diagnostic purpose and also for therapeutics. Research conducted ‘hitherto’ have studied about either two or three of the chosen TME cell markers independently or in dual combinations towards understanding their prognostic value in patients with OSCC. However, this research study has analysed the combination of all the four markers and its prognostic value in patients with OSCC. This has not been recorded in any study thus far.

It is a well-known fact that TME has a major role that it co-evolves and exists along with cancer cells (Fig. 1) [38]. mTOR has been a predominant regulator of various TME cells (Fig. 2). This work is a pioneering study where mTOR along with the combination of immune cell markers such as, CD163, FOXp3 and α-SMA have been analysed using immunohistochemical methods in 260 samples of OSCC.

Materials and methods

Study design and samples

Sample size

Population size (for finite population correction factor or fpc) (N): 1,000,000.

Hypothesized % frequency of outcome factor in the population (p): 20%+/−5.

Confidence limits as % of 100(absolute +/- %)(d): 5%.

Design effect (for cluster surveys-DEFF): 1.

Sample Size(n) for Various Confidence Levels- Confidence Level (%) : 95%, Sample Size: 246.

Two hundred sixty patients with OSCC (with histopathological examinations) in the period January 2016 to December 2019 were included in this study design. Their three-year survival status was noted to conduct survival analysis. For patients diagnosed in 2016; survival period until 2019, for those diagnosed in 2017; survival till 2020, for those diagnosed in 2018; survival till 2021 and for those diagnosed in 2019; survival period till 2022 was accounted. International classification of diseases (ICD) 10th revision with reference codes of C01-C06 [39] were applied for patient data who were diagnosed with primary oral cancer at Sri Ramachandra Medical Centre (Chennai, India). The retrospective study was conducted according to the principles stated in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent versions and was approved by the Institutional Ethical committee (IEC NI/21/APR/78/53) of Sri Ramachandra Institute of Higher Education & Research. The demographical and clinicopathological characters were taken from the patient’s histopathological reports retrospectively which included year of diagnosis, age, sex and subsites of the tumor. The primary tumor sites were buccal mucosa, anterior part of the tongue, floor of the mouth, palate and lip. Patients with cancers on other sites of head and neck cancer, posterior part of the tongue and patients with HPV positivity were excluded based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria for our study. As stated in the International Union Against Cancer (UICC), TNM classification of T1, T2, T3, T4a were included and nodal involvement of N0, N1, N2, N3 Stage I, Stage II, Stage III, Stage IV were also included. WHO classification of tumors (Pathology and Genetics) of Head and neck tumors, three grades of tumor differentiation namely poorly differentiated, moderately differentiated and well differentiated were included. Patients who received surgery as primary therapy were included. Confirmation of diagnosis was determined with pre-surgical reports. The survival status was calculated from the time of diagnosis till death irrespective of all causes.

Immunohistochemical analysis (IHC)

IHC was performed by taking 4-µm serial sections of paraffin-embedded samples de-paraffinised in xylene and alcohol, rinsed using distilled water, soaked in Tris-EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) and kept at 100℃ for antigen retrieval. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked by incubating the sections with peroxidase blocker from The Mouse/Rabbit PolyDetector Plus DAB HRP Brown Detection System (BIOSB, BSB 0257, Batch no.0257FMD12) for 5 min. It was followed by incubating the sections with primary monoclonal antibodies targeting FoxP3 + T-cells (prediluted; clone 236 A/E7; Lot: 02122 A; BIOCARE, USA), CD163 + Macrophages (prediluted; clone EP324; Lot:6305FME18; BIOSB, USA), mTOR of tumor cells (prediluted; clone 59.Ser 2448; Lot: MC0509R0622; MEDAYSIS, USA), α-SMA of Cancer associated fibroblasts (prediluted; clone 1A4; Lot:1105092D-3 F; ZETA Corporation, USA) at room temperature overnight. Secondary antibodies and Immunolabel were used as per the instruction given in the DAB System (Mouse/Rabbit PolyDetector Plus DAB HRP Brown Detector System, BIOSB, USA). The sections were counterstained with hematoxylin and then dehydrated, cleared and mounted (DPX Mountant, Sisco Research Laboratories Pvt. Ltd., India). Positive controls (Table 1) were processed in the same manner.

Positive controls used for IHC Analysis

The percentage of expression & intensity of CD163, FoxP3, mTOR and α-SMA were analysed in oral squamous cell carcinoma tissues in three independent high-power microscopic fields for immunohistochemistry by two pathologists independently (Supplementary Figs. 1–4). The intensity was denoted as 1+, 2+, and 3 + respectively based on mild, moderate and severe expression. The H score was calculated on the intensity of the staining. H score was calculated based on the value using the formula (3+)x3+(2+)x2+(1+)x1 [40, 41].

Statistical analysis

The sample data were recorded and categorised using Microsoft excel datasheet and analysed using The R statistical software version 4.2.2 (R Core Team (2022) R: A language and environment for statistical computing (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria.URL https://www.R-project.org/).The demographic and clinico-pathological parameters were analysed as percentages and frequencies for the categorical distribution and also cross tabulated to achieve descriptive statistics. The cut-off of for each marker into high and low values was based on ROC curves generated with negative outcome being the state variable. The ‘pROC’ package in R statistical software [42] was used for this purpose and the cut-off was selected using the Youden Index. For comparing the distribution of high and low value of markers with various demographic and clinical variables Chi-square test was used. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. For computing the survival analysis ‘survival’ package for R was used (Therneau T (2022). A Package for Survival Analysis in R. R package version 3.4-0, <https://CRAN.Rproject.org/package=survival>.)

Results

Patient, tumor and pathological factors

260 archived samples were collected and analysed; of which 193(74.43%) were males and 67 (25.56%) were females. They were categorised based on their mean age of 55, where the age ranges from 24 to 85. 54(20.85%), 85(33.82%), 44(16.99%), 76(29.34%) patients were diagnosed with OSCC in the years 2016, 2017,2018 and 2019 respectively. The samples were analysed based on tumor site in which tongue 97 (37.3%) and buccal mucosa 91(35.0%) were predominantly affected. Based on histological analysis and grading, 45% (117 patients) of the samples were poorly differentiated, 37.31% (97 samples) was well differentiated and 17.69% (46 samples) were moderately differentiated. Among the pathological T characteristics, most of them were T2 (109-41.92%) followed by T3, T4a and T1. In case of N distributions most patients were pN0 (70%). The patient’s clinical staging was also included in the clinico-pathological characteristics where, among 260 samples, 97 patients (37.31%) were presented with clinical stage IV. In the category of treatment, majority of the patients 121 (46.54%) received surgery and chemotherapy (Fig. 3). The detailed list of the patients with clinico-pathological features are summarised in (Supplementary Table: S1).

Tissue markers in OSCC specimens and relationship between markers and clinicopathological conditions

Out of 260 samples included in the survival analysis; 141 patients died, 110 patients were alive and data of 9 patients were unavailable. The expression of tissue markers (CD163, α-SMA, FOXp3, and mTOR) was reported as low and high based on the Receiver Operating Characteristics curve (ROC) analysis. H values were different with abnormal intervals for each marker. Therefore, ROC analysis was used to determine an optimal cut-off value. ROC value of CD163 was found to be ( > = 187.5), α-SMA showed an optimal value of ( > = 197.5), cut-off value of mTOR was ( > = 167.5) and cut-off value of FOXp3 was ( > = 37.5). Therefore, patients with H value below the cut off was considered as lower expression and above the cut off were considered as higher expression. Details are summarised in (Supplementary Table S2). CD163 and α-SMA expression were the highest among the samples noted. It was then compared with the features included in the study and determined (Fig. 4). It showed p-value of < 0.005 which is considered to be statistically significant.

Survival analysis in patients with OSCC

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to analyse the patient’s overall survival probability with the expression of markers.

Single marker analysis

The individual marker survival analysis in patients with OSCC revealed that the higher the expression of tissue markers (CD163, FOXp3, mTOR, α-SMA) in the patients, lower the survival probability. CD163(OS, median-12 months) α-SMA (OS, median- 10 months) expression had lower survival than the other markers (Fig. 5). Lower marker expression has higher survival probability with P-Value of < 0.001 which is considered to be statistically significant.

Discussion

mTOR is one of the predominantly activated pathways in cancer. Overexpression of the protein mTOR results in upregulated activation of oncogene and downregulation of controller genes. Their complete functional properties associated with OSCC is still under research. mTOR protein associated with AKT/PI3K pathway is related to development and progression of OSCC [43]. However, its prognostic value still remains unclear particularly in case of OSCC. Further insights and clarity is required to use it as a prognosticator. Many studies on various types of head and neck cancers and other cancers have identified the importance of p-mTOR as a potential biomarker. Results of this study showed a significant increase in the expression of mTOR yet lesser than CD163 or α-SMA in patients with OSCC. This expression could be related with clinicopathological parameters and useful in predicting the overall survival. A study was conducted in 2010 and 2017, where they observed analysis of p-mTOR and mTOR expression and its relationship with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and tongue squamous cell carcinoma (TSCC) respectively. The results suggested that p-mTOR and mTOR was associated with poor prognosis of patients and it could also be used as a therapeutic target [44, 45]. Another research in 2015 studied the significance of prognosis of mTOR and PTEN in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma as a retrospective study and observed that mTOR showed elevated expression compared to PTEN in patients. This led to the conclusion that mTOR acts independently as a prognostic marker by means of multivariate analysis [11]. A research work done in 2017 analysed the correlation between the prognostic significance of mTOR and periostin in locally advanced stage of ESCC and their results determined similar expressions of both mTOR and periostin which are associated with clinicopathological parameters and represented as independent factors in deciding the overall and disease-free survival of patients [12]. A recent research in 2023 identifies the correlation between the p-mTOR expression with the survival of the patients with HPV negative head and neck cancers where it was concluded that higher expression of p-mTOR predicted poor outcome of overall survival in patients [46]. All these studies revealed the expression of mTOR individually or along with other proteins in HNC or other cancer types. This suggests that mTOR or p-mTOR is associated with survival outcome and it could be a potential biomarker. All these results are in concordance with results of the present study.

CAFs have also been analysed along with other markers in various cancer types in which their expression is elevated and could also act as a potential biomarker. The current study revealed higher expression of α-SMA along with CD163 compared to other two markers related to overall outcome of the patients with poor prognosis. A 2023 study on the prognostic significance of cancer associated fibroblast derived exosomal protein in patients with ESCC indicated higher expression of α-SMA and poor survival [47]. In 2017, a study on nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPCs) analysed expression of α-SMA on different tissues of NPCs determined that overexpression of α-SMA was intensely associated with clinical outcomes of the patients [48]. A study on OSCC in 2020 analysed the expression and significance of CD34 and α-SMA. The results concluded that α-SMA was over expressed and CD34 was comparatively low emphasising the use of α-SMA to predict the survival status in OSCC patients [23]. In 2014, a research study performed prognostic significance of CAFs in ESCC where they concluded that α-SMA expression was higher compared to other CAFs markers and associated to large tumor size, TNM staging, poor survival rate and also highlighted on its relationship with increased tumor associated macrophages (TAMs) [49]. In the same year, another study revealed that, in patients affected with TSCC, α-SMA is strongly associated with progression, metastatic rate and survival prognosis of the patients. It also has high impact on EMT & lymphogenesis [50]. All the studies revealed that α-SMA independently could act as predictive biomarker. The results of this study are in concordance with the research conducted ‘hitherto’ revealing that higher expression of markers leads to the poor overall survival of the patients.

CD163 expression has been analysed in many cancers. Many two marker combination studies in HNCs and other cancers including α-SMA/CD163 or CD163/FOXp3 have been recorded. Here, we observed overexpression of CD163 with α-SMA, associated with the clinical data and survival outcome of the patients. Increased expression of CD163 revealed poor prognosis. Two studies in the year 2018 analysed the significance of α-SMA and CD163 in NPC and OSCC. Both the markers are included in the current study along with FOXp3 and mTOR. The results of the study showed significant association between the expression of α-SMA and CD163 which could serve as independent prognosticators for overall survival and also help patients for counselling and treatment [51, 52]. In patients affected with NPC, a research observed and analysed the prognostic signature of the TAM markers and T regs. They included patients (positive and negative) for Epstein Bar Virus (EBV) through which they established higher expression of CD163 & FOXp3 combinations in EBV negative patients, suggesting that overall FOXp3 expression had better overall survival [53]. A study in OSCC patients determined the prognostic nature of CD163, IL10, IFN-γ. The results demonstrated higher expression of CD163 was associated with overall poor survival [54]. Also, in OSCC they observed the expression of TAMs like CD68 and CD163 with cancer stem cells (CSCs) markers. Their results showed overexpression of CD163 in patients was associated poor overall survival [55]. Other findings in OSCC, observed expression of TAMs subtypes and also observed to have high expression of CD163 than CD68 in macrophages and cancer cells was associated with poor survival [56]. These findings strongly suggested that CD163 could be used as a biomarker. This study result correlated with the earlier studies where CD163 is expressed and results in least survival of the patients.

Tumor infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) specifically Tregs is expressed in most cancers and their expression is associated with prognosis. In our analysis, expression of FOXp3 was comparatively lower than other markers. A clinical study in 2017 emphasised the significance of prognosis based on the expression of FOXp3 in HNSCC. It suggested that high expression of FOXp3 is associated with better survival in patients [37]. In 2020, a study on establishing prognostic factor of PDL1 and FOXp3 along with other TILs has been recorded. The results concluded, higher expression of FOXp3 and CD8 associated with better survival of HPC patients [57]. A research study in 2022 focused on immunohistochemical analysis of CD8, FOxp3, CD68 and prediction of prognosis in patients affected with HNC. The results highlighted that different density and distribution of these markers could be a self-sufficient prognosticator beyond demographical characteristics [58]. All these studies concluded FOXp3 could be an independent biomarker. The current research is concordance with previous studies. It suggested that survival rate of patients is lower in low FOXp3 expression and vice-versa.

Research studies(retrospective) conducted have reported higher expression of FOXp3 & COX-2 in Larynx SCC and Oro-hypopharynx SCC(OHSCC). Higher expression of FOXp3 is related with poor survival in OHSCC not in LSCC. Combination of FOXp3 and COX2 is associated with prognostic accuracy only in OHSCC [59]. Higher expression of FoXp3 has also been recorded in nasopharyngeal carcinoma [60]. Systematic and Meta-analysis have established poor overall survival with higher expression of p-mTOR, mTOR& periostin in HNC and esophageal SCC. Retrospective research studies and meta-analysis have also reported association of CD68+ & CD163 in HNSCC, CD11c & CD163 in tongue squamous cell carcinoma and SOX2 in OSCC. Other markers studied include PDL-1, PCP4/PEP19 and HER2 [61–64]. It is important to note that all these studies are specific to single/dual markers and are associated over a spectrum of HNC.

This study summarizes that among the markers, α-SMA and CD163 are expressed at a higher rate followed by mTOR and FOXp3. Also, individual high and low expression of markers observed were related to low and high survival respectively. Higher α-SMA expression tend to have lower survival in all the possible combinations. Our findings suggested that combinations of these markers help in identifying the survival rate of the patients rather than analysing an individual marker towards predicting the survival. Although independent marker could be a predictor, but combination of markers will help in better survival outcome and also relate significantly to clinicopathological parameters. These markers are expressed in tumor microenvironment which is regulated by mTOR. mTOR pathway is altered in cancer cells which acts as a regulatory center for other TME immune cells for its upregulation or downregulation. Although there are several research studies that included two or three marker combinations, all the four markers chosen in combination to analyze prognosis of patients with OSCC have not been studied. Thus, our finding is a pioneer in identifying the prognostic rate of patients affected with OSCC. They therefore can act as prospective combination of biomarkers. This combination analysis is the first of its kind globally in OSCC research. However, there are certain limitations like analyzing disease free survival of the patients, sample size, including patients with factors like smoking and HPV positive patients and depth of invasion. Future research aspects can propose to minimize limitations and implement it towards clinical diagnostics and thus would help in translating the use of these markers as predictive and prognostic factors.

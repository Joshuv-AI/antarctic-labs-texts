Measurement protocol for detecting correlated topological insulators in synthetic quantum systems
Yixin Ma, Chao Xu, Shenghan Jiang

Abstract
Two-dimensional topological insulators, characterized by symmetry-protected anomalous boundary modes, have been generalized to the strongly correlated regime for both bosonic and fermionic systems. As correlated topological insulators (TI) approach experimental realization in quantum simulators, conventional probes, such as transport measurements, are not easily applicable to these synthetic platforms. In this study, we focus on two examples of correlated TI: a bosonic TI protected by ℤ 2 × U ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathbb{Z}_{2}\times U(1) symmetry and the fermionic quantum spin Hall insulator protected by time-reversal symmetry. We propose a unified, readily implementable protocol based on measuring the disorder parameter ⟨ exp ⁡ ( i ⁡ θ ​ Q ^ A ) ⟩ \langle\exp(\operatorname{i}\theta\hat{Q}_{A})\rangle for a large subregion A A , with Q ^ A \hat{Q}_{A} the total charge operator within A A . Our key finding is that this quantity exhibits non-analytical dependence on θ \theta for correlated TI, a signature robust against decoherence. We establish this diagnostic through both numerical simulations and analytical derivations. This protocol is well-suited for implementation on near-term quantum simulation platforms, providing a direct route to experimentally confirm correlated TI.

Disorder parameters from RDM.–

The analytical derivation for the disorder parameter is most naturally formulated using RDM.
Our key observation is that as for a gapped ground state, the entanglement of region
A
with the rest of the system is concentrated on the 1D boundary
\partial A
, this allows representing the RDM by
a one-dimensional mixed state

\rho_{\partial A}
defined on
\partial A
.

\langle U_{A}(\theta)\rangle
then reduces to evaluating a specific expectation value in this 1D system.
Crucially, symmetry action on
\rho_{\partial A}
is anomalous, which results in the non-smooth behavior in
f(\theta)
.

We begin with the RDM of region
A

\displaystyle\rho_{A}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\lvert\psi\rangle\langle\psi\rvert=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\chi_{A}}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha}}|\phi_{A}^{\alpha}\rangle\langle\phi_{A}^{\alpha}|.

(3)

where
\{\lvert\phi^{\alpha}\rangle\}
are orthonormal Schmidt states defined in Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}_{A}
, and
\{\lambda_{\alpha}\}
constitute the entanglement spectra.
The Schmidt indices
\{\alpha\}
define a
\chi_{A}
-dimensional
entanglement Hilbert space
, denoted as
\mathbb{H}_{\partial A}
, isomorphic to
\mathrm{span}\{\lvert\phi^{\alpha}_{A}\rangle|\alpha=1,\dots\chi_{A}\}
.
For the case where
\lvert\psi\rangle
is represented by a projected entangled pair state (PEPS),
\mathbb{H}_{\partial A}
is identified as virtual legs at the boundary.
More generally, for a non-chiral gapped phase, we assume that
\mathbb{H}_{\partial A}
can be well approximated by the Hilbert space of a 1D chain at
\partial A
:
\mathbb{H}_{\partial A}=\otimes_{j\in\partial A}\mathbb{H}_{j}
.

\displaystyle\langle U_{A}(\theta)\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\partial A}W_{\partial A}(\theta)]

The anomalous symmetry on RDM.–

Physically,
W_{\partial A}(g)
are identified as the anomalous boundary symmetry, encoding the bulk topological data.
While
W_{\partial A}
admits a natural matrix product unitary operator (MPUO) representation, reflecting its non-onsite nature
11
;
61

W_{\partial A}(g)=\prod_{j}W_{j}(g)
.
This is achieved in two key steps
59
;
28
;
65

1.

Enlarging the entanglement space to accommodate an extended symmetry group with on-site representation;

2.

Projecting back to the original entanglement space, and recovering the non-onsite MPUO form.

As the on-site formulation is more tractable for the analytical derivation, we will use it henceforth.

To illustrate the above procedure, we examine the

Experimental related issues.–

The scaled disorder parameter
f(\theta)
can be measured by counting
U(1)
charges
50
inside region
A
for each sample and post-processing the statistical data.
Specifically, after performing
N_{M}
times projective measurements of the many-body state, we record the frequency distribution
q(n)
of observing
n
charges in
A
.
The disorder parameter is then estimated as

\langle U_{A}(\theta)\rangle\approx\frac{1}{N_{M}}\sum_{n}q(n)\exp(\operatorname{i}\theta n)\,.

(15)

The protocol remains effective under noise, provided that the dominant noises preserve
strong

U(1)
symmetry.
To see this, we model noise as a finite depth local unitary
V
coupling the system to a environment state
\lvert 0\rangle_{\rm{E}}
:
\lvert\tilde{\psi}\rangle=V(\lvert\psi\rangle_{\rm{S}}\otimes\lvert 0\rangle_{\rm{E}})
.
Hence,
\lvert\tilde{\psi}\rangle
belongs to the same phase as
\lvert\psi\rangle_{\rm{S}}
.
If
V
commutes with
U(1)
symmetry of the system – forbidding charge exchange between the system and the environment – then the measured charge statistics in
A
remain uncontaminated, and our diagnosis is still valid (see supplemental section V for more details).

The projection onto the
n
-particle subspace
\hat{P}_{n}=\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert
suffers shot noise.
After
N_{M}
measurements, the deviation of the empirical average
\mathbb{E}_{N_{M}}(\hat{P}_{n})
from the true probability
p_{n}
is bounded by Chebyshev’s inequality:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}[|\mathbb{E}_{N_{M}}(\hat{P}_{n})-p_{n}|\geq\epsilon]\leq\frac{1}{4N_{M}\epsilon^{2}}\,.

(16)

Accurate resolving the non-analytic feature in
f(\theta)
near
\theta=\pi
requires controlling the statistical error
\epsilon\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\partial A\right\rvert f(\pi)}
.
Setting
\epsilon=\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\partial A\right\rvert f(\pi)}
, the number of measurements needed to achieve a failure probability below

N_{M}\gtrsim\frac{1}{4\eta^{2}\delta}e^{2f(\pi)\left\lvert\partial A\right\rvert}
.
For example, in the bosonic correlated TI model investigate here, a

subregion with
\eta=\delta=0.1

.
In a quantum processor with imaging time
\sim 10^{3}\mu

, this corresponds to a few minutes of data acquisition.

Now, two key consideration arise:

1.

The measurement cost to resolve

2.

The sub-leading corrections in Eq. (
1

These constraints restrict the protocol to intermediate region sizes – a regime ideally matched to the capabilities of current noise intermediate-Scale quantum platforms

Outlook.–

Our work reveals a fundamental duality between 2D correlated TI and 1D mixed state topological phases
35
;
66
realized by RDM at the entanglement boundary.
While we have focused on the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Ising symmetric bosonic TI and the quantum spin Hall insulator, we anticipate this framework will lead to new diagnostic tools for a broader classes of correlated TI beyond the specific cases studied here, which we leave for future work.

A natural extension of this work involves generalizing this pure-mixed duality to broader classes of topological phases, such as symmetry-enriched topological (SET) phases.
In such systems, the one-form symmetry of topological order manifests as the strong symmetry in the 1D mixed state, leading to interplay between strong and weak symmetries, potentially enabling the extraction of novel non-local order parameters of SET phases.

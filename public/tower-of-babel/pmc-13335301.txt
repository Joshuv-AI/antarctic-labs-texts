miR-1307-5p mediates endothelial dysfunction and inflammation induced by acute coronary syndrome by targeting PIM3
Authors: ChangMing Jin, BaoBin Sun, XiaoHong Li, Hao Chen, Tao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to investigate the clinical value of miR-1307-5p in acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patients and its role in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs).MethodsEnrollment consisted of 124 ACS cases and 124 controls. Reverse transcription quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was used to measure the miR-1307-5p and proviral integration site for Moloney murine leukemia virus 3 (PIM3) levels in serum samples, and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was utilized to assess the diagnostic utility of miR-1307-5p. ACS cell model was established using hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R). enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was employed to detect the von Willebrand factor (vWF), heart-type fatty acid-binding protein (H-FABP), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-6, and IL-8 in serum and HUVECs. Apoptosis was analyzed by flow cytometry, while cell viability was evaluated through Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8) assay. The interaction between miR-1307-5p and PIM3 was validated via dual-luciferase reporter (DLR) and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) experiments.ResultsmiR-1307-5p is significantly downregulated in ACS patients’ serum and effectively distinguishes controls from ACS patients. It also shows negative correlations with vWF, H-FABP, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-8. miR-1307-5p directly targets PIM3. Upregulating miR-1307-5p enhances HUVEC viability and suppresses apoptosis. Furthermore, this intervention alleviates endothelial injury and reduces inflammation. Notably, PIM3 overexpression reversed these protective effects of miR-1307-5p.ConclusionReduced serum miR-1307-5p expression holds promise as a potential diagnostic biomarker for ACS. Upregulation of miR-1307-5p alleviates endothelial dysfunction and inflammation by targeting PIM3, suggesting that the miR-1307-5p/PIM3 axis represents a potential therapeutic target for ACS.Keywords: ACS, miR-1307-5p, PIM3, Diagnosis, Inflammation

Introduction

Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) is a clinical syndrome caused by thrombosis triggered by coronary artery plaque rupture, leading to severe narrowing or occlusion of the coronary artery [1–3]. ACS is classified into unstable angina (UA) and acute myocardial infarction (AMI), based on myocardial cell necrosis [4]. Statistically, ACS is among the leading causes of death and disability related to cardiovascular disease worldwide [5]. Despite significant advances in reperfusion and pharmacotherapy, myocardial injury, microvascular dysfunction, and long-term heart failure (HF) risk remain prevalent after ACS, posing major clinical challenges [6–8]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to better understand the pathogenesis of ACS and to identify novel therapeutic targets.

In recent years, microRNAs (miRNAs) have gained significant attention for their roles in cardiac injury and cardiovascular diseases [9, 10]. For example, miR-522-3p restricts vascular growth and impairs cardiac regeneration by targeting FOXP1 [11]. Among these, miR-1307-5p, located on chromosome 10, has been demonstrated to exhibit dysregulated expression in multiple cardiovascular pathological states. A study employing small RNA sequencing to analyze plasma miRNAs in ACS patients revealed abnormal expression of miR-1307-5p [12]. Furthermore, miR-1307-5p is dysregulated in HF patients [13]and may also be associated with vascular endothelial dysfunction [14]. Therefore, this study investigates the relationship between ACS and miR-1307-5p, aiming to elucidate its underlying mechanisms.

Using bioinformatics tools, we identified possible miR-1307-5p binding sites within the mRNA sequence of the proto-oncogene serine/threonine kinase PIM3 (proviral integration site for Moloney murine leukemia virus 3). The PIM kinase family is widely expressed in cardiovascular tissues and serves as a risk factor for multiple cardiovascular diseases [15]. Nevertheless, the role of PIM3 in ACS remains poorly understood.

Based on this background, we hypothesize that miR-1307-5p may target the PIM3 gene to mediate the onset and progression of ACS. This study aims to evaluate the diagnostic value of miR-1307-5p in ACS and to preliminarily explore the mechanism of action of the miR-1307-5p/PIM3 axis in endothelial cell protection, with the goal of providing new insights for targeted therapy of ACS.

Materials and methods

Subjects and serum collection

This study included 124 ACS patients treated at Beijing Mentougou District Hospital from July 2021 to July 2023 and 124 controls matched for the same period. Based on coronary angiography results, participants were enrolled if they had confirmed significant coronary artery stenosis (≥ 50%) and had experienced their first episode of chest discomfort within the recent period (within 2 months). Patients with autoimmune diseases, malignant tumors, or pregnancy were excluded. Five milliliters of venous blood were collected from participants. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Mentougou District Hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was collected from all participants.

Cell culture and transfection

Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were obtained from SUNNCELL and cultured in endothelial medium (SNLM-503, SUNNCELL, Wuhan) containing 5% fetal bovine serum, 1% endothelial cell growth supplement, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. To establish an in vitro ACS model, HUVECs underwent hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) induction: cells were cultured in hypoxic conditions (95% N₂–5% CO₂) in serum-free Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium for 24 h. Reoxygenation was performed for 6 h in a 95% air–5% CO₂ environment. To regulate miR-1307-5p and PIM3 expression in HUVECs, negative control (NC) mimics, miR-1307-5p mimics, and PIM3 overexpression (oe PIM3), NC overexpression (oe NC) were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA).

Reverse transcription quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated from serum and cells by means of TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA). Complementary DNA was synthesized from RNA with the PrimeScript RT Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). miR-1307-5p and PIM3 levels were quantified using SYBR Green I Master Mix Reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and the CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, USA). miR-1307-5p/PIM3 levels were normalized using U6/ glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) as internal controls and calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method. The specific primer sequences are as follows: miR-1307-5p, forward: 5’-CGGGCTCGACCAGGACCTCG-3’, reverse: 5’-CAGCCACAAAAGCACAAT-3’; PIM3, forward: 5’-CTGCTCAAGGACACGGTCTACAC-3’, reverse: 5’-CCCCACACACCATATCGTAGAGA-3’; GAPDH, forward: 5’-CGGAGTCAACGGATTTGGTCGTAT-3’, reverse: 5’-AGCCTTCTCCATGGTGGTGAAGAC-3’; U6, forward: 5’-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3’, reverse: 5’-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGGT-3’.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum and cellular von Willebrand factor (vWF) levels were measured using the Human vWF ELISA Kit (EHVWF, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Heart-type fatty acid-binding protein (H-FABP) was quantified by a kit (S11181, Saitosen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan). Proinflammatory cytokines were detected using ELISA kits (Abcam, USA).

Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Cell viability of HUVECs was assessed using the CCK8 assay. HUVECs treated with H/R and transfected with NC mimic, miR-1307-5p mimic, miR-1307-5p mimic + oe NC, or miR-1307-5p mimic + oe PIM3 were cultured for an additional 48 h. HUVECs were seeded at 4 × 10³ cells/well in 96-well plates. At 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-transfection, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai) was added. Absorbance was recorded at 450 nm on a microplate reader (BioTek, USA).

Flow cytometry

Apoptosis in HUVECs was analyzed by a commercially available Annexin V-FITC apoptosis detection kit (C1062M-1, Biotest, Shanghai). HUVECs cultured for 48 h following different transfection treatments were digested with trypsin, centrifuged to collect cells, and resuspended. Subsequently, 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 10 µL propidium iodide were added and incubated for 10 min at room temperature in the dark. Analyze within 1 h using FACScan flow cytometry (Becton Dickinson) to calculate apoptosis rates for each group.

Dual-luciferase reporter (DLR) assay

Bioinformatics analysis revealed that the 3’ untranslated region (UTR) of PIM3 contains a miR-1307-5p binding site. The wild type / mutant (WT / MUT) 3’UTR fragments of PIM3 were amplified and inserted into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, USA). Recombinant vectors were co-transfected with miR-1307-5p mimics/NC mimics or miR-1307-5p inhibitors/ NC inhibitors into HUVECs by Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA). Cells were cultured for 48 h post-transfection. Fluorescence activity was measured by a DLR assay kit (Promega, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity, and the relative luciferase activity was calculated as the ratio of Firefly to Renilla.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RIP assays were performed in HUVECs through the Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (17–700, Millipore, USA). Transfected cells were lysed using RIP lysis buffer, and the resulting lysates were subjected to a 4-hour incubation with beads conjugated to either anti-Ago2 or anti-IgG antibodies. Finally, the precipitated RNA was isolated and purified, and the results were analyzed by RT-qPCR.

Data analysis

All data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and analyzed using SPSS 22.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. All experiments were repeated at least three times. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of the data. For data that followed a normal distribution, t-tests were used for two-group comparisons, and two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for multi-group comparisons. When significant interactions were present, simple effects analysis for multiple comparisons was performed using the Bonferroni correction; for one-way comparisons among multiple groups, ANOVA was conducted first, followed by post-hoc comparisons using Tukey’s HSD test. Frequencies and proportions of categorical data were analyzed using the chi-square test.

Results

Clinical baseline analysis of subjects

We first conducted a comparative analysis of clinical baseline data between enrolled ACS patients and controls. Among the enrolled ACS patients, 50 cases were UA and 74 were AMI, with a Gensini score of 61.06 ± 11.12. Furthermore, no significant differences were observed between the ACS and control groups in age, gender, hypertension, diabetes, smoking status, TC, TG, LDL, or HDL (P > 0.05, Table 1), indicating good comparability between the two groups.

Expression of miR-1307-5p in ACS patients’ serum and its diagnostic value

We observed significantly downregulated miR-1307-5p in ACS patients’ serum relative to controls (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1A), suggesting this differentially expressed miRNA may serve as markers for ACS. To further evaluate the diagnostic potential of miR-1307-5p, we constructed a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. The results demonstrated that miR-1307-5p effectively distinguished ACS patients from healthy individuals. The area under the curve (AUC) was 0.9182 (95% CI: 0.8829–0.9534), with 89.52% sensitivity and 87.90% specificity (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1B).

Correlation analysis of miR-1307-5p with endothelial injury and inflammation in ACS

Both endothelial injury markers (vWF and H-FABP) and key inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-8) were significantly augmented in ACS patients’ serum compared to controls (P < 0.0001, Table 2). Through Pearson correlation analysis, we further investigated the relationship between the abnormally downregulated miR-1307-5p and those mediators in ACS patients. It was found that the miR-1307-5p correlated negatively with vWF (r = − 0.644, P < 0.0001, Fig. 2A), H-FABP (r = − 0.577, P < 0.0001, Fig. 2B), TNF-α (r = − 0.668, P < 0.0001, Fig. 2C), IL-6 (r = − 0.595, P < 0.0001, Fig. 2D), and IL-8 (r = − 0.625, P < 0.0001, Fig. 2E). This suggests that abnormal miR-1307-5p expression may be associated with endothelial dysfunction and inflammatory dysregulation.

miR-1307-5p directly targets PIM3

Figure 3A shows the intersection of downstream target genes predicted for miR-1307-5p using three platforms (TargetScan 8.0, TarBase v9.0, and miRwalk). Among the nine overlapping genes predicted, we successfully predicted a potential binding site between the 3’UTR of PIM3 and miR-1307-5p on the TargetScan 8.0 website (Fig. 3B). To investigate their relationship, we performed a DLR assay. miR-1307-5p mimic markedly lessened the fluorescence activity of PIM3-WT, while miR-1307-5p inhibitor significantly increased PIM3-WT fluorescence activity, with no significant effect on PIM3-MUT (P < 0.001, Fig. 3C). RIP experiments demonstrated significant enrichment of miR-1307-5p and PIM3 in Anti-Ago2 (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3D), further confirming their target relationship. Furthermore, PIM3 expression was markedly enhanced in ACS serum compared to controls (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3E), and was significantly negatively correlated with miR-1307-5p in ACS patient serum (r = − 0.727, P < 0.0001, Fig. 3F). These expression changes in miR-1307-5p and PIM3 were validated in H/R-induced HUVECs (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3G-H).

The co-regulation of miR-1307-5p and PIM3 on HUVECs

To investigate whether miR-1307-5p regulates HUVECs’ function by targeting PIM3, we first examined the effects of separately overexpressing miR-1307-5p, separately overexpressing PIM3, and co-overexpressing both miR-1307-5p and PIM3 on their respective expression levels. Results showed that under H/R injury conditions, transfection with miR-1307-5p mimics significantly increased miR-1307-5p expression. While enhancing PIM3 expressions had no significant effect on miR-1307-5p expression, it partially reversed the downregulation of PIM3 induced by upregulated miR-1307-5p (P < 0.05, Fig. 4A-B). Following H/R treatment, HUVECs exhibited markedly reduced viability and significantly enhanced apoptotic capacity. PIM3 overexpression significantly attenuated the miR-1307-5p-induced increase in HUVEC viability and its anti-apoptotic effect (P < 0.01, Fig. 4C-D). Under H/R conditions, vWF and H-FABP concentrations significantly increased in HUVECs. Transfection with miR-1307-5p markedly reduced both markers, mitigating H/R-induced endothelial injury, whereas PIM3 overexpression reversed this protective effect (P < 0.01, Fig. 4E-F). Furthermore, H/R treatment enhanced the TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-8 levels. Upregulation of PIM3 partially reversed the suppression of these inflammatory mediators induced by miR-1307-5p overexpression (P < 0.05, Fig. 4G-I).

Discussion

Numerous miRNAs have been reported to be associated with ACS. For instance, miR-30c-5p correlates with ACS severity [16]. MiR-377 has been shown to alleviate ACS by improving endothelial injury and inflammatory responses [17]. Furthermore, miRNAs hold potential as diagnostic biomarkers for ACS [18–20]. For example, miR-19a has been validated as a biomarker for AMI [21]. Abnormally expressed miR-361-5p may serve as a potential biomarker for ACS diagnosis and prediction of major adverse cardiovascular events [22]. Previously, miR-1307-5p was recognized as a regulator in various cancers [23–25]. Recent studies suggest its potential association with cardiovascular abnormalities. Our study demonstrates that miR-1307-5p is significantly downregulated in serum of ACS patients and can effectively distinguish ACS patients from controls. Endothelial dysfunction and inflammatory responses are key pathological mechanisms in ACS occurrence and development, with related intervention strategies applicable to clinical ACS treatment [17, 26, 27]. Concurrently, our study revealed significant relationships linking miR-1307-5p to vWF and H-FABP, which are closely associated with ACS progression. miR-1307-5p also correlates with inflammatory cytokine levels, reflecting ACS severity [28, 29]. Thus, we hypothesize that down regulation of miR-1307-5p may promote ACS by modulating endothelial cell function and inflammation.

In order to examine the mechanism of miR-1307-5p in ACS, we conducted a mechanistic study using a H/R model of HUVECs [30]. In H/R-induced HUVECs, miR-1307-5p exhibited the same expression trend as in patient serum. miR-1307-5p overexpression significantly decreased concentrations of endothelial injury markers and inflammatory factor levels, suggesting miR-1307-5p may act as a protective factor in ACS progression. The biological function of miRNAs is primarily achieved through targeted regulation of downstream gene expression. Through bioinformatics prediction and experimental validation, we confirmed PIM3 as a direct target of miR-1307-5p. As a serine/threonine kinase, PIM3 has been reported to participate in endothelial cell injury and angiogenesis in cardiovascular diseases [31–33]. Myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury (MI/RI) induces elevated PIM3 expression [34]. Silencing PIM3 suppresses ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes and MI/RI [35]. Under hyperglycemic conditions, PIM3 upregulation participates in mediating myocardial hypertrophy and HF progression [36].

This study has several limitations that warrant clarification. First, the sample size was relatively small, and all participants were recruited from a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, this study utilized only HUVECs as an in vitro model; further validation in animal models is required. Third, although we have confirmed the diagnostic value of miR-1307-5p, its prognostic value in patients with ACS still needs to be validated through long-term follow-up studies. Furthermore, the downstream signaling pathways of PIM3 require further investigation.

This study reveals that PIM3 is upregulated in ACS patients and negatively regulated by miR-1307-5p. Rescue experiments demonstrate that under ACS conditions, the inhibitory effect of miR-1307-5p on PIM3 is attenuated, leading to increased PIM3 expression. Elevated PIM3 subsequently exacerbates vascular endothelial cell injury and amplifies local inflammatory responses, collectively promoting ACS progression.

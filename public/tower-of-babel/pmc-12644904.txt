PER3 suppresses lung adenocarcinomatous cell proliferation/migration through activating AMPK/mTOR pathway
Authors: Guanli Yuan, Yinfeng Liu, Dawei Wu, Jingping Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Dysregulation of circadian rhythm genes is related to the increased risk of development of various human cancers. This study investigated the biological roles of Period circadian regulator 3 (PER3) in the development and progression of lung adenocarcinoma and related underlying mechanisms. The expression of PER3 was decreased in lung adenocarcinoma and its low expression was correlated with advanced disease stages. Lentiviral-transduced overexpression of PER3 inhibited but PER3 silencing promoted proliferation, migration and invasion of lung adenocarcinomatous cells, suggesting that it played an anti-carcinogenic role in the development and progression of lung adenocarcinoma. Mechanistically, PER3 overexpression elevated the phosphorylation level of AMPK and reduced the phosphorylation level of mTOR, but PER3 silencing produced the opposite effects. More importantly, the inhibitory effects of PER3 overexpression on cellular proliferation, migration and invasion were partially reversed by the AMPK/mTOR pathway inhibitor. Correspondingly, the promoting effects of PER3 silencing on proliferation, migration and invasion was partially reversed by the AMPK/mTOR pathway activator. These findings collectively suggest that PER3 regulated biological behaviors of lung adenocarcinomatous cells through activating the AMPK/mTOR pathway. PER3 may be a promising biomarker and therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common histological type of lung cancer, and one type of NSCLC is adenocarcinoma1. Due to aggressive behavior of cancer cells, patients with lung adenocarcinoma are usually diagnosed at an advanced stage, resulting in a poor survival rate2–4. The lack of specific diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers is an urgent problem that need be addressed in the clinical management of patients with lung adenocarcinoma. Identifying valuable biomarkers will advance our understanding of molecular mechanisms of carcinogenesis of lung adenocarcinoma and further optimize treatment strategies.

Many physiological and biochemical processes in the human body, including cell cycle, genetic transcription, hormone release, anabolism and catabolism, are regulated by the circadian rhythm5. The circadian rhythm is driven by a series of circadian clock genes, which affect the expression of cytokines and cellular functions6,7. Recently, the increasing body of epidemiologic evidence suggests that mutation or dysregulation of circadian rhythm genes is related to increased risk of the development of various human cancers8,9. Many studies have reported differential expression of circadian rhythm genes in hepatocellular carcinoma, breast cancer and colorectal cancer samples10–12.

Period circadian regulator 3 (PER3), which is localized in chromosome 1p36, is a major member of the circadian rhythm gene family13. The genetic evidence of human being suggested that PER3 gene polymorphism contributes to susceptibility of human cancers such as pancreatic cancer, prostate cancer and multiple myeloma14–16. Also, altered expression of PER3 was reported to be involved in carcinogenesis and progression of prostate cancer, acute leukemia and colorectal cancer17–19. Therefore, the PER3 gene might be a promising target of anti-cancer treatment. However, little is known about its biological function and underlying mechanisms in the pathogenesis of lung adenocarcinoma. In this study, we examined the expression and clinical significance of PER3 in lung adenocarcinoma and investigated the effects of its ectopic expression on the malignant phenotype of tumor cells in vitro. Our findings advanced the understanding of biological roles and molecular mechanisms of PER3 in the development of lung adenocarcinoma, providing evidence for PER3 as a potential target for lung adenocarcinoma treatment.

Materials and methods

Data source and bioinformatics analysis

The transcriptomic data of 515 lung adenocarcinoma and 59 non-cancer samples with clinical information were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The mRNA expression profile of PER3 in lung adenocarcinoma was analyzed, and the relationships between its expression level and clinicopathological features were evaluated. The GSEA KEGG and GSEA GO enrichment analyses were conducted to identify specific signal pathways involving PER3.

Clinical samples and cell culture

A total of 8 matched cancer and para-cancerous tissues were collected from lung adenocarcinoma patients treated with curative resection in our hospital between April 2022 and December 2022. The histological diagnosis was verified by two independent pathologists using hematoxylin-eosin stain and immunohistochemistry. All patients did not receive any medical treatment (e.g., chemotherapy, radiotherapy or PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy) before surgery. All participants submitted informed consent before collection of clinical samples. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The study procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of our Hospital.

Four human lung adenocarcinoma cell lines (Calu-3, NCI-H1395, HCC827 and NCI-H1975) and normal lung epithelial cell line BEAS-2B were cultured in DMEM medium containing (Gibco, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA), and all cell lines were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 atmosphere. All cells were obtained from the Shanghai Cell Bank (Shanghai, China).

Plasmid construction and cell transfection

The plasmid overexpressing PER3 was constructed in a recombinant lentiviral vector (Lenti-blast-PER3) (Genomeditech, Shanghai, China), and the plasmid with nonsense sequence (Lenti-blast-KB) was used as the negative control. The small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting PER3 was synthesized (5ʹ-GCTTCAGAACACACTTCCAAA-3ʹ) and then subcloned into the pGenesil-1 vector (pGenesil1-PER3). A scrambled control was designed and generated by Genesil Biotechnology Corporation (Wuhan, China).

Lung adenocarcinomatous cell lines H1975 and HCC827 were seeded into six-well plates prior to transfection. Cell transfection was performed when cell density reached about 80% confluence. H1975 and HCC827 cells were subjected to lentiviral transduction for PER3 overexpression with a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10. Transfection with RNA interference of PER3 was carried out using an equal value of MOI. The efficiency of cell transfection was evaluated by Western blotting 48 h post transfection.

5-Ethynyl-2’-Deoxyuridine (EdU) assay

The proliferative capability of lung adenocarcinomatous cells was evaluated by EdU assay. In brief, cells were inoculated with approximately 5.0 × 103 cells per well in the 96-well plate and cultured overnight in a 37 °C incubator. Next, cells were treated with 10 µM of EdU reagent (Beyotime, China) for 2 h, followed by fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, China). The Apollo 594 working solution was added into each well, and the nuclei was counterstained with DAPI (Sigma, Germany). The number of cells with EdU-positive staining was counted, and the images were captured under a fluorescent microscope (Leica, Germany).

Cell counting kit (CCK-8)

Cell Counting Kit (CCK-8) assay (Promega #G7572) was used to investigate cell growth in vitro. Briefly, H1975 and HCC827 cells were inoculated with 5000 cells per well, and cell viability was measured under the absorbance at 490 nm.

Migration and invasion assays

The migratory and invasive ability of lung adenocarcinomatous cells were determined using Transwell chambers with an 8 μm pore size (Millipore, USA). To conduct the experiments, H1975 and HCC827 cells were resuspended with serum-free medium and then inoculated with a density of 5 × 104 cells into the top chamber. For the Transwell invasion assay, a total of 100 µL Matrigel gel (Corning, USA) was used to mimic cell membrane in the upper chambers, and 600 µL of complete medium with 20% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA) as a chemokine was added into the bottom chamber. After incubation for 48 h, the cells in the bottom chamber were treated with 4% formaldehyde for fixation followed by 0.1% crystal violet (Solarbio Biotechnology, China) staining. The number of migrating or invading cells were counted under a confocal microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan) with a magnification of ×200.

Western blotting

Total proteins were isolated from tissues and cells of lung adenocarcinoma using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (Solarbio Biotechnology, China). The protein concentrations of samples were determined by the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Solarbio Biotechnology, China). Next, the protein samples were subjected to heat at 100 ℃ for 5 min followed by electrophoretical separation with 10%-15% SDS-PAGE gel. The separated proteins were further transferred onto a PVDF membrane (Millipore, USA), followed by incubation with 5% Bovine albumin (Solarbio BioTechnology, China) at room temperature for 1 h. The primary antibodies used in Western blotting analysis were shown below: anti-PER3 (1:1000 dilution, ab177482, Abcam, USA), anti-mTOR (1:1000 dilution, ab134903, Abcam, USA), anti-AMPK (1:1000 dilution, ab32047, Abcam, USA), anti-p-mTOR (1:1000 dilution, ab109268, Abcam, USA), anti-p-AMPK (1:1000 dilution, ab133448, Abcam, USA) and anti-β-actin (1:1000 dilution, No.SA00001-2, Proteintech, China).

After washing for three times, the membranes were incubated with an appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (1:10,000 dilution, Santa Cruz, USA) for 2 h at room temperature, and then the protein bands were visualized with ECL solution (Solarbio Biotechnology, China). The protein intensity was quantified using the Image analysis software (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA) and normalized to the expression of β-actin protein. In addition, H1975 and HCC827 cells were treated with the inhibitor (Dorsomorphin) and activator (A769662) of the AMPK/mTOR pathways to validate the involvement of PER-3 in the pathophysiology of lung adenocarcinomatous cells.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry was conducted to detect the expression of PER3 in 8 pair of lung adenocarcinoma samples. The primary antibody with mouse monoclonal anti-PER3 (Abcam, USA, ab177482, 1:200) was used to incubate with tissue slides at 4 °C overnight. The IgG-labeled secondary antibody was incubated with the slides at room temperature for 60 min. Immunohistochemical responses were visualized by diaminobenzidine (DAB) solution, and then the tissue samples were counterstained with Hematoxylin.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted by Student’s t test for the comparisons of two groups and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for the comparisons of three or more groups, respectively. The expression level of PER3 mRNA in cancerous and para-cancerous samples was compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. All experimental results were obtained from at least three independent experiments with each carried out in replicates and were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The data were statistically processed with IBM SPSS 24.0 version software, and a two-sided P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

PER3 expression was decreased in lung adenocarcinoma

We downloaded information of 515 lung adenocarcinoma samples and 59 non-cancer samples from the TCGA database, and the basic characteristics of these patients and controls are shown in Supplemental Table 1. The transcription profile of PER3 was analyzed using the TCGA database. The flowchart in Fig. 1A shows the process of this analysis. The result indicated that the PER3 transcription was significantly lower in lung adenocarcinoma samples than in para-cancerous samples (Fig. 1B and C). As depicted in Fig. 1D, the low expression of PER3 was frequently detected in T3-T4 stage (P = 0.004), N2-N3 stage (P = 0.004) and TNM III-IV stage (P = 0.006). Kaplan-Meier Plotter (https://kmplot.com/analysis/) was used to evaluate the prognostic significance of PER3 expression for lung adenocarcinoma patients. The data showed that patients with low PER3 expression had a poorer DFS (P < 0.001) and OS (P < 0.001) than those with the high expression of PER3 (Fig. 1E). Analyses of GSEA KEGG pathway enrichment and GSEA GO pathway enrichment revealed that the biological role of PER3 was mainly involved in mTOR, ERBB and MAPK signaling pathway, and it played a key role in various human diseases such as NSCLC, endometrial carcinoma, Alzheimer disease and Parkinson’s disease (Fig. 1F)20,21. Taken together, these observations suggest that PER3 expression is downregulated in lung adenocarcinoma, which is correlated with poor survival of patients with lung adenocarcinoma.

PER3 played anti-carcinogenic roles in lung adenocarcinoma

To further investigate the biological role of PER3 in vitro, we examined its expression in 4 lung adenocarcinomatous cell lines using Western blotting. Compared with the control human lung epithelial cell (BEAS-2B), PER3 was highly expressed in NCI-H1395 and HCC827 cells and lowly expressed in Calu-3 and NCI-H1975 cells (Fig. 3A). According to the relative expression level of PER3, HCC827 and NCI-H1975 were selected to establish PER3-silenced and PER3-overexpressed cell lines, respectively. Western blot analysis showed that the expression of PER3 was markedly silenced in HCC827 cells treated with RNA interference (pGenesil1-PER3) in comparison to the negative control (pGenesil1-KB). Also, the presence of PER3 was detected in NCI-H1975 cells transfected with Lenti-blast-PER3 (Fig. 3B and C). EDU assay showed that silencing of PER3 expression increased the proportion of EDU positive-staining cells, whereas its overexpression inhibited it (Fig. 4A). CCK-8 assay indicated that the silencing of PER3 promoted the proliferative ability of lung adenocarcinomatous cells, whereas its overexpression inhibited it (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B).

PER3 mediated biological behaviors of lung adenocarcinomatous cells through the AMPK/mTOR pathway

The molecular mechanisms of PER3-mediated malignant phenotype of lung adenocarcinomatous cells were further explored. The GSEA analysis showed that the AMPK and mTOR pathways were enriched, and we therefore decided to examine the expression of the key signaling proteins in the AMPK/mTOR pathway to verify this hypothesis. The results showed that the phosphorylation levels of AMPK and mTOR protein, but not total AMPK and mTOR, were altered by PER3. The PER3-silencing HCC827 cells exhibited an increased level of p-mTOR protein and decreased level of p-AMPK protein. The expression levels of p-mTOR and p-AMPK protein in NCI-H1975 cells were significantly reduced and elevated by PER3 overexpression, respectively (Fig. 5B). Moreover, the phosphorylation levels of the AMPK/mTOR pathway proteins in PER3-silencing HCC827 cells were partially altered by its pathway activator (Fig. 6A). Correspondingly, treatment with dorsomorphin, an inhibitor of AMPK/mTOR pathway, could partially reverse the expression level of p-AMPK and p-mTOR proteins in PER3-overexpressing NCI-H1975 cells (Fig. 6B).

To further determine whether the AMPK/mTOR pathway was involved in PER3-mediated biological behaviors, lung adenocarcinomatous cells were treated with dorsomorphin (an inhibitor of AMPK) or A769662 (an activator of AMPK). As shown in Figs. 7 and 8 (also Supplemental Fig. 1), We found that the AMPK/mTOR activator suppressed the proliferation, migration and invasion of PER3-silencing HCC827 cells. In contrast, the AMPK/mTOR inhibitor partly reversed the inhibitory effects of PER3 overexpression on the proliferation, migration and invasion of NCI-H1975 cells. These findings suggested that PER3 regulated biological behaviors of lung adenocarcinomatous cells at least partially through activating the AMPK/mTOR pathways.

Discussion

Lung adenocarcinoma, a type of NSCLC, is a prevalent and fatal malignant disease around the world, but its pathological mechanisms remain poorly understood.

There is an urgent need to identify specific biomarkers that may be used to monitor carcinogenic progress and develop novel therapeutic targets. In recent years, circadian rhythm genes have attracted attentions owing to their potential roles in the development and progression of human cancers22,23. In this study, we examined the expression of PER3, one circadian rhythm gene, in lung adenocarcinoma and explored its biological role in tumorigenesis and progression. By analyzing the expression profiles of cancerous and para-cancerous samples, we found that PER3 expression decreased in lung adenocarcinoma and that its low expression was associated with aggressive tumor features, including advanced T stage, N stage and TNM stage. On the other hand, overexpression of PER3 inhibited but PER3 silencing promoted the proliferation, migration and invasion of lung adenocarcinoma cells in vitro. Therefore, PER3 functions as a tumor suppressor and may be a promising biomarker and treatment target in lung adenocarcinoma.

The Period gene family contains three members, PER1, PER2, and PER3, which were identified first in fruit bats and mammals. The increasing evidence suggested that genetic alteration of these circadian clock genes was frequently found in various human cancers10,24. For instance, PER3 expression was decreased in colorectal cancer tissues and cell lines, and PER3 overexpression inhibited cell apoptosis and promoted cancer stemness as well as chemoresistance25,26. The underlying mechanisms may involve p53, β-catenin and/or Notch signaling pathways. A previous study reported that the deficiency of PER3 expression is a predictor of tumor recurrence for breast cancer patients with positive estrogen receptor (ER)13. PER3 played anti-carcinogenic roles in the tumorigenesis and progression of breast cancer, as evidenced by the inhibitory effects of its overexpression on malignant phenotype27. However, the expression of PER3 and its biological roles could vary in different types of human cancers. For example, in a recent study, PER3 was highly expressed in astroblastoma tissues and cells, where it promoted aggressive behaviors of astroblastoma cells such as proliferation, anti-apoptosis, migration and invasion28. As a result, a high level of PER3 was related to poor prognosis of astroblastoma patients28. These findings suggested that PER3 may be a valuable target for anti-cancer treatment. However, the expression profile of PER3 and its biological roles in NSCLC were not well known. In the present study, PER3 gene was identified as a critical molecule to regulate biological behaviors of lung adenocarcinomatous cells in vitro, and it may exert an anti-carcinogenic effect in the development and progression of lung adenocarcinoma.

As a vital modulator of cell autophagy, proliferation and energy metabolism, the activation of the AMPK/mTOR signaling pathway was responsible for cancer progression and metastasis29,30. This pathway can be activated by phosphorylated AMPK protein and inactivated by phosphorylated mTOR protein in human cancers, contributing to the malignant phenotype31,32. In the present study, we found that PER3 overexpression elevated the phosphorylation level of AMPK and reduced the phosphorylation level of mTOR in lung adenocarcinomatous cells. Interestingly, the inhibitory effects of PER3 overexpression on cellular proliferation, migration and invasion were partially reversed by the AMPK/mTOR pathway inhibitor. On the other hand, the AMPK/mTOR activator suppressed the increased proliferation, migration and invasion in PER3-silencing HCC827 cells. These findings demonstrate the involvement of the AMPK/mTOR pathway in the PER3-mediated anti-carcinogenic role. These findings together support the notion that PER3 mediates the activity of lung adenocarcinomatous cells via modulating the function of the AMPK/mTOR pathway.

The limitations of our study should be noted. First, the database we used for analysis had many more disease samples than control ones. It would be a good addition to utilizing other databases, if possible, in the future, to validate the findings obtained from this single database. Second, the number of human samples we used to validate our findings obtained from our database analyses was also limited. Third, our study was only performed in vitro, i.e. in cultured cells. Therefore, it will be imperative that we should expand the clinical sample size and employ animal models for further validation of our in vitro findings obtained from the present study in the future.

In summary, the expression of PER3 is decreased in lung adenocarcinoma and its low expression is correlated with advanced disease stages. Functional experiments demonstrate that PER3 overexpression inhibits cellular proliferation, migration and invasion through mediating the AMPK/mTOR pathway. These findings support the premise that PER3 suppress the lung adenocarcinomatous cell proliferation/migration through activating the AMPK/mTOR pathway. Therefore, PER3 may serve as a potential biomarker of lung adenocarcinoma as well as a novel therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma treatment.

MDTeamGPT: A Self-Evolving LLM-based Multi-Agent Framework for Multi-Disciplinary Team Medical Consultation
Kai Chen, Xinfeng Li, Tianpei Yang *, Hewei Wang, Wei Dong, Yang Gao

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) have made significant progress in various fields. However, challenges remain in Multi-Disciplinary Team (MDT) medical consultations. Current research enhances reasoning through role assignment, task decomposition, and accumulation of medical experience. Multi-role collaboration in MDT consultations often results in excessively long dialogue histories. This increases the model’s cognitive burden and degrades both efficiency and accuracy. Some methods only store treatment histories. They do not extract effective experience or reflect on errors. This limits knowledge generalization and system evolution. We propose a multi-agent MDT medical consultation framework based on LLMs to address these issues. Our framework uses consensus aggregation and a residual discussion structure for multi-round consultations. It also employs a Correct Answer Knowledge Base (CorrectKB) and a Chain-of-Thought Knowledge Base (ChainKB) to accumulate consultation experience. These mechanisms enable the framework to evolve and continually improve diagnosis rationality and accuracy. Experimental results on the MedQA and PubMedQA datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves accuracies of 90.1% and 83.9%, respectively, and that the constructed knowledge bases generalize effectively across test sets from both datasets. Our code is publicly available at: https://kaichennj.github.io/MDTeamGPT .

Introduction

When diagnosing and treating complex diseases, a single-specialty medical perspective frequently proves insufficient to address patients’ comprehensive and precise healthcare needs, thereby necessitating the implementation of a Multi-Disciplinary Team (MDT) and diversified clinical perspectives
Macken et al. (2022)
;
Zhou and Xu (2023)
. MDT facilitates the development of more accurate and holistic therapeutic strategies through systematic integration of expertise across medical disciplines
Makary (2011)
. Nevertheless, the organization of interdisciplinary consultations demands substantial temporal and human resources, while remaining vulnerable to procedural oversights, cognitive biases, and communication inefficiencies
Burton et al. (2022)
. Such systemic challenges may ultimately compromise healthcare quality and adversely affect patient outcomes.

Figure 1:
The lead physician consolidates and refines the discussion outcomes from the current round of agents by categorizing them into four detailed categories: consistency, conflict, independence, and integration, thereby enhancing the overall diagnostic clarity.

Large Language Models (LLMs), with their massive parameter sizes and extensive training on vast knowledge bases from diverse domains, demonstrate strong reasoning abilities and zero-shot generalization capabilities
Achiam et al. (2023)
;
Touvron et al. (2023)
. These capabilities have led many researchers to explore the potential application of LLMs in medical MDT diagnostics
Kim et al. (2024)
;
Chen et al. (2024b)
. Leveraging LLMs as autonomous agents offers a promising way to improve medical consultations by human doctors.

However, the application of LLMs in MDT diagnostics faces two primary challenges. First, privacy constraints and the lack of standardized data storage result in scarce medical consultation data, hindering the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of large models. Secondly, although LLMs have been fine-tuned with medical knowledge, they remain prone to hallucination issues
Ye et al. (2023)
;
Pal et al. (2023)
. In the medical field, such hallucinations can result in severe medical incidents even with minor errors
Nori et al. (2023)
.

Multi-agent based on LLMs enhances medical reasoning by stimulating latent medical knowledge through multi-round interactions and significantly reduces the occurrence of hallucinations
Liu et al. (2023)
;
Du et al. (2023)
. Recent research in this domain has primarily focused on role configuration
Tang et al. (2023)
;
Long et al. (2024)
, subtask decomposition
Pandey et al. (2024)
, clinical environment simulation
Schmidgall et al. (2024)
, and the accumulation of medical experience
Wei et al. (2024)
;
Li et al. (2024b)
. However, several challenges remain. First, multi-role interactions often result in excessively long dialogues. As contextual information accumulates, the cognitive load on the model increases, negatively impacting response efficiency and accuracy
Maharana et al. (2024)
. Second, some studies rely on simply storing diagnostic histories without effectively integrating correct diagnostic experiences or reflecting on errors, which may lead to biases when handling detailed information
Li et al. (2024a)
. Finally, enhancing the generalization ability of systems in preserving medical experiences
Qian et al. (2024a)
is crucial for achieving self-evolution and leveraging collective intelligence.

In this work, we propose a self-evolving LLM-based multi-agent framework (MDTeamGPT) for multi-disciplinary team medical consultation. This framework incorporates various doctor roles and supporting roles, where a "patient" role arrives with specific background information and medical questions. The Primary Care Doctor assigns the patient to the most suitable specialist agents based on the patient’s condition. Subsequently, the specialist agents engage in a multi-round of discussion, sharing relevant information. After each round, a lead physician summarizes and organizes the discussion outcomes, categorizing them into
Consistency
,
Conflict
,
Independence
, and
Integration
, as illustrated in Figure
1
. In the subsequent discussion rounds, agents refer to the information stored in the
Historical Shared Pool
based on the residual discussion structure. Once a consensus is reached through multiple rounds of deliberation, the final consultation outcome is reviewed and filtered by a Safety and Ethics Reviewer before presenting the final results and recommendations. At this stage, the consultation outcomes are further categorized based on their correctness and stored in distinct knowledge bases, serving as references to enhance future consultations. Overall, the main contributions of our work are threefold:

•

We propose an MDT-based multi-agent medical consultation framework that incorporates a lead physician mechanism and a residual discussion structure to effectively integrate multi-round discussion outcomes. This design reduces the cognitive load on agents, thereby enhancing the accuracy of reasoning and decision-making.

•

The framework employs an adaptive learning mechanism that leverages both correct and incorrect diagnostic experiences, progressively accumulating and strengthening transferable reasoning capabilities.

•

Experimental results demonstrate that the framework, utilizing 600 rounds of consultation experience, achieves accuracies of 90.1% on the MedQA dataset and 83.9% on the PubMedQA dataset. Cross-dataset tests further confirm that the knowledge bases retain generalizable reasoning skills, not just isolated consultation data.

Related Work

2.1
LLM-based Multi-Agent Collaboration

Well-structured multi-agent systems can significantly reduce errors and enhance interaction efficiency
Qian et al. (2024c)
;
Park et al. (2023)
;
Du et al. (2024)
;
Qian et al. (2024d)
. ChatDev
Qian et al. (2024c)
splits tasks into subtasks managed by an instructor and an assistant, using multi-round inquiries to mitigate hallucinations. MACNET
Qian et al. (2024d)
adopts a directed acyclic graph with topological sorting for interactive reasoning, and MetaGPT
Hong et al. (2024)
encodes SOPs for roles akin to a software company, effectively coordinating specialized expertise. However, these methods primarily focus on software development and face applicability challenges in medical consultations. Medagents
Tang et al. (2023)
assigns distinct doctor roles to each LLM-Agent and uses consensus voting, but lacks a robust strategy, risking collective hallucinations
Chen et al. (2024a)
. Additionally, these methods rely on static structures limited by zero-shot capabilities.

2.2
Experience Utilization

Inspired by human knowledge acquisition mechanisms, equipping LLM-Agents with reflective memory can enhance their problem-solving capabilities
Zhong et al. (2024)
. ExpeL
Zhao et al. (2024)
accumulates experiences from past successes and applies this knowledge during reasoning. Co-Learning
Qian et al. (2024a)
focuses on collecting experience-driven heuristics from prior actions, thus enabling agents to handle novel tasks more flexibly. IER
Qian et al. (2024b)
permits LLM-Agents to iteratively refine accumulated experiences throughout task execution. Selfevolve
Jiang et al. (2023)
employs LLMs both as knowledge providers and as self-reflective programmers, allowing agents to evolve through reflective processes. Agent Hospital
Li et al. (2024c)
utilizes medical record libraries and experience databases to accumulate diagnostic data, enhancing prompts for medical agents and supporting their evolution. However, the lack of abstraction, summarization, and reflection on erroneous cases limits the ability to fully leverage valuable insights from mistakes.

2.3
Discussion and Aggregation

An effective discussion process can significantly enhance LLM-Multi-Agent performance. The Nominal Group Technique (NGT)
Ven and Delbecq (1974)
is a structured decision-making method that moves teams from individual ideation to collective consensus through four stages: personal idea generation, round-robin sharing, focused discussion, and voting. Building on this principle, LLM Discussion
Lu et al. (2024)
augments the creative potential of LLMs by dividing conversations into initiation, discussion, and convergence phases, thereby promoting iterative and divergent exchanges among multiple agents.

All specialists’ statements are sent to the lead physician(as detailed in Appendix
B.1

•

•

4.3
Ablation Studies

To examine the contribution of each component within the MDTeamGPT framework, we conducted experiments combining different configuration settings. Throughout these experiments, gpt-4-turbo served as the backbone model for all agents.

In Table
2
, the first experimental setting permits all agents to engage in a joint discussion without any specific discussion strategy; the system only terminates when either a consensus is reached among all agents or the maximum number of discussion rounds is exhausted. The second experiment introduces the residual discussion mode, resulting in lower accuracy on MedQA but a 0.8% improvement on PubMedQA, leading to a 0.4% decrease in average accuracy compared with the first experiment. In the third experiment, we remove the residual discussion mode and only employ the lead physician to synthesize discussion results. Relative to the first experiment, this yields a 0.3% drop on MedQA but a 1.4% increase on PubMedQA, translating into a 0.5% rise in average accuracy.

Notably, combining the residual discussion mode and the lead physician in the fourth experiment raises the accuracy on MedQA and PubMedQA by 5.8% and 3.6%, respectively, compared with the first experiment, demonstrating that jointly applying residual discussion mode and lead physician offers substantial enhancements in consultation efficiency and accuracy.

We further investigate the role of the two knowledge bases (
CorrectKB
and
ChainKB
) in Experiments 5, 6, and 7, where both knowledge bases store experiences from 600 consultation rounds. Using only
CorrectKB
yields 2.1% and 5.4% higher accuracy on MedQA and PubMedQA, respectively, compared to using only
ChainKB
, and leads to a 3.8% increase in overall accuracy. This finding aligns with intuition, as
CorrectKB
Retain verified correct consultation experiences that can better guide subsequent diagnoses. However, the combined use of both
CorrectKB
and
ChainKB
achieves an accuracy superior to either
CorrectKB
or
ChainKB
alone, corroborating our hypothesis that integrating correct experiences with reflective insights maximally boosts consultation accuracy.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel MDT multi-agent medical consultation framework, MDTeamGPT, which leverages a residual discussion mode and consensus aggregation to minimize information redundancy and improve discussion efficiency. By leveraging two knowledge bases, the framework dynamically improves overall consultation accuracy and, to some extent, demonstrates excellent generalization ability. Although the overall performance of the framework largely depends on the capabilities of the foundational model used to store and utilize consultation experiences—which may constrain its performance—our approach still excels in current medical consultation scenarios.

Ethical Considerations

This study utilizes publicly available academic datasets and accesses LLMs via official APIs, ensuring ethical and responsible research practices. Specifically, the ethical considerations that were taken into account are outlined as follows:

Public Datasets
: The medical datasets used in this research are publicly released and intended for academic purposes. No personal patient data was involved or processed.

Licensed API Usage
: Our use of LLMs adheres to the API usage policies, maintaining compliance with fair use standards and respecting intellectual property rights.

Transparency
: We provide detailed descriptions of our experimental methods to ensure the reproducibility of our results, and we are committed to promoting transparency in scientific practices.

Limitations

Our study provides a comprehensive set of experiments to assess the performance of the proposed MDTeamGPT. However, several areas could be further improved and explored in future research.

Increasing Agent Complexity
: Currently, the agents in our framework are defined quite simply. We believe that by incorporating more advanced techniques, such as Tree-of-Thought
Yao et al. (2024)
, or integrating external tools and knowledge bases, we could significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of the multi-agent medical consultation system.

Expanding the Dataset
: In our experiments, we have used only two datasets—MedQA and PubMedQA. To strengthen the validity of our findings, future work will involve testing on a broader range of datasets, which will help confirm the robustness of our method across different types of data.

Testing in Real-World Scenarios
: Our research has mainly focused on experiments with pre-existing datasets. In future work, we aim to collect more real-world consultation data, which will allow us to assess how well our framework performs in practical, real-life settings and explore its ability to generalize beyond controlled experimental environments.

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of MDTeamGPT, we invited five volunteers to participate in the tests. All volunteers hold at least a bachelor’s degree in clinical medicine, and two of them have medical practitioner qualifications. We randomly selected five questions from the MedQA dataset and five questions from the PubMedQA dataset, resulting in a total of 10 test questions. Volunteers observed the outputs of MDTeamGPT and rated them using the survey questionnaire shown in Table
4
. Each volunteer was assigned integer scores for every test. The evaluation covered 10 dimensions: accuracy, explainability, completeness, rationality, diversity, efficiency, consistency, user experience, safety, and innovation. This portion of the testing was conducted using gpt4-turbo.

As shown in Figure
7
, the volunteers assigned relatively low scores to the "Efficiency" metric, primarily due to the increased time required for multi-round discussions among multiple roles compared to the Single-Agent approach. The "Diversity" metric also received an average score below 3. This may be attributed to the Temperature setting of 0.7, which was chosen to balance determinism and randomness; increasing this value could potentially enhance diversity. For "Completeness", the average score was 3, though some feedback indicated that certain responses lacked sufficient consideration. To address this, future improvements will focus on designing more comprehensive prompts and optimizing the framework’s efficiency. All other metrics received scores above 3, demonstrating the potential value of MDTeamGPT in practical medical applications.

u_{t}-\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,

where
u(x,t)
is the wave amplitude,
x
is the spatial coordinate,
t

u(x,0)=\frac{c}{8}sech^{2}(\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}x),

(2)

Previously, I only dealt with second-order derivatives in the equation when using the finite difference scheme to solve some partial derivative equations (e.g., the 1D heat equation.). But for this KdV equation, I notice that there are first-order
u_{x}
and third-order derivatives
u_{xxx}
in it and they make things difficult.

In order to solve this dilemma, I first use a centered difference approximation for the first and third derivatives. To discrete the problem in spaces and time, I consider
x_{i}=i\Delta x
and
t_{n}=n\Delta t
be the discrete spatial and time coordinates, where
\Delta x
and
\Delta t
are the spatial and time steps, respectively. Therefore, using a centered difference approximation and an upwind scheme for the nonlinear term in the equation, I can obtain the following results:

\displaystyle u_{t}\approx\frac{u_{i}^{n+1}-u_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t},\

(4)

\displaystyle u_{x}\approx\frac{u_{i+1}^{n}-u_{i-1}^{n}}{2\Delta x},\

(5)

\displaystyle u_{xx}=\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-2u^{n}_{i}+u^{n}_{i-1}}{(\Delta x)^{2}},\

(6)

\displaystyle u_{xxx}\approx\frac{u_{i+2}^{n}-2u_{i+1}^{n}+2u_{i-1}^{n}-u_{i-2}^{n}}{2\Delta x^{3}},\

(7)

\displaystyle uu_{x}\approx u_{i}^{n}\frac{u_{i+1}^{n}-u_{i-1}^{n}}{2\Delta x}.

(8)

Then I replace the partial derivative parts in the KdV equation with the above finite difference approximations. I get the following results:

\frac{u_{i}^{n+1}-u_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t}-\frac{3}{2}u_{i}^{n}\frac{u_{i+1}^{n}-u_{i-1}^{n}}{2\Delta x}+\frac{u_{i+2}^{n}-2u_{i+1}^{n}+2u_{i-1}^{n}-u_{i-2}^{n}}{2\Delta x^{3}}=0.

(9)

Then, moving
u^{n+1}_{i}
to the left side and moving other terms to the right side, I get the following expressions:

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{i}=u^{n}_{i}[1+\frac{3\Delta t}{4\Delta x}(u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1})]-\frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x^{3}}(u^{n}_{i+2}-2u^{n}_{i+1}+2u^{n}_{i-1}-u^{n}_{i-2}).

(10)

Based on the above equation, we can get the results of
u^{n+1}_{i}
by iteratively solving for
u
at each time step, starting from an initial condition
u(x,0)
. However, this method is an explicit method, which means that it is conditionally stable. To better solve this equation, I would change the method with the Crank-Nikolson Scheme.

2
Crank-Nikolson Scheme

Since the explicit method of solving the KdV equation is conditionally stable, I try to find an implicit method to better solve the problem.
To discretize the equation using the Crank-Nicolson scheme, we let
x_{i}=i\Delta x
and
t_{n}=n\Delta t
be the discrete spatial and time coordinates, where
\Delta x
and
\Delta t
are the spatial and time steps, respectively. Using a central difference approximation for the spatial derivative and a weighted average of the forward and backward time steps for the time derivative, we obtain:

\displaystyle u_{t}\approx\frac{u_{i}^{n+1}-u_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t},

(11)

\displaystyle u_{x}\approx\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}}{4\Delta x},

(12)

\displaystyle u_{xxx}\approx\frac{(u^{n}_{i+2}-2u^{n}_{i+1}+2u^{n}_{i-1}-u^{n}_{i-2})+(u^{n+1}_{i+2}-2u^{n+1}_{i+1}+2u^{n+1}_{i-1}-u^{n+1}_{i-2})}{4\Delta x^{3}}.

(13)

However, one of the difficulties is how to linearize the non-linear term in the KdV equation. That is, how to linearize the
uu_{x}
in the KdV equation. In the view of implicit central difference, we approximate the terms in the following ways:

\displaystyle uu_{x}\approx{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}u_{i}^{n}}\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}}{4\Delta x},

(14)

or

\displaystyle uu_{x}\approx{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}u_{i}^{n+1}}\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}}{4\Delta x}.

(15)

Then, I substitute these approximations into the KdV Equation
1
and obtain the following results.

First, I define some terms in the following:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{3}}=\alpha,\ \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}=\beta

(16)

If I use the approximation
uu_{x}\approx u_{i}^{n}\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}}{4\Delta x}
, the result will be

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}(u^{n+1}_{i+2}-2u^{n+1}_{i+1}+2u^{n+1}_{i-1}-u^{n+1}_{i-2})+u^{n+1}_{i}-\frac{3\beta}{8}u^{n}_{i}(u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1})

(17)

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{i}[1+\frac{3\beta}{8}(u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1})]+\frac{\alpha}{4}[u^{n}_{i-2}-u^{n}_{i+2}+2u^{n}_{i+1}-2u^{n}_{i-1}].

However, if I use the approximation
uu_{x}\approx u_{i}^{n+1}\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}}{4\Delta x}
, the result will become

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}(u^{n+1}_{i+2}-2u^{n+1}_{i+1}+2u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-2})+

(18)

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{i}[1-\frac{3\beta}{8}(u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}+u^{n}_{i-1}-u^{n}_{i+1})]

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{i}+\frac{\alpha}{4}(u^{n}_{i-2}-u^{n}_{i+2}+2u^{n}_{i+1}-2u^{n}_{i-1})

After gaining the results, we can use matrices to solve these linear equations. Let me first focus on the case of approximation
uu_{x}\approx u_{i}^{n}\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i+1}-u^{n+1}_{i-1}}{4\Delta x}
. In order to solve the above equations, I view the equations as linear and re-organize them as follows:

\displaystyle(\frac{-\alpha}{4})u^{n+1}_{i-2}+(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\beta}{8}u^{n}_{i})u^{n+1}_{i-1}+u^{n+1}_{i}+(-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{3\beta}{8}u^{n}_{i})u^{n+1}_{i+1}+\frac{\alpha}{4}u^{n+1}_{i+2}

(19)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{4}u^{n}_{i-2}+(-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{3\beta}{8}u^{n}_{i})u^{n}_{i-1}+u^{n}_{i}+(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\beta}{8}u^{n}_{i})u^{n}_{i+1}-\frac{\alpha}{4}u^{n}_{i+2}.

Based on the above results, we can re-formulate the problem with the matrix

AU^{n+1}=BU^{n},

(20)

where the
A,B,U^{n},U^{n+1}
are all matrices. To be more specific, I write down the details here

\displaystyle U^{n+1}=[u^{n+1}_{i-2}\ \ u^{n+1}_{i-1}\ \ u^{n+1}_{i}\ \ u^{n+1}_{i+1}\ \ u^{n+1}_{i+2}]^{T},

(21)

\displaystyle U^{n}=[u^{n}_{i-2}\ \ u^{n}_{i-1}\ \ u^{n}_{i}\ \ u^{n}_{i+1}\ \ u^{n}_{i+2}]^{T},

Let
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\beta}{8}u^{n}_{i}
for convenience. And the matrices
A,B
can be represented as

\displaystyle A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&-\gamma&\frac{\alpha}{4}&0&0\\
\gamma&1&-\gamma&\frac{\alpha}{4}&0\\
-\frac{\alpha}{4}&\gamma&1&-\gamma&\frac{\alpha}{4}\\
0&-\frac{\alpha}{4}&\gamma&1&-\gamma\\
0&0&-\frac{\alpha}{4}&\gamma&1\end{array}\right),\

(22)

\displaystyle B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&\gamma&-\frac{\alpha}{4}&0&0\\
-\gamma&1&\gamma&-\frac{\alpha}{4}&0\\
\frac{\alpha}{4}&-\gamma&1&\gamma&-\frac{\alpha}{4}\\
0&\frac{\alpha}{4}&-\gamma&1&\gamma\\
0&0&\frac{\alpha}{4}&-\gamma&1\end{array}\right).

Then, I can use formulate the equation as
Ax=b
, where
x=U^{n+1},b=BU^{n}
. This is equation can be easily solved in programming. For example, I can use the Python numpy linear algebra library to solve it (i.e., x = np.linalg.solve(A,b)).

2.1
Stability

After solving the KdV equation, I need to see whether the Crank-Nicolson scheme is unconditionally stable for this problem. In order to validate its stability of this scheme, I use the Von Neumann stability analysis.

Firstly, I assume
u_{i}^{n}=\lambda^{n}e^{ikj\Delta x}
, where
\lambda
is a complex number and
k_{i}
is the wave number at grid point
i
. Considering this transformation in the solution I calculated above, I can gain the discrete equation. And divide two parts by
\lambda^{n}e^{ikj\Delta x}
, I can gain the initial results as follows:

Set

r=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\beta}{8}\lambda^{n}e^{ikj\Delta x},

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}\lambda(e^{i2k\Delta x}-e^{-i2k\Delta x})+\lambda+(-1)r\lambda(e^{ik\Delta x}-e^{-ik\Delta x})

(23)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{4}\lambda(e^{-i2k\Delta x}-e^{i2k\Delta x})+1+r(e^{ik\Delta x}-e^{-ik\Delta x}),

Directly solve this equation is hard for me, but I can use the Euler’s formula here:

sinx=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i},\ cosx=\frac{e^{i}x+e^{-ix}}{2}.

(24)

Based on this, I can further transform the equation to the following form:

\lambda=\frac{-0.5\alpha i\ sin(2k\Delta x)+2ri\ sin(k\Delta x)+1}{0.5\alpha i\ sin(2k\Delta x)-2ri\ sin(k\Delta x)+1},

(25)

Set

Q=0.5\alpha i\ sin(2k\Delta x)-2ri\ sin(k\Delta x)+1,

\lambda=\frac{2}{Q}-1.

(26)

Now I just need to check the value of
Q
to see whether
|\lambda|
is unconditionally stable. Rewrite the formula of
Q
as

Q=2ri\ [sin(k\delta x)-\frac{\alpha}{4r}sin(2k\delta x)]+1,

(27)

It is cristal clear that
r>0
,
\alpha>0
,
\frac{\alpha}{4r}<1
, and therefore, I can obtain the result
Q>1
. For any
Q
, I have
|\frac{2}{Q}-1|<1
. Therefore, I derive that

|\lambda|<1.

(28)

It is safe to conclude that the Crank-Nicolson scheme in this KdV problem is unconditionally stable.

2.2
Consistency

In order to analyze the consistency of the Crank-Nicolson scheme for problem, I need to determine if this method converges to the continuous solution as the discretization parameters (time step
\Delta t
and spatial step
\Delta x
) approach zero.

Let us begin with the Taylor series of function
u
in
t
and
x
, I have the following formulas:

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{i}=u^{n}_{i}+ku^{n}_{t,i}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}u^{n}_{tt,i}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}),

(29)

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{i\pm 1}=u^{n}_{i}\pm ku^{n}_{t,i}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}u^{n}_{tt,i}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}),

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{i\pm 2}=u^{n}_{i}\pm 2ku^{n}_{t,i}+2^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{2}u^{n}_{tt,i}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3});

\displaystyle u^{n}_{i\pm 1}=u^{n}_{i}\pm hu_{x}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}u_{xx}-\frac{h^{3}}{6}u_{xxx}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}),

\displaystyle u^{n}_{i\pm 1}=u^{n}_{i}\pm 2hu_{x}+2^{2}\frac{h^{2}}{2}u_{xx}\pm 2^{3}\frac{h^{3}}{6}u_{xxx}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}),

where
k=\Delta t
and
h=\Delta x
. Then, replace the relevant terms in Equation
19
with the above Taylor expansion series, I can obtain:

\displaystyle u^{n}_{i}+(k-\frac{3k\beta u^{n}_{i}}{4})u^{n}_{t,i}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}u^{n}_{tt,i}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3})

(30)

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{i}+\frac{3\beta hu^{n}_{i}}{4}u_{x}+(\frac{-\alpha h^{3}}{2}+\frac{3\beta u^{n}_{i}}{8})u_{x}xx+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

Then, I define the error function
Error(x,t)
, which use the original KdV equation to minus the above equation. And the error function can be written as:

\displaystyle Error(x,t)=

\displaystyle(1-k+\frac{3\beta u^{n}_{i}}{4})u_{t}+(1-\frac{\alpha h^{3}}{2}+\frac{h^{3}\beta u^{n}_{i}}{8})u_{xxx}

(31)

\displaystyle+(\frac{3h\beta}{4}-1.5)uu_{x}-\frac{k^{2}}{2}u_{tt}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}+h^{4}).

Notice that in the previous definitions, I have
k=\Delta t,h=\Delta x,\alpha=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{3}},\beta=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
. And then, I use these terms in the above
Error(x,t)
functions:

\displaystyle Error(x,t)=

\displaystyle(\frac{4-4\Delta t+3\Delta tu^{n}_{i}/\Delta x}{4})u_{t}+(\frac{8-4\Delta t+\Delta t\Delta x^{2}u^{n}_{i}}{8})u_{xxx}

(32)

\displaystyle+(\frac{3\Delta t-6}{4})uu_{x}-\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}u_{tt}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}+h^{4}).

Based on the above result, when
\Delta t\rightarrow 0,\Delta x\rightarrow 0
, the error function
Error(x,t)\rightarrow 0
. Therefore, the Crank-Nicolson scheme is consistent for the KdV equation here, and I expect it to converge to the exact solution as I refine the discretization parameters.

2.3
Convergence

Convergence means that the numerical solution obtained using the scheme converges to the exact solution of the KdV equation as the time step and the spatial discretization are refined.

In order to demonstrate the convergence of the proposed scheme, I need to demonstrate that this finite difference method can be written as a two-level scheme. It follows that I need to demonstrate the matrix
A

\displaystyle U^{n}=[u^{n}_{i-2}\ \ u^{n}_{i-1}\ \ u^{n}_{i}\ \ u^{n}_{i+1}\ \ u^{n}_{i+2}]^{T},

,

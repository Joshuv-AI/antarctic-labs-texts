On the generalized Bass–Quillen conjecture in dimension 2
A. Stavrova

Abstract
. Let A A be a regular ring of dimension ≤ 2 \leq 2 . Let G G be a reductive group over A A such that its derived group is a split, i.e. a Chevalley–Demazure, semisimple group. We prove that every Zariski-locally trivial principal G G -bundle over A ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] is extended from A A , for any n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . This result generalizes to split reductive groups the dimension 2 2 case of the Bass–Quillen conjecture on finitely generated projective modules, settled in positive by M. P. Murthy.



1.
Introduction

The following conjecture was initially stated by H. Bass as a problem
[
Ba
, Problem IX]
,
and then popularized as the Bass–Quillen conjecture after it was rehearsed by D. Quillen in
[
Q
]
; see e.g.
[
La
]

for a detailed historical account.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Bass–Quillen conjecture)
Let
A
be a regular ring of finite Krull dimension. Then every finitely generated projective module over
A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

is extended from
A
for any
n\geq 1
.

This conjecture can be viewed as a specialization to
G=GL_{m}
,
m\geq 1
, of the following more general statement.
Let
A
be a regular ring, and let
G
be a strictly isotropic reductive group over
A
, then every Zariski-locally
trivial principal
G
-bundle over
A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is extended from
A
for any
n\geq 1
.
Here a reductive group (scheme)

G
in the sense of
[
SGA3
]
is called strictly isotropic, if
G
contains a parabolic subgroup
that intersects properly every semisimple normal subgroup of
G
, see e.g.
[
St22
]

(the same property is sometimes called “totally isotropic”). This generalized version of the Bass–Quillen conjecture
is known for equicharacteristic (i.e. containing a field) regular rings
A
of arbitrary dimension
and every strictly isotropic reductive group
G
defined over
A

[
St22
, Corollary 5.5]
(see also
[
C3
]

for a different proof). Also, it is known for all rings
A
which are ind-smooth over a discrete valuation ring
D

and for all strictly isotropic reductive groups
G
defined over
D

[
GuLi
, Theorem 1.8]
.
See also
[
La
,
C2
,
SuK
,
K
,
CTO
,
AHW
,
RR
,
R89
]
for earlier results and discussion of the assumptions.

We establish the dimension
\leq 2
case of the generalized Bass–Quillen conjecture
for all reductive groups
G
whose derived group
G^{der}
in the sense of
[
SGA3
]
is a split, i.e. Chevalley–Demazure,
semisimple group. Such groups
G
are automatically strictly isotropic, since all of their semisimple normal subgroups
are split and contain a Borel subgroup.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
be a regular ring of dimension
\leq 2
.
Let
G
be a
reductive group over
A
such that
G^{der}
is a split, i.e. Chevalley–Demazure,
semisimple group.
Then the map

H^{1}_{Zar}(A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],G)\xrightarrow{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{n}=0}H^{1}_{Zar}(A,G)

is bijective for any
n\geq 1
.
That is, every Zariski-locally trivial principal
G
-bundle over

A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is extended from
A
.

If
G=GL_{n}
, then Theorem
1.2
is due to M. P. Murthy
[
Mur66
]
for
n=1
and to
A. Suslin
[
Su
, Theorem 5]
and D. Quillen
[
Q
]
in general. Raman Parimala established the analogous statement
for isotropic quadratic spaces over
A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
2\in A^{\times}

[
Par
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

For any commutative ring
R
, we denote by
R(x)
the localization of the polynomial ring
R[x]
at the set of all monic
polynomials.

Let
G
be an affine locally finitely presented group scheme over
R
.
We denote by
H^{1}_{Zar}(R,G)
,
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R,G)
and
H^{1}_{fppf}(R,G)
the first non-abelian cohomology of

\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)
with values in
G
with respect to Zariski, étale and fppf topologies, respectively. Equivalently, these are the sets of
isomorphism classes of principal
G
-bundles (or
G
-torsors) over
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)
which are trivial locally in the corresponding
topology. We write
\xi=1
to signify that the principal
G
-bundle
\xi
is trivial.
For brevity, we also denote

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],G)_{0}=\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],G)\xrightarrow{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{n}=0}H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R,G)\right),

and similarly for the fppf and Zariski topologies.

The following lemma is a slightly stronger version of
[
St19
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be a regular semilocal domain,
K
be the fraction field of
R
. Let
G,G^{\prime}
be reductive
R
-groups, and
T
be an

R
-group of multiplicative type such that there is a short exact sequence

(a)\quad 1\to G^{\prime}\to G\to T\to 1\quad\mbox{or}\quad(b)\quad 1\to T\to G^{\prime}\to G\to 1

of
R
-group schemes.
Fix any
n\geq 1
, and write
\mathbf{x}
for the string of variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
The natural map

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[\mathbf{x}],G)

induces an isomorphism

(1)

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})\right)\xrightarrow{\cong}\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[\mathbf{x}],G)_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[\mathbf{x}],G)\right).

Proof.

Since
G
and
G^{\prime}
are smooth, we can replace their étale cohomology by fppf, see e.g.
[
Mi
, p. 120]
.

Consider first the case
(a)
. Let
S
be any of
R
,
R[\mathbf{x}]
,
K
,
K[\mathbf{x}]
, then we have
an exact sequence of pointed sets

T(S)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,G^{\prime})\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,G)\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,T).

Since
R
is regular,
T
is isotrivial
[
SGA3
, Exp. X, Th. 5.16]
, and hence subject to the results of
[
CTS
]
. Namely,
by
[
CTS
, Lemma 2.4]
we have
H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,T)\cong H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S[x],T)
,
and by
[
CTS
, Theorem 4.1]
the map
H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R,T)\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K,T)
is injective.
Hence
H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],T)\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],T)
is also injective.
Hence any

\xi\in\ker\bigl(H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G)\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],G)\bigr)
lifts to an element
\eta\in H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})
.
Let
\bar{\eta}\in H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})
be the image of
\eta
. Then there is
\theta\in T(K[\mathbf{x}])
such that

\bar{\eta}
is the image of
\theta
. Since
T
is a group of multiplicative type, we have
T(K[\mathbf{x}])=T(K)
. Hence

\bar{\eta}
is extended from
K
.

Assume that, moreover,
\xi\in\ker\bigl(H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G)_{0}\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],G)\bigr)
.
Then
\eta|_{\mathbf{x}=(0,\ldots,0)}\in H^{1}_{fppf}(R,G^{\prime})

has a preimage
\sigma\in T(R)
. Clearly, the image of
\sigma
in
T(K)
maps to

\bar{\eta}|_{\mathbf{x}=(0,\ldots,0)}\in H^{1}_{fppf}(K,G^{\prime})
under
\delta
.

The group
T(R[\mathbf{x}])
acts on

H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})
by right shifts, and the orbits are precisely the fibers of the map

H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G),

see
[
Se02
, §5.5]
.
Since
R
is a domain, we have
T(R[\mathbf{x}])=T(R)
.
Then
\eta\cdot\sigma^{-1}\in H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})_{0}
, and,
since
\bar{\eta}

is extended from
K
, the image of
\eta\cdot\sigma^{-1}
in
H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})
is trivial.
Hence

\eta\cdot\sigma^{-1}\in\ker\bigl(H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})_{0}\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})\bigr).

Since the image of
\eta\cdot\sigma^{-1}
in
H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G)
coincides with
\xi
, the surjectivity
of the map (
1
) is proved. The injectivity of (
1
) follows immediately from the fact that its fibers
are orbits under
T(R[x])=T(R)
.

Consider the case
(b)
. For each
S
as above, we have
an exact sequence

H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,T)\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,G^{\prime})\to H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,G)\to H^{2}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(S,T).

By
[
CTS
, Theorem 4.3]
the map

H^{2}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],T)\to H^{2}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K(\mathbf{x}),T)

is injective, hence the map

H^{2}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],T)\to H^{2}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(K[\mathbf{x}],T)
is also injective.
The rest of the proof is the same as in the previous case, with the only difference that one uses the action
of the commutative group
H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],T)\cong H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R,T)
on

H^{1}_{\text{\rm fppf}}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{\prime})
, which is well-defined since
T
is central in
G^{\prime}
, see
[
Se02
, §5.7]
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
R
be a regular semilocal domain,
K
be the fraction field of
R
. Let
G
be a reductive group over
R
.
Let
G^{sc}
be the simply connected cover of the derived group of
G
in the sense of
[
SGA3
]
.
Fix any
n\geq 1
, and write
\mathbf{x}
for the string of variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
The natural homomorphism
G^{sc}\to G
induces an isomorphism

(2)

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[\mathbf{x}],G^{sc})_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[\mathbf{x}],G^{sc})\right)\xrightarrow{\cong}\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(R[\mathbf{x}],G)_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[\mathbf{x}],G)\right).

Proof.

Let

\der(G)
be the derived group of
G
in the sense of
[
SGA3
]
.
There are two short exact sequences of
R
-groups
1\to\der(G)\to G\to\corad(G)\to 1
and

1\to C\to G^{sc}\to\der(G)\to 1
,
where
\corad(G)
and
C
are
R
-groups of multiplicative type
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXII]
.
These two short exact sequences are subject to
Lemma
2.1
.
∎

The proof of our main results crucially depends on the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
H67
,
Ni84
,
Gu
]

Let
D
be a Dedekind domain and let
K
be its fraction field. Let
G
be a simply connected quasi-split reductive group

. Then

H^{1}_{Zar}(D,G)=\ker(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(D,G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K,G))=1.

One has
\ker(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(D,G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K,G))\subseteq H^{1}_{Zar}(D,G)

,

.
By
[

(see
[

]

for a more general statement).
∎

For any commutative ring

That is,

Lemma 2.4
.

[

Let

be a Noetherian ring of Krull dimension

3.
The case of one variable

We deduce the
n=1
case of Theorem
1.2
from the following result about étale-locally trivial
principal
G
-bundles.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
be a 2-dimensional regular local ring with the fraction field
K
. Let
G
be a
reductive group over
A
such that its derived group
G^{der}
in the sense of
[
SGA3
]
is a split, i.e. Chevalley–Demazure,
semisimple group.
Then

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A[x],G)_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[x],G)\right)=\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A[x],G)_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K(x),G)\right)=1.

Proof.

The equality between kernels holds since

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K(x),G)\right)=1

by
[
CTO
, Prop. 2.2]
.
By Corollary
2.2
it is enough to prove the claim for the simply connected cover
G^{sc}
of
G^{der}
.
Since
G^{der}
is split, it follows that
G^{sc}
is split as well. So, from now on, we assume that
G=G^{sc}

is a simply connected split semisimple group.

The kernel of the map
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A(x),G)
is trivial
[
St22
, Theorem 4.1]
(see
also
[
PStV
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
C1
, Prop. 8.4]
, and
[
PSt2
]
for other versions of this result).
Thus, it is enough to show that for every

\xi\in\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A[x],G)_{0}\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K[x],G)\right)

the image of
\xi
in
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A(x),G)
is trivial.

Let
s
be a regular parameter of
A
.
We have the commutative diagram

\textstyle{A[x]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A(x)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{s}[x]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A(x)_{s}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{s}(x)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K(x).}

The kernel of
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A_{s}[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A_{s}(x),G)
is trivial (again, by
[
St22
, Theorem 4.1]
).
Since
A_{s}
is a Dedekind domain,
A_{s}(x)
is also a Dedekind domain
[
La
, Ch. IV, Cor. 1.3]
, hence
the kernel of
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A_{s}(x),G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K(x),G)
is trivial by Theorem
2.3
.
Then the kernel of
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A_{s}[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K(x),G)
is trivial.
Hence the image of
\xi
in
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A(x)_{s},G)
is trivial.

Consider the Cartesian square

(3)

\textstyle{A(x)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A(x)_{s}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\widehat{A(x)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where

denotes the

, and
\widehat{A(x)}_{s}
is its localization at

.
This is a patching square for
G

[

.

We claim that the restriction of
\xi
to
\widehat{A(x)}
is trivial.
Indeed, since
\bigl(\widehat{A(x)},\,s\widehat{A(x)}\bigr)
is a henselian pair, by
[

we have

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\widehat{A(x)},G)\cong H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\widehat{A(x)}/s,G).

Furthermore, we have
\widehat{A(x)}/s\cong(A/s)(x)
is a regular domain of dimension

, and its fraction field

\Frac\left((A/s)(x)\right)\cong\Frac(A/s)(x)\cong A_{(s)}(x)/s,

. Then by Theorem

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}((A/s)(x),G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A_{(s)}(x)/s,G)\right)=1.

Since
A_{(s)}(x)
is itself a regular domain of dimension
\leq 1

we have

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(A_{(s)}(x),G)\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(K(x),G)\right)=1.

Since
\xi
is trivial over

, it follows that
\xi
is trivial over

, hence over

(A/s)(x)
. Then by (

) the restriction of

Thus,

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a regular local ring of dimension
\leq 2
, and let
s\in A
be a regular parameter. Let
\widehat{A(x)}
be the

s
-adic completion of
A
. Then the localization
D=\widehat{A(x)}_{s}
of
\widehat{A(x)}
at
s

is a regular domain of dimension
\leq 1
,
and
G(D)=E(D)
for any simply connected Chevalley–Demazure group scheme
G
.

Proof.

We have

\widehat{A(x)}/s\cong A(x)/s\cong(A/s)(x).

If
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits A=1
, then
(A/s)(x)
is a field, and hence

SL_{n}((A/s)(x))=E_{n}((A/s)(x))
for any
n\geq 2
.
If
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits A=2
, then
A/s
is a discrete valuation ring, and

(A/s)(x)
is a special principal ideal domain in the sense of
[
La
, Ch. IV, § 6]
. Consequently,

SL_{n}((A/s)(x))=E_{n}((A/s)(x))
for any
n\geq 2
by
[
La
, Ch. IV, Corollary 6.3]
.

Since
\bigl(\widehat{A(x)},\,s\widehat{A(x)}\bigr)
is a henselian pair, by
[
GiSt
, Prop. 7.7]
we have

SL_{n}(\widehat{A(x)})/E_{n}(\widehat{A(x)})\cong SL_{n}((A/s)(x))/E_{n}((A/s)(x))=1

for any
n\geq 2
.
Since, again,
(A/s)(x)
is a principal ideal domain, by
[
La
, Ch. IV, Lemma 6.1]
we have that, for every
n\geq 2
,

SL_{n}(D)=E_{n}(D)
, where
D=\widehat{A(x)}_{s}
.

Note that
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits(D)\leq 1
. Indeed,
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits(\widehat{A(x)})\leq 2

and all maximal ideals in
\widehat{A(x)}
contain
s
.
It follows that all maximal
ideals in
D
have height
\leq 1
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
we have
G(D)=E(D)

for any simply connected Chevalley–Demazure group scheme
G
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
A
be a regular ring of dimension
\leq 2
.
Let
G
be a
split (i.e. a Chevalley–Demazure) reductive group over
\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
H^{1}_{Zar}(A[x],G)=H^{1}_{Zar}(A,G)
.
In other words, every Zariski-locally trivial principal
G
-bundle over

A[x]
is extended from
A
.

Proof.

The group
G
is linear by
[
Th
, Corollary 3.2]
, since it is defined over the regular ring
\mathbb{Z}
.
Then by the generalized Quillen’s local-global principle
[
AHW
, Theorem 3.2.5]
(see also
[
Mo
]
), to prove that
\xi\in H^{1}_{Zar}(A[x],G)
is extended from
A
, it is enough to prove that
for every maximal ideal
m
of
A

\xi|_{A_{m}[x]}
is trivial. The latter holds by Theorem
3.1
,
since
(\xi|_{A_{m}[x]})|_{x=0}
is a Zariski-locally trivial
G
-torsor over
A_{m}
, hence trivial.
∎

4.
The
n
-variable case

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A
be a regular ring of dimension
\leq 2
such that the fraction field of
every maximal localization of
A
has characteristic
0
.
Let
G
be a
split (i.e. a Chevalley–Demazure) simply connected semisimple group over
\mathbb{Z}

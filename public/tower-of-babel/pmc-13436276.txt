TGFβ-1 induces the release of BAG3 protein by human fibroblasts
Authors: Paola Manzo, Anna Lisa Cammarota, Anna Basile, Nicoletta Del Papa, Matteo Iervolino, Vittorio Unfer, Maria Caterina Turco, Margot De Marco
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

The identification and study of molecules involved in the fibrogenic process can disclose novel biomarkers and players of the pathogenetic mechanisms underlying fibrotic diseases [1]. Recent studies support a possible role for BAG3 protein in fibrosis. Indeed, while intracellular BAG3 regulates, by interacting with several partner proteins, cell survival, autophagy and other pathways, circulating BAG3, released by some cell types following exposure to various stressors, can activate fibroblasts and macrophages [2] and its levels are high in the blood of patients affected by diffuse systemic sclerosis (SSc) [3] or idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis [4]. Furthermore, circulating BAG3 appears to behave as a biomarker of patients’ response to antifibrotic therapy, since its levels are down-modulated in patients who respond positively to Nintedanib [5]. Finally, BAG3 neutralization by anti-BAG3 antibodies results in the decrease of fibrosis in some animal models [2], suggesting a pro-fibrotic activity of the circulating protein.

To explore the mechanisms potentially responsible for BAG3 release in pro-fibrotic conditions, we investigated whether such release could be induced in human fibroblasts by the activity of the pro-fibrotic cytokine TGFβ-1. To this end, we stimulated human skin or lung fibroblasts with recombinant TGFβ-1 and analyzed BAG3 presence in cell supernatants by immunoprecipitation with an anti-BAG3 antibody. As shown in Fig. 1A, BAG3 was clearly detectable in the supernatant of TGFβ-1- stimulated cells; furthermore, its release was inhibited by cell treatment with myo-inositol, a compound capable of antagonizing the effect of TGFβ1 on the secretion of collagen and fibronectin. TGFβ-1- induced BAG3 was also detectable by ELISA (Fig. 1B).

The effects of TGFβ-1 and myo-inositol on BAG3 release appeared to run parallel to those already reported on COL1A1 expression. Indeed COL1A1 levels were enhanced by TGFβ-1 stimulation and lowered by myo-inositol administration, as shown in Fig. 1C.

These results indicate that BAG3 can be released from human fibroblasts following stimulation by TGFβ-1, disclosing a possible mechanism underlying the presence of circulating BAG3 in fibrotic diseases such as systemic sclerosis. The induction of BAG3 release by TGFβ-1 (Fig. 1) and the observed presence of circulating BAG3 in SSc patients with diffuse systemic sclerosis [3], as well as its down-modulation in response to antifibrotic therapy [5], support a potential utility of circulating BAG3 as a marker of fibrotic disease severity and response to therapy. Furthermore, the possibility that fibroblast- released BAG3 may contribute to the stimulation of the fibrotic process appears worthy of investigation.

Fig. 1BAG3 protein release is stimulated by TGFβ-1 and inhibited by myo-inositol in human fibroblasts. (A) Immunoprecipitation of BAG3 protein from lung or skin fibroblast supernatants. Human foreskin fibroblast-1 (HFF-1, ATCC) and normal human lung fibroblast (NHLF, Lonza) cells were seeded at approximately 60% confluence and incubated for 72 h at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere in medium supplemented with 0.1% serum, in the absence or presence of recombinant TGFβ-1 (20 ng/ml) and/or myo-inositol (10 mM). Supernatants were then immunoprecipitated with a monoclonal anti-BAG3 antibody and analyzed by western blotting using a polyclonal anti-BAG3 antibody. The heavy chain of the immunoprecipitating antibody was visualized as a loading control. Recombinant BAG3 protein (rBAG3) served as a positive control for immunoprecipitation. The results are representative of two independent experiments. (B) Detection of BAG3 protein by ELISA in fibroblast cell line supernatants. NHLF and HFF-1 cells were seeded at approximately 60% confluence and incubated for 72 h at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere in medium supplemented with 0.1% serum, in the absence or presence of recombinant TGFβ-1 (20 ng/ml) and/or myo-inositol (10 mM). The concentration of BAG3 protein in the supernatants was subsequently determined by ELISA. Data represent the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of technical duplicates from two independent experiments. Statistical significance was calculated using the Student’s t-test (*p < 0.001). (C) HFF-1 and NHLF cell lines were seeded at approximately 60% confluence and incubated for 72 h at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere in medium supplemented with 0.1% serum, in the absence or presence of recombinant TGFβ-1 (20 ng/ml) and/or myo-inositol (10 mM). Cells were then harvested, and total protein lysates were analyzed by western blotting (anti-COL1A1 mAb: ab260043; anti-GAPDH mAb: sc-32233). The results are representative of two independent experiments

Identifying early tumour states in a Cahn–Hilliard-reaction-diffusion model


Abstract
In this paper we tackle the problem of reconstructing earlier tumour configurations starting from a single spatial measurement at a later time. We describe the tumour evolution through a diffuse interface model coupling a Cahn–Hilliard-type equation for the tumour phase field to a reaction-diffusion equation for a key nutrient proportion, also accounting for chemotaxis effects. We stress that the ability to reconstruct earlier tumour states is crucial for calibrating the model used to predict the tumour dynamics and also to identify the areas where the tumour initially began to develop. However, backward-in-time inverse problems are well-known to be severely ill-posed, even for linear parabolic equations. Moreover, we also face additional challenges due to the complexity of a non-linear fourth-order parabolic system. Nonetheless, we can establish uniqueness by using logarithmic convexity methods under suitable a priori assumptions. To further address the ill-posedness of the inverse problem, we propose a Tikhonov regularisation approach that approximates the solution through a family of constrained minimisation problems. For such problems, we analytically derive the first-order necessary optimality conditions. Finally, we develop a computationally efficient numerical approximation of the optimisation problems by employing standard C 0 C^{0} -conforming first-order finite elements. We conduct numerical experiments on several pertinent test cases and observe that the proposed algorithm consistently meets expectations, delivering accurate reconstructions of the original ground truth. Key words: Cahn–Hilliard equation, reaction-diffusion equation, backward inverse problem, Tikhonov regularization, first-order optimality conditions, finite elements approximation, tumour growth models. AMS (MOS) Subject Classification: 35G31, 35Q92, 35R30, 49K20, 65M32, 92C50.

Introduction

In the last few decades, several efforts have been spent to develop mathematical models for tumour growth, in order to predict the tumour progression at its different stages of evolution by appropriately modelling the dynamics of multiple tumour cell species and their interaction with the tumour microenvironment (e.g. extracellular matrix and water). See for example the review papers
[
40
,
20
,
8
]
. These efforts aim to create computational tools to assist clinicians in enhancing diagnosis and optimising therapy schedules. The modelling approaches fall into two main categories: discrete modelling, to describe cell-based mechanisms at the molecular scales, and continuum models, to describe the multiple tumour cell species interactions and their invasive dynamics in the microenvironment at the tissue scale.

Since tumours consist of multiple interacting phases, and due to the fact that the biomechanics of invasion of tumour cells in the microenvironment is characterised by non-sharp interfaces between the tumour and the host tissues, where tumour cells infiltrate into the parenchyma deteriorating the extracellular matrix, the natural modelling framework to describe tumour growth at the tissue level is the multiphase approach based on diffuse-interface mixture theory. In the latter approach, sharp interfaces are replaced by narrow transition layers where cell species can mix and interact due to their differential adhesive forces. The solid tumour and its microenvironment are described as a saturated medium composed of different tumour and healthy cell species and liquid phases, with possible additional phases to model growth and invasion mechanisms at different stages of evolution, like nutrients and neovascularisation (at late stages) components. The governing equations consist of mass and momentum balance equations for each phase, which, due to the diffuse interface description of interfaces between the phases, take the form of Cahn–Hilliard type system of equations. The mass and momentum equations are complemented with mass and momentum exchange terms between the phases, and appropriate constitutive laws to close the model equations. Recent theoretical and numerical studies of Cahn–Hilliard type tumour growth models, which describe tumour progression in avascular early stages, can be found e.g. in

The problem we want to address is the identification of the initial data

This step is of paramount importance in the development of patient-specific tumour forecasts, which can be then leveraged to better inform clinical decision-making

Moreover, standard monitoring strategies may not enable the assessment of the tumour status often enough to capture its growth or its treatment response in detail.
Consequently, the possibility of reconstructing earlier states of the tumour evolution could be crucial in having better-calibrated models to be later used to predict and manage the tumour dynamics.
Furthermore, the proposed backward problem could also be useful to locate with more precision the areas where tumours started growing.
For instance, this is especially useful in the case of brain tumours, where the presence of cancerous cells in different areas of the brain can affect the patient in a multitude of different ways.
Hence, locating precisely the earlier position of the tumour could give valuable suggestions to the therapy decision-making.

In practice, to reconstruct the solution to the ill-posed inverse problem, we propose a Tikhonov regularisation approach to approximate the previous problem with a family of constrained minimisation ones.
Tikhonov regularisation is a common method in approximating solutions to inverse problems
[
23
,
24
]
.
It generally consists of replacing the ill-posed inverse problem with a one-parameter family of well-posed constrained minimisation ones.
The key to this strategy is the addition of a regularising term in the minimisation functional.
In our case, due to the inherent structure of the phase field system, we use the Ginzburg–Landau energy as a regularisation term for
\varphi_{0}
and the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-norm for
\sigma_{0}
.
More precisely, we consider the following problem:

\begin{split}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})=\argmin_{(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\,\in\,\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}}&\Bigg(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\lVert\varphi(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\lVert\sigma(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad+\alpha_{1}\int_{\Omega}\Big(F(\varphi_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\lVert\sigma_{0}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Bigg),\end{split}

(1.7)

where
(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))=\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}=\left\{\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid 0\leq\varphi_{0}\leq 1\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}\times\left\{\sigma_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid 0\leq\sigma_{0}\leq 1\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}

Here,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0
are two non-negative parameters that can be used to calibrate the relative orders of magnitude of the variables, while
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
are the Tikhonov-regularisation parameters.
We assume to be reconstructing unknown initial data
(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
belonging to the admissible set
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
. Hence, they are assumed to have minimal regularity for the existence of weak solutions, as well as some physical bounds representing the fact that they are respectively a phase field and a concentration of nutrient.
Even if we can prove uniqueness in the more regular class
H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
, we choose to reconstruct the initial data in the broader class introduced above.
The motivation behind such a choice is twofold.
First of all, for application purposes, it may be useful to allow the reconstruction of less regular initial data in some realistic situations.
Secondly, in view of the numerical applications, we wanted to avoid putting higher-order regularisation terms in the minimisation functional, because they would then impose the employment of computationally demanding discretisation schemes.
Nevertheless, regarding the constrained minimisation problem (
1.7
), we first show the existence of minimisers (Theorem
5.2
) and then characterise such minimisers through first-order necessary optimality conditions (Theorem
5.8
).
The key ingredient in proving these kinds of results is the Fréchet differentiability of the forward map
\mathcal{R}:H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, together with the study of the linearised and adjoint systems.
In particular, since we just assume the initial data to be in
H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, we mention that in Theorem
5.5
we prove the Fréchet differentiability of the solution operator starting only from weak solutions to (

To do this, we need some stricter hypotheses on the non-linearities in the system, which, however, still encompass the physically relevant cases (

This fact can be seen as a new technical achievement of this paper, as in optimal control applications of this kind of model the Fréchet-differentiability of the forward map was always proved starting from strong solutions

We also emphasize that, although Gateâux differentiability might suffice to establish optimality conditions for the Tikhonov-regularised problem, we endeavour to prove the Fréchet differentiability of the forward map.
This property is valuable as it provides additional insights into the inverse problem and is crucial in developing convergent and robust reconstruction algorithms

We finally mention that a similar Tikhonov-regularised problem was considered in

In comparison, here we not only derive the optimality conditions, but also prove some theoretical results on the inverse problem and perform a numerical study to explore the practical feasibility of the reconstruction.
Indeed, the last sections of this paper are devoted to the numerical discretisation of the model (

)–(

In order to design a computationally efficient numerical approximation of the optimisation problem, we employ standard

-conforming first-order finite elements to discretise the forward and adjoint problem in space and a backward Euler scheme in time, together with a mesh refinement technique to better track the diffuse interface

We observe that the use of

-conforming finite elements does not allow us to consider initial data and regularisation terms of class

at the discrete level. This would require

Then, we derive the discrete version of the optimality conditions, employing a “first discretise then optimise” strategy (see e.g.

Consequently, we conduct numerical experiments in some relevant test scenarios, getting highly encouraging results on the performance of the reconstruction algorithm.
We must observe that, while the tumour distribution at time

can be obtained by segmentation of conventional MRI data, the distribution of nutrients, e.g. oxygen, or metabolites associated with tumour growth at time

are more difficult to identify and may be inferred through non-conventional MR data, like perfusion MRI and MR spectroscopy, which are scarcely available from retrospective datasets acquired in clinical protocols. Also, the reconstruction of the nutrient distribution from the blood volume maps obtained from the perfusion MRI is difficult due to the presence of edema and blood-brain-barrier leakage induced by tumour growth. Hence, in our numerical tests, after considering a few test cases where we assume to have measurements of both
\varphi(T)
and

, we will further investigate the behaviour of the numerical algorithm (like convergence, dependence from the regularization parameter and the final time

In our numerical experiments, we explore the behaviour of the algorithm by varying in different ways all the following parameters: the Tikhonov regularisation parameter, the final time horizon, the position of the initial guess and the spatial dimension (

We also consider the case in which the end-time measurement is affected by some noise, confirming that the Tikhonov-regularised problem acts as a regularisation method

In all situations, the proposed algorithm performed within the expectations, showing faithful reconstructions of the original ground truth.
Hence, we believe this is a first promising step in the direction of applying these techniques to more sophisticated models and eventually to real medical data.

Mathematical model

In the present paper, we consider the tumour growth model introduced in
[
35
]
, describing avascular tumour growth and based on a four-phase continuum mixture composed by a tumour cell phase, with volume fraction
\varphi
, and a healthy cell phase, with volume fraction
h
, together with nutrient-rich and nutrient-poor extracellular water phases, with volume fractions
\sigma
and

respectively. The mixture is closed and saturated, and as a further assumption the total volume concentration of cells is assumed to be constant, i.e.
\varphi+h=C
, and hence
\sigma+w=1-C

C
and

0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-M_{\sigma}\Delta\mu_{\sigma}=\gamma_{\sigma}\hskip 20.00003pt

The first term in (

The function

is generally a double-well potential with equal minima on the pure phases, such as

F(\varphi)=\frac{1}{4}\varphi^{2}(\varphi-1)^{2}.

(2.8)

The system, without source terms, can be seen as an
H^{-1}
-gradient flow of the free energy (
2.7
).
As one can see, the energy penalises frequent transitions on the interface and drives the evolution of
\varphi
towards the minima of

.
Moreover, the chemotactic term related to the coefficient
\chi\geq 0

A further assumption in

]
is that the mass exchange terms in (

) are given as chemical kinetic rates of the form

\gamma_{\varphi}=\delta P_{0}P(\varphi)\left(\mu_{\sigma}-\mu_{\varphi}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{\sigma}=-\gamma_{\varphi},

where

With assumption (

is constant and the dynamics is fully dissipative, i.e. the free energy of the system is decreasing along solutions. The latter property is peculiar to system (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\frac{M_{\varphi}}{M_{\sigma}}\Delta\mu=\tilde{\delta}P(\varphi)\left(\frac{\sigma}{\tilde{\delta}}+\tilde{\chi}(1-\varphi)-\mu\right)-\tilde{c}(x,t)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\hskip 20.00003pt

in
Q_{T}
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mu=-\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta\varphi+F^{\prime}(\varphi)-\tilde{\chi}\sigma\hskip 20.00003pt

in
Q_{T}
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta\sigma+\tilde{\chi}\Delta\varphi=-\tilde{\delta}P(\varphi)\left(\frac{\sigma}{\tilde{\delta}}+\tilde{\chi}(1-\varphi)-\mu\right)+\tilde{\kappa}(1-\sigma)\hskip 20.00003pt

in
Q_{T}
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\mu=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\sigma=0\hskip 20.00003pt

in

,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

in
\Omega
.

(2.16)

Since in the following analysis all the a-dimensional combinations of parameters in (

(i.e.

With the above-mentioned choices, the tumour growth system then takes the form (

3
Preliminaries and weak well-posedness

We first introduce some notation that will be used throughout the paper.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N=2,3
, be an open and bounded domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
boundary and outward unit normal vector
\boldsymbol{n}
.
Let
T>0
be the final time and denote
Q_{t}=\Omega\times(0,t)
and
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\Omega\times(0,t)
, for any
t\in(0,T]
.
We recall the usual conventions regarding the Hilbertian triplet used in this context.
If we define

H=L^{2}(\Omega),\hskip 10.00002ptV=H^{1}(\Omega),\hskip 10.00002ptW=\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\mid\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},

then we have the continuous and dense embeddings:

W\hookrightarrow V\hookrightarrow H\cong H^{*}\hookrightarrow V^{*}\hookrightarrow W^{*}.

We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
the duality pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
and by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
the scalar product in
H
.
Regarding Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, we will use the notation
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
for the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norm and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}
for the
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
-norm, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Moreover, by standard elliptic regularity estimates, we equip
W
with the equivalent norm

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W}:=\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{H}.

Finally, we recall the Riesz isomorphism
\mathcal{N}:V\to V^{*}
:

\langle\mathcal{N}u,v\rangle_{V}:=\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\hskip 10.00002pt\forall u,v\in V.

It is well-known that for
u\in W
we have
\mathcal{N}u=-\Delta u+u\in H
and that the restriction of
\mathcal{N}
to
W
is an isomorphism from
W
to
H
.
Additionally, by the spectral theorem, there exists a sequence of eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots
, with
\lambda_{j}\to+\infty
, and a family of eigenfunctions
w_{j}\in W
such that
\mathcal{N}w_{j}=\lambda_{j}w_{j}
, which forms an orthonormal Schauder basis in
H
and an orthogonal Schauder basis in
V
.
In particular,
w_{1}
is constant.
We also recall some useful inequalities that will be used throughout the paper:

•

Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
bounded Lipschitz,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq r,q\leq\infty
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq j\leq m
and
j/m\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{N}+\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{N}\right)\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{q},

then

\lVert D^{j}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\lVert f\rVert^{\alpha}_{W^{m,r}(\Omega)}\lVert f\rVert^{1-\alpha}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

(3.1)

In particular, we recall the following versions with
N=2,3
:

\begin{split}&\lVert f\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert^{1/2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert f\rVert^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ if }N=2,\\
&\lVert f\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert^{1/2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert f\rVert^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ if }N=3.\end{split}

As mentioned in the introduction, we consider for simplicity the a-dimensionalised tumour growth model with all constants, aside from
\chi
, equal to
1
. Namely, we examine the equations (
1.1
)–(
1.5
), which we recall here for the reader’s convenience:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\Delta\mu=P(\varphi)\left(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\right)-c(x,t)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\hskip 20.00003pt

in
Q_{T}
,

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\varphi+F^{\prime}(\varphi)-\chi\sigma\hskip 20.00003pt

in
Q_{T}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta\sigma+\chi\Delta\varphi=-P(\varphi)\left(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\right)+(1-\sigma)\hskip 20.00003pt

in
Q_{T}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\mu=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\sigma=0\hskip 20.00003pt

in
\Sigma_{T}
,

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

in
\Omega
.

)–(
1.5
):

\chi\geq 0
,

and

boundary.

F\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and there exist

\lvert F^{\prime}(y)\rvert\leq c_{3}F(y)+c_{4}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for any $y\in\mathbb{R}$.}

\mathbbm{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

\varphi_{0}\in V
and

.

Remark 3.1
.

Observe that, by

,

has polynomial growth up to degree

We have the following result about the existence of weak solutions for (

)–(

Theorem 3.2
.

Under assumptions
A1
–
A7
, there exists a weak solution
(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
), such that

\displaystyle\varphi\in H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;V),

\displaystyle\sigma\in H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),

which satisfies

\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }V\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }H

and the following variational formulation for a.e.

and for any
\zeta\in V
:

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{t},\zeta\rangle_{V}+(\nabla\mu,\nabla\zeta)_{H}=(P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu),\zeta)_{H}-(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)c,\zeta)_{H},

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\mu,\zeta)_{H}=(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\zeta)_{H}+(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\zeta)_{H}-\chi(\sigma,\zeta)_{H},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{t},\zeta\rangle_{V}+(\nabla\sigma-\chi\nabla\varphi,\nabla\zeta)_{H}=-(P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu),\zeta)_{H}+(1-\sigma,\zeta)_{H}.

(3.4)

In particular, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the parameters of the model and on the data
\varphi_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
, such that:

\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))}+\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}\\
&\quad+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}\leq C.\end{split}

(3.5)

Proof.

The argument is heavily inspired by those of
[
26
, Theorem 1]
and
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
.
One should just highlight the differences due to the presence of the additional source terms.
For this reason, we postpone the proof of Theorem

to the Appendix.
We just mention that, in our regularity setting, the initial value
\varphi(0)
makes sense in

due to the embedding

, where with

we denote the space of continuous functions with respect to the weak topology in

.
∎

Next, we pursue a continuous dependence result on the initial data for solutions to (

)–(

).

To get a strong enough continuous dependence estimate, we need to assume a bit more regularity on the functions

and

We need this to be able to prove the Fréchet differentiability of the forward map, starting only from weak solutions to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
).
In Section
5
, we will then use such properties to treat a Tikhonov-regularised version of the proposed inverse problem with minimal assumptions on the initial data.
Indeed, we now further assume that:

A8.

P\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbbm{h}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, we call
P_{\infty}=\lVert P\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
,
P^{\prime}_{\infty}=\lVert P^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}_{\infty}=\lVert\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Then, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
and
A8
. Let
{\varphi_{0}}_{1}
,
{\sigma_{0}}_{1}
and
{\varphi_{0}}_{2}
,
{\sigma_{0}}_{2}
be two sets of data satisfying
A7
and let
(\varphi_{1},\mu_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(\varphi_{2},\mu_{2},\sigma_{2})
two corresponding weak solutions as in Theorem
3.2
. Then, there exists a constant
K>0
, depending only on the data of the system and on the norms of
\{({\varphi_{0}}_{i},{\sigma_{0}}_{i})\}_{i=1,2}
, but not on their difference, such that

\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}+\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\\
&\quad+\lVert\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}\leq K\left(\lVert{\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert{\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}\rVert_{H}\right).\end{split}

(3.6)

Proof.

Let
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
,
\mu=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
,
\sigma=\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}
,
\varphi_{0}={\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}
and
\sigma_{0}={\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}
, then, up to adding and subtracting some terms, they solve:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\Delta\mu=P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi-\mu)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+(P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2}))(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2})-(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2}))c\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{in }Q_{T},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\varphi+F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})-\chi\sigma\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{in }Q_{T},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta\sigma+\chi\Delta\varphi=-P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi-\mu)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-(P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2}))(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2})-\sigma\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{in }Q_{T},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\mu=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\sigma=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{on }\Sigma_{T},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega.

(3.11)

Now, to deduce our continuous dependence estimate, we test (
3.7
) by
\varphi
, (
3.8
) by
\Delta\varphi
, (
3.9
) by
\sigma
and we sum them up to obtain:

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\nabla\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}\\
&\quad=(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}),\Delta\varphi)_{H}-2\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi)_{H}+(P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi-\mu),\varphi-\sigma)_{H}\\
&\qquad+((P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2}))(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}),\varphi-\sigma)_{H}\\
&\qquad-((\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2}))\,c,\varphi)_{H}-(\sigma,\sigma)_{H}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Hence, we proceed with estimating all the terms on the right-hand side one by one.
First, by using Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we see that

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}),\Delta\varphi)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert(\sup_{z\in[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]}F^{\prime\prime}(z))\,\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\sup_{z\in[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]}F^{\prime\prime}(z)\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\underbrace{\left(1+\lVert\varphi_{1}\rVert^{s-2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert^{s-2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}}_{:=g(t)}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H},

where we used Lagrange’s Theorem, since
F\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
by
A2
, and the growth bound given by hypothesis
A4
for
s\in[2,6)
.
Now, in order to use Gronwall’s lemma later, we need the function
g(t)
above to be uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(0,T)
.
Indeed, observe that, by Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s interpolation inequality (
3.1
) with
p=\infty
,
j=0
,
N=3
,
r=2
,
m=3
,
\alpha=1/4
and
q=6
, it follows that

L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow L^{8}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)).

(3.13)

Then, since by (
3.5
) and (
3.13
)
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are bounded in
L^{8}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
, we have that
g(t)\in L^{1}(0,T)
if
2(s-2)\leq 8
, which is guaranteed by hypothesis
A4
.
Next, we can easily estimate the second and the last term of (
3.12
) by means of Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities:

-2\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi)_{H}-(\sigma,\sigma)_{H}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}.

Observe also that, by proceeding similarly as above, we can say that

\lVert\mu\rVert_{H}\leq\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}\leq\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\sqrt{g(t)}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}.

(3.14)

Hence, by using (
3.14
) and Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we can estimate the third term on the right-hand side of (
3.12
) as follows:

\displaystyle(P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi-\mu),\varphi-\sigma)_{H}\leq P_{\infty}\left(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\mu\rVert_{H}\right)\left(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\left(\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\sqrt{g(t)}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}\right)\left(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\left(1+\sqrt{g(t)}+g(t)\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H},

where
\sqrt{g(t)}+g(t)
is still uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(0,T)
.
Next, by using hypothesis
A8
, Sobolev embeddings and Hölder and Young’s inequalities, we can estimate also the fourth term:

\displaystyle((P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2})(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}),\varphi-\sigma)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\lVert P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{H}\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\sigma\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq CP^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert_{V}\left(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{V}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{V}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert_{V}\left(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{V}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert_{V}\left(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{V}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{V}+C\left(1+\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H},

where
\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert^{2}_{V}\in L^{1}(0,T)
by (
3.5
).
Finally, by using again hypothesis
A8
, we also have that

((\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2}))c,\varphi)_{H}\leq\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}_{\infty}c_{\infty}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq C\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}.

Therefore, by putting all together and integrating on
(0,t)
for any
t\in(0,T)
, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\sigma_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(1+\sqrt{g(t)}+g(t)+\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})-\mu_{2}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t,

which, by Gronwall’s lemma, eventually implies that

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}+\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}\leq C\left(\lVert{\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert{\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

Moreover, starting from (
3.14
) and integrating in time, we can also easily see that

\displaystyle\lVert\mu\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\chi^{2}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\left(\lVert{\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert{\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H}\right),

since
g\in L^{1}(0,T)
.
Finally, by comparison in (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), it is also straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})}+\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})}\leq C\left(\lVert{\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert{\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Remark 3.4
.

Since we have the embeddings
H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)
and
H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)
, the continuous dependence estimate (
3.6
) also implies that

\lVert\varphi_{1}(T)-\varphi_{2}(T)\rVert_{H}+\lVert\sigma_{1}(T)-\sigma_{2}(T)\rVert_{H}\leq K\left(\lVert{\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}\rVert_{V}+\lVert{\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}\rVert_{H}\right).

This means that the forward operator
\mathcal{R}:V\times H\to H\times H
such that
\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})=(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))
is well-defined and moreover it is Lipschitz continuous.
Observe that, in estimate (
3.6
), the norm of the difference of the initial data
{\varphi_{0}}_{1}
and
{\varphi_{0}}_{2}
is taken in
H
, however, to get such estimate, we needed to assume that
{\varphi_{0}}_{1},{\varphi_{0}}_{2}\in V
.
For this reason, we can only say that
\mathcal{R}
is Lipschitz continuous from
V\times H
and not from
H\times H
.

4
Uniqueness for the inverse problem

In this section, we prove uniqueness results for the inverse problem of identifying the initial data by setting the value of the solution at the final time
T
.
Hence, we aim to prove the injectivity of the forward operator
\mathcal{R}:V\times H\to H\times H
such that
\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})=(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))
, which is equivalent to a backward uniqueness property for the system (
1.1
)–(
1.5
).
Unfortunately, to prove such a result, we need to assume more regularity on the initial data, namely that
\varphi_{0}\in W
and
\sigma_{0}\in V
.
We stress that this is a common procedure in the analysis of inverse problems and it is linked to our chosen proof strategy, involving logarithmic convexity methods
[
1
]
.
Indeed, we first obtain more regular solutions to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) in Theorem
4.1
and then prove injectivity of the operator
\mathcal{R}:W\times V\to H\times H
in Theorem
4.4
.
Finally, as a byproduct of the backward uniqueness result, we get a stronger continuous dependence estimate, which would then pave the way to a Lipschitz stability estimate for the inverse problem in finite-dimensional subspaces through standard results
[
13
]
.
We discuss this possibility in Remark
4.8
at the end of this section.
Such results further cement the fact that the inverse problem is well-posed, at least in more regular spaces, thus it is reasonable to try and approximate its solutions through a Tikhonov regularisation procedure, as done in the next Section
5
.

4.1
Strong solutions

In this subsection, we prove higher regularity results for the solutions to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
).
To do this, we further assume the following:

B1.

F\in\mathcal{C}^{4}(\mathbb{R})
.

B2.

P,\mathbbm{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and the exponent
q
of hypothesis
A5
is such that
q\in[1,2]
.

B3.

\varphi_{0}\in W
and
\sigma_{0}\in V
.

Then, we have the following result about strong solutions to our tumour growth model.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under assumptions
A1
–
A6
and
B1
–
B3
, a solution
(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) enjoys the following higher regularities:

\displaystyle\varphi\in H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];W)\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;W),

\displaystyle\sigma\in H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

In particular, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the parameters of the model and on the data
\varphi_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
, such that:

\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W)\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\Omega))}+\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;W)}\\
&\quad+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}\leq C.\end{split}

(4.1)

Proof.

We proceed again by formal estimates; to be rigorous, one should go back to a Galerkin discretisation scheme, as hinted in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
First, we consider equation (
1.3
) and observe that it can be seen as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta\sigma=-\chi\Delta\varphi-P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu)+(1-\sigma)\hskip 10.00002pt

in
Q_{T}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\sigma=0\hskip 10.00002pt

in
\Sigma_{T}
,

\displaystyle\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt

in
\Omega
.

In particular, due to the regularity of the weak solution given by (
3.5
), we can easily infer that the right-hand side is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;H)
.
Indeed, the only non-trivial term to check is the reaction one, but thanks to the fact that
P(\varphi)
can grow up to an exponent
q\leq 2
by
B2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lVert P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu)\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert P(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\lVert\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\rVert_{L^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\underbrace{\left(\int_{\Omega}c_{5}(1+\lvert\varphi\rvert^{q})^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/3}}_{\in\,L^{\infty}(0,T)}\lVert\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\rVert_{V}\in L^{2}(0,T),

since
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
and
\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;V)
by Sobolev embeddings and (
3.5
).
Then, since
\sigma_{0}\in V
and the right-hand side is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;H)
, by standard parabolic regularity theory, we can immediately infer that

\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}\leq C,

(4.2)

for some constant
C>0
depending only on the initial data and the parameters of the system.

Next, for the second estimate, we test (
1.1
) by
\partial_{t}\varphi
, (
1.2
) by
\Delta\partial_{t}\varphi
and we sum them up to obtain, after cancellations, that

\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}\\
&\quad=(P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu),\varphi_{t})_{H}-(c\mathbbm{h}(\varphi),\varphi_{t})_{H}-\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi_{t})_{H}+(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\Delta\varphi_{t})_{H},\end{split}

(4.3)

where we used the notation
\varphi_{t}=\partial_{t}\varphi
for simplicity.
We can now estimate the first two terms on the right-hand side of (
4.3
) by means of Hölder and Young’s inequalities, indeed:

\displaystyle(P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu),\varphi_{t})_{H}-(c\mathbbm{h}(\varphi),\varphi_{t})_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\lVert P(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\lVert\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\rVert_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi_{t}\rVert_{H}+c_{\infty}\mathbbm{h}_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\varphi_{t}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\varphi_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert P(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\lVert\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\rVert^{2}_{V}+C,

where
\lVert P(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{3}(\Omega)}
is again uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T)
by (
3.5
) and
B2
, hence we can bound it by a constant (depending on
T
).
For the last two terms, instead, we use Leibinz’s rule to exchange time-derivatives and then Hölder and Young’s inequalities to see that

\displaystyle-\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi_{t})_{H}+(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\Delta\varphi_{t})_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(-\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi)_{H}+(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\Delta\varphi)_{H}\right)+\chi(\sigma_{t},\Delta\varphi)_{H}-(F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\varphi_{t},\Delta\varphi)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(-\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi)_{H}+(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\Delta\varphi)_{H}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\varphi_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\sigma_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H},

where we note that
\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\in L^{1}(0,T)
by (
3.13
) and
A4
.
Hence, starting from (
4.3
), we deduced that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\varphi_{t}\rVert+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\overbrace{\left(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi)_{H}-(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\Delta\varphi)_{H}\right)}^{:=g(t)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\lVert\sigma_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\rVert^{2}_{V}+C.

Moreover, by integration by parts and Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we can see that

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\chi(\sigma,\Delta\varphi)_{H}-(F^{\prime}(\varphi),\Delta\varphi)_{H}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}-\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}-C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+(F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi)_{H}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}-C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}-C\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H},

where we used the fact that
F^{\prime\prime}(y)\geq-C
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
for some constant
C>0
, which easily follows by hypothesis
A4
.
Additionally, since
\varphi_{0}\in W\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
F\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
, we also have that

g(0)\leq C\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{W}+C\lVert\sigma_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{0})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{V}\leq C\left(\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert\sigma_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

By putting all together and integrating on
(0,t)
, for any
t\in(0,T)
, we finally arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\varphi_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\left(\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert\sigma_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H}\right)+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\sigma_{t}\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\rVert^{2}_{V}\,\mathrm{d}t+C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}+C\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}.

Therefore, by Gronwall’s inequality, as well as, (
3.5
) and (
4.2
), we conclude that

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W)}\leq C,

(4.4)

for some constant
C
, depending only on the initial data and on the parameters.
Moreover, by comparison in (
1.1
), it is now easy to see that also

\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;W)}\leq C.

(4.5)

In particular, since by Sobolev embeddings
W\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, from (
4.4
) we can now infer that

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C,

(4.6)

hence, given that
F\in\mathcal{C}^{4}(\mathbb{R})
, we also have that

\lVert F^{(i)}(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for any $i=1,\dots,4$.}

Finally, we formally take the laplacian of (
1.2
) and then estimate the norm of the bilaplacian of
\varphi
in
H
as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta^{2}\varphi\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Delta\mu\rVert_{H}+\lVert\Delta F^{\prime}(\varphi)\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\Delta\sigma\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Delta\mu\rVert_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi)\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\Delta\sigma\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Delta\mu\rVert_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\chi\lVert\Delta\sigma\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Delta\mu\rVert_{H}+C\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+C\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+C\lVert\Delta\sigma\rVert_{H}.

Then, we observe that the right-hand side is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T)
, due to (
4.2
), (
4.5
) and (
4.4
), therefore, by elliptic regularity theory, we deduce that

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\Omega))}\leq C.

(4.7)

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

4.2
Backward uniqueness

We can now state and prove our result about backward uniqueness for (
1.1
)–(
1.5
).
However, we need the following additional hypothesis:

B4.

The chemotaxis coefficient
\chi
is such that
\chi^{2}<2
.

Hypothesis
B4
is needed to make the leading differential operator of (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) uniformly elliptic, even in the presence of chemotaxis.
Such a condition is a cornerstone of our proof strategy, relying on the logarithmic convexity method
[
1
]
.
We later comment on the biological feasibility of this hypothesis in Remark
4.6

Then, under this additional assumption, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
,
B1
–
B2
and
B4
.
Let
(\varphi_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(\varphi_{2},\sigma_{2})
be two solutions of (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) corresponding to two pairs of initial data
(\varphi_{0}^{i},\sigma_{0}^{i})
for
i=1,2
, satisfying hypothesis
B3
.

If
(\varphi_{1},\sigma_{1})(T)=(\varphi_{2},\sigma_{2})(T)
, then
(\varphi_{1},\sigma_{1})(t)=(\varphi_{2},\sigma_{2})(t)
for any
t\in[0,T]
. In particular,
(\varphi_{0}^{1},\sigma_{0}^{1})=(\varphi_{0}^{2},\sigma_{0}^{2})
in
W\times V
.

Proof.

We adapt the arguments of
[
45
, Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2]
, which are based on the logarithmic convexity method by Agmon and Nirenberg
[
1
]
.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.3
we let
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
,
\mu=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
,
\sigma=\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}
,
\varphi_{0}={\varphi_{0}}_{1}-{\varphi_{0}}_{2}
and
\sigma_{0}={\sigma_{0}}_{1}-{\sigma_{0}}_{2}
.
Then, we rewrite the system (
3.7
)–(
3.11
) solved by the difference of two solutions in the following way:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi+\Delta^{2}\varphi+\chi\Delta\sigma=f_{\varphi}\hskip 10.00002pt

in
Q_{T}
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta\sigma+\chi\Delta\varphi=f_{\sigma}\hskip 10.00002pt

in
Q_{T}
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\Delta\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\sigma=0\hskip 10.00002pt

on
\Sigma_{T}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt

in
\Omega
,

(4.11)

where the right-hand sides are

\displaystyle f_{\varphi}=\Delta(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1}))-\Delta(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}))+P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi+\Delta\varphi-(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}))+\chi\sigma)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+(P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2}))(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})+\Delta\varphi_{2}-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})+\chi\sigma_{2})-c(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2})),

\displaystyle f_{\sigma}=-P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi+\Delta\varphi-(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}))+\chi\sigma)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt-(P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2}))(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})+\Delta\varphi_{2}-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})+\chi\sigma_{2})-\sigma.

In particular, we note that boundary conditions (
3.10
) are equivalent to those in (
4.10
).
We now frame system (
4.8
)–(
4.11
) in the abstract setting of
[
45
, Section 6.1]
.
To do this, we introduce the Hilbert spaces

\mathbb{H}:=H\times H,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{V}:=W\times V,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{W}:=\{u\in H^{4}(\Omega)\mid\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\Delta u=0\}\times W,

as well as the linear self-adjoint unbounded operator
\mathbb{A}:D(\mathbb{A})=\mathbb{W}\subset\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
such that

\mathbb{A}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{2}+\mathrm{Id}&\chi\Delta\\
\chi\Delta&-\Delta+\mathrm{Id}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{2}u+u+\chi\Delta v\\
-\Delta v+v+\chi\Delta u\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathrm{Id}:H\to H
is the identity operator.
We now check that
\mathbb{A}
is also positive definite if
B4
holds. Indeed, by using integration by parts, boundary conditions, as well as Sobolev embeddings and Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we see that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{A}\binom{u}{v},\binom{u}{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{H}-2\chi(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{H}

\displaystyle\geq\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{V}-(1-\delta)\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{H}-\frac{\chi^{2}}{1-\delta}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\geq\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W}+\delta\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{V}-\frac{\chi^{2}}{2(1-\delta)}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W}

\displaystyle\geq\underbrace{\left(1-\frac{\chi^{2}}{2(1-\delta)}\right)}_{:=\gamma}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W}+\delta\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{V},

(4.12)

for any
0<\delta<1
.
Then, by
B4
, we can choose
0<\delta<1
such that also
\gamma>0
, which implies the positivity of
\mathbb{A}
.
In particular, to justify the positivity of
\mathbb{A}
, we used the inequality

\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H}=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W},

which holds for any
u\in W
and motivates the choice of
\chi^{2}<2
in hypothesis
B4
.
Consequently, the operator
\mathbb{A}^{1/2}
is well-defined and one can easily check that
D(\mathbb{A}^{1/2})=\mathbb{V}
and that the natural norm in
D(\mathbb{A}^{1/2})
is equivalent to the one of
\mathbb{V}
.
For the rest of this proof, we redefine the scalar product and the corresponding norm in
\mathbb{V}
as the equivalent ones in terms of the operator
\mathbb{A}
, namely

(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathbb{V}}:=(\mathbb{A}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{u},\mathbb{A}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathbb{H}},\hskip 10.00002pt\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}=(\mathbb{A}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{u},\mathbb{A}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathbb{H}},

for any
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}
.
By what we have just shown above, it is clear that there exist two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\left(\lVert u_{1}\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}\leq C_{2}\left(\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)

for any
\boldsymbol{u}=(u,v)\in\mathbb{V}
.
Now, (
4.8
)–(
4.11
) can be rewritten in abstract form as

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}+\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}=\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}},\\
&\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(0)=\boldsymbol{\uppsi}_{0},\end{split}

(4.13)

where

\boldsymbol{\uppsi}=\binom{\varphi}{\sigma},\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{\uppsi}_{0}=\binom{\varphi_{0}}{\sigma_{0}},\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}=\binom{f_{\varphi}+\varphi}{f_{\sigma}+\sigma}.

Note that the additional terms in
\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}
are due to the inclusion of the identity operator in the definition of
\mathbb{A}
.
We now aim to show that if
\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(T)=0
, then necessarily
\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)=0
for any
t\in[0,T]
.
To do this, we apply
[
45
, Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2]
.
Hence, we have to verify the following two conditions.
Firstly, equation (
4.13
) has to admit strong solutions with regularity

\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{V})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{W}).

(4.14)

We observe that such a regularity is already available, since
\boldsymbol{\uppsi}
is defined through the difference of two strong solutions given by Theorem
4.1
.
Secondly, the right-hand side has to satisfy the following estimate

\lVert\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\alpha(t)\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{V}}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{a.e.\penalty\ $t\in(0,T)$}

(4.15)

for some function
\alpha\in L^{2}(0,T)
.
Therefore, we now need to prove (
4.15
), namely we estimate:

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\lVert f_{\varphi}\rVert_{H}+\lVert f_{\sigma}\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\lVert\Delta(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1}))-\Delta(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}))\rVert_{H}+2\lVert P(\varphi_{1})(\sigma-\chi\varphi+\Delta\varphi-(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}))+\chi\sigma)\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+2\lVert(P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2}))(\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})+\Delta\varphi_{2}-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})+\chi\sigma_{2})\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\lVert c(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}\,.

Now, by computing the laplacians and using the local Lipschitz continuity of
F^{\prime\prime}
and
F^{\prime\prime\prime}
, guaranteed by
B1
, the fact that
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are globally bounded by Remark
4.2
, as well as Hölder’s inequality, we see that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{1})\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert(F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{2}))\Delta\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi_{1})(\nabla\varphi_{1}+\nabla\varphi_{2})\cdot\nabla\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert(F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi_{2}))\nabla\varphi_{2}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{1})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+C\lVert\Delta\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi_{1})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{1}+\nabla\varphi_{2}\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+C\lVert\nabla\varphi_{2}\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+C\lVert\Delta\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+C\lVert\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+C\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert_{W}+\lVert\varphi_{1}\rVert_{W}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert^{2}_{W}\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W},

where we extensively used the Sobolev embeddings
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,4}(\Omega)
and
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, we also estimate similarly the remaining terms:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert P(\varphi_{1})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\sigma-\chi\varphi+\Delta\varphi-(F^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2}))+\chi\sigma\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W}\right),

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert(P(\varphi_{1})-P(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\sigma_{2}+\chi(1-\varphi_{2})+\Delta\varphi_{2}-F^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})+\chi\sigma_{2}\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(1+\lVert\sigma_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert_{W}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\lVert\sigma_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert_{W}\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W},

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert c\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\lVert\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{H}\leq C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H}.

Putting it all together, we get that

\lVert\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq C\left(1+\lVert\sigma_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi_{1}\rVert_{W}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert^{2}_{W}\right)\left(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W}\right)\leq\alpha(t)\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert_{\mathbb{V}},

where

\alpha(t)=C\left(1+\lVert\sigma_{2}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi_{1}\rVert_{W}+\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert^{2}_{W}\right)\in L^{\infty}(0,T),

since
\varphi_{i}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W)
and
\sigma_{i}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)
,
i=1,2
, by Theorem
4.1
.

Hence, one can now easily use the logarithmic convexity method highlighted in
[
45
, Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2]
, with Dirichlet quotient

\Lambda(t)=\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}},

to conclude the proof of Theorem
4.4
.
For the sake of completeness, we report below the main steps of the cited logarithmic convexity method.
We recall that we aim to show that if
\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(T)=0
, then necessarily
\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)=0
for any
t\in[0,T]
.
Indeed, assume by contradiction that
\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t_{0})\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\neq 0
for some
t_{0}\in[0,T)
.
Then, by continuity,
\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\neq 0
on some interval
(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta)
and we denote by
t_{1}\leq T
the largest time for which
\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\neq 0
on
[t_{0},t_{1})
.
Hence, necessarily
\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t_{1})\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}=0
.
Now,
\Lambda(t)
is well-defined on
[t_{0},t_{1})
, then we can compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{V}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}-\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{4}_{\mathbb{H}}}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{1}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}-\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}=-\frac{\lVert\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{H}}+\frac{(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}},\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}^{2}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lVert\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{H}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lVert\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{H}}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\Lambda,

where we respectively used the equivalent definition of the norm
\mathbb{V}
through
\mathbb{A}
, the definition of
\Lambda
, the equation (
4.13
), the fact that
(\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi}-\Lambda\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}=0
, Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities and the estimate (
4.15
).
Then, by integrating on
[t_{0},t)
, for any
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
, and applying Gronwall’s inequality we deduce that

\Lambda(t)\leq\Lambda(t_{0})\exp\left\{\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\alpha^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}\leq C\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for any $t\in[t_{0},t_{1})$,}

(4.16)

since
\alpha\in L^{2}(0,T)
.
Now, we consider the function
t\mapsto\log 1/\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}
, which is still well-defined on
[t_{0},t_{1})
, and by differentiation we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log\frac{1}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=-\frac{(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}}-\mathbb{A}\boldsymbol{\uppsi},\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}=\Lambda-\frac{(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\uppsi}},\boldsymbol{\uppsi})_{\mathbb{H}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda+\alpha\Lambda^{1/2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\Lambda+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2},

where we used similar techniques to the ones used above.
Finally, we integrate again in
[t_{0},t)
, for any
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
, and we use (
4.16
) to find that

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}}\leq\log\frac{1}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t_{0})\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left(\frac{3}{2}\Lambda(s)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}(s)\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C.

This shows that
1/\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}
is bounded from above as
t\to t_{1}^{-}
, thus contradicting the fact that
\lVert\boldsymbol{\uppsi}(t_{1})\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}=0
.
The proof of Theorem
4.4
is then concluded.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Due to standard embeddings, if
(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\in W\times V
, then also
(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))\in W\times V
.
This means that Theorem
4.4
gives a uniqueness result for the inverse problem

.
However, in practical situations, one could not have such a regular measurement, which is an additional reason why in Section

H\times H
, even if the uniqueness is not guaranteed.

Remark 4.8
.

As a side comment, we mention that the strong regularity of the solutions proven in Theorem
4.1
and the strong continuous dependence estimate (

) would be enough to prove that the operator

is Fréchet-differentiable between these stronger spaces and that its Fréchet derivative is continuous as a function from
W\times V
to the space
\mathcal{L}(W\times V,H\times H)
, as similarly done in
[
10
]
.
Moreover, one should also be able to prove the injectivity of the Fréchet derivative
\mathrm{D}\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
for any fixed point
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in W\times V
.
Indeed, this can be done by proving a backward uniqueness result in the spirit of Theorem

for the linearised system which will be introduced in the following (

)–(

).
Such results, then, by a suitable version of the inverse map Theorem proved in

However, the possibility of refining this Lipschitz stability estimate by quantifying its constant, as in

5
Tikhonov Regularisation

In this section, we aim to approximate the solution to the proposed inverse problem (
1.6
) employing a Tikhonov regularisation.
This will be done in the most useful setting in practice, which is when the final measurements are taken in
H\times H
and the initial data are assumed to be in
V\times H
.
Thus, to carry out our analysis, we only use the regularity of weak solutions by Theorem
3.2
and the corresponding continuous dependence estimate by Theorem
3.3
.
In this way, we just assume the minimal assumptions on the initial data that guarantee that the final values
(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))
are well-defined in
H\times H
, namely that
(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\in V\times H
.
We recall that the results presented in the previous Section
4
show that the solution to the inverse problem is unique in the more regular class
H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence, one could also think of introducing a higher-order Tikhonov regularisation to approximate the initial data in this more regular space, where uniqueness is guaranteed.
However, this would mean that the computational cost of solving the problem numerically would become significantly higher, due to the need for higher-order finite element spaces.
For this reason and also because we are still technically able to characterise the solutions to the Tikhonov-regularised problem even by starting only from weak solutions, we stick to the above-mentioned less regular setting.

We now introduce the Tikhonov-regularisation of our inverse reconstruction problem and then study it by interpreting it as a constrained minimisation problem on the initial data.
Indeed, we study the following:

(MP)
Minimise the cost functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\alpha_{1}\int_{\Omega}\left(F(\varphi_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\sigma(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\sigma_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

subject to the constraints

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}=\left\{\varphi_{0}\in V\mid 0\leq\varphi_{0}\leq 1\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}=\left\{\sigma_{0}\in H\mid 0\leq\sigma_{0}\leq 1\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\},

and where

(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))=\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
.

Notice that, in the cost functional, we use an
L^{2}(\Omega)
-penalisation for
\sigma_{0}
, whereas a Ginzburg–Landau energy penalisation for
\varphi_{0}
.
Indeed, since we are dealing with a phase-field model, we believe that the most natural choice to stabilize the inverse reconstruction is to use the phase-field version of the perimeter of the interface
[
41
]
.
As a matter of fact, in most situations, the initial condition to be reconstructed is constituted by domains of well-separated phases, with homogeneous values given by minima of
F
.
The same procedure was already used in
[
29
]
when studying a similar problem on a phase-field model for two-phase flows.
It is also a common penalisation in many inverse problems solved by the phase-field method
[
7
,
11
,
21
]
.

Regarding the parameters at play, we make the following hypotheses:

C1.

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
, but not all equal to
0
.

C2.

\varphi_{\text{meas}},\sigma_{\text{meas}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

C3.

F\in\mathcal{C}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
and the exponent
s
in hypothesis
A4
is such that
s\in[2,4]
.

C4.

P,\mathbbm{h}\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 5.1
.

In hypothesis
C3
we only allow growth up to the fourth order for the convex part of the potential
F
, while in
C4
we assume higher regularity and boundedness for
P
and
\mathbbm{h}
.
Note that the key examples (
2.8
) and (
2.11
) are still included in this setting, up to some smoothing of
P
.
Such stronger hypotheses are needed to prove Fréchet differentiability of the operator
\mathcal{R}
, without assuming additional regularity on the initial data and consequently using the strong regularity of the solutions.
This can be seen as a technical novelty of this work, as in similar papers on optimal control problems for Cahn–Hilliard tumour growth models (e.g.,
[
16
,
14
,
18
]
) the Fréchet differentiability was always proved starting from strong solutions.
As a matter of fact, we use only the regularity of weak solutions, given by Theorem
3.2
, to characterise the solutions to the Tikhonov-regularised problem (MP).
In this way, we can resort to a lower order Tikhonov regularisation, namely only in
H^{1}(\Omega)
for
\varphi_{0}
and
L^{2}(\Omega)
for
\sigma_{0}
.
This gives great advantages when dealing with the numerical approximation of the problem.
Indeed, we can limit the computational cost of the numerical methods by avoiding using higher-order finite element spaces and using standard conforming finite element methods with the lowest order instead.

First, we prove the existence of a solution to the regularised minimisation problem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
and
C1
–
C2
. Then, the constrained minimisation problem (MP) admits at least one solution
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
, such that if
(\overline{\varphi}(T),\overline{\sigma}(T))=\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
is the solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) associated to
\overline{\varphi_{0}}
and
\overline{\sigma_{0}}
, one has that

\mathcal{J}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})=\min_{(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\,\in\,\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}}\,\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0}).

(5.1)

Proof.

First of all, we note that
\mathcal{J}
is bounded from below, since most of its terms are non-negative and hypothesis
A2
holds on
F
.
Then, we let
\{(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
be a minimising sequence such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})=\inf_{(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\,\in\,\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\geq-C,

Since
\{(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
, we have that
\{(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, therefore we deduce that there exist
\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

\varphi_{0}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\overline{\varphi_{0}}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma_{0}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\overline{\sigma_{0}}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{weakly star in $L^{\infty}(\Omega)$.}

Moreover, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{J}
is uniformly bounded along the minimising sequence, i.e.
\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\leq C
for some constant
C>0
.
In particular, this means that the sequence
\{\nabla\varphi_{0}^{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, hence we also infer that, up to a further subsequence,

\varphi_{0}^{n}\rightharpoonup\overline{\varphi_{0}}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{weakly in $H^{1}(\Omega)$.}

Finally, since
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
is convex and closed in
H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is also weakly-star sequentially closed and thus
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
.

Next, we consider the corresponding weak solutions
(\varphi^{n},\mu^{n},\sigma^{n})
to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) and observe that, since
\{(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\}
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, they are uniformly bounded in the spaces of weak solutions by Theorem
3.2
.
Therefore, by Banach-Alaoglu’s Theorem, we deduce that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\varphi^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\overline{\varphi}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{weakly star in $H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))$,}

\displaystyle\mu^{n}\rightharpoonup\overline{\mu}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{weakly in $L^{2}(0,T;V)$,}

\displaystyle\sigma^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\overline{\sigma}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{weakly star in $H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)$.}

Now, by the compact embeddings of Aubin–Lions–Simon (see
[
42
, Section 8, Corollary 4]
), it follows that
\varphi^{n}\to\overline{\varphi}
strongly in
\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega))
, for any
2\leq p<6
.
In particular, up to a further subsequence,
\varphi^{n}\to\overline{\varphi}
a.e. in
Q_{T}
, hence, due to the continuity of
F^{\prime}
,
P
and
\mathbbm{h}
, also
F^{\prime}(\varphi^{n})\to F^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})
,
P(\varphi^{n})\to P(\overline{\varphi})
and
\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{n})\to\mathbbm{h}(\overline{\varphi})
a.e. in
Q_{T}
.
Then, by standard application of Lebesgue, Egorov and Vitali’s convergence theorems, with such pieces of information and hypotheses
A4
,
A5
and
A6
on the growth of
F
,
P
and
\mathbbm{h}
respectively, one can pass to the limit in the weak formulation (
3.2
)–(
3.4
) and deduce that
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma})
is a weak solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) with
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
.
Then, we can infer that

\inf_{(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\,\in\,\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\leq\mathcal{J}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}}).

Finally, we call
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\varphi(T),\sigma(T),\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
the functional defined on
H\times H\times V\times H
such that
\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0}),\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
.
Then, we observe that
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
is weakly lower-semicontinuous as a functional defined on the space
H\times H\times V\times H
, being a sum of weakly lower-semicontinuous functionals.
Indeed, it is easy to see that most of the terms are weakly lower-semicontinuous, as they are essentially
L^{2}(\Omega)
-norms.
We only have to check the term
\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x
, but, since
V
is compactly embedded
L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p<6
and
F
has growth up to the power
s<6
, we easily deduce that it is weakly continuous in
V
.
Consequently, we can now infer that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle=\inf_{(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\,\in\,\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\leq\mathcal{J}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}}),

which means that
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
is optimal.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.2
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

As a consequence of the central part of the proof of Theorem
5.2
, we can immediately say that the operator
\mathcal{R}:V\times H\to H\times H
is weakly sequentially closed.
Namely, this means that if
(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\rightharpoonup(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
in
V\times H
and
\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0}^{n},\sigma_{0}^{n})\rightharpoonup(\xi,\eta)
in
H\times H
, then
(\xi,\eta)=\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})=(\overline{\varphi}(T),\overline{\sigma}(T))
.
This is essentially due to the fact that the limit variables
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\sigma})
still satisfy the forward system.

5.1
Differentiability of the solution mapping

We now prove Fréchet differentiability of the operator
\mathcal{R}:V\times H\to H\times H
.
As an
ansatz
for the Fréchet derivative, we first introduce the corresponding linearised system to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
), which takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\xi-\Delta\eta=P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi+P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)-\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})c\,\xi\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $Q_{T}$},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\eta=-\Delta\xi+F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi-\chi\rho\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $Q_{T}$},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho-\Delta\rho+\chi\Delta\xi=-P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi-P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)-\rho\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $Q_{T}$},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\xi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\eta=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rho=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{on $\Sigma_{T}$},

(5.5)

\displaystyle\xi(0)=h,\hskip 10.00002pt\rho(0)=k\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $\Omega$},

(5.6)

where
(h,k)\in V\times H
are increments and
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma})
is the weak solution to (
1.1
)–(

).
Then, we state a well-posedness result for the linearised system (

)–(

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
,
A8
and
B1
–
B2
. Let
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma})
be the weak solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
), corresponding to some initial data
(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\in V\times H
. Then, for any
(h,k)\in H\times H
, the linearised system (
5.2
)–(
5.6
) admits a unique weak solution, which is uniformly bounded in the following spaces

\displaystyle\xi\in H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

\displaystyle\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;H),

\displaystyle\rho\in H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),

and fulfils (
5.2
)–(
5.6
) in variational form, i.e. it satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{t},w\rangle_{W}-(\eta,\Delta w)_{H}=(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi+P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)-\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})c\,\xi,w)_{H},

(5.7)

\displaystyle(\eta,w)_{H}=-(\Delta\xi,w)_{H}+(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi,w)_{H}-\chi(\rho,w)_{H},

(5.8)

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{t},v\rangle_{V}+(\nabla\rho-\chi\nabla\xi,\nabla v)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=-(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi+P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)-\kappa\rho,v)_{H},

(5.9)

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and for any
(w,v)\in W\times V
, and
\xi(0)=h
,
\rho(0)=k
.

Proof.

Since the system is linear, we proceed formally.
The argument can then be made rigorous by employing a Galerkin discretisation scheme.
To begin, we test (
5.2
) by
\xi
, (
5.3
) by
\Delta\xi
, (
5.4
) by
\rho
and sum them up to obtain:

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}\\
&\quad=(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi,\Delta\xi)_{H}-2\chi(\rho,\Delta\xi)_{H}+(P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta),\xi-\rho)_{H}\\
&\qquad+(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi,\xi-\rho)_{H}-(\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})c\,\xi,\xi)_{H}-\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}.\end{split}

(5.10)

We now estimate the terms on the right-hand side one by one.
For the first two ones, we use Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities to infer that

\displaystyle(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi,\Delta\xi)_{H}-2\chi(\rho,\Delta\xi)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H},

where
\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\in L^{1}(0,T)
, since
\overline{\varphi}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{8}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
by (
3.13
) and
A4
.
Next, by using equation (
5.3
), we immediately see that

\lVert\eta\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq\lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\chi\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}.

(5.11)

Hence, by recalling also hypothesis
A8
, we can similarly estimate

\displaystyle(P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta),\xi-\rho)_{H}-(\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)c\,\xi,\xi)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq P_{\infty}(\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}+\chi\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{H})(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H})+\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}_{\infty}c_{\infty}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}\,.

Finally, by using again
A8
, Hölder and Young’s inequalities and Sobolev embeddings, we also deduce that

\displaystyle(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi,\xi-\rho)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq P^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq P^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{V}(\lVert\Delta\xi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\xi\rVert_{H})(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}(\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}),

where
\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(0,T)
by Theorem
3.2
.
Therefore, by integrating (
5.10
) on
(0,t)
, for any
t\in(0,t)
, and putting everything together, we get the inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\xi(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\rho(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert h\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert k\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(1+\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, by Gronwall’s inequality, we deduce that

\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}+\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

(5.12)

Then, by (
5.11
) and (
5.12
), we immediately infer that also

\lVert\eta\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

(5.13)

Moreover, by comparison in (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), by exploiting also
A8
, we additionally get that

\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})}+\lVert\rho\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

Thus, the first part of Theorem
5.4
is proved.
Uniqueness follows by the linearity of the system.

Next, we are now able to prove that the map
\mathcal{R}
is Fréchet differentiable and characterise its derivative as the solution to the linearised system.

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
and
C3
–
C4
. Then,
\mathcal{R}:V\times H\to H\times H
is Fréchet differentiable, i.e. for any
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in V\times H
there exists a unique Fréchet derivative
\mathrm{D}\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{L}(V\times H,H\times H)
such that, as
\lVert(h,k)\rVert_{V\times H}\to 0
,

\frac{\lVert\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}}+h,\overline{\sigma_{0}}+k)-\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})-\mathrm{D}\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})[h,k]\rVert_{H\times H}}{\lVert(h,k)\rVert_{V\times H}}\to 0.

(5.14)

Moreover, for any
(h,k)\in V\times H
, the Fréchet derivative at
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
in
(h,k)
is defined as

\mathrm{D}\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})[h,k]=(\xi(T),\rho(T))

where
\xi(T)
and
\rho(T)
are the solutions to the linearised system (
5.2
)–(
5.6
) with initial data
(h,k)
, evaluated at the final time.

Proof.

We observe that it is sufficient to prove the result for any small enough perturbation
(h,k)
, i.e. we fix
\Lambda>0
and consider only perturbations such that

\lVert(h,k)\rVert_{V\times H}\leq\Lambda.

(5.15)

Now, we fix
\overline{\varphi_{0}}
,
\overline{\sigma_{0}}
,
h
and
k
as above and consider

\displaystyle(\varphi,\mu,\sigma):=\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}}+h,\overline{\sigma_{0}}+k),

\displaystyle(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma}):=\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}}),

\displaystyle(\xi,\eta,\rho)\text{ as the solution to \eqref{eq:xi}--\eqref{icl} with respect to }(h,k).

In order to show Fréchet differentiability, then, it is enough to show that there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the parameters of the system and possibly on
\Lambda
, and an exponent
q>2
such that

\lVert(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))-(\overline{\varphi}(T),\overline{\sigma}(T))-(\xi(T),\rho(T))\rVert^{2}_{H\times H}\leq C\lVert(h,k)\rVert^{q}_{V\times H}.

To do this, we introduce the additional variables

\displaystyle\psi:=\varphi-\overline{\varphi}-\xi\in H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

\displaystyle\zeta:=\mu-\overline{\mu}-\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;V),

\displaystyle\theta:=\sigma-\overline{\sigma}-\rho\in H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),

which, by Theorems
3.2
and
5.4
enjoy the regularities shown above. Then, this is equivalent to showing that

\lVert(\psi(T),\theta(T))\rVert_{H\times H}^{2}\leq C\lVert(h,k)\rVert^{q}_{V\times H}.

(5.16)

By inserting the equations solved by the variables in the definitions of
\psi
,
\zeta
and
\theta
and exploiting the linearity of the involved differential operators, we infer that
\psi
,
\zeta
and
\theta
formally satisfy the equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi-\Delta\zeta=Q^{h}-U^{h}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\text{in }Q_{T},

(5.17)

\displaystyle\zeta=-\Delta\psi+F^{h}-\chi\theta\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle\text{in }Q_{T},

(5.18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta-\Delta\theta+\chi\Delta\psi=-Q^{h}-\theta\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle\text{in }Q_{T},

(5.19)

together with boundary and initial conditions:

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\psi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\zeta=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\theta=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{on }\Sigma_{T},

(5.20)

\displaystyle\psi(0)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\theta(0)=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(5.21)

where:

\displaystyle F^{h}

\displaystyle=F^{\prime}(\varphi)-F^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})-F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi,

\displaystyle Q^{h}

\displaystyle=P(\varphi)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu)-P(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)-P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi,

\displaystyle U^{h}

\displaystyle=c\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)-c\mathbbm{h}(\overline{\varphi})-c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi.

Note that, to be precise, system (
5.17
)–(
5.21
) has to be understood in weak sense, i.e. through a variational formulation, since only weak regularity is available.
Before going on, we can rewrite in a better way the terms
F^{h}
,
Q^{h}
and
U^{h}
, by using the following version of Taylor’s theorem with integral remainder for any real function

at a point

\displaystyle F^{h}=F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\psi+R_{1}^{h}(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})^{2},

\displaystyle U^{h}=c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\psi+cR_{2}^{h}(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})^{2},

and also, up to adding and subtracting some additional terms, that

\displaystyle Q^{h}

\displaystyle=P(\overline{\varphi})(\theta-\chi\psi-\zeta)+P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\,\psi

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+(P(\varphi)-P(\overline{\varphi}))[(\sigma-\overline{\sigma})-\chi(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})-(\mu-\overline{\mu})]+R_{3}^{h}(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})^{2}

\displaystyle R_{1}^{h}=\int_{0}^{1}(1-z)F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi}+z(\varphi-\overline{\varphi}))\,\mathrm{d}z,\hskip 10.00002ptR_{2}^{h}=\int_{0}^{1}(1-z)\mathbbm{h}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi}+z(\varphi-\overline{\varphi}))\,\mathrm{d}z,

Next, we observe that, since hypothesis
A4
holds with
s\in[2,4]
,
F^{\prime\prime\prime}
grows at most linearly, thus we can estimate:

\displaystyle\lVert R_{1}^{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\lvert 1-z\rvert\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi}+z(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lvert 1-z\rvert\,\mathrm{d}z\right)\left(2\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)

Therefore, since
\overline{\varphi},\varphi
are uniformly bounded in

(5.22)

meaning that
R^{h}_{1}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{8}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.
Moreover, since
P,\mathbbm{h}\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
by
C4
, we can also easily infer that

\lVert R^{h}_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lVert R^{h}_{3}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C.

(5.23)

To show (
5.16
), we now proceed by performing a priori estimates on the system (
5.17
)–(
5.21
).
Indeed, the main estimate is done by testing (
5.17
) by
\psi
, (
5.3
) by
\Delta\psi
and (
5.19
) by
\theta
and summing them up to obtain:

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}\\
&\quad=(F^{h},\Delta\psi)_{H}-2\chi(\theta,\Delta\psi)_{H}+(Q^{h},\psi-\theta)_{H}-(U^{h},\psi)_{H}-\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}.\end{split}

(5.24)

We now proceed to estimate each term on the right-hand side of (

).
First, by using
C3

) and Hölder and Young’s inequalities, we infer that

\displaystyle(F^{h},\Delta\psi)_{H}

\displaystyle=(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\psi,\Delta\psi)_{H}+(R^{h}_{1}(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})^{2},\Delta\psi)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{H}+\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)},

where
\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
by (

) and
A4
.
Moreover, after integrating on
(0,T)
, the last term in the above inequality can be handled by using Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s inequality (

) in space with

,

,

,

,

,

in the following way:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{3/2}_{W}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{5/2}_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{W}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{3/4}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{8}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{10}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\underbrace{\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{L^{8}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}}_{\leq\,C_{\Lambda}}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{3/2}_{L^{2}(0,T;W)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{5/2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}.

Hence, by (
5.22
) and the continuous dependence estimate (
3.6
), we deduce that

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{4}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{4}_{H}\right).

(5.25)

Next, we continue estimating the terms on the right-hand side of (
5.24
).
The second one can be easily bounded by means of Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, indeed

2\chi(\theta,\Delta\psi)_{H}\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}.

Then, we pass on to the third term and we use again Hölder and Young’s inequalities, together with (
5.23
) and the Sobolev embeddings
W\hookrightarrow V
,
W\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
V\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
, to deduce that

\displaystyle(Q^{h},\psi-\theta)_{H}

\displaystyle=(P(\overline{\varphi})(\theta-\chi\psi-\zeta),\psi-\theta)_{H}+(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\psi,\psi-\theta)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+((P(\varphi)-P(\overline{\varphi}))[(\sigma-\overline{\sigma})-\chi(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})-(\mu-\overline{\mu})],\psi-\theta)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+(R^{h}_{3}(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})^{2},\psi-\theta)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq P_{\infty}\lVert\theta-\chi\psi-\zeta\rVert_{H}\lVert\psi-\theta\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+P^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{H}\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\psi-\theta\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\lVert P(\varphi)-P(\overline{\varphi})\rVert_{H}\lVert(\sigma-\overline{\sigma})-\chi(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})-(\mu-\overline{\mu})\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\psi-\theta\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\lVert R^{h}_{3}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\lVert\psi-\theta\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\lVert\theta\rVert_{H}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}\right)\left(\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{H}\lVert\psi\rVert_{W}\left(\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert_{H}\left(\lVert\sigma-\overline{\sigma}\rVert_{V}+\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert_{W}+\lVert\mu-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{V}\right)\left(\lVert\psi\rVert_{W}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{V}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{V}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{V}\left(\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\zeta\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{8}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{W}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{V}+C\left(1+\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\left(\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{H}\left(\lVert\sigma-\overline{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{V}+\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert\mu-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)+C\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{V},

where
\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\in L^{1}(0,T)
by Theorem
3.2
.
Additionally, after integration on
(0,T)
, the last two terms can be bound by using the continuous dependence estimate (
3.6
) and Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s inequality (
3.1
) as follows:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{H}\left(\lVert\sigma-\overline{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{V}+\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert\mu-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\leq\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\left(\lVert\sigma-\overline{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}+\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;W)}+\lVert\mu-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}\right)\\
&\qquad\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{4}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{4}_{H}\right),\\
&\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{V}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{H}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{W}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\leq\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;W)}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{4}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{4}_{H}\right).\end{split}

(5.26)

Moreover, by comparison in (
5.18
), we can easily see that

\lVert\zeta\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\chi^{2}\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H},

(5.27)

where
\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
by (
3.13
) and
A4
and the term
\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}
can be treated as in (
5.25
).
Finally, we can estimate the fourth term in (
5.24
) with similar procedures, getting that

\displaystyle(U^{h},\psi)_{H}

\displaystyle=(c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\psi,\psi)_{H}+(cR^{h}_{2}(\varphi-\overline{\varphi})^{2},\psi)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\infty}\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+c_{\infty}\lVert\mathbb{R}^{h}_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{V}.

Hence, putting all together and integrating on
(0,t)
, for any
t\in(0,T)
, from (
5.24
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\psi(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\theta(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(1+\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert R^{h}_{1}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{4}_{V}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{H}\left(\lVert\sigma-\overline{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{V}+\lVert\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\rVert^{2}_{W}+\lVert\mu-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{V}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, by means of Gronwall’s inequality, together with (
5.25
) and (
5.26
), we conclude that

\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}+\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{4}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{4}_{H}\right).

(5.28)

Then, by (
5.27
), together with (
5.25
) and (
5.28
), we also immediately infer that

\lVert\zeta\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{4}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{4}_{H}\right).

(5.29)

Moreover, by comparison in (
5.17
) and (
5.19
), starting from (
5.28
) and (
5.29
), we additionally deduce that

\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})}+\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})}\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert^{4}_{H}+\lVert k\rVert^{4}_{H}\right).

(5.30)

Then, due to the standard embeddings
H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)
as well as
H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)
, estimates (
5.28
) and (
5.30
) imply that

\lVert(\psi(T),\theta(T))\rVert^{2}_{H\times H}\leq C\lVert(h,k)\rVert^{4}_{H\times H},

which subsequently implies (
5.16
) with
q=4>2
.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.5
.
∎

5.2
Adjoint system and necessary optimality conditions

We now introduce the adjoint system in order to deduce the necessary optimality conditions.
We fix an optimal state
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma})
corresponding to an optimal control
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
, then the associated adjoint variables
(p,q,r)
formally satisfy the following system:

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}p-\Delta q+F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q+\chi\Delta r+\chi P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt-P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r)+c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})p=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $Q_{T}$},

(5.31)

\displaystyle-q-\Delta p+P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $Q_{T}$},

(5.32)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}r-\Delta r-\chi q-P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)+r=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $Q_{T}$},

(5.33)

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}p=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}q=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}r=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{on $\Sigma_{T}$},

(5.34)

\displaystyle p(T)=\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}}),\hskip 10.00002ptr(T)=\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\hbox{in $\Omega$}.

(5.35)

First, we state a well-posedness result for the adjoint system.

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
,
A8
and
C1
–
C3
. Let
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma})
be the weak solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
), corresponding to
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
. Then, the adjoint system (
5.31
)–(
5.35
) admits a unique weak solution such that

\displaystyle p\in H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

\displaystyle q\in L^{2}(0,T;H),

\displaystyle r\in H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),

which fulfils (
5.31
)–(
5.35
) in variational formulation, i.e. it satisfies

\displaystyle\langle-\partial_{t}p,w\rangle_{W}-(q,\Delta w)_{H}+(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q,w)_{H}+\chi(\nabla r,\nabla w)_{H}+\chi(P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r),w)_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt-(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r),w)_{H}+(c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})p,w)_{H}=0,

(5.36)

\displaystyle(q,w)_{H}=-(\Delta p,w)_{H}+(P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r),w)_{H},

(5.37)

\displaystyle\langle-\partial_{t}r,v\rangle_{V}+(\nabla r,\nabla v)_{H}-\chi(q,v)_{H}-(P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r),v)_{H}+(r,v)_{H}=0,

(5.38)

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and for any
(w,v)\in W\times V
, and the final conditions
p(T)=\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})
,
r(T)=\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})
.

Proof.

Since we are dealing with a backward
linear
system, we proceed with formal estimates that can be made rigorous through a Faedo–Galerkin scheme.
For the main estimate, we choose
w=p
in (
5.36
),
w=\Delta p
in (
5.37
),
v=r
in (
5.38
) and sum them up to obtain

\begin{split}&-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\nabla r\rVert^{2}_{H}\\
&\quad=-(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q,p)_{H}-\chi(\nabla r,\nabla p)_{H}+\chi(q,r)_{H}+(P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r),-\chi p+\Delta p+r)_{H}\\
&\qquad+(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r),p)_{H}-(c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})p,p)_{H}-\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H}.\end{split}

(5.39)

We begin by observing that, by comparison in (
5.32
), it follows that

\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H},

(5.40)

where we used the boundedness of
P
.
Next, we can start estimating the terms on the right-hand side of (
5.39
).
Indeed, by using Hölder and Young’s inequalities, together with
C4
and the Sobolev embedding
W\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we can see that the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q,p)_{H}\leq\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert q\rVert_{H}\lVert p\rVert_{H}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\left(1+\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H},

\displaystyle\chi(\nabla r,\nabla p)_{H}=-\chi(r,\Delta p)_{H}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H},

\displaystyle\chi(q,r)_{H}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H},

\displaystyle(P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r),-\chi p+\Delta p+r)_{H}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H},

\displaystyle(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r),p)_{H}\leq P^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{H}(\lVert p\rVert_{H}+\lVert r\rVert_{H})\lVert p\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{W}+C\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{H}(\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H}),

\displaystyle(c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})p,p)_{H}\leq c_{\infty}\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H},

where
\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
by (
3.13
) and
A4
and
\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{H}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
by Theorem
3.2
.
Then, by gathering all estimates and integrating on
(t,T)
, for any
t\in(0,T)
, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert p(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert r(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{t}^{T}\lVert\Delta p\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t}^{T}\lVert\nabla r\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\lVert\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+C\int_{0}^{T}(1+\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{H})(\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H})\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, by applying Gronwall’s inequality, we conclude that

\begin{split}&\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)}+\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}\\
&\quad\leq C\left(\lVert\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).\end{split}

(5.41)

Moreover, by (
5.40
) and (
5.41
), we also easily deduce that

\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C\left(\lVert\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

(5.42)

Finally, by exploiting the boundedness of
P
and
\mathbbm{h}
by
C4
and arguing by comparison in (
5.31
) and (
5.33
), starting from the estimates proved in (
5.41
) and (
5.42
), we also infer that

\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;W^{*})}+\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;V^{*})}\leq C\left(\lVert\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

(5.43)

Indeed, the only non-trivial terms to treat are the following ones:

\displaystyle\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{*})}=\int_{0}^{T}\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q\rVert^{2}_{W^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{\lVert w\rVert_{W}=1}(F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q,w)_{H}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{\lVert w\rVert_{W}=1}\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert_{H}\lVert q\rVert_{H}\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{H}\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\lVert F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C\lVert q\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)},

\displaystyle\lVert P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{*})}=\int_{0}^{T}\lVert P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r)\rVert^{2}_{W^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{\lVert w\rVert_{W}=1}(P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r),w)_{H}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(P^{\prime}_{\infty}\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert_{H}\lVert p-r\rVert_{H}\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\lVert\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\lVert p-r\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\leq C\left(\lVert p\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}+\lVert r\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\right),

where
F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)
by
C3
and the fact that
\overline{\varphi}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
by Theorem
3.2
and the Sobolev embedding
V\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
, while
\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
by Theorem
3.2
.

Then, starting from the uniform estimates (
5.41
), (
5.42
) and (
5.43
), one can easily pass to the limit in the Galerkin discretisation and, due to the linearity of the system, prove the uniqueness of the solution.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Note that, due to the regularity of the solution to the adjoint system (
5.31
)–(
5.35
), the initial value
(p(0),r(0))
is well-defined in
H\times H
.

Finally, we can derive the first-order necessary optimality conditions for the constrained minimisation problem.

Theorem 5.8
.

Assume hypotheses
A1
–
A6
and
C1
–
C4
.
Let
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
be an optimal pair for
(MP)
and let
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma})
be the corresponding optimal state, i.e. the weak solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) with such
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
.
Let also
(p,q,r)
be the adjoint variables to
(\overline{\varphi},\overline{\sigma},\overline{\mu})
, i.e. the solutions to the adjoint system (
5.31
)–(
5.35
).
Then, they satisfy the following variational inequality:

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}p(0)(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{1}F^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi_{0}})(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})+\alpha_{1}\nabla\overline{\varphi_{0}}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}r(0)(\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{2}\overline{\sigma_{0}}(\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,\quad\hbox{for any $(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}$.}\end{split}

(5.44)

Proof.

We recall the notation used in Theorem
5.2
, namely that we call
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\varphi(T),\sigma(T),\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
the funtional such that
\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0}),\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
.
First, observe that the cost functional
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\varphi(T),\sigma(T),\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})
is Fréchet differentiable as a functional from the space
H\times H\times V\times H
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed, most of the terms are quadratic, so it is easy to see that they are differentiable in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The only non-trivial term is the one related to the Ginzburg–Landau energy

E(\varphi_{0})=\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\nabla\varphi_{0}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

However, it is a standard matter to prove that this is also Fréchet differentiable in
V
, with derivative given by

\mathrm{D}E(\varphi_{0})[v]=\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime}(\varphi_{0})v+\nabla\varphi_{0}\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for any $v\in V$,}

due to the growth of
F
up to the power
6
by
A4
and the Sobolev embedding
V\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
.
Next, in Theorem
5.5
we showed that the solution operator
\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})=(\varphi(T),\sigma(T))
is Fréchet differentiable from
V\times H
to
H\times H
.
Thus, the functional
\mathcal{J}:V\times H\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0}):=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0}),\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})

is also Fréchet differentiable by the chain rule.

Then, since
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}
is closed and convex and
\mathcal{J}
is Fréchet differentiable, an optimal pair
(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})
has to satisfy the necessary optimality condition

\mathrm{D}\mathcal{J}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})[(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}},\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})]\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for any $(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}$.}

By computing explicitly the derivative of
\mathcal{J}
, we equivalently get that for any
(\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\text{ad}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rho(T)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\alpha_{1}\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi_{0}})(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})+\nabla\overline{\varphi_{0}}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x+\alpha_{2}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\sigma_{0}}(\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,

where
(\xi(T),\rho(T))=\mathrm{D}\mathcal{R}(\overline{\varphi_{0}},\overline{\sigma_{0}})[(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}},\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})]
are the components of the solution to the linearised system (
5.2
)–(
5.6
) corresponding to
h=\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}}
and
k=\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}}
.

Now, to show (
5.44
), we just need to prove that the following identity holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rho(T)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{\Omega}p(0)(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}r(0)(\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x.

To do this, we consider the weak formulations of the adjoint system (
5.36
)–(
5.38
) and of the linearised system (
5.7
)–(
5.9
).
Indeed, we test (
5.36
) by
\xi
, (
5.37
) by
\eta
, (
5.38
) by
\rho
, sum them up and integrate on
(0,T)
to obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle-p_{t},\xi\rangle_{W}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(-q\Delta\xi+F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})q,\xi+\chi\nabla r\cdot\nabla\xi+\chi P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt-P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})(p-r)\xi+c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})p\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-q\eta-\Delta p\eta+P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle-r_{t},\rho\rangle_{V}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla r\cdot\nabla\rho-\chi q\rho-P(\overline{\varphi})(p-r)\rho+r\rho\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

We now integrate by parts in time and space and, after regrouping some terms, we deduce the following:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle\xi_{t},p\rangle_{W}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(-\eta\Delta p-P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)p

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi p+c\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi p\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\eta-\Delta\xi+F^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\varphi})\xi-\chi\rho\right)q\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle\rho_{t},r\rangle_{V}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla r+\chi\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla r+P(\overline{\varphi})(\rho-\chi\xi-\eta)r

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+P^{\prime}(\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\sigma}+\chi(1-\overline{\varphi})-\overline{\mu})\xi r-\rho r\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{\Omega}p(T)\xi(T)-p(0)\xi(0)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}r(T)\rho(T)-r(0)\rho(0)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

In the first three integrals, we now recognise the weak formulations of the linearised system (
5.7
)–(
5.9
), tested respectively by
p
,
q
and
r
.
Then, such terms vanish and we are only left with

\int_{\Omega}p(T)\xi(T)+r(T)\rho(T)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}p(0)\xi(0)+r(0)\rho(0)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, by recalling the final conditions (
5.35
) on the adjoint system and the initial conditions (
5.6
), with
h=\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}}
and
k=\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}}
, on the linearised system, we finally infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(\overline{\varphi}(T)-\varphi_{\text{meas}})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{2}(\overline{\sigma}(T)-\sigma_{\text{meas}})\rho(T)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{\Omega}p(0)(\varphi_{0}-\overline{\varphi_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}r(0)(\sigma_{0}-\overline{\sigma_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}x.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.8
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Through a discrete version of the variational inequality (
5.44
), we can then approximate the solution to the Tikhonov-regularised inverse problem through gradient descent methods.
This is the starting point of Sections
6
and
7
on numerical methods and experiments.

6
Numerical methods

In this section, we introduce the finite element and time discretisations of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) and of the constrained minimisation problem (
1.7
)
We recall that in Section
5
we found the optimality conditions for a Tikhonov-regularised problem aiming to reconstruct the initial data in
V\times H
.
This may be in contrast with the fact that in Section
4
, we showed that the inverse problem of reconstructing the initial data for the operator
\mathcal{R}
has a unique solution in the more regular space
W\times V
.
However, such a choice is largely motivated by the numerical implementation carried out here.
Indeed, using only
C^{0}
conforming finite elements with first-order basis functions reduces the computational cost of the algorithms, while still giving good reconstruction results. Reconstructing initial data
\varphi_{0}
in
W
would require the employment of
C^{1}
conforming finite elements to approximate the space
W
, which are non-standard and computationally demanding.

We now start introducing the discretised problem.
We adopt here the “first discretise then optimise” strategy to numerically solve the optimisation problem, which directly preserves the structure which is inherent in the infinite-dimensional optimisation problem, in contrast with straightforward “first optimise then discretise” strategies (see e.g.
[
36
, Chapter 3]
).
Let

be a quasi-uniform conforming decomposition of

into

where

We set

for a

and

,

to

and from

\Pi_{\sigma}(\overline{\sigma}_{h}^{0})\in S^{h}
such that, for all
\psi\in S^{h}
,

\displaystyle(\Pi_{\sigma}(\overline{\sigma}_{h}^{0}),\psi)=\left(r_{h}^{1}+\alpha_{2}\overline{\sigma}_{h}^{0},\psi\right).

Then, (
6.12
) can be rewritten as

(\Pi_{\sigma}(\overline{\sigma}_{h}^{0}),\tilde{\sigma}-\overline{\sigma}_{h}^{0})\geq 0.

We solve the latter variational inequalities through an iterative algorithm based on the projected gradient method, with a learning rate chosen according to a line search along the
descent directions to guarantee global convergence (see e.g.
[
12
]
).

In order to report the employed optimisation algorithm, let us introduce the projection function
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}^{\rho}:\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{h}\to\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{h}
,
i\in\{\varphi,\sigma\}
, which associates to an element
\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{h}
an element
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}^{\rho}(\varphi_{h})\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{h}
such that

\mathcal{P}_{k,i}^{\rho}(\varphi_{h}):=\pi^{h}_{[0,1]}\left(\varphi_{h}-\rho\Pi_{i}(\varphi_{h})\right),

depending on a parameter
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where

\pi^{h}_{[0,1]}(\varphi_{h}):=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}\max\{0,\min\{1,\varphi_{h}(\mathbf{x}_{j})\}\}\chi_{j}.

Here,
\chi_{j}\in S^{h}
, for
j=1,\dots,N_{h}
are the Lagrangian basis functions of
S^{h}
associated to the
j-
th node of coordinates
\mathbf{x}_{j}
, with
N_{h}
the number of nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We observe that
\chi_{j}(\cdot)\in[0,1]
.

At each iterative step of the optimisation algorithm, we refine the mesh based on a Dörfler marking scheme, with an error indicator given cell-wise as the sum of the jumps of the normal derivatives of the updated value of the initial condition
\varphi_{h}^{0}
at the previous step. See e.g.
[
28
]
for the use of this kind of indicator for mesh adaptation for the Cahn–Hilliard equation. Then, we interpolate the current initial condition on the new mesh. We thus define the following:

Problem
\mathcal{M}^{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{k},\varphi_{h})
: mesh refinement.

Given a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{k}
and
\varphi_{h}\in S^{h}
, define a new mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{k+1}
obtained by refinement according to the previously described indicator associated to
\varphi_{h}
and interpolate
\varphi_{h}
on
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{k+1}
.

The argument is heavily inspired by those of

and

.
For this reason, we proceed formally and mostly highlight the differences due to the presence of the additional source terms.
We just note that, to make the argument rigorous, one should employ a Faedo–Galerkin discretisation with discrete spaces generated by the eigenvectors of the operator
\mathcal{N}
.
At the same time, if
s\in(4,6)
in hypothesis
A4
, one should approximate the potential

with a sequence of approximating potentials having lower growth, as done in
[
26
, Lemma 2]
.
Therefore, we will just assume
2\leq s\leq 4
here and refer to the proofs of
[
26

and
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
for the more general case
2\leq s<6
.

For the main energy estimate, we insert
\zeta=\mu
in (
3.2
),
\zeta=-\partial_{t}\varphi
in (
3.3
),
\zeta=\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)
in (
3.4
) and sum them all up to obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+(\sigma,\chi(1-\varphi))_{H}\right)\\
&\qquad+\lVert\nabla\mu\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\nabla(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi))\rVert^{2}_{H}+\lVert\sqrt{P(\varphi)}(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu)\rVert^{2}_{H}\\
&\quad=-(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)c,\mu)_{H}+(1-\sigma,\sigma)_{H}.\end{split}

(A.1)

Now, for any

, we estimate the energy term

E(t)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}+F(\varphi)+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\sigma\rvert^{2}+\chi\sigma(1-\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x

from below by using hypothesis
A2
and Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities. Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}-\chi\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}\lVert 1-\varphi\rVert_{H}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c_{1}}{2}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}-\frac{c_{2}}{2}\lvert\Omega\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}-\alpha\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}-\frac{\chi^{2}}{4\alpha}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}-C

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+\left(\frac{c_{1}}{2}-\frac{\chi^{2}}{4\alpha}\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right)\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}-C,

where this holds for any

.
However, given that

, we can choose

to get:

E(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+\underbrace{\left(\frac{c_{1}}{2}-\frac{\chi^{2}}{2-4\delta}\right)}_{:=\gamma>0}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\delta\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}-C.

Next, we test (
3.3
) by
\zeta=1
, which is possible within Galerkin’s discretisation since the first eigenfunction of

A3

\mu_{\Omega}
of
\mu

\lvert\Omega\rvert\lvert\mu_{\Omega}\rvert=\left\lvert\int_{\Omega}\mu\,\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert\leq\int_{\Omega}\lvert F^{\prime}(\varphi)\rvert+\chi\lvert\sigma\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{3}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\lvert\Omega\rvert.

(A.2)

At this point, we can estimate the terms on the right-hand side of (
A.1

\displaystyle\lvert(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)c,\mu)_{H}\rvert\leq\mathbbm{h}_{\infty}c_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\mu-\mu_{\Omega}\rvert+\lvert\mu_{\Omega}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla\mu\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x+\lvert\Omega\rvert\lvert\mu_{\Omega}\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\mu\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x+C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+C,

\displaystyle(1-\sigma,\sigma)_{H}\leq C\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+C,

where we used Poincaré–Wirtinger’s inequality for the first term.
Then, by putting all together and integrating on
(0,t)
, for any

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\gamma\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}_{H}+\delta\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla\mu\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi))\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\sqrt{P(\varphi)}(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu)\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\gamma\right\}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{V}+\delta\lVert\sigma_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H}+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}F(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\sigma\rVert^{2}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t+C.

\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;V)}\leq C,\quad\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}\leq C\\
&\lVert\nabla\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C,\quad\lVert\nabla(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi))\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C\\
&\lVert F(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C,\quad\lVert\sqrt{P(\varphi)}(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi)-\mu)\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C.\end{split}

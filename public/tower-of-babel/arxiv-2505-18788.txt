Betti numbers and almost complete intersection monomial ideals
Amir Mafi and Rando Rasul Qadir

Abstract
. Let R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] be the polynomial ring in n n variables over a field K K and let I I be a monomial ideal of R R . In this paper, we present an explicit formula for the Betti numbers of almost complete intersection monomial ideals, which enables a rapid construction of their minimal free resolutions. In addition, we characterize the Cohen-Macaulayness of these ideals and also we show the same result for dominant monomial ideals.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
, and let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. We denote by
G(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}\}
the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. For a monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
, we define its support as
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}|a_{i}>0\}
.

Suppose
\Delta
is a simplicial complex, and let
I=I_{\Delta}
be its Stanley-Reisner ideal generated by all squarefree monomials
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{r}}
such that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
is not a face of
\Delta
. The Stanley-Reisner ring is then given by
K[\Delta]=R/I
. The squarefree monomial ideal
I
with a minimal prime decomposition
I=\cap_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{p_{i}}
, where each
\mathfrak{p_{i}}
is a monomial prime ideal. Then the
Alexander dual

I^{\vee}
of
I
is generated by monomials
u_{i}=\prod_{x_{j}\in G(\mathfrak{p_{i}})}x_{j}
, where
G(\mathfrak{p_{i}})
is the minimal set of variables generating
\mathfrak{p_{i}}
.

In the context of combinatorial topology, Dress
[
6
]
established that a simplicial complex
K[\Delta]
is shellable in the non-pure case if and only if
K[\Delta]

is clean. Also, Herzog, Hibi, and Zheng
[
14
]
proved that
K[\Delta]
is shellable in the non-pure case if and only if the Alexander dual ideal
I^{\vee}
has linear quotients.

The minimal free resolution of the cyclic module
R/I
is considered as:

0\longrightarrow R^{\beta_{n}}\longrightarrow\ldots\longrightarrow R^{\beta_{1}}\longrightarrow R^{\beta_{0}}\longrightarrow R/I\longrightarrow 0,

where the integers
\beta_{i}
are the
Betti numbers
of
R/I
. The Taylor resolution
[
8
, p. 439]
provides a construction for a free resolution of
R/I
. Alesandroni
[
1
, Theorem 4.4]
characterized the monomial ideals for which this resolution is minimal. Generally, the Taylor resolution is far from minimal, but it provides an upper bound for the Betti numbers of

R/I
as follows:
\beta_{i}(R/I)\leq{q\choose i}
, for
0\leq i\leq q
. In
[
13
, Remark 1.6]
, it is shown that if
\beta_{i}(R/I)={q\choose i}
for some
0\leq i\leq q
, then
\beta_{j}(R/I)={q\choose j}
for all
j\leq i
. Furthermore, Brun and Römer
[
4
, Corollary 4.1]
established a lower bound for the Betti numbers as follows:
\beta_{i}(R/I)\geq{p\choose i}
for
0\leq i\leq p
, where
p
denotes the projective dimension of
R/I
.

In this paper, we present an explicit formula for the Betti numbers of almost complete intersection monomial ideals. Furthermore, we show that an almost complete intersection monomial ideal is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if it is unmixed. Furthermore, we show the same result for dominant monomial ideals.
For any unexplained notion or terminology, we refer the reader to
[
12
]
and
[
8
]
. Several explicit examples were performed with help of the computer algebra system Macaulay 2
[
10
]
.

The following lemma is known, but we cannot find an appropriate reference; therefore, we will provide an easy proof for the readers.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
u=u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}
be monomial elements in
R
. Then
u
is a regular sequence if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq q
. In particular,

I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q})
is complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq q
.

Proof.

Since
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(u_{j}:u_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq q
, we deduce that

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):u_{i}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1})
for all
1\leq i\leq q
if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq q
. This completes the proof.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. The cyclic module
R/I
is defined
clean
if there exists a chain of monomial ideals

\mathcal{F}:I=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset I_{2}\subset\ldots\subset I_{r-1}\subset I_{r}=R

such that
I_{i}/I_{i-1}\cong R/{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
with
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is a minimal prime ideal of
I
. In other words, for
i=1,\ldots,r
, there exists a monomial element
u_{i}\in I_{i}
such that
I_{i}=(I_{i-1},u_{i})
and
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(I_{i-1}:u_{i})
. This filtration is called a
monomial prime filtration
of
R/I
. The set
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}\}
is called the support of the prime filtration
\mathcal{F}
. It is known that
\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq V(I)
. By applying
[
6
]
, for a clean filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
R/I
, one has
\operatorname{min}\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})
. Herzog and Popescu in
[
15
]
defined that the module
R/I
is
pretty clean
when there is a prime filtration

\mathcal{F}
with the following property: if
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{j}
, then
j<i
. It is clear that the clean modules are pretty clean and if
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, then
R/I
is pretty clean if and only if
R/I
is clean, see
[
15
, Corollary 3.5]
. However, this equivalence for any monomial ideal is not true, see
[
15
, Example 3.6]
. In addition, Herzog and Popescu
[
15
, Corollary 4.3]
showed that if
R/I
is pretty clean, then
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

The following definition observed in

A monomial ideal

In the outlined below, we consider that a monomial ideal

According to

The following proposition, which appeared in

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q},v)
be a squarefree almost complete intersection ideal. Then
R/I
is clean. In particular,
R/I
is pretty clean.

Proof.

Since
I
is almost complete intersection, by using Lemma
1.4
we deduce that
\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq q
. Thus
I^{\vee}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{q}\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{p}
, where

\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(x:\penalty\ x\in\operatorname{supp}(u_{i}))
and
\mathfrak{p}=(x:\penalty\ x\in\operatorname{supp}(v))
. It therefore follows that
I^{\vee}=J\cap\mathfrak{p}
, where
J=\prod_{i=1}^{q}\mathfrak{p_{i}}
. It is clear that
I^{\vee}
is weakly polymatroidal and so it is linear quotients. Hence
R/I
is clean, as desired.

Corollary 1.9

Let

be an almost complete intersection. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

is Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

I
is unmixed.

(iii)

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)

\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{min}\operatorname{Ass}(I)

(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)

is clean, we have
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{min}\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Since

is almost complete intersection, it therefore follows that

is unmixed. Also, by applying Proposition
1.7
we deduce that

is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. Hence from
[

∎

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q},v)
be an almost complete intersection ideal. Then
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
I
is non-dominant.

Proof.

Since
I
is almost complete intersection, it follows that
\operatorname{height}(I)=\mid G(I)\mid-1=q
and also
q\leq\operatorname{pd}(R/I)=p\leq q+1
. By applying
[
4
, Corollary 4.1]
, we have
\beta_{i}(I)\geq{p\choose{i+1}}
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,p-1
. Hence, if
I
is non-dominant, then by
[
1
, Corollary 4.9]

p=q
and so
\operatorname{height}(I)=\operatorname{pd}(R/I)
. Therefore,
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay. Conversely, if
I
is Cohen-Macaulay then
p=\operatorname{height}(I)=\operatorname{pd}(R/I)
and so
I
is non-dominant, as desired.

Suppose
I
is a monomial ideal with minimal generating set
G(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}\}
. The
Scarf simplicial complex
, denoted
\Delta_{I}
, is the collection of all subsets
\sigma\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}
for which the least common multiple of the corresponding generators
u_{\sigma}=\operatorname{lcm}\{u_{i}:i\in\sigma\}
is unique:

\Delta_{I}=\{\sigma\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}:u_{\sigma}\neq u_{\tau}
for all
\sigma\neq\tau\}
, see for details
[
3
]
.

For the differential maps, we follow the construction analogous to Taylor’s resolution:
d(e_{\sigma})=\Sigma_{i\in\sigma}sign(i,\sigma)\frac{u_{\sigma}}{u_{\sigma\setminus\{i\}}}e_{\sigma\setminus\{i\}}
, where
sing(i,\sigma)=(-1)^{j+1}
if
i
is the
j
th element in the ordering set
\sigma=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
, and
e_{\sigma}=e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{i_{r}}
.

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
I=\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q},v\right)
be an almost complete intersection monomial ideal such that
v\mid\operatorname{lcm}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
. Then, the Betti numbers are given by
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}
for
i=0,1
and

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{q+1-i}-\binom{q-1}{q+1-i},

for
i=2,3,\ldots,q
.

Proof.

Since
I=\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q},v\right)
is an almost complete intersection monomial ideal with
v\mid\operatorname{lcm}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
, it follows that
I
is a semidominant monomial ideal. It is evident that all the elements of the bases with homological degrees 0 and 1 appear in the minimal free resolution of
\frac{R}{I}
. Consequently, we find that
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}
for
i=0,1
. Now, we will determine the Betti numbers for
i=2,\ldots,q
. Since
I
is semi-dominant, the minimal free resolution fulfilled by Scarf complex and now by applying
[
1
, Corollary 6.7]
we deduce that
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{i}+\#L_{i-1}
, where
L_{i}=\left\{\left\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{i}\right\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\};v\nmid\right.

\left.\operatorname{lcm}\left(u_{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{j_{i}}\right)\right\}
. To find
\beta_{2}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)
, we note that since
v\nmid u_{j}
, it follows that
\#L_{1}=q=\binom{q}{1}
. Moreover,
v
divides
\operatorname{lcm}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
but does not divide
\operatorname{lcm}\left(u_{r},u_{s}\right)
for each pair
r\neq 1,2
or
s\neq 1,2
. Consequently, the only basis elements removed from the minimal free
resolution of
\frac{R}{I}
in homological degree 2 is
e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
. Therefore
\#L_{2}=\binom{q}{2}-1=\binom{q}{2}-\binom{q-2}{0}
and so
\beta_{2}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{2}+\#L_{1}=\binom{q}{2}+q-1=\binom{q+1}{q-1}-\binom{q-1}{q-1}
. To find
\#L_{3}
, we observe that since
v\mid lcm\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
, it follows that
v
divides all basis that include
\left\{u_{1},u_{2}\right\}
. Thus, we have
\#L_{3}=\binom{q}{3}-\binom{q-2}{1}
. Next, we deduce that
\beta_{3}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{3}+

\#L_{2}=\binom{q}{3}-\binom{q-2}{1}+\binom{q}{2}-\binom{q-2}{0}=\binom{q+1}{q-2}-\binom{q-1}{q-2}
. Now, to determine
\#L_{j}
for
3<j\leq q
, we again use the fact that
v\mid lcm\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
, which means
v
divides all basis containing
\left\{u_{1},u_{2}\right\}
. Consequently, we have
\#L_{j}=\binom{q}{j}-\binom{q-2}{j-2}
. Hence, we have

\beta_{j}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{j}+\#L_{j-1}=\binom{q}{j}-\binom{q-2}{j-2}+\binom{q}{j-1}-\binom{q-2}{j-3}.

Hence, we easily obtain that
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{q+1-i}-\binom{q-1}{q+1-i},
for
i=2,3,\ldots,q
,
as required.

In the following result, we will prove Proposition

Theorem 1.14
.

Let

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}
and for
i=s,s+1,\ldots,q

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{q+1-i}-\binom{q+1-s}{q+1-i}
.

Proof.

Since
I=\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q},v\right)
is an almost complete intersection monomial ideal with
v\mid\operatorname{lcm}\left(u_{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{j_{s}}\right)
, for
\left\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\right\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}
and
I

[
1

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{i}+\#L_{i-1}
, where

is defined as before. For

, it is evident that all basis elements of homological degrees

appear in the minimal free resolution of
\frac{R}{I}
. Specifically, we have

\#L_{i}=\binom{q}{i}
, which yields
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{i}+

. For

and

if there exist

for

, and so the only basis element removed from the minimal free resolution of
\frac{R}{I}
in homological degree

. Hence we deduce that
\#L_{s}=\binom{q}{s}-1=\binom{q}{s}-\binom{q-s}{0}

v\mid lcm\left(u_{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{j_{s}}\right)

q

\left\{u_{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{j_{s}}\right\}

\binom{q}{j}-\binom{q-s}{j-s}

\displaystyle\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)

\displaystyle=\#L_{i}+\#L_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\binom{q}{i}-\binom{q-s}{i-s}+\binom{q}{i-1}-\binom{q-s}{i-s-1}

\displaystyle=\binom{q+1}{i}-\binom{q+1-s}{i-s}.

Consequently, we have
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{q+1-i}-\binom{q+1-s}{q+1-i}

2\leq j<q-1
and consider the ideal

I=(x_{1}y_{1},x_{2}y_{2},\ldots,x_{q}y_{q},y_{1}\ldots y_{j})
of
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{q},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]
.
Applying Theorem
1.14
, we deduce that
\beta_{i}(I)=\binom{q+1}{i+1}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,j-2
and
\beta_{i}(I)=\binom{q+1}{q-i}-\binom{q+1-j}{q-i}
for
i=j-1,\ldots,q-1
.

Example 1.16
.

Consider an almost complete intersection ideal

I=(x_{1}^{a_{1}},x_{2}^{a_{2}},\ldots,x_{n}^{a_{n}},x_{1}^{b_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{b_{n}})
, where
a_{i}>b_{i}>0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.
Then the Betti numbers are
\beta_{i}(I)=\binom{n+1}{i+1}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2
and
\beta_{n-1}(I)=n
.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
I
be an almost complete intersection squarefree monomial ideal. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

if
I
satisfies in the conditions in case
(i)
of Theorem
1.1
, then for
i=0,1,\ldots,p-1
, we have
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}
and
it holds that

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{q+1-i}-\binom{q+1-p}{q+1-i}
, for
i=p,p+1,\ldots,q
.

(b)

if
I
satisfies the conditions in cases
(ii)
and
(vi)
of Theorem
1.1
, then

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,q
.

(c)

if
I
satisfies the conditions in cases
(iii)
,
(iv)
and
(v)
of Theorem
1.1
, then

\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}-\binom{q-1}{i-2}

for
i=0,1,\ldots,q
.

Proof.

Case
(a)
is satisfied by Theorem
1.14
. In case
(b)
, since

I
is a dominant ideal, it admits a minimal free resolution given by the Taylor resolution. Consequently, the Betti numbers are
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,q
.

For case
(c)
, we assume that
I
satisfies the condition
(iii)
of Theorem
1.1
. The proofs for the other cases
(iv)
and
(v)
of Theorem
1.1
are similar.
Since
v_{2}v_{3}\mid lcm\left(v_{1}v_{2},v_{1}v_{3}\right)
, it follows that
v_{2}v_{3}
divides only those subsets of generators that contain
v_{1}v_{2},v_{1}v_{3}
. Hence, the division process does not occur in any other cases. By applying the argument from Theorem
1.14
, we find
\#L_{i}=\binom{q}{i}-\binom{q-2}{i-2}
. Therefore, the Betti numbers satisfy
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\#L_{i}+\#L_{i-1}=\binom{q}{i}-\binom{q-2}{i-2}+\binom{q}{i-1}-\binom{q-2}{i-3}
. Simplifying yields
\beta_{i}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\binom{q+1}{i}-\binom{q-1}{i-2}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,q
, as required.

or
[
9
]
), we can now state the following result:

Theorem 1.18
.

Let

I
be an almost complete intersection monomial ideal. Then

I
satisfies the conditions outlined in Theorem
1.17
.

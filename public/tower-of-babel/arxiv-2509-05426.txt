Sparse Seemingly Unrelated Regression (SSUR) Copula Mixed Models for Multivariate Loss Reserving
Pengfei Cai, Anas Abdallah, Pratheepa Jeganathan

Abstract
Insurance companies often operate across multiple interrelated lines of business (LOBs), and accounting for dependencies between them is essential for accurate reserve estimation and risk capital determination. In our previous work on the Extended Deep Triangle (EDT), we demonstrated that a more flexible model that uses multiple companies’ data reduces reserve prediction error and increases diversification benefits. However, the EDT’s limitation lies in its limited interpretability of the dependence structure, which is an important feature needed by insurers to guide strategic decisions. Motivated by the need for interpretability and flexibility, this paper proposes a Seemingly Unrelated Regression (SUR) copula mixed model to handle heterogeneous data across multiple companies. The model incorporates random effects to capture company-specific heterogeneity, uses flexible marginal distributions across LOBs, and treats development and accident year effects as fixed effects with shrinkage via LASSO to enhance robustness. We estimate the model using an iterative two-stage procedure and generate predictive reserve distributions via a modified bootstrap that accounts for systematic effects, dependence structure, and sparse fixed-effect coefficients. Through simulation studies and real data from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, we show that the proposed model outperforms the SUR copula regression model in terms of reserve accuracy and generates larger risk capital gain. Overall, the SUR copula mixed model achieves better predictive performance, greater risk diversification, and retains interpretability.



1
Introduction

Dependence modeling of loss ratios between multiple loss triangles is critical in predicting loss reserves and risk capital analysis, which depends on the predictive distribution of the reserve. Incorporating dependency into reserve leverages the risk diversification benefit between incremental paid losses of different lines of business (LOBs) for the insurer. For example,
Cai et al., 2025
show that capturing the pairwise and sequence dependence in multiple loss triangles reduces risk capital. On the other hand,
Abdallah et al., 2015
demonstrate that a parametric approach that models these relationships can provide improved interpretability of the dependence structure, albeit with reduced prediction accuracy, while still accurately determining reserve ranges and the amount of risk capital needed.

Neural networks-based method utilizes GAN techniques to generate the predictive distribution, and parametric models utilize model-based and rank-based bootstrapping to generate the predictive distribution. In the presence of dependence, copula GAN leads to the largest risk capital gain
(
Cai et al., 2025
)
. Among parametric approaches, the residual rank-based bootstrapping of copula coordinates yields the largest risk capital gain and highest risk diversification benefit
(
Abdallah and Wang, 2023
)
.

Both neural network-based and parametric approaches have notable limitations. Neural methods, while flexible and capable of quantifying predictive uncertainty as demonstrated in the Extended Deep Triangle (EDT) model, often lack interpretability, particularly in how they represent dependence structures
(
Cai et al., 2025
)
. This hinders their usefulness for decision-making in actuarial contexts where understanding pairwise dependence is essential. Parametric models, on the other hand, are more interpretable but may suffer from model misspecification and fail to fully leverage multiple company data, leading to biased reserve and capital estimates
(
Shi and Frees, 2011
;
Abdallah et al., 2015
)
.

Kuo, 2019
introduced the Deep Triangle (DT), a recurrent neural network framework that leverages loss triangles from multiple companies to enhance the predictive accuracy of traditional stochastic reserving methods. Building on this, the Extended Deep Triangle (EDT) incorporates dependence between two LOBs by modeling pairwise and sequential relationships in loss ratios
(
Cai et al., 2025
)
. To address heterogeneity across companies, the EDT encodes company identifiers such that companies with similar incremental paid loss patterns are represented with similar codes. Additionally, dropout is applied within DT to induce sparsity and improve prediction. Trained on data from multiple companies, the EDT generates reserve estimates that closely align with actual reserves. Moreover, by learning development year patterns and latent dependence structures directly from data, EDT yields lower risk capital than copula regression models, which rely on fixed effects to represent development year behavior and company heterogeneity.

While SUR-based models provide a flexible regression framework to capture dependencies, they have typically been limited to single-company settings or often model company and development year effects as fixed. For example,
Zhang, 2010
extend the classical chain ladder to a multivariate SUR framework, while
Shi and Frees, 2011
and
Abdallah et al., 2015
use SUR copula regression to account for dependence between two LOBs.
Shi, 2017
propose a Bayesian hierarchical model to analyze intercompany claim triangles, in which copula regression is employed to join multiple triangles of each insurer, and a hierarchical structure is speciﬁed on major parameters to allow for borrowing strength across insurers. However, these models do not fully leverage multiple company data and may suffer from biased estimates due to heterogeneity in company data. Moreover, without regularization, including many predictors across LOBs can lead to overfitting and reduced interpretability.

Few studies in the loss reserving literature have incorporated random effects to address hierarchical data structures, but not heterogeneity across companies.
Abdallah et al., 2016
explore using the Sarmanov bivariate distribution to model dependence between loss triangles and introduce random effects to capture shared calendar year influences within and across triangles. This formulation highlights the potential of random effects to account for heterogeneity in multivariate reserving.
More recently,
Karataş and Karabey, 2025
propose a method that introduces random effects to capture unobserved variation both between and within lines of business, modeling these effects using copula regression.
However, applications of random effects within the SUR framework, particularly for modeling cross-company variability, remain limited.

Regularized regression has recently gained traction in the loss reserving literature to handle high-dimensional predictors and improve model interpretability.
Williams et al., 2015
applied the elastic net penalty, a convex combination of L1 and L2 penalties, to a dataset with over 350 covariates, enhancing predictive performance in insurance claims modeling.
McGuire et al., 2018
introduced a loss reserving LASSO framework
(
Tibshirani, 1996
)
, modeling individual claim data with complex features and applying L1 regularization to stabilize the estimation of loss development factors. Regularization is particularly beneficial in loss reserving, where collinearity and sparse observations can compromise the robustness of the model fit. Notably, it is straightforward to incorporate LASSO regularization into the SUR framework, enabling effective variable selection while preserving model predictive performance.

Motivated by these limitations, we propose a sparse SUR copula mixed model that extends SUR copula regression to accommodate hierarchical data from multiple companies to address this gap. The model includes fixed effects to capture accident and development year patterns, and random effects to represent heterogeneity across companies. Sparsity is introduced through regularization on the fixed effects, which promotes variable selection and improves interpretability. The SUR component jointly models loss development across multiple LOBs, while the copula component captures the residual dependence structure. This mixed effects formulation allows the model to borrow strength across companies and improve predictive accuracy.

Parametric bootstrapping has been a widely used approach for resampling incremental paid losses, followed by generating the predictive distribution of the loss reserve.
Davison and Hinkley, 1997
provides a comprehensive framework for bootstrap methodologies in the context of generalized linear models using the pseudo residuals.
Kirschner et al., 2008
employs a synchronized parametric bootstrap to account for dependencies between correlated lines of business.
Taylor and McGuire, 2007
further develops this approach in the context of generalized linear models. While parametric bootstrap remains a natural choice for simulating future reserves, it requires adaptation for sparse models. In the sparse SUR copula mixed model, standard bootstrap methods may fail when some fixed effect coefficients are thresholded to zero, leading to instability in the resampling process. To address this, we adopt a modified residual bootstrap based on the method of
Chatterjee and Lahiri, 2011
, using residual ranks to construct a more robust resampling procedure that accommodates sparsity. This approach preserves the dependence structure while enabling stable estimation of the predictive distribution of reserves in the presence of regularized effects.

We summarize our contributions as follows

1.

We introduce the seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) copula mixed models to the multivariate loss reserving literature, to capture dependence between heterogeneous LOBs across multiple companies.

2.

We propose a two-stage iterative approach to estimate the parameters of the SUR copula mixed model using rank-based techniques. In the first stage, we estimate the parameters for the fixed effects and random effects. In the second stage, we estimate the copula dependence parameter using the ranks of the residuals.

3.

We propose a sparse model that combines the SUR copula mixed model with the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) to increase the model’s robustness and to mitigate the impact of reduced observed paid losses in later accident and development years.

4.

We extend the coefficient-thresholded residual bootstrapping
(
Chatterjee and Lahiri, 2011
)
to generate the predictive distribution of the reserve.

The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 details the methodologies of SUR mixed models for loss reserving and predictive distribution estimation followed by the sparse SUR (SSUR) copula mixed model to handle the shrinkage of the fixed effects parameters. Section 3 applies and calibrates the sparse SUR copula mixed model using a dataset that includes personal and commercial automobile LOBs from multiple companies. Section 4 presents a simulation study that further demonstrates the superior performance of the sparse SUR copula mixed model. Finally, Section 5 summarizes our findings.

We adopt an iterative two-stage approach, in which marginal models, including systematic effects and company random effects, are estimated in one step, and copula parameters are estimated from rank-transformed residuals in the next. Then, the copula parameters are plugged into the complete likelihood to optimize it with respect to the marginal distribution parameters. This process is repeated until the convergence of marginal distribution parameter estimates.

The rationale for the iteration is twofold. First, while copulas are invariant to monotone transformations of marginal distributions, in practice, we only observe estimated marginals; iterating ensures that the marginal estimation remains consistent with the estimated dependence structure between lines of business across multiple companies. Second, we retain robustness to marginal misspecification by using rank-based estimation for the copula at each step.

The iterative two-stage procedure, therefore, balances computational feasibility as in
Côté et al., 2016
and
Abdallah and Wang, 2023
with statistical reliability in the multi-company loss triangle setting. Moreover, to obtain a reliable predictive distribution of total reserves, we must account for uncertainty in the estimated systematic effects and ensure a robust dependence fit for diversification. The iteration mitigates bias from marginal mis-specification, maintains computational feasibility, and allows us to produce stable predictive distributions of reserves that directly support capital and diversification analysis.

2.2
Sparse SUR Copula Mixed Model

In this section, we describe how we incorporate the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) into the SUR copula mixed model to shrink the fixed effects coefficients toward zero and reduce their variability.

We deliberately refrain from penalizing
\theta
in the copula layer. For our parametric families
C_{\theta}
, independence is nested at
\theta=0
; a penalty would shrink
\theta
toward 0 and bias the implied Kendall’s
\tau
(and tail-dependence, when applicable), undermining the interpretability of cross-LOB dependence. If sparsity in the dependence structure were desired, a principled route would be model selection of the independence copula for weak pairs. Given limited companies and the mixed model’s other parameters, penalizing
\theta
trades variance for systematic bias in dependence, which we avoid here.

We construct the loss function as the negative log-likelihood of equation (
10
) combined with an

\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(1)}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(2)}

\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
corresponding to the penalties on
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(1)}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}L^{(\ell)}(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(\ell)},\sigma_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}\mid\boldsymbol{y}_{1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,\boldsymbol{y}_{C}^{(\ell)})-\sum_{c=1}^{C}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j=1}^{I+1-i}\log c(F_{ijc}^{(1)},F_{ijc}^{(2)};{\theta_{c}})+\lambda_{1}||\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(1)}||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(2)}||_{1}

. These pairs are transformed via the empirical quantile function of pseudo-residuals to obtain
\epsilon_{ijc}^{*(\ell)}
. Finally, the bootstrap samples of the incremental paid losses is calculated as
y_{ijc}^{*(\ell)}=\epsilon_{ijc}^{*(\ell)}*(\hat{\mu}_{ijc}^{(\ell)}*\hat{\gamma}_{ijc}^{(\ell)})^{1/2}+\hat{\mu}_{ijc}^{(\ell)}
.

Given the bootstrap sample pairs
(y_{ijc}^{*(\ell)},y_{ijc}^{*(\ell^{\prime})})
, we apply the sparse SUR copula mixed model estimation approach to obtain the loss reserve for company c as

\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{I}\sum_{j=I-i+2}^{I}\omega_{ic}^{(\ell)}\cdot\exp(\hat{\eta}_{ijc}^{*(\ell)}),

(15)

where
\hat{\eta}_{ijc}^{*(\ell)}=\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}^{(\ell)}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{*(\ell)}+\boldsymbol{z}_{c}^{(\ell)}\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}^{*(\ell)}
and
\omega_{ic}^{(\ell)}
is the premium for accident year
i
and company
c
in LOB
\ell
. Note that for all bootstrap samples, we use penalization parameters
\hat{\lambda}_{1}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{2}
obtained using the observed data. We repeat the procedure a large number of times (5000 times here) in order to obtain the approximate distribution of the reserve.

5
Summary and Discussion

We have proposed a class of seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) copula mixed models that extend SUR copula regression to incorporate mixed effects, thereby improving loss prediction and risk capital estimation. The model consists of three components: fixed effects to capture systematic accident year and development year patterns, company-specific random effects to capture heterogeneity across insurers, and a copula with flexible marginal distributions to model dependence between lines of business. To enhance robustness under a reduced number of observations in recent accident and development years, we integrate the SUR copula mixed model with the LASSO, producing a sparse version that shrinks negligible coefficients toward zero. This sparse specification mitigates the impact of limited data in the tails of the loss triangles, leading to more stable and accurate predictions.

Parameters are estimated using a iterative two-stage procedure that alternates between estimating the fixed and random effects from the marginals and given these values, updating the copula dependence parameters. We also adapt the bootstrap approach to account for sparsity by applying coefficient thresholding in the resampling step, which ensures that the predictive distributions reflect the penalized estimates. The proposed algorithm converged reliably in all real-data and simulation settings, confirming its numerical stability.

Empirical and simulation results show that the sparse SUR copula mixed model consistently achieves smaller prediction errors, reduced variability, and larger risk capital gains than both the SUR copula mixed model without sparsity and the SUR Gaussian copula model. The ability to capture dependence between lines of business, account for variability across companies, and remain robust under sparsity leads to more accurate reserve estimates and greater diversification benefits.

One limitation of the current formulation is the assumption that errors within equations are independent. In practice, errors may exhibit autocorrelation due to the development year effect over time. Although AR(1) or higher-order dependence structures or random effects as in
Karataş and Karabey, 2025
could be incorporated into the SUR copula mixed model, doing so would substantially increase the complexity of the estimation procedure. An alternative is to retain the copula component for dependence interpretation while estimating the other components of the model using flexible architectures such as our recent work using recurrent neural networks (EDT)
(
Cai et al., 2025
)
.

The copula component is currently capturing the cell-level residual dependence between LOBs, while we are not capturing the company-level dependence in the reserves due to the independence assumption of the random effects.
Another extension would be to relax the independence assumption of the company random effects and introduce a bivariate random effect, allowing for correlation between the random effects of different LOBs.

In summary, the SUR copula mixed model improves reserve estimation and produces larger risk capital gains when using loss triangles from multiple companies and appropriate marginals, while effectively capturing dependence between lines of business and accounting for heterogeneity across companies. Thus, the insurance companies can leverage multiple companies’ data with the proposed model to improve risk diversification than a single company.

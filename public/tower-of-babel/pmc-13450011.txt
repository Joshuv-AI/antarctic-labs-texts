Systemic intravascular large B-cell lymphoma relapsing as an extravascular cutaneous large B-cell lymphoma after 8 years from initial diagnosis
Authors: Seongbae Kong, Joseph S. Durgin, Tera L. Mayer, Dahlia Sano, Alexandra C. Hristov, Trilokraj Tejasvi
Published: 2026

Introduction

Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma (IVLBCL) is a rare, aggressive lymphoma where malignant lymphocytes grow predominantly within blood vessel lumina. In classical IVLBCL, fever of unknown origin, with or without B symptoms, is the most common presenting symptom, often accompanied by signs of microvascular occlusion including retiform purpura and mental status changes.1,2 In contrast, the hemophagocytic syndrome-associated variant is characterized by fever, hepatosplenomegaly, and thrombocytopenia.2 Immunochemotherapy has improved outcomes, particularly with the addition of rituximab to combination regimens.2,3

IVLBCL has presented alongside extravascular lymphoma, raising the possibility that the former is merely an angiotropic subclone of a “solid” B-cell lymphoma.4 For example, IVLBCL has been described following treatment of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), with polymerase chain reaction (PCR) suggesting a clonal tumor origin.4 A cardiac DLBCL patient also had subcutaneous IVLBCL lesions detected by random skin biopsy.5 Interestingly, random skin biopsy has good sensitivity for detecting IVLBCL, supporting its distributed nature.6 Whether IVLBCL represents a primary entity, or a peculiar transformation of conventional lymphoma, remains unclear.5,7

We present a case where a primarily thyroid IVLBCL, initially responding completely to treatment, recurred 8 years later as a DLBCL involving the skin. Immunophenotypic and gene rearrangement studies showed common clonality between tumors, suggesting a shared pathogenetic origin.

Case report

A 53-year-old female, with a history of multinodular goiter since childhood, presented to clinic with a recently diagnosed autonomous functioning nodule in the right thyroid lobe, complicated by subclinical hyperthyroidism. Thyroid ultrasound demonstrated an enlarged right lobe (10.4 × 6.0 × 3.8 cm) and left lobe (8.0 × 4.0 × 3.7 cm), both containing heterogeneous nodules. By I-123 thyroid scintigraphy, a 1.9 cm right lobe nodule showed increased uptake, but the largest nodule (2.8 × 2.3 × 2.3 cm) in the left lobe was isointense. Fine needle aspiration of the largest nodule disclosed malignant cells with cytologic features “consistent with a high-grade neoplasm.” MRI confirmed the heterogeneously enlarged thyroid gland with enhancing left lobe nodule. A left adrenal mass was incidentally identified on a separate MRI for low back pain. Whole body positron emission tomography (PET) imaging was conducted, revealing heterogenous [18F] Fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) uptake in the thyroid, intense FDG-avidity in an enlarged left adrenal gland, and further uptake in the left mid-abdomen mesentery, suspicious for metastatic carcinoma versus lymphoma (Fig 1, A). Total thyroidectomy was performed, and histopathology demonstrated intravascular large B-cell lymphoma (Fig 2, A-C). The patient completed 6 cycles of rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP) and 4 treatments with intrathecal methotrexate. Surveillance with complete blood counts, metabolic panels, lactate dehydrogenase levels, peripheral blood flow cytometry, and PET scan showed no disease after completing treatment. Four years after diagnosis, peripheral blood flow cytometry demonstrated a small population of abnormal CD5-positive B-cells with lambda light chain restriction. However, repeat PET scan found no evidence of recurrent disease. Flow cytometry studies over the following 4 years were normal.

Eight years after initial presentation, the patient noted 2 rapidly growing, deep, painless nodules on the left lateral calf (Fig 3, A). Biopsy of the largest nodule (1.6 cm) was obtained. Histopathology showed dermal infiltrate of large CD20-positive B-cells expressing BCL6, MUM1, and weak BCL2, with lambda light chain restriction and rare kappa-positive plasma cells (Fig 3, B-H). Ki-67 marked approximately 60% and C-MYC highlighted 20% to 30% of large cells. CD5 expression was indeterminate given numerous background T-cells. Repeat PET scan showed 3 hypermetabolic left lower extremity cutaneous lesions and a sub-centimeter hypermetabolic right breast lesion (Fig 1, B). Peripheral blood flow was negative for intravascular lymphoma. Brain MRI was negative for intracranial involvement. She underwent right breast biopsy that showed triple-positive invasive carcinoma with ductal and lobular features. This was treated with lumpectomy and sentinel lymph node biopsy, disclosing no lymph node involvement. She received 2 cycles of salvage rituximab, dexamethasone, cytarabine, and oxaliplatin, followed by autologous stem cell transplant. Post-chemotherapy PET scan showed a complete metabolic response. She remained disease-free at her 2-year follow-up post-transplant.

Discussion

Among large B-cell lymphomas, IVLBCL is distinctive for its heterogenous clinical presentation and tropism for blood vessels, with a high risk of CNS, skin, and endocrine organ involvement. Several authors have suggested that IVLBCL may occur simultaneously with extravascular B-cell lymphoma.4,5 Prior reports of concurrent or sequential intravascular and extravascular large B-cell lymphomas further support a shared disease biology between these entities.1,5 In our case, a primarily thyroid IVLBCL initially responded completely to R-CHOP and intrathecal methotrexate but recurred after 8 years as a cutaneous extravascular large B-cell lymphoma. Although concurrent extravascular B-cell lymphoma at initial diagnosis cannot be excluded, as the FDG-avid left adrenal gland lesion was not biopsied, the shared clonality between lesions reinforces a single lymphoma clone capable of both intravascular and extravascular growth.

While IVLBCL displays immunophenotypic overlap with other large B-cell lymphomas, there are distinctive features. Typically, IVLBCL cells have CD20+CD79a+ immunophenotype of mature peripheral B-cells, and more than 80% of cases have a non-germinal center phenotype (CD10-, MUM1+).8 In reported cases of IVLBCL and DLBCL with cutaneous recurrence, CD20 was consistently detected with available immunohistochemistry (Table I). Clearly, some IVLBCL cases share an overlapping pathogenesis with other large B-cell lymphomas. Given its subtle clinical signs, concurrent IVLBCL is likely underrecognized in patients with high-grade extravascular B-cell lymphoma. With emerging genomic profiling highlighting immune checkpoint abnormalities in IVLBCL,10 and high quality clinical trials,3 the recognition of this histopathologic entity will likely have growing therapeutic importance.

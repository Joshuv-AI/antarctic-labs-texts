Spectral method for low-dose Poisson and Bernoulli phase retrieval
Sjoerd Dirksen, Felix Krahmer, Patricia Römer, Palina Salanevich

Abstract
We consider the problem of phaseless reconstruction from measurements with Poisson or Bernoulli distributed noise. This is of particular interest in biological imaging experiments where a low dose of radiation has to be used to mitigate potential damage of the specimen, resulting in low observed particle counts. We derive recovery guarantees for the spectral method for these noise models in the case of Gaussian measurements. Our results give a quantitative insight in the trade-off between the employed radiation dose per measurement and the overall sampling complexity.



Keywords
phase retrieval, spectral method, low-dose imaging, Poisson noise, quantized measurements

Introduction

In biological imaging, one aims to characterize small-scale objects such as proteins or viruses. This is done, for example, via X-ray or electron microscopy, where photons or electrons, respectively, are shot onto the object of interest to measure information about the absorption and the scattering properties of it. The radiation wave comprising this information propagates then to a pixelated detector placed behind the object which quantizes the incoming wave. Essentially, the detector counts how many particles arrive in each of the pixels separately.
Biological specimens are typically highly sensitive to the radiation dose
[
1
]
. If this is the case, the damage of the specimen can be mitigated by reducing the radiation dose, resulting in low counts of particles per detector pixel.
Moreover, one can only observe the intensity of the incoming energy, the phase information of the radiation wave cannot be measured by the detector due to the high-frequency oscillation of the radiation required for high-resolution imaging
[
2
]
. This gives rise to solving a phase retrieval problem.

Motivated by such experiments, we study the problem of reconstructing the
object of interest
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from its phaseless
measurements

\displaystyle y_{i}\approx|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2},\quad i\in[m],

given a set of measurement vectors
\left\{a_{i}\right\}_{i\in[m]}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In this paper, we assume that
\left\{a_{i}\right\}_{i\in[m]}
are i.i.d. standard Gaussian vectors.

While the experimental interpretation would be that the radiation dose is a parameter of the measurement setup, we fix the normalization of the measurement vectors and incorporate the dose in the norm
\|x\|^{2}_{2}
of the object
x
for notational convenience.

Due to the particle counting procedure, the measurements are assumed to be independent and drawn from a Poisson distribution, that is

\displaystyle y_{i}\sim\textit{Poisson}\bigl(\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\bigr).

(P)

This amounts to a random quantization of
\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}
to values in
\mathbb{N}
.

Commonly, the phase retrieval problem with Poisson noise is approached via the maximum likelihood method, combined with optimization algorithms such as ADMM
[
3
]
or Wirtinger flow
[
4
]
. While these methods perform well for large values of
|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2}
, their applicability is limited for experiments that use an extremely low dose
[
5
,
6
]
.

If the values
|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2}
are so small that, with high probability, merely zero or one measurements are observed, one has only binary data and the Poisson distribution can be well approximated by a Bernoulli distribution. That is, one can model the measurements as Bernoulli random variables,

\displaystyle y_{i}\sim\textit{Bernoulli}\left(1-\exp(-|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2})\right).

(B)

which corresponds to a random one-bit quantization of
\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}
.

This one-bit measurement model is further motivated by 4D-scanning transmission electron microscopy (4D-STEM)
[
7
]
, where the image acquisition is performed by an event-driven detector with dead time so that it can only record the first particle arrival, resulting in binary ‘count/no count’ observations.

For solving the noisy phase retrieval problem, we work with a constrained minimization problem optimizing an approximation of an
\ell_{2}
loss.
For a similar recovery method, a more general setting with measurements
y_{i}=f_{i}(\langle a_{i},x\rangle)
involving a non-linear function
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
had been studied in
[
8
]
. This approach can also be extended to the problem of phase retrieval; see
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we propose and analyze a new approach for phase retrieval with Poisson or Bernoulli measurements.

2
Problem formulation

The Bernoulli measurements can be interpreted as noisy one-bit representations of the squared phaseless measurements. Consequently, the theory for one-bit compressed sensing can serve as an inspiration for reconstruction algorithms. We follow the idea of
[
11
]
to seek a vector
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose noiseless measurements
\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}
maximize the correlation with the noisy measurements
y_{i}
.
Thus, for both measurement models (
P
) and (
B
), we aim to obtain a good approximation for the ground-truth object
x
by solving the constrained optimization problem

maximize

\displaystyle\quad f_{x}(z)

(1)

subject to

\displaystyle\quad\left\|z\right\|_{2}^{2}=\alpha,

with the objective function
f_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle f_{x}(z)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}|\langle a_{i},z\rangle|^{2}.

(2)

The constraint
\left\|z\right\|_{2}^{2}=\alpha
specifies the dose
\alpha
, which is part of the experimental setup and therefore is known in advance.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}|\langle a_{i},z\rangle|^{2}=z^{\mathrm{T}}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right)z,

the solution to (
1
) is the leading eigenvector of

\displaystyle Y\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}

(3)

that satisfies the norm constraint.

Interestingly, computing the leading eigenvector of this random matrix
Y
is exactly the spectral method proposed in
[
12
]
.
The spectral method is motivated by the
observation that for Gaussian random measurement vectors
a_{i}\overset{\text{{i.i.d.}}}{\sim}~\mathcal{N}(0,I_{n})
and the corresponding noise-free phaseless measurements
y_{i}=\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}
, the random matrix
Y
concentrates around its expectation
2xx^{\mathrm{T}}+\lVert x\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot I_{n}
, whose leading eigendirection is exactly parallel to the underlying signal
x
.

While we arrive at the same strategy through a different motivation, the resulting algorithm is the same. Consequently, the theoretical analysis can be approached analogously to

[
12
]
, and the main challenge is to incorporate the effect of the Poisson (
P
) and the Bernoulli distribution (
B
), respectively.

For the high-dose scenario, recovery guarantees have been derived in
[
4
]
for a truncated version of the spectral method; see also
[
13
,
14
]
for an asymptotic analysis of such truncated spectral method for more general noise models that also includes Poisson noise as a special case.

More precisely, a truncation
\mathcal{T}(y)=y\cdot\mathds{1}_{y\leq t}
is applied to the observations, so
Y
is replaced by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}(y_{i})a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}.

For an appropriate truncation, the resulting method is shown to recover the ground truth with a sample complexity of
m=\mathcal{O}(n)
. However, the results do not extend to the low-dose setting, but only apply for
\left\|x\right\|_{2}\geq\log^{1.5}(m)
.
In this paper, we show that we can go beyond this limiting minimal dose condition with a sampling complexity only increased by logarithmic factors.

3
Recovery guarantees

In this section, we adopt the following notation and assumptions. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the measured object
renormalized by the radiation dose parameter
\alpha\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\|x\|_{2}^{2}
and assume that the measurements vectors
a_{i},\ i\in[m],
are drawn independently from
\mathcal{N}(0,I_{n})
. For the phaseless measurements
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
drawn from the Poisson (
P
) or Bernoulli (
B
) observation models, we define the matrix
Y
as in (
3
). We denote by
x_{0}
the eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue of
Y
, normalized so that
\left\|x_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\alpha
.

First, we formulate a technical lemma that allows us to compute the expectation of
Y
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
a\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{n})
and
u,v,w\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Let
(g,h_{1},h_{2})
be multivariate Gaussian with
g,h_{1},h_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, with
g
and
h_{i}
independent for
i=1,2
, and

\mathbb{E}(h_{1}h_{2})=\frac{\langle v,w\rangle-\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\left\langle u,w\right\rangle}{\sqrt{1-\left\langle u,v\right\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{1-\left\langle u,w\right\rangle^{2}}}.

Then,
(\langle a,u\rangle,\langle a,v\rangle,\langle a,w\rangle)
is identically distributed with
(g,g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})
for

g^{\prime}=\langle u,v\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}h_{1}

and

g^{\prime\prime}=\langle u,w\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\langle u,w\rangle^{2}}h_{2}.

Proof.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the inner products
\langle a,u\rangle
and
\langle a,v\rangle
are standard Gaussian random variables with covariance

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(\left\langle a,u\right\rangle,\left\langle a,v\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left\langle a,u\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle a,v\right\rangle\right)-\mathbb{E}\left\langle a,u\right\rangle\cdot\mathbb{E}\left\langle a,v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=u^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left(aa^{\mathrm{T}}\right)v

\displaystyle=\langle u,v\rangle.

We determine
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(\langle a,u\rangle,\langle a,v\rangle)\overset{d}{\sim}(g,\gamma_{1}g+\gamma_{2}h_{1})
.
Since
\mathbb{V}(\langle a,v\rangle)=1
and
\text{Cov}\left(\left\langle a,u\right\rangle,\left\langle a,v\right\rangle\right)=\langle u,v\rangle
, we find

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(g,\gamma_{1}g+\gamma_{2}h_{1}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\gamma_{1}g^{2}+\gamma_{2}gh_{1}\right)-\mathbb{E}(g)\cdot\mathbb{E}(\gamma_{1}g+\gamma_{2}h_{1})=\gamma_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{2})=\gamma_{1}.

That is,
\gamma_{1}=\langle u,v\rangle
. Furthermore,

\mathbb{V}(\gamma_{1}g+\gamma_{2}h_{1})=\gamma_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{2}^{2},

so that
\gamma_{2}=\sqrt{1-\gamma_{1}^{2}}=\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}
.

Analogously, we obtain
(\langle a,u\rangle,\langle a,w\rangle)\overset{d}{\sim}(g,\gamma_{1}g+\gamma_{2}h_{2})
with

\gamma_{1}=\langle u,w\rangle
and
\gamma_{2}=\sqrt{1-\langle u,w\rangle^{2}}
.

Using that
(\langle a,v\rangle,\langle a,w\rangle)\overset{d}{\sim}(\langle u,v\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}h_{1},\langle u,w\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\langle u,w\rangle^{2}}h_{2})
and the independence of
g
and
h_{i}
for
i=1,2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\langle a,v\rangle\langle a,w\rangle)=\mathbb{E}\left(\langle u,v\rangle\langle u,w\rangle g^{2}+\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{1-\langle u,w\rangle^{2}}h_{1}h_{2}\right).

This, together with
\mathbb{E}(aa^{\mathrm{T}})=I_{n}
, suggests to pick
h_{1},h_{2}
such that

\mathbb{E}(h_{1}h_{2})=\frac{\langle v,w\rangle-\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\left\langle u,w\right\rangle}{\sqrt{1-\left\langle u,v\right\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{1-\left\langle u,w\right\rangle^{2}}}.

∎

Using
Lemma

1
as a trick to rewrite random variables, we derive the expectation of the random matrix
Y
for both the Poisson and the Bernoulli model.

Lemma 2
.

If the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are independent and distributed according to the Poisson model (
P
), then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=2xx^{\mathrm{T}}+\|x\|_{2}^{2}\cdot I_{n}.

If the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are distributed according to the Bernoulli model (
B
), then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=\frac{2}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot xx^{\mathrm{T}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot I_{n}.

Proof.

We start with the Poisson model. Conditioned on the vectors
a_{i},\ i\in[m]
, the expectation of
Y
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid A\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[y_{i}\mid A\right]a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}},

where
A
denotes the matrix with rows
a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Taking the expectation of this yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid A\right]\right]=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}a_{1}a_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\right].

We determine the expectation of the random matrix entry-wise. That means, for all
k,\ell\in[n]
, we need to find

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}a_{1}a_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\right]\right)_{k,\ell}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right],&\quad k=\ell,\\[8.0pt]
\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle a_{1},e_{\ell}\right\rangle\right],&\quad k\neq\ell,\end{cases}

where
e_{k}
denotes the
k
th standard basis vector.

Based on
Lemma

1
, we denote in the following
g\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\langle a_{1},\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}}\right\rangle
, and rewrite

\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{1}

and

\left\langle a_{1},e_{\ell}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{2},

with
g,h_{1},h_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, where
g
and
h_{i}
are independent for
i=1,2
. Using that
\mathbb{E}\left(a_{1k}a_{1\ell}\right)=0
if
k\neq\ell
, we find that

\mathbb{E}(h_{1}h_{2})=-\frac{\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle^{2}}}.

(4)

Using
\mathbb{E}[g^{4}]=3
and the independence of
g
and
h_{1}
, we can compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2}\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{1}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle x,e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}g^{4}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\lVert x\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\langle x,e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right)g^{2}h_{1}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=3\left\langle x,e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}+\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\langle x,e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2x_{k}^{2}+\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2},

and, using also (
4
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle a_{1},e_{\ell}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2}\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{1}\right)\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\langle x,e_{k}\rangle\langle x,e_{\ell}\rangle g^{4}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert x\rVert_{2}^{2}\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle^{2}}g^{2}h_{1}h_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=3\left\langle x,e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle x,e_{\ell}\right\rangle-\left\langle x,e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle x,e_{\ell}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2x_{k}x_{\ell}.

Combining all entries, we find

\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=2xx^{\mathrm{T}}+\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\cdot I_{n}.

For the Bernoulli model, we have to investigate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid A\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[y_{i}\mid A\right]a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|^{2})\right)a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2})\right)a_{1}a_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\right].

To obtain the expectation of the random matrix
Y
, we again study its entries

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2})\right)a_{1}a_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\right]\right)_{k,\ell}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2})\right)\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right],&\quad k=\ell,\\[8.0pt]
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2})\right)\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle a_{1},e_{\ell}\right\rangle\right],&\quad k\neq\ell,\end{cases}

for all
k,\ell\in[n]
.

We use the same trick as before to rewrite the random variables
\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle,\left\langle a_{1},e_{\ell}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle
.
However, for the Bernoulli model, we need to further determine

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}t^{2})\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot t\right)^{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)du\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g^{2}\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{2}\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}t^{2})\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\left[-\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\exp\left(-\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)t^{2}\right)\cdot t\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}+\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\int_{\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{2\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)t^{2}}{2}\right)dt\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)du\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Now, we can compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2})\right)\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right)\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{1}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\left(1-\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right)g^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\left(1-\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right)h_{1}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2x_{k}^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

and, using (
4
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2})\right)\left\langle a_{1},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle a_{1},e_{\ell}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right)\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{1}\right)\cdot\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.\cdot\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle^{2}}h_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle\left(1-\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right)g^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle^{2}}\left(1-\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right)h_{1}h_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle-\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}},e_{\ell}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2x_{k}x_{\ell}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

The respective last equalities use

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=\frac{2}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot xx^{\mathrm{T}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot I_{n}.

∎

Finally, we need to bound
\left\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right\|
for both the Poisson and the Bernoulli model.
As the Poisson random variables are unbounded, we first want to control the probability that
\max_{i\in[m]}y_{i}
is larger than a fixed bound.

Lemma 3
.

If the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are distributed according to the Poisson model (
P
), there exists
\beta>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\geq\tau\log(m)\right)\leq 3m^{-\beta}

for
\tau=\max\left\{e^{2}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2},\beta+1\right\}
.

Proof.

For any
\xi\geq 0
and
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil t\rceil-1}\frac{\xi^{k}}{k!}\

\displaystyle=\ \exp(\xi)-\sum_{k=\lceil t\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{k}}{k!}\ =\ \exp(\xi)-\xi^{\lceil t\rceil}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{k}}{(k+\lceil t\rceil)!}\ =\ \exp(\xi)-\xi^{\lceil t\rceil}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{k}}{k!}\prod_{s=k+1}^{k+\lceil t\rceil}\frac{1}{s}

\displaystyle\geq\ \exp(\xi)-\frac{\xi^{t}}{\lceil t\rceil!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{k}}{k!}\ =\ \exp(\xi)\left(1-\frac{\xi^{\lceil t\rceil}}{\lceil t\rceil!}\right).

Hence, for all
i\in[m]
and some
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(y_{i}<t\mid A\right)\

\displaystyle=\ \mathbb{P}\left(y_{i}\leq\lfloor t\rfloor\mid A\right)\ =\ \mathbb{P}\left(y_{i}\leq\lceil t\rceil-1\mid A\right)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\lceil t\rceil-1}\frac{\exp\left(-\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\left(\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{k}}{k!}\ =\ \exp\left(-\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil t\rceil-1}\frac{\left(\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\ \geq\ 1-\frac{\left(\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil t\rceil}}{\lceil t\rceil!}.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\geq t\mid A\right)\

\displaystyle=\ \mathbb{P}\left(\exists i\in[m]:y_{i}\geq t\mid A\right)\ \leq\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}\left(y_{i}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\left(\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil t\rceil}}{\lceil t\rceil!}\ \leq\ m\cdot\frac{\left(\max_{i\in[m]}\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil t\rceil}}{\lceil t\rceil!}.

(5)

Since
a_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{n})
, for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\in[m]}\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq t\right)\

\displaystyle=\ \mathbb{P}\left(\exists i\in[m]:\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq t\right)\ \leq\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq t\right)\ =\ 2m\exp\left(-\frac{ct}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}\right)

(6)

for an absolute constant
c>0
.
Hence, there exists
\beta>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\in[m]}\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\log(m)\right)\leq 2m\exp\left(-(\beta+1)\log(m)\right)=2m^{-\beta}.

Combining (
5
) for
t=\tau\log(m)
and (
6
), we find that for all
\tau>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\geq\tau\log(m)\right)\leq m\cdot\frac{\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\log(m)\right)^{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}}{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil!}+2m^{-\beta}.

Using Stirling’s approximation (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Equation (C.13)]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\log(m)\right)^{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}}{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\log(m)\right)^{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}}{\sqrt{2\pi\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil^{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}e^{-\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{e\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\log(m)}{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}\right)^{\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}.

If
\tau\geq e^{2}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\geq\tau\log(m)\right)\leq m\cdot e^{-\lceil\tau\log(m)\rceil}+2m^{-\beta}.

If, furthermore,
\tau\geq\beta+1
, we obtain the statement.

∎

Based on this good event that holds with high probability, we can prove the following deviation bound for the Poisson model. Since Bernoulli measurements are bounded by
1
, we similarly find a deviation bound also for the Bernoulli model.

Lemma 4
.

Let
m\geq n\log(n)
. For any
\beta>1
, there exist absolute constants
C_{\beta},\hat{C}_{\beta},c_{\beta}>0
, such that,
with probability at least
1-n^{-\beta}-m\exp\left(-c_{\beta}n\right)-m^{-\beta}
,

\displaystyle\left\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right\|\leq C_{\beta}\left(\|x\|_{2}^{2}+\hat{C}_{\beta}\right)\log(m)\sqrt{\log(n)}\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}

if the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are distributed according to the Poisson model (
P
), and, with probability at least
1-n^{-\beta}-m\exp\left(-c_{\beta}n\right)
,

\displaystyle\left\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right\|\leq C_{\beta}\sqrt{\log(n)}\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}

if the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are distributed according to the Bernoulli model (
B
).

Proof.

We start with a symmetrization trick. By
[
16
, Equation (6.3)]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right\|\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-z^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]z\right|\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-y_{i}^{\prime}\left|\left\langle a_{i}^{\prime},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right|\geq\frac{t}{2}\right)+\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-z^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]z\right|\geq\frac{t}{2}\right),

where
y_{i}^{\prime},a_{i}^{\prime},\ i\in[m]
, are independent copies of
y_{i},a_{i},\ i\in[m]
.
With Rademacher random vector
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{i})_{i\in[m]}
, independent of
y_{i}^{\prime},a_{i}^{\prime},y_{i},a_{i},\ i\in[m]
, the first term can be rewritten and bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-y_{i}^{\prime}\left|\left\langle a_{i}^{\prime},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right|\geq\frac{t}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\left(y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-y_{i}^{\prime}\left|\left\langle a_{i}^{\prime},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right|\geq\frac{t}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right|\geq\frac{t}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=2\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|\geq\frac{t}{4}\right),

using the triangle inequality in the second step.

Let
p\geq\log(n)
.
With the equivalence of the
p
-Schatten norm and the spectral norm for
p\geq\log(n)
and the non-commutative Khintchine inequality (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 5.26]
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq e^{p}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|_{C^{n}_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\sqrt{p}\cdot\frac{1}{m}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{2}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{C^{n}_{p}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\sqrt{p}\cdot\frac{1}{m}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{2}\|a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{p},

with an absolute constant
C>0
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{2}\|a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\|z\right\|_{2}=1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{2}\|a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\langle a_{i},z\rangle^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We use
\mathbb{E}_{A}\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}=n
and expand

\displaystyle\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left|\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{A}\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}+n\right|.

Since the random vectors
a_{i}
are standard Gaussian, and, hence, their squared entries
a_{ik}^{2},\ k\in[n]
, are independent, mean zero, sub-exponential random variables with norm
\left\|a_{ik}^{2}\right\|_{\psi_{1}}=1
, we can use Bernstein’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 2.8.1]
) to
show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{A}\left(|\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{A}\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}|\geq n\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{A}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{A}a_{ik}^{2}\right|\geq n\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{n^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|a_{ik}^{2}\right\|^{2}_{\psi_{1}}},\frac{n}{\max_{k\in[n]}\left\|a_{ik}^{2}\right\|_{\psi_{1}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-cn\right),

with an absolute constant
c>0
. Consequently, with probability at least
1-2\exp\left(-cn\right)
with respect to
a_{i},\ i\in[m]
,

\displaystyle\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left|\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{A}\left\|a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}+n\right|\leq 2n.

It remains to bound
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|
. We use
[
17
, Theorem 5.39]
to show that, with probability at least
1-2\exp\left(-\tilde{c}n\right)
with respect to
a_{i},\ i\in[m]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sup_{\left\|z\right\|_{2}=1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle a_{i},z\rangle^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sup_{\left\|z\right\|_{2}=1}\left\|Az\right\|_{2}=\sigma_{\max}(A)\leq\sqrt{m}+\tilde{C}\sqrt{n},

where
\tilde{C},\tilde{c}>0
are absolute constants.

Let us define the following events for
\tau>0
:

E_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\{\max_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\leq\tau\right\},\quad\quad E_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{m}+\tilde{C}\sqrt{n}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{3}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\{\max_{i\in[m]}\left\|a_{i}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq 2n\right\},

and let
E\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap E_{3}
. Note that in the Bernoulli model
E_{1}
holds true for
\tau=1
with probability 1.

We summarize that, in case of the Bernoulli model, there exist absolute constants
C,c>0
so that, for all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\sqrt{p+\log(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)

with probability at least
1-4m\exp\left(-cn\right)
with respect to
a_{i},\ i\in[m]
.
For the Poisson model, we use
Lemma

3
to find that
there exist absolute constants
C,c,\beta>0
so that, if
\tau\geq\max\left\{e^{2}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2},\beta+1\right\}\log(m)
, for all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\tau\sqrt{p+\log(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)

with probability at least
1-4m\exp\left(-cn\right)-3m^{-\beta}
with respect to
a_{i},\ i\in[m]
.

Consequently,
[
19
, Lemma A.1]
yields that there exist absolute constants
C,c>0
so that, for all
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|\geq eC\tau\left(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\log(n)}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(-t\right)+4m\exp\left(-cn\right)+\mathds{1}_{P}\cdot 3m^{-\beta},

where
\tau=1
and
\mathds{1}_{P}=0
for the Bernoulli model and
\tau=\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\beta+1\right)\log(m)
and
\mathds{1}_{P}=1
in case of the Poisson model.
With
t=\beta\log(n)
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|\geq eC\tau\left(\sqrt{\beta}+1\right)\sqrt{\log(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right)\leq n^{-\beta}+4m\exp\left(-cn\right)+\mathds{1}_{P}\cdot 3m^{-\beta}.

Hence, we found that there exist absolute constants
C,c,\beta>0
so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\|\geq C\tau\sqrt{\log(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2n^{-\beta}+8m\exp\left(-cn\right)+6m^{-\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-z^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]z\right|\geq C\tau\sqrt{\log(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right).

The last step is to bound the second term on the right-hand side. Using
[
20
, Lemma C.2]
,
we get that, for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-z^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]z\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-y_{i}^{\prime}\left|\left\langle a_{i}^{\prime},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right|\geq t-V\right)

where
y_{i}^{\prime},a_{i}^{\prime},\ i\in[m]
, are independent copies of
y_{i},a_{i},\ i\in[m]
, and

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left(y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right]^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]^{2}-m^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left((m^{2}-m)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}+m\mathbb{E}\left(y_{1}^{2}|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right)-m^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(y_{1}^{2}|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right)-\left(\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-y_{i}^{\prime}\left|\left\langle a_{i}^{\prime},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right|\geq t-V\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\|v\|_{2}=1}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},v\right\rangle\right|^{2}-y_{i}^{\prime}\left|\left\langle a_{i}^{\prime},v\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right|\geq t-V\right),

we can proceed as before to find

\displaystyle\sup_{\|z\|_{2}=1}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\left|\left\langle a_{i},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}-z^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]z\right|\geq C\tau\left(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\log(n)}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 4\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(C\tau\left(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\log(n)}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)-V\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+16m\exp(-cn)+\mathds{1}_{P}\cdot 12m^{-\beta}.

It is left to analyze
V
.
In case of the Poisson model, it is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(y_{1}^{2}|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}^{2}|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\ \Big|\ A\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle|^{4}+|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle|^{2}\right)|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right]

and

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\ \Big|\ A\right]\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}.

According to
Lemma

1
, we rewrite
g=\left\langle a_{1},\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}}\right\rangle
and

\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}}h,

for
g,h\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
with
g,h
independent. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle|^{4}+|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle|^{2}\right)|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|x\|_{2}^{4}g^{4}+\|x\|_{2}^{2}g^{2}\right)\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}}h\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=105\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{4}+90\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)+9\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+15\|x\|_{2}^{2}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{4}+18\|x\|_{2}^{2}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)+3\|x\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=24\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{4}+72\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}+9\|x\|_{2}^{4}+12\|x\|_{2}^{2}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}+3\|x\|_{2}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|x\|_{2}^{2}g^{2}\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}}h\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=2\|x\|_{2}^{2}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}+\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left(20\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{4}+68\|x\|_{2}^{4}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}+8\|x\|_{2}^{4}+12\|x\|_{2}^{2}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}+3\|x\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left(10\|x\|_{2}^{2}+4\|x\|_{2}\right).

For the Bernoulli model, we have to consider

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(y_{1}^{2}|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp\left(-\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right)|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right]

and

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp\left(-\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right)\left|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}.

Apart from the expectations derived in the proof of
Lemma

2
, we require

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g^{4}\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}g^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{4}\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}t^{2})\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\left[-\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\exp\left(-\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)t^{2}\right)\cdot t^{3}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\ \frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\int_{\infty}^{\infty}3t^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{2\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)t^{2}}{2}\right)dt\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{2}\exp(-\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}t^{2})\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\left[-\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\exp\left(-\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)t^{2}\right)\cdot t\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\ \frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\int_{\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{2\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)t^{2}}{2}\right)dt\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)du\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{5}{2}}}.

Then, rewriting
\left\langle a_{1},\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle
as before for the Poisson model, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp\left(-\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right)|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp\left(-\|x\|_{2}^{2}g^{2}\right)\right)\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}}h\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{4}\left(3-\frac{3}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\right)+6\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)^{2}\left(3-\frac{3}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp\left(-\left|\left\langle a_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)\right)|\left\langle a_{1},z\right\rangle|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\exp\left(-\|x\|_{2}^{2}g^{2}\right)\right)\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle g+\sqrt{1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}}h\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{4}\left(2-\frac{3}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{5}{2}}}+\frac{2}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{6}{2}}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+2\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)\left(2-\frac{2}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{4}{2}}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+\left(1-\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},z\right\rangle^{2}\right)^{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{(2\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+1)^{\frac{4}{2}}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{12}{m}}.

We summarize that in both cases there exists a constant
\hat{C}>0
so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(C\tau\left(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\log(n)}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)-V\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}y_{i}\mathds{1}_{E}a_{i}a_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{C}\tau\left(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\log(n)}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right)\right),

which we already know is bounded from above by
n^{-\beta}
.

All together, we showed that there exist absolute constants
C_{\beta},\hat{C}_{\beta},c_{\beta},\beta>0
so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\|\geq C_{\beta}\tau\sqrt{\log(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}+\frac{n}{m}\right)\right)\leq n^{-\beta}+m\exp\left(-c_{\beta}n\right)+\mathds{1}_{P}\cdot m^{-\beta},

where
\tau=1
for the Bernoulli model and
\tau=\left(\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}+\hat{C}_{\beta}\right)\log(m)
in case of the Poisson model.

Under the assumption
m\geq n\log(n)
we obtain the statement.

∎

With this deviation bound at hand, we can evaluate how well
x_{0}
approximates the ground-truth object
x
in the case of the Poisson and the Bernoulli measurement model. For this purpose, we define the phaseless distance between two vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

\textit{dist}(u,v)=\min_{\gamma\in\{-1,1\}}\|u-\gamma v\|_{2}.

Theorem 5
.

Let
m\geq n\log(n)
.
If the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are independent and distributed according to the Poisson model (
P
), there exist absolute constants
{C_{\beta},c_{\beta},\beta>0}
, such that,
with probability at least
1-n^{-\beta}-m\exp\left(-c_{\beta}n\right)-m^{-\beta}
,
the vector
x_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\textit{dist}(x_{0},x)^{2}}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}\leq 2C_{\beta}\left(1+\frac{\hat{C}_{\beta}}{\alpha}\right)\log(m)\sqrt{\log(n)}\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}.

If the
y_{i},\ i\in[m]
, are independent and distributed according to the Bernoulli model (
B
), there exist absolute constants
{C_{\beta},c_{\beta},\beta>0}
, such that,
with probability at least
1-n^{-\beta}-m\exp\left(-c_{\beta}n\right)
,
the vector
x_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\textit{dist}(x_{0},x)^{2}}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}\leq 2C_{\beta}\frac{\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\alpha}\sqrt{\log(n)}\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}.

Proof.

We aim to bound the phaseless distance

\displaystyle\textit{dist}(x_{0},x)^{2}

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in\left\{-1,1\right\}}\left\|x_{0}-\gamma x\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in\left\{-1,1\right\}}\left(\left\|x_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\gamma\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle+\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|x_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|+\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\alpha-2\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|.

Thus, we need to establish a lower bound for
\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|
. We proceed similarly to
[
12
]
, using that
x_{0}
is the eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
Y
normalized so that
\left\|x_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\alpha
. We use that

\displaystyle\left|\alpha\lambda_{0}-x_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]x_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\ =\left|x_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}Yx_{0}-x_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]x_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\ =\left|x_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\left(Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right)x_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right\|.

Setting
\delta\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right\|
, the inequality above yields

\displaystyle x_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]x_{0}\geq\alpha\lambda_{0}-\alpha\delta.

Further, the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
Y
satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}x^{\mathrm{T}}Yx=\frac{1}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}x^{\mathrm{T}}\left(Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right)x

\displaystyle\geq-\delta+\frac{1}{\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}}x^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]x,

so that

\displaystyle x_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]x_{0}\geq-2\alpha\delta+x^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]x.

For the Poisson model,
Lemma

2
provides
\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=2xx^{\mathrm{T}}+\|x\|_{2}^{2}\cdot I_{n}
, and hence

\displaystyle 2\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\|x_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}\geq-2\alpha\delta+3\|x\|_{2}^{4},

so we conclude

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq-\alpha\delta+\alpha^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\textit{dist}(x_{0},x)^{2}}{\alpha}\leq 2-2\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\delta\ }\ \leq 2\frac{\delta}{\alpha}

if
\delta\leq\alpha
.
Applying the first part of
Lemma

4
to estimate
\delta
yields the result for the Poisson model.

For the Bernoulli model,
Lemma

2
yields

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot\|x_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq-2\alpha\delta+\frac{2}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot\|x\|_{2}^{4}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2\|x\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle x_{0},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\left(\alpha^{2}\cdot\frac{2}{\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}-2\alpha\delta\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}-\alpha\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\textit{dist}(x_{0},x)^{2}}{\alpha}\leq 2-2\sqrt{1-\frac{\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\alpha}\cdot\delta\ }\ \leq 2\cdot\frac{\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\alpha}\cdot\delta.

Applying the second part of
Lemma

4
to obtain an upper bound for
\delta
completes the proof.

∎

Remark 6
.

From the bound in
Theorem

5
, we conclude that

\frac{m}{\log(m)^{2}}=\mathcal{O}\left(n\log(n)\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\right)

(7)

measurements are required to recover the ground-truth object up to a small constant relative error from Poisson phaseless measurements by
solving (
1
).

Furthermore, we find that

m=\mathcal{O}\left(n\log(n)\cdot\frac{\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{3}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)

(8)

measurements are required to recover the ground-truth object up to a small constant relative error from Bernoulli phaseless measurements when
solving the optimization problem (
1
).

We note that the factor
\left(2\alpha+1\right)^{3}/\alpha^{2}
in (
8
) is minimal for
\alpha=1
and its contribution can be interpreted as follows. For very small, as well as for rather large values of
\alpha
, we need a lot of oversampling. In the former case, this is due to the small amount of information captured by the measurements. This phenomenon is naturally the same for general Poisson observations, hidden in the factor
\left(1+1/\alpha\right)^{2}
in the sampling complexity (
7
). In the latter case, this is due to a loss of information because of the truncation in the Bernoulli model.

Abstract
. We compute extension groups in the category of duals of p p -adic Banach space representations of GL 2 ​ ( ℚ p ) \mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) . Focusing on representations arising from the p p -adic local Langlands correspondence for generic Galois representations, we classify these extensions completely. These results are then applied to prove the vanishing of extensions between the duals of reducible representations and supercuspidal isotypic components of the ètale cohomology of the finite level Drinfeld spaces.

1.
Introduction

Fix a prime
p
and let
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}:=\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
denote the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The study of
\ell
-adic representations of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, in the case where
\ell=p
, was pioneered by Fontaine, who laid the foundations of
p
-adic Hodge theory, and was further developed by Berger
[
1
]
and Colmez
[
9
]
. The
p
-adic Langlands program, initiated by Breuil and further developed by Berger, Colmez, Dospinescu, Emerton, Kisin, and Paskunas, associates a
p
-adic admissible unitary Banach space representation
\Pi(V)
of the group
\mathrm{G}:={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
to a representation
V
of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of dimension
\leq 2
.

While the bijection between the isomorphism classes of the above categories of representations is well-understood, a deeper understanding of the correspondence requires analyzing the derived category of representations; specifically, the extension groups between them. These extensions are central to the compatibility between the deformation theory of Galois representations and the deformation theory of
\mathrm{G}
-representations
[
21
]
.

Significant progress has been made in computing extension groups in the context of smooth mod
p
representations of
\mathrm{G}
. Emerton
[
13
]
calculated the
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}
groups in the category of locally admissible mod
p
representations of
\mathrm{G}
as an application of his computation of the right derived functors of the ordinary parts. Breuil and Paskunas investigated similar extensions for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
(where
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the number ring of some finite extension
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
), while Colmez
[
9
]
also performed foundational calculations for
\mathrm{G}
in characteristic
p
. In
[
20
]
, Paskunas completed the computation of
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(V_{1},V_{2})
for absolutely irreducible mod
p
representations
V_{1},V_{2}
(treating the previously open cases where
V_{1}
is supersingular and
V_{2}
is either supersingular or a twist of the Steinberg).

In characteristic
0
, however, the literature is sparse. Hauseux
[
17
,
18
]
has determined the extensions between unitary continuous
p
-adic and smooth mod
p
principal series representations of
G^{\prime}(F)
in the generic case for split connected reductive groups
G^{\prime}
. Paskunas has calculated extensions between two supercuspidal representations of
\mathrm{G}
in the
p
-adic setting
[
23
]
.

The novelty of this short note lies in the computation of extensions in the category of duals of finite length admissible Banach space representations, specifically when one of the representations is the dual of a non-ordinary representation. We say that a
p
-adic Banach space representation is non-ordinary if it is not a subquotient of a principal series representation. This dual category arises naturally in Colmez’s construction of the
p
-adic Langlands correspondence
[
9
]
. If
D
denotes the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module attached to a
p
-adic Galois representation

via Fontaine’s equivalence
[

(where

p
-adic cyclotomic character), the global sections of Colmez’s sheaf

fit into the fundamental exact sequence
[
9
]
:

(1)

0\to\Pi(V)^{*}\otimes\delta\to D\boxtimes_{\delta}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\Pi(V)\to 0.

Here
\Pi(V)^{*}
denotes the continuous dual equipped with the weak topology. The functor
\mathbf{V}
(Colmez’s Montréal functor) and
\Pi
satisfy the relation
\mathbf{V}(\Pi(V))\cong V
.

Since the category of admissible representations on
p
-adic Banach spaces does not have enough injectives, we utilize Yoneda’s construction to compute the extension groups in

(cf. Sec

with the ring of integers

\overline{V}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be an absolutely irreducible two-dimensional representation of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
over

3.1.
Two dimensional pseudocharacters

We begin by reviewing some properties regarding
2
-dimensional pseudocharacters. Since these facts are standard, we quote much of this and the following paragraph directly from
[
21
, Appendix A]
. Let
\mathcal{G}
denote a profinite group, and let
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
be a local artinian
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. Throughout, we assume that
p>2
. A continuous map
T:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow A
is defined as a
2
-dimensional
A
-valued pseudocharacter if it adheres to the following conditions:

(1)

T(1)=2
;

(2)

T(gh)=T(hg)
for every
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
;

(3)

The relation
T(g)T(h)T(k)-T(g)T(hk)-T(h)T(gk)-T(k)T(gh)+T(ghk)+T(gkh)=0
holds for all
g,h,k\in\mathcal{G}
.

It is a well-known result that for any continuous representation
\rho:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(A)
, the associated trace function
\mathrm{tr}\rho
constitutes a
2
-dimensional pseudocharacter. Conversely, for a given
2
-dimensional pseudocharacter
T:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow A
, one can define a continuous group homomorphism
\mathcal{G}\rightarrow A^{\times}
via the formula
D(g):=\frac{T(g)^{2}-T(g^{2})}{2}
(see
[
4
, Proof of Prop. 7.23]
). As demonstrated in
[
4
, Lemma 7.7, Prop. 7.23]
, the mapping
T\mapsto(T,D)
establishes a bijection between the set of
2
-dimensional pseudocharacters and the set of pairs
(T,D)
, where
D:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow A^{\times}
is a continuous group homomorphism and
T:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow A
is a continuous function which satisfies:
T(1)=2
,
T(gh)=T(hg)
, and the identity
D(g)T(g^{-1}h)-T(g)T(h)+T(gh)=0
for all
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
.

Consider a continuous representation
\rho:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
. Let
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps}}
denote the functor from local artinian augmented
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with residue field
k
to the category of sets, where
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps}}(A)
consists of all
2
-dimensional
A
-valued pseudocharacters
T
satisfying the condition
T\equiv\mathrm{tr}\rho\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{A}}
. Provided that
\mathrm{Hom}^{cont}(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space for every open subgroup
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
, this functor
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is pro-represented by a complete local noetherian
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. Notably, this finiteness criterion is met when
\mathcal{G}
represents the absolute Galois group of a local field.

In practice, it is common to utilize a modified version where the determinant is fixed. Let
\psi:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character that lifts
\det\rho
. We define
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
as the subfunctor of
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps}}
where
T\in D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}(A)
if and only if
\frac{T(g)^{2}-T(g^{2})}{2}
coincides with (the image of)
\psi(g)
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. We term
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
as a deformation problem with fixed determinant. If
R_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps}}
pro-represents
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps}}
, it follows that
D_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
is pro-represented by a quotient of this ring, which we denote by
R_{\mathrm{tr}\rho}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
.

Recall that for the identity functor
I_{\mathcal{C}}

of any category
\mathcal{C}
, the natural transformations
\eta:I_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow I_{\mathcal{C}}

form a commutative monoid, called the center of the category
\mathcal{C}
. The following theorem characterizes the center of the category
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{lfin}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
, as established in
[
23
, Theorem 1.3]
(refer also to
[
21
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose the block
\mathfrak{B}
is given by (1) or (2) above. There exists a natural isomorphism between the center of the category
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{lfin}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
and the ring
R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
.

In the context of Banach space representation, for a block
\mathfrak{B}
, let
\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}
be the full subcategory of
\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)

consisting of those
\Pi
, such that for some (equivalently any) open bounded
\mathrm{G}
-invariant lattice

\Theta
, all the irreducible subquotients of
\Theta\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}k
lie in
\mathfrak{B}
. It is shown in
[
21
, Prop. 5.36]
that
\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)
decomposes into a direct sum of subcategories:

(3)

\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)\cong\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{B}}\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}.

The Chinese remainder theorem, see
[
21
, Theorem 4.36]
, yields an equivalence of categories

(4)

\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}.\mathrm{fl}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}\cong\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{maxSpec}R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}[1/p]}\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}.\mathrm{fl}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}_{\mathfrak{n}},

where, for a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}[1/p]
,
\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}.\mathrm{fl}}_{G,\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}_{\mathfrak{n}}
consists of those
finite length representations that are killed by a power of
\mathfrak{n}.

Consider
\Pi\in\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}
equipped with an open bounded
G
-invariant lattice
\Theta
. Then for every
n\geq 1
,

\Theta/\varpi^{n}
is an object of
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{lfin}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
for all
n\geq 1
. Theorem
3.1
gives a natural action of
R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
on
\Theta/\varpi^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Upon taking the inverse limit and inverting
p
, we obtain a natural homomorphism
x:R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}[1/p]\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\Pi)
. Given that
\Pi
is absolutely irreducible, Schur’s Lemma
[
21
, Corollary 4.42]
implies the isomorphism
\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\Pi)\cong E.
Finally, assuming the block
\mathfrak{B}
corresponds to types (1) or (2) as defined in the preceding section,
[
23
, Cor. 2.25]
provides the subsequent commutative diagram:

(5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}[1/p]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t^{\mathrm{univ}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tr}\check{\mathbf{V}}(\Pi)}

where
t^{\mathrm{univ}}
is the universal pseudocharacter of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the maximal ideal of
R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}[1/p]
corresponding to
\Pi
in the above diagram. Then by the construction of the specialization map
x
, we have
\eta\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}_{R_{\mathrm{tr}\overline{\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}[1/p]}(\Pi).
Therefore
\Pi
is an element of
\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}.\mathrm{fl}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(E)^{\mathfrak{B}}_{\mathfrak{n}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
\pi
and
\Pi(V)
are absolutely irreducible, their continuous duals
\pi^{*}
and
\Pi(V)^{*}
are also absolutely irreducible. Therefore, by
[
8
, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.6]
, they are of finite length. Now if
\pi^{*}
and
\Pi(V)^{*}
are in
Ban_{\mathrm{G},\zeta}^{\mathrm{adm}}(E)^{\mathcal{B}}
for different
\mathcal{B}
, then by the block decompoition
3
, we are done. Assume they lie in
Ban_{\mathrm{G},\zeta}^{\mathrm{adm}}(E)^{\mathcal{B}}
for the same block
\mathcal{B}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have
\check{\mathbf{V}}(\Pi(V)^{*})=V
, which is an absolutely irreducible representation of dimension
2
. Set
r:=\check{\mathbf{V}}(\pi^{*})
, which is also an absolutely irreducible
E
-representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the maximal ideal of
R^{ps,\zeta\epsilon}_{tr\,\overline{\rho_{\mathcal{B}}}}[1/p]
corresponding to
\mathrm{tr}\,V
, and let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
R^{ps,\zeta\epsilon}_{tr\,\overline{\rho_{\mathcal{B}}}}[1/p]
corresponding to
\mathrm{tr}\,r
. Since
\overline{V}
is generic, the above discussion implies
\Pi(V)^{*}\in Ban_{\mathrm{G},\zeta}^{\mathrm{adm}}(E)^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{n}}
and
\pi^{*}\in Ban_{\mathrm{G},\zeta}^{\mathrm{adm}}(E)^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. By the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem, the trace character is determined by the semisimplification, so
\mathrm{tr}\,V
and
\mathrm{tr}\,r
depend only on
V^{ss}\cong V
and
r^{ss}\cong r
, respectively. Thus
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{m}
can occur only when
V\cong r.

If
\pi
is ordinary, then by the isomorphisms in (
2
),
r
is one-dimensional, so
r
can never be isomorphic to
V
. When
\pi
is non-ordinary, first isomorphism in (
2
) tells us
r=\mathbf{V}(\pi)
. Therefore if
\mathbf{V}(\pi)\neq V
, then
[
19
, Corollary 6.8]
yields
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{G}}^{i}(\pi^{*},\Pi(V)^{*})=0
for all
i\geq 0
.

For the case
\mathbf{V}(\pi)=V=\mathbf{V}(\Pi(V))
, we have the equality
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{m}
between the maximal ideals of
R^{ps,\zeta\epsilon}_{tr\,\overline{\rho_{\mathcal{B}}}}[1/p]
. Following the proof of
[
23
, Corollary 2.26]
, we get that
\Ban^{\mathrm{adm}.\mathrm{fl}}_{\mathrm{G},\psi}(L)^{\mathfrak{B}}_{\mathfrak{n}}
is anti-equivalent to the category of
A
-modules of finite length, where
A
denotes the completion of
R^{ps,\zeta\epsilon}_{tr\,\overline{\rho_{\mathcal{B}}}}[1/p]
at
\mathfrak{n}
. Via this anti-equivalence, the objects
\pi^{*}
and
\Pi(V)^{*}
are both identified
with the residue field
k
of
A
. Consequently, we obtain the identification:

\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{G}}^{i}(\pi^{*},\Pi(V)^{*})\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{i}(k,k).

Moreover in the proof of
[
23
, Corollary 2.26]
, it is shown that
A
is a complete regular local ring of dimension
3
. Using the Koszul complex, we compute
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{i}(k,k)
, and by
[
24
, Exercise 4.5.6]
this group is a
\binom{3}{i}
-dimensional
k
-vector space. As the residue field
k
is identified with
E
via the specailization map
x
, we have the required dimension results.
∎

4.
Rational Cohomology of Drinfeld Space

We recall the construction of the Drinfeld spaces as described in
[
5
, §0.1]
. For
l\neq p
, works of Faltings, Fargues, Harris, and Taylor establish that the étale cohomology of the Drinfeld spaces encode both the classical local Langlands and the classical Jacquet-Langlands for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. It is expected that the
p
-adic étale cohomology groups similarly encode the hypothetical
p
-adic local Langlands.

Let
\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,
D
denotes the quaternion algebra with center
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\check{\mathrm{G}}
be the group of invertible elements
D
. We write
\mathcal{O}_{D}
for the maximal order of
D
, and
\varpi_{D}
is a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}_{D}
. The level structure is given by the sequence of subgroups

\check{\mathrm{G}}_{n}:=\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}&\text{if }n=0,\\
1+\varpi^{n}_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}&\text{if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Let
\Omega_{Dr,p}:={\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}-{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
denote Drinfeld’s
p
-adic upper half-plane. In
[
10
]
, Drinfeld introduced certain covers
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
of
\Omega_{Dr,p}
.
This covering is defined over
\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}:=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}^{nr}_{p}}
and the action of the Weil group
W_{p}
is compatible with the natural action of
\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. There is a natural covering map
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{n+1}\rightarrow\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\rightarrow\Omega_{Dr,p}
compatible with the action of
\mathrm{G}
and
\check{\mathrm{G}}
. The zeroth level of this tower is given by
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=\mathbb{Z}\times\Omega_{\text{Dr},p}
, while for
n\geq 1
, the space
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is a Galois cover of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
, with the Galois group
\check{\mathrm{G}}_{0}/\check{\mathrm{G}}_{n}=\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/(1+\varpi_{D}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D})
. Define
\mathcal{M}_{n,\mathbb{C}_{p}}:=\mathbb{C}_{p}\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
and let
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
denote the (non-complete) projective limit of
\mathcal{M}_{n,\mathbb{C}_{p}}
. We now study the structure of the
p
-adic étale cohomology of the Drinfeld spaces as a
\mathrm{G}
-representation.

Let
E
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with the residue field
\kappa
, and let
V
be a de Rham representation of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}:=\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
of dimension
2
with Hodge-Tate weights in
(0,1)
. One can associate to
V
the filtered
E-(\varphi,N,\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-module
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{pst}}(V)
, a rank-two module over
E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{nr}}
. By Fontaine’s construction
[
15
]
, we have the associated Weil–Deligne representation
WD(V):=WD(\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{pst}}(V))
of dimension
2
over
E
. We write
LL(V):=LL(WD(V))
for the irreducible smooth
E
-representation of
\mathrm{G}
arising via the classical local Langlands correspondence. It is an infinite-dimensional
\mathrm{G}
-representation and may be recovered as the space of
\mathrm{G}
-smooth vectors inside the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence
\Pi(V)
. We say that
V
is supercuspidal if
\mathrm{WD}(V)
is irreducible (in which case

\mathrm{LL}(V)
is supercuspidal). This implies, in particular, that
N=0
on

D_{\mathrm{pst}}(V)
(i.e.
V
is potentially crystalline). Likewise, we denote by
JL(V):=JL(LL(V))
the smooth irreducible
E
-representation of
\check{\mathrm{G}}
, obtained via the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence. The representations
\mathrm{WD}(V)
,
\mathrm{LL}(V)
and
\mathrm{JL}(V)
depend only on the

E
–
(\varphi,N,G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-module
D_{\mathrm{pst}}(V)
, whereas
\Pi(V)
genuinely depends on
V
itself. Unlike
LL(V)
, the representation
JL(V)
is finite-dimensional over
E
.

The following fundamental result of Colmez–Dospinescu–Nizioł (
[
5
, Thm. 0.2]
; see also
[
6
, Thm. 0.14]
) describes the
V
-isotypic component of the first ètale cohomology of the finite-level Drinfeld spaces as a
G\times\check{G}-
representation.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V
be an absolutely irreducible
E
-representation of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of dimension
\geq 2
.

(i)

If
V
is supercuspidal of dimension
2
and of Hodge-Tate weights
0
and
1
, then

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\rm W}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\!\left(V,\,E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}({\mathcal{M}}_{\infty},\mathbb{Q}_{p}(1))\right)={\rm JL}(V)\otimes_{L}\Pi(V)^{*}.

(ii)

In all other cases,

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\rm W}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\!\left(V,\,E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}({\mathcal{M}}_{\infty},\mathbb{Q}_{p}(1))\right)=0.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

As
JL(V)
is a finite-dimensional
E
-vector space, from the above Theorem
4.1
,we obtain the following isomorphism of
\mathrm{G}
-representations:

(6)

H^{1}_{\rm et}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty},E(1))[V]\cong(\Pi(V)^{*})^{\oplus r}

where
V
is an absolutely irreducible representation of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and

r=\begin{cases}\dim_{E}(JL(V))&\text{if $V$ is supercuspidal of dimension $2$ and H-T weights $0$ and $1$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By the isomorphism
6
, it is enough to prove that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{G}}^{i}(\Pi(\rho_{p})^{*},\Pi(V)^{*})=0
for all
i\geq 0
. From Emerton’s description of
\Pi(\rho_{p})
in section
2
, we get the exact sequence of duals:

(7)

0\rightarrow\pi_{2}^{*}\rightarrow\Pi(\rho_{p})^{*}\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{*}\rightarrow 0,

where
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are (twists of) principal series representation on a
p
-adic Banach space. Applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{G}}(\_,\Pi(V)^{*})
on the above short exact sequence, we get the following long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow...\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{G}}^{i}(\pi_{1}^{*},\Pi(V)^{*})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{G}}^{i}(\Pi(\rho_{p})^{*},\Pi(V)^{*})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{G}}^{i}(\pi_{2}^{*},\Pi(V)^{*})\rightarrow...

Using
1.1
on this exact sequence, we get the result.
∎

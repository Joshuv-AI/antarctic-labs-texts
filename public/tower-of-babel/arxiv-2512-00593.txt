Spin-wave emission with current-controlled frequency by a PMA-based spin-Hall oscillator
M. Bechberger, D. Breitbach, A. Koujok, B. Heinz, C. Dubs, A. Hamadeh, P. Pirro

Abstract
Spin-torque and spin-Hall oscillators (SHOs) have emerged as promising candidates for building blocks in neuromorphic computing due to their ability to synchronize mutually, a process that can be mediated by propagating spin waves. We demonstrate a SHO that takes advantage of a low-damping magnetic garnet with dominant perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA), namely gallium-substituted yttrium-iron-garnet (Ga:YIG). In-plane magnetized Ga:YIG allows for the operation at a high efficiency level while also enabling resonant spin-wave emission. A nonlinear self-localization of the excitation is avoided by exploiting the positive nonlinear frequency shift, which facilitates a current-controlled frequency of the emitted spin waves. Via micro-focused Brillouin light scattering spectroscopy, we investigate the properties of the local auto-oscillation and its spin-wave emission. Multiple modes are excited and compete internally, with two propagating modes detected up to distances larger than 10 µ ​ m 10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m} . Their frequencies combine to an extended frequency bandwidth of approximately 1.6 GHz 1.6\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz} . The experimentally observed two-mode system and its transition to a single mode at higher currents are reproduced via micromagnetic simulations, which account for spatial variation of the PMA arising due to the microstructures on Ga:YIG. Our results propose a promising platform for hosting SHOs, interconnected via propagating spin waves with particular relevance to neuromorphic computing.



I
Introduction

The field of neuromorphic computing is inspired by the human brain, a highly interconnected network of nonlinear neurons, which achieves unprecedented energy efficiency for information processing. Consequently, the development of nanoscaled elements for hardware-based neuromorphic approaches has become subject of significant interest
Ross et al. 2023
;
Rodrigues et al. 2023
.
In this regard, spin-torque oscillators and spin-Hall oscillators (SHOs) are of particular interest in the field of spintronics
Demidov et al. 2017a
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Grollier et al. 2020
. The two approaches rely on the application of spin-orbit torques (SOT), such as the spin-transfer torque
Slonczewski 1996
;
Slonczewski 1999
;
Ralph and Stiles 2008
, which compensate the natural damping of a magnetic material to achieve coherent auto-oscillations
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2009
. In particular, their ability to synchronize to external sources
Rippard et al. 2005
;
Demidov et al. 2014
or to interact mutually
Kaka et al. 2005
is a key feature for their implementation in neural networks. While short-range coupling can be mediated through dipolar or exchange interactions
Locatelli et al. 2015
;
Kurokawa et al. 2022
, long-range coupling has been demonstrated utilizing electric currents, requiring energy-inefficient conversions between magnetic and electric domains
Grollier, Cros, and Fert 2006
;
Lebrun et al. 2017
.
Therefore, using spin waves to couple through the magnetic medium itself emerges as a promising approach.
A key requirement for this coupling is the ability of the auto-oscillation to resonantly emit spin waves into the surrounding spin-wave medium. This is only possible if the auto-oscillation frequency within the spin-injection region corresponds to a propagating mode in the surrounding area.
It has been demonstrated that inducing perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) is effective for SHOs to operate within the positive nonlinear frequency shift regime, which appears to enable spin-wave emission
Fulara et al. 2019
;
Succar and Haidar 2023
. Nevertheless, those systems often require the magnetization oriented at an oblique out-of-plane angle, reducing the efficiency of the spin Hall effect
Hirsch 1999
(SHE). Accordingly, the (anti-)damping torque is maximized for in-plane magnetized SHOs
Divinskiy et al. 2017
.
In such systems based on metallic ferromagnets, spin-wave mediated synchronization has already been achieved, but the devices are limited to short distances due to high spin-wave damping
Kaka et al. 2005
;
Zahedinejad et al. 2020
;
Kumar et al. 2025
. Consequently, low-damping materials such as yttrium iron garnet (YIG) offer great potential for longer interaction ranges
Dubs et al. 2020
;
Heinz et al. 2020
. Spatially localized magnetization dynamics have been observed in YIG/Pt-based SHOs
Collet et al. 2016
;
Demidov et al. 2016a
;
Schneider et al. 2020
;
Schneider et al. 2021
due to the negative nonlinear frequency shift for the in-plane magnetized configuration. In this case, spin-wave emission can still be enabled by exploiting the band structure, achieving nonlinear scattering to other spin-wave modes, such as PSSWs
Schlitz et al. 2025
.
Another approach has been demonstrated in Bi-substituted YIG
Soumah et al. 2018
, whose PMA was chosen to eliminate the nonlinear frequency shift, leading to the emission of coherent spin waves with notable propagation distances
Evelt et al. 2018
. However, both approaches are limited by the absence of frequency tunability, which can be crucial for neuromorphic computing
Romera et al. 2018
.

In this work, we present a garnet-based SHO that operates within the positive nonlinear frequency shift regime while being magnetized in the film plane, enabling efficient spin-wave emission tunable in frequency with long-range propagation.

This is achieved by using a gallium-substituted yttrium-iron-garnet thin film
Dubs and Surzhenko 2025
(Ga:YIG), which exhibits uniaxial anisotropy
H_{\mathrm{U}}
along the film normal that is larger than the saturation magnetization
M_{\mathrm{S}}
, resulting in a dominant PMA
Carmiggelt et al. 2021
;
Böttcher et al. 2022
;
Breitbach et al. 2024
;
Breitbach et al. 2025
. This is characterized by a negative effective magnetization
M_{\mathrm{eff}}=M_{\mathrm{S}}-H_{\mathrm{U}}<0
, leading to a positive nonlinear frequency shift for systems saturated in-plane by external fields, which represents the key distinction from pure YIG in this study.
Consequently, the SHO based on in-plane magnetized Ga:YIG can operate at maximum SHE efficiency while being within the positive nonlinear shift regime to overcome the self-localized excitation.
Our measurements confirm the emission of spin waves originating from the confined auto-oscillation region with overall tunability in frequency of approximately
1.6\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
by varying the direct charge current, which corresponds to a relative change of more than
150\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
. In our study, we measure the excited spin waves up to a distance of more than
10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
, demonstrating that Ga:YIG is a potential candidate for efficient long-range coupling of multiple SHOs via spin waves.

Characteristics of Ga:YIG and the microstructure

The auto-oscillation is generally observed to occur at the minimum frequency of the local dispersion relation

k=0

For large spin-wave amplitudes, the nonlinear nature of magnetization dynamics emerges, including the nonlinear frequency shift

\tilde{\omega_{0}}=\omega_{0}+N\cdot|c_{0}|^{2}.

(1)

Here, it can be defined by the nonlinear self-shift coefficient

\tilde{\omega_{0}}
compared to the linear FMR frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{0}}

is shifted locally above or below

77.5\text{\,}\mathrm{mT}

In conclusion, it is demonstrated that the mode structure of Ga:YIG/Pt can be modified through the fabrication of microstructures, revealing a richer mode spectrum. The local modification of material parameters has been identified as a method for enabling spin-wave emission
Demidov et al. 2016b
;
Divinskiy et al. 2018
. While these approaches rely on the variation of magnetic material geometry, the underlying microstructure exhibits a local change of magnetic properties. However, the present study focuses on the utilization of the nonlinear positive frequency shift for tunable emission of spin waves, independent of this effect.

Local auto-oscillation

To investigate the auto-oscillation properties of the SHO, the BLS spectrum on Ga:YIG/Pt is shown in Figure

I_{\mathrm{DC}}

In Fig.

These can be associated with higher-order width modes, following theoretical calculations

V
Micromagnetic study

To verify our interpretations, we study the underlying system using micromagnetic simulations. Figure
5
(a) provides a schematic illustration of the simulation setup consisting of a waveguide with a confined spin-current injector, including two edge regions with reduced out-of-plane anisotropy.
Figure
5
(b) depicts the normalized Fourier spectra of the dynamic magnetization
\mathcal{F}(m_{y}(t))
obtained on the injector and in a distance of
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
as a function of the current density
j
for different edge widths
w_{\mathrm{edge}}
.
It can be observed that a homogeneous pad with either
H_{\mathrm{U,Ga:YIG}}
(
w_{\mathrm{edge}}=0
) or
H_{\mathrm{U,edge}}
(
w_{\mathrm{edge}}=b
) generates a single-mode auto-oscillation and emission, which exhibits either a frequency bandwidth from
1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
to
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
or from
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
to
2.0\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
, respectively.
The case featuring edge regions is illustrated in the center, whose edge width of
w_{\mathrm{edge}}=\frac{2}{5}b
correspond to the actual size of the gold contacts on the microstructure. Here, both individual modes coexist within a specific current range, with the fundamental mode being dominant elsewhere. At intermediate currents, the edge mode is more pronounced in emission, while the fundamental mode becomes dominant at higher currents. This agrees well with the experiment and confirms that the two-mode system originates from edge regions of lower PMA, effectively creating distinct oscillators on the injector. However, the ratio between auto-oscillation and emission of the fundamental mode is less pronounced for the two-mode system than it is for single-mode, revealing a certain dependence of both oscillators. This might be either due to an induced nonlinear frequency (cross-)shift from the edge mode to the fundamental mode, which has not been considered in Sec.
II
, or simply because of a smaller effective oscillator size.

As the frequency shift of the fundamental mode aligns for both simulation and experiment, mere heat does not have a significant impact within the mainly used current regime. However, the frequency shift of the edge mode is similar to that of the fundamental mode in the simulation, which contradicts to the experimentally observed three-fold enhancement. This effect can be attributed to an additional strain induced via the thermal expansion of the Pt pad or gold contacts, to which the edge mode is more sensitive.
This phenomenon effectively extends the frequency bandwidth of the SHO within the accessible current range.
Note that the experimentally observed frequency shift for negative currents is not reproduced by simulations (see Supplementary material, Sec. 5B), which aligns with Eq.
1
but contradicts with the experiment.

Figure
5
(c) depicts the normalized Fourier spectrum
\mathcal{F}(m_{y}(t))
as a function of the position across the width on the Pt pad for two current density values, which represent the two- and single-mode domain.
The two-mode profile aligns with Fig.
2
(c), with the edge mode being present at the edges and in the center of the Pt Pad, while the fundamental mode is situated only in the center.
The transition to the single-mode system is observed when the frequency of the fundamental mode approaches the initial frequency level of the edge region, resulting in a single coherent auto-oscillation across the entire width, as evidenced by its width profile in Fig.
2
(c). This suggests that a single-mode system emerges whenever the spatial overlap between the fundamental and edge mode can be established, which corresponds to an increased cross-mode coupling according to a recent theoretical model
Slobodianiuk et al. 2025
.

VI
Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrated a garnet-based SHO that utilizes low-damping Ga:YIG with strain-induced PMA to obtain an operation regime, which provides large SHE efficiency while also enabling resonant spin-wave emission.
Current-control allows to tune the operation frequency over a continuous bandwidth of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
, while the low spin-wave damping of the system provides a significant interaction length of at least
10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
in potential oscillator networks. We revealed different regimes of operation, ranging from single- to multi-mode oscillation and emission, extending the full SHO bandwidth to approximately
1.6\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
. Our studies indicate that the emergence of an edge region with reduced PMA due to microstructuring is responsible for the two-mode behavior, which we confirm using micromagnetic simulations. These insights enable future experimental designs to accurately control the SHO mode structure by reducing structure dimensions or by adjustments in the fabrication process. Ultimately, the implementation of spin-wave sources based on low-damping magnetic garnets with PMA represents a promising candidate for a building block in future magnonic circuits.

Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence and pre-compactness
Andrea Mondino, Clemens Sämann

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to introduce a notion of convergence à la Gromov–Hausdorff for Lorentzian spaces, based on ε \varepsilon -nets formed by causal diamonds and depending solely on the time-separation function. This provides a geometric framework for convergence that applies both to synthetic Lorentzian spaces (Lorentzian pre-length spaces) and to smooth spacetimes. Among our main results, we establish a Lorentzian analogue of Gromov’s celebrated precompactness theorem for metric spaces, where controlled covers by balls are replaced with controlled covers by diamonds. This leads to two geometric precompactness results for families of globally hyperbolic n n -dimensional spacetimes ( M n , g ) (M^{n},g) . The first relies on suitable causality control together with the existence of a Cauchy hypersurface Σ \Sigma satisfying a uniform doubling property. The second assumes: • the existence of a compact Cauchy surface Σ \Sigma with bounded second fundamental form and bounded diameter; • a lower bound on the ambient Ricci curvature along Σ \Sigma ; • a lower bound on timelike sectional curvature on M M . This is the first Lorentzian precompactness result obtained under curvature–diameter assumptions. In the final part of the paper, we present several applications. We show that Chruściel–Grant approximations [ CG12 ] are special cases of the Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence introduced here, prove that timelike sectional curvature bounds are stable under this convergence, introduce timelike blow-up tangents, and discuss connections with the main conjecture of causal set theory. Keywords: Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, Gromov pre-compactness, nonsmooth spacetime geometry, general relativity, Lorentzian length spaces, low regularity, synthetic Lorentzian geometry. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 28A75, 51K10, 53C23, 53C50, 53B30, 53C80, 83C99.

Main results and outline of the paper

In Section
2
, we introduce the basic setting and fix notations. In particular, a Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell)
is a topological space
X
with a time-separation function
\ell\colon X\times X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
that satisfies the reverse triangle inequality

\ell(x,y)+\ell(y,z)\leq\ell(x,z)\,,

for all
x,y,z\in X
and with the convention that the left-hand-side is
-\infty
if it were undefined otherwise.
Then, in Section
3
, we introduce the basic ingredient of the Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, namely
\varepsilon
-nets. These are collections of causal diamonds whose timelike diameter (which is the time-separation of its vertices) is smaller than a given
\varepsilon>0
and whose union covers a given set. More precisely, a collection
\mathcal{J}=(J(p_{i},q_{i}))_{i\in I}
of causal diamonds is an
\varepsilon
-net for a subset
A\subset X
if:

•

\ell(p_{i},q_{i})\leq\varepsilon
, for all
i\in I
, and

•

A\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}J(p_{i},q_{i})
.

For a study of causal diamonds in Lorentzian length spaces see
[
BMdOS24
]
.

The Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of subsets is defined by using
\varepsilon
-nets and correspondences of their vertices. To extend the convergence from subsets to the whole space, it is convenient to specify a covering. This aspect differs from the metric case, where it suffices to fix a base point
p\in X
and then use the exhaustion of metric balls
B_{N}(p)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, centered at
p
with diverging radii. This will be called

(
\mathrm{pLGH}
for short) and denoted by

(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}}).

In Section
4
we show uniqueness of the limit within the class of globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length spaces (actually we prove uniqueness in higher generality, see Theorem
4.7
, Proposition
4.9
, and Remark
4.10

Afterwards, in Section
5

Remark 1.1
.

One important form of Gromov’s celebrated pre-compactness theorem states that the class of
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below by a constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
and diameter bounded above by
D>0
is pre-compact with respect to Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. Theorem
8.9
is similar in spirit—curvature and diameter bounds imply pre-compactness—but with key differences. In particular, the global curvature lower bound is assumed
only along timelike vectors
(rather than for all vectors as in the Riemannian case). This provides merely a “polarized” control of the geometry, seemingly too weak by itself to guarantee pre-compactness. To also obtain control in the spacelike directions (and thus over the full geometry), two additional assumptions appear both natural and necessary:

(ii)

a lower bound on the ambient Ricci curvature along
\Sigma
,

(iii)

a bound on the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
.

These assumptions are also well motivated from both the physical and PDE viewpoints. Lorentzian manifolds provide the natural geometric framework for Einstein’s equations (EE) of general relativity, which are evolution equations posed on a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
serving as initial data. In this context:

(ii)

controls the ambient gravitational field along
\Sigma
, expressed through Ricci curvature, which the EE relate directly to matter and energy;

(iii)

controls the extrinsic curvature of the initial hypersurface
\Sigma
. Since

h^{\Sigma}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}g^{\Sigma},

where
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
is the Lie derivative along the unit normal
\nu
and
g^{\Sigma}
the induced Riemannian metric, a bound on the second fundamental form provides control of the rate of change of the spatial metric. Moreover, in the Hamiltonian formulation, the induced metric
g^{\Sigma}
plays the role of the configuration variable (“position”), while the second fundamental form
h^{\Sigma}
serves as its canonical conjugate momentum.

Thus, assumptions (ii)–(iii) furnish geometric control of the initial data
\Sigma
, which propagates into the spacetime
M
under assumption (i), the lower bound on timelike sectional curvature.

In light of Gromov’s Riemannian pre-compactness theorem, it is natural to conjecture that assumption (i) could be relaxed from a lower bound on timelike
sectional
curvature to one on timelike
Ricci
curvature.

Here we present a variant of Lorentzian pre-length spaces introduced in
[

]
. Indeed, its basic axiomatization is still evolving, as is apparent from the variations used in e.g.
[

,
BM23
,

]
. For different approaches to synthetic Lorentzian geometry see for instance

]
based on the null distance and the bounded Lorentzian metric spaces studied in

Definition 2.1

(Reverse triangle inequality and causal relations)
.

Let
X
be a set and let
\ell\colon X\times X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
satisfy the
reverse triangle inequality

(1)

\ell(x,y)+\ell(y,z)\leq\ell(x,z)\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x,y,z\in X\,.

Here we employ the convention that
-\infty+\infty=\infty+(-\infty)=-\infty
on the left-hand-side.
The
timelike

\ll
and
causal
\leq
relations
are defined by

\ll:=\ell^{-1}((0,\infty])\,\hskip 20.00003pt\leq:=\ell^{-1}([0,\infty])\,.

Moreover, the
chronological
and
causal future (resp. and past)
of a point
x\in X
are defined by

\displaystyle I^{+}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in X:x\ll y\}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptI^{-}(x):=\{y\in X:y\ll x\}\,,

\displaystyle J^{+}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in X:x\leq y\}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptJ^{-}(x):=\{y\in X:y\leq x\}\,.

Finally,
chronological
and
causal diamonds
are sets of the form

I(x,y):=I^{+}(x)\cap I^{-}(y)\,,\hskip 20.00003ptJ(x,y):=J^{+}(x)\cap J^{-}(y)\,,

and we set
\ell(I(x,y)):=\ell(J(x,y)):=\ell(x,y)
for
x,y\in X
.

Definition 2.2

(Topologies from the causal relations)
.

Let
X
be a set and
\ell\colon X\times X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
. One can endow
X
with the topologies generated by the sub-base of

(i)

(
x\in X

,

(ii)

I(x,y)
(
x,y\in X
), called the
Alexandrov topology
,

(iii)

complements of causal futures and pasts
X\backslash J^{\pm}(x)
(
x\in X
), called the
causal topology
, and

(iv)

I^{\pm}(x)
,
X\backslash J^{\pm}(x)
(
x\in X
), called the

.

We generalize the definition of a
Lorentzian pre-length space

[
KS18
, Def. 2.8]
as

Definition 2.3

(Lorentzian pre-length space)
.

Let
X
be a set, let

\ell\colon X\times X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]

satisfy the reverse triangle inequality (
1
) and
\ell(x,x)\geq 0
for all

Definition 2.4

(Causality conditions)
.

A Lorentzian pre-length space

is called

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

]

Note that this definition of global hyperbolicity is stronger than the one in

]
. However, by

]
, the two notions agree in large classes of Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

Finally, we introduce

Definition 2.5

(Causal convexity)
.

A subset

(

Definition 3.4

(Correspondences and their distortions)

Let

for all

The

with the convention that
\pm\infty-\pm\infty=0

\pm\infty-\mp\infty=\mp\infty-\pm\infty=+\infty
. However, later we will only use this construction when the time-separation functions do not attain the value

if and only of

for all

Definition 3.5

(Composition of correspondences)
.

Let

be sets,

and

Clearly,

is a correspondence between

and

and if all three spaces are Lorentzian pre-length spaces, then the distortion satisfies a useful sub-additivity property under composition

, Lem. 4.8]

(2)

The

of a correspondence

and

Clearly,

is a correspondence between

and

and

are Lorentzian pre-length spaces, then

. Any correspondence

yields a map

Conversely, a surjective map

As discussed in the introduction, there is no canonical cover of a pointed, unbounded, Lorentzian space. Therefore, it is convenient to specify a cover when discussing convergence of pointed, unbounded, Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

Definition 3.8

(Lorentzian pre-length spaces with a cover)
.

A
covered Lorentzian pre-length space

(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
is a pointed Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell,o)
,

of

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

If all

is

.

Remark 3.9

(Analogies and differences with pointed metric spaces)
.

Let us stress that the key structure in Definition
3.8
, added on top of a Lorentzian pre-length space, is the covering
{\mathcal{U}}=(U_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. The only assumption on the marked point
o\in X
is to be contained in each element of the covering; a careful reader will notice that, throughout the paper, one could take any other marked point
o^{\prime}\in U_{0}
without affecting the arguments and the convergence. In this sense, one may argue that specifying the marked point
o\in X
is slightly redundant. We decided to keep it in the notation, although not strictly necessary, in order to stress that all the elements of the covering must contain the marked point
o
, and for analogy with pointed metric spaces. Indeed, for a non-compact metric space
(X,\mathsf{d})
, it is convenient to fix a marked point
\bar{x}
. A natural covering of
X
is then given by the metric balls
B_{N}(\bar{x})
centered at
\bar{x}
and diverging radii
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For a Lorentzian manifold, there is not such a natural exhaustion by bounded subsets around a marked point (observe that the sub-level sets of the time-separation are typically non-compact, as for instance it is the case in Minkowski spacetime). Fixing an exhaustion by “bounded” subsets also in Lorentzian signature will be key in the pre-compactness results proved below.

Remark 3.10

(A covered Lorentzian pre-length space is chronological)
.

The time-separation function of a covered Lorentzian pre-length space is finite-valued and hence zero on the diagonal. In particular, the space is chronological, i.e.,
x\not\ll x
for all
x\in X
.

Lemma 3.11

(Covering of spacetimes)
.

Let

2

Example 3.13

(Convergence of Lorentzian products)
.

Let
((X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of compact metric spaces that converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a compact metric space
(X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty})
. Consider the Lorentzian products
Y_{n}:=[-1,1]\times X_{n}
with time-separation functions

\displaystyle\ell_{n}((t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})):=\begin{cases}\sqrt{(1+\frac{1}{n})^{2}(t^{\prime}-t)^{2}-\mathsf{d}_{n}(x,x^{\prime})^{2}}&t\leq t^{\prime}\text{ and}\\
&\mathsf{d}_{n}(x,x^{\prime})\leq(1+\frac{1}{n})(t^{\prime}-t)\,,\\
-\infty&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

where
t,t^{\prime}\in[-1,1]
,
x,x^{\prime}\in X_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
(and
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
). We slightly enlarge the causal relation of
\ell_{n}
by adding the
\frac{1}{n}
-term so that causally related points in the limit will be eventually also causally related with respect to
\ell_{n}
. Clearly, each product
(Y_{n},\ell_{n})
is a Lorentzian pre-length space with the product topology, which is finer than the chronological topology (see
[
AGKS23
]
for more details). We claim that, up to taking subsequences:

\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]\times X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{LGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]\times X_{\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{strongly.}

To see this, let
0<\varepsilon<1
. By the Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, see e.g.
[
BBI01
, Prop. 7.4.12]
, there are metric
\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
-nets
S_{n}^{\varepsilon}\subseteq X_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that (for large
n\in\mathbb{N}
) they have the same (finite) cardinality and
S_{n}^{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{GH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
as
n\to\infty
. Thus, without loss of generality, we assume that
S_{n}^{\varepsilon}=\{s^{n,\varepsilon}_{1},\ldots,s^{n,\varepsilon}_{N}\}
for some fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Moreover, by (possibly) taking subsequences, we can ensure that

|\mathsf{d}_{n}(s_{i}^{n,\varepsilon},s_{j}^{n,\varepsilon})-\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(s_{i}^{\infty,\varepsilon},s_{j}^{\infty,\varepsilon})|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i,j
.

Set
L:=\lceil\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\rceil
,
t_{i}:=i\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for
i=-1,\ldots,L+1
, and set
x_{i,j}^{n,\varepsilon}:=(t_{i},s_{j}^{n,\varepsilon})
for
i=-1,\ldots,L+1
,
j=1,\ldots,N
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and

\mathcal{J}^{n,\varepsilon}:=\{J(x_{i-1,j}^{n,\varepsilon},x_{i+2,j}^{n,\varepsilon}):i=0,\ldots,L-1;j=1,\ldots,N\}.

Then
\mathcal{J}^{n,\varepsilon}
is a collection of causal diamonds

, satisfying

\tau_{n}(J)=\tau_{n}(x_{i-1,j}^{n,\varepsilon},x_{i+2,j}^{n,\varepsilon})=t_{i+2}-t_{i-1}=\varepsilon,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Next, we show that
[0,\frac{1}{2}]\times X_{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{n,\varepsilon}}J
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
y=(t,x)\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]\times X_{n}
, then there exists
i\in\{0,\ldots,L\}
such that
t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}
and

such that

. Consequently, we have
\mathsf{d}_{n}(x,s_{j}^{n,\varepsilon})<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}<\min(t_{i+2}-t,t-t_{i})

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(x^{n,\varepsilon}_{i-1,j},x)\geq\sqrt{(t-t_{i-1})^{2}-\mathsf{d}_{n}(x,s_{j}^{n,\varepsilon})^{2}}\geq 0,

for all

. Analogously, one shows that

for all

\displaystyle\Bigl|\ell_{n}(x_{i,j}^{n,\varepsilon},x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{n,\varepsilon})-\ell_{\infty}(x_{i,j}^{\infty,\varepsilon},x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\infty,\varepsilon})\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\sqrt{(1+\frac{1}{n})^{2}(t_{i^{\prime}}-t_{i})^{2}-\mathsf{d}_{n}(s_{j}^{n,\varepsilon},s_{j^{\prime}}^{n,\varepsilon})^{2}}-\sqrt{(t_{i^{\prime}}-t_{i})^{2}-\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(s_{j}^{\infty,\varepsilon},s_{j^{\prime}}^{\infty,\varepsilon})^{2}}\,\Bigr|\,.

A similar construction is carried out later for smooth globally hyperbolic spacetimes in the context of the geometric pre-compactness theorem, see Lemma

4
Uniqueness of limits

In analogy with Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of general metric spaces, also in the Lorentzian signature, limits may not be unique. The key fact in positive signature is that the Gromov–Hausdorff limit of a sequence of
compact
metric spaces is unique (up to isometry), cf. e.g.
[
BBI01
, 7.3.30]
. In the non-compact case, the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff limit of
complete
metric spaces is unique, see for instance
[
BBI01
, Thm. 8.1.7]
. In Lorentzian signature, the natural analog of proper metric spaces are
globally hyperbolic
Lorentzian pre-length spaces, while the natural analog of complete metric spaces are
forward complete
Lorentzian pre-length spaces. Inspired by this analogy, in this section we will show that Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff limits are unique in a suitable class of forward complete Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

First, we introduce the
point distinction property
(cf.
[
MS24
, Def. 1.1,(iii)]
for a similar property required for
\tau
).

Definition 4.1

(Point distinction property)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
S\subseteq X
. We say that
S
has the
point distinction property
if,
for all
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
, there exists
z\in S
such that

(
\mathrm{PDP}
)

\displaystyle\ell(x,z)\neq\ell(y,z)\,\text{ or }\,\,\ell(z,x)\neq\ell(z,y)\,.

We say that
X
has the point distinction property, if
S=X
does so.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that the point distinction property (

PDP

) holds for
S=X
if
X
is
distinguishing
(cf. e.g.
[
ACS20
, Def. 3.4]
), i.e., if
I^{+}(x)=I^{+}(y)
or
I^{-}(x)=I^{-}(y)
implies
x=y
. In the setting of Lorentzian length spaces distinguishability is implied by strong causality
[
ACS20
, Thm. 3.17]
.

In our setting, the point distinction property (

PDP

) implies causality, i.e., that the causal relation is a partial order.

Lemma 4.3

(PDP implies causality)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space satisfying the point distinction property (

PDP

). Then
(X,\ell)
is causal, i.e.,
\leq
is antisymmetric, hence a partial order.

Proof:
.

Let
x\leq y\leq x
and
z\in X
. Since
\ell(x,y)\geq 0
and
\ell(y,x)\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\ell(x,z)\leq\ell(y,x)+\ell(x,z)\leq\ell(y,z)\leq\ell(x,y)+\ell(y,z)\leq\ell(x,z)\,.

Similarly, one shows that
\ell(z,x)=\ell(z,y)
. Consequently, the point distinction property (

PDP

) implies that
x=y
.
∎

We next introduce the notion of isometry between Lorentzian pre-length spaces and note that we call a classical isometry between spacetimes a

\tilde{\ell}(f(x),f(y))=\ell(x,y)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in X\,,

(ii)

\tilde{\tau}(f(x),f(y))=\tau(x,y)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in X\,,

and

(iii)

-preserving and bijective.

Clearly, if

has the point-distinction property (

), then an a

-preserving map is injective.

Next we recall the notion of

Definition 4.5

•

g^{l^{\prime}}_{n}\colon V(\tilde{S}^{l^{\prime}})\rightarrow V(\tilde{S}^{l^{\prime}}_{n})
such that
\mathrm{dis}(g^{l^{\prime}}_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
. As in the proof of Theorem
4.7
we can extend
f^{l}_{n}
and
g^{l}_{n}
to bijective maps

\displaystyle f_{n}

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{V}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}V(S^{l})\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{n}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}V(S^{l}_{n}),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{dis}(f_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\,,

\displaystyle g_{n}

\displaystyle\colon\tilde{\mathcal{V}}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}V(\tilde{S}^{l})\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}V(\tilde{S}^{l}_{n}),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{dis}(g_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\,.

We construct a map
h_{n}\colon\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\overline{\tilde{U}}
with
\mathrm{dis}(h_{n})\leq\frac{2}{n}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{V}
. If
f_{n}(x)\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
we set
h_{n}(x):=g_{n}^{-1}(f_{n}(x))\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
. Otherwise, there is, by the assumption of timelike forward density, a sequence
\tilde{x}_{k}\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
with
\tilde{x}_{k}\to f_{n}(x)
and
x_{k}\ll x_{k+1}\ll f_{n}(x)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, there is a
\tilde{z}\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
with
f_{n}(x)\leq\tilde{z}
. As
g_{n}^{-1}
preserves the causal relations we get a sequence
g_{n}^{-1}(x_{k})\ll g_{n}^{-1}(x_{k+1})\ll g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{z})
. Consequently, by forward completeness, this sequence converges and we set
\lim_{k\to\infty}g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{k})=:h_{n}(x)
. This is well-defined: Let
\tilde{x}_{k}^{\prime}\ll\tilde{x}_{k+1}^{\prime}\ll f_{n}(x)
with
\tilde{x}_{k}^{\prime}\to f_{n}(x)
. Then, for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{r}
we have that
\tilde{x}_{r}^{\prime}\ll\tilde{x}_{k}\ll f_{n}(x)
, hence
g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{r}^{\prime})\ll g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{k})
. Taking the limit
k\to\infty
and using the closedness of
\leq
implies that
\tilde{g}_{n}^{-1}(x_{r}^{\prime})\leq h_{n}(x)
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking now the limit
r\to\infty
yields
\lim_{r\to\infty}g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{r}^{\prime})\leq h_{n}(x)
. Swapping the roles of
(\tilde{x}_{k})_{k}
and
(\tilde{x}_{r}^{\prime})_{r}
yields
h_{n}(x)\leq\lim_{r\to\infty}g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{r}^{\prime})
and so
h_{n}(x)=\lim_{r\to\infty}g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{r}^{\prime})
as
\leq
is a partial order.

At this point we claim that
\mathrm{dis}(h_{n})\leq\frac{2}{n}
. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
. The case
f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
is straightforward. We only consider the case
f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y)\not\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
(the mixed case can be handled in a simpler manner, cf. the proof of Theorem
4.7
). Also we can assume that
x\leq y
, hence
f_{n}(x)\leq f_{n}(y)
and only estimate the difference of the time-separation functions. By construction, there are sequences
(\tilde{x}_{k})_{k}
,
(\tilde{y}_{k})_{k}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
such that
\tilde{x}_{k}\to f_{n}(x)
,
\tilde{y}_{k}\to f_{n}(y)
and
\tilde{x}_{k}\ll\tilde{x}_{k+1}\ll f_{n}(x)
,
\tilde{y}_{k}\ll\tilde{y}_{k+1}\ll\tilde{f}_{n}(y)
. Then, we estimate

\displaystyle|\tau(x,y)-\tilde{\tau}(h_{n}(x),h_{n}(y))|

\displaystyle\leq|\tau(x,y)-\tau_{n}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y))|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+|\tau_{n}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y))-\tau_{n}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k}))|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+|\tau_{n}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})-\tilde{\tau}(g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{k}),g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{y}_{k}))|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+|\tilde{\tau}(g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{k}),g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{y}_{k}))-\tilde{\tau}(h_{n}(x),h_{n}(y))|\,.

The first term on the left-hand-side is bounded by
\mathrm{dis}(f_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
and the third one by
\mathrm{dis}(g_{n}^{-1})=\mathrm{dis}(g_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
. The second term converges to zero as
\tau_{n}
is continuous and
\tilde{x}_{k}\to f_{n}(x)
,
\tilde{y}_{k}\to f_{n}(y)
. Similarly, the last term converges to zero as
\tilde{\tau}
is continuous and
g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{x}_{k})\to h_{n}(x)
,
g_{n}^{-1}(\tilde{y}_{k})\to h_{n}(y)
by construction. This shows that
\mathrm{dis}(h^{l}_{n})\leq\frac{2}{n}
.

Swapping the roles of
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
we obtain a map
\tilde{h}_{n}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\overline{U}
as
\tilde{h}_{n}(\tilde{x})=\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{n}^{-1}(x_{k})
, where
g_{n}(\tilde{x})\not\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
and
x_{k}\ll x_{k+1}\ll g_{n}(\tilde{x})
,
x_{k}\to g_{n}(\tilde{x})
.
At this point we can conclude the proof as in Theorem
4.7
, i.e., by extending
h_{n}
to
h_{n}\colon U\rightarrow\tilde{U}
while preserving
\mathrm{dis}(h_{n})\leq\frac{2}{n}
and then by extending it to
\overline{U}
while preserving
\mathrm{dis}(h_{n})\leq\frac{2}{n}
. Analogously, we extend
\tilde{h}_{n}
to
\overline{\tilde{U}}
while preserving
\mathrm{dis}(\tilde{h}_{n})\leq\frac{2}{n}
. Then, as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
we take the limit
n\to\infty
to obtain maps
h\colon\overline{U}\rightarrow\overline{\tilde{U}}
,
\tilde{h}\colon\overline{\tilde{U}}\rightarrow\overline{U}
with
\mathrm{dis}(h)=\mathrm{dis}(\tilde{h})=0
, i.e., they are
\ell
- and
\tilde{\ell}
-preserving, respectively.

Finally (and again as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
) we do this iteratively for each covering set, while extending the previous maps. This gives global
\ell
- and
\tilde{\ell}
-preserving maps
h\colon X\rightarrow\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{h}\colon\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
, which are continuous and injective as
X
and
\tilde{X}
satisfy the point distinction property (

PDP

). Setting
F:=\tilde{h}\circ h\colon X\rightarrow X
yields a
\ell
-preserving map such that for each covering set
U
as above we have
F(\overline{U})\subseteq\overline{U}
, and
\overline{U}
is compact as
X
is properly covered. Thus we can apply Lemma
4.8
to conclude that
U\subseteq F(\overline{U})
, which shows surjectivity of
F
as the
U
s cover
X
and
F(\overline{U})\subseteq\overline{U}
. This yields surjectivity of
h
and shows that
h
is an isometry.
∎

Note that we could have used Lemma
4.8
at the end of the proof of Theorem
4.7
too, but we opted for the more direct way of exhibiting the inverse maps explicitly. It seems not clear how one would establish
\tilde{h}=h^{-1}
here directly.

Remark 4.10

(Uniqueness for globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length spaces)
.

The class
\mathfrak{X}
, in which we have uniqueness of limits both in Theorem
4.7
and Proposition
4.9
, includes the class of covered globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length spaces, as they are forward complete by Remark
4.6
, the time-separation functions are continuous by
[
KS18
, Thm. 3.28]
, they are distinguishing (hence satisfy the (

PDP

)) by
[
ACS20
, Prop. 3.17]
, have closed causal relation by
[
ACS20
, Prop. 3.15]
and the chronological topology is the Alexandrov topology, which is metrizable by assumption. Moreover, they are properly covered, as it follows from the proof of Lemma
3.11
.

5
Quotients of Lorentzian pre-length spaces

In this section we show that, by identifying points that cannot be distinguished by the time-separation function
\ell
, one can always assume that the point distinction property (

PDP

) holds. Such a procedure does not affect convergence. For an analogous construction in the setting of bounded Lorentzian metric spaces, see
[
MS24
, Subsec. 1.3]
.

Definition 5.1

(Identifying points with same time-separations)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and define the following relation
\sim
on
X

(4)

x\sim y\ {:\Leftrightarrow}\ \ell(x,z)=\ell(y,z)\,,\ \ell(z,x)=\ell(z,y)\,\ \forall z\in X\,.

Proposition 5.2

(Quotient time-separation)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space. Then the relation
\sim
defined in (
4
) is an equivalence relation and the quotient
X/_{\sim}
is a Lorentzian pre-length space with the quotient topology and the time-separation function

\ell^{\sim}([x],[y]):=\ell(x,y)\,,

where
[x],[y]
are the equivalence classes of
x,y\in X
. Finally,
(X/_{\sim},\ell^{\sim})
satisfies the point distinction property (

PDP

).

Proof:
.

Clearly
\sim
is symmetric, transitive and reflexive. Moreover,
\ell^{\sim}
is well-defined and the quotient topology is finer than the chronological topology on
X/_{\sim}
as
\pi^{-1}(I^{\pm}([x]))=\bigcup_{x\in[x]}I^{\pm}(x)
, where
\pi\colon X\rightarrow X/_{\sim}
is the quotient map.
∎

If a sequence converges in the pointed Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a limit
X
, it also converges to its time-separation quotient
X/_{\sim}
.

Theorem 5.3

(Time-separation quotient preserves limits)
.

If

(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(resp.\ strongly),}

then

(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X/_{\sim},\ell^{\sim},\pi(o),(\pi(U_{k}))_{k})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(resp.\ strongly),}

where
{\mathcal{U}}=(U_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\pi\colon X\rightarrow X/_{\sim}
is the quotient map.

Proof:
.

Any
\varepsilon
-net for a subset
A\subseteq X
is an
\varepsilon
-net for
\pi(A)\subseteq X/_{\sim}
with respect to
\ell^{\sim}
. Moreover, the distortion of correspondences does not change, the extension property of correspondences holds trivially and forward density of the vertices follows from the continuity of
\pi
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space satisfying the point-distinction property (

PDP

). Passing to the time-separation quotient identifies all points that are either not causally related, or only null-related, to the past and future with one of three distinguished equivalence classes. Depending on the situation, these classes may or may not be represented by actual points of the space. One can characterize the following three distinguished points, whenever they belong to
X
:

(i)

The point
i^{0}
is characterized as

\ell(x,i^{0})=\ell(i^{0},x)=-\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in X\backslash\{i^{0}\}\,,

and
\ell(i^{0},i^{0})=0
. Such
i^{0}
is called
spacelike boundary
in
[
MS24
, Rem. 1.2,2]
in the setting of bounded Lorentzian metric spaces.

(ii)

The
future null infinity

n^{0}_{+}
is characterized as

\ell(x,n^{0}_{+})=0\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\ell(n^{0}_{+},x)=-\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in X\backslash\{n^{0}_{+}\}\,,

and
\ell(n^{0}_{+},n^{0}_{+})=0
.

(iii)

The
past null infinity

n^{0}_{-}
is characterized as

\ell(x,n^{0}_{-})=-\infty\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\ell(n^{0}_{-},x)=0\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in X\backslash\{n^{0}_{-}\}\,,

and
\ell(n^{0}_{-},n^{0}_{-})=0
.

6
Pre-compactness

Here we give a first pre-compactness result in a completely general setting. To start, we introduce the timelike diameter of a subset of a Lorentzian pre-length space.

Definition 6.1

(Timelike diameter)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and let
A\subseteq X
be a subset. The
timelike diameter
of
A
is defined by

\mathrm{diam}^{\tau}(A):=\sup_{x,y\in A}\tau(x,y)\,.

Theorem 6.2

(Pre-compactness I)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a class of covered Lorentzian pre-length spaces such that each
(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})\in\mathfrak{X}
, with covering
{\mathcal{U}}=(U_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, satisfies the following properties.

(i)

For each fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the timelike diameter of
U_{k}
is uniformly bounded; i.e.,
\mathrm{diam}^{\tau}(U_{k})\leq T_{k}
for a constant
T_{k}\geq 0
(independent of
X
).

(ii)

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a (finite) constant
N=N(k,\varepsilon)>0
(independent of
X
) such that
U_{k}
admits an
\varepsilon
-net
S_{\varepsilon}^{k}
of cardinality at most
N
.

(iii)

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that
S_{\varepsilon}^{k}\subseteq S_{\varepsilon}^{k+1}
.

Then any sequence in
\mathfrak{X}
has a converging subsequence; i.e., for any sequence
\big((X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{X}
there exists a subsequence
(n_{j})_{j}\subset\mathbb{N}
and a covered Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
such that

(X_{n_{j}},\ell_{n_{j}},o_{n_{j}},{\mathcal{U}}_{n_{j}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{strongly, as $j\to\infty$}.

Proof:
.

Fix a sequence
\big((X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{X}
and write
{\mathcal{U}}_{n}=(U_{k,n})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We will inductively construct the limit space
X
by constructing a cover
{\mathcal{U}}=(U_{k,\infty})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Step 1:
Constructing a limit of
(U_{1,n})_{n}
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{N},m\geq 1
there is a
\frac{1}{m}
-net
S_{1,n,m}
for
U_{1,n}
, with

S_{1,n,m}=\left\{J\big(x_{1,n,m}^{1},y_{1,n,m}^{1}\big),\ldots,J\big(x_{1,n,m}^{N_{1,m}},y_{1,n,m}^{N_{1,m}}\big)\right\}.

By assumption,
|S_{1,n,m}|\leq N(1,\frac{1}{m})=:N_{1,m}
. By a diagonal argument, we can assume that, up to subsequences:

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{1,n,m}^{i},y_{1,n,m}^{j})\,,\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{1,n,m}^{i},x_{1,n,m}^{j})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{1,n,m}^{i},o_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{1,n,m}^{i},o_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(o_{n},x_{1,n,m}^{i})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(o_{n},y_{1,n,m}^{i})\,,

for all
1\leq i,j\leq N_{1,m}
. Thus, we define the (countable) space
U_{1,\infty}
as

U_{1,\infty}:=\{x_{1,\infty,1}^{1},y_{1,\infty,1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1,\infty,1}^{N_{1,1}},y_{1,\infty,1}^{N_{1,1}},x_{1,\infty,2}^{1},\ldots\}\cup\{o_{\infty}\}\,.

Step 2:
Induction step
k-1\mapsto k
.

We assume that we have constructed
U_{1,\infty}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq U_{k-1,\infty}
. By assumption, the
\varepsilon
-nets for
U_{k-1,n}
are contained in the
\varepsilon
-nets for
U_{k,n}
(for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
). By assumption,
|S_{k,n,m}|\leq N(k,\frac{1}{m})=:N_{k,m}
. Arguing as in the first step (by using a diagonal procedure twice), we preserve the convergence properties with respect to
U_{k-1,\infty}
and overall we get that for all
1\leq i,j\leq N_{k,m}
:

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{1,n,m}^{i},y_{1,n,m}^{j})\,,\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{1,n,m}^{i},x_{1,n,m}^{j})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{1,n,m}^{i},o_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{1,n,m}^{i},o_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(o_{n},x_{1,n,m}^{i})\,,

\displaystyle\exists\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(o_{n},y_{1,n,m}^{i})\,,

where
U_{k,\infty}:=U_{k-1,\infty}\,\cup\,\{x_{k,\infty,1}^{1},y_{k,\infty,1}^{1},\ldots,x_{k,\infty,1}^{N_{k,1}},y_{k,\infty,1}^{N_{k,1}},x_{k,\infty,2}^{1},\ldots\}
.

Step 3:
Construction of the limit space.

We set
X_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}U_{k,\infty}
with covering
{\mathcal{U}}_{\infty}=(U_{k,\infty})_{\infty}
. The time-separation function
\ell_{\infty}
is given as

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(x_{k,\infty,m}^{i},y_{k^{\prime},\infty,m^{\prime}}^{j})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{k,n,m}^{i},y_{k^{\prime},n,m^{\prime}}^{j})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(y_{k^{\prime},\infty,m^{\prime}}^{j},x_{k,\infty,m}^{i},)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{k^{\prime},n,m^{\prime}}^{j},x_{k,n,m}^{i})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(x_{k,\infty,m}^{i},x_{k^{\prime},\infty,m^{\prime}}^{j})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{k,n,m}^{i},x_{k^{\prime},n,m^{\prime}}^{j})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(y_{k,\infty,m}^{i},y_{k^{\prime},\infty,m^{\prime}}^{j})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{k,n,m}^{i},y_{k^{\prime},n,m^{\prime}}^{j})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(x_{k,\infty,m}^{i},o_{\infty})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(x_{k,n,m}^{i},o_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(y_{k,\infty,m}^{i},o_{\infty})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(y_{k,n,m}^{i},o_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(o_{\infty},x_{k,\infty,m}^{i})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(o_{n},x_{k,n,m}^{i})\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(o_{\infty},y_{k,\infty,m}^{i})

\displaystyle:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{n}(o_{n},y_{k,n,m}^{i})\,,

for all
i,j,k,k^{\prime},m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
. From this definition it readily follows that
\ell_{\infty}
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality and
\ell_{\infty}(x,x)\geq 0
for all
x\in X_{\infty}
.

Finally, we use the chronological topology on
X_{\infty}
, hence
(X_{\infty},\ell_{\infty})
is a Lorentzian pre-length space.

Step 4:

X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{\infty}
.

Convergence of the vertices is clear by construction. Since
X_{\infty}
only consists of the vertices and the distinguished point
o_{\infty}
, the covering property, and the timelike forward density property are trivially satisfied. Moreover, the extension property holds by construction.
This finishes the proof.
∎

7
Completion of a Lorentzian pre-length space

In the pre-compactness theorem
6.2
the limit is countable and not a continuum, hence one might wish to complete it. This is particularly relevant when showing that the limit of globally hyperbolic spaces is globally hyperbolic (cf. Theorem
A.15
, in the appendix).
To that aim, in this section we construct a
forward completion
of a Lorentzian pre-length space. This builds on the notion of forward completeness for Lorentzian length spaces introduced in
[
BBC+24
]
and it is inspired by the recent work of Gigli
[
Gig25
]
, who obtained existence and uniqueness of a forward completion in the general setting of partial orders. We refer the reader to
[
Gig25
]
for more details and results on forward completing partially ordered and Lorentzian spaces. In Appendix
A
, we also discuss an independent way of obtain a completion, more tailored to the globally hyperbolic setting.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space. A
forward completion
of
X
is a Lorentzian pre-length space
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
is forward complete (cf. Definition
4.5
);

(ii)

J^{\pm}_{\overline{\leq}}(\bar{x})\subset\overline{X}
is closed, for all
\bar{x}\in\overline{X}
;

(iii)

There exists an isometric embedding of
(X,\ell)
into
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
such that
X
is
forward dense
in
\overline{X}
, i.e., for all
\bar{x}\in\overline{X}
there exists a sequence
(x_{k})_{k}
in
X
such that
x_{k}\,\overline{\leq}\,x_{k+1}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{x}
and
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
.

Theorem 7.2

(Existence of a forward completion)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space. Then
X
admits a completion
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
. Moreover, there exists at most one completion
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
(up to isometry), such that:

(i)

The time-separation function
\overline{\tau}
is continuous;

(ii)

The causal relation
\overline{\leq}
is a closed partial order;

(iii)

For all
\bar{x}\in\overline{X}\backslash X
, it holds that
\bar{x}\in\overline{I^{\pm}(\bar{x})}
.

Proof:
.

Step 1
. Construction of the Lorentzian pre-length space.

Define

Y:=\{(x_{k})_{k}\in X^{\mathbb{N}}\text{ such that }x_{k}\leq x_{k+1}\leq z\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and for some }z\in X\}\,.

We define a time-separation function
\ell^{\prime}
on
Y
as follows. Let
(x_{k})_{k},(y_{k})_{k}\in Y
, then we set

\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l})):=\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}\ell(x_{k},y_{l})\,.

The function
\ell^{\prime}
is valued in
\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
and
\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(x_{k})_{k})\geq 0
for all
(x_{k})_{k}\in Y
. Indeed,
\ell(x_{k},x_{k})\in\{0,\infty\}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, thus

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(x_{k})_{k}))=\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}\ell(x_{k},x_{l})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\ell(x_{k},x_{k})\geq 0\,.

We next show that
\ell^{\prime}
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality.

Let
(x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l},(z_{r})_{r}\in Y
. Then, for all
k,l,r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\ell(x_{k},y_{l})+\ell(y_{l},z_{r})\leq\ell(x_{k},z_{r})
. Thus,

\displaystyle L:=\limsup_{k,l,r\to\infty}\Bigl(\ell(x_{k},y_{l})+\ell(y_{l},z_{r})\Bigr)\leq\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(z_{r})_{r})\,.

We know that
L\leq\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l})+\ell^{\prime}((y_{l})_{l},(z_{r})_{r})
; we claim that actually equality holds. Let us first consider the case
L=-\infty
. The monotonicity of the sequence
(y_{l})_{l}
, implies that if
\ell(x_{k},y_{l})=-\infty
, then
\ell(x_{k},y_{l^{\prime}})=-\infty
for all
l^{\prime}\leq l
. Thus,
L=-\infty
implies that
\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l}))+\ell^{\prime}((y_{l})_{l},(z_{r})_{r}))=-\infty
and the reverse triangle inequality is trivially satisfied. To prove the claim for
L\in\mathbb{R}
, assume by contradiction that there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that
L+\varepsilon<\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l})+\ell^{\prime}((y_{l})_{l},(z_{r})_{r})
. There are
k_{0},l_{0},r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0},l\geq l_{0},r\geq r_{0}
we have
\ell(x_{k},y_{l})+\ell(y_{l},z_{r})\leq L+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Moreover, there are subsequences
(k_{n})_{n},(l_{n^{\prime}})_{n^{\prime}},(l_{n^{\prime\prime}})_{n^{\prime\prime}},(r_{n^{\prime\prime\prime}})_{n^{\prime\prime\prime}}
with

\displaystyle\ell(x_{k_{n}},y_{l_{n^{\prime}}})

\displaystyle>\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l})-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\,,

\displaystyle\ell(y_{l_{n^{\prime\prime}}},z_{r_{n^{\prime\prime\prime}}})

\displaystyle>\ell^{\prime}((y_{l})_{l},(z_{r})_{r}))-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\,.

Take
n,n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime\prime}
large enough such that
k_{n}\geq k_{0},l_{n^{\prime}}\geq l_{0},l_{n^{\prime\prime}}\geq l_{n^{\prime}},r_{n^{\prime\prime\prime}}\geq r_{0}
. Then
\ell(x_{k_{n}},y_{l_{n^{\prime}}})\leq\ell(x_{k_{n}},y_{l_{n^{\prime\prime}}})
and we can estimate

\displaystyle L+\varepsilon

\displaystyle<\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{l})_{l}))+\ell^{\prime}((y_{l})_{l},(z_{r})_{r}))

\displaystyle\leq\ell(x_{k_{n}},y_{l_{n^{\prime}}})+\ell(y_{l_{n^{\prime\prime}}},z_{r_{n^{\prime\prime\prime}}})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ell(x_{k_{n}},y_{l_{n^{\prime\prime}}})+\ell(y_{l_{n^{\prime\prime}}},z_{r_{n^{\prime\prime\prime}}})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq L+\varepsilon\,,

which is a contradiction since
L\in\mathbb{R}
.

Step 2
. Construction of an equivalence relation
\sim
on
Y
.

Define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
Y
as follows:
(x_{k})_{k}\sim(y_{k})_{k}
if there is a
z\in X
and subsequences
(x_{k_{l}})_{l}
and
(y_{k_{l}})_{l}
of
(x_{k})_{k}
and
(y_{k})_{k}
, respectively such that

x_{k_{l}}\leq y_{k_{l}}\leq x_{k_{l+1}}\leq y_{k_{l+1}}\leq z\ \forall l\in\mathbb{N}\,.

In particular, the sequence
(z_{l})_{l}
given by

\displaystyle z_{l}:=\begin{cases}&x_{k_{l}}\qquad l\equiv 0\text{ mod }2\,,\\
&y_{k_{l}}\qquad l\equiv 1\text{ mod }2\,,\end{cases}

is in
Y
. Clearly,
\sim
is reflexive and symmetric by shifting the index of the sequence by
\pm 1
. To show transitivity, let
(x_{k})_{k},(y_{k})_{k},(z_{k})_{k}\in Y
with bounds
x,y,z
, respectively. If
(x_{k})_{k}\sim(y_{k})_{k}
and
(y_{k})_{k}\sim(z_{k})_{k}
, let
(x_{k_{l}})_{l}
,
(y_{k_{l}})_{l}
,
(y_{n_{l}})_{l}
,
(z_{k_{l}})_{l}
be the corresponding subsequences such that, for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
:

x_{k_{l}}\leq y_{k_{l}}\leq x_{k_{l+1}}\leq y_{k_{l+1}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pty_{n_{l}}\leq z_{k_{l}}\leq y_{n_{l+1}}\leq z_{k_{l+1}}\leq z\,.

Then, for
l=0
we can find
n^{\prime}_{0}
such that
n^{\prime}_{0}\geq k_{0}=:k^{\prime}_{0}
. Hence:

x_{k^{\prime}_{0}}\leq y_{k^{\prime}_{0}}\leq y_{n^{\prime}_{0}}\leq z_{n^{\prime}_{0}}\leq y_{n^{\prime}_{0}+1}\leq x_{n^{\prime}_{0}+2}\,.

Setting
k^{\prime}_{1}:=n_{0}^{\prime}+2
, we obtain the desired subsequences iteratively; since all
z_{k}
s are bounded by
z
, we conclude.

Step 3
. Endowing the quotient
\overline{X}:=Y/\sim
with the quotient time-separation
\bar{\ell}
.

Define
\overline{X}:=Y/\sim
and

\bar{\ell}([x],[y]):=\ell^{\prime}(x,y)\,,

where
[x]
is the equivalence class of
x\in Y
. First, we show that the time-separation function
\bar{\ell}
is well-defined. It suffices to fix one slot, so let
(x^{\prime}_{k})_{k}\sim(x_{k})_{k}
in
Y
and without loss of generality we can assume that
\ell(x_{k},y_{m})\to\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{m})_{m})
and
\ell(x_{k}^{\prime},y_{m^{\prime}})\to\ell^{\prime}((x_{k}^{\prime})_{k},(y_{m})_{m})
(otherwise take subsequences). By assumption, there are subsequences
(x_{k_{l}})_{l},(x^{\prime}_{k_{l}})_{l}
of
(x_{k})_{k}
and
(x^{\prime}_{k})_{k}
, respectively, such that

x_{k_{l}}\leq x^{\prime}_{k_{l}}\leq x_{k_{l+1}}\leq x^{\prime}_{k_{l+1}}\ \forall l\in\mathbb{N}\,.

By the reverse triangle inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\ell(x^{\prime}_{k_{l}},y_{m^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq\ell(x_{k_{l}},x^{\prime}_{k_{l}})+\ell(x^{\prime}_{k_{l}},y_{m^{\prime}})\leq\ell(x_{k_{l}},y_{m^{\prime}})\text{ and }

\displaystyle\ell(x_{k_{l+1}},y_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\ell(x^{\prime}_{k_{l}},x_{k_{l+1}})+\ell(x_{k_{l+1}},y_{m})\leq\ell(x^{\prime}_{k_{l}},y_{m})\,.

Now taking the limit superior as
l,m^{\prime}\to\infty
in the first inequality we get

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}((x_{k}^{\prime})_{k},(y_{m})_{m})\leq\limsup_{l,m^{\prime}\to\infty}\ell(x_{k_{l}},y_{m^{\prime}})\leq\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{m})_{m}))\,,

and
taking the limit superior as
l,m\to\infty
in the second one we get

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{m})_{m})\leq\limsup_{l,m\to\infty}\ell(x_{k_{l}}^{\prime},y_{m})\leq\ell^{\prime}((x_{k}^{\prime})_{k},(y_{m})_{m}))\,.

In summary, this gives
\ell^{\prime}((x_{k})_{k},(y_{m})_{m})=\ell^{\prime}((x_{k}^{\prime})_{k},(y_{m})_{m})
.

Step 4
.
\overline{X}
is forward complete.

Now, we topologize
\overline{X}
with the chronocausal topology (i.e., the topology generated by the subbase
I^{\pm}(y)
,
X\backslash J^{\pm}(y)
for
y\in Y
) and show that
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
is forward complete. It suffices to work in
(Y,\ell^{\prime})
: Let
y^{m}\leq y^{m+1}\leq z
in
Y
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
y^{m}=(y^{m}_{k})_{k}
,
z=(z_{k})_{k}
with
y^{m}_{k}\leq y^{m}_{k+1}\leq z^{m}
and
z_{k}\leq z_{k+1}\leq\hat{z}
for some
z^{m},\hat{z}\in X
for all
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
. The assumption
y^{m}\leq y^{m+1}\leq z
implies that there are subsequences such that
\ell(y^{m}_{k_{l}},y^{m+1}_{k_{l}^{\prime}})\to\ell^{\prime}(y^{m},y^{m+1})\geq 0
and
\ell(y^{m+1}_{\tilde{k}_{l}^{\prime}},z_{k_{l}^{\prime\prime}})\to\ell^{\prime}(y^{m+1},z)\geq 0
. In particular, since
\ell
takes values in
\{-\infty\}\cup[0,+\infty)
, we get that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
there is
l_{0}^{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
l\geq l_{0}^{m}
we have
\ell(y^{m}_{k_{l}},y^{m+1}_{k^{\prime}_{l}})\geq 0
and there is
\tilde{l}_{0}^{m}\geq l_{0}^{m}
such that for all
l\geq\tilde{l}^{m}_{0}
we have
\ell(y^{m+1}_{\tilde{k}^{\prime}_{l}},z_{k^{\prime\prime}_{l}})\geq 0
. By always choosing
\tilde{k}_{l}^{\prime}\geq k_{l}^{\prime}
we can without loss of generality assume that
\tilde{k}_{l}^{\prime}=k_{l}^{\prime}
. This yields that

y^{m}_{k_{l}}\leq y^{m+1}_{k^{\prime}_{l}}\leq z_{k^{\prime\prime}_{l}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\;m\in\mathbb{N},\,l\geq\tilde{l}_{0}^{m}.

Moreover, we can choose
(l^{m}_{0})_{m}
and
(\tilde{l}^{m}_{0})_{m}

to be non-decreasing in
m
.

We define the limit
y=(y_{k})_{k}
inductively:

First, we set
y_{0}:=y^{0}_{k_{\tilde{l}^{0}_{0}}}\leq z_{k_{\tilde{l}_{0}^{0}}^{\prime\prime}}\leq\hat{z}
. Then, we assume that we already have defined
y_{r}
for
r=0,\ldots,n
such that

(i)

y_{r}\leq y_{r+1}\leq\hat{z}
for
r=0,\ldots,n-1
, and

(ii)

y_{r}=y^{r}_{k_{l(r)}}
for some increasing function
l\colon\{0,\ldots,n\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
.

Let
l(n+1)\geq\max(l(n),\tilde{l}_{0}^{n+1})
such that
k_{l(n)}^{\prime}\leq k_{l(n+1)}
. Then, we set

y_{n+1}:=y^{n+1}_{k_{l(n+1)}}\leq z_{k_{l(n+1)}^{\prime\prime}}\leq\hat{z}\,,

hence we get that

y_{n}=y^{n}_{k_{l(n)}}\leq y^{n+1}_{k_{l(n)}^{\prime}}\leq y^{n+1}_{k_{l(n+1)}}=y_{n+1}\,.

Consequently,
y:=(y_{n})_{n}\in Y
.

Now we show that
y^{m}\to y
in
Y
with respect to the chronological topology. Let
w\in I^{-}(y)
, i.e.,

\ell^{\prime}(w,y)>0
and
w=(w_{k})_{k}
with
w_{k}\leq w_{k+1}\leq\hat{w}
for some
\hat{w}\in X
. There are subsequences such that

\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon:=\ell^{\prime}(w,y)=\limsup_{k,r\to\infty}\ell(w_{k},y_{r})=\lim_{t,n\to\infty}\ell(w_{k_{t}},y_{r_{n}})\,.

In particular, there are
t_{0},n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
t\geq t_{0},n\geq n_{0}
we have
\ell(w_{k_{t}},y_{r_{n}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0
. Let
s\geq k_{l(r_{n_{0}})}
, then

\displaystyle\ell(w_{k_{t}},y^{r_{n_{0}}}_{s})\geq\ell(w_{k_{t}},y^{r_{n_{0}}}_{k_{l(r_{n_{0}})}})=\ell(w_{k_{t}},y_{r_{n_{0}}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}(w,y^{r_{n_{0}}})=\limsup_{k,s\to\infty}\ell(w_{k},y^{r_{n_{0}}}_{s})\geq\limsup_{t,s\to\infty}\ell(w_{k_{t}},y^{r_{n_{0}}}_{s})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0\,,

i.e.,
w\ll y^{r_{n_{0}}}
. Hence for all
r^{\prime}\geq r_{n_{0}}
we have
w\ll y^{r_{n_{0}}}\leq y^{r^{\prime}}
, i.e.,
y^{r^{\prime}}\in I^{+}(w)
for all
r^{\prime}\geq r_{n_{0}}
as required.

Similarly, let
w\in I^{+}(y)
, then there are subsequences such that

\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon:=\ell^{\prime}(y,w)=\limsup_{k,r\to\infty}\ell(y_{r},w_{k})=\lim_{t,n\to\infty}\ell(y_{r_{n}},w_{k_{t}})\,.

In particular, there are
t_{0},n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
t\geq t_{0},n\geq n_{0}
, we have
\ell(y_{r_{n}},w_{k_{t}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0
. Then for
t\geq t_{0}
,
n\geq n_{0}
and
s\geq k_{l(r_{n})}
we get that

\displaystyle y^{r_{n}}_{s}\leq y^{r_{n}+1}_{k_{l(r_{n})}^{\prime}}\leq y^{r_{n}+1}_{k_{l(r_{n}+1)}}=y_{r_{n}+1}\leq\ldots\leq y_{r_{n+1}}\ll w_{k_{t}}\,.

Thus we obtain
\ell(y^{r_{n}}_{s},w_{k_{t}})\geq\ell(y_{r_{n+1}},w_{k_{t}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and so

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}(y^{r_{n}},w)\geq\limsup_{s,t\to\infty}\ell(y^{r_{n}}_{s},w_{k_{t}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0\,.

This means that
y^{r_{n}}\in I^{-}(w)
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Consequently, for
r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
we have
r^{\prime}\leq r_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq n_{0}
, so
y^{r^{\prime}}\leq y^{r_{n}}\ll w
, so
y^{r^{\prime}}\in I^{-}(w)
as required. This concludes the proof of
y^{m}\to y
in the chronological topology.

It remains to show that
y^{m}\to y
with respect to the causal topology. Let
y\not\in J^{+}(w)
, i.e.,

\lim_{k,n\to\infty}\ell(w_{k},y_{n})=-\infty\,.

Thus, there are
k_{0},n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
, we have
\ell(w_{k},y_{n})=-\infty
. Let
n\geq n_{0}
and
r\geq k_{l(n)}
, then
y^{n}_{r}\leq y^{n+1}_{k_{l(n)}^{\prime}}\leq y^{n+1}_{k_{l(n+1)}}=y_{n+1}
. So for all
k\geq k_{0},n\geq n_{0}
and
r\geq k_{l(n)}
we have
\ell(w_{k},y^{m}_{r})\leq\ell(w_{k},y_{n+1})=-\infty
, hence
\ell^{\prime}(w,y^{n+1})=-\infty
and
y^{n+1}\not\in J^{+}(w)
for
n\geq n_{0}
.

Now assume that
y\not\in J^{-}(w)
, then analogously as in the previous case we have

\lim_{k,n\to\infty}\ell(y_{n},w_{k})=-\infty\,,

and so
\ell(y_{n},w_{k})=-\infty
for all
n\geq n_{0},k\geq k_{0}
for some
n_{0},k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume by contradiction that there is a sequence
m_{r}\nearrow\infty
such that
y^{m_{r}}\in J^{-}(w)
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, there are subsequences such that for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
there are
t_{0}(r),s_{0}(r)\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell(y^{m_{r}}_{k_{t}},w_{k_{s}})\geq 0
for all
t\geq t_{0}(r),s\geq s_{0}(r)
. Then, let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\geq s_{0}(r)
. If
t_{0}(r)\leq l(m_{r})
then by the above

0\leq\ell(y^{m_{r}}_{k_{l(m_{r})}},w_{k_{s}})=-\infty\,,

a contradiction. If, on the other hand,
t_{0}(r)>l(m_{r})
we have

0\leq\ell(y^{m_{r}}_{k_{t_{0}(r)}},w_{k_{s}})\leq\ell(y^{m_{r}}_{k_{l(m_{r})}},w_{k_{s}})=-\infty\,,

again a contradiction. This concludes the proof of the forward completeness of
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
.

Step 5
. Forward dense isometric embedding of
X
into
\overline{X}
.

We can embed
X
into
\overline{X}
as constant sequences and show that these are forward dense in
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
. Let

(5)

y=(y_{k})_{k}\in Y\,.

Define the sequence
(y^{m})_{m}
in
Y
by setting

y^{m}:=(y_{m})_{k}\,.

In other words, for every

, the element

is given by the constant sequence

, where

is the

term in the sequence defining

as in (

).

First, we show that
y^{m}\leq y^{m+1}\leq y
, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, by construction,
\ell^{\prime}(y^{m},y^{m+1})=\ell(y_{m},y_{m+1})\geq 0
and
\ell^{\prime}(y^{m},y)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\ell(y_{m},y_{k})\geq 0
, as eventually

.

We now prove that
y^{m}\to y
, as
m\to\infty
. Let
w\in I^{-}(y)
,
w=(w_{k})_{k}\in Y
.
Then there are subsequences such that
0<\varepsilon:=\ell^{\prime}(w,y)=\lim_{s,t\to\infty}\ell(w_{k_{t}},y_{m_{s}})
. Thus, there are

such that, for all

, we have
\ell(w_{k_{t}},y_{m_{s}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0
. Then, for

, it holds that

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}(w,y^{m_{s}})=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\ell(w_{k},y_{m_{s}})\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\ell(w_{k_{t}},y_{m_{s}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0\,,

i.e.,

for all

. Consequently, for all

with

, and so

as required.

Now let

. Analogously to the previous case, there are subsequences such that
0<\varepsilon:=\ell^{\prime}(y,w)=\lim_{s,t\to\infty}\ell(y_{m_{s}},w_{k_{t}})
. Thus, there exist

such that
\ell(y_{m_{s}},w_{k_{t}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0
, for all

. Then, for

, we get

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}(y^{m_{s}},w)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\ell(y_{m_{s}},w_{k})\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\ell(y_{m_{s}},w_{k_{t}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0\,,

i.e.,
y^{m_{s}}\in I^{-}(w)
for all

. Consequently, for all
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
there is an
s\geq s_{0}
such that
m^{\prime}\leq m_{s}
, hence
y^{m^{\prime}}\leq y^{m_{s}}\ll w
, and so

as required.

It remains to check convergence for the open sets

. First, assume that

, then for

large we have
\ell(w_{n},y_{k})=-\infty
. This immediately implies that

, for all large

hence

for all large

. Similar, if

, then

for

large, hence

for

large. This concludes the proof that

in

.

Step 6
. Uniqueness.

Assume there exist two completions
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
and
(\overline{X}^{\prime},\overline{\ell}^{\prime})
such that the assertions (i)–(iii) hold. We construct a map
f\colon\overline{X}\rightarrow\overline{X}^{\prime}
as follows.

On
X
,
f
is the identity between the inclusions
X\subset\overline{X}
and
X\subset\overline{X}^{\prime}
.

Next we define
f
on
\overline{X}\setminus X
. For
\bar{x}\in\overline{X}\backslash X
, let
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
with

and
x_{k}\,\overline{\leq}\,x_{k+1}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{x}
, for all

. By item (iii) we have

; so let
\bar{w}\in I^{+}_{\overline{\ll}}(\bar{x})
and let
\bar{w}=\lim w_{l}
, where

,
w_{l}\,\overline{\leq}\,w_{l+1}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{w}
. Then, there exists

such that
\bar{x}\,\overline{\ll}\,w_{l}
. Consequently, for all

, we have
x_{k}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{x}\,\overline{\ll}\,w_{l}
. Then, in
\overline{X}^{\prime}
we also have
x_{k}\,\overline{\leq}^{\prime}\,x_{k+1}\,\overline{\leq}^{\prime}\,w_{l}
. Thus, by forward completeness of
\overline{X}^{\prime}
, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k}\subset\overline{X}^{\prime}
converges to some point
\bar{x}^{\prime}\in\overline{X}^{\prime}
. Set
f(\bar{x}):=\bar{x}^{\prime}
.

We next show that

is well-defined. Let
y_{k}\,\leq\,y_{k+1}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{x}
with
y_{k}\to\bar{x}
in
\overline{X}
be another sequence of elements in
X
converging to
\bar{x}
in
\overline{X}
and to
\bar{y}^{\prime}
in
\overline{X}^{\prime}
. Let
\bar{w}\in I^{+}_{\overline{\ll}^{\prime}}(\bar{y}^{\prime})
with
w_{l}\to\bar{w}
,
w_{l}\,\overline{\leq}^{\prime}\,\bar{w}
,

for all

. Therefore, there exists

such that, for all

, we have
\bar{y}^{\prime}\,\overline{\ll}^{\prime}\,w_{l}
. Since

in
\overline{X}
, for every

there exists

such that for all

we have

, i.e.,
y_{k}\,\ll\,w_{l}
. Taking the limit as

in
\overline{X}^{\prime}
and using the closedness of

, we get

; taking the limit as

yields
f(\bar{x})\,\overline{\leq}^{\prime}\,\bar{w}
. Finally, letting
\bar{w}\to\bar{y}^{\prime}
thanks to item (iii), we obtain that
f(\bar{x})\,\overline{\leq}^{\prime}\,\bar{y}^{\prime}
. By swapping the roles of the sequences
(x_{k})_{k}
and
(y_{k})_{k}
, yields the reverse inequality
\bar{y}^{\prime}\overline{\leq}^{\prime}\bar{x}^{\prime}
. Since by item
(ii)
the causal relation

Since

and

is dense and the time-separations are continuous, we get that

. It remains to show

or, equivalently, that the causal relations coincide. Assume by contradiction that there exist
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in\overline{X}

\overline{\ell}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=-\infty<0=\overline{\ell}^{\prime}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\overline{\tau}^{\prime}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\,.

Then, assertion (iii) implies that there exists a sequence

for all

. Let

, where

for all

Taking the limit as

∎

Finally, we establish that taking the completion does not affect convergence (under mild conditions that will always be satisfied in the applications below). Let

\{o\}\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}V(S^{l})=U_{k}\,.

For example, this is the case in the pre-compactness Theorem

Theorem 7.3

(Forward completion preserves convergence)
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
strongly and let
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})

•

\overline{X}

•

{\mathcal{U}}=(U_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}=(\overline{U}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

\overline{U}_{k}
is the closure of

.

Fix

\varepsilon

for all

To show forward density, let

8
Geometric pre-compactness

8.1
A pre-compactness result under causal assumptions

The goal of this section is to apply the Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence developed in the paper, in particular the pre-compactness theorem, to smooth spacetimes. An interesting feature of the geometric pre-compactness result proved below is that it heavily relies on the causal structure, and it has no immediate counterpart in Riemannian signature.

We start with the case of a Lorentzian product of the real line with a Riemannian manifold. Let
(\Sigma,h)
be a compact Riemannian manifold; in particular,
(\Sigma,h)
is
totally bounded
. Here, total boundedness means that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite (metric)
\varepsilon
-net
S_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Sigma
; i.e.,
\Sigma=\bigcup_{s\in S_{\varepsilon}}B_{\varepsilon}^{{\mathsf{d}}^{h}}(s)
and
|S_{\varepsilon}|\leq N(\varepsilon)<\infty
, where
{\mathsf{d}}^{h}
is the metric induced by
h
. Now we consider the product spacetime
M:=\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
with Lorentzian metric
-{\rm d}t^{2}+h
, which is globally hyperbolic since
\Sigma
is complete. Set
\Sigma_{t}:=\{t\}\times\Sigma
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 8.1

(Cover by causal diamonds in the future of
\Sigma_{0}
)
.

Let
A\subseteq J^{+}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})
for some
t_{0}>0
. Then for all
0<\varepsilon\leq t_{0}
, for all (metric)
\varepsilon
-nets
S
in
\Sigma

A\subseteq I^{+}(\{0\}\times S)
.

Proof:
.

Let
a=(t,\sigma)\in A
, then
t\geq t_{0}
. Let
0<\varepsilon\leq t_{0}
and
S
be an
\varepsilon
-net in
\Sigma
. Thus there is an
s\in S
such that
{\mathsf{d}}^{h}(\sigma,s)<\varepsilon
. Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\rightarrow M
,
\gamma(r):=(tr,\vec{\gamma}(r))
, where
\vec{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\Sigma
is a minimizing geodesic from
\vec{\gamma}(0)=s
to
\vec{\gamma}(1)=\sigma
with

. Now clearly
\gamma
is a future directed timelike curve from the point
(0,s)\in\{0\}\times\Sigma
to
a=(t,\sigma)\in\{t\}\times\Sigma
.
∎

In the next lemma we obtain a bound on the cardinality of (Lorentzian)

\varepsilon

Lemma 8.2

(Bounding the cardinality of Lorentzian
\varepsilon
-nets)
.

Let

be an
\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\varepsilon
-net of cardinality at most
\lceil\frac{3(t_{+}-t_{-})}{\varepsilon}\rceil\cdot N

.

Proof:
.

Set
L:=\lceil\frac{3(t_{+}-t_{-})}{\varepsilon}\rceil
and
t_{i}:=t_{-}+i\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for
i=-1,\ldots,L+1
. Moreover, set

for

,

and

\mathcal{J}:=\{J(x_{i-1,j},x_{i+2,j}):i=0,\ldots,L-1;j=1,\ldots,N\}.

Then

is a collection of causal diamonds

, which satisfy

.

Next, we show that

. For each

there exist

such that

and

\displaystyle g(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})=-(t-t_{i-1})^{2}+{\mathsf{d}}^{h}(s_{j},\sigma)^{2}<-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{9}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{9}=0\,,

(Scaled product metric)

(Geometric pre-compactness)

Step 1

U_{k}\subseteq U_{k+1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N},k\geq 1
. Thus each
(M,\ell_{g},o,(U_{k})_{k\geq 1})
is a covered Lorentzian pre-length space satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
6.2
. Therefore, Theorem
6.2
yields a subsequence strongly converging to a covered Lorentzian pre-length space. Then, Theorem
5.3
establishes that this subsequence also strongly convergences to the time-separation quotient (which satisfies the point distinction property (

PDP

)). Moreover, Theorem
7.2
and Proposition
7.3
give that the subsequence converges also to the forward completion of the limit.

Step 2
. Uniqueness of the limit in appropriate classes.

First, we can apply Proposition
4.9
to get uniqueness in the class of properly covered Lorentzian pre-length spaces with continuous time-separation functions
\tau
, closed anti-symmetric causal relation, metrizable chronological topology and that satisfy the point-distinction property (

PDP

) (as the vertices are timelike forward dense in each covering set
U_{k}
).

For the second part, assume there are two smooth globally hyperbolic spacetimes, arising as pLGH strong limits of the converging subsequence. Observe that the chronological topologies coincide with the manifold topologies; moreover, the above ensures that the induced Lorentzian pre-length spaces are isometric. In particular, the manifolds are homeomorphic (since isometries are continuous) and have the same dimension (as manifolds) by invariance of domain. Thanks to the Hawking–King–McCarthy theorem
[
HKM76
]
(cf.
[
MS08
, Prop. 3.34]
,
[
BEE96
, Thm. 4.17]
) such a time-separation preserving homeomorphism is actually a smooth isometry, yielding the claimed uniqueness.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

Notice that the condition
\rho_{C(T)}\preceq-\beta{\rm d}t^{2}+h_{t}
is conformally invariant; in particular, it does not depend on the curvature of
(\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma,g)
.
The interesting feature of Theorem
8.4
is that it implies that a control on the causality and a control of the cardinality of (metric)
\varepsilon
-nets of the spacelike slice
(\Sigma,h_{0})
allows to control the cardinality of
\varepsilon
-nets for the spacetime. This feature heavily relies on the causal structure and is markedly Lorentzian; we are not aware of pre-compactness results in Riemannian signature having this flavour.

8.2
A pre-compactness result under curvature assumptions

Let
(M^{n+1},g)
be a Lorentzian manifold,
n\geq 2
.
We adopt the following sign conventions for the Riemann curvature tensor
R^{M}
of
(M^{n+1},g)
:

\displaystyle R^{M}(X,Y)Z:=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,

\displaystyle{\rm Sec}^{M}(X,Y):=\frac{g(R^{M}(X,Y)Y,X)}{g(X,X)g(Y,Y)-g(X,Y)^{2}},

whenever
X,Y\in T_{p}M
span a non-degenerate plane. Notice the opposite sign (with respect to Riemannian setting) when
X
is spacelike and
Y
is timelike. For this reason, we say that
(M,g)
has
timelike sectional curvature bounded below
by
K_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}
at some
p\in M
and write
{\rm TSec}^{M}_{p}\geq K_{\perp}
if

-{\rm Sec}^{M}(X,Y)\geq K_{\perp},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $X,Y\in T_{p}M$ with $g(X,X)<0$ and $g(Y,Y)>0$.}

The Ricci tensor of
M
is defined as

{\rm Ric}^{M}(X,Y):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\;g(R^{M}(e_{i},X)Y,e_{i}),

where
\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is a Lorentz-orthonormal basis of
T_{p}M
, i.e.

\epsilon_{i}:=g(e_{i},e_{i})=1\;\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n,\hskip 10.00002pt\epsilon_{0}:=g(e_{0},e_{0})=-1.

Let
\iota\colon\Sigma^{n}\hookrightarrow M
be a smooth, spacelike hypersurface with:

•

future-pointing unit normal field
\nu
,

•

second fundamental form
h^{\Sigma}(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\nu,Y)
,

•

induced metric
g^{\Sigma}=\iota^{*}g
,

•

mean curvature
H=\mathrm{tr}_{g^{\Sigma}}h^{\Sigma}
.

Lemma 8.6

(Ricci-lower-bound inheritance for spacelike hypersurfaces)
.

Let
(M^{n+1},g)
be a Lorentzian manifold,
n\geq 1
. Let
\Sigma^{n}\hookrightarrow M
be a smooth, spacelike hypersurface.
Assume there exist constants
K,K_{\perp},\Lambda\geq 0
such that:

(i)

Ambient Ricci lower bound along
\Sigma
:

\mathrm{Ric}^{M}\geq-K\,g
along
\Sigma
,

(ii)

Timelike sectional lower bound along
\Sigma
:
For all
p\in\Sigma
, it holds that
{\rm TSec}^{M}_{p}\geq-K_{\perp}
;

(iii)

Bounded second fundamental form:

\lvert h^{\Sigma}\rvert\leq\Lambda
.

Then the intrinsic Ricci tensor of
\Sigma
satisfies:

(9)

\mathrm{Ric}^{\Sigma}\geq-\left(K+K_{\perp}+2n\Lambda^{2}\right)\,g^{\Sigma}.

Proof.

Fix a tangent vector
X\in T_{p}\Sigma
, and extend it locally. The contracted Gauss equation gives:

\mathrm{Ric}^{\Sigma}(X,X)=\mathrm{Ric}^{M}(X,X)+g(R^{M}(\nu,X)X,\nu)+H\,h^{\Sigma}(X,X)-\lVert h^{\Sigma}(X,\cdot)\rVert^{2}.

Using the three assumptions, we can estimate each term and obtain:

\mathrm{Ric}^{\Sigma}(X,X)\geq-\left(K+K_{\perp}+2n\Lambda^{2}\right)|X|^{2}.

Since
X
was arbitrary, this proves the claim.
∎

The next step is to use lower bounds on the timelike sectional curvature to control the causal relation. Let us start by recalling some classical facts of smooth differential geometry. Assume that

r\colon U\subset M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{is smooth with}\hskip 20.00003ptg(\nabla r,\nabla r)=-1.

Let

\mathbf{T}:=\partial_{r}:=-\nabla r,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{Hess}\,(-r)(X,Y):=-g(\nabla_{X}\nabla r,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{T}}g(X,Y),

where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{T}}
denotes the Lie derivative in the direction of the vector field
{\mathbf{T}}
.

Proposition 8.7
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{Hess}\,r+\mathrm{Hess}^{2}r

\displaystyle=g(R(\cdot,\mathbf{T})\mathbf{T},\cdot).

Proof.

Insert
N=\mathbf{T}
with
g(N,N)=-1
into the computation of

[
Pet16
, Thm. 3.2.2]
; only the sign of
g(N,N)
changes.
∎

Set
\Sigma_{r}:=\{r=\mathrm{const}\}
the spacelike level hypersurfaces.
Along each integral curve of
\mathbf{T}
,
we obtain Gaussian normal coordinates:

(10)

g=-\mathrm{d}r^{2}+g_{r},\hskip 20.00003ptg_{r}:=g|_{T\Sigma_{r}}.

Using the expression (
10
), Proposition
8.7

simplifies to

(11)

\partial_{r}g_{r}=2\,\mathrm{Hess}\,r,\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{r}\mathrm{Hess}\,r+\mathrm{Hess}^{2}r=g(R(\cdot,\mathbf{T})\mathbf{T},\cdot).

These two transport equations encode the evolution of the induced
metric and the second fundamental form along the timelike normal flow.

Let
\Sigma^{n}\subset M^{n+1}
be a spacelike achronal hypersurface.
The
signed time-separation
from
\Sigma
is the function
\tau_{\Sigma}:M\to[-\infty,+\infty]
defined by

(12)

\tau_{\Sigma}(x):=\begin{cases}\sup_{y\in\Sigma}\tau(y,x),&\quad\text{ for }x\in I^{+}(\Sigma)\\
-\sup_{y\in\Sigma}\tau(x,y),&\quad\text{ for }x\in I^{-}(\Sigma)\\
0&\quad\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Until the end of this section, we will work under the assumption that
(M^{n+1},g)
is globally hyperbolic and
\Sigma
is a compact Cauchy hypersurface. Under such conditions, it is standard to check that, for all
x\in I^{+}(\Sigma)
(resp. for all
x\in I^{-}(\Sigma)
) there exists

y_{x}\in\Sigma
with
\tau_{\Sigma}(x)=\tau(y_{x},x)>0
(resp.
\tau_{V}(x)=-\tau(x,y_{x})<0
). Let

(13)

U_{\Sigma}:=\{x\in M\colon\tau_{\Sigma}\text{ is smooth at $x$ with }g(\nabla\tau_{\Sigma}(x),\nabla\tau_{\Sigma}(x))=-1\}.

The following are classical facts:

•

U_{\Sigma}\subset M
is an open set of full measure;

•

U_{\Sigma}\cap I^{+}(\Sigma)
is future dense in
I^{+}(\Sigma)
; resp.
U_{\Sigma}\cap I^{-}(\Sigma)
is past dense in
I^{-}(\Sigma)
;

•

for each
x\in U_{\Sigma}
there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma^{\Sigma,x}
maximizing the time-separation between
x
and
\Sigma
, and the support of
\gamma^{\Sigma,x}
is entirely contained in
U_{\Sigma}
;

•

U_{\Sigma}
is diffeomorphic, via the exponential map
\exp_{\Sigma}
based on
\Sigma
, to a suitable open subset
\tilde{U}_{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
. Identify
U_{\Sigma}
with
\tilde{U}_{\Sigma}
via such a diffeomorphism. Setting
r=\tau_{\Sigma}
on
U_{\Sigma}
, the metric
g
can be written as in (
10
) on
U_{\Sigma}
.

Proposition 8.8
.

Let
(M^{n+1},g)
be a smooth globally hyperbolic spacetime and let
\Sigma^{n}\subset M^{n+1}
be a compact Cauchy hypersurface. Let
\tau_{\Sigma}
and
U_{\Sigma}
be defined in (
12
) and (
13
), respectively.
Assume:

(i)

L^{1}
-lower bound on the timelike sectional curvature
. There exists
\alpha\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(14)

{\rm TSec}^{M}_{x}\geq\alpha(\tau_{\Sigma}(x)),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }x\in M;

(ii)

Bounded second fundamental form of
\Sigma
.
There exists
\Lambda>0
such that the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
in
M
satisfies
\lvert h^{\Sigma}\rvert\leq\Lambda
.

Then there exists a continuous function
C(\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
, depending only on
\alpha(\cdot)
and
\Lambda
, such that

(15)

-C(r)^{2}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+g_{|T_{\sigma}\Sigma}\preceq g^{(r,\sigma)}\text{ for all }(r,\sigma)\in U_{\Sigma},

where
g^{(r,\sigma)}
is the Lorentzian metric
g
at the point
(r,\sigma)\in U_{\Sigma}
, and
g_{|T_{\sigma}\Sigma}
is the Riemannian metric on
T_{\sigma}\Sigma
obtained by restriction of
g
.

Of course, the assumption (
14
) is satisfied if, more strongly, there exists
K_{\perp}>0
such that

{\rm TSec}^{M}\geq-K_{\perp}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }M.

Proof.

Fix
x=(r,\sigma)\in U_{\Sigma}\cap I^{+}(\Sigma)
; the arguments for
(r,\sigma)\in U_{\Sigma}\cap I^{-}(\Sigma)
are completely analogous. Let
\gamma^{\Sigma,x}
be the unique geodesic maximizing the time-separation between

and
\Sigma
. Recall that the support of
\gamma^{\Sigma,x}

. Therefore, the coupled differential equations (
11
) hold in a neighborhood of

.

Since

is written as in (

), for the claim (
15
) it is sufficient to show that

(16)

g_{r}(X,X)\leq C(r)^{-2}\;g_{0}(X,X),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall X\in T_{\sigma}\Sigma.

Therefore, we would like an upper bound
\partial_{r}g_{r}\leq f(r)g_{0}
for some

. Observe that
\mathrm{Hess}\;r|^{r=0}_{T\Sigma\times T\Sigma}
coincides with the second fundamental form of

, which is bounded by the second assumption.
If the timelike sectional curvature satisfies the first assumption, then the coupled differential equations (

∎

Theorem 8.9

(Pre-compactness under curvature assumptions)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and
K,K_{\perp},\Lambda,D>0
.
Consider the family
\mathcal{M}_{n,K,K_{\perp},\Lambda,D}
of globally hyperbolic spacetimes
(M^{n+1},g)
with

(a) Timelike sectional bounded below:

For all
x\in M
, it holds that
{\rm TSec}^{M}_{x}\geq-K_{\perp}
;

admitting a compact Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma^{n}\subset M
with

(b) Ambient Ricci lower bound along
\Sigma
:

\mathrm{Ric}^{M}\geq-K\,g
along
\Sigma
,

(c) Bounded second fundamental form:

\lvert h^{\Sigma}\rvert\leq\Lambda
,

(d) Bounded diameter:

the diameter of
\Sigma
with respect to the induced Riemannian metric is bounded by
D
; i.e.,
{\rm diam}_{g_{|T\Sigma}}(\Sigma)\leq D
.

Then:

(i)

\mathcal{M}_{n,K,K_{\perp},\Lambda,D}
is sequentially pre-compact in the strong
\mathrm{pLGH}
-convergence; i.e., for each sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{n,K,K_{\perp},\Lambda,D}
there is a subsequence that strongly
\mathrm{pLGH}
-converges to a covered Lorentzian pre-length space that satisfies the point distinction property (

PDP

).

(ii)

This limit as in (i) can be forward completed and is still the limit of such a subsequence.

(iii)

The forward completed limit as in (ii) is
unique
in the class of properly covered Lorentzian pre-length spaces with continuous time-separation functions
\tau
, closed anti-symmetric causal relation, metrizable chronological topology and that satisfy the point-distinction property (

PDP

).
In particular (see Remark
4.10
), it is unique within the class of covered, globally hyperbolic, Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

(iv)

In case the limit is smooth: there exists at most one smooth globally hyperbolic spacetime arising as such a strong limit, up to smooth isometries.

Proof.

We follow the strategy of the proof of Theorem
8.4
but will not use the theorem per se. Instead, we will apply the abstract precompactness result Theorem
6.2
. Since by assumption
\Sigma
is a Cauchy hypersurface, without loss of generality, we can assume that
M=\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
, up to a diffeomorphism. From Lemma
8.6
, we get that the Riemannian metric
g_{|T\Sigma}
on
\Sigma\subset M
has Ricci curvature bounded from below. By the Bishop–Gromov inequality, it follows that
(\Sigma,g_{|T\Sigma})
is doubling and thus, using the bound on the diameter, it satisfies the assumption on the existence of Riemannian
\epsilon
-nets as in Theorem
8.4
. Proposition
8.8
implies that the assumption on the causal cones in Theorem
8.4
is satisfied on
U_{\Sigma}
. We can thus apply the first part of Theorem
8.4
to
U_{\Sigma}
and infer the existence of
\epsilon
-nets as follows.

Fix
T>0
, then there is a
R=R(T)>0
such that

\bigr([-T,T]\times\Sigma\bigl)\cap U_{\Sigma}\subseteq\exp_{\Sigma}^{-1}\bigr([-R,R]\times\Sigma\bigl).

Let
C(\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
be given by Proposition
8.8
and set

C^{-}:=\min_{r\in[-R,R]}C(r)>0.

The combination of (
10
) and (
15
) yields that

-(C^{-})^{2}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+g_{0}\preceq-C(r)^{2}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+g_{0}\preceq-\mathrm{d}r^{2}+g_{r}=g

on
U_{\Sigma}
(suppressing the diffeomorphism
\exp_{\Sigma}\colon U_{\Sigma}\rightarrow\tilde{U}_{\Sigma}
).
Consequently, Corollary
8.3
ensures the existence of an
\varepsilon
-net having an explicit bound on its cardinality and covering
\exp_{\Sigma}^{-1}\bigr([-R,R]\times\Sigma\bigl)
; i.e., we obtain a finite covering of causal diamonds (with respect to the causal relation in
U_{\Sigma}
):

\bigl([-T,T]\times\Sigma\bigr)\cap U_{\Sigma}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N(T)}J(x_{i},y_{i};U_{\Sigma})\,,

where
x_{i},y_{i}\in U_{\Sigma}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,N(T)
.
Since the operation of taking the topological closure commutes with finite unions, we obtain that

\overline{\bigl([-T,T]\times\Sigma\bigr)\cap U_{\Sigma}}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N(T)}\overline{J(x_{i},y_{i};U_{\Sigma})}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N(T)}J(x_{i},y_{i})\,,

giving an
\varepsilon

\overline{\bigl([-T,T]\times\Sigma\bigr)\cap U_{\Sigma}}
with causal diamonds in
M
.
We next claim that the sets
\overline{\bigl([-T,T]\times\Sigma\bigr)\cap U_{\Sigma}}
for

yield an exhaustion of

. To this aim, let
p\in\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
and choose

such that

, which is open in
M
. The density of

in

implies that

. As

is bounded on each

yields a cover of

fitting the assumptions of Theorem

. We thus conclude as in the proof of Theorem

.
∎

9
Measured pLGH convergence

Let us start by defining a
measured Lorentzian pre-length space
, as a Lorentzian pre-length space (in the sense of Definition
2.3
) endowed with a reference non-negative Borel measure. Recall that a topology is said to be
Polish
if it can be induced by a complete and separable metric.

Definition 9.1

(Measured Lorentzian pre-length space)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space whose topology is Polish and such that causal diamonds are Borel. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a non-negative Borel measure, finite on causal diamonds. Then the triplet
(X,\ell,\mathfrak{m})
is called
measured
Lorentzian pre-length space.

Next, we discuss how to induce a measure into an
\epsilon
-net. Recall Definition
3.1
of vertices of a family of causal diamonds, and Definition
3.2
of
\varepsilon
-net for a set.

Definition 9.2

(Measured
\varepsilon
-net)
.

Let
(X,\ell,\mathfrak{m})
be a measured Lorentzian pre-length space.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
A\subseteq X
be a Borel subset.
Let
S=(J_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}=\big(J(p_{i},q_{i})\big)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable (or finite)
\varepsilon
-net for
A
.
Define the measure

(17)

\mathfrak{m}_{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}\Big((J_{i}\cap A)\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}J_{j}\Big)\left(\delta_{p_{i}}+\delta_{q_{i}}\right).

The pair
(S,\mathfrak{m}_{S})
is called
measured
\epsilon
-net
for
A
.

Note that (
17
) defines a measure. Indeed
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
is the monotone limit of the increasing family of finite measures
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}\left((J_{i}\cap A)\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{i-1}J_{j}\right)\left(\delta_{p_{i}}+\delta_{q_{i}}\right)
.

We next give a measured version of the LGH convergence of subsets. Roughly, we add the weak convergence of measures to the LGH convergence (see Definition
3.6
). The weak convergence of measures is understood in duality with real valued continuous functions with compact support.

Definition 9.3

(Measured LGH-convergence of subsets)
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},\mathfrak{m}_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(X,\ell,\mathfrak{m})

be a Borel subset of
X_{n}
and let

be a Borel subset of

converges to

for short), and write
A_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{mLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A

A_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{LGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
(resp. strongly) and if there exist Borel maps
g^{l}_{n}\colon V(S^{l}_{n})\rightarrow V(S^{l})

1/l-

V(S^{l}_{n})
and

(18)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}(g^{l}_{n})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{S^{l}_{n}}=\mathfrak{m}\llcorner A\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly as measures}.

Here
\mathfrak{m}\llcorner A
denotes the restriction of the measure
\mathfrak{m}
to the Borel set
A
.

We can now define a measured version of the pointed Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence (Definition
3.12
).

Definition 9.4

(Measured pLGH-convergence of covered measured Lorentzian pre-length spaces)
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},\mathfrak{m}_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})

\bigl((X_{n},\ell_{n},\mathfrak{m}_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

.

From the construction of the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
as in (

Theorem 9.5
.

Each smooth globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)

We next refine the pre-compactness Theorem

(ii)

For every

admits a finite
1/m

\displaystyle V(S_{k,j,m-1})\subset V(S_{k,j,m})\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg_{k,j,m}|_{V(S_{k,j,m-1})}=g_{k,j,m-1}

We endow

Step 1
. Case

.

Due to the finiteness of the set, a measure on

\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}(g_{1,j,m})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{S_{1,j,m}}=\mathfrak{m}_{1,\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly as measures}.

Step 2

\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}(g_{k,j,m})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{S_{k,j,m}}=\mathfrak{m}_{k,\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly as measures}.

Moreover, arguing as step 1, we have that

\frac{1}{C_{k}}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{k,\infty}(U_{k,\infty})\leq C_{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since, by construction

10
Applications

In this final section we give four applications of the Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence developed above. First, we show that Chruściel–Grant approximations
[
CG12
]
of continuous spacetimes are an instance of the Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of their underlying Lorentzian pre-length spaces. Second, we show that timelike sectional curvature bounds are stable under Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. Third, we introduce blow-up tangents and finally, we prove a precise statement about the main conjecture of causal set theory, an approach to Quantum Gravity.

10.1
Chruściel–Grant approximations viewed as Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence

Given a continuous Lorentzian metric

10.2
Stability of timelike sectional curvature bounds

In this subsection we show stability of lower timelike sectional curvature bounds in the form of the four-point condition. Other (more-or-less) equivalent notions of synthetic timelike sectional curvature bounds will be stable as well, provided that the approximating spaces have curvature bounds in the global sense and that the limit has a continuous time-separation function.

Before we introduce the timelike four-point condition
[
BKR24
]
, we introduce the two-dimensional Lorentzian model spaces of constant curvature
K\in\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.
[
O’N83
]
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{2}(K):=\begin{cases}\tilde{S}^{2}_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}})&K>0\,,\\
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{1}&K=0\,,\\
\tilde{H}^{2}_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{-K}})&K<0\,,\end{cases}

which have diameter
D_{K}:=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{-K}}
if
K<0
and
D_{K}:=\infty
otherwise. Here
\tilde{S}^{2}_{1}(r)
is the simply connected covering manifold of the two-dimensional Lorentzian pseudosphere of radius
r>0
(
r=1
is de Sitter space),
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{1}
is two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime and
\tilde{H}^{2}_{1}(r)
is the simply connected covering manifold of two-dimensional Lorentzian pseudohyperbolic space (
r=1
is anti-de Sitter space).

Definition 10.2

(Four-point configurations)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space.

(i)

A
timelike future endpoint-causal four-point configuration
is a quadruple
(y,x,z_{1},z_{2})\in X^{4}
such that
y\ll x\ll z_{1}\leq z_{2}
.

(ii)

Similarly, a
timelike past endpoint-causal four-point configuration
is a quadruple
(z_{2},z_{1},x,y)\in X^{4}
such that
z_{2}\leq z_{1}\ll x\ll y
.

(iii)

Given a timelike future endpoint-causal four-point configuration
(y,x,z_{1},z_{2})
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
, a
four-point comparison configuration
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
is a quadruple
(\bar{y},\bar{x},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)^{4}
such that

(a)

\tau(y,x)=\bar{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{x})
,

(b)

\tau(y,z_{i})=\bar{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z}_{i})
(
i=1,2
) ,

(c)

\tau(x,z_{i})=\bar{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{z}_{i})
(
i=1,2
) , and

(d)

\bar{z}_{1}
,
\bar{z}_{2}
lie on opposite sides of the line through
\bar{y}
,
\bar{x}
.

(iv)

Similarly, one defines a four-point comparison configuration for a timelike past endpoint-causal four-point configuration.

Next we recall the definition of synthetic timelike sectional lower curvature bounds in the form of the four-point condition as defined by Beran–Kunzinger–Rott
[
BKR24
, Def. 4.6]
following a similar construction in the positive signature case, cf. e.g.
[
AKP23
]
.

Definition 10.3

(Four-point condition)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
K\in\mathbb{R}
. A
\geq K
-comparison neighborhood
is an open set
U\subseteq X
such that

(i)

the time-separation function
\tau
is continuous on the open set
(U\times U)\cap\tau^{-1}([0,D_{K}))
and

(ii)

for every timelike future endpoint-causal four-point configuration
(y,x,z_{1},z_{2})
in
U
with
\tau(y,z_{2})<D_{K}
and its comparison configuration
(\bar{y},\bar{x},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
one has

(27)

\tau(z_{1},z_{2})\geq\bar{\tau}(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})\,.

Moreover, for every timelike past endpoint-causal four-point configuration
(z_{2},z_{1},x,y)
in
U
with
\tau(z_{2},y)<D_{K}
and its comparison configuration
(\bar{z}_{2},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{x},\bar{y})
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)

\tau(z_{2},z_{1})\geq\bar{\tau}(\bar{z}_{2},\bar{z}_{1})\,.

Finally, we say that
(X,\ell)
has

K
if
X

The four-point condition is equivalent to the other synthetic timelike sectional curvature bounds for large classes of Lorentzian pre-length spaces, and hence to smooth timelike sectional curvature bounds, see
[

We are now in a position to establish stability of the global four-point condition under pointed Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. An analogous statement in the setting of bounded Lorentzian metric spaces
[

[

and in the global case in
[

Theorem 10.4

(Stability of the four-point condition)
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
. Assume that each
(X_{n},\ell_{n})
has global timelike sectional curvature bounded below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
and that
\tau
is continuous. Then
(X,\ell)
has global timelike sectional curvature bounded below by
K
.

Proof:
.

Let
y\ll x\ll z_{1}\leq z_{2}
be a timelike future endpoint-causal four-point configuration with
\tau(y,z_{2})<D_{K}
and
(\bar{y},\bar{x},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
a comparison configuration in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
. As the covering sets are increasing there is one fixed
U_{k}\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that
y,x,z_{1},z_{2}\in U_{k}
. First, we consider the case that all four points are vertices of causal diamonds given by the convergence. Hence, there are corresponding points
y^{n},x^{n},z_{1}^{n},z_{2}^{n}\in X_{n}
such that their time-separations converge. In particular, for large
n\in\mathbb{N}
they form a timelike future endpoint-causal four-point configuration
(y^{n},x^{n},z_{1}^{n},z_{2}^{n})
with
\tau_{n}(y^{n},z_{2}^{n})<D_{K}
. Consequently, for these
n
we have by Equation (
27
) that
\tau_{n}(z_{1}^{n},z_{2}^{n})\geq\bar{\tau}(\bar{z}_{1}^{n},\bar{z}_{2}^{n})
, where
(\bar{y}^{n},\bar{x}^{n},\bar{z}_{1}^{n},\bar{z}_{2}^{n})
form a comparison four-point configuration in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
. Since the side-lengths of the comparison configurations also converge, we infer that the right-hand-side converges to
\bar{\tau}(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
, whereas the left-hand-side converges to
\tau(z_{1},z_{2})
. Thus, we conclude
\tau(z_{1},z_{2})\geq\bar{\tau}(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
. The analogous argument works for timelike past endpoint-causal four-point configurations. Finally, for general
(y,x,z_{1},z_{2})
, one approximates them by vertices (they are dense by the definition of the convergence) and uses continuity of
\tau
to conclude the proof.
∎

10.3
Blow-up tangents

The study of blow-up tangents (or tangent cones) has been very useful to study the infinitesimal structure of non-smooth metric measure spaces with curvature bounds
[
BBI01
,
CC97
,
OS94
]
. Here we initiate this program by providing such a notion in the Lorentzian setting and establish existence under suitable local assumptions.

Definition 10.5

(Scaling a Lorentzian pre-length space)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
\lambda>0
. Denote by
\lambda(X,\ell)
the topological space
X
with time-separation function
\lambda\ell
.
For a pointed Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell,o)
, we define a
\lambda
-blow-up around
o
as

X^{\lambda}_{o}:=I(o^{\lambda}_{-},o^{\lambda}_{+})\,,

where
o^{\lambda}_{-}\ll o\ll o^{\lambda}_{+}
,
\tau(o^{\lambda}_{-},o^{\lambda}_{+})<\frac{1}{\lambda}
and with time-separation
\lambda\ell
, i.e., it is the pointed Lorentzian pre-length space
(X^{\lambda}_{0},\lambda\ell,o)
. Finally, for a covered Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
we define the
\lambda
-blow-up around
o
as
(X^{\lambda}_{o},\lambda\ell,o)
with covering
{\mathcal{U}}^{\lambda}:=(U_{k}\cap X^{\lambda}_{o})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
{\mathcal{U}}=(U_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We easily see that
\lambda
-blow-ups exist under fairly natural assumptions.

Lemma 10.6

(Existence of
\lambda
-blow-up)
.

Let
\lambda>0
and let
(X,\ell,o)
be a pointed Lorentzian pre-length space where
\tau
is upper semi-continuous with respect to the Alexandrov topology and finite at
(o,o)
. If,
o\in\overline{I^{\pm}(o)}
, then there exists a
\lambda
-blow-up
X^{\lambda}_{0}
of
o
.

At this point we can introduce the timelike blow-up tangent by taking the limit
\lambda\to\infty
in
\lambda
-blow-ups around a fixed base point.

Definition 10.7

(Timelike blow-up tangent)
.

Let
(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
be a covered Lorentzian pre-length space. A pointed Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff strong limit (as
\lambda\to\infty
) of
\lambda
-blow-ups
(X^{\lambda},\lambda\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}}^{\lambda})_{\lambda}
around
o
is called
a blow-up tangent
of
(X,\ell,o)
.

Blow-up tangents do not exist in general (as in the positive signature case). However we obtain subsequential existence, under a suitable local control on
\varepsilon
-nets. To this aim, we next introduce a Lorentzian counterpart to the doubling property for a metric space cf.
[
Hei01
, Sec. 10.13]
.

Definition 10.8

(Doubling property of causal diamonds)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
U\subseteq X
. Then
U
is said to have the
doubling property
if there exists a constant
N\in\mathbb{N}
, called the
doubling constant
, such that every causal diamond
J(x,y)\subseteq U
can be covered by
N
causal diamonds
J(x_{i},y_{i})
with
\tau(x_{i},y_{i})\leq\frac{\tau(x,y)}{2}
and
x_{i},y_{i}\in U
(
i=1,\ldots,N
).

Theorem 10.9

(Subsequential existence of blow-up tangents)
.

Let
(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
be a covered Lorentzian pre-length space, where each covering set
U_{k}\in{\mathcal{U}}
has the doubling property with doubling constant
N_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then any sequence
(X^{\lambda},\lambda\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}}^{\lambda})_{\lambda}
of
\lambda
-blow-ups around
o
admits a subsequence which converges in the strong pointed Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff sense (for
\lambda\to\infty)
.

Proof:
.

We verify that the assumptions of the general pre-compactness theorem
6.2
are satisfied. First, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>0
, we have

where
U_{k}^{\lambda}:=U_{k}\cap X^{\lambda}_{o}

\varepsilon>0

10.4
The Hauptvermutung of causal set theory

Causal set theory is an approach to Quantum Gravity based on the principle that, at the fundamental level, spacetime is a discrete partially ordered set and the continuum spacetime emerges macroscopically from order and volume, see e.g.
[
Sur19
]
for an introduction. To make this program viable the so-called
Hauptvermutung
(main conjecture) has to hold
[
BLMS87
]
. In short, if two spacetimes
(M,g)
and
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
approximate a given causal set
(C,\leq)
(up to some scale) then
(M,g)
and
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
should be “close” (up to some scale). One of the obstacles to tackling this problem was that there was no notion of “closeness” of spacetimes and no notion of convergence of causal sets or spacetimes. With the pointed Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff convergence developed in this article, we can now prove a precise statement. However, it does not yet provide a complete solution to the conjecture, as it involves converging sequences and no fixed scale. A similar approach has been taken recently by Müller in
[
Mül25
]
using his notion of Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of Cauchy slabs
[
Mül22
]
. Before we come to the statement we briefly recall the basics of causal sets viewed as Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

As shown in
[
KS18
, Subsec. 5.3]
causal sets are Lorentzian pre-length spaces. Indeed for a partially ordered set
(C,\leq)
, one can define
\ell(x,y)
to be the length of the longest chain connecting
x
to
y
. This yields the Lorentzian pre-length space
(C,\ell)
with the discrete topology.
Causal sets are partially ordered sets that are
locally finite
, i.e., each causal diamond contains only finitely many points. Moreover, a causal set
(C,\leq)

faithfully embeds
into a spacetime
(M,g)
if there is a map
\phi\colon C\rightarrow M
that is
\leq
-preserving, i.e., for all
x,y\in C
we have
\phi(x)\leq_{g}\phi(y)
.

Theorem 10.10

(Smooth globally hyperbolic limits of causal sets are unique)
.

Let
(C_{n},\leq_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of causal sets such that their induced Lorentzian pre-length spaces
(C_{n},\ell_{n})
(pointed) Lorentzian Gromov–Hausdorff converge strongly to two smooth globally hyperbolic spacetimes
(M,g)
and
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
(using some covering of the
C_{n}
s and
M
,
M^{\prime}
). Moreover, assume that each
(C_{n},\leq_{n})
faithfully embeds into both spacetimes. Then
(M,g)
and
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
are isometric as smooth spacetimes.

Proof:
.

We construct covers
{\mathcal{U}}_{n}
of
C_{n}
such that each covering set
U_{k,n}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n}
is finite (by local finiteness). Using the faithful embeddings, we can construct corresponding covers of
(M,g)
and
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
. By finiteness of the covering sets in
C_{n}
, the set of vertices of
\frac{1}{l}
-nets for
U_{k,n}
always contains
U_{k,n}
(for
l
large). Consequently, these vertices are trivially dense and we can apply Proposition
4.9
to conclude that
(M,\ell_{g})
and
(M^{\prime},\ell_{g^{\prime}})
are isometric as Lorentzian pre-length spaces and hence
M
and
M^{\prime}
are homeomorphic. Thus, they have the same manifold dimension (by invariance of domain) and so we can apply the Hawking–King–McCarthy theorem
[
HKM76
]
(cf.
[
MS08
, Prop. 3.34]
,
[
BEE96
, Thm. 4.17]
) to conclude that they are smoothly isometric.
∎

Appendix A
Limits of globally hyperbolic spacetimes

The goal of this appendix is to show that there is a way to obtain that the limit of smooth globally hyperbolic spacetimes is a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian pre-length space (if the limit is non-degenerate). However, this uses a different topological set-up than the main part of the article. Thus we opted to include it in a separate appendix, which might be of independent interest.

The idea is to use a uniform structure, see e.g.
[
Kel75
, Ch. 6]
, on the limit space to define a topology and, more importantly, a notion of Cauchy sequences and hence a completion with respect to such a uniform structure. This should be compared with
[
BMS25
]
, performing a similar procedure in the different setting of Lorentzian metric spaces. For the reader’s convenience and the sake of completeness, we briefly recall the definition of a uniform structure. Uniform spaces, i.e., spaces with a uniform structure, can be viewed as intermediate structures between topological spaces and metric spaces. They are topological spaces, additionally having an intrinsic notion of (relative) “closeness” of points, which allows to define uniformly continuous maps and Cauchy sequences.

Definition A.1

(Uniform space)
.

A
uniform space

(X,\mathfrak{U})
is a set
X
with a
uniform structure (or uniformity)

\mathfrak{U}
, where
\mathfrak{U}
consists of a family of subsets of
X\times X
, satisfying the following properties.

(i)

Each
U\in\mathfrak{U}
contains the diagonal, i.e.,
\Delta:=\{(x,x):x\in X\}\subseteq U
,

(ii)

The uniformity is closed under inversion, i.e., for all
U\in\mathfrak{U}
, we have
U^{-1}:=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:(y,x)\in U\}\in\mathfrak{U}
.

(iii)

Each
U\in\mathfrak{U}
contains an element of the uniformity of “half” the size, i.e., there is
V\in\mathfrak{U}
with

V\circ V:=\{(x,z)\in X\times X:\,\exists y\in X\text{ such that }(x,y),(y,z)\in V\}\subseteq U\,.

(iv)

The uniformity is closed under intersection, i.e., if
U,V\in\mathfrak{U}
then
U\cap V\in\mathfrak{U}
, and

(v)

it is closed under supersets, i.e., if
U\in\mathfrak{U}
and
V\supseteq U
, then
V\in\mathfrak{U}
.

Finally, for
U\in\mathfrak{U}
and
x\in X
we define

U[x]:=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in U\}\,.

A uniform space has the natural topology generated by the neighborhoods
U[x]
for
x\in X,U\in\mathfrak{U}
. A map
f\colon(X,\mathfrak{U})\rightarrow(Y,\mathfrak{V})
between uniform spaces is
uniformly continuous
if for all
V\in\mathfrak{V}
there is
U\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
f(U):=\{(f(x),f(x^{\prime})):(x,x^{\prime})\in U\}\subseteq V
. Moreover, uniform spaces a are the natural setting for Cauchy nets (or sequences) and hence for defining completeness.

Definition A.2

(Cauchy nets)
.

Let
(X,\mathfrak{U})
be a uniform space. A net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
in
X
is a
Cauchy net
if for all
U\in\mathfrak{U}
there is
\alpha_{0}
such that for all
\alpha,\beta\geq\alpha_{0}
we have that
(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\in U
. The uniform space
(X,\mathfrak{U})
is
complete
if every Cauchy net converges.

Each uniform space
(X,\mathfrak{U})
has a
completion
, i.e., a complete uniform space
(\overline{U},\overline{\mathfrak{U}})
such that
X
embeds densely and uniformly continuously into
\overline{X}
, cf. e.g.
[
Kel75
, Ch. 6, Thm. 28]
.

Next, we define a uniform structure on a Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell)
, given by a distinguished subset
S\subseteq X
. For
x\in X
we denote by
\ell_{x}
and
\ell^{x}
the time-separation from
x
and to
x
, respectively. To be precise,
\ell_{x},\ell^{x}\colon X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
are defined as
\ell_{x}(y):=\ell(x,y)
and
\ell^{x}(y):=\ell(y,x)
for
y\in X
.

Lemma A.3

(Uniform structure on Lorentzian pre-length space)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space with
\tau
finite-valued and
\emptyset\neq S\subseteq X
. Then there is a uniform structure
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
on
X
generated by the basic entourages (subbase)

V^{s}_{\delta}:=\{(y,z)\in X\times X:|(\ell_{y}-\ell_{z})(s)|<\delta,|(\ell^{y}-\ell^{z})(s)|<\delta\}\,,

for
s\in S
and
\delta>0
. The uniform structure
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
is pseudo-metrizable if
S
is countable and metrizable if additionally
S
has the point distinction property (

PDP

).

Proof:
.

The uniform structure consists of all finite intersections of sets
V^{s}_{\delta}
for
s\in S
and
\delta>0
. If
S
is countable then the topology induced by
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
is pseudo-metrizable by
[
Kel75
, Thm. 6.13]
. If additionally
S
distinguishes points then so does
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
: Let
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
, then there is an
s\in S
such that without loss of generality
\ell(x,s)<\ell(y,s)
. Let
0<\delta<\ell(y,s)-\ell(x,s)
(this works even for
\ell(x,s)=-\infty
), then
V^{s}_{\frac{\delta}{2}}[x]\cap V^{s}_{\frac{\delta}{2}}[y]=\emptyset
. This also implies that
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
is metrizable.
∎

Proposition A.4

(Topology and convergence)
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
. Then there exists a natural pseudo-metrizable uniform structure on

.

Let

∎

Lemma A.8
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space with finite-valued time-separation
\tau
. Let
S\subseteq X
be countable. If
\tau
is uniformly continuous with respect to
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
, then the completion
\overline{X}
of

is a Lorentzian pre-length space, with closed causal relation
\overline{\leq}
. Moreover, we can also take the quotient with respect to
\overline{\tau}
and so without loss of generality
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
satisfies the
\tau
-point distinction property (

⁢
τ
PDP

), hence is causal.

Proof:
.

By uniform continuity we can uniquely extend
\tau
to the completion
\overline{X}
and denote it by
\overline{\tau}
. From the reverse triangle inequality on
X
we immediately get that
\overline{\tau}
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality for all points
\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z}\in\overline{X}
with
\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>0
,
\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})>0
. To obtain a useful causal relation on
\overline{X}
we follow
[

, Def. 5.1, Thm. 5.7]

\bar{x}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{y}

\displaystyle\forall\bar{z}\in\overline{X}:\ \overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{y})\geq\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})\ \text{ and }\ \overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\geq\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})\,.

This is clearly a reflexive and transitive relation on
\overline{X}\times\overline{X}
, and by continuity of
\overline{\tau}
it is also closed. Also, it contains the chronological relation
\overline{\ll}:=\overline{\tau}^{-1}((0,\infty))
. To see this let
\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>0
. Let
\bar{z}\in\overline{X}
. If
\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})>0
, then by the reverse triangle inequality for chronologically related points we obtain
\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{y})\geq\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})+\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\geq\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})
. If
\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})=0
, then
\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{y})\geq 0=\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})
anyway. Analogously, one shows the other inequality and so
\bar{x}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{y}
.

Moreover,
\overline{\tau}
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality for all points
\bar{x}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{y}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{z}
. To see this, note that if
\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})

\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})
are both zero or both positive, the required inequality is trivial or already established above, respectively. So without loss of generality let
\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0
,
\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})>0

\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})=\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})\leq\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
as
\bar{x}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{y}
(by the defining inequality of
\overline{\leq}
).

Finally, we define
\overline{\ell}\colon\overline{X}\times\overline{X}\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\overline{\ell}(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=\begin{cases}\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\qquad&\bar{x}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{y}\,,\\
-\infty&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Then
\overline{\ell}
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality for all points in
\overline{X}
. Moreover, by continuity of
\overline{\tau}
, the topology is finer than the chronological one and so
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
is a Lorentzian pre-length space.

Finally, taking the quotient with respect to
\tau
as in Section
5
, where the quotient was taken with respect to
\ell
, we see that all constructions above are well-defined, so we can without loss of generality assume that
(\overline{X},\overline{\ell})
satisfies the
\tau
-point distinction property (

⁢
τ
PDP

). It remains to show that causality holds. Let
\bar{x}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{y}\,\overline{\leq}\,\bar{x}
. Then by definition of the causal relation
\overline{\leq}
we have for all
\bar{z}\in\overline{X}
that
\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{y})=\overline{\tau}(\bar{z},\bar{x})
and
\overline{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{z})=\overline{\tau}(\bar{y},\bar{z})
, hence by (

⁢
τ
PDP

)
\bar{x}=\bar{y}
, as required.
∎

Finally, we show that the limit of globally hyperbolic spaces is globally hyperbolic if the limit is non-degenerate, i.e., if it does not contain chronologically isolated points, cf.
[

, Subsec. 1.4]
and
[

, Fig. 1, p. 6]
.

Definition A.12

(Isolated points)
.

(i)

A Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell)
is said to have a
future/past chronologically isolated point
if there is
x\in X
with
I^{+}(x)=\emptyset
or
I^{-}(x)=\emptyset
, respectively.

(ii)

A Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell)

without chronologically isolated points
does not have future or past chronologically isolated points.

Remark A.13
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space. If
X
is
localizable

[
KS18
, Def. 3.16]
or every point of
X
is an interior point of a timelike curve, then
X
has no chronologically isolated points. Moreover, if
X
satisfies the
\tau
-point distinction property (

⁢
τ
PDP

), then there is at most one future and past chronologically isolated point. Minguzzi and Suhr call such a point
spacelike boundary
and denote it by
i^{0}

[

in the main part of the present article.

We will use the following characterization of global hyperbolicity, which is well-known for smooth spacetimes and has been recently studied in relation to non-smooth approaches to Lorentzian geometry in
[
BM25
]
.

Lemma A.14

(Characterization of global hyperbolicity)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space without chronologically isolated points and with closed causal relation
\leq
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

The causal diamonds
J(p,q)
are compact for all
p,q\in X
.

(ii)

The chronological diamonds
I(p,q)
are relatively compact for all
p\ll q
.

Proof:
.

First, if
J(p,q)
is compact, then
\overline{I(p,q)}\subseteq J(p,q)
and so
\overline{I(p,q)}
is compact as a closed subset of a compact set.

Second, assume that the chronological diamonds are relatively compact and let
p\leq q
(otherwise there is nothing to prove). Let
p^{-}\ll p\leq q\ll q^{+}
, then
J(p,q)
is a closed subset of the compact set
\overline{I(p^{-},q^{+})}
.
∎

We denote by
I^{\pm}(A):=\bigcup_{a\in A}I^{\pm}(a)
for a subset
A\subseteq X
.

Theorem A.15

(Global hyperbolicity is preserved in the timelike interior)
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
, where
\tau
is uniformly continuous (with respect to a limit uniform structure). If all
(X_{n},\ell_{n})
are globally hyperbolic, then the
timelike interior

I_{\overline{\ll}}^{+}(\overline{X})\cap I_{\overline{\ll}}^{-}(\overline{X})
of the completion
\overline{X}
of
X
is globally hyperbolic.

Proof:
.

First, let
S=(s_{m})_{m}
be a countable dense set used to construct the limit uniform structure
\mathfrak{U}_{S}
on
X
. Let
\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}\in I_{\overline{\ll}}^{+}(\overline{X})\cap I_{\overline{\ll}}^{-}(\overline{X})
with
\bar{x}\,\overline{\ll}\,\bar{x}^{\prime}
. We claim that
I_{\overline{\ll}}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})
is relatively compact, which suffices by Lemma
A.14
as the causal relation
\overline{\leq}
is closed on
\overline{X}
by Lemma
A.8
. Let
(\bar{x}_{k})_{k}
be a sequence in
I_{\overline{\ll}}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})
.

Case 1:

\bar{x}=x,\bar{x}^{\prime}=x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{k}=x_{k}\in S
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

By Lemma
A.11
,
(x_{k})_{k}
has a Cauchy subsequence, hence this converges in
\overline{X}
.

Case 2:

\bar{x}=x,\bar{x}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}\in S
.

In this case we have that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}:\bar{x}_{k}=\lim_{l\to\infty}x^{k}_{l}
, where
(x^{k}_{l})_{l}
is a Cauchy sequence in
X
. Eventually, each sequence
(x^{k}_{l})_{l}
lies in
I(x,x^{\prime})
, hence we are in the setting of Case 1 above.
Thus, by a diagonal argument we obtain a Cauchy subsequence of
(x_{k})_{k}
and the claim follows.

Case 3:
The general case.

By density of
S
in
I_{\overline{\ll}}^{+}(\overline{X})\cap I_{\overline{\ll}}^{-}(\overline{X})
there are
s^{-}\in I^{-}_{\overline{\ll}}(\bar{x})\cap S
and
s^{+}\in I^{+}_{\overline{\ll}}(\bar{s}^{\prime})\cap S
. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\bar{x}_{k}\in I_{\overline{\ll}}(s^{-},s^{+})
and so the claim follows by Case 2 above.

Finally, note that
\overline{X}
is causal by Lemma
A.8
, hence globally hyperbolic.
∎

A direct consequence of the theorem above is that if the completion
\overline{X}
has no chronologically isolated points, then the timelike interior of
\overline{X}
is
\overline{X}
itself. Hence it is globally hyperbolic.

Corollary A.16
.

Let
(X_{n},\ell_{n},o_{n},{\mathcal{U}}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pLGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\ell,o,{\mathcal{U}})
, where each
(X_{n},\ell_{n})
is globally hyperbolic and
\tau
is uniformly continuous (with respect to a limit uniform structure). If the completion
\overline{X}
of
X
has no chronologically isolated points, then it is globally hyperbolic.

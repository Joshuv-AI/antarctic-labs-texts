Abstract
In this paper we study the following zero mass Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system with critical growth { − Δ ​ u + q 2 ​ ϕ ​ u = μ ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u + | u | 4 ​ u , x ∈ ℝ 3 , − Δ ​ ϕ + a 2 ​ Δ 2 ​ ϕ = 4 ​ π ​ u 2 , x ∈ ℝ 3 , \begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{4}u,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\ -\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\ \end{array}\right.\end{array} where a > 0 a>0 , q ≠ 0 q\neq 0 , μ > 0 \mu>0 is a parameter and p ∈ ( 3 , 6 ) p\in(3,6) . By introducing a new functional framework developed by Caponio et al. [ 7 ] , we first establish the existence of positive ground state solutions for the case of p ∈ ( 3 , 6 ) p\in(3,6) . Moreover, for the case of p ∈ ( 4 , 6 ) p\in(4,6) , multiplicity results are obtained by applying an abstract critical point theorem due to Perera [ 30 ] .



Keywords:
Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system; zero mass problem; critical growth; multiplicity.

1
Introduction and main results

Recently, there have been growing interests in the study of the following Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+\omega u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(1.1)

where
u,\ \phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\omega,\ a>0
and
q\neq 0
. In
[
11
]
, system (
1.1
) arises when the authors coupled
a Schrödinger field
\psi=\psi(t,x)
with its electromagnetic field in the Bopp-Podolsky theory, specifically
in the electrostatic case for the standing waves
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x)
. In (
1.1
),
q\neq 0
denotes the coupling constant of the interaction between the particle
and its electromagnetic field.
\omega
is the frequency of the standing wave
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x)
.
The unknown
u
represents the modulus of the wave function of the particle and
\phi
is the
electrostatic potential.

The Bopp-Podolsky theory developed independently by Bopp
[
2
]
and Podolsky
[
31
]
is a second-order
gauge theory for the electromagnetic field.
It was also introduced to address the so-called infinity problem associated with
a point charge in the classical Maxwell theory, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
29
]
. In fact, in this Maxwell theory, by the well-known Gauss law
(or Poisson equation), for a given charge distribution with density
\rho
, the electrostatic potential
\phi
satisfies
the equation

-\Delta\phi=\rho,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.2)

Let
\rho=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}}
with
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then equation (
1.2
) has a fundamental solution
\mathcal{G}(x-x_{0})
with

\mathcal{G}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|},

and the energy of the corresponding electrostatic field is not finite since

\mathcal{E}_{\text{M}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{2}dx=+\infty.

However, in the Bopp-Podolsky theory, equation (
1.2
) is replaced by

-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=\rho,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.3)

Take
\rho=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}}
again, the fundamental solution of equation (
1.3
) is
\mathcal{K}(x-x_{0})
with

\mathcal{K}(x)=\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x|}{a}}}{|x|},

and its energy is finite since

\mathcal{E}_{\text{BP}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathcal{K}|^{2}dx+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\Delta\mathcal{K}|^{2}dx<+\infty.

In addition, the Bopp-Podolsky theory can be regarded as effective theory for short distances, and it is experimentally
indistinguishable from the Maxwell theory for large distance, see
[
15
]
. Here
a>0

is the Bopp-Podolsky parameter that has the dimension of the inverse of mass and can be interpreted as a cut-off distance or can be linked to an effective
radius for the electron.

Motivated by the strong physical background, there have been growing interest around system (
1.1
) in the last few years.
In
[
11
]
, based on variational methods, d’Avenia and Siciliano first studied system (
1.1
) from a mathematical point of view.
Precisely, they proved that (
1.1
) admits a nontrivial solution when
p\in(2,6)
and
|q|
small enough or
p\in(3,6)
and
q\neq 0
,
and also showed that, in the radial case, the solutions tend to solutions of the classical Schrödinger-Poisson
system as
a\rightarrow 0
. Meanwhile, they obtained that
(
1.1
) does not admit any nontrivial solution for
p\geq 6
via Pohozaev identity.
Subsequently, Siciliano and Silva
[
33
]
proved that for
p\in(2,3]
,
(
1.1
) has no solution for large
q
but admits two radial solutions for small
q
via the fibering approach.
They also given qualitative properties about the energy level of the solutions and a variational characterization of these extremal values of
q
.
Using different variational techniques,
[
8
,
22
]
considered system (
1.1
) in the critical case and obtained the existence of ground state solutions.
Furthermore, we refer the readers
to the interesting results obtained in
[
34
,
36
,
37
]
concerning sign-changing solutions, in
[
13
,
19
,
23
,
24
]
for normalized solutions,
in
[
9
,
12
]
for semiclassical states and in
[
10
,
16
,
17
]

for nonlinear Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky-Proca system on manifolds.

Note that all the papers mentioned above to system (
1.1
) only deal with
\omega>0
. When
\omega=0
, system (
1.1
) reduces to the following
static case

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\\
\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(1.4)

Problem (
1.4
) can be thought of as a zero mass problem (see
[
1
]
), which arises in certain problems
related to the Yang-Mills equations.

Furthermore, let
a=0
, system (
1.4
) turns into the zero mass Schrödinger-Poisson
system

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi=4\pi u^{2},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\\
\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(1.5)

It is well known that the second equation in system (
1.5
) is called the Poisson equation, which can be solved by

\phi(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}*u^{2}.

In such case, problem (
1.5
) can be rewritten as a single equation in the following
form:

-\Delta u+q^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|x|}*u^{2}\right)u=|u|^{p-2}u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.6)

The absence of a phase term makes the usual Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
not to
be a good framework for studying the problem (
1.6
). It seems
quite clear that the right space should be (see
[
32
]
):

E(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ \Big|\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy<+\infty\right\}.

Based on this, for
p\in(\frac{18}{7},3)
, Ruiz
[
32
]
showed that the corresponding functional has a minimizer at a negative level, which is a positive solution of (
1.6
) in
E(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. For
p\in(3,6)
, Ianni and Ruiz
[
20
]
obtained the existence of a ground state solution by using monotonicity trick and a
compactness splitting lemma. Moreover, they were concerned with the existence of infinitely many radial bound
state solutions via Krasnoselskii genus. Later, the higher-dimensional version of zero mass Schrödinger-Poisson
system (
1.5
) has also been investigated in
[
28
]
. Recently, Liu et al.
[
27
]
generalized the results in
[
20
]
to the critical case that
replacing
|u|^{p-2}u
by
\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{4}u
.
Particularly, by combining a new perturbation approach and the well-known mountain pass theorem, when
p\in(3,6)
,
they obtained the existence of positive ground state solutions.

Notably, inspired by
[
32
]
, the authors in
[
7
]

introduced an appropriate working space and established some fundamental properties for this space including
embeddings into Lebesgue spaces. Moreover a general lower bound for the Bopp-Podolsky energy was
obtained. Based on these facts, by applying a perturbation argument, they finally proved the existence of a
weak solution to system (
1.4
) for
p\in(3,6)
. Motivated by the cited papers
[
7
,
27
]
, the main goal of this paper is to investigate the zero
mass Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system with critical growth, namely

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{4}u,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(1.7)

where
\mu>0
is a parameter and
p\in(3,6)
.

In this paper we mainly investigate two types of results. The first type is concerned with the existence of positive ground state solutions to system (
1.7
)
for the case of
p\in(3,6)
.
Instead of applying some perturbation argument as in
[
7
,
27
]
, we will employ a simpler method to address it.
Our first result read as

Theorem 1.1
.

If
p\in(4,6)
, system (
1.7
) has a positive ground state solution for all
\mu>0
, while if
p\in(3,4]
, system (
1.7
) still has a positive ground state solution for
\mu>0
sufficiently large.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on variational method. The main difficulties in proving Theorem
1.1
lie in two aspects. Firstly,
it is not yet known if the (PS) sequences are bounded or not, since the corresponding (AR) condition does not hold for
p\in(3,4]
.
In a prior work
[
27
]
, the authors dealt with
this issue by adding a nonlocal perturbation term into the corresponding functional. We also note that this approach is helpful to recover the boundedness of the (PS) sequence. However, different from
[
27
]
, we shall analyze the natural functional directly in the current paper,
therefore is somewhat simpler. Precisely,
motivated by
[
18
,
21
]
,
we first study the behavior of
J(e^{2\theta}v(e^{\theta}x))
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
then we construct a
\text{(PS)}_{c}
sequence
\{u_{n}\}
with an extra asymptotically property (see Proposition
3.4
below)

\displaystyle G(u_{n})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty,

(1.8)

where
c
is the mountain pass level of the functional
J
and
G(u)=2\big\langle J^{\prime}(u),u\big\rangle-P(u)

(see (
3.2
) below).
From (
1.8
), we can obtain the boundedness of the
\text{(PS)}_{c}
sequence easily.
Secondly, the unboundedness of the domain
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the nonlinearity
with critical growth lead to the lack of compactness.
This problem can be solved by giving an important upper bounded estimate on the mountain pass level
c
together with
compactness lemma due to Lions
[
25
]
.
Proceeding by the standard variational method, the existence of ground state solutions to problem (
1.7
) follows.

The second type of results deals with the multiplicity result of system (
1.7
)
for the case of
p\in(4,6)
. To the best of our knowledge, there is no result in the literature on the existence of multiple solutions to
Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system in the zero mass case. Thanks to an abstract critical point theorem recently obtained by Theorem 2.1 in
[
30
]
,
we will prove our second result as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\in(4,6)
. For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\mu_{m}>0
such that system (
1.7
) has
m
pairs of solutions with positive energy for all
\mu\geq\mu_{m}
.

To get multiplicity results like this theorem, a crucial step is to prove the (PS) condition.
Since system (
1.7
) involves critical growth, the most we can expect is the local (PS) condition, that is the
\text{(PS)}_{c}
condition
for
c\in(0,c^{*})
being
c^{*}
some positive constant.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, some preliminary results and lemmas are presented.
In Section 3, we show the existence of positive ground state solutions to system (
1.7
)
for the case of
p\in(3,6)
. Section 4 is devoted to the multiplicity result of system (
1.7
)
for the case of
p\in(4,6)
.

Finally, we present some notations used in this paper.

L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the usual Lebesgue space with the standard norm

\|u\|_{q}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},\ 1\leq q<\infty.

\bullet

X^{-1}
denote the dual space of
X
.

\bullet
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
R>0
,
B_{R}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ \big|\ |y-x|<R\}
.

\bullet

o_{n}(1)
means a vanishing sequence as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

\bullet

C
will be used to denote various positive constants which may change from line to line.

2
Notations and preliminaries

In this section, we provide some notations and preliminary lemmas. Let

\mathcal{A}:=\Big\{\phi\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\ \big|\ \Delta\phi\in{L^{2}}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\Big\}

equipped with the scalar product

(\phi,\varphi)_{\mathcal{A}}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\nabla\phi\nabla\varphi dx+a^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\Delta\phi\Delta\varphi dx.

We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
the associated norm. From
[
11
]
, we know that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is dense in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{A}
and

\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{for}\ q\in[6,+\infty].

In order to study the zero mass problem (
1.7
), we need first to consider an appropriate working space instead of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Inspired by

[
7
]
, we introduce the space
E
of the functions in
D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
with finite Bopp-Podolsky energy, i.e.,

E:=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ \Big|\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy<+\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|:=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It has been proved in
[
7
]
that

E=\Big\{u\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\ \big|\ \phi_{u}\in\mathcal{A}\Big\},

where

\phi_{u}(x):=(\mathcal{K}*u^{2})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(y)dy.

Moreover, for each
u\in E
,
\phi_{u}
is the unique weak solution in
\mathcal{A}
of

-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

and

\displaystyle\|\phi_{u}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\phi_{u}|^{2}+a^{2}|\Delta\phi_{u}|^{2}\right)dx=4\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx=4\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy.

(2.1)

In addition, the functional space
E
has the following useful embedding properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
7
]
)
The space
(E,\|\cdot\|)
is a uniformly convex Banach space. Moreover, the space
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is dense in
(E,\|\cdot\|)
. The space
E

is continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
q\in[3,6]
.

Moreover, denoting by
E_{r}
the subspace of radial functions in
E
, we can say something more on the embedding properties.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
7
]
)
The space
E_{r}

is continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
q\in(\frac{18}{7},6]
. The embedding is compact for
q\in(\frac{18}{7},6)
.

For
\phi_{u}
, we have the following convergence properties.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
7
]
)
Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence in
E
. We have that

(i) if
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
E
, then
\phi_{u_{n}}\rightharpoonup\phi_{u}
in
\mathcal{A}
;

(ii)
u_{n}\rightarrow u
if and only if
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\phi_{u_{n}}\rightarrow\phi_{u}
in
\mathcal{A}
.

We also state here an adaptation to the space
E
of a result due to Lions, see Lemma I.1 of
[
25
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
E
,
q\in[3,6)
, and assume that

\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{B_{R}(y)}|u_{n}|^{q}dx\rightarrow 0\quad\text{for some}\ R>0.

Then
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any
\alpha\in(3,6)
.

Proof.

By applying Lemma I.1 of
[
25
]
with
p=2
, we obtain that
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any
\alpha\in(q,6)
.
Recall now that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E
, and hence in
L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by Lemma
2.1
. We conclude by interpolation.
∎

The following lemma is a generalization of Proposition 2.2 in
[
33
]
for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
7
]
)
For all
u\in E
,

\|u\|_{3}^{3}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\|\phi_{u}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}.

Define
M:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

M(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx.

We can easily obtain the fact that for any
u\in E
,

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{4}\leq M(u)\leq\|u\|^{2},\quad\text{if either}\ \|u\|\leq 1\ \text{or}\ M(u)\leq 1.

(2.2)

At this point, we have

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq CM(u)^{\frac{2p-3}{3}}

for
u\in E
with
p\in[3,6]
.

Proof.

Since
D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, for
\lambda=\frac{6-p}{p}\in[0,1]
and by Lemma
2.5
, one has

\begin{split}\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}&\leq\|u\|_{3}^{\lambda}\|u\|_{6}^{1-\lambda}\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq C\left(\|\phi_{u}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\right)^{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq C\left(\|\phi_{u}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\lambda}{3}}M(u)^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}.\end{split}

From (
2.1
), we get that

\|u\|_{p}\leq CM(u)^{\frac{\lambda}{3}}M(u)^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}=CM(u)^{\frac{3-\lambda}{6}}.

So the conclusion follows.
∎

The following General Minimax Principle is due to Willem
[
35
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
[
35
]
)
Let
X
be a Banach space. Let
M_{0}
be a closed subspace of the metric space
M
and
\Gamma_{0}\subset C(M_{0},X)
. Define

\Gamma:=\big\{\eta\in C(M,X)\ \big|\ \eta\big|_{M_{0}}\in\Gamma_{0}\big\}.

If
\varphi\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\infty>c:=\inf\limits_{\eta\in\Gamma}\sup\limits_{u\in M}\varphi(\eta(u))>a:=\sup\limits_{\eta_{0}\in\Gamma_{0}}\sup\limits_{u\in M_{0}}\varphi(\eta_{0}(u)),

then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,(c-a)/2),\ \delta>0
and
\eta\in\Gamma
such that
\sup\limits_{M}\varphi\circ\eta\leq c+\varepsilon
,
there exists

such that

(a)
c-2\varepsilon\leq\varphi(u)\leq c+2\varepsilon
,

(b)

,

(c)
\|\varphi^{\prime}(u)\|\leq 8\varepsilon/\delta

3
Existence for
p\in(3,6)

In this section, we study the existence of ground state solutions to system (
1.7
) in the case
p\in(3,6)
.
The energy functional associated with system (
1.7
) is denoted as

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx.

In view of
[
7
]
,
J
is a
C^{1}
functional on
E
and

\big\langle J^{\prime}(u),\varphi\big\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi dx+q^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u\varphi dx-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}u\varphi dx,

for all
\varphi\in E
. Moreover, critical points of
J
in
E
corresponding to weak solutions of system (
1.7
).
If
u\in E
is a weak solution of system (
1.7
),
by
[
11
]
we can deduce the following Pohozaev type identity

\begin{split}\displaystyle P(u)&:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{5\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right)}{|x-y|}+\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{a}\right]u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy\\[2.84526pt]
&\quad-\frac{3\mu}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx=0.\end{split}

(3.1)

Now,
we introduce the following Nehari-Pohozaev manifold:

\mathcal{M}:=\big\{u\in E\setminus\{0\}\ \big|\ G(u)=0\big\},

where

\begin{split}\displaystyle G(u)&:=\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{3q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}-\frac{|x-y|}{3a}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy\\[2.84526pt]
&\quad-\frac{\mu(2p-3)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}dx-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx\\[2.84526pt]
&=2\big\langle J^{\prime}(u),u\big\rangle-P(u).\end{split}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=J(\tilde{u})-\frac{1}{3}G(\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{12a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\tilde{u}^{2}(x)\tilde{u}^{2}(y)dxdy+\frac{\mu(2p-6)}{3p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{u}|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{u}|^{6}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{q^{2}}{12a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\tilde{u}_{n}^{2}(x)\tilde{u}_{n}^{2}(y)dxdy+\frac{\mu(2p-6)}{3p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{6}dx\right]

\displaystyle=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[J(\tilde{u}_{n})-\frac{1}{3}G(\tilde{u}_{n})\right]=c.

Hence,
J(\tilde{u})=c
.

Finally, we end this proof by showing the existence of a ground state solution for problem (
1.7
).
Set

m=\inf\big\{J(u)\ \big|\ u\in E\setminus\{0\},\ J^{\prime}(u)=0\big\}.

We first claim that
m>0
. Let
u
be such that
J^{\prime}(u)=0
and
J(u)=m
, we derive that

\begin{split}\displaystyle m&=J(u)-\frac{1}{2p-3}G(u)\\[2.84526pt]
&=\frac{p-3}{2p-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}(p-3)}{2(2p-3)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx\\[2.84526pt]
&\quad+\frac{q^{2}}{4a(2p-3)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy+\frac{6-p}{3(2p-3)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx\\[2.84526pt]
&\geq\frac{p-3}{2p-3}\min\left\{1,\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right\}M(u).\end{split}

(3.17)

Applying the inequality

1-e^{-t}-te^{-t}\geq 0,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

we get from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mu(2p-3)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}dx+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}-|x-y|e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{3|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right\}M(u).

Combining this with Lemma
2.6
, we have

M(u)\leq C\left(\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\|u\|_{6}^{6}\right)\leq C\left[M(u)^{\frac{2p-3}{3}}+M(u)^{3}\right],

that is
M(u)\geq C>0
. Hence, the claim follows by (
3.17
).

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset E
be a sequence of nontrivial critical points of
J
satisfying
J(u_{n})\rightarrow m\leq J(\tilde{u})=c<\frac{1}{3}S^{\frac{3}{2}}
.Using the earlier arguments, we can deduce that

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E
. Arguing as before,
there exists a nontrivial
u\in E
such that
J(u)=m
with
J^{\prime}(u)=0
, that is,

u
is a ground state solution of problem (
1.7
).
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The solutions we obtain are easily seen to be positive. Indeed, if we repeat all the arguments above for the functional

J^{+}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u^{+})^{p}dx-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u^{+})^{6}dx,

where
u^{+}=\max\{u,0\}
. By the maximum principle, we can conclude
u>0
.

Remark 3.10
.

We can also restrict ourselves to the subspace of radial functions
E_{r}\subset E
from the beginning. In this case the proof of the convergence of
bounded (PS) sequence is easier by compactness of the embedding
E_{r}\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see Lemma
2.2
).
By using the similar arguments as the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can also conclude that problem (
1.7
) has a positive ground state solution in
E_{r}
.

4
Multiplicity for
p\in(4,6)

To establish the existence of multiple solutions for (
1.7
) in the case
p\in(4,6)
, we will focus on finding multiple critical points of
J
.
For this purpose, we will make use of an abstract critical point theorem recently developed by Perera
[
30
]
.

Let
X
be Banach space. For a
symmetric subset
A
of
X\setminus\{0\}
, we denote by
i(A)
the cohomological index of
A
,
which was introduced by Fadell and Rabinowitz
[
14
]
. If
A
is homeomorphic to
the unit sphere
S^{m-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
i(A)=m
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(
[
30
]
)
Let
X
be a Banach space,
\Phi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an even
C^{1}
-functional satisfying
\textrm{(PS)}_{c}
condition
for
c\in(0,c^{*})
, where
c^{*}
is some positive constant. If
0
is a strict local minimizer of
\Phi
and there are
R>0
and a compact symmetric set
A\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{R}=\{u\in X\ \big|\ \|u\|<R\}
,
such that
i(A)=m
,

\max\limits_{u\in A}\Phi(u)\leq 0,\quad\max\limits_{u\in B}\Phi(u)<c^{*},

(4.1)

where
B=\{tu\ \big|\ t\in[0,1],\ u\in A\}
, then
\Phi
has
m
pairs of nonzero critical points
with positive critical values.

From now on we restrict ourselves to the radial subspace
E_{r}
. Then we apply Proposition
4.1
by setting
X=E_{r}
and
\Phi=J
.
First we prove a local (PS) condition for the functional
J
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
p\in(4,6)
, the functional
J
defined on
E_{r}
satisfies
\textrm{(PS)}_{c}
condition if

0<c<\frac{1}{3}S^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset E_{r}
be a
\textrm{(PS)}_{c}
sequence, i.e.,

J(u_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{6}dx\rightarrow c

and

\begin{split}\big\langle J^{\prime}(u_{n}),\varphi\big\rangle&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\varphi dx+q^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}\varphi dx-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}\varphi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{4}u_{n}\varphi dx\\[2.84526pt]
&=o_{n}(1)\|\varphi\|\end{split}

(4.3)

for all
\varphi\in E_{r}
. Since
p\in(4,6)
, we have

\begin{split}\displaystyle c+1+o_{n}(1)\|u_{n}\|&\geq J(u_{n})-\frac{1}{p}\big\langle J^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\big\rangle\\[2.84526pt]
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{p}\right)q^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}dx+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{6}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{6}dx\\[2.84526pt]
&\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{p}\right)q^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}dx,\end{split}

then it follows that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E_{r}
.
Up to a subsequence, by Lemma
2.2
we may assume that there is a
u\in E_{r}
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in}\quad E_{r},\\
u_{n}\rightarrow u\quad\text{in}\quad L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\ q\in(\frac{18}{7},6),\\
u_{n}\rightarrow u\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}.\\
\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(4.4)

Similar to (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we conclude
u
is a critical point of
J
, that is
J^{\prime}(u)=0
. From this, we infer that

\begin{split}J(u)&=J(u)-\frac{1}{p}\big\langle J^{\prime}(u),u\big\rangle\\[2.84526pt]
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{p}\right)q^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{6}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx\\[2.84526pt]
&\geq 0.\end{split}

(4.5)

Set
v_{n}=u_{n}-u
. It follows from Brezis-Lieb lemma that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx+o_{n}(1)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{6}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}|^{6}dx+o_{n}(1).

Combining this with (
4.4
) and
J(u_{n})\rightarrow c
, we have

J(u)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx\right]-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}|^{6}dx=c+o_{n}(1).

(4.6)

Taking
\varphi=u_{n}
in (
4.3
), from
J^{\prime}(u)=0
we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx+q^{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx\right]-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}|^{6}dx=o_{n}(1).

(4.7)

Then, (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) yield

J(u)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{12}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}|^{6}dx=c+o_{n}(1).

(4.8)

Up to a subsequence, we may assume that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx\rightarrow l_{1}\geq 0

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}|^{6}dx\rightarrow l_{2}\geq 0.

By Fatou’s lemma, together with (
4.7
), indicates that
l_{1}\leq l_{2}
. Similar to (
3.13
) we have

\displaystyle l_{1}\geq Sl_{2}^{\frac{1}{3}}\geq Sl_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}.

If
l_{1}>0
, we get
l_{1}\geq S^{\frac{3}{2}}
. On the other hand, thanks to (
4.5
) and (
4.8
), we get a contradiction with (
4.2
):

c\geq\frac{1}{4}l_{1}+\frac{1}{12}l_{2}\geq\frac{1}{3}l_{1}\geq\frac{1}{3}S^{\frac{3}{2}}.

So,
l_{1}=0
, which means
v_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Finally, we will show that
v_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
E_{r}
. Arguing as in Proposition 4.1 in
[
28
]
, we obtain similar inequality as follows:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{v_{n}}v_{n}^{2}dx+o_{n}(1).

(4.9)

Combining this with (
4.7
) gives

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx+q^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{v_{n}}v_{n}^{2}dx&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}|^{6}dx+o_{n}(1)\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq S^{-3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx\right)^{3}+o_{n}(1)\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

Therefore,
v_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
E_{r}
, that is
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
E_{r}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
(i), it is clear that
u=0
is a strict local minimizer of
J
. By Lemma
4.2
,
J

satisfies
\textrm{(PS)}_{c}
condition
for
c\in(0,c^{*})
with
c^{*}=\frac{1}{3}S^{\frac{3}{2}}
. Then it suffices to find the subsets
A
and
B
satisfying the geometric assumption
(
4.1
) in applying Proposition
4.1
. Borrowing the argument from
[
26
]
, for fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Z_{m}
be an
m
-dimensional subspace of
E_{r}
.
For any
u\in Z_{m}
, we have

\begin{split}J(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}dx\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\|u\|^{4}-\mu C_{1}\|u\|^{p}-C_{2}\|u\|^{6},\end{split}

(4.10)

where we used the fact that all norms on
Z_{m}
are equivalent. Take
R>0
large enough such that

f(R):=\frac{1}{2}R^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4}R^{4}-C_{2}R^{6}<0.

(4.11)

Let
A=Z_{m}\cap\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}
, then
i(A)=m
. If
\mu>0
, then for any
u\in A
, (
4.10
) gives that
J(u)\leq f(R)<0
. Thus

\max\limits_{u\in A}J(u)\leq 0.

In view of (
4.11
), we may take
\delta\in(0,R)
small enough such that

f(s)<c^{*}\quad\text{for}\ s\in[0,\delta].

(4.12)

Set

\mu_{m}=1+\max\limits_{s\in[\delta,R]}\left|\frac{f(s)-c^{*}}{C_{1}s^{p}}\right|.

If
\mu\geq\mu_{m}
we have

f(s)-\mu C_{1}s^{p}<c^{*}\quad\text{for}\ s\in[\delta,R].

(4.13)

Then we claim that for
B=\{tu\ \big|\ t\in[0,1],\ u\in A\}
, there holds

\max\limits_{u\in B}J(u)<c^{*}.

Now we consider two cases.

(i) If
t\in[0,\frac{\delta}{R}]
, then
\|tu\|\in[0,\delta]
, and (
4.12
) implies

J(tu)\leq f(\|tu\|)<c^{*}.

(ii) If
t\in[\frac{\delta}{R},1]
, then
\|tu\|\in[\delta,R]
, and (
4.13
) implies

J(tu)\leq f(\|tu\|)-\mu C_{1}\|tu\|^{p}<c^{*}.

Hence, the claim follows. By Proposition
4.1
,
J
has
m
pairs of nontrivial critical points
with positive critical values, which are
m
pairs of nontrivial solutions for problem (
1.7
).
∎

Regulation of texture properties, water distribution, and starch retrogradation by Artemisia sphaerocephala Krasch gum in refrigerated precooked alkaline noodles
Authors: Zhili Ji, Man Li, Xinyan Chai, Ji’an Xia, Zhan Wang, Wangyang Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigated the effects of 0.5% Artemisia sphaerocephala Krasch gum (ASKG) on precooked alkaline noodles during refrigeration and reheating. Results showed ASKG significantly reduced recooking loss, suppressed hardness increases by 2.77 N, and boosted tensile strength by approximately 30% after 4-day storage. ASKG enhanced water retention by increasing strongly bound water increased by 11.4%, effectively restricting moisture migration. Microscopic and structural analyses revealed that ASKG reinforced the gluten network, improved starch encapsulation, and inhibited long-term starch recrystallization driven by amylopectin. After reheating, precooked alkaline noodles containing ASKG exhibited improved texture recovery, reduced starch leaching, and a more continuous internal structure. The findings confirm ASKG's efficacy as a natural hydrocolloid in enhancing the shelf-life and convenience of refrigerated noodle products.

Introduction

In recent years, the growing demand for convenience foods has stimulated the development of precooked noodles, which can be stored under refrigerated conditions and reheated before consumption (Joyner et al., 2016). Such products meet the demand for convenience while maintaining the sensory qualities of fresh noodles. However, the cold-chain process frequently leads to textural deterioration, including increased hardness, loss of elasticity, and surface stickiness, largely due to starch retrogradation and gluten network weakening (Gong et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2025). These effects are mainly associated with starch retrogradation, gluten network weakening, and water redistribution within the noodle matrix, posing major challenges to the industrial production and quality control of refrigerated noodle products (Liu et al., 2023).

Precooked alkaline noodles represent an important category within Asian wheat-based foods. Products such as Wuhan hot-dry noodles and Japanese ramen are recognized for their strong hardness, elasticity, and characteristic yellow color. These attributes are closely linked to the addition of alkaline salts – mainly sodium (Na2CO3) and potassium (K2CO3) carbonates (Tao et al., 2019). Alkaline salts increase dough pH, promote gluten deprotonation, and strengthen protein–protein interactions. This results in a tighter gluten network and a firmer, more elastic texture. Higher pH also accelerates flavonoid and phenolic pigment transformations, which contribute to the bright-yellow appearance and distinctive alkaline flavor (Fan et al., 2018; Tao et al., 2019). In traditional formulations, alkaline noodles typically contains 1–1.5% alkaline salts based on flour weight, ranges that define standard product quality (Fan et al., 2018; Hou et al., 2015). However, high sodium intake is a major dietary risk factor associated with hypertension and cardiovascular diseases. Reducing sodium in cereal-based foods has therefore become a significant nutritional goal. Lowering alkaline salt levels, however, often leads to weakened gluten development, reduced dough elasticity, inferior texture, reduced chewiness, and undesirable color (Malik et al., 2025). To address these issues, food hydrocolloids are increasingly used to compensate for structural and functional losses by improving water binding, stabilizing the gluten–starch matrix, and modulating viscoelasticity (Li et al., 2019).

Artemisia sphaerocephala Krasch gum (ASKG) is a natural heteropolysaccharide derived from the seeds of Artemisia sphaerocephala Krasch and commonly obtained as a by-product during seed oil processing. It is mainly composed of L-arabinose, D-xylose, d-ribose, D-mannose, d-glucose, and D-galactose, and contains abundant hydroxyl groups that confer strong water-binding capacity and high viscosity (Guo et al., 2011). In addition, ASKG possesses a branched molecular structure with a linear backbone and side chains, which contributes to network formation and thickening properties (Wang et al., 2023). Compared with conventional hydrocolloids that have been extensively studied–such as guar gum (Lu et al., 2024), xanthan gum (Huang et al., 2025), or konjac glucomannan (Zhou et al., 2014)– ASKG offers unique advantages, including strong water-binding capacity, high viscosity, and excellent film-forming ability (Li et al., 2016). These properties enable ASKG to reinforce gluten network organization, regulate the hydration behavior of starch, and potentially enhance the textural stability of alkaline noodles during storage and reheating. In wheat starch–based matrices, moderate levels of ASKG (around 0.5%) were found to delay starch retrogradation, increase gelatinization enthalpy, and stabilize the semi-crystalline lamellar structure (Nguyen et al., 2020). In gluten-containing systems, ASKG enhanced the viscoelastic properties of dough and promoted the formation of a more ordered and compact gluten network, thereby improving structural integrity under alkaline conditions (Zhang et al., 2023). These findings suggest that ASKG may serve as a potential functional ingredient to enhance the storage stability and reheating performance of precooked alkaline noodles (Jia et al., 2020). However, despite its promising functionality, systematic studies on the effects of ASKG on the refrigeration and reheating properties of precooked alkaline noodles remain limited.

To date, research has primarily focused on the influence of ASKG on dough rheology and refrigeration stability or starch retrogradation in isolated systems without alkaline salts. Limited attention has been paid to their behavior in complex alkaline noodle matrices during refrigerated storage and subsequent reheating. The interaction among starch, gluten, alkali and ASKG in such a multi-component system may play a decisive role in determining the final product quality, including texture, color, water mobility, and microstructure. Understanding these effects is crucial for elucidating the mechanism by which ASKG modulates the quality evolution of precooked alkaline noodles throughout storage and reheating.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the effects of 0.5% ASKG on the texture characteristics, color, water distribution, microstructure, and cooking properties of precooked alkaline noodles after refrigeration and reheating. The concentration was selected based on our previous studies, in which 0.5% ASKG showed the best performance in delaying starch retrogradation and enhancing gluten network stability under alkaline conditions (Chai et al., 2025) Preliminary noodle trials also confirmed that 0.5% ASKG provided good processing properties, whereas higher levels caused excessive stickiness. By combining texture analysis, low-field nuclear magnetic resonance (LF-NMR), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and recooking loss measurements, the study sought to elucidate the role of ASKG in maintaining noodle quality after cold storage. The results are expected to provide a theoretical basis for the application of ASKG as a natural hydrocolloid in precooked alkaline noodle production and offer new insights into the quality regulation of refrigerated wheat-based products.

Materials and methods

Materials

Wheat flour (food grade; 0.41% protein, 0.24% ash, 1.32% crude fat, and 3.07% crude fiber) was obtained from Xinxiang Liangrun Whole Grain Food Co., Ltd.. ASKG (food grade, commercial product) was purchased from Guangdong Huayu Biotechnology Co., Ltd.. Sodium carbonate (Na2CO3, food grade) was purchased from Wuhan Junfa Technology Co., Ltd.. Potassium bromide (analytical grade) was supplied by Sinopharm Reagent Co., Ltd.. All other reagents and chemicals were of analytical purity.

Preparation of precooked alkaline noodles

A sample of 200 g of wheat flour was thoroughly mixed with 0.8 g of Na2CO3. For the experimental group, 1 g of ASKG was added to this mixture, while the control group was prepared without ASKG. The mixture was transferred into the dough mixer (MK15, Muren Electrical Hardware Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) and 80 g of distilled water was gradually added with continuous stirring for 10 min until a crumb-like dough was formed (Chang et al., 2024). The resulting dough crumbs were sealed in a plastic bag and rested at 30 °C and 75% relative humidity for 15 min to promote water absorption and gluten hydration. After resting, the dough was repeatedly passed through a noodle machine (FKR-240, Fuyang Xinbaihui Technology Co., Ltd., Anhui, China) ten times, resulting in a sheet with a final thickness of 1.5 mm. The sheet was then cut into noodles of 2 mm width, 1.5 mm thickness and 20 mm length.

Twenty segments of fresh noodles were parboiled in boiling water for 90 s. After parboiling, the noodles were immediately transferred into an ice-water bath for cooling. The cooled noodles were drained to eliminate excess surface water. The noodles were then stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C for 0, 2, and 4 days to obtain the refrigerated precooked alkaline noodles. For the reheated noodles, the precooked alkaline noodles after being refrigerated for 0, 2 or 4 days were reheated in a microwave oven for 1 min before quality evaluation. Microwave reheating was selected because it represents the most common and convenient consumer preparation method for ready-to-eat refrigerated noodles. In addition, microwave heating provides rapid volumetric heating, which allows the structural and textural properties of noodles to be evaluated under practical consumption conditions. The precooked alkaline noodles without and with ASKG were labeled as NN and NN-ASKG, respectively.

Cooking properties

For cooking quality analysis, including recooking loss and water absorption, samples were reheated in boiling water according to standard noodle cooking procedures. Conventional boiling was adopted because these indices require direct contact with excess water to determine the amount of solids released from noodles and the extent of water uptake during reheating. Twenty noodle segments (M1, g) after being refrigerated for 2, 4 or 6 days were placed into a 500 mL beaker and parboiled in boiling water for 90 s. Surface moisture was removed with filter paper, and the precooked alkaline noodles were weighed (M2, g). After transferring the noodle cooking water into a 500 mL volumetric flask, the beaker was rinsed with distilled water, with the rinsates being added to the flask. The solution was subsequently diluted to volume and mixed thoroughly. A 100 mL aliquot of this solution was transferred to a 250 mL beaker, and most of the water was evaporated on an electric hotplate. The concentrate was transferred to an aluminum box and dried in an oven at 105 °C until a constant weight was achieved (M3, g). The water absorption and recooking loss of the noodles were calculated using the following equations:(1)Water absorption%=M2−M1M1×100%(2)Recooking loss rate%=5×M3M1×100%

The color of noodles

Referring to the method of Koh et al. (2022), the color of the noodles after refrigeration and reheating was measured using a colorimeter (CS-10, Hangzhou CHNSpec Technology Co., Ltd., China). Before measurement, the colorimeter has been standardized using white and black standard plates. The tristimulus values L, a* and b* parameters of the noodles were recorded.

Texture and tensile properties

The textural properties of the noodles were evaluated using a texture analyzer (TMS-Pro, FTC, USA) equipped with specific probes. For texture profile analysis (TPA), a P75 probe was used. Three noodle strands were aligned in parallel on the base plate and subjected to a two-cycle compression test with a 75% strain. The test parameters included a 60 s interval between compressions, and crosshead speeds of 1 mm/s for both the pre-test and compression phases, and 10 mm/s for the return phase. The hardness, cohesiveness, springiness, gumminess, and chewiness were obtained.

For tensile properties, noodles were cut to 5 cm in length and tested using an A/SPR probe. The initial grip separation was set to 50.0 mm. The test speeds were 1 mm/s (pre-test and extension) and 10 mm/s (return). A trigger force of 5 g was applied. The maximum tensile force (strength) and the distance at rupture (extensibility) were recorded.

Moisture distribution

The spin-spin relaxation time (T2) of the samples was measured using a low-field nuclear magnetic resonance (LF-NMR, NMI20-040V-1, Niumag, China) analysis (Wang et al., 2022). A 2 g noodle sample was sealed in plastic film and placed in an NMR tube. The Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) pulse sequence was employed with the following parameters: 180,016 data points, a spectral bandwidth of 200 kHz, a recycle delay of 2000 ms, 8 scans, and an echo time of 0.300 ms. The resulting NMR data were processed by inverse Laplace transformation to obtain the transverse relaxation time (T2) distribution.

Scanning electron microscope

The cross-section of the noodles was observed using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, S-3000, Hitachi Instruments, Ltd., Japan) (Chen et al., 2023). The precooked alkaline noodles after refrigeration and reheating were freeze-dried and fixed on the sample stage for gold spraying treatment. The acceleration voltage is 3 kV and the amplification factor is 200×.

Short-range order degree

The short-range order structure of precooked alkaline noodles was analyzed by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, NEXUS 670, Nicolet Instrument Corp., USA) (Xiao et al., 2024). The freeze-dried noodles were grounded into powder and mixed with potassium bromide at a ratio of 1:100 (w/w). Then, it was thoroughly ground in a agate mortar until there was no granular sensation. The samples were formed into thin sheets by a vacuum tablet press. The scanning wavelength range was 400–4000 cm−1 with a resolution of 4 cm−1, and the number of scans is 32 times.

X-ray diffraction

The freeze-dried samples were analyzed using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, SmartLab SE, Beijing Science Star Instruments Co., Ltd., China) (Dong et al., 2020). The scanning angle range was 5 to 50° with a scanning speed of 5°/min. The relative crystallinity (RC) was calculated by the ratio of the area of the crystalline region to the total areas of the crystalline and amorphous regions.

Data analysis

All experiments were repeated at least three times and the experimental data were expressed in the form of mean ± standard deviation. Data processing and graphing were performed using Origin 2017 software (OriginLab Corp., USA), and the statistical analysis of the data was conducted using the Duncan test in SPSS 19 software (IBM, USA).

Results and discussion

Color

Color is a critical sensory attribute that significantly influences the overall quality of noodles. In general, alkaline substances can induce color changes in the flavonoids present in flour, resulting in a yellowish appearance of the noodles (Morris, 2018). Increases in the L, a*, and b* values indicate that the noodles become brighter, redder, and more yellow, respectively, whereas decreases in these values suggest that the noodles become darker, greener, and bluer (Ma et al., 2022). The effects of ASKG on the color characteristics of precooked alkaline noodles after refrigeration and reheating conditions are presented in Table 1, Table 2, respectively. The L values of all noodle samples exhibited a increasing trend with refrigeration time increased. This increase was likely related to moisture redistribution during storage. Part of the internal water gradually transformed into a more mobile state and migrated toward the noodle surface, increasing surface free moisture. The resulting thin water layer could enhance light scattering and surface reflectance, thereby increasing L values. In addition, partial dehydration of the outer layer may induce subtle changes in surface roughness, which could further influence optical properties. The b* value initially increased and then decreased. This phenomenon may be explained by reactions between alkali and the protein and starch components of flour during early refrigeration stages (< 2d), producing compounds that enhanced the yellow coloration. However, as refrigeration time was prolonged (> 2d), the slow degradation of endogenous yellow pigments in the alkaline environment led to a reduction in yellow color intensity (Koh et al., 2022). NN-ASKG exhibited higher L and b* values and lower a* values than NN under 0 day, indicating a brighter and more yellow appearance. It may be attributed to the high viscosity of ASKG, which could alter the gluten network structure of the noodles, thereby influencing light reflectivity and color variation (Li, Shi, et al., 2024). Cooking characteristics

Cooking loss is one of the key factors affecting the quality of noodle cooking. Excessive cooking loss is usually associated with poor cooking tolerance, sticky texture, and turbid soup. The recooking loss rate and water absorption rate of refrigerated alkaline noodles are shown in Fig. 1. The recooking loss rate of both NN and NN-ASKG increased after refrigeration (Fig. 1A). During cold storage, starch retrogradation formed a denser crystalline structure with reduced swelling ability. Upon reheating, these recrystallized regions were more prone to structural disruption, resulting in increased solid loss (Meng et al., 2024). Compared with NN, the addition of ASKG reduced the recooking loss rate of the noodles by 18.3% for 0 day, 11.8% for 2 days and 11.7% for 4 days. This effect may be attributed to the three-dimensional hydrated network formed by ASKG, which enhanced matrix cohesion and limited starch leaching during cooking. In addition, hydroxyl groups in ASKG could form hydrogen bonds with gluten proteins and water molecules, helping maintain gluten network continuity during storage and reheating. Similar results were reported by Gasparre and Rosell (2019) for xanthan gum.Fig. 1The recooking loss rate (A) and water absorption rate (B) of refrigerated and reheated alkaline noodles with or without ASKG. After refrigeration, the water absorption rate of the noodles with or without ASKG increased significantly (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1B). This increase was attributed to starch retrogradation under low-temperature conditions, which increased water uptake during reheating. ASKG further enhanced noodle water absorption. Its abundant hydroxyl groups strongly interacted with water molecules through hydrogen bonding, thereby increasing water retention (Yang et al., 2021). Meanwhile, ASKG could associate with gluten proteins through hydrogen bonding and physical entanglement, improving hydration of the gluten network and reducing structural collapse (Li et al., 2019). These combined effects contributed to better texture retention and delayed quality deterioration of refrigerated noodles.

Texture characteristics

Texture analysis serves as an effective method for assessing noodle quality. Hardness and chewiness are key textural parameters that correlate closely with sensory evaluation (Huang et al., 2024). Fig. 2 illustrates the variation in hardness and chewability of precooked alkaline noodles after refrigerated and reheated conditions. Both hardness and chewability of NN increased progressively with refrigeration time increased from 0 to 4 days. This trend can be primarily attributed to moisture loss, which caused the internal structure of the noodles to become denser over time. As water migrated within the noodle matrix, the interactions between gluten proteins and starch molecules were progressively strengthened, leading to a more compact and rigid structure. The process of moisture loss also altered the starch–gluten network, further contributing to the increased hardness of the noodles (Li, Shao, et al., 2024).Fig. 2The textural properties (A and C: hardness; B and D: chewiness) of refrigerated and reheated alkaline noodles with and without ASKG. After adding ASKG, the rate of increase in noodle hardness and chewiness during refrigeration was reduced, indicating that ASKG effectively inhibited the hardening of noodles and delayed the aging process during storage. At the early stage of refrigeration (0 and 2 days), ASKG slightly promoted noodle hardness, whereas with prolonged storage, both hardness and chewiness were lower compared with NN. It may be attributed to the formation of a viscous colloidal phase by ASKG. Through hydrogen bonding and physical entanglement with gluten proteins, ASKG promoted the formation of a denser and more continuous gluten network, thereby initially increasing noodle firmness and chewiness (Zhang et al., 2022). As the refrigeration time increased to 4 days, the hardness of NN-ASKG significantly decreased by 2.77 N compared with NN. In the complex alkaline noodle matrix, ASKG likely interacted simultaneously with starch, gluten, and water. The abundant hydroxyl groups of ASKG could associate with hydroxyl groups on starch chains through hydrogen bonding, which restricted starch chain mobility and reduced the reassociation of amylopectin during storage. Meanwhile, ASKG could bind and immobilize water molecules, limiting water migration and reducing the availability of free water required for starch recrystallization. In addition, interactions between ASKG and gluten proteins may help maintain gluten network continuity, thereby buffering structural collapse caused by starch retrogradation. These combined effects contributed to the lower rate of hardness increase in alkaline noodles.

After reheating, the hardness of the noodles was lower than that of noodles under refrigeration conditions. During the reheating process, water molecules vibrated in the microwave field, leading to water redistribution. It allowed the starch and protein in the noodles to become fully hydrated, restoring their softness and elasticity. In addition, the partially gelatinized starch from the precooking stage further gelatinized upon heating, resulting in reduced hardness. For the reheated noodles, the hardness and chewiness of the noodles without ASKG increased by 15.13 N and 14.2 mj respectively with refrigeration days increasing to 4 d. After adding ASKG, the hardness and chewiness of the reheated noodles increased by 8.67 N and 6.95 mj respectively. What's more, the hardness and chewiness of NN-ASKG after reheating were significantly lower than these of NN after reheating. It might due to the enhancement of water-holding capacity after adding ASKG, which improved the structural stability and reduced the increase in hardness and chewiness of noodles after refrigeration and reheating (Han & Zhu, 2024).

Tensile properties

The tensile characteristics of refrigerated and reheated alkaline noodles with and without ASKG are shown in Fig. 3. With the extension of refrigeration time, the maximum tensile resistance and breaking distance of the noodles gradually decreased. On one hand, the interaction between starch and gluten weakened during refrigeration, resulting in a less cohesive gluten network structure. On the other hand, starch retrogradation led to water separation, increasing the free water content. The elevated free water, acting as a plasticizer, facilitated the movement of gluten proteins, thereby progressively reducing the tensile strength and breaking distance (Yang et al., 2025). The addition of ASKG increased the maximum tensile resistance and breaking distance of the noodles under the same refrigeration period. In particular, the tensile resistance of NN-ASKG refrigerated for 4 days increased by approximately 30% compared with NN. It was mainly caused by the thickening effect of ASKG and its interaction with gluten protein and starch molecules. ASKG promoted the formation of more hydrogen bonds in the system, thereby enhancing the strength of the gluten network and helping noodles maintain better tensile properties (Xu et al., 2020). For the reheated noodles, the tensile properties increased overall. It might be due to the thermal effect generated by microwave reheating, which led to an increase in the internal pressure of the starch granules that were not completely gelatinized in the precooked noodles, As a result, the starch granules expanded and formed a denser structure with the gluten network, thereby enhancing the tensile properties of the reheated noodles (Xu et al., 2023). In addition, the tensile strength and breaking distance of NN-ASKG refrigerated for 2 days increased by 27.3% and 7.3% after reheating, and these of NN-ASKG refrigerated for 4 days increased by 16.8% and 22.4% after reheating, respectively, compared with those of NN. ASKG increased the maximum tensile resistance and breaking distance by promoting the formation of more hydrogen bonds within the system. This enhancement strengthened the gluten network structure and stabilized it against the weakening effects of refrigeration (Qi et al., 2023).Fig. 3The tensile properties (A and C: maximum tensile resistance; B and D: breaking distance) of refrigerated and reheated alkaline noodles. Moisture distribution

The moisture distribution of refrigerated and reheated alkaline noodles with or without ASKG is presented in Table 3, Table 4, respectively. T21 and A21 represents strongly bound water, which is directly associated with the polar groups of macromolecules within the noodles. T22 and A22 corresponds to weakly bound water, indirectly linked to macromolecules via hydrogen bonds or directly attached to strongly bound water. T23 and A23 reflects free water, located on the surface of the noodles and characterized by high mobility (Tuersuntuoheti et al., 2019). With prolonged refrigeration time, both NN and NN-ASKG showed decreases in A21 and A22, accompanied by an increase in A23. This trend was likely caused by starch retrogradation and weakening of water-holding capacity during storage, which promoted water migration from the internal matrix toward the surface prior to evaporation. The increase in free water was consistent with the higher L values observed during storage. Compared with NN, the A21 values of NN-ASKG samples increased by 7.4% and 11.4% after 2 days and 4 days of refrigeration, respectively. It suggested that NN-ASKG could effectively retain a higher amount of strongly bound water, thereby slowing the rate of moisture loss and contributing to the noodles' moisture-preservation properties. The enhanced hydration capacity of ASKG was mainly related to its numerous hydroxyl groups, which strongly interacted with water molecules. During gluten hydration and network development, ASKG also competed for water and redistributed moisture between starch and gluten phases, which influenced protein cross-linking and matrix organization (Xu et al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2020). Microstructure

The microscopic network structure of noodles is closely associated with their overall quality. Fig. 4 illustrates the microstructural changes in precooked alkaline noodles after refrigeration and reheating. The samples stored for 0 day showed small pores and the gluten network appeared continuous. As refrigeration time increased, the pores became more apparent, and the gluten matrix began to fracture. The internal structure became discontinuous and defective. Most starch granules were exposed on the surface rather than tightly embedded, indicating that refrigeration disrupted the cross-linking of the gluten network (Zhang et al., 2025). With ASKG addition, the structural continuity of the noodles improved. Fractures were no longer observed, and most starch granules were embedded within the matrix. The gluten network appeared denser and more cohesive. These findings suggested that ASKG promoted a more uniform distribution of water and contributed to a more ordered and stable gluten network (Zhao et al., 2021).Fig. 4The microstructure of precooked alkaline noodles with and without ASKG. Compared with NN-ASKG after 0-day storage, the reheated NN-ASKG developed cracks. This was likely due to rapid moisture evaporation after precooking. The outer layer of the noodles dried quickly, while the interior remained moist, leading to uneven expansion and contraction. After 2 and 4 days of refrigeration, fewer starch granules were observed following microwave reheating, and the gluten network became more continuous. Short-range order structure

The short-range order of refrigerated and reheated alkaline noodles with and without ASKG is shown in Fig. 5. As refrigeration time increased to 4 days, the R1049/1022 ratio of NN gradually rose by 11.3%, indicating progressive starch retrogradation. During storage, starch molecules increasingly interacted and formed more ordered structures, thereby enhancing short-range order (Bai et al., 2023). The effect of ASKG on the short-range order of starch components showed a distinct pattern. In the early stage of refrigeration, the high viscosity of ASKG strengthened interactions among starch molecules, leading to an weak increase in short-range order. However, as refrigeration continued, moisture in the noodles gradually decreased. ASKG competed with starch for the remaining water, which weakened starch–starch interactions and reduced short-range order. For the reheated alkaline noodles, water molecules absorbed microwave energy and vibrated rapidly, and this vibration was sufficient to break hydrogen bonds between starch molecules. As a result, the molecular structure became looser, further decreasing short-range order (Padalino et al., 2019).Fig. 5The short-range order of refrigerated (A) and reheated (B) alkaline noodles with and without ASKG. Long-range order structure

Fig. 6 shows the X-ray diffraction pattern and relative crystallinity (RC) values of the precooked alkaline noodles. In Fig. 6A and B, a diffraction peak at 20° was observed in the XRD patterns, representing the characteristic signature of the V-type crystalline structure. This feature indicated the formation of starch–lipid complexes (Maache-Rezzoug et al., 2011). All samples also showed a peak at 17° during refrigeration, suggesting the B-type crystallites. B-type crystals are typically formed during the storage of gelatinized starch, where amylose and amylopectin chains reassociate to form double-helix structures (Arp et al., 2020). Therefore, the intensity of the peak at 17° can be used to evaluate the degree of starch retrogradation. During refrigeration, water was redistributed among starch molecules. Part of the water was reabsorbed, facilitating the reformation of hydrogen bonds and starch retrogradation. RC values increased gradually with prolonged refrigeration (Fig. 6C and D). This trend was mainly due to continuous molecular interactions and the formation of more ordered structures during storage, as well as recrystallization associated with starch retrogradation (Lee et al., 2019). For the precooked alkaline noodles stored with 0 day, the RC values of NN-ASKG were 2.4% higher that of NN, indicating that ASKG promoted starch rearrangement through hydrogen bonding with starch chains (Wang et al., 2023). After 4 days, the RC of NN-ASKG was 1.8% lower than that of NN. These findings demonstrated that ASKG promoted short-term retrogradation but inhibited long-term retrogradation. This behavior was attributed to ASKG's ability to restrict the reassociation of amylopectin, thereby limiting its crystallization.

Microwave treatment further reduced the RC values of the precooked alkaline noodles. This reduction may be due to the thermal effect of microwaves, which can cause depolymerization or melting of the microcrystalline regions within starch granules, disrupting their ordered molecular structure (Song et al., 2025).

Interaction mechanism of ASKG in precooked alkaline noodles

A schematic illustration of the interaction mechanism of ASKG in refrigerated precooked alkaline noodles is presented in Fig. 7. During refrigerated storage, starch retrogradation and moisture redistribution progressively altered the stability of the noodle matrix. In the absence of ASKG, amylopectin chains gradually reassociated and recrystallized, leading to the formation of a denser starch phase. Meanwhile, water molecules migrated from the internal matrix to regions with higher mobility, weakening gluten hydration and reducing the continuity of the gluten network. These structural changes resulted in increased hardness, structural defects, and quality deterioration during storage.Fig. 7Mechanistic diagram of ASKG in refrigerated alkaline noodles.Fig. 7

Mechanistic diagram of ASKG in refrigerated alkaline noodles.

After ASKG incorporation, the molecular interactions within the alkaline noodle matrix were significantly modified. Due to its abundant hydroxyl groups and branched polysaccharide structure, ASKG exhibited strong hydration ability and formed extensive hydrogen bonds with water molecules. This interaction restricted water mobility and reduced moisture migration during refrigeration. In addition, ASKG competed with starch molecules for available water, thereby limiting the water required for amylopectin reassociation and recrystallization. As a result, long-term starch retrogradation was effectively suppressed. ASKG also interacted with gluten proteins through hydrogen bonding and physical entanglement, which enhanced the continuity and stability of the gluten network. The presence of ASKG promoted a more uniform distribution of water within the noodle matrix and hindered the structural disruption caused by starch retrogradation during storage. Consequently, NN-ASKG samples maintained a more compact and continuous microstructure, lower hardness increase, improved water retention, and reduced cooking loss compared with NN samples.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that ASKG effectively improved the refrigeration and reheating performance of precooked alkaline noodles. ASKG promoted more uniform water distribution and improved the stability of the gluten network, thereby alleviating structural defects induced by refrigerated storage and helping maintain noodle brightness. Although refrigeration increased noodle hardness due to moisture loss and starch retrogradation, ASKG effectively moderated long-term hardening and improved textural recovery after reheating. In addition, ASKG reduced recooking loss by limiting starch leaching and enhanced water absorption capacity. These effects were mainly attributed to the strong hydration ability of ASKG. The abundant hydroxyl groups of ASKG formed hydrogen bonds with water molecules, thereby restricting water mobility and reducing the availability of free water required for amylopectin reassociation and recrystallization. Meanwhile, interactions between ASKG and gluten proteins helped maintain the continuity and stability of the gluten network during storage. Microstructural and crystallographic analyses further demonstrated that ASKG promoted short-term molecular ordering while inhibiting long-term starch recrystallization, revealing a clear relationship between molecular interactions and macroscopic quality changes in refrigerated alkaline noodles. These findings indicate that ASKG has practical potential for industrial production of refrigerated, ready-to-heat noodles and provides a basis for future work aimed at optimizing formulation ratios and quantifying structure–function mechanisms under extended storage conditions. Moreover, future research should clarify the molecular basis of ASKG–gluten interactions, particularly the role of hydrogen-bonding patterns in strengthening network continuity, to support the development of precooked noodle products with superior structural resilience.

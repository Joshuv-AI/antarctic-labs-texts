Multidimensional poverty and health: an analysis of multidimensional poverty and its influencing factors among rural residents in Western China from a health ecology perspective
Authors: Chunsheng Li, Qi Hu, Ximin Ma, Jiahui He, Yongxin Xie, Hui Qiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough absolute poverty in rural China has been systematically eradicated, rural residents continue to suffer deprivation in terms of education, healthcare and living conditions, which has become an urgent obstacle to China’s goal of rural revitalisation and common prosperity. In addition, the impact of health on multidimensional poverty remains unclear.MethodsThis study constructs four periods of balanced panel data on the basis of baseline survey data from 2012 and follow-up survey data from 2015, 2019 and 2022 to conduct a longitudinal study of multidimensional poverty among rural residents. The Alkire-Foster method was used to measure the multidimensional poverty of the rural population. A fixed-effects logit model with panel data and health ecology theory is used to analyse the impact of health-related factors on multidimensional poverty.ResultsFrom 2012 to 2022, the Multidimensional Poverty Index (M₀) of rural residents decreased from 0.288 to 0.093, and the multidimensional poverty incidence (H) decreased from 58.0% to 20.2%. Among them, the contribution rate of the education dimension to multidimensional poverty always ranked first. The contribution rate of the health dimension increased significantly from 14.7% to 32.9%. The results of the fixed effects model show that increasing age (OR 2.123/4.350, 95% CI 1.802 to 2.501/3.411 to 5.547), chronic diseases (OR 1.969, 95% CI 1.757 to 2.206), catastrophic health expenditure (OR 1.446, 95% CI 1.262 to 1.658) could significantly increase the risk of multidimensional poverty in rural residents. Using outpatient health services (OR 0.632, 95% CI 0.542 to 0.738), using inpatient health services (OR 0.599, 95% CI 0.525 to 0.684), being married (OR 0.553, 95% CI 0.418 to 0.732) could significantly reduce the risk of multidimensional poverty.ConclusionIn the past decade, the multidimensional poverty of rural residents in Western China has been significantly improved, but the impact of the health dimension continues to expand. Increasing investment in medical infrastructure in rural areas and improving residents’ utilisation of health services are important ways to reduce multidimensional poverty in rural areas. Meanwhile, attention should be paid to the vulnerable position of patients with chronic diseases and families incurring catastrophic health expenditures in multidimensional poverty.

Background

With the rise of antiglobalisation sentiment and frequent geopolitical turmoil, regional poverty issues have become increasingly complex, posing new challenges for poverty governance. To meet the needs of poverty governance in the current era and provide policymakers with adequate theoretical support, the multidimensional poverty concept developed based on Amartya Sen’s viability theory has gradually become a more representative method of poverty identification.1 2 Multidimensional poverty is the deprivation of an individual or a family along multiple basic dimensions of life, including not only economic income shortfalls but also deprivations related to education, health, housing conditions, access to infrastructure (eg, clean water, sanitation), nutritional status, employment opportunities and social integration.3 According to the latest edition of the Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) report released by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) and the Oxford University Centre for the Study of Poverty and Human Development (OPHI), 1.1 billion people (more than 18% of the total population of the 110 countries covered in the report) live in severe multidimensional poverty, two-thirds of whom live in middle-income countries and 83% of whom live in rural areas.4 It follows that multidimensional poverty is a global challenge and its impact on rural areas deserves attention.

Although China achieved its goal of eliminating absolute poverty as early as 2020, deprivation in areas such as education, healthcare and living conditions still exists in rural areas of China. These forms of deprivation are intertwined, creating multidimensional poverty issues. Currently, the problem of multidimensional deprivation in China’s rural areas has constituted an urgent impediment to the goals of rural revitalisation and common prosperity. In this study, we chose to examine multidimensional poverty in rural areas of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region of China, hoping to explore the problem of multidimensional poverty in China from a micro perspective. Over the past few decades, the governance of poverty issues in rural areas of Ningxia has been one of the representative achievements of China’s poverty alleviation efforts. Therefore, a decade-long observational study of rural residents in this region can reflect the multidimensional poverty trends and governance effectiveness in rural China. It is worth noting that summarising the development characteristics of multidimensional poverty in rural China can provide a reference for multidimensional poverty governance in other developing countries.

Currently, multidimensional poverty and its association with health factors have garnered significant attention. In measuring multidimensional poverty, the mainstream approach typically employs short-term panel data and cross-sectional data as the data foundation,58 with the household serving as the basic unit of measurement.913 Such approaches often fail to accurately capture the multidimensional poverty trends of research subjects and may overlook deprivation from an individual perspective. Additionally, when examining the relationship between multidimensional poverty and health, some studies often combine multidimensional poverty status with one or more specific health factors or diseases to analyse the relationship between multidimensional poverty and health.14 15 This approach ignores the complexity and diversity of personal health issues as a whole. Therefore, this study is the first to attempt to introduce health ecology theory into multidimensional poverty research and use its multidimensional theoretical framework to understand the relationship between multidimensional poverty and health. Health ecology theory was originally established by psychologist Bronfenbrenner, who broke down the environmental factors affecting individual health into individual, interpersonal, community and societal levels, and systematically explained the view that human health is affected by the complexity of multilevel environmental factors.16 17 It is evident that this perspective also applies to multidimensional poverty at the individual level, where the determinants of multidimensional poverty are similarly composed of a set of multilevel risks, which also exhibit systemic and structural characteristics of ecological distribution. Therefore, we attempt to integrate potential health factors influencing individual multidimensional poverty from a micro to macro level through a health ecology lens and analyse the impact effects of health factors at each level on multidimensional poverty. This approach represents an organic integration of multidimensional poverty and ecological theory, offering a new perspective on understanding the relationship between multidimensional poverty and health, and providing a scientifically sound and effective research framework for promoting precise and systematic governance of multidimensional poverty.

This study aimed to understand the dynamics and trends of multidimensional poverty among rural residents in Western China and to explore the impact of health-related factors on multidimensional poverty among rural residents.

Methods

Data source

Data were obtained from the project ‘Innovating payment system and improving health benefits’ (2012) and its follow-up study (2015, 2019 and 2022), which is a joint project of Harvard University, Oxford University, Fudan University and Ningxia Medical University. Research projects for 2015, 2019 and 2022 originate from the National Natural Science Foundation of China. The project systematically obtained information on the rural population, including demographic characteristics, personal health status, utilisation of health services and household economic status, via a face‒to‒face questionnaire. The field survey employed a multistage stratified cluster random sampling method. First stage: Simple random sampling was used to select four sample counties in Ningxia (Yanchi, Haiyuan, Xiji and Pengyang County); Second stage: All townships in the sample counties were divided into three strata based on their level of economic development (good, medium and poor), and a random number table method was used to select sample villages at a 40% proportion from each stratum; Third stage: A systematic sampling method was used to select 20–33 currently residing households as sample households in each sample village, and all permanent household members (residing locally for ≥6 months) were included as survey subjects.

In this study, 2012 was selected as the base year, and three periods of data from 2015, 2019 and 2022 were used as the subsequent follow-up observation years to construct 10-year, four-period balanced sample data. As shown in online supplemental figure S1, after excluding lost-to-follow-up data (including samples of deceased, relocated, long-term absentee and refusal to participate individuals) and missing data for important variables, and after rigorous collation and review, the study obtained a total of 6153 balanced samples.

Alkire-Foster method

The Alkire-Foster method overcomes the bias caused by using income as the sole criterion for measuring poverty by considering the deprivation that individuals or families may face in multiple areas (such as income, education, health), providing a more comprehensive measure of individual or family poverty.18

Measurement dimensions and indicator setting

Before embarking on multidimensional poverty measurement, it is necessary to select suitable and appropriate dimensions and indicators to construct the identification matrix of poverty and to determine the indicator weights and the critical value K of multidimensional poverty. This study constructs an indicator system suitable for measuring the MPI by referring to the indicator settings of the traditional Alkire-Foster method to establish a dimension matrix. First, assume an n×d-dimensional matrix y=yij, where n is the total number of research subjects, d is the dimension of poverty, i represents the individuals under study and j represents each dimension. Therefore, yij represents the score of individual i on dimension j. Meanwhile, zj is defined as the deprivation cut-off vector, which determines the deprivation of an individual on dimension j. Second, assign weights to dimensions and indicators. Third, set the k value for the multidimensional deprivation threshold.

This study constructed an MPI indicator system for individuals based on the Alkire-Foster method, which includes four dimensions: income, education, health and living standards. Deprivation in the income dimension is reflected by the level of income, which in this study is measured by the annual per capita household income of rural residents. The study uses China’s officially established poverty line standard of ¥2300/person/year (US$356.11/person/year) in 2011 and combines it with China’s annual inflation rate to calculate the poverty line for each survey year. Rural residents are deprived if their income level falls below the annual poverty line. Deprivation in the education dimension is reflected by the level of education. The level of education is a generally accepted and used indicator of an individual’s educational status. If the rural population has an illiterate level of education, it means deprivation. Deprivation in the health dimension is reflected by self-rated health (SRH), with unhealthy SRH signalling deprivation. SRH is a widely used indicator of an individual’s overall health status in survey research and clinical practice, and SRH is also commonly used as an outcome variable in survey research or clinical trials to predict health risks and health equity.19 20 The indicators for the standard of living dimension are based on six indicators used in global MPI measurements, including cooking fuel, sanitation facilities, drinking water, electricity, housing and household assets. However, since electricity coverage has been achieved in all rural areas in China, this study only selected the five indicators other than electricity. If a rural inhabitant lives in a traditional house, uses non-clean fuels, uses a decentralised water supply to obtain drinking water, or uses non-sanitary toilets, this means that the inhabitant is deprived of the corresponding indicator. Households are deprived of the household asset indicator if they do not own more than one of the following assets: radio, TV, telephone, bicycle, motorbike or refrigerator, and do not own a car.

Additionally, it is worth emphasising that this study adheres to the standard framework of the MPI developed jointly by the UNDP and the OPHI. Equal weighting is used for the weighting of indicators in each dimension. The equal-weighting method avoids biases in indicator sensitivity caused by subjective allocation and ensures comparability of the MPI across different periods or with similar studies. Additionally, the equal-weighting method avoids overemphasising the importance of a single dimension (such as physical health or economic status) when measuring poverty, ensuring that poverty is measured from a rights–equality perspective. This study quantifies the differences in contributions across dimensions by decomposing the contribution rates of multidimensional poverty from an equal-weighting perspective.18 At the same time, the cut-off point for multidimensional poverty is set at 0.33, and when the MPI is equal to or higher than this cut-off point, it is classified as multidimensional poverty. Fixed effects logit models

In this study, fixed effects logit models were used to analyse health-related drivers of multidimensional poverty among rural residents. Fixed-effects Logit model is an effective tool for dealing with unobservable heterogeneity in panel data, which is suitable for analysing dichotomous or multicategorical dependent variables with a panel data structure, and is able to effectively control for individual or temporal heterogeneity in the data, thus improving the accuracy of the estimation. The model assumes that the error term ϵit follows a logistic distribution.

As shown in online supplemental table S1, the dependent variable Y of the study is the multidimensional poverty status of rural residents (0=non-multidimensional poverty; 1=multidimensional poverty), and the explanatory variable is an indicator system based on health ecology theory. The individual characteristic dimension mainly reflects the individual’s predisposing factors and lifestyle and includes three indicators: age, chronic disease and occupational type. The interpersonal network dimension focuses on an individual’s social network and social support and includes two indicators: marriage status and household size. The community conditions dimension focuses on the accessibility of medical resources and health services in the community where an individual lives and includes four indicators: catastrophic health expenditure, utilisation of outpatient services, utilisation of inpatient services and time required to reach the nearest medical point. The public policy dimension mainly reflects local health policies and policy protection, including two indicators on the target of policy assistance and participation in medical insurance.

Before regression analysis, the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) test showed that the VIF values of all variables were less than 5, with an average value of 1.13, indicating that there was no significant multicollinearity.

Statistical methods

The study used EpiData V.3.1 for questionnaire data entry, Stata V.17.0 for data cleaning, collation and statistical analysis and R for data visualisation. To ensure the accuracy of data collection, the survey uses a multilevel review mechanism and double entry for data entry. The statistical significance threshold for this study was set at 0.05.

Patient and public involvement

Rural households and individuals are not involved in the design of the survey content and the analysis of the data. However, they continue to optimise the mechanism and implementation of field surveys through feedback.

Results

Description of basic characteristics of the sample population

This study included a total of 6153 rural residents. The sample population was slightly more male than female, with the age distribution primarily consisting of middle-aged and young adults. The primary occupation of the sample population was farming, and the majority were married. In terms of educational attainment, the illiteracy rate has steadily decreased, from 44.82% in 2012 to 27.48% in 2022. Additionally, the prevalence of chronic diseases among the sample population is concerning, with the chronic disease prevalence rate increasing from 17.13% in 2012 to 29.97% in 2022. Detailed information on the basic characteristics of the sample population is provided in online supplemental table S2.

Incidence of poverty by single indicator

First, we measure the incidence of poverty for a single indicator, as shown in online supplemental table S3. The results show that the incidence of poverty shows a clear trend between the single indicators for the period of 2012−2022 and that most of the indicators show a decreasing trend. Income level, educational level, housing type, fuel type, drinking water type, improved sanitation and assets of households showed a downward trend in the incidence of poverty, which means that these indicators of deprivation have improved.

Multidimensional poverty measurement

The results of the multidimensional poverty measurement are shown in online supplemental table S4. The MPI (M0) and incidence of poverty (H) decrease and the average deprivation score (A) increases as the value of K increases for the same survey year. Further observation of trends in multidimensional poverty through multiperiod data comparisons reveals that the multidimensional poverty situation of rural residents has continued to improve, with the M0 and H showing a downward trend and the A basically trending downward as well. As shown in figure 1, when K=0.33, the M0 decreases from 0.288 to 0.093 from 2012 to 2022, the H decreases from 58.0% to 20.2% from 2012 to 2022, the A decreases from 49.7% in 2012 to 46.1% in 2015, and there is no significant change from 2015 to 2022, which implies that the improvement in multidimensional poverty during this decade is due mainly to the multidimensional poverty reduction in the incidence rate.

Health-related drivers of multidimensional poverty among rural residents

Prior to conducting the regression analysis, we conducted a Hausman test to determine whether to use a fixed effects model or a random effects model to analyse the panel data. Therefore, we rejected the original hypothesis that the random effects model is consistent and chose the fixed effects model as the basis for our analysis. Discussion

Multidimensional poverty trends among rural residents

The research findings indicate that between 2012 and 2022, the multidimensional poverty situation of rural residents improved significantly, with the MPI and multidimensional poverty incidence rate continuing to decline. This finding is also supported by the same type of studies in China.122123 An important reason is that China’s efforts to eliminate absolute poverty in recent years have effectively promoted the governance of multidimensional poverty. Over the past few decades, the Chinese government has formulated comprehensive and sustainable policies to eliminate absolute poverty in rural areas: in terms of income, it has continuously increased the income of rural residents through measures such as ensuring employment for rural labour and promoting the circulation and trade of agricultural products; In terms of education, the government has implemented a national compulsory education policy to ensure rural residents’ equitable access to education; in terms of healthcare, the government has continuously improved the rural healthcare security system to provide basic medical insurance, major illness insurance and medical assistance for the poor, ensuring equitable and accessible healthcare services in rural areas; in terms of living conditions, the government has continuously invested in rural infrastructure development over the past few decades, aiming to address shortcomings in transportation, housing, drinking water and energy conditions for rural residents.24 The model of combating poverty through the formulation of comprehensive poverty intervention policies, the formation of a broad social consensus and the integration of multisectoral cooperation is an effective experience drawn from China’s long history of poverty governance and it is equally applicable to the governance of poverty in the vast majority of low- and middle-income countries.

MPI contribution rate analysis

A decomposition analysis of the MPI reveals that the education dimension is the main dimension affecting multidimensional poverty among rural residents during the period of 2012–2022, with a contribution of more than 30%. We attempt to explain this phenomenon: some middle-aged and elderly people in the sample population dropped out of school during their childhood due to a lack of financial support or geographical restrictions. As they grew older, it became extremely difficult for them to overcome educational deprivation through continuing education or other forms of adult education. Therefore, although the deprivation of educational opportunities has improved significantly over time, for some rural residents, the impact of educational deprivation remains persistent and difficult to overcome. It is worth noting that the contribution rate of the health dimension to multidimensional poverty rose from 14.7% in 2012 to 32.9% in 2022. We attempt to explain this phenomenon. First, there are obvious disadvantages in the allocation of health resources and the accessibility of health services in poor rural areas, leading to inequalities in access to and utilisation of health services by rural residents,25 26 which in turn exacerbates the deprivation of rural residents in the dimension of health. Second, changes in the disease spectrum in rural areas pose unprecedented challenges to primary healthcare systems. As the prevalence and burden of chronic non-communicable diseases continue to increase among the sample population, rural residents face growing health risks and economic pressures.27 28 The above reasons do not fully explain the formation of this phenomenon, and more research needs to be supplemented.

The impact of health-related factors on multidimensional poverty

We find that among the individual characteristic dimension, age, chronic disease and occupation significantly affect multidimensional poverty among rural residents. There are significant differences in multidimensional poverty risk among different age subgroups, with older people facing higher multidimensional poverty risk. This phenomenon may be due to the fact that older people are more susceptible to health and economic shocks.29 A common explanation for the increased risk of poverty due to chronic diseases is that chronic diseases not only affect a patient’s physical health and ability to work but also result in an increased burden of disease and economic loss.30 However, another often overlooked reason is the lack of investment in chronic disease prevention and treatment in rural areas, which leads to low rates of standardised patient management and long-term health and economic impacts on patients with chronic diseases.31 The impact of different occupational types on the multidimensional poverty of a population may be realised by influencing the level of income, social status, health, education and access to social resources of an individual.32 33 Unemployed and freelance workers tend to be more vulnerable to instability and high risk in the economic and health spheres as a result of job and income insecurity and limited social participation.34 35

In the interpersonal network dimension, we find that married groups and moderate family size are effective in protecting against multidimensional poverty risk. Married rural residents are at lower risk of multidimensional poverty, possibly because married people are able to obtain additional sources of income through their spouses, which increases the economic well-being of family members and reduces the impact of economic risks by sharing the risks faced even when experiencing a family economic crisis.36 The effect of household size on poverty has also been shown in studies of poor areas in Africa, where small households reduce the risk of poverty, whereas increasing household size leads to an increase in the risk of poverty.37 However, other studies have reported a non-linear effect of household size on poverty, with both the smallest and very large household sizes leading to a greater likelihood of poverty.38

Within the community conditions dimension, incurring catastrophic health expenditures increases the risk of multidimensional poverty, whereas health service utilisation, both outpatient and inpatient services, significantly reduces the risk of multidimensional poverty. The negative impact of catastrophic health expenditures on poverty has been widely documented, especially for rural and low-income populations.3941 Reducing poverty and reducing the risk of poverty through improved access to health services for the poor has been practised and proven to be effective in poverty alleviation in China.42 The use of health services improves the health status of the population, effectively reduces the spillover of disease risk and circumvents the long-term health and economic impacts of illness on individuals.

Unfortunately, neither of the two indicators of the public policy dimension has a significant effect on multidimensional poverty, a result that exceeds our expectations. The target group for policy assistance in this study mainly receives economic assistance, but the deprivation experienced by rural residents in terms of health, education and living conditions often cannot be quickly alleviated through short-term economic assistance, and the effectiveness of assistance in addressing multidimensional poverty may require a longer observation period to be verified. In addition, the study found that basic medical insurance had no significant impact on multidimensional poverty. One objective reason is that the research sample was deeply involved in the reform of the basic medical insurance system from 2012 to 2022. Due to the dynamic adjustments in medical insurance policies, the impact of medical insurance on multidimensional poverty among rural residents has not been fully reflected. There are different perspectives from this study in previous studies, with some finding that China’s Health Poverty Alleviation Programme plays an important role in reducing the financial burden of illness for patients,43 44 as well as that China’s extensive health insurance coverage helps to reduce the risk of poverty and alleviate expenditure-based poverty due to illness in rural areas.45 46 Nonetheless, there are studies that support the current study’s finding that the impact of helping policies and health insurance on multidimensional poverty may not always be significant, and that they have a limited impact on supporting rural residents in mitigating health shocks and economic risks.41 47 These studies suggest that when formulating and improving policies, we need to consider the widespread nature of poverty risk among rural residents, while ensuring that the poorest people can derive more practical benefits from support policies.48

In summary, although the multidimensional poverty trend among rural residents has improved, multidimensional poverty still exists and continues to evolve. Given the complex impact of the health dimension on rural residents' multidimensional poverty, there is an urgent need to enhance the precision of health policies and develop more comprehensive health-related poverty prevention strategies.

Innovation and limitations

An important innovation of this study is the use of balanced panel data based on four field surveys conducted over a 10-year period to conduct a longitudinal analysis of the multidimensional poverty status and incidence trends of rural residents in Ningxia. Another significant innovation lies in the incorporation of health ecology theory into the analysis of factors influencing multidimensional poverty, thereby expanding the application scope of health ecology theory and broadening the perspectives and approaches in multidimensional poverty research. A major limitation of this study is that it uses SRH to measure deprivation in the health dimension of multidimensional poverty among rural residents, rather than the more commonly used body mass index indicator. The subjective nature of the SRH indicator may affect estimates of health deprivation. The multidimensional poverty measurement method adopted the traditional and most commonly used equal-weighting method to ensure the comparability and objectivity of the results, but it did not adequately consider the potential differentiated impacts of indicators across different regions, thereby weakening the regional characteristics of the measurement indicators. Additionally, the research method failed to clearly establish the connection between the trend of multidimensional poverty reduction and the dynamic contributions of health-related factors, which is an important area for future research to explore further.

Conclusions

In summary, the main conclusions of this study are as follows: Between 2012 and 2022, the multidimensional poverty status of rural residents in Ningxia, China, improved significantly, with the MPI and incidence rate continuing to decline. The educational dimension has the greatest impact on rural residents’ multidimensional poverty, while the health dimension’s influence is growing. Health-related factors profoundly influence rural residents' multidimensional poverty status through multilevel, multidimensional mechanisms. Increasing rural residents’ utilisation of healthcare services is an important pathway to reducing their multidimensional poverty. Additionally, attention should be paid to the vulnerable status of chronic disease patients, unemployed residents and households experiencing catastrophic healthcare expenditures within the context of multidimensional poverty.

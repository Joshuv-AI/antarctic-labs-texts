Temporal Anchoring in Deepening Embedding Spaces: Event-Indexed Projections, Drift, Convergence, and an Internal Computational Architecture
Faruk Alpay Bugra Kilictas Hamdi Alakkad Lightcap Institute Bahcesehir University Bahcesehir University alpay@lightcap.ai bugra.kilictas@bahcesehir.edu.tr hamdi.alakkad@bahcesehir.edu.tr

Abstract
We develop an operator-theoretic framework for temporal anchoring in embedding spaces, modeled as drift maps interleaved with event-indexed blocks culminating in affine projections. We provide complete proofs for a variable-block contraction lemma (products of Lipschitz factors), a drift–projection convergence theorem with explicit uniform-gap envelopes, and ontological convergence under nested affine anchors with a robustness variant. We formalize an internal Manuscript Computer (MC) whose computations are defined purely by these operators and prove a rigorous finite-run equivalence theorem (with perturbation bounds). For attention layers, we give a self-contained proof that softmax is 1 / 2 1/2 -Lipschitz in ℓ 2 \ell_{2} and derive sufficient layer-contraction conditions (orthogonal/non-orthogonal heads), linking to Attention Is All You Need [ 7 ] . Throughout, we connect our arguments to Fejér-monotone projection methods and convex feasibility [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 ] and to Krasnosel’skii–Mann iteration [ 5 , 6 ] . All floats are placed exactly where written; the manuscript uses only in-paper pseudocode and appendix figures.

Anchors, Classicality, and Context

Anchored implication.
Let
E_{A},E_{B}
be orthogonal projections (propositions) and
P\neq 0
an anchor. In the
P
-commutant
C(P)=\{E:[E,P]=0\}
,
E_{\,\neg A\lor B}=I-E_{A}+E_{A}E_{B}
, so anchored implication reduces to material implication within the relevant commuting family (cf.
[
1
, §2]
).

Proposition 3.1

(Boolean algebra in the P-commutant)
.

Let
E_{A},E_{B},P
be projections with
pairwise commutation
:
[E_{A},P]=[E_{B},P]=[E_{A},E_{B}]=0
. Then the unital
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{vN}(E_{A},E_{B},P)
is abelian; its projections form a Boolean algebra with meet
E\wedge F=EF
, join
E\vee F=E+F-EF
, and complement
E^{\complement}=I-E
. In particular,

E_{\neg A\lor B}=I-E_{A}+E_{A}E_{B}\in\mathcal{A}.

If
[E_{A},E_{B}]\neq 0
,
C(P)
is only an orthomodular lattice and distributivity may fail; the identity above is then not guaranteed. Equivalently: the reduction is valid exactly when
E_{A},E_{B},P
are simultaneously diagonalizable.

Variable-Block Contraction and Envelopes

Lemma 4.1

(Variable-block contraction; vanishing product)
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 1}
satisfy
\mathrm{Lip}(T_{t})\leq\tau_{t}<\infty
. Fix events
1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
and blocks
\Phi(n_{k}:n_{k-1}+1):=T_{n_{k}}\cdots T_{n_{k-1}+1}
. If
T_{t}z=z
for all
t
, then

\left\lVert\Phi(n_{k}:n_{k-1}+1)\cdots\Phi(n_{1}:1)x_{0}-z\right\rVert\leq\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\ \prod_{t=n_{j-1}+1}^{n_{j}}\tau_{t}\Bigg)\,\left\lVert x_{0}-z\right\rVert.

If
\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\ \prod_{t=n_{j-1}+1}^{n_{j}}\tau_{t}=0
, event-time iterates converge to
z
; between events the same envelope applies.

Full proof.

Write
x_{t}=T_{t}(x_{t-1})
and
z=T_{t}(z)
so
\left\lVert x_{t}-z\right\rVert\leq\tau_{t}\left\lVert x_{t-1}-z\right\rVert
. Iterate to
n_{1}
to get
\left\lVert x_{n_{1}}-z\right\rVert\leq\big(\prod_{t=1}^{n_{1}}\tau_{t}\big)\left\lVert x_{0}-z\right\rVert
. Between
n_{1}{+}1
and
n_{2}
,

\left\lVert x_{n_{2}}-z\right\rVert\leq\big(\prod_{t=n_{1}+1}^{n_{2}}\tau_{t}\big)\left\lVert x_{n_{1}}-z\right\rVert
; multiplying gives the displayed inequality. If the infinite product tends to
0
, the event-time distances vanish. Between events the same argument applies because partial products are bounded by the next completed block’s product.
∎

Where it holds / where not

Holds with a common fixed point
z
and finite per-step moduli
\tau_{t}
; no commutativity is needed. Fails if products of
\tau_{t}
do not tend to
0
and there is no compensating event contraction.

.

The proof is a product-of-moduli estimate compatible with Fejér decreases under projections (cf.
[

); the nonstationary schedule is handled via event-indexed block factors.

Example 4.2

(Explicit numeric envelope)
.

Let
M=5
,
\rho_{t}\equiv 1.01
, and each event applies a single contraction by
\alpha=0.8
(so
\mu_{k,1}=0.8
). Then per block
\lambda=\rho^{M-1}\alpha=1.01^{4}\cdot 0.8\approx 1.0406\times 0.8\approx 0.8325
. After
k
events,

\left\lVert x_{n_{k}}-z\right\rVert\leq 0.8325^{\,k}\left\lVert x_{n_{0}}-z\right\rVert
, e.g.,
k=10
gives a factor
\approx 0.16
.

Corollary 4.3

(State-dependent along-orbit envelope)
.

Suppose there exist local factors
\tau_{t}(x_{t-1})
such that, for
u

x_{t-1}
and

\left\lVert T_{t}u-T_{t}v\right\rVert\leq\tau_{t}(x_{t-1})\,\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert
. Then

holds with

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{t=n_{j-1}+1}^{n_{j}}\tau_{t}(x_{t-1})=0

(Uniform-gap envelope)

If
\tau_{t}\leq\bar{\tau}
and

\left\lVert x_{n}-z\right\rVert\leq\bar{\tau}^{\,1+\lfloor(n-n_{1})/M\rfloor}\,\left\lVert x_{n_{1}}-z\right\rVert.

(KM specialization under contraction)

(Krasnosel’skii–Mann) Let
S_{t}=I
,

A_{k,1}=(1-\alpha)I+\alpha T

Example 4.6

(Tightness of the exponent)

Let inter-event maps be isometries (

Drift–Projection Convergence with Variable Blocks

Definition 5.1

(Affine anchors and event blocks)
.

Let closed affine anchors
(\mathcal{A}_{k})
with projections
P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}
be given (firmly nonexpansive in Hilbert
[
1
, Prop. 4.8]
). Drifts
S_{t}
satisfy
\mathrm{Lip}(S_{t})\leq\rho_{t}
. At event
k
, apply
B_{k}:=P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}A_{k,m_{k}}\cdots A_{k,1}
with
\mathrm{Lip}(A_{k,j})\leq\mu_{k,j}\leq 1
. Define

\Psi_{k}:=B_{k}\,S_{n_{k}-1}\cdots S_{n_{k-1}+1}
.

Theorem 5.2

(Drift–Projection Convergence; uniform-gap rate)
.

Assume
z\in\bigcap_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
S_{t}z=z
,
A_{k,j}z=z
. Let

\lambda_{k}:=\big(\prod_{t=n_{k-1}+1}^{n_{k}-1}\rho_{t}\big)\big(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\mu_{k,j}\big)
.
Then

\left\lVert x_{n_{k}}-z\right\rVert\ \leq\ \Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\Big)\,\left\lVert x_{n_{0}}-z\right\rVert.

If
\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}=0
, then
x_{n}\to z
. If
\rho_{t}\leq\rho
and
n_{k+1}-n_{k}\leq M
, then for
n\geq n_{1}
,

\left\lVert x_{n}-z\right\rVert\ \leq\ (\rho^{M-1}\,\bar{\mu})^{\,1+\lfloor(n-n_{1})/M\rfloor}\,\left\lVert x_{n_{1}}-z\right\rVert,

where
\bar{\mu}:=\sup_{k}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\mu_{k,j}\leq 1
.

Proof (firmly nonexpansive chain; explicit inequalities).

Fix
k\geq 1
and write the pre-event drift state

y:=S_{n_{k}-1}\cdots S_{n_{k-1}+1}\,x_{n_{k-1}}.

Because
S_{t}z=z
, for each drift step
\left\lVert S_{t}u-z\right\rVert\leq\rho_{t}\left\lVert u-z\right\rVert
; chaining yields

\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert\leq\Big(\prod_{t=n_{k-1}+1}^{n_{k}-1}\rho_{t}\Big)\,\left\lVert x_{n_{k-1}}-z\right\rVert.

Apply the intra-event maps
A_{k,1},\ldots,A_{k,m_{k}}
(each fixes
z
and has modulus
\mu_{k,j}\leq 1
):

y^{\prime}:=A_{k,m_{k}}\cdots A_{k,1}y,\qquad\left\lVert y^{\prime}-z\right\rVert\leq\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\mu_{k,j}\Big)\,\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert.

Finally, project onto
\mathcal{A}_{k}
:

x_{n_{k}}:=P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}y^{\prime}.

Since
z\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}
is
firmly nonexpansive
, for all
u
and all
w\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
,

\left\lVert P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}u-w\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert u-w\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert(I-P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}})u\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert u-w\right\rVert^{2}.

With
u=y^{\prime}
and
w=z
this gives
\left\lVert x_{n_{k}}-z\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert y^{\prime}-z\right\rVert
. Combining the three displays,

\left\lVert x_{n_{k}}-z\right\rVert\leq\underbrace{\Big(\prod_{t=n_{k-1}+1}^{n_{k}-1}\rho_{t}\Big)\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\mu_{k,j}\Big)}_{=\ \lambda_{k}}\,\left\lVert x_{n_{k-1}}-z\right\rVert.

Iterating over
k
proves the product rate. The uniform-gap bound follows by
\rho_{t}\leq\rho
and

n_{k+1}-n_{k}\leq M
, whence
\lambda_{k}\leq\rho^{M-1}\bar{\mu}
for all
k
.
No commutativity is required
among the
A_{k,j}
nor between
A_{k,j}
and
P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}
.
∎

Ontological Convergence via Affine Projections

Proposition 7.1

(Nested affine anchors
\Rightarrow
unique limit)
.

Let nonempty closed affine
\mathcal{A}_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{k}
with
\bigcap_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}=\{z\}
. Define
x^{(k)}:=P_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}(x^{(k-1)})
. Then
x^{(k)}\to z
in norm for every
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By nestedness,
z\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
for all
k
. Firm nonexpansiveness of metric projections gives, for every
k
,

\left\lVert x^{(k)}-z\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert x^{(k)}-x^{(k-1)}\right\rVert^{2}\ \leq\ \left\lVert x^{(k-1)}-z\right\rVert^{2}.

Hence
\{\left\lVert x^{(k)}-z\right\rVert\}
is nonincreasing and
\sum_{k}\nolimits\left\lVert x^{(k)}-x^{(k-1)}\right\rVert^{2}<\infty
, so
\left\lVert x^{(k)}-x^{(k-1)}\right\rVert\to 0
. Any weak cluster point
w
of
\{x^{(k)}\}
must lie in every
\mathcal{A}_{k}
(since
x^{(k)}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{k}
), hence
w\in\bigcap_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}=\{z\}
. As the distance to
z
converges and all cluster points equal
z
, we have
x^{(k)}\to z
strongly.
∎

Where it holds / where not

Holds in Hilbert/CAT(0) with single-valued nearest-point maps; fails if sets are not closed/convex or if the intersection is not a singleton (then convergence is to the projection onto the intersection).

Lemma 7.2

(Approximate nesting with vanishing diameters)
.

Let closed convex
C_{k}
satisfy
C_{k+1}\subseteq C_{k}\oplus\delta_{k}B
with
\sum_{k}\delta_{k}<\infty
and
\mathrm{diam}(C_{k})\to 0
. Then
x^{(k)}:=P_{C_{k}}(x^{(k-1)})
is Cauchy with limit
z
; uniqueness follows from
\mathrm{diam}(C_{k})\to 0
.

Proof.

Since
C_{k+1}\subseteq C_{k}\oplus\delta_{k}B
, for any
u\in C_{k+1}
there exists
v\in C_{k}
with
\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert\leq\delta_{k}
. Hence
d(x^{(k)},C_{k+1})\leq d(x^{(k)},C_{k})+\delta_{k}=\delta_{k}
. As
x^{(k+1)}
is the projection of
x^{(k)}
onto
C_{k+1}
, we have

\left\lVert x^{(k+1)}-x^{(k)}\right\rVert=d(x^{(k)},C_{k+1})\leq\delta_{k}.

Thus
\sum_{k}\left\lVert x^{(k+1)}-x^{(k)}\right\rVert<\infty
, so
\{x^{(k)}\}
is Cauchy and converges in the complete space to some
z^{\star}
. Because
x^{(k)}\in C_{k}
and
\mathrm{diam}(C_{k})\to 0
, necessarily
z^{\star}
is unique and equals the (setwise) limit
z
.
∎

Where it holds / where not

Requires summable
\delta_{k}
and shrinking diameters; fails if diameters do not vanish (multiple limit points possible).

Multi-Head Attention as Anchored Contractions

8.1
Softmax is
1/2
-Lipschitz in
\ell_{2}

For
\sigma(x)_{i}=e^{x_{i}}/\sum_{j}e^{x_{j}}
,
J(x)=\mathrm{Diag}(p)-pp^{\top}
with
p=\sigma(x)
. For any
v
,

v^{\top}J(x)v=\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i}^{2}-(\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i})^{2}=\mathrm{Var}_{p}[v].

By Cauchy–Schwarz, if
a,b
realize
\max v
and
\min v
then

\max v-\min v=\langle v,e_{a}-e_{b}\rangle\leq\|v\|_{2}\,\|e_{a}-e_{b}\|_{2}=\sqrt{2}\,\|v\|_{2},

a dimension-free bound (tight when
v
has mass on two coordinates with opposite signs). Popoviciu then yields

\mathrm{Var}_{p}[v]\leq\tfrac{1}{4}(\max v-\min v)^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

so
\|J(x)\|_{2\to 2}\leq 1/2
and
\sigma
is
1/2
-Lipschitz; with temperature
\beta>0
,
\mathrm{Lip}(\sigma_{\beta})\leq\beta/2
. The constant
1/2
is tight: in the binary case with
p=(1/2,1/2)
,
J=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/2\\
-1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}
has spectral norm
1/2
.

Where it holds / where not

Global Lipschitz constant
1/2
is with respect to
\ell_{2}
; with other norms constants differ. Temperature scaling is linear in
\beta
; tight in the
\ell_{2}
binary case.

8.2
Head-wise and layer-wise contraction; link to
Attention Is All You Need

Notation.
Here
H
denotes the
number of heads
. Let
P_{h}
be (possibly non-orthogonal) projectors onto subspaces
V_{h}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
U_{h}
denote the head map (query–key softmax, value/output) with
\mathrm{Lip}(U_{h})\leq L_{h}

Proposition 8.1

(Orthogonal heads
\Rightarrow
layer contraction)
.

Assume
P_{h}P_{h^{\prime}}=0
for
h\neq h^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{Lip}(U_{h})\leq\lambda_{h}<1
. Let
W_{o}
be linear and
isometric
on the orthogonal direct sum
\bigoplus_{h}\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}
. Define

U(x)=W_{o}\,[\,U_{1}(P_{1}x)\ \|\ \cdots\ \|\ U_{H}(P_{H}x)\,].

Then
\mathrm{Lip}(U)\leq\max_{h}\lambda_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=x-y
. Because
W_{o}
is an isometry on the orthogonal sum and
P_{h}\Delta
are pairwise orthogonal, we have

\left\lVert U(x)-U(y)\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\lVert U_{h}(P_{h}\Delta)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\lambda_{h}^{2}\left\lVert P_{h}\Delta\right\rVert^{2}\leq(\max_{h}\lambda_{h})^{2}\,\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\lVert P_{h}\Delta\right\rVert^{2}\leq(\max_{h}\lambda_{h})^{2}\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert^{2}.

Taking square roots yields the claim.
∎

Proposition 8.2

(Non-orthogonal quantitative bound)
.

For general projectors
P_{h}
and linear
W_{o}
acting from
\bigoplus_{h}\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left\lVert U(x)-U(y)\right\rVert\leq\|W_{o}\|\,\Big(\sum_{h=1}^{H}L_{h}^{2}\,\|P_{h}\|^{2}\Big)^{\!1/2}\,\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert.

Hence a sufficient contraction condition is

\|W_{o}\|\,\Big(\sum_{h}L_{h}^{2}\,\|P_{h}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}<1.

Proof.

With
\Delta=x-y
,

\left\lVert U(x)-U(y)\right\rVert=\Big\|W_{o}\big[\,U_{1}(P_{1}\Delta)\ \|\ \cdots\ \|\ U_{H}(P_{H}\Delta)\,\big]\Big\|\leq\|W_{o}\|\Big(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\|U_{h}(P_{h}\Delta)\|^{2}\Big)^{\!1/2}.

Each head satisfies
\|U_{h}(P_{h}\Delta)\|\leq L_{h}\|P_{h}\Delta\|\leq L_{h}\|P_{h}\|\,\|\Delta\|
. Insert and factor
\|\Delta\|
to obtain the bound.
∎

Definition 8.3

(Overlap index)
.

Define the
overlap index

\Omega\ :=\ \Big\|\Big(\sum_{h=1}^{H}P_{h}^{\!*}P_{h}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|\ =\ \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}\!\Big(\sum_{h=1}^{H}P_{h}^{\!*}P_{h}\Big)}.

Equivalently,
\Omega=\sup_{u\neq 0}\Big(\sum_{h}\|P_{h}u\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}/\|u\|
. If the heads are pairwise orthogonal and
\sum_{h}P_{h}\preceq I
, then
\Omega\leq 1
; if
P_{h}=P
for all
h
, then
\Omega=\sqrt{H}
.

Corollary 8.4

(Overlap-aware contraction test)
.

With
S:=\sum_{h}L_{h}^{2}P_{h}^{\!*}P_{h}
,

\left\lVert U(x)-U(y)\right\rVert\ \leq\ \|W_{o}\|\,\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(S)}\,\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\ \leq\ \|W_{o}\|\,(\max_{h}L_{h})\,\Omega\,\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert.

Thus a practical sufficient condition is
\|W_{o}\|\,\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(S)}<1
; for equal
L_{h}=L
, this becomes
\|W_{o}\|\,L\,\Omega<1
. This refines
Proposition

8.2
since
\lambda_{\max}(S)\leq\sum_{h}L_{h}^{2}\|P_{h}\|^{2}
.

Practical estimation (overlap sensitivity).

Compute
\Omega
(or
\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(S)}
when
L_{h}
differ) by power iteration on
\sum_{h}P_{h}^{\!*}P_{h}
(or on
S
). In the orthogonal/complete case
\Omega=1
; heavy overlap inflates
\Omega
up to
\sqrt{H}
, tightening the contraction margin linearly in
\Omega
.

9
The Manuscript Computer (MC): Operators as Computation

Definition 9.1

(MC)
.

An MC is
\mathsf{MC}=(\mathcal{X},\ \mathcal{O},\ \mathcal{S},\ R,\ \iota,\ \pi)
with: (i) complete metric space
\mathcal{X}=(X,d)
; (ii) nonexpansive primitives
\mathcal{O}
(averaged maps, isometries, nonexpansive retractions onto closed convex/affine sets); (iii) schedule
\mathcal{S}=(U_{t})
; (iv) readout
R:X\to Y
; (v) encoders
\iota,\pi
between bitstrings and
X,Y
. Execution:
x_{t}=U_{t}(x_{t-1})
, output
o=\pi(R(x_{N}))
(cf. operator-composition view of differentiable programming
[
8
]
).

Lemma 9.2

(Primitive realizability)
.

Affinely writing/erasing bits, coordinate permutations, and singleton anchors (constant writes) are nonexpansive; compositions remain nonexpansive (closedness under composition follows from
[
1
, Ch. 4]
).

Worked branching example (guarded blocks with an explicit 1-Lipschitz bound).

Let state
s=(x,y,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
b\in\{0,1\}
is a guard register. Consider

y:=F_{0}(x)\ \text{if }b=0,\qquad y:=F_{1}(x)\ \text{if }b=1,

with
F_{0},F_{1}

nonexpansive affine
maps. Define affine guards
H_{0}:=\{b=0\}
and
H_{1}:=\{b=1\}
; let
E_{H_{i}}
be the orthogonal affine projections. Define branch sets

C_{0}:=\{(x,y,b):y=F_{0}(x),\ b=0\},\quad C_{1}:=\{(x,y,b):y=F_{1}(x),\ b=1\}.

Anchored-implication style blocks

\mathcal{B}_{0}:=I-E_{H_{1}}+E_{H_{1}}P_{C_{1}},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{1}:=I-E_{H_{0}}+E_{H_{0}}P_{C_{0}}

apply the active branch while leaving the inactive branch unchanged.

Lemma 9.3

(Direct
1
-Lipschitz (and firm) bound for
\mathcal{B}_{0}
)
.

Work in the product Hilbert space
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d}_{y}\oplus\mathbb{R}_{b}
with the standard inner product. Let
H_{1}=\{b=1\}
and let
P
denote the
linear part
of
E_{H_{1}}
, i.e., the orthogonal projector onto the subspace
V:=\{(u_{x},u_{y},u_{b}):u_{b}=0\}
. Assume the constraint set
C_{1}
is contained in
H_{1}
and is of the form
C_{1}=\mathrm{Graph}(F_{1})\times\{1\}
with
F_{1}
nonexpansive affine. Then for all
u,v\in\mathcal{X}
,

\|\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v\|^{2}=\|(I-P)(u-v)\|^{2}+\|P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v\|^{2}\leq\|u-v\|^{2},

and moreover

\|\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v\|^{2}\ \leq\ \langle\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v,\ u-v\rangle,

so
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is
firmly nonexpansive
.

Proof.

Since
C_{1}\subset H_{1}
, we have
E_{H_{1}}P_{C_{1}}=P_{C_{1}}
and the difference of two projections lies in
V
, i.e.,
P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v\in V
. Using the affine-linearity of
E_{H_{1}}
on differences,
\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v=(I-P)(u-v)+P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v
with the two summands orthogonal. Hence

\|\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v\|^{2}=\|(I-P)(u-v)\|^{2}+\|P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v\|^{2}.

Because
P_{C_{1}}
is the metric projection onto a closed affine set in a Hilbert space, it is firmly nonexpansive; therefore

\|P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v\|^{2}\leq\langle P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v,\ u-v\rangle.

Orthogonality of
(I-P)(u-v)
and
P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v
yields

\langle\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v,\ u-v\rangle=\|(I-P)(u-v)\|^{2}+\langle P_{C_{1}}u-P_{C_{1}}v,\ u-v\rangle,

which dominates
\|\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v\|^{2}
by the previous display. Finally,

\|\mathcal{B}_{0}u-\mathcal{B}_{0}v\|^{2}\leq\|(I-P)(u-v)\|^{2}+\|P(u-v)\|^{2}=\|u-v\|^{2},

establishing the
1
-Lipschitz bound.
∎

Remark

(Symmetry and scope)
.

The same argument applies to
\mathcal{B}_{1}
with
H_{0}
and
C_{0}
. The key structural assumption is that the constraint set
C_{i}
is contained in
H_{i}
; then
E_{H_{i}}P_{C_{i}}=P_{C_{i}}
and the guarded block

\mathcal{B}_{i}=(I-E_{H_{1-i}})+E_{H_{1-i}}P_{C_{i}}=(I-E_{H_{1-i}})+P_{C_{i}}

is
firmly nonexpansive
by
Lemma

9.3
.

Theorem 9.4

(Finite-run equivalence)
.

Trace realization:
For any terminating trace
(s_{0},\dots,s_{N})
and output
o
, an MC with
U_{t}=P_{\{s_{t}\}}
yields
x_{t}=s_{t}
and
\pi(R(x_{N}))=o
(constant projections;
\mathrm{Lip}=0
).

Program-step realization:
If each instruction is (i) an affine write/permute/translate on a scaled encoding (operator norm
\leq 1
), or (ii) a guarded update realized by
\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{1}
with nonexpansive
F_{b}
and affine guards as above, then a program-dependent (input-independent) MC schedule reproduces the run for any encoded input using only firmly nonexpansive/nonexpansive operators.

Proposition 9.5

(Perturbation envelope)
.

Let
\tilde{U}_{t}
satisfy
\left\lVert\tilde{U}_{t}(x)-U_{t}(x)\right\rVert\leq\delta_{t}
and
\mathrm{Lip}(\tilde{U}_{t})\leq\tau_{t}
. Then at event times
n_{k}
,

\left\lVert\tilde{x}_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k}}\right\rVert\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\prod_{i=j+1}^{k}\tau_{n_{i}}\Big)\,\delta_{n_{j}}.

If
\prod_{k}\tau_{n_{k}}=0
and
\sum_{k}\delta_{n_{k}}<\infty
, then
\tilde{x}_{n_{k}}\to x_{n_{k}}
(variation-of-constants style bound).

Proposition 9.6

(Step complexity and Lipschitz budget)
.

Let a program have
K
primitive instructions, each realized by one nonexpansive operator, and suppose each guarded instruction uses at most two guard projections plus one constraint projection. Then the number of operator applications is
\leq K+2\,(\#\text{guards})
. The end-to-end Lipschitz constant of the realized map is
\leq 1
.

9.1
Geometry: Hilbert, CAT(0), Banach (constructive retractions)

Hilbert.
Projections onto closed convex sets are firmly nonexpansive and single-valued
[
1
, Prop. 4.8]
.

Proposition 9.7

(Radial retraction onto a norm-ball is
1
-Lipschitz in Hilbert spaces)
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a real Hilbert space with norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
r>0
. Define the
radial retraction

R_{r}(x):=\begin{cases}x,&\|x\|\leq r,\\[2.0pt]
\dfrac{r}{\|x\|}\,x,&\|x\|>r.\end{cases}

Then
\|R_{r}(x)-R_{r}(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

The case
\|x\|\leq r
and
\|y\|\leq r
is trivial. If
\|x\|\leq r\leq\|y\|
, set
u:=\frac{r}{\|y\|}y
; then

\|x-u\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}-2\frac{r}{\|y\|}\langle x,y\rangle+r^{2},\qquad\|x-y\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2}.

Because
\|x\|\leq r
and Cauchy–Schwarz,
2(1-\tfrac{r}{\|y\|})\langle x,y\rangle\leq 2(r)(\|y\|-r)\leq(\|y\|+r)(\|y\|-r)
, hence
\|x-u\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}
. Finally, if
\|x\|\geq r
and
\|y\|\geq r
, write
x=\|x\|\hat{x}
,
y=\|y\|\hat{y}
with
\|\hat{x}\|=\|\hat{y}\|=1
. Then

\|R_{r}(x)-R_{r}(y)\|=r\|\hat{x}-\hat{y}\|,\qquad\|x-y\|^{2}=(\|x\|-\|y\|)^{2}+2\|x\|\,\|y\|\big(1-\langle\hat{x},\hat{y}\rangle\big).

Since
\|\hat{x}-\hat{y}\|^{2}=2(1-\langle\hat{x},\hat{y}\rangle)
, we obtain

\|x-y\|^{2}\ \geq\ 2\,\min\{\|x\|,\|y\|\}^{2}\,\big(1-\langle\hat{x},\hat{y}\rangle\big)=\min\{\|x\|,\|y\|\}^{2}\,\|\hat{x}-\hat{y}\|^{2}\ \geq\ r^{2}\,\|\hat{x}-\hat{y}\|^{2},

whence
\|R_{r}(x)-R_{r}(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|
.
∎

CAT(0).
In a CAT(0) (Hadamard) space, the nearest-point projection
P_{C}
onto a nonempty closed convex set
C
is single-valued and
1
-Lipschitz.
Constructive examples:
(i) On an
\mathbb{R}
-tree (a CAT(0) space), the projection onto a closed connected subtree
C
maps
x
to the unique gate point on the simple path from
x
to
C
; this is
1
-Lipschitz by the four-point inequality. (ii) On a Hadamard manifold (e.g., hyperbolic space), projection onto a closed geodesic ball or a totally geodesic halfspace is realized by following the unique minimizing geodesic to the set; convexity of the squared distance implies nonexpansiveness
[
9
,
10
]
.

Banach (explicit nonexpansive retractions).
Metric projections need not be nonexpansive in general Banach spaces, so we employ explicit
1
-Lipschitz retractions in their native norms:

•

Axis-aligned box clamp in
\ell_{p}
.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
C=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots\times[a_{d},b_{d}]
, the coordinatewise clamp

R(x)_{i}=\min\{b_{i},\max\{a_{i},x_{i}\}\}

is
1
-Lipschitz under any
\ell_{p}
norm because

|R(x)_{i}-R(y)_{i}|\leq|x_{i}-y_{i}|

for each coordinate, and product norms satisfy

\|R(x)-R(y)\|_{p}\leq\|x-y\|_{p}.

•

Product retractions.
If
X=X_{1}\times X_{2}
with norm
\|(x_{1},x_{2})\|=\max\{\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}},\|x_{2}\|_{X_{2}}\}
(or the sum norm), and
R_{i}
are
1
-Lipschitz retractions onto
C_{i}\subset X_{i}
, then
R(x_{1},x_{2})=(R_{1}x_{1},R_{2}x_{2})
is
1
-Lipschitz onto
C_{1}\times C_{2}
.

Remark

(On radial retractions beyond Hilbert)
.

The proof of
Proposition

9.7
uses inner-product identities. In arbitrary Banach spaces, nonexpansiveness of radial retractions may fail without additional structure; our non-Hilbert deployments therefore rely on the box/product retractions above.

Scheduling Patterns: Periodic, Random, Adversarial

Periodic.
With at most
M\!-\!1
drift steps (factor
\rho
) per block and per-event contraction
\prod_{j}\mu_{k,j}\leq\bar{\mu}
, we have
\left\lVert x_{n_{k}}-z\right\rVert\leq(\rho^{M-1}\bar{\mu})^{k}\left\lVert x_{n_{0}}-z\right\rVert
.

Random (i.i.d.).
Let
Y_{k}:=\sum_{t=n_{k-1}+1}^{n_{k}}\log\tau_{t}
be i.i.d. with
\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}|]<\infty
. By the strong law (e.g.,
[
12
, Thm. 2.4.6]
),
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}Y_{k}\to\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]
. If
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]<0
, then
\sum_{k=1}^{K}Y_{k}\to-\infty
and thus
\prod_{t\leq n_{K}}\tau_{t}=e^{\sum_{k\leq K}Y_{k}}\to 0
, giving convergence via
Lemma

4.1
. If
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]>0
, divergence occurs almost surely.

Adversarial counterexample.
Take
S_{t}=(1+\varepsilon)I
for all
t
(
\rho_{t}=1+\varepsilon>1
), no intra-event contraction (
\mu_{k,j}=1
), and unbounded gaps
n_{k+1}-n_{k}\to\infty
. Then
\lambda_{k}=(1+\varepsilon)^{n_{k}-n_{k-1}-1}
and
\prod_{j=1}^{K}\lambda_{j}=(1+\varepsilon)^{(n_{K}-n_{0})-K}\to\infty
, showing that without event contractions or bounded gaps the product condition fails.

Proposition 10.1

(SLLN-based random scheduling criterion)
.

Under the i.i.d. assumption and
\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}|]<\infty
, convergence
\prod_{t\leq n_{K}}\tau_{t}\to 0
holds a.s. iff
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]<0
. In practice, estimate
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]
by
\hat{m}_{K}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}Y_{k}
and declare the convergent regime when
\hat{m}_{K}<-\epsilon
for a safety margin
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

The SLLN yields
\hat{m}_{K}\to\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]
a.s. The claim follows by continuity of the exponential and the equivalence between
\sum Y_{k}\to-\infty
and
e^{\sum Y_{k}}\to 0
.
∎

Deployment Choices, Operational Risks, and Mitigations

Scheduling & drift parameters.
If gaps
M
and drifts
\rho_{t}
are such that the block product
\prod_{k}\lambda_{k}
does not decay, trajectories can diverge.
Section

10
gives an adversarial counterexample with
\rho_{t}=1+\varepsilon>1
, no intra-event contraction, and unbounded gaps (envelope explosion).
Mitigation:
enforce
(\rho^{M-1}\bar{\mu})<1
or monitor
\hat{m}_{K}
in
Proposition

10.1
and trigger anchor intensification (reduce
M
, enlarge contraction) when
\hat{m}_{K}\geq 0
.

Anchor feasibility.

Theorem

5.2
requires
\bigcap_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}\neq\varnothing
; otherwise guarantees fail, risking oscillations under projections.
Mitigation:
validate feasibility via a pre-run check or include a slack anchor that collapses to a feasible
\{z\}
when conflicts are detected.

Attention layer design.
Contraction holds under orthogonal heads with isometric
W_{o}
(
Proposition

8.1
) or under the quantitative bound in
Proposition

8.2
. Overlapping heads or
\|W_{o}\|>1
can make the layer expansive.
Mitigation:
regularize toward head orthogonality, constrain
\|W_{o}\|\leq 1
(spectral norm clipping), and estimate
L_{h},\|P_{h}\|
to verify
\|W_{o}\|(\sum_{h}L_{h}^{2}\|P_{h}\|^{2})^{1/2}<1
; optionally compute the overlap index
\Omega
in
Definition

8.3
.

Geometry assumptions.
Extensions beyond Hilbert require nonexpansive retractions (§
9.1
); lacking these, anchors may be expansive.
Mitigation:
deploy only in spaces with known Chebyshev properties (uniformly convex Banach, CAT(0)), or implement explicit nonexpansive retractions (examples above).

MC execution.

Theorem

9.4
applies to finite runs with nonexpansive primitives and unique guarded intersections; it is not a universality claim.
Mitigation:
restrict instruction sets to nonexpansive operations; ensure guarded constraints define singletons (or small diameters per
Lemma

7.2
); audit step complexity via
Proposition

9.6
.

Perturbation margin.
Tracking under approximation errors needs
\prod_{k}\tau_{n_{k}}\to 0
and
\sum_{k}\delta_{n_{k}}<\infty
(
Proposition

9.5
).
Mitigation:
bound per-event error budgets and decay the Lipschitz product when cumulative error crosses a threshold.

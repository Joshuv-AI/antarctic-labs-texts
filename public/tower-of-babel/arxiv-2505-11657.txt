Adrian Gomez, Cesar Guayasamin

Abstract

Under the condition 1 < ρ δ + H ≤ e 1<\dfrac{\rho}{\delta+H}\leq e , we establish the connection between the equilibria 0 0 and ln ⁡ ( ρ / ( δ + H ) ) \ln(\rho/(\delta+H)) using the Wu and Zou monotone iteration method adapted for two delays ( σ ≠ r \sigma\neq r ). The proof combines explicit upper and lower solutions construction with characteristic equation analysis, supported by numerical simulations.

Introduction

The study of population dynamics plays a fundamental role across biological disciplines, from epidemiology to ecology and resource management. Mathematical models have emerged as essential tools for understanding and predicting complex population behaviors
[
4
]
. A key challenge in modeling biological systems lies in accounting for temporal delays inherent to ecological processes - including maturation periods, disease incubation, resource regeneration, and population response times
[
12
]
. Delay differential equations (DDEs) provide a powerful framework for incorporating these critical time lags, as demonstrated by their extensive applications in theoretical biology
[
14
,
13
,
16
]
.

Among the most influential case studies in population dynamics is the Australian sheep blowfly (
Lucilia cuprina
), a pest species whose population oscillations were first systematically documented by Nicholson in 1954
[
15
]
. Gurney et al.
[
6
]
later formulated the canonical Nicholson blowfly model:

\frac{dx(t)}{dt}=\rho x(t-\tau)e^{-ax(t-\tau)}-\delta x(t)

(1)

where
x(t)
represents adult population density,
\rho
the maximum reproduction rate,
\delta
mortality rate, and
\tau
the maturation delay. This equation belongs to the important class of recruitment-delay models that have shaped our understanding of delayed population dynamics
[
3
]
.

Recent decades have witnessed significant extensions of the Nicholson model, including (non-exhaustive list): Variable coefficient formulations
[
3
]
, Distributed delay approaches, Integro-differential versions, diffusive versions, among others.
These developments have yielded important insights into: Existence and stability of positive solutions
[
2
]
, Oscillation dynamics, Periodic and almost-periodic behaviors. Particularly relevant to our work, when
\frac{\rho}{\delta}\in(1,e^{2}]
, the system exhibits: Monotone heteroclinic connections for
r\in[0,r^{*}]
(Theorem 2.3
[
3
]
), Eventually monotone, non-monotone (Doing
c=+\infty
in Theorem 1
[
5
]
), Slowly oscillatory heteroclinic solutions (Corollary 2.4
[
3
]
)

Figure 1:
Heteroclinic solution types: monotone (left), eventually monotone (center), and slowly oscillatory (right)

The current study, which aims to prove the existence of heteroclinic connections for a Nicholson’s model with harvesting, builds upon the foundational framework established by Wu and Zou
[
10
]
, which was originally designed to analyze traveling wave solutions in delayed reaction-diffusion systems of the form:

\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+f(u_{t}(x)).

(2)

Their approach combines three key elements: the systematic construction of ordered upper-lower solution pairs, a convergent monotone iteration scheme based on integral operators, and the critical exponential quasi-monotonicity condition, which enables the treatment of non-monotone reaction terms through nonstandard ordering techniques.

While Wu and Zou’s theory was originally developed for proving the existence of traveling wave solutions, in this work, we adapt their methodology to a different setting: the study of heteroclinic orbits in a Nicholson-type equation with harvesting and two delays. Specifically, we focus on

\frac{dx(t)}{dt}=\rho x(t-r)e^{-x(t-r)}-\delta x(t)-Hx(t-\sigma),

(3)

where
H
denotes the harvesting rate,
\sigma
represents the harvesting delay, and
\rho,\tau,\delta
are the same as in equation (1).

The delay
\sigma
can be interpreted as follows: The parameter
\sigma
represents the remaining lifetime that an individual, removed from the population at the present time, would have had in the absence of intervention. From a demographic perspective, this can be interpreted as the additional time the individual would have remained in the population according to the survival function. In the context of ecological or fisheries models, this quantity reflects the anticipated impact of harvesting on future dynamics: by removing an individual at the present time, not only is the current population size reduced, but also its future trajectory is altered, since that individual would have contributed to the population for an additional period of length
\sigma
had it not been harvested.

Due to the introduction of the additional delay
\sigma>0
, the model (
3
) becomes a system whose global dynamics remain largely unexplored. In particular, the case with delays
r\neq\sigma
corresponds to one of the open problems proposed in
[
3
]
.

A key challenge in adapting Wu and Zou’s method to equation (
3
) is the presence of two distinct delays, which significantly complicates the construction of upper and lower solutions. To the best of our knowledge, the only prior attempt to extend this approach to equations with two delays appears in
[
17
,
18
]
and in the recent preprint
[
1
]
, where they develop a upper and lower solution framework for a diffusive Nicholson model. However, their models differs substantially from the one developed here. Our contribution provides a complementary perspective by addressing the existence of heteroclinic orbits through a novel adaptation of Wu and Zou’s methodology.

It is not difficult to see that if
\frac{\rho}{\delta+H}>1
, then the Nicholson model with a linear harvesting term (4) has two equilibria: The null equilibrium
x_{0}\equiv 0
and a positive equilibrium
x_{\kappa}\equiv\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{\delta+H}\right)
.
The main result obtained in this work is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<\frac{\rho}{\delta+H}\leq e
, and
\lambda>0
the positive root of the equation

-z-\delta-He^{-\sigma z}+\rho e^{-rz}=0.

(4)

If the delays
r,\sigma
satisfy the conditions:

1.

\sigma<r
;

2.

\sigma\in\left(0,\sigma_{0}\right]
, where
\sigma_{0}
satisfies the relation
H\sigma_{0}e^{1+\sigma_{0}\delta}=1
;

3.

He^{-(\lambda+\epsilon)\sigma}-\rho e^{-(\lambda+\epsilon)r}\geq 0
, for some
0<\epsilon<\lambda
;

Then the Nicholson equation with delay and linear harvesting term (
3
) has a monotone heteroclinic solution connecting the equilibria
x_{0}=0
and
x_{\kappa}=\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{\delta+H}\right)>0
.

The paper is organized as follows: In the section 1 we introduces the theoretical framework adapted from
[
10
]
to our model. In the section 2 we apply this theory to the Nicholson equation (
3
) and build the upper and lower solutions. Finally we presents numerical experiments based on the analytical approximation to the desired solution.

2
Theoretical framework

To make this article self-contained, we adapt the theory in
[
10
]
to functional first order equations having the form

-u^{\prime}(t)+f\left(u_{t}\right)=0,

(5)

for
f:C([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a continuous functional and
u_{t}\in C([-\tau,0];\mathbb{R})
given by

u_{t}(s)=u(s+t),\quad s\in[-\tau,0],t\geq 0,

Note that our case in (
3
) is a particular case of (
5
), with
\tau=\max\{\sigma,r\}
and
f(\phi)=\rho\phi(-r)e^{-\phi(-r)}-\delta\phi(0)-H\phi(-\sigma)
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
u_{0}\equiv 0
and
u_{\kappa}\equiv K
are two equilibrium solutions of (
5
),
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is monotone, satisfies equation (
5
) and additionally verifies the asymptotic conditions

\lim_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\phi(s)=0,\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\phi(s)=K

Then
\phi
is said to be a monotone heteroclinic solution connecting
u_{0}
and
u_{\kappa}
.

Furthermore, given
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define
\widehat{w}\in C([-\tau,0];\mathbb{R})
as
\widehat{w}(s)=w
for all
s\in

[-\tau,0]
.

Now, we assume the following hypotheses about the functional
f
:

(H1)

There exists
K>0
such that
f(\widehat{0})=f(\widehat{K})=0
and
f(\widehat{u})\neq 0
for arbitrary
u\in(0,K)
,

(H2)

(Exponential quasi-monotonicity). There exists
\beta\geq 0
such that

f(\phi)-f(\psi)+\beta[\phi(0)-\psi(0)]\geq 0

for all
\phi,\psi\in C([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R})
such that

(i)

0\leq\psi(s)\leq\phi(s)\leq K,s\in[-\tau,0]
,

(ii)

e^{\beta s}(\phi(s)-\psi(s))
is non-decreasing for
s\in[-\tau,0]
.

We also define the following set
\Gamma
, called the profile set,

\Gamma=\left\{\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\left\lvert\,\begin{array}[]{c}\phi(-\infty)=0,\phi(+\infty)=K,\\
\phi\text{ is non-decreasing in }\mathbb{R},\\
e^{\beta t}(\phi(t+s)-\phi(t))\text{ is non-decreasing in }t\in\mathbb{R},\text{ for all }s>0,\end{array}\right.\right\}.

In this case, the constant
\beta
is the same as in hypothesis (H2). Additionally, we define the operator
\mathbf{H}:C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\rightarrow C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
by

\mathbf{H}(\phi)(t):=f\left(\phi_{t}\right)+\beta\phi_{t}(0),\quad\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),t\in\mathbb{R}

(6)

The following result provides some properties about the monotonicity of
\mathbf{H}
on the profile set
\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose (H1) and (H2) are satisfied. Then for any
\phi\in\Gamma
, we have that

(i)

\boldsymbol{H}(\phi)(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(ii)

\boldsymbol{H}(\phi)(t)
is non-decreasing in
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(iii)

\boldsymbol{H}(\psi)(t)\leq\boldsymbol{H}(\phi)(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, if
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
satisfies
0\leq\psi(t)\leq\phi(t)\leq K
and
e^{\beta t}[\phi(t)-\psi(t)]
is non-decreasing in
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) follows directly from (H1) and (H2) by taking
\psi\equiv 0
. To prove (ii), let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
s>0
. Then

0\leq\phi_{t}(z)\leq\phi_{t+s}(z)\leq K,\quad z\in[-\tau,0]

and

e^{\beta z}\left[\phi_{t+s}(z)-\phi_{t}(z)\right]=e^{-\beta t}e^{\beta(t+z)}[\phi(t+z+s)-\phi(t+z)]

is non-decreasing in
z\in[-\tau,0]
, since
\phi\in\Gamma
. It follows from (H2) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(\phi)(t+s)-\mathbf{H}(\phi)(t)

\displaystyle=f\left(\phi_{t+s}\right)-f\left(\phi_{t}\right)+\beta[\phi(t+s)-\phi(t)]

\displaystyle=f\left(\phi_{t+s}\right)-f\left(\phi_{t}\right)+\beta\left[\phi_{t+s}(0)-\phi_{t}(0)\right]

\displaystyle\geq 0

Thus,
\mathbf{H}(\phi)(t)
is non-decreasing in
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Finally, for (iii). Observe that

0\leq\psi_{t}(z)\leq\phi_{t}(z)\leq K,\quad z\in[-\tau,0]

and

e^{\beta z}\left[\phi_{t}(z)-\psi_{t}(z)\right]=e^{-\beta t}e^{\beta(t+z)}[\phi(t+z)-\psi(t+z)]

is non-decreasing in
z\in[-\tau,0]
. Then, by (H2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(\phi)(t)-\mathbf{H}(\psi)(t)

\displaystyle=f\left(\phi_{t}\right)-f\left(\psi_{t}\right)+\beta[\phi(t)-\psi(t)]

\displaystyle=f\left(\phi_{t}\right)-f\left(\psi_{t}\right)+\beta\left[\phi_{t}(0)-\psi_{t}(0)\right]

\displaystyle\geq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

In order to initiate our iterative scheme, a upper solution of (
5
) is needed, which is defined as follows:

Definition 2.2
.

A upper solution of (
5
) is a continuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
whose first derivative
\varphi^{\prime}
exists almost everywhere, is essentially bounded on
\mathbb{R}
, and satisfies the inequality

-\varphi^{\prime}(t)+f\left(\varphi_{t}\right)\leq 0,\quad\text{a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R}

A lower solution of (
5
) is a function defined similarly with the reversed inequality.

In the following results, in addition to assuming that
(H1)
and
(H2)
are satisfied, we also suppose that equation (
5
) has a upper solution
\bar{\varphi}\in\Gamma
and a lower solution
\underline{\varphi}
(not necessarily in
\Gamma
) satisfying:

(C1)

0\leq\underline{\varphi}(t)\leq\bar{\varphi}(t)\leq K,t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(C2)

\underline{\varphi}\not\equiv 0
.

(C3)

e^{\beta t}[\bar{\varphi}(t)-\underline{\varphi}(t)]
is non-decreasing for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The first iteration involves the following linear non-homogeneous ordinary differential equation

x_{1}^{\prime}(t)=-\beta x_{1}(t)+\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

(7)

Among all solutions of equation (
7
), we choose an appropriate solution. The definition and some properties of this particular solution are shown below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Define
x_{1}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

x_{1}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}\boldsymbol{H}(\bar{\varphi})(s)ds,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

(8)

Then

(i)

x_{1}
is a solution of (
7
).

(ii)

x_{1}\in\Gamma
.

(iii)

\underline{\varphi}(t)\leq x_{1}(t)\leq\bar{\varphi}(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) is immediate, since from (
8
) we have

e^{\beta t}x_{1}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\beta s}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(s)ds,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

Then,

e^{\beta t}x_{1}^{\prime}(t)+\beta e^{\beta t}x_{1}(t)=e^{\beta t}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

This proves that
x_{1}
satisfies equation (
7
). For (ii), we first prove that
x_{1}
satisfies the two limit conditions, that is

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}x_{1}(t)=0\text{ and }\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}x_{1}(t)=K.

Indeed, applying l’Hôpital’s rule to (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}x_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{e^{\beta t}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\beta s}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(s)ds

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)}{\beta}.

Since
\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)=f\left(\bar{\varphi}_{t}\right)+\beta\bar{\varphi}(t)
, using
(H1)
and the fact that
\bar{\varphi}\in\Gamma
, we get

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)=f(\widehat{0})=0\text{ and }\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)=f(\widehat{K})+\beta K=\beta K.

Consequently,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}x_{1}(t)=0\text{ and }\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}x_{1}(t)=K
.

Now we prove that
x_{1}
is non-decreasing in
\mathbb{R}
. Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
s>0
be given. Then

\displaystyle x_{1}(t+s)-x_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t+s}e^{-\beta(t+s-\theta)}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)d\theta-\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-\theta)}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-\theta)}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta+s)d\theta-\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-\theta)}\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-\theta)}[\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta+s)-\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)]d\theta

By
lemma

2.1
(ii), we have that
\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta+s)-\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)\geq 0
and therefore
x_{1}(t+

s)-x_{1}(t)\geq 0
. It remains to prove that given
s>0
,
e^{\beta t}\left[x_{1}(t+s)-x_{1}(t)\right]
is non-decreasing in
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle e^{\beta t}\left[x_{1}(t+s)-x_{1}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=e^{\beta t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-\theta)}[\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta+s)-\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)]d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\beta\theta}[\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta+s)-\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(\theta)]d\theta

Then, again by
lemma

2.1
(ii) we obtain that

\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{\beta t}\left[x_{1}(t+s)-x_{1}(t)\right]\right)=e^{\beta t}[\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t+s)-\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)]\geq 0

showing (ii).

To prove (iii), let
w(t)=\bar{\varphi}(t)-x_{1}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows from (
6
) and the fact that
\bar{\varphi}
is a upper solution of (
5
) that
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(t)\geq-\beta\bar{\varphi}(t)+\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
. Combining this with (
7
), we obtain

w^{\prime}(t)+\beta w(t)\geq 0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

Let
r(t)=w^{\prime}(t)+\beta w(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
and note that
r
is essentially bounded. From the fundamental theory of first-order linear ordinary differential equations, we get

w(t)=ce^{-\beta t}+\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}r(s)ds,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

where
c
is a constant. Now note that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}w(t)=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}x_{1}(t)-\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\bar{\varphi}(t)=0.

Therefore, we must have
c=0
. Thus, since
r(t)\geq 0,t\in\mathbb{R}
,

w(t)=\bar{\varphi}(t)-x_{1}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}r(s)ds\geq 0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

(9)

This proves that
x_{1}(t)\leq\bar{\varphi}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Similarly defining
v(t)=x_{1}(t)-\underline{\varphi}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
we have, by
lemma

2.1
(iii) and hypotheses (C1) and (C3), that

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{\prime}(t)-\underline{\varphi}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta x_{1}(t)+\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)+\beta\underline{\varphi}(t)-\mathbf{H}(\underline{\varphi})(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta v(t)+[\mathbf{H}(\bar{\varphi})(t)-\mathbf{H}(\underline{\varphi})(t)]

\displaystyle\geq-\beta v(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
h(t)=v^{\prime}(t)+\beta v(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
h(t)\geq 0,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
h
is essentially bounded. Thus,

v(t)=ce^{-\beta t}+\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}h(s)ds,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

Note that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}v(t)=0
, since
0\leq\underline{\varphi}(t)\leq\overline{\varphi}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This fact and the boundedness of
h
allows us to deduce that
c=0
must hold. Then,

v(t)=x_{1}(t)-\underline{\varphi}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}h(s)ds\geq 0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

(10)

This completes the proof.

∎

.

Let

be as in

(ii)

\displaystyle x_{1}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle x_{m}^{\prime}(t)

\underline{\varphi}(t)\leq x(t)\leq

\bar{\varphi}(t)

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
x(t)=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}x_{m}(t)

\lim_{t\to-\infty}x(t)=0\text{ and }\lim_{t\to+\infty}x(t)=K.

\displaystyle x(t)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}x_{m}(t)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(t)

Now from (P2) and the fact that

\lim\limits_{t\to-\infty}x(t)=0

Conclusions

In this paper we demonstrate the existence of monotone heteroclinic solutions for the Nicholson model with two distinct delays (
\sigma\neq r
) and linear harvesting term, addressing a scenario that has so far not been treated with monotone iteration techniques in the literature. By connecting the equilibria
0
and
\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{\delta+H}\right)
under the conditions
1<\frac{\rho}{\delta+H}\leq e
and
\sigma<r
, we partially answer the open problem proposed in
[
3
]
on global dynamics with multiple delays for the Nicholson model, via an adaptation of Wu and Zou’s method in
[
10
]
that included the explicit construction of new upper- and lower-solutions with their respective handling of the analytical complexities of having two different delays. Finally, the numerical simulations performed validate our results.

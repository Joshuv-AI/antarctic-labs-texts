Rare obstructive mixed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection with dual bidirectional drainage via a persistent left superior vena cava assessed prenatally with multimodal ultrasound: a case description
Authors: Lin Chen, Dan Liu, Ying-Kun Guo, Xing-Yuan Zhang
Published: 2026

Introduction

Mixed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC) accounts for only 5–10% of all TAPVC cases and 0.1–0.3% of congenital heart diseases. Its heterogeneous venous drainage easily induces early pulmonary venous obstruction (PVO), often requiring urgent neonatal surgery and leading to poor perinatal outcomes(1,2). Unlike classic multichannel mixed TAPVC, single common pulmonary venous trunk (CPVT) accompanied by bidirectional outflow is extremely rare, with only a few scattered reports describing this condition(3,4). PVO is the most powerful independent prognostic factor for TAPVC, and severe obstruction may cause fetal pulmonary congestion and cardiac decompensation(5).

Two-dimensional (2D) fetal echocardiography is the routine screening tool for TAPVC but cannot display complex three-dimensional (3D) vascular spatial relations. Although four-dimensional (4D) spatiotemporal image correlation combined with high-definition live flow (STIC-HD live flow) enables volumetric reconstruction, most of the published work on this modality lacks mention of standardized STIC acquisition quality control parameters, which are essential for reliable 3D reconstruction(6,7). In this case report, we present full quantitative sonographic indicators, propose a documented STIC acquisition protocol with quality assurance parameters, and describe a previously unreported mixed TAPVC anatomical variant.

Case report

A 36-year-old multiparous woman (G4P1) underwent fetal echocardiography at 23+3 weeks’ gestation due to suspected fetal cardiac malformation on routine scan. The pregnancy was uncomplicated, with no teratogen exposure, gestational complications, or family history of congenital heart disease. Imaging was performed on a Voluson E10 device (GE HealthCare, Chicago, IL, USA) with a 2 to 5-MHz volumetric convex probe, complying with International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology scanning guidelines and STIC quality standards (8). On the four-chamber view, the fetal left atrium (LA) was somewhat small, and there was no pulmonary venous inflow identified. A CPVT measuring 3.8 mm was visualized posterior to the LA (Figure 2A). The persistent left superior vena cava (PLSVC) had a diameter of 5.3 mm, which was 1.6 times that of the right superior vena cava (RSVC) (3.3 mm) (Figure 2B). Turbulent flow was detected throughout its lumen (Figure 2C).

The PLSVC drained caudally into the right atrium (RA) through a dilated coronary sinus (CS) (diameter 4.2 mm) (Figure 2D,2E). Bidirectional flow was present within the PLSVC: part of the blood flowed caudally into the RA via the CS, while the remaining portion drained cephalad into the dilated left innominate vein (LIV) (diameter 3.0 mm) (Figure 2F-2H and Video S1). The coronal view of the pulmonary venous system demonstrated that all four pulmonary veins converged into the single CPVT, which connected to the PLSVC through a vertical vein (VV) (Figure 2I,2J).

Severe focal stenosis at the VV-PLSVC junction yielded a peak velocity of 145 cm/s and a pressure gradient of 8.4 mmHg (Figure 2K). Additionally, all pulmonary veins exhibited reduced peak flow velocity (maximum velocity =8.9 cm/s) and blunted flow waveforms, consistent with diffuse pulmonary venous hemodynamic changes, which may reflect secondary remodeling from severe downstream obstruction (Figure 2L). Two independent drainage routes formed: a supracardiac pathway (PLSVC-LIV-RSVC-RA) and an intracardiac pathway (PLSVC-CS-RA).

STIC volumetric acquisition was conducted under standard parameters, specifically, a sweep angle of 25°–30°, an acquisition time of 7.5–10 s, and a frame rate of 18–22 fps. All volumes passed four international quality checks: no motion artifacts, complete anatomical display, uniform slice thickness, and undistorted Doppler signals. Postprocessing successfully reconstructed the full 3D drainage anatomy (Figure 2M and Video S2). STIC-HD live flow provided added value by being able to render the entire dual-drainage pathway in a single volume, clarifying spatial relationships that were difficult to reconstruct from sequential 2D slices.

After comprehensive prenatal genetic evaluation and detailed clinical counseling, the family opted to terminate the pregnancy. Postnatal autopsy was performed under parental written informed consent. The pathological findings confirmed all ultrasound-based diagnoses (Figure 3).

Postmortem gross pathological specimen verified the presence of a solitary CPVT with focal stenosis at the junction of the VV and PLSVC, along with an intact dual bidirectional drainage anatomy, showing full concordance with prenatal ultrasound findings. Discussion

This case represents a unique mixed TAPVC phenotype—namely solitary CPVT draining bidirectionally via the PLSVC concurrent with combined focal stenosis and diffuse pulmonary venous hemodynamic changes—a condition which has not been reported in the prenatal setting. The dual drainage pattern increases right heart volume overload and markedly elevates fetal risks.

Conventional 2D echocardiography serves as the routine first-line imaging tool for prenatal TAPVC screening; however, it fails to fully resolve the complex 3D spatial architecture of fetal cardiovascular structures. Accurate STIC reconstruction depends strictly on standardized volumetric acquisition and not on simple equipment usage. An appropriate sweep angle, acquisition time, and artifact exclusion avoids incomplete sampling and image distortion, which is critical for identifying rare dual-drainage malformations. STIC-HD live offers the ability to further render the entire dual-drainage pathway in a single volume, clarifying spatial relationships that may be difficult to reconstruct from sequential 2D slices. Early reports on STIC-related TAPVC or other congenital heart diseases lack descriptions of formal quality control, limiting cross-center reproducibility(6,7,9,10). Our report presents a fully documented STIC acquisition protocol with quality control parameters for complex fetal venous anomalies.

The pulmonary venous Doppler waveform pattern (reduced peak velocity and blunted waveform) is consistent with diffuse pulmonary venous hemodynamic changes. Although this may represent true diffuse pulmonary venous remodeling, it should be noted that chronic severe downstream focal stenosis can produce similar hemodynamic profiles (11). The measured hemodynamic indices (stenosis peak velocity and vessel diameter ratio) provide preliminary reference values for PVO assessment. These single-case measurements should be validated in larger cohorts before diagnostic or prognostic thresholds can be established.

From an anatomical perspective, our case represents a rare variant within the dual-drainage TAPVC spectrum. Arciprete et al. (12) and Lee et al. (13) described double connections in mixed TAPVC where two anatomically separate drainage channels were present. In contrast, our case exhibited bidirectional flow through a single PLSVC, representing a distinct hemodynamic arrangement. Tanabe et al. (14) reported a novel form of double-drainage TAPVC; however, their case did not exhibit the combined focal and diffuse PVO pattern observed in our case. More recently, Satsangi et al. (15) described a closely related anatomical configuration in which a CPVT drained into a PLSVC with bidirectional outflow through the CS and innominate vein—an arrangement strikingly similar to our case, but documented only postnatally. Our case represents the first prenatal demonstration of this rare anatomy with comprehensive quantitative Doppler and STIC assessment. Alanís-Naranjo et al. (16) recently summarized previously reported variants of dual-drainage TAPVC, providing a useful contemporary context for our case. Our findings expand the known phenotypic spectrum of this rare entity.

In summary, this report describes a previously undescribed variant of obstructive mixed TAPVC, expanding the known phenotypic spectrum. The complementary use of 2D echocardiography and STIC-HD live flow reconstruction may represent a promising diagnostic strategy for complex fetal venous anomalies; however, its generalizability requires validation in larger studies.

Spatiotemporal image correlation combined with high-definition live flow four-dimensional ultrasound reconstruction revealed the intricate three-dimensional spatial anatomy of the bidirectional venous drainage system. All four pulmonary veins converged to form the common pulmonary venous trunk, which coursed superiorly and leftward along the vertical vein before draining into the PLSVC. Two independent venous drainage circuits were fully visualized: a cephalad pathway via the superior PLSVC-LIV-RSVC-RA and a caudal pathway via the inferior PLSVC-CS-RA, illustrating the unique and complex vascular morphology of this rare congenital cardiovascular malformation.

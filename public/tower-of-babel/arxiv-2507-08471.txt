Most Fatou and Julia components are small for polynomials
Jinsong Zeng

Abstract
. We prove that Julia components of polynomials are generally small in diameter. For polynomials without irrationally neutral cycles, Fatou components are also typically small, even when the Julia set is not locally connected.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 37F45; Secondary: 37F10

1.
Introduction

We study the dynamics of polynomials
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
of degree
d\geq 2
. A well-known result regarding the Branner-Hubbard Conjecture states that non-trivial Julia components of polynomials are eventually periodic
[
KS
,
QY
]
. These countably many components (if any) are everywhere dense in the Julia set. We prove that most Julia components are small. Precisely,

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial. For any
\epsilon>0
, there are at most finitely many Julia components of
f
with diameters greater than
\epsilon
.

Theorem
1.1
is not true for rational maps. McMullen provided an example of a rational Julia set homeomorphic to a Cantor set of circles, containing uncountably many components with diameters bounded below by some
\epsilon_{0}>0

[
Mc
]
.

For polynomials, Roesch and Yin showed that every bounded Fatou component not eventually onto a Siegel disk is a Jordan domain
[
RY
]
. While Siegel disks, i.e., Fatou components on which the map is conjugate to irrational rotations, are conjectured to be Jordan domain. We focus on the asymptotic shrinking of non-Siegel Fatou components. The main result is that

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial without irrationally neutral cycles. For any
\epsilon>0
, there are at most finitely many Fatou components of
f
with diameters greater than
\epsilon
.

Kiwi
[
Ki
]
proved that for polynomials with connected Julia sets and no irrationally neutral cycles, local connectivity of the Julia set is equivalent to the absence of wandering non-trivial connected Julia subsets. Infinitely renormalizable polynomials with connected but non-locally connected Julia sets exist
[
M2
]
; Theorem
1.2
demonstrates that most Fatou components remain small despite such complexities.

The local connectivity of Julia sets is crucial in holomorphic dynamics. Partly because the dynamics on locally connected Julia sets can be accurately described via symbolic dynamics. It is a fruitful area of research on polynomials, see
[
M2
,
Pe
,
KL2
,
KS
,
Le
,
LG
,
Ya
,
CST
]
and others.

Roesch obtained the breakthrough result for rational maps in this area. She proved that except for some special cases, cubic Newton maps have locally connected Julia sets. This includes some maps that are infinitely renormalizable or contain Cremer points, unlike the case for polynomials
[
Roe
]
. These findings have been generalized to high degree Newton maps, as discussed in
[
DS
,
RYZ
]
. In
[
QWY
]
they showed that the Julia sets of McMullen maps are locally connected in many cases.

Recently, it has been proven in
[
WYZZ
,
FY
]
that the Julia sets of a number of rational maps with bounded type Siegel disks are locally connected. Their approach goes by analyzing the contraction property when pulling back certain disks near the boundaries of the bounded type Siegel disks.

Theorem
1.2
has significant applications in the study of local connectivity of rational Julia sets. Specifically, a renormalizable rational map usually contains copies of polynomial Fatou components. By Whyburn’s criterion, to establish the local connectivity, it is necessary to demonstrate that the majority of these components are small. As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, Julia sets of Newton maps without irrationally neutral cycles are locally connected. Compare
[
DS
, Corollary 1.2]
.

A key point in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, as stated in Corollary
2.7
, is the disjointness of bounded Fatou components and non-trivial
ends

E
in their boundaries. This guarantees the finiteness of hyperbolic diameters of Fatou components with respect to the domain
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
. Nevertheless, when
f
has irrationally neutral cycles, this property no longer holds.

In the case of a cubic Newton map
f
that is exactly once renormalizable and has a Cremer point, similar difficulties occur: the components of root basins, acting as “carrots”, intersect the quadratic Julia set copy at the iterated preimages of the
\beta
-fixed point in the boundary, resulting in each carrot having an infinite hyperbolic diameter. Intuitively, the shrinking of carrots depends on the dynamic behavior of the critical point in the Julia set copy. In the proof of
[
DS
, Corollaries 1.2 and 1.3]
the convergence of carrot diameter does not seem to be discussed. Generally, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial. Then there are at most finitely many Fatou components of
f
with diameters greater than
\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
.

There are many parallel phenomenons in parameter spaces. For instance, fix a strictly copy
\mathcal{M}_{0}
of the Mandelbrot set
\mathcal{M}
, components of
\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{0}
are referred to as
carrots
; each of them intersects
\mathcal{M}_{0}
at a dyadic tip of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
in its closure
[
DH2
,
PR
]
. Petersen-Roesch and Dudko independently established the shrinking of these carrots in size
[
Du
,
PR
]
.

Sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Let
\{X_{n}\}
be a sequence of the closure of bounded Fatou components that converges to a continuum
X_{\infty}
in the Hausdorff topology. Without loss of generality, we assume that the Julia set is connected and periodic Fatou components are fixed.

We begin by constructing the Branner-Hubbard-Yoccoz puzzles from invariant graphs, which are mainly composed of external rays landing at biaccessible fixed points and rays in bounded Fatou components. The infinite intersections of nested closed puzzle pieces decompose the entire Julia set into dynamical invariant ends, namely satellite and primitive ends. The limit set
X_{\infty}
must be contained in one of these ends.

We apply standard distortion lemmas for non-persistently recurrent critical ends and well-known results on the existence of complex bounds for persistently recurrent critical ends to show the triviality of wandering ends. Consequently, we can assume that
X_{\infty}
belongs to some periodic non-trivial end
e
.

We find two strictly nested (thickened) puzzle pieces
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
containing
e
and study the dynamics of
g=f^{k}:P_{1}\setminus\textbf{e}\to P_{0}\setminus\textbf{e}
restricted to the two annuli. A key point is that all
X_{n}
are disjoint from
e
under the assumption of the absence of irrationally neutral cycles. Therefore, the hyperbolic diameter of each
X_{n}
is finite. The weaker expansivity of
g
would imply the uniform boundedness of these hyperbolic diameters. As a result, the Euclidean diameter has to approach zero.

2.
Puzzles and ends

In this section, we will construct a specific Branner-Hubbard-Yoccoz puzzle for polynomials and discuss the nested properties of puzzles, as well as the dynamics around periodic ends.

As a start, we recall some classical facts from
[
DH1
]
; See also
[
M1
]
. Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
. The
filled Julia set
is defined as

K_{f}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:f^{n}(z)\textup{ is bounded}\},

whose boundary is the Julia set
J_{f}
. The
basin of infinity

B_{\infty}
is the complement of
K_{f}
. When
K_{f}
is connected, the polynomial
f
is conjugated to
z\mapsto z^{d}
on
B_{\infty}
. The conjugacy
\phi:B_{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}
tangent to the identity at infinity is called the
Böttcher coordinate
of
f
on
B_{\infty}
.

The
external ray
of argument
\theta
for
B_{\infty}
is defined as
R(\theta)=\phi^{-1}(]1,\infty[e^{2\pi i\theta})
. Every periodic external ray
lands, i.e.,
\lim_{r\to 1}\phi(re^{2\pi i\theta})
exists. The landing point is either repelling or parabolic. Conversely, a repelling or parabolic point receives at least one and a finite number of external rays, all of which are necessarily periodic of the same period. The preimage
\phi^{-1}(\{e^{r+2\pi i\theta}:0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\})
is called the
equipotential curve
of level
r>0
.

Associated to a periodic non-Siegel Fatou component, P. Roesch and Y. Yin provided a decomposition of the entire filled Julia set by limbs
[
RY
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
RY
, Theorems A and B]

Let
f
be a polynomial with connceted Julia set. Let
U
be a bounded periodic Fatou component of
f
that is either attracting or parabolic. Then
\partial U
is a Jordan curve. Moreover, the filled Julia set
K_{f}=\overline{U}\cup\bigsqcup_{t\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}L_{t}
with the following properties:

(1)

L_{t}
is compact and connected;

(2)

L_{t}\cap\overline{U}
is one point, denoted by
\gamma(t)
;

(3)
If
L_{t}\neq\gamma(t)
, two external rays land at
\gamma(t)
that separate
L_{t}
from
\overline{U}
; In this case,
L_{t}
is called a
limb
of
K_{f}
and
\gamma(t)
is called the
root
of the limb.

(4)
If
L_{t}\neq\gamma(t)
, then
L_{t}
is eventually bijectively mapped onto a limb that contains a critical point of
f
.

2.1. Puzzles

Throughout this section, we assume
f
is a polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
having connected Julia set
J_{f}
and without irrationally neutral cycles; By replacing
f
by certain iterate
f^{n}
, we adopt the following assumptions:

(A1) periodic Fatou components are fixed by
f
;

(A2) periodic roots are fixed by
f
; They are finitely many by Theorem
2.1
.

To create puzzles, we introduce a graph
\Gamma
that partitions
J_{f}
into pieces. Let
U
be a fixed bounded Fatou component.

When
U
is attracting, take a Jordan disk
D\subset U
containing the only fixed point in
U
such that
f(D)\Subset D
, and
\partial D
avoids the critical orbits. Then a component of
(f|_{U})^{-N}(D)
, for a large integer
N
, contains all critical points in
U
. This component, denoted by
D_{N}
, is a Jordan disk in
U
.

By a classic result from complex dynamics, there exists an arc
\gamma:[0,1)\to U\setminus f(D_{N})
such that
\gamma\subset f(\gamma)
and
\gamma
joins
\partial f(D_{N})
to a fixed point (i.e.,
\textup{lim}_{t\to 1}\gamma(t)
exists). We define

\Gamma_{U}=\partial D_{N}\cup\overline{(f|_{U})^{-1}(\gamma)},

where
(f|_{U})^{-1}(\gamma)
consists of exactly
\textup{deg}(f|_{U})
arcs in
U\setminus D_{N}
.

When
U
is parabolic, take an attracting petal
D\subset U
of the parabolic point in
\partial U
and ensure that
\partial D
avoids critical orbits in
U
. For a large
N
, a component of
(f|_{U})^{-N}(D)
covers all critical points in
U
, which is denoted by
D_{N}
. We define
\Gamma_{U}=\partial D_{N}
. In this case,
\Gamma_{U}
is a Jordan curve that intersects
\partial U
in a maximum of
\mathrm{deg}(f|_{U})^{N}
pre-parabolic points.

Let
E
be an equipotential curve around the filled Julia set
K_{f}
. We may define the graph as follows

(2.1)

\Gamma=E\cup\bigcup\Gamma_{U}\bigcup\overline{R},

where
R
runs over all external rays that land at
\bigcup\Gamma_{U}
or
biaccessible
fixed points. By a
biaccessible
point we mean a point receiving at least two external rays. Note that under Assumptions (A1) and (A2), periodic roots are biaccessible and contained within
\Gamma
, with external rays landing at them remaining fixed.

Definition 2.1
.

The
puzzle pieces
of
depth

n
are the connected components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-n}(\Gamma)
that intersect
J_{f}
for any
n\geq 0
.

By construction, puzzle pieces are Jordan domains, and two distinct puzzle pieces are either disjoint (that is,
P\cap Q=\emptyset
) or nested (that is,
P\subset Q
or
Q\subset P
).
In particular, when
P\subset Q
, then
P\Subset Q
if and only if
\partial P
and
\partial Q
have no common points in
J_{f}
, since rays do not cross.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P
be a puzzle piece of depth
\geq 1
, whose boundary contains a fixed point
z
, such that
P\subset f(P)
. Then there is a small open disk
\Delta\subset P
with
\partial\Delta
formed by

(1)
an arc
\alpha
in
\partial P
passing through
z
, and

(2)
another arc in
P
joining the two endpoints of
\alpha
,

such that
f(\partial\Delta)\cap\Delta=\emptyset
.

Proof.

The fixed point
z
is repelling or parabolic. All external rays landing at
z
are fixed and contained in
\Gamma
. If
z
is repelling, in a disk neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
z
with
f(\mathcal{N})\subset\mathcal{N}
, the graph
\Gamma\cap\mathcal{N}
is
f
-invariant in the sense that

\mathcal{N}\cap\Gamma\subset f(\mathcal{N}\cap\Gamma).

Let
\Delta
be the component of
\mathcal{N}\setminus\Gamma
in
P
whose boundary contains
z
. Then
\Delta
is as required.

If
z
is parabolic, let
\mathcal{N}
be a small neighborhood of
z
such that
\mathcal{N}\setminus\{z\}
is formed by repelling and attracting petals; See
[
M1
, Theorem 10.7]
. We may require that the petal in an immediate attracting basin
U
of
z
is bounded by
f^{n}(\Gamma\cap U)
for a large
n
.

Claim that the disk
\Delta=\mathcal{N}\cap P
is as required. Indeed, two immediate attracting basins of
z
, if they exist, are separated by a pair of fixed external rays landing at
z
. Thus
\Delta
belongs to one of the repelling petals of
\mathcal{N}
whose boundary contains the arc
\beta=\partial\Delta\cap P
. Then
f(\beta)\cap\Delta=\emptyset
. Let
\alpha=\partial\Delta\setminus\beta\subset\partial P
. Then
f(\alpha)
is contained in the boundary of the puzzle piece
f(P)
. Since
P\subset f(P)
, we have
f(\alpha)\cap P=\emptyset
. Thus
f(\alpha)\cap\Delta=\emptyset
. The proof is complete.
∎

A puzzle piece is always properly mapped onto another puzzle piece with one depth less through the map
f
. If the two puzzle pieces are nested, we can determine the degree by counting the number of fixed points.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
P
be a puzzle piece of depth
\geq 1
. If
P\subset f(P)
, then
\overline{P}
contains exactly
\textup{deg}(f|_{P})
distinct fixed points.

Proof.

The proof is based on the Lefschetz Fixed Point Theorem, which states that the degree of a weakly polynomial-like map, with isolated fixed points, is equal to the sum of Lefschetz indices at fixed points; See
[
GM
, Lemma 3.7]
.

Note that the map
g=f:\overline{P}\to f(\overline{P})
is a weakly polynomial-like map, with isolated fixed points; See
[
GM
, Definition 3.6]
. We claim that the Lefschetz index
\iota(g,z)
of each
g
-fixed point
z
equals
+1
. In fact, an interior fixed point in
P
cannot be parabolic, then its Lefschetz index is
+1
. In the case of a boundary fixed point
z\in\partial P
, then
z
is repelling or parabolic. In both cases, there is a degree one weakly polynomial-like map
f:\overline{\Delta}\to f(\overline{\Delta})
according to Lemma
2.2
. The Lefschetz Fixed Point Theorem implies that
\iota(f|_{\overline{\Delta}},z)=+1
. Since the Lefschetz index is local invariant, we have
\iota(g,z)=+1
as well.

Since all fixed points of
g
have Lefschetz indices
+1
, the number of fixed point equals
\textup{deg}(g)
by the Lefschetz Fixed Point Theorem. The proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

2.2. Ends

We now study the infinite intersections of nested closed puzzle pieces, which divide the whole Julia sets into dynamic invariant full continua, known as “ends”.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
Z
be the collection of fixed points in
\Gamma
(see (
2.1
) for
\Gamma
) and
Z_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}f^{-n}(Z)
. For
z\in Z_{\infty}
, we denote

Y_{n}(z)=\bigcup\overline{P},

where
P
runs over all depth-
n
puzzle pieces whose closures contain
z
. The
satellite end
of the point
z
is defined as

\textbf{e}(z)=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}Y_{n}(z).

If
z\in J_{f}\setminus Z_{\infty}
is not contained in any satellite end, its
primitive end
is defined as

\textbf{e}(z)=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\overline{P_{n}(z)},

where
P_{n}(z)
is the unique depth-
n
puzzle piece containing
z
. By an
end
we mean a satellite or primitive end.

Remarkably, the number of depth-
n
puzzles in
Y_{n}(z)
remains constant regardless of
n
, as long as
n
is sufficiently large. Moreover,
Y_{n}(z)
is a closed Jordan disk containing
z
in its interior, whenever
z\in Z
is repelling; If
z\in Z
is parabolic, then
z\in\partial Y_{n}(z)
for all
n\geq 0
.

From the construction of
\Gamma
, ends are
full continua
, i.e., compact, connected, and having only one complementary component, all of which are contained in the Julia set. Ends also possess other basic properties as stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

(1)
the map
f
sends satellite and primitive ends onto satellite and primitive ends, respectively.

(2)
Let
\textbf{e}(z)
be a primitive end and
n\geq 0
be an integer. Then there is a puzzle piece
P_{n^{\prime}}(z)
such that

P_{n^{\prime}}(z)\Subset P_{n}(z).

Consequently,
\textbf{e}(z)
is disjoint from the boundary of any puzzle piece; and if
\textbf{e}(z)\cap\textbf{e}(z^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
then
\textbf{e}(z)=\textbf{e}(z^{\prime})
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose
\textbf{e}(z)
is satellite with
z\in Z_{\infty}
. Then
Y_{n}(f(z))=f(Y_{n}(z))
for
n\geq 1
. Thus
\textbf{e}(f(z))=\bigcap f(Y_{n}(z))=f(\bigcap Y_{n}(z))=f(\textbf{e}(z))
, where the second equality follows from the fact that
Y_{n+1}(z)\subset Y_{n}(z)
. Similarly, in the case of a primitive, we have
\textbf{e}(f(z))=f(\textbf{e}(z))
.

(2) Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{l}
be the points in
\partial P_{n}(z)\cap J_{f}
. Then
\xi_{i}\in Z_{\infty}
. By definition,
z\notin\textbf{e}(\xi_{i})
. Thus
\xi_{i}\notin\partial P_{n_{i}}(z)
for some
n_{i}>n
; for otherwise,
z\in\textbf{e}(\xi_{i})
, a contradiction. Let
n^{\prime}=\textup{max}\{n_{i}\}
. It follows that
\partial P_{n^{\prime}}(z)\cap\partial P_{n}(z)\cap J_{f}=\emptyset
. Hence
P_{n^{\prime}}(z)\Subset P_{n}(z)
.

Assume by contradiction that
\textbf{e}(z)\neq\textbf{e}(z^{\prime})
. Then
P_{n}(z)\cap\textbf{e}(z^{\prime})=\emptyset
for some
n
. Thus
\textbf{e}(z)\subset\overline{P_{n^{\prime}}(z)}\subset P_{n}(z)
for a large
n^{\prime}>n
. We have
\textbf{e}(z)\cap\textbf{e}(z^{\prime})=\emptyset
, a contradiction.
∎

We observe that it is possible for two satellite ends to intersect, resulting in a non-trivial continuum.
For example, consider the tuning of the Douady rabbit polynomial using the “dendrite” map
g(z)=z^{2}+\textbf{i}
. The resulting map can be denoted by
f(z)=z^{2}+c_{0}
. Let
\xi
be the
\alpha
-fixed point of
f
. Then both satellite ends
\textbf{e}(\xi)
and
\textbf{e}(-\xi)
are formed by three copies of
J_{g}
. The intersection
\textbf{e}(\xi)\cap\textbf{e}(-\xi)
is exactly the copy of
J_{g}
containing zero.

2.3. Triviality of satellite ends

Most satellite ends are trivial under our assumption that
f
admits no irrationally neutral cycles.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
z\in Z
with each external ray landing at
z
being fixed. Then
\textbf{e}(z)=\{z\}
.

Proof.

Fix a puzzle piece
P_{0}
of depth-0 in
Y_{0}(z)
. It determines a sequence of depth-
n
puzzle pieces
P_{n}\subset Y_{n}(z)
such that
P_{n+1}\subset P_{n}\subset\cdots\subset P_{0}
. By condition, we have
f(P_{n+1})=P_{n}
. Let
K=\bigcap\overline{P_{n}}
. For the proof, it suffices to show that
K=\{z\}
.

We assume by contradiction that
K
contains more than one point. Note that the degree of
f:P_{n+1}\to P_{n}
eventually stabilizes at a number, say
\delta
, for all
n\geq N
. By Lemma
2.3
,
f
has exactly
\delta
distinct fixed points in
\overline{P_{N}}
. Therefore,
K
contains exactly
\delta
fixed points. From the choice of
\Gamma
, none of these fixed points is attracting. Thus, they are repelling or parabolic by the assumption that
f
admits no irrationally neutral cycles.

Let
\xi
be a fixed point in
K
. Claim that
f
fixes each external ray that lands at
\xi
. Indeed, it holds clearly if
\xi
receives just one external ray. In other cases,
\xi\in\partial P_{0}
. Let
\Delta\subset P_{0}
be the disk in Lemma
2.2
based at
\xi
. Then
\partial\Delta\cap\partial P_{0}
contains a segment
\alpha
from an external ray
R
that lands at
\xi
. Since
f(P_{1})=P_{0}
, we have
\alpha\subset f(\alpha)
and so
f(R)\subset R
. The claim is proved.

Consider a conformal map
h:\mathbb{C}\setminus K\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.
According to
[
M1
, Corollary 17.10]
,
\gamma=\overline{h(\partial P_{N}\setminus K)}
is a Jordan curve outside of
\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, the intersection

X=\gamma\cap\partial\mathbb{D}=\gamma\setminus{h(\partial P_{N}\setminus K)}

is a finite set.

Let
g:P^{\prime}_{N+1}\to P^{\prime}_{N}
defined by
g(w)=h\circ f\circ h^{-1}(w)
, where
P_{N}^{\prime}=h(P_{N}\setminus K)
. Using the Schwartz reflexion principle, the map
g
can be extended to
\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus X
and, by continuity, to an orientation preserving selfcovering
G:\partial\mathbb{D}\to\partial\mathbb{D}
. By counting preimages of a point where
h^{-1}
has radial limit, we have
\textup{deg}(G)=\delta
. Note that the image of a fixed ray landing at
K
under
h
is
g
-fixed and it then lands at a
G
-fixed point. Thus
G
has at least
\delta
fixed points by the above claim.

We now claim that fixed points of
G
are repelling. For otherwise, suppose
w\in\partial\mathbb{D}
is a one- or two-side attracting
G
-fixed point. In both cases, an attracting domain of
w
associate to
g
must intersect
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then, in the domain we take a point
w^{\prime}
close to
\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
g^{n}(w^{\prime})\in P_{N+1}^{\prime}
for all
n\geq 0
. Let
\widetilde{w}=h^{-1}(w^{\prime})
. It follows that
\widetilde{w}\notin K
and
f^{n}(\widetilde{w})\subset P_{N+1}
for all
n\geq 0
. This contradicts to the fact that
K
is exactly the non-escaping set

\{\xi\in\overline{P_{N+1}}:f^{n}(\xi)\in\overline{P_{N+1}}\textup{ for all }n\geq 0\}.

The claim is proved.

It is well known that a degree-
\delta
self-covering expansion on the circle is topologically conjugate to the map
x\mapsto x^{\delta}
on
\partial\mathbb{D}

[
Sh
]
. Therefore,
G
has
\delta-1
fixed points. This contradicts the previous count of
\delta
fixed points. Thus
K
is a singleton. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
z\in Z
with each external ray landing at
z
being fixed. Then for any
n\geq 0
, there is an integer
n^{\prime}>n
such that

(1)
if
z
is parabolic,
\partial Y_{n^{\prime}}(z)\cap\partial Y_{n}(z)=\{z\}
; Otherwise,

(2)

\partial Y_{n^{\prime}}(z)\cap\partial Y_{n}(z)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

(1) Note that
z\in\partial Y_{k}(z)
for any
k\geq 0
in this case. Since
\textbf{e}(z)=\{z\}
by Proposition
2.5
, there is an integer
n^{\prime}>n
such that
\partial Y_{n^{\prime}}(z)
is disjoint from the finite set
\partial Y_{n}(z)\cap J_{f}\setminus\{z\}
. Therefore,
\partial Y_{n^{\prime}}(z)\cap\partial Y_{n}(z)=\{z\}
.

(2) In this case,
Y_{n}(z)
contains
z
in its interior. Since
\textbf{e}(z)=\{z\}
by Proposition
2.5
, there is an integer
n^{\prime}>n
such that

3.
Wandering ends

The aim of this section is to prove that wandering ends are trivial. We will show that non-persistently recurrent ends satisfy the bounded degree condition, making them trivial according to Lemma
3.4
. While the triviality of persistently recurrent ends follows from the well-known result on the existence of complex bounds.

There are arguments in
[
WYZ
,
RYZ
]
concerning the triviality of ends, which are similar in spirit to our approach here. However, what sets this paper apart is the existence of non-trivial satellite ends, which brings about a significant distinction. See Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.4
.

Throughout this section we still assume that
f
is a polynomial having connected Julia set and without irrationally neutral cycles, satisfying Assumptions (A1) and (A2).

3.1. Distortion and shrinking lemmas

Let
A\subset\mathbb{C}
be an annulus with non-degenerated boundary components. Then there exists a confromal map sending
A
to a standard annulus
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0<r<|z|<1\}
, where
r>0
is uniquely determined by
A
. The
modulus
of
A
is defined as
\textup{mod}(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\textup{log}(1/r)
, which is invariant under conformal maps.

Consider a Jordan disk
U\subset\mathbb{C}
and a point
z\in U
. The
shape
of
U
about
z
is defined by

{\rm Shape}(U,z)=\frac{\sup_{x\in\partial U}|x-z|}{\inf_{x\in\partial U}|x-z|}.

It is clear that
{\rm Shape}(U,z)=1
if and only if
U
is a round
disk centered at the point
z
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
RYZ
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
U\Subset V\subset\mathbb{C}
be Jordan disks with
\textup{mod}(V\setminus\overline{U})\geq m>0
. Then there is a positive constant
C=C(m)

such that for any two points
x,y\in U
,

{\rm Shape}(V,y)\leq C\cdot{\rm Shape}(V,x).

The following lemma tells us that shapes by pullback via proper holomorphic maps can be controlled by some constants depending only on degrees and moduli.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
RY
, Lemma 4.7]

Let
U_{i}\Subset V_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, be Jordan disks with
\textup{mod}(V_{2}\setminus U_{2})\geq m>0
. Suppose that
g:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is
a proper holomorphic map of degree
\leq\delta
, and
U_{1}
be
a component of
g^{-1}(U_{2})
. Then, there is a positive constant
C=C(\delta,m)
such that for all
z\in U_{1}

{\rm Shape}(U_{1},z)\leq C\cdot{\rm Shape}(U_{2},g(z)).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E\subset J_{f}
be a full continuum. Let
U\Subset V
be Jordan disks such that
f^{n_{k}}(E)\subset U
with
n_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Suppose the maps
f^{n_{k}}:V_{k}\to V
have bounded degree
\delta
, where
V_{k}
are components of
f^{-n_{k}}(V)
containing
E
. Then
E
is a singleton.

Proof.

We assume by contradiction that
\textup{diam}(E)=\lambda>0
. Let
W
be a Jordan disk such that
U\Subset W\Subset V
. Let
W_{k}
be the component of
f^{-n_{k}}(W)
containing
E
. By Lemma
3.1
, there is a constant
C_{1}
depending on
\textup{mod}(W\setminus\overline{U})
such that for all
x,y\in U

\textup{Shape}(W,y)\leq C_{1}\cdot\textup{Shape}(W,x).

Fix a point
z\in E
. By Lemma
3.2
, there is a constant
C_{2}
such that

\textup{Shape}(W_{k},z)\leq C_{2}\cdot\textup{Shape}(W,f^{n_{k}}(z))

for all
k
.
Since
f^{n_{k}}(z)\in U
, it follows from the above two inequalities that
\textup{Shape}(W_{k},z)
are bounded by a constant
C
. Then we have

\textup{dist}(z,\partial W_{k})\geq\frac{1}{C}\cdot\frac{\textup{diam}(W_{k})}{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2C}=\epsilon_{0}.

Thus the standard Euclidean disk
\mathbb{D}(z,\epsilon_{0})\subset W_{k}
for all
k
. Then
f^{n_{k}}(\mathbb{D}(z,\epsilon_{0}))\subset W
is bounded. It contradicts the fact that
\mathbb{D}(z,\epsilon_{0})
contains points that escape to infinity.
∎

3.2. Bounded degree condition

An end
e
is called
periodic
if
f^{p}(\textbf{e})=\textbf{e}
for some positive integer
p
; and is called
wandering
if
f^{n}(\textbf{e})\cap f^{m}(\textbf{e})=\emptyset
for any
n\neq m\geq 0
. By definition, wandering ends are primitive.

Definition 3.1
.

A wandering end
e
is said to satisfy the
bounded degree condition
, if there is a puzzle piece
V
and integers
n_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
such that

\textup{deg}(f^{n_{k}}:V_{k}\to V)\leq\delta

for all
n_{k}
, where
V_{k}
are components of
f^{-n_{k}}(V)
containing
e
and
\delta
is a constant.

Lemma 3.4
.

A wandering end
e
satisfying the bounded degree condition is trivial.

Proof.

We may assume the puzzle piece
V
contains infinitely many ends
f^{n_{k}}(\textbf{e})
, and the degree of
f^{n_{k}}:V_{k}\to V
is bounded by
\delta
. If there is a puzzle piece
U\Subset V
such that
\#\{k:f^{n_{k}}(\textbf{e})\subset U\}=\infty
, we are done by Lemma
3.3
.

We assume a point
\xi\in\partial V\cap J_{f}\subset Z_{\infty}
satisfies that, for each sufficiently large
n
, the depth-
n
puzzle piece
P_{n}\subset Y_{n}(\xi)\cap V
contains infinitely many ends from
\{f^{n_{k}}(\textbf{e})\}
. By taking an iterate of
f
on a high depth puzzle piece in
V
, whose boundary contains
\xi
, we assume further that
\xi
is fixed and
V\subset Y_{0}(\xi)
is a puzzle piece of depth zero.

Let
N_{0}
be the integer in Lemmas
2.8
or
2.11
. Then by passing to a subsequence of
\{f^{n_{k}}(\textbf{e})\}
, there are integers
m_{k}
and two Jordan disks
U^{\prime}\Subset V^{\prime}
such that
f^{m_{k}+n_{k}}(\textbf{e})\subset U^{\prime}
and the maps
f^{m_{k}}:V^{\prime}_{k}\to V^{\prime}
are conformal. Here
V_{k}^{\prime}\subset V
are the components of
f^{-m_{k}}(V^{\prime})
containing
f^{n_{k}}(\textbf{e})
. Let
V_{k}^{\prime\prime}
be the components of
f^{-n_{k}}(V_{k}^{\prime})
containing
e
. Then the compositions
f^{m_{k}+n_{k}}:V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\to V^{\prime}
have bounded degree
\delta
as
V_{k}^{\prime}\subset V
. By Lemma
3.3
again,
e
is a singleton. The proof is complete.
∎

3.3. Combinatorial accumulation ends

Definition 3.2
.

Let
e
be a wandering end. The orbit of

is denoted by
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})=\{\textbf{e},f(\textbf{e}),\ldots\}
. An end
\textbf{\emph{w}}(z)
is called a

of the orbit if for any integer

, the puzzle piece

(or

, in the satellite case) contains infinitely many elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
. The collection of such end
\textbf{\emph{w}}(z)
is denoted by

.

By definition, an end
\textbf{{w}}(z)\notin\omega(\textbf{e})
if and only if

or

contains at most finitely many elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
for any integer

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be a wandering end.

(1)
If

contains a primitive end cycle
\textbf{\emph{w}}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{\emph{w}}_{p}
, then there is a puzzle piece

disjoint from the cycle such that

\#\{k:f^{k}(\textbf{e})\subset P\}=\infty.

(2)
If
\omega(\textbf{e})
contains a fixed satellite end
\textbf{e}(z),z\in Z
, then there is a puzzle piece
P
whose boundary is disjoint from
z
such that

\#\{k:f^{k}(\textbf{e})\subset P\}=\infty.

Proof.

(1) Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the union of depth-
n
puzzle pieces that contain one of
\textbf{{w}}_{i}
.
If the statement is not true, only finitely many elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
lie outside

for each

. Thus, there are integers
n_{0}
and
q
such that
\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}
contains all elements of
\textup{orb}(f^{q}(\textbf{e}))
. We may assume

is sufficiently large such that
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is formed by
p
disjoint depth-
n
puzzle pieces and the degree of

is a constant for each

. We claim that

(3.1)

\bigcap\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}:f^{k}(\xi)\in\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}\textup{ for all }k\geq 0\}=:E.

Indeed, let
\xi\in E
and
\xi_{k}=f^{k}(\xi)
. Fix an integer
k\geq 1
. Assume
\xi_{k}\in P_{n_{0}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}
. Then the preimage
(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}})^{-1}(P_{n_{0}})
has just one component in
\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}
, which is a puzzle piece of depth
n_{0}+1
in
\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}+1}
. Since
\xi_{k-1}\in\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}
, we have
\xi_{k-1}\in\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}+1}
. By successively processing the discussion on
\xi_{k-1},\ldots,\xi_{1}
, we conclude that
\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}+k}
. Thus
\xi\in\bigcap\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

It follows from the claim that

is contained in
\bigcap\mathcal{P}_{n}
, which is the union of
\textbf{{w}}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{{w}}_{p}
. This contradicts the condition that
e
is wandering.

(2) The proof is similar as above. In this case, let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the union of depth-
n
puzzle pieces whose boundaries contain
z
. If the statement is not true, then elements of
\textup{orb}(f^{q}(\textbf{e}))
are contained in
\mathcal{P}_{n_{0}}
for some integers
n_{0}
and
q
. Assume
n_{0}
is large enough such that the degree of
f:P_{n}\to P_{n-1}
stabilizes for each

, where
P_{k}
are the depth-
k
puzzle pieces in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
. Based on the same discussion as above, the claim (
3.1
) holds as well in this case. Therefore,
f^{q}(\textbf{e})
belongs to
\bigcap\mathcal{P}_{n}
, which is a subset of
\textbf{e}(z)
. Once again, this contradicts the fact that
e
is wandering.
∎

We shall prove that non-persistently recurrent ends have bounded degree condition; See Lemmas

,
3.8
,
3.9
and

Definition 3.3
.

Let

of

into
P
is the minimal positive integer
k
such that
f^{k}(\textbf{e})\subset P
. We denote by
L_{\textbf{e}}(P)
the unique puzzle piece containing
e
such that
f^{r_{\textbf{e}}(P)}(L_{\textbf{e}}(P))=P
.

We denote by
\kappa
the number of critical points in
{J}_{f}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
e
be a wandering end. Let
P
be a puzzle piece such that the first entry time
r=r_{\textbf{e}}(P)
exists. Then the degree of
f^{r}:L_{\textbf{e}}(P)\to P
is bounded by
d^{\kappa}
.

Proof.

Let
Q_{k}:=f^{k}(L_{\textbf{e}}(P)),0\leq k\leq r
. Claim that the
r
puzzle pieces
Q_{0},\cdots Q_{r-1}
are pairwise disjoint. For otherwise, assume
Q_{r_{1}}\cap Q_{r_{2}}\neq\emptyset
with
r_{1}<r_{2}
. Then
f^{r_{1}}(\textbf{e})\in Q_{r_{1}}\subset Q_{r_{2}}
. We pull back
Q_{r_{2}}
by
f^{r_{1}}
at
e
, and then obtain a puzzle piece
W
containing
e
whose depth is strictly less than that of
Q_{0}
. Therefore,
f^{r_{1}+r-r_{2}}(W)=P
with
r_{1}+r-r_{2}<r_{\textbf{e}}(P)
. It is a contradiction.

By the claim, each critical point of
f
appears at most once in the orbit
Q_{0},\cdots,Q_{r-1}
. Thus, the degree of
f^{r}:L_{\textbf{e}}(P)\to P
is bounded by
d^{\kappa}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
e
be a wandering end. If
\omega(\textbf{e})
contains a preperiodic end, then
e
satisfies the bounded degree condition.

Proof.

Since
f(\omega(\textbf{e}))\subset\omega(\textbf{e})
by definition, we may assume
\textbf{{w}}\in\omega(\textbf{e})
is periodic. We first consider the case that
w
is primitive. By Lemma
3.5
(1), there is a puzzle piece
U
, disjoint from elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{{w}})
, such that
\#\{k:f^{k}(\textbf{e})\subset U\}=\infty
.

Let
P_{n}
be the depth-
n
puzzle piece containing
w
. Then
P_{n}
contains at least an element of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
. Let
r_{n}
be the first entry time of
e
into
P_{n}
. Since
\textbf{{w}}\notin\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
, it implies that
r_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. The end
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})
will eventually run into the puzzle piece
U
. The first entry time of
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})
into
U
is denoted by
s_{n}
. Let
Q_{n}=L_{f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})}(U)
. Since
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})\subset Q_{n}\cap P_{n}
, we have
Q_{n}\subset P_{n}
or
P_{n}\subset Q_{n}
. The latter case cannot happen, for otherwise
f^{s_{n}}(\textbf{{w}})\subset f^{s_{n}}(P_{n})\subset f^{s_{n}}(Q_{n})=U
, contradicting the assumption on
U
. By Lemma
3.6
, both maps

(3.2)

f^{r_{n}}:L_{\textbf{e}}(P_{n})\to P_{n}\textup{ and }f^{s_{n}}:Q_{n}\to U

have bounded degree
d^{\kappa}
. Their composition
f^{r_{n}+s_{n}}:Q_{n}^{\prime}\to U
has degree less than
d^{2\kappa}
, where
Q_{n}^{\prime}\subset L_{\textbf{e}}(P_{n})
is the component of
f^{-r_{n}}(Q_{n})
containing
e
.

In the case that
w
is satellite, we assume
\textbf{{w}}=\textbf{e}(z),z\in Z
. Let
U
be the puzzle piece in Lemma
3.5
(2) satisfying that
z\notin\overline{U}
and
\#\{k:f^{k}(\textbf{e})\subset U\}=\infty
. For any integer
n
, there is a depth-
n
puzzle piece
P_{n}\subset Y_{n}(z)
containing
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})
with
r_{n}
the first entry time of
e
into
P_{n}
. It is clear that
r_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. By the same process as above, we obtain an integer
s_{n}
and a puzzle piece
Q_{n}
that contains
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})
. Then either
Q_{n}\subset P_{n}
or
P_{n}\subset Q_{n}
. Again, the latter case implies that
z=f^{s_{n}}(z)\in f^{s_{n}}(\overline{P_{n}})\subset f^{s_{n}}(\overline{Q_{n}})=\overline{U}
, impossible. Therefore, by substituting
P_{n}
with
Q_{n}
, the composition of the two maps in (
3.2
) has bounded degree
d^{2\kappa}
as well. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
e
be a wandering end. If there is a wandering end
\textbf{\emph{w}}\in\omega(\textbf{e})
such that
\omega(\textbf{\emph{w}})\neq\omega(\textbf{e})
, then
e
satisfies the bounded degree condition.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
we may assume ends in
\omega(\textbf{e})
are wandering. Since wandering ends are primitive, it follows from Lemma
2.4
(2) and the definition that
\omega(\textbf{{w}})\subset\omega(\textbf{e})
.

We take a wandering end
\textbf{{w}}^{\prime}\in\omega(\textbf{e})\setminus\omega(\textbf{{w}})
. This implies that there is a puzzle piece
U
, containing
\textbf{{w}}^{\prime}
, of sufficiently high depth, such that

U
is disjoint from elements in
\textup{orb}(\textbf{{w}})
but contains infinitely many elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
.

For any depth-
n
puzzle piece
P_{n}
containing
w
, let
r_{n}
be the first entry time of
e
into
P_{n}
. Since
\omega(\textbf{e})=\omega(f^{k}(\textbf{e}))
for any
k\geq 1
, we have
\textbf{{w}}\notin\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
. Thus
r_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
It follows from the choice of
U
that
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})
eventually runs into
U
; the first entry time
s_{n}
exists.

Let
Q_{n}=L_{f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})}(U)
. Since
f^{r_{n}}(\textbf{e})\subset Q_{n}\cap P_{n}
, either
Q_{n}\subset P_{n}
or
P_{n}\subset Q_{n}
. The latter case implies that
\textbf{{w}}\subset Q_{n}
, and then
f^{s_{n}}(\textbf{{w}})\subset f^{s_{n}}(Q_{n})=U
, a contradiction.

Let
Q_{n}^{\prime}\subset L_{\textbf{e}}(P_{n})
be the component of
f^{-r_{n}}(Q_{n})
containing

. By Lemma
3.6
, the composition of the two maps
f^{r_{n}}:Q_{n}^{\prime}\to Q_{n}\subset P_{n}
and
f^{s_{n}}:Q_{n}\to U
has bounded degree

. The proof is complete.
∎

An end is called
critical
if it contains a critical point of
f
. The collection of critical ends in
\omega(\textbf{e})
is denoted by
\omega\textup{Crit}(\textbf{e})
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
e
be a wandering end. If
\omega\textup{Crit}(\textbf{e})=\emptyset
, then

e
satisfies the bounded degree condition.

Proof.

We may assume
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
contains no critical ends. Let
\textbf{{w}}_{1},\ldots\textbf{{w}}_{m}
be the critical ends of
f
. If
\textbf{{w}}_{i}
is primitive, there is an integer
n_{i}
such that the depth-
n
puzzle piece
P_{n}
containing
\textbf{{w}}_{i}
is disjoint from elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
for all
n\geq n_{i}
. If
\textbf{{w}}_{i}=\textbf{e}(z_{i})
is satellite with
z_{i}\in Z_{\infty}
, then
Y_{n}(z_{i})
is disjoint from elements of
\textup{orb}(\textbf{e})
for all
n\geq n_{i}
.

Let
N=\textup{max}\{n_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
. Take a puzzle piece
U
of depth

such that
f^{n_{k}}(\textbf{e})\subset U
with
n_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Then the map
f^{n_{k}}:Q_{k}\to U
is conformal, where
Q_{k}
is the component of
f^{-n_{k}}(U)
containing

. Indeed,
f^{i}(Q_{k})
is a puzzle piece of depth
N+n_{i}-i
containing
f^{i}(\textbf{e})
for

; thus it is disjoint from critical ends by the choice of

. Therefore,
e
satisfies the bounded degree condition.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
e
be a wandering critical end. Let
P_{n}
be the depth-
n
puzzle piece containing
e
. Then
P_{n+k}
is called a
successor
of
P_{n}
if
f^{k}(P_{n+k})=P_{n}
and the itinerary
P_{n+k},f(P_{n+k}),\ldots,f^{k}(P_{n+k})=P_{n}

meets each critical end of
f
at most twice.

By definition, the degree of
f^{k}:P_{n+k}\to P_{n}
is bounded by
d^{2\kappa-1}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
e
be a wandering end. If there is a critical end
\textbf{{w}}\in\omega\textup{Crit}(\textbf{e})
and an integer
n_{0}
such that
P_{n_{0}}
, containing
w
, has infinitely many successors, then

satisfies the bounded degree condition.

Proof.

Let
P_{n_{0}+n_{k}}
be the successors of
P_{n_{0}}
with
n_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. By definition, the degrees of maps

f^{n_{k}}:P_{n_{0}+n_{k}}\to P_{n_{0}}

are bounded by
d^{2\kappa-1}
. Let
r_{k}
be the first entry time of

into

and

be the component of

∎

A wandering end

is called

if it satisfies that

\omega\textup{Crit}(\textbf{e})\neq\emptyset
;

(2) any end
\textbf{{w}}\in\omega(\textbf{e})
is wandering with
\omega(\textbf{{w}})=\omega(\textbf{e})
;

(3) any depth-
n
puzzle piece

, containing a critical end of

The collection of persistently recurrent ends is denoted by

. Combining Lemmas

,
3.7
,
3.8
,
3.9

, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.11
.

Any wandering end not contained in
\mathcal{E}
is trivial.

We do not know whether a persistently recurrent end

satisfies the bound degree condition. However, there is a sequence of principal nested puzzle pieces

containing

such that

\textup{mod}(P_{n_{k+1}}\setminus\overline{P_{n_{k}}})\geq\mu,

for all

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
J_{\infty}
be the Hausdorff limit of a sequence of distinct Julia components
J_{n}
. Assume by contradiction that
J_{\infty}
is not a singleton. Clearly
J_{\infty}\subset J_{f}
and
J_{\infty}
is contained in a component
K
of the filled Julia set. Then by
[
QY
, Main Theorem]
,
K
is eventually periodic. By considering some iterate of
J_{n}
and
J_{\infty}
, we may assume that
f^{p}(K)=K
.

Take a disk
D
bounded by an equipotential curve of the Green’s function of the basin of infinity, such that
K\subset D
and
D\cap\textup{crit}(f)=K\cap\textup{crit}(f)
. Let
D^{\prime}
be the component of
f^{-p}(D)
containing
K
. Then
\overline{D^{\prime}}\subset D
.

Claim that there is an arc
\gamma
in the annulus
H=D\setminus\overline{D^{\prime}}
joining
\partial D
and
\partial D^{\prime}
such that it is disjoint from the filled Julia
K_{f}
. Indeed, since
K_{f}\cap H
is compact, for each sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, it is covered by finitely many open Euclidean round disks in
H
whose centers are in
K_{f}
and diameters equal
\epsilon
. Let
E_{\epsilon}
be the closure of the union of these disks. Then
K_{f}\cap H=\bigcap_{\epsilon}E_{\epsilon}
. Since
\partial H
can not be separated by components of
K_{f}
, for a small
\epsilon_{0}
the set
E_{\epsilon_{0}}
does not separate
\partial H
. Then one can find an arc
\gamma\subset H
avoiding the finitely many components of
E_{\epsilon_{0}}
joining
\partial D
and
\partial D^{\prime}
.

Consider the covering
g=f^{p}:D^{\prime}\setminus K\to D\setminus K
whose degree is
k\geq 2
. Then
g
is expanding with respect to the hyperbolic metric
\rho
on
D\setminus K
. Precisely, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leq\rho(g(z),g(z^{\prime}))

whenever
\rho(z,z^{\prime})<\delta
.
Let
D^{\prime\prime}=g^{-1}(D^{\prime})
. The arcs in
g^{-1}(\gamma)
cut the annulus
D^{\prime}\setminus\overline{D^{\prime\prime}}
into
k
Jordan domains
W_{i}
. Let
M
be the maximal
\rho
-diameters among these domains. Note that
g^{n}
sends any component of
g^{-n}(W_{i})
homeomorphically onto
W_{i}
by the Riemann-Hurwitz formula. It follows from the expansion of
g
that
\rho\textup{-diameter}(W)\leq M,
where
W
runs over all components of
g^{-n}(W_{i})
for all
n\geq 0
and
1\leq i\leq k
.

All
\{J_{n}\}
except finitely many ones are contained in
D^{\prime\prime}
. Suppose
J_{n}\subset D^{\prime\prime}
. Then
J_{n}
is eventually iterated into one of
W_{i}
under
g
. It follows that
J_{n}
is contained in a component the iterated preimage of some
W_{i}
. Thus, we have
\rho\textup{-diameter}(J_{n})\leq M
.

Since
\{J_{n}\}
converges to
\partial K
, the Euclidean diameters of
J_{n}
tend to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Then
J_{\infty}
must be a singleton, a contradiction. The proof is complete. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

According to Theorem
1.1
. We may assume
J_{f}
is connected and
f
satisfies Assumptions (A1) and (A2) in Subsection
2.1
. Let
\{\Omega_{i}\}
be a sequence of distinct bounded Fatou components. Suppose
\overline{\Omega_{i}}
Hausdorff converges to a limit
K
. Then
K\subset J_{f}
. For the proof, it suffices to show that
K
is a singleton. We argue by contradiction and assume it is not true.

Let
z\in K\setminus Z_{\infty}
and
P_{n}
be the depth-
n
puzzle piece containing
z
. Denote by
E=\bigcap\overline{P_{n}}
. We claim that
K\subset E
. Indeed, there are at most finitely many Fatou components whose boundaries intersect
\partial P_{n}
. Thus all
\Omega_{i}
except finitely many ones are contained in
P_{n}
. This implies that
K\subset E
.

By definition,
E
is contained in an end
e
, which is satellite or primitive. It follows from Corollary
3.11
and Theorem
3.12
that
e
can not be wandering. Thus, by applying an iterate of
f
, we may assume that
e
is periodic.

We first consider the case that
e
is primitive. In this case,
\textbf{e}=E
. By Lemma
2.4
(2), take two puzzle pieces such that
P_{N}\Subset P_{N^{\prime}}
and

f^{N-N^{\prime}}(P_{N})=P_{N^{\prime}}
. Assuming that
N^{\prime}
is sufficiently large, we can consider the map

g=f^{N-N^{\prime}}:P_{N}\setminus\textbf{e}\to P_{N^{\prime}}\setminus\textbf{e}

which acts as a covering between the two annuli.
Let
\mathcal{K}
be the collection of depth-
N
puzzle pieces in the annuli
A=P_{N^{\prime}}\setminus\overline{P_{N}}
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be the collection of Fatou components in
P_{N}
whose closures intersect either
\partial P_{N}
or some
\partial P
with
P\in\mathcal{K}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{U}
has finitely many elements.

Each
\overline{\Omega_{i}}\subset P_{N}
under an iterate of
g
will either run into the annulus
A=P_{N^{\prime}}\setminus\overline{P_{N}}
or intersect with
\partial P_{N}
. The first time that
\overline{\Omega_{i}}
meets

is denoted by
r_{i}
. Then

for some

. By the Riemann-Hurwitz formula,

is conformal, where

is the component of
g^{-r_{i}}(Q_{i})
containing
\Omega_{i}
.

Note that boundaries of bounded Fatou components are disjoint from
e
by Corollary
2.7
. Thus, the maximum
\rho
-diameters of elements in

are bounded by some constant
C
, where

is the hyperbolic metric on

with

\overline{P_{N^{\prime}}}
.

Since
g
is expanding with respect to the metric
\rho
, it follows that
\rho\textup{-diameter}(\overline{\Omega_{i}})\leq C
for all
\overline{\Omega_{i}}\subset P_{N}
. This implies that the Euclidean diameters of
\overline{\Omega_{i}}
tend to zero as

. Therefore,

is a singleton, contradicting our assumption.

We are reduced to the case that
e
is satellite. The approach is similar. Assume
\textbf{e}=\textbf{e}(\xi)
with
\xi\in Z
. By Proposition
2.5
,
\xi\in E
is repelling and
f^{p}(E)=E
with

. By Lemma
2.10
we obtain two thicken puzzle pieces
\widehat{P}_{N}\Subset\widehat{P}_{N^{\prime}}
containing
E
in their interiors such that

g=f^{p(N-N^{\prime})}:\widehat{P}_{N}\setminus E\to\widehat{P}_{N^{\prime}}\setminus E

is covering between the two annuli. In this case, the annulus

is divided by
\partial P^{*}_{N^{\prime}}
into finitely many quadrilaterals, whose collection is denoted by
\mathcal{K}
. By construction, only finitely many Fatou components whose closures intersect

. The collection of them is denoted by

.

For each

, the first entrance

or intersects some

, are formed by rays in the Fatou set, we have

with respect to the hyperbolic metric

on

, we conclude that
\rho
-diameter(

. Thus, the Euclidean diameters of

tend to zero as

. This contradicts the assumption that

is not a singleton. The proof is complete.
∎

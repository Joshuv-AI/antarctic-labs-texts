1.
Introduction

Let
f(x)
be an irreducible polynomial with rational coefficients. An interesting problem is to characterize the Galois group of
f(x)
using its coefficients. A classical example is that if
f(x)=x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
, then its Galois group can be determined by testing whether
b
and
b(a^{2}-4b)
are rational squares
[
12
]
.

In recent years much attention has been devoted to
power compositional polynomials
, that is, irreducible polynomials of the form
g(x^{m})
for some monic
g(x)\in\Q[x]
and positive integer
m\geq 2
. If
g(x)=x^{2}+ax+b
, there are complete characterizations for the cases where
m=2
and
m=3
given by
[
12
]
and
[
2
]
, respectively. There are also partial results when
m=4

[
1
,
9
]
, eventually leading to a complete characterization in
[
6
]
. Some other power compositional polynomials, namely
x^{6}+ax^{4}+bx^{2}+c

[
3
]
,
x^{8}+ax^{6}+bx^{4}+ax^{2}+1

[
5
]
, and
x^{9}+ax^{6}+bx^{3}+c

[
4
]
have been completely characterized too.

In this paper, we will prove a complete characterization for the Galois group
G_{12}
of
f(x)=x^{12}+ax^{6}+b
(Theorem
3.8
), motivated by the observation that
f(x)
can be expressed as a power compositional polynomial in two different ways. In particular, we have
f(x)=g_{4}(x^{3})=g_{6}(x^{2})
, where
g_{4}(x)=x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
and
g_{6}(x)=x^{6}+ax^{3}+b
, respectively. To the best of our knowledge, this complete classification has not yet appeared in the literature in such a streamlined form.

We highlight that this classification is valuable on its own, as seen by its application in the recent work
[
11
]
, which used it to completely classify polynomials of the form
x^{12}+ax^{6}+b
which are monogenic. We also view this classification as a stepping stone towards a more systematic study of
x^{2m}+ax^{m}+b
for composite
m
; see also
[
10
]
for a similar analysis for prime
m
.

Preliminaries

We begin by recalling some important results on linear resolvents.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
f(x)\in\Q[x]
be an irreducible polynomial of degree
n
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
be all the roots of
f(x)
. Let
F(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\Q[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and let

H=\{\sigma\in S_{n}:F(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})=F(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\}.

Then the resolvent polynomial of
f(x)
corresponding to
F
is

R(x):=\prod_{\sigma\in S_{n}//H}\left(x-F(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})\right)\in\Q[x]

where
S_{n}//H
is a complete set of right coset representatives of
H
in
S_{n}
. The irreducible factors of
R(x)
that occur with multiplicity one correspond to the orbits of the action of
Gal(f)
on the cosets in
S_{n}/H
, and the Galois group for any of these irreducible factors is the image of the permutation representation of this action on its corresponding coset.

The linear resolvent corresponding to
F=x_{1}+x_{2}
can be computed using resultants in Mathematica
[
16
]
as the polynomial
R(x)
satisfying the relation

(2.1)

R(x)^{2}=\frac{\Res_{y}(f(y),f(x-y))}{2^{n}\cdot f(x/2)}.

Likewise, the linear resolvent corresponding to
F=x_{1}x_{2}
can also be determined similarly by the relation

(2.2)

R(x)^{2}=\frac{\Res_{y}(f(y),y^{n}f(x/y))}{\Res_{y}(f(y),x-y^{2})}.

For both cases, the list of degrees of irreducible factors of the linear resolvent
R(x)
is equivalent to the list of orbit lengths for the action of
\Gal(f)
on all 2-sets of
n
letters, which can be computed in GAP
[
15
]
.

We now state a result on the irreducibility of composition of polynomials, which we will later specialize with
g(x)=x^{m}
to determine the factorization patterns of power compositional polynomials we are interested in.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
13
, Section 2.1]
)
.

Let
f(x),g(x)\in\Q[x]
where
f(x)
is irreducible, and let
\alpha
be a root of
f(x)
. Then
f(g(x))
is reducible over
\Q
if and only if
g(x)-\alpha
is reducible over
\Q(\alpha)
. Furthermore, if

\displaystyle g(x)-\alpha=c_{1}u_{1}(x)^{e_{1}}\cdots u_{k}(x)^{e_{k}}\in\Q(\alpha)[x]

where
u_{1}(x),\dots,u_{k}(x)
are distinct monic polynomials irreducible over
\Q(\alpha)
, then

\displaystyle f(g(x))=c_{2}\N(u_{1}(x))^{e_{1}}\cdots\N(u_{k}(x))^{e_{k}}\in\Q[x]

where the norms
\N(u_{1}(x)),\dots,\N(u_{k}(x))
are distinct monic polynomials irreducible over
\Q
.

We now recall some results on Galois groups. We identify Galois groups as transitive permutation groups up to conjugacy. Let
f(x)=g(x^{m})
be an irreducible power compositional polynomial,

and

be stem fields of

and

and

be subgroups of

is the point stabilizer of 1 under

and the splitting field of

commonly referred to the

[

]
. In particular, this list must contain

. We also recall that

if and only if

.

Lastly, we recall known characterizations of

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f(x)=x^{12}+ax^{6}+b\in\Q[x]
be an irreducible polynomial. Then

(1)

\disc(f)\in\Q^{2}
if and only if
G_{4}
is
4T2
.

(2)

|\Gal(f)|\leq\min\{18|G_{4}|,4|G_{6}|\}
.

Proof.

(1)
Note that

\displaystyle\disc(f)=2^{12}3^{12}b^{5}(a^{2}-4b)^{6}=b\big(2^{6}3^{6}b^{2}(a^{2}-4b)^{3}\big)^{2},

so
\disc(f)\in\Q^{2}
if and only if
b\in\Q^{2}
if and only if
G_{4}
is
4T2
by Proposition
2.3
.

(2)
Let
\pm\alpha,\pm\beta
be the roots of
x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
and
\omega
be a primitive third root of unity. Then
\pm\sqrt[3]{\alpha},\pm\sqrt[3]{\alpha}\omega,\pm\sqrt[3]{\alpha}\omega^{2},\pm\sqrt[3]{\beta},\pm\sqrt[3]{\beta}\omega,\pm\sqrt[3]{\beta}\omega^{2}
are the roots of
f(x)
. Set
K=\Q(\alpha,\beta)
and it follows that

\begin{split}|\Gal(f)|&=[\Q(\sqrt[3]{\alpha},\sqrt[3]{\beta},\omega):\Q]\\
&=[K(\sqrt[3]{\alpha},\sqrt[3]{\beta},\omega):K(\sqrt[3]{\beta},\omega)][K(\sqrt[3]{\beta},\omega):K(\omega)][K(\omega):K][K:\Q]\\
&\leq(3)(3)(2)|G_{4}|\\
&=18|G_{4}|.\end{split}

Now let
\gamma,\gamma\omega,\gamma\omega^{2},\delta,\delta\omega,\delta\omega^{2}
be the roots of
x^{6}+ax^{3}+b
, where
\omega=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2
is a primitive third root of unity. Then
\pm\sqrt{\gamma},\pm\sqrt{\gamma}\sqrt{\omega},\pm\sqrt{\gamma}\omega,\pm\sqrt{\delta},\pm\sqrt{\delta}\sqrt{\omega},\pm\sqrt{\delta}\omega
are the roots of
f(x)
. Note that
\sqrt{\omega}=(1+\sqrt{-3})/2=\omega+1\in\Q(\omega)
. Set
K=\Q(\gamma,\delta,\omega)
and it follows that

\begin{split}|\Gal(f)|&=[\Q(\sqrt{\gamma},\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{\omega}):\Q]\\
&=[K(\sqrt{\gamma},\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{\omega}):K(\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{\omega})][K(\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{\omega}):K(\sqrt{\omega})][K(\sqrt{\omega}):K][K:\Q]\\
&\leq(2)(2)(1)|G_{6}|\\
&=4|G_{6}|.\end{split}

The result follows from combining both inequalities.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f(x)=x^{4}+ax^{2}+b\in\Q[x]
be an irreducible polynomial and
\theta
be a root of
f(x)
. Then for every
r\in\Q\setminus\Q^{2}
, we have
r\in\Q(\theta)^{2}
if and only if
r(a^{2}-4b)
,
r(-a+2\sqrt{b})
or
r(-a-2\sqrt{b})
is in
\Q^{2}
.

Proof.

Set
\sqrt{r}=a_{3}\theta^{3}+a_{2}\theta^{2}+a_{1}\theta+a_{0}
for some
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\Q
. We claim that either
a_{1}=a_{3}=0
or
a_{0}=a_{2}=0
. Suppose to the contrary that this is not the case. Since
\Gal(f)
acts transitively on the roots of
f(x)
, there is a mapping
\tau\in\Gal(f)
satisfying
\tau(\theta)=-\theta
. It follows that

\displaystyle\tau(\sqrt{r})=a_{2}\theta^{2}+a_{0}-(a_{3}\theta^{3}+a_{1}\theta),

and so
\tau(\sqrt{r})\neq\sqrt{r}
. Similarly,
\tau(\sqrt{r})
is another root of the minimal polynomial of
\sqrt{r}
over
\Q
. Since
r\in\Q
, the minimal polynomial is
x^{2}-r
and hence,
\tau(\sqrt{r})=-\sqrt{r}
. It follows that
a_{0}=a_{2}=0
, a contradiction.

Case 1:

a_{1}=a_{3}=0
. Then

\displaystyle r=(a_{2}\theta^{2}+a_{0})^{2}=(2a_{0}a_{2}-aa_{2}^{2})\theta^{2}+(a_{0}^{2}-ba_{2}^{2}).

The coefficient of
\theta^{2}
is zero, so
a_{2}\neq 0
and
a_{0}={aa_{2}}/2
. It follows that
r=(a^{2}-4b)(a_{2}/2)^{2}
and hence,
r(a^{2}-4b)\in\Q^{2}
.

Case 2:

a_{0}=a_{2}=0
. Then

\displaystyle r=(a_{3}\theta^{3}+a_{1}\theta)^{2}=(a_{1}^{2}-2aa_{1}a_{3}+a^{2}a_{3}^{2}-ba_{3}^{2})\theta^{2}+(aba_{3}^{2}-2ba_{1}a_{3}).

The coefficient of
\theta^{2}
is zero, so
a_{3}\neq 0
and
a_{1}=a_{3}(a\pm\sqrt{b})
. It follows that
r=(-a\mp 2\sqrt{b})(a_{3}\sqrt{b})^{2}
and hence, either
r(-a+2\sqrt{b})\in\Q^{2}
or
r(-a-2\sqrt{b})\in\Q^{2}
.

This proves the necessity. The sufficiency follows from construction.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f(x)=x^{12}+ax^{6}+b\in\Q[x]
be an irreducible polynomial and
\theta
be a root of
f(x)
. Then for every
r\in\Q\setminus\Q^{2}
, we have
r\in\Q(\theta)^{2}
if and only if
r(a^{2}-4b)
,
r(-a+2\sqrt{b})
or
r(-a-2\sqrt{b})
is in
\Q^{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
r\in\Q(\theta)^{2}
. Then

(3.1)

\begin{split}\sqrt{r}&=a_{11}\theta^{11}+a_{10}\theta^{10}+a_{9}\theta^{9}+a_{8}\theta^{8}+a_{7}\theta^{7}+a_{6}\theta^{6}\\
&+a_{5}\theta^{5}+a_{4}\theta^{4}+a_{3}\theta^{3}+a_{2}\theta^{2}+a_{1}\theta+a_{0}\end{split}

for some
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{11}\in\Q
. Since
f(x)
is irreducible,
\Gal(f)
acts transitively on the roots of
f(x)
. In particular, there exist mappings
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gal(f)
satisfying
\sigma_{1}(\theta)=\theta\omega
and
\sigma_{2}(\theta)=\theta\omega^{2}
where
\omega
is a primitive third root of unity. Then

(3.2)

\begin{split}\sigma_{1}(\sqrt{r})&=(a_{11}\theta^{11}+a_{8}\theta^{8}+a_{5}\theta^{5}+a_{2}\theta^{2})\omega^{2}+(a_{10}\theta^{10}+a_{7}\theta^{7}+a_{4}\theta^{4}+a_{1}\theta)\omega\\
&+(a_{9}\theta^{9}+a_{6}\theta^{6}+a_{3}\theta^{3}+a_{0}),\end{split}

(3.3)

\begin{split}\sigma_{2}(\sqrt{r})&=(a_{11}\theta^{11}+a_{8}\theta^{8}+a_{5}\theta^{5}+a_{2}\theta^{2})\omega+(a_{10}\theta^{10}+a_{7}\theta^{7}+a_{4}\theta^{4}+a_{1}\theta)\omega^{2}\\
&+(a_{9}\theta^{9}+a_{6}\theta^{6}+a_{3}\theta^{3}+a_{0}).\end{split}

By construction,
\sigma_{1}(\sqrt{r})
and
\sigma_{2}(\sqrt{r})
are roots of the minimal polynomial of

over
\Q
. Since

and hence,

\{\sigma_{1}(\sqrt{r}),\sigma_{2}(\sqrt{r})\}=\{\pm\sqrt{r}\}

Similar to Lemma

Let

(G_{4},G_{6})\notin\{(4T1,6T1),(4T1,6T2),(4T1,6T5)\}.

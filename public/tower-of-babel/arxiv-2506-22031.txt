Inducing coverings on Hilbert schemes
Lucas Li Bassi, Filippo Papallo

Abstract
. We find an explicit geometric description of all coverings of Hilb 2 ⁡ ( Σ ) \operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma) when Σ \Sigma is a normal, complex, quasi-projective surface with finite fundamental group. We then apply this construction to show that if Σ \Sigma is an irreducible symplectic surface then Hilb 2 ⁡ ( Σ ) \operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma) is an irreducible symplectic variety.



Introduction

Since their discovery, irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds (or ISM) have attracted the interest of the mathematical community. These varieties are compact Kähler manifold X that are simply connected and such that
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})
is generated by an everywhere non-degenerate holomorphic 2-form, which is referred to as the
symplectic
form. The first examples of such manifolds in dimension higher than two were constructed by
Beauville
in his seminal paper
[
1
, Deuxième partie]
using the construction of Hilbert schemes of
n
points

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(\Sigma)
on a surface
\Sigma

(this surface being an abelian or a
K3
surface).
Their importance is further underscored by the very well known
BB decomposition
.

Theorem

(
Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth connected compact Kähler manifold with
c_{1}(X)=0
. There exists a finite étale cover of X which is a product of a complex tori, irreducible symplectic manifolds and irreducible Calabi-Yau manifolds.

In recent years, attention has shifted towards extending this theory to the singular settings. There are many ways to generalize the notion of ISM in order to allow singularities. Three of the most used are surely the notions of
irreducible symplectic orbifold
(ISO),
irreducible symplectic variety
(ISV) and
primitive symplectic variety
(PSV) and they are related by the chain of implications

\text{{ISO}}\implies\text{{ISV}}\implies\text{{PSV}}.

We refer to
[
6
, Section 1]
for definitions and a detailed overview. In that article,
Garbagnati–Penegini–Perego

[
6
, Theorem 1.8]
classified all projective symplectic surfaces. Moreover, they were able to prove that, if the surface
\Sigma
is a PSV (resp. ISO), then
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
is a PSV (resp. ISO). However, they leave open the case where
\Sigma
is an ISV. The goal of this paper is to bridge this gap. We prove:

Theorem A
.

Let
\Sigma
be a projective irreducible symplectic surface,
then
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
is an irreducible symplectic variety
of dimension
4
.

In order to verify the definition of ISV (
3.1
) a crucial point is to be able to control, for every finite quasi-étale covering, the behavior of the pull-back of the symplectic form. This led to the more general question of explicitly constructing all (quasi-)étale coverings of the Hilbert scheme of two points on a normal surface. Indeed, at the moment we are writing this article, there exist no general method to induce from a (quasi-)étale covering of a surface
\Sigma
a (quasi-)étale covering of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
. Such a result can be found in
[
11
]
when
\Sigma
is an Enriques surface. We were able to modify his construction to answer our question and we proved the following theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
\Sigma
be a normal quasi-projective complex surface with ADE singularities and finite
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
.
Then, every (quasi-)étale cover of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
is induced by a (quasi-)étale cover of
\Sigma
.

Plan of the paper.

In
Section

1
we recall the necessary background on (quasi-)étale maps and on the geometry of Hilbert schemes of points on normal surfaces. In
Section

2
we construct all (quasi-)étale coverings of the Hilbert scheme of two points on a normal quasi-projective surface with finite fundamental group, showing that they are induced by coverings of the surface itself.
Section

3
is devoted to the study of symplectic structures: we recall the notion of irreducible symplectic variety and apply the results of
Section

2
to prove that if a surface is irreducible symplectic, then so is its Hilbert square.

Definition

2.2.
Approximating étaleness

Let now
\Sigma
be a quasi-projective surface over

with, at worst, ADE singularities and denote

its smooth locus. Normality ensures
the singular locus

has complex codimension
2
in

thus it consists of finitely-many isolated points.
In this subsection we would like to
characterize

By describing the Hilbert scheme as a

,

and

proved in
[

that
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
is a normal variety.
Thus, by purity we know that
\pi
branches along the singular locus of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
,
which still has codimension

; thus,
\pi
is quasi-étale
if and only if

is étale, where
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}:=\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)^{sm})
.
Now, one might wonder if this relates to the coverings of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(S)

somehow, for
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(S)
can be embedded in
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)^{sm}

notice this embedding is neither open, nor closed in general,
for one may describe

rather explicitly, as done in the proof

[
6
, Proposition 7.10]

let

and

if we denote by

one deduces that

Notice that for each component

;
it remains to study the fiber of each

differs from

After setting

Lemma 2.5
.

Given

, then

\pi^{sm}

Proof.

Since étaleness is stable under base change,
then it is clear that
\pi^{sm}

is étale too. Conversely,
if
\pi^{sm}
is not étale, then by purity

∎

Thanks to the results obtained in the smooth case,
it seems more manageable to build covers of the smooth

and hope to extend these to

3.
Application to irreducible symplectic surfaces

In this final section of the paper,
we will apply the machinery of
Section
2

in case
\Sigma
is any
projective irreducible symplectic surface

in order to prove
Theorem A

(that is
3.3
in the second paragraph).
Before that, we recall in the first subsection the definition of ISV.

3.1.
Irreducible symplectic varieties

We now recall the notion of
irreducible symplectic variety
,
a singular analogue of a
hyperkähler manifold

proposed by Greb-Kebekus-Peternell in
[
9
, Definition 8.6.2]
:
while in the smooth setting these manifolds carry a
nowhere-degenerate alternating
2
-form, in the singular setting
we require the existence of such a structure only
on the smooth locus, in such a way that it extends
via the above mentioned quasi-étale coverings.
Let us recall here the definition: given
X
a complex normal Kähler variety,
let the
sheaf of reflexive
p
-forms
on
X
be

\Omega_{X}^{[p]}:=(\Omega_{X^{sm}}^{p})^{\vee\vee}\simeq\iota_{*}\Omega_{X^{sm}}^{p}\,,

where
\iota_{*}
denotes the pushforward along the embedding

\iota:X^{sm}\hookrightarrow X
. We can then define
the
reflexive Dolbeaut cohomology
of
X
as

H^{[p],q}(X):=H^{q}\left(X,\Omega_{X}^{[p]}\right)\,.

Definition 3.1
.

An
irreducible symplectic variety
(shortly,
ISV
) is a normal compact
Kähler space
X
that verifies the three following conditions:

(1)

it has canonical singularities,

(2)

it admits a holomorphic symplectic form

\sigma
on its smooth locus
X^{sm}
, that we will view as a global section of
the sheaf of reflexive
2
-forms
\Omega^{[2]}_{X}=\left(\Omega_{X}^{2}\right)^{\vee\vee}
;

(3)

for every
finite quasi-étale covering

f:Y\to X

we have that

\bigoplus_{p}H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[p]})\simeq\mathbb{C}\left[f^{[*]}\sigma\right]\,,

as a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, where

f^{[*]}\sigma:=\left(f^{*}\sigma\right)^{\vee\vee}
.

In case
X
is
2
-dimensional, then it will be called
irreducible symplectic surface
.

Note that condition
3
in the above Definition is a
mimicking of the external edge of the Hodge diamond
of a hyperkälher manifold, realized via reflexive forms.
It is known by
[
13
, Proposition 2.19]
that
any ISV
X
has
h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
and
h^{[2],0}(X)=1
.

Remark 3.2
.

A finite quasi-étale covering
f:Y\to X
of
an ISV
X
is itself an ISV: for quasi-étale maps
preserve canonical singularities and the smooth
locus of
Y
is endowed with the form
f^{[*]}\sigma
.
Then condition 3. holds by naturality of the
reflexive pullback.

3.2.

Let now
\Sigma
be an irreducible symplectic surface.
In this case,
\operatorname{Sing}(\Sigma)=\Sigma\setminus S

consists of finitely many singular points of type ADE (see
[
12
, Theorem 6]
) and by
Xiao’s classification
[
14
, Theorem 3]
we know that

\pi_{1}(S)
is always a finite group,
hence we conclude that
the bijection described in
Section
2
between
all finite quasi-étale coverings of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)
and subgroups of

\displaystyle\pi_{1}\left(\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(S)\right)\simeq\frac{\pi_{1}(S)}{[\pi_{1}(S),\pi_{1}(S)]}\,,

holds true.

If

Proof.

It is already known that

i.e.
\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(\Sigma)

;
thus it is sufficient to prove condition 3.

Given a finite quasi-étale cover

,
we know by Zariski’s Main Theorem that it is determined by

,
which is Galois by abelianess of

;
thus,

is Galois with deck transformation group

is induced by a quasi-étale Galois covering

i.e.

As

is the blow-up

, then
it is birational to

, where

; moreover,
notice

where the action of

on the symmetric product is the diagonal one, i.e.

Since by
[

the external reflexive Hodge numbers are birational invariants,
for every

on the other hand,

inherits a reflexive

(for it is a PSV) and

By putting everything together, we conclude that

for every

; in particular,

is

of the generator
\sigma\in H^{2,0}(\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(S))
.
∎

Remark
.

Notice that by the existence of a (reflexive) symplectic form on

, we deduced

that

acts symplectically on its covering
\mathfrak{Y}
.
In case
\Sigma
did not have any

and some covering space

then we would deduce that

does not preserve any symplectic form;
in particular, by construction also the quotient

and hence

is a variety of Calabi-Yau type,
as in the case of

[

.

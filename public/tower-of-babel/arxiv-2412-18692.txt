Most subrings of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} have large corank
Kelly Isham, Nathan Kaplan

Abstract
. If Λ ⊆ ℤ n \Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n} is a sublattice of index m m , then ℤ n / Λ \mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda is a finite abelian group of order m m and rank at most n n . Several authors have studied statistical properties of these groups as we range over all sublattices of index at most X X . In this paper we investigate quotients by sublattices that have additional algebraic structure. While quotients ℤ n / Λ \mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda follow the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics and are very often cyclic, we show that if Λ \Lambda is actually a subring, then once n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 these quotients are very rarely cyclic. More generally, we show that once n n is large enough the quotient typically has very large rank. In order to prove our main theorems, we combine inputs from analytic number theory and combinatorics. We study certain zeta functions associated to ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} and also prove several results about matrices in Hermite normal form whose columns span a subring of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} .



(With an Appendix by Gautam Chinta)

1.
Introduction

The focus of this article is about a family of random finite abelian groups that arise in number theory. There has recently been extensive interest in this subject; see for example the survey of Wood
[
22
]
. We show that quotients of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by random subrings do not look like quotients of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by random sublattices. This has an interpretation in terms of the distribution of cokernels of families of random integer matrices and the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, a research topic that has been quite active in recent years.

In order to describe our results, we introduce some notation. A
sublattice

\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a finite index subgroup, that is, we reserve this term for full-rank sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For vectors
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we write
u\circ w
for the vector given by the componentwise product,
u\circ w=(u_{1}w_{1},\ldots,u_{n}w_{n})
. A sublattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
multiplicatively closed
if
u,w\in\Lambda
implies
u\circ w\in\Lambda
. A multiplicatively closed sublattice is a
subring
if it also contains the multiplicative identity
(1,1,\ldots,1)
. In this article, we address questions of the following type.

Question 1.1
.

Let
X
be a positive real number and
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a subring of index at most
X
chosen uniformly at random. What does the quotient
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/R
‘look like’? For example, as
X\rightarrow\infty
how often is this group cyclic?

A main point of this article is to show that the additional algebraic structure possessed by subrings leads these quotients to very often have large rank. In order to set up the contrast with quotients of random sublattices, we highlight some results about
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda
where
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a sublattice of index at most
X
chosen uniformly at random.

A finite abelian group
G
can be written uniquely in terms of its invariant factors,

G\cong\mathbb{Z}/\alpha_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\alpha_{2}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\alpha_{n}\mathbb{Z},

where
\alpha_{i+1}\mid\alpha_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. The
rank
of
G
is the largest
i
for which
\alpha_{i}>1
. The
corank
of a sublattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the rank of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda
. A sublattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
cocyclic
if either
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
or
\Lambda
has corank
1
.

Nguyen and Shparlinski compute the proportion of sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
that are cocyclic
[
17
]
, a result which also follows by a different method from earlier work of Petrogradsky
[
18
]
. Chinta, Kaplan, and Koplewitz generalize this result
[
4
]
, determining the proportion of sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
that have corank at most
k
for any
k\in[1,n]
. Throughout this paper we use
p
to denote a prime number and write
\prod_{p}
for a product over all primes.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
4
, Corollary 1.2]

Let
n,k
be positive integers satisfying
1\leq k\leq n
. Then,

\displaystyle p_{n,k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\left\{\text{Sublattices }\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\Lambda]<X\text{ and }\Lambda\text{ has corank at most }k\right\}}{\#\left\{\text{Sublattices }\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\Lambda]<X\right\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-p^{-j})^{2}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{1}{p^{i^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{i}(1-p^{-j})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n-i}(1-p^{-j})}\right).

These probabilities arise in the famous Cohen–Lenstra heuristics, which were developed during the study of statistical questions about Sylow
p
-subgroups of class groups of families of number fields
[
5
]
. Let
P_{n}
be the distribution on finite abelian
p
-groups of rank at most
n
that chooses a finite abelian
p
-group
G
of rank
r\leq n
with probability

(1)

P_{n}(G)=\frac{1}{\#\Aut(G)}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\right)\left(\prod_{i=n-r+1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\right).

Theorem
1.2
states that the proportion of
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
that have corank at most
k
is equal to the product over all primes of the probability that a finite abelian
p
-group chosen from the distribution
P_{n}
has rank at most
k
. These connections are explained in
[
4
]
. Even for small values of
k
, when
n
goes to infinity these probabilities are quite large. For example, for large
n
the proportion of cocyclic sublattices
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is approximately
85\%
, the proportion with corank at most
2
is approximately
99.4\%
, and the proportion with corank at most
3
is approximately
99.995\%
.

The main theorem of this paper is that for large
n
, while the vast majority of random sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
have small corank, it is extremely rare for a random subring of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to have small corank.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n
and
k
be positive integers satisfying
1\leq k<n
. Define

p^{R}_{n,k}=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\left\{\text{Subrings }R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon R]<X\text{ and }R\text{ has corank at most }k\right\}}{\#\left\{\text{Subrings }R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon R]<X\right\}}.

(1)

If
k\in\{1,2,3\}
and
n\geq 6
, then
p^{R}_{n,k}=0
.

(2)

If
n\geq 7
, then
p^{R}_{n,4}=0
.

(3)

If
k\leq(6-4\sqrt{2})n+2\sqrt{2}-\frac{8}{3}
and
n\geq 7
, then
p^{R}_{n,k}=0
.

For example, while approximately
85\%
of sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{7}
are cocyclic, the proportion of subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{7}
that are cocyclic is
0
.

We prove Theorem
1.3
by studying the asymptotic rate of growth of the function

H_{n,k}(X)=\#\left\{\text{Subrings }R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon R]<X\text{ and }R\text{ has corank at most }k\right\}

and comparing it to the asymptotic growth rate of the function

N_{n}(X)=\#\left\{\text{Subrings }R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon R]<X\right\}.

We use techniques from the theory of zeta functions of rings to prove upper bounds for the growth rate of
H_{n,k}(X)
and compare these results to lower bounds of Isham for
N_{n}(X)

[
10
, Theorem 1.6]
.

A key part of our argument involves counting special classes of matrices in Hermite normal form. Every finite index sublattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the column span of a unique
n\times n
matrix
H(\Lambda)
in Hermite normal form. Moreover,
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda
is isomorphic to the cokernel of this matrix. We discuss matrices in Hermite normal form and their cokernels at the start of Section
2
and in Proposition
4.2
.

Theorem
1.2
says that the probability
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda
has rank at most
k
is what one would expect if these random groups were distributed according to the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics. In
[
4
]
, the authors also show that Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Lambda
are distributed according to the distribution
P_{n}(G)
from (
1
). These results can be interpreted by saying that cokernels of random integer matrices in Hermite normal form follow the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics. There is a general philosophy that cokernels of families of random integer and
p
-adic matrices should be distributed according to the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, except when there is additional algebraic structure that must be taken into account; see for example
[
22
, Section 3.2]
. Theorem
1.3
says that once
n
is not too small, cokernels of
n\times n
matrices in Hermite normal form whose columns span a subring of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
are not distributed according to the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics. The additional algebraic structure of having a column span that is closed under componentwise multiplication leads to a completely different looking distribution of finite abelian groups.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
we need only give an upper bound on the growth rate of
H_{n,k}(X)
, but for small values of
k
we can prove something much more precise. We give asymptotic formulas for
H_{n,k}(X)
when
k\leq 3
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
k\in\{1,2,3\}
and
n>k
. There exists a positive real number
C_{n,k}
so that

H_{n,k}(X)\sim C_{n,k}X(\log X)^{\binom{n}{2}-1}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

We give a more precise description of
C_{n,k}
in Section
3
. We discuss the growth of
H_{n,4}(X)
at the end of that section. In Section
5
we use our knowledge of
N_{n}(X)
when
n\leq 5
to compute
p_{n,k}^{R}
for all
k
when
n\leq 4
. We prove that
p_{n,n-1}>0
for all
n
, and as a consequence of these results, we show that
p_{5,k}>0
for each
k\in\{1,2,3,4\}
.
Finally, an appendix by Gautam Chinta defines a multivariate zeta function which encodes not only the coranks of subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, but also the full cotypes. A conjecture explicitly describing this
cotype zeta function
for
\mathbb{Z}^{4}
is presented.

2.
Counting lattices and counting subrings

In this paper, we study the functions
H_{n,k}(X)
and
N_{n}(X)
by studying analytic properties of certain zeta functions associated to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We begin by introducing the subgroup zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
a_{k}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
denote the number of sublattices
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\Lambda]=k
. Define

\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})k^{-s},

where
s
is a complex variable.

Since
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a finitely generated nilpotent group, this zeta function has an Euler product and we can write

\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(s)=\prod_{p}\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},p}(s),\ \text{ where }\ \ \ \zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},p}(s)=\sum_{e=0}^{\infty}a_{p^{e}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})p^{-es}.

Let
M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the set of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{Z}
. An invertible matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with entries
a_{ij}
is in
Hermite normal form
if:

(1)

A
is upper triangular, and

(2)

0\leq a_{ij}<a_{ii}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the set of invertible matrices
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
that are in Hermite normal form. Every sublattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the column span of a unique matrix
H(\Lambda)\in\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, and moreover,
[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\Lambda]=\det(H(\Lambda))
. Counting matrices in
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with given determinant proves that

(2)

\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(s)=\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)\cdots\zeta(s-(n-1)).

See the book of Lubotzky and Segal for five proofs of this result
[
14
]
. For an extensive introduction to this topic see the survey of Voll
[
20
]
or the book of du Sautoy and Woodward
[
9
]
.

Applying a standard Tauberian theorem allows one to deduce an asymptotic formula for
\sum_{k<X}a_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k)
in terms of analytic properties of
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(s)
. The following Tauberian theorem is due to Delange. See
[
16
, Ch III, pages 121-122]
for an English translation.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
7
]

Let
F(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)n^{-s}
be a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients that converges for
\Re(s)>\alpha>0.
If

(1)

F(s)
is analytic on
\Re(s)=\alpha
except for
s=\alpha
and

(2)

for
s\sim\alpha
with
\Re(s)>\alpha
,

F(s)=\frac{G(s)}{(s-\alpha)^{\beta}}+H(s)

where
G(s)
and
H(s)
are analytic at
s=\alpha
with
G(\alpha)\neq 0

then

\sum_{n\leq X}a(n)\sim\frac{G(\alpha)}{\alpha\Gamma(\beta)}X^{\alpha}(\log X)^{\beta-1}.

See the recent survey of Alberts
[
1
]
for several concrete examples of how Tauberian theorems are applied to Dirichlet series constructed from Euler products.

The function on the right-hand side of equation (
2
) has its right-most pole at
s=n
, and this pole is a simple pole. It is not difficult to see that this function satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.2
, and then to carry out the application of this theorem. This leads to the following result.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer
n
and define

L_{n}(X)=\#\{\text{Sublattices }\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\Lambda]<X\}.

We have

L_{n}(X)\sim\frac{\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(2)}{n}X^{n},

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

A main idea of this paper is to follow a similar strategy for the subring zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

Definition 2.4

Let

denote the number of subrings

with

. The

Just as

The subring zeta function of

3.
The corank at most
k
zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

In order to prove Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, and
2.9
we introduce the
corank at most
k
zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and study its analytic properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{h}_{n,k}(j)
be the number of subrings
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
that have corank at most
k
and index
j
and
{h}_{n,k}(j)
be the number of these subrings that have corank exactly
k
. Clearly,

\tilde{h}_{n,k}(j)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{h}_{n,i}(j)\;\textrm{ and }\;H_{n,k}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor X\rfloor}\tilde{h}_{n,k}(j).

We define the

zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by

\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R,(k)}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\tilde{h}_{n,k}(j)j^{-s}.

A finite abelian group has rank at most
k
if and only if each of its Sylow
p
-subgroups has rank at most
k
. For the same reason that the subring zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R}(s)
has an Euler product, we see that
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R,(k)}(s)

\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R,(k)}(s)=\prod_{p}\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},p}^{R,(k)}(s),\ \text{ where }\ \ \ \zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},p}^{R,(k)}(s)=\sum_{e=0}^{\infty}\tilde{h}_{n,k}(p^{e})p^{-es}.

In order to prove Theorem
1.4
, we find the right-most pole of
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R,(k)}(s)
for

and then apply Theorem
2.2
. In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
, first for
k=1
, then
k=2
, and finally for
k=3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose

.

We prove this result in Section

.

Corollary 3.4

Let

Proof.

The right-most pole of

Multiplying the zeta function by

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R,(1)}(s)\prod_{p}(1-p^{-s})^{t}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(\left(1+\left(\binom{n}{2}-1\right)p^{-s}\right)(1-p^{-s})^{t-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(\left(1+\left(\binom{n}{2}-1\right)p^{-s}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{t-1}{k}p^{-ks}\right).

From here it is clear that

We now apply Theorem

.
To find

∎

In order to state the analogous results for subrings of corank

Definition 3.5

A subring

The motivation for this definition comes from the following decomposition result of Liu.

[

A subring

Liu uses this result to prove a recurrence for subrings of

4.
Subring matrices and the proofs of Theorems

A main idea that we use to prove upper bounds for
h_{n,k}(p^{e})
is to consider matrices in
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
whose columns span a subring of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of corank at most
k
. Throughout this paper, if
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
we write
\col(A)
for the column span of
A
. We write
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
for the columns of
A
and
a_{ij}
for the entries of
A
.

Definition 4.1
.

A matrix
A\in\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is a
subring matrix
if

(1)

the identity
(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}\in\col(A)
, and

(2)

for any columns
v_{i},v_{j}
of
A
, we have
v_{i}\circ v_{j}\in\col(A)
.

We see that
A
is a subring matrix if and only if
\col(A)
is a subring of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Moreover,
\det(A)=[\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\col(A)]
.

Suppose
A\in\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is a subring matrix. Since
(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}\in\col(A)
, it is clear that
a_{nn}=1
. If
\det(A)=p^{e}
, then the diagonal entries of
A
are
(p^{e_{1}},p^{e_{2}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
, where each
e_{i}\geq 0
and
e_{1}+\cdots+e_{n-1}=e
. That is,
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1})
is a weak composition of
e
into
n-1
parts. Let
\alpha=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1})
and define
g_{\alpha}(p)
to be the number of irreducible subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with diagonal entries
(p^{e_{1}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
.

We now prove that the corank of a subring
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is at most
n-1
. Recall that the
cokernel
of a matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is
\cok(A)=\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\col(A)
. If
A
is a subring matrix then the corank of
\col(A)
is the number of nontrivial invariant factors of
\cok(A)
. We recall some basic facts about the Smith normal form of an integer matrix.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be invertible. There exist
P,Q\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
PAQ=S
is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries
(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n})
are positive integers satisfying
s_{i}\mid s_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Since
\cok(A)\cong\cok(PAQ)\cong\cok(S)
, we have

\cok(A)\cong\mathbb{Z}/s_{1}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/s_{2}\mathbb{Z}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/s_{n}\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, these
s_{i}
are uniquely determined and

s_{1}\cdots s_{i}=\gcd(i\times i\text{ minors of }A).

We have seen that an
n\times n
subring matrix
A
has an entry equal to
1
, and therefore the greatest common divisor of the
1\times 1
minors of
A
is
1
. By Proposition
4.2
,
\cok(A)
has at most
n-1
nontrivial invariant factors, and therefore
\col(A)
has corank at most
n-1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

We have
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}^{R}(s)=\zeta(s)
. Every proper subring
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
has corank
1
.

Proof.

Suppose
A\in\mathcal{H}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a subring matrix with
\det(A)=k
. Then
A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}k&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
0\leq x<k
. Since
(1,1)^{T}\in\col(A)
we see that

Liu has determined the conditions under which the column span of a subring matrix is an irreducible subring.

Proposition 4.4
.

[
13
, Proposition 3.1]

Suppose

is a subring matrix with columns
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
and determinant equal to a power of

. Then

is an irreducible subring if and only if

and for each

every entry of

is
0
modulo

.

If

is a subring matrix with last column
(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}
, determinant equal to a power of

, and every entry of its first

columns is divisible by

, then we say that

is an
irreducible subring matrix
. We see that

is equal to the number of

irreducible subring matrices with determinant

. The diagonal entries of an irreducible subring matrix

are of the form

where each

and

. That is,

is a composition of

into

is equal to the sum of

taken over all compositions

of

into

Proposition 4.5
.

[
13
, Proposition 3.3]

Let

be a subring matrix with diagonal entries
(p^{e_{1}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
.
If

for all

is irreducible.

Liu remarks that Propositions

and

give a sufficient condition for determining whether a subring matrix is irreducible. Namely, it suffices to check that the diagonal is of the form

where each

. These remarks lead to the following proposition.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose

is an irreducible subring. The corank of

is

.

Proof.

Let

be the irreducible subring matrix for which

. Proposition

implies that the last column of

is
(1,\ldots,1)^{T}
. In every other column of

is divisible by

We next show that in a subring matrix
A
with a given diagonal certain collections of entries must be divisible by
p
and a particular submatrix constructed from

is an irreducible subring matrix.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
subring matrix with diagonal
(p^{e_{1}},p^{e_{2}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
and define
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
where
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n-1
and
e_{j}\neq 0
if and only if

(1)

For any

satisfying

, we have
v_{i_{j_{1}}}\circ v_{i_{j_{2}}}\in\Span(v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}})
.

(2)

Consider the

matrix obtained by first taking the

matrix with columns
v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}
and then deleting the
j
th
row for any

. Now append a row where every entry is
0
to the bottom of this matrix, and append a final column where every entry is
1
. This is a
(k+1)\times(k+1)
irreducible subring matrix.

(3)

For any
j_{1},j_{2}
satisfying
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k
, we have
p\mid a_{i_{j_{1}}i_{j_{2}}}
.

(4)

If
i\in I
, then every entry in the column
v_{i}
is divisible by
p
.

Proof.

We prove these statements in order.

(1)

Since
\col(A)
is multiplicatively closed, for any pair
i_{j_{1}},i_{j_{2}}\in I
there exist unique
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

v_{i_{j_{1}}}\circ v_{i_{j_{2}}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}c_{\ell}v_{\ell}.

Suppose
j\not\in I
, so that
p^{e_{j}}=1
. Since
A
is in Hermite normal form, the only nonzero entry in the
j
th
row of

is this
1
in column
j
. In particular, the entry of
v_{i_{j_{1}}}\circ v_{i_{j_{2}}}
in row

is
0
. This implies

. We conclude that
v_{i_{j_{1}}}\circ v_{i_{j_{2}}}
is in the span of the columns
v_{i}
where

.

(2)

Consider the
n\times k
matrix with columns
v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}
. Part (1) implies that the column span of this matrix is multiplicatively closed. If

, then every entry of the

th
row of this matrix is

. We delete these rows and see that the column span of this

matrix is still multiplicatively closed. Appending a row where every entry is

to the bottom of the matrix and a final column where every entry is equal to

gives a

matrix whose column span is multiplicatively closed. The diagonal of this matrix consists of positive powers of

in the first

entries and then a

in the last entry. Proposition

implies that this is an irreducible subring matrix.

(3)

The entry

is contained in one of the first

columns of the irreducible subring matrix described in Part (2). Proposition
4.4
implies that

.

(4)

Suppose

and consider

. Part (3) implies that any entry of

in a row corresponding to an element of

is divisible by

. Since

is in Hermite normal form, the other entries of

are

.

∎

The next result is the key observation that allows us to use combinatorial properties of subring matrices to prove upper bounds for
h_{n,k}(p^{e})
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let

be a

subring matrix with diagonal
(p^{e_{1}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
and define

where
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n-1
and
e_{j}\neq 0
if and only if

. Then

.

The analogue of this statement for sublattices is not true. For example, the column span of the matrix in Hermite normal form

0&2&1\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

has corank 1 since the gcd of the

minors of

is
1
, but
A
has two diagonal entries that are positive powers of
2
.

Proof.

We bound the corank from above and below. There is an
(n-k)\times(n-k)
submatrix of
A
that is upper triangular and has diagonal
(1,1,\ldots,1)
. Therefore, Proposition
4.2
implies that
\corank(A)\leq k
.

Proposition
4.7
implies that if
i\in I
, then every entry of the column
v_{i}
is divisible by
p
. Therefore, every
(n-k+1)\times(n-k+1)
submatrix of

contains a column in which every entry is divisible by

, so every
(n-k+1)\times(n-k+1)
minor of

is divisible by

. Proposition
4.2
then implies that

, completing the proof.
∎

At the end of the introduction we discussed how cokernels of
n\times n
subring matrices ordered by determinant are not distributed according to the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics. For example, the cokernel of such a matrix is cyclic much less often than the heuristics would predict. We give a kind of rough explanation for why this should be true. A main issue is that the number of subring matrices of index equal to a power of
p
that have cyclic cokernel is quite small. If we require that the column space of a matrix
A
in Hermite normal form is closed under componentwise multiplication, a large collection of entries is forced to be congruent to
0
modulo
p
, and so it seems much more likely that every
k\times k
minor of
A
is divisible by
p
. Proposition
4.2
then explains why we should expect the corank of
A
not to be too small.

We now prove Proposition
3.2
, which counts cocyclic subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of determinant
p^{e}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Suppose
A\in\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is a subring matrix with determinant
p^{e}
and that
\col(A)
is a cocyclic subring of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. By Theorem
4.8
exactly one diagonal entry of
A
is equal to
p^{e}
and the rest of the diagonal entries are equal to
1
. We first consider the case where
A
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}p^{e}&a_{12}&a_{13}&\cdots&a_{1n}\\
&1&0&\cdots&0\\
&&1&\cdots&0\\
&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\col(A)
is multiplicatively closed, for any
i
satisfying
2\leq i\leq n
there exist unique
c_{1},c_{2}\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

v_{i}\circ v_{i}-v_{i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}c_{\ell}v_{\ell}.

Since the only nonzero entry in
v_{i}\circ v_{i}-v_{i}
is an
a_{1i}^{2}-a_{1i}
in the first row, we must have
p^{e}\mid(a_{1i}^{2}-a_{1i})
. If
i\neq j
satisfy
2\leq i,j\leq n
, then the only nonzero entry of
v_{i}\circ v_{j}
is an
a_{1i}a_{1j}
in the first row. So we must have
p^{e}\mid a_{1i}a_{1j}
.
These conditions are satisfied if and only if there is at most one
j
for which
p^{e}\mid(a_{1j}-1)
, and
p^{e}\mid a_{1i}
for all
i\neq j
. We cannot have every
a_{1i}
divisible by
p
since
\col(A)
must contain
(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}
. Thus, there are a total of
n-1
cocyclic subring matrices with this diagonal.

For each
i\in[2,n-1]
we repeat this analysis for the case where
a_{ii}=p^{e}
. In this case there are exactly
n-i
cocyclic subring matrices. Taking a sum over
i
completes the proof.
∎

In order to prove Theorems

and

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
A
be a subring matrix with diagonal
(p^{e_{1}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
and define

where
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n-1
and
e_{j}\neq 0
if and only if
j\in I
. If
i\in I
and
j\not\in I
, then

.

Proof.

Since
\col(A)
is multiplicatively closed there exist unique
c_{1},\ldots c_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

v_{j}\circ v_{j}-v_{j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}c_{\ell}v_{\ell}.

All of the nonzero entries of
v_{j}\circ v_{j}-v_{j}
are contained in rows
1,2,\ldots,j-1
. Therefore,
c_{\ell}=0
if
\ell\geq j
.

If
\ell\not\in I
, then
e_{\ell}=0
and

. Since
A
is in Hermite normal form the only nonzero entry in the
\ell
th

is this

on the diagonal. Therefore, if

, then

Consider the subset of

consisting of integers less than

, then

and we define

The entry of

in row

is

cannot divide both

and

in row

We now see that it is not possible for

to have any nonzero entries outside of rows

. That is,

shows that

to have any nonzero entries outside of rows

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
A
be a subring matrix with diagonal
(p^{e_{1}},\ldots,p^{e_{n-1}},1)
and define

This conjecture was obtained by first enumerating all the subrings (and cotypes) of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}
of index less than

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{4}

Remark A.3
.

Note the functional equation

F(1/p;1/x,1/y,1/z)=-p^{3}xyzF(p;x,y,z).

This functional equation and the ones in (
6
) above are consistent with the results of Voll
[
19
]
.
Furthermore, the specialization
F(p;p^{-s},p^{-s},p^{-s})
agrees with the local factor of
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{4}}^{R}(s)
in Theorem
2.5
.

Remark A.4
.

We can show that the conjecture is compatible with the results of Section
3
for
n=4
. Explicitly, the corank at most
k
zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be obtained from the cotype zeta function as follows:

(7)

\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R,(k)}(s)=\lim_{s_{k+1},\ldots,s_{n-1}\to\infty}\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{R}(s,\ldots,s,s_{k+1},\ldots,s_{n-1}).

When
n=4
and
k=1
or 2, we substitute the expression from Conjecture A
.2
into the righthand side of (
7
) to get

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{4}}^{R,(1)}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\frac{1+5p^{-s}}{1-p^{-s}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}^{4}}^{R,(2)}(s)

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)^{6}\cdot\prod_{p}\frac{\left(1+4p^{-s}+2p^{-2s}+(3p-4)p^{-3s}-6p^{1-5s}\right)(1-p^{-s})^{4}}{1-p^{1-3s}}.

These expressions for the corank at most 1 and 2 zeta functions agree with Proposition
3.3
when
n=4
and Corollary
5.2
.

Remark A.5
.

Using the expression for the cotype zeta function of
\mathbb{Z}^{4}
, it should be possible to similarly obtain results for counting subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of corank 1,2 or 3 for arbitrary
n
. This will follow from an analogue of Liu’s recursion relation
[
13
, Proposition 4.4]
expressing reducible subrings in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in terms subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, for
m
less than
n
. In fact, suitable refinement of Liu’s relation will identify subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of corank
m
with products of subrings of
\mathbb{Z}^{m^{\prime}}
with
m^{\prime}\leq m.

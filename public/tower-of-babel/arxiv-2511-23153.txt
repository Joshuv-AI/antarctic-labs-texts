Moment Approximations to Magnetic Rotating Shallow Flows
Julian Koellermeier, Michael Redle, Manuel Torrilhon

Abstract
Originally introduced to describe a transition region in stars, the magnetic rotating shallow water (MRSW) model is now used in many solar physics and geophysical applications. Derived from the 3-D incompressible magnetohydrodynamic system, the shallow nature of these applications motivates depth-averaging of both the velocities and magnetic fields. This is advantageous in terms of computational efficiency—but at the loss of vertical information, thus limiting the predictive power of the MRSW model. To overcome this problem, we employ higher-order vertical moments, but now in the context of conductive fluids. In doing so, the new approximation maintains non-constant vertical profiles of both the horizontal magnetic fields and horizontal velocities, while still remaining in the simplified 2-D framework corresponding to depth integration. In this work, we extend the derivation of the shallow water moment equations to derive the MRSW moment system of arbitrary order; i.e., we represent the vertical profiles of the velocities—and now additionally the magnetic fields—by arbitrary-order polynomial expansions, and close the new expanded 2-D system with evolution equations for these polynomial coefficients, found via Galerkin projection. Through numerical experiments for MRSW moment systems up to third-order, we demonstrate that these moment approximations reduce model error without significantly sacrificing computational efficiency.



Keywords:
Free-surface flows, magnetohydrodynamics, moment approximation, hyperbolic systems, magneto-geostrophic adjustment

Introduction

The rotating shallow water magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations, also referred as magnetic rotating shallow water (MRSW) equations, describe the behavior of a thin layer, conductive
fluid with a free surface.
Since their introduction in
[
15
]
in the context of solar tachocline (the thin
layer of the Sun between the convective and radiative zones), the number of applications
has vastly grown; some examples include neutron star atmosphere dynamics
[
21
,
52
]
and accreting
matter flows
[
25
]
, and exoplanets with magnetically active atmospheres
[
7
,
20
,
22
,
44
]
.
While these applications
could alternatively be described by the full, spatially 3-D incompressible MHD system, the flow in the vertical direction is much smaller than that in the horizontal directions—motivating the introduction of the ‘shallow fluid’ assumption.

Through a suitable vertical averaging, the shallow fluid assumption ultimately leads to a reduced complexity, two-dimensional depth-averaged model (e.g., the MRSW system).
Consequently, computational costs of the MRSW model are significantly lower than its fully 3-D incompressible MHD counterpart—where the difficulties lie not only in the extra vertical dimension, but also in the computational complexities that arise with a free surface.
Unfortunately, however, depth-averaging the velocity and the magnetic field comes at the cost of losing vertical information.
In some cases, this may not be critical; for example, in cases without a magnetic field, so called ‘plug-flow’ arises often in geophysical contexts; see, e.g.,
[
45
]
.
Many other applications, though, have significant vertical profiles of velocity and magnetic fields; see, e.g.,
[
11
]
, where results in the context of a solar tachocline are presented.

In contexts without magnetic fields, such as the standard shallow water equations, the accuracy loss of depth-averaged models when dealing with varying velocity profiles is already well-known
[
42
,
8
]
.
This has led to a variety of more advanced models for free-surface flows.
Two typical directions can be distinguished:
(i) the consideration of multiple layers with piecewise constant fluid velocities—the so-called multi-layer models
[
13
]
, which unfortunately lose important mathematical properties for the interesting case of more than two layers; and
(ii) using the Green-Naghdi approach to model vertical variation with additional shape parameters; see, e.g.,
[
19
,
35
]
; leading to additional equations that have to be derived on a case-by-case basis.
Note that both of these methodologies to improve vertical profile approximations have also been extended to the MRSW system; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
24
,
53
]
for examples of multilayer model extensions and, e.g.,
[
10
]
for the extension of the Green-Naghdi approach to MRSW.

A promising new method has been recently employed in
[
29
]
in which the authors (i) represent the vertical profile of the velocity by an
M
th-degree polynomial; and (ii) derive additional evolution equations for the
M
polynomial coefficients using a Galerkin projection of (a rescaled version of) the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations.
Consequently, one obtains the hierarchical model known as the Shallow Water Moment Equations (SWME).
Since their derivation, several studies have subsequently analyzed hyperbolicity
[
28
]
, steady state solutions
[
27
]
, and the behavior near equilibrium manifolds
[
23
]
of the SWME, further revealing several beneficial analytical properties of the models.
In addition, versions of the SWME have been applied to sediment transport
[
14
]
, and extended to 2-D
[
2
,
50
]
, including open channel flows
[
46
]
and non-hydrostatic cases
[
43
]
.

In this work, we extend this SMWE approach, i.e., employing higher-order vertical moments, to magnetic rotating flows. This new approximation of the 3-D incompressible MHD system maintains non-constant vertical profiles of the magnetic field in addition to those of the horizontal velocities, while still remaining in the computationally efficient 2-D framework corresponding to depth integration.
We present the derivation of this extended approach, resulting in the MRSW moment system of arbitrary order.
The obtained system naturally gives rise to the so-called Godunov-Powell source—a nonconservative term often used to numerically treat the ever-important divergence-free constraint of the magnetic field.
Utilizing this term, we discretize both the moment and the (rescaled) incompressible Navier-Stokes systems using a direct extension of that introduced in
[
5
]
; namely, we use the Godunov-Powell source to help treat the divergence of the magnetic field, and discretize the nonconservative system using the second-order path-conservative central-upwind (PCCU) scheme—an unstaggered Riemann-problem-solver-free finite volume scheme.
Through 1-D numerical results of MRSW moment systems of up to third-order (i.e., third-degree polynomial representations of the vertical profiles), we show that the proposed model hierarchy better approximates the derived 2-D reference system than the constant-profile MRSW model.
After demonstrating that these models can increase the predictive power of conductive shallow flow models without significantly sacrificing computational efficiency,
we present an exploratory example on magneto-geostrophic adjustment.
We show that an initial velocity vertical profile induces a vertical profile of the magnetic field and alters wave behavior (particularly in the Alfvén waves), further demonstrating the importance of additionally representing magnetic field vertical profiles.

In §

We follow with the derivation of the arbitrary-order MRSW moment equations in §

.
Section
4

) and the reference system (§
4.2
), as each requires its own discretization.
We follow by presenting numerical tests of the MRSW moment system in §
5
. Finally, we make our concluding remarks in §
6
.

2
Vertically resolved magnetic rotating shallow water reference model

To derive the MRSW moment approximation, we first map the vertical variable
z\in[Z,h+Z]
in the ideal incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations (
Z
and
h

The resulting mapped equations will be referred to as the ‘vertically resolved MRSW reference system,’ and forming these equations benefits twofold.
First, the 3-D mapping obtained in this section is directly used to derive the reduced-dimension 2-D MRSW moment model, which uses polynomial expansions to form higher-order vertical profiles and additional evolution equations for the polynomial coefficients; see §
3
for further details.
Second, the vertically resolved MRSW reference system provides a crucial validation aspect of the model hierarchy we form in §
3
.
This is because the reference model still fully resolves the

Furthermore, this MRSW reference system renders a full 3-D representation of the problem of interest—thus providing a precise model to evaluate the error of the MRSW moment approximation, which reduces the dimension through vertical averaging.

The original equations used to derive the vertically resolved MRSW reference model are the incompressible ideal MHD equations with rotation.

3
A moment closure for magnetic rotating shallow flow

Standard shallow flow models—in this paper the most relevant being the MRSW equations—spatially reduce the mapped reference system in (
2.26
)–(
2.31
) from 3-D to 2-D by replacing the velocities and magnetic fields with their depth-averaged mean values
u_{m}
,
v_{m}
,
a_{m}
, and
b_{m}
.
This comes with the potential loss of information regarding the corresponding vertical profiles.
In this section, we also reduce the reference system to 2-D, but to mitigate this vertical information loss, we follow the work of
[
29
]
and express variables with vertical profiles through a polynomial expansion:

\widetilde{\bm{W}}(t,x,y,\zeta)=\bm{W}_{m}(t,x,y)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\bm{\Psi}_{\ell}(t,x,y)\phi_{\ell}\left(\zeta\right),

(3.1)

where
\widetilde{\bm{W}}=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v},\tilde{a},\tilde{b})^{\top}
,
\bm{W}_{m}=(u_{m},v_{m},a_{m},b_{m})^{\top}
,
\bm{\Psi}_{\ell}=(\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{\ell},\gamma_{\ell},\eta_{\ell})^{\top}
are the vertical profile polynomial coefficients, and
\phi_{\ell}
are basis functions on the domain
[0,1]
for
\ell=1,\dots,M
.
Following
[
29
]
, we define
\phi_{\ell}
using the scaled (orthogonal) Legendre polynomials on the domain
[0,1]
normalized such that
\phi_{\ell}(0)=1
.
For example, the first three basis polynomials read

\phi_{1}(\zeta)=1-2\zeta,\quad\phi_{2}(\zeta)=1-6\zeta+6\zeta^{2},\quad\phi_{1}(\zeta)=1-12\zeta+30\zeta^{2}-20\zeta^{3}.

(3.2)

The expansions in (
3.1
) imply that the vertical profiles of velocity and magnetic field are now modeled by the spatially 2-D functions in
\bm{W}_{m}(t,x,y)
, and their corresponding 2-D polynomial coefficients within
\bm{\Psi}_{\ell}
for
\ell=1,\dots,M
.
Furthermore, it is clear that as the number
M
increases, the approximation of the vertical profile will improve.
Additionally, note that the depth-averaged quantities
\bm{W}_{m}
are essentially an expansion of the zeroth-order Legendre polynomial
\phi_{0}(\zeta)=1
, thus matching the single-variable depth-averaged vertical profiles of standard shallow flow approximations.

By (
3.1
),
{\bm{W}}_{m}
and
\bm{\Psi}_{\ell}
together provide an approximate 3-D rendering of
\widetilde{\bm{W}}
.
Thus, the remainder of this section presents the derivation of the evolution equations for the 2-D variables within
{\bm{W}}_{m}
and
\bm{\Psi}_{\ell}
.
In doing so, this will achieve the overall goal of reducing (
2.26
)–(
2.31
) from 3-D to an approximate system dependent on only two spatial dimensions.
The presented derivation closely parallels that of
[
29
]
, where the vertically resolved reference system for the shallow water equations is derived together with their corresponding moment approximations—except we must now consider contributions of the magnetic field.
Even so, the magnetic contributions interestingly parallel that of the velocity, as we will show in the remainder of Section
3
.
For the remainder of this paper, the tildes seen in (
2.26
)–(
2.31
) will be dropped for readability.

We will first successively derive the evolution equations for the mean variables
u_{m},v_{m},a_{m},b_{m}
, before deriving the remaining evolution equations for the additional coefficients
\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{\ell},\gamma_{\ell},\eta_{\ell}
for
\ell=1,\dots,M
.

3.1
Mean momentum and magnetic field evolution derivation

To derive the mean momentum and magnetic field evolution equations, we take the equations (
2.28
)–(
2.31
) and (i) integrate over
\zeta
from zero to one, and (ii) substitute in the polynomial expansions of (
3.1
) and simplify.
Starting with the
x
-direction momentum equation (
2.28
), and integrating over
\zeta
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\left(hu_{m}\right)}_{t}+{\left[h\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\ d\zeta+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}-h\int_{0}^{1}a^{2}\ d\zeta\right]}_{x}

\displaystyle+{\left[h\int_{0}^{1}uv\ d\zeta-h\int_{0}^{1}ab\ d\zeta\right]}_{y}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}hu\omega[h,u,v]-ha{\mathcal{C}}[h,a,b]\ d\zeta=fhv_{m}-ghZ_{x}.

We then wish to implement the polynomial expansions from (
3.1
). To do so, we must simplify the higher order integrals seen above.
Using the orthogonality and properties of Legendre polynomials, we find

\int_{0}^{1}W^{(j)}W^{(k)}\ d\zeta=W_{m}^{(j)}W^{(k)}_{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\Psi_{\ell}^{(j)}\Psi_{\ell}^{(k)}}{2\ell+1},

(3.3)

where
W^{(j)}
,
W_{m}^{(j)}
, and
\Psi_{\ell}^{(j)}
denote the
j
th component of
\bm{W}
,
\bm{W}_{m}
, and
\bm{\Psi}_{\ell}
in (
3.1
), respectively.
Then, by using the definitions of the mean velocities and magnetic fields, and the corresponding vertical coupling terms in (
2.21
)–(
2.22
), we can see that for an arbitrary function
\psi
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\left(\psi h\omega[h,u,v]\right)}_{\zeta}\ d\zeta=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\left(\psi h{\mathcal{C}}[h,a,b]\right)}_{\zeta}\ d\zeta=\psi\Big|_{\zeta=1}{\left[(ha_{m})_{x}+(hb_{m})_{y}\right]}.

Note that the integral of the magnetic vertical coupling term multiplied by an arbitrary function
\psi
also equals zero through the divergence-free condition (
2.27
), but keeping it as written above proves beneficial in the discretization; see further discussion in §
3.3
.

Combining the simplifications in (
3.3
)–(
3.4
) with the
\zeta
-integrated
x
-direction momentum equation, we recover the evolution equation for
hu_{m}
purely in 2-D:

\displaystyle{\left(hu_{m}\right)}_{t}+

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left[h{\left(u_{m}^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}^{2}}{2\ell+1}\right)}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}-h{\left(a_{m}^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\gamma_{\ell}^{2}}{2\ell+1}\right)}\right]}_{x}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left[h{\left(u_{m}v_{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}}{2\ell+1}\right)}-h{\left(a_{m}b_{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\gamma_{\ell}\eta_{\ell}}{2\ell+1}\right)}\right]}_{y}

\displaystyle=fhv_{m}-ghZ_{x}-a\Big|_{\zeta=1}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}.

The derivations of the equations for
hv_{m},\ ha_{m},
and
hb_{m}
follow the same procedure; for the sake of brevity, we refrain from presenting these evolution equations until the final system in §
3.3
.

3.2
Vertical coefficient evolution equations

To obtain the full 3-D approximation to the velocity and magnetic fields as seen in (
3.1
), we need expressions for the vertical profile coefficients
\bm{\Psi}_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i},\eta_{i})^{\top}
; in this work, these come in the form of evolution equations.
To recover these evolution equations, we again follow
[
29
]
and take moments of the velocity and magnetic fields with respect to the Legendre polynomials.
By orthogonality of the selected basis functions
\phi_{i}(\zeta)
, doing so results in

\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}\bm{W}\ d\zeta=\frac{\bm{\Psi}_{i}}{2i+1},

(3.6)

where
\bm{W}
is defined in (
3.1
).
Therefore, by multiplying the reference velocity and magnetic field equations (
2.28
)–(
2.31
) by
\phi_{i}(\zeta)
and integrating over
\zeta\in[0,1]
, we will obtain evolution equations for the vertical profile coefficients
\bm{\Psi}_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i},\eta_{i})^{\top}
.
Due to the similarities of this process with the computation of moments, we will also refer to these as ‘moment’ equations.
We present this derivation for the
x
-velocity moments
\alpha_{i}
in this section, and the moment equations for
\beta_{i},\ \gamma_{i},
and
\eta_{i}
are derived analogously.

Multiplying the
x
-momentum reference equation (
2.28
) by
\phi_{i}(\zeta)
and integrating over
[0,1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left(h\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}u\ d\zeta\right)}_{t}+{\left[h\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}u^{2}\ d\zeta-h\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}a^{2}\ d\zeta\right]}_{x}+{\left[h\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}uv\ d\zeta-h\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}ab\ d\zeta\right]}_{y}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}{\left(hu\omega-ha{\mathcal{C}}\right)}_{\zeta}\ d\zeta=\int_{0}^{1}fh\phi_{i}v\ d\zeta.

Note that here we already applied orthogonality to the
\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}
term in the
x
-direction flux and to the bottom topography source term.
The term inside of the time derivative and the remaining source term can be simplified using (
3.6
).
In addition, all integrals residing in the flux terms of (
3.7
) have an integrand of the form
\phi_{i}W^{(j)}W^{(k)}
, where
W^{(j)}
again denotes the
j
th component of
\bm{W}
in (
3.1
). Therefore, all flux term integrals can be simplified by substituting in the vertical expansions of (
3.1
) and evaluating, resulting in

\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}W^{(j)}W^{(k)}\ d\zeta=\frac{1}{2i+1}{\left(W^{(j)}_{m}\Psi^{(k)}_{i}+W^{(k)}_{m}\Psi^{(j)}_{i}+\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}\Psi^{(j)}_{\ell}\Psi^{(k)}_{n}\right)},

where

A_{i\ell n}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}\phi_{\ell}\phi_{n}\ d\zeta.

Lastly, we must evaluate the integrals in (
3.7
) associated with the vertical coupling.
We present these computations in Appendix
B
.
Using these results presented in (
B.3
) and (
B.4
), we obtain the following evolution equation for the
x
-momentum vertical profile coefficients
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,M
:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left(h\alpha_{i}\right)}_{t}+{\left[h{\left(2u_{m}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{n}\right)}-h{\left(2a_{m}\gamma_{i}+\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}\gamma_{\ell}\gamma_{n}\right)}\right]}_{x}

(3.8)

\displaystyle+{\left[h{\left(u_{m}\beta_{i}+v_{m}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{n}\right)}-h{\left(a_{m}\eta_{i}+b_{m}\gamma_{i}+\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}\gamma_{\ell}\eta_{n}\right)}\right]}_{y}

\displaystyle=fh\beta_{i}+u_{m}{\left[(h\alpha_{i})_{x}+(h\beta_{i})_{y}\right]}-\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}\alpha_{n}{\left[(h\alpha_{\ell})_{x}+(h\beta_{\ell})_{y}\right]}

\displaystyle-a_{m}{\left[(h\gamma_{i})_{x}+(h\eta_{i})_{y}\right]}+\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}\gamma_{n}{\left[(h\gamma_{\ell})_{x}+(h\eta_{\ell})_{y}\right]}-{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\gamma_{\ell}{\left(1+\Gamma_{i\ell}\right)}\right]},

where
B_{i\ell n}
is defined in (
B.2
), and

\Gamma_{i\ell}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}\ d\zeta.

The moment equations for
\beta_{i}
,
\gamma_{i}
, and
\eta_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,M
, are derived in an analogous manner, and their evolution equations are presented in §
3.3
.

3.3
The arbitrary-order magnetic rotating shallow water moment equations

Using the derivation techniques presented in §
3.1
and §
3.2
, we obtain the full, arbitrary
M
th-order Magnetic Rotating Shallow Water Moment Equations (MRSWME):

\displaystyle h_{t}+{\left(hu_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hv_{m}\right)}_{y}=0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}=0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\left(hu_{m}\right)}_{t}+&{\left[hu_{m}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}-ha_{m}^{2}+h\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}^{2}-\gamma_{\ell}^{2}}{2\ell+1}\right]}_{x}+{\left[hu_{m}v_{m}-ha_{m}b_{m}+h\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell}\eta_{\ell}}{2\ell+1}\right]}_{y}\\[4.0pt]
&\hskip 213.39566pt=fhv_{m}-ghZ_{x}-a\Big|_{\zeta=1}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]},\end{aligned}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\left(hv_{m}\right)}_{t}+&{\left[hu_{m}v_{m}-ha_{m}b_{m}+h\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell}\eta_{\ell}}{2\ell+1}\right]}_{x}+{\left[hv_{m}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}-hb_{m}^{2}+h\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\beta_{\ell}^{2}-\eta_{\ell}^{2}}{2\ell+1}\right]}_{y}\\[4.0pt]
&\hskip 213.39566pt=-fhu_{m}-ghZ_{y}-b\Big|_{\zeta=1}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]},\end{aligned}

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{t}+{\left[ha_{m}v_{m}-hb_{m}u_{m}+h\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\beta_{\ell}\gamma_{\ell}-\alpha_{\ell}\eta_{\ell}}{2\ell+1}\right]}_{y}=-u\Big|_{\zeta=1}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]},

(3.13)

\displaystyle{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{t}+{\left[hb_{m}u_{m}-ha_{m}v_{m}+h\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}\eta_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell}\gamma_{\ell}}{2\ell+1}\right]}_{x}=-v\Big|_{\zeta=1}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\left(h\alpha_{i}\right)}_{t}&+{\left[2{\left(hu_{m}\alpha_{i}-ha_{m}\gamma_{i}\right)}+h\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}{\left(\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{n}-\gamma_{\ell}\gamma_{n}\right)}\right]}_{x}\\[4.0pt]
&+{\left[hu_{m}\beta_{i}+hv_{m}\alpha_{i}-ha_{m}\eta_{i}-hb_{m}\gamma_{i}+h\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}{\left(\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{n}-\gamma_{\ell}\eta_{n}\right)}\right]}_{y}\\[4.0pt]
&\quad=fh\beta_{i}+u_{m}D_{i}-a_{m}{\mathcal{D}}_{i}-{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\gamma_{\ell}{\left(1+\Gamma_{i\ell}\right)}}-\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}{\left(\alpha_{n}D_{\ell}-\gamma_{n}{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\right)},\end{aligned}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\left(h\beta_{i}\right)}_{t}&+{\left[hu_{m}\beta_{i}+hv_{m}\alpha_{i}-ha_{m}\eta_{i}-hb_{m}\gamma_{i}+h\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}{\left(\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{n}-\gamma_{\ell}\eta_{n}\right)}\right]}_{x}\\[4.0pt]
&+{\left[2{\left(hv_{m}\beta_{i}-hb_{m}\eta_{i}\right)}+h\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}{\left(\beta_{\ell}\beta_{n}-\eta_{\ell}\eta_{n}\right)}\right]}_{y}\\[4.0pt]
&\quad=-fh\alpha_{i}+v_{m}D_{i}-b_{m}{\mathcal{D}}_{i}-{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\eta_{\ell}{\left(1+\Gamma_{i\ell}\right)}}-\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}{\left(\beta_{n}D_{\ell}-\eta_{n}{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\right)},\end{aligned}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\left(h\gamma_{i}\right)}_{t}&+{\left[ha_{m}\beta_{i}+hv_{m}\gamma_{i}-hb_{m}\alpha_{i}-hu_{m}\eta_{i}+h\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}{\left(\beta_{\ell}\gamma_{n}-\alpha_{\ell}\eta_{n}\right)}\right]}_{y}\\[4.0pt]
&=a_{m}D_{i}-u_{m}{\mathcal{D}}_{i}-{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\alpha_{\ell}{\left(1+\Gamma_{i\ell}\right)}}-\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}{\left(\gamma_{n}D_{\ell}-\alpha_{n}{\mathcal{D}}_{n}\right)},\end{aligned}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\left(h\eta_{i}\right)}_{t}&+{\left[hb_{m}\alpha_{i}+hu_{m}\eta_{i}-ha_{m}\beta_{i}-hv_{m}\gamma_{i}+h\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}A_{i\ell n}{\left(\alpha_{\ell}\eta_{n}-\beta_{\ell}\gamma_{n}\right)}\right]}_{x}\\[4.0pt]
&=b_{m}D_{i}-v_{m}{\mathcal{D}}_{i}-{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\beta_{\ell}{\left(1+\Gamma_{i\ell}\right)}}-\sum_{\ell,n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}{\left(\eta_{n}D_{\ell}-\beta_{n}{\mathcal{D}}_{n}\right)},\end{aligned}

(3.18)

where
i=1,\dots M
, the notation
\displaystyle u\Big|_{\zeta=1}=u_{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\alpha_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(1)
, and similarly for
v,\ a,
and
b
evaluated at
\zeta=1
, and

\displaystyle A_{i\ell n}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}\phi_{\ell}\phi_{n}\ d\zeta,\qquad B_{i\ell n}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}^{\prime}{\left(\int_{0}^{\zeta}\phi_{\ell}\partial\hat{\zeta}\right)}\phi_{n}\partial\zeta,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i\ell}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}\ d\zeta,\qquad D_{i}={\left(h\alpha_{i}\right)}_{x}+{\left(h\beta_{i}\right)}_{y},\qquad{\mathcal{D}}_{i}={\left(h\gamma_{i}\right)}_{x}+{\left(h\eta_{i}\right)}_{y}.

The complete
M
th-order MRSWME above is a system of
4M+5
evolution equations in addition to the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field.
In the case that vertical deviations from the depth-averaged value are negligible, (
3.9
)–(
3.19
) simplifies to the the MRSW system often used in geophysical studies, and it is clear that
\alpha_{i},\ \beta_{i},\ \gamma_{i},
and
\eta_{i}
will remain zero for all time if all moments are zero initially.
In addition, the Coriolis force plays a role in the velocity moment equations for
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
, implying the rotational source influences the vertical profile.
This is unlike the gravitational forces (magneto-hydrostatic pressure and bottom topography source), which do not enter the moment evolution equations.

Within the derivation of the MRSWME, the structure of the
\gamma_{\ell},\eta_{\ell}
pieces in the fluxes of (
3.9
)–(
3.18
) significantly parallel those of
\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{\ell}
.
Even the vertical coupling contribution of the magnetic field in (
B.4
) is identical in form to that of velocity vertical coupling contribution in (
B.3
), if the zero-divergence magnetic field equation (
3.10
) is applied.
However, in opting to keep this term (and all other source terms scaled by
[(ha_{m})_{x}+(hb_{m})_{y}]
), we see the appearance of the so-called Godunov-Powell source; see, e.g.,

[
16
,
38
,
39
]
;
which, much like that of MHD or MRSW, helps recover a missing eigenvector of the MRSWME.
This additional source has proven quite beneficial in numerical approximations of MHD systems; see, e.g.,

[
4
,
38
,
39
,
51
]
;
and is a key component of the discretizations presented in §
4.1
and §
4.2
.

As shown for the shallow water moment equations in
[
29
]
, one downside of the moment approximation is the loss of global hyperbolicity for
M\geq 2
.
We discuss how this takes effect in the magnetic extension in §
3.4.2
.

3.4
MRSWME examples

In this section, we present two examples of the model hierarchy shown in (
3.9
)–(
3.18
).
For the sake of simplicity, we only present the one-dimensional system in the
y
-direction.
Note that, due to the Coriolis force further coupling the
u
and
v
equations, we may not simply cancel out the
x
-direction velocity and magnetic field—even if
u
and
a
are initially zero.

3.4.1
Zeroth order system: magnetic rotating shallow water equations

Using
M=0
and thus assuming a constant profile in the vertical direction for velocity and magnetic field, all higher order moment evolutions drop from the system, resulting in the 5-equation model

\begin{pmatrix}h\\
hu_{m}\\
hv_{m}\\
ha_{m}\\
hb_{m}\end{pmatrix}_{t}+\begin{pmatrix}hv_{m}\\
hu_{m}v_{m}-ha_{m}b_{m}\\
hv_{m}^{2}-hb_{m}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}\\
ha_{m}v_{m}-hb_{m}u_{m}\\
0\end{pmatrix}_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
fhv_{m}-a_{m}(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-fhu_{m}-ghZ_{y}-b_{m}(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-u_{m}(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-v_{m}(hb_{m})_{y}\end{pmatrix},

(3.20)

paired with the 1-D divergence-free condition
(hb_{m})_{y}=0
.
These are known as the shallow water MHD or MRSW equations.
Due to the addition of the Godunov-Powell source term, this system is strongly hyperbolic for all
h>0
. The characteristic wave speeds are given by

\lambda_{1}=v_{m},\qquad\lambda_{2,3}=v_{m}\pm{\left|b_{m}\right|},\qquad\lambda_{4,5}=v_{m}\pm\sqrt{b_{m}^{2}+gh},

(3.21)

and known as the material wave, Alfvén waves, and magnetogravity waves, respectively.
In §
5
, we will see how vertical profiles change the solutions of these traveling waves.

3.4.2
First order system

Taking
M=1
in system (
3.9
)–(
3.18
) implies linear velocity and magnetic field profiles, giving the first-order MRSWME consisting of 9 equations:

\begin{pmatrix}h\\
hu_{m}\\
hv_{m}\\
ha_{m}\\
hb_{m}\\
h\alpha_{1}\\
h\beta_{1}\\
h\gamma_{1}\\
h\eta_{1}\end{pmatrix}_{t}+\begin{pmatrix}\textstyle hv_{m}\\
\textstyle hu_{m}v_{m}+\frac{1}{3}h\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}-ha_{m}b_{m}-\frac{1}{3}h\gamma_{1}\eta_{1}\\
\textstyle hv_{m}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}h\beta_{1}^{2}-hb_{m}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}h\eta_{1}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\\
\textstyle ha_{m}v_{m}+\frac{1}{3}h\beta_{1}\gamma_{1}-hb_{m}u_{m}-\frac{1}{3}h\alpha_{1}\eta_{1}\\
0\\
hu_{m}\beta_{1}+hv_{m}\alpha_{1}-ha_{m}\eta_{1}-hb_{m}\gamma_{1}\\
2hv_{m}\beta_{1}-2hb_{m}\eta_{1}\\
ha_{m}\beta_{1}+hv_{m}\gamma_{1}-hb_{m}\alpha_{1}-hu_{m}\eta_{1}\\
0\end{pmatrix}_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
fhv_{m}-(a_{m}-\gamma_{1})(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-fhu_{m}-ghZ_{y}-(b_{m}-\eta_{1})(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-(u_{m}-\alpha_{1})(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-(v_{m}-\beta_{1})(hb_{m})_{y}\\
fh\beta_{1}+u_{m}(h\beta_{1})_{y}-a_{m}(h\eta_{1})_{y}-2\gamma_{1}(hb_{m})_{y}\\
-fh\alpha_{1}+v_{m}(h\beta_{1})_{y}-b_{m}(h\eta_{1})_{y}-2\eta_{1}(hb_{m})_{y}\\
a_{m}(h\beta_{1})_{y}-u_{m}(h\eta_{1})_{y}-2\alpha_{1}(hb_{m})_{y}\\
b_{m}(h\beta_{1})_{y}-v_{m}(h\eta_{1})_{y}-2\beta_{1}(hb_{m})_{y}\end{pmatrix},

(3.22)

which is again paired with the 1-D divergence-free condition
(hb_{m})_{y}=0
.
Looking into the hyperbolicity of the system, we see that five of the nine wave speeds have a simple closed form:

\lambda_{1}=v_{m}-\beta_{1},\qquad\lambda_{2,3}=v_{m}-b_{m}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}{\left|\beta_{1}-\eta_{1}\right|},\qquad\lambda_{4,5}=v_{m}+b_{m}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}{\left|\beta_{1}+\eta_{1}\right|}.

The remaining four eigenvalues of the flux Jacobian come from finding the roots
\widetilde{\lambda}
of the quartic

\widetilde{\lambda}^{4}-3{\left(\frac{3b_{m}^{2}}{b_{m}^{2}+gh}+1+3\widetilde{\beta}_{1}^{2}+\widetilde{\eta}_{1}^{2}\right)}\widetilde{\lambda}^{2}-72b_{m}\widetilde{\beta}_{1}\widetilde{\eta}_{1}\widetilde{\lambda}+27b_{m}^{2}{\left(3-\widetilde{\beta}_{1}^{2}-3\widetilde{\eta}_{1}^{2}\right)}=0,

(3.23)

where

\widetilde{\lambda}=\lambda_{i}+3v_{m},\qquad\widetilde{\beta}_{1}=\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{b_{m}^{2}+gh}},\qquad\widetilde{\eta}_{1}=\frac{\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{b_{m}^{2}+gh}},

(3.24)

i=6,\dots,9
, are defined to increase readability.

The region in which the system (
3.22
) is hyperbolic, i.e., where the quartic in (
3.23
) has purely real roots, is presented in Figure
3.1
for
gh=1
.
Note that for the sake of visibility of the rest of the region, the value
b_{m}=0
is omitted, as the system (
3.22
) is globally hyperbolic when
b_{m}=0
.
In addition, note that the colors in Figure
3.1
are present purely to provide depth to the 3-D region.
In comparison to the shallow water moment equations of
[
29
]
, which is globally hyperbolic for
M=1
, the MRSWME loses
global
hyberbolicity for any
M>0
.
This is due to the introduction of a magnetic field, rather than a magnetic field vertical profile, which can be seen in Figure
3.1
along the slice
\widetilde{\eta}=0
.

This loss of global hyperbolicity is potentially problematic, as it can result in unmitigated growth of linear instabilities.
The breakdown of hyperbolicity is a well known challenge in the contexts of moment models; see e.g.,
[
49
]
where this arises in moment models for the Boltzmann equation.
However, all tests presented in the §
5
are either in the hyperbolic region, or barely outside of it (i.e.,
\mathfrak{Im}(\lambda)/\mathfrak{Re}(\lambda)\ll 1
).
For those cases that do produce eigenvalues with a relatively small complex portion, we project these eigenvalues back into the real plane, and do not see any instabilities arise.

For problems with significant breakdown in hyperbolicity (i.e.,
\mathfrak{Im}(\lambda)/\mathfrak{Re}(\lambda)
larger than some threshold), there are several different techniques proposed to treat potential issues; see, e.g.,
[
12
,
31
,
28
,
27
]
.The numerical treatment of such cases where hyperbolicity is lost will be considered in future work on the MRSWME.

4.1.1
Path-conservative central upwind scheme

Following that of
[
5
]
, we assume that the numerical solution, realized in terms of its cell averages

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t)\approx\frac{1}{\Delta y}\int\displaylimits_{C_{j}}\bm{U}(y,t)\ dy,

is available at time
t
.
Here, we introduce a uniform Cartesian mesh with finite volume cells
C_{j}=[y_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}},y_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}]
with
y_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}-y_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}}=\Delta y
,
j=1,\dots,N_{y}
.
Note we will omit the time dependence in the semi-discretization for the sake of brevity.

To evolve the cell averages
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}
in time, we extend the Path-Conservative Central-Upwind (PCCU) scheme introduced in
[
3
]
to the system (
4.1
)–(
4.5
), resulting in the following semi-discretization for the MRSWME:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta y}{\left[\bm{{\cal G}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{{\cal G}}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{Q}_{j}-\frac{s_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}}{s_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-s_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}}\,\bm{Q}_{\bm{\Upsilon},{j-\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}}{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}}\,\bm{Q}_{\bm{\Upsilon},{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\right]},

(4.6)

where

\bm{{\cal G}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\bm{G}\big(\bm{U}^{\rm N}_{j}\big)-s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\bm{G}\big(\bm{U}^{\rm S}_{j+1}\big)}{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}}+\frac{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}}{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}}\left(\bm{U}^{\rm S}_{j+1}-\bm{U}^{\rm N}_{j}\right),

(4.7)

are the CU numerical fluxes from
[
30
]
,
\bm{U}_{j}^{\rm N,S}
denote the reconstructed right and left cell interface point values of cell
C_{j}
, respectively (see §
4.1.2
),
s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}
denote the one-sided local speeds of propagation, estimated by using the maximum and minimum eigenvalues of the matrix
J(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{G}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})-Q(\bm{U})
:

\displaystyle s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}=\max{\left\{\lambda_{i}{\left(J(\bm{U}_{j}^{\rm N})\right)},\lambda_{i}{\left(J(\bm{U}_{j+1}^{\rm S})\right)},0\right\}},

(4.8)

\displaystyle s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}=\min{\left\{\lambda_{i}{\left(J(\bm{U}_{j}^{\rm N})\right)},\lambda_{i}{\left(J(\bm{U}_{j+1}^{\rm S})\right)},0\right\}},

for
i=1,\dots,5+4M
, where
M
is the number of moments, and
\bm{Q}_{j},\ \bm{Q}_{\bm{\Upsilon},{j+\frac{1}{2}}}
denote the discretization of the nonconservative products appearing on the right hand side of (
4.1
) (see §
4.1.3
).

Since (
4.6
) is a system of ODEs, we must numerically integrate in time via an appropriate ODE solver.
In the reported numerical results presented in §
5
, we opt for the explicit three-stage third-order strong stability preserving (SSP) Runge-Kutta (RK) method; see, for example,

[
18
,
17
]
;
in which we meet the CFL condition

\Delta t\leq\nu\frac{\Delta y}{\max\limits_{j}{\left\{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+},-s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\right\}}},

(4.9)

with CFL number
\nu\leq 0.5
, to ensure stability.

4.1.2
Reconstruction

To compute the cell interface point values
\bm{U}_{j}^{\rm N,S}
, we use a conservative piecewise linear reconstruction:

\widetilde{\bm{U}}(y)=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+{\left(\bm{U}_{y}\right)}_{j}(y-y_{j}),\qquad y\in C_{j},

(4.10)

resulting in the following right and left cell interface values, respectively:

\bm{U}_{j}^{\rm N}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}{\left(\bm{U}_{y}\right)}_{j},\qquad\bm{U}_{j}^{\rm S}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}{\left(\bm{U}_{y}\right)}_{j}.

(4.11)

To avoid oscillations, the slopes
{\left(\bm{U}_{y}\right)}_{j}
are computed using a nonlinear limiter; we opt for the generalized minmod limiter (see, e.g.,

[
32
,
34
,
47
]
):

{\left(\bm{U}_{y}\right)}_{j}={\textrm{minmod}}{\left(\theta\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{\Delta y},\ \frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1}}{2\Delta y},\ \theta\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1}}{\Delta y}\right)},

(4.12)

for
\theta\in[1,2]
, and

\mbox{minmod}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots)&&\mbox{if}\penalty\ z_{i}>0,\penalty\ \forall i,\\
&\max(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots)&&\mbox{if}\penalty\ z_{i}<0,\penalty\ \forall i,\\
&0&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.13)

is applied component-wise.
Note that, in general, additional treatment must be used to implement the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field.
However, in the 1-D case, the divergence-free condition implies
hb_{m}
is constant for all time.
Therefore, if
hb_{m}
is constant initially, then the reconstruction (
4.11
) with limiter (
4.12
) will preserve the divergence-free constraint for all time.

\displaystyle\int\limits_{y_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}^{y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\widetilde{\chi}(y)}{\widetilde{h}(y)}\widetilde{\psi}(y)_{y}\,dy

4.2
Scheme for MRSW reference system

The 2-D MRSW reference system stems from that in (
2.26
)–(
2.31
), but is now reduced to only have
y
- and
\zeta
-spatial dependence.
This reduction is applied so that it represents the expected solution of the 1-D MRSWME (which is spatially dependent on
y
but reduces the
\zeta
-dependence with a polynomial expansion).

For computational purposes, we additionally make two adjustments to the
y\zeta
-reduced form of (
2.26
)–(
2.31
): (i) we replace
v_{m}
with
\omega
in the height balance (
2.26
) using (
2.23
); and (ii) we include the Godunov-Powell source term for purposes of numerically treating the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field.
Directly using the reference system divergence condition in (
2.27
), the Godunov-Powell source would be associated to
(hb_{m})_{y}
, but this does not account for the the
\zeta
-dependence.
Alternatively, we also replace
(hb_{m})_{y}
using condition in (
2.24
); that is, we substitute in
(hb_{m})_{y}=(h\tilde{b})_{y}+(h{\mathcal{C}})_{\zeta}=0
for the divergence-free condition.
Applying these modifications to the reference system (
2.26
)–(
2.31
), omitting tildes for the scaled variables, and rewriting in vector form, the 2-D MRSW reference system used for numerical experiments reads

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}+\bm{H}(\bm{U})_{\zeta}=\bm{Q}(\bm{U}){\left[(hb)_{y}+(h{\mathcal{C}})_{\zeta}\right]}+\bm{S}(\bm{U}),

(4.17)

where
\bm{U}=(h,hu,hv,ha,hb)^{\top}
,

\bm{G}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}hv\\
huv-hab\\
\textstyle hu^{2}+\frac{g}{2}h^{2}-hb^{2}\\
hav-hbu\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bm{H}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}h\omega\\
hu\omega-ha{\mathcal{C}}\\
hv\omega-hb{\mathcal{C}}\\
ha\omega-hu{\mathcal{C}}\\
hb\omega-hv{\mathcal{C}}\end{pmatrix},

(4.18)

are the fluxes in the
y
- and
\zeta
-directions, respectively,
\bm{Q}(\bm{U})=-(a,b,u,v)^{\top}
is the only non-zero vector of the full nonconservative matrix
Q(\bm{U})
, and
\bm{S}(\bm{U})=(0,fhv,-fhu-ghZ_{y},0,0)^{\top}
is the source term.
The system (
4.17
)–(
4.18
) is paired with the updated
y\zeta
divergence-free condition
(hb)_{y}+(h{\mathcal{C}})_{\zeta}=0
, and is closed by the definitions of
\omega
and
{\mathcal{C}}
in (
2.21
)–(
2.22
).

4.2.1

Again following the scheme in

]

\bm{{\mathcal{G}}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

[

\bm{U}_{j,k}^{\rm N,S,U,D}

),

and

\displaystyle s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}

\displaystyle s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}

\displaystyle s_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}

In addition,
\bm{Q}_{j,k}^{(y)}

\bm{Q}_{j,k}^{(\zeta)}
,
\bm{Q}_{\Upsilon,{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{(y)}

\bm{Q}_{\Upsilon,j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\zeta)}

and
\omega_{j,k},\ \omega_{j,k}^{\rm U,D}
and

\omega

{\mathcal{C}}
from (

), (

), respectively (see §

To advance the system of ODEs in (

Similarly to that of the MRSWME, the numerical results presented in §

[

\Delta t\leq\min{{\left(\frac{\nu\Delta y}{\max\limits_{j,k}{\left\{s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+},-s_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\right\}}},\frac{\nu\Delta\zeta}{\max\limits_{j,k}{\left\{s_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+},-s_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\right\}}}\right)}},

(4.21)

for CFL number

4.2.2
Reconstruction

The reconstruction for most variables in (
4.17
)–(
4.18
) is a 2-D extension of that presented in §
4.1.2
.
To compute the cell interface point values we use the conservative piecewise linear reconstruction

\widetilde{\bm{U}}(y,\zeta)=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+{\left(\bm{U}_{y}\right)}_{j,k}(y-y_{j})+{\left(\bm{U}_{\zeta}\right)}_{j,k}(\zeta-\zeta_{k}),\qquad y,\zeta\in C_{j,k}.

(4.22)

This results in the following cell interface values:

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{\rm N}_{j,k}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k},\quad\bm{U}^{\rm S}_{j,k}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k},

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{\rm U}_{j,k}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta\zeta}{2}(\bm{U}_{\zeta})_{j,k},\quad\bm{U}^{\rm D}_{j,k}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}-\frac{\Delta\zeta}{2}(\bm{U}_{\zeta})_{j,k},

where the slopes
(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k}
are computed using the generalized minmod limiter in (
4.12
), and the slopes
(\bm{U}_{\zeta})_{j,k}
are computed analogously in the
\zeta
-direction:

{\left(\bm{U}_{\zeta}\right)}_{j,k}={\textrm{minmod}}{\left(\theta\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}{\Delta\zeta},\ \frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k-1}}{2\Delta\zeta},\ \theta\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k-1}}{\Delta\zeta}\right)},

(4.23)

where the minmod function is defined in (
4.13
).

Unlike the 1-D MRSWME, the 2-D reference system now requires additional treatment to implement the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field
(hb)_{y}+(h{\mathcal{C}})_{\zeta}=0
.
To ensure a locally-divergence-free scheme, we use the approach of
[
5
]
;
that is, we (i) write an evolution equation for
B=(hb)_{y}
by differentiating the
hb
equation in (
4.17
)–(
4.18
), which reads

B_{t}+{\left(vB-h{\mathcal{C}}v_{\zeta}\right)}_{y}+{\left(\omega B+hb\omega_{y}\right)}_{\zeta}=0;

(ii) evolve
B
simultaneously with (
4.17
)–(
4.18
) using the same CU fluxes presented in (
4.7
) and (
4.20
), where we compute
v_{\zeta}
and
\omega_{y}
using the generalized minmod limiter slopes
(v_{\zeta})_{j,k}
from (
4.23
) and
(\omega_{y})_{j,k}
from (
4.12
);
and (iii) use the evolved values
B_{j,k}
for the
y
-direction slopes within the reconstruction of
hb
.
However, we also cannot reconstruct
hb
using
B_{j,k}
without including some limiter to avoid oscillations.
Therefore, to reconstruct
hb
in the
y
-direction, we use

(hb)^{\rm N}_{j,k}=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$hb$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}B_{j,k}\sigma_{j,k},\qquad(hb)^{\rm S}_{j,k}=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$hb$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{j,k}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}B_{j,k}\sigma_{j,k},

(4.24)

with

\sigma_{j,k}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min\bigg\{1,\frac{((hb)_{y})_{j,k}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}\bigg\}&&\mbox{if}\penalty\ ((hb)_{y})_{j,k}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}>0,\\
&0&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

where
((hb)_{y})_{j,k}
is computed using the generalized minmod limiter in (
4.12
).

Since we only have the integral equation in (
2.22
) for
h{\mathcal{C}}
, as opposed to an evolution equation, we cannot exactly follow discretization in
[
5
]
for this term.
We share the details on how
(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}^{\rm U,D}
are computed in §
4.2.4
.

Remark 4.1
.

Notice that, to ensure a second-order method for
hb
, the evolution of
B
only needs to be first-order accurate. This is because
B
is utilized in the reconstruction (
4.24
) and always multiplied by
\Delta y
.

4.2.3

Discretization of nonconservative products

The discretization of the nonconservative terms
\bm{Q}(\bm{U}){\left[(hb)_{y}+(h{\mathcal{C}})_{\zeta}\right]}
in (
4.17
) follow very closely to that presented in §
4.1.3
.
Following
[
3
,
9
]
, we compute the terms
\bm{Q}_{j,k}^{(y)}
,
\bm{Q}_{j,k}^{(\zeta)}
,
\bm{Q}_{\Upsilon,{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{(y)}
, and
\bm{Q}_{\Upsilon,j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\zeta)}
in (

) by evaluating the following integrals exactly:

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{j,k}^{(y)}=\int\limits_{y_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}^{y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{Q}{\left(\widetilde{\bm{U}}(y,\zeta_{k})\right)}(\widetilde{hb})(y,\zeta_{k})_{y}\,dy,\qquad\bm{Q}_{\Upsilon,{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{(y)}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\bm{Q}{\left(\bm{U}{\left(\Upsilon_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(s)\right)}\right)}\Upsilon_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\prime}(s)\,ds,

(4.25)

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{j,k}^{(\zeta)}=\int\limits_{\zeta_{k-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\zeta_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{Q}{\left(\widetilde{\bm{U}}(y_{j},\zeta)\right)}(\widetilde{h{\mathcal{C}}})(y_{j},\zeta)_{\zeta}\,d\zeta,\qquad\bm{Q}_{\Upsilon,j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\zeta)}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\bm{Q}{\left(\bm{U}{\left(\Upsilon_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}(s)\right)}\right)}\Upsilon_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\prime}(s)\,ds,

where
\bm{Q}(\bm{U})=-(a,b,u,v)^{\top}
,
\widetilde{\bm{U}}(y,\zeta)
is the global interpolant defined in (
4.22
), and

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(s)

\displaystyle=(hb)^{\rm N}_{j,k}+s[hb]_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\qquad

\displaystyle[hb]_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=(hb)_{j+1,k}^{\rm S}-(hb)_{j,k}^{\rm N},

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=(h{\mathcal{C}})^{\rm U}_{j,k}+s[h{\mathcal{C}}]_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\qquad

\displaystyle[h{\mathcal{C}}]_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k+1}^{\rm D}-(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}^{\rm U},

are linear paths connecting states
(hb)_{j,k}^{\rm N}
to
(hb)_{j+1,k}^{\rm S}
and
(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}^{\rm U}
to
(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k+1}^{\rm D}
, respectively.

Notice that, due to the definition of
\bm{Q}(\bm{U})
, all four integrals in (
4.25
) also fall under the general form in (
4.16
).
We thus omit the final evaluations of (
4.25
) for the sake of brevity.

4.2.4

Discretization of vertical coupling terms

To discretize the vertical coupling term corresponding to velocity
\omega
at each time step, we follow that in
[
29
]
; that is, we compute vertical cell interface values such that the
\omega
discretization is continuous across cells.
Starting from the definition in (
2.21
), we obtain the discretization of
\omega_{j,k}^{\rm U}=\omega_{j,k+1}^{\rm D}\equiv\omega_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
by
(i) evaluating (
2.21
) at
\zeta_{k+\frac{1}{2}}
, noticing that the lower bound is
\zeta_{\frac{1}{2}}=0
;
(ii) apply midpoint rule to (
2.21
); and
(iii) use the
y
-direction numerical fluxes (
4.7
) of the
h
equation (
2.26
) to compute
((hv)_{y})_{j,k}
and the corresponding vertical average
((hv_{m})_{y})_{j}
.
Doing so gives the following discretization of the velocity vertical coupling term:

\omega_{j,k}^{\rm U}=\omega_{j,k+1}^{\rm D}=\frac{2}{h_{j,k+1}^{\rm D}+h_{j,k}^{\rm U}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\Delta\zeta{\left({\left((hv_{m})_{y}\right)}_{j}-\frac{1}{\Delta y}{\left[{\mathcal{G}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ell}^{(h)}-{\mathcal{G}}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},\ell}^{(h)}\right]}\right)},

(4.26)

where

{\left((hv_{m})_{y}\right)}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\zeta}}\frac{\Delta\zeta}{\Delta y}{\left[{\mathcal{G}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{(h)}-{\mathcal{G}}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}^{(h)}\right]}.

(4.27)

The vertical coupling for the magnetic field must be computed differently since it contributes to the 2-D divergence-free condition
(hb)_{y}+(h{\mathcal{C}})_{\zeta}=0
.
To ensure the preservation of the local divergence-free condition, the reconstruction of
h{\mathcal{C}}
in the
\zeta
-direction must be consistent with that of
hb
in the
y
-direction.
Therefore, to get the magnetic field vertical coupling approximation, we
(i) take an average of both sides of (
2.22
) through integration over cell
C_{j,k}
;
(ii) apply trapezoid rule to the averaging integral;
(iii) apply midpoint rule to the remaining integrals originating from (
2.22
); and
(iv) divide by
h_{j,k}
and use the reconstruction slope from (
4.24
) for the computation of
[(hb)_{y}]_{j,k}
.
Doing so gives the following cell-centered point value of the magnetic field vertical coupling:

(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}=-{\left(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{j,k}B_{j,k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sigma_{j,\ell}B_{j,\ell}\right)}.

(4.28)

Then, to ensure the reconstruction of
(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}
within the divergence-free condition is consistent, we simply use
-[(hb)_{y}]_{j,k}
as the slope, in turn giving the cell interface point values of the magnetic field coupling:

(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}^{\rm U}=(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta\zeta}{2}{\left(-\sigma_{j,k}B_{j,k}\right)},\qquad(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}^{\rm D}=(h{\mathcal{C}})_{j,k}-\frac{\Delta\zeta}{2}{\left(-\sigma_{j,k}B_{j,k}\right)}.

(4.29)

Note that the discretizations of
\omega
in (
4.26
)–(
4.27
) and
{\mathcal{C}}
in (
4.28
)–(
4.29
) are updated in each stage of the time update.

Numerical Simulations

In this section, we demonstrate the viability of the 1-D MRSWME equations in comparison to the corresponding 2-D reference solution through four numerical tests.
The implementation of these numerical experiments has been made publicly available
[
40
]
.
All simulations use a CFL number of
\nu=0.45
in (
4.9
) and (
4.21
), gravitational constant
g=1
, and, unless otherwise stated, a generalized minmod limiting parameter
\theta=1.3
.

As discussed in §
3.4.2
, Examples 2-4 of the moment model experiments do indeed produce complex eigenvalues; however, the complex portions are several orders of magnitude smaller than those of their real parts. Therefore, in these simulations, we project the eigenvalues to the real plane to compute the wave speeds in (
4.8
).
We plan to address the difficulties regarding hyperbolicity regularization in future work.

Example 1—Small hump without magnetic field

While fairly simple to see within (
4.1
)–(
4.5
), we first confirm numerically that the MRSWME reduce to the SWME when a magnetic field is not present.
Furthermore, this example also tests the reference solution and the viability of the proposed methods on an existing test case.
To do so, we consider the smooth bump initial conditions suggested in
[
29
]

h(y,0)=1+\exp{\left(3\cos{\left(\pi(y+0.5)\right)}-4\right)},\qquad v(y,0,\zeta)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}&{\rm constant\ case},\\
\frac{1}{2}\zeta&{\rm linear\ case},\\

First, we present the reference solutions of

5.1

Geostrophic adjustment

For the remaining examples, we study the dynamical regimes of magneto-geostrophic adjustment problems, and the influence of vertical velocity and magnetic field profiles on these problems.
The (magneto-)geostrophic adjustment process describes the evolution toward the corresponding (magneto-)geostrophic equilibrium—which is of particular importance in astrophysical and geophysical applications; see, e.g.,
[
41
,
48
,
54
]
.
The steady-state magneto-geostrophic equilibria for the MRSW equations read

fu=-g(h+Z)_{y}+bb_{y},\qquad v=0.

In the absence of a magnetic field, it has been shown that any non-trivial initial condition evolves toward the state of corresponding geostrophic equilibrium through emission of inertia-gravity waves; see
[
55
]
and references therein.
However, the presence of the magnetic field makes the magneto-geostrophic adjustment process more complicated, as the additional equation for
a_{m}
adds a restriction on the equilibrium solution of
u_{m}
; see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.
More so, the addition of the vertical profiles complicates the (magneto-)geostrophic equilibria further, as expansion terms remain present in the equilibrium solution—even when taking
v=v_{m}=0
everywhere along the vertical profile.

Recall that the regimes near (magneto-)geostrophic equilibria are characterized by small Rossby and magnetic Rossby numbers

{\rm Ro}=\frac{\mathfrak{U}}{f\mathfrak{L}},\qquad{\rm Ro}_{m}=\frac{\mathfrak{B}}{f\mathfrak{L}},

where
\mathfrak{U}
and
\mathfrak{B}
are the scales of velocity and magnetic field, respectively, and
\mathfrak{L}
is the typical length scale of the motions under study.
In the following examples, we look at two cases: Example 3 considers a low Rossby number (
{\rm Ro}<1
and
{\rm Ro}_{m}<1
), and Example 4 considers a high Rossby number (
{\rm Ro}>1
and
{\rm Ro}_{m}>1
).
In addition, both cases will have an initial sinusoidal vertical profile in the
y
-velocity, allowing us to analyze the influence of this vertical profile on (i) the magneto-geostrophic solution; and (ii) how it introduces a vertical profile in the magnetic field.

Discussion

In this paper, we developed a novel 2-D reduced order model of the 3-D incompressible ideal MHD system in the context of magnetic rotating shallow flows.
This is done by representing the vertical profiles of the horizontal velocities and magnetic fields by a polynomial expansion, in which the polynomial coefficients are now only spatially dependent on the horizontal components.
By Galerkin projection onto polynomial test functions, i.e. ‘taking moments’, of the the vertically resolved system, we obtain evolution equations for these polynomial coefficients, thus closing the new system.
This hierarchical moment approximation, referred as the MRSWME, maintains non-constant vertical profiles of the horizontal velocities and magnetic fields, ergo increasing the predictive power of these models in comparison to standard shallow fluid approaches (i.e., the MRSW system), while staying within the computationally efficient 2-D framework corresponding to depth-integration.

Through this moment approximation, the new system gives rise to the Godunov-Powell source term—a nonconservative source dependent on the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field.
While theoretically zero, keeping this term proves fruitful in the numerical treatment of the magnetic field.
We take advantage of this naturally arising term to numerically treat the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field, which, after a direct extension of the method proposed in
[
5
]
, results in a second-order PCCU scheme that is stable in the magnetic field treatment and not dependent on Riemann problem solvers.

Using this method, we present four different numerical examples demonstrating the importance of non-constant vertical profiles of the horizontal magnetic field.
In particular, we present two magneto-geostrophic adjustment problems where we introduce sinusoidal vertical perturbation in the velocity, in which the depth-averaged conditions coincide with the MRSW examples from
[
6
]
.
Using the 2-D vertically-resolved MRSW reference system, we see that this perturbation has significant influence on the outward-propagating Alfvén waves, in addition to inducing a vertical profile on the magnetic field.
Furthermore, we show that the standard MRSW system does not properly capture these differences, while the MRSWME does so in an accurate manner.

While the MRSW moment approximation increases model accuracy, it unfortunately comes at the cost of losing global hyperbolicity of the system.
A well known challenge in moment models, this breakdown of hyperbolicity currently limits the capabilities of the presented numerical method.
Numerical approaches in cases where hyperbolicity is lost will be considered and developed in future work together with hyperbolic regularizations. Further research could consider the numerical preservation of steady-states or an entropy-preserving numerical scheme based on the derivation of a suitable energy equation.

Appendix A

Scaling and Dimensionless System

To non-dimensionalize the ideal incompressible MHD system in (
2.1
), we use the following scaling for the spatial dimensions:

\widehat{x}=\frac{x}{\ell_{0}},\quad\widehat{y}=\frac{y}{\ell_{0}},\quad\widehat{z}=\frac{z}{H}.

(A.1)

Since we are interested in free-surface shallow flow, the horizontal length scale
\ell_{0}
is much larger than the vertical length scale
H
, and thus
H/\ell_{0}=\varepsilon\ll 1
.
Furthermore, due to shallowness, the vertical velocity and magnetic field will be substantially smaller than their horizontal counterparts, and thus suggest the following scaling:

\widehat{u}=\frac{u}{u_{0}},\quad\widehat{v}=\frac{v}{u_{0}},\quad\widehat{w}=\frac{w}{\varepsilon u_{0}},\quad\widehat{a}=\frac{a}{u_{0}},\quad\widehat{b}=\frac{b}{u_{0}},\quad\widehat{c}=\frac{c}{\varepsilon u_{0}},

where
u_{0}
denotes the characteristic horizontal velocity.
Note again that
\bm{b}=(a,b,c)^{\top}
of (
2.1
) is in units of velocity, and hence is scaled by characteristic velocities as well.
Finally, we introduce a time scale given by the ratio of horizontal spatial and velocity scales, and assume the thermal pressure scales with the hydrostatic pressure with characteristic height
H

\widehat{t}=\frac{u_{0}}{\ell_{0}}t,\quad\widehat{p}=\frac{p}{\rho gH}.

(A.2)

Applying the scalings in (

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{\widehat{t}}+{\left[\widehat{u}^{2}+{\left(\frac{1}{\rm Fr^{2}}\widehat{p}+\frac{1}{2}{\left|\widehat{\bm{b}}\right|}^{2}\right)}-\widehat{a}^{2}\right]}_{\widehat{x}}+{\left[\widehat{u}\widehat{v}-\widehat{a}\widehat{b}\right]}_{\widehat{y}}+{\left[\widehat{u}\widehat{w}-\widehat{a}\widehat{c}\right]}_{\widehat{z}}=\frac{1}{\rm Ro}\widehat{v},

\displaystyle\ \boxed{\star}-\phi_{i}(1){\left(a_{m}+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}\phi_{n}(1)\right)}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}{\left(\phi_{i}\right)}_{\zeta}{\left(a_{m}+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}\phi_{n}\right)}{\left[{\left(h\int_{0}^{\zeta}a_{m}\ d\hat{\zeta}\right)}_{x}+{\left(h\int_{0}^{\zeta}b_{m}\ d\hat{\zeta}\right)}_{y}\right]}\ d\zeta,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \boxed{\star}-\phi_{i}(1){\left(a_{m}+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}\phi_{n}(1)\right)}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}

\displaystyle+{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\left[a_{m}\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}^{\prime}\ d\zeta+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}^{\prime}\phi_{n}\ d\zeta\right]}.

Notice that all remaining terms outside of
\boxed{\star}
contain the divergence-free condition of the magnetic field in (
2.27
), and thus are all theoretically zero.
However, as stated before, keeping these terms has proven beneficial in discretizations when having to manage the divergence-free condition, and therefore we will simplify these terms as well.
One can simplify the remaining integrals using integration by parts and orthogonality of the Legendre polynomials:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}^{\prime}\ d\zeta

\displaystyle=\phi_{i}(1),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}\phi_{n}\ d\zeta

\displaystyle=\phi_{i}(1)\phi_{n}(1)-\frac{1}{2i+1}-\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}\phi_{n}^{\prime}\ d\zeta.

In turn, this gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}{\left(ha{\mathcal{C}}\right)}_{\zeta}\ d\zeta=

\displaystyle\ \boxed{\star}-\phi_{i}(1){\left(a_{m}+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}\phi_{n}(1)\right)}{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}

\displaystyle+{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\left[a_{m}\phi_{i}(1)+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}{\left(\phi_{i}(1)\phi_{n}(1)-\frac{1}{2i+1}-\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}\phi_{n}^{\prime}\ d\zeta\right)}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \boxed{\star}-{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}{\left[\frac{1}{2i+1}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}{\left(1+\Gamma_{in}\right)}\right]},

where
\Gamma_{in}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\phi_{i}\phi_{n}^{\prime}d\zeta
.
Substituting in
\boxed{\star}
, we obtain the integral term involving the magnetic field vertical coupling:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}{\left(ha{\mathcal{C}}\right)}_{\zeta}\ d\zeta=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2i+1}\Bigg(a_{m}{\left[{\left(h\gamma_{i}\right)}_{x}+{\left(h\eta_{i}\right)}_{y}\right]}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}B_{i\ell n}\gamma_{n}{\left[{\left(h\gamma_{\ell}\right)}_{x}+{\left(h\eta_{\ell}\right)}_{y}\right]}

(B.4)

\displaystyle+{\left[{\left(ha_{m}\right)}_{x}+{\left(hb_{m}\right)}_{y}\right]}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\gamma_{\ell}{\left(1+\Gamma_{i\ell}\right)}\Bigg).

As discussed in §
3.3
, the last term is the influence of the Godunov-Powell source term on the evolution equations for the vertical profile coefficients.

Single crystal X-ray structurally characterized dual functioning oxovanadium(iv) complex of amide–imine derivative: studies on anti-diabetic and Th(iv) recognition properties
Authors: Prasenjit Mandal, Sangita Maji, Shuli Barik, Monoj Patra, Surjyo Jyoti Biswas, Debasis Das
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
An amide based imine derivative, 4-hydroxy-benzoic acid (phenyl(pyridine-2-yl)methanone-hydrazide) (PHP) forms a µ-oxo-bridged dinuclear oxovanadium(iv) complex (PHPV), the structure of which is authenticated by single crystal XRD analysis. The antidiabetic potential of PHPV has been established upon its oral treatment on mice, suffering from streptozotocin-induced diabetes. On the other hand, PHPV is also useful for selective fluorescence detection of thorium (Th4+) via a metal-ion displacement protocol where Th4+ displaces V(iv) from PHPV as established by spectroscopic studies. The lowest detection limit (LOD) of Th4+ is 12.27 nM while the displacement binding constant for Th4+ is 6.7 × 104 M−1.

Introduction

Conformational diversity, stability, and polarity of amide functionality has attracted special attention in biological and pharmaceutical chemistry,1,2 that allows its ∼25% stake in existing drugs.3,4 Oxo-vanadium complexes are important because of their dynamic role in biochemical activities like antidiabetic,5–8 insulin mimicking activities,9–11 hydrocarbon oxidation,12 nitrogen fixation,13 halo peroxidation,14 phosphorylation15 and epoxidation.16,17

On the other hand, streptozotocin (STZ) is commonly used to induce diabetes, and thereafter investigate the effect of various antidiabetic medicines on an induced diabetogenic animal model. It is extracted from Streptomyces achromogenes, a Gram-positive soil bacterium, a persistent diabetogenic chemical. The STZ toxicity is caused by preferential absorption into β cells via GLUT2, a low-affinity glucose transporter in the plasma membrane. The STZ is hydrophilic as a result of the glucose moiety replacement. Because phospholipid bilayer plasma membranes are hydrophobic, STZ's hydrophilic character limits its unhindered passage through them.18 Lower toxicity of the vanadyl core, compared to vanadate, better affinity for membrane transporter and blood, shorter body retention time and easy excretion via kidney are the causes of more intensive biological studies using V(iv) species. STZ's 2-deoxy glucose moiety, which is structurally identical to glucose, promotes preferential absorption into β cells via GLUT-2. There is a considerable gender variation in STZ sensitivity among mice. Male mice are more likely to develop STZ-induced diabetes.19,20 Male mice given equal amounts develop severe hyperglycemia, whereas female mice show little to no reaction. This variance can be substantial owing to estradiol's ability to protect pancreatic β cells from oxidative stress-induced apoptosis may explain females' lower sensitivity.21 The experimental development of diabetes in mice with STZ followed by its treatment with vanadium compounds is a potential research paradigm to study diabetic and therapeutic potential of vanadium compounds. This is a useful model as it closely resembles the clinical and metabolic aspects of real diabetes, providing insights into the disease's course. Therefore, the present study has undertaken to check the potentiality of the new dinuclear oxovanadium(iv) complex to modulate the STZ induced diabetogenic condition in mice, considering several biomarkers of toxicity and, employing histo-pathological approach.22–25

Another important aspect of contemporary research includes selective fluorescence recognition of actinide, relatively less studied, compared to transition metal ions. Thorium (Th4+), a natural radioactive and carcinogenic element, present in soil, water, rock, plant, and animals at low abundance. Prolonged exposure to thorium may cause lung, liver diseases, and bone cancer.26–30 Probable sources of thorium to environment includes mining, processing and accidental release from nuclear power plants.31,32 Hence, it is highly relevant and time-worthy to detect and determine thorium at trace level.

An instantaneous, non-invasive, inexpensive and simple method like fluorescence is always preferable33 over other pioneering techniques namely, chromatography, electrochemistry, inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), atomic absorption, and synchrotron radiation X-ray spectrometry for determination of Th4+. In addition, metal ion displacement approach enhances selectivity of fluorescence detection of cations over free organic probe.34–37 All these inspired us to take the initiative for selective recognition of trace level Th4+ following metal ion displacement protocol, utilizing the new dinuclear oxovanadium(iv) complex (PHPV). In this context, Th4+ assisted ratiometric fluorescence enhancement of the PHPVvia metal displacement protocol is a significant progress in sensory research too.

Experimental

Materials and physical measurements

High-purity HEPES buffer, 4-hydroxybenzoic acid, 2-benzoyl pyridine, hydrazine (NH2NH2) and thionyl chloride (SOCl2) have been purchased from SigmaAldrich (India) while reagent grade VOSO4 from Merck (India). Spectroscopic grade solvents are used. Milli-Q Millipore 18.2 MΩ cm−1 water is used as per requirement. Crystal structure of PHPV (CCDC No. 23690) is solved by standard direct methods using the SHELXS38 and refined by full-matrix least-squares method by SHELXL39 and within the OLEX2 (ref. 40) software. Significant crystal parameters, refinement data, bond lengths and angle etc. are presented in Table S1a and b (SI). Structural features and images are illustrated with Mercury software.41

Crystallographic materials are available at Cambridge Crystallographic Data Center (CCDC). Details about solvents, reagents and instruments used in this work are described as SI.

Synthesis

4-Hydroxybenzoicacid hydrazide (PHH)

Calculated amount of thionyl chloride is used to convert 4-hydroxy benzoic acid to corresponding acid chloride which is subsequently converted to 4-hydroxy benzoic acid hydrazide (PHH) (Scheme 1) using hydrazine hydrate. Details are available in the SI (Fig. S1–S4).

Synthesis of PHH, PHP, PHPV and PHPT.

4-Hydroxy-benzoic acid (phenyl(pyridine-2-yl)methanone-hydrazide) (PHP)

Mixture of PHH (152.15 mg, 1 mmol) and 2-benzoyl pyridine (183.21 mg, 1 mmol) in methanol is refluxed for 6 h and kept for slow evaporation to obtain the PHP at 76% yield. Its molecular formula is C19H15N3O2 (MW = 317.36). Detailed physical measurements have been described in the SI (Fig. S5–S8).

Dinuclear oxo-vanadium(iv) complex (PHPV)

Solution of VOSO4 in methanol (1 g, 6.13 mmol, 5 mL) is added to PHP (0.5 g, 1.57 mmol, 10 mL) in MeOH–DCM (1 : 1, v/v) media under stirring condition for 2 h (Scheme 1). Red crystals of PHPV are found after slow evaporation of the media and its structure is authenticated by SC-XRD analysis. The MS and FTIR spectra are provided in the SI (Fig. S9 and S10).

[PHPV-Th(iv)] adduct (PHPT)

To magnetically stirred MeOH/H2O (1 : 1, v/v) solution of PHPV, methanol solution of thorium nitrate is added drop-wise for 5 minutes. The mixture is kept for slow evaporation to yield PHPT. Detailed characterization that support the adduct formation is described in the SI (Fig. S11 and S12).

Methods and materials for anti-diabetic studies

Details about materials for antidiabetic studies are described in the SI.

In brief, 30 males (Swiss albino mice, Mus musculus) weighing ∼25 ± 1.2 g has been randomized into following groups:

Group I: normal mice without any treatment only food and water ad libitum (n = 5).

Group II: only 2.5% DMSO (conc. 275 mg kg−1, intragastrically).

Group III: only streptozotocin (STZ at a concentration of 65 mg per kg b. wt, diabetic group, intravenous, n = 5).

Group IV: STZ + PHPV (concentration 1.2 mg per kg b. wt., continued for 10 days, intragastrically, n = 5).

Group V: STZ + PHPV (concentration of 3.3 mg per kg.b.wt, continued for 10 days, intragastrically, n = 5).

Group VI: STZ + metformin (MTF, 200 mg per kg b.wt, intragastrically).

Streptozotocin (STZ dose are induced for 3 days intravenous) and PHPV is introduced intra-gastrically for 10 days and after 2 days of the final day streptozotocin dose administration. STZ treated blood glucose level is monitored everyday with the aid of Instant Blood Glucose Glucometer (ACCU-CHEK, Instant) while blood glucose level over 140 mg dL−1 is considered as diabetic. At the end of the experiment i.e. on 11th day, blood is quickly collected from mice, rested for 10 minutes for collection of serum and then the mice are sacrificed. Parts of pancreas were quickly dissected out and fixed as per routine histological procedure followed by double staining. On the other hand, part of liver is homogenized, centrifuged (Eppendorf 5804R, Merck) and the supernatant collected and kept at −80 °C (Thermo Fischer Scientific) until further use.

Estimation of SGPT and SGOT

The Bergmeyer and Bernt (1965) method was used to estimate SGPT and SGOT with a few minor modifications.42 For SGOT, 100 µL of tissue homogenate or serum sample was combined with 500 µL of the substrate solution l-aspartate and alpha ketoglutaric acid. The resulting mixture was then incubated for 60 minutes at 37 °C. Following the addition of 500 µL of dinitrophenol hydrazine (DNPH), 5 mL of 0.4 N NaOH was used to eliminate the mixture. The OD was recorded at 510 nm. 500 µL of the substrate solution (l-alanine and alpha ketoglutaric acid) was combined with 100 µL of tissue homogenate or serum for the SGPT analysis, and the mixture was incubated for 30 minutes at 37 °C. The optical density was measured at 510 nm against a suitable blank (Shimadzu UV1800), and the subsequent phases of this method were similar to those of SGOT.

Estimation of GGT assay

The gamma glutamyl transferase (GGT) was studied using Szasz's (1976) methodology.43 In summary, 1000 µL of working reagent (glycylglycine and gamma glutamyl p-nitroanilide) and 100 µL of serum were mixed together and the rate of increase in absorbance at 405 nm was measured using a Shimadzu UV1800 in comparison to an appropriate blank.

Estimation of blood glucose

The approach of Kaplan group (1984) is used to estimate blood glucose.44 In short, 1500 µL of glucose working reagent (phenol and 4-amino-antipyrine) are combined with 20 µL of serum to create a coloured quinoneimine dye. The absorbance is measured at 505 nm. Here, Glucose oxidase converts/oxidises glucose to gluconic acid and hydrogen peroxide in presence of Peroxidase. The released H2O2 couple with phenol and 4 amino-antipyrine to form coloured quinoneimine, the absorbance of which is measured at 505 nm and directly proportional to glucose concentration.

Estimation of cholesterol

Estimation of cholesterol was conducted by the kit procured from Coral diagnostics (India) and as per the manufacturer's instruction following the method of Allain et al. (1974).45 In brief, 1000 µL of enzyme reagent (containing cholesterase, cholesterol oxidase, peroxidase and 4-aminoantipyrine) was added to three cuvettes designated as blank (B), standard (S) and test (T). It was followed by addition of 10 µL of standard solution (200 µg mL−1) to S cuvette, 10 µL of serum to T cuvette and 10 µL of milliQ water to B cuvette. They were allowed to stand for 10 minutes at 37 °C. Subsequently, the absorbance of two cuvettes (test and standard) were noted against blank within 30 minutes at 505 nm.

Estimation of alkaline phosphatise

The estimation of alkaline phosphatase is conducted by the method of Kind and King (1954) with the kit procured from Arkray Co. Ltd and following manufacturer instruction.46 The estimation of the activity is done from liver and serum samples. In brief, 500 µL of working buffered substrate is mixed with 1500 µL of double distilled water and incubated for 3 minutes at 37 °C. Then 50 µL of the sample (liver homogenate and serum) is added to the above. After additions of each reagent, it is mixed well and measured the OD of reagent, blank, test and control against purified water at 550 nm.

Calculation of hepatic glycogen content

The glycogen content of the liver is calculated using the Hassid and Abraham (1957) method.47 In a nutshell, 50 mg of liver is dissected and digested in boiling KOH. After cooling, 800 µL of 95% ethanol is mixed together and heated until bubbles appeared. To obtain the residues, the tubes are cooled and centrifuged at 1000 rpm and the supernatant is rejected. The residues are washed with 5 mL distilled water followed by addition of 10 mL 0.2% anthrone reagent and maintained at ice temperature for multiple wash. Colour development is achieved by incubating tubes at 100 °C, and absorbance is measured at 620 nm (Shimadzu, UV-1800). Distilled water and glucose solutions are utilized for blank and standard tests.

Molecular docking of PHPV with GLUT4, GSK3 and PTP1B proteins

Three-dimensional crystal structures of the proteins under study, namely GLUT4, GSK3 and PTP1B are not available for Mus musculus. Therefore, for docking studies, we have utilized corresponding human (Homo sapiens) protein structures, retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB) [DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf1187]. GLUT4 (PDB ID: 7WSM, Chain: A) GSK3 (PDB ID: 1 R0E, Chain: A) and, PP1B (PDB ID: 2P8E, Chain: A).

To evaluate cross-species similarity, the amino acid sequences of GLUT4 from human (GenBank ID: AAA52569.1) and mouse (GenBank ID: BAB03251.1), the amino acid sequences of GSK3 from human (GenBank ID: KAI4031109.1) and mouse (GenBank ID: AAD39258.2) and the amino acid sequences of PTP1B from human (NCBI ID: NP_002697.1) and mouse (NCBI ID: NP_001152968.1) were retrieved from NCBI GenBank [DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1114] and compared. The analysis revealed a sequence identity of 94.65% between these two species for GLUT4, 99.04% for GSK3 and 91.19% for PTP1B respectively.

After retrieving the proteins and ligand (PHPV) structure, both were subjected to pre-processing before proceeding with the docking analysis.

To study the binding affinity between the PHPV and protein GLUT4, GSK3 and PP1B molecules, we have performed molecular docking employing AutoDock, using the Lamarckian Genetic Algorithm (LGA). A total of 10 GA runs were performed to achieve an optimal balance between computational efficiency and accuracy. The grid box dimensions are set to (200 × 120 × 200) Å3, with the grid center at coordinates (104.7, 102.7, 105.097) for GluT4, the grid box dimensions are set to (202 × 200 × 158) Å3, with the grid center at coordinates (54.044, 36.634, 41.912) for GSK3 and the grid box dimensions are set to (160 × 150 × 120) Å3, with the grid center at coordinates (12.425, 4.792, 35.941) for PP1B.

Statistical analysis

The significance test between the untreated control, DMSO control, only STZ and STZ+ antidiabetogenic drug treated series was carried out as per routine Student's t-test method.48 During biochemical estimation and histopathological studies, the observer was kept blinded of the animal whether it belonged to treated or control group, to remove any bias in observation so that uniformity can be maintained in scoring data of both treated and control sets of mice. The results were presented for each group as a mean ± SD. Dunnet's test and one-way ANOVA were used to determine any significant difference among the groups. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically noteworthy.

Anti-diabetic studies

SGPT test of liver homogenate

Serum Glutamic Pyruvic Transaminase (SGPT) activity in liver homogenate is an important indicator of liver function and health. Fig. S13 (SI) demonstrates the potential protective effect of the PHVP on the liver, as revealed from lower SGPT activity compared to the STZ group, known to induce liver damage. Thus, mice treated with 3.3 mg per kg body weight of PHPV has lower ALT enzyme level compared to those treated with 1.2 mg per kg body weight of PHPV.

SGPT test of blood serum

The SGPT activity in blood serum is also a measure of liver health, the higher value indicates potential liver damage/disease. Fig. S14 (SI) indicates that the mice treated with 3.3 mg per kg body weight of PHPV has lower ALT activity compared to those treated with 1.2 mg per kg body weight of PHPV.

SGOT of liver homogenate test

Serum glutamate-oxaloacetate transaminase (SGOT) test, also known as AST (aspartate aminotransferase) test, measures the level of this enzyme in the liver homogenate. Elevated SGOT level indicate liver damage/disease. Fig. S15 (SI) reveals that the liver damage due to Streptozotocin is reduced in terms of SGOT activity for both the dosages of PHPV (1.2 mg kg−1 and 3.3 mg kg−1), suggesting protective effect of PHPV against liver enzymes, however, 3.3 mg per kg dose of PHPV is more efficient to reduce the effect of streptozotocin.

Spectroscopic studies

Emission spectroscopic studies

It has been observed that in DMSO/H2O media, Th4+ assisted “turn on fluorescence” of PHPV (λex = 340 nm). In presence of Th4+, the emission intensity of PHPV changes in a ratio metric manner. Upon gradual increase of Th4+ concentration, emission intensity at 404 nm decreases while it increases significantly at 507 nm with an iso-emissive point at 450 nm (Fig. 6).

Changes in the emission spectra of PHPV upon gradual addition of Th4+.

Selectivity of PHPV towards Th4+ is significant, all other relevant cations viz. UO22+, Pr3+, Ce4+, Sn2+, Zr4+, Ga3+, Tl3+, In+, WO22+, La3+, Pr3+, Nd3+, Sm3+, Eu3+, Gd3+, Er3+, Tb3+, Ho3+, Lu3+, Yb3+, Y3+, Mn2+ almost remain silent as far as emission profile is concerned (Fig. 7). The optimum performance has been observed near physiological pH, 7.4 that indicates Th4+ triggers the fluorescence to maximum at neutral pH (Fig. S25, SI). Competitive experiment in presence of common cations indicate non-interference behaviour towards emission characteristics of PHPV-Th4+ system, which also hints about the selectivity of the recognition processes (Fig. S26, SI). The emission intensity vs. Th4+ concentration plot is sigmoidal (Fig. S27, SI). The lower limit of detection of Th4+ (towards PHPV) calculated from linier portion (Fig. S28, SI) of sigmoidal plot is found to be 12.27 nM. The corresponding binding constants for PHPV-Th4+ system is 6.74 × 104 M−1 determined with help of Benesi–Hildebrand equation (Fig. S29, SI). 1 : 1 stoichiometry for the adduct viz.PHPV-Th4+ is verified by Job's plot (Fig. S30, SI).

Emission spectra of PHPV in presence of different cations.

Absorption spectroscopic studies

The PHPV shows UV-Vis peak at 386 nm. A ratiometric spectral change is observed upon gradual addition of Th4+ to PHPV (Fig. 8), where the absorbance of 386 nm gradually decreases with associated blue shift to 365 nm (hypsochromic) while a new peak at 307 nm appears through an isobestic point at 332 nm, indicating the formation of new adduct. Selectivity studies indicate that other relevant cations do not affect the absorption spectra significantly (Fig. 9). Excitation spectra of PHPV is presented in Fig. S31 (SI).

Proposed sensing mechanism (Scheme 2)

Chelation of PHP with V(iv) results rigid structure of PHPV, responsible for its weak emission. Upon addition of Th4+, the ratiometric change in the emission spectra of PHPV with the appearance of a new peak at 507 nm is attributed to the displacement of V(iv) from PHPV by Th4+. This replacement fact is supported by the ESI-MS and FTIR spectra of the resulting system. The molecular ion peaks at m/z, 734.97 [M]+ and 740.97 [M + Li]+ indicate the presence of PHPT system. Job's plot corroborates 1 : 1 (mole ratio) formation of the product. Plausible degree of dissociation of PHPV as a function of pH has been monitored from the absorption spectroscopic studies (Fig. S32, SI) and presented in Table S1c (SI).

Molecular docking studies

The docking of PHPV and human GluT4 revealed the binding free energy of −12.05 kcal mol−1 (Fig. 10). The ligand was found to be interacting with the residues of GLUT4 are PHE38, ILE99, SER153, GLY154, PRO157, MET158, VAL160, GLY173, THR174, ASN176, GLN177, ILE180, VAL181, GLN298, GLN299, THR337, PHE395, GLU396, TRP404, ASN427, TRP428, and ASN431.

(A) PHPV bound GLUT4 (top view), (B) PHPV bound GLUT4 (side view), (C) 3D crystal structure of PHPV molecule, (D) 2D plot of PHPV interacting with GLUT4 protein's amino acid residues.

The interaction involves a combination of hydrophobic residues (PHE38, ILE99, PRO157, MET158, VAL160, ILE180, VAL181, PHE395, TRP404, and TRP428), which stabilize the ligand through hydrophobic and π-interactions, and polar residues (SER153, THR174, ASN176, GLN177, GLN298, GLN299, THR337, ASN427, and ASN431), which enable hydrogen bonding and improve binding specificity. Other stabilization is provided by GLY154 and GLY173 through van der Waals interactions, while GLU396 contributes electrostatic interactions. The extensive interaction network suggests that PHPV occupies the GLUT4 binding pocket in a favorable orientation, supporting the possibility that it may influence GLUT4 conformation or function.

The docking of PHPV and human GSK3 revealed the binding free energy, −9.43 kcal mol−1 (Fig. 11). The PHPV interacts with the GSK3 residues, namely LEU207, VAL208, ARG209, GLY210, GLU211, ASN213, GLU226, ALA231, ASP233, TYR234, THR235, GLU283, PRO325, THR326. Conspicuously, residues such as ARG209, GLU211, ASN213, ASP233, TYR234, and THR235 possess polar or charged side chains capable of forming hydrogen bonds or electrostatic interactions, whereas LEU207, VAL208, ALA231, and PRO325 contribute hydrophobic interactions that augment ligand steadiness within the binding cavity. The extensive interaction network and favorable docking score suggest that PHPV may effectively bind to GSK3 and possibly modulate its kinase activity.

The docking of PHPV and human PTP1B revealed the binding free energy of −8.89 kcal mol−1 (Fig. 12). The PHPV was found to be interacting with the residues of PTP1B are HIS13, ASN14, ALA15, HIS16, GLY17, ALA18, TYR24, LEU26, ALA39, HIS40, THR41, ALA42, VAL43, VAL44, SER72, THR73, LEU76, GLU77, GLU269.

The interaction is mediated by hydrophobic residues (ALA15, ALA18, LEU26, ALA39, ALA42, VAL43, VAL44, and LEU76), which stabilize the ligand through hydrophobic contacts, while polar residues (ASN14, THR41, SER72, THR73, GLU77, and GLU269) contribute hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions that enhance binding specificity. Aromatic residue TYR24 and histidine residues HIS13, HIS16, and HIS40 further reinforce ligand binding through π-interactions and hydrogen bonds, whereas GLY17 facilitates close ligand packing via van der Waals interactions. Collectively, these interactions indicate that PHPV is favorably accommodated within the PTP1B binding pocket. Since PTP1B negatively regulates insulin signaling by dephosphorylating the insulin receptor, the observed binding suggests that PHPV may inhibit PTP1B activity, thereby enhancing insulin signaling and promoting glucose homeostasis.

GLUT4 (Glucose Transporter Type 4) is the principal insulin-regulated protein that facilitates the transfer of glucose from the bloodstream into adipose tissue and striated muscle. Further, a crucial enzyme in the pathophysiology of diabetes is glycogen synthase kinase-3 (GSK3), which functions as a negative regulator of insulin signaling by deactivating both glycogen synthase and insulin receptor substrate-1 (IRS-1). Inhibiting GSK3 is a possible therapeutic strategy to increase insulin sensitivity since GSK3 overactivity results in insulin resistance and poor glucose elimination. Protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B), a major negative regulator of insulin signalling. While PTP1B inhibition improves insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance, PTP1B overexpression in skeletal muscle, adipose tissue, and the liver leads to insulin resistance. Docking simulations indicate that, among the three protein targets analysed, the PHPV exhibits the highest binding affinity toward the Glut4 protein. The PHPV is an organovanadium complex having pronounced lipophilic character. Its molecular architecture is dominated by saturated carbocyclic frameworks and fused nitrogen-heterocycles, that collectively generate an extensive non-polar surface area. Although the structure incorporates localized polar functionalities, primarily oxo-vanadium(v) centre and hydroxyl (–OH) groups, hydrophilic moieties are spatially overshadowed by the predominant hydrocarbon skeleton. Consequently, the overall physicochemical profile of the complex is strongly hydrophobic. This property facilitates preferential stabilization within the hydrophobic binding cavity of the GluT4 protein, resulting stronger interactions compared to the alternative protein targets GSK3 and PTP1B. PTP1B is an upstream negative regulator of insulin signaling. It dephosphorylates the insulin receptor (IR) and IRS-1, thereby suppressing the PI3K/Akt signaling cascade. Inhibiting PTP1B restores insulin signaling, leading to enhanced Akt activation and subsequent GLUT4 translocation to the plasma membrane. PHPV antidiabetic effects primarily by inhibiting protein tyrosine phosphatases, particularly PTP1B. GLUT4 is the final effector, not the primary regulatory target. Although the docking score with GLUT4 is the strongest (−12.05 kcal mol−1), GLUT4 is a transporter rather than a signaling regulator. Binding to GLUT4 alone does not necessarily imply activation of glucose transport. Physiologically, GLUT4 activity is predominantly controlled by insulin-mediated translocation rather than direct ligand binding. Inhibition of GSK3 endorses glycogen synthesis by triggering glycogen synthase and improving insulin sensitivity. However, its effect is downstream of insulin receptor signaling and is generally considered complementary rather than the principal mechanism.

The sturdier docking score of GLUT4 (−12.05 kcal mol−1) indicates that PHPV has a higher predicted binding affinity for GLUT4 than for GSK3 (−9.43 kcal mol−1) or PTP1B (−8.89 kcal mol−1). However, we are aware that binding affinity alone should not be used to infer the primary biological mechanism also the biological role of the target is likewise important. Since PTP1B is a validated upstream regulator of insulin signaling and a well-established molecular target of vanadium-based antidiabetic agents, inhibition of PTP1B provides a more mechanistically plausible explanation for the observed antidiabetic activity. Activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway following PTP1B inhibition would subsequently promote GLUT4 translocation and glucose uptake, while concurrent inhibition of GSK3 would enhance glycogen synthesis. Therefore, the overall antidiabetic effect of PHPV may be explained by PTP1B inhibition as the primary mechanism, with GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake and GSK3 inhibition contributing as important downstream events.

EPR studies

The X-band EPR spectrum of PHPV exhibits signal characteristic of an EPR active, oxo-vanadium(iv), d1 system (Fig. 14). On the other hand, whereas oxo-vanadium(v), a 3d0 system, diamagnetic and EPR silent. The observed spectrum displays characteristic hyperfine splitting resulting from the interaction of the unpaired electron with the52 V nucleus (I = 7/2), a well-established fingerprint of oxovanadium(iv) (VO2+) complexes.52 The anisotropic line shape observed in the spectrum is also consistent to commonly encountered oxovanadium(iv) complexes.53–55 Moreover, the g value, 2.1 supports oxovanadium(iv).56,57 Therefore, oxidation state of the vanadium centre, observed in SC-XRD analysis matches to that observed in EPR studies. The equation used for the calculation is hν = gµBB where g = g-factor (dimensionless); h = Planck's constant, 6.626 × 10−34 J s; ν = microwave frequency (Hz) = 9.392 × 104 Hz; µB = Bohr magneton, 9.274 × 10−24 J T−1, B = resonance magnetic field (Tesla) = 3346 (obtained from the spectra). Studies on toxicity of PHPV and anti-diabetic efficacy in comparison to standard drug metformin

The details toxicity studies on PHPV in terms of LD50 have been described in SI. Fig. S34 reveals that the oral LD50 of PHPV is 2500 mg kg−1 which means moderately low acute toxicity, having reasonable margin of safety at lower doses.

On the other hand, the PCA biplot (Fig. S35, SI) further supports this interpretation by showing clear separation between different experimental groups, such as STZ-induced diabetic rats and those treated with PHPV at different doses and metformin, along principal components. The clustering pattern indicates that STZ significantly alters liver and serum biochemical profiles, while PHPV treatment (especially at 3.3 mg per kg b. wt. dose) brings these markers closer to the normal group. Vectors for SGOT, SGPT, and ALP point in a similar direction, reinforcing their shared influence on the principal components. Glucose and cholesterol, while also contributing, exhibit a slightly different orientation, implying distinct but relevant roles in group discrimination. Corelation matrix and PCA biplot analysis together suggest that PHPV has a dose-dependent restorative effect on liver and metabolic profiles in diabetic conditions, potentially normalizing key biochemical markers disrupted by STZ. Several vanadium complexes, namely BMOV, BEOV, vanadyl acetylacetonate etc. show prospective antidiabetic/insulin-mimetic properties. However some of them suffer from severe limitations in terms of toxicity, stability and, dose. Our present vanadium complex, PHPV have potential antidiabetic efficacy, excellent solution state stability, low toxicity and adverse effects. Conclusion

A new dinuclear oxovanadium(iv) complex (PHPV) of amide–imine conjugate have been synthesised and characterized spectroscopically while its stereochemical structure has been determined from single crystal X-ray diffraction analysis. The PHPV turns out as an excellent anti-diabetic compound. In addition, PHPV is capable of selective fluorescence recognition of Th4+via metal ion displacement protocol. Th4+ displaces oxo-vanadium ion from PHPV complex as established from spectroscopic and analytical studies.

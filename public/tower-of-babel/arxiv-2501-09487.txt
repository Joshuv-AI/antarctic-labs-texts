Abstract
. We develop a systematic study of the interior Sobolev regularity of weak solutions to the mixed local and nonlocal ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplace equations, which model the superposition of two stochastic processes such as Brownian motion and Lévy flight. To be precise, we show that the weak solution u u belongs to W loc 2 , 2 W^{2,2}_{\rm loc} Sobolev spaces in the subquadratic case, while | ∇ u | p − 2 2 ∇ u |\nabla u|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla u is of the class W loc 1 , 2 W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}} in the superquadratic scenario, both of which coincide with that of the classical p p -Laplace equations. Moreover, an improved higher fractional differentiability and integrability result u ∈ W loc 1 + β , γ u\in W^{1+\beta,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}} is proved in the full range p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) for any γ ∈ [ max ⁡ { p , 2 } , ∞ ) \gamma\in[\max\{p,2\},\infty) and β ∈ ( 0 , 2 γ ) \beta\in(0,\frac{2}{\gamma}) . The main analytical tools are the finite-diﬀerence quotients, carefully tailored energy estimates, and nonlocal tail estimates. As far as we know, our results are new within the context of such mixed problems, even in the p p -growth setting.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the higher Sobolev regularity, in both differentiability and integrability, of weak solutions to the mixed local and nonlocal equation

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

where

s\in(0,1),\quad sq<p\leq q<\infty

and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, is a bounded domain. The local
p
-Laplace operator
\Delta_{p}
and the fractional
q
-Laplace operator
(-\Delta_{q})^{s}
are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u=\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)

and

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u(x)=2\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sq}}\,dy.

Here
\nabla u
denotes the gradient of
u
and
\mathrm{P.V.}
denotes the Cauchy principal value. Such equations, which simultaneously exhibit the classical and fractional features, are not scale-invariant and therefore cannot be regarded as a simple superposition.

In recent years, mixed local and nonlocal problems have received increasing attention owing to their far-ranging applications in the real
world. On one hand, the analysis of mixed operators is motivated by the superposition of two stochastic processes such as Brownian motion
and Lévy flight. On the other hand, such problems arise in optimal search strategies, animal foraging, and biomathematics, where they describe diffusion patterns across multiple scales; see
[
29
,
28
]
and the references therein.

1.2.
Main results

For (
1.1
), one could expect that the regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
) goes beyond that allowed by purely nonlocal operators, since the local operator
\Delta_{p}
plays the leading role in view of
sq<p
.
To this end, we implement a difference-quotient method to obtain higher Sobolev estimates in both differentiability and integrability. More precisely, we differentiate the equation at the discrete level using carefully chosen test functions. The main difficulty is to control both the local integral and the nonlocal tail generated by
(-\Delta_{q})^{s}
in a manner compatible with the estimates furnished by the
p
-Laplacian. This yields matching powers of the increment
\delta_{h}u
and the step size
|h|
, allowing us to pass rigorously from finite differences to weak derivatives. Consequently, we obtain stronger Sobolev regularity than is generally available for the purely fractional equation
(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u=0
.

The main contributions of this paper are threefold. First, we derive estimates for the first-order difference quotients of
|\nabla u|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla u
for
p\geq 2
and of
\nabla u
for
1<p\leq 2
, respectively. These estimates further yield their
W^{1,2}
-regularity. However, this cannot be achieved for the purely fractional
q
-Laplace equation, which does display the regularizing impact of
\Delta_{p}
. Second, the relation between difference quotients and weak derivatives in
W^{1,p}
yields an increment of order
|h|^{p}
, which is sharper than the order
|h|^{sq}
supplied by the
W^{s,q}
-energy. As a consequence, the order of differentiability of solutions in Theorem
1.3
below is higher than that of purely fractional problems. On the other hand, compared to the Sobolev regularity of
\nabla u
for purely (local)
p
-Laplacian mentioned above due to Mingione, Miśkiewicz in
[
48
,
49
]
, where
\nabla u
is shown to be almost
W^{\frac{2}{p},p}
-regular in
[
48
]
for
p\geq 2
and almost
W^{\theta,\frac{2}{\theta}}
-regular with any
\theta\in\left[\frac{2}{p},\frac{2}{p-1}\right)
in
[
49
]
for
p\geq 3
, our results improve the gradient integrability and provide an analogous theory in the singular range. Finally, it is worth pointing out that even though the classical operator
\Delta_{p}
plays the dominant role, we cannot directly apply the methods used for the
p
-Laplace equation, owing to the nonlocal effects and a lack of scale-invariance. Overcoming these obstacles is essential to establish the desired Sobolev regularity.

Throughout this paper, we denote

\mathfrak{M}_{r}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)=\left(r^{sq}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u|^{q-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}

2.
Preliminaries

This section introduces the notation and auxiliary results used in the sequel. Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a positive constant that may change from line to line. Dependencies are displayed when relevant; for example,
C(p,q,n)
depends only on
p
,

, and

and abbreviate it to

For
1<q<\infty
, the Sobolev space
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
is defined as

\displaystyle W^{1,q}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{q}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\,dx<\infty\right\},

and for any
s\in(0,1)
, we also define the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,q}(\Omega)
endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,q}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,q}(\Omega)},

where the seminorm
[u]_{W^{s,q}(\Omega)}
is defined as

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{s,q}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+sq}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

To ensure that the nonlocal contribution

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{n+sq}}\,dy

is finite, we use the tail space

\displaystyle L^{q-1}_{sq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{q-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{q-1}}{1+|x|^{n+sq}\,dx<\infty}\right\}.

For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
, we define the so-called nonlocal tail

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)=\left(R^{sq}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u|^{q-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

One can easily see that
\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)
is well-defined as long as
u\in L^{q-1}_{sq}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We also use the monotone function

\displaystyle J_{p}(t):=|t|^{p-2}t,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The first- and second-order difference operators are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\delta_{h}u(x):=u_{h}(x)-u(x):=u(x+h)-u(x)

and

\displaystyle\delta^{2}_{h}u(x):=\delta_{h}(\delta_{h}u(x))=u(x+2h)+u(x)-2u(x+h),

where
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

.

We refer to

3.

3.1.
Regularity for nonlinear expressions of gradients

We begin by differentiating (

Lemma 3.1

(Energy estimate)

Let

,

Proof.

We first set
\tilde{r}:=r+\frac{d}{2}

\displaystyle 0

3.2.
Fractional Sobolev regularity for gradients

For clarity, we first reduce the problem by rescaling to the normalized regime
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
and
\mathrm{Tail}(u;0,1)\leq 1
. We establish higher fractional differentiability in this regime and then scale back to obtain the general estimates.

3.2.1.
Reduction of the problem

Assume that
1<p<\infty
and
u
is a locally bounded weak solution to (
1.1
) in
\Omega
.
Let
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
.

Moreover, let

Since the desired estimates are local, it suffices to work on a ball. Thus, if
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in

, then
\tilde{u}
is a weak solution of

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\tilde{u}+R^{p-sq}\mathfrak{M}_{R}^{q-p}(-\Delta_{q})^{s}\tilde{u}=0\quad\text{in }B_{2}(0).

In addition, we know that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}}\frac{|\tilde{u}|^{q-1}}{|x|^{n+sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\leq 1.

Because the coefficient

appears in front of

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+A(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u=0\quad\text{in }B_{2}(0)

with

.

3.2.2.
Fractional differentiability

The next lemma provides an energy estimate for the second-order differences of solutions, from which we derive the fractional differentiability of the gradient.

Lemma 3.2
.

\displaystyle\leq C(n,s,q,\gamma,R)\|\nabla u\|^{\gamma-2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\left(1+\|\nabla u\|^{2-p}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)^{2}\left(\mathfrak{M}_{2R}^{2q-2}+\|\nabla u\|^{2q-2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C(n,s,q,R)\|\nabla u\|^{\gamma-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\left(1+\|\nabla u\|^{2-p}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)\left(\mathfrak{M}_{2R}^{q-1}+\|\nabla u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right).

Note that
\nabla J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(\delta_{h}u)=\frac{\gamma}{2}|\delta_{h}u|^{\frac{\gamma-2}{2}}\nabla\delta_{h}u
. The preceding display becomes

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\eta^{2}\left|\nabla J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(u_{h}-u)\right|^{2}\,dx\leq C(n,s,p,q,\gamma,R)\left(1+\mathfrak{M}_{2R}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)^{\gamma-2p+2q}|h|^{\gamma}.

(4.32)

Applying Lemma
2.4
, and observing that
J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(\delta_{h}u)\in W^{1,2}(B_{R})
and
|\delta_{h}J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(\delta_{h}u)|\geq|\delta_{h}(\delta_{h}u)|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}|\delta_{h}(\delta_{h}u)|^{\gamma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\left|\nabla J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(u_{h}-u)\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{2}\int_{B_{R}}\eta^{2}\left|\nabla J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(u_{h}-u)\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(n,s,p,q,\gamma,R)\left(1+\mathfrak{M}_{2R}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)^{\gamma-2p+2q}|h|^{\gamma+2}.

For any
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
, Lemma
2.6
yields
\nabla u\in W^{\beta,\gamma}(B_{\frac{R}{8}})

and furthermore,

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{\gamma}_{W^{\beta,\gamma}(B_{\frac{R}{8}})}\leq C(n,s,p,q,\gamma,\beta,R)\left(1+\mathfrak{M}_{2R}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)^{\gamma-2p+2q}

for any
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{2}{\gamma}\right)
. Thus, we conclude
\nabla u\in W^{\beta,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
\gamma\geq 2
and
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{2}{\gamma}\right)
.
∎

Taking
\gamma=2
in the preceding argument yields the
W^{2,2}_{\rm loc}
regularity; in this case, second-order differences are unnecessary.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

(ii).

In the case
\gamma=2
, there is no need to consider the relationship between
\nabla J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(\delta_{h}u)
and
\delta_{h}J_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(\delta_{h}u)
. Therefore, one can readily infer from (
4.32
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u_{h}-\nabla u\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\eta^{2}\left|\nabla u_{h}-\nabla u\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(n,s,p,q,\gamma,R)\left(1+\mathfrak{M}_{2R}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)^{2-2p+2q}|h|^{2}.

The preceding inequality and the relation between differences and derivatives imply
\nabla u\in W^{1,2}\left(B_{\frac{R}{8}}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, and

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{2}_{W^{1,2}(B_{\frac{R}{8}}(x_{0}))}\leq C(n,s,p,q,\gamma,R)\left(1+\mathfrak{M}_{2R}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\right)^{2-2p+2q}.

Hence
u\in W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, which completes the proof.
∎

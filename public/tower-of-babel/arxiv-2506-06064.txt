Squeezing and quantum control of antiferromagnetic magnon pseudospin
Anna-Luisa E. Römling, Johannes Feist, Francisco J. García-Vidal, Akashdeep Kamra

Abstract
Antiferromagnets have been shown to harbor strong magnon squeezing in equilibrium, making them a potential resource for quantum correlations and entanglement. Recent experiments have also found them to host coherently coupled magnonic excitations forming a magnon pseudospin, in analogy to electronic spin. Here, we delineate the quantum properties of antiferromagnetic magnon pseudospin by accounting for spin non-conserving interactions and going beyond the rotating wave approximation. Employing concrete examples of nickel oxide and hematite, we find strong squeezing of the magnon pseudospin highlighting its important role in determining the eigenmode quantum properties. Via ground state quantum fluctuations engineering, this pseudospin squeezing enables an enhancement and control of coupling between the magnonic modes and other excitations. Finally, we evaluate the quantum superpositions that comprise a squeezed pseudospin ground state and delineate a qubit spectroscopy protocol to detect them. Our results are applicable to any system of coupled bosons and thus introduce quantum fluctuations engineering of a general bosonic pseudospin.

Discussion and conclusions.—

Antiferromagnets as a platform hosting interacting bosonic modes offer a convenient tunability of the system parameters
\omega
,
r
,
D_{r}
and
D_{s}
via external magnetic fields and shape anisotropy
Parvini et al. 2020
;
Janus et al. 2023
;
Jungwirth et al. 2016
. However, our results are applicable to other bosonic systems and theoretically introduce quantum engineering of bosonic pseudospin and its fluctuations.

As detailed in the SM
SM
, the easy-plane AFMs nickel oxide and hematite above Morin temperature can be described by a Hamiltonian in the form of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{AFM}}
[Eq. (
3
)] and hence realize the model presented here. However, a similar effective Hamiltonian can be achieved for easy-axis AFMs in the spin-flop and the canted phase
Rezende et al. 2019
. While the intrasublattice interactions responsible for the magnon pseudospin squeezing are typically weaker than exchange and are often neglected, the coupling strengths
D_{r}
and
D_{s}
are greatly enhanced by intrinsic squeezing
\propto\sinh(2r)
and
\propto\cosh(2r)
respectively
SM
making the magnon pseudospin squeezing a large effect. In the case of nickel oxide, the coupling strengths are estimated to be
D_{r}/\omega\approx 0.48
and
D_{s}/\omega\approx 0.24
. Thus, the relatively large values of
D_{r}
and
D_{s}
evaluated here highlight the importance of taking SNC interactions into account
SM
. Furthermore, the model we study here can be used to describe recently realized qubit-magnon platforms, such as the antiferromagnetic van der Waals magnet CrSBr with a defect acting as a spin qubit
Klein and Ross 2024
;
Melendez et al. 2025
.

Considering atomic ensembles as an example, spin squeezing has been mathematically proposed as an entanglement test for bosons
Dalton et al. 2017
. Here, we present a concrete platform exhibiting the spin squeezing
Dalton et al. 2017
along with a method of probing squeezing via qubit spectroscopy. Moreover, this platform provides squeezed Fock states
Nieto 1997
;
Král 1990
which have recently been proposed as a resource for quantum error correction
Korolev et al. 2024
. Because of this and the possibility of state control via a qubit, our analysis highlights new opportunities for quantum technologies. At the same time, the spin-non conserving interactions and the concomitant magnon pseudospin squeezing introduce a knob to tune and engineer quantum fluctuations, coupling to electrons and mode selection expected to be useful in designing spintronic devices
Jungwirth et al. 2016
;
Jungwirth et al. 2014
;
Wu et al. 2013
;
Jungwirth et al. 2011
.

I.1

Here, we derive the Hamiltonian

We start from classical ground state of an AFM, called Néel state, that consists of two fully and oppositely polarized sublattices below the Néel temperature. NiO has a hard-axis and easy-axis anisotropy. The hard-axis anisotropy is along the
\langle 111\rangle

Hutchings and Samuelsen 1972

\displaystyle|\gamma|H_{0}\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}\left(\hat{S}_{i}^{z^{\prime}}+\hat{S}_{j}^{z^{\prime}}\right)+2J\sum_{\left\langle i,j\right\rangle}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}_{j}+K_{x}\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}\left[\left(\hat{S}_{i}^{x}\right)^{2}+\left(\hat{S}_{j}^{x}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\frac{A}{2}\hat{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\frac{B}{2}\hat{b}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{\boldsymbol{k}}+C_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{b}_{-\boldsymbol{k}}+D\left(\hat{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{a}_{-\boldsymbol{k}}+\hat{b}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{b}_{-\boldsymbol{k}}\right)+\mathrm{h.c.},

(S38)

(S39)

(S40)

\displaystyle=0,

(S41)

where we used the property of unitary operators
\hat{S}_{+}^{\dagger}\!\left(r_{+}\right)\hat{S}_{+}\!\left(r_{+}\right)=1
and
\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\hat{R}_{y}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=1

) and (

\displaystyle\ket{m_+,n_-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!n!}}\hat{\psi}_{+}^{\dagger m}\hat{\psi}_{+}^{\dagger m}\ket{0_+,0_-}

(S42)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!n!}}\hat{S}_{+}\!\left(r_{+}\right)\hat{R}_{y}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger m}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\hat{S}_{+}^{\dagger}\!\left(r_{+}\right)\times

\displaystyle~~~~\times\hat{S}_{-}\!\left(r_{-}\right)\hat{R}_{y}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\hat{\beta}^{\dagger m}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\hat{S}_{-}^{\dagger}\!\left(r_{-}\right)\hat{S}_{+}\!\left(r_{+}\right)\hat{S}_{-}\!\left(r_{-}\right)\hat{R}_{y}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\ket{0_\alpha,0_\beta}

(S43)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!n!}}\hat{S}_{+}\!\left(r_{+}\right)\hat{S}_{-}\!\left(r_{-}\right)\hat{R}_{y}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger m}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger n}\ket{0_\alpha,0_\beta}

(S44)

(S45)

which shows the relation in Eq. (

and
[\hat{S}_{+}\!\left(r_{+}\right),\hat{S}_{-}\!\left(r_{-}\right)]=0

II
Pseudospin squeezing

In Sec.
I.2
, we introduce the concept of pseudospin
\hat{\boldsymbol{L}}
. The pseudospin components fulfil commutation relations
[\hat{L}_{i},\hat{L}_{j}]=i\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_{k}
with the Levi-Civita symbol
\epsilon_{ijk}
. Because of that, pseudospin components obey Heisenberg uncertainty relation
\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\rangle\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\rangle\geq|\langle\hat{L}_{x}\rangle|^{2}/4
. In this section, we demonstrate that pseudospin component
\hat{L}_{y}
is squeezed according to Heisenberg uncertainty in an eigenstate
\ket{m_+,n_-}
[Eq. (
S36
)].

II.1
Pseudospin squeezing in a general excited state

In this subsection, we evaluate the expectation values and variances of pseudospin components
\hat{L}_{i}
for
i=x,y,z
for a general
eigenstate
\ket{m_+,n_-}
[Eq. (
S36
)] with
m
(
n
) excitation in eigenmode
\hat{\psi}_{+}
(
\hat{\psi}_{-}
) [Eq. (
S30
)]. We denote the expectation values by
\langle\hat{L}_{i}\rangle_{mn}=\bra{m_+,n_-}\hat{L}_{i}\ket{m_+,n_-}
and the variances
\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{i}^{2}\rangle_{mn}=\bra{m_+,n_-}\hat{L}_{i}^{2}\ket{m_+,n_-}-\langle\hat{L}_{i}\rangle_{mn}^{2}
. We find the following expectation values

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{L}_{x}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{m\left(\cosh^{2}\!r_{+}+\sinh^{2}\!r_{+}\right)+\sinh^{2}\!r_{+}}{2}-\frac{n\left(\cosh^{2}\!r_{-}+\sinh^{2}\!r_{-}\right)+\sinh^{2}\!r_{-}}{2},

(S46)

and
\langle\hat{L}_{y}\rangle_{mn}=\langle\hat{L}_{z}\rangle_{mn}=0
. The corresponding variances of interest read

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{2mn+m+n}{4}\left(\cosh\!r_{+}\cosh\!r_{-}-\sinh\!r_{+}\sinh\!r_{-}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2mn+m+n+1}{4}\left(\sinh\!r_{+}\cosh\!r_{-}-\sinh\!r_{-}\cosh\!r_{+}\right)^{2},

(S47)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{2mn+m+n}{4}\left(\cosh\!r_{+}\cosh\!r_{-}+\sinh\!r_{+}\sinh\!r_{-}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2mn+m+n+1}{4}\left(\sinh\!r_{+}\cosh\!r_{-}+\sinh\!r_{-}\cosh\!r_{+}\right)^{2}.

(S48)

Neglecting the intrasublattice coupling
D_{s}
, we find

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{L}_{x}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{m-n}{2},

(S49)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{2mn+m+n}{4},

(S50)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{2mn+m+n}{4},

(S51)

such that
\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\rangle_{mn}=\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{mn}
. From this, we can deduce that if
D_{s}=0
, there is no squeezing present since the variances of
\hat{L}_{y}
and
\hat{L}_{z}
are the same.

As in the main text, let us analyze a simpler case with
m\neq 0
,
n=0
and
D_{s}\neq 0
. We find that the expectation value of
\hat{L}_{x}
and the variances of
\hat{L}_{y}
and
\hat{L}_{z}
[Eqs. (

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{L}_{x}\right\rangle_{m0}

\displaystyle=\frac{m+\frac{1}{2}}{2}\cosh 2r_{+}-\frac{\cosh 2r_{-}}{4},

(S52)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{m0}

\displaystyle=\frac{m+\frac{1}{2}}{4}\cosh\left(2r_{+}-2r_{-}\right)-\frac{1}{8},

(S53)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{m0}

\displaystyle=\frac{m+\frac{1}{2}}{4}\cosh\left(2r_{+}+2r_{-}\right)-\frac{1}{8}.

(S54)

After some algebra, we find the following uncertainty relation

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{m0}\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{m0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left|\left\langle\hat{L}_{x}\right\rangle\right|_{m0}^{2}+\frac{m^{2}+m}{8}\left(\cosh\!4r_{-}-1\right),

(S55)

so that the state we consider here is not minimum uncertainty.
We check if
\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{m0}<\frac{1}{2}\left|\left\langle\hat{L}_{x}\right\rangle\right|_{m0}

is fulfilled and find that this condition is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left(2m+1\right)\left(\cosh\!2r_{+}-\cosh(2r_{+}-2r_{-})\right)>

\displaystyle\cosh\!2r_{-}-1.

(S56)

This is fulfilled if
r_{-}>0
and
r_{+}>r_{-}
. This means that
L_{y}

is squeezed with respect to
L_{x}
and that
\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\rangle<\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\rangle
. We can quantify squeezing by the ellipticity of Heisenberg’s uncertainty relation [Eq. (
S55
)]
Gerry et al. 2005
, defining

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=e^{-2\xi_{mn}}\sqrt{\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}},

(S57)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}

\displaystyle=e^{2\xi_{mn}}\sqrt{\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}},

(S58)

with pseudospin squeezing factor
\xi_{mn}
. Squeezing factor
\xi_{mn}

\displaystyle\xi_{mn}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}}{\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{mn}}.

(S59)

In the main text, we mention that pseudospin squeezing

and

\displaystyle\xi_{m0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(e^{-2r_{-}}+e^{-4r_{+}+2r_{-}}\right)-e^{-2r_{+}}}{\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(e^{2r_{-}}+e^{-4r_{+}-2r_{-}}\right)-e^{-2r_{+}}}\right).

(S60)

At the instability, the
\psi_{+}
mode becomes unstable as quadrature squeezing diverges

\displaystyle\xi_{\text{inst}}\approx-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{e^{-2r_{-}}}{e^{2r_{-}}}\right)

\xi_{\text{inst}}\approx r_{-}

II.2
Pseudospin squeezing in the ground state

In this subsection, we discuss the special case of pseudospin squeezing in the ground state since it differs qualitatively from the previously known case of spin squeezing in its ground state
\ket{0_+,0_-}
[Eq. (
S36
)]. Considering
m=0
and
n=0
for pseudospin squeezing
\xi_{mn}
[Eq. (
S59
)], we can already see that
\xi_{00}
is decreasing with increasing
D_{s}
. This is unconventional since
D_{s}
is what is causing squeezing. The limit
D_{s}\rightarrow 0
therefore has to be treated carefully in case of the ground state. One reason is that the operator
\hat{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}=\left(\hat{N}/2\right)\left(\hat{N}/2+1\right)

Dalton et al. 2017
, with the number operator
\hat{N}=\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{\alpha}+\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}
has a vanishing expectation value for the magnon vacuum. Hence, in the case where we consider the ground state and the coupling
D_{s}=0
, the magnitude of pseudospin
\langle\hat{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}\rangle_{00}
vanishes. Therefore, our Bloch sphere has zero radius and our basis is ill-defined. This is the difference between electron spin, where
S
is always finite, and bosonic pseudospin.

Let’s carefully evaluate the limit
D_{s}\rightarrow 0
by expanding Eqs. (
S52
)–(
S54
) for
m=0
in the small
|D_{s}|\ll(\omega-D_{r})/2
limit. We find

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{L}_{x}\right\rangle_{00}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega-D_{r})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\omega+D_{r})^{2}}\right)|D_{s}|^{2},

(S62)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{00}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{\omega-D_{r}}-\frac{1}{\omega+D_{r}}\right)^{2}|D_{s}|^{2},

(S63)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\right\rangle_{00}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{\omega-D_{r}}+\frac{1}{\omega+D_{r}}\right)^{2}|D_{s}|^{2}.

(S64)

It follows that
\xi_{00}
in the small
|D_{s}|\ll(\omega-D_{r})/2
limit has a finite value

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{\omega}{D_r}).

(S65)

However, the
D_{s}=0
limit is ill defined since
\langle\hat{L}_{x}\rangle_{00}=\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{y}^{2}\rangle_{00}=\langle\Delta\hat{L}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{00}=0
. This is because it requires the presence of a nonzero
D_{s}\neq 0
in order to break symmetry and therefore induce squeezing. The intrasublattice coupling
D_{s}
is hence causing squeezing but demonstrates unconventional behaviour in the ground state
\ket{0_+,0_-}
[Eq. (
S36
)].

III
Coupling to spin qubit

In this section, we analyze the interaction between an antiferromagnet with strong intrasublattice coupling as described by
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{AFM}}
[Eq. (
S13
)] and a spin qubit in the dispersive regime. In Sec.
III.1
, we derive the direct dispersive coupling from a spin Hamiltonian. In Sec.
III.2
, we diagonalize the resulting Hamiltonian describing the AFM, qubit and direct dispersive coupling and discuss state control via the qubit. Finally, in Sec.
III.3
we discuss if pseudospin squeezing can be resolved via qubit spectroscopy. Here we focus on the signatures of pseudospin squeezing in two coupling regimes.

III.1
Dispersive coupling

In this subsection, we derive the direct dispersive coupling between the AFM and the spin qubit. The Hamiltonian describing the spin qubit is given by
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{q}=\omega_{q}\hat{\sigma}_{z}/2
, where
\omega_{q}
denotes the level splitting of the qubit and
\hat{\sigma}_{z}
the third Pauli matrix. We assume that the AFM and qubit interact via exchange interaction between the spin qubit and the spins belonging to the AFM interfacing with the qubit
Skogvoll et al. 2021
;
Römling et al. 2023
;
Kamra and Belzig 2017
. This interaction can be described by the following Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=J_{\text{int},A}\sum_{l\in A}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}_{l}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}_{l}+J_{\text{int},B}\sum_{m\in B}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}_{m}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}_{m}

(S66)

where
l
and
m
label the interfacial site belonging to sublattices

A
and
B
,
J_{\text{int},A(B)}
is the interfacial coupling strength

Bender and Tserkovnyak 2015
;
Kamra and Belzig 2016b
;
Takahashi et al. 2010
;
Zhang and Zhang 2012
with lattice site belonging to sublattice
A(B)
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}

denotes the spin operator and
\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}
spin of the
electronic states comprising the qubit. As argued in
Skogvoll et al. 2021
and
Römling et al. 2023
;
Römling and Kamra 2024
, terms
\propto\hat{S}_{i}^{x}\hat{s}_{i}^{x}
and
\propto\hat{S}_{i}^{y}\hat{s}_{i}^{y}
result in a coherent interaction, leading to a hybridization between magnons and the qubit. Here, we want to suppress coherent exchange by choosing a large detuning between the magnon resonance frequency
\omega
and the qubit level splitting
\omega_{q}
and hence focus on the terms
\propto\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i}^{z}
. Using Holstein-Primakoff transformations [Eq. (
S2
)], we find that the terms
\propto\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i}^{z}
in the interaction Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{int}}
[Eq. (
S66
)] have one constant term renormalizing the qubit level splitting
Skogvoll et al. 2021
and a coupling term resulting in dispersive interaction
Römling et al. 2023

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{dis}}

\displaystyle=\left|\phi\right|^{2}\left(\frac{J_{\text{int},A}N_{\text{int},A}}{2N}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}-\frac{J_{\text{int},B}N_{\text{int},B}}{2N}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{z},

(S67)

where
|\phi_{l}|
denotes the qubit wave function at lattice site
l
, which here is assumed to be homogeneous
Skogvoll et al. 2021
;
Römling et al. 2023
, and

the number of interfacial lattice sites belonging to sublattice
A(B)
.

Defining the direct dispersive coupling strengths

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi_{a}&=\frac{J_{\text{int},A}\left|\phi\right|^{2}}{2N}N_{\text{int},A},\end{split}\begin{split}\chi_{b}&=\frac{J_{\text{int},B}\left|\phi\right|^{2}}{2N}N_{\text{int},B},\end{split}

(S68)

we can deduce that the coupling between the AFM and qubit depends on the size of the magnet and the structure of the interface. Assuming a compensated magnet with a compensated interface, such that
\chi_{a}=\chi_{b}\equiv\chi
, and using the Bogoliubov transformation [Eq. (

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{dis}}
[Eq. (
S67

Eigenmodes of the coupled AFM and qubit system

III.3
Ground state composition

In this section, we discuss if pseudospin squeezing as defined in Sec.
II
manifests itself via a characteristic signature in qubit spectroscopy
Schuster et al. 2007
;
Lachance-Quirion et al. 2017
;
Kono et al. 2017
. For this purpose, we follow the method in
Römling et al. 2023
;
Römling and Kamra 2024
and investigate if there is a nonvanishing overlap between the ground state
\ket{0,0}_{g}
[Eq. (
S92
)] and an excited state
\ket{m,n,}_{e}
[Eq.
S93
].

If there is a nonvanishing overlap between the ground state and an excited state
\vphantom{\ket{0}}{}_{e}\!\innerproduct{m,n}{0,0}_{g}\neq 0
and the qubit is driven, e.g. by an external microwave drive, at a frequency that matches the energy difference between the states
\ket{0,0}_{g}
and
\ket{m,n}_{e}
, then the transition
\ket{0,0}_{g}\rightarrow\ket{m,n}_{e}
occurs with a probability of
|\vphantom{\ket{0}}_{e}\!\innerproduct{m,n}{0,0}_{g}|^{2}
. When performing qubit spectroscopy by driving the qubit in a range of frequencies and measuring its population, the excitation probability
|\vphantom{\ket{0}}_{e}\!\innerproduct{m,n}{0,0}_{g}|^{2}
should manifest itself in as a nontrivial peak.

Our goal in the following is to find out if there are excited states
\ket{m,n}_{e}
[Eq.
S93
] that have a non-vanishing overlap with the ground state
\ket{0,0}_{g}
[Eq. (
S92
)], hence demonstrating if pseudospin squeezing has a non-trivial signature in qubit spectroscopy. However, since the operators
\hat{\psi}_{1}^{g}
and
\hat{\psi}_{2}^{g}
don’t commute
with
\hat{\psi}_{1}^{e\dagger}
and
\hat{\psi}_{2}^{e\dagger}
, the analytical calculation of the overlap is a nontrivial task. We therefore expand the operators in the two limits
\chi/D_{r}\ll 1
and
D_{r}/\chi\ll 1
and calculate the overlap in these limits.

\chi

(S95)

with the squeeze factors

(S112)

with the modified coupling strengths

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{D}_{r}=D\left(\sinh(2r)-\frac{E}{2D}\sinh(2r)\right),\end{split}\begin{split}\tilde{D}_{s}=D\left(\cosh(2r)-\frac{E}{D}\cosh(2r)\right).\end{split}

(S113)

Our analysis from Secs.
I.2
,
II
and
III
can therefore be conveniently applied to hematite by substituting
D_{r}\rightarrow\tilde{D}_{r}
and
D_{s}\rightarrow\tilde{D}_{s}
.

V
Rotation transformation for pseudospin

In this section, we demonstrate the relations
\psi_{s}=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\alpha}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)
and
\psi_{a}=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\beta}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)
from Eq. (
S27
) with the rotation matrix
\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)=\exp\left(-i\theta\hat{L}_{y}\right)
and the pseudospin component
\hat{L}_{y}=\frac{i}{2}\left(\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}-\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}\right)
.
From the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula
Baker 1901
follows

\displaystyle e^{\hat{A}}\hat{\alpha}e^{-\hat{A}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\alpha}+\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\hat{A},\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]\right]+\dots

(S114)

\displaystyle=\hat{\alpha}+\sum_{n=1}\frac{1}{n!}\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{n},

(S115)

where
\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{n}
is the
n
-fold nested
commutator
\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{n}=\left[\hat{A},\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{n-1}\right]

and
\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{1}=\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]
. Our rotation operations
\psi_{s}=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\alpha}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)
and
\psi_{a}=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\beta}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)
from Eq. (
S27
)
can be expressed with the expansion expansion in Eq. (
S115
). Let’s define for convenience

\hat{A}=\frac{\theta}{2}\left(\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}-\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}\right)

and determine
\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{n}
. We find

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k+1}\left(-1\right)^{k}\hat{\beta}&\text{for }n=2k+1,\\
\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k}\left(-1\right)^{k}\hat{\alpha}&\text{for }n=2k,\end{cases}

(S116)

with
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This can be shown with mathematical induction. Let’s start with
n=1

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta}{2}\left[\left(\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}-\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}\right),\hat{\alpha}\right]=\frac{\theta}{2}\hat{\beta},

(S117)

and
n=2

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}\left[\left(\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}-\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}\right),\hat{\beta}\right]=-\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}\hat{\alpha},

(S118)

which is both in accordance with the formula for the nested commutator in Eq. (
S116
). We now perform the induction step for
n+1
, where we have to make a distinction between
n+1
even and
n+1
odd. We begin with
n+1=2k+1
. The nested commutator for
2k+1
then reads

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{A},\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2k}\right]=\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k}\left(-1\right)^{k}\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]

(S119)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k+1}\left(-1\right)^{k}\hat{\beta},

(S120)

which reproduces the formula in Eq. (
S116
). Now we take an even
n+1=2k+2
. The corresponding nested commutator reads

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2k+2}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{A},\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2k+1}\right]=\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k+1}\left(-1\right)^{k}\left[\hat{A},\hat{\beta}\right]

(S121)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k+2}\left(-1\right)^{k+1}\hat{\alpha},

(S122)

which is also in accordance with the formula in Eq. (
S116
). We can now separate
odd and even
n
in the expansion of
e^{\hat{A}}\hat{\alpha}e^{-\hat{A}}
[Eq. (
S115
)], which becomes

\displaystyle e^{\hat{A}}\hat{\alpha}e^{-\hat{A}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}\frac{1}{\left(2k\right)!}\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2k}+\sum_{k=0}\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)!}\left[\hat{A},\hat{\alpha}\right]_{2k+1}

(S123)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}\frac{1}{\left(2k\right)!}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k}\left(-1\right)^{k}\hat{\alpha}+\sum_{k=0}\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)!}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k+1}\left(-1\right)^{k}\hat{\beta}

(S124)

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\hat{\alpha}+\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\hat{\beta}.

(S125)

With this we show

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\alpha}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)=

\displaystyle\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\hat{\alpha}+\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\hat{\beta},

(S126)

and therefore

\displaystyle\psi_{s}

\displaystyle=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\alpha}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right).

(S127)

Under the same considerations, we can show that
\psi_{a}=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\beta}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right).

Now the nested commutators become

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A},\hat{\beta}\right]_{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k+1}\left(-1\right)^{k+1}\hat{\alpha}&\text{for }n=2k+1,\\
\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2k}\left(-1\right)^{k}\hat{\beta}&\text{for }n=2k,\end{cases}

such that

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\beta}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)=

\displaystyle\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\hat{\beta}-\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\hat{\alpha},

(S128)

from which follows indeed
\psi_{a}=\hat{R}_{y}\left(\theta\right)\hat{\beta}\hat{R}_{y}^{\dagger}\left(\theta\right)
.

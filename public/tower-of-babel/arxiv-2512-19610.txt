Abstract
. Let G G and H H be unital associative algebras over a field K K , such that G G satisfies the identity [ x 1 , … , x p ] = 0 [x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]=0 for some integer p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 and H H satisfies the identities [ x 1 , x 2 , x 3 ] = 0 [x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=0 and [ x 1 , x 2 ] ⋯ [ x 2 ​ k − 1 , x 2 ​ k ] = 0 [x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]=0 for some k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . In this paper, extending results of Deryabina and Krasilnikov, we show that the tensor product G ⊗ H G\otimes H is again a Lie nilpotent associative algebra, i.e., it satisfies [ x 1 , … , x q ] = 0 [x_{1},\dots,x_{q}]=0 for some q ≥ p q\geq p . We also determine an explicit value of q q in the case k = 2 k=2 , i.e., when H H satisfies the identity [ x 1 , x 2 ] ​ [ x 3 , x 4 ] = 0 [x_{1},x_{2}][x_{3},x_{4}]=0 . As a corollary, we reprove a result of Drensky saying that any product of Grassmann algebras of the form E ⊗ E i 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ E i s E\otimes E_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{i_{s}} or E j 1 ⊗ E j 2 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ E j t E_{j_{1}}\otimes E_{j_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{j_{t}} , where E E denotes the Grassmann algebra over a countable dimensional vector space and E r E_{r} denotes the Grasmann algebra over an r r -dimensional vector space, satisfies an identity of the form [ x 1 , … , x q ] = 0 [x_{1},\dots,x_{q}]=0 for some integer q ≥ 3 q\geq 3 . In addition, we show that for products of the form E ⊗ E i 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ E i s E\otimes E_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{i_{s}} the minimal value of q q is always and odd integer. We also provide several particular cases in which a value of q q can be explicitly computed. As an application, we consider a field of characteristic zero, the variety 𝔑 p \mathfrak{N}_{p} of Lie nilpotent associative algebras of index at most p p and the corresponding relatively free algebras of finite rank, F n ​ ( 𝔑 p ) F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p}) . We exhibit many explicit irreducible S n S_{n} -modules in the S n S_{n} -module decomposition of the space of proper multilinear polynomials in F n ​ ( 𝔑 p ) F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p}) for any p p . This gives a lower bound for the dimensions of the spaces of multilinear and proper multilinear polynomials in F n ​ ( 𝔑 p ) F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p}) .

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field and let
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
denote the free unital associative algebra generated by the countably infinite set
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\dots\}
over
K
. We say that an associative unital
K
-algebra
A
satisfies a polynomial identity if there is a polynomial
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in K\left\langle X\right\rangle
such that
f(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=0
for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in A
. If
A
satisfies a polynomial identity then
A
is called an algebra with polynomial identity, or simply a PI-algebra. We also define left-normed long commutators in
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
recursively in the following way:
[u_{1},u_{2}]=u_{1}u_{2}-u_{2}u_{1}
and
[u_{1},\dots,u_{p+1}]=[[u_{1},\dots,u_{p}],u_{p+1}]
for any
p\geq 2
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{p+1}\in K\left\langle X\right\rangle
. A unital associative algebra is called Lie nilpotent of index at most
p
if it satisfies the identity
[x_{1}.\dots,x_{p+1}]=0
.

Let
E
denote the Grassmann (or exterior algebra) algebra over a countable dimensional
K
-vector space and let
E_{r}
denote the Grassmann algebra over an
r
-dimensional
K
-vector space, where
r\geq 2
and
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2
. It is well-known that both
E
and
E_{r}
are Lie nilpotent algebras and satisfy the identity
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=0
. In his paper
[
5
]
, Drensky shows that moreover any product of Grassmann algebras of the form
E\otimes E_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{i_{s}}
or
E_{j_{1}}\otimes E_{j_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{j_{t}}
satisfies an identity of the form
[x_{1},\dots,x_{q}]=0
for some integer
q\geq 3
. There are several cases, in which an explicit value of
q
is known. For example, it is well-known that
E_{2}\otimes E_{2}
satisfies the identity
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]=0
and that
E_{2}\otimes E_{2}\otimes E_{2}
satisfies the identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{5}]=0
. Furthermore, in
[
2
]
, the authors show that the algebras
E\otimes E_{2k}
and
E\otimes E_{2k+1}
satisfy the identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{2k+3}]=0
. This is a corollary of a more general result that they prove in the same paper, namely that if
G
and
H
are unital associative algebras satisfying
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=0
and such that in addition
H
satisfies
[x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]=0
for some
k\geq 2
, then
G\otimes H
satisfies
[x_{1},\dots,x_{2k+1}]=0
. This result is valid over any field
K
. Notice that the condition on
H
to satisfy the identity
[x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]=0
is important, since for example the algebra
E\otimes E
is not Lie nilpotent.

In the first part of this paper, we extend the above statement using the same approach as in
[
2
]
. We show that if
G
satisfies the identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]=0
and
H
is as before, i.e.,
H
satisfies
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=0
and
[x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]=0
for some
k\geq 2
, then the tensor product
G\otimes H
satisfies
[x_{1},\dots,x_{q}]=0
for some value of
q
. In the case
k=2
, we determine explicitly a value of
q
, under the requirement that
\operatorname{char}K\neq 3
. These results are given in Theorem
2.5
and Proposition
2.8
. Since every finitely generated Lie nilpotent associative algebra statisfies an identity of the form
[x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]=0
(see e.g.
[
12
]
or
[
14
]
), an immediate corollary of Theorem
2.5
is that if
G
is any Lie nilpotent associative algebra and
H
is finitely generated associative and satisfying
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=0
then
G\otimes H
is again Lie nilpotent.
As another application of Theorem
2.5
, we obtain a new proof of Drensky’s result on the tensor products of Grassmann algebras.
The advantage of this approach is that we are able to provide several examples in which a value of
q
is explicitly determined. We also show that when we have a product of the form
E\otimes E_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{i_{s}}
, then the minimal value of
q
for which
[x_{1},\dots,x_{q}]=0
is an identity is necessarily an odd integer.

In the second part of the paper, we take
K
to be a field of characteristic
0
. By
\mathfrak{N}_{p}
we denote the variety of all Lie nilpotent associative algebras of index at most
p
. Let
X_{n}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset X
and let
K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle
denote the free associative algebra generated by
X_{n}
. By
P_{n}
we denote the subspace of multilinear polynomials of degree
n
in
K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle
and by
\Gamma_{n}
we denote the subspace of proper multilinear polynomials of degree
n
. The spaces
P_{n}
and
\Gamma_{n}
are naturally modules over the symmetric group
S_{n}
, where the action of the symmetric group is given by permutation of the variables. One of the main questions in the quantitative study of PI-algebras is to determine the
S_{n}
-cocharacter and codimension sequence of a given PI-algebra. In this paper, we are interested in the relatively free algebra of rank
n
in the variety
\mathfrak{N}_{p}
. This algebra is defined as the quotient
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})=K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(I_{p+1}\cap K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle)
, where
I_{p+1}
is the two-sided associative ideal in
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
generated by all commutators of length
p+1
. The subspaces of multilinear and proper multilinear polynomials in
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
, denoted respectively by
P_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
and
\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
, inherit the
S_{n}
-module structure of
P_{n}
and
\Gamma_{n}
. The
S_{n}
-module structure of
\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
is known for
p=1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
(see
[
16
]
for
p=3
and
[
15
]
for
p=4
). In addition, for general values of
p
, we have the following special case of a theorem of Berele.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
1
]
Let
M(\lambda)
denote the irreducible
S_{n}
-module corresponding to the partition
\lambda
. Let

\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})=\bigoplus_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}M(\lambda).
Then there exist integers
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
such that the non-zero multiplicities
m_{\lambda}
are supported in diagrams of the form
s_{1}
s_{2}
1

Moreover, only the first column in the Young diagram of
\lambda
can grow arbitrarily large when
n
grows.

Furthermore, in
[
10
]
, we show that
s_{2}=p-1
, i.e., there can be at most
p-1
boxes in the first row of the Young diagram of
\lambda
from Theorem

.

In this paper, we find many explicit classes of partitions
\lambda\vdash n
, such that

appears with non-zero multiplicity in the

-module decomposition of
\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
for arbitrary values of

. More precisely, let

or

. Then, for the following partitions

, we have that

appears in

:

(i)

(p-1,1)
,
(p-1,p-1)
;

(ii)

(a+2,2^{b},1^{c})
such that

,
b+c>0
, and

.

(iii)

(l+3,l+1,1^{n-2l-4})
,
(l+2,l+2,1^{n-2l-4})
, and
(l+2,l+1,1^{n-2l-3})
, where
0\leq l\leq k-2
.

(iv)

If
n
is odd, then we also have the partition
(1,1,\dots,1)
.

In addition, if
p=2k+1
we have also the following partitions
\lambda\vdash n
for
n\leq 4k
:

(v)

(a+2,2^{b})
for
a\geq 0
,
b>0
, and
a+b+1=2k
.

(vi)

(a+2,2^{b},1)
for

,

, and

These results are given in Section

. In the same section,
using these results, we give lower bounds for the dimensions of
\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
and

). For

or

with

and Corollary

2.
Tensor product theorems for Lie nilpotent associative algebras

Let
K
be any field. Let
L_{p}
denote the Lie ideal in
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
generated by all commutators of length
p
. In other words,
L_{p}
is defined inductively by the formula
L_{p}=[L_{p-1},K\left\langle X\right\rangle]
, where
L_{1}=K\left\langle X\right\rangle
. Let
I_{p}=K\left\langle X\right\rangle\cdot L_{p}
denote the two-sided associative ideal in
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
generated by all commutators of length
p
. Hence,
I_{p}
is the T-ideal generated by the element
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]
.

First we recall the following two theorems on products of commutators which will be very useful in the sequel. Theorem
2.1
was first proven by Latyshev
[
13
]
in 1965 and then was independently rediscovered by Gupta and Levin
[
9
]
in 1983. The statement holds over any field
K
. Theorem
2.2
was proven by many authors for different restrictions on the characteristic of
K
(see
[
3
]
for a short account on the existing results). The most general result that we give below is due to Deryabina and Krasilnikov
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
13
]
,
[
9
]

For any integers
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
it holds that

I_{m}I_{n}\subset I_{m+n-2}.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
3
]

Let
\operatorname{char}K\neq 3
. If one of
m
and
n
is odd, then

I_{m}I_{n}\subset I_{m+n-1}.

We also need the following lemma from
[
3
]
, which can be found also in
[
8
]
for
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2,3
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
3
]

Let
\operatorname{char}K\neq 3
and let
n\geq 2
. Let
u\in I_{n}
and
x,y\in K\left\langle X\right\rangle
. Then

[u,x,y]\in I_{n+2}.

In other words, for all
n\geq 2
it holds that

[I_{n},x,y]\subset I_{n+2}.

Remark 2.4
.

We notice that when
\operatorname{char}K=3
, if we go over the proof of Lemma
2.3
given in
[
3
]
and instead of the property
I_{n}I_{3}\subset I_{n+2}
, we use the property
I_{n}I_{3}\subset I_{n+1}
(which follows from Theorem
2.1
for any characteristic of
K
), then we obtain the property

[I_{n},x,y]\subset I_{n+1}.

For completeness of the exposition, we will give a short proof of Remark
2.4
following the proof of Lemma
2.3
given in
[
3
]
.

Proof.

Let
c\in L_{n}
, and
u,v,w\in K\left\langle X\right\rangle
. To proof the remark it suffices to show that

[cu,v,w]\in I_{n+1}.

We have,

[cu,v,w]=c[u,v,w]+[c,v][u,w]+[c,w][u,v]+[c,v,w]u.

By Theorem
2.1
,
c[u,v,w]\in I_{n+1}
. It is clear that
[c,v,w]u\in I_{n+2}
. Finally, as it is shown in
[
3
]
,

[c,v][u,w]+[c,w][u,v]=[v,c][w,u]+[v,u][w,c]+[[c,w],[u,v]]\in I_{n+2}

since

[v,c][w,u]+[v,u][w,c]=-[c,vw,u]+v[c,w,u]+[c,v,u]w.

∎

We are now ready to prove the following statement, which is a generalization of Corollary 2.2 from
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
G
and
H
be unital associative algebras over any field
K
such that
G
satisfies the identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]=0
for some
p\geq 3
and
H
satisfies the identities
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=0
and
[x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]=0
for some
k\geq 2
. Then
G\otimes H
satisfies
[x_{1},\dots,x_{q}]=0
for some integer
q\geq p
.

Proof.

Using the same notations as in
[
2
]
, we set

c_{k}=[g_{1}\otimes h_{1},\dots,g_{k}\otimes h_{k}],

where
k\geq 2
,
g_{i}\in G
, and
h_{i}\in H
. Then, as it is shown in
[
2
]
,

\displaystyle c_{2}=g_{1}g_{2}\otimes h_{1}h_{2}-g_{2}g_{1}\otimes h_{2}h_{1}\pm g_{2}g_{1}\otimes h_{1}h_{2}=

\displaystyle[g_{1},g_{2}]\otimes h_{1}h_{2}+g_{2}g_{1}\otimes[h_{1},h_{2}].

We compute also
c_{3}
.

\displaystyle c_{3}=[c_{2},g_{3}\otimes h_{3}]=[g_{1},g_{2}]g_{3}\otimes h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}-g_{3}[g_{1},g_{2}]\otimes h_{3}h_{1}h_{2}+

\displaystyle g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}\otimes[h_{1},h_{2}]h_{3}-g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}\otimes h_{3}[h_{1},h_{2}]\pm g_{3}[g_{1},g_{2}]\otimes h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}.

Using the fact that
[h_{1},h_{2}]
is central in
H
, we obtain

c_{3}=[g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}]\otimes h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}+g_{3}[g_{1},g_{2}]\otimes[h_{1}h_{2},h_{3}]+[g_{2}g_{1},g_{3}]\otimes[h_{1},h_{2}]h_{3}.

We notice that in
c_{3}
there are three types of elements, which motivates the following definition. We say that an element
x\in G\otimes H
is an element of type I if
x=A\otimes f
, where
A\in L_{i}
for some integer
i
and
f\in H
;
x
is of type II if
x=B\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]
, where

B\in I_{i}
for some integer
i
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{2j}\in H
for some integer
j
; and
x
is of type III if
x=C\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1}
, where
C\in I_{i}
for some
i
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{2j+1}\in H
for some integer
j
.

The first goal is to prove that for each
k
, only elements of type I, II, and III appear in
c_{k}
. The statement trivially holds for
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
. Assume that the statement holds for
c_{k}
. Then in
c_{k+1}
we will have elements of the following types:

(1)

\displaystyle[A\otimes f,g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1}]=Ag_{k+1}\otimes fh_{k+1}-g_{k+1}A\otimes h_{k+1}f\pm g_{k+1}A\otimes fh_{k+1}=

\displaystyle[A,g_{k+1}]\otimes fh_{k+1}+g_{k+1}A\otimes[f,g_{k+1}].

Hence,
[A\otimes f,g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1}]
contains only elements of types I and II. Next, we consider

\displaystyle[B\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}],g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1}]=

\displaystyle Bg_{k+1}\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]h_{k+1}-g_{k+1}B\otimes h_{k+1}[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}].

Since,
[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]
is central in
H
, we obtain

(2)

\displaystyle[B\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}],g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1}]=

\displaystyle[B,g_{k+1}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]h_{k+1},

which is an element of type III.

Finally, we consider

\displaystyle[C\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1},g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1}]=

\displaystyle Cg_{k+1}\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1}h_{k+1}-g_{k+1}C\otimes h_{k+1}[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1}\pm

\displaystyle g_{k+1}C\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1}h_{k+1}.

Hence,

(3)

\displaystyle[C\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1},g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1}]=

\displaystyle[C,g_{k+1}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1}h_{k+1}+

\displaystyle g_{k+1}C\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}][f_{2j+1},h_{k+1}],

which consists of an element of type II and an element of type III.

Since
G
satisfies the identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]=0
, Equation (
1
) implies that for
k\geq p
there are only elements of type II and III in
c_{k}
. Furthermore, Equations (
2
) and (
3
) imply the following:

(4)

\displaystyle[B\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}],g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1},g_{k+2}\otimes h_{k+2}]=

\displaystyle[B,g_{k+1},g_{k+2}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]h_{k+1}h_{k+2}+

\displaystyle g_{k+2}[B,g_{k+1}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}][h_{k+1},h_{k+2}].

(5)

\displaystyle[C\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1},g_{k+1}\otimes h_{k+1},g_{k+2}\otimes h_{k+2}]=

\displaystyle[C,g_{k+1},g_{k+2}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}]f_{2j+1}h_{k+1}h_{k+2}+

\displaystyle g_{k+2}[C,g_{k+1}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}][f_{2j+1}h_{k+1},h_{k+2}]+

\displaystyle[g_{k+1}C,g_{k+2}]\otimes[f_{1},f_{2}]\cdots[f_{2j-1},f_{2j}][f_{2j+1},h_{k+1}]h_{k+2}.

By Lemma
2.3
, for
\operatorname{char}K\neq 3
if

, then
[B,g_{k+1},g_{k+2}]\in I_{i+2}
and similarly if
C\in I_{j}
, then
[C,g_{k+1},g_{k+2}]\in I_{j+2}
. Therefore, Equation (

) implies the following: If
c_{k}
contains the element

with

. Analogous conclusions follow from Equation (

imply that for sufficiently large

.

3.
Some concrete modules in the
S_{n}
-module structure of
\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
for arbitrary
n
and
p

In this section we take
K
to be a field of characteristic zero. We start by introducing the necessary definitions coming from the theory of algebras with polynomial identities. Then we describe concrete irreducible
S_{n}
-modules that appear with nonzero multiplicity in the decomposition of the subspace of proper multilinear polynomials in
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
. As a consequence we obtain lower bounds on the dimensions of the space of proper multilinear polynomials and the space of multilinear polynomials in
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
.

3.1.
Preliminaries

We say that a commutator
[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}}]\in K\langle X\rangle
is
pure
if all the entries in the commutator are elements of the set
X
. A polynomial in
K\langle X\rangle
is called
proper
if it is a linear combination of products of pure commutators. One important property of proper polynomials is that all identities of an algebra with a unit follow from its proper ones (
[
6
]
, Proposition 4.3.3). We recall that by
P_{n}
we denote the vector space of multilinear polynomials of degree
n
in
K\langle X_{n}\rangle
and by
\Gamma_{n}
the subspace of
P_{n}
of proper polynomials. To any polynomial from
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
one can associate its complete multilinearization,
which is a polynomial in
P_{n}
. It is well known that for an algebra over a field of characteristic zero, a polynomial is an identity if and only if its complete multilinearization is also an identity. Hence, in the study of polynomial identities for unital algebras over fields of characteristic zero, we can restrict ourselves to proper multilinear polynomials.

For an algebra
A
, we denote by
T(A)
the set of all polynomial identities of
A
.
T(A)
is an ideal in
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
that has the additional property that it is closed under all
K
-algebra endomorphisms of
A
. Such ideals are called
T-ideals
. We also take the natural quotient map

(8)

\displaystyle K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle\rightarrow K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle\cap T(A))

and define
P_{n}(A)
(respectively,
\Gamma_{n}(A)
) to be the image of
P_{n}
(resp.,
\Gamma_{n}
) under this map.
Then the dimensions

c_{n}(A)=\dim P_{n}(A)\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{n}(A)=\dim\Gamma_{n}(A)

are called respectively the
n
-th
codimension
and the
n
-th
proper codimension
of
A
(or of
T(A)
).

The following relations between
c_{n}(A)
and
\gamma_{n}(A)

We have that

c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})\geq c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq 2^{n}r(n)+s(n),

,
\deg s(n)\leq 2k-1

\frac{C_{k}}{(2k-2)!}

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 3.5 (ii) from
[

]
.
By Corollary
3.10
we have that for

\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq p_{k}(n),

where

and the leading coefficient of

is equal to
\alpha_{n}=\frac{2^{2k-2}}{(2k-2)!}C_{k}

For

we set

where

is the same polynomial as above.
We set

and for each

we have that

.

The polynomials
\binom{n+m}{m}
for

form a basis for

. Therefore, for some coefficients

p_{k}(n)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k-2}a_{m}\binom{n+m}{m}.

Notice that the leading coefficient of
\binom{n+m}{m}
for each
m
is equal to
\frac{1}{m!}
. Hence, for the coefficient
a_{2k-2}
we obtain the following expression

a_{2k-2}=\alpha_{n}(2k-2)!=2^{2k-2}C_{k}.

We define the function
p^{\prime}_{k}(n)
in the following way:

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{k}(n)=\begin{cases}p_{k}(n)&\text{ if }n\geq 2k\\
p_{k}(n)+\nu_{n}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, for all
n
it holds that
\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq p^{\prime}_{k}(n)
.

The next step is to consider the series

\displaystyle p_{k}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{\prime}_{k}(n)t^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m=0}^{2k-2}a_{m}\binom{n+m}{m}t^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{2k-1}\nu_{n}t^{n}.

Now we use the property that

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n+m}{m}t^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{m+1}}.

Hence,

p_{k}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k-2}\frac{a_{m}}{(1-t)^{m+1}}+\theta_{k}(t),

where
\theta_{k}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{2k-1}\nu_{n}t^{n}
and
\theta_{k}(t)
is a polynomial in
t
of degree at most
2k-1
.

Next, we define the series

q_{k}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{k}(n)t^{n},

where

q_{k}(n)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}p^{\prime}_{k}(n).

By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that for each
n

c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq q_{k}(n).

To compute the numbers
q_{k}(n)
we notice that

q_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{1-t}p_{k}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle q_{k}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k-2}\frac{a_{m}(1-t)^{m}}{(1-2t)^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{1-t}\theta_{k}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right).

First, we simplify the expression

\displaystyle\frac{(1-t)^{m}}{(1-2t)^{m+1}}=\frac{(1-(1-2t))^{m}}{2^{m}(1-2t)^{m+1}}=\frac{1}{2^{m}(1-2t)^{m+1}}+\rho_{m}\left(\frac{1}{1-2t}\right),

where
\rho_{m}(t)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
and
\deg\rho_{m}(t)\leq m
.

We also set

\frac{1}{1-t}\theta_{k}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)=\tau_{k}\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right),

where
\tau_{k}(t)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
and
\deg\tau_{k}(t)\leq 2k
. We also notice that
\tau_{k}(t)
has no free term.

Therefore,

\displaystyle q_{k}(t)=\frac{a_{2k-2}}{2^{2k-2}(1-2t)^{2k-1}}+\sum_{m=0}^{2k-3}\frac{a_{m}^{\prime}}{(1-2t)^{m+1}}+\tau_{k}\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right).

Here, the coefficient
a_{m}^{\prime}
comes from
a_{m}
and
\rho_{m}\left(\frac{1}{1-2t}\right)
.

We also set
\tau_{k}(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{2k}\tau_{km}t^{m}
for some coefficients
\tau_{km}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. Hence,

\tau_{k}\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{2k}\frac{\tau_{km}}{(1-t)^{m}}=\sum_{m=0}^{2k-1}\frac{\tau_{k,m+1}}{(1-t)^{m+1}}.

Now, we use again property (
9
) and obtain

\displaystyle q_{k}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\frac{a_{2k-2}}{2^{2k-2}}\binom{n+2k-2}{2k-2}+\sum_{m=0}^{2k-3}a^{\prime}_{m}\binom{n+m}{m}\right)2^{n}t^{n}+

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{2k-1}\tau_{k,m+1}\binom{n+m}{m}t^{n}.

The last equation implies that

q_{k}(n)=\left(\frac{a_{2k-2}}{2^{2k-2}}\binom{n+2k-2}{2k-2}+\sum_{m=0}^{2k-3}a^{\prime}_{m}\binom{n+m}{m}\right)2^{n}+\sum_{m=0}^{2k-1}\tau_{k,m+1}\binom{n+m}{m}.

Hence, we have that
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})\geq c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq q_{k}(n)=r(n)2^{n}+s(n)
, where
\deg r(n)=2k-2
and
\deg s(n)\leq 2k-1
and the leading coefficient of
r(n)
is equal to

\frac{a_{2k-2}}{2^{2k-2}(2k-2)!}=\frac{C_{k}}{(2k-2)!}.

∎

The bounds for
\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
and
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
that we obtain in Corollaries
3.10
and
3.11
are slightly better than the respective bounds from Corollary
3.7
. Moreover, when
k\geq 4
we have that the polynomials
g_{i}^{(j)}[x_{1},x_{2}]^{k-2}
correspond to partitions that do not appear in the decomposition of
E\otimes E_{2k-2}
. Therefore, for
k\geq 4
we can add the lower bounds found in Corollary
3.7
(i) and Corollary
3.10
to obtain a better lower bound for
\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})
and
\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})
. Namely, we have the following statement.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
k\geq 4
. Then, for
n\geq 2k
,

\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})\geq\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq P(n),

where
P(n)
is a polynomial in
n
with rational coefficients of degree
2k-2
with leading coefficient equal to

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2k-3}}{(2k-2)!}+\frac{2^{2k-2}}{(2k-2)!}C_{k}=\frac{2^{2k-3}}{(2k-2)!}(1+2C_{k}),

where again
C_{k}
is the
k
-th Catalan number.

For
n\leq 2k
, it also holds that
\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})=\Gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})=\Gamma_{n}
.

Furthermore, for
2k+1\leq n\leq 4k
we have that
\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})
is strictly bigger than
\gamma_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})
.

Similarly, if we combine Corollary
3.7
(ii) and Corollary
3.11
we obtain the following lower bound for
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})
and
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})
.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
k\geq 4
. For
n\geq 2k+1
,

c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})\geq c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})\geq 2^{n}R(n)+S(n),

where
R(n),S(n)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[n]
,
\deg R(n)=2k-2
,
\deg S(n)\leq 2k-1
and the leading coefficient of
R(n)
is

\frac{1+2C_{k}}{2(2k-2)!},

where again
C_{k}
is the
k
-th Catalan number.

For
n\leq 2k
, we have that
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})=c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})=\dim P_{n}=n!
.

Furthermore, for
2k+1\leq n\leq 4k
we have that
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k+1})
is strictly bigger than
c_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{2k})
.

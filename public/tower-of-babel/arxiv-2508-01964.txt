Optimal K − K- dual frames and pairs in the presence of erasures
Shankhadeep Mondal, Deguang Han, R. N. Mohapatra

Abstract
This paper explores the structure of optimal K − K- dual frames for a given K − K- frame and optimal K − K- dual pairs, within the context of erasures which occur during the transmission of frame coefficients. We address two distinct erasure scenarios and examine their impact on the reconstruction process. The optimality criteria are defined in terms of minimizing the spectral radius and the operator norm of the associated error operators. Through this approach, we provide a comprehensive framework for understanding and mitigating the effects of erasures in frame theory, contributing to enhanced robustness in data transmission and recovery.

Introduction

The concept of frames in Hilbert spaces were first introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer
[
16
]
in the context of addressing issues related to non-harmonic Fourier analysis. Frames can be understood as overcomplete systems that extend the concept of Riesz bases. The redundancy inherent in frames proves to be highly beneficial in various practical applications, including signal and image processing, sampling theory, data compression, where stability and flexibility in representation are crucial. In real-world communication system data loss often arises from unfavorable network conditions including channel noise, limited bandwidth or congestion. Such disruptions may cause the loss of certain frame coefficients during transmission, commonly referred to as
erasures.
. Frame redundancy plays a crucial role in counteracting these losses and improving the reliability of signal reconstruction. Motivated by these advantages, a substantial amount of recent research has been directed toward developing frame-based methods for enhancing data recovery in the presence of erasures.

Casazza and Kovačevi’c
[
24
]
have thoroughly analyzed the robustness of equal-norm tight frames against erasures, highlighting their advantage over others. Similarly, Goyal, Kovačevi’c, and Kelner
[
28
]
explored uniform tight frames within a coding-theoretic framework, identifying them as optimal for single erasures. The notion of optimal dual frames which is crucial for minimizing maximum reconstruction errors, was introduced by Holmes and Paulsen
[
30
]
, who considered the operator norm as a measure of optimality. They established both necessary and sufficient conditions under which Parseval frames and their canonical dual pairs achieve minimal reconstruction error for any possible location of erasures. In
[
22
]
, the problem was studied by defining the reconstruction error numerically as a type of average—specifically, averaging the operator norms of the error operators over all possible erasure patterns of a fixed size and then minimizing this average. Subsequent research has further refined these concepts. Lopez and Han
[
36
]
investigated sufficient conditions ensuring that the canonical dual frame is the uniquely optimal dual in the case of both one and more erasures. In the context of
1-
erasure, Pehlivan, Han and Mohapatra
[
1
]
identified dual frames that minimize the maximum error by taking spectral radius as error measurement of the associated error operators. This line of work was subsequently extended to address the case of two erasures in
[
21
,
27
]
. Given the inherent randomness in erasure events, a probabilistic model for analyzing such problems is indispensable. Leng, Han, and Huang
[
34
,
32
]
incorporated probability-weighted models to explore optimality under operator norms, providing conditions for probabilistic optimality for erasures. Optimal dual frames under probabilistic erasures were further studied in
[
3
,
4
]
.

In recent years, numerous generalized frame structures have emerged, including fusion frames, group representation frames, and
K-
frames. Among these,
K-
frames introduced by Găvruţa
[
5
]
, have received considerable attention for their utility in theoretical analysis and practical applications, particularly in the context of data loss and signal recovery. A
K-
frame extends the classical notion of frames by allowing reconstruction only within the range of a bounded linear operator
K
. Several authors have contributed to the structural and constructive development of
K
-frames and their duals. For example,
[
6
]
presents new constructions of
K
-
g
-frames by exploiting the interplay between the frame operator and a positive operator, providing necessary and sufficient conditions for their existence. The paper
[
7
]
studies
K-
frames generated via operator iterates of the form
\{T^{k}f_{0}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, focusing on the boundedness and stability of the operator
T
in relation to
K
. In
[
8
]
, the authors explore strategies for constructing
K
-dual frame pairs and identifying common
K-
dual Bessel sequences. Generalized approaches appear in
[
9
]
, where dual systems are studied in the setting of Hilbert
C^{*}
modules, including conditions under which the sum of two such frames forms a valid
c
-
K
-
g
-frame. The work in
[
10
]
investigates additive properties of
K-
frames in
n
-Hilbert spaces, while
[
11
]
introduces continuous
K
-frames (or
ck-
frames) and provides methods for constructing their duals. Additionally,
[
12
]
offers a characterization of
K
-
g
-frames using quotient operator theory, deriving necessary and sufficient conditions for duality. Together, these contributions advance the landscape of
K-
frame theory and lay the groundwork for further exploration into optimal dual design under erasure and reconstruction constraints. The literature on
K-
frames continues to grow, with additional insights and generalizations found in the works of various authors, such as those in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

This paper extends these studies by focusing on two key aspects of the erasure problem:

1.

Optimal
K-
dual pairs:
We investigate the existence and characterization of
K-
dual pairs that minimize the maximum reconstruction error across all possible erasure patterns, with error measured by the operator norm and the spectral radius.

2.

Optimal
K-
dual frames:
For a fixed
K-
frame, we examine the existence and structure of optimal
K-
dual frames under the same error measurement.

Section 2 develops the foundational framework for modeling coefficient erasures in
K-
frame systems. Section 3 addresses the pairwise optimization problem, where we analyze all possible
(N,n)

K-
dual pairs
(F,G)
. We introduce the notions of
1-
uniform
and
2-
uniform

K-
dual pairs, and show that
1-
uniformity ensures optimality for one erasure with respect to both the operator norm and the spectral radius. On the other hand,
2-
uniformity guarantees optimality for two erasures with respect to the spectral radius. We derive tight lower bounds for the maximum error in the two-erasure case and identify conditions under which these bounds are attained. In particular, we show that if a
2-
uniform
K-
dual pair exists and it achieves the optimality, which we express explicitly in terms of the trace and Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the operator
K
. We also prove that both operator norm and spectral radius optimality are preserved under unitary transformations that commute with
K
. Section 4 is devoted the fixed-frame optimization problem, where we fix a given Parseval
K-
frame
F
and consider all possible
K-
dual frames
G
of
F
. We define the maximum reconstruction error for one erasure using both the operator norm and the spectral radius, and derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the canonical
K-
dual of a Parseval
K-
frame, namely
K^{\dagger}F,
to be optimal. Furthermore, we show that when the number of frame elements exceeds the dimension of the space (
N>n
), there exist uncountably many
K
-duals of
F
that achieve the same optimal error, illustrating the non-uniqueness of optimal duals in this setting. We also provide carefully constructed examples of Parseval
K-
frames and their duals to support and clarify the theoretical results. These examples highlight both cases where the canonical dual is the unique optimal choice and cases where multiple optimal duals exist. Our findings not only extend classical results in frame theory to the setting of
K-
frames, but also provide a rigorous framework for analyzing optimal reconstruction in the presence of erasures, which is an area of fundamental importance in signal processing, coding theory, and various other engineering disciplines.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
be an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
denote the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. A finite sequence of vectors
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{H}_{n}
, with
N\geq n
, is said to form a
frame
for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
if there exist positive constants
A
and
B
such that

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2},\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.

The values
A
and
B
are referred to as the lower and upper frame bounds respectively. A frame is called
tight
if
A=B
and
Parseval
if
A=B=1
. Given a frame
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, the associated analysis operator is the map
\Theta_{F}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
, defined by
\Theta_{F}(f)=\{\langle f,f_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Its adjoint
\Theta_{F}^{*}:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathcal{H}_{n}
is called the synthesis operator and satisfies
\Theta_{F}^{*}(\{c_{i}\})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}f_{i}
. The frame operator
S_{F}=\Theta_{F}^{*}\Theta_{F}
is given by
S_{F}f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle f_{i}
, is self-adjoint, positive and invertible. A sequence
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a dual frame of
F
if every
f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
can be reconstructed as
f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle g_{i}
, equivalently
\Theta_{G}^{*}\Theta_{F}=I
. The canonical dual of
F
is
\{S_{F}^{-1}f_{i}\}
and all duals are of the form
\{S_{F}^{-1}f_{i}+u_{i}\}
, where
\{u_{i}\}
satisfies a biorthogonality condition i.e.
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle u_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,u_{i}\rangle f_{i}=0,
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.
Moreover, any dual pair
(F,G)
satisfies the relation
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(I)=n
. For further details, we refer to
[
25
]
.

Now, for any bounded operator
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
, a sequence
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{H}_{n}
is called a
K-
frame
if there exist constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

A\|K^{*}f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2},\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.

If equality holds in the lower bound, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}=A\|K^{*}f\|^{2}
, then
F
is called a
tight

K-
frame. In particular, when
A=1
, the frame is a
Parseval

K-
frame.

For a
K-
frame
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, we define the associated operators as follows:

1.

The
analysis operator

\Theta_{F}^{K}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
is defined by

\Theta_{F}^{K}(f)=\{\langle f,f_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}.

2.

The
synthesis operator

(\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathcal{H}_{n}
is given by

(\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}(\{c_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}f_{i}.

3.

The
frame operator

S_{F}^{K}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathcal{H}_{n}
is defined as the composition

S_{F}^{K}f=(\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}\Theta_{F}^{K}f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle f_{i}.

If
K
has closed range and
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a Parseval
K-
frame, then the sequence
\{K^{\dagger}f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
forms a
canonical

K-
dual of
F
, where
K^{\dagger}
is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of
K
. A Bessel sequence
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a
K-
dual of the
K-
frame
F
if it satisfies

Kf=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,g_{i}\rangle f_{i},\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.

(1)

From (
1
), it follows that
K^{*}f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle g_{i}.
In general, the sequences
\{f_{i}\}
and
\{g_{i}\}
are not interchangeable in (
1
) unless
K
is self-adjoint. A pair
(F,G)
of
N
element sequences is called an
(N,n)

K-
dual pair if
G
is a
K-
dual of the
K-
frame
F
and
F
is a
K^{*}-
dual of
G
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
G
is a
K-
dual of
F
, then
F
is a
K^{*}-
dual of
G
.

In practical scenarios, due to data loss some partial coefficients of
\Theta_{F}^{K}(f)
may be unavailable. Let
E
be a diagonal matrix with entries 0 or 1 indicating erased or received coefficients, and define
D=I-E
. The reconstructed signal is given by

\widehat{Kf}=(\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}E\Theta_{G}^{K}f,

and the corresponding reconstruction error becomes

Kf-\widehat{Kf}=(\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{G}^{K}f.

If
\Lambda
denotes the index set of the erased coefficients, then

Kf-\widehat{Kf}=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\langle f,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}.

For a
K-
frame
F
and a
K-
dual
G
, the worst-case error for all possible
m-
erasures is expressed as

\max\left\{\mathcal{M}\left((\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}\right):D\in\mathcal{D}_{m}\right\},

where
\mathcal{M}
is an appropriate error measure and
\mathcal{D}_{m}
is the collection of all diagonal matrices with exactly
m
ones.

3
Characterization of Optimal
K-
Dual Pairs

Our objective in this section is to determine the optimal
K-
dual pairs that minimize the maximum reconstruction error. To facilitate this analysis, we introduce the concepts of
1-
uniform and
2-
uniform
K-
duals, which play a crucial role in studying optimality with respect to both the operator norm and the spectral radius of the corresponding error operator.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a
K-
frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. A
K-
dual
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
F
is called a
1-
uniform
K-
dual
if there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=c\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq N.
In this case, the pair
(F,G)
is called
1-
uniform
K-
dual pair
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
(F,G)
is a
1-
uniform
K-
dual pair, then the constant
c
turns out to be
\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(K)}{N}
, since

\operatorname{tr}(K)=\operatorname{tr}\left((\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}\Theta_{G}^{K}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(\Theta_{G}^{K}(\Theta_{F}^{K})^{*}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=cN.

Definition 3.4
.

A
1-
uniform
K-
dual pair
(F,G)
is called
2-
uniform
K-
dual pair
if there exists a constant
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\langle f_{i},g_{j}\rangle\langle f_{j},g_{i}\rangle=c^{\prime},\quad\text{for all }1\leq i,j\leq N\text{ with }i\neq j.
In this case, the sequence
G
is called a
2-
uniform
K-
dual
of
F
.

3.1
Operator norm based optimality of
K-
dual pairs

In this subsection, we study optimality of an
(N,n)

K-
dual pair
(F,G)
for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
by using the operator norm as the error measurement
\mathcal{M}
. Specifically, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G):=\max\left\{\left\|(\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}\right\|:D\in\mathcal{D}_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{1}:=\inf\left\{\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G):(F,G)\;\text{is an }(N,n)\;K\text{-dual pair for }\mathcal{H}_{n}\right\}.

Here,
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)
quantifies the worst-case reconstruction error for
1-
erasures and
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{1}
represents the infimum of this error across all possible
(N,n)

K
-dual pairs. A
K-
dual pair
(F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
is said to be a
1-
erasure optimal with respect to the operator norm
if
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F^{\prime},G^{\prime})=\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{1}.

For a given
(N,n)

K
-dual pair
(F,G)
, if the erasure occurs in the
i-
th position, the associated operator norm of the error operator becomes
\left\|(\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}\right\|=\sup\limits_{\|f\|=1}\|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle g_{i}\|=\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|g_{i}\|.
Therefore, we have the identity:

\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)=\max\bigg\{\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|g_{i}\|:1\leq i\leq N\bigg\}.

(2)

We now proceed to characterize those
(N,n)

K-
dual pairs that achieve
1-
erasure optimality with respect to the operator norm.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
be a positive operator. Then,
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{1}=\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(K)}{N}.
Moreover, an
(N,n)

K-
dual pair
(F,G)
is a
1-
erasure optimal
K-
dual pair with respect to the operator norm if and only if
\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|g_{i}\|=\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(K)}{N},\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq N.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)<1,
for some
(N,n)

K-
dual pair
(F,G).
Then,
\|f_{i}\|\,\|g_{i}\|<\frac{tr(K)}{N},
for all
1\leq i\leq N.
This leads to
tr(K)=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle\right|\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}\|\,\|g_{i}\|<\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}=tr(K),
which is not possible. Thus,
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)\geq\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},
for every
K-
dual pair
(F,G)
. Let
F^{\prime}
be a uniform Parseval frame, whose existence is guaranteed by Theorem 2.1 in
[
43
]
. In particular, we consider the case where
S
is the identity operator, whose spectrum consists only of the eigenvalue
1
and we take
a_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{N}}
for all
i
.
As
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}=\frac{kn}{N}\leq k,
for
1\leq k\leq n
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}^{2}=n,
there exists a Parseval frame
\{f^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
such that
\|f^{\prime}_{i}\|^{2}=\frac{n}{N}.
Then
K^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\prime}
is a
K-
frame with
K-
dual
K^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\prime},
since
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\rangle K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}=K^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle K^{\frac{1}{2}}f,f^{\prime}_{i}\rangle f^{\prime}_{i}=Kf,
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.
Also,
\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\|\leq\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\,\|f^{\prime}_{i}\|
and
\|f^{\prime}_{i}\|=\|K^{-\frac{1}{2}}K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\|\leq\|K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\,\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\|,
for all
i.
Thus,
\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\|=\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\,\|f^{\prime}_{i}\|
and hence,
K^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\prime}
is a uniform
K-
frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
K-
dual
K^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\prime}.
Using the fact that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i},K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\rangle=tr(K)
we have
\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\|=\sqrt{\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}}.
Therefore,
\mathbb{O}_{1}(K^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\prime},K^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\prime})=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\left\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}f^{\prime}_{i}\right\|^{2}=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}.

Now, let
(F,G)
be a
1-
erasure optimal
K-
dual pair. Then,
\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\|f_{i}\|\;\|g_{i}\|=\frac{tr(K)}{N}.
If for any
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\},

\|f_{j}\|\;\|g_{j}\|<\frac{tr(K)}{N},
then
tr(K)=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle\right|\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}\|\;\|g_{i}\|<\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}=tr(K),
which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\|f_{i}\|\;\|g_{i}\|=\frac{tr(K)}{N},\,\forall\;i.
Conversely, if
\|f_{i}\|\;\|g_{i}\|=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;\forall\,1\leq i\leq N,
then obviously,
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}
and hence
(F,G)
is a
1-
erasure optimal
K-
dual pair.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

For any positive operator
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n}),
there always exists a
K
-frame
F
such that the
K-
dual pair
(F,F)
is a

The following corollary establishes a link between

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
be a positive operator. Then any
K-
dual pair that is
1-
erasure optimal with respect to the operator norm is necessarily a
1-
uniform
K-
dual pair.

Proof.

Let
(F,G)
be a
1-
erasure optimal
K-
dual pair. It follows from Proposition
3.5
that,
\|f_{i}\|\,\|g_{i}\|=\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(K)}{N},\quad\forall i.
So,

tr(K)=\displaystyle{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle\right|}\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle\right|}\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}\|\;\|g_{i}\|}=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{tr(K)}{N}=tr(K)}.

This implies that
\left|\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle\right|=\|f_{j}\|\,\|g_{j}\|,\;\forall\,1\leq j\leq N.
Taking
\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle=a_{j}+ib_{j},\,\text{where}\;a_{j},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\;1\leq j\leq N,
we get
\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}=tr(K)}
,
\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}=0}
and
\sqrt{a_{j}^{2}+b_{j}^{2}}=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;\;1\leq j\leq N.
This gives

tr(K)=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}}\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sqrt{a_{j}^{2}+b_{j}^{2}}}=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{tr(K)}{N}}=tr(K).

This shows that
b_{j}=0
and
a_{j}=|a_{j}|,\,\forall\,j.
Therefore,
\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle=\frac{tr(K)}{N},

\forall\,1\leq j\leq N.

∎

3.2
Spectral Radius based optimality of
K-
Dual Pair

Here we take the error measure
\mathcal{M}
to be the spectral radius
\rho
of the corresponding error operator. For an
(N,n)

K-
dual pair
(F,G)
for
\mathcal{H}_{n},
let us define
:

\displaystyle r_{m}(F,G):=\max\bigg\{\rho\left((\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}\right):D\in\mathcal{D}_{m}\bigg\},\;1\leq m\leq N

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{1}:=\inf\bigg\{r_{1}(F,G):\textit{$(F,G)$ \text{is an $(N,n)\;$ $K-$dual pair for} $\mathcal{H}_{n}$}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}:=\bigg\{(F,G):r_{1}(F,G)=\tilde{r}_{1}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{m}:=\inf\bigg\{r_{m}(F,G):(F,G)\in\mathcal{R}_{m-1}\bigg\},\;\;1<m\leq N

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}:=\bigg\{(F,G)\in\mathcal{R}_{m-1}:r_{m}(F,G)=\tilde{r}_{m}\bigg\},\;\;1<m\leq N.

The members of
\mathcal{R}_{m}
, for
1\leq m\leq N
, are referred to as
m-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual pairs
. In the specific case when
m=1
, it is straightforward to observe that if the erasure occurs at the
i^{\text{th}}
position, then the spectral radius of the error operator satisfies
\rho\left((\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}\right)=|\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|,
since the matrix
(\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}
has eigenvalues
0
and
\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle r_{1}(F,G)=\max\bigg\{|\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|:1\leq i\leq N\bigg\}.

(3)

We now derive an useful and explicit expression for
r_{2}(F,G)
, which quantifies the maxium error under two erasures. Consider

\displaystyle r_{2}(F,G)=\max\left\{\rho\left((\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}D\Theta_{F}^{K}\right):D\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\right\}=\max\left\{\rho\left(D\Theta_{F}^{K}(\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}\right):D\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\right\}.

By setting
D=diag[0,0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0],
the matrix representation
\mathcal{A}
of
D\Theta_{F}^{K}(\Theta_{G}^{K})^{*}
with respect to the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\alpha_{1i}&\alpha_{2i}&\cdots&\alpha_{ii}&\cdots&\alpha_{ji}&\cdots&\alpha_{Ni}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\alpha_{1j}&\alpha_{2j}&\cdots&\alpha_{ij}&\cdots&\alpha_{jj}&\cdots&\alpha_{Nj}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha_{ij}:=\langle g_{i},f_{j}\rangle,\forall\,i,j.

The characteristic polynomial of the above matrix
\mathcal{A}
is given by

\displaystyle\det(\mathcal{A}-xI)

\displaystyle=(-x)^{i-1}(-x)^{N-j}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}-x+\alpha_{ii}&q_{i}\alpha_{(i+1)i}&\cdots&\alpha_{(j-1)i}&\alpha_{ji}\\
0&-x&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&-x&0\\
\alpha_{ij}&\alpha_{(i+1)j}&\cdots&\alpha_{(j-1)j}&-x+\alpha_{jj}\\
\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=(-x)^{N-2}\left((x-\alpha_{ii})(x-\alpha_{jj})-\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{ji}\right).

Therefore, the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
are
0,\,\dfrac{\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}\pm\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{ji}}}{2}.
Hence,

r_{2}(F,G)=\max_{i\neq j}\left|\frac{\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}\pm\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{ji}}}{2}\right|.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let

Proof.

For any

3.5

\langle K^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{i},K^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{i}\rangle=\left\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{i}\right\|^{2}=\left\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}\,\left\|f_{i}\right\|^{2}=\frac{tr(K)}{N}

Thus,

Now, suppose

Then,
\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\;|\langle f^{\prime}_{j},g^{\prime}_{j}\rangle|=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}.

Clearly,

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\langle f^{\prime}_{j},g^{\prime}_{j}\rangle=tr(K)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\langle f^{\prime}_{j},g^{\prime}_{j}\rangle|.

Then,
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}=tr(K)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sqrt{a_{j}^{2}+b_{j}^{2},}

Conversely, if a

In the preceding section, we examined the notion of optimality by considering all possible

4.1
Optimal
K-
duals with respect to operator norm

Let
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
. Given a frame
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, we define

\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{1}(F):=\inf\left\{\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G):G\text{ is a $K-$dual of }F\right\},

A dual frame
G^{\prime}
of
F
is said to be a
1-
erasure optimal
K
-dual of
F
under the operator norm
if

\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G^{\prime})=\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{1}(F),

where
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)
is defined as in Subsection 3.1.

The following proposition gives an adequate condition ensuring that the canonical
K-
dual of
F
attains this optimality. To that end, define

L:=\max\left\{\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|K^{\dagger}f_{i}\|:1\leq i\leq N\right\},

and set

I_{1}:=\left\{i:\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|K^{\dagger}f_{i}\|=L\right\},\quad I_{2}:=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\setminus I_{1}.

Let
V_{j}:=\mathrm{span}\{f_{i}:i\in I_{j}\}
for
j=1,2
. The sufficient condition for optimality will be expressed in terms of these subspaces.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a Parseval
K-
frame for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, where
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})

V_{1}
and
V_{2}

K-
dual of
F
achieves

Proof.

We proceed by proof of contradiction. Suppose there exists a
K-

G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
F
such that
\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,G)\leq\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,K^{\dagger}F).
Then by (
2

We can express the dual
G
as
\{K^{\dagger}f_{i}+u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},
where
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,u_{i}\rangle f_{i}=0,

So, we may write

which in turn leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\|u_{i}\|^{2}+2Re\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\langle K^{\dagger}f_{i},u_{i}\rangle\bigg)\leq 0.

(8)

Now, the condition

In other words, taking

Thus, from (
8

which gives

Therefore,

and hence

is a
1-
erasure optimal
K-
dual of
F.

\displaystyle f_{i_{1}}=cf_{i_{2}}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}c_{\ell_{k}}f_{\ell_{k}},\;\;c,c_{k}\neq 0

(11)

and
\{f_{i_{2}},f_{\ell_{1}},f_{\ell_{2}},\ldots,f_{\ell_{m}}\}
is linearly independent. As,
f_{i_{2}}\notin span\{f_{\ell_{1}},f_{\ell_{2}},\ldots,f_{\ell_{m}}\},
there exists
v\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that
\langle v,f_{\ell_{k}}\rangle=0,
for all
1\leq k\leq m
and
\langle v,f_{i_{2}}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{\bar{c}}\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}-\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{2}},f_{i_{2}}\rangle\right)\neq 0.
Taking
V=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
as

\displaystyle v_{i}=\begin{cases}-v,\;\;&for\;i=i_{1},\\
\bar{c}v,\;\;&for\;i=i_{2},\\
\bar{c_{i}}v,\;\;&for\;i=\ell_{k},1\leq k\leq m,\\
0,\;\;&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

we get
G^{\prime\prime}=\{g^{\prime}_{i}+v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
as a dual of
F
for,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\langle f,v_{i}\rangle f_{i}=\langle f,-v\rangle f_{i_{1}}+\langle f,\bar{c}v\rangle f_{i_{2}}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}\langle f,\overline{c_{\ell_{k}}}v\rangle f_{\ell_{k}}=\langle f,v\rangle\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}c_{\ell_{k}}f_{\ell_{k}}+cf_{i_{2}}-f_{i_{1}}\right)=0,\;f\in\mathcal{H}_{n},

by (
11
). Now,
\langle g^{\prime\prime}_{i_{2}},f_{i_{2}}\rangle=\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{2}}+\bar{c}v,f_{i_{2}}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}
and for

Further, for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{i_{1},i_{2},\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\},\;\langle g^{\prime\prime}_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{i}\rangle.
This suggests that
I_{G^{\prime}}\subset I_{G^{\prime\prime}}
and
i_{2}\in I_{G^{\prime\prime}}.
In other words,
|I_{G^{\prime\prime}}|>|I_{G^{\prime}}|.
By iteratively applying this procedure, we eventually obtain after at most

steps, a dual
\tilde{G}
for which

. This implies that
r_{1}^{q}(F,\tilde{G})=1
. Consequently, even in this scenario, we conclude that
\tilde{r}_{1}(F)=\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(K)}{N}
, and the dual
\tilde{G}
is indeed a
1-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual of
F
.

Next we want to extend the preceding theorem to an arbitrary

\mathcal{H}_{n}
corresponding to a

\delta_{i}^{K}:=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K|_{H_{i}})}{|\Lambda_{i}|},\quad\text{for }i=1,2,\ldots,J,

denotes the trace of the restriction of

to the subspace

redundancy distribution
of the frame

.

Lemma 4.24
.

Let

As

\displaystyle f_{i_{1}}=cf_{i_{2}}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}c_{\ell_{k}}f_{\ell_{k}},\;\;c,c_{k}\neq 0

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\langle f,u_{i}\rangle f_{i}=\langle f,-u\rangle f_{i_{1}}+\langle f,\bar{c_{2}}u\rangle f_{i_{2}}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}\langle f,\overline{c_{\ell_{k}}}u\rangle f_{\ell_{k}}=\langle f,u\rangle\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}c_{\ell_{k}}f_{\ell_{k}}+cf_{i_{2}}-f_{i_{1}}\right)=0,\;f\in\mathcal{H}_{n},

Lemma 4.25
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a
K-

\mathcal{H}_{n},
where
K
be a positive semi-definite operator. Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}=H_{1}\bigoplus H_{2}\bigoplus\cdots\bigoplus H_{i},
where
{H_{i}}^{\prime}s
are from linearly connected decomposition of
F.
Let each
H_{i},\,1\leq i\leq J
is
K-
invariant. Then there exists a
K-
dual
G_{j}
of
F_{j}
such that
\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\delta_{j}^{K},
for all

and for each

Moreover, there exist a
K-
dual
G^{\prime}
of
F
such that
\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\delta_{j}^{K},
for all
i\in\Lambda_{j}.

Proof.

First we will prove that each
F_{j}=\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}
is a linearly connected
K|_{H_{j}}
frame for
H_{j}.
For any
f\in H_{j},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{2}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\cdots+\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{J}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq A\|K^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=A\|K^{*}|_{H_{j}}f\|^{2},

for some constant
A>0,
as
K^{*}
is invariant on
H_{j}.
Also,
\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq\|f\|^{2}\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\|f_{i}\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2},
where
B=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}\|^{2}>0.
Therefore,
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}
satisfy the
K|_{H_{j}}-
frame inequality for
H_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq J.
Hence, by Theorem
4.23
, there exist a
K|_{H_{j}}-
dual
G^{j}=\{g^{j}_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}
such that
\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K|_{H_{j}})}{|\Lambda_{j}|}=\delta_{j}^{K},
for all
1\leq j\leq J.

Consider the collection
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{J}(P_{j})^{*}G^{j},
where
P_{j}
is the projection of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
onto
H_{j}.
We shall show that
G
is a dual of
F.
Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
x
has the unique representation

where
f_{j}=P_{j}(x),\;j=1,2,\ldots,J.
Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\langle f,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{2}}\langle f,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\cdots+\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{J}}\langle f,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\langle f,{(P_{1})}^{*}g^{1}_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{2}}\langle f,{(P_{2})}^{*}g^{2}_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\cdots+\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{J}}\langle f,{(P_{J})}^{*}g^{J}_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\langle f_{1},g^{1}_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{2}}\langle f_{2},g^{2}_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\cdots+\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{J}}\langle f_{J},g^{J}_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{1}+f_{2}+\cdots+f_{J}=f,

by utilizing the dual relationship between the sequences
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}
and
\{g^{j}_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}},

1\leq j\leq J
. Furthermore, for

\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\langle(P_{j})^{*}g^{j}_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\langle g^{j}_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K|_{H_{j}})}{|\Lambda_{j}|}=\delta_{j}^{K}.

Theorem 4.26
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a
K
-frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, where
K
is a positive semi-definite operator and
\{\Lambda_{j}\}
,
\{H_{j}\}
denote the index sets and corresponding subspaces arising from a linearly connected decomposition of
F
. Assume that each
H_{j}
is invariant under
K
. If
\{\delta_{j}^{K}\}_{j=1}^{J}
denotes the
K-
redundancy distribution of
F
, then
\tilde{r}_{1}(F)=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq J}\delta_{j}^{K}=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq J}\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(K|_{H_{j}})}{|\Lambda_{j}|}.

Proof.

For any
K-
dual
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
F,\,

\left\{P_{j}g_{i}\right\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}
is a
K|_{H_{j}}-
dual of
F_{j}=\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}},
for all
1\leq j\leq J.
For any
h\in H_{j},

\displaystyle K|_{H_{j}}(h)

\displaystyle=K(h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle h,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle P_{j}h,g_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\langle h,P_{1}g_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{2}}\langle h,P_{2}g_{i}\rangle f_{i}+\cdots+\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{J}}\langle h,P_{J}g_{i}\rangle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\langle h,P_{j}g_{i}\rangle f_{i}.

It is easy to see that
\langle P_{j}g_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\langle g_{i},P_{j}f_{i}\rangle=\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle,
for all
i\in\Lambda_{j}
and
1\leq j\leq J.
Thus,

r_{1}\left(F_{j},\left\{P_{j}g_{i}\right\}_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\right)=\max\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\left|\langle f_{i},p_{j}g_{i}\rangle\right|\geq\dfrac{tr(K|_{H_{j}})}{|\Lambda_{j}|}=\delta_{j}^{K}
and hence,
\max\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\left|\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right|\geq\delta_{j}^{K},\,
for all

Consequently,
r_{1}(F,G)=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right|=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq J}\max\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\left|\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right|\geq\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq J}\delta_{j}^{K}.
By Lemma
4.25
,
F
has a
K-
dual
G^{\prime}
such that
\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\delta_{j}^{K},
for all
i\in\Lambda_{j}
and for each

Thus
r_{1}(F,G^{\prime})=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq J}\delta_{j}^{K}
and hence,
\tilde{r}_{1}(F)=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq J}\delta_{j}^{K}.

The following theorem establishes a sufficient condition ensuring that the canonical dual serves as a
1-
erasure spectrally optimal dual for a given Parseval
K-
frame
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Define
M=\max\left\{\left\|K^{\dagger\,1/2}f_{i}\right\|^{2}:1\leq i\leq N\right\},\\
J_{1}=\left\{i:\left\|K^{\dagger\,1/2}f_{i}\right\|^{2}=M\right\},\;J_{2}=\{1,2,\dots,N\}\setminus J_{1}
and
W_{j}=\operatorname{span}\left\{f_{i}:i\in J_{j}\right\}.

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a Parseval
K-
frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n},
where
K
be a positive semi-definite operator on
\mathcal{H}_{n}.
If
W_{1}\cap W_{2}=\{0\}
, then the canonical
K-
dual is indeed a
1-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual of
F
.

Proof.

Let
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}=\{K^{\dagger}f_{i}+u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a dual of
F,
wherein
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle f,u_{i}\rangle f_{i}=0},\;\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.
Further, using the condition
W_{1}\cap W_{2}=\{0\},
we obtain
\sum\limits_{i\in J_{1}}\langle f,u_{i}\rangle f_{i}=0,\;f\in\mathcal{H}_{n}.
This leads to
\Theta_{F_{1}}^{*}\Theta_{U_{1}}=0,\;\textit{where}\;F_{1}=\{f_{i}\}_{i\in J_{1}}\;\textit{and}\;\;U_{1}=\{u_{i}\}_{i\in J_{1}},
and so
tr\left(\Theta_{F_{1}}^{*}\Theta_{U_{1}}\right)=tr\left(\Theta_{U_{1}}\Theta_{F_{1}}^{*}\right)=\sum\limits_{i\in J_{1}}\langle f_{i},u_{i}\rangle=0.
Consequently,

\displaystyle Re\left(\sum\limits_{i\in J_{1}}\langle f_{i},u_{i}\rangle\right)=0.

(13)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\;|\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|\;\geq\;\max_{i\in J_{1}}\;|\langle f_{i},K^{\dagger}f_{i}\rangle+\langle f_{i},u_{i}\rangle|=\max_{i\in J_{1}}\left|M+\langle f_{i},u_{i}\rangle\right|.

If
Re\langle f_{i},u_{i}\rangle=0,\,\forall i\in J_{1},
then
\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\;|\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|\geq M.
Now, assume that there is an element
j\in J_{1}
satisfying
Re\langle f_{j},u_{j}\rangle>0,
then
\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|>M.
We observe that if
Re\langle f_{j},u_{j}\rangle<0
for some
j\in J_{1},
then there exists a
j^{\prime}\in J_{1}
for which
Re\langle f_{j^{\prime}},u_{j^{\prime}}\rangle>0,
by (
13
). Therefore,
r_{1}(F,G)\geq M=r_{1}(F,K^{\dagger}F).
Consequently, the canonical dual emerges as a
1-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual corresponding to
F
.

Proof.

Proposition 4.30

for all

Now, for all

\displaystyle\langle K^{\dagger}f_{i}+tv_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\left\|\left(K^{\dagger}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{i}\right\|^{2}+t\langle v_{i},f_{i}\rangle=\left|\langle K^{\dagger}f_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right|+t\langle u_{i},f_{i}\rangle=M+t\langle u_{i},f_{i}\rangle

\left|\langle K^{\dagger}f_{j}+tv_{j},f_{j}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle K^{\dagger}f_{j},f_{j}\rangle+t\langle v_{j},f_{j}\rangle\right|<M,
for each
j\in\Lambda_{2}.
Taking
\widetilde{t}=\min\limits_{i\in\Lambda_{1},j\in J_{2}}\left\{t_{i},t^{\prime}_{j}\right\},
we have, for
t\in(-\tilde{t},\tilde{t}),\;

\left|\langle K^{\dagger}f_{i}+tv_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right|<M,
for any
1\leq i\leq N.
Hence, for the dual
G_{t}=\{K^{\dagger}f_{i}+tv_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},

r_{1}(F,G_{t})<M=r_{1}(F,G),\;\forall t\in(-\tilde{t},\tilde{t}).

∎

Next, under suitable conditions, we derive a simplified expression for
r_{2}(F,G)
in terms of
r_{1}(F,G)
, as stated in the following two theorems.

Proposition 4.31
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a
K-
frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}.
Let
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a dual of
F
satisfying

(i)

\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\geq 0,\;\textit{for all}\;1\leq i\leq N

(ii)

\langle g_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g_{j},f_{i}\rangle=c\geq 0,\;\text{for all}\;\;i\neq j.

Then,

\displaystyle r_{2}(F,G)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\max\limits_{i\in\Delta^{c}}\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle+\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\max\limits_{i\in\Delta^{c}}\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right)^{2}+4c}\right),\;\;&\text{if}\;c>0\;\text{and}\;|\Delta|=1,\\
~\\
r_{1}(F,G)+\sqrt{c},\;\;&\text{if}\;c>0\;\text{and}\;|\Delta|>1,\\
~\\
r_{1}(F,G),\;\;&\text{if}\;c=0,\par\end{cases}

where
\Delta=\bigg\{i:\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle=r_{1}(F,G)\bigg\}.

Proof.

Suppose that
c>0.
We begin by considering the case when
\Delta=\{k\}.
Let
\Delta_{1}:=\left\{i:\max\limits_{j\in\Delta^{c}}\,\langle g_{j},f_{j}\rangle=\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right\}
and
\ell\in\Delta_{1}.
A straightforward computation shows that for any
i
the function
\theta_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left|x+\alpha_{ii}+\sqrt{(x-\alpha_{ii})^{2}+4c}\right|,
where
\alpha_{ij}=\langle g_{i},f_{j}\rangle,
is an increasing function on
[0,\infty).
This would result in
\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq N\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\theta_{i}(\alpha_{jj})=\theta_{i}(\alpha_{kk}),
when
i\neq k
and
\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq N\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}\theta_{k}(\alpha_{jj})=\theta_{k}(\alpha_{\ell\ell}).
Therefore,

\displaystyle r_{2}(F,G)=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq N\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\theta_{i}(\alpha_{jj})=\max\bigg\{\theta_{k}(\alpha_{\ell\ell}),\theta_{i}(\alpha_{kk}):1\leq i\leq N,i\neq k\bigg\}.

As
\theta_{i}(\alpha_{jj})=\theta_{j}(\alpha_{ii}),
for
i\neq j,
we have

\displaystyle r_{2}(F,G)

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{i\neq k}\theta_{k}(\alpha_{ii})=\theta_{k}(\alpha_{\ell\ell})=\frac{1}{2}\left|\alpha_{\ell\ell}+\alpha_{kk}+\sqrt{(\alpha_{\ell\ell}-\alpha_{kk})^{2}+4c}\right|

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\max\limits_{i\in\Delta^{c}}\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle+\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\max\limits_{i\in\Delta^{c}}\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\right)^{2}+4c}\right).

On the other hand, suppose
|\Delta|\geq 2.
Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\Delta.
Then, proceeding along the lines of the earlier argument, we get
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
in the place of
k
and
\ell
in (
14
). Hence,
r_{2}(F,G)=r_{1}(F,G)+\sqrt{c}.

Let us now assume that
c=0
. Utilizing part (i) and the representation of
r_{2}(F,G)
as provided in (
4
), it follows that

r_{2}(F,G)=\frac{1}{2}\max\limits_{i\neq j}\left\{\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}+\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}}\right\}=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\alpha_{ii}=r_{1}(F,G),

thereby completing the proof of the theorem.

∎

Theorem 4.32
.

Let
F=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a
K-
frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. Let
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a
K-
dual of
F
satisfying

(i)

\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle\geq 0,\;\textit{for all}\;1\leq i\leq N.

(ii)

\langle g_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g_{j},f_{i}\rangle=c<0,\;\text{for all}\;\;i\neq j.

If
\Delta=\bigg\{i:\langle g_{i},f_{i}\rangle=r_{1}(F,G)\bigg\}
contains more than one element, then
r_{2}(F,G)=\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.

Proof.

By using condition (ii) in (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle r_{2}(F,G)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\max_{i\neq j}\left|\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}\pm\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c}\right|.

One can verify that
r_{2}(F,G)=\frac{1}{2}\max\limits_{i\neq j}\left|\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}+\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c}\right|,
whether
\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c}
is real or complex. Define
\gamma_{ij}:=\frac{1}{2}\left|\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}+\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c}\right|.
It is an easy computation that

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{ii}+\alpha_{jj}+\sqrt{(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c}\right),\;\;&\text{if}\;(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c\geq 0,\\
~\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{ii}\alpha_{jj}-c},\;\;&\text{if}\;(\alpha_{ii}-\alpha_{jj})^{2}+4c<0.\end{cases}

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\Delta.
Then clearly,
\gamma_{k_{1}k_{2}}=\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.
Let
\ell_{1}\notin\Delta.
When
\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}\right)^{2}+4c\geq 0,
we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{k_{1}\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}+\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}\right)^{2}+4c}\;\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}+\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=r_{1}(F,G),

as
\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}<r_{1}(F,G).
Moreover,
r_{1}(F,G)<\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.
So,
\gamma_{k_{1}\ell_{1}}<\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.
On the other hand, when
\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}\right)^{2}+4c<0,
then clearly

\gamma_{k_{1}\ell_{1}}=\sqrt{r_{1}(F,G)\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}-c}<\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.

Now, let us take
\ell_{2}\notin\Delta.
All that remains to prove is that
\gamma_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.
First, we consider the case when
\left(\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c\geq 0.
Suppose, on the contrary, we have
\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}<\gamma_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}+\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}+\sqrt{\left(\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c}\right).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\leq\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}.
Moreover, as
\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}<r_{1}(F,G),
we obtain
0\leq\left(\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c<\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c.
Therefore, we get
\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}<\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}+\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c}\right).
Then,

\displaystyle\sqrt{4\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-4c}-\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c}<r_{1}(F,G)+\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}.

(15)

Now,
\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}<4\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2},
which implies that
\sqrt{4\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-4c}\;-\\
\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c}>0.
Therefore, squaring (
15
) and simplifying, we obtain

\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-r_{1}(F,G)\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}<\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}\;\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c}.

The left hand side is positive and hence on squaring again, we get

\displaystyle\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle<\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}-4c^{2},

which may be simplified as

0<c\left(\left(2{r_{1}}(F,G)\right)^{2}-\left(r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}-4c\right)=c\bigg(\left(3r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)-4c\bigg).

We observe that each of the terms
\left(3r_{1}(F,G)-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)
,
\left(r_{1}(F,G)+\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)
, and the term
-4c
on the right-hand side is strictly positive. Consequently, the above inequality is not valid. Therefore,
\gamma_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.

Finally, if
\left(\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}-\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}\right)^{2}+4c<0,
then
\gamma_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}=\sqrt{\alpha_{\ell_{1}\ell_{1}}\alpha_{\ell_{2}\ell_{2}}-c}<\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.
Thus, we conclude that
r_{2}(F,G)=\max\limits_{i\neq j}\gamma_{ij}=\sqrt{\left(r_{1}(F,G)\right)^{2}-c}.

∎

We now present a sufficient condition under which a given
K-
dual frame achieves spectral optimality with respect to both one and two erasures.

Theorem 4.33
.

Let
F
be a
K-
frame for a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. If
G
is a
2-
uniform
K-
dual of
F
, then
G
is spectrally optimal for two erasures.

Proof.

First consider the case when
tr(K)\geq 0.
For a
2-
uniform
K-
dual frame
G
of
F,
we have,
\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;\forall i
and so
r_{1}(F,G)=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}.
As
r_{1}(F,G^{\prime})\geq\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},
for any
K-
dual
G^{\prime}
of
F,
as established in Theorem
3.9
, we have that
\tilde{r}_{1}(F)=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}
and further,
G
is a
1-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual of
F.
The fact that
\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;\forall\,i
also suggests that
\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle\geq 0\;\forall\;i
and
|\Delta|=N,
where
\Delta
is as in Theorem
4.31
. Therefore, by Theorems
4.31
and
4.32
,

r_{2}(F,G)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{c},\;\;&\text{if $c\geq 0,$}\\
~\\
\sqrt{\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}-c},\;\;\;\;&\text{if $c<0$},\end{cases}

where
c=\langle g_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g_{j},f_{i}\rangle,\;i\neq j.
We may write

cN(N-1)=\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\langle g_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g_{j},f_{i}\rangle=tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N},

using (
7
), from which we obtain

\displaystyle c=\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}.

(16)

Explicitly,

r_{2}(F,G)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}},\;\;&\text{if $tr(K)\geq\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N},$}\\
~\\
\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}},\;\;\;\;&\text{if $tr(K)<\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}$}.\end{cases}

Next, we shall show that
r_{2}(F,G^{\prime})\geq\begin{cases}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{c},\;\;&\text{if $c\geq 0,$}\\
~\\
\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-c},\;\;\;\;&\text{if $c<0$},\end{cases}

for any
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(F),
so that
\tilde{r}_{2}(F)=r_{2}(F,G).
For
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(F),\;r_{1}(F,G^{\prime})=\tilde{r}_{1}(F)=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}.
In other words,
\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\langle f_{i},g^{\prime}_{i}\rangle\right|=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},
which implies
\langle f_{i},g^{\prime}_{i}\rangle\leq\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;\forall\,i,
as
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a real Hilbert space. We note that
tr(K)=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\langle f_{i},g^{\prime}_{i}\rangle\leq\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}=tr(K),
which forces
\langle f_{i},g^{\prime}_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\,\forall\,i.
Equivalently,
G^{\prime}
is a
1-
uniform
K-
dual of
F.
Therefore,

r_{2}(F,G^{\prime})=\max_{i\neq j}\left|\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j},f_{i}\rangle}\right|,

analogous to (
5
).

Let us now analyze the scenario in which
c\geq 0
. There exist
i_{0}\neq j_{0}
such that
\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{0}},f_{j_{0}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j_{0}},f_{i_{0}}\rangle\geq\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j},f_{i}\rangle}{N(N-1)}=\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)},
as demonstrated in the proof of Theorem
3.12
. By employing the
1-
uniformity property of
G^{\prime},
(
7
) and (
16
), we get
\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{0}},f_{j_{0}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j_{0}},f_{i_{0}}\rangle\geq c,
which in turn leads to

r_{2}(F,G^{\prime})\geq\left|\frac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{0}},f_{j_{0}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j_{0}},f_{i_{0}}\rangle}\right|\geq\frac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{c}.

On the other hand, when
c<0
then
tr(K)<\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}.
Therefore,
\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j},f_{i}\rangle=tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}<0
gives
\min\limits_{i\neq j}\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j},f_{i}\rangle\leq\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}.
There exist
i_{1}\neq j_{1}
such that
\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{1}},f_{j_{1}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j_{1}},f_{i_{1}}\rangle\leq\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\langle g^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j},f_{i}\rangle}{N(N-1)}=\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}=c<0.
This leads to

r_{2}(F,G^{\prime})\geq\left|\frac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{1}},f_{j_{1}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j_{1}},f_{i_{1}}\rangle}\right|=\sqrt{\left(\frac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\langle g^{\prime}_{i_{1}},f_{j_{1}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}_{j_{1}},f_{i_{1}}\rangle}\geq\sqrt{\left(\frac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-c}.

Hence,
G
is a
2-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual of
F
. Employing the expression for
c
from (
16
), we may express

\tilde{r}_{2}(F)=r_{2}(F,G)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}},\;\;&\text{if $tr(K)\geq\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N},$}\\
~\\
\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}},\;\;\;\;&\text{if $tr(K)<\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}$}.\end{cases}.

Now consider the case when
tr(K)<0.
For a
2-
uniform
K-
dual frame
G
of
F,
we have,
\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;\forall i
and so
r_{1}(F,G)=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\langle f_{i},g^{\prime\prime}_{i}\rangle\right|=-\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}.
For any
K-
dual
G^{\prime\prime}
of
F,\,

r_{1}(F,G^{\prime\prime})\geq-\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}.
If not, then
\left|\langle f_{i},g^{\prime\prime}_{i}\rangle\right|<-\dfrac{tr(K)}{N},\;1\leq i\leq N.
Therefore,
-tr(K)=\left|\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\langle f_{i},g^{\prime\prime}_{i}\rangle\right|\leq\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\langle f_{i},g^{\prime\prime}_{i}\rangle\right|<-tr(K),
which is not possible. Thus,
G
is a
1-
erasure spectrally optimal
K-
dual of
F.
As
G
is a
2-
uniform
K-
dual of
F,
we then have
\langle f_{i},g_{j}\rangle\langle f_{j},g_{i}\rangle=\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}<0,
for all
i\neq j.
By (
5
),

r_{2}(F,G)=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}}.

Now we shall show that for any
K-
dual
G^{\prime\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(F),\;

r_{2}(F,G^{\prime\prime\prime})\geq\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}}.
As
G^{\prime\prime\prime}
is a
1-
uniform
K-
dual of
F

we have
\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\langle f_{i},g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{j}\rangle\langle f_{j},g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i}\rangle=tr(K)-\dfrac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}.
Thus,
\min\limits_{i\neq j}\langle f_{i},g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{j}\rangle\langle f_{j},g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i}\rangle\leq\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}<0.
Therefore, there exists
i_{2}\neq j_{2}
such that
\langle f_{i_{2}},g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{j_{2}}\rangle\langle f_{j_{2}},g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i_{2}}\rangle\leq\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}<0.
This in turn leads to

r_{2}(F,G^{\prime\prime\prime})\geq\left|\frac{tr(K)}{N}+\sqrt{\langle g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i_{2}},f_{j_{2}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{j_{2}},f_{i_{2}}\rangle}\right|=\sqrt{\left(\frac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\langle g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i_{2}},f_{j_{2}}\rangle\langle g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{j_{2}},f_{i_{2}}\rangle}\;\geq\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}}.

Hence,
\tilde{r}_{2}(F)=r_{2}(F,G)=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{tr(K)}{N}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{tr(K)-\frac{\left(tr(K)\right)^{2}}{N}}{N(N-1)}}

∎

The following remark illustrates the effect of a unitary operator on spectral optimality. The proof approach for the above remark is similar to the one used in Theorem
4.21
.

Remark 4.34
.

Let
F
be a
K-
frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
U
be a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that
UK=KU.
Then,
G\in\mathcal{R}_{i}(F)
if and only if
UG\in\mathcal{R}_{i}(UF),\,i=1,2.

Example 4.35
.

Consider the
K
-frame
F=\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\}
for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where

f_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{2}\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},\quad f_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{2}\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},\quad f_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},\quad f_{4}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},

and let

K=\begin{bmatrix}2&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

It is easy to verify that
F
is a Parseval
K-
frame for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The pseudo inverse of
K
is

K^{\dagger}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Hence, the canonical
K
-dual of
F
is given by:

K^{\dagger}F=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}\right\}.

We compute
\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|K^{\dagger}f_{i}\|=1
for all
1\leq i\leq 4
. Thus,

\mathbb{O}_{1}(F,K^{\dagger}F)=1=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K)}{N},

and by Proposition
3.5
, the pair
(F,K^{\dagger}F)
is a
1
-erasure optimal
(4,3)\;K-
dual pair with respect to the operator norm. Moreover, any
K-
dual
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
of
F
must be of the form:

g_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{1}\\
\gamma_{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad g_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\alpha_{1}\\
-\beta_{1}\\
\gamma_{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad g_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},\quad g_{4}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{R}.

If
G
is a

under operator norm, then
\|f_{i}\|\cdot\|g_{i}\|=1
for all

. Now,

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|\cdot\|g_{1}\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+\alpha_{1}\right)^{2}+\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{1}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|f_{2}\|\cdot\|g_{2}\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\alpha_{1}\right)^{2}+\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{1}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|f_{3}\|\cdot\|g_{3}\|

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\|f_{4}\|\cdot\|g_{4}\|

\displaystyle=1.

Equating the norms implies
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}=\gamma_{1}=0
. Therefore, the canonical dual is the unique
1-
erasure optimal
K-

under operator norm.

, so that

. By Theorem

,

Conclusion

In this paper, we explored the problem of optimal reconstruction in the presence of erasures within the framework of
K
-frames. We addressed two main questions: identifying optimal
(N,n)

K
-dual pairs and determining optimal
K
-dual frames for a fixed Parseval
K
-frame under both operator norm and spectral radius criteria. We introduced and characterized
1
-uniform and
2
-uniform
K
-dual pairs, demonstrating their role in achieving optimality for one and two erasures, respectively. Tight lower bounds for reconstruction errors were established, and explicit conditions under which these bounds are attained were provided. For fixed Parseval
K
-frames, we derived both sufficient and necessary conditions under which the canonical dual is the unique optimal dual. Several examples illustrating the uniqueness and non-uniqueness of optimal duals were presented to highlight the geometric and algebraic structure of the solution space. Overall, our results extend and unify existing studies in classical frame theory and offer a robust operator theoretic framework for analyzing erasures and recovery in redundant systems. An interesting open direction is to study the case when
K
is a negative definite operator, which remains unresolved within the current framework.

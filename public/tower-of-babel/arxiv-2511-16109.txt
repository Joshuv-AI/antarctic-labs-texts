1.
introduction

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring with residue field
k
and let
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module. By
\ell(-)
we denote the length function on
A
-modules. Let
\beta_{n}^{A}(M)=\ell(\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{n}(M,k))
for
n\geq 0
be the
n^{th}
-betti number of
M
.
We set the
curvature
of
M
to be

\operatorname{curv}_{A}(M)=\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(M)}.

We drop the subscript
A
if it is clear from the context.
It can be shown that
\operatorname{curv}(M)\leq\operatorname{curv}(k)
, see
[
2
, 4.1.9]
and
\operatorname{curv}(k)<\infty
; see
[
2
, 4.1.5]
. It can be shown that
A
is
not
a complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{curv}(k)>1
; see
[
2
, 8.2.1]
.

In this paper we study the set
c(A)=\{\operatorname{curv}M\mid\operatorname{projdim}_{A}M=\infty\}
when
A
is Cohen-Macaulay and
not
a complete intersection. We note that
c(A)\subseteq[1,\operatorname{curv}k]
. It is not known whether
c(A)
is a finite set. We show that if
c(A)
is not a singleton set then under mild hypotheses it yields an obstruction of
\operatorname{curv}(k)
. We also show that if
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{n}(M,N)=0
for
n\gg 0
then it imposes a constraint on
\operatorname{curv}(M)
and
\operatorname{curv}(N)
.

1.1
.

Setup:
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
. Further assume
A
is
NOT
a complete intersection. Let

e(A)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d!}{n^{d}}\ell(A/\mathfrak{m}^{n})\quad\text{be the multiplicity of $A$}.

All modules considered will be finitely generated.

Our first result is

Theorem 1.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
). Further assume that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}(k)}
exists. Suppose there exists an
A
-module
M
with
\operatorname{projdim}_{A}M=\infty
and
\operatorname{curv}(M)<\operatorname{curv}(k)
.
Then

(1)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{curv}(k)\leq\frac{e(A)}{2}-1.}

(2)

\operatorname{curv}(M)<\sqrt{e(A)}-1
.

Remark 1.3
.

All known examples of modules satisfy

\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}(M)}=\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}(M)}

and so in all known cases the limit exists. See
[
2
, 4.3.6]
.

1.4
.

We note that for any Cohen-Macaulay local ring
A
it is known that
\operatorname{curv}(k)\leq e(A)-1
; see
[
2
, 4.2.7]
. The bound is attained for the so called rings of minimal multiplicity, see
[
2
, p. 50]
.

As an easy consequence of Theorem
1.2
we obtain

Corollary 1.5
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.2
). Assume
\operatorname{curv}(k)>e(A)/2-1
.
If
\operatorname{projdim}M=\infty
then
\operatorname{curv}(M)=\operatorname{curv}(k)
.

1.6
.

To study infinite injective resolutions the notion of
injective curvature
was introduced in the paper
[
1
]
.
We define

\operatorname{injcurv}(M)=\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\ell(\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{A}(k,M))}.

Our next result is also unexpected.

Theorem 1.7
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
). Let
M,N
be
A
-modules.

(1)

If
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N)=0
for
i\gg 0
then

(a)

\min\{\operatorname{curv}{M},\operatorname{curv}{N}\}\leq\sqrt{e(A)}-1.

(b)

Further assume
\operatorname{projdim}M=\operatorname{projdim}N=\infty
. Then

\max\{\operatorname{curv}{M},\operatorname{curv}{N}\}\leq\frac{e(A)}{2}-1.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N)=0
for
i\gg 0
then

(a)

\min\{\operatorname{curv}{M},\operatorname{injcurv}{N}\}\leq\sqrt{e(A)}-1.

(b)

Further assume
\operatorname{projdim}M=\operatorname{injdim}N=\infty
. Then

\max\{\operatorname{curv}{M},\operatorname{injcurv}{N}\}\leq\frac{e(A)}{2}-1.

As an easy consequence of Theorem
1.7
we obtain

Corollary 1.8
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.2
). Assume
\operatorname{curv}(k)>e(A)/2-1
.

(a)

If
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n\gg 0
then
\operatorname{projdim}_{A}M<\infty
or
\operatorname{projdim}_{A}N<\infty
.

(b)

If
\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{A}(M,N)=0
for

then
\operatorname{projdim}_{A}M<\infty
or
\operatorname{injdim}_{A}N<\infty
.

Example 1.9
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring with

]

1.10
.

We note that to compute curvature of a module

we need to have knowledge of the asymptotic behaviour of betti numbers of a module.
So the following result is also un-expected.

.

(with hypotheses as in

Let

\frac{\beta_{i_{0}+1}(M)}{\beta_{i_{0}}(M)}>\frac{e(A)}{1+\operatorname{curv}(k)}-1,

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we discuss a few preliminary results that we need.

2.1
.

For most of our results we need that the residue field of
A
is infinite. For one particular application we need to assume that
A
is complete. Both these base changes are of the type
(A,\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow(B,\mathfrak{n})
flat with
\mathfrak{m}B=\mathfrak{n}
. If the residue field of
A
is finite then we take
B=A[X]_{\mathfrak{m}A[X]}
. We note that the residue field of
B
is
k(X)
which is infinite.
If
A
is not complete then set
B
to be the completion of
A
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow(B,\mathfrak{n})
be flat with
\mathfrak{m}B=\mathfrak{n}
. Set
l=B/\mathfrak{n}
. If
M
is an
A
-module then set
M^{\prime}=M\otimes_{A}B
. We note that
k^{\prime}=l
. We have the following properties which are well-knowm:

(1)

\dim M=\dim M^{\prime}
and
0pt_{A}M=0pt_{B}M^{\prime}
. In particular
A
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
B
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

\beta_{n}^{A}(M)=\beta_{n}^{B}(M^{\prime})
for all
n\geq 0
.

(3)

\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{n}(M,N)\otimes_{A}B=\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{n}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
for all
n\geq 0
. As
B
is faithfully flat over
A
we obtain
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{n}(M,N)=0
if and only if

\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{n}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})=0
.

(4)

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\otimes_{A}B=\operatorname{Ext}_{B}^{n}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
for all
n\geq 0
. As
B
is faithfully flat over
A
we obtain
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
if and only if

\operatorname{Ext}_{B}^{n}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})=0
.

(5)

\operatorname{curv}_{A}(M)=\operatorname{curv}_{B}(M^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{injcurv}_{A}(M)=\operatorname{injcurv}_{B}(M^{\prime})
.

(6)

e(A)=e(B)
.

2.2
.

The reason why we need the Cohen-Macaulay assumption is that we can reduce to dimension zero easily. To enable this effectively we need the notion of superficial elements.
An element
x\in\mathfrak{m}
is said to be
A
-superficial if there exists non-negative integers
c,n_{0}
such that
(\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\colon x)\cap\mathfrak{m}^{c}=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. It is well-known that superficial elements exist when the residue field of
A
is infinite. If
0ptA>0
and
x
is
A
-superficial then
x
is
A
-regular and
(\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\colon x)=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
for all
n\gg 0
.
Furthermore
e(A)=e(A/(x))
, cf.
[
6
, Corollary 10]
. We note that if
\dim A>0
and
x
is
A
-superficial then
x\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.

We need the following result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and let
x\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}
be
A
-regular. Set
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
and
B=A/(x)
Then

(1)

\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}=\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
.

(2)

\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}=\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
.

(3)

If
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}
exists then so does
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
and both are equal.

Proof.

(1) and (2): We have by
[
2
, 3.3.5]
,
\beta_{n}^{B}(k)+\beta_{n-1}^{B}(k)=\beta_{n}^{A}(k)
for all
n\geq 1
.
In particular
\beta_{n}^{B}(k)\leq\beta_{n}^{A}(k)
. So we have

\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}\geq\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
and

\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}\geq\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
.

By
[
3
, Corollary 3]
, we have
\beta_{n}^{B}(k)\geq\beta_{n-1}^{B}(k)
for all
n\geq 1
. So

\beta_{n}^{A}(k)\leq 2\beta_{n}^{B}(k)
. As
\lim_{n}\sqrt[n]{2}=1
it follows that

\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}\leq\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
and

\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}\leq\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
.

The result follows.

(3) This follows from (1) and (2).
∎

2.4
.

We will need the following easily proven fact. Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be sequences. Assume
a_{n}>0
and
b_{n}>0
for all
n
.
If
\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{a_{n}}<\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{b_{n}}
then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=0
.

2.5
.

We will also need the following, see
[
7
, 3.37]
. Let
\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of positive numbers. Then

\liminf_{n}\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_{n}}\leq\liminf_{n}\sqrt[n]{c_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{n}\sqrt[n]{c_{n}}\leq\limsup_{n}\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_{n}}.

2.6
.

By
\Omega^{i}(M)
we denote the
i^{th}
-syzygy of
M
. Also
\mu(M)
denotes the number of minimal generators of
M
.

2.7
.

Let
U,V
be finite length modules. Set
r(V)=\ell(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(k,N))
. Then

(1)

\ell(U\otimes V)\geq\mu(U)\mu(V)
.

(2)

\ell(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(U,V))\supseteq\mu(U)r(V)
.

To see (1) we note that we have a surjective map
U\otimes V\rightarrow U\otimes V\otimes k
. The result follows.

(2) We have a surjection
U\rightarrow U/\mathfrak{m}U
. This creates an inclusion

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(U/\mathfrak{m}U,V)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(U,V)
. The result follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first note that we may assume that the residue field of
A
is infinite, see
2.1
. After taking appropriate syzygy we may also assume that
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. We reduce to the case when
\dim A=0
as follows. If
\dim A>0
then as the residue field of
A
is infinite there exists an
A
-superficial element
x
. As
A
is Cohen-Macaulay we have
0ptA=\dim A>0
. So
x
is
A
-regular.
As
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay it is also
M
-regular. Set
B=A/(x)
. We note that if
\mathbf{F}
is a minimal free
A
-resolution of
M
then
\mathbf{F}\otimes_{A}B
is a minimal free
B
-resolution of
M/xM
. So
\operatorname{curv}_{A}(M)=\operatorname{curv}_{B}(M/xM)
. We note that
x\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. So by
2.3
we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{B}(k)}
exists and is equal to
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}
. Thus it suffices to prove the result for
B
. Iterating we may reduce to the case when
\dim A=0
.

Set
\operatorname{curv}(k)=\alpha
and let
\operatorname{curv}(M)=\theta
. We have
\theta<\alpha
and
\lim\sqrt[n]{\beta_{n}^{A}(k)}
exists (and so is equal to
\alpha
). So by
2.4
we have

\lim\beta_{n}(M)/\beta_{n}(k)=0
. Set
X_{i}=\Omega^{i}(k)
.

We have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(M)\rightarrow A^{\beta_{0}(M)}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
. Tensoring this exact sequence with
X_{i}
we obtain

0\rightarrow k^{\beta_{i+1}(M)}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(M)\otimes X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i}^{\beta_{0}(M)}\rightarrow M\otimes X_{i}\rightarrow 0.

So we have by
2.7

\beta_{i+}(M)+\beta_{0}(M)\ell(X_{i})\geq\beta_{i}(k)(\beta_{0}(M)+\beta_{1}(M)).

So

\ell(X_{i})\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}(M)}\left(-\beta_{i+1}(M)+\beta_{i}(k)(\beta_{0}(M)+\beta_{1}(M))\right).

Similarly we obtain

\ell(X_{i+1})\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}(M)}\left(-\beta_{i+2}(M)+\beta_{i+1}(k)(\beta_{0}(M)+\beta_{1}(M))\right).

We note that
\ell(X_{i})+\ell(X_{i+1})=e(A)\beta_{i}(k)
. Adding the above two inequalities and dividing by
\beta_{i}=\beta_{i}(k)
we obtain

e(A)\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}(M)}\left(-(\beta_{i+1}(M)+\beta_{i+2}(M))/\beta_{i}+(\beta_{0}(M)+\beta_{1}(M))(1+\beta_{i+1}/\beta_{i})\right).

We take limsup on both sides. By our hypotheses and
2.4
we have

\limsup_{i}-(\beta_{i+1}(M)+\beta_{i+2}(M))/\beta_{i}=0.

By
2.5
we have
\limsup_{i}\beta_{i+1}/\beta_{i}\geq\alpha
.
So we obtain

e(A)\geq(1+\beta_{1}(M)/\beta_{0}(M))(1+\alpha).

Replacing
M
by
\Omega^{i}(M)
we obtain

e(A)\geq(1+\beta_{i+1}(M)/\beta_{i}(M))(1+\alpha).

Taking limsup and by
2.5
we obtain

(*)

e(A)\geq(1+\theta)(1+\alpha).

(1) We note that
\theta\geq 1
. So we have by (*)

(1+\alpha)\leq e_{0}(A)/2.

The result follows.

(2) As
\alpha>\theta
, by (*) we get
e(A)>(1+\theta)^{2}
. The result follows.

We first give

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

(1) We assume
\operatorname{projdim}M=\operatorname{projdim}N=\infty
. We first reduce to the case when
\dim A=0
. Suppose
\dim A>0
.
By Lemma
2.1

A
is infinite. After taking sufficient high syzygies we can assume that
M
and
N
are both maximal Cohen-Macaulay
A
-modules. Let
x\in\mathfrak{m}
be
A
-superficial. Then
x
is also
A
-regular. Set
B=A/(x)
. We note that
e(B)=e(A)
. As
M
and
N
are MCM we get that
x
is both
M
and
N
-regular.
The exact sequence
0\rightarrow N\rightarrow N\rightarrow N/xN\rightarrow 0
yields
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N/xN)=0
for
i\gg 0
. We have
\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{i}(M/xM,N/xN)=\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N/xN)
for all
i\geq 0
,
see
[
5
, Lemma 2, section 18]
. So
\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{i}(M/xM,N/xN)=0
for
i\gg 0
. We also note that if
\mathbf{F}
(respectively
\mathbf{G}
) is a minimal free
A

M
(respectively
N
) then

\mathbf{F}\otimes_{A}B
(respectively
\mathbf{G}\otimes_{A}B
) is a minimal free
B

(respectively
N/xN
). So
\operatorname{curv}_{B}(M/xM)=\operatorname{curv}_{A}(M)
and
\operatorname{curv}_{B}(N/xN)=\operatorname{curv}_{A}(N)
.
Thus it suffices to prove the result for
B
. Iterating we can reduce to the case when
\dim A=0
. After taking a high syzygy of
M
we may assume that
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N)=0
for
i>0
.

We have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(N)\rightarrow A^{\beta_{0}(N)}\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
. Set
M_{j}=\Omega^{j}(M)
. As
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N)=0
for all

M_{n}
an exact sequence

\beta_{0}(N)\ell(M_{n})\geq(\beta_{0}(N)+\beta_{1}(N))\beta_{n}(M).

So we have

\ell(M_{n})\geq\left(1+\frac{\beta_{1}(N)}{\beta_{0}(N)}\right)\beta_{n}(M).

Similarly we obtain

\ell(M_{n+1})\geq\left(1+\frac{\beta_{1}(N)}{\beta_{0}(N)}\right)\beta_{n+1}(M).

We note that
\ell(M_{n})+\ell(M_{n+1})=e(A)\beta_{n}(M)
. Adding the two equations and dividing by
\beta_{n}(M)
we obtain

e(A)\geq\left(1+\frac{\beta_{1}(N)}{\beta_{0}(N)}\right)\left(1+\frac{\beta_{n+1}(M)}{\beta_{n}(M)}\right).

Taking limsup and by
2.5
we obtain

e(A)\geq\left(1+\frac{\beta_{1}(N)}{\beta_{0}(N)}\right)\left(1+\operatorname{curv}(M)\right).

Replacing
N
with
\Omega^{n}(N)
we obtain

e(A)\geq\left(1+\frac{\beta_{n+1}(N)}{\beta_{n}(N)}\right)\left(1+\operatorname{curv}(M)\right).

Taking limsup and by
2.5
we obtain

(*)

e(A)\geq\left(1+\operatorname{curv}(N)\right)\left(1+\operatorname{curv}(M)\right).

(a) If
\operatorname{projdim}M
or
\operatorname{projdim}N
is finite then we have nothing to prove. So assume
\operatorname{projdim}M=\operatorname{projdim}N=\infty
.
If
\min\{\operatorname{curv}{M},\operatorname{curv}(N)\}>\sqrt{e(A)}-1
then by (*) we obtain
e(A)>e(A)
. This is a contradiction. The result follows.

(b) As
\operatorname{projdim}M=\operatorname{projdim}N=\infty
we have
\operatorname{curv}(M)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{curv}(N)\geq 1
. The result follows easily from (*).

(2) We assume
\operatorname{projdim}M=\operatorname{injdim}N=\infty
. We first reduce to the case when
\dim A=0
. Suppose
\dim A>0
.
By Lemma
2.1
we can assume that the residue field of
A
is infinite and that
A
is complete. So
A
has a canonical module
\omega
. After taking sufficient high syzygies we can assume that
M
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay

Replacing

2.5

(**)

(a) If
\operatorname{projdim}M

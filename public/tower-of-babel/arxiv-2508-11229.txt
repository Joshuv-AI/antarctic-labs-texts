On the P ​ G ​ L 2 ​ ( q ) PGL_{2}(q) -orbits of lines of P ​ G ​ ( 3 , q ) PG(3,q) and binary quartic forms in characteristic three
Krishna Kaipa 1, Puspendu Pradhan 2,∗

Abstract
. We consider the problem of classifying the lines of the projective 3 3 -space P ​ G ​ ( 3 , q ) PG(3,q) over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} into orbits of the group P ​ G ​ L 2 ​ ( q ) PGL_{2}(q) of linear symmetries of the twisted cubic C C . The problem has been solved in literature in characteristic different from 3 3 , and in this work, we solve the problem in characteristic 3 3 . We reduce this problem to another problem, which is the classification of binary quartic forms into P ​ G ​ L 2 ​ ( q ) PGL_{2}(q) -orbits. We first solve the latter problem and use to solve the former problem. We also obtain the point-line and the line-plane incidence structures of the point, line, and plane orbits.

Introduction

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
(n+1)
over a field
F
. Let
\mathbb{P}(V)
denote the
n
-dimensional projective space associated with it. Let
PGL_{n+1}(V)
denote the group of projectivities of
\mathbb{P}(V)
. When
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a finite field of order
q
, we denote the associated
n
-dimensional projective space by
PG(n,q)
and the group of projectivities by
PGL_{n+1}(q)
. Let
C
be a
twisted cubic curve
in
PG(3,q)
defined by the image of the embedding

PG(1,q)\hookrightarrow PG(3,q),\quad(s,t)\mapsto(s^{3},s^{2}t,st^{2},t^{3}).

For
q\geq 5
, the subgroup of
PGL_{4}(q)
which fixes
C
is isomorphic to
PGL_{2}(q)
which we denote as
G
. The group
G
acts triply transitively on the points of
C
, since its action on
PG(1,q)
is triply transitive. The
osculating plane
at a point
(s^{3},s^{2}t,st^{2},t^{3})
of
C
is given by

t^{3}X_{0}-3st^{2}X_{1}+3s^{2}tX_{2}-s^{3}X_{3}=0.

We say that a line
L
of
PG(3,q)
is
generic
if it neither intersects
C
, nor lies in any of the osculating planes of
C
. We say a line is
non-generic
if it is not generic.

The problem studied in this paper is:

Problem 1.1
.

Classify the generic lines of
PG(3,q)
into
G
-orbits.

This problem was recently solved in
[
9
]
in the case when characteristic of the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is different from
2
and
3
. In the case of characteristic
2
, the problem was solved by M. Ceria and F. Pavese in
[
2
]
. In this work, we solve the problem for the remaining case of characteristic
3
. Throughout this work, we assume char
(\mathbb{F}_{q})=3
.
The class of generic lines is denoted as
\mathcal{O}_{6}
in the book
[
7
, p.236]
.
The classification of non-generic lines into ten
G
-orbits is well-known in literature, for example
[
1
, Theorem 8.1]
,
[
7
, p.236]
,
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
.
It has been an open problem to classify the class
\mathcal{O}_{6}
of generic lines into
G
-orbits.
A detailed conjecture was made by A. Davydov, S. Marcugini and F. Pambianco in
[
5
, Conjecture 8.2]
. The part of this conjecture relevant to characteristic
3
is:

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
5
]
The number of
G
-orbits of generic lines of
PG(3,q)
is
(2q-3)
, of which there are
q/3,(q-1)
and
(2q-6)/3
orbits of size
|G|,|G|/2
and
|G|/4
, respectively.

Our main result is:

Theorem
.

The above conjecture is true.

The detailed result is given in Theorem
1.6
below. We briefly explain our approach to the problem: A binary quartic form

f(X,Y)=z_{0}X^{4}+z_{1}X^{3}Y+z_{2}X^{2}Y^{2}+z_{3}XY^{3}+z_{4}Y^{4},

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
4
in two variables
X,Y
. The group
GL_{2}(q)
acts on such forms by

gf(X,Y)=\det(g)^{-4}f(dX-bY,aY-cX),\quad g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right).

This gives an action of the group
G=PGL_{2}(q)
on the projective space of binary quartic forms.
The form
f(X,Y)
has repeated factors if and only if its discriminant

\Delta(f)=(z_{0}z_{4}-z_{1}z_{3})^{3}+(z_{0}z_{4}-z_{1}z_{3})^{2}z_{2}^{2}-z_{2}^{3}(z_{0}z_{3}^{2}+z_{4}z_{1}^{2}-z_{0}z_{2}z_{4}),

vanishes. The
\jmath
-invariant of a form with
\Delta(f)\neq 0
is given by

\jmath(f)=z_{2}(f)^{6}/\Delta(f).

We will show that:

There is a
G
-equivariant correspondence between the set of generic lines of
PG(3,q)
and the set of binary quartic forms over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
whose discriminant and
\jmath
-invariant are nonzero.

This correspondence arises as follows: A generic line
L
intersects the tangent line to
C
at four distinct points
(s^{3},s^{2}t,st^{2},t^{3})
of
C(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
if and only if the associated quartic form
f_{L}
vanishes at
(s,t)
, that is,
(Xt-Ys)
is a factor of
f_{L}(X,Y)
. Therefore, Problem
1.1
is equivalent to
decomposing the projective space of binary quartic forms with non-zero discriminant and
\jmath
-invariant into
G
-orbits.

There are two problems closely related to Problem
1.1
as follows: First, we recall that the points of
PG(3,q)
decompose into five

-orbits denoted
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{AX},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{RC},\mathcal{IC}

For representatives

of the

\frac{|\mathcal{\mathcal{P}}\cap\mathcal{M}|}{q+1},\quad\mathcal{M}\in\{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{AX},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{RC},\mathcal{IC}\}.

2.
Geometric Setup

Let
V
denote the vector space
F^{2}
over a field
F
, and let
e_{1}=(1,0),e_{2}=(0,1)

denote the standard basis of
V
. Let
X,Y
denote the dual basis for the dual vector space
V^{*}
. The group
GL_{2}(F)
acts on
V
, by
g(e_{1})=ae_{1}+ce_{2}
and
g(e_{2})=be_{1}+de_{2}
for
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in GL_{2}(F)
. The action of
GL_{2}(F)
on the dual vector space is given by
g(X)=(dX-bY)/\text{det}(g)
,
g(Y)=(aY-cX)/\text{det}(g)
. Let Sym
{}^{m}(V^{*})
denote the
m
-th symmetric power of
V^{*}
. In terms of the basis
X^{m-i}Y^{i},i=0,\dots,m
, we realize Sym
{}^{m}(V^{*})
as the vector space of degree
m
homogeneous polynomials (or binary forms)
f(X,Y)
in
X,Y
. The action of
GL_{2}(F)
on Sym
{}^{m}(V^{*})
is given by

g_{m}f(X,Y)=\det(g)^{-m}f(dX-bY,aY-cX).

Let
D_{m}V
denote the dual vector space to Sym
{}^{m}(V^{*})
and let
B_{0},\dots,B_{m}
denote the basis of
D_{m}V
dual to the basis
X^{m-i}Y^{i},i=0,\dots,m
of Sym
{}^{m}(V^{*})
. For
g\in GL_{2}(F)
, we denote by
g^{[m]}
, the action of
GL_{2}(F)
on
D_{m}V
dual to the action
g_{m}
on Sym
{}^{m}(V^{*})
. For
(s,t)\in V
, the evaluation
f\mapsto f(s,t)
gives a map
\nu_{m}:V\to D_{m}V
given by
\nu_{m}(s,t)=s^{m}B_{0}+s^{m-1}tB_{1}+\dots+t^{m}B_{m}
. At the projective level we get the Veronese map

\nu_{m}:\mathbb{P}(V)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(D_{m}V),\quad(s,t)\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{m}s^{m-i}t^{i}B_{i}.

The image of
\nu_{m}
is the degree
m
rational normal curve (in short RNC)
C_{m}
.
By construction,

\nu_{m}(g\cdot v)=g^{[m]}\nu_{m}(v),

or more explicitly
\nu_{m}(as+bt,cs+dt)=g^{[m]}(s^{m},s^{m-1}t,\dots,st^{m-1},t^{m})
.

For the rest of this section, we assume char
(F)=3
. We denote the curve
C_{3}
as just
C
. We record the matrices
g^{[3]}
and
g_{4}
for
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in GL_{2}(F)
, in the case when char
(F)=3
:

(1)

g^{[3]}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{3}&0&0&b^{3}\\
a^{2}c&a(ad-bc)&-b(ad-bc)&b^{2}d\\
ac^{2}&-c(ad-bc)&d(ad-bc)&bd^{2}\\
c^{3}&0&0&d^{3}\end{pmatrix},

(2)

g_{4}=\tfrac{1}{\text{det}(g)^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}d^{4}&-d^{3}c&d^{2}c^{2}&-dc^{3}&c^{4}\\
-d^{3}b&d^{3}a&dc(bc+ad)&c^{3}b&-c^{3}a\\
0&0&(ad-bc)^{2}&0&0\\
-b^{3}d&b^{3}c&ab(bc+ad)&a^{3}d&-ca^{3}\\
b^{4}&-b^{3}a&a^{2}b^{2}&-ba^{3}&a^{4}\end{pmatrix}.

It follows from (
1
) that the line
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{P}(D_{3}V)
spanned by
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
is
PGL_{2}(F)
-invariant. The osculating plane
O_{(s,t)}
to
C
at a point
\nu_{3}(s,t)
consists of points (see
[
7
, eq. 21.4]
)

O_{(s,t)}=\{(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\colon y_{0}t^{3}-3t^{2}sy_{1}+3ts^{2}y_{2}-s^{3}y_{3}=0\}.

Since char
(F)=3
, we have

O_{(s,t)}=\{(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\colon y_{0}t^{3}-s^{3}y_{3}=0\}.

We note that
O_{(s,t)}
is the join of
\mathcal{A}
and
\nu_{3}(s,t)
, and that
\mathcal{A}
is the line of intersection of any two and hence all osculating planes of
C
. The line
\mathcal{A}
is called the

. It also follows that a line
L
of
\mathbb{P}(D_{3}V)
meets

if and only if

is contained in some osculating plane of

.

We also note from (
2
) that the linear subspace of
\text{Sym}^{4}(V^{*})
consisting of those quartic forms for which the coefficient of

is zero, is a

-invariant subspace.

The basis of the second exterior power

associated with the basis

of

is
B_{ij}=B_{i}\wedge B_{j},0\leq i<j\leq 3
. The Plücker coordinates with respect to this basis are denoted by
p_{ij},0\leq i<j\leq 3
. It will be convenient to use the basis

E_{0}=B_{23},E_{1}=B_{13},E_{2}=B_{03},E_{3}=B_{02},E_{4}=B_{01},E_{5}=B_{12},

and let

(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4},z_{5})=(p_{23},p_{13},p_{03},p_{02},p_{01},p_{12})

be the coordinates with respect to the basis
E_{0},\dots,E_{5}
. The lines of
\mathbb{P}(D_{3}V)
are given by the Klein quadric

\mathcal{Q}:p_{01}p_{23}-p_{02}p_{13}+p_{03}p_{12}=0,\;\text{equivalently }z_{0}z_{4}-z_{1}z_{3}+z_{2}z_{5}=0.

The induced action of
GL_{2}(q)
on
\wedge^{2}D_{3}V
is represented by the matrix

\det(g)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\det(g)^{4}g_{4}&0\\
\hline\cr b^{2}d^{2}\text{ }bd(ad+bc)\text{ }abcd\text{ }ac(ad+bc)\text{ }a^{2}c^{2}&\det(g)^{2}\end{array}\right),

with respect to the basis
E_{0},\dots,E_{5}
. From the form of this matrix, the following two observations are clear. Let
\hat{A}=F\cdot E_{5}
denote the one dimensional subspace of
\wedge^{2}D_{3}V
corresponding to the point
\mathcal{A}=E_{5}
of
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}D_{3}V)
. (We use the same symbol
\mathcal{A}
for the axis in

and the corresponding point
E_{5}
on the Klein quadric
\mathcal{Q}
in
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}D_{3}V)
under the Klein correspondence. The intended meaning of

will be clear from the context in which it appears.)

(1)

As observed above the point
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}D_{3}V)
is fixed by
PGL_{2}(F)
. It represents the common line of intersection of all osculating planes of
C
.

(2)

The quotient vector space

(\wedge^{2}D_{3}V)/\hat{A}\simeq(\wedge^{2}V)^{\otimes 5}\otimes\text{Sym}^{4}(V^{*}),

as
GL_{2}(F)
-modules. In particular, at the projective level we get a

\mathcal{E}_{r}
are isomorphic. Our main idea is inspired by Theorem 2 in §10 of Mordell’s book
[

]
.
Let
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
denote the points of
\mathcal{E}_{f}
with coordinates
(XW/Y^{2},X/Y)
being
(1,0)
and
(-1,0)
respectively. Let
P_{\infty}
denote the point at infinity of
\mathcal{E}_{r}
with coordinates
(1/S,T/S)=(0,0)
. We define a map

\psi:\mathcal{E}_{f}\to\mathcal{E}_{r}
as follows: away from
\{P_{+},P_{-}\}
, we define
\psi
in terms of the coordinates
(S,T)
on
\mathcal{E}_{r}
by:

\psi:(1,Y,W)\mapsto(S,T)=((Y^{2}+W-1)Y+z_{1},(z_{0}-1)-(Y^{2}+W-1)).

We check that
\psi(1,Y,W)
lies on
\mathcal{E}_{r}
:
we can write the equation of
\mathcal{E}_{f}
as

\mathcal{E}_{f}:(Y^{2}-1)^{2}-W^{2}+z_{0}+z_{1}Y-1=0,

and we can write the equation of
\mathcal{E}_{r}
in terms of
(\tilde{S},\tilde{T})=(S-z_{1},T-(z_{0}-1))
as

\mathcal{E}_{r}:\tilde{T}^{3}+\tilde{T}^{2}-\tilde{S}^{2}+z_{1}\tilde{S}+(1-z_{0})\tilde{T}=0,

(where we have used the fact that
r^{-1}=-z_{1}^{2}+(z_{0}-1)^{2}z_{0}
). Therefore, for
(\tilde{S},\tilde{T})=((Y^{2}+W-1)Y,-(Y^{2}+W-1))
, we get

\tilde{T}^{3}+\tilde{T}^{2}-\tilde{S}^{2}+z_{1}\tilde{S}+(1-z_{0})\tilde{T}
evaluates to

(Y^{2}+W-1)((Y^{2}-1)^{2}-W^{2}+z_{1}Y+z_{0}-1)=0.

Near
P_{-}
, writing the equation of
\mathcal{E}_{f}
as

W+Y^{2}-1=\frac{z_{0}/Y^{2}+z_{1}/Y-1-W/Y^{2}}{W/Y^{2}-1},

we see that
W+Y^{2}-1
evaluates to
0
at
P_{-}
. Similarly, writing

Y(W+Y^{2}-1)=\frac{(z_{0}-1)/Y+z_{1}-(Y^{2}+W-1)/Y}{W/Y^{2}-1},

we see that
Y(W+Y^{2}-1)
evaluates to
z_{1}
at
P_{-}
. Therefore
\psi(P_{-})=(-z_{1},z_{0}-1)
.

Near
P_{+}
, we can express
\psi
in terms of the coordinates
(1/S,T/S)
near
P_{\infty}
as:

(1/S,T/S)=(\frac{\frac{1}{Y^{3}}}{(1+\frac{W}{Y^{2}}-\frac{1}{Y^{2}})+\frac{z_{1}}{Y^{3}}},\frac{\frac{z_{0}-1}{Y^{3}}-(1+\frac{W}{Y^{2}}-\frac{1}{Y^{2}})/Y}{(1+\frac{W}{Y^{2}}-\frac{1}{Y^{2}})+\frac{z_{1}}{Y^{3}}}).

Since
(1+\frac{W}{Y^{2}}-\frac{1}{Y^{2}})
and
1/Y
evaluate to
-1
and
0
respectively at
P_{+}
, we see that the right hand side above evaluates to
(1/S,T/S)=(0,0)
at
P_{+}
, or in other words
\psi(P_{+})=P_{\infty}
. We also note that
\psi^{-1}(z_{1},z_{0}-1)
is the unique point
(1,Y,W)=(1,\tfrac{1-z_{0}}{z_{1}},1-(\tfrac{1-z_{0}}{z_{1}})^{2})
if
z_{1}\neq 0
, and
P_{-}
if
z_{1}=0
.

We define a map
\psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{E}_{r}\to\mathcal{E}_{f}
by

\psi^{\prime}\colon(S,T)\mapsto(1,Y,W)=(1,\tfrac{S-z_{1}}{z_{0}-1-T},z_{0}-T-(\tfrac{S-z_{1}}{z_{0}-1-T})^{2}),

away from the points
(S,T)=\{(\pm z_{1},z_{0}-1),P_{\infty}\}
. It is readily checked that the point on the right hand side lies on
\mathcal{E}_{f}
. Near
P_{\infty}
, we express
\psi^{\prime}
in terms of the coordinates
(1/S,T/S)
around
P_{\infty}
and the coordinates

(W/Y^{2},1/Y)
around
P_{+}
by
(1/S,T/S)\mapsto

(W/Y^{2},1/Y)=(\frac{z_{0}(\tfrac{z_{0}-1}{S}-\tfrac{T}{S})^{2}-(z_{0}-1)^{2}\tfrac{T}{S}\tfrac{1}{S}-(z_{0}-1)(\tfrac{T}{S})^{2}-\tfrac{T^{3}}{S^{2}}}{(1-z_{1}/S)^{2}}-1,\frac{\tfrac{z_{0}-1}{S}-\tfrac{T}{S}}{1-z_{1}/S}).

Writing the equation of
\mathcal{E}_{r}
as

\tfrac{T^{3}}{S^{2}}=1+\tfrac{r^{-1}}{S^{2}}-(\tfrac{T}{S})^{2},

we see that
T^{3}/S^{2}
evaluates to
1
at
P_{\infty}
. Therefore,
\psi^{\prime}(P_{\infty})=P_{+}
.

Near
(-z_{1},z_{0}-1)
we express
\psi^{\prime}
in terms of the coordinates
(W/Y^{2},1/Y)
near
P_{-}
as

(S,T)\mapsto(W/Y^{2},1/Y)=(\frac{(z_{0}-T)(z_{0}-T-1)^{2}}{(S-z_{1})^{2}}-1,\tfrac{z_{0}-1-T}{S-z_{1}}).

Therefore
\psi^{\prime}(-z_{1},z_{0}-1)=P_{-}
. If
z_{1}=0
, this completes the definition of
\psi^{\prime}
. If
z_{1}\neq 0
, then we show that
\psi^{\prime}(z_{1},z_{0}-1)
is the point
(1,Y,W)=(1,\tfrac{1-z_{0}}{z_{1}},1-(\tfrac{1-z_{0}}{z_{1}})^{2})
: as above, we use the coordinates
(\tilde{S},\tilde{T})=(S-z_{1},T-(z_{0}-1))
in terms of which
\mathcal{E}_{r}
can be written as
\tilde{T}^{3}+\tilde{T}^{2}-\tilde{S}^{2}+z_{1}\tilde{S}+(1-z_{0})\tilde{T}=0
. We have:

\frac{\tilde{S}}{\tilde{T}}=\frac{z_{0}-1-\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}^{2}}{z_{1}-\tilde{S}},

evaluates to
(z_{0}-1)/z_{1}
at
(\tilde{S},\tilde{T})=(0,0)
, that is,
(S,T)=(z_{1},z_{0}-1)
.
We can express
\psi^{\prime}
as

(\tilde{S},\tilde{T})\mapsto(1,Y,W)=(1,\tfrac{-\tilde{S}}{\tilde{T}},1-\tilde{T}-(\tfrac{\tilde{S}}{\tilde{T}})^{2}).

Therefore,
\psi^{\prime}(z_{1},z_{0}-1)=(1,\tfrac{1-z_{0}}{z_{1}},1-(\tfrac{1-z_{0}}{z_{1}})^{2})
.

It is readily checked that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi
is the identity map on
\mathcal{E}_{f}
and
\psi\circ\psi^{\prime}
is the identity map on
\mathcal{E}_{r}
, and hence
\mathcal{E}_{r}
and
\mathcal{E}_{f}
are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We conclude that
\zeta_{L}=\#\mathcal{E}_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})/2=\#\mathcal{E}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q})/2
.
∎

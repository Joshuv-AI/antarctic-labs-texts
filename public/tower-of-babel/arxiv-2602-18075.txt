Hidden-charm u ​ d ​ s ​ c ​ c ¯ uds\,c\bar{c} pentaquarks as flavor eigenstates in a constituent quark model
M.C. Gordillo, J.M. Alcaraz-Pelegrina, J. Segovia

Abstract
We use a diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) algorithm to solve the Schrödinger equation that describes u ​ d ​ s ​ c ​ c ¯ udsc\bar{c} pentaquarks within the framework of a non-relativistic constituent quark model. We considered only multiquark states with defined values of parity, color, spin and isospin, selected to be compatible with the experimentally favored assignment J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} for one of the candidates, and assumed I = 0 I=0 . However, we found that, to explain the existence of the P c ​ s ​ ( 4338 ) P_{cs}(4338) and P c ​ s ​ ( 4459 ) P_{cs}(4459) pentaquarks, we need the total wavefunction to be also an eigenvector of the SU(3) flavor operator. When we impose that condition, we obtain two structures compatible with the masses extracted from the J / ψ ​ Λ J/\psi\Lambda spectrum. In addition, two states are predicted below the J / ψ ​ Λ J/\psi\Lambda threshold but above the η c ​ Λ \eta_{c}\Lambda one that would not appear in that channel. If we only impose the I = 0 I=0 condition, we obtain a single (not two) structure compatible with the experimental quantum numbers, with a mass below the J / ψ ​ Λ J/\psi\Lambda threshold.

2
Formalism

We chose to describe the
udsc\bar{c}
pentaquarks by the non-relativistic constituent quark model Hamiltonian:

H=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\left(m_{i}+\frac{\vec{p\,}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}\right)-\frac{\vec{P\,}_{\text{c}m}^{2}}{2M_{\text{tot}}}+\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}\,,

(5)

where
m_{i}
are the constituent quark masses,
P_{\text{c}m}
is the total center-of-mass momentum, and
V_{ij}
denotes the pairwise interaction between the
i
and
j
quarks.
That interquark interaction was taken to be the standard AL1 potential defined in Refs.
[
53
,
55
]
:

\displaystyle V_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\vec{\lambda}_{i}^{c}\cdot\vec{\lambda}_{j}^{c})\Bigg[\frac{\kappa}{r_{ij}}-\lambda r_{ij}+\Lambda

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi}{3m_{i}m_{j}}\kappa^{\prime}(\vec{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{j})\frac{e^{-r_{ij}^{2}/r_{0}^{2}}}{\pi^{3/2}r_{0}^{3}}\Bigg]\,,

(6)

where
\vec{\lambda}^{c}
are the SU(3) color Gell-Mann matrices and
\vec{\sigma}
are the Pauli matrices. The regulator is defined as

r_{0}(m_{i},m_{j})=A\left(\frac{2m_{i}m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\right)^{-B}\,,

(7)

with quark masses

\displaystyle m_{u}=m_{d}

\displaystyle=0.315\,\text{GeV}\,,

\displaystyle m_{s}

\displaystyle=0.577\,\text{GeV}\,,

\displaystyle m_{c}

\displaystyle=1.836\,\text{GeV}\,,

and the remaining parameters defined as

\displaystyle\kappa=0.0951\,,

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}=0.3489\,,

\displaystyle\lambda=0.0310\,\text{GeV}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda=0.1560\,\text{GeV}\,,

\displaystyle B=0.2204\,,

\displaystyle A=1.6553\,\text{GeV}^{B-1}\,.

The diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) technique was developed in the context of condensed matter physics to solve many-body Schrödinger equations. This makes it particularly well suited
to deal with large ensembles of quarks
[
36
]
.
In the DMC method, we start by writing the Schrödinger equation in imaginary time (
\hbar=c=1
) as:

-\frac{\partial\Psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\bm{R},t)}{\partial t}=\sum_{\alpha}(H_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha}-E_{s}\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha})\,\Psi_{\alpha}(\bm{R},t)\,,

(8)

where
E_{s}
is an appropriate energy shift and
\bm{R}\equiv(\vec{r}_{1},\ldots,\vec{r}_{n})
stands for the positions of the
n
quarks and
\alpha
denotes each possible spin-flavor-color channel,
with given quantum numbers. Then, we expand
\Psi_{\alpha}(\bm{R},t)
in terms of the complete set of the Hamiltonian’s eigenfunctions as

\Psi_{\alpha}(\bm{R},t)=\sum_{i}c_{i,\alpha}\,e^{-(E_{i}-E_{s})t}\,\phi_{i,\alpha}(\bm{R})\,,

(9)

where the
E_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the system’s Hamiltonian operator. The ground state wave function,
\phi_{0,\alpha}(\bm{R})
, is obtained as the asymptotic solution of
Eq. (
8
) when
t\to\infty
for any
\alpha
-channel. The initial approximation to
\phi_{0,\alpha}(\bm{R})
, the so-called trial function,
that must include all the information known
a priori
about the hadron system was chosen to be of the form:

\displaystyle\Psi_{T}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle\equiv\Psi_{T}(\vec{r}_{1},\ldots,\vec{r}_{n};s_{1},\ldots,s_{n};f_{1},\ldots,f_{n};c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})

\displaystyle=\phi_{T}(\vec{r}_{1},\ldots,\vec{r}_{n})\times\big[\chi_{s}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})

\displaystyle\otimes\chi_{f}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\otimes\chi_{c}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\big]\,,

(10)

where, explicitly,
\vec{r}_{j}
,
s_{j}
,
f_{j}
and
c_{j}
stand for the position, spin, flavor and color of the
j
-quark inside the
n
-quark cluster.
In Eq. (
10
),
\chi_{s}
,
\chi_{f}
and
\chi_{c}
must be eigenfunctions of the spin, flavor and color operators, given by:

\displaystyle S^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{5}\frac{\vec{\sigma}_{i}}{2}\right)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad F^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{f}}\frac{\vec{\lambda}_{i}^{f}}{2}\right)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad C^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{5}\frac{\vec{\lambda}_{i}^{c}}{2}\right)^{2}\,,

(11)

with well-defined eigenvalues.
\vec{\lambda}
are the standard Gell-Mann matrices acting in the color space, while
\vec{\lambda}_{i}^{f}
are the same Gell-Mann matrices but this time acting in flavor space.
The flavor operator acts only on the three light quarks
u,d,s
(
n_{f}=3
); the charm quark and antiquark being excluded from the SU(3) flavor classification.

\vec{\sigma}
’s stand for the Pauli matrices.

In this work we restrict ourselves to total orbital angular momentum
L^{2}=0
, corresponding to the
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
state
assigned experimentally to the
P_{cs}(4338)
pentaquark
[
1
]
.
Under this assumption, the spatial part of the trial wave function should depend only on the set of inter-particle distances, what makes it symmetric under permutations of the spatial coordinates. To fulfill this requirement, we adopt the Jastrow-type ansatz,

\phi_{T}(\vec{r}_{1},\ldots,\vec{r}_{n})=\prod_{j>i=1}^{n}\exp(-a_{ij}r_{ij})\,,

(12)

where the coefficients
a_{ij}
are chosen so as to satisfy the cusp conditions derived from the Coulomb-like part of the interquark interaction for each pair of constituents.
The spin sector is constructed by coupling the spins of the five quarks to the desired total spin
S=1/2
and thus there are 5 possible (degenerate) spin wavefunctions. On the other hand, the total wavefunction has to be colorless. For a 5-quark system, there are 3 linearly independent
such configurations, all of them included in the calculation.

Special care must be taken when defining the flavor wave function. Both
udsc\bar{c}
pentaquark candidates,
P_{cs}(4338)
and
P_{cs}(4459)
, have been observed as enhancements in the
J/\psi\,\Lambda
invariant-mass distribution. This implies that both pentaquarks should have isospin
I=0
. The three eigenfunctions of the isospin operator with eigenvalue equal to zero are:

\displaystyle\chi_{f1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(sud-sdu)\,,

\displaystyle\chi_{f2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(uds-dus)\,,

\displaystyle\chi_{f3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(usd-dsu)\,.

None of these functions are independently eigenvectors of the flavor operator. On the other hand, we can combine them to produce two sets of
independent eigenfunctions of that operator, one singlet with eigenvalue
F^{2}
= 0:

\chi_{f}^{(1)}\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\Big(uds-dus+dsu-sdu+sud-usd\Big)\,.

(13)

that can be expressed as the linear combination:

\chi_{f}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\chi_{f1}+\chi_{f2}-\chi_{f3}\right)\,,

(14)

and two of the functions of the octet manifold with
F^{2}
=3;

\chi_{f,1}^{(8)}\;=\;\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\Big[(sd-ds)u+(us-su)d+2(ud-du)s\Big]\,,

(15)

\chi_{f,2}^{(8)}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\Big[(sd+ds)u-(su+us)d\Big]\,.

(16)

that can be written as (respectively):

\chi_{f,1}^{(8)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(-\chi_{f1}+2\,\chi_{f2}+\chi_{f3}\right)\,,

(17)

\chi_{f,2}^{(8)}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\chi_{f1}+\chi_{f3}\right)\,.

(18)

Those two functions are the same as the corresponding to the
\Lambda
baryon
[
6
]
, and have, by construction an isospin
I=0
.

Taking everything into account, the pentaquark system allows for two independent families of states with definite quantum numbers:

\displaystyle 3\,\text{(colorless)}\times 5\,(S=1/2)\times 1\,(F^{2}=0,I=0)

\displaystyle=15\,\text{functions}\,,

\displaystyle 3\,\text{(colorless)}\times 5\,(S=1/2)\times 2\,(F^{2}=3,I=0)

\displaystyle=30\,\text{functions}\

that share the same spatial part given by Eq.
12
.
However, not all these functions are admissible, since antisymmetry must be imposed with respect to the exchange of
identical
quarks, since quarks are fermions. A wavefunction
is antisymmetric when it is an eigenvector of the operator:

\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{{\alpha}=1}^{N_{p}}(-1)^{P}\mathcal{P_{\alpha}}\,,

(19)

applied only to the spin-color-flavor part of wavefunction, since the radial part is symmetrical with respect to the exchange of any quarks. In Eq. (
19
),
N_{p}
is the number of possible permutations of the quark indexes,
P
is the order of the permutation, and
\mathcal{P_{\alpha}}
’s stand for the matrices that define those permutations. Once constructed the matrix derived from the operator in Eq. (
19
), a particular spin-color-flavor wavefunction is antisymmetric with respect to the exchange of quarks if it is an eigenvalue of that operator with eigenvalue equal to one.

From the way
we impose that antisymmetry condition on the two spin-color-flavor families of functions defined above, we can define two
different physically sound subsets of the Hilbert space for each:

1.

Wavefunctions belonging to the permutation space corresponding to
(uds)\,\otimes\,c\,\otimes\,\bar{c}
((
\Lambda
+
\bar{c}c
)-like coupling); that is,
the
\mathcal{P_{\alpha}}
permutation matrices entering the antisymmetry operator act only on the three first quarks of each element of the linear combinations that define the 15 flavor singlet functions or, alternatively, the 30
F^{2}
= 3 functions. This produces 2 antisymmetric functions with
F^{2}
=0
(out of 15)
and 5 antisymmetric functions with
F^{2}
= 3 (out of 30). We have then two possible pentaquark states, corresponding each to linear combinations of functions with the same symmetry and quantum numbers that we name Ia and Ib, respectively.

2.

Wavefunctions belonging to the permutation space corresponding to
(qqc)\,\otimes\,q^{\prime}\,\otimes\,\bar{c}
, with
q,q^{\prime}=u,d,s
considered
on equal footing
((
\Xi_{c}
+
\bar{D}
)-like coupling). This means that we start with the
same
spin-color-flavor wavefunctions as in the previous case, but now we consider only two of the three light quarks to be indistinguishable from each other. This is done by
selecting permutation matrices in the antisymmetry operator that act only on the first two quarks. This gives us 7 antisymmetric functions with
F^{2}
=0
and 15 antisymmetric functions with
F^{2}
= 3. Those combinations produce two other pentaquarks, IIa and IIb.

These two sets simply represent different symmetry realizations of the internal wave function under quark exchange, rather than different assumed hadronic-molecule partitions.
This implies that the structure of the four possible pentaquark configurations is jointly determined by (i) the existence of four well-defined quantum numbers (including flavor), and (ii) the internal symmetry constraints imposed by fermionic antisymmetry.

4
Conclusions

We have investigated the hidden-charm pentaquark system with quark content
uds\,c\bar{c}
within a non-relativistic constituent quark model using the AL1 interaction, solving the corresponding Schrödinger equation by means of the Diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) technique. The calculation has been performed by constructing trial wavefunctions with well-defined quantum numbers in the spin, color and flavor (not only isospin) spaces, and imposing the appropriate antisymmetry constraints on the internal degrees of freedom.

By combining those flavor eigenvectors with the full colorless and
S=1/2
spin functions, we obtain
four
physically admissible
J^{P}=1/2^{-}

uds\,c\bar{c}
pentaquark configurations. Two of them lie above the
J/\psi\,\Lambda
threshold, with masses
M_{\mathrm{Ia}}=4473\pm 5~\mathrm{MeV}
and
M_{\mathrm{IIa}}=4350\pm 6~\mathrm{MeV}
, and are naturally compatible with the experimentally reported
P_{cs}(4459)
and
P_{cs}(4338)
signals, respectively. The remaining two states have masses below the
J/\psi\,\Lambda
threshold but above the
\eta_{c}\,\Lambda
one, suggesting that they should predominantly decay into the
\eta_{c}\,\Lambda
channel, which, to our knowledge, has not yet been explored experimentally.

If, instead of considering the full
I=0
flavor subspace, the strange quark is treated as distinguishable and the flavor sector is restricted to a single isospin eigenfunction, only one pentaquark state is obtained. Its predicted mass,
M_{\mathrm{III}}=4200\pm 4~\mathrm{MeV}
, lies below the
J/\psi\,\Lambda
threshold, and therefore it cannot be assigned to any of the observed structures. This implies that an exact treatment of the flavor space is necessary to reproduce the existence of the two experimentally reported
P_{cs}
pentaquarks.

Discovery of a Unique Close Quasar-DSFG Pair Linked by a [C II] Bridge at z = 5.63 z=5.63 Facilities: ALMA CARTA ( Comrie et al. 2021 ) , CASA ( McMullin et al. 2007 ; CASA Team et al. 2022 )
Yongda Zhu, Tom J. L. C. Bakx, Ryota Ikeda, Hideki Umehata, George D. Becker, Christopher Cain, Jaclyn B. Champagne, Xiaohui Fan, Yoshinobu Fudamoto, Xiangyu Jin, Hai-Xia Ma, Yang Sun, Tsutomu T. Takeuchi, Wei Leong Tee

Abstract
We report the discovery of a unique quasar-dusty star-forming galaxy (DSFG) system at z = 5.63 z=5.63 , consisting of the bright quasar J1133+1603 ( M UV = − 27.42 M_{\rm UV}=-27.42 ) and its compact, dust-obscured companion, J1133c. ALMA observations reveal a prominent [C II] bridge connecting the quasar and DSFG, indicating ongoing interaction at a projected separation of 1.8″ ( ∼ \sim 10 proper kpc). J1133c exhibits unusually bright and broad [C II] emission ( L [ CII ] > 10 43 ​ erg ​ s − 1 L_{\rm[CII]}>10^{43}\,\rm erg\,s^{-1} , FWHM > 500 ​ km ​ s − 1 \rm FWHM>500\,\rm km\,s^{-1} ), with a [C II] luminosity five times that of the quasar, suggesting intense star formation or potential AGN activity. The inferred star formation rate from [C II] is approximately 10 3 ​ M ⊙ ​ yr − 1 10^{3}M_{\odot}\rm yr^{-1} . The remarkable properties of this pair strongly suggest that galaxy interactions may simultaneously trigger both starburst and quasar activity, driving rapid evolution in the early universe.



I
Introduction

Mergers are considered significant triggers of quasar activity and star formation, with up to 50% of luminous high-redshift quasars linked to major mergers
(
Di Matteo et al. 2005
;
Hopkins et al. 2006
;
Decarli et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
. High-redshift quasar-dusty star-forming galaxy (DSFG), quasar-quasar, and quasar-submillimeter galaxy (SMG) pairs
(
Lee et al. 2019
;
Izumi et al. 2024
;
Decarli et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
are rare but essential for studying galaxy-quasar co-evolution during the reionization epoch. Such systems typically reside in dense environments where interactions profoundly impact both star formation and black hole growth, as seen in SMGs and active galactic nuclei (AGNs)
(
Alexander et al. 2005
;
Alexander et al. 2008
;
Umehata et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
.

The [C II] 158
\mu
m line can trace star formation and gas dynamics, offering insights into interstellar medium properties of AGNs and star-forming galaxies
(
Stacey et al. 1991
;
Lagache et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. The quasar-DSFG pair reported here, connected by a [C II] bridge, provides a unique opportunity to explore the effects of such interactions on the growth of massive galaxies. Similar [C II] bridges have been noted in other high-redshift systems, indicating active gas flows between interacting galaxies
(
Ginolfi et al. 2020
;
Umehata et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. This work uses the
Planck18
cosmology
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
)
for luminosity calculations.

In Cycle 9 (2022.1.00662.S; PI: Zhu), we conducted ALMA Band 7 observations targeting 21 quasars at
z\sim 5.5
identified in
Yang et al. 2017
;
Yang et al. 2019
, aiming to determine their precise systemic redshifts (
\Delta z\sim 10^{-4}
) through [C II] 158
\mu
m line emission (details in
Zhu et al. 2023
). Each observation included two overlapping spectral windows centered on the estimated redshift for [C II] line coverage, along with two additional spectral windows for dust continuum measurements. The observations utilized the C43-(1, 2, and 3) configurations, achieving an angular resolution of
\sim 1^{\prime\prime}
across the whole sample.

The data were calibrated and reduced using the CASA pipeline (version 6.4.1.12;
McMullin et al. 2007
;
CASA Team et al. 2022
). We created data cubes and imaged the [C II] emission following
Eilers et al. 2020
. Continuum subtraction was performed using
uvcontsub
, and imaging was executed with
tclean
using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 2 (natural weighting) to enhance sensitivity. The mean RMS noise level across our dataset was
\sim 0.25\ {\rm mJy\,beam^{-1}}
per 30 MHz bin.

III
Results

The quasar J1133+1603 (RA=11:33:08.78, Dec=+16:03:55.79) was observed with an integration time of 1118.88 seconds, yielding a continuum sensitivity of 0.0293
{\rm mJy\,beam^{-1}}
and an angular resolution of 0.632″. The companion, J1133c (RA=11:33:08.90, Dec=+16:03:55.47), lies southeast of the quasar at a projected separation of 1.8″ (
\sim
10 kpc; Figure
1
a). Both the quasar and companion appear relatively compact in the continuum, with FWHMs of
\sim 0.84\arcsec
and
\sim 0.7\arcsec
along the major axis, respectively. The continuum flux density is
9.57\times 10^{-4}
Jy for the quasar and
1.07\times 10^{-3}
Jy for J1133c. Despite J1133c being as bright as the quasar in the continuum and brighter in [C II] (see below), it is undetected in Pan-STARRS
z
-band imaging, likely due to the survey’s shallow depth (
m_{z}=20.9
; Figure
1
e).

We analyzed the [C II] emission, finding a luminosity of
2.88\times 10^{42}\,\rm erg\,s^{-1}
for the quasar and
1.43\times 10^{43}\,\rm erg\,s^{-1}
for J1133c, with the latter being 4.97 times higher. The star formation rate for J1133c, estimated from [C II], is
\log\rm[SFR/(M_{\odot}\,yr^{-1})]\sim 3

(
Lagache et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. Figures
1
(b) and (d) show [C II] emission from J1133c and the quasar, respectively. Notably, J1133c exhibits strong [C II] emission, while Figure
1
(c) shows a [C II] bridge linking the two, suggesting active interaction. Extended emission north of the quasar (panel d) further supports this scenario.

Panel (f) displays the [C II] spectra of both objects with best-fit Gaussians. The quasar’s [C II] redshift is
5.6253\pm 0.0005
with FWHM
567.0\pm 58.4\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
, while J1133c has
z=5.6391\pm 0.0001
and FWHM
511.6\pm 15.3\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
. The velocity offset between them is
\Delta v=626.4\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
. These redshifts may be affected by kinematics within the system, complicating separation measurements along the line of sight.

J1133c’s large velocity width (
\rm FWHM>500\,km\,s^{-1}
) is broader than the average [C II] FWHM of
\sim 300
km s
-1
in typical ALPINE galaxies
(
Béthermin et al. 2020
)
, marking it as a notable outlier. This suggests strong dynamical processes or feedback within J1133c, potentially due to interaction with the quasar.

IV
Discussion

The discovery of this quasar-DSFG system at
z=5.63
, linked by a [C II] bridge, provides a valuable case for studying galaxy and supermassive black hole co-evolution during reionization. The relatively compact morphology of J1133c in both its dust continuum (FWHM
\sim
0.84″) and [C II] emission (FWHM
\sim
1.01″) suggests a small star-forming region or gas stripped due to interaction. This compactness is particularly noteworthy compared to other known quasar companions at
z>5.5

(
Decarli et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
and raises the possibility of an obscured AGN within J1133c. The intense star formation and/or potential AGN activity in J1133c could result from an ongoing merger.

This system displays a smaller distance between the quasar and DSFG compared to other well-studied pairs, such as BRI 1202-0725 at
z=4.7
, with a separation of 3.8″ (25 kpc)
(
Lee et al. 2019
;
Drake et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. The [C II] bridge observed between J1133+1603 and J1133c suggests a direct interaction, potentially indicating a merger process. Similar gas bridges have been observed in other high-redshift systems, such as PSOJ167
-
13 and J1306+0356 at
z>6
, where [C II] and dust continuum emission connect both galaxies
(
Neeleman et al. 2019
, see also
Stacey & Arrigoni Battaia 2022
for a CO bridge at
z\sim 2.6
)
. Extended [C II] emission seen to the north of the quasar, spanning several kpc, further supports complex gas dynamics from this interaction. These characteristics highlight the role of mergers and gas flows in early galaxy formation and black hole growth, making this system a great case for exploring galaxy evolution and quasar activity in dense environments.

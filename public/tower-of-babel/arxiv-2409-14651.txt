On cobordism groups of Lagrangian immersions
Dominique Rathel-Fournier

Abstract
. We compute the cobordism group Ω lag ​ ( M ) \Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M) of Lagrangian immersions into a symplectic manifold ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) in terms of a stable homotopy group of a Thom spectrum constructed from M M . This generalizes a result of Eliashberg [ Eli84 ] in the case of exact symplectic manifolds. As applications, we compute Ω lag ​ ( M ) \Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M) when M M is a closed surface and give a partial description of Ω lag ​ ( M ) \Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M) when M M is a monotone manifold.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold.
In this work, we
consider the Lagrangian cobordism group
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
of
Lagrangian immersions of closed manifolds in
M
.
The study of these cobordism groups is a
classical topic in symplectic topology.
They were introduced by Arnold
[
Arn80
]
,
who computed them for
M=\mathbb{C}
and
M=T^{*}S^{1}
.

In the case of an exact symplectic manifold
(M,d\lambda)
,
the group
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
was computed by Eliashberg
[
Eli84
]

in terms of the stable homotopy groups of a certain Thom spectrum constructed from
M
.
The key ingredient of the proof is the h-principle for Lagrangian immersions
[
Gro71
,
Lee76
]
,
which reduces the existence problem for Lagrangian immersions to a purely topological problem.
More detailed computations of the structure of
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)

were carried out by Audin
[
Aud85
,
Aud87a
,
Aud87b
]
in the case of

\mathbb{C}^{n}
and of cotangent bundles.

The goal of this work is to extend Eliashberg’s computation of
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
to general symplectic manifolds.
As in the exact case, we will show that
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)

is isomorphic to a stable homotopy group of a Thom spectrum constructed from
M
.

1.1.
Main result

In the remainder of this work, we assume that all manifolds and cobordisms are oriented.
The non-oriented case can be treated by similar methods.

In order to state the main result,
we give a brief description of the relevant spectrum.
A more precise definition is given in Section
4
.
As in the classical setting, this spectrum is the Thom spectrum associated to a
cobordism theory of manifolds equipped with a certain stable tangential structure
(see Section
2.3
for the definitions relevant to cobordism theory).

Inspired by the h-principle, we consider the cobordism theory of manifolds
equipped with a
stable formal Lagrangian immersion
into
M
.
Here, a formal Lagrangian immersion of a manifold
L
is a Lagrangian bundle map
TL\to TM
covering
a map
f:L\to M
satisfying
[f^{*}\omega]=0\in H^{2}(L;\mathbb{R})
,
along with a choice of trivialization of

f^{*}\omega
.
A trivialization of
f^{*}\omega
can be equivalently seen
as a cohomology class of primitives of
f^{*}\omega
, or as a homotopy class of lifts of
f
in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\omega}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where

is the homotopy fiber of a map

that classifies
[\omega]\in H^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover,
stabilization
of formal solutions corresponds to replacing

by
\mathbb{R}^{k}\oplus TL
and
TM
by
\mathbb{C}^{k}\oplus TM
for large

In Section

,
we construct a natural classifying space
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{\omega}M
for stable formal Lagrangian immersions.
Roughly speaking,
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{\omega}M
is obtained from the stable Lagrangian Grassmannian
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}M

killing the cohomology class of
\omega
.
The space
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{\omega}M
carries a "tautological" stable vector bundle

,
with the property that stable formal Lagrangian immersions of
L

correspond to maps of stable bundles

.
We denote by

the stable inverse of
\gamma^{\omega}
,
which has the property that a
\gamma^{\omega}
-structure on

to a
-\gamma^{\omega}
-structure on the stable normal bundle

.
We let
\Th(-\gamma^{\omega})
be the associated Thom spectrum.
The main result of this work is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
of dimension
2n
, there is a natural isomorphism

(1)

\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M,\omega)\cong\pi_{n}\,\Th(-\gamma^{\omega}).

Remark 1.2
.

The spectrum
\Th(-\gamma^{\omega})

is canonically defined up to a contractible choice.
Moreover, it is natural in the sense that it
defines a functor from the category of symplectic manifolds (of a given dimension)
and symplectic maps to the stable homotopy category.
The isomorphism (
1

a natural isomorphism
of functors.

1.2.
Computations

As applications of the main theorem,
we compute the group
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
when
M
is a closed surface
and investigate the structure of
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
when

1.2.1.
Monotone manifolds

Definition 1.3
.

A symplectic manifold

is

if there

such that
[\omega]=\tau\,c_{1}(M,\omega)

Examples of monotone manifolds include exact manifolds, closed surfaces of genus

.

If

(2)

Remark 1.5

As the proof of Proposition

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cobordism groups of Lagrangian immersions

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.

Definition 2.1
.

An immersion
f:L\to M
of a manifold
L
of dimension
n
is
Lagrangian
if
f^{*}\omega=0
.

We recall the definition of a Lagrangian cobordism between two Lagrangian immersions,
which is due to Arnold
[
Arn80
]
.
Let
f_{0}:L_{0}\to M

and
f_{1}:L_{1}\to M

be Lagrangian immersions of (possibly disconnected) closed manifolds into
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
Lagrangian cobordism
from
f_{0}
to
f_{1}
consists
of a compact manifold
V
with boundary
\partial V=L_{0}\sqcup L_{1}
,
along with
a Lagrangian immersion
F:V\to T^{*}[0,1]\times M
satisfying the following assumptions:

•

F^{-1}(T^{*}_{k}[0,1]\times M)=L_{k}
for
k=0,1
.

•

There is a collar embedding
j_{0}:L_{0}\times[0,\varepsilon)\to V

such that
F\circ j_{0}(x,t)=(t,0,f_{0}(x))
.
Similarly, there is a collar embedding
j_{1}:L_{1}\times(1-\varepsilon,1]\to V

such that
F\circ j_{1}(x,t)=(t,0,f_{1}(x))
.

One obtains variations on this definition by requiring that the manifolds be equipped
with extra structures.
In this work, we will only consider the
oriented
theory, i.e. we shall assume
that the manifolds
L_{i}
are oriented and that
V
is an oriented cobordism.
The unoriented case can be treated by similar methods.

Lagrangian cobordism defines an equivalence relation on Lagrangian immersions.
We let
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
be the associated Lagrangian cobordism group,
where the group operation is disjoint union.
Note that the inverse in
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)

corresponds to reversing the orientation.

If
f:(M,\omega)\to(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
is a symplectic map between manifolds of the same dimension,
then there is an induced pushforward morphism
f_{*}:\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)\to\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M^{\prime})
.
In this way, the Lagrangian cobordism group defines a functor
on the category of symplectic manifolds of a given dimension.

2.2.
The h-principle for Lagrangian immersions

Let
L
be a manifold.
A map of vector bundles
\varphi:TL\to TM

is
Lagrangian
if
\varphi(T_{x}L)
is a Lagrangian subspace for every
x\in L
.
Equivalently, a Lagrangian bundle map
is a section of the differential relation
\mathcal{R}_{\operatorname{lag}}\subset J^{1}(L,M)

of Lagrangian immersions, where
J^{1}(L,M)
denotes the 1st order jet space.
1
1

1

Sections of
\mathcal{R}_{\operatorname{lag}}
are often called
formal Lagrangian immersions
, but we shall reserve this name for the objects introduced in Definition
3.3
below, which
carry the additional datum of a trivialization of the pullback of
\omega
.

A Lagrangian bundle map is
holonomic
if it is equal to
Df:TL\to TM
for some Lagrangian immersion
f:L\to M
.

We shall need the following form of the h-principle for Lagrangian immersions, due to Gromov
[
Gro71
,
Gro86
]
and Lees
[
Lee76
]
.
See also
[
EM02
]
for a textbook account.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\varphi:TL\to TM
be a Lagrangian bundle map covering a map
f:L\to M
.
If
[f^{*}\omega]=0\in H^{2}(L;\mathbb{R})
,
then
\varphi
is homotopic to a holonomic solution.
Moreover,
if
\varphi
is holonomic on a neighborhood of a closed subset
A\subset L
,
then
\varphi
is homotopic rel.
A
to a holonomic solution
provided that
[f^{*}\omega]=0\in H^{2}(L,A;\mathbb{R})
.

We shall also need the following special case of the parametric h-principle for exact Lagrangian immersions.
Let
(M,\omega)
be an exact symplectic manifold.
Given a
1
-form
\lambda
with
d\lambda=\omega
, a Lagrangian immersion
f:L\to M

is called
\lambda
-exact
if
f^{*}\lambda
is exact.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
EM02
, 16.3.1]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be an exact symplectic manifold and
\lambda_{t}

be a smooth path of
1
-forms with
d\lambda_{t}=\omega
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Let
f_{t}:L\to M
be a path of Lagrangian immersions
such that
f_{0}
is
\lambda_{0}
-exact.
Then there exists a path of Lagrangian immersions
(g_{t})

such that
g_{0}=f_{0}
and
g_{t}
is
\lambda_{t}
-exact for all
t\in[0,1]
.

3.
Cobordism groups of formal Lagrangian immersions

In this section, we use the h-principle for Lagrangian immersions
to show that
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)
is isomorphic
to a cobordism group
\Omega^{\operatorname{form}}(M)

of formal Lagrangian immersions.
Inspired by the h-principle, the group
\Omega^{\operatorname{form}}(M)
that
we consider
has generators given by Lagrangian bundle maps
TL\to TM

equipped with a cohomology class of primitives of the pullback of
\omega
,
which we call a
trivialization
.
In Section
3.1
, we introduce trivializations
of exact forms
and prove some basic properties.
In Section
3.2
,
we introduce the cobordism group
\Omega^{\operatorname{form}}(M)

and prove the isomorphism
\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(M)\cong\Omega^{\operatorname{form}}(M)
.

3.1.
Trivializations of exact differential forms

Let
L
be a manifold, possibly with boundary.
Let
\alpha
be an exact
q
-form on
L
.
A primitive of
\alpha
is a form
\lambda
of degree
q-1

with
d\lambda=\alpha
.
Two primitives
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}

of
\alpha
are called cohomologous
if the closed form
\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}
is exact.

Definition 3.1
.

A
trivialization
of an exact form
\alpha
is a cohomology class of primitives of
\alpha
.
The set of trivializations of
\alpha
is denoted
\Triv(\alpha)
.

Note that if
\alpha=0
, then
\Triv(\alpha)=H_{\dR}^{q-1}(L)
.
In this case, there is a preferred trivialization given by
0\in H_{\dR}^{q-1}(L)
.
In the general case,
\Triv(\alpha)
is an affine space
over
H_{\dR}^{q-1}(L)
,
but there is no canonical identification with
H_{\dR}^{q-1}(L)
.

The set of trivializations defines a functor
in the following way:
if
(L,\alpha)
and
(L^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})

are manifolds equipped with exact
q
-forms
and
f:L\to L^{\prime}
is a smooth map such that
f^{*}\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
,
then there is a natural pullback map
f^{*}:\Triv(\alpha^{\prime})\to\Triv(\alpha)

which is equivariant with respect to the actions of
H^{q-1}_{\dR}(L)
and

H^{q-1}_{\dR}(L^{\prime})
.

3.1.1.

Suppose now that
V
is a compact manifold with boundary,
\alpha
is an exact
q
-form on
V
,
and we are given a trivialization
\tau
of the restriction
\alpha|_{\partial V}
.
The obstruction to the existence of a trivialization on

6.2.

Recall that we denote by

and by

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a monotone manifold.
Then for every

,
the form
\pi_{k}^{*}\omega

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of
[

, Corollary A.3]
,
which we recall for completeness.

For a symplectic vector bundle
E\to B
,
the tautological bundle
\gamma\to\mathcal{L}(E)

is a Lagrangian subbundle of the pullback
\pi^{*}E
, where
\pi:\mathcal{L}(E)\to B

is the projection.
This gives an isomorphism of complex vector bundles
\pi^{*}E\cong\gamma\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
In turn, this implies that

\pi^{*}c_{1}(E)=c_{1}(\pi^{*}E)=c_{1}(\gamma\otimes\mathbb{C})=0,

since
\gamma
is oriented.

Suppose now that
M
is a monotone symplectic manifold.
Then
[\omega]=\tau\,c_{1}(TM)

\pi_{k}^{*}[\omega]=\tau\,\pi_{k}^{*}c_{1}(TM)=\tau\,\pi_{k}^{*}c_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{k-n}\oplus TM)=0.

∎

We now give the proof of the splitting
\Th(-\gamma^{\omega})\simeq\Th(-\gamma)\wedge K(\mathbb{R},1)_{+}

for a monotone manifold.

.

,

.

the pullback by

is fiber-homotopically trivial.

.

we have

\Omega^{\operatorname{lag}}(\Sigma)\cong H_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\oplus\mathbb{R}/\operatorname{Per}_{\omega},

where the term
\mathbb{R}/\operatorname{Per}_{\omega}
is computed in Lemma
6.7
.
To finish the proof, we observe
that the inclusion
\mathcal{L}\Sigma\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\Sigma

is an isomorphism on
\pi_{1}
by Corollary
4.3
, and therefore is an isomorphism on
H_{1}(-;\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Appendix A
Classifying fibrations for trivializations of forms

In this appendix, we
construct the classifying fibrations
E^{\alpha}\to M

whose existence was asserted in Section
4.2
,
and give the proof of Proposition
4.4
.
The construction is based on simplicial methods.
The basic ingredient is the fact that, in the world of simplicial sets,
the representation theorem for cohomology
holds at the cochain level.
This allows us to define classifying maps for
closed forms that are well-defined up to a canonical homotopy equivalence.
4
4

4

It is plausible that this method in fact yields classifying maps that
are canonical up to a contractible choice, but we shall not prove this.

A.1.
A simplicial model

We start by defining a convenient simplicial model for the path fibration over
K(\mathbb{R},q)
.
In the following, we shall make use of some well-known facts about simplicial sets;
standard references for this material are
[
May67
,
GJ09
]
.

For a simplicial set
X
, let
C^{*}(X;\mathbb{R})

be the complex of normalized
\mathbb{R}
-valued simplicial cochains on
X
.
Denote by
Z^{*}(X;\mathbb{R})
the cocycles of this complex.

Following
[
May67
, §23]
,
we consider the simplicial abelian group
C(\mathbb{R},q)
whose set of

n
-simplices is
C^{q}(\Delta^{n};\mathbb{R})
,
where
\Delta^{n}
is the standard
n
-simplex.
Let
Z(\mathbb{R},q)
the simplicial subgroup of
C(\mathbb{R},q)

given by the cocycles.
The differential of the normalized cochain complex defines a simplicial map
d:C(\mathbb{R},q-1)\to Z(\mathbb{R},q)

whose kernel is
Z(\mathbb{R},q-1)
.

We shall need the following
well-known properties of these simplicial sets,
whose proofs are given in
[
May67
]
.

(i)

The geometric realization
|Z(\mathbb{R},q)|
is an
Eilenberg-MacLane space
K(\mathbb{R},q)
.

(ii)

C(\mathbb{R},q)
is contractible.

(iii)

d
is a Kan fibration between Kan complexes.

It follows from these properties that the geometric realization
|d|:|C(\mathbb{R},q-1)|\to|Z(\mathbb{R},q)|

is a Serre fibration
and a model for the path fibration over
K(\mathbb{R},q)
.
From now on, we shall use this fibration as our preferred model
for the path fibration.
To simplify the notation, we will write

K(\mathbb{R},q)=|Z(\mathbb{R},q)|
,

P(\mathbb{R},q)=|C(\mathbb{R},q-1)|

and

\delta=|d|
.

The simplicial set
C(\mathbb{R},q)
represents the normalized cochains functor in the sense
that for any simplicial set

there is a natural isomorphism
C^{q}(X;\mathbb{R})\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{sSet}}(X,C(\mathbb{R},q))
,
see
[
May67
, Lemma 24.2]
.
Similarly, there is a natural isomorphism

see
[
May67
, Lemma 24.3]
.

If
\alpha

-cocycle on

,
then in the following diagram:

(15)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathbb{R},q-1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z(\mathbb{R},q)}

It follows from

Recall that for any simplicial set

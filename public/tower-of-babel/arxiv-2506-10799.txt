Abstract
Cold gaseous systems play important roles in galaxy evolution by possibly providing fuel to ignite active galactic nuclei (AGN) activity and star-formation. In this work, we analyze [C ii ] 158 ​ μ ​ m 158\rm\mu m and continuum observations from ALMA for a sample of four radio AGN at z ≈ 3.5 z\approx 3.5 , focusing on eight associated companion cloud systems discovered within projected distances of tens of kiloparsecs or less. The spatial distribution of these companions indicates that the majority of cold gas is not located at the AGN position, i.e., not in their host galaxies. With the assistance of [C ii ] at 0.2 ​ ″ 0.2\arcsec resolution, we further confirm the gas-poor nature of the hosts by re-analyzing archival [C i ] (a tracer of H 2 ) at ∼ 2 ​ ″ \sim 2\arcsec resolution. Our sample has [C ii ] luminosities in a range of 2.8 × 10 8 < L [ C ii ] / L ⊙ < 4.2 × 10 9 2.8\times 10^{8}<L_{[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}/L_{\odot}<4.2\times 10^{9} . The L [ C ii ] / L IR L_{\rm[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}/L_{\rm IR} ratio, ∼ 9.4 × 10 − 4 \sim 9.4\times 10^{-4} , is consistent with sources discussed in the literature. Our findings show the gas-poor radio AGN hosts have nearby gas-rich companions. We propose that these companions, which may be stripped clouds in merger process and by interaction with the energy output from the AGN, could also be a trigger of radio-loud AGN. They may also be a signature of negative AGN feedback (e.g., shock heating) on these infalling companions and on the host galaxy. In general, our analysis shows that powerful AGN at and before Cosmic Noon are impacting and being impacted by cold gaseous clouds in their circum-galactic or proto-intracluster media.



I
Introduction

Active galactic nuclei (AGN) feedback regulating galaxy mass build-up is critical for galaxy evolution
(
Harrison & Ramos Almeida 2024
, e.g.,)
. Molecular gas, the fuel for star formation, can be ejected, disturbed, and/or heated by various feedback processes, leading to galaxy quenching. What triggers AGN activities is not fully understood, but mergers may play a critical role
(
Chiaberge et al. 2015
;
Wang et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. The class of high-redshift radio galaxies (HzRGs,
z\gtrsim 1
,
L_{\rm 500\rm MHz}>10^{27}\,\rm W\,Hz^{-1}
), type-2 AGN with the most powerful radio jets, quasar level AGN activities, and no contamination of the central point source due to central obscuration
(
Vernet et al. 2001
;
Miley & De Breuck 2008
;
Drouart et al. 2014
;
Falkendal et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
are ideal sources to examine these processes. Residing in the center of proto-cluster environments and being the progenitors of bright cluster galaxies, HzRGs can help us to understand the evolution of massive systems in dense regions of the Universe
(
Wylezalek et al. 2013
;
Wylezalek et al. 2014
;
Noirot et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
.

Based on spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting
(
Seymour et al. 2007
;
De Breuck et al. 2010
;
Falkendal et al. 2019
)
, it has been shown that the massive,
M_{\star}\sim 10^{11}\,M_{\odot}
, host galaxies of these radio AGN are quenched or in the process of being quenched. Further observations focusing on ionized gas on large circumgalactic scales showed evidence of feedback
(
Nesvadba et al. 2006
;
Nesvadba et al. 2017b
;
Nesvadba et al. 2017a
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023
, tens to hundreds of kiloparsecs, e.g.,)
.
Kolwa et al. 2023
reported that the observed [C
i
] (1-0) (a tracer for H
2
,
\nu_{\rm rest}=492.16\,
GHz, hereafter [C
i
]) emission is faint in a sample of HzRGs suggesting low content of cold neutral and molecular gas. It remains unclear whether the [C
i
] traced H
2
gas is located within the host galaxy under
\sim 2\arcsec
resolution, if a brighter emission is detected. For example, some well-known HzRGs are found to be gas-rich and undergoing starbursts, but much of the gas and elevated star formation activity is associated with nearby companions revealed in zoom-in views
(
Emonts et al. 2015a
;
Zhong et al. 2024
;
Nesvadba et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. Therefore, observations at higher resolution and higher sensitivity will help reveal the locations of gas and star formation activity, leading to a better understanding of the role of AGN
(
Wang et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.

Combining resolution-matched
JWST
/NIRSpec IFU and Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) observations,
Wang et al. 2025
reported the ubiquitous detection of gaseous companion systems around
z\approx 3.5
radio AGN. Following up on this discovery, in this Letter, we analyze these companion systems focusing on the ones detected to have cold gas. [C
ii
]
158\,\rm\mu m
(
\nu_{\rm rest}=1900.537\,
GHz, hereafter [C
ii
]) is the brightest far infrared (FIR) emission line and an important gas coolant in galaxies
(
Lagache et al. 2018
)
. Directly associated with photodissociation region (PDR), the low ionization potential of C atom, 11.3 eV, makes it a tracer of multiple gas phases. Although it is not a tracer of H
2
in the presence of hard radiation fields of AGN, this fine structure line is critical for studying regular gas motion, stellar and AGN-driven outflows, and shocks
(
Appleton et al. 2017
;
Zanella et al. 2018
;
Ginolfi et al. 2020
;
Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. 2020
;
Lelli et al. 2021
;
Decarli et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
. In addition, the FIR continuum under or around [C
ii
] offers a direct measure of star formation rate
(
Falkendal et al. 2019
;
Schaerer et al. 2020
, SFR, e.g.,)
. Hence, we will study the cold gas content, dynamical masses, and star formation in these companions using the
400\,\rm GHz
ALMA observations with the assistance of Band 3 and
JWST
data. For this Letter, we assume a flat
\Lambda
cosmology with
H_{0}=70\,\rm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
and
\Omega_{m}=0.3
. Following this luminosity distance,
\rm{1\,arcsec=7.25-7.32\,kpc}
at the redshifts of this work.

III
Analysis

In
Wang et al. 2025
, we reported the ubiquitous discovery of nearby,
\lesssim 18\,
kpc, companion systems in the fields around the four
z\approx 3.5
radio AGN. This sample of HzRGs was selected to have diverse properties in terms of, e.g., SFR and radio morphologies, with no requirement that they reside in known over-dense environments. “Companions” under their definition represent systems either with (i) peculiar warm ionized gas kinematics different from the bulk gas motion or (ii) a detection of [C
ii
] emission. Hence, these companions may be gaseous clouds off-center from the AGN. We primarily focus on the [C
ii
] companions in this Letter to analyze the cold gas content. We briefly summarize the selection here
(see
Wang et al. 2025
, for details)
. The [C
ii
] companions are selected first based on visual check in the field of ALMA Band 8 primary beam (
\sim 10\arcsec\times 10\arcsec
) over the spectral bandwidth of
\sim 5300\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
. The companions are then checked to have a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)
\gtrsim 3
collapsed over
125\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
and are validated spectrally. Following the naming convention in
Wang et al. 2025
, the companions are named by the central radio AGN following the detection method.

We show the [C
ii
] moment 0 intensity contours in Fig.
1
a,
2
a, and
3

\sim 0.7\arcsec

We compute the emission line luminosity,
L_{\rm line}
, with the equation
(
Carilli & Walter 2013
;
Béthermin et al. 2020
;
Falkendal et al. 2021
)
:

\frac{L_{\rm line}}{L_{\odot}}=1.04\times 10^{-3}\times\left[\frac{I_{\rm line}}{\rm Jy\,km\,s^{-1}}\right]\left[\frac{D_{\rm L}}{\rm Mpc}\right]^{2}\left[\frac{\nu_{\rm obs,line}}{\rm GHz}\right],

(1)

where
I_{\rm line}
is the line intensity,
D_{\rm L}
is the luminosity distance in Mpc, and
\nu_{\rm obs,line}
is the observed line frequency.

In the radiation field of bright quasars, the relation between [C
ii
] and
M_{\rm H_{2}}
calibrated for star forming galaxies may not hold
(
Zanella et al. 2018
;
Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. 2020
;
Decarli et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
. The neutral carbon fine structure transitions are found to be a good tracer of cold molecular gas in these extreme fields
(
Papadopoulos & Greve 2004
;
Huang et al. 2024
;
Emonts et al. 2023
, or at least tracing cold neutral gas, e.g.,)
. Without additional data on higher order [C
i
] transitions, we calculate

solely based on [C
i
](1-0) following
Kolwa et al. 2023
:

\frac{M_{\rm H_{2},[\textup{C\,{i}}]}}{M_{\odot}}=\frac{1375.8}{Q_{10}(1+z)}\times\\
\left[\frac{D_{\rm L}}{\rm Mpc}\right]^{2}\left[\frac{X_{\rm[\textup{C\,{i}}]}}{10^{-5}}\right]^{-1}\left[\frac{A_{10}}{10^{-7}\,{\rm s^{-1}}}\right]^{-1}\left[\frac{I_{\rm[\textup{C\,{i}}]}}{\rm Jy\,km\,s^{-1}}\right],

(2)

where the excitation factor
Q_{10}
is assumed as
Q_{10}=0.48
, the [C
i
]-to-
\rm H_{2}
ratio is assumed as
X_{[\textup{C\,{i}}]}=3\times 10^{-5}
, and
A_{10}
is the Einstein A-coefficient with
A_{10}=7.93\times 10^{-8}\,\rm s^{-1}

(
Weiß et al. 2003
;
Papadopoulos & Greve 2004
;
Falkendal et al. 2021
)
.

If the [C
ii
] emitting gas clouds are virialized, we can estimate the dynamical masses of these systems assuming that they are dispersion-dominated
(
Übler et al. 2023
;
Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. 2020
;
Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020
, i.e.,
v_{\rm rot}/\sigma_{v}\lesssim\sqrt{3.36}
, where
v_{\rm rot}
is the rotation velocity,)
:

M_{\rm dyn}^{\rm dis}=K(n)K(q)\frac{\sigma_{v,\rm[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}^{2}r_{e}}{G}

(3)

where
G
is the gravitational constant,
r_{e}
is the effective radius,
\sigma_{v,\rm[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}
is the fitted Gaussian line width (after spectral resolution correction),
K(n)=8.87-0.831n+0.0241n^{2}
is a function of Sérsic index,
n
, and
K(q)=[0.87+0.38e^{-3.71(1-q)}]^{2}
is a function of axis ratio,
q
. We perform a 2D Gaussian fit to the [C
ii
] moment 0 maps using
lmfit
task in CASA and take
r_{e}=\sqrt{\rm FWHM_{x}\times FWHM_{y}}/2
, where FWHM
x
and FWHM
y
are fitted Gaussian widths after deconvolution with the beam. For J0205-[C
ii
], we use
r_{e}=2\sqrt{\theta_{\rm 400\,GHz}}
. Since
K(q)
is monotonically increasing with
q\in[0,1]
and
K(n)
is monotonically decreasing with
n\in[1,8]
, we take
q=1
and
n=1
in this calculation which returns the upper limit of
M_{\rm dyn}^{\rm dis}

(
Übler et al. 2023
;
Maiolino et al. 2023
)
. In

, we estimated the

assuming disk rotation using

Barolo
for J0121-[C

]-B and 4C19-[C

]
(

, Table

.

IV.1
Cold gas clouds around but not at radio AGN

The immediate conclusion from our [C
ii
] detections is that there are cold gas systems in the surroundings of
z\approx
3.5 radio AGN, but the majority of the gas is not at the AGN position. The AGN positions are determined from the cores of the radio maps. The systemic redshifts are derived based on He
ii
\lambda 1640
for TN J0121+1320 and TN J0205+2242 or [C
i
] if He
ii
\lambda 1640
is unavailable for 4C+19.71
(
z_{\rm sys}
from VLT/SINFONI [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
was used for 4C+03.24, see
Kolwa et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2025
, for more discussions)
. Our companions have
2.8\times 10^{8}<L_{[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}/L_{\odot}<4.2\times 10^{9}
with a median of
1.3\times 10^{9}\,L_{\odot}
.
L_{[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}
upper limits at the radio core are estimated to be
\lesssim 10^{9}\,L_{\odot}
(Sect.
III
and Table
1
). As shown in Fig.
1
b,
2
b, and
3
b, the [C
ii
] companions can have, in three cases, similar kinematic counterparts as in [O
iii
]
\lambda
5007 (J0121-[C
ii
]-C, 4C03-[C
ii
]-B, and J0205-[C
ii
]). This indicates that the companions are in the same potential well as the radio AGN host. The [C
ii
] line widths are generally narrower than the pure ionized gas tracer with a median
\sigma_{v}=105\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
and most of the [C
ii
] emitting clouds are spatially unresolved, indicating that the companion clouds are less perturbed by the AGN or they have much lower mass (4C+03.24 may be an exception).

In TN J0121+1320 and 4C+19.71, we detect [C
i
] emissions using extraction apertures centered at the [C
ii
] companions
(
Kolwa et al. 2023
, see also)
. We find that the two carbon line profiles are in agreement. In Fig.
1
b zoom-in panel, we compare the [C
i
] lines extracted at J0121-[C
ii
]-B (red) and radio core (green). Despite the overlap in extraction due to large
\theta_{\rm 100\,GHz}
(Sect.
III
),
L_{[\textup{C\,{i}}]}
at J0121-[C
ii
]-B is
\sim 2
times brighter than at the radio core (Table
1
). This implies that the H
2
gas (or at least cold neutral gas) is located more likely at the companion position. Taking also the fact that J0121-[C
ii
]-C extraction region has a larger overlap with [C
ii
]-B (
\sim 28\%
versus
\sim 15\%
, Sect.
III
) but having fainter
L_{[\textup{C\,{i}}]}
, this may further imply that the H
2
gas is located slightly towards east than west if not solely at [C
ii
]-B. A further inference may be that there is a gas exchange between the companion and host. The [C
i
] spectrum at J0121-[C
ii
]-A is consistent with the broad profile of [C
ii
] and is not contaminated by [C
ii
]-B (Sect.
III
, Fig.
1
b). It is new evidence of off-center H
2
which was missed in
Kolwa et al. 2023
. As discussed in Sect.
III
, cold gas traced by [C
ii
] and [C
i
] is not in the AGN host of 4C+19.71. The [C
i
] detection in 4C+03.24 by
Kolwa et al. 2023
is reported to be at
\Delta v_{\rm[\textup{C\,{i}}],K23}=1123\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
from the ionized gas (Fig.
2
). This may be a false detection as no [C
ii
] has been detected at the same
\Delta v
(see Appendix
A
). We stress that the main conclusion that the companions are rich in cold gas is based on [C
ii
] detections, such that overlap in some of the [C
i
] extractions does not affect it. Deeper and higher spatial resolution [C
i
] are required to more accurately determine the
M_{\rm H_{2},[\textup{C\,{i}}]}

Literature studies have demonstrated that [C
ii
], which is closely associated with PDRs, may serve as a tracer of H
2

(
Zanella et al. 2018
;
Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. We compare the molecular gas mass derived from [C
i
],
M_{\rm H_{2},[\textup{C\,{i}}]}
, and
L_{[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}
for our sample in Fig.
4
a together with the
Zanella et al. 2018

M_{\rm H_{2}}-L_{\rm\textup{C\,{ii}}}
relation. Three of our detections generally follow the relation while the majority have a tendency to fall below it especially for brighter [C
ii
] emitters (
>10^{9}\,L_{\odot}
). This is expected because in the vicinity,
\lesssim 18\,
kpc, of bright quasars with the energetic jets, a larger fraction of [C
ii
] may be associated with ionized and/or neutral gas than molecular gas
(
L_{\rm bol}\sim 10^{47}\rm erg\,s^{-1}
,
Miley & De Breuck 2008
;
Drouart et al. 2014
;
Falkendal et al. 2019
;
Fernández Aranda et al. 2024
;
Fudamoto et al. 2025
, also discussion in Sect.
IV.2
)
. Nevertheless, our finding indicates that the compaion systems are relatively gas-rich.

IV.2
Nature of [C
ii
] companions

In Fig.
2
b, we show the
L_{\rm IR}
versus
L_{[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}/L_{\rm IR}
ratio, along with comparison samples: the Spiderweb, a powerful and well-studied
z=2.16
radio galaxy, which is known to be gas-rich, undergoing a starburst, and residing in a dense environment
(
Miley et al. 2006
;
Seymour et al. 2012
;
Gullberg et al. 2016
;
Emonts et al. 2018
;
De Breuck et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
;
z>5
radio-loud quasar hosts
(
Mazzucchelli et al. 2025
, five with [C
ii
] detections from)
;
4<z<6
star-forming galaxies
(
Béthermin et al. 2020
;
Faisst et al. 2020
;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024
, ALPINE-CRISTAL, e.g.,)
. Even though five out of eight of our companions only have
L_{\rm IR}
upper limits, we find that our sample is on the same inversely correlated trend as the comparison samples
(
Díaz-Santos et al. 2013
, see also)
. Our sample has a median
L_{\rm[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}/L_{\rm IR}
ratio of
9.4\times 10^{-4}
which may also suggest an impact of bright quasars on C
+
ions, i.e., excitation to higher energy states
(
Mazzucchelli et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. J0121-[C
ii
]-B and 4C19-[C
ii
] relatively agree with Spiderweb CGM. This indicates that the environment of some of our
z\approx 3.5
radio galaxies is similar to the ones at
z\sim 2
. As
De Breuck et al. 2022
Spiderweb [C
ii
] observation was carried out using single-dish data with limited spatial information, it is possible that the cold gas, both of their CGM and AGN components, does also not coincide spatially with the radio AGN host in Spiderweb
(
Gullberg et al. 2016
, see also,)
.

Taking the zeroth-order estimate of
M_{\rm dyn}
and assuming
M_{\star}=M_{\rm dyn}/2
(Sect.
III
), we constrain our sample on the
M_{\star}
-SFR plane in Fig.
4
c
(
Faisst et al. 2020
, SED based
M_{\star}
and SFR for ALPINE galaxies,)
. For the
Mazzucchelli et al. 2025
sample, we take their estimated
M_{\rm dyn,dis}
and assume
M_{\star}=M_{\rm dyn}/2
, consistent with our approach. We calculate their SFR using the
L_{\rm FIR}
reported by
Mazzucchelli et al. 2025
, in the same way as for our sample. We note that the contribution of jet synchrotron emission to the continuum at frequencies around [C
ii
] is negligible,
\lesssim 0.1\%
, for HzRGs, i.e., our SFR estimates based on FIR continuum are not affected by non-thermal contribution
(
Falkendal et al. 2019
, see)
. Synchrotron emission might be a source of contamination for type-1 radio quasars such as the
Mazzucchelli et al. 2025
sample which has less steep radio SED index than HzRGs. We caution that, given most of our measurements are upper limits, the positions of our companions on the SFR-
M_{\star}
plane are uncertain. If we take the
M_{\star}
of J0121-[C
ii
]-B estimated based on the optical continuum, it may reside above the main sequence
(
Wang et al. 2025
, yellow-filled diamond with a gray border,
10^{9.9}\,M_{\odot}
,)
. Our findings may indicate that these are starbursting clumps. The non-detection of FIR continuum in five out of eight cases may suggest low SFR. However, we note that the star formation timescale probed by FIR dust continuum (
\sim 100\,\rm Myr
) is longer than, for example, measures from H
\alpha

(
De Looze et al. 2014
,
\sim 10\,\rm Myr
, e.g.,)
. Hence, there is a possibility that some of these companions are super bursty events. This may further indicate a positive feedback scenario by the radio jet
(
Nesvadba et al. 2020
)
.

A more complicated case is the 4C+03.24 system. The [C
ii
] companions around 4C+03.24 is the only example in our sample that shows a morphological connection with the jet. This may resemble the discovery by
Walter et al. 2025
where the jet is interacting with [C
ii
] in a type-1 quasar at
z\approx 5.8
. Specifically, we see that the [C
ii
] clouds around 4C+03.24 are distributed along the radio jet and along the optical continuum (Fig.
2
). As discussed in
Wang et al. 2025
, 4C+03.24 system may be a post-major-merger where the cold gas was ram pressure stripped
(
Bekki 2009
, the optical continuum may be merger debris, e.g.,)
. Hence, another possibility is that these [C
ii
] emissions may be shock heated by the jet or the merger
(see
Ogle et al. 2012
;
Appleton et al. 2017
;
Appleton et al. 2018
, for a correlation of shock tracer, warm H
2
emission, and [C
ii
])
. Base on the morphology and distribution of 4C03-[C
ii
]-A, [C
ii
]-C, and the radio jet, it may be that the jet penetrates through these two clouds which may be evidence of jet-gas interaction.

IV.3
Impact on evolution

Regardless of the nature of the [C
ii
] companions, our detections show that there are gas-rich (or pure gas) systems close to the radio AGN. Similar results are seen in other high-
z
radio quasars. For example, the Dragonfly galaxy, a
z=1.92
radio galaxy, has a gas-rich “wet” major merger near the radio AGN
(
Emonts et al. 2015a
;
Emonts et al. 2015b
;
Zhong et al. 2024
)
.
Vayner et al. 2021
found that large molecular gas reservoirs are not located at the position of the quasar. In addition,
Lee et al. 2023
reported massive H
2
companions around a
z=5.2
radio galaxy.
Wang et al. 2025
suggested that these candidate mergers can be a trigger of radio AGN activities
(
Chiaberge et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
. Similarly,
Meyer et al. 2025
argued that the [C
ii
]-traced gas in a
z\approx 6.6
quasar may be related to merger stripping. In this Letter, we further propose an evolution scenario of the whole system. The massive hosts of the radio AGN formed their stellar mass fast and depleted most of the gas in the host galaxies
(
De Breuck et al. 2010
;
Falkendal et al. 2019
,
M_{\star}\sim 10^{11}
,)
. The gas-rich companions go through minor mergers or interactions with the central galaxies through which they brought gas (or gas being passively stripped) into the central supermassive black holes
(
Lehnert et al. 2016
, SMBHs, e.g.,)
. The interaction facilitates the dissipation of angular momentum, enabling gas accretion, leading AGN ignition and jet launching. The AGN feedback may later compress the gas and start off-center starbursts. Alternatively, the feedback may be negative such that the jets heat up the gas through shocks leading to the observed [C
ii
]
(
Ogle et al. 2012
;
Vayner et al. 2024
)
. This further implies the radio AGN may quench the CGM. The active galaxy, NGC 3079, and its companions may be a local example of this quenching where the “AGN stripping” is happening, i.e., outflow blows gas out of the companions
(
Irwin et al. 1987
;
Shafi et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
.

IV.4
Conclusion

In this Letter, we presented the analysis of cold gas companion systems traced by [C
ii
] emissions at
\lesssim 18\,\rm kpc
around four
z\approx 3.5
radio galaxies observed by ALMA in Band 8
(
Wang et al. 2025
)
. The distribution of [C
ii
] and comparison with [O
iii
]
\lambda
5007 observed by
JWST
/NIRSpec IFU suggests that the majority of the cold gas clouds are located away from the radio core and do not always follow the fully ionized gas. Guided by the high-resolution [C
ii
] data, we find that the [C
i
], with
\sim 10
times poorer resolution, is located at the companion position. Our sample has a median
L_{[\rm\textup{C\,{ii}}]}
of
1.3\times 10^{9}\,L_{\odot}
and is relatively below the
M_{\rm H_{2}}
-
L_{[\textup{C\,{ii}}]}
relation with a low H
2
mass derived from [C
i
]. Compared to the cold gas content at the radio core position, we find that our companions are more gas-rich than the AGN hosts. We propose that these [C
ii
] companions may ignite the radio AGN by bringing gas into the SMBHs through merger interactions. The [C
ii
] emitters may be starbursting clumps and/or shock heated gas implying the feedback from the radio AGN impacting the CGM of dense HzRG systems. Future analysis of line ratios and kinematics with ionization and shock modeling, jointly with optical lines in NIRSpec IFU
(
Wang et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2025
;
Vayner et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
and UV lines in VLT/MUSE
(
Kolwa et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2023
, especially C
iv
1548,1551 and C
iii
]1907,1909,)
, will reveal the complex scenarios of feedback on kiloparsecs scales. In addition,
JWST
/MIRI observations of direct warm H
2
detection will be crucial for shock analysis of these systems.

On the number of components of folds of image simple fold maps
Rustam Sadykov, Osamu Saeki

Abstract
. A smooth map between manifolds is said to be image simple if its restriction to its singular point set is a topological embedding. We study the parity of the number of connected components of the singular point set for image simple fold maps from a closed manifold of dimension ≥ 2 \geq 2 to a surface. It is known that this parity is a homotopy invariant when the source manifold is of even dimension and the target surface is orientable. In this paper we show that for an arbitrary image simple fold map from a closed odd-dimensional manifold of dimension ≥ 3 \geq 3 to a (possibly non-orientable) surface, this parity is not a homotopy invariant. We give two constructive proofs: one uses techniques from open book decompositions and round fold maps, and the other uses allowable moves. The constructed homotopies add one new component to the singular point set. We also give a new proof of the homotopy invariance of the parity of the number of connected components of the singular point set for image simple fold maps from a closed even-dimensional manifold to an orientable surface together with an example showing that this does not hold for maps into non-orientable surfaces in general.

Introduction

Given a smooth map

between manifolds of dimensions
m
and
n
, respectively, with

we say that

is a

if
the differential

df_{x}\colon T_{x}M\to T_{f(x)}N
does not have the maximal rank

.
It is known that when

is a
generic
smooth map into a surface

, the set
\Sigma(f)
of its singular points
consists of folds and cusps (defined in Section
2
), see
[
16
,
17
]
.
More specifically, for a given
closed
manifold
M
of
\dim{M}\geq 2
and a surface

the set of smooth maps

which have only folds and cusps as their

of smooth maps of

into

endowed with the Whitney

topology (for example, see
[
5
]
).
In this sense, generic maps to surfaces can be considered as generalizations of
Morse functions on manifolds which are generic maps into the
1

manifold
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, it is expected that
the topology of the source manifold
M
is somehow related to
various properties of generic maps
M\to S
on that manifold.

In this paper, we focus on generic maps to surfaces without cusp singularities.
A smooth map
f\colon M\to S
that has only folds
as its singularities is called a
fold map
.
Note that if
M
is a closed manifold,
then the singular point set
\Sigma(f)
is a finite disjoint union of circles in
M
,
and the restriction
f|_{\Sigma(f)}
is an immersion.
Among fold maps, we are mainly interested in
those whose singular point sets have well-behaved images.
We say that a smooth map

between manifolds is

if the restriction of
f
to the set

\Sigma(f)
of its singular points is a topological embedding.
In other words, the image
f(\Sigma(f))
of the singular point set has no self-intersections in the target manifold.
Image simple smooth generic maps
have recently attracted some detailed studies (see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
15
]
).

Thus, our main objective
is to study certain topological properties of image simple fold maps from a closed manifold
M
of dimension

m\geq 2
to a surface
S
. The image
f(\Sigma(f))
of the singular point set
\Sigma(f)
of such a map
is a finite disjoint union of embedded closed curves in
S
.
The main aim of the paper is to study one of the basic characteristics of an image simple fold map
f
,
namely the parity of the number
\#|\Sigma(f)|
of components of the singular point
set, and specifically, whether this parity is a homotopy invariant of

or not.

This question is a generalization of the first of the two Takase problems formulated in
[
13

.
Takase considered image simple generic maps
f\colon S^{3}\to S^{2}
without definite fold singular points.
(For the definition of a definite fold singularity, refer to Section
2
.)
The first Takase problem asks if the number
\#|\Sigma(f)|
of components of
\Sigma(f)
is congruent modulo two to
d+1
,
where
d\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{3}(S^{2})
is the homotopy class represented by

.
The second problem asks if the minimum number of components of
\Sigma(f)
is equal to
|d|+1
.

The second author
[
14
]
later answered the first Takase problem in the negative by
constructing two homotopic image simple fold maps

and
g
without definite folds such that

\#|\Sigma(f)|\not\equiv\#|\Sigma(g)|\pmod{2}
,
see Section
4
for details.
Thus, in the case of image simple fold maps of manifolds of dimension
3
to surfaces,
the parity of the number of fold components is not a homotopy invariant.
It is worth mentioning that the second author constructed an explicit homotopy
F\colon M\times[0,1]\to S\times[0,1]

between
f
and
g
.
The singular point set of a generic
homotopy between fold maps to a surface is, in general, a smooth surface properly embedded in
M\times[0,1]
, and we see that
for the specific homotopy
F
constructed in
[
14
]
,
the surface
\Sigma(F)
of the singular points of

is the disjoint union of
\Sigma(f)\times[0,1]
and a Möbius band.
Furthermore, the restriction of
F
to the Möbius band has a triple self-intersection point.

The mentioned results raised the broader question of identifying the precise conditions under which
the parity of the number of fold components
is a homotopy invariant.
It was partially answered by the first author and Kahmeyer in
[
7
]
, who proved that
for an image simple fold map

of
a closed manifold

of dimension
\geq 2
to a surface

, the parity of the number of
connected components of

does not change under homotopy

if any of the following conditions holds:

(1)

the dimension of

is even and
S
is orientable (refer to

5.1
of the present paper as well),

(2)

the singular point set
\Sigma(F)
of the homotopy is an orientable surface, or

(3)

the restriction
F|_{\Sigma(F)}
of homotopy to its singular point set does not have triple self-intersection points,
and either
M
is a closed orientable manifold of dimension
3
and
S
is orientable, or

M
is a closed manifold of odd dimension
m>2
and
S
is
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
S^{2}
.

In the present paper we will study the cases excluded in the above result (1):
when the dimension of

is odd and is at least

and

is an arbitrary surface, and when
the dimension of

is even and

is a non-orientable surface.
Our first main result shows that in the odd-dimensional case, the parity is not a homotopy invariant
as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a closed odd-dimensional manifold with

m=\dim M\geq 3
, and
S
be a possibly non-orientable surface.
Then, for every image simple fold map

, there exists a homotopy

connecting

to another image simple fold map

have different parities.
Furthermore, the homotopy

In fact, we will construct an explicit homotopy

satisfying the conclusion of Theorem

Its singular point set
\Sigma(F)

contains a Möbius band component, and

has a triple self-intersection point.
This shows that conditions (2) and (3) above are necessary in general.
On other other hand, since Theorem

permits the target surface

to be non-orientable,
it is not guaranteed that every homotopy satisfying

must have a triple self-intersection point.
However, if we remove the condition that
the homotopy should be supported in a small disc, then, as we
will see below, in the case where

is even-dimensional, and both

and

are non-orientable, the absence of a triple self-intersection
is not a necessary condition.

Our second main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
n\geq 1
, there exist a closed
2n
-dimensional non-orientable
manifold

and image simple fold maps

and
g\colon M\to\mathcal{M}

to the open Möbius band

such that

and

are homotopic, but

\#|\Sigma(f)|\not\equiv\#|\Sigma(g)|\pmod{2}
.

We also construct an explicit homotopy

between

and

in Theorem
1.2

such that the singular point set

is non-orientable, but that
it has no triple self-intersections.
This again shows that condition (2) above is necessary in general: however,
condition (3) is not necessary in general.

To summarize, let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension at least

, and

be a surface.
Let

be a homotopy between
two image simple fold maps

and

.
Suppose that the dimension of

is even.

•

If

]

or by Theorem

.

•

If

.

•

If

, then

is non-orientable,

]
and

.

Suppose that the dimension

•

The parity of the number of fold components
is not a homotopy invariant in general by

]
in the case

and

in the case

.

•

If
\Sigma(F)
is an orientable surface, then the parity is an invariant by

]
.

Furthermore, suppose that either

is a closed orientable manifold of dimension

and

is orientable, or

is a closed manifold of odd dimension

and

is
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or

.

•

If

, then

has a triple self-intersection point by

]
.

We do not know if the last statement remains true without the assumptions on

.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

In Section

A smooth map

Definition 2.3
.

The following has been obtained

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M
be an even-dimensional closed manifold of dimension

a connected orientable surface. Then, for image simple fold

of the singular point set is a homotopy

Proof.

Consider an image simple fold
map
f\colon M\to S
. Then,
f(\Sigma(f))
is a finite disjoint
union of embedded closed curves in

, and we denote
by
R_{1},R_{2},\ldots,R_{s}
the connected components of

.
We also denote by
\chi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
the Euler characteristic
of the fiber over a point in
R_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,s
.
When
R_{i}
is not contained in the image of
f
, we set
\chi_{i}=0
.
Note that the regular fibers of
f
are of even dimension
m-2

and that the parity of their Euler characteristics
is a homotopy invariant of
f
. We denote it by
\chi_{f}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
Set
r_{f}=0\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
if
\chi_{f}=0
and set
r_{f}=1\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}

otherwise.

Let us consider a point in
S
crossing a component of
f(\Sigma(f))
.
Then the Euler characteristic of the corresponding fiber always
changes by
\pm 2
. Let
\tilde{R}_{0}
(resp.
\tilde{R}_{1}
)
be the union of
R_{i}
’s such that
\chi_{i}\equiv r_{f}\pmod{4}

(resp.
\chi_{i}\equiv r_{f}+2\pmod{4}
).
Note that
S\setminus f(\Sigma(f))
is the disjoint
union
\tilde{R}_{0}\cup\tilde{R}_{1}
.

First suppose that
S
is a closed connected orientable surface.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\chi(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\chi_{i}\chi(R_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle r_{f}\chi(\tilde{R}_{0})+(r_{f}+2)\chi(\tilde{R}_{1})\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle r_{f}\chi(\tilde{R}_{0})+(r_{f}+2)(\chi(S)-\chi(\tilde{R}_{0})\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2\chi(\tilde{R}_{0})+(r_{f}+2)\chi(S)\pmod{4}.

As the closure of
\tilde{R}_{0}
is a compact orientable surface
with boundary
f(\Sigma(f))
, we see that
2\chi(\tilde{R}_{0})\equiv 2\#|\Sigma(f)|\pmod{4}
.
Since
\chi(M)
and
(r_{f}+2)\chi(S)\pmod{4}
are homotopy invariants of
f
,
we see that the parity of
\#|\Sigma(f)|
is a homotopy
invariant of
f
.

In the general case where

may not be closed,
for two homotopic image simple fold maps
f
and
g\colon M\to S
,
we consider an appropriate compact sub-surface
\bar{S}
of
S

that contains the image of the homotopy between
f
and
g
.
Let
\widehat{S}
be the closed orientable surface
obtained by attaching
2
-discs to the boundary circles of
\bar{S}
.
Then, for image simple fold maps
f
and
g\colon M\to\widehat{S}
,
the above argument implies that
\#|\Sigma(f)|\equiv\#|\Sigma(g)|\pmod{2}
.

This completes the proof.

S^{1}\tilde{\times}S^{2n-1}
into the open Möbius band

\mathcal{M}=S^{1}\tilde{\times}\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{w}
has a single fold component,
while
\tilde{z}
has two fold components, as follows.

The function
w
is the standard height function on the unit sphere
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
where
n\geq 1
, i.e.,
w
is the restriction of the last coordinate function
x_{2n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
to
S^{2n-1}
.
Let
\sigma\colon S^{2n-1}\to S^{2n-1}
be the orientation reversing involution defined by
the reflection with respect to the coordinate hyperplane
x_{2n}=0
. Note that

w\circ\sigma=-w
.

To define the function
z
, let us observe that
S^{2n-1}
can be obtained from the disjoint union
of two solid tori
S^{n-1}\times D^{n}
and
D^{n}\times S^{n-1}
by identifying their boundaries
via the identity map:

\partial(S^{n-1}\times D^{n})=S^{n-1}\times S^{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{id}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{n-1}\times S^{n-1}=\partial(D^{n}\times S^{n-1}).

Here, in order to get
S^{2n-1}
which is oriented, we need to orient

S^{n-1}\times D^{n}
and
D^{n}\times S^{n-1}
in such a way that the identity
map as above should be orientation reversing.

Since the solid torus
S^{n-1}\times D^{n}
is a union of a
0
-handle and an
(n-1)
-handle,
this description yields a handle decomposition of
S^{2n-1}
that consists of four handles of
indices
0
,
n-1
,
n
and
2n
respectively. In turn, such a handle decomposition gives
rise to an associated Morse function
z
on
S^{2n-1}
. There is an involution
\tau
on

S^{2n-1}
that swaps the two solid tori
S^{n-1}\times D^{n}
and
D^{n}\times S^{n-1}
.
By the above remark,
\tau
should be orientation reversing. Furthermore,
we can choose
z
so that
z\circ\tau=-z
.
Then, the rest of the argument is parallel to the above argument.

This proves Theorem
1.2
.

We can construct a generic homotopy between the image
simple fold maps
\tilde{w}

\tilde{z}\colon S^{1}\tilde{\times}S^{2n-1}\to\mathcal{M}
as follows. We first apply the birth move (see

\tilde{w}
in such a way that we have
a wrinkle consisting of
two cusps together with fold arcs of indices
n-1

n

\mathcal{M}

inside the region
R

\mathcal{M}\setminus\tilde{w}(\Sigma(\tilde{w}))

Note that if we reverse the normal direction, the indices turn to
n

n-1

Then, we merge the two cusps using a path connecting them in the

This is possible for

.
Then, we get an image simple fold map equivalent to
\tilde{z}

The singular point set of the homotopy thus constructed

and it has no triple self-intersections.

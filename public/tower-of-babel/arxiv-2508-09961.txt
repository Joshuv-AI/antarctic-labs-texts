The Sylow p p -subgroups of the finite simple groups
Hannah Knight

Abstract
In this short article, we give a summary of the Sylow p p -subgroups of the finite simple groups of classical Lie type.



In
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
, for
p\neq l
primes,
q=p^{r}
, we have found the Sylow
p
-subgroups and the Sylow
l
-subgroups of
PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
PSp(2n,q)
, and
P\Omega(2m,p^{r})
,
\Omega(2m,2^{r})
, and
\Omega(2m+1,p^{r})
as well as the Sylow
l
-subgroups of
PSU(n,p^{r})

p
-subgroups of

(

Let

Definition 0.2
.

Let
v_{l}(n)
denote the largest integer
i
such that
l^{i}\bigm|n
. Let
\zeta_{n}
denote a primitive
n
th root of unity.

Definition 0.3
.

Define
\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
to be the unitriangular
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
under multiplication. (Unitriangular matrices are upper triangular matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal).

Definition 0.4
.

The generalized quaternion groups are groups of order
4n
with the following presentation:

Q_{4n}=\langle w,v:w^{n}=v^{2},w^{2n}=1,vwv^{-1}=w^{-1}\rangle.

1
PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
p
be a primes,
n\geq 2
. Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
,

P\cong\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}).

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
p
be a prime,
q=p^{r}
,
l
a prime with
l\neq p
, and
s=v_{l}(q-1)
. Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{l}(PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
,

P\cong\{(\mathbf{b},\tau)\in(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n}/\{(x,\dots,x):x^{n}=1\}\rtimes P_{l}(S_{n}):\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=\text{sgn}(\tau)\},

where the action of
P_{l}(S_{n})
on
(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n}
is given by permuting the indices.
.

2
PSp(2n,p^{r})

Definition 2.1
.

For any prime
p
, define
\operatorname{Sym}(n,p^{r})
as the group of
n\times n
symmetric matrices under addition (with entries from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For any prime
p
,
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(PSp(2n,p^{r})
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\cong\{\begin{pmatrix}A&0_{n}\\
0_{n}&(A^{-1})^{T}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{n}&B\\
0_{n}&\text{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}:A\in\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}),B\in\operatorname{Sym}(n,p^{r})\}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Sym}(n,p^{r})\rtimes\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}),

where the action of
A\in\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
B\in\operatorname{Sym}(n,p^{r})
is given by
A(B)=ABA^{T}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime and
l
a prime with
l\neq 2,p
. Let
d
be the smallest positive integer such that
l\bigm|q^{d}-1
. If
d
is even then the Sylow
l
-subgroups of
PSp(2n,q)
are isomorphic to Sylow
l
-subgroups of
GL_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. And if
d
is odd, then the Sylow
l
-subroups of
PSp(2n,q)
are isomorphic to Sylow
l
-subgroups of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p
be a prime,
q=p^{r}
, and
l
a prime with
l\neq p
. Let
d
be the smallest positive integer such that
l\bigm|q^{d}-1
,
s=v_{l}(q^{d}-1)
, and
n_{0}=\lfloor\frac{n}{d}\rfloor.
Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{l}(GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
,

P\cong(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n_{0}}\rtimes P_{l}(S_{n_{0}}).

where the action of
P_{l}(S_{n_{0}})
on
\mathbf{b}\in(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n_{0}}
is given by permuting the
b_{i}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
p\neq 2
be prime. Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(PSp(2n,p^{r})

P\cong(Q_{2^{s}})^{n}/\langle(w^{2^{s-2}},\dots w^{2^{s-2}})\rangle\rtimes P_{2}(S_{n}).

where the action of
P_{2}(S_{n})
on
\mathbf{a}
is given by permuting the
a_{i}
.

3
P\Omega(2m,p^{r})
,
\Omega(2m,2^{r})
, and
\Omega(2m+1,p^{r})

Definition 3.1
.

For any prime
p
, define
\operatorname{Antisym}(m,p^{r})
as the group of
m\times m
anti-symmetric matrices under addition (with entries from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
).

Definition 3.2
.

For
p=2
, define
\operatorname{Antisym0}(m,2^{r})\subset\operatorname{Antisym}(m,2^{r})=\operatorname{Sym}(m,2^{r})
as the subgroup of symmetric/antisymmetric matrices with 0’s on the diagonal. That is,

\operatorname{Antisym0}(m,2^{r})=\{B\in\operatorname{Sym}(m,2^{r})=\operatorname{Antisym}(m,2^{r}):B_{i,i}=0,\text{ }\forall i\}.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p\neq 2
. Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(P\Omega^{\epsilon}(2m,p^{r}))
,

P\cong\operatorname{Antisym}(m,p^{r})\rtimes\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}),

where the action of
A\in\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
B\in\operatorname{Antisym}(m,p^{r})
is given by
A(B)=ABA^{T}.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(\Omega^{\epsilon}(2m,2^{r}))
,

P\cong\operatorname{Antisym0}(m,2^{r})\rtimes\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}),

where the action of
A\in\operatorname{Up}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
B\in\operatorname{Antisym0}(m,p^{r})
is given by

A(B)=ABA^{T}.

Note that
O^{\epsilon}(2m+1,2^{r})\cong Sp(2m,2^{r})
. So we only consider
p\neq 2
for the odd orthogonal groups.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p\neq 2
. Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(\Omega(2m+1,p^{r}))
,

P\cong\left((\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{+})^{m}\times\operatorname{Antisym}(m,p^{r})\right)\rtimes\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})),

where the action of
A\in\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
B\in\operatorname{Antisym}(m,p^{r})
is given by
A(B)=ABA^{T}.
and the action of
A\in\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{+})^{m}
is given by
A(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}A^{T}.

Definition 3.7
.

The dihedral groups are groups of order
2n
with the following presentation:

D_{2n}=\langle x,y:x^{n}=1=y^{2},yxy=x^{-1}\rangle.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(O^{+}(2m,q))
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}(D_{2^{s+1}})^{m}\rtimes P_{2}(S_{m}),&q\equiv 1\mod 4\\
&q\equiv 3\mod 4,\text{ }m\text{ even}\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times((D_{2^{s+1}})^{m-1}\rtimes P_{2}(S_{m-1})),&q\equiv 3\mod 4,\text{ }m\text{ odd}\\

And for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(O^{-}(2m,q))
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times((D_{2^{s+1}})^{m-1}\rtimes P_{2}(S_{m-1})),&q\equiv 1\mod 4\\

(D_{2^{s+1}})^{m}\rtimes P_{2}(S_{m}),&q\equiv 3\mod 4,\text{ }m\text{ odd}\end{cases}

.

For

,

for

P^{\prime}\in\begin{cases}\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(O^{+}(2m,q)),&q\equiv 1\mod 4\\
&q\equiv 3\mod 4,\text{ }m\text{ even}\\
\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(O^{-}(2m,q)),&q\equiv 3\mod 4,\text{ }m\text{ odd}\end{cases}.

4
PSU(n,p^{r})

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\operatorname{antisym*}(m,p^{2r})=\{B\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}}):B^{T}=-\overline{B}\}
under addition.

The kernel of the natural homomorphism
U(n,p^{2r})\to PSU(n,p^{2r})
has order prime to
p
, so it maps the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
U(n,p^{2r})
isomorphically onto Sylow
p
-subgroups of
PSU(n,p^{2r})
, so it suffices to consider the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
U(n,p^{2r})
. It is a straightforward calculation to check the following propositions.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
n=2m
, any
p
,
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(U(2m,p^{2r}))
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\cong\{\begin{pmatrix}A&0_{m}\\
0_{m}&(\overline{A^{-1}})^{T}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{m}&B\\
0_{m}&\text{Id}_{m}\end{pmatrix}:A\in\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}}),B\in\operatorname{antisym*}(m,p^{2r})\}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{antisym*}(m,p^{2r})\rtimes\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}}),

where the action of
A\in\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}})
on
B\in\operatorname{antisym*}(m,p^{2r})
is given by
A(B)=AB\overline{A}^{T}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

S=\{(\mathbf{y},B):\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}})^{m},B\in M_{m\times m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}}),B^{T}+\overline{B}=-\mathbf{y}^{T}\overline{\mathbf{y}}\},

where the multiplication is given by
(\mathbf{y},B)(\mathbf{y}^{\prime},B^{\prime})=(\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y^{\prime}},B+B^{\prime}-\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{T}\mathbf{y}^{\prime})
.

Then for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(U(2m+1,p^{2r}))
,

P\cong S\rtimes\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}}),

where the action of
\mathbf{A}\in\operatorname{Up}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2r}})
on
(\mathbf{y},B)\in S
is given by

A(\mathbf{y},B)=(\mathbf{y}\overline{A}^{T},AB\overline{A}^{T}).

Proposition 4.4

(
[
1
]
, Sections 9.4 and 9.3)
.

For
l\neq p
,
q=p^{2}
, the Sylow
l
-subgroups of
PSU(n,q^{2})
are isomorphic to Sylow
l
-subgroups of

\begin{cases}PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}),&l\bigm|n,l\bigm|q+1\\
SL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}),&l\nmid n,l\bigm|q+1\\
U(n,q^{2}),&l\nmid n,l\nmid q+1\end{cases}.

Proposition 4.5

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 9.7)
.

Let
l\neq p
be primes and
q=p^{r}
. Let
d
be the smallest positive integer such that
l\bigm|q^{d}-1
. Then Sylow
l
-subgroups of
U(n,q^{2})
are isomorphic to Sylow
l
-subgroups of

\begin{cases}GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}),&d=2\mod 4\\
GL_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}),&d\neq 2\mod 4\end{cases}.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
l\neq p
be primes and
q=p^{r}
. Let
d
be the smallest positive integer such that
l\bigm|q^{d}-1
,
s=v_{l}(q^{d}-1)
, and

P\cong(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n_{0}}\rtimes P_{l}(S_{n_{0}}).

Let

P\cong\{(\mathbf{b},\tau)\in(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n}\rtimes P_{l}(S_{n}):\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=\text{sgn}(\tau)\},

where the action of
P_{l}(S_{n})
on
\mathbf{b}\in(\mu_{l^{s}})^{n}

.

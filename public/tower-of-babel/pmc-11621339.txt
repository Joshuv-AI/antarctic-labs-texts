Checkpoint data-driven GCN-GRU vehicle trajectory and traffic flow prediction
Authors: Deyong Guan, Na Ren, Ke Wang, Qi Wang, Hualong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the development of information technology, massive traffic data-driven short-term traffic situation analysis of urban road networks has become a research hotspot in urban traffic management. Accurate vehicle trajectory and traffic flow prediction can provide technical support for vehicle path planning and road congestion warning. Unlike most studies that use GPS data to predict vehicle trajectories, this paper combines the broad coverage, high reliability, and lighter weight of traffic checkpoint data to propose a method that uses trajectory prediction technology to forecast the traffic flow in urban road networks accurately. The method adopts a checkpoint data-driven approach for data collection, combines graph convolutional neural network (GCN) and gated recurrent unit (GRU) models to more effectively learn and extract spatiotemporal correlation features of vehicle trajectories, which significantly improves the accuracy of vehicle trajectory prediction, and uses the output of the trajectory prediction model to forecast traffic flow more accurately. Firstly, transforming the checkpoint data into daily vehicle trajectories with time series characteristics, realizing the vehicle trajectory travel chain division. Secondly, the adjacency matrix is established by using the spatial relationship of each checkpoint, and the feature matrix of the vehicle’s driving trajectory over time is established, which is used as the input of GCN to learn the spatial characteristics of the vehicle while driving on the road network, and then GRU is added to further process the data after GCN training, constructing a GCN-GRU vehicle trajectory prediction model for vehicle trajectory prediction. Finally, the traffic flow of each checkpoint is calculated based on the prediction result of vehicle trajectory and compared with the real checkpoint flow. This paper conducts many experiments on the Qingdao City Shinan district checkpoint dataset. The results show that compared with the single models GCN, GRU, BiGRU, and BiLSTM, the GCN-GRU model has reduced the MAE by 0.75, 0.46, 0.52, and 0.57, and the RMSE by 0.76, 0.52, 0.58, and 0.68, respectively, demonstrating stronger spatial and temporal correlation characteristics and higher prediction accuracy. The MAPE between the forecasted flow and the real flow is 0.18, which verifies the reliability of the proposed method.Keywords: Vehicle trajectory prediction, Traffic flow forecast, Checkpoint data, Graph Convolutional Neural Networks, Gated Recurrent UnitsSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

Accurate vehicle trajectory prediction can provide important information for location-based services1, which is of great significance for enriching multiple application areas such as transportation planning2, traffic congestion warning, and management3,4. With the rapid development of modern communication technologies such as sensors, mobile positioning, and mobile Internet, etc., obtaining vehicle trajectory data is more convenient and accurate. These technologies can track and record a vehicle’s position, speed, driving route, and other information, providing data support for traffic management, intelligent navigation, traffic planning and other fields5–7.

There are many ways to acquire vehicle trajectory data, including Global Navigatsion Satellite System (GNSS)8, radar9, and high-definition checkpoint systems10. On urban roads, high-definition checkpoint systems are widely used, which store a huge amount of vehicle trajectory data, which can be used to analyze the road network OD, conduct statistical analysis of traffic flow, etc.11. GPS data mainly relies on the cell phone APP, and when the APP is not open, it may not be able to collect trajectory data. Whereas the collection range of the checkpoint system is much wider, and the data dimension of its data is much lower, which allows for experimental analysis without extracting many features. Checkpoint data enables in-depth analysis of traffic flow and road conditions12 and provides important support for traffic management and planning. The lightweight nature of the data facilitates analysis and application.

The checkpoint data collected from the high-definition checkpoint system can provide data support for collecting many private car trajectory data13. Researchers have conducted numerous studies using data from traffic checkpoints. Aiming at sparse trajectory recovery of checkpoint data, Li et al.14 proposed a spatial trajectory reconstruction algorithm for road network based on the Markov decision process. Zhan et al.15 proposed a model for estimating real-time lane queue length through mining checkpoint data, combined with a machine learning algorithm and traffic flow theory. Guan et al.16 proposed a deep learning model utilizing the Bi-LSTM self-attention mechanism to predict the next position of a vehicle based on the historical vehicle trajectory data collected by the checkpoint system. Huang et al.17 constructed a traffic network based on the traffic checkpoint context and used a bi-directional gated recurrent unit (Bi-GRU) for vehicle trajectory prediction.

Markov and traditional machine learning models were widely used in earlier vehicle trajectory prediction studies. Wang et al.18 proposed to utilize the Adaboost algorithm to generate different orders of fused Markov models for location prediction, which improves the prediction accuracy of the low-order Markov model while solving the high sparsity rate of the high-order Markov model. Du et al.19 proposed a Continuous Time Series Markov Model (CTS-MM) to predict the user’s location, which considers the sequence of the user’s location movement while incorporating time-dependent information. He et al.20 utilized historical information and massive data from GPS trajectory datasets to create a Markov model using a data-driven weight selection method for trajectory prediction of vehicles based on intersection trajectory data. Early Markov models have limited ability to utilize historical location sequence information, and most of them are based on location prediction of individual trajectories and lack comprehensive analysis of group trajectories, which has strong limitations. Traditional machine learning models, such as support vector machines, decision trees, etc., require the manual definition of suitable features by extracting useful information from individual historical trajectories as model inputs to achieve the next trajectory point prediction. Feng et al.21 used support vector machine regression to achieve vehicle lane-changing behavior and trajectory prediction.

With the rise and rapid development of deep learning22, many scholars have begun to use deep learning algorithms to study vehicle trajectory prediction. The change of vehicle trajectory is not only affected by temporal factors but also by the spatial structure of the road network. In terms of temporal features, Su et al.23 addressed the fact that traditional trajectory prediction models did not fully use trajectory contextual information and proposed using the LSTM and bi-LSTM models in deep learning for next-position prediction. Sun et al.24 used hybrid LSTM and sequential LSTM models to obtain the location and time series patterns and their dependencies in the trajectory data to predict the next location and travel time. The above methods only extracted the temporal features of traffic data sequences and ignored the spatial features. The GRU model used in this paper is simpler, faster, and more accurate than other time series models. For the spatial topology characteristics of the road network, Tang et al.25 proposed using diffusion convolutional neural networks to capture dynamic features from historical trajectory information to improve the accuracy of vehicle trajectory prediction. Fan et al.26 used context information for the next position prediction of vehicle trajectory by building a deep learning model with multiple convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and bi-directional long-short-term memory (bi-LSTM) networks. Yu et al.27 used deep convolutional neural networks to extract structural features of the road network and long short-term memory networks to obtain temporal dynamic change patterns. The above methods use CNN to model spatial relationships, which can only deal with Euclidean-structured data, and there are limitations in the application of CNN models for non-Euclidean-structured traffic data.

The spatiotemporal model combines spatial dependencies with temporal features to enhance the accurate capture of dynamic changes in complex environments. This approach not only adapts to short-term prediction needs but also captures long-term trends based on historical data, resulting in significantly improved prediction accuracy. Liu et al.28 established a GCN-LSTM next-position prediction model, which used GCN to obtain spatial features of historical trajectories and LSTM to obtain temporal dependencies in historical trajectories. The model has a high prediction accuracy when the prediction time is short, but the model structure is more complex. When the prediction time is extended, the prediction accuracy decreases significantly. Seng et al.29 introduced spatiotemporal correlation and global characteristics between different regions into the GCN model and realized traffic flow prediction in irregular regions by combining the GCN with the GRU model. However, this model assumes the urban expressway network as an undirected graph structure, which fails to express the topological structure of the prediction object effectively. This paper employs the GCN-GRU model for vehicle trajectory prediction. The GCN model extracts the topological characteristics of the road network and constructs a directed graph, utilizing the adjacency matrix to better reflect the true features of urban roads. The introduction of the GRU model further captures the temporal dependencies of vehicle trajectories. This GCN-GRU model integrates spatial and temporal features, significantly enhancing prediction accuracy.

Traffic flow prediction is important in modern Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) applications. Chen et al.30 designed a graph convolution model with an attention mechanism for traffic flow prediction, which can effectively capture the spatiotemporal and dynamic features of nodes. Wu et al.31 proposed a traffic flow prediction method based on a deep learning framework by extracting spatial features of traffic flow data using deep convolutional neural networks and employing recurrent neural networks to learn temporal features. Zhu et al.32 analyzed the traffic volume, density, and speed to establish an optimization model for predicting the traffic flow state. Chen et al.33 proposed a traffic flow prediction model based on a Dynamic Spatio-Temporal Graph Attention Network (DSTGAT) traffic flow prediction model to predict traffic flow. The above studies are based on existing traffic data and use machine learning, deep learning, and other methods to predict traffic flow.

Although some progress has been made in the above work, large-scale urban road network trajectory prediction still needs to be solved due to the complex topology. Deep learning methods are widely used34,35. They can be applied to traffic flow forecasting and vehicle trajectory prediction36. In this paper, we propose a GCN-GRU vehicle trajectory prediction model, using a graph convolutional neural network to improve the prediction model’s accuracy and the gated recurrent unit to optimize the running time. Through the study of vehicle trajectory prediction, the traffic flow of each checkpoint can be forecasted, which can predict the traffic condition in advance, enhance the road traffic safety37, and alleviate the traffic congestion problem38. Trajectory construction

To improve the accuracy of vehicle trajectory prediction, it is necessary to preprocess the raw checkpoint data. This paper mainly follows four criteria for data preprocessing: (1) Data from checkpoints that appear only once within the study area or from checkpoints outside the study area are removed. (2) Short trips lasting less than 1 minute or unreasonably long trips exceeding 12 hours are excluded from the analysis. (3) Due to imaging recognition by checkpoint devices, duplicate data entries may result from vehicles passing through sections. These duplicate data entries are removed, retaining only a single record. (4) The data utilization rate of this study is approximately 97.6%. For important fields (such as vehicle passing time, vehicle number, and checkpoint number) with missing values, missing records are typically deleted to ensure data integrity. The median imputation method is used for data completion for fields with a lower missing rate. Outliers are removed using the mean squared deviation test.

Graph convolutional neural network model (GCN)

In the spatial dimension, the road network has non-Euclidean spatial characteristics. Different from the traditional network model, GCN can deal with structured data with complex topological graph structure, and it can learn the representation of the nodes by propagating the information among the nodes of the graph to realize the deep mining of the data features and regularities39. This paper utilizes the GCN for the checkpoint data with spatial topological graph structure road network for spatial feature extraction, and its model structure is shown in Fig. 6. First, the spatial features for each road segment are extracted in sequence. After two convolution operations with ReLu and Sigmoid activation functions, obtain the matrix that integrates the spatial structure of the road network.

Graph Convolutional Neural Network structure diagram.

Graph convolutional neural networks operate in two main ways: spectral and vertex domains. The vertex domain, also known as the spatial domain, represents the space defined by the graph’s structure. This paper uses a graph convolutional network based on the spectral domain, which consists of the degree matrix D and the adjacency matrix A represented in the graph structure, and the eigenvalues of the matrix are computed by encoding the data of the graph structure in the form of a matrix.

Gated recurrent unit model (GRU)

In the temporal dimension, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) excel in handling sample data characterized by temporal sequences. However, gradient vanishing and explosion constrain their effectiveness in tasks involving long sequence data and modeling long-term dependencies. To ameliorate these problems, Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) and Long Short Memory (LSTM) units were introduced. Compared to the LSTM model, the GRU model fuses the forgetting gate and the input gate into a single update gate with fewer parameters, which reduces the time required to optimize the parameters of the model while maintaining the prediction accuracy and has a simpler structure and faster running speed. Considering the timeliness of traffic checkpoint data and the amount of vehicle trajectory sequence data tends to be large, to effectively obtain the time-varying features of vehicle trajectory data, this paper adopts the GRU network cell model to extract the temporal features, and its structure is shown in Fig. 7, the update gate ut is used to control how much of the previous sequence model’s output information ht-1 is retained in the moment t to the output result ht, Xt is the input information, Yt is the predicted information, the reset gate rt is used to control the amount of information from the previous moment that needs to be retained or forgotten, and ct is the reset data, which only retains the input information at the current moment.

Definition 3 (Adjacency Matrix A): The adjacency matrix A represents the spatial connectivity relationships among road checkpoints and can be formed into an n×n dimensional matrix. Experimental design

To verify the effectiveness of the model, this paper conducts model training and testing on true datasets, verifies the prediction effect of the model through the analysis of experimental results and model comparisons, and applies the experimental results to traffic flow forecasting to analyze the traffic flow changes at future roadway checkpoints.

Datasets

In this study, the dataset is the checkpoint data collected by the road network traffic checkpoint system in Shinan District, Qingdao City. The checkpoint system has wide coverage in the urban road network and can obtain real-time, accurate, high-quality traffic information. Evaluation index

To evaluate the performance of the prediction model, this paper validates the vehicle trajectory prediction model with mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE) and the traffic flow forecasting results with mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). In vehicle trajectory prediction, MAE is simple to compute and can effectively reflect the error between predicted and actual values. RMSE emphasizes larger errors more and can rigorously evaluate the errors in vehicle trajectory prediction. MAPE is suitable for traffic flow assessment as it provides relative error metrics, which better reflect the relative differences between forecasted and true traffic flows. The smaller the values of these three evaluation metrics, the smaller the error between the model’s prediction results and the true data, and the more accurate the prediction results. Eqs. Hyper-parameter tuning

The preprocessed dataset is divided into three subsets: 70% of the data is selected as the training set, 20% as the testing set, and 10% as the validation set. Based on the experimental results, it is best to use the historical trajectory data of the first 10 steps to predict the trajectory of the 11th step. When performing hyper-parameter tuning, to seek the best balance between memory efficiency and capacity, the batch size is set to 32 in this experiment, the Adam optimizer is selected, and the epoch is set to 64 times. To prevent model overfitting, this paper adopts the dropout method during model training. Specifically, the dropout rate is set to 0.2, meaning that 20% of the neurons will be randomly dropped in each training iteration. With the increase of training rounds, the number of iterations of updates in the model increases, gradually transforming from the initial unfitted state to the optimized fit state. The single GCN model lacks the support of the GRU layer, leading to issues with long prediction time series, which results in losing the influence of the real trajectory data on the prediction results, greatly reducing prediction accuracy. Due to the lack of consideration for the spatial characteristics of the road network structure, the prediction performance of the single GRU model remains inferior to that of the GCN-GRU model. Compared to the single BiGRU and BiLSTM models, the GCN-GRU model considers the dependencies between various nodes in the road network through a graph structure, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamic changes in traffic flow.

A paired t-test was used to verify the significant difference between the GCN-GRU and the other models. The results of the specific statistical tests are shown in Table 4. As shown in Table 4, p-values are less than 0.05, indicating a significant difference between these models. Based on these p-values, the null hypothesis can be rejected, i.e., the predictions of the combined GCN-GRU model are considered to be statistically significantly different from the other models.

Conclusion

This study selects the checkpoint data collected by a high-definition checkpoint system as the dataset source to solve traditional data collection equipment issues such as excessively high frequency and data redundancy. The high-definition checkpoint system has the advantages of extensive data coverage capability, real-time access to vehicle and personnel flow information, and accurate recording of vehicle number, type, speed, and license plate number, which helps to understand the traffic condition of urban road networks comprehensively. In this paper, a traffic flow forecasting method based on trajectory prediction is proposed by combining the checkpoint data, and it is proposed to predict vehicle trajectories using a combined GCN-GRU-based model. The main conclusions are as follows:

This paper uses the acquired checkpoint data to divide the complete travel trajectory of each vehicle and, according to the vehicle trajectory data, screens out the vehicles that meet the experimental requirements, which results in the average number of trajectories of the vehicle each month is 574 times, of which there are a total of 3,191 vehicles larger than 574 times and 3,191 vehicles are selected as experimental objects.

This paper uses the acquired checkpoint data to divide the complete travel trajectory of each vehicle and, according to the vehicle trajectory data, screens out the vehicles that meet the experimental requirements, which results in the average number of trajectories of the vehicle each month is 574 times, of which there are a total of 3,191 vehicles larger than 574 times and 3,191 vehicles are selected as experimental objects.

The spatiotemporal combined vehicle trajectory prediction model combining graph convolutional neural network (GCN) and gated recurrent unit (GRU) can effectively capture the spatiotemporal dependence of vehicle trajectories. The effectiveness of the GCN-GRU vehicle trajectory prediction model is verified using Qingdao traffic checkpoint data as the experimental dataset. Compared with the traditional GCN model, the MAE and RMSE of the GCN-GRU model are reduced by 0.75 and 0.76, respectively; compared with the single GRU model, the MAE and RMSE are reduced by 0.46 and 0.52, respectively; compared with the BiGRU model, the MAE and RMSE are reduced by 0.52 and 0.58, respectively; compared with the BiLSTM model, the MAE and RMSE are reduced by 0.57 and 0.68, respectively, and the model converges faster, demonstrating stronger spatiotemporal correlation characteristics and higher prediction accuracy. By comparing the real data, the predicted vehicle trajectory matches the true vehicle trajectory by more than 95%.

The results show that the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) between the real checkpoint flow data and the forecasted checkpoint flow data is 0.18, and the forecasted checkpoint traffic flow based on the predicted vehicle trajectories is consistent with the trend of the real traffic flow.

Although the GCN-GRU model has a high prediction match with real data, the model is mainly applicable to routine and stable vehicle trajectory prediction, and there are limitations in trajectory prediction for unexpected events and out-of-area vehicles. Specifically, the model may encounter difficulties when dealing with out-of-area vehicles that are not represented in historical data, resulting in potentially inaccurate predictions that need to be countered by combining other methods. Seamlessly integrating the GCN-GRU model with existing traffic management systems ensures that it can operate in real-time without affecting the stability and performance of the existing system, and by establishing a feedback loop between the model predictions and the traffic management system, the model’s performance and adaptability can be continuously improved. At the same time, it allows the model to be dynamically updated and adjusted based on real-time traffic data, thus improving prediction accuracy and the ability to respond to unexpected situations. In the future, we can consider combining the trajectory prediction model with multiple factors, such as weather conditions, road conditions, and special events, to enhance the model’s adaptability to the complex traffic environment and the accuracy of the prediction. Based on the GCN-GRU vehicle trajectory prediction model established in this paper, a real-time vehicle trajectory prediction and traffic congestion warning system can be developed to provide technical support for real-time traffic management and emergency response.

Lactylation-related lncRNAs can forecast prognosis and immune response of lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Le Zhou, Zhongjiang Lan, Bo Han, Yinjiao Yuan, Zhuowei Dong, Yijing Shi, Ruinian Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) represents a significant proportion of lung cancer cases, with immunotherapy serving as a cornerstone of treatment. Although immunotherapy has advanced significantly, the prognosis for patients in advanced stages remains suboptimal. Lactylation, a newly discovered metabolic pathway, has been recognized as a critical mechanism driving the initiation and development of many cancers, including LUAD. Using machine learning, this research aims to pinpoint lactylation-related lncRNAs (LRLs) that could serve as prognostic markers in LUAD, shedding light on tumor development and novel therapeutic avenues.ResultsFour LRLs (AC090115.1, AC092718.4, AC092718.6, and AL451064.1) were incorporated into a prognostic model. This model successfully categorized patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, revealing differences in prognosis. Patients in the low-risk group demonstrated a better prognosis, while the other category showed aggressive tumor characteristics and reduced responses to immunotherapy.ConclusionsThis study developed an association between LRLs and outcomes in lung adenocarcinoma, suggesting that they can be used as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target. The observed differences in their expression between tumor and normal tissues further underline their significance in LUAD pathogenesis. By advancing the understanding of LUAD biology, this predictive model provides a novel platform for precision medicine, paving the way for personalized therapeutic strategies and improved prognostic accuracy for LUAD patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04306-0.

Introduction

According to the 2022 Global Cancer Epidemiology data, lung cancer is still the largest cancer in incidence and mortality rates. Among males, it is the foremost cause of death, while in females, it ranks second [1, 2]. Based on pathological classifications, Lung cancer is divided into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Among these, LUAD, a subtype of NSCLC, represents the most frequently type, comprising roughly 35–40% of all cases [3]. Although LUAD exhibits slow growth, its cells possess a high metastatic potential, making early metastasis—especially through hematogenous spread—a common occurrence [4].

In recent years, immunotherapy, particularly therapies targeting immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has significantly improved outcomes for lung cancer patients [5–7]. However, prolonged administration of the same therapeutic agent often leads to drug resistance, necessitating the exploration of novel mechanisms underlying LUAD progression and the identification of alternative therapeutic targets to ensure more effective treatment options [8, 9].

Since its discovery in 2019, lactate-derived histone lysine lactylation has been identified as a groundbreaking epigenetic modification. Research has shown that it can activate gene transcription at the chromatin level, sparking considerable scientific interest [10]. Subsequent studies revealed its involvement in several biological processes, particularly in the regulation of glycolysis-associated cellular functions, macrophage polarization, neurodevelopment, and tumor proliferation [11–14]. This highlights the critical need to elucidate the functional roles of lactylation in LUAD.

Long non-coding RNAs are important members of the non-coding RNAs family and are involved in important biological processes such as chromatin remodeling, transcription, post-transcriptional modification, and signal transduction. With the continuous progress of biotechnology, many long non-coding RNAs have been found to play an important role in the development of cancer [15]. For instance, LINC01559 interacts with vimentin, preventing its ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, thereby enhancing LUAD cell migration, invasion, and metastasis [16]. Similarly, lncRNA CERS6-AS1 facilitates LUAD progression and drug resistance by sponging miR-424-5p to upregulate ANLN expression [17]. These findings emphasize the regulatory influence of lncRNAs on LUAD pathogenesis and progression, presenting promising avenues for therapeutic intervention.

In this research, we utilized LUAD data from TCGA database to establish a prognostic model based on LRLs to improve prognostic predictions for LUAD patients. We performed enrichment and immune profiles analysis of the two risk groups. Furthermore, we predicted the potential impact of anti-tumor drugs on these distinct risk groups. Overall, our findings underscore the regulatory significance of LRLs in LUAD development and progression, offering novel insights into precision medicine and clinical prognosis for LUAD patients.

Materials and methods

Data analysis

Lung adenocarcinoma data was retrieved from the TCGA database, which included 524 tumor samples and 58 normal tissue samples. After excluding incomplete data, 480 patients diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma were analyzed in this study. They were randomly divided into two equal groups (1:1 ratio): the training cohort consisting of 240 patients and the test cohort comprising another 240 patients. The data from the training cohort were utilized for identifying LRLs associated with prognosis and constructing a risk prediction model. The test cohort was subsequently utilized to assess the model’s accuracy. Lactylation-related genes were sourced from the Genecards database(https://www.genecards.org).

Development of a risk model for LncRNAs associated with lactylation

The number of 140 lactylation-related genes downloaded from Genecards. The relationship between lncRNA expression patterns and lactylation-related genes was analyzed by using Pearson correlation (P < 0.001, |R| > 0.4). Univariate Cox analysis and Lasso regression were employed to identify significant lncRNAs. In the LASSO regression analysis, the optimal penalty parameter (λ) was determined by 10-fold cross-validation. As shown in Fig. 1B, the minimum partial likelihood deviance was achieved at λ = 0.0025 (log(λ) ≈ − 6, lambda.min). To ensure model parsimony, we also considered the λ within 1 standard error of the minimum deviance (λ = 0.067, log(λ) ≈ − 2.7, lambda.1se).Further screening was performed by multivariate Cox analysis. A risk score was calculated for each sample, and they were divided into two groups through the median score.

Evaluation of the accuracy and independence of the risk model

Kaplan-Meier curve and ROC curve were used for survival analysis and prediction ability of the model. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is used to understand the characteristics of each sample, cluster the samples, and observe the correlations and differences between them. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were utilized to evaluate the reliability of the risk model as a prognostic predictor. In addition, nomograms were generated from clinical characteristics and risk scores, and calibration curves were used to evaluate the correctness of the prediction.

Functional enrichment analysis

Different expression genes (DEGs) were acknowledged by |log2FC| > 1.0, p < 0.05 (Table S2). Gene ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis examined signaling pathways. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed to analyze the expression of gene sets.

Analysis of tumor microenvironment, somatic mutation, and drug prediction

CIBERSORT algorithm evaluated the infiltration expression level. ssGSEA algorithm assess the immune function. Using “oncoPredict” R package to foresee the sensitivity of two risk populations to antitumor drugs. Tumor mutation burden (TMB) was measured by somatic mutation data in both groups.

Statistical analysis

Data was performed by using R software (version 4.3.1). The T-test is used to check differences. The log-rank test was used to assess the differences in survival. Univariate and multifactorial survival were used for Cox regression analysis. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Setting of a prognostic model based on lactylation-related LncRNAs

The association between lncRNAs and mRNAs was assessed through Pearson’s correlation analysis, identifying 116 relevant lncRNAs (Table S1). A co-expression network was established to link 45 lactylation genes with these 116 lncRNAs(Fig. 2A). Subsequently, six prognostics correlated lncRNAs were obtained by LASSO regression analysis and univariate Cox regression (Fig. 2B–D). Further refinement through multivariate Cox regression identified four lncRNAs—AC090115.1, AC092718.4, AC092718.6, and AL451064.1—that were used to construct the model. To visualize the expression patterns of these four key lncRNAs, a heatmap was generated, showcasing their expression profiles across the cohort (Fig. 2E). These lncRNAs were deemed critical in influencing the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma.

Establishment and validation of the model

The heat map describes the distribution of four lncRNAs in the sample. Risk scores and survival status of samples are also described. The patients were arranged along a horizontal axis from low to high risk, with their corresponding risk scores plotted on the vertical axis(Fig. 3A–C). Survival status analysis demonstrated that the high-risk group experienced elevated mortality rates and reduced survival durations (represented by yellow circles), whereas the low-risk group showed the opposite trend (depicted by blue circles). Interestingly, AL451064.1 and AC090115.1 exhibited significantly higher expression in the low-risk group, indicating their potential roles as protective factors. Conversely, AC092718.6 and AC092718.4 increased in the high-risk group, signifying they may serve as risk factors in LUAD. Next, we revealed significantly better overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS )in the low-risk group across the training, test, and combined datasets(Fig. 3D–G) (p < 0.001).

Validation using clinical characteristics

We sought to further assess the model’s predictive capability by examining several clinical variables, including age, gender, and TNM staging. The K-M survival curve showed that for age ≤ 65 years, age > 65 years, sex, stage I-II, and stage III-IV in the low-risk group had better OS (Fig. 4A–F). Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was utilized to investigate whether this model could distinguish between patients with high and low risk based on the identified LRL. And we indicated that the model was indeed effective in segregating the two risk groups(Fig. 5A–D). This division suggests a variable sensitivity to lactylation within the two risk groups, further supporting the prognostic value of the identified LRLs.

Independent prognostic analysis and nomogram

As mentioned above, this prognostic model can well predict several clinical features such as gender, age and stage, so we want to evaluate the relationship between these clinical factors and prognosis. Therefore, univariate and multifactor Cox regression analyses were conducted, and the results of univariate Cox regression analysis showed that the differences in age, stage and risk score were statistically significant and correlated with OS (p < 0.05) (Fig. 6A). Multivariate Cox regression analysis showed that risk scores (p < 0.001) independently predicted OS in patients with lung adenocarcinoma (Fig. 6B). These results suggest that a risk model based on four LRLs is an independent prognostic factor for lung adenocarcinoma.

ROC curves and AUC values were used to evaluate the model’s effectiveness in predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year OS (Fig. 6D). The AUC achieved by the risk score was 0.657 (Fig. 6C), and the predictive power was higher than that of clinical factors. We then combined risk scores and clinical parameters to generate calibration graphs to estimate OS. The 1 -, 3 - and 5-year survival rates were 90.2%, 60.7% and 40.2%, respectively (Fig. 6F). The calibration curve supports the accuracy of these predictions (Fig. 6E). These findings confirm that the LRL-based risk model is a reliable predictor of survival outcomes in patients with LUAD, independent of other clinical factors.

Functional enrichment analysis

To investigate the biological functions of differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we conducted functional enrichment analysis. Biological Process (BP): The DEGs were primarily involved in processes such as ciliary movement, actinin complex assembly, gene filament assembly, and epithelial ciliary movement associated with extracellular fluid movement and granulocyte chemotaxis. Cellular Component (CC): These genes were enriched in cellular structures such as the outer motor arm, gene filament, cytoplasm of ciliated cells, axion complex, kinetoprotein complex, motile cilia, plasma membrane-bound cell projection, and the 9 + 2 active cilia, as well as various cytoplasmic regions. Molecular Function (MF): Enriched pathways included alcohol dehydrogenase [NAD(P)+] activity, RAGE receptor binding, estradiol 17-β dehydrogenase [NAD(P)] activity and so on (Fig. 7A, B).

KEGG showed that pathways like the IL-17 signaling pathway, pancreatic secretion, retinol metabolism, linolenic acid metabolism, motor protein activity, folate biosynthesis, cytochrome P450 metabolism of exogenous drugs, galactose metabolism, and others, including Wnt signaling and ABC transporter-related pathways (Fig. 7C).

Immune infiltration landscape and immunotherapy efficacy

Immune cells show an important function in tumor, and their infiltration into the tumor microenvironment was assessed using several methods. We analyzed the proportion of 22 immune cell types infiltrating tumors by CIBERSORT algorithm and found significant differences (Fig. 8A). As shown in Fig. 8B, the distribution of immune cells varied, with CD4 memory T cells, Monocytes, Dendritic cells, M2 macrophages, and Mast cells being more abundant in the low-risk group. CD8+T cells, Tregs, and M0 macrophages were more prevalent in the high-risk group.

TMB analysis

Somatic mutation data were used to analyze the relationship between TMB and risk score. The top 20 mutated genes and their mutation types are shown in Fig. 9A, B. Additionally, tumor mutational burden (TMB) was different in both groups (p < 0.05) (Fig. 9C). To investigate the prognostic impact of TMB, patients were classified into high and low TMB groups based on the median TMB score. Fig. 9D indicated that patients in the low-risk group had better survival rates. The high-risk, low-TMB group demonstrated the shortest survival and the poorest prognosis, whereas the low-risk, high-TMB group exhibited the most favorable survival outcomes. These results emphasize the utility of risk scores as accurate predictors of immunotherapy outcomes in LUAD patients.

Drug response prediction

Next, we sought to identify potential drugs for precision therapy. The oncoPredict R package was employed to predict patient responses to various anti-cancer agents based on biomarkers. We identified that patients in the low-risk group exhibited lower IC50 values for Axitinib, Ribociclib, and Tozasertib, indicating better sensitivity to these drugs. Conversely, other drugs showed higher IC50 values in the low-risk group, indicating greater efficacy for patients in the high-risk group (Fig. 10A–I). This differential drug response highlights the potential for precision medicine tailored to specific risk profiles in lung adenocarcinoma.

Discussion

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most prevalent histological subtype of lung cancer, especially among females, where it exhibits a higher incidence rate [1]. Many patients are diagnosed with LUAD only after symptoms become apparent, typically through imaging or pathological examinations. This cancer is prone to metastasis, commonly spreading to critical organs such as the brain, bones, and liver [18–20]. A primary problem in LUAD management is short of reliable biomarkers for early detection, contributing to suboptimal prognostic outcomes for affected patients. Current treatment approaches for LUAD include chemotherapy, targeted therapies, and immunotherapy. Among these, immune checkpoint inhibitors like pembrolizumab, camrelizumab, and nivolumab have emerged as first-line therapeutic options [21]. However, prolonged use of these therapies may lead to drug resistance, which is often attributed to immune escape mechanisms facilitated by the tumor microenvironment, allowing cancer cells to evade therapeutic effects [22–24] .Given these challenges, we employed bioinformatics tools to identify prognostic biomarkers for LUAD, aiming to improve early diagnosis and support the development of personalized immunotherapy strategies.

In this study, we developed and validated a novel prognostic model for lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) using four lncRNA-related lactate metabolites (LRLs): AC090115.1, AC092718.4, AC092718.6, and AL451064.1. The model’s reliability was evaluated through ROC curves, calibration curves, and Cox regression analysis, demonstrating stable performance and independent predictive capacity. The model effectively classified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups with distinct overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) outcomes, while also showing correlations with specific immune infiltration patterns, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and differential responses to immunotherapy and drug treatment. This represents one of the first systematic studies integrating lactate-mediated epigenetic regulation with lncRNA biology to construct a LUAD prognostic model.

The emerging concept of lactylation has attracted considerable attention in cancer research, underscoring its multifaceted roles in tumor biology. Metabolites generated during lactate metabolism contribute to tumor growth, proliferation, metastasis, therapeutic resistance, and immune suppression [25–27]. For example, lactate-driven acidification of the tumor microenvironment (TME) enhances protein expression and thereby strengthens tumor resistance to therapy [28, 29]. Moreover, lactate profoundly influences macrophage polarization through histone deacetylation, highlighting its role in epigenetic regulation [30, 31]. Thus, lactic acidification has become a critical focus for elucidating tumor development and progression. To address this, we constructed a prognostic model based on lncRNA-related lactic acidification modifications. This model identified AC092718.4 and AC092718.6 as highly expressed in high-risk patients. Previous studies have shown that AC092718.4 knockdown suppresses LUAD cell growth and induces apoptosis [32]. Similarly, knockout of SRA lncRNA disrupts phosphorylation of lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) at Tyr10, reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, suppresses lactate secretion, and promotes apoptosis [33]. In addition, HITT, a lncRNA that functions as a transcriptional inhibitor of HIF-1α, has been reported to limit lactate generation through a PKM2-dependent mechanism [34]. Collectively, these findings suggest that these lactate-related lncRNAs (LRLs) may foster a lactate-enriched TME by enhancing glycolytic flux or impairing lactate clearance. In contrast, protective LRLs such as AC090115.1 and AL451064.1, which are enriched in the low-risk group, may inhibit these pathways, although their precise molecular functions require further investigation. Another important observation is the strong association between high-risk features and immunosuppressive TMEs. Elevated lactate promotes histone lactylation in macrophages, driving their polarization toward an M2 phenotype that dampens anti-tumor immunity while supporting angiogenesis and tissue remodeling [29–31]. Lactate also disrupts monocyte differentiation and antigen presentation through epigenetic reprogramming, impairs cytotoxic functions of CD8 + T cells and NK cells, and facilitates recruitment of immunosuppressive cells—including M2 macrophages, N2 tumor-associated neutrophils, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), and regulatory T cells (Tregs)—into acidic TMEs, thereby promoting immune evasion [35, 36]. Furthermore, dendritic cell polarization toward an inhibitory phenotype in lactate-acidified TMEs directly compromises NK and CD8 + T cell activity, while simultaneously upregulating immune checkpoints such as PD-L1 [37]. This immunosuppressive landscape may underlie the poor response to immunotherapy observed in high-risk patients, suggesting that targeting lactate metabolism or LRL-mediated signaling could help reverse immune resistance and improve prognosis in this subgroup.

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) analysis further revealed that high-risk patients harbor a greater mutational load, largely driven by TP53 alterations [38]. While TP53 loss-of-function mutations impair DNA damage repair, promote genomic instability, and facilitate immune escape, the coexistence of high TMB and immunosuppressive TMEs creates a paradox in high-risk patients [39, 40]. Although elevated TMB is generally predictive of improved responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), profound immunosuppression may negate this benefit. These findings highlight the need for integrated biomarkers that combine genetic and microenvironmental features to better predict immunotherapy efficacy.

From a therapeutic standpoint, our risk model also holds promise for guiding treatment selection. Patients in the low-risk group exhibited greater sensitivity to agents such as Ribociclib, Axitinib, and Tozasertib. This difference may reflect the underlying metabolic state of the tumor; indeed, lactate accumulation can activate pro-survival pathways, including HIF-1α and autophagy, thereby inducing resistance to diverse therapies [41, 42]. Consequently, interventions targeting lactate production or signaling could potentially synergize with conventional and targeted treatments to overcome therapy resistance.

Of course, there are some limitations to this study. First, both the training and validation sets were derived solely from the TCGA-LUAD cohort, without external validation from GEO, ICGC, or clinical samples. This restricts the generalizability of our findings. Second, although we attempted to control statistical errors, multiple comparisons may still result in false positives. Third, the observed imbalance in M stage distribution between training and test sets may affect model stability. Finally, this work is entirely computational and lacks experimental validation. Future investigations should determine whether these LRLs directly modulate lactate production or accumulation using both in vitro and in vivo models, and assess their impact on macrophage polarization, Treg recruitment, and T-cell functionality. Furthermore, analyzing LRLs expression and lactate levels in clinical cohorts before and after immunotherapy could provide critical validation of their utility as predictive biomarkers.

Conclusion

In a word, we found four key LRLs, which can predict the prognosis of LUAD. The model provides a new approach for personalized treatment strategies and a potential target for future precision tumor therapy. By improving the accuracy of cancer treatment, the model could improve patient survival while reducing the social and family burden associated with lung adenocarcinoma. However, external validation and in-depth biological experiments are required to confirm the clinical applicability and mechanistic basis of our model.

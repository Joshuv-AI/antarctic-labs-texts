Analysis of the Association between In Vitro Fertilization/Assisted Conception and the Development of Retinopathy of Prematurity in Very-Low-Birth Weight Newborns
Authors: Jose Uberos, Elisabeth Fernández-Marin, Ana Campos-Martínez, Aida Ruiz-López, Jose Luis García-Serrano
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe objective is to study to what extent the development of retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is associated with assisted conception (AC) and in vitro fertilization (IVF) in a tertiary referral hospital.Materials and MethodsThis study is a retrospective observational cohort study. Very-low-birth weight (VLBW) infants with gestational age (GA) <32 weeks or birth weight <1500 g were admitted to the neonatal unit of a tertiary care hospital between January 1, 2011, and December 31, 2021. The study determined the degree of ROP developed according to the type of fertilization used for conception. Main outcome measures were ROP prevalence in pregnancies obtained after IVF/intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) in the period from January 1, 2011, to December 31, 2021, in a tertiary hospital.ResultsOf the 408 infants included in the study sample, 105 (25.7%) were born following AC and 12.4% of these developed ROP. In our sample, the practice of IVF was not associated with an increase in the incidence of VLBW infants (R 2 = 0.12; P = .29).ConclusionConception achieved via AC or IVF is not significantly associated with ROP in any degree of severity.

What is already known on this topic?

Assisted conception (AC) has been linked to an increase in prematurity and related comorbidities such as retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), although some recent studies seem to indicate that these AC-related complications could be more related to the delay in the age of conception than to AC itself.

Introduction

In recent decades, the average age of first-time mothers has gradually risen. This progressive postponement of motherhood, which has been associated with women’s participation in the labor market, is also related to women’s reproductive health and is considered the main factor underlying the current increase in the use of assisted conception (AC).1 Assisted conception techniques for infertility include intrauterine insemination (IUI), in vitro fertilization (IVF), and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). Of these, IVF has been most commonly employed since its introduction in 1978, and more than 8 million children worldwide (2 million in Europe) have been conceived by this method.2 However, IVF has been associated with premature births, low birth weight, and multiple gestation.3 Furthermore, many studies have indicated that the use of AC could be related to obstetric and perinatal complications, increasing the risk of preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, preterm birth, low birth weight, and death.4,5 On the other hand, conflicting results have been reported concerning the obstetric and perinatal effects of AC.6,7

In addition to these concerns, it has been suggested that the use of AC or IVF may be a risk factor for retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) in newborn preterm infants.8 The evolution of ROP is conditioned by an interaction of risk factors, including birth weight, gestational age, and systemic ones such as sepsis, anemia, and multiple blood transfusions.9,10 Poor postnatal growth in the first weeks of life is also associated with the development of ROP.11

The aim of this study is to investigate to what extent the development of ROP is associated with AC and IVF in a tertiary referral hospital.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

Retrospective observational study of newborns with gestational age (GA) <32 weeks or birth weight <1500 g admitted to the Intensive Care Unit (NICU) of a tertiary hospital in the period from January 1, 2011, to December 31, 2021. The degree of ROP of the newborns and remaining neonatal comorbidities is determined, and whether conception was achieved by AC or IVF is recorded. The maternal and perinatal variables listed in Table 1 are taken as adjustment variables.

Ethical Approval

In all cases, consent was requested from parents or guardians. This study was conducted in accordance with the guidelines set out in the Declaration of Helsinki and was authorized by the Provincial Bioethics Committee of Granada (Spain) (approval no: 80ed2fa1b452eca15f1715306dd309110af92a95, date: 14-06-2020). Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects.

Assessment of Retinopathy of Prematurity

The aim of the retinopathy program was to identify premature babies in need of treatment and to facilitate short- and long-term follow-up.12,13 It has been suggested that initial screening be based on the postmenstrual age of the infant. The onset of ROP is better related to chronological age than to GA. Therefore, the ROP screening program begins screening premature infants with a birth weight ≤1500 g or GA ≤30 weeks from 4 weeks of chronological age. Premature infants with a birth weight of 1500-2000 g or GA >30 weeks with poor clinical picture who were considered to be at high risk of developing ROP were also screened.14

Assisted Reproduction Techniques

The types of AC used were IVF (including IVF without micromanipulation and the new technique of ICSI), IUI, and the use of clomiphene as an ovulation inducer. The term IVF refers to techniques with and without micromanipulation.

Neonatal Morbidity

In accordance with the thresholds proposed by the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development (NICHD) and by Jobe and Bancalari,15,16 bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) is defined as a need for supplemental oxygen >21% at 28 days of life and/or a need for supplemental oxygen >21% or for positive airway pressure at 36 weeks’ corrected GA, similar to that reported in previous works.17

Chorioamnionitis is an acute inflammation in pregnant women with a history of foul-smelling amniotic fluid, fever, and increased acute phase reactants.18 The history of late or nosocomial sepsis and the history of early sepsis were recorded.19,20 Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) is diagnosed by Doppler ultrasound. The diagnosis of intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) is based on the Papile classification and was determined by brain ultrasound.21 Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in the study patients was classified according to Bell’s criteria.22

Statistical Analysis of the Results

A preliminary analysis of normality is performed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Medians and interquartile ranges were determined for continuous values and frequency distributions for categorical variables. Since the variances of the continuous variables between comparison groups were not homogeneous, nonparametric comparisons were used for univariate comparisons of continuous variables, and the chi-square test for categorical variables. The existence of associations between variables was determined using multinomial logistic regression. The regression models are adjusted for the variables that show differences in the comparative study between groups (Tables 1 and 3) with P < .2. The significance level was set at P < .05. All statistical analyses were carried out using the IBM SPSS® 20.0 for Windows package (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Between January 2011 and December 2021, 26 589 newborns were born at our hospital. Of these, 451 (1.69%) were <32 weeks’ GA or weighed <1500 g at birth (Figure 1). In our series, 25.7% of these VLBW infants had been born following the use of an assisted reproduction technique, and among this population, 12.4% developed some degree of ROP, and 6 newborns (5.7%) developed severe ROP requiring laser treatment. Among pregnant women who did not use assisted reproduction techniques, 24 children (7.9%) developed severe ROP and required laser treatment. The subsequent evolution of all treated patients was satisfactory. Figure 2 shows the percentage of VLBW infants admitted during the study period and of those for whom in vitro fertilization was performed.

Assisted Conception and Retinopathy of Prematurity

Of the 408 newborns included in our study cohort, 105 were born after the use of AC. Among the latter group, the percentage of VLBW babies born at the limit of viability was significantly lower than in the non-AC newborns (12.3% vs. 21.1%; P = .048). Moreover, these 105 newborns presented a higher proportion of cesarean sections (82.5% vs. 69.6%; P = .001) and multiple pregnancies (76.2% vs. 27.4%; P = 0.001) (Table 1). As shown in Table 2, the AC interventions consisted of IUI (12 cases), ovulation-inducing treatments (8 cases), and IVF/ICSI (85 cases). Of the 85 pregnancies obtained after IVF/ICSI, stage 1 ROP was observed in 4 cases, stage 2 in 3 cases, and stage 3 in 6 cases. In the remaining 72, ROP was not observed. No significant differences were recorded between the groups with or without ROP, or by type of AC used (ꭓ 2 = 3.29; P = .95).

In Vitro Fertilization and Retinopathy of Prematurity

In our sample, the use of IVF did not produce an increase in the proportion of births with VLBW (R 2 = 0.12; P = .29). Of the VLBW infants, 43 died and 408 survived and met the criteria for the ROP protocol to be applied. Among this cohort of newborns, the mean GA was 29.3 (SD 2.6) weeks and the mean birth weight was 1223 (SD 350) g. In total, 60 (14.7%) developed some stage of ROP. These had a gestational age of 27.2 (SD 2.2) weeks and a birth weight of 948.1 (SD 282) g. Both of these values were significantly lower than those presented by the newborns without ROP (P < .001).

As shown in Table 3, comparison of the cohorts obtained after IVF/ICSI and after natural fertilization revealed no significant differences in most of the obstetric variables considered, with the exception of a higher percentage of multiple pregnancies and deliveries by cesarean section among the newborns conceived with AC. Retinopathy of prematurity was observed in 16.6% of the multiple pregnancies compared to 18.5% in the single pregnancies (ꭓ 2 = 0.14; P = .707).

The prevalence of ROP and other neonatal comorbidities among the VLBW infants was similar among the pregnancies achieved with or without the use of IVF/ICSI after neonatal fertilization. No significant association was observed between the stage of ROP and the use of AC vs. IVF (Table 4).

Discussion

Our analysis did not reveal a higher prevalence of ROP or other neonatal comorbidities whether conception was achieved by AC or IVF. Neither were any significant differences observed between the 2 methods with respect to the rate of VLBW.

McKibbin et al23 who first analyzed the prevalence of ROP in newborns after AC, reported that ROP of any stage was present in 23% of the infants born following the application of this technique. In our series, 12.4% of the VLBW infants born following AC developed some degree of ROP. These findings are similar to those observed in previous studies of pregnancies after unassisted fertilization and to those reported by other authors in this context.24

Some studies have reported AC-related increases in ocular comorbidities other than ROP, such as Coats disease, congenital cataracts, congenital glaucoma, coloboma, microphthalmos, and retinoblastoma.25 Watts et al26 observed that of all newborns who required treatment for ROP, 28.6% were born after AC and 83.3% of the latter were conceived via IVF. These authors concluded that IVF is the main risk factor for the development of threshold ROP and recommend greater vigilance in the detection of ROP in VLBW newborns conceived using IVF methods. These findings differ from ours, as we observed no association between AC in general or IVF in particular with any stage of ROP, which is in line with Davir et al27 according to whom IVF does not independently increase the risk of ROP.

Friling et al28 report that assisted conception per se does not appear to be a risk factor for ROP. These authors observed that singleton newborns with a birth weight ≤1500 g were more likely to develop stage 2 or 3 ROP than twins or triplets. These authors conclude that gestational age and birth weight are the most important factors associated with ROP. In our sample, the percentage of multiple pregnancies was significantly higher when AC was involved. This association, which is implicit in IVF protocols and with the concurrent implantation of several embryos, could be related to a greater prevalence of ROP after IVF, as reported by Watts et al.26 In twin births, although the newborns have the same gestational age, they may present differing courses of ROP, depending on prenatal factors such as intrauterine growth restriction29,30 or postnatal factors such as differences in nutrition, blood transfusions, or oxygen therapy.10 In some studies, discordant twins (defined as a difference of 15% or more in the birth weight of the twins) may have an increased risk of ROP due to factors related to their unequal growth (Fellows et al., 1995).31 These factors associated with ROP and IVF could be confounding and should be taken into account. However, in this regard, our findings do not seem conclusive, and neither our own nor other studies have recorded significant differences in the presence or severity of ROP between premature newborns from singleton pregnancies and those from multiple pregnancies.32,33

Various studies have associated the use of AC with a higher prevalence of manifestations of obstetric morbidity, such as pregnancy-induced hypertension, preeclampsia, gestational diabetes mellitus, premature birth and low birth weight, higher perinatal mortality, respiratory disorders, and cerebral palsy.4,34 A meta-analysis by Gao et al35 concludes that IVF use was associated with an increased risk of ROP development and that AC is more likely to cause severe ROP and ROP in singleton fetuses. Nevertheless, our analysis did not show VLBW to be related to IVF, and the obstetric and perinatal morbidities recorded, including ROP, were similar to those observed in VLBW infants born after natural fertilization.

Our study has several important limitations, derived mainly from its retrospective nature. We think that our findings can contribute to future meta-analysis studies by adding more evidence. Although we have included most factors related to ROP in the analysis, we are aware that there may be unmeasured or unadjusted confounders that could bias our findings.

Conclusion

Assisted conception has been linked to an increase in prematurity and related comorbidities such as ROP, although some recent studies seem to indicate that these AC-related complications could be more related to the delay in the age of conception than to AC itself.

From the study results obtained, we conclude that conception achieved via AC or IVF is not significantly associated with a greater possibility of developing ROP, in any of its degrees of severity.

Prediction of intraoperative acquired pressure injury in elderly cardiac surgery patients via machine learning: model development with the MIMIC-IV and external validation
Authors: Yi Cheng, Leqi Li, Jiang Zhang, Jifang Zhou, Lihai Chen, Hongwei Shi, Yun Wang, Wei He, Fan Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntraoperative acquired pressure injury (IAPI) represents a major risk in elderly cardiac surgery patients due to skin fragility and impaired perfusion. Reliable risk prediction is essential for prevention and personalized care.MethodsUsing data from 12,222 patients aged ≥60 years in the MIMIC-IV database, we developed machine learning models with 24 clinically relevant features. Seven classifiers were trained and integrated via a stacking ensemble. Internal evaluation employed stratified fivefold cross-validation with 10 repetitions and bootstrapped confidence intervals (2,000 resamples). Multiple imputations (m = 2000) quantified uncertainty from missing data. External validation was performed in 355 patients from Nanjing First Hospital, with resampling to address class imbalance. Calibration metrics and SHAP analysis with bootstrapping were used to assess reliability and interpretability.ResultsThe ensemble model showed strong discrimination in both internal (AUC = 0.857) and external (AUC = 0.783) validation, with stable performance across imputations (mean AUC: 0.997 ± 0.004). Calibration analysis confirmed good agreement between predicted and observed risks. Key predictors as atrial fibrillation, lymphocyte count, and operative time were clinically plausible and robustly identified.ConclusionsThis study presents an externally validated and interpretable ensemble learning framework for early IAPI risk prediction in elderly cardiac surgery patients. Incorporating multiple imputation, bootstrapped calibration, and SHAP-based robustness, this approach improves methodological transparency and offers a clinically actionable tool for perioperative risk stratification and prevention.Clinical trial numberNCT05855954. Registration date: March 21, 2023. Registered on ClinicalTrials.gov.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12911-025-03325-9.

Introduction

Intraoperative acquired pressure injury (IAPI) occurs during surgery due to prolonged pressure, friction, or shear forces, leading to compromised blood circulation, tissue ischemia, and subsequent reperfusion injury. Geriatric people are especially susceptible to IAPI due to delicate skin, inadequate circulation, prevalent chronic ailments, malnutrition, and extended surgical placement. The aging process diminishes their capacity to withstand pressure, increasing the risk of injury during and after surgery [1, 2]. Most occurrences present one to three days postsurgery; however, they may occur up to six days later. The global incidence of surgical pressure injuries ranges from 0.3% to 57.4%, with cardiovascular surgeries showing rates of up to 29.1%. Timely identification of risk factors is essential for an effective response. Geriatric patients face an increased risk due to age-associated skin vulnerability, reduced perfusion, and the presence of several comorbidities.

Risk prediction models for IAPI amalgamate clinical data and utilize approaches including multiple regression and machine learning, with effectiveness evaluated by discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Nonetheless, most models rely on a single ideal approach, which may insufficiently address clinical complexities, especially in elderly populations.

Recent research depicts IAPI as a complex issue. Shi et al. utilized decision trees to identify preoperative Braden scores, surgical position, duration, age, prealbumin levels, and BMI as relevant variables. Tang et al. conducted potential research employing logistic regression to identify preoperative hyperglycemia, emergency surgery, vasoactive medications, and duration of operation as important risk factors [3–5]

We conducted a quantitative analysis of pressure injuries incurred during cardiovascular surgery via sample data from the MIMIC-IV database. The study focused on patient demographics, laboratory indicators, hemodynamic metrics, and preoperative comorbidities. We developed 7 predictive models for intraoperative acquired pressure injuries, which were later incorporated into an ensemble model. The model’s effectiveness was evaluated via five metrics. Additionally, dataset partitioning algorithms were employed on a recognized healthcare system to identify common indicators among cardiovascular surgery patients, hence improving the model’s generalizability. This method, especially when used in elderly patients, functions as a crucial tool for early identification and risk mitigation in high-risk populations.

Methods

Study population

Institutional review board (IRB) approval from Nanjing First Hospital Institution was obtained in August 2022 (KY20220805-04), and the trial was registered on clinicaltrials.gov (NCT05855954) in May 2023. External validation was performed via the retrospective Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) cohort, which includes data from various critical care units at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center between 2020 and 2022. Both the primary outcomes and subgroup analyses, as well as the statistical analysis plans, were finalized prior to accessing the data.

Selection criteria

The inclusion criteria for the MIMIC-IV internal validation database were established to guarantee that only patients who genuinely underwent cardiovascular surgery were considered. This was predicated on ICD codes, with ICD version 9 encompassing codes that begin with the digits ‘35’, ‘36’, or ‘37’, and ICD version 10 comprising codes that commence with the digits ‘02’. Clinical data for patients who underwent cardiovascular surgery were obtained via the concept table in R and Navicat Premium (17) [6]. The explicit inclusion criteria were: a) patients aged over 60 years; b) an ICU stay exceeding 24 hours; c) only the initial ICU admission was considered for patients with multiple admissions; d) patients must have undergone cardiovascular surgery within 24 hours preceding their first ICU admission (to mitigate interference from other surgical procedures).

The inclusion criteria for the external validation dataset comprised patients aged 60 years or older who underwent cardiovascular procedures, including valve repair or replacement, coronary artery bypass grafting, or aortic surgery. These operations necessitated general anesthesia and lasted a minimum of 2 hours. Patients were excluded if they had pre-existing pressure ulcers, skin problems that could interfere with observations, were unconscious, had mental disorders, or were hesitant to give informed consent.

Data collection

The final feature set comprised 24 indicators shared across both databases, encompassing four key domains: (1) demographics (etc, age, sex, BMI), (2) laboratory parameters (etc, neutrophil count and percentage, lymphocyte count, platelets), (3) hemodynamic variables (e.g., systolic blood pressure, heart rate, AST/ALT ratio, red blood cell count), and (4) preoperative comorbidities (etc, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, diabetes). Surgical types included isolated coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), valve surgery, combined CABG and valve procedures, aortic surgery, and other major cardiac operations. The MIMIC-IV database was selected for its high-resolution ICU data, which are widely recognized in predictive modeling research involving vital signs, lab results, and perioperative management [7]. These variables were chosen based on their clinical plausibility in reflecting pathophysiological mechanisms—such as impaired perfusion or immune dysfunction—that contribute to IAPI risk.

Model building and statistical analysis

To address missing data, this study systematically evaluated the extent and pattern of missingness across variables (Supplementary Figure S1). Variables with 60% missingness, such as albumin, were excluded from further analysis. For the remaining features, multiple imputation was performed by applying predictive mean matching (PMM) to preserve the underlying data distribution and mitigate potential imputation bias. In addition to applying single PMM imputation, we performed multiple imputations (m = 2000 resamples) to assess the uncertainty introduced by missing data. For each imputed dataset, model training and evaluation were repeated, and the distribution of AUC values was summarized. This approach allowed us to quantify the potential variability in predictive performance attributable to imputation. Outliers were identified using the Interquartile Range (IQR) technique, where outliers were defined as data points falling below the first quartile minus 1.5 times the IQR or above the third quartile plus 1.5 times the IQR. The outliers were then handled using nearest neighbor substitution, replacing them with the closest boundary values. Data standardization involved leaving categorical data unchanged, while numerical data fields were standardized using Z-score normalization. This process adjusted the data to have a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1. The response variable was the occurrence of intraoperative acquired pressure injuries (IAPI) in patients who underwent cardiovascular surgery, with other patient characteristics treated as explanatory variables. A P-value threshold of 0.2 was set to select indicators for univariate logistic regression analysis. To address data imbalance, we applied combined over- and undersampling using the ovun.sample function from the ROSE package, achieving a balanced 1:1 class ratio. Comparative analysis with SMOTE and ADASYN (Supplementary Figure S2) showed that ROSE yielded the highest and most stable AUC (0.987) and F1 scores (0.936), supporting its suitability for this dataset. The dataset was split, reserving 10% of the original data as a validation set to assess model performance prior to resampling. The remaining 90% was used for internal training and testing. Post-resampling, the data was again divided randomly into a 70% training set and a 30% test set as shown in Fig. 1(A), maintaining positive to negative sample ratios of approximately 1.048:1 and 1.051:1, respectively. Seven predictive models—logistic regression, LASSO regression, naive Bayes, random forest, support vector machine, XGBoost, and GBDT. Performance was assessed across metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and AUC. All models were trained and tuned under a unified framework using five-fold, ten-times repeated stratified cross-validation (5-fold repeated CV, repeats = 10). Stratification was applied to maintain class balance across folds. The same pre-specified hyperparameter grid was applied consistently across all folds, and the optimal parameters were selected by maximizing the mean ROC-AUC across repeats. The hyperparameter grids and final selected values for each algorithm are summarized in Supplementary Table S4, including C and σ for SVM, the number of rounds, depth, and learning rate for XGBoost, the number of trees and depth for GBDT, mtry for RF, and λ for LASSO/Ridge regression. The models were then combined into an ensemble model via the super learner algorithm. The Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) framework was employed to interpret the ensemble model, providing detailed insights into the contribution of each feature to the prediction outcomes. This enhanced the interpretability of the model and aided in understanding the potential biological mechanisms and risk factors associated with IAPI.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 12,222 patients (7,802 males, 4,420 females) were included in the MIMICIV data (derivation cohort), with 253 developing pressure injuries—an incidence rate of approximately 1.17% for males (144 cases) and 0.89% for females (109 cases). The mean values of each indicator and their significance between the IAPI and non-IAPI groups are detailed in Table 1 and Supplementary Table S3. In the hospital data (internal cohort), 355 samples were analyzed, comprising 224 males and 131 females, with 37 patients who developed pressure injuries, including 26 males and 11 females (Table 1). The incidence rate in this cohort was higher than that in the internal MIMICIV dataset. Model feature selection

Across multiple imputations, the mean AUC was 0.997 ± 0.004. The distribution of AUC values demonstrated that predictive performance remained consistently high with minimal variability across imputations (Supplementary Figure S6), indicating that imputation-related uncertainty had negligible impact on model stability. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression studies were performed to determine the parameters affecting intraoperative acquired pressure injury (IAPI) and to formulate an accurate risk prediction model (Supplementary file 1 Figure S3). The analysis commenced with univariate logistic regression to mitigate dimensionality, given the hospital dataset’s extensive feature count, constrained sample size, and feature redundancy. The logistic regression model included 23 indicators (Supplementary file 1 Table S1), with the incidence of IAPI as the dependent variable and other characteristics as independent variables. Ensemble learning techniques, namely, random forest and XGBoost, were utilized to assess feature importance, yielding 14 and 13 features, respectively. The convergence of significant attributes from both methodologies resulted in a definitive compilation of 12 features, which included age, weight, ICU duration, AST/ALT ratio, PT activity, neutrophil count, absolute neutrophil count, absolute lymphocyte count, albumin, absolute platelet count, hematocrit, and BMI. The amalgamation of significant attributes yielded a total of 21 features, which included age, sex, height, weight, ICU time, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, heart failure, the AST/ALT ratio, PT activity, neutrophils, absolute neutrophil count, lymphocytes, absolute lymphocyte count, albumin, red blood cell count, platelet count, hemoglobin, BMI, and PT-INR.To mitigate potential multicollinearity, we conducted both correlation analysis and variance inflation factor (VIF) assessment across the selected features. Variables with VIF values exceeding 10 were considered highly collinear. As a result, PT-INR was excluded from the union feature set (Supplementary Figure S4). The final intersection set comprised 12 features, whereas the union set contained 20 features after this adjustment.

Training and evaluation of a single model

Using the IAPI indicator system described earlier, seven models were developed: logistic regression, LASSO regression, naive Bayes, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), XGBoost, and gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT). To evaluate and compare each model’s predictive performance, we employed fivefold cross-validation with stratified sampling to ensure that the class distribution remained consistent across folds0.9 This process was repeated 10 times to reduce variability due to random partitioning. Performance metrics included accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and AUC. The models were trained and tested on MIMICIV data, with in_hospital data used for external validation. The results are presented in Table 2 and Figs. 2 and 3. Table 2 reveals that all the models exhibit varying degrees of overfitting or underfitting, indicating that without a validation set, their predictive performance may be overstated. Within the intersection set, the SVM model demonstrated superior performance, achieving the greatest accuracy, recall, F1 score, and AUC value on the testing set, whereas the GBDT model secured the second-highest AUC. In the validation set, the SVM model performed excellently in terms of accuracy, recall, F1 score, and AUC, attaining scores of 0.750, 0.789, 0.763, and 0.839, respectively, although the LASSO regression model had the highest precision. The SVM model had the most robust performance, followed by the GBDT model and LASSO regression model, while the remaining four models were significantly more robust. In the external validation cohort, all models showed reduced performance, reflecting limited generalizability to small, imbalanced datasets. Bootstrapping with 95% confidence intervals was used to assess robustness (Fig. 2D). In addition to reporting performance metrics, we formally compared the AUCs between models using DeLong’s test. As summarized in Supplementary Table S5, the Random Forest model (AUC = 0.78) significantly outperformed logistic regression (AUC = 0.67, p < 0.001) and GBDT (AUC = 0.76, p < 0.001). While XGBoost achieved the second highest AUC (0.77), the difference with RF was also statistically significant (p = 0.00065). These statistical comparisons corroborate that XGBoost provided robust and competitive performance across evaluation criteria, serving as a strong baseline for further ensemble modeling. SVM achieved the highest recall (0.821) and balanced overall performance, GBDT the highest precision (0.918), while LASSO yielded the highest AUC (0.630) but with unstable calibration. Calibration analyses indicated that SVM and GBDT maintained relatively good fit, whereas LASSO and NB exhibited poor calibration and high variability, suggesting overfitting. Across models, calibration performance varied substantially. LR achieved the lowest Brier score (0.318) but showed systematic over- and under-prediction (Supplementary Figure S5). SVM and GBDT demonstrated closer agreement between predicted and observed risks, consistent with moderate Brier scores (0.463 and 0.381) and lower ECE (≈0.50). In contrast, RF and XGB showed poorer calibration (Brier > 0.41, ECE > 0.52), with a tendency to overestimate risk at higher probabilities. Training and evaluation of ensemble models based on the super learner algorithm

The super learner algorithm was utilized to train and assess the ensemble model incorporating the seven aforementioned individual models. This strategy involves assigning weights to each base model by reducing the loss risk function. The weight coefficients indicate each model’s contribution to the super learner, with the magnitude of the weight directly related to the model’s influence on the overall ensemble. These findings indicate that, according to the hospital dataset, the SVM model has a weight coefficient of 0.3254, whereas the GBDT model has a weight value of 0.6745. The remaining five unique models do not contribute to the intersection set. In the union set, the SVM model has a weight coefficient of 0.3011, whereas the GBDT model has a weight coefficient of 0.6989, with the other models exhibiting no coefficients. Although only SVM and GBDT contributed non-zero weights, we retained the ensemble framework because of its demonstrated robustness. A comparison of the performance of individual models with that of the ensemble model revealed that while SVM and GBDT individually performed well, the ensemble consistently achieved the highest AUC and narrower confidence intervals, with reduced variability under bootstrap resampling (Supplementary Table S6). This indicates that the ensemble model provides improved stability and generalizability compared to relying on a single best-performing model, which is particularly important in small and imbalanced clinical datasets. The integrated model was subsequently tested with an external hospital dataset, utilizing identical single model weights. The evaluation results are detailed in Supplementary File 1 Table S2, and the ROC curve is illustrated in Fig. 4, along with its associated AUC value. Notably, although the super learner did not always achieve the maximum performance on certain evaluation measures, its performance on the validation dataset was consistently superior. This finding indicates that the ensemble model successfully mitigated the overfitting or underfitting problems inherent in the individual models, resulting in enhanced generalizability. Additionally, the super learner model outperformed the individual models in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and AUC, achieving a superior AUC value beyond 0.7, particularly for the external datasets [11]. Model interpretation based on the SHAP framework

The SHAP framework and Shapley values intuitively highlight the key factors that increase an individual’s IAPI risk, helping doctors understand the contribution of each feature to the prediction [12, 13]. By examining the length of the bars, we may assess the relative significance of the features incorporated in the model, as depicted in Fig. 5. To further ensure robustness, we computed bootstrapped confidence intervals for the average SHAP values across 2000 resamples. This additional analysis provides variability estimates that were previously lacking and supports the stability of key predictors identified by the model. Atrial Fibrillation rated highest, exhibiting an absolute average SHAP value of 0.42. Atrial Fibrillation (AF) induces irregular cardiac rhythms that hinder circulation and diminish tissue perfusion, particularly under stress, such as during surgical procedures. This diminishes the body’s capacity to regenerate skin, heightening the danger of pressure injuries. Patients with atrial fibrillation may experience cardiovascular instability, resulting in hypotension and inadequate blood circulation, hence elevating the risk of pressure injuries [14–16]. The Absolute Lymphocyte Count was rated second, exhibiting an average SHAP value of 0.33. Lymphocytes are essential for immune response and tissue repair. Insufficient levels can compromise immunity, hinder wound healing, and elevate the risk of pressure injuries. Discussion

Key findings

This study leveraged the MIMIC-IV database to investigate the impact of demographic and clinical variables on the development of pressure injuries among patients undergoing cardiovascular surgery, and further validated the findings with an external dataset from Jiangsu Province, China. By analyzing 24 frequently utilized predictors and constructing both union and intersection feature sets, we developed a prediction model (IAPI) with robust internal and external validity. The support vector machine model achieved the highest performance in internal validation (AUC = 0.839) and maintained good discriminative ability in the test set (AUC = 0.758). In external validation, LASSO regression outperformed other models (AUC = 0.679), while ensemble approaches consistently exceeded an AUC of 0.7, highlighting the stability of combining multiple predictors. SHAP analysis further identified atrial fibrillation and absolute lymphocyte count as key contributors to prediction, aligning with established clinical relevance.

Beyond descriptive reporting, we conducted formal statistical comparisons of model performance using DeLong’s test. These analyses confirmed that the XGBoost model achieved significantly superior discrimination compared with most alternative algorithms, while the Random Forest model also demonstrated competitive robustness. Such evidence-based statistical validation strengthens confidence in our findings, supporting the practical applicability and generalizability of the IAPI model. Collectively, the integration of rigorous validation, ensemble learning, and interpretability through SHAP enhances the clinical utility of our work by providing a reliable tool to inform perioperative decision-making and mitigate the risk of pressure injuries in cardiovascular surgery patients.

Relationship to literature

Previous studies predominantly utilized unified models for predicting IAPI risk, frequently neglecting the influence of essential demographic characteristics such as sex and age. Older patients are particularly vulnerable to pressure injuries during cardiovascular surgery because of age-related physiological alterations, such as diminished skin elasticity, compromised circulation, and delayed wound healing. Indeed, PIs in older adults are independently associated with increased length of stay, complications such as pneumonia and DVT, and higher healthcare costs [21, 22], For example, surgical PIs are linked to a 2.6-day longer hospital stay and a 21% higher odds of 30-day mortality in older cohorts. These findings underscore the clinical significance of precisely identifying high-risk elderly patients undergoing cardiac surgery. Future research should prioritize the augmentation of the dataset by collaboration with additional centers to improve the model’s adaptability across diverse situations and mitigate the risk of overfitting. Furthermore, the use of regularization methods such as L1 or L2 regularization may assist in controlling model complexity and enhancing its therapeutic significance [5, 11, 22–25]. In addition to enhancing existing models, investigating advanced techniques such as reinforcement learning and deep learning could present significant opportunities. These strategies, by applying intricate data patterns and including novel biomarkers and clinical parameters, could substantially improve the prediction accuracy [26]. These developments are essential for creating models that are precise and resilient across various patient demographics, particularly for elderly patients who are at increased risk for IAPI after cardiac surgery. This study underscores the potential of machine learning models, especially ensemble methods, in predicting IAPI; however, additional research is necessary to enhance their application in wider clinical contexts. Augmenting the dataset and exploring advanced modeling techniques are crucial for attaining these objectives.

Implications of study findings

The model-building strategy used in this study differs from traditional IAPI risk prediction approaches, which often neglect the impact of key demographic factors such as gender and age. By analyzing these variables, we revealed their specific contributions to risk prediction, highlighting the importance of precision medical care. Previous studies have relied primarily on conventional statistical methods such as logistic regression, whereas some have incorporated single machine learning algorithms [27–29]. In contrast, our study used a diversified approach, employing multiple techniques—including logistic regression, naive Bayes, random forests, support vector machines, and XGBoost. We also applied an ensemble learning strategy, specifically stacking, to increase the predictive accuracy for IAPI occurrence [30, 31]. These findings emphasize the value of multifaceted strategies in risk prediction, ultimately aiming to improve clinical outcomes in critical care settings. Calibration analyses showed that strong discrimination does not ensure reliable probability estimates. For clinical use, calibration is essential to avoid risk misestimation. Complementary metrics such as Brier score and ECE highlighted that SVM and GBDT maintained more stable calibration, whereas LASSO and NB performed poorly, underscoring the need to assess calibration alongside discrimination when developing predictive tools.

Limitations

The study’s single-source data and small sample size are major drawbacks. This shortcoming limits the understanding of the causes of IAPI in cardiovascular surgery patients. The model’s poor performance on the MIMIC-IV database suggests that the small sample size may hinder generalization and dependability. The small sample size may not account for the heterogeneity and complexity of the patient group. Owing to high marker missing values, some data were discarded. This exclusion may have missed factors affecting IAPI development in these patients [14, 15, 19, 20, 24, 26–28]. By eliminating variables, the study may have reached biased conclusions since crucial elements were not examined, altering its grasp of relevant influences.

In addition, several methodological limitations warrant attention. First, although calibration plots and bootstrapped confidence intervals were included, more comprehensive calibration assessment could further clarify whether certain models systematically over- or under-predict risk. Second, while ensemble modeling was employed, only two models contributed non-zero weights, raising the question of whether an ensemble strategy offered tangible benefit over the best-performing single model. An ablation analysis could better quantify this effect [32, 33]. Third, hyperparameter settings and tuning details were not exhaustively documented; future studies should provide full transparency of tuning procedures to enhance reproducibility. Fourth, although performance differences were evaluated descriptively, formal statistical testing such as DeLong’s test for AUC or confidence interval overlap analysis is essential to substantiate claims of superiority. Fifth, the use of single predictive mean matching (PMM) imputation did not reflect imputation uncertainty. Multiple imputation could further stabilize estimates and help assess its impact on SHAP-based interpretation. Finally, we addressed collinearity among laboratory predictors by conducting correlation and VIF analyses, resulting in the exclusion of PT-INR (VIF > 10) [34]. While this adjustment mitigates distortions in feature importance rankings, multicollinearity among correlated hematological parameters (e.g., hemoglobin, RBC count, hematocrit) remains a potential concern.

Conclusion

We performed an extensive analysis employing descriptive statistics and the SHAP interpretation framework to evaluate the determinants influencing the incidence of IAPI. These methods illustrate the importance of several characteristics in forecasting IAPI risk and highlight their utility in model construction. Our research developed seven distinct predictive models, comprising logistic regression, LASSO regression, naive Bayes, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), XGBoost, and GBDT, to assess the likelihood of IAPI. Nonetheless, each model exhibited varying degrees of overfitting or underfitting, with the LASSO regression model demonstrating the highest predictive performance regarding union characteristics. The super learner ensemble model successfully addresses overfitting by weighting the predictions of seven distinct models, leading to improved evaluation performance. Internal training and testing, as well as external validation, were conducted, confirming that measures such as atrial fibrillation and absolute lymphocyte count are critical reference characteristics for the IAPI risk prediction model. These findings validate the model’s efficacy as a valuable instrument for the early identification of patients, particularly senior individuals, at risk for IAPI, hence assisting physicians in making informed therapy decisions.

Space-time regions of high baryon density and baryon stopping in heavy-ion collisions
Yuri B. Ivanov

Abstract
Four-volumes ( V 4 = V_{4}= spatial-3-volume × \times lifetime) are calculated within the model of three-fluid dynamics (3FD) and compared with those of the the JET AA Microscopic Transport Model (JAM). The calculations are performed for central Au+Au collisions at energies s N ​ N = \sqrt{s_{NN}}= 3 – 19.6 GeV. These V 4 V_{4} indicate optimal collision-energy ranges for realizing macroscopic high baryon-density matter. It is found that the 3FD four-volumes noticeably exceed those in the JAM, which indicates a stronger baryon stopping in the 3FD model as compared to that JAM. It is argued that this difference in the baryon stopping correlates with stiffness of the EoS implemented in these models. Contrary to JAM, the four-volume, where a baryon density ( n B n_{B} ) exceeds three times the normal nuclear density ( n 0 n_{0} ), does not exhibit a maximum as a function of s N ​ N \sqrt{s_{NN}} . It decreases monotonically with increasing s N ​ N \sqrt{s_{NN}} , remaining at a fairly macroscopic level (i.e. V 4 ∼ > 5.5 4 V_{4}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{ \lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip-3.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}}5.5^{4} fm 4 /c). For higher baryon densities, V 4 V_{4} exhibits maxima in its dependence on s N ​ N \sqrt{s_{NN}} . The optimal energy range for densities n B / n 0 > n_{B}/n_{0}> 4 is located at s N ​ N = \sqrt{s_{NN}}= 3.2–8 GeV. Even for n B / n 0 > n_{B}/n_{0}> 6, the four-volume remains quite macroscopic ( V 4 ∼ > 4 4 V_{4}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{ \lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip-3.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}}4^{4} fm 4 /c) at s N ​ N = \sqrt{s_{NN}}= 4.5–9 GeV contrary to the JAM.

II
3FD simulations

The 3FD model is a further development of the multi-fluid approaches previously worked out
by Los-Alamos group

;
Clare:1986qj

the Kurchatov Institute

The 3FD model
Ivanov:2005yw
;
Ivanov:2013wha
simulates nonequilibrium at
the early stage of nuclear collisions by means of two counterstreaming baryon-rich fluids,
which is minimal way to model the early-stage nonequilibrium.
Newly produced particles predominantly populate the midrapidity region.
Therefore, they are attributed to a third (“fireball”) fluid.
These three fluids, i.e. the projectile (p), target (t), and fireball (f),
are governed by conventional hydrodynamic equations coupled by
friction terms in the right-hand sides of the Euler equations. The friction terms
describe the energy–momentum exchange between the fluids.
In the hadronic phase, the friction was estimated
in Ref.
Sat90
, this friction is used in the 3FD simulations.
The phenomenological friction in the QGP was fitted to reproduce the baryon stopping
at high collision energies within the deconfinement scenarios as it is described in
Ref.
Ivanov:2013wha
in detail.
The 3FD model describes a nuclear collision from the stage of the incident cold nuclei
approaching each other, to the final freeze-out

Russkikh:2006aa
;
Ivanov:2008zi
.

The physical input of the 3FD calculations is
described in Ref.
Ivanov:2013wha
. The 3FD simulations are traditionally
performed with three different EoSs: a
purely hadronic EoS
Mishustin:1991sp
and two versions of the EoS
involving the deconfinement transition
Toneev06

and a smooth crossover one. This paper mainly demonstrates results with crossover EoS
as the most successful one in reproduction of various observables in a wide range of collision energies,
see, e.g., Ref.
Ivanov:2025vru
and references therein. Results for the 1PT are also demonstrated
to illustrate dependence on the EoS.

The counterstreaming of the p and t fluids takes place
only at the initial stage of the nuclear collision. At later
stages the baryon-rich (p and t) fluids have already either
partially passed though each other or partially stopped

IV
Baryon Stopping vs EoS Stiffness

As mentioned above, the baryon stopping in the 3FD is stronger than in JAM.
In fact, this difference in the baryon stopping correlates with stiffness of the EoS implemented in the 3FD and JAM
models. The 3FD uses soft (in the hadronic phase) crossover and 1PT EoSs with incompressibility of 210 MeV

Toneev06
. This incompressibility was one of the constraints in construction of these crossover and 1PT EoSs
because the soft EoS is required for reproduction of the directed flow
(more precisely, the directed transverse flow
\langle P_{x}/A\rangle

Danielewicz:1985hn
)

Danielewicz:2002pu
, as it was a common viewpoint that time.
A stiff EoS produces a higher pressure in colliding nuclei, hence a stronger directed flow, which
disagrees with data
Danielewicz:1985hn
.
At the same time,
the directed flow is insensitive to the stiffness of the EoS within the JAM model

Nara:2020ztb
;
Nara:2021fuu
. This looks counterintuitive, because the produced pressure
turns out to be insensitive to the stiffness of the EoS.

On the other hand, essential dependence on the EoS stiffness still remains in the UrQMD model
Steinheimer:2022gqb
.
The stiff EoS (
K\approx
360 MeV) is reported to be preferable at
\sqrt{s_{NN}}<
7 GeV.
This definitely contradicts the conclusion of Ref.
Danielewicz:1985hn
.
A natural question arises: what has happened since the analysis
Danielewicz:1985hn
,
in which the constraint on a soft EoS was deduced from data on the directed flow of protons?
The EoS stiffness and baryon stopping are two key factors in description of the directed flow.
The pressure, that is required for the directed-flow formation, consists of two parts: the kinetic pressure,
that results from the baryon stopping (i.e. randomization of nucleon momenta and production of secondary hadrons),
and the potential pressure due to the EoS stiffness. If the kinetic pressure is high (strong baryon stopping)
the lower potential pressure (a soft EoS) is needed to reproduce the directed flow.
This is precisely the conclusion of the analysis in Ref.
Danielewicz:1985hn
.
The 3FD supports this conclusion
Ivanov:2025vru
;
Ivanov:2024gkn
;
Konchakovski:2014gda
;
Ivanov:2014ioa
;
Ivanov:2016sqy
.
If the preference of stiff EoS is found
Steinheimer:2022gqb
, that would mean that the
baryon stopping in the UrQMD model
Steinheimer:2022gqb
is weaker than in the analysis
of Ref.
Danielewicz:1985hn
and in the 3FD model.

) allows a direct comparison of
the baryon stopping within different models.

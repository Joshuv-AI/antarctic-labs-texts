Computation of Interpolants for Description Logic Concepts in Hard Cases
Jean Christoph Jung, Jędrzej Kołodziejski, Frank Wolter

Abstract
While the computation of Craig interpolants for description logics (DLs) with the Craig Interpolation Property (CIP) is well understood, very little is known about the computation and size of interpolants for DLs without CIP or if one aims at interpolating concepts in a weaker DL than the DL of the input ontology and concepts. In this paper, we provide the first elementary algorithms computing (i) 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 \mathcal{ALC} interpolants between 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 \mathcal{ALC} -concepts under 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℋ \mathcal{ALCH} -ontologies and (ii) 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 \mathcal{ALC} interpolants between 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒬 \mathcal{ALCQ} -concepts under 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒬 \mathcal{ALCQ} -ontologies. The algorithms are based on recent decision procedures for interpolant existence. We also observe that, in contrast, uniform depth restricted interpolants might be of non-elementary size.

Introduction

Interpolants between description logic (DL) concepts have found many applications. For instance, they can be used as explicit concept definitions or referring expressions, as explanations for concept inclusions, as rewritings of queries, and as separating concepts in the context of concept learning
ten Cate et al. 2006
;
Artale et al. 2021
;
Toman and Weddell 2021
;
Jung et al. 2022
;
Jung et al. 2021
. The computation of interpolants has been investigated extensively, both by the DL community
ten Cate et al. 2013
;
Fortin et al. 2022
;
Lyon and Karge 2024
;
Koopmann and Schmidt 2015
but also in modal logic and related fragments of FO
Sofronie-Stokkermans 2008
;
Benedikt et al. 2016
;
Fitting and Kuznets 2015
. We quickly remind the reader how this is done: A Craig interpolant between
C
and
D
is a concept
E
in the shared signature of
C
and
D

such that
\models C\sqsubseteq E
and
\models E\sqsubseteq D
(for simplicity we drop the ontology). A DL has the Craig Interpolation Property (CIP), if the existence of such an interpolant follows from
\models C\sqsubseteq D
. DLs such as
\mathcal{ALC}
,
\mathcal{ALCQ}
, and
\mathcal{ALCI}
have the CIP
ten Cate et al. 2013
. Then, an interpolant
E
can typically be extracted from a proof of
\models C\sqsubseteq D
(or, equivalently, of non-satisfiability of
C\sqcap\neg D
) in standard calculi in the field such as tableau, the chase, sequent calculi, or type elimination
ten Cate et al. 2013
;
Fortin et al. 2022
;
Lyon and Karge 2024
;
Benedikt et al. 2016
;
ten Cate 2022
.

The situation is very different for DLs that do not enjoy the CIP or if one is interested in interpolating concepts in a weaker DL than the concepts used in the inclusion. In this case, the existence of an interpolating concept does not follow from the validity of the inclusion and extracting interpolating concepts from proofs becomes much harder. In fact, very little is known about how this could be done and research has so far focused on deciding the existence of interpolants rather than constructing them
Jung and Wolter 2021
;
Artale et al. 2023
;
Kurucz et al. 2023
. It is worth noting, however, that for extensions of
\mathcal{EL}
, the chase can be used to compute interpolants even without CIP
Fortin et al. 2022
.

It is well known that Craig interpolants of
{\cal ALC}
concept inclusions under
{\cal ALCH}
ontologies do not necessarily exist
Konev et al. 2009
;
ten Cate et al. 2013
and that not every
{\cal ALCQ}
concept inclusion has an
{\cal ALC}
interpolant (take
\models C\sqsubseteq C
for any
{\cal ALCQ}
concept
C
not equivalent to an
{\cal ALC}
concept). Existence of
{\cal ALC}
interpolants in these settings is, however, decidable
Artale et al. 2023
;
Kuijer et al. 2025
. To explain the proof, assume that
\models C\sqsubseteq D
and let
\Sigma
be any signature (again we drop the ontology for simplicity). It is known that an interpolating
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
concept exists if no

\Sigma
-bisimilar nodes satisfying
C
and
\neg D
exist. Hence it suffices to decide
whether a pair of concepts is satisfiable in
\Sigma
-bisimilar nodes.
It turns out that to decide this problem it is crucial to decide the more general problem
whether a
set
of concepts (and not just a pair) is satisfiable in mutually
\Sigma
-bisimilar nodes. By completing concepts to types containing them, it suffices to decide the latter problem for sets of types, often called
mosaics
. In fact, the decision algorithms in

Artale et al. 2023
;
Kuijer et al. 2025

use mosaics and generalize the well-known
type elimination procedures
deciding satisfiability of concepts to
mosaic elimination procedures
deciding
\Sigma
-bisimilar satisfiability.

Mosaic elimination procedures decide the existence of interpolants, but they do not construct any interpolants. The aim of this paper is to give the first elementary algorithms constructing
\mathcal{ALC}
interpolants
whenever they exist under ontologies in
\mathcal{ALCH}
and under ontologies and concept inclusions in
\mathcal{ALCQ}
. Our algorithms are not restricted to computing Craig interpolants, but work for arbitrary signatures. The idea of the algorithms is to run the mosaic elimination procedures discussed above and construct, in addition and inductively, for each eliminated mosaic entailed
\mathcal{ALC}(\Sigma)
concepts that witness non
\Sigma
-bisimilar satisfiability of its types. The witness concepts we propose are not aggregated at each step, but are polyadic in the sense that we define, for any set
T
of concepts (types in the case of mosaics) which are not satisfiable in
\Sigma
-bisimilar nodes, for each
C\in T

an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
concept
\mathsf{Sep}(C)
such that the following holds:

•

\models C\sqsubseteq\mathsf{Sep}(C)
for all
C\in T
;

•

\models\mathop{\mathop{\mbox{\bigmathxx\char 117\relax}}}\limits_{C\in T}\mathsf{Sep}(C)\sqsubseteq\bot
.

The concept
\mathsf{Sep}(C)
constructed for
T=\{C,\neg D\}
is then the desired interpolant. We note that an earlier attempt
to construct interpolants while running a mosaic elimination procedure without using polyadic separators does not work as stated
Artale et al. 2023
. Hence one main contribution of this paper is to correct that proof. Our second main contribution is to show that our approach also works in the case of
{\cal ALCQ}
ontologies.

As usual, the semantics is defined in terms of

The

.

of

.

We next introduce the studied notions and associated problems. A

is a set
\Sigma
of concept and role names. An
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)

is an
{\cal ALC}

\mathcal{O}\models E\sqsubseteq D

;

A relation
Z\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{J}}

Back

(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})\in Z

for all role names

(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})\in Z

A

is a pair

\mathcal{I}

\Sigma

.

{\cal ALCQ}

\mathcal{I}_{1},d_{1}\sim_{{\cal ALC},\Sigma}\mathcal{I}_{2},d_{2}

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\{{\cal ALCH},{\cal ALCQ}\}
,
\mathcal{O}
be an
\mathcal{L}

C,D
be
\mathcal{L}

1.

{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)

\mathcal{O}\models C\sqsubseteq D

2.

\sim_{{\cal ALC},\Sigma}

The proof of Lemma

\Sigma

{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)

and crucially relies on the use of compactness. In particular, it is not constructive in the sense that in the proof of implication

We study here the associated computation problems, that is, compute the interpolants if they exist.
A notion dual to the notion of an interpolant is that of a

3
Role Inclusions

In this section, we are concerned with computing
{\cal ALC}
interpolants of concept inclusions under
{\cal ALCH}
ontologies. We start with an example that illustrates the failure of the computation algorithm given in
Artale et al. 2023
.

Example 2
.

Fix
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{O}=\{r\sqsubseteq s_{i},s_{i}\sqsubseteq s^{\prime}_{i}\mid i\leq k\}
,
\Sigma=\{s^{\prime}_{i},A_{i}\mid i\leq k\}
, and

\displaystyle C=\exists{r}.B\sqcap\forall r.(B\to\mathop{\mathop{\mbox{\bigmathxx\char 116\relax}}}\limits_{i\leq k}A_{i})\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptD=\mathop{\mathop{\mbox{\bigmathxx\char 117\relax}}}\limits_{i\leq k}\forall{s_{i}}.\neg A_{i}.

We show that
C,D
are not jointly
\sim_{{\cal ALC},\Sigma}
-consistent under
\mathcal{O}
.
Indeed, if
C,D
are jointly
\sim_{{\cal ALC},\Sigma}
-consistent, then all concepts in
S=\{B\sqcap(B\to\textstyle\mathop{\mathop{\mbox{\bigmathxx\char 116\relax}}}\limits_{i\leq k}A_{i})\}\cup\{\neg A_{i}\mid i\leq k\}
are satisfied in mutually
\Sigma
-bisimilar nodes, which is clearly not the case. By Lemma
1
, there is an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
interpolant for
\mathcal{O}\models C\sqsubseteq\neg D
. For instance,
E=\mathop{\mathop{\mbox{\bigmathxx\char 116\relax}}}\limits_{i\leq k}\exists{s^{\prime}_{i}}.A_{i}
is an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
interpolant.
The algorithm from
Artale et al. 2023
, however, computes a concept of shape
\exists s_{i}^{\prime}.E
for a single
i\leq k
and one can easily see that a concept of this shape cannot serve as an interpolant. The mistake in the algorithm is a confusion in the quantifier order in the assumptions of the interpolant construction.

We first show how a natural idea for computing interpolants, which works in several other settings, fails to compute elementary sized interpolants in the presence of role inclusions. Next we provide an algorithm which does compute elementary interpolants.

A natural idea to compute interpolants could be to show first that, if there is an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
interpolant for
\mathcal{O}\models C_{0}\sqsubseteq D_{0}
, then there is one of small role depth
n
, and then use the strongest
{\cal ALC}
consequence of
C_{0}
of this role depth
n
.
Let
n\geq 0
. A
(\Sigma,n)
-uniform interpolant of
C_{0}
under
\mathcal{O}

is an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
concept
U
such that
\mathcal{O}\models C_{0}\sqsubseteq U
and
\mathcal{O}\models U\sqsubseteq E
for every
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
concept
E
of role depth at most
n
with
\mathcal{O}\models C_{0}\sqsubseteq E
.
A
(\Sigma,n)
-uniform interpolant for
C_{0}
under
\mathcal{O}
always exists, and can be used as an interpolant for
\mathcal{O}\models C_{0}\sqsubseteq D
whenever an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
interpolant for
\mathcal{O}\models C_{0}\sqsubseteq D
of role depth
n
exists.
This idea has been used to compute elementary sized modal logic interpolants of
\mu
-calculus formulae
Jung and Kolodziejski 2025
and it follows from its proof that it applies to computing interpolants under
{\cal ALC}
ontologies. We actually conjecture that it works for the majority of DLs enjoying the CIP, but we leave an elaboration for future work. Unfortunately, contrary to these settings, in our case
(\Sigma,n)
-uniform interpolants need not be elementary in
n
, and consequently do not lead to elementary sized interpolants.
In what follows we denote by
Tower
the iterated exponential function, that is,
\textnormal{\sc Tower}(0)=1
and
\textnormal{\sc Tower}(n+1)=2^{\textnormal{\sc Tower}(n)}
.

Theorem 3
.

There is an
{\cal ALCH}
ontology
\mathcal{O}
, an
{\cal ALC}
concept
C_{0}
, and signature
\Sigma
such that there is no
(\Sigma,n)
-uniform interpolant of
C_{0}
under
\mathcal{O}
smaller than
\textnormal{\sc Tower}(n-2)
.

Proof.

Consider the
{\cal ALCH}
ontology
\mathcal{O}=\{r\sqsubseteq s,r\sqsubseteq s^{\prime}\}
, the concept

C_{0}=\exists r.\top

and
\Sigma=\{s,s^{\prime}\}
. We claim that no concept
U
of size smaller than
\textnormal{\sc Tower}(n-2)
is a
(\Sigma,n)
-uniform interpolant for
C_{0}
under
\mathcal{O}
.
Assume towards contradiction that there is such an

. Observe that for every
C\in{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
of depth
n-1

\forall s.C\to\exists s^{\prime}.C
is an
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)

n
and
\mathcal{O}\models C_{0}\sqsubseteq(\forall s.C\to\exists s^{\prime}.C)

\mathcal{O}\models U\sqsubseteq(\forall s.C\to\exists s^{\prime}.C)

Consider all trees of depth

, choose one for every equivalence class of
\Sigma
-bisimulation and denote the set of all these chosen trees by

. We have

and

whose respective roots

are distinguished by some

of depth

-uniform interpolant. This is witnessed by an interpretation

constructed as follows. First take the disjoint union of

. Then take two fresh points

, and add edges

and

satisfy the same subconcepts of

On the positive side, we show the following second main result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an
{\cal ALCH}

C_{0},D_{0}

{\cal ALC}

{\cal ALCH}

\mathcal{O}

We say that a type

of a concept

Intuitively, a mosaic

describes a collection of elements in an interpretation

which realize precisely the types in

\Sigma
-bisimilar. Naturally, not every set of types can be realized in this way, and we use a mosaic elimination procedure to determine which can.
We write
t\leadsto_{r}t^{\prime}
if an element of type

t

\{C\mid\forall r.C\in t\}\subseteq t^{\prime}

\{C\mid\forall r.C\in t\}=t_{/_{r}}
.
We write

with
t\leadsto_{r}t^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a set of mosaics. A mosaic

is

if it violates one of the following conditions:

(Atomic Consistency)

for every

and

;

(Existential Saturation)

for every
t\in T

\exists r.C\in t

such that (a)
C\in t^{\prime}
for some

a_{r}(t,t^{\prime})\subseteq\{T_{C}\mid C\in\mathcal{B}\}
as

\{T_{C}\mid\text{ there is $d\in C^{\mathcal{I}}$ with $(d_{t},d)\in r^{\mathcal{I}}$
and $\mathsf{tp}_{\mathcal{I}}(d)=t^{\prime}$}\}

To see that
a_{r}(t,t^{\prime})
is as required, first observe that
t^{\prime}\in T_{C}
for any
T_{C}\in a_{r}(t,t^{\prime})
. Next assume that a
T_{C}
with
C\in\mathcal{B}
and
t\in T
are given.
From
d_{t}\in(t\sqcap\nabla_{r}(\mathcal{B}))^{\mathcal{I}}
we obtain a
d
with
(d_{t},d)\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
and
d\in C^{\mathcal{I}}
. Let
t^{\prime}=\mathsf{tp}_{\mathcal{I}}(d)
. Then
T_{C}\in a_{r}(t,t^{\prime})
. The condition
|a_{r}(t,t^{\prime})|\leq w_{r.t}(t^{\prime})
follows directly from the definitions.
∎

Using Lemma
10
we obtain a general
{\cal ALC}(\Sigma)
separator
\mathsf{Sep}_{n+1}
for
\mathcal{E}_{n}\cup\{T\}
by setting
\mathsf{Sep}_{n+1}(t)=\mathsf{Sep}(t)\sqcap\mathsf{Sep}_{n}(t)
for all
t\in T

and
\mathsf{Sep}_{n+1}(t)=\mathsf{Sep}_{n}(t)
for all remaining types.

This finishes the construction of separators for every eliminated mosaic. One can now construct
the actual interpolants in exactly the same way as in the previous section for
{\cal ALCH}
via Lemma
6
. To compute the size of the interpolants, observe that in the construction above
\lVert\mathsf{Sep}_{n+1}(t)\rVert\leq\lVert\mathsf{Sep}_{n}(t)\rVert\times 2^{2^{2^{f(m)}}}
with
f
a polynomial function and
m=\lVert\mathcal{O}\rVert+\lVert C_{0}\rVert+\lVert D_{0}\rVert
. As
\mathsf{Sep}_{n}
stabilizes
after at most double exponentially many elimination steps,
\lVert\mathsf{Sep}_{n}\rVert
is bound by a
3-exponential function in
\lVert\mathcal{O}\rVert+\lVert C_{0}\rVert+\lVert D_{0}\rVert
. This bound remains 3-exponential under DAG representation. The construction of
\mathsf{Sep}_{n}(t)
involves satisfiability checks for concepts of 3-exponential size, so overall the interpolant can be constructed in 4-exponential time.

5
Conclusion

We have presented first non-trivial algorithms for computing
{\cal ALC}
interpolants under
{\cal ALCH}
and
{\cal ALCQ}
ontologies, relying on the new notion of polyadic separators tailored to store witnesses for the fact that a mosaic (or set of types) cannot be realized in mutually bisimilar models. Theorems
4
and
8
demonstrate the inherent difficulty of the problem and explain why previously known techniques do not easily apply in the cases of
{\cal ALCH}
and
{\cal ALCQ}
. It is worth to note that Theorem
4
can be easily modified to obtain non-elementary lower bounds for the size of uniform interpolants at the concept level in the presence of
{\cal ALCH}
ontologies. These lower bounds, in turn, translate to non-elementary lower bounds for the size of uniform interpolants at the ontology level in
{\cal ALCH}
. This implies that the resolution based calculus for computing uniform interpolants of
{\cal ALCH}
ontologies from
Koopmann and Schmidt 2014
cannot run in elementary time, answering a question posed by the authors.

In the future, we would like to extend our algorithms to other standard DL constructors. While we believe that this will be rather easy for some constructors, like inverse roles or the universal role, we expect it to be much more involved in other cases such as nominals. In fact, already unifying the two presented algorithms into one for computing
{\cal ALC}
interpolants under
{\cal ALCHQ}
ontologies appears to be challenging.
It would be also interesting to analyze our procedures in the ontology-free cases (or with an ontology containing only role inclusions), for which we expect smaller interpolants.
From a practical perspective, it would be interesting to extend implemented tableaux algorithms to be able to compute interpolants. Beyond description logics, it would be very interesting to compute interpolants in the guarded and/or the two-variable fragments of first-order logic
Jung and Wolter 2021
or in first-order modal logics
Kurucz et al. 2023
.

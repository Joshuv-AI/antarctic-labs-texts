Fisheries of the Exclusive Economic Zone Off Alaska; Application 
for an Exempted Fishing Permit

AGENCY: National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS), National Oceanic and 
Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), Commerce.

ACTION: Notice; receipt of application for exempted fishing permit.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: This notice announces NMFS' receipt of an application from 
SeaShare, Inc. and Glacier Fish Co. and the public comment period for 
an exempted fishing permit (EFP). If issued, this permit would allow 
the applicants to retain Pacific halibut up to a poundage limit and 
donate it through the Prohibited Species Donation (PSD) Program, after 
Pacific halibut deck sorting and after sampling by a fisheries 
observer. This EFP would exempt one catcher/processor vessel from 
complying with regulations that prohibit the retention of Pacific 
halibut and donation of Pacific halibut prohibited species. The EFP 
would place a poundage limit on the allowable retention of Pacific 
halibut. Effective dates of the EFP would be from May 1, 2027, to 
December 31, 2028. This experiment would allow retention of Pacific 
halibut to test an additional way to donate dead Pacific halibut to 
hunger relief causes that would otherwise be discarded at sea. If 
successful, this would promote the food-supply and conservation 
objectives of the Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management 
Act (Magnuson-Stevens Act).

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: NMFS manages the groundfish fisheries in the 
exclusive economic zone of the Bering Sea and Aleutian Islands 
Management Area (BSAI) under the Fishery Management Plan (FMP) for 
Groundfish of the BSAI (BSAI FMP). The Council prepared the BSAI FMP 
under the authority of the Magnuson-Stevens Act, 16 U.S.C. 1801 et seq. 
Regulations governing the BSAI groundfish fisheries appear at 50 CFR 
parts 600 and 679. The FMP and the EFP-implementing regulations at 
Sec. Sec.  600.745(b) and 679.6 allow the NMFS Regional Administrator 
to authorize, for limited experimental purposes, fishing that would 
otherwise be prohibited. Under the Magnuson-Stevens Act, fishing is 
defined to include, among other things, ``the catching, taking, or 
harvesting of fish.''

Background and Need for Exempted Fishing Permit

    Current regulations prohibit trawl catcher/processor vessels from 
retaining and donating Pacific halibut under the PSD Program. In the 
Bering Sea trawl limited access (TLAS) yellowfin sole fishery, 
incidentally caught Pacific halibut are sorted on deck and then 
discarded at sea, following regulations for the Pacific halibut deck 
sorting program at Sec.  679.102. Pacific halibut that are unable to be 
sorted and discarded on deck within the time limits indicated on the 
Observer Sampling Station Inspection Report are then sorted in the 
vessel's factory and provided to the fisheries observer for sampling 
before they are discarded at sea, as required by regulations.
    This EFP proposes to allow a portion of the Pacific halibut that 
remain in the catch, after Pacific halibut deck sorting and after 
sampling by a fisheries observer, to be retained, processed, and 
donated through the PSD Program, instead of being discarded at sea as 
required by regulation. Regulatory exemptions are requested, as 
described below, to allow the proposed EFP to test the logistical and 
operational requirements required for the donation of Pacific halibut 
for hunger relief purposes from one trawl catcher/processor vessel.

Exempted Fishing Permit

    On August 21, 2026, Ms. Hannah Lindoff with SeaShare, Inc. and Mr. 
Glenn Merrill with Glacier Fish Co. submitted an application for an EFP 
to test the operational and logistical requirements needed for one 
trawl catcher/processor to donate Pacific halibut under the PSD 
Program. The proposed EFP aims to test donation of Pacific halibut on 
one trawl catcher/processor vessel in the TLAS yellowfin sole fishery 
in the Bering Sea. Gear and fishing operations will be conducted in 
compliance with current management regulations subject to exemptions 
under the EFP.
    The EFP applicants requested to retain and donate a maximum of 
5,000 net pounds of headed and gutted Pacific halibut, or 3.0 metric 
tons of round weight equivalent, to the PSD Program for hunger relief 
purposes through SeaShare, Inc, a NMFS-authorized distributor under the 
PSD program. The proposed EFP does not seek any exemptions from 
existing Pacific halibut Prohibited Species Catch limits established by 
regulation, nor from applicable requirements for Pacific halibut deck 
sorting. Those limits and requirements would continue to apply. The 
proposed EFP also does not seek any changes in catch monitoring or 
catch accounting.

Exemption

    To meet the proposed EFP's objective, two exemptions to Alaska 
Region regulations are necessary:
     50 CFR 679.21(a)(2)(ii), which prohibits the retention of 
prohibited species catch. If granted, the exemption would allow one 
trawl catcher/processor vessel to retain, for donation only, up to 
5,000 net pounds, or 3.0 mt round weight equivalent, of headed and 
gutted Pacific halibut.
     50 CFR 679.26(a)(2), which allows the donation of Pacific 
halibut delivered by catcher vessels using trawl gear to shoreside 
processors and stationary floating processors, thereby prohibiting a 
catcher/processor vessel from participating in the PSD program.

Permit Conditions, Review, and Effects

    If issued, the EFP vessel will comply with observer requirements at 
Sec.  679.50, deck sorting requirements at Sec.  679.102, and PSD 
Program requirements at Sec.  679.26. The EFP vessel will work with 
NMFS on necessary communication and monitoring protocols.
    If issued, the EFP would contain several conditions. Within 6 
months of the end of EFP fishing, the applicant would be required to 
submit to NMFS a final report that describes how well EFP objectives 
were accomplished, including any problems and successes, total amount 
of retained Pacific halibut, and total amount of donated Pacific 
halibut during the EFP. The Alaska Fisheries Science Center (AFSC) 
reviewed a draft EFP application that was submitted on August 21, 2026. 
The AFSC determined that the scientific design is valid and provided 
minor comments. All AFSC feedback has been incorporated in the final 
EFP application submitted to NMFS on September 15, 2026.
    The activities that would be conducted under this EFP are limited 
in size, magnitude, and duration with no potential for significant 
individual or cumulative impacts, as detailed in the draft categorical 
exclusion for this action (see ADDRESSES).
    In accordance with Sec. Sec.  679.6 and 600.745, NMFS has 
determined that the application warrants further consideration and has 
forwarded the application to the Council to initiate consultation. The 
Council is scheduled to consider the EFP application during its October 
2026 meeting, which will be held in Anchorage, Alaska. The applicants 
have been invited to speak in support of the application.

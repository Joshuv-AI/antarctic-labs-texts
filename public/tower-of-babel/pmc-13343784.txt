Morphological and postoperative functional comparison of patellar dislocation with or without avulsion fracture of the medial inferior border of the patella
Authors: Houxin Sun, Yang Zhao, Qiankun Chang, Zhihui Wang, Yuhang Sun, Wei Qin, Jia Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate specific radiological risk factors in patients with patellar dislocation accompanied by inferomedial patellar margin avulsion fractures and provide evidence for clinical treatment strategy selection, this study conducted comparative analysis of radiographic measurements and clinical function between patients with patellar dislocation with and without such avulsion fractures.MethodsA total of 73 patients with patellar dislocation who underwent medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstruction at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University were included. Basic patient data, affected side, injury mechanism, and type of patellar dislocation were collected. The subjects were subsequently divided into an experimental group and a control group based on the presence or absence of a concomitant inferomedial patellar marginal avulsion fracture. Imaging evaluations, including MRI, CT, and X-ray examinations, were comprehensively performed for comparative analysis. MRI was used to assess the height of the vastus medialis obliquus (VMO); CT was used to measure patellar thickness, patellar width, lateral patellar facet angle, Wiberg angle, Wiberg index, the length ratio of lateral to medial patellar facets, trochlear depth index, sulcus angle, lateral trochlear inclination, tibial tubercle–trochlear groove (TT-TG) distance, the cross-sectional area ratio of VMO to vastus lateralis muscle (VLM), medial-lateral width of the femoral condyle, and the length of different regions of the femoral condyle, as well as to classify the patella and trochlea. The Caton-Deschamps index was measured on X-ray. Additionally, clinical function was evaluated and compared using Lysholm score, IKDC score, Tegner score, and VAS score preoperatively and at 6 months postoperatively.ResultsStatistically significant differences were observed between the experimental and control groups regarding injury mechanism, type of patellar dislocation, the ratio of lateral to medial patella facet length, and Wiberg scores (P < 0.05).ConclusionInjury mechanism, types of patellar dislocation, the Wiberg angle, and the length ratio of the lateral to medial patellar facets have significant predictive value for the occurrence of avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin following patellar dislocation. Furthermore, patients with patellar dislocation, whether accompanied by avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin or not, undergoing isolated medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction show no difference in postoperative clinical function.Keywords: Patellar dislocation, Chondral injury, Medial patellofemoral ligament, Medial patellotibial ligament

Introduction

Patellar dislocation (PD) is a common acute knee injury in children and adolescents, accounting for approximately 3% of all knee injuries [1]. Currently, the primary causes of PD are categorized into osseous and soft-tissue factors associated with patellofemoral instability [2], such as patella alta and trochlear dysplasia. Additionally, risk factors contributing to PD include female sex and a positive family history.

Soft-tissue injuries to the medial aspect of the knee and osteochondral fractures (OCF) are common concomitant lesions following PD. The insertion of the medial patellotibial ligament (MPTL) is located at the distal one-third of the medial patellar margin; therefore, an avulsion fracture of the inferomedial patellar border often indicates an injury to the MPTL. Currently, there is ongoing debate regarding the necessity and surgical indications for MPTL reconstruction in clinical practice. In addition, there is a lack of research on whether avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin during patellar dislocation are associated with the patellofemoral joint anatomy or quadriceps muscle of the patient. In this study, we performed a comparative analysis of radiographic measurements and clinical function in patients with patellar dislocation with or without an avulsion fracture, to provide evidence to guide clinical management strategies for patellar dislocation injuries.

Materials and methods

Inclusion criteria

(1) Age range: 10–50 years. (2) A definitive history of patellar dislocation, accompanied by knee instability, joint effusion, medial tenderness, and a positive apprehension test. (3) Imaging evidence indicating Medial patellofemoral ligament injury. (4) Patients undergoing patellar dislocation surgery at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Recurrent or fixed patellar dislocation. (2) History of prior knee surgery. (3) Incomplete clinical records. (4) Postoperative follow-up period less than 6 months.

General information

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a retrospective review was conducted of 73 patients with acute patellar dislocation treated in the Department of Joint Surgery at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University from January 2019 to May 2023. Among them, there were 37 males and 36 females; the age ranged from 10 to 48 years, with a mean age of 18.34 ± 8.00 years; 39 cases involved the left knee and 34 the right knee; 60 cases were primary dislocations and 13 were recurrent dislocations. The BMI ranged from 15.70 to 39.68 kg/m2, with a mean of 25.50 ± 5.02 kg/m2. Mechanism of Injury: Patellar dislocation mechanisms were categorized as contact and non-contact injuries. Among the enrolled patients, 30 cases were attributed to contact injuries, while 43 cases were non-contact injuries. Patients with concomitant avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar border were enrolled in the study group (n = 37). The cohort comprised 15 males and 22 females, with 20 cases of contact injuries and 17 cases of non-contact injuries. The participants’ ages ranged from 12 to 48 years, with a mean age of 18.08 ± 7.92 years. Nineteen cases involved the left knee and 18 the right knee. Primary dislocation occurred in 35 cases, while recurrent dislocation was observed in 2 cases. The body mass index (BMI) ranged from 17.43 to 37.55 kg/m2, with a mean of 24.90 ± 4.71 kg/m2. Patients without avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar border were assigned to the control group (n = 36). This group included 22 males and 14 females, with 10 cases of contact injuries and 26 cases of non-contact injuries. There were 25 cases of primary dislocation and 11 cases of recurrent dislocation. The age ranged from 10 to 45 years, with a mean of 18.61 ± 8.20 years. Twenty cases involved the left knee and 16 the right knee. The BMI ranged from 15.70 to 39.68 kg/m2, with a mean of 26.12 ± 5.31 kg/m2.

Measurement and surgical methods

Measurement method

Preoperatively, all patients underwent full-length standing anteroposterior (AP) radiographs of both lower extremities, standard AP and lateral knee radiographs, patellar axial views, CT and MRI scans of the knee joint. All imaging data were independently evaluated by two trained orthopedic surgeons blinded to the clinical details.and the following parameters were measured. To minimize measurement bias, the mean value of each parameter was recorded.

(1) Patellar Position and Morphological Measurements [3–8]: On CT axial images at the level of the maximal transverse diameter of the patella cartilage surface, the following parameters were assessed: ① Patellar width and thickness; ② Lateral Patellar Angle (LPA): the angle between the patellar transverse axis and the lateral patellar facet; ③ Ratio of the lengths of the lateral to medial patellar facets (EPIP); ④ Wiberg index (WI): the ratio of the transverse length of the lateral patellar facet to the total patellar width; ⑤ Wiberg angle (WA): the angle between the medial and lateral patellar facets. On lateral radiographs of the knee; ⑥ Caton-Deschamps index (CDI): the ratio of the length of the patellar articular surface to the distance between the inferior edge of the patellar articular surface and the anterior corner of the tibial plateau; ⑦ Wiberg classification: used to classify patellar morphology; ⑧ Assessment of patellar fractures: the presence of an avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin was evaluated on knee CT and radiographs.See Figs. 1, 2 and 3.

Clinical knee function and pain assessment [18]

Knee function scores (Lysholm score, IKDC score, Tegner score) and Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) [18] scores were collected from both patient groups preoperatively and at 6 months postoperatively for comparative analysis.

Surgical approach

All patients underwent arthroscopic medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction surgery.

Surgical procedure

After successful anesthesia, standard anteromedial and anterolateral portals were established for arthroscopy to evaluate the intra-articular structures, focusing on the patellofemoral tracking, joint congruence, and osteochondral lesions. Chondral lesions were debrided, and any loose bodies were removed. A longitudinal incision was made at the inferomedial aspect of the tibial tubercle to expose the semitendinosus tendon, which was harvested using a tendon stripper and and prepared for grafting. A medial patellar incision was made to expose the medial border of the patella and the medial retinaculum. Two 3.0 mm suture anchors were inserted at the middle and the junction of the middle and upper thirds of the medial patellar margin. The midportion of the prepared tendon was then sutured and fixed into the bony groove along the medial patellar border. The femoral attachment site was identified by palpating the depression between the medial femoral epicondyle and the adductor tubercle. A Kirschner wire was used for positioning posterosuperior to the medial femoral condyle, followed by the creation of a bone tunnel using a 6.0 mm cannulated drill. The other end of the tendon was passed into and through the femoral tunnel. The knee joint was then repeatedly flexed and extended to maintain appropriate tension, which was subsequently secured with a 6.0 mm bioabsorbable interference screw. The knee was repeatedly flexed and extended to confirm satisfactory patellar tracking without redislocation or impingement, indicating appropriate tension of the reconstructed patellofemoral ligament. The incisions were closed layer by layer, and sterile dressings were applied.

Postoperative management

Patients were instructed to undergo proprioception and muscle strength training. Full weight-bearing was permitted 4–6 weeks postoperatively, with the mandatory use of a hinged knee brace for protection. Regular follow-ups were conducted at 1, 3, and 6 months after surgery. No cases of recurrent patellar dislocation were observed during the follow-up period.

Statistical methods

SPSS 27.0.1 software was employed to perform normality tests on quantitative data. Normally distributed data underwent independent samples t-tests, while skewed data and ordinal data were analysed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Comparisons of count data between the two groups were conducted via chi-square tests. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Baseline characteristics between the two groups were analysed using the Mann-Whitney U test for age, independent t-tests for BMI, and chi-square tests for gender, affected side, injury mechanism, and dislocation type. Results indicated statistically significant differences in contact injuries as the injury mechanism and primary dislocations as the dislocation type (P < 0.05). Discussion

In recent years, with a deeper understanding of the medial anatomy and biomechanics of the knee, the role of the MPTL in patellar stability has increasingly become a research hotspot. However, there is currently limited research regarding the presence of specific radiographic risk factors in patients with patellar dislocation combined with inferomedial patellar avulsion fractures, and the optimal surgical strategy remains poorly defined.

In the case data of this study, 60% of patients with patellar dislocation were found to have associated OCF, with 50% of these patients presenting avulsion fractures of the medial inferior border of the patella. These findings are consistent with results reported in both domestic and international studies. In a study of 131 patients with first-time acute traumatic patellar dislocation, Mochizuki et al. [19] found that 84% of patellar avulsion fractures occurred at the inferomedial margin, corresponding to the insertion site of the MPTL. Tompkins et al. [20] analyzed 157 cases with primary lateral patellar dislocation and reported that in 47.2% of cases (67 patients), the patellar insertion of the MPTL exhibited a tear injury. In addition, the results of this study demonstrated that avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin occur more frequently in contact injuries and in cases of primary patellar dislocation. We hypothesize that, regarding the injury mechanism, the MPTL possesses higher stiffness and lower deformation capacity compared to the MPFL [21]. During contact injury, the normal tracking of the patella within the trochlear groove is abruptly disrupted, and the resulting high load leads to rupture of the MPTL, thereby causing an avulsion fracture of the inferomedial patellar margin. Regarding the type of dislocation, in patients experiencing primary patellar dislocation, the periacticular stabilizing structures of the knee have not yet undergone adaptive changes or laxity from recurrent episodes. Consequently, these structures are more susceptible to failure when subjected to trauma, thereby leading to the occurrence of avulsion fractures at the inferomedial patellar border.

By comparing the radiological differences between the two groups, it was found that the experimental group had a smaller Wiberg angle and a higher EPIP compared to the control group, which is consistent with the findings of Zhou [22] and Gao [14] et al. This suggests that patella articular surface may be one of the risk factors contributing to avulsion fractures of the medial inferior border of the patella following patellar dislocation. A lower Wiberg angle indicates a flatter patellar articular surface, which results in poorer congruence of the patellofemoral joint and further compromising the patellar tracking and stability within the trochlear groove. EPIP may be related to stress distribution across the patella. A higher EPIP indicates a shorter medial patellar articular surface, leading to greater lateralization of the patella. This results in increased stress at the inferomedial margin of the patella and, consequently, thereby predisposing to avulsion fractures.Although factors such as Wiberg index (WI), patella alta, and tibial tuberosity-trochlear groove (TT-TG) distance are established risk factors for patellar dislocation, they appear to have no significant clinical relevance in the subgroup of patellar dislocations accompanied by avulsion fractures.

In terms of biomechanics [22], the MPFL provides 50–60% of the restraining force during 0°–30°of knee flexion, while the MPTL serves as the main stabilizing structure preventing lateral patellar dislocation when knee flexion exceeds 30 degrees. Its role in maintaining patellar tilt and rotation increasing as the degree of knee flexion increases. The vastus medialis obliquus (VMO) consists of the distal fibers of the vastus medialis and plays a crucial role in maintaining patellar positioning and restricting lateral displacement. Structural abnormalities of the VMO have been associated with the occurrence of patellar dislocation.The present study found no association between the VMO and avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin.The present study found no association between the VMO and avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin. This may be explained by the functional role of the VMO, which is more involved in the overall regulation of patellar tracking and acts synergistically with the MPFL.In contrast, avulsion fractures reflect peak tensile stress at the local osseous attachment site and are therefore unrelated to the morphological characteristics of the VMO.

Currently, surgical treatment of patellar dislocation primarily focuses on reconstruction of the MPFL. Although the postoperative outcomes of MPFL reconstruction have been extensively studied, there is still no clear consensus on whether patients with concomitant avulsion fractures at the inferomedial margin of the patella require simultaneous MPTL reconstruction.Vittorio et al. [23] demonstrated that MPTL reconstruction can be considered as a relevant surgical option in cases of patellofemoral instability; however, their study did not compare knee function outcomes between patients undergoing MPFL reconstruction and those receiving MPTL reconstruction. Hinckel et al. [24] proposed that combined MPFL and MPTL reconstruction is appropriate for patients with subluxation in knee extension, instability during flexion, hyperextension with ligamentous laxity, skeletal immaturity, and other associated risk factors. Yang et al. [25] reported that, compared with isolated MPFL reconstruction, combined MPFL and MPTL reconstruction reduced the need for procedures such as tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO) and associated surgical complications.In this study, we compared preoperative and postoperative knee function scores in patients with and without avulsion fractures at the inferomedial margin of the patella who underwent MPFL reconstruction. The results showed no statistically significant differences between the two groups in preoperative and two-month postoperative Lysholm scores, IKDC scores, Tegner scores, or VAS scores. This suggests that for patients with patellar dislocation accompanied by avulsion fractures of the inferomedial patellar margin, isolated medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction is sufficient, with no additional procedure required for the MPTL.

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design may introduce unmeasured confounding factors, such as selection bias, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, the sample size in this study was relatively small, and cases with severe skeletal abnormalities, such as habitual or fixed patellar dislocation, were excluded. Consequently, whether the additional reconstruction of the MPTL is necessary for this specific patient population warrants further investigation. Finally, the follow-up duration in this study was relatively short, which precludes a precise evaluation of the long-term prognosis.Further follow-up will be conducted to address this limitation.

Through this study, we have gained a clearer understanding of the risk factors and postoperative functional recovery associated with patellar dislocation combined with inferomedial border avulsion fractures. These findings provide a clinical basis for selecting optimal surgical strategies for patients presenting with this specific injury pattern.

Conclusion

The injury mechanism, type of patellar dislocation, Wiberg angle, and the ratio of lateral-to-medial patellar length demonstrate significant predictive and diagnostic value for the occurrence of inferomedial patellar avulsion fractures following dislocation. Furthermore, patients with patellar dislocation, whether accompanied by these avulsion fractures or not, underwent isolated medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstruction, with no significant differences observed in postoperative clinical functional outcomes between the two groups.

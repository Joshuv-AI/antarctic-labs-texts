POISSON-DIRICHLET APPROXIMATION FOR THE STATIONARY DISTRIBUTION OF THE INCLUSION PROCESS
Han L. Gan

Abstract
We consider the approximation of the stationary distribution of the finite inclusion process with the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution. Using Stein’s method, we derive an explicit bound for the approximation error, which is of order 1 / N 1/N in the thermodynamic limit. The results are achieved from a minor modification to Stein’s method for Poisson-Dirichlet distribution approximation developed in Gan and Ross, 2021 . The derivatives used on test functions in Gan and Ross, 2021 were directional type derivatives specifically chosen for their measure preserving properties. Depending upon the application, these derivatives can prove cumbersome. In this note, we show that for certain test functions we can instead use more traditional derivatives, which simplifies the bounds for the Stein factors and is more amenable to the approximation of the inclusion process.



1
Introduction

The one parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution with parameter
\theta>0
,
\mathrm{PD}(\theta)
, is a probability measure on the infinite dimensional ordered simplex,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\infty}:=\{(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots):p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}=1\}.

The distribution lacks an explicit form, but it can be represented in a variety of ways, such as a limit of Dirichlet distributions or via a stick breaking process, see
Feng, 2010
for more details and examples. As working with order statistics can be technically challenging, rather than using non-increasing ordering,
Gan and Ross, 2021
use a labelled version of the process with labels from a compact metric space
E
, and define the Dirichlet process in the following manner.

Definition 1.1

(Dirichlet process)
.

Let
\theta>0
and let
\pi
be a probability measure on a compact metric space
E
.
Let
(P_{1},P_{2},\ldots)\sim\mathrm{PD}(\theta)
be independent of

\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
, which are i.i.d. with distribution
\pi
.
Define the probability measure on
M_{1}:=M_{1}(E)
, the space of probability measures on
E
, by

(1.1)

\mathrm{DP}(\theta,\pi):=\mathscr{L}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}\delta_{\xi_{i}}\Bigr).

In
Gan and Ross, 2021
, Stein’s method for Dirichlet process approximation and Poisson-Dirichlet approximation was developed and a general theorem for bounding the approximation error between a random measure of interest and a Dirichlet process was derived. Motivated by the approximation of the stationary distribution of the inclusion process, we make a minor refinement to the framework of
Gan and Ross, 2021
. This refinement makes the framework more amenable to the application for approximation of the stationary distribution of the inclusion process, and moreover may make Poisson-Dirichlet and Dirichlet process approximation using Stein’s method more accessible and easier to use in a further applications.

1.1
Application to the inclusion process

Originating in
Giardinà et al., 2007
and
Giardinà et al., 2009
, the discrete inclusion process is a stochastic interacting particle system with
N
particles on
L
sites, which we can index as
\{1,\ldots,L\}
. We define the set of configurations corresponding to the proportions of the
N
particles on
L
sites as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{L}=\left\{\mu\in[0,1]^{L}:\sum_{i=1}^{L}\mu_{i}=1\right\},

where
\mu_{i}
is the proportion of the
N
particles in site
i
. As the indices for the sites are arbitrary, we can embed the atomic masses of the process onto a lattice of width
1/L
on
[0,1]
. If we set
x_{i}=i/L
, for any
\theta>0
, the generator of this discrete inclusion process is as follows,

(1.2)

\mathcal{A}_{1}f(\mu)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}N\mu_{i}\left(N\mu_{j}+\frac{\theta}{L}\right)\left[f\left(\mu-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{j}}\right)-f(\mu)\right].

The process can be briefly described in the following manner. At unit rate, for each (ordered) pair of particles on sites
(x_{i},x_{j})
a particle is moved from site
x_{i}
to site
x_{j}
. In addition, for each particle in the system, at rate
\theta/L
independently, the particle is removed and a new particle is added at one of the
L
sites chosen uniformly at random. Notably this model is also equivalent to a population genetics model, a type of Moran model with uniform mutation.

The limiting behaviour of this process has been extensively studied in a variety of different limiting regimes, see
Chleboun et al., 2022
,
Jatuviriyapornchai et al., 2020
,
Bianchi et al., 2017
and
Kim, 2021
for example. Let
W
follow stationary distribution associated with
\mathcal{A}_{1}
. In this paper, our goal is to assess the distribution of the masses in
W
, and compare this to distribution of the masses of the stationary distribution of the Dirichlet process with
E=[0,1]
and
\pi=\mathrm{U}[0,1]
. We now state the main approximation result. Note that the definition of the family of test functions
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is formally defined in Section
2
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
N
,
L
and
\theta>0
, let
W
be distributed as the stationary probability measure associated with
\mathcal{A}_{1}f
in (
1.2
). Set
E=[0,1]
,
\pi=\mathrm{U}[0,1]
and let
Z\sim\mathrm{DP}(\theta,\pi)
. Then for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that
h(\mu)=\langle\varphi,\mu^{k}\rangle
,

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{E}h(W)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)|\leq\bigg[\frac{k(k-1)}{2L(\theta+1)}+\frac{2k(k-1)\theta}{N(\theta+1)}+\frac{8k(k-1)(k-2)}{9N(\theta+2)}\bigg]\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

This bound is general, holds for any choice of
N,L
and
\theta
, and will converge to zero as
N\to\infty
when
L
scales with
N
in a positive manner. In
[
Jatuviriyapornchai et al., 2020
, Section 3]
, it is stated that if
d\rightarrow 0
and
dL\rightarrow\alpha>0
, then the limiting distribution is the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution. In the notation of this manuscript,
d=\theta/L
, and hence our bound is entirely consistent with existing results. Furthermore, in the so called thermodynamic limit, where
N,L\to\infty
such that
N/L
converges to a positive constant
\theta
, the bound is therefore of order
\frac{1}{N}
.

Moreover, using
[
Gan and Ross, 2021
, Lemma 1.15]
we can show convergence in sampling formulas. Given a random sample of size
n
from
W
denoted by
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
, let
\mathcal{S}_{n}(W)
denote the denote the probability measure on set partitions of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
induced by the relation
i\sim j\Leftrightarrow y_{i}=y_{j}
. Similarly, let
\mathcal{S}_{n}(Z)
be analogous for
Z\sim\mathrm{DP}(\theta,\pi)
. Notably
\mathcal{S}_{n}(Z)
is known to have an explicit form, namely the Ewens sampling formula. We then have the following result.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
N
,
L
and
\theta>0
, let
W_{N}
be distributed as the stationary probability measure of
\mathcal{A}_{1}f
in (
1.2
). Then for
Z\sim\mathrm{DP}(\theta,\pi)
and any
n>0
,

\displaystyle d_{TV}(\mathcal{S}_{n}(W_{N}),\mathcal{S}_{n}(Z))\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{2L(\theta+1)}+\frac{2n(n-1)\theta}{N(\theta+1)}+\frac{8n(n-1)(n-2)}{9N(\theta+2)}

To interpret the bound in the above corollary, suppose
L=\mathrm{O}(N^{\alpha})
for some
\alpha>0
. Then the bound will be small when the sample size
n\ll N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
and
n\ll N^{\frac{1}{3}}
.

The remainder of the paper will consist of two sections. In the first section we will briefly outline Stein’s method for Poisson-Dirichlet and Dirichlet process approximation and refine the bounds of
Gan and Ross, 2021
for the derivatives of the solutions to the Stein equation for test functions
h\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
. In the final section we prove the approximation error bounds for the inclusion process.

2
Refining the Stein method for the Dirichlet process

Pioneered in
Stein, 1972
for Normal approximation, Stein’s method is a powerful probabilistic tool used to explicitly bound the distributional distance between two distributions. Since the original seminal paper, it has been developed for a wide variety of distributions. For many examples and applications, see for example the surveys or monographs
Ross, 2011
;
Chatterjee, 2014
;
Barbour and Chen, 2005
;
Ley et al., 2017
.

We first give a brief summary of the Stein’s method approach used in
Gan and Ross, 2021
. For any random probability measure
W
, and
Z\sim\mathrm{DP}(\theta,\pi)
, our goal is to bound quantities of the form
|\mathbbm{E}h(W)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)|
, for all functions
h
in a sufficiently rich class of test functions. To achieve this, we begin by characterising
\mathrm{DP}(\theta,\pi)
as the unique stationary distribution of a Fleming-Viot process
Fleming and Viot, 1979
with generator,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}f(\mu)=\frac{\theta}{2}\int_{E}\partial_{x}f(\mu)\bigl(\pi-\mu\bigr)(dx)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{E^{2}}\bigl(\mu(dx)\delta_{x}(dy)-\mu(dx)\mu(dy)\bigr)\partial_{xy}f(\mu).

The specific variant of Stein’s method we use is often called the generator method, first introduced in
Barbour, 1988
;
Barbour, 1990
and
Götze, 1991
. The general idea of the approach is that as
Z
follows the stationary distribution of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, then
\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f(Z)=0
for all
f
. If for some random measure
W
that is close to
Z
in distribution, then one would expect
\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f(W)\approx 0
. The next step is then to choose specific test functions
f
that quantify the distributional distance between
W
and
Z
. More precisely, for any function
h
from some family of functions
\mathcal{H}
, we solve for the function
f_{h}
that satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(\mu)=h(\mu)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z).

We then set
\mu=W
, which implies
|\mathbbm{E}h(W)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)|=|\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(W)|
, and we then seek a bound for
|\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(W)|
.

Two different choices of families of test functions for
\mathcal{H}
are used in
Gan and Ross, 2021
. Let
\mathrm{C}(E^{K})
denote the set of continuous functions from
E^{k}\mapsto\mathbbm{R}
. For
\mu\in M_{1}
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}(E)
, set
\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle=\int_{E}\varphi(x)\mu(dx)
and
\mathrm{BC}^{2,1}(\mathbbm{R}^{k})
to be the set of functions with two bounded and continuous derivatives with second derivative Lipschitz. The first family of test functions is defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}=\{h(\mu):=H(\langle\varphi_{1},\mu\rangle,\ldots,\langle\varphi_{k},\mu\rangle):k\in\mathbbm{N},H\in\mathrm{BC}^{2,1}(\mathbbm{R}^{k}),\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{C}(E),i=1,\ldots,k\}.

The second family of test functions is defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}=\{h(\mu):=\langle\varphi,\mu^{k}\rangle:k\in\mathbbm{N},\varphi\in\mathrm{C}(E^{k})\}.

To successfully apply Stein’s method, bounds on
f_{h}
in (
2.2
) and its derivatives play a critical role. For each of the two families of test functions
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, two different approaches were used to bound the derivatives of
f_{h}
. For
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, a probabilistic coupling approach that utilises properties of the coalescent process was used and for
\mathcal{H}_{2}
a different approach based upon a dual process was used. The differential operator for the derivatives used was defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}F(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{F((1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})-F(\mu)}{\varepsilon},

as opposed to a more standard definition of derivative of

(2.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}F(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{F(\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})-F(\mu)}{\varepsilon}.

Derivatives of the form of (
2.3
) were used as for the family
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, the probabilistic coupling arguments requires the measures to be evaluated by
F
to be probability measures. For consistency, the same form of derivative was then also used for the family
\mathcal{H}_{2}
as well. It turns out that the proof method for
\mathcal{H}_{2}
does not require the measures to be probability measures, and as such, the more commonly form for derivatives as in (
2.4
) (see
Ethier and Kurtz, 1993
;
Ethier and Griffiths, 1993
for example) can be used and this is advantageous for our application to approximate of the stationary distribution of the inclusion process.

Let
W
follow the stationary distribution of the discrete inclusion process associated with
\mathcal{A}_{1}f
in (
1.2
). Then noting that this implies
\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{1}f(W)=0
, for any function
h\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
, our approach will be to derive a bound for

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{E}h(W)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)|=|\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(W)|=|\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(W)-\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{1}f_{h}(W)|,

by exploiting properties of
f_{h}
and its derivatives. This method is known as the generator comparison method in Stein’s method literature. To evaluate the right hand side of the above, the natural approach is to take the Taylor expansion of
f_{h}(\mu-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{i/L}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{j/L})-f_{h}(\mu)
in
\mathcal{A}_{2}f
to match the derivatives in
\mathcal{A}_{2}f
. At this point, derivatives of the form (
2.4
) are more useful as they naturally match the terms in the Taylor expansion, whereas derivatives of the form (
2.3
) do not. Hence we rederive the results of
[
Gan and Ross, 2021
, Theorem 2.7]
but for derivatives of the form (
2.4
). We note that the proof method used in this section is effectively a simplification of the proof of
[
Gan and Ross, 2021
, Theorem 2.7]
for test functions in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
using a different form of derivative, and as a result we attempt to only include the main ideas and details, and refer the reader to
Gan and Ross, 2021
for the full details.

For a test function
h\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
, where
h(\mu)=\langle\varphi,\mu^{k}\rangle
, elementary computations show that the generator
\mathcal{A}_{2}
can be written in the form

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}h(\mu)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\big[\langle\Phi^{(k)}_{ij}\varphi,\mu^{k-1}\rangle-\langle\varphi,\mu^{k}\rangle\big]+\frac{\theta}{2}\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\big[\langle\varphi,\mu^{i-1}\pi\mu^{k-i}\rangle-\langle\varphi,\mu^{k}\rangle\big],

where
\Phi^{(k)}_{ij}\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})=\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},x_{i},x_{j},\ldots,x_{k-1})
. Following
[
Ethier and Kurtz, 1993
, Section 3, page 353]
, we define a dual process
(Y_{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\cup_{j\geq 0}\mathrm{C}(E^{j})
where we set
Y(0)=\varphi
. Using the generator (
2.6
), for each
i<j
, the process jumps from
\varphi
to
\Phi_{ij}^{(k)}\varphi
at rate 1, and at rate
\frac{\theta}{2}
, for each
1\leq i\leq k
,
\varphi
transitions to a
k-1
dimensional function by integrating dimension
i
with respect to
\pi
. Notably this process has similarities to the inclusion process, hence the approximation result in Theorem
1.2
is unsurprising. Using standard duality arguments
[
Ethier and Kurtz, 1993
, Theorem 3.1]
, setting
Z_{\mu}(t)
to be the Fleming-Viot processes governed by
\mathcal{A}_{2}
with
Z_{\mu}(0)=\mu
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\big[h(Z_{\mu}(t))\big]=\mathbbm{E}\big[\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),\mu^{k-M_{k}(t)}\rangle\mathbf{1}[M_{k}(t)<k]\big],

where
M_{k}(t)
is the number of transitions of the process
Y_{\varphi}(\cdot)
up to time
t
. Note that we can without loss of generality we assume that
\mathbbm{E}h(Z)=0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
h\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that
h(\mu)=\langle\varphi,\mu^{k}\rangle
, the solution
f_{h}
given in (
2.2
) is well defined and

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\|\partial_{x}f_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k}{\theta}\|\varphi\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}\|\partial_{xy}f_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{\theta+1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y,z}\|\partial_{xyz}f_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k(k-1)(k-2)}{3(\theta+2)}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

We begin by noting that noting that
Gan and Ross, 2021
already show that
f_{h}
is well defined and satisfies (
2.2
). In particular the solution is given by

\displaystyle f_{h}(\mu)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mathbbm{E}h(Z_{\mu}(t))-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)]dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{E}\big[\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),\mu^{k-M(t)}\rangle\mathbf{1}[M_{k}(t)<k]\big]dt,

where
(Z_{\mu}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Fleming-Viot process following generator
\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
Z_{\mu}(0)=\mu
in the first line and the second line follows from using the duality (
2.7
) and the assumption that
\mathbbm{E}h(Z)=0
. For the first derivative,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}f_{h}(\mu)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{E}\bigg[\big[\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),(\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})^{k-M(t)}\rangle-\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),\mu^{k-M(t)}\rangle\big]\mathbf{1}[M_{k}(t)<k]\big]\bigg]dt

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbbm{E}\int_{\tau_{i-1}}^{\tau_{i}}\big[\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),(\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})^{k-i+1)}\rangle-\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),\mu^{k-i+1}\rangle\big]dt,

where
\tau_{i}
denotes the
i
-th transition for for the process
Y_{\varphi}(t)
and
\tau_{0}=0
. Noting that

\|\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),(\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})^{s)}\rangle-\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),\mu^{s}\rangle\|\leq\varepsilon s\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{o}(\varepsilon),

and
\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}
is exponentially distributed with rate
(k-i+1)(k-i+\theta)/2
, yields

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\|\partial_{x}f_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\|\varphi\|_{\infty}(k-i+1)\varepsilon\cdot\mathbbm{E}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})+\mathrm{o}(\varepsilon)\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{2}{k-i+\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k}{\theta}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

For the second and third derivatives, using the same arguments and that

\displaystyle\|\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),(\mu+\varepsilon_{1}\delta_{x}+\varepsilon_{2}\delta_{y})^{s}

\displaystyle\rangle-\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),(\mu+\varepsilon_{1}\delta_{x})^{s}\rangle-\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),(\mu+\varepsilon_{2}\delta_{y})^{s}\rangle+\langle Y_{\varphi}(t),\mu^{s}\rangle\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}s(s-1)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{o}(\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2})

for the second derivative and analogously for the third derivative yields the following bounds.

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}\|\partial_{xy}f_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}(k-i+1)(k-i)\mathbbm{E}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{2(k-i)}{k-i+\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{\theta+1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y,z}\|\partial_{xyz}f_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}(k-i+1)(k-i)(k-i-1)\mathbbm{E}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\frac{2(k-i)(k-i-1)}{k-i+\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k(k-1)(k-2)}{3(\theta+2)}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

∎

3
Proof of the inclusion process approximation results

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall the generator we use for the Fleming-Viot process (
2.1
) with
E=[0,1]
and
\pi
the uniform measure on
E
is

(3.1)

\mathcal{A}_{2}f(\mu)=\theta\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f(\mu)\bigl(\pi-\mu\bigr)(dx)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\mu(dx)\delta_{x}(dy)-\mu(dx)\mu(dy)\bigr)\partial_{xy}f(\mu),

and that the generator for the (discrete) inclusion process is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}f(\mu)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}N\mu_{i}\left(N\mu_{j}+\frac{\theta}{L}\right)\left[f\left(\mu-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{i/L}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{j/L}\right)-f(\mu)\right].

Furthermore, recall from (
2.5
) that our goal is to bound

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{E}h(W)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)|=|\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(W)|=|\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{2}f_{h}(W)-\mathbbm{E}\mathcal{A}_{1}f_{h}(W)|.

where
W
to be a random measure following the stationary distribution associated with
\mathcal{A}_{1}
. For convenience we write
W=\sum_{i=1}^{L}W_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
, where
x_{i}=i/L
.
Our goal is to use the Taylor expansion
\mathcal{A}_{1}f(W)
, match terms to
\mathcal{A}_{2}f(W)
and then carefully bound the remainder. We note the Taylor expansion,

\displaystyle f\left(W-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{j}}\right)-f(W)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\partial_{x_{i}}f(W)+\frac{1}{N}\partial_{x_{j}}f(W)

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{i}}f(W)+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{j}}f(W)-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f(W)

\displaystyle\ \ +R(W).

Then given we can sum either over all
i,j
or
i\neq j
when mathematically convenient as for
i=j
,
f\left(W-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{j}}\right)-f(W)=0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}f(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}^{L}N^{2}W_{i}W_{j}\bigg[-\frac{1}{N}\partial_{x_{i}}f(W)+\frac{1}{N}\partial_{x_{j}}f(W)

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{i}}f(W)+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{j}}f(W)-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f(W)+\varepsilon_{3}(W)\bigg]

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{\theta}{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}NW_{i}\bigg[-\frac{1}{N}\partial_{x_{i}}f(W)+\frac{1}{N}\partial_{x_{j}}f(W)+\varepsilon_{2}(W)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{L}W_{i}(1-W_{i})\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{i}}f(W)-\sum_{i\neq j}^{L}W_{i}W_{j}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}f(W)+N^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}^{L}W_{i}W_{j}\varepsilon_{3}(W)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\ \ -\theta\sum_{i=1}^{L}W_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}f(W)+\frac{\theta}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\partial_{x_{j}}f(W)+\frac{N\theta}{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}W_{i}\varepsilon_{2}(W),

where
\varepsilon_{2}(W)
and
\varepsilon_{3}(W)
are higher order expansion terms that we currently suppress for readability but will explicitly bound later. Again using the representation
W=\sum_{i=1}^{L}W_{i}\delta x_{i}
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}f(W)

\displaystyle=\theta\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f(W)dx-\theta\sum_{i=1}^{L}W_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}f(W)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i=1}^{L}W_{i}(1-W_{i})\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{i}}f(W)-\sum_{i\neq j}^{L}W_{i}W_{j}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f(W).

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) yields

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{E}h(W)-\mathbbm{E}h(Z)|\leq N\theta|\varepsilon_{2}(W)|+N^{2}|\varepsilon_{3}(W)|+\theta\biggl|\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f_{h}(W)dx-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{1}{L}\partial_{x_{j}}f_{h}(W)\biggr|.

For the final term of the above, note that this is simply the Riemann sum approximation for the integral, and hence via the mean value theorem and Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle\biggl|\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f_{h}(W)dx-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{1}{L}\partial_{x_{j}}f_{h}(W)\biggr|\leq\frac{\sup_{x,y}\|\partial_{xy}f_{h}\|_{\infty}}{2L}\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{2L(\theta+1)}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

The final result follows from bounding the higher order error terms as below.

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}(W)=\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{i}}f_{h}(W^{*})+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{j}}f_{h}(W^{*})-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f_{h}(W^{*}),

where
W^{*}=(W+s_{1}(-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{j}}))
for some
s_{1}\in[0,1]
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{2}(W)|\leq\frac{2}{N^{2}}\sup_{x,y}\|\partial_{xy}f_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2k(k-1)}{N^{2}(\theta+1)}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3}(W)=-\frac{1}{6N^{3}}\partial^{3}_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{i}}f_{h}(W^{*})+\frac{1}{2N^{3}}\partial^{3}_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{j}}f_{h}(W^{*})-\frac{1}{2N^{3}}\partial^{3}_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{j}}f_{h}(W^{*})+\frac{1}{6N^{3}}\partial^{3}_{x_{j}x_{j}x_{j}}f_{h}(W^{*}),

where
W^{*}=(W+s_{2}(-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}+\frac{1}{N}\delta_{x_{j}}))
for some
s_{2}\in[0,1]
. Hence

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{3}(W)|\leq\frac{4}{3N^{3}}\sup_{x,y,z}\|\partial_{xyz}f_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{8k(k-1)(k-2)}{9N^{3}(\theta+2)}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

∎

Influence of Joule heating and activation energy on bioconvective casson nanofluid transport over a curved stretching surface
Authors: A. Divya, Ali Alkhafaji, Muhammad Jawad, Walid Abdelfattah, Liaqat Hamdard, Abeer A. Shaaban
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study numerically investigates magnetohydrodynamic Casson nanofluid flow over a curved stretching sheet under the influence of Joule heating, thermal radiation, activation energy, and chemical reaction. The Buongiorno model incorporating Brownian motion and thermophoresis describes nanoscale transport, while gyrotactic motile microorganisms stabilize the nanoparticle suspension through bioconvection. A systematic comparison between Newtonian and non-Newtonian (Casson) fluid models highlights the yield stress effects on transport characteristics. The governing equations of the Casson nanofluid flow are transformed into coupled ordinary differential equations by using similarity variables. The obtained system is solved by using the built-in bvp4c method of MATLAB. Key findings reveal that the Casson nanofluid exhibits lower velocity but higher temperature and concentration profiles than the Newtonian fluid due to enhanced viscous resistance and internal friction. Velocity decreases with increasing magnetic parameter (M) and buoyancy ratio (Nr), while it increases with mixed convection (λ). Temperature rises with higher thermal radiation (Rd), Brownian motion (Nb), thermophoresis (Nt), and thermal Biot number (β₁). Concentration enhances with increasing Brownian motion, mass Biot number (β₂), and activation energy (E). Microorganism density increases with motile Biot number (β₃) and curvature (A), but decreases with Peclet number (Pe) and bioconvection Lewis number (Lb). Skin friction rises with magnetic and Casson parameters, while the Nusselt, Sherwood, and motile density numbers show strong Biot number dependence. Results agree excellently with existing literature. This comparative analysis demonstrates that non-Newtonian behavior significantly alters thermal and transport characteristics, making the Casson model suitable for biomedical and industrial applications involving yield-stress fluids such as blood and polymer solutions.

Introduction

Improvement of the heat and mass transfer processes has emerged as a key theme of the contemporary thermal engineering field because of its versatile application in energy system, biomedical equipment, material processing as well as in the microfluidic technology. The thermal conductivity of conventional heat transfer fluids like water, ethylene glycol and oils is low and this limits their use in high- efficiency systems. Nanofluids that have been engineered through dispersions of nanoscale solid particles in the base fluids have been considered to be one of the potential solutions to overcome such limitations since they exhibit better thermal and transport properties [1–3]. Khan et al. [4] surveyed the bioconvective heat transfer performance of magnetohydrodynamic flow of nanofluid with multiple slip situations. Their review has shown that the parameters of velocity and thermal slips considerably alter the structure of the boundary layer, and the bioconvection increases the mass transport through the stabilization of nanoparticles distribution. Using the situation of sophisticated manufacturing operations, Mahboobtosi et al. [5] conducted the three-dimensional study of penta-hybrid nanofluid flow to a extending sheet with nonlinear thermal radiation. Also experimented in bioconvective flows of nanofluids are the combined influences of thermal radiation, chemical reactions, and porous media. Jat et al. [6] studied the bioconvective and chemically reacting nanofluid induced by stretchable spongy surface. When applied to biomedical use, Ahmed et al. [7] considered the heat transport of hybrid nanofluid flow with bioconvection and reactive species acting induced by spongy curved surface. Aldandani et al. [8] applied the artificial neural network method in order to model the thermal performance of Reiner Philippoff nanofluid flow past a stretching curved surface. Under a wide range of physical influence, nanofluids have been studied to be of much interest because of their superior thermal conductivity, greater convective heat transfer, and flow behavior. Nevertheless, it is known that several industrial and biological fluids have non-Newtonian behaviors that cannot be precisely modeled using classical Newtonian physics. One of the most popular non-Newtonian fluid models is the Casson fluid model that is capable of modeling yield stress fluids like blood, polymer solutions, printing inks and some types of suspensions. The casson nanofluids therefore are a better model to explain complex transport phenomena in the process of industry and biomedical processes [9–11]. Pooja et al. [12] explored the nature of heat and mass transfer of Casson fluid flow with tri-hybrid nanoparticles in curved surface with special focus on its medical application. Their findings showed that hybrid nanoparticle dispersion could significantly improve thermal performance and curvature effects were very strong in determining transport characteristics. Saleem et al. [13] inspected the applications of heat transfer in non-Newtonian fluid flow through nonlinear curved sheet. They found out that curvature and non-Newtonian parameters are important in regulating irreversibility and thermal efficiency which is critical in designing the advanced heat transfer devices. By using Joule heating, thermal radiations, Dufour effect, and activation energy, Reddy and Goud [14] analyzed numerically the transportation of MHD Casson fluid on an exponentially stretching surface. Razzaq et al. [15] created an AI-optimized model to examine thermal transport in the Casson fluids including non-fourier double diffusion effects. Their findings affirmed their claim that intelligent modeling methods are able to give valid predictions and greater understanding of complex transport process in non-Newtonian fluid ecosystems. Electrically conductive fluids When an external magnetic field exists in an electrically conducting fluid, there exists Joule heating (Ohmic dissipation) as an inherent heat production process, as a result of electrical resistance. Joule heating is a major change in the distribution of temperature, and is important in processing of electromagnetic materials, nuclear reactors, and energy conversion systems. It has been demonstrated in recent research that failure to consider Joule heating may result in a lack of accuracy in calculating thermal energy especially in high magnetic environments [16, 17]. The impact of double diffusion and Soret mechanism on the electromagnetics braking of liquid metal trapped within an enclosed rotating cylindrical annulus was studied by Dash [18]. Salahuddin and Awais [19] studied the effect of thermal and solutal transport in the magnetohydrodynamic Williamson fluid flow through the Cattaneo-Christov heat and the effect of Joule heating. A numerical study of the Sisko fluid flow on a stretching surface including the effects of viscous dissipation and thermal radiation was done by Islam et al. [20]. They found that internal heat generating mechanisms, comprising Joule heating and viscous dissipation, considerably increased the temperature distribution within the fluid and a large decrease in the rates of heat transfer at the surfaces. Thermal radiation is another major part of heat transfer and is of concern especially in high temperature environments such as combustion systems, solar cell devices and aerospace engineering. Thermal radiation is incorporated into the thermal model to account for radiative heat flux, which becomes increasingly important at elevated temperatures. A number of researchers have reported that thermal radiation has significant influence on boosting the temperature field and thickness of thermal boundary layer during nanofluid flows [21–23]. In order to enhance the mass transport and the stability of nanoparticles bioconvection on the basis of gyrotactic motile microorganisms has been presented to the nanofluid systems. These microorganisms swim against gravity and this makes density gradient create convective movement. Bioconvection increases mixing of fluids, eliminates agglomeration of nanoparticles and increases efficiency of mass transfer. Motile microorganisms and nanoparticles interaction have introduced novel research patterns in bio-inspired heat transfer, microbial fuel cells, and bioreactors [24–26]. Mondal and Pal [27] premeditated the outcome of the activation energy on the magnetohydrodynamic bioconvective flow of carbon nanofluid over an inclined stretchable sheet. Ameen [28] studied the nature of hydrothermal and transport of chemical reaction on hybrid nanofluid by considering the effects of activation over an expanding surface. Sharaf and Saleh established a mathematical approach to the flow of the nanofluid of Carreau with quantum dots and motile microorganisms on a stretch sheet over a magnetized sheet [29]. Several recent studies have also contributed to the understanding of nanofluid flow and heat transfer. Banakar et al. [30] analyzed the impact of surface roughness, a horizontal magnetic field, and the nanoparticle shape factor on the flow characteristics of a ternary hybrid nanofluid using the Levenberg–Marquardt artificial neural network method. The off-centered stagnation-point flow of a chemically reactive fluid, along with particle transport and accumulation, was analyzed by Banakar et al. [31]. Kattimani et al. [32] studied the combined impacts of thermal diffusion and diffusion-thermo phenomenon in the presence of bioconvection using an artificial neural network (ANN) algorithm.

In recent years, more realistic and complete transport models have been developed by considering nanofluids, non-Newtonian fluid models, Joule heating, thermal radiation, and bioconvection. However, the literature on the flow of Casson nanofluid over the curved or stretchable surface with simultaneous impacts of Joule heating, activation energy, and bioconvection is quite limited. In the present investigation, a unified mathematical model is established to bridge this gap which facilitates the combined impacts of Joule heating, brownian motion, thermal radiation, activation energy, chemical processes, thermophoresis and gyrotactic motile microorganisms. Moreover, a systematic comparison between Newtonian and non-Newtonian (Casson) fluid behavior is carried out to highlight the role of yield stress on transport characteristics. The findings are expected to provide useful information for the design of future thermal systems, electromagnetic equipment, microfluidic devices, bioreactors and bio-inspired engineering applications.

Methodology

The governing equations for flow and heat transmission are expressed as a system of partial differential equations with adequate boundary conditions. These equations are turned into a set of ordinary differential equations by applying sufficient similarity variables. The resulting nonlinear ODE system and the boundary constraints are solved numerically using the bvp4c procedure provided in MATLAB. This solver is based on a finite difference method using the three-stage Lobatto IIIa collocation formulation [36] which is fourth order accurate and suitable for two-point boundary value issues. Before the bvp4c approach is used, higher-order ODEs are expressed as an analogous first-order system by creating new dependent variables. Results and discussion

This work is concerned with the analysis of combined effects of activation energy and thermal radiation along with Joule heating on Casson nanofluid flow over curved elongating sheet in presence of Brownian motion, magnetic effects, thermophoretic diffusion and gyrotactic microorganisms. Governing equations of Casson nanofluid model are derived and translated to a system of ODEs by suitable similarity variables. The above resulting ODEs, and the associated boundary conditions, are mathematically evaluated by the BVP4C package of MATLAB. In this part, the effects that various dimensionless variables have on microorganism density profiles, velocity, concentration, and temperature are discussed and indicated graphically. Temperature profile analysis

The change in the distribution of temperature (θ θ) versus the thermal radiation parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Rd}$$\end{document}Rd was shown in Fig. 7. The physical aspect of radiation parameter is the proportionality of radiative heat transfer to thermal conduction. Radiative processes are dominant in the energy exchange process in systems with high temperatures or where the material is optically thick, and hence this parameter is necessary to properly describe the temperature field. The impact of Brownian motion (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Nb}$$\end{document}Nb) on the temperature function (θ θ) is conveyed in Fig. 8. Physically, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Nb}$$\end{document}Nb is a measure of the random motion of nanoparticles due to thermal motion which is important in altering the mode of heat transfer. An upsurge in E leads to a gradual escalate in temperature, as higher activation energy requires more energy to initiate chemical reactions, resulting in additional thermal energy accumulation within the fluid. Although this effect is relatively modest compared to other parameters, activation energy plays a critical role in temperature-sensitive chemical systems such as combustion, polymer degradation, and biochemical reactions, where even small changes can significantly affect reaction rates and system performance over time. Its inclusion is therefore essential for accurate modeling of reactive flows in practical engineering applications. Conclusions

This paper has discussed the flow behaviour of a Casson nanofluid with swimming microbes flowing across a curved extending sheet. The main results of the research are:Curvature significantly controls flow and transport: The higher the curvature (A) the lower the velocity but the better the temperature, concentration, and microbe density. This makes curved surfaces useful for applications that require better mixing and heat transfer, such as curved heat exchangers and biomedical devices.Joule heating substantially elevates temperature: The Joule heating parameter considerably enhances the thermal field, which should be carefully handled in electromagnetic applications to avoid overheating, and provides potential for thermal control in energy systems.Magnetic field effectively regulates flow: The magnetic parameter M reduces velocity and increases skin friction, offering a practical mechanism for flow control and drag management in magnetohydrodynamic devices.Bioconvection parameters govern microorganism distribution: The motile Biot number β3, Peclet number Pe, and bioconvection Lewis number Lb critically control microorganism density, providing design levers for optimizing bioreactors, wastewater treatment, and biofuel production systems where microbial activity is essential.

Future research

Future work may extend the present model to include unsteady effects, three-dimensional flow, and variable fluid properties for more realistic applications. Additionally, the analysis can be extended to other non-Newtonian fluid models such as Williamson, Carreau, and Cross fluids for comparative study.

Limitations of the study

This study assumes steady-state and two-dimensional flow with constant fluid properties, which may not fully capture real transient or complex systems.

Airway clearance management for bronchiectasis in China: a multicentre cross-sectional study from the Chinese bronchiectasis registry (BE-China)
Authors: Yang Liu, Yi-Fan Wu, Yuan-Lin Song, Wei-Jie Guan, Xiang-Huai Xu, Hai-Wen Lu, Hai-Yan Ge, Rong-Chang Chen, Jie-Ming Qu, Jin-Fu Xu, BE-China Registry Investigators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundAirway clearance techniques (ACTs) are guideline-recommended in bronchiectasis, while contemporary patterns and determinants of ACTs adoption remain unclear in China.MethodsA multicentre cross-sectional study was conducted using data from the BE-China cohort between 10 Jan 2020 and 31 Mar 2024. Clinical characteristics and prespecified ACT modalities were collected across 92 hospitals. Multivariable logistic regression identified important associations of ACTs utilization.FindingsAmong 9647 patients, 12.2% (1177) used regular ACTs at baseline, and regional heterogeneity in ACTs utilization was observed. Moreover, ACTs users exhibited prolonged disease duration, a higher proportion of severe disease, greater comorbidity, heavier cough and sputum burden, and more frequent pharmacological and ventilatory interventions. Variables associated with increased ACTs utilization included comorbid burden (OR 1.47, 1.28–1.70; p < 0.0001), daily sputum (OR 1.68, 1.33–2.10; p < 0.0001), expectorants usage (OR 3.19, 2.74–3.71; p < 0.0001), inhaled antibiotics (OR 2.04, 1.35–3.07; p = 0.00065), macrolide therapy (OR 2.31, 1.78–2.99; p < 0.0001), other long-term oral antibiotics (OR 1.37, 1.13–1.66; p = 0.0013), noninvasive ventilation requirement (OR 4.11, 3.14–5.39; p < 0.0001), and disease duration (per SD increase, OR 1.08, 1.01–1.15; p = 0.027). Both BE-China and the similar European Bronchiectasis Registry (EMBARC) cohorts showed distinct etiological and comorbidity profiles, yet consistently revealed a higher medication burden among ACTs users.InterpretationIn China, ACTs utilization is primarily concentrated among patients with bronchiectasis who have a heavier symptom burden and greater treatment intensity. Clinicians may leverage the identified associations to optimize adherence and guide phenotype-targeted ACTs implementation strategies, and these findings may serve as a reference for future research.FundingNoncommunicable Chronic Diseases-National Science and Technology Major Project, National Natural Science Foundation of China, Innovation Program of Shanghai Municipal Education Commission, Program of Shanghai Municipal Science and Technology Commission, Major Project of Guangzhou National Laboratory.Keywords: Bronchiectasis, Airway clearance technique, BE-China registry, Associations

Research in context

Evidence before this study

International guidelines generally recommend ACTs as first-line maintenance therapy during the stable phase of bronchiectasis. Despite these strong endorsements, real-world adoption rates of ACTs remain persistently low, largely owing to the paucity of robust evidence from randomized controlled trials. To address this evidence gap and elucidate the extent of ACTs utilization in China, we systematically searched PubMed for articles published from database inception to June 18, 2025, using the terms (“Bronchiectasis”) AND (“Airway clearance management” OR “Airway clearance therapy” OR “Airway clearance techniques”) AND (“China” OR “Chinese”) without language restrictions. However, contemporary patterns and determinants of ACTs use in Chinese patients with bronchiectasis have not been systematically described. To our knowledge, this study represents the first large-scale, multicenter investigation to delineate ACTs utilization patterns in China and to identify key clinical associations linked to their use.

Added value of this study

Using data from the BE-China registry, we included a total of 9647 patients with available information on ACTs utilization, who were recruited from 92 centers across 22 provinces in China. Overall, only 12.2% (1177) of patients reported regular use of ACTs. Even among those with daily sputum production, the proportion was recorded at 13.5% (1060/7834). Marked geographic variation was observed in ACTs utilization across Chinese provinces. Compared with non-users, patients with bronchiectasis receiving regular ACTs exhibited higher symptom burden and greater disease severity. In multivariable analysis, prolonged disease duration, greater comorbidity burden, worse symptom profiles, and concomitant use of expectorants or antibiotics were associated with increased likelihood of ACTs adoption. When comparing across cohorts, BE-China and EMBARC patients displayed divergent etiological and comorbidity profiles; however, in both registries, regular ACTs users consistently exhibited a higher burden of concomitant medication compared with non-users.

Implications of all the available evidence

This study represents the first comprehensive analysis of ACTs utilization patterns for bronchiectasis in China, systematically delineating both facilitators and barriers to sustained ACTs adherence. We found that ACTs were predominantly prescribed for patients with higher symptom burden or greater treatment intensity, consistent with the 2023 ERS statement emphasizing prioritization of ACTs in severe cases to reduce exacerbation risk and alleviate symptoms. The significance of this work is providing real-world data on ACTs utilization and its associated factors for bronchiectasis in China, which fills a critical gap in the global literature where such large-scale, multicenter data from the Chinese patients have been lacking. And evidence from our study will be crucial for developing targeted strategies to guide phenotype-aligned ACTs implementation, and improve treatment adherence, particularly for populations with limited access to specialized care. Additionally, these findings highlight the pressing need for high-quality clinical trials to determine which ACTs deliver optimal clinical benefits in specific bronchiectasis subpopulations.

Introduction

Bronchiectasis is a chronic airway disease characterized by irreversible bronchial dilation.1,2 Rising global prevalence of the disease imposes a substantial economic burden on patients and society.3 The core pathological features of bronchiectasis include impaired mucociliary clearance and a vicious cycle of infection and inflammation, leading to mucus retention, recurrent infections, and progressive decline in lung function.4,5 Notably, airway mucus retention is a potential marker of disease severity and future risk of exacerbations in patients with bronchiectasis.6 Therefore, multiple international guidelines have recognized airway mucus clearance as a key component in the management of the disease.7, 8, 9, 10 Airway clearance management refers to a range of physical or pharmacological interventions aimed at promoting sputum expectoration.11 These strategies were designed to interrupt the “vicious vortex” model by enhancing mucus mobility and cough effectiveness, thereby improving ventilation and reducing the risk of infections.2 Studies have demonstrated that ACTs can enhance sputum clearance and shorten hospitalization duration in bronchiectasis.12, 13, 14 Therefore, ACTs were recommended as first-line maintenance therapy during stable disease phase in some international guidelines, including those issued by the British Thoracic Society and the European Respiratory Society, although the overall quality of evidence was relatively low due to the lack of randomized controlled trials.8,10 Data from the EMBARC cohort showed that airway clearance management was utilized regularly by 52.3% (8739/16,723) of patients with bronchiectasis across Europe, with significant geographical heterogeneity.15 These disparities may be closely related to uneven distribution of medical resources and limited patient awareness.

To date, large-scale data on the contemporary utilization patterns and determinants of ACTs in Chinese patients remain unclear. This study aimed to compare baseline characteristics between ACTs users and non-users and identify associations linked to ACTs adoption.

Methods

Study design and participants

The BE-China registry is a prospective, multicenter, observational cohort study platform that enrolled patients with radiologically confirmed bronchiectasis in China.16,17 Patient enrollment began in January 2020 and continues on an ongoing basis. The BE-China had been registered in the clinical trials registry (www.clinicaltrials.gov) with an identifier of NCT03643653. We conducted a multicentre cross-sectional study using data from the BE-China cohort between 10 Jan 2020 and 31 Mar 2024. All patients have signed the informed consent. Eligible patients met the following criteria: age ≥ 18 years; evidence of bronchiectasis affecting at least one pulmonary lobe on a chest high resolution computed tomography (HRCT) scan performed within the previous year; clinical symptoms consistent with bronchiectasis, including chronic cough, daily sputum, or a history of exacerbations; and remaining clinically stable upon recruitment. Exacerbations were defined based on the international consensus as a deterioration involving three or more of the following symptoms, lasting for at least 48 h and requiring an immediate modification of routine therapy: worsening cough; increased sputum volume, increased sputum purulence, or both; dyspnea and/or exercise intolerance; fatigue and/or malaise; and hemoptysis.18 Patients with exacerbations were eligible for enrollment in the registry at least 4 weeks after the discontinuation of antibiotic therapy. Exclusion criteria included: confirmed diagnosis of cystic fibrosis, traction bronchiectasis secondary to interstitial lung disease or other pulmonary diseases, and inability or unwillingness to provide informed consent.17 Details of the baseline characteristics of the whole BE-China cohort have been published previously.16 Detailed list of participated centers has been provided in the Supplementary Table S1.

Ethics approval

The study received central ethical approval from the ethical committee in Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital (approval number K18-167; approval date 14/08/2018). All collaborating centres received approvals from their local ethics committees.

Data collection

We collected clinical data from patients within the BE-China registry. At each participating center, comprehensive clinical data and diagnostic test results were systematically collected. Etiology of disease was initially documented by the treating physician and subsequently reassessed using all available diagnostic evidence. All data were captured via standardized online questionnaires to ensure consistency and reliability across centers. Collected clinical variables encompassed demographics, comorbidities, pharmacological treatments, microbiology testing, radiographic findings, lung function, and medical history. Respiratory symptoms were defined as daily cough, daily sputum, or a history of hemoptysis. Dyspnea was assessed using the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scale. Airflow obstruction was defined as a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.7. Preserved ratio impaired spirometry (PRISm) was characterized by FEV1 < 80% of predicted with an FEV1/FVC ≥ 0.7. Normal spirometry required both FEV1 and FVC ≥ 80% of predicted.16 Health-related quality of life and symptom burden were assessed using the Bronchiectasis Health Questionnaire (BHQ) and Quality of Life–Bronchiectasis Respiratory Symptom Scale (QoL-B-RSS), respectively.19,20 Radiological severity was quantified via the modified Reiff score, and disease severity was assessed with the Bronchiectasis Severity Index (BSI) score.21 Frequent exacerbation phenotype referred to patients with three or more acute exacerbations per year. We extracted relevant variables from the central database of the BE-China cohort, and trained personnel calculated the BSI and Reiff scores for each patient individually. Long-term macrolide therapy was defined as macrolide antibiotic use for at least three months. BE-China registry was developed in collaboration with the EMBARC and collected identical data points using the same definitions, thus enabling direct comparative analyses to identify regional differences in clinical characteristics and management strategies.

To standardize data collection, predefined options for ACTs were incorporated into the electronic case report forms (eCRFs) in the BE-China registry. The checklist was developed by the BE-China Steering Committee, which included respiratory physiotherapists. For the purposes of this study, ACTs options listed in the CRF included: postural drainage, chest percussion, active cycle of breathing techniques (ACBT), regular exercise, airway clearance devices (ACDs), and none. Although exercise was not strictly classified as an ACT, it was included based on patient-reported utilization for aiding mucus clearance. For expectorant treatment, the CRF options included: ambroxol, carbocisteine/N-acetylcysteine, GeloMyrtol forte, and none. To meet the registry’s definition of routine use, participants were required to indicate the use of the technique/device/medication during clinically stable periods. Data on ACTs frequency or duration were not collected within the registry.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages (%), and intergroup comparisons were performed using the Pearson χ2 test. Specifically, Yates’ continuity correction was applied to 2 × 2 contingency tables to account for continuity bias, while the standard Pearson χ2 test was used for variables with multiple categories. In instances where expected cell counts were less than 5, Fisher’s exact test was employed. Continuous variables were summarized as mean (standard deviation, SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR) based on the normality of distribution, and were compared using Student's t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, respectively. Multivariable logistic regression was conducted to identify factors independently associated with ACTs utilization. Variables selection for the final models followed a hybrid strategy integrating a priori clinical relevance with statistical screening. Firstly, variables with established clinical plausibility or significance based on prior literature (e.g., age, sex, frequent exacerbations and antibiotic usage) were included independent of univariate statistical thresholds, while additional covariates with potential association in univariate analysis (defined as p < 0.10) were also considered. Missing data were common for certain tests that were not routinely performed (e.g., microbiological assays, lung function parameters) in clinical practice. All data included in each analysis was described in Supplementary Table S2. Given high missingness (>50%) observed in BSI score and lung function parameters, which posed selection bias risk in complete-case analysis, a hierarchical modeling strategy was adopted. To maximize sample size and representativeness, primary multivariable analysis focused on covariates with <20% missingness (e.g., demographics, comorbidity, exacerbations, therapies, etc.). Sensitivity analyses were subsequently performed to specifically evaluate the impact of disease severity while managing missing data. These included a complete-case analysis incorporating the BSI score (or its individual components) and an analysis employing a “missing indicator” method, where missing values for categorical variables were treated as a distinct category to preserve the full sample size. All selected variables were simultaneously included in the multivariable analysis using the forced entry method. The robust sample size and adequate outcome events ensured sufficient statistical power for this comprehensive adjustment strategy. Collinearity was assessed using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) and Spearman correlation coefficient (r), with no severe multicollinearity defined as VIF < 5 and |r| < 0.7. Results were presented as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p < 0.05. Given the large sample size, p-values were interpreted with caution. Emphasis was placed on the magnitude of the effect estimates (ORs) and the precision of the 95% CIs to determine clinical relevance. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Chicago, IL, USA), while graphical visualizations were generated with OriginPro 2019 (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA).

Results

Current practice of ACTs for bronchiectasis in the BE-China cohort

At the time of this study, the overall size of the BE-China cohort registry was 10,324 patients, of whom 9647 (93.4%) were finally included in the analysis strictly based on the completeness of their ACTs utilization data. Demographics and clinical characteristics were summarized in Table 1. The cohort had a median age of 64 years (IQR 54–72), with 5615 (58.2%) females and a median disease duration of 7.4 years (IQR 4.2–15.5). Post-infection was the predominant etiology (n = 4080, 43.7%), while cardiovascular diseases constituted the most common comorbidity (n = 1265, 13.1%). Despite 81.5% (7834) of the patients reporting daily sputum production, only 27.3% (2626) received expectorants, 3.9% (372) received long-term macrolides, and 1.6% (151) used inhaled antibiotics. Within the BE-China cohort, only 1177 (12.2%) employed at least one form of ACTs, and 389 (4.0%) reported more than one ACT modality. Etiological distributions differed between the groups of ACTs users and non-users (Fig. 1A). Compared with non-users, ACTs users exhibited longer disease duration (10.3 years (4.6–20.7) vs. 6.9 years (4.0–15.4)), higher proportion of patients with severe disease (62.4% (412/660) vs. 53.5% (2116/3959)), greater comorbid burden (46.2% (544) vs. 31.4% (2658)), and worse symptom profiles, including daily sputum production (90.3% (1060/1174) vs. 80.2% (6774/8443)) and cough (94.1% (1105/1174) vs. 88.4% (7463/8443)). Additionally, ACTs users were also more likely to exhibit a frequent exacerbation phenotype (18.1% (210/1158) vs. 11.6% (938/8099)), airway obstruction (50.5% (326/645) vs. 41.6% (1770/4258)) and increased reliance on pharmacological and ventilatory interventions (Fig. 1B–E, Table 1). Although some other variables varied between ACTs users and non-users, the magnitude of these difference was small and should be interpreted with clinical context in mind. To provide broader context, we compared our cohort data with the EMBARC cohort, which included 16,723 patients from 28 countries in Europe and Israel,15 as detailed in Supplementary Table S3. Patients in both cohorts exhibited similar clinical profiles between the airway clearance related intervention and non-intervention groups, with a greater disease burden observed among intervention recipients. Key inter-cohort differences included divergent demographic characteristics, etiological distributions and comorbidity trends. Concomitant use of inhaled corticosteroids and antibiotics were significantly more frequent among ACTs users in both cohorts, which was in line with heavier symptom burden in this population.

Geographic variation in the utilization of ACTs among bronchiectasis in China

Geospatial analysis revealed marked regional heterogeneity in ACTs utilization across China. The highest use rates of ACTs occurred in Shandong (68.2%, 60/88), Beijing (36.4%, 86/236) and Ningxia (27.0%, 10/37), while the lowest use rates were observed in Sichuan (1.7%, 2/116), Liaoning (1.1%, 1/94) and Yunnan (0.5%, 1/190) (Figs. 2 and 3A). Overall, patients residing in central and northern China exhibited higher ACTs adoption compared to those in southern regions, with significantly lower adoption in southwestern provinces. Substantial variation also existed in ACT modalities employed within the BE-China cohort. Among 1177 patients receiving ACTs, traditional methods remained predominant, with manual chest percussion (40.9%, 728/1778) and regular exercise (27.3%, 486/1778) being the most frequently reported, followed by postural drainage (21.3%, 378/1778). Advanced techniques such as the ACBT were rarely adopted (0.6%, 10/1778), and ACDs constituted 9.9% (176/1778) of the reported approaches. At the provincial level, distinct patterns of technique preference emerged. Manual percussion was prevalent in Chongqing, Shanghai, and Anhui; whereas regular exercise predominated in Zhejiang, Ningxia, and Hubei; and postural drainage was favored in Beijing, Jiangxi, and Shandong. Notably, Hunan showed relatively concentrated use of ACDs, whereas interpretability of ACDs metrics was limited in Liaoning and Yunnan due to suboptimal overall ACTs utilization (Fig. 3B).

Geographic variation in expectorants utilization for bronchiectasis in China

In the present study, although 81.5% (7834/9617) of patients with bronchiectasis reported daily sputum production, the overall expectorant utilization rate was 27.3% (2626/9636). Notably, among patients receiving ACTs, the expectorant use rate increased substantially to 55.0% (647). Geographic disparities in expectorant utilization across China were depicted in Fig. 4A, with the highest rates observed in Shandong (71.6%, 63/88), Hunan (38.3%, 239/624), and Beijing (36.9%, 87/236), and the lowest in Shanxi (8.1%, 3/37), Anhui (7.6%, 11/144), and Sichuan (3.4%, 4/116). Among the three major categories of expectorants recorded, GeloMyrtol forte was the most commonly prescribed, accounting for 43.5% (1420/3263) of total expectorant reports, followed by carbocisteine/N-acetylcysteine 39.4% (1287/3263) and Ambroxol 17.0% (556/3263). In addition, distinct regional variations in prescribing patterns were evident. GeloMyrtol forte was predominantly used in Anhui, Liaoning, and Hunan, whereas carbocisteine/N-acetylcysteine was prevalent in Ningxia, Chongqing, Sichuan, Shanxi, and Guizhou. Although ambroxol usage was generally limited nationwide, relatively higher rates were noted in Guangxi, Yunnan, and Guangdong (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

It is widely recommended in international guidelines to use ACTs for disease management of bronchiectasis.7, 8, 9, 10 To our knowledge, this study represented the first comprehensive analysis of ACTs utilization patterns for bronchiectasis in China, systematically identifying facilitators and barriers to sustained ACTs adherence. Base on the BE-China registry covering 92 centers across 22 provinces in China, only 12.2% (1177) of patients reported regular ACTs utilization. Even when restricted to those with daily sputum production, the proportion remained similarly low at 13.5% (1060/7834). Notably, ACTs were confined primarily to patients with bronchiectasis who had prominent symptoms and greater treatment intensity. Our work provides novel real-world evidence on ACTs utilization patterns in Chinese patients, and data derived from this study can serve as a reference for the design of future clinical trials.

Data from international surveys, audits, and registries revealed substantial global heterogeneity in the use of ACTs among patients with bronchiectasis. In a study from the Bronchiectasis Research Registry (BRR), 63.8% (577/905) of patients reported ACTs utilization in the United States.22 Similar rates were observed in Australian (Australian Bronchiectasis Registry (ABR):57.7%, 266/461).23 However, the relatively small sample sizes in the United States and Australian cohorts may limit the generalizability of these findings. Collectively, these data highlighted that, despite guideline recommendations, ACTs adoption in China exhibited a marked disparity from that observed in the United States, and Australia. Multiple factors may affect the use of ACTs. The EMBARC registry identified that a primary barrier to regular ACTs use was clinicians’ perception that ACTs were not necessary.15 Other possible contributors to variations in implementation included the lack of clear guidance for selecting optimal ACT modalities in bronchiectasis, limited access to specialist care or ACT devices, and regional disparities in education and training on ACTs implementation.24, 25, 26, 27 Despite the association of ACTs with improved quality of life and symptom, and potentially fewer exacerbations, no specific ACT modality had demonstrated clear superiority, thus their application should be personalized.10,13,28 ACTs is generally recommended for bronchiectasis in multiple international guidelines, while the evidence base supporting the effectiveness of ACTs remains limited, and it does not represent a strong consensus endorsed unanimously by experts. Although the 2025 updated bronchiectasis guidelines issued by the ERS strongly recommend ACTs for the majority of patients with bronchiectasis, and even expand the indication population from “patients with chronic cough and sputum production” (as specified in the 2017 version) to include certain dry cough patients, especially those with mucus plugging evidenced by chest CT, and stated that instruction in ACTs may also assist patients during periods of increased symptoms.10 The recommendation is assigned a low level of evidence due to the paucity of high-quality randomized controlled trials (RCTs), which is precisely the key factor contributing to the varying preferences among clinicians and the substantial global heterogeneity in ACTs adoption observed in clinical practice.23,29 Therefore, there is an urgent need for high-quality clinical trials to evaluate the long-term clinical outcomes of ACTs based interventions in bronchiectasis management.

We observed geographic heterogeneity in ACTs adoption, with higher rates in central and northern China, and lower rates in the southwest, alongside geographic mismatch in ACTs and expectorant utilization. ACTs are non-pharmacological interventions targeting airway mucus hypersecretion, mainly indicated for bronchiectasis and other mucus-overproducing chronic airway diseases, while expectorants are broad-spectrum respiratory medications whose utilization correlates with regional disease burden and clinical prescribing habits. Therefore, above variations may be driven by differences in intervention application scopes, regional priorities for specialized disease management, and clinical practice preferences. Specifically, regions with relatively higher ACTs utilization are primarily shaped by early standardized bronchiectasis management training, optimized allocation of specialized clinicians, and formalized clinical protocols for mucus clearance. Shandong exemplifies this, as its early implementation of standardized training programs in recent years. Notably, even top-tier medical hubs with comparable resources (e.g., Beijing, Shanghai) exhibit divergent ACTs utilization, potentially reflecting north-south variations in clinical decision-making paradigms and patient treatment preference, factors increasingly recognized as critical to ACTs adherence in bronchiectasis management.30 Conversely, despite robust economic and medical resources, Chongqing demonstrates relatively low ACTs utilization, as local respiratory subspecialty resources are disproportionately allocated to diseases with higher burden and greater clinical attention (e.g., COPD, lung cancer), thereby deprioritizing standardized treatment for bronchiectasis. Meanwhile, expectorants utilization may be driven by other factors, such as its widespread use in COPD treatment.31 Similarly, high expectorants utilization in Hunan may stem from a large COPD patient population and the resultant clinical prescribing preferences, a pattern potentially distinct from that of bronchiectasis-specific ACTs management. Notably, although the overall sample size of this study was large, provincial enrollment varied substantially. Consequently, estimates derived from provinces with smaller cohorts may lack representativeness. It should be noted that direct objective evidence linking specific regional factors to ACTs utilization remains limited. Therefore, these explanations should be viewed as contextual hypotheses rather than causal relationships, and further prospective studies are needed to clarify the underlying drivers of these geographic disparities.

Data from the EMBARC and BRR studies indicated that frequent exacerbations, Pseudomonas aeruginosa colonization, advanced disease severity, antibiotic usage, and prior hospitalizations due to pulmonary exacerbations were associated with ACTs utilization in patients with bronchiectasis.15,22 Consistent with previous findings, our study revealed that longer disease duration, comorbidity burden, daily sputum production, expectorants or antibiotic therapy, and noninvasive ventilation dependence were associated with increased likelihood of ACTs use among bronchiectasis in China. Given the marked heterogeneity of bronchiectasis, these findings provide large-scale Asian data that complement existing international cohort evidence. Although we adjusted for confounders and addressed statistical collinearity, these identified factors likely represent different manifestations of underlying disease severity or disease burden. Thus, these should be interpreted as interrelated clinical features rather than completely independent risk factors. Our findings highlight a clinical portrait of patients with bronchiectasis who receive ACTs, rather than isolating a single driver of ACTs utilization. Besides, given that dry bronchiectasis is a typical phenotype of bronchiectasis in the Asian population,32 stratified analysis of our cohort further corroborated this trend. Among patients with dry bronchiectasis phenotype, defined as no sputum production or daily sputum production of less than 10 ml, the ACTs utilization rate was only 8.1% (332/4111), whereas the rate reached 15.4% (840/5461) among those with non-dry bronchiectasis phenotype. This phenotype-specific utilization feature may also explain why the overall ACTs utilization rate in China was much lower than that in other countries, and provide a more refined understanding of current treatment practices, helping clinicians recognize the gaps between real-world application and guideline recommendations. We emphasize the urgent need to enhance clinician awareness of phenotype-specific ACTs indication and develop patient-centered education strategies to promote individualized ACTs utilization. This will help shift the current practice from reactive crisis management to phenotype-adapted disease control. Enhancing patients’ understanding of the personalized relevance of ACTs to their specific clinical phenotype is essential to promoting sustained adherence when such interventions are clinically warranted. To date, most studies have focused narrowly on evaluating the efficacy of single ACT modalities.12,33, 34, 35 Future research should prioritize assessing the effectiveness of combined ACTs strategies through the application of two or more techniques in patients with distinct bronchiectasis phenotypes. Evidence generated from such studies would elucidate the clinical utility of real-world ACTs regimens, overcome adherence barriers through personalized strategies, and ultimately guide individualized ACTs management strategies for bronchiectasis in clinical practice.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, our analysis was restricted to BE-China registry patients who had complete ACTs data, and additional substantial missingness in lung function and BSI scores further reduced the sample size available for disease severity-focused complete-case analysis. The exclusion of patients with incomplete data may compromise the generalizability of findings to the full registry cohort and broader bronchiectasis population in China. However, rigorous sensitivity analyses corroborated our findings, confirming the robustness of core conclusions against attrition bias. Secondly, in this large-scale registry study, statistical significance did not equate to clinical imperative, findings should be interpreted by clinical significance rather than isolated p-values alone. Lastly, the absence of standardized international terminology for ACTs may have introduced classification inconsistencies across reporting sites, potentially confounding the interpretation of technique-specific utilization data, and compromised the full reliability of ACTs utilization reporting.

Conclusion

Our study showed that 12.2% (1177) of patients with bronchiectasis reported regular ACTs usage in the BE-China cohort. Key factors including prolonged disease duration, comorbid burden, worse symptom profiles, concomitant use of expectorant or antibiotic agents and noninvasive ventilation dependence were associated with an increased likelihood of ACTs adoption. Integrating these associations into clinical practice may guide more individualized ACTs implementation strategies, and these findings may serve as a valuable reference for future researches and contribute to a global understanding of airway clearance management in bronchiectasis.

Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “Strengthening Causal Interpretation: Home Blood Pressure Phenotypes and Cardiovascular Remodeling in Hemodialysis”
Authors: Meng-Kan Chen, Chih-Cheng Wu
Published: 2026

The Author Replies:

We thank Li and Dong1 for their thoughtful letter, “Strengthening Causal Interpretation: Home Blood Pressure Phenotypes and Cardiovascular Remodeling in Hemodialysis,” regarding our article, “Home Blood Pressure Phenotypes and Cardiovascular Remodeling in Hemodialysis.”2 They raise important issues regarding causal inference, environmental stressors, genetic heterogeneity, and dialysis treatment factors.

We agree that our cross-sectional design precludes determination of temporal sequence or causality. Our aim was not to prove that specific blood pressure phenotypes cause cardiovascular remodeling, but to determine whether home blood pressure–based phenotyping identifies clinically meaningful differences in structural cardiovascular burden. The use of standardized 7-day home blood pressure monitoring, objective echocardiographic and vascular assessments, and consistent findings across alternative blood pressure thresholds support the robustness of our central observation—dialysis-unit blood pressure alone may misclassify blood pressure burden, whereas home blood pressure phenotypes identify distinct remodeling patterns.

We also agree that seasonality, hydration status, and thermal response may influence blood pressure and cardiovascular risk in hemodialysis.3 We attempted to reduce unstable volume-related confounding by excluding patients with poorly controlled fluid volume status, recent dry-weight or antihypertensive medication changes, symptomatic heart failure, or recent hospitalization. Interdialytic weight gain and dialysis-related covariates were also considered. Nevertheless, residual confounding by volume status, temperature, and seasonal variation cannot be excluded and should be incorporated in future longitudinal studies.

The suggestion to consider genetic heterogeneity is scientifically reasonable. However, apolipoproteinL1 G1/G2 kidney-risk variants are largely concentrated in individuals with recent sub-Saharan African ancestry and are absent or extremely rare in Chinese and broader East Asian populations.4 Therefore, apolipoproteinL1-related risk stratification is less directly applicable to our regionally derived Taiwanese cohort, although future multiethnic studies incorporating genetic markers remain important.

Finally, dialysis modality and treatment intensity deserve further investigation. Our cohort was restricted to thrice-weekly maintenance hemodialysis, reducing schedule heterogeneity but limiting evaluation of modality-specific effects.5 More data on hemodiafiltration, dialysate temperature, ultrafiltration strategy, dry-weight probing, medication adherence, and antihypertensive treatment intensity should be incorporated into future prospective studies.

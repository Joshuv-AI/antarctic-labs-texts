Inner approximations of doubling weights with applications to Beurling-Malliavin theory in Toeplitz kernels
Alex Bergman

Abstract
Let f f be a strictly increasing smooth function, such that f ′ f^{\prime} is comparable to a weight α ′ \alpha^{\prime} which is locally doubling and satisfies a non-triviality condition to be explained in the paper. We construct a meromorphic inner function J J , such that f − arg ⁡ ( J ) f-\arg(J) is bounded and arg ⁡ ( J ) ′ \arg(J)^{\prime} is comparable to α ′ \alpha^{\prime} up to polynomial loss. We give two applications of this result. The first is a sufficient density condition for a set Λ \Lambda to be a zero set for a Toeplitz kernel with real analytic and unimodular symbol. Our second application is to describe a class of admissible Beurling-Malliavin majorants in model spaces. The generality considered here lets us treat most cases of model spaces generated by one-component inner functions.

Introduction

An inner function is a holomorphic map
\Theta:\mathbb{C}_{+}\to\mathbb{D}
which is maximal in the sense that

\lim_{y\to 0}\lvert\Theta(x+iy)\rvert=1,\text{ for almost every }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Inner functions play a central role in the theory of Banach and Hilbert spaces of analytic functions and, in particular, with its connections to harmonic analysis and spectral theory in one variable. Let us give a brief sketch of how they arise in the theory of Schrödinger operators. Let
V\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,b)
, where
0<b\leq\infty
and consider the eigenvalue problem

-\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,z)+V(x)u(x,z)=zu(x,z),\text{ for }x\in(0,b),\;z\in\mathbb{C}.

(1)

We suppose that the left end-point is regular (i.e.
V
is integrable at
0
), but we allow the right end-point to be singular. We fix a separated self-adjoint boundary condition at
b
. For example in the limit point case this means that we assume our solution belongs to
L^{2}
and if
b<\infty
and
V
is integrable on all
(0,b)
we may choose, for example,
u^{\prime}(b,z)=0
(Dirichlet) or
u^{\prime}(b,z)=0
(Neumann). The Weyl
m
-function is then

m(z)=\frac{u^{\prime}(0,z)}{u(0,z)},\;z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R},

where the derivative is in
x
. It is well-known that
m
maps the upper half-plane into itself and hence

\Theta(z)=\frac{m(z)-i}{m(z)+i},

maps into the unit disc. It turns out that
\Theta
is an inner function if and only if the spectrum is purely singular. Moreover, the solution

\omega(x,z)=\frac{u(x,z)}{u(0,z)+iu(0,z)},

does not depend on the solution
u
and the map

Uf(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{b}f(x)\omega(x,z)dx,\text{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

is a unitary map from
L^{2}(0,b)
onto the model space
K_{\Theta}=H^{2}\ominus\Theta H^{2}
(to be defined shortly). Indeed, the reproducing kernel for the space
\left\{Uf:f\in L^{2}(0,b)\right\}
is given by the formula

K(\lambda,z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{b}\omega_{\overline{\lambda}}(x)\omega_{z}(x)dx=\frac{1-\overline{\Theta(\lambda)}\Theta(z)}{2\pi i(z-\overline{\lambda})}.

Thus this procedure transforms basis problems and spectral problems for Schrödinger equations to problems in complex function theory. The most well-known example of this approach is the work of Khrushchev, Nikolski, and Pavlov on Pavlov’s solution to the Riesz basis problem for exponentials, see the classical paper
[
12
]
. This method has recently found great success in the inverse spectral theory, see for example
[
6
,
23
,
16
]
. Let us also mention that this program is realizable for more general equations, such as canonical systems, see for example
[
17
,
2
,
24
]
.

In this article we shall be concerned only with those inner functions which extend to meromorphic functions in the whole complex plane. In the language of spectral theory this corresponds to the assumption of the resolvent of the self-adjoint operator associated with equation (
1
) being compact. Meromorphic inner functions are characterized by their Nevanlinna factorization being of the form

\Theta(z)=B(z)S_{a}(z),

where
B
is a Blaschke product whose zeros
\left\{z_{n}\right\}
accumulate only at infinity and
S_{a}(z)=\exp(iaz)
, for some
a\geq 0
. Since
\Theta
is unimodular on the real-line we may write

\Theta(x)=\exp(i\arg(\Theta)(x)).

Note that the argument of a meromorphic inner function on the real line can be chosen to be a real-analytic and increasing function. It is unique if we assume that it is continuous and fix the value at the origin to be zero (which we do from now on). Its derivative can be expressed in terms of the Nevalinna factorization as follows

\arg(\Theta)^{\prime}(x)=a+2\sum_{n}\frac{y_{n}}{(x-x_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2}},\text{ for }z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}.

Central problems in the theory of analytic functions spaces include the distribution of zeros. A typical problem encountered in the theory is that of approximating a smooth and strictly increasing map
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by the argument of a meromorphic inner function,
\Theta
, see the articles
[
25
,
22
]
which are dedicated to this problem and the articles
[
14
,
15
,
21
,
20
]
where such an approximation plays a direct and decisive role. If one settles for the requirement that
f-\arg(\Theta)
be bounded this is not a difficult problem. However, typically one is also required to control the derivative
\arg(\Theta)^{\prime}(x)
for real
x
by a quantity related to the derivative of
f
. Our main result allows one to do this in the case that
f^{\prime}
behaves like
\alpha^{\prime}
for some
\alpha
which is the argument of a meromorphic inner function and such that the measure

\mu_{\alpha}(I)=\int_{I}\alpha^{\prime}(x)dx,

is a local doubling measure. In fact, we shall also obtain more general results when the measure (not necessarily analytic) satisfies a local doubling condition (to be stated shortly).

Since it may be quite difficult to see the applicability of such a result for the non-expert we include two applications of our result. The first is a general criterion for a set
\Lambda
to be a zero set in a Toeplitz kernel generated by a unimodular and real-analytic symbol. This includes model and de Branges spaces. The second concerns the construction of a model space function
f
which is majorized on the real-line by a given weight function
\omega
. This is in the theme of the Beurling-Malliavin majorant theorem which looks for weights which have a non-trivial minorant with Fourier spectrum contained in a fixed interval. We begin with some terminology.

Let
H^{p}
denote the Hardy space of the upper half-plane,
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, consisting of all analytic functions subject to the growth condition

\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sup_{y>0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert f(x+iy)\rvert^{p}dx<\infty,\text{ for }1\leq p<\infty.

For
p=\infty
we let
H^{\infty}
denote the set of bounded analytic function in the upper half-plane. Functions in
H^{p}
have non-tangential boundary limits Lebesgue almost-everywhere on the real line. We freely identify an
H^{p}
function with its extension to the real-line. Moreover, the analytic function can be recovered from its boundary values by the means of the Poisson integral formula.

For an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
we denote by
2I
the interval with twice the length of
I
and the same center. A Borel measure
\mu
is called doubling if there exists a constant
C>0
, such that for all bounded interval
I
the inequality
\mu(2I)\leq C\mu(I)
is satisfied. It is called locally doubling if
\mu(2I)\leq C\mu(I)
, whenever
\mu(2I)\leq 1
. If
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly increasing we define the metric

d_{\alpha}(x,y)=\lvert\alpha(x)-\alpha(y)\rvert.

This will be the natural metric for our problems. If
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the argument of a meromorphic inner function then the measure
d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha^{\prime}(x)dx
is locally doubling if and only if one (and hence both) of the following conditions hold

(i)

There exists constants
M,m>0
, such that
m\alpha^{\prime}(y)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(x)\leq M\alpha^{\prime}(y)
, for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
d_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq 1
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
, such that
\lvert\alpha^{\prime\prime}(x)\rvert\leq C\alpha^{\prime}(x)^{2}
.

For a proof we refer to
[
18
]
. Let us also remark that Aleksandrov has shown that property
(ii)
characterizes the so-called
one-component
inner functions. More on this in the section on preliminaries. We take these properties to be the definition of a
regular locally doubling weight
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth strictly increasing map. We say that
\alpha
is a regular locally doubling weight if

(i)

there exists constants
M,m,R\geq 0
, such that
m\alpha^{\prime}(y)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(x)\leq M\alpha^{\prime}(y)
, for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
d_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq 1
and
\lvert x\rvert,\lvert y\rvert\geq R
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C,R>0
, such that
\lvert\alpha^{\prime\prime}(x)\rvert\leq C\alpha^{\prime}(x)^{2}
, for
\lvert x\rvert\geq R
.

Note that we only require the properties to hold away from the origin. The simplest examples of locally doubling weights are the power functions
\lvert x\rvert^{\kappa}
with
\kappa>-1
. We are now ready to state our main theorem. The asymptotic notation used is explained at the end of the introduction in the section on notation. We use the notation
\langle x\rangle=(1+x^{2})^{1/2}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth increasing function satisfying
f^{\prime}(x)\sim\alpha^{\prime}(x)
for some regular locally doubling weight
\alpha
satisfying

\langle x\rangle^{-\kappa}\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x),\text{ for some }\kappa<1.

Then there exists a meromorphic inner function
J
and a positive integer
N_{0}
, such that

\lvert f(x)-J(x)\rvert\leq 2\pi,\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R},

and

\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x)^{N_{0}}\langle x\rangle^{N_{0}},\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R},

and

\langle\lambda\rangle^{-N_{0}}\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda)^{-N_{0}}\lesssim\lvert J^{\prime}(\lambda)\rvert\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda.

1.1
Applications

Since the proof of the main construction will be quite technical we wish to describe our applications first. The two applications and their corresponding sections can be read independently of each other.

Zero sets in Toeplitz kernels

Let
P_{+}
denote the orthogonal projection from
H^{2}
onto
L^{2}
and
U\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. The Toeplitz operator with symbol
U
is the map

T_{U}:H^{2}\to H^{2},\;\;T_{U}f=P_{+}(Uf).

Toeplitz operators are ubiquitous in harmonic analysis, complex analysis, and operator theory. In this article we shall be concerned with their kernels. The kernel of the operator
T_{U}
is given by

N^{2}(U)=\left\{f\in H^{2}:\overline{Uf}\in H^{2}\right\}.

Toeplitz kernels are important in part because they include the so-called model subspaces and closely related de Branges spaces which generalize the classical Paley-Wiener space. Because of this connection and the connection between such spaces and spectral theory, as outlined above, Toeplitz kernels give a unified approach to study completeness problems of many systems of special functions in one variable, see for example
[
12
,
14
]
.

In this article we shall study the more general
L^{p}
Toeplitz kernels. We denote by
N^{+}
the Smirnoff class in the upper half-plane

\begin{split}N^{+}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}_{+}):f=h/g\text{, with }h,g\in H^{\infty}\text{, and }g\text{ outer}\right\},\\
\text{ where }\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\text{ is the set of analytic functions in }\mathbb{C}_{+}.\end{split}

In analogy with the case
p=2
we define the
p
-Toeplitz kernel of
U
as

N^{p}(U)=\left\{f\in H^{p}:\overline{Uf}\in H^{p}\right\},

and the Smirnoff Toeplitz kernel

N^{+}(U)=\left\{f\in N^{+}\cap L^{1}_{loc}:\overline{Uf}\in N^{+}\right\}.

Note that the elements in
N^{+}(U)
are assumed to be locally summable. This is is a natural condition when
U
has only one singularity (the one at infinity) and will make the elements real analytic if
U
is.

The problem which we consider is the problem of describing the discrete sets
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
which are zero sets in
N^{\infty}(U)
. In
[
15
]
Makarov and Poltoratski gave an answer to this problem (up to an
\epsilon
gap) for symbols of the form
U=\overline{\Theta}
with
\Theta
a meromorphic inner function satisfying
\arg(\Theta)^{\prime}\sim\lvert x\rvert^{\kappa}
, for some
\kappa\geq 0
. Their conditions are in terms of triviality (and non triviality) of certain Toeplitz kernels. Here we shall give a geometric condition in terms of an analogue of the usual Beurling density. Our result also allows for more general locally doubling weights. Although, the results are not as strong as in
[
15
]
they also require less work and may indicate that the results in
[
15
]
extend to a larger class of locally doubling weights.

We shall restrict our attention to unimodular and real analytic symbols of the form

U=\exp(i\arg(U)),\;\;\arg(U)(x)=-\alpha(x)+h(x),

with
\alpha
a real analytic locally doubling weight such that there exists
N,C,c>0
and
\kappa<1
, such that

c\langle x\rangle^{\kappa}<\alpha^{\prime}(x)<C\langle x\rangle^{N},

and
h
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous, or more generally is bounded and satisfies an estimate of the form

\lvert h^{\prime}(x)\rvert\leq C\langle x\rangle^{K},\text{ for some }K\geq 0.

In this case all elements of
N^{p}(U)
will be real analytic functions. This motivates the discreteness condition on
\Lambda
.

To formulate our main result we introduce the
\alpha
upper density for any smooth and strictly increasing function
\alpha
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
.
For such
\alpha
and
\Lambda
we define

D_{\alpha}^{+}(\Lambda)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha(I)=r}\frac{\#(\Lambda\cap I)}{r}.

If
\alpha=x
we obtain the usual Beurling upper density.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\arg(U)=-\alpha+h
,
\alpha,h
real-analytic. Suppose
\alpha
is a regular locally doubling measure satisfying

\langle x\rangle^{\kappa}\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x)\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K_{0}},\text{ for some }K_{0}\geq\kappa>-1.

and
h\in L^{\infty}
satisfies

\lvert h^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K_{1}},\text{ for some }K_{1}\geq 0.

Then for any subset of the line,
\Lambda
, such that
D_{\alpha}^{+}(\Lambda)<1/2\pi
. Then there exists
T\in N^{\infty}(U)
, such that

T(\lambda)=0,\;\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda,

and

\langle\lambda\rangle^{-N}\lesssim\lvert T^{\prime}(\lambda)\rvert,\;\;\lambda\in\Lambda.

We also show that the condition cannot be far from sharp in the sense that if the corresponding lower density is strictly greater than
1/2\pi
then the set cannot be a zero set. Moreover, we also obtain polynomial lower bounds on the derivative of the function
T
at the zeros which may prove useful when proving interpolation theorems.

Let us also mention the nice paper
[
18
]
which gives sharp interpolation and sampling results for de Branges spaces whose phase function is a doubling measure. In our setting this corresponds to the case
p=2
and
U=\overline{\Theta}
where
\Theta
is a meromorphic inner function satisfying a global doubling condition. In this case they construct a function similar to ours as well.

Beurling-Malliavin majorants in model spaces

We now turn to our second application. Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a measurable function. The classical Beurling-Malliavin admissible majorant problem is to find a non-trivial function
f\in L^{2}
with Fourier support in
(-a,a)
minorizing
\omega
, i.e.

\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq\omega(x),\text{ for almost every }x\in\mathbb{R}.

In this case
\omega
is called an admissible weight. Experience has shown that this is a very delicate problem. Write
\omega(x)=\exp(-\Omega(x))
. Then a simple necessary condition is the convergence of the logarithmic integral

\int\frac{\Omega(x)}{1+x^{2}}dx<\infty.

However, this condition is by no means sufficient. The well-known criterion of Beurling and Malliavin, from
[
5
]
, is that if in addition to the convergence of the logarithmic integral one assumes that
\Omega
is Lipschitz continuous, then
\omega
is an admissible weight. This result of Beurling and Malliavin is one of the most celebrated results in the field of uncertainty principles and has found many applications, for example in the fractal uncertainty principle of Bourgain and Dyatlov,
[
7
]
and in the work of Eremenko and Novikov on oscillation of high pass signals,
[
8
]
.

Since the class of functions with Fourier support in
(-a,a)
coincides with the model subspace
K_{S_{2a}}
(up to multiplication by
S_{-a}
) one is naturally led to study the majorant problem in more general model spaces. Let
\Theta
be an inner function. The model space
K_{\Theta}
is the orthogonal complement of
\Theta H^{2}
in
H^{2}
. In terms of Toeplitz kernels we have
K_{\Theta}=N^{2}(\overline{\Theta})
. The systematic study of majorant problems in model spaces was initiated by Havin and Mashreghi in
[
10
,
11
]
. This was also taken further by other authors in, for example,
[
4
,
19
,
3
]
. In fact, as was proven in
[
11
]
, the condition
\lVert\widetilde{\Omega}^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}<a/2
is strong enough to to conclude that
\omega
is an admissible weight. Other results in terms of the Hilbert transform of
\Omega
were also obtained for certain model spaces. Here we add to that theme.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Theta
be a meromorphic inner function and
\omega=\exp(-\Omega)
a positive function. Suppose

(i)

\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
,

(ii)

\widetilde{\Omega}\in C^{1}
,

(iii)

the function
\alpha=\arg(\Theta)+2\widetilde{\Omega}
is a regular locally doubling weight satisfying

1\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x)\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K_{0}},\text{ for some }K_{0}\geq 0.

Then
\omega
is an admissible majorant for
K_{\Theta}
. That is, there exists a non-trivial function
f\in K_{\Theta}
, such that

\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq\omega(x).

In particular this works whenever the function obeys a power type-law

\arg(\Theta)^{\prime}(x)+2\widetilde{\Omega}^{\prime}(x)\sim\langle x\rangle^{K},\text{ for some }K\geq 0.

1.2
Notation

Before we being let us fix some notation. We set

\langle x\rangle=(1+x^{2})^{1/2},

and use the asymptotic notation
a\lesssim b
to mean that there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

a(x)\leq Cb(x),

where
a
and
b
are two nonnegative functions. By
a\sim b
we mean
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
.

The Poisson measure is given by
d\mathbb{P}(t)=(1+t^{2})^{-1}dt
. Let
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
.

Our real conjugation operator (Hilbert transform) is

\tilde{u}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\text{p.v.}\int\left(\frac{1}{x-t}+\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)u(t)dt.

The Poisson integral is

Pu(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\frac{y}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}u(t)dt.

Our complex conjugation operator is

Qu(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\left(\frac{x-t}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}+\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)u(t)dt.

Our Schwartz integral is

\mathcal{S}u(z)=Pu(z)+iQu(z)=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)u(t)dt.

Preliminaries on Toeplitz kernels with real-analytic symbol

Let
\gamma\in C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
(the space of real analytic functions) and
V(x)=e^{i\gamma(x)}
. Recall that we define the Smirnoff Toeplitz kernel as

N^{+}(V)=\left\{f\in N^{+}:\overline{Vf}\in N^{+}\right\}.

First we show that functions in
N^{+}(V)
are real-analytic.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\gamma\in C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
and set
V=e^{i\gamma}
. Then
N^{+}(V)\subset C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in N^{+}(V)
. Set

F(z)=\begin{cases}f(z),\;\;z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\\
U^{-1}(z)(Uf)(z),\;\;z\in\Omega\end{cases},

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}_{-}
is a domain where
U
extends to a zero free analytic function and
(Uf)
it the extension of the function into the lower half-plane. Then since
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
the function
F
extends to an analytic function across the real-line by Morera’s theorem, see e.g. Lemma 1.10., on page 133, in
[
9
]
. Since
F(x)=f(x)
for almost every real
x
the result follows.
∎

An important class of unimodular symbols is given by the meromorphic inner functions. When
U=\overline{\Theta}
the Toeplitz kernel becomes the well-known model space,
N^{p}(U)=K_{\Theta}^{p}
. Choosing
U=S_{-2a}
we obtain

S_{a}N^{p}(S_{-2a})=S_{a}K_{S_{2a}}^{p}=PW_{a}^{p},

the Paley-Wiener space consisting of entire functions of exponential type at most
a
which are
p
-summable on the real-line. We note that the second equality is well-known, but not entirely obvious. Another important class of symbols are ones of the form
U=\overline{\Theta}J
, where
\Theta
and
J
are meromorphic inner functions.

Let us record the statement in the introduction about arguments of meromorphic inner functions which are locally doubling.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
\alpha
is the argument of a meromorphic inner function. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

\alpha^{\prime}(x)dx
is a locally doubling measure.

(ii)

\alpha^{\prime}(x)\sim\alpha^{\prime}(y)
whenever
d_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq 1
.

(iii)

\lvert\alpha^{\prime\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x)^{2}
.

Proof.

A proof is given in
[
18
]
.
∎

We mention that a theorem of Aleksandrov (Theorem 3.4. in
[
1
]
) shows that in the case of meromorphic inner functions condition
(iii)
characterizes the so-called one-component inner functions (under the condition that
\Theta
is not a finite Blaschke product). One-component inner functions are inner functions with the property that there exists
\epsilon>0
, such that the sub level set

\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}:\lvert\Theta(z)\rvert<\epsilon\right\},

is connected.

2.1
Two lemmas needed for the applications

The next two lemmas are needed for the applications. Similar statements are standard.

Lemma 3
.

Let
m\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
and
\log m\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
. Then
m\in\lvert N^{+}(V)\rvert
if and only if

2\widetilde{\log m}+\arg(V)=-\arg(I)+2\pi n,

for some meromorphic inner function
I
and measurable integer-valued function
n:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
m=\lvert G\rvert
for some
G\in N^{+}(V)
. Without loss of generality we may suppose that
G
is outer in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Moreover,
\overline{VG}\in N^{+}
, and hence there exists an inner function
I
, such that
\overline{VG}=IG
. Since
V
is real analytic the inner function
I
is a meromorphic inner function. Passing to the arguments on the real-line and using that
\log m=\log\lvert G\rvert
we obtain

2\widetilde{\log m}+\arg(V)=-\arg(I)+2\pi n,

where
n
is some measurable integer-valued function.

Conversely, if there exists a
I
and
n
as in the statement let

F(z)=\exp\left(P[\log m](z)+iQ[\log m](z)\right),\;\;z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Then
F
is outer in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
F\in N^{+}
. Moreover by assumption,

VF=e^{i\arg V+i\widetilde{\log m}}\lvert F\rvert=\overline{IF}\in N^{+}.

Thus
F\in N^{+}(V)
as claimed.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and suppose
\lvert f^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K}
, for some
K\geq 0
. Then
\widetilde{f}
is continuous and there exists a constant
C>0
(depending on
f
), such that

\lvert\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(x)\rvert\leq C(\log(1+\lvert x\rvert)+\langle x\rangle^{K}).

Proof.

We estimate

\left\lvert\int_{1\geq\lvert x-t\rvert>\delta}\frac{f(t)}{x-t}dt\right\rvert=\left\lvert\int_{1\geq\lvert s\rvert>\delta}\frac{f(x-s)-f(x)}{s}ds\right\rvert\leq 2(1+\lvert x\rvert^{K}).

Now suppose
t>x>0
, then

\frac{1}{x-t}+\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}=\frac{1+xt}{(x-t)(1+t^{2})}\leq 0.

Thus for
x>0
we may estimate

\begin{split}\left\lvert\int_{x+1}^{\infty}f(t)\left(\frac{1}{x-t}+\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)dt\right\rvert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\int_{x+1}^{\infty}-\left(\frac{1}{x-t}+\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)dt\\
=\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}2^{-1}\log(1+(x+1)^{2}).\end{split}

Similarly if
x>0
,

\begin{split}\left\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{x-1}f(t)\left(\frac{1}{x-t}+\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)dt\right\rvert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\left(-\int_{-\infty}^{-1/x}\frac{1+xt}{(x-t)(1+t^{2})}dt+\int_{-1/x}^{x-1}\frac{1+xt}{(x-t)(1+t^{2})}dt\right).\end{split}

The first integral on the right hand-side is

-\int_{-\infty}^{-1/x}\frac{1+xt}{(x-t)(1+t^{2})}dt=2^{-1}\log(1+x^{2}).

The second integral is

\int_{-1/x}^{x-1}\frac{1+xt}{(x-t)(1+t^{2})}dt=2^{-1}\log\left(1+x^{2}\right)+2^{-1}\log(1+(x-1)^{2}).

Combining the estimates gives the desired result for
x>0
. The case
x<0
can be treated similarly.

3
Construction of the approximating inner function

Let
\alpha
be a smooth function strictly increasing function. Set
d_{\alpha}(x,y)=\lvert\alpha(x)-\alpha(y)\rvert
. We assume that
\alpha^{\prime}(x)dx
is a regular locally doubling weight. We remind the reader that this means that
\alpha
satisfies

(i)

\alpha^{\prime}(x)\sim\alpha^{\prime}(y)
, whenever
d_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq 1
and
\lvert x\rvert,\lvert y\rvert\geq R
, for some
R\geq 0
.

(ii)

\lvert\alpha^{\prime\prime}(x)\rvert\leq C\alpha^{\prime}(x)^{2}
, whenever
\lvert x\rvert,\lvert y\rvert\geq R
, for some
R\geq 0
.

We shall also need to assume that
\alpha^{\prime}(x)\gtrsim\langle x\rangle^{\kappa}
, for some
\kappa>-1
. Then if
f
is any smooth function, such that
f^{\prime}\sim\alpha^{\prime}
we shall construct a meromorphic inner function
J
approximating
f
and with control on
J^{\prime}
.

We say that a set is
\alpha
-uniformly separated if there exists
\delta>0
, such that

d_{\alpha}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})>\delta,\text{ for all }\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda\text{ with }\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}.

We say that
\Lambda
is
\alpha
-uniformly dense if there exists
M>0
, such that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\neq\lambda
in
\Lambda
, such that

d_{\alpha}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})<M.

Remark 1
.

Let us note that the next theorem is much simpler if we do not require any estimates on
J^{\prime}
. Then we could simply choose

\frac{1+J}{1-J}=\int\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)\frac{d\sigma}{i\pi},

for some discrete measure
\sigma
supported on
\Lambda
with
\sigma(\lambda)\lesssim 1
. The difficulty lies in choosing
\sigma
, such that we can control
J^{\prime}
.

The idea behind the proof of the following theorem is similar to a lemma appearing in Section 6 of
[
15
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\alpha
be a regular locally doubling weight, satisfying

\langle x\rangle^{-\kappa}\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x),\text{ for some }\kappa<1.

Suppose
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is an
\alpha
-uniformly discrete with
\alpha
-uniformly bounded gaps. Then there exists a meromorphic inner function
J
, such that

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:J(x)=1\right\}=\Lambda

and there exists a constant
N_{0}
, such that

\langle\lambda\rangle^{-N_{0}}\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda)^{-N_{0}}\lesssim\lvert J^{\prime}(\lambda)\rvert\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda,\text{ and }\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{N_{0}}\langle\alpha^{\prime}(x)\rangle^{N_{0}},\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We write
\Lambda=\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
\lambda_{n}
increasing and
\lambda_{0}
the smallest positive
\lambda
. Throughout the proof we use the fact that

1\sim d_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n+1},\lambda_{n})=\alpha^{\prime}(t_{n})(\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n})\sim\alpha^{\prime}(x)(\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}),\text{ for all }x\in[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n+1}].

In particular we have
\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})^{-1}\sim\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}
for all
n
.

To construct the inner function
J
we use a special case of the so-called Krein spectral shift formula from the spectral theory of perturbations of self-adjoint operators, see
[
13
]
. Let
\omega_{n}
be the mid point of the complementary interval
(\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n+1})
. That is
\omega_{n}=2^{-1}(\lambda_{n+1}+\lambda_{n})
. Define

U=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda_{n},\omega_{n}),

and set

u(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{U}(x)-1/2.

Define
J
by the formula

\frac{1+J}{1-J}=\exp\left(\int\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)u(t)dt\right),\;\;z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

(2)

The right hand side maps into the right half-plane since

\left\lvert\Im\left(\int\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)u(t)dt\right)\right\rvert=\pi\lvert Pu(z)\rvert\leq\pi/2.

The function
J
is thus bounded by
1
and it is inner since
u=1/2
almost everywhere. Moreover, it has a meromorphic extension to the full plane since it extends continuously to
\mathbb{R}
. A straightforward computation shows that
\left\{J=1\right\}=\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\}
and
\left\{J=-1\right\}=\left\{\omega_{n}\right\}
.

To bound
J^{\prime}(x)
on the real line we will use the Aleksandrov-Clark representation of
J
. Let
\sigma_{1}
be the Aleksandrov-Clark measure of
J
and
\sigma_{-1}
the dual measure,

\frac{1+J}{1-J}=-ia-ibz+\frac{-i}{\pi}\int\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)d\sigma_{1}(t),

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\geq 0
and

\frac{1-J}{1+J}=-ic-idz+\frac{-i}{\pi}\int\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)d\sigma_{-1}(t),

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
d\geq 0
. Recall that

\sigma_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{1}(\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\})\delta_{\lambda_{n}}\;\text{ and }\;\sigma_{-1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{-1}(\left\{\omega_{n}\right\})\delta_{\omega_{n}}

are Poisson-finite. In fact,
b=d=0
(but we do not use this fact). Passing to the real line we see that

\sigma_{1}(\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\})=\frac{2\pi}{\lvert J^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})\rvert},

and

\sigma_{-1}(\left\{\omega_{n}\right\})=\frac{2\pi}{\lvert J^{\prime}(\omega_{n})\rvert}.

We claim that we have

\frac{1}{\langle\lambda_{n}\rangle^{K}\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})^{K}}\lesssim\lvert J^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})\rvert,

(3)

and

\sigma_{-1}(\left\{\omega_{n}\right\})\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(\omega_{n})^{-1},\text{ and }\lvert J^{\prime}(\omega_{n})\rvert\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(\omega_{n})^{N_{0}}\langle\omega_{n}\rangle^{N_{0}}.

(4)

Assuming this for the moment we may complete the proof. We have

2\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert=\lvert 1-J(x)\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\sigma_{1}(x)\rvert\;\text{ and }\;2\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert=\lvert 1+J(x)\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\sigma_{-1}(x)\rvert.

Using the first estimate in the middle third and the second estimate in the left and right thirds of the interval
(\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n+1})
gives the upper bound. Indeed, consider
x
in the left or right third of
(\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n+1})
, then

\begin{split}\frac{2\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert}{\lvert 1+J(x)\rvert^{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k}\frac{\sigma_{-1}(\left\{\omega_{k}\right\}}{(x-\omega_{k})^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{k\neq n}\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(\omega_{k})^{-1}}{(x-\omega_{k})^{2}}\\
\sim\left(\int_{\omega_{n+1}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(x-t)^{2}}+\int_{-\infty}^{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{dt}{(x-t)^{2}}\right)\sim\lvert\alpha^{\prime}(\omega_{n})\rvert.\end{split}

In the middle third we use the representation

\frac{2\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert}{\lvert 1-J(x)\rvert^{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k}\frac{\sigma_{1}(\lambda_{k})}{(x-\lambda_{k})^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k}\frac{\sigma_{1}(\lambda_{k})}{(\omega_{n}-\lambda_{k})^{2}}=\lvert J^{\prime}(\omega_{n})\rvert.

It remains to prove (
3
) and (
4
). We isolate this as a lemma.
∎

Proof of equations (
3
) and (
4
).

We begin with (
4
). Note that

\frac{1-J}{1+J}=C\exp\left(-Ku(z)\right),

where
C
is a constant and
Ku
is the improper integral

Ku(z)=\int\frac{1}{t-z}u(t)dt.

To compute the derivative of
J
at
\omega_{n}
we compute the residue of of
\exp(-Ku(z))
at
\omega_{n}

\left\lvert\frac{2}{J^{\prime}(\omega_{n})}\right\rvert=C\lvert\text{Res}(\exp(-Ku))(\omega_{n})\rvert.

The residue of
\exp(-Ku)
can be estimated as follows. Set

A_{n}=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n+1})}\frac{u(t)}{t-\omega_{n}}\right),

and

\lvert g_{n}(z)\rvert=\left\lvert\exp\left(-\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n+1}}\frac{u(t)}{t-z}\right)\right\rvert=\frac{\lvert\lambda_{n}-z\rvert^{1/2}\lvert\lambda_{n+1}-z\rvert^{1/2}}{\lvert\omega_{n}-z\rvert}.

Then

\lvert\text{Res}_{\lambda_{n}}(Ku)\rvert=\lvert A_{n}\rvert\lvert\lambda_{n+1}-\omega_{n}\rvert^{1/2}\lvert\lambda_{n}-\omega_{n}\rvert^{1/2}.

Since the
\lambda_{n}^{\prime}s
are
\alpha
-uniformly dense and
\alpha
-uniformly separated and
\alpha^{\prime}
is a regular locally doubling measure

\lvert\lambda_{n+1}-\omega_{n}\rvert^{1/2}\lvert\lambda_{n}-\omega_{n}\rvert^{1/2}\sim\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})},

with constants independent of
n
. We now turn to bounding
A_{n}
. First we claim that
A_{n}
is bounded above. Indeed, for each term of the form

-2\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}\frac{u(t)}{t-\omega_{n}}=\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}-\omega_{n}}{\omega_{j}-\omega_{n}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{j+1}-\omega_{n}}{\omega_{j}-\omega_{n}}\right)\leq 0,

since the last inequality is equivalent to

\omega_{j}^{2}\geq\lambda_{j+1}\lambda_{j}.

Hence, we obtain
A_{n}\leq 0
.

We now bound
A_{n}
from below. We assume that
\lambda_{n}>1
. The negative
\lambda_{n}^{\prime}s
are treated similarly. For
j>n
We compute

\begin{split}2\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}\frac{u(t)dt}{t-\omega_{n}}=\log(\frac{\omega_{j}-\omega_{n}}{\lambda_{j}-\omega_{n}})-\log(\frac{\lambda_{j+1}-\omega_{n}}{\omega_{j}-\omega_{n}})\\
=-\frac{\lambda_{j}-\omega_{j}}{\omega_{j}-\omega_{n}}+O\left(\frac{(\lambda_{j}-\omega_{j})^{2}}{(\omega_{j}-\omega_{n})^{2}}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{j+1}-\omega_{j}}{\omega_{j}-\omega_{n}}+O\left(\frac{(\lambda_{j+1}-\omega_{j})^{2}}{(\omega_{j}-\omega_{n})^{2}}\right)\\
=O\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{2}}{(\omega_{j}-\omega_{n})^{2}}\right),\end{split}

where
\delta_{j}=\omega_{j}-\lambda_{j}
and we have used the expansion
\log(1+x)=x+O(x^{2})
. Thus

\begin{split}2\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\infty}\frac{u(t)dt}{t-\omega_{n}}=\sum_{j>n}\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}\frac{u(t)dt}{t-\omega_{n}}\lesssim\sum_{j>n}\frac{\delta_{j}^{2}}{(\omega_{j}-\omega_{n})^{2}}.\end{split}

Using that
\alpha^{\prime}
is locally doubling we bound the sum as follows

\sum_{j>n}\frac{\delta_{j}^{2}}{(\omega_{j}-\omega_{n})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j>n}\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}\frac{\delta_{j}}{(\omega_{j}-\omega_{n})^{2}}\lesssim\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\alpha^{\prime}(t)(t-\omega_{n})^{2}}.

We split the last integral into two parts

\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\alpha^{\prime}(t)(t-\omega_{n})^{2}}=\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\lambda_{n}+\omega_{n}}\frac{dt}{\alpha^{\prime}(t)(t-\omega_{n})^{2}}+\int_{\lambda_{n}+\omega_{n}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\alpha^{\prime}(t)(t-\omega_{n})^{2}}=I_{1}+I_{2}.

The first integral we bound using integration by parts,
\lvert\alpha^{\prime\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\alpha^{\prime}(x)^{2}
, and
\alpha^{\prime}\gtrsim\langle x\rangle^{\kappa}

\begin{split}I_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda_{n+1})(\lambda_{n+1}-\omega_{n})}-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda_{n}+\omega_{n})\lambda_{n}}\right)-\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\omega_{n}+\lambda_{n}}\frac{\alpha^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}}\frac{1}{t-\omega_{n}}dt\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n+1}b_{n}^{\kappa}}+\log(\lambda_{n})+\lvert\log(2\delta_{n})\rvert.\end{split}

The second integral
I_{2}
is simpler to bound (we only use that
\alpha^{\prime}\gtrsim\langle t\rangle^{-\kappa}
)

I_{2}\leq\int_{\omega_{n}+\lambda_{n}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\kappa}}{(t-\omega_{n})^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n+1}^{1-\kappa}}.

The case when
j<n
is similar. All in all we obtain a bound of the form

\langle\delta_{n}\lambda_{n}\rangle^{-K_{0}}\lesssim A_{n}\lesssim 1.

Thus we obtain

\alpha^{\prime}(\omega_{n})\lesssim\lvert J^{\prime}(\omega_{n})\rvert\lesssim\langle\omega_{n}\rangle^{N_{0}}\alpha^{\prime}(\omega_{n})^{N_{0}}.

Which is equation (
4
). To obtain the lower bound for
J^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})
we instead consider

\frac{1+J}{1-J}=C\exp(Ku).

The only difference is that the lower bound is the worse bound since
-A_{n}\geq 0
. Exactly the same argument shows that

\langle\lambda_{n}\rangle^{-N}\alpha^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})^{-N}\lesssim\lvert J^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})\rvert.

Which is (
3
).
∎

4
Zero sets in Toeplitz kernels

We shall use the following characterization of functions absolute values of functions in Toeplitz kernels. In this section all symbols are real analytic and unimodular.

Applying Lemma
3
to
\arg(V)=-\alpha+h+\arg(J)+2\epsilon x
we obtain that
m
is the absolute value of a
N^{+}(JUS_{2\epsilon})
function if and only if

2\widetilde{\log m}=\alpha-h-2\epsilon x-\arg(J)-\arg(I)+2\pi n.

Assuming that
m
is smooth the integer valued function
n
is constant and hence may be baked in to the argument of the inner function
I
. The function
m
will be constructed by obtaining sufficiently good bounds on the Hilbert transform of the right hand-side.

4.1
A preparatory lemma

In order to prove our theorem we shall need to regularize the set
\Lambda
to not contain large gaps.

Recall the definition of
\alpha
-Beurling density as

D^{+}_{\alpha}(\Lambda)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha(I)=r}\frac{\#(\Lambda\cap I)}{r},

where the supremum is taken over all interval,
I
, and

\alpha(I)=\int_{I}\alpha^{\prime}(x)dx.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\alpha^{\prime}
be a regular locally doubling measure and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
be an
\alpha
-uniformly discrete set. Suppose
D^{+}_{\alpha}(\Lambda)<1/2\pi
. Then for sufficiently small
\delta>0
there exists an
\alpha
-uniformly discrete and
\alpha
-uniformly dense set
\Lambda\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
(1-\delta)\alpha-2\pi n_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
is bounded.

Proof.

Fix a positive integer
N_{0}>0
and
\delta>0
, such that

\#(\Lambda\cap I)<(1-\delta)\frac{\alpha(I)}{2\pi},

for all intervals
I
of
\alpha
-length
\alpha(I)\geq N_{0}
. Now we decompose the real line into disjoint intervals of
\alpha
-length
N_{0}
,
I_{j}=[a_{j},a_{j+1})
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. That is
d_{\alpha}(a_{j+1},a_{j})=N_{0}
. Now we add points to each interval keeping the set
\alpha
-uniformly separated and such that

\#(\Lambda\cap I_{j})=\left[(1-\delta)\frac{\alpha(I_{j})}{2\pi}\right].

Now we may inductively add one extra point (keeping the uniform separation) to the intervals
I_{j}
if necessary to ensure that

(1-\delta)\alpha(a_{j})\geq 2\pi n_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(a_{j})\geq(1-\delta)\alpha(a_{j})-1.

By construction
\Lambda^{\prime}
is
\alpha
-uniformly separated and
\alpha
-uniformly dense. The previous inequality shows that
(1-\delta)\alpha-2\pi n_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
is bounded.
∎

4.2
Construction of a function with prescribed zeros

We are now ready to construct the vanishing function when the phase function is a locally doubling weight.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Using Lemma
5
for
\delta>0
sufficiently small we construct an an
\alpha
-discrete set
\Lambda^{\prime}
containing
\Lambda
, such that

(1-\delta)\alpha-2\pi n_{\Lambda^{\prime}},\;\text{ is bounded},

and
\Lambda^{\prime}\setminus\Lambda
is infinite. Using Theorem

we construct a meromorphic inner function
J
, such that

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:J(x)=1\right\}=\Lambda^{\prime},

and

\lvert J^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K_{0}},\text{ and }\langle\lambda\rangle^{-K_{0}}\lesssim\lvert J^{\prime}(\lambda)\rvert,\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}.

Then
\arg(J)-2\pi n_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
is bounded.

Let
m
be a locally summable function, such that
\log(m)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
. As we have seen
m
is equal to the absolute value of a
N^{+}(JUS_{2\epsilon})
function if there exists an inner function
I
, such that

2\widetilde{\log m}=\alpha+h-\arg(J)-2\epsilon x-\arg(I).

By construction
(1-\delta)\varphi-\arg(J)
is a bounded function. For sufficiently small

\delta\alpha^{\prime}-2\epsilon>0

that there exists a meromorphic inner function
I

It remains to prove the desired estimates. The function

u=\frac{\alpha+h-\arg(J)-2\epsilon x-\arg(I)}{2},

is bounded and satisfies
\lvert u^{\prime}(x)\rvert\leq C\langle x\rangle^{N}
for some

and

\lvert\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{N}

4.3
Necessity of the density condition

For our necessary condition we shall need the notion of uniform lower density.

D_{\alpha}^{-}(\Lambda)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\inf_{\alpha(I)=r}\frac{\#(\Lambda\cap I)}{r},

where the infimum is taken over all intervals
I
och
\alpha

\ell

Theorem 5
.

Let

be unimodular symbol with

.
\alpha^{\prime}\gtrsim 1
and

Let
\delta>0
and
N_{0}>

Lemma 6
.

Let
\arg(U)=-\alpha+h
with
\alpha,h\in C^{\omega}

\alpha^{\prime}>0

\Lambda

Proof.

First we claim that

5
Beurling-Malliavin majorants for model spaces

We shall use the representation for the absolute values of elements of a Toeplitz kernel from Lemma
3
. Indeed, if we can find a measurable and nonnegative function
m
with
\log m\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
such that
m\omega=\lvert f\rvert
with
f\in N^{+}(\overline{\Theta})
, and such that
m
is bounded then

\lvert f(x)\rvert=m(x)\omega(x)\leq\lVert m\rVert_{\infty}\omega(x),

and we would be done. It turns out to be easiest to first construct
m
which may grow as a polynomial and then correct afterwards.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
m\geq 0
and suppose
m\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
. Then by Lemma
3

m\omega
is the absolute value of a
N^{+}(S_{2\epsilon}\overline{\Theta})
function if there exists an inner function
I
, such that

2\widetilde{\log(m)}=2\widetilde{\Omega}+\arg\Theta-2\epsilon x-\arg I.

Let
\epsilon>0
be so small that
\alpha=2\widetilde{\Omega}+\arg\Theta
satisfies
\alpha^{\prime}(x)-2\epsilon\gtrsim 1
. By assumption the function
\alpha-2\epsilon x
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1
(for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
the perturbation does not destroy the local doubling condition) and hence there exists a meromorphic inner function
I
, such that
\alpha-2\epsilon x-\arg(I)\in L^{\infty}
and

\lvert\alpha^{\prime}(x)-2\epsilon-\arg(I)^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K},\text{ for some }K\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus defining
m
via

2\log(m)=-\widetilde{\left(2\widetilde{\Omega}+\arg\Theta-2\epsilon x-\arg I\right)},

there exists
f\in N^{+}(S_{2\epsilon}\overline{\Theta})
, such that
m=\lvert f\rvert
. it remains to prove the estimates. Set

u(x)=-\widetilde{\Omega}(x)-2^{-1}\arg\Theta(x)-\epsilon x+2^{-1}\arg I(x).

Then
m(x)=\exp(\widetilde{u})
. The function
u
is bounded and satisfies
\lvert u^{\prime}(x)\lvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K}
,
K\geq 1
. Hence, by Lemma
4
, its Hilbert transform satisfies
\lvert\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{K}
. Let
M=\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}
. Then
v=u/M
is bounded by one and hence by the Zygmund estimate (Corollary 2.6. in Chapter 3 of
[
9
]
)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\lvert\widetilde{v}(x)\rvert}d\mathbb{P}(x)<\infty.

We may bound the function
h=m^{1/M}=\exp(\widetilde{u}/M)
as follows

\lvert h(x)\rvert\leq\lvert h(0)\rvert+\frac{1}{M}\int^{x}_{0}\lvert\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(x)\rvert h(x)dx\lesssim 1+\langle x\rangle^{N+2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\lvert\widetilde{u(x)/M}\rvert}d\mathbb{P}(x)\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{N+2}.

Hence, we obtain

\lvert f(x)\rvert=m(x)\omega(x)\lesssim\omega(x)\langle x\rangle^{N_{0}},

for some non-trivial
f\in K^{+}_{\Theta}
. It remains to fix the polynomial growth at infinity. For any
N_{0}>0
we may choose
\delta>0
so small that
N\delta<\epsilon
. Then the function

h(z)=\left(\frac{\sin(z\delta)}{z\delta}\right)^{N_{0}+1},

is an entire function of exponential type less than
\epsilon
satisfying

\lvert h(x)\rvert\leq\langle x\rangle^{-(N_{0}+1)}.

The function
S_{\epsilon}h
belongs to
N^{+}(S_{-2\epsilon})
and hence
hf\in K_{\Theta}
, moreover,

\lvert h(x)f(x)\rvert\lesssim\omega(x).

∎

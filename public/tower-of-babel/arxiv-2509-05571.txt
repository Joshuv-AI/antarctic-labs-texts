The complementarity relations in a multi-path interferometer with quantum memory
Yue Sun 1 , Ming-Jing Zhao 2∗ , Peng-Tong Li 1 1 School of Mathematics, Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Nanjing 210016, PR China 2 School of Science, Beijing Information Science and Technology University, Beijing 102206, PR China ∗ Corresponding author: zhaomingjingde@126.com

Abstract
The complementarity relations impose the constraints on different aspects of quantum states. We study the complementarity relation within a multi-path interferometer that includes detectors and quantum memory. Here we consider the mixed states as the input states. We establish a duality relation between the visibility and the path distinguishability. Based on this duality, two triality relations, one is related with visibility, path distinguishability, and mixedness, the other is related with visibility, path distinguishability, and entanglement, were derived respectively. Therefore the role of entanglement in multi-path interferometer is characterized quantitatively. These complementarity relations are all complete for the two-path interferometer.



I
Introduction.

Complementarity relation is a fundamental law in quantum mechanics. It has profound implications for both theoretical and experimental development of quantum physics. In the
interferometer, the complementarity is manifested by the wave-particle features of the particle. The wave feature is exhibited by the visibility of the interference fringe and the particle feature is exhibited by path information in the interference.
The first analysis of the wave-particle duality in a quantitative way is given by Wootters and Zurek
zuizao
. Then the wave-particle duality is put into a delicate formula by Greenberger and Yasin as
GY

P^{2}+V^{2}\leq 1,

where
P
stands for the path information of the particle and
V
stands for the visibility of the interference fringes.
The first quantitative wave-particle duality in multi-path interferometers was established by Dürr
durr
. In his work, he introduced the criteria for wave measures and particle measures, and provided specific expressions through the moments of functions
durr
. Since then, much attention has been paid to characterize the wave-particle duality quantitatively
n1
;
n2
;
n5
;
TQ
. Especially the emergence of quantum coherence resource theory promotes further the development of the complementarity relation in multi-path interference. Different types of wave-particle duality are proposed from different perspectives
n6
;
GR
;
EK
;
hh1
. To explore the gap between the unity and the wave-particle features, the mixedness
n3
;
n2
, entanglement
JC1
;
JC2
;
JC3
, and entropy
s1
;
s2
;
s3
are involved and the corresponding trialities emerges in the complementarity relation.
The complementarity relations in form of inequalities are called incomplete, while those in form of identities are called complete
Basso20
;
Basso21
.

To detect the path information of the particle,
the interference affiliated with path detectors is introduced and a wave-particle duality relation is derived as
Englert

D^{2}+V^{2}\leq 1,

where
D
, referred to as
path distinguishability
, quantifies the particle properties in the two-path interferometer, and
V
, defined in terms of
l_{2}
norm of coherence, quantifies the visibility of the interference fringes.
Later, the duality between the path distinguishability and visibility is presented
in the
n
-path interference with path detectors
Bera
.
This duality is then improved by Ref.
du1
to a tighter one as

\displaystyle(P_{s}-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+X^{2}\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2},

(I.1)

where
P_{s}
is the optimal success probability to discriminate detector states
and
X
is the normalized
l_{1}
norm coherence. Subsequently, this duality in Eq. (
I.1
) is improved further by assisting the particle with a quantum memory
bkf

(P^{A}_{s}-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+X_{A}^{2}\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}({\rm Tr}{\rho}_{A}^{2}-{\rm Tr}{\rho}_{AB}^{2}),

(I.2)

where subscript A represents the particle we concerned and subscript B represents the quantum memory.
Apparently, the participation of quantum memory improves the duality relation between the visibility and the path distinguishability.

In this work, we mainly study the complementarity relation in the multi-path interferometers with detectors and quantum memory thoroughly. Our aim is to analyze the impact of quantum memory on the complementarity relation in the multi-path interferometers. As a result, we find the entanglement between the referred particle and the quantum memory plays an essential role in the complementarity relation.

To this end, we first
show the duality relation between the visibility and the path distinguishability, which is complete in the two-path interferometers. As a byproduct, we derive the triality among the visibility, the path distinguishability and mixedness. Next we prove
the complementarity relation among the visibility, path distinguishability, and entanglement analytically, which is illustrated by two explicit examples. This triality relation reveals the essential role of entanglement in the constraint on wave and particle features of particle. Here we should emphasize that these complementarity relations we derived here are all for the multi-path interferometers with detectors and quantum memory.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce
n
-path interferometer equipped with path detectors and quantum memory, as depicted in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Schematic of
n
-path interferometer with path detector and quantum memory. The particle passes through an
n

We suppose
\{|i\rangle_{A}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a set of orthonormal basis states of
n
-dimensional Hilbert space denoted by
H_{A}
with
|i\rangle_{A}
corresponding to the
i
-th path. Let
\{|e_{j}\rangle_{B}\}_{j=1}^{n}
denote another orthonormal basis of the quantum memory, which is an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space denoted by
H_{B}
. Then
the bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
shared between the particle A and the quantum memory B after passing through the beam splitter can be described as

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}\rho_{ij,kl}|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|\otimes|e_{j}\rangle_{B}\langle e_{l}|,

where
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\rho_{ij,ij}=1
.
The reduced state of subsystem
A
is

\rho_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(\rho_{AB})=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{ij,kj}|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|.

(II.3)

Denote the operator

B_{ik}=\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}\rho_{ij,kl}|e_{j}\rangle_{B}\langle e_{l}|
with
{\rm Tr}(B_{ik})=(\rho_{A})_{ik}
, then the bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
can also be written as

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|\otimes B_{ik}.

Within the interferometer, the particle A will interact with a system
D
known as the detector. The detector starts in the state
|d_{0}\rangle
. At this moment, the initial state of the whole system is

\displaystyle{\rho}_{ABD}=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|\otimes B_{ik}\otimes|d_{0}\rangle_{D}\langle d_{0}|.

After the interaction, the state of the whole system becomes

\widetilde{\rho}_{ABD}=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|\otimes B_{ik}\otimes|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\langle d_{k}|,

(II.4)

where the interaction is described as the controlled unitary operator
U(|i\rangle_{A}\otimes|d_{0}\rangle_{D})=|i\rangle_{A}\otimes|d_{i}\rangle_{D}
.
Tracing out the detector, the density matrix of the composed system
AB
is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{AB}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{D}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ABD})=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\langle d_{k}|d_{i}\rangle|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|\otimes B_{ik},

and the reduced density matrix of the particle A is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{A}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{BD}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ABD})=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\langle d_{k}|d_{i}\rangle(\rho_{A})_{ik}|i\rangle_{A}\langle k|.

(II.5)

Similarly, the density matrix of the detector can be represented as

\widetilde{\rho}_{D}={\rm Tr}_{AB}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ABD})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}|d_{i}\rangle\langle d_{i}|,

(II.6)

where

q_{i}=(\rho_{A})_{ii}.

(II.7)

In the multi-path interference, the particle A behaves like both wave and particle when it goes through the slits.
To characterize the wave behavior of the particle A in the interference, the
l_{2}
norm of coherence is employed as the visibility
durr
. For any quantum state
\rho=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|
, its
l_{2}
norm of coherence is
C_{l_{2}}(\rho)=(\sum_{i\neq j}|\rho_{ij}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Here we consider a normalized version of the
l_{2}
norm of coherence given by
V(\rho)={\frac{\sqrt{2(n-1)}}{n}}C_{l_{2}}({\rho})
.
So the wave feature exhibited by the quantum state
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
before the screen in the interferometer is quantified as

V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}=\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}|\langle d_{k}|d_{i}\rangle|^{2}|(\rho_{A})_{ik}|^{2}.

(II.8)

On the other aspect, the particle behavior of the particle A in the interference is described by the path information which is encoded within the detector states.
To acquire the path information of the particle A, we need to turn to the detectors and discriminate the detector states
\{|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\}
with probabilities
\{q_{i}\}
in Eq. (
II.7
). Here we utilize the minimum-error state discrimination as the detector states
\{|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\}
are not necessarily orthogonal.
For a positive operator valued measure
\bf{(POVM)}

\Pi=\{\Pi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
\Pi_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Pi_{i}=\mathbb{I}
, where
\mathbb{I}
is identity operator,
the success probability to discriminate the states
\{|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\}_{i=1}^{n}
with probabilities
\{q_{i}\}
by
\Pi
is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}{\rm Tr}[\Pi_{i}|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\langle d_{i}|]
.
Thus the optimal success probability among all
\bf{POVM}
s is

P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})=\max_{\{\Pi_{i}\}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}{\rm Tr}[\Pi_{i}|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\langle d_{i}|].

(II.9)

The upper bound for the
optimal success probability
P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})
is given by
du1

P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})\leq\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}||T_{ik}||_{1},

(II.10)

where the operator
T_{ik}=q_{i}|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\langle d_{i}|-q_{k}|d_{k}\rangle_{D}\langle d_{k}|
. The
||\cdot||_{1}
is trace norm and

||T_{ik}||_{1}=2\sqrt{(\frac{q_{i}+q_{k}}{2})^{2}-q_{i}q_{k}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2}}.

(II.11)

The Ref.
du1
indicates that
P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n}
is an indicator of how much performance can be improved by using prior information and detectors compared to random guessing to acquire the path information. Here we regard it as the path distinguishability to describe the particle behavior of particle A passing through the interferometer.

III
The complementarity relation between visibility and path distinguishability in a multi-path interferometer

In this section, we present the complementarity relation between visibility and path distinguishability within an

n
-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory.

Theorem III.1
.

In the
n
-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory, the visibility and path distinguishability satisfy

(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}({\rm Tr}[\widetilde{\rho}_{D}^{2}]-{\rm Tr}[\widetilde{\rho}_{A}^{2}]).

(III.12)

Proof.

In the
n
-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory, by Eq. (
II.10
) and Eq. (
II.11
), we get that the path distinguishability satisfies

\displaystyle(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\sqrt{(\frac{q_{i}+q_{k}}{2})^{2}-q_{i}q_{k}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2}}\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{q_{i}+q_{k}}{2}\Bigg)\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}(\frac{q_{i}+q_{k}}{2}-\frac{2q_{i}q_{k}}{q_{i}+q_{k}}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2})\Bigg],

where we have used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the last inequality.
Note the relation
\sum_{i\neq k=1}^{n}\frac{q_{i}+q_{k}}{2}=n-1
, the inequality above is then bounded by

\displaystyle(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n-1)}{n^{2}}\Bigg[(n-1)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{2q_{i}q_{k}}{q_{i}+q_{k}}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n-1)}{n^{2}}\Bigg[(n-1)-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}q_{i}q_{k}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2}\Bigg],

(III.13)

due to
q_{i}+q_{k}\leq 1
.
Therefore the sum of the path distinguishability and the visibility is limited by

\displaystyle(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n-1)}{n^{2}}\Bigg((n-1)-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}q_{i}q_{k}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2}\Bigg)+\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}|\langle d_{i}|d_{k}\rangle|^{2}|(\rho_{A})_{ik}|^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}({\rm Tr}[\widetilde{\rho}_{D}^{2}]-{\rm Tr}[\widetilde{\rho}_{A}^{2}]),

which deduces the visibility and path distinguishability duality in Eq. (
III.12
).
∎

III.1
shows the visibility and path distinguishability duality in the
n
-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory. For the
n

\displaystyle(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2}.

(III.14)

Especially for the two-path case, Eq. (

) turns into the

Englert

Now we make a comparison between the visibility and path distinguishability dualities in Theorem

Although all mixed states can be purified theoretically, the purification of an unknown mixed state is in fact terribly hard. In view of the fact that the pure state is rather fragile and it is unavoidably affected by the environment and gets mixed.
So the duality for mixed states is more general and is meaningful.
Second, it is straightforward to verify that for pure input states, the upper bound in Eq. (12) is consistent with that in Eq. (2), as the relation

Especially, when

Corollary III.2
.

In the two-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory, the visibility and path distinguishability duality is

(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}({\rm Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}^{2}_{D})-{\rm Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}^{2}_{A})).

(III.16)

Furthermore, for any quantum state
\rho
, if we choose the normalized mixedness as
M(\rho)=\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}(1-{\rm Tr}\rho^{2})
, then by moving the purity
{\rm Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}^{2}_{A})
from the right hand side to the left hand side in Eq. (
III.12
), the duality between the visibility and path distinguishability is transformed into the triality among the visibility, path distinguishability and mixedness.

Theorem III.3
.

In the
n
-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory, the visibility, path distinguishability and mixedness satisfy

(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}+M(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+M(\widetilde{\rho}_{D}).

(III.17)

In particular, when
n=2
, one can derive a complete triality relation about the visibility, path distinguishability and mixedness.

Corollary III.4
.

In the two-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory,
if the detector states satisfy
|\langle d_{1}|d_{2}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
, then
the visibility, path distinguishability and mixedness satisfy the complete triality relation as

(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}M(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})=\frac{1}{4}.

(III.18)

Proof.

In the two-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory, if the detector states satisfy
|\langle d_{1}|d_{2}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
, then the optimal success probability reduces to

(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}=\frac{1}{4}-q_{1}q_{2}|\langle d_{1}|d_{2}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}q_{1}q_{2}.

Along with the expressions of the visibility

V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|(\rho_{A})_{12}|^{2}

and the mixedness of

M(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})=\frac{1}{2}(1-|(\rho_{A})_{12}|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{2}q_{i}^{2}),

it is obvious to derive the complete triality in Eq. (
III.18
).
∎

The duality in Eq. (
III.16
) and triality in Eq. (
III.18
) we obtained above are both complete for the two-path interferometer. These impose the constraints on the visibility, path distinguishability and mixedness quantitatively. For example, Eq. (
III.18
) tells us that the sum of the visibility and path distinguishability increases as the mixedness of
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
decreases.
In contrast, if the output state
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
gets more mixed, then the sum of the visibility and path distinguishability gets less and the wave-particle feature of particle A is transformed partly into its mixedness.

IV
The triality relation among the visibility, path distinguishability, and entanglement in a multi-path interferometer

In this section we explore the role of entanglement in the
n
-path interferometer and establish the triality relation among
the visibility, path distinguishability, and entanglement. Here we utilize the concurrence as the entanglement measure
en
. For any pure state
|\psi\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{i,j}\psi_{ij}|ij\rangle_{AB}
, its concurrence is defined as

C(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=\sqrt{2(1-{\rm Tr}\rho_{A}^{2})}
,
with
\rho_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(|\psi\rangle_{AB}\langle\psi|)
. For any mixed state
\rho_{AB}
, its concurrence is defined with the convex roof construction
C(\rho_{AB})=\min_{\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle_{AB}\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}C(|\psi_{i}\rangle_{AB})
with the minimum running over all possible pure-state decompositions
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle_{AB}\langle\psi_{i}|
. Here we use a normalized version of concurrence as
E(\rho_{AB})=\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{n}C(\rho_{AB})
.

Theorem IV.1
.

In the
n
-path interferometer equipped with a detector and quantum memory,
the visibility, path distinguishability and entanglement satisfy

(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}+E(\rho_{AB})^{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}{\rm Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}^{2}_{AB}).

(IV.19)

Proof.

First we assume the quantum state
{\rho}_{AB}
shared between particle A and quantum memory B is pure,

|\psi\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{p_{i}}|i\rangle_{A}|u_{i}\rangle_{B},

(IV.20)

where
p_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{2}
and
|u_{i}\rangle_{B}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}|e_{j}\rangle_{B}/\sqrt{p_{i}}
. Here
|u_{i}\rangle_{B}
are normalized but not necessarily orthogonal.
After the interaction with the detectors by the controlled unitary
U
, the whole quantum state of particle A, quantum memory B and detectors D are

|\widetilde{\psi}\rangle_{ABD}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{p_{i}}|i\rangle_{A}|u_{i}\rangle_{B}|d_{i}\rangle_{D}.

The reduced density matrix of system
AB
becomes

\widetilde{\rho}_{AB}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle|i\rangle_{A}\langle j|\otimes|u_{i}\rangle_{B}\langle u_{j}|,

and the reduced density matrix of the particle
A
becomes

\widetilde{\rho}_{A}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle\langle u_{j}|u_{i}\rangle|i\rangle_{A}\langle j|.

Moreover, the density matrix of detector is

\widetilde{\rho}_{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}|d_{i}\rangle\langle d_{i}|.

The entanglement of the initial state is

\displaystyle E(|\psi\rangle_{AB})^{2}=\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}(1-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle u_{j}|u_{i}\rangle|^{2}).

(IV.21)

Note that
{\rm Tr}[(\widetilde{\rho}_{AB})^{2}]=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle|^{2}
,
then the visibility, path distinguishability and entanglement satisfy

\displaystyle(P_{s}^{A}(\{p_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}+E(|\psi\rangle_{AB})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{2}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle|^{2}|\langle u_{j}|u_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}(1-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle u_{j}|u_{i}\rangle|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle u_{j}|u_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}p_{i}p_{j}|\langle u_{j}|u_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}{\rm Tr}[(\widetilde{\rho}_{AB})^{2}],

where the first inequality is based on Eq. (
III
) and the second inequality arises from the fact that
|\langle d_{j}|d_{i}\rangle|\leq 1
, for all
i,j.
So the relation among visibility, path distinguishability and entanglement of pure state
|\psi\rangle_{AB}
satisfy

(P_{s}(\{p_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}+E(|\psi\rangle_{AB})^{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}{\rm Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}^{2}_{AB}).

(IV.22)

Second, we assume the quantum state
{\rho}_{AB}
shared between particle A and quantum memory B is mixed,
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{s}r_{s}|\psi_{s}\rangle_{AB}\langle\psi_{s}|
with
0\leq r_{s}\leq 1
,
\sum_{s}r_{s}=1
,
|\psi_{s}\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{s}|i\rangle_{A}|e_{j}\rangle_{B}
. For pure state
|\psi_{s}\rangle_{AB}
, suppose the reduced state of subsystem A is
\rho_{A}^{\psi_{s}}
. Furthermore, we suppose the optimal success probability in the interferometer with respect to
|\psi_{s}\rangle_{AB}
is
P_{s}(\{q_{i}^{s},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})
, that is,

\displaystyle P_{s}(\{q_{i}^{s},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})=\max_{\Pi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\rho_{A}^{\psi_{s}})_{ii}{\rm Tr}[\Pi_{i}|d_{i}\rangle\langle d_{i}|],

with
q_{i}^{s}=(\rho_{A}^{\psi_{s}})_{ii}
.
Then the optimal success probability
P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})
with respect to the mixed state
\rho_{AB}
satisfies

\displaystyle P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\Pi}\sum_{i}(\rho_{A})_{ii}{\rm Tr}[\Pi_{i}|d_{i}\rangle\langle d_{i}|]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\Pi}\sum_{i}(\sum_{s}r_{s}\rho_{A}^{\psi_{s}})_{ii}{\rm Tr}[\Pi_{i}|d_{i}\rangle\langle d_{i}|]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s}r_{s}\max_{\Pi}\sum_{i}(\rho_{A}^{\psi_{s}})_{ii}{\rm Tr}[\Pi_{i}|d_{i}\rangle\langle d_{i}|]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}r_{s}P_{s}(\{q_{i}^{s},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\}),

with
\rho_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(\rho_{AB})
and
q_{i}=(\rho_{A})_{ii}
.
This implies

\displaystyle(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\sum_{s}r_{s}P_{s}(\{q_{i}^{s},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s}r_{s}[P_{s}(\{q_{i}^{s},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n}]^{2},

where we have used the convexity of the square function.
Subsequently, the sum of path-distinguishability, visibility and entanglement is

\displaystyle(P_{s}(\{q_{i},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{A})^{2}+E(\rho_{AB})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s}r_{s}[(P_{s}(\{q_{i}^{s},|d_{i}\rangle_{D}\})-\frac{1}{n})^{2}+V(\widetilde{\rho}_{AB}^{\psi_{s}})^{2}+E(|\psi_{s}\rangle_{AB})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}\sum_{s}r_{s}{\rm Tr}[(\widetilde{\rho}_{AB}^{\psi_{s}})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^{2}}{\rm Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}^{2}_{AB}),

where we have used the convexity of path-distinguishability, concurrence, visibility
V(\rho)
in the first inequality,
Eq. (
IV.22
) in the second inequality, and the convexity of purity
{\rm Tr}(\rho^{2})
in the third inequality, respectively.
Finally this completes the proof.

shows the complementarity relation among visibility, path distinguishability, and entanglement. It demonstrates quantitatively that the constraint of entanglement shared between the referred particle and quantum memory on the duality between the visibility and path distinguishability.

V
Conclusions

In a multi-path interferometer equipped with detectors and a quantum memory, we have presented the duality relation between the visibility and the path distinguishability, and the triality among the visibility, the path distinguishability, and mixedness respectively. Especially for the two-path interferometer, the corresponding results are complete. We have also derived the triality relation among the visibility, the path distinguishability, and entanglement, which reveals the role of entanglement in the multi-path interferometer with quantum memory quantitatively.

Here it is worth to stress that the similar duality and triality can also be derived if we replace the visibility by the
l_{p}
-norm coherence, since the
l_{p}
-norm is less than or equal to the
l_{2}
-norm for
1<p<\infty
. So the complementarity relations we proved in this paper are typical in some sense.

Meanwhile, some similar complementarity relations in the multi-path interferometer have been proposed in the literature. The difference between our results and the previous ones is not the explicit measures, but the physical scenario. For example,
the triality relation among predictability, visibility, and mixedness in
hh1
;
n3
is true in the absence of detectors. And the triality relations involving visibility, predictability, and entanglement in
JC3
;
Ding
is true without quantum memory as well as the input state is pure.
So these complementarity relations apply to different physical scenarios.

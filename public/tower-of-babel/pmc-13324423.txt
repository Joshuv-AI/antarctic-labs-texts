ABSTRACT
AbstractData security is a fundamental objective of all information systems, especially those handling financial data, which involves technologies, strategies, and measures to defend sensitive financial data. Consequently, data compression is regarded as an effective technique for reducing storage requirements for all generated and stored data. Therefore, it has become necessary to design an integrated system that provides security for financial data in addition to preserving the required storage space as much as possible. Accordingly, the proposed approach consists of three main components: the sender, the receiver, and the graphical user interface (GUI). The system accepts two types of input documents (i.e., images and PDF files), extracts their data into a structured format, and then classifies the processed data into sensitive and non-sensitive categories. Watermarking and encryption are subsequently applied only to the sensitive data to ensure privacy preservation and enhance overall security. The most suitable compression algorithm was selected based on the results obtained when comparing standard algorithms (Zstd, LZMA, and Brotli). The results showed that the proposed system, Autoencoder and ZSTD, which achieved compression efficiency improvements ranging from 26.4% to 52.8% compared to the traditional ZSTD algorithm across three different-sized datasets (6,784 bytes, 169,668 bytes, and about 327,000 bytes), while maintaining high entropy values ​​ranging from 5.83 to 5.99 bits/byte. Furthermore, chi-square tests showed high values, reaching 11,015, confirming the high level of statistical randomness and security of the resulting data.Keywords: Chi-square test, Data compression, Financial data compression, Lossless compression, Privacy-preservationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Widespread adoption of the Digital technologies has resulted in a significant amount of digital data through the adoption of technologies such as Internet of Things (IoT)1, artificial intelligence (AI), cloud computing, and social networks, which have introduced an urgent demand for effective and safe data management solutions2–4. This information is being created on a myriad of spheres every second, such as financial data, healthcare, e-governance, and electronic communications. Such a direction of data is characterized by volume, variety, and speed and presents the challenges of enormous dimensions to the storage, transmission, and real-time processing systems5–7.

Conventional data infrastructures cannot keep up with this onslaught, especially when the data formats are heterogeneous, e.g., high-resolution images, PDF files, and time-series data. In this regard, data compression has turned out to be an important technology. Compression techniques are intended to decrease the size of data without having an impact on the integrity and usability of information. Most importantly, lossless compression algorithms are required in a sensitive area where the information has to be accurate, like in financial reporting or in medical diagnostics8–10.

Concurrently, the growing emphasis on data privacy and cybersecurity has transformed how organizations manage digital information11. Research studies have revealed that the use of advanced symmetric encryption techniques, including chaotic system-based encryption schemes, significantly improves the level of data security12. Some research has also focused on protecting the privacy of sensitive data, including classified satellite images, using advanced encryption techniques and security-supporting re-encryption mechanisms13. As such, encryption and privacy protection technologies advance, data safety is no longer an available technical choice, but an obligatory guideline as developed by international systems and laws that focus on the preservation of sensitive information. Legal rules like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) require the existence of strong protection of sensitive information. Poor security of personal or proprietary information may lead to devastating financial, legal and reputational effects14. The necessity to provide confidentiality, integrity and authenticity, particularly in such fields as finance, where even a minor breach of data can cost a lot, is as urgent as ever15.

Nevertheless, solutions that are provided in the literature typically focus on these issues individually. Most of them focus on either compression or encryption, and not both in a combined fashion. Additionally, the systems that do include both of these features often use them in an identical manner on all types of data, creating extraneous computation costs, especially on non-sensitive data16. Also, most of the available tools are not flexible, do not support a variety of file formats, or have user interfaces that are not user-friendly to those who do not consider themselves to be experts17.

To overcome such issues, this study introduces a new hybrid system that incorporates intelligent data classification, lossless compression, dynamic watermarking, and symmetric encryption as a single and scalable system. The most important innovation is the selective processing of data: sensitive data is encrypted and watermarked before compression, and the non-sensitive data is compressed only. The approach will go a long way in minimizing the processing load without losing security. The system accepts two major types of inputs, including PDF documents and images, and based on them, one can extract financial or structured data and process it. A system consisting of three major components. The first is a sender component that preprocesses the data before transmission and applies the required security operations. The second is a receiver component that reconstructs the received data, verifies its authenticity and ownership, and generates a local hash of the data before it is stored. The third component is a graphical user interface (GUI) designed for both technical and non‑technical users. In the proposed system, the Zstandard (Zstd) compression algorithm is combined with autoencoder‑based dimensionality reduction, while data integrity and ownership are validated by a SHA3‑256 hash and a watermark embedded in the least significant bits (LSB) of the data.

In addition, the system performs entropy evaluation and Chi square statistical analysis of the random information to the encrypted data that would ensure resistance to cryptanalytic assaults.

Testing has been done using real-world financial A data; financial data is a sensitive field with highly regulated nature. Based on obtained results, the following testing improvements were established: compression ratio of 52.08%, entropy of 5.9813 bits/byte, and Chi square value of 6925.30 as a measure of statistical randomness.

This study presents a summary of a powerful, versatile, and user-friendly solution to data compression in order to be secure and efficient. It can fulfill the twin needs of efficiency and privacy of data with the inclusion of AI-based optimization, selective encryption, and watermarking in a single framework, which is why it can be used in the field of finance, healthcare, digital archiving, and other high-data-volume industries.

Related works

As the volume of data keeps increasing, there is an immediate need to find effective methods of processing the large and diverse information18. Reacting to this, significant progress has been made in techniques and methodologies of data compression, which are also reported in many research papers. Against this background, the paper of Mao et al. (2025) appeared, which proposed a novel way of text compression generated by a large language model. It operates upon predicting the following word, and has been shown to be able to compress by a factor of up to 20 times that of the traditional algorithm of Gzip, which validates that using linguistic properties to exploit linguistic compression performance19. Whereas, in the case of image processing, Ye (2025) made a framework that was designed to secure images on social networks. This model integrates encryption and digital watermarking algorithm based on discrete wave conversion (DWT), cellular automation (CA) and single value decomposition (SVD). This approach has also demonstrated its capability to withstand different attacks, and it is also simple to implement20. Besides, Xiong et al. (2025) successfully established a sophisticated approach of incorporating honey coding and reversible cellular automation to establish stronger watermarks. The quality of the results (PSNR of over 40 dB) was high, which also placed this mechanism in an advanced position compared to mainstream approaches21.

Within the domain of time data, which underlie the financial information of stock prices and stock market movement, some key researches have been developed over the past years. Such studies show that time series compression technology, either through simple algorithms like the Elf or neural network-based, can be very useful in working with financial data, which is flowing and complex, and as such, is a convergence point with contemporary data compression trends in an effort to provide efficiency and accuracy simultaneously. As an illustration, one of the approaches CORTÉS et al. (2024) suggest is to use an autoencoder to compress the data on global financial indicators. He has shown that such an approach enhances the precision and quality of aggregation over the traditional methods that give a better picture of how the market behaves and contributes to making predictions and investment choices22. Although ACTF (2024) also introduced an effective algorithm to compress the floating-point time data of the type of time series, which was based on the pre-expansion of coding classes, optimal features and achieved an improvement in compression ratio of over 12% and an improvement in efficiency of over 27% across a few datasets23. Also, in 2024, Zhang et al. presented research that includes a method to enhance the reliability of the data source by embedding the LSB watermark within the underlying spaces generated by deep learning models, achieving higher durability against processing and compression while maintaining data quality24. Li et al. (2023) proposed a new method for compressing streaming floating-point data called Elf and Elf+, which relies on deleting unnecessary bits (such as consecutive zeros) with improved encoding, and has succeeded in achieving high compression ratios with low time and space efficiency25. In the same year, a paper was presented that proposed an intelligent time series data compression (Time Series) framework based on a hybrid Autoencoder model that combines Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). The proposed framework reduces data size while maintaining temporal properties and accuracy and achieves a high compression ratio with low reconstruction error compared to traditional methods26. Hsu (2017) introduced an approach based on iterative neural networks (LSTM) with time series divided into similar segments, allowing efficient compression of real-time data while maintaining its ability to reconstruct27.

Within the framework of the survey, a number of studies have explored the textual watermarking with the techniques related to large language models (LLMs). As an example, in 2024, a survey was published to conduct a review of the state-of-the-art text watermarking methods and their evaluation measures, such as detectability, robustness, and usability28. Moreover, Kumar et al. (2024) perform an overall review of lightweight security and privacy solutions that lack power, memory, and processing resources in IoT devices. The study discusses lightweight encryption methods, key management, and authentication algorithms, and the focus is on the balance between security and efficiency of performance. The findings indicate that lightweight solutions can be used to make sure that data is secure without causing severe effects to hardware resources, and that they are applicable to practice-based IoT applications29. Likewise, Yang (2025) divided watermarking approaches into two broad categories, namely training-free and training-based watermarking approaches, which identify the advantages and disadvantages of both approaches30. According to the publication authored by Rai et al., a systematic review of recent technologies used to protect real-time Internet of Things (IoT) images has been conducted. The article evaluates the use of machine learning as well as deep learning techniques to improve image security. In addition, it investigates the use of blockchain technology to ensure the integrity of images and to deter tampering with those images.

The results show that artificial intelligence, encryption systems, and blockchain systems provide improved security, integrity, and access control for intelligent image systems31. Collectively, these studies point to a trend toward employing compression, encryption, and watermarking in conjunction with each other for both image and text data. In particular, this trend is consistent with our research proposal which looks to classify data as either sensitive or non-sensitive and then apply compression, encryption or watermarking only to the sensitive data.

Research methodology

The proposed methodology is based on developing an integrated system for processing PDF or image files of a financial and sensitive nature, while ensuring that data is efficiently compressed, protected by encryption, and its integrity verified via watermarks and hashing algorithms. The general framework consists of three interconnected stages: processing the sender’s data, interacting with the graphical interface, and then processing the receiver’s data. To assess the effectiveness of this system in compressing and protecting financial data, a multi-stage framework was adopted as shown in Fig. 1.

The general diagram of the proposed system.

The complete system architecture for the proposed financial data compression and protection system, which consists of three main components (a sending party, an intermediary graphical interface system, and a receiving party that verifies data integrity), is shown in Fig. 1. The user launches the system and selects the file type (CSV, PDF, or image); then there is a series of processing, compression, and encryption steps before the encrypted packet is transmitted to the receiving party.

In Sender PC Processing, as shown in Fig. 2, the financial data is first processed on the sender’s device. Sensitive data is separated from non-sensitive data, and appropriate encryption and compression are applied. A CSV file is uploaded, after which a SHA3‑256 fingerprint is generated for the sensitive data and embedded as an LSB watermark in the numeric values.

Sender PC processing diagram.

Next, quantization and delta encoding operations are performed. Multiple compression algorithms (Zstd, LZMA, Brotli, AI-based) are compared, and the best one is selected. The resulting data is then encrypted using Fernet, entropy is calculated, and a Chi-Square test is performed before compiling the files and sending the packet.

Finally, once the data is prepared, the files are formatted for interaction with the user interface, and PDFs or images are converted into processable data.

As for the user interface and OCR, Fig. 3, the middle section explains that the system supports PDF files and images via a graphical interface; The user chooses the file type, then the pages are converted into images, and OCR is applied to them to extract the financial lines and convert them into a CSV that is re-entered in the previous processing path.

Receiver PC processing steps.

For Receiver PC Processing, the third party receives the encrypted packet, then uploads the encrypted file and key, decrypts with Fernet, decompresses (Zstd), and then delta decodes to recover the values.

The graphical user interface makes it easy to convert PDF files and images into processable data, extract financial data lines using OCR, and then re-enter them into the previous processing path.

After receiving the encrypted packet, the receiving device decrypts, recovers, and validates the data using the watermark and SHA3-256 recalculated from the retrieved data and compared. If the values match, the data is declared reliable and secure. If they differ, integrity is considered violated, and an integrity check report is issued.

Data preprocessing and secure compression framework

Raw financial records are commonly distributed in heterogeneous formats, such as PDF documents and scanned reports. Such sources may contain formatting artifacts, special symbols, missing indicators (e.g., “–”), and inconsistencies introduced during digitization. So, the data are first extracted and structured into comma-separated value (CSV) format to ensure standardized machine readability. All non-numeric artifacts are removed prior to further processing.

Sensitive and non-sensitive attribute identification

Based on financial disclosure structure and security risk analysis, the dataset attributes are categorized into two groups:

Sensitive attributes: Current Month Gross Receipts, Outlay, and Deficit/Surplus. These fields contain financially critical information and are subjected to secure compression and encryption.

Non-sensitive attributes: Metadata and auxiliary columns, which are used for SHA3-256 cryptographic watermark generation but are not encrypted.

This separation enables selective protection and also helps to preserve structural integrity and enable tamper detection.

Autoencoder-based structural compression

A lightweight autoencoder is applied to the sensitive attributes after preprocessing so as to further enhance compression efficiency. This is because the objective is reconstruction rather than prediction, and the model is trained in an unsupervised manner on the complete dataset. No train–validation–test split is used because the task does not involve generalization to unseen data. The autoencoder performs deterministic reconstruction to compact correlated features into a lower-dimensional latent representation. Performance is evaluated using entropy, chi-square test, memory usage, execution time, and compression ratio and not predictive accuracy.

Full autoencoder training

A simple autoencoder architecture is employed to learn a compact representation of the sensitive financial attributes. The encoder consists of an input layer, an intermediate hidden expansion layer, and a low-dimensional latent bottleneck, followed by a symmetric decoder. However, it is observed that although the encoder has one intermediate hidden layer with four neurons, the overall structure remains undercomplete, because the latent space consists of only two neurons, which is strictly less than the three features in the input space. Hence, the hidden layer is not the compressed space, but it acts as the nonlinear feature transformation stage, which extends the process of feature extraction by incorporating the complex interrelation between the input variables. The compression occurs in the latent space, where the data is transformed into a two-dimensional space Z ∈ R n×2. In this way, the compression is guaranteed because the latent space is smaller compared to the original input space.

NIST standard test results

To find out the results of applying the NIST SP 800 − 22 standard randomization test set to three data sets (dataset1–dataset3), using four basic tests: frequency test (Frequency), block frequency (Block Frequency), chain test (Runs), and longest chain (Longest Run), as shown in Fig. 11.

Multi-dataset NIST SP 800 − 22 standard randomization test results.

On the left side of Fig. 11, it is noted that the overall success rate for each data set is approximately (25%), meaning that each group passed only one test out of four. This ratio is low compared to the high reference limit shown by a dashed line close to (100%), which represents the ideal case for generating strong random numbers. This finding indicates that the resulting sequences lack sufficient randomness when tested using the given NIST test set and that the performance of the generator or mechanism behind the production of the said data is still poor in terms of statistical quality.

The (p)-value values in the individual tests are shown in Fig. 11 on the right side. It happens that only the Block Frequency test provides values with the acceptable statistical significance (i.e., p\ge 0.01) and all three groups obtained relatively high values (respectively 0.31, 0.71, and 0.39). It means that the allocation of bits in subblocks is statistically balanced to a reasonable degree. However, the Frequency, Runs, and Longest Run tests did not reach values of the required threshold, implying that the sequences did not show global randomness properties, i.e., the overall equilibrium of zero and one, the regularity of the sequence and the length of the sequence.

In general, these results suggest that a system or algorithm is able to achieve limited local randomness (within blocks) but fails to achieve comprehensive randomness at the full chain level. Therefore, these sequences cannot be considered suitable for applications requiring high levels of randomness, such as encryption or sensitive security systems, without further improvements to the generation mechanism or the integration of randomness enhancement techniques (Randomness Enhancement).

The unification of security and efficiency by using watermarking, hashing, encryption and multi-algorithm compression to compromise data protection and storage efficiency is one of the major successes of the proposed approach. The performance of the proposed system is discussed in this section through an integrated quantitative analysis that highlights its behavior across multiple datasets.

Overall performance behavior across datasets

The quantitative outcomes received represent the stable response of the suggested system to the datasets of different sizes. It is noted that the compression efficiency curves down slowly with data size, which is reasonable in intelligent compression systems since more complicated structures and diversity of patterns will be present in larger data sets. The given relative reduction notwithstanding, the system ensures a considerable enhancement over the relatively conservative ZSTD algorithm, which proves that the proposed model can be adjusted to the different data properties without affecting its fundamental benefits.

Interpretation of improvement rates and their relationship to dataset size

The compression improvement rate is the highest in Dataset1, and this can be attributed to the high rate of iteration and prediction that can be easily accomplished with small datasets; thus, the intelligent model is able to gain higher compression gains. Contrastingly, the rate of improvement is low in Dataset2 and Dataset3. Nonetheless, this reduction is not a sign of the algorithm’s weakness, but it is an indication of the system transitioning to more complicated datasets, instead of simple local patterns. Such behavior indicates the maturity of the algorithm and its capability to work in real-life situations.

Entropy as a security indicator after compression and encryption

Security-wise, the values of post-processed entropy are extremely stable in all datasets and are very close to the security threshold. This response is to argue that the compression optimization procedure did not adversely affect the chance of the outcome data, and it supports the hypothesis that the suggested system does not create a statistical tendency, which can be used in undo analysis or statistical attack. Moreover, the fact that the values of entropy have significant convergence across various datasets is a sign that the security characteristics are not dependent on the data size.

The chi-squared test and its statistical significance

This was supported by the findings of the chi-squared test, which indicated that the chi-squared test significantly increased as opposed to the theoretical reference values, with extremely low p-values. This means the resulting data are statistically distributed in a way that cannot be explained by any non-random distribution, and therefore, there is no easy way to infer some significant information using the statistical properties of the data. Interestingly, the value of chi-square rises with the size of the data and not the other way around, since there is an improvement in randomness as the size of the sample increases.

Balancing efficiency and security

Summing up all the results obtained, it is evident that the offered system provides a definite balance between enhancing the efficiency of compression and preserving the security features. Although the rate of improvement is inherently different in each of the datasets, the security indicators are high and consistent across all. This is one of the key factors in the comparison of any AI-enhanced compression system because performance gains can frequently be achieved at the expense of security breaches, which is not the situation with the present outcomes.

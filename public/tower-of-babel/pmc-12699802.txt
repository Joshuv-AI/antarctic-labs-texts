Polygenicity and APOE ε4 shape response to intervention in mild cognitive impairment
Authors: Jiyun Hwang, So Young Moon, Harim Lee, Jaejin Lee, Yoo Kyoung Park, Jee Hyang Jeong, Chang Hyung Hong, Jiwoo Jung, Hae Ri Na, Soo Hyun Cho, Joohon Sung, Soo Ji Lee, Seong Hye Choi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultidomain lifestyle interventions have shown effectiveness in preventing dementia, but identifying high-risk groups most likely to benefit remains unclear.MethodsWe re-evaluated the SUPERBRAIN-MEET multidomain intervention study in mild cognitive impairment (MCI) patients, incorporating polygenic risk scores (PRS) for Alzheimer’s disease and APOE ε4 status using Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS) total index as the primary outcome.ResultsBoth intervention and control groups showed cognitive improvement over 24 weeks, with greater gains in the intervention arm. Relative intervention efficacy (RIE) increased with higher genetic risk, being most pronounced among APOE ε4 carriers and individuals with high PRS. When both factors were considered jointly, APOE ε4 carriers with high PRS exhibited the largest RIE (β = 7.54, SE = 2.59, p = 0.005), driven by markedly greater improvement in the intervention group. The secondary outcomes did not show as consistent results as RBANS total index.DiscussionThese findings suggest that MCI individuals who are APOE ε4 carriers with high PRS may benefit most from multidomain interventions. These results support the complementary use of PRS and APOE status for identifying high-risk subgroups most likely to benefit from multidomain interventions.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05023057. Introduction

Multidomain lifestyle interventions have been shown to delay cognitive decline and reduce dementia risk in at-risk older adults [1–4], as evidenced by landmark trials such as the Finnish Geriatric Intervention Study to Prevent Cognitive Impairment and Disability (FINGER) [1, 5] and the Korean SUPERBRAIN trial [6–9]. In a follow-up randomized controlled trial (RCT) known as SUPERBRAIN-MEET (Multidomain interventions via facE-to-facE and video communication plaTforms in mild cognitive impairment (MCI)), a 24-week multidomain program improved cognitive outcomes in older adults with MCI, although benefits differed by APOE ε4 status [10, 11]. Notably, Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) improvements were observed only among APOE ε4 non-carriers, suggesting potential variation in intervention efficacy based on genetic susceptibility [11].

Dementia is a multifactorial disease influenced by both genetic and environmental factors [12]. Polygenic risk scores (PRS), which quantify aggregate genetic liability from numerous common variants, have been shown to enhance prediction of dementia [13–16] and MCI [17–19] beyond APOEgenotyping [20–22]. While APOE ε4 remains the single strongest genetic determinant of Alzheimer’s disease (AD), PRS captures additional genetic susceptibility that APOE alone does not explain. However, the extent to which APOE or PRS modify the cognitive benefits of multidomain lifestyle interventions remains poorly understood. The re-evaluation of FINGER study suggested that interventions may counteract genetic risk, as amyloid accumulation was attenuated in individuals with high genetic risk score of AD [23].

In this study, we reanalyzed data from the SUPERBRAIN-MEET trial to investigate how genetic susceptibility shapes the cognitive efficacy of a 24-week multidomain intervention. To comprehensively represent genetic risk, we incorporated both detailed APOE genotype classification (ε2, ε3, ε4 combinations) and genome-wide PRS, allowing for stratified analyses across distinct genetic profiles. By comparing intervention efficacy across APOE and PRS-defined subgroups, we aimed to identify individuals most likely to benefit from early preventive strategies.

Methods

SUPERBRAIN-MEET was a 24-week, multicenter, outcome assessor-blinded, RCT conducted at 17 hospitals in South Korea. Detailed rationale and methodology have been published elsewhere [10]. Briefly, participants aged 60–85 years who sought medical care for cognitive decline were eligible if they had at least one modifiable risk factor for dementia. Individuals were excluded if they had severe psychiatric conditions, dementia, other neurodegenerative disorders, recent or active malignancies (within 5 years), significant cardiovascular disease, sensory or communication impairments, illiteracy, inability to safely complete exercise programs, or simultaneous participation in other trials. The 24-week multidomain intervention program addressed five key areas: metabolic and vascular risk management, cognitive training, physical exercise, nutritional education, and motivational enhancement. The study was conducted in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) guidelines, and ethical approval was granted by the institutional review boards (IRBs) of all participating institutions. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their inclusion in the study. The trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05023057) [10].

Interventions were delivered through a combination of in-person sessions and remote activities via the SUPERBRAIN application and ZOOM platform. Participants were provided with a tablet PC and exercise materials for home use. The control group received standard care for cognitive impairment and vascular risk factors, educational materials on dementia prevention, and the option to join the multidomain intervention program after study completion.

The primary outcome was the change in the RBANS total scale index from baseline to 24 weeks, using normative data for Korean adults. Secondary outcomes included assessments of mood, disability, quality of life, physical activity, motivation, sleep, and vascular risk factors. All outcome evaluations were conducted at baseline and within 4 weeks of the study completion. Participants who discontinued early were asked to complete endpoint assessments at withdrawal. Higher scores in Clinical Dementia Rating Scale-Sum of Boxes (SB), Prospective Retrospective Memory Questionnaire (PRMQ), Prospective Retrospective Memory Questionnaire by caregiver (PRMQC), Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS), the Bayer- activities of daily living (BADL), and Pittsburg Sleep Questionnaire Index (PSQI) indicate worse health outcomes.

Genome-wide genotyping of DNA samples from a total of 285 participants were performed with Affymetrix Axiom Korean Chip version 1.1 [24]. Genotype quality control excluded single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) with call rate < 95%, Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium P value < 1 × 10–6, or minor allele frequency < 1%. Samples with missing rate > 5% or heterozygosity > 3 standard deviations were also excluded. Genotypes were imputed with the Korean Imputation Server Phase 1 panel [25], resulting in 14,379,527 SNPs. We used PLINK version 1.9 software [26] for the quality control of SNPs and samples and the calculation of polygenic scores. APOE genotypes were separately assessed using quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), and APOE ε4 allele was determined by two SNPs (rs429358 and rs7412). Individual PRSs were calculated using PRS-CS [27]. PRS-CSx [28] method was also used to transfer the ethnic differences in genetic architectures, based on AD genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics from DeRojas et al. (409,435 individuals) [29] for Europeans, and Shigemizu et al. (8,036 individuals) [30] for East Asians. Standard quality control of GWAS summary statistics was performed as in the guidelines for PRS analysis [31]. PRSs using European, East Asian, and trans-ethnic method were compared to select one with optimal discriminative power, assessed via t-tests comparing PRS distributions between the MCI and general population from the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study (KoGES) [32]. We also calculated PRS excluding SNPs surrounding APOE regions (hereafter “PRS(-)APOE”) for the sensitivity analysis. PRS(-)APOE was additionally computed by excluding variants located within 1 Mb upstream and 1 Mb downstream of the APOE locus (chromosome 19, 44,000 to 46,500 kb, GRCh37/hg19) to assess polygenic effects independent of APOE, following prior AD PRS studies [14, 22, 33]. Percentile ranks of PRS in the Korean population, rather than the raw scores, were used as PRS measures in downstream analyses.

PRS levels were regressed on the intervention outcomes, adjusted for age and sex. Changes in outcome measures, representing intervention efficacy (IE, see Table 1) in each intervention arm, were calculated as the percentage change from baseline [(follow-up measured value – baseline measured value)/baseline measured value × 100]. The interaction effect between PRS and the intervention was tested by including the significance of the multiplicative term between PRS and initially allocated group. Same analyses were conducted by stratifying participants according to APOEgenotype: ε2 carriers (ε2/ε2 or ε2/ε3), ε3 homozygotes (ε3/ε3), and ε4 carriers (ε3/ε4 or ε4/ε4). Participants with the ε2/ε4 genotype were excluded due to possible conflicting risk directions of the two alleles [34]. In additional analyses, we compared ε4 carrier vs. non-carrier and ε2 carrier vs. non-carrier groups. Other genes were not considered due to lower impact and frequency. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.3.

Results

Among the PRS models evaluated, the PRS derived from DeRojas et al.’s AD GWAS summary statistics showed the most significant difference in PRS distributions between MCI and the general population (t-test p = 1.22 × 10–4) and was selected for further analysis.

Baseline characteristics between the arms were largely similar (Table 2, Supplemental Table 1 in Additional File 1). Improvement in RBANS total index was greater for intervention group than control (p = 1.76 × 10–4), along with in visuoconstruction index score of RBANS (VISUO, p = 0.001), delayed memory index score of RBANS (DM, p = 0.046), and MMSE (p = 1.50 × 10–4). When stratified by APOE ε4 carrier status (Fig. 1A) and by APOE genotype combinations (Fig. 1B), RIE was positive across all subgroups, indicating greater IE in the intervention than in the control group (Supplemental Fig. 1 A, B in Additional File 1), with the RIE observed among ε4 carriers. Across the PRS continuum (Fig. 1C), RIE increased with higher PRS values, indicating that although overall IE tended to be lower among individuals with higher PRS (Supplemental Fig. 1 C in Additional File 1), the intervention group maintained greater IE relative to the control group. When jointly stratified by APOE genotype and PRS, RIE increased with higher PRS among ε4 carriers, whereas it slightly decreased among non-carriers (Fig. 1D, E and Supplemental Fig. 2 in Additional File 1), with the largest and statistically significant RIE observed among ε4 carriers with high PRS (β = 7.54, SE = 2.59, p = 0.005; Table 3 and Supplemental Table 2, 3 in Additional File 1). To complement Fig. 1, individual data points were visualized to show participant-level variability in RBANS changes (Supplemental Fig. 3 in Additional file 1). Although the interaction between PRS and RIE was not statistically significant in the full sample, it became significant within the ε4 carrier subgroup (interaction p = 0.0404). These findings were replicated when using PRS(-)APOE (Supplemental Fig. 4 in Additional File 1).

Discussion

APOE ε4 is a well-established genetic risk factor for AD and dementia. PRS captures polygenic contributions beyond APOE and has been shown to improve risk stratification, particularly when used in combination with APOE genotype [14, 21, 22, 35]. Our findings extend this utility by demonstrating that PRS may also inform the likely benefit from multidomain interventions, guiding more tailored prevention strategies in individuals with MCI.

Our findings align with prior multidomain intervention trials. In analyses not incorporating PRS, the original SUPERBRAIN-MEET trial demonstrated improvements in RBANS total index for both APOE ε4 carriers and non-carriers, though MMSE gains were limited to non-carriers [11]. The FINGER neuroimaging substudy reported reduced brain amyloid deposition among individuals with high PRS or APOE ε4 non-carriers receiving intervention [23], and the Japan-Multimodal Intervention Trial for the Prevention of Dementia (J-MINT) study reported significant cognitive improvement in high PRS MCI participants [36]. Together, these results suggest that genetic profiles modify IE across the AD continuum, from early amyloid accumulation to established amyloidopathy [14, 37]. Individuals with high AD PRS often carry greater burden of modifiable cardio-metabolic risk factors [38–40], creating greater potential for intervention-mediated improvement [7, 41–44]. This echoes findings in coronary heart disease prevention, where high PRS enhances the benefit from statins and antihypertensives [45, 46]. Thus, polygenic vulnerability may increase the capacity for intervention benefit by capturing latent, modifiable susceptibility.

Importantly, we newly demonstrated that APOE ε4 carriers can be further stratified by PRS. ε4 carriers in the upper PRS stratum showed the clearest RIE, driven by markedly greater IE in the intervention group rather than decline in controls. This subgroup − ε4 carriers with high PRS − appears most responsive, likely because they are at the highest risk for decline in the absence of such intervention. In contrast, among non-carriers, polygenic effects may follow a different trajectory. Mechanistically, previous studies have suggested that certain genetic variants exert differential effects depending on APOE ε4 status [20, 47–49]. While intervention studies remain limited, emerging observational evidence indicates that favorable lifestyle behaviors (e.g., healthy diet, regular physical activity, non-smoking, and cognitive engagement) may more effectively offset the impact of high PRS in ε4 carriers than in non-carriers [50–52]. Moreover, higher PRS accelerates age at onset and increases dementia risk specifically in ε4 carriers but shows weaker or no associations in non-carriers [35]. These findings suggest that the interplay between lifestyle, PRS, and APOE ε4 may shape intervention responsiveness, supporting the rationale for stratification strategies in preventive trials.

Comparing PRS and PRS(-)APOE revealed moderate attenuation when excluding the APOE region. Although both metrics yielded directionally consistent results, PRS(-)APOE showed reduced IE among ε4 carriers. Given the extended genomic region (~ 2.1 Mbp) excluded in PRS(-)APOE, this attenuation likely reflects contributions from surrounding variants beyond ε4 itself. These findings suggest that conventional PRS may better capture biologically relevant risk and provide greater predictive value for prioritizing intervention targets.

This study has several strengths. It is among the few RCTs to incorporate PRS in evaluating their role in modifying intervention response in MCI. Leveraging the relative abundance in ε4 carriers, we were able to conduct stratified analyses and identify high-priority subgroups. Parallel analyses using PRS and PRS(-)APOE provided additional insight into the contribution of APOE-linked versus polygenic risk. Additionally, the use of a multidomain intervention reflects real-world prevention strategies and enhances the translational relevance of the findings for clinical practice.

Several limitations warrant consideration. While the sample size was sufficient to detect overall intervention effects in the mother study, it may have been underpowered to detect more subtle PRS-by-intervention interactions, particularly in stratified analyses by APOE ε4 status or sex. In addition, as most GWAS data are from European populations, the applicability of standardized PRS to non-European populations may be limited, potentially reducing predictive power. However, the transferred PRS demonstrated discriminative validity in the Korean population. The 24-week intervention period may also restrict inference on long-term outcomes. Furthermore, although information on amnestic and non-amnestic MCI subtypes was available, biomarker data to differentiate underlying etiologies such as AD, vascular, or metabolic causes were not collected. Future studies incorporating biomarker-based classification will be essential to disentangle subtype-specific intervention responses. Lastly, because the multidomain intervention was delivered as an integrated program with uniformly high adherence across all domains, domain-specific effects could not be disentangled, limiting mechanistic interpretation.

In conclusion, multidomain intervention efficacy appears modified by both APOE ε4 status and polygenic risk. We newly identified APOE ε4 carriers with high PRS as an additional high-risk group most likely to benefit. If effectively implemented, PRS could support a more personalized approach to AD prevention, ultimately enhancing clinical outcomes.

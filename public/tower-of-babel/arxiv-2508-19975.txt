Abstract
. Bounded composition operators in Paley-Wiener spaces have ones simple forms, and they jsut operators composed through affine mappings of the complex plane. The purpose of this article is to explore some notions about bounded operators and linear dynamics and provide complete answers for composition operators in Paley-Wiener spaces concerning to compactness, spectrum, spectral radius, Li-Yorke chaos, positive expansivity, positive shading property, and Cesàro’s absolute boundedness.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex Hilbert space of analytic function on a subset
\Omega
of the plane complex
\mathbb{C}
and
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
an analytic function. The composition operator
C_{\phi}
as symbol
\phi
is defined by

C_{\phi}f=f\circ\phi,\quad f\in\mathcal{H}.

Operators of this type have been studied on a variety of spaces, such as Bergman, Dirichlet series, Hardy, Fock and Newton spaces see
[
13
,
15
,
19
,
21
,
24
,
26
]
, and with special interest on the Hardy space of the open unit disk. As is known in the literature, there are a variety of basic questions in the study of composition operators; boundedness
[
15
,
18
]
, compactness
[
30
]
, norms
[
11
,
13
,
15
]
, spectra
[
15
,
19
]
, spectral radius
[
15
,
18
]
, normality
[
15
,
19
]
, complex symmetry
[
22
,
23
,
27
,
28
,
31
]
, cyclicity
[
10
,
20
,
22
,
28
,
29
]
, hypercyclicity
[
10
,
20
,
29
]
, supercyclicity
[
14
,
20
]
and more recently notions such as expansivity (and its variants), Li-Yorke chaoticity and the positive shadowing property
[
2
,
7
]
. In many cases, it is difficult to solve these problems completely and some problems are still open, see
[
16
]
for instance.

For
a>0,
the Paley-Wiener space
PW_{a}
consists of all entire functions
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of exponential type less than or equal to
a,
such that the norm

\left\|f\right\|^{2}=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}|f(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Each Paley-Wiener
PW_{a}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space which means the point evaluations of function on
PW_{a}
are bounded linear functionals. Indeed, if
w\in\mathbb{C}
and
f\in PW_{a}
then
f(w)=\langle f,k_{w}^{a}\rangle
where the reproducing kernel function
k_{w}^{a}
is given by

k_{w}^{a}(z)=\frac{\sin a(z-\overline{w})}{\pi(z-\overline{w})},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

The sequence of kernels
(k_{\frac{\pi n}{a}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
constitute a complete orthonormal set on
PW_{a}.
The monographs
[
9
,
12
,
25
,
32
]
can be consulted for more information about these spaces.

In
[
14
]
, Chácon,
Chácon and Giménez initiated the study of composition operators on the classical Paley–Wiener
PW_{\pi}.
They proved that
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
PW_{\pi}
if and only if
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C},
and as consequence showed that such operators are not compact or supercyclic. Similar to
PW_{\pi},
the only bounded composition on
PW_{a}
are those induced by symbols of the form
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}.
In
[
22
]
, Hai, Noor and Severiano studied bounded composition operators on the full range of Paley-Wiener
PW_{a},
and they completely characterized which of these operators are self-adjoint, unitary, normal and complex symmetric
[
22
, Proposition 3]
. They also studied properties concerning linear dynamics such as cyclicity and supercyclicity, and they extended the result of
[
14
, Theorem 2.7]
, showing that no Paley–Wiener space
PW_{a}
supports supercyclic bounded composition operators. On the other hand, the cyclicity of
C_{\phi}
occurs if and only if
c=1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
or
0<|d|\leq\pi/a
with
d\in\mathbb{R}

[
22
, Theorem 4]
.

In this article, we continue the study of composition operators on the Paley-Wiener spaces
PW_{a}.
Here we show that certain properties of composition operators concerning bounded operators and linear dynamics can be completely characterized, more precisely, we compute the spectrum and the spectral radius of each bounded composition operator on
PW_{a}.
Moreover, we also completely characterize which of these operators are Li-Yorke chaotic, positive expansive, absolutely Cesàro bounded and have the positive shadowing property.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Bounded composition operator on
PW_{a}

The only bounded composition operators
C_{\phi}
on
PW_{a}
are those induced by affine symbols of the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phi(z)=cz+d\ \text{where}\ c\in\mathbb{R}\ \text{with}\ 0<|c|\leq 1\ \text{and}\ d\in\mathbb{C},

and the adjoint of
C_{\phi}
acts on reproducing kernels as follows
C_{\phi}^{*}k_{w}^{a}=k_{\phi(w)}^{a}
for
w\in\mathbb{C}.
Hence the only invertible bounded composition operators on
PW_{a}
occur when
c=\pm 1.
Hai, Noor and Severiano
[
22
]
studied bounded composition operators on
PW_{a}
and showed that
C_{\phi}
is normal if and only if
c=1
or
c=-1
and
b\in\mathbb{R}.

Let us denote by
\phi^{[n]}
the
n
-th iterate of
\phi,
where
\phi
is as in (
2.1
), then a simple computation gives

(2.2)

\displaystyle\phi^{[n]}(z)=\begin{cases}z+nd,&\text{if}\ c=1,\\
c^{n}z+\frac{\left(1-c^{n}\right)}{1-c}d,&\text{if}\ c\neq 1.\end{cases}

and
C_{\phi}^{n}=C_{\phi^{[n]}}.
Thus, we see from (
2.2
) that if
|c|<1
and
\alpha:=d/(1-c),
then
\alpha
is an attractive fixed point of
\phi
, that is,
\phi^{[n]}(z)\rightarrow\alpha
pointwise as
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}.

2.2.
Spectra of linear operators

We begin by recalling some basic definitions and facts from general spectral theory that will be used in the subsequent sections. Let
\mathcal{B}(X)
denote the space of all bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X.
The spectrum and spectral radius of
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
are denoted by
\sigma(T)
and
r(T),
respectively. It is well known that
r(T)
is determined by the spectral radius formula
r(T)=\displaystyle\lim\|T^{n}\|^{1/n}.

If the operators
T_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(X_{1})
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(X_{2})
are similar (isometrically similar), i.e. there exists an invertible (isometric invertible) operator
U:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that
T_{1}=U^{-1}T_{2}U,
then their spectra coincide. This is very useful for our purposes, since if
\phi(z)=cz+d
is as in (
2.1
) with
c\neq 1
we can write
\phi=\psi\circ\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
where
\varphi(z)=cz
and
\psi(z)=z+d/(1-c),
then
C_{\psi^{-1}}C_{\phi}C_{\psi}=C_{\varphi}.
This implies that
\sigma(C_{\phi})=\sigma(C_{\varphi}).

The next result was first proved in
[
14
, page 2208]
(and corrected in
[
22
, Proposition 2.2]
) and it shows that each bounded composition operator on
PW_{a}
is isometrically similar to a bounded weighted composition operator on
L^{2}[-a,a].

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
,
d\in\mathbb{C},
then
C_{\phi}
on
PW_{a}
is isometrically similar to the weighted composition operator
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
on
L^{2}[-a,a]
defined by

(\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(t)=\frac{1}{|c|}\chi_{(-|c|a,|c|a)}(t)e^{\frac{\mathrm{i}bt}{c}}F\left(\frac{t}{c}\right)

where
\chi_{(p,q)}
denotes a characteristic function. Moreover, we have
(\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}F)(t)=\overline{e^{idt}}F(ct).

Hence, if
\psi(z)=z+d
with
d\in\mathbb{C}
then
C_{\psi}
is isometrically similar to the multiplication operator
M_{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}}.
Therefore,
\sigma(C_{\psi})=\sigma(M_{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}})
and
\|C_{\psi}\|=\|M_{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}}\|_{L^{2}[-a,a]}.
From the multiplication operator theory, we know that if
\Phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a continuous function essentially bounded then
M_{\Phi}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\Omega),

\|M_{\Phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sup\{|\Phi(t)|:t\in\Omega\}
and
\sigma(M_{\Phi})=\{\Phi(t):t\in\Omega\}.
This allows us to determine the norm and the spectrum of
C_{\psi}
on each
PW_{a},
namely
\|C_{\psi}\|=e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}
and
\sigma(C_{\psi})=\{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}:t\in[-a,a]\}.

2.3.
Notions in linear dynamics

Let us introduce some notions concerning linear dynamics that are relevant to our work. Such notions will be studied in subsequent sections.

Definition 2.2
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is said to be Li-Yorke chaotic if there exists
x\in X
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty.

In this case, such vector
x
is called an irregular vector for
T.

Let
\mathbb{T}
denote the unit circle
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}.
Bermúdez
et al.

[
4
]
studied Li-Yorke operators and showed that if
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is a Li-Yorke operator then
T
is not normal and
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}\neq\emptyset.

Definition 2.3
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is said to be positive expansive if for each
x\in X
with
\|x\|=1,
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|T^{n}x\|\geq 2.

Bernardes
et al.

[
6
]
studied expansivity in linear dynamics and showed that if
T\in\mathcal{B}(X),
then
T
is positively expansive if and only if
\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty
for every non-zero vector
x\in X.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(X).

(i)

For
\delta>0,
we say that a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
is a
\delta-
pseudotrajectory of
T,
if
\|Tx_{n}-x_{n+1}\|\leq\delta
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

We say that
T
has the positive shadowing property if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every
\delta-
pseudotrajectory
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
T
is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
x\in X,
that is, there is
x\in X
such that
\|T^{n}x-x_{n}\|\leq\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

In order, to characterize normal operators that possess the positive shadowing property Bernardes
et al.
showed that if
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is a normal operator, then
T
has the positive shadowing property if and only if
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}=\emptyset

[
6
, Theorem 30]
.

Definition 2.5
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is said to be absolutely Cesàro bounded if there exists a constant
L>0
such

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T^{j}x\|\right\}\leq L\|x\|,

for all
x\in X.

We refer to
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
for more details on Li-Yorke chaotic, positive expansive (and its variants), absolutely Cesàro operator and to have the positive shadowing property.

3.
Main results

3.1.
Compactness

We know from
[
14
, Corollary 2.5]
that there no compact composition operator on
PW_{\pi}.
Here we extend this result by showing that no Paley-Wiener space
PW_{a}
supports compact composition operators.

For
w\in\mathbb{C},
we have
\|k_{w}^{a}\|=\sqrt{a/\pi}
when
w\in\mathbb{R}
while

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|k_{w}^{a}\|=\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{sin}a(w-\overline{w})}{\pi(w-\overline{w})}}=\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{sin}2\mathrm{i}a\mathrm{Im}(w)}{2\mathrm{i}\pi\mathrm{Im}(w)}}=\sqrt{\frac{\sinh 2a{\rm Im}\,(w)}{2\pi\mathrm{Im}(w)}}

when
w\notin\mathbb{R}.
This helps us to show the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

There are no compact composition operators on
PW_{a}.

Proof.

By Schauder Theorem
C_{\phi}
is compact on
PW_{a}
if and only if its adjoint is. If
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C},
then
{\rm Im}\,\left(\phi\left(n\pi/a\right)\right)={\rm Im}\,(d)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
K_{n}
denote the normalized reproducing kernel
k^{a}_{\frac{n\pi}{a}},
by (
3.1
) we get

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{*}K_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|k^{a}_{\phi(\frac{n\pi}{a})}\|^{2}}{\|k^{a}_{\frac{n\pi}{a}}\|^{2}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{sinh}2a\mathrm{Im}(d)}{2a\mathrm{Im}(d)},&\text{if}\ \mathrm{Im}(d)\neq 0\\
1,&\text{if}\ \mathrm{Im}(d)=0.\end{cases}

Since
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
tend weakly to zero and
C_{\phi}^{*}K_{n}
does not converges to zero in norm, it follows that
C_{\phi}^{*}
is not compact.
∎

3.2.
Norm

Let us consider the symbol
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C},
then we can write
\phi=\psi\circ\varphi
where
\varphi(z)=cz
and
\psi(z)=z+b.
This gives
C_{\phi}=C_{\varphi}C_{\psi}
and hence we can estimate the norm of
C_{\phi}
from the norms of
C_{\varphi}
and
C_{\psi}.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\varphi(z)=cz
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1,
then
\sqrt{|c|}C_{\varphi}
is an isometry on
PW_{a}

The proof of the lemma above follows from straightforward computations and it is left to the reader.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C},
then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|c|}}\leq\left\|C_{\phi}\right\|\leq\frac{e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}}{\sqrt{|c|}}

on
PW_{a}
. In particular, if
d\in\mathbb{R}
then (
3.2
) is an equality.

Proof.

To obtain the upper estimate, we first consider the auxiliary symbols
\varphi(z)=cz
and
\psi(z)=z+d.
Since
\phi=\psi\circ\varphi,\|C_{\psi}\|=e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}
and
\|C_{\varphi}\|=1/\sqrt{|c|}
we obtain

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}\|\leq\|C_{\varphi}\|\|C_{\psi}\|=e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}/\sqrt{|c|}.

To obtain the lower estimate, we first assume
0<c\leq 1
. If
w\notin\mathbb{R}
and
\operatorname{Im}(w)\neq-\operatorname{Im}(d)/c
, then (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}\|^{2}\geq\frac{\|C_{\phi}^{*}k_{w}^{a}\|^{2}}{\|k_{w}^{a}\|^{2}}=\frac{\|k_{\phi(w)}^{a}\|^{2}}{\left\|k_{w}^{a}\right\|^{2}}=\frac{\operatorname{Im}(w)}{c\operatorname{Im}(w)+\operatorname{Im}(d)}\frac{\sinh 2a(c\operatorname{Im}(w)+\operatorname{Im}(d))}{\sinh 2a\operatorname{Im}(w)}

and since the function
\sinh(x)
is non-decreasing and
\operatorname{Im}(w)<(1-c)^{-1}\operatorname{Im}(d),
we obtain
\|C_{\phi}\|^{2}\geq{\rm Im}\,(w)/[c{\rm Im}\,(w)+{\rm Im}\,(d)]

and hence

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|C_{\phi}\right\|^{2}\geq\lim_{{\rm Im}\,(w)\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{{\rm Im}\,(w)}{c\operatorname{Im}(w)+\operatorname{Im}(d)}=\frac{1}{c}.

Finally we assume
-1\leq c<0.
In this case, we consider the auxiliary symbols
\tilde{\psi}(z)=-z
and
\tilde{\varphi}(z)=

-cz+d
. For what we have done so far and since
C_{\tilde{\psi}}
is an isometric isomorphism
[
22
, Proposition 3]
and
C_{\tilde{\psi}}C_{\phi}=C_{\tilde{\varphi}},
we obtain

\left\|C_{\phi}\right\|\geq\|C_{\tilde{\psi}}C_{\phi}\|=\left\|C_{\tilde{\varphi}}\right\|\geq(-c)^{-1/2}

which gives the desired estimate.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C},
then the spectral radius of
C_{\phi}
on
PW_{a}
is

(i)

r(C_{\phi})=1/\sqrt{|c|},
when
c\neq 1;

(ii)

r(C_{\phi})=e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a},
when
c=1.

Proof.

If
c=1,
then
C_{\phi}
is normal and hence
r(C_{\phi})=\|C_{\phi}\|=e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}.
Now assume
c\neq 1.
By Proposition
3.3
and the formula of the
n
-iterate established in
(
2.2
), we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|c|}}\leq\|C_{\phi}^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{e^{\frac{(1-c^{n})|{\rm Im}\,(d)|\sigma}{n(1-c)}}}{\sqrt{|c|}}.

Letting
n
go to infinity in (
3.4
), we get the equality
r(C_{\phi})=1/\sqrt{|c|}.

∎

3.3.
Spectra and closed range

In sharp contrast to various spaces of analytic functions, on Paley-Wiener we are able to compute the spectrum of all bounded composition operators.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}.
Then the spectrum of
C_{\phi}
acting on
PW_{a}
is

(i)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\{-1,1\},
when
c=-1.

(ii)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|\leq 1/\sqrt{|c|}\right\},
when
0<|c|<1.

(iii)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\{e^{\mathrm{i}bt}:t\in[-a,a]\},
when
c=1.

Proof.

We first assume
c=-1.
In this case,
\phi^{[2]}
is the identity map and hence
C_{\phi}^{2}=I.
Then for the polynomial
p(t)=t^{2}-1,
we obtain
\sigma(p(C_{\phi}))=\sigma(C_{\phi}^{2}-I)=\{0\}.
Moreover, by Spectral Mapping Theorem
[
17
, Corollary 2.37]
, we get
p(\sigma(C_{\phi}))=\sigma(p(C_{\phi})).
Thus
\lambda\in\sigma(C_{\phi})
if and only if
p(\lambda)=0
or equivalently if
\lambda=\pm 1.

Now we assume
0<|c|<1.
Consider the auxiliary symbols
\varphi(z)=cz
and
\psi(z)=z+d/(1-c),
then
C_{\psi}C_{\phi}C_{\psi^{-1}}=C_{\varphi}.
Since
C_{\phi}
is similar to the operator
C_{\varphi},
we have
\sigma(C_{\phi})=\sigma(C_{\varphi}).
By
Lemma
3.2
follows that
\sqrt{|c|}C_{\varphi}
is an isometry non-invertible operator on
PW_{a}.
The spectrum of an isometry non-invertible operator is the closed unit disk, then we are able to deduce that
\sigma(C_{\varphi})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|\leq 1/\sqrt{|c|}\}.

Finally, we assume
c=1.
In this case,
C_{\phi}
is similar the to multiplication operator
\widehat{C}_{\phi}=M_{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}}
and hence
\sigma(C_{\phi})=\sigma(M_{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}})=\{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}:t\in[-a,a]\}.

∎

Now we deal with the problem of identifying the bounded composition operators
PW_{a}
with closed range.

Proposition 3.6
.

Each bounded composition operator on
PW_{a}
has closed range.

Proof.

Let
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}.
If
a=1
then
C_{\phi}
is invertible and hence has closed range. If
a\neq 1,
consider the auxiliary symbols
\varphi(z)=cz
and
\psi(z)=z+b/(1-c)
then
C_{\psi}C_{\phi}C_{\psi^{-1}}=C_{\varphi}.
By Lemma
3.2
,
C_{\varphi}
has closed range. Since closed range is preserved under similarity, it follows that
C_{\phi}
has closed range.
∎

3.4.
Li-Yorke chaos for
C_{\phi}

Now we study the existence of irregular vectors for bounded composition operators in
PW_{a}
and consequently the problem of identifying which theses operators are Li-Yorke chaotic.

The proof below mimics the steps of the proof from
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
, though we enclose it here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|<1
and
d\in\mathbb{C},
then for each
f\in PW_{a}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq(\delta/\sqrt{|c|^{n}})\|f\|

for sufficiently large
n.

Proof.

For each
n,
we consider the auxiliary symbols
\varphi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
defined as follow

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(z)=c^{n}z\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{n}(z)=z+\frac{1-c^{n}}{1-c}d

then
\phi^{[n]}=\psi_{n}\circ\varphi_{n}.
Let
f\in PW_{a},
if
f
is the identically zero function then the result is immediate. So we can assume that
f
is not the identically zero function. By Lemma
3.2
, we get
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|=\|C_{\varphi_{n}}C_{\psi_{n}}f\|=(1/\sqrt{|c|^{n}})\|C_{\psi_{n}}f\|.

Now we obtain a lower estimate for
\|C_{\psi_{n}}f\|.
For each
w\in\mathbb{C},
the Cauchy Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\|C_{\psi_{n}}f\|\|k_{w}^{a}\|\geq|\langle C_{\psi_{n}}f,k_{w}^{a}\rangle|=|\langle f,C_{\psi_{n}}^{*}k_{w}^{a}\rangle|=|\langle f,k_{\psi_{n}(w)}^{a}\rangle|=|f(\psi_{n}(w))|

for all
n.
Since
\psi_{n}(w)\rightarrow w+d/(1-c)
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
we obtain
|f(\psi_{n}(w))|\rightarrow|f(w+d/(1-c))|
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Let
w_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
f(w_{1})\neq 0,
where
w_{1}=w_{0}+d/(1-c).
Then for sufficiently large
n,
we get

|f(\psi_{n}(w_{0}))|>|f(w_{1})|/2.
If
\delta=|f(w_{1})|/2\|k_{w_{0}}^{a}\|,
we obtain
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|>(\delta/\sqrt{|c|^{n}})\|f\|

for sufficiently large
n.

∎

If
\phi(z)=-z+d
then
\phi^{[2]}
is the identity map. This implies that
C_{\phi}^{2}=I
is the identity operator and hence for each
n,

C_{\phi}^{2n}=I
also is the identity operator and
C_{\phi}^{2n+1}=C_{\phi}.
In this case, from Proposition
3.2
we conclude that
\|C^{n}_{\phi}\|\leq e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}
for all
n.

Proposition 3.8
.

No bounded composition on
PW_{a}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

Proof.

Let
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}.
If
c=1
then
C_{\phi}
is normal on
PW_{a}
and hence it is not Li-Yorke chaotic
[
4
, Corollary 6]
. If
c=-1
then
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\leq e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}\|f\|
for all
n
and all
f\in PW_{a}.

Hence
\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\leq e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}\|f\|,
which implies that
C_{\phi}
does not admit an irregular vector. Now assume
0<|c|<1.
By Lemma
3.7
, for each
f\in PW_{a}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|>(\delta/\sqrt{|c|^{n}})\|f\|.
Since
1/\sqrt{|c^{n}|}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and
f
is not the identically zero function, it follows that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq 2
for sufficiently large
n.
In this case,
\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq 2
and hence
C_{\phi}
does not admit an irregular vector.
∎

3.5.
Positive shadowing property of
C_{\phi}

In order to characterize which bounded composition operators
C_{\phi}
on
PW_{a}
have the positive shadowing property, we analyze the value of
c.
If
c=1,
then
C_{\phi}
is normal and hence we can decide whether
C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property from
\sigma(C_{\phi}).
If
c\neq 1,
then
\phi
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{C}.
Then we can proceed as the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 1]
to decide whether
C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property property.

Proposition 3.9
.

No bounded composition operator on
PW_{a}
has the positive shadowing property.

Proof.

Let
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}.
If
c=1
then
C_{\phi}
is a normal operator on
PW_{a}.
In this case,
[
6
, Theorem 30]
ensures that
C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property if and only if
\sigma(C_{\phi})\cap\mathbb{T}=\emptyset.
By Theorem
3.5
,
\sigma(C_{\phi})\cap\mathbb{T}\neq\emptyset.
Therefore,
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property. Now assume
c\neq 1,
then
\alpha=d/(1-c)
is the fixed point of
\phi.
Choose
f\in PW_{a}
such that
f(\alpha)\neq 0.
For each
\delta>0,
the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined as follows

\displaystyle f_{n}=\frac{\delta}{\|C_{\phi}f\|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-j}C_{\phi}^{n-j}f

is a
\delta-
pseudotrajectory of
C_{\phi}.
Moreover,
f_{n}(\alpha)=\frac{n\delta f(\alpha)}{\|C_{\phi}f\|}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|\|k_{\alpha}^{a}\|\geq|\langle C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n},k_{\alpha}^{a}\rangle|=|g(\alpha)-\frac{n\delta f(\alpha)}{\|C_{\phi}f\|}|\geq\frac{n\delta|f(\alpha)|}{\|C_{\phi}f\|}-|g(\alpha)|

for every
g\in PW_{a}.
Taking
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.5
), we obtain
\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty
which shows that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
cannot be
\epsilon
-shadowed for any
\epsilon>0.

∎

3.6.
Positively expansivity for
C_{\phi}

Now we deal with the problem of identifying the composition operators in
PW_{a}
that are positively expansive. Before proceeding, we need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.10
.

If
\phi(z)=z+d
where
d\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
then for each non-identically zero function
f\in PW_{a}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq\delta e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|na}
for all
n.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, there exists an isometric isomorphism
\mathcal{U}:PW_{a}\rightarrow L^{2}[-a,a]
such that
C_{\phi}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}M_{e^{\mathrm{i}dt}}\mathcal{U}.
Let
f\in PW_{a}
be a non-identically zero function. Then
\mathcal{U}f
is almost whenever not zero on
L^{2}[-a,a].
Hence there exists a real number
\ell>0
such that
A_{\ell}=\{t\in[-a,a]:|\mathcal{U}f(t)|\geq\ell\}
has positive measure. Now observe that

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|M_{e^{\mathrm{i}ndt}}\mathcal{U}f\|_{L^{2}[-a,a]}^{2}=\int_{-a}^{a}|e^{\mathrm{i}ndt}\mathcal{U}f(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{A_{\ell}}|e^{\mathrm{i}ndt}\mathcal{U}f(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\ell^{2}\int_{A_{\ell}}e^{-2{\rm Im}\,(d)nt}\mathrm{d}t\geq\ell^{2}e^{2|{\rm Im}\,(d)|na}\int_{A_{\ell}}\mathrm{d}t.

If
\delta=\ell\left(\int_{A_{\ell}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2},
we obtain
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq\delta e^{|{\rm Im}\,(b)|na}
for all
n.

∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
C_{\phi}
is positively expansive on
PW_{a}
if and only if
0<|c|<1
or
c=1
and
d\notin\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We first assume
c=-1.
In this case, we have already seen that

\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\leq e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)\|a}\|f\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
f\in PW_{a}.
Since the supremum of
\{\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is finite for each
f\in PW_{a}
, it follows from
[
6
, Proposition 19]
that
C_{\phi}
is not positively expansive. If
0<|c|<1
then Lemma
3.7
shows that for each
f\in PW_{a}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq(\delta/\sqrt{|c|^{n}})\|f\|
for sufficiently large
n.
Since
f
is not the identically zero function and
1/\sqrt{|c|^{n}}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
it follows that there exists
n
sufficiently large such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq 2.
Hence
C_{\phi}
is positively expansive. Finally let
c=1.
If
d\in\mathbb{R}
then
C_{\phi}
is a unitary operator
[
22
, Proposition 3]
and hence
C_{\phi}
is not positively expansive. If
d\notin\mathbb{R}
then by Lemma
3.10
for each non-identically zero function
f\in PW_{a}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq\delta e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|na}
for all
n.
Since
e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|na}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
it follows that there exists
n
sufficiently large such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq 2
and hence
C_{\phi}
is positively expansive.
∎

3.7.
Abosulutely Cesàro bounded
C_{\phi}

Finally we study absolutely Cesàro bounded. As we will see below, the main keys to identify which composition operators on
PW_{a}
are absolutely Cesàro bounded are the estimates for its norms obtained in Proposition
3.2
and the inequalities from Lemmas
3.7
and
3.10
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\phi(z)=cz+d
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<|c|\leq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
C_{\phi}
absolutely Cesàro bounded on
PW_{a}
if and only if
c=-1
or
c=1
and
d\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

If
c=-1
or
c=1
and
d\in\mathbb{R},
then
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\leq e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}\|f\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
f\in PW_{a}.
In both cases we obtain

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C^{j}_{\phi}f\|\right\}\ \leq e^{|{\rm Im}\,(d)|a}\|f\|.

If
0<|c|<1
then the sequence
(1/n\sqrt{|c|^{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded. On the other hand, Lemma
3.7
ensures that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C^{n}_{\phi}k_{1}^{a}\|\geq(\delta/\sqrt{|c|^{n}})\|k_{1}^{a}\|
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Taking sufficiently large
n,
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C^{j}_{\phi}k_{1}^{a}\|\geq\frac{1}{n}\|C_{\phi}^{n}k_{1}^{a}\|\geq\frac{\delta}{n\sqrt{|c|^{n}}}\|k_{1}^{a}\|

which implies

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C^{j}_{\phi}k_{1}^{a}\|\right\}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{\delta}{n\sqrt{|c|^{n}}}\right\}\|k_{1}^{a}\|=\infty.

Therefore,
C_{\phi}
is not an absolutely Cesàro bounded operator. If
c=1
and
d\notin\mathbb{R},
we use Lemma
3.10
and and we argue as in the case
0<|c|<1.
Hence, in this case
C_{\phi}
also is not an absolutely Cesàro bounded operator.
∎

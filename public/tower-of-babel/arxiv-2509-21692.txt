A Disk-Originated 329-day Quasi-Periodic Oscillation in the Seyfert 1 Galaxy J1626+5120
Litao Zhu, Zhongxiang Wang, Dong Zheng, Alok C. Gupta, Ju-Jia Zhang

Abstract
The Seyfert 1 galaxy J1626+5120 is estimated to host a 10 8 ​ M ⊙ 10^{8}M_{\odot} black hole (BH) accreting at Eddington ratio m ˙ Edd ≈ 0.043 \dot{m}_{\text{Edd}}\approx 0.043 . Its long-term multi-band light curve data show flicker-like variations, but in a well-sampled g g -band light curve, we are able to determine a ≃ 329 \simeq 329 d quasi-periodic oscillation (QPO) at a ∼ \sim 4.53 σ \sigma significance. Six optical spectra were obtained for the source, three of which were taken by us. The spectra show that the variations were mainly because of flux changes blueward of 4000 Å. We also analyze X-ray and ultraviolet (UV) data obtained with the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory (Swift) , which targeted the source in the past two years. X-ray and UV emissions of the source show variations correlated with optical. Time lags of four UV bands and four optical bands are determined with respect to the X-ray emission, which are consistent with a continuum reprocessing disk model. These properties point out a disk origin for the QPO, likely due to Lense-Thirring (LT) precession of the accretion flow at ∼ \sim 20 gravitational radii of the BH. This QPO could be a key case linking sub-year long QPOs in jets, which have more cases reported, to LT precession.



I
introduction

Quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) are one type of phenomenon frequently
observed in X-ray binaries (XRBs) and occasionally in active galactic
nuclei (AGNs; e.g.,
Remillard & McClintock 2006
;
Ingram & Motta 2019
). XRB QPOs have been
extensively
studied and are believed to reveal the accretion activities of
black holes (BHs; as well as neutron stars). Thus, studies of QPOs can
provide additional information
for the physical picture and processes of BH accretion, besides
regular studies such as X-ray spectroscopy and multi-band observations.
As the QPO frequency
\nu
is considered
to scale inversely with BH mass
M_{\rm BH}
,
\nu\sim
10 Hz
in XRBs would appear to be several orders of magnitude lower in AGNs

(
Abramowicz et al. 2004
)
;
for example, the first convincing QPO case was
\sim
1 hr periodicity in
X-ray emission of the narrow line Seyfert 1 (NLS1) galaxy
RE J1034+396
(
Gierliński et al. 2008
;
Alston et al. 2014
)
. It is relatively hard to
detect long periodicities, sub-days to even tens of days expected for AGN
QPOs. Only in recent years, more and more AGN QPO cases
have been reported (see, e.g.,
Zhang & Wang 2021
;
Tripathi et al. 2024
; references therein),
which has been enabled by large surveys in time domain at different bands.
It should be noted that many reported QPOs appeared in AGNs’ jet emission,
and thus are radio or
\gamma
-ray cases;
for optical QPOs, there are, for example, those reported in
Otero-Santos et al. 2020

and
Ren et al. 2024
.
As jets are coupled to accretion disks, these QPOs in non-thermal emissions
can also potentially reflect accretion activities of AGNs.

QPOs not in obvious association with jets are rare.
Only several NLS1s were found to exhibit
\sim
1–2 hr QPOs at X-rays

(
Gierliński et al. 2008
;
Alston et al. 2015
;
Pan et al. 2016
;
Gupta et al. 2018
)
, besides a 42.2 d one in type 2 AGN
NGC 4945
(
Smith et al. 2020
)
. At optical, a 44 d QPO in the NLS1 KIC 9650712
was reported
(
Smith et al. 2018
)
. The hour-long QPOs as well as the 44 d optical
one follow the relations between
\nu
and
M_{\rm BH}
extending from
high- and low-frequency QPOs (HFQPOs and LFQPOs; see
Ingram & Motta 2019
for details)
of XRBs respectively
(e.g.,
Pan et al. 2016
;
Smith et al. 2018
). While the origin of the QPOs is under
discussion (e.g.,
Pan et al. 2016
;
Rana & Mangalam 2020
), the scaling relations strongly
suggest similar physical processes occurring in accretion of stellar-mass
and massive BHs.

In our sample of
\sim
40 AGNs with large optical variations selected from
Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF;
Bellm et al. 2019
) light-curve (LC)
data
(see
Zhu et al. 2024
, Sect. 1)
, we noted a source,
the Seyfert 1 galaxy J1626+5120
(R.A. =
16^{h}26^{m}11^{s}.60
, Decl.=
51^{\circ}20^{{}^{\prime}}38^{{}^{\prime\prime}}.17
,
J2000.0), showing QPO-like variations.
We have been obtaining its optical spectra and collecting
archival multi-band data for it. By analyzing the data,
we were able to determine the periodicity
P\simeq 329
d and
find evidence for the accretion-disk origin for this QPO.
This rare optical QPO case may provide an explanation for
the formation mechanims of jet QPOs of similar periodicities. In this paper,
we report our analysis and the results.

Throughout this paper, we adopted cosmological parameters from the Planck
mission
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
)
,
H_{0}
= 67.4 km s
-1
Mpc
-1
and
\Omega_{m}
= 0.315.

II.2
Optical spectrum data

We obtained six optical spectra between MJD 56460 and 60758
(the right panel of Figure

).
Three were respectively from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey

The other three were taken by us, with two with the Lijiang 2.4-m Telescope

As the DESI spectrum has the finest pixel scale
(0.67 Å pixel
-1
) over a wide wavelength range (3055–8336 Å),
better than that of SDSS (1.25 Å pixel
-1
),

), LJT (
\sim

), and

\sim

we fit it using

(

)

and found that the optical emission was dominated by the AGN with no

).
From the fitting results,
the full-width at half maximum (FWHM) of the H
\beta
line
and the continuum luminosity at 5100 Å (
L_{\lambda}(5100\,\text{\AA})
),
we estimated
M_{\mathrm{BH}}

from the single-epoch virial mass relation given by
Vestergaard & Peterson 2006
,

\log_{10}(M_{\mathrm{BH}}/M_{\odot})\simeq 8.01^{+0.10}_{-0.11}
.
The Eddington ratio

\dot{m}_{\text{Edd}}=L_{\text{bol}}/L_{\text{Edd}}\simeq 0.043\pm 0.012
was
also obtained, where the bolometric luminosity
L_{\text{bol}}
was estimated
from
L_{\lambda}(5100\,\text{\AA})

using a bolometric correction of 9.26
(
Richards et al. 2006
; applicable for
z<0.8
) and the Eddington luminosity

L_{\text{Edd}}=1.38\times 10^{38}(M_{\mathrm{BH}}/M_{\odot})
erg s
-1
.

Swift
X-ray and ultraviolet data

There were 107 observations of J1626+5120 conducted with

the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory
(
Swift
;
Gehrels et al. 2004
)
between March 7, 2023 and May 17, 2025. The observations were
in 0.3–10 keV with the X-Ray Telescope (XRT;

)
and at Ultraviolet (UV) bands with the Ultraviolet Optical Telescope
(UVOT;
Roming et al. 2005
;

).
Most of the XRT and UVOT exposures range from 500 s to 3000 s.

The XRT Photon Counting (PC) mode data were processed through the UK

.

. The source’s

). Given the quality of the data,

Considering the uncertainties of the flux measurements
(cf., Figure

) and the distribution of the XRT exposure times,
we only used those data with exposure times

The selection resulted in 76 data points.

(

III
Temporal variability analysis

III.1
Periodicity determination

Among the optical LCs, the
zg
one shows clear modulation
(Figure
1
). By performing timing analysis to the LCs at
different bands (e.g.,
zg
,
zr
,
zi
,
ao
),
a QPO signal was found to be most significant in the
zg
data.
We therefore focused on the
zg
band. The method
used to determine the signal was the Lomb-Scargle Periodogram
(LSP;
Lomb 1976
;
Scargle 1982
). The analysis with the method resulted in
a significant QPO at
\simeq 329\pm 18
d (Figure
3
),
which was estimated from a Gaussian fit to the power peak in the LSP spectrum
with the uncertainty being the FWHM of the fit.
To visually illustrate the QPO modulation,
a sinusoidal fit, with 329 d period, to the
zg
LC is shown in the right panel of
Figure
3
.
It can be noted that the overall phase-averaged brightness had small
decreases/variations and then jumped by
\sim-
0.7 mag in the end of the LC.

To evaluate the statistical significance of this detection against red-noise
fluctuations, we generated
3\times 10^{5}
synthetic LCs replicating
the statistical properties (i.e., the power spectral density and the flux probability distribution function)
of the observed
zg
data,
following
Emmanoulopoulos et al. 2013
. Applying the LSP analysis to each synthetic LC,
we determined significance thresholds from the resulting distribution
of maximum noise power levels. Specifically, the
3\sigma
,

4\sigma
, and
4.53\sigma
significance
curves are overlaid on the LSP power spectrum in the left panel of Figure
3
,
and the power of the QPO reaches the
4.53\sigma
curve.

As a cross-check, we also employed the Weighted Wavelet Z-transform
(WWZ;
Foster 1996
), where the Python code was from
Aydin 2017
,
to investigate the temporal evolution of the QPO signal. The result,
presented in Appendix Figure
8
, reveals that the
\sim
329 d
signal was persistent throughout the entire time span of the
zg
data.

III.2
Cross-correlation analysis

Figure 4:
Time lags of the UV
and optical LCs with respect to the X-ray LC determined from
cross-correlation analysis. A green dashed curve
indicates the time delay expected from a disk continuum reprocessing
model.

To examine the correlations between LCs of the different bands, we
used the
PyCCF
Python package
(
Sun et al. 2018
)
for the analysis.
The XRT LC was set as the reference with zero time lag. The time lags of
the UV and optical LCs were determined from the Cross-Correlation Peak
Distribution (CCPD) and Centroid Distribution (CCCD) provided in
PyCCF
.
Our initial analysis indicated that direct cross-correlations of the UV
or optical LCs with the X-ray LC resulted in large uncertainties,
and we thus adopted the following strategy:
1) the
uvw2
was measured directly against the X-ray LC;
2) the lags for the
uvm2
,
uvw1
,
u
, and
zg
bands were first
measured relative to the
uvw2
, and the final lags relative to the X-ray
were obtained by adding the
uvw2
lag;
3) similarly, the lags for the
zr
,
zi
, and
ao

bands were first measured relative to the
zg
, and
their final lags relative to the X-ray
were calculated by adding the
zg
lag.
Time lag uncertainties were estimated using the Flux
Randomization/Random Subsample Selection (FR/RSS) Monte Carlo method. This
technique creates thousands of LC realizations by modifying fluxes within
their errors and selecting random data subsets, and the final lag and
1
\sigma
uncertainty are derived from the resulting CCCD and CCPD
distributions.

Individual analysis plots are provided in Figure
9
.
The obtained time lagsare summarized in Table
1
and plotted as a function of effective
wavelength in Figure
4
.
A trend of larger positive lags at longer wavelengths is seen.
Such a trend is often explained with a continuum-reprocessing disk model.
We calculated the model lags,
\tau(\lambda)
, based on Equation (12) in

Fausnaugh et al. 2016
. For J1626+5120, we adopted
M_{\text{BH}}=10^{8}\ M_{\odot}

and
\dot{m}_{\text{Edd}}=0.043
(Section
II.2
),
and assumed radiative efficiency
\eta=0.1
, external to internal heating
ratio
\kappa=1
, and scaling factor
X=5.04

(
Tie & Kochanek 2018
)
.
This model has a lag-wavelength relationship of

\tau(\lambda)\propto\lambda^{4/3}
.
As shown in Figure
4
, the observed lags are slightly larger
than the model ones. The discrepancy, which suggests larger disk sizes
than the disk model, is commonly reported in AGN reverberation
mapping studies (e.g.,
Fausnaugh et al. 2016
;
Guo et al. 2022
). On the other hand,
recent theoretical studies have shown that more sophisticated X-ray
reverberation models or disk models can account for large lags through
a more physical treatment of the reprocessing mechanism
(e.g.,
Papoutsis et al. 2024
;
Prince et al. 2025
).

IV
Discussion

The type 1 AGN J1626+5120 has shown intriguing LC variations, and our analysis
has indicated that in the well-sampled
zg
LC,
a
P\simeq 329
d QPO existed at a
\sim 4.53\sigma
significance.
The six optical spectra taken over the course of the long-term LCs have revealed
that the flux changes were mainly caused by the varied blue continuum, presumably
arising from the accretion disk.
Also, the
Swift
X-ray and UV flux measurements,
although covering
\sim
2 yr of the last portion of the long-term LCs,
showed variations correlated with the optical ones, and the time lags can
be approximately described with a reprocessing disk model. Combining these
properties together, we believe that this is likely a QPO case
related to physical processes at the inner region of the accretion disk
in J1626+5120.

Considering
M_{\rm BH}\simeq 10^{8}\ M_{\sun}
, the gravitational radius
of the BH
R_{g}=GM_{\rm BH}/c^{2}\simeq 1.5\times 10^{13}
cm. The Keplerian
period of the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO)
is
\simeq
13 hr, which is too short to be related to J1626+5120’s QPO
for discussion of the QPO origin
(e.g.,
Gierliński et al. 2008
;
Tarnopolski & Marchenko 2021
). A truncated disk possibility
(
Cowperthwaite & Reynolds 2012
;
Lohfink et al. 2013
) has been discussed
(see, e.g.,
Tarnopolski & Marchenko 2021
), based on which the disk’s inner edge would be
at
\sim 380R_{g}
in order to match the 329 d period. Such a large truncation
is unusual and would be hard to explain the varied blue continuum (which
is likely the cause of the modulation).
The period of an LFQPO, if scaled with the relationship,

\nu\propto 51.9
Hz
(M_{\rm BH}/M_{\sun})^{-1}
given in
Smith et al. 2018
,
would be
\sim
22 d, also being too short. However,
the LFQPOs of XRBs are observed to have a wide range of frequencies, from
a few mHz to
\sim
30 Hz (see details in
Ingram & Motta 2019
). If this is also true
to AGN QPOs, this QPO could simply correspond to a sub-Hz one in XRBs.
In addition, we noted that the first three cycles of the QPO variations show
a secondary peak (see in the right panel of Figure
3
). This shape of a waveform was seen
in QPOs of XRBs and is the result of the addition of two harmonics
(
Ingram & van der Klis 2015
)
.
Given these, we are inclined to identify the QPO as a counterpart of LFQPOs in XRBs.

For LFQPOs in XRBs and
\gamma
-ray bursts as well,
the Lense-Thirring (LT) precession of the inner accretion flow is a compelling
scenario widely discussed
(
Stella & Vietri 1998
;
Ingram et al. 2009
;
Ingram & Motta 2019
;
Tarnopolski & Marchenko 2021
)
.
In the scenario,
the frame-dragging effect caused by a spinning BH induces
a geometrically thick inner flow or a warped inner region of a thin disk
to precess, resulting in modulated observed emission.
The timescale of LT precession (
P_{\text{LT}}
) for a ring at a radius

R_{p}=r_{p}R_{g}
(where
r_{p}
is the dimensionless radius in units
of
R_{g}
)
around a Kerr BH with dimensionless spin parameter
a_{*}
can
be estimated as
(
Wilkins 1972
;
Stella & Vietri 1998
)

P_{\text{LT}}\approx 0.018
d
(M_{\text{BH}}/10^{8}\ M_{\odot})(r_{p}^{3}/a_{*})
.
For J1626+5120’s QPO,

r_{p}^{3}/a_{*}\approx 15510
(
P_{\text{LT}}=P/(1+z)\simeq 279
d) is
obtained.
Considering
0.014<a_{*}<1
,
r_{p}
ranges from 6, radius of the ISCO,
to 24. The radius range are reasonable values, similar to that estimated from
the QPOs in tidal disruption events (e.g.,
Pasham et al. 2024
).
In addition,
a_{*}
can be estimated through the accretion efficiency
method (e.g.,
Żywucka et al. 2020
), as the radiative efficiency
\eta
is related
to
a_{*}
from
\eta(a_{*})=1-E_{\rm ISCO}(a_{*})
, where
E_{\rm ISCO}
is
the specific energy at ISCO. For a standard thin disk,
\eta
ranges from

\sim 0.057
(
a_{*}=0
) to
\sim 0.32
(
a_{*}\approx 1
)
(
Bardeen et al. 1972
)
.
For J1626+5120, its
\eta\approx 0.09
, given from
the relation
\eta\approx 0.089(M_{\text{BH}}/10^{8}M_{\odot})^{0.52}

(
Davis & Laor 2011
)
,
and this
\eta
value corresponds to a moderately high spin of

a_{*}\approx 0.6
(also see Fig. 6 in
Piotrovich et al. 2025
). Thus,

r_{p}\approx 21\,R_{g}
in J1626+5120.

This J1626+5120 QPO potentially could be a key case for helping understand
those hundreds-day long jet QPOs, such as
\sim
120/150 d in blazar J1359+4011

(
King et al. 2013
)
, 176 d in NLS1 galaxy J0849+5108
(
Zhang & Wang 2021
)
,
or even 345/386 d in NGC 1275
(
Zhang et al. 2022
)
. The sub-year periods are too
short for another often-discussed scenario, signals of binary supermassive BH
(SMBH) systems. Taking J1626+5120 as an example, if it contains two SMBHs
with equal mass, the separation would be
\simeq
0.002 pc, already having
passed the
\sim
Gyr-long hardening phase (e.g.,
Yu 2002
;
Colpi 2014
);
for comparison, several candidate binary SMBH systems have several-year long
periodicities (e.g.,
Valtonen et al. 2008
;
Graham et al. 2015
).
It has been shown
from numerical simulations that jets, along with their disks as a system,
can undergo LT precession
(
Liska et al. 2018
)
.
We note that the BHs in the radio-loud AGNs listed above
have similar
M_{\rm BH}
(
\sim 10^{7-8}\ M_{\sun}
).
Thus due to LT precession, the sources would have similar QPO periods,
while appearing in their jets. Detailed property studies of AGNs
with sub-year QPOs may help fully examine this possibility.

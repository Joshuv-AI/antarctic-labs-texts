h h -topology for rigid spaces and an application to p p -adic Simpson Correspondence
Hanlin Cai, Zeyu Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the h h -topology for rigid spaces. Along the way, we establish several foundational results on morphisms of rigid spaces: we prove generic flatness and openness of the flat locus in the rigid setting, and we show that (for affinoid rigid spaces) strict transforms become flat after a blowup. Moreover, we show that any fppf cover admits a quasi-finite refinement and prove a version of Zariski’s main theorem for rigid spaces. As an application, we deduce that for a proper rigid space X X over C C , the category of pro-étale vector bundles on X X is equivalent to the category of Higgs bundles on the h h -site of X X , thereby generalizing Heuer’s results [ Heu23 ] to the singular setting.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a complete, algebraically closed non-archimedean extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
X
be a rigid space over
C
. The goal of this article is to generalize the
p
-adic Simpson correspondence to the case where
X
is not necessarily smooth. The underlying idea goes back to Deligne, who studied Hodge theory using the
h
-topology and hypercovers.

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

5.4
)
.

Let
X
be a proper rigid space over
C
. Fix a
1
-truncated smooth proper
h
-hypercover of
X
with a lift to
B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}/\xi^{2}
and an exponential map
\exp:C\to 1+\mathfrak{m}
. Then there exists an equivalence of symmetric monoidal categories:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vect}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Higgs}(X_{h}).

The right-hand side can be viewed as a Higgs field in which the sheaf of differentials is replaced by the first Deligne–Du Bois complex. Note that when
X
is smooth, the category of Higgs bundles on
X_{h}
is equivalent to the category of Higgs bundles on
X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
(
Proposition

3.17
). Thus,
Theorem

1.1
generalizes the
p
-adic Simpson correspondence by
[
Heu23
]
to the case of possibly singular proper rigid spaces.

Remark 1.2
.

We remark that even for algebraic varieties over the complex numbers, very little is known about Simpson’s correspondence in the singular case. For example, in
[
GKPT19
]
such a correspondence is studied for projective varieties with klt singularities, where the sheaf of differentials is replaced by its reflexification. However, by
[
HJ14
, Theorem 5.4]
, the
h
-differential sheaf, when restricted to a variety with klt singularities, coincides with the reflexive differentials. Thus, it seems plausible that applying our methods to algebraic varieties over the complex numbers would yield a generalization of the results of
[
GKPT19
]
. Moreover,
h
-differential forms also appear in the study of (higher)
F
-injective singularities in positive characteristic in the work of
[
KW24
]
, so it is reasonable to expect a version of singular non-abelian Hodge theory in positive characteristic generalizing the work of
[
OV07
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

In forthcoming work of Bhargav Bhatt and Mingjia Zhang, they construct a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
\mathcal{P}
over the cotangent bundle of a smooth rigid space
X
over
C
, which they call the Simpson gerbe. This gerbe gives an equivalence between perfect complexes on the pro-étale site and perfect complexes of
\mathcal{P}
-twisted Higgs bundles. It is also reasonable to expect that an
h
-sheafified version of this gerbe would yield a corresponding generalization to arbitrary rigid spaces.

As a
p
-adic analogue of the Hodge decomposition from complex geometry, Scholze
[
Sch13
]
proved that choosing a
B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}/\xi^{2}
lift
\mathbb{X}
of a smooth proper rigid space
X
over
C
induces an isomorphism

H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{n}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}H^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}(-j)),

which was later generalized to the case that
X
is not necessarily smooth by
[
Guo19
, Theorem 1.1.3]
. From this perspective, Heuer’s
p
-adic Simpson correspondence can be reinterpreted as a
p
-adic non-abelian Hodge correspondence, giving a non-abelian categorical generalization of Scholze’s result above. Similarly, our
Theorem

1.1
serves as a non-abelian generalization of
[
Guo19
, Theorem 1.1.3]
, first justified by the following result:

Theorem 1.4

(
Theorem

5.7
)
.

Take a
3n+2
-truncated smooth proper
h
-hypercover of
X
with a lift
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
to
B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}/\xi^{2}
. Given a pro-étale vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, it corresponds to a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)=S_{\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}}(\mathcal{E})
on
X_{h}
under the equivalence in
Theorem

1.1
, then there is an natural isomorphism

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})\simeq R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta)).

Remark 1.5
.

In
[
Guo19
]
, Guo proved the Hodge-Tate decomposition for non-smooth rigid spaces by introducing the
\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-topology for rigid spaces, inspired by the idea of working with the
\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-topology for schemes developed by Geisser in
[
Gei06
]
. On the other hand, in the algebraic setting, there is a finer topology, namely
h
-topology for schemes, see
[
Voe96
]
for details. As an analogy, one might wonder whether the
h
-topology for rigid spaces is well-defined. We give a positive answer to this question and establish several fundamental results for the
h
-topology of rigid spaces in this paper, which not only serve as the key ingredients for proving
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.4
, but also might be of independent interest.

Definition 1.6

(
Definition

2.32
)
.

The
h
-topology on
\mathrm{Rig}
is the Grothendieck topology associated to the pretopology whose covering systems are of the form
\{p_{i}:U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}
, where
p_{i}
is a finite family of quasicompact morphisms that

\coprod p_{i}:\coprod U_{i}\to X

is surjective. Big
h
-site is denoted as
\mathrm{Rig}_{h}
and the
h
-site localized at
X
is denoted as
X_{h}
.

Remark 1.7
.

In comparison with the
\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-topology for rigid spaces studied in
[
Guo19
]
, whose covering families are generated by étale coverings, universal homeomorphisms and coverings associated to blow-ups, the above definition of
h
-covers is easier to work with in practice. Indeed, it’s down-to-earth to check whether a morphism of rigid spaces is surjective. On the other hand, for latter applications, it is more convenient to have a nice decomposition for
h
-coverings (up to refinements), which is given in
Theorem

1.12
. To deduce it, we need several preliminaries.

First, motivated by the classical theorems in algebraic geometry of “the flat locus is open" and “generic flatness", we prove an analog in the setting of rigid spaces:

Theorem 1.8

(
Theorem

2.12
,
Proposition

2.20
)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a quasicompact morphism of rigid spaces with
Y
being reduced and qcqs.

(1)

(Openness of flat locus) The flat locus
\mathrm{Fl}_{X/Y}
is Zariski open in
X
.

(2)

(Generic flatness) If both
X
and
Y
are affinoid and
f
is tft, then there exists a nowhere dense Zariski closed subset
Z
of
Y
such that
f
is flat over
Y-Z
. In particular,
Y-Z
is dense.

Remark 1.9
.

A similar version of the above generic flatness theorem for rigid spaces has been obtained by
[
BH22
, Theorem 2.21]
. Roughly speaking, they showed that for a morphism
X\to Y
, the maximal open subset of
Y
such where
f
is flat is dense. However, it is not clear from their construction that such a locus in
X
is given by the complement of a Zariski closed subset, which is actually necessary for our applications later, hence we offer an alternative proof here. As for the openness of flat locus, the idea goes back to
[
Kie67
]
for complex analytic spaces and later
[
Duc18
]
for Berkovich spaces.

The next topic that we discuss is an analogue of
[
RG71
, Theorem 5.2.2]
in the rigid space setting, which tells us strict transform becomes flat after a blow up.

Proposition 1.10

(
Proposition

2.17
)
.

(Platification by blowup)

Let
f:X\to Y
be a tft morphism of affinoid rigid spaces. Assume there is a Zariski closed subset
Z^{\prime}
of
Y
such that
f
is flat over
U=Y-Z^{\prime}
. Then there exists a
U
-admissible blowup
p_{Z}:\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)\to Y
such that the strict transform
\tilde{f}:\tilde{p}_{Z}(X)\to\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)
is flat.

The final ingredient is the next proposition, which shows that locally
\mathrm{fppf}
covers of rigid spaces can be refined by quasi-finite maps. Moreover, the latter actually have nice decompositions, which could be viewed as an analog of Zariski’s main theorem in the rigid space setting. However, the flavor of Zariski’s main theorem is quite different from the algebraic setting. Such results have been discussed by
[
Con06
, Theorem 4.2.2]
in the setting of analytifications of algebraic maps and by
[
Duc18
, Theorem 9.1.3]
in the setting of Berkovich spaces.

Proposition 1.11

(
Proposition

2.28
,
Lemma

2.25
)
.

Let
X
be a quasicompact rigid space over
C
. Then we have the following.

(1)

(Quasi-finite refinement of fppf covers) Suppose
\{p_{i}:U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}
is a fppf cover of
X
, then there is a locally quasi-finite refinement, i.e. there exists a fppf cover
\{q_{j}:V_{j}\to X\}_{j\in J}
that refines
\{p_{i}:U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}
with each
q_{j}
being locally quasi-finite. Moreover, if
X
is qcqs, then there exists a quasi-finite refinement, namely each
q_{j}
is also quasicompact.

(2)

(Zariski’s Main theorem) If
f:Y\to X
is a quasi-finite map of quasicompact rigid spaces, then there exists a finite affinoid covering
U_{i}
of
Y
, such that for all
i
, the restriction of
f
to
U_{i}
admits a factorization

U_{i}\to W_{i}\to T_{i}\to X

in
\mathrm{Rig}
such that
U_{i}\to W_{i}
is finite,
W_{i}\to T_{i}
is an open immersion and
T_{i}\to X
is étale.

Given
Theorem

1.8
,
Proposition

1.10
and
Proposition

1.11
in hand, we proved that the coverings in the
h
-topology are generated by finite surjective maps and
\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-coverings, where the latter are generated by étale coverings, universal homeomorphisms and coverings associated to blow-ups by
[
Guo19
]
.

Theorem 1.12

(
Theorem

2.37
)
.

Let
X
be a quasicompact rigid space and
\left\{p_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow X\right\}_{i\in I}
be an
h
-cover in
\mathrm{Rig}
. Then there exists a refinement such that the index set
I^{\prime}
is finite and
p_{i}^{\prime}
factors through

V_{i}\xrightarrow{{}_{h_{i}}}Z_{i}\xrightarrow{q_{i}}Z\xrightarrow{q}X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi}X

for which
h_{i}:V_{i}\to Z_{i}
is finite surjective,
\left\{q_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow Z\right\}_{i\in I^{\prime}}
is an open covering in the analytic topology,

is an étale covering and
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
is an
\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-covering by a smooth rigid space

.

In this section, we study the

2.1.

We will layout some foundations of flat morphisms that will be used latter. We refer the reader to

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f:\mathrm{Spa}(B)\to\mathrm{Spa}(A)
be a morphism of rigid spaces and

be a coherent sheaf on

, then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

is flat over
\mathrm{Spa}(A)

(2)

M\coloneq F(\mathrm{Spa}(B))
is a flat

(3)

F
is flat at all classical points.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
Zav24
, Lemma B.4.3]
for the structure sheaf. Recall from the proof of loc. cite. that for any closed point
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Spec}(B)
there is a corresponding point

with support
\mathfrak{m}
and the natural morphism
B_{\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spa}(B),y}
is faithfully flat. We first show that (1) implies (2). Since

is coherent, it is of the form

by
[

, Theorem B.5.2]
. Hence
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
is flat over

by the flatness of

(over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spa}(B),y}
). As for (2) implying (3), note that we have
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{Spa}(B),f(x)}\simeq\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}
for classical points, respectively
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{Spa}(A),x}\simeq\widehat{A}_{\mathfrak{n}}
. Hence by checking flatness reduction modulo the powers of the maximal ideals, we win by the local criterion of flatness. Finally, assume (3) and we want to deduce (1). By considering classical points and their associated maximal ideals, we know that for any rational open set

\mathrm{Spa}(B)
that maps into the rational open set

of
\mathrm{Spa}(A)

is flat over

is flat at all the points.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume that
T=\mathrm{Spa}(B)\to S=\mathrm{Spa}(A)
is a morphism of affinoid rigid spaces. Let

be a scheme locally of finite type over

Proof.

Since the statement is local, it suffices to work affine locally. Hence we assume that

(resp.

Lemma 2.4

Let

Since the problem is local, we may assume

Proposition 2.5

Let

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

{A^{\circ}}\to R_{x}

Now given any nilimmersion

, by taking

On the other hand, suppose that

(

)

(1)

3.1.
Descent of vector bundles

To justify our generalization to the singular case, we verify that Higgs bundles in the smooth case satisfies

We first verify that vector bundles satisfy

. We will denote the natural projection as

Theorem 3.1
.

For any smooth rigid space

, there is an equivalence of groupoids

\rho_{X}^{*}:\mathrm{Vect}((\mathrm{Rig}/X)_{\mathrm{fppf}})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Vect}(X_{h}).

In fact, for any fppf vector bundle

Corollary 3.2
.

The presheaf

sending

to

Remark 3.3
.

Since
\mathrm{SmRig}
does not admit fibre products, in order to talk about the

-topology on it, one can either use the language of covering sieves or require it to be an

-hypersheaf. These notions are equivalent by
[

, Lemma 6.5.2.9]
since the value is

-truncated. Since

forms a basis in

by the local smoothness of the

to a (hyper)sheaf on

which induces an equivalence

\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{SmRig}_{h},\mathrm{Groupoids})\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{Rig}_{h},\mathrm{Groupoids}).

Remark 3.4
.

One can give a alternative proof of

3.1
by arguing that vector bundles on smooth rigid spaces satisfies
v
-descent. Indeed, vector bundles of smooth rigid spaces are equivalent to pro-étale vector bundles having a trivial geometric Sen operator by
[

-descent by arguing similarly as in
[

, Theorem 3.5.8]
. Such facts are also observed in

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a rigid space and

be a vector bundle on

. Then we have the following identifications.

(1)

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{an}}(X,F)\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,F)\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,F).

(2)

\mathrm{Vect}(X_{\mathrm{fppf}})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(X_{\mathrm{an}})

(3)

\mathrm{Vect}((\mathrm{Rig}/X)_{\tau})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(X_{\tau})
for
\tau\in\{{\mathrm{an}},\mathrm{\acute{e}t},\mathrm{fppf}\}
.

All the above facts follow formally from the fact that vector bundles satisfy faithfully flat descent by
[

]
.
One can also prove this concretely by using

and the algebraic faithfully flat descent for the fppf and étale comparison while the analytic and étale comparison is proved by

∎

Due to this lemma, we do not distinguish vector bundles in these topologies in the following and denote them as
\mathrm{Vect}(-)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p_{Z}:\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)\to Y
be a smooth blowup of a nowhere dense smooth rigid space
Y
. Suppose

is the exceptional divisor and

is a vector bundle on

. Then we have the following:

(1)

There is a fibre sequence

R\Gamma(Y,F)\to R\Gamma(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y),F)\oplus R\Gamma(Z,F)\to R\Gamma(E,F).

(2)

The pullback gives an equivalence

\mathrm{Vect}({Y})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}({Z})\times_{\mathrm{Vect}({E})}\mathrm{Vect}{({\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)})}

of groupoids.

Proof.

Denote closed immersions
Z\to Y
,
E\to\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)
as
i
and
i^{\prime}
, respectively, and the projection
E\to Z
as
p_{Z}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
as the ideal sheaf of

. Since the statements are local we may assume that

and

are both affinoid. In this case every vector bundle on

is of the form
\widetilde{M}
for some flat
A
-module

by
[
Zav24
, Theorem B.5.2]
. Hence we may identify

\mathrm{Vect}(Y)\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(Y_{a})

and similarly for
Z
. Moreover, since

is affinoid and blowups of

are relative analytifications of the algebraic blowups, by relative GAGA, c.f.
[
FK13
, Theorem 9.4.1]
, we have

\mathrm{Vect}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y))\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z_{a}}(Y_{a})),\ \mathrm{Vect}(E)\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(E_{a})

where
E_{a}
is the exceptional divisor associated to the algebraic blowup. In the sequential of the proof, we will not distinguish between the algebraic vector bundles and the analytic vector bundles.

We first prove (1). Suppose
F
is of the form
\widetilde{M}
for some flat
A
-module
M
. Since
M
is the direct summand of some free
A
-module, we may assume further that

is trivial. This is proved by
[
Guo19
, Proposition 5.1.1]
. In particular, since

is affinoid, statement (1) implies that there is a short exact sequence,

0\to\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to\Gamma(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y),\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)})\oplus\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})\to\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O}_{E})\to 0.

We now prove (2). For any

, notice that

\mathrm{Hom}(F_{1},F_{2})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{{O}}_{Y},\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(F_{1},F_{2}))\simeq\Gamma(Y,\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(F_{1},F_{2})).

Hence one can deduce fully-faithfulness of the pullback functor immediately from the above short exact sequence. As for essential surjectivity, we have to show that for any vector bundles
F_{1}\in\mathrm{Vect}(Z)
and
F_{2}\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y))
with an isomorphism
p_{Z}^{\prime*}F_{1}\simeq i^{\prime*}F_{2}
there is a vector bundle
G\in\mathrm{Vect}(Y)
gluing this datum. By Zariski descent and faithfully flat descent, we may reduce to the case where
A
is
I
-adically complete. Hence by deformation theorem and the fact that
Y
is affinoid, we know that
\mathrm{Vect}(Z)\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(Y)
. Therefore, there is a vector bundle
G\in\mathrm{Vect}(Y)
corresponding to
F_{1}\in\mathrm{Vect}(Z)
and an isomorphism
\psi_{0}:i^{\prime*}p_{Z}^{*}G\simeq i^{\prime*}F_{2}
. Now it reduces to show that there is a lift
\psi:p_{Z}^{*}G\simeq F_{2}
of
\psi_{0}
on
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y)
. By Grothendieck’s existence theorem, c.f.
[
Sta
, Theorem 08BE]
, it suffices to show that

lifts to any nilpotent thickening

where

is the closed subscheme defined by the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
. By induction we may assume there is a lift
\psi_{n}
on

. Viewing

as a section in
i^{\prime*}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(p_{Z}^{*}G,F_{2})
, the obstruction class that lift from

to

lies in

H^{1}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(Y),\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(p_{Z}^{*}G,F_{2})\otimes\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})=H^{1}(E,p_{Z}^{\prime*}i^{*}\underline{\mathrm{End}}(G)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})

where the isomorphism is given by the

-module structure on

and projection formula. By
[
Har77

and
[

we have

H^{j}(E,\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})=0

for all

and

. We will show that

H^{j}(E,p_{Z}^{\prime*}N\otimes\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})=0

for

and any coherent sheaf

on

which shows the vanishing of the obstruction class. To this end, we first write

as a quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{k}
for some

and denote the kernel as

. Since

is flat and

is a line bundle on

Taking the associated long exact sequence, we have

\cdots\to H^{j}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})\to H^{j}(E,p_{Z}^{\prime*}N\otimes\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})\to H^{j+1}(E,p_{Z}^{\prime*}K\otimes\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})\to\cdots.

The first term vanishes by the claim. By dimension bounds and induction, we know that the third term vanishes. Hence we have the desired vanishing of the second term.

Warning 3.9

We warn the reader that for some rigid space

on the small étale site

which is not étale. Moreover, even when

is not pulled back from

as the rank may varies when restricted on different objects in

which are not étale over

is not even a coherent sheaf on

, namely

. Consequently, there is a natural map

-module.

[

]
. In the following we will use the notion of irreducible component of rigid space by

]

[

]
. To begin with, we start the following observations.

Lemma 3.10

Let

be a finite surjective, generically Galois morphism (in Zariski topology) of normal connected rigid spaces. Suppose the Galois group is

Proof.

Take a Zariski open dense subset

. Observe that

is finite and coincides on a Zariski open subset

3.3.
Descent of Higgs bundles

In this subsection, we assemble everything together and prove that on a smooth rigid space

Definition 3.16

A Higgs bundle

on

where

, is a finite locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\tau}}
-module

\mathcal{O}_{X_{\tau}}
-linear morphism

as

. Given a Higgs field

on

, it is equivalent to giving a global section on

such that its image in
\Gamma(X,\underline{\mathrm{End}}(E)\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{\tau}}(-2))
vanishes.

Proposition 3.17
.

For any smooth rigid space
X
, there is an equivalence of groupoids

\pi_{X,*}:\mathrm{Higgs}(X_{h})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Higgs}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}).

Proof.

Given a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
X_{h}
, by
Theorem

3.1
,

is pulled back from a vector bundle on the étale site. Hence by
Corollary

3.14
and the projection formula of the ringed topoi, the functor
\pi_{X,*}
defines a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism
\pi_{X,*}E\to\pi_{X,*}E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1)
. Again by projection formula, we know that
\pi_{X,*}\theta\wedge\pi_{X,*}\theta=0
showing that
\pi_{X,*}\theta
is indeed a Higgs field. We then construct the converse of
\pi_{X,*}
. Given an étale Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
X
, one can pull back to the big étale site via
\mathrm{Id}_{X}^{*}
. After composing with the natural map in Warning
3.9
, we get a morphism of

-modules

\mathrm{Id}_{X}^{*}E\to\mathrm{Id}_{X}^{*}E\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{X}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1)\to\mathrm{Id}_{X}^{*}E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-1).

After further applying
\pi_{X}^{*}
we get a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{h}
-modules. Since pullback is exact and strongly symmetric monoidal, it clearly preserves the Higgs field condition. Denote the functor as
{\pi_{X}^{*}}^{\prime}
. We need to verify that

and vice versa.

To show that
\pi_{X,*}{\pi_{X}^{*}}^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{Id}
, note first that by

3.1
,
Corollary

3.14
and the projection formula, we have
\pi_{X,*}{\pi_{X}^{*}}^{\prime}E\simeq E
and
\pi_{X,*}{\pi_{X}^{*}}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1))\simeq E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1)
for any étale Higgs bundle

. Hence it suffices to show that
\pi_{X,*}{\pi_{X}^{*}}^{\prime}\theta\simeq\theta
. To this end, observe that

\Gamma(X,\underline{\mathrm{End}}(E)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Gamma(X,\mathrm{Id}_{X}^{*}\underline{\mathrm{End}}(E)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-1))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Gamma(X,\pi_{X}^{*}\underline{\mathrm{End}}(E)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{h}(-1))

are all isomorphic to each other. The first isomorphism is by

, Lemma 03YU]
and the fact that

is a vector bundle and by the same reasoning above. The isomorphisms above actually show the converse direction as well.

Corollary 3.18

The presheaf

is no longer a coherent sheaf anymore since it is not cartesian and thus not satisfies Lemma
4.10
.

4.2.
Simplicial Higgs bundles

In the rest of this subsection, we prove some properties of Higgs bundles in this setting that will be used later. One needs to be slightly careful in the simplicial setting since
\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet}}(-1)
is no longer coherent by Example
4.13
. Moreover due to the subtlety of taking duals as remarked in
Remark

4.11
, we do not use the linear dual of
\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet}}(-1)
in the following.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a simplicial rigid space and
\tau\in\{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},h,v\}
. A Higgs field on
X_{\bullet,\tau}
is a vector bundle
E^{\bullet}
on
X_{\bullet,\tau}
with a morphism

\theta:E^{\bullet}\to E^{\bullet}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet,\tau}}(-1)

such that
\theta\wedge\theta=0
and is
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\bullet,\tau}}
-linear (where in the case of
v
-site,
\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet,\tau}}(-1)
simply means the pullback of
\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet,\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(-1)
). Again the groupoid of Higgs bundles on
X_{\bullet,\tau}
is denoted as
\mathrm{Higgs}(X_{\bullet,\tau})
. Similarly as in
Definition

3.19
, we define the Dolbeault cohomology of a Higgs bundle
(E^{\bullet},\theta)
on
X_{\bullet,\tau}
as the hypercohomology of

C_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(E^{\bullet},\theta)\coloneq(E^{\bullet}\to E^{\bullet}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet,\tau}}(-1)\to E^{\bullet}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{\bullet,\tau}}(-2)\to\cdots).

For a similar reason as before,
C_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(E^{\bullet},\theta)
is only a connective complex even when
X_{\bullet}
is smooth.

Remark 4.15
.

In the following, we also require that Higgs bundles are "cartesian" in the sense that the only failure to be cartesian is given by the sheaf of differentials. Specifically speaking, we require that for any
\phi:X_{m}\to X_{n}
, the Higgs field
\theta_{m}
on
X_{m}
is given by the composition of
\phi^{*}\theta_{n}
and
\phi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{n}}(-1)\to\Omega^{1}_{X_{m}}(-1)
, namely

\theta_{m}:\phi^{*}E^{n}\to\phi^{*}E^{n}\otimes\phi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{n}}(-1)\to\phi^{*}E^{n}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{m}}(-1).

Remark 4.16
.

We warn the reader here that one should not expect a generalization of the Hodge-Tate log exact sequence as in
[
Heu23
, Theorem 2.4]
since there is no good analogue of spectral variety in this context. Specifically speaking, let
X_{\bullet}
be a smooth rigid space and
(E^{\bullet},\theta)
be a Higgs bundle on
X_{\bullet,\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
. Then it is not clear whether there is a simplicial rigid space
S_{\bullet}
that maps to
X_{\bullet}
such that
S_{n}\to X_{n}
corresponds to the spectral variety of
(E^{n},\theta_{n})
for every
n
. Indeed, let
f:X\to Y
be a map of smooth rigid spaces and
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs field on
Y_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
and
f^{*}(E,\theta)
be its pullback. Suppose
S_{Y}
and
S_{X}
be the corresponding spectral varieties, then we have the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{Y}/X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}X(-1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}Y(-1)/X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}Y(-1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

However, the arrow from
S_{Y}/X\to S_{X}
is in the opposite direction to make a simplicial spectral variety.

Finally we prove an analogous statement of
Proposition

3.17
for simplicial rigid spaces. We still denote the projection
X_{\bullet,h}\to X_{\bullet,\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
as
\pi_{X_{\bullet},*}
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a smooth simplicial rigid space. Then the natural projection gives an equivalence of groupoids

\pi_{X_{\bullet},*}:\mathrm{Higgs}(X_{\bullet,h})\to\mathrm{Higgs}(X_{\bullet,\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}).

Proof.

One construct the inverse functor
{\pi_{X_{\bullet}}^{*}}^{\prime}
similarly. And to check that
{\pi_{X_{\bullet}}^{*}}^{\prime}
is indeed an inverse, it suffices to check degreewise by
Proposition

4.7
which follows from
Proposition

3.17
.
∎

Geometric Sen operator also makes sense in this context.

Theorem 4.18

(c.f.
[
Heu23
, Theorem 4.8]
)
.

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a smooth simplicial rigid space. Then for any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}
on
X_{\bullet,v}^{\diamond}
, there exists a unique Higgs field

\eta_{\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}}:\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{\bullet}}(-1)

on

such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

The association

is functorial in
X_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}
;

(2)

If

is constant, then

is the Higgs field defined in

;

(3)

comes from a vector bundle on

Proof.

First we notice that for constant

provides a way of associating Higgs field

to a pro-étale vector bundle

5.
p
-adic Simpson correspondence for proper rigid spaces

In this section, we use all the ingredients above to prove a
p
-adic Simpson correspondence for any proper rigid space over
C
removing the smoothness condition in the work of
[
Heu23
]
.

5.1.
Cohomological dimension bounds

We need to prove that the cohomological dimension bounds of pro-étale vector bundles on a rigid space are the same as expected, i.e., twice as the dimension of the rigid space. To this end, we need the following result, which is well-known to experts.

Lemma 5.1
.

The presheaf

\mathrm{Rig}_{h}\to\mathrm{Groupoids}

sending
Y
to
\mathrm{Vect}(Y_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})
is a
h
-sheaf.

Proof.

By
[
KL16
, Theorem 3.5.8]
, for any affinoid perfectoid space
X
, the category of pro-étale vector bundles on
X
is equivalent to the category of
v
-vector bundles on
X
. Let
Y
be any rigid space. Since the affinoid perfectoid objects form a basis for the pro-étale site of
Y
by
[
Sch13
, Proposition 4.8]
, one immediately deduce that the category of pro-étale vector bundles on
Y
is equivalent to the category of
v
-vector bundles on
Y^{\diamond}
, namely

\mathrm{Vect}(Y_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}(Y^{\diamond}_{v}).

Hence the category of pro-étale vector bundles satisfies
v
-hyperdescent. By
Lemma

2.36
,
v
-topology is finer than
h
-topology, the consequence easily follows.
∎

By (the proof of) the previous lemma, we do not distinguish pro-étale vector bundles and
v
-vector bundles in the following. Now we get to prove the cohomological bound. The following argument was suggested to us by Bogdan Zavyalov.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be a rigid space of dimension
n
and
\mathcal{E}
be a pro-étale vector bundle on
X
. Then
R\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}
is concerntrated in cohomological dimension
[0,n]
of
D^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
. In particular, the cohomology group
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})
lies in cohomological degree
[0,2n]
.

Proof.

We first prove the case where
X
is smooth. We may assume that
X=\mathrm{Spa}(A)
is affinoid. Moreover, by
[
Zav21
, Theorem 1.3.4]
and further replacing
X
by a finite étale cover if necessary, we may assume there is locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\diamond}_{v}}
-sheave
\mathcal{E}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{E}^{+}[1/p]=\mathcal{E}
. Choose an admissible formal model
\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Spf}(R)
of
X
and denote the natural morphism by
t:X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
and the composition by
\Psi:X^{\diamond}_{v}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
. By
[
Zav21
, Lemma 7.8.7]
, we may choose a formal model
\mathfrak{X}
such that it admits a finite open cover
\{\mathfrak{U}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
where the restriction of
\mathcal{E}^{+}
to each
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
is small, i.e. can be trivialized by a finite étale cover. We first claim that

R\Psi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+}\in\widehat{D}^{+}_{\mathrm{qcoh}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}),\ (R\Psi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+})^{a}\in\widehat{D}^{[0,n]}_{\mathrm{acoh}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})^{a}.

For the first claim, it suffices to show that the natural morphism

\widetilde{H^{i}(X^{\diamond}_{v},\mathcal{E}^{+})}\to R^{i}\Psi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+}

is an isomorphism. To this end, it reduces to show that for any affine open
\mathfrak{U}=\mathrm{Spf}(R[1/f]^{\wedge})
with its rigid-generic fibre
U
, the natural morphism of base change

H^{i}_{v}(X^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})\widehat{\otimes}_{R}R[1/f]^{\wedge}\to H^{i}_{v}(U^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})

is an isomorphism. Choose a strictly totally disconnected perfectoid cover
X_{\infty}=\mathrm{Spa}(A_{\infty},A_{\infty}^{+})
of
X
then by
[
Zav21
, Corollary 7.5.2]
the pullback
U_{\infty}=X_{\infty}\times_{X}U
is also a strictly totally disconnected perfectoid space. Since strictly totally disconnected perfectoid spaces are acyclic objects for
\mathcal{E}^{+}
, both cohomology groups are computed by the Čech cohomologys of
\Gamma((X_{\infty}^{\bullet/X})^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})
and
\Gamma((U_{\infty}^{\bullet/U})^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})
respectively. Since the base change is
p
-completely flat, it reduces to showing that the natural morphism

\Gamma((X_{\infty}^{j/X})^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})\widehat{\otimes}_{R}R[1/f]^{\wedge}\to\Gamma(({U}_{\infty}^{j/U})^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})

is an isomorphism. Since strictly totally disconnected perfectoid space does not have any higher cohomology for
\mathcal{E}^{+}
, we have
\Gamma(({U}_{\infty}^{j/U})^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+}/p)=\Gamma(({U}_{\infty}^{j/U})^{\diamond},\mathcal{E}^{+})/p
. Hence by derived Nakayama lemma, c.f.
[
Sta
, Lemma 0G1U]
, it suffices to check the above isomorphism for
\mathcal{E}^{+}/p
where it is proved by
[
Zav21
]
. Hence we win. As for the second claim for almost coherence, since we have already proved that
R\Psi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+}
is quasicoherent, by
[
Bha
, Proposition 7.1.6]
and the assumption that
\mathfrak{X}
is affine, it reduces to show that the
R\Psi_{*}(\mathcal{E}^{+}/p)
is almost coherent. This is proved by
[
Zav21
, Theorem 7.8.8]
. As for the cohomological dimension bounds, it follows from the almost analogue of the derived Nakayama lemma and
[
Zav21
, Theorem 7.10.3]
.

To prove that
R\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}
is in fact coherent, it suffices to show that
t^{*}R\Psi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+}=R\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}
. For any rational open set
U
, by taking stricly totally disconnected perfectoid cover of
U
, it is clear that

\Gamma(U^{\diamond},t^{*}R^{i}\Psi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+})=\Gamma(U^{\diamond},R^{i}\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}).

Thus it suffices to show that for any (standard) étale morphism
V\to U
, the fibre of

\Gamma(U,R^{i}\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+})\widehat{\otimes}\Gamma(V,\mathcal{O}^{+})\to\Gamma(V,R^{i}\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}^{+})

is killed by bounded
p
-torsion. To this end, since the restriction of
\mathcal{E}^{+}
to each
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
is small and hence its rigid-generic fibre. Therefore, we may reduce to the case where
\mathcal{E}^{+}
is trivial. Then we win by
[
Sch13
, Lemma 6.18]
.

Finally, we extend the statement to the general case by
Proposition

2.38
. Again, we may assume that
X
is quasicompact. Since pro-étale vector bundles satisfy
h
-descent, we may freely use properties of
h
-sheaves in the following. Passing to its reduction and use
Proposition

2.38
(3), we may also assume that
X
is reduced. Hence by resolution of singularities, there is a nowhere dense Zariski closed subset
Z
of
X
whose blowup
Y
is smooth with exceptional divisor
X
. By
Proposition

2.38
(2), there is an fibre sequence

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(Z,\mathcal{E})\oplus R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(Y,\mathcal{E})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})

and similarly for
R\nu_{*}\mathcal{E}
. Hence by induction on dimension and the smooth case, we get the desired statements.

∎

Remark 5.3
.

We remark here that there are several other ways to prove the above results. For example, since this problem is local, one may use Faltings’ small correspondence in the smooth case, c.f.
[
Fal05
, Section 3]
to use the corresponding cohomology of small Higgs bundles to compute the pro-étale cohomology. Another way is to use the newly developed theory of perfectoid crystals as in
[
Bha
]
. Namely, one can realize pro-étale vector bundles (étale locally) as rationalized almost overconvergent étale
\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{+}
-vector bundles. One then uses
[
Bha
, Theorem 5.3.5]
to relate it with perfectoid crystals and uses the affineness of perfectoidization in this case to deduce the desired cohomological bounds. In this way one can avoid using the resolution of singularities.

Proof of

.

\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-hypercover

of

are 1-truncated

, namely

\mathrm{Higgs}(\mathrm{sk_{1}}X_{\bullet,h})\simeq\mathrm{Higgs}(\mathrm{sk_{1}}X_{\bullet,\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})

Finally we compare the cohomology of a pro-étale vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
with the cohomology of its corresponding Higgs field on a rigid space
X
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
X
be a proper rigid space of dimension
n
. Take a
3n+2
-truncated smooth proper
h
-hypercover of
X
with a lift
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
to
B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}/\xi^{2}
. Given a pro-étale vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, by
Theorem

1.1
it corresponds to a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)=S_{\mathbb{X}_{\bullet},\exp}(\mathcal{E})
on
X_{h}
under the equivalence in
Theorem

1.1
, then

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})\simeq R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta)).

In particular,
R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta))
is concentrated on cohomological degree
[0,2n]
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

5.5
, there is a
3n+2
-truncated smooth proper
\mathrm{\acute{e}h}
-hypercover
X_{\bullet}
of
X
which admits a compatible lift
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}\to\mathbb{X}
to
B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}/\xi^{2}
. Denote by
\rho:X_{\bullet}\to X
the augmentation map. Then the cone of the natural map

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})

lives in cohomological degree
\geq 3n+1
where
\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}=\rho^{*}\mathcal{E}
. Similarly, the cone of the natural map

R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta))\to R\Gamma_{h}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},(E^{\bullet},\theta))

lives in cohomological degree
\geq 3n+1
. On the other hand, by
Proposition

4.20
and
Proposition

4.17
, we have that

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},(E^{\bullet},\theta))=R\Gamma_{h}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},(E^{\bullet},\theta)).

Combining all of the above arguments together, we deduce that

\tau^{\leq 3n}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})=\tau^{\leq 3n}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})\simeq\tau^{\leq 3n}R\Gamma_{h}(\mathrm{sk}_{3n+2}X_{\bullet},(E^{\bullet},\theta))=\tau^{\leq 3n}R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta)).

By
Corollary

3.24
,
R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta))
is concentrated in cohomological degree
[0,3n]
. On the other hand, by
Proposition

5.2
,
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})
is concentrated on cohomological degree
[0,2n]
, hence

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})=\tau^{\leq 3n}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{E})\simeq\tau^{\leq 3n}R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta))=R\Gamma_{h}(X,(E,\theta))

as desired.
∎

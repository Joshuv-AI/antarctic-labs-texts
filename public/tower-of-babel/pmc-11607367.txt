Ferroptosis-related lncRNA AL136084.3 is associated with NUPR1 in bladder cancer
Authors: Jing Zhou, Li Zhang, Hao Wu, Sheng-Lin Gao, Xiao-Ping Chen, Li-Feng Zhang, Cui-Ping Zhao, Bing-Bing Wei, Yu Bai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLncRNAs are critical regulators of bladder cancer (BLCA), and ferroptosis is a newly discovered cell death responsible for mediating apoptosis and tumorigenesis. The present study aims to establish a prognostic signature of differentially expressed ferroptosis-related lncRNAs (DEFRlncRNAs) and explore the DEFRlncRNA associated with NUPR1 in BLCA.MethodsDEFRlncRNAs in BLCA patients were screened using univariate and multivariate Cox and LASSO regression analyses. In vitro experiments were performed to detect the regulatory effects of DEFRlncRNAs on BLCA cells. A prognostic signature of DEFRlncRNAs in BLCA was created and validated. Moreover, we used RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP) to evaluate the correlated DEFRlncRNA with NUPR1.ResultsA prognostic signature involving 18 DEFRlncRNAs in BLCA was created. Overexpression of AL355353.2 or knockdown of AL136084.3 promoted apoptosis in 5637 and T24 cells in vitro. Results from Starbase database estimated that AL136084.3 was positively associated with NUPR1 (R = 0.229, p < 0.001). RIP analysis revealed the reciprocal binding of NUPR1 and AL136084.3 in BLCA.ConclusionThe identified FRlncRNA pair signature has a good prognostic and clinical predictive value. The ferroptosis-related lncRNA AL136084.3 is correlated with NUPR1 in BLCA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01564-2.

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BLCA) is one of the ten most common cancers globally with increasing prevalence and mortality [1]. Based on the depth of invasion, BLCA can be classified into muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) and non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). The former subtype, accounting for 25% of BLCA cases, is featured by a high possibility of aggression into the muscular layer and a poor prognosis [2, 3]. Despite the sound performances of radical cystectomy and pelvic lymph node dissection, 50% of MIBC patients ultimately experience distant metastases [4–6]. The latter is a prevalent subtype of BLCA, known for its acceptable prognosis but high risks of relapse (5-year recurrence of 50–70%) and progression (5-year progression of 10–30%) [4]. Considering the high mortality of MIBC and high recurrence rate of NMIBC, a model to determine the prognosis of BLCA is particularly necessary.

Ferroptosis is a unique, ancient form of cell death induced by uncontrolled lipid peroxidation [7, 8]. The involvement of ferroptosis in malignancies has been extensively analyzed, although the exact molecular mechanism remains largely unclear [8]. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a class of non-coding RNAs with more than 200 nucleotides in length [9, 10]. They are vital regulators of ferroptosis and human diseases like cancers [11]. In recent years, many studies have revealed that lncRNAs can be involved in the regulation of ferroptosis and can be used to prevent the further deterioration of malignant tumors [12, 13]. A previous study revealed that lncRNA LINC00336 can inhibit ferroptosis in lung malignancies [14]. Another study revealed that LINC00618, a nuclear lncRNA, can participate in promoting iron ptosis in patients with leukemia [15]. To date, there has been a lack of investigation into the involvement of ferroptosis-related long non-coding RNAs (FR)lncRNAs in BLCA.

In this research, we developed a prognostic signature based on ferroptosis-related lncRNAs (FRlncRNAs) in BLCA by data mining and processing [16]. The distinctive feature of this signature is the absence of specific expression of lncRNAs within the samples. Furthermore, the prognostic significance of FRlncRNAs in BLCA patients was validated to confirm its reliability.

Methods

Data sources and processing

Transcriptomic and clinical data from patients with BLCA were acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Transfer format files from Ensembl (http://asia.ensembl.org) were processed using Strawberry Perl (version 5.32.01) to classify mRNAs and lncRNAs. Totally, 60 ferroptosis-related (FR) genes were extracted as previously described [17]. Correlations between FR genes and lncRNAs were analyzed using the limma package in R (version 4.0.2), and significance was determined with a correlation coefficient >0.4 and p < 0.001. Differential analysis of the FRlncRNAs was performed using limma, and significant differences were determined by a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and log fold change (FC) > 1.5. Differentially expressed FRlncRNAs (DEFRlncRNAs) were used for subsequent analyses.

Cell culture

Two classic BLCA cell lines 5637 and T24 (Chinese Academy of Sciences) were used in the current study. The 5637 cell lines were derived from a male patient with grade II BLCA. T24 cell lines originated from a female patient with grade III bladder cancer. The cells were cultured in the RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA), 100 mg/mL streptomycin (Sangon Co. Ltd, Shanghai, China) and 100 U/mL penicillin (Sangon Co. Ltd, Shanghai, China) in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C. The culture media of the cell lines were replaced every 3 days, and cell passage was performed at 80% of cell confluence. The protocol for using cells was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the Affiliated Changzhou Second People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Apoptosis assay

Flow cytometry (FCM) was performed to determine the rate of apoptosis. An Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) detection kit was used according to the manufacturer’s guidelines (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). Briefly, transfected cells were centrifuged at 1000×g for 5 min, and the precipitant was resuspended in Annexin V-FITC binding buffer and incubated with propidium iodide at room temperature for 10–20 min. A flow cytometer (BD, Biosciences, NJ, USA) was used to quantify the number of apoptotic cells. FlowJ was used to analyze the data.

Cell proliferation and RNA binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP)

The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Beyotime Biotechnology Co. Ltd, Shanghai, China) assay was used to evaluate cell proliferation. Transfected cells were incubated for 72 h in 96-well plates in a medium containing 5% fetal bovine serum. Then, they were kept overnight at 37 °C and 5% carbon dioxide. After incubation for 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, and 72 h, 10 μL CCK-8 solution was loaded into each well and cells were cultured for 2 h. In the control group wells, only culture medium and CCK-8 solution were added. A microplate reader was then used to measure the optical density at 450 nm (BioTek Instruments, VT, USA). Each experiment was performed in triplicate to ensure the reliability. The RIP was carried out using a specific RIP kit based on the protocol of the manufacturer (BersinBio Corp., Guangdong). The cells were lysed using RIP lysis buffer. NUPR1 antibodies were introduced to the cell lysate and incubated at 4 degree centigrade. The NUPR1 and AL136084.3 complex was obtained through immunoprecipitation. RNA was extracted from the mixture by adding 1 mL Trizol. The eluted co-precipitated RNA was then subjected to RT-qPCR analysis.

Data analyses

DEFRlncRNA expression in the sample was compared in pairs and the value was defined as “A”. The value of A was denoted as 1 if the expression level of a DEFRlncRNA was greater than that of the second one; otherwise, A was assigned as 0. Cox regression and Lasso method (iteration = 1000) were then performed to create a prognostic signature model of DEFRlncRNAs in BLCA patients as follows: Risk score = ∑i=1kCiAi, where Ci represents the coefficient obtained from multivariate Cox analysis for the ith lncRNA pair, and Ai represents the expression value of the ith lncRNA pair. Subsequently, a time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn for representing a 5 year interval, and the maximum inflection point was considered the cut-off value. A DEFRlncRNA with a risk score higher than the cut-off value was categorized into the high-risk group; otherwise, it was included in the low-risk group.

We evaluated the expression level of NUPR1, COPB2, TIMM44, and PPARG in BLCA using the integrated datasets combining TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analysis was employed to examine the survival differences between the high-risk and low-risk groups. The correlation between the prognostic signature of FRlncRNAs and clinicopathological features of BLCA was evaluated using the Chi-square test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and lncRNAs between BLCA patients of the NUPR1high and NUPR1low groups were screened using R3.6.3. A chord diagram was depicted to investigate the correlation of DEGs in BLCA patients. The expression level of AL136084.3 in BLCA patients and its correlation with key genes were analyzed using the Starbase database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/). The risk scores for immune infiltration was calculated using Spearman’s correlation test, and their correlations with genes involved in immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) were assessed using the limma package in R. IC50 values against common chemotherapeutic drugs in BLCA patients were calculated using the limma and pRRophetic packages in R. A significant difference was set at p < 0.05.

Identification and verification of a prognostic signature

Univariate Cox, LASSO, and multivariate Cox regression analyses of the data revealed 18 DEFRlncRNA pairs, all of which were included in the prognostic signature (Fig. 3A–C). We discovered that AL355353.2 serves as a protective factor against BLCA [hazard ratio: 0.516 (0.382–0.696), p < 0.001]. In contrast, AL136084.3 was identified as a risk factor for BLCA [hazard ratio: 2.038 (1.471–2.824), p < 0.001, Fig. 3C]. Functional experiments were used to assess the impacts of AL355353.2 overexpression (OE) and AL136084.3 knockdown (KD) on cell apoptosis, colony formation, and proliferation. Flow cytometric analyses revealed that, compared with the control groups, AL355353.2 OE promoted apoptosis in both 5637 (Fig. 4A2) and T24 (Fig. 4B2) cell lines (Fig. 4A1, B1). Similarly, AL136084.3 KD elevated apoptosis in both 5637 (Fig. 4A3) and T24 (Fig. 4B3) cell lines. Additionally, colony formation was examined using a light microscope. In cell line 5637, the AL355353.2 OE group (Fig. 5A2) showed a notable reduction in the number of cell colonies compared to the control group (Fig. 5A1). A similar observation was observed among T24 cells (Fig. 5B2). In addition, compared to the control group (Fig. 5A1, B1), the AL136084.3 knockdown group exhibited a marked decrease in the number of cell colonies in both 5637 (Fig. 5A3) and T24 (Fig. 5B3) cell lines. The CCK-8 assay demonstrated a significant reduction in cell proliferation with the AL355353.2 OE in both 5637 (Fig. 6A) and T24 (Fig. 6B) cell lines. Validation of the independent prognostic significance of DEFRlncRNA pairs

We then performed univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses of the risk scores, age, sex at birth, tumor grade, and tumor stage. We found that age, tumor stage, and risk score were independent prognostic factors (Fig. 9A, B). Subgroup analyses based on the risk factors were then conducted. A higher survival was observed in both the young (≤65 years of age) and elderly subgroups (>65 years of age) of the low-risk BLCA patients than in those of the high-risk subgroups (p < 0.001, Fig. 9C, D). Discussion

Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent type of cell death, damages antioxidant capacity and causes accumulation of reactive oxygen species [7, 18]. Ferroptosis inducers can be used to establish antitumor strategies [19–21]. Nevertheless, it has also been suggested that ferroptosis may have both tumor-promoting and tumor-inhibiting effects [8, 22]. Moreover, other studies indicate that lncRNAs play a pivotal role in the development of cancer. Previous study revealed that lncRNAs play a role in fostering the development of various tumor phenotypes through interactions between DNA and RNA [23]. The lncRNAs were proved to influence the onset and progression of urinary cancer and have the potential to serve as biomarkers for such cancer [24]. A previous study explored the FRlncRNAs associated with BLCA and finally screened out five-ferroptosis-related lncRNA signature. However, there are not enough functional experiments to verify the validity of these lncRNAs [25]. Although many genes and lncRNA signatures have been used as prognostic markers in patients with BLCA [26–33], strategies for using FRlncRNA signatures to predict the prognosis of such patients need to be updated. Therefore, we established a FRlncRNA signature and validated its ability to predict the prognosis of patients with BLCA.

After performing numerous analyses of transcriptional and clinical data from the TCGA, we identified 18 lncRNA pairs to construct a signature for BLCA prognosis. Both Kaplan–Meier survival and ROC curve analyses demonstrated that the signature successfully predicted the prognosis of patients with BLCA. Additionally, risk scores calculated based on this signature were positively associated with high tumor grade and stage. Notably, our analysis identified age and tumor stage as independent factors with prognostic significance. The findings consistently demonstrated that the risk score maintains independent prognostic value, establishing it as a standalone prognostic factor. The 18 lncRNA pairs identified in the current study contains some lncRNAs that have been related to several cancers, such as AC010186.3 [34], LINC01605 [35], LINC00641 [36], and MAP3K14—AS [37]. Remarkably, previous studies have reported the involvement of LINC01605 and LINC00641 in BLCA. Some researchers reported that LINC01605 can enhance the proliferation and invasion of BLCA [38]. In contrast, other scholars revealed that LINC00641 can mitigate the progression of BLCA [39]. In addition to discovered lncRNAs, we have also identified new lncRNAs, which we predict that they may be served as new tumor biomarkers. Additionally, we conducted functional experiments to verify the effects of AL355353.2 OE and AL136084.3 KD on apoptosis, colony formation, and cell proliferation. We revealed that OE of AL355353.2 and KD of AL136084.3 both promoted apoptosis in 5637 and T24 cell lines. In the AL355353.2 OE and AL136084.3 KD groups of 5637 and T24 cell lines, the reduction in cell colony count and inhibition of cell proliferation were significant.

In order to explore the impacts of AL136084.3 on patients with BLCA, we employed the Starbase database to assess the expression of AL136084.3 on BLCA. The expression of AL136084.3 was down regulated in BLCA as compare to normal samples. For patients with BLCA, those with high expression of AL136084.3 may have shorter overall survival time than those with low expression. In our previous study, we found that NUPR1 is a potential carcinogen in BLCA [40]. We used the R language to screen the differentially expressed genes and lncRNAs between the NUPR1high and NUPR1low groups among patients with BLCA. A significant correlation was revealed between AL136084.3 and NUPR1, which is also confirmed by the results from the Starbase database. In our another study, we suggested an obvious correlation between the expression of NUPR1 and PPARG in bladder cancer [41]. In the present study, we explored the association between AL136084.3 and NUPR1, PPARG, TIMM44 in BLCA. We revealed that AL136084.3 was positively correlated to NUPR1. While no positive correlation was shown for PPARG. Moreover, we used RIP to verify the result and revealed the reciprocal binding of NUPR1 and AL136084.3 in BLCA.

Regarding to the risk score of immune infiltration, we found that such scores were positively correlated with the infiltration of several cell populations, including CD8+ T cells. In addition, our calculated risk scores correlated with the patient response to ICI, suggesting that our model may predict responses to ICI. ICIs exert an anti-tumor effect mainly by reactivating immune cells and are most effective in inflammatory tumors with high CD8+ T cell infiltration and high PD-L1 expression. Our signature suggests that ICIs are effective for treating BLCA, in line with the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines. When we analyzed ability of our signature to predict tumor sensitivity to commonly used chemotherapeutic agents, the results showed that chemosensitivity was lower in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group. Therefore, we believe that immunotherapy may achieve better results than chemotherapy in high-risk patients. Moreover, there are certain limitations in the present study. We only employed TCGA database and did not validate our model with other external datasets. Our results would benefit from collecting additional samples and clinical trial data that might further validate them. Additionally, more functional experiments are warranted to explore the specific molecular mechanism of the interaction between AL136084.3 and NUPR1 in BLCA. In comparison to prior studies, our approach provides several advantages. Our lncRNA pair signature possesses the advantage of not necessitating the specific expression levels of individual lncRNAs in the sample. Besides, the batch effect was reduced through data mining in online databases [16, 42]. Our research holds significance for the future development of novel therapeutic strategies or diagnostic tools targeting BLCA. In the future, the potential of the prognostic signature involving 18 DEFRlncRNAs in BLCA patients should be further externally validated in multi-center cohorts.

Conclusion

In summary, a prognostic signature involving 18 DEFRlncRNAs exhibited a strong capability in predicting the prognosis and chemotherapy sensitivity in BLCA. AL136084.3 is a risk factor for BLCA. The identified FRlncRNA pair signature has a good prognostic and clinical predictive value. The ferroptosis-related lncRNA AL136084.3 is correlated with NUPR1 in BLCA.

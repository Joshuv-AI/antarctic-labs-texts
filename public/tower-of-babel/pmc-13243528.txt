Polyfluorophenyl quinoxaline derived acceptors with long exciton diffusion length for efficient thin-film/thick-film organic solar cells
Authors: Kai Xiang, Zihao Deng, Wenyan Su, Hairui Bai, Bitao Dong, Na Chen, Ning Su, Haotian Wu, Zhaozhao Bi, Han Liu, Tengfei Li, Yanan Shen, Ke Wang, Dandan Zhang, Rui Sun, Muhammad Hamza Maqsood, Ping Li, Lihe Yan, Guanghao Lu, Yuhang Liu, Jin-Liang Wang, Feng He, Jianhua Chen, Wei Ma, Qunping Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Designing small-molecule acceptors (SMAs) with long exciton diffusion length (LD) and high electron mobility is vital for boosting power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic solar cells (OSCs). However, the limited LD of most advanced SMAs hinders their practical applications in thick-film OSCs. Herein, we develop four SMAs (named Yq-0F, Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F) with fluorine-free/fluorinated phenyl-substituted quinoxaline cores by manipulating the number of fluorine atom. Among them, polyfluorophenyl-substituted Yq-4F exhibits superior 3D network crystal framework and favourable intermolecular packing, leading to optimal blend morphology with polymer donor D18. Therefore, D18:Yq-4F system achieves improved carrier mobilities and suppressed charge recombination loss. Consequently, the D18:Yq-4F based binary OSCs provide a PCE of 18.30%, surpassing these ones based on D18:Yq-0F (11.27%), D18:Yq-2F (17.73%), and D18:Yq-6F (17.00%). When introducing Yq-4F as a guest into D18:L8-BO host to fabricate ternary OSCs, the PCE further increases to 20.77%, as the highest reported value among quinoxaline-derived SMAs. Moreover, ternary D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F OSCs with ~500 nm active layer thickness offer impressive PCEs of >17%, ranking among the top values for thick-film devices with similar thickness. This work offers an effective SMA design strategy for opening a path towards efficient thin-film and thick-film OSCs simultaneously.

Introduction

Organic solar cells (OSCs) have emerged as a promising next-generation energy generators owing to their unique advantages, including lightweight, mechanical flexibility, semi-transparency, and color tunability1–5. Recent progress in photovoltaic material design, particularly developing non-fullerene small-molecule acceptors (SMAs, such as ITIC-series and Y-series)6–8 and wide bandgap polymer donors9,10, has propelled most advanced OSCs to achieve power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 20%11–15. Despite these breakthroughs, efficient OSCs typically require active layers with a thickness of ~100 nm16. However, thin active layers are prone to forming defects and are difficult to control during large-area solution processing17,18, which poses significant challenges for high-throughput manufacturing upgrading to industrial applications in future. Therefore, developing OSCs with high-performance thick active layers (e.g., ≥200 nm), that can largely expand the printing process window, is essential for large-scale fabrication. On the other hand, thick-film OSCs still face multiple limitations, including short exciton diffusion length (LD), low and unbalanced charge mobilities, and aggravated charge recombination, all of which undermine photovoltaic performance.

In OSCs, the photoconversion process involves four key steps: exciton generation, exciton diffusion, exciton separation at donor-acceptor (D/A) interfaces, and charge transport/collection19. Among these, the LD plays a decisive role in determining how efficiently excitons reach the D/A interfaces before recombining. Typically, the LD of organic photovoltaic materials is 10-20 nm, which is much shorter than their phase separation scale in active layers, leading to severe exciton geminate recombination20,21. Recently, the newly developed Y-series SMAs (Y-SMAs) have led to increased exciton diffusion coefficient (D) and lifetime (τ), allowing LD to exceed 20 nm, which improves exciton harvesting and enables PCE breakthrough in thin-film OSCs22. However, as the active layer becomes thicker, the required LD also increases accordingly, while current active layer materials are still fall short in this regard. On the other hand, charge mobilities also strongly affect the photovoltaic performance of thick-film OSCs, due to high charge mobilities not only boost charge extraction, but also suppress charge recombination23–25. Unfortunately, the transition from fullerene derivatives to Y-SMAs has resulted in a shift from isotropic to anisotropic charge transport, often compromising electron mobility (μe). Therefore, designing Y-SMAs that simultaneously possess long LD and high μe is crucial for advancing thick-film OSCs.

Previous studies have demonstrated that improving the crystallinity and optimizing the packing of Y-SMAs can enhance both LD and μe26–28. So far, molecular design strategies, such as fused-core engineering, side-chain adjustment, and end-group modification, are frequently employed to optimize crystallinity and packing of Y-SMAs29–34. Among them, replacing the conventional benzothiadiazole (BT) core of Y-SMAs to benzotriazole (BTA) units with different N-site substituents has proven effective in promoting molecular geometry, intermolecular interaction, and order stacking35,36. For example, Shin et al. reported a Y-SMA named YNPF3 containing trifluoro-phenyl substituted BTA core36. Compared to its alkyl substituted control, YNPF3 with surprisingly varied local dipole moment and molecular crystallinity achieved an impressive PCE of 16.6% in a mini-module device with an aperture size of 12.5 cm2. On the other hand, another method for producing high-efficiency Y-SMAs is to use quinoxaline (Qx) units in place of the BT core37–42. Nevertheless, Qx units derived cores in their Y-SMAs also can lead to increased optical bandgap, less favorable energy levels, and defective active layer morphology, ultimately hindering photovoltaic performance of OSCs43–45. To address these challenges, the halogenation of Qx core has been proposed to fine-tune optoelectronic properties of Y-SMAs46,47. However, the conventional Qx cores offer limited halogenation sites. A more versatile approach is to incorporate a hybrid Qx core by substituting non-fused 2D-conjugated phenyl substitutions and constructing fused dibenzoquinoxaline and dithienoquinoxaline units, which not only expands the halogenation capacity, but also enables the regulation of molecular solubility, energy levels, packing, and film-forming properties48–50. Among these halogen atoms, fluorine (F) has the smallest atomic radius but the strongest electronegativity, making it easily polarizable and facilitating improved molecular packing, crystallinity, and intermolecular interaction51,52. Consequently, in particular, developing polyfluorinated phenyl Qx derived Y-SMAs may be able to provide a promising platform for enhancing intermolecular interaction and crystallinity, which are critical for enhancing both LD and μe.

Herein, we developed four Y-SMAs named Yq-nF (n = 0, 2, 4, and 6) by delicately manipulating the fluorination amount on 2D-conjugated phenyl-substituted Qx core (Fig. 1a). Systematic studies reveal that moderate fluorination (n = 4) leads to favorable energy levels, as well as superior molecular packing, crystallinity, LD, and carrier mobility. As a result, the D18:Yq-4F based binary OSCs achieved a PCE of 18.30%, surpassing these ones based on D18:Yq-0F (11.27%), D18:Yq-2F (17.73%), and D18:Yq-6F (17.00%). Introducing Yq-4F as a guest into D18:L8-BO host to fabricate ternary OSCs, the PCE increased to 20.77%, as the highest value among these reported Qx-derived Y-SMAs. Moreover, the OSCs based on ternary active layers of D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F with a thickness of ~500 nm offered impressive PCEs of >17%, ranking the highest values among the previously reported thick-film devices with similar thickness. Results and discussion

Synthetic routes and molecular structures of Yq-nF series were depicted in Fig. 1a. Polyfluorophenyl derived benzil compounds (a and b) were synthesized using a same method but different raw materials. Then, starting from commercially available precursor 1, a reduction reaction with Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) in tetrahydrofuran (THF) afforded diamine intermediate 2. Without further purification, compound 2 underwent Cyclo-condensation with fluorine-free and fluorinated benzil compounds to obtain 2D phenyl-substituted Qx derivatives (3a–d), each bearing different numbers of F atoms. These intermediates were subsequently converted to aldehyde-functionalized compounds (4a-d) via a Vilsmeier-Haack reaction. Finally, the target Yq-nF (n = 0, 2, 4, and 6) series were synthesized through Knoevenagel condensation. The molecular structures of Yq-nF series and their key intermediates were verified by 1H/13C NMR and mass spectrometry (Supplementary Figs. 1–22).

Thermal properties of our developed Yq-nF series were systematically studied using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), UV-vis deviation metric (DMT), and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), respectively. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 23 of TGA curves, Yq-nF series exhibited excellent thermal stability, while their polyfluorophenyl derived materials offered higher decomposition temperatures. Similarly, based on their DMT results (Supplementary Fig. 24), the polyfluorophenyl derived Yq-nF materials also achieved superior glass-transition temperatures. In their DSC plots (Supplementary Fig. 25), all the Yq-nF series showed obvious melting peaks. With the increase of fluorination degree, Yq-nF series offered gradually increased melting temperatures (Tm) from 224.3 °C to 250.8 °C, primarily due to the sequential increase in their molecular weights. Notably, Yq-4F with a moderate molecular weight obtained a higher melting enthalpy (ΔHm) of −48.28 J g-1 compared to Yq-0F (−46.48 J g-1), Yq-2F (−47.23 J g-1), and Yq-6F (−47.15 J g-1), implying stronger molecular crystallinity and intermolecular interactions. The above results suggest that varying fluorination degrees in 2D phenyl substituted Qx core of Yq-nF series can effectively tailor intermolecular interaction and molecular thermal stability53.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations (B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p)) were carried out to study the optimal molecular geometries, frontier orbitals, and electrostatic potential (ESP) distributions of Yq-nF series. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 26, all Yq-nF molecules exhibit similar and nearly planar geometry between their DA’D core and end-groups. Introducing F atoms makes a significant effect on molecular polarizability, and the relevant molecular dipole moment values can be calculated as 3.18, 1.40, 1.02, and 3.94 Debye for Yq-0F, Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F, respectively. As fluorination in Yn-4F series increases, the corresponding dipole directions were reversed gradually51. Among them, due to the uniform distribution of F atoms between two end groups and two 2D phenyl substitutions, Yq-4F offers smaller dipole moment. Concurrently, the dipole orientation of Yq-4F is nearly vertical to molecular backbone, implying a strong out-of-plane preference for dipole moment26, which is beneficial for boosting intermolecular interaction and charge transport behaviors54,55. The electron clouds of highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) are primarily localized on the DA’D core, while the ones of lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) extend across the entire backbone (Supplementary Fig. 27). Fluorination on 2D phenyl of Qx core down-shifts molecular frontier orbital energy levels, where the calculated HOMO and LUMO values are −5.36/−3.36, −5.39/−3.37, −5.41/−3.38, and −5.44/−3.40 eV for Yq-0F, Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F, respectively. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 28, electrochemical measurements using cyclic voltammetry (CV) confirmed the above DFT trends, wherein fluorination on 2D phenyl of Qx core can down-shift both HOMO and LUMO energy levels of Yq-nF series. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 29, compared to the classic L8-BO56, Yq-nF (n = 0, 2, 4) materials provide up-shifted LUMO levels, indicating that introducing them into D18:L8-BO host will boost open-circuit voltage (VOC) of devices. On the other hand, compared to Yq-nF series, near-infrared absorbing BTP-eC9 has a down-shifted LUMO level but significantly broadened absorption57, which means that introducing Yq-nF into D18:BTP-eC9 host tends to achieve both higher VOC and short-circuit current density (JSC). As shown in Supplementary Fig. 30, Yq-nF series show similar but slightly improved positive ESP distributions as the degree of fluorination increases, indicating their enhanced acceptor character. As a result, their average ESP values rise from 5.15 kcal mol-1 (Yq-0F) to 7.94 kcal mol-1 (Yq-6F) (Supplementary Fig. 31). In contrast, polymer donor D18 displays a negative ESP distribution along most of its conjugated surface (Supplementary Fig. 32), which favors strong intermolecular electrostatic interaction between it and Yq-nF series. Furthermore, as depicted in Fig. 1b, the non-covalent interactions between D18 and Yq-nF were further studied using a reduced density gradient (RDG) method. Among them, D18:Yq-4F offers the highest calculated binding energy of -61.71 kcal mol-1, suggesting their enhanced intermolecular interaction, which helps to achieve more favorable molecular stacking and improved phase compatibility58,59.

As depicted in Fig. 1c and Supplementary Table 1, UV-vis absorption spectra of Yq-nF series show a gradual blue shift in both diluted chloroform solution and film states, as the fluorination degree increases. In solutions, their absorption peaks range from 746 nm (Yq-0F) to 723 nm (Yq-6F). In films, their absorption peaks shift to 802–772 nm, and the corresponding optical bandgaps were estimated as 1.41–1.45 eV. The gradually blue-shifted absorption spectra and peaks are mainly attributed to the reduced intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) effect, which is caused by the decreased electron-donating capacity of fluorinated phenyl substituted Qx core60. Moreover, as displayed in Supplementary Fig. 33, Yq-4F film depicts a much higher extinction coefficient of 0.92 × 105 cm-1 compared to the Yq-0F (0.66×105 cm-1), Yq-2F (0.79×105 cm-1), and Yq-6F (0.70 × 105 cm-1) films, implying its superior intermolecular interaction and ordered packing properties, which is conducive to boosting photon capture. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 34, compared to the fluorine-free Yq-0F, fluorinated Yq-nF ones display stronger intermolecular aggregation, as shown by their increased vibration peak evolution in diluted solutions under different temperatures. As exhibited in Supplementary Fig. 35 of the PL spectra, among the Yq-nF series, Yq-4F offers the strongest PL intensity, implying high film quality with fewer defects to reduce non-radiative recombination14. Furthermore, the PL of Yq-4F can be almost completely quenched upon blending with D18, indicating efficient charge transfer between them.

Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to assess how fluorination on 2D phenyl of Qx core affects molecular conformation and packing (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Fig. 36). High-quality crystals of Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F series were grown in chloroform by the solvent diffusion method using ethanol as the poor solvent. The crystal data of Yq-0F refers to the reported work61. As shown in Fig. 2a, all four crystalline Yq-nF exhibit planar backbones with minor twist angle of 0.78–6.50o, while polyfluorophenyl based Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F (55.46-56.90o) offer smaller dihedral angles between the phenyl substituents in the Qx cores compared to that of Yq-0F (60.66o). As depicted in Supplementary Fig. 36, Yq-2F and Yq-4F in crystals possess four distinct intermolecular packing modes, including end group (IC)/phenyl-1, IC/thienothiophene (TT), TT/phenyl-2, and IC/IC interactions, which can provide multiple charge transport channels62. On the other hands, the Yq-2F and Yq-4F with moderate fluorination show decreased π-π stacking distances, implying the enhanced intermolecular interactions. In details, polyfluorophenyl derived Yq-4F features superior π-π stacking of 3.43–3.44 Å, which facilitates charge transport and minimizes charge recombination of devices. As seen in Fig. 2b, multiple intermolecular noncovalent interactions were observed in the crystals of fluorophenyl derived Yq-2F and Yq-4F, including F···S, F···N, and π···π, which contribute to the formation of a robust 3D interpenetrating packing network. As depicted in Figs. 2c, Yq-2F and Yq-4F crystals form elliptical orthorhombic frames of 28.90 × 17.38 and 28.54×17.37 Å, while Yq-4F has a more compact and ordered intermolecular packing, as well as stronger multiple intermolecular interactions, implying improved spatial charge transport pathways and higher charge mobility51. Differently, due to the large molecular backbone twist and missing F···S interactions exhibited by Yq-0F, while Yq-6F offers longer F···S distance (~6.65 Å), these two molecules are unable to generate IC/phenyl-1 and TT/phenyl-2 interactions, resulting in linear (Yq-0F) and elliptical (Yq-6F) packing arrangements, respectively. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were carried out to further understand the intermolecular packing of Yq-nF series63. As depicted in Supplementary Fig. 37, the gradual fluorination of Yq-nF series leads to distinct molecular packing patterns. In the MD optimized crystal frameworks, Yq-0F, Yq-2F, and Yq-6F form aggregates with 2, 2, and 4 unit cells, respectively. Differently, Yq-4F displays a unique packing with up to 8 molecular unit cells, indicating a more extended and interconnected packing mode. Previous reports have shown that the enhanced molecular packing can improve the dielectric constant of materials64,65. As exhibited in Supplementary Fig. 38, the impedance analysis was carried out to measure the dielectric constants of Yq-0F, Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F films, which were 3.12, 3.64, 4.02, and 3.28, respectively. Among them, Yq-4F film with a significantly larger dielectric constant tends to offer a higher LD64.

Grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurement was utilized to further investigate the molecular crystallinity and packing of Yq-nF series. As depicted in Fig. 1e and Supplementary Fig. 39, all the Yq-nF films achieved pronounced (010) diffraction peaks in the out-of-plane (OOP) direction, indicative of a dominant “face-on” orientation. The corresponding (010) diffraction peaks appear at 1.68, 1.68, 1.69, and 1.57 Å-1 for Yq-0F, Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F films, translating to the π-π stacking distances of 3.74, 3.74, 3.71 and 4.0 Å (Supplementary Table 2), respectively. According to the Scherrer equation, their crystal coherence lengths (CCLs) were calculated to be 8.64-19.47 Å, while Yq-4F film has a superior CCL value. This further demonstrates that moderate fluorination can improve molecular orderliness and crystallinity, thereby obtaining more efficient exciton diffusion and charge transport66. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 40, Fig. 1d, and Supplementary Table 3, the μe values of Yq-nF series were evaluated using space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) method. Among them, Yq-4F achieved a higher μe value compared to these of Yq-0F, Yq-2F, and Yq-6F, which is consistent with their single crystal, MD, and GIWAXS results.

Subsequently, femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy (fs-TAS) was used to evaluate the photoexcited-state dynamics of films, including exciton lifetime (τ), exciton diffusion coefficient (D), and resulted LD values, respectively67. The 2D plots of fs-TAS of D18:Yq-nF blends under different excitation powers of each acceptors were displayed in Supplementary Fig. 41. Photovoltaic performances of Yq-nF series in the OSCs based on both binary D18:Yq-nF and ternary D18:L8-BO:Yq-nF were investigated using a device structure of ITO/2PACz/active layer/PDINN/Ag. The current density-voltage (J-V) curves and related photovoltaic data were summarized in Fig. 3a, b and Table 1. Among the binary OSCs, the D18:Yq-0F control device yielded a decent PCE of 11.27% with a VOC of 0.967 V, JSC of 18.63 mA cm-2, and fill factor (FF) of 62.57%. With introducing two F atoms into 2D phenyl of Qx core to synthesize Yq-2F, the related OSCs based on D18:Yq-2F delivered a much higher PCE of 17.73%, due to both significantly improved JSC (24.56 mA cm-2) and FF (77.62%) values, albeit with a decreased VOC of 0.930 V. Encouragingly, the D18:Yq-4F device achieved a champion PCE of 18.30% among the Yq-nF series, with a VOC of 0.924 V, JSC of 24.99 mA cm-2, and excellent FF of 79.27%, which represents one of the top-performing binary OSCs based on Qx-core derived Y-SMAs. The superior photovoltaic performance of D18:Yq-4F in OSCs can be attributed to the well-balanced trade-off among VOC, JSC, and FF. Considering the slightly blue-shifted absorption of Yq-4F, the boosted JSC and FF of D18:Yq-4F device may profit from favorable blend morphology, molecular packing, and charge transport properties. However, further increasing fluorination led to a decline in photovoltaic performance, with the D18:Yq-6F OSC yielding a reduced PCE of 17.00%, due to the markedly decreased JSC (24.28 mA cm-2) and FF (78.10%). To further explore the effect of fluorination in Yq-nF series on device performance, ternary OSCs were fabricated by introducing Yq-nF series as guest acceptors into D18:L8-BO system (Fig. 3a, b and Table 1). Surprisingly, the D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F ternary OSC achieved a remarkable PCE of 20.77%, outperforming its parental binary systems of D18:Yq-4F (18.30%) and D18:L8-BO (18.66%) (Supplementary Fig. 45) and other ternary counterparts of D18:L8-BO:Yq-0F (16.02%), D18:L8-BO:Yq-2F (19.98%), and D18:L8-BO:Yq-6F (18.19%). Compared to the binary D18:Yq-4F and D18:L8-BO systems, the ternary D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F system achieved a much higher FF, mainly due to its optimized morphology (Supplementary Fig. 46), improved charge dynamics (Supplementary Fig. 42), enhanced charge transports (Supplementary Fig. 47 and Supplementary Table 4), and reduced charge recombination (Supplementary Fig. 48). On the other hand, among these ternary devices, the D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F based OSC obtained the highest VOC, which can be attributed to its low energy loss (Supplementary Figs. 49–50 and Supplementary Table 5). Notably, the PCE of 20.77% for D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F system is currently the highest value for the OSCs using Qx-core derived SMAs. The consistent PCE trends observed in both binary and ternary devices further confirm the effectiveness of controlled fluorination in fine-tuning the optoelectronic and morphological properties of active layers, reinforcing the versatility of the Yq-nF molecular design strategy.

External quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of both binary and ternary OSCs were performed to further confirm the differences in JSC values (Fig. 3c and Table 1). All devices exhibited strong and broad EQE responses from 350 to 900 nm, which is consistent with the complementary absorption profiles of D18 and Yq-nF components. According to the EQE curves, the integrated JSC values of OSCs based on binary D18:Yq-nF and ternary D18:L8-BO:Yq-nF can be calculated as 18.05–23.75 and 25.90–26.87 mA cm-2, respectively, which are very close to their J-V results, with discrepancies below 5%, validating the accuracy of measurements. Notably, among them, the devices with binary D18:Yq-4F and ternary D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F showed higher EQE response in most of the wavelength range, which are consistent with their higher JSC values.

To explore the potential scalability of Yq-4F for practical applications in the future, thick-film OSCs based on ternary D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F systems were fabricated. As shown in Supplementary Figs. 51–52, Fig. 3d, e, and Table 2, using a device structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/ternary active layer/PDINN/Ag, the influence of active layer thicknesses from 110 to 700 nm on device performance was systemically studied. Here, we introduced classic Y-SMAs named L8-BO and BTP-eC9 respectively into D18:Yq-4F system to prepare efficient ternary thick-film OSCs, mainly due to the D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F system has both high FF and PCE values as proven above and the D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 system has broadened near-infrared absorption57. As the active layer thickness increases, the JSC values improved continuously, reaching 27.80 mA cm-2 at 320 nm for the D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F system and 28.85 mA cm-2 at 500 nm for the D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 system, respectively, mainly due to enhanced photon capture. However, both VOC and FF values gradually declined, likely due to increased charge recombination in thicker films. The corresponding PCEs for the OSCs based on D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F with active layer thicknesses of 200, 320, 500, and 700 nm were 18.67% and 19.37%, 18.43% and 18.49%, 17.11% and 17.37%, 13.81% and 13.96%, respectively. Further studies indicate that the above ternary OSCs with an active layer thickness of 500 nm offered ideal morphologies (Supplementary Figs. 53-57 and Supplementary Table 6), as well as high and balanced charge transporting properties (Supplementary Fig. 58 and Supplementary Table 7), which partly explain why the relevant thick-film OSCs can achieve high FF and PCE values. To our knowledge, the above achieved PCEs of >17% rank among the highest values in the previously reported thick-film devices with active layer thicknesses ≥500 nm (Fig. 3e and Supplementary Table 8), highlighting the potential of our developed ternary systems with polyfluorophenyl Qx-core derived Yq-4F in fabricating large-area OSCs. Further, the long-term stabilities of ternary OSCs based on D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F with an active layer thickness of ~500 nm were investigated under a continuous 1-sun simulated illumination at N2-filled glovebox. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 59, typical aging phenomena of their PCEs were observed. The dynamic processes during the film formations of D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F with different thicknesses of 110 and 500 nm were investigated using the in-situ time-resolved absorption spectra (Supplementary Fig. 53). The phase-transition processes of films can be divided into three stages: I) solvent evaporation, II) crystal growth, and III) film stabilization, respectively. In stage I, a significant decrease in the absorption intensity of both donor and acceptor components can be observed, which is attributed to the removal of excess solvent. The D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F film with 500 nm thickness showed an increased solvent evaporation time (0.22 s) compared to the one with 110 nm thickness (0.14 s), which facilitates molecular diffusion. The similar phenomenon has been also observed in the D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 systems with different thicknesses of 110 nm (0.19 s) and 500 nm (0.33 s). In stage II, when the concentration exceeds the solubility, the donor and acceptor components enter the crystal growth. In all ternary films, the slow increase of absorption intensity of acceptors corresponds to their pre-aggregation process, while the rapid increases in absorption intensity of donor and acceptors correspond to their crystallization processes. The 500 nm thick films offered effectively prolonged molecular aggregation time of donor (0.10-0.12 s) compared to these ones of 110 nm thin films (0.06-0.07 s), which also provides better basis for their crystallization process. In stage III of the rapid increase in absorption intensity, the 500 nm thick films also obtained longer film stabilization times of 0.13–0.15 s than these 110 nm thin films (0.09–0.11 s). Therefore, the above 500 nm thick films tend to form ideal morphology with superior molecular crystallinity and packing, resulting in better exciton diffusion and charge transport properties in OSCs. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 54 of GIWAXS profiles, the D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F blends with a 500 nm thickness depicted better molecular crystallinity and packing properties, reflected in their sharper (010) diffraction peaks with higher CCLs of 18.57 and 20.13 Å in the OOP direction (Supplementary Table 6), respectively, compared to the ones with a 110 nm thickness (16.63 and 17.15 Å).

To deeply understand the exciton dissociation dynamics of our developed OSCs, time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were performed. As shown in Fig. 3f, the PL lifetimes of the D18:Yq-0F, D18:Yq-2F, D18:Yq-4F, and D18:Yq-6F blends were 0.61, 0.68, 0.93, and 0.62 ns, respectively. The longer PL lifetime of D18:Yq-4F blend suggests slower exciton decay and more efficient exciton diffusion and dissociation at the D-A interfaces68.

Photocurrent density versus effective voltage ( Jph-Veff) curves were plotted to further estimate the exciton dissociation efficiency (ηd) and charge collection efficiency (ηc) of OSCs, by measuring the ratios between Jph and saturated JSC ( Jsat) under short-circuit condition and maximal power output condition, respectively. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 60 and Fig. 3g, the ηd and ηc values were 97.73% and 82.97%, 98.89% and 88.53%, 99.06% and 89.64%, 98.69% and 86.45% for the D18:Yq-0F, D18:Yq-2F, D18:Yq-4F, and D18:Yq-6F devices, respectively. The D18:Yq-4F based device obtained the highest ηd and ηc values, correlating with its superior JSC and FF values.

As exhibited in Supplementary Figs. 61–62, transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements were conducted to further explore carrier lifetime and charge extraction dynamics of OSCs. The carrier lifetimes of the devices based on D18:Yq-0F, D18:Yq-2F, D18:Yq-4F, and D18:Yq-6F, obtained from their TPV decay curves, were 18.10, 21.29, 22.92, and 19.24 μs, respectively. Meanwhile, by fitting the TPC decay curves, the carrier extraction times of the devices based on D18:Yq-0F, D18:Yq-2F, D18:Yq-4F, and D18:Yq-6F can be estimated as 0.48, 0.27, 0.25, and 0.32 μs, respectively. These results confirm that the D18:Yq-4F device possesses the most favorable dynamics with longer carrier lifetime and faster charge extraction, minimizing charge recombination and boosting device performance.

To complement the above analyses, the SCLC method was carried out to determine charge transport characteristics of these systems (Supplementary Fig. 63 and Supplementary Table 9). Hole mobilities (μh) and μe of the D18:Yq-0F, D18:Yq-2F, D18:Yq-4F, and D18:Yq-6F devices were measured as 4.06 × 10-4/2.95 × 10-4, 6.01 × 10-4/4.49 × 10-4, 7.11 × 10-4/5.71 × 10-4, and 4.72 × 10-4/3.46 × 10-4cm2 V-1 s-1, respectively. The boosted charge transport properties with higher and balanced μh and μe values of the D18:Yq-4F device are attributed to its improved molecular ordering, crystallinity, and stacking (discussed later). Moreover, the devices based on ternary D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F provided further increased μh and μe values compared to these of binary devices based on D18:L8-BO and D18:Yq-4F (Supplementary Fig. 47 and Supplementary Table 4), which supports the observed high JSC and FF values.

The fs-TAS measurements were conducted to study the photogenerated exciton dynamics within active layers of OSCs. Negative and positive ΔT/T signals correspond to the ground state bleaching (GSB) and the regions of excited state absorption (ESA) of materials. As shown in Fig. 4a, b, all blends exhibited a prominent GSB peak at ~810 nm immediately following excitation. Then, a decay in the feature signal was observed alongside the emergence of a new GSB peak at 620 nm, suggesting hole transfer from D18 to Yq-nF. As depicted in Fig. 4c, to quantify the electron transfer kinetics and charge recombination dynamics of active layers, the GSB signal at 590 nm was fitted using a biexponential function comprising two lifetimes (τ1 and τ2). Here, τ1 is assigned to the ultrafast dissociation time at the D/A interfaces of the excitons formed in the acceptor component, and τ2 denotes the exciton diffusion time to the D/A interfaces69. Among these blends, the D18:Yq-4F one gained the shortest τ1 and τ2 values (2.85 and 27.51 ps) compared to the ones of D18:Yq-0F (3.34 and 29.12 ps), D18:Yq-2F (2.99 and 28.05 ps), and D18:Yq-6F (3.25 and 28.87 ps), suggesting faster exciton dissociation and more efficient exciton diffusion. To evaluate active layer surface morphologies, atomic force microscopy (AFM) was employed. As shown in height images (Fig. 5a), the D18:Yq-4F film shows a more textured surface with a root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of 2.90 nm, which is slightly higher than D18:Yq-2F (2.80 nm) but considerably smoother than D18:Yq-0F (4.53 nm) and D18:Yq-6F (5.65 nm). Phase images (Fig. 5b and Supplementary Fig. 64) reveal that the D18:Yq-4F film form a relatively uniform and well-defined fibrillar interpenetrating network, which facilitates efficient charge generation, transport, and extraction70. On the other hand, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 46, ternary D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F film offers a moderate RMS roughness of 1.78 nm compared to these of binary films of D18:L8-BO (1.67 nm) and D18:Yq-4F (2.17 nm), offering more uniform fibrillar network to further boost charge transport and suppress charge recombination. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 55, AFM based infrared (AFM-IR) images of thin-film (110 nm) and thick-film (500 nm) ternary blends of D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F were measured. All the ternary blends exhibited co-continuous interpenetrating network, facilitating exciton dissociation and charge transport in devices. As depicted in Supplementary Fig. 56, the average sizes of donor:acceptor fibers were calculated as 8.3/12.7 and 9.1/13.5 nm for thin-film/thick-film D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F, respectively. Due to the larger donor:acceptor fibers (phases) in thick-film ternary blends, a longer LD is required to ensure that the excitons can effectively diffuse to the D:A interfaces for dissociation in their devices. In addition, the phase separation behavior of ternary blends was explored using Resonant soft X-ray scattering (RESOXS) measurement. As exhibited in Supplementary Fig. 57 of the Lorentz corrected scattering results, the phase separation sizes of the above-mentioned thin-film/thick-film D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F were estimated to be 13.80/16.75 and 14.65/18.05 nm, respectively. The enhanced phase separation of thick-film ternary blends is beneficial for exciton dissociation in the D:A interface, which is also critical for elevating the JSC and FF values of thick-film devices.

Film-depth-dependent light absorption spectroscopy (FLAS) was performed to analyze exciton generation and vertical phase distributions of D18:Yq-nF blends (Supplementary Fig. 65). Among them, D18:Yq-4F blend has a more balanced D:A component phase distributions in vertical direction, especially in the 20-80 nm depth region (Fig. 5c), which is beneficial for exciton dissociation and contributes to the improved JSC and FF values in OSCs. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 65b, the exciton generation profiles of active layers can be numerically simulated using the transfer matrix method from the FLAS curves, wherein the generated excitons primarily occurs near the bottom of active layers. According to the exciton generation contours, the simulated exciton generation rates (G) at different film-depths were estimated. As depicted in Supplementary Fig. 66, the D18:Yq-4F film gained a higher maximum G value of 1.90 × 1028 than these films of D18:Yq-0F (1.62 × 1028), D18:Yq-2F (1.56 × 1028), and D18:Yq-6F (1.59 × 1028), which is consistent with its higher charge extraction efficiency and suppressed charge recombination (Supplementary Figs. 61–62) in devices, leading to higher JSC and FF values. A similar phenomenon has been also observed in the previous reports71.

GIWAXS measurement was performed to further investigate molecular crystallinity and packing of D18:Yq-nF blends (Fig. 5d, e). All the D18:Yq-nF blends show obvious (010) diffraction peaks in the OOP direction and (100) diffraction peaks in the in-plane (IP) direction, suggesting a “face-on” dominant orientation. Among these blends, the D18:Yq-4F one depicts more pronounced (100) lamellar diffraction peak at 0.30 Å-1 in the IP direction and (010) π-π stacking diffraction peak at 1.69 Å-1 in the OOP direction, with the corresponding CCLs of 97.23 and 25.37 Å (Supplementary Table 10), respectively. These features indicate tighter molecular stacking and stronger crystallinity in D18:Yq-4F film, which facilitates the formation of multi-level charge transport channels and underpins the improved JSC and FF values in OSCs.

In summary, four Y-SMAs (named Yq-nF, including Yq-0F, Yq-2F, Yq-4F, and Yq-6F) with fluorine-free/fluorinated phenyl-substituted Qx cores were developed by controlling the number of F atoms. Among them, polyfluorophenyl substituted Yq-4F shows superior 3D network crystal framework and intermolecular packing, resulting in optimal active layer morphology with polymer donor D18. Therefore, binary D18:Yq-4F achieved the superior LD, improved charge transport, and suppressed charge recombination loss. As a result, the D18:Yq-4F based binary OSCs obtained a superior PCE of 18.30% compared to these ones with D18:Yq-0F (11.27%), D18:Yq-2F (17.73%), and D18:Yq-6F (17.00%). Notably, when incorporating Yq-4F as a guest into D18:L8-BO host to fabricate ternary OSCs, the PCE further increased to 20.77%, as the highest reported value among Qx-core derived SMAs. Furthermore, the D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 and D18:L8-BO:Yq-4F based ternary OSCs offered an impressive PCE of over 17% at an active layer thickness of ~500 nm, also ranking among the highest values for thick-film devices with similar thickness. This work offers an effective molecular design strategy for opening a path towards both efficient thin-film and thick-film OSCs.

Methods

Device fabrication and characterization

The patterned indium tin oxide (ITO, with a sheet resistance of 15 Ω square-1) glass substrates were sequentially ultrasonicated with detergent, deionized water, and ethanol, respectively. Then, the ITO glasses were treated with UV-ozone for 25 min. The 2PACz layer, which was dissolved in ethanol at a concentration of 0.27 mg mL-1, the solutions was deposited on the top of ITO using a spin-coating technology at 3000 rpm and then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. Or PEDOT:PSS was deposited on the top of ITO using a spin-coating technology at 4000 rpm and then annealed at 150 °C for 15 min. The mixed D18:acceptors were dissolved in chloroform under 80 °C for 2 h to mix intensively in a N2-filled glovebox. The concentration is 9.0 mg mL-1 in total of D18:Yq-nF (1:1 in weight ratio), D18:L8-BO:Yq-nF (1:0.8:0.2 in weight ratio), and D18:Yq-4F:BTP-eC9 (1:0.6:0.4 in weight ratio). The 1,4-diiodobenzene (DIB, 5 mg mL-1) is used as an additive. The blend solutions were spin-coated on the top of ITO/2PACz substrates at a speed of 2200 rpm for 40 s, followed by a thermal annealing at 100 °C for 10 min. Thick-film devices were obtained by adjusting the concentration of blend solutions and spin-coating speeds. The active layer thickness is 110 ~ 700 nm, which was measured by Bruker Dektak XT. The PDINN solution (1.0 mg mL-1 in methanol) was spin-coated on the top of active layers at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, Ag electrode (100 nm) was deposited under high vacuum (~10-5 Pa) in an evaporation chamber. The device area was exactly fixed at 0.0256 cm2. The J-V curves of OSCs were measured using a Keysight 2400 source meter in glovebox under AM 1.5 G (100 mW cm-2) using a Newport solar simulator. The EQE values were measured with an JingZhu QE solar quantum efficiency test system.

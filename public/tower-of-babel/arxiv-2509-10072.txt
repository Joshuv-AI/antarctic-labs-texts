Retraction Theorems for Group Compactifications
Yair Hartman1, Aranka Hrušková2, Mehrdad Kalantar3, Tomer Zimhoni4

Abstract
We characterize group compactifications of discrete groups for which there exists an equivariant retraction onto the boundary. In particular, we prove an equivariant analogue of Brouwer’s No-Retraction theorem for large classes of group compactifications, which includes actions of hyperbolic groups on their Gromov boundary.



1
Introduction

One of the original inspirations for the modern theory of boundary actions of topological groups was the case of the compact unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, where the harmonic functions are in correspondence with functions on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
.
This inspired Furstenberg
[
Fur63
,
Fur73
]
to introduce far-reaching generalizations of compactifications of topological groups, whose resulting boundary theories have proven to be a powerful tool in the study of non-amenable groups and various structures corresponding to them, most prominently in rigidity theory of semisimple groups and their lattices
[
Fur73
,
Mar78
,
SZ94
,
Bad+22
]
.

Returning to the classical case of the compact disc
\mathbb{D}
, one of the most celebrated results in the context is Brouwer’s No-Retraction theorem, which states that there is no retraction from
\mathbb{D}
onto its boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

The main goal of this paper is to investigate analogues of Brouwer’s theorem in the context of group compactifications considered by Furstenberg
[
Fur71
]
.

Given a countable discrete group
\Gamma
, by a
\Gamma
-compactification we mean a compactification
\overline{\Gamma}
such that the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma
by left multiplication extends to a continuous action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\overline{\Gamma}
. We focus particularly on the case where the resulting action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\partial\overline{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
on the boundary
\partial\overline{\Gamma}:=\overline{\Gamma}\backslash\Gamma
is minimal, that is, has no proper closed
\Gamma
-invariant subspace.

Among the most natural examples of this type of compactifications is the Gromov compactification of the free group
\mathbb{F}_{n}
(or more generally, hyperbolic groups). We prove an equivariant analogue of Brouwer’s No-Retraction theorem in this case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, and denote by
\partial\Gamma
its Gromov boundary and by
\overline{\Gamma}
the Gromov compactification of
\Gamma
. Then there exists
no

\Gamma
-equivariant retraction
\overline{\Gamma}\to\partial\Gamma
.

Some remarks are in order. First, we note that the condition of equivariance is necessary in this context. Indeed, some retraction
\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma\to\partial\Gamma
always exists owing to
\Gamma
being discrete.
Second, we will see that the “no-retraction” property for a
\Gamma
-compactification is essentially related to the manner of “gluing” of the group
\Gamma
to the boundary of the compactification. Concretely, we will see that there is an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-compactification
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
whose boundary
\partial\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
is the Gromov boundary
\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and such that there exists a retraction
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\to\partial\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
.

Let us recall the construction of the
\Gamma
-compactifications studied by Furstenberg in
[
Fur71
]
.

Let
X
be a compact metrizable minimal
\Gamma
-space, and let
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
be a contractive measure (i.e., the weak* closure of the orbit
\{\gamma\nu:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
contains Dirac measures). Define the topology
\tau_{\nu}
on the disjoint union
\Gamma\cup X
to be the one that on
X
restricts to the original topology on
X
, on
\Gamma
to the discrete topology on
\Gamma
, and such that for an unbounded sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma
and
x\in X
,
\gamma_{n}\to x
if and only if
\gamma_{n}\nu\to\delta_{x}
in the weak* topology on
{\rm Prob}(X)
.
In Lemma
3.1
we show that there indeed exists a unique topology on
\Gamma\cup X
that satisfies the above properties, and it does define a
\Gamma
-compactification. We refer to this class of
\Gamma
-compactifications as
orbital
compactifications, and if
\nu=\delta_{x_{0}}
is a Dirac measure at some point
x_{0}\in X
, we call it a
point-orbital
compactification.

Examples
3.10
and
3.11
show that not all
\Gamma
-compactifications are orbital, however, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\overline{\Gamma}
be a
\Gamma
-compactification with minimal boundary
\partial\overline{\Gamma}
. Then there exists a retraction
\overline{\Gamma}\to\partial\overline{\Gamma}
if and only if
\overline{\Gamma}
is point-orbital.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\Gamma
denote a countable discrete group. We denote by
\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
the algebra of all complex-valued bounded functions from
\Gamma
and by

c_{0}(\Gamma)
the ideal of all functions “vanishing at infinity”, namely, the space of all those functions
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
, the set
\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\left|f\left(\gamma\right)\right|>\varepsilon\}
is finite.

An action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
of
\Gamma
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
by homeomorphisms
induces further canonical actions of
\Gamma
. First, the action on the compact convex space
{\rm Prob}(X)
of all Borel probability measures on
X
by affine homeomorphisms given by

(g\cdot\nu)(E)=\nu(g^{-1}E)
for
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
,
g\in\Gamma
.
Second the action on
C(X)
by *-automorphisms, defined by

(g\cdot f)(x)=f(gx)
for
g\in\Gamma
,
f\in C(X)
.

Given a measure
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
, the corresponding
Poisson transform

P_{\nu}\colon C(X)\longrightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
is defined by
P_{\nu}(f)(\gamma)=\int_{X}f\,d\gamma\nu
.
The transform
P_{\nu}
is unital, positive and
\Gamma
-equivariant.
Conversely, every unital positive
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\varphi\colon C\left(X\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
is a Poisson transform, namely,
\varphi=P_{\nu}
for
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
which is defined by
\nu(f)=[\varphi(f)](e)
for every
f\in C\left(X\right)
.

The Poisson transform is multiplicative (hence a *-homomorphism) if and only if
\nu
is a Dirac measure.

By Gelfand duality, a unital
\Gamma
-invariant
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
of
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
is of the form
C(Z)
for some compact
\Gamma
-space
Z
. Furthermore, if
c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)\subset\mathcal{A}
, then
\text{spec}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
contains a copy of
\Gamma
.

2.1
Group compactifications

As mentioned in the introduction, in this paper, we restrict our attention to those group compactifications whose boundary is minimal as a
\Gamma
-space.

Definition 2.1

(
\Gamma
-compactification)
.

Let
X
be a compact minimal
\Gamma
-space.
A topology
\tau
on the set
\Gamma\cup X
is called a
\Gamma
-compactification
if

1.

the space
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau)
is compact and Hausdorff,
\Gamma
is dense in
\Gamma\cup X
, and the induced topologies
\tau|_{\Gamma}
and
\tau|_{X}
are the original topologies on
\Gamma
and
X
respectively,

2.

the actions
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma
(by left multiplication) and
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
together define a continuous action of
\Gamma
on the union
\Gamma\cup X
.

Our main focus will be on the case where
X
is metrizable, and for the rest of the paper,
unless otherwise stated,
X
is assumed to be metrizable
. Note that in this case, any compactification
\Gamma\cup X
is also metrizable since by countability of
\Gamma
, one can choose a countable separating family of continuous functions on
\Gamma\cup X
.
Definition
2.1
is, however, valid for general compact
\Gamma
-spaces
X
, and indeed there are important examples of
\Gamma
-compactifications in which
X
is non-metrizable (e.g. the Furstenberg boundary compactification).
The restriction to the metrizable case simplifies our constructions, however, all our results hold with modified arguments, replacing sequences with nets.

When a compact metrizable minimal
\Gamma
-space
X
is given, in order to define a concrete topology
\tau
on the disjoint union
\Gamma\cup X
that turns it into a

\Gamma
-compactification, our task, informally speaking,

is to describe the “gluing of
\Gamma
to
X
” in a “legal” way: declaring which sequences in
\Gamma
converge to which points in
X
in a way that
(i) every sequence has a convergent subsequence (compactness);
(ii) if a sequence converges to some
x\in X
, then so do all of its subsequences (well-definedness); (iii) if
\gamma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x
, then for every
g\in\Gamma
,
g\gamma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}gx
(continuity of the
\Gamma
-action); and (iv)
for every
x\in X
, there exists a sequence in
\Gamma
which converges to it (density).

We present several such constructions in later sections.

We often work in the dual setup of function algebras associated to
\Gamma
-actions and their compactifications.
Let us record some basic facts that we will use later.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
be a
\Gamma
-compactification, and let
f\in C\left(\Gamma\cup X\right)
. Then
f|_{X}\equiv 0
if and only if
f|_{\Gamma}\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
.

Proof.

If
f|_{\Gamma}\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
, then
f|_{X}\equiv 0
by density of
\Gamma
in
\Gamma\cup X
, as for
x\in X
, there exists an injective sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma
of which
x
is an accumulation point. This sequence must satisfy that
f\left(\gamma_{n}\right)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
by virtue of
f|_{\Gamma}
being in
c_{0}(\Gamma)
, but then necessarily
f(x)=0
by continuity of
f
.

Conversely, suppose that
f|_{X}\equiv 0
, and for the sake of contradiction, suppose that
f|_{\Gamma}\notin c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
and an injective sequence
\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n}
in
\Gamma
such that
\left|f\left(\gamma_{n}\right)\right|>\varepsilon
for every
n
. By compactness,
(\gamma_{n})
must have a convergent subsequence
\left(\gamma_{n_{k}}\right)_{k}
, and by injectivity of
\left(\gamma_{n}\right)
and the fact that
\Gamma
is discrete,
\left(\gamma_{n_{k}}\right)_{k}
converges to some
x\in X
. But then, by continuity of
f
,
f\left(\gamma_{n_{k}}\right)\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}f\left(x\right)=0
in contradiction with that
\left|f\left(\gamma_{n_{k}}\right)\right|>\varepsilon
for all
k
.
∎

Denote by
i\colon c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)\to C\left(\Gamma\cup X\right)
the inclusion defined by
i\left(f\right)|_{\Gamma}=f
and
i\left(f\right)|_{X}\equiv 0
.
Note that
i(f)
is indeed in
C(\Gamma\cup X)
as
\Gamma
is locally compact and hence open in any of its compactifications. Also denote by
\pi\colon C\left(\Gamma\cup X\right)\to C\left(X\right)
the restriction
\pi\left(f\right)=f|_{X}
.
Then we get that a
\Gamma
-compactification gives rise to a short exact sequence of commutative
\Gamma
-
C^{*}
-algebras as follows:

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
\Gamma
-compactification
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
, there exists a (canonical)
\Gamma
-invariant
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}\leq\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
such that
{\mathcal{A}_{\tau}}\cong C\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
.

Proof.

Consider the restriction
*
-homomorphism
r\colon C\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
,
f\mapsto f|_{\Gamma}
,
and let
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}:=\text{Im}\left(r\right)
. The fact that
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
is clear, and the fact that
r
is an isomorphism to its image follows from the fact that
\Gamma
is dense in
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
and hence
r
must be injective.

3
Orbital compactifications

Our main interest in this paper is a class of
\Gamma
-compactifications whose topology is defined by the convergence along orbits of measures on the boundary of the compactification. This construction was presented by Furstenberg in
[
Fur71
]
in the context of boundary actions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a compact metrizable
\Gamma
-space, and let
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
. Then there is a unique topology
\tau_{\nu}
on the union
\Gamma\cup X
satisfying that

(i)

the canonical inclusions of
\Gamma
and
X
into
\Gamma\cup X
are homeomorphisms onto their images, and

(ii)

for every sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma
which leaves every finite set of
\Gamma
and every
x\in X
,
\gamma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x
if and only if
\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\delta_{x}
weak*.

Proof.

We define the topology
\tau_{\nu}
through the closed sets. We declare that
S\subset\Gamma\cup X
is closed if and only if

A.

S\cap\Gamma
and
S\cap X
are closed in
\Gamma
and in
X
respectively, and

B.

whenever a sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
S\cap\Gamma
leaves every finite set of
\Gamma
, the set
S\cap X
contains

\{x\in X:\delta_{x}\text{ is a weak* accumulation point of the sequence }(\gamma_{n}\nu)\}.

The collection of sets
S
which satisfy these two conditions is clearly closed under arbitrary intersections, and contains
\emptyset
and
\Gamma\cup X
. To see that it is closed under finite unions, let
S_{1},S_{2}
be two sets that satisfy our two conditions. Then for
S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, condition A holds evidently. To see that B holds, let
(\gamma_{n})
be a sequence in
(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\cap\Gamma
which leaves every finite set of
\Gamma
and such that
\delta_{x}
is an accumulation point of
(\gamma_{n}\nu)
for some
x\in X
. Without loss of generality, suppose that infinitely many elements in
(\gamma_{n})
are from
S_{1}
, and so form a subsequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})_{k}
in
S_{1}\cap\Gamma
such that
\delta_{x}
is an accumulation point of
\left(\gamma_{n_{k}}\nu\right)_{k}
, and we conclude
x\in S_{1}
and hence
x\in S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. It follows that the topology
\tau_{\nu}
is well defined.

To show uniqueness, let
\tau
be a topology on
\Gamma\cup X
which satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) in the statement. Let
S
be a set closed with respect to
\tau
. It is straightforward to show that it must satisfy conditions A and B and therefore it must be closed in
\tau_{\nu}
.
Conversely, assume
S
is a closed set of
\tau_{\nu}
, and let
(s_{n})
be an injective sequence in
S
with
s_{n}\to y\in(\Gamma\cup X,\tau)
. If
(s_{n})
has a subsequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma
, then by (i) we have
y\in X
, and by (ii) we have
\gamma_{n}\nu\to\delta_{y}
weak*. Since
S
is closed in
\tau_{\nu}
, condition B implies
y\in S
.
If
(s_{n})
has a subsequence
(x_{n})
in
X
, then by (i),
y\in X
and
x_{n}\to y
in
X
. By condition A,
S\cap X
is closed in
X
, which implies
y\in X
. Hence, it follows that
S
is closed in
\tau
.
∎

A useful feature of the topology
\tau_{\nu}
defined above is the following canonical extension property.

Lemma 3.2
.

In the setting of Lemma
3.1
, given
f\in C(X)
, the function
\tilde{f}\colon\Gamma\cup X\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
\tilde{f}|_{X}=f
and
\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f
is a continuous extension of
f
.

Proof.

Suppose
\left(\gamma_{n}\right)
is a sequence in
\Gamma
and
\gamma_{n}\longrightarrow x\in X
. Then by the definition of
\tau_{\nu}
, we have
\gamma_{n}\nu\longrightarrow\delta_{x}
and hence
\tilde{f}\left(\gamma_{n}\right)=\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\longrightarrow f(x)=\tilde{f}\left(x\right)
.
∎

Note however, in general,

\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu}\right)
is not a compactification (not even necessarily compact). For instance, if
\nu
is
\Gamma
-invariant and non-atomic, then
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu}\right)
is just the disjoint union of the topological spaces
\Gamma
and
X
.

Our next goal is to characterize
\nu\in\text{Prob}(X)
for which
\tau_{\nu}
is a
\Gamma
-compactification.
Recall the following definition (see, for example, Section I-3 in
[
Aze06
]
or Section V.2 in
[
Gla76
]
). A measure
\nu
on
X
is said to be
contractible
if for every
x\in X
,
\delta_{x}
is in the weak* closure of the
\Gamma
-orbit of
\nu
, or equivalently, if the Poisson transform
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}
is an isometry.

Lemma 3.3
.

\Gamma
is dense in
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu})
if and only if the Poisson transform
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\colon C\left(X\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
is an isometry.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Gamma
is dense in
\Gamma\cup X
. Since any Poisson transform is always a contraction, we only need to show that

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f\|_{\infty}
for every
f\in C\left(X\right)
. For this, let
f\in C\left(X\right)
and choose
x_{0}\in X
such that
\|f\|_{\infty}=f(x_{0})
. By the assumption,
there exists a sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x_{0}
. This means, by the definition of
\tau_{\nu}
, that
\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\delta_{x_{0}}
, and therefore,

\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f\left(\gamma_{n}\right)=\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{X}f\,d\delta_{x_{0}}=f\left(x_{0}\right)=\|f\|_{\infty},

which implies
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f\|_{\infty}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}
is an isometry, and for the sake of contradiction, suppose that

\Gamma
is not dense in
\Gamma\cup X
. Let
x_{0}\in X
be such that
x_{0}\notin\overline{\Gamma}^{\,\tau_{\nu}}
and choose
f\in C(X)
such that
0\leq f(x)\lneq f(x_{0})
for all
x\in X\setminus\{x_{0}\}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}
is an isometry, there exists a sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma
such that
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f(\gamma_{n})=\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\|f\|_{\infty}=f(x_{0})
. Since
X
is compact and metrizable, it is in particular separable, so
\text{Prob}\left(X\right)
is not only compact but also metrizable, and we may assume without loss of generality that
(\gamma_{n}\nu)
converges to some
\nu_{0}\in\text{Prob}\left(X\right)
. Hence
\int_{X}f\,d\nu_{0}=f(x_{0})
, which by the choice of
f
implies that
\nu_{0}=\delta_{x_{0}}
. That is,
\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\delta_{x_{0}}
, which means
\gamma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}{x_{0}}
in
\tau_{\nu}
, a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Denote by
\pi\colon\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)/c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
the canonical quotient map.
The space
\Gamma\cup X
with the topology
\tau_{\nu}
is compact if and only if
the composition
\varphi:=\pi\circ\mathcal{P}_{\nu}:C\left(X\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)/c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
is a
*
-homomorphism.

Proof.

\implies
Assume that
\Gamma\cup X
is compact; and we will show
\varphi
is a
*
-homomorphism. Since both the maps
\pi
and
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}
are
*
-linear, the same holds for
\varphi
, so it only remains to show that
\varphi
is multiplicative.
For this, let
f,g\in C\left(X\right)
and towards contradiction suppose that
\varphi\left(fg\right)\neq\varphi\left(f\right)\varphi\left(g\right)
, that is,
\big(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}fg-\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f\,\mathcal{P}_{\nu}g\big)\notin c_{0}(\Gamma)
.
Thus,
there exists some
\varepsilon>0
and an injective sequence
(\gamma_{n})
, such that

\left|\int_{X}fg\,d\gamma_{n}\nu-\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\int_{X}g\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\right|>\varepsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By compactness of
\Gamma\cup X
, we may assume
\gamma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x_{0}
for some
x_{0}\in X
, that is,
\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\delta_{x_{0}}
weak*. Then

\lim_{n}\int_{X}fg\,d\gamma_{n}\nu=fg(x_{0})=f(x_{0})g(x_{0})=\lim_{n}\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\int_{X}g\,d\gamma_{n}\nu,

a contradiction.

\impliedby
Conversely, assume
\varphi
is multiplicative.
Since
X
is compact, it is enough to show that every sequence in
\Gamma
has a convergent subsequence.
Let
(\gamma_{n})
be an injective sequence in
\Gamma
and
for every
f,g\in C\left(X\right)
, we have

0=\lim_{n}\left(P_{\nu}fg-P_{\nu}fP_{\nu}g\right)(\gamma_{n})=\lim_{n}\left(\int_{X}fg\,d\gamma_{n}\nu-\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\int_{X}g\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\right).

Since
\text{Prob}\left(X\right)
is compact and metrizable,
(\gamma_{n})
has a subsequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})
such that
\gamma_{n_{k}}\nu\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\nu_{0}
for some
\nu_{0}\in\text{Prob}\left(X\right)
.
It follows that

\int_{X}fg\,d\nu_{0}-\int_{X}f\,d\nu_{0}\int_{X}g\,d\nu_{0}=0

for all
f,g\in C\left(X\right)
, which implies
\nu_{0}=\delta_{x_{0}}
for some
x_{0}\in X
.
Hence, by the definition of
\tau_{\nu}
,
\gamma_{n_{k}}\longrightarrow x_{0}\in X
, which completes the proof.
∎

Combining Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we get the following characterization of measures
\nu\in\text{Prob}(X)
for which
\tau_{\nu}
is a
\Gamma
-compactification.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be a compact metrizable
\Gamma
-space, and let
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
. Then
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu}\right)
is a
\Gamma
-compactification if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(i)

the Poisson transform
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\colon C\left(X\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
is an isometry, and

(ii)

the composition
\pi\circ\mathcal{P}_{\nu}:C\left(X\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)/c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
is a
*
-homomorphism, where
\pi\colon\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)\to\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)/c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
is the canonical quotient map.

In this case, we say
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu}\right)
is an orbital compactification (defined by
\nu
).

The above two conditions in the statement of Theorem
3.5
obviously hold for Dirac measures.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
X
be a compact metrizable
\Gamma
-space,
x_{0}\in X
and let
\nu=\delta_{x_{0}}
. Then
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu}\right)
is a
\Gamma
-compactification, which we call the point-orbital compactification (defined by the point
x_{0}
).

We next give a characterization of orbital compactifications in terms of their corresponding function algebras; recall from Proposition
2.3
that given a
\Gamma
-compactification
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
, we define
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}:=\{f|_{\Gamma}:f\in C\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)\}\subset\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
be a
\Gamma
-compactification. Then
\tau=\tau_{\nu}
is orbital for some
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
if and only if
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}={\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\right)\oplus c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
.

Proof.

Assume
\tau=\tau_{\nu}
for some
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
. Suppose
f\in C(X)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}f\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Let
\tilde{f}\colon\Gamma\cup X\to\mathbb{C}
be the continuous extension as in Lemma
3.2
. Since
\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\left(f\right)\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
it follows by Proposition
2.2
that
f=\tilde{f}|_{X}=0
.
We conclude
{\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\right)\cap c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)=\{0\}
.

Next, let
f\in C(X)
and
\phi\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
, we show
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}(f)+\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
.
Let
\tilde{f}\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
be the extension of
f
as in Lemma
3.2
, and also extend
\phi
to the function
\tilde{\phi}\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
by defining
\tilde{\phi}|_{X}=0
.
Then,
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}(f)+\phi=(\tilde{f}+\tilde{\phi})|_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
.

Conversely,
let
\tilde{f}\in C\left(\Gamma\cup X\right)
and we show

\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}-\mathcal{P}_{\nu}(\tilde{f}|_{X})\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
,
which then implies
\tilde{f}\in{\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\right)\oplus c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
.
For the sake of contradiction, assume otherwise, that is,
there exist
\varepsilon>0
and an injective sequence
(\gamma_{n})\subset\Gamma
, such that

\left|\tilde{f}\left(\gamma_{n}\right)-\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\left(\tilde{f}|_{X}\right)\left(\gamma_{n}\right)\right|>\varepsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By compactness of
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{\nu})
, we may further assume that
\gamma_{n}\to x\in X
, that is,
\gamma_{n}\nu\to\delta_{x}
weak*.
Then, by continuity,
\tilde{f}\left(\gamma_{n}\right)\to\tilde{f}\left(x\right)
, and

\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\left(\tilde{f}|_{X}\right)\left(\gamma_{n}\right)=\int_{X}\tilde{f}|_{X}\,d\gamma_{n}\nu\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\tilde{f}\left(x\right).

Combining the above, we get a contradiction. This completes the proof of the forward implication of the first assertion.

For the converse implication, assume
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
is a
\Gamma
-compactification such that
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}={\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\right)\oplus c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
for some
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(X)
.
Let
\tilde{f}\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
. By the assumption,
\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{P}_{\nu}(\tilde{f}_{1})+\phi
, where
\tilde{f}_{1}\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
and
\phi\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
. Extend
\phi
to
\tilde{\phi}\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
by setting
\tilde{\phi}|_{X}=0
. Then

\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=\tilde{f}_{1}|_{\Gamma}+\tilde{\phi}|_{\Gamma}
, and so by the density of
\Gamma
, we get
\tilde{f}=\tilde{f}_{1}+\tilde{\phi}
.

Let
(\gamma_{n})
be an injective sequence in
\Gamma
. Then
\tilde{\phi}(\gamma_{n})\longrightarrow 0
, and therefore if
(\gamma_{n})
converges in
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
, then

\lim_{n}\tilde{f}(\gamma_{n})=\lim_{n}\tilde{f}_{1}(\gamma_{n})=\lim_{n}\mathcal{P}_{\nu}(\tilde{f}_{1})(\gamma_{n})=\lim_{n}\int_{X}f\,d\gamma_{n}\nu.

Since this holds for every
f\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
, it follows
\gamma_{n}\longrightarrow x\in X
if and only if
\gamma_{n}\nu\longrightarrow\delta_{x}
weak*. This implies
\tau=\tau_{\nu}
.
∎

3.1
Examples

We proceed with several examples of
\Gamma
-compactifications. First, we provide two elementary and natural examples of orbital
\Gamma
-compactifications, one of which is point-orbital, and the other not.

Example 3.8
.

Consider the lamplighter group on
\mathbb{Z}
,

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\ltimes\oplus_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}\cong\left<a,t\mid a^{2},\left(at^{n}at^{-n}\right)^{2}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\right>,

acting on
X:=\prod_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, the space of infinite lamp configurations, equipped with the standard product topology, that makes it a compact space.
The action is defined as follows. For an element
\left(p,C\right)\in\Gamma
(where
p
is the position of the lamplighter and
C
is a finite lamps configuration), and a point
\bar{C}\in X
(a finite or infinite configuration), the the resulted element
\left(p,C\right).\bar{C}
is defined by translating
\bar{C}
by
p
and then summing (xoring, if one prefers) with
C
.

Since every element
\gamma=\left(p,C\right)\in\Gamma
possesses a finite configuration, we can use that to construct a
\Gamma
-compactification by declaring

\gamma_{n}=\left(p_{n},C_{n}\right)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\bar{C}\iff C_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\bar{C}\text{ (in X)}.

Now, one can easily verify that this is indeed a
\Gamma
-compactification, and it is the point-orbital compactification through the empty configuration
\left(...,0,0,0,0,...\right)\in X
. One can easily check manually that the compactification is indeed point-orbital, or by using Theorem
1.2
(consider the retraction that fixes the boundary and “forgets” the pointer on the group, i.e.,
\left(p,C\right)\mapsto C
).

Example 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the free group on two generators, let
\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
be its Gromov boundary, and denote by
\tau_{G}
the usual topology on
\mathbb{F}_{2}\cup\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
of the Gromov-compactification (see
[
Gro87
]
).
Then
\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\cup\partial\mathbb{F}_{2},\tau_{G}\right)
is not a point-orbital compactification.
To see this, suppose on the contrary that
\tau_{G}=\tau_{x_{0}}
is a point-orbital compactification for some
x_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We split the proof into cases and show a contradiction in each of them.
First, consider the case that
x_{0}
has eventually only one letter from
\left\{a^{\pm 1},b^{\pm 1}\right\}
, that is
x_{0}=w_{0}\alpha^{\infty}
, for some finite word
w_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
and letter
\alpha\in\left\{a^{\pm 1},b^{\pm 1}\right\}
. Assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}=w_{0}a^{\infty}
. In this case, the contradiction comes from the sequence
(w_{n})
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
given by
w_{n}:=w_{0}a^{-n}
for every
n
. It is clear that we should have that
w_{n}\to w_{0}a^{-\infty}
in
\tau_{G}
. But
w_{0}a^{-n}a^{\infty}=w_{0}a^{\infty}
, hence
w_{n}x_{0}=w_{0}a^{-n}a^{\infty}\to w_{0}a^{\infty}\neq w_{0}a^{-\infty}
.

Now, assume that
\tau_{G}=\tau_{x_{0}}
, a point-orbital compactification such that
x_{0}
does not eventually contain only one letter. Assume, without loss of generality, that the letter
a
appears infinitely many times in
x_{0}
. Consider the sequence
(w_{n})
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
which is defined by taking the inverse of the prefix of
x_{0}
that goes up to right before the
n
-th power of
a
in
x_{0}
. For instance, if

x_{0}=a^{2}b^{-1}a^{-3}b^{5}aba^{7}...,

then

\begin{split}w_{1}&=e\ \text{(the empty word)}\\
w_{2}&=ba^{-2}\\
w_{3}&=b^{-5}a^{3}ba^{-2}\\
w_{4}&=b^{-1}a^{-1}b^{-5}a^{3}ba^{-2}\\
&\ \ \vdots\end{split}

For every letter
\alpha\in\left\{a^{\pm 1},b^{\pm 1}\right\}
, denote by
\left[\alpha\right]\subset\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
the closed set of infinite words which start with
\alpha
. By construction, the first letter of
w_{n}
is
b
or
b^{-1}
for every
n\geq 2
. Hence, every partial limit of
(w_{n})
must lie in
\left[b\right]\cup\left[b^{-1}\right]
. However,
w_{n}x_{0}\in\left[a\right]\cup\left[a^{-1}\right]
and hence, every partial limit of
(w_{n}x_{0})
is in
\left[a\right]\cup\left[a^{-1}\right]
. Nevertheless, we supposed that
\tau_{G}=\tau_{x_{0}}
, and therefore a subsequence
\left(w_{n_{k}}\right)_{k}
would converge to some
x\in\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
if and only if
w_{n_{k}}x_{0}\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x
, in contradiction with the fact that
(w_{n})
and
\left(w_{n}x_{0}\right)
do not share any partial limit.
This proves the claim.

In Theorem
3.9
we extend the above “hands-on” argument to a more general framework to verify that the Gromov compactifications of hyperbolic groups are not point-orbital.

Non-orbital
\Gamma
-compactifications

At this point, it is important to emphasize that not every
\Gamma
-compactification is orbital.

Example 3.10
.

Consider
\mathbb{Z}
acting on the space
X=\left\{a,b\right\}
of two points transitively (
1.a=b
). One can easily check that the following conditions give rise to a unique
\mathbb{Z}
-compactification,
\tau
,

•

2,4,6,8,...\longrightarrow a

•

-2,-4,-6,-8,...\longrightarrow b

•

1,3,5,7,...\longrightarrow b

•

-1,-3,-5,-7,...\longrightarrow a

Then
\tau
is not point-orbital, since if, for instance,
\tau=\tau_{a}
, we know that the even numbers act trivially, but then

\lim_{n\to\infty}-2n=b\neq a=\lim_{n\to\infty}-2n.a.

Hence, the compactification here is not orbital as well.

Example 3.11
.

Consider the infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}=\left<\rho,\sigma\right>
acting on
\mathbb{Z}
where
\rho
is the right shift and
\sigma
is the reflection. Consider its action on the two-point space
\left\{a,b\right\}
, in which
\rho
acts trivially and
\sigma
permutes
a
and
b
. To obtain a
D_{\infty}
-compactification here, we define a topology
\tau
as follows. Consider the action of
D_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{Z}
described above. We say that a sequence
\left(\gamma_{n}\right)
in
D_{\infty}
converges to

if
\gamma_{n}.0\longrightarrow\infty
and converges to
b
if
\gamma_{n}.0\longrightarrow-\infty
. If
\tau=\tau_{a}
, then since
\rho
fixes both

and

, we would get that

converges to

. Similarly, if

4
No-retraction theorems

Now we restate and prove Theorem
1.2
from the introduction.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a metrizable
\Gamma
-space and
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau)
be a
\Gamma
-compactification. Then there exists a
\Gamma
-equivariant retraction
\varphi\colon\Gamma\cup X\to X
if and only if
\tau=\tau_{x_{0}}
is point-orbital for some
x_{0}\in X
.
In this case, we have
x_{0}=\varphi\left(e\right)
.

Proof.

Assume
\tau=\tau_{x_{0}}
for some
x_{0}\in X
. Define
\varphi:\Gamma\cup X\to X
by
\varphi(\gamma)=\gamma x_{0}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and
\varphi(x)=x
for all
x\in X
. Then
\varphi
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant map with
\varphi|_{X}=id_{X}
. We show it is continuous. For this, let
(\gamma_{n})
be a sequence in
\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{n}\to x\in X
. By the definition of the topology
\tau_{x_{0}}
, this means
\gamma_{n}x_{0}\to x
, and so
\varphi(\gamma_{n})=\gamma_{n}x_{0}\to x=\varphi(x)
. Hence,
\varphi
is continuous.

Conversely, assume
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau)
is a
\Gamma
-compactification such that there exists a
\Gamma
-equivariant retraction
\varphi\colon\Gamma\cup X\to X
.
We show
C\left(\Gamma\cup X\right)|_{\Gamma}=c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)\oplus\text{Im}\big(\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}\big)
, and then apply Proposition
3.7
to conclude the result.

Let
\tilde{f}\in C\left(\Gamma\cup X\right)
, and denote
f=\tilde{f}|_{X}
. Then, for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}f\left(\gamma\right)=f\left(\gamma\varphi\left(e\right)\right)=f\circ\varphi\left(\gamma\right),

and so
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}(\tilde{f}|_{X})=(\tilde{f}|_{X}\circ\varphi)|_{\Gamma}
.

Next, we show
(\tilde{f}-(f\circ\varphi))|_{\Gamma}\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Suppose for contradiction that this is not so, that is, that there exists an
\varepsilon>0
and an injective sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n}
in
\Gamma
such that

\left|\tilde{f}\left(\gamma_{n}\right)-\tilde{f}\left(\varphi(\gamma_{n}\right))\right|\geq\varepsilon

(2)

for all
n
.
By compactness, we may also assume without loss of generality that
\gamma_{n}\longrightarrow x\in X
. Since
\varphi
is a retraction, we get

0=\tilde{f}\left(x\right)-\psi(\tilde{f}|_{X})\left(x\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f\left(\gamma_{n}\right)-\psi(\tilde{f}|_{X})\left(\gamma_{n}\right),

in contradiction.
Thus,

\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=\big(\tilde{f}-(f\circ\varphi)\big)|_{\Gamma}+(f\circ\varphi)|_{\Gamma}=\big(\tilde{f}-(f\circ\varphi)\big)|_{\Gamma}+\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}f\in c_{0}(\Gamma)+{\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}\right),

which shows
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}\subset c_{0}(\Gamma)+{\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}\right)
.

Conversely, let
f\in C(X)
and
\phi\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
. Let
\tilde{f}=f\circ\varphi\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
and let
\tilde{\phi}\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
be the continuous extension of
\phi
by
\tilde{\phi}|_{X}=0
. Then,

f+\phi=(\tilde{f}+\tilde{\phi})|_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{A}_{\tau}.

Hence,
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}=c_{0}(\Gamma)+{\rm Im}(\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}})
.

It remains to show
c_{0}(\Gamma)\cap{\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}\right)=\{0\}
. For this, let
f\in C(X)
be such that
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}f\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
. Then, as shown above, it follows
f\circ\varphi=\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\varphi\left(e\right)}}f\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
. Since
f\in c_{0}(\Gamma)\in C(\Gamma\cup X)
and
\Gamma
is dense in
\Gamma\cup X
, and it follows
(f\circ\varphi)|_{X}=0
, and since
\varphi
is a retraction,
f=(f\circ\varphi)|_{X}=0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

In fact, all orbital group compactifications satisfy a “quasi” version of equivariant no-retraction theorem as follows; the proof is similar to Theorem
4.1
, which we will omit.

Theorem 4.2
.

A
\Gamma
-compactification
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau)
is orbital if and only if there exists a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\varphi:\Gamma\cup X\to{\rm Prob}(X)
such that
\varphi(x)=\delta_{x}
for all
x\in X
.

With the complete characterization given by Theorem
4.1
in hand, we can now prove the equivariant analogue of Brouwer’s No-Retraction theorem for several classes of group compactifications.
We begin with the important case of Gromov compactification of hyperbolic groups.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated hyperbolic group and consider its Gromov-compactification
\left(\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma,\tau_{G}\right)
. Then
\tau_{G}
is an orbital, but not point-orbital compactification.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in{\rm Prob}(\partial\Gamma)
be non-atomic. By
[
Kai00
, Lemma 2.2 & Proposition 7.5]
, if
(\gamma_{n})
is a sequence in
\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{n}\to x\in\partial\Gamma
in
\tau_{G}
, then
\gamma_{n}\nu\to\delta_{x}
weak*. Since both
\tau_{G}
and
\tau_{\nu}
are compact topologies, it follows that
\tau_{G}=\tau_{\nu}
, and hence
\tau_{G}
is orbital.

To prove that
\tau_{G}
is not point-orbital, assume for the sake of contradiction, that
\tau_{G}=\tau_{x_{0}}
for some
x_{0}\in X
.
Let
\omega\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\Gamma
be a geodesic with
\gamma_{0}:=\omega(0)=e
and such that
\gamma_{n}:=\omega(n)\to x_{0}
. For every
m<n\in\mathbb{N}
, since
e
lies in a geodesic segment
[\gamma_{m}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m}^{-1}\gamma_{n}]
, the Gromov product
\langle\,\gamma_{m}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m}^{-1}\gamma_{n}\,\rangle_{e}=0
, and therefore
\langle\,\gamma_{m}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m}^{-1}x_{0}\,\rangle_{e}=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\left(\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}\right)_{j}
be a subsequence that converges to infinity, say
\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}\to z\in\partial\Gamma
. Then we also have
\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}x_{0}\to z
, and so
\exists N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\langle\,\gamma_{m_{i}}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}\,\rangle_{e}>(2\delta+1)
and
\langle\,z\,,\,\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}x_{0}\,\rangle_{e}>(2\delta+1)

for all
i,j\geq N
, where
\delta
is the hyperbolicity constant of
\Gamma
. Fix
j\geq N
, and the second inequality further implies that
\exists M\geq N
such that
\langle\,\gamma_{m_{\ell}}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}\gamma_{n}\,\rangle_{e}\geq(2\delta+1)
for all
\ell,n\geq M
.
However, then for
M<m_{i}<n
,
by
\delta
-hyperbolicity we have

0=\langle\,\gamma_{m_{i}}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m_{i}}^{-1}\gamma_{n}\,\rangle_{e}\geq\min\{\langle\,\gamma_{m_{i}}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}\,\rangle_{e}\,,\,\langle\,\gamma_{m_{j}}^{-1}\,,\,\gamma_{m_{i}}^{-1}\gamma_{n}\,\rangle_{e}\}-2\delta\geq 1,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated hyperbolic group and consider its Gromov-compactification
\left(\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma,\tau_{G}\right)
. Then there exists no
\Gamma
-equivariant retraction from the compactification
\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma
onto its boundary
\partial\Gamma
.

4.1
Projective compactifications

Recall from
[
Kai00
]
that a
\Gamma
-compactification
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau)
is said to be
projective
if the right action
\Gamma\curvearrowleft\Gamma
extends to the trivial action of
\Gamma
on
X
, that is, if
(\gamma_{n})
is a sequence in
\Gamma
and
\gamma_{n}\to x\in X
, then
\gamma_{n}\gamma\to x\in X
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Gromov compactifications of hyperbolic groups are examples of projective compactifications. Although it is not completely evident in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we believe that projectivity is the main obstacle for point-orbitality.
In particular, we conjecture that the equivariant no-retraction theorem holds for all projective group compactifications.

The remainder of this section is devoted to some facts concerning this class of compactifications.

Recall that an action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
of
\Gamma
on a compact metric space
(X,d)
is called
distal
if for every pair of distinct points
x,y\in X
,
\inf\{d(\gamma x,\gamma y):\gamma\in\Gamma\}>0
.

Theorem 4.5
.

A projective group compactification with non-trivial distal boundary is not point-orbital.

Proof.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there is a non-trivial distal minimal compact
\Gamma
-space
X
and a point
x_{0}\in X
such that
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{x_{0}}\right)
is projective.
Let
\left(\gamma_{n}\right)
be a sequence in
\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{n}x_{0}\to x\in X
. Then, by projectivity of
\tau_{x_{0}}
, we also get
\gamma_{n}\gamma x_{0}\to x
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is distal, it follows
\gamma x_{0}=x_{0}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. By minimality, it follows that
X
is trivial, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
be a projective
\Gamma
-compactification such that
X
is a non-trivial distal
\Gamma
-space. Then there is no
\Gamma
-equivariant retraction from
\Gamma\cup X
onto
X
.

In fact, projectivity, together with the point-orbitality of a group compactification, imposes topological restrictions on its boundary, as shown below.

Proposition 4.7
.

The boundary of any projective point-orbital
\Gamma
-compactification is either connected or has infinitely many connected components.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, assume that
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{x_{0}})
is projective for some
x_{0}\in X
, and
X
is disconnected, with finitely many connected components. Thus, there exists a proper clopen connected component
K\subset X
with
x_{0}\in K
. Define
A:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma\,:\,\gamma x_{0}\in K\}
.
Let
g\in\Gamma
. Since
K
is clopen, it follows that if
Ag\setminus A
is infinite, then there is a sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
A
that converges in
K
, but
(\gamma_{n}g)
converges in
X\setminus K
, which contradicts the projectivity. Thus,
A
is right-invariant up to finite sets, that is, for every
g\in\Gamma
, the set
Ag\setminus A
is finite. Moreover, for every
\gamma\in A
, since
x_{0}\in K\cap\gamma^{-1}K
, it follows
\gamma^{-1}K\subset K
, and so
\gamma^{-1}\in A
. Therefore,
A
is symmetric, hence also is left-invariant up to finite sets. By minimality, the set
A
is infinite, and there exists an injective sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
A
such that
\gamma_{n}x_{0}\to x_{0}
, so
\gamma_{n}\to x_{0}
in
\tau_{x_{0}}
.
Let
g\in A^{c}
. Then
g\gamma_{n}\to gx_{0}
, which implies that for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\gamma_{n}\notin A
. This contradicts left-invarinace of
A
by finite sets, and completes the proof.
∎

We have further topological restriction on the boundary in the case of 1-ended groups.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated
1
-ended group. Then the boundary of any projective point-orbital
\Gamma
-compactification must be connected.

Proof.

Suppose
(\Gamma\cup X,\tau_{x_{0}})
is projective for some
x_{0}\in X
, where
X
is disconnected.
Then similarly as in the proof of Proposition
4.7
,
\Gamma
has a subset
A
that is right-invariant up to finite sets, and both
A
and
X\setminus A
, its complement in
X
, are infinite.
The existence of such subset
A\subset\Gamma
implies that
\Gamma
has more than one end.
∎

We observe below that, as one would expect, projective group compactifications of 2-ended groups have finite boundary.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
\Gamma
contains an infinite subgroup whose centralizer has finite index in
\Gamma
(e.g.
\Gamma
is
2
-ended), then every projective
\Gamma
-compactification has finite boundary.
Consequently, the equivariant no-retraction theorem holds for any such compactification with non-trivial boundary.

Proof.

Let
H\leq\Gamma
be an infinite subgroup and assume that its centralizer,
C_{\Gamma}\left(H\right)
, is of finite index in
\Gamma
. Suppose that
\left(\Gamma\cup X,\tau\right)
is a projective
\Gamma
-compactification. Let
x\in X
be a limit point of
H
, that is,
h_{n}\to x
for some sequence
(h_{n})
in
H
.
For every
\gamma\in C_{\Gamma}\left(H\right)
, by projectivity,

x=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{h_{n}}\gamma=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma{h_{n}}=\gamma\lim_{n\to\infty}{h_{n}}=\gamma x,

which shows that
x
is fixed by
C_{\Gamma}\left(H\right)
. Since
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is minimal and
C_{\Gamma}(H)
has finite index in
\Gamma
, it follows that
X
is finite.

In particular, since every finite
\Gamma
-space is distal, it follows from Theorem
4.5
that if
X
is non-trivial, then there is no
\Gamma
-equivariant retraction from
\Gamma\cup X
onto
X
.
∎

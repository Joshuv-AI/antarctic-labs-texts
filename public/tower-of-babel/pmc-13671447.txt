TOMM5 is associated with oral cancer progression: a multi-omics analysis from single-cell atlas to in vitro validation
Authors: Li Gao, Jian-Di Li, Yi-Yang Chen, Ge-Li Tan, Jian-Jia Zeng, Xi Lin, Lu Liang, Shuang Xia, Guo-Jiao Wu, Gang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsTranslocase of Outer Mitochondrial Membrane 5 (TOMM5), a mitochondrial outer membrane translocase subunit implicated in tumorigenesis, remains unexplored in oral cancer. Herein, we aimed to decipher the role of TOMM5 in oral cancer.MethodsThis study comprehensively explored the expression profile, clinical significance, and functional roles of TOMM5 in oral carcinogenesis with global RNA-seq and microarray datasets. In vitro experiments were performed for evaluating the impact of TOMM5 knockdown on the biological functions of oral cancer cells. Distribution heterogeneity of TOMM5 expression and related cellular trajectory was analyzed through scRNA-seq. The therapeutic implications of TOMM5 for oral cancer were analyzed through drug sensitivity prediction and tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) scoring.ResultsTOMM5 was significantly overexpressed in oral cancer tissues and demonstrated moderate diagnostic accuracy. TOMM5 knockdown significantly suppressed aggressive biological behaviors of oral cancer cells. ScRNA-seq data revealed predominant TOMM5 expression in B cells, decreasing with differentiation, and linked distinct pathway activities to specific cellular compartments. High TOMM5 expression was linked to reduced immune cell infiltration and sensitivity to Paclitaxel/Vincristine.ConclusionsOverexpressed TOMM5 shows potential as a biomarker in oral cancer, correlating with tumor progression and immune infiltration.Keywords: TOMM5, oral cancer, scRNA-seq, immune microenvironment, in vitro experiment

Introduction

Oral cancer, a significant health burden worldwide, encompasses various types of malignancies, primarily arising from the mucous membranes of the oral cavity [1]. It is characterized by its aggressive behavior, high recurrence rates, and substantial mortality, particularly in developing nations where access to timely diagnosis and advanced treatment modalities remains limited [2,3]. Epidemiologically, oral cancer incidence displays a marked geographical variation, with tobacco use, alcohol consumption, and human papillomavirus (HPV) infection emerging as the primary etiological factors [4,5]. According to the global statistics of cancer in 2022, oral cancer accounted for 324,398 new cases and 206,839 deaths globally, ranking as the 18th most common cancer by incidence and the 14th by mortality [6]. The incidence of this disease shows marked geographic variation, with notably high rates in Southeast Asia linked to local habits such as betel nut chewing [7]. Despite the progress made in surgical techniques, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, the overall 5-year survival rates for oral cancer patients remain stagnant at below 50% globally in developing nations [8]. The challenges in oral cancer management stem from early-stage symptom non-specificity, late diagnosis, and the development of resistance to conventional therapies [9,10], which calls for deciphering the fundamental molecular mechanisms driving tumorigenesis and progression.

The quest for novel molecular biomarkers has gained immense momentum in the field of oral oncology, aiming to bridge the gap between early detection, personalized treatment planning, and monitoring of therapeutic responses [11]. These biomarkers, often derived from genetic, epigenetic, or proteomic alterations, hold promise for identifying high-risk individuals, stratifying patients according to prognosis, and guiding the selection of targeted therapies [12,13]. By offering insights into the complex molecular landscape of oral cancer, such markers can facilitate the transition from a one-size-fits-all approach to precision medicine, thereby enhancing patient outcomes [14].

Among the myriad of genes implicated in cancer development, Translocase of Outer Mitochondrial Membrane 5 (TOMM5) has garnered increasing attention due to its pivotal role in mitochondrial function and its emerging association with oncogenesis [15]. TOMM5 and TOMM6 have recently been identified in human cells as integral components of the translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane (TOM) complex [16]. These subunits contribute to the structural stability of the complex [16]. Notably, TOMM5 facilitates the assembly and membrane integration of the β-barrel protein TOMM40 by interacting with its precursor during early biogenesis stages [17].

As one of the mitophagy-related genes, dysregulation of TOMM5 has been documented across various human cancers including hepatocellular carcinoma and uveal melanoma, where it has been linked to prognosis of cancer patients [18,19]. However, the expression pattern of TOMM5 and the precise mechanisms of TOMM5 in oral carcinogenesis remain largely unexplored. Further investigation into TOMM5′s role in oral cancer may uncover novel therapeutic targets for precision oncology.

The present study aims to elucidate the clinical significance and molecular basis underlying the abnormal expression of TOMM5 in oral cancer. By analyzing TOMM5 expression patterns in oral cancer tissues and corresponding normal mucosa through global bulk RNA-seq and microarray datasets, we seek to establish its prognostic value and its correlation with clinicopathological parameters. Furthermore, we intend to delve into the functional role of TOMM5 in oral cancer cell lines, employing in vitro experiments to decipher its impact on cellular proliferation, migration and invasion. Our investigation also encompasses scRNA-seq analysis revealing the expression heterogeneity plus the dynamic trajectory of TOMM5 in malignant evolution of oral cancer, and potential gene interaction network regulated by TOMM5 in oral cancer. These efforts might accelerate the development of novel therapeutic strategies targeting mitochondrial proteins (Figure 1).

Materials and methods

Compilation of eligible microarrays and RNA-seq datasets

Transcriptomic data for oral cancer patients, including gene expression profiles (FPKM) and associated clinical information, were sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/repository). Particularly, only 110 oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and four non-cancer oral samples from the TCGA-HNSC project were selected for the following differential expression analysis. To ensure comparability across samples, FPKM values were normalized by conversion to Transcripts Per Million (TPM), followed by a log2 transformation (log2(TPM + 0.001)). Additionally, microarray datasets published before November 24, 2025, were retrieved from the ArrayExpress (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/arrayexpress/) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) databases. Datasets were included if they provided TOMM5 expression values for three or more human oral cancer samples and non-cancerous oral tissue samples. For multiple datasets from the same platform, the total sample size was calculated. If both the total cancer sample size and the total non-cancer sample size are greater than three, these GEO datasets from the same platform were included.

Integrated analysis of TOMM5 expression in oral cancer

TOMM5 expression values from all eligible microarray and RNA-seq datasets were systematically extracted and compiled following established methodologies [20,21]. To quantify the differential expression of TOMM5 in tumor and normal tissues across all studies, we computed the standardized mean difference (SMD) plus its 95% confidence interval (CI) using R’s meta package. Furthermore, the diagnostic utility of TOMM5 expression was appraised through the construction of the summarized receiver’s operating characteristic (SROC) curves in Stata. (v14.0) [22].

The clinical significance of TOMM5 in oral cancer

The association between TOMM5 mRNA expression (derived from bulk RNA-seq data of the TCGA database and microarrays deposited in the GEO database) and clinical parameters of oral cancer patients in multiple centers worldwide was analyzed. For T stage and clinical stage with sufficient data, we performed a pooled analysis across multiple datasets. SMD was calculated for systematic evaluation of the indicative value of TOMM5 expression for these staging parameters in oral cancer patients. The optimal cutoff value for survival analysis was automatically determined by the PanCanSurvPlot prognostic analysis tool using a method that maximizes the difference in survival between groups. Based on this value, we appraised the prognostic impact of TOMM5 overexpression on overall survival, disease-specific survival, and disease-free interval of head and neck cancer patients including oral cancer patients from the TCGA database. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was also performed through SPSS v.22.0 to assess whether TOMM5 expression independently predicts survival of oral cancer patients after adjusting for clinical covariates including tumor stage, nodal status, grade, EGFR status and perineural invasion. P < 0.05 represented prognostic significance.

Potential molecular mechanisms of TOMM5-related genes in oral cancer

Transcriptomic data of oral cancer samples in microarray and RNA-seq datasets were analyzed for identification of TOMM5-correlated genes. Pairwise Pearson correlation analysis was performed for determining genes significantly associated with TOMM5 at the mRNA expression level. Genes exhibiting a significant positive correlation (r > 0.5, FDR-adjusted p < 0.05) or negative correlation (r < −0.5, FDR-adjusted p < 0.05) with TOMM5 were selected. Meanwhile, genes differentially expressed between oral cancer and non-cancerous oral tissues across all collected datasets were identified using Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) values computed with the limma package (v3.56.2) in R (v4.3.1). Significantly upregulated and downregulated genes in oral cancer were retained. TOMM5-related genes in oral cancer were defined as the intersection between TOMM5-negatively correlated genes and significantly downregulated genes in oral cancer (SMD < 0, p < 0.05) or the intersection between TOMM5-positively correlated genes and significantly upregulated genes in oral cancer (SMD > 0, p < 0.05). Venn diagrams illustrating these intersections were generated using the VennDiagram package (v1.7.3). Biological interpretation of the TOMM5-correlated gene sets was achieved via functional enrichment analysis against Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases, implemented with the clusterProfiler R package. (v4.10.0). Terms with a Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p-value < 0.05 represented statistical significance. Results were visualized as ribbon plots or bubble plots using ggplot2 (v3.4.4).

Functional annotations for differentially expressed genes associated with TOMM5

The TCGA-OSCC RNA-seq cohort was split into high or low TOMM5 expression groups based on the mean TOMM5 expression value, and differential gene expression analysis was performed between the two groups. The log2(TPM) normalized matrix was analyzed using the limma package. Genes meeting the thresholds of |log2(Fold Change)| > 0.1 and adjusted p-value < 0.05 were subjected to Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA). Annotation of biological functions and KEGG pathways enriched by these genes were assessed using the clusterProfiler package (R v4.1.0). Normalized Enrichment Score (NES) > 1 and a nominal p-value < 0.05 indicated significant enrichment.

Culture of WSU-HN30 cells

WSU-HN30 cells, a human oral squamous cell carcinoma line, were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) growth medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin antibiotic solution. Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 in 100-mm culture dishes.

Transfection of TOMM5 siRNA into WSU-HN30 cells

WSU-HN30 cells, routinely cultured as described in Section 2.9, were plated and grown to about 70% confluency prior to transfection. Transfections were carried out with RNATransMate transfection reagent (Sangon Biotech), adhering strictly to the supplier’s instructions. Cells were transfected with 40 nmol/L of either TOMM5-targeting siRNA or negative control (NC) siRNA. The TOMM5 siRNA sequences were: sense, 5′-CGGAGGAGAUGAAACGGAA-3′; antisense, 5′-UUCCGUUUCAUCUCCUCCG-3′ (Sangon Biotech). The NC siRNA sequences were: sense, 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′; antisense, 5′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3′ (Sangon Biotech). Cells were collected with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA 48 hours following transfection. Total RNA was isolated using the Tiangen RNAsimple Total RNA Kit (DP419, TIANGEN, Beijing, China), and RNA concentration was quantified using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (RR047A, Takara Bio, Japan). TOMM5 mRNA expression levels were quantified by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) using SYBR Green I dye on a Bio-Rad CFX96 system. GAPDH served as the endogenous control. The primer sequences were: TOMM5 forward, 5′-GATGAAACGGAAGATGCGCG-3′; TOMM5 reverse, 5′-AATGGAGTGACTCGCAGGAG-3′ (Sangon Biotech); GAPDH forward, 5′-CAGGAGGCATTGCTGATGAT-3′; GAPDH reverse, 5′-GAAGGCTGGGGCTCATTT-3′ (Sangon Biotech). Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2(-ΔΔCt) method. Knockdown efficiency exceeding 70% compared to the NC siRNA group was required for successful transfection.

CCK8 assay

Twenty-four hours post-transfection with TOMM5 siRNA or NC siRNA, WSU-HN30 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 103 cells per well (n = 6 replicates per group). Proliferation rates were monitored at 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. CCK-8 reagent (10 µL per well) was introduced at the designated time points. Plates were incubated for 2 hours at 37 °C, after which the absorbance at 450 nm (A450) was measured using a microplate reader.

Scratch wound assay

WSU-HN30 cells transfected for 48 h with TOMM5 siRNA or NC siRNA were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well. Once monolayers reached 90% confluency, a uniform wound was introduced by scraping with a sterile pipette tip. Cells were gently washed three times with PBS to remove debris, and then maintained in serum-free DMEM. Wound closure, indicative of cell migration, was documented by phase-contrast microscopy (200× magnification) at 0, 12, and 24 h post-scratching.

Transwell invasion assay

Matrigel matrix (Corning) was diluted at the ratio of 1:5 with serum-free DMEM. Eighty microliters of the diluted Matrigel were added to the upper chamber of a 24-well Transwell insert (Corning) and allowed to polymerize for several hours at 37 °C. WSU-HN30 cells transfected for 48 hours with TOMM5 siRNA or NC siRNA were trypsinized, resuspended in serum-free DMEM at 5 × 105 cells/mL, and 200 µL of the cell suspension was added to the upper chamber. The lower chamber was filled with 600 µL of DMEM containing 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. Cells were incubated for 48 hours at 37 °C. Non-invading cells on the upper surface of the membrane were carefully removed with a cotton swab. Invading cells on the lower surface were fixed with anhydrous methanol for 30 minutes and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20–30 min. After washing with PBS and air-drying, invaded cells were visualized and photographed under an inverted microscope at 200× magnification.

The associations between TOMM5 expression and immune infiltration of oral cancer

The ESTIMATE algorithm was employed to assess tumor purity and the levels of stromal and immune cell infiltration in oral cancer samples stratified by high or low TOMM5 expression. Furthermore, the relative proportions of 22 distinct immune cell types were estimated using the CIBERSORT algorithm to compare immune infiltration profiles between the high and low TOMM5 expression groups.

The implications of TOMM5 expression-based stratification for immunotherapy of oral cancer patients

To quantitatively evaluate the likelihood of tumor immune escape in oral cancer patients with varying TOMM5 expression levels, we calculated the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) score using the computational framework available at http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/. RNA-seq expression matrix of oral cancer patients in the TCGA database divided by the median value of TOMM5 expression was submitted as TIDE input. The final TIDE score combines both dimensions of immune dysfunction and immune exclusion, with higher values indicating greater immune escape potential. Other indicators such as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) scores, CD8 scores, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSC) scores and interferon gamma (INFG) scores that reflected different aspects of immune activity were also calculated and analyzed.

scRNA-seq analysis of TOMM5 in oral cancer tissues

For scRNA-seq analysis, we integrated GEO datasets including GSE164241, GSE172577, GSE195655 and GSE195832 and removed batch effect through the harmony package of R software. The integrated dataset comprised more than 150,000 cells after quality control from a total of 39 samples across the four datasets. Quality filtering was conducted with Seurat [23], keeping cells that expressed over 200 genes and clusters where TOMM5 was detected in a minimum of three cells. Cells with more than 500 detected genes and less than 25% mitochondrial RNA content were selected. Genes expressed in fewer than three cells were removed. Putative doublets were detected and removed using Scrublet. Batch effects across the four datasets were corrected using the Harmony algorithm. Data normalization, principal component analysis (PCA), and dimensionality reduction were subsequently conducted. Clustering analysis employed 15 principal components at a resolution of 0.2, followed by secondary dimensionality reduction using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). Cell types were annotated based on the expression of canonical marker genes revealed by previous studies [24]. Pseudotemporal trajectories were inferred using Monocle 3, with the root state selected based on the stem‑like or early‑differentiation subpopulations [25]. Finally, pathway activity scores (AUC values) for cancer-related pathways were computed at single-cell resolution using the AUCell package [26].

Drug sensitivity prediction for oral cancer patients with different levels of TOMM5 expression

To identify potential therapeutic agents for oral cancer patients exhibiting high TOMM5 expression, drug sensitivity was predicted for oral cancer samples within the TCGA database using the oncoPredict R package. This analysis encompassed 179 drugs. A training set comprising gene expression matrices and drug response data (IC50 values) was derived from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) project (v2). The TCGA cohort served as the test set, divided at the median TOMM5 expression into high and low groups. Drug sensitivity (predicted IC50) was compared between the two groups using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Drugs demonstrating significantly higher predicted sensitivity (p < 0.05 and log2FC < 0) in the high TOMM5 expression group were identified. Box plots illustrating the predicted drug sensitivity distributions were generated using the ggplot2 package. To computationally evaluate the potential interaction between TOMM5 and candidate compounds identified from drug sensitivity prediction, molecular docking analysis was performed using the CB-Dock2 online platform according to the AutoDock Vina algorithm. The three-dimensional structure of the human TOMM5 protein was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank. The chemical structures of Paclitaxel and Vincristine were obtained from the PubChem database in SDF format. The docking results were evaluated based on the Vina score, where more negative values indicate stronger predicted binding affinity.

Statistical analysis

Associations between TOMM5 expression (reported as mean ± standard deviation) and clinicopathological features were statistically tested using the independent samples t-test. Data of in vitro experiments were analyzed through independent sample’s t test (n ≥ 5 for technical replicates). All experiments were conducted at least three times. All statistical analysis was performed with SPSS 22.0 and GraphpadPrism 8.0. Visualization of comparison was conducted by GraphpadPrism 8.0 or R software. A two-tailed p < 0.05 represented statistical significance.

Results

Overall expression of TOMM5 in oral cancer and clinical value of TOMM5 expression for oral cancer

Details of the final datasets analyzed are compiled in Supplementary Table 1. Expression profiling of TOMM5 in oral cancer incorporated six platform-merged microarray datasets alongside RNA-seq data from TCGA, collectively comprising 310 oral cancer tissues and 239 matched normal oral mucosa samples. Significant upregulation of TOMM5 was observed in oral cancer tissues across the majority of included datasets (Supplementary Figure 1). Subsequent expression analysis corroborated this overexpression, yielding a significant overall standardized mean difference (SMD) of 0.38 (95% CI: 0.13 to 0.63; Supplementary Figure 2A). The diagnostic potential of elevated TOMM5 expression for oral cancer was further demonstrated: receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for individual dataset and a synthesized summary ROC (sROC) curve integrating all samples both indicated moderate discriminatory power (area under curves (AUC) = 0.72, 95% CI: 0.68–0.75); Supplementary Figures 2B and 3). Both single-dataset analyses and integrated analyses based on multiple datasets indicated that TOMM5 overexpression showed no significant correlation with clinicopathological parameters such as clinical stage, lymph node metastasis, tumor grade, or perineural invasion of oral cancer patients (p > 0.05, Supplementary Figure 4). Moreover, TOMM5 overexpression bore prognostic significance as a predictor for worse overall survival, disease-specific survival and disease-free interval in head and neck cancer patients from the TCGA cohort (HR > 1, p < 0.05, Supplementary Figure 5). Multivariate Cox regression analysis suggested that TOMM5 expression did not retain statistical significance as an independent predictor of overall survival for oral cancer patients (HR = 0.80, 95% CI = 0.42–1.51, p > 0.05; data not shown).

Potential molecular basis of TOMM5-related genes in oral cancer

A combined analysis using Pearson correlation and standardized mean difference (SMD) metrics identified 618 genes exhibiting positive correlation with TOMM5 expression and 613 genes showing negative correlation with TOMM5 in oral cancer. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that genes positively correlated with TOMM5 were primarily enriched in biological processes or molecular functions including ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, DNA-templated DNA replication, and helicase activity (p < 0.05, Supplementary Figure 6A, Supplementary Table 2). KEGG pathway analysis further demonstrated significant enrichment of these genes in DNA replication, base excision repair, and mismatch repair (p < 0.05, Supplementary Figure 6C, Supplementary Table 3). Conversely, genes negatively correlated with TOMM5 exhibited enrichment in terms such as cell-substrate adhesion, sulfur compound binding, and heparan sulfate proteoglycan binding (p < 0.05, Supplementary Figure 6B, Supplementary Table 4). As a supplemented analysis of the molecular mechanisms of TOMM5 in oral cancer, GSEA results indicated significant participation of TOMM5-related genes in ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, and regulation of actin cytoskeleton (Supplementary Figure 7).

SiRNA-mediated knockdown of TOMM5 impairs biological functions of oral cancer cells

In vitro experiments demonstrated that transfection with TOMM5 siRNA effectively reduced TOMM5 mRNA expression in WSU-HN30 cells (p < 0.001, Figure 2A). Furthermore, the healing percent of wounded WSU-HN30 cells and the number of WSU-HN30 cells invading through the basement membrane in Transwell assays were markedly reduced in the TOMM5 siRNA group (p < 0.05; Figure 2C-F). The knockdown of TOMM5 expression had no significant effect on the proliferation rate of WSU-HN30 cells (p > 0.05; Figure 2B).

The role of TOMM5 overexpression in immunological microenvironment of oral cancer

Employing the CIBERSORT algorithm, we identified significant associations between TOMM5 expression levels and the abundance of two distinct immune cell populations (Figure 3A). Specifically, infiltration of activated mast cells and neutrophils was significantly elevated in specimens with low TOMM5 expression compared to those with high expression (p < 0.05, Figure 3B and C). Furthermore, analysis via the ESTIMATE algorithm revealed a statistically significant but modest inverse correlation between TOMM5 expression and the global immune infiltration score within the tumor microenvironment (r = −0.23, p < 0.05; Figure 3D). No significant correlations were observed between TOMM5 expression and tumor purity or the abundance of other types of immune cells. This correlation coefficient indicates that TOMM5 expression explained a small proportion of the variance in immune infiltration scores, suggesting that TOMM5 was one of many factors influencing the immune landscape of oral cancer rather than a dominant determinant.

The implications of TOMM5 expression-based stratification for immunotherapy of oral cancer patients

Although, the high TOMM5 expression group exhibited the trend of lower TIDE scores and higher response rate of immunotherapy (p > 0.05 Figure 4A and B), the infiltration score of myeloid-derived suppressor cells was significantly higher in oral cancer patients with high TOMM5 expression (p < 0.01, Figure 4D). Therefore, the predictive value of TOMM5 expression for the outcome of immunotherapy for oral cancer patients remains controversial.

Distribution of TOMM5 expression in oral cancer tissues revealed by scRNA-seq

Following quality control and unsupervised clustering of integrated scRNA-seq datasets (GSE164241, GSE172577, GSE195655, GSE195832), all selected cells were classified into ten major cell populations (Figure 5A). TOMM5 expression was predominantly restricted to B cells, with minimal detection across other cell types (Figure 5B and C). Furthermore, analysis revealed a progressive decrease in TOMM5 transcript levels correlating with advancing stages of cellular differentiation (Figure 5D and E). AUCell enrichment analysis identified distinct compartmentalization of cancer-associated pathway activity. Gene sets for DNA damage response, Reactome TGF-β receptor signaling in EMT (epithelial to mesenchymal transition), and WNT signaling pathway were enriched predominantly within malignant epithelial cells, endothelial cells, and stromal cells, respectively (Supplementary Figure 8).

Drug sensitivity prediction for oral cancer patients with different levels of TOMM5 expression

Computational pharmacogenomic analysis revealed differential sensitivity to 179 targeted agents based on TOMM5 expression levels. Oral cancer specimens with elevated TOMM5 expression showed predicted increased sensitivity to Paclitaxel_1080 and Vincristine_1818 compared to low-expression counterparts (p < 0.05; Supplementary Figure 9). Molecular docking analysis using CB-Dock2 revealed that both Paclitaxel and Vincristine exhibited strong predicted binding affinities with the TOMM5 protein, with Vina scores of −10.1 and −13.2, respectively. Although the docking scores indicate favorable predicted binding, these in silico data should be interpreted as preliminary computational observations that necessitate experimental validation. (Supplementary Figure 10).

Discussion

Mitochondrial-related genes have emerged as critical regulators in cancer development [27,28], and among them, TOMM5 has attracted increasing attention for its potential roles in tumorigenesis across various cancer types [18,19]. However, the expression profile and biological functions of TOMM5 in oral cancer remain largely unexplored. Leveraging the power of computational biology and in vitro cell experiments, our study breaks new ground by systematically investigating TOMM5 in oral cancer using global large samples, providing multi-dimensional evidence for its role in oral carcinogenesis, and offering novel perspectives for biomarker discovery and therapeutic target identification.

Our findings revealed a significant overexpression of TOMM5 in oral cancer tissues compared to normal oral mucosa, aligning with previous reports on the dysregulation of TOMM5 in hepatocellular carcinoma and uveal melanoma [18,19]. The effect sizes observed in our integrated analysis (SMD = 0.38, AUC = 0.72) suggested that TOMM5 expression discriminated tumor from normal tissue to a moderate extent. In line with prior reports, this study confirms that high TOMM5 expression was associated with adverse overall and disease-free survival of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma patients in univariate Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, but multivariate Cox regression adjusting for clinical covariates did not support its independent prognostic role. Several factors may explain this discrepancy. TOMM5 may mediate its effect through established clinical variables such as tumor stage or nodal status. The optimal cutoff used in univariate analysis may overfit and lack reproducibility across independent cohorts. Additionally, its prognostic significance could be context‑dependent and influenced by other molecular or clinical factors not measured here. Larger prospective studies with well‑annotated cohorts are needed to clarify whether TOMM5 adds independent value beyond conventional factors. Intriguingly, our study failed to find a significant association between TOMM5 overexpression and multiple clinic-pathological parameters of oral cancer patients. The dissociation between TOMM5 expression and gross clinicopathological features could be attributed to intratumoral heterogeneity or existence of molecularly defined subtypes, highlighting the necessity for further in-depth investigations with refined stratification within the research group or analyses using spatial transcriptomics.

Crucially, the functional landscape uncovered by GO/KEGG enrichment and GSEA provides mechanistic insights into TOMM5′s pro-tumorigenic roles. Genes positively correlated with TOMM5 were significantly enriched in DNA replication and repair pathways. The enrichment of TOMM5-correlated genes in DNA replication and repair pathways raises the possibility that TOMM5 expression may be associated with genomic instability-related processes, a hallmark of aggressive cancers [29]. Concurrently, GSEA revealed an association between TOMM5-related genes and pathways such as extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, and actin cytoskeleton regulation. This finding raises the possibility that TOMM5 could be involved in metastatic progression, potentially through the modulation of cell-ECM crosstalk and cytoskeletal dynamics. Our in vitro observations indicate that TOMM5 knockdown is associated with reduced migration and invasion in WSU‑HN30 cells, suggesting a potential role of TOMM5 in these processes. However, our findings do not establish a causal relationship, and independent siRNA sequences, rescue experiments, and validation in additional OSCC models are needed to confirm a TOMM5‑specific functional effect. While these functional assays confirm the pro-migratory and pro-invasive role of TOMM5, further mechanistic studies are required to determine whether these effects are directly mediated by cytoskeletal remodeling.

Beyond cell-intrinsic effects, our analysis extended to the immunomodulatory potential of TOMM5 within the microenvironment of oral cancer. High TOMM5 expression showed modest but statistically significant associations with reduced global immune infiltration and specific alterations in immune cell populations, including decreased activated mast cells, neutrophils and CD8+ T cell infiltration. This finding aligns with the emerging understanding of the complex interplay between mitochondrial dysregulation and tumor immunity. The work by Bai et al. comprehensively elaborated on how mitochondrial gene-related changes can impact the tumor immune landscape [30], highlighting the significance of our findings on TOMM5. Mast cells, traditionally studied for their role in allergic reactions, have emerged as key players in anti-tumor immunity [31]. Similarly, neutrophils exhibit dual roles in cancer biology [32]. It should be noted that our immune infiltration analyses revealed partly inconsistent findings. Higher TOMM5 expression was associated with lower infiltration of activated mast cells and neutrophils, as well as a modestly reduced global immune score, suggesting an immunosuppressed microenvironment. Conversely, high TOMM5 expression correlated with elevated MDSC infiltration, yet showed non‑significant trends toward lower TIDE scores and higher predicted immunotherapy response. This paradox likely reflects the complexity of the tumor immune microenvironment, where overlapping and counterbalancing factors regulate distinct immune components. Importantly, immune suppression and treatment response cannot be reliably inferred from bulk estimates alone, because they are strongly shaped by tumor context and immune-cell composition. Li et al. demonstrated that organ-specific and sex hormone-mediated differences define distinct immune microenvironments [33]; tumor-educated cells can acquire immunosuppressive phenotypes not captured by bulk analysis [34]; and treatment itself dynamically remodels immune-cell composition [35]. Therefore, elevated MDSCs may reduce overall infiltration, whereas other factors, such as tumor mutational burden, neoantigen load, or additional checkpoint pathways, might influence TIDE predictions [36]. The modest correlations revealed by our work indicate that TOMM5 is likely one of many contributors to the oral cancer immune microenvironment rather than a major driver of immunosuppression. The negative correlation with immune infiltration suggests a possible involvement, but causal inference is precluded. Future studies with TOMM5 manipulation in immunocompetent models are required to determine its active role versus indirect effects.

The single-cell transcriptomic atlas further unveiled TOMM5′s spatial heterogeneity and developmental dynamics. To ensure robust and comparable quantification across cohorts, we integrated all datasets and corrected for batch effects rather than analyzed each dataset separately, thereby minimizing platform-specific technical noise and increasing statistical power. TOMM5 expression was predominantly restricted to B-lineage cells, with progressive downregulation during differentiation. This compartmentalization suggests a potential role in B-cell-mediated immune regulation, corroborated by our immune profiling showing elevated myeloid-derived suppressor cells in high-TOMM5 tumors. B cells are known to have multifaceted functions in the tumor microenvironment. In a study by Tan et al., it was emphasized that B cells can function as antigen-presenting cells, presenting tumor antigens to T cells, thereby potentially activating anti-tumor immune responses [37]. They can also differentiate into plasma cells that produce antibodies against tumor antigens [38]. However, the role of B cells in the tumor microenvironment is complex. For instance, regulatory B cells can secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-10 (IL-10) and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), which can suppress the activity of other immune cells, including CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells and dendritic cells [39–42]. In the context of our study, it is possible that B cells expressing high levels of TOMM5 secrete factors that selectively promote the expansion and activation of myeloid - derived suppressor cells, thus suppressing the immune response against tumors. Notably, AUCell analysis revealed that malignant epithelial cells exhibited enriched activity in DNA damage response pathways, while stromal compartments showed activation of TGF-β-mediated EMT and WNT signaling. The spatial segregation of pathway activities observed in our scRNA-seq analysis—with DNA damage response enriched in malignant cells, TGF-β signaling in endothelial cells, and WNT signaling in stromal cells—suggests complex intercellular communication networks within the tumor microenvironment.

An intriguing finding of our study is the discrepancy between bulk RNA-seq analysis showing TOMM5 overexpression in oral cancer tissues and scRNA-seq data revealing predominant TOMM5 expression in B cells. This contradiction could be explained by several factors. First, bulk RNA-seq measures average gene expression across all cell types in a tissue sample; thus, elevated TOMM5 in tumor tissues could reflect increased infiltration of TOMM5-expressing B cells into the tumor microenvironment rather than upregulation in cancer cells themselves. Second, the WSU-HN30 cell line used for in vitro validation represents a pure epithelial cancer cell population, and our successful knockdown in these cells confirms that TOMM5 is indeed expressed in oral cancer epithelial cells, although at levels that may be lower than in B cells. Collectively, these observations suggest that TOMM5 may exert its oncogenic effects through both tumor cell-intrinsic mechanisms and immune-related mechanisms involving B cells in the tumor microenvironment. This dual role is not mutually exclusive and warrants further investigation with experiments including spatial transcriptomics, multiplex immunohistochemistry, or RNA in situ hybridization.

Therapeutically, TOMM5 overexpression correlated with enhanced sensitivity to microtubule-targeting agents, laying a foundation for personalized treatment strategies. As reported by [43], mitochondrial proteins could interact with tubulin to impact microtubule dynamics. It is possible that abnormal mitochondrial protein import due to TOMM5 dysregulation can affect the stability of microtubule networks; thereby increasing tumor sensitivity to microtubule-targeting agents such as paclitaxel and vincristine. Recent studies have examined mitochondrial processes in cancer therapy and docking workflows combined with experimental validation [44,45]. These reports underscore the importance of experimental confirmation for computational predictions. Nevertheless, our in silico binding and IC50 estimates alone do not demonstrate direct TOMM5 target engagement or drug sensitivity; experimental validation is required to confirm these findings. If the enhanced sensitivity to microtubule-targeting agents in these tumors can be exploited, it may offer an alternative treatment approach for oral patients with a high TOMM5-expressing phenotype.

Despite these findings, our study has several limitations. First, our in vitro validation is limited to knockdown experiments in a single cell line without confirmation in additional models. Second, many proposed mechanisms including TOMM5′s roles in genomic instability, immune modulation, and tumor-stroma crosstalk were inferred from computational analyses rather than direct experimental evidence. Third, the drug sensitivity predictions lack experimental validation through pharmacological assays. Future research should incorporate additional cell lines, functional immune assays, in vivo experiments, and pharmacological validation to verify the molecular basis and therapeutic potential of TOMM5 in oral cancer.

In conclusion, our study provides comprehensive evidence of the overexpression of TOMM5 in oral cancer and verified its prognostic value as well as the promoting effects on migration and invasion ability of oral cancer cells.

Polynomials in molecules
Yan Gao, Jinsong Zeng

Abstract
. This paper characterizes polynomials within molecules. We show that a geometrically finite polynomial of degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 lies in a molecule if and only if all its critical points belong to maximal Fatou chains, and show that distinct molecules are mutually disjoint. We also establish a necessary and sufficient condition for subhyperbolic polynomials to be on the closures of bounded hyperbolic components.

Primary 37F20; Secondary 37F10

1.
Introduction

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
. Its
basin of infinity

U_{f}(\infty)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:f^{n}(z)\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty\}

is an unbounded domain satisfying
f^{-1}(U_{f}(\infty))=U_{f}(\infty)
. The complement
K_{f}
is the
filled Julia set
, with boundary
J_{f}=\partial K_{f}
defining the
Julia set
and
F_{f}=\mathbb{C}\setminus J_{f}
the
Fatou set
. The set of finite critical points of
f
is denoted by
C_{f}
, and the set of
postcritical points
is

P_{f}=\overline{\bigcup_{n\geq 1}f^{n}(C_{f})}.

Let
\mathscr{P}_{d}\simeq\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{d-1}
denote the space of monic and centered polynomials of degree
d\geq 2
. The
connectedness locus

\mathscr{C}_{d}=\{f\in\mathscr{P}_{d}:\text{the Julia set of $f$ is connected}\}

is a canonical subset of
\mathscr{P}_{d}
. A central problem in complex dynamics is to study the bifurcation of connected Julia sets, which amounts to
characterizing the structure of
\mathscr{C}_{d}
.

When
d=2
, the connectedness locus
\mathscr{C}_{2}
is the famous Mandelbrot set, which has been deeply studied, though the MLC (Mandelbrot locally connected) Conjecture remains open. For degrees
d\geq 3
, the structure of
\mathscr{C}_{d}
becomes significantly more complex, yet it is known that
\mathscr{C}_{d}
is connected and cellular
[
BH
,
La
]
.

A class of building blocks for
\mathscr{C}_{d}
comprises
bounded hyperbolic components
, i.e., the connected components of hyperbolic polynomials
in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
.
Milnor
[
Mil2
]
showed that each bounded hyperbolic component is a topological disk of real dimension
2d-2
. Characterizing bifurcations along these components, while simpler than the full problem, remains highly non-trivial. The subsets of
\mathscr{C}_{d}
that capture such bifurcation phenomena are referred as “molecules”.

Definition 1.1
.

The set
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{H})
is called a
(polynomial) molecule
if there exists a bounded hyperbolic component
\mathscr{H}
such that

\mathscr{M}=\overline{\bigcup_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow\mathscr{H}}\mathscr{H}^{\prime}},

where the notation
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow\mathscr{H}
indicates that there exists a sequence of bounded hyperbolic components
\mathscr{H}_{1}=\mathscr{H}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathscr{H}_{m}=\mathscr{H}
such that
\overline{\mathscr{H}_{i}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{H}_{i+1}}\not=\emptyset
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
.

The paper aims to describe the polynomials in molecules. We focus particularly on the geometrically finite maps
due to their key role in addressing the bifurcation problem.
To state the main result, we first introduce some notations.

For any
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
, there exists a unique conformal map
\chi_{f}:U_{f}(\infty)\to\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, called the
Böttcher coordinate
, satisfying

\chi_{f}(z)/z\to 1\textup{ as }z\to\infty\textup{ and }\chi_{f}(z)^{d}=\chi_{f}\circ f(z).

For any
\theta\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}/\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, the set

R_{f}(\theta)=\chi^{-1}_{f}(\{re^{2\pi\textbf{i}\theta}:r>1\})

is called the
external ray
of angle
\theta
. If the accumulation set of
R_{f}(\theta)
on
J_{f}
is a single point, we say that
R_{f}(\theta)

lands
at this point, which is denoted by
\pi_{f}(\theta)
.
By
[
Mil1
, Theorem 18.10]
, the ray
R_{f}(\theta)
lands when
\theta
is rational.

The
rational lamination

\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)\subseteq(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}
of
f
consists of the pairs
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}
for which the external rays
R_{f}(\theta_{1})
and
R_{f}(\theta_{2})
land at the same point.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a bounded hyperbolic component. By an unpublished theorem of McMullen, there exists a unique postcritically finite polynomial
f_{0}
contained in
\mathscr{H}
.

Suppose that
g
is a polynomial in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
with
\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(g)
. Fix a bounded Fatou component
U
of
f_{0}
. We define a connected and compact subset of
K_{g}
associated to
U
by

(1.1)

K_{g,U}=\bigcap_{(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})}\overline{S_{g}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})}\cap K_{g},

where
S_{g}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
is the component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{R_{g}(\theta)\cup R_{g}(\theta^{\prime})}
which contains external rays
R_{g}(t)
such that
R_{f_{0}}(t)
land on
\partial U\setminus\{\pi_{f_{0}}(\theta)\}
.

A bounded hyperbolic component
\mathscr{H}
is called
primitive
if the bounded Fatou domains of
f
have disjoint closures for
f\in\mathscr{H}
. A point
z
of a connected set
E\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}
is called a
cut point
of
E
if
E\setminus\{z\}
has at least two components, a
branched point
if it has at least three components, and an
endpoint
if it remains connected.

Theorem A.

Every molecule
\mathscr{M}
contains a unique primitive hyperbolic component
\mathscr{H}
and
\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(g)
for every
g\in\mathscr{M}
, where
f_{0}
is the unique postcritically finite map in
\mathscr{H}
. Furthermore, a geometrically finite polynomial
g
lies in
\mathscr{M}
if and only if

(1)

\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(g)
, and

(2)

for any Fatou domain
U
of
f_{0}
, the set
K_{g,U}
is a maximal Fatou chain of
g
, or equivalently,

K_{g,U}
has at most countably many cut points.

Remark.
We add the following remarks regarding Theorem A.

(1) The notion of (maximal) Fatou chains was originally introduced in
[
CGZ
,
DHS
]
to study the combinatorics of Julia sets for rational maps. It may apply to polynomials with minor adaptation, see Section
2
. Theorem A implies that:

A geometrically finite polynomial lies in a molecule if and only if each of its critical points is contained in a maximal Fatou chain.

Thus, maximal Fatou chains correspond precisely to molecules in parameter space.

(2) Condition (2) of Theorem A is equivalent to a finite condition:
K_{g,U}
is a maximal Fatou chain for each periodic Fatou component
U
of
f_{0}
.

(3) Theorem A shows that each molecule
\mathscr{M}
is closed under quasiconformal deformation for geometrically finite maps. That is, if a geometrically finite
f\in\mathscr{M}
is quasiconformally conjugate to some
g\in\mathscr{P}_{d}
, then
g
also lies in
\mathscr{M}
. However, this property is known to fail for the closure of a single bounded hyperbolic component, see
[
Tan
]
.

As we will see in Section
2
, the maximal Fatou chains of a polynomial are pairwise disjoint. Their counterparts in parameter space, the molecules, share this property.

Theorem B.

Two distinct molecules are always disjoint.

The unique hyperbolic component containing
z\mapsto z^{d}
is called the
main hyperbolic component

\mathscr{H}(d)
. The molecule containing
\mathscr{H}(d)
is called the
main molecule
, denoted by
\mathscr{M}(d)
.

Luo and Park
[
LP
]
undertook the first systematic study of the so-called extended main molecule.
For a postcritically finite polynomial
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
, let
\mathscr{H}_{f}
denote the connected component of

(1.2)

\{g\in\mathscr{P}_{d}:\ g\text{ is quasiconformal conjugate to }f\text{ near the Julia set}\}

that contains
f
, referred to as a
relative hyperbolic component
in
[
LP
]
. Note that
\mathscr{H}_{f}=\{f\}
if
f
has no bounded Fatou domains, and
\mathscr{H}_{f}
is a hyperbolic component if
f
is hyperbolic.

They defined the
extended main molecule
as

{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(d)=\overline{\bigcup_{\mathscr{H}_{f}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{H}(d)}\mathscr{H}_{f}},

where
f
is a postcritically finite polynomial, and the notion
\mathscr{H}_{f}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{H}(d)
means that
there exists a finite sequence of postcritically finite polynomials
f=f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}(z)=z^{d}
such that
\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i}}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i+1}}}\not=\emptyset
for all
i=1,\ldots,m-1
.

One main result of
[
LP
]
is as follows.

Theorem LP
(
[
LP
, Theorm 1.1]
).
Let
f\in\mathscr{P}_{d}
be a postcritically finite polynomial.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The topological entropy of
f
restricted to its Hubbard tree
T_{f}
is zero;

(2)

f
is contained in the extended main molecule
\mathscr{M}_{+}(d)
;

(3)

f
is obtained from
z^{d}
via a finite sequence of simplicial tunings;

(4)

J_{f}\cap T_{f}
is at most a countable set.

It is clear that
\mathscr{M}(d)\subseteq{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(d)
, and it is known that
\mathscr{M}(2)={\mathscr{M}}_{+}(2)
. A natural question then arises: does the equality
\mathscr{M}(d)={\mathscr{M}}_{+}(d)
hold for all
d\geq 3
?

Since the lamination of
\mathscr{H}(d)
is trivial, it follows from Theorem A that, a geometrically finite map
f
lies in
\mathscr{M}(d)
if and only if
K_{f}
has countably many cut points. This condition is in fact equivalent to property (4) in Theorem LP, see Proposition
2.8
. We thus obtain the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every degree
d\geq 2
, we have
\mathscr{M}(d)={\mathscr{M}}_{+}(d)
.

This corollary yields the density of hyperbolic maps in
{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(d)
, thereby positively answering Question 1.6 raised in
[
LP
]
.

Theorem A classifies the geometrically finite polynomials within a molecule. A subsequent question is: which of these polynomials lie in the closure of a bounded hyperbolic component?

For
d=2
, every geometrically finite map in a molecule
lies in the closure of a bounded hyperbolic component. For higher degrees, however, the situation is more subtle.
As shown in
[
LP
, Section 3.2]
, there exists a postcritically finite map in
\mathscr{M}(3)

outside the closure of any bounded hyperbolic component.
In addition, the combinatorial classification of geometrically finite polynomials on
\partial\mathscr{H}(d)
was established in
[
Luo
]
.

Our third main result completely characterizes the subhyperbolic polynomials in the closures of bounded hyperbolic components.

Theorem C.

Let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
be a subhyperbolic polynomial. Then
f
lies in the closure of a bounded hyperbolic component if and only if every critical point of
f
is eventually mapped into the maximal Fatou chain generated by a single periodic bounded Fatou domain.

For definition of maximal Fatou chains generated by single Fatou domains, see Definition
2.4
.

The proofs of the above theorems rely on three main tools.

\bullet
The first is the combinatorial notion of Fatou chains mentioned earlier. To our knowledge, this is the first application of Fatou chains in the study of parameter spaces.

\bullet
The second is the hyperbolic-parabolic deformation theory established in
[
CT2
]
.

\bullet
The third is a new dynamical perturbation technique for geometrically finite polynomials (Theorem
3.3
), which provides a hyperbolic–subhyperbolic deformation framework, thus complementing the hyperbolic-parabolic deformation theory when studying the polynomial bifurcations.

The organization of the paper is as follows.

In Section 2 we define two kinds of Fatou chains and establish their basic properties. Section 3 proves the dynamical perturbation theorem (Theorem
3.3
) for geometrically finite polynomials, and Section 4 introduces laminations and geodesic laminations

We show in Section 5 that the conclusion of Theorem A holds for “extended molecules” (Theorem
5.1
), and then prove in Section 6 that extended molecules coincide with molecules (Theorem
6.1
). This completes the proof of Theorems A and B.
Theorem C is proved in Section 7.

2.
Fatou chains of polynomials

A
continuum
is a connected and compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
containing at least two points, and a continuum is
full
if its complement is connected. A
disk
always means a Jordan domain in
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
f
be a polynomial in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
. For a continuum
E
satisfying
f^{p}(E)\subseteq E
, we define the
continuum generated by

E
as

K_{E}=\overline{\bigcup_{i\geq 0}E^{i}},

where
E^{i}
denotes the component of
f^{-ip}(E)
containing
E
. Since
E^{i}\subseteq E^{i+1}
for all
i\geq 0
, the set
K_{E}
remains unchanged whether we use the original period
p
or any multiple
p^{\prime}=kp
.

If
K_{E}=E
, or equivalently, if
E
is a connected component of
f^{-p}(E)
, then
E
is called a
stable continuum
of
f
.
We say that a full stable continuum
E

induces renormalization
if there exists disks
W\Subset V
such that
f^{p}:W\to V
is proper and
E=\{z\in W:f^{ip}(z)\in W,\forall\,i\geq 0\}
.

The following is a criterion when a full stable continuum induces renormalization.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
BOPT
]
, Theorem B)
.

Let
E
be a full stable continuum of
f
with
\mathrm{deg}f|_{E}\geq 2
. Then,
E
induces renormalization if and only if for every attracting or parabolic point
z\in E
of
f
, the immediate attracting
basin of
z
or the union of all immediate parabolic domains of
z
is a subset of
E
.

We now recursively define the (periodic) Fatou chains of
f
in each level:

•

Level-0:
The closures of all bounded periodic Fatou domains.

•

Level-1:
Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
be the components of the union of all level-0 Fatou chains. The level-1 Fatou chains are defined as the continua generated by
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
, respectively.

•

Level-
\mathbf{(n+1)}
:
Assume that the level-
n
periodic Fatou chains have been defined. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
be the components of their union. The level-
(n+1)
Fatou chains are defined as the continua generated by these components.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists an integer
N
such that each level-
N
periodic Fatou chain is also a level-
(N+1)
one. Consequently, each level-
N
periodic Fatou chain is a stable continuum of
f
.

Proof.

Let
k(n)
denote the number of level-
n
Fatou chains of
f
. Since
k(n)
is decreasing, there exists
n_{0}
such that
k(n)
is constant for
n\geq n_{0}
, and distinct level-
n
chains are pairwise disjoint.

For a periodic bounded Fatou domain
U
of period
p
, let
K_{n}(U)
be the level-
n
Fatou chain containing
U
. Then
f^{p}(K_{n}(U))=K_{n}(U)
, and for
n\geq n_{0}
,
K_{n}(U)
is the unique level-
n
Fatou chain in
K_{n+1}(U)
. If
K_{n}(U)
is not a component of
f^{-p}(K_{n}(U))
, then

\deg(f^{p}|_{K_{n+1}(U)})>\deg(f^{p}|_{K_{n}(U)}).

Since
\deg(f^{p}|_{K_{n+1}(U)})\leq(\deg f)^{p}
, the sequence
\deg(f^{p}|_{K_{n}(U)})
must stabilize. Thus, there exists
n(U)
such that for
n\geq n(U)
,
K_{n}(U)
is a component of
f^{-p}(K_{n}(U))
and
K_{n+1}(U)=K_{n}(U)
.

Taking
N=\max n(U)
over all periodic bounded Fatou domains
U
, every level-
n
Fatou chain for
n\geq N
is also a level-
N
Fatou chain.
∎

These level-
N
Fatou chains together with all components of their iterated preimages form the
maximal Fatou chains
of
f
, which are pairwise disjoint by construction.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
J_{f}
is locally connected. Then
each Fatou chain of any level contains at most countably many cut points, with only finitely many being periodic. Furthermore, if each critical point in
J_{f}
is preperiodic, all these cut points are preperiodic.

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on the level of Fatou chains.
Clearly, level-
0
Fatou chains contain no cut points. Assume that every level-
n
Fatou chain satisfies the stated property. Let
E
be a component of the union of all level-
n
Fatou chains, and denote by
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
the level-
n
Fatou chains contained in
E
.

Let
z
be a cut point of
E
that is not a cut point of any
B_{i}
. Then
z\in B_{i}\cap B_{j}
for some
i\neq j
. Since
J_{f}
is locally connected, there exist external rays
R_{f}(\alpha)
and
R_{f}(\beta)
landing at
z
such that
\gamma=R_{f}(\alpha)\cup\{z\}\cup R_{f}(\beta)
separates
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
, which implies
\{z\}=B_{i}\cap B_{j}
. As
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
are periodic,
z
is periodic. Combined with the induction hypothesis, this shows
E
satisfies the conclusion of the proposition.

For
k\geq 1
, let
E_{k}
be the component of
f^{-kp}(E)
containing
E
, where
p
is the period of
E
. Any cut point of
E_{k}
maps under iteration to either a critical point of
f^{p}
or a cut point of
E
. Therefore, every
E_{k}
, and hence
\bigcup_{k\geq 0}E_{k}
, satisfies the conclusion of the proposition.

Let
K=\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq 0}E_{k}}
be the level-
(n+1)
Fatou chain generated by
E
. We claim that no point
x\in K\setminus\bigcup_{k\geq 0}E_{k}
is a cut point of
K
. Suppose otherwise: then there exist external rays
R_{f}(\theta_{\pm})
landing at
x
such that both components
W_{\pm}
of
\mathbb{C}\setminus(R_{f}(\theta_{+})\cup\{x\}\cup R_{f}(\theta_{-}))
intersect
K
. Since
x
avoids all
E_{k}
(
k\geq 1
), the set
\bigcup_{k\geq 0}E_{k}
lies entirely in one component, say
W_{+}
. Then
K
is disjoint from
W_{-}
, a contradiction. Thus
K
satisfies the required property. ∎

Similarly, we can define the Fatou chains generated by single Fatou domains.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
U
be a bounded periodic Fatou domain of
f
. The level-
0
Fatou chain generated by
U
is defined as
\overline{U}
. Inductively, if
E
denotes the level-
n
Fatou chain generated by
U
, then the level-
(n+1)
Fatou chain generated by
U
is defined as the continuum generated by
E
.

Analogous to Proposition
2.2
, this process stabilizes, yielding a
maximal Fatou chain generated by
U
, which is still a stable continuum of
f
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
J_{f}
is locally connected. Then the maximal Fatou chain
E_{U}
generated by a fixed Fatou domain
U
has no periodic cut points. In particular,
E_{U}=\overline{U}
if and only if
\partial U
contains no critical points.

Proof.

Note that
\overline{U}
, the level-
0
Fatou chain generated by
U
, has no cut points. By induction, assume the level-
n
Fatou chain
E
generated by
U
has no periodic cut points.

For each
k\geq 1
, let
E_{k}
be the component of
f^{-k}(E)
containing
E
.
We claim that
E_{k}
has no periodic cut points.
Suppose otherwise: let
z
be a periodic cut point of
E_{k}
, and let
R_{f}(\theta_{\pm})
be external rays landing at
z
such that
\gamma=R_{f}(\theta_{+})\cup\{z\}\cup R_{f}(\theta_{-})
separates
E_{k}
. Since
f^{k}
is injective near
z
and
f^{k}(E_{k})=E
, it follows that
z
would disconnect
E
, a contradiction.

Thus, the level-
(n+1)
Fatou chain
K=\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq 0}E_{k}}
generated by
U
has no periodic cut points.
∎

Using Lemma
2.1
, we immediately obtain the following.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
f
has no irrational indifferent periodic points, then any periodic maximal Fatou chain induces renormalization. If
f
has no indifferent periodic points, then any maximal Fatou chain generated by a single periodic Fatou domain induces renormalization.

Next, we present several equivalent characterizations of stable continua in maximal Fatou chains for geometrically finite polynomials.
Factually, any combinatorial issue can be first addressed in the postcritically finite case and then extended to geometrically finite case via the following result, established independently by Kawahira
[
Ka
]
and Cui-Tan
[
CT2
]
using different methods.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f
be a geometrically finite polynomial in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
. Then, there exists a unique postcritically finite polynomial
g\in\mathscr{P}_{d}
, and a homeomorphism
\phi:\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\to\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}
with
\phi(z)/z\to 1
as
z\to\infty
, such that
\phi(J_{f})=J_{g}\textup{ and }\phi\circ f=g\circ\phi\textup{ on }J_{f}.

For a postcritically finite polynomial
g
, its
Hubbard tree

T_{g}
is the minimal
g
-invariant and finite regulated tree in
K_{g}
containing
P_{f}
; see
[
DH1
, Proposition 2.7]
. Here, a finite tree
T\subseteq K_{g}
is called
regulated
if for any edge of
T
, its intersection with the closure of any Fatou domain consists of one or two internal rays together with the landing points. It is well-known that

Proposition 2.8
.

Every cut point of
J_{g}
is eventually iterated into
T_{g}
.

The Hubbard tree
T_{f}
for a geometrically finite polynomial
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
is then defined as a finite tree in
K_{f}
such that
P_{f}\subseteq T_{f}
and
T_{f}\cap J_{f}=\phi^{-1}(T_{g}\cap J_{g})
. Here
\phi
and
g
are the maps obtained in Theorem
2.7
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
K
be a full stable continuum of a geometrically finite polynomial
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
with
\partial K\subseteq J_{f}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

K
is contained in a maximal Fatou chain of
f
;

(2)

each cut point of
K
is preperiodic;

(3)

K
contains at most countably many cut points.

(4)

K
contains finitely many periodic cut points;

The proof of this proposition is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f
be a postcritically finite polynomial. Then, given any regulated arc
I_{0}\subseteq K_{f}
not lying in any maximal Fatou chain, there exist arcs
I^{\prime}\subseteq I_{0}
,
I=[x,y]\subseteq K_{f}
, two disjoint sub-arcs
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq I
and positive integers
l,m_{1},m_{2}
, such that

(1)

x,y
are pre-repelling and their orbits avoid the critical points;

(2)

f^{l}:I^{\prime}\to I
is a homeomorphism;

(3)

I
is disjoint from
P_{f}
and branched points of the Hubbard tree
T_{f}
;

(4)

the maps
f^{m_{k}}:I_{k}\to I
are homeomorphisms for
k=1,2
.

As a consequence, for any
n>0
, there exists a preperiodic point
z_{n}\in I^{\prime}
whose period exceeds
n
.

A result similar to this lemma is proved in
[
LP
, Lemma 6.4]
by Cantor-Bendixson decomposition of graphs. Here we present a direct proof based on the approach in
[
GT
, Lemma 9.14]
.

Proof.

Let
X_{1}
be the union of all maximal Fatou chains of
f
containing critical or postcritical points, and let
X_{2}
be the set of branch points of
T_{f}
. Define
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup P_{f}
. Then
f(X)\subseteq X
.

Let
Y
be the set of points in
J_{f}
whose orbits avoid
X

3.2.
Dynamical Perturbation Theorem

By a

of a polynomial

in

such that

Theorem 3.3

(Dynamical perturbation)
.

Let

be geometrically finite with non-empty bounded Fatou domains. Let

be a last critical point of

. Then there exists a sequence of geometrically finite maps
\{f_{n}\}\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{d}
uniformly converging to

such that:

(1)

(Critical point correspondence)
For every
c\in C_{f}
,
f_{n}
has a unique critical point
c_{n}
with
{\rm deg}(f,c)={\rm deg}(f_{n},c_{n})
and

as

, such that

, and

(2)

(Critical relation consistency)
For distinct

, then
f_{n}^{k}(c_{n})=c_{n}^{\prime}
;

, then

(3)

(Parabolic point correspondence)
For each parabolic periodic point

of

converging to

as

, and sharing the same multiplier, period, and multiplicity as

does for

.

(4)

(Fatou chain correspondence)
If

, then there exists a bijection

•

and

•

(5)

(Quasiconformal deformation and semiconjugacy)

When

The following theorem provides a criterion for determining when a marked semi-rational polynomial is c-equivalent to a marked polynomial.

A Jordan curve

\beta\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\setminus P
is called

if its endpoints

belong to
P
and either
z_{0}\not=z_{1}
, or
z_{0}=z_{1}
and both components of
\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\setminus\overline{\beta}
intersects
P
.
A

in
\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\setminus P
is a finite collection
\Gamma
of pairwise disjoint, pairwise non-isotopic, essential Jordan curves in
\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\setminus P
.

Let
(F,P)
be a semi-rational polynomial. A multicurve
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{p}\}
in

to a component of

(Thurston-Cui-Tan

]

induces a Levy cycle of

.

The following result is well-known.

Proposition 4.3

Every subhyperbolic map in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
is contained in a relative hyperbolic component, which contains a unique postcritically finite map. Moreover, two subhyperbolic maps in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
lie in the same relative hyperbolic component if and only if they have the same rational lamination.

Sketch of the proof.

Let

be subhyperbolic. If

has no bounded Fatou domains, then it is postcritically finite and then
\mathscr{H}_{f}=\{f\}
. Otherwise, a standard quasiconformal surgery
[

]
provides a path

from

to a postcritically finite map

, with each

near the Julia set. Hence

.

Let
\mathscr{H}_{f}
be the relative hyperbolic component containing

such that for all

,

\iota(\overline{U_{f}(\infty)},g)=\overline{U_{g}(\infty)},\quad\iota(\overline{R_{f}(\theta)},g)=\overline{R_{g}(\theta)}\quad(\theta\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}).

So all maps in
\mathscr{H}_{f}
share the same lamination.

Now take another subhyperbolic

with

. By the argument above, there exist postcritically finite polynomials

such that

,

, and

[

.∎

There is a natural geometric interpretation for laminations, originally due to Thurston
[
Th
]
.
The map
\sigma_{d}:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}/\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\to\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}/\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
is defined by
\sigma_{d}(\theta)=d\theta\penalty\ {\rm mod}\penalty\ \mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
. We identity
\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}/\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
with the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
via the map
\theta\mapsto e^{2\pi i\theta}
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
be a geometrically finite polynomial. The
geodesic lamination

L(f)
of
f
is defined as the union of
\partial\mathbb{D}
and all geodesics that form the boundaries of the hyperbolic convex hulls (within
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
) of the
\lambda(f)
-equivalence classes. Each such geodesic (an open arc) is called a
leaf
of
L(f)
. A leaf with endpoints
a,b\in\partial\mathbb{D}
is denoted by
\overline{ab}
. Since
\lambda(f)
is closed,
L(f)
is a closed subset of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

A
gap

\Omega
of
L(f)
is a connected component of
\mathbb{D}\setminus L(f)
. The leaves of
L(f)
contained in
\partial\Omega
are called the
edges
of
\Omega
, and the points in
\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial\mathbb{D}
are its
vertices
. We call
\Omega
a
finite
or
infinite
gap according to wheter it has finitely or infinitely many edges. Note that if
\Omega
is an infinite gap, then
\partial\Omega\cap\partial\mathbb{D}
is a Cantor set; moreover,
\partial\Omega
is always a Jordan curve.

The projection
\pi_{f}:\partial\mathbb{D}\to J_{f}
extends naturally to
L(f)
: for any leaf
\overline{ab}
of
L(f)
, we define
\pi_{f}(\overline{ab})=\pi_{f}(a)
. Consequently, for any gap
\Omega
of
L(f)
:

\bullet

If
\Omega
is finite, then
\pi_{f}(\partial\Omega)
is a branched point of
J_{f}
.

\bullet

If
\Omega
is infinite, then
\pi_{f}(\partial\Omega)=\partial U
for some Fatou domain
U
of
f
.

The covering
\sigma_{d}:\partial\mathbb{D}\to\partial\mathbb{D}
also extends to
\sigma_{d}:L(f)\to L(f)
. For any leaf
\ell=\overline{ab}
of
L(f)
: if
\sigma_{d}(a)=\sigma_{d}(b)
, we define
\sigma_{d}(\ell)=\sigma_{d}(a)
; otherwise, we define
\sigma_{d}(\ell)=\overline{\sigma_{d}(a)\sigma_{d}(b)}
. The extension satisfies
\pi_{f}\circ\sigma_{d}=f\circ\pi_{f}
on
L(f)
. Furthermore, if
\sigma_{d}(\partial\Omega)=\partial\Omega^{\prime}
for two gaps
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
of

, we define
\sigma_{d}(\Omega)=\Omega^{\prime}
.

Let

denote the union of
\partial\mathbb{D}
and all leaves of

with rational angles. Then the following result follows directly from Proposition
2.8
and Lemma

.

.

Let

contains the edges of all gaps of

.

Moreover, Lemma

In this section, we shall characterize the geometrically finite maps in extended molecules.

Theorem 5.1

Every extended molecule

5.1.

We borrow several key results from the general theory of hyperbolic-parabolic deformation in

Proposition 5.2

(Simple pinching

Let

and a continuous onto map

(1)

;

(2)

\phi:J_{f}\to J_{g}
is a topological conjugacy between between

and

;

(3)

for each

is a parabolic point of

5.2.
Reduction along relative hyperbolic components

For a geometrically finite map
g\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
, its

is defined as

\kappa(g)=\#\{\text{all periodic Fatou domains of $g$}\}+\#\big(C_{g}\cap J_{g}\big).

It is straightforward to verify the following result.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\{f_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a quasiconformal path starting from a subhyperbolic map
f=f_{0}\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
and converging to
g
. If
g
and
\{f_{t}\}
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
5.2
, then
\kappa(f)=\kappa(g)
. If
g
and
\{f_{t}\}
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
5.4
, then
\kappa(f)<\kappa(g)
.

For a subhyperbolic map
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
,

\mathscr{H}_{f}
denotes the relative hyperbolic component containing
f
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
be a non-primitive subhyperbolic map. Then there exists a subhyperbolic map
f_{1}\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
satisfying

\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{1}}}\not=\emptyset
and
\kappa(f_{1})<\kappa(f)
. Furthermore,
for any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
such that
\pi_{f}(\alpha)
avoid maximal Fatou chains of
f
, we have that
(\alpha,\beta)\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{1})
and
\pi_{f_{1}}(\alpha)
avoids maximal Fatou chains of
f_{1}
.
In particular, if
f
is hyperbolic, then
f_{1}
is also hyperbolic.

Proof.

Since
f
is non-primitive, there are two bounded Fatou domains
U
and
V
of
f
such that their boundaries intersect at some point
y
. Then either
y
is (pre-)critical, or
f^{k}(U)\neq f^{k}(V)
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus at least one of the following holds:

(1)

A periodic point
z
lies on the boundaries of at least two bounded Fatou domains, and all these Fatou domains belong to the same cycle;

(2)

The boundaries of two bounded Fatou domains
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
from distinct cycles intersect at a periodic point
z
;

(3)

A critical point
c
lies on the boundary of a bounded Fatou domain.

We will prove the lemma by considering each of these cases separately.

Case (1). Let
U
be a Fatou domain of
f
containing an attracting periodic point
x
such that
z\in\partial U
. By applying Proposition

5.2
to
f
and
\{x,z\}
, we obtain a geometrically finite map
g\in\partial\mathscr{H}_{f}
with a unique parabolic cycle
{\mathcal{O}}_{g}
, and a continuous map
\phi
on
\mathbb{C}
such that
\phi:J_{f}\to J_{g}
is a homeomorphism and
\phi(x)=\phi(z)\in{\mathcal{O}}_{g}
.
The map
\phi
sends maximal Fatou chains of
f
to those of
g
. Consequently,
\pi_{g}(\alpha)
and
\pi_{g}(\beta)
avoid the maximal Fatou chains of
g
, and are eventually repelling. Thus, Lemma
3.2
(1) implies
(\alpha,\beta)\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(g)
.

Note that
g
satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
5.4
(i).
We thus obtain a subhyperbolic map
f_{1}\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
with a marked attracting cycle
\mathcal{O}_{f_{1}}
and a continuous map
\phi_{1}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, such that
\phi_{1}(J_{f_{1}})=J_{g},\,\phi_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{f_{1}})={\mathcal{O}}_{g}
and
g\in\partial\mathscr{H}_{f_{1}}
.

We conclude from Proposition
5.5
that
\kappa(f_{1})<\kappa(g)=\kappa(f)
. Moreover, the map
\phi_{1}
lifts the maximal Fatou chains of
g
to those of
f_{1}
and is injective elsewhere. Since
\phi_{1}(\pi_{f_{1}}(\alpha))=\pi_{g}(\alpha)
and
\phi_{1}(\pi_{f_{1}}(\beta))=\pi_{g}(\beta)
, and since
\pi_{g}(\alpha)=\pi_{g}(\beta)
avoids the maximal Fatou chains of
g
, it follows that
\pi_{f_{1}}(\alpha)=\pi_{f_{1}}(\beta)
, and this point is disjoint from the maximal Fatou chains of
f_{1}
.

If
f
is hyperbolic, then
g
has no critical points in its Julia set, which implies that
f_{1}
is hyperbolic. This completes the proof of the lemma in Case (1).

Case (2). Let
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
be the attracting points in
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, respectively. We apply Proposition
5.2
to
f
and the pairs
\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
with
z_{1}=z_{2}=z
, obtaining a geometrically finite map
g\in\partial\mathscr{H}_{f}
with a unique parabolic cycle. The rest argument parallels Case (1), except that
g
now satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
5.4
(ii). We omit the details.

Case (3). Without loss of generality, assume that
c
is a last critical point of
f
. Applying Theorem
3.3
(5) to
(f,c)
yields a subhyperbolic map
f_{1}\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
with
f\in\partial\mathscr{H}_{f_{1}}
and a semiconjugacy
\phi
from
f_{1}:J_{f_{1}}\to J_{f_{1}}
to
f:J_{f}\to J_{f}
. The properties of
\phi
implies
\#(C_{f}\cap J_{f})>\#(C_{f_{1}}\cap J_{f_{1}})
, hence
\kappa(f)>\kappa(f_{1})
. Moreover, letting
z:=\pi_{f}(\alpha)
, the point
z_{1}=\phi^{-1}(z)
is disjoint from the maximal Fatou chains of
f_{1}
and satisfies
\pi_{f}^{-1}(z)=\pi_{f_{1}}^{-1}(z_{1})
. Consequently,
(\alpha,\beta)\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{1})
. ∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
be a subhyperbolic polynomial. Then, there exists a unique primitive postcritically finite polynomial
f_{0}
such that
\mathscr{H}_{f}
and
\mathscr{H}_{f_{0}}
are contained in a finite chain of relative hyperbolic components. Moreover, we have

(1)

\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
, and for any infinite gap
\Omega
of
L(f_{0})
(if exists), the set
K_{f,\Omega}
defined in (
4.1
) is a maximal Fatou chain of
f
;

(2)

if
(\alpha,\beta)\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
and
\pi_{f}(\alpha)
avoids maximal Fatou chains, then
(\alpha,\beta)\in\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})
;

(3)

if each critical point of
f
lies in a maximal Fatou chain, then
f_{0}
is hyperbolic;

(4)

if
f
is hyperbolic, then
\mathscr{H}_{f}
and
\mathscr{H}_{f_{0}}
lie in a finite chain of hyperbolic components.

Proof.

By successive applying Lemma
5.6
, we obtain a sequence of subhyperbolic maps
f=f_{m},\ldots,f_{0}\in\mathscr{C}_{d}
such that
\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i+1}}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i}}}\not=\emptyset
for
i=m-1,\ldots,0
, the polynomial
f_{0}
is primitive and postcritically finite, and that Statements (2) and (4) of the proposition are satisfied.

By Proposition
4.9
, we have
\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f_{0})\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
. To prove (1), it suffices to verify the property for every infinite periodic gap
\Omega
of
L(f_{0})
. Proposition
4.7
implies
K_{f,\Omega}
is a full stable continuum.

Fix
g_{i}\in\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i}}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i+1}}}
for every
i=0,\ldots,m-1
. Then there exists
N>0
such that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
with period larger than
N
, the point
\pi_{g_{i}}(\theta)
is pre-repelling and its orbit avoids the critical points of
g_{i}
, for every
i=0,\ldots,m-1
.

Assume that
K_{f,\Omega}
is not a maximal Fatou chain. Then it is also not contained in a maximal Fatou chain by Proposition
4.7
. Due to Proposition
2.9
, there exists a periodic cut point
z
of
K_{f,\Omega}
with period larger than
N
. Choose angles
\alpha,\beta\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\mathbb{D}
such that

and
R_{f}(\beta)
land at

and
\overline{\alpha\beta}\subseteq\Omega
. Successively applying Lemma

(1) to each triple
\{\mathscr{H}_{f_{i+1}},g_{i},\mathscr{H}_{f_{i}}\}
(

) shows that

and
R_{f_{0}}(\beta)
also land at a common point, contradicting that

is a gap of
L(f_{0})
. This completes the proof of Statement (1).

Suppose every critical point of
f
lies in a maximal Fatou chain. Then by Statement (1) and a degree argument, each critical point of
f_{0}
lies in
K_{f_{0},\Omega}
for some infinite gap
\Omega
of
L(f_{0})
, which is just the closure of a bounded Fatou domain of
f_{0}
. Since
f_{0}
is primitive, it follows that
f_{0}
is hyperbolic. This completes the proof of Statement (3).

The uniqueness of
f_{0}
follows from Propositions
4.3
and
4.9
.
∎

5.3.
Geometrically finite maps in extended molecules

This section is devoted to proving Theorem

.
From the definition of extended molecules, we immediately obtain the following.

Proposition 5.8
.

Given an extended molecule
\mathscr{M}_{+}=\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathscr{H})
, any bounded hyperbolic component is either disjoint from
\mathscr{M}_{+}
or connected to
\mathscr{H}

\mathscr{M}_{+}=\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathscr{H}^{\prime})

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{+}
be an extended molecule. From Propositions

contains a unique primitive hyperbolic component.

Let
f_{0}
be the unique postcritically finite map in this primitive hyperbolic component.
By definition, for any

, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of subhyperbolic maps converging to

, each connected to

by a finite chain of relative hyperbolic components. By Proposition
4.9
, we have

We start to check the properties for geometrically finite polynomials lying in

.

Claim 1
.

Proof of Theorem C.

By Theorem

According to

, if a subhyperbolic rational map

. By induction, it follows that

.

For the necessity, let
\mathscr{H}
be a bounded hyperbolic component with a sequence

converging to

. Fix a critical point

is eventually mapped into a maximal Fatou chain generated by a periodic Fatou domain.

Let

be a critical point of

with

.
Since

, there exists a periodic attracting Fatou domain

We claim that for every sufficiently large

is contained in

Note that

From the claim, we have

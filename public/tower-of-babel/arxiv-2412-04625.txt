Global minimization of a minimum of a finite collection of functions
Guillaume Van Dessel a ID and François Glineur a,b ID

Abstract
We consider the global minimization of a particular type of minimum structured optimization problems wherein the variables must belong to some basic set, the feasible domain is described by the intersection of a large number of functional constraints and the objective stems as the pointwise minimum of a collection of functional pieces . Among others, this setting includes all non-convex piecewise linear problems. The global minimum of a minimum structured problem can be computed using a simple enumeration scheme by solving a sequence of individual problems involving each a single piece and then taking the smallest individual minimum. We propose a new algorithm, called Upper-Lower Optimization ( ULO ), tackling problems from the aforementioned class. Our method does not require the solution of every individual problem listed in the baseline enumeration scheme, yielding potential substantial computational gains. It alternates between (a) local-search steps, which minimize upper models, and (b) suggestion steps, which optimize lower models. We prove that ULO can return a solution of any prescribed global optimality accuracy. According to a computational complexity model based on the cost of solving the individual problems, we analyze in which practical scenarios ULO is expected to outperform the baseline enumeration scheme. Finally, we empirically validate our approach on a set of piecewise linear programs (PL) of incremental difficulty.

Contributions

We summarize our main contributions as follows.

•

First, we give a valuable insight (Section

) into (

Third, we demonstrate the validity of our approach with some numerical experiments on practical tasks (Section

According to (

It represents the set of points that satisfy all the

2.3
Upper and lower models

We derive now global models of the objective
F
that ultimately lead to subproblems of (

), either yielding upper bounds

or lower bounds

on the optimal value

such that the value of the models and the value of

Upper models

Let

. For both

and

, all the

are included in the model. In general,

but equality holds, for example, if all the

, one only chooses a minimal value among

in

for

, hence the inequality

F^{(H,[m])}(x)\geq F(x)\quad\forall x\in\text{dom}\,F^{(H,[m])}.

(33)

If
\mathcal{A}(x)\cap H=\emptyset
for every

,

is not tight since
pieces
from
H
are nowhere

on the feasible set.
Minimizing both sides of (

) on
\mathcal{X}
yields

\hat{F}(H):=\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\,F^{(H,[m])}(x)=\min_{i\,\in\,H}\,\nu^{(i)}\geq F^{*}.

(34)

Lower models

Symmetrically to (

) where all the
constraints
are considered in
S
in the expression

, here one keeps all the

in

. For any

,

matches

in the sense that either

We end this section by proving a lemma providing a convenient sufficient condition (

as understood in Definition

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
i^{*}\in[n]
,
\rho\geq 0
and
x^{*}\in\arg\min_{x\,\in\,\mathcal{X}}\,F^{(\{i^{*}\},[m])}(x)
. If it holds

\min_{x\,\in\,\mathcal{X}}\,F^{(\{i\},[m])}(x)=\nu^{(i)}\geq\nu^{(i^{*})}=F^{(\{i^{*}\},[m])}(x^{*})\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})

(37)

then
x^{*}
is a local optimum of (

).

Proof.

Let
\rho\geq 0
be fixed, Proposition
1.4
implies the existence of a radius
\alpha>0
such that
\mathcal{A}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})
for every
x\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)
. By Definition
1.3
of

,

F(x)=\min_{i\,\in\,[n]}\,f^{(i)}(x)=\min_{i\,\in\,\mathcal{A}(x)}\,f^{(i)}(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{D}^{([m])}.

Combining both last statements, we can write that for any
x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{([m])}
,

F(x)=\min_{i\,\in\,\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})}\,f^{(i)}(x).

Moreover, we notice that

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)}\,F(x)

\displaystyle=\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{([m])}}\,F(x)

\displaystyle=\min_{i\,\in\,\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})}\,\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{([m])}}\,f^{(i)}(x)

\displaystyle=\min_{i\,\in\,\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})}\,\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)}\,F^{(\{i\},[m])}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{i\,\in\,\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})}\,\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\,F^{(\{i\},[m])}(x)=\min_{i\,\in\,\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(x^{*})}\,\nu^{(i)}.

\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathbb{B}_{2}(x^{*}\,;\,\alpha)}\,F(x)\geq F^{(\{i^{*}\},[m])}(x^{*})\geq F(x^{*}).

The last inequality comes from the fact that

\text{dom}\,F
.

Upper-Lower Optimization algorithm

We postpone the full description of our Upper-Lower Optimization algorithm (

3.1
Rationale

Looking back at the motivating examples and, more broadly, at the core definition of (
MS
), one could ask which
functional constraints
really influence the value of
F^{*}
.

Recalling Assumption
1
(B), the minimization of any
piece

f^{(i)}
on
\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{([m])}
yields a well-defined
x^{*}(i):=x^{*}(\{i\},[m])
. The set of
active constraints
at

is

\mathcal{C}(i)=\{j\in[m]\,|\,c^{(j)}(x^{*}(i))=0\}.

(38)

Removing the

does not alter the optimization, i.e.

\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{([m])}}\,f^{(i)}(x)=\nu^{(i)}=\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{(\mathcal{C}(i))}}\,f^{(i)}(x).

(39)

The naive
enumeration
visits the candidates
x^{*}(i)
for
i=1,\dots,n
without keeping in memory which
constraints
were active at previously computed
x^{*}(1),,\dots,x^{*}(i-1)
.

A global minimum

of (

\{x^{*}(i)\}_{i=1}^{n}
for a certain
i^{*}\in[n]
.

Two favourable situations can occur at this stage. When constraints are fairly different from each other, e.g. affine and linearly independent, most likely only a small subset of them will be active at a given
x^{*}(i)
. For instance, if the problem defining
x^{*}(i)
is an LP, usually
|\mathcal{C}(i)|\in\Theta(d)

[
10
]
, which can be significantly smaller than

.

It could also happen that sets
\mathcal{C}(i)
share a substantial part of their indices in common, lowering considerably the cardinality of their union (
40
). Based on both these statements, if one was able to detect the overall union of
active constraints
sets

\bar{\mathcal{C}}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\,\mathcal{C}(i),

(40)

then even the baseline
enumeration
scheme efficiency could be enhanced in the sense that every

could be obtained for less computational efforts as

x^{*}(i)=\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\,\cap\,\mathcal{D}^{(\bar{\mathcal{C}})}}\,f^{(i)}(x)=x^{*}(\{i\},\bar{\mathcal{C}}).

In this paper, we intend in this paper to iteratively build a set
S\subseteq[m]
of indices that will serve as surrogate to
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
. Roughly speaking, the minimization of upper models (
33
) will give us new candidates

with new active indices
\mathcal{C}(\bar{i})
to be added to our set

. Furthermore, we also keep track of visited indices in

to avoid testing a

twice. The minimization of lower models (

) includes a minimization pass over every

not in
H
but only taking into account constraints belonging to set

, ideally close from set
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
. By minimizing both upper and lower models, we obtain accordingly upper and lower bounds on
F^{*}
that hopefully allow us to skip testing most of the
pieces
in the

Restarted Alternating Minimization

As developed in the previous section, starting from a piece

that were not visited yet. This corresponds to phase (a) of

Every

and

Conversely, fixing

and minimizing over

Phase (b) has been devised to produce meaningful bounds on

and provide a clever restart of phase (a), which are the main principles behind

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have introduced and motivated the framework of
minimum structured
optimization problems, dubbed as (
MS
). Although solvable in a straightforward fashion by a simple
enumeration scheme
, we have shown how global upper/lower models could be of help to significantly reduce its computational complexity. Based on these models, we have devised and analyzed a new global optimization algorithm,
ULO
, for which exactness guarantees are provided. We have first conducted theoretical experiments to assess when
ULO
should exhibit a better performances than the
enumeration scheme
. Then we have also tested
ULO
and another local-search heuristic, on a practical optimization task. The results are very convincing,
ULO
is definitely the algorithm of choice for instances of (
MS
) linked to bigger ratios
m/n
, as is typically the case for
optimistic robust
linear programs (Example
1.1
). We leave the possible parallelization of phase (a) and (b) in
ULO
with asynchronous communication steps for further research, which could enhance even more its practical performance.

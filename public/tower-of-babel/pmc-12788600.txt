Comparative Evaluation of Autograft and Freeze-Dried Bone Allograft in the Treatment of Intrabony Defects: A Clinico-Radiographic Study
Authors: Prajwal S. Tomar, Amit Wadhawan, Prashant Tyagi, Manini Singh, Nidhi Yadav
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Periodontal intrabony defects are a key therapeutic target in regenerative periodontal therapy. Autografts and freeze-dried bone allografts (FDBA) are widely used for grafting purposes.Aim:To clinically and radiographically compare the efficacy of autograft and FDBA in the treatment of intrabony defects over 12 months.Materials and Methods:A total of 24 systemically healthy patients with chronic periodontitis presenting with comparable intrabony defects were randomly allocated into two groups: Group A (Autograft) and Group B (FDBA). Clinical parameters—gingival index (GI), plaque index (PI), oral hygiene index-simplified (OHI-S), probing pocket depth (PPD), and relative attachment level (RAL)—were recorded at baseline, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. Radiographic assessment using cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) was performed at baseline and 12 months to evaluate percentage bone fill.Results:Both groups demonstrated statistically significant improvements in all clinical parameters (P < 0.05). Group A showed a slightly greater reduction in PPD and gain in RAL compared to Group B at 12 months. CBCT analysis revealed a mean bone fill of 58.6% in Group A and 50.3% in Group B.Conclusion:Both autograft and FDBA showed clinically and radiographically favorable outcomes in intrabony defect regeneration. However, autograft demonstrated slightly superior efficacy, though the difference was not statistically significant.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontal regeneration remains a cornerstone in the management of advanced periodontitis. Among the regenerative techniques, the use of bone grafts is well established for the treatment of intrabony defects.[1] Autografts, being osteogenic, osteoinductive, and osteoconductive, are considered the gold standard. However, limitations, such as donor site morbidity and limited availability, have prompted the use of alternatives like allografts.[2]

Freeze-dried bone allograft (FDBA) is a commonly used allograft with good osteoconductive potential. Although not inherently osteoinductive, it serves as an excellent scaffold for bone regeneration.[3] Advanced imaging techniques, like cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT), allow precise evaluation of bone defect fill post-treatment.[4]

This study aims to clinically and radiographically compare the regenerative efficacy of autograft and FDBA in the management of periodontal intrabony defects over a 12-month period.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This randomized, controlled, single-blinded clinical study included 24 patients aged 25–50 years diagnosed with chronic periodontitis, each with at least one comparable two- or three-walled intrabony defect. Ethical clearance was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee, and informed consent was secured from all participants.

Radiographic evaluation

CBCT scans were obtained at baseline and 12 months. Bone fill was calculated using pre- and postoperative defect depth measurements, expressed as percentage bone fill.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Paired t-tests and unpaired t-tests were used. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrates that both autograft and FDBA are effective in managing periodontal intrabony defects. Autografts showed slightly superior results, aligning with previous studies highlighting their osteogenic capacity.[5]

FDBA, though lacking osteogenic potential, provided satisfactory outcomes, likely due to its osteoconductive nature and biocompatibility.[6] CBCT proved valuable in objectively assessing bone fill, as supported by Kamburoglu et al.[4]

Findings are consistent with the literature, suggesting that while autografts may offer marginally better results, FDBA remains a viable, less invasive alternative.[78]

CONCLUSION

Both autografts and FDBA produced significant clinical and radiographic improvements in periodontal intrabony defects. Autografts showed marginally higher efficacy in terms of bone fill and clinical attachment gain. CBCT remains an invaluable tool for accurate radiographic assessment. Further large-scale studies with histological correlation are recommended.

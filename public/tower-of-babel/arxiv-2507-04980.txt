Quaternionic Kolyvagin systems and Iwasawa theory for Hida families
Francesco Zerman

Abstract
. We build a modified universal Kolyvagin system for the Galois representation attached to a Hida family of modular forms, starting from the big Heegner point Euler system of Longo–Vigni [ 24 ] built in towers of Shimura curves. We generalize the work of Büyükboduk [ 2 ] to a quaternionic setting, relaxing the classical Heegner hypothesis on the tame conductor of the family. As a byproduct of this construction, we give a proof of one divisibility of the anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for Hida families.

Introduction

Let
N
be a positive integer,

be a prime number and
\omega\colon(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\bm{\mu}_{p-1}
be the Teichmüller character. Let

f(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)q^{n}\in S_{k}\big(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\omega^{j}\big)

be an ordinary
p
-stabilized newform, where

. Assume that the residual representation attached to
f
is absolutely irreducible and
p
-distinguished (see Assumption
2.1
).

In this paper, we are interested in the study of the Galois representation attached to the
p
-adic
Hida family
passing through

. Thanks to the work of Hida
[
15
,
16
]
, this is a free rank-

module
\bm{\mathrm{T}}
over a
2

\mathcal{R}
of residue characteristic

of
\mathbb{Q}
. This
big
representation is related to the Galois representations attached to the cusp forms that lie in the Hida family via
arithmetic specializations
, as we briefly recall in Section

Fix an imaginary quadratic field

of discriminant prime to
Np
and class number prime to
p
. We assume that

satisfies the
generalized Heegner hypothesis
with respect to

(see Assumption

), i.e. that

where the primes dividing

are split in

and
N^{-}
consists in the square-free product of an even number of primes that are inert in
K
. In this setting, Longo–Vigni
[
24

built a set of
big Heegner classes

\kappa_{c}\in H^{1}(K[c],\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger})

for every positive integer

coprime with

, where

is the ring class field of

of conductor

and
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}
is the self-dual twist of the representation

(see Definition

). These classes satisfy some rigidity properties (see
[
24

) and can be easily modified (see Definition

) into a

-complete anticyclotomic Euler system, in the sense of
[
26
]
.

One of the goals of this paper is to show how one can use these classes to study some conjectures on the anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory for
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}
. Let
K_{\infty}/K
be the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
, set
\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}:=\Gal(K_{\infty}/K)
and denote by
K_{t}/K
the subextension of

of degree

, for every

. One of the main topics of Iwasawa theory is the study of the structure of

. By Shapiro’s lemma (see Section

), we shift the above problem to the study of the arithmetic of the anticyclotomic twist
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}:=\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}\llbracket\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}\rrbracket
over
K
.

Under some technical assumptions on the ramification of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}
at the primes dividing
Np
(see Assumptions
4.1
and
4.4
) together with a big image assumption (Assumption
4.13
), in Section
4
we pursue a suitable Kolyvagin’s descent to the big Heegner classes

. This process, inspired by the seminal works of Kolyvagin
[
20
,
22
,
21
]
and later refined in the anticyclotomic setting by Howard
[
17
]
for elliptic curves, Nekovář
[
29
]
and Longo–Vigni
[
25
]
for modular forms and Büyükboduk
[
2
]
for non-quaternionic families of modular forms, gives rise to an explicit set of elements (see Definition
4.10
)

(1)

\bm{\kappa}(n)_{t,m,s}\in H^{1}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s,t}),

defined over some finite quotients
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s,t}
of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
(defined in Section
2.7
) and with
n
varying in the set of square-free products of primes
\ell
that are inert in
K
, coprime with
Np
and such that the arithmetic Frobenius
\Fr_{\ell}
at
\ell
is conjugated with the complex conjugation in
\Gal(K(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})/\mathbb{Q})
.

The main result of this paper is to show that a slight perturbation of these classes forms a modified universal Kolyvagin system. This notion, studied in detail in
[
26
]
, is a generalization of the classical notion of (universal) Kolyvagin system, first defined in
[
28
]
and later studied by many other authors, e.g.
[

,
3
]
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}
be the Greenberg Selmer structure on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
over

(see Definition

be a suitable set of primes that are inert in

and denote by
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
the set of square-free products of elements of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. For every couple
(n,\ell)
with
n\ell\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
and
\ell
prime, let
\chi_{n,\ell}
be an automorphism of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. In Section
3
we recall the definition of the module of (modified) universal Kolyvagin systems for the quadruple
(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
, to be denoted by
\overline{\bm{\ks}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
. The automorphisms
\{\chi_{n,\ell}\}
are used to modify the classical notion of universal Kolyvagin system (see
[
3
, Definition 3.4]
). Then, the main result of this paper reads as follows.

Theorem A

(Theorem
4.17
)
.

There is an infinite set of primes
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, a set of automorphisms
\{\chi_{n,\ell}\}
of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and a universal Kolyvagin system
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}\in\overline{\bm{\ks}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
such that

\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)=\varprojlim_{t}\big(\Cor^{K[p^{t+1}]}_{K_{t}}U_{p}^{-t}\kappa_{p^{t+1}}\big)\in H^{1}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}),

where
U_{p}\in\mathcal{R}^{\times}
is the
p
-th Hecke operator.

Sections
4.3
–
4.5
are devoted to the proof of this theorem. There, we explicitly define the set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of admissible primes (see Definition
4.14
), the automorphisms
\chi_{n,\ell}
and the modified universal Kolyvagin system
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}
(see Section
4.5
), built after the classes (
1
). Usually, it is not possible to massage a modified universal Kolyvagin system into a classical universal Kolyvagin system, and we don’t have any evidence that this can be done in our setting. However, in Section
5
, we show that there is no detectable difference between the two notions when it comes to applications.

Indeed, in the last section we explain how the existence of a non-trivial modified universal Kolyvagin system yields to the proof of one divisibility of the big Heegner point main conjecture for
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
(
[
24
, Conjecture 10.8]
) if we assume that
\mathcal{R}
is regular (Assumption
5.1
). Let
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}:=\mathcal{R}\llbracket\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}\rrbracket
and
\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}:=\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\vee}
, where
(\bullet)^{\vee}:=\Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}(\bullet,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is the Pontryagin dual. For
X
equal to
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
or
\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
, denote by
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,X)
the Greenberg Selmer module of
X
over
K
. If
M
is a finitely generated torsion
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-module, its
characteristic ideal

\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(M)=\prod_{\hight\mathfrak{p}=1}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{length}_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}

classifies
M
up to pesudo-isomorphism, where the product runs over the set of high-one primes of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. Our second main result reads as follows.

Theorem B

(Theorem
5.9
)
.

Assume that the system
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}\in\overline{\bm{\ks}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
of Theorem
A
is such that
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)\neq 0
. Then

(a)

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
is a torsion-free
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-module of rank 1.

(b)

\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}\big(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\vee}_{\mathrm{tors}}\big)\supseteq\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}\big(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})/\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}\cdot\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)\big)^{2}
.

The proof of this theorem follows closely the arguments of Fouquet
[
12
, §5-6]
. If we further assume that
p\nmid\varphi(N)
, the work of Cornut–Vatsal
[
8
]
(see also
[
18
, Corollary 3.1.2]
) implies that
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)\neq 0
, therefore the statement of Theorem

simplifies. We conclude by observing that the full big Heegner point main conjecture was obtained by Castella–Wan
[
5

a few years ago in a similar setting, but with some remarkable differences in the assumptions. However, the focus of this paper is mainly on the construction of the modified universal Kolyvagin system and on its usage.

2.
Big Galois representations and big Heegner classes

In this section we study the big Galois representation attached to a Hida family of cusp forms and we review the definition of the big Heegner classes of
[
24
]
, which live in the cohomology of the self-dual twist of this representation.

2.1.
Setting

Let
N^{-}
and
N^{+}
be coprime positive integers, with
N^{-}
consisting of the square-free product of an even number of primes. Let
N=N^{+}N^{-}
and fix a prime
p\nmid 6N
. Fix once and for all embeddings of algebraic closures
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Call
\bm{\mu}_{p-1}
the group of
(p-1)
-st roots of unity. Define
\omega\colon(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\bm{\mu}_{p-1}
to be the Teichmüller character, where we see
\bm{\mu}_{p-1}
both inside
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let

f(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)q^{n}\in S_{k}\big(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\omega^{j}\big)

be a normalized cusp form of weight
k\geq 2
and character
\omega^{j}
, which is an eigenform for the Hecke operators
T_{\ell}
for
\ell\nmid Np
and
U_{\ell}
for
\ell\mid Np
. Here
j
is an integer that necessarily satisfies
j\equiv k\pmod{2}
. Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which contains all Fourier coefficients of
f
and let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be its valuation ring. We assume further that
f
is an ordinary
p
-stabilized newform, in the sense that
a_{p}(f)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
and that the conductor of
f
is divisible by
N
(see
[
37
, Section 2.4]
).

According to Deligne
[
10
]
, there is a representation

\rho_{f}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\GL(T_{f})\cong\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\subseteq\GL_{2}(F)

of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
into a
2
-dimensional free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module
T_{f}
on which the arithmetic Frobenius
\Fr_{\ell}
at any prime
\ell\nmid Np
has characteristic polynomial

X^{2}-a_{\ell}(f)X+\omega^{j}(\ell)\ell^{k-1}.

If
\pi_{F}
is a uniformizer for
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the
residual
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-representation of
\rho_{f}
is the composition

\bar{\rho}_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\GL(T_{f})\longrightarrow\GL(T_{f}/\pi_{F}T_{f}).

The representation
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is said to be
p
-distinguished
if its restriction to a decomposition group
\mathfrak{D}_{p}
at
p
can be put in the shape
\bar{\rho}_{f}|_{\mathfrak{D}_{p}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon_{1}&*\\
0&\varepsilon_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for characters
\varepsilon_{1}\neq\varepsilon_{2}
(see
[
13
, Definition 4]
). Notice that, since
f
is
p
-ordinary, it is a result due to Mazur and Wiles that
\bar{\rho}_{f}|_{\mathfrak{D}_{p}}
is always upper triangular (see
[
13
, §1]
).

Assumption 2.1
.

Assume that the residual
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-representation
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible and
p
-distinguished.

Since
p\neq 2
, the representation
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible if and only if it is absolutely irreducible (see
[
37
, Remark 2.5]
).

Assumption 2.2
.

Fix an imaginary quadratic field
K
different from
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
with the following properties:

(a)

The class number of
K
is prime to
p
.

(b)

The discriminant of
K
is prime to
Np
.

(c)

All primes dividing
N^{+}
(respectively,
N^{-}
) are split (respectively, inert) in
K
.

2.2.
Hida families

For the convenience of the reader, we recall the construction of the branch of the Hida family where
f
lives. For simplicity, we mainly follow
[
18
, §2.1]
,
[
24
, §5]
and
[
31
, §1.4]
. The reader may also refer to
[
15
,
16
]
and
[
30
, §12.7]
for more details. To start with, let’s abbreviate

\Gamma:=1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{F}:=\mathcal{O}_{F}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F}\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rrbracket

and fix a profinite generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. For every
r\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
, let
\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}
be the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra generated by all Hecke operators
T_{\ell}
for
\ell\nmid Np
, together with the operators
U_{\ell}
for
\ell\mid Np
and the diamond operators
\langle d\rangle
for
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, acting on the space of cusp forms
S_{r}(\Gamma_{0}(N)\cap\Gamma_{1}(p^{m}),\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. We make
\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}
into an
\mathcal{O}_{F}\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rrbracket
-algebra via the map

[z]\longmapsto z^{r-2}\langle z\rangle

for every
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, where we indicate with square brackets the group elements in
\mathcal{O}_{F}\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rrbracket
. Hida’s ordinary projector
e^{\ord}:=\lim_{s}U_{p}^{s!}
defines an idempotent in each
\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}
and these are compatible with respect to the natural surjections
\mathfrak{h}_{r,m+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}
. If we define
\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}^{\ord}:=e^{\ord}\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}
, the algebra

\mathfrak{h}_{r}^{\ord}:=\varprojlim_{m}\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}^{\ord}

is finite over
\Lambda_{F}
and the image of
U_{p}
is invertible in
\mathfrak{h}_{r}^{\ord}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For every
r\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
, define

•

\omega_{r,m}=[\gamma]^{p^{m-1}}-\gamma^{(r-2)p^{m-1}}\in\Lambda_{F}
;

•

P_{r,\varepsilon}=[\gamma]-\varepsilon(\gamma)\gamma^{r-2}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}[\varepsilon]\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
for every homomorphism
\varepsilon\colon\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{m-1}}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
.

An elementary computation shows that
\omega_{r,m}=\prod_{\varepsilon}P_{r,\varepsilon}
, where the product is taken over all homomorphisms
\varepsilon:\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{m-1}}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
. We now recall the following structure theorem about big ordinary Hecke algebras.

Theorem 2.4

(Hida)
.

Let
r,r^{\prime}\geq 2
.

(a)

There is a canonical isomorphism of
\Lambda_{F}
-algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{r}^{\ord}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{r^{\prime}}^{\ord}
that sends Hecke operators
T_{\ell}
,
U_{\ell}
and
d^{r-2}\langle d\rangle
to
T_{\ell}
,
U_{\ell}
and
d^{r^{\prime}-2}\langle d\rangle
, respectively.

(b)

The canonical map
\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{\ord}\overset{\cong}{\to}\mathfrak{h}_{r}^{\ord}\to\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}^{\ord}
induces an isomorphism

\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{\ord}/\omega_{r,m}\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{\ord}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{h}_{r,m}^{\ord}

for every

.

Proof.

See
[
31
, Proposition 1.4.3]
.
∎

From now on, we will then work with the algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\ord}:=\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{\ord}
. By duality, the fixed cuspform
f
determines an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra map

(2)

\theta_{f}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{\ord}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{k,1}^{\ord}\to\mathcal{O}_{F}

characterized by

for
\ell\nmid Np
,
\theta_{f}(U_{\ell})=a_{\ell}(f)
for
\ell\mid Np
and
\theta_{f}([\gamma])=\gamma^{k-2}
. By
[

, Corollary 7.6]
, the
\Lambda_{F}
-algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\ord}
decomposes as the direct sum of its completions at maximal ideals, and we let
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}^{\ord}
be the unique local summand through which
\theta_{f}
factors. It is also known (see
[
30
, §12.7.5]
or
[
15
, Corollary 1.4]
) that the localization of
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}^{\ord}
at
\ker(\theta_{f})
is a discrete valuation ring, unramified over the localization of
\Lambda_{F}
at
\Lambda_{F}\cap\ker(\theta_{f})
. Therefore, there is a unique minimal prime
\mathfrak{a}_{f}
contained in
\ker(\theta_{f})
. We set

.

Definition 2.5
.

The integral closure
\mathcal{R}
of
R_{f}
is called the
branch
of the Hida family where
f
lives. We denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}}
the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{R}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Our choice to work with the normal ring
\mathcal{R}
in place of
R_{f}
is done in order to follow
[
24
]
. Howard
[
18
]
, instead, works directly with the ring

. However, for the purpose of this article, there is no substantial difference between the two choices. For more on the relations between these two rings and equivalent constructions, see
[
15
,
31
]
and
[

, §3.2]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The ring
\mathcal{R}
is a complete local Noetherian domain which is finitely generated over
\Lambda_{F}
. Moreover, it is a Cohen-Macaulay ring, free over
\Lambda_{F}
.

Proof.

For the first part, see
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
. Since
\mathcal{R}
has Krull dimension
2
and is integrally closed, Serre’s criterion for normality implies that it is a Cohen-Macaulay ring. By miracle flatness, we conclude that
\mathcal{R}
is flat (hence free) over
\Lambda_{F}
.
∎

2.3.
Arithmetic primes

Let
A
be a finite commutative
\Lambda_{F}
-algebra. An
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra map
A\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is called

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A^{\times}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma\mapsto[\gamma]}

\gamma\mapsto\psi(\gamma)\gamma^{r-2}

\psi

of

As noted in

are exactly all primes lying above

for some

,
F^{\prime}
finite extension of

and
\varepsilon:\Gamma\to\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}}

Given an arithmetic prime

has the form
\gamma\mapsto\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\gamma)\gamma^{r_{\mathfrak{p}}-2}
for a finite order character
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\Gamma\to F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
, called the

\mathfrak{p}
, and an integer

.

2.4.
Big Galois representations

For every integer

Lemma 2.21
.

Let

[

(b) By Lemma

(c) Clear from point (b).

(d) We prove the equality only for

2.8.
Galois invariants on abelian extensions of
K

The following lemma is a variant of Nakayama’s lemma.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
A
be a local Noetherian ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and
T
be a finitely generated
A
-torsion-free module. Let also
G
be a topological group that acts continuously and
A
-linearly on
T
. If
H^{0}(G,T/\mathfrak{m}_{A}T)=\{0\}
, then
H^{0}(G,T)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

The submodule
(T^{G}+\mathfrak{m}_{A}T)/\mathfrak{m}_{A}T
is contained in
(T/\mathfrak{m}_{A}T)^{G}
, hence it is trivial. This implies that
T^{G}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{A}T
. Then, we can write any nonzero element
x\in T^{G}
as
x=ay
with
a\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in T
. For every
\sigma\in G
, one has the relation

0=\sigma(ay)-ay=a(\sigma(y)-y).

Since
T
is
A
-torsion-free, we obtain that
\sigma(y)-y=0
, i.e.
y\in T^{G}
. Hence,
T^{G}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{A}T^{G}
. Since
T
is finitely generated over the Noetherian ring
A
, then also
T^{G}
is finitely generated over
A
, and we conclude by applying Nakayama’s lemma.
∎

We now apply this criterion to show that some relevant representations have no invariants over the absolute Galois group of some ring class fields. Once again, fix
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

and call
D_{K}
the discriminant of
K
.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let

be a positive integer coprime with

and let

be any of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m}
,
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s}
and
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}
. Then
H^{0}(K[n],T)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be one of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m}
or
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s}
and denote by
\bar{T}
the residual

-representation of

, which is a free module of rank

over a finite field, unramified outside

. Let

and let

be a prime that ramifies in

. Since the extension

is unramified outside

, we must have that

. From the fact that the extension

By Lemma

Let

Let now

3.
Selmer structures and Kolyvagin systems

The aim of this section is to give the definition of
modified universal Kolyvagin system
for the representation
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. This is the slight generalization of the classical notion of universal Kolyvagin system (see
[
28
,
17
,
3
]
) used and studied in
[
26
]
.

3.1.
Local conditions and Selmer structures

Let
L
be a finite extension of
K
, let
R
be a complete local Noetherian ring with finite residue field of characteristic
p
and let
M
be an
R\llbracket G_{L}\rrbracket
-module which is finitely generated and free as an
R
-module and unramified outside
Np
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
local condition

\mathcal{F}
on
M
at a place
w
of
L

is the choice of an
R
-submodule
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(L_{w},M)\subseteq H^{1}(L_{w},M)
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
w
is an archimedean prime of
L
, then
L_{w}=\mathbb{C}
and so
H^{1}(L_{w},M)=\{0\}
. Therefore, the choice of local conditions at archimedean primes is uniquely determined and will not play a role.

Definition 3.3
.

Given an
R\llbracket G_{L}\rrbracket
-submodule (resp. quotient)
N
of
M
and a local condition
\mathcal{F}
on
M
, the
propagated condition
on
N
is the preimage (resp. image) of
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(L_{w},M)
under the natural map
H^{1}(L_{w},N)\to H^{1}(L_{w},M)
(resp.
H^{1}(L_{w},M)\to H^{1}(L_{w},N)
).

Definition 3.4
.

A
Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}
on
M
is a collection of local conditions on
M
at every prime
w
of
L
. The
Selmer module
attached to
\mathcal{F}
is

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(L,M):=\ker\bigg(H^{1}(L,M)\to\prod_{w}\frac{H^{1}(L_{w},M)}{H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(L_{w},M)}\bigg),

where
w
runs over all places of
L
.

We will be primarily concerned with local conditions of the following type.

(i)

The
unramified
local condition on
M
at
w
is

H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(L_{w},M):=\ker\big(H^{1}(L_{w},M)\xrightarrow{\res}H^{1}(L_{w}^{\mathrm{ur}},M)\big),

where
L_{w}^{\mathrm{ur}}
is the maximal unramified extension of
L_{w}
.

(ii)

If
L_{w}
has residue characteristic different from
p
, the
F
-transverse
local condition on
M
at
v
is

H^{1}_{F-\mathrm{tr}}(L_{w},M):=\ker\big(H^{1}(L_{w},M)\xrightarrow{\res}H^{1}(F,M)\big),

where
F
is a maximal totally tamely ramified abelian
p
-extension of
L_{w}
.

Let
K(\ell)
be the maximal
p
-subextension of
K[\ell]/K
. When
L=K
and
w=\lambda
is a prime of
K
that lies above an element of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
for some
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, local class field theory implies that
F=K(\ell)_{\lambda_{\ell}}
is a maximal totally tamely ramified abelian
p
-extension of
K_{\lambda}
, where
\lambda_{\ell}
is the unique prime of
K(\ell)
above
\lambda
(see
[
39
, Lemma 4.1.21]
). For simplicity, we set
H^{1}_{\mathrm{tr}}(K_{\lambda},M):=H^{1}_{F-\mathrm{tr}}(K_{\lambda},M)
and call it the
transverse condition
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
M
is unramified at
\lambda
, using the fact that
M
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module and that
K(\ell)_{\lambda_{\ell}}
is the maximal
p
-extension of
K_{\lambda}
contained in
K[\ell]_{\lambda_{\ell}}
, one can also easily prove that

H^{1}_{\mathrm{tr}}(K_{\lambda},M)=\ker\big(H^{1}(K_{\lambda},M)\xrightarrow{\res}H^{1}(K[\ell]_{\lambda_{\ell}^{\prime}},M)\big)

for every prime
\lambda_{\ell}^{\prime}
of
K[\ell]
above
\lambda_{\ell}
. For more details, see
[
39
, Lemma 4.2.8]
.

Definition 3.6
.

When
L_{w}
has residue characteristic different from
p
and
M
is unramified at
w
, we define the
singular quotient

H_{\mathrm{s}}^{1}(L_{w},M):=\frac{H^{1}(L_{w},M)}{H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(L_{w},M)}.

3.2.
Greenberg conditions

Let
v
be a place of
K
above
p
and fix an absolute decomposition group
\mathfrak{D}_{v}
for

. Since
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
is flat over
\mathcal{R}
, by tensoring the sequence in (
4

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
we obtain an exact sequence of

(7)

where

and
F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
are free
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-modules of rank

.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
K
. The
(strict) Greenberg Selmer structure

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}
on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
over

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}):=\begin{cases}H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\quad&\text{if $w\nmid p$}\\
\ker\big(H^{1}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\to H^{1}(L_{w},F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))\big)\quad&\text{if $w\mid p$}\end{cases}

where

runs over all places of

and the unnamed map is induced by (

In the same way, one defines the (strict) Greenberg Selmer structure for the representation
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}
, just using the exact sequence (
4
) in place of (

). Moreover, the Selmer structure

propagates (in the sense of Definition

) to the quotients
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m}
,
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s}
and

.

3.3.
Modified Kolyvagin systems

Fix now
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
t\geq 0
,
\ell\in\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
and call
\lambda=(\ell)
the prime of
K
above

. There is a chain of functorial isomorphisms

(8)

\phi_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{fs}}\colon H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\overset{\alpha_{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}\overset{\beta_{\lambda}^{-1}}{\longrightarrow}H^{1}_{\mathrm{s}}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{\ell}

that come from

.

Remark 3.8
.

As noticed in the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 1.1.7]
, the isomorphism
\alpha_{\lambda}
is given by evaluating cocycles at the Frobenius automorphism
\Fr_{\lambda}
, whereas
\beta_{\lambda}
is given by sending the class of
\xi\otimes\sigma
to
\xi(\tilde{\sigma})
, for any lift
\tilde{\sigma}\in\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/K_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{ur}})
of

. For more details, see also
[
26
, Lemma 2.8]
.

We need to twist the finite-singular isomorphism. Indeed, for every
\chi\in\Aut_{\mathcal{R}_{t,m,s}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})
, we define

\phi_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{fs}}(\chi)\colon H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\overset{\alpha_{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}\overset{\chi}{\longrightarrow}\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}\overset{\beta_{\lambda}^{-1}}{\longrightarrow}H^{1}_{\mathrm{s}}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{\ell}.

By
[
2
, Lemma 4.10]
(see also
[
28
, Lemma 1.2.4]
), there is a functorial splitting

H^{1}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})=H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\oplus H_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}),

so that
H_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})
projects isomorphically onto
H_{\mathrm{s}}^{1}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})
. We now use the transverse condition to modify existing Selmer structures.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Selmer structure on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}
over
K
. For every
n\in\mathcal{N}_{m,s}
, the
modified Selmer structure

\mathcal{F}(n)
on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}
is given by

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}(n)}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}):=\begin{cases}H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})&\text{if $v\nmid n$}\\
H^{1}_{\mathrm{tr}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})&\text{if $v\mid n$}\end{cases}

for every place
v
of
K
.

Definition 3.10
.

For every
n\in\mathcal{N}
set
\mathcal{G}(n)=\bigotimes_{\ell\mid n}\mathcal{G}_{\ell}
, where the tensor product runs over all primes dividing
n
.

Let now
\mathcal{F}
be a Selmer structure on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}
over
K
with the property that
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})=H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})
for every

. Following

and

\psi_{n\ell}^{\ell}\colon H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}(n\ell)}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\otimes\mathcal{G}(n\ell)\to H_{\mathrm{s}}^{1}(K_{\lambda},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s})\otimes\mathcal{G}(n\ell)

•

Let now
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}
be an infinite subset of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
and define
\mathcal{N}_{m,s}^{\prime}
to be the set of all squarefree products of primes that lie below elements of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}

(n,\ell)
with

prime and

\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}

Definition 3.11
.

A
Kolyvagin system
for the quadruple

with

(K2)

\psi_{n}^{\ell}(\chi_{n,\ell})(\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n)_{t,m,s})=\psi_{n\ell}^{\ell}(\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n\ell)_{t,m,s})

We denote by
\bm{\ks}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
the
\mathcal{R}_{t,m,s}
-module of all Kolyvagin systems for the quadruple
(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
.

Let now
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{1,1}
such that
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
is an infinite subset of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
for any

, and denote by
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
the set of square-free products of elements of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. For every couple
(n,\ell)
with
\ell
prime and
n\ell\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, let
\chi_{n,\ell}\in\Aut_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
and denote with the same letter the induced automorphism of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s,t}
, for every

and
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Selmer structure on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
over
K
with the property that
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})=H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})

.

Definition 3.12
.

The
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-module of

for

\overline{\bm{\ks}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\}):=\varprojlim_{t,m,s}\varinjlim_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\bm{\ks}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\}),

Assume now that

4.
The big Heegner point Kolyvagin system

In this chapter we show how to choose a suitable subset
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{P}_{1,1}
and build a modified universal Kolyvagin system for the triple
(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
, starting from the set of Longo–Vigni’s big Heegner classes recalled in Section
2.5
. Before starting the actual construction, we pursue a deeper study of the ramification of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
at primes dividing
Np
.

4.1.
Controlling ramification

Let
v
be a prime of
K
dividing
N
and let
\mathfrak{I}_{v}
be a fixed inertia group at
v
. For the rest of this article, we assume the following condition.

Assumption 4.1
.

For every
v\mid N
, the
\mathcal{R}
-module
H^{1}(\mathfrak{I}_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}})
is free.

This condition has been widely studied in
[
4
, §4.3]
, so we refer to that work for a discussion and equivalent formulations. Since
\mathfrak{I}_{v}
acts trivially on
\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
-\ \hat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}
is exact, we have that
H^{1}(\mathfrak{I}_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\cong H^{1}(\mathfrak{I}_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}})\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}
, therefore
H^{1}(\mathfrak{I}_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
is a free
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-module. Here we present an important consequence of Assumption
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

A ring homomorphism
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{R}\to S
induces an isomorphism
(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}S\cong(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}S)^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}
and the module
H^{1}(\mathfrak{I}_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}S)
is
S
-torsion-free. The same holds replacing
\mathcal{R}
with
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}
with
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
.

Proof.

Call
T_{S}=\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}S
. By
[
4
, Proposition 4.17]
and Assumption
4.1
, we obtain that
(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}S\cong T_{S}^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}
. Then, for every
x\in S\setminus\{0\}
, the map
T_{S}^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}\to(T_{S}/xT_{S})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}
is surjective. Taking the long exact sequence in cohomology of the multiplication by
x
, we obtain that the multiplication by
x
in
H^{1}(\mathfrak{I}_{v},T_{S})
is injective. The same argument applies replacing
\mathcal{R}
with
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}
with
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Assumption
4.1
can probably be translated into a condition on the representation attached to a single member of the Hida family passing through
f
. In particular, as suggested in
[
4
, Proposition 4.30]
, it should be enough to assume that the family passes through a form whose
p
-part of the Tamagawa factors at every prime dividing
N
is trivial.

We now study the behaviour at
p
. Inspired by
[
2
, Hypothesis H.stz]
, for the rest of this article we work under the following assumption.

Assumption 4.4
.

For every prime
v\mid p
of
K
we assume that

H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{{\dagger}}))=\{0\},

where
F_{v}^{-}(\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{{\dagger}}):=F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}}
.

By Lemma
2.20
, the residual
G_{K}
-representation
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{\dagger}
coincides with
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. Therefore, Assumption
4.4
is equivalent to assume that
H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))=\{0\}
.

Remark 4.5
.

Notice that
F_{v}^{-}(\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\cong F_{v}^{-}(\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{{\dagger}})
is a vector space of dimension
1
over a finite field. Moreover, the action of
G_{K_{v}}
on it factors through the product of characters
\eta_{v}\Theta^{-1}
, where
\eta_{v}\colon G_{K_{v}}\to\mathcal{R}^{\times}
is the unramified character defined by sending
\Fr_{v}
to

(see
[

, p. 300]
). Therefore, Assumption
4.4
is equivalent to requiring that the character
\eta_{v}\Theta^{-1}
is not identically congruent to

modulo
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}}
.

An important consequence of Assumptions
4.1
and
4.4
is an easier understanding of the Greenberg local conditions on the quotients of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

and

Definition 4.7

Fix now

4.3.
Kolyvagin primes and main result

The aim of the rest of this chapter is to show that a slight modification of the classes
\bm{\kappa}(n)_{t,m,s}
form a modified universal Kolyvagin system. In order to do this, besides our running assumptions, we will need the following big image assumption on the representation
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}}
. For a deeper look on this type of hypotheses, see
[
7
]
.

Assumption 4.13
.

The image of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
in
\Aut_{\mathcal{R}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{{\dagger}})
contains the scalars
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

We now need to shrink the set
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
of admissible primes. As a consequence of Lemma
2.23
, the two quantities
\ell+1\pm T_{\ell}
are divisible by
p^{s}
in
\mathcal{R}_{m}
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.14
.

For every
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
let
\mathcal{L}_{m,s}
be the set of primes of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
such that the two quantities
\frac{\ell+1\pm T_{\ell}}{p^{s}}
are units in
\mathcal{R}_{m}
. Define also

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{s\geq m>0}\mathcal{L}_{m,s}.

Set
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
and let
\mathcal{N}_{m,s}^{\prime}
be the set of all square-free products of elements of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

For every
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with
s\geq m
the set
\mathcal{L}_{m,s}
is infinite.

Proof.

First, notice that an element
r\in\mathcal{R}_{m}
is a unit if and only if its projection to one (hence any) nonzero quotient of
\mathcal{R}_{m}
is a unit.

Fix a prime
\ell_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
. By Lemma
2.24
, we have that

\Tr(\Fr_{\ell_{0}}\mid\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m})=(-1)^{\frac{k+j}{2}-1}T_{\ell_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\det(\Fr_{\ell_{0}}\mid\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m})=\ell_{0}.

Let now
\alpha\in 1+p^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\sigma_{\alpha}\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that its image in
\Aut(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m})
is the scalar
\alpha
, whose existence is granted by Assumption
4.13
. Then one obtains

\Tr(\Fr_{\ell_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}\mid\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m})=(-1)^{\frac{k+j}{2}-1}\alpha T_{\ell_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\det(\Fr_{\ell_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}\mid\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m})=\alpha^{2}\ell_{0}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{m,s}(\ell_{0},\alpha)
be the set consisting of those primes
\ell
such that
\Fr_{\ell}
is conjugated to
\Fr_{\ell_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}
in
\Gal(K(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,2s})/\mathbb{Q})
. By Chebotarev’s density theorem, the set
\mathcal{L}_{m,s}(\ell_{0},\alpha)
has infinite cardinality. For any
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{m,s}(\ell_{0},\alpha)
, one has

(11)

T_{\ell}\equiv\alpha T_{\ell_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell\equiv\alpha^{2}\ell_{0}

as elements of
\mathcal{R}_{m,2s}
. Reducing to
\mathcal{R}_{m,s}
the above congruences and using the fact that
\alpha\equiv 1\bmod p^{s}
, one easily sees that
\mathcal{L}_{m,s}(\ell_{0},\alpha)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
.

We now look for an
\alpha\in 1+p^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that the division of the two elements

(12)

\alpha^{2}\ell_{0}+1\pm\alpha T_{\ell_{0}}

by
p^{s}
is a unit of
\mathcal{R}_{m,2s}
. The existence of such an
\alpha
would imply that the set of primes
\mathcal{L}_{m,s}(\ell_{0},\alpha)\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{m,s}
, since, by (
11
), we have the congruences

\ell+1\pm T_{\ell}\equiv\alpha^{2}\ell_{0}+1\pm\alpha T_{\ell_{0}}

in
\mathcal{R}_{m,2s}
, and this would conclude the proof.

Writing
\alpha=1+p^{s}x
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, dividing the elements (
12
) by
p^{s}
and reducing them modulo the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{R}_{m}
, one finds that in order to get such an
\alpha
it is sufficient to require that the reduction of
x
avoids two explicit values. Since
p>2
, this is always possible.
∎

As a consequence of this lemma, we also have that the sets
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}
are infinite. If
\ell\in\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}
, let

be the unique positive integer such that such that
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{m,s(\ell)}
. In this case, thanks to Lemma
4.15
, the values
\frac{\ell+1\pm T_{\ell}}{p^{s(\ell)}}
are units in
\mathcal{R}_{m}
. Moreover, we have the following result.

Corollary 4.16
.

If
\ell\in\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime}
, the image of
\frac{(\ell+1)\Fr_{\ell}\pm T_{\ell}}{p^{s(\ell)}}
in
\Aut(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s})
is invertible.

Proof.

By point (b) of Definition

, we have that
\Fr_{\ell}
is conjugated to the complex conjugation
\tau_{c}
in
\Gal(K(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s})/\mathbb{Q})

\mathcal{R}_{m,s}
-basis of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s}
made of eigenvectors (with eigenvalues

and

) for the action of
\Fr_{\ell}
. Using this basis one can easily compute the determinants of the morphisms

and conclude that they are units.
∎

acts as the identity on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s}
, we can re-write the numerator of the coefficient of the right-hand side as
((\ell+1)\Fr_{\ell}-(-1)^{\frac{k+j}{2}-1}T_{\ell})\Fr_{\ell}
. Thanks to Corollary
4.16
, the coefficients of the left-hand side and the right-hand side are invertible endomorphisms of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s}
. Therefore, there is
\vartheta_{\lambda^{\prime}}\in\Aut(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s})
such that

\alpha_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{\kappa}^{\prime}(n)_{t,m,s})=\vartheta_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\beta_{\lambda^{\prime}}([\bm{\kappa}^{\prime}(n\ell)_{t,m,s}]_{\mathrm{s}}\otimes\sigma_{\ell}).

Since there were no constraints in the choice of the prime
\lambda_{n}
made at the beginning of the proof, the above relation holds for every prime
\lambda^{\prime}
of
K_{t}
above
\lambda
. We then conclude by applying semi-local Shapiro’s map to both sides.
∎

With this result in hand, we can conclude the proof of Theorem
4.17
.

Proof of Theorem
4.17
.

To begin with, let’s fix
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
t\geq 0
. For every couple
(n,\ell)
with
n\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{m,s}^{\prime}
and
\ell
prime, define
\chi_{n,\ell}:=\vartheta_{\ell}^{-1}
to be the inverse of the automorphisms that appear in Lemma
4.21
. Define

\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n)_{t,m,s}:=\bm{\kappa}(n)_{t,m,s}\otimes\bigotimes_{\ell^{\prime}\mid n}\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}\in H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{m,s,t})\otimes\mathcal{G}(n).

Combining Theorem
4.20
with Lemma
4.21
, we conclude that
\{\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n)_{t,m,s}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{t,m,s}^{\prime}}
is an element of
\bm{\ks}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
.

When varying
t,m,s
, we need to prove that these classes interpolate into a modified universal Kolyvagin system. First, the automorphisms
\chi_{n,\ell}
don’t depend on
t,m,s
. Indeed, fix
(n,\ell)
such that
\ell
is prime and
n\ell\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, and choose
t,t^{\prime},m,m^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}
with the property that
n\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{m,s}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{N}_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{\prime}
. Then, it is clear from the proof of Lemma
4.21
that the automorphisms
\vartheta_{\ell}=\chi_{n,\ell}^{-1}
on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s}
and
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t^{\prime},m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
both are the image of a unique automorphism
\vartheta_{\ell}
of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
.

Then, it is enough to check that, whenever
t^{\prime}\geq t
,
m^{\prime}\geq m
and
s^{\prime}\geq s
, the image of
\{\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n)_{t^{\prime},m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}}
under the natural map

\bm{\ks}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t^{\prime},m^{\prime},s^{\prime}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})\longrightarrow\bm{\ks}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{t,m,s},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})

coincides with
\{\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n)_{t,m,s}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}}
. When

, this follows easily from the fact that the classes
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(n)_{t^{\prime},m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and

are built as the image of the same big Heegner class

. When, instead,
m^{\prime}=m
,

4.12
.

Lastly, it is clear from the definitions that
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)_{t,m,s}=\bm{\kappa}(1)_{t,m,s}=(\sh_{t}\circ\Cor^{K_{t}[1]}_{K_{t}})(\mathfrak{z}(1)_{t,m,s})

.
∎

5.
Anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory

In this section we recover a classical consequence of the existence of a nontrivial Kolyvagin system to the Iwasawa theory of the representation
\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}
. In particular, following the ideas of
[
12
]
, we give a proof of one divisibility of the big Heegner point main conjecture in this context (see
[
24
, Conjecture 10.8]
). From now on, we will work under the following assumption.

Assumption 5.1
.

The ring
\mathcal{R}
is regular.

5.1.
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-modules

We start by describing in detail the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The rings
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
are complete Noetherian regular local integrally closed UFDs, whose height 1 prime ideals are principal.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
2.7
with Assumption
5.1
, we have that
\mathcal{R}
is a complete Noetherian regular local ring. By
[
27
, Theorem 3.3, Exercise 8.6, Theorem 19.5]
, the same is true for
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. Then, by Auslander-Buchsbaum’s theorem (see
[
27
, Theorem 20.3]
), both
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
are UFDs. Therefore, they are also integrally closed (see
[
27
, Example 9.1]
) and every high
1
prime ideal is principal by
[
27
, Theorem 20.1]
.
∎

There is a rich theory of pseudo-isomorphisms between finitely generated
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-modules (see e.g.
[
1
, §VII.4.4]
). For what is needed here, let’s recall that the
characteristic ideal
of a finitely generated torsion
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-module

is

\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(M)=\prod_{\hight\mathfrak{p}=1}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{length}_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})},

where the product runs over the set of high-one primes of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. This is a principal ideal of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
with the property that
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}/\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(M)
.

5.2.
Specializations

We shall now study the behaviour of Greenberg (and other) Selmer modules when applying some families of specialization maps, in the following sense.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a complete local Noetherian
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra with finite residue field of characteristic
p
.

(i)

An
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra morphism

, where

is a complete local Noetherian domain with finite residue field of characteristic

S-specialization
of

.

(ii)

If
T
is an
R
-module, denote by
T_{\mathfrak{s}}
(or

, when the map
\mathfrak{s}
is clear from the context) the
S
-module

.

Notice that we allow the case
\mathfrak{s}=\id\colon R\to R
. When
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}\to S
is a specialization of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
, define
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
,
F_{v}^{\pm}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
,
F_{v}^{\pm}(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
and

to be
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}\otimes_{S}\Frac(S)
,
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}\otimes_{S}S^{\vee}
,

One can define the Greenberg Selmer structure on

and on
\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}:=\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\id}
exactly as in Definition
3.7
. For any specialization
\mathfrak{s}
, this induces a Selmer structure on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
by propagation, to be denoted again by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}

Definition 5.4
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
K
,
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}\to S
be a specialization of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and let

be any of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. The Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}
on

over

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(L_{w},X_{\mathfrak{s}}):=\begin{cases}H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(L_{w},X_{\mathfrak{s}})\quad&\text{if $w\nmid p$}\\
\ker\big(H^{1}(L_{w},X_{\mathfrak{s}})\to H^{1}(L_{w},F_{w}^{-}(X_{\mathfrak{s}}))\big)\quad&\text{if $w\mid p$}\end{cases}

where

runs over all places of

.

When

we have that

, but in general they are different. Notice that the argument of Lemma
4.6
doesn’t work here because

(22)

5.3.
Comparison with other Selmer structures

Let now
X
be any of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. In this setting, Nekovář defined some
extended Selmer groups

\tilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(K,X)
using the theory of Selmer complexes. These Selmer groups are linked to the
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}
-Selmer groups via the exact sequence (see
[
30
, Lemma 9.6.3]
)

(23)

\bigoplus_{v\mid p}H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(X))\to\tilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(K,X)\to H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(K,X)\to 0.

We show that this exact sequence, combined with Assumption
4.4
, gives an isomorphism between the two Selmer groups in some relevant cases.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
X
be one of
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
or
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. Then

(i)

H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(X))=\{0\}
for every
v\mid p
;

(ii)

\tilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(K,X)\cong H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(K,X)
.

Proof.

Let
X=\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
. Since the residue field of
S
is finite, the residual representation of
F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})=F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\otimes_{\mathfrak{s}}S
is a finite base change of the 1-dimensional representation
F_{v}^{-}(\bar{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
, that has no
G_{K_{v}}
-invariants by Assumption
4.4
. Therefore,
H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})/\mathfrak{m}_{S})=\{0\}
, and we obtain point (i) by applying Lemma
2.25
. Point (ii) descends from (i) by the exact sequence (
23
).

Let now
M:=H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))^{\vee}
be the Pontryagin dual of
H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))
. By Nekovář’s duality theory (see the first paragraphs of
[
19
, §2]
for a compact treatment),
M
is isomorphic to the module of
G_{K_{v}}
-coinvariants of
\Hom_{\mathcal{R}}(F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}),S)
, that is a finitely generated
S
-module. Therefore, by Nakayama’s lemma, in order to show (i) it is enough to show that
M/\mathfrak{m}_{S}M=\{0\}
. For this, taking again Pontryagin duals, we obtain that

(M/\mathfrak{m}_{S}M)^{\vee}\cong M^{\vee}[\mathfrak{m}_{S}]=H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))[\mathfrak{m}_{S}]=H^{0}(K_{v},F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})[\mathfrak{m}_{S}]).

Now, the Galois representation
F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})[\mathfrak{m}_{S}]
is isomorphic to
F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})/\mathfrak{m}_{S}F_{v}^{-}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
, which was shown to have no
G_{K_{v}}
-invariants in the first part of the proof, therefore we obtain point (i). Point (ii) follows again from the exact sequence (
23
).
∎

When
S
is a discrete valuation ring (DVR) finite over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, there is the notion of the Bloch-Kato condition on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
, which we now recall following
[
12
, Definition 5.20]
. To begin with, notice that the natural maps
\iota\colon\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}}\to\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\eta\colon\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}\to\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
extend to maps in cohomology, to be denoted with the same name.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
K
and
\mathfrak{s}
be a specialization of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
to a discrete valuation ring
S
finite over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Define the
Bloch–Kato Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}
on
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
by setting the local conditions

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}\big(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}\big)=\begin{cases}\iota^{-1}\big(H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\big)\quad&\text{if $w\mid N$}\\
\iota^{-1}\big(H^{1}(L_{w},F_{v}^{+}(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))\big)\quad&\text{if $w\mid p$}\\
H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})&\text{if $w\nmid Np$}.\end{cases}

where

and

. Similarly, we define the
Bloch–Kato Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}
on
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})=\begin{cases}\eta\big(H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(L_{w},\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\big)\quad&\text{if $w\mid N$}\\
\eta\big(H^{1}(L_{w},F_{v}^{+}(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}))\big)\quad&\text{if $w\mid p$}\\

In general, thanks to
[
34
, Lemma 3.5]
, there are inequalities
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(L,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\subseteq H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(L,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
and
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(L,\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\supseteq H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(L,\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
. However, thanks to the assumptions of Section
4.1
, we have the following stronger result when

.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}\to S
be a specialization to a discrete valuation ring
S
finite over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Then

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})=H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\quad\text{and}\quad H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})=H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}).

Proof.

If
v
is a place of
K
not dividing
Np
, by
[
34
, Lemma 3.5]
the
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}
and the
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}
local conditions coincide at

. If

, by considering the
\mathfrak{I}_{v}
-cohomology of the exact sequence

and noting that the image of
(\bm{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}
in
(\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}
is the maximal divisible subgroup
(\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}_{\mathrm{div}}
, we obtain that
(\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}/(\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})^{\mathfrak{I}_{v}}_{\mathrm{div}}
injects into

, which is torsion-free thanks to Proposition
4.2
. Therefore, by point (iii) of
[
34
, Lemma 3.5]
, we conclude that
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})=H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(K_{v},\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
and

Assume now that

5.5
.
∎

5.4.

Following the arguments of

.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}\to S
be a specialization to a discrete valuation ring finite over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and let

is free of rank
1
and there exists a torsion module
M
finite over
S

\mathrm{length}_{S}(M)\leq\mathrm{length}_{S}\big(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})/S\cdot\bm{\kappa}_{\mathfrak{s}}(1)\big)

such that
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\cong\Frac(S)/S\oplus M\oplus M

Proof.

First notice that, if we show that
\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}
satisfy Hypotheses H.0-H.5 of
[
17
, §1.3]
, we can apply
[
17
, Theorem 1.6.1]
in order to conclude. Indeed,
[
17
, Lemma 1.6.2]
can be replaced by
[
26
, Lemma 2.39]
, and one can easily check that a modified universal Kolyvagin system has exactly the same usage of the Kolyvagin system appearing in the proof of
[

. Therefore, we are left to show that Hypotheses H.0-H.5 of

hold.

Hypothesis H.0 is true since

is free of rank 2 over
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}

has finite residue field, the residual representation

\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}

By Lemma

\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BK}}
on

Theorem 5.9
.

Assume that the system
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}\in\overline{\bm{\ks}}(\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\chi_{n,\ell}\})
of Theorem
4.17
is such that
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)\neq 0
. Then

(a)

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
is a torsion-free
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}
-module of rank 1.

(b)

\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}\big(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\vee}_{\mathrm{tors}}\big)\supseteq\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}\big(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})/\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}\cdot\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)\big)^{2}
.

Proof.

The proof of this fact is a direct consequence of the arguments of
[
12
, §6]
(see in particular the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 6.3]
). We explain in detail how our setting fits in Fouquet’s theory.

(a) By Lemma
5.5
, we have the equality
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})\cong\tilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
. Therefore, the torsion-freeness of
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})
is a consequence of
[
12
, Proposition 6.2.(i)]
(or of
[
33
, §1.3.3]
, as explained in
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
). Moreover, the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 6.5]
goes along with Corollary 5.21 of
loc. cit.
replaced by our Lemma
5.8
, giving the rank-1 result. Notice that the number
r
that appears in the statement of
[
12
, Proposition 6.2.(i)]
is zero thanks to Assumption
4.4
.

(b) Assume that
\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}})/\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1))^{2}\not\subseteq\Char_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ac}}}(H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Gr}}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\vee}_{\mathrm{tors}})
. Exactly as in the proof of

, by applying some density results one finds a specialization

to a DVR finite over

\mathrm{length}_{S}\big(\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{f}}^{1}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})^{\vee}_{\mathrm{tors}}\big)>2\cdot\mathrm{length}_{S}\big(\tilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(K,\bm{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})/\bm{\kappa}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathrm{ac}}(1)\big),

where
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is the image of
\bm{\kappa}^{\mathrm{ac}}
with respect to (

). However, as a consequence of the inequality of Lemma

, we have that

This is a contradiction, as
\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{f}}^{1}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})\cong H_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{s}}}^{1}(K,\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\mathfrak{s}})
and

∎

Appendix A
Explicit unramified Galois cohomology

Let
\ell\neq p
be two distinct odd primes and let
T
be an unramified
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\rrbracket
-module which is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Let
F
be a finite extension of the local field
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and let
\Fr_{F}\in\Gal(F^{\mathrm{ur}}/F)
be the Frobenius element, that is a profinite generator of
\Gal(F^{\mathrm{ur}}/F)\cong\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
T
is unramified, the action of
\Fr_{F}
on
T
is well defined. Denote by

\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\colon\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F)\twoheadrightarrow\Gal(F^{\mathrm{ur}}/F)

the natural projection. Then, to any
g\in\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F)
we can attach a unique exponent
u(g)\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}(g)=\Fr_{F}^{u(g)}
.

Let
x\in Z^{1}(F,T)
be a cocycle whose cohomology class
[x]
lies in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(F,T)
. Then, the cocycle
x
is inflated by a cocycle
\tilde{x}\in Z^{1}(F^{\mathrm{ur}}/F,T)
, up to summing a coboundary. Then, for every
g\in\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F)
such that
u(g)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
there is
b_{x}\in T
such that

(25)

x(g)=\tilde{x}(\Fr_{F}^{u(g)})+(g-1)b_{x}=(1+\Fr_{F}+\dots+\Fr_{F}^{u(g)-1})a_{x}+(\Fr_{F}^{u(g)}-1)b_{x},

where
a_{x}:=\tilde{x}(\Fr_{F})\in T
. In fact, the first equality holds for every
g\in\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F)
, while the requirement
u(g)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is needed only for the second equality. Via the isomorphism

H^{1}(F^{\mathrm{ur}}/F,T)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}T/(\Fr_{F}-1)T

induced by evaluating cocycles at
\Fr_{F}
(see
[
34
, Lemma B.2.8]
), the class
[x]
corresponds to the class of
a_{x}
in the codomain.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
x\in Z^{1}(F,T)
be an unramified cocycle as above and let
\tau\in\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F^{\mathrm{ur}})
. Then
\tau x=x
.

Proof.

We need to prove that
\tau x(\tau^{-1}g\tau)=x(g)
for every
g\in\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F)
. First, notice that
\tau x(\tau^{-1}g\tau)=x(\tau^{-1}g\tau)
, as
T
is unramified. Then, by the explicit description of (
25
), it is enough to show that
u(g)=u(\tau^{-1}g\tau)
, i.e. that
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}(g)=\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}(\tau^{-1}g\tau)
. The relation
\tau^{-1}g\tau=\tau^{-1}g\tau g^{-1}g
together with the normality of
\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F^{\mathrm{ur}})
in
\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/F)
yields that
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}(g)=\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}(\tau^{-1}g\tau)
, as desired.
∎

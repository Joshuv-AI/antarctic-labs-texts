Abstract
. Analysis of motion algorithms for autonomous systems operating under variable external conditions leads to the concept of parametrized topological complexity [ 4 ] . In [ 4 ] , [ 5 ] the parametrized topological complexity was computed in the case of the Fadell - Neuwirth bundle which is pertinent to algorithms of collision free motion of many autonomous systems in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} avoiding collisions with multiple obstacles. The parametrized topological complexity of sphere bundles was studied in detail in [ 11 ] , [ 12 ] , [ 10 ] . In this paper we make the next step by studying parametrized topological complexity of bundles of real projective spaces which arise as projectivisations of vector bundles. This leads us to new problems of algebraic topology involving theory of characteristic classes and geometric topology. We establish sharp upper bounds for parametrized topological complexity 𝖳𝖢 [ p : E → B ] {\sf TC}[p:E\to B] improving the general upper bounds. We develop algebraic machinery for computing lower bounds for 𝖳𝖢 [ p : E → B ] {\sf TC}[p:E\to B] based on the Stiefel - Whitney characteristic classes. Combining the lower and the upper bounds we compute explicitly many specific examples.

1.
Introduction

The concept of topological complexity is inspired by the robot motion planning problem of robotics
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
it reflects the
complexity of motion algorithms for autonomous systems. Parametrized topological complexity is a version of topological complexity adopted to situations when autonomous systems move under variable external conditions
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
. Mathematically, parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]
is a numerical invariant of a fibration
p:E\to B
where the base
B
represents the configuration space of the external conditions and
E
is the configuration space of the compound system consisting of the controlled system and the external conditions.

In this paper we analyse in detail the case when the bundle
p:E\to B
is obtained from a vector bundle
\xi
of rank
d+1
over a CW complex
B
by projectivisation, i.e. by taking the projective space of each fibre. The obtained
projective bundle
is a locally trivial bundle with fibre
{\mathbb{RP}}^{d}
, the
d
-dimensional real projective space, and our goal is to express its parametrized topological complexity through the classical invariants of the vector bundle
\xi
. The Stiefel - Whitney classes of
\xi
play a central role in our approach.

The techniques we employ are slightly different in the cases
d=1
and
d>1
, and we study these cases separately.

In §
3
we focus on the case
d=1
- bundles with fibre the circle. We show that the general upper bound of Proposition
4
is never sharp for bundles with fibre
S^{1}
. The main results of this section, Corollary
9
and Theorem
10
, give more informative upper bounds. Combining these upper bounds with the lower bound given by Theorem 2 from
[
11
]
we are able to compute many examples and evaluate precisely the parametrized topological complexity.
Material of this section is supported by Appendix
A
which is related to a result of Ghys
[
14
]
.

In section §
4
we study the cohomology algebras of projective bundles and derive the cohomological lower bound of Corollary
23
. This lower bound operates with algebraic structures built upon the cohomology algebra of the base
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
and the Stiefel - Whitney classes
w_{i}(\xi)
of the vector bundle
\xi
.

In the following section §

. The main result of this section is Theorem

2.
Parametrized topological complexity

In this section we briefly recall the notion of parametrized topological complexity which was introduced in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
.
It is a generalisation of the concept of topological complexity of robot motion planning problem,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
, which takes into account the state of external conditions.

Let
X
be a path-connected topological space viewed as the space of states of a mechanical system. The motion planning problem of robotics asks for an algorithm which takes as input an initial state and a desired state of the system, and produces as output a continuous motion of the system from the initial state to the desired state, see
[
17
]
.
Given a pair of states
(x_{0},x_{1})\in X\times X
, the algorithm should produce a continuous path
\gamma:I\to X
with

\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=x_{1}
, where
I=[0,1]
denotes the unit interval representing time.

Let
X^{I}
denote the space of all continuous paths in
X
, equipped with the compact-open topology.
The map
\pi:X^{I}\to X\times X
, where
\pi(\gamma)=(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))
, is a fibration in the sense of Hurewicz. A solution of the motion planning problem,
a motion planning algorithm
, is a section of this fibration, i.e. a map
s:X\times X\to X^{I}
with
\pi\circ s={\rm id}_{X\times X}
.
If
X
is not contractible, no section can be continuous, see
[
7
]
.

The topological complexity
of
X
is defined to be the sectional category, or Schwarz genus, of the fibration

\pi:X^{I}\to X\times X
; notation:
{\sf TC}(X)={\sf{secat}}(\pi)
. Explicitly,
{\sf TC}(X)
is the smallest integer
k
for
which there exists an open cover

X\times X=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}

such that
the fibration
\pi
admits a continuous section
s_{j}:U_{j}\to X^{I}
for each
j=0,1,\dots,k
.

In the parametrized setting initiated in
[
4
]
, one assumes that the motion of the system is constrained
by external conditions, such as obstacles or variable geometry of the containing domain.
The initial and terminal states of the system, as well as the motion between them, must live under the same external conditions.
This situation is modelled by a fibration

(1)

\displaystyle p:E\to B

with path-connected fibres, where the base
B
is a topological space encoding the variety of external conditions. For
b\in B
, the fiber

X_{b}=p^{-1}(b)
is the space of achievable configurations of the system given the constraints imposed by
b
.

A parametrized motion planning algorithm
takes as input the pairs of the initial and the terminal states of the system (consistent with identical external conditions), and produces a continuous motion between them, achievable under the same external conditions.
In other words, the initial and the terminal states, as well as the path between them produced by the algorithm, all lie within the same fibre of the fibration (
1
). The state of the external conditions is implicitly also part of the input, as it coincides with the image of the initial and the final states under the projection (
1
).

To define the notion of
parametrized topological complexity
of the fibration
p:E\to B
one needs to introduce
the associated fibration
\Pi:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{2}_{B},
where
E^{2}_{B}
denotes the space of all pairs of configurations
(x_{0},x_{1})\in E^{2}=E\times E
lying in the same fiber of
p
, i.e.
p(x_{0})=p(x_{1})
, while
E_{B}^{I}
stands for the space of continuous paths in
E
lying in a single fiber of
p
; the map
\Pi
sends a path to the pair of its end points.

Definition 1
.

The parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]

of the fibration
p:E\to B
is defined as the sectional category of the associated fibration

(2)

\displaystyle\Pi:E_{B}^{I}\to E^{2}_{B},\quad\Pi(\gamma)=(\gamma(0),\gamma(1)).

In other words,

{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]:={\sf{secat}}[\Pi:E_{B}^{I}\to E^{2}_{B}],

is the minimal integer
k
such that the space

E^{2}_{B}
admits an open cover

E^{2}_{B}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}

with the property that each set
U_{i}
admits a continuous section

s_{i}:U_{i}\to E^{I}_{B}

of
\Pi
, where
i=0,1,\dots,k
.

Note that
\Pi:E_{B}^{I}\to E^{2}_{B}
is a Hurewicz fibration assuming that
p:E\to B
is a Hurewicz fibration, see
[
5
]
, Proposition 2.1.

The statement below is partially based on
[
13
]
, it explains the relevance of the notion of parametrized topological complexity to the problem of constructing motion planning algorithms:

Proposition 2

(Proposition 4.7 from
[
4
]
)
.

If
p:E\rightarrow B
is a locally trivial fibration, and the spaces
E
and
B
are metrizable separable ANRs, then in Definition
1
, instead of open covers one may use arbitrary covers of
E^{2}_{B}
or, equivalently, arbitrary partitions

E^{2}_{B}=F_{0}\sqcup F_{1}\sqcup\cdot\cdot\cdot\sqcup F_{k},\quad F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset,\quad i\not=j

admitting continuous sections
s_{i}:F_{i}\rightarrow E_{B}^{I}
of
\Pi
, where
i=0,1,...,k.

Next we mention the property of invariance with respect to fibre homotopy equivalence:

Proposition 3

(Proposition 5.1 from
[
4
]
)
.

If the fibrations
p:E\to B
and
p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B
are fiberwise homotopy equivalent,
then
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]={\sf TC}[p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B].

Finally we state the upper and the lower bounds for parametrized topological complexity.

Proposition 4
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow B
be a Hurewicz fibration with fiber
X
, where the spaces

E,B,X
are CW-complexes. Then the parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]
satisfies the inequalities

(3)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}(X)\,\leq\,{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,\dim E^{2}_{B}=\dim B+2\dim X.

Inequalities (
3
) follow directly from Definition
1
and the well-known properties of the sectional category. Compare Proposition 7.2 from
[
4
]
.

Proposition 5

(Proposition 7.3 from
[
4
]
)
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow B
be a fibration with path-connected fiber. Consider the diagonal map
\Delta:E\rightarrow E^{2}_{B}
, where
\Delta(e)=(e,e)
for
e\in E
. Then the parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}[p:E\rightarrow B]
is greater than or equal to the
cup-length of the kernel

\ker[\Delta^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(E^{2}_{B};R)\rightarrow H^{\ast}(E;R)],

where
R
is an arbitrary coefficient ring.

3.
Parametrized topological complexity of circle bundles

In this section we consider locally trivial bundles with fibre
S^{1}
(the circle) and describe their parametrized topological complexity.
The results of this section serve two major purposes: they give a demonstrative illustration of the general method, and they are used later in this paper as the case of projective bundles of dimension 1.

Let

p:E\to B

be a locally trivial bundle with fibre the circle
S^{1}
, where the base
B
is a finite CW-complex,
a circle bundle
for short.
We shall describe a cohomology class

w(p)\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})

associated with the bundle
p:E\to B
, which we call
the orientation class
.
Each fibre
X_{b}=p^{-1}(b)
, where
b\in B
, is topologically a circle
and the homology group
H_{1}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
with real coefficients is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
. The family of groups

{\mathcal{H}}=\{H_{1}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}})\}_{b\in B}

forms a real line bundle over
B
, and the class
w(p)
is defined as

w(p)=w_{1}(\mathcal{H}),

where
w_{1}(\mathcal{H})
is the first Stiefel - Whitney class of
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open cover of
B
such that the bundle
p:E\to B
can be trivialised over each
U_{i}\subset B
with the transition functions

g_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to{\rm Homeo}(S^{1}).

Composing
g_{ij}
with the obvious homomorphism
{\rm Homeo}(S^{1})\to{\rm Aut}(H_{1}(S^{1};{\mathbb{Z}}))=\{1,-1\}
we obtain a
\{1,-1\}
-cocycle representing the orientation class
w(p)
.

It is quite obvious that if the circle bundle
p:E\to B
arises as the unit sphere bundle of a rank 2 vector bundle
\xi
over
B
then the orientation class

w(p)\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
coincides with the first Stiefel - Whitney class
w_{1}(\xi)\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

Lemma 6
.

The following properties of a locally trivial circle bundle
p:E\to B
over a finite CW-complex
B
are equivalent:

(a)

The class
w(p)=0\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
vanishes;

(b)

Each fibre
X_{b}=p^{-1}(b)
, can be oriented with orientation continuous in
b\in B
;

(c)

The bundle
p:E\to B
is isomorphic to an
SO(2)
-principal bundle;

(d)

The bundle
p:E\to B
is isomorphic to the unit circle bundle of a complex line bundle over
B
.

Proof.

If
w(p)=0
then the real line bundle
\mathcal{H}
is trivial and hence it admits a non-vanishing section. The section is a continuous function associating with each
b\in B
a nonzero element in
H_{1}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
, i.e. an orientation of the circle
X_{b}
. This shows that (a)
\implies
(b).

To show that (b)
\implies
(c) suppose that the fibres
X_{b}
can be continuously oriented.
The results of §4 from
[
14
]
imply that every orientable locally trivial circle bundle over a finite CW-complex admits
SO(2)
structure group,
i.e. it arises from a
SO(2)
-principal bundle. This means that for an
SO(2)
-principal bundle
P\to B
one can identify
E
with the space

P\times_{SO(2)}S^{1}
.
But clearly

P\times_{SO(2)}S^{1}=P

and hence the circle bundle
p:E\to B
coincides with the principal bundle
P\to B
.

It is well-known that the classifying space
BSO(2)
for principal
SO(2)
-bundles coincides with
{\mathbb{CP}}^{\infty}
, the universal space for complex line bundles. This shows equivalence between (c) and (d).

The implications (d)
\implies
(a) and (d)
\implies
(b) are well-known: complex vector bundles are always orientable.
∎

A locally trivial circle bundle satisfying the conditions of Lemma
6
will be called
orientable
.

The notion of genus of a cohomology class is well-known and appears in literature under several different names.

Definition 7
.

The genus
{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)
of a cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is defined as the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that
B
admits an open cover

B=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
with the property that the restriction of the class
\alpha
on each of the open sets
U_{i}
vanishes,

\alpha|_{U_{i}}=0
, where
i=0,1,\dots,k
.

It is well-known that the genus
{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)
coincides with the minimal integer
n
such that the class
\alpha
can be induced by a continuous map
B\to{\mathbb{RP}}^{n}
from the generator

\beta\in H^{1}({\mathbb{RP}}^{n};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

Every nonzero cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
determines a 2-fold regular covering space
p_{\alpha}:\tilde{B}\to B

and, clearly,
{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)
coincides with the sectional category
{\sf{secat}}[p_{\alpha}:\tilde{B}\to B]
of this covering.

The upper and lower bounds for the genus

(4)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha)\leq{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)\leq\dim B

are well-known. Here
{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha)
denotes
the height of the class

\alpha
, which is defined as the largest integer
m\geq 0
, such that
\alpha^{m}\not=0
.

The following result uses the genus of the orientation class of a circle bundle to give a sharp upper bound for the parametrized topological complexity.

Theorem 8
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial bundle with fibre the circle
S^{1}
and
let
w(p)\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
be its orientation class.
Then one has

(5)

\displaystyle 1\leq{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\leq{\mathfrak{genus}}(w(p))+1.

In particular,
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=1
if the circle bundle
p:E\to B
is orientable.

An orientable circle bundle is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle (by Lemma
6
) and our statement
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=1
is consistent with Proposition 4.3 from
[
4
]
.

Combining the upper bound (
5
) with the inequality (
4
) we obtain:

Corollary 9
.

For any circle bundle
p:E\to B
over a finite CW-complex one has

(6)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\leq\dim B+1.

Note that the upper bound (
6
) is by one stronger than the general upper bound of Proposition
4
.
We shall see below Example
12
, Example
13
and Example
14
, when the upper bound (
6
) is sharp.

It would be of interest to compare the results about the sharp upper bounds for the parametrized topological complexity (Corollary
9
and
Theorem
27
)
with the results of
[
15
]
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

The inequality
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\geq 1
follows from (
3
) since
{\sf TC}(S^{1})=1
.
To prove the upper bound, we suppose that
B=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
is an open cover with
w(p)|_{U_{i}}=0
for
i=0,1,\dots,k
, where
k={\mathfrak{genus}}(w(p))
.
The restricted bundle
p:p^{-1}(U_{i})\to U_{i}
is orientable and we shall denote by
A_{i}\subset E^{2}_{B}
the set

A_{i}=\{(e,e^{\prime})\in E^{2}_{B};\,e\not=e^{\prime},\,p(e)=p(e^{\prime})\in U_{i}\},\quad i=0,1,\dots,k.

Besides, let
\Delta(E)\subset E^{2}_{B}
be the diagonal. We obtain a cover

E^{2}_{B}=\Delta(E)\cup A_{0}\cup A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{k}

and (having in mind Proposition
2
) we show below that over each of the sets of this cover one can construct a continuous section of the bundle
\Pi:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{2}_{B}
.

A section
s:\Delta(E)\to E^{I}_{B}
over the diagonal is obvious: we can set
s(e,e)\in E^{I}_{B}
to be the constant path at
e\in E
.

A section
s_{i}:A_{i}\to E^{I}_{B}
for
i=0,1,\dots,k
can be defined as follows. By Lemma
6
the bundle
p:p^{-1}(U_{i})\to U_{i}
is orientable and for
(e,e^{\prime})\in A_{i}
we define

s_{i}(e,e^{\prime})(t)
to be the path moving along the fibre in the positive direction starting from
e
and ending at
e^{\prime}
. This proves the inequality (
5
).
∎

We can further strengthen the upper bound of Theorem
8
under an additional assumption that the base
B
is a closed manifold:

Theorem 10
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial bundle with fibre
S^{1}
, where the base
B
is a closed manifold of dimension
n
. If the
n
-th power of the orientation class
w(p)
vanishes, i.e.

w(p)^{n}=0\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}),

then

(7)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\leq\,n\,=\,\dim B.

Proof.

By Theorem
38
in Appendix II, see §
B
,
we know that
w(p)^{n}=0
, where
n=\dim B
, implies that
{\mathfrak{genus}}(p)<\dim B
.
Our result now follows from the right inequality (
5
).
∎

Finally we mention a result from
[
11
]
, which gives a lower bound for the parametrised topological complexity of circle bundles and complements Theorem
8
.

Theorem 11

(Theorem 2 in
[
11
]
; see also Theorem 9.1 from
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\xi
be a rank 2 vector bundle over a finite CW-complex
B
and let
p:E\to B
be the unit sphere bundle of

\xi
. Then one has

{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\geq{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{1}(\xi)|w_{2}(\xi))+1,

where
w_{1}(\xi)=w(p)
and
w_{2}(\xi)
are the Siefel-Whitney classes of
\xi
, with
w_{i}(\xi)\in H^{i}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. The relative height
{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{1}(\xi)|w_{2}(\xi))
is defined as the maximal integer
k\geq 0
such that the cohomology class
w_{1}(\xi)^{k}
does not belong to the ideal of the algebra
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
generated by the class
w_{2}(\xi)
.

We illustrate the results of this section by several examples.

Example 12
.

Consider the canonical line bundle
\eta
over the real projective space
{\mathbb{RP}}^{n}
. Let
p:E\to B
be the unit sphere bundle of the vector bundle
\xi=\eta\oplus\epsilon
where
\epsilon
is the trivial line bundle. The total Stiefel - Whitney class of
\xi
is
1+\beta
, where
\beta\in H^{1}({\mathbb{RP}}^{n};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the generator, i.e.
w_{1}(\xi)=\beta
and
w_{2}(\xi)=0
. Thus, we find that the relative height
{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{1}(\xi)|w_{2}(\xi))
equals
n
and Theorem
11
gives

{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\geq n+1
. On the other hand, Corollary
9
gives the opposite inequality implying
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=n+1
.

Example 13
.

Consider the 2-dimensional torus
T^{2}
and two real line bundles
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
over
T^{2}
which have the Stiefel - Whitney classes
w_{1}(\ell_{1})=a_{1}
and

w_{1}(\ell_{2})=a_{2}
, where
a_{1},\,a_{2}\in H^{1}(T^{2};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
are the standard generators. Let
\xi
be the Whitney sum
\xi=\ell_{1}\oplus\ell_{2}
and let

p:E\to B=T^{2}
be the unit sphere bundle of
\xi
. The total Stiefel - Whitney class of
\xi
is

w(\xi)=(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})=1+(a_{1}+a_{2})+a_{1}a_{2}.

We see that
w_{1}(\xi)=a_{1}+a_{2}
and
w_{2}(\xi)=a_{1}a_{2}
and the relative height
{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{1}(\xi)|w_{2}(\xi))=1
equals 1. Applying Theorem
11
we get

{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\geq 2
. On the other hand, Theorem
10
gives the opposite inequality, and we finally obtain
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=2
.

Example 14
.

The Hopf bundle
S^{3}\to S^{2}
is orientable and hence
{\sf TC}[S^{3}\to S^{2}]=1
according to Theorem
8
.
Consider also the bundle
p:{\mathbb{RP}}^{3}\to S^{2}
with fibre
{\mathbb{RP}}^{1}=S^{1}
obtained from the Hopf bundle
S^{3}\to S^{2}
by factorising with respect to the antipodal
involution on
S^{3}
. The obtained bundle is again orientable and hence we obtain
{\sf TC}[p:{\mathbb{RP}}^{3}\to S^{2}]=1
as above.
Example
26
contains further information on this circle bundle.

4.
Projective bundles and cohomology algebras

4.1.

Let
\xi
be a vector bundle of rank
d+1>1
over a CW-complex
B
.
We shall denote by
E(\xi)
the total space of
\xi
, and the bundle map is denoted
\xi:E(\xi)\to B
.
The projectivisation of
\xi
yields a locally trivial bundle

(8)

\displaystyle p:E\to B

with fibre

\mathbb{RP}^{d}
, the real projective space of dimension
d
. Our aim is to understand the parametrized topological complexity

(9)

{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]

(as defined in §
2
)
in terms of topological invariants associated with the original vector bundle
\xi
.
We know that
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]

reflects the complexity of algorithms which take as input pairs of lines lying in the same fibre of
\xi
and moving the first line continuously towards the second line while staying in the same fibre, see
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
.

4.2.

An obvious first step is to compare
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]
with
{\sf TC}({\mathbb{RP}}^{d})
, the usual topological complexity of the fibre, which serves as a lower
bound,

(10)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\geq{\sf TC}({\mathbb{RP}}^{d}).

From
[

Definition 17

the enhancement of the bundle (

The cohomology class

Lemma 18
.

Let
\xi
be a rank
d+1
vector bundles of over a CW complex
B
. Let

\xi^{\prime}=\xi\otimes L
, where
L
is a line bundle over
B
. Then the projectivisation
p:E\to B
of
\xi
can be naturally identified with the projectivisation of
\xi^{\prime}
. If

{\mathfrak{v}},{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}\,\in\,H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
denote the enhancements corresponding to the vector bundles
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
correspondingly, then

{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{v}}+p^{\ast}(w_{1}(L)),

where

w_{1}(L)\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the first Stiefel - Whitney class of
L
.

Proof.

Suppose that
V
and
W
are vector spaces and
\dim W=1
. Any line
\ell\subset V
and a nonzero element
w\in W
determine a line
\ell\otimes w\subset V\otimes W
and this line
\ell\otimes w
is independent of the choice of
w\in W
. Thus, the projectivisations of
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}=\xi\otimes L
can be naturally identified. This fact is well known, see
[
1
]
, §2.2.

To prove the second statement of Lemma
18
consider the unit sphere bundle
\tilde{E}\to B
of
\xi
and the unit sphere bundle
\tilde{S}\to B
of
L
.
Both
\tilde{E}
and
\tilde{S}
carry natural action of the cyclic group of order two
G=\{1,-1\}
. A closed loop
\gamma:[0,1]\to E
lifts to a closed loop in
\tilde{E}
if and only if its evaluation
\langle{\mathfrak{v}},[\gamma]\rangle=0
vanishes. Similarly, the loop
p\circ\gamma
in
B
lifts to a closed loop in
\tilde{S}
if and only if the evaluation

\langle w_{1}(L),p_{\ast}[\gamma]\rangle=0
vanishes.
The unit sphere bundle
\tilde{E}^{\prime}
of
\xi^{\prime}=\xi\otimes L
can be identified with the quotient of the space
\tilde{E}\times_{B}\tilde{S}
with respect to the diagonal action of

, i.e.
\tilde{E}^{\prime}=(\tilde{E}\times_{B}\tilde{S})/G
. Hence a loop
\gamma:[0,1]\to E
lifts to a closed loop in
\tilde{E}^{\prime}
if and only if the lift of
\gamma
into
\tilde{E}\to E
and the lift of

into
\tilde{S}\to B
are either both closed or both not closed. Thus, we see that
\gamma
lifts to a closed loop in
\tilde{E}^{\prime}
iff the evaluation of the class
{\mathfrak{v}}+p^{\ast}(w_{1}(L))\in H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
on the homology class
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
vanishes. This shows that the enhancement corresponding to
\xi^{\prime}
equals
{\mathfrak{v}}+p^{\ast}(w_{1}(L)).

∎

Next we describe the cohomology algebra of the projective bundle.

Lemma 19
.

Consider the bundle of projective spaces
p:E\to B
obtained as the projectivization of a vector bundle
\xi
of rank
d+1
, where
d\geq 1
, over a CW-complex

(a)

every cohomology class

has a unique representation in the form

\displaystyle u=a_{k}+a_{k-1}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}+a_{k-2}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}^{2}+\dots+a_{k-d}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}^{d},

where

and the product

is understood as the cup-product

(b)

In the algebra

one has the following relation

(17)

{\mathfrak{v}}^{d+1}=w_{d+1}+w_{d}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}+w_{d-1}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}^{2}+\dots+w_{1}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}^{d},

where

Proof.

The restriction of the class
{\mathfrak{v}}
on each fibre

is the nonzero class

Therefore the powers

Statement (a) follows directly from the Leray - Hirsch theorem. Statement (b) is one of the definitions of the Stiefel - Whitney classes, see
[
16
]
, chapter 16.

Lemma 21

Corollary 22
.

The homomorphism

\Delta^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to H^{\ast}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}),

induced on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-cohomology by the
diagonal map,
\Delta:E\to E^{2}_{B}
, satisfies

(20)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\ast}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L})=\Delta^{\ast}({\mathfrak{v}}_{R})={\mathfrak{v}}.

Moreover, the sum
u={\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}
is the only nonzero cohomology class in
H^{1}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
satisfying
\Delta^{\ast}(u)=0
.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
consider the projection
\pi_{i}:E^{2}_{B}\to E
onto the first or the second factor correspondingly. Then clearly
\pi^{\ast}_{1}({\mathfrak{v}})={\mathfrak{v}}_{L}
and
\pi_{2}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{v}})={\mathfrak{v}}_{R}
as follows from the definitions. Since
\pi_{1}\circ\Delta=1_{E}=\pi_{2}\circ\Delta
, we obtain
\Delta^{\ast}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L})=\Delta^{\ast}(\pi^{\ast}_{1}({\mathfrak{v}}))=(\pi_{1}\circ\Delta)^{\ast}({\mathfrak{v}})={\mathfrak{v}}
and similarly
\Delta^{\ast}({\mathfrak{v}}_{R})={\mathfrak{v}}
.

To prove the second statement consider a class
u\in H^{1}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. By Lemma
20
class

has a unique representation in the form

u=a+\alpha_{L}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+\alpha_{R}\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}
where
a\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
and
\alpha_{L},\alpha_{R}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Then
\Delta^{\ast}(u)=a+(\alpha_{L}+\alpha_{R})\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}
. We see that
\Delta^{\ast}(u)=0
iff
a=0
and
\alpha_{L}=\alpha_{R}
. This shows that the sum
u={\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}
is the only nonzero class in
H^{1}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})

satisfying
\Delta^{\ast}(u)=0
.
∎

Example 24

Let
\eta
be the standard line bundle over the projective space

. Consider the rank 2 vector bundle

Combining Lemma

and Theorem

is always less or equal than

(31)

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{n+2d}=0\in H^{\ast}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

This is established in Theorem

For a rank
d+1
vector bundle
\xi
over a CW-complex

we shall consider together with the Stiefel - Whitney classes
w_{i}=w_{i}(\xi)\in H^{i}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
the dual Stiefel - Whitney classes
\bar{w}_{i}=\bar{w}_{i}(\xi)
, which are defined by the relation

(32)

\displaystyle w\cdot\bar{w}=1,

where
w=1+w_{1}+w_{2}+\dots
and
\bar{w}=1+\bar{w}_{1}+\bar{w}_{2}+\dots
are the total Stiefel - Whitney and total dual Stiefel - Whitney classes. The equality (

\prod_{i\geq 0}H^{i}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
of formal power series.

For
i=0,1,\dots,d+1
let
Q_{i}(x)
denote the following polynomial of degree

:

Q_{i}(x)=x^{i}+w_{1}x^{i-1}+w_{2}x^{i-2}+\dots+w_{i},\quad Q_{i}\in H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})[x].

Lemma 30
.

For any
i>0
in the ring
H^{\ast}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
one has the following relations for the powers of the left and the right enhancements

(33)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+i}=\bar{w}_{i}Q_{d}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L})+\bar{w}_{i+1}Q_{d-1}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L})+\dots

and similarly

(34)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d+i}=\bar{w}_{i}Q_{d}({\mathfrak{v}}_{R})+\bar{w}_{i+1}Q_{d-1}({\mathfrak{v}}_{R})+\dots

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{L}
denote the class
{\mathcal{V}}_{L}=1+{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{2}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{3}+\dots
understood as an element of the ring of formal power series

\mathcal{R}=\prod_{i\geq 0}H^{\ast}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

The ring
\prod_{i\geq 0}H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is naturally a subring of
\mathcal{R}
. Consider in
\mathcal{R}
the product
w\cdot{\mathcal{V}}_{L}
. For the
i
-th degree component of this product,
using the relation (
17
),
we have

\displaystyle(w\cdot{\mathcal{V}}_{L})_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}Q_{i}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L})&\mbox{for}&i\leq d,\\
0&\mbox{for}&i>d.\end{array}\right.

We see that the product

is a “polynomial”  of degree

Multiplying it by

in the ring

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{L}=\bar{w}\cdot(w\cdot{\mathcal{V}}_{L}).

From this, taking into account (

Lemma

leads to the following Corollary:

Corollary 31
.

Let
m\geq 0
be the maximal integer such that the dual Stiefel - Whitney class
\bar{w}_{m}=\bar{w}_{m}(\xi)\not=0\in H^{m}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is nonzero. Then the highest nonzero power of the class
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}\in H^{1}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{m+d}
; moreover,
in the group
H^{m+d}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
one has the equality

(39)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{m+d}=\bar{w}_{m}\cdot\left[{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}+w_{1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d-1}+w_{2}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d-2}+\dots+w_{d}\right].

Similarly, the highest nonzero power of the class
{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}
is
{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{m+d}
, and
in the group
H^{m+d}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
one has

(40)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{m+d}=\bar{w}_{m}\cdot\left[{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}+w_{1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d-1}+w_{2}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d-2}+\dots+w_{d}\right].

Now we are in position to proof the following result:

Theorem 32
.

Let
\xi
be a vector bundle of rank
d+1\geq 3
over a finite CW-complex
B
of dimension
n=\dim B
.
Let
p:E\to B
be the bundle of real projective spaces
{\mathbb{RP}}^{d}
obtained as projectivization of
\xi
.
Let
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L},{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}\in H^{1}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
be the left and the right enhancements, see (
18
). Then

(41)

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{n+2d}=0.

In other words,

{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})\leq n+2d-1.

Proof.

Let
m
denote the highest dimension in which
\bar{w}_{m}(\xi)\not=0\in H^{m}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. Clearly,
m\leq n
.

Assume first that
m=n
. Then

(42)

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{m+2d}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m+2d}{d+i}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+i}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{m+d-i}.

Using (
40
) we see that

{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{m+d}={\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}\bar{w}_{m}\left[{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}+w_{1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d-1}+\dots\right]=\bar{w}_{m}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}

and similarly

{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{m+d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}=w_{m}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}.

Hence the terms with
i=0
and
i=m
in the sum (
42
) are equal and they cancel each other.
If
1\leq i\leq m-1
then the product
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+i}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{m+d-i}
equals
\bar{w}_{i}\bar{w}_{m-i}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}
as follows from Lemma
30
.
Thus the terms with the indexes
i
and
m-i
cancel each other. If
m=2k
is even then the central term appears with the coefficient

\binom{m+2d}{d+k}
which is always even.

Consider now the case
m<n
.
If
2m<n
then in each product of the form
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{i}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{n+2d-j}
one has either
j>m+d
or
n+2d-j>m+d
and hence the product vanishes due to Corollary
31
.
Finally we shall assume that
m<n\leq 2m
. Then

(43)

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{n+2d}=\sum_{j=n-m}^{m}\binom{n+2d}{d+j}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+j}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{n+d-j}.

In this sum the lower limit
j=n-m
does not exceed the upper limit
j=m
.
Using Lemma
30
we have

{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+j}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{n+d-j}=\bar{w}_{j}\bar{w}_{n-j}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}

and therefore

({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{n+2d}=\left(\sum_{j=n-m}^{m}\binom{n+2d}{d+j}\bar{w}_{j}\bar{w}_{n-j}\right)\cdot{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}.

Since
\binom{n+2d}{d+j}=\binom{n+2d}{d+(n-j)}
, the terms of this sum with
j
and
n-j
cancel each other. If
n=2k
is even and
j=k
then
j=n-j
and the binomial coefficient
\binom{n+2d}{d+k}
is even, i.e. it vanishes modulo 2.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Examples

6.1.

Let
B={\mathbb{RP}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{RP}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{RP}}^{2}
and let
a,b,c\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
denote the generators of the corresponding factors. Let
\xi
be the vector bundle of rank
3
obtained as the Whitney sum of the canonical lines bundles associated to
a
,
b
and
c
. We calculate below the parametrized topological complexity
of the bundle of real projective spaces
p:E\to B
with fibre
{\mathbb{RP}}^{2}
over
B
using the results of the previous sections.

We note that the total Stiefel - Whitney class of
\xi
is given by

w=(1+a)(1+b)(1+c)=1+w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}\,\in H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})

and the dual Stiefel - Whitney class is

\bar{w}=(1+a+a^{2})(1+b+b^{2})(1+c+c^{2})\,\in H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

The highest nontrivial class
\bar{w}_{m}
is
\bar{w}_{6}=(abc)^{2}\in H^{6}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

Let
E^{2}_{B}\subset E^{2}
be the space of pairs of lines lying in the same fibre, as described in Lemma
20
.
Let
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L},{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}\in H^{1}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
be the left and the right enhancements. Applying Corollary
31
we find

(44)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{8}=(abc)^{2}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{8}=(abc)^{2}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{2}

and
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{9}=0={\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{9}
, i.e.
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L})={\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{v}}_{R})=8.
Thus, we obtain

({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{8}={\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{8}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{8}=(abc)^{2}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{2}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{2})\not=0

and

({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{9}=(abc)^{2}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{2}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{2})({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})=(abc)^{2}({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{2}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{2}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L})\not=0

is nonzero, as one sees by applying Lemma
20
.
On the other hand, using (
44
) we find

({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{10}=({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{8}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{8})\cdot({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{2}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{2})=(abc)^{2}\cdot({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{4}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{4})=0.

The vanishing of the class
(abc)^{2}\cdot({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{4}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{4})\in H^{10}(E^{2}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
can be seen using Lemma
30
; indeed, the class
(abc)^{2}\in H^{6}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is a top degree class of
B
and the expansions of
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{4}
and
{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{4}
involve classes of the base of positive degree.

As
\dim B=6
and
d=2
, we may apply Corollary
23
combined with Theorem
27
to conclude that
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=9
.

6.2.

Consider the circle
B=S^{1}
and the vector bundle
\xi
over
B
of rank
d+1
where

\xi=\eta\oplus\epsilon\oplus\epsilon\dots\oplus\epsilon
where
\eta
is a non-orientable line bundle and
\epsilon
is the trivial line bundle.
We shall assume that
d\geq 2
.
We are interested in the projectivization
p:E\to B
of
\xi
which is a locally trivial bundle with fibre
{\mathbb{RP}}^{d}
over the circle.
The total Stiefel - Whitney class
w=w(\xi)=1+\beta
, where
\beta\in H^{1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the generator. The relations (
17
) for the left and right enhancements in this case read

(45)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+1}=\beta{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d},\quad{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d+1}=\beta{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}

and
{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+2}=0={\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d+2}.

The upper bound of Theorem
27
gives

(46)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]\leq 2d,

and to match it with the lower bound of Corollary
23
we need to have
({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{2d}\not=0
.
The binomial expansion of
({\mathfrak{v}}_{L}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{R})^{2d}
contains the terms

\binom{2d}{d-1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d-1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d+1}+\binom{2d}{d+1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d+1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d-1}=\binom{2d}{d-1}\beta\left[{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d-1}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d}+{\mathfrak{v}}_{L}^{d}{\mathfrak{v}}_{R}^{d-1}\right].

We see that the parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]
is maximal and equals
2d
if the binomial coefficient
\binom{2d}{d-1}
is odd.

In general, if
a
and
b
are two integers with their binary expansions
a=a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{k}
and
b=b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{k}
, where
a_{i},b_{i}\in\{0,1\}
,
then

\binom{a}{b}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{a_{i}}{b_{i}}\mod 2.

Here one understands that
\binom{0}{0}=\binom{1}{1}=\binom{1}{0}=1
and
\binom{0}{1}=0
.
Thus, the binomial coefficient
\binom{a}{b}
is odd iff
a_{i}=0
implies
b_{i}=0
, i.e. if the binary expansion of
b
has zeros on all positions where the expansion of
a
has zeros.

Using the above general criterion, it is easy to see that the binomial coefficient
\binom{2d}{d-1}
is odd when
d+1
is a power of 2.
This leads to the following Corollary:

Corollary 33
.

If
d+1
is a power of 2 then one has
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=2d
in the above example.

In particular, one may take
d=15
and get
{\sf TC}[p:E\to B]=30
for the associated bundle of real projective spaces
{\mathbb{RP}}^{15}
over the circle.
It is interesting to compare this result with fact that
{\sf TC}({\mathbb{RP}}^{15})=22
, see (
12
).
This example illustrates a surprising phenomenon that, even in the case of a one-dimensional base, the parametrized topological complexity may significantly exceed the usual topological complexity.

Appendix A
Circle bundles and vector bundles of rank 2

The proof of the following statement uses arguments of
[
14
]
, §4.

Lemma 34
.

If
B
is a finite CW complex, then every locally trivial bundle
p:E\to B
with fibre the circle is isomorphic to the unit sphere bundle of a rank 2 vector bundle

\xi
over
B
. Moreover, such bundle
\xi
is unique up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
H(S^{1})=H_{+}(S^{1})\sqcup H_{-}(S^{1})
be the group of homeomorphisms of the circle
S^{1}
with
H_{\pm}(S^{1})\subset H(S^{1})
denoting the subgroups of homeomorphisms preserving (reversing) the orientation. Clearly, one has
O(2)\subset H(S^{1})
and

SO(2)\subset H_{+}(S^{1})
.

Lemma 35
.

The induced map of classifying spaces

(47)

\displaystyle BO(2)\to BH(S^{1})

is a fibration with contractible fibre. Therefore, for any finite CW complex
B
any continuous map
B\to BH(S^{1})
admits a unique up to homotopy lift to a map

B\to BO(2)
.

Clearly Lemma
35
implies Lemma
34
.

Proposition 4.2 from
[
14
]
states that the inclusion
SO(2)\to H_{+}(S^{1})
is a homotopy equivalence, but this fact is not enough for our purposes.

We show below (using the method of
[
14
]
)
that
the group
H(S^{1})
, viewed as a left
O(2)
-space, is the total space of a principal
O(2)
-bundle with contractible orbit space
H(S^{1})/O(2)
and
therefore the canonical map
BO(2)\to BH(S^{1})
is a fibration with contractible fibre
H(S^{1})/O(2)
.

Let
{\mathbf{e}}:{\mathbb{R}}\to S^{1}
denote the universal covering of the circle,
{\mathbf{e}}(t)=\exp(2\pi it)
, where
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Any homeomorphism
f:S^{1}\to S^{1}
admits a lift
\tilde{f}
into the universal covers

\textstyle{{\mathbb{R}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{f}}
\scriptstyle{{\mathbf{e}}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{R}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\mathbf{e}}}
\textstyle{S^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{S^{1}}

and
\tilde{f}
is unique, up to addition of an integer. Explicitly,
f(\exp(2\pi it))=\exp(2\pi i\tilde{f}(t))
.
The lift
\tilde{f}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies either
\tilde{f}(t+1)=\tilde{f}(t)+1
, or
\tilde{f}(t+1)=\tilde{f}(t)-1
, for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
depending on whether
f
preserves or reverses the orientation of the circle; it follows that the lift
\tilde{f}
of every homeomorphism
f:S^{1}\to S^{1}
is a homeomorphism
\tilde{f}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, it is monotone increasing iff
f
preserves the orientation and it is monotone decreasing otherwise.

The lift
\tilde{R}_{c}
of a rotation
R_{c}\in SO(2)
, where
R_{c}(e^{2\pi it})=e^{2\pi i(t+c)}
, has the form
\tilde{R}(t)=t+c
, where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant.
The lift of a reflection
Q_{c}\in O(2)
, where
Q_{c}(e^{2\pi it})=e^{2\pi i(-t+c)}
, has the form
\tilde{Q}_{c}(t)=-t+c
. Note the formulae

(48)

\displaystyle Q_{c^{\prime}}\circ R_{c}=Q_{c^{\prime}-c}=R_{-c}\circ Q_{c^{\prime}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{c^{\prime}}\circ Q_{c}=R_{c^{\prime}-c}.

Let
F\subset C({\mathbb{R}})
denote the space of all continuous function
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
having the following properties:

(a)

g(t+1)=g(t)
;

(b)

\int_{0}^{1}g(t)dt=0;

(c)

for any
t<t^{\prime}
one has
\frac{g(t^{\prime})-g(t)}{t^{\prime}-t}>-1
.

The space
F
is convex and hence is contractible.

We claim that
there exists an
O(2)
-equivariant homeomorphism

(49)

\displaystyle\psi:H(S^{1})\to O(2)\times F.

For
f\in H_{+}(S^{1})
the lift
\tilde{f}
can be written uniquely in the form

(50)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(t)=t+c+g(t),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant (defined up to adding an integer)
and
g\in F
. The condition (c) is equivalent for
\tilde{f}
to be monotone. The map (
49
) sends
f\in H_{+}(S^{1})
to the pair

\psi(f)=(R_{c},g)\in O(2)\times F,

where

R_{c}\in SO(2)
is the rotation by the angle
2\pi c
. If
f\in H_{-}(S^{1})
is a homeomorphism reversing the orientation then the lift
\tilde{f}
can be uniquely written in the form

(51)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(t)=-t-c-g(t),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
g\in F
, and we define

\psi(f)=(Q_{c},g)\in O(2)\times F,

where
Q_{c}
is the reflection described above.

It is obvious that
\psi
is a homeomorphism and using formulae (
48
) one checks that
\psi
is
O(2)
-equivariant, i.e. if
\psi(f)=(T,g)
, where
T\in O(2)
and
g\in F
, then

\psi(S\circ f)=(S\circ T,g)\quad\mbox{ for every}\quad S\in O(2).

Thus we see that the subgroup
O(2)\subset H(S^{1})
is admissible, i.e. the quotient map
H(S^{1})\to H(S^{1})/O(2)
is a principal bundle (see p. 5 of
[
18
]
) and fibre of the canonical fibration
BO(2)\to BH(S^{1})
is homeomorphic to
H(S^{1})/O(2)=F

hence is contractible. This completes the proof of Lemma
34
.
∎

Appendix B
Sectional category of 2-fold covering

Let
\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y
be a 2-fold cover of a finite CW-complex

, where
\tilde{Y}
and
Y
are connected. Let

\alpha\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
,
\alpha\not=0
, be the characteristic class of
\phi
. This means that
the kernel of the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(Y)\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
determined by
\alpha
coincides with the subgroup
\pi_{1}(\tilde{Y})\subset\pi_{1}(Y)
.
For the sectional category of the covering
\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y
one has

(52)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha)\leq{\sf{secat}}[\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y]={\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)\leq\dim Y.

The left inequality is the standard cohomological lower bound using the fact that
\phi^{\ast}(\alpha)=0
.

Lemma
36
and Lemma
37
stated below give stronger upper bounds for the sectional category and for the genus of
\alpha
, compared to (
52
). Roughly, these statements claim that the sectional category

{\sf{secat}}[\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y]
attains the upper bound
n=\dim Y
only when
{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha)=n
, i.e.
\alpha^{n}\not=0
.

Lemma 36
.

Let
Y
be a finite cell complex of even dimension
n\geq 2
,
and let
\alpha\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
be a nonzero cohomology class. Assume that the top integral cohomology group
H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})

has no direct summands of type
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{k}}
with
k>1
. If
{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha)\leq n-1
, i.e.
\alpha^{n}=0
, then

(53)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)={\sf{secat}}[\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y]\,\leq n-1.

Here
\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y
denotes the 2-fold covering corresponding to the class
\alpha
.

To state Lemma
37
which is the odd-dimensional version of Lemma
36
, we need to introduce the following notation.
Given a cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, let
I_{\alpha}
denote the local coefficient system of groups
{\mathbb{Z}}
over
Y
such that its monodromy along any loop
\gamma
in
Y
equals

(-1)^{\alpha(\gamma)}\in\{1,-1\}.

Here
\alpha(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\{0,1\}
and
(-1)^{0}=1
and
(-1)^{1}=-1
. In other words, the monodromy of
I_{\alpha}
along a loop
\gamma
is nontrivial iff the value of the class
\alpha
on
\gamma
is nonzero.

Lemma 37
.

Let
Y
be a finite cell complex of odd dimension
n\geq 3
,
and let
\alpha\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
be a nonzero cohomology class.
Assume that the cohomology group
H^{n}(Y;I_{\alpha})

has no direct summands of type
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{k}}
with
k>1
.
If
{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha)\leq n-1
then

(54)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)={\sf{secat}}[\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y]\,\leq n-1.

Proof of Lemma
36
and Lemma
37
.

According to a theorem of A. Schwarz
[
19
]
, one has
{\sf{secat}}[\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y]\leq n-1
if and only if the
n
-fold fibrewise join

\phi_{n}:\ast^{n}_{Y}\tilde{Y}\to Y

of
n
copies of the covering
\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y
admits a continuous section.
The fibre of
\phi_{n}
is the join
S^{0}\ast S^{0}\ast\dots\ast S^{0}=(S^{0})^{\ast n}
of
n
copies of

S^{0}
, it is homeomorphic to
S^{n-1}
and is
(n-2)
-connected.
The primary obstruction
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
for a section of
\phi_{n}
lies in the cohomology group

(55)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{o}_{n}\in H^{n}(Y;\pi_{n-1}((S^{0})^{\ast n}))\simeq H^{n}(Y;H_{n-1}((S^{0})^{\ast n};{\mathbb{Z}})),

where the coefficients
H_{n-1}((S^{0})^{\ast n};{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
are understood as a local coefficient system. In fact, for dimensional reasons,
{\mathfrak{o}}_{n}
is the only obstruction, i.e. the section of
\phi_{n}
exists iff
{\mathfrak{o}}_{n}=0
.

According to Schwarz
[
19
]
, the primary obstruction
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
can be computed as follows.

Let
G=\{1,t\}
denote the multiplicative cyclic group of order 2, i.e.
t^{2}=1
.
The class
\alpha\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
determines a group homomorphism
\pi_{1}(Y)\to G
, where
g\mapsto t^{\alpha(g)}
, which allows to view the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[G]
as a local coefficient system over
Y
. If
\epsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}[G]\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is the augmentation and
I=\ker\epsilon
denotes the augmentation ideal, then

(56)

\displaystyle 0\to I\to{\mathbb{Z}}[G]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0

is an exact sequence of local systems over
Y
.

As an abelian group, the ideal
I
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
, it has generator
t-1
and the action of
\pi_{1}(Y)
on the generator

(t-1)\in I
is as follows: the action of a group element

g\in\pi_{1}(Y)
on
(t-1)
equals

g\cdot(t-1)=t^{\alpha(g)}\cdot(t-1).

Explicitly, this means that
if
g\in\pi_{1}(\tilde{Y})\subset\pi_{1}(Y)
, i.e.
\alpha(g)=0
, then
g\cdot(t-1)=(t-1)
and if
g\notin\pi_{1}(\tilde{Y})
, i.e.

\alpha(g)=1
, then
g\cdot(t-1)=t(t-1)=-(t-1)
. Thus, we see that
the local system
I
is isomorphic to
I_{\alpha}
.

The short exact sequence (
56
) gives a long exact sequence

(57)

\displaystyle H^{0}(Y;I)\to H^{0}(Y;Z[G])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\to}}H^{0}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}H^{1}(Y;I).

Here
\delta
is the Bockstein homomorphism.

The class
{\sf w}=\delta(1)\in H^{1}(Y;I)
is
the homological obstruction
, in terminology of
[
19
]
.
According to the general theory, see
[
20
]
, chapter 6, the cohomology class
\sf w
is represented by a cross-homomorphism

\psi:\pi_{1}(Y)\to I
, which is given by
\psi(g)=t^{\alpha(g)}-1\in I
.
Hence

\displaystyle\psi(g)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0,&\mbox{if}&\alpha(g)=0,\\
t-1,&\mbox{if}&\alpha(g)=1.\end{array}\right.

Applying Theorem 3.2 from
[
20
]
, chapter 6, we obtain that in the exact sequence (
57
) one has
H^{0}(Y;I)=0
and
H^{0}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}[G])\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The latter group is generated by the class of the invariant element
1+t\in G
. Since
\epsilon(1+t)=2
we conclude that the homological obstruction
\sf w
has order 2, i.e.

(61)

\displaystyle 2\cdot{\sf w}=0.

Theorem 2 of Schwarz
[
19
]
states that the local coefficient system
H_{n-1}((S^{0})^{\ast n};{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to the tensor power

I^{\otimes n}=I\otimes I\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I
(
n
factors)
and the primary obstruction
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
equals the
n
-th power of the homological obstruction
\sf w
, i.e.

(62)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{o}_{n}={\sf w}^{n}\in H^{n}(Y;I^{\otimes n}).

We show below that
{\sf w}^{n}=0\in H^{n}(Y;I^{\otimes n})
if and only if
\alpha^{n}=0\in H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, and therefore
{\sf{secat}}[\phi:\tilde{Y}\to Y]\leq n-1
if and only if

\alpha^{n}=0
.

Note that
I\otimes I\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
, isomorphism of local coefficient systems. Hence the local system
I^{\otimes n}
is the trivial local system
{\mathbb{Z}}
for
n
even, and
I^{\otimes n}=I=I_{\alpha}
for
n
odd.

Next we consider the homomorphism
r:I\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
of the local systems (where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is a constant local system) given by
r(t-1)=1
. Every element of
I

has the form
x=k(t-1)
with
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
r(x)\equiv k\mod 2
. The induced homomorphism

r_{\ast}:H^{1}(Y;I)\to H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})

satisfies
r_{\ast}({\sf w})=\alpha
as follows from (
B
). Thus, we see that
{\sf w}^{n}=0
implies
\alpha^{n}=0
.

To show the inverse implication, we first assume that
n\geq 2
is even. Then
I^{\otimes n}={\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\sf w}^{n}\in H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
We have the exact sequence

H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\times 2}}{{\to}}\,H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{\ast}}}{{\to}}\,H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})

with
r_{\ast}({\sf w}^{n})=\alpha^{n}
. We see that if
\alpha^{n}=0
then the class
{\sf w}^{n}
must be divisible by
2
. On the other hand, we have
2\cdot{\sf w}^{n}=0
, because of (
61
). Our assumption on the absence of summands of type
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{k}}
with
k>1
in the group
H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
implies that

{\sf w}^{n}=0
.

Consider now the case when
n
is odd. Then
I^{\otimes n}\simeq I

and the exact sequence of local coefficient systems over
Y

0\to I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\times 2}}{{\to}}I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\to 0.

gives the cohomological sequence

H^{n}(Y;I)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\times 2}}{{\to}}\,H^{n}(Y;I)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{\ast}}}{{\to}}\,H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

We see that
{\sf w}^{n}\in H^{n}(Y;I)
and

r_{\ast}({\sf w}^{n})=\alpha^{n}
. If
\alpha^{n}=0
then the class
{\sf w}^{n}
must be divisible by
2
.
On the other hand this class has order
2
and we obtain
{\sf w}^{n}=0
, using our assumption about the absence of summand of type
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{k}}
with
k>1
in the cohomology group
H^{n}(Y;I)
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result is a generalisation of Theorem 3.5 of
[
2
]
.

Theorem 38
.

Let
Y
be a closed manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
. Then, for a nonzero cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, one has
{\mathfrak{genus}}(\alpha)=n
if and only if
\alpha^{n}\neq 0
.

Proof.

If the dimension
n
is even we apply Lemma
36
. The top cohomology group
H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
is either
{\mathbb{Z}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and hence the assumption of Lemma
36
is fulfilled.

In the case when the dimension
n
is odd we apply Lemma
37
. We need to verify that the cohomology group
H^{n}(Y;I_{\alpha})

has no direct summands of type
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{k}}
with
k>1
. Applying the Poincaré duality, we get

H^{n}(Y;I_{\alpha})\simeq H_{0}(Y;I_{\alpha}\otimes\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})
where
where
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}
is the orientation local system of the manifold
Y
. We observe that
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}
coincides with the local coefficient system
I_{\alpha^{\prime}}
where

\alpha^{\prime}=w_{1}(Y)\in H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, the first Stiefel - Whitney class of
Y
.
We find that
H^{n}(Y;I_{\alpha})=H_{0}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}
iff
\alpha=w_{1}(Y)
and
H^{n}(Y;I_{\alpha})={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
if
\alpha\not=w_{1}(Y)
. Hence, we see that the assumption of Lemma

37
is satisfied.

This completes the proof.
∎

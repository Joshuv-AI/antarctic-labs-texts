Quasinormal modes of massive scalar fields in five-dimensional Myers-Perry black holes with two arbitrary rotation parameters
Zi-Yang Huang, Jia-Hui Huang

Abstract
We investigate the quasinormal modes of massive scalar fields in the background of five-dimensional Myers-Perry black holes. In particular, we explore the case for Myers-Perry black holes with two arbitrary rotation parameters. Since the Klein-Gordon equation for the scalar field is separable, we numerically compute the scalar quasinormal modes by using the radial and angular equations. Two methods, the continued fraction method and matrix method, are used in the numerical calculation. We find that all obtained modes have negative imaginary parts and are decaying modes. We also consider the impact of the rotation parameters, scalar field mass μ \mu and azimuthal numbers on the scalar quasinormal modes. Besides, when the scalar mass μ \mu becomes relatively large, we also find the long-living scalar modes. Our numerical results also demonstrate the symmetries of the QNMs explicitly.

In this work, we concentrate on

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{M}{\rho^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{r^{2}\rho^{2}}{\Delta}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\rho^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}

\displaystyle+\cos^{2}\theta\left(r^{2}+b^{2}+\frac{Mb^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\psi^{2},

\displaystyle\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+b^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)\left(r^{2}+b^{2}\right)-Mr^{2}.

The metric(

\displaystyle r_{1,4}=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{\sqrt{\left(M-a^{2}-b^{2}\right)+\sqrt{(M-a^{2}-b^{2})^{2}-4a^{2}b^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle r_{2,3}=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{\sqrt{\left(M-a^{2}-b^{2}\right)-\sqrt{(M-a^{2}-b^{2})^{2}-4a^{2}b^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}.

The dynamics of a scalar perturbation
\Psi(x)
with mass
\mu
on
5D
MPBH is governed by the covariant Klein-Gordon equation

\Box\Psi(x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\alpha}\left[g^{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{-g}\ \partial_{\beta}\Psi(x)\right]=\mu^{2}\Psi(x),

(4)

where
g=\det(g_{\alpha\beta})
is the determinant of the metric. The above equation is separable in the Boyer-Lindquist-type coordinates
x^{\mu}=\left\{t,r,\theta,\varphi,\psi\right\}

[
64
]
. We choose the following ansatz for the scalar field

\Psi(x)=e^{-i\omega t}e^{im_{1}\varphi}e^{im_{2}\psi}R(r)S(\theta),

(5)

where
\omega
is the angular frequency and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
are azimuthal numbers. Plugging the above ansatz into Eq.(
4
), we obtain the radial and angular EOMs.
The angular EOM obeyed by
S(\theta)
is

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\left(\sin\theta\cos\theta\dfrac{\mathrm{d}S(\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\right)

\displaystyle+\bigg[\left(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)\cos^{2}\theta

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}-\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta}+\lambda_{\begin{subarray}{c}km_{1}m_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigg]S(\theta)=0.

(6)

The above equation is the
5D
spheroidal equation of which the solutions are scalar spheroidal harmonics
[
64
,
65
]
. Since the above equation is invariant under
m_{1}\rightarrow-m_{1},m_{2}\rightarrow-m_{2}
, we restrict our consideration to
m_{1}\geq 0,m_{2}\geq 0
.

\lambda_{\begin{subarray}{c}km_{1}m_{2}\end{subarray}}
is the separation constant and
k=\left\{0,1,2,\cdots\right\}
labels the discrete eigenvalues of
S(\theta)
for given
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
.
When
|\left(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)|\ll 1
,
\lambda_{\begin{subarray}{c}km_{1}m_{2}\end{subarray}}
can be expanded as a series

\lambda_{\begin{subarray}{c}km_{1}m_{2}\end{subarray}}=\ell(\ell+2)+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{p}c^{p},

(7)

where
\ell=2k+m_{1}+m_{2}
and
c=\sqrt{\left(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)}
.
\ell
is an integer and
\ell\geq m_{1}+m_{2}
. More details of
\tilde{f}_{p}
can be found in
[
65
]

The radial EOM obeyed by
R(r)
is

\dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{r}}\left(\dfrac{\Delta}{r}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}R(r)}{\mathrm{d}r}\right)+U(r)R(r)=0,

(8)

where

\displaystyle U(r)=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(b^{2}+r^{2})^{2}a^{2}}{\Delta r^{2}}-\frac{b^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]m_{1}^{2}+\left[\frac{(a^{2}+r^{2})^{2}b^{2}}{\Delta r^{2}}-\frac{a^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]m_{2}^{2}-\frac{2Mr^{2}am_{1}\omega}{\Delta}\left(1+\frac{b^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle-\frac{2Mr^{2}bm_{2}\omega}{\Delta}\left(1+\frac{a^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{2Mabm_{1}m_{2}}{\Delta}+\left(\frac{M}{r^{2}}+\frac{M^{2}}{\Delta}\right)r^{2}\omega^{2}+(r^{2}+b^{2})\left(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)-\lambda_{\begin{subarray}{c}km_{1}m_{2}\end{subarray}}.

It’s easy to check that the above radial EOM reduces to that of a singly rotating
5D
MPBH case when

[
62
]
.

Next we study the asymptotical behaviors of the scalar perturbation near boundaries. It is useful to define the tortoise coordinate
r_{*}

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}r_{*}}{\mathrm{d}r}=\dfrac{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)\left(r^{2}+b^{2}\right)}{\Delta}
and a new radial function
\tilde{R}(r)=\sqrt{\dfrac{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}{r}}R(r)
. The radial EOM can be rewritten as a Schrödinger-like equation,

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\tilde{R}(r)}{\mathrm{d}r_{*}^{2}}+\tilde{U}(r)\tilde{R}(r)=0.

(10)

Then, the limits of
\tilde{U}(r)
at the event horizon and spatial infinity are

\displaystyle\tilde{U}(r)\sim\begin{cases}\left(\omega-m_{1}\Omega_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ha\end{subarray}}-m_{2}\Omega_{\begin{subarray}{c}Hb\end{subarray}}\right)^{2},\quad{}\,r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty\,(r\rightarrow r_{H})\\
\\
\omega^{2}-\mu^{2},\quad{}\,r_{*}\rightarrow+\infty\,(r\rightarrow+\infty),\end{cases}

(11)

where
\Omega_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ha\end{subarray}}=\dfrac{a}{r_{H}^{2}+a^{2}}
,
\Omega_{\begin{subarray}{c}Hb\end{subarray}}=\dfrac{b}{r_{H}^{2}+b^{2}}
, which can be easily found by using the trick proposed in
[
71
]
. With the QNM boundary conditions, the asymptotic solutions of the radial equation (
10

R(r)\sim\begin{cases}(r-r_{H})^{-i\sigma},&\quad r\rightarrow r_{H},\\
r^{-3/2}{\rm e}^{qr},&\quad r\rightarrow\infty,\end{cases}

(12)

where

\displaystyle q^{2}=\mu^{2}-\omega^{2},

\displaystyle\sigma=

\displaystyle\left(\omega-\dfrac{m_{1}a}{r_{H}^{2}+a^{2}}-\dfrac{m_{2}b}{r_{H}^{2}+b^{2}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left(r_{H}^{2}+a^{2}\right)\left(r_{H}^{2}+b^{2}\right)}{2r_{H}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}-M+2r_{H}^{2}\right)}.

(13)

At spatial infinity, the QNM condition imposes purely outgoing waves at
r\rightarrow\infty
. In this case,

{\rm Re}(\omega)>\mu
and
q=i\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}

The angular and radial EOMs and the chosen boundary conditions single out a discrete set of complex frequencies

\omega\equiv\omega_{R}+i\omega_{I}

. We approximate the expansions up to
(N-1)
-th order, then we can obtain following equations

\displaystyle\Delta F=MD,

(29)

where

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}x_{1}-x_{j}&\frac{(x_{1}-x_{j})^{2}}{2}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{1}-x_{j})^{i}}{i!}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{1}-x_{j})^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\\
x_{2}-x_{j}&\frac{(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2}}{2}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{2}-x_{j})^{i}}{i!}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{2}-x_{j})^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
x_{j-1}-x_{j}&\frac{(x_{j-1}-x_{j})^{2}}{2}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{j-1}-x_{j})^{i}}{i!}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{j-1}-x_{j})^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\\
x_{j+1}-x_{j}&\frac{(x_{j+1}-x_{j})^{2}}{2}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{j+1}-x_{j})^{i}}{i!}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{j+1}-x_{j})^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
x_{N}-x_{j}&\frac{(x_{N}-x_{j})^{2}}{2}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{N}-x_{j})^{i}}{i!}&\cdots&\frac{(x_{N}-x_{j})^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\Delta F=\left(\mathcal{Z}(x_{1})-\mathcal{Z}(x_{j}),\mathcal{Z}(x_{2})-\mathcal{Z}(x_{j}),\cdots,\mathcal{Z}(x_{j-1})-\mathcal{Z}(x_{j}),\mathcal{Z}(x_{j+1})-\mathcal{Z}(x_{j}),\cdots,\mathcal{Z}(x_{N})-\mathcal{Z}(x_{j})\right)^{T},

(37)

\displaystyle D=\left(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(x_{j}),\mathcal{Z}^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}),\cdots,\mathcal{Z}^{(n)}(x_{j}),\cdots\mathcal{Z}^{(N-1)}(x_{j})\right)^{T}.

(38)

According to Cramer’s rule, the derivative terms in Eq.(
38
) can be formally solved from Eq.(
29
). In particular, the first and second derivatives are

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(x_{j})=\dfrac{\text{det}(M_{1})}{\text{det}(M)},~~\mathcal{Z}^{\prime\prime}(x_{j})=\dfrac{\text{det}(M_{2})}{\text{det}(M)}.

(39)

M_{i}(i=1,2)
is a matrix obtained by replacing the
i
-th column of
M
with
\Delta F
. In Eq.(
39
), the derivatives are expressed as linear combinations of function values at the
N
discrete points.

With Eq.(
39
), the differential equation (
26
) can be written as a matrix equation,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}=0,

(40)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\left(\mathcal{Z}(x_{1}),\mathcal{Z}(x_{2}),\cdots,\mathcal{Z}(x_{N})\right)^{T}.

(41)

The boundary conditions
\mathcal{Z}(0)=\mathcal{Z}(1)=0
can be imposed by approximating Eq.(
40
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\mathcal{F}=0,

(42)

where

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{M}\right)_{j,i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta_{j,i},&j=1\text{ or }N,\\
\left(\bar{\mathcal{M}}\right)_{j,i},&j=2,3,\cdots,N-1\end{array}\right.

In order to have a nonzero solution for
\mathcal{F}
in Eq.(
42
), the determinant of
\mathcal{M}
should vanish. The QNM frequencies are thus determined by

\text{det}(\mathcal{M})=0.

(45)

IV
Summary

In this paper, we study the massive scalar QNMs of a
5D
doubly rotating MPBH with two arbitrary rotation parameters. Two numerical methods are used, namely, the continued fraction method and matrix method. We make extensive calculation for the fundamental QNMs with various model parameters
\{a,b,\mu,k,m_{1},m_{2}\}
, and show our numerical results in several tables and figures.

It is found that all the obtained fundamental QNMs are decaying modes. The absolute values of the imaginary parts of the QNMs always decrease as the rotation parameters increase.
As the scalar mass
\mu
increases, the imaginary parts of the QNMs have a tendency to zero, which results in the existence of long-living modes.

As the rotation parameters increase, it is found that the real parts of the corotating QNMs with
k=0
increase while the real parts of the counterrotating QNMs with
k=0
decrease. The corotating QNMs with higher azimuthal numbers vary more rapidly with rotation parameters.

From the radial and angular EOMs, we know the QNMs are invariant under
(a,m_{1})\leftrightarrow(b,m_{2})
or in particular,
a\leftrightarrow b
when
m_{1}=m_{2}
. The calculated numerical results in the tables and figures demonstrate these symmetries explicitly.

From our numerical results, we also see that the real parts of the QNMs with
k>0
have a big depression while
a=b
, which hits the enhanced symmetry of the degenerate MPBH.

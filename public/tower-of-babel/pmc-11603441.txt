Combined Anterior Cruciate Ligament and Anterior Oblique Ligament Reconstruction Using the Rectus Femoris Tendon: Surgical Technique Description
Authors: Rego MCF, Barroso BG, Helito CP, Rego MCF, Canuto SMG, Jorge PB
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Objectives:Present a combined anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) and anterior oblique ligament (AOL) reconstruction technique using a rectus femoris tendon graft harvested in a minimally invasive approach.Methods:Examination under anesthesia should confirm ACL insufficiency and associated anteromedial rotational instability. The graft is harvested from the lateral portion of the rectus femoris tendon using a closed tendon stripper with the knee at 20 degrees of flexion. The distal portion is folded to form a double graft for the ACL, while the proximal remainder is used for the AOL without folding. The femoral tunnel is made using an outside-in technique, and the tibial tunnel is created within the native ACL tibial footprint. ACL graft fixation is performed with an interference screw in both the femur and tibia, with the knee at 20° of flexion. The medial epicondyle is identified by palpation (or fluoroscopy) for AOL graft fixation. The remaining graft is tunneled subcutaneously and fixed with an interference screw at the femoral tunnel in neutral rotation and full extension.Results:The technique improved knee stability and controlled anteromedial rotational instability. It is reproducible and presents few complications.Conclusion:The described technique using the rectus femoris graft harvested via a minimally invasive approach is a viable alternative for combined ACL reconstruction and anteromedial reinforcement in both primary and revision surgeries.

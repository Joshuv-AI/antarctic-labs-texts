Abstract
. We apply thermodynamic formalism to a generalized horseshoe map. We prove that a tailored anisotropic Banach space with weighted norms yields a spectral gap for the transfer operator, implying the existence of a unique physical measure. Under the virtually expanding condition, this measure is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, with density in the Sobolev space H μ H_{\mu} , for some μ < 1 / 2 \mu<1/2 .

1.
Introduction

In chaotic systems, ergodic theory provides powerful tools for analyzing long-term behavior by examining the asymptotic properties of empirical measures derived from orbits. This reveals their convergence to invariant measures and their statistical properties. Hyperbolic dynamics, characterized by stable and unstable manifolds where the dynamics contract and expand, respectively, are central to the theory of chaos. A key class of such systems is the
generalized horseshoe map
, introduced by Jakobson and Newhouse
[
11
]
. These maps, defined on the unit square, involve a diffeomorphism defined on a countable partition of vertical strips. Jakobson and Newhouse proved the existence of an physical measure for these maps. Actually, it seems that GHM configuration is the main tool to derive the existence of the physical measures (see
[
4
]
). It was recently shown in
[
8
]
that this measure is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure under some stable conditions. The natural next step, which we undertake in this paper, is to establish finer statistical properties.

Our approach for the existence of physical measure is based on the powerful functional analytic method pioneered by Dolgopyat, Liverani, Baladi, Gouëzel, and others. The core idea is to study the spectral properties of the transfer operator on a carefully constructed Banach space. Because the dynamics exhibit both expansion and contraction, the space must have an
anisotropic structure
, with norms that treat stable and unstable directions differently. The main technical challenge in applying this framework to generalized horseshoes lies in managing the countable partition and ensuring sufficient control over geometric distortion to prove that the transfer operator has a spectral gap on our space.

A classical approach to prove the absolute continuity of an invariant measure is the
transversality
method. Tansversality expresses a diverse set of expanding directions, ensuring that cone mappings across preimages avoid tangential obstructions almost everywhere (see
[
12
,
13
]
). Building on transversality and using a functional analytic approach, the authors in
[
3
]
define appropriate Banach spaces in which the transfer operator has good spectral properties.
The notion of
virtually expanding
, initially defined by Tsujii, extends transversality to non-hyperbolic contexts by encoding directional expansion in a quantitative base-point–dependent manner that does not rely on a global hyperbolic structure. In
[
14
]
, the author uses the concept of virtual expansion in Sobolev spaces to prove the absolute continuity of the measures. The regularity of the Sobolev space improves as the degree of virtual expansion increases. We choose this strategy and treat the Tsujii’s principal result as a toolbox. A benefit is the higher regularity of the density function than the
L^{2}
regularity.

2.
Existence of Physical Measure

Our analysis relies on the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}
is formally defined by the duality relation
\int(\mathcal{L}h)\varphi\,dm=\int h(\varphi\circ F)\,dm
for smooth functions
h,\varphi
. For a density
h
, this corresponds to

\mathcal{L}h(x):=\sum_{F(y)=x}\frac{h(y)}{|\Jac F(y)|}.

The transfer operator encodes the evolution of densities under the dynamics. In the hyperbolic setting, it has a regularizing effect: it smooths densities along unstable manifolds. Dually, the Koopman operator acts on observables, improving their regularity along stable manifolds. This differentiated treatment is what make the space of distribution where the operator acts to be anisotropic. Inspired by
[
6
]
, we construct a Banach space whose norm is adapted to the dynamic of a GHM. The space consists of distributions, and its norm measures regularity differently along stable and unstable directions. The norm is designed so that the transfer operator is quasi-compact, which ensures a spectral gap. An alternative approach with the same conceptual flavor is provided by the anisotropic Banach spaces
H_{s,t}
defined by Gouëzel and Baladi in
[
1
]
, which encode differential regularity in the unstable direction (
s>0
) and controlled singularity in the stable direction (
t<0
) via adapted Fourier charts. While technically distinct, both frameworks exploit the same dynamical principle, strong regularity under expansion, weak regularity under contraction, leading to analogous spectral properties of the transfer operator.

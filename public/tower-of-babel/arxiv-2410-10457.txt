Strong solution and approximation of time-dependent radial Dunkl processes with multiplicative noise
Minh-Thang Do, Hoang-Long Ngo, and, Dai Taguchi

Abstract
We study the strong existence and uniqueness of solutions within a Weyl chamber for a class of time-dependent particle systems driven by multiplicative noise. This class includes well-known processes in physics and mathematical finance. We propose a method to prove the existence of negative moments for the solutions. This result allows us to analyze two numerical schemes for approximating the solutions. The first scheme is a θ \theta -Euler–Maruyama scheme, which ensures that the approximated solution remains within the Weyl chamber. The second scheme is a truncated θ \theta -Euler–Maruyama scheme, which produces values in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} instead of the Weyl chamber 𝕎 \mathbb{W} , offering improved computational efficiency. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 65C30; 60H35; 91G60; 17B22 Keywords : Non-colliding particle systems; Dyson’s Brownian motions; Radial Dunkl processes; Strong solution; Numerical schemes

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a stochastic particle system given by the following stochastic differential equations (SDEs)

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma(t,X(t))\,\mathrm{d}B(t)+b(t,X(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{k(t,\alpha)}{\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}t,~X(0)=\xi\in\mathbb{W},~t\in[0,T],

(1)

where
B
is an
r
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with the filtration
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying the usual condition,
R_{+}
is a positive root system in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{W}
is a Weyl chamber in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
and
k:[0,T]\times R_{+}\to(0,\infty)
are measurable functions.

If
r=d
,
b\equiv 0
,
\sigma
is the identity matrix, and
k
is a multiplicity function defined on
R_{+}
, then
X=(X(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
represents a classical radial Dunkl process. Consequently, equation (
1
) can be interpreted as a generalized Dunkl process with drift and multiplicative noise.

The class of radial Dunkl processes encompasses several well-known processes, including Bessel processes, the square root of multidimensional Wishart processes, and Dyson’s Brownian motions.
The square root of the Bessel process, also known as the Cox–Ingersoll–Ross process in mathematical finance, and the square root of the multidimensional Wishart process serve as models for the evolution of spot interest rates in both one-dimensional and multidimensional settings, respectively.
Dyson’s Brownian motions, which describe non-colliding particle systems, have been extensively studied in mathematical physics and random matrix theory, as they share the same distribution as the ordered eigenvalues of certain matrix-valued Brownian motions.

The study of radial Dunkl processes (
1
) is challenging due to the presence of stiff terms
\frac{1}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}
in the drift coefficient.
The existence and uniqueness of strong solutions for some classes of non-colliding particle systems, including Dyson’s Brownian motions, have been extensively studied (see, for example,
[
7
,
8
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
32
]
).
We will demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to equation (
1
), which corresponds to a more general class of particle systems than those considered in
[
28
,
29
]
. It is important to note that in
[
29
]
, the existence of solutions to drifted Dunkl processes with additive noise was proven by using the Girsanov theorem.
However, applying that approach to equation (
1
) is hardly possible due to multiplicative noise.
Our new approach involves considering a sequence of truncated SDEs and showing that they converge to a process satisfying equation (
1
).

The numerical approximation of SDEs whose solutions remain confined within a specific domain poses one of the most significant challenges in stochastic numerics. This challenge stems from the requirement that any approximate solution must also remain within this domain. The explicit Euler–Maruyama schemes fail to satisfy this essential property.
Many authors have studied numerical schemes for one-dimensional SDEs whose solutions remain within a subset of
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
9
,
13
,
27
]
).
However, despite their considerable practical significance, there are relatively few results on the numerical analysis of non-colliding particle systems (
[
24
,
25
,
28
,
29
]
).
To study the convergent rates of numerical scheme for SDEs whose coefficient involves singular terms of the form
\frac{1}{\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle}
, it is crucial to show that the negative moments
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-p}]
are finite for some
p>0
.
This result has been obtained for systems of interacting Brownian particles and Brownian particles with nearest neighbor repulsion (see Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 3.11 in
[
28
]
).
Unfortunately, the bounds for negative moments in
[
28
]
are obtained only for systems with a relatively small number of particles.
In
[
29
]
, utilizing the relationship between root systems and harmonic functions, the existence of negative moments has been demonstrated for random systems associated with Dunkl processes involving a large number of particles.
The approach in
[
29
]
, which relies on a change of probability measure, is effective for SDEs with additive noise.

In this paper, we introduce a novel technique to establish the existence of negative moments for a broad class of non-colliding particle systems governed by SDE (
1
) with multiplicative noise (see Theorem
2.7
).
This enables us to analyze two numerical schemes for approximating the solution of SDE (
1
).
The first scheme is a
\theta
-Euler–Maruyama scheme, ensuring that the approximate solution remains within the Weyl chamber
\mathbb{W}
.
The second scheme is a truncated
\theta
-Euler–Maruyama scheme, which produces values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
rather than within the Weyl chamber
\mathbb{W}
, but offers the advantage of efficient computational implementation.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
addresses the existence, uniqueness, and moment properties of the stochastic particle system (
1
).
Section
3
outlines a general procedure for verifying the convergence of the
\theta
-scheme applied to SDEs with values in an open set and a one-sided Lipschitz drift.
Finally, Section
4
introduces two numerical schemes for the stochastic particle system (
1
) and examines their convergence rates in the

-norm.

Radial Dunkl processes with multiplicative noise

2.1
Root systems, Weyl chamber and harmonicity

This section provides a concise overview of root systems, Weyl groups, Weyl chambers, and their relation to harmonic function. We refer the reader to
[
14
,
19
,
20
]
for a more detailed exposition.

A
root system

R
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a finite set of nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that:
(R1)
R\cap\{c\alpha~;~c\in\mathbb{R}\}=\{\alpha,-\alpha\}
for any
\alpha\in R
;
(R2)
\sigma_{\alpha}(R)=R
for any
\alpha\in R
, where
\sigma_{\alpha}
is the reflection through the hyperplane perpendicular to
\alpha
.
The element of a root system is called root.
A sub-group
W=W(R)
of
O(d)
is called the
Weyl group
generated by a root system
R
, if it is generated by the reflections
\{\sigma_{\alpha}~;~\alpha\in R\}
.
Each root system can be written as a disjoint unions
R=R_{+}\cup(-R_{+})
, where
R_{+}
and
-R_{+}
are separated by a hyperplane
H_{\beta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle\beta,x\rangle=0\}
with
\beta\not\in R
. Such a set
R_{+}
is called a
positive root system
.
A function
\mathbf{k}:R\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
multiplicity function
if it is invariant under the natural action of
W
on
R
, that is,
\mathbf{k}(\alpha)=\mathbf{k}(\beta)
whenever there exists
w\in W
such that
w\alpha=\beta
.
A connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in R}H_{\alpha}
is called a
Weyl chamber
of the root system
R
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~|~\langle\alpha,x\rangle>0,~\forall\alpha\in R_{+}\}

is called the
fundamental Weyl chamber
.

A remarkable property of (reduced) root systems in stochastic calculus is that the polynomial function
\prod_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\langle\alpha,x\rangle
, where
x\in\mathbb{W}
, is
\Delta
-harmonic, meaning that
\Delta\prod_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\langle\alpha,x\rangle=0
, (see, Theorem 6.2.6 in
[
14
]
).
Moreover, by using this property, for any
x\in\mathbb{W}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle\langle\beta,x\rangle},

(2)

(see, e.g. Lemma 2.2 in
[
29
]
).

2.2
Existence and uniqueness of radial Dunkl processes with multiplicative noise

We define
f_{k}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\overline{\sigma}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle f_{k}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{k(t,\alpha)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}\alpha,

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}|\sigma_{i,i}(t,x)|,&\text{ if }r=d\text{ and }\sigma_{i,j}(t,x)=0,~\text{ for all }i\neq j,\\
\displaystyle|\sigma(t,x)|,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Throughout the paper, we will work under the following assumptions on the coefficients
\sigma
,
b
, and
k
.

Assumption 2.1
.

(i)

The drift coefficient
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is Lipschitz continuous uniformly in time, that is,
\|b\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\neq y}\frac{|b(t,x)-b(t,y)|}{|x-y|}<\infty
, and
\|b(\cdot,0)\|_{\infty}<\infty
.

(ii)

The diffusion coefficient
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
is Lipschitz continuous uniformly in time, that is
\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\neq y}\frac{|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)|}{|x-y|}<\infty
.

(iii)

For any
(t,\alpha)\in[0,T]\times R_{+}
, it holds that
\|\overline{\sigma}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 2k(t,\alpha)
and
\|k(\cdot,\alpha)\|_{\infty}<\infty
.

(iv)

There exists
K:[0,T]\to[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\text{ and }\sup_{(\alpha,x)\in R_{+}\times\mathbb{W}}\frac{\langle\alpha,-b(t,x)\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}\leq K(t).

(v)

For any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{k(t,\alpha)|\alpha|^{2}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}=\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{k(t,\beta)\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle\langle\beta,x\rangle}.

.

Under Assumption

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X(t)|^{p}\Big]<\infty.

(3)

We consider several classes of non-colliding particle systems which satisfy Assumption

(Radial Dunkl process)
.

Suppose that

(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{W}
,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,f_{k}(t,x)-f_{k}(t,y)\rangle\leq 0.

An
\varepsilon

\displaystyle f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,x):=\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}k(t,\alpha)g_{\varepsilon}(\langle\alpha,x\rangle)\alpha.

We set

\displaystyle L_{k}=\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\|k(\cdot,\alpha)\|_{\infty}|\alpha|^{2}.

(10)

Then it follows from (
7
), (
8
), and (

) that

\displaystyle|f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,x)-f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,y)|\leq L_{k}\varepsilon^{-2}|x-y|,~\text{ for any }t\in[0,T],~x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(11)

\displaystyle\langle x,f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,x)\rangle\leq\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\|k(\cdot,\alpha)\|_{\infty},~\text{ for any }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(12)

\displaystyle\langle x-y,f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,x)-f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,y)\rangle\leq 0,~\text{ for any }t\in[0,T],~x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(13)

\displaystyle|f_{k}(t,x)-f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{\|k(\cdot,\alpha)\|_{\infty}|\alpha|}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}},~\text{ for any }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}.

(14)

Under Assumption
2.1
, by the global Lipschitz continuity of

,
the following SDE has a unique strong solution for any

,

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X_{\varepsilon}(t)=\sigma(t,X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}B(t)+b(t,X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+f_{k,\varepsilon}(t,X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,~X_{\varepsilon}(0)=X(0)=\xi\in\mathbb{W}.

(15)

Note that by (

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{p}]<\infty.

(16)

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

.
The proof is based on a Lyapunov function, which is inspired by the proof of Lemma 4.3.3 in
[

]
for the case of Dyson’s Brownian motion.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We set the Lyapunov function
f
by

\displaystyle f(x):=|R_{+}||x|^{2}-\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\log\langle\alpha,x\rangle,~x\in\mathbb{W}.

Then we have for any
\beta\in R_{+}

\displaystyle-\log\langle\beta,x\rangle\leq f(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\alpha|^{2}.

(17)

Indeed, if
|R_{+}|=1
, then the estimate (
17
) is trivial.
If
|R_{+}|>1
, then it follows from Cauchy–Schwartz’s inequality and the fact that
\log|\alpha|\leq|\alpha|^{2}
, that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\log\langle\alpha,x\rangle\leq\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\{\log|\alpha|+\log|x|\}\leq\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\{|\alpha|^{2}+|x|^{2}\}\leq|R_{+}||x|^{2}+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\alpha|^{2}.

Thus we obtain (
17
). For each
0<\varepsilon<\min_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle
, we define a stopping time
\tau_{\varepsilon}
by

\displaystyle\tau_{\varepsilon}=\inf\{t\in(0,T]~|\min_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle\leq\varepsilon\}.

Then by Itô’s formula and Assumption
2.1
(v), we have

\displaystyle f(X_{\varepsilon}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t))

\displaystyle=f(\xi)+M^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{\langle\alpha,b(s,X_{\varepsilon}(s))\rangle}{\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(s)\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+|R_{+}|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\Big\{2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}k(s,\alpha)+2\langle X_{\varepsilon}(s),b(s,X_{\varepsilon}(s))\rangle+|\sigma(s,X_{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(s)\rangle^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\frac{\langle\alpha,\sigma_{\cdot,\ell}(s,X_{\varepsilon}(s))\rangle^{2}}{2}-k(s,\alpha)|\alpha|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
M^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}
is a stochastic integral defined by

\displaystyle M^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\Big\langle 2|R_{+}|X_{\varepsilon}(s)-\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(s)\rangle}\alpha,\sigma(s,X_{\varepsilon}(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\Big\rangle.

Using Cauchy–Schwartz’s inequality and Assumption
2.1
(iii), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(s)\rangle^{2}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\frac{\langle\alpha,\sigma_{\cdot,\ell}(s,X_{\varepsilon}(s))\rangle^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{\overline{\sigma}(s,x)^{2}}{2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(s)\rangle^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{k(s,\alpha)|\alpha|^{2}}{\langle\alpha,X_{\varepsilon}(s)\rangle^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, by Assumption
2.1
(i) and (iv), we have

\displaystyle f(X_{\varepsilon}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t))

\displaystyle\leq f(\xi)+M^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+|R_{+}|\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\|k(\cdot,\alpha)\|_{\infty}+2|X_{\varepsilon}(s)|(\|b(\cdot,0)\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}|X_{\varepsilon}(s)|)+\|\sigma\|_{\infty}^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let
n_{0}=|f(\xi)|^{1/2}+1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq n_{0}
, we set
\varepsilon_{n}:=\exp(-n^{2}-\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\alpha|^{2})\wedge\min_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle/2
and define a stopping time
\rho_{n}
by

\displaystyle\rho_{n}:=\inf\{t\in(0,T]~|~f(X_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(t))\geq n^{2}\}.

Then, since
M^{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}\wedge\rho_{n}}
is a martingale, by using (
16
), there exists a positive constant
C_{T}>0
which is independent of
\varepsilon
and
n
such that

\displaystyle n^{2}\mathbb{P}(\rho_{n}<T)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[f(X^{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}\wedge\rho_{n}}(T))]\leq f(\xi)+C_{T}.

This implies that
\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\rho_{n}<T)<\infty
.
We set

\Omega_{0}:=\bigcup_{N\geq n_{0}}\bigcap_{n\geq N}\{\rho_{n}=T\}
.
Then by Borel–Cantelli lemma, we have
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})=1
.

For each
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, there exists
N(\omega)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}\vee N(\omega)
,
\rho_{n}(\omega)=T
.
Then it holds that for any
\beta\in R_{+}
and
t\in[0,T)
,
w\in\Omega_{0}
,
n\geq n_{0}\vee N(w)
,

\displaystyle-\log\langle\beta,X_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(t,\omega)\rangle\leq f(X_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(t,\omega))+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\alpha|^{2}<n^{2}+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\alpha|^{2}.

This implies that, for any
t\in[0,T)
,
w\in\Omega_{0}
,
n\geq n_{0}\vee N(w)
,

\displaystyle\langle\beta,X_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(t,\omega)\rangle>\exp\Big(-n^{2}-\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\alpha|^{2}\Big)\geq\varepsilon_{n}.

Thus, for any
w\in\Omega_{0}
,
n\geq n_{0}\vee N(w)
, we have
\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\omega)=T
.
Now for each
w\in\Omega
, we set
X(t,\omega):=\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,\omega){\bf 1}_{\Omega_{0}}(\omega)
.
Since
X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,\omega)=X_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\omega)
for any

if

, we have
X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,\omega)=X(t,\omega)
whenever

.
It follows from (
15

\displaystyle X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}})=\xi+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\sigma(s,X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}b(s,X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}f_{k,\varepsilon_{n}}(s,X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.~

This implies that

\displaystyle X(t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}})=\xi+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\sigma(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}b(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}}f_{k}(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.~

Since
\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to T
as

, we conclude that

satisfies the following SDE

\displaystyle X(t)=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}f_{k}(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}s,~\text{a.s.}

The estimate (
3
) follows from (

) and the Fatou lemma.

Finally, the pathwise uniqueness for equation (
1
) can be derived from the fact that

2.4
The existence of negative moments

We will prove that the negative moments
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-p}]
and
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-p}]
are finite for some
p>0
.
This fact plays a crucial role in the numerical approximation of
X
.

We set
p_{*}
by

p_{*}:=\inf_{t\in[0,T],\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{2k(t,\alpha)}{\|\overline{\sigma}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}}-1.

Then by Assumption
2.1
(iii),
p_{*}\geq 0
. In case
p_{*}=+\infty
, we set
p_{*}
to be any constant which is greater than
6
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
holds.
If
p_{*}>0
, then for any
p\in(0,p_{*})
, it holds that

\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-p}]<\infty.

(18)

Moreover, if
p_{*}>2
, then for any
p\in(0,p_{*}-2)
, it holds that

\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-p}\Big]<\infty.

(19)

Proof.

We first prove (
18
).
For
0<\varepsilon<\min_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle
, we define a stopping time

\tau_{\varepsilon}(w):=\inf\{t\in(0,T]~|~\text{there exsits }\alpha\in R_{+}\text{ such that }\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle\leq\varepsilon\}.

By using Itô’s formula, we have for any
\beta\in R_{+}
,

\displaystyle\log\langle\beta,X^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\log\langle\beta,\xi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\langle\beta,\sigma(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\rangle}{\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\langle\beta,b(s,X(s))\rangle}{\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\frac{k(s,\alpha)\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha,X(s)\rangle\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\langle\beta,\sigma_{\cdot,j}(s,X(s))\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

By Assumption
2.1
(iv) and (v), we have

\displaystyle\log\Big(\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,X^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)\rangle^{-p_{*}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\log\Big(\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,\xi\rangle^{-p_{*}}\Big)-M(t)-H(t)+p_{*}\int_{0}^{T}K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(20)

where we define stochastic processes
M
and
H
by for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle:=p_{*}\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\langle\beta,\sigma(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\rangle}{\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle},

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle:=p_{*}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle^{2}}\Big\{k(s,\beta)|\beta|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\langle\beta,\sigma_{\cdot,j}(s,X(s))\rangle^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Now we prove that
\langle M\rangle(t)\leq 2H(t)
for any
t\in[0,T]
.
Note that by the definition of
M
its quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle(t)=p_{*}^{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Big\langle\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\beta,X(s)\rangle}\beta,\sigma_{\cdot,j}(s,X(s))\Big\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence it is sufficient to prove that for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{W}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=p_{*}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Big\langle\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\beta,x\rangle}\beta,\sigma_{\cdot,j}(t,x))\Big\rangle^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{\langle\beta,\sigma_{\cdot,j}(t,x)\rangle^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}\leq\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{2k(t,\beta)|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}.

(21)

When
\sigma
is a diagonal square matrix and
\bar{\sigma}(t,x)=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}|\sigma_{i,i}(t,x)|
,
then thanks to Assumption
2.1
(iii) and (
2
), it holds that for any
x\in\mathbb{W}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\leq p_{*}\bar{\sigma}(t,x)^{2}\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Big(\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\beta,x\rangle}\beta_{j}\Big)^{2}+\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{(p_{*}+1)\overline{\sigma}(t,x)|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}\leq\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{2k(t,\beta)|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}.

On the other hand, if
\sigma
is not a diagonal square matrix,
\bar{\sigma}(t,x)=|\sigma(t,x)|
.
By Schwarz’s inequality, Assumption
2.1
(iii) and (
2
), it holds that for any
x\in\mathbb{W}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\leq p_{*}\Big|\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{1}{\langle\beta,x\rangle}\beta\Big|^{2}|\sigma(t,x)|^{2}+\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}|\sigma(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{(p_{*}+1)|\sigma(t,x)|^{2}|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}\leq\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\frac{2k(t,\beta)|\beta|^{2}}{\langle\beta,x\rangle^{2}}.

This proves (
21
), and thus
\langle M\rangle(t)\leq 2H(t)
for any
t\in[0,T]
.

By the definition of the stopping time
\tau_{\varepsilon}
,
(\exp(-M(t)-\langle M\rangle(t)/2))_{t\in[0,T]}
is a martingale. Thus, thanks to (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,X^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)\rangle^{-p_{*}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,\xi\rangle^{-p_{*}}\exp\Big(p_{*}\int_{0}^{T}K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)\mathbb{E}[\exp(-M(t)-\langle M\rangle(t)/2)\exp(\langle M\rangle(t)/2-H(t))]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,\xi\rangle^{-p_{*}}\exp\Big(p_{*}\int_{0}^{T}K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)\mathbb{E}[\exp(-M(t)-\langle M\rangle(t)/2)]

\displaystyle=\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,\xi\rangle^{-p_{*}}\exp\Big(p_{*}\int_{0}^{T}K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big).

Hence by Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,X(t)\rangle^{-p_{*}}\Big]\leq\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,\xi\rangle^{-p_{*}}\exp\Big(p_{*}\int_{0}^{T}K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big).

Finally, by using Hölder’s inequality and (
3
), for any
p\in(0,p_{*})
, it holds that,

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-p}\Big]=\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,X(t)\rangle^{-p}\prod_{\beta\neq\alpha}\langle\beta,X(t)\rangle^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\beta\in R_{+}}\langle\beta,X(t)\rangle^{-p_{*}}\Big]^{p/p_{*}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\beta\neq\alpha}(|\beta||X(t)|)^{\frac{pp_{*}}{p_{*}-p}}\Big]^{(p_{*}-p)/p_{*}}<\infty.

This concludes the proof of (
18
).

Now we prove (
19
).
Let
X_{\alpha}(t)=\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle
and
Y_{\alpha}(t)=X_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}
,
t\in[0,T]
and
\alpha\in R_{+}
.
Then
X_{\alpha}
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle X_{\alpha}(t)=\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\alpha,\sigma(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\alpha,b(s,X(s))\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{k(s,\beta)\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{X_{\beta}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s.

By using Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha}(t)^{p}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle^{-p}-p\int_{0}^{t}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}\langle\alpha,\sigma(s,X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\rangle-p\int_{0}^{t}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}\langle\alpha,b(s,X(s))\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-p\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{t}k(s,\beta)\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}Y_{\beta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{p(p+1)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}\langle\alpha,\sigma_{\cdot,j}(s,X(s))\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let
p\in(0,p_{*}-2)
.
Note that by Assumption
2.1
,
\sigma
and
k
are bounded, and
b
is of linear growth.
Hence, by using Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, Schwarz’s inequality, and Young’s inequality,
there exist positive constants
C_{1}(p),C_{2}(p)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{\alpha}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(p)\Big\{\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle^{-p}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{2p+2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{1/2}\Big]+\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}Y_{\beta}(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 196.32384pt\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}(1+|X(s)|^{p})+Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}]\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(p)\Big\{\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle^{-p}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{\alpha}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)^{p/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{1/2}\Big]+\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\beta}(s)^{p+2}]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}+Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}+|X(s)|^{p(p+2)}]\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\}.

By using Young’s inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle C_{2}(p)\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{\alpha}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)^{p/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{\alpha}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)^{p}\Big]+\frac{C_{2}(p)^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big].

Hence, by using (
3
) and (
18
), there exist a positive constant
C_{3}(p)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{\alpha}^{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(p)\Big\{\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle^{-p}+\sum_{\beta\in R_{+}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\beta}(s)^{p+2}]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+1}+Y_{\alpha}(s)^{p+2}+|X(t)|^{p(p+2)}]\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\}.

By Fatou’s lemma, we have
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{\alpha}(t)^{p}]<\infty
.
This concludes the proof of (

).

p_{*}>1
, then for any

Then since

and

) and (

3
A general theory on
\theta
-scheme for SDEs with one-sided Lipschitz drift

In this section, we propose a general procedure to verify the convergence of
\theta
-scheme applied for SDEs valued in an open set with one-sided Lipschitz drift.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
,
d,r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open set, and let
V=(V(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
be a solution of the SDEs of the form

\,\mathrm{d}V(t)=\sigma(t,V(t))\,\mathrm{d}B(t)+b(t,V(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+g(t,V(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,~t\in[0,T],~V(0)=\xi\in D,

where
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
,
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are mesurable functions.

We need the following assumptions for
V
and the coefficients
\sigma
,
b
and
g
.

Assumption 3.1
.

(i)

It holds that

\mathbb{P}(V(t)\in D,~\forall t\in[0,T])=1
.

(ii)

There exists
K>0
which satisfies for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times D
that
\langle x,g(t,x)\rangle\leq K
.
Moreover, it holds for all
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times D^{2}
that
\langle x-y,g(t,x)-g(t,y)\rangle\leq 0
.

(iii)

There exists
L_{b,\sigma}\in(0,\infty)
which satisfies that

\sup_{f\in\{b,\sigma\}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{1/2}}+\frac{|f(t,x)-f(t,y)|}{|x-y|}+|\sigma(t,x)|\bigg)\leq L_{b,\sigma}.

(iv)

There exists
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
h\in(0,h_{0})
, the system of equation
y=x+hg(t,y)
has a unique solution in
D
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq Th_{0}^{-1}
, and
\theta\in[0,1/2)
, and we set
\Delta t:=T/n
,
\Delta B_{\ell}:=B(t_{\ell+1})-B(t_{\ell})
and
t_{\ell}:=\ell\Delta t
for each
\ell=0,\ldots,n
.
Under Assumption
3.1
, we define the
\theta
-scheme
V^{(n,\theta)}=(V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))_{\ell=0}^{n}
for
V
as follows:

V^{(n,\theta)}(0):=V(0)=\xi\in D
and for each
\ell=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
V^{(n)}(t_{\ell+1})
is the unique solution in
D
of the following equation:

\begin{split}V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell+1})&=V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})+\sigma(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\Delta B_{\ell}+b(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\Delta t\\
&\quad+\theta g(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\Delta t+(1-\theta)g(t_{\ell+1},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell+1}))\Delta t.\end{split}

We define the approximation error
e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)
for
\ell=0,\ldots,n
by

\displaystyle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell):=V(t_{\ell})-V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}).

Then we use the following representation for
e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)
:

\displaystyle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)

\displaystyle=e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)+R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+\theta R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)+(1-\theta)R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)+r^{(n)}(\ell),

(22)

where
R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell):=R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)+R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)
,
r^{(n)}(\ell):=r^{(n)}(\sigma,\ell)+r^{(n)}(b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(g,\ell)
and

\displaystyle R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{\sigma(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))-\sigma(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\}\Delta B_{\ell},

\displaystyle R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{b(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))-b(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\}\Delta t,

\displaystyle R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{g(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))-g(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\}\Delta t,

\displaystyle r^{(n)}(\sigma,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big\{\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))\big\}\,\mathrm{d}B(s),

\displaystyle r^{(n)}(b,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big\{b(s,V(s))-b(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))\big\}\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle r^{(n)}(g,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big\{g(s,V(s))-\theta g(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))-(1-\theta)g(t_{\ell+1},V(t_{\ell+1}))\big\}\,\mathrm{d}s.

We obtain the following estimate for the
\theta
-scheme
V^{(n,\theta)}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.1
holds.
Assume that there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq Th_{0}^{-1},
it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}\mathbb{E}[|r^{(n)}(g,\ell)|^{2}]\leq C(\Delta t)^{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{\ell=0,1,\dots,n-1}\sup_{s\in[t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}]}\mathbb{E}[|V(s)-V(t_{\ell})|^{2}]\leq\frac{C}{n}.

(23)

Then for any
\theta_{0}\in[0,1/2)
, there exists
C_{\theta_{0}}>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}\Big|V(t_{\ell})-V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})\Big|^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\leq\frac{C_{\theta_{0}}}{\sqrt{n}}.

Remark 3.3
.

Higham, Mao, and Stuart
[
18
]
studied a backward Euler–Maruyama scheme. Under the assumptions of a one-sided Lipschitz condition and local Lipschitz continuity for the drift coefficient, they showed that the scheme converges in the
L^{2}
-sup norm with an order of
1/2
(see Theorem 5.3 in
[
18
]
). However, in our context, the drift coefficient
g
is not assumed to satisfy the local Lipschitz condition. Therefore, their result does not directly lead to the estimate presented in Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We first prove

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]}\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|V(t_{\ell})-V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})\Big|^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\leq\frac{C_{\theta_{0}}}{\sqrt{n}}.

(24)

Let
\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]
be fixed.
By using the representation (
22
), for each
\ell\in 0,1,\dots,n-1
, we have

|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)-(1-\theta)R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)|^{2}=|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)+\theta R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)+R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}.

By Assumption
3.1
(ii), it holds that
\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)\rangle\leq 0
.
Hence we obtain

\begin{split}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)|^{2}&\leq|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}+2\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)+R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)\rangle\\
&\quad+2\theta\langle R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)\rangle+|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}\\
&\quad+\theta^{2}|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)|^{2}-(1-\theta)^{2}|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)|^{2}.\end{split}

(25)

By using Young’s inequality,
2\theta|\langle a,b\rangle|\leq(1-2\theta)|a|^{2}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}|b|^{2}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

2\theta\langle R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)\rangle\leq(1-2\theta)|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)|^{2}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}.

Note that
\theta^{2}+(1-2\theta)=(1-\theta)^{2}
.
It follows from (
25
) and the fact that
\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]
that

\displaystyle|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}+2\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)+R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\{1+\frac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{1-2\theta_{0}}\Big\}|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\theta)^{2}\big\{|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)|^{2}-|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)|^{2}\big\}.

We consider the term
\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)\rangle
.
By using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Assumption
3.1
(iii), we have

\displaystyle\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),b(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))-b(t_{\ell},V^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\rangle\Delta t\leq L_{b,\sigma}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}\Delta t.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)|^{2}&\leq|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}+L_{b,\sigma}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}\Delta t+2\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)\rangle\\
&\quad+\Big\{1+\frac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{1-2\theta_{0}}\Big\}|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}\\
&\quad+(1-\theta)^{2}\big\{|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)|^{2}-|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)|^{2}\big\}.\end{split}

(26)

Since
\sigma
is bounded, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)\rangle]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),\{\sigma(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))-\sigma(t_{\ell},V^{n,\theta}(t_{\ell}))\}\mathbb{E}[\Delta B_{\ell}|\mathscr{F}_{t_{\ell}}]\rangle]=0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),r^{(n)}(\sigma,\ell)\rangle]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big\{\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))\big\}\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\Big|\mathscr{F}_{t_{\ell}}\Big]\Big\rangle\Big]=0.

Therefore by taking the expectation in the both sides in (
26
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)|^{2}]&\leq\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]+2L_{b,\sigma}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]\Delta t+2\mathbb{E}[\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),r^{(n)}(\ell)\rangle]\\
&\quad+\Big\{1+\frac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{1-2\theta_{0}}\Big\}\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}]\\
&\quad+(1-\theta)^{2}\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)|^{2}-|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)|^{2}].\end{split}

(27)

Next we consider the estimate for
\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,\ell)+r^{(n)}(\ell)|^{2}]
.
By the definition of
R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)
,
R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)
,
R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)
, the assumption of
r^{(n)}(g,\ell)
and Assumption
3.1
(iii), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,\ell)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]\Delta t,

(28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(b,\ell)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}](\Delta t)^{2},

(29)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|r^{(n)}(b,\ell)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\Delta t)^{3}.

(30)

Moreover, by using Itô’s isometry, Assumption
3.1
(iii), and (
23
), there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|r^{(n)}(\sigma,\ell)|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big\{\sigma_{i,j}(s,V(s))-\sigma_{i,j}(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))\big\}\,\mathrm{d}B_{j}(s)\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big\{\sigma_{i,j}(s,V(s))-\sigma_{i,j}(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))\big\}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]

\displaystyle\leq d\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big|\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(t_{\ell},V(t_{\ell}))\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]\leq C_{2}(\Delta t)^{2}.

(31)

By using Young’s inequality, assumption (
23
), and estimate (
30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),r^{(n)}(b,\ell)\rangle]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]+\frac{(\Delta t)^{-1}}{2}\mathbb{E}[r^{(n)}(b,\ell)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]+\frac{C_{1}}{2}(\Delta t)^{2},

(32)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell),r^{(n)}(g,\ell)\rangle]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]+\frac{(\Delta t)^{-1}}{2}\mathbb{E}[r^{(n)}(g,\ell)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]+\frac{C_{g}}{2}(\Delta t)^{2}.

(33)

Combining (
23
), (
27
), (
28
), (
31
), (
32
), (
33
), there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]+C_{3}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]\Delta t+C_{3}(\Delta t)^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\theta)^{2}\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell)|^{2}-|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,\ell+1)|^{2}].

Therefore, by induction with the fact that
R^{(n,\theta)}(g,0)=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell+1)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\Delta t\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(i)|^{2}]+C_{3}T(\Delta t)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\theta)^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}[|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,i)|^{2}-|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,i+1)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\Delta t\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(i)|^{2}]+C_{3}T\Delta t.

By using discrete Gronwall’s inequality (e.g. Chapter XIV, Theorem 1 and Remark 1,2 in
[
26
]
, page 436-437), there exists
C_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]}\sup_{\ell=0,\ldots,n}\mathbb{E}[|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\Delta t.

This concludes (
24
).

By using (
26
) and induction, we have

\displaystyle|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{b,\sigma}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}|e^{(n,\theta)}(m)|^{2}\Delta t+2\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(m),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,m)+r^{(n)}(m)\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\{1+\frac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{1-2\theta_{0}}\Big\}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,m)+r^{(n)}(m)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\theta)^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\big\{|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,m)|^{2}-|R^{(n,\theta)}(g,m+1)|^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq(1+L_{b,\sigma})\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}|e^{(n,\theta)}(m)|^{2}\Delta t+2\Big|\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(m),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,m)+r^{(n)}(\sigma,m)\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+(\Delta t)^{-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}|r^{(n)}(b,m)+r^{(n)}(g,m)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\Big\{1+\frac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{1-2\theta_{0}}\Big\}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\Big\{|R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,b,m)|^{2}+|r^{(n)}(m)|^{2}\Big\}.

Therefore, by taking the supremum with respect to
\ell=1,\ldots,n
and using (
23
), (
24
), (
28
),(
29
), (
30
) and (
31
), there exists
C_{5}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\Delta t+2\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}\Big|\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(m),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,m)+r^{(n)}(\sigma,m)\rangle\Big|\Big].

By the definitions of
R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,m)
and
r^{(n)}(\sigma,m)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\langle e^{(n,\theta)}(m),R^{(n,\theta)}(\sigma,m)+r^{(n)}(\sigma,m)\rangle=M^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}),

where
M^{(n,\theta)}
is a martingale defined by

\displaystyle M^{(n,\theta)}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\langle V(\eta(s))-V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s)),\{\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(\eta(s),V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s)))\}\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\rangle.

Here
\eta
is defined by
\eta(s):=t_{m}
if
s\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
.
By using Burkholder–Davis–Gundy’s inequality and Young’s inequality, there exists
C_{6}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}|M^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|M^{(n,\theta)}(t)|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|V(\eta(s))-V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s))|^{2}|\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(\eta(s),V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s)))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(\eta(s),V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s)))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}\Big]+C_{6}^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(\eta(s),V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s)))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big].

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}\Big]\leq 2C_{5}\Delta t+4C_{6}^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\sigma(s,V(s))-\sigma(\eta(s),V^{(n,\theta)}(\eta(s)))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big].

Finally, by using Assumption
2.1
(ii), Assumption
3.1
(iii) and (
24
), there exists
C_{7}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=0,\ldots,n}|e^{(n,\theta)}(\ell)|^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{C_{7}}{\sqrt{n}}.

This concludes the assertion.
∎

4
Numerical schemes for non-colliding particle systems

Throughout this section, we always suppose that Assumption
2.1
holds.
Additionally, we assume that the functions
b
,
\sigma
, and
k
are
1/2
–Hölder continuous in time, that is,

\displaystyle\begin{split}[f]_{1/2}&:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{W},~t,s\in[0,T],~t\neq s}\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{1/2}}<\infty,~f\in\{b,\sigma\},\\
[k]_{1/2}&:=\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+},~t,s\in[0,T],~t\neq s}\frac{|k(t,\alpha)-k(s,\alpha)|}{|t-s|^{1/2}}<\infty.\end{split}

(34)

4.1
\theta
-Euler–Maruyama scheme

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
c:R\to(0,\infty)
be a measurable function.
Then from Lemma 4.1 in
[
29
]
, there exists a unique solution
y
in the Weyl chamber
\mathbb{W}
of the following equation:

y=x+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{c(\alpha)}{\langle\alpha,y\rangle}\alpha.

(35)

Building on this fact, for any
\theta\in[0,1/2)
, we define a
\theta
-Euler–Maruyama scheme
X^{(n,\theta)}=(X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))_{\ell=0}^{n}
for
X
as follows:
we set
X^{(n,\theta)}(0):=X(0)=\xi\in\mathbb{W}
, and for each
\ell=0,\ldots,n-1
, we define
X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell+1})
as the unique solution in
\mathbb{W}
of the following equation:

\displaystyle X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell+1})

\displaystyle=X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})+\sigma(t_{\ell},X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\Delta B_{\ell}+b(t_{\ell},X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell}))\Delta t

\displaystyle\quad+\theta\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{k(t_{\ell},\alpha)}{\langle\alpha,X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})\rangle}\alpha\Delta t+(1-\theta)\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{k(t_{\ell+1},\alpha)}{\langle\alpha,X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell+1})\rangle}\alpha\Delta t.

We obtain the following result concerning the rate of strong convergence.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
p_{*}>6
.
Then for any
\theta_{0}\in[0,1/2)
, there exists
C_{\theta_{0}}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}\Big|X(t_{\ell})-X^{(n,\theta)}(t_{\ell})\Big|^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\leq\frac{C_{\theta_{0}}}{\sqrt{n}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
and Theorem
3.2
, we need to prove that there exists
C>0
such that

\sup_{\ell=1,\ldots,n}\mathbb{E}[|r^{(n)}(f_{k},\ell)|^{2}]\leq C(\Delta t)^{3}.

By the definition of
r^{(n)}(f_{k},\ell)
and using Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|r^{(n)}(f_{k},\ell)|^{2}&\leq 2\theta^{2}\Delta t\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big|f_{k}(s,X(s))-f_{k}(t_{\ell},X(t_{\ell}))\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+2(1-\theta)^{2}\Delta t\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big|f_{k}(s,X(s))-f_{k}(t_{\ell+1},X(t_{\ell+1}))\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(36)

By using Assumption (iii), (
34
) and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for any
s,t\in[0,T]
with
s\neq t
and
x,y\in\mathbb{W}
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{k}(s,x)-f_{k}(t,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|f_{k}(s,x)-f_{k}(t,x)|^{2}+|f_{k}(t,x)-f_{k}(t,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2[k]_{1/2}^{2}|R_{+}||t-s|\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}+2|x-y|^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\|k(\cdot,\alpha)\|^{2}_{\infty}|R_{+}|\frac{|\alpha|^{4}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}\langle\alpha,y\rangle^{2}}.

Therefore, by (
36
), Hölder’s inequality, Theorem
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
, there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|r^{(n)}(f_{k},\ell)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\Delta t)^{3}\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-2}]

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}\Delta t\sup_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[\langle\alpha,X(t)\rangle^{-6}]^{1/3}\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\big\{\mathbb{E}[|X(s)-X(t_{\ell})|^{6}]^{1/3}+\mathbb{E}[|X(s)-X(t_{\ell+1})|^{6}]^{1/3}\big\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\Delta t)^{3}.

The proof is thus completed.

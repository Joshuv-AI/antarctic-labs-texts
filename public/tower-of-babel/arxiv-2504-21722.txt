Discrete Generating Series and Linear Difference Equations
V. S. Alekseev 1, T. Cuchta 2, A. P. Lyapin 1

Abstract
We define discrete generating series for arbitrary functions f : ℤ n → ℂ f\colon\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C} and derive functional relations that these series satisfy. For linear difference equations with constant coefficients, we establish explicit functional equations linking the generating series to the initial data, and for equations with polynomial coefficients, we introduce an analogue of Stanley’s D D -finiteness criterion, proving that a discrete generating series is D D -finite if and only if the corresponding sequence is polynomially recursive. The framework is further generalized to multidimensional settings, where we investigate the interplay between discrete generating series and solutions to Cauchy problems for difference equations. Key structural properties are uncovered through the introduction of polynomial shift operators and projection techniques. The theory is illustrated with concrete examples, including the tribonacci recurrence and Schröder’s second problem.

2.2
D
-Finite Discrete Generating Series

In this section, we consider discrete generating series for solutions of linear difference equations with polynomial coefficients and prove an analogue of R. Stanley’s theorem that derives conditions for the

Let
\Delta f(z)=f(z+1)-f(z)

\Delta z^{\underline{x}}=xz^{\underline{x-1}}

\Delta
and the discrete monomial are a discrete analogue of the power rule for classical derivatives. Further,

\Delta F(\xi;\ell;z)=\Delta\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}=\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}\ell xf(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x-1}}.

Define the operator
\theta=\ell^{-1}z\rho\Delta

15
]

\theta z^{\underline{\ell x}}=xz^{\underline{\ell x}}.

(4)

Let

be a polynomial difference operator. With these definitions, we know from
[
5

\theta^{n}F(\xi;\ell;z)=\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}x^{n}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}},

\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}p(x)f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}=p(\theta)\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}.

(5)

Now we prove a simple lemma.

Lemma 1

The following relation holds for all
x=1,2,3,\ldots
:

(\theta-x)z^{\underline{\ell x}}=0.

Proof

Using (
4
), compute

(\theta-x)z^{\underline{\ell x}}=\theta z^{\underline{\ell x}}-xz^{\underline{\ell x}}=xz^{\underline{\ell x}}-xz^{\underline{\ell x}}=0,

which completes the proof.

We now introduce a generalization of the difference equation (
1

Definition 3

Let
\{p_{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{r}
be a set of polynomials, where
p_{r}(x)
is not identically zero. A homogeneous linear difference equation with polynomial coefficients is a relation of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r}p_{k}(x)f(x+k)=0.

(6)

Solving the Cauchy problem (
2
), (
6
) means finding a function

that satisfies (

In

, sequences

that satisfy (

\displaystyle\left(P_{r}(z)\frac{\partial\,^{r}}{\partial z^{r}}+\cdots+P_{1}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+P_{0}(z)\right)F(z)=0.

It was proven that the generating series

-finite if and only if the sequences

is

-recursive

-finiteness for discrete generating series resembles (

Definition 4

The discrete generating series

\displaystyle\Big(P_{r}(\xi,\ell;z)\theta^{r}+\cdots+P_{1}(\xi,\ell;z)\theta+P_{0}(\xi,\ell;z)\Big)F(\xi,\ell;z)=0.

Now we prove an analogue of

The discrete generating series

is

-recursive.

Proof

First assume that the series

\begin{array}[]{ll}0&=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{r}p_{k}(x)f(x+k)\xi^{x+r}z^{\underline{\ell(x+r)}}\\

Now let

Conversely, assume that the sequence

\Delta_{j}z^{\underline{x}}=x_{j}z^{\underline{x-e^{j}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\geqslant 0}\ell_{j}x_{j}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x-e^{j}}}.

Example 1

multiply both sides by

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\geqslant 3}^{\infty}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}-\xi z^{\underline{\ell}}\rho^{\ell}\sum_{x\geqslant 2}^{\infty}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\xi^{2}z^{\underline{2\ell}}\rho^{2\ell}\sum_{x\geqslant 1}^{\infty}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}-\xi^{3}z^{\underline{3\ell}}\rho^{3\ell}\sum_{x\geqslant 0}^{\infty}f(x)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}.

Example 3

We consider a functional equation

\begin{gathered}\xi^{2}z^{\underline{2\ell}}\rho^{2\ell}\theta^{3}F(\xi;\ell;z)+\xi^{3}z^{\underline{3\ell}}\rho^{3\ell}\theta F(\xi;\ell;z)=0.\end{gathered}

Changing the summation indices in each sum yields

\sum_{x\geqslant 3}\left((x-2)^{3}f(x-2)+(x-3)f(x-3)\right)\xi^{x}z^{\underline{\ell x}}=0,

that leads to a difference equation

(x-2)^{3}f(x-2)+(x-3)f(x-3)=0,

or equivalently

\begin{gathered}(x+1)^{3}f(x+1)+xf(x)=0.\end{gathered}

5
Conclusion

We have laid the foundation for the theory of discrete generating series in the context of multidimensional difference equations with polynomial coefficients. By introducing a multidimensional polynomial shift operator, we derived three functional equations that these discrete generating series satisfy, uncovering key structural properties. The falling factorial functions in this framework are referred to as generalized polynomials in the context of time scales. This connection hints at potential extensions of this research to time scales, a direction that was arguably foreshadowed by the definition of moment generating series for distributions in
[
36
]
. A particularly intriguing question is identifying the appropriate analogue of (
9
) for an arbitrary time scale. Exploring this problem from a generating series perspective may yield new insights and deepen our understanding of the subject.

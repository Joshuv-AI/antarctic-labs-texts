Introduction

A classical line of research in extremal graph theory concerns about the minimum degree condition that guarantees the existence of certain spanning subgraphs. Once such a result is established, a natural further question is to measure the
robustness
of the graph with respect to the property of containing the given spanning structure. This direction of research aims to strengthen classical results in extremal and probabilistic combinatorics.

Hamiltonicity is one of the
most central problems in graph theory. A classical result proved by Dirac in 1952
[
6
]
states that for
n\geq 3
, every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
(known as
Dirac graph
) contains a Hamilton cycle.
A natural way to strengthen Dirac’s theorem is to ask how many Hamilton cycles a Dirac graph must contain. Sárközy, Selkow and Szemerédi
[
12
]
proved that every Dirac graph contains at least
c^{n}n!
Hamilton cycles for some small positive constant
c
, and conjectured that
c
can be improved to
\frac{1}{2}-o(1)
. This conjecture was resolved by Cuckler and Kahn
[
5
]
.
We refer the reader to a survey of Sudakov
[
24
]
where various measures of robustness and relevant results are collected.

We introduce the robustness problem from the following two perspectives.
Let
G
be a graph with a property
\mathcal{P}
.
On the one hand, we sample each edge uniformly at random in
G
and study the threshold for the property
\mathcal{P}
of the resulting random subgraph.
More precisely, let

be a random subgraph of
G
such that every edge in
G
is kept independently and randomly with probability

Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov
[
22
]
studied the threshold for hamiltonicity and showed that there exists a constant
C
such that for any
n
-vertex Dirac graph
G
and
p\geq\frac{C\log n}{n}
,
with high probability
1

G(p)

On the other hand, one may sample vertices rather than edges at random and investigate the probability that the induced subgraph on such a random vertex set still possesses property

Conjecture 1.1

(

Any

1.1
The Hajnal-Szemerédi theorem

Given graphs

A

Theorem 1.2

(

Let

n

The minimum degree condition is best possible by considering slightly unbalanced complete

[

By analogy with Conjecture

Conjecture 1.3

(

For any

In the following, we make some remarks on this conjecture.

(1)

The regularity condition

cannot be lowered to

.
Indeed, consider the balanced complete

-partite graph

is

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{r}\leq n\cdot\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}^{r}\leq n\left(\frac{2^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n/2}}\right)^{r}=n\frac{2^{rn}}{(\pi n/2)^{r/2}}.

(2)

The regularity assumption cannot be reduced to a minimum degree of

Consider the graph

(with vertex partition

) by adding a spanning star in each part.
In

induces a subgraph that contains a

by less than
r
2

2

Note that each

. Then the number of such subsets

\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|s_{j}-s_{1}|<2r\\

Our main contribution is to resolve Conjecture

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
r\geq 2
, there is a constant
c>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large

G

((r-1)n+1)

Theorem 1.5

Notation

For a graph
G
, let

and

denote the vertex set and edge set of

and

. For a vertex

, let

be the neighborhood of

. For two vertex subsets

, let

and

. We let

denote the degree of

and write
d_{G}(v,U)=|N_{G}(v,U)|
. As we do elsewhere, we omit

from the subscript whenever there is no risk of confusion. The minimum and maximum degree of

is denoted by

and

respectively.

For a subset

, let

denote the graph induced by

. Let

be the graph induced by

. Given a subset

, let

denote the bipartite graph with bipartition

and edges of the form

with

and

. Denote

.

For any integers

, define
[a,b]:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:a\leq i\leq b\}
and
[b]:=[1,b]
. Given real numbers

. When we write

, we always mean that

are constants in

Let

Theorem 2.1

(Non-extremal case)
.

Suppose that

Theorem 2.2

(Extremal case)

Suppose that

,

-factor with probability at least

In the extremal case, the existence of a

-independent

-independent

-sets

\gamma
-independent set of size

.
By suitably reassigning some vertices,

of

(see Definition

Definition 2.3
.

Let

and
\alpha>0
. Suppose that

is a graph on
rn

\mathcal{P}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{t}\}

•

•

•

We now introduce the following definition of our desired partition.

Definition 2.4

(Good partition)
.

For

with

, and positive constants

3
Non-extremal case

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, which concerns the case when the host graph
G
contains no large sparse set. The existence of a
K_{r}
-factor in such a graph
G
was established by Gan, Han, and Hu
[
13
]
, under a slightly weaker minimum degree condition.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Suppose
r\geq 3
and
\frac{1}{n}\ll\alpha\ll\gamma\ll\frac{1}{r}
. Let
G
be an
rn
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq(r-1-\alpha)n
. If
G
has no
\gamma
-independent set of size
n
, then there is a
K_{r}
-factor in
G
.

Next, we introduce the Frieze-Kannan Regularity Lemma
[
11
]
, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
11
]
)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there are
T,n_{0}>0
such that the following holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Every
n
-vertex graph
G
admits a vertex partition
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{t}\}
with
t\leq T
and
|V_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|V_{t}|\leq|V_{1}|+1
satisfying for any
A,B\subseteq V(G)
we have

\Big|e(G[A,B])-\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq t}\frac{|A\cap V_{i}||B\cap V_{j}|}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}e(G[V_{i},V_{j}])\Big|<\varepsilon n^{2}.

Now we are ready to present a short proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Choose
r\geq 3,n\in\mathbb{N}
and a constant
\gamma
satisfying
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\frac{1}{r}
. Let
G
be an
((r-1)n+1)
-regular graph on
rn
vertices such that
G
contains no
\gamma
-independent set of size
n
. Let
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{t}\}
be a partition of
V(G)
obtained from Lemma
3.2
applied with
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{10}\gamma
. Consider
G[\frac{1}{2}]=G[S]
, where
S
is a random subset of
V(G)
with
r\mid|S|
. Note that
r\mid|S|
is a necessary condition for
G[S]
to contain a
K_{r}
-factor, which occurs with probability
\frac{1}{r}
. By Chernoff bound, w.h.p.
|S|=\frac{r}{2}n\pm n^{0.6}
,
|S\cap V_{i}|=\frac{rn}{2t}\pm n^{0.6}
for each
i\in[t]
, and every vertex has degree
\frac{r-1}{2}n\pm n^{0.6}
in
G[S]
.
Conditioned on these events, we prove the following claim.

Claim 3.3
.

G[S]
contains no
\frac{\gamma}{25}
-independent set of size
\frac{|S|}{r}
.

Proof.

Given any
A^{\prime}\subseteq S
with
|A^{\prime}|=\frac{|S|}{r}
, we are to show that
e(G[A^{\prime}])\geq\frac{\gamma}{25}n^{2}
.
Construct
A\subseteq V(G)
so that
|A\cap V_{i}|=2|A^{\prime}\cap V_{i}|\pm 2n^{0.6}
for all
i\in[t]
. Notice that
|A|=n\pm 3tn^{0.6}
and
e(G[A])\geq\frac{4}{5}\gamma n^{2}
as
G
itself contains no
\gamma
-independent set of size
n
. Lemma
3.2
implies that

•

e(G[A])
differs by at most
\frac{\gamma}{10}n^{2}
from
\sum:=\sum_{i,j}\frac{|A\cap V_{i}||A\cap V_{j}|}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}e(G[V_{i},V_{j}])
;

•

e(G[A^{\prime}])
differs by at most
\frac{\gamma}{10}n^{2}
from
\sum_{i,j}\frac{|A^{\prime}\cap V_{i}||A^{\prime}\cap V_{j}|}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}e(G[V_{i},V_{j}])\geq{\frac{1}{5}}\sum
.

It follows that

e(G[A^{\prime}])\geq\frac{1}{5}\sum-\frac{1}{10}\gamma n^{2}\geq\frac{1}{5}\Big(e(G[A])-\frac{\gamma}{10}n^{2}\Big)-\frac{\gamma}{10}n^{2}\geq\frac{7}{50}\gamma n^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{10}n^{2}=\frac{\gamma}{25}n^{2}.

That is,
G[S]
contains no
\frac{\gamma}{25}
-independent set of size
\frac{|S|}{r}
.
∎

Combined with the condition
\delta(G[S])\geq\frac{r-1}{2}n-n^{0.6}
, Theorem
3.1
implies that
G[S]
contains a
K_{r}
-factor, which happens with probability at least
\frac{1}{r}-o(1)
.
∎

Let

Lemma 4.2

4.1
Proof of Theorem

In this subsection we prove Theorem

As the size of a random subset of

Lemma 4.3

(
[

]
)

If

\mathbb{P}(a\sqrt{n}/2\leq X-n/2\leq b\sqrt{n}/2)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-t^{2}/2}\,dt+o(1)
.

We will also use the following version of Chernoff bound.

Lemma 4.4

(Chernoff bound)
.

Let

be a binomial random variable and

\mathbb{P}[|X-\mathbb{E}X|\geq\delta\mathbb{E}X]\leq 2\exp(-\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}X/(2+\delta)).

The following lemma ensures that for any
rn
-vertex
((r-1)n+1)
-regular graph

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be an

-regular graph on
rn
vertices and

is a minimum vertex cover of

.

Remark A

Let

. Without loss of generality, assume that
N(w_{1})\cap V(G_{1})\neq\emptyset
. If
K\in\mathcal{F}
, then update
\mathcal{F}
by replacing the triangle
K
with
T_{2}
in
G_{2}
. By Lemma
5.5
, the updated subgraph induced by
(V(G_{1}\cup G_{2})\setminus V(T_{2}))\cup\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\}
has a perfect matching, as desired. If
K
is chosen from some
K_{r}
in
\mathcal{H}
, denoted as
K^{2}
, then update
\mathcal{H}
by replacing
K^{2}
with
K^{2,j}
for some
j
such that they have the same index vector with respect to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. By Lemma
5.5
, we obtain that the current subgraph induced by
(V(G_{1}\cup G_{2})\setminus V(K^{2,j}))\cup\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\}
contains a perfect matching, as desired.

It is easy to see that the updated tiling
\mathcal{K}
has index vector
|\mathcal{K}|(1,\ldots,1,r-s)
, while the index vectors of updated
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{F}
stay unchanged. Moreover,
\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{R}
uses at most one edge in
G^{\prime}[U\setminus U^{\prime}]
. In all possible cases,
G^{\prime}[B^{\prime\prime}]
contains a
K_{r-s}
-factor, which is denoted as
\{K^{1},\ldots,K^{t}\}
for
t:=\frac{|B^{\prime\prime}|}{r-s}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{K^{t+1},\ldots,K^{t+(r-s)b}\}
(could be empty if
b=0
).

Step 2.3. Contracting and then verifying
(B1)
-
(B3)
.

Contracting each
K^{i}
(
i\in[t]
) into a vertex
w_{i}
and each
K^{j}
(
j\in[t+1,t+(r-s)b]
) into an edge
w_{j}w_{j}^{\prime}
, yields a new vertex set
B^{*}
of size
t+2(r-s)b
.
We then construct an auxiliary graph
G^{*}
with
V(G^{*})=\big(\bigcup_{i\in[s]}A_{i}^{\prime}\big)\cup B^{*}
, and
xy\in E(G^{*})
if and only if one of the following holds:

•

x\in A_{i}^{\prime}
,
y\in A_{j}^{\prime}
, and
xy\in E(G)
for distinct
i,j\in[s]
;

•

x=w_{i}\in B^{*}
,
y\in A_{j}^{\prime}
, and
y\in N_{G}(V(K^{i}))
for
j\in[s]
and
i\in[t]
;

•

x\in\{w_{j},w_{j}^{\prime}\}\subseteq B^{*}
,
y\in A_{i}^{\prime}
, and
y\in N_{G}(V(K^{j}))
for
i\in[s]
and
j\in[{t+1,t+(r-s)b}]
;

•

\{x,y\}=\{w_{j},w_{j}^{\prime}\}
for some
j\in[t+1,t+(r-s)b].

Note that
\mathcal{P}^{*}=\{A_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{s}^{\prime},B^{*}\}
is a partition of
G^{*}
. We first estimate the sizes of
|G^{*}|
and
|B^{*}|
to verify
(B1)
.

(i) Verify
|B^{*}|
.

Notice that

\displaystyle|B^{*}|=\frac{|B^{\prime\prime}|}{r-s}+2(r-s)b

\displaystyle=\frac{|B^{\prime}|-(r-s+1)(r-s)b}{r-s}+2(r-s)b

\displaystyle=\frac{|B^{\prime}|}{r-s}+(r-s-1)b

\displaystyle=\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}+(r-s)b.

It follows that

\displaystyle|G^{*}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|A_{i}^{\prime}|+|B^{*}|=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|A_{i}^{\prime}|+\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}+(r-s)\cdot\Big(\frac{|B^{\prime}|}{r-s}-\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|A_{i}^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|+\frac{1-r+s}{r}|G^{\prime}|=|G^{\prime}|+\frac{1-r+s}{r}|G^{\prime}|=(s+1)\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}.

(8)

As
b\leq r\alpha n
and
\frac{|G|}{r}\leq 2\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}
, (
5.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}=\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}\ \text{and}\ |B^{*}|-\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}=(r-s)b\leq r^{2}\alpha n\leq 2r^{2}\alpha\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}.

(9)

Hence,
B^{*}
satisfies
(B1)
with parameter
2r^{2}\alpha
in place of
\alpha
.

(ii) Verify
|A_{i}^{\prime}|
.

By (
5
) and (
9
), for each
i\in[p]
we have

\displaystyle|A_{i}^{\prime}|-\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}\leq\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}-\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}=0,

for each
i\in[p+1,s]
we have

\displaystyle|A_{i}^{\prime}|-\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}

\displaystyle=|A_{i}^{\prime}|-\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}\leq|A_{i}|-\frac{|V(\mathcal{K})|}{r}-\frac{|V(\mathcal{R})|}{r}-\frac{|G|-|V(\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{R})|}{r}

\displaystyle=|A_{i}|-n+q\leq 2\alpha n\leq 3\alpha\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}.

In a summary,
G^{*}
satisfies
|A_{1}^{\prime}|,\ldots,|A_{s}^{\prime}|,|B^{*}|\leq\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}+2r^{2}\alpha\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}
as desired in
(B1)
under the partition
\mathcal{P}^{*}
.

(iii) Verify
(B2)
and
(B3)
.

Notice that after covering all bad or exceptional vertices in Step 1, each

satisfies
\Delta(G^{\prime}[D^{\prime}])\leq\max\{\beta^{\prime}n,\alpha^{1/5}n\}
.
As
\alpha\ll\beta^{\prime}
, for each vertex
v\in A_{i}^{\prime}
with

and each

we have

d_{G^{*}}(v,D^{*})\geq|D^{*}|-\beta^{\prime}n-|V(\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{R})|\geq\big(1-2\beta^{\prime}\big)|D^{*}|;

for each vertex
v\in B^{*}
and each
i\in[s]
we have

d_{G^{*}}(v,A_{i}^{\prime})\geq|A_{i}^{\prime}|-(r-s)\beta^{\prime}n-|V(\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{R})|\geq\big(1-2(r-s)\beta^{\prime}\big)|A_{i}^{\prime}|.

Therefore,
(B2)
holds with parameter
2(r-s)\beta^{\prime}
in place of
\beta
.

It follows from the discussion in
(ii)
that
|A_{i}^{\prime}|>\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}
only if
|A_{i}|>n
.
Therefore,

•

for each

with
|A_{i}^{\prime}|>\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}
,
G[A_{i}^{\prime}]
contains a matching of size at least
|A_{i}^{\prime}|-\frac{|G^{\prime}|}{r}=|A_{i}^{\prime}|-\frac{|G^{*}|}{s+1}
, by combining (
6
), (

uses at most one edge in

;

•

and

contains a matching of size

.

Thus
(B3)
holds. Notice that

. By Lemma

has a

for some

5.3
Proof of Lemma
4.5

In this subsection, we prove Lemma
4.5
, which establishes the existence of a subgraph with a large minimum vertex cover.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Let
G
be an
((r-1)n+1)
-regular graph on
rn
vertices, and let
\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{r}\}
be a balanced partition of
V(G)
. Suppose that
A_{i}^{1}
is a minimum vertex cover of
G[A_{i}]
for each
i\in[r]
. Without loss of generality, assume that
|A_{1}^{1}|\geq|A_{2}^{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|A_{r}^{1}|
. Clearly, there exists a vertex cover
C_{i}
of
G[A_{i}]
with size
|A_{2}^{1}|
for each
i\in[2,r]
. For convenience, let
C_{1}:=A_{1}^{1}
. Denote
x_{i}:=|C_{i}|
and
B_{i}:=A_{i}\setminus C_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
. Hence,
G[B_{i}]=\emptyset
for each
i\in[r]
. We proceed by considering the following two possible cases.

Case 1.
e\Big(B_{1},\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j}\Big)\leq e\Big(C_{2},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)
.

Since
G
is
((r-1)n+1)
-regular, one has

e(C_{2},B_{1})\leq|C_{2}|((r-1)n+1)-e\Big(C_{2},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big).

Note that as
x_{2}=x_{3}=\cdots=x_{r}
,

e\Big(B_{1},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)\leq\sum_{j\neq 1}(n-x_{1})(n-x_{j})=(n-x_{1})(r-1)(n-x_{2}).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle e\Big(B_{1},\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|((r-1)n+1)-e(B_{1},C_{2})-e\Big(B_{1},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq(n-x_{1})((r-1)n+1)-x_{2}((r-1)n+1)+e\Big(C_{2},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)-e\Big(B_{1},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq(n-x_{1}-x_{2})((r-1)n+1)+e\Big(C_{2},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)-(n-x_{1})(r-1)(n-x_{2}).

By our assumption in this case, one has

\displaystyle(n-x_{1}-x_{2})((r-1)n+1)\leq(n-x_{1})(r-1)(n-x_{2}).

It follows that
n\leq(r-1)x_{1}x_{2}+(x_{1}+x_{2})\leq(r-1)x_{1}^{2}+2x_{1}.
Thus,
x_{1}\geq\frac{1}{r}\sqrt{n}
, as desired.

Case 2.
e\Big(B_{1},\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j}\Big)>e\Big(C_{2},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)
.

Notice that

e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j},\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big)\leq\Big|\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j}\Big|((r-1)n+1)-e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j},B_{1}\Big).

Therefore,

\displaystyle e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j},C_{2}\Big)=

\displaystyle\Big|\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big|((r-1)n+1)-e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j},\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j}\Big)-e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j},\bigcup_{t\in[r]}B_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big|\bigcup_{j\neq 1}B_{j}\Big|((r-1)n+1)-\Big|\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j}\Big|((r-1)n+1)+e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j},B_{1}\Big)-\sum_{j\neq 1}\sum_{t\neq j}e(B_{j},B_{t})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle((r-1)n+1)\Big((r-1)n-\sum_{j\neq 1}x_{j}-\sum_{j\neq 2}x_{j}\Big)+e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j},B_{1}\Big)-\sum_{j\neq 1}\sum_{t\neq j}(n-x_{j})(n-x_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((r-1)n+1)\Big((r-1)n-(r-1)x_{2}-x_{1}-(r-2)x_{2}\Big)+e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j},B_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle-(r-1)(n-x_{2})\Big((r-1)n-x_{1}-(r-2)x_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r-1)n-x_{1}-(2r-3)x_{2}-(r-1)x_{1}x_{2}-(r-1)(r-2)x_{2}^{2}+e\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq 2}C_{j},B_{1}\Big).

By the assumption in this case, one has

(r-1)n\leq x_{1}+(2r-3)x_{2}+(r-1)x_{1}x_{2}+(r-1)(r-2)x_{2}^{2}\leq(r-1)\big((r-1)x_{1}^{2}+2x_{1}\big).

Thus,
x_{1}\geq\frac{1}{r}\sqrt{n}
, as desired.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

Draganić, Keevash and Müyesser
[
7
]
proposed a plausible class of extremal construction for Conjecture
1.3
: slightly unbalanced complete
r
-partite graphs with a suitable factor in the largest part
A
. They conjectured that the optimal constant
c
should be
\frac{1}{r^{2}}
, where in the random induced subgraph
G[S]
we have one
probability factor of
\frac{1}{r}
for
r
dividing
|S|
and another for
A\cap S
being the largest part. In what follows, we present a construction to demonstrate that these two events alone are insufficient to guarantee the existence of a
K_{r}
-factor in
G[S]
.

Assume that
r\geq 3
. We first consider an
rn
-vertex complete
r
-partite graph
G_{0}
with vertex partition
A_{1}\cup\ldots\cup A_{r}
, where
|A_{1}|=n+r-1
and
|A_{2}|=\cdots=|A_{r}|=n-1
. Let
G
be a graph obtained from
G_{0}
by embedding an
r
-regular graph into
A_{1}
such that
G[A_{1}]
is triangle-free. Clearly,
G
is
((r-1)n+1)
-regular and
G[A_{i}]
is empty for every
i\in[2,r]
. Consider an arbitrary vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
. A necessary condition for
G[S]
to contain a
K_{r}
-factor is that

•

r\mid|S|
;

•

\frac{|S|}{r}\leq|A_{1}\cap S|\leq\frac{2|S|}{r}
;

•

|A_{i}\cap S|\leq\frac{|S|}{r}
for all
i\in[2,r]
.

This implies that the optimal constant

c
in Conjecture
1.3
must be strictly less than
\frac{1}{r^{2}}
for every
r\geq 3
.

In Theorem
1.4
we assume that
G
is
((r-1)n+1)
-regular, and we have remarked that the regularity requirement cannot be relaxed to a mere minimum degree condition. The need for regularity, however, arises only in Lemma
4.5
. In fact, by carefully examining the proof of Lemma
4.5
, we can replace the regularity assumption by a weaker degree condition:

\displaystyle(r-1)n+1\leq\delta(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq(r-1)n+n^{0.5}.

(10)

Therefore, we can slightly extend Theorem
1.4
under (
10
). Furthermore, one may also consider the more general setting of a
d
-regular graph where
d\geq(r-1)n+1
, and ask for determining the probability that its random induced subgraph
G[p]
contains a
K_{r}
-factor, where
p\in(0,1)
.

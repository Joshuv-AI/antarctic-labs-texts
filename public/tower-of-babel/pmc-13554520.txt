An adjunctive dermocosmetic regimen improves clinical outcomes and tolerability in Japanese patients on topical acne therapy: A real-world observational study
Authors: Ichiro Kurokawa, Miwa Kobayashi, Yuko Nomura, Masatoshi Abe, Xuebing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveA real-world observational study was conducted to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of an adjunctive dermocosmetic regimen in topical acne therapy.MethodsFifty Japanese patients who had been undergoing topical prescription treatment for >4 weeks adjunctively used two dermocosmetic products, a facial cleanser and moisturizer cream for 3 months.ResultsThe Global Evaluation Acne scale score, sebum production, skin findings, subjective symptoms, and patient quality of life were significantly improved after 3 months dermocosmetics of use. Tolerability for the dermocosmetics use was rated as “well-tolerated” or “tolerated” by 75.5% of physicians and 73.5% of patients. Satisfaction with dermocosmetics use was high, with 87.5% of physicians and 92.0% of patients rating their satisfaction level as “very satisfied” or “satisfied.” Most patients (86.0%) agreed or strongly agreed that they preferred using the study dermocosmetics over their previous cosmetics. Furthermore, 70.0% and 72.0% of the patients agreed that the dermocosmetic facial cleanser and moisturizing cream, respectively, improved the tolerability of their acne treatment.ConclusionsThese findings suggest that the dermocosmetic regimen involving the use of a facial cleanser and moisturizing cream, when used in combination with topical prescription drugs is beneficial, well-tolerated, and acceptable among patients for the treatment of acne in real-world routine clinical practice in Japan.Keywords: Adjunctive dermocosmetics, facial cleanser, moisturizer cream, acne, real-world observational study, Japan

Introduction

Acne is a common chronic inflammatory disease resulting from increased sebum production, altered keratinization, inflammation, and bacterial colonization. 1 The disease can persist into adulthood, exerting detrimental effects on self-esteem, and usually requires long and continuous treatment during adolescence.1,2 Prescription drugs are key agents for chronic acne management. However, they often cause local skin-related adverse effects, such as skin dryness and irritation, which can compromise treatment adherence during the chronic management course.3–5

Dermocosmetics are skincare solutions that use sophisticated, dermatologically active ingredients to directly treat symptoms of various skin conditions (in ways that would not occur with the use of a vehicle alone).6,7 The use of dermocosmetics for acne management reduces both inflammatory and non-inflammatory acne lesion counts in milder forms of acne as standalone skin care, reduces the adverse effects of pharmacological treatments, and contributes to high levels of patient satisfaction and treatment adherence.8–11

A specific dermocosmetic facial cleanser, Effaclar H Isobiome Cleanser,® containing active ingredients such as niacinamide, glycerin, mannose, aqua posae filiformis (APF), and shea butter, has demonstrated beneficial effects and good tolerance in Japanese women when used as an adjunct to topical prescription drugs for acne management. 12 Effaclar H Isobiome Cream® has been developed as a dermocosmetic moisturizer cream and contains niacinamide, 2-oleamido-1,3-octadecanediol, panthenol, maltodextrin, bixa orellana seed extract, and APF, which contribute to its anti-inflammatory, sebum controlling, and skin barrier and microbiome protecting effects. 13 This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of this combination dermocosmetic regimen in Japanese patients undergoing active treatment with topical prescription drugs.

Materials and methods

Study design

This prospective, multicenter, observational study evaluated the effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of the combination use of two dermocosmetic products, Effaclar H Isobiome Cleanser® and Effaclar H Isobiome Cream® (hereafter referred to as facial cleanser and moisturizer cream, respectively) in Japanese patients undergoing acne treatment with topical prescription drugs. This study was conducted at four dermatological institutions in Japan between June 2024 and December 2024. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Meiwa Hospital (approval number: MH-2024-6, approved on 20 May 2024) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki of 1975, as revised in 2024, and ethical guidelines for medical and health research involving human participants. Written informed consent was obtained from all study participants. This observational study was performed according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 14 All patient details has been anonymized to ensure that patients cannot be identified in any way.

Patients

Japanese men and women aged ≥15 years who had been undergoing acne treatment with topical prescription drugs for >4 weeks were selected as study participants. According to the inclusion criteria, patients with a Global Evaluation Acne (GEA) score between 1 and 5 were enrolled. Pregnant, possibly pregnant, and nursing women; patients with a history of allergies to cosmetics; and any other patient deemed unsuitable by the investigator were excluded. Study patients were enrolled consecutively. The required sample size was determined to be 60.

Dermocosmetics use

If the patients used other facial cleansers (including solid soap and powder cleansers) and moisturizing creams before the study initiation, they replaced these with the investigational dermocosmetic products. The ingredient list of the two dermocosmetics is shown in Supplementary Table. The dermatologist instructed patients on the use of the dermocosmetics based on their symptoms. The patients used the dermocosmetics for 3 months according to the dermatologist's instructions. During this period, the patients continued using the topical prescription drugs for acne treatment that they had been using before study initiation. When topical prescription drugs were applied, the moisturizing cream was applied first, followed by the prescription drugs. The use of cleansing products (for makeup removal), sunscreen, and makeup cosmetics was permitted. To ensure patient compliance, doctors were allowed to give detailed instructions to each patient regarding the amount, frequency, and method of use for each product. The used dermocosmetic products were collected from all patients after 3 months to evaluate the patients’ usage.

Assessments

Patient characteristics

Demographic and baseline data included information on sex, age, acne duration, GEA scale score, skin type, and prescribed topical drug use. The GEA scale scores from 0 to 5 as follows: (a) 0 = clear (no lesions), with possible residual pigmentation and erythema; (b) 1 = almost clear (almost no lesions), with a few scattered open/closed comedones and very few papules; (c) 2 = mild, easily recognizable, with <50% of the face involved, a few open/closed comedones, and a few papules and pustules; (d) 3 = moderate, with >50% of the face involved, many papules and pustules, many open/closed comedones, and possible involvement of one nodule; (e) 4 = severe, with involvement of the entire face involved, covered with many papules, pustules, comedones, and rare nodules; (f) 5 = very severe, with highly inflammatory acne covering the face and presence of nodules. 15

GEA scale and sebum production

The GEA scale was administered by physicians as described in the above section. Sebum production was assessed based on skin oiliness by physicians using a visual analogue scale (VAS) ranging from 0 (none) to 10 (extremely oily/seborrheic condition), based on a 10-point numeric rating scale proposed for assessing facial skin oiliness. 16

Skin findings and subjective symptoms

Physicians performed all clinical assessments. Objective skin findings of erythema, desquamation, and dryness were graded on a 4-point severity scale (0 = none, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, and 3 = severe). In the same manner, physicians asked patients regarding subjective symptoms of itching, tingling, burning, and pain and recorded the severity on an identical 4-point scale.

Patient quality of life (QOL)

Patient QOL was evaluated using the Cardiff Acne Disability Index, a well-established and validated acne-specific QOL assessment. 17 The following five QOL domains were evaluated: (a) emotion; (b) daily activities and personal relationship; (c) leisure; (d) feeling for skin status; and (e) self-assessment of acne. Patients were asked the following questions:

Patients replied with any of the following answers (scores). For questions 1–3: (a) never (0 point); (b) sometimes (1 point); (c) often (2 points); and (d) very often (3 points). For question 4: (a) not concerned (0 point); (b) sometimes concerned (1 point); (c) always concerned (2 points); and (d) feeling greatly depressed and miserable (3 points). For question 5: (a) no problem (0 point); (b) minor problem (1 point); (c) major problem (2 points); and (d) worst severity (3 points).

Tolerability and satisfaction for dermocosmetics use

Physicians and patients evaluated treatment tolerability on a 4-level scale as “well-tolerated,” “tolerated,” “neutral,” or intolerable” and satisfaction using a 5-level scale as “very satisfied,” “satisfied,” “neutral,” “dissatisfied,” or “very dissatisfied.”

Patient-reported treatment experience

Patients were asked the questions: “do you prefer using the dermocosmetics to previous cosmetics?” and “do you feel that the dermocosmetics use improve the tolerability of acne treatment?,” to which they replied “strongly agree,” “agree,” “neutral,” or “disagree.”

Patient perspectives regarding the dermocosmetics

At the beginning and end of the study, all participants completed a questionnaire for the evaluation of the cosmetic properties and perceived efficacy of the cleanser and moisturizer. Questions were answered on a 5-point Likert scale, with responses, including “strongly agree,” “agree,” “neutral,” “disagree, “ and “strongly disagree.”

Statistical analyses

The safety analysis set consisted of all patients who used the two dermocosmetic products at least once. The effectiveness analysis set included patients of the safety analysis set, excluding patients who received acne treatment with topical prescription drugs of antibiotics only. Descriptive statistics were calculated for all variables; continuous variables are presented as mean, SD, SE, and median values, whereas categorical variables are presented as frequency counts and percentages (%). Changes from baseline to 3 months for all scores and scales (e.g. GEA scale, skin findings, and Cardiff Acne Disability Index) were analyzed using the non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The statistical test was two-sided, and a p-value <0.05 was considered significant. Missing values were not imputed. All analyses were performed using R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Patient characteristics

In total, 50 patients with acne were enrolled in this study. They used the two dermocosmetic products at least once and were included in the safety analysis set. The effectiveness analysis set included 48 patients of the safety analysis set, excluding two patients who received acne treatment with topical prescription drugs comprising only antibiotics. In the effectiveness analysis set, the mean (±SD) patient age was 23.6 ± 7.1 years, and 75.0% of the patients were female (Table 1). Most patients (79.2%) presented with mild (GEA 2) to moderate (GEA 3) acne. Benzoyl peroxide (BPO) was most commonly used topical prescription drug for acne in >50% of the patients (58.3%).

Tolerability and satisfaction for dermocosmetics use

In the safety analysis set, tolerability was rated as “well-tolerated” or “tolerated” by 75.5% of the physicians and 73.5% of the patients. No patient classified the products as “intolerable” in either evaluation (Figure 5). Satisfaction was high, with 87.5% of physicians and 92.0% of patients rating their satisfaction level as “very satisfied” or “satisfied.” No physician or patient reported being “dissatisfied” or “very dissatisfied” (Figure 6).

Patient-reported treatment experience

In the questionnaire survey, most patients (86.0%) agreed or strongly agreed that they preferred using the study dermocosmetics over the cosmetics they used previously. Furthermore, when asked if the facial cleanser and moisturizing cream improved the tolerability of their acne treatment, 70.0% and 72.0% of patients “agreed” or “strongly”, respectively (Figure 7).

Patient-reported treatment experience regarding the dermocosmetics used. Evaluation was performed in the safety analysis set.

Patient perceptions regarding the dermocosmetics

Patients’ evaluation of the cosmetic properties and perceived efficacy of the products was highly positive (Figures 8 and 9). With regard to the use of the facial cleanser, >90% of patients agreed that it was “non-oily/non-sticky,” had “no sticky feeling,” and was “not shiny” (95.8%, 91.7%, and 91.7%, respectively). For the moisturizing cream, key attributes such as “easy to apply and spread” (93.8% agreement) and being “non-drying” (93.8% agreement) were among the most highly rated cosmetic qualities.

Discussion

This observational study was conducted in real-world routine clinical practice to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of the combined use of two dermocosmetic products, a facial cleanser and moisturizing cream, in Japanese patients undergoing acne treatment with topical prescription drugs. We found that acne severity, sebum production, skin findings such as the erythema and dryness, and subjective symptoms such as itching and burning were significantly improved after 3 months of therapy combining the two dermocosmetic products with topical prescription drugs for acne. Additionally, the questionnaire survey demonstrated that the use of the facial cleanser and moisturizing cream improved the tolerability of acne treatment using topical prescription drugs in 70.0% and 72.0% of patients, respectively. The Japanese guidelines for the treatment of acne vulgaris strongly recommend a management algorithm centered on topical drugs such as adapalene and BPO. 18 However, these topical drugs can cause several adverse effects such as erythema, dryness, and irritation, thereby reducing treatment adherence and effectiveness.3–5 Therefore, adjunctive dermocosmetic products may contribute to overall treatment effectiveness by mitigating the adverse effects of topical prescription drugs, thereby improving adherence to the prescribed drugs for acne. Consistent with our findings, in a previous study involving 50 patients with acne, the combination of BPO (5% gel) with liquid cleanser and moisturizer (sun protection factor (SPF) 30) applied for 12 weeks resulted in high levels of patient satisfaction, good tolerability, and treatment adherence. 19 These findings suggest that the combined use of the two dermocosmetic products is effective for treating patients undergoing acne treatment with topical prescription drugs.

Dermocosmetics may exert synergistic effects by improving the efficacy of other treatments. 10 The two investigated dermocosmetics contain active ingredients such as niacinamide and APF. Niacinamide is known to reduce sebum outflow, whereas APF has been reported to reduce inflammation and help restore the natural skin barrier. 20 Therefore, the two investigated dermocosmetics may exert beneficial effects through these mechanisms of action in acne management even without the concomitant use of topical prescription drugs.

In our study, among the evaluated five QOL domains, feeling for skin status and self-assessment of acne were significantly improved after the 3 months of therapy. This improvement may be attributed to the above-mentioned effectiveness. Acne is associated with substantial psychosocial burden, and patient QOL is an important factor in acne management. 21 The therapy combining two dermocosmetic products with topical prescription drugs is suggested to be beneficial for improving the QOL of patients with acne.

The tolerability of two dermocosmetics was favorable, as indicated by the fact that 75.5% and 73.5% of patients classified them as “well-tolerated” or “tolerated” in the physician evaluation and patient report, respectively; no patient classified them as “intolerable” in either evaluation. Due to their effectiveness and tolerability in acne management when used in combination with topical prescription drugs, most physicians (87.5%) and patients (92.0%) were “very satisfied” or “satisfied” with the dermocosmetics. Moreover, in the questionnaire survey, most patients (86.0%) reported that they preferred the dermocosmetics over the cosmetics that they used previously. These data suggest that the two dermocosmetic products, facial cleanser and moisturizing cream, were readily accepted by patients with acne as a combination therapy with topical prescription drugs in real-world routine clinical practice.

In our study, approximately >50% of the patients reported several benefits and effectiveness of the two dermocosmetic products. In particular, >90% of the patients reported that the facial cleanser was non-oily/non-sticky, had no sticky feeling, and was not shiny and that the moisturizing cream was easy to apply and spread, non-drying, and skin-friendly, suggesting the importance of these features of the dermocosmetics for acne management. A limitation of this evaluation is that post-cleansing sensory attributes related to barrier disruption, such as tightness and freshness, were not evaluated in addition to residual oiliness–related attributes (non-oily, non-sticky, and not shiny after washing). Future studies should incorporate both attribute types for a more comprehensive characterization of cleanser tolerability.

Sensitive skin is a concern in certain population segments, especially among East Asians. 22 Previous studies have reported that compared with people of other ethnicities, including Caucasians, those of Asian ethnicity are more prone to skin irritation caused by the application of topical agents, including BPO and sensitivity may be greatest in the East Asian population.23,24 Therefore, dermocosmetics use is important for preventing skin irritation, especially in East Asian populations, including Japanese individuals. Skin sensitivity is also affected by the aging process in patients with acne, and adult acne typically requires a specific, tailored therapeutic approach, including maintenance treatment.22,25 Skin sensitivity must be considered when choosing dermocosmetics.

The primary limitation of this study is its open-label, observational design and the absence of a parallel control group. Therefore, the observed benefits cannot be definitively separated from the effects of the ongoing prescription therapy. Considering the study design, it was not possible to determine whether facial cleansing or moisturizing contributed more or whether both were essential. Furthermore, the observed effects cannot be attributed to a specific active ingredient. However, the study's strength lies in its real-world design, which suggests the benefit and high patient acceptance of this treatment regimen in routine clinical practice.

The findings of this observational study suggest that the dermocosmetic regimen, when used in combination with the topical prescription drugs may be beneficial and well-tolerated in real-world routine clinical practice in Japan. The regimen may be acceptable to patients, be highly satisfactory, and may benefit acne management by contributing to a reduction in the adverse effects of topical prescription drugs and improving treatment adherence.

Dietary taurine supplementation enhances growth performance of Japanese seabass (Lateolabrax japonicus) fed low-fishmeal diets by improving antioxidant status, non-specific immunity, and tissue health
Authors: Jinqi Xu, Yi Luo, Tianli Ren, Runzhe Zhang, Jiangyang Yin, Qiang Chen, Jiteng Wang, Tao Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study evaluated the comprehensive effects of dietary taurine supplementation in low-fishmeal (FM) diets on physiological functions of Japanese seabass (Lateolabrax japonicus). A 3 × 3 factorial design was employed in this study. Three FM levels were set as 25% (FM25, basal level), 20% (FM20), and 15% (FM15), where soybean meal and soy protein concentrate were used to replace 20% and 40% of FM protein, respectively. Each FM group was further supplemented with 0%, 0.5%, or 1.0% taurine. A total of 540 juvenile fish with an initial body weight of 6.95 ± 0.18 g were randomly divided into 27 tanks (3 tanks per group, per tank 20 fish) and fed the experimental diets for 8 weeks. The results demonstrated that weight gain rate and specific growth rate were significantly influenced by both FM level and taurine level (P < 0.05). The FM20 group supplemented with 1.0% taurine showed optimal growth performance. Taurine supplementation significantly enhanced antioxidant capacity across multiple tissues, as evidenced by increased glutathione (GSH) content in serum (P = 0.028), liver (P < 0.001) and muscle (P < 0.001), along with reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels in intestine (P < 0.001), liver (P < 0.001), and muscle (P < 0.001). In terms of immunoregulation, taurine significantly improved non-specific immunity, indicated by elevated acid phosphatase (ACP) activity (P < 0.001), albumin (ALB) content (P < 0.001), as well as reduced alanine aminotransferase (ALT) (P < 0.001) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) (P < 0.001) activities in serum, though responses varied with FM levels. Histological analysis revealed that taurine alleviated hepatocyte vacuolation induced by replacing FM with plant-based protein. Additionally, 1.0% taurine addition significantly increased intestinal muscularis thickness and villus height (P < 0.05) and alleviated the increase relative mRNA expression of inflammatory cytokines (il1β, il2, il4, and il8) (P < 0.05) and apoptotic factors (caspase 3, 8, and 9) (P < 0.05) caused by FM substitution. Increased myofiber frequency and upregulation relative mRNA expression levels of mtor and s6k1 (P < 0.05) suggested that taurine supplementation enhanced muscle protein synthesis. In conclusion, taurine exerts multiple beneficial effects in low-FM diets for Japanese seabass, providing important theoretical and practical insights for developing sustainable aquafeeds and promoting aquaculture development.

Introduction

Protein is the material basis of life and serves as a core nutrient in the growth and development of aquatic animals (Wu et al., 2017). Fishmeal (FM) has long been regarded as an ideal protein source in aquafeeds due to its high protein content, balanced amino acid profile, and abundance of polyunsaturated fatty acids, minerals, and vitamins (Hussain et al., 2024). However, global declines in fishery resources, particularly catch quota restrictions in Peru, have led to a sharp reduction in FM supply and a continuous surge in its price (Zhi et al., 2025). This trend poses serious challenges to the sustainable development of aquaculture, creating an urgent need to identify alternative protein sources to replace FM in feed formulation.

Plant proteins offer a feasible pathway for developing sustainable aquafeeds owing to their wide availability, stable supply, and lower cost (Hussain et al., 2024). Nevertheless, compared to FM, plant proteins contain higher levels of fiber and carbohydrates, as well as various anti-nutritional factors, which can impair growth performance, antioxidant capacity, non-specific immunity, and protein metabolism in aquatic species. Such negative effects have been confirmed in species such as largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) (Xu et al., 2024), red sea bream (Pagrus major) (Dossou et al., 2018), yellow catfish (Pelteobagrus fulvidraco) (Han et al., 2022), and Ussuri catfish (Pseudobagrus ussuriensis) (Bu et al., 2017). To mitigate the adverse impacts of plant proteins, the use of functional feed additives has become an important nutritional strategy (Abdul Kari, 2025).

Taurine is a critical nutrient in fish and plays a key role in promoting growth, enhancing non-specific immunity, and improving antioxidant capacity (Salze and Davis, 2015). It supports fish health through multiple pathways, including maintaining hepatocyte structural integrity, preserving intestinal barrier function, and promoting muscle protein synthesis (Hao et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2018; Zheng et al., 2024). These beneficial effects have been demonstrated in African catfish (Clarias gariepinus) (Adeshina and Abdel-Tawwab, 2020), grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella) (Sun et al., 2024), and Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) (Vilvert et al., 2024). However, taurine is inherently deficient in plant-based ingredients (Lunger et al., 2007b). Dietary taurine supplementation may represent a practical strategy to improve the utilization of plant proteins in aquatic feed, as has been effectively demonstrated in largemouth bass (Wu et al., 2021).

Japanese seabass (Lateolabrax japonicus), also known as sea bass or spotted seabass, is characterized by its strong adaptability (eurythermal and euryhaline), rapid growth, and excellent meat quality. As a carnivorous fish, its demand for feed protein reaches more than 40% (Huang et al., 2025). Fishmeal serves as the core protein source in Japanese seabass feed. With the continuous expansion of its farming scale, the conflict between growing market demand and limited FM resources has become increasingly prominent. To alleviate this contradiction, a variety of plant protein sources have been tried to replace part of FM, including soybean meal (Wang et al., 2022), soy protein concentrate (Shi et al., 2025), and peanut meal (Yao et al., 2025). However, the replacement of FM with plant proteins can exert adverse effects on Japanese seabass. How exogenous taurine supplementation alleviates the potential adverse effects induced by low-FM diets remains unclear, particularly the regulatory mechanisms underlying its impacts on the multiple physiological functions of the organism. Therefore, this study formulated three experimental diets with FM levels of 25%, 20%, and 15% by replacing FM protein with soybean meal and soy protein concentrate, supplemented with exogenous taurine (0.5% and 1.0%). This study systematically evaluated the effects of taurine supplementation in low-FM diets on growth performance, tissue morphology, antioxidant capacity, non-specific immunity, and protein synthesis in Japanese seabass. This research provides valuable insights into the application of taurine in Japanese seabass feeds to alleviate FM pressure, thereby offering theoretical support for developing low-FM feeds and promoting sustainable aquaculture development.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

In the present study, all fish experiments were conducted under the supervision and inspection of Animal Experimental Ethical Inspection, Institutional Animals Care and Use Committee of Zhejiang Ocean University (approval number: 2025127).

Feed preparation

The experimental diets were formulated using FM, soybean meal, soy protein concentrate, chicken powder, peanut meal, cottonseed protein, egg powder, plasma powder, and mycelial protein as primary protein sources; fish oil, soybean oil, and soybean lecithin as lipid sources; and Ca(H2PO4)2 as the phosphorus source. Calcium propionic acid/fumaric acid (w:w = 1:1) and ethoxyquin were incorporated as preservative and antioxidant, respectively. Vitamins and minerals were supplemented through premixes to meet the nutritional requirements of the fish.

A 3 × 3 two-factor experimental design was adopted, with FM inclusion levels set at 25%, 20%, and 15%. Soybean meal and soy protein concentrate were used to replace 20% and 40% of the FM protein, respectively. Based on the calculated amino acid profiles of the protein sources (Table S1), crystalline methionine and lysine were supplemented to meet the essential amino acid requirements of Japanese seabass (Mai et al., 2006). At each FM level, taurine (provided by Zhejiang NHU Co., Ltd., Shaoxing, Zhejiang, China; purity ≥ 99%) was added at 0%, 0.5%, and 1.0%. Special emphasis was placed on examining the interaction between FM level and taurine level in the 20% and 15% FM groups. The feed preparation procedure was as follows: all ingredients were ground using an ultra-fine grinder and passed through a 60-mesh sieve. They were then mixed stepwise from the smallest to the largest proportion to ensure homogeneity. A blend of fish oil, soybean oil, soybean lecithin, and ethoxyquin was added and thoroughly mixed, followed by the addition of an appropriate amount of distilled water to adjust moisture content. The mixture was subsequently processed into sinking pellets with a diameter of 2.0 mm using a twin screw extruder (F-75Q, Huagong Optical, Mechanical & Electrical Technology Co., Ltd., Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). The pellets were dried in a constant-temperature oven at 45 °C until a constant weight was achieved, then packaged in double-layered sealed bags and stored at −20 °C for later use.

Experimental procedure and sample collection

Japanese seabass were purchased from Xinnong Aquatic Breeding Co., Ltd. (Zhangzhou, Fujian, China) and reared in an indoor recirculating aquaculture system at Zhejiang Ocean University (Zhoushan, Zhejiang, China). Prior to the formal experiment, fish were acclimated for two weeks using a control diet containing 25% FM to adapt to the experimental environment and feeding regime. After acclimation, fish were fasted for 24 h, and healthy, uniformly sized individuals (initial body weight: 6.95 ± 0.18 g, 540 fish) were selected and randomly distributed into 27 polyethylene tanks (750 L each). Each tank was stocked with 20 fish, and three replicates were assigned per experimental diet.

The feeding trial lasted for 56 d. Fish were fed twice daily (08:00 and 18:00) to apparent satiation. Uneaten feed was collected and recorded 1.5 h after each feeding. Mortality and fish behavior were monitored daily. Water quality parameters were maintained within the following ranges throughout the trial: temperature 26 to 28 °C, dissolved oxygen content > 9 mg/L, pH 7.2 ± 0.3, ammonia nitrogen content < 0.05 mg/L, and nitrite content < 0.1 mg/L.

At the end of the experiment, all fish were fasted for 24 h and anesthetized with MS-222 (100 mg/L; 886–86-2, Sigma–Aldrich Trading Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) before sampling. The total number and body weight of fish in each tank were recorded. Three fish per tank were randomly collected for whole-body proximate composition analysis and stored at −20 °C. Another four fish per tank were dissected to measure body weight, viscera weight, and liver weight. Twelve additional fish per tank were used for tissue collection: the intestine, liver, and muscle were rapidly excised, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and subsequently stored at −80 °C (two fish per cryotube). Blood was drawn from the caudal vein using a syringe, allowed to clot for 6 to 8 h at 4 °C, and then centrifuged at 3440 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant serum was aliquoted and stored at −80 °C for subsequent analysis. Finally, two fish per tank were sampled for histomorphological examination; tissues (intestine, liver, and muscle) were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at 4 °C for 24 h.

Proximate composition analysis

The moisture, crude protein, crude lipid, and ash contents in experimental fish and feed samples were determined according to the standard methods of the Association of Official Analytical Chemists (AOAC, 2000). Specifically, moisture content was determined by the gravimetric method (method 934.01), wherein samples were dried to a constant weight using a vacuum-dryer (51014552, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). Crude protein content was analyzed using the Kjeldahl nitrogen method (method 960.52) with an automatic analyzer (K355/K437, BÜCHI Labortechnik AG, Flawil, St. Gallen, Switzerland). Crude lipid content was analyzed by Soxhlet extraction (method 945.16) with an Extraction unit (E−816, BÜCHI Labortechnik AG, Flawil, St. Gallen, Switzerland.) Ash content was analyzed by combusting samples in a muffle furnace (SRJX-8-13AS, Kewei Yongxing Instrument Co., Ltd., Beijng, China) at 550 °C for 12 h (method 942.05), followed by cooling and weighing. The energy of feeds was measured with microcomputer automatic calorimeter (ZDHW-9000DH, Hebi Xinyu Intelligent Equipment Co., Ltd., Hebi, Henan, China). The organic matter content was calculated by subtracting the ash content from the dry matter of sample. The carbohydrate content was computed as: Carbohydrate = 100 − Moisture − Crude protein − Crude lipid − Ash.

Analysis of serum, intestinal, hepatic, and muscle tissues and their physiological-biochemical indices

All biochemical assay kits used in this study were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). All experimental procedures were strictly performed in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. Serum samples were appropriately diluted or directly used to determine the following parameters: acid phosphatase (ACP; cat. No. A060-2-1), alkaline phosphatase (AKP; cat. No. A059-2-2), alanine aminotransferase (ALT; cat. No. C009-2-1), aspartate aminotransferase (AST; cat. No. C010-2-1), albumin (ALB; cat. No. A028-2-1), reduced glutathione (GSH; cat. No. A006-2-1), superoxide dismutase (SOD; cat. No. A001-3-2), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC; cat. No. A015-2-1). Intestinal, hepatic, and muscle tissue samples were homogenized in a 1:9 (weight/volume) ratio with pre-cooled 0.9% physiological saline. The homogenates were centrifuged at 597 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the resulting supernatants were stored at −80 °C for subsequent analysis. The intestinal tissue supernatants were used to measure ACP (cat. No. A060-2-1), AKP (cat. No. A059-2-2), lipase (LPS; cat. No. A054-2-1), amylase (AMS; cat. No. C016-1-2), malondialdehyde (MDA; cat. No. A003-1-2), SOD (cat. No. A001-3-2), and T-AOC (cat. No. A015-2-1). The hepatic tissue supernatants were analyzed for ACP (cat. No. A060-2-1), AKP (cat. No. A059-2-2), GSH (cat. No. A006-2-1), SOD (cat. No. A001-3-2), T-AOC (cat. No. A015-2-1) and MDA (cat. No. A003-1-2). The muscle tissue supernatants were assessed for GSH (cat. No. A006-2-1), SOD (cat. No. A001-3-2), T-AOC (cat. No. A015-2-1), and MDA (cat. No. A003-1-2). After the system was prepared and the reaction was completed, the absorbance values were measured using a microplate reader (MULTISKAN GO, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The mentioned physiological and biochemical indicators above in the intestine, liver, and muscle were normalized to the total protein content.

Histological observation of intestinal, hepatic, and muscle tissues by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

Fresh samples of intestinal, hepatic, and muscle tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h, followed by gradient dehydration with anhydrous ethanol, clearing, paraffin embedding, and sectioning at a thickness of 5 μm. The sections were stained with H&E, mounted with neutral balsam, and observed under an optical microscope (BX53, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) for histological examination and image acquisition. The H&E staining sections were quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from intestinal, hepatic, and muscle tissues using Trizol reagent (R401–01, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). RNA integrity and concentration were assessed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and a spectrophotometer (NanDrop 2000, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), respectively. High-quality RNA was reverse-transcribed into first-strand complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (RR037A, Takara Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Dalian, Liaoning, China).

Real-time quantitative PCR was performed using the SYBR qPCR Master Mix (q711–03, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) on a QuantStudio 5 real-time PCR system (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). A 20 μL qPCR reaction system was prepared as follows: 0.5 μL of each forward and reverse primer, 4 μL of cDNA, 10 μL of qPCR mix, and the volume was adjusted to 20 μL with diethyl pyrocarbonate (DEPC)-treated water. The amplification protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, followed by 39 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s, annealing at 58 °C for 10 s, and extension at 72 °C for 10 s. The ef1a gene, which showed stable expression across treatment groups, was used as the internal reference. Primers for target genes were designed based on sequences retrieved from the NCBI database and synthesized by Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The relative mRNA expression levels of target genes were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001). The gene sequence of the primers used is presented in Table 2. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 24.0 software (International Business Machines Corporation, Chicago, IL, USA). A two-way ANOVA was conducted to examine the effects of FM level, taurine level, and their interaction on all measured parameters. Statistical methods refer to previously published articles (Esmaeili et al., 2025). The present study has conducted comparisons of taurine's effects at the same FM level and FM's effects at the same taurine level with Tukey's test (i.e., a total of six one-way ANOVA analyses were performed). The significance level for all tests was set at P < 0.05. The statistical analytical model for two-way ANOVA in this study was as follows:Yijk=μ+αi+βj+(αβ)ij+εijk,where Yijk denotes the observed value of dependent variable; μ denotes the overall mean; αi denotes the effect of ith level of FM (i= 25, 20, and 15); βj denotes the effect of j-th level of taurine (j = 0, 0.5%, and 1.0%); (αβ)ij denotes the interaction effect between i-th level of FM and j-th level of taurine; and εijk denotes the random error.

Growth performance indices were calculated using the following formulas:Survivalrate(SR,%)=100×(Finalnumberoffish)/(Initialnumberoffish);Weightgainrate(WGR,%)=100×(Finalbodyweight[FBW]-Initialbodyweight[IBW])/IBW;SpecificgrowthrateSGR,%/d=100×lnFBW-lnIBW/Culture period;Hepatosomaticindex(HSI,%)=100×Liverweight/Bodyweight;Viscerosomaticindex(VSI,%)=100×Visceralmassweight/Bodyweight;Feedconversionratio(FCR)=Totaldryfeedintake/Wetweightgainoffish;Feedintake(FI,g/fish)=Dryfeedintake/Fishnumber.

Discussion

Interactive effects of FM level and taurine level on growth performance and body composition of Japanese seabass

Taurine plays a crucial role in fish nutrition, metabolism, and growth processes (Rotman et al., 2017; Velasquez et al., 2015). As a conditionally essential amino acid in aquatic animals (Ripps and Shen, 2012), taurine is abundantly present in FM protein but occurs only in trace amounts in plant proteins (Yamamoto et al., 1998). Therefore, the growth retardation induced by plant protein-based feed may be attributed to taurine deficiency, which has been confirmed in studies on white grouper juveniles (Epinephelus aeneus) (Koven et al., 2016) and rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) (Hernández et al., 2017). The present study demonstrated that taurine supplementation in plant protein-based diets significantly improved the FBW, WGR, and SGR of Japanese seabass. Consistent findings have been reported in meagre (Argyrosomus regius) (de Moura et al., 2018), juvenile cobia (Rachycentron canadum) (Lunger et al., 2007a), and rainbow trout (Gaylord et al., 2006). Furthermore, taurine supplementation in plant protein-based diets showed no significant effects on the body composition of red drum (Sciaenops ocellatus) (Velasquez et al., 2015) or European sea bass (dicentrarchus labrax) (Martins et al., 2018), which aligns with the results observed in this study.

Interactive effects of FM level and taurine level on antioxidant-related parameters in different tissues of Japanese seabass

Under normal physiological conditions, fish maintain a dynamic balance between oxidative and antioxidant systems. Disruption of this balance leads to excessive accumulation of ROS, resulting in increased lipid peroxidation products and subsequent tissue damage (Abdel-Daim et al., 2019). The organism employs both enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant substances to combat oxidative damage (Gu et al., 2020). The T-AOC serves as a comprehensive indicator reflecting the overall efficacy of the organism in scavenging free radicals and maintaining redox homeostasis (Wu et al., 2024). Superoxide dismutase is a key enzyme responsible for eliminating superoxide anion radicals, and its activity directly reflects the antioxidant capacity of the organism (Han et al., 2022). Reduced glutathione directly neutralizes ROS through its active sulfhydryl groups, thereby reducing lipid peroxidation (Liang et al., 2024). Malondialdehyde, as the end product of lipid peroxidation, indicates the extent of oxidative damage (Shi et al., 2023). Studies have demonstrated that plant-based protein feeds can easily induce oxidative stress in aquatic animals (Lee et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025, 2023). Taurine, as an important functional amino acid (Sampath et al., 2020), enhances the antioxidant capacity of the organism through multiple pathways, including direct free radical scavenging, maintenance of mitochondrial electron transport chain function, and promotion of antioxidant enzyme expression (Surai et al., 2021). In the present study, exogenous taurine supplementation significantly increased T-AOC, SOD activity, and GSH content in the serum, liver, intestine, and muscle of Japanese seabass, while reducing MDA levels. These findings indicate that taurine comprehensively enhances both enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant defense systems, effectively inhibits lipid peroxidation, and alleviates oxidative damage in multiple tissues. Consistent results have been verified in species such as totoaba juveniles (Totoaba macdonaldi) (Banuelos-Vargas et al., 2014), juvenile yellow catfish (Pelteobagrus fulvidraco) (Li et al., 2016), grass carp (Sun et al., 2024), and European seabass (Coutinho et al., 2017), further supporting the positive role of taurine in mitigating oxidative stress in aquatic animals.

Interactive effects of FM level and taurine level on immune-related enzyme activities in different tissues of Japanese seabass

Serum contains various ions, metabolites, carbohydrates, and lipids, making its biochemical parameters important indicators of tissue organ status and overall health. Serum immune indices serve as crucial markers for assessing fish health and nutritional status (Li et al., 2020b). Acid phosphatase and AKP, as key hydrolytic enzymes in lysosomes, play vital roles in non-specific immunity and constitute the first line of defense (Zhu et al., 2022). In this study, exogenous taurine supplementation increased the activities of ACP and AKP in the serum of Japanese seabass, indicating that taurine can enhance non-specific immune function. This finding is consistent with observations in Culter alburnus (Chen et al., 2026). Albumin content reflects protein absorption, synthesis, and metabolism, playing important roles in maintaining plasma colloid osmotic pressure, transport, and nutrition (Verheyen et al., 2007). The activities of ALT and AST correlate with the extent of liver injury (McGill, 2016). In this study, exogenous taurine supplementation increased serum ALB content and decreased AST and ALT activities in Japanese seabass, suggesting that taurine promotes protein anabolism and protects hepatocyte structure. The ALB results are consistent with findings in black sea bream (Spondyliosoma cantharus) (Tong et al., 2020). However, elevated transaminase activities were observed in striped catfish (Pangasianodon hypophthalmus) (Peter et al., 2025), indicating species-specific differences in taurine's effects on transaminase activities. The above serum biochemical results demonstrate that exogenous taurine supplementation significantly improves liver function and non-specific immunity in Japanese seabass. To comprehensively evaluate taurine's protective effects on liver health, further histomorphological verification was conducted along with detection of ACP and AKP activities in liver tissue. Long-term intake of high-plant-protein diets induces hepatocyte vacuolation and liver damage (Prisingkorn et al., 2017). Normal hepatocyte morphology is essential for liver health (Pan et al., 2024). In this study, FM replacement caused severe hepatocyte vacuolation, while exogenous taurine supplementation significantly alleviated this vacuolation phenomenon and promoted the restoration of hepatic histological integrity. This protective effect has been confirmed in meagre (Güroy et al., 2024). The increased activities of ACP and AKP in liver tissue further verify that taurine enhances non-specific immune defense capacity in the liver.

Effects of FM level and taurine level on intestinal health of Japanese seabass

The intestine serves as the primary site for nutrient digestion and absorption in fish, and its health is crucial for growth and development (Zhou et al., 2020). Intestinal health can be evaluated through a series of histomorphological parameters: intestinal villus height directly reflects the surface area available for nutrient absorption (Refstie et al., 2000), while muscular layer thickness is associated with intestinal motility. Both parameters are indicative of intestinal health status and immune function (Yin et al., 2021). In this study, exogenous taurine supplementation improved both intestinal villus height and muscular layer thickness in Japanese seabass, findings that have been corroborated in studies on Monopterus albus (Shi et al., 2022) and juvenile golden pompano (Trachinotus ovatus) (Liu et al., 2022). Beyond histomorphological assessment, this study further investigated the effects of taurine on digestive enzyme activities in the intestinal tract of Japanese seabass. In fish nutrition research, digestive enzymes that facilitate nutrient digestion and absorption serve as key indicators of digestive capacity, nutritional status, and growth performance (Awad et al., 2012). Lipase and AMS are major digestive enzymes in the fish intestine. Lipase plays an essential role in dietary lipid breakdown, particularly for triglycerides (Cai et al., 2016), while amylase participates in carbohydrate digestion, with its activity closely related to fish feeding habits. In this study, exogenous taurine supplementation significantly enhanced both LPS and AMS activities. Similar results have been reported in hybrid grouper (♀ Epinephelus fuscoguttatus × ♂ E. lanceolatus) (Qian et al., 2021), turbot (Scophthalmus maximus) (Zhang et al., 2019), and cobia (Salze et al., 2012). The intestinal immune system, composed of physical and immunological barriers, constitutes the core of immune homeostasis in aquatic animals. The intestinal immune barrier function in fish partially relies on humoral immune factors such as ACP and AKP to execute its defensive role (Pan et al., 2017). Specifically, ACP serves as a key marker enzyme for macrophage phagocytosis of pathogens, while AKP plays an important role in regulating intestinal inflammation (Broeg, 2003; Lallès, 2019). Together, they form a crucial defense line against pathogen invasion, with their activities serving as key indicators for assessing intestinal immune status in fish. In this study, exogenous taurine supplementation increased intestinal ACP and AKP activities in Japanese seabass, indicating that taurine supplementation can enhance intestinal immune function. This result has been confirmed in grass carp (Yan et al., 2019). In addition to these humoral immune factors, inflammatory cytokines are key signaling molecules in intestinal immune responses in fish (Rombout et al., 2011). Inflammatory responses are regulated by cytokines, among which il1β and il8 function as pro-inflammatory factors, while il2 and il4 act as immunoregulatory factors. Previous studies have shown that low-FM diets can induce pro-inflammatory factor expression in Nile tilapia (Li et al., 2020a) and blunt snout bream (Megalobrama amblycephala) (Xu et al., 2019). In this study, reduced dietary fishmeal protein levels led to upregulation of il1β, il2, il4, and il8 relative mRNA expression in the intestine of Japanese seabass, while exogenous taurine supplementation alleviated this elevated cytokine expression. This demonstrates that dietary taurine supplementation can enhance the anti-inflammatory capacity of Japanese seabass, thereby improving immunity and promoting intestinal health. Taurine has been confirmed as an immunomodulator in both yellow catfish (Zhang et al., 2018) and grass carp (Yan et al., 2019). Persistent inflammatory stress can further activate apoptotic genes in the intestine. Therefore, to comprehensively evaluate the protective effects of taurine on the intestine of Japanese seabass, this study further investigated the regulatory effects on intestinal apoptosis-related gene expression. Apoptosis primarily occurs through two pathways: the extrinsic and intrinsic pathways. Caspase 8 initiates the extrinsic death receptor pathway, caspase 9 initiates the intrinsic mitochondrial pathway, and caspase 3 serves as the executioner of apoptosis (Van Opdenbosch and Lamkanfi, 2019). In this study, feeding with low-FM diets increased the relative mRNA expression levels of both caspase 8 and caspase 9 in the intestine of Japanese seabass, collectively leading to upregulation of caspase 3 relative mRNA expression. However, exogenous taurine supplementation downregulated the relative mRNA expression levels of caspase 3, caspase 8, and caspase 9, suggesting that taurine may alleviate oxidative damage and inhibit the activation of apoptotic signaling, thereby protecting intestinal health in Japanese seabass. Similar protective effects have been confirmed in shrimp (Penaeus vannamei) (Shi et al., 2023), pufferfish (Takifugu obscurus) (Cheng et al., 2018), and largemouth bass (Zhou et al., 2025).

Interactive effects of FM level and taurine level on protein synthesis-related gene expression of Japanese seabass

Myofiber diameter and density are critical indicators of muscle quality. In mammals and birds, myofiber hyperplasia declines and ceases shortly after birth, with subsequent growth primarily dependent on myofiber hypertrophy. This process is accompanied by increased connective tissue and intramuscular fat deposition, leading to a reduction in myofiber density (De Almeida et al., 2010; Rehfeldt et al., 2011). In contrast, fish exhibit a distinct growth pattern characterized by the synergistic contribution of both myofiber hyperplasia and hypertrophy to muscle development. Although the capacity for hyperplasia gradually declines with age, its duration and intensity significantly influence individual growth rates and ultimate body size (Valente et al., 1999). Previous studies have demonstrated that fish muscle development is continuously regulated by nutritional and environmental factors (Canosa and Bertucci, 2023). Appropriate exogenous nutrient supplementation can effectively promote myofiber hyperplasia, increase myofiber number, and thereby optimize muscle yield and quality (Silva et al., 2017).

In the present study, low-FM diets reduced myofiber frequency in Japanese seabass, while exogenous taurine supplementation counteracted this adverse effect. The ability of taurine to enhance myofiber frequency has also been confirmed in juvenile turbot (Wiriduge et al., 2020) and largemouth bass (Zhou et al., 2025). Myofiber hyperplasia relies on intramuscular protein synthesis. Mechanistic Target of Rapamycin and s6k1 are two key regulators of protein synthesis, functioning to integrate signals from nutrients, energy status, and growth factors, ultimately initiating and promoting protein synthesis to drive muscle cell growth and proliferation (Jiang et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2025). In this experiment, dietary taurine supplementation significantly up-regulated the relative mRNA expression levels of mtor and s6k1, indicating enhanced muscle protein synthesis capacity in Japanese seabass. Therefore, exogenous taurine supplementation may promote myofiber hyperplasia by activating the mTOR/S6K signaling pathway and subsequently increasing intramuscular protein synthesis.

Conclusion

In summary, dietary taurine supplementation in low-FM diets significantly improves the growth performance of Japanese seabass and effectively enhances the antioxidant capacity in multiple tissues (serum, intestine, and muscle). Meanwhile, taurine significantly elevates non-specific immune parameters, improves hepatic and intestinal tissue structure, downregulates the expression of inflammatory and apoptotic factors, and promotes muscle protein synthesis. This study confirms that taurine can serve as an important functional additive in low-FM feeds for Japanese seabass, providing a theoretical basis for the development of sustainable aquatic feeds. Future studies are recommended to explore the efficacy of exogenous taurine supplementation when FM is replaced by other non-grain protein sources, as the development of non-grain proteins has emerged as a pivotal strategy for promoting the sustainable development of the aquatic feed industry.

Pryms of ℤ 3 × ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3} coverings of genus 2 curves
Paweł Borówka, Anatoli Shatsila

Abstract
. We study unramified Galois ℤ 3 × ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3} -coverings of genus 2 curves and the corresponding Prym varieties and Prym maps. In particular, we prove that any such covering can be reconstructed from its Prym variety, that is, the Prym–Torelli theorem holds for these coverings. We also investigate the Prym map of unramified G G -coverings of genus 2 curves for an arbitrary abelian group G G . We show that the generic fiber of the Prym map is finite unless G G is cyclic of order less than 6.

1.
Introduction

Given a finite morphism between smooth curves, one can canonically associate to it a polarised abelian variety, the Prym variety. This induces a map from the moduli space of coverings to the moduli space of polarised abelian varieties, known as the Prym map. Classically, the main aim of the Prym theory has been to understand the Prym map for unramified double coverings. However, more recently, other types of coverings have gained a lot of interest. Before going into details, let us introduce some notation.

Let
f:C^{\prime}\to C
be a covering of smooth curves of genera
g^{\prime}
and
g
. It induces the so-called Norm map
Nm_{f}:JC^{\prime}\to JC
that is defined as a linear extension of the formula
Nm_{f}(P)=f(P)
(where we identify the Jacobian variety with the Picard group of degree
0
divisors on a curve). Then, the Prym variety
P(f)=P(C^{\prime}/C)
of a covering
f
can be defined as an abelian subvariety that is the connected component of the kernel of the Norm map. It carries a natural polarisation given by the restriction of the principal polarisation on
JC^{\prime}
. The Prym map assigns to a covering its (polarised) Prym variety. In some cases, the geometry of a Prym map is quite surprising. In particular, there are Prym maps of degree
27,16,10
and
3
, see the survey
[
Ort20
]
and references therein. On the other hand, not much is known for many types of coverings. Even if we assume that the genus of the bottom curve
2
and the covering is unramified, we have only partial results. Recently, the generic injectivity of the Prym map of unramified cyclic coverings of genus 2 curves of degrees equal to Sophie Germain primes was proved in
[
NOS24
]
. This result was generalised in the preprint
[
NOPS24
]
to other primes and genera.
In
[
Ago20
]
, there is a result stating that the Prym maps of unramified cyclic coverings of degree greater than 5 are generically finite. In
[
BO20
]
, the authors have described
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coverings and showed the global injectivity of the Prym map in this case.

Following the results mentioned above, we have decided to investigate more closely abelian coverings of genus
2
curves, i.e. Galois coverings
f:\tilde{C}\to C
with the Galois group being an abelian group. By
[
Par91
]
, any such covering can be constructed from a curve
C
and a subgroup of its Jacobian
JC
. The Prym maps of such coverings have been investigated in
[
Moh21
]
, however, the results obtained there cannot be applied to genus
2
curves.

The first part of the manuscript is devoted to the study of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-coverings of genus two curves and their Prym maps.
Similarly to
[
BO20
]
, the moduli space
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-coverings consists of two irreducible components: the component
(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{iso}
of isotropic coverings and
(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{ni}
of non-isotropic coverings, see Proposition
2.1
and Definition
2.4
.
The main result of the paper is the global injectivity of the Prym map on both components:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.10
)
.

The Prym maps

\mathcal{P}^{iso}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{iso}\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,3,3)}_{8}

\mathcal{P}^{ni}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{ni}\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,9)}_{8}

are injective.

Since the proof is quite technical and has a few steps, let us start with an overview of the proof.
Let
[f:\tilde{C}\to H]\in\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
be a covering with
H=\tilde{C}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
and
(P(f),\Theta_{P(f)})
be its polarised Prym variety. First, we show that the automorphisms of
P(f)
induced by
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
can be recovered from the polarisation
\Theta_{P(f)}
. The action of
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
on the Prym variety then induces an isotypical decomposition of
P(f)
as a product of four smaller Prym varieties. The induced polarisation on each small Prym uniquely determines three elliptic curves inside it. These twelve elliptic curves are, in fact, quotients of
\tilde{C}
by the subgroups of
\Aut(\tilde{C})
isomorphic to
S_{3}
and generated by an element in
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
and a lift of the hyperelliptic involution
\iota
on
H
to
\tilde{C}
.

Having distinguished elliptic curves, we use them to recover the set of involutions of the Prym variety
P(f)
induced by the aforementioned lifts. Each such involution corresponds to a
4
-tuple of elliptic curves, on which it acts as the identity, and
every such
4
-tuple generates a Jacobian of a genus four curve, which is the quotient of
\tilde{C}
by one of the nine lifts of
\iota_{H}
. Thus, we recover the coverings of elliptic curves by genus four curves. The coverings are non-Galois and their Galois closures give the coverings of elliptic curves by
\tilde{C}
, and determine the covering
f:\tilde{C}\to H
uniquely.

The approach above works in both isotropic and non-isotropic cases. However, the isotropic case requires more careful treatment due to the fact that the small Pryms are not embedded in the Prym variety
P(f)
. In particular, one has to pass to the quotient of each small Prym by a carefully chosen
3
-torsion point to conclude that elliptic curves are uniquely determined by the polarisation (see Proposition
3.4
).

It is important to note that the results of this paper cannot be obtained from known results. The methods of
[
BO20
]
rely on an explicit description of hyperelliptic curves via Weierstrass points. The methods of
[
NOS24
,
NOPS24
]
use genericity assumption in many ways, so one cannot hope for results like global injectivity that we have been able to obtain.
As an application of the first part, in the second part of the paper we focus on the general structure of the Prym map for abelian Galois coverings of genus
2
. We show that the Prym map is generically finite apart from a few exceptions. The exact result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
G
be an abelian group.

(1)

If
G
is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
, then the generic fiber of the Prym map of unramified
G
-coverings of genus 2 curves

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}\to\mathcal{A}^{G}

is positive dimensional.

(2)

If
G
is not isomorphic to a cyclic group of order less than 6, then the Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}\to\mathcal{A}^{G}

is generically finite.

Note that when the rank of
G
(i.e. the minimal number of generators) is at least 5 then
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}
is empty.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the notation and prove results about the Prym varieties of unramified
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-coverings of genus 2 curves.

We would like to start by computing the number of irreducible components of the moduli of coverings. As stated in the introduction, we define the moduli space of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-coverings as

\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}=\{(H,\left<\eta,\zeta\right>):H\text{ is a smooth genus 2 curve},\eta,\zeta\in JH[3]\,\left<\eta,\zeta\right>\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}\}

where
JH[3]
is the set of 3-torsion points of the Jacobian
JH
, see
[
Par91
]
.

Let
(H,\left<\eta,\zeta\right>)\in\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
be a covering. Then
JH[3]
is endowed with the symplectic form
\omega
, usually called the Weil form or the Commutator map (see
[
BL04
, Chap 6.3]
), hence
(JH[3],\omega)
is a symplectic vector space over the field of 3 elements. If
\omega(\eta,\zeta)\neq 0
then
e_{1}:=\eta,f_{1}:=\omega(\eta,\zeta)^{-1}\zeta
can be extended to a symplectic basis
e_{1},e_{2},f_{1},f_{2}
. If
\omega(\eta,\zeta)=0
then
\left<\eta,\zeta\right>
is a maximal isotropic subgroup. We can take
e_{1}:=\eta,e_{2}:=\zeta
and then find
f_{1}\in e_{2}^{\perp}\setminus\left<e_{1},e_{2}\right>
such that
\omega(e_{1},f_{1})=1
and
f_{2}\in\left<e_{1},f_{1}\right>^{\perp}
such that
\omega(e_{2},f_{2})=1
. Therefore, the set
\{\eta,\zeta\}
can always be extended to a symplectic basis of
(JH[3],\omega)
.

Now, we can consider the moduli of principally polarised abelian surfaces with level 3–structure, see
[
BL04
, Chap 8.3.2]
, i.e. the space

\mathcal{A}_{2}[3]=\{(A,e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2}):A\in\mathcal{A}_{2},\ e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2}\in A[3]\text{ is a symplectic basis for }\omega\}

Let
\mathcal{J}_{2}[3]\subset\mathcal{A}_{2}[3]
be an open dense subset of Jacobians of smooth curves.
The first result of the paper is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
contains precisely two disjoint irreducible components,
(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{iso}
and
(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{ni}
called isotropic and non-isotropic
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
coverings.

Proof.

Consider the following two forgetful maps

\displaystyle\pi_{iso}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{J}_{2}[3]\to(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{iso},\

\displaystyle\pi_{iso}(JH,e_{1},e_{2},f_{1},f_{2})=(H,\left<e_{1},e_{2}\right>)

\displaystyle\pi_{ni}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{J}_{2}[3]\to(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{ni},\

\displaystyle\pi_{ni}(JH,e_{1},e_{2},f_{1},f_{2})=(H,\left<e_{1},f_{1}\right>)

By what we have written before, the maps are well-defined and surjective. Since the domain is irreducible, both spaces are irreducible.

The pullback of the Jacobian

Proposition 2.6
.

We have the following decompositions:

J\tilde{C}=f^{*}JH\boxplus k_{\sigma}^{*}P(h_{\sigma})\boxplus k_{\tau}^{*}P(h_{\tau})\boxplus k_{\sigma\tau}^{*}P(h_{\sigma\tau})\boxplus k_{\sigma^{2}\tau}^{*}P(h_{\sigma^{2}\tau}).

The symmetric idempotent of

\varepsilon_{f^{*}JH}=\frac{\sum_{0\leq a,b\leq 2}\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}}{9}.

Note that the idempotent of

\varepsilon_{k_{\sigma}^{*}P(h_{\sigma})}=\frac{(1+\sigma+\sigma^{2})(2-\tau-\tau^{2})}{9}.

since on

Lemma 2.9
.

The (restricted) kernel

3.
A proof of the main Theorem

Our aim is to prove that the following Prym maps are globally injective

\mathcal{P}^{iso}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{iso}\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,3,3)}_{8}

\mathcal{P}^{ni}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{ni}\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,9)}_{8}

In this section, when we do not want to distinguish isotropic and non-isotropic cases we denote the Prym map simply by
\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
.

We start with following proposition, where we show that the action of the Galois group
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
on the Prym variety
P
can be recovered from the restricted polarisation
\Theta_{P}
. The Proposition is analogous to
[
NOS24
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(P,\Theta_{P})\in\im\mathcal{P}
. Then the subgroup of automorphisms

\{\psi\in\Aut(P,\Theta_{P})\>|\>\psi\text{ has odd order and}\>\>\psi(x)=x\>\>\forall x\in K(\Theta_{P})\}.

is isomorphic to
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}

Proof.

Let
f:\tilde{C}\to H
be a covering with
{P}(f)=P
and Galois group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Note that
K(\Theta_{P})=f^{*}JH\cap P(f)\subset\Fix(\tau)\cap\Fix(\sigma)
. Let
\psi
be as in the statement of Proposition. Then, there is an automorphism
\tilde{\psi}:J\tilde{C}\to J\tilde{C}
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\Theta_{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}JH\times P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J\tilde{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\Theta_{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}JH\times P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J\tilde{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,\psi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\psi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

where

\tilde{\psi}
is polarised.

From the diagram we get that
\mu^{*}\tilde{\psi}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{J\tilde{C}}(\tilde{\Theta})

\mu^{*}\mathcal{O}_{J\tilde{C}}(\tilde{\Theta})

Now, since

\Pic^{0}(J\tilde{C})

Therefore,
\tilde{\psi}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{J\tilde{C}}(\tilde{\Theta})

\tilde{\Theta}
on

Since
\tilde{\psi}

\tilde{\psi}_{0}
of
\tilde{C}
inducing
\tilde{\psi}

\tilde{\psi}_{0}\in\Gal(\tilde{C}/H)
we have to show that
\tilde{\psi}_{0}

\Nm_{f}\circ(Id_{J\tilde{C}}-\tilde{\psi})=0
. Writing

with

we have

\Id_{J\tilde{C}}-\tilde{\psi}(x)=y+f^{*}(z)-(\tilde{\psi}(y)+f^{*}(z))=y-\tilde{\psi}(y).

Since
\tilde{\psi}(y)\in P

\Nm_{f}\circ(Id_{J\tilde{C}}-\tilde{\psi})=0
. Let
p_{1},p_{2}\in\tilde{C}
be two points in the same fiber of

\Nm_{f}(p_{1}-p_{2}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}(p_{1})+\tilde{\psi}_{0}(p_{2}))=0
, hence

\tilde{\psi}_{0}
descends to an automorphism

on
C
. Denoting the corresponding automorphism of

f^{*}\circ\psi_{0}=\tilde{\psi}_{0}\circ f^{*}=f^{*}

implying

f^{*}\circ(\Id_{JC}-\psi_{0})=0.

Since
\Gal(\tilde{C}/H)=\ker f^{*}
we get
\psi_{0}=\Id_{JC}
, hence
\psi_{0}\in\Aut(C)
is an identity. Therefore,
\tilde{\psi}_{0}
lies in

hence
\psi\mapsto\tilde{\psi}_{0}
gives a desired isomorphism.
∎

The next step of the proof is to recover the twelve elliptic curves inside the Prym variety. The following two propositions deal with non-isotropic and isotropic cases, respectively.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
f:\tilde{C}\to H
is non-isotropic.
Then the restricted polarization on

is equal to

(P_{\sigma},\Theta_{\sigma})\simeq(E\times E,\mathcal{O}(E\times\{0\}+\{0\}\times E+A))

It follows from
[
Rie83
, Theorem]
that
(P(h_{\sigma}),\Theta_{\sigma})\simeq(E_{\sigma,j}\times E_{\sigma,j},L)

\varphi_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\
-1&2\end{pmatrix}.

The first part of the proposition follows from
[

In particular, the curves are uniquely determined by the polarisation. Since

is non-isotropic, the Prym variety

is the image of

by the embedding

P_{\sigma}
.
∎

Remark 3.3

One can also see the Proposition

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
f:\tilde{C}\to H
is isotropic. Then the curves

Proof.

Note that by Lemma

for some

Since

.
Combining these together, we conclude that

Now, using results from the non-isotropic case, we know that

(1,3)

∎

Definition 3.6
.

We say that an involution
q\in\Aut(P(f))
is
\langle\sigma\rangle
-compatible if there exist
a,b
such that
q|_{P_{\sigma}}=j\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}|_{P_{\sigma}}
. Analogously, we define
\langle\tau\rangle
-compatibility,
\langle\sigma\tau\rangle
-compatibility and
\langle\sigma^{2}\tau\rangle
-compatibility by taking other small Pryms.

We say that an involution
q\in\Aut(P(f))
is group-compatible if it is
\langle\sigma\rangle
-compatible,
\langle\tau\rangle
-compatible,
\langle\sigma\tau\rangle
-compatible and
\langle\sigma^{2}\tau\rangle
-compatible.

Note that an involution
q
is
\langle\sigma\rangle
-compatible if and only if it acts as the identity on either
E_{\sigma,j},E_{\sigma,j\tau},E_{\sigma,j\tau^{2}}
and as
(-1)
on the corresponding complementary curve.
It follows from the definition that all involutions in
M
are group-compatible. Below, we will show that these are the only involutions on
P(f)
with this property.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
q
be a group-compatible involution on
P(f)
. Then
q\in M
.

Proof.

Let
q\in\Aut(P(f))
be a group-compatible involution.
Since
q
is
\langle\sigma\rangle
-compatible and
\langle\tau\rangle
-compatible without loss of generality we can assume that
q
acts as the identity on
E_{\sigma,j}\subset P_{\sigma}
and
E_{\tau,j}\subset P_{\tau}
. In particular
q=j
on
P_{\sigma}+P_{\tau}
(where
+
denotes an algebraic sum of abelian subvarieties).

By the correspondence, for any two elliptic curves in
P_{\sigma\tau}
and
P_{\sigma^{2}\tau}
there is a unique involution
j\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}
which acts as the identity on both curves. Hence
q=j\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}
on
P_{\sigma\tau}+P_{\sigma^{2}\tau}
for some
a,b
.

Thus, from the isotypical decomposition of
P(f)
(Corollary
2.7
) we have the following equality for
x_{1}\in P_{\sigma},\ x_{2}\in P_{\tau},\ x_{3}\in P_{\sigma\tau},\ x_{4}\in P_{\sigma^{2}\tau}
:

q(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4})=j(x_{1}+x_{2})+j\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}(x_{3}+x_{4}).

Assume that
q\notin M
, hence
\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}\neq\id
.

To find a contradiction, assume that
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=0
. Then
0=j(x_{1}+x_{2})+j\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}(-x_{1}-x_{2}),
so that
x_{1}+x_{2}=\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}(x_{1}+x_{2})
. Analogously,
x_{3}+x_{4}=\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}(x_{3}+x_{4})
.
Since one of the small Pryms is fixed by
\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}
, without loss of generality we can assume
\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}=\sigma
, which implies that
\sigma(x_{2})=x_{2}
.
Therefore, for any
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
in the kernel of the addition map

\mu:P_{\sigma}\times P_{\tau}\times P_{\sigma\tau}\times P_{\sigma^{2}\tau}\to P(f)

we have

. Note that
x_{3}=\sigma(\sigma^{2}(x_{3}))
and, since
\sigma^{2}(x_{3})\in P_{\tau}
we have
\sigma^{2}(x_{3})\in\Fix(\sigma)
which implies that
x_{3}\in\Fix(\sigma)
. Similarly, we get
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\in\Fix(\sigma,\tau)
. Now,

P_{\sigma}\cap\Fix(\sigma,\tau)=P_{\sigma}\cap f^{*}JH=k_{\sigma}^{*}P(h_{\sigma})\cap k_{\sigma}^{*}h_{\sigma}^{*}JH=k_{\sigma}^{*}(P(h_{\sigma})\cap h_{\sigma}^{*}JH),

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\ker k_{\sigma}^{*}|_{P(h_{\sigma})}\subset P(h_{\sigma})\cap h_{\sigma}^{*}JH
in both isotropic and non-isotropic cases. Note that
P(h_{\sigma})\cap h_{\sigma}^{*}JH\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
since the restricted polarization from
JC_{\sigma}
to
P(h_{\sigma})
is of type
(1,3)
. It follows from Lemma
2.9
that in the non-isotropic case
P_{\sigma}\cap\Fix(\sigma,\tau)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and in the isotropic case
P_{\sigma}\cap\Fix(\sigma,\tau)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

It follows from the above that in the non-isotropic (resp. isotropic) case the cardinality of
\ker\mu
is at most
3^{8}
(resp.
3^{4}
), which contradicts Lemma
2.11
. Therefore,
q
must be in

∎

Definition 3.8
.

We say that a 4-tuple of elliptic curves
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4})
with
E_{1}\in P_{\sigma},\ E_{2}\in P_{\tau},\ E_{3}\in P_{\sigma\tau},\ E_{4}\in P_{\sigma^{2}\tau}

For instance, the 4-tuple (

Remark 3.9
.

There are precisely nine compatible 4-tuples. It follows from Proposition
2.6
that each compatible 4-tuple generates the Jacobian

inside
P(f)
for some

and there is a one-to-one correspondence between compatible 4-tuples and Jacobians

inside
P(f)
.

We are finally ready to give the proof of the main theorem.

Theorem 3.10
.

The Prym maps

\mathcal{P}^{iso}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{iso}\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,3,3)}_{8}

\mathcal{P}^{ni}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})^{ni}\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,9)}_{8}

are injective.

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{P}:(\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}})\to\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,3,3)}_{8}\cup\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,9)}_{8}

be the global Prym map and
(P,\Theta_{P})
be an element of
\im\mathcal{P}
.
Fix a covering
f:\tilde{C}\to H
with
P(f)\cong P
. By Proposition
3.1
we can recover the action of
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
on
P
induced by
\Gal(\tilde{C}/H)
from the polarisation
\Theta_{P}
. According to the proof of Proposition
2.6
the isotypical decomposition of
P
is as follows:

P=P_{\sigma}\boxplus P_{\tau}\boxplus P_{\sigma\tau}\boxplus P_{\sigma^{2}\tau},

where
P_{\sigma}
is a subvariety of
P
with a symmetric idempotent

\varepsilon_{P_{\sigma}}=\frac{(1+\sigma+\sigma^{2})(2-\tau-\tau^{2})}{9},

and analogously for other components. Our aim is to show that we can recover
f
uniquely from
P
.

It follows from Proposition
3.2
in the non-isotropic case and Proposition
3.4
in the isotropic case that the restricted polarization uniquely determines three elliptic curves inside each isotypical component of
P
. We denote the elliptic curves inside
P_{\sigma}
by
E_{\sigma}^{1},E_{\sigma}^{2}
and
E_{\sigma}^{3}
. Now, define involutions
j_{\sigma}^{i}
on
P_{\sigma}
given by the identity on
E_{\sigma}^{i}
and
(-1)
on its complementary abelian subvariety
(E_{\sigma}^{1})^{c}
inside
P_{\sigma}
. We define analogous involutions on other isotypical components.

It follows from Proposition
3.7
that we can glue the involutions
j_{\sigma}^{i_{1}},j_{\tau}^{i_{2}},j_{\sigma\tau}^{i_{3}},j_{\sigma^{2}\tau}^{i_{4}}
to an involution on the whole
P
if and only if
(E_{\sigma}^{i_{1}},E_{\tau}^{i_{2}},E_{\sigma\tau}^{i_{3}},E_{\sigma^{2}\tau}^{i_{4}})
is a compatible 4-tuple for the covering
f
. Therefore, we can recover nine compatible 4-tuples for
f
.
Moreover, Proposition
3.7
also shows that compatible 4-tuples are the same for any coverings
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
with
P(f_{1})\cong P(f_{2})\cong P
. Therefore, it remains to show that compatible 4-tuples determine the covering uniquely.

Fix a compatible 4-tuple
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4})
and consider an abelian subvariety
J
of
P
generated by
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}
. Note that the restricted polarisation
\Theta_{P}|_{J}
is twice the principal polarisation, and it follows from Remark
3.9
that
J
is a Jacobian of a genus 4 curve
C
corresponding to one of the curves
C_{kl}
. The embedding of
E_{i}
in
JC
, yields a covering
C\to E_{i}
and by taking its Galois closure, we recover the curve
\tilde{C}
with the
S_{3}
deck group of automorphisms. By taking all
i
, we see that automorphisms generates Smallgroup(18,4) and there is a unique
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
subgroup such that the quotient
f:\tilde{C}\to H
has the property
P(f)=P
. Note that all nine compatible 4-tuples give the same covering
f
by Remark
3.9
, hence the Prym map is injective.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Our prove is not constructive in the sense that we use the inverse to the Torelli map to find genus
4
curves
C_{ab}
.

4.
Prym map of abelian unramified coverings of genus two curves

The purpose of this section is to study finiteness of Prym maps associated to abelian Galois unramified coverings of genus two curves. We will show that the Prym maps are generically finite with only a few exceptions. We start by briefly recalling the setup of abelian Galois unramified coverings following
[
Par91
,
Moh21
]
.

Let
f:\tilde{C}\to H
be an unramified Galois covering of a smooth genus 2 curve
H
with an abelian Galois group
G
. Then
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}=\oplus_{\chi\in G^{*}}L_{\chi}^{-1}
, where
L_{\chi}
is an invertible sheaf on
H
on which
G
acts via the character
\chi
. The algebra structure of
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}
is compatible with the
G
-action, so the multiplication is given by
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}
-linear maps

\mu_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}}:L_{\chi}^{-1}\otimes L_{\chi^{\prime}}^{-1}\to L_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}^{-1}

for all
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in G^{*}
. For unramified abelian coverings, the data
(\{L_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in G^{*}})
completely determines the covering
[
Par91
, Theorem 2.1]
. Therefore (see also
[
Moh21
, Proposition 2.3]
), we can define the moduli space
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}
of Galois unramified coverings of genus 2 curves with an abelian Galois group
G
as the moduli space of objects
(H,\{L_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in G^{*}})
with line bundles
L_{\chi}
as above.

Recall that Prym varieties
P(f)
of coverings
f:\tilde{C}\to H
with a non-cyclic group
G
may have different polarisation types. Therefore, both domain
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}
and co-domain
\mathcal{A}^{G}
of the Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}
may be reducible.

We begin the study of generic finiteness of the Prym map with the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f:\tilde{C}\to H
be an unramified Galois covering of a smooth genus 2 curve
H
with an abelian Galois group
G
. If
G
is cyclic of order at least
6
or
G
is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{5}\times\mathbb{Z}_{5}
, then the Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}\to\mathcal{A}^{G}

is generically finite.

Proof.

For the proof in the cyclic case see
[
Ago20
]
and for the proof in the case
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
see
[
BO20
]
. Moreover, we have already proved injectivity in the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
case. It is left to prove the statement in the case
G=\mathbb{Z}_{5}\times\mathbb{Z}_{5}
, but, since our proof works in a more general setting, we present it below in full generality.

Let
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
with an odd prime
p
.
It follows from
[
Rie83
]
that the hyperelliptic involution
\iota\in\Aut(H)
lifts to an involution
j\in\Aut(\tilde{C})
so that
\sigma,\tau,j
generate the subgroup
F\subseteq\Aut(\tilde{C})
, given by

F=\langle\sigma,\tau,j\>|\>\sigma^{p}=\tau^{p}=j^{2}=(j\sigma)^{2}=(j\tau)^{2}=1,\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma\rangle.

As in section 2, we consider the quotients
\pi_{ab}:\tilde{C}\to C_{ab}:=\tilde{C}/\langle j\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}\rangle
for
0\leq a,b\leq p-1
.
Since all involutions in
F
are conjugate (the proof is analogous to the case
p=3
in Lemma
2.2
),
C_{ab}
are pairwise isomorphic curves of genus
\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}
, see
[
Ort03
]
. Therefore, there is a well-defined map

\psi:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}.

Moreover, composing
\psi
with the Torelli map we obtain a map

T:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\ni(H,\eta,\zeta)\mapsto JC_{00}\times JC_{10}\in\mathcal{A}_{p^{2}-1}.

By repeating the reasoning from Remark
2.8
we can see that the abelian varieties
P
and
JC_{00}\times JC_{10}
are isogenous. Assume that
\psi
has finite fibers.
Let
P\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
and

T_{P}=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\>|\>\text{there exists an isogeny }f:T(\alpha)\to P\}.

Note that
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}})^{-1}(P)\subseteq T_{P}
.

We claim that there are at most countably many curves
C
(up to isomorphism) such that there is an isogeny
\beta:JC\times JC\to P
. Indeed,
P
has countably many abelian subvarieties, and the images under
\beta
of
JC
are isogenous to
JC
. Since there is at most countably many abelian varieties isogenous to
JC
, and the Torelli map is injective, the claim is proved.

It follows from the claim and finiteness of
\psi
that
T_{P}
is at most countable. Therefore,
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}})^{-1}(P)
is zero-dimensional, hence it is finite.

Now let us show that
\psi
indeed has finite fibers. We claim that the map

\gamma:\tilde{C}\to C_{00}\times C_{01}

given by

\gamma(x)=(\pi_{00}(x),\pi_{10}(x))

is an embedding. First we show that that
\gamma
is injective. If
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\tilde{C}
with
\gamma(x_{1})=\gamma(x_{2})
then
x_{1}=j(x_{2})
and
x_{1}=j\sigma(x_{2})
implying that
x_{2}
is a fixed point of
\sigma
, which is impossible.
On the other hand, the map
\gamma
is not smooth at
x
if and only if
x
is a fixed point of both
j
and
j\sigma
, which is again impossible. Therefore,
\gamma
is an embedding.

Now, we compute the self-intersection of
\gamma(C)
inside
C_{00}\times C_{10}
. We claim that

\gamma(C)^{2}=12-2p^{2}.

Indeed, it follows from the adjunction formula that

\gamma(C)^{2}=2p^{2}-\gamma(C).K_{C_{00}\times C_{10}}.

Since the degree of
\pi_{kl}
is equal to 2, we have

\gamma(C).(C_{00}\times\{q\})=\gamma(C).(\{q\}\times C_{10})=2.

Therefore,

\gamma(C).K_{C_{00}\times C_{10}}=2\cdot(2g(C_{00})-2+2g(C_{10})-2)=4p^{2}-12,

and the result follows.

Now, assume that the map
\psi
has positive dimensional fibres. Then
\gamma(C)
must move in an algebraic family. Therefore,
\gamma(C)^{2}\geq 0
, which is possible only for
p^{2}\leq 6
. Hence, for all odd primes
p
the fibres are finite, which finishes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.

(1)

If
G
is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
, then the generic fiber of the Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}\to\mathcal{A}^{G}

is positive dimensional.

(2)

If
G
is not isomorphic to a cyclic group of order less than 6, then the Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}\to\mathcal{A}^{G}

is generically finite.

Note that
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}
is non-empty if and only if the minimal number of generators of
G
is at most 4 and in particular if and only if there is an embedding of
G
into a Jacobian of a genus
2
curve.

Proof.

For the dimensions of the generic fiber of Prym for
G
isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
see
[
AP16
]
. For
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the Prym variety is an elliptic curve, so the generic fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
is two-dimensional.

Now, let
f:\tilde{C}\to H:=\tilde{C}/\langle\sigma\rangle
be an element in
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{4}}
and
P
be its Prym variety. The hyperelliptic involution on
H
lifts to an involution on
\tilde{C}
denoted by
j
and
\langle\sigma,j\rangle\simeq D_{4}
. There are two conjugacy classes of involutions in
D_{4}
, namely
\{j,j\sigma^{2}\}
and
\{j\sigma,j\sigma^{3}\}
. It follows that
E_{j}:=\tilde{C}/\langle j\rangle
is isomorphic to
E_{j\sigma^{2}}:=\tilde{C}/\langle j\sigma^{2}\rangle
and
E_{j\sigma}:=\tilde{C}/\langle j\sigma\rangle
is isomorphic to
E_{j\sigma^{3}}:=\tilde{C}/\langle j\sigma^{3}\rangle
. The curve
C_{\sigma^{2}}:=\tilde{C}/\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle
is hyperelliptic of genus 3 and
E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}=\tilde{C}/\langle j\sigma,\sigma^{2}\rangle
is an elliptic curve. It follows from Accola’s lemma that
g(E_{j})+g(E_{j\sigma})=3
. Since these curve cannot have genus 0, let us assume
g(E_{j})=1
and
g(C_{j\sigma})=2
.
Note that
E_{j},E_{j\sigma^{2}}
and
E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}
have exponents
2,2,4
as subvarieties of
J\tilde{C}
respectively since the corresponding coverings are of degree
2,2,4
. Therefore, their symmetric idemponents are given by:

\varepsilon_{E_{j}}=\frac{1+j}{2},

\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{1+j\sigma^{2}}{2},

\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{1+j\sigma+\sigma^{2}+j\sigma^{3}}{4}.

Denoting
C_{\sigma^{2},j}=\tilde{C}/\langle\sigma^{2},j\rangle
and
C_{\sigma,j}=\tilde{C}/\langle\sigma,j\rangle
we see that
g(C_{\sigma^{2},j})=g(C_{\sigma,j})=0
, hence

1+j+\sigma^{2}+j\sigma^{2}=\sigma+\sigma^{3}+j\sigma+j\sigma^{3}=0.

Using these facts we have

\varepsilon_{E_{j}}+\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma^{2}}}+\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{3-\sigma-\sigma^{2}-\sigma^{3}}{4}=1-\varepsilon_{JC_{\sigma}}=\varepsilon_{P}.

Thus,
P
is isogenous to
E_{j}^{2}\times E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}
, so the dimension of the codomain of the Prym map is 2, and the generic fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{4}}
is one-dimensional. There is an ongoing project of the second author to describe the fibers of the Prym map of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
coverings in more detail
[
Sha
]
.

Now, assume that
G
is not isomorphic to a cyclic group of order less than six. It follows that
G
contains a subgroup
B
, which is either cyclic of order at least six or isomorphic to one of the groups
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{5}\times\mathbb{Z}_{5}
. Since, by Proposition
4.1
the Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{B}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{B}\to\mathcal{A}^{B}

is generically finite, we know that there exists a character
\chi\in B^{*}
such that the map of the corresponding line bundle
L_{\chi}
in the building data for the covering of
H
given by
B

H^{0}(H,\omega_{H}\otimes L_{\chi})\otimes H^{0}(H,\omega_{H}\otimes L_{\chi^{-1}})\to H^{0}(H,\omega_{H}^{2})

is surjective. Since
B^{*}\subseteq G^{*}
and
L_{\chi}
is a line bundle in the data defining the covering
f
, we see that the codifferential
(d\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G})^{*}
is surjective, hence the Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{G}
is generically finite.

By
[
Par91
]
, the necessary and sufficient condition for the construction of unramified
G
-covering is an embedding of
G
into the Jacobian
JH
, hence
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{G}
is non-empty if and only if the rank of
G
is at most four.
∎

On large queue lengths in generalised Jackson networks
Anatolii A. Puhalskii

Abstract
This paper proves a large deviation principle (LDP) for the stationary distribution of queue lengths in a subcritical generalised Jackson network assuming a Cramer condition on the interarrival and service times. The deviation function is given by the quasipotential.



1
Introduction and summary

We are concerned with the asymptotics of large deviations of
stationary queue lengths
in an ergodic generalised Jackson network.
It is common to characterise large deviations
of the stationary distributions of Markov processes
in terms of quasipotentials, which involve
trajectorial deviation
functions, see
Freidlin
and Wentzell
[
4
]
, Shwartz and Weiss
[
15
]
.
Although the queue length process in a generalised Jackson network is not
Markovian, trajectorial LDPs for that process are
available in the literature, see, e.g., Puhalskii
[
8
]
. Thus,
a quasipotential can be defined, at least formally.
In Theorem 2.2 in Puhalskii
[
9
]
it was asserted that
the quasipotential governs large
deviations of the
stationary distribution.
Unfortunately the proof there is flawed as to be detailed
later.
The purpose of this note is to give a
correct proof.
Our proof builds on the exponential tightness of the stationary distribution
established in Puhalskii
[
14
]

and identifies the large deviation
(LD) limit point as the time limit
of the idempotent distribution of the transient LD limit of the queue length
process.

More specifically, the proof implements the following strategy,
cf. Puhalskii

[
11
,
14
]
.
Given a stationary stochastic process

X_{n}(t)
with invariant measure
\mu_{n}
, we have that
for a bounded continuous
nonnegative function
f
on the state space,

\int f(x)^{n}\mu_{n}(dx)=\int\int f(x)^{n}\,\mathbf{P}(X_{n}(t)\in dx|X_{n}(0)=y)\mu_{n}(dy)\,.

(1.1)

Suppose
\mu_{n}
is exponentially tight and let
\Pi
represent an LD
limit point of
\mu_{n}
.
(A review of the basics
of
LD convergence, which is just a different form
of
the LDP, and of idempotent processes, can be found in
Puhalskii

[
8
]
–
[
12
]
.)
If, as
n\to\infty
, the transient distribution
\mathbf{P}(X_{n}(t)\in dx|X_{n}(0)=y)
continuously
LD converges to certain deviability
\Pi_{y,t}(x)
(or
obeys the continuous LDP with deviation function
I_{y,t}(x)=-\ln\Pi_{y,t}(x)

for that matter) and
\mu_{n}
LD converges to
deviability
\Pi
(along a subsequence), then (
1.1
) implies that

\sup_{x}f(x)\Pi(x)=\sup_{y}\sup_{x}f(x)\Pi_{y,t}(x)\Pi(y)\,.

(1.2)

If
\Pi_{y,t}(x)
converges to limit
\hat{\Pi}(x)
, as

t\to\infty
, which does not depend on
y
, then letting
t\to\infty

in (
1.2
) and noting that
\sup_{y}\Pi(y)=1

implies, modulo certain technical conditions,
that
\sup_{x}f(x)\Pi(x)=\sup_{x}f(x)\hat{\Pi}(x)
so that

\Pi(x)=\hat{\Pi}(x)
.
Thus,

\Pi(x)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Pi_{y,t}(x)\,.

(1.3)

In the setup of this paper,

X_{n}(t)=Q(nt)/n
, where
Q(t)
represents the queue length vector
at
t
. In order to be able to work with the conditional
distribution on the righthand side of (
1.1
), we need
to append
X_{n}(t)
with a vector
of initial residual service times and initial excess exogenous
arrival times.
Thus,
y
is a

3K
-vector that records the initial queue lengths at the stations as well as
the initial excess exogenous arrival times and residual service times. Therefore, the
trajectorial LD
convergence in Puhalskii
[
8
]
which concerned ordinary renewal
processes needs to be extended to the case of delayed renewal
arrival and service
processes. The same device as in Puhalskii
[
8
]

of establishing exponential tightness and
identifying the LD limit as a unique weak solution to a system of
idempotent equations proves its worth. With regard to taking a limit
as
t\to\infty
in (
1.3
), the
case of
ordinary renewal processes was dealt with in
Puhalskii
[
9
]
. This paper extends the proof to the delayed
case.
In a nutshell, since the network is subcritical, the solution of the idempotent
equations converges to a limit which implies the convergence of the solution’s
idempotent distribution.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section

2
a precise specification of the model is
given and the main result is stated.
In Section
3
the LD trajectorial convergence for the
delayed case is addressed. The proof of the main result is discussed in
Section
4
.

2
An LDP for stationary queue lengths

We consider a queueing network with
a homogeneous customer population which comprises
K

Customers arrive exogenously and are served in the

All the stochastic processes are assumed to have

We denote

For

For given realisations of

,
A_{k}
,
S_{k}
, and
R_{k}

Let nonnegative random variables

Let

We assume that the network is subcritical, i.e.

\mu>(I-P^{T})^{-1}\lambda

In addition, it is assumed that the generic interarrival times are

More specifically, it is assumed that

1.

\mathbf{P}(\xi_{k}>u)>0
, for all

k\in\mathcal{K}
and

,

2.

for each
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
there exist nonnegative function

Under the

[
3
]

[
1
]

We now recall the trajectorial LDP for the queue length process in
Puhalskii
[
8
]
. Suppose, in addition, that,
for all

k\in\mathcal{K}
,
A_{k}
and
S_{k}
are

\mathbf{P}(\xi_{k}=0)=\mathbf{P}(\eta_{k}=0)=0,

Let also

,

,

,

Let

Given vectors

\displaystyle=\sup_{\vartheta<\beta_{k}}\bigl(\vartheta-a_{k}\ln\mathbf{E}\exp(\vartheta\xi_{k})\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle\psi^{S}_{k}(d_{k})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\vartheta<\gamma_{k}}\bigl(\vartheta-d_{k}\ln\mathbf{E}\exp(\vartheta\eta_{k})\bigr)\,,

For

3
Trajectorial LD convergence

The purpose of this section is to prove trajectorial LD convergence
for the delayed case. It is assumed that

A_{k}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq u_{k}\}}(\tilde{A}_{k}(t-u_{k})+1)

and that

S_{k}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq v_{k}\}}(\tilde{S}_{k}(t-v_{k})+1)\,

where
\tilde{A}_{k}
and

\tilde{S}_{k}
are ordinary renewal processes
and
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
are nonnegative numbers.
More specifically, as in Puhalskii
[
8
]
, we
look at a sequence of networks indexed by
n
, so, some of the pieces of
notation are appended
with subscript
n
. We assume that the delays are
nu_{n,k}
and

nv_{n,k}
and that time is accelerated by a factor of
n
.
Thus, the model is as follows,

Q_{n,k}(nt)=Q_{n,k}(0)+\mathbf{1}_{\{nt\geq nu_{n,k}\}}(\tilde{A}_{k}(nt-nu_{n,k})+1)+\sum_{l=1}^{K}R_{lk}\bigl(D_{n,l}(nt)\bigr)-D_{n,k}(nt),

(3.1)

D_{n,k}(nt)=\mathbf{1}_{\{B_{n,k}(nt)\geq nv_{n,k}\}}\bigl(\tilde{S}_{k}(B_{n,k}(nt)-nv_{n,k})+1\bigr)\,,

(3.2)

and

B_{n,k}(nt)=\int_{0}^{nt}\mathbf{1}_{\{Q_{n,k}(u)>0\}}\,du\,,

(3.3)

where

u_{n,k}\geq 0
and
v_{n,k}\geq 0
. By convention,

v_{n,k}>0
if and only if
Q_{n,k}(0)>0
.
As a result,
B_{n,k}(nv_{n,k})=nv_{n,k}
.
It will be assumed that, furthermore,

u_{n,k}\to u_{k}
and
v_{n,k}\to v_{k}
.

Let

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{n,k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{n,k}(nt)}{n}\,,\quad\overline{A}_{n,k}(t)=\frac{\tilde{A}_{k}(nt)}{n}\,,\quad\overline{S}_{n,k}(t)=\frac{\tilde{S}_{k}(nt)}{n}\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{n,k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{k}(nt)}{n}\,,\quad\overline{D}_{n,k}(t)=\frac{D_{n,k}(nt)}{n}\,,\quad\overline{B}_{n,k}(t)=\frac{B_{n,k}(nt)}{n}\,.

Suppose that (
2.6
) holds for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
.
It can be proved as in Puhalskii
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
that the
stochastic processes in (
3.4
)
are
\mathbb{C}
-exponentially tight of order
n
as random elements of

\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
. The joint distributions of these processes are

\mathbb{C}
–exponentially tight in

\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{6K})
. The proof of the LD convergence is over if
the idempotent distribution of an LD subsequential limit of

((\overline{Q}_{n,k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})\,,k\in\mathcal{K})
is specified uniquely.

Let
\Pi
represent a deviability on

\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{6K})
that is an LD limit point of the joint
distributions of processes in (
3.4
) for rate
n
.
The stochastic processes

(\overline{A}_{n,k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
(\overline{S}_{n,k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
and

(\overline{R}_{n,k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
are independent and
LD converge to respective independent under
\Pi
idempotent processes

\alpha_{k}=(\alpha_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\sigma_{k}=(\sigma_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
and

\rho_{k}=(\rho_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})

nondecreasing absolutely continuous trajectories such that

\alpha_{k}(0)=0
,
\sigma_{k}(0)=0
, and
\rho_{k}(0)=0
. The respective
idempotent distributions of the latter idempotent processes are as
follows, see Puhalskii
[
8
]
,

\displaystyle\Pi^{\alpha}_{k}(a_{k})=\exp(-\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{A}_{k}(\dot{a}_{k}(t))\bigr)\,dt,\;

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\sigma}_{k}(s_{k})=\exp(-\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{k}^{S}(\dot{s}_{k}(t))\bigr)\,dt,\;,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}^{\rho}(r_{k})=\exp(-\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{k}^{R}(\dot{r}_{k}(t))\,dt\bigr)\,,

(3.7)

provided the functions
a_{k}=(a_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

s_{k}=(s_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
r_{k}=(r_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})

,
\overline{D}_{n,k}(t)

\overline{B}_{n,k}(t)
,

These idempotent processes have

The idempotent processes

(\sigma_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
(\delta_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

(\beta_{k}(t)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and

Taking LD subsequential limits in

\chi_{k}(t)=q_{0,k}+\alpha_{k}(t-u_{k})+\sum_{l=1}^{K}\rho_{lk}\bigl(\delta_{l}(t)\bigr)-\delta_{k}(t)

(3.8)

and

\delta_{k}(t)=\sigma_{k}(\beta_{k}(t)-v_{k})\,,

(3.9)

where

and

In addition, passing to a subsequential LD limit in the equation

which is a consequence of (

), implies that (see also
Puhalskii
[
8

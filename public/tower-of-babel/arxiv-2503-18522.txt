Radial canonical Λ < 0 \Lambda<0 gravity
Nele Callebaut and Blanca Hergueta

Abstract
We apply an ADM deparametrization strategy to radial canonical Λ < 0 \Lambda<0 gravity in three dimensions. It gives rise to a concise notation for previous holographic interpretations in terms of an identified radial ‘volume time’ and ‘true’ ADM degrees of freedom. We further discuss York time and conformal boundary conditions in this context, and construct a BTZ wavepacket solution to the radial WdW equation.

Introduction

This proceeding is based on the paper

In that work, we are interested in studying the canonical, or Wheeler-DeWitt (WdW), approach to quantizing gravity, applied to gravity with negative cosmological constant
\Lambda<0

The holographic approach consists of deciphering the dictionary from conformal field theory to gravitational language.

2
Parametrization in mechanics

To review the idea of ADM deparametrization, we start with a simple mechanical system with
M
degrees of freedom, described by an action

\displaystyle S=\int dt\,\left(p_{i}\partial_{t}q_{i}-H(p_{i},q_{i},t)\right),\qquad(i=1\cdots M).

(1)

By interpreting time and Hamiltonian as an extra conjugate pair
(t,-H)\equiv(q_{M+1}(\tau),p_{M+1}(\tau))
, in addition to the
M
conjugate pairs of coordinates and momenta
(q_{i},p_{i})
, we can bring
S
in a form parametrized by the arbitrary parameter
\tau
,

\displaystyle S=\int d\tau\,\left.\left(p_{j}\partial_{\tau}q_{j}\right)\right|_{p_{M+1}=-H},\qquad(j=1\cdots M+1).

(2)

We will distinguish between the original set of conjugate pairs and the extended one by using labels
i
and
j
running over respectively
M
or
M+1
values.
The condition identifying
p_{M+1}
with
-H
can be enforced by a Lagrange multiplier
N(\tau)
,

\displaystyle S=\int d\tau\,\left(p_{j}\partial_{\tau}q_{j}-N\mathcal{H}\right),\qquad(j=1\cdots M+1),

(3)

with variation with respect to
N
imposing the constraint
\mathcal{H}(q_{i},p_{i},q_{M+1},p_{M+1})=0
with solution
p_{M+1}=-H(q_{i},p_{i},q_{M+1})
.

The first description (
1
)
comes in a ‘deparametrized’ form of the action, and the second (
3
) in a ‘parametrized’ form. The latter has more degrees of freedom, but also more symmetry, in the form of reparametrization invariance under
\tau\rightarrow\tau^{\prime}(\tau)
.
Reversely, to go from the parametrized to deparametrized description, (
3
) to (
1
), requires a two-step process: first substituting the constraint solution imposed by varying
N
, and subsequently imposing what is called a “coordinate condition” in the original ADM paper
[
12
]
. The latter step is a gauge choice or identification of a ‘preferred’ time,
q_{M+1}(\tau)=\tau
combined with calling
q_{M+1}\equiv t
.

Schematically, the variation of the action (
2
) takes the form

\delta S=\int(\text{EOM})\delta q_{j}+p\delta q|_{b}-H\delta t|_{b}
,
such that the on-shell variation
[
13
]

\delta S_{cl}
only contains boundary contributions, from which one can read off

\displaystyle p_{cl}(\tau_{b})=\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta q_{b}},\qquad H_{cl}(\tau_{b})=-\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta t_{b}}.

(4)

Here, the subscript ‘cl’ refers to evaluation on a classical solution
q_{j}(\tau)

and the subscript ‘b’ to evaluation in the boundary values
of the integration variable
\tau=\tau_{b}
, i.e.
q_{b}\equiv q(\tau_{b})
and
t_{b}\equiv t(\tau_{b})
.
This leads to the
Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta t}=-H(q,\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta q},t)

(5)

where now explicit subscripts (except for
S_{cl}
) have been dropped for readability.

3
Deparametrization in canonical gravity

In the Hamiltonian formulation of General Relativity, in
(3+1)
-dimensional
\Lambda=0
gravity originally, one makes use of the ADM parametrization of the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-N^{2}d\tau^{2}+\gamma_{ij}(dx^{i}+N^{i}d\tau)(dx^{j}+N^{j}d\tau)

(6)

with
N(\tau,x)
the lapse,
N^{i}(\tau,x)
the shift, and
\gamma_{ij}(\tau,x)
the intrinsic metric on spatial slices
\Sigma
that foliate the spacetime. In terms of these fields, the gravitational action (up to boundary contributions
[
14
]
) takes the form

\displaystyle S=\int d^{3}xd\tau\,\left(\pi^{ij}\partial_{\tau}\gamma_{ij}-N\mathcal{H}-N^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right).

(7)

It can thus be recognized as being of a deparametrized form such as (
3
).
Here,
(\gamma_{ij},\pi^{ij})
take the role of conjugate pairs
(q_{j},p_{j})
of the previous section, the momenta
\pi^{ij}
defined from the Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}
as

\displaystyle\pi^{ij}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\,\partial_{\tau}\gamma_{ij}}.

(8)

The lapse and shift impose respectively the Hamiltonian constraint

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=0

(9)

and momentum constraint
\mathcal{H}_{i}=0
, with
\mathcal{H}
quadratic in the momenta. Upon canonical quantization, the momenta are promoted to variational derivatives with respect to the 3-metric,
\pi^{ij}\rightarrow-i\delta/\delta\gamma_{ij}
, as they act on a gravitational wavefunctional
\psi
in the ‘coordinate representation’
\psi(\gamma_{ij})=\int_{g_{ij}(\Sigma)=\gamma_{ij}}\mathcal{D}g\exp{\{iS[g]\}}
. The Hamiltonian constraint then becomes a condition on allowed
\psi
in the form of a differential equation called the WdW equation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}\,\psi=0.

(10)

There are normal ordering ambiguities
concerning the action of the momenta as derivative operators. In the semi-classical approximation, however, where
\psi
is dominated by its saddle point,
the WdW equation reduces back to the gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi equation,
which is obtained from the Hamiltonian constraint by replacing
\pi^{ij}
by their classical value
\delta S_{cl}/\delta\gamma_{ij}
.

To bring (
7
) in a deparametrized form, in analogy to the discussion in the mechanical system, one requires a two-step process involving both substituting the solution to the constraints and a coordinate condition
[
12
]
. Discussions of such a procedure in
(2+1)
-dimensional
\Lambda=0
gravity can be found in
[
15
,
16
]
.

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{-g}\left(R_{(3)}+\frac{2}{l^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{\kappa}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{-\gamma}K-\frac{1}{\kappa l}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{-\gamma}

(13)

with momenta
\pi^{ij}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa}\sqrt{-\gamma}(K\gamma^{ij}-K^{ij}+\frac{1}{l}\gamma^{ij})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=-\frac{\sqrt{-\gamma}}{2\kappa}R_{(2)}-\frac{2\kappa}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}(\pi^{ij}\pi_{ij}-\pi^{2})+\frac{2}{l}\pi\,,

(14)

and momentum density
\mathcal{H}_{i}=-2\nabla_{j}^{(2)}\pi_{i}^{j}

For later reference, the gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi equation (from
\pi^{ij}\rightarrow\delta S_{cl}/\delta\gamma_{ij}
in
\mathcal{H}=0

\displaystyle-\frac{l}{2\kappa}R_{(2)}-\frac{2\kappa l}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{ij}}\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{kl}}\gamma_{ki}\gamma_{jl}-\left(\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{ij}}\gamma_{ij}\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{2}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{ij}}\gamma_{ij}=0,

(15)

which should be read as an equation for the on-shell action’s dependence on boundary data
\gamma_{ij}
.

To proceed, we first make a symmetric, ‘mini-superspace’ ansatz for the metric, in which all metric fields only depend on the radial coordinate:
N=N(r)
,
N^{i}=N^{i}(r)
, and
\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ij}(r)
.
This implies the
hypersurfaces
\Sigma

and the momentum constraint is automatically satisfied
\mathcal{H}_{i}\equiv 0
. Then we perform the exercise of
applying an ADM deparametrization procedure to the remaining system, i.e. the action (
13
) (with
\mathcal{H}_{i}\equiv 0
) and corresponding Hamiltonian constraint
\mathcal{H}=0
in (

\displaystyle S=\int dv\,d^{2}x\left(\tilde{\pi}^{ij}\partial_{v}\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}-\mathcal{H}_{ADM}\right)

(16)

and corresponding
rewritten Hamiltonian constraint

\displaystyle\pi_{v}=-\mathcal{H}_{ADM},

(17)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ADM}(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij},\tilde{\pi}^{ij},v)\equiv\frac{1}{\kappa l}-\frac{1}{\kappa l}\sqrt{1+2\kappa^{2}l^{2}\frac{\tilde{\pi}^{ij}\tilde{\pi}_{ij}}{v^{2}}+\frac{l^{2}R_{(2)}}{2}}.

(18)

\Lambda=0

\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}

\displaystyle v=\sqrt{-\gamma},\quad\pi_{v}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{-\gamma}},\quad\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}=\frac{\gamma_{ij}}{\sqrt{-\gamma}},\quad\tilde{\pi}^{ij}=\sqrt{-\gamma}(\pi^{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{ij}\pi)

As such, the original canonical pairs are decomposed

\displaystyle(\gamma_{ij},\pi^{ij})\rightarrow(v,\pi_{v},\tilde{\gamma}_{ij},\tilde{\pi}^{ij})

4
Holographic interpretation

In this section we move on to the holographic interpretation of the ADM deparametrization results.

The true degrees of freedom
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}
represent the
flat conformal boundary metric of AdS
3
,
and the asymptotic (
v\rightarrow\infty
) WdW equation contains Weyl anomaly physics of the dual CFT, namely its behavior under variation of scale or volume.
Indeed, the small
\kappa
(or large
v
, near-boundary)
Hamilton-Jacobi equation
(
17
) takes the form

\displaystyle\pi_{v}=\frac{l}{4\kappa}R_{(2)},

(24)

which expresses the Weyl anomaly

\displaystyle\langle T^{i}_{i}\rangle=\frac{c}{24\pi}R_{(2)}

(25)

of the large
c
holographic CFT.
The CFT has a central charge
c=12\pi l/\kappa

[
23
]
and stress tensor expectation value given by the Brown-York stress tensor

T^{ij}=2\pi^{ij}/\sqrt{-\gamma}

for the corresponding classical gravity solution (with
T^{i}_{i}=2\pi_{v}
).
At the level of the WdW equation (
21
), its near-boundary form

\displaystyle-i\frac{\delta}{\delta v}\psi=\frac{l}{4\kappa}R_{(2)}\psi

(26)

expresses the conformal Ward identity

\displaystyle-i\frac{\delta}{\delta v}Z_{CFT}=\frac{c}{48\pi}R_{(2)}Z_{CFT}

(27)

of the dual CFT, with path integral
Z_{CFT}(\gamma)=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\exp{\{iS_{CFT}[\phi]\}}
.

The above gives a volume time reformulation of Freidel’s formulation of AdS/CFT
[
10
]
that the asymptotic WdW equation is solved by
\psi=Z_{CFT}
, and of holographic renormalization
[
24
,
25
]
.

Removing the near-boundary limit, the full Hamilton-Jacobi equation
(
14
)

\displaystyle\pi_{v}=\frac{l}{4\kappa}R_{(2)}+\frac{\kappa l}{v^{2}}\tilde{\pi}^{ij}\tilde{\pi}_{ij}-\frac{\kappa l}{2}\pi_{v}^{2}

(28)

or

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{ij}}\gamma_{ij}=\frac{l}{2\kappa}R_{(2)}+\frac{2\kappa l}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{ij}}\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{kl}}\gamma_{ki}\gamma_{jl}-\left(\frac{\delta S_{cl}}{\delta\gamma_{ij}}\gamma_{ij}\right)^{2}\right)

(29)

has been
recognized in
[
9
]
to express the trace flow equation

\displaystyle\langle T_{i}^{i}\rangle=\frac{c}{24\pi}R_{(2)}-\lambda\left(\langle T_{ij}\rangle\langle T^{ij}\rangle-\langle T^{i}_{i}\rangle^{2}\right)

(30)

of dual
T\bar{T}
theories
[
26
]
with
T\bar{T}
coupling
\lambda=\kappa l/2
.
In
[
10
]
, a closed expression was given for the
\psi
solution to the radial WdW equation in vielbein formalism, in the form of
an integral transform of the CFT partition function. It is this closed form in vielbein notation that has been interpreted as
T\bar{T}

[
9
,
27
]
.

5
Laplace transform between Dirichlet and conformal boundary condition problem

For this section we switch to Euclidean signature. This leads to some sign changes, including the action
S
in (
12
) to
-S
.
The Euclidean gravitational wavefunctional

\displaystyle\psi(v,\tilde{\gamma}_{ij})=\int_{g_{ij}(\Sigma)=\gamma_{ij}}\mathcal{D}g\exp{\{-S[g]\}},

(31)

with
\delta S_{cl}=-\int d^{2}x\,(\tilde{\pi}^{ij}\delta\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}+\pi_{v}\delta v)
for the saddle point, can be Laplace transformed
[
21
]
to

\displaystyle\phi(\pi_{v},\tilde{\gamma}_{ij})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}v\,e^{-\int d^{2}x\,\pi_{v}v}\psi(v,\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}).

(32)

It gives a wavefunction
\phi=\int_{CBC}\mathcal{D}g\exp{\{-\tilde{S}[g]\}}
with a transformed action
\tilde{S}=S+\int d^{2}x\,\pi_{v}v
whose on-shell variation

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{S}_{cl}=\int d^{2}x\,(-\tilde{\pi}^{ij}\delta\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}+v\delta\pi_{v})

(33)

is
the suitable one for conformal boundary conditions (CBC) (in three bulk dimensions, the action with a well-defined variational principle for CBC is the Einstein-Hilbert action plus 1/2 times the Gibbons-Hawking-York term, compared to Einstein-Hilbert plus Gibbons-Hawking-York for Dirichlet boundary conditions). Namely,
\delta\tilde{S}_{cl}
vanishes for fixed conformal metric
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}
and fixed ‘York time’
\pi_{v}
. Equivalently, as
\pi_{v}=-(K+\frac{2}{l})/(2\kappa)
, we can replace the
\pi_{v}
-dependence of
\phi
in (
32
) by
K
-dependence and
\delta\pi_{v}
by
\delta K
in (
33
). This makes clear that CBC fix the conformal metric and the trace of extrinsic curvature at

\Sigma
. These boundary conditions are discussed in
[
6
]
to lead to well-defined wavefunctions
\phi
in perturbation theory.

The corresponding Hamilton-Jacobi equation for
\tilde{S}_{cl}
will contain variations
\delta\tilde{S}_{cl}/\delta\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}
and
\delta\tilde{S}_{cl}/\delta\pi_{v}
, and the corresponding WdW equation for
\phi
, similarly as the discussion in Section
3
, allows an interpretation as a Schrödinger-like (in Euclidean signature) evolution equation in
York time

\pi_{v}
or
K
.

Where in statistical physics the Laplace transform takes us between different ensemble descriptions,
here the Laplace transform takes us from a Dirichlet problem, with volume time
v
as radial time, to a conformal boundary condition problem, with York time
K
as radial time.

6
BTZ solution from Hamilton-Jacobi

In this section we discuss the classical, non-rotating BTZ solution of AdS
3
-gravity by solving the gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi equation, and next construct a corresponding wavepacket solution to the WdW equation. In this and the next section, we follow closely
[
8
]
, applied to one lower dimension.

The starting point is the mini-superspace (all metric components functions of
r
only), radial ADM ansatz of (
11
), with
N^{i}=0

and in the volume coordinates introduced in (
19
),

\displaystyle ds^{2}=N^{2}dr^{2}+v\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}=N^{2}dr^{2}+v\tilde{\gamma}_{tt}dt^{2}+v\tilde{\gamma}_{tt}^{-1}dt^{2}.

(34)

In the second equality we made use of the fact that
\sqrt{-\tilde{\gamma}}=1
(and have set
\gamma_{t\varphi}=0
to restrict to non-rotating solutions for simplicity).
Working with this ansatz, we would obtain the outside-horizon region of BTZ (where
r
is a spacelike coordinate). To continue constructing instead the inside-horizon region (where
r
is a timelike coordinate), we work with the ansatz

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-N^{2}dr^{2}+ve^{k}\frac{dt^{2}}{(\Delta t)^{2}}+ve^{-k}\frac{d\varphi^{2}}{(\Delta\varphi)^{2}}.

(35)

Aside from the sign of the
dr^{2}
term, there are two more changes. One is using the notation
e^{k}
for
\tilde{\gamma}_{tt}
.
The second is that

v
is now the actual volume (not volume density)
of the radial slices
\Sigma
, with dimension length squared.
This is realized
by working with rescaled coordinates
t/\Delta t
and
\varphi/\Delta\varphi
.

With this ansatz, the
Hamilton-Jacobi equation takes the form

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\partial S_{cl}}{\partial k}\right)^{2}+v^{2}\left(\frac{\partial S_{cl}}{\partial v}\right)^{2}+\frac{v^{2}}{\kappa^{2}l^{2}}=0,

(36)

where
S_{cl}
, to be complete, is now referring to the gravitational action excluding the counterterm (in contrast to the notation in previous sections).
It is solved by

\displaystyle S_{cl}=\frac{v}{\kappa l}\sinh(k+k_{0})

(37)

with integration constant
k_{0}
. The Hamilton-Jacobi procedure further imposes
\partial S_{cl}/\partial k_{0}=\epsilon_{0}
for
\epsilon_{0}
another constant. This can be solved for the metric components in terms of the integration constants,

\displaystyle v=\kappa l\epsilon_{0}\sech(k+k_{0}),

(38)

and one obtains indeed the BTZ metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(\frac{dz^{2}}{f(z)}-f(z)d\tilde{t}^{2}+d\varphi^{2}\right)

(39)

with
z=1/r
,
\tilde{t}=te^{-k_{0}}
and
f(z)=1-2\kappa\epsilon_{0}z^{2}
(for
l=1
). It represents an AdS
3
-gravity black hole with mass
\epsilon_{0}
.

7
BTZ wavepacket

Going to the quantum level, the WdW equation in the
(v,k)
-notation of Section
6
is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{k}^{2}\psi-v\partial_{v}(v\partial_{v}\psi)+\frac{v^{2}}{\kappa^{2}l^{2}}\psi=0.

(40)

The applied normal ordering choice is that of Laplace normal ordering. Let us expand a bit on this.
In WdW gravity, the
\Sigma
-metric
\gamma_{ij}
takes the role of configuration space coordinates
q^{A}
.
The metric
G_{AB}
on that configuration space is called the DeWitt metric, and the configuration space itself superspace.
Working with the ansatz (
35
), it is the functions
v(r)
and
k(r)
that form the superspace coordinates
q^{A}
(
A=1,2
). Their corresponding momenta
p_{v}
and
p_{k}
, or
p_{A}
(
A=1,2
), are obtained in the usual way from derivatives of the Lagrangian with respect to
\partial_{r}q^{A}
.
The DeWitt metric
can be read off from the gravitational action in Hamiltonian form (
13
) or from the Hamiltonian density (
14
) (evaluated on the ansatz (
35
)) from matching to the expected kinetic plus potential term expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2}G^{AB}p_{A}p_{B}+V(q^{A}).

(41)

Namely,

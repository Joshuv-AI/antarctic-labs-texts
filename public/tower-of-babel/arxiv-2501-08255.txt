Q Q -shaped derived categories as derived categories of differential graded bimodules
Gustavo Jasso

Abstract
. We prove that, under mild assumptions, the Q Q -shaped derived categories introduced by Holm and Jørgensen are equivalent to derived categories of differential graded bimodules over differential graded categories. This yields new derived invariance results for Q Q -shaped derived categories that allow us to extend known descriptions of such categories as derived categories of differential graded bimodules over (possibly graded) algebras.

1.
Introduction

Q
-shaped derived categories are recent generalisations of derived categories
of algebras introduced by Holm and Jørgensen in
[
HJ22
]
based on ideas of
Iyama and Minamoto
[
IM15
]
. In this generalisation, cochain complexes of
modules over an algebra
A
are replaced by ‘
Q
-shaped’ analogues, where
Q
is
a small category satisfying suitable assumptions, see
[
HJ24
]
for a recent
survey and
[
DSS17
,
EEGR08
,
GH10
,
HJ19
]
for previous related work. The

Q
-shaped derived category
\operatorname{D}_{Q}\!\left(A\right)
is an algebraic triangulated
category with small coproducts and, moreover, it is known to be compactly
generated
[
HJ24a
]
. Therefore, by Keller’s Recognition Theorem
[
Kel94
]
,
the
Q
-shaped derived category
\operatorname{D}_{Q}\!\left(A\right)
is equivalent as a triangulated
category to the usual derived category
\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
of a small differential
graded category
\mathcal{A}
. On the other hand, for certain choices of
Q
, it is known
that there is an equivalence of triangulated categories of the form

\operatorname{D}_{Q}\!\left(A\right)\simeq\operatorname{D}\!\left(A\otimes\Gamma\right)

where
\Gamma
is the (possibly graded) endomorphism algebra of a well-chosen

-shaped derived category of the ground commutative

[

,
IKM17
,
Sai23
]
. One reason why the latter descriptions are not
immediately apparent is that the
Q
-shaped derived category is defined as a
localisation of the category of
(A\otimes Q)
-modules, rather than as a
localisation of the category of cochain complexes thereof. The main result in
this article is that, under mild assumptions on
A
, such a description is
available for arbitrary

categories.

2.1.1.
Differential graded
\mathbf{k}
-modules

We denote the category of
differential graded (=dg)
\mathbf{k}
-modules
(equivalently,
cochain complexes of
\mathbf{k}
-modules) by
\operatorname{dgMod}\!\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
. Thus, the objects of

\operatorname{dgMod}\!\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
are pairs
(V,d_{V})
consisting of a graded
\mathbf{k}
-module

V=\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{i}\qquad d_{V}(V^{i})\subseteq V^{i+1}

and a degree
1
morphism
{d_{V}\colon V\to V}
such that
d_{V}\circ d_{V}=0
. A

morphism of dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules

{f\colon(V,d_{V})\to(W,d_{W})}
is a (degree 0)
morphism
f\colon V\to W
of graded

\mathbf{k}
-modules,
f(V^{i})\subseteq W^{i}
, that commutes with the differentials:
d_{W}\circ f=f\circ d_{V}
.

Recall that the tensor product
V\otimes W
of two dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules
{V=(V,d_{V})}

and
{W=(W,d_{W})}
is the graded
\mathbf{k}
-module whose homogeneous component of degree

i
is the
\mathbf{k}
-module

(2.1)

(V\otimes W)^{i}\coloneqq\coprod_{i=j+k}V^{j}\otimes W^{k}

and which is equipped with the differential

d_{V\otimes W}\coloneqq d_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W}+\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes d_{W},

that is

(2.2)

d_{V\otimes W}(v\otimes w)=d_{V}(v)\otimes w+(-1)^{j}v\otimes d_{W}(w),\qquad v\in V^{j}.

2.3
.

If
V=V^{0}
is concentrated in degree
0
(hence necessarily
d_{V}=0
) and
W

is arbitrary, then

V\otimes W=\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V\otimes W^{i},\qquad d_{V\otimes W}(v\otimes w)=v\otimes d_{W}(w).

The tensor product of dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules endows
\operatorname{dgMod}\!\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
with a symmetric
monoidal structure with symmetry constraint given by the Koszul sign rule

V\otimes W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}W\otimes V,\qquad v\otimes w\longmapsto(-1)^{ij}w\otimes v,

where
v\in V^{i}
and
w\in W^{j}
. This monoidal structure is
closed
[
Rie14
, Def. 3.3.6]
with the following internal
\operatorname{Hom}

functor
[
Kel06
, Sec. 2.1]
: Given dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules
V=(V,d_{V})
and

W=(W,d_{W})
, the dg
\mathbf{k}
-module of morphisms between them is the graded

\mathbf{k}
-module

\mathbf{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\bullet}\!\left(V,W\right)\coloneqq\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}^{i}\!\left(V,W\right)

whose homogeneous component of degree
i
is the
\mathbf{k}
-module

(2.4)

\mathbf{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}^{i}\!\left(V,W\right)\coloneqq\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}\!\left(V^{j},W^{i+j}\right),

and which is equipped with the differential

(2.5)

\partial(f)\coloneqq d_{W}\circ f-(-1)^{i}f\circ d_{V},\qquad f\in\mathbf{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}^{i}\!\left(V,W\right);

in coordinates:

(2.6)

\partial(f)=(d_{W}^{i+j}\circ f^{j}-(-1)^{i}f^{j+1}\circ d_{V}^{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.

For example, the
0
-cocycles in
\mathbf{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\bullet}\!\left(V,W\right)
are precisely the morphisms
of dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules
{V\to W}
, and two
0
-cocycles are cohomologous if and
only if they are cochain-homotopic in the usual sense.

A
differential graded category
is a category enriched in dg

\mathbf{k}
-modules
[
Kel06
, Sec. 2.2]
, and hence all notions of enriched category
theory such as enriched functors, enriched opposite categories, …, apply in
this context (see the standard reference
[
Kel05
]
or
[
Rie14
, Ch. 3]

for a modern account). In slightly more detail, the morphisms between two
objects in a dg category form a dg
\mathbf{k}
-module and the (graded) composition law
satisfies the (graded) Leibniz rule, corresponding to the fact that it is given
by a morphism of dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules. Similarly, a dg functor
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

between dg
\mathbf{k}
-categories is a
\mathbf{k}
-linear functor that preserves the grading
and the differentials on the dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules of morphisms, which is to say that
its components

F\colon\mathcal{A}(a_{1},a_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(F(a_{1}),F(a_{2})),\qquad a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A},

are morphisms of dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules, etc.

For example, the above internal
\operatorname{Hom}
allows us to promote

\operatorname{dgMod}\!\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
to a dg category that we denote by
\mathbf{dgMod}\!\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
(compare with

[
Rie14
, p. 37]
). Notice that the composition of homogeneous morphisms

{f\colon U\to V}
and
{g\colon V\to W}
with
f
of degree
i
is given by

(2.7)

g\circ f=(g^{i+j}\circ f^{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.

2.8
.

In what follows we identify dg algebras with dg categories with a single
object in the obvious way.

2.9
.

The above definitions extend verbatim to define the dg category

of
cochain complexes in an additive category

(which itself need not have a

Each dg category

with the same objects as

and whose graded

-modules of

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)(x,y)\coloneqq\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(\mathcal{A}(x,y)\right),\qquad x,y\in\mathcal{A},

are equipped with the induced composition law. The homogeneous morphisms of
degree
0
in
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
span the
0

\operatorname{H}^{0}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

which is an ordinary category.

\mathbf{dgMod}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

We adopt the convention that dg modules over a small dg

2.1.3.
The derived category of a differential graded category

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a small dg category. A morphism of dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules
f\colon M\to N

is a
quasi-isomorphism
if the induced morphisms

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(f_{a}\right)\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(M_{a}\right)\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(N_{a}\right),\qquad a\in\mathcal{A},

are isomorphisms of graded
\mathbf{k}
-modules. The
derived dg category of
\mathcal{A}

is the full dg subcategory
\mathbf{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
of
\mathbf{dgMod}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
spanned by the
cofibrant dg modules, that is the the dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules
P
with the following
property: for every object-wise surjective quasi-isomorphism of dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules

L\to M
, every morphism of dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules
P\to M
factors through
L
. The

derived category of
\mathcal{A}
is the
0
-th cohomology category

\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\coloneqq\operatorname{H}^{0}\!\left(\mathbf{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right),

which is locally small but large. We recall that there are canonical
isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}\!\left(\operatorname{h}_{a},M[i]\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{i}\!\left(M_{a}\right),\qquad M\in\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right),

which induce an isomorphism between the graded subcategory of the derived
category spanned by the representable dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules and the cohomology
category
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
of
\mathcal{A}

[
Kel06
, Sec. 3.2]
.

The derived category of
\mathcal{A}
is a triangulated category with small coproducts
and the set

\left\{\operatorname{h}_{a}\in\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\,\middle|\,a\in\mathcal{A}\right\}\subseteq\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

of representable dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules forms a set of compact
generators
[
Kel06
, Sec. 3.2–3.5]
. The latter statement means that the
following two conditions hold:

•

For each object

the functor

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}\!\left(\operatorname{h}_{a},-\right)\colon\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}\!\left(\mathbf{k}\right)

preserves small coproducts, which by definition means that

\operatorname{h}_{a}\in\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is a
compact object
.

•

A dg
\mathcal{A}
-module
M\in\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is a zero object if and only if

\forall a\in\mathcal{A},\ \forall i\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}\!\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}\!\left(\operatorname{h}_{a},M[i]\right)\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}\!\left(M_{a}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0.

The above definitions generalise in the obvious way to define a set of compact
generators in a triangulated category with small coproducts, see for
example
[
Nee01
]
.

2.2.
The homotopy theory of dg categories

We denote by

its (locally small)

2.2.1.
The tensor product of dg categories

The tensor product of two dg categories
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is the dg category

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
with objects the pairs
(a,b)
,
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
, and whose
dg
\mathbf{k}
-modules of morphisms

(2.11)

(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B})((a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2}))\coloneqq\mathcal{A}(a_{1},a_{2})\otimes\mathcal{B}(b_{1},b_{2}),

are endowed with the differential
d_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{B}}+1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes d_{\mathcal{B}}
, that is

d(f\otimes g)\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{A}}(f)\otimes g+(-1)^{i}f\otimes d_{\mathcal{B}}(g)

for a homogeneous morphism
f\in\mathcal{A}(a_{1},a_{2})^{i}
and
g\in\mathcal{B}(b_{1},b_{2})
(compare
with (
2.2
)). The composition law in

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
is determined by those in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
according the the formula

(2.12)

(f_{2}\otimes g_{2})\circ(f_{1}\otimes g_{1})\coloneqq(-1)^{ij}(f_{2}\circ f_{1})\otimes(g_{2}\circ g_{1}),

where
f_{1}
is a homogeneous morphism of degree
i
and
g_{2}
is a homogeneous
morphism of degree
j
. The unit of the tensor product of dg categories is the
one-object dg category associated to the ground commutative ring
\mathbf{k}
.

2.13
.

If
\mathcal{A}=A
is an ordinary algebra and
\mathcal{B}
is arbitrary then the dg

\mathbf{k}
-modules of the tensor product
A\otimes\mathcal{B}
are given by

A\otimes\mathcal{B}(b_{1},b_{2})=\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A\otimes\mathcal{B}(b_{1},b_{2})^{i},\qquad b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B},

and are endowed with the differential

d_{A\otimes\mathcal{B}}(a\otimes f)=a\otimes d_{\mathcal{B}}(f),

see

2.3
. Furthermore, no Koszul signs appear in the
composition law for

since

is concentrated in degree
0
.

The category

where

denotes the passage to the dg category of

\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

[
Kel06
, Sec. 2.3]
(see
also

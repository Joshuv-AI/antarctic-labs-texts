Box dimension of the graphs of recurrent fractal interpolation functions Thanks: Corresponding author: Lai Jiang
Lai Jiang, Xiao-Hui Li, Zhen Liang, Huo-Jun Ruan

Abstract
. Let f f be a generalized affine recurrent fractal interpolation function with vertical scaling functions. In this paper, by introducing underlying local iterated function systems of f f , we define restricted vertical scaling matrices. Then we prove the monotonicity of spectral radii of these matrices without additional conditions. We also prove the irreducibility of these matrices under the assumption that vertical scaling functions are positive. With these results, we estimate the upper and lower box dimensions of the graphs of f f by the limits of spectral radii of restricted vertical scaling matrices. In particular, we obtain an explicit formula of the box dimension of the graph of f f under certain constraint conditions.

Primary 28A80; Secondary 41A30.

1.
Introduction

Fractal functions, including Weierstrass functions and Takagi functions, are known as continuous but nowhere differentiable functions. There are numerous works on these functions. One of interests focuses on fractal dimensions of the graphs of these functions, including Hausdorff dimension and box dimension, see
[
3
,
4
,
16
,
22
,
25
]
and the references therein.

The classical Weierstrass function is defined by

W_{\lambda,b}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\cos(2\pi b^{n}x),\quad x\in[0,1],

where
b\geq 2
is an integer and
1/b<\lambda<1
. It is well known that the graphs of these functions are invariant sets of certain iterated function systems (IFSs), see
[
19
, section 6]
for example.

In 1986, Baransley
[
6
]
introduced fractal interpolation functions (FIFs). Basically, these functions interpolate give data while the graphs of these functions are invariant sets of certain IFSs. As a result, the classical Weierstrass functions and the classical Takagi function are all FIFs. Since 1986, FIFs have attracted many attentions to study their analytic properties
[
2
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
27
]
and fractal dimensions
[
1
,
5
,
10
,
14
,
17
,
23
]
. Meanwhile, these functions are suitable to fit fractal-like data, including speech data and the profiles of mountain ranges
[
21
,
26
]
.

Notice that the graph of a FIF is an invariant set of certain IFS. Thus, roughly speaking, a small piece of the graph is a copy of the whole graph. In order to generalize FIFs, Barnsley, Elton and Hardin
[
7
]
used recurrent IFSs to construct recurrent FIFs as follows.

Let
N\geq 2
and
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n=0}^{N}
be a given data set, where
x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N}
. Fix the index pairs
\{\ell(n),r(n)\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
with
\ell(n)<r(n)
for all
n
. For each
1\leq n\leq N
, we define
I_{n}=[x_{n-1},x_{n}]
and
D_{n}=[x_{\ell(n)},x_{r(n)}]
, and assume that the following conditions hold.

(1)

L_{n}:D_{n}\to I_{n}
is a contractive homeomorphism.

(2)

F_{n}:D_{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, and there exists
0<\beta_{n}<1
such that

|F_{n}(x,y^{\prime})-F_{n}(x,y^{\prime\prime})|\leq\beta_{n}|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|,\quad x\in D_{n},\;\;y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

(3)

If we define
W_{n}:D_{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to I_{n}\times\mathbb{R}
by

W_{n}(x,y)=(L_{n}(x),F_{n}(x,y))
, then

W_{n}
satisfies the following interpolation constraint:

W_{n}\big(\{(x_{\ell(n)},y_{\ell(n)}),(x_{r(n)},y_{r(n)})\}\big)=\{(x_{n-1},y_{n-1}),(x_{n},y_{n})\}.

Given a function
g
on
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote the graph of
g
by
\Gamma g=\{(x,g(x)):\,x\in E\}
. Additionally, assume that
J
is a subset of
E
, we write
g|_{J}
the restriction of
g
on
J
, and let
\Gamma g|_{J}
be the graph of
g|_{J}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
,
24
]
)
.

There exists a unique continuous function
f
on
[x_{0},x_{N}]
such that
f(x_{n})=y_{n}
for all
0\leq n\leq N
, and

(1.1)

\Gamma f|_{I_{n}}=W_{n}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{n}}\big),\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

The function
f
in this theorem is called a
recurrent fractal interpolation function
(RFIF) determined by the recurrent IFS
\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{N}\}
. In the original work
[
7
]
, all
W_{n}
are affine maps. The corresponding RFIF is called an
affine RFIF
. Barnsley et. al.
[
7
]
obtained the box dimension of the graphs of affine RFIFs under certain conditions. This result was generalized by Ruan, Xiao and Yang
[
24
]
. We remark that in
[
24
]
,
L_{n}
can be not contractive.

We call the function
f
in Theorem
1.1
a
generalized affine RFIF
if for each
1\leq n\leq N
,

L_{n}(x)=a_{n}x+b_{n},\quad F_{n}(x,y)=S_{n}(x)y+q_{n}(x),

where
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
are real numbers,
S_{n}(x)
and
q_{n}(x)
are continuous function on
D_{n}
with
|S_{n}(x)|<1
for all
x\in D_{n}
.
It is very challenging to calculate the box dimension of the graphs of generalized affine RFIFs without any restriction. In this paper, we will study
\dim_{B}\Gamma f
, the box dimension of
\Gamma f
, where the following conditions are satisfied for each
n
.

(A1)

x_{n}-x_{n-1}=(x_{N}-x_{0})/N
.

(A2)

S_{n}
is a continuous function of bounded variation on
D_{n}
and
|S_{n}(x)|<1
for all
x\in D_{n}
.

(A3)

q_{n}
is a continuous function of bounded variation on
D_{n}
.

In
[
18
,
19
]
, the first author and the last author introduced the sequences of upper and lower vertical scaling matrices, and used the limits of spectral radii of these matrices to estimate the box dimension of the graphs of generalized affine FIFs.
Naturally, we want to use similar method in the recurrent case. Notice that in
[
18
,
19
]
, we have the following key property: lower vertical scaling matrices are irreducible if vertical scaling functions are positive. However, this key property does not hold in the recurrent case. Thus we need new method to estimate the box dimension of
\Gamma f
in the present setting.

Now we sketch our strategy. Firstly, by using the relationship between the intervals
\{I_{n};D_{n};n=1,2\ldots,N\}
, we define strongly connected components
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}
of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Then we introduce underlying local IFSs
\{L_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
of the generalized affine RFIF
f
. By using these local IFSs, we define restricted vertical scaling matrices. With these matrices, we can estimate the box dimension of
\Gamma f
on a sequence of basic sets related with local IFS
\{L_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
. On the other hand, we directly estimate the box dimension of
\Gamma f
on the complement of basic sets.
Finally, we successfully estimate the box dimension of the graphs of generalized affine RFIFs. See Section 2 for the definition of
\Lambda_{r}
, restricted vertical scaling matrices, and basic sets.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we give some definitions including restricted vertical scaling matrices, and also present main results.
In Section 3, we study irreducibility of restricted vertical scaling matrices and the monotonicity of spectral radii of these matrices. In Section 4, we present some inequalities and equalities for the box dimension of the graphs of generalized affine RFIFs on various sub-intervals of
[x_{0},x_{N}]
. By using these results, in Section 5, we estimate the box dimension of the graphs of generalized affine RFIFs by the limits of spectral radii of restricted vertical scaling matrices. In Section 6, we present an example to illustrate our results.

2.
Preliminaries and main results

2.1.
Basic sets and related notations

For any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we say there is an
edge
from
j
to
i
if
I_{j}\subset D_{i}
.
Let
V
be a nonempty subset of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. For any
i,j\in V
, a finite sequence
\{i_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{t}
is called a
path
in
V
from
j
to
i
, if
i_{0}=j
,
i_{t}=i
,
i_{\ell}\in V
for all
0\leq\ell\leq t
, and there is an edge from
i_{\ell-1}
to
i_{\ell}
for each
1\leq\ell\leq t
. The set
V
is called
strongly connected
if for any
i,j\in V
, there is a path in
V
from
j
to
i
. Furthermore,
V
is called a
strongly connected component
of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
, if it is strongly connected and there is no strongly connected subset
U
of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that
V\subsetneq U
.

In the sequel of the paper, we always assume that
\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}\}
is the family of all strongly connected components of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Given
r=1,\ldots,m
,
we naturally have a local IFS
\{D_{n};L_{n}:n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
(or
\{L_{n}:n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
for short), while the corresponding local Hutchinson-Barnsley operator
W_{\textrm{local},r}:\,\mathcal{X}_{r}\to\mathcal{X}_{r}
is defined by

W_{\textrm{local},r}(A)=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}L_{n}(A\cap D_{n}),\quad A\in\mathcal{X}_{r}.

Here
\mathcal{X}_{r}
is the family of all subsets of
\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}
. A nonempty set
K
in
\mathcal{X}_{r}
is called an invariant set of the local IFS
\{L_{n}:n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
if
W_{\textrm{local},r}(K)=K
. We call
\{L_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
,
1\leq r\leq m
, underlying local IFSs of the generalized affine RFIF
f
. For more details on local IFS, please see
[
9
,
20
]
and the references therein.

For
r=1,\ldots,m
, we define
B_{r,0}=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}
and

(2.1)

B_{r,k}=W_{\textrm{local},r}(B_{r,k-1})=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}L_{n}(B_{r,k-1}\cap D_{n}),\quad k\geq 1.

The set
B_{r,k}
is called a
k
-th basic set
of
\{L_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
.
Notice that
B_{r,1}=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{n}\subset B_{r,0}
since
\Lambda_{r}
is a strongly connected components of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Thus
\{B_{r,k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a decreasing sequence of nonempty compact sets with respect to
k
. As a result,
K_{r}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{r,k}
is a nonempty compact set. It is routine to check that
K_{r}
is the maximal invariant set of the local IFS
\{L_{n}:n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
, while we do not need the fact in this paper.

There is another way to obtain basic sets. Let
1\leq r\leq m
.
For each
k\geq 0
, we recursively define
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}\}_{k\geq 0}
by letting
{\mathcal{B}}_{r,0}=\{D_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
and

{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}=\big\{L_{n}(E):\,n\in\Lambda_{r},E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k-1}\mbox{ with }E\subset D_{n}\big\},\quad k\geq 1.

By definition,
{\mathcal{B}}_{r,1}=\{I_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
. Each set
E
in
{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
is called a

k
-th level basic interval
of the local IFS
\{L_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
. It is obvious that
B_{r,k}=\cup{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
for all
r
and
k
.

In this paper, we always assume that the local IFS
\{L_{n}:n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
satisfies the following uniformly contracting condition for all
1\leq r\leq m
.

(A4)

For any
r=1,\ldots,m
, there exists an integer
T_{r}\geq 2
such that

\frac{|D_{n}|}{|I_{n}|}=T_{r},\quad n\in\Lambda_{r},

where we use
|J|
to denote the length of an interval
J
.

Write the cardinality of
\Lambda_{r}
by
d_{r}
. Assume that
\Lambda_{r}=\{a_{r,1},a_{r,2},\ldots,a_{r,d_{r}}\}
with

a_{r,1}<a_{r,2}<\cdots<a_{r,d_{r}}
.
For any
k\geq 1
,
1\leq t\leq d_{r}
, and
1\leq j\leq{T_{r}}^{k-1}
, we define

(2.2)

I_{r,(t-1){T_{r}}^{k-1}+j}^{k}=\Big[x_{(a_{r,t}-1)}+\frac{(j-1)(x_{N}-x_{0})}{N{T_{r}}^{k-1}},x_{(a_{r,t}-1)}+\frac{j(x_{N}-x_{0})}{N{T_{r}}^{k-1}}\Big].

For example, for each
1\leq t\leq d_{r}
,

(2.3)

I_{r,t}^{1}=I_{r,(t-1){T_{r}}^{0}+1}^{1}=I_{a_{r,t}}.

Notice that for each
E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
with
k\geq 1
, there exists unique
1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}
such that
E=I_{r,i}^{k}
. Thus, for any
k\geq 1
, we define

\Theta_{r,k}=\big\{1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}:\,I_{r,i}^{k}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}\big\}.

2.2.
Restricted vertical scaling matrices

Given
1\leq r\leq m
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we first define
d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\times d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}
matrices
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}
and
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}
as follows.
For any
1\leq i,j\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}
, let
1\leq n\leq N
be the unique element satisfying
I_{r,i}^{k}\subset I_{n}
. We define

D_{r,i}^{k}=L_{n}^{-1}(I_{r,i}^{k}).

Furthermore, we write

{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}=\begin{cases}\max\big\{\big|S_{n}(x)\big|:\,x\in I_{r,j}^{k}\big\},\quad&\mbox{if }I_{r,j}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k},\\
0,\quad&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}=\begin{cases}\min\big\{\big|S_{n}(x)\big|:\,x\in I_{r,j}^{k}\big\},\quad&\mbox{if }I_{r,j}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k},\\
0,\quad&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

With the help of
{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}
and
{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}
, we define the matrices
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}
and
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}
by letting

\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}\big)_{i,j}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k},\quad\big({\underline{M}}_{r,k}\big)_{i,j}={\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}.

The matrix
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}
(resp.
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}
) is called the
level-
k
upper (resp. lower) vertical scaling matrix
associated with
\Lambda_{r}
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
be a matrix. For any
\Theta\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, we write
A|_{\Theta}=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in\Theta}
and call it the
principal submatrix
of
A
. The matrix
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
(resp.
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
) is called a
upper (resp. lower) restricted vertical scaling matrix
associated with
\Lambda_{r}
and
\Theta_{r,k}
. We remark that these matrices play a crucial role in our study.

2.3.
Main results

First, we have the following results on the irreducibility of restricted vertical scaling matrices.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
.

(1)

Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Then
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
are irreducible for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.

(2)

Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Then there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
such that
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
are irreducible for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Given an
n\times n
matrix
A
, we write

\rho(A)=\max\{|\lambda_{i}|:1\leq i\leq n\}
, where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
are eigenvalues of
A
.
We call
\rho(A)
the
spectral radius
of
A
.
Using the above theorem, we study the monotonicity of spectral radii of
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
.

(1)

The sequence
\{\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is decreasing with respect to
k
. Thus, the limit
\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})
exists, denoted by
{{\rho}}_{r}
.

(2)

The sequence
\{\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is increasing with respect to
k
. Thus, the limit
\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})
also exists. Moreover, if
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})={{\rho}}_{r}.

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
. In the case that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, let
k_{r}^{*}
be the smallest positive integer such that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{r}^{*}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. We define

(2.4)

d_{r}^{*}=\begin{cases}1+\log\rho_{r}/\log T_{r},&\textrm{if }\mathrm{Var}(f,E)=+\infty\textrm{ for some }E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k_{r}^{*}},\\
1,&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here
\mathrm{Var}(f,E)
is the classical total variation of
f
on
E
.
We obtain the following result on the box dimension of
\Gamma f
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f
be a generalized affine RFIF satisfying conditions (A1)-(A4). Then the following results hold.

(1)

\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma f\leq 1+\max\big\{\log{{\rho}}_{1}/\log T_{1},\log{{\rho}}_{2}/\log T_{2},\ldots,\log{{\rho}}_{m}/\log T_{m},0\big\}
.

(2)

If for all
1\leq r\leq m
and all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, the function
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
, then

\dim_{B}\Gamma f=\max\{d_{1}^{*},d_{2}^{*},\ldots,d_{m}^{*},1\}.

3.
Analysis on restricted vertical scaling matrices

3.1.
Some facts of basic sets and basic intervals

By definitions of basic sets and basic intervals, we have the following simple facts. Since their proofs are trivial, we omit the details.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
and
k\geq 1
.

(1)

For any two distinct elements
i,j
in
\{1,2,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}
, we have
(I_{r,i}^{k}\cap I_{r,j}^{k})^{\circ}=\emptyset
, where we use
(\cdot)^{\circ}
to denote the interior of a set.

(2)

If
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}\setminus\Theta_{r,k}
, then
(I_{r,i}^{k}\cap B_{r,k})^{\circ}=\emptyset
. As a result,
(I_{r,i}^{k}\cap B_{r,k+1})^{\circ}=\emptyset
.

(3)

\Theta_{r,k}=\big\{1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}:\,I_{r,i}^{k}\subset B_{r,k}\big\}
. In particular,
\Theta_{r,1}=\{1,2,\ldots,d_{r}\}
.

(4)

For any
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
and
k^{\prime}>k
, the set
\{i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k^{\prime}}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\}
is nonempty.

(5)

For any
1\leq t\leq d_{r}
,
D_{r,t}^{1}=D_{a_{r,t}}
. Furthermore, for any
1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}
, there exist
n_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
p_{i}\in\{1,2,\ldots,{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}
dependent of
r
and
k
, such that

(3.1)

D_{r,i}^{k+1}=[x_{(n_{i}-1)}+(p_{i}-1)\varepsilon_{r,k},x_{(n_{i}-1)}+p_{i}\varepsilon_{r,k}],

where
\varepsilon_{r,k}=(x_{N}-x_{0})N^{-1}{T_{r}}^{-k+1}
. As a result, we have either
D_{r,i}^{k+1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
or
(D_{r,i}^{k+1}\cap B_{r,k})^{\circ}=\emptyset
.

By (
2.2
), for any
1\leq r\leq m
,
k\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq d_{r}T_{r}^{k-1}
,

I_{r,i}^{k}=\bigcup_{\ell=(i-1)T_{r}+1}^{iT_{r}}I_{r,\ell}^{k+1}.

Hence,

B_{r,k}=\bigcup_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}I_{r,i}^{k}=\bigcup_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}\bigcup_{\ell=(i-1)T_{r}+1}^{iT_{r}}I_{r,\ell}^{k+1}
.
We define

\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}=\big\{(i-1)T_{r}+t:\,i\in\Theta_{r,k},1\leq t\leq T_{r}\big\}.

It is clear that

(3.2)

\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}=\big\{1\leq\ell\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}:\,I_{r,\ell}^{k+1}\subset B_{r,k}\big\}.

Notice that
B_{r,k+1}\subset B_{r,k}
. Thus, combining (
3.2
) with

(3.3)

\Theta_{r,k+1}=\big\{1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}:\,I_{r,i}^{k+1}\subset B_{r,k+1}\big\},

we have

(3.4)

\Theta_{r,k+1}\subset\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}.

Now we prove the following simple lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{n}
.

(2)

B_{r,1}=B_{r,2}
.

(3)

B_{r,k}=B_{r,k+1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(4)

K_{r}=B_{r,1}
.

(5)

\Theta_{r,k+1}=\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Proof.

By definition, (1) is equivalent to
B_{r,0}=B_{r,1}
. Notice that
\{B_{r,k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is decreasing with respect to
k
. Hence, from (
2.1
) and
K_{r}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{r,k}
, it is clear that

(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)\Leftrightarrow(4).

Meanwhile, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that
(3)\Leftrightarrow(5)
.

From above arguments, it suffices to prove that
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Assume that (2) holds. Then for any
n\in\Lambda_{r}
,
we have
I_{n}\cap B_{r,1}=I_{n}=L_{n}(D_{n})
and

I_{n}=I_{n}\cap B_{r,2}=L_{n}(D_{n}\cap B_{r,1})=L_{n}\Big(\bigcup_{j\in\Lambda_{r}:I_{j}\subset D_{n}}I_{j}\Big).

Notice that
L_{n}
is a bijection.
Thus, for each
n\in\Lambda_{r}
,

D_{n}=\bigcup_{j\in\Lambda_{r}:I_{j}\subset D_{n}}I_{j}\subset\bigcup_{j\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{j},

and therefore
\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}\subset\bigcup_{j\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{j}
. On the other hand,
\bigcup_{j\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{j}\subset\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}
always holds since
\Lambda_{r}
is a strongly connected component of
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. Thus (1) holds. ∎

3.2.
The irreducibility of restricted vertical scaling matrices

We recall some notations and definitions in matrix analysis
[
15
]
.
Given a matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
, we say
A
is
nonnegative
, denoted by
A\geq 0
, if
a_{ij}\geq 0
for all
i
and
j
.
Similarly, given
{\mathbf{u}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}),{\mathbf{v}}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we write
{\mathbf{u}}\geq{\mathbf{v}}
if
u_{i}\geq v_{i}
for all
i
. We say
{\mathbf{u}}
is positive, denoted by
{\mathbf{u}}>0
, if
u_{i}>0
for all
i
.

A nonnegative matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
is called
irreducible
if for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists a finite sequences
i_{0},\ldots,i_{t}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
i_{0}=i,i_{t}=j
, and
a_{i_{\ell-1},i_{\ell}}>0
for all
1\leq\ell\leq t-1
. Please see
[
15
]
for details.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Then for any
i,j\in\Theta_{r,k}
, there exists a finite sequence
\{i_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{t}
in
\Theta_{r,k}
such that
i_{0}=j
,
i_{t}=i
and
I^{k}_{r,i_{\ell-1}}\subset D_{r,i_{\ell}}^{k}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq t
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on
k
. Fix
1\leq r\leq m
.

First we assume that
k=1
. Notice that
\Lambda_{r}=\{a_{r,1},\ldots,a_{r,d_{r}}\}

and
\Theta_{r,1}=\{1,\ldots,d_{r}\}
. From (
2.3
) and Lemma
3.1
(5), for all
1\leq i\leq d_{r}
, we have
I_{r,i}^{1}=I_{a_{r,i}}
and
D_{r,i}^{1}=D_{a_{r,i}}
. Combining this with the fact that
\Lambda_{r}=\{a_{r,1},\ldots,a_{r,d_{r}}\}
is strong connected in
\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we know the statement holds for
k=1
.

Assume that the statement holds for
k=p
, where
p
is a positive integer. Arbitrarily pick
i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,p+1}
. Let
j
be the element in
\{1,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{p-1}\}
satisfying
I_{r,j^{\prime}}^{p+1}\subset I_{r,j}^{p}
. Then
I_{r,j}^{p}\cap B_{r,p+1}\supset I_{r,j^{\prime}}^{p+1}
. Thus from Lemma
3.1
(2),
j\in\Theta_{r,p}
.
From
i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,p+1}
, there exists
n_{0}\in\Lambda_{r}
and
E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,p}
with
E\subset D_{n_{0}}
, such that
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{p+1}=L_{n_{0}}(E)
. From
E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,p}
, there exists
1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{p-1}
such that
E=I_{r,i}^{p}
. Thus,
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{p+1}=L_{n_{0}}(I_{r,i}^{p})
so that

I_{r,i}^{p}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{p+1}
. Noting that from
I_{r,i}^{p}=E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,p}
, we have
i\in\Theta_{r,p}
. By the inductive assumption, there exists a finite sequence
\{\ell_{t}\}_{t=0}^{q}
such that

\ell_{0}=j
,
\ell_{q}=i
and
I_{r,\ell_{t-1}}^{p}\subset D_{r,\ell_{t}}^{p}
for all
1\leq t\leq q
.

For each
1\leq t\leq q
, let
n_{t}
be the unique element in
\Lambda_{r}
such that
I_{r,\ell_{t}}^{p}\subset I_{n_{t}}
. It follows from
I_{r,\ell_{t-1}}^{p}\subset D_{r,\ell_{t}}^{p}
that there exists
\ell_{t}^{\prime}\in\{(\ell_{t}-1)T_{r}+1,\ldots,\ell_{t}T_{r}\}
such that
L_{n_{t}}(I_{r,\ell_{t-1}}^{p})=I_{r,\ell_{t}^{\prime}}^{p+1}
.
Thus
I_{r,\ell_{t-1}}^{p}=D_{r,\ell_{t}^{\prime}}^{p+1}
and
I_{r,\ell_{t}^{\prime}}^{p+1}\subset I_{r,\ell_{t}}^{p}
for all
1\leq t\leq q
. In other words,
I_{r,\ell_{t-1}^{\prime}}^{p+1}\subset I_{r,\ell_{t-1}}^{p}
for all
2\leq t\leq q+1
.
As a result, for all
2\leq t\leq q
,

(3.5)

I_{r,\ell_{t-1}^{\prime}}^{p+1}\subset I_{r,\ell_{t-1}}^{p}=D_{r,\ell_{t}^{\prime}}^{p+1}.

Define
\ell_{0}^{\prime}=j^{\prime}
and
\ell_{q+1}^{\prime}=i^{\prime}
. From
\ell_{0}=j
,
\ell_{q}=i
and definitions of
i
,
j
, (
3.5
) still holds for
t=1
and
t=q+1
. Thus the statement holds for
k=p+1
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
. First we prove (1). Fix
k\geq 1
. From Lemma
3.3
, for any
i,j\in\Theta_{r,k}
, there exists a finite sequence
\{i_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{t}
in
\Theta_{r,k}
such that
i_{0}=j
,
i_{t}=i
and

I^{k}_{r,i_{\ell-1}}\subset D_{r,i_{\ell}}^{k}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq t
. Hence, by using the assumption that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, we know from definitions of vertical scaling matrices that

(3.6)

{\overline{s}}_{r,i_{\ell},i_{\ell-1}}^{k}\geq{\underline{s}}_{r,i_{\ell},i_{\ell-1}}^{k}>0,\quad 1\leq q\leq t.

As a result, both
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
are irreducible.

Now we prove (2). Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Then from the fact that
S_{n}
is continuous on
D_{n}
and
K_{r}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{r,k}
is the intersection of decreasing compact sets, there exists
k_{1}\in{{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}}
such that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
and
k\geq k_{1}
.
Fix
k\geq k_{1}+1
. From Lemma
3.3
, for any
i,j\in\Theta_{r,k}
, there exists a finite sequence
\{i_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{t}
in
\Theta_{r,k}
such that
i_{0}=j
,
i_{t}=i
and

I^{k}_{r,i_{\ell-1}}\subset D_{r,i_{\ell}}^{k}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq t
. From Lemma
3.1
(5),
D_{r,i_{\ell}}^{k}\subset B_{r,k-1}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq t
. Hence, (
3.6
) still holds since
k\geq k_{1}+1
. Thus both
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
are irreducible.
By letting
k_{0}=k_{1}+1
, (2) holds.
∎

3.3.
Convergence of spectral radii of restricted vertical scaling matrices

The following four lemmas are well known. Please see
[
15
, Chapter 8]
for details.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be a nonnegative matrix.
If
A^{\prime}
is a principal submatrix of
A
, then
\rho(A^{\prime})\leq\rho(A)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be two nonnegative matrices with
A\leq A^{\prime}
. Then
\rho(A)\leq\rho(A^{\prime})
.

Lemma 3.6

(Perron-Frobenius Theorem)
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
be an irreducible nonnegative matrix. Then
\rho(A)
is a positive eigenvalue of
A
and has a positive eigenvector.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
A
be a nonnegative matrix.
Then
\rho(A)
is an eigenvalue of
A
and there
is a nonnegative nonzero vector
{\mathbf{x}}
such that
A{\mathbf{x}}=\rho(A){\mathbf{x}}
.

For each
1\leq r\leq m
and
k\geq 1
, we introduce a
d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}\times d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}
matrix
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}
as follows. Given
1\leq i,j\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}
, let
n
be the unique element in
\Lambda_{r}
such that
I_{r,i}^{k+1}\subset I_{n}
. We define

\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\max\big\{\big|S_{n}(x)\big|:x\in D_{r,i}^{k+1}\big\},\quad&\mbox{if }I_{r,j}^{k+1}\subset D_{r,i}^{k+1},\\
0,\quad&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
1\leq r\leq m
and
k\geq 1
, we have

\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}).

Proof.

Notice that both
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}}
are principal submatrices of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}
. From (
3.4
),
\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}\supset{\Theta}_{r,k+1}
. Thus

{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}
is a principal submatrix of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}}
.
As a result, by Lemma
3.4
,

\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})\geq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}).

Now we prove the another inequality.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\lambda=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})>0
and
{\Theta}_{r,k+1}\subsetneq\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}
.
From Lemma
3.7
,

\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}}
and there is a nonnegative nonzero vector

\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}=(\widetilde{u}_{i})_{i\in{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}}}
such that

(3.7)

({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{T}=\lambda\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{T}.

Fix
i\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}\backslash{\Theta}_{r,k+1}
. Since
i\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}
, there exists
i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k}
such that
I_{r,i}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k}
. It follows from
i\not\in{\Theta}_{r,k+1}
that
D_{r,i}^{k+1}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
. Thus by Lemma
3.1
(5),
(D_{r,i}^{k+1}\cap B_{r,k})^{\circ}=\emptyset
.
Hence, for any
j\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}
, by letting
j^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k}
the unique element satisfying
I_{r,j}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,j^{\prime}}^{k}
, we have
(D_{r,i}^{k+1}\cap I_{r,j^{\prime}}^{k})^{\circ}=\emptyset
. As a result,

I_{r,j}^{k+1}\not\subset D_{r,i}^{k+1}.

Hence

({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*})_{i,j}=0,\quad j\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}.

Thus from (
3.7
) and
\lambda>0
,
we have

(3.8)

\widetilde{u}_{i}=0,\quad i\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}\backslash{\Theta}_{r,k+1}.

As a result, the vector
{\mathbf{u}}=(u_{i})_{i\in\Theta_{r,k+1}}
defined by
{\mathbf{u}}=\widetilde{{\mathbf{u}}}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}
is also a nonnegative nonzero vector.
Furthermore, for any
i\in\Theta_{r,k+1}
, by (
3.8
) and the definitions of
{\mathbf{u}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}
,

\sum_{j\in{\Theta}_{r,k+1}}({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*})_{i,j}{u}_{j}=\sum_{j\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}}({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*})_{i,j}\widetilde{u}_{j}=\lambda\widetilde{u}_{i}=\lambda u_{i}.

From the arbitrariness of
i
, we have
\lambda{\mathbf{u}}^{T}=({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}){\mathbf{u}}^{T}
.
Thus,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}
, which implies
\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})=\lambda\leq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}})
.
∎

Given
1\leq r\leq m
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\Omega\subset\{1,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}
, we define

\widetilde{\Omega}(r)=\big\{(i-1)T_{r}+\ell:\,i\in\Omega,1\leq\ell\leq T_{r}\}.

We write
\widetilde{\Omega}=\widetilde{\Omega}(r)
if there is no confusion. It is clear that
\widetilde{\Omega}(r)=\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}
if
\Omega=\Theta_{r,k}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any
1\leq r\leq m
,
k\geq 1
and
\Omega\subset\{1,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}
, we
have

\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega})=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}).

Proof.

Firstly, we are going to prove
\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega})\geq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}})
.
Write
\lambda=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}})
.
From Lemma
3.7
,

\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
and there is a nonnegative nonzero vector

\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}=(\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}
such that
({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}})\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{T}=\lambda\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{T}
.

Define a vector
{\mathbf{u}}=(u_{i})_{i\in\Omega}
by letting

{u}_{i}=\sum_{i^{\prime}=(i-1)T_{r}+1}^{iT_{r}}\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}},\quad i\in\Omega.

Obviously,
{\mathbf{u}}
is also nonnegative and nonzero.

Now we fix
i\in\Omega
. For any
(i-1)T_{r}<i^{\prime}\leq iT_{r}
, by the definition of
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{T}
,

\lambda\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\widetilde{u}_{j^{\prime}}.

Let
n
be the unique element in
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
satisfying
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\subset I_{n}
.

In the case that
D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\not\subset\bigcup_{p\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{p}
, it is clear that for all
j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, we have
I^{k+1}_{r,j^{\prime}}\not\subset D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}
so that
\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=0
. Hence,
\lambda\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}}=0
.

In the case that
D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset\bigcup_{p\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{p}
, from Lemma
3.1
(5), there exists
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}
such that
I_{r,\ell}^{k}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}
. If
\ell\not\in\Omega
, then for all
j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, we have
I^{k+1}_{r,j^{\prime}}\not\subset I_{r,\ell}^{k}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}
so that
\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=0
, which gives
\lambda\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}}=0
. If
\ell\in\Omega
, then

\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\not=0
if and only if
I^{k+1}_{r,j^{\prime}}\subset D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}=I^{k}_{r,\ell}
, which is equivalent to
(\ell-1)T_{r}<j^{\prime}\leq\ell T_{r}
. Furthermore, if
(\ell-1)T_{r}<j^{\prime}\leq\ell T_{r}
, then

\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=\max\big\{|S_{n}(x)|:\,x\in D^{k+1}_{r,i^{\prime}}\big\}=\max\big\{|S_{n}(x)|:\,x\in I^{k}_{r,\ell}\big\}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k},

where the last equality follows from the fact that
I_{r,\ell}^{k}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}
. Hence

\lambda\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}\sum_{j^{\prime}=(\ell-1)T_{r}+1}^{\ell T_{r}}\widetilde{u}_{j^{\prime}}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}{u}_{\ell}.

Notice that the map

i^{\prime}\mapsto\ell
defined by
I^{k}_{r,\ell}=D^{k+1}_{r,i^{\prime}}
is a bijection from

\big\{i^{\prime}:\,(i-1)T_{r}<i^{\prime}\leq iT_{r}\mbox{ and }D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}=I_{r,\ell}^{k}\mbox{ for some }\ell\in\Omega\big\}

to
\{\ell\in\Omega:I_{r,\ell}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}\}
. Thus from the above arguments,

\lambda{u}_{i}=\sum_{i^{\prime}=(i-1)T_{r}+1}^{iT_{r}}\lambda\widetilde{u}_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{\ell\in\Omega:I_{r,\ell}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}}{\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}u_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell\in\Omega}({\overline{M}}_{r,k})_{i,\ell}u_{\ell},

where the last equality follows from the definitions of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}
and
{\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}
.

From the arbitrariness of
i
, we have
\lambda{\mathbf{u}}^{T}=({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega}){\mathbf{u}}^{T}
.
Thus,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega}
, which implies
\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}})=\lambda\leq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega})
.

In the second part, we are going to prove
\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega})\leq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that

\mu=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega})>0
.
From Lemma
3.7
, there is a nonnegative nonzero vector
{\mathbf{v}}=(v_{i})_{i\in\Omega}
such that
\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Omega}\big){\mathbf{v}}^{T}=\mu{\mathbf{v}}^{T}
.

For any
i\in\Omega
and
(i-1)T_{r}<i^{\prime}\leq iT_{r}
, if there exists
\ell\in\Omega
such that
I_{r,\ell}^{k}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}
, then we define
\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}v_{\ell}
; otherwise, we define
\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}}=0
. We remark that in the former case,
I_{r,\ell}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}
. Hence,
it is straightforward to see that

(3.9)

\sum_{i^{\prime}=(i-1)T_{r}+1}^{iT_{r}}\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{\ell\in\Omega:I_{r,\ell}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}}{\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}v_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell\in\Omega}({\overline{M}}_{r,k})_{i,\ell}v_{\ell}=\mu v_{i}.

As a result, the vector
\widetilde{{\mathbf{v}}}=(\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}
is nonnegative and nonzero.

Fix
i\in\Omega
and
i^{\prime}\in\{(i-1)T_{r}+1,\ldots,iT_{r}\}
. Assume that
D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\not\subset\bigcup_{p\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{p}
or
D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}=I_{r,\ell}^{k}
for some
\ell\not\in\Omega
. By definition,
\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}}=0
.
Notice that in this case, for all
j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, we have
I^{k+1}_{r,j^{\prime}}\not\subset D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}
so that
\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=0
. Hence,

\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\widetilde{v}_{j^{\prime}}=0=\mu\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}}.

Assume that
D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}=I_{r,\ell}^{k}
for some
\ell\in\Omega
. By arguments in the first part of the proof,
\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\not=0
if and only if
(\ell-1)T_{r}<j^{\prime}\leq\ell T_{r}
, and if this holds,
\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}
. Thus,

\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\widetilde{v}_{j^{\prime}}=\sum_{j^{\prime}=(\ell-1)T_{r}+1}^{\ell T_{r}}\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\widetilde{v}_{j^{\prime}}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}\sum_{j^{\prime}=(\ell-1)T_{r}+1}^{\ell T_{r}}\widetilde{v}_{j^{\prime}}.

Combining this with (
3.9
),

\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\widetilde{v}_{j^{\prime}}={\overline{s}}_{r,i,\ell}^{k}\mu v_{\ell}=\mu\widetilde{v}_{i^{\prime}}.

From the arbitrariness of
i^{\prime}
, we have
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\widetilde{{\mathbf{v}}}^{T}=\mu\widetilde{{\mathbf{v}}}^{T}
so that
\mu
is an eigenvalue of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}
. Hence,

\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k})=\mu\leq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
. First we prove (1). Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. From the definitions of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}
and
{\overline{M}}_{r,k+1}
, it is easy to see that
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\geq{\overline{M}}_{r,k+1}\geq 0
. Thus from Lemma
3.5
,

\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}})\geq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k+1}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}).

Combining this with Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
,

\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})=\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{{\Theta_{r,k+1}}})\geq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k+1}|_{{\Theta_{r,k+1}}}).

Thus
\{\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is decreasing. As a result, there exists a nonnegative constant
{{\rho}}_{r}
such that

{{\rho}}_{r}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}).

Now we prove (2). For each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we define

\big({\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\big)_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\min\big\{\big|S_{n}(x)\big|:x\in D_{r,i}^{k+1}\big\},\quad&\mbox{if }I_{r,j}^{k+1}\subset D_{r,i}^{k+1},\\
0,\quad&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}\leq{\underline{M}}_{r,k+1}
so that

\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}})\leq\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k+1}|_{\Theta_{r,k+1}}).

Using similar arguments in the proofs of Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
,
we can obtain

\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})=\rho({\underline{M}}_{k}^{*}|_{{\Theta_{r,k+1}}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})=\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}}).

Hence

\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})=\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{r,k}})=\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}^{*}|_{{\Theta_{r,k+1}}})\leq\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k+1}|_{{\Theta_{r,k+1}}}).

As a result, the sequence
\{\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is increasing.

In the sequel of the proof, we assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Using similar arguments in the proof of Theorem
2.1
(2), we know that there exists
k_{1}\in{{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}}
such that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Define

{\underline{s}}_{r}=\min\{|S_{n}(x)|:\,x\in D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}},n\in\Lambda_{r}\}.

Then
{\underline{s}}_{r}>0
.
Notice that
S_{n}
is uniformly continuous on
D_{n}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\sup\big\{|S_{n}(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})|:\,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in D_{r,i}^{k}\big\}\leq{\underline{s}}_{r}\varepsilon

for all
k>k_{2}
and all
1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}
.

Assume that
k>k_{2}
and
i,j\in\Theta_{r,k}
.
In the case that
I_{r,j}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}
,
let
n
be the unique element in
\Lambda_{r}
such that
I_{r,i}^{k}\subset I_{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle 0\leq{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}-{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}=

\displaystyle\sup\big\{|S_{n}(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})|:\,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in I_{r,j}^{k}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\big\{|S_{n}(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})|:\,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in D_{r,i}^{k}\big\}\leq{\underline{s}}_{r}\varepsilon\leq{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}\varepsilon.

In the case that
I_{r,j}^{k}\not\subset D_{r,i}^{k}
, we have
{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}={\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}=0
.
Hence, for all
k>k_{2}
and
i,j\in\Theta_{r,k}
,

\displaystyle{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}\leq{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}\leq(1+\varepsilon){\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}.

From the definitions of
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
,

{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}\leq{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}\leq(1+\varepsilon){\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}.

Thus

\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\leq\rho({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})
.
By letting
k
tend to infinity,

\frac{{{\rho}}_{r}}{1+\varepsilon}\leq\varliminf_{k\to\infty}\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\leq\varlimsup_{k\to\infty}\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})\leq{{\rho}}_{r}.

From the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})={{\rho}}_{r}
.
∎

4.
Some lemmas for box dimension estimate

4.1.
The relationship between box dimension and oscillation sum

For any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we call
[k_{1}\varepsilon,(k_{1}+1)\varepsilon]\times[k_{2}\varepsilon,(k_{2}+1)\varepsilon]
an
\varepsilon
-coordinate square in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
E
be a bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(E)
the least number of
\varepsilon
-coordinate squares that cover
E
.
We define

(4.1)

\overline{\dim}_{B}E=\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(E)}{\log 1/\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\dim}_{B}E=\varliminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(E)}{\log 1/\varepsilon},

and call them the
upper box dimension
and the
lower box dimension
of
E
, respectively. If
\overline{\dim}_{B}E=\underline{\dim}_{B}E
, then we use
\dim_{B}E
to denote the common value and call it the
box dimension
of
E
.
It is well known that in (
4.1
), it is enough to consider limits as
\varepsilon
tends to
0
through any decreasing sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
such that
\varepsilon_{k+1}\geq c\varepsilon_{k}
for some constant
0<c<1
, in particular for
\varepsilon_{k}=\alpha c^{k}
, where
\alpha>0
is a constant.
It is well known that
\underline{\dim}_{B}E\geq 1
when
E
is the graph of a continuous function on a closed interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Please see
[
13
]
for details.

Let
g
be a continuous function on
J=[a,b]
. For each
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
1\leq r\leq m
,
we define

O_{r,p}(g,J)=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{{T_{r}}^{p}}O\Big(g,\Big[a+\frac{\ell-1}{{T_{r}}^{p}}(b-a),a+\frac{\ell}{{T_{r}}^{p}}(b-a)\Big]\Big),

where we use
O(g,U)
to denote the
oscillation
of
g
on
U\subset J
, that is,

O(g,U)=\sup\limits_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in U}|g({x}^{\prime})-g({x}^{\prime\prime})|.

We call
O_{r,p}(g,J)
the
oscillation sum
of
g
on
J
with level
p
and associated with
r
.
It is clear that
\{O_{r,p}(g,J)\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
is increasing with respect to
p
. Thus
\lim_{p\to\infty}O_{r,p}(g,J)
always exists.
Recall that
\mathrm{Var}(g,J)
is the classical total variation of
g
on
J
. We have the following fact.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
19
]
, Lemma 4.2)
.

Let
g
be a continuous function on a closed interval
J
. Then for any
1\leq r\leq m
,
\lim_{p\to\infty}O_{r,p}(g,J)=\mathrm{Var}(g,J)
.

The following lemma presents a useful method to estimate the upper and lower box dimensions of the graph of a continuous function by its oscillation sum. Similar results can be found in
[
13
,
18
,
19
,
23
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
g
is a continuous function on a closed interval
J
. Then for any
1\leq r\leq m
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g=1+\varliminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}},\quad\mbox{and}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g=1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}.

Proof.

Assume that
J=[a,b]
. For any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we call
[a+k_{1}\varepsilon,a+(k_{1}+1)\varepsilon]\times[k_{2}\varepsilon,(k_{2}+1)\varepsilon]
a generalized
\varepsilon
-coordinate square with center
(a,0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We use
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}^{(a,0)}(E)
to denote the least number of generalized
\varepsilon
-coordinate squares with center
(a,0)
to cover
E
. It is clear that in (
4.1
), we can replace
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(E)
by
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}^{(a,0)}(E)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{p}=|J|/{T_{r}}^{p}
for all
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.
It is straightforward to check that

\max\{{T_{r}}^{p},\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}O_{r,p}(g,J)\}\leq\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(a,0)}(\Gamma g)\leq\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}O_{r,p}(g,J)+2{T_{r}}^{p}.

Thus, by using
\max\{x,y\}\geq(x+y)/2
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(4.4)

\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+|J|\big)\leq\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(a,0)}(\Gamma g)\leq\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+2|J|\big).

As a result,

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}E

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(a,0)}(\Gamma g)}{-\log(\varepsilon_{p})}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+|J|\big)-\log 2}{p\log T_{r}-\log|J|}=1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}E

\displaystyle\leq 1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+2|J|\big)}{p\log T_{r}-\log|J|}=1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}.

Combining these two inequalities, (
4.3
) holds.

Using similar arguments, we know that (
4.2
) holds.
∎

The following result is a direct corollary of the above lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
g
is a continuous function on a closed interval
J
.
Then for any
1\leq r\leq m
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(4.5)

c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}{T_{r}}^{p\big(\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1-\varepsilon\big)}\leq O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\leq c_{\varepsilon}{T_{r}}^{p\big(\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1+\varepsilon\big)},\quad p\geq 1.

Proof.

For any
1\leq r\leq m
and
\varepsilon>0
, from Lemma
4.2
, there exists
P\in{{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}}
such that for all
p>P
,

\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1-\varepsilon\leq\frac{\log\big(O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1+\varepsilon,

and therefore

(4.6)

{T_{r}}^{p\big(\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1-\varepsilon\big)}\leq O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\leq{T_{r}}^{p\big(\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1+\varepsilon\big)},\quad p>P.

Choose
c_{\epsilon}>1
large enough, such that for
p=1,2,\ldots,P
,

c_{\epsilon}^{-1}{T_{r}}^{p\big(\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1-\varepsilon\big)}\leq O_{r,p}(g,J)+1\leq c_{\epsilon}{T_{r}}^{p\big(\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma g-1+\varepsilon\big)}.

Combining this with (
4.6
), we know that (
4.5
) holds.
∎

4.2.
A crucial lemma for box dimension estimate

By the definition of
W_{n}
, it is easy to see that
W_{n}(x,y)=(L_{n}(x),F_{n}(x,y))
, where

F_{n}(x,y)=S_{n}(x)y+q_{n}(x)
.
From (
1.1
),

W_{n}(x,f(x))=(L_{n}(x),f(L_{n}(x))),\quad x\in D_{n}.

Thus, we have the following useful equality:

(4.7)

f(L_{n}(x))=S_{n}(x)f(x)+q_{n}(x),\quad x\in D_{n},\;n=1,2,\ldots,N.

Write
M_{f,J}=\sup_{x\in J}|f(x)|
for any
J\subset[x_{0},x_{N}]
.
By using similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma 4.4 in
[
19
]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
1\leq n\leq N
,
J\subset D_{n}
and
x\in J
,

(4.8)

\Big|O(f,L_{n}(J))-|S_{n}(x)|\cdot O(f,J)\Big|\leq 2M_{f,J}O(S_{n},J)+O(q_{n},J).

Proof.

From (
4.7
),

O(f,L_{n}(J))=\sup_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in J}\big|S_{n}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})f(x^{\prime\prime})+q_{n}(x^{\prime})-q_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})\big|.

Thus

(4.9)

-O(q_{n},J)\leq O(f,L_{n}(J))-\sup_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in J}\big|S_{n}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})f(x^{\prime\prime})\big|\leq O(q_{n},J).

For any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in J
, we have

\big|S_{n}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})f(x^{\prime\prime})-S_{n}(x)\big(f(x^{\prime})-f(x^{\prime\prime})\big)\big|\leq 2M_{f,J}O(S_{n},J).

Thus

\sup_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in J}\big|S_{n}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})f(x^{\prime\prime})|\geq|S_{n}(x)|O(f,J)-2M_{f,J}O(S_{n},J)

and

\sup_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in J}\big|S_{n}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})-S_{n}(x^{\prime\prime})f(x^{\prime\prime})|\leq|S_{n}(x)|O(f,J)+2M_{f,J}O(S_{n},J).

Combining these two inequalities with (
4.9
), we can easily see that (
4.8
) holds.
∎

The following lemma plays a crucial role to estimate the box dimension of
\Gamma f
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
1\leq n\leq N
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Assume that
J\subset D_{n}
is a closed interval. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{J}\big).

(2)

If
S_{n}
is positive on
J
, then

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{J}\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)=\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{J}\big).

Proof.

Write
{\overline{s}}=\max\big\{\big|S_{n}(x)\big|:x\in J\big\}
and

{\underline{s}}=\min\big\{\big|S_{n}(x)\big|:x\in J\big\}
.

From Lemma
4.4
, for any closed interval
E\subset J
, we have

\displaystyle O(f,L_{n}(E))

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in E}|S_{n}(x)|\cdot O(f,E)+2M_{f,E}O(S_{n},E)+O(q_{n},E)

\displaystyle\leq{\overline{s}}\cdot O(f,E)+2M_{f,J}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},E)+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},E).

and

\displaystyle O(f,L_{n}(E))

\displaystyle\geq\min_{x\in E}|S_{n}(x)|\cdot O(f,E)-2M_{f,E}O(S_{n},E)-O(q_{n},E)

\displaystyle\geq{\underline{s}}\cdot O(f,E)-2M_{f,J}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},E)-\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},E).

By these two inequalities, it is straightforward to see that

(4.10)

{\underline{s}}\cdot O_{r,p}(f,J)-\xi\leq O_{r,p}(f,L_{n}(J))\leq{\overline{s}}\cdot O_{r,p}(f,J)+\xi,\quad p\geq 1,

where
\xi=2M_{f,J}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},J)+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},J)<\infty
.

Write
{\overline{d}}=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{J}\big)
and
{\underline{d}}=\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{J}\big)
.
From Lemma
4.3
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

(4.11)

c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}{T_{r}}^{p({\underline{d}}-1-\varepsilon)}\leq O_{r,p}(f,J)+1\leq c_{\varepsilon}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{d}}-1+\varepsilon)}.

Thus, from Lemma
4.2
and
{\overline{d}}-1+\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big({\overline{s}}O_{r,p}(f,J)+\xi+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big({\overline{s}}c_{\varepsilon}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{d}}-1+\varepsilon)}+\xi+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}={\overline{d}}+\varepsilon.

From the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we have

(4.12)

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)\leq{\overline{d}}=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{J}\big).

Hence, the first statement of this lemma holds.

From now on, we assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
J
. Then
{\overline{s}}\geq{\underline{s}}>0
. In the case that
{\underline{d}}=1
, it is obvious that

(4.13)

\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)\geq{\underline{d}}.

In the case that
{\underline{d}}>1
. For any
0<\varepsilon<{\underline{d}}-1
, we choose a positive constant
c_{\varepsilon}
such that (
4.11
) holds for all
p\in{{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}}
. It is easy to see that for sufficiently large
p
,

{\underline{s}}c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}{T_{r}}^{p({\underline{d}}-1-\varepsilon)}-\xi+1>0.

Thus, from Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{B}(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)})

\displaystyle\geq 1+\varliminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big({\underline{s}}\cdot O_{r,p}(f,J)-\xi+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\varliminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big({\underline{s}}c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}{T_{r}}^{p({\underline{d}}-1-\varepsilon)}-\xi+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}={\underline{d}}-\varepsilon.

From the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, the inequality (
4.13
) still holds. Therefore, this equality holds for all
{\underline{d}}\geq 1
. Combining this with (
4.12
), we have

(4.14)

{\underline{d}}\leq\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)\leq{\overline{d}}.

From (
4.10
), we have

{\overline{s}}^{-1}\big(O_{r,p}(f,L_{n}(J))-\xi\big)\leq O_{r,p}(f,J)\leq{\underline{s}}^{-1}\big(O_{r,p}(f,L_{n}(J))+\xi\big).

Using similar arguments in the proof of (
4.14
), we can obtain

\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big)\leq{\underline{d}}\leq{\overline{d}}\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{L_{n}(J)}\big).

Combining this with (
4.14
), the second statement of this lemma holds.
∎

By replacing
J
with
D_{r,i}^{k}
in the above lemma, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
,
k\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{r,i}^{k}}\big)
.

(2)

Let
n
be the unique element in
\{1,\ldots,N\}
satisfying
I_{r,i}^{k}\subset I_{n}
.
Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{r,i}^{k}
. Then

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}\big)=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{r,i}^{k}}\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}\big)=\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{r,i}^{k}}\big).

4.3.
Estimate on upper box dimension of
\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}\setminus B_{r,k}}

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
. Recall that
{\mathcal{B}}_{r,0}=\{D_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{r,1}=\{I_{n}:\,n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
,
B_{r,0}=\cup{\mathcal{B}}_{r,0}
and
B_{r,1}=\cup{\mathcal{B}}_{r,1}
. In the case that
B_{r,0}=B_{r,1}
, we define
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}=1
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, otherwise, we define

\displaystyle{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}

\displaystyle=\max\big\{\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big):I_{i}\subset B_{r,0}\textrm{ and }I_{i}\not\subset B_{r,1}\big\},\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}

\displaystyle=\max\big\{\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}\big):I_{r,i}^{k}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}\big\},\quad k\geq 2.

From Corollary
4.6
, we can obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that
1\leq r\leq m
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

We have
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}
for all
k\geq 2
. That is,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}\setminus B_{r,k}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}.

(2)

If
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Then
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}={\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}
for all
k\geq 2
. We denote the common value by
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
B_{r,0}\not=B_{r,1}
.

First, we prove (1). Fix
k\geq 1
. For any
I_{r,j}^{k+1}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k+1}
, let
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
be the unique element satisfying
I_{r,j}^{k+1}\subset I_{n}
.
In the case that
D_{r,j}^{k+1}\not\subset B_{r,1}
, there exists
t\notin\Lambda_{r}
such that
D_{r,j}^{k+1}\subset I_{t}
. From Corollary
4.6
, we have

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,j}^{k+1}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{r,j}^{k+1}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{t}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}.

In the case that
D_{r,j}^{k+1}\subset B_{r,1}
, by using (
3.1
), it is easy to see that there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k-1}\}
such that
I_{r,i}^{k}=D_{r,j}^{k+1}
. From
L_{n}(I_{r,i}^{k})=I_{r,j}^{k+1}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k+1}
, we have
I_{r,i}^{k}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
. Combining this with Corollary
4.6
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,j}^{k+1}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{r,j}^{k+1}}\big)=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}.

From the arbitrariness of
j
, we have
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k+1}\leq\max\{{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\}
.
By the arbitrariness of
k
, we have
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}
for all
k\geq 2
.

Now we prove (2). Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. From (1), it suffices to show that
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k+1}
for all
k\geq 1
. In the case that
k\geq 2
, we have by definition that

{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}\setminus B_{r,k}}\big).

Combining this with the fact that
B_{r,k+1}\subset B_{r,k}
, we obtain that
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k}\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k+1}
for all
k\geq 2
.

In the case that
k=1
, for any
i
with
I_{i}\subset B_{r,0}
and
I_{i}\not\subset B_{r,1}
, we have
i\not\in\Lambda_{r}
and there exists
n\in\Lambda_{r}
satisfying
I_{i}\subset D_{n}
. Let
j
be the unique element in
\{1,\ldots,d_{r}T_{r}\}
such that
I_{r,j}^{2}=L_{n}(I_{i})
. Then
I_{i}=D_{r,j}^{2}
. From
i\not\in\Lambda_{r}
, we have
I_{i}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,1}
so that
I_{r,j}^{2}=L_{n}(I_{i})\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,2}
.
Combining this with Corollary
4.6
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{r,j}^{2}}\big)=\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,j}^{2}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,2}.

From the arbitrariness of
I_{i}
,
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,2}
.
∎

5.
Box dimension of generalized affine RFIFs

5.1.
Oscillation sum of generalized affine RFIFs

Recall that
M_{f,J}=\sup_{x\in J}|f(x)|
for any
J\subset[x_{0},x_{N}]
.
By using
{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}
and
{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}
, we can obtain the following inequalities on the oscillation sum of
f
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
. Assume that
1\leq n\leq N
is the unique element satisfying
I_{r,i}^{k}\subset I_{n}
.
Then for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k+1}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1})\leq\sum_{j\in\Theta_{r,k}}{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})+\xi_{r,k,i},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k+1}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1})\geq\sum_{j\in\Theta_{r,k}}{\underline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})-\xi_{r,k,i},

where
\xi_{r,k,i}=2M_{f,B_{r,k}}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})
.

Proof.

For each
i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k+1}
satisfying
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}
, there exists
j\in\Theta_{r,k}
such that
I_{r,j}^{k}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}
.
Notice that
I_{r,j}^{k}=D_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}
. Thus, by letting
J=I_{r,j}^{k}
in (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle O_{r,p}(f,L_{n}(I_{r,j}^{k}))\leq{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})+2M_{f,B_{r,k}}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},I_{r,j}^{k})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},I_{r,j}^{k}),

where we use the fact that
M_{f,I_{r,j}^{k}}\leq M_{f,B_{r,k}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k+1}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Theta_{r,k}:\,I_{r,j}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}}O_{r,p}(f,L_{n}(I_{r,j}^{k}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Theta_{r,k}:\,I_{r,j}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}}{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})+2M_{f,B_{r,k}}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Theta_{r,k}}{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})+2M_{f,B_{r,k}}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},D_{r,i}^{k}).

Thus, (
5.1
) holds. Using similar arguments, (
5.2
) holds.
∎

Now we define
V(f,r,k,p)
to be the transpose of the vector
\big(O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})\big)_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}
, i.e.,

\big(V(f,r,k,p)\big)_{i}=O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i}^{k}\big),\quad i\in\Theta_{r,k}.

We also define
\xi_{r,k}
to be the transpose of the vector
\big(\xi_{r,k,i}\big)_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}
. It is obvious that

(5.3)

\lVert\xi_{r,k}\rVert_{1}=\sum_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}\xi_{r,k,i}\leq\sum_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}\Big(2M_{f,B_{r,k}}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},D_{n})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},D_{n})\Big),

where
\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\rVert_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{q}|v_{i}|
for any
{\mathbf{v}}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{q})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
.

Similarly, we define

O_{r,p}^{*}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\sum_{i^{\prime}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\setminus(B_{r,k+1})^{\circ}}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}),\quad i\in\Theta_{r,k},

and let
\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)
be the transposition of the vector
\big(O_{r,p}^{*}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})\big)_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}
.

From Lemma
5.1
, the following result holds.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
. For any
k,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle V(f,r,k,p+1)\geq

\displaystyle\big({\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}\big)V(f,r,k,p)+\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)-\xi_{r,k},

(5.5)

\displaystyle V(f,r,k,p+1)\leq

\displaystyle\big({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}\big)V(f,r,k,p)+\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)+\xi_{r,k}.

Proof.

It is clear that for any
1\leq r\leq m
,
k,p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and any
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
,

O_{r,p+1}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\sum_{i^{\prime}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}).

Notice that for any
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}
such that
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}
, we have either
i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k+1}
or
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\setminus(B_{r,k+1})^{\circ}
. Thus, from Lemma
5.1
,

O_{r,p+1}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})\leq\sum_{j\in\Theta_{r,k}}{\overline{s}}_{r,i,j}^{k}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})+\xi_{r,k,i}+O_{r,p}^{*}(f,I_{r,i}^{k}),

which implies that (
5.5
) holds. Similarly, we know that (
5.4
) holds.
∎

5.2.
Upper box dimension of
\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}

Using the finite stability of box dimension, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
. For any
k,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)

\displaystyle=1+\varliminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(\lVert V(f,r,k,p)\rVert_{1}+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)

\displaystyle=1+\varlimsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(\lVert V(f,r,k,p)\rVert_{1}+1\big)}{p\log T_{r}}.

Proof.

Fix
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. For each
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
, by letting
J=I_{r,i}^{k}
,
g=f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}
and
a=x_{0}
in (
4.4
), we can obtain

\max\big\{|I_{r,i}^{k}|,O_{r,p}(f,{I_{r,i}^{k}})\big\}\leq\varepsilon_{p}\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(x_{0},0)}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}\big)\leq O_{r,p}(f,{I_{r,i}^{k}})+2|{I_{r,i}^{k}}|,

where
\varepsilon_{p}=|I_{r,i}^{k}|/{T_{r}}^{p}=(x_{N}-x_{0})N^{-1}{T_{r}}^{-k-p+1}
.
Notice that

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(x_{0},0)}(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}})=\sum_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(x_{0},0)}(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i}^{k}}).

Thus, from
\lVert V(f,r,k,p)\rVert_{1}=\sum_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}O_{r,p}(f,{I_{r,i}^{k}})
,

\max\big\{\beta_{r,k},\lVert V(f,r,k,p)\rVert_{1}\big\}\leq\varepsilon_{p}\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{p}}^{(x_{0},0)}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)\leq\lVert V(f,r,k,p)\rVert_{1}+2\beta_{r,k},

where
\beta_{r,k}=(x_{N}-x_{0})N^{-1}{T_{r}}^{-k+1}{\rm card\,}(\Theta_{r,k})
.
Using similar arguments in Lemma
4.2
,
we know that (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) hold.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
.
Then for any
k\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon,r,k}>0
such that for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

V(f,r,k,p+1)\leq({\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}})V(f,r,k,p)+c_{\varepsilon,r,k}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}{\mathbf{e}}_{r,k},

where
{\mathbf{e}}_{r,k}=(1,\ldots,1)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{{\rm card\,}(\Theta_{r,k})}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
5.2
, it suffices to show that for any
k\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
c_{\varepsilon,k}>0
such that for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

(5.8)

\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)+\xi_{r,k}\leq c_{\varepsilon,k}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}{\mathbf{e}}_{r,k}.

Recall that for each
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
,

\big(\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)\big)_{i}=O_{r,p}^{*}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\sum_{i^{\prime}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\setminus(B_{r,k+1})^{\circ}}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}).

From Lemma
4.7
,
for each
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq d_{r}{T_{r}}^{k}
with
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}
and
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\not\in{\mathcal{B}}_{k+1}
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,k+1}\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.3
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}(r,k,i,i^{\prime})>0
such that

O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1})\leq c_{\varepsilon}(r,k,i,i^{\prime})\cdot{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Notice that

{\rm card\,}\big(\{i^{\prime}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\setminus(B_{r,k+1})^{\circ}\}\big)\leq T_{r}.

Thus, we define

c_{\varepsilon,r,k}^{\prime}=T_{r}\max_{i\in\Theta_{r,k}}\Big(\max_{i^{\prime}:\,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}\setminus(B_{r,k+1})^{\circ}}c_{\varepsilon}(r,k,i,i^{\prime})\Big).

Then
c_{\varepsilon,r,k}^{\prime}>0
and for all
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
and
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

(5.9)

\big(\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)\big)_{i}\leq c_{\varepsilon,r,k}^{\prime}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}.

By (
5.3
), for each
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
,

(\xi_{r,k})_{i}\leq\lVert\xi_{r,k}\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}\Big(2M_{f,B_{r,k}}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},D_{n})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},D_{n})\Big)<\infty.

Combining this with
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1\geq 0
, we have
(\xi_{r,k})_{i}\leq\lVert\xi_{r,k}\rVert_{1}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}
. By letting
c_{\varepsilon,r,k}=c_{\varepsilon,r,k}^{\prime}+\lVert\xi_{r,k}\rVert_{1}
, we know from this inequality and (
5.9
) that for all
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
and
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

\big(\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)\big)_{i}+(\xi_{r,k})_{i}\leq c_{\varepsilon,r,k}{T_{r}}^{p({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)},

which implies that (
5.8
) holds.
∎

In order to estimate the upper box dimension of
\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}
, we will use similar method in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 4.6]
.
For every nonnegative
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
, we define

\|A\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}.

Let
{\mathbf{e}}=(1,\ldots,1)^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then it is clear that

(5.10)

A{\mathbf{e}}\leq\|A\|_{\infty}{\mathbf{e}}.

From
[
15
, Example 5.6.5]
,
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
is a matrix norm. Thus

(5.11)

\|A^{q}\|_{\infty}\leq(\|A\|_{\infty})^{q}.

Furthermore, from
[
15
, Corollary 5.6.14]
,

(5.12)

\lim_{p\to\infty}(\rVert A^{p}\|_{\infty})^{1/p}=\rho(A).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
. Then

(5.13)

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\Big\{1+\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{r}}{\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\Big\}.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
.
In this proof, for simplicity, we denote
{\overline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
and
V(f,r,k,p)
by
{\overline{M}}_{k}
and
V(k,p)
, respectively.
Write
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}=(1,\ldots,1)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{{\rm card\,}(\Theta_{r,k})}
.

For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and
\varepsilon>0
, from Lemma
5.4
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon,k}>0
such that

V(k,t+1)\leq{\overline{M}}_{k}V(k,t)+c_{\varepsilon,k}{T_{r}}^{t({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}{\mathbf{e}}_{k},\quad t\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}.

Hence, for all
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

(5.14)

V(k,(q+1)p)\leq\big({\overline{M}}_{k}\big)^{p}V(k,qp)+c_{\varepsilon,k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\big({\overline{M}}_{k}\big)^{p-1-\ell}{T_{r}}^{(qp+\ell)({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

Fix
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.
Choose
\alpha_{k,p}>0
big enough such that

V(k,p)\leq\alpha_{k,p}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
and

c_{\varepsilon,k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\big({\overline{M}}_{k}\big)^{p-1-\ell}{T_{r}}^{\ell({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\leq\alpha_{k,p}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

Let
\beta={T_{r}}^{({\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon)}
.
Then
\beta\geq{T_{r}}^{\varepsilon}>1
. From (
5.14
),

V(k,(q+1)p)\leq\big({\overline{M}}_{k}\big)^{p}V(k,qp)+{\beta}^{pq}\alpha_{k,p}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

By induction and using
V(k,p)\leq\alpha_{k,p}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
,

V(k,(q+1)p)\leq\alpha_{k,p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}{\beta}^{p(q-\ell)}\big({\overline{M}}_{k}\big)^{p\ell}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

Notice that from (
5.10
) and (
5.11
),

({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p\ell}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\leq\|({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p\ell}\|_{\infty}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\leq\big(\|({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\|_{\infty}\big)^{\ell}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

Combining this with the fact that for all
a>1
,
b\geq 0
and
0\leq\ell\leq q
,

a^{q-\ell}b^{\ell}\leq\max\{a^{q},b^{q}\}\leq a^{q+1}+b^{q+1},

we have

V(k,(q+1)p)\leq(q+1)\alpha_{k,p}\Big(\big(\lVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\rVert_{\infty}\big)^{q+1}+{\beta}^{p(q+1)}\Big){\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

Thus,

\lVert V(k,qp)\rVert_{1}\leq q\alpha_{k,p}\big(\big(\lVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\rVert_{\infty}\big)^{q}+{\beta}^{pq}\big)\lVert{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\rVert_{1}

for all
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{q\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(\lVert V(k,pq)\rVert_{1}+1\big)}{pq\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{q\to\infty}\frac{\log\Big(q\alpha_{k,p}\Big(\big(\lVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\rVert_{\infty}\big)^{q}+{\beta}^{pq}\Big)\lVert{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\rVert_{1}+1\Big)}{pq\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\log\big(\max\big\{\lVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\rVert_{\infty},\beta^{p}\big\}\big)}{p\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\frac{\log\big(\lVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\rVert_{\infty}\big)}{p\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}-1+\varepsilon\Big\}.

Thus, from Lemma
5.3
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\varlimsup_{q\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(\lVert V(k,pq)\rVert_{1}+1\big)}{pq\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{1+\frac{\log\big(\lVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\rVert_{\infty}\big)}{p\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}+\varepsilon\Big\}.

Notice that
\lim_{p\to\infty}(\rVert({\overline{M}}_{k})^{p}\|_{\infty})^{1/p}=\rho({\overline{M}}_{k})
(see (
5.12
)). Thus, from the arbitrariness of
p
and
\varepsilon
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)\leq\max\Big\{1+\frac{\log\rho({\overline{M}}_{k})}{\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\Big\}.

From Lemma
4.7
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}\setminus B_{r,k}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}

for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Thus,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\Big\{1+\frac{\log\rho({\overline{M}}_{k})}{\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\Big\},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}.

By letting
k
tend to infinity, we know from Theorem
2.2
that (
5.13
) holds.
∎

5.3.
Lower box dimension of
\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
1\leq r\leq m
,
k_{1}\geq 1
and
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
.
If there exists a closed interval
J\subset B_{r,k_{1}}
such that
\mathrm{Var}(f,J)=\infty
,
then
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\infty
for all
k\geq 1
and all
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
.

Proof.

Define
{\underline{s}}^{*}_{r}=\min\{|S_{n}(x)|:\,x\in D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}},n\in\Lambda_{r}\}
.
Since
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, we have
{\underline{s}}^{*}_{r}>0
.

First we claim that
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\infty
for all
k>k_{1}
and all
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
.
We prove this by contradiction. Assume that there exist
k>k_{1}
and
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
such that
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})<\infty
. Notice that
D_{r,i}^{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{r,k-1}
. Thus

(5.15)

\min\{|S_{n}(x)|:\,x\in D_{r,i}^{k}\}\geq s_{r}^{*}.

Let
n\in\Lambda_{r}
satisfy
I_{r,i}^{k}\subset I_{n}
. From (
4.10
) and (
5.15
),

O_{r,p}(f,D_{r,i}^{k})\leq({\underline{s}}_{r}^{*})^{-1}\big(O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})+\xi_{r,k,i}\big),

where
\xi_{r,k,i}\leq 2M_{f,[x_{0},x_{N}]}\mathrm{Var}(S_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})+\mathrm{Var}(q_{n},D_{r,i}^{k})<\infty
. Hence, by letting
p
tend to infinity, we know from
Lemma
4.1
that

\mathrm{Var}(f,D_{r,i}^{k})\leq({\underline{s}}_{r}^{*})^{-1}\big(\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})+\xi_{r,k,i}\big)<\infty.

As a result, for all
\ell\in\Theta_{r,k}
with
I_{r,\ell}^{k}\subset D_{r,i}^{k}
, we have

\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,\ell}^{k})\leq\mathrm{Var}(f,D_{r,i}^{k})<\infty.

From Lemma
3.3
, for any
j\in\Theta_{r,k}
, there exists a finite sequence
\{i_{t}\}_{t=0}^{p}
in
\Lambda_{r}
, such that
i_{0}=j
,
i_{p}=i
, and

I_{r,i_{t-1}}^{k}\subset D_{r,i_{t}}^{k}
for all
1\leq t\leq p
.
It follows from
i_{p}=i
that
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i_{p}}^{k})<\infty
, which gives
\mathrm{Var}(f,D_{r,i_{p}}^{k})<\infty
. Combining this with
I_{r,i_{p-1}}^{k}\subset D_{r,i_{p}}^{k}
, we have
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i_{p-1}}^{k})<\infty
so that
\mathrm{Var}(f,D_{r,i_{p-1}}^{k})<\infty
. Continuing this process, we can obtain
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i_{t}}^{k})<\infty
and
\mathrm{Var}(f,D_{r,i_{t}}^{k})<\infty
for all
0\leq t\leq p
. Thus
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})<\infty
since
i_{0}=j
.

From the arbitrariness of
j
, we have
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,j}^{k})<\infty
and
\mathrm{Var}(f,D_{r,j}^{k})<\infty
for all
j\in\Theta_{r,k}
.
Notice that

\big\{D_{r,j}^{k}:\,I_{r,j}^{k}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k}\big\}={\mathcal{B}}_{r,k-1}.

Thus,
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,j}^{k-1})<\infty
for all
j\in\Theta_{r,k-1}
whenever
k>k_{1}
.
By induction,
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,j}^{k_{1}})<\infty
for all
j\in\Theta_{r,k_{1}}
. Combining this with
J\subset B_{r,k_{1}}=\bigcup_{j\in\Theta_{r,k_{1}}}I_{r,j}^{k_{1}}
, we have
\mathrm{Var}(f,J)<\infty
, which is a contradiction.

For any
1\leq k\leq k_{1}
and
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
, by Lemma
3.1
(4), there exists
i^{\prime}\in\Theta_{r,k_{1}+1}
such that
I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k+1}\subset I_{r,i}^{k}
. Thus, by the claim,
\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})\geq\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}})=+\infty
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
and
k_{1}\in{{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}}
. Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, and
\mathrm{Var}(f,E)=\infty
for some
E\in\mathcal{B}_{r,k_{1}}
. Then

(5.16)

\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\geq 1+\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{r}}{\log T_{r}}.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq r\leq m
.
In this proof, for simplicity, we denote
{\underline{M}}_{r,k}|_{\Theta_{r,k}}
,
V(f,r,k,p)
and
\xi_{{r,k}}
by
{\underline{M}}_{k}
,
V(k,p)
and
\xi_{k}
, respectively.
Notice that
\{B_{r,k}\}
is decreasing on
k
and
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{1}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Thus
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k}
for all
k\geq k_{1}
and all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Hence, from the proof of Theorem
2.1
(2),
{\underline{M}}_{k}
is irreducible for all
k>k_{1}
.

Now we prove (
5.16
). Without loss of generality, we may assume that
{{\rho}}_{r}>1
. From Theorem
2.2
, there exists a positive integer
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
, such that
\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})>1
for all
k>k_{2}
. Fix
k>k_{2}
.
From Theorem
5.2
, for all
p\geq 1
,

(5.17)

V(k,p+1)\geq{\underline{M}}_{k}V(k,p)+\widetilde{V}(f,r,k,p)-\xi_{k}\geq{\underline{M}}_{k}V(k,p)-\xi_{k}.

Given
1<\tau<\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})
,
by Perron-Frobenius Theorem, we can choose a positive eigenvector
{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
of
{\underline{M}}_{k}
such that
{\underline{M}}_{k}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=\rho({\underline{M}}_{k}){\mathbf{w}}_{k}
and

{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\geq\frac{1}{\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})-\tau}\xi_{k}.

From Lemma
5.6
, for all
i\in\Theta_{r,k}
, we have
\lim_{p\to\infty}O_{r,p}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\mathrm{Var}(f,I_{r,i}^{k})=\infty
.
Thus, there exists
p_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
V(k,p_{0})\geq{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
.

From (
5.17
),

V(k,p_{0}+1)\geq\rho({\underline{M}}_{k}){\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\xi_{k}\geq\rho({\underline{M}}_{k}){\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\big(\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})-\tau\big){\mathbf{w}}_{k}=\tau{\mathbf{w}}_{k}.

Notice that for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})\tau^{p}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})(\tau^{p}-1){\mathbf{w}}_{k}+\rho({\underline{M}}_{k}){\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\xi_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\tau(\tau^{p}-1){\mathbf{w}}_{k}+\tau{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=\tau^{p+1}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}.

Thus, by induction,
V(k,p_{0}+p)\geq\tau^{p}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Hence, from Lemma
5.3
,

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq 1+\varliminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(\lVert V(k,p_{0}+p)\rVert_{1}+1\big)}{(p_{0}+p)\log T_{r}}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\varliminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(\tau^{p}\lVert{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\rVert_{1}+1\big)}{(p_{0}+p)\log T_{r}}=1+\frac{\log\tau}{\log T_{r}}.

From the arbitrariness of
\tau
, we have

\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\geq\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k}}\big)\geq 1+\frac{\log\rho({\underline{M}}_{k})}{\log T_{r}}.

By letting
k
tend to infinity, we know from Theorem
2.2
that (
5.16
) holds.
∎

5.4.
Box dimension of RFIFs

For
1\leq i,j\leq N
, we denote by
i\sim j
if they belong to the same strongly connected component of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Otherwise,
i\not\sim j
.
Let

{\mathcal{A}}(i)=\{j:j\not\sim i\mbox{ and there is a path in $\{1,\dots,N\}$ from $j$ to $i$}\}.

Similarly as in
[
24
]
, we define the position
P(i)
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
recursively as follows:
P(i)=1
if
{\mathcal{A}}(i)=\emptyset
, otherwise
P(i)=1+\max\{P(j):\,j\in{\mathcal{A}}(i)\}
.
Clearly,
P(i)\leq N
for all
i
, and
P(i)=P(j)
if
i\sim j
.

Theorem 5.8
.

We have

\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma f\leq 1+\max\Big\{\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{1}}{\log T_{1}},\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{2}}{\log T_{2}},\ldots,\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{m}}{\log T_{m}},0\Big\}.

Proof.

Write
d^{*}=1+\max\Big\{\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{1}}{\log T_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{m}}{\log T_{m}},0\Big\}
.
Since
\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma f=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big),

it suffices to show that
\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq d^{*}
for all
1\leq i\leq N
. We prove this by induction on the position of
i
.

Assume that
P(i)=1
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}(i)=\emptyset
. From this fact, it is easy to see that

\big\{j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}:\,\mbox{there is a path in $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ from $j$ to $i$}\big\}

is a strongly connected component
\Lambda_{r}
of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
for some
1\leq r\leq m
and
i\in\Lambda_{r}
.
Furthermore, we have
\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{n}
. Thus
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}=1
. From Lemma
5.5
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\Big\{1+\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{r}}{\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\Big\}\leq d^{*}.

Fix
1\leq\ell<\max\{P(i):\,1\leq i\leq N\}
. Assume that
\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{j}}\big)\leq d^{*}
for all
1\leq j\leq N
with
P(j)\leq\ell
. Let
1\leq i\leq N
such that
P(i)=\ell+1
.

Firstly, we assume that
i
is not a vertex of any strongly connected component.
Then

i^{\prime}\not\sim i
for all
i^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
.
For any
1\leq j\leq N
with
I_{j}\subset D_{i}
, it is clear that there exists a path in
\{1,\ldots,N\}
from
j
to
i
. Combining this with
j\not\sim i
, we have
j\in{\mathcal{A}}(i)
, which implies that

P(j)\leq\ell
. From Lemma
4.5
and inductive assumption,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{i}}\big)=\max\big\{\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{j}}\big):\,I_{j}\subset D_{i}\big\}\leq d^{*}.

Now we assume that
i\in\Lambda_{r}
for some
1\leq r\leq m
. From Lemma
5.5
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\Big\{1+\frac{\log{{\rho}}_{r}}{\log T_{r}},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\Big\}.

It follows from
P(i)\geq 2
that
\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}I_{n}\subsetneq\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda_{r}}D_{n}
, which gives

\displaystyle{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}=\max\Big\{\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{n}}\big):I_{n}\subset B_{r,0}\textrm{ and }I_{n}\not\subset B_{r,1}\Big\}.

Notice that
P(n)\leq\ell
for all
n
with
I_{n}\subset B_{r,0}
and
I_{n}\not\subset B_{r,1}
.
Hence, by inductive assumption,
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\leq d^{*}
. As a result,
\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq d^{*}
.
∎

In the case that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
, we can obtain better estimate on the upper and lower box dimensions of
\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}
than Lemmas
5.5
and
5.7
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
1\leq r\leq m
and
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Then

d_{r}^{*}\leq\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\{d_{r}^{*},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\},

where
d_{r}^{*}
is defined by (
2.4
).

Proof.

It is clear that
\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\geq 1
.
Let
k_{r}^{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
be the smallest element such that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap B_{r,k_{r}^{*}}
for all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
.

In the case that
\mathrm{Var}(f,E)=\infty
for some
E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k_{r}^{*}}
, we have by definition that
d_{r}^{*}=1+\log{{\rho}}_{r}/\log T_{r}
.
Thus, the lemma holds from Lemmas
5.5
and
5.7
.

In the case that
\mathrm{Var}(f,E)<\infty
for all
E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k_{r}^{*}}
, we have by definition that
d_{r}^{*}=1
. Thus
\underline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\geq 1=d_{r}^{*}
. On the other hand, by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
,
\dim_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{E}\big)=1
for all
E\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r,k_{r}^{*}}
so that
\dim_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,k_{r}^{*}}}\big)=1
.
From Lemma
4.7
, we have

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}\setminus B_{r,k}}\big)\leq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}

for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Thus,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\{1,{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

From Theorem
5.8
, it suffices to prove the second part of the theorem. Assume that
S_{n}
is positive on
D_{n}\cap K_{r}
for all
1\leq r\leq m
and all
n\in\Lambda_{r}
. Write
d_{0}^{*}=\max\{d_{1}^{*},d_{2}^{*},\ldots,d_{m}^{*},1\}.

It directly follow from Lemma
5.9
and
\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma f\geq 1
that

\underline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma f\geq d_{0}^{*}
.

By using similar arguments in Theorem
5.8
, we can show that
\overline{\dim}_{B}\Gamma f\leq d_{0}^{*}
. We sketch the proof for completeness.

In the case that
P(i)=1
, there exists
1\leq r\leq m
such that
i\in\Lambda_{r}
and
{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}=1
. From Lemma
5.9
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\{d_{r}^{*},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\}=\max\{d_{r}^{*},1\}\leq d_{0}^{*}.

Fix
1\leq\ell<\max\{P(i):\,1\leq i\leq N\}
. Assume that
\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{j}}\big)\leq d_{0}^{*}
for all
1\leq j\leq N
with
P(j)\leq\ell
. Let
1\leq i\leq N
such that
P(i)=\ell+1
.

Firstly, we assume that
i
is not a vertex of any strongly connected component.
Then from Lemma
4.5
and inductive assumption,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{D_{i}}\big)=\max\big\{\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{j}}\big):\,I_{j}\subset D_{i}\big\}\leq d_{0}^{*}.

Now we assume that
i\in\Lambda_{r}
for some
1\leq r\leq m
.
It follows from
P(i)\geq 2
and inductive assumption that

\displaystyle{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}=\max\Big\{\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{n}}\big):I_{n}\subset B_{r,0}\textrm{ and }I_{n}\not\subset B_{r,1}\Big\}\leq d_{0}^{*}.

Thus, from Lemma
5.9
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{I_{i}}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(\Gamma f|_{B_{r,1}}\big)\leq\max\{d_{r}^{*},{\overline{\lambda}}_{r,1}\}\leq d_{0}^{*}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

An example

In this section, we present an example to illustrate our result.

Let

,
(x_{5},y_{5})=(5/6,0)
and
(x_{n},y_{n})=(n/6,1)
for all other

. Set

(6.1)

D_{1}=D_{4}=[1/3,5/6],\quad D_{2}=D_{3}=[1/6,2/3],\quad D_{5}=D_{6}=[2/3,1].

For each
1\leq n\leq 6
, we define functions
L_{n}(x)
,
S_{n}(x)
and
q_{n}(x)
on
D_{n}
as follows.

\displaystyle L_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{3}x-\frac{1}{9},

\displaystyle S_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle q_{1}(x)=x+\frac{1}{6},

\displaystyle L_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{3}x+\frac{1}{9},

\displaystyle S_{2}(x)=-\frac{2}{5}x+\frac{49}{60},

\displaystyle q_{2}(x)=\frac{2}{5}x+\frac{11}{60},

\displaystyle L_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{3}x+\frac{5}{18},

\displaystyle S_{3}(x)=-\frac{1}{5}x+\frac{47}{60},

\displaystyle q_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{5}x+\frac{13}{60},

\displaystyle L_{4}(x)=\frac{1}{3}x+\frac{7}{18},

\displaystyle S_{4}(x)=\frac{3}{5}x+\frac{2}{5},

\displaystyle q_{4}(x)=\frac{6}{5}x,

\displaystyle L_{5}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{3},

\displaystyle S_{5}(x)=\frac{2}{5}x+\frac{13}{30},

\displaystyle q_{5}(x)=-\frac{17}{5}x+\frac{77}{30},

\displaystyle L_{6}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{4}{3},

Then for any

, we set

Let

be the unique function determined by the recurrent IFS

.

), there are two strongly connected

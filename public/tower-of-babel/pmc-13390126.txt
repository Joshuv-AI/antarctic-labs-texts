Immediate postoperative urine dipstick occult blood positivity: a simple and early indicator of high-risk acute kidney injury after on-pump cardiac surgery
Authors: Han Zhang, Jing Wang, Tianlong Wang, Jieru Zhang, Jiayu Zou, Zhiyuan Bo, Qiaoni Zhang, Gang Liu, Jian Wang, Yuan Teng, Shujie Yan, Bingyang Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWe evaluated whether immediate postoperative urine dipstick occult blood after on-pump cardiac surgery could serve as an early indicator of high-risk cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury (CSA-AKI).MethodsThis single-center retrospective study included 11,782 adult patients undergoing elective on-pump cardiac surgery (2018–2024), excluding those with preoperative chronic kidney disease (CKD, stage 3–5), positive preoperative urine occult blood, or postoperative microscopic hematuria. The primary outcome was any-stage CSA-AKI within 7 days after surgery. Multivariable logistic regression was used to evaluate the independent association between immediate postoperative urine occult blood and CSA-AKI. The predictive value was assessed by C-index, the net reclassification improvement index, and integrated discrimination improvement. The goodness-of-fit of models was evaluated through Akaike information criterion, Bayesian information criterion and likelihood ratio test.ResultsCSA-AKI occurred in 32.8% of patients, rising progressively across urine occult blood categories from 28.8% (negative group) to 37.9% (3 + group). After multivariable adjustment, 3 + urine occult blood was independently associated with higher odds of any-stage CSA-AKI (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 1.630; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.463–1.817; P < 0.001) and early CSA-AKI within 48 h (aOR, 1.692; 95% CI, 1.513–1.889; P < 0.001). Both 2 + and 3 + urine occult blood were independently associated with stage 2–3 CSA-AKI. Integrating urine occult blood into a clinical risk model that included established CSA-AKI predictors yielded a modest improvement in prognostic prediction and model fit.ConclusionImmediate postoperative urine dipstick occult blood positivity was independently associated with an increased risk of CSA-AKI. As a simple and easily available biomarker, it may serve as an early indicator to identify patients at high risk of CSA-AKI in this selected cohort of adult patients undergoing elective on-pump cardiac surgery. Because direct markers of hemolysis or urinary heme proteins were not measured, the underlying biological mechanisms remain uncertain and require prospective validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-026-05072-z.

Introduction

Cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury (CSA-AKI) is a frequent and serious postoperative complication, occurring in 20% to 40% of patients and leading to increased morbidity and mortality, prolonged hospital stays, and greater healthcare costs [1–5]. The implementation of evidence-based care bundles for patients at risk of AKI, which include optimized fluid management and hemodynamic stabilization, has been shown to reduce the incidence and severity of AKI and improve clinical outcomes [6]. Therefore, the early identification of patients at high risk for CSA-AKI is essential.

Multiple mechanisms contribute to CSA-AKI after cardiac surgery, including ischemia-reperfusion injury, inflammation, oxidative stress, and microcirculatory disturbances [5, 7, 8]. Urinary abnormalities are common after cardiac surgery and may include oliguria, proteinuria, and hematuria. Among these findings, oliguria is incorporated into the diagnostic criteria for AKI and is widely recognized as an important marker of renal dysfunction [9]. In clinical practice, urine dipstick testing is inexpensive, rapid, and easily accessible, and abnormal dipstick findings are increasingly recognized as markers associated with adverse renal outcomes, including progression to end-stage renal disease [10]. However, perioperative dipstick urinary abnormalities, particularly urine occult blood positivity independent of overt hematuria, remain insufficiently investigated in relation to postoperative AKI. We therefore hypothesized that immediate postoperative urine occult blood would be associated with the subsequent development and severity of CSA-AKI. To test this hypothesis, we assessed its relationship with CSA-AKI incidence and stage and evaluated its incremental prognostic value.

Method

Study design and patients

This single-center retrospective study used electronic medical record of our center. This study was reviewed and approved by Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital in Beijing (No. 2024–2379, approved in July 2024), and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design.

A total of 11,782 adult patients (age ≥ 18 years old) who underwent cardiac surgery with CPB between January 2018 and December 2024 in our center were included in the study. The exclusion criteria were as follows. These were applied sequentially, with each patient counted only once: (1) Patients without either preoperative or postoperative urine occult blood test; (2) Patients with positive preoperative urine occult blood; (3) Patients without postoperative urinary microscopic red blood cell (RBC) counts or with a postoperative urinary microscopic RBC count above the upper normal limit (≥ 7/ul); (4) Patients with preoperative chronic kidney disease (CKD, stages 3–5: eGFR < 60 ml/min/1.73m2 for at least 3 months before surgery), patients on dialysis, or those with preoperative mechanical assisted circulation devices (including intra-aortic balloon pump [IABP], extracorporeal membrane oxygenation [ECMO], and left ventricular assist device [LVAD]); (5) Patients undergoing emergency surgery; (6) Patients undergoing other types of surgery (including heart transplantation, re-sternotomy, LVAD implantation, and thoracoscopic surgery); (7) Patients with missing data for categorical variables and ≥ 10% missing continuous values. Urine occult blood testing

Immediate postoperative urine occult blood was defined as the first urine dipstick result obtained within 2 h upon arrival to the intensive care unit (ICU). Urine occult blood was measured using a fully automated dry-chemistry urine analyzer (UA-5600, Mindray, Shenzhen, China) in the emergency laboratory. The degree of urine occult blood was semi-quantitatively classified into four levels according to the dry chemistry method: negative, 1+ (positive for small amounts), 2+ (positive for moderate amounts), and 3+ (positive for large amounts). Urine samples were collected directly from the urinary catheter to minimize variability, ensuring sample consistency. The same sample was used for both the urine occult blood dipstick test and microscopic RBC analysis. All urine sample handling, including collection, transport, and analysis, was performed according to the standardized protocols established by the hospital’s clinical laboratory center, ensuring consistency and reproducibility of the results. Immediate postoperative urine dipstick testing was not a mandatory routine for all patients in our center, but was ordered at the discretion of the attending clinical team based on individual patient characteristics and clinical judgment.

Data collection and definitions

The study involved comprehensive patient data including demographics (age, sex, and body mass index [BMI]), EuroSCORE (European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation), NYHA (New York Heart Association) class, medical history, echocardiography data, laboratory tests, perioperative medication, procedural characteristics and postoperative data. Fluid balance represents the total volume of fluid intake minus the total fluid output, including urine output, blood loss and the amount of conventional ultrafiltration. Preoperative anemia was defined as hemoglobin value < 120 g/L in women and < 130 g/L in men. Postoperative first creatinine (Cr) was measured from a blood sample collected at the same time as the first urine sample.

Outcome

The primary outcome was CSA-AKI within seven days following operation, which was ascertained by Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria based on the perioperative Cr level [11]. CSA-AKI was classified into stage 1 (an increase in serum Cr (≥ 26.5 umol/L) within 48 h postoperatively or 1.5–1.99 fold over baseline within 7 days postoperatively), stage 2 (an increase in serum Cr 2.0-2.99 fold over baseline), or stage 3 (an increase in serum Cr ≥ 3.0 fold over baseline or Cr level > 353.6 umol/L or initiation of dialysis) according to the KDIGO criteria [11, 12]. The baseline serum Cr level was defined as the last available measurement within 48 h before surgery, and this value was available for all included patients. Postoperative urine output data were not consistently available, so serum Cr criteria were used exclusively for defining CSA-AKI.

The secondary outcomes included postoperative complications (CSA-AKI within 48 h, hepatic injury, prolonged mechanical ventilation, chest drainage > 1000 mL, repeat thoracotomy, IABP insertion, and ECMO insertion), ICU and hospital length of stay (LOS), and in-hospital mortality.

Statistical analysis

Patients were divided into two groups according to the occurrence of postoperative CSA-AKI within 7 days. Missing data in continuous variables were estimated using multiple imputation techniques with 5 data sets (Supplementary Table 2). Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD, whereas skewed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Comparisons of continuous variables among groups were assessed using the independent-samples Student’s t test or Mann–Whitney U test, depending on the normality of the distribution. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies (%) and analyzed for significance using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Multivariable logistic regression models were employed to assess the adjusted association between urine occult blood after on-pump cardiac surgery and the risk of CSA-AKI. Covariates for the multivariable model were based on clinical relevance and prior literature, with statistical significance in univariable analyses (p < 0.10) used as supportive guidance. The forward selection approach was performed as an exploratory secondary analysis to evaluate the robustness of the findings. The adjusted major covariates included age, sex, BMI, EuroSCORE, NYHA class, hypertension, diabetes, history of myocardial infarction, peripheral vascular disease, preoperative atrial fibrillation within 2 weeks, preoperative anemia, preoperative cerebrovascular diseases, preoperative left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), preoperative estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), myoglobin and serum creatinine (Cr), preoperative medication (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers [ACEI/ARB] and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists [MRA]), type of surgery, CPB time, operative time, intraoperative RBC transfusion, fluid balance, intraoperative medication use (norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, and furosemide), peak intraoperative lactate level, lowest mean arterial pressure (MAP) during CPB and use of conventional ultrafiltration. Variance inflation factor (VIF) was used to assess multicollinearity. All variables had a VIF of less than 10, indicating absence of collinearity. A multivariable logistic regression model was performed to identify risk factors associated with postoperative positive urine occult blood (2 + AND 3+). To ensure model stability and prevent overfitting, a minimum of 10 events per predictor was required for all analyses. A test for trend was conducted by including urine occult blood categories as an ordinal variable in the multivariable logistic regression model.

The discriminative capability of urine occult blood was determined through C-index, the net reclassification improvement (NRI) index, and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI). The goodness-of-fit of models was evaluated through Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC) and likelihood ratio (LR) test. Differences among the various urine occult blood grade groups and the laboratory tests were evaluated using Kruskal-Wallis H test with Bonferroni-corrected post hoc comparisons.

To avoid temporal overlap between urine occult blood (exposure) and CSA-AKI (outcome), we excluded patients diagnosed with CSA-AKI on the first postoperative creatinine test in a sensitivity analysis and re-examined their association in the remaining cohort where occult blood testing preceded AKI onset. We also included patients with microscopic hematuria who were excluded from the primary analysis and re-evaluated the association between postoperative urine occult blood and CSA-AKI. To account for potential confounding from urinary red blood cells, urinary RBC count was additionally included as a covariate in the multivariable model. All analyses were performed using R software version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and all reported two-sided p-values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline and procedural characteristics of the study population

The included patients had a mean age of 56.9 ± 12.2 years, and male patients accounted for 71.7% (n = 8449). According to postoperative outcomes, patients were categorized into two groups: the CSA-AKI group (N = 3865, 32.8%) and the no CSA-AKI group (N = 7917, 67.2%). The CSA-AKI group exhibited higher rates of preoperative comorbidities such as hypertension (57.9% vs. 43.9%, P < 0.001), diabetes (31.6% vs. 22.3%, P < 0.001), hyperlipidemia (55.1% vs. 43.3%, P < 0.001), peripheral vascular disease (5.8% vs. 4.0%, P < 0.001), atrial fibrillation within 2 weeks (12.7% vs. 9.3%, P < 0.001), cerebrovascular disease (12.8% vs. 9.3%, P < 0.001), unstable angina (23.9% vs. 19.1%, P < 0.001), previous myocardial infarction (11.5% vs. 9.4%, P < 0.001), and anemia (25.8% vs. 17.6%, P < 0.001).

Patients in the CSA-AKI group had a significantly longer duration of CPB time and aortic cross-clamp (ACC) time compared to those in the no CSA-AKI group [116 (90, 152) min vs. 106 (82, 137) min, P < 0.001; 85 (64, 113) min vs. 77 (57, 103) min, P < 0.001, respectively].

The median fluid balance was significantly higher in the no CSA-AKI group [571 (5, 1139) mL vs. 466 (− 150, 1057) mL, P < 0.001]. The baseline and procedural characteristics were shown in Table 1.

Postoperative laboratory tests and outcomes

Patients in the CSA-AKI group exhibited significantly higher postoperative levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), cystatin C, creatinine and myoglobin, and had significantly lower postoperative levels of eGFR and hemoglobin (all P < 0.001, Table 2). The postoperative first laboratory tests, peak and nadir values within the first 48 h after surgery, stratified by first postoperative urine occult blood category, were provided in Supplementary Table 3. Patients with 3 + urine occult blood exhibited higher peak values within the first postoperative 48 h for direct bilirubin (DBil), total bilirubin (TBil), AST, LDH, creatine kinase (CK), creatine kinase–myocardial band (CK-MB), creatinine, BUN and cystatin C, together with a lower nadir eGFR (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Postoperative laboratory tests and clinical outcomes

CSA-AKI, cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury; eGFR, estimated glomerular filtration rate; DBil, direct bilirubin; TBil, total bilirubin; ALT, alanine aminotransferase; AST, aspartate aminotransferase; LDH, lactate dehydrogenase; BUN, blood urea nitrogen; CK, creatine kinase; CK-MB, creatine kinase–myocardial band; CRRT, continuous renal replacement therapy; ECMO, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation; ICU, intensive care unit; LOS, length of stay

According to KDIGO criteria, 3865 (32.8%) patients developed CSA-AKI (7 days) after cardiac surgery. Of them, 3353 patients located in stage 1, and another 438 and 74 cases were stage 2 and stage 3, respectively. The rate of continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) was 0.1% (n = 11). The incidence of early CSA-AKI (48 h) was 29.6% (n = 3491). Of them, 3281 patients located in stage 1, and another 200 and 20 cases were stage 2 and stage 3, respectively. The incidence of AKI in each urine occult blood group was 28.8% (1,165/4,045) for negative group, 31.5% (764/2,423) for 1 + group, 31.9% (417/1,306) for 2 + group, and 37.9% (1,519/4,008) for 3 + group. The CSA-AKI group had a significantly higher incidence of hepatic injury (P = 0.003), prolonged mechanical ventilation (P < 0.001), chest drainage > 1000 mL (P = 0.002), repeat thoracotomy (P < 0.001), IABP insertion (P < 0.001), ECMO insertion (P < 0.001) and in-hospital mortality (P < 0.001, Table 2).

The association between postoperative urine occult blood and the risk of CSA-AKI

Using the negative urine occult blood group as the baseline reference, 3 + urine occult blood was significantly associated with an increased risk of any-stage CSA-AKI within 7 days after surgery (aOR, 1.630; 95% CI, 1.463–1.817; P < 0.001) and early any-stage CSA-AKI within 48 h after surgery (aOR, 1.692; 95% CI, 1.513–1.889; P < 0.001). Both 2 + and 3 + urine occult blood were independently associated with increased odds of stage 2–3 CSA-AKI within 7 days after surgery (aOR, 2.299; 95% CI, 1.762–2.993; P < 0.001, and aOR, 2.304; 95% CI, 1.655–3.202; P < 0.001 respectively) and early stage 2–3 CSA-AKI within 48 h after surgery (aOR, 2.419; 95% CI, 1.479–3.957; P < 0.001, and aOR, 2.345; 95% CI, 1.587–3.465; P < 0.001 respectively, Table 3). A dose-response relationship was observed between increasing urine occult blood categories and CSA-AKI, with consistent trends across both 48-hour and 7-day outcomes (all P for trend < 0.001, Supplementary Table 4).The potential risk factors for postoperative positive urine occult blood (2 + AND 3+) were assessed by multivariable logistic regression analysis (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Incremental predictive value of urine occult blood and assessment of model goodness-of-fit

According to the C-index, continuous NRI and IDI, adding urine occult blood to the base model improved prediction of CSA-AKI (Table 4). The NRI was 0.164 (0.124–0.203) for any-stage CSA-AKI (7 days), 0.439 (0.354–0.524) for stage 2–3 CSA-AKI (7 days), 0.166 (0.125–0.205) for any-stage CSA-AKI (48 h), and 0.416 (0.287–0.545) for stage 2–3 CSA-AKI (48 h). The addition of urine occult blood to the base model modestly improved model fit, demonstrated by the lower AIC and BIC values and a significant likelihood ratio test.

Sensitivity analysis

We excluded 411 patients diagnosed with CSA-AKI by the first postoperative creatinine test. In the remaining 11,371 patients with occult blood tested before CSA-AKI onset, the association between postoperative urine occult blood and CSA-AKI remained consistent with the primary analysis (Supplementary Table 5). In the sensitivity analysis including patients with postoperative microscopic hematuria (N = 5866), the association between postoperative urine occult blood and CSA-AKI remained consistent with the primary analysis (Supplementary Table 6).

Discussion

This large, retrospective observational study showed that immediate postoperative moderate to strong positive urine occult blood was independently associated with the development of CSA-AKI within 7 days after on-pump cardiac surgery, as well as with an increased risk of CSA-AKI within the first 48 h. Urine occult blood positivity improved risk discrimination beyond established clinical predictors, with modest increases in C-index, NRI, and IDI. These findings suggest that, as a simple, rapid, and easily accessible biomarker, immediate postoperative urine occult blood positivity holds potential value for identifying patients at high risk of CSA-AKI.

Several studies in other clinical settings have also linked urine occult blood to early risk of AKI [13–15]. In infants undergoing CPB, Strong et al. reported that postoperative urine dipstick blood (3+) showed predictive performance comparable to urine neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), with an approximately eight-fold higher AKI risk among patients with positive results [13]. A retrospective observational study including critically ill patients reported that higher degrees of hematuria (dipstick test) were progressively associated with increased risks of AKI [15]. Together with our findings, these results support the potential utility of urine occult blood as a simple and inexpensive marker for early risk stratification across different clinical contexts.

This study found that strong positive urine occult blood was accompanied by abnormalities in multiple systemic biomarkers, including higher renal function indicators (serum creatinine, BUN, and cystatin C), laboratory abnormalities potentially associated with perioperative tissue injury and physiological stress (DBil, TBil, and LDH), and myocardial injury markers (CK and CK-MB) within the first 48 h postoperatively. These associations suggest that urine occult blood positivity may be linked to a broader pattern of perioperative physiological disturbances. However, as direct measurements of hemolysis or urinary heme proteins were not available in this study, these relationships should be interpreted cautiously and do not establish a causal pathway. Future studies incorporating direct biomarkers and prospective designs are warranted to further clarify the underlying biological processes and validate the clinical utility of this parameter.

Many of the currently utilized urinary biomarkers were validated in the post-CPB setting, including urine NGAL, kidney injury molecule (KIM) -1, IL-18 and cystatin C. Concentrations of these renal injury biomarkers rise in plasma and urine within hours of kidney damage and tend to be more specific and more sensitive than traditional markers. However, their clinical application remains limited due to restricted availability, lack of reimbursement, and high costs; as a result, most of these biomarkers are still confined to research settings rather than routine clinical practice [16]. In contrast, urine occult blood, as a simple dipstick reaction, remains inexpensive and universally accessible, which makes it attractive for routine early risk stratification. These findings should also be interpreted in the context of the study population, which consisted of adult patients undergoing elective on-pump cardiac surgery with available perioperative urine occult blood testing and postoperative urinary microscopic red blood cell counts, and may not be generalizable to patients undergoing off-pump or emergency cardiac surgery, pediatric patients, patients with pre-existing stage 3–5 CKD, or centers with different cardiopulmonary bypass practices. Further prospective multicenter studies with standardized perioperative urine testing are needed to validate these findings and to evaluate their generalizability in broader cardiac surgical populations.

Positive urine occult blood highlights the need to optimize perioperative management [17]. Patients with positive urine occult blood and those at high risk may warrant closer monitoring and assessment. Identifying patients at high risk may help inform preoperative assessment, intraoperative management, and postoperative surveillance. In our study, female sex, previous cardiac surgery, hypertension, smoking, higher preoperative hemoglobin and BUN, lower eGFR, longer CPB duration, higher intraoperative peak lactate, conventional ultrafiltration, and radiofrequency catheter ablation were associated with postoperative 2 + or 3 + urine occult blood. Avoidance of nephrotoxic exposures and individualized perioperative management strategies may be considered in high-risk patients based on existing clinical guidelines [2]. However, whether specific targeted interventions can improve renal outcomes remains uncertain and should be evaluated in future prospective studies.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective and single-center design, and the fact that postoperative urine dipstick testing was not performed for all patients, may introduce inherent selection bias and unmeasured confounding factors, thereby limiting the external validity and generalizability of the findings to other settings or populations (including off-pump surgery, emergency surgery, pediatric patients, patients with stage 3–5 CKD, or centers with different CPB practices). Second, urine occult blood was assessed using a semi-quantitative dry-chemistry dipstick method on an automated analyzer, which avoids operator-dependent visual interpretation but remains susceptible to analytical limitations inherent to dipstick chemistry, including the influence of urine concentration, pH, and interfering substances. However, our study excluded patients with abnormal urinary RBC counts, reducing interference from hematuria, and the relatively large sample size helped to minimize random error and enhance the robustness of the findings. Third, plasma free hemoglobin or other direct biomarkers potentially related to perioperative hemolysis were not available in this study. Therefore, the biological mechanisms underlying the association between urine occult blood positivity and CSA-AKI could not be directly evaluated. Fourth, CSA-AKI was identified solely based on serum creatinine criteria because detailed postoperative urine output data were not consistently recorded; therefore, some cases of oliguria-defined AKI may have been missed, potentially underestimating AKI incidence and affecting risk estimation. Fifth, potential temporal overlap between urine occult blood testing and CSA-AKI diagnosis in the primary analysis could not be fully excluded. To mitigate this bias, we performed a sensitivity analysis excluding early AKI cases, and the results remained consistent with the primary findings.

Conclusion

Immediate postoperative urine dipstick occult blood positivity was independently associated with an increased risk of CSA-AKI and may serve as a simple early prognostic marker in selected adult patients undergoing elective on-pump cardiac surgery. Because direct markers of hemolysis or urinary heme proteins were not measured, the underlying biological mechanisms remain uncertain and require prospective validation.

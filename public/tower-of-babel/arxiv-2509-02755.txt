Intrinsic Bottleneck Distance for Merge Trees
David Beers, Gillian Grindstaff

Abstract
Merge trees are a topological descriptor of a filtered space that enriches the degree zero barcode with its merge structure. The space of merge trees comes equipped with an interleaving distance d I d_{I} , which prompts a naive question: is the interleaving distance between two merge trees equal to the bottleneck distance between their corresponding barcodes? As the map from merge trees to barcodes is not injective, the answer as posed is no, but (as conjectured in Gasparovic et al.) we prove that it is true for the intrinsic metrics d ^ I \widehat{d}_{I} and d ^ B \widehat{d}_{B} realized by infinitesimal path length in merge tree space. This result suggests that in some special cases the bottleneck distance (which can be computed quickly) can be substituted for the interleaving distance (in general, NP-hard).

Introduction

One central topic in applied topology is the multiscale study of shape. Beginning with a family of nested topological spaces

Crucially, when merging of path components occurs, the barcode marks the death of the later-born component, but retains no information about the component it has merged with. When this information is retained, the components of

[

The intrinsic interleaving geodesics, then, are locally isometric lifts of bottleneck geodesics, and induce the same length-space structure on

In Theorem

\widehat{d}_{B}=\widehat{d}_{I}.

This result extends the computational advantages of bottleneck distance from the space of barcodes to merge trees with known geodesics, and elucidates the relationship between the two geometries.

2.2
The bottleneck distance

In this section we define the bottleneck distance on merge trees. Given a merge tree
(T,f)
, we have a nested collection of topological spaces
\{f^{-1}(-\infty,t]\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. By applying degree zero homology
H_{0}
with coefficients in a field

\{H_{0}(f^{-1}(-\infty,t])\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

\mathbb{F}
. Whenever
s\leq t
there are linear maps
H_{0}(f^{-1}(-\infty,s])\to H_{0}(f^{-1}(-\infty,t])
induced by inclusion of spaces. As a result, we have an example of the following construction.

Definition 7
.

A

Two persistence modules

\phi_{t}\circ V_{s,t}=W_{s,t}\circ\phi_{s}

.

We define

to be the persistence module with
(V\oplus W)_{t}=V_{t}\oplus W_{t}
and
(V\oplus W)_{s,t}=V_{s,t}\oplus W_{s,t}

.

An

\phi
is the minimum of the following three values.

\displaystyle\sup_{I\in S}\max\big(|L(I)-L(\phi(I))|,|R(I)-R(\phi(I))|\big)

\displaystyle\sup_{I\in B-S}\frac{R(I)-L(I)}{2}

\displaystyle\sup_{I\in B^{\prime}-\mathrm{im}\,\phi}\frac{R(I)-L(I)}{2}.

In the above expressions any difference
a-b
is assumed to be infinite if either
a
or
b
is infinite, unless
a=b
, in which case we take
a-b=0
.

The
bottleneck distance
between barcodes
B
and
B^{\prime}
is defined as the infimum of the cost over all partial matchings between
B
and
B^{\prime}
.

It is well known that the bottleneck distance is an extended pseudometric, i.e. it satisfies all of the axioms of a metric, except that
d(B,B^{\prime})
may be infinite, and may be equal to zero even if
B\neq B^{\prime}
.

This formulation of the bottleneck distance is rather combinatorial, however a result of Bauer and Lesnick
[
BL14
]
(called
the Isometry Theorem
) shows that the bottleneck distance can be defined purely algebraically. Before we state this result, we need to define the notion of an interleaving on persistence modules.

Definition 13
.

Suppose
V
and
W
are two persistence modules. An
\varepsilon
-interleaving between
V
and
W
is a collection of linear maps
\phi_{t}:V_{t}\to W_{t+\varepsilon}
and
\psi_{t}:W_{t}\to V_{t+\varepsilon}
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\circ V_{s,t}=W_{s+\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon}\circ\phi_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad\psi_{s}\circ W_{s,t}=V_{s+\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon}\circ\psi_{t}

\displaystyle\psi_{t+\varepsilon}\circ\phi_{t}=V_{t,t+2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad\phi_{t+\varepsilon}\circ\psi_{t}=W_{t,t+2\varepsilon}

for all real numbers
s\leq t
.

If there exists an
\varepsilon
interleaving between
V
and
W
, we say
V
and
W
are
\varepsilon
-interleaved.

Theorem 14

(The Isometry Theorem
[
BL14
]
)
.

Suppose
V
and
W
are two persistence modules with
V_{t}
and
W_{t}
finite dimensional for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the bottleneck distance between the barcode
B
arising from
V
and the barcode
B^{\prime}
of
W
is equal to the value

\inf\{\varepsilon\geq 0:V\text{ and }W\text{ are }\varepsilon\text{-interleaved}\}.

The result above has antecedents in
[
CCSG+09
]
and
[
CDSGO16
]
. It will be at times convenient for us to compute the bottleneck distance via the isometry theorem.

Remark 2
.

The value
\inf\{\varepsilon\geq 0:V\text{ and }W\text{ are }\varepsilon\text{-interleaved}\}
is typically called the
interleaving distance
between
V
and
W
. In this paper, we do not use this nomenclature as to avoid confusion with the interleaving distance between merge trees. These two notions of distance are closely related, and this relationship is made explicit via a categorical framework for interleavings described in
[

Definition 15
.

We define the bottleneck distance between merge trees
(T,f)
and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
, denoted

, the barcode of the persistence module

, and

, the barcode of the persistence module
\{H_{0}((f^{\prime})^{-1}(-\infty,t])\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

Given a merge tree

arising from the persistence module

1.

If any branch points remain in the resulting forest, pick such a

2.3
Intrinsic distances

For any (pseudo)metric on a topological space, one can define another related (pseudo)metric via continuous path length:

Definition 17
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
d:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
be a (pseudo)metric. If
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
is a continuous path (with respect to the topology on
X
), we define the length of
\gamma
with respect to
d
as

L_{d}(\gamma):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
0=t_{0}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d\big(\gamma(t_{i}),\gamma(t_{i-1})\big).

Then we set

\widehat{d}(x_{0},x_{1})=\inf_{\gamma}L_{d}(\gamma),

where the infimum is to be taken over all paths
\gamma
that are continuous in the topology on
X
, start at
x_{0}
, and end at
x_{1}
. We call
\widehat{d}
the
intrinsic (pseudo)metric induced by
d
on the space
X
.

.

The goal of this paper is to prove a conjecture from
[
GMO+25
]
that on
\mathrm{MT}
, we have that
\widehat{d}_{B}=\widehat{d}_{I}
. However the definition above deviates from the definition of an intrinsic metric in the
[
GMO+25
]
and so a few remarks are in order. In the definition of an intrinsic metric given in
[
GMO+25
]
, the authors assume
d
is a metric and the infimum is taken over paths
\gamma
that are continuous with respect to the topology given by
d
.
Hence
\widehat{d}_{I}
as defined here is identical to
\widehat{d}_{I}
as defined in
[
GMO+25
]
.

We want to consider pseudometrics because
d_{B}
is a pseudometric on
\mathrm{MT}
: there are non-isomorphic merge trees
(T,f)
and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
with
d_{B}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)=0
(see Figure

and
[

]
). Thus paths in
\mathrm{MT}
that are continuous in

can ‘teleport’ between distinct merge trees

and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
. Hence, taking the definition of intrinsic distance as in
[

]
, the conjecture from the same paper is false for trivial and uninteresting reasons:
d_{B}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)=0
, which implies
\widehat{d}_{B}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)=0
but
\widehat{d}_{I}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)\geq d_{I}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)\neq 0
. Our own definition avoids this technical issue and with it the aforementioned conjecture holds true. We remark that our definition of intrinsic distances is unoriginal, even in topological data analysis, with previous uses of essentially the same definition appearing in
[
CO17
]
and
[
Vip20
]
, for similar reasons.

A key result of
[

]
is the following.

Theorem 18

On
\mathrm{MT}
,
d_{I}=\widehat{d}_{I}
.

3
Proving the conjecture

Our goal is to strengthen Theorem

to the following.

Theorem 19
.

On
\mathrm{MT}

.

At the heart of our argument is a general proposition about metric spaces and intrinsic distances.

Proposition 20
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space that is also equipped with a pseudometric
\rho
. Suppose
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, for closed sets
X_{i}
such that
\rho=d
when restricted to
X_{i}\times X_{i}
for any index
i
.
Then
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{d}\geq d
.

Proof.

It is always true that
\widehat{d}\geq d
, so it remains to show that
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{d}
. For numbers
0=t_{0}\leq\ldots\leq t_{l}=1
and
0\leq s_{0}\leq\ldots\leq s_{m}\leq 1
, we say
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{m})
is subordinate to
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{l})
if for each
0\leq i\leq l
there is an index
j
such that
s_{j}=t_{i}
.

We claim that given any path
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
(continuous with respect to
d
), and any
0=t_{0}\leq\ldots\leq t_{l}=1
, there is a tuple
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{m})
subordinate to
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{l})
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho\big(\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i})\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d\big(\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i})\big).

(2)

The claim and the triangle inequality together imply that
L_{\rho}(\gamma)=L_{d}(\gamma)
for any path
\gamma
in
X
, which implies
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{d}
. To prove the claim, it suffices to prove the case
l=1
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
be a path which is continuous with respect to
d
and set
s_{0}=t_{0}=\gamma(0)
, and
t_{1}=\gamma(1)
.
After reordering indices we may assume
\gamma(s_{0})\in X_{1}
. Set
s_{1}
to be the supremum of all values
s
such that
\gamma(s)\in X_{1}
. Since
X_{1}
is closed, it follows that
\gamma(s_{1})\in X_{1}
. Inductively, assume we have defined values
s_{1},\ldots s_{k}
, where
k\leq n
, such that

1.

\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i})\in X_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, and

2.

s_{i}
is the supremum of all values
s
with
\gamma(s)\in X_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.

If
s_{k}=1
we terminate the process. Note that this must happen if
k=n
. Otherwise, for all
s>s_{k}
,
\gamma(t_{i})
is not in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}X_{k}
. A pigeonhole principle argument then shows that one of the sets
X_{j}
for
j=k+1,\ldots,n
has
\gamma(s_{k}+\varepsilon_{r})\in X_{j}
for
\{\varepsilon_{r}\}_{r=1}^{\infty}
some sequence of positive numbers approaching zero. By reordering sets we may assume
j=k+1
. Since
X_{k+1}
is closed it follows that
\gamma(s_{k})\in X_{k+1}
. Now set
s_{k+1}
to be the supremum of all values
s
such that
\gamma(s)\in X_{k+1}
. Since
X_{k+1}
is closed,
\gamma(s_{k+1})\in X_{k+1}
.

Having completed the induction we have a sequence
0=s_{0}\leq\ldots\leq s_{m}=1
with
\gamma(s_{i}),\gamma(s_{i-1})\in X_{i}
, giving us

\sum_{i=1}^{m}d(\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho(\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i})).

This proves the claim and hence the proposition.
∎

Continuing to view

Lemma 23

Given

\sim

m
be the maximum of number of leaves among the merge trees
\gamma(t_{i})
for
i=0,\ldots,k
. For
t\in[0,1]
, we have that
t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
for some
i
. Therefore
d_{I}\big(\gamma(t_{i}),\gamma(t)\big)<\varepsilon/2
. Setting
(T,f)=\gamma(t_{i})
and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})=\gamma(t)
, we may pick a
\kappa
-interleaving
\alpha:T\to T^{\prime},\beta:T^{\prime}\to T
between
\gamma(t_{i})
and
\gamma(t)
, where
\kappa<\varepsilon/2
. Note that
T
has at most
m
leaves, so
i^{2\kappa}(T^{\prime})=\alpha\circ\beta(T^{\prime})\subseteq\alpha(T)
must have at most
m
leaves as well. Therefore,
i^{\varepsilon}(T^{\prime})=i^{2(\varepsilon/2-\kappa)}\circ i^{2\kappa}(T^{\prime})
has at most
m
leaves.

Thus for all
\varepsilon>0
there is an
m
such that
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma
has image contained in
\mathrm{MT}_{m}
. We can restrict any interleaving of
\gamma(s)
and
\gamma(t)
to
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(s)
and
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(t)
, showing that

d_{I}\big(i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(s),i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(t)\big)\leq d_{I}\big(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)\big).

This implies that
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma
is continuous in
d_{I}
, so
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma
defines a path in
\mathrm{MT}_{m}
.

Now we must prove the result about bottleneck distances. For
s,t\in[0,1]
, let
M_{s},M_{s}^{\prime},M_{t},M_{t}^{\prime}
be the
H_{0}
persistence modules associated to
\gamma(s)
,
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(s)
,
\gamma(t)
, and
i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(t)
respectively.
It is straightforward to check that there are injective inclusion maps
M_{s}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow M_{s}
and
M_{t}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow M_{t}
and that any interleaving of
M_{s}
and
M_{t}
restricts to an interleaving of
M_{s}^{\prime}
and
M_{t}^{\prime}
via these inclusions. As such, the isometry theorem (see e.g.
[
BL14
, Theorem 6.4]
) implies

d_{B}\big(i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(s),i^{\varepsilon}\gamma(t)\big)\leq d_{B}\big(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)\big),

proving all but the last statement of the proposition.

Suppose
(T_{0},f_{0})
and
(T_{1},f_{1})
both have finitely many leaves, so we may suppose
(T_{0},f_{0}),(T_{1},f_{1})\in\mathrm{MT}_{n}
. We let
\gamma_{2}:=i^{\varepsilon}\circ\gamma
and note that the above inequality implies that
L_{d_{B}}(\gamma_{2})\leq L_{d_{B}}(\gamma)
. We define
\gamma_{1}(t):=i^{t\varepsilon}(T_{0},f_{0})
and
\gamma_{3}(t):=i^{(1-t)\varepsilon}(T_{1},f_{1})
. Since
(T_{0},f_{0})
has at most
n
leaves we have
\gamma_{1}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{MT}_{n}
. For
s<t
in
[0,1]
, let
(T,f)=i^{s\varepsilon}(T_{0},f_{0})
and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})=i^{t\varepsilon}(T_{0},f_{0})
. Setting
r=t-s
it follows that
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})=i^{r\varepsilon}(T,f)
. Viewing
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
as a subobject of
(T,f)
we see that
i^{r\varepsilon}
defines a
r\varepsilon
-interleaving between
(T,f)
and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
. As such,
\gamma_{1}
is
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz in
d_{I}
, and hence the same is true for
d_{B}
since
d_{B}\leq d_{I}
. Therefore
\gamma_{1}
is continuous in
d_{I}
and
L_{d_{B}}(\gamma_{1})\leq\varepsilon
. Similarly,
\gamma_{3}
is continuous in
d_{I}
and
L_{d_{B}}(\gamma_{3})\leq\varepsilon
. The part of the proposition already proven implies
\gamma_{2}
is a map into
\mathrm{MT}_{m}
for some
m
. By potentially increasing
m
, we may assume
m\geq n
. Hence by concatenating paths
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
, and
\gamma_{3}
we get a path
\gamma^{\prime}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{MT}_{m}
from
(T_{0},f_{0})
to
(T_{1},f_{1})
with
L_{d_{B}}(\gamma^{\prime})\leq 2\varepsilon+L_{d_{B}}(\gamma)
, finishing the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
19
.

Pick any
(T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\in\mathrm{MT}
. Take any path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathrm{MT}
continuous in
d_{I}
from
(T,f)
to
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
. Proposition
25
implies that there is a path
\gamma^{\prime}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{MT}_{m}
for some
m
from
(T,f)
to
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})
with
L_{d_{B}}(\gamma^{\prime})\leq L_{d_{B}}(\gamma)+\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon>0
.

As before, we let
\widehat{(d_{B})}_{m}
denote the intrinsic pseudometric induced by the pseudometric
d_{B}
on the metric space
\mathrm{MT}_{m}
and note that the inclusions
\mathrm{MT}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{MT}
imply that
\widehat{(d_{B})}_{m}\geq\widehat{d}_{B}
, since inclusions induce inclusions of path spaces. Propositions
20
and
21
imply that
\widehat{(d_{B})}_{m}\geq d_{I}
. As such, we have

L_{d_{B}}(\gamma)+\varepsilon\geq L_{d_{B}}(\gamma^{\prime})\geq\widehat{(d_{B})}_{m}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)\geq d_{I}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big).

Taking the infimum over all paths
\gamma
and all positive numbers
\varepsilon
we conclude that

\widehat{d}_{B}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)\geq d_{I}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big).

Hence
\widehat{d}_{B}\geq d_{I}
. Since
d_{B}\leq d_{I}
we also have the inequality
\widehat{d}_{B}\leq\widehat{d}_{I}
. Since
d_{I}=\widehat{d}_{I}
(Theorem
18
), we see that
\widehat{d}_{B}=\widehat{d}_{I}=d_{I}
.
∎

4
Discussion

4.1
Efficient computation of merge tree interleaving

The barcode
B(T,f)
of a merge tree
(T,f)
can be computed in
O\big(k\log(k)\big)
time, where
k
is the number of leaves and branch points in
(T,f)
(see e.g.
[
EH10
,
RS24
]
). The bottleneck distance between
B{(T,f)}
and
B{(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})}
, can be computed in
O\big(n^{1.5}\log(n)\big)
, where
n
is the combined number of intervals in the barcodes or, equivalently, the number of leaves in
(T,f)
and
(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})

[
KMN17
]
. Consequently the bottleneck distance between merge trees can be efficiently computed. By contrast, computing the interleaving distance between merge trees is NP-hard
[
AFN+18
,
TW22
]
. Our proof suggests that computing the interleaving distance between a pair of merge trees may be possible when a shortest path between them is known, e.g. when both merge trees lie in one of the
X_{i}\subseteq\mathrm{MT}_{n}
of Proposition
21
.

4.2
Future work and conjectures

There are several natural open questions that are closely related to the work undertaken here, and in the spirit of
[
GMO+25
]
we state them here for others to consider.

First, we have shown that
\widehat{d}_{B}=\widehat{d}_{I}=d_{I}
, and
\widehat{(d_{B})}_{n}=\widehat{(d_{I})}_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
. One wonders how the restricted metric relates: is
(\mathrm{MT}_{n},\widehat{(d_{I})}_{n})
a convex subset of
(\mathrm{MT},d_{I})
? Since
\mathrm{MT}_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{MT}
, we have
\widehat{(d_{I})}_{n}\geq\widehat{d}_{I}=d_{I}
. We conjecture:

Conjecture 26
.

When
n=1,2
,

\widehat{(d_{I})}_{n}=\widehat{d}_{I}=d_{I}.

When
n>2
, there exist merge trees
(T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\in\mathrm{MT}_{n}
such that

\widehat{(d_{I})}_{n}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big)>d_{I}\big((T,f),(T^{\prime},f^{\prime})\big).

Closely related is the open problem of finding the smallest
m(n)
such that there is a path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathrm{MT}_{m(n)}
connecting any two merge trees in
\mathrm{MT}_{n}
with
L_{d_{I}}(\gamma)
equal to the interleaving distance between the endpoints; we believe such an integer exists.

If such an
m(n)
does indeed exist, one could use ideas from our Propositions
20
and
21
to show:

Conjecture 27
.

There exists a number
M(n)
such that for any
y_{0},y_{1}\in\mathrm{MT}_{n}
such that, there exists
x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}
with
k<M(n)
,
y_{0}=x_{0}
,
y_{1}=x_{1}
satisfying:

d_{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{B}(x_{i-1},x-i).

It would be interesting to understand how quickly this number
M(n)
grows as
n
increases.

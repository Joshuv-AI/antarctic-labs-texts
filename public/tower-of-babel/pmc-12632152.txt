Inhalable targeted CD8 + T cell nanoparticles loaded ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1 alleviate acute lung injury
Authors: Chunhua Ma, Zhaocong Yang, Jing Wang, Xuemei Li, Wei Zhou, Tao Li, Liangming Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Acute lung injury (ALI) is a common clinical complication of sepsis and ferroptosis plays an important role in ALI. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of CD8 antibodies modified PD-1 engineered CD8+ T lymphocytes (CTL) membrane loaded poly lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA)/ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1) (CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1) on acute lung injury (ALI). High expression PD-1 CD8 + T (CTL) was established and the membrane (PD-1-CTL-M) was extracted, CD8 monoclonal antibody modified PD-1-CTL-M was used to enhance the targeting capability of PLGA/Fer-1 nanoparticles to target CD8 + T (CTL). ALI model was established by lipopolysaccharide (LPS) with mice and CD8 + T (CTL). The results showed that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M significantly enhanced the protective effects of PLGA/Fer-1 on ALI and targetability of PLGA/Fer-1 nanoparticles on CD8 + T (CTL). The mechanism was related to CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 targeting regulation of PI3K/Akt and MAPK signal pathway in CD8 + T (CTL). Introduction

Acute lung injury (ALI) is a common clinical complication of sepsis [1, 2] and usually caused by severe infection and can further develop into acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) [3, 4]. In terms of pathophysiology, ALI/ARDS is mainly characterized by large amount of inflammatory cells infiltration in different degrees of alveolar epithelial. The pathogenesis of ALI is very complex. More and more studies have shown that in addition to cytokine inflammatory storms, the accumulation of oxidative products were another significant characteristic of ALI [5, 6].

Multiple immune cells are involved in ALI [7]. Neutrophils, as the first immune cells recruited to the site of inflammation during the inflammatory responses and play a crucial role in the progression of ALI [8,9]. During ALI, a large number of activated neutrophils release cytotoxic substances that damaged adjacent tissues, leading to local pathological damage. As is well known, neutrophils are involved in the pathogenesis of ALI, but increasing evidence suggested that immune cells were also involved in regulating inflammatory responses [10]. In addition, according to immune function, T cells can be divided into helper T cells (Th), cytotoxic T cells, and regulatory T cells (Treg). Among them, CD8 + T cells released cytokines and performed killing virus-infected or bacterial infected cells after activation [10]. There wasreport that CD8 + T ferroptosis occured in various tissues including lung tissues [11]. Therefore, inhibition of CD8 + T ferroptosis may be an effective mean to prevent and treat ALI. On the other hand, PD-1 + CD8 + T cells are a type of immune cell with specific surface markers, characterized by the simultaneous expression of CD8 protein (a cytotoxic T cell marker) and PD-1 protein (an immune checkpoint molecule). These types of cells typically appear in chronic infections, tumor microenvironments and have the potential to resist tumors or infections [12], PD-1 is an important immunosuppressive molecule and its high expression on CD8 + T cells is closely related to the exhaustion state of T cells [13, 14]. The above results indicated that PD-1 was an important marker for CD8 + T and PD-1 may serve as a biomarker for targeting CD8 + T.

Nanoparticles modified with cell membrane not only possess the physical and chemical properties of the nanoparticle core, but also have specific biological functions of the cell membrane. T cells are a type of immune cell that can help the body perform immune functions and exhibit many characteristics, such as promoting immune evasion, targeting inflammation or tumor sites specifically [15, 16]. Cell membrane biomimetic modified nanoparticles have been used for targeted therapy of diseases in recent years, but mainly in the field of tumor diseases and its application in ALI is rare. Therefore, the aim of this study was to evaluate the effects and mechanisms of CD8 monoclonal antibody modified PD-1 engineered CD8+ (CTL) membrane loaded with poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA)/ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1) (CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@ Fer-1) on ALI (Fig. 1).

CD8+ T cells was decreased and occurred ferroptosis in lung tissues of ALI mice

To investigate whether CD8 + T cells and its ferroptosis were involved in lung tissues injury following ALI. Single-cell sequencing of lung tissues and ferroptosis related parameters were detected. Prussian blue staining showed lung tissues appeared iron (Fe2+) overloaded and the contents of Fe2+ was increased in ALI with LPS (i.p, 8 mg/kg) (Fig. 2A-B). Single-cell sequencing results of lung tissues showed that ferroptosis (ACSL4, indicator gene) mainly increased in CD 8 T cells which was also called cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTL) (Fig. 2C-F and FigS1A-F). Further research showed the levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione (GSH) were significantly decreased and the levels of 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), malondialdehyde (MDA) and Fe2+ were significantly increased in LPS-treated CD8 + T cells. The levels of GPX4, SLC7A11 and ferritin light chain (FTL) were significantly decreased in LPS-treated CD8 + cells as compared with control group (FigS2A-F).

Construction and identification of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1

Construction and identification of PD-1-CTL-M

In order to obtain the membrane of high expression PD-1 CTL (PD-1 CTL-M), the mouse spleen tissue was utilized to obtain CTL via magnetic bead separation (Fig. S3A). The results showed that the purity of CTL reached 97.2% after separation by magnetic beads, which was suitable for following research (Fig. S3B-C).

Overexpressed PD-1 was constructed in CTL to obtain PD-1-CTL (Fig. S4A). The increase of PD-1 GFP fluorescence intensity in CTL indicated the successful transfection of lentiviral vectors with PD-1 GFP fluorescence tags into CTL, also the PD-1 mRNA level was increased. These results indicated that the PD-1 gene was successfully transferred to CTL (Fig. S4B-C). Compared to the wild type of CTL, the protein level of the PD-1 in the PD-1-CTL was significantly increased (Fig. S4D-E). Similarly, the mRNA and protein levels of PD-1 in PD-1-CTL-M were also increased (Fig. S4F-H). The above results indicated that PD-1-CTL-M has been successfully constructed.

The construction of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (CPPF) and the characteristics

In order to increase the targeting of nanoparticles for CTL, CD8 monoclonal antibody was used to modify PD-1-CTL-M to form CD8@PD-1-CTL-M (CPP). Then co-localization of CD8 and PD-1 was observed the under laser scanning confocal microscopy (CLSM). The immunofluorescence results showed that PD-1 and CD8 presented good co-localization on CTL-M (Fig. S4I). Subsequently, we prepared PLGA/Fer-1 (PF) onto CD8@PD-1-CTL-M via one-pot synthesisto form CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (CPPF). The infrared spectrum results showed that the absorption peak of PD-1-CTL-M at 3500–3300 cm−1 and the characteristic absorption peak of CD8 monoclonal antibody at 1688 cm−1 (NH2) shifted towards lower frequencies and became wider which indicated the formation of hydrogen bonding effects between PD-1-CTL-M and CD8 monoclonal antibody and formed intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonds (Fig. 5A-B). The appearance of the prepared solution showed that PLGA/Fer-1 (PF) and CPPF solution was clearer and the drug loading rate (DLR) and encapsulation rate (ER) of PF were 38.5 ± 4.6% and 78.4 ± 6.4%, the drug loading rate (DLR) and encapsulation rate (ER) of CPPF were 47.75 ± 4.62% and 82.62 ± 5.46% (Fig. 5C-D). Under transmission electron microscopy, the CD8@PD-1-CTL-M and CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 exhibited a cup-shaped lipid bilayer structure with distinct boundaries. The nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) results showed that the size range of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 were 110–120 nm (Fig. 5E-I). Sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) protein gel electrophoresis was used to detect PD-1-M, PD-1-M-M and CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1. The protein band positions of PD-1-CTL, PD-1-CTL-M and CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 were consistent (Fig. 6A-C). The nanoparticles inherited the intact biological activity of CD8 + T cell membrane.

The targeting of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (CPPF) for CD8 + T (CTL) and lung tissues

CTL uptake of nanoparticles is one of the important indicators for its effectiveness. The results showed that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1/DiR (1, 1’-dioctyl-3, 3, 3’, 3’- tetramethylindolecarbonylcyanine perchlorate, DiR) has the strongest fluorescence intensity (Fig. 6D). This result suggested that CTL has the highest uptake for CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1. In addition, we evaluated the lung targeting of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 in mice. The fluorescence intensity of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1/cyc5.5 (Cyanine 5.5, cyc5.5) in lung tissues was significantly increased which was higher than PLGA/Fer-1/cyc5.5 and PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1/cyc5.5 (Fig. 6E). Moreover, imaging results in other organs showed that the above nanoparticles were mainly metabolized in the liver and excreted in the kidney (Fig. 6E and Fig. S5).

The role and the mechanism of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 in treating ALI

Discovery of therapeutic mechanism of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 on ALI

To further elucidate the therapeutic mechanism of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1, RNA seq analysis was used to establish a transcriptome profile of mouse lung tissues. As shown in the Venn diagram, there are 122 genes shared among the three groups of differentially expressed genes (Fig. 7A). All shared differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were clustered in the heatmap, showing significant differences in mRNA levels between the control group (A) and the LPS group (B). Compared with the LPS group, mRNA in the CPPF group (C) was recovered towards the control group (Fig. 7B). Enrichment through signaling pathways showed that the main differential signals were concentrated in PI3-Akt pathway and MAPK signal pathway, with the most significant changes observed (Fig. 7C-F). The above results suggest that PI3-Akt pathway and MAPK signal pathway may be the main signaling pathway through which CPPF exerts its treatment effect on ALI.

The role of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 on ALI mice

Compared with the control group, the levels of lung wet dry density (w/d) and myeloperoxidase (MPO) in the LPS group was significantly increased. CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (CPPF, containing 5 mg/kg Fer-1, inhalation) significantly decreased the levels of w/d and MPO. The branching structure of bronchi in ALI mice was incomplete, alveolar epithelial cells presented vacuolar degeneration and necrosis, uneven staining or vacuolization of cytoplasm, nuclear condensation or fragmentation, and a small number of cells shed into the alveolar cavity. Compared with the LPS group, the pathological changes of lung tissues in the CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 group were significantly alleviated. After LPS injection, the inspiratory and expiratory resistance of mice were significantly higher than those of the control group, while CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly reduced the inspiratory and expiratory resistance. In addition, LPS significantly reduced lung dynamic compliance and maximum ventilation per min in mice. While CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly increased lung dynamic compliance and maximum ventilation per min in mice. Compared with the control group, the levels of MDA, interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were significantly increased, while the level of SOD was significantly decreased in LPS group. CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly increased the level of SOD and decreased the levels of MDA, IL-1β and IL-18 (Fig. 8A-N).

In LPS-treated CD8 + T (CTL) model

Since the above experiment evaluated the effects and mechanism of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 on ALI mice, it is crucial to evaluate the role of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 in LPS-treated CTL model. Therefore, this study evaluated the role and mechanism of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 in LPS-treated CTL model (Fig. S6A). Compared with the control group, the levels of reactive Oxygen Species (ROS), MDA and GSH-px, 4HEN, Fe2+ and IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α were significantly increased and the levels of SOD and GSH were significantly decreased in the LPS group. CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (containing 1µmol/L Fer-1) significantly increased the levels of SOD and GSH and decreased the levels of ROS, MDA and GSH-px, 4HEN, Fe2+ and IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α (Fig. S6B-K). Since RNA sequencing results indicated that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 may exert activities via the signaling pathway of via the PI3-Ak and MAPK signal pathways. We evaluated the affinity of Fer-1 for PI3K and P38MAPK via molecular docking (Fig. 10A). The results showed that Fer-1 has good affinity with PI3K and P38MAPK (Fig. 10A). Next, we evaluated the effects of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 on the PI3-Akt and MAPK signaling pathways, as well as ferroptosis (Fig. 10C). Compared with the control group, the levels of ACSL4 and GPX4 were significantly decreased, while the levels of p-PI3K, p-Akt and p-P38 MAPK were significantly increased in the LPS group. CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly increased the levels of ACSL4 and GPX4 and decreased the levels of p-PI3K, p-Akt and p-P38 MAPK (Fig. 10D-E). The effect of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 on mitochondrial function were evaluated. The results showed that LPS significantly reduced mitochondrial respiration (OCR) and ATP content, while CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly increased OCR and ATP (Fig. 10F-G). LPS stimulation reduced decreased mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) of CTL and however CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly increased MMP (Fig. 10H).

In vivo safety evaluation and Pharmacokinetic assessment of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1

The in vivo safety and pharmacokinetics of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 play an important role in its activity. Therefore, the safety and pharmacokinetics of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 were evaluated in this study. Compared with the control group, CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 had no damage effect on vital organ function. The levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TBIL), albumin (ALB), creatinine (Cr), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatine kinase (CK), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels had no changes after CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 administration (1.25, 2.5, 5 mg/kg, inhalation) (Fig. 11A). Compared with the control group mice, the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 group mice all maintained good integrity and no obvious injury (Fig. 11B-F).

Discussion

Sepsis is a systemic inflammatory response syndrome caused by infection, which can further develop into multiple organ dysfunction [17–19]. At present, although there has been great progress in the treatment of sepsis and its complications ALI, the treatment is not satisfactory [20–23]. In recent years, nanotechnology have been increasingly integrated into the pharmaceutical field. However, there were few reports on sepsis ALI. In our study, we found that ferroptosis was occurred in CTL via single-cell sequencing. Therefore, CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 were prepared via biomimetic technology to target CTL and intervene the ferroptosis of CD8 + T (CTL) to alleviate ALI.

Ferroptosis plays an important role in the body’s immune response and infectious diseases. Some research has showed that ferroptosis plays a crucial role in ALI [24]. There were reports that m6A-modification on ACSL4 promoted ferroptosis [25], targeted ACSL4 inhibitor could treat ferroptosis-related diseases including ALI [26]. Inhiation of SHP2 attenuated LPS-induced ferroptosis via downregulating ACSL4 in ALI [27]. The above results showed that ACSL4 was significantly increased of ferroptosis in ALI and the effective target genes (markers). In this study, single cell sequencing results of lung tissues revealed ACSL4 was mainly elevated in CD8 + T cells of ALI mice. Our research findings were consistent with the above studies. Recently, there were reports that Fer-1 significantly alleviated LPS induced sepsis ALI. Fer-1 alleviated sepsis ALI by suppressing oxidative stress [28]. Fer-1 ameliorated airway epithelial E-cadherin dysfunction in ALI [29]. Fer-1 reversed lipid peroxidation to attenuate ALI [30]. However, these studies only reported the role and mechanism of Fer-1 on sepsis ALI and there are few reports on targeted therapy of Fer-1. In this study, our study found that CD8 + T cells exhibited significant ferroptosis in sepsis ALI mice. Targeting CD8 + T cells to inhibit ferroptosis was a potential treatment strategy for ALI. Therefore, in this study, Fer-1, an ferroptosis inhibitor was used and present good effect for sepsis ALI via inhibiting the ferroptosis of CD8 + T.

PD-1 was originally cloned from the apoptotic mouse T-cell hybridoma 2114.11 and was a type I transmembrane protein containing 288 amino acid residues [31]. It has been widely studied due to its involvement in mediating the exhaustion of CD8 + T cells function in chronic viral infections [28]. PD-1 belongs to the CD28 superfamily and the signaling pathway negatively regulates the intensity of the body’s immune response and participating in pathological and physiological processes such as infectious immunity, autoimmune diseases, and inflammatory responses [32]. One study in cecal ligation and puncture mice model found that PD-1 gene knockout mice had significantly higher survival rates than wild-type mice [33]. Research found that compared with the non-sepsis group, the sepsis group showed a significant decrease in T lymphocyte subsets, an increase in the total expression rate of PD-1, and a linear positive correlation between the PD-1 expression of T cell subsets and APACHE.II score and SOFA score [34]. Related multicenter studies [35] also found that the expression rate of PD-1 on the surface of T cells in sepsis patients in the death group was higher than that in the survival group. The above results indicated that PD-1 was not only a potential indicator of immune suppression, but also a potential therapeutic target.

In order to further improve the targeting role and precise treatment of Fer-1, it was necessary to use nanotechnology to play enhancing effects. There was report that polydopamine nanoparticles encapsulating ferritin inhibitor Ferstatin-1 have been used to treat and alleviate myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury [36]. This study used polydopamine loaded Fer-1, which only increased the bioavailability of Fer-1, but the targeting of Fer-1 was not resolved. Therefore, the present study constructed biomimetic nanoparticles (PD-1-CTL-M) targeting CD8 + T by overexpressing the PD-1 gene of CD8 + T cells (PD-1-CTL). In order to enhance the target ability of biomimetic nanoparticles, this study modified the surface of PD-1-CTL-M with CD8 monoclonal antibodies, thereby achieving precise treatment with biomimetic nanoparticles. Small animal imaging and in vitro cell uptake results showed that PD-1-CTL-M modification with CD8 monoclonal antibody significantly improved the targeting of Fer-1 for CD8 + T (CTL) and lung tissues. On the other hand, pharmacokinetic studies in vivo have found that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly improves utilization and sustained-release of Fer-1 in vivo. Excitingly, the in vivo safety evaluation results showed that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 has almost no toxic effects on mice. The above results suggested that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 not only solved the bioavailability of Fer-1, but also addressed the targeting of Fer-1. CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 was a safe and inhalable targeted CD8 + T novel ferroptosis inhibitor.

In order to elucidate the mechanism of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 targeting CD8 + T (CTL) to improve ALI, this study used single-cell sequencing and found that PI3K-AKT and MAPK were potential signaling pathways of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1. The PI3K-AKT signaling pathway is a signaling pathway that regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, and apoptosis [37]. PI3K can be activated by various growth factors and signal transduction complexes, and AKT is the main downstream effector of PI3K. Activated AKT regulates cell function by activating multiple downstream factors. Recent studies have shown that the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway plays an important role in pneumonia response, cell survival [38], and oxidative stress [39]. More and more evidences suggested that targeted the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway has a protective effect on ALI [40]. The mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway plays an important role in immune regulation in bacterial infection models [41]. Research reports that p38 participates in immune suppression induced by high mobility group protein B-1, leading to the occurrence of complications such as post-traumatic infection and severe sepsis [42]. In this study, the results showed CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly inhibited PI3K-AKT and MAPK signaling pathways activation. More importantly, the phagocytic activity of CD8 + T (CTL) is closely related to the iron metabolism and production of other tissue and cells [43]. Therefore, regulation of CD8 + T (CTL) ferroptosis was an effective measure. Ferroptosis is closely related to mitochondrial function and mitochondrial function participate in ferroptosis in various diseases including cancer [44–47] and ALI [48–50] and so on. In this study, targeted CD8 + T (CTL) to deliver ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 to form CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 and effectively improved ALI via inhibition of ACSL4 and enhanced GPX4 expressions. In addition, in this study, CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 significantly improved mitochondrial morphology and increased mitochondrial membrane potential as compared with ALI group. The above results indicated that CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 improved mitochondrial function in CD8 + T cells ferroptosis of ALI model.

Conclusion

This study constructed an inhalable and pathological microenvironment responsive biomimetic nanoparticles targeting CD8 + T (CTL) for the treatment of ALI. The mechanism was related to the regulation of ferroptosis via PI3K-AKT and MAPK signaling pathways. This study provided a novel strategy for the treatment of ALI.

Methods and materials

Reagent

Fer-1 was purchased from Shanghai Zeye Biotechnology Co., Ltd. was changed to Fer-1 was purchased from MeilunBio (MB4718-1). LPS was purchased from Sigma Company. LY294002 (cat. no. HY-10108; MedChemExpress, USA) and SB203580 (Selleck, S1076) were purchased from Selleckchem (Houston, TX, USA). CD8 (#98941), PD-1(#13684), p-P38 (#4511), P38 (#4511), p-PI3K (#17366), PI3K (#17366), p-Akt (#4060), Akt (#4685) and GAPDH (#2118) monoclonal antibodies were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology Company.

Animals

Babl/c mice (male, 6 weeks, 20–22 g) and SD rats (half male and half female, 8 weeks, 200–250 g) were obtained from the Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. All animals’ operations were approved by the Research Council and Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Medical University (IACUC-2507088).

Isolation of CD8 + T cells and overexpressed PD-1-CTL (PD-1-CTL) was established and identified

A suitable amount of pre cooled cell sorting buffer was added to peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) for washing with PBMCs and centrifuged at 300 g for 8 min. The supernatant was discarded and an appropriate amount of pre-cooled cell sorting buffer was also added. The concentration of PBMCs was adjusted to 1 × 108 cells/ml and pre-cooled in ice bath or 4 ℃ for 5 min. Biotinylated antibody was added to PBMCs at a ratio of 20 µL/mL and incubated at 4 ℃ for 15 min and cell sorting buffer was added and centrifuged at 300 g for 8 min. The supernatant was discarded and adjusted the concentration of PBMCs to 108/ml. Streptavidin magnetic beads was added to PBMCs at a ratio of 20 µL magnetic beads/mL and incubated at room temperature for 10 min. 2.5mL of cell separation buffer was added for 3 min to remove the unmagnetized liquid and the cells were collected and an appropriate amount of pre-cooled cell sorting buffer was add and centrifuged for 300 g, 8 min to remove the supernatant, and the resulting precipitates were the target cells. Fresh culture medium containing PD-1 plasmid virus solution was added into CTL for 24 h. The PD-1-CTL were established and identified via PCR and Western Blot.

Extraction of PD-1-CTL membrane (PD-1-CTL-M) and CD8 monoclonal antibody modified PD-1-CTL-M

PD-1-CTL was resuspended in Tri’s magnesium (pH = 7.40, 0.01 M Tris containing 0.001 mMgC12) membrane protein extraction buffer pre-cooled at 4 ℃ and were dispersed by pipetting and mixed with 1 M sucrose to a final concentration of 0.25 M and then crushed via an ultrasonic cell disruptor at 4200 rpm for 5 min and centrifuged at 2000 g and 4 ℃ for 10 min. The supernatant was collected and centrifuged at 3000 g at 4 ℃ for 30 min to obtain cell membrane precipitates and was washed once with 0.25 M sucrose pre-cooled at 4 ℃ and PD-1-M-M was obtained.

1.5 times the amount of catalyst [1-Ethyl-3- (3-dimethylaminopropyl) carbodiimide] (EDC) and n-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) were added to PD-1-CTL-M and centrifuged at low-temperature overspeed (20000 rpm × 20 min) and resuspended in 10 ml of PBS with pH = 6.5. An appropriate amount of CD8 monoclonal antibody was added for 4 h. CD8 monoclonal antibody molecules, 1-Ethyl-3- (3-dimethylaminopropyl) carbodiimide (EDC), and N-Hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) reagents that were not coupled to PD-1-CTL-M were separated multiple times by low-temperature ultracentrifugation with deionized water at pH = 7.4. The precipitate was resuspended in PBS to obtain CD8@PD-1-CTL-M.

Preparation of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 nanoparticles and characterization

Preparation of PLGA/Fer-1

4 mg PLGA was dissolved in 1 mL N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and then 1 mg Fer-1 was added to obtain a solvent phase containing the Fer-1 and polymer. 1mL of the above solvent phase was slowly added to 0.75 mL of pure water at a rate of 50 µL/min and then the organic solvent was evaporated to obtain PLGA/Fer-1.

Drug loading rate and encapsulation efficiency

10 mL of PLGA/Fer-1 solution was freeze-dried using a freeze dryer and dissolved in 1 mL DMF and the absorbance was measured at 280 nm. The calculation methods for PLGA/Fer-1 loading rate and encapsulation efficiency are as follows:

Fer-1 loading rate (%) = (Fer-1content in PLGA/Fer-1/total mass of PLGA/Fer-1) × 100%.

Fer-1 encapsulation rate (%) = (Fer-1 content in PLGA/Fer-1/total Fer-1) × 100%.

Preparation of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@ PLGA/Fer-1

In brief, CD8@PD-1-CTL-M solution and PLGA/Fer-1 solution (Fer-1 amount of 1 mg) were thoroughly mixed and were extruded for10 times and PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 solution was obtained.

Characterization of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@ PLGA/Fer-1

The particle size and potential of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 were analyzed using a nano zeta potential and particle size analyzer and the morphology was observed by transmission electron microscope.

Co-localization of CD8 and PD-1

Primary antibody was added into CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1/DiR solution overnight at 4 ℃ and incubated secondary antibody for 2 h and was observed by confocal laser scanning microscopy.

Surface protein detection of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1

Cell membrane proteins of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 were detected by SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis and the operations were carried out according to the instructions of the reagent kit.

Distribution of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 in animals

Distribution of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1/cyc5.5 was detected via a small animal live imaging device and the operations were carried out to the instructions.

Safety evaluation

Both sexes of 40 Babl/c mice were randomly divided into the following groups: control group and CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (1.25, 2.5, 5 mg/kg, inhalation, inh). The control group was treated with 0.2 ml of physiological saline (inh), while the other groups were treated with CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 at concentrations of 1.25, 2.5, 5 mg/kg (inh) and the mice were observed continuously for 14 days. The levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TBIL), albumin (ALB), creatinine (Cr), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatine kinase (CK), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) in the blood of each group of mice were detected. Hematoxylin-eosin staining (HE) was used to evaluate pathological changes in the hearts, livers, spleens, lungs, and kidneys of mice in each group and the coefficients of each organ, organ coefficient (%) = organ wet weight (g)/body mass (g) × 100% were calculated.

In vitro release

1 mg Fer-1, 7.41 mg PLGA/Fer-1 (containing 1 mg Fer-1) and 7.41 CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (containing 1 mg Fer-1) were placed in the spectrum labs dialysis bag (with a retained relative molecular weight of 2000), the two ends of the dialysis bag were sealed with plastic clips, placed in 100 ml of pH = 7.4 PBS buffer solution, and 1.0 g of polysorbate 80 was added as surfactant to ensure that Fer-1 reached the groove leakage condition during the release process. The water temperature at 37 ℃ was set and samples were taken at 1, 2, 5, 8, 10, 12, 16, 18, 24, 48, 72 and 96 h at the predetermined time points with a mobile gun, 0.5 ml sample was sucked each time, the same volume of PBS was supplemented, the cumulative release rates of Fer-1 in the nanoparticles were calculated.

Pharmacokinetics of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1

24 SD rats (half male and half female, 200–250 g) were randomly divided into 3 groups (n = 10): Fer-1 (5 mg/kg) group, PLGA/Fer-1 (5 mg/kg) group, CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 (5 mg/kg) group. Blood was taken from the orbit at 5 min, 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h, 3 h, 4 h, 6 h, 8 h, 10 h, 12 h and 24 h after administration. 150 µL of plasma was aspirated with 300 µL methanol to precipitate protein and was whirled for 5 min, then was centrifuged at 15,000 r/min for 10 min, and 350 µL supernatant was absorbed and was dried by liquid nitrogen. The contents of Fer-1 in plasma samples were detected by high-performance liquid chromatography-diode array detection (HPLC-DAD), the blood drug concentration at each time point was calculated.

Stability

CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 nanoparticles were placed in a 4 ℃ refrigerator for 60 days, and taken out at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 days respectively. The particle size and zeta potential of the nano micelles were measured by the above method.

The effects of CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 on ALI mice and LPS-induced CD8+ cells

The mice were randomly divided into the following groups: control group, LPS group (10 mg/kg, tail vein injection), LPS + PLGA/Fer-1 group (PF, containing 5 mg/kg Fer-1, inhalation, inh), LPS + CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 group (CPPF, containing 5 mg/kg Fer-1, inhalation, inh). LPS + PI3K inhibitor LY294002 [intraperitoneally injected (10 mg/kg/day),] + CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 group (CPPF, containing 5 mg/kg Fer-1, inhalation, inh), LPS + P38 MAPK inhibitor SB203580 [intraperitoneally injected (5 mg/kg/day)] + CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 group (CPPF, containing 5 mg/kg Fer-1, inhalation, inh). The mice in the PF group and CPPF group were placed in a nebulization box, and PF and CPPF were respectively poured into nebulization cups. They were sonicated at a concentration of 0.1 mg/mL for 20 min/once a day; The control group and LPS group mice were only given equal amounts of 0.9% sodium chloride injection via ultrasonic nebulization and once a day.

CD8+ T cells were randomly divided into the following groups: control group, LPS (50 µM) group, LPS + PLGA/Fer-1 group (PF, containing 1µmol/L Fer-1) group, LPS + CD8@PD-1-CTL-M@PLGA/Fer-1 group (CPPF, containing 1µmol/L Fer-1) group. PF and CPPF were co-incubated with LPS for 24 h, cell supernatant and cells are collected for the detection of related parameters.

Single cell sequencing of the lung tissues

The lung tissues of mice were obtained and a single-cell suspension with a concentration of 118 cells per milliliter was prepared. The 10×Genomics Chromium TM system was used to free mRNA from the single-cell microreactor system gel beam in impulse (GEMs), mRNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA. After amplification, purification, and enrichment, the qualified cDNA was constructed into a 3 ‘transcriptome library. The NovaSeq ™ XPlus second-generation sequencing platform was used PE150 sequencing mode for sequencing.

Pathological examination of lung tissues of ALI mice

At the end of the experiment, the same lung lobes of mice were fixed in 10% formalin for 24 h and then embedded in paraffin. After further dewaxing and dehydration, it was sliced into 5 µ M sections using a slicer, and finally stained with hematoxylin and eosin staining (HE). The single blind method was used to perform pathological scoring on HE stained slides, scoring pathological sections based on edema, bleeding, inflammatory cell infiltration, and lung atrophy (atelectasis), with each item rated from 0 (no injury) to 4 (severe injury). Scoring criteria: 0 points (no damage); 1 point (damage degree ≤ 25%); 2 points (25% ≤ degree of damage ≤ 25%); 3 points (50% ≤ degree of damage ≤ 75%); 4 points (75% ≤ degree of damage).

Western blot

The lower lobe of the right lung was added to RIPA lysis buffer in a weight to volume ratio (1:10), ground with steel balls, and placed on ice for 20 min to fully lyse the protein. After centrifugation, the supernatant was taken for ultrasonic lysis. The BCA method was used to determine the total protein concentration. All samples were electrophorized on SDS-PAGE gel and transferred to PVDF membrane. The primary antibodies were incubated overnight after being blocked with rapid blocking solution. The membrane was washed; the secondary antibody was incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The ECL chemiluminescence method was used for developing strips, and the grayscale values of the bands were analyzed using Image J image analysis software.

Immunohistochemistry and Immunofluorescence

The thickness of the fixed and embedded lung tissue slices was 3 μm, and immunostaining was performed using specific antibodies against the relevant proteins. Slices were immunostained using diluted rabbit anti-related polyclonal antibodies. The primary antibody was incubated overnight at 4 ℃, and the following day, the secondary antibody was incubated at room temperature for 2 h. Slices were stained with DAPI at room temperature for 10 min and scanned using a tissue section digital scanner.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as mean ± standards deviation (SD) and analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey multiple comparison test using GraphPad prism 8 (GraphPad Software, USA). p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

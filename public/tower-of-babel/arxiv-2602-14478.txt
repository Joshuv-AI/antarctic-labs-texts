Constrained and Composite Sampling via Proximal Sampler
Thanh Dang, Jiaming Liang

Abstract
We study two log-concave sampling problems: constrained sampling and composite sampling. First, we consider sampling from a target distribution with density proportional to exp ⁡ ( − f ⁡ ( x ) ) \exp(-f(x)) supported on a convex set K ⊂ ℝ d K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , where f f is convex. The main challenge is enforcing feasibility without degrading mixing. Using an epigraph transformation, we reduce this task to sampling from a nearly uniform distribution over a lifted convex set in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} . We then solve the lifted problem using a proximal sampler. Assuming only a separation oracle for K K and a subgradient oracle for f f , we develop an implementation of the proximal sampler based on the cutting-plane method and rejection sampling. Unlike existing constrained samplers that rely on projection, reflection, barrier functions, or mirror maps, our approach enforces feasibility using only minimal oracle access, resulting in a practical and unbiased sampler without knowing the geometry of the constraint set. Second, we study composite sampling, where the target is proportional to exp ⁡ ( − f ⁡ ( x ) − h ⁡ ( x ) ) \exp(-f(x)-h(x)) with closed and convex f f and h h . This composite structure is standard in Bayesian inference with f f modeling data fidelity and h h encoding prior information. We reduce composite sampling via an epigraph lifting of h h to constrained sampling in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} , which allows direct application of the constrained sampling algorithm developed in the first part. This reduction results in a double epigraph lifting formulation in ℝ d + 2 \mathbb{R}^{d+2} , on which we apply a proximal sampler. By keeping f f and h h separate, we further demonstrate how different combinations of oracle access (such as subgradient and proximal) can be leveraged to construct separation oracles for the lifted problem. For both sampling problems, we establish mixing time bounds measured in Rényi and χ 2 \chi^{2} divergences. Key words. Constrained sampling, composite sampling, epigraph lifting, proximal sampler, mixing times.

Introduction

This paper studies two log-concave sampling problems: constrained sampling and composite sampling. Using an epigraph transformation, we reduce both problems to nearly uniform sampling in lifted spaces, which can then be solved via a proximal sampler.
The proximal sampler was introduced by
[
40
]
as an unbiased method for sampling from log-concave targets, and was later substantially extended by
[
6
]
to cover distributions satisfying general functional inequalities. The algorithm is closely related to the proximal point method in optimization, hence its name. Given a step size
\eta>0
and a target
\pi(x)\propto\exp(-f(x))
, the proximal sampler performs Gibbs sampling on the augmented distribution
\Pi^{X,Y}(x,y)\ \propto\ \exp\!\left(-f(x)-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\eta)\right)
, whose
X
-marginal is exactly
\pi
. Each iteration alternates between sampling
y\sim\Pi^{Y|X}
, a Gaussian distribution, and sampling
x\sim\Pi^{X|Y}
, known as the restricted Gaussian oracle (RGO). In general, the RGO is unavailable or difficult to implement. In this work, we provide efficient unbiased implementations of the RGO in both sampling problems under various oracle models.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex set.
In the first part of this paper,
we consider sampling from a distribution
\nu
for which

\nu(x)\propto\exp(-f(x))\mathbf{1}_{K}(x)=\exp\left(-f(x)-I_{K}(x)\right),

(1)

where
I_{K}
is the extended indicator function of the set
K
, taking the value
0
if
x\in K
and
+\infty
otherwise; while
\mathbf{1}_{K}(x)
equals
1
on
K
and
0
otherwise. To motivate the sampling approach studied in this paper, let us consider the analogy with convex optimization. The problem
\min\{f(x):x\in K\}
can be rewritten in epigraph form
\min\{t:(x,t)\in Q\}
,
where
Q=\{(x,t)\in K\times\mathbb{R}:f(x)\leq t\}
is the
epigraph
of
f
restricted to
K
.
This reformulation lifts the problem from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and indirectly handles
f
through the constraint
f(x)\leq t
instead of an objective function. It has been widely used in optimization, for example, the bundle methods
[
43
,
42
,
57
]
.

Motivated by this idea from optimization, we reformulate constrained sampling (
1
) in an epigraph form. Let us define the lifted set

Q=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:x\in K,f(x)\leq at\},

(2)

for some scaling constant
a>0
, and consider the distribution

\pi(x,t)\propto e^{-at}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x,t)=\exp(-at-I_{Q}(x,t)).

(3)

The crucial observation is that the
X
-marginal of
\pi
coincides with the target distribution
\nu
.
Indeed, integrating out
t
gives
\pi^{X}(x)=\int\pi(x,t)\mathrm{d}t\propto\left(\int_{f(x)/a}^{\infty}e^{-at}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathbf{1}_{K}(x)=\frac{1}{a}e^{-f(x)}\mathbf{1}_{K}(x)\propto\nu(x)
. Thus, if one can efficiently sample
(x,t)
from
\pi
over the lifted set
Q
, then discarding the
t
-coordinate produces samples from the target
\nu
. There are advantages to sampling from the lifted target
\pi
in (
3
) rather than directly from
\nu
in (
1
).

First
, aside from the constraint
Q
, the lifted
\pi
in (
3
) depends only on the last coordinate
t
through an exponential factor, and is therefore a nearly uniform distribution on the convex set
Q
. This links our setting to uniform sampling on convex bodies where a broad range of algorithmic tools are available. In particular, motivated by the recent works
[
11
,
32
]
, we will employ the proximal sampler by
[
40
]
, to sample from the nearly uniform lifted measure
\pi
.

Second
, recall that the proximal sampler relies on the RGO, which is typically difficult to implement. For the target
\pi
in (
3
), however, the RGO reduces to sampling a Gaussian distribution truncated to
Q
, which can be achieved via rejection sampling combined with the cutting-plane (CP) method by
[
23
]
. This implementation requires only a subgradient oracle for
f
and a separation oracle for
K
. Unlike existing constrained samplers that depend on
projections, reflections, barrier functions, or mirror maps,
our approach enforces feasibility using only minimal separation-based oracle access.

Let
f
and
h
be closed convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In the second part of the paper,
we study the composite sampling problem of drawing samples from

\tilde{\nu}(x)\ \propto\ \exp\left(-f(x)-h(x)\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4)

Such composite structure arises naturally in Bayesian models:
f
typically represents the negative log-likelihood, while
h
plays the role of a negative log-prior. Although one may instead seek a maximum a posteriori estimator by solving the corresponding composite optimization problem
\min\{f(x)+h(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
, sampling from
\tilde{\nu}
is often more informative, as it enables uncertainty quantification beyond a single point estimate.
Moreover, the composite sampling formulation is especially valuable when
f
and
h
are provided through different oracle models, which can be analyzed in a unified way by the sampling algorithm developed in this work but cannot be handled as naturally in the composite optimization setting.

We now describe a two-step epigraph lifting (
double lifting
) that turns sampling
\tilde{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into sampling a nearly uniform
distribution over a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{d+2}
.
We first apply the epigraph lifting to
h
:

\gamma(x,s)\ \propto\ \exp\left(-\tilde{f}(x,s)-I_{\widetilde{K}}(x,s)\right),\,\,\widetilde{K}=\{(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:\ h(x)\leq as\},\,\,\tilde{f}(x,s)=f(x)+as.

(5)

Since
\gamma
is supported on the convex set
\widetilde{K}
, the first lifting reduces the composite problem to constrained sampling. We then apply epigraph lifting again to
\tilde{f}
and obtain a nearly uniform distribution in
\mathbb{R}^{d+2}
:

\tilde{\pi}(x,s,t)\ \propto\ \exp\left(-bt-I_{\widetilde{Q}}(x,s,t)\right),\qquad\widetilde{Q}=\{(x,s,t):\ (x,s)\in\widetilde{K},\ \tilde{f}(x,s)\leq bt\}.

(6)

Integrating out
(s,t)
shows that
\tilde{\pi}^{X}=\tilde{\nu}
, since
\int_{h(x)/a}^{\infty}\int_{\tilde{f}(x,s)/b}^{\infty}e^{-bt}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{ab}\exp\!\left(-f(x)-h(x)\right)
; thus sampling
(x,s,t)\sim\tilde{\pi}
and discarding
(s,t)
yields samples from
\tilde{\nu}
. As in the single-lifting case (
3
), we develop a proximal sampler for the double-lifted target (
6
), with the RGO implemented via a separation oracle for
\widetilde{Q}
and the CP method. The composite viewpoint is useful here, since different oracles for
f
and
h
can be combined to construct a separation oracle for
\widetilde{Q}
.

Oracles.

3.1
Proximal sampler for the lifted distribution
\pi

Per the idea of the proximal sampler, to sample

\Pi((x,t),(y,s))\propto\exp\left(-I_{Q}(x,t)-at-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert(x,t)-(y,s)\right\rVert^{2}\right).

For fixed

Lemma 3.4

Assume conditions (A1) and (A2) hold. For any

3.2.1.1. Proof sketch of Lemma
3.4

The integrand depends on

only through

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}e^{-at}\exp\left(-\frac{(\operatorname{dist}(w,Q)-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}w=\int_{Q}\ldots\mathrm{d}w+\int_{Q^{C}}\ldots\mathrm{d}w.

\int_{Q^{c}}e^{-at}\exp\left(-\frac{(\operatorname{dist}(w,Q)-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:ZandB}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(r-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)B_{Q_{r}}\mathrm{d}r.

Lemma 3.5

Assume conditions (A1) and (A2) hold. For every
\tau\geq 0
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(r-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)B_{Q_{r}}\mathrm{d}r\ \leq\ Z_{Q}C_{L}\sqrt{2\pi\eta}\;\exp\left(\tau C_{L}+\frac{\eta C_{L}^{2}}{2}\right)+Z_{Q}\left(e^{C_{L}\tau}-1\right).

Proof sketch of Lemma
3.5
(full proof in Appendix
C.2
):
Bounding
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(r-\tau)^{2}/(2\eta)}B_{Q_{r}}\mathrm{d}r
directly is difficult since

B_{Q_{r}}=\int_{\partial Q_{r}}e^{-at}\mathrm{d}S
as a boundary integral is sensitive to the geometry of the boundary
\partial Q_{r}
.
In contrast,
Z_{Q_{r}}=\int_{Q_{r}}e^{-at}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t
is a volume integral that can be bounded by enclosing
Q_{r}
in an
envelope set. Hence, we will eliminate
B_{Q_{r}}
in favor of
Z_{Q_{r}}
via integration by parts (IBP) and

\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}r}Z_{Q_{r}}=B_{Q_{r}}
a.e.

Step 1 (envelope set).

We bound
Z_{Q_{r}}
by replacing
Q_{r}
with a slightly larger set whose
integral is easy. Recall
Q=\{(x,t):x\in K,\ f(x)\leq at\}
and

Q_{r}=Q+r\mathrm{B}_{d+1}(0)
. Define the envelope
\mathcal{E}_{r}=\left\{(x,t):x\in K_{r},\ f(x)\leq at+rC_{f}\right\}
. The point is that moving a distance at most
r
from a feasible point
(x,t)\in Q

can only (i) move
x
into the enlarged set
K_{r}=K+r\mathrm{B}_{d}(0)
, and (ii) violate the inequality

f(x)\leq at
by at most a constant times
r
(per the Lipschitz property of
f
). This gives the inclusion
Q_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{r}
and
Z_{Q_{r}}\leq Z_{\mathcal{E}_{r}}
. Then note that
\mathcal{E}_{r}
is designed so
Z_{\mathcal{E}_{r}}
is easy to compute exactly, and we arrive at

Z_{Q_{r}}\leq Z_{\mathcal{E}_{r}}\leq e^{rC_{L}}Z_{Q}
, which relates
Z_{Q_{r}}
back to
Z_{Q}
, a quantity of our interest.

Step 2 (IBP).

Lemma
B.9
reveals a key observation
\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}r}Z_{Q_{r}}=B_{Q_{r}}
a.e. Thus, using IBP, we can rewrite
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(r-\tau)^{2}/(2\eta)}B_{Q_{r}}\mathrm{d}r
into an expression in terms of
Z_{Q_{r}}
instead of
B_{Q_{r}}
.
This is advantageous because Step 1 controls

by

, while the Gaussian weight
w_{\eta,\tau}(r)=e^{-(r-\tau)^{2}/(2\eta)}
decays fast enough that
the boundary term at

vanishes. The problem then reduces to bounding a
one-dimensional integral of the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{rC_{L}}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)\mathrm{d}r
. This last part is simple and yields the desired final estimate.

that the minimizer
\operatorname{proj}_{\widetilde{Q}}(q)

belongs to
\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
a.s. Moreover, the CP method requires a subgradient oracle for the objective
\|p-q\|^{2}
in (
22
), which is available explicitly; and a separation oracle for
\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. The latter is obtained by combining the separation oracle for
\widetilde{Q}
(which can be constructed from oracles for
f
and
h
per the earlier explanation) and a simple separation oracle for the local ball
2r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)
.

Having verified the requirements, the CP method of
[
23
]
applies and returns a
(d+2)^{-1}
-solution
\tilde{p}
to
\min_{p\in\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}}\|p-q\|^{2}
, with the complexity stated in Theorem
D.9
. We then center the RGO rejection proposal at
\tilde{p}
, i.e., the proposal density is proportional to
\exp\!\left(-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(p)\right)
, where

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(p)=\frac{\|p-\tilde{p}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{p}-q\|^{2}}{2\eta}-\frac{\delta}{\eta}(\|p-\tilde{p}\|+\|\tilde{p}-q\|)+bv-\frac{b^{2}\eta}{2}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\eta}.

(24)

In summary, the RGO implementation (i.e., Algorithm
5
) consists of two steps: first, solving for
\tilde{p}
via the CP method to define the potential
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
; and second building a rejection sampler with the proposal
\exp\left(-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(p)\right)
, which can be implemented by Algorithm
6
in Appendix
D.5
.

The following is our main result for the RGO implementation; see its proof in Appendix
D.2
. Since this result will be paired with the proximal sampler iteration complexity in Theorem
4.1
(see the Contributions paragraph in the introduction), we specialize to the scaling
a=b=d
.

Theorem 4.2

Assume (B1)-(B4) holds,
a=b=d
, and
\eta=1/d^{2}
. Then regarding Algorithm
5
,

(a)

(oracle complexity)
there are at most
{\cal O}\left((d+2)\log\frac{(d+2)\gamma}{\alpha}\right)
calls to the separation oracle of

\widetilde{Q}
. Here
\gamma=\frac{2r_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}})}

\displaystyle\gamma\ \leq\ \frac{2(3+\mu)}{\mu}\quad\text{for}\quad\mu=\min\left\{\frac{d}{\sqrt{d^{2}+L_{h}^{2}}},\ \frac{d}{\sqrt{2d^{2}+L_{f}^{2}}}\right\};

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\alpha\ \leq\ \min\left\{\frac{1}{2(d+2)(\sqrt{d+2}+d/2+1)^{2}},\ \frac{1}{2}\right\}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{8}\right).

Thus,
{\cal O}\left((d+2)\log\frac{(d+2)\gamma}{\alpha}\right)={\cal O}\left(d\log d\right)
in high probability.

(b)

(proposal complexity)
the average number of proposals is at most

M\exp\!\left(\frac{7}{4}+\frac{16}{d}+\frac{3\widetilde{C}_{L}}{d^{3/2}}\right)\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\widetilde{C}_{L}}{d}\exp\!\left(\frac{\widetilde{C}_{L}^{2}}{2d^{2}}\right)+1\right].

(25)

Moreover, if we assume
L_{f}={\cal O}(d)
,
L_{h}={\cal O}(d)
, and
M={\cal O}(1)
, then (
25
) becomes
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

The proposal complexity proof follows the argument of Theorem
3.3
, but the oracle-complexity proof is subtler. In particular, to bound the number of separation-oracle calls we need a lower bound on
\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}})
. We do this by inscribing a ball
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
of radius
r_{\mathrm{in}}
, giving
\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}})\geq\mbox{minwidth}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}})=2r_{\mathrm{in}}
. Constructing
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}
in Lemma
D.8
is delicate and requires a slight enlargement of the local set, i.e., working with
\widetilde{Q}\cap cr_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)
for some
c>1
(the argument fails at
c=1
); this is why we take
\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\widetilde{Q}\cap 2r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)
.

5
Conclusions

This paper develops sampling algorithms for log-concave constrained and composite sampling via epigraph lifting and the proximal sampler.
Our algorithms are based on a sequence of liftings that connect these general settings to nearly uniform sampling in lifted spaces. Central to our approach is an efficient and implementable RGO used within proximal sampler.
Our RGO implementation is based on rejection sampling and the CP method. It requires only separation oracles for the lifted constraint sets
\widetilde{K}
and
\widetilde{Q}
, and we provide concrete constructions of these oracles under various oracle models for accessing
f
,
h
, and
K
.
For both problems, we establish
\mathcal{O}\left(d^{2}\log d\log\epsilon^{-1}\right)
mixing time guarantees measured in Rényi and
\chi^{2}
-squared divergences.

A natural future direction is to relax the global Lipschitz continuity assumption in both the constrained and composite settings. In our analysis, global Lipschitz continuity provides uniform control that is crucial for the envelope arguments in Lemmas
3.5
and
D.6
. It would be interesting to replace this requirement with weaker conditions, such as local Lipschitz continuity. For instance,
[
34
]
adopts a ground-set assumption in place of global Lipschitz continuity.

Proposition B.1

(
[
34
, Lemma 2.9]
) Let
\pi
be a probability measure on
Q
that is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue, and let
P
be the Markov kernel of the proximal sampler with step size
\eta
. Denote by
C_{\mathrm{PI}}
the Poincaré constant of
\pi
. Then for any
\mu\ll\pi
and any
q\geq 2
with

\chi^{2}(\mu P\|\pi)\leq\frac{\chi^{2}(\mu\|\pi)}{(1+\eta/C_{\mathrm{PI}}(\pi))^{2}},\quad\mathcal{R}_{q}(\mu P\|\pi)\leq\frac{\mathcal{R}_{q}(\mu\|\pi)}{(1+\eta/C_{\mathrm{PI}}(\pi))^{2/q}}.

B.2
Function and set oracles

This subsection collects elementary results about oracles for functions (evaluation, subgradient, and proximal) and sets (membership, separation, and projection).
Throughout this subsection, we assume that both
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are closed and convex.

Lemma B.2

The proximal oracle for
f(x)
gives a proximal oracle for
\tilde{f}(x,s)
defined in (
5
).

Proof
: Let
(x_{0},s_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
be given. By definitions of proximal mapping and
\tilde{f}(x,s)
in (
5
), we have

\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda\tilde{f}}(x_{0},s_{0})=\underset{x,s}{\mathrm{argmin}}\left\{f(x)+as+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}(s-s_{0})^{2}\right\}.

This objective splits into an
x
-term and an
s
-term, so the proximal mapping can be easily obtained as

\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda\tilde{f}}(x_{0},s_{0})=(\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{f}}(x_{0}),s_{0}-\lambda a).

Hence, the lemma is proved.

Lemma B.3

For a closed and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a projection oracle for
K
implies a separation oracle.

Proof
: Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it suffices to discuss the case
x\notin K
. Let
p=\operatorname{proj}_{K}(x)
. Since
x\neq p
, then
g=x-p\neq 0
satisfies
\left\langle g,x\right\rangle>\left\langle g,p\right\rangle\geq\left\langle g,y\right\rangle
for every
y\in K
. Hence,
g
is a separator for
x
and
K
.

Lemma B.4

Evaluation and subgradient oracles of
f
give a valid separation oracle for
\operatorname{epi}(f)
.

Proof
: Consider a query point
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. First, evaluating
f(\hat{x})\leq\hat{t}
checks whether
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in\operatorname{epi}(f)
. If infeasible (i.e.,
f(\hat{x})>\hat{t}
), then pick a subgradient
g\in\partial f(\hat{x})
, and convexity of
f
gives

f(y)\geq f(\hat{x})+\left\langle g,y-\hat{x}\right\rangle\quad\forall y.

For
(y,s)\in\operatorname{epi}(f)
,

s\geq f(y)\geq f(\hat{x})+\left\langle g,y-\hat{x}\right\rangle,

and hence

\left\langle(g,-1),(y,s)\right\rangle\leq\left\langle g,\hat{x}\right\rangle-f(\hat{x})<\left\langle g,\hat{x}\right\rangle-\hat{t}=\left\langle(g,-1),(\hat{x},\hat{t})\right\rangle.

Therefore,
(g,-1)
is a valid separator for
(\hat{x},\hat{t})
and
\operatorname{epi}(f)
.

Lemma B.5

Evaluation and proximal oracles of
f
give a valid projection oracle for
\operatorname{epi}(f)
.

Proof
: Consider a query point
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. First, evaluating
f(\hat{x})\leq\hat{t}
checks whether
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in\operatorname{epi}(f)
. If infeasible (i.e.,
f(\hat{x})>\hat{t}
), we consider

\min_{x,t}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|x-\hat{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(t-\hat{t})^{2}:f(x)\leq t\right\}.

Optimality conditions give
\lambda\geq 0
such that

\hat{x}-x\in\lambda\partial f(x),\quad t-\hat{t}-\lambda=0,\quad\lambda(f(x)-t)=0.

So, we have
f(x)=t=\hat{t}+\lambda
and

\hat{x}-x\in\lambda\partial f(x)\Longleftrightarrow x=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(\hat{x}).

Define

\phi(\lambda)=f(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(\hat{x}))-\hat{t}-\lambda.

Then
\phi
is continuous and strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, with
\phi(0^{+})=f(\hat{x})-\hat{t}>0
and
\phi(\lambda)\rightarrow-\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore, there is a unique
\lambda_{*}>0
such that
\phi(\lambda_{*})=0
, and the exact projection is

x_{*}=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{*}f}(\hat{x}),\quad t_{*}=\hat{t}+\lambda_{*}.

Finally, we verify the monotonicity of
\phi(\lambda)
. For
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
, let
x_{1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{1}f}(\hat{x})
and
x_{2}=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{2}f}(\hat{x})
, by optimality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{2}-\hat{x}\|^{2}+\lambda_{2}f(x_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}-\hat{x}\|^{2}+\lambda_{2}f(x_{1}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}-\hat{x}\|^{2}+\lambda_{1}f(x_{1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x_{2}-\hat{x}\|^{2}+\lambda_{1}f(x_{2}).

Summing the two inequalities and rearranging, we have

(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(f(x_{2})-f(x_{1}))\leq-\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|^{2}<0,

and hence
f(x_{2})<f(x_{1})
, i.e.,
\phi
is strictly decreasing. Therefore,
\lambda_{*}
can be solved analytically or numerically using a bisection search.

We are ready to prove Lemma
3.2
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
:

Without loss of generality, we assume
a=1
in (
2
), i.e.,
Q=(K\times\mathbb{R})\cap\operatorname{epi}(f)
. Consider a query point
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. There are two infeasible cases: i)
\hat{x}\notin K
and ii)
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\notin\operatorname{epi}(f)
.

Case i). Suppose
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a separator for
\hat{x}
and
K
, i.e.,
\left\langle p,y\right\rangle<\left\langle p,\hat{x}\right\rangle
for every
y\in K

Then the hyperplane given by
(p,0)
separates
(\hat{x},\hat{t})
and
Q
. Indeed,

\left\langle(p,0),(y,s)\right\rangle\leq\left\langle(p,0),(\hat{x},\hat{t})\right\rangle,\quad\forall(y,s)\in Q.

Case ii) directly follows from Lemma
B.4
.

Lemma B.6

Evaluation and proximal oracles of
f
, and membership and separation oracles of
K
give a valid separation oracle for
Q
as in (
2
).

Proof
: We consider a query point
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
and the same two cases as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
The same argument applies to case i) here. For case ii), Lemma
B.5
gives a projection oracle for
\operatorname{epi}(f)

\operatorname{epi}(f)

Lemma B.7

The following statements hold for any

The next lemma says that if you thicken an epigraph by radius
r
, you still stay inside an epigraph, but with the defining function shifted downward by an amount of order
r
.

Lemma B.8

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
\mathrm{epi}(\phi)
be the epigraph of
\phi
, while
(\mathrm{epi}(\phi))_{r}
is the associated parallel set. Then

(\mathrm{epi}(\phi))_{r}\ \subseteq\ \left\{(u,v):\ v\ \geq\ \phi(u)-r\sqrt{1+L^{2}}\right\}.

Proof
: If
(x,s)\in(\mathrm{epi}(\phi))_{r}
then there exists
(y,t)\in\mathrm{epi}(\phi)
with

\|(x,s)-(y,t)\|\leq r
and
t\geq\phi(y)
. Put
\alpha:=\|x-y\|
,
\beta:=|t-s|
so
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\leq r^{2}
.
Then

\phi(x)-s\leq|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|+(\phi(y)-s)\leq L\alpha+(t-s)\leq L\alpha+\beta\leq\sqrt{1+L^{2}}\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\leq r\sqrt{1+L^{2}},

which is equivalent to
s\geq\phi(x)-r\sqrt{1+L^{2}}
.

In the main paper, we repeatedly convert integrals over the parallel sets
Q_{r}
into integrals over the boundary sets
\partial Q_{r}
. The following lemma formalizes this conversion.

Lemma B.9

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a measurable set,
Z_{Q_{r}}:=\int_{Q_{r}}e^{-bt}\mathrm{d}y
, and

B_{Q_{r}}:=\int_{\partial Q_{r}}e^{-bt}\mathrm{d}S^{m-1}(y)
where
t=t(y)
is the last coordinate in
y
.
Then,
Z_{Q_{r}}
is absolutely continuous in
r
and for a.e.
r\geq 0
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}Z_{Q_{r}}=B_{Q_{r}}.

(26)

Proof
: The co-area formula (e.g.,
[
18
, Thm. 3.2.12]
) says: if
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz and

h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}h(y)|\nabla\varphi(y)|\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\{y:\varphi(y)=p\}}h(y)\mathrm{d}S^{m-1}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}p.

For
y=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, set
\varphi(y)=\operatorname{dist}(y,Q)
and

h(y)=\frac{e^{-bt}}{\|\nabla\varphi(y)\|}\mathbf{1}_{\{y:\varphi(y)\leq r\}}
.
Then, noting that
\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,Q)\leq r\}=Q_{r}
and
\|\nabla\varphi(y)\|=\|\nabla\operatorname{dist}(y,Q)\|=1
for
\mathcal{L}^{m}
a.e.
y
, we have

Z_{Q_{r}}=\int_{\{y:\varphi(y)\leq r\}}e^{-bt}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\int_{\{y:\varphi(y)=p\}}{e^{-bt}}\mathrm{d}S^{m-1}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}p.

(27)

Thus,
Z_{Q_{r}}
is absolutely continuous in
r
and a.e. with respect to
r\geq 0
,

\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}r}Z_{Q_{r}}=\int_{\{y:\varphi(y)=r\}}{e^{-bt}}\mathrm{d}S^{m-1}(y).

Moreover, we know
\partial Q_{r}=\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,Q)=r\}
, so that (
26
) holds.

We record some elementary identities in the upcoming result.

Lemma B.10

The following facts hold.

a)

Given
\eta>0
and
\tau>0
, set
w_{\eta,\tau}(r):=\exp\left(-\frac{(r-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)
and
let
C\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,

\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\frac{r-\tau}{\eta}e^{Cr}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)\mathrm{d}r=C\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{Cr}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)\mathrm{d}r+e^{C\tau}.

(28)

b)

Let
a>0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, and
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
be measurable functions. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x)\left(\int_{t\geq\psi(x)-\gamma}e^{-at}dt\right)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{e^{a\gamma}}{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x)e^{-a\psi(x)}\mathrm{d}x.

(29)

Proof
: Part a is due to integration by parts. Define
F_{C}(r):=e^{Cr}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)
. Then

F_{C}^{\prime}(r)=Ce^{Cr}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)+e^{Cr}w_{\eta,\tau}^{\prime}(r)=\left(C-\tfrac{r-\tau}{\eta}\right)F_{C}(r),

since
w_{\eta,\tau}^{\prime}(r)=-(r-\tau)/\eta\;w_{\eta,\tau}(r)
.
Rearranging gives
\frac{r-\tau}{\eta}F_{C}(r)=CF_{C}(r)-F_{C}^{\prime}(r)
.
Integrating over
[\tau,\infty)
and using
F_{C}(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
and

F_{C}(\tau)=e^{C\tau}
, we obtain the stated result (
28
).

Part b is due to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x)\left(\int_{t\geq\psi(x)-\gamma}e^{-at}dt\right)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x)\left(\frac{1}{a}e^{-a(\psi(x)-\gamma)}\right)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{e^{a\gamma}}{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x)e^{-a\psi(x)}\mathrm{d}x.

This completes the proof.

At several locations in the main paper, we use a simple rejection-sampling scheme: propose from a tractable density proportional to
\exp(-\mathcal{P})
, and accept with the standard likelihood-ratio rule in order to target a density proportional to
\exp(-\Theta)
.
The following standard lemma records the resulting target law, the acceptance probability, and the average number of proposals.

Lemma B.11

Let
\Theta,\mathcal{P}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be measurable and assume

0<N_{\Theta}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\Theta(z)}\mathrm{d}z<\infty,\qquad 0<N_{\mathcal{P}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\mathcal{P}(z)}\mathrm{d}z<\infty,

and that
\Theta(z)\geq\mathcal{P}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Consider the rejection sampler that repeats the following trial until acceptance:

1.

draw a proposal
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with density
k(z)=e^{-\mathcal{P}(z)}/N_{\mathcal{P}}
;

2.

draw
U\sim{\cal U}[0,1]
independent of
Z
and accept if and only if

U\leq\exp\left(-\Theta(Z)+\mathcal{P}(Z)\right).

(30)

Then the output has density

\pi(z)=\frac{e^{-\Theta(z)}}{N_{\Theta}}.

Moreover, let
E
denote the acceptance event (
30
) for a single trial and
F
denote the number of trials until acceptance. Then, the acceptance probability and the average number of proposals are

p:=\Pr(E)=\frac{N_{\Theta}}{N_{\mathcal{P}}},\qquad\mathbb{E}[F]=\frac{1}{p}=\frac{N_{\mathcal{P}}}{N_{\Theta}}.

(31)

Proof
: For one trial, let
E
denote the event (
30
) happens.
Since
\Theta\geq\mathcal{P}
, we have
0\leq\Pr(E|Z=z)=\exp(-\Theta(z)+\mathcal{P}(z))\leq 1
,
so the acceptance rule is well-defined.

We first compute the acceptance probability:

\displaystyle p=\Pr(E)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Pr(E|Z=z)k(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\exp\left(-\Theta(z)+\mathcal{P}(z)\right)\cdot\frac{e^{-\mathcal{P}(z)}}{N_{\mathcal{P}}}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{P}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\Theta(z)}\mathrm{d}z=\frac{N_{\Theta}}{N_{\mathcal{P}}}.

Next, we compute the conditional density of the accepted proposal:

\displaystyle k(z|E)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Pr(E|Z=z)k(z)}{\Pr(E)}=\frac{\exp\left(-\Theta(z)+\mathcal{P}(z)\right)\cdot e^{-\mathcal{P}(z)}/N_{\mathcal{P}}}{N_{\Theta}/N_{\mathcal{P}}}=\frac{e^{-\Theta(z)}}{N_{\Theta}}.

Lemma C.8

Algorithm
3
generates

.

Proof
: We rewrite
\Lambda(w)
in polar coordinates.
Let

.
Since
\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=r^{d}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}S(\theta)
,
where
\mathrm{d}S
is surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, for

and
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}

[

Then sample

q_{d+2}=v-b\eta
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+2}}e^{-bq_{d+2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\operatorname{dist}(q,\widetilde{Q})-\tau\right)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)dq

\displaystyle\leq Z_{\widetilde{Q}}\exp(\tau\widetilde{C}_{L})\left[\widetilde{C}_{L}\sqrt{2\pi\eta}\exp\left(\frac{\eta\widetilde{C}_{L}^{2}}{2}\right)+1\right].

Proof
: We split the proof into two cases.

Case I:
When
q\in\widetilde{Q}
, we have
\operatorname{dist}(q,\widetilde{Q})=0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{Q}}\exp\left(-bq_{d+2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\operatorname{dist}(q,\widetilde{Q})-\tau\right)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{Q}}\exp\left(-bq_{d+2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:ZandB}}}{{=}}e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\eta}}Z_{\widetilde{Q}}.

Case II:
When
q\notin\widetilde{Q}
, we denote
r(q)=\operatorname{dist}(q,\widetilde{Q})
and
w_{\eta,\tau}(r)=\exp\left(-\frac{(r-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)
. Using the co-area formula, we write

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{Q}^{C}}\exp\left(-bq_{d+2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\operatorname{dist}(q,\widetilde{Q})-\tau\right)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(r-\tau)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\left(\int_{\partial\widetilde{Q}_{r}}\exp\left(-bq_{d+2}\right)\mathrm{d}S^{d+1}(q)\right)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}B_{\widetilde{Q}_{r}}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)\mathrm{d}r.

We apply the crucial bound in Lemma
D.6
to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}B_{\widetilde{Q}_{r}}w_{\eta,\tau}(r)\mathrm{d}r\leq Z_{\widetilde{Q}}\left(\widetilde{C}_{L}\sqrt{2\pi\eta}\;\exp\left(\tau\widetilde{C}_{L}+\frac{\eta\widetilde{C}_{L}^{2}}{2}\right)+\exp(\tau\widetilde{C}_{L})-1\right).

Combining Case I, Case II and the fact that
e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\eta}}\leq 1
yields the desired bound.

Per Lemma

and

\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c):=\widetilde{Q}\cap cr_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)

Lemma D.8

Assume condition (B2) on
f
and
h
. Fix any
c>1
and define

\mathrm{B}_{c,\mathrm{loc}}=cr_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q),\qquad\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)=\widetilde{Q}\cap\mathrm{B}_{c,\mathrm{loc}},

where
q
and
r_{\mathrm{loc}}
are defined at (
23
). Recall the notations
\widetilde{C}_{h}=\sqrt{L_{h}^{2}+a^{2}},\widetilde{C}_{f}=\sqrt{L_{f}^{2}+a^{2}+b^{2}}
at (
19
) and set

\mu=\min\left\{\frac{a}{\widetilde{C}_{h}},\ \frac{b}{\widetilde{C}_{f}}\right\},\quad\lambda=2+\frac{a}{b},\quad\kappa=\frac{c-1}{\lambda+\mu}.

Moreover, define

\bar{p}=p_{*}+\left(0,\ \Delta s,\ \frac{a}{b}\Delta s+\Delta t\right),\qquad\Delta s=\Delta t=\kappa r_{\mathrm{loc}}.

Then we have the inclusion
B\left(\bar{p},\ \kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\right)\subseteq\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
. In particular,

\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))\geq 2\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma(c):=\frac{cr_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))}\leq\frac{c(\lambda+\mu)}{(c-1)\mu}.

Proof
: Let us provide a sketch of the three steps of the proof.
To control
\gamma(c)=\frac{cr_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))}
, it suffices to lower bound

\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))
, which we do by constructing an inscribed ball inside

\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
.

Step 1 constructs a nontrivial ball
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}
contained in
\widetilde{Q}
.
A subtlety is that Lemma
D.1
only guarantees
p_{*}\in\widetilde{Q}\cap r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)

and gives no quantitative slack for the constraints at
p_{*}
; in particular,
p_{*}
may lie on or arbitrarily close to

\partial\widetilde{Q}
, so a ball centered at
p_{*}
and inside
\widetilde{Q}
need not exist.
We therefore shift
p_{*}
to

\bar{p}=p_{*}+\left(0,\Delta s,\frac{a}{b}\Delta s+\Delta t\right)
with
\Delta s=\Delta t=\kappa r_{\mathrm{loc}}
,
which creates slack for both constraints: increasing
s
relaxes
h(x)\leq as
, while increasing
t
by

\frac{a}{b}\Delta s+\Delta t
restores and adds slack for
f(x)+as\leq bt
.
The Lipschitz bounds on
f
and
h
then imply that all points sufficiently close to
\bar{p}
remain feasible,
yielding
\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}
. Thus,
\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}(\bar{p})
is the inscribed ball
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}
in
\widetilde{Q}
that we need. Note that this construction is nontrivial only when
c>1
, since then
\kappa=\frac{c-1}{\lambda+\mu}>0
.

Step 2 shows that
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}=\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})
also lies in
cr_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)
and hence in
\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
(by combining with Step 1).
Using
\|p_{*}-q\|\leq r_{\mathrm{loc}}
and

\|\bar{p}-p_{*}\|=\Delta s+\left|\frac{a}{b}\Delta s+\Delta t\right|=\kappa\lambda r_{\mathrm{loc}}
,
the triangle inequality gives

\|\bar{p}-q\|+\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\leq r_{\mathrm{loc}}+\kappa(\lambda+\mu)r_{\mathrm{loc}}=cr_{\mathrm{loc}}
. The last estimate implies
\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq cr_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)
.

Step 3 uses the inclusion
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}=\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
to lower bound
\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))
and finally upper bound
\gamma(c)
.

Step 1:
Showing the nontrivial ball
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}:=\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}
for
c>1
.

Set

g_{1}(p)=g_{1}(x,s,t)=h(x)-as,\qquad g_{2}(p)=g_{2}(x,s,t)=f(x)+as-bt,

so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}=\{p:\ g_{1}(p)\leq 0,\ g_{2}(p)\leq 0\}.

(103)

Because
h
is
L_{h}
-Lipschitz and
f
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz, for all
p=(x,s,t)
and
p^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle|g_{1}(p)-g_{1}(p^{\prime})|\leq L_{h}\|x-x^{\prime}\|+a|s-s^{\prime}|\leq\sqrt{L_{h}^{2}+a^{2}}\|p-p^{\prime}\|=\widetilde{C}_{h}\|p-p^{\prime}\|,

(104)

and similarly
|g_{2}(p)-g_{2}(p^{\prime})|\leq\widetilde{C}_{f}\|p-p^{\prime}\|
.

Since
p_{*}\in\widetilde{Q}
, we have
g_{1}(p_{*})\leq 0
and
g_{2}(p_{*})\leq 0
.
Define
\bar{p}
as in the statement of the lemma then

\displaystyle g_{1}(\bar{p})=g_{1}(p_{*})-a\Delta s\leq-a\Delta s,\qquad g_{2}(\bar{p})=g_{2}(p_{*})-b\Delta t\leq-b\Delta t.

(105)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}=\min\left\{\frac{a\Delta s}{\widetilde{C}_{h}},\ \frac{b\Delta t}{\widetilde{C}_{f}}\right\}.

(106)

Then for any
\zeta
with
\|\zeta\|\leq\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}
, by (
104
), (
105
), (
106
), we can write

\displaystyle g_{1}(\bar{p}+\zeta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dolif:consequencelipschitz}}}{{\leq}}g_{1}(\bar{p})+\widetilde{C}_{h}\|\zeta\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dolif:compareg1g2}}}{{\leq}}(-a\Delta s)+\widetilde{C}_{h}\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dolif:compareradiusincribedball}}}{{\leq}}0,

\displaystyle g_{2}(\bar{p}+\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq(-b\Delta t)+\widetilde{C}_{f}\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\leq 0.

Thus, in view of (
103
), we conclude
\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}
. Notice in particular that the inscribed ball
\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})
is non-trivial only if
\kappa=\frac{c-1}{\lambda+\mu}>0
, and for that we require

\displaystyle c>1.

(107)

Step 2:
Showing
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}=\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
.

Next, since
\|p_{*}-q\|\leq\|p_{k-1}-q\|=r_{\mathrm{loc}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{p}-q\|\leq\|p_{*}-q\|+\|\bar{p}-p_{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq r_{\mathrm{loc}}+\left(\Delta s+\left|\frac{a}{b}\Delta s+\Delta t\right|\right)

\displaystyle=r_{\mathrm{loc}}+\left(\left(1+\frac{a}{b}\right)\Delta s+\Delta t\right)=r_{\mathrm{loc}}+\kappa\lambda r_{\mathrm{loc}},

which combined with
\kappa=\frac{c-1}{\lambda+\mu}
leads to

\displaystyle\|\bar{p}-q\|+\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\leq r_{\mathrm{loc}}+\kappa(\lambda+\mu)r_{\mathrm{loc}}=r_{\mathrm{loc}}+(c-1)r_{\mathrm{loc}}=cr_{\mathrm{loc}}.

(108)

Take any point
p\in\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})
, then we get
\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert p-\bar{p}\right\rVert+\left\lVert q-\bar{p}\right\rVert\leq\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}+\|q-\bar{p}\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dolif:inequalityconnectcenters}}}{{\leq}}cr_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
This implies
\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq cr_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(q)=\mathrm{B}_{c,\mathrm{loc}}
. Combining with Step 1 to get

\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}\cap\mathrm{B}_{c,\mathrm{loc}}=\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
.

Step 3:
Finally, since
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{in}}=\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}\mathrm{B}_{d+2}(\bar{p})\subseteq\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)
per Step 2, we have
\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))\geq 2\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}
,
so that

\gamma(c)=\frac{cr_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\mbox{minwidth}(\widetilde{Q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(c))}\leq\frac{cr_{\mathrm{loc}}}{2\kappa\mu r_{\mathrm{loc}}}=\frac{c(\lambda+\mu)}{2(c-1)\mu}\leq\frac{c(\lambda+\mu)}{(c-1)\mu}.

In the upcoming result, via Theorem

, we describe the oracle complexities of solving the minimization problem

D.5
Sampling
p\sim\exp\left(-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(p)\right)
in Algorithm

This part is similar to Appendix
C.5
. Recall
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
is defined at (
24
) and
\tilde{p}=(\tilde{x},\tilde{s},\tilde{t})
is defined in Algorithm

By completing the square, one can easily see that sampling

is equivalent to

\displaystyle p\sim\Lambda(p)\propto\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(\left\lVert p-\tilde{p}\right\rVert-\sqrt{\frac{2\eta}{d+2}}\right)^{2}\right).

While

is not a Gaussian density, generating

is straightforward since it can be turned into a one-dimensional sampling problem. We state here a generic procedure for this sampling problem.

Algorithm 6

Sample
p\sim\Lambda(p)

1. Generate
W\sim{\cal N}(0,I_{d+2})
and set
\theta=W/\|W\|
;

2. Generate
r\propto r^{d+1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(r-\sqrt{\frac{2\eta}{d+2}}\right)^{2}\right)
by Adaptive Rejection Sampling for one-dimensional log-concave distribution by
[
20
]
.

3. Output
p=\tilde{p}+r\theta
.

A note on codimension 2 2 spun embedding
Sneha Banerjee, Shital Lawande, Subhadeep Rana, Kuldeep Saha

Abstract
. We prove that if a closed manifold B B is a connected component of the binding of an open book decomposition of a manifold M M , then every open book decomposition of B B spun embeds in M M . As an application, we prove that every open book decomposition of a simply connected spin 5 5 -manifold spun embeds in 𝕊 7 \mathbb{S}^{7} and every 3 3 -dimensional open book spun embeds in 𝕊 5 \mathbb{S}^{5} . We also define a notion of spun embedding for Morse open books.



1.
Introduction

An open book decomposition of a manifold
M^{m}
is a pair
(V^{m-1},h)
, such that
M^{m}
is diffeomorphic to the quotient space
\mathcal{MT}(V^{m-1},h)\cup_{id}\partial V^{m-1}\times D^{2}
. Here,
V^{m-1}
is called the
page
and
\partial V
is called the
binding
of the open book. The map
h
, called
monodromy
, is a diffeomorphism of
V^{m-1}
that restricts to identity near the boundary
\partial V
, and
\mathcal{MT}(V^{m-1},h)
denotes the mapping torus of
h
. We denote the total space of such an open book by
\textrm{OB}(V,h)
.

An embedding
f
of
\textrm{OB}(V_{1},h_{1})
in
\textrm{OB}(V_{2},h_{2})
is called a
spun embedding
(or an
open book embedding
), if
f
takes the page
V_{1}
to the page
V_{2}
and
f
is compatible with the monodromies :
h_{2}\circ f=f\circ h_{1}
, up to isotopy. We say
M^{n}
spun embeds in
N^{n+k}
if there exists an open book decomposition of
M
that spun embeds in an open book decomposition of
N
. In recent times, the study of codimension
2

spun embedding
(or
open book embedding
) has attracted much attention (eg.
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
12
]
). The notion of spun embedding of an open book decomposition is strictly stronger than ordinary smooth embedding. Obstructions to spun embedding an open book in a given open book has been discussed in
[
12
]
. Etnyre and Lekili
[
4
]
gave the first constructions of codimension
2
spun embeddings of
3
-manifolds by embedding pages as fibers in a Lefschetz fibration. Later, an alternate construction of spun embedding was given by Pancholi, Pandit and the fourth author
[
14
]
, based on the observation that there is a proper embedding of a annulus
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{D}^{4}
such that, the Dehn twist along the core of
\mathcal{A}
is induced by an isotopy of
\mathbb{D}^{4}
, relative to the boundary. This construction, called the
annulus trick
, was later adapted to the setting of non-orientable
3
-manifolds by Ghanawat, Pandit and Selvakumar
[
5
]
. In this note, we observe a generalization of this construction and give some applications.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a closed orientable
n
-manifold with an open book decomposition
\textrm{OB}(P^{n-1},\rho)
. Let
B^{n-2}
be a connceted component of the binding
\partial P
and let
\textrm{OB}(V_{B},\phi_{B})
be an open book decomposition of
B
. Then,
\textrm{OB}(V_{B},\phi_{B}^{k})
spun embeds in
\textrm{OB}(P^{n-1},\rho)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Here,
\phi_{B}^{k}
denotes the
k
-times composition of
\phi_{B}
. An immediate corollary of Theorem
1.1
is the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

Every open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
spun embeds in
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1},\textrm{id})
for
n\geq 4
.

More generally, one can think of any codimension
2
fibered submanifold of
\mathbb{S}^{m}
. In particular, every
(2n-1)
-dimensional Brieskorn manifold appears as the binding of an open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
.

Let
Q^{5}
be a simply connected spin manifold. Kwon and Van Koert (Corollary
3.9
in
[
10
]
) have shown that every simply connected spin
5
-manifold is a connected sum of Brieskorn manifolds. Thus,
M=B_{1}\#B_{2}\#\cdots\#B_{l}
for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, where the
B_{i}
s are
5
-dimensional Brieskorn manifolds. Let
\textrm{OB}(V_{i},\phi_{i})
be an open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{7}
with binding
\partial V_{i}=B_{i}
. Then,
\textrm{OB}(V_{1}\#_{b}V_{2}\#_{b}\cdots\#_{b}V_{l},\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{l})=\textrm{OB}(V_{1},\phi_{1})\#\textrm{OB}(V_{2},\phi_{2})\#\cdots\#\textrm{OB}(V_{l},\phi_{l})
(see section
2.4
in
[
2
]
) is an open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{7}
with binding
Q^{5}
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
implies the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
Q
be a closed simply connected spin
5
-manifold. Then, every open book decomposition of
Q
spun embeds in
\mathbb{S}^{7}
with a fixed page bounded by
Q
.

Saeki (Theorem
6.1
in
[
15
]
) has shown that every closed oriented
3
-manifold can be realized as the connected binding of an open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{5}
. Together with Theorem
1.1
this implies the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M^{3}
be a closed orientable
3
-manifold. Then, every open book decomposition of
M^{3}
spun embeds in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
with a fixed page bounded by
M
.

We note that an analogue of
1.3
and
1.4
for contact manifolds can not hold. It is known by the work of Thurston–Winkelenkemper and Giroux that every contact structure on a
3
-manifold corresponds to an open book decomposition. By Kasuya
[
11
]
, a contact manifold
(M,\zeta)
admits a contact embedding in a contact
\mathbb{S}^{5}
only if its first Chern class
c_{1}(\zeta)
vanishes. However, in general, it is not true that every spun embedding of a
3
-manifold in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
corresponds to a contact embedding of the corresponding contact
3
-manifold. In particular, many of the spun embeddings given by Corollary
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
are examples of codimension
2
proper embeddings (embedding between pages of open books) which are not isotopic to a symplectic embedding.

It is not known yet if there exists a fixed open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{5}
where all
3
-dimensional open books admit spun embedding. We call such an open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{5}

universal
. A recent work (Theorem
1.4
in
[
12
]
) by the second and the fourth author shows that none of Saeki’s open books (given by Theorem
6.1
in
[
15
]
) can be universal.

Theorem
1.1
is proved using a simple generalization of the
Hopf annulus trick
(see section
2.5
), which we call the
page trick
. In section
4
, we discuss some more applications of the page trick to construct spun embeddings. In particular, the recent finding of various new open book decompositions by Hsueh
[
9
]
, and a generalization of the notion of plumbing by Ozbagci and Popescu-Pampu
[
13
]
give us a large class of novel examples where the page trick can be applied.

Finally, in section
5
, we discuss a notion of spun embedding for
Morse open books
and describe a version of the page trick in this context (see Proposition
5.2
and Example
5.1
).

Unless stated otherwise, we always work in the category of smooth manifolds.

2.
preliminaries

2.1.
Open books and embeddings

An open book decomposition of a closed
(2n+1)
-manifold
M
consists of a codimension
2
closed submanifold
B
and a fibration map
\pi:M\setminus B\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
, such that in a tubular neighborhood of
B\subset M
, the restriction map
\pi:B\times(\mathbb{D}^{2}\setminus\{0\})\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
is given by
(b,r,\theta)\mapsto\theta
. The fibration
\pi
determines a unique fiber manifold
N^{2n}
whose boundary is
B
. The closure
\bar{N}
is called the
page
and
B
is called the
binding
. The monodromy of the fibration map
\pi
determines a diffeomorphism
\phi
of
\bar{N}
such that
\phi
is identity near the boundary
\partial\bar{N}
. In particular,
M=\mathcal{MT}(\bar{N},\phi)\cup_{id,\partial}\partial\bar{N}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
. We denote such an open book decomposition of
M
by
\textrm{OB}(\bar{N},\phi)
. The map
\phi
is called the
monodromy
of the open book. This description of an open book decomposition in terms of its page and monodromy is known as an
abstract open book
. However, these two notions are equivalent and we will always describe an open book decomposition of a manifold as an abstarct open book. Two open book decompositions with the same page are
equivalent
if their monodromies are isotopic, relative to a collar neighborhood of the binding in a page.

Definition 2.1

(Spun embedding)
.

We say,
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma_{1},\phi_{1})

spun embeds
in
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma_{2},\phi_{2})
, if there exists a proper embedding
g:(\Sigma_{1},\partial\Sigma_{1})\rightarrow(\Sigma_{2},\partial\Sigma_{2})
such that
g\circ\phi_{1}
is isotopic to
\phi_{2}\circ g
, relative to the boundary. A spun embedding is also known as an
open book embedding
in the literature.

By embedding
\partial\Sigma_{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
in
\partial\Sigma_{2}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
via the map
(b,(r,\theta))\mapsto(g(b),(r,\theta))
, we get a genuine embedding between the total spaces of the open books.

2.2.
Seifert type embedding

Let
\Sigma^{n-1}
be an oriented compact submanifold of an oriented closed manifold
M^{n}
with non empty boundary
\partial\Sigma
. Consider the product manifold
M\times[a,b]
(
a<b)
. We construct a proper embedding
f
of
\Sigma
in
M\times[a,b]
in the following way. We isotope the interior of
\Sigma\times\{a\}\subset M\times\{a\}
into the interior of
M\times[a,b]
, fixing the boundary
\partial\Sigma\times\{a\}
pointwise. The resulting embedding
f
of
\Sigma
is such that
f(\Sigma)\cap(M\times\{b\})=\Sigma\times\{b\}
and
f(\Sigma)\cap(M\times\{(1-t)a+tb\})=\partial\Sigma\times\{(1-t)a+tb\}
for all
t\in[0,1)
. See Figure
1
. We call
f
a
Seifert type embedding
of
\Sigma
in
M\times[a,b]
.

\Sigma\subset M\times\{0\}
to a Seifert type embedding in
M\times[0,1]
.

2.3.
Isotopy via the flow of an open book

Given an open book decomposition
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma,\phi)
of a manifold
M
, there is a flow on
M
whose time-
1
map takes a page to itself with diffeomorphism
\phi
. The vector field corresponding to this flow is transverse to the interior of each page. In a neighborhood of the binding,
(\partial\Sigma\times\mathbb{D}^{2},(x,r,\theta))
, it is given by
r\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
. In particular, the flow fixes the binding pointwise. Thus, there is an isotopy
\psi_{t}
(
t\in[0,1]
) of
M
such that
\psi_{0}=\textrm{id}_{M}
and
\psi_{1}|\Sigma=\iota\circ\phi
, where
\iota
is an embedding of
\Sigma
in
M
as a page of the open book.

2.4.
The annulus trick

A Hopf annulus is an embedding of an annulus in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
with boundary a Hopf link. A Hopf annulus is called positive (or negative) if the linking number of its boundary is
1
(or
-1
). Let
\tau_{c}
denote the Dehn twist along the core curve of an annulus.

Figure 2.
Hopf annuli

Proposition 2.2

(The Hopf annulus trick
[
7
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

There exists a Seifert type embedding
f
of an annulus in
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[-1,1]
such that there exists an isotopy
\Gamma_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) of
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,1]
, relative to boundary, such that
\Gamma_{0}=\textrm{id}
and
\Gamma_{1}\circ f=f\circ\tau_{c}
.

The main idea to prove Proposition
2.2
is to observe that
\mathbb{S}^{3}
admits an open book decomposition with page a Hopf annulus
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
and monodromy a Dehn twist along the core curve
c
of
\mathcal{A}
. Consider a Seifert type embedding
f
of an annulus in
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,1]
corresponding to
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Let
\Psi_{t}
be the isotopy of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
such that
\Psi_{1}
realizes the Dehn twist monodromy on
\mathcal{A}
via the flow of the open book
\textrm{OB}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{c})
. One can then define an isotopy
\Gamma_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) of
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[-1,1]
such
\Gamma_{0}=\textrm{id}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[-1,1]}
and
\Gamma_{1}|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\{0\}}=\Psi_{1}.

\Gamma_{s}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Psi_{s(1-t)}(x)&\mbox{if }t\geq 0\\
\Psi_{s(t+1)}(x)&\mbox{if }t\leq 0\end{array}\right.

It is now easy to see that
\Gamma_{1}\circ f=f\circ\tau_{c}
. By applying
\Gamma_{1}
repeatedly on
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[-1,1]
, one can induce the diffeomorphism
\tau_{c}^{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.5.
A generalization of the annulus trick

The Hopf annulus trick has a straightforward generalization for any open book decomposition. Let
N^{n}=\textrm{OB}(V^{n-1},\phi)
. Consider the Seifert type embedding
h
of
V
in
N\times[-1,1]
corresponding to the embedding of
V
in
N
as a page of the open book. We can follow the proof of the Hopf annulus trick (described in the previous section) verbatim by replacing
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathcal{A}
and
\tau_{c}
with
N
,
V
and
\phi
, respectively. Thus, we get an isotopy
H_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) of
N\times[-1,1]
such that
H_{0}=\textrm{id}_{N\times[0,1]}
and
H_{1}\circ h=h\circ\phi
. Similar to the previous case, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the diffeomorphism
\phi^{k}
can be induced by a repeated application of
H_{1}
.

Proposition 2.3

(The page trick)
.

There exists a Seifert type embedding
h
of
V
in
N\times[-1,1]
such that there is an isotopy
\tilde{\Gamma}_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) of
N\times[-1,1]
, relative to boundary, such that
\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}=\textrm{id}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}\circ h=h\circ\phi
.

If
N
is a boundary component of an oriented manifold
W^{n+1}
, then we can extend
\Gamma_{1}
to a diffeomorphism of
W
via the identity map, and
\Gamma_{1}
is also isotopic to identity, where the isotopy is supported in a collar neigborhood of
N
.

Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Recall that
M^{n}=\textrm{OB}(P^{n-1},\rho)
, where
\rho
restricts to the identity map on a collar neighborhood
\mathcal{C}(B)
of
B=\partial P
in
P
. Let us identify this collar neighborhood with
B\times[-1,1]
such that
B\times\{-1\}=\partial P
. Consider an open book decomposition
\textrm{OB}(V_{B},\phi_{B})
of
B
and let
f
be the Seifert type embedding of
V_{B}
in
B\times[-1,1]
such that
f(\partial V_{B})\subset B\times\{-1\}
. This gives a proper embedding of
V_{B}
in
P
. Given an integer
k
, by Proposition
2.3
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\Gamma_{k}
of
P
with the following properties.

(1)

\Gamma_{k}
is supported on
\mathcal{C}(B)
and
\Gamma_{k}
is isotopic to the identity map, relative to
P\setminus\mathcal{C}(B)
.

(2)

\Gamma_{k}\circ f=f\circ\phi_{B}^{k}
.

Since
\rho|_{\mathcal{C}(B)}=\textrm{id}_{P}
,
\textrm{OB}(V_{B},\phi^{k}_{B})
spun embeds in
\textrm{OB}(P,\rho)
.

∎

4.
The page trick in a more general setup

Let
M^{n}
be a component of the binding of on open book
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma,\phi)
. Let
\textrm{OB}(P_{1}^{n-1},\rho_{1})
and
\textrm{OB}(P_{2}^{n-1},\rho_{2})
be two open book decompositions of a closed oriented manifold
M^{n}
. Let
g_{i}
denote the embedding of
P_{i}
in
M
as a fixed page of the open books for
i=1,2
. Let
V
be a compact bounded submanifold of
M
such that
g_{1}(P_{1})\cup g_{2}(P_{2})
is a submanifold of
V
.

Lemma 4.1
.

\textrm{OB}(V,\eta)
spun embeds in
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma,\phi)
for any
\eta\in\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(V)
.

.

The
5
-sphere admits an open book decomposition with page the disk cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and monodromy a Dehn-seidel twist :
\textrm{OB}(DT^{*}\mathbb{S}^{2},\tau_{2})
(see
[

]
). Another open book on

has page is given by
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{3}\#_{b}\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,1],\sigma)
, where

is the

map (see
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
). Let
g_{1}
and

, for any diffeomorphism

.

5.
The page trick for Morse open books

A
Morse open book
is similar to an open book
(M^{n},B^{n-1},\pi)
, except that the fibration map
\pi:M\setminus B\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
may have singular fibers, with singularities in the interiror of a fiber.

Let
K^{n-2}
be a closed oriented submanifold of a closed oriented manifold
L^{n}
with trivial normal bundle and let
K
be nullhomologous in
L
. Then, there exists a hypersurface
V^{n-1}\subset L
such that
\partial V=K
. Given such a
Seifert surface

V
, there exists a Morse open book on
L
with a regular page
V
(see remark
9.6
in
[
13
]
). In particular,
L\setminus V
can be seen as a relative cobordism
W^{n}
from
V
to itself. Let
\psi
be the monodromy map of the regular page
V
. Let
h:W^{n}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a Morse function with
h^{-1}(0)=h^{-1}(1)=V
and
h
has critical points only in the interior of
W
. According to Milnor’s terminology,
h
is a Morse function on the
triad

(W,h^{-1}(0),h^{-1}(1))
. The monodromy
\psi
is then a diffeomorphism (relative to boundary) between
h^{-1}(0)
and
h^{-1}(1)
. In this case, the analogue of the mapping torus of an open book is the quotient space
\mathcal{M}(W,h,\psi)=\frac{W}{x\sim\psi(x)}
. Thus, the triple
(W,h,\psi)
characterizes a Morse open book structure on
L
.

Definition 5.1

(spun embedding for Morse open books)
.

Let
L_{1}^{n}
and
L_{2}^{n+k}
be two closed oriented manifolds. Let
(W_{1},h_{1},\psi_{1})
and
(W_{2},h_{2},\psi_{2})
be Morse open book decompositions of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, respectively. We say
(W_{1},h_{1},\psi_{1})
spun embeds in
(W_{2},h_{2},\psi_{2})
if there exists an embedding
\iota
of
L_{1}
in
L_{2}
such that the followings hold.

(1)

h_{2}\circ\iota
restricts to a Morse function on the triad
(W_{1},h_{1}^{-1}(0),h_{1}^{-1}(1))
.

(2)

The embedding
\iota
takes a regular page of
h_{1}
to a regular page of
h_{2}
and
\iota\circ\psi_{1}|_{h_{1}^{-1}(1)}=\psi_{2}\circ\iota|_{h_{1}^{-1}(1)}
.

Let
(W,h,\phi)
be a Morse open book decomposition of a closed oriented manifold
N
. We take a Seifert type embedding of a regular page
V
(i.e. a regualr level set of
h
) in
N\times[-1,0]\subset N\times[-1,1]
such that
\partial\bar{V}\subset N\times\{-1\}
. Let
f
be the inclusion map of
\mathcal{M}(W,h,\psi)
in
N\setminus V
. Let
f_{t}=f|_{h^{-1}(t)}
for
t\in[0,1]
. Note that restricted to the regular page
V
,
f_{1}=f_{0}\circ\phi
. We now define a proper embedding
F
of
W
in
N\times[-1,1]\times[0,1]
. We first embed
W
in
N\times\{0\}\times[0,1]
by an embedding
\hat{f}
such that
\hat{f}(x)=(f_{t}(x),0,t)
for
x\in h^{-1}(t)
. Since
f_{t}|_{\partial\bar{V}}=\textrm{id}_{\partial\bar{V}}
for all
t\in[0,1]
, we can extend this to a proper embedding by adding the cylindrical region
\partial\bar{V}\times[-1,0]\times\{t\}\subset N\times[-1,1]\times[0,1]
. We summerize the discussion above in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(W,h,\phi)
be a Morse open book decomposition of a closed oriented manifold
N
. Then, there exists an embedding
F:W\rightarrow N\times[-1,1]\times[0,1]
with the following property.

(1)

The projection map
N\times[-1,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
restricts to a Morse function on the triad
(F(W),F(h^{-1}(0)),F(h^{-1}(1)))
, and

(2)

F\circ\phi|_{h^{-1}(1)}=F|_{h^{-1}(0)}
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
K^{n-2}
be a closed oriented submanifold of a closed oriented manifold
L^{n}
with trivial normal bundle and let
K
be nullhomologous in
L
. Thus, there exists a Morse open book
(W,h,\phi)
on
L
, such that the closure of
L\setminus K
is the relative cobordism
W
between a regular page
V
to itself and the monodromy or the return map is
\phi
. We partition the trivial mapping cylinder
(L\times[-1,1])\times[0,k]
in
k
parts :
L\times[-1,1]\times[i,i+1]
(
0\leq i\leq k-1
). By Proposition
5.2
, we can embed a copy of
W
in
L\times[-1,1]\times[i,i+1]
via an embedding
F_{i}
such that the projection map
L\times[-1,1]\times[i,i+1]\rightarrow[i,i+1]
restricts to a Morse function on the triad
(F_{i}(W),F_{i}(h^{-1}(0)),F_{i}(h^{-1}(1)))
, and
F_{i}\circ\phi|_{h^{-1}(1)}=F_{i}|_{h^{-1}(0)}
. By concatenating
F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{k-1}
, we get a spun embedding of a Morse open book
(W_{k},h_{k},\phi^{k})
in
\textrm{OB}(L\times[-1,1],\textrm{id})=L\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, where
W_{k}
is a relative cobordism obtained by concatenating
k
copies of
W
and
h_{k}
is the corresponding Morse function given by
h\ast h\ast\cdots\ast h
(
k
times). By construction, the total space
\widetilde{L}
of the Morse open book
(W_{k},h_{k},\phi^{k})
is a
k
-fold cyclic branched cover of
L
branched along
K
.

Abstract
Let { Z m , m ≥ 0 } \{Z_{m},m\geq 0\} be a critical branching process in random environment and { S m , m ≥ 0 } \{S_{m},m\geq 0\} be its associated random walk. Assuming that the increments distribution of the associated random walk belongs without centering to the domain of attraction of an α \alpha -stable law we prove conditional limit theorems describing, as n → ∞ n\rightarrow\infty , the distribution the number of particles in the process { Z m , 0 ≤ m ≤ n } \{Z_{m},0\leq m\leq n\} given Z n > 0 Z_{n}>0 and S n ≤ c ​ o ​ n ​ s ​ t S_{n}\leq const . Key words : stable random walks, branching processes, survival probability, extreme random environment AMS subject classification : Primary 60G50, Secondary 60J80, 60K37

Introduction and main results

We study the population size of a critical branching process evolving in an
unfavorable random environment. To describe the problems we plan to consider
denote by
\mathfrak{F}

=\left\{\mathfrak{f}\right\}
the space of all
probability measures on the set
\mathbb{N}:\mathbb{=}\{1,2,...\}
and put
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\left\{0\right\}\cup\mathbb{N}
. For notational reason,
we identify a measure
\mathfrak{f}=\left\{\mathfrak{f}(\left\{0\right\}),\mathfrak{f}(\left\{1\right\}),...\right\}\in

\mathfrak{F}
with the
respective probability generating function

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{f}(\left\{k\right\})z^{k},\quad z\in[0,1],

and make no difference between
\mathfrak{f}
and
f
. Equipped with the
metric of total variation,
\mathfrak{F}

=\left\{\mathfrak{f}\right\}=\left\{f\right\}
becomes a polish space. Let

F(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F\left(\left\{j\right\}\right)z^{j},\quad z\in[0,1],

be a random variable taking values in
\mathfrak{F}
, and let

F_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F_{n}\left(\left\{j\right\}\right)z^{j},\quad z\in[0,1],\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

be a sequence of independent probabilistic copies of
F
. The infinite
sequence
\mathcal{E}=\left\{F_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is called a
random environment.

A sequence of nonnegative random variables
\mathcal{\{}Z_{0},Z_{1},Z_{2},...\}
specified on a probability space

is called a branching process in random environment

is independent of
\mathcal{E}
and given
\mathcal{E}
the process

is a Markov chain with

\mathcal{L}\left(Z_{n}|Z_{n-1}=k_{n-1},\mathcal{E}=(f_{1},f_{2},...)\right)=\mathcal{L}(\xi_{n1}+\ldots+\xi_{nk_{n-1}})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f_{1},f_{2},...\in\mathfrak{F}
, where
\xi_{n1},\xi_{n2},\ldots
is a
sequence of i.i.d. random variables with distribution
f_{n}
. Given
\mathcal{E}=\left\{F_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
the sequence

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{n}=X_{1}+...+X_{n},\ n\geq 1,

where
X_{i}=\log F_{i}^{\prime}(1),i=1,2,...,
is called the associated
random walk for the brancning process in random environment. Under our
assumptions
\left\{X_{i},i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is a sequence of i.i.d.
random variables.

Let

A:=\{\alpha\in(0,2)\backslash\{1\},\,|\beta|<1\}\cup\{\alpha=1,\beta=0\}\cup\{\alpha=2,\beta=0\}

be a subset in
\mathbb{R}^{2}.
For

and, in addition,
\mathbf{E}X=0

corresponding to

is not one-sided.

We impose of the following restrictions on the properties of the
random environment.

Condition B0.

The random variables
X_{n},n\in\mathbb{N},
are independent copies of a random variable
X\in D\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
whose distribution is under
\mathbf{P}
non-lattice

Sometimes we will need a stronger assumption:

Condition B1.

The random variables

are independent copies of a random variable
X\in D\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
whose distribution is under
\mathbf{P}
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on

1

\left\{\frac{S_{\left[nt\right]}}{a_{n}},t\geq 0\right\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}=\left\{Y_{t},t\geq 0\right\}

Our third assumption on the environment concerns reproduction laws

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\mathbf{E}\left[h_{\phi}(F_{0,v}(0),K-S_{v});L_{v}\geq 0\right].

(39)

Using this fact and summing (
39
) over
v
from
0
to
J

we get

\displaystyle G_{right}\left(K,\phi\right):=\lim_{J\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}\left[\phi\left(\mathcal{Y}^{\theta n,n}\right);\mathcal{R}(n,K),\tau_{n}\in[n-J+1,n\right]}{b_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad=g_{\alpha,\beta}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}U(y)dy\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(F_{0,v}(0)\in du,S_{v}\in dx,L_{v}\geq 0\right)h_{\phi}(u,K-x)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad=\int\phi(w)\lambda_{right}(dw),

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{right}(dw)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\alpha,\beta}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}U(y)dy

\displaystyle\times\mathbf{E}\left[\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}h_{\phi}(F_{0,v}(0),K-S_{v})\lambda_{S_{v},F_{0,v}(0)}^{\ast}(dw)\mathbf{I}\left\{L_{v}\geq 0\right\}\right]

with

\lambda_{x,u}^{\ast}(dw)=\int\int\Psi_{K-x}(w_{1},b,-y)\mathbf{P}_{y}^{+}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\infty}\in d\mathbf{w}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-}\left(B_{\infty}(u)\in db\right)\mu_{1}(dy)

and

\int\lambda_{right}(dw)=G_{right}(K).

(40)

Finally,
G_{right}(K,\phi)\leq G_{right}(K)<\infty
in view of (
38
).

Thus,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E[}\phi\left(\mathcal{Y}^{\theta n,n}\right);\mathcal{R}(n,K)]}{b_{n}}=G_{left}(K,\phi)+G_{right}(K,\phi),

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E[}\phi\left(\mathcal{Y}^{\theta n,n}\right)|\mathcal{R}(n,K)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{left}\left(K,\phi\right)+G_{right}\left(K,\phi\right)}{G_{left}(K)+G_{right}(K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi(w)\lambda(dw),

where
\lambda
is a measure on
D[0,1]
given by

\lambda(dw)=\frac{\lambda_{left}(dw)+\lambda_{right}(dw)}{G_{left}(K)+G_{right}(K)}.

It is not difficult to check that
\lambda(dw)
puts entire mass on
strictly positive constant functions and it is a probability measure in view
of (
34
) and (
40
).

Theorem
2
is proved.

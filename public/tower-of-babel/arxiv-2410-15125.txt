On the order of Brauer classes capturing Brauer-Manin obstruction
Mridul Biswas, Divyasree C Ramachandran, Biswanath Samanta

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth projective variety defined over a number field k k that has Brauer-Manin obstruction to the existence of rational points. We study the orders of the Brauer classes which capture the obstruction. We prove that one may require elements of arbitrarily large order to capture the obstruction. In other words, the exponent of the group which captures the obstruction has no upper bound.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically integral variety defined over a global field
k
. A central question in arithmetic geometry is whether
X
has a
k
-point. A necessary condition for the existence of such a point is that
X
has
k_{v}
-points for all places
v
of
k
. We know that
X(k_{v})\neq\emptyset
for all most all places
v
of
k

[
6
, Theorem 7.7.2]
, which allows us to check whether
X(k)\neq\emptyset
in a finite process. However, this condition is not sufficient for many varieties; it is possible for
X(k_{v})\neq\emptyset
for all places
v
of
k
while
X(k)=\emptyset
. This is referred to as the failure of the Hasse principle.

In 1970, Manin introduced a construction that explains the failure of the Hasse principle for all the varieties known at that time, which is now known as the Brauer–Manin obstruction. While there are counterexamples to the Hasse principle that cannot be captured by the Brauer–Manin obstruction
[
10
]
, Colliot-Thélène conjectured that for smooth projective geometrically integral rationally connected varieties, the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle. When it is known to be the only obstruction, this gives a finite procedure for checking the existence of rational points.

By functoriality of Brauer group, we have a pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\times\text{Br}X\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. The Brauer–Manin set is defined to be the set all adelic points orthogonal to
\text{Br}X
and is denoted by
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\text{Br}}
. We have
X(k)\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\text{Br}}\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
. Due to the compactness of
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\text{Br}}
, if
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\text{Br}}=\emptyset
, then this is captured by a finite collection of Brauer classes. In the literature, most of the examples of Brauer-Manin obstruction are captured by a single element of
\text{Br}X
. Recently, Jennifer Berg and their collaborators in an inspiring paper
[
1
]
, have shown that it may require arbitrarily many Brauer classes to capture the obstruction. This emphasises that
“… the examples in the literature are not representative of what
is theoretically possible.”

It is interesting to study the constraints on the order of elements capturing the obstruction. In particular, whether the
d
-primary part of the Brauer group can capture the obstruction has been examined in the literature
[
4
]
. For a cubic surface, it has been shown that
\text{Br}X[3]
completely captures the obstruction
[
8
]
. It has also been established that odd-order elements of the Brauer group do not obstruct the Hasse principle for certain types of Kummer varieties
[
7
]
. For a survey on this topic, refer to
[
9
]
.

Our main result focuses on the order of the elements that capture the obstruction. Since a single Brauer class captures the obstruction in most cases, we investigate whether there is a bound on the order of that class.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given an odd prime
p
and a number field
k
which contains a primitive
p
th root of unity, there exists a smooth projective geometrically integral rationally connected variety
X
over
k
which has Brauer-Manin obstruction, captured by a single Brauer class and no class of order less than
p
captures the obstruction.

We prove this theorem towards the end in section
5
and as a consequence, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

Given an odd prime
p
and a number field
k
which contains a primitive
p
th root of unity, there exists a smooth projective geometrically integral rationally connected variety
X
over
k
which has Brauer-Manin obstruction and no subgroup of exponent less than
p
captures the obstruction.

Throughout the paper,
k
is a number field that contains a primitive
p
th root of unity for a fixed odd prime
p
, unless stated otherwise. A variety
X
over
k
is considered ‘nice’ if it is smooth projective geometrically integral rationally connected. We begin this paper by recalling some key concepts related to Severi-Brauer varieties and Brauer-Manin obstruction in Section
2
. We then come up with a specific choice of a cyclic
p
-algebra associated to a unique Severi-Brauer bundle over a dense subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
. By choosing a smooth model we obtain a ‘nice’ variety over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
which has global rational points. In Section
4
, we ensure that the Brauer group of this variety is a non-trivial
p
-torsion group by base change to a Henselian local ring and using purity theorem. To eliminate global points while preserving local points in the variety, we use a pull-back method which first appeared in
[
2
]
and later generalized in
[
1
]
. This results in a local-global failure, captured by an element of order
p
, described in section
5
.
The proofs in section
5
are inspired by the work of Jennifer Berg and their collaborators
[
1
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

For any field
k
, let
\text{Br}\,k
denote its Brauer group. Let
A
be a central simple algebra over
k
. Then
A
is said to be split over

if the class of
A
in
\text{Br}\,k
is zero. Equivalently,
A
is split if and only if there exists an integer
m>0
such that
A\simeq M_{m}(k)
as
k
-algebras.

Given a central simple algebra
A
of degree
n
over an arbitrary field
k
, there exists an associated projective
k

of dimension
n-1
known as a Severi-Brauer variety associated with
A
.
The existence of a
k
-point in

is characterized below.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a central simple algebra of degree
n
over an arbitrary field
k
and
X
be an associated Severi-Brauer variety over
k
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

X
has
k
-rational point.

(b)

X
is isomorphic to the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
over
k
.

(c)

A
splits over
k
.

Proof.

The equivalence of statements (a) and (b) is a part of Chátelet’s theorem. The implication
(b)
if and only if
(c)
follows from the base-point preserving bijection of central simple algebras of degree
n
over
k
and Severi-Brauer varieties of dimension
n-1
over
k
as explained in
[
3
]
.
∎

Now we discuss an important class of central simple algebras known as cyclic algebras. These are generalizations of quaternion algebras to arbitrary degree. When the base field
k
contains a primitive
m
-th root of unity

, a degree

cyclic algebra has a nice presentation. Given any

, there exists a cyclic
m
-algebra denoted by

, which can be described as

\langle x,y\,|\,x^{m}=a,y^{m}=b,xy=\omega yx\rangle
.

We now present a useful result concerning cyclic algebras, whose proof is given in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
be a field containing a primitive
m
-th root of unity
\omega
and let
a,b\in k^{\times}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

The cyclic algebra
(a,b)_{\omega}
is split over
k
.

(b)

The element
b
is a norm from the field extension
k(\sqrt[m]{a})
.

(c)

The element
a
is a norm from the field extension
k(\sqrt[m]{b})
.

Now we recall some terminologies related to Brauer-Manin obstruction. When
k
is a global field, we denote the completion at each place
v
of

by

. For any scheme

, let

be the set of codimension 1 points. For

\kappa(X)
. For any scheme
X
over

\text{Br}X:=H^{2}_{et}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
, and its subgroup of constant classes as
\text{Br}_{0}\,X:=im(\text{Br}\,k\rightarrow\text{Br}X)
. We write
\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
for the quotient
\text{Br}X/\text{Br}_{0}\,X
.

Let

be a regular integral Noetherian scheme. Then the following sequence

0\rightarrow\text{Br}X\rightarrow\text{Br}\,\kappa(X)\rightarrow\displaystyle\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(1)}}H^{1}(\kappa(x),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})

is exact
[
6
, Theorem 6.8.3]
. This is known as the purity theorem of the Brauer group.

For
A\in\text{Br}X
, we have the following commutative diagram

For any subgroup

denote

We can define the Brauer–Manin pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\times\text{Br}\,X\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
by summing the local pairings, defined as

3.
Number Theoretic Results

We prove some number theoretic results, which are needed to construct our variety over
k
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k
be a number field and let
a\in k
be such that
a
is not a
p
th power in
k
. Then, there exists a place
\nu
of
k
such that
a
is not a
p
th power in
k_{\nu}
.

Proof.

It is a weaker version of the Grunwald–Wang theorem.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\zeta_{p}\in k
and
\nu
be a prime in
k
. Any finite extension
L
of
k
can contain only finitely many elements from the set
\{\sqrt[p]{\nu\ell}:\ell\text{ are primes in }k\}
.

Proof.

Since there are infinitely many primes in a number field
k
, let
\Omega
be any infinite set of primes in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
. Since
\zeta_{p}\in k
, by Kummer theory and the unique factorization of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
, we have
k(\sqrt[p]{\nu\ell}:\ell\in\Omega)
is an infinite extension of
k.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For any extension of number fields
L/k
, there exists infinitely many primes in
k
that splits completely in
L
.

Proof.

By replacing
L
with its Galois closure, assume
L/k
is Galois extension. Since
\{id\}
is stable under conjugation, we can apply Chebotarev’s density theorem to this set to obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
L/K
be an unramified extension of local fields of degree
n
with the norm map
N:L^{\times}\to K^{\times}
. Then, the valuation of the norm group
N(L^{\times})
is given by
v(N(L^{\times}))=n\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Since
L/K
is an unramified extension, we have
N(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times})=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
and
v(N(\pi_{L}))=n
. As
L^{\times}=\pi_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
where
\pi_{L}
is a uniformizer in
L
, and
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
is the unit group of the ring of integers of
L
. Taking the norm, we have
N(L^{\times})=N(\pi_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}})\times N(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times})=N(\pi_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}})\times\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. Hence,
v(N(L^{\times}))=n\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

Let

For any sufficiently divisible positive integer

.

Let
h\in k[x]
be an irreducible polynomial, and let

4.
Severi-Brauer bundles and Brauer Groups

From now on,
k
is a number field that contains a primitive
p
th root of unity for a fixed odd prime
p
. Given an element
a\in k^{\times}
and a polynomial
f(u)\in k[u]
, we describe the construction of a ‘nice’ variety over
k
. Note that, there is a one-one correspondence between
p
th power free homogeneous polynomials
F\in k[u_{0},u_{1}]
whose degrees are a multiple of
p
and
p
th power-free polynomials
f\in k[u]
given by

F(u_{0},u_{1})\mapsto F(u,1)

and

f(u)\mapsto f\left(\frac{u_{0}}{u_{1}}\right)u_{1}^{p\lceil\deg f/p\rceil}.

Construction 4.1
.

Consider the cyclic algebra
(a,f)\in\text{Br}(k(u))
of degree
p
. By purity theorem, there exists an open set U
\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
such that
(a,f)\in\text{Br}\,U
. We have
U=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\setminus V(F)
, where
F
is the homogeneous polynomial corresponding to
f
. Let
X_{U}
represent the family of Severi-Brauer varieties over
U
associated with the algebra
(a,f)
. Using Hironaka’s theorem over
k
, choose a smooth projective model for
X_{U}\to U
denoted by
\pi:X_{a,f}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. Note that its generic fiber corresponds to the Severi-Brauer variety associated with
(a,f)
, which uniquely determines
\pi:X_{a,f}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
up to birationality. Since a Severi-Brauer variety is geometrically integral rationally connected, we observe that
X_{a,f}
is a ‘nice’ variety over
k
.

Now, we examine some properties of the Brauer group of
X_{a,f}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f=f_{1}\dots f_{r}
with

, where each
f_{i}
is a distinct irreducible separable polynomial, and

is not a
p
th power in
k[u]/\langle f_{i}(u)\rangle
for any

. Let
F\in k[u_{0},u_{1}]
be the homogeneous polynomial corresponding to

. Consider the variety
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, where

. We have

(a)

\text{Br}\,k\rightarrow\text{Br}X
is injective.

(b)

\overline{\text{Br}}X
is

-torsion.

(c)

Under the map
\pi^{*}:\text{Br}\,\kappa(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k})\rightarrow\text{Br}\,\kappa(X)
,
(a,f_{i})
lands in
\text{Br}X

.

(d)

\pi^{*}(a,f_{i})

Proof.

(a) Let

be the generic fiber of
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. We know that the kernel of the map
\text{Br}\,k\rightarrow\text{Br}\,X\hookrightarrow\text{Br}\,\kappa(X)
is equal to the kernel of the composition
\text{Br}\,k\hookrightarrow\text{Br}\,k(t)\rightarrow\text{Br}\,X_{\theta}\hookrightarrow\text{Br}\,\kappa(X)
. Since the kernel of the map
\text{Br}\,k(t)\to\text{Br}\,X_{\theta}
is
\langle X_{\theta}\rangle
and
\langle X_{\theta}\rangle\cap\text{Br}\,k=\{0\}
, it follows that the restriction
\text{Br}\,k\to\text{Br}\,X_{\theta}
is also injective. Thus the claim follows.

th power in
\kappa(f_{i})
, we again have
A\notin\text{Br}\,k
.
In both cases, we obtain a contradiction to
A\in\text{Br}\,k
. Hence our initial assumption is wrong and we conclude that
\pi^{*}(a,f_{i})
is a non-trivial element in
\overline{\text{Br}}\,\kappa(X)
.
∎

5.
Proof of Main theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
by constructing a specific variety for each odd prime
p
. For a fixed odd prime
p
, choose any
r\in k^{\times}\setminus k^{\times p}
. Consider the polynomials
f_{1}(x)=x^{p}-r
and
f_{2}(x)=x^{p}-r^{p-1}\in k[x]
. These are distinct irreducible separable monic polynomials
[
5
, Theorem 9.1]
. Let
f:=f_{1}f_{2}
and
F
be the corresponding homogeneous polynomial as in section
4
. By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a place
v
such that
f_{1}(0)
and
f_{2}(0)
are not
p
th powers, but
f(0)
is a
p
th power in
k_{v}
. Next, consider an element
a\in k^{\times}
such that
a
is not a
p
th power in
k[u]/\langle f_{i}(u)\rangle
for
i=\{1,2\}
and
v(a)=1
. We can choose such an
a
due to Lemma
3.2
. For this choice of
a
and
f
, let
\tilde{X}
be the variety
X_{a,f}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as constructed in
4.1
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The variety
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
constructed above satisfy the following.

(a)

For all Archimedean places
w
, the Severi-Brauer bundle
\tilde{X}_{k_{w}}
is birational to a
\mathbb{P}^{p-1}
-bundle.

(b)

There exists a non-Archimedean place
v
of
k
not lying over
p
and a specific subgroup
H
of
\mathrm{Br}\,\tilde{X}
such that the map
\tilde{\varphi}_{v}:\tilde{X}(k_{v})\to\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is nonzero.

(c)

For all places
\omega
, we have
0\in\,im\tilde{\varphi}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

(a) The generic fiber of
\tilde{X}
is associated with the cyclic algebra
(a,f)
. After base change to an Archimedean place
w
,
a
becomes a
p
th power in the base field, that is the algebra
(a,f)
splits over
k_{w}
. Thus by Lemma
2.1
, we can conclude that the resulting scheme
\tilde{X}_{k_{w}}
is birational to a
\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k_{w}}
bundle.

(b) Since the cyclic algebra
(a,f(0))
splits in
\mathrm{Br}\,k_{v}
, the fiber
\tilde{X}_{0}
has a
\tilde{X}(k_{v})
-point, say
P
. Let
H=\langle(a,f_{1})\rangle\subset\mathrm{Br}\,\tilde{X}
. Consider the map
\tilde{\varphi}_{v}:\tilde{X}(k_{v})\to\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
.
\tilde{\varphi}_{v}(P)
sends
(a,f_{1})
to
\mathrm{inv}_{v}(a,f_{1}(0))
, which is non-zero according to Lemma
3.4
given the assumption that
v(a)=1
.

(c) Since
f
is monic, the variety
\tilde{X}
has a
k
-point
Q
over infinity which implies
Q^{*}(a,f_{1})=(a,1)=0
. It follows that
\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(Q)=0
for all places
\omega
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists a smooth projective geometrically integral rationally connected variety
X
over
k
such that

(a)

\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
is a non-trivial
p
-torsion group,

(b)

X
has
k_{\nu}
points for all places
\nu
of
k
,

(c)

The Brauer-Manin set
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{B}
is empty, where
B
is a subgroup of
\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{X}
over
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
be the variety considered in Proposition
5.1
.
Let
H=\langle(a,f_{1})\rangle\subset\mathrm{Br}\,\tilde{X}
. By Proposition
5.1
(b), we know there exists a non-archimedean place
v
of
k
such that the map
\tilde{\varphi}_{v}:\tilde{X}(k_{v})\rightarrow\text{Hom}(H,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is non-zero. In particular
\tilde{\varphi}_{v}
is non-constant. For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
\nu_{i}
be the non-Archimedean places where the map
\tilde{\varphi}_{\omega}
is non-constant with
\nu_{1}=v
. Since
0\in im(\tilde{\varphi}_{\omega})
for all places by Proposition
5.1
, we define sets
S_{i}\subset\text{im}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\nu_{i}})
as,
S_{1}:=\text{im}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\nu_{1}})\setminus\{0\}
and
S_{i}:=\{0\}
for
2\leq i\leq r
. Part (a) of Proposition
5.1
ensures that
S_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{i}}:=\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\nu_{i}}^{-1}(S_{i}))
be the non-empty open subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k_{\nu_{i}})
. In the case where
\nu_{1}=v
, we note that
\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{1}}
is the union of non-empty open subsets
\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{1},s}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k_{\nu_{1}})
defined by

\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{1},s}:=\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\nu_{1}}^{-1}(s))

for each
s\in S_{1}
. Using weak approximation, we can choose a
k
-point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that lies outside
V(F)
and
c\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{i}}
for every
i
. We assume that
c=\infty
after making an appropriate change of coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This ensures that the fiber
\tilde{X}_{\infty}
is a Severi-Brauer variety, and

\tilde{X}_{\infty}(k_{\nu_{i}})\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

Condition (1): Suppose
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are the roots of
{f}_{1}
and
{f}_{2}
, respectively, in some finite extension of
k
. For
j=1,2
, by Lemma
3.3
and by
[
6
, Theorem 7.7.2.]
we can choose distinct non-archimedean places
\omega_{j}
such that
\omega_{j}\notin\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{r}\}
,
\tilde{X}_{\infty}(k_{\omega_{j}})\neq\emptyset
and
\omega_{j}
splits completely in
k(\sqrt[p]{a},\theta_{j})
. Further there exists a polynomial
h_{\omega_{j}}\in k_{\omega_{j}}[x]
such that
h_{\omega_{j}}-\theta_{j}
is irreducible and separable over
k_{\omega_{j}}
.

Condition (2): There exists only finitely many places
\omega
for which
\tilde{X}_{\infty}(k_{\omega})=\emptyset
by
[
6
, Theorem 7.7.2.]
. Applying Lemma
3.5
to the set
\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{X}(k_{\omega}))
for each such place
\omega
, we conclude that there exists a polynomial
h_{\omega}\in k_{\omega}[x]
which satisfies
h_{\omega}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(k_{\omega}))\cap\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{X}(k_{\omega}))\neq\emptyset.

Condition (3): For each
1\leq i\leq r
, Lemma
3.5
guarantees the existence of polynomials
h_{\nu_{i}}\in k_{\nu_{i}}[x]
such that
h_{\nu_{i}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(k_{\nu_{j}}))\subset\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{j}}
. Moreover,
h_{\nu_{1}}
can be chosen in such a way that,
h_{\nu_{1}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(k_{\nu_{1}}))\cap\mathcal{U}_{\nu_{1},t}\neq\emptyset
for each
t\in S_{1}
.

Using weak approximation, we choose a polynomial
g\in k[x]
such that it satisfies all the three conditions listed above. Let
\pi:X=X_{a,{f}\circ g}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the pullback of
\tilde{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
along
g
. By construction of
X_{a,{f}\circ g}
described in
4.1
, we know that
X
is a ‘nice’ variety over
k
.

Condition (1) on
g
and Lemma
3.6
imply that each
{f}_{i}\circ g
is irreducible and separable over
k(\sqrt[p]{a})
. Consequently,
a
is not a
p
th power in
k[u]/({f}_{i}\circ g)
. Hence, by Theorem
4.2
, we conclude that
\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
is a non-zero
p
-torsion group. This proves part (a).
Condition (2) on
g
guarantees that
X(k_{\omega})\neq\emptyset
for each place
\omega
where
\tilde{X}_{\infty}(k_{\omega})=\emptyset
. Thus we conclude
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\neq\emptyset
. This proves part (b). Let
\tilde{g}
be the induced map from
X
to
\tilde{X}
corresponding to the polynomial
g
.
Note that for all places
\omega
of
k
, the map
\varphi_{\omega}=\tilde{g}\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{\omega}:X(k_{\omega})\rightarrow\text{Hom}(B,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is defined for the subgroup
B=\langle(a,f_{1}\circ g)\rangle
of
\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
. For each
1\leq i\leq r
, Condition (3) on
g
ensures that the image of
\varphi_{\nu_{i}}
is precisely
S_{i}
. For places
\omega\notin\{\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{r}\}
, we have
\text{im}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\omega})=\{0\}
which implies that
\text{im}(\varphi_{\omega})=\{0\}
for all places
\omega
except
\nu_{1}
. Hence it follows that
0
does not belong to the image of the map
\varphi:X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\rightarrow\text{Hom}({B},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. In other words,
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{B}=\emptyset
. This proves part (c).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Consider the ‘nice’ variety
X
over
k
from Theorem
5.2
.
Since
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\neq\emptyset
and
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{B}=\emptyset
where
B=\langle(a,f_{1}\circ g)\rangle
, we conclude that
X
has Brauer-Manin obstruction to the existence of
k
-rational points. Further, the fact that
\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
is
p
-torsion implies that all of its non-trivial elements have order
p
. In particular the Brauer class of
(a,f_{1}\circ g)
which captures the obstruction has order
p
. Moreover, no elements of
\overline{\text{Br}}\,X
with order less than
p
can capture the obstruction. Hence the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Consider the ‘nice’
k
-variety
X
with Brauer-Manin obstruction to the existence of
k
points constructed in Theorem
5.2
. The subgroup
B=\langle(a,f_{1}\circ g)\rangle
of
\overline{\text{Br}}X
which captures the obstruction has exponent
p
. Since
\overline{Br}\,X
is a
p
-torsion group, no subgroups of exponent less than
p
can capture the obstruction. Hence the proof.
∎

On new identities involving zeros of Bessel functions
Bartosz Langowski, Adam Nowak

Abstract
. We derive new identities involving zeros of the Bessel function J ν J_{\nu} and some related functions. These are special cases of more general identities obtained in this note, which might also be of interest.

1.
Introduction

Let
\nu>-1
and denote by
j_{n}^{\nu}
,
n\geq 1
, the sequence of successive strictly positive zeros of
the Bessel function
J_{\nu}
, cf.
[
20
, Chap. XV]
.
There is no explicit expression for
j_{n}^{\nu}
except for the two special cases when

\nu=\pm 1/2
in which one has
j_{n}^{-1/2}=\pi(n-1/2)
and
j_{n}^{1/2}=\pi n
.
Nevertheless, there are many striking summation identities involving the zeros of
J_{\nu}
where apart from the zeros
everything else is explicit. A classic example here is Raighley’s formula (cf.
[
20
, Chap. XV, Sec. 15
\cdot
51]
)

(1)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(j_{k}^{\nu})^{2}}=\frac{1}{4(\nu+1)}.

Actually, the above series is computable explicitly if one replaces
2
by any other positive even power, see

[
14
,
11
,
19
,
9
]
.
Another classic identity involving the zeros of
J_{\nu}
is the Mittag-Leffler expansion
(cf.
[
20
, Chap. XV, Sec. 15
\cdot
41]
)

\frac{J_{\nu+1}(x)}{J_{\nu}(x)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2x}{(j_{k}^{\nu})^{2}-x^{2}},

where, in particular, the left-hand side vanishes with the choice
x=j_{n}^{\nu+1}
,
n\geq 1
.

Further examples of this kind are formulas found by Calogero
[
7
,
8
]

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1,k\neq n}\frac{(j_{n}^{\nu})^{2}}{(j_{k}^{\nu})^{2}-(j_{n}^{\nu})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu+1}{2},\qquad n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1,k\neq n}\frac{(j_{n}^{\nu})^{4}}{[(j_{k}^{\nu})^{2}-(j_{n}^{\nu})^{2}]^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(j_{n}^{\nu})^{2}-(\nu+1)(\nu+5)}{12},\qquad n\geq 1.

Curiously enough, the research of Calogero had some physical background, actually
it was mainly a spin-off of the study of certain integrable many-body problems, cf.
[
1
]
and references there.
It is also possible to compute analogous series with the numerator and the denominator of the summand in (
2
)
raised separately to various integer positive powers, see
[
8
,
3
,
1
]
.
Other formulas in this spirit can be found in
[
19
]
,
[
18
, Sec. 5.7.33]
,
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
]
)
and
[
9
]
, among others.

It is perhaps interesting that all the above displayed formulas were indispensable in our recent study devoted to some questions
in harmonic analysis of Fourier-Bessel expansions
[
13
]
. This inspired the present note in which we
derive several up to our best knowledge new identities including, for instance,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(j_{k}^{\nu})^{2}}{[(j_{k}^{\nu})^{2}-(j_{n}^{\nu+1})^{2}]^{2}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(j_{k}^{\nu+1})^{2}}{[(j_{k}^{\nu+1})^{2}-(j_{n}^{\nu})^{2}]^{2}}=\frac{1}{4},\qquad n\geq 1.

Comparing to most of other known formulas,
our identities involve zeros of more than one Bessel or related function (in the two cases above,

J_{\nu}
and
J_{\nu+1}
).
The method we use is rather elementary, but relies on a quite deep fact that the Fourier-Bessel and Fourier-Dini
systems form orthogonal bases in
L^{2}((0,1),dx)
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we first briefly describe the general notation used in the paper.
Then we present for further reference various facts and formulas related to Bessel functions.
Finally, we give basic information on Fourier-Bessel and Fourier-Dini systems.

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this note we use fairly standard notation.
By
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
we denote the usual inner product in
L^{2}((0,1),dx)
.
For a
condition
that can have logical value true or false we shall denote

\chi_{\{\textsf{condition}\}}:=\begin{cases}1,&\textrm{if}\;\,\textsf{condition}\;\,\textrm{is true},\\
0,&\textrm{if}\;\,\textsf{condition}\;\,\textrm{is false}.\end{cases}

When denoting zeros of Bessel and related functions, we choose to write the index indicating the order as superscript,
i.e.
j_{n}^{\nu}
,
j_{n}^{\nu,H}
rather than
j_{n,\nu}
or
j_{n,\nu,H}
.
This is to make the notation more compact and stick to notation of other quantities or objects depending on the order.

2.2.
Facts and formulas concerning the Bessel functions
J_{\nu}
and
I_{\nu}

For the material presented in this section we refer to Watson’s monograph
[
20
]
; for an easy
online access to many facts and formulas related to Bessel functions see
[
16
, Chap. 10]
.

Let
J_{\nu}
be the Bessel function of the first kind and order
\nu
and let

I_{\nu}
be the modified Bessel function of the first kind and order
\nu
. In this paper, for our purpose,
we shall essentially always consider
J_{\nu}
and
I_{\nu}
as functions on the positive half-line. Further, throughout the paper
we assume that the parameter
\nu>-1
.

For further reference, we list some useful identities

(3)

\displaystyle J_{\nu-1}(x)+J_{\nu+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\nu}{x}J_{\nu}(x),

\displaystyle I_{\nu-1}(x)-I_{\nu+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\nu}{x}I_{\nu}(x),

(4)

\displaystyle xJ^{\prime}_{\nu}(x)-\nu J_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=-xJ_{\nu+1}(x),

\displaystyle xI^{\prime}_{\nu}(x)-\nu I_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=xI_{\nu+1}(x),

(5)

\displaystyle\big[x^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(x)\big]^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-x^{-\nu}J_{\nu+1}(x),

\displaystyle\big[x^{-\nu}I_{\nu}(x)\big]^{\prime}

\displaystyle=x^{-\nu}I_{\nu+1}(x).

We also note the asymptotics

(6)

J_{\nu}(x)=\mathcal{O}(x^{\nu}),\qquad I_{\nu}(x)=\mathcal{O}(x^{\nu}),\qquad x\to 0^{+},

and the special cases

J_{-1/2}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\cos x,\qquad J_{1/2}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\sin x.

In general,
J_{\nu}
expresses directly via elementary functions if and only if
\nu
is an odd half-integer.

Both
J_{\nu}
and
I_{\nu}
are smooth on
(0,\infty)
and
I_{\nu}
is strictly positive there,
but
J_{\nu}
has infinitely many isolated zeros in
(0,\infty)
. We denote the sequence of successive strictly positive zeros
of
J_{\nu}
by
\{j_{n}^{\nu}:n\geq 1\}
.
The derivative
J^{\prime}_{\nu}
also has infinitely many isolated zeros in
(0,\infty)
. We denote them
†
, in an ascending order, by

\{(j_{n}^{\nu})^{\prime}:n\geq 1\}
.

†
†

{\dagger}
We do not make an exception appearing sometimes in the literature, see e.g.
[
16
, §10.21(i)]
,
that when
\nu=0
the zeros of
J^{\prime}_{0}
are counted from
x=0
.

For a parameter
H\in\mathbb{R}
we consider the auxiliary functions

J_{\nu,H}(x):=xJ^{\prime}_{\nu}(x)+HJ_{\nu}(x)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad I_{\nu,H}(x):=xI^{\prime}_{\nu}(x)+HI_{\nu}(x).

By convention, we define
J_{\nu,\infty}:=J_{\nu}
and
I_{\nu,\infty}:=I_{\nu}
.
Observe that making use of (
4
) and (
3
) we get

(7)

\displaystyle J_{\nu,H}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{1-H}\big[x^{H}J_{\nu}(x)\big]^{\prime}=(H+\nu)J_{\nu}(x)-xJ_{\nu+1}(x)=(H-\nu)J_{\nu}(x)+xJ_{\nu-1}(x),

(8)

\displaystyle I_{\nu,H}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{1-H}\big[x^{H}I_{\nu}(x)\big]^{\prime}=(H+\nu)I_{\nu}(x)+xI_{\nu+1}(x)=(H-\nu)I_{\nu}(x)+xI_{\nu-1}(x).

The function
J_{\nu,H}
has infinitely many isolated zeros in
(0,\infty)
. We denote their sequence in an ascending order
of magnitude by
\{j_{n}^{\nu,H}:n\geq 1\}
.
The function
I_{\nu,H}
has no zeros in
(0,\infty)
when
H+\nu\geq 0
, and exactly one
strictly positive zero otherwise
‡
which we denote by
j_{0}^{\nu,H}

(note that
♮
when
\nu+H<0
,
\pm ij_{0}^{\nu,H}

(\cdot)^{-\nu}J_{\nu,H}
,

[
20
, Chap. XVIII, Sec. 18
\cdot

).
In case
H+\nu=0
, we also set
j_{0}^{\nu,H}:=0
.
By convention, we define
j_{n}^{\nu,\infty}:=j_{n}^{\nu}
.

{\ddagger}
This can immediately be seen from (

) and the Mittag-Leffler expansion

\frac{xI_{\nu+1}(x)}{I_{\nu}(x)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{1+(j_{k}^{\nu}/x)^{2}}
.

\natural
It is easy to check that
(ix)^{-\nu}J_{\nu,H}(ix)=x^{-\nu}I_{\nu,H}(x)
for
x>0

and
H\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Observe that, in view of (

), for the special values of
H=-\nu,\nu,0
one has
(in the second identity we consider
\nu>0
)

(9)

j_{n}^{\nu,-\nu}=j_{n}^{\nu+1},\qquad j_{n}^{\nu,\nu}=j_{n}^{\nu-1},\qquad j_{n}^{\nu,0}=(j_{n}^{\nu})^{\prime},\qquad n\geq 1,\qquad\textrm{and}\quad j_{0}^{\nu,0}=(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}\quad\textrm{for}\;\;\nu<0,

where
(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}
is the only strictly positive zero of

.

We will also need the relations, see (

J_{\nu+1}\big((j_{n}^{\nu})^{\prime}\big)=\frac{\nu}{(j_{n}^{\nu})^{\prime}}J_{\nu}\big((j_{n}^{\nu})^{\prime}\big),\qquad n\geq 1,\qquad\qquad I_{\nu+1}\big((j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}\big)=-\frac{\nu}{(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}}I_{\nu}\big((j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}\big)\quad\textrm{if}\;\;\nu<0.

2.3.
Fourier-Bessel and Fourier-Dini systems

Define the following quantities:

c_{n}^{\nu}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{|J_{\nu+1}(j_{n}^{\nu})|},\qquad c_{n}^{\nu,H}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{|J_{\nu}(j_{n}^{\nu,H})|}\frac{j_{n}^{\nu,H}}{\sqrt{\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H}\big)^{2}-\nu^{2}+H^{2}}},\qquad n\geq 1,

and, in case
\nu+H<0
, define also

c_{0}^{\nu,H}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{I_{\nu}(j_{0}^{\nu,H})}\frac{j_{0}^{\nu,H}}{\sqrt{\big(j_{0}^{\nu,H}\big)^{2}+\nu^{2}-H^{2}}}.

By convention, we set
c_{n}^{\nu,\infty}:=c_{n}^{\nu}
,
n\geq 1
.
Note that the constants
c_{n}^{\nu,H}
,
H\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
,
n\geq\chi_{\{H+\nu\geq 0\}}
,
are well-defined and strictly positive. This follows from evaluation of
(c_{n}^{\nu,H})^{-2}

as certain strictly positive integrals, see the comments preceding Theorem
2.1
.

Next, define the following functions on the interval
(0,1)
:

\psi_{n}^{\nu}(x):=c_{n}^{\nu}\sqrt{x}J_{\nu}\big(j_{n}^{\nu}x\big),\qquad\psi_{n}^{\nu,H}(x):=c_{n}^{\nu,H}\sqrt{x}J_{\nu}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H}x\big),\qquad n\geq 1.

In addition, in case
\nu+H\leq 0
, define

\psi_{0}^{\nu,H}(x):=\begin{cases}c_{0}^{\nu,H}\sqrt{x}I_{\nu}\big(j_{0}^{\nu,H}x\big),&\;\;\textrm{if}\;\;H+\nu<0,\\
\sqrt{2(\nu+1)}x^{\nu+1/2},&\;\;\textrm{if}\;\;H+\nu=0.\end{cases}

By convention, we set
\psi_{n}^{\nu,\infty}:=\psi_{n}^{\nu}
,
n\geq 1
.

The result stated below is well-known, see
[
20
, Chap. XVIII]
and
[
10
]
.
The difficult part of the proof is completeness of the two systems, cf.
[
10
]
.
On the other hand, orthogonality and values of the normalizing constants
c_{n}^{\nu,H}
are deduced from Lommel’s integrals, cf.
[
20
, Chap. V, Sec. 5
\cdot
11]
. Convenient references to the formulas needed here are the following:

[
18
, Sec. 1.8.3, Form. 10]
,
[
18
, Sec. 1.11.5, Form. 2]
,
[
18
, Sec. 1.8.1, Form. 21]
(orthogonality) and

[
18
, Sec. 1.8.3, Form. 11]
,
[
18
, Sec. 1.11.3, Form. 4]
(values of the normalizing constants).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\nu>-1
and
H\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed. Then the Fourier-Bessel and the Fourier-Dini systems

\big\{\psi_{n}^{\nu}:n\geq 1\big\}\qquad\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\qquad\big\{\psi_{n}^{\nu,H}:n\geq\chi_{\{H+\nu>0\}}\big\},

respectively, are orthonormal bases in
L^{2}((0,1),dx)
.

Observe that we can incorporate the Fourier-Bessel system into the family of the Fourier-Dini systems by allowing
H=\infty
.
This has a deeper background, since in some aspects the Fourier-Bessel system can be seen as the limiting case of the Fourier-Dini
system as
H\to\infty
, cf.
[
20
, Chap. XVIII, Sec. 18
\cdot
11]
.

The next result is also known and can be easily verified by a direct computation with the aid of (
5
).
It says that the Fourier-Bessel and the Fourier-Dini systems consist of eigenfunctions of the Bessel differential operator

(12)

\mathbb{L}^{\nu}f(x):=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}f(x)-\frac{1/4-\nu^{2}}{x^{2}}f(x)=-x^{-\nu-1/2}\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x^{2\nu+1}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}f(x)\big]\Big).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\nu>-1
and
H\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then

\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H}=(-1)^{\chi_{\{n=0\}}}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H}\big)^{2}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H},\qquad n\geq\chi_{\{H+\nu>0\}}.

Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
are crucial in what follows.
Note that in the context of Theorem
2.1
Parseval’s identity can be written as

\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,1),dx)}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq\chi_{\{H+\nu>0\}}}\big|\big\langle f,\psi_{k}^{\nu,H}\big\rangle\big|^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}((0,1),dx),

where
\nu>-1
and
H\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.

3.
General result

For
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
and
n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu\geq 0\}}
,
k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu\geq 0\}}
,
define

b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}:=c_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}c_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big[j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}J_{\nu+1}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)J_{\nu}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)-j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}J_{\nu}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)J_{\nu+1}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)\Big],

where we use the following convention:
in case
n=0
the Bessel functions
J_{\nu}
and
J_{\nu+1}
which are evaluated at
j_{0}^{\nu,H_{1}}
must be replaced
by
I_{\nu}
and
I_{\nu+1}
, respectively, with analogous replacement in case
k=0
and the Bessel functions evaluated
at
j_{0}^{\nu,H_{2}}
. Further, with the same convention in force, to cover the relevant corner cases we set

b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}:=\begin{cases}-(-1)^{\chi_{\{k=0\}}}\sqrt{2(\nu+1)}c_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}J_{\nu+1}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)&\textrm{if}\;\;H_{1}+\nu=n=0\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu\geq 0\}},\\
(-1)^{\chi_{\{n=0\}}}\sqrt{2(\nu+1)}c_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}J_{\nu+1}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)&\textrm{if}\;\;H_{2}+\nu=k=0\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu\geq 0\}}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\nu>-1
and
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be such that
H_{1}\neq H_{2}
.
Then

\Big\langle\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle=\Big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle+b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}},\qquad n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu>0\}},\quad k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu>0\}}.

Proof.

We shall use the divergence form of
\mathbb{L}^{\nu}
, see (
12
), and integrate twice by parts. Denote

\mathcal{I}:=\Big\langle\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\,dx.

Clearly, the last integral converges since
\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}
(see Proposition
2.2
) and

\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}
are in
L^{2}((0,1),dx)
.

In what follows we assume that
n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu\geq 0\}}
and
k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu\geq 0\}}
.
The remaining corner cases are simpler and left to the reader.
We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\nu-1/2}\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x^{2\nu+1}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)\big]\Big)\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-x^{\nu+1/2}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)\big]\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\Big|_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)\big]\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\big]x^{2\nu+1}\,dx

\displaystyle=-x^{\nu+1/2}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)\big]\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\Big|_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+x^{\nu+1/2}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\big]\Big|_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}(x)x^{-\nu-1/2}\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x^{2\nu+1}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{-\nu-1/2}\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}(x)\big]\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}+\mathcal{I}_{3}.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{3}=\big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\mathbb{L}^{\nu}\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big\rangle
it remains to show that

\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}=b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}
.

For symmetry reasons, it suffices to evaluate
\mathcal{I}_{1}
. Plugging in the explicit expressions for
\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}

and
\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}
, and then using (
5
), shows that

\mathcal{I}_{1}=c_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}c_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}xJ_{\nu+1}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}x\big)J_{\nu}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}x\big)\Big|_{0}^{1};

here and below we use the same convention concerning the replacement of
J_{\nu}
and
J_{\nu+1}
by
I_{\nu}
and
I_{\nu+1}

as in the definition of
b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}
. In view of the asymptotics (
6
) we infer that

\mathcal{I}_{1}=c_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}c_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}J_{\nu+1}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)J_{\nu}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big).

This also implies

\mathcal{I}_{2}=-c_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}c_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}J_{\nu}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)J_{\nu+1}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)

and thus finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\nu>-1
and assume that
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
are such that
H_{1}\neq H_{2}
.
Then the following identities hold:

\sum_{k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu>0\}}}\frac{\big|b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}\big|^{2}}{\Big[(-1)^{\chi_{\{n=0\}}}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)^{2}-(-1)^{\chi_{\{k=0\}}}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)^{2}\Big]^{2}}=1,\qquad n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu>0\}}.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.1
with Proposition
2.2
we get, for

n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu>0\}}
and
k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu>0\}}
,

(-1)^{\chi_{\{n=0\}}}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)^{2}\Big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle=(-1)^{\chi_{\{k=0\}}}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)^{2}\Big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle+b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}.

Solving for
\big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big\rangle
we arrive at

(13)

\Big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle=\frac{b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}}{(-1)^{\chi_{\{n=0\}}}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)^{2}-(-1)^{\chi_{\{k=0\}}}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)^{2}}.

Here the following remark is in order (recall that
H_{1}\neq H_{2}
): in the above fraction no singularity occurs.
To see this, assume first that
n,k\geq 1
. We may further assume that
H_{1},H_{2}<\infty
, since if one of them is infinite then
the argument simplifies.
Suppose a contrario that
j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}=j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}=:j^{\nu}>0
. Then, in view of (
7
),

j^{\nu}J_{\nu+1}(j^{\nu})=(H_{1}+\nu)J_{\nu}(j^{\nu})=(H_{2}+\nu)J_{\nu}(j^{\nu})
. Here the
second equality implies
J_{\nu}(j^{\nu})=0
and then the first one forces
J_{\nu+1}(j^{\nu})=0
.
But this is a contradiction, since positive zeros of
J_{\nu}
and
J_{\nu+1}
are interlaced,
cf.
[
20
, Chap. XV, Sec. 15
\cdot
22]
.
If the condition
n,k\geq 1
does not hold, the only non-trivial case is when
n=k=0
and
\nu<0
. But then, supposing that

j_{0}^{\nu,H_{1}}=j_{0}^{\nu,H_{2}}=:j^{\nu}>0
, we would have, see (
8
),

-j^{\nu}I_{\nu+1}(j^{\nu})=(H_{1}+\nu)I_{\nu}(j^{\nu})=(H_{2}+\nu)I_{\nu}(j^{\nu})
,
which implies
I_{\nu}(j^{\nu})=0
, a contradiction.

Using now Theorem
2.1
and Parseval’s identity, and then (
13
), we can write for
any
n\geq\chi_{\{H_{1}+\nu>0\}}

\displaystyle 1=\big\|\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big\|_{L^{2}((0,1),dx)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu>0\}}}\Big|\Big\langle\psi_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}},\psi_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\Big\rangle\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq\chi_{\{H_{2}+\nu>0\}}}\frac{\big|b_{n,k}^{\nu,H_{1},H_{2}}\big|^{2}}{\Big[(-1)^{\chi_{\{n=0\}}}\big(j_{n}^{\nu,H_{1}}\big)^{2}-(-1)^{\chi_{\{k=0\}}}\big(j_{k}^{\nu,H_{2}}\big)^{2}\Big]^{2}}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

5.
Comments and further results

In this section we comment on the size of the “imaginary” zero
j_{0}^{\nu,H}
, since this is the least studied
in the literature zero we are dealing with. Thus, from now on, we consider
\nu
and
H
such that
H+\nu<0
.

The equation
I_{\nu,H}(x)=0
for
x\in(0,\infty)
can be written equivalently as, see (
8
),

(14)

x\frac{I_{\nu+1}(x)}{I_{\nu}(x)}=-(H+\nu).

Here the left-hand side is continuous and strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
(this is seen from its Mittag-Leffler expansion) and
has limits
0
and
\infty
as
x\to 0^{+}
and
x\to\infty
, respectively. Therefore, given an arbitrary
x_{0}>0
, for a fixed

\nu
one can always find
H
such that
j_{0}^{\nu,H}=x_{0}
. In other words, in general the size of
j_{0}^{\nu,H}
can be arbitrary.

However, the situation is much different in the special case
H=0
considered in Section
4
, see
Formulas (
F6
), (
F8
), (
F12
) and (
F15
). We will show the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
-1<\nu<0
. Then

(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}<j_{1}^{\nu}<j_{1}^{\nu+1}.

Proof.

The second inequality is well known, see
[
20
, Chap. XV, Sec. 15
\cdot
22]
, so we prove only the first one.

Since
(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}
is the strictly positive zero of
I_{\nu,0}
, it is, see (
14
), the only strictly positive root
of the equation

\frac{I_{\nu+1}(x)}{I_{\nu}(x)}=-\frac{\nu}{x}.

We now estimate the left-hand side here from below by a function
F(x)
so that the (first) positive root of
the equation
F(x)=-\nu/x
is bigger than
(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}
.

Define

F(x):=\frac{2(\nu+2)x}{4(\nu+1)(\nu+2)+x^{2}}.

From
[
15
, Theorem 3(a)]
, see also
[
15
, p. 11, formula for
L_{\nu,0,1}
]
, it follows that

F(x)<\frac{I_{\nu+1}(x)}{I_{\nu}(x)},\qquad x>0.

Solving the equation
F(x)=-\nu/x
we see that its only positive root is

x_{0}^{\nu}:=2\sqrt{\frac{(-\nu)(\nu^{2}+3\nu+2)}{3\nu+4}}.

Thus we get
(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}<x_{0}^{\nu}
.

On the other hand, from e.g. Raighley’s formula (
1
) we know that
j_{1}^{\nu}>2\sqrt{\nu+1}
.
Therefore, to finish the proof it suffices to verify the estimate

x_{0}^{\nu}<2\sqrt{\nu+1},\qquad\nu\in(-1,0),

which is elementary to check.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The method from the proof of Proposition
5.1
together with other rational bounds for
I_{\nu+1}/I_{\nu}
in

[
15
]
allows one to get more precise upper and also lower bounds for
(j_{0}^{\nu})^{\prime}
. Moreover, the same method can be
used to estimate
j_{0}^{\nu,H}
for other
H
and
\nu
such that
H+\nu<0
.

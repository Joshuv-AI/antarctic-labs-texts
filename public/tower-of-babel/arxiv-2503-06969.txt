Axiomatic Sectional category, Topological Complexity, and Homotopic distance
Jean-Paul Doeraene and Mohammed El Haouari

Abstract




2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
55M30

Abstract.

Many of the properties of sectional category, topological complexity and homotopic distance are in fact derived from a small number of basic properties, which, once established, lead to all the others without further recourse to topology.
On the other hand, there are several variants of these notions: with open covers or else Whitehead-Ganea constructions, also with spaces and maps that are unpointed or else pointed, fibrewise, equivariant, etc. or even with algebraic models of spaces and maps.
These are two reasons why we build an axiomatic approach to all these notions, based on just three simple axioms.

We also introduce the notion of ‘lifting category’ which unifies the notions of sectional category, topological complexity, and homotopic distance, all of which are special cases of lifting category.

Résumé.

Beaucoup des propriétés de la catégorie sectionnelle, de la complexité topologique et de la distance homotopique découlent en réalité d’un petit nombre de propriétés de base qui, une fois établies, permettent de déduire toutes les autres sans recours supplémentaire à la topologie.
D’autre part, il existe plusieurs variantes de ces notions : avec des recouvrements ouverts ou bien des constructions de Whitehead-Ganea, avec des espaces et applications qui sont non pointés, pointés, fibrés, équivariants, etc., voire avec des modèles algébriques d’espaces et applications.
Ce sont deux raisons pour lesquelles nous développons une approche axiomatique de toutes ces notions, fondée sur seulement trois axiomes simples.

Nous introduisons également la notion de « catégorie de relèvement » qui unifie les notions de catégorie sectionnelle, de complexité topologique et de distance homotopique, toutes étant des cas particuliers de la catégorie de relèvement.

The
lifting category
from a map

to another

with same codomain is the least integer

,

, and for each

is homotopic to

. If

are embeddings, the existence of

means that

is deformable in

into
\iota(A)
. On the other hand, if

is a fibration, “homotopic” can be replaced by “equal”.

The lifting category is a generalization of both the
(Clapp-Puppe) category
of a map and the
sectional category
(or

) of a map.
We define and study the lifting category with all its particular cases: Lusternik-Schnirelmann category, sectional category, and also
topological complexity
and

, all at once.

Our aim is to make a clear distinction between topological considerations and, say, category-theoretic considerations.
We develop this second aspect on the basis of a small number of axioms from which all the expected properties follow. This is done in Sections

8
and
9
.
The first aspect is dealt with in Sections

After unpacking our tools in Section

In Section

We then show that the notions based on open covers, see Sections

We look at the pointed case in Section

In Section

For applications and examples, we will limit ourselves to the category

1.
Useful lemmas

All constructions here are made up to ‘homotopy equivalences’, in such a way that the numbers we define are invariant with respect to these equivalences. We use the symbol
\simeq
to indicate that two maps are homotopic. Spaces are non-empty.

We will use a lot of ‘homotopy pullbacks’. Roughly speaking, a homotopy pullback is a homotopy commutative diagram that satisfies the universal property of pullbacks up to homotopy equivalences.
A homotopy commutative square whose two opposite sides are homotopy equivalences is a homotopy pullback.
Warning: Not every pullback is a homotopy pullback. However a pullback of two maps, one of which is a fibration, is a homotopy pullback. To build a homotopy pullback of two maps which are not fibrations, we have to replace one of them with a homotopy equivalent fibration. The ‘homotopy pushout’ is the (Eckmann-Hilton) dual notion of the homotopy pullback.
For a more detailed description of this, see
[
9
]
in a strict topological context, or
[
2
]
in the more general context of ‘J-categories’.

Lemma 1
.

Assume we have any homotopy commutative diagram:

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y}

If there is a map

Assume there exists a map

\iota\circ l=\iota\circ p\circ s\simeq f\circ q\circ s\simeq f\circ g

\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{g}

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}

The map

2.
Abstract Sectional and Lifting category

In this section, we give the axioms of the (abstract) ‘sectional category’ and the properties that follow directly from them. We also introduce the notion of ‘lifting category’, which generalizes the sectional category.

Axioms 6

(Sectional category)
.

We work with an (abstract) integer
\mathrm{secat}(\iota)
defined for any map
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
, called ‘sectional category’ of
\iota
.
We assume that the following three axioms are satisfied:

S0.

For any map
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathrm{secat}(\iota)=0
if and only if
\iota
has a homotopy section, i.e. there is a map
s\colon Y\to A
such that
\iota\circ s\simeq{\rm id}_{Y}
.

S1.

If we have a homotopy commutative diagram in
\mathcal{T}
:

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{X}

then
\mathrm{secat}\,(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{secat}\,(\kappa)
.

S2.

If we have a homotopy pullback in
\mathcal{T}
:

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y}

then
\mathrm{secat}(q)\leqslant\mathrm{secat}(\iota)
.

Let’s already give an elementary but instructive example:

Definition 7
.

The ‘boolean sectional category’
\mathrm{secat}^{\mathrm{b}}(\iota)
of a map
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
is 0 if
\iota
has a homotopy section, and 1 otherwise.

It is obvious that
\mathrm{secat}^{\mathrm{b}}
satisfies axioms S0 and S1. If we have a homotopy pullback as in Axiom S2, and
\iota
has a homotopy section
s\colon Y\to A
, then
q
has a homotopy section which is the whisker map
({\rm id}_{X},s\circ f)
. So
\mathrm{secat}^{\mathrm{b}}
also satisfies S2.

Definition 8

(Lifting category)
.

For any pair of maps
f\colon X\to Y
and
\iota\colon A\to Y
,
consider a homotopy pullback as in Axiom S2.
The ‘lifting category’ from
f
to
\iota
, is the sectional category of the map
q\colon P\to X
. We denote:

\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}\,(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}(q)
.

Remark 9
.

The homotopy pullback
P
and the map
q
are only defined up to homotopy equivalences, but if we have another choice:
P^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
, we have whisker maps
s\colon P\to P^{\prime}
such that
q^{\prime}\circ s\simeq q
and
s^{\prime}\colon P^{\prime}\to P
such that
q\circ s^{\prime}\simeq q^{\prime}
, so by Axiom S1,
\mathrm{secat}(q^{\prime})=\mathrm{secat}(q)
.

Although
\mathrm{liftcat}
is defined above in terms of
\mathrm{secat}
,
\mathrm{secat}
can also be seen as a special case of
\mathrm{liftcat}
.
Indeed, by applying Definition
8
with
f={\rm id}_{Y}
,
p={\rm id}_{A}
and
q=\iota
, we have:

Observation 10
.

For any map
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
,

\mathrm{secat}(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}_{{\rm id}_{Y}}\,(\iota)
.

Now let’s establish the consequences of axioms S0 to S2.

Proposition 11
.

For any pair of maps
f\colon X\to Y
and
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
,

\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)=0
if and only if there is a map
l\colon X\to A
such that
\iota\circ l\simeq f
.

Proof.

Use Axiom S0 and Lemma
1
with the homotopy pullback of
f
and
\iota
, and
g={\rm id}_{X}
.∎

We can rephrase Axiom S2 as follows:

Observation 12
.

For any pair of maps
f\colon X\to Y
and
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
,

\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}\,(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{secat}(\iota)
.

Proposition 13
.

Assume we have any homotopy commutative diagram in
\mathcal{T}
:

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y.}

Then we have:

\mathrm{liftcat}_{h}\,(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{liftcat}_{g}\,(\kappa).

If in addition the square is a homotopy pullback (in particular if

and
\zeta
are homotopy equivalences), then
\mathrm{liftcat}_{h}\,(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}_{g}\,(\kappa)
.

Proof.

Using the Prism lemma, extend the homotopy commutative diagram of the hypothesis to this one:

\textstyle{P^{\prime\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q^{\prime\prime}}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\textstyle{P^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q^{\prime}}

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}

where all squares and rectangles are homotopy pullbacks, and dashed arrows are whisker maps. By definition,
\mathrm{liftcat}_{h}(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}(q^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{liftcat}_{g}(\kappa)=\mathrm{secat}(q^{\prime\prime})

\mathrm{secat}(q^{\prime})\leqslant\mathrm{secat}(q^{\prime\prime})
. So
\mathrm{liftcat}_{h}(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{liftcat}_{g}(\kappa)

If in addition, the square of the hypothesis is a homotopy pullback, then we can assume that

,

. So
\mathrm{liftcat}_{h}(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}_{g}(\kappa)
.

Corollary 14

(Homotopy invariance)
.

Let
g,h\colon W\to Y
and
\iota,\kappa\colon A\to Y
be maps in
\mathcal{T}
such that
h\simeq g
and
\iota\simeq\kappa
. Then
\mathrm{liftcat}_{h}(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}_{g}(\kappa)
.

Proof.

This is a special case of Proposition
13
with
\zeta={\rm id}_{A}
and
f={\rm id}_{Y}
.∎

We bring together the essential equalities and inequalities in the following result:

Proposition 15
.

Assume we have any homotopy commutative diagram in
\mathcal{T}
:

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y}

(1)
In any case,
\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)\leqslant\inf\{\mathrm{secat}(\kappa),\mathrm{secat}(\iota)\}
.

(2)
If the square is a homotopy pullback, then
\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}(\kappa)\leqslant\mathrm{secat}(\iota)
.

(3)
If

has a homotopy section, then
\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{secat}(\kappa)
.

(4)
If

\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}(\kappa)=\mathrm{secat}(\iota)
.

Proof.

(1 and 2)
The inequality or equality with
\mathrm{secat}\,\kappa
is a special case of Proposition
13
with

. The inequality with
\mathrm{secat}\,\iota
is Observation

.

(3)
If we have a homotopy section
s\colon Y\to X
of

, then (using the Prism lemma) build the following homotopy pullbacks:

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}

We have
\mathrm{secat}\,\iota\leqslant\mathrm{secat}\,q\leqslant\mathrm{secat}\,\iota

\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}\,q
by definition, so
\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(\iota)=\mathrm{secat}\,\iota
. The inequality with
\mathrm{secat}\,\kappa
is given by (1).

(4)
If

and

are homotopy equivalences, then both the conditions of (2) and (3) are satisfied, so we have equalities.

Corollary 16
.

For any map
\iota\colon A\to Y
and space

in
\mathcal{T}
, we have:

\mathrm{secat}(\iota\times{\rm id}_{Z})=\mathrm{secat}(\iota).

Proof.

The projection
{\rm pr}_{1}\colon Y\times Z\to Y
has an obvious section. We get the result applying Proposition
15
(2 and 3) to the (homotopy) pullback of Remark

.

Proposition 17
.

For any maps
f\colon X\to Y
,
g\colon W\to X
, and
\iota\colon A\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
,

\mathrm{liftcat}_{f\circ g}\,(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}\,(\iota)\leqslant\mathrm{secat}(\iota).

If
g
is a homotopy equivalence, then
\mathrm{liftcat}_{f\circ g}\,(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}\,(\iota)
.

4.
Lifting category with open covers

In this section, we give a version of the lifting category based on open covers, which is an extension of the (more or less) original definition of the sectional category.
We show that axioms S0 to S2 are well satisfied.

Definition 33
.

For continuous maps
f\colon X\to Y
and
\iota\colon A\to Y
, let us denote
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{f}\,(\iota)
the least integer
n
for which there exists an open cover
(U_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}
of
X
and maps
l_{i}\colon U_{i}\to A
such that
\iota\circ l_{i}\simeq f|_{U_{i}}
for all
i
. Such a cover is said to be ‘categorical’. If no categorical cover exists for any
n
, we denote
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{f}\,(\iota)=\infty
.

If
f={\rm id}_{Y}
, we write
\mathrm{secat}^{\mathrm{op}}\,(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{{\rm id}_{Y}}\,(\iota)
.

Remark 34
.

As there is only one cover of
X
with one open set (which must be
X
itself),
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{f}(\iota)=0
if and only if there is map
l\colon X\to A
such that
\iota\circ l\simeq f
.

It is obvious that:

Observation 35
.

Let
f,f^{\prime}\colon X\to Y
and
\iota,\iota^{\prime}\colon A\to Y
be maps such that
f\simeq f^{\prime}
and
\iota\simeq\iota^{\prime}
. Then
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{f}(\iota)=\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{f^{\prime}}(\iota^{\prime})
.

It should be noticed that if we work in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
, all maps preserve basepoints, so each
U_{i}
is supposed to include the basepoint of
X
; also homotopies are defined on the reduced cylinder, and preserve basepoints like all maps, in other words these are pointed homotopies.
However, if we look carefully at the proofs, we see that being in
\mathrm{\bf Top}
or in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
makes no difference.
We’ll come back to this issue in Section
6
.

Observation 36
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
and
\iota\colon A\to Y
are embeddings, then an open cover
(U_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}
of
X
is categorical for
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\mathrm{op}}_{f}(\iota)
if and only if for all

,

is deformable in

into
\iota(A)
.

Indeed if

and
\iota
are embeddings, for any open subset

of
X
, we can identify

with
U
and
\iota(A)
with

.
A deformation

\forall\penalty\ u\in U

H(u,0)=u
and

, corresponds to a homotopy between

and
g=H(\text{--},1)
such that

.

Example 37

Let
\iota\colon\bm{S}^{1}_{d}\to\bm{S}^{1}
be the identity between the circle with the discrete topology and the circle with the usual topology. Obviously, as

\bm{S}^{1}
is deformable in itself into

So
\mathrm{secat}^{\mathrm{op}}(\iota)=1

Since we’ve defined

These characterizations of homotopic distance and topological complexity as lifting categories to the path fibration are the link with the original definitions.
Indeed,

is exactly Farber’s definition of topological complexity, and for the homotopic distance, the equality

of Lemma

allows us to prove the following result that matches Macías-Virgós and Mosquera-Lois’s definition of homotopic distance:

Proposition 43
.

Let

be maps in
\mathcal{T}
. Then

is the least integer

for which there exists an open cover
(U_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}
of

such that

for all

Proof.

If we have a homotopy
H_{i}\colon U_{i}\times I\to Y
between

and

, then we can define

by

and we have

.

Conversely if we have a map
l_{i}\colon U_{i}\to Y^{I}
such that
\pi\circ l_{i}\simeq(f,g)|_{U_{i}}
, then we also have a map

such that

(because

is a fibration), and we can define a homotopy

between

∎

6.
The pointed case

As noted above, in
\mathrm{\bf Top}
, the open sets of a categorical cover are not supposed to include any specific point, while in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
they must include the basepoint; also in
\mathrm{\bf Top}
, homotopies are free while in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
, homotopies are pointed.

By the way, we don’t want to work in the category
\mathrm{\bf Top^{\ast}}
of pointed spaces and maps, because there, the usual pointed homotopy does not fit entirely with the ‘model category’ framework. See
[
13
, Chapter 10]
.

A priori
, (apart from the trivial case where
x_{0}
is isolated) there is no reason why the lifting category should be equal whether it is calculated in
\mathrm{\bf Top}
or in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
; the latter could be greater than the former. To avoid ambiguity, we will write
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\circ}
when the lifting category is calculated in
\mathrm{\bf Top}
, and
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\ast}
when it is calculated in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
(we drop the superscript “
op
”).
Fortunately, we have the following result:

Theorem 44
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
,
X
normal, and
\iota\colon A\to Y
be maps in
\mathrm{\bf Top^{w}}
.
If
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\circ}_{f}(\iota)=n>0
when
f
and
\iota
are regarded in
\mathrm{\bf Top}
(via the forgetful functor), then
\mathrm{liftcat}^{\ast}_{f}(\iota)=n
.

In this theorem and others below, we require
X
to be normal, i.e. any two disjoint closed subsets of
X
have disjoint open neighborhoods; we do
not
require
X
to be Hausdorff. Normality is not a homotopy invariant, it can be annoying. But since the lifting category is a homotopy invariant, we can replace “normal” by “with same (strong) homotopy type as a normal space”.

Proof.

We can assume that

By hypothesis, there exists an open cover

U_{i}\subsetneq X

and
\iota\circ l_{i}
for all

. But not every

contains the basepoint
x_{0}
of

, the maps

are not pointed, and the homotopies

are free.

As

of

deformable in

into

with a bit smaller open subset, keeping the covering of

. Assume that

for

8.
Triangle inequality

The ‘triangle inequality’ is the following relation for maps
f,g,h
in
\mathcal{T}
with same domain and codomain:

D(f,h)\leqslant D(f,g)+D(g,h).

Remark 61
.

From Definition
7
, we get a ‘boolean homotopic distance’
D^{\mathrm{b}}
. By Proposition
24
,
D^{\mathrm{b}}(f,g)
is 0 if
f\simeq g
and 1 otherwise. Clearly
D^{\mathrm{b}}
satisfies the triangle inequality for any maps.

Theorem 62
.

[
8
, Proposition 3·16]

Let
X
be a normal space and
Y
be any space. Then
D^{\mathrm{op}}
satisfies the triangle inequality for any maps
f,g,h\colon X\to Y
.

Proposition
24
, Proposition
25
and Theorem
62
show that for spaces
X
and
Y
, with
X
normal, the homotopic distance is indeed a distance on the homotopy class of maps from
X
to
Y
.

The triangle inequality is not a consequence of the axioms S0 to S2 because it is not true if the domain of the maps is not normal, see Example
74
. In this section, we list properties that follow from axioms S0 to S2, combined with the triangle inequality, which can be seen as an additional fourth axiom.

Proposition 63
.

For any maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
and
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\colon Y\to Z
such that
X
is normal, we have:

D(f^{\prime}\circ f,g^{\prime}\circ g)\leqslant D(f,g)+D(f^{\prime},g^{\prime}).

Proof.

Apply the triangle inequality to
f^{\prime}\circ f
,
f^{\prime}\circ g
and
g^{\prime}\circ g
. So we have:

D(f^{\prime}\circ f,g^{\prime}\circ g)\leqslant D(f^{\prime}\circ f,f^{\prime}\circ g)+D(f^{\prime}\circ g,g^{\prime}\circ g)\leqslant D(f,g)+D(f^{\prime},g^{\prime}).

The last inequality is given by Propositions
27
and
26
.
∎

Proposition 64
.

For any maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
and
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
such that
X\times X^{\prime}
is normal, we have:

D(f\times f^{\prime},g\times g^{\prime})\leqslant D(f,g)+D(f^{\prime},g^{\prime}).

Proof.

Apply the triangle inequality to
f\times f^{\prime}
,
g\times f^{\prime}
and
g\times g^{\prime}
. So we have:

D(f\times f^{\prime},g\times g^{\prime})\leqslant D(f\times f^{\prime},g\times f^{\prime})+D(g\times f^{\prime},g\times g^{\prime})=D(f,g)+D(f^{\prime},g^{\prime}).

The last equality is given by Proposition
29
.
∎

As a particular case we have:

and

64

(resp.

(resp.

9.
Lusternik-Schnirelmann category

In this section, we come to notions of category that were actually the very first of their kind, known as the ‘Lusternik-Schnirelmann category’ of a space or ‘Clapp-Puppe category’ of a map. We insert them into the axiomatic framework constructed in the previous sections.

We didn’t need constant maps so far. In this section, we work with ‘constant maps’
c\colon\ast\to Y
, where
\ast
denotes the space with a single element. A map

is ‘constant’ (resp. ‘nullhomotopic’) if there is a commutative (resp. homotopy commutative) diagram:

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

A space
Y
is path-connected if all constant maps

are homotopic.

Definition 66

(LS category)
.

The ‘category of a map’

in
\mathcal{T}
, with
Y

f
to any constant map

. We denote
\mathrm{cat}(f)=\mathrm{liftcat}_{f}(c)
.

The category of
{\rm id}_{Y}
is called ‘category of the space’
Y
. We denote
\mathrm{cat}(Y)=\mathrm{cat}({\rm id}_{Y})=\mathrm{secat}(c)
.

The path-connectedness is required in

and

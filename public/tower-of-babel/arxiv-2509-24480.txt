Magnus submonoids and membership problems in one-relator, surface and hyperbolic groups Thanks: The research of the authors was supported by the EPSRC Fellowship grant EP/V032003/1 ‘Algorithmic, topological and geometric aspects of infinite groups, monoids and inverse semigroups’.


Abstract
. Motivated by its applications to the word problem for one-relator inverse monoids, via results of Ivanov, Margolis, and Meakin (2001), we prove several decidability and undecidability results about the submonoid membership problem in one-relator, surface and hyperbolic groups. The membership problem in free-by-cyclic one-relator groups is undecidable in general. We prove a general result that gives sufficient conditions under which membership in submonoids of certain free-by-cyclic one-relator groups is decidable. We apply this result to show that membership in various families of submonoids of surface groups (both orientable and non-orientable) is decidable. We study graded submonoids of one-relator groups, proving results that give conditions under which such a submonoid has decidable membership with linear distortion. These results are then applied to improve on a result of Margolis–Meakin–Šunik by showing the prefix monoid of a surface group has linear distortion. Our results significantly extend previously known positive results proving decidability of membership in (non group) submonoids of surface groups. We also apply our general results, and other techniques, to show that the Magnus submonoid membership problem is decidable in several families of one-relator groups. Here a Magnus submonoid of a one-relator group ⟨ A | r = 1 ⟩ \bigl\langle A\>|\>r=1\bigr\rangle , where every letter of A A appears in r r , is a submonoid generated by a strict subset of A ∪ A − 1 A\cup A^{-1} . In particular we show the Magnus submonoid membership problem is decidable in surface groups, Baumslag–Solitar groups, and in certain free-by-cyclic one-relator groups. We also prove results about the related positivity problem (also called the quasi-Magnus problem) which asks whether membership in the submonoid generated by A A is decidable. We resolve a problem posed by McCammond and Meakin in 2006 by showing that there is a hyperbolic group G G generated by a finite set A A such that it is undecidable whether an element can be represented by a positive word on the generators A A . We show in addition this hyperbolic group can be chosen to be residually finite. We do this by giving a new general method for constructing finitely presented groups with undecidable positivity problem.

1.
Introduction

Fundamental work of Magnus in the 1930s revealed that a key step in understanding the structure and properties of a one-relator group is given by studying the subgroups that are generated by subsets of the generating set of the group. These subgroups are now referred to as
Magnus subgroups
in the literature. More precisely, a Magnus subgroup of a one-relator group
\bigl\langle A\>|\>r=1\bigr\rangle
is a subgroup generated by a subset of
A
that omits one of the letters appearing in the defining relator word
r
. Magnus proved that such subgroups are always free, and showed that membership in Magnus subgroups is decidable
[
37
,
36
]
.
As the trivial subgroup is a Magnus subgroup, in particular Magnus’s results imply that one-relator groups have decidable word problem.
Since then numerous other important results about Magnus subgroups have been proved for one-relator groups (and more generally for one-relator products) and they continue to play a key role in the development of the theory of this class of groups (see e.g.
[
29
]
and the introduction to
[
20
]
). While membership in Magnus subgroups of one-relator groups is decidable, in contrast it is a well-known open problem whether membership is decidable in arbitrary finitely generated subgroups; see e.g.
[
29
, Question 2.8.7]
. The subgroup membership problem for one-relator groups, also called the generalised word problem, has received serious attention in the literature and has been resolved in certain cases; see e.g.

[
22
,
26
,
28
,
40
]
.

More generally one can ask about deciding membership in finitely generated submonoids of a one-relator group. It has only quite recently been discovered that there are one-relator groups with undecidable submonoid membership problem; the first example was given in
[
16
]
, and then further examples have been constructed in
[
46
]
and
[
14
]
. The motivation for studying this question comes in part from connections between this problem and the word problem for one-relator monoids and one-relator inverse monoids. These connections were established in work of Ivanov, Margolis and Meakin
[
24
]
and also of Guba
[
17
]
. In more detail, it is a longstanding open problem whether the word problem is decidable for one-relation monoids. Important work of Ivanov, Margolis and Meakin
[
24
]
gives an approach to the word problem for one-relation monoids that goes via a class of monoids that lie between arbitrary monoids and groups, called inverse monoids. Their results show that to solve the word problem for one-relation monoids it would suffice to show that all one-relator inverse monoids of the form
M=\operatorname{Inv}\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>uv^{-1}=1\bigr\rangle
with
u,v\in\{a,b\}^{+}
and
uv^{-1}
a reduced word have decidable word problem. The word problem for
M=\operatorname{Inv}\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>uv^{-1}=1\bigr\rangle
is even open in the case that
uv^{-1}
is a cyclically reduced word, and resolving this case would be an important first step towards resolving the general problem. The word problem for this class of inverse monoids has received attention and been solved in several instances in the papers
[
11
,
23
,
39
]
. This problem is closely related to the study of submonoids of one-relator groups via the following result which is a straightforward corollary of

[
24
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
24
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
M=\operatorname{Inv}\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>uv^{-1}=1\bigr\rangle
with
u,v\in A^{+}
and
uv^{-1}
cyclically reduced. Let
P=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle a,b\bigr\rangle
be the submonoid of the one-relator group
G=\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>uv^{-1}=1\bigr\rangle
generated by the set
\{a,b\}
. If membership in
P\leq G
is decidable then
M
has decidable word problem.

We note that the word problem for one-relator inverse monoids with relators of the form
uv^{-1}
, with
u
and
v
both positive and
uv^{-1}
a cyclically reduced word, is open. However, in general there do exist one-relator inverse monoids with undecidable word problem; see
[
16
]
.

Theorem
1.1
provides motivation for studying the following problem which is one of the topics of the present article. Given a one-relator group
G=\bigl\langle A\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
, is there an algorithm that takes a word over
A\cup A^{-1}
and decides whether it can be written as a word in
A^{+}
, that is, whether it can be written as a positive word over the generators. Equivalently this question asks whether membership is decidable in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle\leq G
generated by
G
. We call this the
positivity problem
.
More generally given a finitely presented group
\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
we call the monoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle
the
positive submonoid
of
G
(with respect to the generating set
A
) and the positivity problem asks whether membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle
of the group
\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
is decidable.
For general finitely presented groups this problem has been studied in the literature where it is called the
quasi-Magnus problem
. For example, this problem was studied by Bill Boone in his Ph.D. dissertation of 1952 where he gave the first example of a finitely presented group for which this problem is undecidable. These examples with undecidable positivity problem (also called quasi-Magnus problem) were in fact an important ingredient in the construction of the first finitely presented groups with undecidable word problem; see e.g.

[
51
]
.

In addition to the positivity problem, we shall in this paper consider the natural generalisation of this problem to the study of membership in Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups.
In more detail, let
G=\bigl\langle A\>|\>r=1\bigr\rangle
be a one-relator group. Given a subset
X
of
A\cup A^{-1}
such that there is a letter
a
appearing in
r
such that at least one of
a
or
a^{-1}
does not belong to
X
, then we call
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle\leq G
a
Magnus submonoid
of
G
.
In particular, if every generator from
A
appears in the word
r
, then a Magnus submonoid is simply one that is generated by some strict subset of
A\cup A^{-1}
.
Of course every Magnus subgroup is a Magnus submonoid, and also the positive submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle
is a Magnus submonoid.
Note that not every Magnus submonoid is contained in a Magnus subgroup e.g. this is the case for the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle
. We are interested in the question of whether membership in Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups is decidable. Of course a positive answer to this question would in particular show that membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle
is decidable and thus resolve the open question of decidability of the word problem for the class of one-relator inverse monoids in the statement of Theorem
1.1
above.

In the setting of
Theorem

1.1
the positive submonoid in question is in fact a one-relation monoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>u=v\bigr\rangle
which naturally embeds in
\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>u=v\bigr\rangle
by a result of Adian
[
1
]
. The discussion above explains our motivation for studying the problem of deciding membership in this submonoid of this one-relator group. This question is part of a broader study of the membership problem in the positive submonoids of finitely presented groups in general. Moving away from the class of one-relator groups the study of positive submonoids of finitely presented groups arises naturally in other contexts. As explained above Boone gave the first examples of finitely presented groups for which membership in the positive submonoid, what he called the quasi-Magnus problem, is undecidable.
There are other situations in geometric group theory where the positive submonoid of a finitely presented group plays an important role. For instance this is the case in the theory of Artin groups. By a result of Paris
[
47
]
the positive submonoid of any Artin group naturally embeds into that group.
For certain families of Artin groups
algorithmic questions about the Artin group can be translated into corresponding questions about the Artin monoid. This can be useful since Artin monoids better behaved algorithmically than Artin groups; e.g they have nice normal forms
[
3
,
43
]
.
The membership problem for Artin monoids in their Artin groups is interesting since it is straightforward to show that if it is decidable for an Artin group then it solves the word problem for that Artin group, and the word problem for Artin groups remains open in general.
We refer the reader to the survey article
[
41
]
for more background on Artin groups.
Returning to finitely presented groups in general, there are some contexts where membership in the positive submonoid will be decidable. For instance, if the group if virtually free this will be the case since by a result of Benois
[
2
]

free groups have decidable rational subset membership problem from which it follows that virtually free groups also do; see
[
31
]
.
In 2006 McCammond and Meakin posed the natural question of whether this might hold more generally for hyperbolic groups;
specifically, there they ask the following question:

(McCammond and Meakin (2006)
[
42
, Problem 1]
)
Can one decide whether an element in a hyperbolic group
G
generated by
S
is represented by a positive word in the generators?

In Section
5
below we shall resolve this problem by giving
a new general method for constructing finitely presented groups with undecidable positivity problem, and then applying it to give examples of hyperbolic groups for which the problem is undecidable. In addition we show that the hyperbolic group can be chosen to also be residually finite
still with the positivity problem being undecidable.

The investigation of the membership problem in Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups in this paper leads us to prove several general results showing that we can decide membership in particular submonoids of certain free-by-cyclic one-relator groups, including surface groups. Our results increase the known examples of submonoids of surface groups in which membership is decidable. The general submonoid membership problem
remains open for surface groups.
The submonoid membership problem for surface groups is a natural and well-motivated problem due to its connection with other open problems. For example, the submonoid membership problem is open for the special linear group
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
;
see the introduction to the paper
[
9
]
for more background on membership problems in matrix groups.
The group
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
is known to embed the hyperbolic orientable surface group of genus two
[
33
]
and since decidability of the submonoid membership problem is inherited by finitely generated subgroups, a solution to the problem for surface groups would shed light on the corresponding problem for
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
.
If the submonoid membership problem were undecidable for hyperbolic surface groups then it would be especially interesting due to the many examples of groups that have hyperbolic surface subgroups e.g. infinite fundamental groups of closed, hyperbolic, irreducible 3-manifolds
[
25
]
.
See
[
14
, Section 3.1]
for a more detailed discussion of the submonoid membership problem in surface groups.
The submonoid membership problem for surface groups has been considered in the literature e.g. in the work of Margolis–Meakin–Šunik who prove in
[
39
, Proposition 2.10]
that for orientable surface groups membership in the prefix monoid is decidable. They do this by proving there is a quadratic upper distortion function for the prefix monoid;
see Section
6.2
below for definitions of these terms.

In this paper we shall prove both decidability and undecidability results. The main results of this article are the following:

2.1.
Group presentations

We use
A^{*}
to denote the
free monoid
of all words over an alphabet
A
including the empty word denoted by
\varepsilon
. By the
length

l(w)
of a word
w\in A^{*}
we mean the number of letters in that word. We denote by
F_{A}
the
free group
on the alphabet
A
and use
\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
to denote the group defined by the presentation with generators
A
and defining relators
R\subseteq F_{A}
.
We typically write defining relations for a group presentation in the form
w=1
or in the form
u=v
.
When working with a presentation
\langle A\mid R\rangle
of a group
G
, given two words
u
and
v
from
(A\cup A^{-1})^{*}
we write
u\equiv v
to mean that
u
and
v
are equal as words in the free monoid
(A\cup A^{-1})^{*}
, and write
u=_{G}v
, or often just
u=v
, to mean that
u
and
v
represent the same element of the group
G
. Given a subset
X
of a monoid we use
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
to denote the submonoid generated by the set
X
and given a subset
Y
of a group we use
\bigl\langle Y\bigr\rangle
to denote the subgroup generated by
Y
.

Given a monoid
M
generated by a finite set
X
for each element
m\in M
we define

|m|_{X}=\min\{k\mid m=x_{1}\ldots x_{k},\;\mbox{for some}\;x_{i}\in X,\;i=1,\ldots k\}.

Given a word
r\in(A\cup A^{-1})^{*}
and a letter
a\in A
we say that
a
appears in
r
if either
a
or
a^{-1}
is a letter in the word
r
. So for example the letters
a
and
b
both appear in the word
ab^{-1}
. As usual, we use
[a,b]
to denote the commutator
aba^{-1}b^{-1}
.

Given a group
G
generated by a finite set
A
we use
Cay_{A}(G)
to denote the
Cayley graph
of
G
with respect to
A
, which has vertex set
G
and edges between pairs
\{g,ga\}
where
g\in G
and
a\in A
. We view
Cay_{A}(G)
as a metric space with the usual metric on graphs where the distance between two vertices is the length of a shortest path between them.

2.2.
Submonoid membership problem

Let
M
be a monoid finitely generated by a set
A
and let
\phi:A^{*}\rightarrow M
be the canonical homomorphism.
Let
W
be a finite subset of
A^{*}
and let
T=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle W\bigr\rangle
be the submonoid of
M
generated by the subset
\phi(W)
of
M
.
We say that
membership in the submonoid
T\leq M
is decidable
if there is an algorithm that solves the following decision problem:

•

Input
: A word
u\in A^{*}

•

Question
:
\phi(u)\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle W\bigr\rangle
?

Also, we say that the monoid
M
has
decidable (uniform) submonoid membership problem
if
there is an algorithm that solves the following decision problem:

•

Input
: A finite set of words
W\subseteq A^{*}
and a word
w\in A^{*}

•

Question
:
\phi(w)\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle W\bigr\rangle
?

Whether or not a finitely generated monoid
M
has decidable submonoid membership problem
does not depend on the choice of finite generating set for the monoid
M
.
When considering the submonoid membership problem in a group
G
generated by a finite set
A
the definitions are as above working with the finite monoid generating set
A\cup A^{-1}
for the group
G
.

The submonoid membership is well behaved when passing to finitely generated submonoids, in the following sense. If
M
is a finitely generated monoid with decidable submonoid membership problem and if
T
is a finitely generated submonoid of
M
then
T
also has decidable submonoid membership problem. Furthermore, for any submonoid
S\leq T
of
T\leq M
, if membership in the submonoid
S\leq M
is decidable then membership in the submonoid
S\leq T
is decidable; see
[
31
, §5]
.

The
prefix monoid
of a one-relator group
\bigl\langle A\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
is the submonoid
P
generated by the set of all prefixes of the word
w
. We say
\bigl\langle A\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
has decidable
prefix membership problem
if membership in the prefix monoid is decidable. Note that the prefix monoid of a one-relator group depends on the choice of presentation for the group.

We use
A(P_{4})
to denote the group defined by the presentation

\langle a,b,c,d\mid ab=ba,bc=cb,cd=dc\rangle

which is the right-angled Artin group with underlying graph the path
P_{4}
with four vertices
a,b,c,d
. It was proved in
[
32
, Theorem 7]
that this group
A(P_{4})
contains a fixed finitely generated submonoid in which membership is undecidable.

2.3.
One-relator groups, the Magnus hierarchy and Magnus subgroups and submonoids

Let
X
be a finite set.
For a word
w\in(X\cup X^{-1})^{*}
and an element
y\in X\cup X^{-1}
we denote by
|w|_{y}
the number of occurrences of
y
in
w
.
For
t\in X
, the
exponent sum
of
t
in
w
is the number
|w|_{t}-|w|_{t^{-1}}
.
Let
w\in(X\cup X^{-1})^{*}
be a word
such that
w
contains a letter

t\in X
with exponent sum zero. For
w
, we define a word
\rho_{t}(w)
over the infinite alphabet

\Omega=\{x_{l}:\ x\in X\setminus\{t\},l\in\mathbb{Z}\}

obtained from
w
in the following way.
For every letter
x^{\epsilon}
of the word
w
with
x\neq t
upon writing

w\equiv w_{1}x^{\epsilon}w_{2}
rewrite letter
x^{\epsilon}
to
x_{i}^{\epsilon}
, where
i
is the exponent sum of
t
in
w_{1}
. Then at the end delete every occurrence of
t
from the resulting word.
For example if
X=\{a,b,t\}
then

\rho_{t}(b^{-1}t^{-1}a^{-2}t^{2}bt^{-1}a)=b_{0}^{-1}a_{-1}^{-2}b_{1}a_{0}.

Then for each
x\in X\setminus\{t\}
let
\mu_{x}
and
m_{x}
be respectively the smallest and the greatest value of
j
such that
x_{j}
actually appears in
\rho_{t}(w)
.
For instance, in the example in the displayed equation above we have
\mu_{a}=-1
and
m_{a}=0
.

The following result gives the well-known method for expressing certain one-relator groups as HNN extensions of one-relator groups with a shorter defining relator;
see
[
35
, Page 198]
. The formulation of the statement of the result here follows
[
11
, Proposition 7.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
w\in(X\cup X^{-1})^{*}
be a word in which
t\in X
has exponent sum zero such that
\rho_{t}(w)
is cyclically reduced. Then
the group
G=\bigl\langle X\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
is an HNN extension of the group

H=\bigl\langle\Omega_{w}\>|\>\rho_{t}(w)=1\bigr\rangle

where
\Omega_{w}=\{x_{l}:\ x\in X\setminus\{t\},\mu_{x}\leq l\leq m_{x}\}
. The associated subgroups
A
and
B
in this extension
are free groups freely generated by
\Omega_{w}\setminus\{x_{m_{x}}:\ x\in X\setminus\{t\}\}
and
\Omega_{w}\setminus\{x_{\mu_{x}}:\ x\in X\setminus\{t\}\}
, respectively, with the isomorphism
\phi:x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1}
for all
x\in X\setminus\{t\}

and
\mu_{x}\leq i<m_{x}
.

We end this section by recalling the definition of Magnus submonoid from the introduction.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G=\bigl\langle A\>|\>r=1\bigr\rangle
be a one-relator group. A
Magnus submonoid
of
G
is a submonoid generated by a subset
X\subseteq A\cup A^{-1}
with the property that there is a letter
a
appearing in

such that

is not a subset of

. In particular, if every generator from

appears in the word

, then a Magnus submonoid is simply one that is generated by some strict subset of

.

3.
Membership in submonoids of free-by-cyclic one-relator groups

There are free-by-cyclic one-relator groups with undecidable submonoid membership problem. In fact, the group
\bigl\langle a,t\>|\>a(tat^{-1})=(tat^{-1})a\bigr\rangle
is a one-relator group, it is free-by-cyclic of the form
F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
since it is isomorphic to the group
\langle x,y,t\mid txt^{-1}=xy,tyt^{-1}=y\rangle
, yet it has undecidable submonoid membership problem
[
6
,
16
]
. We note also that this group is a 3-manifold group; see
[
45
]
. As discussed in the introduction, surface groups, which are also examples of free-by-cyclic one-relator groups, form an important class of one-relator groups for which the submonoid membership problem remains open.

In this section, we present some general results that identify sufficient conditions under which membership in certain submonoids of some free-by-cyclic one-relator groups is decidable. These general results will subsequently be applied to examples, including the membership problem for certain submonoids of surface groups, later in the article.

We shall now state and prove a general result that provides a sufficient condition for membership in a submonoid of certain one-relator groups to be decidable. The key condition employed in the proof is a max/min-style criterion, reminiscent of Brown’s condition
[
5
]
. The max/min condition in the statement of Theorem
3.1
will imply that the one-relator groups in the statement of that theorem are all free-by-cyclic. In Section
4
below we shall apply Theorem
3.1
to give a large family of submonoids of surface groups in which we can prove membership is decidable; see Theorem
4.1
. In particular that result shows that Theorem
3.1
can be applied to finitely generated free-by-cyclic one-relator groups of the form
F\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
where
F
is an infinite rank free group, as is the case for surface groups.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G=\bigl\langle A,t\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
be a one-relator group with
\sigma_{t}(w)=0
, where
A=\{a,b,c,\ldots\}
is a finite set. Applying the Magnus rewriting process to
G
, we obtain

G=\bigl\langle a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots,t\>|\>\overline{w}=1,\quad ta_{i}t^{-1}=a_{i+1},\quad tb_{i}t^{-1}=b_{i+1},\quad tc_{i}t^{-1}=c_{i+1},\ldots\bigr\rangle

where
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\overline{w}
is the word over
\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}^{\pm 1}
obtained from
w
by applying the Magnus procedure with respect to the letter
t
.

Assume that for some letter
x\in A
, the element
x_{\mathrm{max}(x)}
appears exactly once in the word
\overline{w}
, and
x_{\mathrm{min}(x)}
also appears exactly once in
\overline{w}
, where

\mathrm{max}(x)=\max\{i\mid x_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{min}(x)=\min\{i\mid x_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\}.

Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in FG(A,t)
be elements each with
t
-exponent sum
\geq 0
. Then membership in the submonoid
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle\leqslant G
is decidable.

Remark 3.2
.

The condition in the statement of Theorem
3.1
, that the words
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in FG(A,t)
each have
t
-exponent sum
\geq 0
is essential and cannot be omitted. Indeed, consider the group

G=\bigl\langle a,t\>|\>tata^{-1}t^{-1}at^{-1}a^{-1}=1\bigr\rangle.

Rewriting the relator with respect to
t
yields the word
\overline{w}=a_{1}a_{2}^{-1}a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}
, which does satisfy the max/min condition stated in the theorem. However, it is known (see
[
16
]
) that this group contains a finitely generated submonoid for which the membership problem is undecidable.

The rest of this section will be devoted to the proof of
Theorem
3.1
.
A key lemma we shall need to prove the theorem is the following.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
11
, Corollary 6.10]
)
.

Let
X
be a finite alphabet, and let

G=F_{X}\;\ast_{t,\phi:P\rightarrow Q}

be an HNN extension, where
P
and
Q
are both finitely generated. Then, for any finite subsets
W_{0},W_{1},\ldots,W_{d}
and
W_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,W_{d}^{\prime}
of
F_{X}
, the membership problem for the submonoid

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle W_{0}\cup W_{1}t\cup\ldots\cup W_{d}t^{d}\cup tW_{1}^{\prime}\cup t^{2}W_{2}^{\prime}\cup\ldots\cup t^{d}W_{d}^{\prime}\bigr\rangle

is decidable.

A key observation is that, in the lemma, the subgroups
P
and
Q
are arbitrary subgroups of the free group
F_{X}
, and
\phi
is an isomorphism between them. In particular, there is no assumption that
P
is generated by a subset of
X
.

We now fix some notation that will remain in place throughout the rest of this section. As in the statement of Theorem
3.1
we let
G=\bigl\langle A,t\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
be a one-relator group with
\sigma_{t}(w)=0
. Let
A=\{a,b,c,\ldots\}
. Applying Magnus rewriting to
G
, we obtain

G=\bigl\langle a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots,t\>|\>\overline{w}=1,\quad ta_{i}t^{-1}=a_{i+1},\quad tb_{i}t^{-1}=b_{i+1},\quad tc_{i}t^{-1}=c_{i+1},\ldots\bigr\rangle

where
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\overline{w}
is the word over
\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}^{\pm 1}
obtained from
w
by applying the Magnus procedure with respect to the letter
t
. We assume that, for some letter
x\in A
, the letter
x_{\mathrm{max}(x)}
appears exactly once in the word
\overline{w}
, and
x_{\mathrm{min}(x)}
also appears exactly once in
\overline{w}
, where

\mathrm{max}(x)=\max\{i\mid x_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{min}(x)=\min\{i\mid x_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\}.

Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in F_{A\cup\{t\}}
be words all having
t
-exponent sum
\geq 0
. The rest of this section will be devoted to the proof that membership in the submonoid
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle\leqslant G
is decidable. We shall divide the proof into a sequence of lemmas.

In the following statement, we use
\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})
to denote the word obtained by adding
i
to the subscript of every letter appearing in
\overline{w}
. For example, if
\overline{w}\equiv[x_{0},z_{0}]y_{0}y_{1}^{-1}
, then
\sigma_{2}(\overline{w})\equiv[x_{2},z_{2}]y_{2}y_{3}^{-1}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The group
G
admits the infinite presentation

G=\bigl\langle a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots,t\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(i\in\mathbb{Z}),\quad ta_{i}t^{-1}=a_{i+1},\quad tb_{i}t^{-1}=b_{i+1},\quad tc_{i}t^{-1}=c_{i+1},\ldots\bigr\rangle

where
\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})
is the word obtained by adding
i
to the subscript of every letter appearing in
\overline{w}
.

Proof.

All the relations
\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1
hold, since they are obtained from the relation
\overline{w}=1
by conjugating with appropriate powers of
t
.
∎

A central idea in the proof of Theorem
3.1
will be that, for every interval
[n,m]
of integers with
n<m
, we can extract the corresponding subset of generators and relations from the infinite presentation above to obtain a finite presentation. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

For any pair of integers
n<m
, we define

L_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]},t\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m),\quad C_{[n,m]}\bigr\rangle

where
A_{[n,m]}
is the set of all letters that appear in the set of words
\{\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\mid n\leq i\leq m\}
, and
C_{[n,m]}
is the set of all relations of the form
tx_{i}t^{-1}=x_{i+1}
such that both
x_{i}\in A_{[n,m]}
and
x_{i+1}\in A_{[n,m]}
, where
x\in\{a,b,c,\ldots\}
.

Example 3.6
.

In the case of the surface group

S_{2}=\langle a,b,c,d\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}cdc^{-1}d^{-1}=1\rangle=\langle a,b,c,t\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}ctc^{-1}t^{-1}=1\rangle,

Magnus rewriting with respect to
t
gives the word
[a_{0},b_{0}]c_{0}c_{1}^{-1}
and then an example of such an interval presentation is the following:

\displaystyle L_{[0,2]}=\bigl\langle

\displaystyle a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\ t\ \mid\

\displaystyle[a_{0},b_{0}]c_{0}=c_{1},\quad[a_{1},b_{1}]c_{1}=c_{2},\quad[a_{2},b_{2}]c_{2}=c_{3},\quad

\displaystyle ta_{0}t^{-1}=a_{1},\quad ta_{1}t^{-1}=a_{2},\quad tb_{0}t^{-1}=b_{1},\quad tb_{1}t^{-1}=b_{2},\quad

\displaystyle tc_{0}t^{-1}=c_{1},\quad tc_{1}t^{-1}=c_{2},\quad tc_{2}t^{-1}=c_{3}\quad\bigr\rangle

This presentation corresponds to the interval
[0,2]
, where we use the index
i
to label the relation
[a_{i},c_{i}]b_{i}=b_{i+1}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

The group
L_{[n,m]}
is isomorphic to
G
for all
n<m
.
In fact, the natural injective map from the generators of
L_{[n,m]}
to the generators of
G
extends to an isomorphism.

Proof.

We begin with the presentation for
L_{[n,m]}
, and then adjoin the remaining (infinitely many) redundant generators to obtain the full generating set of
G
. Next, we add the infinite family of relations
\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, each of which clearly holds in
G
as they can be derived by conjugating existing relations by powers of
t
. Since these additions do not alter the isomorphism type of the group, the map extends to an isomorphism.
∎

In the next two results we invoke the max/min conditions from the statement of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For all
n<m
, the group

H_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]}\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m)\bigr\rangle

is a free group.

Proof.

Up to relabelling, we may assume that the letter
a\in A
satisfies the following:
a_{\mathrm{max}(a)}
appears exactly once in the word
\overline{w}
, and
a_{\mathrm{min}(a)}
appears exactly once in
\overline{w}
, where

\mathrm{max}(a)=\max\{i\mid a_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\},\quad\mathrm{min}(a)=\min\{i\mid a_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\}.

Since
a_{\mathrm{max}(a)}
appears exactly once in
\overline{w}
, it follows that for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the letter
\sigma_{i}(a_{\mathrm{max}(a)})=a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+i}
appears exactly once in the word
\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})
, for all
n\leq i\leq m
.

We now perform a sequence of Tietze transformations to eliminate the generators

\{a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+n},\ldots,a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+m}\},

starting with
\sigma_{m}(\overline{w})
to eliminate
a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+m}
and remove the relator
\sigma_{m}(\overline{w})
. We then proceed inductively, working down one generator at a time, until we use
\sigma_{n}(\overline{w})
to eliminate
a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+n}
and remove the relator
\sigma_{n}(\overline{w})
. This process yields a presentation with the generating set

A_{[n,m]}\setminus\{a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+n},\ldots,a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+m}\},

and no remaining relators. Hence,
H_{[n,m]}
is a free group on this set of generators.
∎

Note that the previous proof relied only on the max condition and did not make use of the min condition.

Remark 3.9
.

The max (or min) condition used in proof of Lemma
3.8
is necessary. Consider the example

\bigl\langle a,t\>|\>aatatat^{-2}=1\bigr\rangle,

which, working with respect to the generator
t
, rewrites to

\bigl\langle a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\>|\>a_{0}^{2}a_{1}a_{2}^{2}=1\bigr\rangle,

a presentation of a free group. However, when we consider a shift of this presentation, for example:

\bigl\langle a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\>|\>a_{0}^{2}a_{1}a_{2}^{2}=1,\ a_{1}^{2}a_{2}a_{3}^{2}=1\bigr\rangle,

we find that eliminating the generator
a_{2}
yields a group isomorphic to the amalgamated free product
\langle a_{0}\rangle\ast_{a_{0}^{2}=a_{3}^{2}a_{1}^{2}a_{3}^{2}a_{1}}\langle a_{1},a_{3}\rangle
, which is not a free group.
To see that this is not a free group it suffices (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 1.1(a)]
) to verify that
a_{0}^{2}
is not primitive in the free group
F_{a_{0}}
, which is clear, and also that
a_{3}^{2}a_{1}^{2}a_{3}^{2}a_{1}
is not primitive in
F_{\{a_{1},a_{3}\}}
. For the latter, consider the group
\bigl\langle a_{1},a_{3}\>|\>a_{3}^{2}a_{1}^{2}a_{3}^{2}a_{1}=1\bigr\rangle
and make the substitution
a_{1}=a_{3}^{-2}b
to obtain the isomorphic group

\bigl\langle b,a_{3}\>|\>ba_{3}^{-2}bb=1\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle b,a_{3}\>|\>b^{3}=a_{3}^{2}\bigr\rangle,

which is a torus knot group that is not a free group. Hence
\bigl\langle a_{1},a_{3}\>|\>a_{3}^{2}a_{1}^{2}a_{3}^{2}a_{1}=1\bigr\rangle
is not a free group and thus the word
a_{3}^{2}a_{1}^{2}a_{3}^{2}a_{1}
is not primitive, as required.
This illustrates that the max (or min) condition is essential in Lemma
3.8
to ensure that the groups
H_{[n,m]}
are free.

Lemma 3.10
.

Fix some
n<m
. Let

H_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]}\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m)\bigr\rangle.

Define

P=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m-1]}\bigr\rangle\leq H_{[n,m]},\quad\text{and}\quad Q=\bigl\langle A_{[n+1,m]}\bigr\rangle\leq H_{[n,m]}.

Then

P\cong H_{[n,m-1]}\quad\text{and}\quad Q\cong H_{[n+1,m]},

and the map
\phi:P\rightarrow Q
defined by

x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1},\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{[n,m-1]},

is an isomorphism between
P
and
Q
.

Proof.

Up to relabelling, we may assume that the letter
a\in A
satisfies that
a_{\mathrm{max}(a)}
appears exactly once in the word
\overline{w}
, and
a_{\mathrm{min}(a)}
appears exactly once in
\overline{w}
, where

\mathrm{max}(a)=\max\{i\mid a_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{min}(a)=\min\{i\mid a_{i}\text{ appears in }\overline{w}\}.

We can use the relation
\sigma_{m}(\overline{w})

to eliminate the generator
a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+m}
and remove the relator
\sigma_{m}(\overline{w})
from the presentation. This yields

H_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]}\setminus\{a_{\mathrm{max}(a)+m}\}\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m-1)\bigr\rangle.

It follows that

P=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m-1]}\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m-1]}\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m-1)\bigr\rangle\cong H_{[n,m-1]}.

Similarly, using the relation

\sigma_{n}(\overline{w})

we may eliminate the generator
a_{\mathrm{min}(a)+n}
and remove the relator
\sigma_{n}(\overline{w})
, giving

H_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]}\setminus\{a_{\mathrm{min}(a)+n}\}\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n+1\leq i\leq m)\bigr\rangle.

It then follows that

Q=\bigl\langle A_{[n+1,m]}\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle A_{[n+1,m]}\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n+1\leq i\leq m)\bigr\rangle\cong H_{[n+1,m]}.

It is clear that
\phi
defines an isomorphism from
P
to
Q
, since the respective presentations differ only by a uniform shift of indices in the generators and relators.
∎

Note that in the previous lemma, all of the groups
H_{[n,m]}
,
P
, and
Q
are free groups. However, none of them is free on the set of generators given in the presentations that define them in the statement of the lemma (that is, there exist relations among the generators).
Combining the previous lemma with the earlier lemmas in this section we can now express the group
G
as an HNN extension of the free group
H_{[n,m]}
with respect to the isomorphism
\phi:P=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m-1]}\bigr\rangle\rightarrow Q=\bigl\langle A_{[n+1,m]}\bigr\rangle
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n<m
. Then

G\cong L_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]},t\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m),\quad C_{[n,m]}\bigr\rangle

is isomorphic to the HNN extension of the free group
H_{[n,m]}
with respect to the isomorphism

\phi:P=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m-1]}\bigr\rangle\rightarrow Q=\bigl\langle A_{[n+1,m]}\bigr\rangle

between finitely generated subgroups, where
\phi
is defined by

x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1},\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{[n,m-1]}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.10
that this is a well-defined HNN extension. The result then follows immediately from Lemma
3.7
and the relevant definitions.
∎

The significance of the previous lemma is that it expresses
L_{[n,m]}
as an HNN extension of a free group, where the associated map
\phi
is an isomorphism between finitely generated subgroups of that free group. Consequently, Lemma
3.3
may be applied to
L_{[n,m]}
using this specific decomposition of
L_{[n,m]}
as an HNN extension. When we apply Lemma
3.3
to the group
L_{[n,m]}
, we obtain the following result:

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n<m
. Let

G\cong L_{[n,m]}=\bigl\langle A_{[n,m]},t\>|\>\sigma_{i}(\overline{w})=1\;(n\leq i\leq m),\quad C_{[n,m]}\bigr\rangle.

Let
W_{0},W_{1},\ldots,W_{d}
and
W_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,W_{d}^{\prime}
be finite
subsets of
(A_{[n,m]}^{\pm 1})^{\ast}
.
Then the membership problem for the submonoid

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle W_{0}\cup W_{1}t\cup\ldots\cup W_{d}t^{d}\cup tW_{1}^{\prime}\cup t^{2}W_{2}^{\prime}\cup\ldots\cup t^{d}W_{d}^{\prime}\bigr\rangle

is decidable.

Proof.

It follows from the definitions together with Lemma
3.8
that the subgroup of
L_{[n,m]}
generated by
A_{[n,m]}
is a free group (although it is not free on the generating set
A_{[n,m]}
itself). By Lemma
3.11
,
L_{[n,m]}
is an HNN extension of this free group, with respect to an isomorphism between finitely generated subgroups. Thus, the hypotheses of Lemma
3.3
are satisfied and the result now follows by applying that lemma.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
3.1
. The basic idea of the proof is that, given a finite generating set for a submonoid of
G
satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem, we can choose an interval
[n,m]
sufficiently large such that, when working in
L_{[n,m]}
, the generating set can be expressed in the form required by Lemma
3.12
. The theorem then follows directly from an application of that lemma.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in F_{A\cup\{t\}}
all have
t
-exponent sum
\geq 0
. Working with respect to the infinite presentation

G=\bigl\langle a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots,t\>|\>\overline{w}=1,\quad ta_{i}t^{-1}=a_{i+1},\quad tb_{i}t^{-1}=b_{i+1},\quad tc_{i}t^{-1}=c_{i+1},\ldots\bigr\rangle,

we may write each
w_{l}
in the form
w_{l}=_{G}t^{j_{l}}u_{l}
, where
u_{l}
is a word in
(\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots\}^{\pm 1})^{*}
. By the assumptions of the theorem, each
j_{l}
is greater than or equal to
0
. Let
F
denote the finite set of generators from
\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\ldots\}
that appear in the finite collection of words
\{u_{l}:1\leq l\leq k\}
. Choose integers
n<m
such that
F\subseteq A_{[n,m]}
, the generating set of the finite presentation of
L_{[n,m]}
. By
Lemma

3.7
it follows that the equality
w_{l}=_{L_{[n,m]}}t^{j_{l}}u_{l}
holds in
L_{[n,m]}
for all
l
.
Now, by Lemma
3.12
, membership in the submonoid

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t^{j_{1}}u_{1},\ldots,t^{j_{k}}u_{k}\bigr\rangle\leqslant L_{[n,m]}

is decidable. Hence by
Lemma

3.7
it follows that membership in
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle\leqslant G
is decidable.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Note that all steps involved in the proof of
Theorem

3.1
are effective. In particular, we can explicitly write down the finite presentation
L_{[n,m]}
and compute the expressions of the form
t^{j}u
required in the proof. So the proof of
Theorem

3.1
actually shows that there is a uniform algorithm that takes any one-relator group and any collection of words
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
satisfying the conditions in the statement of Theorem
3.1
, and any word
\tau
over
(A\cup\{t\})^{\pm 1}
, and decides whether or not
\tau
represents an element in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle
.

It is natural to ask whether

Theorem

3.1
can be generalised in the following way.

Question 3.14
.

Let
G=\bigl\langle A,t\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
be a one-relator group with
\sigma_{t}(w)=0
. Suppose that applying the Magnus procedure with respect to
t
yields a free group. If
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in F_{A\cup\{t\}}
all have
t
-exponent sum at least
0
(or dually, at most
0
), is the submonoid membership problem decidable in
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle\leqslant G
?

4.
Membership in surface groups and Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups

In this section we first show how Theorem
3.1
can be applied to show membership can be decided in a large family of finite generated submonoids of surface groups. Then we shall go on to consider the membership problem in Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups proving that it is decidable for various families.

4.1.
Surface Groups

We can apply
Theorem

3.1
to prove the following result concerning surface groups:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S_{g}
be the genus-
g
orientable surface group:

S_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\>|\>[a_{1},b_{1}]\cdots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1\bigr\rangle.

Let
t\in\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\}
be any generator. If
\,W
is a finite set of words in the free group on

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\}

such that each word in
W
has
t
-exponent sum at least
0
, then membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle W\bigr\rangle\leq S_{g}
is decidable.

Proof.

Since permuting the subscripts cyclically gives an isomorphic group, and interchanging
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
for all
i
gives an isomorphic group, we may assume without loss of generality that
t=a_{1}
. Rewrite the presentation as

S_{g}=\bigl\langle t,a_{2},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\>|\>[t,b_{1}][a_{2},b_{2}]\cdots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1\bigr\rangle.

The defining relator may be rewritten as

tb_{1}t^{-1}=[b_{g},a_{g}]\cdots[b_{2},a_{2}]b_{1}.

Applying Magnus rewriting will rewrite the defining relation as

b_{1,1}=[b_{g,0},a_{g,0}]\cdots[b_{2,0},a_{2,0}]b_{1,0},

where we use a pair of subscripts rather than a double subscript for clarity. This yields the following infinite presentation for the group
S_{g}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gp}\bigl\langle t,\ a_{2,i},\ldots,a_{g,i},\ b_{1,i},\ldots,b_{g,i}\ (i\in\mathbb{Z})\ \mid\

\displaystyle b_{1,1}^{-1}[b_{g,0},a_{g,0}]\cdots[b_{2,0},a_{2,0}]b_{1,0}=1,

\displaystyle ta_{m,n}t^{-1}=a_{m,n+1},

\displaystyle tb_{p,n}t^{-1}=b_{p,n+1}\quad(2\leq m\leq g,\ 1\leq p\leq g,\ n\in\mathbb{Z})\bigr\rangle

The word
b_{1,1}^{-1}[b_{g,0},a_{g,0}]\cdots[b_{2,0},a_{2,0}]b_{1,0}
satisfies the max–min condition of
Theorem

3.1
(with
b_{1,1}
and
b_{1,0}
appearing exactly once), so we can apply that theorem to conclude the result.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The previous theorem generalizes the result proved in
[
39
]
that the surface group
S_{g}
has a decidable prefix membership problem—since every prefix of the defining relator clearly has
a_{1}
-exponent sum at least
0
. In fact, for any cyclic conjugate of the defining relator,
Theorem

4.1
shows that the prefix membership problem remains decidable, thereby also recovering the result
[
11
, Theorem 5.10, Example 5.11]
.

As a corollary, we also obtain the following:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

S_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\>|\>[a_{1},b_{1}]\cdots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1\bigr\rangle.

Then for any finite set of elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in S_{g}
, there exists a choice of signs
\epsilon_{i}\in\{-1,+1\}
such that membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle x_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,x_{k}^{\epsilon_{k}}\bigr\rangle
is decidable.

This corollary has a connection to a notion arising in the theory of left-orderable groups. As explained, for example, in
[
8
, Theorem 1.48]
, a group
G
is left-orderable if and only if for every finite subset
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}
, there exist signs
\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1
such that the identity element does not lie in the subsemigroup
\operatorname{Sgp}\bigl\langle x_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}^{\epsilon_{n}}\bigr\rangle
.

We now turn our attention to Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups. First, we apply the results above to show that this problem is decidable for surface groups, and then later in this section, we shall extend our analysis to certain other families of one-relator groups, including the Baumslag–Solitar groups and some
2
-generator free-by-cyclic one-relator groups.

As already discussed in the introduction, the general question of deciding membership in Magnus submonoids of one-relator groups, however, remains open – and is likely to be difficult as it would imply solutions to other challenging open problems about the word problem for one-relator inverse monoids; see Theorem
1.1
and the discussion in the introduction above.

Theorem 4.4
.

The membership problem in Magnus submonoids of orientable surface groups

S_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\>|\>[a_{1},b_{1}]\ldots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1\bigr\rangle

is decidable. Furthermore, membership in Magnus submonoids of the non-orientable surface groups

\mathcal{N}_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g}\>|\>a_{1}^{2}\ldots a_{g}^{2}=1\bigr\rangle

is also decidable.

Proof.

Let
M
be a Magnus submonoid of
S_{g}
. Then there is some generator
x
such that either
x
or
x^{-1}
does not belong to the generating set. Hence, the generating set for
M
satisfies that every generator has
x
-exponent sum either
\geq 0
or
\leq 0
. The result then follows by an application of
Theorem

4.1
or its dual.

Now let
M
be a Magnus submonoid of
\mathcal{N}_{g}
. If
g=2
, then
\mathcal{N}_{g}
is the fundamental group of the Klein bottle, which is virtually abelian and thus has decidable
submonoid membership problem (see e.g.
[
31
]
);
in particular, membership in Magnus submonoids is decidable. Hence, assume
g\geq 3
.

Let
X
be the subset of
\{a_{1},a_{1}^{-1},\ldots,a_{g},a_{g}^{-1}\}
generating
M
. If all positive letters
a_{i}
are included in
X
, then
M=\mathcal{N}_{g}
since every inverse can be expressed positively. Dually, if all negative letters
a_{i}^{-1}
are included in
X
, then by the inverse of the defining relator we can generate all positive letters, so again
M=\mathcal{N}_{g}
.

Therefore, there must exist distinct indices
i,j
such that
a_{i}\in X
but
a_{i}^{-1}\notin X
, while
a_{j}^{-1}\in X
but
a_{j}\notin X
. By cyclically permuting the generators (an isomorphism), we may assume
a_{1}\in X
but
a_{1}^{-1}\notin X
, and
a_{j}^{-1}\in X
but
a_{j}\notin X
for some
j\neq 1
. Suppose first that
j\neq g
, and perform the linear substitution
a_{j}=xa_{1}^{-1}
. Consider the isomorphic one-relator group

\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},x,a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{g}\>|\>a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{2}\ldots a_{j-1}^{2}(xa_{1}^{-1})^{2}a_{j+1}^{2}\ldots a_{g}^{2}=1\bigr\rangle.

Since
j\neq g
, this relator is cyclically reduced, and has
a_{1}
-exponent sum zero. Applying Magnus rewriting with respect to
a_{1}
yields

a_{2,2}^{2}\ldots a_{j-1,2}^{2}x_{2}x_{1}a_{j+1,0}^{2}\ldots a_{g,0}^{2},

which satisfies the max–min property from the statement of
Theorem

3.1
with respect to the symbol
x
.

In this new presentation, the generators in
X
include
a_{j}^{-1}=a_{1}x^{-1}
,
a_{1}
, and some subset of
\{a_{2},a_{2}^{-1},\ldots,a_{g},a_{g}^{-1}\}
. Observe that all these generators have
a_{1}
-exponent sum at least zero. Hence, since the max–min condition
from the statement of
Theorem

3.1
holds, we can apply that result to complete the proof.

If
j=g
, then we argue similarly using the substitution
a_{j}=a_{1}^{-1}x
. The defining relator remains cyclically reduced for
g\geq 3
. This completes the proof.
∎

It is worth noting that not every Magnus submonoid is contained in a Magnus subgroup, and Magnus submonoids are typically not free monoids. The exact structure of Magnus submonoids is interesting and worthy of future study.

We can generalise Theorem
4.4
slightly to obtain the following.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
M
be a Magnus submonoid of

S_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\>|\>[a_{1},b_{1}]\ldots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1\bigr\rangle

Then membership in any finitely generated submonoid of
M
is decidable.
Similarly, if
N
is a
Magnus submonoid of

\mathcal{N}_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g}\>|\>a_{1}^{2}\ldots a_{g}^{2}=1\bigr\rangle

then membership in any finitely generated submonoid of
N
is decidable.

Proof.

Let
X=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\}^{\pm 1}
. Since
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle Y\bigr\rangle
is a Magnus submonoid of
S_{g}
one has
Y\subsetneq X
. Pick an element
x\in X\setminus Y
. Now, let
N=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle Z\bigr\rangle
, be a finitely generated submonoid of
M
. Every element
z
of
Z
has
x
-exponent sum
\geq 0
, as of product of such elements. Now the result follows from
Theorem

3.1
by arguing in the same way as in the proof of
Theorem

4.4
. The proof for
\mathcal{N}_{g}
follows analogously.
∎

4.2.
Baumslag–Solitar groups

In this subsection, we show that membership in Magnus submonoids is decidable in all Baumslag–Solitar groups. We remark that the general submonoid membership problem for Baumslag–Solitar groups remains open in general, although it was proved in
[
7
]
that it is decidable in
BS(1,n)
.

Theorem 4.6
.

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, membership in Magnus submonoids of the Baumslag–Solitar group

BS(m,n)=\langle a,t\mid ta^{m}t^{-1}=a^{n}\rangle

is decidable.

Remark 4.7
.

One might wonder whether for any
2
-generator one-relator group we can show that membership in Magnus submonoids is decidable. This question is likely to be hard since if it is decidable then by Theorem
1.1
it would resolve the currently open word problem for
2
-generator one-relator inverse monoids with cyclically reduced relator
uv^{-1}
where
u
and
v
are both positive words.

For the proof of
Theorem

4.6
, we begin with the case
m,n\geq 1
, as established in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

For all
1\leq m<n
, membership in Magnus submonoids of Baumslag–Solitar groups
BS(m,n)
is decidable.

To prove the lemma, we use the following result:

Proposition 4.9

(
[
10
, Section 2]
)
.

Let
1\leq m<n
, and consider the Baumslag–Solitar group

BS(m,n)=\langle a,t\mid ta^{m}t^{-1}=a^{n}\rangle.

Set
A=a^{-1}
,
T=t^{-1}
.
The following finite rewriting system is complete:

\displaystyle aA

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle Aa

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle tT

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle Tt

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle a^{n}t

\displaystyle\to ta^{m},

\displaystyle At

\displaystyle\to a^{n-1}tA^{m},

\displaystyle a^{m}T

\displaystyle\to Ta^{n},

\displaystyle AT

\displaystyle\to a^{m-1}TA^{n}.

We refer the reader to
[
18
, Chapter 12]
for the definition of complete rewriting system.
It follows from
4.9
that the irreducible words with respect to that rewriting system give a set of normal forms for the elements of
BS(m,n)
where a word is irreducible if none of the rewrite rules can be applied to it. When working with this complete rewriting system given any word
w
over the generators we use
\overline{w}
to denote the unique irreducible word such that
w=\overline{w}
in the group.

Proof of
Lemma

4.8
.

Given a nonempty proper subset
S\subsetneq\{a,A,t,T\}
, we want to decide whether an element
g\in BS(m,n)
belongs to the Magnus submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
.

Note that for
S=\emptyset
, we have
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle=\{1\}
, and the problem reduces to the word problem in
BS(m,n)
, which is decidable since
BS(m,n)
is an HNN extension
\mathbb{Z}*_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Next, for
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\{a,A,t,T\}
we consider the following cases:

(1) If
S\subseteq\{a,A\}
or
S\subseteq\{t,T\}
, then by Magnus’ Freiheitssatz,
and Magnus’ Theorem (see
[
35
, Chapter 2, Section 5]
),
the subgroup
\langle S\rangle
is free in
BS(m,n)
and membership in the Magnus subgroup
\langle S\rangle
is decidable. Hence membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
(which lies inside the free group
\langle S\rangle
) is decidable by Benois’ theorem
[
2
]
.

(2) If
\{t\}\subseteq S\subseteq\{a,A,t\}
or
\{T\}\subseteq S\subseteq\{a,A,T\}
, the result follows from Lemma
3.3

by taking
W_{0}=S\cap\{a,A\}
and
W_{1}=\{1\}
.

(3) The remaining cases to consider are

S_{1}=\{a,t,T\}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}=\{A,t,T\}.

(a)
Let
w\in S_{1}^{\ast}=\{a,t,T\}^{\ast}
. Since the only rewrite rules in the complete rewriting system in the statement of Proposition
4.9
with left-hand side belonging to
S_{1}^{\ast}
are

tT\to 1,\quad Tt\to 1,\quad a^{n}t\to ta^{m},\quad a^{m}T\to Ta^{n}

it follows that the the reduced form
\overline{w}
also belongs to
S_{1}^{\ast}
.
Given any word
u\in\{a,A,t,T\}^{\ast}
, if
u\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S_{1}\bigr\rangle
this means that there exists
w\in S_{1}^{\ast}
such that
u=w
in
BS(m,n)
. Since the system is complete it follows that
\overline{u}\equiv\overline{w}\in S_{1}^{*}
.
Conversely, given any word
u\in\{a,A,t,T\}^{\ast}
, if
\overline{u}\in\{a,t,T\}^{\ast}=S_{1}^{*}
then
u\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S_{1}\bigr\rangle
.
Hence,
u\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S_{1}\bigr\rangle
if and only if
\overline{u}\in S_{1}^{\ast}
.

(b) For
S_{2}=\{A,t,T\}
, we observe that it is straightforward to verify that the rewriting system

\displaystyle Aa

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle aA

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle tT

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle Tt

\displaystyle\to 1,

\displaystyle A^{n}t

\displaystyle\to tA^{m},

\displaystyle at

\displaystyle\to A^{n-1}ta^{m},

\displaystyle A^{m}T

\displaystyle\to TA^{n},

\displaystyle aT

\displaystyle\to A^{m-1}Ta^{n}.

is a (different) finite complete rewriting system defining the same group

BS(m,n)=\langle a,t\mid ta^{m}t^{-1}=a^{n}\rangle

Now working with respect to this complete rewriting system we can prove that membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S_{2}\bigr\rangle\leq BC(m,n)
is decidable using exactly the same argument as in the previous case (a).
∎

Now we show that membership in Magnus submonoids of any Baumslag–Solitar groups
BS(m,n)
is decidable.

Proof of
Theorem

4.6
.

If one of
m,n
is equal to
0
, or if
m=n
, then the group
BS(m,n)
is virtually a direct product of
\mathbb{Z}
with a finite-rank free group, hance it has decidable
submonoid membership problem

[
32
, Theorem 1]
, and hence membership on Magnus submonoids is decidable as well.

If
1\leq m<n
, then
Lemma

4.8
gives the result.
The case
1\leq n<m
follows, as
BS(m,n)\cong BS(n,m)
.
The case
m,n<0
follows from above, because
BS(-m,-n)=BS(n,m)
.
The last case to consider is
m<0
, and
n>0
. The case
m>0,n<0
is analogous.
Note that points (1) and (2) in the proof of
Lemma

4.8
above do not use rewriting systems, so we can apply the same proof here as well. On the other hand, for

S_{1}=\{a,t,T\}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}=\{A,t,T\}.

one can easily verify that
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S_{1}\bigr\rangle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S_{2}\bigr\rangle=BS(m,n)
and membership in these submonoids is clearly decidable.
∎

4.3.
Two-generator free-by-cyclic one-relator groups

We follow the explanation of Brown’s criterion given in
[
13
, Section 5]
.
Let

G=\langle a,b\mid w=1\rangle

be a two-generator free-by-cyclic group. By definition, this means there is a surjective homomorphism

\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}

such that
\ker(\phi)
is a free group.

It follows from work of Moldavanskii
[
44
]
and Brown
[
5
]

that in this situation, the map
\phi
can be chosen so that
\ker(\phi)
is finitely generated free. Moreover, given such a homomorphism
\phi
, the fact that the kernel is finitely generated is equivalent to a certain max-min condition.
We now state this result as formulated in
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 4.10
.

[
5
, Theorem 4.3]

Let

G=\langle a,b\mid w=1\rangle

be a two-generator free-by-cyclic group. Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\equiv w
denote the set of all prefixes of the word
w
.
Then there exists a surjective homomorphism
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\ker(\phi)
is a finitely generated free group, and one of the following holds:

(1)

\phi(a)
and
\phi(b)
are both nonzero, and the sequence
\phi(w_{1}),\ldots,\phi(w_{n})
has a unique minimum and a unique maximum;

(2)

Exactly one of
\phi(a)
or
\phi(b)
is zero (and thus the other is nonzero since
\phi
is surjective), and the sequence
\phi(w_{1}),\ldots,\phi(w_{n})
has exactly two maxima and exactly two minima, and
w
is not equal to
a^{2}
or
b^{2}
.

Combining the result above with
Theorem

3.1
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
G=\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>r=1\bigr\rangle
be a
2
-generator free-by-cyclic one-relator group. Further, suppose that the relator
r
has
a
-exponent sum equal to zero and
b
-exponent sum not equal to zero.
Then for any finite set of words
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
all with
a
-exponent sum at least zero, the membership problem in the submonoid

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\bigr\rangle\leqslant G

is decidable.
In particular,
G
has decidable positivity problem.

Proof.

It follows from these assumptions that the homomorphism
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
is essentially unique,
in the sense that
\phi(b)=0
and
\phi(a)\in\{1,-1\}
. Then it follows from
Theorem
4.10
(2) that the max-min condition in the statement of
Theorem

3.1
is satisfied (this is explained in detail in the two paragraphs following
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
).
Hence, the result follows by applying
Theorem

3.1
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

It is interesting to contrast the above theorem with the result

[
39
, Theorem 2.4]

which gives a condition on 2-generator one-relator groups under which membership in positively and finitely generated submonoids is decidable; meaning the generators for the submonoid are positive words.

Remark 4.13
.

It may be shown that there do exist examples
of
2
-generator free-by-cyclic one-relator groups

G=\bigl\langle a,b\>|\>r=1\bigr\rangle
, where both the
a
- and
b
-exponent sums are zero, but
carrying out the Magnus rewriting procedure
with respect to each of
a
or
b
does not yield a word satisfying the min-max condition from the statement of

Theorem

3.1
.
This occurs because, in such cases, there are many possible homomorphisms onto
\mathbb{Z}
.
What makes the case considered in the previous theorem easier to handle is that the conditions imply the homomorphism to
\mathbb{Z}
is essentially unique.

In the case where both the
a
- and
b
-exponent sums are zero
Brown’s theory does imply that the group admits
some
one-relator presentation
\bigl\langle c,d\>|\>w=1\bigr\rangle
with
c
-exponent sum zero such that rewriting with respect to
c
satisfies the max-min condition from the statement of
Theorem

3.1
. This allows us to decide the positivity problem in this new presentation. However, typically this does not provide insight into the positivity problem for the original presentation.

It is currently unknown whether all finitely generated free-by-cyclic
2
-generator one-relator groups have decidable positivity problem.
It is worth noting, as explained in the introduction, that there do exist finitely generated free-by-cyclic
2
-generator one-relator groups with undecidable submonoid membership problem; for example, the group

G=\bigl\langle a,t\>|\>t(ata^{-1})=(ata^{-1})t\bigr\rangle.

However, we can show the following.

Proposition 4.14
.

Membership in Magnus submonoids of

G=\bigl\langle a,t\>|\>t(ata^{-1})=(ata^{-1})t\bigr\rangle

is decidable.

Proof.

Performing Magnus rewriting with respect to the letter
t
on the defining relator
r
yields the word

\overline{r}=a_{1}a_{2}^{-1}a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}
,
which satisfies the max-min condition of
Theorem

3.1
.
It then follows from that result that membership in any Magnus submonoid generated by any subset
S
of
\{a,a^{-1},t,t^{-1}\}
that does not contain both
t
and
t^{-1}
is decidable. The only remaining Magnus submonoids to consider are

\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},a\bigr\rangle
and
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},a^{-1}\bigr\rangle
.
We give the proof only for the first case
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},a\bigr\rangle
, as the second one goes analogously.
As a free-by-cyclic group over the free group
F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
,
G
has the presentation

G=\bigl\langle a_{0},a_{1},t\>|\>ta_{0}t^{-1}=a_{1},\quad ta_{1}t^{-1}=a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}a_{1}\bigr\rangle.

Working backwards by substituting the redundant generator
a_{1}
, one recovers the original presentation with
t=t
and
a=a_{0}
. Deciding membership in

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},a\bigr\rangle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},a_{0}\bigr\rangle\leq G

reduces to deciding membership in the infinitely generated submonoid

N=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t^{i}a_{0}t^{-i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle\leq F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}},

which is a submonoid of the free group
F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
.
Indeed, any word
\alpha
over
\{t,t^{-1},a_{0}\}
can be written in free-by-cyclic normal form as
\alpha=t^{j}\beta
,
where
\beta\in F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
. Since
t
and
t^{-1}
are in the generating set,
\alpha
belongs to
M
if and only if
\beta
belongs to
M
, which in turn holds if and only if
\beta\in N
. This is because any word over
\{t,t^{-1},a_{0}\}
with
t
-exponent sum zero can be expressed as a product of elements from
N
.

Let
\theta\in\Aut(F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
denote the automorphism given by conjugation by
t
, i.e.
\theta(g)=tgt^{-1}
. Then

N=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\theta^{i}(a_{0})\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t^{i}a_{0}t^{-i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle.

Recall:

\theta(a_{0})=a_{1},\quad\theta(a_{1})=a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}a_{1},\quad\theta^{-1}(a_{0})=a_{0}a_{1}^{-1}a_{0},\quad\theta^{-1}(a_{1})=a_{0}.

Hence,

N=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\ldots,\,(a_{0}a_{1}^{-1})^{2}a_{0},\,(a_{0}a_{1}^{-1})a_{0},\,a_{0},\,a_{1},\,(a_{1}a_{0}^{-1})a_{1},\,(a_{1}a_{0}^{-1})^{2}a_{1},\,\ldots\bigr\rangle.

Denote
X=\{\theta^{i}(a_{0}):i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
We may observe that
\theta^{i+1}(a_{0})
is strictly longer than
\theta^{i}(a_{0})
for all
i\geq 1
, and also

\theta^{-(i+1)}(a_{0})
is strictly longer than
\theta^{-i}(a_{0})
for all
i\geq 0
.
Moreover, in this example, no pair of elements of
X
admit cancellation when multiplied together since the first and last letters of every word in
X
are all positive letters.
This means that every word in
X
is a reduced word in the free group, and any finite concatenation of words from
X
is also a reduced word.
Thus, to decide membership of any
\gamma\in F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
in
N
, one only needs to consider all products of elements of
X
with total length up to the length of
\gamma
as a reduced word, which is finite and computable. Hence membership in
N
, and therefore in
M
, is decidable.
This completes the proof.
∎

Note that in the previous proof the defining relator also has
a
-exponent sum zero; however, performing Magnus rewriting with respect to
a
produces the word

t_{0}t_{1}t_{0}^{-1}t_{1}^{-1},

which does not satisfy the max-min condition of
Theorem

3.1
.

Looking at the proof of
4.14
, we observe that the decidability of the membership problem in Magnus submonoids of
2
-generator free-by-cyclic one-relator groups is closely connected to the following natural problem in the theory of free groups and automorphisms:

Question 4.15
.

Let
F=F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
be the free group on two generators, and let
\theta:F\to F
be an automorphism. Define the submonoid

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\theta^{i}(a_{0})\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle.

Is there an algorithm that, given any word
w\in F
, decides whether
w\in M
?

Remark 4.16
.

A solution to this “shifted membership problem” is likely to be essential for resolving the Magnus submonoid membership problem in
2
-generator one-relator free-by-cyclic groups.
Furthermore, since they are free-by-cyclic, similar questions arise in the context of surface groups. In that setting, resolving the submonoid membership problem often reduces to questions of the following kind: given a finite subset
Z
of a free group and an automorphism
\theta
, determine membership in the submonoid

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\theta^{i}(Z)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle.

where

\{\theta^{i}(Z)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta^{i}(Z)
.

It was proved by Brinkmann in
[
4
]

that if
\phi
is an automorphism of a free group
F_{n}
, then there exists an algorithm that, given any pair
u,v\in F_{n}
, decides whether there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi^{n}(u)=v
. More recently, this result was extended to arbitrary (not necessarily injective) endomorphisms by Logan
[
30
]
.
However, the following result shows that while membership in the
set

\{\phi^{i}(X)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi^{i}(X)
, where
X
is a finite set, is decidable by Brinkmann’s theorem, membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\phi^{i}(X)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle
is, in general, undecidable.
The question of whether membership in the subgroup
\bigl\langle\phi^{i}(X)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\rangle
is decidable remains open and is closely related to the open subgroup membership problem for free-by-cyclic groups.

Theorem 4.17
.

There is a finite subset
X\subseteq F_{2}
of the free group
F_{2}
of rank
2
an a single automorphism
\phi\in\Aut(F_{2})
such that membership in the submonoid

\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\phi^{i}(X)\;\;(i\in\mathbb{Z})\bigr\rangle

is undecidable.

At the same time we shall prove the following result.

Theorem 4.18
.

There is a free by cyclic group

G=F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}=\bigl\langle a,b,t\>|\>tat^{-1}=\alpha,tbt^{-1}=\beta\bigr\rangle

and a finite subset
X\subseteq\{a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\}^{*}
such that membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},X\bigr\rangle\leq G
is undecidable.

Note this contrasts with the result

Lemma

3.3

showing that under the same conditions membership in both
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,X\bigr\rangle
and
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t^{-1},X\bigr\rangle
will be decidable.

Proof of
Theorem

4.17
and
Theorem

4.18
.

Let

G=\bigl\langle a,t\>|\>t(ata^{-1})=(ata^{-1})t\bigr\rangle.

It may be shown (see e.g. the argument in the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
that the subgroup of
G
generated by
\{t,a^{2}ta^{-2},a^{3}ta^{-3},a^{4}ta^{-4},a^{5}ta^{-5}\}
is isomorphic to the free product
\mathbb{Z}\ast A(P_{4})
where
\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
t
and
A(P_{4})
is generated by
\{a^{2}ta^{-2},a^{3}ta^{-3},a^{4}ta^{-4},a^{5}ta^{-5}\}
where
a^{i}ta^{-i}
commutes with
a^{i+1}ta^{-{(i+1)}}
for all
i
.
Let

K\leq\bigl\langle a^{2}ta^{-2},a^{3}ta^{-3},a^{4}ta^{-4},a^{5}ta^{-5}\bigr\rangle\cong A(P_{4})

be a finitely generated submonoid in which membership is undecidable. Such a submonoid
K
exists by
[
32
, Theorem 7]
. Define

M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},K\bigr\rangle\leq G.

We claim that
M\leq G
is a finitely generated submonoid of
G
in which membership is undecidable.
To see this first note that
G
has decidable subgroup membership problem since
G
virtually embeds into
A(P_{4})
by
[
45
, proof of Theorem 1.2]
,
A(P_{4})
has decidable subgroup membership problem by e.g.
[
27
]
, and having decidable subgroup membership problem is inherited by finite index extensions; see e.g.
[
32
]
. It follows that if we set

L=\bigl\langle a^{2}ta^{-2},a^{3}ta^{-3},a^{4}ta^{-4},a^{5}ta^{-5}\bigr\rangle\cong A(P_{4})\leq G

then membership in the subgroup
L\leq G
is decidable.
Working with free product normal forms for elements in the group

\mathbb{Z}\ast A(P_{4})=\mathbb{Z}\ast L\cong\bigl\langle t,L\bigr\rangle=\langle t,a^{2}ta^{-2},a^{3}ta^{-3},a^{4}ta^{-4},a^{5}ta^{-5}\rangle\leq G

it is straightforward to verify that

M\cap L=K
.
Hence since membership in
L
is decidable but in
K
is undecidable, membership in
M\leq G
must be undecidable. Performing Magnus rewriting with respect to the letter
t
on
G
to the defining relator

r=t(ata^{-1})((ata^{-1})t)^{-1}

gives the word
\overline{r}=a_{1}a_{2}^{-1}a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}
.
As a free-by-cyclic group, over the free group
F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
, the group

G
has the form

G=\bigl\langle a_{0},a_{1},t\>|\>ta_{0}t^{-1}=a_{1},ta_{1}t^{-1}=a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}a_{1}\bigr\rangle

Note that every element of
G
can be written uniquely in the form
ut^{j}
for some
u\in F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\{u_{1}t^{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{k}t^{j_{k}}\}
be a finite generating set for the submonoid
K\leq G
. Then we have

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},K\bigr\rangle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},u_{1}t^{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{k}t^{j_{k}}\bigr\rangle=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\bigr\rangle

where
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
. Taking
X=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}\subseteq F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
this completes the proof of
Theorem

4.18
.

For
Theorem

4.17
we take the same set
X=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}\subseteq F_{\{a_{0},a_{1}\}}
and let
\phi
be the automorphism sending
a_{0}\mapsto a_{1}
and
a_{1}\mapsto a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}a_{1}
. Then for any element
\alpha\in F(a_{0},a_{1})
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle\alpha t^{i}\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\alpha\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t^{i}u_{l}t^{-i}\;\;(i\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq l\leq k)\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\alpha\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\phi^{i}(X)\;\;(i\in\mathbb{Z})\bigr\rangle.

Since membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle t,t^{-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\bigr\rangle
is undecidable it follows that membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle\phi^{i}(X)\;\;(i\in\mathbb{Z})\bigr\rangle
is undecidable. This completes the proof of
Theorem

4.17
. ∎

Remark 4.19
.

The surface group
S_{2}
is isomorphic to

H=\bigl\langle t,b_{0},c_{i},d_{i}\;(i\in\mathbb{Z})\>|\>tb_{0}t^{-1}=(d_{0}c_{0}d_{0}^{-1}c_{0}^{-1})b_{0},\ tc_{i}t^{-1}=c_{i+1},\ td_{i}t^{-1}=d_{i+1}\bigr\rangle

This gives an explicit description of

as an (infinite rank) free by cyclic group, with respect to the automorphism
\theta
of the infinite rank free group
F
with basis
\{b_{0},c_{i},d_{i}\;(i\in\mathbb{Z})\}
where
\theta
maps
b_{0}\mapsto(d_{0}c_{0}d_{0}^{-1}c_{0}^{-1})b_{0}
,
c_{i}\mapsto c_{i+1}
and
d_{i}\mapsto d_{i+1}
. A key step towards solving the submonoid membership problem in

is to first show membership can be decided in submonoids generated by sets of the form

where

are all elements of the infinite rank free group

.

5.
Hyperbolic groups with undecidable positivity problem

In the previous section we have seen how our results may be applied to show that for various families of one-relator groups the membership problem in Magnus submonoids is decidable. In particular we have seen that for certain one-relator groups we can decide membership in the positive submonoid, although the question remains open for one-relator groups in general.
As mentioned in the introduction, it follows from classical work of Boone that there are finitely presented groups for which the problem of deciding membership in the positive submonoid,
called the quasi-Magnus problem, is undecidable.
In
[
42
]
McCammond and Meakin asked whether this problem is decidable in hyperbolic groups. Specifically they ask:

Question.
(McCammond and Meakin, 2006
[
42
, Problem 1]
). Can one decide whether an element in a hyperbolic group
G
generated by
S
is represented by a positive word in the generators?

In this section we shall prove the following result giving a negative answer to their question.

Theorem 5.1
.

There is a hyperbolic group
G
generated by a finite set
S
such that there is no algorithm that takes an element of
G
as a word over
S\cup S^{-1}
and decides whether it can be written as a word in the generators
S
.

We shall also prove the following slightly stronger result.

Theorem 5.2
.

There is a residually finite hyperbolic group
G
generated by a finite set
S
such that there is no algorithm that takes an element of
G
as a word over
S\cup S^{-1}
and decides whether it can be written as a word in the generators
S
.

Note that it is a long standing open question of Gromov whether every hyperbolic group is residually finite.
The results above are all obtained as corollaries of the following general construction by combining it with the Rips construction (and a variant of it from the literature), and the fact that each of the classes defined above (e.g. hyperbolic groups, residually finite groups) is closed under taking free products.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely presented group and suppose that
G
contains a finitely generated submonoid in which membership is undecidable. Then there is a finite group presentation
\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
for
G\ast\mathbb{Z}
such that there is no algorithm that takes a word
w\in(A\cup A^{-1})^{*}
as input and decides whether
w
can be written as a positive word, that is, whether there exists a word
\pi_{w}\in A^{*}
such that
w=\pi_{w}
in
\langle A\mid R\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a finitely presented group that contains a finitely generated submonoid
M
in which membership is undecidable. Let
Y\subseteq G
be a finite monoid generating set for the group
G
, and
X\subseteq G
a finite generating set for the submonoid
M
. Take the free product
K=G\ast\mathbb{Z}\cong G\ast F_{\{t\}}
where
F_{\{t\}}
is the free group on
t
. Set

A=Y\cup\{t\}\cup\{txt^{-1}:x\in X\}.

Then
A
is a group generating set for
G\ast\mathbb{Z}
, since it contains
Y\cup\{t\}
which already is a group generating set for
G\ast\mathbb{Z}
.

We claim that there is no algorithm that takes an element of
G\ast\mathbb{Z}
as a word over
A\cup A^{-1}
and decides whether that element can be written as a positive word in the generators
A
.
In other words, we want to show that
membership in the submonoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle\leq K
is undecidable.

It follows from
[
16
, Lemma 3.6]
that for all
g\in G
we have

tgt^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle\Leftrightarrow g\in M.

Hence for any word
w\in F_{A}
we have

twt^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle\Leftrightarrow w\in M.

But by assumption there is no algorithm that takes a word
w\in F_{A}
and decides whether
w\in M
. Thus there is no algorithm that takes a word
w\in F_{A}
and decides whether
twt^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle
. Hence membership in

\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle A\bigr\rangle\leq K
is undecidable.
This then completes the proof of the theorem by taking any finite presentation

\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
for
G\ast\mathbb{Z}
with respect to the generating set
A
, which is possible since

G\ast\mathbb{Z}
is finitely presented.
∎

We end this section by showing how Theorem
5.3
can be applied to establish the results above about hyperbolic groups. The key point is that the classes in question are preserved under taking a free product with
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

By Rips’ result
[
49
]
there is a finitely presented hyperbolic group
G\cong\bigl\langle Y\>|\>Q\bigr\rangle
containing a finitely generated submonoid (in fact a subgroup) in which membership is undecidable. Then the group
K=\bigl\langle Y,t\>|\>Q\bigr\rangle\cong G\ast\mathbb{Z}
is a finitely presented hyperbolic group and by
Theorem

5.3
there is a finite presentation
K\cong\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
such that the positivity problem for
\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
is undecidable. The property of a group presentation being hyperbolic group is independent of the choice of finite presentation for that group.
Hence
\bigl\langle A\>|\>R\bigr\rangle
is a finitely presented hyperbolic group with undecidable positivity problem.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

5.2
.

By Wise’s result
[
53
]
there is a residually finite version of Rips’s construction. So there is a finitely presented residually finite hyperbolic group
G\cong\bigl\langle Y\>|\>Q\bigr\rangle
containing a finitely generated submonoid (in fact a subgroup) in which membership is undecidable. The rest of the proof follows the proof of
Theorem

5.1
above.
∎

6.
Membership in Other Submonoids of Surface Groups

As mentioned above, it remains an open problem whether the submonoid membership problem is decidable in general for surface groups.
Several of the results proved above in previous sections
show that membership in certain submonoids is decidable. For instance, Theorem
4.1
establishes that membership is decidable in the submonoid of
S_{g}
consisting of elements whose generators all have non-negative exponent sum with respect to a fixed generator of
S_{g}
.

In this section we shall prove several new results which show how to decide membership in some other families of submonoids of surface groups not covered by the previous results in this paper.
We also include a discussion about what we believe is the primary obstacle to a general solution. This difficulty connects back to our earlier discussion of certain decision problems in free-by-cyclic groups, particularly those discussed in Remark
4.16
.

6.1.
Low Rank Submonoids

It is a classical fact that subgroups of surface groups generated by sufficiently small sets must be free.
Combining this with Benois’ Theorem and the fact that surface groups have decidable subgroup membership problem gives the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a subset of

S_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}\>|\>[a_{1},b_{1}]\ldots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1\bigr\rangle.

If
|X|<2g
, then membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle\leq S_{g}
is decidable. Furthermore, let
Y
be a subset of

\mathcal{N}_{g}=\bigl\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{g}\>|\>a_{1}^{2}\ldots a_{g}^{2}=1\bigr\rangle.

If
|Y|<g
, then membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle Y\bigr\rangle\leq\mathcal{N}_{g}
is decidable.

Proof.

A well-known fact about subgroups of surface groups is that any subgroup
H_{1}
of
S_{g}
generated by fewer than
2g
elements, or any subgroup
H_{2}
of
\mathcal{N}_{g}
generated by fewer than
g
elements is a free group.

Hence given a subset
X\subseteq S_{g}
with
|X|<2g
it follows that
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle\leq\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle\leq S_{g}
where
\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
is a free group of finite rank (not necessarily with basis
X
).
Then given any word
w
over the generators of
S_{g}
by

[
50
]
there is an algorithm that decides whether
w
represents an element in

\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
. If it does not, then clearly
w\not\in\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
. On the other hand, if
w
does represent an element of
\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
, then since this is a free group it then follows from
Benois’ Theorem that we can decide whether or not
w
represents an element of
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
.
The proof for
\mathcal{N}_{g}
is analogous using the fact that any subgroup of

\mathcal{N}_{g}
generated by fewer than
g
elements is a free group.
∎

Remark 6.2

(Low-rank subgroups of one-relator groups)
.

Important work of Louder and Wilton
[
34
]
proves a freeness result for one-relator groups that
generalises the result mentioned in the previous proof that membership in low-rank subgroups of surface groups is decidable.
Their result can also be applied to deduce decidability of the submonoid membership problem in low-rank submonoids of other one-relator groups.

Specifically, let
F
be a free group, and let
w\in F
be a non-primitive element (meaning that it does not belong to any basis of
F
). The
primitivity rank
of
w
, denoted
\pi(w)
, is the smallest rank of a subgroup of
F
in which
w
is non-primitive. Louder and Wilton show that any subgroup of the one-relator group
G=F/\langle\langle w\rangle\rangle
generated by fewer than
\pi(w)
elements is free.
In this situation, provided that
G=F/\langle\langle w\rangle\rangle
has decidable subgroup membership problem (just membership in free subgroups would be enough), membership in submonoids generated by fewer than
\pi(w)
elements is decidable.

We note, however, that the general subgroup membership problem for one-relator groups remains open but is known to be decidable for one-relator groups with sufficiently high torsion; see e.g.
[
40
]
.

Note that in the case of the surface groups, one has
\pi([a_{1},b_{1}]\ldots[a_{g},b_{g}])=2g
, and
\pi(a_{1}^{2}\ldots a_{g}^{2})=g
respectively
[
48
]
, agreeing with the claim of Proposition
6.1
.

6.2.
Distortion functions

In the remainder of this section we shall develop and extend some tools from
[
39
]
to show how to decide membership in several other families of finitely generated submonoids of surface groups, and other related groups. We begin by recalling some definitions and results from
[
39
]
about distortion functions.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
be a submonoid of a monoid
G=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
, where
S
and
X
are finite.
A
distortion function
for
M
in
G
with respect to
S
and
X
is any non-decreasing function
\delta\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that

|g|_{S}\leq\delta(|g|_{X})

for all
g\in M
.

We say that the distortion is
linear
if there are constants
a,b>0
such that
\delta(n)\leq an+b
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows easily from the definitions that the distortion being linear is independent of the choice of finite generating sets for
M
and
G
. We say that a finitely generated submonoid
M
of a finitely generated monoid
G
is
undistorted
if
M
has linear distortion in
G
.

The following result follows from

[
39
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
be a submonoid of the monoid
G=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
, where
S
and
X
are finite.
Suppose that
G
has decidable word problem.
Then the membership problem for
M
in
G
is decidable if and only if there exists a recursive distortion function for
M
in
G
with respect to
S
and
X
.

Definition 6.5
.

A monoid
M
is
graded
if it admits a finite generating set
S
such that every element of
M
can be expressed as a word over
S
in only finitely many ways.

Examples of graded monoids include free monoids and Artin monoids.

Remark 6.6
.

A graded monoid
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
cannot contain any nontrivial subgroups (in particular, it cannot have any idempotents other than
1
). The identity element
1
can be written using the generators in
S
only as the empty word; in particular,
1\notin S
.

Definition 6.7
.

Let
M
be a graded monoid with respect to the generating set
S
. The function
\lambda_{S}\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
defined by

\lambda_{S}(g)=\max\{\,k\,|\,g=s_{1}\dots s_{k}\text{ for some }s_{i}\in S,\;i=1,\dots,k\}

is called the
upper word length function
of
M
with respect to
S
.

It may be shown that for any two finite generating sets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of a monoid
M
, provided
1\not\in S_{1}
and
1\not\in S_{2}
, then
M
is graded with repect to
S_{1}
if and only if
M
is graded with respect to
S_{2}
.
Similarly, the upper word length function is independent of the system of generators, as long as they do not contain the identity; see
[
39
, Proposition 1.6]
.

Definition 6.8
.

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
be a graded submonoid of
G=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
with both
S
and
X
finite. An
upper distortion function
for
M
in
G
with respect to
S
and
X
is any non-decreasing function
\lambda\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that:

\lambda_{S}(g)\leq\lambda(|g|_{X})

for all
g\in M
.

The actual upper distortion function

\lambda_{S,X}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is defined by

\lambda_{S,X}(n)=\max\{\lambda_{S}(g)|g\in B_{X}^{G}(n)\cap M\}

where
B_{X}^{G}(n)
is the ball of radius
n
with respect to
X
in
G
, consisting of the elements
g
of
G
such that

|g|_{X}\leq n
.

Definition 6.9
.

A graded submonoid
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
of
G=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
, with
S
and
X
finite, is
recursively embedded
if the upper distortion function
\lambda_{S,X}
is recursive.

A submonoid can be graded without admitting a recursive upper distortion function; that is, the membership problem may be undecidable even for graded submonoids embedded in groups with decidable word problem; see the discussion between Definition 1.9 and Definition 1.10 in
[
39
]
.

The following two propositions
enable us to deduce that certain graded monoids have recursive embeddings by looking at homomorphic images.

Proposition 6.10
.

[
39
, Proposition 1.11]

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
and
M^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S^{\prime}\bigr\rangle
be monoids, with
S
and
S^{\prime}
finite, and let
\varphi\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
be a homomorphism with
\varphi(S)\subseteq S^{\prime}
. If
M^{\prime}
is graded with respect to
S^{\prime}
then
M
is graded with respect to
S
and for
g\in M
one has
\lambda_{S}(g)\leq\lambda_{S^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})
where
g^{\prime}=\varphi(g)
.

Proposition 6.11
.

[
39
, Proposition 1.12]

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
be a submonoid of
G=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
and
M^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S^{\prime}\bigr\rangle
a submonoid of
G^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X^{\prime}\bigr\rangle
with
S,\,X,\,S^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}\penalty\
finite.
Suppose
M^{\prime}
is graded with respect to
S^{\prime}
, and suppose that there is a homomorphism

\varphi\colon G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
such that
\varphi(S)\subseteq S^{\prime}
.
Then
M
is graded with respect to
S
and if
\lambda^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is an upper distortion function for

in

, then
\lambda_{S,X}(n)\leq\lambda^{\prime}(Cn)
where

M
in
G
.

The following corollary, also from
[
39
]

provides
conditions under which recursive upper distortion functions can be lifted from homomorphic images and used to solve membership problems in the original monoid.

Corollary 6.12
.

[
39
, Corollary 1.13]

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
be a submonoid of
G=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X\bigr\rangle
and
M^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S^{\prime}\bigr\rangle
a submonoid of
G^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle X^{\prime}\bigr\rangle
with

6.3.
Submonoid membership problem for surface groups: distortion and fully residually free groups

The authors of
[
39
]
solved the prefix membership problem for the surface group
S_{2}
by mapping to the Heisenberg group. They use this in
[

, Proposition 2.10]
to prove that
every orientable surface group

has decidable prefix membership problem with quadratic upper distortion function

In Theorem

below we shall improve this result by showing all surface groups (including the non-orientable case) have decidable prefix membership problem with linear upper distortion function.
Surface groups are limit groups and so admit many homomorphisms to free groups. Our interest here is to see how such homomorphisms can be used to decide membership in certain submonoids of surface groups. In particular this approach will give examples that are not covered by the results from Section
4
above.
We shall need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.13
.

Finitely generated submonoids of free groups are undistorted, that is, they have linear distortion function.

Proof.

Let
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
, with
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\}
, be a finitely generated submonoid of the free group
F=F_{A}
.
Let
\tilde{A}=A\cup A^{-1}
, and
set

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\max\{|s|_{\tilde{A}}\mid s\in S\},

\displaystyle L^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\max\{|u|_{S}\mid u\in M,|u|_{A}\leq L+1\}.

For
w\in M
, write
w=v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
, with
v_{i}\in S
and some minimal
n
. For any
0\leq k\leq n
, denote by
w_{k}=v_{1}\ldots v_{k}
the prefixes of
w
. Define

\varphi_{w}\colon[0,n]\rightarrow Cay_{A}(F)

with
\varphi_{w}(0)=1
, and
\varphi_{w}(k)=w_{k}
for
0<k\leq n
; and ask that
\varphi_{w}
maps any interval
[k-1,k]
homeomorphically into a geodesic
[\varphi_{w}(k-1),\varphi_{w}(k)]
– which is the geodesic between
w_{k-1}
and
w_{k}
, for all
0<k\leq n
.

Suppose
0\leq i<j\leq n
, and consider a geodesic polygon of the form

[w_{i},w_{i+1}]\cup\ldots\cup[w_{j-1},w_{j}]\cup[w_{j},w_{i}].

Denote the geodesic
[w_{j},w_{i}]
as

w_{j}=q_{0}\xrightarrow{a_{1}}q_{1}\xrightarrow{a_{2}}\dots\xrightarrow{a_{r}}q_{r}=w_{i}

with
a_{i}\in A\cup A^{-1}
. Let
1\leq l\leq r
. The Cayley graph
Cay_{A}(F)
is a tree, so it is 0-hyperbolic.
There exists
i\leq t\leq j-1
and
x\in[w_{t},w_{t+1}]
with
d_{A}(x,q_{l})=0
. Since
d_{A}(w_{t},w_{t+1})=|v_{t+1}|_{A}\leq L
, there exists
i\leq z_{l}\leq j
with
d_{A}(q_{l},w_{z_{l}})\leq\frac{L}{2}
. Fix also
z_{0}=j
and
z_{r}=i
. One has:

\displaystyle d_{A}(w_{z_{l-1}},w_{z_{l}})

\displaystyle\leq d_{A}(w_{z_{l-1}},q_{l-1})+d_{A}(q_{l-1},q_{l})+d_{A}(q_{l},w_{z_{l}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}+1+\frac{L}{2}=L+1,

which means that
|w_{z_{l}}^{-1}w_{z_{l-1}}|_{A}\leq L+1
. Note that it follows from the definition of
w_{k}
that
w_{z_{l}}^{-1}w_{z_{l-1}}
is an element of
M
. Also, by definition of
L^{\prime}
we have:

|z_{l}-z_{l-1}|=|w_{z_{l}}^{-1}w_{z_{l-1}}|_{S}\leq L^{\prime},

for
1\leq l\leq r
. Now it follows that:

|j-i|=|z_{0}-z_{r}|\leq\sum_{l=1}^{r}|z_{l}-z_{l-1}|\leq L^{\prime}r=L^{\prime}d_{A}(w_{j},w_{i})=L^{\prime}d_{A}(\varphi_{w}(j),\varphi_{w}(i)).

Now given
x,y\in[0,n]
, the last inequality above gives:

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle\leq|\operatorname{}\lfloor x\rfloor-\operatorname{}\lfloor y\rfloor|+1\leq L^{\prime}d_{A}(\varphi_{w}(\operatorname{}\lfloor x\rfloor),\varphi_{w}(\operatorname{}\lfloor y\rfloor))+1

\displaystyle\leq L^{\prime}\left[d_{A}(\varphi_{w}(\operatorname{}\lfloor x\rfloor),\varphi_{w}(x))+d_{A}(\varphi_{w}(x),\varphi_{w}(y))+d_{A}(\varphi_{w}(y),\varphi_{w}(\operatorname{}\lfloor y\rfloor))\right]+1

\displaystyle\leq L^{\prime}d_{A}(\varphi_{w}(x),\varphi_{w}(y))+2L^{\prime}L+1.

This means that the non-decreasing function
\delta\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, given by

n\mapsto L^{\prime}n+2L^{\prime}L+1,

is a linear distortion function for the finitely generated monoid
M=\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle S\bigr\rangle
, inside the free group
F=F_{A}
.
∎

Results like Theorem

Surface groups, and in fact a larger class of groups - known as pinched word groups, are fully residually free.

Definition 6.20

Let
F

2

\langle z\rangle\leqslant F
a cyclic subgroup closed under taking roots. The amalgamated free products

Definition 6.21
.

Let

, and suppose

centralizes

associated to

is the automorphism
\delta_{z}\colon G\rightarrow G

\delta_{z}(a)=a\text{ for }a\in A,\text{ and }\delta_{z}(b)=zbz^{-1}\text{ for }b\in B.

Proposition 6.22

(Proposition 2.9 in
[
52

)
.

A pinched word group

(and in particular any surface group) is fully residually free.

Recall that a group is said to be fully residually free if
for any finite set of non-identity elements in the group there is a homomorphism to a free group which is injective on that finite set.

Remark 6.23
.

In the proof in
[

]
of the proposition above, for every finite

In particular, if

would be decidable.

P
be a finite subset of powers of the generators

\{a_{1},a_{1}^{-1},\ldots,a_{g},a_{g}^{-1},b_{1},b_{1}^{-1},\ldots,b_{g},b_{g}^{-1}\}

of
S_{g}
. Then membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle P\bigr\rangle
is decidable.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases:

(i)

There is a generator
s
of
S_{g}
with
\sigma_{s}(p)\geq 0
(or
\leq 0
) for all
p\in P
. In this case we can use Theorem
4.1
to decide membership in
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle P\bigr\rangle
.

(ii)

If conditions of case (i) are not satisfied, then
\operatorname{Mon}\bigl\langle P\bigr\rangle
is a group (see Lemma
6.28
), and membership in subgroups of surface groups is decidable as they are LERF.

∎

6.5.
Higher dimensions: 3-manifold groups

In general the submonoid membership problem for hyperbolic surface groups remain open.
It is natural to ask what happens in dimensions above two.
In particular can one classify the 3-manifold groups with decidable submonoid membership problem?
We conclude the article with some very brief observations about this question including some infinite families of examples of such groups for which the submonoid membership problem is undecidable.
In contrast all 3-manifold groups are known to have decidable subgroup membership problem
[
15
]
.

Any right-angled Artin group
A(\Gamma)
whose defining graph
\Gamma

is a forest
is known to be a
a
3
-manifold group; see
[
12
]
.
Since the group
A(P_{4})
is known to have undecidable submonoid membership problem by
[
32
]
, for any forest
F
containing the path
P_{4}
the group
A(F)
will be a
3
-manifold group with undecidable submonoid membership problem.
Further examples of 3-manifold groups with undecidable submonoid membership problem arise in the work of Niblo and Wise
[
45
]
who show that if
M
is a compact graph manifold such that
\pi_{1}(M)
is not subgroup separable, then
\pi_{1}(M)
contains
A(P_{4})
and hence has undecidable submonoid membership problem.

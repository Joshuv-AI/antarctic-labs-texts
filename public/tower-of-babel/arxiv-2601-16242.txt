Scalable Screw-Theoretic Synthesis for PDE-Based Dynamic Modeling of Multibody Flexible Manipulators
S. Yaqubi, J. Mattila

Abstract
This paper presents a novel and scalable screw-theoretic multibody synthesis framework for PDE-based dynamic modeling of serial robotic manipulators with an arbitrary number of flexible links in three-dimensional space. The proposed approach systematically constructs screw-theoretic PDE models for individual flexible links and rigorously enforces holonomic joint constraints through interaction forces. The dynamics of each link are formulated using a set of dual screws expressed in body-fixed coordinates: one describing the motion of the body-fixed frame relative to the inertial frame, a second relating the body-fixed frame to the undeformed configuration, and a third capturing elastic deformations. By expressing the system energy and applying variational principles, the governing dynamics of each link had been previously derived in a unified manner. Synthesizing the individual link models yields an infinitely scalable multibody representation capable of capturing both local (subsystem-level) and global (system-level) dynamics. The framework explicitly recovers all dynamic states, including the motion of each body-fixed frame and the distributed deformation fields of the flexible links. For computational tractability and mathematical rigor, the resulting governing equations are formulated as a semi-explicit index-1 differential-algebraic system. Furthermore, by applying separation of variables, the PDE model is recast as an abstract Cauchy problem, and well-posedness of the resulting system is established.



I
Introduction

The demand for lighter mechanical designs has grown in modern engineering, driven by goals such as improved energy efficiency and higher payload capacity. This trend has encouraged industries, including aerospace and robotics, to increasingly consider flexible components over purely rigid ones
[
23
,
25
]
. While flexible structures offer notable advantages, they also introduce significant challenges: displacements resulting from link flexibility can compromise precision and overall system performance, sometimes negating the intended benefits. Consequently, there is a strong research focus on developing modeling tools that accurately capture the dynamics of flexible systems
[
19
,
21
]
, supporting the design of more reliable and efficient machines. As system complexity grows and multiple flexible bodies interact, modeling and control tasks become increasingly difficult. Scalability thus emerges as a key challenge, since many existing methods struggle to extend to high-degree-of-freedom flexible systems
[
11
,
5
]
.

A promising approach for developing scalable dynamic models of multi-link flexible systems involves a two-step strategy: (i) formulating a modeling framework for individual flexible links in their respective body-fixed frames, and (ii) leveraging these single-link models to construct multi-link systems by ”chaining” them together through holonomic joint constraints. While this strategy is well-established for rigid systems using screw-theoretic frameworks to enable modular and scalable design
[
12
]
, extending it to flexible systems has been challenging due to the substantial analytical complexity involved in capturing coupled rigid and elastic dynamics across multiple links.

Modeling flexible robots presents challenges that differ fundamentally from those of rigid structures. Ideally, the behavior of a flexible li/lo————————80unk is described by a continuum of distributed states along its length
[
4
]
, typically leading to infinite-dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs)
[
26
]
. However, solving such PDEs analytically or numerically is often computationally demanding. To mitigate this, many studies approximate the PDEs using finite-dimensional ordinary differential equations (ODEs), which are more tractable for practical applications
[
10
]
. While this approach reduces computational load, it can compromise accuracy, particularly at higher frequencies, due to the truncation of high-frequency modes
[
14
]
. Alternative methods, such as finite-element discretization, provide detailed flexibility modeling but are computationally expensive and generally unsuitable for real-time control
[
8
]
. These challenges are amplified in multibody flexible robotic systems, where multiple links and interactions must be modeled simultaneously.

Several well-established approaches exist for modeling multibody flexible robots. Among the most commonly employed is the Floating Frame of Reference (FFR) method
[
16
,
18
]
, which decomposes a body’s motion into a rigid-body reference frame and an elastic deformation relative to that frame. In FFR, the deformation field is typically expressed using predefined shape functions, resulting in a finite-dimensional system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) rather than a partial differential equation (PDE) representation. The Absolute Nodal Coordinate Formulation (ANCF)
[
17
]
represents nodal positions and slopes directly via finite elements, supporting large-deformation analysis, though it often produces large system matrices. The Geometrically Exact Beam Formulation (GEBF)
[
20
]
models the beam’s centerline and director fields in the inertial frame, capturing large deformations; however, the position vector couples rigid-body motion and deformation, complicating analytical derivation of displacement modes and usually requiring computationally intensive numerical methods such as FEM, shooting, or spectral approaches
[
2
,
27
]
. Finally, the Co-Rotational FEM
[
22
]
assigns a local rigid rotation to each element, which similarly relies on finite-element-based numerical solutions instead of supporting closed-form modal representations.

An alternative approach to scalable modeling of flexible robotic systems is to express the dynamics of individual links in body-fixed frames as unified algebraic objects, enabling modular “chaining” of bodies to construct more complex systems. While this strategy is widely used in rigid multibody dynamics
[
27
,
9
]
and effective for modeling and control, extending it to flexible systems is challenging due to the lack of flexible-body models that integrate naturally into robotics frameworks, particularly in three-dimensional space. Screw theory and Lie groups provide efficient tools for rigid systems by representing both translational and rotational motion in a single algebraic object
[
3
,
12
]
. A key distinction for flexible bodies is that their configuration cannot be captured by a single screw; deformations must be represented as a continuous field of screws along the body. Consequently, standard
\mathfrak{so}(3)
formulations are insufficient, requiring distributed or field-based extensions of screw theory. Notable examples include linearized screw-based models for flexible manipulators
[
1
]
and finite-element screw-based models for serial-parallel flexible manipulators
[
6
]
, which improve computational efficiency and scalability.

For rigid systems, dynamic models formulated using Lie algebra have demonstrated high scalability
[
27
,
12
]
. Extending this methodology to flexible bodies offers the potential for efficient modeling of multi-body flexible systems. Motivated by this opportunity, the present work builds on a previously developed screw-theoretic model for a single flexible link
[
24
]
, which represents both linear and rotational motion in inertial space using body-fixed coordinates. In this study, the model is employed to synthesize multibody robotic systems in three-dimensional motion by enforcing joint screws as holonomic constraints. The resulting formulation produces a well-posed set of governing equations incorporating link screws, joint screws, and interaction forces, enabling computation of all dynamic states, including the motion of each body-fixed frame and the distributed flexibility-induced displacements along the links. While the prior model was limited to a single flexible body, the present work aims to extend this framework to provide a complete, scalable screw-theoretic synthesis for the dynamics and flexibility of an entire multibody robotic system.

The main advantages and novelties of the proposed framework are: (i) it introduces, for the first time, an infinitely scalable screw-theoretic synthesis framework for multi-link flexible manipulators; (ii) it circumvents the extreme analytical complexity of Hamiltonian-based full-system modeling, which requires intricate variational analysis of coupled energy terms and repeated derivations for any change in configuration or link count; (iii) it is inherently scalable to
n
-link serial manipulators, enabling application to high-DOF robotic systems; and (iv) well-posedness of the dynamic system is rigorously established by reformulating the PDE model as an abstract Cauchy problem using separation of variables and semigroup theory.

Problem statement

This work considers the general dynamics of a multi-link serial flexible manipulator operating in three-dimensional space, where each link may be actuated by forces and torques applied at its endpoints. Such a formulation is particularly relevant for robotic systems, as it allows each link to be modeled individually while accounting for actuation inputs and interaction forces. The present work builds on a previously developed screw-theoretic model for a single flexible link
[
24
]
, shifting the focus from individual link modeling to systematic synthesis for multibody systems. The original model was derived via variational analysis of the link’s Hamiltonian, accounting for the interplay between rigid-body motion and deformation by representing the flexible body as a continuum of infinitesimal elements. To streamline the formulation, the motion of each element was expressed using a set of dual screws: one describing the motion of the body-fixed frame relative to the inertial frame, a second connecting the body-fixed frame to the undeformed geometry of the elements, and a third capturing the deformation of each element due to flexibility. All screws are represented in a rotating body-fixed frame, which may be attached to any point of the flexible body. In this representation, the first and third screws are time-varying and require dynamic evaluation, while the second screw is time-invariant in the body-fixed frame and encodes the reference geometry of the link prior to deformation.

V
Conclusion

This paper has introduced a novel screw-theoretic PDE-based framework for modeling the full dynamics of multi-link flexible robotic systems in three-dimensional space. The formulation is constructed by synthesizing the PDE models of individual flexible links using a screw-theoretic representation, while rigorously incorporating holonomic joint constraints through interaction forces. This approach yields an infinitely scalable model capable of capturing both system-level and subsystem-level dynamics across all links, explicitly recovering all dynamic states, including the motion of each body-fixed frame relative to the inertial frame and the distributed deformation of the flexible links. The well-posedness of the governing equations is established by reformulating the system as a semi-explicit, index-1 differential-algebraic system, and numerical implementation strategies are discussed to ensure computational tractability.

Future work includes the development of efficient numerical schemes for simulating high-degree-of-freedom systems, as well as experimental validation of the proposed model against physical multi-link flexible manipulators. Additionally, the framework can be extended to design scalable feedback control strategies that exploit the detailed representation of link flexibility and interaction forces, enabling precise trajectory tracking and vibration suppression in complex robotic systems. Incorporation of these control methodologies will further demonstrate the practical applicability of the screw-theoretic synthesis framework in real-world robotic applications.

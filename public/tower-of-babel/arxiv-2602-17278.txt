Derivation of variational membrane models in the context of anisotropic nonlocal hyperelasticity
Dominik Engl, Anastasia Molchanova, Hidde Schönberger

Abstract
. Motivated by the analysis of thin structures, we study the variational dimension reduction of hyperelastic energies involving nonlocal gradients to an effective membrane model. When rescaling the thin domain, isotropic interaction ranges naturally become anisotropic, leading to the development of a theory for anisotropic nonlocal gradients with direction-dependent interaction ranges. Unlike existing nonlocal derivatives with finite horizon, which are defined via interaction kernels supported on balls of positive radius, our formulation is based on ellipsoidal interaction regions whose principal radii may vanish independently. This yields a unified framework that interpolates between fully nonlocal, partially nonlocal, and purely local models. Employing these tools, we present a Γ \Gamma -convergence analysis for the nonlocal thin-film energies. The limit functional retains the structural form of the classical membrane energy, and the classical local model is recovered precisely when all interaction radii vanish. MSC (2020): 49J45 (primary), 74G65, 74K15, 35R11, 74A70, 70G75 Keywords: nonlocal gradients, nonlocal hyperelasticity, localization, dimension reduction, membrane, Γ \Gamma -convergence Date: August 10, 2026.



1.
Introduction

Peridynamics is a nonlocal extension of classical continuum mechanics introduced in
[
61
]
, that naturally admits discontinuities such as fractures and cracks.
Unlike classical elasticity, which relies on derivatives of deformations or displacements and hence becomes ill-posed near singularities, peridynamics formulates the governing equations of continuum mechanics in a nonlocal form.
Thus, the energy is based on pairwise force interactions between material points within a defined interaction range known as the peridynamic horizon.

Peridynamics is typically classified into two categories.
Bond-based peridynamics
, introduced in
[
61
]
, restricts force interactions to pairwise bonds between individual material points, where the force magnitude depends only on the relative displacement between two points.
State-based peridynamics
, proposed in
[
60
]
, generalizes this framework by allowing interaction forces to depend on the deformation state of all bonds connected to the endpoints, not merely the bond stretch itself.
The second approach allows for more realistic modeling of material behavior.
However, the mathematical structure of state-based models is more complex, particularly in nonlinear and variational settings.
For an overview of recent developments in theory, as well as their numerical implementations, the reader is referred to the reviews
[
30
,
31
,
37
,
42
,
51
]
and references therein.

Nonlocal hyperelasticity

This work addresses a specific class of state-based peridynamics called
nonlocal hyperelasticity
. In this framework, the material response is governed by an energy functional that mirrors the classical hyperelastic setting but replaces the local gradient
\nabla
by a nonlocal counterpart
D_{\rho}
defined by

D_{\rho}u(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{|y-x|}\otimes\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\rho(y-x)\,dy\quad\text{for $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$},

with a suitable radial interaction kernel
\rho\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to[0,\infty)
and
u\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Precisely, we consider stored energies of the form

\displaystyle u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}W(D_{\rho}u){\,d}x

(1.1)

where
u
describes the deformation of the reference configuration
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and
W\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}\to[0,\infty)
denotes the material energy density. These models were already proposed in
[
60
, Section 18]
, but have only recently been subjected to a rigorous variational analysis. The first work to investigate the Sobolev spaces associated with the nonlocal gradients was
[
58
]
(see also
[
59
,
23
]
), in which the Riesz fractional gradient was considered that arises by choosing the interaction kernel
\rho=|\cdot|^{-(n+s-1)}
for some fractional exponent
s\in(0,1)
. There, the authors established fractional Poincaré inequalities and compact embeddings, and addressed existence of minimizers for convex energy densities. These existence results were later generalized to polyconvex and quasiconvex energy densities
[
5
,
39
]
, which are typically employed in applications of hyperelasticity. To better align with peridynamic models, the extension to finite-horizon fractional gradients was considered in
[
8
,
26
]
. More recently, a theory for general compactly supported interaction kernels
\rho
was developed
[
11
]
, a setting that we also adopt in this paper. Beyond the results on the function spaces and the existence of minimizers, it has furthermore been shown in
[
27
]
(see also
[
47
,
10
]
) that nonlocal models
\Gamma
-converge to local ones as the horizon vanishes, in particular for families of kernels
\rho_{\delta}:=\delta^{-n}\rho(\cdot/\delta)
for
\delta>0
.

Classical dimension reduction

We are particularly interested in the study of thin structures, which are known to show fundamentally different responses to external loading compared to bulk materials.
A natural way to analytically capture these effects within classical nonlinear hyperelasticity is to study the asymptotic behavior of three-dimensional energy functionals on domains whose thickness tends to zero and to identify the resulting lower-dimensional models.
Depending on the underlying scaling, one obtains qualitatively different theories in the limit.
Early rigorous results in this direction include limits that yield string
[
1
]
and membrane models
[
44
]
.
The subsequent analysis of thin structures was strongly shaped by the seminal paper
[

]
, which led, among other things, to a systematic derivation of plate theories in several scaling regimes
[
35
,
36
]
and the generalization to non-Euclidean elasticity
[

]
, as well as the development of rod theories
[
48
,
49
,
54
,
55
]
.
In the last two decades, variational dimension reduction has seen extensions in several different directions.
A recurring theme is the incorporation of non-convex differential constraints, motivated for instance by incompressibility
[
24
,
25
,
32
,
33
]
, non-self-interpenetration of matter and (global) invertibility
[

[

,

]
with applications in elasticity or micromagnetism.
As the latter indicates, thin-structure limits have also been pursued well beyond the purely elastic setting.
Further extensions include elastoplasticity
[
34
,
28
,
29
]
, magnetoelasticity
[
18
,

]

[

,

Much of the existing literature on lower-dimensional peridynamic models is based on heuristic reductions or formal asymptotics, often complemented by numerical simulations: peridynamic membranes were introduced in

In contrast, a rigorous variational thin-structure theory for peridynamics is still scarce and, at present, is represented by only a small number of recent contributions, notably

[

, the authors identify the critical scaling for the

The follow-up paper

Despite these recent advances in the fractional, infinite-horizon setting, a

Contributions of the current work

The contribution of this work is a thin-film analysis of finite-horizon nonlocal hyperelastic materials, in which
the stored energy is given by (

) in the reference configuration
\Omega^{\varepsilon}:=\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)

\bar{\delta}
controls the in-plane interaction radius and
\delta_{3}
the out-of-plane interaction radius, see Figure

. This yields a unified framework that interpolates between fully nonlocal, partially nonlocal, and purely local regimes; extensions to higher dimensions and multiple radii are discussed in Remark

The basic space of interest for our analysis is

O_{\delta}:=O+T_{\delta}B_{1}(0)\quad\text{with}\quad T_{\delta}:=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\delta}&0&0\\

with

We impose the assumption

The core result in Section
2
is the translation mechanism between
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}
and
W^{1,p}
for any
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
in Proposition
2.15
, which was first developed in
[
26
]
for isotropic positive horizons. It constitutes an isomorphism that turns the local gradient into the nonlocal one, and provides a powerful tool for reducing proofs in the nonlocal setting to the well-known local case. While the definition of the translation operator arises from the isotropic one via a straightforward change of variables, a key challenge is to obtain estimates uniformly in
\delta
, as the usual Fourier methods do not carry over to the anisotropic case. Additionally, new difficulties arise in the degenerate case where one component of
\delta
vanishes.
There, we rely on a careful approximation by strictly positive horizons
(\delta_{k})_{k}\subset(0,1]^{2}
and on the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}
-functions in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}
(see Propositions
2.13
and
2.14
), which we prove before via a suitable Leibniz rule (Proposition
2.11
). Compared to the existing literature, establishing these two results becomes substantially more delicate in the partially local setting. The translation mechanism provides several immediate consequences needed for the membrane analysis, most notably a Poincaré-type inequality and a reduction principle for the out-of-plane variable.
More precisely, since there exist functions with vanishing nonlocal gradient on
O
that are not constant (cf. Remark
2.6
and
[
40
]
), it is not immediate that
(D_{\rho,\delta}u)e_{3}=0
enforces independence of
x_{3}
.
We show, however, that there exists
h\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
D_{\rho,\delta}h=0
such that
u-h
is independent of
x_{3}
, see Lemma
2.20
.
In fact, Lemma
2.7
then guarantees the fact that the first two columns of
D_{\rho,\delta}(u-h)
actually coincide with a (non)local two-dimensional in-plane gradient.

In the thin-film limit of Section
3
, the out-of-plane rescaling maps the physical horizon
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
to the rescaled horizon

\delta^{\varepsilon}:=\bigg(\bar{\delta},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon}\bigg)\in[0,1]^{2},

where neither
\delta
nor
\delta^{\varepsilon}
is assumed to be isotropic. For technical reasons, we also implicitly assume that
\delta_{3}\leq\varepsilon
.
While
\delta_{3}\sim\varepsilon
can be treated similarly, the regime
\delta_{3}\gg\varepsilon
is beyond the scope of this work. The main obstruction is geometric: these nonlocal energies require deformations to be defined on the (in practice only slightly) enlarged interaction domain
\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}
, where
\Omega=\omega\times(0,1)
. However, this set explodes in the out-of-plane direction if
\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon}\gg 1
. A particular consequence is that
\delta_{3}=\delta_{3}(\varepsilon)
should depend on
\varepsilon
. In fact, we also allow the cross-section horizon
\bar{\delta}=\bar{\delta}(\varepsilon)
to depend on
\varepsilon
, which makes it possible to recover both nonlocal and local dimensionally reduced models, see (
1.6
). We mention that the tools developed in Section
2
make it possible to treat anisotropic varying horizon limits without dimension reduction as well, but we do not carry this out here (see
[
27
,
10
]
for the isotropic case).

The rescaled stored elastic energy, normalized per unit volume, is now given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to[0,\infty],\ u\mapsto\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}W\big((D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u)T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1}\big){\,d}x&\text{ if }u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

We emphasize that the choice
\delta=0
recovers the classical hyperelastic setting
[
44
]
up to boundary conditions, which we omit here.
With the theoretical framework of anisotropic nonlocal gradients in place, the dimension reduction becomes conceptually straightforward. Given
\delta^{\varepsilon}:=(\bar{\delta},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon})\to\delta^{0}\in[0,1]^{2}
, we prove in Theorem
3.1
that
({\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
from (
1.3
)

L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}\colon L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to[0,\infty],\ u\mapsto\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u){\,d}x&\text{ if }u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),(D_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u)\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{\Omega}e_{3}=0,\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(1.4)

Here,
\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}
denotes the first two columns of
D_{\rho,\delta^{0}}
, the density
\overline{W}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 2}\to[0,\infty)
emerges from

through minimization in the last column, and
(\cdot)^{\rm qc}
stands for the quasiconvex envelope. The lower-dimensional character of (
1.4
) becomes particularly transparent once one chooses a representative
\bar{u}
of

that is independent of
x_{3}
as in Remark
3.2
, so that we can express

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}){\,d}\bar{x},

(1.5)

where
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}
is the in-plane nonlocal gradient of horizon
\bar{\delta}^{0}
with respect to the reduced kernel
\bar{\rho}
from Lemma
2.7
. It is worth pointing out that this in-plane gradient only depends on the limit
\bar{\delta}^{0}
and is thus independent of the relation between
\delta
and
\varepsilon
as long as
\delta_{3}\leq\varepsilon
.
The
\Gamma
-convergence is complemented by a compactness result, which holds up to subtraction of some functions with vanishing (non)local gradient; if
\delta=0
, this reduces to subtracting mean values.
The proofs rely heavily on the translation mechanism, which allows us to pass back and forth between the nonlocal and local settings.

To illustrate the result, we highlight the two main cases of interest:

\text{(i)}\ \ \delta(\varepsilon)=(1,\varepsilon)\quad\text{and}\quad\text{(ii)}\ \ \delta(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon,\varepsilon).

(1.6)

The former reflects a physical horizon that is anisotropically scaled in the vertical direction equal to the thickness of the domain; one can think of this as arising from vertically squeezing an elastic material with isotropic horizon. The rescaled horizon
\delta^{\varepsilon}=(1,1)
has no more dependence on
\varepsilon
, and one obtains from (
1.5
) a nonlocal
\Gamma
-limit given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(D_{\bar{\rho}}\bar{u}){\,d}\bar{x}.

The case (ii) corresponds to an isotropic physical horizon which scales like
\varepsilon
in all directions; this can be interpreted as zooming out from an infinite length strip with finite thickness and a fixed horizon. We find that
\bar{\delta}^{0}=0
, meaning that (
1.5
) becomes the local hyperelastic functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\nabla\bar{u}){\,d}\bar{x}.

A technical and modeling nuisance for the energies (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) is their invariance under the addition of functions with vanishing gradient.
In the setting
\delta=0
, this invariance is harmless, since the only functions with zero gradient are constants.
The nonlocal setting, however, gives rise to highly nontrivial functions
h\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}h=0
, see Remark
2.6
and
[
40
]
; as a consequence, such large deformations are energetically invisible.
In the spirit of
[
62
, Section 6]
, we therefore add a stabilization term that penalizes such contributions and, in particular, discourages them from appearing along minimizing, or almost-minimizing, sequences:

{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{|u(\bar{x},x_{3})-u(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|\varepsilon(x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime})|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}\quad\text{for $u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$}.

The first term penalizes large deformations with zero nonlocal gradient, while the second, purely out-of-plane, nonlocal term enforces convergence to functions that are constant in the third variable (and hence vanishes on the limiting class). We conclude this paper with a final
\Gamma
-convergence result, namely for
({\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
, which is complemented with a weak compactness result that does not require the subtraction of functions with zero (nonlocal) gradient.

2
, we establish the full theory for anisotropic nonlocal gradients, while Section
3
deals with the asymptotic variational analysis of thin films in nonlocal hyperelasticity.

2.
Anisotropic nonlocal gradients

Typically, nonlocal derivatives with finite horizon have been defined through kernels supported on balls of positive radius
[
8
,
11
,
26
,
40
]
, that is,

D_{\rho}\varphi(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)}{|y-x|}\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\rho(y-x)\,dy\quad\text{for $\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})$.}

(2.1)

Throughout the paper, the kernel
\rho\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to[0,\infty)
with
n=3
is assumed to be radial and satisfying

(H0)

\inf_{B_{\eta_{0}(0)}}\rho>0
for some
\eta_{0}>0
,
\supp(\rho)=\overline{B_{1}(0)}
and
\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\rho{\,d}z=n
.

Moreover, we impose the same technical conditions on the radial representation
\rho^{\rm rad}
as in
[
11
,
27
]
; in fact, we have marginally strengthened (H1) compared to those sources in order for this set of conditions to remain preserved in the lower-dimensional model, cf. Lemma
2.7
below. Precisely, with
\eta_{0}
as in (H0) and some
\nu>0
and
0<\sigma\leq\gamma<1
we assume:

(H1)

the function
f_{\rho}\colon(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}},\ r\mapsto r^{n-2}\rho^{\rm rad}(r)
is such that
r\mapsto r^{\nu}f_{\rho}(r)
is non-increasing on
(0,\infty)
;

(H2)

f_{\rho}
is smooth outside the origin and for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a
C_{k}>0
with

\left|\frac{d^{k}}{dr^{k}}f_{\rho}(r)\right|\leq C_{k}\frac{f_{\rho}(r)}{r^{k}}\quad\text{for $r\in(0,\eta_{0})$};

(H3)

the function
r\mapsto r^{n+\sigma-1}\rho^{\rm rad}(r)
is almost non-increasing on
(0,\eta_{0})
;

(H4)

the function
r\mapsto r^{n+\gamma-1}\rho^{\rm rad}(r)
is almost non-decreasing on
(0,\eta_{0})
.

A function
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called almost non-increasing or non-decreasing if there is some fixed constant
C>0
so that for all
t_{1}<t_{2}
it holds that
f(t_{1})\geq Cf(t_{2})
or
f(t_{1})\leq Cf(t_{2})
, respectively. We refer to
[
11
]
for more about these conditions and examples that satisfy them. For our purposes, we are primarily interested in the following case.

Example 2.1 (Truncated fractional kernel).

Let
\chi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth and non-increasing function with
\supp(\chi)=[0,1]
and
\chi(0)>0
. Then, for any
s\in(0,1)
the truncated fractional kernel

\rho(z):=\frac{\chi(|z|)}{|z|^{2+s}}\quad\text{for $z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$},

satisfies (H1)–(H4). Moreover, up to rescaling by a constant, (H0) is also satisfied.
∎

In
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
, the authors proved that the nonlocal gradient is a type of average of the classical gradient in the sense that

D_{\rho}\varphi=Q_{\rho}*\nabla\varphi=\nabla(Q_{\rho}*\varphi)\quad\text{for all $\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})$,}

(2.2)

for

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad z\mapsto\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\rm rad}(t)}{t}{\,d}t,

(2.3)

where
\rho^{\rm rad}
is the radial representation of
\rho
.
Using (H0), one can verify that

\|Q_{\rho}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=1.

(2.4)

In the analysis of thin films, however, the thickness of the body becomes vanishingly small, and one commonly introduces a rescaling in the out-of-plane direction.
This rescaling naturally produces anisotropic interaction neighborhoods, whose geometry falls outside the isotropic setting commonly treated in the literature
[
8
,
11
,
26
,
40
]
.
The aim of this section is therefore to introduce nonlocal gradients
D_{\rho,\delta}
with an anisotropic horizon.
Specifically, we will replace the typical positive omnidirectional horizon
\delta\in(0,1]
with two (possibly vanishing) horizons
\delta=(\bar{\delta},\delta_{3})\in[0,1]^{2}
, where
\bar{\delta}
describes the in-plane interaction radius in the cross-section variables
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\delta_{3}
represents the out-of-plane horizon in
x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
In this way, the corresponding nonlocal gradients measure all interactions within ellipsoids with principal radii
\bar{\delta},\bar{\delta}
, and
\delta_{3}
, see Figure
1
.
This approach can also be generalized to higher dimensions and all different radii, see Remark
2.22
below.

We base our definition of the nonlocal gradient on the identity (
2.2
) with an anisotropic kernel and a suitable change of variables, which provides a simple way to define a meaningful anisotropic nonlocal gradient that is amenable to the tools already developed in
[
11
,
27
]
.

Lemma 2.2 (Anisotropic nonlocal gradients).

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
, and
Q_{\rho}
is given by (
2.3
), then the function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi
given by

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)\varphi(x-T_{\delta}z)\,dz\quad\text{for $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$}

satisfies
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Moreover, the corresponding (nonlocal) gradient and divergence

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\delta}\coloneqq\nabla\circ{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\circ\nabla\quad\text{and}\quad\Div_{\rho,\delta}\coloneqq\Div\circ{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\circ\Div

(2.5)

satisfy the integration by parts formula

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\cdot\psi{\,d}x=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\varphi\Div_{\rho,\delta}\psi{\,d}x\quad\text{ for all }\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\text{ and }\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Proof.

The smoothness of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi
is a consequence of the smoothness of
\varphi
and the fact that
Q_{\rho}
is integrable and has compact support. The claims in (
2.5
) follow immediately from pulling the derivative inside the integral.

The integration by parts formula can be proved by exploiting its classical counterpart for local gradients as well as the changes of variables
\tilde{x}=x-T_{\delta}z
and
\tilde{z}=-z
, given that
Q_{\rho}
is radial.
∎

Now, Lemma
2.2
allows us to define our new nonlocal gradients in a weak sense.
We remind the reader here that
O_{\delta}=O+T_{\delta}B_{1}(0)
for every subset
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
\delta=(\bar{\delta},\delta_{3})\in[0,1]^{2}
, where
T_{\delta}B_{1}(0)
is the ellipsoid with principal axes aligned with the coordinate axes and (possibly vanishing) principal radii
\bar{\delta},\bar{\delta},\delta_{3}
, see Figure
1
.

Definition 2.3 (Weak anisotropic nonlocal gradients).

Let
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be an open set, and
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
.
We say that
U\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is the weak nonlocal gradient of
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(O_{\delta})
, and write
D_{\rho,\delta}u=U
, if

\int_{O}U\cdot\psi{\,d}x=-\int_{O_{\delta}}u\Div_{\rho,\delta}\psi{\,d}x\quad\text{for all }\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

If
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(O_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then
D_{\rho,\delta}u
is defined component-wise as usual.
Moreover, we introduce the space

\displaystyle H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\coloneqq\{u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):u=0\text{ in $(O_{\delta})^{c}$ and $(D_{\rho,\delta}u)\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{O}\in L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})$}\}

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\coloneqq\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\|D_{\rho,\delta}u\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(O_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\|D_{\rho,\delta}u\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}

for
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and set

\displaystyle N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\coloneqq\{u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):(D_{\rho,\delta}u)\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{O}=0\}.

(2.6)

It is important to highlight that a vanishing nonlocal gradient does, in general, not imply constancy, see Remark
2.6
and
[
40
]
for more details.

Remark 2.4 (Anisotropic nonlocal gradients of affine functions).

If
\varphi_{A}(x):=Ax+b
as an affine function with
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, then a direct computation yields that

D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi_{A}(x)=A\quad\text{for all $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$ and $\delta\in[0,1]^{2}$,}

which coincides with the classical gradient.
∎

Remark 2.5 (Anisotropic nonlocal gradients with positive horizons).

Let
\delta\in(0,1]^{2}
. Similar to the typical radial case, the anisotropic nonlocal gradient (
2.5
) can be rewritten as a convolution of the classical gradient with a suitably rescaled kernel.
Indeed, for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
it holds that

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi=\nabla\big(Q_{\rho,\delta}\ast\varphi\big)=Q_{\rho,\delta}\ast\nabla\varphi\quad\text{with}\quad Q_{\rho,\delta}=\det(T_{\delta}^{-1})Q_{\rho}\circ T_{\delta}^{-1},

(2.7)

cf. (
2.3
). Note that, by a change of variables and (
2.4
), we find that

\|Q_{\rho,\delta}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=1\quad\text{for all $\delta\in(0,1]^{2}$.}

Moreover, via a straightforward calculation, one shows that

D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x)=D_{\rho}\big(\varphi\circ T_{\delta}\big)\big(T_{\delta}^{-1}x\big)T_{\delta}^{-1}\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\text{ and every }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

where
D_{\rho}
is the typical common nonlocal gradient with horizon
1
as in (
2.1
).
∎

Remark 2.6 (On the set
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
).

a) If
O
is connected, then the translation mechanism in Proposition
2.15
(
i
) below shows that
h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
if and only if
h\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
(with
h=0
in
(O_{\delta})^{c}
) and there is some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}h=c\quad\text{a.e.~in $O$.}

Here, we assume that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}
has been canonically extended to
L^{p}
as in Proposition
2.15
. Indeed,
\tilde{h}:={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}h\in W^{1,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfies
\nabla\tilde{h}=D_{\rho,\delta}h
and thus the characterization follows.

b) Suppose
O
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
\delta=(1,1)
. Then, it holds that
N^{\rho,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):=N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is an infinite-dimensional space and hence, contains many non-constant functions. Indeed, this can be argued similarly as in the case for truncated fractional kernels from
[
40
, Proposition 3.3]
using the more general results from
[
11
]
. In fact, in the case that
\rho
is as in Example
2.1
with
\chi
locally constant around the origin,
p\in(1,\frac{2}{1-s})
and
O
is a
C^{1,1}
-domain, then it follows from
[
40
, Theorem 3.8]
that for every boundary condition
g\in L^{p}(O_{\delta}\setminus O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and every
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
there is a unique
h\in N^{\rho,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
h=g
in
O_{\delta}\setminus O
and

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}h=c\quad\text{a.e.~in $O$.}

This characterizes the set
N^{\rho,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
completely. When
p\geq\frac{2}{1-s}
, uniqueness still holds but existence may fail due to possible singularities forming at the boundary
\partial O
. This characterization does not readily extend to general kernels
\rho
, but we mention at least that uniqueness for
p\geq 2
is still true by following the argument in
[
40
, Proposition 3.9]
together with
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
. These facts will not be needed for our analysis though.

c) In light of Remark
2.5
, it immediately follows that for
\delta\in(0,1]^{2}
we have the identification

N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cong N^{\rho,p}(T_{\delta}^{-1}O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\quad h\mapsto h\circ T_{\delta}.

Hence, the dependence on
\delta
reduces to a simple dependence on the domain. In particular,
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is still infinite-dimensional in light of part b) of this remark.

d) When one of the principal radii vanishes, the set
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
can also be described using the dimensionally reduced nonlocal gradients. For example, if
\bar{\delta}>0
and
\delta_{3}=0
, then in light of part a) of this remark and Lemma
2.7
below, we deduce that
h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
if and only if

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}(h(\cdot,x_{3}))=c\quad\text{a.e.~on $O_{x_{3}}$}\quad\text{with}\quad O_{x_{3}}:=\{\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,:\,(\bar{x},x_{3})\in O\}.

Thus,
h(\cdot,x_{3})
lies in the set
N^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}(O_{x_{3}};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for a.e.
x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, with
c
not depending on
x_{3}
.
∎

We are particularly interested in applications to thin films, where admissible limit deformations do not depend on the out-of-plane variable
x_{3}
. In this case, the anisotropic gradient above reduces to a two-dimensional (non)local gradient.

Lemma 2.7 (Dimensionally reduced (non)local gradient).

Let
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
,
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and define
\varphi(x):=\bar{\varphi}(\bar{x})
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Then, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
it holds that

D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{\varphi}(\bar{x}),0)&\text{if $\bar{\delta}>0$,}\\
(\bar{\nabla}\bar{\varphi}(\bar{x}),0)&\text{if $\bar{\delta}=0$,}\end{cases}

where
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}
is the two-dimensional in-plane analogue of (
2.5
) (see also (
2.7
)) based on a
Q_{\bar{\rho}}
defined via (
2.3
) and

\bar{\rho}(\bar{z}):=|\bar{z}|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\rho^{\rm rad}(|\bar{z}|\sqrt{1+t^{2}})}{1+t^{2}}\,dt\quad\text{for }\bar{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

Moreover,
\bar{\rho}
satisfies the two-dimensional analogues of (H0)–(H4).

Proof.

The case
\bar{\delta}=0
follows from a direct computation. If
\bar{\delta}\not=0
, we find with
\bar{Q}_{\rho}(\bar{z}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}Q_{\rho}(\bar{z},z_{3})\,dz_{3}
for
\bar{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
that

D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x)=((\bar{Q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}*\bar{\nabla}\bar{\varphi})(\bar{x}),0),

where
\bar{Q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
is the two-dimensional in-plane analogue of (
2.7
).
Hence, it remains to prove that
\bar{Q}_{\rho}=Q_{\bar{\rho}}
in the sense that

\bar{Q}_{\rho}(\bar{z})=\int_{|\bar{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\rho}^{\rm rad}(t)}{t}\,dt\quad\text{for all }\bar{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

This follows from the following computation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\rho}(\bar{z},z_{3}){\,d}z_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\rm rad}(t)}{t}{\,d}t{\,d}z_{3}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\sqrt{z_{3}^{2}+|\bar{z}|^{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\rm rad}(t)}{t}{\,d}t{\,d}z_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|\bar{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{r\rho^{\rm rad}\left(\sqrt{z_{3}^{2}+r^{2}}\right)}{z_{3}^{2}+r^{2}}{\,d}r{\,d}z_{3}=\int_{|\bar{z}|}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{r^{2}\rho^{\rm rad}\left(\sqrt{\tilde{z}_{3}^{2}r^{2}+r^{2}}\right)}{\tilde{z}_{3}^{2}r^{2}+r^{2}}{\,d}\tilde{z}_{3}{\,d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{|\bar{z}|}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\rho^{\rm rad}\left(r\sqrt{1+\tilde{z}_{3}^{2}}\right)}{1+\tilde{z}_{3}^{2}}{\,d}\tilde{z}_{3}{\,d}r\ =\int_{|\bar{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\rho}^{\rm rad}(r)}{r}{\,d}r,

where we have used the substitutions
t=\sqrt{z_{3}^{2}+r^{2}}
and
z_{3}=r\tilde{z}_{3}
. The conditions (H0)–(H4) can be verified in a straightforward manner.
Indeed, (H0) is a consequence of

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\bar{\rho}(\bar{z})\,d\bar{z}=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\bar{Q}_{\rho}(\bar{z})\,d\bar{z}=2\|Q_{\rho}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=2,

using polar coordinates, the behavior of
\rho
from (H0) and (H3).
Condition (H1) is immediate from the definition of
\bar{\rho}
. For condition (H2), we compute that

f_{\bar{\rho}}(r)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{f_{\rho}(r\sqrt{1+t^{2}})}{(1+t^{2})^{3/2}}{\,d}t,

so that by interchanging derivatives and integrals we infer (H2) from the same condition on
\rho
; note that (H2) only holds on
(0,\eta_{0})
while the integral also integrates beyond this point, but the extra part is a smooth and bounded function which can be accounted for in the constants
C_{k}
. Finally, (H3) and (H4) also follow in a straightforward manner from the definition of
\bar{\rho}
, again using that the integral for large values is smooth and can be absorbed into the constant of the almost monotonicity.
∎

Remark 2.8.

a) The previous lemma shows that
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}
is again a nonlocal gradient that fits into the setting of nonlocal gradients of
[
11
]
, but in dimension 2. We will thus use the notation

\displaystyle\begin{split}H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})&=\{u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):u=0\text{ in $(O_{\delta})^{c}$ and }D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}u\in L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 2})\},\\
N^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})&=\{u\in H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}u)\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{O}=0\}\end{split}

(2.8)

for open sets
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

b) Let
\Omega=\omega\times I
for an open set
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and a bounded open interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. Via distributional arguments as in the classical local case, one shows that if
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
u(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta}}(x)\bar{u}(\bar{x})
for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and some
\bar{u}\colon\omega_{\delta}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, then it holds that

\mathbbm{1}_{\omega_{\delta}}\bar{u}\in H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\quad\text{with}\quad\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta}u(x)=D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{u}(\bar{x})\text{ for a.e.~}x\in\Omega,

where
\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta}
describes the first two columns of
D_{\rho,\delta}
.

c) If
\rho
is as in Example
2.1
, then it can be computed that

\bar{\rho}(\bar{z}):=\frac{\bar{\chi}(|\bar{z}|)}{|\bar{z}|^{1+s}},\ \bar{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\bar{\chi}(r):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\chi(r\sqrt{1+t^{2}})}{(\sqrt{1+t^{2}})^{4+s}}\,dt,\ r\in[0,\infty).

Hence,
D_{\bar{\rho}}
is again a truncated fractional gradient of order
s
.
∎

In the following, we discuss the Leibniz rule for anisotropic nonlocal gradients, beginning with two smooth functions.
This will then be used to prove the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}
-functions in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}
.

Lemma 2.9 (Leibniz rule for smooth functions).

Let
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
and
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with bounded derivative. Then, it holds that

D_{\rho,\delta}(\chi u)=\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+K_{\delta}^{\chi}(u),

where

\displaystyle K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)\left[(\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)-\chi(x))\nabla u(x-T_{\delta}z)+u(x-T_{\delta}z)\nabla\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)\right]\,dz.

(2.9)

Moreover, there exists a
C>0
independent of
\delta
such that

\|K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)(e_{1}|e_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{C}{\bar{\delta}}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}&\text{if $\bar{\delta}>0$,}\\
C(\|\bar{\nabla}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\|\partial_{3}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\bar{\nabla}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})})&\text{if $\bar{\delta}=0$,}\end{cases}

(2.10)

and

\|K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)e_{3}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{3}}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}&\text{if $\delta_{3}>0$,}\\
C(\|\partial_{3}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\|\bar{\nabla}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\partial_{3}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})})&\text{if $\delta_{3}=0$.}\end{cases}

(2.11)

Proof.

Using the definition of
D_{\rho,\delta}
in (
2.5
) and the classical Leibniz rule, we find that

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\delta}(\chi u)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(\nabla(\chi u))={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(\chi\nabla u+u\nabla\chi)

\displaystyle=\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(\chi\nabla u)-\chi{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(\nabla u)+{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(u\nabla\chi))=\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u).

For the estimates, we only prove (
2.11
) since the proof of (
2.10
) is entirely analogous. Suppose that
\delta_{3}>0
, then we can use integration by parts on the first term of
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)e_{3}
in (
2.9
) to find

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)(\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)-\chi(x))\partial_{3}u(x-T_{\delta}z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\delta_{3}}\partial_{3}Q_{\rho}(z)(\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)-\chi(x))u(x-T_{\delta}z)-Q_{\rho}(z)\partial_{3}\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)u(x-T_{\delta}z)\,dz.

This second term cancels with the second term of
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)
, so we find that

\displaystyle K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)(x)e_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\delta_{3}}\partial_{3}Q_{\rho}(z)(\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)-\chi(x))u(x-T_{\delta}z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\delta_{3}}\frac{-z_{3}\rho(z)}{|z|^{2}}(\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)-\chi(x))u(x-T_{\delta}z)\,dz,

(2.12)

where the second line uses the definition of
Q_{\rho}
from (
2.3
). Using the Lipschitz continuity of
\chi
, we find that

\displaystyle|K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)(x)e_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{3}}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|z_{3}||T_{\delta}z|}{|z|^{2}}\rho(z)|u(x-T_{\delta}z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{3}}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\rho(z)|u(x-T_{\delta}z)|\,dz,

since
|z_{3}||T_{\delta}z|\leq|z|^{2}
. We conclude the first estimate in (
2.11
) after applying Minkowski’s integral inequality and a change of variables.

For the case
\delta_{3}=0
, we directly use the definition of
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)
to find that

\displaystyle|K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)(x)e_{3}|\leq\|\bar{\nabla}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)|T_{\delta}z||\partial_{3}u(x-T_{\delta}z)|\,dz+\|\partial_{3}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)|u(x-T_{\delta}z)|\,dz,

where we have used that
(x-T_{\delta}z)\cdot e_{3}=x_{3}
for the first term in order to only have to use a Lipschitz estimate in the first two variables. Using that
|T_{\delta}z|\leq|z|
together with the fact that
Q_{\rho}
and
|\cdot|Q_{\rho}
are integrable, we can conclude as before with Minkowski’s integral inequality.
∎

Remark 2.10 (Some properties of
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)
).

a) Similar to (
2
), we write for reference the expression for the first two components of
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)
when
\bar{\delta}>0
:

K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)(x)(e_{1}|e_{2})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\bar{\delta}}\frac{-\bar{z}\rho(z)}{|z|^{2}}(\chi(x-T_{\delta}z)-\chi(x))u(x-T_{\delta}z)\,dz.

(2.13)

b) The quantity
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)
in (
2.9
), or an alternative formulation, exists also for less regular functions
u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with suitable properties.

For example, if
\bar{\delta}>0
and
\delta_{3}>0
, then we use the formulations (
2
) and (
2.13
) instead of (
2.9
), which exist almost everywhere without any additional assumptions on
u
.
If only one component of
\delta
vanishes, say
\delta_{3}=0
, and
\bar{\nabla}\chi=0
, then (
2.9
) does not require any extra regularity for
u
as it does not see
\nabla u
anymore since it is multiplied by zero.
On the other hand, if
\delta_{3}=0
and
\bar{\nabla}\chi\neq 0
, then the last column of (
2.9
) exists almost everywhere if

has at least an integrable weak
x_{3}
-derivative;
for the first two columns, we employ the form (

) to avoid derivatives of

.
One argues analogously for
\bar{\delta}=0
and

.

These case studies and the different representations of

are a central part of the next lemma.
∎

We now deal with the Leibniz rule of smooth functions and ones in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for open and bounded sets
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
The proof essentially follows the structure of
[
26
, Lemma 12]
and uses the calculations and estimates in Lemma
2.9
.

Lemma 2.11 (Leibniz rule in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}
).

Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be open and bounded, and
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
.
Given
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, their product satisfies
\chi u\in L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
D_{\rho,\delta}(\chi u)\in L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
, i.e.,
\chi u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
if

\displaystyle(\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega)\cap\supp(\chi)=\emptyset

(2.14)

and if one of the following conditions is satisfied

a)

\bar{\delta}>0
and
\delta_{3}>0
;

b)

\bar{\delta}>0
and
\delta_{3}=0
and

(i)

\bar{\nabla}\chi=0
or

(ii)

the weak derivative
\partial_{3}u
exists in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
;

c)

\bar{\delta}=0
and
\delta_{3}>0
and

(i)

\partial_{3}\chi=0
or

(ii)

the weak derivative
\bar{\nabla}u
exists in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 2})
.

Proof.

We prove here only the cases a), b)
i)
and b)
ii)
. The case c) can be handled analogously to b). Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be given, then
\chi\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
since (
2.14
) and it holds that
\Div_{\rho,\delta}(\chi\varphi)=\chi\Div_{\rho,\delta}\varphi+L_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi)
with

\displaystyle L^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi)(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{3}K_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi_{i})e_{i}

(2.15)

where
K_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi_{i})
is given as in (
2.9
) for scalar-valued functions.
Now we distinguish all the different cases.

a) If
\bar{\delta},\delta_{3}>0
, then we use for
K_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi_{i})e_{i}
the representation as in (
2
) and (
2.13
), which emerges through integration by parts.
Using Fubini’s theorem, a suitable change of coordinates, together with
\supp\varphi\subset\Omega^{\prime}
and
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}(\chi u)\Div_{\rho,\delta}\varphi{\,d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}u\big(\Div_{\rho,\delta}(\chi\varphi)-L^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi)\big){\,d}x=-\int_{\Omega}D_{\rho,\delta}u(\chi\varphi){\,d}x-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}^{\prime}}uK_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi_{i})e_{i}{\,d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}D_{\rho,\delta}u(\chi\varphi){\,d}x-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\varphi_{1}K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)e_{1}+\varphi_{2}K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)e_{2}+\varphi_{3}K_{\delta}^{\chi}(u)e_{3}{\,d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big(\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+K_{\delta}^{\chi}(u)\big)\varphi{\,d}x.

The quantities
K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)e_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,2,3\}
are written in the form of (
2
) and (
2.13
) so that no weak derivatives of
u
are needed, see Remark
2.10
.
This proves that

D_{\rho,\delta}(\chi u)=\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+K_{\delta}^{\chi}(u)

on
\Omega^{\prime}
.
The first term is in
L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
due to (
2.14
) and the second one can be estimated as in Lemma
2.9
.

b)
i)
If now
\delta_{3}=0
and
\partial_{1}\chi=\partial_{2}\chi=0
, then (
2.15
) turns into

\displaystyle L^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)\partial_{3}\chi(x)\varphi_{3}(x-T_{\delta}z){\,d}z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)\nabla\chi(x)\cdot\varphi(x-T_{\delta}z){\,d}z

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}(\chi u)\Div_{\rho,\delta}\varphi{\,d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}u\big(\Div_{\rho,\delta}(\chi\varphi)-L^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi)\big){\,d}x=-\int_{\Omega}D_{\rho,\delta}u(\chi\varphi){\,d}x-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}uL^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi){\,d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}D_{\rho,\delta}u(\chi\varphi){\,d}x+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)u(x-T_{\delta}z)\nabla\chi(x){\,d}z\Big)\varphi(x){\,d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\Big(\chi(x)D_{\rho,\delta}u(x)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)u(x-T_{\delta}z)\nabla\chi(x){\,d}z\Big)\varphi(x){\,d}x.

In this case, we find

D_{\rho,\delta}(\chi u)=\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+\nabla\chi\cdot{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u

on
\Omega^{\prime}
. The first term is as before and the second one can be estimated analogously to Lemma
2.9
.

ii)
On the other hand, if
\delta_{3}=0
and the weak derivative
\partial_{3}u
exists and lies in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then we use (
2.15
) with
K_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi_{i})
as in (
2.13
) for the cases
i=1
and
i=2
, while we use (
2.9
) for
i=3
.
We repeat a similar argument as before to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}(\chi u)\Div_{\rho,\delta}\varphi{\,d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}u\big(\Div_{\rho,\delta}(\chi\varphi)-L^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi)\big){\,d}x=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}D_{\rho,\delta}u(\chi\varphi){\,d}x-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}uL^{\chi}_{\delta}(\varphi){\,d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u)\varphi{\,d}x-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}^{\prime}}uK_{\delta}^{\chi}(\varphi_{i})e_{i}{\,d}x=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big(\chi D_{\rho,\delta}u+K^{\chi}_{\delta}(u)\big)\varphi{\,d}x.

Note that the first and second column of
K_{\delta}^{\chi}(u)
are written in the form (
2.13
) as to avoid weak differentiability of
u
in the in-plane variables. For the third column, we exploit that (
2.9
) also holds with
\partial_{3}u
as the weak derivative, cf. Remark
2.10
. All terms above can now be estimated just like in Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Remark 2.12.

We note that the additional assumptions in Lemma
2.11
b) and c) are not merely of a technical nature. For example, in the setting b) and with
\Omega=\omega\times I
, we can consider a non-constant function
\bar{v}\in N^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{v}(\bar{x})=0
, which exists in light of Remark
2.6
. Then, for any
w\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
it holds that
u(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(x)v(\bar{x})w(x_{3})\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Indeed, one has that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u(x)=w(x_{3}){\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{v}(\bar{x})=0\quad\text{for a.e.~$x\in\Omega$.}

However, once we multiply
\bar{v}
by a function
\bar{\chi}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, it may happen that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}(\bar{\chi}\bar{v})\not=0
. In that case, and if
w|_{I}\not\in W^{1,p}(I)
, we find that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(\bar{\chi}u)(x)=w(x_{3}){\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}(\bar{\chi}\bar{v})(\bar{x}),

is not a
W^{1,p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
function. In light of Proposition
2.15
(
i
) below, this shows
\bar{\chi}u\not\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
∎

Having established the Leibniz rule, we turn to the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
on cylindrical bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega
.
After a regularization in the out-of-plane variable, the proof follows the same arguments as in
[
26
, Theorem 1 and Lemma 13]
.

Proposition 2.13 (Density on bounded Lipschitz domains).

Let
\Omega=\omega\times I
with
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a bounded Lipschitz domain,
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
an open interval and let
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
. For each
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{k})_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\varphi_{k}\to u
in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, that is,
\varphi_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi_{k}\to D_{\rho,\delta}u
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
I=(0,1)
.

The case
\delta\in(0,1]^{2}
can be argued as in
[
26
, Theorem 1]
using the Leibniz rule in Lemma
2.9
and
2.11
a) and does not even require the Cartesian product structure of
\Omega
.
The case
\delta=(0,0)
is the classical density result for Sobolev spaces.
It remains to prove the case
\bar{\delta}>0
and
\delta_{3}=0
and the case
\bar{\delta}=0
and
\delta_{3}>0
.
Since the proof strategy is analogous for both cases, we only detail the former case and split the proof into two steps.

Step 1: Approximation by functions with higher regularity in the third variable.
In this step, we prove that there is a sequence
(u_{k})_{k}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
D_{\rho,\delta}u_{k}\to D_{\rho,\delta}u
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as
k\to\infty
and the weak derivatives
\partial_{3}^{n}u_{k}
exist for all
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and lie in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
The strategy is to use a standard mollification and partition of unity approach in the
x_{3}
-direction.

Consider two cut-off functions
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};[0,1])
such that
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=1
on
(0,1)
and
\supp(\chi_{1})\subset(-\infty,3/4)
and
\supp(\chi_{2})\subset(1/4,\infty)
.
We then define
u_{1}(x)=\chi_{1}(x_{3})u(x)
and
u_{2}(x):=\chi_{2}(x_{3})u(x)
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
so that
u_{1}+u_{2}=u
on
\Omega_{\delta}=\omega_{\bar{\delta}}\times(0,1)
(note that
\delta_{3}=0
) and
u_{1}=u_{2}=0
on
(\Omega_{\delta})^{c}
.
By Lemma
2.11
b)
i)
, we find that
u_{1}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\omega\times(0,\infty);{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
u_{2}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\omega\times(-\infty,1);{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
since
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
only depend on the third variable. If we denote translation by a vector
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
by
\tau_{\zeta}(v):=v(\cdot-\zeta)
, then we can define the sequence

\tilde{u}_{k}:=\tau_{-e_{3}/k}(u_{1})+\tau_{e_{3}/k}(u_{2})\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\omega\times(-\tfrac{1}{k},1+\tfrac{1}{k});{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

which satisfies
\tilde{u}_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and by translation invariance of the nonlocal gradient

D_{\rho,\delta}\tilde{u}_{k}=\tau_{-e_{3}/k}(D_{\rho,\delta}u_{1})+\tau_{e_{3}/k}(D_{\rho,\delta}u_{2})\to D_{\rho,\delta}u_{1}+D_{\rho,\delta}u_{2}=D_{\rho,\delta}(u_{1}+u_{2})=D_{\rho,\delta}u\ \text{in $L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})$}

as
k\to\infty
.
Therefore, if
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{k}
and
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};[0,1])
with
\supp\eta\subset B_{1}(0)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta{\,d}z_{3}=1
, we can define

u_{k,\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\eta(z_{3})\tau_{\varepsilon z_{3}e_{3}}(\tilde{u}_{k})(x)\,dz_{3}\quad\text{for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$},

which is the function
\tilde{u}_{k}
mollified only in the
x_{3}
-direction. In particular, it satisfies
u_{k,\varepsilon}\to\tilde{u}_{k}
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
as well as the regularity requirement in the third variable due to Fubini, changes of variables and integration by parts. Additionally, on
\Omega
the nonlocal gradient of
u_{k,\varepsilon}
is also given by mollification as

D_{\rho,\delta}u_{k,\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\eta(z_{3})\tau_{\varepsilon z_{3}e_{3}}(D_{\rho,\delta}\tilde{u}_{k})(x)\,dz_{3}\quad\text{for a.e.~$x\in\Omega$}.

This shows that
D_{\rho,\delta}u_{k,\varepsilon}\to D_{\rho,\delta}\tilde{u}_{k}
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence, we find that a suitable diagonal sequence
u_{k}:=u_{k,\varepsilon_{k}}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfies all the desired properties.

Step 2: Smoothing the remaining variables.

If
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, then we can use Lemma
2.11
b)
ii)
to conclude that
\chi u_{k}\colon x\mapsto\chi(\bar{x})u_{k}(x)
satisfies
\chi u_{k}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\omega^{\prime}\times(0,1);{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as long as
(\omega^{\prime}\setminus\omega)\cap\supp(\chi)=\emptyset
; here, we crucially used that the third partial derivative of
u_{k}
exists in the weak sense.
Hence, we can completely follow the proof of
[
26
, Theorem 1 and Lemma 13]
using partition of unity, translation and mollification – albeit only in the first two variables – in order to be able to approximate each
u_{k}
with a sequence of functions
(u_{k,j})_{j}
whose weak in-plane gradients exist of all orders and are in
L^{p}
.
Combining this with the fact that the weak derivative
\partial_{3}^{n}u_{k}
exists for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
yields that
u_{k,j}\in W^{n,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
u_{k,j}
vanish outside of a compact set (uniformly in
k,j
).
Standard Sobolev embedding results yield that all of these functions are actually smooth. A diagonalization argument then finishes the proof.
∎

Density on the full space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is even easier to prove and simply follows from mollification and a cut-off argument.

Proposition 2.14 (Density on the full space).

Let
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
be given. For each
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{k})_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\varphi_{k}\to u
in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

Proof.

Let
(\eta_{j})_{j}
be a standard sequence of mollifiers and set
u_{j}:=u\ast\eta_{j}
for
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).
From Fubini’s theorem, it is apparent that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u_{j}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(u\ast\eta_{j})=({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u)\ast\eta_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\rho,\delta}u_{j}=(D_{\rho,\delta}u)\ast\eta_{j}.

As
j\to\infty
, this gives
u_{j}\to u
in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

Now, fix
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and choose some
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\chi=1
on
B_{1}(0)
and
\chi=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus B_{2}(0).
We set
\chi_{k}=\chi(\frac{\cdot}{k})
and define
u_{j,k}:=\chi_{k}u_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Obviously, it holds that
u_{j,k}\to u_{j}
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as
k\to\infty
. We now deal with the convergence of the nonlocal gradient. To this end, we use the classical product rule and obtain

D_{\rho,\delta}u_{j,k}-D_{\rho,\delta}u_{j}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\big((\chi_{k}-1)\nabla u_{j}+(\nabla\chi_{k})u_{j}\big).

From Minkowski’s integral inequality,
\|Q_{\rho}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=1
(cf. (
2.4
)) and a change of variables, we conclude that

\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\psi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. This furnishes the estimate

\displaystyle\|D_{\rho,\delta}u_{j,k}-D_{\rho,\delta}u_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\chi_{k}-1)\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}+\|(\nabla\chi_{k})u_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\chi_{k}-1)\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}+\frac{C}{k}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})},

for a constant
C>0
independent of
j
and
k
, which proves the convergence of the gradients as
k\to\infty
. A standard diagonal argument concludes the proof.
∎

One of the main aspects when dealing with nonlocal gradients of finite horizon is a translation mechanism, which allows one to connect the nonlocal theory with the classical one. While this relation works perfectly on the full space, when restricting to bounded domains it only works up to functions with vanishing nonlocal gradient. We prove that the translation mechanism, first introduced in
[
26
]
, works for all radii
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
with constants independent of the choice of
\delta
. In particular, the result holds for sequences
(\delta^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
\delta^{\varepsilon}:=(\bar{\delta},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon})\to\delta^{0}\in[0,1]^{2}
where
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{2}
and
\varepsilon\to 0
, as is its main application in the proof of Theorem
3.1
below.

Proposition 2.15 (Translation mechanism).

Let
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
and
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be open, then the following statements hold:

(i)

The linear operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\colon H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to W^{1,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\ u\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)u(\cdot-T_{\delta}z){\,d}z

satisfies

\nabla({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u)=D_{\rho,\delta}u\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u\|_{W^{1,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

for all
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

(ii)

There is a linear operator
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\colon W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that

{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}u\|_{H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

for every
u\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and a constant
C>0
that is independent of
\delta
. Moreover, for every
\lambda\in(0,\sigma)
with
\sigma
from (H3), the map
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\colon W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to H^{\lambda,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is bounded with an operator norm independent of
\delta
.

(iii)

If
O
has Lipschitz boundary, then there is an isomorphism

H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})/N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cong W^{1,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})/{\rm Const}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

with operator norms uniformly bounded in
\delta
, where
{\rm Const}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is the space of constant functions on
O
with values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as in (
2.6
).

Proof.

(i)
Let
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then
v\coloneqq{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u\in L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\|v\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
, which can be established exactly as in the proof of Proposition
2.14
.
Moreover, if
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{O}D_{\rho,\delta}u\cdot\psi{\,d}x=-\int_{O_{\delta}}u\cdot\Div_{\rho,\delta}\psi{\,d}x=-\int_{O_{\delta}}u\cdot{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}(\Div\psi){\,d}x=-\int_{O}v\cdot\Div\psi{\,d}x,

where we have used Lemma
2.2
, Fubini’s theorem, and a suitable change of variables.
This furnishes
D_{\rho,\delta}u=\nabla v=\nabla({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u)
on
O
, which finishes the proof of (
i
).

(ii)

Step 1: Construction of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
.

First, we recall that when
\delta=(1,1)
, there exists a bounded linear operator

{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho}\colon W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to H^{\rho,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

which is the inverse of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho}:={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,(1,1)}
, see
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
. Moreover, for
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, it holds by
[
20
, Equation (31)]
that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}V_{\rho}(x-y)\cdot\nabla\varphi(y)\,dy\quad\text{for all $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$,}

(2.16)

where
V_{\rho}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is the radially symmetric kernel from the nonlocal fundamental theorem of calculus (
[
11
, Theorem 5.2]
), which satisfies

|V_{\rho}(z)|+|z||\nabla V_{\rho}(z)|\leqslant C\max\left\{\frac{1}{|z|^{3-\sigma}},\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\right\}\quad\text{for all $z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$},

(2.17)

and
\Div V_{\rho}
is a radial function which agrees with a Schwartz function on
B_{1}(0)^{c}
, see
[
20
, Lemma 4.8]
. We claim that

{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\varphi(x+h)-\varphi(x))\Div V_{\rho}(h)\,dh\quad\text{for all $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$.}

(2.18)

Indeed, we can compute that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\int_{B_{r}(x)^{c}}V_{\rho}(x-y)\cdot\nabla_{y}(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}-\int_{B_{r}(x)^{c}}\Div_{y}(V_{\rho}(x-y))(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\,dy+\int_{\partial B_{r}(x)}V_{\rho}(x-y)(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\Div V_{\rho}(y-x)\,dy,

where we have used integration by parts in the first equality, and (
2.17
) together with the fact that
\varphi
is Lipschitz to find that the boundary integral vanishes in the limit
r\downarrow 0
.
For
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
, we then define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\varphi(x+T_{\delta}h)-\varphi(x))\Div V_{\rho}(h)\,dh\quad\quad\text{for $\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$}

(2.19)

as the candidate for the inverse of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}
.

Step 2: Invertibility and estimates of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
.

In the following, we first discuss the case
\delta\in(0,1]^{2}
, so that the inverse operator
T_{\delta}^{-1}
exists.
Using this rescaling, it is straightforward to prove that
({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\circ{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta})\varphi=\varphi
for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
by using the equivalent formulations (
2.16
) and (
2.18
) as well as the auxiliary function
\psi:=\varphi\circ T_{\delta}
. The reverse identity can be handled analogously after pointing out that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as well.

We now prove uniform estimates of the
L^{p}
-norm of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi
and its nonlocal gradient.
To this aim, we use Minkowski’s integral inequality and
|T_{\delta}h|\leq|h|
for all
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, to find for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\|\varphi(\cdot+T_{\delta}h)-\varphi(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}|\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|T_{\delta}h||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh\leq C\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|h||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh.

(2.20)

The final integral satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|h||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(0)}|h||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh+\int_{B_{1}(0)^{c}}|h||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|h|^{3-\sigma}}{\,d}h+\int_{B_{1}(0)^{c}}\frac{1}{|h|^{4}}\,dh\Big)<\infty,

where we have used (
2.17
) and that
\Div V_{\rho}
is a radial Schwartz function on
B_{1}(0)^{c}
.
Moreover, it holds that
({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\circ{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta})\varphi=\varphi
by design, so that

(D_{\rho,\delta}\circ{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta})\varphi=(\nabla\circ{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\circ{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta})\varphi=\nabla\varphi
, which allows us to immediately deduce

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\|_{H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

(2.21)

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Hence,
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
extends to a linear and bounded operator from
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with operator norm bounded uniformly in
\delta
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}^{-1}
.
Note that we have exploited here both the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
in
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, see Proposition
2.14
.

It remains to prove the same properties for
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
.
To this end, we first select a sequence
(\delta_{k})_{k}\in(0,1]^{2}
with
\delta_{k}\to\delta
as
k\to\infty.

First consider
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
again, and repeat similar calculations as in (
2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C|\delta-\delta_{k}|\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\to 0;

(2.22)

in particular,
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
extends to a continuous map from
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Exploiting the fact that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}^{-1}
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we now prove that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi=\varphi
where we understand
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}
as a map from
L^{p}
to
L^{p}
.
Indeed, it holds that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-\varphi={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi=({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}){\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi+{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}({\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi)

where the right-hand side vanishes as
k\to\infty
.
We treat the first term via Minkowski’s integral inequality, and dominated convergence to show that

\displaystyle\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}){\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(\cdot-(T_{\delta}-T_{\delta_{k}})z)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}{\,d}z\to 0,

(2.23)

while the second term can be handled via

\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}({\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\to 0

due to (
i
) and (
2.22
).
Hence, we obtain
D_{\rho,\delta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi=\nabla({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi)=\nabla\varphi
so that (
2.21
) also holds for
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
.
The map
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
then extends to a bounded operator from
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with operator norm bounded uniformly in
\delta
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
is the right-inverse of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}
.
That
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
is also the left-inverse of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}
can be similarly established via

{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-\varphi=({\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}){\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi+{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi)

on
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and requires the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
from Proposition
2.14
.

Step 3: Compact embedding.

Finally, we prove the boundedness into
H^{\lambda,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
For technical reasons, we will work with both Bessel and Gagliardo spaces as well as their respective norms and exploit that
W^{t,p}
is continuously embedded in
H^{\lambda,p}
for some suitable
t
.
Given
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\sigma)
and
\lambda_{2}\in(1-\sigma,1)
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
we compute for any
h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\cdot+T_{\delta}h+h^{\prime})-\varphi(\cdot+h^{\prime})-

\displaystyle\varphi(\cdot+T_{\delta}h)+\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C|T_{\delta}h|^{\lambda_{2}}\|\varphi(\cdot+h^{\prime})-\varphi\|_{H^{\lambda_{2},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\lambda_{2}}\|\varphi(\cdot+h^{\prime})-\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\lambda_{1}}\|\varphi(\cdot+h^{\prime})-\varphi\|^{\lambda_{2}}_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\lambda_{2}}|h^{\prime}|^{\lambda_{1}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\lambda_{1}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\lambda_{2}}|h^{\prime}|^{\lambda_{1}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

where the first and third inequality use a bound on translations in (fractional) Sobolev spaces, see e.g.
[
19
, Proposition B.2]
, and the second inequality is the interpolation inequality (
1.7
) for
s=\lambda_{2}
, cf.
[
9
, Corollary 3.19]
. We now obtain as before with Minkowski’s integral inequality that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(\cdot+h^{\prime})-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C|h^{\prime}|^{\lambda_{1}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|h|^{1-\lambda_{1}}|\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh,

and the final integral is uniformly bounded in
\delta
just like before given that
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\sigma)
.
Hence, for any
t\in(\lambda,\lambda_{1})
, we can estimate the Gagliardo semi-norm as follows

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi]^{p}_{W^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(\cdot+h^{\prime})-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{p}}{|h^{\prime}|^{3+tp}}{\,d}h^{\prime}+\int_{B_{1}(0)^{c}}\frac{C\|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}}{|h^{\prime}|^{3+tp}}{\,d}h^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\Big(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|h^{\prime}|^{3-(\lambda_{1}-t)p}}{\,d}h^{\prime}+\int_{B_{1}(0)^{c}}\frac{1}{|h^{\prime}|^{3+tp}}{\,d}h^{\prime}\Big)\leq C\|\varphi\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Therefore, we deduce that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\colon W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to W^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is bounded with a uniform bound in
\delta
, and together with the embedding
W^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\hookrightarrow H^{\lambda,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as in
[
9
, Theorem 3.24]
this finishes the proof.

, Theorem 4.1]
by using Part (i) and (ii).

The main reasons for developing the translation mechanism in the previous proposition are the following Poincaré inequality and compactness result, which hold uniformly in
\delta
.

Corollary 2.16 (Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality).

Let
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\delta
such that

\min_{h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u-h\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|D_{\rho,\delta}u\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}\quad\text{for all $u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$.}

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.15
(iii), cf.
[
40
, Lemma 4.7]
for more details.
∎

Corollary 2.17 (Uniform compactness).

Let
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
(\delta_{k})_{k}\subset[0,1]^{2}
be a sequence of horizons with
\delta_{k}\to\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
as
k\to\infty
.
If
u_{k}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

\sup_{k}\|D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}<\infty,

then, up to a non-relabeled subsequence, there exist
h_{k}\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
u_{k}-h_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for some
u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

Proof.

Define
v_{k}:={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}u_{k}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then we find by Proposition
2.15
(i) that
(v_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with

\sup_{k}\|\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}=\sup_{k}\|D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}<\infty.

Hence, by subtracting mean values and extension, we find a bounded sequence
(\tilde{v}_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\nabla\tilde{v}_{k}=D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}u_{k}
on
O
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using Proposition
2.15
(ii), we deduce that
\tilde{u}_{k}:={\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\tilde{v}_{k}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{\lambda,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Hence, up to a non-relabeled subsequence, we deduce that
\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta_{k}}}\tilde{u}_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
cf.
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
. Moreover, it holds that
D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\tilde{u}_{k}=\nabla\tilde{v}_{k}=\nabla v_{k}=D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}u_{k}
on
O
. Hence, setting
h_{k}:=u_{k}-\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta_{k}}}\tilde{u}_{k}\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(O;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
yields the desired result.
∎

We now turn to the convergence of the nonlocal gradients with varying interaction radii.

Lemma 2.18.

Let
(\delta_{k})_{k}\subset[0,1]^{2}
with
\delta_{k}\to\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
as
k\to\infty
. For any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, it holds that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\to D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\quad\text{uniformly as $k\to\infty$.}

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the first statement in light of (
2.5
). For
\delta_{k}=(\bar{\delta}_{k},\delta_{k,3}),\delta=(\bar{\delta},\delta_{3})\in[0,1]^{2}
and any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi(x)-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)|\varphi(x-T_{\delta_{k}}z)-\varphi(x-T_{\delta}z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)|((\bar{\delta}_{k}-\bar{\delta})\bar{z},(\delta_{k,3}-\delta_{3})z_{3})|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)|\delta_{k}-\delta|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\rho}(z)|z|\,dz\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}

where
\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
.
∎

Lemma 2.19.

Let
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be an open set,
(\delta_{k})_{k}\subset[0,1]^{2}
with
\delta_{k}\to\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
as
k\to\infty
, and let
(v_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be a sequence weakly converging to
v\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\supp v_{k}\subset B_{R}(0)
for some
R>0
. Then, it holds that

\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta_{k}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}v_{k}\to\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}v\quad\text{in $L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$ as $k\to\infty$.}

Proof.

First consider
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then we can use the Lipschitz continuity of
\varphi
to compute for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi(x)-{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\varphi(x+T_{\delta_{k}}h)-\varphi(x+T_{\delta}h)||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh

\displaystyle\leq C|\delta_{k}-\delta|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|h||\Div V_{\rho}(h)|\,dh\to 0,

as
k\to\infty
. This shows that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi
uniformly in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. For the general case, we note that
({\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}v_{k})_{k}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{\lambda,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
by Proposition
2.15
(ii). Hence, up to a non-relabeled subsequence, we deduce that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}v_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Now, we can compute for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}v_{k}\varphi\,dx=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v_{k}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}v\varphi\,dx,

where we have used Fubini’s theorem to move
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}
from one function to the other, and the fact that
v_{k}\to v
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\supp v_{k}\subset B_{R}(0)
and the uniform convergence
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}\varphi
. We conclude that
u={\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}v
, and since the limit is independent of the chosen subsequence, the convergence
\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta_{k}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta_{k}}v_{k}\to\mathbbm{1}_{O_{\delta}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\delta}v
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
also holds for the full sequence.
∎

Since in the
\Gamma
-limit of the thin-film analysis later on we find the constraint
(D_{\rho,\delta}u)e_{3}=0
in
\Omega
, we want to show that this implies that
u
is constant in the third variable up to an equivalent representative.

Lemma 2.20.

Let
\Omega=\omega\times I
with
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a bounded Lipschitz domain,
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
an open interval, and let
\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
. If
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfies
(D_{\rho,\delta}u)e_{3}=0
a.e. in
\Omega

h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
(see (
2.6
)) such that
\partial_{3}(u-h)=0
in
\Omega_{\delta}
in a distributional sense.

Proof.

Setting
v:={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we get
\nabla v=D_{\rho,\delta}u
on
\Omega
, see Proposition
2.15
(i).
In particular,
\partial_{3}v=0
, so
v
is constant in the third variable. Hence, by extending to alll of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, there exists a
\bar{v}\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
v(x)=\bar{v}(\bar{x})
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.
Now, we can define the operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\colon W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

(2.24)

associated to the kernel
\bar{\rho}
from Lemma
2.7
as in Proposition
2.15
(ii) since
\bar{\rho}
satisfies all the same assumptions as
\rho
, cf. Remark
2.8
.
If
\bar{\delta}=0
, the operator
{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}
is simply the identity and we interpret
H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})=W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. On the other hand, the case
\bar{\delta}>0
leads back to the well-known two-dimensional nonlocal theory with radial symmetry of the kernel.
We now set
\bar{u}:={\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{v}
, which satisfies
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{u}=\bar{v}
, where
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}
is defined analogously to
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}
as its two-dimensional in-plane counterpart with kernel
\bar{\rho}
. The function
\tilde{u}
defined as
\tilde{u}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(x)\bar{u}(\bar{x})
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
then satisfies

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\delta}\tilde{u}(x)=\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta}\tilde{u}(x)=(\bar{\nabla}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}}\bar{u}(\bar{x}),0)=(\bar{\nabla}\bar{v}(\bar{x}),0)=\nabla v(x)=D_{\rho,\delta}u(x),

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Hence, setting
h:=u-\tilde{u}\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
finishes the proof, given that
\partial_{3}\tilde{u}=0
in
\Omega_{\delta}
.
∎

Finally, to make sure that we can strongly approximate the specific representative which is constant in the last variable (see Lemma
2.20
), we need an approximation result for the spaces
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, which, in turn, relies on the density result in Proposition

.

Lemma 2.21.

Let
\Omega=\omega\times I
with
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a bounded Lipschitz domain,
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
an open interval, and let
(\delta_{k})_{k}\subset[0,1]^{2}
with
\delta_{k}\to\delta\in[0,1]^{2}
as
k\to\infty
. For every
h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, there exists
h_{k}\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
h_{k}\to h
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

Proof.

First we show that there exist
\tilde{h}_{k}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\tilde{h}_{k}\to h
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\tilde{h}_{k}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
as
k\to\infty
.
To this aim, we use the density result of Proposition
2.13
, to find a sequence
(\varphi_{j})_{j}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\varphi_{j}\to h
in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\delta};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi_{j}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
.
In particular, using Lemma
2.18
, we deduce that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|D_{\rho,\delta}\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}=0.

Hence, we can find a suitable diagonal sequence such that
\tilde{h}_{k}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta_{k}}}\varphi_{j_{k}}
satisfies
\tilde{h}_{k}\to h
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\tilde{h}_{k}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
as
k\to\infty
.

We now define our actual desired sequence as

h_{k}:=\argmin_{h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\tilde{h}_{k}-h\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\quad\text{for $k\in{\mathbb{N}}$;}

note that the minimization problem has a unique minimizer due to the strict convexity of the
L^{p}
-norm and the fact that
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta_{k}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is weakly closed in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
It remains to prove that

in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
This follows from the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality in Corollary
2.16
because

\|h_{k}-\tilde{h}_{k}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|D_{\rho,\delta_{k}}\tilde{h}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})}\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$,}

where we used that the constant is uniform in
k
.
Hence, the sequence
(h_{k})_{k}
has the same limit as
(\tilde{h}_{k})_{k}
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, which is
h
.
∎

Remark 2.22 (Anisotropic nonlocal gradients in
n
dimensions).

The definition of our nonlocal gradients, whose interaction neighborhoods are ellipsoids with a (possibly vanishing) in-plane and out-of-plane principal radius, is primarily motivated by the analysis of thin films in nonlocal hyperelasticity, see Section
3
below.
However, this theory can be easily generalized to arbitrary dimensions
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
without coupling any directions, giving rise to
n
(different) principal radii.

More precisely, we define for
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}
and for
\rho
satisfying (H0)–(H4) the anisotropic nonlocal gradient
D_{\rho,\delta}
analogously to Lemma
2.2
and Definition
2.3
, where
T_{\delta}
is now the diagonal matrix with entries
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}\in[0,1]
.
In particular,
D_{\rho,\delta}
can again be represented in terms of a convolution if
\delta\in(0,1]^{n}
as in Remark
2.5
.

Also the Leibniz rule in Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.11
have natural generalizations to
n
dimensions with
n
principal radii; the distinctions about vanishing components of
\delta
simply become more cumbersome.
While the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}
-functions in
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}
on the full space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(see Proposition
2.14
) is straightforward, we need to be more careful when working with bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
If
\delta\in[0,1]^{n}
, then our proof strategy in Proposition
2.13
requires
\Omega
to be an
n
-dimensional cuboid.
We would like to point out that this restriction is completely absent if no component of
\delta
vanishes, as this product structure is merely a technical requirement in light of the different cases in Lemma
2.11
.

The translation mechanism in Proposition
2.15
can be generalized canonically without any additional effort. Hence, the follow-up results in Corollary
2.16
–Proposition
2.19
as well as Lemma
2.21
also hold in
n
dimensions.

The results in Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.20
are more specific to our thin-film analysis later on but can be generalized as well. Particularly, the strategy in the proof of Lemma
2.7
can be iterated since integrating
\rho
in one variable preserves the assumptions (H0)–(H4).
If one is not interested in consistency with lower-dimensional gradients, then assumption (H1) can even be weakened again to its source version in
[
11
,
27
]
.
∎

3.
Thin films in nonlocal hyperelasticity

For the hyperelastic nonlocal thin membrane with reference configuration
\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)
, where
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and has height
\varepsilon>0
, we consider two (in-plane and out-of-plane) interaction radii
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{2}
.
We thus define its stored elastic energy per unit volume
{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to[0,\infty]
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon}(v)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}W(D_{\rho,\delta}v){\,d}y&\text{ if }v\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega^{\varepsilon};{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(3.1)

with
D_{\rho,\delta}
and
H^{\rho,p}_{\delta}(\Omega^{\varepsilon};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
as in Definition
2.3
.
The energy density
W\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}\to[0,\infty)
shall be continuous and satisfy the growth condition

\displaystyle c|F|^{p}-C\leq W(F)\leq C(|F|^{p}+1)\quad\text{ for all }F\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}

(3.2)

for some constants
C,c>0
.
After the typical thin-film rescaling argument

\displaystyle u(x)=v(y)\quad\text{with}\quad x=T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1}y,

the energy (
3.1
) turns into
{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to[0,\infty]
, given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}W\big((D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u)T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1}\big){\,d}x&\text{ if }u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

(3.3)

with
\Omega:=\Omega_{1}
and
\delta^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(\bar{\delta},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon})\in[0,1]^{2}
, where we implicitly assume that
\delta_{3}\leq\varepsilon
.
Note that the choice
\delta=(0,0)
leads to the classical hyperelastic energies
[
44
]
aside from boundary conditions (or
[
35
,
36
]
if scaled accordingly), so that our approach is a natural generalization of the local theory to the nonlocal setting.

Theorem 3.1.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{2}
for
\varepsilon>0
be given and assume that
\delta^{\varepsilon}:=(\bar{\delta},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon})\to\delta^{0}\in[0,1]^{2}

Then, the following holds:

(i)

The sequence

in (
3.3
)
\Gamma
-converges with respect to the strong topology in

to the functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u){\,d}x&\text{ if }u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),(D_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u)\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{\Omega}e_{3}=0,\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(3.4)

where
\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}
are the first two columns of

(ii)

For every sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\sup_{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty
, there is a subsequence (not relabeled) such that

converges in

– up to representatives in

– to some

with

on

, i.e., there exist

such that

in

.

Remark 3.2 (Lower-dimensional model).

The functional in (

) can be reformulated to highlight its two-dimensional character.
If
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
(D_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u)e_{3}=0
on
\Omega
, then

satisfies
\partial_{3}v=0
.
In particular, the first two columns
\bar{\nabla}v=(\partial_{1}v\,|\,\partial_{2}v)
of

are independent of
x_{3}
.
Since
\nabla v=D_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u
, the first two columns
\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u
of

are also independent of
x_{3}
.

Moreover, there exists an

such that

is independent of
x_{3}
, see Lemma
2.20
, and can thus be interpreted as a function on

These two observations lead to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}){\,d}\bar{x},

where

is the two-dimensional (non)local gradient defined in Lemma

.
∎

.

Step 1: Compactness.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

\sup_{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty.

Then, due to the lower bound (
3.2

, we find that
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

We conclude from Corollary
2.17
that there are (up to a non-relabeled subsequence)
\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon}\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that

in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. To show that

, we first note that
\tilde{u}=0
in
(\Omega_{\delta^{0}})^{c}
since
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
on
(\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}})^{c}
for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta^{\varepsilon}\to\delta^{0}
.

Moreover, we find
D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup U
in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}U\cdot\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\psi\,dx=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon})\Div_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}\psi\,dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\tilde{u}\Div_{\rho,\delta^{0}}\psi\,dx,

where we have used Lemma
2.18
in the last equality together with the convergence
u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{u}
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and the fact that

\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
. We conclude that
\tilde{u}\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

By Lemma
2.20

such that
u:=\tilde{u}-h
is constant in

on
\Omega_{\delta^{0}}
. Using Lemma
2.21
, we can find
h_{\varepsilon}\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

in

Given the previous compactness step, we now define
v_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon})
for
\varepsilon>0
and
v:={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u
with
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}
as in Proposition
2.15
. Then, it holds that
\nabla v_{\varepsilon}=D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}
and
\nabla v=D_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u
. We then use the bound in Proposition
2.15
(i) and Lemma
2.18
to infer that

in

. In the spirit of the classical dimension reduction result from

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W((\nabla v_{\varepsilon})T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1})\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\bar{\nabla}v)\,dx={\mathcal{I}}(u).

\Omega

As an application of the
\Gamma
-convergence result, we present the convergence of minimizers. In this case, we can also include applied forces, but they have to be orthogonal to the functions with zero nonlocal gradient, since otherwise the energy would become unbounded from below. Since
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
depends on
\varepsilon

\varepsilon
. We can choose such a sequence by fixing
V\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
such that

in
\Omega^{c}
for which

\Div_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}V\to\Div_{\rho,\delta^{0}}V\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3});

in particular, all these operators need to exist in a weak sense. Then, we can define our sequence of densities as

f_{\varepsilon}:=\Div_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}V\quad\text{and}\quad f_{0}:=\Div_{\rho,\delta^{0}}V,

(3.6)

for which we find

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot h\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Div_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}V\cdot h\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}V\cdot D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}h\,dx=0\quad\text{for all $h\in N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$}

and the same for

and

; here, we used the nonlocal integration by parts for two non-smooth functions, whose validity can be shown by approximating

by smooth functions, cf. Proposition

Corollary 3.3.

Let

and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{2}

up to subsequence and representatives in

to a minimizer of

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem

by using the properties of
\Gamma
-convergence together with the convergence of the force contributions and the fact that all functionals are invariant under translations in

-limit from Remark

.
∎

While this is a mathematically satisfactory result, from a practical perspective, having to deal with the functions in
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is quite unnatural; not least for the fact that this implies that there are non-constant deformations which do not incur additional stored energy. Therefore, we will now also pursue an approach where we include a penalty or regularization term in the spirit of
[
62
, Section 6]
, which accounts for the functions in
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. This should stabilize the functional so that the compactness and convergence of minimizers holds without taking different representatives.

We consider the stabilizing term

v\mapsto\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}|v|^{p}\,dy+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}\frac{|v(\bar{y},y_{3})-v(\bar{y},y_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|y_{3}-y_{3}^{\prime}|}\,dy\,dy^{\prime},

which will be added to the energy
{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon}
; the first term ensures that large deformations with zero nonlocal gradient are penalized, while the second nonlocal term is there to make sure that the limiting functions are constant in the third argument. After rescaling, the stabilizing term takes the form

{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{|u(\bar{x},x_{3})-u(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|\varepsilon(x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime})|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}\quad\text{for $u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$.}

Hence, we are interested in the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals

{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm stab}:={\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}+\lambda{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon}

(3.7)

with
{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}
as in (
3.3
) and for some stabilization parameter
\lambda>0
. We have the following result.

Theorem 3.4.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{2}
for
\varepsilon>0
be given and assume that
\delta^{\varepsilon}:=(\bar{\delta},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\varepsilon})\to\delta^{0}\in[0,1]^{2}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Then, the following holds:

(i)

The sequence
({\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm stab})_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.7
)
\Gamma
-converges with respect to the weak topology in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to the functional
{\mathcal{I}}^{\rm stab}\colon L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to[0,\infty]
defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}^{\rm stab}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u){\,d}x+\lambda\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}|u|^{p}\,dx&\text{ if $u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$ with $\partial_{3}u|_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}=0$},\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, if
\bar{u}\in H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
(cf. (
2.8
)) is the two-dimensional representation of
u
, we have

{\mathcal{I}}^{\rm stab}(u)=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u})\,d\bar{x}+\lambda\int_{\omega_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}}d_{\delta^{0}}|\bar{u}|^{p}\,d\bar{x}

with

d_{\delta^{0}}\colon\omega_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}\to[0,\infty),\ \bar{x}\mapsto\begin{cases}1+2\delta_{3}^{0}\sqrt{1-(\frac{1}{\bar{\delta}^{0}}\dist(\bar{x},\omega))^{2}}&\text{ if }\bar{\delta}^{0}\neq 0,\\
1+2\delta_{3}^{0}&\text{ if }\bar{\delta}^{0}=0.\end{cases}

(ii)

For every sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\sup_{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty
, there is a subsequence (not relabeled) such that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to some
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\partial_{3}u=0
on
\Omega_{\delta^{0}}
.

Proof.

Step 1: Compactness.
The compactness with respect to the weak topology is clear due to the term

u\mapsto\lambda\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|u|^{p}\,dx=\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p}{\,d}x

present in the stabilization functional.

Step 2: Liminf-inequality.
For the liminf-inequality, let
u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be a sequence converging weakly to
u
. We further suppose without loss of generality that

\sup_{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm stab}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty.

It is not hard to see that one can adapt the proof of the liminf-inequality of Theorem
3.1
(i) to the case of weak convergence; that is, we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\bar{D}_{\rho,\delta^{0}}u){\,d}x.

(3.8)

Furthermore, the weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{p}
-norm yields

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|u|^{p}\,dx

for any set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}
for all
\varepsilon
large enough. Hence, letting
\Omega^{\prime}
tend to
\Omega_{\delta^{0}}
with the monotone convergence theorem, we find

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}|u|^{p}\,dx.

(3.9)

In a similar manner, we find using the weak lower semicontinuity of convex integral functionals, that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},x_{3})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|\varepsilon(x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime})|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},x_{3})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime}|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{|u(\bar{x},x_{3})-u(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime}|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime},

where
\eta>0
is arbitrary and
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}
for all large
\varepsilon
. Letting first
\Omega^{\prime}
tend to
\Omega_{\delta^{0}}
and subsequently
\eta\to 0
, we infer that

\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}\frac{|u(\bar{x},x_{3})-u(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime}|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}=0.

Therefore, we find that
u(\bar{x},x_{3})=u(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})
for a.e.
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\delta^{0}}
. Hence, we deduce that
\partial_{3}u=0
on
\Omega_{\delta^{0}}
in a distributional sense. Adding (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) together, now yields the liminf-inequality.

Step 3: Recovery-sequence.
We split this step into two parts. We first recover the energy without the quasiconvexification of
\overline{W}
and deal with approximating the quasiconvexification via relaxation separately.

Step 3a: Recovery without relaxation.
Let
u\in H^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{0}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
{\mathcal{I}}^{\rm stab}(u)<\infty
, and denote by
\bar{u}\in H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
its two-dimensional representative. In view of measurable selection principles and the lower bound in (
3.2
) we find
d\in L^{p}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}W(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}|d){\,d}x=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}){\,d}x.

We extend
d
trivially to all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and choose a sequence
(d_{k})_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
d_{k}\to d
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and pointwise almost everywhere. Moreover, we take a sequence
(\bar{u}_{k})_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\bar{u}_{k}\to\bar{u}
in
H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
In particular, the continuity of
W
and its upper bound in (
3.2
) produce

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\omega}W(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}_{k}|d_{k}){\,d}\bar{x}=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}){\,d}\bar{x}

(3.10)

due to dominated convergence.

We set
b_{k,\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}d_{k}
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}
as in (
2.24
), and observe that
b_{k,\varepsilon}\to b_{k}:={\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}d_{k}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
To see this, we first observe

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}d_{k}(\bar{x}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(d_{k}(\bar{x}+\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}\bar{h})-d_{k}(\bar{x}))\Div V_{\bar{\rho}}(\bar{h}){\,d}\bar{h}\quad\text{ for every $\bar{x}\in\omega$,}

(3.11)

which is the two-dimensional version of (
2.19
), and
V_{\bar{\rho}}
is the two-dimensional kernel from the nonlocal fundamental theorem of calculus (cf. (
2.16
) and (
2.17
)).
The Lipschitz continuity of
d_{k}
then produces uniform convergence. After taking the derivative in (
3.11
) and switching the integral with the gradient (note that
d_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
), we repeat the same argument as before to obtain uniform convergence of the derivative as well.

We then define the first step towards a recovery sequence as

\displaystyle u_{k,\varepsilon}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}(x)(\bar{u}_{k}(\bar{x})+\varepsilon x_{3}b_{k,\varepsilon}(\bar{x}))\quad\text{for $x=(\bar{x},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$.}

The rescaled nonlocal gradient of
u_{k,\varepsilon}
then satisfies

\displaystyle(D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u_{k,\varepsilon})T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\big(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{k}|0\big)+\varepsilon D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}(p_{3}b_{k,\varepsilon})T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1}=\big(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{k}|0\big)+\varepsilon\left({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}(p_{3}\bar{\nabla}b_{k,\varepsilon})|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}b_{k,\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\big(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{k}|0\big)+\varepsilon\left({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}(p_{3}\bar{\nabla}b_{k,\varepsilon})|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}d_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\big(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{k}|d_{k}\big)+\varepsilon\big(({\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}(p_{3}\bar{\nabla}b_{k,\varepsilon})|0\big)

(3.12)

where
p_{3}(x)=x_{3}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and using that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}
is the inverse of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}
.
Note also that
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{k}\to D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}_{k}

due to the smoothness of

, cf. the strategy in Lemma

The second term on the right-hand side of (

.
The first energy term then satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}W((D_{\rho,\delta^{\varepsilon}}u_{k,\varepsilon})T_{(1,\varepsilon)}^{-1}){\,d}x\to\int_{\omega}W(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}_{k}|d_{k})\,d\bar{x}\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0

in light of (

For the

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{p}{\,d}x\to\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}|\bar{u}_{k}|^{p}{\,d}x\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(3.14)

Finally, for the double integral we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{|u_{k,\varepsilon}(\bar{x},x_{3})-u_{k,\varepsilon}(\bar{x},x_{3}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|\varepsilon(x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime})|}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}}|x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime}|^{p-1}|b_{k,\varepsilon}(\bar{x})|^{p}\,dx\,dx^{\prime}\to 0

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm stab}(u_{k,\varepsilon})=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u})\,d\bar{x}+\lambda\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}|\bar{u}|^{p}{\,d}x.

Step 3b: Relaxation.
Let
\bar{u}\in H^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then we set
v={\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}\in W^{1,p}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
so that
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}=\bar{\nabla}v
in
\omega

(v_{j})_{j}\subset W^{1,p}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
v_{j}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{1,p}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\overline{W}(\bar{\nabla}v_{j}){\,d}\bar{x}\to\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(\bar{\nabla}v){\,d}\bar{x}=\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}){\,d}\bar{x}.

If
E\colon W^{1,p}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is any bounded linear extension operator, then we define
\tilde{u}_{j}=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}{\mathcal{P}}_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}(Ev_{j})
and observe that
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\tilde{u}_{j}=\bar{\nabla}v_{j}
so that

\displaystyle D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\tilde{u}_{j}\rightharpoonup D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}\quad\text{in $L^{p}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 2})$ as $j\to\infty$}.

Combining this with a two-dimensional version of Corollary
2.17
(which holds in exactly the same way by Remark
2.8
) yields
h_{j}\in N^{\bar{\rho},p}_{\bar{\delta}^{0}}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that

\bar{u}_{j}:=\tilde{u}_{j}-h_{j}
satisfies
D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}_{j}=\bar{\nabla}v_{j}
and
\bar{u}_{j}\to\bar{u}
in
L^{p}(\omega_{\bar{\delta}^{0}};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. In particular, (
3.15
) and dominated convergence then give

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\overline{W}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u}_{j})\,d\bar{x}+\lambda\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}|\bar{u}_{j}|^{p}{\,d}x\to\int_{\omega}\overline{W}^{\rm qc}(D_{\bar{\rho},\bar{\delta}^{0}}\bar{u})\,d\bar{x}+\lambda\int_{\Omega_{\delta^{0}}}|\bar{u}|^{p}{\,d}x.

We then repeat Step 3a for each
j
and select a suitable diagonal sequence to conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5.

a) The result is phrased with respect to the weak convergence in
L^{p}
since we still cannot exclude the functions in
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
Indeed, while the stabilization term ensures that they do not become too large, the fact that
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is infinite-dimensional, see Remark
2.6
, makes it so that we still can find bounded sequences in
N^{\rho,p}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
which do not converge strongly.

b) Since the stabilization term
\lambda{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.7
) is non-negative, the compactness result in Theorem
3.1
(
ii
) still holds. Theorem
3.4
(
ii
) additionally provides the weak convergence of the sequence itself (without taking suitable representatives).

c) In light of b), we can also prove the convergence of minimizers as in Corollary
3.3
when
{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\mathcal{I}}
are replaced by their stabilized counterparts. In this setting, in fact, we do not require assumption (
3.6
), which was needed in the non-stabilized setting to ensure a generalized force balance and prevent the minimum energy (even for fixed
\delta,\varepsilon
) from becoming
-\infty
. The stabilized energies avoid this issue by design, so that we merely require the force densities
f_{\varepsilon},f_{0}\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to satisfy

f_{\varepsilon}\to f_{0}\quad\text{in $L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$.}

∎

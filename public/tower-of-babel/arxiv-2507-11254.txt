Order-Dependent Dissimilarity Measures on Phylogenetic Trees
Simone Linz, Katherine St. John, Charles Semple, Kristina Wicke

Abstract
Ordered leaf attachment, Phylo2Vec, and HOP are three recently introduced vector representations for rooted phylogenetic trees where the representation is determined by an ordering of the underlying leaf set X X . Comparing the vectors of two rooted phylogenetic X X -trees T T and T ′ T^{\prime} for a fixed ordering on X X leads to polynomial-time computable measure for the dissimilarity of T T and T ′ T^{\prime} , albeit dependent on the choice of the leaf ordering. For each of ordered leaf attachment, Phylo2Vec, and HOP, we compare this measure with the rooted subtree prune and regraft distance (rSPR), the hybrid number, and the temporal tree-child hybrid number of T T and T ′ T^{\prime} . Although there is no direct relationship between rSPR and any of the three vector-based measures, we show that, when minimized over all orderings, the hybrid number is equivalent to HOP, and an upper bound on the other two. Moreover, when minimized over all orderings induced by common cherry-picking sequences of T T and T ′ T^{\prime} , the temporal tree-child hybrid number of T T and T ′ T^{\prime} is equivalent to each of the three vector-based measures.



Keywords: phylogenetic trees, vector representations, order-dependent measures
.

Introduction

The task of quantifying the disagreement between phylogenetic trees is essential for evaluating the accuracy of tree inference methods and for comparing phylogenetic trees
[
7
,
13
]
.
One of the most common ways of doing this for two rooted binary phylogenetic trees is by using the tree rearrangement operation of rooted subtree prune and regraft (rSPR)
[
12
]
.
The rSPR distance between two arbitrary rooted binary phylogenetic
X
-trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
is the minimum number of rSPR operations that transforms
T
into
T^{\prime}
.
However, in general, computing the rSPR distance between
T
and
T^{\prime}
is computationally hard
[
2
]
.
To circumvent this computational hardness but to also replicate the applicability of rSPR, three new approaches have been recently introduced for this task. All three approaches, namely, ordered leaf attachment (OLA)
[
10
]
, Phylo2Vec (P2V)
[
8
]
, and HOP
[
4
]
, are based on imposing an external ordering
\sigma
on
X
and, depending on
T
and
\sigma
, using this ordering to assign a vector to
T
. For a particular measure, the distance between
T
and
T^{\prime}
under
\sigma
is made by a comparison of the vectors assigned to
T
and
T^{\prime}
. Regardless of the measure, the assignment of a vector to
T
and the comparison of the vectors assigned to
T
and
T^{\prime}
can be computed in polynomial time, thereby eliminating the hardness of calculating the rSPR distance.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the relationship between each of the three order-dependent measures (OLA, P2V, and HOP) and the tree rearrangement operation rSPR that they seek to replicate. If we are allowed to choose an ordering on
X
, then there is little relationship between any of the three measures and rSPR. However, if we choose an ordering that minimizes the measure and broaden the investigation to include the hybridization number
[
1
]
, a notion closely related to rSPR, then more direct relationships exist for OLA and HOP. In particular, across all orderings of
X
, we show that the minimum OLA measure of two rooted binary phylogenetic
X
-trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
is bounded above by a linear function in the rSPR distance between
T
and
T^{\prime}
, while the minimum HOP measure of
T
and
T^{\prime}
equates (exactly) to the hybrid number of
T
and
T^{\prime}
. The latter resolves a conjecture in
[
4
]
that computing the minimum HOP measure between two rooted binary phylogenetic trees is NP-hard as computing the hybrid number of two such trees is NP-hard
[
3
]
. Furthermore, by only considering the orderings of
X
that are induced by a common cherry-picking sequence of
T
and
T^{\prime}
and minimizing across these orderings, we show that the minimum OLA, P2V, and HOP measures all equate to the temporal tree-child hybrid number of
T
and
T^{\prime}

[
5
]
.

The above results require a number of concepts that are localized in their use, and so we delay their introduction until the relevant sections of the paper. The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we formally define each of OLA, P2V, and HOP. Section
3
establishes the above relationships between OLA and rSPR, and between HOP and the hybrid number, while Section
4
establishes the relationship between all three order-dependent measures and the temporal tree-child hybrid number. The relationships in Section
3
make use of agreement forests, while the relationship in Section
4
involves cherry-picking sequences. We end the paper with a discussion and some open problems.

Introduced in

2.4
Order-dependent distances between phylogenetic trees

The primary motivation for the introduction of the OLA, P2V, and HOP vector representations is to quantify the dissimilarity between two phylogenetic
X
-trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
. This dissimilarity depends on the choice of
\sigma
, an ordering of
X
, and is computed as follows:

1.

The
OLA distance with respect to
\sigma
between
T
and
T^{\prime}
, denoted as
d^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{OLA}}(T,T^{\prime})
, is defined as the Hamming distance between the OLA vectors of
T
and
T^{\prime}
under
\sigma
.

2.

Similarly, the
\mathrm{P2V}
distance with respect to
\sigma
between
T
and
T^{\prime}
, denoted as
d^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{P2V}}(T,T^{\prime})
, is defined as the Hamming distance between the P2V vectors of
T
and
T^{\prime}
under
\sigma
.

3.

The analogue for HOP is a two-step process. Let
\mathbf{u}=[1,\mathbf{u}_{1},\underline{1},\mathbf{u}_{2},\underline{2},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{n-1},\underline{n-1},\underline{n}]
and
\mathbf{v}=[1,\mathbf{v}_{1},\underline{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\underline{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n-1},\underline{n-1},\underline{n}]
be the HOP vectors of
T
and
T^{\prime}
under
\sigma
. The
HOP similarity with respect to
\sigma
is defined as

\text{Sim}^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{HOP}}(T,T^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\left|\text{LCS}(\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})\right|.

In turn, the
HOP distance with respect to
\sigma
between
T
and
T^{\prime}
is given by

d^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{HOP}}(T,T^{\prime})=n-\text{Sim}^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{HOP}}(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})=n-\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\left|\text{LCS}(\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})\right|.

It has been shown in
[
10
]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
4
]
, respectively, that under a fixed
\sigma
each of
d_{\mathrm{OLA}}^{\sigma}(T,T^{\prime})
,
d_{\mathrm{P2V}}^{\sigma}(T,T^{\prime})
, and
d_{\mathrm{HOP}}^{\sigma}(T,T^{\prime})
satisfies the triangle inequality and, therefore, is a distance on the set of phylogenetic
X
-trees.

Lastly, for each
\Theta\in\{\mathrm{HOP},\mathrm{OLA},\mathrm{P2V}\}
, we define

d^{*}_{\Theta}(T,T^{\prime})=\min\big\{d^{\sigma}_{\Theta}(T,T^{\prime}):\mbox{$\sigma$ is an ordering on $X$}\big\},

and refer to
d^{*}_{\Theta}(T,T^{\prime})
as the
\Theta
measure between

T
and
T^{\prime}
.

The reason for calling it a measure is because
d^{*}_{\Theta}(T,T^{\prime})
is not a distance on the set of phylogenetic
X
-trees. We give concrete examples showing that
d^{*}_{\Theta}(T,T^{\prime})
does not satisfy the triangle inequality in Figure
5
(for OLA) and Figure
7
(for HOP and P2V).

3
Bounding order-dependent measures by agreement forests

There are several measures to compute the dissimilarities between two phylogenetic trees on the same label set that are based on agreement forests. These measures include the rooted subtree prune and regraft distance and the hybrid number that we formally define next.

Let
T
be a rooted phylogenetic
X
-tree with root
\rho
. Furthermore, let
T^{\prime}
be a rooted phylogenetic tree that can be obtained from
T
by deleting an edge
(u,v)
in
T
with
u\neq\rho
, suppressing
u
, and then
adjoining
the subtree with root
v
to the phylogenetic tree that contains
\rho
by subdividing an edge of the latter with a new vertex,
u^{\prime}
say, and then adding the edge
(u^{\prime},v)
. We say that
T^{\prime}
has been
obtained from
T
by a
rooted subtree prune and regraft (rSPR) move
.
Moreover, we define the
rSPR distance
, denoted
d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T,T^{\prime})
, to be the minimum number of rSPR moves that transforms
T
to
T^{\prime}
. Note that an rSPR move is reversible, so
d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T,T^{\prime})=d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T^{\prime},T)
.

Now, let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees whose roots are labeled with
\rho
. For the purpose of the upcoming definition, we view
\rho
as an element of the label set of
T
and
T^{\prime}
.
An
agreement forest
for
T
and
T^{\prime}
is a collection
\{T_{\rho},T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{k}\}
of rooted trees with label sets
L_{\rho},L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{k}
such that the following properties are satisfied:

1.

The label sets
L_{\rho},L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{k}
partition
X\cup\{\rho\}
and, in particular,
\rho\in L_{\rho}
.

2.

For all
i\in\{\rho,1,2,\ldots,k\}
, the rooted tree
T_{i}\simeq T|{L_{i}}\simeq T^{\prime}|{L_{i}}
.

3.

The trees in
\{T(L_{i}):i\in\{\rho,1,2,\ldots,k\}\}
and
\{T^{\prime}(L_{i}):i\in\{\rho,1,2,\ldots,k\}\}
are vertex-disjoint rooted subtrees of
T
and
T^{\prime}
, respectively.

A
maximum agreement forest
for
T
and
T^{\prime}
is an agreement forest
\{T_{\rho},T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{k}\}
in which
k
(the number of components minus one) is minimized. The minimum possible value for
k
is denoted by
m(T,T^{\prime})
. The next theorem is due to Bordewich and Semple
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
and establishes a characterization of the rSPR distance between two phylogenetic
X
-trees in terms of agreement forests.

Theorem 1
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. Then
d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T,T^{\prime})=m(T,T^{\prime})
.

Following on from Theorem
1
, Bordewich and Semple
[
2
]
showed that computing the rSPR distance between two phylogenetic trees is NP-hard via a reduction from Exact Cover by
3
-Sets to maximum agreement forests.

In order to define the hybrid number of two phylogenetic
X
-trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
, we first introduce phylogenetic networks. A
rooted binary phylogenetic network
N
on
X
is a rooted acyclic directed graph with no parallel edges that satisfies the following properties:

1.

the unique root has in-degree zero and out-degree one,

2.

vertices with out-degree zero have in-degree one, and the set of vertices with out-degree zero is
X
, and

3.

all other vertices have either in-degree one and out-degree two or in-degree two and out-degree one.

Furthermore, we use
h(N)
to denote the number of vertices with in-degree two in
N
. Since all phylogenetic networks in this paper are rooted and binary, we refer to a rooted binary phylogenetic network on
X
as simply a
phylogenetic network
from now on.

We next define two particular classes of phylogenetic networks. The first features prominently in the literature. Let
N
be a phylogenetic network on
X
. We say that
N
is
tree-child
if each non-leaf vertex has a child with in-degree one. Moreover, we say that
N
is
temporal
if there exists a map
t:V\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that, for each edge
(u,v)
in
N
, we have
t(u)=t(v)
if
v
has in-degree two, and
t(u)<t(v)
if
v
has in-degree one.

Now, let
N
be a phylogenetic network on
X
, and let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. We say that
N

displays

T
if there exists a subtree of
N
that is a subdivision of
T
.

With this definition in hand, we set

h(T,T^{\prime})=\min\{h(N):N\text{ is a phylogenetic network on }X\text{ that displays }T\text{ and }T^{\prime}\}

and refer to
h(T,T^{\prime})
as the
hybrid number
of
T
and
T^{\prime}
. Similarly, we set

h_{tc}(T,T^{\prime})=\min\{h(N):N\text{ is a tree-child network on }X\text{ that displays }T\text{ and }T^{\prime}\}

and

h_{t}(T,T^{\prime})=\min\{h(N):N\text{ is a temporal tree-child network on }X\text{ that displays }T\text{ and }T^{\prime}\},

and refer to
h_{tc}(T,T^{\prime})
and
h_{t}(T,T^{\prime})
as the
tree-child hybrid number
and the
temporal tree-child hybrid number
, respectively, of
T
and
T^{\prime}
. The following lemma was essentially established as part of the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 2]
and formally noted in
[
5
]
. It shows that the hybrid number of two phylogenetic trees is equal to their tree-child hybrid number.

Lemma 1
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. Then
h(T,T^{\prime})=h_{tc}(T,T^{\prime})
.

We next define a concept that is closely related to agreement forests. Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees, and let
F=\{T_{\rho},T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{k}\}
be an agreement forest for
T
and
T^{\prime}
. We say that
F
is
acyclic
if the directed graph with vertex set
F
and for which
(T_{i},T_{j})
with
i,j\in\{\rho,1,2,\ldots,k\}
and
i\neq j
is an edge precisely if the root of
T(L(T_{i}))
is an ancestor of the root of
T(L(T_{j}))
, or the root of
T^{\prime}(L(T_{i}))
is an ancestor of the root of
T^{\prime}(L(T_{j}))
has no directed cycles. Note that such an agreement forest always exists. Now, let
F=\{T_{\rho},T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{k}\}
be an acyclic agreement forest for
T
and
T^{\prime}
. Similar to agreement forests, we say that
F
is a
maximum acyclic agreement forest
for
T
and
T^{\prime}
if
k
is minimum over all acyclic agreement forests for
T
and
T^{\prime}
. We denote this minimum number by
m_{a}(T,T^{\prime})
. The relevance of acyclic agreement forests is the next theorem established in
[
1
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. Then
h(T,T^{\prime})=m_{a}(T,T^{\prime})
.

Similar to computing the rSPR distance, Bordewich and Semple
[
3
]
showed that computing the hybrid number for
two phylogenetic trees is NP-hard. Moreover, by Lemma
1
, it immediately follows that computing the tree-child hybrid number is also NP-hard.

Notational remark.
In what follows, we sometimes compare the
\Theta
labelings of two phylogenetic trees where
\Theta\in\{\mathrm{HOP},\mathrm{OLA},\mathrm{P2V}\}
. In this case, we write
f_{\Theta}^{T}
instead of
f_{\Theta}
to make a direct reference to a phylogenetic tree
T
with
n
leaves. For
\Theta=\mathrm{P2V}
, we write
f_{\mathrm{P2V}}^{T}
to refer to the
\mathrm{P2V}
labeling of
T_{n}\simeq T
.

3.1
Bounding by the rSPR distance

In this section, we focus on the relationship between the order-dependent measures OLA, P2V, and HOP, and the rSPR distance.
We begin by showing that given two phylogenetic
X
-trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
, the measure
d^{\ast}_{\mathrm{OLA}}(T,T^{\prime})
is bounded from above by a function that is linear in the rSPR distance
d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T,T^{\prime})
of
T
and
T^{\prime}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. Then, there exists an ordering
\sigma
such that

d^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{OLA}}(T,T^{\prime})\leq 28\cdot d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T,T^{\prime}).

In particular,
d^{\ast}_{\mathrm{OLA}}(T,T^{\prime})\leq 28\cdot d_{\mathrm{rSPR}}(T,T^{\prime})
.

In order to prove this statement, we first show that two tree reduction rules can be used to reduce the size of the label set of a pair of phylogenetic
X
-trees while preserving the OLA distance with respect to
\sigma
between them. These two rules coincide with the two tree reduction rules used by
[
2
]
for the rSPR distance.

Let
T
be a phylogenetic
X
-tree, and let
C=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})
be a sequence of elements in
X
with
l\geq 3
. We say that
C
is a
chain
of
T
if the parent of
x_{1}
coincides with the parent of
x_{2}
or the parent of
x_{2}
is the parent of the parent of
x_{1}
, and, for each
i\in\{3,4,\ldots,l\}
, the parent of
x_{i}
is the parent of the parent of
x_{i-1}
. In what follows, we sometimes abuse notation and write
L(C)
to denote the set
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}\}
.

Now, let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. Each of the following reductions applied to
T
and
T^{\prime}
results in two new phylogenetic trees
S
and
S^{\prime}
with fewer leaves.

•

Subtree reduction:
Let
P
be a maximal common pendant subtree of
T
and
T^{\prime}
with at least two leaves. Obtain
S
and
S^{\prime}
from
T
and
T^{\prime}
, respectively, by replacing
P
with a single leaf with a new label that is not in
X
. Thus, a subtree reduction replaces a common pendant subtree with a single leaf.

•

Chain reduction:
Let
C=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})
be a maximal common chain of
T
and
T^{\prime}
with
l\geq 4
. Obtain
S
and
S^{\prime}
from
T|(X-\{x_{4},x_{5},\ldots,x_{l}\})
and
T^{\prime}|(X-\{x_{4},x_{5},\ldots,x_{l}\})
respectively, by replacing leaf labels
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
, and
x_{3}
with three new labels that are not in
X
. Thus, a chain reduction replaces a common chain of length at least four with a common chain of length three.

We next describe a
2
-step construction to obtain two phylogenetic trees from
T
and
T^{\prime}
by repeated applications of the subtree and chain reductions. For
m\geq 0
, let
P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{m}
be distinct maximal pendant subtrees with at least two leaves that are common to
T
and
T^{\prime}
. Observe that

for two distinct elements

. If

, then set

and
S^{\prime}=T^{\prime}
and, otherwise, obtain two phylogenetic
X^{\prime}
-trees

and
S^{\prime}
from
T
and
T^{\prime}
, respectively, by applying the subtree reduction to each

such that each

is replaced with a single leaf labeled

, where

. Set

Next, for
m^{\prime}\geq 0
, let

be distinct maximal chains of length at least four that are common to

and
S^{\prime}
. Again, by the maximality of each such chain, there exists no element in
X^{\prime}
that is a leaf of
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
for two distinct elements
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m^{\prime}\}
. If
m^{\prime}=0
, set
R=S
and
R^{\prime}=S^{\prime}
and, otherwise, obtain two phylogenetic
X^{\prime\prime}
-trees
R
and
R^{\prime}
from
S
and
S^{\prime}
, respectively, by applying the chain reduction to each

such that

is replaced with

, respectively. Set

If at least one subtree or one chain reduction has been applied in the process of obtaining

from

.

Now let
\sigma^{\prime\prime}

(resp.

(resp.
\mathbf{v}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{U^{\prime}}

(iii)

Finally, all elements of

and

(resp.

are greater than

3.2
Bounding by the hybrid number

In this section, we relate each of the three order-dependent measures to the hybrid number. Specifically, for two phylogenetic
X
-trees

and

, we establish that

are equivalent, which also proves the conjecture of

concerning the computational complexity of computing

d^{*}_{\mathrm{OLA}}(T,T^{\prime})

Theorem 4
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees. Then, for each
\Theta\in\{\mathrm{HOP},\mathrm{OLA},\mathrm{P2V}\}

We note that the hybrid number is an upper bound on the rSPR distance between two phylogenetic
X
-trees and that both measures can differ by up to

.

Lemma 7

Let

and
T^{\prime}
be two phylogenetic
X
-trees, where

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be an ordering on
X
.
Consider the following process: Let
\mathbf{v}_{T}^{\sigma}
and
\mathbf{v}_{T^{\prime}}^{\sigma}
be the HOP vectors for

and
T^{\prime}
, respectively. Let LCS denote
{\rm LCS}(\mathbf{v}_{T}^{\sigma},\mathbf{v}_{T^{\prime}}^{\sigma}

Set

. For each element on the path from the non-leaf vertex

to the element

with

in

We complete the proof by showing that
F
is an acyclic agreement forest for

and
T^{\prime}
. To this end, we use induction on

is isomorphic to

, that is,

is an agreement forest for
T
and
T^{\prime}

is also acyclic. If

, then

is isomorphic to

, and so

is an acyclic agreement forest for
T
and
T^{\prime}
.

Now suppose that the statement holds for all pairs of phylogenetic
X
-trees with
|X|\leq n-1
, where

. Let
x
be the element in
X
with
\sigma(x)=n
,

\sigma_{-x}

5
Conclusion and open problems

Our paper explores the relationship between three novel dissimilarity measures on phylogenetic trees (each of which, given a fixed ordering of the leaf set, takes polynomial time to compute) and the popular, but computationally hard, rSPR distance. While there is little relationship between these measures and rSPR, we show that direct relationships exist when we compare these measures with the hybrid number and temporal hybrid number. We end the paper with some open problems concerning these intriguing classes of measure.

Capturing the rSPR distance

The first problem is to develop an order-dependent measure that is efficient to compute but, when minimized over all orders, is equivalent to the rSPR distance. For the order-dependent measures developed thus far, the ordering of the leaves introduces additional structure that allows for polynomial running time to compute the distances. For example, the HOP ordering provides a clever way to decompose the tree into paths, and for each leaf, the path between it and the root can be viewed as a backbone where leaves later in the ordering are attached. This decomposition captures the agreement forests induced by rSPR moves, albeit well-behaved rSPR moves that do not move a component with a lower minimal element to one with a higher minimal element. Theorem
5
shows that this decomposition precludes cycles in the agreement forests and equates HOP to the hybrid number. Is it possible to keep the efficient running time that the order provides but capture more general rSPR moves?

Open Problem 1
.

Is there an order-dependent measure
\varphi

X
-trees

and

, there exists an ordering

Automata Learning of Preferences over Temporal Logic Formulas from Pairwise Comparisons footnoteinfo
Hazhar Rahmani, Jie Fu

Abstract
Many preference elicitation algorithms consider preference over propositional logic formulas or items with different attributes. In sequential decision making, a user’s preference can be a preorder over possible outcomes, each of which is a temporal sequence of events. This paper considers a class of preference inference problems where the user’s unknown preference is represented by a preorder over regular languages (sets of temporal sequences), referred to as temporal goals. Given a finite set of pairwise comparisons between finite words, the objective is to learn both the set of temporal goals and the preorder over these goals. We first show that a preference relation over temporal goals can be modeled by a Preference Deterministic Finite Automaton (PDFA), which is a deterministic finite automaton augmented with a preorder over acceptance conditions. The problem of preference inference reduces to learning the PDFA. This problem is shown to be computationally challenging, with the problem of determining whether there exists a PDFA of size smaller than a given integer k k , consistent with the sample, being NP-Complete. We formalize the properties of characteristic samples and develop an algorithm that guarantees to learn, given a characteristic sample, the minimal PDFA equivalent to the true PDFA from which the sample is drawn. We present the method through a running example and provide detailed analysis using a robotic motion planning problem.

Temporal logic is often employed to describe temporal behavior of a system or time series data. Motivated by the need to construct a succinct specification of system behaviors, learning temporal logic formulas from a sample, consisting of positive and negative

The approach assumes the oracle produces a preference relation that is

Our learning problem differs from traditional temporal logic inference in that, while the goal of temporal logic inference is typically to infer a single formula, our objective is to learn an automaton that represents the user’s preference over a set of temporal logic formulas, which are also unknown.

Learning user preferences through human feedback employs active preference learning with online planning

,
1

Another closely related line of work is reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)(see a recent survey
[

), which has attracted significant research interests due to its application in human-AI-alignment. RLHF includes two classes of methods: One class of methods directly learns a reward function from pairwise comparisons, and another class of methods directly learns a policy from the feedback. Most RLHF methods consider the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) preference model, which assumes the preference is a total order and can be captured by a reward function. These methods do not extend to learning a preorder over temporally evolving outcomes.

Another related class of methods is preference-based reinforcement learning
(PbRL) (see a survey
[

(RL) in situations where it is challenging to design a numerical reward function.
The robot learns the policy via learning the user preference over states, actions, or trajectories.
Overall, these methods make RL to be more accessible to non-expert users. In comparison to RLHF and PbRL, the proposed method focuses on learning preferences over temporal goals, each of which can be satisfied by a subset of trajectories.

\in{\rm NP}

\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w))=\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w^{\prime}))
, and thus

;

•

If

\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w))=1
and
\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w^{\prime}))=2
, and because

,
w\succ w^{\prime}
.

Therefore,
\mathcal{A}
is consistent with
S
, and because
\mathcal{A}
has

y
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose MCPDFA produces Yes for instance
y
, i.e., there exists a
pdfa

\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_{0},O,\succeq,\lambda)
consistent with
S
such that
|Q|\leq k
.
Because
S^{+}
and
S^{-}
are non-empty, there exists a tuple
(w,w^{\prime},1)\in S
.
This means that

contains at least two distinct ranks

and
o_{2}
such that
\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w))=o_{1}
,
\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w^{\prime}))=o_{2}
, and either

or
o_{2}\succ o_{1}
.
Additionally, because

is consistent with
\mathcal{A}
, it must be the case that for all

,
\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w))=o_{1}
, for all

,
\lambda(\delta(q_{0},w^{\prime}))=o_{2}
, and that

.
Let

.
Accordingly, for the DFA

.
Because DFA

and

and that

This completes the proof.
∎

\in{\rm NP}

The impact of this result is that assuming

Theorem

shows that our automata inference problem, the MCPDFA problem, is generally hard.
In the next section, we present an efficient algorithm that learns the canonical presentation of the true

4
Algorithm Description

In this section, we present an algorithm to learn a
pdfa
from a given sample.
This algorithm first partitions the words within the sample
and derives a partial order between the subsets, referred to as blocks, within the partition.
The algorithm then constructs a prefix tree automaton (defined next) from those words appeared in the sample, and extends the RPNI algorithm
[
24
]
,
to iteratively merge the states of the prefix tree automaton until all the states of the tree automaton are processed.

We introduce some notations: Let
W\subseteq\Sigma^{\ast}
be a set and
\pi=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{\lvert\pi\rvert}\}
be a partition of the set
W
, that is,
B_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i
’s,
\cup_{i=1}^{\lvert\pi\rvert}B_{i}=W
, and
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
.

Definition 6
.

Given a partition
\pi
over a set

block
in partition

Definition 7
.

Given a sample
S\subset\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}\times\{0,1,\perp\}
, the
indifference graph
for sample
S
is an undirected graph
G_{\sim}=(W_{S},E_{\sim})
where for each

,

\{w,u\}\in E_{\sim}
iff
\{(w,u,0),(u,w,0)\}\cap S\neq\emptyset
.

Note that even though the sample might not directly compare two words
u,w
, we may derive these two words to be indifferent using the transitive closure of this relation.
We let
\pi_{S}
to be the set of strongly connected components (SCCs) of
G_{\sim}
, i.e.,
\pi_{S}={\rm SCCs}(G_{\sim})
.
Clearly,
\pi_{S}
is partition of
W_{S}
. Also, for each

, any

is indifferent to

by construction.
Using these, we create a partial order in the following.

Let
O_{S}
be the index set of
\pi_{S}
, i.e.,
O_{S}=\{1,\ldots,|\pi_{S}|\}
. Let
C_{i}
be the
i
-th SCC in
\pi_{S}
.
We construct a relation
R_{S}
over the set of indices
O_{S}
as follows:

R_{S}=\{(i,j)\mid\exists w\in C_{i},w^{\prime}\in C_{j}:(w,w^{\prime},1)\in S\}

(2)

The partial order, denoted
\succeq_{S}
, is constructed as the union of the reflexive closure and the transitive closure of
R_{S}
.

Definition 8
.

Given a sample
S
, its indifference graph

, the index set

of SCCs and relation

constructed from

, a prefix tree

{\rm PT}_{S}=({\rm Pref}(W_{S})=\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{r}\},\Sigma,\delta_{S},u_{0}\coloneqq\epsilon,O\coloneqq O_{S},\succeq_{S},\lambda_{S})

in which

•

{\rm Pref}(W_{S})
is a set of states in the prefix tree
pnfa
, which is the set of all prefixes of the words appeared in the sample. In particular, let
u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{r}
where
r=|{\rm Pref}(W_{S})|
be the states within
{\rm PT}_{S}
indexed with the lexicographical ordering.

•

\Sigma
is the alphabet.

•

The transition function
\delta_{S}
is defined as follows:
For each
w\in{\rm Pref}(W_{S})
,
a\in\Sigma
and

,
\delta_{S}(w,a)=wa
.

•

u_{0}=\epsilon
, the empty string, is the initial state.

•

O_{S}
is the set of ranks.

•

\succeq_{S}
is the partial order relation on
O_{S}
.

•

\lambda_{S}:{\rm Pref}(W_{S})\rightarrow O_{S}\cup\{\varnothing\}
is defined such that
each prefix

is mapped to the index
i
of the SCC that contains

. That is, if

contains

, then

. For each

,

, meaning

is undefined for

.

By construction of SCCs, the rank of each state in the prefix-tree automaton is unique.

Slightly abusing notation, we use each subset (block) of the partition

to denote a rank. It can be understood as each subset is associated with a unique index and the ranks are the set of indices.

Definition 9

(Quotient of a
pnfa
)
.

Given a
pnfa

\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_{0},O,\succeq,\lambda)
, a partition
\pi
of state set
Q
is
consistent
with the
ranking

\lambda
of
\mathcal{A}
if for any
B\in\pi
, for any
q,q^{\prime}\in B
, either 1)
\lambda(q)=\lambda(q^{\prime})
or 2)
\lambda(q)={\varnothing}
or
\lambda(q^{\prime})={\varnothing}
. Such a partition
\pi
is said to be
ranking-consistent
.
The quotient of
\mathcal{A}
under a ranking-consistent partition
\pi
, denoted
\mathcal{A}/\pi
, is a
pnfa

\mathcal{A}/\pi=(\pi,\Sigma,\delta^{\prime},B(q_{0},\pi),O,\succeq^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})

where

•

\pi
is the set of states. Each state
B\in\pi
is a subset of
Q
.

•

\Sigma
is the alphabet.

•

the transition relation
\delta^{\prime}
is defined by:
Given
B,B^{\prime}\in\pi
, and
a\in\Sigma
,
(B,a,B^{\prime})\in\delta^{\prime}
iff there are states
q\in B
and
q^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
such that
\delta(q,a)=q^{\prime}
.
That is, if there is a state
q
in the block
B
, and the transition given input
a
from
q
is defined, then a transition from
B
to
B^{\prime}
with input
a
is defined, where
B^{\prime}
includes all states in
Q
that can be reached with input
a
from some state in
B
.

•

O
is the set of ranks.

•

\succeq^{\prime}\coloneqq\succeq
is the partial order over ranks and is the same as in
\mathcal{A}
.

•

\lambda^{\prime}
is the ranking function, defined by: For each
B\in\pi
, if there exists
q\in B
such that
\lambda(q)\neq{\varnothing}
, then
\lambda^{\prime}(B)=\lambda(q)
, otherwise,
\lambda^{\prime}(B)={\varnothing}
.

It is noted that for a given
B\in\pi
and an input

, there may exist more than one subset

to be reached, i.e., it is possible that
(B,a,B^{\prime})\in\delta^{\prime}
and
(B,a,B^{\prime\prime})\in\delta^{\prime}
where
B^{\prime}\neq B^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, even if
\mathcal{A}
is deterministic, the quotient of
\mathcal{A}
under
\pi
may be nondeterministic.

It is also noted that due to
\pi
being ranking-consistent, the ranking function
\lambda^{\prime}
of the quotient of
\mathcal{A}
is well-defined.

Definition 10
.

Given a partition
\pi
of
Q
, and two blocks
B_{i},B_{j}\in\pi
, the
join
of
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
, denoted
J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})
, is the partition obtained from
\pi
by combining
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
, i.e.,

J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})=\left(\pi\setminus\{B_{i},B_{j}\}\right)\cup\{B_{i}\cup B_{j}\}.

This ‘join’ operation is also called
merging
in the context of automata learning.

Given a quotient
\mathcal{A}/\pi
, the
determinization
of
\mathcal{A}/\pi
, denoted as
D(\mathcal{A}/\pi)
, is computed
recursively
given three different cases:
Case 1: Suppose there exists
B_{k}\in\pi
and

such that
B_{i},B_{j}\in\delta(B_{k},a)
but
B_{i}\neq B_{j}
, and either 1)
\lambda(B_{i})={\varnothing}
or 2)
\lambda(B_{j})={\varnothing}
or 3)
\lambda(B_{i})=\lambda(B_{j})
, then

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A}/\pi)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D(\mathcal{A}/J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j}))&\text{ if $D(\mathcal{A}/J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j}))\neq\varnothing$,}\\
\varnothing&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})
is the join of
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
in partition
\pi
and
\varnothing
means that the output is undefined.

Case 2: Suppose there exist
B_{k}\in\pi
and
a\in\Sigma
such that
B_{i},B_{j}\in\delta(B_{k},a)
but
B_{i}\neq B_{j}
,
\lambda(B_{i})\neq{\varnothing}
,
\lambda(B_{j})\neq{\varnothing}
, and
\lambda(B_{i})\neq\lambda(B_{j})
, then

D(\mathcal{A}/\pi)={\varnothing}.

Case 3: if there is no non-deterministic transitions in
\mathcal{A}/\pi
, that is,
|\delta(B,a)|\leq 1
for any
B\in\pi
,
a\in\Sigma
, then

D(\mathcal{A}/\pi)=\mathcal{A}/\pi.

It is observed that the function
D(\cdot)
operates on a
pnfa
and is a recursive function (self-referential). It is similar to determinization of an
nfa
, except that each state here is a block and the join of two blocks is carried out when the ranking consistency condition is satisfied. That is, If
B_{i}
is joined with
B_{j}
in a ranking-consistent partition
\pi
in this determinization operation, then the new partition
J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})
is ranking-consistent.

The joining and determinizing operations repeat until no more joins can be made or the input to function is a
pdfa
.

Accordingly, we define a new operation
DJ
, called the
deterministic join
, which produces a quotient automaton:

\mathcal{A}_{0}/DJ(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})=D(\mathcal{A}_{0}/J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})).

In words, this operation first joins two blocks
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
, and then may join more blocks recursively as the result of the determinization of the quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
under the new partition
J(\pi,B_{i},B_{j})
. After the operation, the obtained partition is denoted by
DJ(\pi_{i},B_{i},B_{j})
.

Finally, the

We lift the lexicographical ordering

7
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we consider the problem of learning a preference deterministic finite automaton, representing a user preference over a set of temporally extended goals. The learner is presented with a sample containing a collection of pairwise comparisons between temporal sequences/words (trajectories).
We demonstrate that this problem is computationally challenging and propose an inference algorithm based on a modification of the RPNI automata learning algorithm
[
24
]
. The proposed algorithm incorporates similar state-merging operations as RPNI but uses distinct decision rules to determine whether a merge is accepted or not.

We define the concept of characteristic samples and prove that, given a characteristic sample for the preference automaton, our algorithm successfully learns the minimal
pdfa
equivalent to the true
pdfa
from which the sample is drawn. The correctness and effectiveness of our algorithm are validated through several case studies, including scenarios with both characteristic and non-characteristic samples.

Future work will explore an active learning of preference, where the learner can query a limited number of words and solicit human feedback on pairwise comparisons of specifically selected pairs based on the current dataset. In this context, the learner must strategically select pairs to minimize the number of queries required from the human. It is noted that our proposed algorithm is a passive, batch learner: If a new datum is added, then the learner must be restarted from scratch.
Incremental learning algorithms could also be desired, where the learner receives data sequentially and incrementally updates the learned model with newly seen data. Additionally, future research will investigate integrating preference learning with preference-based decision-making in online planning setups. This approach aims to enable sample-efficient learning of control strategies directly from human feedback.

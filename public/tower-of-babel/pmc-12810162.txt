Evaluation of Effectiveness of an Educational Intervention Program on the Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice Regarding Use and Storage of Toothbrush among Parents and Children
Authors: Patilla Amreen Shaban, K Reshma Pai, KP Mohamed Labeeb, Mohammad Hashir
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundToothbrushes are crucial for oral hygiene, but improper storage and maintenance can lead to contamination, posing risks of dental and systemic diseases. Educating children and parents on proper toothbrush care is essential to prevent contamination and promote good oral health.ObjectivesThis study aimed to assess knowledge on toothbrush use and storage among parents and children, educate on proper management, evaluate the microbial load and storage site relationship, and assess the outcome of an educational program on toothbrush practices.Materials and methodsA randomized controlled trial was conducted among 60 children (7–9 years) and their parents in three phases: baseline knowledge assessment, microbial load analysis of used toothbrushes, and evaluation of an educational program's effectiveness through a follow-up survey.ResultsThe study found a significant reduction in microbial load on toothbrushes and improvement in knowledge, attitude, and practice regarding proper toothbrush use and storage among children and parents after the educational intervention.ConclusionEducational programs can significantly improve knowledge and attitudes toward proper toothbrush use and storage. Dentists play a crucial role in promoting good oral hygiene practices, and regular toothbrush disinfection is essential for preventing infections and maintaining overall health.Clinical significanceThis study highlights the importance of proper toothbrush storage and maintenance in preventing contamination and promoting oral and systemic health, particularly through targeted education for parents and children.How to cite this articleShaban PA, Pai KR, KP ML, et al. Introduction

Toothbrush is an essential part of everyone's day-to-day life, but it plays an important role in disease transmission and increases the risk of infection.1 Most children and parents use toothbrush for cleaning teeth, but there is very little public awareness that toothbrushes can become contaminated with use.2 If not stored and maintained properly, toothbrush may induce not only dental diseases but also many systemic problems such as respiratory, gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, septicemia, and renal problems.3 American Dental Association issued health advisory of changing toothbrush every 3 months in 1996.4 Studies have shown that the effectiveness in removing dental plaque reduces owing to flaring of bristles.5 However, microbial contamination was not taken into account while advocating the number of times a toothbrush needs to be changed.5 By maintaining good oral hygiene and using clean disinfected toothbrushes, oral and systemic diseases can be controlled vastly.6 After brushing, toothbrushes are mostly rinsed with normal water and often kept in bathrooms or bathrooms with attached toilet, which become perfect hubs for millions of microorganisms, as they may be exposed to pathogenic bacteria that are dispersed during flushing.7 Millions of bacteria are released in the atmosphere through flushing of toilets.8 Toothbrushes stored together in one container get contaminated when they rub against each other.9 Moreover, toothbrushes should not be exchanged between individuals, which could happen by mistake if kept in one container.6 With children, the possibility of toothbrush contamination is higher compared to adults, and as they have less immunity than adults, they are more susceptible to infection and diseases.10 Hence, proper knowledge and attitude among parents toward children is necessary for the prevention of contamination. Therefore, it is important to educate children and parents regarding proper maintenance and storage of toothbrush.

Materials and Methods

A randomized controlled trial was conducted in three phases after obtaining the ethical clearance from the Institutional Ethics Committee (SIDSEC) with reference number 2022/8/1-44.

First phase: A preliminary visit was given to a local school, and the purpose of the study was explained to the school principal, children, and parents, and written informed consent was obtained. A list of 60 children from the age of 7–9 years were obtained from local school registry and randomly divided into study group and control group by lottery method. Considering mean difference in bacterial count of 2.12 for test group and 2.10 for control group, standard deviation = 4.8,11 confidence interval = 95%, power = 80%, margin of clinical significance = 0.5, and sampling ratio 1:1 for test and control groups, a total sample of 60, that is, 30 in each group, was estimated. Parents of study participants were requested to take part in educational program. A closed-ended self-structured questionnaire both in English and regional language was given to the study participants of both groups. The questions were explained to the respondents, who were asked to tick the appropriate answer according to their judgment, to assess their knowledge and attitudes toward the management, use, and storage of toothbrushes. Based on the result obtained, participants were divided into control group and experimental group. Control group kept their toothbrushes outside the washroom and study group kept their toothbrushes inside the washroom with or without attached toilet.

Second phase (sample collection): Used toothbrushes were collected from the participants, A sterile zip-lock pouch was given to the participants prior to the day of collection, and they were instructed to put their toothbrushes in the pouch and close it tightly. A new toothbrush was given to participants for the replacement of toothbrush. The samples were then sent to the laboratory for the microbial load analysis for any of the following microorganisms: Escherichia coli, Pseudomonas, Enterococcus, and Staphylococcus species.

MacConkey agar plates were prepared and autoclaved at 121°C and 15 lbs for 15 minutes. The medium was poured onto sterile 100 mm Petri dishes and allowed to solidify, and plates were exposed to ultraviolet (UV) light for 15–20 minutes. The toothbrushes were immersed in 5 mL of sterile distilled water. A 100 mL of the sample was pipetted out on to the plate containing solidified media, and with the help of sterile spreader, the sample was spread evenly to the entire plate. Plates were incubated at 37°C for 24 hours. The plates were observed for bacterial growth.

Based on the results obtained from laboratory, an educational program was conducted with a Microsoft PowerPoint presentation to create awareness among the parents and children of study group for proper maintenance, use, and storage of toothbrush.

Third phase: A follow-up survey was done after the educational program to know the outcome of informative lecture on the knowledge of parents and children, using the same set of questionnaires. The demographic variables and responses were assessed statistically. At the end of the study, educational program was conducted for the control group as a moral obligation through online platforms and asked for their feedback; few people who could not attend the meeting because of some prior commitments were called personally and given talk.

Discussion

Literature review has shown that toothbrushes are very good source for the germination of various microorganisms.6 It has also been noted in the literature that the place of storage has direct and indirect impacts on the growth of microorganisms.12 Almost all the toothbrushes used in the present study showed growth of microorganisms such as E. coli, Pseudomonas, Enterococcus, and Staphylococci, which is in accordance with the study done by Kuhn et al.

In the present study, microorganisms isolated from toothbrushes can be a risk factor for the etiology of numerous general and oral diseases. E. coli, usually found in the gut of humans, can be transmitted through aerosol contamination. Hence, transmission of E. coli to toothbrush vastly depends on the storage place, especially in attached-toilet bathrooms.

Pseudomonas can cause urinary tract infection (UTI), Enterococci and Staphylococci cause abscess, pustules, carbuncle, septicemia, etc., and can be transmitted by storing all toothbrushes in the same container.13

The result of the present study shows the presence of all the above-mentioned organisms in the toothbrushes, irrespective of place of storage; hence, decontamination of used toothbrushes with proper disinfecting solutions should be stressed upon.

Literature search has shown that the use of chlorhexidine solution spraying every day vastly reduces the load of organisms present in toothbrushes.14

It was seen in the present study, after intervention program, that there was marked difference in the knowledge, attitude, and practice of children and parents regarding proper storage of the used toothbrushes, as well as disinfecting the toothbrushes with proper disinfecting solution. In the course of the study, it was also seen that parents and children had insufficient knowledge regarding disposal of used toothbrushes, which was vastly improved after receiving educational intervention.

Toothbrushes, which are contaminated, can transmit infection among vulnerable population.15 Thus, our finding accentuates that many more educational programs are needed on a larger sample size to improve the knowledge of parents and children regarding proper storage and disposal of used toothbrushes, as oral health vastly influences general health.

Conclusion

Toothbrushes stored in a bathroom, with or without attached toilet, are likely to become a place of origin for contamination. The results of the study show that educational program vastly improved the knowledge and attitude of the population. Hence, dentists can play an important role by advising the patients about proper usage, storage, and disposal of used toothbrush. It is also very important for every person to disinfect his or her toothbrush at regular interval to prevent the spread of infection and maintain good oral and general health for the well-being of an individual.

Clinical Significance

The result of the study accentuates that many more such educational programs should be conducted to educate the population on the harmful effect of organisms present in toothbrushes, to improve the knowledge regarding proper storage and use of proper disinfecting solution, which helps in reducing microbial load present in toothbrushes and to promote proper disposal of used toothbrushes, as oral health vastly influences general health.

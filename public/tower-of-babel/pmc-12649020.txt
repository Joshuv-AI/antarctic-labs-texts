Systemic therapy followed by liver resection improved prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma associated with macrovascular invasion
Authors: Jinyu Zhang, Zirui Zhang, Qionglan Wu, Jinhua Zeng, Jingfeng Liu, Jianxing Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThere is no standard treatment for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) with macrovascular invasion (MaVI). This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of liver resection (LR) and the timing of systemic therapy in HCC with MaVI.MethodsThe SEER database included 106,613 HCC patients, 1,892 of whom had MaVI. Patients were divided into the LR group (n = 126) and Non-LR group (n = 1,766). The LR group was further split into those receiving preoperative systemic treatment (pre-AST, n = 32) and postoperative systemic treatment (post-AST, n = 37). Overall survival (OS) was compared using Kaplan–Meier survival curves. Cox regression analysis identified independent risk factors, and propensity score matching (PSM) was used to reduce selection bias.ResultsLiver resection was performed in 12.8% of cases, including 9.4% of stage III and 2.2% of stage IV patients. After PSM, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates in the LR group were 65.9%, 37.7%, and 28.6%, respectively, compared to 29.3%, 10.1%, and 5.4% in the Non-LR group (p < 0.01). Multivariate Cox analysis identified LR as an independent factor for improved OS (HR = 0.36, 95%CI = 0.25–0.50, p < 0.01). In the pre-AST and post-AST groups, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates were 81.2%, 50.0%, 40.6% and 45.9%, 21.6%, 13.5%, respectively (p = 0.01).ConclusionsLiver resection is linked to better survival outcomes for HCC patients with macrovascular invasion, especially when preoperative systemic treatment is given.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03126-w.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Macrovascular invasion, Liver resection, Prognosis, SEER

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a common cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. Despite the widespread application of screening programs and improvements in diagnostic imaging, a significant number of patients are being diagnosed at an advanced stage with treatment options limited to mostly palliative systemic therapy leading to poor outcomes [2, 3]. Macrovascular (portal or hepatic vein) invasion (MaVI) is one of the main adverse factors for the prognosis of HCC and plays an important role in the clinical staging system of liver cancer [4]. Portal venous tumor thrombus can increase portal pressure, leading to esophagogastric variceal hemorrhage, jaundice, and peritoneal effusion and cause intrahepatic metastasis [5]. Hepatic venous tumor thrombus can extend into the inferior vena cava, leading to the formation of tumor thrombus in the right atrium, pulmonary metastasis [6].

At present, there is still no consensus on the diagnostic and treatment standards for HCC combined with macrovascular invasion internationally. According Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer Staging (BCLC), HCC patients with macrovascular invasion are categorized as advanced-stage HCC (BCLC stage C), and systemic treatment is recommended [4]. However, even with recommended systemic therapy such as sorafenib and Lenvatinib only has marginal survival benefits for those with vascular invasion [7].

Currently, liver resection is the most effective curative treatment for HCC [8]. As a result of recent advances in surgical techniques and conversion therapy, the indications of liver resection (LR) are expanding. Scholars from Southeast Asian countries believe that surgery, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE), radiotherapy and a combination of multiple treatments can achieve more satisfactory results for HCC with MaVI [9, 10].

Therefore, the aim of this study was to assess the efficacy of liver resection (LR) and timing of systemic therapy for HCC associated with MaVI through an analysis of data recorded in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results Database (SEER).

Method

Object and eligibility

Prospectively collected data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database maintained by the National Cancer Institute were used for analysis in this study (Incidence -SEER Research Plus Data,17 Registries, Nov 2021 Sub [2000–2019]). The study is exempt from Ethical Committee approval as a de-identified dataset from the SEER was analyzed in the current study.

1,892 HCC patients with AJCC 7th clinical stage T3BNOM0 (tumor of any size involving a major branch of the portal vein or hepatic vein without lymph node or extrahepatic metastasis) who were diagnosed between 2010 and 2015 were identified from the SEER. The inclusion criteria included: (1) Primary site-labeled = C22.0-Liver; (2) ICD-O-3 Hist/behav, malignant = 8170–8175/3; (3) HCC with MaVI. HCC with distant metastasis or lymphatic metastasis, had incomplete data were excluded from the analysis. Flow chart of data processing is shown in Fig. 1.

The flowchart of selected HCC patients

This study does not require Ethical Committee approval as the study analyzed a deidentified dataset obtained from the SEER. However, to ensure compliance with ethical guidelines, the necessary authorizations and permissions were obtained from the SEER program to access and utilize the dataset.

Definition of liver resection and systemic treatment

Liver resection was defined using the variable “RX Summ–Surg Prim Site (1998 +)” which records the surgical procedure performed at the primary site. Specimen sent to pathology from surgical events (20–75) were regard as liver resection. The sequence of LR and systemic treatment was defined using the variable “RX Summ–Systemic/Sur Seq”.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean (standard deviation) and continuous variables as number (%). Continuous variables were used t test or Wilcoxon rank sum test and categorical variables were used Chi-square or Fisher exact tests for comparative analysis in our study. Kaplan–Meier method was used to draw the survival curve, the COX regression was used for univariate and multivariate analysis, and the corresponding Hazard Ratio (HR) and 95% Confidence interval (CI) were analyzed. PSM is performed by the ratio (1:1). p < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant. Our statistical analyses were performed with R version 4.0.4(http://www.r-project.org/).

Application of liver resection in hepatocellular carcinoma

We analyzed the proportion of all HCC patients receiving treatment modalities in the SEER database and performed subgroup analyses for different 7th AJCC TNM stages. We found that LR was the most important treatment for the primary site of HCC, accounting for 12.8%. Subgroup analysis showed that the proportion of patients receiving LR in TNM stage III and IV were 9.4% and 2.2%, respectively (Fig. 2).

Impact of liver resection on prognosis

Before PSM, the OS rates at 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year were 62.4%, 33.8%, 23.9%, respectively, in LR group and 24.2%, 8.2%, 5.3%, respectively, in Non-LR group (p < 0.01, Fig. 3A). After PSM, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates in the LR group were 65.9%, 37.7%, and 28.6%, respectively, while the rates in the Non-LR group were 29.3%, 10.1%, and 5.4% (p < 0.01, Fig. 3B).

Further subgroup analysis showed that the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates in the pre-AST group and post-AST group were 81.2%, 50.0%, 40.6%, and 45.9%, 21.6%, 13.5%, (p = 0.01, Fig. 4), respectively. Prognostic factors in HCC associated with MaVI

The univariate and multivariate Cox analyses for determining the risk factors associated with OS were shown in supplementary Table 2. Before PSM, multivariate cox analysis revealed that liver resection (HR = 0.39, 95%CI = 0.31–0.49, p < 0.01), chemotherapy (HR = 0.60, 95%CI = 0.54–0.67, p < 0.01), and living in metropolitan (HR = 0.89, 95%CI = 0.79–0.99, p = 0.03) were the independent protected factors associated with OS. And male (HR = 1.17, 95%CI = 1.01–1.35, p = 0.03), tumor size 5–10 cm (HR = 1.25, 95%CI = 1.10–1.43, p = 0.03), tumor size > 10 cm (HR = 1.52, 95%CI = 1.31–1.76, p < 0.01), AFP positive (HR = 1.61, 95%CI = 1.37–1.89, p < 0.01) were the independent risked factors associated with OS. After PSM, multivariate cox analysis revealed that liver resection (HR = 0.36, 95%CI = 0.25–0.50, p < 0.01), were the independent protected factor associated with OS. Tumor size > 10 cm (HR = 1.87, 95%CI = 1.21–2.89, p < 0.01) were the independent risked factor associated with OS (Fig. 5).

Discussion

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a hypervascular tumor and has a tendency to invade portal vein (PV) or hepatic vein (HV). The incidence of portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT) has been reported to be as high as 44–62% [11], and hepatic vein tumor thrombosis (HVTT) is present in around 1.4–4.9% [12–15]. HCC with macrovascular invasion (MaVI) is associated with poor prognosis.

At present, liver resection (LR) is the most commonly used treatment to cure HCC and associated with 5-year survival exceeding 60% [16, 17]. Our study shows that LR is the most important treatment for the primary site in all HCC patients, accounting for 9.4% (AJCC TNM III) and 2.2% (AJCC TNM IV) even for partially advanced HCC, respectively. Kumada from Japan first reported liver resection for HCC with PVTT [18]. Since then, many scholars, especially from East Asia, have also reported the survival benefits of liver resection for the treatment of HCC with MaVI [10, 19–21]. A nationwide multicenter analysis in Japan and showed that the median survival time (MST) in the LR group was 1.77 years longer than that in the Non-LR group (2.87 years vs 1.10 years) and 0.88 years longer than that in the Non-LR group (2.45 years vs 1.57 years) in a propensity score-matched cohort [9]. There is growing evidence that MaVI should not be considered an absolute contraindication to liver resection, especially for some vascular tumor thrombi that are technically resectable originally or after preoperative conversion therapy, active surgical treatment can bring survival benefits to patients. Recently, a meta-analysis included 40 studies involving 8,218 patients showed that overall median survival was 14.39 months for HCC patients with MaVI following resection and median survival was higher in PVTT with segmental/2nd order involvement at 20.41 versus 6.41 months if the main trunk was involved [22]. In our study, both KM survival analysis and Cox regression analysis suggested that LR improved OS and was a protective factor for prognosis. In contrast to most studies from East Asia, it is worth mentioning that the data we analyzed came from the western country.

The treatment of HCC complicated with MaVI is challenging, although there are currently treatments including surgery, TACE, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy. However, there is still no international consensus on the treatment options for this group of patients. Many previous studies have shown that preoperative or postoperative systemic therapy can improve the prognosis of patients with high-risk hepatocellular carcinoma [23–27]. In this study, we attempted to explore the effect of the timing of systemic therapy on survival after hepatocellular carcinoma complicated with MaVI. The results revealed that patients receiving preoperative systemic therapy exhibited better outcomes compared to those receiving postoperative systemic therapy. It is hypothesized that the observed phenomenon may be attributed to the efficacy of preoperative therapies, such as chemotherapy, transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), and radiotherapy, in facilitating surgical procedures by diminishing vascular tumor thrombi and decreasing tumor size, thereby enabling sufficient resection margins and preserving residual liver volume. Postoperative therapy primarily aims to eradicate residual cancer cells and micro-metastases. The optimal timing of treatment for HCC remains an area of ongoing investigation, necessitating further clinical validation in future studies.

There are some limitations in our study: firstly, this is a retrospective study, and some pertinent data were not available or were missing. For example, the Child–Pugh score and cirrhosis status. Secondly, the SEER database determined macrovascular invasion based on imaging examinations for non-LR patients. Finally, these data did not include the extent of vascular invasion, precluding further subgroup analysis.

In conclusion, Liver resection is the most commonly used treatment for the primary site of HCC. For patients with HCC complicated with MaVI who undergo LR combined with systemic therapy, patients receiving preoperative systemic therapy exhibited better outcomes compared to those receiving postoperative systemic therapy.

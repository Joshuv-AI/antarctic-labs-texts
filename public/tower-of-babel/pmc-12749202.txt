A scalable post quantum secure blockchain framework with adaptive time consensus in cloud environments
Authors: M. Velmurugan, M. Rajeev Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Blockchain deployments continue to face challenges related to scalability, energy consumption, and susceptibility of classical cryptographic primitives to emerging quantum attacks. Conventional systems employing RSA or DSA signatures and consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work (PoW) or Proof of Stake (PoS) incur substantial computational overhead and are not well suited for cloud-scale execution. This study presents PQ-PoETChain, a post-quantum-secure blockchain model integrating NTRU-based signatures, an adaptive Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET) protocol executed within Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs), and a Lightweight Hash Validation (LHV) mechanism. The framework was implemented in Python and evaluated in a controlled simulation environment using 50–1000 nodes, with repeated trials to measure variability across throughput, latency, and energy metrics. NTRU demonstrated sub-2 ms signature operations, while the adaptive PoET configuration reduced consensus delay under load-dependent conditions. Across multiple experimental runs, the system achieved an average throughput of ~ 195 TPS with a latency of 189 ± 4 ms at 500 nodes. Energy consumption reduced by up to 91.8% (± 1.6%) when compared with PoW under identical conditions. LHV further lowered verification cost by replacing Merkle-tree traversal with constant-time hash-pointer validation. Results indicate that PQ-PoETChain offers a balanced combination of quantum-resilient security and improved performance characteristics suitable for cloud-native and large-scale deployments.

Introduction

The blockchain technology has been used as a backbone infrastructure to decentralized, transparent and tamper-resistant data management within financial systems, supply-chain networks and healthcare settings1. Nevertheless, classical blockchain usage is still facing three long-standing challenges, namely (i) limits to the computational scalability, (ii) high-energy costs of consensus mechanisms, and (iii) long-term insecurity due to quantum-computing development2. The consensus algorithms like Proof of Work (PoW) have huge energy and hardware investment implications, whereas Proof of Stake (PoS) results in validator-centralization vulnerability and unstable performance with heterogeneous cloud settings3. More importantly, the susceptibility of RSA- and ECC-based digital signatures to quantum attackers is more severe4. With a Shor-model attacker, a model attacker who can execute Shor model factoring and discrete-logarithm algorithms with a fault-tolerant quantum computer, these classical schemes break down in the course of a polynomial time5. Moreover, the algorithm of Grover undermines the security margin of hash-based primitives, which require more digest sizes to provide long-term blockchain integrity6. Therefore, classical blockchain security models cannot be made in post-quantum environments, especially in long-lived or high-value applications7.

Cloud-native blockchain deployments further amplify these challenges8. It is necessary to have consensus mechanisms which are adaptive, lightweight, and time-efficient, due to heterogeneous nodes, elastic resource allocation and the lack of a consistent bandwidth9. Current solutions take quantum safety, energy efficiency, or cloud scalability separately, but hardly combine them into a common and practical system10. Lattice-based schemes like NTRU which are post-quantum have high computational efficiency and quantum hardening, but have not been widely integrated into blockchain systems and in most cases only been experimentally demonstrated at theoretical scale11. Similarly, PoET includes a low-energy alternative to PoW but has no flexibility to changing network loads and node performance changes12. Classical Merkle-tree validation scales up to greater overhead as the blockchain increases, and is the reason behind seeking the existence of lighter verification structures like compact hash-chain mechanisms13. To address these shortcomings, this study presents PQ-PoETChain, a single architecture post-quantum blockchain based on cloud-friendly NTRU cryptography and adaptive PoET consensus, and LHV.

Recent innovation and limits

Recent advances on blockchain studies have been more concerned with the realization of quantum resistance, scalability, and energy overhead. The lattice-based post-quantum cryptography, especially NTRU has become widely known because it is resistant to Shor-based attacks, and it has a more desirable performance profile than other PQC families. Nevertheless, the majority of models based on NTRU-based blockchains are still experimental, have not been tested on a real-world scale, and frequently add extra processing overhead that enables their utilization only in limited scope in cloud environments. Correspondingly, consensus mechanisms are no longer energy-intensive PoW but more efficient, i.e. PoS, PoET14. Although PoET can decrease energy usage by utilizing TEEs, its design (fixed-wait-time) is incompatible with dynamically and heterogeneously configured cloud architectures, where the performance of nodes and network latency can differ greatly. Simultaneously, lightweight validation structures have been suggested to help lower the rising verification cost of Merkle-tree-based structures15. Hash-chain based approaches that have smaller overheads exist, but existing ones do not support post-quantum cryptography and are not designed to be deployed at cloud scale as well16. Overall, while cryptography, consensus design, and verification mechanisms have innovations, they are not coherent, and none of them can resolve the issue of quantum resistance, adaptiveness, and cloud scalability simultaneously.

Research gap

Despite the various studies conducted on the topic of post-quantum blockchain security, there are still serious limitations. First, most lattice-based blockchain prototypes are characterized by complexity in terms of computation and do not optimize to integrate into real-time clouds. Existing research does not assess performance at realistic cloud workloads, acceleration to scale, or heterogeneous node setups17. Second, quantum-resistant algorithms are likely to add cost when it comes to signing and verification services, which means that the existing models cannot be deployed in lightweight, distributed, or resource-constrained environments. Third, the vast majority of consensus mechanisms, be it PoW, PoS or PoET, are yet to be sufficiently adaptive. They do not dynamically respond to node load, network congestion or varying delay conditions that are common in the cloud system. Lastly, the vast majority of previous research relates to the abstract, theory-level security without the explicit definition of quantum adversarial models18. The difference between Shor-based signature compromise and Grover-based hash weakening rarely is covered and thus has not been fully addressed in terms of threat modeling. This leaves a distinct research gap: a cloud-scalable, quantum-secure, and adaptively timed blockchain system confirmed in realistic working conditions and with the ability to provide high throughput and low energy consumption.

Research motivation

The long-term security of classical blockchain cryptographic primitives is imperilled by the fast emerging nature of fault-tolerant quantum processors. The algorithm by Shor makes RSA and ECC insecure and the algorithm of Grover makes weak components of a symmetric-hash based cryptography like PoW difficulty computations. This increases the necessity to switch to quantum resistant signatures and more efficient consensus algorithms. At the same time, the world blockchains are also migrated to cloud architecture with different performance, network delays, and available resources. The mechanisms used today do not include adaptive timing and load-sensitive decision rules, and thus, cause bottlenecks in performance and resource wasteage. Driven by the convergent issues, this study will develop a single blockchain framework, which will be resistant to post-quantum adversaries, dynamically optimize the consensus delay in response to real-time cloud environment dynamics, and minimize the computational and energy overheads in large-scale deployments.

Research significance

This research is important in three primary ways. First, it offers an effective combination of NTRU-based post-quantum cryptography to a blockchain architecture that can be executed on clouds, and long-term cryptographic security against Shor-based attacks. Second, it proposes a dynamically changing PoET that determines the waiting time of blocks based on real-time information on node performance, network latency, and congestion levels, which improves performance in heterogeneous cloud settings. Thirdly, LHV allows reduction of verification overhead without affecting integrity which allows higher throughput and quicker validation than traditional Merkle-tree structures do. The given framework is of particular relevance to the real-time and high-stakes fields like financial transactions, data management in healthcare facilities and distributed IoT systems, where resilience, rapidity, and cloud scalability are all demanded simultaneously.

Related works

More recent developments in post-quantum secure blockchain designs have been to add lattice-based cryptography, lightweight consensus, and optimization of validation strategies, especially when for an IoT, cloud, and resource-constrained environment. In the literature, two key research directions are identified, namely, quantum-resistant signature schemes and scalable, efficient blockchain consensus and validation.

Quantum-resistant signature schemes

NTRU and lattice-based cryptography has been broadly used to counter quantum threats. Wang et al.17 proposed NTRU-MCF, a lattice-based signature system based on multidimensional poly rings and fractional-order chaotic systems, which has high quantum resistance and can withstand side-channel attack. Both Kim et al.19 and Bagchi et al.20 presented NTRU-based linkage ring signatures and single-round multi-signatures schemes with a secure and trapdoor-free design to the decentralized blockchain and IoT systems. Likewise, Yuan et al.21 and Kumar Yadav et al.22 used NTRU lattice schemes in IoT and automotive networks, which allow communication without any tampering and efficient computation. All these studies put NTRU in the position of a suitable post-quantum security solution, though the high-frequency transaction, small-resource device, and infrastructure integration still pose challenges.

Consensus mechanisms and lightweight validation

Another center of focus has been scalable, energy efficient as well as low-latency consensus mechanisms. Kim et al.18 introduced PQ-dPoL, which integrates Falcon signatures and a delegated proof-of-luck consensus and Ayub Khan et al.13 proposed B-LPoET, a lightweight PoET-based protocol with multithreaded leaders selection. Both are better when it comes to fairness, quantum resistance and energy efficiency, although they have lower throughput and higher signature latency thus restricting larger scale performance in public blockchains. Simultaneously, Aboshosha et al.23 and Chen et al.24 explored lightweight blockchain architecture and validation by hash functions to streamline storage and computation of IoT and federated learning. These solutions prove resilient to attacks and use less resources, and are limited in scale in environments that are highly dynamic or bandwidth-constrained. Irshad et al.25 applied these principles to the hybrid cloud-IoT networks, which result in better responsiveness and multi-user scalability, but the complexity of the system grows when using a large deployment.

Hybrid and auditing frameworks

Frameworks that combine auditing and identity-based verification also promote the reliability of blockchains. Gautam et al.20 and Levina et al.26 suggested lattice-cryptography-based auditing and cryptanalysis models to enhance post-quantum integrity checks and minimize the third-party auditors. Nevertheless, real-time and high-throughput applications have not been an easy task, since these frameworks are mainly oriented in terms of security rather than optimization of performance.

Research gap

The main common limitation that can be identified throughout the reviewed literature is that high computational complexity of post-quantum signature schemes impedes transaction throughput and limits their implementation on devices with limited computational resources; time-based consensus protocols and multi-signatures are only scalable to a limited extent; and the lightweight block-validation methods are not integrated with the lightweight quantum-resistant signature schemes and adaptable consensus mechanisms. The available models of PQC-enabled consensus, such as PQ-dPoL, B-LPoET and their variants, partially overcome these issues but do not provide quantum security, low-latency validation and scalability to clouds at the same time, leaving a large gap in the state of the art.

By synergistically combining NTRU signatures to post-quantum security with lower computational cost, an Adaptive PoET consensus mechanism based on TEE-enabled dynamic leader-timer adjustment to achieve fairness, energy efficiency and scalability, and LHV scheme with a constant block verification and low memory cost, the proposed framework will be situated in this gap. These elements, when combined, address the weaknesses of previous methods and are jointly additive to create a quantum-resilient, energy-efficient, low-latency, and intrinsically compatible with cloud and large-scale distributed, blockchain architecture, which becomes the basis of forward-compatible next-generation decentralized applications.

Proposed post quantum secure blockchain model

The suggested blockchain architecture combines three main components to become post-quantum, low-latency consensus, and efficient cloud-friendly block validator. To initiate the process, a transaction is initiated and each user-created transaction is signed within the NTRU lattice-based cryptographic scheme, which provides high resistance to quantum-based attacks by ensuring it’s signing and verification processes are efficient. The signed transactions are then relayed to cloud-hosted blockchain nodes where they are held in the mempool. Such transactions are performed in the Adaptive PoET consensus mechanism. The secure base wait time is created in each node which runs within a Trusted Execution Environment. Instantaneous network indicators such as latency, TPS, CPU load and mempool size are gathered and fed into a moving equation that calculates a dynamic wait time. The block proposer is the node having the shortest adjusted timer. The node then uses the LHV mechanism to create a new block of verified transactions and a concise cryptographic pointer to the last block. This module substitutes the complicated Merkle tree structures with the efficient hash pointers making the complexity of validation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:O\left(1\right)$$\end{document}. After validation, the blocks are added to the distributed ledger and the metrics regarding the run-time are fed back to the system to be constantly optimized. Transaction initialization and signing using NTRU

The classical blockchain systems require the transaction signing operation, which is commonly based on either ECC or RSA, and both of these are vulnerable to quantum attacks because of the strength of Shor’s algorithm. Lattice-based cryptographic algorithms, in this case the NTRU (Nth degree truncated polynomial ring units) signature scheme, benefit the field of post-quantum security greatly when it comes to security and computation efficiency. Blockchain also provides the security of digital signatures, providing authentication, data integrity, and non-repudiation using asymmetric cryptographic algorithms. In order to ensure quantum threats, the study uses the NTRU Signature Scheme, a type of post-quantum cryptography. With embedded NTRU, the system should be able to secure tamper-proof and verifiable transactions in the future, in a world where quantum computing would be a threat to conventional means. The NTRU encryption scheme is a lattice-based cryptosystem aiming at supporting fast encryption and digital signatures. It is constructed on top of the ring of truncated polynomials and uses the intractability of some lattice problems, which are, despite being intractable to quantum algorithms. In contrast to RSA and ECC, whose security is grounded in number-theoretic problems, NTRU is grounded on problems in ideal lattices, specifically those of the Shortest Vector Problem and of Learning With Errors variants. Quantum computers also fail to solve these problems, which makes NTRU post-quantum. The NTRU uses polynomial rings. Adaptive PoET-based consensus initiation

Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET) is a time-based consensus mechanism introduced by Intel which was proposed initially to be used in permissioned blockchain networks. PoET uses Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs) to make sure that a validator node has to wait a randomly selected period of time before proposing a block. The node whose timer is finished first gets an opportunity to add a block to the blockchain. In this work, this baseline PoET model is modified to an adaptive PoET consensus, where the waiting time serves as an adjustable parameter that adapts to real-time dynamically changing network load, node responsiveness, mempool state and block difficulty level (measured in the blockchain dataset indicators). The innovation leads to a fairer block proposal, decreased resource competition, and responsive consensus, especially in cloud deployment blockchain systems. The proposed framework is designed with an adaptive PoET mechanism, which is created based on four key elements. The TEE allows secure and tamper-proof operation of consensus logic. Dynamic Wait-Time Computation Module creates an adaptation of the wait times corresponding to the system and network conditions. The Runtime Monitoring Module monitors the behavior of the validators to identify anomalies and the Difficulty Controller and Dataset-Driven Tuning provides dynamic tuning of consensus difficulty based on real-time data providing the best performance and security. In the previous part of the system, a digital signature of every block chain transaction is executed with the NTRU post quantum cryptographic system. Upon signing, the transaction is then broadcasted to all active validator nodes running in the setting of virtualized or container-based cloud. The transactions received in each node are archived in a local mempool, which acts as a FIFO (first-in, first-out) buffer to operationalize pending transaction details. The proposed Lightweight Hash Validation (LHV) mechanism attains O(1) validation in practice by linking each block to its prior block using a hash pointer, rather than traversing full Merkle Trees. The validator simply computes a hash of the previous block’s metadata, timestamp, and the digest all NTRU-signed transactions, as specified in Eq. (9). The individual transactions in the block are separately verified via the NTRU signature scheme, which can be computed efficiently via polynomial operations with constant or approximately constant complexity for common transaction sizes in cloud-hosted systems. To support the practical O(1) claims, an analysis was performed empirically in an environment simulating cloud elements. Validation times were effectively constant between block sizes of 50 to 500 transactions, with an average of less than 5 milliseconds in high-frequency validation latency per block based upon a typical virtualized node. The integration of cryptographic digest linking and independent transaction NTRU signature verification both validate the integrity of blocks without needing an iterative traversal or recursive traversal, maintaining the claim of constant validation, and assuring post-quantum security.

The suggested blockchain system will reach post-quantum security by combining the NTRU signature scheme and adaptive PoET consensus. The digital signature scheme used, NTRU, is provably secure in Existential Unforgeability under Chosen Message Attack (EUF-CMA), where lattice problems, including the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP) and Learning With Errors (LWE) are hard enough that even quantum attackers cannot solve them. The authenticity, integrity, and forward secrecy are guaranteed in a post-quantum environment with each transaction signed with NTRU and verified by all the validator nodes. In the adaptive PoET consensus, security is enhanced with the aid of TEE producing cryptographically attested random timers. This ensures that only node identities that are verifiable propose the blocks, eliminating threats of Sybil attacks, replay attacks and malicious use of timer. The adaptive PoET dynamically tunes waiting times by adding runtime metrics like network latency, mempool size, and node responsiveness to ensure that leaders are chosen fairly and that consensus is achieved uniformly. Though consensus integrity is not provable in a trivial way, a combination of TEE attestation, adaptive timer regulation, and dataset-based tuning offers a well-constructed mechanism of ensuring ledger integrity in a post-quantum blockchain in the cloud, which cannot be tampered with easily.

The suggested structure uses the Intel SGX to impose trusted execution of the Adaptive PoET timer; the design is not based on perfect enclave security. SGX has been known to be vulnerable to various types of attacks, such as, but not limited to, cache-based side channels, page-fault probing, branch shadowing, and LVI/Foreshadow-type speculative execution attacks, which can reveal confidential information or support partial information leakage. In response to such fears, it is seen that the current work has not based on the secrecy of enclave-resident data or on the secrecy of enclave-controlled wait-time execution but instead has based itself on the attested integrity of the wait-time execution based on the enclave-resident data, thus decreasing the attack-surface when compared to SGX-dependent cryptographic key storage or secure state machine. Moreover, the chain of trust of remote attestation is recognized to have a clear point of failure and therefore consensus correctness in the suggested system does not depend on a central attestation service but merely that all the involved nodes provide mutually consistent measurement identities. Although SGX is the reference TEE to be evaluated experimentally, the framework will be compatible with upcoming options, including Intel TDX and AMD SEV-SNP as well as ARM Confidential Compute Architecture. A deeper discussion on TEE vulnerabilities and migration plans is also provided to make sure that the use of SGX does not make unrealistic deployment assumptions.

The proposed research is distinguished by its synergetic approach to integrating three advanced elements in order to build a blockchain system that is, not only scalable and cloud-friendly, but also quantum-resistant, such as NTRU-based post-quantum cryptography, adaptive PoET consensus, and LHV. In contrast to conventional blockchain frameworks, which use RSA or ECDSA, and can hence be broken by a quantum computer, the NTRU algorithm maintains a lattice-based security guarantee, which gives efficient and safe digital marks without a lot of computational burden. Meanwhile, the adaptive PoET mechanism adds its contribution to the efficiency of the consensus by taking into account real-time network parameters, including latency, the responsiveness of nodes, and transactional volumes. This dynamic leader selection timer positioning, enabled by TEEs, can achieve energy-efficient and fair leader selection, and tamper-resistance. The use of LHV, a new alternative to computationally costly Merkle Trees, is another distinguishing feature of this framework. A validation with LHV allows verifying a block in constant time with small hash pointers, which minimizes the memory requirements and validation latency. The architecture is a three-layered structure that is ideally applicable in the cloud-hosted blockchain networks where the performance, scalability, and security are to co-exist. The proposed model is a forward compatible, lightweight, and tamper-proof ledger infrastructure to support next-generation decentralized applications against the rising quantum and computational challenges.

The comparative evaluation of the discussed consensus mechanisms shows that the specified schemes respond to a particular security concern and possess varying vulnerabilities. Strong resistance to Sybil attacks is offered by PoW which offers probabilistic finality based on computational difficulty, but also allows 51% hash-power attacks, selfish mining and energy-exhaustion exploits. PoS prevents excessive energy use, centralization based on hardware but it creates stake-related weaknesses: Nothing-at-Stake, long-range attacks, and the formation of validator cartels. PoA is also fast with finality and minimal computational cost, and is based on trusted validators, implying that it is vulnerable to identity compromise, censorship, as well as insider collusion. The PoET solves the problem of energy waste by utilizing TEE-based secure randomness generation, making the mining centralization harder to achieve; PoET, like its TEE-based counterpart, is also vulnerable to the security dependency of TEEs, as well as to attacks stemming from enclave compromise or counterfeit attestation certificates. All these constraints drive the development of a hybrid design that will integrate the following components to achieve superior security and provide scalability in clouds at the same time: PQ-secure signature, adaptive TEE controlled wait times, and lightweight validation.

PoET ensures fairness and Sybil resistance by employing TEE-enabled random wait times and remote attestation. PoW creates security on the chain by making attacks computationally expensive, thus resisting Sybil and majority attacks. PoS addresses Sybil attacks through stake-based identities and slashing, and mitigates long-range attacks through checkpointing. PoA prevents Sybil attacks by relying on known, verified validators to provide accountability in semi-trusted networks. The achieved latency and throughput increased features of the proposed PQ-PoETChain are the result of underlying architectural benefits and not the simple numerical ones. In contrast to PoW is expensive and requires per-block hash competition, and PoS is reliant on deterministic stake assessment, Adaptive PoET reduces block-generation delay with dynamically adjusted and dynamically changing wait time for the validators based on real-time network metrics that include mempool occupancy, TPS and CPU load. This adaptive timer helps in avoiding a congestion time in which block-creation time is inflated. More so, the random wait created by TEE removes computational races, which means that the system can scale to large node sets without contention. Besides, the LHV module, instead of O(n) block verification, the module offers O(1) block verification, which does not re-compute Merkle structures iteratively, and cuts down the processing overhead per block. Lastly, NTRU signatures are associated with less cryptographic verification cost compared to ECDSA that does not require scalar multiplication and it also multi-transactions block validation faster. All these architectural optimizations are the reason why latency is only 189 ms with 500 nodes, and the PoS (460 ms) and the PoW (950 ms) architecture severely worsen with the same workloads.

Adaptive PoET is superior to PoW, PoS, and PoA because of the adaptive waiting time mechanism that utilizes a node’s real-time state and transition data to select an ideal leader. Discussion

The suggested PQ-PoETChain system manifests important developments compared to the conventional blockchain constructions with a mixture of post-quantum NTRU cryptography, adaptive Proof of Elapsed Time derivation, and Lightweight Hash Validation. The signature generation and verification is faster in NTRU compared to RSA, DSA and ECC at a rate of 1.82 ms and 1.27 ms respectively, and is very secure even to quantum attacks in comparison, which is proved by the 256-bit NIST-equivalent security rating of the scheme. The key sizes in RSA and ECC, on the other hand, are vulnerable and do not withstand quantum attacks, which are associated with increased computational costs. Adaptive PoET mechanism facilitates high performance in terms of transactions throughput, lesser latency by changing consensus delays according to the network load. Simulations indicates that PQ-PoETChain scales to ~ 195 TPS with less than 200 ms of latency at 500 nodes, surpassing the PoW and PoS models, which experience a decline in throughput and latency in excess of 500 ms at comparable scales.

Also, the amount of energy used is sharply decreased as the proposed approach requires only 1.43 Wh of energy per node instead of 17.6 Wh of PoW one. This behavior is further improved as it allows O(1), block validation, giving further space and time advantage in the system, otherwise incurred with Merkle trees. The efficiency results in quicker block finality (~ 2.27 s at 500 nodes) and efficiency in resource usage, verifying the aptness of the framework to be utilized by cloud-hosted and large-scale networks. In spite of these, PQ-PoETChain assumes the availability of TEE at the moment, which can restrict the implementation on resource-limited IoT settings. Also, there is yet to be any formal security proofs against side-channel attacks on TEEs to extend.

Conclusion and future scope

The proposed study presents PQ-PoETChain, a new and quantum-resistant blockchain design to handle the rising security risks of commodity quantum computers and scale the shortcomings of blockchain consensus and validation processes. The combination of NTRU-based post-quantum cryptography, adaptive Proof of Elapsed Time consensus protocol that is based on the TEEs and the Lightweight Hash Validation scheme offers a highly scalable, secure and energy-efficient means of running secure transactions within a distributed cloud computing setting. Simulated experiments done on a variety of networks (with 50 to 500 nodes) indicate that PQ-PoETChain supports high throughput (up to 195 TPS), low latency (< 200 ms), and block finality 2.27 s after the block is published, which is far better than PoW, PoS, and PoA in terms of performance and energy use. The NTRU cryptosystem is extremely resistant to quantum computers and yet has signature operations under 2 ms, faster than the counterparts RSA, ECC and DSA and more resistant to quantum attack. LHV model also optimizes verification by lowering the complexity of validation to O (1), thereby lowing the cryptographic overhead found in standard blockchain systems. Although the presented frame provides great benefits, it depends on the existence and soundness of TEEs to carry out safe consensus activities. Moreover, side-channel vulnerabilities of some TEE implementations provide a place of future research.

In this work, PQ-PoETChain which combines NTRU-based post-quantum signatures, Adaptive PoET and Lightweight Hash Validation has been presented to improve the security and performance of blockchain. Although the findings are positive in terms of latency, throughput and energy efficiency, the findings should be construed within a number of limitations. To start with, the integrity and availability of trusted hardware (Intel SGX) is critical to the consensus mechanism, thus leaving the system susceptible to enclave attacks, side-channel attacks, and attestation-chain manipulation. Second, the test was performed in controlled cloud environments instead of permissionless, adversarial-scale public networks and could not be used to draw conclusions about scalability and adversarial resilience in the real world. Third, the given architecture is not yet production ready as it does not have formal security proofs of fairness, liveness and quantum-adversarial robustness.

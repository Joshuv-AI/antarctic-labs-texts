A substitution lemma for multiple context-free languages
Andrew Duncan, Murray Elder, Lisa Frenkel, Mengfan Lyu

Abstract
. We present a new criterion for proving that a language is not multiple context-free, which we call a Substitution Lemma . We apply it to show a sample selection of languages are not multiple context-free, including the word problem of F 2 × F 2 F_{2}\times F_{2} . Our result is in contrast to Kanazawa et al. [2014, Theory Comput. Syst.] who proved that it was not possible to generalise the standard pumping lemma for context-free languages to multiple context-free languages, and Kanazawa [2019, Inform. and Comput.] who showed a weak variant of generalised Ogden’s lemma does not apply to multiple context-free languages. We also record that groups with multiple context-free word problem have decidable rational subset membership problem.

2.1.
Multiple context-free grammars

See
[
25
,
34
]
for more precise
definitions; for the purposes of this paper we rely on an equivalent definition
in terms of tree stack automata given in Subsection
2.3
below, so the grammar definition we present here
is somewhat informal.

Fix an alphabet
N
of
non-terminals
and an alphabet
\Sigma
of
terminals
.
In a context-free grammar
G_{0}=(N,\Sigma,R,S)
, rules
in
R
are of the form

A\to w_{1}B_{1}w_{2}B_{2}\dots w_{n}B_{n}w_{n+1}

where
A,B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\in N
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{n+1}\in\Sigma^{*}
, and
S\in N
is a distinguished
start
non-terminal.
We could think of rules in another way:

A(s_{1})\leftarrow B_{1}({x_{1}}),B_{2}({x_{2}}),\dots,B_{n}({x_{n}})

where
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
are variables,

s_{1}=w_{1}{x_{1}}w_{2}{x_{2}}\dots w_{n}{x_{n}}w_{n+1}
,
t_{1}\in\Sigma^{*}
,
and non-terminals are regarded as functions, with a single variable, with the rule taking what has already been produced by each
B_{i}
,
i\in[1,n]
(a word
u_{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
in place of the variable
x_{i}
, which we can call the
value
of
B_{i}
) and inserting these into the string
s_{1}
, with the resulting word becoming the value of the variable for
A
. Also, rules of the form
A\rightarrow t
, where
t\in\Sigma^{*}
take the form

A(t)\leftarrow

and are used to initialise non-terminals.
The language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
of a context-free grammar defined in this way
is the set of all strings
w\in\Sigma^{*}
that can be obtained by a sequence of rules finishing with the non-terminal
S(w)

(this is tantamount to working
from the leaves of the derivation tree to the root, as opposed the usual process of beginning with the start non-terminal and working towards the leaves.)

In a multiple context-free grammar
G=(N,\Sigma,R,S)
, each non-terminal in
N
is allowed to have more than one variable, rules are of the form:

A(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\leftarrow B_{1}({x_{1}^{1}},\dots,{x_{k_{1}}^{1}}),B_{2}({x_{1}^{2}},\dots,{x_{k_{2}}^{2}}),\dots,B_{n}({x_{1}^{n}},\dots,{x_{k_{n}}^{n}})

and

A(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\leftarrow

where
s_{1},\dots,s_{m}
are strings of terminals and
variables
x_{j}^{i}
, where the
x_{j}^{i}
are distinct and can only be used at most once in
s_{1}\cdots s_{m}
(the concatenation of all the
s_{i}
),
t_{i}
are strings of terminals only (so that the grammar is
initialised
with words in place of variables by rules of the form
A(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\leftarrow
), and
S\in N
is a distinguished
start
non-terminal which has rank 1 (and is
applied last). A non-terminal
N(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
with
m
variables is said to have
rank

m
.

We call a multiple context-free grammar
k
-multiple context-free
, abbreviated as
k
-MCF, if each non-terminal has rank at most
k
. By this construction, any context-free grammar is
1
-MCF.
The language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
of a multiple context-free grammar is the set of all strings
w\in\Sigma^{*}
that can be obtained by a sequence of rules finishing with the non-terminal
S(w)
.
A language
L
of a
k
-multiple context-free grammar
G=(N,\Sigma,R,S)
is called a
k
-multiple context-free language, and
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is called multiple context-free if it is
k
-multiple context-free for some integer
k\geqslant 1
.

Example 2.1
.

The language
\{a^{m}b^{m}c^{m}d^{m}\mid m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is 2-multiple context-free, produced by the grammar

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)&\leftarrow&\\
T(a{x_{1}}b,c{x_{2}}d)&\leftarrow&T({x_{1}},{x_{2}})\\
S({x_{1}}{x_{2}})&\leftarrow&T({x_{1}},{x_{2}})\\
\end{array}

Example 2.2
.

The language
\{a^{n}b^{m}c^{n}d^{m}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is 2-multiple context-free, produced by the grammar

\begin{array}[]{rcl}P(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)&\leftarrow&\\
Q(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)&\leftarrow&\\
P(a{x_{1}},c{x_{2}})&\leftarrow&P({x_{1}},{x_{2}})\\
Q(b{x_{1}},d{x_{2}})&\leftarrow&Q({x_{1}},{x_{2}})\\
S({x_{1}}{y_{1}}{x_{2}}{y_{2}})&\leftarrow&P({x_{1}},{x_{2}}),Q({y_{1}},{y_{2}})\\
\end{array}

Proposition 2.3

(See for example
[
35
]
)
.

If
\Sigma,\Gamma
are alphabets,
L,L_{1}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
are
k
-multiple context-free and
R\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is a regular language then

(i)

L\cup L_{1}
is
k
-multiple context-free;

(ii)

L\cap R
is
k
-multiple context-free;

(iii)

\psi(L),\tau^{-1}(L)
are

.

2.2.
Semi-linear sets

An important concept in formal language theory is that of semi-linear sets. See for example
[
3
,
4
,
15
]
for more details.
For
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
a set

A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
is called
linear
if there exist elements (vectors)
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
so that

A=\{v_{0}+\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{m}v_{m}\mid\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

and
A
is called
semi-linear
if it is equal to a finite union of linear subsets of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
.
If
\Sigma=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
then the map
\psi\colon\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
defined by
\psi(w)=(|w|_{a_{1}},\dots,|w|_{a_{d}})
is called the
Parikh map
. Parikh showed that if
L
is context-free then
\psi(L)
is semi-linear.
Vijay-Shanker, Weir, and Joshi
[
37
]
showed that if
L
is multiple context-free then
\psi(L)
is semi-linear. We list in the next lemma further facts about semi-linear sets.

Lemma 2.4

(See for example
[
15
]
)
.

The class of semi-linear sets is closed under union, intersection, and complement. Moreover,

(1)

if

L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
and
\psi(L)
is semi-linear then there exists a regular language
R\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
so that
\psi(L)=\psi(R)
;

(2)

if
L
has semi-linear Parikh image and
R
is a regular language then
L\cap R
has semi-linear Parikh image.

We also observe the following (
c.f.
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
).

Lemma 2.5

(Gap lemma for preimages of semi-linear sets)
.

If
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is an infinite language
such that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|w_{1}|-|w_{2}|>m
for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in L
with
|w_{1}|>|w_{2}|>N_{m}
,
then the Parikh image of
L
is not semi-linear.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4

there exists a regular language
R
with
\psi(R)=\psi(L)
.
Fix a deterministic finite state automaton (DFA) accepting
R
and let
p
be the number of states of this DFA.
Take
m>p
, and let
N_{m}
be as in the hypothesis.
Since
L
is infinite,
R
is infinite so we can find a word
u_{1}\in R
with
|u_{1}|>N_{m}+m+p
, and by the pumping lemma for regular languages
u_{1}=xyz
where
|y|\in[1,p]

and
u_{2}=xz\in R
. However,
\psi(u_{1})
and
\psi(u_{2})
have preimages
w_{1},w_{2}
in
L
with
|u_{i}|=|w_{i}|
for
i=1,2
. Thus
|w_{1}|>|w_{2}|>N_{m}+m
, but
|w_{1}|-|w_{2}|\leqslant p
, which is a contradiction.
∎

It follows that languages such as

–

L_{1}:=\{a^{2^{n}}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

–

L_{2}:=\{a^{n^{2}}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

–

L_{3}(\alpha):=\{a^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\},\alpha>1
,

–

L_{4}:=\{a^{\lfloor n\log n\rfloor}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}

do not have semi-linear Parikh image, and therefore are not multiple context-free.
More generally we have the following.

Corollary 2.5.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function such that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
so that
|f(n_{1})-f(n_{2})|>m
for all
n_{1}>n_{2}>n
.
Then the language
L:=\{a^{f(n)}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}

does not have semi-linear Parikh image, and thus is not multiple context-free.

Denkinger

Definition 2.6

(Tree with labels in

)

Let

Let

Note that

Definition 2.9

(Tree stack automata)
.

A

is a tuple

\mathcal{A}=(Q,C,\Sigma,q_{0},\delta,Q_{f})

where

\delta=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{|\delta|}\}

is a finite set of

of the form
\sigma_{i}=(q,x,p,f,q^{\prime})
where

(1)

and

\sigma_{i}
,

(2)

is the

\sigma_{i}
,

(3)

p
is the predicate of
\sigma_{i}
(i.e. either

, or
\operatorname{true}
),

(4)

is the

of
\sigma_{i}
, so is one of the functions
\operatorname{id}
,
\operatorname{push}_{n}(c)
,
\operatorname{up}_{n}
,
\operatorname{down}

Note that each coordinate of

The tree stack automaton operates by starting in state
q_{0}
with a tree stack initialised at
(\{(\varepsilon,@)\},\varepsilon)
,
reading each letter of an input word

with arbitrarily many
\varepsilon

a well defined function which consumes input letters whilst moving between tree stacks.
An allowed sequence of transitions
\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{r}\in\delta^{*}
is called a

A run is
valid

if it
finishes in a state in
Q_{f}

is the input word consumed during a valid run we say that
\mathcal{A}

accepts

(we do not necessarily put a requirement on the final position of the pointer, but see Remark
3.1
).
Define the language

to be the set of all words

accepted by
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 2.11

Remark 2.12

Without loss of generality

Definition 2.13

(Below/above)

In a rooted tree

4.
Substitution Lemma

In this section, we state and prove the Substitution Lemma for infinite multiple context-free languages.
We assume further all tree stack automata have at least one push command and therefore have positive degree.
We shall use the following lemma. In the statement of the lemma,
a word
u\in\Sigma^{+}

is said to be

read by a run of a TSA while the pointer is at a vertex
\nu
of a tree stack

if each letter of
u
is read by a transition
which does not move the pointer and the pointer is at
\nu
before and after
applying each transition.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
L
be an infinite
k
-MCF language accepted by
\mathcal{A}
and let
D
be the degree of
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{R}

be a proper run accepting a word
w
,

T
be the corresponding final tree  and
\nu
be a vertex of
T
.
Let

\mu\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
assume that the following strong condition holds:

•

if
w^{\prime}
is a factor of
w
such that the pointer remains at
\nu
while
\mathcal{R}
is reading
w^{\prime}
, then
|w^{\prime}|\leqslant\mu|C||Q|
.

Then
the number
n_{w,\nu}
of letters of
w
read by
\mathcal{R}
while the pointer is at
\nu
is bounded by

(1)

n_{w,\nu}\leqslant\mu k|C||Q|(D+1)
if
\nu
is a non-root, non-leaf vertex of
T
;

(2)

n_{w,\nu}\leqslant\mu kD|Q|
if
\nu=\varepsilon
is the root vertex of
T
;

(3)

n_{w,\nu}\leqslant\mu k|C||Q|
if
\nu
is a leaf of
T
.

Proof.

From the definitions,
\nu
has at most
D
children and (at most) one parent. As
\mathcal{A}
is
k
-restricted
the number of transitions of
\mathcal{R}
which move the pointer to
\nu
from below (from its parent) is at most
k
; and the
number of transitions that move the pointer to
\nu
from one of its children is at most
kD
. Let
r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}
be the maximal subsequences
of the
sequence of transitions
\mathcal{R}
such that the pointer is kept at
\nu
while executing subsequence
r_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,t
.
Then
t\leqslant k(D+1)
.
By assumption and the fact that
\mathcal{R}
is proper, none of these subsequences
r_{i}
has length greater than
\mu|C||Q|
.
Therefore the number of letters of
w
read by transitions of
\mathcal{R}
with the pointer at
\nu
is at most
\mu k|C||Q|(D+1)
. Note also that the root has no parent and always has label
@
so if
\nu=\varepsilon
then this bound is
\mu kD|Q|
. Furthermore,
if
\nu
is a leaf of
T
, so has no children, then the bound is

\mu k|C||Q|
.
∎

Definition 4.2

(pumpable words)
.

Let
L
be a language over an alphabet
\Sigma
and let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
with
\alpha\leqslant\beta
.
A word
w\in L
is called
(\alpha,\beta)
-pumpable
if there exists
x,y,z\in\Sigma^{*}
with
|y|\in[\alpha,\beta]

such that
w=xyz
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
xy^{n}z\in L
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
L
be an infinite
k
-MCF language accepted by
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{R}

be a proper run accepting a word
w
,
let
T
be the corresponding final tree  and let
\nu
be a vertex of
T
.
Assume that, for some
m\geqslant 1
, there is a sequence
\hat{r}
of more than
m|C||Q|
consecutive transitions of
\mathcal{R}
,
during which the pointer remains at
\nu
. Then

is
(m,m|C||Q|)
-pumpable.

Proof.

We may assume, by considering a prefix if necessary, that
\hat{r}
consists of precisely

\hat{r}=\tau_{a}\cdots\tau_{a+p-1}
. Then there are integers

a\leqslant j_{1}<\cdots<j_{m+1}\leqslant a+p-1
and
(c,q)\in C\times Q

such
that, for all
i\in[1,m+1]
, after reading
\tau_{j_{i}}

\nu
is
c
and the state of
\mathcal{A}
is
q
.
Let
y
be the factor of

\tau_{j_{1}+1}\tau_{j_{2}}\cdots\tau_{j_{m+1}}
.
It follows that
w=w_{0}yw_{1}
, for

and that
w_{0}y^{n}w_{1}\in L
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Moreover, as
\mathcal{R}
is proper,
m\leqslant|y|\leqslant p-1
.
∎

The next definition allows us to refer to the

Definition 4.4

(
U
-switchable factorisation)
.

Let

be a language over an alphabet
\Sigma

(1)

A

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s})\in U
.

(2)

A word

.

-factorisation of

U
-switchable
factorisation.

Given a word
w
over an alphabet
\Sigma
we may distinguish, by marking, some of the letters of

.

Theorem 4.5

(Substitution Lemma)
.

Let

be an infinite

-MCF language over an alphabet
\Sigma
.
Given

is

-pumpable

(b)

there exists

, such that

is infinite, and

U_{i}
-switchable factorisation

.

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{c}\\

.

. For

(

(

be a proper accepting run

5.1.
The word problem of
F_{2}\times F_{2}

Recall that the word problem for a group
G
with generating set
X
, denoted
\operatorname{WP}(G,X)
, is the set of words in
\left(X\cup X^{-1}\right)^{*}
which equal the identity element of the group (where
X^{-1}=\{x^{-1}\mid x\in X\}
contains the inverse element for each
x\in X
).
Since multiple context-free languages are closed under inverse homomorphism (Proposition
2.3
(iii)), having multiple context-free word problem is independent of choice of finite generating set.
Salvati’s result
shows that
that the word problem of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is 2-multiple context-free (see also
[
31
,
5
]
).
Ho
[
16
]
extended this to show that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
k
-multiple context-free for some
k>n
, and later Gebhardt
et al.

[
11
]
sharpened this by showing that in fact the word problem of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
n
-multiple context-free.
Since having a multiple context-free word problem is closed under finite index supergroup
[
12
]
,
it follows that all finitely generated virtually abelian groups have multiple context-free word problem, and since context-free languages are multiple context-free,
virtually free groups have multiple context-free word problem by Muller-Schupp
[
30
]
.
In addition, Kropholler and Spriano
[
25
]
show that having multiple context-free word problem is closed under taking free products.
Gilman, Kropholler and Schleimer
[
14
]
proved that
nilpotent groups and some right-angled Artin groups including
F_{2}\times F_{2}
(where
F_{2}
is the free group of rank
2
)
and
\mathbb{G}(P_{4})
the right-angled Artin group with commutation graph
P_{4}
(the tree with
2
leaves and
2
vertices of degree
2
)
do not have multiple context-free word problem by showing that the word problem intersected with some regular language over the generators yields a language with Parikh image that is not semi-linear (this contradicts that the word problem is multiple context-free since multiple context-free languages are closed under intersection with regular languages, and have semi-linear Parikh image). Thus having a multiple context-free word problem is not closed under direct product.
It is unknown if
F_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}
has multiple context-free word problem.

Here
we give a short alternative proof that the word problem for
F_{2}\times F_{2}
is not multiple context-free via the Substitution Lemma (Theorem
4.5
).
Later, Section
6
, we briefly consider decision problems for rational subsets of groups with multiple context-free word problem, which yields
even shorter proofs that the word problems of
F_{2}\times F_{2}
and
\mathbb{G}(P_{4})
are not multiple context-free.

y_{n}=p_{1},\ x_{n}=q_{1},y_{n-1}=p_{2},\ x_{n-1}=q_{2},\dots,y_{1}=p_{t},x_{1}=q_{t}\textrm{ and }n=t.

From the second factor we have

c^{x_{1}}d^{y_{1}}\cdots c^{x_{n}}d^{y_{n}}\left(d^{-1}\right)^{q_{1}}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{p_{2}}\left(d^{-1}\right)^{q_{2}}\cdots\left(c^{-1}\right)^{p_{t}}\left(d^{-1}\right)^{q_{t}}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{p_{t+1}}=_{C}1,

and, since
n=t
, we can write this as

c^{x_{1}}d^{y_{1}}\cdots c^{x_{n}}d^{y_{n}}\left(d^{-1}\right)^{q_{1}}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{p_{2}}\left(d^{-1}\right)^{q_{2}}\cdots\left(c^{-1}\right)^{p_{n}}\left(d^{-1}\right)^{q_{n}}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{p_{n+1}}=_{C}1,

which implies

(5.2)

y_{n}=q_{1},x_{n}=p_{2},y_{n-1}=q_{2},x_{n-1}=p_{3},\dots,y_{1}=q_{n},x_{1}=p_{n+1}.

Putting Equations (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) together we get

p_{1}=y_{n}=q_{1}=x_{n}=p_{2}=y_{n-1}=q_{2}=x_{n-1}=\dots=p_{n}=y_{1}=q_{n}=x_{1}=p_{n+1}

and so all powers in
w
must be identical. Setting
x_{i}=y_{i}=p_{i}=q_{i}=m
we obtain

w=((ca)^{m}(db)^{m})^{n}(b^{-1})^{m}\left((d^{-1}a^{-1})^{m}(c^{-1}b^{-1})^{m}\right)^{n-1}(d^{-1}a^{-1})^{m}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{m}.

It follows that

T\cap L\subseteq\{((ca)^{m}(db)^{m})^{n}(b^{-1})^{m}\left((d^{-1}a^{-1})^{m}(c^{-1}b^{-1})^{m}\right)^{n-1}(d^{-1}a^{-1})^{m}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{m}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}.

Conversely since every word of the form

((ca)^{m}(db)^{m})^{n}(b^{-1})^{m}\left((d^{-1}a^{-1})^{m}(c^{-1}b^{-1})^{m}\right)^{n-1}(d^{-1}a^{-1})^{m}\left(c^{-1}\right)^{m}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
evaluates to the identity in
F_{2}\times F_{2}
, and has the form of words in
T
, the above set inclusion is equality.
Applying the erasing homomorphism

defined by

for all
x\in\{c,c^{-1},d,d^{-1},a^{-1},b^{-1}\}
we obtain

\psi\left(T\cap L\right)=\{(a^{m}b^{m})^{n}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}

which is not multiple context-free by Lemma
5.2
(itself a direct consequence of Theorem
4.5
(
3

∎

5.2.1.
Substitution lemma for context-free languages

Since

Recall Ogden’s lemma.

Lemma 5.5

(Ogden’s lemma

]
)
.

Let

such that, for all

of

(2)

and

(3)

for all

Proof that Theorem
4.5

for
1
-multiple context-free languages follows from Lemma

Let

be an infinite context-free language and let

Given

define sets

and

, and note that this is a finite collection of infinite sets.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
let

so, from Ogden’s lemma,

|uv|_{\#}\leqslant P
.
If

let

be factors of

such that

is a suffix of

b_{0}
is a prefix of
b
and

is a prefix of

Thus

implies that

Item (3) follows from the previous paragraph after replacing

6.
Rational subsets of groups with multiple context-free word problem

The following
gives yet another
way to obtain Proposition
5.4
.

Given a monoid
M
generated by
\Sigma
, with generating map
\pi:\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow M
, a subset
R\subseteq M
is said to be
rational
if

R=\pi(L)
for some regular language
L
in
\Sigma^{*}
.
(The subset
R
is said to be
recognisable
if
\pi^{-1}(R)
is a regular language.)
The
rational subset membership problem
for
M
is to decide, given a
finite state automaton (FSA) over
\Sigma
accepting a
language

L
and a word
w\in\Sigma^{*}
, whether or not
\pi(w)\in\pi(L)
.
The
rational subset intersection (resp. equality) problem
for
M
is to decide, given two FSAs over
\Sigma
accepting regular languages

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
in
\Sigma^{*}
, whether or not
\pi(L_{1})\cap\pi(L_{2})=\emptyset
(resp.
\pi(L_{1})=\pi(L_{2})
).
The
regular intersection
decision problem for a language
L
is to decide, given one FSA over
\Sigma
accepting a regular language
L_{1}
, whether
L\cap L_{1}
is non-empty (see
[
20
, Section 3]
).

It is straightforward to see that, in the setting of finitely generated groups,
the
rational subsets intersection problem is Turing equivalent to the rational subset membership problem: given two FSAs over
\Sigma
accepting

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
in
\Sigma^{*}
,

\pi(L_{1})\cap\pi(L_{2})
is non-empty if and only if
1\in\pi(L_{1}L_{2}^{-1})
(which is a rational subset since
L_{1}L_{2}^{-1}
is regular);
conversely, given an FSA over
\Sigma
accepting
L
and a word

x\in\Sigma^{*}
, the singleton set
\{\pi(x)\}
is rational and

\{\pi(x)\}\cap A
is non-empty if and only if
\pi(x)\in\pi(L)
.
By

[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
, a finitely generated group
G
has decidable rational subset membership problem if and only if
WP(G)
has decidable regular intersection problem.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group.
If
WP(G)
is multiple context free then the rational subset membership is
decidable in

(and hence the rational subset intersection problem is decidable,
and

Proof.

Assume
\Sigma

.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a FSA accepting a language
L(\mathcal{B})
, and let
R=\pi(L(\mathcal{B}))
.
As
\Sigma
is inverse closed, if

represents

w^{-1}\in\Sigma^{*}
and
\pi(w^{-1})=g^{-1}
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\mathcal{B}
has a unique accept state

Attach a path labelled by

from

to a new state

\mathcal{B}_{w}
be the resulting FSA, with single accept state

, whence

.
It follows that

if and only if

has the form
vw^{-1}
, where

and

.
Hence

Then the following algorithm decides membership in

build the FSA

(2)

for

This is possible since the TSA (or grammar) for the intersection of a multiple context

(3)

check whether or not

[

(see also
[

∎

(2)

(5)

(6)

.

The PDA operates as follows. On input a word
w\in\Sigma^{*}
, the PDA starts in state
q_{0}
with the stack containing the symbol
@
. The stack is arranged vertically, with the symbol
@
at the bottom.
If the next letter of
w
is
x\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
, the current state is
q_{1}
and the top of the stack is
z\in\Gamma
, the PDA may perform the transition
(q_{1},x,\operatorname{push}(z,s),q_{2})
moving to state
q_{2}
, adding
s
to the top of the stack if
s\neq\varepsilon
, and moving to read the next letter of
w
if
x\neq\varepsilon
.
If the next letter of
w
is
x\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
, the current state is
q_{1}
and the top of the stack is
t\in\Gamma\setminus\{@\}
, the PDA may perform the transition
(q_{1},x,\operatorname{pop}(t),q_{2})
moving to state
q_{2}
, removing
t
from the top of the stack, and moving to read the next letter of
w
if
x\neq\varepsilon
.
The PDA accepts
w
if it is possible to perform a sequence of transitions which reads every letter of
w
and leaves the PDA in a state in
Q_{f}
. There is no requirement for the stack to be returned to empty.

The language of the PDA is the set of all words that can be accepted.
Note that the PDA never pops the bottom of stack symbol.

By definition a 1-TSA is a TSA with no transition containing an
\operatorname{up}
partial-function.

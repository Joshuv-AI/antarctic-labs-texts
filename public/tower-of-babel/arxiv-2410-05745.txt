Hamiltonian structure of single-helicity, incompressible magnetohydrodynamics and application to magnetorotational instability
M. Furukawa, M. Hirota

Abstract
A four-field reduced model of single helicity, incompressible MHD is derived in cylindrical geometry. An appropriate set of noncanonical variables is found, and the Hamiltonian, the Lie–Poisson bracket and the Casimir invariants are clarified. Detailed proofs of properties of the Lie–Poisson bracket, (i) antisymmetry, (ii) Leibniz rule, and (iii) Jacobi identity, are given. Two applications are presented: the first is that the local dispersion relation of axisymmetric magnetohydrodynamics (MRI) is properly reproduced, and the second is that linear stability analyses including negative-energy MRI were successfully performed.



I
Introduction

Ideal fluid and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) systems have
noncanonical Hamiltonian structure.
The dynamics is described by a Hamiltonian and a Poisson bracket.
Because the dynamical variables are noncanonical, the Poisson bracket
has a null space that corresponds to Casimir invariants.
Many aspects of the system such as equilibrium and stability can be
understood in the context of dynamical systems.

The Hamiltonian structure of the ideal fluid and MHD in
three-dimensional space was found in
Ref.
Morrison and Greene 1980
.
Also for reduced MHD systems,

Strauss 1976
;
Strauss 1977

the Hamiltonian structure was clarified in
Ref.
Morrison and Hazeltine 1984
.
Reviews of the Hamiltonian description of a variety of systems can be
found, for example, in Refs.
Holm et al. 1985
;
Morrison 1998
.

Such reduced models have played essential roles
in studies of magnetically confined fusion plasmas.
The reduction relies basically on an assumption that the aspect ratio is
large or the wave length parallel to magnetic field is
much larger than that perpendicular to the magnetic field.
The reduced MHD models are often valid under strong ambient magnetic field.

An accretion disk, however, can be a different situation, where
a magnetorotational instability (MRI) can occur under presence of
a weak ambient magnetic field

Velikhov 1959
;
S. Chandrasekhar 1960
;
Balbus and Hawley 1991
;
Balbus and Hawley 1998
.
The simplest situation for the MRI to occur is
that a homogeneous magnetic field is in the
z
direction of the
cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
, and the plasma rotates in the

\theta
direction. Naively, this situation may be realized by using
the low-beta reduced MHD in cylindrical geometry, leaving the
assumption of strong ambient magnetic field made in the derivation
aside.
However, MRI cannot be expressed by the low-beta reduced
MHD model because it only retains the shear Alfvén wave.
MRI needs a coupling between the shear Alfvén wave and the slow
magnetosonic wave.

In the present paper, we derive
a four-field reduced MHD model that is a minimum model to MRI,
and elucidate its Hamiltonian structure.
The model is derived by taking appropriate components of
the ideal MHD equations under assumptions of
single helicity dynamics and incompressibility.
No assumption is made on the aspect ratio and plasma beta.
A choice of noncanonical variables is essential in clarifying
the Hamiltonian structure.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
The governing equations of the four-field reduced MHD model
under assumptions of single helicity and incompressibility
are derived in Sec.
II
.
Then, an appropriate set of noncanonical variables,
together with the Hamiltonian, the Lie–Poisson bracket, and the Casimir
invariants are found in
Sec.
III
.
Moreover, important properties of the Lie–Poisson bracket,
(i) antisymmetry, (ii) Leibniz rule, and (iii) Jacobi identity,
are proven.
In Sec.
IV
, two applications are presented.
The first one demonstrates that the derived model can successfully
reproduce the well-known local dispersion relation of axisymmetric MRI.
The other application shows linear stability analysis of MRI including
negative energy modes, which was also studied in
Refs.
Khalzov et al. 2006
;
Khalzov, Smolyakov, and Ilgisonis 2008
;
Ilgisonis, Khalzov, and Smolyakov 2009

by using the Frieman–Rotenberg equation
Frieman and Rotenberg 1960
.
Conclusions are given in Sec.
V
.
In Appendix
A
,
a formula for calculating perturbed energy of MRI is given.

II
Derivation of governing equations

Let us consider a cylindrical plasma with a minor radius
a
and
an axial length
2\pi R_{0}
.
If we consider single-helicity dynamics, a physical quantity
f
can be
expressed in a Fourier series as

f(r,\theta,z,t)=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell(M\theta+N\zeta)}

(1)

in cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
.
Here,
{\rm i}\,
is the imaginary unit,

\zeta:=z/R_{0}
is an angle coordinate in the axial direction,

\ell
is an integer,

M
and
N
are principal poloidal and toroidal mode numbers,
respectively.
We assume
N\neq 0
.

It is noted that the “single-helicity” means that the
dynamics includes a single family of Fourier modes expressed by

\ell M
and
\ell N
.
It is not the usual helicity that is defined by
{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla\times{\bm{u}}
for
a velocity field
{\bm{u}}
.

By introducing a set of coordinates
(r,\vartheta,\alpha)
via

\displaystyle\vartheta

\displaystyle:=\theta,

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle:=R_{0}\left(\frac{M}{N}\theta+\zeta\right)\quad=\frac{M}{K}\theta+z

(3)

with
K:=N/R_{0}
,
Eq. (
1
) can be expressed as

f(r,\alpha,t)=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha}.

(4)

Thus any physical quantity does not depend on
\vartheta

in the single-helicity dynamics.

Now, let us introduce an incompressible vector field

{\bm{h}}:=\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}(-Kr\hat{\bm{\theta}}+M\hat{\bm{z}})

(5)

with

K_{0}^{2}r^{2}:=M^{2}+K^{2}r^{2}=\frac{1}{|{\bm{h}}|^{2}},

(6)

where
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
and
\hat{\bm{z}}
are unit vectors in
\theta
and
z

directions, respectively.
Then, for an arbitrary function
f(r,\alpha,t)
, we have

{\bm{h}}\cdot\nabla f=0.

(7)

Using the vector field
{\bm{h}}
, an incompressible flow velocity field and
a magnetic field can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle={\bm{h}}\times\nabla\varphi(r,\alpha,t)+u_{h}(r,\alpha,t){\bm{h}},

(8)

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}

\displaystyle=\nabla\psi(r,\alpha,t)\times{\bm{h}}+B_{h}(r,\alpha,t){\bm{h}}.

(9)

As usual in reduced MHD models, let us introduce a Lie bracket
as

[f,g]:={\bm{h}}\cdot\nabla f\times\nabla g

(10)

for arbitrary functions
f(r,\alpha,t)
and
g(r,\alpha,t)
.
The Lie bracket is shown to satisfy the following three properties:

(i)

Antisymmetry:

[f,g]=-[g,f],

(11)

(ii)

Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[fg,h]

\displaystyle=g[f,h]+f[g,h],

(12)

\displaystyle[f,gh]

\displaystyle=g[f,h]+h[f,g],

(13)

(iii)

Jacobi identity:

[f,[g,h]]+[g,[h,f]]+[h,[f,g]]=0.

(14)

Here
h(r,\alpha,t)
is another arbitrary function.
Furthermore, the Lie bracket satisfies the following property

\int{\rm d}V\,f[g,h]=\int{\rm d}V\,h[f,g]=\int{\rm d}V\,g[h,f]

(15)

under an appropriate boundary condition,
where the volume integral is taken inside the plasma.
The appropriate boundary condition means that any boundary term that
arise from an integration by parts vanishes.

By using the Poisson bracket, the following relations are obtained
for an arbitrary physical quantity
f
:

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla f

\displaystyle=[\varphi,f],

(16)

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}\cdot\nabla f

\displaystyle=-[\psi,f].

(17)

The basic equations for deriving the reduced model are ideal MHD
equations, which are
normalized by using a typical length
L_{0}
,
a typical magnetic field
B_{0}
, a typical mass density
\rho_{0}
,
a typical velocity
v_{0}:=B_{0}/\sqrt{\mu_{0}\rho_{0}}

with
\mu_{0}
being the vacuum permeability, and a typical time

t_{0}:=L_{0}/v_{0}
.
Explicitly, they are the equation of motion

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla\right){\bm{u}}=(\nabla\times{\bm{B}})\times{\bm{B}}-\nabla p

(18)

and its rotation, the vorticity equation

\frac{\partial(\nabla\times{\bm{u}})}{\partial t}=\nabla\times({\bm{u}}\times(\nabla\times{\bm{u}}))+\nabla\times((\nabla\times{\bm{B}})\times{\bm{B}}),

(19)

the ideal Ohm’s law

\frac{\partial\bm{A}}{\partial t}={\bm{u}}\times{\bm{B}}-\nabla\phi

(20)

and its rotation, the induction equation

\frac{\partial{\bm{B}}}{\partial t}=\nabla\times({\bm{u}}\times{\bm{B}}).

(21)

Here,

{\bm{u}}
is the fluid velocity,
{\bm{B}}
is the magnetic field,

p
is the pressure,

\bm{A}
is the vector potential, and

\phi
is the electrostatic potential.
The mass density is constant due to incompressibility,
and the normalized mass density is
1
.

Taking a dot product of the vorticity equation (
19
)
with
{\bm{h}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\mathcal{L}\varphi-\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}u_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[u_{h},\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}u_{h}\right]-\left[\varphi,\mathcal{L}\varphi\right]+\left[\varphi,\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}u_{h}\right]-\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}[u_{h},\varphi]

\displaystyle\quad+\left[B_{h},\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}B_{h}\right]+\frac{1}{\mu_{0}\rho}\left[\psi,\mathcal{L}\psi\right]+\left[\psi,\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}B_{h}\right]-\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}[B_{h},\psi],

(22)

where

\mathcal{L}:=\frac{1}{Kr}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{Kr}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{1}{K^{2}r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}.

(23)

Next, a dot product of the equation of motion (
18
)
with
{\bm{h}}
yields

\frac{\partial u_{h}}{\partial t}=[u_{h},\varphi]+[B_{h},\psi].

(24)

Note that
{\bm{h}}\cdot\nabla p(r,\alpha,t)\equiv 0
.
Multiplying (
24
) with

2MK/(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}

and adding it to Eq. (
22
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\mathcal{L}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[u_{h},\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}u_{h}\right]-\left[\varphi,\mathcal{L}\varphi\right]+\left[\varphi,\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}u_{h}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\left[B_{h},\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}B_{h}\right]+\left[\psi,\mathcal{L}\psi\right]+\left[\psi,\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}B_{h}\right].

(25)

Third, let us take a dot product of the ideal Ohm’s law (
20
)
with
{\bm{h}}
.
The components of the vector potential
\bm{A}
and the magnetic field
{\bm{B}}

can be related as follows.
The magnetic field is expressed as

{\bm{B}}=\frac{1}{Kr}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\alpha}{\bf e}_{r}-\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\frac{1}{r}\left(M\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}+KrB_{h}\right){\bf e}_{\vartheta}-\frac{1}{Kr}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}{\bf e}_{\alpha},

(26)

where
{\bf e}_{r}:=\partial{\bm{r}}/\partial r
,

{\bf e}_{\vartheta}:=\partial{\bm{r}}/\partial\vartheta
,
and
{\bf e}_{\alpha}:=\partial{\bm{r}}/\partial\alpha

with
{\bm{r}}
being a position vector.
On the other hand,
if we express
the vector potential as

\bm{A}=A_{r}\nabla r+A_{\vartheta}\nabla\vartheta+A_{\alpha}\nabla\alpha
,
where where
A_{r}=\bm{A}\cdot{\bf e}_{r}
,

A_{\vartheta}=\bm{A}\cdot{\bf e}_{\vartheta}
,
and
A_{\alpha}=\bm{A}\cdot{\bf e}_{\alpha}

are covariant components,

\nabla\times\bm{A}

reads

\nabla\times\bm{A}=-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial A_{\vartheta}}{\partial\alpha}{\bf e}_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{\partial A_{r}}{\partial\alpha}-\frac{\partial A_{\alpha}}{\partial r}\right){\bf e}_{\vartheta}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial A_{\vartheta}}{\partial r}{\bf e}_{\alpha}.

(27)

By comparing Eqs. (
26
)
and (
27
),
the components can be related as

\displaystyle A_{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\int B_{h}{\rm d}\alpha,

(28)

\displaystyle A_{\vartheta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{K}\psi,

(29)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{M}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}{\rm d}r.

(30)

Then,

{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{A}=\psi/(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})
.
The resultant equation is obtained as

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=[\psi,\varphi].

(31)

Note that
{\bm{h}}\cdot\nabla\phi(r,\alpha,t)\equiv 0
.

Last equation is obtained by a dot product of the induction equation
(
21
) with
{\bm{h}}
as

\frac{\partial B_{h}}{\partial t}=-K_{0}^{2}r^{2}\left[\psi,\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}u_{h}\right]-K_{0}^{2}r^{2}\left[\varphi,\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}B_{h}\right]+\frac{2MK}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}[\varphi,\psi].

(32)

The energy of the system

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}V\,\left(|{\bm{u}}|^{2}+|{\bm{B}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+u_{h}^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}+B_{h}^{2}\right),

(33)

is conserved according to Eqs. (
25
),
(
24
), (
31
), and (
32
).

III
Hamiltonian structure

III.1
Hamiltonian and Lie–Poisson bracket

The single-helicity, incompressible MHD model derived in
Sec.
II
can be written in the
Hamiltonian form with noncanonical variables.
Let us introduce a set of phase-space variables

v=(v^{1},v^{2},v^{3},v^{4})^{\mathrm{T}}:=(U,u_{h},\psi,B_{h}^{\star})^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Here,
U
and
B_{h}^{\star}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{L}\varphi,

(34)

\displaystyle B_{h}^{\star}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\left(B_{h}+\frac{2MK}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\psi\right).

(35)

The Hamiltonian is taken to be the energy inside the plasma as

H[v]=\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}V\,\left(-U(\mathcal{L}^{-1}U)+gu_{h}^{2}-\psi(\mathcal{L}\psi)+\frac{1}{g}\left(B_{h}^{\star}+f\psi\right)^{2}\right),

(36)

where

\displaystyle f(r):=

\displaystyle\,{\bm{h}}\cdot\nabla\times{\bm{h}}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{2MK}{(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}},

(38)

\displaystyle g(r):=

\displaystyle\,|{\bm{h}}|^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}r^{2}}

(40)

are introduced for simplicity of notation.
Then, functional derivatives of
H[v]
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H}{\delta U}

\displaystyle=-\varphi,

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H}{\delta u_{h}}

\displaystyle=gu_{h},

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H}{\delta\psi}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}\psi+fB_{h},

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H}{\delta B_{h}^{\star}}

\displaystyle=B_{h}.

(44)

The Lie–Poisson bracket
for arbitrary functionals
F[v]
and
G[v]

is defined by

\displaystyle\{F,G\}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(U+fu_{h}),\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta U},\frac{\delta G}{\delta U}\right]\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{h},\left(\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta U},\frac{\delta G}{\delta u_{h}}\right]+\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta u_{h}},\frac{\delta G}{\delta U}\right]\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\left\langle\psi,\left(\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta U},\frac{\delta G}{\delta\psi}\right]+\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta\psi},\frac{\delta G}{\delta U}\right]+\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta u_{h}},\frac{\delta G}{\delta B_{h}^{\star}}\right]+\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta B_{h}^{\star}},\frac{\delta G}{\delta u_{h}}\right]\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\left\langle gB_{h},\left(\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta U},\frac{\delta G}{\delta B_{h}^{\star}}\right]+\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta B_{h}^{\star}},\frac{\delta G}{\delta U}\right]\right)\right\rangle

(45)

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\mathcal{J}^{ij}\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}},

(46)

where the angle bracket denotes an inner product, and
the Poisson tensor is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=

\displaystyle\,(\mathcal{J}^{ij})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\,\begin{pmatrix}\left[\circ,U+fu_{h}\right]&\left[\circ,u_{h}\right]&\left[\circ,\psi\right]&\left[\circ,gB_{h}\right]\\
\left[\circ,u_{h}\right]&0&0&\left[\circ,\psi\right]\\
\left[\circ,\psi\right]&0&0&0\\
\left[\circ,gB_{h}\right]&\left[\circ,\psi\right]&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(47)

Then the evolution equation for
v
is written as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{ij}\frac{\delta H}{\delta v^{j}}

(48)

\displaystyle=\{v^{i},H\}.

(49)

The single-helicity dynamics focused in this paper forms an invariant
manifold in the phase space of the dynamics of the full set of ideal MHD,
for which the Hamiltonian structure was derived in
Ref.
Morrison and Greene 1980
. However, it is not trivial what
phase-space variables are appropriate for a reduced model.
A naive choice cannot lead to a Lie–Poison bracket.
The phase-space variables
v

in the present model
includes the magnetic flux function
\psi
, of which spatial derivatives
correspond to the components of the magnetic field.
The components of the magnetic field are part of the phase-space variables
of the full set of ideal MHD.
Therefore, a simple variable transformation
cannot drive the Lie–Poisson bracket of the present four-field model.

The Lie–Poisson bracket can be shown to satisfy the following three
properties:

(i)

Antisymmetry:

\{F,G\}=-\{G,F\},

(50)

(ii)

Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle\{FG,H\}

\displaystyle=G\{F,H\}+F\{G,H\},

(51)

\displaystyle\{F,GH\}

\displaystyle=G\{F,H\}+H\{F,G\},

(52)

(iii)

Jacobi identity:

\{F,\{G,H\}\}+\{G,\{H,F\}\}+\{H,\{F,G\}\}=0,

(53)

where
H[v]
is another arbitrary functional.
Note that
H[v]
here is not the Hamiltonian
(
36
).
The detailed proofs are given in
the next subsection.

Note that
f(r)
, defined in Eq. (
37
),
is helicity of
{\bm{h}}
, which is different from the one in the
“single-helicity”.
This vanishes when the system is axisymmetric with
M=0
or
is translationally symmetric in
z
with
K=0

as seen in Eq. (
38
).
As we have seen, and will observe in the proofs of properties of the
Lie–Poisson bracket, nonzero
f
remains in the helically symmetric
cases, for which it is not trivial to show the Hamiltonian structure
especially the Jacobi identity.

III.2
Proof of properties of Lie–Poisson bracket

III.2.1
Antisymmetry

Let us write the Poisson tensor

\mathcal{J}=(\mathcal{J}^{ij})=([\circ,J^{ij}])

with
J^{ij}=J^{ji}
.
Then, the antisymmetry can be shown directly as

\displaystyle\{F,G\}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\left[\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}},J^{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=-\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}},J^{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=-\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{i}}\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{j}},J^{ji}\right]

\displaystyle=-\{G,F\}.

(54)

Here, Eq. (
15
) and
the antisymmetry (
11
)
are used in the second equality,
and the dummy indices
i
and
j
are interchanged
in the third equality.
In the last equality
J^{ij}=J^{ji}
is used.

III.2.2
Leibniz rule

According to a property of the Fréchet derivative

\frac{\delta{(FG)}}{\delta v^{i}}=\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}G+F\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{i}},

(55)

it is easily shown that

\displaystyle\left\{FG,H\right\}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta{(FG)}}{\delta v^{i}}\mathcal{J}^{ij}\frac{\delta H}{\delta v^{j}}

\displaystyle=G\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\mathcal{J}^{ij}\frac{\delta H}{\delta v^{j}}+F\int{\rm d}V\,\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{i}}\mathcal{J}^{ij}\frac{\delta H}{\delta v^{j}}

\displaystyle=G\left\{F,H\right\}+F\left\{G,H\right\},

(56)

where
H[v]
is another arbitrary functional.
By using the antisymmetry, it is obvious to see

\{F,GH\}=G\{F,H\}+H\{F,G\}
.

III.2.3
Jacobi Identity

Let us recall that the elements of the Poisson tensor
(
47
)
have the form
\mathcal{J}^{ij}=[\circ,J^{ij}]
.
Moreover,
J^{ij}
can be expressed as
J^{ij}=v^{k}a^{ij}_{k}
,
where

a^{11}_{1}=a^{12}_{2}=a^{21}_{2}=a^{13}_{3}=a^{31}_{3}=a^{24}_{3}=a^{42}_{3}=a^{14}_{4}=a^{41}_{4}=1

and

a^{11}_{2}=a^{14}_{3}=a^{41}_{3}=-2MK/(K_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}=f
,
and

a^{ij}_{k}=0
otherwise.

The Lie–Poisson bracket (
45
)
can be written as

\displaystyle\{F,G\}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,v^{k}a^{ij}_{k}\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\right]

\displaystyle=:\left\langle v,\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]\right\rangle,

(57)

where
\langle\,,\rangle
denotes an inner product
and

\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]_{k}:=a^{ij}_{k}\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\right].

(58)

A first variation of
\{F,G\}
can be calculated,
by using arbitrary functions
\tilde{v}=(\tilde{v}^{k})
satisfying
the same boundary condition as
v
, as

\displaystyle\delta\left\langle v,\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\left(\tilde{v}^{k}\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]_{k}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+v^{k}a^{ij}_{k}\left(\left[\frac{\delta^{2}F}{\delta v^{i}\delta v^{\ell}}\tilde{v}^{\ell},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\right]+\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}},\frac{\delta^{2}G}{\delta v^{j}\delta v^{\ell}}\tilde{v}^{\ell}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\left(\tilde{v}^{k}\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]_{k}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+\left(\frac{\delta^{2}F}{\delta v^{i}\delta v^{\ell}}\tilde{v}^{\ell}\left[\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}},v^{k}a^{ij}_{k}\right]+\frac{\delta^{2}G}{\delta v^{j}\delta v^{\ell}}\tilde{v}^{\ell}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\tilde{v}^{\ell}\left(\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]_{\ell}-\frac{\delta^{2}F}{\delta v^{i}\delta v^{\ell}}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\right]+\frac{\delta^{2}G}{\delta v^{j}\delta v^{\ell}}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\right]\right).

(59)

Therefore,

\frac{\delta}{\delta v^{\ell}}\left\langle v,\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]\right\rangle=\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]_{\ell}-\frac{\delta^{2}F}{\delta v^{i}\delta v^{\ell}}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\right]+\frac{\delta^{2}G}{\delta v^{j}\delta v^{\ell}}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\right].

(60)

Then, using also another arbitrary functional

H[v]
,

\displaystyle\{H,\{F,G\}\}

\displaystyle=\left\langle v,\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta H}{\delta v},\frac{\delta}{\delta v}\left\langle v,\left[\!\!\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta v},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v}\right]\!\!\right]\right\rangle\right]\!\!\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int{\rm d}V\,v^{n}a^{m\ell}_{n}\left[\frac{\delta H}{\delta v^{m}},-\frac{\delta^{2}F}{\delta v^{i}\delta v^{\ell}}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta G}{\delta v^{j}}\right]+\frac{\delta^{2}G}{\delta v^{j}\delta v^{\ell}}\left[v^{k}a^{ij}_{k},\frac{\delta F}{\delta v^{i}}\right]\right]

III.3
Casimir invariants

At the last of Sec
III
,
the Casimir invariants
C[v]
are found by integrating

\mathcal{J}^{ij}\delta C/\delta v^{j}=0
for
i=1,2,3,4

as

\displaystyle C[v]

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\left(UF_{1}(\psi)+\left(u_{h}F_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)+F_{2}^{\prime}(\psi)\right)\left(B_{h}^{\star}+f(r)\psi\right)-f(r)F_{2}(\psi)+u_{h}F_{3}(\psi)+F_{4}(\psi)\right)

(66)

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}V\,\left({\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{B}}F_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)+|{\bm{h}}|^{2}B_{h}F_{2}^{\prime}(\psi)-f(r)F_{2}(\psi)+u_{h}F_{3}(\psi)+F_{4}(\psi)\right),

(67)

where
F_{i}(\psi)\,(i=1,2,3,4)
are arbitrary functions of

\psi
.
Note that the Casimir invariants Eq. (
66
) or
(
67
) cannot be derived from the Casimir invariants of the
full set of ideal MHD since the ideal MHD has only two Casimir
invariants; the magnetic helicity and the cross helicity.

Although the present model is applicable to only single-helicity
dynamics of plasmas in cylindrical geometry,
it can be used for
equilibrium and stability analysis of helically symmetric systems
through the energy–Casimir method
based on the Hamiltonian structure presented in this Section.
This will be reported elsewhere.
Such a study might be of interest in relations to quasi-single-helicity
states observed in reversed field pinches.

An extension to a toroidal geometry may be interesting, however,
it seems difficult to find an incompressible vector field
such as
{\bm{h}}
in the present paper. We may need some approximations such as
large aspect ratio.

IV
Applications

In this Section
IV
,
we apply the derived model to two specific problems both related to the
MRI.
The first application is the local dispersion relation of axisymmetric
MRI, which will be described in Sec.
IV.1
.
The second application is the linear stability analyses
and the calculation of mode energy in a setting of
an accretion disk, which will be presented in
Sec.
IV.2
.
Both applications show that the derived model successfully reproduce the
results of previous studies.

IV.1
Local dispersion relation of axisymmetric MRI

Suppose that a cylindrically symmetric equilibrium with a
velocity field
{\bm{u}}_{0}
and a magnetic field
{\bm{B}}_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}_{0}

\displaystyle=u_{\theta 0}(r)\hat{\bm{\theta}}+u_{z0}(r)\hat{\bm{z}},

(68)

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}_{0}

\displaystyle=B_{\theta 0}(r)\hat{\bm{\theta}}+B_{z0}(r)\hat{\bm{z}}.

(69)

By comparing these expressions with
Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
),
we obtain the following relations

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\varphi_{0}}{{\rm d}r}

\displaystyle=Mu_{\theta 0}+Kru_{z0},

(70)

\displaystyle u_{h0}

\displaystyle=-Kru_{\theta 0}+Mu_{z0},

(71)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\psi_{0}}{{\rm d}r}

\displaystyle=-MB_{\theta 0}-KrB_{z0},

(72)

\displaystyle B_{h0}

\displaystyle=-KrB_{\theta 0}+MB_{z0}.

(73)

Let us consider an equilibrium with

u_{\theta 0}=r\varOmega(r)
,
u_{z0}=0
,

B_{\theta 0}=0
, and
B_{z0}=B_{0}=\mathrm{const}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0,

(74)

\displaystyle u_{h0}

\displaystyle=-Kr^{2}\varOmega,

(75)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-KrB_{0},

(76)

\displaystyle B_{h0}

\displaystyle=0.

(77)

Here the prime denotes a derivative with respect to
r
.

For deriving a local dispersion relation of axisymmetric MRI,
let us set
M=0
,
and drop
\partial/\partial r
operating on perturbed quantities.
This is justified under the WKB approximation
explained in IV.A of Ref.
Balbus and Hawley 1998
for example,
where the perturbation is assumed to behave as

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,({\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}-\omega t)}

with
{\bm{k}}=k\hat{\bm{z}}
and
kr\gg 1
. Then, the terms with

\partial/\partial r
become higher order.
From the linearized Eqs. (
25
),
(
24
), (
31
), and (
32
)
about this equilibrium,
we obtain

\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{{\rm i}\,\ell}{r}K(r^{2}\varOmega)^{\prime}&{\rm i}\,\omega&0&{\rm i}\,\ell KB_{0}\\
{\rm i}\,\omega\left(-\frac{\ell^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)&\frac{2{\rm i}\,\ell\varOmega}{Kr^{2}}&\frac{{\rm i}\,\ell^{3}KB_{0}}{r^{2}}&0\\
0&{\rm i}\,\ell KB_{0}&-{\rm i}\,\ell Kr\varOmega^{\prime}&{\rm i}\,\omega\\
-{\rm i}\,\ell KB_{0}&0&{\rm i}\,\omega&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\varphi}\\
\tilde{u}_{h}\\
\tilde{\psi}\\
\tilde{B}_{h}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},

(78)

where
\tilde{\varphi}
,

\tilde{u}_{h}
,
\tilde{\psi}
, and
\tilde{B}_{h}

are the perturbed quantities.

Setting the determinant of the
4\times 4
matrix on the left-hand side
zero,
we obtain

\omega^{2}=\omega_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}+2\varOmega^{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}+2\varOmega^{2}\right)^{2}+4\omega_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}\varOmega^{2}},

(79)

where the Alfvén frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{A}}
is given by

\omega_{\mathrm{A}}:=\sqrt{\ell^{2}K^{2}B_{0}^{2}}.

(80)

Equation (
79
) agrees with,
for example, the
dispersion relation Eq. (111) of Ref.
Balbus and Hawley 1998
.
If we assume

\displaystyle{\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}+2\varOmega^{2}\right|\gg\left|2\omega_{\mathrm{A}}\varOmega\right|}
,
which means that the magnetic field is sufficiently weak so that the
Alfvén frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{A}}
is small enough compared to the
plasma rotation frequency,
the square root term of Eq. (
79
)
can be approximated to yield

\omega^{2}\simeq\omega_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}+2\varOmega^{2}\pm\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}+2\varOmega^{2}\right|\pm\frac{2\omega_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}\varOmega^{2}}{\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}+2\varOmega^{2}\right|}.

(81)

Then, we find that
\omega^{2}<0
when

-4\varOmega^{2}<\frac{{\rm d}\varOmega^{2}}{{\rm d}\ln r}<0.

(82)

By introducing the epicyclic frequency
\kappa
through

\kappa^{2}:=\frac{1}{r^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}(r^{4}\varOmega^{4})}{{\rm d}r},

(83)

the instability criterion (
82
) can be written
as

0<\kappa^{2}<4\varOmega^{2}.

(84)

This agrees with the known condition for the instability.

IV.2
Negative Energy modes in an accretion disk

It was shown that negative energy modes can be found in an accretion

Khalzov, Smolyakov, and Ilgisonis 2008

V
Conclusions

We derived a four-field reduced model of single-helicity, incompressible MHD
in cylindrical geometry.
We clarified the Hamiltonian structure of the model,
where the appropriate noncanonical variables were found,
and the corresponding Lie–Poisson bracket were constructed.
The Lie–Poisson bracket was proven to satisfy
the antisymmetry, the Leibniz rule, and the Jacobi identity.
We found the Casimir invariants including four arbitrary functions.

In the present model, the direction of symmetry is defined by the vector
field
{\bm{h}}
,
which has nonzero helicity. This is different from systems with
axisymmetry or translational symmetry in the axial direction of the
cylindrical plasma. The Hamiltonian structure of the present model was
clarified even though the helicity of
{\bm{h}}
remains finite.

The model was applied to derive the well-known local dispersion relation
of the axisymmetric magnetorotational instability, and was shown to
successfully yield the same result. Also, the model was applied to
linear stability
analyses of the magnetorotational instability in an accretion disk system,
and was shown to reproduce the results in the previous study including
negative energy modes.

Although the Hamiltonian structure of the four-field reduced model was
not fully utilized in the linear stability analyses.
However, the calculation of the perturbed energy was based on the
Hamiltonian formulation, and it was clearly shown that the collision of
positive and negative energy modes leads to instability.
The Hamiltonian structure should be useful if we consider more complex
problems such as a situation where
a negative energy mode becomes unstable due to dissipation.

Appendix A
Calculation of mode energy

This appendix describes how
the perturbed energy of an eigenmode was calculated.
The calculation is based on Ref.
Hirota and Fukumoto 2008a
.
For a reference, several expressions of the energy of perturbed modes
are compared in Ref.
Fukumoto and Zou 2023
.
The perturbed energy calculation including Alfvén continua
was formulated in a general manner in Ref.
Hirota and Fukumoto 2008b
.

As described in Ref.
Hirota and Fukumoto 2008a
, linearized equations of a
Hamiltonian system
is written in the form

\frac{\partial\tilde{v}}{\partial t}=(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{B})\tilde{v}.

(91)

Here,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle:=\left.\left(\frac{\delta^{2}H}{\delta v^{i}\delta v^{j}}\right)\right|_{v_{\mathrm{e}}},

(92)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle:=\left.\mathcal{J}\right|_{v_{\mathrm{e}}},

(93)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle:=\left.\left(\left[\frac{\delta H}{\delta v^{k}},\circ\,a^{ik}_{j}\right]\right)\right|_{v_{\mathrm{e}}},

(94)

where
v_{\mathrm{e}}
denotes an equilibrium.

For the specific model in this paper,
the perturbed variables are

v(r,\alpha,t):=(U,u_{h},\psi,B_{h}^{\star})^{\mathrm{T}}
,
where tildes representing perturbation are omitted
in this Appendix
A

for simplicity.
The operators
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{H}
,
and
\mathcal{B}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=

\displaystyle\,\begin{pmatrix}-\mathcal{L}^{-1}&0&0&0\\
0&g&0&0\\
0&0&-\mathcal{L}-2MKfg&-2MKg\\
0&0&-2MKg&1/g\end{pmatrix},

(95)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=

\displaystyle\,\begin{pmatrix}[\circ,U+fu_{h}]&[\circ,u_{h}]&[\circ,\psi]&[\circ,gB_{h}]\\
[\circ,u_{h}]&0&0&[\circ,\psi]\\
[\circ,\psi]&0&0&0\\
[\circ,gB_{h}]&[\circ,\psi]&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(96)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=

\displaystyle\,\begin{pmatrix}[-\varphi,\circ]&[-\varphi,f\circ]+[gu_{h},\circ]&[-\mathcal{L}\psi+fB_{h},\circ]+[B_{h},f\circ]&[B_{h},\circ]\\
0&[-\varphi,\circ]&[B_{h},\circ]&0\\
0&0&[-\varphi,\circ]&0\\
0&0&[-\varphi,f\circ]+[gu_{h},\circ]&[-\varphi,\circ]\end{pmatrix},

(97)

where equilibrium quantities are substituted in each element
of
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.
The functions
f(r)
and
g(r)
are defined in
Eqs. (
37
) and (
39
),
respectively. The operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined
in Eq. (
23
).
For general definitions of the operators

\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathcal{B}
, see Ref.
Hirota and Fukumoto 2008a
.

Let us introduce an adjoint equation
Hirota and Fukumoto 2008a
as

\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial t}=(\mathcal{H}\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}^{*})\zeta,

(98)

where an adjoint operators are defined through,
for example,

\left\langle\zeta,\mathcal{H}v\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{*}\zeta,v\right\rangle.

(99)

The relations

\mathcal{H}^{*}=\mathcal{H}
,

\mathcal{A}^{*}=-\mathcal{A}
,

(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A})^{*}=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}

were shown to hold, where the last one was derived from the Jacobi identity
(
53

Using these properties, it was also shown that

(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{B})\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{H}\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}^{*})
.

Operating
\mathcal{A}
on Eq. (
98
),
we obtain

\frac{\partial(\mathcal{A}\zeta)}{\partial t}=(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{B})(\mathcal{A}\zeta)

(100)

Therefore, if we obtain a solution
\zeta

of Eq. (
98

v=\mathcal{A}\zeta
is a solution to
Eq. (
91
).
This perturbation restricted on the range of

Morrison 1998

Let us express the perturbed quantities by a Fourier series

v(r,\alpha,t)=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}v_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha}.

(101)

Substituting this expression into the linearized equation
(
91
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}v_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{B})\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}v_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha}

\displaystyle=-{\rm i}\,\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}v_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha},

\frac{\partial v_{\ell}(r,t)}{\partial t}=-{\rm i}\,\mathcal{L}_{\ell}v_{\ell}(r,t),

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}

(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{B})v=:-{\rm i}\,\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}v_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha}.

Note that

\zeta(r,\alpha,t)=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\zeta_{\ell}(r,t){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\,\ell K\alpha}

\omega_{\ell n}

115

Recovering Initial States in Certain Quasilinear Parabolic Problems from Time Averages
Bogdan–Vasile Matioc, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. The inverse problem of reconstructing the initial state in quasilinear parabolic equations from time averages is investigated. Under suitable regularity assumptions on the quasilinear structure and a superlinear growth condition near zero for the semilinear part, it is shown that the initial state can be uniquely recovered from small time averages taken over an arbitrary time period. The applicability of the result is demonstrated for certain chemotaxis models and reaction–diffusion systems.

35K59, 47D99

1.
Introduction and Main Result

Recovering the past state of a system from given observations is a central challenge in the study of evolution equations. In this work, we focus our attention on quasilinear parabolic problems of the form

u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,

(1.1a)

subject to

\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M\,.

(1.1b)

We assume that
E_{1}
and
E_{0}
are Banach spaces such that
E_{1}
is continuously and densely embedded into
E_{0}
and that

A(u)\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
. That is, for fixed
u
, the operator
A(u)
with domain
E_{1}
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on
E_{0}
. Hereby,
A(u)
may depend nonlinearly upon
u
. Moreover,
f=f(u)
is a nonlinear function,
\mathrm{w}
is a nontrivial weight function, and
M\in E_{1}
. In (
1.1
), the usual initial condition is replaced by a nonlocal condition (
1.1b
).
Roughly speaking, we shall show that if
A=A(u)
and
f=f(u)
depend Lipschitz continuously upon
u
and
f
has a superlinear growth near zero, then one can recover the (unique) initial state
u(0)
from small values of time averages over arbitrary time
periods. Hence, problem (
1.1
) is well-posed for arbitrary
T>0
and small values of
M
. The smallness condition is due
to the fact that
T>0
is a priori fixed and one thus seeks for global solutions to a nonlinear problem. We shall later also consider related problems, where the nonlocal condition (
1.1b
) is replaced by conditions of the form

u(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M

(1.2)

or (with a constant
\mathrm{w}_{0}
)

u(0)-\mathrm{w}_{0}u(T)=M\,,

(1.3)

which, however, are significantly easier to handle.

Before presenting our results in detail, let us mention that the semilinear counterpart of (
1.1
) (i.e., when
A(u)
is independent of
u
) was recently investigated in
[
29
]
. As we shall see herein, however, the quasilinear setting turns out to be substantially more involved.

The linear counterpart of problem (
1.1
) (also subject to the other nonlocal conditions
(
1.2
)-(
1.3
)
) was considered in
[

]

In order to explain the difficulties associated with (
1.1
), we proceed formally and assume the existence of a (Hölder continuous in time) solution
u
such that the mapping
t\mapsto A(u(t))
generates a parabolic evolution operator
U_{A(u)}(t,s)
,
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, on
E_{0}
with regularity subspace
E_{1}
in the sense of
[
5
]
. Then,
u
solving (
1.1a
) can be written in form of the variation-of-constants formula

u(t)=U_{A(u)}(t,0)u(0)+\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,s)f(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

and, when plugging this into (
1.1b
), we obtain an equivalent reformulation of (
1.1
) as a fixed point problem

u(t)=U_{A(u)}(t,0)\Xi(u)+\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,s)f(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

(1.4a)

where
u(0)=\Xi(u)
is given by

\Xi(u)=\Phi_{A(u)}^{-1}\big(M-\Psi(u)f(u)\big)

(1.4b)

with

\Phi_{A(u)}:=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)\,U_{A(u)}(t,0)\,\mathrm{d}t

(1.5)

and

\Psi(u)g:=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

(1.6)

Clearly, solving the fixed point problem (
1.4
) requires on the one hand the existence of the evolution operator
U_{A(u)}
and, on the other hand, a careful analysis of the operator
\Phi_{A(u)}
that encodes the initial condition in (
1.4
), in particular concerning its invertibility and its dependence on
u
.
It turns out that, under rather mild assumptions,
\Phi_{A(u)}
is an isomorphism between
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
, so that the inverse operator
\Phi_{A(u)}^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
is of the same order as
A(u)
.
Hence, one can only expect that the initial value
u(0)=\Xi(u)
in (
1.4
) lies in
E_{0}
, without any additional a priori regularity. It is well-known, however, that an initial value in the phase space
E_{0}
is usually insufficient to guarantee the well-posedness of quasilinear problems, e.g. see
[
4
,
2
,
28
]
. Furthermore, it is necessary that
M\in E_{1}
.
In view of these restrictions, and in order to retain greater flexibility for later applications, it is natural to reformulate the problem in spaces of higher regularity within an interpolation–extrapolation scale (see below). Moreover, the use of time-weighted spaces enables us to handle the singular behavior of the evolution operator
U_{A(u)}(t,s)
in this scale at
t=s
and to accommodate a sufficiently broad class of semilinearities. A further crucial step in solving the fixed point problem (
1.4
) is then establishing the (Lipschitz) continuity of the mapping
u\mapsto\Phi_{A(u)}^{-1}
in the operator norm of
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
, which will require some effort.

If

Some remarks are in order regarding the assumptions imposed in Theorem

Remarks 1.3
.

(a)
Assumption (

For uniformly elliptic differential operators, assumptions (

(b)
In applications, assumption (

]

(c)
Assumption (

In a first step, we investigate in Section

In Section

In Section

Finally, Section

4.
Applications

We provide two examples for recovering the initial state in quasilinear parabolic equations from time averages.

4.1.
A Chemotaxis System with Local Sensing

As a first application, we consider a chemotaxis system with local sensing related to the classical Keller–Segel model
[
23
]
, recently studied in
[
1
,
22
]
.
This system describes the evolution of a cell population with density
u
in response to a chemical signal with concentration
v
, and reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\left(a(v)\nabla u-\chi(v)u\nabla v\right)\,,

\displaystyle t\in(0,T)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

(4.1a)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Delta v+u-v\,,

\displaystyle t\in(0,T)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,.

(4.1b)

Here,
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\{1,2,3\}
,
T>0
is a fixed time,
a(v)
represents the diffusion coefficient of the cells, while
\chi(v)
denotes
their sensitivity to the chemical gradient.
These equations are subject to homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\,\,.

(4.1c)

Instead of an initial condition we assume that the local cumulative population
M
over time has been observed; that is,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}u(t,x)\,{\rm d}t=M(x)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,.

(4.1d)

For simplicity we take here a constant time weight (though more general time weights
\mathrm{w}=\mathrm{w}(t)
can easily be considered).
We demonstrate that this problem is well-posed, hence the cumulative population identifies a unique initial state
u(0)
.

Again, in order to keep things simple, we impose that

a,\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\qquad\text{and}\qquad a(0)>0\,,

(4.2)

but less regularity is needed, of course. In the Keller–Segel models, natural choices for
a
include the function
a(s)=e^{-\delta s}
with
\delta>0
(while
\chi=-a^{\prime}
); see, e.g.,
[
22
]
, which motivates assumption (
4.2
).

In order to tackle (
4.1
), we write it in the form of (
1.1
). For this purpose, we denote by
\Delta_{N}
the Neumann-Laplacian. Then, since
v=(1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u
by (
4.1b
)-(
4.1c
) and since

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(a(v)\nabla u-\chi(v)u\nabla v\right)

\displaystyle=a(v)\Delta u+(a-\chi)^{\prime}(v)\nabla v\cdot\nabla u-u\chi(v)\Delta v

\displaystyle=a(v)\Delta u+(a-\chi)^{\prime}(v)\nabla v\cdot\nabla u-\chi(v)uv-\chi(v)u^{2}\,,

we see that (
4.1
) can formally be written in the form of (
1.1
)

u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t\in(0,T)\,,\qquad\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M\,,

by setting

\displaystyle A(u)w

\displaystyle:=a\big((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\big)\Delta w+(a-\chi)^{\prime}(1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\nabla w\cdot\nabla\big((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\big)

(4.3)

\displaystyle-\chi\big((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\big)w(1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u

and

f(u):=-\chi\big((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\big)u^{2}\,.

(4.4)

We will see that in this form the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
can be verified in order to get the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume (
4.2
) and choose
s\in(2,5/2)
.
Then, there exists a constant
m_{0}>0
such that for each
M\in H^{s}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{\nu}M=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\|M\|_{H^{s}}\leq m_{0}

there exists a unique solution to (
4.1
) satisfying

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in C^{\min\{s/2-1,1-2\mu\}}\big([0,T],L_{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big([0,T],H^{s-2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big((0,T],H^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big((0,T],L_{2}(\Omega)\big)

such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s/4}}=0
for
1-s/4<\mu<1/2
.

Proof.

Set
E_{0}:=L_{2}(\Omega)
and

E_{1}:=H^{2}_{N}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\,:\,\partial_{\nu}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}\,.

Then,
E_{1}
is compactly and densely embedded into
E_{0}
and
1-\Delta_{N}\in\mathcal{L}is(H^{2}_{N}(\Omega),L_{2}(\Omega))
.
Thus,
A
from (
4.3
) is a well-defined mapping
A:E_{0}\to\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
with
A(0)=a(0)\Delta_{N}\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
. It then follows from
[
4
, Theorem 7.1; Equation (7.5)]
and
[
30
, Theorem 5.5.1]
that the interpolation-extrapolation scale generated by
(E_{0},1-A(0))
and the complex interpolation functor
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta}
is given by

E_{\theta}\doteq H_{N}^{2\theta}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{v\in H^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,:\,\partial_{\nu}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}\,,&3/2<2\theta<7/2\,,\\[3.0pt]
H^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,,&0\leq 2\theta<3/2\,.\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

Note that

A\in C^{\infty}\big(E_{0},\mathcal{L}(E_{1+\theta},E_{\theta})\big)\,,\qquad\theta\in[0,1/4)\,.

(4.6)

Indeed, if
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, it is straightforward to prove that the Nemitskii operator
F(\cdot):=[u\mapsto F(u)]
satisfies

F(\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(E_{1},E_{1})

(4.7)

and is uniformly Lipschitz continuous on bounded subsets of
E_{1}
.
Moreover, the bilinear pointwise multiplications

\displaystyle H^{2}(\Omega)\bullet H^{2\vartheta}(\Omega)\longrightarrow H^{2\vartheta}(\Omega)\,,\quad 0\leq 2\vartheta\leq 2\,,

(4.8a)

\displaystyle H^{1}(\Omega)\bullet H^{1}(\Omega)\longrightarrow H^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,,\quad 0<2\theta<\frac{1}{2}\,,

(4.8b)

are both continuous, where (
4.8a
) is due to the fact that (see
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
)

[u\mapsto au]\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap\mathcal{L}\big(H^{2}(\Omega)\big)\,,\quad a\in H^{2}(\Omega)\,,

and interpolation, while (
4.8b
) is due to
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
. The regularity properties (
4.6
) follow now from (
4.5
), (
4.7
)–(
4.8
), and the fact that (see
[
30
, Theorem 5.5.1]
)

1-\Delta_{N}\in\mathcal{L}is\big(H^{2\theta}_{N}(\Omega),H^{2\theta-2}_{N}(\Omega)\big)\,,\qquad 2\leq 2\theta<7/2\,.

(4.9)

As for the semilinear part we point out that, due to the quadratic term
u^{2}
, the term
f(u)
cannot be incorporated in the quasilinear structure without violating property (
4.6
).
Instead, recalling that
s\in(2,5/2)
, we may choose exponents
\alpha,\alpha_{0},\gamma,\xi\in(0,1)
such that

0=\beta<\alpha:=\frac{s}{2}-1<\alpha_{0}<\gamma<\frac{1}{4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{2}<\xi:=\frac{s}{4}<\frac{s-1}{2}<\frac{3}{4}\,.

(4.10)

Then, using the continuity of pointwise multiplication (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
)

\displaystyle H^{2}(\Omega)\bullet H^{2\xi}(\Omega)\bullet H^{2\xi}(\Omega)\longrightarrow H^{2\gamma}(\Omega)\,,

we infer that

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(w)\|_{H^{2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\chi((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u)-\chi((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}w)\|_{H^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{2\xi}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\chi((1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}w)\|_{H^{2}}\|u+w\|_{H^{2\xi}}\|u-w\|_{H^{2\xi}}\,.

Taking (
4.5
), (
4.7
), and (
4.9
) into account, we can find for a given
r_{0}>0
a constant
c=c(r_{0})>0
such that, for
v,w\in E_{\xi}\cap\mathbb{B}_{E_{\beta}}(0,r_{0})
,

\|f(u)-f(w)\|_{E_{\gamma}}\leq c(r_{0})\big(\|u\|_{E_{\xi}}^{2}\|v-w\|_{E_{\beta}}+\big[\|u\|_{E_{\xi}}+\|w\|_{E_{\xi}}\big]\|u-w\|_{E_{\xi}}\big)\,.

(4.11)

Finally, writing the operator

\Phi_{A(0)}=\int_{0}^{T}e^{ta(0)\Delta_{N}}\,{\rm d}t\in\mathcal{K}(L_{2}(\Omega))

in a Fourier series expansion in
L_{2}(\Omega)
with respect to a orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{N}
, the injectivity assumption (
1.7f
) is easily verified (since all eigenvalues of
a(0)\Delta_{N}
are nonnegative); see
[
29
, Proposition 6.1]
.
Consequently, gathering (
4.6
) and (
4.10
)-(
4.11
), we see that the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
are satisfied, and the proof of Theorem
4.1
is complete.
∎

Remarks 4.2
.

(a)
Clearly, we did not strive for the most general result when stating Theorem

. In fact, less assumptions on the data are required and it is also possible to consider the problem in an

-setting with

what can be useful for other nonlinearities.
In this case, one derives the injectivity of

from the

or the subsequent Subsection

for details).

(b)
Instead of the chemotaxis model, one may also consider other reaction-diffusion systems, e.g. of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\big(d_{i}(T)\nabla u_{i})+f_{i}(u_{1},...,u_{N},T)\,,\qquad t\in(0,T)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,\quad 1\leq i\leq N\,,

\displaystyle-\Delta T

\displaystyle=g(u_{1},...,u_{N})\,,\qquad t\in(0,T)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

involving
N
reactants
u_{i}
and a quasi-stationary temperature
T
,
subject to suitable boundary conditions (e.g. of Dirichlet type for the temperature) and the conditions

\int_{0}^{T}u_{i}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}t=M_{i}(x)\,,\qquad x\in\Omega\,,\quad 1\leq i\leq N\,,

where

are polynomials.

4.2.
Quasilinear Reaction-Diffusion Systems with Nonlocal Coefficients

We consider the problem of recovering the initial state in nonlinear reaction-diffusion systems with nonlocal diffusion coefficients (e.g. see
[

,
9
,
10
,
17
]
). More precisely, we investigate abstract formulations of the quasilinear prototype problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(x,u)u+f(u)\,,\qquad t\in(0,T)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

(4.12a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}u

\displaystyle:=(1-\delta)u+\delta\partial_{\nu}u=0\,,\qquad t\in(0,T)\,,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\,,

(4.12b)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\mathrm{w}(t)u(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}t=M(x)\,,\qquad x\in\Omega\,,

(4.12c)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a second-order operator on a bounded and smooth domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x,u)v=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{j}\big(a_{j,k}(x,\ell_{j,k}(u))\partial_{k}v\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x,\ell_{j}(u))\partial_{j}v+a_{0}(x,\ell_{0}(u))v\,.

Here, the coefficients satisfy

\displaystyle a_{j,k},a_{j},a_{0}\in C^{\infty}\big(\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}\big)

(4.13a)

and
\ell_{j,k},\ell_{j},\ell_{0}
are real-valued smooth (possibly nonlinear) functionals on
L_{p}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\ell_{j,k},\ell_{j},\ell_{0}\in C^{\infty}\big(L_{p}(\Omega),\mathbb{R}\big)\,.

(4.13b)

Examples of the latter include linear functionals of the form

\ell(u)=\int_{\Omega}m(x)u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

with some weight
m\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.
We assume in (
4.12b
) that either
\delta=0
(Dirichlet case) or
\delta=1
(Neumann case).
Moreover, we assume uniform ellipticity of
\mathcal{A}(x,0)
in the sense that there exists a constant
\underline{a}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}a_{j,k}(x,\ell_{j,k}(0))\xi^{j}\xi^{k}\geq\underline{a}|\xi|^{2}\,,\quad(x,\xi)\in\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

(4.13c)

and impose (for simplicity) the symmetry assumption

\displaystyle a_{j,k}(\cdot,\ell_{j,k}(0))=a_{k,j}(\cdot,\ell_{k,j}(0))\,,\quad a_{j}(\cdot,\ell_{j}(0))=0\,,\qquad 1\leq j,k\leq n\,.

(4.13d)

We consider a weight function

\displaystyle\mathrm{w}\in C^{1}\big([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{+})\,,\qquad\mathrm{w}(0)>0\,.

(4.13e)

As for the semilinearity we assume that

\displaystyle f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\,,\qquad f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0\,,

(4.13f)

and there is
\ell\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(r)-f^{\prime}(s)|\leq c\big(|r|^{\ell-1}+|s|^{\ell-1}\big)|r-s|\,,\quad r,s\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(4.13g)

We can now show that the problem (
4.12
) is well-posed under these assumptions. Indeed, let

E_{0}:=L_{p}(\Omega)\,,\qquad E_{1}:=H_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega):=\{u\in H_{p}^{2}(\Omega)\,;\,\mathcal{B}u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}

for some
p\geq 2
(to be specified later). Setting

A_{p}(u):=\mathcal{A}(\cdot,u)|_{H_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)}\,,\quad u\in L_{p}(\Omega)\,,

it follows from (
4.13a
)-(
4.13b
) that

\displaystyle A_{p}\in C^{\infty}\big(L_{p}(\Omega),\mathcal{L}(H_{p}^{2+2\theta}(\Omega),H_{p}^{2\theta}(\Omega))\big)\,,\quad 2\theta\geq 0\,,

(4.14)

while (
4.13c
)-(
4.13d
) imply (see
[
4
]
) that

\displaystyle A_{p}(0)\in\mathcal{H}\big(H_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega)\big)

(4.15)

with spectrum consisting of countably many real eigenvalues and independent of
p\geq 2
.
In particular, the Fourier series expansion of

\Phi_{A_{2}(0)}=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)e^{tA_{2}(0)}\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathcal{K}(L_{2}(\Omega))

with respect to an orthonormal basis in
L_{2}(\Omega)
consisting of eigenfunctions of
A_{2}(0)

has coefficients
\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)e^{t\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t>0
with
\lambda\in\sigma(A_{2}(0))
(due to (
4.13e
)) and is thus injective.
In view of the property
\Phi_{A_{2}(0)}|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}=\Phi_{A_{p}(0)}
for
p\geq 2
, it thus follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ker}\big(\Phi_{A_{p}(0)}\big)=\{0\}\,.

(4.16)

Next, using
f^{\prime}(0)=0
, we deduce exactly as in
[
25
, Lemma 4.1]
from (
4.13f
) that

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{W_{p}^{2\theta}}\leq c\big(\|u\|_{W_{p}^{2\theta}}^{\ell}+\|v\|_{W_{p}^{2\theta}}^{\ell}\big)\|u-v\|_{W_{p}^{2\theta}}\,,\qquad u,v\in W_{p}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,,

(4.17)

provided that
2\theta>n/p
.
Now,
[
4
, Theorem 7.1; Equation (7.5)]
and
[
30
, Theorem 5.5.1]
yield again that the interpolation-extrapolation scale generated by
(L_{p}(\Omega),\omega-A_{p}(0))
(with
\omega>0
sufficiently large) and the complex interpolation functor
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta}
is given by

E_{\theta}\doteq H_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{v\in H_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,:\,\mathcal{B}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}\,,&1+1/p<2\theta<2+\delta+1/p\,,\\[3.0pt]
H_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,,&0\leq 2\theta<1+1/p\,.\end{array}\right.

(4.18)

Let
(\ell+1)n<p<\infty
and choose

0<\alpha<\alpha_{0}<\min\left\{\frac{n}{p},\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta+\frac{1}{p}\right)\right\}\,.

(4.19)

Thus, we find
\xi
such that

\frac{n}{p}<\xi<\min\left\{\alpha+\frac{1}{\ell+1},1\right\}

(4.20)

and
\gamma
with

0=:\beta<\alpha<\alpha_{0}<\gamma<\xi\,.

(4.21)

Observe then from (
4.17
), (
4.18
), (
4.20
), and (
4.21
) that

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{E_{\gamma}}\leq c\big(\|u\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\ell}+\|v\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\ell}\big)\|u-v\|_{E_{\xi}}\,,\quad u,v\in E_{\xi}\,,

(4.22)

while (
4.14
), (
4.15
), and (
4.18
) give

\displaystyle A_{p}\in C^{\infty}\big(E_{0},\mathcal{L}(E_{1+\theta},E_{\theta})\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\alpha,\alpha_{0}\}\,,\qquad A_{p}(0)\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\,.

(4.23)

Consequently, writing (
4.12
) in the form

u^{\prime}=A_{p}(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t\in(0,T)\,,\qquad\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M\,,

and gathering (
4.16
), (
4.21
), (
4.22
), and (
4.23
), we may invoke Theorem
1.1
to deduce:

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume (
4.13
). Consider
(\ell+1)n<p<\infty
and let
\alpha
, and
\xi
be as in (
4.19
) respectively (
4.20
).
Then, there is a constant
m_{0}>0
such that for each
M\in H_{p}^{2+2\alpha}(\Omega)
with
\mathcal{B}M=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\|M\|_{H_{p}^{2+2\alpha}}\leq m_{0}
,
there exists a unique solution to (
4.12
) satisfying

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in C^{\min\{\alpha,1-\mu(\ell+1)\}}\big([0,T],L_{p}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big([0,T],H_{p}^{2\alpha}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big((0,T],H_{p}^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big((0,T],L_{p}(\Omega)\big)

such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2\xi}_{p}}=0
for
\xi-\alpha<\mu<(\ell+1)^{-1}
.

Although the assumptions and, in particular, the choice of the parameter
p
in Theorem
4.3
are sufficient for our purposes, they are not claimed to be optimal and may admit further refinement.

5.
Quasilinear Equations with Other Nonlocal Conditions

As announced in the introduction one may also consider the evolution equation (
1.1a
) subject to other nonlocal conditions such as (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).
These problems are studied below.

Well-Posedness for the Nonlocal Condition (
1.2
)

We consider

u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{w}(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M\,.

(5.1)

Formally, a solution to (
5.1
) solves

u(t)=U_{A(u)}(t,0)\big(1+\Phi_{A(u)}\big)^{-1}\big(M+\Psi(u)f(u)\big)+\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,s)f(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

with
\Phi_{A(u)}
and
\Psi(u)
defined in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), respectively.
This problem is substantially easier than (
1.1
) since, under suitable additional assumptions, the operator
(1+\Phi_{A(u)})^{-1}
is bounded on
E_{0}
(and on any interpolation space) and is thus of order zero. Therefore, we do not need to shift to extrapolation spaces of higher regularity and may require correspondingly weaker conditions.

More precisely, we assume that

\text{$E_{1}$ is compactly and densely embedded in $E_{0}$}\,.

(5.2a)

Given
\theta\in(0,1)
, we fix an exact admissible interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
of exponent
\theta
and introduce again the Banach space
E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}
.
Let

0\leq\beta<\alpha<1

(5.2b)

and assume that there is
r_{0}>0
such that

A\in C^{1-}\big(\mathbb{B}_{E_{\beta}}(0,r_{0}),\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\,,\qquad A(0)\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\,.

(5.2c)

As for the weight function we merely require that

\mathrm{w}\in C([0,T])\,,

(5.2d)

while for the semilinear part we fix

\ell>0\,,\qquad 0<\gamma\leq 1\,,\qquad 0<\xi<\min\left\{\alpha+\frac{1-(\alpha-\gamma)_{+}}{\ell+1},1\right\}\,,

(5.3a)

and assume that
f:E_{\xi}\cap\mathbb{B}_{E_{\beta}}(0,r_{0})\to E_{\gamma}

satisfies for
v,w\in E_{\xi}\cap\mathbb{B}_{E_{\beta}}(0,r_{0})

\|f(v)-f(w)\|_{E_{\gamma}}\leq c(r_{0})\left(\left[\|v\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\ell}+\|w\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\ell}\right]\|v-w\|_{E_{\xi}}+\left[\|v\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\ell+1}+\|w\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\ell+1}\right]\|v-w\|_{E_{\beta}}\right)

(5.3b)

for some constant
c(r_{0})>0
, and that

\qquad f(0)=0\,.

(5.3c)

Moreover, the operator
\Phi_{A(0)}\in\mathcal{K}(E_{0})
(see the arguments leading to (
2.14
)) is assumed to satisfy

-1\notin\sigma_{p}(\Phi_{A(0)})\,.

(5.4)

Under these conditions, we can show that (
5.1
) is well-posed:

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume (
5.2
), (
5.3
), and (
5.4
).
Then, there exists
m_{0}>0
such that
(
5.1
)
has for each
M\in E_{\alpha}
with
\|M\|_{E_{\alpha}}\leq m_{0}
a unique solution

u\in C^{\min\{\alpha-\beta,1-\mu(\ell+1)\}}\big([0,T],E_{\beta}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{\alpha}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)

such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{E_{\xi}}=0
for
(\xi-\alpha)_{+}<\mu<(\ell+1)^{-1}
.

Proof.

The proof relies on Banach’s fixed point theorem.
To start, we choose
\mu
and
\rho
such that

(\xi-\alpha)_{+}<\mu<\frac{1-(\alpha-\gamma)_{+}}{\ell+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<\rho<\min\{\alpha-\beta,1-\mu(\ell+1)\}\,.

For
L\in(0,r_{0})
, we then set

\displaystyle Y_{L}:=\left\{u\in C([0,T],E_{\beta})\cap C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\xi})\,:\,\text{\parbox[c]{199.52637pt}{$\|u(t)\|_{C([0,T],E_{\beta})}+\|u\|_{C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\xi})}\leq L$,\\
$\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{E_{\beta}}\leq(t-s)^{\rho}\,,\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T$
}}\right\}\,.

This closed subspace of
C\big([0,T],E_{\beta}\big)\cap C_{\mu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)
is equipped with the distance function

d_{Y_{L}}(u,\bar{u}):=\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C([0,T],E_{\beta})}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\xi})}\,,\quad u,\bar{u}\in Y_{L}\,,

and is thus a complete metric space.
Making
r_{0}>0
smaller if necessary, we first note from (
5.2c
) that
A(u)\in C^{\rho}\big([0,T],\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)
for any
u\in Y_{L}
, hence

\Phi_{A(u)}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})\cap\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{1})\cap\mathcal{K}(E_{\alpha})

(5.5)

is well-defined due to (
5.2d
)
[
5
, II.Lemma 5.1.3]
.
Then
[
5
, II.Lemma 5.1.4]
implies that we may choose
L\in(0,r_{0})
small enough such that

\|\Phi_{A(u)}-\Phi_{A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha})}\leq c_{0}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C([0,T],E_{\beta})}\,,\quad u,\bar{u}\in Y_{L}\,.

(5.6)

Now, (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) ensure that
1+\Phi_{A(0)}\in\mathcal{L}is(E_{\alpha})
. Since
\mathcal{L}is(E_{\alpha})
is open in
\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha})
, it follows from (
5.6
) (making
L>0
smaller, if necessary) that

1+\Phi_{A(u)}\in\mathcal{L}is(E_{\alpha})
for
u\in Y_{L}
and

\|(1+\Phi_{A(u)})^{-1}-(1+\Phi_{A(\bar{u})})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha})}\leq c_{0}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C([0,T],E_{\beta})}\,,\quad u,\bar{u}\in Y_{L}\,.

(5.7)

Analogously to Lemma
3.4
, we infer for
\nu:=\mu(\ell+1)\in(0,1)
that there is a constant
c=c(L)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{L}(C_{\nu}((0,T],E_{\gamma}),E_{\alpha})}\leq c

(5.8)

and

\displaystyle\|\Psi(u)-\Psi(\bar{u})\|_{\mathcal{L}(C_{\nu}((0,T],E_{\gamma}),E_{\alpha})}\leq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C([0,T],E_{\beta})}

(5.9)

Defining

\Sigma(u):=(1+\Phi_{A(u)})^{-1}\big(M+\Psi(u)f(u))\,,\quad u\in Y_{L}\,,

and recalling (
5.3
) (see also (
3.24
)-(
3.25
)), it thus follows from (
5.7
), (
5.8
), and (
5.9
) that
\Sigma
satisfies

\|\Sigma(u)-\Sigma(\bar{u})\|_{E_{\alpha}}\leq c\big(\|M\|_{E_{\alpha}}+L^{\ell}+L^{\ell+1}\big)d_{Y_{L}}(u,\bar{u})\,,\quad u,\bar{u}\in Y_{L}\,,

(5.10a)

and

\|\Sigma(u)\|_{E_{\alpha}}\leq c\big(\|M\|_{E_{\alpha}}+L^{\ell+1}\big)\,,\quad u\in Y_{L}\,.

(5.10b)

It is now a standard argument to show that the mapping

u\mapsto U_{A(u)}(t,0)\Sigma(u)+\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,s)f(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s

is a contraction on
Y_{L}
provided that
\|M\|_{E_{\alpha}}
and
L
are sufficiently small. Hence, using the regularity result of
[
5
, II.Theorem 1.2.2]
we conclude that (
5
) has a unique solution as stated in Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Well-Posedness for the Nonlocal Condition (
1.3
)

Finally, we consider the problem

u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)-\mathrm{w}_{0}u(T)=M\,,

(5.11)

with
\mathrm{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. In this case, solutions obey the fixed point equation

u(t)=U_{A(u)}(t,0)\big(1-\mathrm{w}_{0}U_{A(u)}(T,0)\big)^{-1}\big(M+N_{u}(T)f(u)\big)+N_{u}(t)f(u)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

(5.12)

with

N_{u}(t)g:=\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Also this problem is much simpler than (
1.1
) and weaker assumptions are sufficient.
That is, in this case we assume (
5.2
)-(
5.3
) together with

1\notin\mathrm{w}_{0}e^{T\sigma(A(0))}\,.

(5.13)

Then, the well-posedness of (
5.11
) can also be guaranteed:

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume (
5.2
)-(
5.3
) and (
5.13
).
Then, there exists
m_{0}>0
such that (
5.11
)
has for each
M\in E_{\alpha}
with
\|M\|_{E_{\alpha}}\leq m_{0}
a unique solution

u\in C^{\min\{\alpha-\beta,1-\mu(\ell+1)\}}\big([0,T],E_{\beta}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{\alpha}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\,.

such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{E_{\xi}}=0
for
(\xi-\alpha)_{+}<\mu<(\ell+1)^{-1}
.

Proof.

We can fix

(\xi-\alpha)_{+}<\mu<\frac{1-(\alpha-\gamma)_{+}}{\ell+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<\rho<\min\{\alpha-\beta,1-\mu(\ell+1)\}\,.

The proof now is the same as for Theorem
5.1
noticing that (
5.13
) implies that

1-\mathrm{w}_{0}U_{A(0)}(T,0)=1-\mathrm{w}_{0}e^{TA(0)}\in\mathcal{L}is(E_{\alpha})

(5.14)

and hence
1-\mathrm{w}_{0}U_{A(u)}(T,0)\in\mathcal{L}is(E_{\alpha})

for
u\in Y_{L}
with
L>0
small enough and that

\Sigma(u):=\big(1-\mathrm{w}_{0}U_{A(u)}(T,0)\big)^{-1}\big(M+N_{u}(T)f(u)\big)

satisfies (
5.10
) since, for
\nu:=\mu(\ell+1)\in(0,1)
,
\theta\in\{\alpha,\xi\}
, and
t>0
,

\|N_{u}(t)g\|_{E_{\theta}}\leq c(1+t^{1+\gamma_{0}-\theta-\nu})\|g\|_{C_{\nu}((0,t],E_{\gamma})}\,,

with
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\gamma)
chosen such that
\mu(\ell+1)<1+\gamma_{0}+\alpha
, and

\|(N_{u}(t)-N_{\bar{u}}(t))g\|_{E_{\theta}}\leq ct^{1+\gamma-\theta-\nu}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C([0,T],E_{\beta})}\|g\|_{C_{\nu}((0,t],E_{\gamma})}\,,

where
c=c(L)>0
is a suitable constant. This yields Theorem
5.2
.
∎

Fair sharing ratios of Profit and Loss sharing contracts
Abass Sagna

Abstract
We consider islamic Profit and Loss (PL) sharing contract, possibly combined with an agency contract, and introduce the notion of c c -fair profit sharing ratios ( c = ( c 1 , … , c d ) ∈ ( ℝ ⋆ ) d c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in(\mathbb{R}^{\star})^{d} , where d d is the number of partners) which aims to determining both the profit sharing ratios and the induced expected maturity payoffs of each partner ℓ \ell according to its contribution, determined by the rate component c ℓ c_{\ell} of the vector c c , to the global success of the project. We show several new results that elucidate the relation between these profit sharing ratios and various important economic factors as the investment risk, the labor and the capital, giving accordingly a way of choosing them in connection with the real economy. The design of our approach allows the use of all the range of econometrics models or more general stochastic diffusion models to compute or approximate the quantities of interest.

3
The PL sharing - mudharabah contract

A
mudharabah
contract between two entities (which may be, for example, a bank and a company) is a partnership contract where one partner provide the capital (the owner of the capital is called the
rabb al-maal
) and the other partner (the
mudarib
) brings his labor or his expertise to manage the funds in order to make a profit. The profit, if any, is shared between the partners according to an allocation key that must be known at the conclusion of the contract. Each partner may terminate the contract at any time as long as a maturity is not fixed or before the contract execution. After contract completion and when the maturity is contractual, the contract will still be irreversible up to execution of book value of cash assets or up to the maturity. However, with a mutual agreement between the partners, the profit sharing ratios may be updated at any time before the maturity.

It is very important to notice that this contract is a participative contract on the fact that since the investor does not participate to the work, the counterpart of his capital raising is the labour or the expertise of the worker. Then, as a basic principle, the capital owner is compensated only on the profit (in the same way as the manager) of the investment and has, in the same way as the manager who loses the reward of his labor in case of loss, to support the losses, if any, except in the event of mismanagement (a risk that the funding owner may cover by requiring guarantees from the worker).

Finally, the
mudharabah
may be absolute or restrictive. It is restrictive when the funding owner makes restrictions on the domain of activity, for example, or on any other aspect he consider appropriated. Otherwise, the
mudharabah
is said to be absolute.

Our aim in the next section is to determine a fair (in the sense that equalise the payoff of the partners at the maturity of the contract) profit sharing ratios of the partners of a
mudharabah
contract.

4
The PL sharing - musharakah contract

A
musharakah
contract is a partnership between two or several partners to combine their assets, their labour, or their liabilities in order to make profits. The share contribution of each partner to the capital must be determined in advance. The profit sharing ratios are determined according to an agreement between the partners and may be amended at any time before the maturity. However, losses are absorbed by all the partners, proportionally to their contribution to the capital. Our aim in this section is to determine the
c
-fair profit sharing ratios between the partners of a
PL sharing - musharakah
contract.

4.1
The
c
-Fair profit sharing ratios

Suppose that we have two partners that contributed to the amounts
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
of the capital
L
so that their respective share contributions to the capital are
\kappa_{1}=L_{1}/L
and
\kappa_{2}=L_{2}/L
. Denote by
\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})
and by
\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2})
the expected payoff of the partners. Then,

\displaystyle\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=\gamma_{1}\,\mathbb{E}(R_{T}-L)^{+}-\kappa_{1}\,\mathbb{E}(L-R_{T})^{+}

and

\displaystyle\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2})=\gamma_{2}\,\mathbb{E}(R_{T}-L)^{+}-\kappa_{2}\,\mathbb{E}(L-R_{T})^{+},

where
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
stand respectively for the profit ratios of partner
1
and partner
2
. Now, let us discuss on the choice of the fair profit sharing ratios
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
according to the type of management of the
musharakah
project.

1.

If the partners manage together the project with an equal contribution to the management, then, the difference on their global contributions on the project lies in their share contributions
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
to the capital. It could be reasonable to choose the fair profit sharing ratios to be
\gamma_{1}=\kappa_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}=\kappa_{2}
and in this case we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}\,\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=\kappa_{1}\,\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2})\quad

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=c\,\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2}),\ \mbox{with }c=\kappa_{1}/\kappa_{2}.

Now, one question of interest may be to determine the
(c_{1},c_{2})
-fair profit sharing ratios
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
heeding the contribution of partner

\displaystyle c_{1}\,\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=c_{2}\,\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2})\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=c\,\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2}),\ \mbox{with }c=c_{2}/c_{1}.

We have to solve the system of equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}c_{1}\,\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=c_{2}\,\mbox{Pay}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2})\\

(30)

4.2
Fair profit sharing ratio: the general framework

We consider a
PL sharing - musharakah
contrat between
d

\ldots
, and Partner

(when

, the partnership may be for example between a bank and a company, between two companies, between two banks, etc), who contributed amounts to the capital are
L_{1}
,
\ldots
, and
L_{d}
, respectively. So, let
L=L_{1}+\cdots+L_{d}
be the initial capital, and, define
\kappa_{\ell}=L_{\ell}/L
, as the equity stake of partners
\ell
, for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Finally, let us denote by
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d})
, with
\gamma_{1}

+

\cdots

+

\gamma_{d}=1
, the profit sharing ratios of the partners. We want to determine the fair profit sharing between the partners.

As previously,
R_{T}
denotes the net income process of the investment at the maturity
T
of the contract. Then, at the maturity of the contract, the expected payoff of any Partner
\ell
is given by

\mbox{Pay}_{\ell}(\gamma_{\ell},\kappa_{\ell})=\gamma_{\ell}\,\mathbb{E}(R_{T}-L)^{+}-\kappa_{\ell}\,\mathbb{E}(L-R_{T})^{+}.

(51)

We next define a
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
-fair
PL - musharakah
contract.

Definition 4.1
.

A viable
T
-term and
R
-asset based
PL - musharakah
contract of
d
partners is said to be
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
-
fair
if

c_{1}\mbox{Pay}_{1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1})=\cdots=c_{d}\,\mbox{Pay}_{d}(\gamma_{d},\kappa_{d}).

(52)

Now, define some sets that will be used in the sequel: we denote the set

\mathcal{P}_{d}=\{1,\ldots,d\}\qquad\mbox{ and }\quad\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(\ell)=\mathcal{P}_{d}\backslash\{\ell\}.

The following result gives the profit sharing ratio of each partner of a
PL - musharakah
contract.

Proposition 4.2
.

Consider a viable
T
-term and
R
-asset based PL - musharakah contract of
d
partners whom manage themselves the project and agree, according to their contribution to the funding and the management of the project, to rate their respective payoff by the coefficients
c_{1}
,
\ldots
and
c_{d}
, then, the
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
-fair profit sharing ratios are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,\beta(R_{T},L)+\kappa_{\ell}\,\rho(R_{T},L)

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,\big(1-\rho(R_{T},L)\big)+\kappa_{\ell}\,\rho(R_{T},L),\quad\mbox{ for any }\ \ell\in\mathcal{P}_{d},

(54)

where the weights
(c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}})_{\ell\in{\cal P}_{d}}
are defined for every
\ell\in{\cal P}_{d}
by

c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}=\Big(\prod_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(\ell)}c_{i}\Big)\ \big/\,\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\prod_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(j)}c_{i}\Big).

(55)

Furthermore, the expected payoff of each partner
\ell
is given by

\mbox{Pay}_{\ell}(\gamma_{\ell},\kappa_{\ell})=c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,\Delta(R_{T},L),\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,d.

(56)

This means that the partners will share the expected investment profit
\Delta(R_{T},L)
with the respective weights
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}
.

A particular case of interest is the situation where
d-1
partners of a
PL-sharing musharakah
contract appoint a manager (Partner
d
) outside of them and are bounded to him by a
PL-sharing mudharabah
contract. This appears to be a particular case of Proposition

that we state as a corollary.

Corollary 4.3

Consider a viable
T
-term and
R
-asset based PL - musharakah contract of
d-1
partners who appoint a manager (Partner
d
) outside of them and are bounded to him by a PL-sharing mudharabah contract. If they agree, according to their contribution to the funding and the management of the project, to rate their respective payoff by the coefficients
c_{1}
,
\ldots
and
c_{d}
, then, the
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
-fair profit sharing ratios are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\ell}\ =c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,\big(1-\rho(R_{T},L)\big)+\kappa_{\ell}\,\rho(R_{T},L),\quad\ell=1,\ldots,d-1,

(57)

\displaystyle\gamma_{d}\ =\ c_{d,\bar{d}}\,\big(1-\rho(R_{T},L)\big),

(58)

where for any
\ell\in{\cal P}_{d}
,
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}
is defined from (
55
). Furthermore, the expected payoff of each partner
\ell
is given by

\mbox{Pay}_{\ell}(\gamma_{\ell},\kappa_{\ell})=c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,\Delta(R_{T},L),\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,d.

(59)

Let us make the following remarks before giving the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that the coefficients
(c_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\cal P}_{d}}
are put to rate the contribution on the management of the project. The associated coefficients we define as the function

\displaystyle\mbox{w}:{\cal P}_{d}\longrightarrow{\cal P}_{d}

\displaystyle\hskip 15.6491ptc_{\ell}\,\longmapsto\mbox{w}(c_{\ell})=c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}

are the corresponding sharing weights of the
expected investment profit

\Delta(R_{T},L)
(note in fact that
\mbox{w}(c_{1})+\cdots+\mbox{w}(c_{d})=1
). It is clear from Equation (
55
) that the smaller is the rate coefficient
c_{\ell}
, the bigger will be the associated weight
\mbox{w}(c_{\ell})=c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}
.

Besides, owing to Equation (
54
) (or to equations (
57
) and (
58
)) that may be decomposed as

\gamma_{\ell}=\underbrace{c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,\big(1-\rho(R_{T},L)\big)}_{\footnotesize\mbox{labor reward}}+\underbrace{\kappa_{\ell}\,\rho(R_{T},L)}_{\footnotesize\mbox{funding reward}},

we see that the
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
-fair profit sharing ratio
\gamma_{\ell}
of each partner
\ell
appears to be a reward
\kappa_{\ell}\,\rho(R_{T},L)
coming from his contribution to the capital and a reward
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\,(1-\rho(R_{T},L))
due to his contribution to the management (or the labor) of the project. We still deduce that a moderated risky investment (means, the situation where the investment opportunity is greater than the investment risk, or, equivalently,
\rho(R_{T},L)\leq 1/2
) rewards more a contribution to the management of the project than a participation to the capital, whereas a high risky investment (means,
\rho(R_{T},L)\geq 1/2
) rewards more a contribution to the capital. In other words, if we (a firm, for example) are looking for raising a capital to finance our business, it will be in our best interest to find a highly profitable (or a low-risk) activity that will increase our share of the profit.

Remark 4.3
.

We still deduce from Equation (
54
) that for any
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in{\cal P}_{d}
,

\gamma_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}=(c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}})\,\big(1-\rho(R_{T},L)\big)+(\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\rho(R_{T},L).

(60)

Then, the gap
\gamma_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}
on the profit sharing ratios of two partners
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
is the balance between the gap
\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}
on their contributions to the capital and the gap
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}
on their rating weighted contributions to the management of the project. So, it follows from Equation (
60
) that:

1.

If
\kappa_{\ell}\geq\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}
and
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}\geq c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}
(meaning that the partner
\ell
has contributed more than partner
\ell^{\prime}
to the funding of the capital and to the management of the project) then,
\gamma_{\ell}\geq\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}
.

2.

If
\kappa_{\ell}<\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}
and
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}>c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}
(partner
\ell
has contributed less that partner
\ell^{\prime}
to the funding but, more to the management of the project), then,

\gamma_{\ell}\geq\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\rho(R_{T},L)\,\leq\,\frac{c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}}{(c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}})-(\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}})}\in(0,1).

(61)

If in addition
\rho(R_{T},L)\leq 1/2
,
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}>\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}-\kappa_{\ell}
(partner
\ell
catches up the gap on the funding of the capital through his contribution to the management), then,
\gamma_{\ell}\geq\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}.

3.

If
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}<c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}
and
\kappa_{\ell}>\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}
(partner
\ell
has contributed less that partner
\ell^{\prime}
to the management of the project but, more to the funding), then,

\gamma_{\ell}\geq\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\rho(R_{T},L)\,\geq\,\frac{c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}}{(c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}-c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}})-(\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}})}\in(0,1).

(62)

If in addition
\rho(R_{T},L)\,\geq\,1/2
,
\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}>c_{\ell^{\prime},\bar{\ell^{\prime}}}-c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}
(partner
\ell
catches up the gap on the contribution to the management through his contribution to the capital), then,
\gamma_{\ell}\geq\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}
.

Remark 4.4
.

We may also remark that in the particular case where
c_{i}=1
, for any
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,\}
, we have
c_{\ell,\bar{\ell}}=1/d
and Equation (

In this case, we have for any

\gamma_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}=(\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}})\rho(R_{T},L),

so that the difference on the fair sharing profit ratio of two different partners comes from the difference from their share contribution to the capital. If all have contributed to the same amount of the capital, means:

, for any

This also tell that the best way of absorbing the investment risk for a

contract is to encourage to an equal participation to the capital and to the labor.

Remark 4.6

(
on perspectives).

We may note that all the challenge in our approach is the computation of the quantities
\rho(R_{T},L)
and
\Delta(R_{T},L)
given the dynamics of
R
. For example, we may model the dynamics of
R
as (the Black-Scholes model)

dR_{t}=\mu R_{t}\,dt+\sigma R_{t}\,dW_{t},\qquad R_{0}=L,\quad t\in[0,T],

(70)

where
W
is a brownian motion defined on
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma(>0)
are resp. the instantaneous return and the instantaneous volatility parameters. In this case, the solution of the stochastic differential equation (
70
) reads, for any
t\in[0,T]
and especially for
t=T
,

R_{t}=L\exp\big((\mu-\sigma^{2}/2)t+\sigma W_{t}\big),

meaning in particular that the distribution of
R
is log-normal. Furthermore, we may show that an
R
asset-based investment is viable if and only if
\mu>0
and that
\rho(R_{T},L)
and
\Delta(R_{T},L)
are explicitly given by (setting
\vartheta(\mu,\sigma)=(2\mu-\sigma^{2})T\,/\,(2\sigma\sqrt{T})
and denoting
\Phi(\cdot)
the c.d.f. of the standard normal distribution)

\displaystyle\rho(R_{T},L)=-\frac{\Phi(-\vartheta(\mu,\sigma))-e^{\mu T}\Phi(-\vartheta(\mu,\sigma)-\sigma\sqrt{T})}{\Phi(\vartheta(\mu,\sigma))-e^{\mu T}\Phi(\vartheta(\mu,\sigma)+\sigma\sqrt{T})}

(71)

and

\displaystyle\Delta(R_{T},L)=L(e^{\mu T}-1).

(72)

Even if these previous quantities may somewhere be considered as some benchmarks values (that typically must be closed to what we may expect from the use of some more general models in a lower volatility framework), it is however well known that most of the financial data set distribution are not log-normal. Then, one of the challenging tasks is to use all the range of econometrics models (like the
ARIMA
or the
(G)ARCH
model) or more general stochastic diffusion models (like local volatility or stochastic volatility models) to find the appropriate model for
R
, given an asset data set.

Conclusion remark
. The present paper introduces and studies the notion of
c
-fair profit sharing ratios for islamic profit and loss sharing contracts, possibly combined with an agency contract. We show that the
c
-fair profit sharing ratio is decomposed as a reword from the funding of the capital and a reward from the labor or from the management of the project. Although the profit sharing ratios is determined with a common agreement between the partners of PL contracts, this work may help the islamic finance institutions, first, understanding its link with several key economic factors as: the expected investment profit, the investment risk, the investment opportunity, etc, and, secondly, the present results may also help them taking informed decisions about the profit sharing ratios and the related parameters. Furthermore, the work open new perspectives in the (dynamic) stochastic modelling and their applications to islamic finance engineering.

Random Attractors for McKean-Vlasov SDEs
Mengyu Cheng, Xianjin Cheng, Zhenxin Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we mainly focus on the existence of random attractors for McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations on a separable Hilbert space H H . A significant challenge arises from the distribution-dependence of the coefficients, thereby causing the lack of the stochastic flow property on H H . To address this issue, we first transform the original equation into a system on the product space H × 𝒫 ⁡ ( H ) H\times\mathcal{P}(H) and consider the existence of random attractors on this space. We then analyze cocycles associated with two parametric dynamical systems. Within this framework, we define the corresponding pullback random attractor and develop a general theory for the existence of random attractors for such cocycles. Finally, we apply our theoretical results to McKean-Vlasov stochastic ordinary differential equations, McKean-Vlasov stochastic reaction-diffusion equations, and McKean-Vlasov stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equations. In the case where the attractor reduces to a singleton set 𝒜 ⁡ ( ω ) := ( ξ ⁡ ( ω ) , μ ∞ ) {\mathcal{A}}(\omega):=(\xi(\omega),\mu_{\infty}) , we show that ξ \xi corresponds to the stationary solution for the decoupled SPDE, satisfying ℙ ∘ [ ξ ] − 1 = μ ∞ {\mathbb{P}}\circ[\xi]^{-1}=\mu_{\infty} .

1.
Introduction

Consider the following McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation (SDE) on separable Hilbert space
H
:

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}=b(X_{t},{\mathscr{L}}_{X_{t}}){\rm d}t+\sigma(X_{t}){\rm d}W_{t},

(1.1)

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{X_{t}}
is the law or distribution of
X_{t}
and
W_{t}
is a
Q
-Wiener process.
McKean-Vlasov SDEs, also known as mean-field SDEs, were initially proposed by Kac
[
26
,
27
]

in the study of the Boltzmann equation for the density of particles in a diluted monatomic gas and the Vlasov dynamics equation used to describe a stochastic toy model.
These equations are closely related to mean-field interacting particle systems.
The propagation of chaos
[
47
]
indicates that such equations are the limiting equations for mean-field interacting particle systems as the number of particles tends to infinity.
They have a wide range of applications in mean-field game theory, stochastic control, financial mathematics, and other fields
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
13
,
33
]
.
The well-posedness of solutions to McKean-Vlasov SDEs and
the existence and uniqueness of invariant measures have been proven under various conditions,
such as in
[
33
,
52
,
18
,
40
,
37
,
42
,
23
]
.

Note that the solutions of (
1.1
) describe nonlinear diffusion
[
39
]
,
where the motion of particles is influenced not only by their own positions
but also by the effect of all other particles in the system (i.e., the mean-field effect).
This leads to many interesting phenomena in the motion of particles,
such as the existence of multiple invariant measures
[
17
]
and
the occurrence of phase transitions
[
48
]
under central interactions.
This also makes many classical frameworks of dynamical systems inapplicable,
such as the inability to generate a random dynamical system (RDS)
for (
1.1
) as in the classical case.
Therefore, to study the dynamics of (
1.1
),
we need to develop new methods and theories.

The attractor is an important concept in dynamical systems,
which can be used to characterize the long time asymptotic behavior of systems.
To study the asymptotic behavior of RDS,
the concept of random attractors was introduced and studied in

[
15
,
16
,
19
,
43
]
.
Since then, it has attracted the attention of many scholars, such as semilinear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDE)
with additive noise
[
11
,
4
,
34
,
50
,
53
]
.
As for the research on non-semilinear SPDEs, see e.g.
[
5
,
7
,
20
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, there is no work on random attractors for McKean-Vlasov SDE.
Existing studies on pullback attractors for McKean-Vlasov SDE mainly focus on measure attractors; see e.g.
[
44
]
.
In contrast to these results, the primary aim of this paper is to establish
the existence of random attractors for system (
1.1
) from a pathwise perspective.

It is worth noting that when
b(x,\mu)\equiv b(x)
, equation (
1.1
) reduces to a classical SDE.
Under standard assumptions, there exists a unique solution
X(s,t,x),t\geq s
with
initial condition
X(s,s,x)=x\in H
.
Moreover, one can show that there exists a subset
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
of full measure
such that
X(s,t,x)
forms a
stochastic flow
, that is,
for any
s<r<t
,
x\in H
and
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
,

X(r,t,X(s,r,x)(\omega))(\omega)=X(s,t,x)(\omega),

(1.2)

which implies that the Chapman–Kolmogorov equation is satisfied.
However, a major difficulty in the McKean-Vlasov SDE framework arises from the distribution-dependent nature of the coefficeints,
which prevents (
1.1
) from satisfying the stochastic flow property
(
1.2
)
on
H
.
As a result, the McKean-Vlasov SDE does not generate an RDS on
H
.
To address this issue, we adopt the method from
[
8
]
and
[
41
]

to transform equation (
1.1
) into

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}=b(X_{t},{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{t}})\,{\rm d}t+\sigma(X_{t}){\rm d}W_{t},\quad X_{s}=x\in H,

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\rm d}Y_{t}=b(Y_{t},{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{t}})\,{\rm d}t+\sigma(Y_{t}){\rm d}W_{t},~{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{s}}=\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(H).

(1.4)

Specifically, once a solution to the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
1.4
) is obtained,
the time marginal law
{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{t}}
is determined.
Fixing this law in (
1.3
), it is straightforward that
(
1.4
) becomes a time-inhomogeneous SDE.
Define
P_{s,t}^{*}\mu:={\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{t}}
with the initial distribution
{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{s}}=\mu
.
It can be shown that
P_{s,t}^{*}
forms a semi-flow on
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(H)
, i.e.
for any
0\leq s<r<t
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(H)
,

P_{s,t}^{*}\mu=P_{r,t}^{*}P_{s,r}^{*}\mu.

Note that (
1.3
) is non-autonomous,
as its evolution over time depends on
P_{s,t}^{*}\mu
.
To emphasize the dependence of the solution on the initial distribution

\mu
of
{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{t}}
, we denote it by
X_{s,t}^{x,\mu}
.

To analyze the long-term behaviour of the coupled system
(
1.3
)-(
1.4
),
we employ the classical method of skew product,
a technique widely used in the study of non-autonomous dynamical systems.
Then we have the following stochastic flow property on
the product space
H\times{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(H)
:

\displaystyle(X_{s,t}^{x,\mu},P_{s,t}^{*}\mu)=\Big(X_{r,t}^{X_{s,r}^{x,\mu},P_{s,r}^{*}\mu},P_{r,t}^{*}P_{s,r}^{*}\mu\Big),~s\leq r\leq t.

(1.5)

This structure enables the system to generate an RDS
on
H\times{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(H)
.

We now turn to the long-term behaviour of the system on
H
. Consider the cocycle

\displaystyle\varphi:\quad

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\times{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(H)\times H\rightarrow H,

\displaystyle(t,\omega,\mu,x)\mapsto\varphi(t,\omega,\mu,x):=X_{0,t}^{x,\mu}(\omega).

Since the evolution of
\varphi
depends not only on
\omega
but also on
\mu
,
we first develop a general framework for
random attractors of cocycle defined over two parametric dynamical systems

(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{1,t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}})
and

({\mathcal{P}},\{\theta_{2,t}\}_{t\geq 0})
,
where the later one is just a semi-dynamical system with time forward.
To formulate the pullback attractor, we introduce a time backward extension

\{\Theta_{2,t}\}_{t\leq 0}
of
\theta_{2,\cdot}
on the global attractor
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
of
\{\theta_{2,t}\}_{t\geq 0}

(see e.g.
[
22
,
28
]
).
Since
\{\Theta_{2,t}\}_{t\leq 0}
is a set-valued semi-dynamical system,
we give the definition of random attractors of

\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\times{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\times{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
over
the driving systems in the sense of Definition
2.4
.
Let

\displaystyle\Phi:\quad

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\times{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle(t,\omega,(p,x))\mapsto\Phi(t,\omega)(p,x):=\left(\theta_{2,t}p,\varphi(t,\omega,p,x)\right)

be a continuous RDS over
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{1,t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}})
.
If
\varphi
is asymptotically compact and have a closed attracting subset on
{\mathcal{X}}
,
then we show that
\Phi
admits a random attractor

{\mathscr{A}}:=\left\{{\mathscr{A}}(\omega):\omega\in\Omega\right\}
(Proposition
2.2
).
Furthermore, we prove that
\varphi
also admits a random attractor
{\mathcal{A}}
,
where

{\mathcal{A}}(\omega,p):=\left\{x\in{\mathcal{X}}:(x,p)\in{\mathscr{A}}(\omega)\right\};

see Theorem
2.8
.

Subsequently, we apply this theoretical result to several representative models:
McKean-Vlasov stochastic ordinary differential equations (SODE),
McKean-Vlasov stochastic reaction-diffusion equations,
and McKean-Vlasov stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equations.
In the case where the attractor
{\mathscr{A}}
reduces to a singleton set,
denoted as
\{(\xi(\omega),\mu_{\infty}):\omega\in\Omega\}
,
it holds that
{\mathscr{L}}_{\xi}=\mu_{\infty}
, where

\xi
is the stationary solution for the decoupled SPDE (
1.3
) and

\mu_{\infty}
is the unique invariant measure
of (
1.4
); see Theorem
2.15
for more details.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we present our main results.
In Section
3
, we give the proof of the general framework for
the existence of random attractors.
In Section
4
, we show the existence of random attractors for
McKean-Vlasov SODEs with multiplicative noise by the general theorem stated in Section
2
.
In Section
5
, we apply this general framework to McKean-Vlasov stochastic reaction-diffusion equation
and McKean-Vlasov stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equation separately.

In this section,

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}=b(X_{t},{\mathscr{L}}_{X_{t}})\,{\rm d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\circ{\rm d}W_{t},

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rm d}X_{t}=b(X_{t},{\mathscr{L}}_{Y_{t}})\,{\rm d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\circ{\rm d}W_{t},~X_{s}=x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\

(2.5)

[
33
]
suggests that the existence and uniqueness of solutions to equation (

(
C1)

\sigma

(

Here

(

(2.7)

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{x}V(x,\mu),\overline{b}(x,\mu)\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}(x)\partial^{2}_{xx}V(x,\mu)\sigma_{i}^{T}(x)

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\langle\partial_{\mu}V(x,\mu)(y),\overline{b}(y,\mu)\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}(y)\partial_{y}\partial_{\mu}V(x,\mu)(y)\sigma_{i}^{T}(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y).

(2.8)

Let
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
be a separable Hilbert space with dual space
H^{*}
.
Let
(V,\|\cdot\|_{V})
be a reflexive Banach space such that
V\subset H

continuously and densely. By identifying
H
with its dual
H^{*}
via the Riesz isomorphism,
we then have the following Gelfand triple

V\subseteq H\subseteq V^{*}.

For any
T>0
, consider the nonlinear evolution equation

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)=A(t,u(t)),\quad 0<t<T,\\
u(0)=u_{0}\in H,\end{cases}

(B.1)

where
u^{\prime}
is the generalized derivative of
u
on (0,T) and

A:[0,T]\times V\rightarrow V^{*}
is restrictedly measurable, i.e. for each
{\rm d}t
-version
of
u\in L^{1}([0,T];V)
,
t\mapsto A(t,u(t))
is
V^{*}
-measurable on
[0,T]
.

Let us introduce the following conditions.
Suppose that for fixed
\alpha>1
and
\beta\geq 0
there exist constants
\delta>0
,

C\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a positive function
f,g\in L^{1}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}})
such that for all
t\in[0,T]

and
v,v_{1},v_{2}\in V
:

(A1)

(Hemicontinuity)
The map
\theta\mapsto~_{V^{*}}\langle A(t,v_{1}+\theta v_{2}),v\rangle_{V}
is continuous
on
{\mathbb{R}}
.

(A2)

(Local monotonicity)

~{}_{V^{*}}\langle A(t,v_{1})-A(t,v_{2}),v_{1}-v_{2}\rangle_{V}\leq\left(f(t)+\rho(v_{1})+\eta(v_{2})\right)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H}^{2},

where
\rho,\eta:V\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
are measurable and
satisfy that there exist
C,\gamma\in[0,+\infty)
such that

\rho(v)+\eta(v)\leq C\left(1+\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}\right)\left(1+\|v\|_{H}^{\gamma}\right).

(A3)

(Coercivity)

~{}_{V^{*}}\langle A(t,v),v\rangle_{V}\leq-\delta\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}+g(t)\|v\|_{H}^{2}+f(t).

(A4)

(Growth)

\|A(t,v)\|_{V^{*}}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\leq\left(f(t)+C\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}\right)\left(1+\|v\|_{H}^{\beta}\right).

Theorem B.1
.

Assume that
V\subseteq H
is compact and (A1)-(A4) hold.

, there exists a unique solution

\langle u(t),v\rangle_{H}=\langle u_{0},v\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}~{}_{V^{*}}\langle A(s,u(s)),v\rangle_{V}{\rm d}s.

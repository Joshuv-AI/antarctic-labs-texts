Liouville Brownian motion and quantum cones in dimension d > 2 d>2
Federico Bertacco Imperial College London Ewain Gwynne University of Chicago

Abstract
For d > 2 d>2 and γ ∈ ( 0 , 2 ​ d ) \upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d}) , we study the Liouville Brownian motion associated with the whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We compute its spectral dimension, i.e., the short-time asymptotics of the heat kernel along the diagonal, which, in contrast to the two-dimensional case, depends on both γ \upgamma and on the thickness of the starting point. Furthermore, for even dimensions d > 2 d>2 , we show that the spherical average process of the whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} can be identified with the integral of a stationary Gaussian Markov process of order ( d − 2 ) / 2 (d-2)/2 . Exploiting this representation, we construct the higher-dimensional analogue of the β \upbeta -quantum cone for β ∈ ( − ∞ , Q ) \upbeta\in(-\infty,Q) , with Q = d / γ + γ / 2 Q=d/\upgamma+\upgamma/2 . Lastly, for α = Q − Q 2 − 4 \upalpha=Q-\sqrt{Q^{2}-4} , we prove that the law of the d d -dimensional α \upalpha -quantum cone is invariant under shifts along the trajectories of the associated Liouville Brownian motion.

Main results

We now present our main results. First, we identify the spherical average process in any arbitrary even dimension. Next, we provide the result regarding the spectral dimension of the LBM. 2.2
Spectral dimension of the LBM in arbitrary dimension

The construction of LBM in dimensions
d>2
closely follows the approach used for its construction in dimension
d=2

[
Ber15

GRV16
]

For
\upalpha\in(0,2)

(\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}

\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\lim_{\upvarepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}\upvarepsilon^{\upalpha^{2}/2}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{\upvarepsilon}(B_{s})}ds\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\geq 0\;.

(2.5)

The convergence of the limit in the right-hand side of the above display falls within the scope of the theory of subcritical GMC measures, which guarantees that, for all

We then aim to define the LBM as

\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}B_{\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}^{-1}(t)}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\geq 0\;.

Recall that we want the LBM to be compatible with the coordinate transformation rule (
1.2

[

\upphi

\bigl(\upphi^{-1}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t})\bigr)_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha,t})_{t\geq 0}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha,t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\overline{B}_{\overline{\mathrm{F}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha}(t)}\;,

with
(\overline{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\overline{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi\rvert,\upalpha}

\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert

For a bounded domain

Inspired by

3.2
The spherical average

In this section, we introduce the spherical average process of the whole-space LGF. Most of the material presented here is drawn from
[
LSSW16
, Section 11]
. However, due to the significance of the spherical average in this paper, we provide a summary of the main concepts and results.

For a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define its spherical average process around
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the function
(0,\infty)\ni r\mapsto f_{r}(x)
given by

f_{r}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x+r\upsigma)d\upsigma\;.

A straightforward calculation shows that for all
\upphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
it holds that

\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{r}(x)\upphi(r)dr=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\frac{\upphi(\lvert z-x\rvert)}{\lvert z-x\rvert^{d-1}}dz\;.

This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.6
.

For
h
the whole-space LGF and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the
spherical average
around
x
modulo a global additive constant
h_{\cdot}(x)
by letting

(h_{\cdot}(x),\upphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert^{-1}\Biggl(h,z\mapsto\frac{\upphi(\lvert z-x\rvert)}{\lvert z-x\rvert^{d-1}}\Biggr)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\upphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\;.

We observe that if
\upphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the mapping
z\mapsto\upphi(\lvert z-x\rvert)/\lvert z-x\rvert^{d-1}
is in
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and so the previous definition makes sense.
In order to find the covariance kernel of the spherical average process, we fix
\upphi
,
\uppsi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and we observe that for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[(h_{\cdot}(x_{1}),\upphi)(h_{\cdot}(x_{2}),\uppsi)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\Biggl(h,z\mapsto\frac{\upphi(\lvert z-x_{1}\rvert)}{\lvert z-x_{1}\rvert^{d-1}}\Biggr)\Biggl(h,z\mapsto\frac{\uppsi(\lvert z-x_{2}\rvert)}{\lvert z-x_{2}\rvert^{d-1}}\Biggr)\Biggr]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\rvert\frac{\upphi(\lvert z_{1}-x_{1}\rvert)\uppsi(\lvert z_{2}-x_{2}\rvert)}{\lvert z_{1}-x_{1}\rvert^{d-1}\lvert z_{2}-x_{2}\rvert^{d-1}}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\log\lvert(x_{1}+r_{1}\upomega)-(x_{2}+r_{2}\upsigma)\rvert d\upsigma d\upomega\Biggr)\upphi(r_{1})\uppsi(r_{2})dr_{1}dr_{2}\;,

from which we can deduce that, for all

\mathcal{K}_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[h_{r_{1}}(x_{1})h_{r_{2}}(x_{2})\bigr]=-\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\log\lvert(x_{1}+r_{1}\upomega)-(x_{2}+r_{2}\upsigma)\rvert d\upsigma d\upomega\;.

(3.5)

In particular, if

Proposition 3.10

(Coordinate change formula for

Specifically, for all

2

\int_{B(0,R)\times B(0,R)}\frac{1}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{\mathfrak{s}-\upvarepsilon}}\upsigma_{t}(dx)\upsigma_{t}(dy)<\infty\;,

We refer to

\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dx)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\lim_{\upvarepsilon\to 0}\upvarepsilon^{\frac{\upalpha^{2}}{2}}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{\upvarepsilon}(x)}\upsigma(dx)\;.

In order to prove the conformal invariance of the LBM, we need the following auxiliary result.

Proposition 5.1

(Coordinate change formula for
\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
)
.

Consider the same setting as in Proposition
3.10
. For
Q>2
, let
\upalpha\in(0,2)
be as specified in (
2.7
). Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds
\mathbf{P}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(\upphi(A))=\upnu_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log|\upphi^{\prime}|,\upalpha}(A)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\;.

(5.3)

Proof.

This proof is completely analogous to the proof of Proposition
3.10
. However, we provide the details in order to highlight where the relation (
2.7
) between
Q
and
\upalpha
originates from.

We start by observing that, for every
d\geq 2
, the measure
\upsigma
defined in (
5.1
) has two-dimensional scaling, i.e., if
\upphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a conformal automorphism which can be expressed as a composition of translations, dilations and rotations of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then for all Borel subsets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\upsigma(\upphi(A))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}|\upphi^{\prime}|^{2}\upsigma(A)\;.

Now, let
c>0
and consider the field
\mathbf{h}^{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}(c\,\cdot)
. For any
\upvarepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have the following relation between the spherical averages:
\mathbf{h}^{c}_{\upvarepsilon}(x)=\mathbf{h}_{c\upvarepsilon}(cx)
. For
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that

\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{c},\upalpha}(A)=\lim_{\upvarepsilon\to 0}\upvarepsilon^{\upalpha^{2}/2}\int_{A}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}^{c}_{\upvarepsilon}(x)}\upsigma(dx)=\lim_{\upvarepsilon\to 0}\upvarepsilon^{\upalpha^{2}/2}\int_{A}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{c\upvarepsilon}(cx)}\upsigma(dx)=\lim_{\updelta\to 0}(\updelta/c)^{\upalpha^{2}/2}c^{-2}\int_{cA}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{\updelta}(x)}\upsigma(dx)\;,

where in the last equality we set
\updelta=c\upvarepsilon
, we did the change of variables
x\mapsto x/c
, and we used use the two-dimensional scaling of the occupation measure of the Brownian motion. We note that the term on the right-hand side of the above display equals
c^{-2-\upalpha^{2}/2}\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(cA)
, and so we have that

c^{2+\upalpha^{2}/2}\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{c},\upalpha}(A)=\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(cA)\hskip 20.00003pt\iff\hskip 20.00003pt\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{c}+Q\log c,\upalpha,t}(A)=\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(cA)\;.

where here we used the fact that, for
\upalpha=\upalpha(Q)
as in (
2.7
), it holds that
\upalpha Q=2+\upalpha^{2}/2
. Finally, as for the proof of Proposition
3.10
, we observe that the same argument also works if instead of just a scaling we have a composition of a scaling, rotation, and translation.
∎

5.1.1
Conformal invariance of the LBM

We now have all the necessary elements to prove that if
\upalpha\in(0,2)
is chosen as specified in (
2.7
), then the LBM is compatible with the coordinate change rule.

Proposition 5.2
.

Consider the same setting as in Proposition
3.10
. For
Q>2
, let
\upalpha\in(0,2)
be as specified in (
2.7
). Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds
\mathbf{P}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\bigl(\upphi^{-1}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t})\bigr)_{t\geq 0}=\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha,t})_{t\geq 0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ where }\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha,t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\overline{B}_{\overline{\mathrm{F}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha}(t)}\;,

a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\overline{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi\rvert,\upalpha}
the associated clock process with respect to the field
\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert

Proof.

For
c>0
, define
(\overline{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}(c^{-1}B_{c^{2}t})_{t\geq 0}
and consider the dilation
\upphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
give by
\upphi(x)=cx
. Then, for all

, it holds that

\upphi^{-1}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t}\bigr)=c^{-1}B_{\mathrm{F}^{-1}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)}=\overline{B}_{c^{-2}\mathrm{F}^{-1}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)}=\overline{B}_{\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,c}^{-1}(t)}\;,

where we set
\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,c}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(c^{2}t)
. Furthermore, for all
t\geq 0
, thanks to Proposition
5.1
, we observe that

\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,c}(t)=\upnu_{\mathbf{h}}([B]_{c^{2}t})\overset{\eqref{eq:coordinate_change_clock}}{=}\upnu_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha}(c^{-1}[B]_{c^{2}t})=\overline{\upnu}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha}([\overline{B}]_{t})=\overline{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha}(t)\;,

where
\overline{\upnu}_{\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert,\upalpha}
denotes the GMC with respect to the field
\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}\rvert
along the trajectory of the Brownian motion
(\overline{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Finally, as for the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we observe that the same argument also works if instead of just a scaling we have a composition of a scaling, rotation, and translation.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The reason why, in Proposition
5.1
, we only considered conformal automorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which can be expressed as a composition of translations, dilations and rotations, instead of a general conformal maps, is that the Brownian motion in dimension three and higher is not conformally invariant. Indeed, consider the inversion map
\upphi(z)=z/\lvert z\rvert^{2}
and let

be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for

. Then of course
(\upphi(B_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
cannot be expressed as a time-change of a Brownian motion.

5.2.1
Potential theory of the GMC measure in arbitrary dimension
d>2

For each

-\Delta G_{R}(x,\cdot)=\updelta(x-\cdot)\;,\hskip 20.00003ptG_{R}(x,\cdot)_{|\partial B(0,R)}=0\;.

(5.4)

Definition 5.6
.

The
R
-potential of a positive Borel measure

d

[

In order to associate a PCAF to the measure

Lemma 5.7

Let

Lemma 5.8

Let

Fix

\upvarepsilon>0

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{\{y\in B(0,R)\,:\,\lvert x-y\rvert\in(e^{-(n+1)}2R,e^{-n}2R]\}}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)

Remark 5.9
.

We expect that if

\updelta\to 0

\lim_{\updelta\to 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}\sup_{x\in(0,R)}\int_{B(0,R)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upvartheta_{\updelta}(x-y)\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,(n)}(dy)=0\;.

This holds since, by using Lemma
A.2
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
x\in B(0,R)
, and any
\upvarepsilon>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upvartheta_{\updelta}(x-y)\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,(n)}(dy)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq\lfloor\log(R/(2\updelta))\rfloor}\int_{\{y\in B(0,R)\,:\,\lvert x-y\rvert\in(e^{-(k+1)}R,e^{-k}R]\}}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,(n)}(dy)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq\lfloor\log(R/(2\updelta))\rfloor}e^{-k(2-d)}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,(n)}\bigl(B(x,e^{-(k-1)}R)\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq\lfloor\log(R/(2\updelta))\rfloor}e^{-k(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upalpha-\upvarepsilon)}\;,

where the (random) implicit constants depend only on
R
. For all
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
the latter series converges to
0
as
\updelta\to 0
by taking
\upvarepsilon>0
small enough.
Therefore, to conclude, it suffices to show that for each fixed
\updelta>0
, the family
\overline{\mathrm{I}}_{(n),\updelta}
converges uniformly on
B(0,R)
to
\smash{\overline{\mathrm{I}}_{(\infty),\updelta}}
as
n\to\infty
. The fact that this convergence holds pointwise is a direct consequence of the fact that the measure
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,(n)}
converges weakly to
\smash{\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}}
as
n\to\infty
. Hence, it suffices to show that the family
\smash{(\overline{\mathrm{I}}_{(n),\updelta})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}
is relatively compact for the topology of uniform convergence in
B(0,R)
. This follows since for each
\updelta>0
, the mapping
B(0,R)\times B(0,R)\ni(x,y)\mapsto G_{R}(x,y)\overline{\upvartheta}_{d}(x-y)
is uniformly continuous in
B(0,R)\times B(0,R)
, the fact that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,(n)}(B(0,R))<\infty
, and Arzelá–Ascoli theorem.

Finally, the proofs of
(G2)
and
(G3)
rely on similar ideas and are entirely analogous to the proofs of the corresponding properties established in
[
GRV16
, Proposition 2.3]
. Therefore, their proofs are omitted.
∎

The proof of this result is similar to the proof of

, Theorem 2.7]

It remains to prove

[
GRV16
]
since in that paper, the authors leverage on the exponential decay of correlation of the massive GFF. We note that it suffices to check that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for a fixed

\mathbf{P}_{x}(\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)=\infty)=1
. To get the desired result, it is then sufficient to apply Lemma

. Therefore, we take

and our goal is to show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)=\infty

\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{0}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(\uptau_{n})=\infty
,
\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{0}
-almost surely. For all

modulo an additive constant, we have that
\smash{\mathbf{h}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}(n\cdot)-\mathbf{h}_{n}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}}

\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(\uptau_{n})=\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}\bigl([B]_{\uptau_{n}}\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}n^{\upalpha Q}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{n}(0)}\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{n},\upalpha}\bigl(n^{-1}[B]_{\uptau_{n}}\bigr)\;,

where we recall that the measure

\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\upalpha Q}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{n}(0)}=\infty\Bigr)=1\;.

(5.10)

Moreover, we note that for each

\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{0}\Biggl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{n},\upalpha}(n^{-1}[B]_{\uptau_{n}}\bigr)\geq\updelta\Biggr)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{0}\bigl(\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{n},\upalpha}(n^{-1}[B]_{\uptau_{n}})\geq\updelta\bigr)\geq 1-\upvarepsilon\;.

∎

We note that the continuity of

5.2.3
The Liouville Heat Kernel

From the construction of the LBM and from the general theory of time changes of Markov processes, we can deduce some of its properties. First of all, for
d>2
and
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, the LBM
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
is a diffusion process, i.e., a continuous strong Markov process, and it is transient (cf.
[
CF12
, Theorems A.2.12 and 6.2.3]
). Furthermore, thanks to
[
CF12
, Theorem 6.2.1 (i)]
, the LBM is
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
-symmetric, i.e., its transition semigroup
(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t})_{t\geq 0}
given by

\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t}[f](x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{E}_{x}\bigl[f(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t})\bigr]\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}),\;\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

satisfies the following equality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t}[f](x)g(x)\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t}[g](x)f(x)\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dx)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,f,g\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})\;.

(5.13)

We are now ready to prove Proposition
2.5
on the absolute continuity of the semigroup
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
with respect to the measure
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

We will not provide a detailed proof of this result since it is identical to the proof of
[
GRV14
, Theorem 2.5]
. We observe that, since the semigroup
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
is symmetric with respect to
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
, by
[
CF12
, Theorem 4.2.4]
, it suffices to prove that the resolvent of the LBM
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
. We recall that the resolvent family
(\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
associated to the LBM is defined as follows

\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}[f](x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,t}[f](x)dt\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\lambda>0\;,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})\;.

Therefore, it suffices to prove that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(A)=0
, it holds that
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}](x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\lambda>0
. To this end, thanks to (
5.13
), we have that

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}](x)\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dx)=\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(A)\;.

Hence, if
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(A)=0
, it follows that
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}](x)=0
for
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since the measure
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
has full support, this implies the existence of a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
on which the function
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}]
is identically zero. Therefore, the conclusion holds if the mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}](x)
is continuous. By following the proof of
[
GRV14
, Theorem 2.4]
, one can show that the resolvent operator
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\lambda}
maps the set of measurable bounded functions into the set of continuous bounded functions.
More precisely, we observe that the proof of
[
GRV14
, Theorem2.4]
relies on Lemma
5.13
and the fact that, for any fixed
R>0
, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{x\in B(0,R)}\mathbf{E}_{x}\bigl[\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)\bigr]=0\;.

It is easy to see that the uniform limit above holds in any dimension
d>2
. Indeed, fix
R>0
, take any
x\in B(0,R)
, and let
\updelta\in(0,1/2)
. Then, by Lemma
5.14
, we can write

\mathbf{E}_{x}\bigl[\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(t)\bigr]=\int_{B(x,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(x,y)ds\,\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)+\int_{B(x,t^{1/2-\updelta})^{c}}\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(x,y)ds\,\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)\;.

(5.14)

We now proceed to show that both terms on the right-hand side of the above expression converge to
0
as
t\to 0
, uniformly for all
x\in B(0,R)
. Regarding the first term, by Lemma
B.2
and following the same approach as in the proof of Proposition
5.10
, we obtain
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that for any
\upvarepsilon>0
,

\int_{B(x,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(x,y)ds\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)\lesssim\int_{B(x,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)\lesssim\sum_{k\geq\lfloor\lvert\log t\rvert(1/2-\updelta)\rfloor}e^{-k(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upalpha-\upvarepsilon)}\;,

and we note that, for all
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, by choosing
\upvarepsilon>0
sufficiently small, the term on the right-hand side of the above expression converges to
0
as
t\to 0
.
Hence, it remains to control the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.14
). To this end, recalling the explicit expression of the heat kernel for the standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion and applying a change of variables, we obtain that for
\lvert x-y\rvert\geq t^{1/2-\updelta}
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(x,y)ds\lesssim\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\int_{\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{t}}^{\infty}s^{\frac{d}{2}-2}e^{-\frac{s}{2}}ds\lesssim t^{(1/2-\updelta)(2-d)}e^{-c\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{t}}\;.

(5.15)

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,t^{1/2-\updelta})^{c}}e^{-c\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{t}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-ct^{-2\updelta}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}\bigl(B(x,2)\bigr)+\int_{B(x,2)^{c}}e^{-c\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{t}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-ct^{-2\updelta}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}\bigl(B(x,2)\bigr)+t^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-c\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)\;.

(5.16)

Therefore, combining (
5.15
) and (
5.16
), we have that

\int_{B(x,t^{1/2-\updelta})^{c}}\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(x,y)ds\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)\lesssim t^{(1/2-\updelta)(2-d)}\Biggl(e^{-ct^{-2\updelta}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}\bigl(B(x,2)\bigr)+t^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-c\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}(dy)\Biggr)\;,

from which the claim follows since the expression on the right-hand side of the display converges to
0
as
t\to 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.
∎

5.3.1

We denote by

p_{t}(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}(2\uppi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{2t}}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\geq 0,\;\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;.

(5.17)

x
,

and

\mathbf{P}_{x,y,t}
, the process
(B_{s})_{s\in[0,t]}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian bridge of length
t
from
x
to
y
.
We begin with the following key lemma.

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, and
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
. Then,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for all continuous functions
\upeta:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
-almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following equality holds

\int_{0}^{\infty}\upeta(t)\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,t}(x,y)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x,y,t}\bigl[\upeta(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))\bigr]p_{t}(x,y)dt\;.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
RV14b
, Theorem 3.4]
and is included here for completeness. Let
\upeta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a continuous function, and let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, thanks to Lemma
5.14
with
\mathbf{h}
replaced by the field
\overline{\mathbf{h}}
, for all continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\upeta(t)\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,t}(x,y)dt\Biggr)f(y)\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\upeta(t)\Biggr(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,t}(x,y)\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)\Biggr)dt

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Biggl[\int_{0}^{\infty}\upeta(t)f(\mathrm{B}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,t})dt\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Biggl[\int_{0}^{\infty}\upeta\bigl(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\bigr)f(B_{t})d\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Biggl[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x}\bigl[\upeta\bigl(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\bigr)\,|\,\upsigma(B_{t})\bigr]f(B_{t})d\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:StrenghRevuz}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x,y,t}\bigl[\upeta\bigl(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\bigr)\bigr]p_{t}(x,y)dt\Biggr)f(y)\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)\;,

and so the claim follows.
∎

We emphasise that, for
\upchi\geq 0
, by choosing
\mathbb{R}^{+}\ni t\mapsto\upeta(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}t^{\upchi}e^{-t}
in Lemma
5.16
, we obtain precisely the relation (
2.12
).
We are now in a position to state the main result of this section, from which Theorem
B
follows directly.

Proposition 5.17
.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and define

\overline{\upchi}=\overline{\upchi}(\upbeta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\frac{d-2}{2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta}\;.

(5.18)

Then, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}(0)=\infty\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upchi\in[0,\overline{\upchi})\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}(0)<\infty\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upchi>\overline{\upchi}\;,

where we recall that
\smash{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}}
is defined in (
2.11
).

Before proceeding with the rigorous proof of Proposition
5.17
, we provide a formal scaling argument to explain where the exponent
\overline{\upchi}
comes from. Recalling the definition (
2.11
) of
\smash{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}(0)}
, we note that, for all
\upchi\geq 0
, the mapping
[0,\infty)\ni u\mapsto u^{\upchi}e^{-u}
is uniformly bounded by a constant
c=c(\upchi)
. In particular, since the heat kernel
p_{t}(0,0)
is equal to a constant times
t^{-d/2}
, which is integrable over the unbounded interval
(1,\infty)
, the part of the integral in
\smash{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}(0)}
that determines the spectral dimension is the behaviour of the following quantity,

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\;.

(5.19)

By the scale invariance of the law of
\mathbf{h}
modulo an additive constant, we have that
\mathbf{h}^{\sqrt{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}(\sqrt{t}\cdot)-\mathbf{h}_{\sqrt{t}}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}
. In particular, using the coordinate change formula for the measure
\upnu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
(Proposition
5.1
), we obtain that

\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)=\upnu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}\bigl([B]_{t}\bigr)=t^{\upalpha(Q-\upbeta)/2+o_{t}(1)}\upnu_{\mathbf{h}^{\sqrt{t}}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upalpha}\bigl(t^{-1/2}[B]_{t}\bigr)\;,

(5.20)

Therefore, plugging (
5.20
) into (
5.19
) and using the fact that the law of
t^{-1/2}[B]_{t}
does not depend on
t
, we obtian that

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\approx\int_{0}^{1}t^{\upchi\upalpha(Q-\upbeta)/2-d/2+o_{t}(1)}dt\;.

Since
\upalpha(Q-\upbeta)=2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta
, the integral on the right-hand side of the above expression converges (resp. diverges) if
\upchi>\overline{\upchi}
(resp.
\upchi\in[0,\overline{\upchi})
).
However, there are several reasons why this scaling argument is not rigorous. For instance, while it is true that the law of the field
\mathbf{h}^{\sqrt{t}}
does not depend on
t
, this is insufficient for our purposes since we need statements that hold
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, rather than just in distribution.

5.3.2
Computation of the spectral dimension

We now proceed with the rigorous proof of Proposition
5.17
. The proof is divided into two parts: in Lemma
5.18
, we address the case
\upchi\in[0,\overline{\upchi})
, and in Lemma
5.20
, we handle the case
\upchi>\overline{\upchi}
.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and let
\overline{\upchi}
be as defined in (
5.18
). Then, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}(0)=\infty\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upchi\in[0,\overline{\upchi})\;.

Proof.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and consider
\upchi\in[0,\overline{\upchi})
. We begin by observing that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\gtrsim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\leq 1\}}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\;.

Next, for any
t>0
, we introduce the fields

\mathbf{h}^{\sqrt{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}(\sqrt{t}\cdot)-\mathbf{h}_{\sqrt{t}}(0)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt{\overline{\mathbf{h}}}^{\sqrt{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}^{\sqrt{t}}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert\;.

Recall that
\mathbf{h}_{\sqrt{t}}(0)
is centred Gaussian with variance of order
\lvert\log\sqrt{t}\rvert
(Lemma
3.9
), so it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that
\mathbf{h}_{\sqrt{t}}(0)/\lvert\log\sqrt{t}\rvert=o_{t}(1)
as
t\to 0
. By applying the coordinate change formula for
\mathrm{F}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}
(Proposition
5.1
), it follows
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that, for any sufficiently small
\overline{t}\in(0,1)
and for all
t\in(0,\overline{t})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)\leq 1\}}\bigr]

\displaystyle=t^{\upalpha\upchi(Q-\upbeta)/2}e^{\upalpha\upchi\mathbf{h}_{\sqrt{t}}(0)}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,1}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{{\overline{\mathbf{h}}}^{\sqrt{t}},\upalpha}(1))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{F}_{{\overline{\mathbf{h}}}^{\sqrt{t}},\upalpha}(1)t^{\upalpha(Q-\upbeta)/2}e^{\upalpha\mathbf{h}_{\sqrt{t}}(0)}\leq 1\}}\bigr]

\displaystyle\geq t^{\upchi(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)/2+o_{t}(1)}\underbrace{\mathbf{E}_{0,0,1}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{{\overline{\mathbf{h}}}^{\sqrt{t}},\upalpha}(1))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{F}_{{\overline{\mathbf{h}}}^{\sqrt{t}},\upalpha}(1)\leq 1\}}\bigr]}_{\mathrm{Z}_{t}}\;,

where to get the inequality in the second line, we used that
\upbeta<Q
and the fact that
\upalpha(Q-\upbeta)=2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta
. Hence, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\gtrsim\int_{0}^{\overline{t}}t^{\upchi(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)/2-d/2+o_{t}(1)}\mathrm{Z}_{t}dt\;.

(5.21)

We next observe that by the invariance of the law of
\mathbf{h}
modulo an additive constant, the law of
\mathrm{Z}_{t}
does not depend on
t>0
. Moreover, for any
\upvarepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
s_{\upvarepsilon}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Z}_{t}\geq s_{\upvarepsilon})\geq 1-\upvarepsilon^{2}
. Then, by Markov’s inequality, for all
r\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{r}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{Z}_{t}<s_{\upvarepsilon}\}}dt>\upvarepsilon r\Biggr)\leq\frac{1}{\upvarepsilon r}\int_{0}^{r}\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Z}_{t}<s_{\upvarepsilon})dt<\upvarepsilon\;.

By downward continuity of measures, it follows that

\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{Z}_{t}\geq s_{\upvarepsilon}\}}dt\geq\frac{1-\upvarepsilon}{n}\Biggr)\Biggr)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{Z}_{t}\geq s_{\upvarepsilon}\}}dt\geq\frac{1-\upvarepsilon}{n}\Biggr)\geq 1-\upvarepsilon\;.

Hence, with
\mathbb{P}
-probability at least
1-\upvarepsilon
, there are infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{Z}_{t}\geq s_{\upvarepsilon}\}}dt\geq\frac{1-\upvarepsilon}{n}\;.

On that event, by choosing
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
1/n<\overline{t}
, the right-hand side of (
5.21
) is bounded below by

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}t^{\upchi(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)/2-d/2+o_{t}(1)}\mathrm{Z}_{t}dt\geq s_{\upvarepsilon}\int_{\frac{\upvarepsilon}{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}t^{\upchi(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)/2-d/2+o_{t}(1)}dt\to\infty\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ as }n\to\infty\;,

where we used the fact that
-\upchi(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)/2+d/2-1>0
for all
\upchi\in[0,\overline{\upchi})
.
∎

Next, we address the case
\upchi>\overline{\upchi}
. A crucial element in the proof of this case is the following result, which is proved in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, and
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
. Then, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
t\in(0,1]
,

\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t}\subseteq B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]\lesssim t^{n[(1/2-\updelta)(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon]}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upvarepsilon,\;\updelta>0\;,

(5.22)

for some random implicit constant.

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and let
\overline{\upchi}
be as defined in (
5.18
). Then, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha,\upchi}(0)<\infty\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upchi>\overline{\upchi}\;.

Proof.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and consider
\upchi>\overline{\upchi}
. Since the function
[0,\infty)\ni u\mapsto u^{\upchi}e^{-u}
is uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on
\upchi
, it holds that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-d/2}dt<\infty\;.

Therefore, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt<\infty\;.

Hence, from now on, we can restrict our attention to the following quantity

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\;.

(5.23)

By the time-reversal symmetry of the Brownian bridge, the fact that for all
\upchi>0
and
a
,
b>0
it holds that
(a+b)^{\upchi}\lesssim a^{\upchi}+b^{\upchi}
for some implicit constant depending only on
\upchi
, and the monotonicity of the mapping
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\ni t\mapsto\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t)
, one obtains
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that the term in (
5.23
) can be bounded above by a constant multiple of the following sum,

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt+\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\;,

where we note that the two terms in the above display are equal.
Next, we recall that for any
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in[0,t)
, it holds that

\frac{d\mathbf{P}_{x,y,t}}{d\mathbf{P}_{x}}|_{\mathcal{\mathbf{F}}_{s}}=\Biggl(\frac{t}{t-s}\Biggr)^{d/2}\exp\Biggl(\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{2t}-\frac{\lvert B_{s}-y\rvert^{2}}{2(t-s)}\Biggr)\;,

Therefore, invoking this absolute continuity result, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,0,t}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2)}\bigr]p_{t}(0,0)dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}e^{-\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2)}\bigr]t^{-d/2}dt\;.

(5.24)

For any
\updelta>0
, using again the fact that for each
\upchi>0
, the function
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\ni u\mapsto u^{\upchi}e^{-u}
is uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on
\upchi
, we observe that the term on the right-hand side of (
5.24
) can be estimated from above by a constant multiple of

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t/2}\subseteq B(0,(t/2)^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]t^{-d/2}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\bigl([B]_{t/2}\not\subseteq B(0,(t/2)^{1/2-\updelta})\bigr)t^{-d/2}dt\;.

(5.25)

The second term in (
5.25
) is easily controlled since, by the reflection principle, for each
\updelta>0
and some
c>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\bigl([B]_{t/2}\not\subseteq B(0,(t/2)^{1/2-\updelta})\bigr)\lesssim e^{-ct^{-2\updelta}}\;.

Hence, plugging this estimate into the second term in (
5.25
), we readily get that it is finite for any
\updelta>0
.
For the first term in (
5.25
), let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\upchi\in[n-1,n)
. By concavity of the map
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\ni u\mapsto u^{\upchi/n}
and Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t/2}\subseteq B(0,(t/2)^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]\leq\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t/2}\subseteq B(0,(t/2)^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]^{\upchi/n}\;.

(5.26)

Invoking Lemma
5.19
, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
, we find
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t/2))^{\upchi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t/2}\subseteq B(0,(t/2)^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]\lesssim t^{\upchi[(1/2-\updelta)(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon]}\;.

In particular, for any
\upchi>\overline{\upchi}
one has
\upchi(2+\upalpha^{2}-\upalpha\upbeta)/2-d/2>-1
. Hence, by choosing
\upvarepsilon
,
\updelta>0
small enough (depending on
\upchi
,
\upalpha
,
\upbeta
), we obtain that also the first term in (
5.25
) is finite. Therefore, this concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.17
.

The proof follows directly by combining Lemma
5.18
with Lemma
5.20
.
∎

6.1
Tightness of the recentred process

Fix

\upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})

Proposition 6.1
.

For
d>2
even, the collection of processes
(\mathrm{S}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}
is tight in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with the local uniform metric.

Remark 6.2
.

In dimension
d=2
, the circle average process of the whole-plane GFF is a two-sided Brownian motion. In this case, it follows from
[
DMS21
, Proposition 4.13]
that the collection of processes
\smash{(\mathrm{S}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}}
converges weakly as
b\to\infty
to a process
\smash{(\widetilde{B}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
, defined as follows. Let
\smash{(B^{1}_{t})_{t\geq 0}}
be a standard Brownian motion, and let
\smash{(B^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}}
be a Brownian motion conditioned to stay above the line
t\mapsto-(Q-\upbeta)t
for all
t>0
. Then, we let

\widetilde{B}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}B^{1}_{t}\;,\qquad&\forall t\geq 0\;,\\
B^{2}_{-t}\;,\qquad&\forall t<0\;.\end{cases}

(6.1)

The proof of Proposition
6.1
relies on the observation that the spherical average process can be viewed, in a loose sense, as a smoothed version of a two-sided Brownian motion (see Theorem
A
and Figure
1
). In particular, to prove Proposition
6.1
, we first introduce a family of processes
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}}
for which we can prove tightness by leveraging a suitable invariance in law. We then show that the tightness of
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}}
implies the tightness of
\smash{(\mathrm{S}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}}
.

We introduce here some notation that will be used throughout this section. Recalling the representation (
2.4
) of the spherical average process, we introduce the function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

g(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}(d-2)e^{2s}(1-e^{2s})^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,s\in\mathbb{R}\;.

We define the process
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
by letting

\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)\bigl(\widetilde{B}_{s+t}-\widetilde{B}_{s}\bigr)ds\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\;,

(6.2)

where
(\widetilde{B}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is defined as in (
6.1
). Furthermore, for
i\in[\mathfrak{c}_{d}]
, let
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}^{(i)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
denote the
i
-th derivative of
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
. A straightforward computation yields that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{t}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\int_{-\infty}^{0}g^{(i)}(s)\widetilde{B}_{s+t}ds\;,\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\;,\;\forall\,i\in[\mathfrak{c}_{d}-1]\;,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(\mathfrak{c}_{d})}_{t}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}+1}g^{(\mathfrak{c}_{d}-1)}(0)\widetilde{B}_{t}+(-1)^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}g^{(\mathfrak{c}_{d})}(s)\widetilde{B}_{s+t}ds\;,\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\;.

For
b>0
, we introduce the stopping times
\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}
and
\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}
by letting

\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\inf\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\widetilde{B}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t=-b\bigr\}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\inf\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t=-b\bigr\}\;.

(6.4)

For each
b>0
and
i\in[\mathfrak{c}_{d}]
, we also define recentred process
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and its
i
-th derivative
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{b,t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
by letting

\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}+t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{b,t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}+t}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\;.

(6.5)

The main reason why it is convenient to work with the process
(\widetilde{B}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is that it is invariant under the following random recentring

\bigl(\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+t}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}}\bigr)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\bigl(\widetilde{B}_{t}\bigr)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\;.

(6.6)

The invariance in law (
6.6
) follows directly from the definition of the process
\smash{(\widetilde{B}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
or from
[
DMS21
, Proposition 4.13]
.
Moreover, from the definition (
6.2
) of
\smash{(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
and from (
6.3
), it is readily seen that (
6.6
) implies that

\bigl(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}}\bigr)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{0}\bigr)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,i\in[\mathfrak{c}_{d}]_{0}\;,

(6.7)

where here and in what follows, with a slight abuse of notation, we use the superscript
(0)
to denote the process itself.

For
b
,
c>0
, we define the moduli of continuity
\smash{\upomega_{b,c}}
,
\smash{\widetilde{\upomega}_{b,c}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}
of

and

, respectively, by letting

\upomega_{b,c}(\updelta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\sup_{s,t\in[-c,c],\,\lvert t-s\rvert\leq\updelta}\lvert\mathrm{S}^{(i)}_{b,t}-\mathrm{S}^{(i)}_{b,s}\rvert\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\upomega}_{b,c}(\updelta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\sup_{s,t\in[-c,c],\,\lvert t-s\rvert\leq\updelta}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{b,t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{b,s}\rvert\;.

(6.8)

Similarly, for
c>0

of
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t})_{t\in[-c,c]}
by letting

6.1.1
The collection
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}
is tight

We now proceed to show that the collection of processes
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}
is tight.

Lemma 6.3
.

For
d>2
even, the collection of processes
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}
is tight in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with the local uniform metric.

The proof of Lemma
6.3
relies on the fact that, with high probability and uniformly for
b>0
,
\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}
and
\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}
are close to each other.

Lemma 6.4
.

For
b>0
let
\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}
and
\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}
as defined in (
6.4
). Then, it holds that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{b>0}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\lvert\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\rvert\geq\lambda\bigr)=0\;.

Proof.

For
\lambda\geq 0
, we have the following trivial inclusion

\bigl\{\lvert\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\rvert\geq\lambda\bigr\}\subseteq\bigl\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\;,

and so we can focus on finding suitable upper bounds for the probabilities of the two events on the right-hand side of the above display.

Step 1:
We start by bounding the probability of the event
\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\geq\lambda\}
. To this end, for any
c>0
, we note that

\bigl\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\subseteq\bigl\{\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}\rvert\geq c\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\;.

(6.10)

To see why this is true, it is more convenient to consider the complement of the given relation and prove that the intersection of the complements of the two events on the right-hand side is contained within the complement of the event on the left-hand side. Indeed, noting that

\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}\rvert<c\hskip 10.00002pt\implies\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}<\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}\;,

and combining this fact with the complement of the second event on the right-hand side of (
6.10
), we readily obtain that it must hold that
\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}<\lambda
.

Hence, our task now is to bound the two probabilities appearing on the right-hand side of (
6.10
). By recalling the definition (
6.2
) of
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, we can write

\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}\rvert=\Biggl\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)(\widetilde{B}_{s+\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}})ds-\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)\widetilde{B}_{s}ds\Biggr\rvert\;.

Therefore, thanks to the triangle inequality, the union bound, and the invariance in law (
6.6
), we have that

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{\uptau_{b+c}}-\widetilde{B}_{\uptau_{b+c}}\rvert\geq c\bigr)\lesssim\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\Biggl\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)\widetilde{B}_{s}ds\Biggr\rvert\geq\frac{c}{2}\Biggr)\;.

(6.11)

Moreover, using the strong Markov property of the Brownian motion, we have that

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr)=\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\inf_{s\in[0,\lambda]}\bigl(\widetilde{B}_{s}-(Q-\upbeta)s\bigr)\geq-c\Biggr)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{B}_{\lambda}\geq(Q-\upbeta)\lambda-c\bigr)\lesssim e^{-a((Q-\upbeta)\lambda-c)^{2}/\lambda}\;,

(6.12)

for some constant
a>0
.
Therefore, choosing
c=c(\lambda)=\sqrt{\lambda}
, using the union bound, and estimates (
6.11
), (
6.12
), we readily get that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{b>0}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr)=0\;.

Step 2:
We now have to bound the probability of the event
\{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\geq\lambda\}
. To this end, we note that, for any
c>0
, it holds that

\bigl\{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\subseteq\Bigl\{\inf_{t\leq\widetilde{\uptau}_{b-c}}\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t\leq-b\Bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b-c}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\;.

(6.13)

Also here, to see why this is true, it is more convenient to consider the complement of the given relation. Indeed, noting that

\inf_{t\leq\widetilde{\uptau}_{b-c}}\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t>-b\hskip 10.00002pt\implies\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}>\widetilde{\uptau}_{b+c}\;,

and combining this fact with the complement of the second event on the right-hand side of (
6.13
), we readily obtain that it must hold that
\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}<\lambda
.

Now, writing
\widetilde{\uptau}
as a shorthand for
\widetilde{\uptau}_{b-c}
, we note that the first event on the right-hand side of (
6.13
) is contained in the following union of events

\underbrace{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Biggl\{\sup_{t\in[\widetilde{\uptau}-n,\widetilde{\uptau}-(n-1)]}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}-\widetilde{B}_{t}\rvert\geq(\log n)^{2}+c/2\Biggr\}}_{\mathrm{E}_{1}}\cup\underbrace{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Biggl\{\inf_{t\in[\widetilde{\uptau}-n,\widetilde{\uptau}-(n-1)]}(\widetilde{B}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t)<(\log n)^{2}-(b-c/2)\Biggr\}}_{\mathrm{E}_{2}}\;.

We now proceed to bound the probabilities of the events
\mathrm{E}_{1}
and
\mathrm{E}_{2}
. We start by noting that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\sup_{t\in[\widetilde{\uptau}-n,\widetilde{\uptau}-(n-1)]}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{t}-\widetilde{B}_{t}\rvert=\sup_{t\in[-n,-(n-1)]}\left\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)(\widetilde{B}_{s+t+\widetilde{\uptau}}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}+t})ds-\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)\widetilde{B}_{s}ds\right\rvert\;.

By using the invariance in law (
6.6
), it holds that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)(\widetilde{B}_{s+t+\widetilde{\uptau}}-\widetilde{B}_{\widetilde{\uptau}+t})ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)(\widetilde{B}_{s+t}-\widetilde{B}_{t})ds\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\;.

Therefore, applying the triangle inequality and the union bound, we obtain that the probability of the event
\mathrm{E}_{1}
is bounded from above, up to a finite multiplicative constant, by

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\sup_{t\in[-n,-(n-1)]}\left\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(s)(\widetilde{B}_{s+t}-\widetilde{B}_{t})ds\right\rvert\geq(\log n)^{2}/2+c/4\Biggr)\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}e^{-a((\log n)^{2}+c)}\;,

(6.14)

for some constant
a>0
and where the implicit constant is independent of everything else. The upper bound on the right-hand side of (
6.14
) follows from the Gaussian tail bound.

On the other hand, using once again the invariance in law (
6.6
) and recalling that
\widetilde{\uptau}
is a shorthand for
\widetilde{\uptau}_{b-c}
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\inf_{t\in[\widetilde{\uptau}-n,\widetilde{\uptau}-(n-1)]}(\widetilde{B}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t)\leq(\log n)^{2}-(b-c/2)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\inf_{t\in[-n,-(n-1)]}(\widetilde{B}_{t}-(Q-\upbeta)t)\leq(\log n)^{2}-c/2\;.

In particular, using the union bound, we obtain that the probability of the event
\mathrm{E}_{2}
is bounded from above, up to a finite multiplicative constant, by

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\inf_{t\in[-n,-(n-1)]}\widetilde{B}_{t}\leq-(Q-\upbeta)(n-1)+(\log n)^{2}-c/2\Biggr)\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}e^{-a(n+c)}\;,

(6.15)

for some constant
a>0
and where the implicit constant is independent of everything else. Also here, the upper bound on the right-hand side of (
6.15
) follows from the Gaussian tail bound.

Furthermore, proceeding exactly as in (
6.12
), we have that

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{\uptau}_{b-c}\geq\lambda\bigr)\lesssim e^{-a((Q-\upbeta)\lambda-c)^{2}/\lambda}\;,

(6.16)

for some constant
a>0
.
Finally, the conclusion follows thanks to estimates (
6.14
), (
6.15
), and (
6.16
) by choosing
c=c(\lambda)=\sqrt{\lambda}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
6.3
.

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

By recalling the definitions in (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), and applying the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, to prove the tightness of
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{b,\cdot})_{b>0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with the local uniform metric, it suffices to verify that for all
c>0
and any
\upvarepsilon>0
, it holds that

\lim_{\updelta\to 0}\limsup_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upomega}_{b,c}(\updelta)>\upvarepsilon\bigr)=0\;.

To this end, we note that for
c>0
and
\widetilde{c}>c
, on the event
\smash{\{[\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-c,\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}+c]\subseteq[\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{c},\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+\widetilde{c}]\}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\upomega}_{b,c}(\updelta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\sup_{s,t\in[\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-c,\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}+c],\,\lvert t-s\rvert\leq\updelta}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{s}\rvert\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\sup_{s,t\in[\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{c},\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+\widetilde{c}],\,\lvert t-s\rvert\leq\updelta}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{s}\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\sup_{s,t\in[-\widetilde{c},\widetilde{c}],\,\lvert t-s\rvert\leq\updelta}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+s}\rvert\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{\mathfrak{c}_{d}}\sup_{s,t\in[-\widetilde{c},\widetilde{c}],\,\lvert t-s\rvert\leq\updelta}\lvert\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{t}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}^{(i)}_{s}\rvert\;,

where, in order to get the last equality, we used the invariance in law (
6.7
).
Therefore, this fact implies that, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upomega}_{b,c}(\updelta)>\upvarepsilon\bigr)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upomega}_{\widetilde{c}}(\updelta)>\upvarepsilon\bigr)+\mathbb{P}\bigl([\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-c,\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}+c]\not\subseteq[\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}-\widetilde{c},\widetilde{\uptau}_{b}+\widetilde{c}]\bigr)\;.

(6.17)

The conclusion follows by first taking
\limsup_{b\to\infty}
, then
\lim_{\updelta\to 0}
, and finally
\limsup_{\widetilde{c}\to\infty}
in the above expression. Indeed, since for any fixed
\widetilde{c}>0
it holds that
\lim_{\updelta\to 0}\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\upomega}_{\widetilde{c}}(\updelta)>\upvarepsilon)=0
, it suffices to apply Lemma
6.4

∎

Lemma 6.5

For

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{s}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{s}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(r-s)\bigl(B_{r}-\widetilde{B}_{r}\bigr)dr-\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{0}g(r)\bigl(B_{r}-\widetilde{B}_{r}\bigr)dr}_{\mathrm{D}}\;.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\sup_{s\geq t}\lvert\mathrm{S}_{s}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{s}+\mathrm{D}\rvert>1\Biggr)=0\;.

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\limsup_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b+c}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}>\lambda\bigr)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\limsup_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b-c}>\lambda\bigr)=0\;.

Step 1:

\{\upsigma_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\geq\lambda\}\subseteq\Biggl\{\sup_{s\geq\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}}\lvert\mathrm{S}_{s}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{s}+\mathrm{D}\rvert>1\Biggr\}\cup\bigl\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b+c}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\;.

6.18

6.19

\limsup_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\sup_{s\geq\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}}\lvert\mathrm{S}_{s}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{s}+\mathrm{D}\rvert>1\Biggr)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\limsup_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b+c}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}\geq\lambda\bigr)=0\;,

where here we implicitly used the fact that

Step 2:
We now focus on bounding the probability of the event
\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\upsigma_{b}\geq\lambda\}

\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\upsigma_{b}\geq\lambda\}\subseteq\Biggl\{\inf_{s\in[0,T]}\bigl(\mathrm{S}_{s}-(Q-\upbeta)s\bigr)\leq-b\Biggr\}\cup\Biggl\{\sup_{s\geq T}\lvert\mathrm{S}_{s}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{s}+\mathrm{D}\rvert>1\Biggr\}\cup\bigl\{\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b-c}\geq\lambda\bigr\}\;.

(6.21)

Also here, to see why this is true, it is more convenient to consider the complement of the given relation. Indeed, we note that by proceeding analogously to the previous step, one can easily verify that on the intersection of the complement of first two events on the right-hand side of (

\inf_{t\in[0,\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b-c}]}\bigl(\mathrm{S}_{s}-(Q-\upbeta)s\bigr)\geq-b\hskip 20.00003pt\implies\hskip 20.00003pt\upsigma_{b}\geq\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b-c}\;.

Combining this fact with the fact that we are on the complement of the third event on the right-hand side of (
6.21
), we readily obtain that
\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\upsigma_{b}<\lambda
. Therefore, the desired result follows by observing that, thanks to (
6.18

6.19

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\sup_{s\geq T}\lvert\mathrm{S}_{s}-\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{s}+\mathrm{D}\rvert>1\Biggr)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\limsup_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b}-\widetilde{\upsigma}_{b-c}\geq\lambda\bigr)=0\;,

\displaystyle\upomega_{b,c}(\updelta)

started from the origin at time 0. Moreover, let
\uptau_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,+}
(resp.
\uptau_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,-}
) be the first positive (resp. negative) time that
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\cdot}
exits the ball
B(0,1)
. Let
\mathrm{U}
be a point sampled uniformly at random from the interval
[\uptau_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,-},\uptau_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,+}]
. For
b>0
, we let

\upsigma_{b}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\inf\bigl\{s\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\mathbf{h}_{e^{-s}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}})-Qs=-b\bigr\}\;,

and we consider the conformal automorphism
\smash{\upphi_{b}(x)=e^{-\upsigma_{b}}x}+\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}}
. Thanks to
[
BP24
, Corollary 3.17]
, we know that if we condition on the Brownian motion modulo time change, then the conditional law of
\mathbf{h}
near
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}}
locally looks like a whole-space LGF plus an
\upalpha
-log singularity at
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}}
. Therefore, by Proposition
6.9
, we have the following joint convergence in law as
b\to\infty
,

\bigl(\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi_{b}+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}_{b}\rvert+b,\,e^{\upsigma_{b}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}+e^{-\upalpha b}\cdot}-\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}})\bigr)\overset{\law}{\longrightarrow}(\mathbf{h}^{\star},\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\upalpha})\;.

(6.28)

We note that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the total variation distance between
(\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\upalpha},\mathrm{U}+e^{-\upalpha b}t)
and
(\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\upalpha},\mathrm{U})
tends to
0
as
b\to\infty
.
This is a consequence of the fact that the conditional law of
\mathrm{U}
given
(\mathbf{h},B)
is that of a uniform random variable.
Hence, by defining
\upsigma_{b,t}
as follows

\upsigma_{b,t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\inf\bigl\{s\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\mathbf{h}_{e^{-s}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}+e^{-\upalpha b}t})-Qs=-b\bigr\}\;,

and by considering the conformal automorphism
\smash{\upphi_{b,t}(x)=e^{-\upsigma_{b,t}}x}+\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}+e^{-\upalpha b}t}
, we get that

\bigl(\mathbf{h}\circ\upphi_{b,t}+Q\log\lvert\upphi^{\prime}_{b,t}\rvert+b,e^{\upsigma_{b,t}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}+e^{-\upalpha b}t+e^{-\upalpha b}\cdot}-\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha,\mathrm{U}+e^{-\upalpha b}t})\bigr)\overset{\law}{\longrightarrow}(\mathbf{h}^{\star},\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\upalpha})\;,

(6.29)

and this it sufficient to conclude. Indeed, the couple on the left side of (
6.29
) is obtained from the couple on the left side of (
6.28
) by translating time for the LBM by
t
, translating space so that the position of the LBM at time
t
is mapped to the origin, and re-scaling to get the usual normalisation for the field. Hence, by (
6.28
), the joint law of the left sides of (
6.29
) and (
6.28
) converges to the joint law of
(\mathbf{h}^{\star},\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\upalpha})
and the field/process pair obtained by translating time by
t
, translating space by
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{*}\!,\upalpha,t}
, and re-scaling to get the usual normalisation for the field. Therefore, the conclusion follows since by (
6.29
), the second field/process pair has the same law as
(\mathbf{h}^{\star},\mathrm{B}_{\mathbf{h}^{\star}\!,\upalpha})
.
∎

Appendix A
Some basic estimates for GMC measures

In this appendix, we collect some basic estimates for GMC measures. We recall that for each
\upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})
, the collection of regularised GMC measures
(\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma,(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined in (
5.9
). Furthermore, with a slight abuse of notation, we denote by
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma,(\infty)}
the limiting measure
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for any
r\in(0,1]
that

r^{(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\upbeta\upgamma)+\upvarepsilon}\lesssim\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot-x\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,r)\bigr)\lesssim r^{(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\upbeta\upgamma)-\upvarepsilon}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upvarepsilon>0\;,

for some random implicit constants.

Proof.

The proof of this result is standard and follows from a union bound over dyadic scales, and the coordinate change formula (Proposition
3.10
).
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})
, and
R>0
. It holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, all
x\in B(0,R)
, and any
r\in(0,1]
that

\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma,(n)}\bigl(B(x,r)\bigr)\lesssim r^{(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma)-\upvarepsilon}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upvarepsilon>0\;,

for some random implicit constant.

Proof.

For
d=2
, this fact is proved in
[
GRV16
, Theorem 2.2]
, and the proof easily generalises to any dimension
d>2
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})
,
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and
R>0
. It holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for all
x\in B(0,R)
, and any
r\in(0,1]
that

\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,r)\bigr)\lesssim r^{(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{2d})\upgamma)-\upvarepsilon}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upvarepsilon>0\;,

for some random implicit constant.

Proof.

For
\upbeta\leq 0
this is an immediate consequence of Lemma
A.2
.
We fix
\upbeta\in(0,Q)
and, for simplicity, we take
R=1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Sigma_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B(0,1)
be the lattice
2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
restricted to the ball
B(0,1)
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the annulus
A_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}B(0,2^{-k})\setminus B(0,2^{-(k+1)})
.
By using the coordinate change formula (Proposition
3.10
), we have that for all
n\geq 2
,
k\in[n-2]_{0}
, and any
x\in\Sigma_{n}\cap A_{k}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)

\displaystyle=2^{-k\upgamma(Q-\upbeta)}e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k\upgamma(Q-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}))}e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k}}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)\;.

where, by the scale invariance of the law of
\mathbf{h}
modulo additive constant,
\mathbf{h}^{k}(\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}(2^{-k}\cdot)-\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}
. Moreover, the last inequality in the previous display follows from the fact that for all
n\geq 2
,
k\in[n-2]_{0}
, and any
x\in\Sigma_{n}\cap A_{k}
, the ball
B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})
is at distance at most
1/4
from the origin, and so the
\upbeta
-
\log
singularity can be bounded above by a finite constant. Then, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
, by letting
q=(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})/\upgamma
, which is strictly less than
2d/\upgamma^{2}
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\max_{x\in\Sigma_{n}\cap A_{k}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\geq 2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)},\;e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}\leq 2^{n\upvarepsilon/2}\Bigr)

(A.1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{x\in\Sigma_{n}\cap A_{k}}2^{(n-k)[q(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma)]}2^{-qn\upvarepsilon/2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k}}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)^{q}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(n-k)[q(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma)+d]}2^{-qn\upvarepsilon/2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k}}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)^{q}\bigr]

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(n-k)[d+q(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma)-\upxi_{\upgamma}(q)]}2^{-nq\upvarepsilon/2}\leq 2^{-nq\upvarepsilon/2}\;,

where we used Markov’s inequality, and the fact that thanks to
[
BP24
, Theorem 3.26]
, the multifractal spectrum of GMC measures
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}
is given by
\smash{\upxi_{\upgamma}(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}(d+\upgamma^{2}/2)q-\upgamma^{2}q^{2}/2}
. Moreover, since
\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)
is centred Gaussian with variance of order
k
(see Lemma
3.9
), the probability that
\smash{e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}>2^{n\upvarepsilon/2}}
is summable over
n\geq 2
and
k\in[n-2]_{0}
. Therefore, combining this fact with the bound in (
A.1
), we obtain that

\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\max_{x\in\Sigma_{n}\cap A_{k}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\geq 2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}\Bigr)<\infty\;.

Hence, thanks to Borel–Cantelli lemma, we get that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for all
n\geq 2
and
k\in[n-2]_{0}
, it holds that

\max_{x\in\Sigma_{n}\cap A_{k}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\lesssim 2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}\;,

(A.2)

for some random implicit constant. Thanks to Lemma
A.1
, we also have that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it holds that

\upmu_{\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert,\upgamma}\bigl(B(0,2^{-n})\bigr)\lesssim 2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-(\upbeta\vee\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}\;,

(A.3)

Therefore, the conclusion follows by combining (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) and to the fact that for any
r\in(0,1]
, and
x\in B(0,1)
, there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
2^{-(n+1)}<r\leq 2^{-n}
, and
B(x,r)
is contained in a finite numbers of balls of the form
B(z,2^{-n})
with
z\in\Sigma_{n}
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\upgamma\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})
. It holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for any
s\in(0,1]
, any
r\in[0,s]
, and all
x\in B(0,s)
that

\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,r)\bigr)\lesssim s^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon}r^{(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma)-\upvarepsilon}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upvarepsilon>0\;,

for some random implicit constant.

Proof.

For
k\leq n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, by using the coordinate change formula (Proposition
3.10
), we have that for all
x\in B(0,2^{-k})
, it holds that

\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}2^{-k\upgamma Q}e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k}}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)\;,

where, by the scale invariance of the law of
\mathbf{h}
modulo additive constant,
\mathbf{h}^{k}(\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}(2^{-k}\cdot)-\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm law}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}
. We let
\smash{\Sigma_{k,n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B(0,2^{-k})}
be the lattice
2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
restricted to the ball
B(0,2^{-k})
. For any
\upvarepsilon>0
, by letting
q=\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}/\upgamma
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\max_{x\in\Sigma_{k,n}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\geq 2^{-k(\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)},\;e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}\leq 2^{(k+n)\upvarepsilon/2}\Bigr)

(A.4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\max_{x\in\Sigma_{k,n}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k},\upgamma}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)\geq 2^{(k-n)(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma)+(k+n)\upvarepsilon/2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\Sigma_{k,n}}2^{-(k+n)q\upvarepsilon/2}2^{(n-k)(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma)+(k+n)\upvarepsilon/2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\mathbf{h}^{k},\upgamma}\bigl(B(2^{k}x,2^{k-n})\bigr)^{q}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(k+n)q\upvarepsilon/2}2^{(n-k)[d+q(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma)-\upxi_{\upgamma}(q)]}=2^{-(k+n)q\upvarepsilon/2}\;,

where we used Markov’s inequality, and the fact that thanks to
[
BP24
, Theorem 3.26]
, the multifractal spectrum of GMC measures
\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}
is given by
\smash{\upxi_{\upgamma}(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}(d+\upgamma^{2}/2)q-\upgamma^{2}q^{2}/2}
. Moreover, since
\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)
is centred Gaussian with variance of order
k
(see Lemma
3.9
), the probability that
\smash{e^{\upgamma\mathbf{h}_{2^{-k}}(0)}>2^{(k+n)\upvarepsilon/2}}
is summable over
k\leq n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Therefore, combining this fact with the bound in (
A.4
), we get that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\max_{x\in\Sigma_{k,n}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\geq 2^{-k(\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}\Bigr)<\infty\;.

Hence, thanks to Borel–Cantelli lemma, we get that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for all
k\leq n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it holds that

\max_{x\in\Sigma_{k,n}}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upgamma}\bigl(B(x,2^{-n})\bigr)\lesssim 2^{-k(\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}2^{-n(d+\upgamma^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma-\upvarepsilon)}\;,

for some random implicit constant. This is sufficient to conclude since for any
s\in(0,1]
,
r\in(0,s]
, and
x\in B(0,s)
, there exist
k\leq n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
2^{-(k+1)}<s\leq 2^{-k}
,
2^{-(n+1)}<r\leq 2^{-(n+1)}
, and
B(x,r)
is contained in a finite numbers of balls of the form
B(z,2^{-n})
with
z\in\Sigma_{n,k}
.
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
5.19

Throughout this appendix, we fix
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
, and we define the field

\overline{\mathbf{h}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{h}-\upbeta\log\lvert\cdot\rvert\;.

For
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
, we denote by
p_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}}(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
the transition probability density of the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion started from
0
, i.e.,

\mathbf{P}_{0}(B_{s_{1}}\in dx_{1},\;\ldots B_{s_{n}}\in dx_{n})=p_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}}(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\;.

Furthermore, for
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define the function
\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by letting

\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny\rm def}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(x,y)ds\;.

(B.1)

Lemma B.1
.

For
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\geq 0
, and
\updelta>0
it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t}\subseteq B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]\lesssim\int_{(B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta}))^{n}}\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{t}(0,x_{1})\cdots\mathcal{G}_{t}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\bigr)\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{n})\cdots\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{1})\;,

(B.2)

where the implicit constant only depends on
n
.

Proof.

By an immediate generalisation of Lemma
5.14
, we observe that for all
t\geq 0
, the left-hand side of (
B.2
) is bounded above by a constant (only depending on
n
) times

\int_{(B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta}))^{n}}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s_{1}}^{t}\cdots\int_{s_{n-1}}^{t}p_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}}(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})ds_{n}\cdots ds_{1}\Biggr)\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{n})\cdots\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{1})\;.

For
0<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{n}
, we have that the following decomposition holds

p_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}}(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=p_{s_{1}}(0,x_{1})p_{s_{2}-s_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})\cdots p_{s_{n}-s_{n-1}}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\;,

and so, by performing the change of variables
s_{j}\mapsto s_{j}-s_{j-1}
for all

, the desired result follows readily.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
B.1
, in order to prove Lemma
5.19
, is suffices to estimate the quantity on the right-hand of (
B.2
). We start with the following lemma.

Lemma B.2
.

For
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, consider the function
\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y)\lesssim\lvert y-x\rvert^{2-d}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;.

(B.3)

Proof.

For
t\geq 0
and
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have that

\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\infty}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(d/2-1)}{2\uppi^{d/2}}\lvert y-x\rvert^{2-d}\;,

where the last equality in the above display follows from a simple computation (see e.g.
[
MP10
, Theorem 3.33]
).
∎

Now, combining (
B.2
) with (
B.3
), it suffices to estimate and get the “right asymptotic” as
t\to 0
of the following quantity

\int_{B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{2-d}\cdots\int_{B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\lvert x_{n}-x_{n-1}\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{n})\cdots\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{1})\;.

To this end, we start with the following lemma.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
d>2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, and
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
. It holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for any
r\in(0,1]
and all

\int_{B(0,r)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)\lesssim r^{(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\upvarepsilon>0\;,

(B.4)

for some random implicit constant.

Proof.

For
\upbeta<0
the result is a straightforward consequence of Lemma
A.1
. Hence, we can focus on the case
\upbeta\in(0,Q)
.
In what follows, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

For

into four different regions as depicted in Figure

.

from

\upvarepsilon>0

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,\lvert x\rvert/2)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{A(x,e^{-k}\lvert x\rvert/2,e^{-(k+1)}\lvert x\rvert/2)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(e^{-k}\lvert x\rvert)^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}\bigl(B(x,e^{-k}\lvert x\rvert/2)\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{2-d-\upalpha\upbeta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-k(2-d)}\upmu_{\mathbf{h},\upalpha}\bigl(B(x,e^{-k}\lvert x\rvert/2)\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-k[(d+\upalpha^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upalpha)-\upvarepsilon]}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}\leq r^{(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}\;,

where here we used the fact that
d+\upalpha^{2}/2-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upalpha>0
, for all
\upalpha\in(0,2)
.

Region 3:
The third region consists in the green set
B(\midpoint(0,x),2\lvert x\rvert)\setminus B(0,\lvert x\rvert/2)\setminus B(x,\lvert x\rvert/2)
. In this case, using the fact that all points in this region are at distance at least
\lvert x\rvert/2
from
x
, and using Lemma
A.1
, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
small enough, we have
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\displaystyle\int_{B(\midpoint(0,x),2\lvert x\rvert)\setminus B(0,\lvert x\rvert/2)\setminus B(x,\lvert x\rvert/2)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}\bigl(B(0,2\lvert x\rvert)\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{2-d}\lvert x\rvert^{(d+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq r^{(2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}\;.

Region 4:
The fourth region consists in the grey set
B(0,r)\setminus B(\midpoint(0,x),2\lvert x\rvert)
. In this case, using Lemma
A.1
, we get that, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
small enough, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)\setminus B(\midpoint(0,x),2\lvert x\rvert)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{-\log(\lvert x\rvert/(2r))-1}\int_{A(x,2e^{-k}r,2e^{-(k+1)}r)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dy)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{-\log(\lvert x\rvert/(2r))-1}(e^{-k}r)^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}\bigl(B(0,\lvert x\rvert+2e^{-k}r)\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{-\log(\lvert x\rvert/(2r))-1}(e^{-k}r)^{2-d}(\lvert x\rvert+2e^{-k}r)^{(d+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{-\log(\lvert x\rvert/(2r))-1}(e^{-k}r)^{2-d}(e^{-k}r)^{(d+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{(d+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta)-\upvarepsilon}\;,

where to get the penultimate inequality, we used the fact that
\lvert x\rvert\lesssim e^{-k}r
for all
k\leq-\log(\lvert x\rvert/(2r))-1
, and in the last inequality we used the fact that
2+\upalpha^{2}/2-\upalpha\upbeta>0
, for all
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
.

Therefore, the conclusion follows by the arbitrariness of
x\in B(0,r)
and by combining the four bounds in the different regions.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
5.19
.

Proof of Lemma
5.19
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
Q>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, and
\upbeta\in(-\infty,Q)
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\upvarepsilon>0
,
\updelta>0
, and
t\in(0,1]
, we have
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0}\bigl[(\mathrm{F}_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(t))^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{[B]_{t}\subseteq B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})\}}\bigr]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\overset{\eqref{eq:boundChiMoment}}{\lesssim}\int_{(B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta}))^{n}}\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{t}(0,x_{1})\cdots\mathcal{G}_{t}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\bigr)\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{n})\cdots\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\overset{\eqref{eq:trivialBoundGreen}}{\lesssim}\int_{B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{2-d}\cdots\int_{B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})}\lvert x_{n}-x_{n-1}\rvert^{2-d}\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{n})\cdots\upmu_{\overline{\mathbf{h}},\upalpha}(dx_{1})\;.

(B.5)

Thanks to Lemma
B.3
, since
x_{n-1}
is sampled inside the ball
B(0,t^{1/2-\updelta})
, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
, the inner most integral in (
B.5
) can be bounded above
\mathbb{P}

for some random implicit constant. By applying the same techniques
n-1

Testing the use of a biomarker (PlGF) to evaluate superimposed preeclampsia in pregnant women with chronic hypertension (B.I.P.E.S.): a randomized clinical trial study protocol
Authors: Rodolfo Rosa Japecanga, Juliana da-Costa-Santos, Guilherme Moraes Nobrega, Ana Beatriz Sousa, Fernando Guimarães, Fernanda G Surita, José Paulo de Siqueira Guida, Maria Laura Costa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPlacental growth factor (PlGF) is a promising biomarker for differential diagnosis between worsening chronic hypertension (CH) and superimposed preeclampsia (SPE) after 20 weeks of gestation. The aim of this study is to compare maternal and perinatal outcomes and costs in suspected cases of SPE, evaluated with and without the use of PlGF, for clinical management in women with CH.MethodsRandomized Clinical Trial (RCT). Pregnant women with CH and suspected SPE (due to worsening blood pressure control, excessive weight gain, onset or worsening of proteinuria, or signs of placental insufficiency after 20 weeks of gestation) will be eligible. Exclusion criteria: diagnosis of severe disease (HELLP syndrome, eclampsia, signs of imminent eclampsia, hypertensive crisis, or acute fetal distress). The calculated sample size is 140 women with an estimated inclusion period of 12 months. Eligible women will be invited to participate and will be randomly selected for standard follow-up or intervention (rapid PlGF test) in addition to the routine laboratory protocol for investigating SPE. The test result will be made available to the healthcare professionals within 30 min. When PlGF is considered normal (value ≥ 100 pg/ml), woman will be discharged for outpatient follow-up scheduled in 7 or 14 days (depending on gestational age (GA)). Cases with abnormal PlGF (between 12 and 100 pg/ml) will be evaluated as probable SPE, with hospitalization or early follow-up, and cases with suggestive severe placental insufficiency (PlGF < 12 pg/ml) will be hospitalized for strict maternal–fetal surveillance. Primary outcome will be the frequency of preterm childbirth. Maternal and perinatal outcomes, length of hospital stay, outpatient follow-up visits, serial collection of exams and costs of suspected cases of SPE, with and without the use of the rapid PlGF test (Quidel-Triage) will be compared.DiscussionThe use of PlGF may contribute to better distinguishing between SPE and CH and therefore avoiding medically indicated preterm delivery and other surveillance interventions during follow-up.Trial registrationThis protocol was registered on REBEC (The Brazilian Registry of Clinical Trials) platform under the code RBR-2zx5y9r in 07/09/2024.Keywords: Chronic Hypertension, Superimposed preeclampsia, PlGF biomarker, Pregnancy

Background

Chronic hypertension (CH) remains the most prevalent risk factor for cardiovascular diseases globally [1]. During pregnancy, CH is associated with adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes, such as superimposed preeclampsia (SPE), cesarean section, strokes, fetal growth restriction, premature birth, and maternal and perinatal death [1].

Preeclampsia (PE) is one of the main causes of maternal morbidity and mortality worldwide, especially in low and middle-income (LMIC) settings. Pregnant women with CH develop SPE in frequency that may variate from 20 to 60% of cases, being associated with increased maternal and perinatal morbidity compared to preeclampsia alone [2, 3]. SPE is diagnosed by worsening high blood pressure in previously hypertensive pregnant women, after 20 weeks, in the onset or worsening of proteinuria, or, in the absence of proteinuria, if clinical or laboratory evidence of organ damage, such as thrombocytopenia, renal or hepatic failure, pulmonary edema or seizures [3].

The challenge lies in distinguishing between SPE and worsening CH [2]. The prevalence of medically indicated prematurity, when pregnancy termination is indicated due to maternal or fetal causes, is as high as 40% in the group of patients with SPE, with 12% occurring before 34 weeks of gestation, and is most often associated with adverse outcomes [4]. In addition, the investigation of SPE results in recurrent hospitalizations, serial collection of laboratory tests, and more frequent antenatal follow-up visits. Therefore, the identification of biomarkers that could contribute to the accurate diagnosis of SPE (or that could rule out this diagnosis) would be valuable for the clinical monitoring of these cases and possibly for reducing complications [2].

The placental growth factor (PlGF) is an angiogenic protein synthesized in the placenta and, as a healthy pregnancy progresses, its concentration increases. It reaches its peak at 30 weeks, when it slowly decreases in uncomplicated pregnancies [5]. However, its concentration is abnormally low in pregnancies affected by PE, and this precedes the onset of clinical manifestations. Similarly, a PlGF concentration above the 5th percentile (≥ 100 pg/ml) is a good rule-out test for to the diagnosis of PE within the next 2 weeks, with a very high negative predictive value [6].

The use of PlGF, when compared to routine care, in women with suspected preeclampsia, significantly reduced the time to diagnosis of preeclampsia [7]. This earlier detection is clinically important, as it enables more timely interventions, close monitoring, and appropriate management strategies.

Given the limitations found in distinguishing CH and SPE, this project aims to implement a protocol for a randomized clinical trial of suspected cases of SPE, with and without the PlGF evaluation, for clinical management in women with CH.

Trial design {8}

The BIPES (Biomarker in Superimposed Preeclampsia) study is a two-arm, parallel, unblinded, randomized controlled trial with 1:1 equivalence. The study will compare maternal and perinatal outcomes between groups of suspected SPE with and without the use of the PlGF for clinical management.

Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes

Study setting {9}

The study will be conducted at a referral maternity hospital, within the Universidade Estadual de Campinas (UNICAMP), southwest of Brazil. The hospital is the reference maternity hospital in the region for both high-risk antenatal care and newborn care, with around 150 births a month. It has specialized outpatient clinics for the care of high-risk pregnancies, with at least one day a week specifically dedicated to pregnant women with CH (with around 4 new cases per week and 25 follow-up visits).

Eligibility criteria {10}

Pregnant women with CH and suspected SPE without signs of severe disease (GA over 20 weeks, worsening of blood pressure control, exacerbated weight gain (more than 1 kg per week), edema, onset or worsening of proteinuria (protein/creatinine ratio above 0.3 mg/dl or threefold rise from baseline levels)), with singleton pregnancies, over 18 years of age. (Fig. 1).

The exclusion criteria will be women with suspected SPE with signs of severe disease (HELLP syndrome, eclampsia, signs of imminent eclampsia (persistent headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain or altered mental status), hypertensive crisis—SBP ≥ 160 and/or DBP ≥ 110 mmHg), or acute fetal distress. Patients will be selected from the high-risk antenatal outpatient clinics, emergency care, or among obstetric admissions to clinical wards.

Who will obtain informed consent or assent from potential trial participants or authorized surrogates and how? {26a}

The research team will be responsible for obtaining the consent form. Women will be approached after their clinical appointment and prior to collecting their requested laboratory exams, in a private medical office. Patients with suspected SPE, according to the eligibility criteria above, will be invited to participate in the study. Then they will be informed about the study design, clarifying any doubts they may have. After agreeing to participate and signing the consent form, women will be randomized. After reading and signing the consent form, peripheral blood will be drawn to run the routine laboratory workup and the PlGF test (exclusively for the intervention group). Afterwards, samples of cases and controls will be stored in the institutional biobank.

All screened women will only be included in the study after signing the consent forms, which include the study objective, risks, potential benefits and associated procedures.

Interventions

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Included women will be removed from the study if they withdraw consent at any time. Cases will be discontinued, considered loss to follow-up, if maternal and perinatal outcomes are not available. Overall, cases are supposed to have their antenatal care and childbirth at the institution, however, if that does not happen, cases might be discontinued if the participant cannot be further reached.

Strategies for adherence to follow-up in the study {11c}

A close follow-up has been proposed, through more rigorous management of antenatal appointment scheduling, monitoring of possible hospitalizations, and postpartum review consultations within the institution, thus allowing a control of potential complications associated with preeclampsia, so that they remain adherent throughout the pregnancy period.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

PlGF testing will not be routinely available during the study period. Women will mostly be identified at the outpatient antenatal clinic. The routine care of pregnancies complicated by CH includes a nutritional evaluation, exercise manual, baseline organ damage investigation (with an appointment with a referral cardiologist), with a chest x-ray, electrocardiograph and fetal growth and well-being evaluation throughout pregnancy. All women receive prophylaxis for PE with low-dose aspirin and calcium (before 16 weeks) and are advised about physical exercise.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

If there is benefit, the protocol will be suggested for policy makers to ascertain it´s use into routine care, especially if it safely reduces preterm birth and shows cost-effectiveness.

Outcomes {12}

The main outcome is the frequency of preterm birth.

As secondary outcomes: number of hospitalizations, outpatient visits and collection of laboratory exams. A cost-effectiveness analysis will also be performed.

Participant schedule {13}

Our clinical trial will monitor pregnant women with CH and suspected SPE. (Fig. 1).

For randomization, a sealed sequential envelope (numbers 1 to 140) will be opened, following a random sequence generated by computer software (PlGF or Control). Based on the group selected, the PlGF test will be performed or not.

All patients included will be monitored during antenatal care until the postpartum visit, in order to obtain all data on maternal and perinatal outcomes. This follow-up involves weekly appointments, with fetal monitoring (cardiotocography or obstetric Doppler) and exams.

Sample size {14}

The sample size calculation was based on a previous study developed at the same maternity hospital (UNICAMP), using the Epi Info 7.2 program. The study evaluated 385 pregnant women with CH from January 2012 to May 2017, of which 167 developed SPE (43%). This group had 42% premature births, while the group without SPE had 13%. It is estimated that the intervention will lead to a 20% reduction in premature births (< 37 weeks) in the group where the PlGF test result is normal, with a power of 80% and a two-tailed alpha of 0.05. Based on this analysis, the required sample size will be 140 women [3].

Treatment, techniques, tests and/or exams

Collection and pre-analytical processing of maternal peripheral blood samples

For sample collection, maternal peripheral blood will be drawn simultaneously with routine laboratory investigation for PE. For this study, plasma samples will be considered, collecting the clear upper phase obtained by centrifugation of blood in an EDTA-coated tube. An aliquot of at least 300 µL will be stored into 3 cryotubes using calibrated micropipettes. For cases randomized to intervention, PlGF will be analyzed immediately after sampling and for the controls, the material will be stored for evaluation of the biomarker at the end of the study.

Pre-analytical processing of maternal peripheral blood samples and biobank storage

After sampling, the blood samples will be transported to the institutional`s biobank laboratory. Ideally, the samples will be processed within 30 min after sample collection. If the blood cannot be processed immediately, it is ensured that it remains at 4 °C until processing becomes possible in a maximum of 3 h.

At the end of the pre-analytical processing, the following samples will be obtained: 1 aliquot of maternal peripheral blood plasma for the Quidel Triage PlGF test and 3 aliquots of maternal peripheral blood stored in the institutional Biobank at −80ºC.

Quidel triage PlGF test

The Quidel Triage PlGF test is a rapid quantitative fluorescence immunoassay that can be read on the Quidel Triage Meter equipment. The test is used to measure circulating maternal PlGF in plasma samples. To perform the test, the plasma aliquot obtained in pre-analytical processing has its volume transferred using a pipette provided by the Quidel kit to a chromatographic cassette. The cassette is inserted into the Quidel Triage Meter equipment to read the result in pg/mL. This process takes 15 min. The result is recorded in a report and issued as an attachment to the patient's medical record. The remainder of the research team are immediately notified of the result so that they can advise the medical team, which will provide adequate care. The process of collecting blood samples, processing the sample, performing the test and obtaining the result takes 30 to 40 min in total.

The samples will be evaluated on the Quidel Triage Meter equipment (provided by Quidel) by the research team.

Intervention assignment: allocation

Randomization {16a}

The randomization sequence comprises the options control or PlGF. It was generated with an online software (http://www.random.org/) and transferred to opaque, sealed envelopes prior to the beginning of the study. These envelopes are stored in individualized tabs within a folder, together with the ethics consent forms, and will only be accessed at the time of each woman's inclusion. This folder will be kept in the office of one of the research team members, ensuring data security and control. After signing the informed consent form and including the pregnant woman in the study, a sequential envelope (numbers 1 to 140) will be opened, and the woman will be allocated.

Allocation of subjects {16b}

To ensure confidentiality of allocation, sealed and opaque envelopes will be used sequentially by the research assistant.

Subject follow-up {16c}

This study will be prospective, with subjects being followed during antenatal visits, hospitalizations, up to the postpartum period (six weeks), for maternal and perinatal outcomes. After inclusion in the study, data on skin color, marital status, education (in years), occupational activity, socioeconomic profile, obstetric history, present comorbidities, cigarette use, history of PE, PE prophylaxis with aspirin and calcium, GA at beginning of PE prophylaxis, use of antihypertensive drugs prior to pregnancy, use of antihypertensive drugs during pregnancy, body mass index(BMI), gestational weight gain, use of corticosteroids for fetal lung maturation during pregnancy, GA at suspected and/or confirmed SPE diagnosis, GA and mode of birth, five minute Apgar score, birthweight, gestational complications, need for neonatal Intensive Care Unit(NICU), need for magnesium sulphate severe features (HELLP syndrome, imminent eclampsia or eclampsia), postpartum visit, use of contraceptive methods postpartum, use of antihypertensive medication postpartum, number of days hospitalized during pregnancy and postpartum period, number of laboratory exams requested/collected during pregnancy and postpartum, estimated cost associated with diagnostic investigation and treatment, including expenses related to length of hospital stay.

Allocation of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded? {17a}

The study will be an open randomized clinical trial. All participants and the research team will be aware of the type of intervention selected, since the intervention depends on knowing the PlGF test results among cases.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

This will not be necessary, since the study is unblinded.

Data collection and management

Data processing and analysis {18a}

All participant data will be entered into an Excel for Windows spreadsheet. First, we will compare the sociodemographic characteristics to ensure the similarity between the groups, then compare the outcomes. Continuous variables will be presented as means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges, while categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and proportions. Statistical tests will be used according to the characteristics and distributions of the samples. Kaplan–Meier analysis will be used to assess the prolongation of pregnancy in the intervention group.

Quality control {18b}

All patients included in the study will be monitored following institutional protocols by the healthcare team together with the research team, through rigorous prospective monitoring of women until the postpartum period. Likewise, all samples collected for the PlGF testing follow strict standard operating procedures (SOP) for processing and storage. For data processing, double data entry will be performed by different individuals at different times and there will be constant procedures for data consistency checking.

Data management {19}

A systematic monitoring will be implemented throughout antenatal care to identify and evaluate both confirmed and suspected cases of SPE and subsequently follow up on diagnostic test results and perinatal outcomes. The data will be stored in an Excel spreadsheet, with access permission only by the research group. The statistical analyses will be structured in codes for later interpretation and analysis, with double checking by the team responsible.

Ethical aspects {27 and 33}

This study is based on all the principles in the Brazilian National Health Council Resolution 466/12. It has ethical approval, by the number from Research Ethics Committee: 77,909,823.0.0000.5404, as well as registration on the Brazilian Clinical trials Registry platform(Rebec): RBR-2zx5y9r (link: https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR-2zx5y9r). All the regulations on the use of biological samples will be respected.

For blood sample collection, eligible pregnant women are requested to provide an informed consent form, with due clarification on the research protocol and use of blood samples. Furthermore, the informed consent form for the institutional Biobank will be applied to authorize the storage of biological samples, with due clarification on the Biobank and possible future use of the samples. Personal data and the identification of the participants will remain confidential, ensuring the preservation of their confidentiality.

After the participant's authorization, all collected blood samples will be processed and stored in the institutional Biobank—a specific location with all the structure and skills to preserve the material (the Biobank approval number B-056).

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for analysis for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Hypothesis

The hypothesis is that among cases (randomized to use the PlGF test), medically induced preterm birth will reduce, with less required laboratory exams, length of hospital stays, and outpatient visits, resulting in cost reduction.

Definition of outcomes

The primary outcome is the frequency of preterm birth and SPE between the groups (cases and controls). Preterm delivery is defined as a delivery at a gestational age of less than 37 weeks. SPE is defined by the result of the PLGF test < 100 pg/ml in the intervention group, and the standard criteria in the control group (in the presence or worsening of proteinuria, or, in the absence of proteinuria, when there is clinical or laboratory evidence of organ damage, such as thrombocytopenia, renal failure or liver failure, pulmonary edema or seizures).

The secondary outcome includes gestational age at childbirth, described in complete weeks, number of hospitalizations, mode of delivery, frequency of c-sections, perinatal outcomes (birthweight, 5-min Apgar score, admission to the neonatal intensive care unit).

Analysis methods

All cases will be entered into the Excel for Windows spreadsheet. Continuous variables will be presented as means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges, while categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and proportions.

Statistical analyses will be conducted using independent samples t-tests for continuous variables with normal distribution and homoscedasticity. In cases where these assumptions are not met, non-parametric alternatives will be employed. Categorical variables will be expressed as frequencies and proportions. Ordinal data will be assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test, and nominal variables will be compared using the chi-square test of Fisher’s exact test, depending on data distribution. All analyses will be two-tailed, and statistical significance will be defined as p-value less or equal to 0.05. P-values of less than 0.05 will be statistically significant.

Statistical software

Statistics will be conducted using SPSS version 21 software (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA).

Adherence

The women who will be followed in this study receive care in a specialized antenatal care program, provided by a multidisciplinary team that maintains strict monitoring, particularly regarding absences from appointments. Therefore, this ensures better control over adherence, both to the study and to antenatal care.

Missing data

For missing data, we will attempt to contact the patient by telephone or message to fill any gaps. Analyses will be performed with available data and detailed in the sample if there are missing data.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The coordinating center is composed of an multidisciplinary team involved in the outpatient clinic, with medical doctors from different specialties, such as obstetricians, cardiologists, sonographers, nurses and nutritionists. Our hospital has a specialized outpatient clinic for the care of cases that require greater monitoring due to comorbidities, with at least one day a week dedicated specifically to pregnant women with CH, thus we can keep intensive care with those patients.

Our team of researchers is composed of RRJ, JCS, FGS, JPG and MLC, all of which are medical doctors and obstetricians. FGS, JPG and MLC are the professors responsible for the hypertension outpatient clinic, while RRJ and JCS are postgraduate students.

The protocol committee will be responsible for protocol setting, correction and writing the data. The coordinating team meets weekly to discuss the cases included and manage recruitment.

There will be a Steering Committee, with experts in the field, to analyze preliminary results halfway through the study and ensure quality and safety of the procedures.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

Our hospital is overseen by an ethics committee, composed of doctors of obstetrics, neonatology and anesthetics, which follow established safety protocols. All adverse outcomes are evaluated and documented. There will be an external committee as described above, to evaluate the ongoing data collection and preliminary results.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

If any case present clinical deterioration, they will be managed according to the institutional protocol, regardless of the PlGF test value or study allocation. Besides, being in the intervention group do not add any risk.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

Regular weekly meetings will be conducted with the research team to monitor and assess the progress and conduct of the clinical trial.

Before the trial concludes, when 50% of women are included, at least one interim statistical evaluation will be carried out to verify that the absence of intervention may be negatively impacting the control group.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Protocol amendments, if needed, will be submitted to respective regulatory committees, such as the Registro Brasileiro de Ensaios Clínicos (ReBec) and the National Committee of Ethics in Research (CONEP) if needed.

Dissemination plans {31a}

The dataset will be stored on the university repository, REDU (Repositório de Dados de Pesquisa da Unicamp—https://www.sbu.unicamp.br/sbu/repositorio-de-dados-de-pesquisa-da-unicamp/). The end of the trial will be reported on the relevant trial registry platforms. We intend to present the data at Obstetrics and Gynecology conferences and will produce a paper to be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal. We also plan to present the work in international Conferences with important impact in the area, such as the International Society for the Study of Hypertension in Pregnancy (ISSHP).

Intervention description {11a}

On intervention group, a rapid PlGF test being collected, in addition to the routine laboratory protocol for investigating SPE. The current laboratory investigation for SPE has an estimated wait time of 4 to 5 h for the result, with blood count, creatinine, aspartate transaminase (AST), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), total bilirubin and fractions (BT), and urinary protein/creatinine ratio being collected. The PlGF test will take an average of 30 min, after sample collection. When the is PLGF ≥ 100 pg/ml, the pregnant woman will not need to wait until the results of the other laboratory tests are available. Conservative management of these cases will be considered safe, if the blood pressure is satisfactory (SBP < 140 and/or DBP < 90 mmHg) and the woman has no signs of severe disease, thus allowing for a scheduled outpatient appointment in 7 or 14 days (depending on the GA).

The standard laboratory protocol will be monitored by the research and healthcare assistant teams. When the results are available, they will be communicated to patients at the hospital or at the next antenatal appointment and entered in their medical records, ensuring safe and rigorous management of these cases. If abnormal values are reported, patients will be called for hospital evaluation/admission. Cases with a rapid Quidel Triage test with abnormal PLGF (between 12 and 100 pg/ml) will be assessed as probable SPE, with hospitalization or early reassessment, and cases with suggestive severe placental insufficiency (PLGF < 12 pg/ml) will be hospitalized for strict maternal–fetal surveillance (Fig. 2).

Discussion

Biomarkers serve as essential tools for the investigation, assessment and clinical management of cardiovascular risk, as well as kidney, neurological and rheumatic diseases, including lupus [8, 9]. The use of placental biomarkers, detectable in maternal peripheral blood, offers potential utility in diagnosing and monitoring cases of preeclampsia that are suspected but not yet confirmed [10]. An evaluation of PLGF test performance among women with suspected preeclampsia in Mozambique demonstrated that lower PLGF concentrations correlated with increased diagnostic rates and a shorter latency period between diagnosis and delivery [11].

Notably, among women with a low-intermediate PlGF level (12–100 pg/ml)—a group that might otherwise be screened and classified as lower risk based on traditional assessments—PlGF testing was associated with a meaningful reduction in serious adverse maternal outcomes [7, 12]. This suggests that biochemical markers such as PlGF can uncover hidden risk in patients who might be misclassified by clinical symptoms alone. Thus, incorporating PlGF testing into standard practice may improve maternal safety by preventing delays in diagnosis and treatment, particularly in a subset of patients who would otherwise not be prioritized for intensive monitoring [7].

The prevalence of hypertension among Brazilian women of reproductive age has increased over the years. In 2019, the Brazilian National Health Survey reported that 14% of women in this age group had a medical diagnosis of hypertension, an increase from 2013, when the prevalence was 11.7% [13]. Although around 20% of adult women have not had their blood pressure measured in the past year, over 98% of pregnant individuals receive antenatal care, during which blood pressure is routinely monitored [11]. In our center, pregnant women with CH develop SPE in up to 40% of cases, being associated with increased maternal and perinatal morbidity compared to preeclampsia alone [2, 3].

There are several large trials testing the use of biomarkers for suspected PE, however there is a significant gap in the scientific literature regarding the impact of using the PlGF test in the management of CH in pregnancy in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). While studies conducted in high-income settings have demonstrated that the sFlt-1/PlGF ratio can accurately predict preeclampsia and its severe features, there is a lack of research evaluating its applicability and effectiveness in LMIC populations, where the burden of hypertensive disorders is high and access to advanced diagnostic tools – such as biomarker testing – is often limited [14].

Several studies have demonstrated the challenges in differentiating SPE from CH, especially in cases without target-organ dysfunction but poorly controlled blood pressure. In a cohort conducted in the United States between 2006 and 2009, involving 120 pregnant women with CH, a significant increase in preterm birth rate was observed, reaching 56% in the group with uncontrolled CH, which was even higher than the rate among with SPE (53%) [15]. This group also presented reduced birth weight, greater need for neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission and higher proportion of cesarean deliveries among women with SPE or uncontrolled CH compared to those with well-controlled CH [15]. Another study revealed that the prevalence of medically indicated preterm birth when pregnancy resolution becomes unavoidable, is 40% among patients with preeclampsia, with 12% occurring before 34 weeks of gestation [4].

One of the negative impacts of SPE relays on the high prevalence of preterm birth in this group. A study in United Kingdom revealing the growing number of prolonged Pediatric Intensive Care Unit (PICU) admissions among infants and young children– many of whom are preterm or require extended respiratory support – illustrates the long-term consequences [16, 17].

Among cases of CH, the main limitation lies in distinguishing between worsening blood pressure control and SPE, where the test may provide more precise diagnostic support in this specific group.

Conclusion

The use of biomarkers in obstetric clinical practice, already taking place in many countries (recently including the United States, with approval of the use of biomarkers by the FDA-food and drug administration), is a reality, but their use to aid in the management of more challenging clinical cases, such as cases of CH, is not yet fully established. The present study protocol aims to evaluate the impact of this intervention in the diagnosis of SPE, evaluating frequency of medically indicated preterm birth, length of hospital stays, and maternal and perinatal outcomes.

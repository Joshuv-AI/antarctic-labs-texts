Trends and prescribing patterns of antiosteoporosis medicines in Chinese patients: a real-world retrospective study
Authors: Shuo Zhang, Yuqing Fan, Mengyao Xue, Linfeng Jiang, Chen Mu, Nan Peng, Dongning Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe availability of new anti-osteoporosis medications (AOMs) and updates to clinical guidelines in recent years may have substantially influenced real-world prescribing patterns.AimsTo assess trends in the utilization, prescribing patterns of AOMs and related influencing factors in Eastern China.MethodsPatients with osteoporosis who had at least one prescription for AOMs were identified from a provincial healthcare database in Eastern China (2019–2024). Patients were followed until the occurrence of Paget’s disease, malignant tumors, death, loss to follow-up, or October 29, 2024. The prescribing patterns and utilization trends of AOMs were analyzed. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify factors influencing AOMs choice.ResultsA total of 363,529 patients were included (mean age 65.1 years; 73.0% female). Active vitamin D analogs (71.9%), bisphosphonates (22.0%), traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) (15.3%) and denosumab (14.2%) were the most commonly prescribed AOMs. The use of denosumab increased significantly (2.9% vs. 21.8%, P < 0.001), while active vitamin D analogs (80.0% vs. 64.8%, P < 0.001), bisphosphonate (26.9% vs. 16.7%, P < 0.001) and TCM (15.3% vs. 11.6%, P < 0.001) declined from 2019 to 2024. Most patients received monotherapy (92.2%), with only 7.8% receiving combination therapy. The most frequent combination was vitamin D and analogs with bisphosphonates (55.6%). Women, older adults, and those with fracture history were more likely to receive calcitonin and denosumab, but less likely to receive vitamin D analogs.ConclusionsOsteoporosis treatment was predominantly monotherapy, with active vitamin D analogs being the most commonly prescribed agents, followed by bisphosphonates, TCM, and denosumab. Attention should be given to weak evidence-based TCM use.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40520-026-03382-8.

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a systemic skeletal disorder that is characterized by reduced bone mass and the microarchitectural deterioration of bone tissue, leading to increased fragility and fracture risk [1]. Patients with osteoporotic fractures often require hospitalization, experience a reduced quality of life, and face long-term disability or death, thereby contributing to a significant burden on healthcare systems, public health, and the global economy [2–5]. Globally, the prevalence of osteoporosis was 18.3% [6]. In 2021, low bone mineral density was associated with 219,552 deaths and 7.76 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) among postmenopausal women worldwide, corresponding to an age-standardized DALY rate of 979.2 per 100,000 population [7]. While osteoporosis poses a worldwide challenge, its impact in China is especially concerning. The prevalence in individuals aged 65 years and older was reported to be 32.0%, with 51.6% in women and 10.7% in men [8]. It is estimated that the number of osteoporotic fractures will rise to 4.83 million by 2035 and 5.99 million by 2050, with the associated healthcare costs expected to reach USD 19.92 billion and USD 25.43 billion, respectively [9].

Clinical evidence suggests that current pharmacological treatments for osteoporosis can inhibit bone resorption and/or stimulate bone formation, thereby enhancing bone mineral density and reducing fracture risk [10–12]. Over time, the choice of initiated AOMs and the characteristics of patients have been shaped by the availability of new treatment options, updates in practice guidelines, and changes in healthcare policies [13]. Several real-world studies have shown that over the past few decades, denosumab has increased rapidly, while bisphosphonates have declined, and prescribing patterns have varied across countries in response to guideline updates and healthcare policy changes [13–15]. In China, owing to the relatively recent approval of certain medications, such as denosumab (2020) and eldecalcitol (2020), the Chinese guideline for osteoporosis management have also changed [16–18]. Nevertheless, real-world evidence regarding trends in the AOMs utilization in China remain unclear, and existing evidence on treatment patterns is limited by inadequate population representativeness and short observation periods [19, 20].

As such, with the increasing diversity of treatment options, understanding real-world medication utilization among Chinese population is essential for promoting adherence to guidelines and ensuring optimal therapeutic benefit for patients. This study aimed to evaluate trends in the utilization and prescribing patterns of anti-osteoporosis medications among patients with osteoporosis in Eastern China, as well as to identify factors influencing the choice and use of these medications. Specifically, it sought to assess the annual trends in the prevalence and cost of these AOMs, analyze prescribing patterns including monotherapy and combination regimens, and explore patient and clinical factors associated with AOMs use, in line with current clinical guidelines.

Methods

Data source

The study was a retrospective, observational cohort study utilizing electronic medical records (EMRs) obtained from a provincial healthcare database in Eastern China. This provincial healthcare database integrates longitudinal electronic health records from public hospitals, including tertiary, secondary, community hospitals and community health service centers across both urban and rural areas. It covers over 68 million residents (approximately 80% of the provincial population) and includes anonymized demographic information, diagnostic records, prescription records, in-hospital laboratory and imaging examination records and other healthcare services.

Study design

The study cohort included patients who were diagnosed with osteoporosis and had at least one prescription record for anti-osteoporosis medications between January 2019 and October 2024. The diagnosis of osteoporosis was identified using International Classification of Diseases, 10th edition (ICD-10) codes M80-M82. According to the osteoporosis treatment guideline, AOMs include bisphosphonates, denosumab, calcitonin, menopausal hormone therapy (MHT), selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs), parathyroid hormone analogue (PTHa), vitamin D analogs, menatetrenone and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) [18]. The detailed types of AOMs are provided in Supplementary Table S1. We excluded patients who had been prescribed AOMs in the year prior to enrollment, were aged < 18 years, had incorrect or missing basic demographic information, or were diagnosed of Paget’s disease or malignant tumors at baseline. The index date was defined as the date of the first prescription after the osteoporosis diagnosis. Patients were followed until the occurrence of Paget’s disease, malignant tumors, death, loss to follow-up, or October 29, 2024, whichever occurred first [21].

Trends and patterns of AOMs

During the six-year observation period, the overall trends in the number of patients prescribed AOMs and the corresponding drug costs were described. The annual utilization rate of each specific AOM was calculated as the number of individuals who used the medication in a given calendar year divided by the total number of individuals who used any AOM in the same year. Prescribing patterns were categorized as monotherapy or combined therapy. Monotherapy was defined as the prescription of a single AOM, whereas combination therapy was defined as the prescription of two or more AOMs within the same prescription. Combination therapy was further classified according to the number of concurrently prescribed drugs: two-drug, three-drug, or four-drug combinations. The proportions of different AOMs within each prescribing pattern were calculated.

Covariates

Covariates included patient demographics, comorbidities, and fracture history within one year before the index date. Demographic characteristics included age and sex. Age was categorized into the following groups: 0–44 years, 45–54 years, 55–64 years, 65–74 years, and ≥ 75 years. Comorbidities included hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, gastrointestinal bleeding, peptic ulcer, pneumonia, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), ischemic heart disease, heart failure, ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, renal failure, Parkinson’s disease, dementia, depression, schizophrenia, glaucoma, cataracts, and HIV/AIDS, based on ICD-10 codes provided in Supplementary Table S2. Fracture history was classified into hip fractures, vertebral fractures, and non-hip, non-vertebral fractures.

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables were presented as percentages. The chi-square test was used for categorical variables, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for normally distributed continuous variables, and the Kruskal–Wallis test was applied for non-normally distributed continuous variables. The logistic regression was used to evaluate trends in the proportion of patients with use of each medication, corresponding costs and prescribing patterns across years. To explore factors associated with the use of specific anti-osteoporosis medications, univariable and multivariable logistic regression models were constructed for each commonly prescribed medication among patients receiving AMOs. A two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R version 4.4 and MySQL version 8.0.

Stratified analyses were further performed according to age, sex, and fracture history, which are recognized risk factors for osteoporotic fractures [22].

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the institutional review board of the Nanjing Medical University (Approval Number: 2025140). Because this study utilized anonymized electronic hospital records and did not involve any direct interaction with patients, informed consent from participants was not required.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

A total of 363,529 patients with osteoporosis were identified between January 2019 and October 2024 (Fig. 1). The median follow-up time was 433 days (IQR: 122–906 days). At baseline, the mean age was 65.2 years (SD = 13.5), with the largest proportion (30.5%) belonging to the 65–74-year age group. Among all patients, 73.0% were female. Hypertension (38.8%) was the most prevalent comorbidity, followed by diabetes (18.4%), and ischemic stroke (12.3%). A total of 15.0% of patients had a history of fractures within one year before enrollment, comprising 2.4% with hip fractures, 11.3% with vertebral fractures, and 1.8% with non-hip, non-vertebral fractures (Table 1).

Treatment trends

Active vitamin D analogs (71.9%), bisphosphonates (22.0%), TCM (15.3%) and denosumab (14.2%) were the most commonly prescribed AOMs, accounting for 36.6%, 24.3%, 5.9%, and 20.0% of total costs, respectively (Supplementary Table S3-S4). From 2019 to 2024, the use of active vitamin D analogs (80.0% in 2019 vs. 64.8% in 2024, P < 0.001) and bisphosphonates (26.9% in 2019 vs. 16.7% in 2024, P < 0.001) declined, while the use of denosumab increased after 2021 from 2.9% to 21.8% in 2024 (P < 0.001). The use of menatetrenone (0.7% in 2019 vs. 2.5% in 2024, P < 0.001), MHT (0.5% in 2019 vs. 1.0% in 2024, P < 0.001), PTHa (0.0% in 2019 vs. 0.2% in 2024, P < 0.001) showed a slight increase, while the use of calcitonin (8.3% in 2019 vs. 6.9% in 2024, P < 0.001), SERMs (0.4% in 2019 vs. 0.0% in 2024, P < 0.001), and TCM (15.3% in 2019 vs. 11.6% in 2024, P < 0.001) showed a slight decrease (Fig. 2a).

Similar longitudinal trends in the use of denosumab, calcitonin, MHT, PTHa, SERMs and TCM were observed during patient follow-up. However, as the disease progressed, the use of active vitamin D analogs (66.6% at enrollment vs. 80.0% at the 5-year follow-up, P < 0.001) significantly increased, whereas menatetrenone (2.4% on the enrollment day vs. 2.0% at the 5-year follow-up, P < 0.001) significantly decreased (Fig. 2b).

Treatment patterns

From 2019 to 2024, the proportion of monotherapy prescriptions increased steadily (89.8% in 2019 vs. 92.5% in 2024, P < 0.001), whereas dual drug prescriptions (9.5% in 2019 vs. 7.1% in 2024, P < 0.001) and combined prescriptions of three or four drugs (0.7% in 2019 vs. 0.4% in 2024, P < 0.001) declined gradually (Fig. 3a). Similar trends in treatment patterns were observed as patients’ conditions progressed (Fig. 3b).

Prevalence of osteoporosis patterns. (a) from 2019 to 2024; (b) from the enrollment day to the 5-year follow-up

From 2019 to 2024, monotherapy prescriptions showed marked declines in bisphosphonates, active vitamin D analogs, calcitonin, and SERMs, whereas denosumab, PTHa, menatetrenone, and TCM demonstrated notable increases (all P < 0.001) (Supplementary Figure S1a). In dual prescriptions, calcitonin prescriptions increased significantly, TCM prescriptions remained stable, and the trends of other drugs were generally similar to those observed in monotherapy (Supplementary Figure S1b). In combination of three or four drugs therapy, the prescription trends for bisphosphonates, denosumab, active vitamin D analogs, menatetrenone, SERMs, and calcitonin were similar to those observed in monotherapy (Supplementary Figure S1c).

As patients’ conditions progressed, the prescription rate of MHT increased across all regimens, whereas TCM exhibited a downward trend. In both monotherapy and dual-drug prescriptions, the prescription rates of vitamin D analogs, denosumab, and bisphosphonates showed an upward trend, while menatetrenone use declined. However, the prescription rate of bisphosphonates decreased in combination of three or four drugs therapy. In combination prescriptions, an increase in PTHa prescriptions was observed, accompanied by a decrease in calcitonin prescriptions (Supplementary Figure S2).

Drug combination patterns

The five most frequently prescribed dual-drug prescriptions in the overall patient population are presented in Supplementary Table S5. A total of 108,912 prescriptions were identified, accounting for approximately 7.4% of all prescriptions. The combination of vitamin D analogs and bisphosphonates was the most frequently prescribed, representing 55.6% of dual-drug prescriptions.

The five most frequently prescribed combinations of three or four drugs in the overall patient population are presented in Supplementary Table S6. A total of 6,309 prescriptions were identified, accounting for approximately 0.4% of all prescriptions. The combination of vitamin D analogs, bisphosphonates, and TCM was the most frequently prescribed, representing 36.6% of combined prescriptions of three or four drugs.

Factors associated with the use of specific AOMs

Factors associated with specific AOM use were assessed through univariable and multivariable logistic regression, with crude associations and adjusted independent effects detailed in Supplementary Table S7 and Table 2, respectively. Due to the relatively small number of patients using MHT, SERMs, PTHa, and menatetrenone, these medications were not included in the multivariable analyses.

Demographic characteristics were significantly associated with the use of specific anti-osteoporosis medications. Female patients were more likely to initiate denosumab (OR = 3.74, 95% CI: 3.65–3.84) and calcitonin (OR = 1.78, 95% CI: 1.75–1.81), whereas the use of active vitamin D analogs was slightly less common among women (OR = 0.86, 95% CI: 0.85–0.86). Comorbidities were associated with distinct prescribing patterns. Renal failure and rheumatic arthritis were independently associated with a higher likelihood of receiving active vitamin D analogs (OR = 2.01 and 2.49, respectively; both P < 0.001). Fracture history was a consistent determinant of medication selection, with all fracture types independently increasing the likelihood of receiving calcitonin and denosumab. Patients with vertebral fractures exhibited the strongest association with calcitonin use (OR = 3.50, 95% CI: 3.45–3.54), followed by those with hip fractures (OR = 3.10, 95% CI: 3.03–3.18) and non-hip, non-vertebral fractures (OR = 1.72, 95% CI: 1.66–1.79). Similarly, the likelihood of denosumab prescription was highest in the vertebral fracture group (OR = 2.52, 95% CI: 2.48–2.56), while substantial associations were also observed among patients with non-hip, non-vertebral fractures (OR = 1.89, 95% CI: 1.81–1.97) and hip fractures (OR = 1.42, 95% CI: 1.37–1.48).

Subgroup analysis

Compared to male patients, female patients had higher usage rates of bisphosphonates, calcitonin, and denosumab, but lower usage rates of active vitamin D analogs (Supplementary Figure S3). Similarly, compared with younger patients, those aged between 65 and 74 and over 75 years were more frequently prescribed with bisphosphonates, calcitonin, and denosumab, and less active vitamin D analogs. MHT was most frequently prescribed in patients younger than 45 years (3.7% in 2024) and those aged 45–54 years (4.1% in 2024), with prescription rates below 1% in other age groups (Supplementary Figure S4). In addition, osteoporosis patients with a history of fractures were more frequently prescribed bisphosphonates, calcitonin, denosumab, TCM, and menatetrenone than those without fracture history, whereas prescriptions of active vitamin D analogs were less frequent (Supplementary Figure S5).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study using longitudinal electronic health record data to simultaneously evaluate trends in the utilization and prescribing patterns of AOMs with the emergence of new therapies and as the disease progresses. Three key findings were observed in this study. First, the osteoporosis treatment was predominantly based on monotherapy, with vitamin D analogs being the most frequently prescribed, followed by bisphosphonates, TCM and denosumab. Second, prescription of AOMs shifted over the study period, with decreases in the use of vitamin D analogs, bisphosphonates, and TCM, whereas denosumab utilization rose. In contrast, the use of vitamin D analogs increased as the disease progressed. Third, trends in the use of anti-osteoporosis medications varied according to prescribing patterns and patient characteristics, including age, sex, and prior fracture history.

Most guidelines, including those in China, recommended bisphosphonates as the first-line drugs in the treatment of osteoporosis, whereas denosumab is recommended for patients who cannot tolerate bisphosphonates [18, 23–25]. In our study, the usage rates of bisphosphonates and denosumab were 22.0% and 14.2%, respectively. In addition, a significant decline was observed in the use of bisphosphonates, whereas denosumab usage increased markedly. The decreasing trend of bisphosphonate use is similar to findings from previous studies in other countries, possibly due to efforts to improve medication adherence and to avoid the gastrointestinal adverse events [26, 27]. Since denosumab was approved in China in 2020, it has become the most commonly used AOM after vitamin D analogs in 2024, accounting for 21.7%. This may be due to evidence from several studies suggesting that denosumab has superior efficacy compared to bisphosphonates in preventing fractures [28–30]. Additionally, as a long-interval dosing medication, denosumab has become increasingly favored as the first-line AOM due to its convenience [26]. However, unlike bisphosphonates, which confer residual benefits even after discontinuation, cessation of denosumab has been reported to be associated with rebound bone loss and an increased risk of vertebral fractures [31]. Further research is required on how to safely discontinue denosumab when needed.

In our study, vitamin D analogs were the most commonly used AOMs. In contrast, due to the lack of strong evidence for fracture prevention, as well as its narrow therapeutic window and increased risk of hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria, not all countries recommend active vitamin D analogs as routine anti-osteoporosis drugs [23, 24, 32, 33]. This discrepancy can be attributed to several factors. Firstly, Chinese osteoporosis patients are willing to receive treatments with higher clinical efficacy and lower out-of-pocket costs [34]. In our study, we found that 66.7% of patients initiated active vitamin D analogs which consumed only 36.7% of the AOMs costs. Secondly, vitamin D analogs are more recommended for the elderly, patients with impaired renal function, and those with 1α-hydroxylase deficiency or reduction [35, 36]. They have the effects of increasing bone density, reducing falls, and lowering the risk of fractures. The prevalence of vitamin D deficiency was notably higher in China (70.3%) than in the US (17.4%), with severe deficiency affecting 30.6% and 3.4% of the population, respectively [37]. It is estimated that the prevalence rates are 8.2% for chronic kidney disease, 2.2% for impaired kidney function, and 6.7% for albuminuria [38]. But vitamin D analogs cannot replace vitamin D to treat vitamin D deficiency or insufficiency. Furthermore, vitamin D analogs can be used in combination with other AOMs [18]. This is consistent with our research, in which most combination therapies are based on vitamin D analogs. In addition, the usage rate of eldecalcitol (1.9%) has been significantly lower than that of other vitamin D analogs (calcitriol 32.5%, alfacalcidol 46.9%). However, studies have shown that compared to alfacalcidol, eldecalcitol has a longer serum half-life, stronger inhibition of osteoclast activity, and a more pronounced increase in bone density [39]. Additionally, eldecalcitol is more cost-effective than alfacalcidol and calcitriol for the Chinese women with postmenopausal osteoporosis [40]. In the future, the proportion of eldecalcitol in the use of vitamin D analogs should be increased.

Notably, there were also 15.3% of TCM prescriptions, which were unique among China population. Whether in monotherapy or combination therapy, they were second only to active vitamin D analogs and bisphosphonates. While several meta-analyses found that TCM has a beneficial effect on improving BMD and pain relief, its widespread adoption in clinical practice has taken place in the absence of robust supporting evidence. Existing evidence regarding TCM is primarily limited to bone density, biochemical indicators, and pain VAS [41–45]. However, there is currently insufficient clinical evidence supporting the use of TCM to reduce the risk of osteoporotic fractures, which remains the ultimate goal of anti-osteoporosis therapy. Without definitive evidence of efficacy regarding fracture outcomes, patients may remain at increased risk of debilitating fractures despite receiving treatment. Furthermore, the safety profile of TCM, particularly with respect to long-term administration and potential drug interactions in combination regimens, has not been well characterized. Therefore, evidence-based research is needed in areas such as adverse reactions associated with combined use, reduction of fracture risk, and larger sample sizes.

Beyond overall prescribing trends, our study also identified several patient characteristics associated with the use of different anti-osteoporosis medications. Female patients were more likely to receive denosumab and bisphosphonates compared with male patients. This finding is consistent with previous studies showing that women are more frequently treated for osteoporosis, likely reflecting the higher prevalence of osteoporosis and fracture risk among postmenopausal women and the greater clinical awareness of osteoporosis in female patients [46, 47]. For example, a large cohort study reported that men were significantly less possibility than women to use calcitonin, bisphosphonates or denosumab [48]. Age is a critical factor influencing both the risk and clinical management of osteoporosis and fragility fractures, and is routinely considered by physicians when making diagnostic and therapeutic decisions [6]. In this study, older age, especially ≥ 75 years, was associated with increased use of denosumab and calcitonin, which may also reflect clinicians’ preference for injectable anti-resorptive therapies in older patients, as elderly individuals often have poorer adherence to oral bisphosphonates and may have contraindications to oral treatment [49]. Fracture history was strongly associated with prescribing patterns in our study. Patients with hip or vertebral fractures were more likely to receive denosumab and calcitonin, consistent with guideline recommendations that individuals at very high fracture risk should preferentially receive zoledronic acid or denosumab [18].

This study has several limitations. First, prescription records only indicate the issuance of a prescription, but they are not necessarily equivalent to the actual consumption or use of the prescribed therapies. Second, our study relied on hospital-based prescription data, and medications from community pharmacies were not included. This could potentially lead to an underestimation of the prevalence of AOMs. However, as all AOMs assessed are prescription-only in China, the majority of dispensing occurs within hospital sector. The observed temporal trends are unlikely to be materially affected. Third, due to the lack of fracture risk variables in the database, medication trends could only be evaluated based on fracture risk factors, including sex, age, and prior fracture history, precluding assessment of adherence to guideline-recommended treatments stratified by fracture risk. Future studies should incorporate more comprehensive data to evaluate prescribing patterns and trends across different fracture risk groups. Finally, as the study was conducted in a single province, the findings may not be fully generalizable to other regions in China. Future studies using multi-regional collaborative databases or nationally integrated databases are warranted to more accurately assess medication use patterns and further validate the robustness of our findings.

Conclusion

This study based on real-world data presents the antiosteoporosis prescription patterns and trends of osteoporosis patients in Eastern China. Osteoporosis treatment was predominantly based on monotherapy, with active vitamin D and analogs being the most commonly used foundational agents, followed by bisphosphonates, TCM, and denosumab. The use of vitamin D analogs, bisphosphonates and TCM decreased significantly, while denosumab increased significantly. Bisphosphonates, calcitonin, and denosumab are used more frequently in women, elderly patients, and patients with a history of fractures. Attention should be given to weak evidence-based TCM use.

Benford behavior resulting from stick and box fragmentation processes
Bruce Fang, Steven J. Miller

Abstract




Benford’s law is the statement that in many real-world data set, the probability of having digit
d
in base
B
, where
1\leq d\leq B
, as the first digit is
\log_{B}\left((d+1)/d\right)
. We sometimes refer to this as weak Benford behavior, and we say that a data set exhibits strong Benford behavior in base
B
if the probability of having significand at most
s
, where
s\in[1,B)
, is
\log_{B}(s)
. We examine Benford behaviors in two different probabilistic model: stick and box fragmentation. Building on the joint work of
[
B–
]
on the 1-dimensional stick fragmentation model, we employ combinatorial identities on multinomial coefficients to reduce the high dimensional stick fragmentation model to the 1-dimensional model and provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the lengths of the stick fragments to converge to strong Benford behavior. Then we answer a conjecture posed by
[
BDMMM
]
on the high dimensional box fragmentation model. Using tools from order statistics, we prove that under some mild conditions, faces of any arbitrary dimension of the box have total volume converging to strong Benford’s behavior.

1.
Introduction

At the beginning of the
20
th
century, astronomer and mathematician Simon Newcomb
[
Ne
]
noticed an unusual pattern in the logarithmic tables he used at work. The early pages of the books were far more worn than the later ones, suggesting that numbers starting with smaller digits were consulted more often. From this, Newcomb inferred that people had a tendency to encounter numbers beginning with
1
more frequently. Specifically, he found that
1
appeared as the leading digit about
30\%
of the time,
2
about
17\%
, with the frequency decreasing for larger digits. Although he formulated a mathematical explanation for this curious phenomenon, his discovery initially went largely overlooked.

It took another 57 years after Newcomb’s discovery for physicist Frank Benford to make the exact same observation as Newcomb: the first pages of logarithmic tables were used far more than others. He formulated this law as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

[
Ben
, Page 554]
Data exhibits
(weak) Benford behavior base
B
if the frequency
F_{d}
of leading digit
d
is

\displaystyle F_{d}\ =\ \log_{B}\left(\frac{d+1}{d}\right).

(1.1)

Nowadays, Benford’s Law is used in detecting many different forms of fraud, and its prevalence in the world fascinates not only mathematicians, but many other scientists as well (to learn more about Benford’s law and its many applications, we recommend
[
BeHi
,
Nig
,
Mil1
,
NiMi
]
to name a few).

In 1986, Lemons
[
Lem
]
proposed using Benford’s law to analyze the partitioning of a conserved quantity. Since then, driven by the potential application to nuclear fragmentation, mathematicians and physicists have taken an interest in the Benford behavior of various fragmentation processes. Among these processes of interest is
stick fragmentation
.
In a 1-dimensional stick fragmentation model, one begins with a stick of length
L
. Draw
p_{1}
from a probability distribution on
(0,1)
. This fragments the stick into two sub-sticks of lengths
p_{1}L
and
(1-p_{1})L
. For each sub-stick, draw two probabilities
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
from the same distribution. We perform this fragmentation process for a total of
N
stages, where at each stage we fragment all the sub-sticks created during the previous stage according to probabilities. Hence, by the end of
N
stages, there are
2^{N}
sticks. Of particular interest is whether or not this fragmentation process converges to Benford’s behavior.

1.1.
Previous Work on Fragmentation

An important definition when studying a more precise statistical version of Benford behavior is the notion of the significand of a real number, i.e., its leading digits in scientific notation.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a positive real number
x
, we say that its
significand base
B>1
, denoted
S_{B}(x)
, is the unique real number
S_{B}(x)\in[1,B)
such that
x=S_{B}(x)\cdot B^{k}
, where
k
is an integer.

As is common practice with these techniques involving proofs of Benford behavior, we define a more general version of Benford behavior that allows for processes that do not necessarily exhibit Benford behavior at first but converge to it in the limit.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a sequence of random variables
X^{(n)}

converges to strong Benford behavior
base
B
if and only if

\mathbb{P}(S_{B}(X^{(n)})\leq s)\to\log_{B}(s)

(1.2)

for all
s\in[1,B]
. Notice by compactness that this implies uniform convergence of (
1.2
).

An equivalent formulation to the above is the
Uniform Distribution Characterization
, which is especially suited for investigation of products of random variables; see
[
Dia
]
for a proof.

Proposition 1.4

(Uniform Distribution Characterization)
.

[
Dia
]

A sequence of random variables
X^{(n)}
converges to strong Benford behavior in base
B
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\log_{B}(X^{(n)})\textup{
mod }1\leq t\right)\rightarrow t,

(1.3)

for all
t\in[0,1]
. If (
1.4
) is satisfied, then we say that
\log_{B}(X^{(n)})

converges to being equidistributed mod 1
.

We may now state the previous results on stick fragmentation.
Becker et al.
[

Theorem 1.5
.

[
B–

Choose any

N

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle\ =\ Lp^{N}

\displaystyle x_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ Lp^{N-1}(1-p)

\displaystyle x_{3}

\displaystyle\ =\ Lp^{N-2}(1-p)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

\displaystyle x_{N}

\displaystyle\ =\ Lp(1-p)^{N-1}

\displaystyle x_{N+1}

\displaystyle\ =\ L(1-p)^{N},

where the frequency of

.

For any integer

Definition 1.7
.

We say that a set

Definition 1.8

A

The random variables

(2)

.

(3)

The distribution

(4)

(5)

\mathbb{E}[\log_{B}P_{i}]=\mu_{P}\in\mathbb{R}

Definition 1.9

Given an

, we say the

-

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}_{d}(\mathfrak{B})\ :=\ 2^{m-d}\sum_{|I|=d}\prod_{i\in I}(b_{i}-a_{i}),

(1.6)

where we are summing over all subsets

.

[

Theorem 1.10

(Maximum Criterion)
.

[

Let

-dimensional box and

In the same paper, the Maximum Criterion was verified for

Theorem 1.11
.

Let

.

Suppose that

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

2.1.
Preliminary

In
[
FILM
]
, Fang, Irons, Lippelman and Miller proved that if
y_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
, then the distribution of the stick lengths does not follow Benford behavior as
N\rightarrow\infty
. To be exact, they were able to show that the distribution of the logarithms of the stick lengths converges to a discrete distribution, contrary to a uniform distribution on
[0,1]
which is an equivalent condition for convergence to strong Benford behavior by Uniform Distribution Characterization
1.4
. Hence, having
y_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
for some
1\leq i\leq m-1
is a necessary condition for the distribution of the stick lengths to follow Benford behavior.

For the remainder of this section, we show that having
y_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
for some
1\leq i\leq m-1
is also a sufficient condition for the distribution of the stick lengths to converge to Benford’s behavior. Since we can always reorder the
p_{i}^{k_{i}}
’s in the stick length
A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}:=p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}}
and thus the order of
y_{i}
’s in the factorization, then it suffices to show that the first exponent
y_{1}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
is a sufficient condition for the distribution of the stick lengths to converge to Benford’s behavior.

We know that after stage
N
, the number of sticks of length
A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}
is equal to the multinomial coefficient

\displaystyle\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\ =\ \binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}.

(2.1)

From the proof of Theorem , we know that the stick length
A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}
has the factorization

\displaystyle A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\ =\ p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}}\ =\ \left(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{p_{2}}{p_{3}}\right)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{p_{m-1}}{p_{m}}\right)^{\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}k_{j}}(p_{m})^{N},

(2.2)

which motivates us to define the exponents
y_{i}=\log(p_{i}/p_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Now, fix an interval
(a,b)\subset(0,1)
. Let

\displaystyle\chi_{(a,b)}(x)\ :=\ \mathbbm{1}\Big(\log(x)\textup{ mod 1}\in(a,b)\Big)

(2.3)

be the indicator function for
\log(x)\textup{ mod 1}\in(a,b)
. Then after stage
N
, the probability that a stick length mod 1 is in
(a,b)
equals

\displaystyle F_{N}(a,b)\ :=\ \frac{1}{m^{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\dots,k_{m}\leq N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\chi_{(a,b)}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{k_{i}}\right).

(2.4)

Our goal is to prove that as
N\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle F(a,b)\ :=\ \lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}F_{N}(a,b)\ =\ b-a,

(2.5)

which is a sufficient condition for Benford behavior.

2.2.
Combinatorial identities

The key steps to our proof are two combinatorial identities related to multinomial coefficients, which we present and prove below. We first review the multinomial coefficients.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
n,k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}\geq 0
and
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n
, then we define the multinomial coefficients by

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\ :=\ \frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{m}!}.

(2.6)

The following identity on the factorization of multinomial coefficients allows us to express multinomial coefficients in terms of product of binomial coefficients. As we shall see, this forms the key ingredient that reduces our multi-proportion problem into the original single-proportion problem in
[
B–
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Stan
]

For
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\ =\ \prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}}{k_{i}}.

(2.7)

The proof for the identity above is a straightforward induction through the expansion of the multinomial and binomial coefficients. Below is another identity that is especially useful later for evaluating sums of multinomial coefficients.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
Stan
]

For
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

\displaystyle m^{N}\ =\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}\leq N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\ =\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},\dots,k_{m-1}\leq N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m-1}=N\end{subarray}}2^{k_{1}}\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m-1}}.

(2.8)

The most straightforward proof of the identity above relies on two different ways of representing and expanding
m^{N}
: one as
(\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{\textrm{$N$}})^{N}
and the other as
(2+\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{\textrm{$N-2$}})^{N}
. See
[
Stan
]
for more.

2.3.
Truncation

We prove that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the contribution to
F_{N}(a,b)
of multinomial coefficients with
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}
is negligible. This allows us to only consider
k_{1}+k_{2}
that are sufficiently large for these stick lengths
A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\dots,k_{m}\leq N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\end{subarray}}\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}\ \leq\ (m-2)^{N}N^{N^{\epsilon}+2}.

(2.9)

Proof.

We know that if
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=N
, then

\displaystyle\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{N(N-1)\cdots(N-(k_{1}+k_{2})+1)}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}\cdot\frac{(N-(k_{1}+k_{2}))!}{k_{3}!k_{4}!\cdots k_{m}!}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ N^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\cdot\binom{N-(k_{1}+k_{2})}{k_{3},k_{4},\dots,k_{m}}.

(2.10)

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}\leq N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\end{subarray}}\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\end{subarray}}N^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{3},k_{4},\dots,k_{m}\leq N-(k_{1}+k_{2})\\
k_{3}+k_{4}+\cdots+k_{m}=N-(k_{1}+k_{2})\end{subarray}}\binom{N-(k_{1}+k_{2})}{k_{3},k_{4},\dots,k_{m}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ (m-2)^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\end{subarray}}N^{k_{1}+k_{2}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ (m-2)^{N}(N^{\epsilon})^{2}N^{N^{\epsilon}}\ \leq\ (m-2)^{N}N^{N^{\epsilon}+2}.

(2.11)

∎

We know the probability that a stick length
p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}}
satisfies
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\dots,k_{m}\leq N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=N\\
k_{1}+k_{2}<N^{\epsilon}\end{subarray}}\binom{N}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}.

(2.12)

According to Proposition
2.4
, we have that (
2.12
) is bounded above by
((m-2)/m)^{N}N^{N^{\epsilon}+2}
.
Hence, the logarithm of the probability is bounded above by

\displaystyle\log\left(\left(\frac{m-2}{m}\right)^{N}N^{N^{\epsilon}+2}\right)\ =\ N\log\left(\frac{m-2}{m}\right)+(N^{\epsilon}+2)\log(N).

(2.13)

It is clear that (
2.13
) goes to
-\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Thus the probability goes to 0 as
N\rightarrow\infty
. So it suffices to consider the case where
k_{1}+k_{2}\geq N^{\epsilon}
. Now, fix
k(N)\geq N^{\epsilon}
and let
0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\leq k(N)
with
k_{1}+k_{2}=k(N)
and
0\leq k_{3},k_{4},\dots,k_{m}\leq N-k(N)
with
k_{3}+k_{4}+\dots+k_{m}=N-k(N)
. By
[
B–
]
, we know that the frequency
k_{1}
of stick length
A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}=p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots p_{m}^{k_{m}}
follows a binomial distribution with mean
k(N)/2
and standard deviation
\sqrt{k(N)}/2
. Pick some
\delta\in(0,\epsilon/10)
. We see that it suffices to consider cases where
|k_{1}-k(N)/2|<(\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil\sqrt{k(N)})/2
, because the probability that
k_{1}
is outside this range is asymptotically small by Chebyshev’s inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|k_{1}-\frac{k(N)}{2}\right|\ \geq\ \frac{\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil\sqrt{k(N)}}{2}\right)\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil^{2}}.

(2.14)

2.4.
Near uniform probability within small intervals

We keep the same notation and definition and fix
\epsilon
,
\delta
,
k(N):=k_{1}+k_{2}
,
k_{3},k_{4},\dots,k_{m}
as before. Our goal is to prove that the stick length
A_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}}
is roughly uniformly distributed over small intervals of

. Since
\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil=o(\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil\lceil\sqrt{k(N)}/2\rceil)
, we can evenly divide the range of
k_{1}
between
k(N)/2\pm\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil\lceil\sqrt{k(N)}/2\rceil
into intervals of
\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil
values of
k_{1}
, where the
\ell
th
interval starts with
k_{1,\ell}=k_{1,\ell,0}
and ranges over
k_{1,\ell,i}
for
0\leq i\leq\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil-1
, as defined below:

\displaystyle k_{1,\ell}\ :=\ f_{\ell}\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}\right)+\ell\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil,\quad k_{1,\ell,i}\ :=\ f_{\ell}\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}\right)+\ell\lceil N^{\delta}\rceil+i,\quad 0\leq i\leq N^{\delta}-1,

where

when

when

to
\lceil\sqrt{k(N)}/2\rceil

is asymptotically smaller than

uniformly for all

. Since the binomial distribution is symmetric around its mean, it suffices to look at

\displaystyle\left|\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell,i}}-\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell,j}}\right|

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \left|\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}-\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell+1}}\right|.

(2.16)

We follow Section (5.52) of
[
B–
]

Proposition 2.5

For

\displaystyle\left|\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}-\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell+1}}\right|\ \leq\ O\left(\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}\cdot N^{-\frac{3\epsilon}{10}}\right).

(2.17)

Since the proof follows similar argument to Section (5.52) of

2.5.
Equidistribution within small intervals

For fixed

[

3.
Proof of Theorem

3.1.
Preliminary

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.13
, i.e., resolve Conjecture 4.1 of
[
BDMMM
]
that we mentioned in Section
1
under some mild conditions. Let us first recall the conjecture.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Every linear fragmentation process satisfies the maximum criterion in all dimensions
1\leq d\leq m
.

[
BDMMM
]
has verified the conjecture in the case of
d=1
and arbitrary
m
for i.i.d. log-uniform distributions, i.e., the proportion at which each dimension is cut is
P_{i}
, where
\log P_{i}\sim\textup{Uniform}(a,b)
and
a<b\leq 0
. They decide to normalize
P_{i}
to
\textup{Uniform}(-\sqrt{3},\sqrt{3})
with mean 0 and variance 1, which simplifies the calculation later on and does not affect the nature or the solution to the problem. After stage
N
, the side length of the
i
th
dimension of the box, after taking logarithm and normalizing, is

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{(N)}\ :=\ \frac{\log_{B}(P_{i}^{(1)}\cdots P_{i}^{(N)})-N\mu_{P}}{\sqrt{N}\sigma_{P}}\ =\ \frac{\log_{B}(P_{i}^{(1)}\cdots P_{i}^{(N)})}{\sqrt{N}}.

(3.1)

By the CLT,
Z_{i}^{(N)}
converges to
N(0,1)
. Moreover, Lemma 3.1 states that if
f_{Z_{i}^{(N)}}(z)
is the probability density function of
Z_{i}^{(N)}
and
\varphi(z)
is the probability density function of
N(0,1)
, then for all
z\in[-\sqrt{3}N,\sqrt{3}N]
and any

, we have

\displaystyle f_{Z_{i}^{(N)}}(z_{i})\ =\ \varphi(z_{i})+O(N^{-1+4\epsilon}),

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle\varphi(x)\ :=\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}.

(3.3)

Due to the presence of the error term, to establish the strong Benford behavior of the process, they need to rely on the fact that the distributions for the cuts are finitely supported with uniformly bounded supports. They also need to rely on the fact that the exponent of the error term is less than
-1/2
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, which allows the error term from integrating

over the support to be asymptotically 0.

For the rest of this paper, we prove Conjecture 4.1 of
[
BDMMM
]
for any

and

. Physically, this means that we consider any arbitrary dimensional volume of faces of that dimension of the
m
-dimensional box. We assume that the

are i.i.d., have mean 0 and variance 1, are finitely supported with support

is

is given by

, where
\Phi
is the CDF of
N(0,1)
and

. We prove that the maximum
d
-dimensional volume
\mathfrak{m}_{d}^{(N)}
of a

-dimensional face of the

-dimensional box converges to strong Benford behavior. Once we prove this statement, then by the maximum criterion established in
[

]

The key idea behind our proof is the use of the Mean Value Theorem and a recursive method of change of variables and consistent cancellation that provide upper bounds on the error terms in terms of Gaussian densities. We shall explore this more in Section

To begin, we use knowledge from order statistics to find the PDF of

.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose that

are random variables, and
[]_{i}
returns the
i
th
largest number among a list
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})

\displaystyle X_{(k-i+1)}(w)\ :=\ [(X_{1}(w),X_{2}(w),\dots,X_{k}(w))]_{k-i+1}.

(3.4)

We say that the random variable

is
the
i
th

, or the
i
th
largest random variable among
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}
.
Hence, as random variables,
X_{(1)}\leq X_{(2)}\leq\dots\leq X_{(k)}

Let

Proposition 3.3
.

[
DL
]

\displaystyle\ =\ C_{m}^{m+1-d}\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})+B(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\varphi(z_{m+i-d})+A(z_{m+i-d})\right).

(3.7)

By binomial expansion,

\displaystyle\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})+B(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}\ =\ \sum_{j=0}^{m-d}\binom{m-d}{j}(\Phi(z_{m+1-d}))^{m-d-j}(B(z_{m+1-d}))^{j}.

(3.8)

Note that

\displaystyle\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})+B(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}\ =\ \left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}+O(B(z_{m+1-d})).

(3.9)

We make a slight abuse of notation here to write the error term above as

\displaystyle f_{Z_{(m+1-d)},\dots,Z_{(m)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})

\displaystyle\ =\ C_{m}^{m+1-d}\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})^{m-d}+B(z_{m+1-d})\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\varphi(z_{m+i-d})+A(z_{m+i-d})\right).

(3.10)

3.2.

In this section, we find the PDF of

Z_{(m+1-d)}^{(N)}\leq\cdots\leq Z_{(m)}^{(N)}
. In general, we know that any
1\leq j\leq d
,
z_{m+j-d}
can take value from
z_{m+(j-1)-d}
to
(y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d})/(d-j+1)
. The only exception is
z_{m+1-d}
, whose upper bound is already correctly stated but the lower bound is
-C\sqrt{N}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle F_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}f_{Z_{(m+1-d)},\dots,Z_{(m)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d},

(3.11)

where we use the product of integrals to denote

\displaystyle\prod_{j=k}^{d}\int_{z_{j}}^{y_{j}}\ :=\ \int_{z_{k}}^{y_{k}}\cdots\int_{z_{j}}^{y_{j}}\cdots\int_{z_{d}}^{y_{d}}.

(3.12)

We separate out the main term
C_{m}^{m+1-d}\Phi(z_{m+1-d})^{m-d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})
of the joint PDF (
3.1
) and denote it by

\displaystyle m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})\ :=\ C_{m}^{m+1-d}\Phi(z_{m+1-d})^{m-d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d}).

(3.13)

We also denote the error term of the joint PDF (
3.1
) by

e_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
and
\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
be the main term and the error term of
F_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
. Then

\displaystyle F_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)+\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}e_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.14)

We are now ready to find the PDF
f_{Y^{(N)}}
of
Y^{(N)}
. We know that

\displaystyle f_{Y^{(N)}}(y)\ =\ \frac{d}{dy}F_{Y^{(N)}}(y)\ =\ \frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)+\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y).

(3.15)

To differentiate
\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
and
\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
, we apply the
Leibniz integral rule
for differentiate under the integral sign.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
F
]

Suppose that
f(x,t)
is a function such that both
f(x,t)
and
df(x,t)/dx
are continuous in
t
and
x
in the
xt
-plane, and
a(x)
and
b(x)
are also continuously differentiable. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\int_{a(x)}^{b(x)}f(x,t)dt\ =\ f(x,b(x))\cdot\frac{d}{dx}b(x)-f(x,a(x))\cdot\frac{d}{dx}a(x)+\int_{a(x)}^{b(x)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,t)dt.

(3.16)

For the rest of this paper, one can easily check the regularity conditions on
f(x,t)
,
a(x)
, and
b(x)
each time we apply the Leibniz integral rule, so there will not be explicit mention of the regularity conditions again. We first make some observations on the effect of
d/dy
on

\displaystyle\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}g(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

(3.17)

for any Riemann integrable function
g(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})
. One can check that each application of the Leibniz integral rule to (

) gives two integrals, one of which has the lower bound of the integration coinciding with the upper bound of the integration and thus equals to 0. As a result, we are always left with one integral that interchanges the order of the differentiation and the integration signs. Eventually,
(
3.17
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\int_{z_{m-1}}^{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}}g(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\right)dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}g\left(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m-1},y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.18)

Applying (
3.2
) to
d\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
and
d\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
, where
\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
and
\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
are as defined in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}\left(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m-1},y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)dz_{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}e_{Y^{(N)}}\left(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m-1},y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)dz_{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.19)

3.3.
Outline of problem

We are ready to formulate our problem. We want to show that
\mathfrak{m}_{d}^{(N)}
converges to strong Benford behavior, which is equivalent to showing that
\log_{B}(\mathfrak{m}_{d}^{(N)})
converges to being equidistributed mod 1. Hence, in terms of the probability density function
f_{Y^{(N)}}(y)
of
Y^{(N)}:=\log_{B}(\mathfrak{m}_{d}^{(N)})/\sqrt{N}
, we can formulate our problem as to showing

\displaystyle F_{N}(a,b)\ :=\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}f_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy\ \approx\ b-a,

(3.20)

for all
(a,b)\subset(0,1)
for sufficiently large
N
. Let the main term and the error term of
F_{N}(a,b)
be defined respectively as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ :=\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy;

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ :=\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy.

(3.22)

Our goal is to show

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)\ \approx\ b-a,\quad\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)\ \approx\ 0.

(3.23)

3.4.
Upper bound on error term
\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)

We want to show that the error term
\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)
is negligible. We start by giving some bounds on
d\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
from (
3.2
). Recall that
e_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})
is the error term of the joint density function (
3.1
). Let
[\ell]
be short hand for
\{1,\dots,\ell\}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{m}^{m+1-d}}e_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})

\displaystyle\quad\ =\ \Phi(z_{m+1-d})^{m-d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\varphi(z_{m+i-d})+A(z_{m+i-d}))-\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+B(z_{m+1-d})\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\varphi(z_{m+i-d})+A(z_{m+i-d}))

\displaystyle\quad\ =\ \left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})^{m-d}+B(z_{m+1-d})\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset[d]\\
S\neq d\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+B(z_{m+1-d})\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset[d]\\
S\neq d\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right)+N^{-\delta}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d}).

(3.24)

We know from (
3.2
) that to bound
d\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
, it suffices to bound

\displaystyle D_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\ :=\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset[d]\\
S\neq[d]\end{subarray}}\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right)\Bigg|_{z_{m}=y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

(3.25)

as well as

\displaystyle D_{2}(y)\ :=\ N^{-\delta}\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\Bigg|_{z_{m}=y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}}dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d},

(3.26)

because then we have
d\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}dy\ll D_{1}(y)+D_{2}(y)
. We first look at
D_{1}(y)
. Suppose that
k_{S}
is the largest index such that
k\in[d]\setminus S
. Such an index
k
exists, since
S\neq[d]
. First, assume that we have
S
’s where
k_{S}=d
. Then the integrand of (
3.4
) becomes

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right)\Bigg|_{z_{m}=y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}}

\displaystyle\quad\ =\ \left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d-1]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right)A\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d-1]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right),

(3.27)

where in the last line we use the fact that
A(x)=O(N^{-1/2-\delta})
. Note that the lower and upper bounds on the interval of integration for each integral in (
3.4
) are
O(\sqrt{N})
, since
y,z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m-1}
are all
O(\sqrt{N})
. Choose a constant
C^{\prime}
such that
C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}
is an upper bound on the upper bounds on the interval of integration for all the integrals in (
3.4
) and
-C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}
is a lower bound on the interval of integration for all the integrals in (
3.4
). Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d]\setminus S}A(z_{m+i-d})\right)\Bigg|_{z_{m}=y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}}

\displaystyle dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ N^{-1/2-\delta}\underbrace{\int_{-C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}^{C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}\cdots\int_{-C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}^{C\sqrt{N}}}_{d-1}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\prod_{i\in[d-1]\setminus S}N^{-1/2-\delta}\right)dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ N^{-1/2-\delta-|[d-1]\setminus S|\delta}\prod_{i\in S}\left(\int_{-C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}^{C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})dz_{m+i-d}\right)\ \ll\ N^{-1/2-(1+|[d-1]\setminus S|)\delta}\ \ll\ N^{-1/2-\delta},

(3.28)

where in the last line we use the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{-C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}^{C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}\varphi(x)dx\ \ll\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(x)dx\ =\ 1,

(3.29)

which we shall use frequently without reference from now on. Since the number of subsets
S
of
[d]
is finite, then (
3.4
) gives us

\displaystyle D_{1}(y)\ \ll\ N^{-1/2-\delta}.

(3.30)

The case when
k_{S}<d
is similar, though more care is needed to identify the
O(N^{-1/2-\delta})
decay, which essentially comes from the
A(z_{m+k_{S}-d})
term. With this in mind, using the same method for the case when
k_{S}=d
as well as the convolution formula for Gaussian PDF, we can again show that
D_{1}(y)\ll N^{-1/2-\delta}
. We leave the details to Appendix
B
.

We now turn to
D_{2}(y)
as defined in (
3.26
), which is relatively more straightforward to bound. We have

\displaystyle D_{2}(y)\ \ll\ N^{-\delta}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-1}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\varphi\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.31)

We recognize that the integral is the convolution of
d
standard Gaussian density functions. We know that the convolution is the probability density function of sum of
d
i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables, which itself is also with mean 0 and variance
d
. Hence,

\displaystyle D_{2}(y)\ \ll\ N^{-\delta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}e^{-y^{2}/(2d)}.

(3.32)

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ll\ D_{1}(y)+D_{2}(y)\ \ll\ N^{-\delta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}e^{-y^{2}/(2d)}+N^{-1/2-\delta}.

(3.33)

We are now ready to bound the error term
\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)
from (
3.22
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{E}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy

\displaystyle\ \ll\ N^{-\delta}\int_{-dC\sqrt{N}}^{dC\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}e^{-y^{2}/(2d)}dy+\int_{-dC\cdot\sqrt{N}}^{dC\cdot\sqrt{N}}N^{-1/2-\delta}dy\ \ll\ N^{-\delta}.

(3.34)

3.5.
Strategy for remainder of proof

Our next task is to show that the main term
\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)
defined in (
3.21
) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)\ :=\ \sum_{-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy\ \approx\ b-a.

(3.35)

Our strategy is the following. First, we want to write the integral of
d\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
over each interval
[(a+n)/\sqrt{N},(b+n)/\sqrt{N}]
as the sum of a main term
m_{n}(a,b)
that is constant with respect to
y
within the interval and an error term
e_{n}(a,b)
for each
n
. Essentially, this shows that
d\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
is almost constant within a small interval, incurring an negligible cost that, as we shall prove, does not accumulate, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)\ =\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}e_{n}(a,b)\ \approx\ 0.

(3.36)

Finally, we prove that the sum of the main term

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)\ =\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}m_{n}(a,b)

(3.37)

is a Riemann sum that converges to
b-a
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)\ =\ \mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)+\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)\ \approx\ b-a.

(3.38)

Thus, based on the estimate on the error term in (
3.22
),

\displaystyle F_{N}(a,b)\ =\ \mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)+\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)\ \approx\ b-a,

(3.39)

which establishes the equidistribution result.

3.6.
Equidistribution within small interval

In this section, we show that
d\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
is equidistributed within each small interval
[(a+n)/\sqrt{N},(b+n)/\sqrt{N}]
. For each
n
, we write the integral over
[(a+n)/\sqrt{N},(b+n)/\sqrt{N}]
in terms of a main term
m_{n}(a,b)
that is constant with respect to
y
and an error term
e_{n}(a,b)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy\ =\ m_{n}(a,b)+e_{n}(a,b),

(3.40)

where

\displaystyle m_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ :=\ \int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)dy,

\displaystyle e_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ :=\ \int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)-\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)dy.

(3.41)

Since
d\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(n/\sqrt{N})/dy
is constant, then the main term
m_{n}(a,b)
is

\displaystyle m_{n}(a,b)\ =\ \frac{b-a}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

(3.42)

We now quantify the error term. We start by providing an upper bound on

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)-\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right|,

(3.43)

for
y\in[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]\supset[(a+n)/\sqrt{N},(b+n)/\sqrt{N}]
. As we shall see,
d\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy
is a continuous function over
[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]
and a differentiable function over
(n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N})
. By the Mean Value Theorem, for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]
with
y_{1}<y_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y_{1})-\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y_{2})\ =\ \frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(c_{n})(y_{1}-y_{2})

(3.44)

for some
c_{n}\in(y_{1},y_{2})
. Hence

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y_{1})-\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y_{2})\right|\ \leq\ \left|\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(c_{n})\right||y_{1}-y_{2}|\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left|\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(c_{n})\right|.

(3.45)

We want to provide an upper on
|d^{2}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy^{2}|
over the interval
[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]
. Recall from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}\left(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m-1},y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)dz_{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\ =\ \ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\varphi\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad dz_{m-1}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.46)

Following the same procedure as shown in Section
3.2
, we can again exchange the order of differentiation and integration.
Since the product in the integrand in (
3.6
) goes from
j=2
to
d-1
, we need to discuss the cases when
d=2
and
d>2
separately. If
d=2
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{2}}m_{Y^{{(N)}}}\left(z_{m-1},y-z_{m-1}\right)dz_{m-1}.

(3.47)

By the Leibniz integral rule and (
3.13
),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{1}{2}m_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{y}{2},\frac{y}{2}\right)+\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m-1},y-z_{m-1})\right)dz_{m-1}

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{2}}(\Phi(z_{m-1}))^{m-2}\varphi(z_{m-1})\cdot(-1)\cdot(y-z_{m-1})\varphi(y-z_{m-1})dz_{m-1}.

(3.48)

We know that
y
is an upper bound on the value of
z_{m-1}+z_{m}
, then
y\geq-2C\sqrt{N}
, and
y/2\geq-C\sqrt{N}
. We now break into cases when
y\leq 0
and
y\geq 0
. When
y\leq 0
,
\varphi(z_{m-1})
is at most
\varphi(y/2)
on
[-C\sqrt{N},y/2]
. So (
3.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|y-z_{m-1}|\varphi(y-z_{m-1})dz_{m-1}

\displaystyle\ =\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z_{m-1}|\varphi(z_{m-1})dz_{m-1}\ \ll\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right),

(3.49)

where in the last line we use the fact that the integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|\varphi(x)dx
is the expected value of the absolute value of a standard normal random variable, which is finite. When
y\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle y-z_{m-1}

\displaystyle\ \geq\ y-\frac{y}{2}\ =\ \frac{y}{2}\ \geq\ 0.

(3.50)

Hence, (
3.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{2}}(y-z_{m-1})\varphi(y-z_{m-1})dz_{m-1}

\displaystyle\ =\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\varphi(y-z_{m-1})\Bigg|_{z_{m-1}=-C\sqrt{N}}^{z_{m-1}=\frac{y}{2}}\ \ll\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\varphi\left(y+C\sqrt{N}\right),

(3.51)

where in the second line we employ the fact that
\int(y-x)\varphi(y-x)dx=\varphi(y-x)
. Thus, if
d=2
, regardless of whether
y\leq 0
or
y\geq 0
,
d^{2}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy^{2}
is on the order of
\varphi(y/2)+\varphi(y+C\sqrt{N})
. This concludes the estimate of
d^{2}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy^{2}
for
d=2
.

If
d>2
, we repeatedly apply Leibniz integral rule as we have demonstrated in Subsection
3.4
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\quad\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-2}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Bigg(\int_{z_{m-2}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}}m_{Y^{(N)}}\Bigg(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m-1},y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad dz_{m-1}\Bigg)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d-2}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\varphi\left(\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\Bigg(\int_{z_{m-2}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}}(\Phi(z_{m+1-d}))^{m-d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)(-1)\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\cdot\varphi\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)dz_{m-1}\Bigg)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.52)

Now, similar to the case when
d=2
, we estimate the second integrand in (
3.6
) by breaking down into cases when
(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d})/2\leq 0
and
(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d})\geq 0
. We leave the details to Appendix
C
. Hence, we have
d^{2}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)/dy^{2}
in (
3.6
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\varphi\left(\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}\right)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\varphi\left(y-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}\right)-z_{m-2}\right)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.53)

We first obtain some estimate on the first integral of (
3.6
). The idea behind estimating this integral is recursive uses of change of variables that lead to consistent cancellation. We first do the change of variable
z_{m-2}\mapsto z_{m-2}+(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d})/5
. We have

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\varphi\left(\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}\right)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z^{2}_{m+i-d}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\left(z_{m-2}+\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d}\right)/5\right)^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\cdot e^{-\frac{\frac{1}{4}\left(-z_{m-2}+4\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d}\right)/5\right)^{2}}{2}}dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.54)

Notice that in the expansion of the exponents, we get a cancellation of the term
2z_{m-2}((y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d})/5)
. Hence, (
3.6
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-3}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d}^{2}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\frac{1}{5}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d}\right)^{2}}{2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\frac{5}{4}z_{m-2}^{2}}{2}}dz_{m-2}\right)dz_{m-3}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-3}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d}^{2}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\frac{1}{5}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}z_{m+i-d}\right)^{2}}{2}}dz_{m-3}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

(3.55)

We see that the estimate in (
3.6
) has a similar structure, which allows us to use induction to estimate the interval. For an arbitrary
k
such that
1\leq k\leq d-2
, we want to look at

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{k}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}z^{2}_{m+i-d}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{A_{k}(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{m+i-d})^{2}}{2}}dz_{m+k-d}\cdots dz_{m+1-d},

(3.56)

where
A_{k}>0
. Using the change of variable
z_{m+k-d}\mapsto z_{m+k-d}+\frac{A_{k}}{1+A_{k}}(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d})
, (
3.56
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{k}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z^{2}_{m+i-d}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\left(z_{m+k-d}+\frac{A_{k}}{1+A_{k}}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)\right)^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\cdot e^{-\frac{A_{k}\left(-z_{m+k-d}+\frac{1}{1+A_{k}}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)\right)^{2}}{2}}dz_{m+k-d}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.57)

Expanding the exponents also gives a cancellation of the term

\displaystyle\frac{2A_{k}}{1+A_{k}}z_{m+k-d}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d}\right).

(3.58)

Hence, (
3.56
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{k}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z^{2}_{m+i-d}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{A_{k-1}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)^{2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{(1+A_{k})z^{2}_{m+k-d}}{2}}dz_{m+k-d}\cdots dz_{m+1-d},

(3.59)

where

\displaystyle A_{k-1}\ :=\ \frac{A_{k}^{2}}{1+A_{k}^{2}}+\frac{A_{k}}{(1+A_{k})^{2}}.

(3.60)

Note that since
A_{k}>0
, then
A_{k+1}>0
. Hence, (
3.56
) becomes bounded above by

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{k-1}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z^{2}_{m+i-d}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{A_{k-1}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)^{2}}{2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(1+A_{k})z_{m+k-d}^{2}}{2}}dz_{m+k-d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad dz_{m+(k-1)-d}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{k-1}e^{-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z^{2}_{m+i-d}}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{A_{k-1}\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)^{2}}{2}}dz_{m+(k-1)-d}\cdots dz_{m+1-d},

(3.61)

Thus, by induction, (
3.6
) becomes bounded above by

\displaystyle e^{-(A_{0}y^{2})/2},

(3.62)

where
A_{0}>0
is recursively defined by (
3.60
) with
A_{d-2}=1/4
. Similarly, we can show by induction that the second integral in (
3.6
) has the estimate

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\varphi\left(y-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}\right)-z_{m-2}\right)\right)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}\ \ll\ e^{-(B_{0}y^{2})/2}

(3.63)

for some
B_{0}>0
. Hence, if
d>2
, (
3.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ll\ e^{-(A_{0}y^{2})/2}+e^{-(B_{0}y^{2})/2}\ \ll\ e^{-(C_{0}y^{2})/2},

(3.64)

where
C_{0}:=\min\{A_{0},B_{0}\}
. Recall from (
3.6
) that when
d=2
,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)\ \ll\ \varphi\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+\varphi\left(y+C\sqrt{N}\right).

(3.65)

Thus, regardless of whether
d=2
or
d>2
,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)\ \ll\ e^{-\frac{D_{0}y^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(y+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}},

(3.66)

where
D_{0}:=\min\{C_{0},1/4\}
. Note that
e^{-D_{0}y^{2}/2}
and
\varphi(\sqrt{D_{0}}(y+C\sqrt{N}))
each has only one global extreme, at
y=0
and
y=-C\sqrt{N}
respectively. Hence, locally on
[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]
the functions are monotonic and can be bounded above by the sum of its values at the two end points. Hence, for all
n
,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)\ \ll\ e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}},

(3.67)

for all
y\in[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]
. Thus, returning to (
3.45
), for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in[n/\sqrt{N},(n+1)/\sqrt{N}]
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y_{1})-\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}\right).

(3.68)

Thus, the error term
e_{n}(a,b)
as defined in (
3.6
) is bounded by

\displaystyle e_{n}(a,b)\ \leq\ \int_{\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{N}}}^{\frac{b+n}{\sqrt{N}}}\left|\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)-\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}\left(e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}\right).

(3.69)

3.7.
Upper bound on error term
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)

In this section, we want to prove that the error term of the main term
\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)
is small, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err,N}}(a,b)\ =\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}e_{n}(a,b)\ \approx\ 0.

(3.70)

Based on the estimate
e_{n}(a,b)
in (
3.6
), we can pull out one of the
1/\sqrt{N}
factors in the bracket and obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)\ =\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}e_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\left(e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{D_{0}\left(\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{N}}+C\sqrt{N}\right)^{2}}{2}}\right)\right).

(3.71)

We observe that the above in the parentheses are Riemann sums for
e^{-D_{0}x^{2}/2}
and
e^{-D_{0}(x+C\sqrt{N})^{2}/2}
on
(-\infty,\infty)
, which are both finite. Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)\ \ll\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}.

(3.72)

We have thus established that the error term
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)
of the main term
\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)
is negligible.

3.8.
Evaluation of main term
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)

Finally, we establish the main term
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)
of the main term
\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)
. From (
3.37
) and (
3.42
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}m_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ =\ (b-a)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=-dC\cdot N}^{dC\cdot N-1}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right).

(3.73)

Since the term in the parentheses above is a Riemann sum for
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=-2C\cdot N}^{2C\cdot N-1}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)dy+o(1)

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(\infty)-\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(-\infty)+o(1).

(3.74)

Hence the main term
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)
becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)\ =\ (b-a)(\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(\infty)-\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(-\infty))+o(1).

(3.75)

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-C\sqrt{N}}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}.

(3.76)

To simplify our calculation, we want to extend the interval of integration of the outer integral to
-\infty
. We do so by showing that the tail of the integral is small, i.e.,

\displaystyle T_{N}\ :=\ \int_{-\infty}^{-C\sqrt{N}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}\ \approx\ 0.

(3.77)

We have

\displaystyle T_{N}

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \int_{-\infty}^{-C\sqrt{N}}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-1}(\Phi(z_{m+1-d}))^{m-d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\ \ll\ \int_{-\infty}^{-C\sqrt{N}}\varphi(z_{m+1-d})dz_{m+1-d},

(3.78)

Hence, we have reduced the problem to showing the smallness of the Gaussian tail. We first make a definition.

Definition 3.5
.

For functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
, we say that
f(x)=\Theta(g(x))
if
f(x)=O(g(x))
and
g(x)=O(f(x))
.

The following result is a straightforward calculation that provides an upper bound on the Gaussian tail.

Proposition 3.6
.

[
Mil1
]

Suppose that
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a function such that
g(N)=\Theta(N^{\epsilon^{\prime}})
for some
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-g(N)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx\ =\ \int_{g(N)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx\ \ll\ e^{-g(N)/2}.

(3.79)

Back to (
3.8
), by Proposition
3.6

\displaystyle T_{N}\ \ll\ e^{-C\sqrt{N}}.

(3.80)

Thus, (
3.76
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{-\infty}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}+O\left(e^{-C\sqrt{N}}\right).

(3.81)

Now, by Proposition
3.3
,

\displaystyle m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})\ :=\ C_{m}^{m+1-d}\left(\Phi(z_{m+1-d})\right)^{m-d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})

(3.82)

is the joint PDF of
W_{(m+1-d)},\dots,W_{(m)}
, where
W_{1},\dots,W_{m}
are i.i.d.
\sim N(0,1)
. Thus, following our derivation in Subsection
3.2
,

\displaystyle H(y)\ :=\ \int_{-\infty}^{\frac{y}{d}}\prod_{j=2}^{d}\int_{z_{m+(j-1)-d}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}z_{m+i-d}}{d-j+1}}m_{Y^{(N)}}(z_{m+1-d},\dots,z_{m})dz_{m}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

(3.83)

is the CDF of
\sum_{i=1}^{d}W_{(m+i-d)}
. We know that for a cumulative density function
H(y)
,
H(-\infty)=0
and
H(\infty)=1
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(-\infty)

\displaystyle\ =\ H(-\infty)+O(e^{-C\sqrt{N}})\ =\ O\left(e^{-C\sqrt{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(\infty)

\displaystyle\ =\ H(\infty)+O(e^{-C\sqrt{N}})\ =\ 1+O\left(e^{-C\sqrt{N}}\right).

(3.84)

Thus, substituting these two estimates into (
3.75
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)\ =\ (b-a)\left(1+O\left(e^{-C\sqrt{N}}\right)\right)+o(1))\ =\ (b-a)+o(1).

(3.85)

Returning to (
3.39
) and (
3.38
), combined with the estimate for the error term
\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)
in (
3.4
) and the error term
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)
of
\mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)
in (
3.72
), we have

\displaystyle F_{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathcal{M}_{N}(a,b)+\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathcal{M}_{\textup{main},N}(a,b)+\mathcal{M}_{\textup{err},N}(a,b)+\mathcal{E}_{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle\ =\ (b-a)+o(1)+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)+O(N^{-\delta})

\displaystyle\ =\ (b-a)+o(1).

(3.86)

We conclude that
\log_{B}(\mathfrak{m}_{d}^{(N)})
converges to being equidistributed mod 1, and therefore by Uniform Distribution Characterization
\mathfrak{m}_{d}^{(N)}
converges to strong Benford behavior.
∎

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
2.5

In this section, we prove Proposition
2.5
which provides a quantitative bound on the difference among probabilities within an interval. Let us first recall the statement of Proposition
2.5
.

Proposition
2.5
.

For
\ell\leq\sqrt{k(N)}/2
,

\displaystyle\left|\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}-\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell+1}}\right|\ \leq\ O\left(\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}\cdot N^{-\frac{3\epsilon}{10}}\right).

(A.1)

Proof.

We first factor out
\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}
from the difference:

\displaystyle\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}-\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell+1}}\ =\ \binom{k(N)}{\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}}-\binom{k(N)}{\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{k(N)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!}-\frac{k(N)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{k(N)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!-k(N)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{k(N)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!-\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!}

\displaystyle\ =\ \binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}\left(1-\frac{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!}\right).

(A.2)

Now, we analyze the term

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell,N}\ :=\ \frac{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)!}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)!}.

(A.3)

We want to show that
\alpha_{\ell,N}\rightarrow 1
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, so that the difference in (
A
) is asymptotically much smaller than the main term
\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)^{N^{\delta}}}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+(\ell+1)N^{\delta}\right)^{N^{\delta}}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \alpha_{\ell,N}\ \leq\ \frac{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}-\ell N^{\delta}\right)^{N^{\delta}}}{\left(\frac{k(N)}{2}+\ell N^{\delta}\right)^{N^{\delta}}}

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{4(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}\right)^{N^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \alpha_{\ell,N}\ \leq\left(1-\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\right)^{N^{\delta}}.

(A.4)

Since
\ell\leq\sqrt{k(N)}/2
, then

\displaystyle 0\ \leq\ \frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\ \leq\ \frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)}\ \leq\ \frac{2N^{\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}.

(A.5)

Since
k(N)\geq N^{\epsilon}
, and
\delta\in(0,\epsilon/10)
, then

\displaystyle 0\ \leq\ \frac{2N^{2\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \frac{2N^{\epsilon/10}}{N^{\epsilon/2}}\ =\ 2N^{-2\epsilon/5}\ =\ O(N^{-2\epsilon/5}).

(A.6)

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{4(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}\ =\ O(N^{-2\epsilon/5}).

(A.7)

Hence, for sufficiently large
N
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\ <\ 1-\frac{4(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}\ <\ 1

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\ <\ 1-\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\ <\ 1.

(A.8)

Returning to (
A
), given (
A
), we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{4(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}\right)^{N^{\delta}}\ \leq\ \alpha_{\ell,N}\ \leq\ \left(1-\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\right)^{N^{\delta}}.

(A.9)

Using binomial expansion,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}\binom{N^{\delta}}{j}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{4(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \alpha_{\ell,N}-1\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}\binom{N^{\delta}}{j}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\right)^{j}.

(A.10)

We first bound the right sum in (
A
). Using the assumption that
\ell\leq\sqrt{k(N)}/2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}\binom{N^{\delta}}{j}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\right)^{j}\right|

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}N^{j\delta}\left(\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}N^{j\delta}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{k(N)}N^{\delta}}{k(N)}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}\left(\frac{2N^{2\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{2N^{2\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}\cdot\frac{1-\left(\frac{2N^{2\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}\right)^{N^{\delta}}}{1-\frac{2N^{2\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}},

(A.11)

where on the last line we use the geometric series formula. Since
k(N)\geq N^{\epsilon}
, and
\delta\in(0,\epsilon/10)
, then

\displaystyle 0\ \leq\ \frac{2N^{2\delta}}{\sqrt{k(N)}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \frac{2N^{\epsilon/5}}{N^{\epsilon/2}}\ =\ 2N^{-3\epsilon/10}.

(A.12)

Hence

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}\binom{N^{\delta}}{j}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{4\ell N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2\ell N^{\delta}}\right)^{j}\right|

\displaystyle\ \leq\ 2N^{-3\epsilon/10}\cdot\frac{1}{1-2N^{-3\epsilon/10}}

\displaystyle\ =\ O\left(N^{-3\epsilon/10}\right).

(A.13)

Similarly, for the left sum in (
A
), we also have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\delta}}\binom{N^{\delta}}{j}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{4(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}{k(N)+2(\ell+1)N^{\delta}}\right)^{j}\right|\ =\ O\left(N^{-3\epsilon/10}\right).

(A.14)

Applying (
A
) and (
A.14
) to (
A
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{\ell,N}-1\right|\ =\ O\left(N^{-3\epsilon/10}\right).

(A.15)

Thus, substituting the estimate (
A.15
) back to (
A
) gives us

\displaystyle\left|\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}-\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell+1}}\right|\ \leq\ O\left(\binom{k(N)}{k_{1,\ell}}\cdot N^{-3\epsilon/10}\right).

(A.16)

∎

Appendix C

when

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle z_{m-2}\ \leq\ \frac{z_{m-1}+z_{m}}{2}\ \leq\ \frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}.

\displaystyle\int_{z_{m-2}}^{\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}}(\Phi(z_{m+1-d}))^{m-d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)(-1)\left(y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{m+i-d}\right)

Hence, (

) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{Y^{(N)}}(y)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\left(\varphi\left(\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}\right)+\varphi\left(y-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}\right)-z_{m-2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ll\ \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\varphi\left(\frac{y-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}}{2}\right)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{d-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2}\varphi(z_{m+i-d})\right)\varphi\left(y-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}z_{m+i-d}\right)-z_{m-2}\right)dz_{m-2}\cdots dz_{m+1-d},

(C.9)

which is exactly the estimate we need.

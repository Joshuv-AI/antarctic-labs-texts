Experimental Study on the Effect of H2O Concentration on the Degradation of Sulfur Hexafluoride by Two-Stage Tandem DBD
Authors: Ying Zhang, Mingwei Wang, Huiyu Tan, Yalong Li, Lei Yu, Zhaodi Yang, Kun Wan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSF6 is a greenhouse gas widely used in the power industry that cannot be directly emitted. This study employs a self-designed two-stage dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) plasma series method that was used to degrade SF6 gas and analyzes the effect of H2O concentration. The results indicate that the SF6 degradation rate (DR) initially increased and then decreased with the rise in the water vapor content. When the added H2O concentration increased from 0.5 to 1.5%, the degradation rate of SF6 increased from 89.49 to 100%, achieving complete degradation of SF6. However, when the H2O concentration further increases to 2.5%, the degradation rate of SF6 decreased to 92.83%. The energy efficiency of SF6 degradation in the primary reaction system first increased and then decreased with the increase of H2O concentration, while that of SF6 in the secondary reaction system first decreased and then increased. It was found that the products of SF6 were SO2F2, SO2, SOF2, and SOF4. The addition of H2O increased the selectivity of SO2 in the decomposition products to 75.17%, and inhibit the formation of SO2F2 and SOF2. This study provides experimental support for the degradation of SF6 waste gas and provides a research direction for its industrial application.

Introduction

Sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) is an artificial gas, where a central sulfur atom is covalently bonded to six surrounding fluorine atoms to form an octahedral structure.1 SF6 gas is colorless, odorless, and nontoxic.2 It is nonflammable, slightly soluble in water, and chemically very stable.3 Due to its strong electron affinity,4 SF6 can self-recover after bond breaking, making it have excellent insulation and arc-extinguishing performances.5 Therefore, SF6 is widely used in the power industry.6 However, SF6 is a strong greenhouse gas, its global warming potential (GWP) value is 23,500 times that of CO2, and its atmospheric life is as long as 3200 years.7 The harmless degradation of SF6 waste gas is of great significance to environmental protection.8

In recent years, the degradation of SF6 waste gas has become a popular research direction.9 Currently, the mainstream SF6 degradation methods include thermal (catalytic) degradation, photocatalytic degradation, and nonthermal plasma degradation.10−11,12,13 All of these methods can create an effective active reaction zone, leading to the decomposition of SF6 gas molecules. Among them, nonthermal plasma technology represented by dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) plasma utilizes a large number of high-energy electrons and active particles in the plasma to collide with SF6 and decompose it in a short time.14 This method has the advantages of energy saving, high efficiency, and simple operation, while also providing excellent selectivity of products, thus receiving widespread attention.15

In the DBD degradation process of SF6, the addition of different active gases can effectively improve the degradation rate (DR) and energy yield (EY) of SF6, and also affect the distribution of decomposition products.16 In 2004, Lee et al. used an AC plasma generated by a coaxial cylindrical DBD reactor to degrade the SF6/N2/O2 gas mixture. The results showed that the degradation rate of SF6 reached 91% when the concentration was 300 ppm.17 In 2014, Zhuang et al. used DBD to degrade SF6 and found that the addition of air and CO2 could significantly increase the degradation rate; the degradation rate of SF6 with the concentration of 3600 ppm was as high as 94%.18 In recent years, Zhang et al. explored the effects of adding NH3, H2O, and O2 on the degradation of SF6 by DBD. It was found that the synergistic effect was best when the concentration of NH3 and SF6 was 1:1, and that the involvement of NH3 also led to the precipitation of solid S, NH4F, and NH4HF2.1919 When adding H2O and O2 alone or both, the degradation rate of SF6 increased significantly. In terms of degradation products, the participation of H2O makes SF6 tend to produce more SO2 in the degradation process, while the participation of O2 makes SF6 tend to produce more SO2F2, which also provides a research direction for controlling the degradation products.20−23 It can be seen that the addition of H2O has a better promoting effect on the degradation of SF6 by DBD, which is beneficial to the decomposition of SF6 to produce more SO2.

Currently, the research on the degradation of high concentration SF6 by DBD is faced with the challenges of low degradation rates and high content of intermediate decomposition products such as SO2F2. In this paper, a two-stage tandem DBD plasma degradation experimental platform was built to study the effect of H2O concentration on the degradation of SF6 by the DBD two-stage tandem. The degradation rate, EY, product selectivity of SF6, and the reaction mechanism of SO2F2 in the secondary reactor were analyzed, which provided insights for efficiently degrading SF6 waste gas. Experimental Platform

The experimental platform of SF6 degradation by two-stage tandem DBD is shown in Figure 2. Since pure SF6 gas is difficult to discharge, the SF6 gas is first diluted using a gas distribution system to obtain the desired concentration of the SF6 gas mixture. The mixtures passes through a humidity generator to mix with water of a specific concentration, and then enters the two-stage tandem plasma reactor. The primary reaction system is the first reaction tube of the two-stage tandem reaction system, and the secondary reaction system is the second reaction tube of the two-stage tandem reaction system. The DBD reactor, driven by the power system, generates plasma to initiate degradation reactions. The degraded gas products are analyzed by the detection system, and the exhaust gas is treated by absorption in NaOH solution after detection.

Experimental platform.

The experiment in this paper utilized a CTP-2000K AC power supply produced by Nanjing Suman Technology Co., Ltd. With a voltage regulator, it can achieve AC voltage output from 0 to 30 kV, and the frequency can be adjusted from 1 to 100 kHz, with an output power range of 0–500 W. The frequency used in the experiment was approximately 8.5–9.0 kHz, and the input power of the power supply was controlled within 120 W. The gas distribution system of the experimental platform mainly consists of a dynamic gas distributor and a precision water gas generator. The dynamic gas distributor used is the GC500 model produced by Jiangsu Tanggao Electric Co., Ltd., with an accuracy of up to 0.2% F.S. and a maximum dilution ratio of 300:1. The precision humidity generator was custom-made by Suzhou Furen De Experimental Equipment Co., Ltd. The flow rate range is 0–500 mL/min, and the accuracy is 1% F.S. The gases used in the experiment were provided by Newradar Gas Co., Ltd. In this experiment, the concentration of SF6 was 2%, the background gas was Ar, the gas pressure was atmospheric pressure, and the gas flow rate was 150 mL/min. The input power of the power supply was 90 W except for special instructions.

The reactor used in the experiment is a double-layer quartz dielectric coaxial cylindrical structure. The inner electrode is a copper rod, and the outer electrode is a stainless steel mesh wound around the outer surface of the dielectric tube. The length of the discharge area is 20 cm, with a discharge volume of approximately 52 cm3, and the reaction time is about 21 s. The detection system mainly includes gas chromatography (GC), Fourier transform infrared spectrometry (FTIR), gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GCMS), and optical emission spectroscopy (OES). The OES used in this experiment is a three-channel spectrometer model MX2500+ produced by Ocean Optics. It operates in the wavelength range of 300–880 nm, the integration time is 60 ms, the average integration times are 10 times, and the minimum resolution is 0.1 nm.

Results and Analysis

Effect of Addition of H2O on Degradation Rate and EY

In the absence of H2O, the variation trend of the SF6 degradation rate and EY change with input power is shown in Figure 3. It can be found that with the increase of input power, the degradation rate of SF6 increases, while the EY increases first and then decreases. On the whole, the degradation rates of SF6 and EY are low.

Degradation rate and EY of SF6 without H2O.

The distribution of the SF6 degradation rate under different H2O concentrations is shown in Figure 4. With the increase of the concentration of H2O, the degradation rate of SF6 in all three systems shows a trend of initially increasing and then decreasing. In the primary reaction system, the addition of H2O significantly enhances the SF6 degradation rate, reaching a maximum of 74.11% when the added H2O concentration is 1%. However, as the added H2O concentration further increases, the degradation rate decreases. The main reason for this is that H2O will be ionized in the reaction system to produce OH and H active particles. These active particles can combine with the low-fluorine sulfides (SFx) dissociated from SF6, preventing them from recombining with free F atoms to form SF6. This impedes the self-recovery reaction of SF6 and promotes its degradation. However, H2O is also an electronegative gas, and as its concentration increases, more high-energy electrons are adsorbed by H2O, which limits the degradation reaction of SF6 and leads to a decrease in its degradation rate.20

Degradation rate of SF6 under different H2O concentrations.

In addition, it can be seen from Figure 4 that the degradation rate of SF6 in the secondary reaction system and the two-stage tandem reaction system has a similar trend, and is significantly higher than that in the primary reaction system, indicating that the secondary reaction system contributes more to the SF6 degradation rate in the entire degradation system. When the H2O concentration increased from 0.5 to 1.5%, the SF6 degradation rate in the secondary reaction system increased from 73.25 to 90.8%, and in the two-stage tandem reaction system, it increased from 89.49 to 100%. However, as the H2O concentration further increased to 2.5%, the degradation rate in the secondary reaction system gradually decreased to 83.83%, and in the two-stage tandem reaction system, it decreased to 92.83%. Compared with the primary reaction system, the optimal H2O concentration for achieving the highest SF6 degradation rate in the secondary reaction system and the two-stage tandem reaction system increased from 1 to 1.5%.

The EY distribution of SF6 degradation under different H2O concentrations is shown in Figure 5. The EY distribution curve of SF6 degradation in the primary reaction system is higher than that in the secondary reaction system. The main reason is that the main degradation object of the primary reaction system is SF6, while the degradation object of the secondary reaction system is SF6 and the decomposition products from the primary reaction system. In the secondary reaction system, the reionization of these decomposition products consumes some discharge energy, thus reducing the EY of the SF6 degradation process. As the H2O concentration increases from 0.5 to 1%, the EY of SF6 in the primary reaction system increased from 6.68 g/kWh to a peak of 8.15 g/kWh, while in the secondary reaction system, it decreased from 3.17 g/kWh to a minimum volume of 2.33 g/kWh. When the H2O concentration further increases from 1 to 2.5%, the EY of SF6 in the primary reaction system gradually decreases to 6.12 g/kWh, whereas in the secondary reaction system, it gradually increases to 4.09 g/kWh. The EY distribution in the secondary reaction system shows a trend of initially decreasing and then increasing, which is opposite to the degradation rate and EY distribution pattern observed in the primary reaction system. It is inferred that when the degradation rate of SF6 is higher in the primary reaction system, the secondary reaction system primarily involves dissociation reactions of the decomposition products from the primary reaction system. In the secondary reaction system, the concentration of SF6 is lower, and fewer SF6 molecules are involved in the reaction. The EY of SF6 in the secondary reaction system shows an inverse correlation with the inlet concentration. Therefore, the higher the degradation rate in the primary reaction system, the lower the degradation EY in the secondary reaction system.

EY of SF6 under different H2O concentrations.

Effect of H2O on Tandem DBD Degradation of SF6 Products

Detection and Analysis of Active Particles in the Discharge Process

To further clarify the promotion mechanism of H2O in the SF6 decomposition process, emission spectroscopy was employed in this study to detect active particles in the reaction area. Figure 6 shows the emission spectra of the first and second-stage reaction systems with an added H2O concentration of 1.5%. Figure (a) shows the emission spectrum of the first reaction system, Figure (b) shows the emission spectrum of the second-stage reaction system, and Figure (c) shows the emission spectrum of the single-tube system without the addition of H2O. The spectral detection wavelength ranges are 300–400 and 600–800 nm. Since the background gas used in this experiment was Ar, under the action of high-energy particles, Ar transitions into excited states, resulting in numerous Ar I spectral lines in the discharge system, such as Ar 696.5, 708.68, 750.29, 763.5, and 794.88 nm (reaction 4). Through comparison, it is found that the Ar spectral lines in the secondary reaction system are significantly higher than those in the primary reaction system, indicating a stronger discharge effect in the secondary reaction system.20 Comparing the emission spectra before and after the addition of H2O, it was found that the discharge effect after the addition of H2O was significantly enhanced. In addition, SF6 molecules undergo gradual dissociation under the action of high-energy particles, as depicted in reactions 5 and 6(23) forming F atomic lines, such as F 738.95 nm.

Emission spectra of the two-stage tandem reaction system with added H2O and the reaction system without added H2O. (a) Emission spectra of the primary reaction system. (b) Emission spectra of the secondary reaction system. (c) Emission spectra of the SF6 degradation system without H2O.

By comparing the emission spectra of the first- and second-stage reaction systems, it was found that in the 600–800 nm wavelength range of the secondary reaction system, H 656.3 nm emission was observed, and in the 300–400 nm wavelength range, OH 308.9 nm emission was observed. However, no existence of H and OH radicals was observed in the primary reaction system. The main reason is that the higher concentration of SF6 in the primary reaction system hinders the discharge and weakens the decomposition reaction of H2O in the reaction area, hence the absence of H and OH spectral lines. However, after the degradation of SF6 in the primary reaction system, when the reaction gas enters the secondary reaction system, the SF6 content significantly decreased, making discharge easier. This also results in more thorough ionization of H2O compared to the primary reaction system, leading to a strong activity of OH radicals produced from decomposition. These OH radicals can combine with the low-fluorine sulfides produced from SF6 decomposition to form stable compounds such as HF, SOF4, SO2F2, and SOF2, inhibiting the recombination reaction of low-fluorine sulfides and thereby enhancing the degradation rate of SF6.

As shown in reactions 7–11, it can be observed that OH radicals generated by the ionization of H2O significantly promote the degradation process.21 However, with an excessive increase in H2O concentration, the decomposition of H2O intensifies, leading to an increased consumption of high-energy electrons. This limits the decomposition reactions of SF6 (reactions 5 and 6), thereby reducing the content of low-fluorine sulfur compounds such as SF3, SF4, and SF5. Consequently, reactions 7–11 are hindered, leading to a decrease in degradation efficiency.20

Detection and Analysis of SF6 Degradation Products

The infrared spectrum of SF6 degradation products from the secondary reaction system with H2O concentration of 1.5% were shown in Figure 7. Figure (a) shows the FTIR of the first reaction system, and Figure (b) shows the FTIR of the second-stage reaction system. It was found that the main degradation products of SF6 are SO2F2, SO2, SOF2, and SOF4. This is consistent with the types of decomposition products observed in single-stage SF6 degradation as reported in literature,24−26 indicating that the degradation of SF6 by two-stage tandem DBD does not change the types of SF6 degradation products.

(a) FTIR spectra of degradation products of the primary reaction system at H2O concentration of 1.5%. (b) FTIR spectra of degradation products of the secondary reaction system at H2O concentration of 1.5%.

In order to further determine the variation of SF6 degradation products, quantitative detection of SO2F2, SO2, SOF2, and SOF4 was conducted by using GCMS. Figure 8 shows the selective distribution of SF6 degradation products from the primary reaction system under different H2O concentrations. When the H2O concentration increased from 0.5 to 2.5%, the selectivity of SO2 gradually increased, while the selectivity of SO2F2, SOF2, and SOF4 decreased. The selectivity of SO2 increased from 7.11 to 42.29%, while that of SO2F2 decreased from 73.93 to 48.8%. SO2 and SO2F2 were the two main decomposition products in the primary reaction system. When the concentration of H2O is low, fewer H and OH radicals are produced. The low-fluorine sulfides produced from SF6 dissociation primarily combine with OH radicals to form the decomposition product SO2F2. With the increase of H2O concentration, the deep decomposition of SF6 is promoted, and the decomposition process of SF6 tends to produce more SO2.

Selectivity distribution of SF6 degradation products in the primary reaction system with different concentrations of H2O.

Figure 9 shows the selective distribution of SF6 degradation products from the secondary reaction system under different H2O concentrations. With the increase of H2O concentration, the product selectivity of SO2 increased from 33.72 to 75.17%, while the selectivity of SOF2 remains unchanged with H2O concentration. Meanwhile, the selectivity of SO2F2 decreased from 38.57 to 0.11%, and SOF4 decreases slowly from 7.2 to 1.49%, and there was almost no SO2F2 in the products. This indicates that the types of degradation products in the secondary reaction system are similar to those in the primary reaction system and the trend of product selectivity change is also similar to that in the primary reaction system. It is worth noting that in the secondary reaction system, the selectivity of SO2 is significantly higher than that of other degradation products. Moreover, as the H2O concentration increases, the selectivity of SO2 gradually increases. This is mainly because the addition of H2O can significantly promote reactions 12 and 13, leading to the decomposition of SO2F2 into SOF2 and SO2, thereby enhancing the selectivity of SO2 in the products. The selectivity of SOF2 in the degradation products remains essentially unchanged as the H2O concentration increases, indicating that SO2F2 is a relatively stable decomposition product, and SOF2 is more likely to decompose to form other more stable products. From the perspective of environmentally friendly treatment of SF6 decomposition products, since SO2F2 has relatively stable physicochemical properties and is difficult to remove, it is preferable for the selectivity of SO2F2 in the products to be as low as possible, and the increase of H2O concentration is beneficial to the harmless treatment of SF6 degradation products.

Selective distribution of SF6 degradation products in the secondary reaction system at different concentration of H2O.

Conclusions

In this article, the effects of H2O concentration on the degradation rate, EY, and degradation products of SF6 were studied by the two-stage tandem DBD degradation experimental platform. The following conclusions were drawn:

(1)The degradation rate of SF6 can be significantly improved by the two-stage tandem DBD, and the degradation products can be significantly regulated. In the primary reaction system, SF6 is the main degradation object, while in the secondary reaction system, the main decomposition reaction involves SO2F2.

In the primary reaction system, SF6 is the main degradation object, while in the secondary reaction system, the main decomposition reaction involves SO2F2.

(2)With increasing H2O concentration, the degradation rate of SF6 by the two-stage tandem DBD degradation system showed a trend of initially increasing and then decreasing. When the H2O concentration is 1.5%, the degradation rate of SF6 reaches its maximum value at 100%, achieving complete degradation of SF6.

With increasing H2O concentration, the degradation rate of SF6 by the two-stage tandem DBD degradation system showed a trend of initially increasing and then decreasing. When the H2O concentration is 1.5%, the degradation rate of SF6 reaches its maximum value at 100%, achieving complete degradation of SF6.

(3)The main degradation products of SF6 were SO2F2, SO2, SOF2, and SOF4. H2O can significantly regulate the distribution of SF6 degradation products, promoting the decomposition of SO2F2 in the secondary reaction system, leading to SO2 being the predominant degradation product.

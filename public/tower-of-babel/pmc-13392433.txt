Physics-based design and simulation of hollow-core anti-resonant fiber plasmonic sensors
Authors: Mohammad Al Mahfuz, Abdullah Al Mamun, Md Selim Habib
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNext-generation sensing technologies require high-performance sensors that offer ultra-low loss with enhanced sensitivity. To achieve this, we propose a hollow-core anti-resonant fiber (HC-ARF) refractive index (RI) sensor utilizing the surface plasmon resonance (SPR) detection mechanism for superior optical sensing performance. The proposed sensor model features a single ring of uniformly arranged regular cladding tubes, each coated with a thin plasmonic metal layer to facilitate strong coupling between the core and surface plasmon modes. Through rigorous finite-element modeling (FEM) simulations, the fiber structure is carefully optimized and sensing performance is investigated to obtain ultra-low loss and high detection sensitivity via wavelength interrogation. The simulation results show that the proposed HC-ARF sensor achieves losses up to two orders of magnitude lower than conventional solid-core fiber sensors. Notably, the sensor achieves enhanced wavelength sensitivity (WS) of 6500 and 11,500 nm/RIU for analyte RIs of 1.33 and 1.43, respectively, effectively spanning the visible to near-infrared spectrum with a broad sensing range from 1.25 to 1.44. Moreover, it shows stable performance under tight bending conditions with 10 cm bend radius and ±4% variations in geometric parameters. The combination of relatively low-loss, wide detection range, and high sensing performance makes this sensor effective for real-time RI monitoring applications such as glucose and sucrose concentration analysis, cancer cell detection, and SARS-CoV-2 identification.Subject terms: Engineering, Nanoscience and technology, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of technology, researchers are continuously striving to develop novel bio-sensing devices that offer early detection, real-time monitoring, high accuracy, and enhanced sensing response. Among the various sensing platforms, optical fiber sensors have gained significant attention from the optics community due to their compactness, cost-effectiveness, and proven efficiency in various applications such as bio-sensing1, temperature sensing2, environmental monitoring3, and cancer biomarker detection4. The SPR sensing technique has become instrumental in modern optical sensing due to its outstanding properties, such as label-free measurement and efficient light control capabilities. Incorporating SPR into fiber-optic platforms represents a significant advancement towards high-performance sensing technologies5–9. Recently, total internal reflection (TIR)-guided microstructure optical fiber (MOF)-based SPR sensors have shown promising performance in RI detection due to their flexible design and improved sensitivity10–13. However, their practical implementation is often limited by the lossy silica core and the complexity of the fabrication process. In contrast, anti-resonant guided hollow-core fibers (HCFs) allow most of the light to propagate through an air-filled core, potentially minimizing overlap with the glass structure in cladding and thereby achieving extremely low loss14–17. These unique properties have made HC-ARF highly attractive for diverse sensing applications such as gas sensing18,19, temperature sensing20, RI sensing21, label-free protein monitoring22, biolayer sensing23,24, and so on. Despite these advantages, SPR-based sensing using HC-ARF remains relatively unexplored25,26. Integrating SPR phenomena with analyte-filled HC-ARFs provides a substantial benefit, as spectral sensitivity can be determined through the resonance wavelength shift caused by variations in the RI of the sensing medium. Efficient SPR excitation is generally realized by depositing a plasmonic layer, and gold (Au) has been used as a popular choice due to its longer stability in the aqueous environment and showing broader resonance peak shifting phenomena27,28. Recently, alternative plasmonic materials have also been investigated to enhance sensor performance29. Most of the reported SPR sensors based on MOF demonstrate reasonable sensitivity for high RI detection around > 1.410,13, however, their performance remains inadequate in the lower RI region around 1.33. This low RI regime is crucial for many real-time applications such as water pollution or quality monitoring30,31, glucose level monitoring32, and sucrose concentration determination33. Several recent studies have highlighted ongoing challenges and performance limitations in optical fiber-based plasmonic sensors, particularly for sensing analytes near 1.33. For instance, Guo et al.33 fabricated a microstructured optical fiber sensor capable of determining sucrose concentrations, but its spectral sensitivity reached only 3961 nm/RIU at RI of 1.33, which is relatively low. Islam et al.34 studied a photonic biosensor designed for SARS-CoV-2 detection at a similar RI range that offered limited performances such as maximum WS of 2745 nm/RIU and a figure of merit (FOM) of 196 RIU, while the loss is relatively high. Researchers also proposed a plasmonic biosensor35, where the sensitivity was limited to 1000 nm/RIU. Inhibited-coupling hollow-core fiber sensor24 and solid-core fiber sensor27 are reported with additional biolayer coatings at the cladding tube surface, which extends the RI detection range from 1.1 to 1.6, however, the resonance wavelength shifts were comparatively narrow. The above reports underscore the need for further improvement in sensing technology to potentially address several research gaps in the literature by enhancing sensing performance and reducing loss, especially in the RI regime near 1.33.

In this paper, a 6-tube HC-ARF plasmonic sensor is proposed to detect various analytes with improved performance, particularly near the RI of 1.33. A plasmonic material layer is integrated on the surface of the cladding tube to introduce SPR. The core region is considered as the sensing medium, which is filled with liquids or analytes having RIs of various samples or RI equivalents to different concentrations. The loss characteristics are thoroughly investigated by FEM analysis to observe the sensing performance of the proposed sensor. First, to obtain the expected sensing response, the geometric parameters are thoroughly tuned for accurate optimization. Next, using the optimized parameters, the dispersion characteristics of the sensor are rigorously studied, showcasing the interaction of core modes and cladding modes (CMs) in the density of photonic states (DOPS). Then, spectral sensitivity is determined by observing the resonance wavelength shift induced by variations in the RI of the sensing medium. The other performance metrics, such as FOM, maximum theoretical resolution (MTR), signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and limit of detection (LOD), are also evaluated to ensure the superior sensing performances. Rigorous finite-element analysis confirms that the proposed sensor is capable of detecting analytes across a broad spectral range. This highlights its potential for various sensing applications such as sucrose, glucose, cancer cells, and coronavirus detection.

Simulation and theory

In this study, the simulation is performed using the FEM in Comsol Multiphysics software v6.2. To ensure accurate prediction of the RI and loss of the sensor, a perfectly matched layer (PML) boundary condition is applied to the outermost region of the fiber structure with a mesh element size of 0.7 that effectively absorbs the outgoing waves and minimizes reflections44. The computational domain comprises 143,888 mesh elements and 3,904 boundary elements. The electromagnetic waves, frequency domain (emw) module is utilized to analyze the modal properties of the proposed design. As the optical field propagates through the core, a portion of the guided light interacts with the surrounding cladding and jacket tubes, leading to partial leakage of energy in the form of evanescent waves. This leakage is predicted as the confinement loss of the guided mode33. Results and discussion

To maximize the sensing performance, the structural parameters of the proposed sensor are systematically tuned to select the best performing parameters. Figure 2a–d demonstrate the optimization of the core dimension, cladding tube dimension, metal thickness, and cladding tube number, respectively. Figure 2a shows the loss spectra with changing core diameter () from 35 to 45 for analyte RIs of 1.33 and 1.34. From the loss spectra, it is evident that the loss peaks exhibit a decreasing trend, accompanied by a slight blue shift, for both analytes. This trend is attributed to stronger light confinement within a larger core, resulting in reduced loss. Here, the loss values are obtained about 147, 95, and 63 dB/m for RI = 1.33, whereas reduced losses are achieved about 129, 84, and 54 dB/m for RI = 1.34, with = 35, 40, and 45 , respectively. It is noteworthy that no deviation in WS is observed for different core sizes. Although the lowest loss is experienced for = 45 , the micro-bending loss15 is also increasing for much bigger core sizes. Therefore, 40 is chosen as the optimized core diameter.

Figure 2b depicts the optimization of cladding tube diameter () from 15 to 25 . With the increase in tube diameter, the loss peaks are reached around 90 and 85 dB/m for RI of 1.33 and 1.34. A sharp resonance peak is observed for = 20 , while for = 15 and 25 , the loss curves are broadening, which may limit the sensing range. Therefore, the optimized tube diameter is fixed as = 20 to obtain a broader sensing range. Figure 2c presents the optimization of the plasmonic metal layer thickness, where the Au coating is varied from 25 to 35 nm to evaluate its impact on sensing performance. As the Au thickness increased, the loss gradually decreased from 105 to 82 dB/m for analyte RI of 1.33, whereas it decreased from 102 to 70 dB/m for analyte RI of 1.34. This reduction occurs due to stronger damping of the evanescent field with thicker metallic layers, which suppresses optical leakage27,47. However, excessive thickness leads to weaker coupling between the core mode and surface plasmon mode, resulting in broader resonance peaks. Therefore, plasmonic metal thickness is selected as nm, which offers a balanced trade-off between coupling strength and loss. Notably, in the absence of the plasmonic metal layer ( nm), no resonance peak appears in this spectrum. This emphasizes the essential role of the plasmonic coating in achieving effective SPR excitation within the HC-ARF structure. Figure 2d depicts the influence of the number of cladding tubes (N) on the loss characteristics. For N = 5, the overall loss decreases because of lower leakage through the limited number of tubes. However, the resonance spectrum in the loss curve becomes broader, indicating weaker interaction between the core and plasmon mode and reduced spectral sensitivity. Conversely, for N = 7, the addition of more tubes increases the loss because more light passes through the cladding tube regions. Taking into account both the spectral sharpness and loss, the optimal result is found for N = 6, which provides a proper balance between performance and sensor length.

Utilizing the optimum design parameters of the fiber, the sensing performance is thoroughly investigated by filling the analytes with RIs from 1.25 to 1.44 in the sensing medium. Figure 3a–b present the loss spectra of the proposed sensor for the desired sensing range within the visible to near-infrared regime. As the RI of the analyte in the core increases, the resonance wavelength experiences a noticeable blue shift, significantly maintaining the resonance and anti-resonance guiding condition of the HC-ARF. Consequently, the loss peaks gradually move toward shorter wavelengths with higher analyte RIs. This shift is accompanied by a reduction in coupling strength between the core mode and the SPP mode, leads to a gradual decrease in loss intensity across the entire RIs range of 1.25–1.44. Conventional solid-core fiber-based SPR sensors typically demonstrate weak performance near the RI of 1.33, potentially limiting their real-world applicability. The proposed HC-ARF sensor addresses this limitation by exhibiting a significantly enhanced WS of 6500 nm/RIU at RI = 1.33. Moreover, the sensor achieves a maximum WS of 11,500 nm/RIU at RI = 1.44 and showcases a satisfactory WS of 5000 nm/RIU even at the low RI of 1.25. Although the loss increases to approximately 334 dB/m at RI = 1.25, it drops sharply 7 dB/m for RI = 1.44, that is nearly two orders of magnitude lower than the reported solid-core TIR guided fiber sensors11,13. These results confirm the superior performance and detection accuracy of the proposed HC-ARF-based plasmonic sensor compared to existing reports.

Possible fabrication method

For real-time sensing applications, it is essential to practically fabricate the fiber, thus, we are discussing a possible fabrication technique in this section. The HCF can be fabricated using advanced methods such as the stack-and-draw technique, where HCF involves drawing a structured glass preform in a fiber drawing tower53. Differential gas pressure is applied to selectively control and maintain the shape of the hollow core and cladding during the draw process. Real-time optimization and modeling are essential to achieve the targeted geometry and high optical performance, as the transverse structure drastically changes between the preform and finished fiber25,52–54. The plasmonic metal layer on the surface of the cladding tubes can be applied using chemical vapor deposition (CVD) or atomic layer deposition (ALD)55,56. In Ref57, the authors explain in detail the coating of metal in hollow core fiber structure.

Conclusion

In summary, the proposed HC-ARF plasmonic sensor demonstrated great potential for RI sensing applications. Comprehensive finite-element simulations were performed to optimize and analyze the sensing response of the proposed sensor. The simulation results reveal that the proposed design effectively covers a broad sensing range from 1.25 to 1.44 in the visible to near-infrared spectrum, achieving the lowest loss of 7 dB/m for RI of 1.44, with maximum WS of 6500 and 11500 nm/RIU for analyte RI of 1.33 and 1.43, respectively. Other key performance metrics, including FOM about 1150 RIU and a minimum detectable resolution of , confirms the remarkable detection accuracy of the proposed sensor. The sensor also demonstrates excellent bending tolerance with maintaining stable loss characteristics even under 10 cm bending radius. It is worth mentioning that this work can be expanded to several other real time biomedical and biochemical sensing applications, achieving maximum WS of 8000, 9000, and 10000 nm/RIU to detect sucrose concentration, cancer cells, and glucose concentration, respectively. Another key point is that the proposed structure demonstrates compatibility with alternative plasmonic materials, and fabrication tolerance analyses indicate that the sensing performance remains stable even ±4% geometric parameter variation. Overall, the factors discussed above establish the proposed HC-ARF plasmonic sensor as a promising candidate for real-time biosample detection in lab-on-a-chip platforms.

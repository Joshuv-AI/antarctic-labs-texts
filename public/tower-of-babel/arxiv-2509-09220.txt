On the annihilator condition for skew PBW extensions
Karol Herrera, Sebastián Higuera, Andrés Rubiano

Abstract
. We investigate the annihilator condition ( a . c . ) (a.c.) for skew Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt extensions. We prove that some results about the annihilator condition ( a . c . ) (a.c.) for skew polynomial rings also hold for skew PBW extensions. We also demonstrate that the behavior of annihilators is determined by the defining relations of the extension. Our results extend those corresponding presented for skew polynomial rings

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, every ring is associative (not necessarily commutative) with identity unless otherwise stated. The center and the set of all idempotent elements of
R
is denoted by
C(R)
and
I(R)
, respectively. In addition,

,
\mathrm{nil}^{*}(R)
and
\mathrm{nil}(R)
denote the prime radical of
R
, the upper nilradical of
R
(that is, the sum of all nil ideals of
R
) and the set of nilpotent elements of
R
, respectively. If
P(R)=\mathrm{nil}(R)
then
R
is called
2-primal
, and if
\mathrm{nil}^{*}(R)=\mathrm{nil}(R)
then
R
is an
NI
ring. An idempotent element

is called

(resp.,

) if
re=ere
(resp.,
er=ere
) for all
r\in R
. The sets of left and right semi-central idempotents of

will be written as
S_{l}(R)
and
S_{r}(R)
. If
S\subseteq R
, we define the
right annihilator
and the

as
r_{R}(S)=\{a\in R\mid Sa=0\}
and
\ell_{R}(S)=\{a\in R\mid aS=0\}
, respectively.

Jerison
[
26
]
introduced the concept of the
annihilator condition

(a.c.)
for commutative reduced rings (
R
is called
reduced
if it has no non-zero nilpotent elements). In the commutative context,
R
satisfies the annihilator condition
(a.c.)
(or has Property
(a.c.)
) if the annihilator of each finitely generated ideal
I
of
R
coincides with the annihilator of a single element of
R
. Later, Lucas
[
45
]
generalized this definition to all commutative rings. The class of rings satisfying Property
(a.c.)
is quite extensive. Some examples include subdirect sums of totally ordered integral domains, Bézout rings, direct sums of rings with Property
(a.c.)
, reduced Noetherian rings, as well as certain nontrivial reduced graded rings (see
[
26
,
45
]
).

Hong et al.
[
33
]
extended this notion to the noncommutative case. A ring
R
is said to satisfy
Property
(a.c.)
on the right
(respectively,
on the left
) if for every finitely generated right ideal
I
of
R
, there exists some
c\in R
such that
r_{R}(I)=r_{R}(cR)
(respectively,
\ell_{R}(I)=\ell_{R}(Rc)
). They investigated many classes of rings that enjoy Property
(a.c.)
, proving that this includes subdirect products of fully ordered semiprime rings, semiprime rings with many minimal prime ideals, semiprime rings with the ascending chain condition on annihilators, and others. They showed that matrix rings over rings with Property
(a.c.)
also satisfy the same property
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
, and that the skew polynomial rings
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
introduced by Ore
[
50
]
, with
\sigma
an automorphism of
R
and
\delta
a
\sigma
-derivation of
R
has Property
(a.c.)

[
33
, Theorem 1.10]
.

Following Rege and Chhawchharia
[
54
]
, a ring
R
is

if for any two polynomials in
R[x]
,
whenever their product is zero, then the product of each pair of their coefficients is also zero. Hirano
[
30
]
introduced the

,
which generalize Armendariz rings. This class of rings satisfies the following condition:
for any two polynomials
p(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}p_{i}x^{i}
,

,

p(x)R[x]q(x)=0
, then
p_{i}Rq_{j}=0
for all

. It is well-known that reduced rings are Armendariz, and Armendariz rings are quasi-Armendariz rings, but the converse are not true in general
[
54
, Proposition 2.1]
. Hong et al.
[
34
]
extended this definition to the case of skew polynomial rings as follows: a ring

is called
\sigma
-skew quasi-Armendariz
if for

p_{i}R\sigma^{i}(q_{j})=0
for all
i,j
. The Armendariz rings over skew polynomial rings has been widely studied by several authors (see
[
1
,
4
,
35
]
).

If

is an endomorphism of

and we have that

of

\sigma
-

-compatible

Malki and Louzari

investigated the relationship between Armendariz properties and the annihilator condition

Thinking about the above results on the annihilator condition and Armendariz properties for skew polynomial rings, a natural task is how these ideas can be extended to more general noncommutative contexts. Our purpose in this paper is to study the connection between the annihilator condition and Armendariz properties on

[

Definition 2.1

(

4.
Examples

The importance of our results is appreciated when we extend their application to algebraic structures that are more general than those considered by Malki and Louzari, that is, some noncommutative rings which cannot be expressed as skew polynomial rings
R[x;\sigma]
. In this section, we consider several families of rings that have been studied in the literature which are subfamilies of skew PBW extensions. Of course, the list of examples is not exhaustive.

Example 4.1
.

Algebras whose generators satisfy quadratic relations such as Clifford algebras, Weyl-Heisenberg algebras, and Sklyanin algebras, play an important role in analysis and mathematical physics. Motivated by these facts, Golovashkin and Maximov
[
20
]
considered the algebras
Q(a,b,c)
, with two generators
x
and
y
, defined by the quadratic relations

(4.1)

yx=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2},

where the coefficients
a
,
b
, and
c
belong to an arbitrary field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. They presented conditions on these elements under which such an algebra has a PBW basis of the form
\mathcal{B}=\{x^{m}y^{n}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Golovashkin and Maximov given a necessary and sufficient condition for a PBW basis as above to exist when
ac+b\neq 0

[
20
, Section 3]
. If
ac+b=0
, they proved that if
b\neq 0,-1
then
Q(a,b,c)
has a PBW basis, and if
b=-1
then the set
\mathcal{B}
is linearly independent but do not form a PBW basis of
Q(a,b,c)

[
20
, Section 5]
.

We can prove that if
a
,
b
, and
c
are not all zero, then
Q(a,b,c)
is neither a skew polynomial ring of
\Bbbk
,
\Bbbk[x]
, nor of
\Bbbk[y]
. If
b\neq 0
and
c=0
, one can verify that
Q(a,b,c)
is an SPBW extension of
\Bbbk[x]
, that is
Q(a,b,c)\cong\sigma(\Bbbk[x])\langle y\rangle
. Theorem
3.3
establishes that the p.p.-property on the right
(resp., left) guarantees that
Q(a,b,c)

satisfies Property
(a.c.)
. When the extension is bijective, Theorem
3.4

shows that this property is preserved for
Q(a,b,c)
.
Furthermore, Theorem
3.5
proves that the same holds
whenever
\Bbbk[x]
is a
(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible right (resp., left)
p.q.-Baer ring.

Example 4.2
.

Jordan
[
43
]
introduced a class of iterated skew polynomial rings
R(B,\sigma,c,p)
, known as

, which include different examples of noncommutative algebras. These structures have been investigated by Jordan at various levels of generality in several papers
[
39
,
40
,
41
,
42

\beta\neq 0

Let
B

\Bbbk

\sigma

\Bbbk

B

\sigma

\sigma(x)=px

\sigma

\delta(B)=0

[
16

(4.2)

Equivalently,

can be presented as

\sigma(y)=p^{-1}y

\sigma

is Baer and
(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible satisfying condition (SA1),

the right (resp., left) p.p.-property of

\Sigma

still holds on the right (resp., left).
In addition, Theorem
3.5

(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible right (resp., left) p.q.-Baer ring,
then Property
(a.c.)
on the right (resp., left).

Example 4.3
.

Skew bi-quadratic algebras
were introduced by Bavula
[
7

as a framework for classifying bi-quadratic algebras on three generators having a PBW basis.

If

\displaystyle x_{i}r

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}+\delta_{i}(r),\quad\text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq n,\text{ and }r\in R,

The definition of SBQA makes it clear that

(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible

(a.c.)

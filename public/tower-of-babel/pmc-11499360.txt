Single-cell omics and machine learning integration to develop a polyamine metabolism-based risk score model in breast cancer patients
Authors: Xiliang Zhang, Hanjie Guo, Xiaolong Li, Wei Tao, Xiaoqing Ma, Yuxing Zhang, Weidong Xiao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer remains the leading malignant neoplasm among women globally, posing significant challenges in terms of treatment and prognostic evaluation. The metabolic pathway of polyamines is crucial in breast cancer progression, with a strong association to the increased capabilities of tumor cells for proliferation, invasion, and metastasis.MethodsWe used a multi-omics approach combining bulk RNA sequencing and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to study polyamine metabolism. Data from The Cancer Genome Atlas, Gene Expression Omnibus, and Genotype-Tissue Expression identified 286 differentially expressed genes linked to polyamine pathways in breast cancer. These genes were analyzed using univariate COX and machine learning algorithms to develop a prognostic scoring algorithm. Single-cell RNA sequencing validated the model by examining gene expression heterogeneity at the cellular level.ResultsOur single-cell analyses revealed distinct subpopulations with different expressions of genes related to polyamine metabolism, highlighting the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment. The SuperPC model (a constructed risk score) demonstrated high accuracy when predicting patient outcomes. The immune profiling and functional enrichment analyses revealed that the genes identified play a crucial role in cell cycle control and immune modulation. Single-cell validation confirmed that polyamine metabolism genes were present in specific cell clusters. This highlights their potential as therapeutic targets.ConclusionsThis study integrates single cell omics with machine-learning to develop a robust scoring model for breast cancer based on polyamine metabolic pathways. Our findings offer new insights into tumor heterogeneity, and a novel framework to personalize prognosis. Single-cell technologies are being used in this context to enhance our understanding of the complex molecular terrain of breast cancer and support more effective clinical management.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the leading malignant neoplasm globally among women, characterized by its occurrence in mammary tissues and exhibiting significant heterogeneity with diverse molecular subtypes (Barzaman et al. 2020). It represents a grave threat to women’s health and significantly impacts the quality of life for those affected (Kashyap et al. 2022). The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), part of the World Health Organization, reported in 2020 that breast cancer accounted for nearly 2.26 million new cases globally, which is about 11.7% of all new cancer diagnoses (Sung et al. 2021). While breast cancer rates differ markedly across various geographic regions and ethnic groups, the overall trend indicates an increase, with a particularly notable rise in developing nations (Michaels et al. 2023).

The diagnosis and treatment strategies for breast cancer are constantly advancing (Upadhyay and Bazan 2023; Phoebe et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2014). Early detection and treatment are key to improving patient survival rates. However, the prevention and treatment of breast cancer still face many challenges, especially in personalized treatment and prognosis assessment (Sarhangi et al. 2022). In recent years, with a deeper understanding of tumor biology, the metabolic changes in the tumor microenvironment have become a key research direction (Yuan et al. 2022; Arner and Rathmell 2023), in particular, the role of polyamine metabolism in tumor development (Holbert et al. 2022; Lian et al. 2022).

Polyamine metabolism encompasses a suite of cellular biochemical reactions involving the production, transformation, and role of polyamines like putrescine, spermidine, and spermine. These polyamines play a crucial role in cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis, and their dysregulation is often linked to the onset and progression of a variety of diseases, with a notable association with cancer (Novita Sari et al. 2021; Chia et al. 2022). Disruptions in polyamine metabolism are correlated with increased tumor cell proliferation, invasiveness, and metastatic potential (Cervelli et al. 2014; Akinyele and Wallace 2021). Furthermore, polyamine metabolic pathways are implicated in the resistance of breast cancer (Cervelli et al. 2014; Akinyele and Wallace 2021). Immune modulation within the tumor microenvironment, and the overall prognosis of the disease (Dias et al. 2023; Fernando et al. 2024).Evidence suggests that targeting polyamine metabolism can alter the TME to enhance immune system activity against tumors. Inhibitors of polyamine synthesis have been shown to reduce tumor growth and improve the efficacy of immunotherapies. This highlights the potential of polyamine-targeted therapies as a strategy to modify the TME and improve cancer treatment outcomes (Lian et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2023).

Single cell sequencing provides a powerful tool for in-depth research and personalized treatment of breast cancer, reveals different cell types and their functional status in the tumor, helps understand tumor heterogeneity and progress, analyzes the interaction between tumor and surrounding immune cells and stromal cells, and provides clues about immune escape and tumor growth (Kaur et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2024).

The objective of this research is to develop a risk scoring model for breast cancer patients utilizing machine learning techniques, with a focus on genes implicated in polyamine metabolism. Through the comprehensive analysis of extensive gene expression profiles and clinical data from breast cancer patients, we have pinpointed key genes associated with polyamine metabolism and investigated their predictive value for breast cancer outcomes. Subsequently, we employed machine learning methodologies to devise and validate a scoring model for the identified genes. The goal is to offer a scientific foundation for tailored therapeutic strategies and prognostic evaluations in breast cancer management.

Methods

Data source

Bulk analysis was performed using breast cancer expression matrices and clinical information. There were a total of 1212 cases of breast cancer from TCGA, 104 cases from GSE42568, and 179 cases of normal tissue data from GTEx. The scRNA-seq data of breast cancer patient tissues are from GSE118389.

Differentially expressed genes related to polyamine-associated breast cancer

Polyamine metabolism genes were obtained from the GeneCard (https://www.genecards.org/), MalaCards (https://www.malacards.org/), Harmonizome (https://maayanlab.cloud/Harmonizome/), and MSigDB database (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/), from the gene set “REACTOME_METABOLISM_OF_POLYAMINES,” totaling 2157 genes. By integrating bulk analysis of breast cancer data with normal tissue samples for differential analysis, 286 differentially expressed genes related to polyamine metabolism in breast cancer were identified (logFC filter = 1, fdr filter = 0.05). Furthermore, single-factor COX analysis and expression correlation analysis were conducted by combining clinical information.

Consensus clustering analysis

Consensus clustering was executed utilizing prognosis-associated polyamine metabolism genes derived from univariate COX analysis. This unsupervised method employed the K-means algorithm and the “ConsensusClusterPlus” package in R to categorize cases into various distinct clusters, guided by specific markers or characteristics. Signature gene sets, indicative of particular biological conditions or pathways, demonstrated stable expression patterns.The consensus clustering process involved 1000 iterations, with each iteration sampling 80% of the dataset. The optimal cluster count was determined by examining the Item-Consensus plot, applying the Proportion of Ambiguity Clusters (PAC) algorithm, and evaluating the incremental change in the area under the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) curve. The analysis identified two optimal clusters, namely the “polyamine metabolism” and “non-polyamine metabolism” clusters. Subsequently, Kaplan-Meier survival curves were constructed for these clusters to assess and compare their overall survival (OS) outcomes.

Machine learning and model construction and validation

The breast cancer datasets were divided into the TCGA and GEO cohorts to facilitate comprehensive analysis. These datasets were integrated using a suite of 10 distinct machine learning algorithms, including methods such as random forests, support vector machines, and neural networks. Additionally, 101 algorithmic combinations were explored to optimize model development and enhance predictive accuracy.Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was employed to identify disparities in overall survival between the identified risk clusters, providing insights into the prognostic significance of the models. The R package “ggplot2” was utilized for data visualization, specifically to create Sankey diagrams. These diagrams effectively illustrated the interrelationships between the two risk clusters and their respective grouping categories, offering a clear visual representation of the data flow and cluster dynamics.For further validation, samples were segmented into three categories: overall, TCGA-specific, and GEO-specific. This segmentation allowed for tailored Kaplan-Meier survival analyses and the construction of ROC curves for each category, ensuring a robust evaluation of the model’s predictive capabilities across different datasets. The use of ROC curves facilitated the assessment of model sensitivity and specificity, providing a comprehensive understanding of performance metrics.Advanced machine learning techniques, such as cross-validation, feature selection, and hyperparameter tuning, were employed to refine the models. These techniques ensured that the models were not only accurate but also generalizable, minimizing the risk of overfitting. This comprehensive approach underscores the potential of machine learning in enhancing breast cancer prognosis and tailoring patient-specific treatment strategies.

Enrichment analysis of function and immune infiltration

Utilizing Gene Ontology (GO) classification analysis, we ascertained the potential roles of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) within the polyamine metabolism and non-polyamine metabolism groups. A False Discovery Rate (FDR) threshold of less than 0.05 was applied to denote statistical significance.We deployed the CIBERSORT and ESTIMATE algorithms, implemented in R, to scrutinize the variances in infiltration status among 22 immune cell subtypes between the high-risk and low-risk cohorts. The ESTIMATE algorithm assessed the expression data from malignant tumor tissues to quantify the stromal and immune cell components.

Copy number variation and tumor mutation burden

Somatic mutation and copy number variation datasets were sourced from the TCGA GDC platform (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The “ggplot2” R package was employed to graphically represent the copy number variations in the form of a bar chart. Subsequently, the “maftools” R package was utilized to generate a waterfall plot that delineates the mutational landscape for both high-risk and low-risk patient groups. Lastly, the “ggpubr” and “reshape2” R packages were harnessed to chart the correlation between mutational burden and the risk score.

Single-cell level validation

We assembled gene expression data from six distinct individuals, yielding raw matrices through CellRanger, version 3.0.1. The subsequent data processing was conducted with the Seurat R package, version 2.3.4. High-quality cells were filtered according to specific criteria: (1) a cell’s gene count ranged from 200 to 8000; (2) a cell’s total UMI count did not exceed 50,000; (3) the ratio of mitochondrial gene expression, an apoptosis-related indicator, was capped at 25%. Employing Seurat’s t-SNE for nonlinear dimensionality reduction, we projected complex cellular data onto a two-dimensional plane, effectively grouping cells with analogous expression profiles and distancing those with divergent patterns. This approach facilitated a clearer visualization of cellular distinctions.Further, we applied SingleR for cell type annotation, leveraging the expression pattern similarities between the query cells and a reference dataset. Pseudotime analysis was executed with the monocle package, a technique frequently employed for forecasting the developmental trajectories of cellular subpopulations and pathways of cell death or differentiation, particularly in the context of disease progression.

Statistics

The Student’s t-test is deployed for assessing differences in continuous variables that exhibit a normal distribution, while the χ2 test serves as the instrument for categorical data comparison across subgroups. For assessing disparities among multiple groups, the Kruskal-Wallis test, a non-parametric alternative to one-way ANOVA, is applied. Unless specified otherwise, a p-value of less than 0.05 is adopted as the threshold for statistical significance.

Machine learning and model construction

To construct a diagnostic model for polyamine metabolism and breast cancer, we utilized a combination of 15 machine learning algorithms to analyze the prognostic-related genes obtained above. The breast cancer dataset was divided based on sources into TCGA and GEO, and 101 prediction models were fitted using tenfold cross-validation framework, and the C-index was calculated (Fig. 4A). After comprehensive consideration, the SuperPC model was selected, and single-factor and multi-factor COX analysis of risk scoring, as well as consistency index analysis of clinical features, were conducted (Fig. 4B, C, H). Single-factor and multi-factor COX analysis indicated that the risk model can independently evaluate the prognosis of breast cancer. There were differences in risk scoring between the polyamine metabolism group and the non-polyamine metabolism group, with the polyamine metabolism group having higher risk scores (Fig. 4D). Samples were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups (based on the median) according to risk scores, and survival analysis was performed, showing better prognosis in the low-risk group compared to the high-risk group (P = 0.01) (Fig. 4I). Subsequently, mappings of the polyamine metabolism group and non-polyamine metabolism group with the high-risk and low-risk groups were conducted, and a Sankey diagram was drawn. The results indicated consistency between the polyamine metabolism group and non-polyamine metabolism group with the high-risk and low-risk groups (Fig. 4E). ROC analysis for one year, three years, and five years, as well as C-index testing of the SuperPC model, demonstrated that the SuperPC model could predict the survival status of breast cancer at one year (AUC = 0.661), three years (AUC = 0.653), and five years (AUC = 0.718) with certain accuracy (C-index: 0.750 (95% CI: 0.704–0.795)) (Fig. 4F, G). Based on clinical information, line graphs were plotted, and a randomly selected sample was displayed (Fig. 4J).

Model validation

A differential heatmap was generated for the model genes between the high-risk group and the low-risk group, showing a consistent trend with the model coefficients (Fig. 4A). The C-index of the risk model was evaluated for one, two, and three years in breast cancer patients, indicating good predictive accuracy (Fig. 4B). The samples were divided into all, TCGA group, and GEO group for survival difference analysis, ROC analysis, and DCA analysis. The results show that regardless of all, TCGA group, or GEO group, the risk model can effectively evaluate the prognosis of breast cancer (Fig. 4C-G).

Discussion

Breast cancer remains the most common malignant tumor among women worldwide, with its incidence steadily increasing, causing a huge burden on health and economy (Winters et al. 2017).According to data from the World Health Organization, approximately 2.26 million new cases of breast cancer were reported in 2020, accounting for about 11.7% of all new cancer diagnoses. This disease poses a significant threat to women’s health and profoundly impacts their quality of life (Kashyap et al. 2022).Despite the availability of multiple treatment strategies, including surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, and targeted therapy (Faridah et al. 2024; Jokar et al. 2021), However, challenges still exist in personalized treatment and accurate prognostic assessment (Grabinski and Brawley 2022; Odle 2017). 。Therefore, continuous research aimed at improving breast cancer management strategies is crucial.

Polyamines are essential for cell growth and proliferation. In breast cancer, elevated levels of polyamines such as putrescine, spermidine, and spermine have been linked to increased tumor cell proliferation and survival.Tumor Microenvironment: Polyamines contribute to shaping the tumor microenvironment by promoting angiogenesis and modulating immune responses. This can lead to enhanced tumor growth and metastasis (Akinyele and Wallace 2022; Kim and Lee 2022).

This study focuses on the role of polyamine metabolism in breast cancer patients, emphasizing its relationship with cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis, which are crucial in cancer progression. Previous research has indicated a significant correlation between dysregulated polyamine metabolism and various cancers including breast cancer (Cervelli et al. 2014; Akinyele and Wallace 2022; Islam et al. 2022). By utilizing machine learning algorithms and differential expression analysis, we have constructed a risk scoring model based on genes related to polyamine metabolism, providing a new method for enhancing prognostic evaluation of breast cancer and guiding personalized treatment strategies (Tran et al. 2021; Chiu et al. 2021). The following discussion will elaborate on our findings and their potential implications for future therapeutic interventions.

PSMD14, also known as POH1 or RPN11, is a subunit of the 19 S regulatory particle of the proteasome. PSMD14 is involved in the ubiquitin-proteasome system, which regulates protein degradation. This process is crucial for maintaining cellular protein homeostasis and influences cancer cell proliferation and survival. By affecting the degradation of cell cycle regulators, PSMD14 can impact cell cycle progression, contributing to uncontrolled cell division in breast cancer (Lee et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2024).

The identification of 286 differentially expressed genes related to polyamine metabolism in breast cancer highlights the importance of metabolic pathways in tumor biology. The results indicate that polyamines are intricately involved in cellular processes such as proliferation and apoptosis, which are critical for cancer progression. The differential expression of these genes suggests that they may serve as potential biomarkers for prognosis and therapeutic targets. Previous studies have emphasized the role of polyamines in various cancers, including breast cancer, where the dysregulation of polyamines is associated with enhanced cell growth and invasion (Akinyele and Wallace 2022; Reza et al. 2024). In addition, the association of these genes with different subtypes of breast cancer can reveal specific molecular mechanisms of tumor heterogeneity, thereby guiding personalized treatment strategies (Akinyele and Wallace 2021; Wei et al. 2024). The functional significance of these findings is worth further investigation to elucidate the pathways through which polyamine metabolism influences cancer behavior, potentially leading to the development of novel therapeutic interventions targeting these metabolic changes.

The c-index of the SuperPC model is 0.750, validating the robustness of our risk scoring mechanism in predicting the prognosis of breast cancer patients. This performance metric indicates that our model has a significant capability to differentiate survival probabilities based on risk profiles derived from polyamine metabolism-related genes. The ability of the SuperPC model to combine high-dimensional gene expression data with clinical information demonstrates the potential of machine learning in improving prognostic accuracy (Rossi et al. 2019).

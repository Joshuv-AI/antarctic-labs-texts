Eggshell Derived Nanohydroxyapatite as a Bone Graft for Intrabony Defects was Evaluated Using (CBCT) - A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Vishnuvarthan Ganapathy, Anitha Balaji, T S Sampath Kumar, Mohanasatheesh Shanmugam
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:The study aims to assess the effectiveness of eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA) as a bone graft material for treating intrabony defects, using cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) for evaluation.Materials and Methods:Ten patients were divided into two groups: Group 1: individuals with 2-wall defects, Group 2: those with 3-wall defects. Follow-up appointments at 1-, 3-, and 6-month intervals included assessments of clinical attachment level (CAL), probing depth (PD), and gingival recession (GR). CBCT scans were conducted pre-operative, immediately post-surgery, and at 1-month, 3-month, and 5-month follow-ups.Results:In both groups, clinical parameters such as CAL, PD, and CBCT measurements reveal significant decreases in CAL and PD values alongside positive trends in CBCT measurements. Additionally, Group 2 exhibited enhancements in periodontal health, suggesting the potential effectiveness of EnHA.Conclusion:EnHA demonstrates potential as a substitute for bone grafting in intrabony defects, showing favourable trends in both clinical and CBCT evaluations during a 6-month observation period.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontal diseases are prevalent globally, resulting in the degradation of essential periodontal structures.[1] Clinical studies supported by histological evidence suggest that bone grafting is a highly effective treatment for regenerating these structures and establishing a functional periodontal ligament.[2]

Intrabony defects, as outlined by the Academy of Periodontology, are periodontal defects found within the bone and enclosed by one, two, or three bony walls based on the number of osseous walls involved. However, it’s crucial to consider their biological aspects, including the role of the periodontal ligament. The primary objective of periodontal therapy is to regenerate the periodontium by stimulating new cementum formation and facilitating the integration of periodontal ligament fibers, along with bone filling. Bone grafting surgery offers a potential remedy for replacing deficient or damaged bone tissues, drawing from various sources.[3456]

Although autografts are often considered, the gold standard because of their compatibility. They have post-operative complications and donor site morbidity. Similarly, allografts, and Xenografts also have potential drawbacks.[789] Additionally, artificial bone graft materials such as bioactive glass, glass ionomer, aluminium oxide, calcium sulphate, and calcium phosphate have been developed specifically to ensure their suitability for use in patients’ bone graft surgeries.[1011]

Eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatites (EnHA) are emerging as effective and biocompatible alternatives to traditional bone graft materials. However, there is a lack of histological studies examining grafting outcomes in human subjects.[12131415] The main aim of this study is to improve our understanding of EnHA as a bone graft material, specifically in addressing intrabony defects in human patients. This will be achieved by utilizing advanced imaging methods such as cone beam computed tomography (CBCT).[161718]

Sample selection

A total of ten subjects (6 males and 4 females) were divided into two groups: Group 1, comprising individuals with 2-wall defects (3 males and 2 females), and Group 2, consisting of individuals with 3-wall defects (3 males and 2 females). The evaluation spanned 6 months.

Preparation of hen’s eggshell as a bone graft material

The process involves washing eggshells, heating them to 900°C to decompose organic matter and convert calcium carbonate to calcium oxide, grinding the resulting calcium hydroxide, and suspending it in water. This suspension is then reacted with diammonium hydrogen phosphate, and microwave irradiation, washing, and drying to remove unwanted ions. Further heating enhances crystallinity, and particle size is adjusted before mixing with saline to form a paste for bone grafting.[1920]

Surgical procedure

Patients underwent scaling and root planning before surgery. With local anesthesia, Intrasulcular incisions were made, comprehensive debridement, and granulation tissue was removed. Root surfaces were conditioned with topical tetracycline hydrochloride. Osseous defects were filled with EDnHA paste before suturing, and non-eugenol periodontal dressings were applied for one week [Figure 1a-f]. Follow-up appointments at 1-, 3-, and 6-month intervals included assessments of clinical attachment level (CAL), probing depth (PD), and gingival recession (GR). CBCT scans were conducted pre-op, immediate post-surgery, 1-month, 3-month, and 5-month follow-ups [Figure 2a-f]. Bone density measurements were performed using SIDEXIS XG-Sirona software, comparing density values between the defect sites, and surrounding normal bone. The information was tabulated in Microsoft Excel 2010, and statistical analysis was performed using the R statistical package.

DISCUSSION

Synthetic and naturally derived alternatives, such as EnHA, are being explored. Uraz et al. demonstrated the biocompatibility of EnHA with traditional hydroxyapatite across various parameters.[212223]

Recent advancements aim to create grafts from naturally occurring sources with constituents similar to bone, such as hydroxyapatites and tricalcium phosphates. One example of this trend is the development of EnHA bone grafts.[2425]

Jayasree et al.[26] observed a significant decrease in PD and CAL following 6 months of treatment with cement-based cement. A radiographic study during the 6-month follow-up demonstrated good bone healing. Our study observed that there were no significant modifications in PD and CAL, but there was no three-dimensional view, and only a two-dimensional view was given.[2728]

Tammina Mohana Sri Vani et al.[29] used CBCT to assess bone density and fill in intrabony defects. Their findings revealed that both treatment groups exhibited significantly higher bone density and fill values, indicating the efficacy of periosteal pedicle and EnHA in promoting bone regeneration.

In summary, EnHA emerges as an effective alternative for bone grafting, providing both biocompatibility and cost-effectiveness. Nonetheless, our study encountered limitations, notably a small sample size and the absence of long-term follow-up data.

CONCLUSION

The Results showed positive trends in clinical attachment level, probing depth, and CBCT measurements over a 6-month. Group 1 showed superior results, emphasizing individual patient responses. The study supports the potential of EnHA in promoting healing and improving periodontal health. Further research is needed to validate treatment protocols and establish their clinical significance.

Introduction

The primary objective of every pediatric dentist should be to preserve the primary tooth in its optimal condition. In order to preserve the integrity of the arch, primary teeth should be maintained for a longer period of time since they are thought to be the finest space maintainers.1 The development of permanent teeth and the growth of the jaws are both significantly influenced by the presence of primary teeth. Primary teeth are also important for the overall health of young patients. Dental caries and stress on primary teeth can cause inflammation of the pulp, which can be painful and warrant treatment.2 When a tooth has a pulpal infection, pulpectomy is one of the indicated options for therapy.3 To avoid the premature loss of teeth, Pulpectomy is a conservative treatment that helps prevent premature tooth loss. The goals of pulpectomy are to lower the bacterial load and encourage the periradicular tissues to heal.4 The primary teeth are preserved after a pulpectomy, which allows them to continue functioning as an integral part of the dental arch for many years to come.5 Pulpectomy is a more rational course of therapy than extraction since it promotes the eruption of the permanent teeth that will replace the primary teeth and assures necessary exfoliation of those teeth.6

Substantial access, accurate cleaning and shaping, and exceptional quality of obturation are essential for the pulpectomy to be successful. The primary means available to assess the effectiveness of any new tool, material, or technique utilized for pulpectomy in primary teeth is the quality of obturation. For primary teeth, there is just one criterion available for evaluating obturation quality, and that is considered the gold-standard method given by Coll and Sadrian in 1996.7 According to this,7 optimal filling means one or more canals with obturating material ending at the radiographic apex or up to 2 mm short of the apex, underfilling means all the canals were filled more than 2 mm short of the apex, and overfilling means if any canal shows obturated material extending beyond the radiographic apex.

There is no consideration in the current criterion to identify any voids in the canal. The chance of survival of a treated tooth is reduced when there are voids in obturated root canals. Furthermore, the current criteria's classification of optimal fill vs underfill is debatable since, in a multi-rooted tooth, if one canal has an ideal fill but the other canals fall well short of the apex, the optimal fill classification is still used. This needs to be altered. The restriction of the current classification must be addressed with a proposed canal-specific classification. Furthermore, it is important to emphasize the distinct classification for maxillary and mandibular primary teeth, as well as anterior and posterior primary teeth. To overcome these limitations, a new classification has been proposed in 2023, namely the KEDOO classification,8 which is canal, segmental and arch-specific in assessing the obturation quality of the performed therapeutic pulpectomy procedure. As a technological advancement in the field of pediatric rotary endodontics, rotary file systems have been designed exclusively for usage in primary teeth by KEDO file systems.9 In order to achieve optimal preparation, single rotary file systems were introduced in 2019, named KEDO S SQUARE, and in 2021, named KEDO S plus. There is a paucity of literature on the obturation quality of teeth treated with these file systems and the newer classification.

Hence, the current study aimed to compare and evaluate the obturation quality of pediatric single rotary file systems used for pulpectomy procedure through the latest classification.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Sample Selection

A double-blinded, randomized controlled clinical trial was used to conduct the study. The sample size estimation was done using G*Power software (version 3.1) for t-tests–means: difference between two independent means (two groups). The inputs for sample size estimation are a minimum 80% power for an alpha of 0.05 and a 5% significance level (significant at 95% confidence level) with a 0.6 effect size and one measurement of parameter.1 With all these factors, sample size was estimated as 90, with 45 in each group. Criteria for Case Selection

The study includes the visiting outpatients of the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Manav Rachna Dental College, Faridabad. Inclusion criteria include normal healthy children aged 4–8 years with Frankl behavior ratings of 3 (positive) or 4 (definitely positive) affected with irreversible pulpitis in primary molars with no signs of pathological resorption and an intraoral periapical radiograph exhibiting 2/3rd of each root remaining. Whereas, Children with Special healthcare needs, primary molars with mobility, unrestorable crown structures, resorbed roots, and perforated pulpal floor were excluded from case selection.

Informed Consent and Randomization

Every parent or legal guardian was requested to sign an informed consent form before their child was allowed to participate in this study. For an unbiased study, the sample size was divided in a computer-generated randomized block design. The randomization of instrumentation included:

Group I (45 teeth): Kedo S Square files

Group I (45 teeth): Kedo S Square files

Group II (45 teeth): Kedo S Plus files

Group II (45 teeth): Kedo S Plus files

Blinding

Parents and children were kept blinded about the instrumentation technique. An examiner who was likewise blinded evaluated the quality of obturation. As the instrumentation had to be performed, the investigator was not blinded.

Study Protocol

In teeth selected through inclusion criteria, pulpectomy was done by administration of local anesthesia using lignocaine 2% with 1:80,000 adrenaline. After administration of local anesthesia, access cavity preparation was performed using BR–31/41/46/49 depending on the extent of cavity, succeeded by deroofing of pulp chamber using DG-16 explorer for detecting orifices.10 Access cavity was extended in the crown of tooth from the occlusal aspect using Endo-Z Tungsten carbide bur and then using K-file 10, 15 patency of canal and working length is estimated.

Based on randomization, in group I, Kedo S Square file and in group II, Kedo S Plus file were used with AI-powered Woodpecker Endo motor for biomechanical preparation of the canals. In both groups EDTA was used as a chelating agent and saline as an irrigant solution. Once irrigated, the canals were dried using size 30 paper points followed by obturation using Vitapex (Neo Dental, Tokyo, Japan) as obturating material through disposable syringe tip technique. Once treated, the teeth were given a GIC post-obturation restoration11 followed by a stainless steel crown in a further follow-up visit.

An intraoral periapical radiograph was taken using the paralleling technique immediately after obturation, which was assessed by a blinded evaluator through radiographic viewing box. The assessment of Obturation quality is done and evaluated through KEDOO Classification in Figure 2.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 24. Continuous variables are summarized as means and SDs, while categorical variables are summarized as counts and percentages (%). Inferential statistics of obturation quality were calculated by a chi-square test.

Discussion

Debridement of the root canal and filling the root canal with a biocompatible obturating material that would resorb at the same rate as the tooth's root are merely two aspects of addressing primary teeth infection.12 The potential risk of apical percolation and coronal leakage diminishes when the obturation is of superior quality.13 In primary teeth, the quality of obturation is crucial because numerous accessory canals found in the root canal of these teeth make it difficult to completely remove infectious agents.14–17 Any obturating material's ultimate objective is to adhere to the canal walls and completely fill the root without overfilling.18 Methods used to assess obturation quality include tooth sectioning, radiography, dye penetration, radioisotope techniques, and clearing methods can be used to assess the quality of the obturation.19

Many studies have been conducted in the literature to examine the quality of obturation utilizing various materials and methodologies; nevertheless, the standards for evaluating obturation quality have remained consistent. Hence, the newer KEDOO classification was framed to overcome the limitations of the existing Coll and Sadrian criteria.8

When assessed using the newly framed KEDOO classification, Kedo S Plus files produced better obturation quality when compared with Kedo S Square files. When Kedo S Plus produced 90.3% and 66.67% of ideal obturation in mandibular and maxillary teeth, respectively, Kedo S Square produced 66.7% and 50% of ideal obturation in mandibular and maxillary teeth, respectively.

A recent study conducted by Foram et al. showed a similar result where KEDO S Square and KEDO S Plus showed 65% and 85% ideal obturation quality, respectively. However, a study done by Ranjith et al.,20 reported that KEDO S Plus showed only 53.3% optimal obturation and Shim et al.21,21 reported in his study that KEDO S Square produced a satisfactory, efficient ideal obturation of 85%.

Limitations

Though the newer KEDOO classification rectified the existing limitations of Coll and Sadrian criteria, it did not include the aspects of accessory canals in the teeth requiring pulpectomy treatment.

Conclusion

With the observed results, it can be concluded that in terms of obturation quality, Kedo S Plus files produce better obturation when compared with Kedo S Square files, and KEDOO classification eliminated the existing limitation criterion of the Coll and Sadrian criteria method.

Though the study was found to be statistically nonsignificant, these observed assessments through KEDOO classification aid in improving and enhancing the operator in achieving ideal obturation, which is the main objective of pulpectomy, eliminating the presence of infectious agents inside the canals of the pulp chamber.

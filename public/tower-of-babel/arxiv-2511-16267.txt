Abstract
. In this study, the geometric properties of null helices on a totally umbilical submanifold within a three-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold are investigated. The pseudo-Riemannian metric structure of semi-Riemannian manifolds and the fact that the submanifold is totally umbilical complicate the differential geometry of null helices. The study uses the given null Frenet frame to reveal the local properties of null helices (the curvatures h , k 1 , k 2 h,k_{1},k_{2} of the null curves). By considering the degenerate metric condition of null helices due to the null tangent vector and the structure of the totally umbilical submanifold, equations and invariants characterizing null helices are obtained. This study aims to contribute to the theory of null curves on semi-Riemannian manifolds and the geometry of special submanifolds.

1.
Introduction

Semi-Riemannian manifolds are geometric structures of critical importance,
particularly in physics, for modelling spacetime and gravity. The study of
the geometric and topological properties of semi-Riemannian manifolds with
general dimensions is an important area of research in differential
geometry. For example, higher-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifolds with
different metric signs can provide theoretical groundwork for alternative
cosmological models or for understanding the early universe, current
theoretical physics models, such as string theory (10 or 11 dimensions),
M-theory (11 dimensions), and Kaluza-Klein theories (5 dimensions), are
often formulated on semi-Riemannian (or supermanifold, in the context of
supergravity) manifolds with more than four dimensions.

Null curves are a special class of curves derived directly from the metric
structure of semi-Riemannian manifolds. They have a degenerate induced
metric, which presents special geometric challenges, such as the
inapplicability of the standard Frenet-Serret frame. Therefore, the
differential geometry of null curves is a separate field of study and is
closely related to structures such as lightlike cones. Null (light-like)
curves defined on these manifolds are a special class of curves derived from
their metrics.

The geometric interpretation of null helices on semi-Riemannian manifolds
combines both the degenerate metric properties of a light-like trajectory
and the helical effect of the tangent vector (or related vector) making a
constant pseudo-angle with a fixed null direction. This interpretation is
particularly critical for modelling light propagation along helical paths in
curved spacetimes or the trajectories of massless particles, and for
understanding the differential geometry of curves on manifolds with a
degenerate metric. Null helices are a special class of curves that exhibit
both the mathematical depth and the physical relevance of semi-Riemannian
geometry. To briefly summarize their applications, in quasi-Riemannian
spaces that model spacetime, such as Lorentzian manifolds, null curves
represent light rays or the trajectories of massless particles. Null helices
can be used to model light trajectories that follow a ”spiral” path in
curved spacetime. For example, such geometries can arise when analysing
light trajectories near rotating black holes or light propagation in certain
cosmological models. Furthermore, the geometric interpretation of null
helices is crucial for understanding how concepts such as curvature,
torsion, and moving frames behave within this degenerate structure. Tools
such as the null Frenet frame play a critical role in this interpretation.
Null helices are a special subclass within the class of null curves on
semi-Riemannian manifolds. Their study enriches the general theory of null
curves and demonstrates the existence of light-like orbits with specific
geometric properties. For example, null orbits are central to the study of
the behaviour of massless fields or particles (photons, gravitons) in
theoretical physics. Null helices geometry holds theoretical potential for
modelling the spiral behaviour that these fields or particles may exhibit in
specific field configurations or space-time structures.

4.
Conclusion

In this study, the null helices on a totally umbilical submanifold within a
three-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold are investigated from a
geometrical perspective. The pseudo-Riemannian metric structure of
semi-Riemannian manifolds and the effects of the totally umbilical nature of
the submanifold on the differential geometry of null helices are
demonstrated. The null Frenet frame is shown to be an effective tool for
characterizing the local properties of null helices. By considering the
degenerate metric condition of null helices due to the null tangent vector
and the structure of the totally geodesic submanifold, the equations and
invariants characterizing null helices are obtained.

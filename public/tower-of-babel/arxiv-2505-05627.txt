On the structure of sequences with minimal maximal pattern complexity
Casey Schlortt

Abstract
In 2002, Kamae and Zamboni introduced maximal pattern complexity and determined that any aperiodic sequence must have maximal pattern complexity at least 2 ​ k 2k . In 2006, Kamae and Rao examined the maximal pattern complexity of sequences over larger alphabets and showed that sequences which have maximal pattern complexity less than ℓ ​ k \ell k , for ℓ \ell the size of the alphabet, must have some periodic structure. In this paper, we investigate the structure of sequences of low maximal pattern complexity over ℓ \ell letters where lim inf k → ∞ p α ∗ ​ ( k ) − 3 ​ k = − ∞ \liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty . In addition, we show that the minimal maximal pattern complexity of an aperiodic sequence which uses all ℓ \ell letters is p α ∗ ​ ( k ) = 2 ​ k + ℓ − 2 p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2 , and give an exact structure for aperiodic sequences with this maximal pattern complexity.



1
Introduction

There are various methods to study the complexity of symbolically defined dynamical systems. Block (word) complexity, which counts the number of blocks of size
k
which appear in a subshift, was introduced in 1938 by Morse and Hedlund as a way to determine the ’size’ of different subshifts. Their first paper,
[
MH38
]
, contains the proof of the Morse-Hedlund Theorem which states that any aperiodic sequence must have block complexity at least
k+1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Their second paper,
[
MH40
]
, introduces
Sturmian sequences
, defined to be infinite sequences over two letters which have block complexity
k+1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and which can be represented as a coding of an irrational circle rotation.

For larger alphabets, a similar proof shows that the block complexity of an aperiodic sequence over
\ell\geq 2
letters must be at least
k+\ell-1
. A
Sturmian sequence over
\ell\geq 2
letters
is defined to be an aperiodic sequence with block complexity
k+\ell-1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

[
Fog02
]
.

The maximal pattern complexity of an infinite sequence
\alpha
, denoted
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)
, was introduced by Kamae and Zamboni in 2002 in
[
KZ02b
]
as a way to link word complexity and sequence entropy. In their first paper, they showed that the maximal pattern complexity of any aperiodic sequence must be at least
2k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which is a direct analogue to the Morse-Hedlund Theorem.

In
[
KZ02b
]
, they also introduced
pattern Sturmian sequences
as an analogue to Sturmian sequences, and defined pattern Sturmian sequences over two letters to be aperiodic sequences with the lowest possible maximal pattern complexity,
2k
. In
[
KZ02a
]
, they furthered their study of pattern Sturmian sequences, showing that sequences generated by a coding of an irrational circle rotation by any two interval partition of a circle and simple Toeplitz sequences are examples of pattern Sturmian sequences over two letters.

In 2006, Kamae and Rao extended the study of maximal pattern complexity for sequences over finite alphabets of size greater than two in
[
KR06
]
. They determined that a sequence over
\ell\geq 2
letters which has maximal pattern complexity less than
\ell k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
is
periodic by projection
, meaning there is a nonconstant, coordinatewise, factor map
f
which maps the sequence to a periodic sequence. They extended the definition of pattern Sturmian sequences over two letters to
pattern Sturmian sequences over
\boldsymbol{\ell\geq 2}
letters
, defining them to be sequences of maximal pattern complexity
\ell k
which are not periodic by projection.

As stated in their paper, their results hold for sequences in which every letter appears infinitely often. We will make this same assumption for all sequences in this paper, since the ability for a letter to show up a finite number of times can be eliminated with a finite shift.

Although
[
KR06
]
isolates the minimal maximal pattern complexity for aperiodic sequences which are not periodic by projection, there are aperiodic sequences with lower pattern complexity. The goal of this work is to isolate the lowest maximal pattern complexity for any aperiodic sequence and determine the structure of such sequences. We define a
strong pattern Sturmian sequence
over
\ell\geq 2
letters to be an aperiodic sequence with maximal pattern complexity
2k+\ell-2
. As seen in
Theorem
2.11
, this is the lowest possible maximal pattern complexity an aperiodic sequence can have (given all letters of the alphabet appear in the sequence), and this notion aligns closely with the definition of a Sturmian sequence over
\ell
letters. Furthermore, since the notions of pattern Sturmian sequences and strong pattern Sturmian sequences coincide when the alphabet is two letters, we will restrict our study to sequences for which the size of the alphabet is greater than two.

In this paper, we introduce three main results. The first result,
Theorem
1.1
, gives a general structure for aperiodic sequences with low maximal pattern complexity.
Example
6.3
demonstrates that the bound of
4k
in
Theorem
1.1
cannot be substantially improved.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha
be an aperiodic sequence such that
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
.
If

\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty

(1.1)

then there exists some decomposition of
\alpha
into
m>1
residues

\{\alpha^{(i)}=\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i+m}\alpha_{i+2m}...:0\leq i\leq m-1\}

such that exactly one of these residues is eventually aperiodic over two letters,
a
and
b
, and any residue containing finitely many
a
and
b
symbols is eventually constant.

If
\alpha
is uniformly recurrent, then the bound in Equation (1.1) can be improved to
4k
.

The second result,
Theorem
1.2
, states that any strong pattern Sturmian sequence must consist of a pattern Sturmian sequence over two letters threaded together with constant sequences.
Example
6.1
demonstrates the existence of sequences which are strong pattern Sturmian.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha
be an aperiodic sequence such that
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
.
If
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then the aperiodic residue from
Theorem
1.1
must be a pattern Sturmian over two letters and all other residues must be constant.

The third result,
Theorem
1.3
, is a partial converse of
Theorem
1.2
which states that a sequence which can be decomposed into a pattern Sturmian sequence threaded together with constant sequences must have maximal pattern complexity
2k+C
for some constant
C\in\mathbb{N}
.
Example
6.2
demonstrates that a literal converse to
Theorem
1.2
is untrue. When
C=\ell-2
, the sequence is strong pattern Sturmian.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha
be an aperiodic sequence such that
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
.
If
\alpha
can be decomposed into residues such that one residue is a pattern Sturmian over two letters and all other residues are constant, then for sufficiently large
k
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+C
for some
C\in\mathbb{N}
with
C\geq\ell-2
.

It is important to note that
Theorem
1.1
allows for certain residues to be eventually periodic over two letters, but any nonconstant periodic residue must be periodic over the alphabet of the aperiodic residue. However,
Theorem
1.2
requires that any residue be aperiodic or constant. Strong pattern Sturmian sequences also cannot have eventually constant residues and the aperiodic residue can only witness two letters.

It is also important to recognize that in the case where
\ell=3
, these results give a general structure for all aperiodic sequences
\alpha
which have maximal pattern complexity less than
3k
. These sequences require a single aperiodic residue which is eventually over two letters,
a
and
b
, threaded together with eventually constant sequences of the third letter
c
.

2
Definitions and Preliminaries

This section is dedicated to introducing definitions and results needed in the following sections.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set and
\alpha=\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}...\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Then
\alpha
is a
(one-sided) infinite sequence over finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, we will only consider sequences in which a letter appearing once in the sequence means the letter appears infinitely in the sequence.

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is endowed with the product topology.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\sigma:\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the
shift map
defined by
\sigma(\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}...)=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}...
. We say that a
finite shift of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is the sequence
\sigma^{t}(\alpha)=\alpha_{t}\alpha_{t+1}\alpha_{t+2}...
for any integer
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We say that the
orbit
of
\alpha
, denoted
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
, is the set
\mathcal{O}(\alpha):=\{\sigma^{t}(\alpha):t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
. The
orbit closure
of
\alpha
is the orbit of
\alpha
together with its limit points and is denoted
\overline{\mathcal{O}(\alpha)}
.

While
\alpha
uses letters from
\mathcal{A}
, it may not use all letters from
\mathcal{A}
. Thus to denote the letters which appear in an infinite sequence
\alpha
, we will use

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}:=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\exists i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ such that }\alpha_{i}=a\}.

Definition 2.3
.

A sequence
\alpha
is
periodic
if there exists some
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha=\sigma^{t}(\alpha)
. A sequence
\alpha
is
eventually periodic
if there exists some
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma^{t}(\alpha)
is periodic. A sequence
\alpha
is
aperiodic
if
\alpha
is not periodic or eventually periodic.

Definition 2.4
.

A sequence
\alpha
is
recurrent
if for all
L\in\mathbb{N}
there exists some
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{0}...\alpha_{L-1}=\alpha_{M}...\alpha_{M+L-1}
. If for all
L\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
R_{L}=\{M:\alpha_{0}...\alpha_{L-1}=\alpha_{M}...\alpha_{M+L-1}\}
is syndetic (there are bounded gaps between subsequent elements), then
\alpha
is
uniformly recurrent
.

Definition 2.5
.

A
\boldsymbol{k}
-sized window
\boldsymbol{\tau}
is an increasing set of
k
indices beginning with 0:

\tau:=\{0=\tau(0)<\tau(1)<...<\tau(k-1)\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We say a
(k+1)
-sized window
\tau^{\prime}
is an
immediate extension
of a
k
-sized window
\tau
if
\tau^{\prime}=\{\tau(0),\tau(1),...,\tau(k-1),h\}
for some
h>\tau(k-1)
.

Given a
k
-sized window
\tau
, we will use the notation
m\tau
to denote the
k
-sized window
m\tau:=\{m\tau(0),m\tau(1),...,m\tau(k-1)\}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A
\boldsymbol{\tau}
-word over
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a finite word of length
k
denoted
\alpha[n+\tau]
and defined to be

\alpha[n+\tau]:=\alpha_{\tau(0)+n}\alpha_{\tau(1)+n}...\alpha_{\tau(k-1)+n}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

The
set of all
\boldsymbol{\tau}
-words over
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is the set denoted by
F_{\alpha}(\tau)
and defined by

F_{\alpha}(\tau):=\{\alpha[n+\tau]:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

Since
F_{\alpha}(\tau)
is finite, there exists some
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that
F_{\alpha}(\tau)=\{\alpha[n+\tau]:n\in\mathbb{N}\cap[0,L)\}
.

Definition 2.7
.

The
maximal pattern complexity
of an infinite sequence
\alpha
is the function
p_{\alpha}^{*}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
defined by

p_{\alpha}^{*}(k):=\sup_{\tau}\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)

where the supremum is taken over all
k
-sized windows
\tau
.

We note that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
k
-sized window
\tau
such that
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)
and we will call any such window
\tau
a
maximal
\boldsymbol{k}
-sized window
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.

In
[
KZ02b
]
, Kamae and Zamboni proved the following two theorems for maximal pattern complexity, the first which acts as an analogue to the Morse-Hedlund Theorem for block complexity and the second which relates the number of
\tau
-words to the number of
\tau^{\prime}
-words in an aperiodic sequence
\alpha
for some immediate extension
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau
.

Theorem 2.8
(
[
KZ02b
]
).

An infinite sequence
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
for a finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
is eventually periodic if and only if
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\leq 2k-1
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 2.9
(
[
KZ02b
]
).

For an arbitrary word
\alpha
over a finite set
\mathcal{A}
, if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a window
\tau
of size
k
such that
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\leq\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)+1
holds for any immediate extension
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau
, then
\alpha
is eventually periodic.

The following fact is an extension of the Morse-Hedlund Theorem for any window
\tau
, which follows from the same proof.

Fact 2.10
.

For any aperiodic sequence
\alpha
, any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and any
k
-sized window
\tau
,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\geq k+1
.

Using
Theorem
2.9
, it is easy to extend
Theorem
2.8
to the case when
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{A}
as seen in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.11
.

If an infinite sequence
\alpha
is aperiodic and
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 2
, then
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq 2k+\ell-2
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof:

Suppose
\alpha
is aperiodic and
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 2
. Clearly, since
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(1)=\ell
.

Suppose for some
k>1
we have
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq 2k+\ell-2
.

Let
\tau
be a maximal
k
-sized window for
\alpha
. Then
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\geq 2k+\ell-2
. By
Theorem
2.9
, since
\alpha
is aperiodic, there exists some immediate extension
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau
such that
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\geq\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)+2
. Thus,

p_{\alpha}^{*}(k+1)\geq\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\geq\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)+2\geq 2(k+1)+\ell-2.

Hence for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq 2k+\ell-2
.

In
[
KZ02b
]
, Kamae and Zamboni defined the maximal pattern complexity analogue for a Sturmian sequence over a two letter alphabet.

Definition 2.12
.

A
pattern Sturmian sequence over a two letter alphabet
is an aperiodic sequence
\alpha
for which
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Kamae and Rao introduced the singular decomposition of an infinite sequence in
[
KR06
]
. Below is a quick outline of this process, but a more in depth exploration can be found in
[
KR06
]
.

In
Definition
2.15
, the singular decomposition of a recurrent sequence is defined and in
Definition
2.17
, the singular decomposition of a non-recurrent sequence is defined. A singular decomposition requires a recurrent sequence, so for a non-recurrent sequence
\alpha
, we need to find some recurrent sequence
\beta
in the orbit closure of
\alpha
which satisfies some technical conditions outlined in
Definition
2.16
, then decompose
\alpha
with respect to the decomposition of
\beta
. The following definitions outline the singular decomposition of any sequence
\alpha
.

Definition 2.13
.

A sequence
\alpha
is
periodic by projection
if there exists some proper, non-empty subset of the alphabet,
S\subset\mathcal{A}
, such that the sequence
1_{S}(\alpha_{0})1_{S}(\alpha_{1})1_{S}(\alpha_{2})...\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is eventually periodic.

A letter
b\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is
singular
if the sequence
1_{\{b\}}(\alpha_{0})1_{\{b\}}(\alpha_{1})1_{\{b\}}(\alpha_{2})...\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is periodic.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\beta
be some recurrent sequence. We say
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the
decomposition cycle of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
if
m
is the smallest integer which is a period of
1_{\{b\}}(\alpha_{0})1_{\{b\}}(\alpha_{1})1_{\{b\}}(\alpha_{2})...
for each singular letter
b
of
\beta
. If
\beta
has no singular letters, then we set
m=1
.

Definition 2.15
.

The
singular decomposition of a recurrent sequence

\boldsymbol{\beta}
is the set of sequences

\{\beta^{(i)}=\beta_{i}\beta_{i+m}\beta_{i+2m}...:0\leq i<m\}

where
m
is the decomposition cycle of
\beta
.

Definition 2.16
.

Choose some
r\in\{0,1,...,m-1\}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sigma^{mk_{n}+r}(\alpha)
converges for some sequence
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\beta=\sigma^{m-r}\left(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sigma^{mk_{n}+r}(\alpha)\right)
.

\beta
is an
auxiliary word of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
if the following conditions are true:

1.

\beta
is recurrent

2.

\beta\in\overline{\mathcal{O}(\alpha)}

3.

\beta^{(i)}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{(i)})}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}\cap[0,m-1]

In
[
KR06
]
, Kamae and Rao proved that any aperiodic sequence has at least one auxiliary word.

Note that if
\beta
is an auxiliary word of
\alpha
, then
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq p_{\beta}^{*}(k)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
since for any
k
-sized window
\tau
,
F_{\beta}(\tau)\subseteq F_{\alpha}(\tau)
.

Definition 2.17
.

The
singular decomposition of a non-recurrent word
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
with respect to an auxiliary word
\boldsymbol{\beta}
is the set of sequences

\{\alpha^{(i)}=\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i+m}\alpha_{i+2m}...:0\leq i<m\}

for
m
the decomposition cycle of the auxiliary word
\beta
.

Definition 2.18
.

Define an undirected graph
\Gamma
on the residues of the singular decomposition of
\alpha
by
V(\Gamma)=\{0,1,2,...,m-1\}
, the residues of
\alpha
, and
E(\Gamma)=\{(i,j):\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}\neq\emptyset\}
.

Definition 2.19
.

For any infinite sequence
\alpha
,
k
-sized window
\tau
, and residue
i
,
0\leq i\leq m-1
for
m
the decomposition cycle of
\alpha
, we will define the
set of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
-words beginning on the
\boldsymbol{i}
th residue
as the set

C_{i}(\tau,\alpha):=\{\alpha[n+\tau]:n\equiv i\mod m\}

We note that
\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)=F_{\alpha}(\tau)
.

In
[
KR06
]
, Kamae and Rao also proved the following theorems about the maximal pattern complexity of sequences and found a threshold under which a non-trivial singular decomposition is forced.

Theorem 2.20
(
[
KR06
]
).

Let
\beta
be a recurrent sequence over
\ell
letters,
\ell\geq 2
. If
p_{\beta}^{*}(k)<\ell k
holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\beta
contains at least one singular letter.

Theorem 2.21
(
[
KR06
]
).

If
\ell\geq 2
and
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)<\ell k
holds for some

, then
\Gamma
is disconnected.

Definition 2.22
(
[
KR06
]
).

A sequence
\alpha
over
\ell\geq 2
letters is called a
pattern Sturmian sequence
if
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=\ell k
and
\alpha
is not periodic by projection.

We define the following slightly more restrictive condition:

Definition 2.23
.

A sequence
\alpha
over
\ell\geq 2
letters is called a
strong pattern Sturmian sequence
if
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2
.

Clearly, if
\ell=2
, the notions of pattern Sturmian and strong pattern Sturmian are the same.

In general, two sequences may have very different maximal windows.
However, when both sequences are uniformally recurrent, the following theorem shows we can find a window which gives at least
2k
words for each sequence. This is a shared maximal window when both sequences are pattern Sturmian. We prove this with an argument from the proof of
Theorem
2.9
, which can be found in
[
KZ02b
]
.

Theorem 2.24
.

For any two aperiodic sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
which are uniformly recurrent and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
k
-sized window
\tau
such that
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\geq 2k
and
\#F_{\beta}(\tau)\geq 2k
.

Proof:

Consider the product space
\overline{\mathcal{O}(\alpha)}\times\overline{\mathcal{O}(\beta)}
with the map
\sigma\times\sigma
.
\overline{\mathcal{O}(\alpha)}\times\overline{\mathcal{O}(\beta)}
has a recurrent point (by a simple application of the Poincaré Recurrence Theorem), call it
(\gamma,\eta)
.

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are uniformly recurrent,
\gamma
and
\eta
are both aperiodic.

Clearly,
\gamma
and
\eta
share a window of size one, and since they are both aperiodic, this window gives at least two words of length one for each sequence.

Now suppose for some
k>1
there exists a
(k-1)
-sized window
\tau
such that
F_{\gamma}(\tau)\geq 2(k-1)
and
F_{\eta}(\tau)\geq 2(k-1)
.

Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}...\gamma_{L-1}
and
\eta_{0}\eta_{1}...\eta_{L-1}
witness all
\tau
words over
\gamma
and
\eta
respectively. Since
(\gamma,\eta)
is recurrent, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
0\leq i<L
,
(\gamma,\eta)_{i}=(\gamma,\eta)_{n+i}
. Therefore, for all
0\leq i<L
,
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i+n}
and
\eta_{i}=\eta_{i+n}
.

Define
\tau^{\prime}
to be an immediate extension of
\tau
by
\tau^{\prime}(i)=\tau(i)
for all
0\leq i\leq k-2
and
\tau^{\prime}(k-1)=n
.

In the proof of
Theorem
2.9
from
[
KZ02b
]
, the following fact was proved: if
\delta
is aperiodic and there is some
\tau^{\prime}=\tau\cup\{n\}
for some
n
such that
awa\in\#F_{\delta}(\tau^{\prime})
for all

,
a
a single letter and
w
a
k-2
-sized
\tau\setminus\{0\}
word, then
\#F_{\delta}(\tau^{\prime})\geq\#F_{\delta}(\tau)+2
.

Thus, for
\delta\in\{\gamma,\eta\}
,
\#F_{\delta}(\tau^{\prime})\geq\#F_{\delta}(\tau)+2
, and by the inductive hypothesis,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\geq 2k
and
\#F_{\beta}(\tau^{\prime})\geq 2k
.

3
Recurrent Case

The purpose of this section is to prove
Theorem
1.1
in the case where
\alpha
is recurrent.

For the entirety of this section, we will assume
\alpha
is aperiodic, recurrent,
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
, and
\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty
(or in the case where
\alpha
is uniformly recurrent, that
\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-4k=-\infty
).

By
Theorem
2.20
, since
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)<3k\leq\ell k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha
has a singular letter. Let
m
be the decomposition cycle of
\alpha
,
m\geq 2
since the decomposition is non-trivial by the existence of a singular letter.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\alpha
is aperiodic, recurrent, and
\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty
, then exactly one residue in the singular decomposition of
\alpha
is aperiodic. In the case where
\alpha
is uniformly recurrent, the implication holds for
4k
instead of
3k
.

Proof:

Suppose
\alpha
is aperiodic, recurrent, and
\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty
. Let
m
be the decomposition cycle of
\alpha
,
m\geq 2
.

Since
\alpha
is aperiodic, there exists some
p
,
0\leq p\leq m-1
, such that
\alpha^{(p)}
is aperiodic. Let
k>m
and let
\tau
be a maximal
(k-(m-1))
-sized window over
\alpha^{(p)}
. Then
\#F_{\alpha^{(p)}}(\tau)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
by
Theorem
2.8
.

Define a
k
-sized window
\tau^{\prime}
over
\alpha
by
\tau^{\prime}=m\tau\cup\{h_{i}:1\leq i\leq m-1\}
for
h_{1}>m\tau(k-(m-1)-1)
,
h_{i+1}>h_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m-2
, and
h_{i}\equiv i\mod m
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
.

Define
x\in(\mathcal{A}\cup\{?\})^{\mathbb{N}}
by
x_{i}=\alpha_{i}
if
\alpha_{i}
is a singular letter and
x_{i}=\,?
if not. Then
x
has least period
m
since a smaller least period for
x
would result in the singular letters of
\alpha
sharing a period smaller than
m
.

Therefore,
C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\,\cap\,C_{j}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=\emptyset
for all
0\leq i<j\leq m-1
since the position of the singular letters uniquely determines to which set
C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
a word belongs. Furthermore,
\#C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
since every word in
F_{\alpha^{(p)}}(\tau)
must appear as a prefix for some word in
C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
.

Suppose towards a contradiction that there exists an
i
,
0\leq i\leq m-1
and
i\neq p
, such that
\alpha^{(i)}
is also aperiodic. Consider
\tau
over
\alpha^{(i)}
. Since
\alpha^{(i)}
is aperiodic, then by
Fact
2.10
,
\#F_{\alpha^{(i)}}(\tau)\geq k-(m-1)+1
. Thus,
\#C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq k-(m-1)+1
, and since
C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
and
C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
are disjoint,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\geq 3k-3(m-1)+1
. Therefore,

\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k\geq\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}3k-3(m-1)+1-3k\geq-3(m-1)+1.

Since
m
is fixed, this is a contradiction.

In the case where
\alpha
is uniformly recurrent, any aperiodic residue must also be uniformly recurrent. This is because the appearance of singular letters in any block of
\alpha
forces the points where the block begins again to fall on exactly the same residue as the original block. Thus, blocks in each residue must also appear syndetically, as they directly correspond to the uniform recurrence of the original sequence. Therefore, if
\alpha^{(p)}
and
\alpha^{(i)}
are both aperiodic, then they are both uniformly recurrent, and by
Theorem
2.24
, there exists a
(k-(m-1))
-sized window
\tau
such that
\#F_{\alpha^{(p)}}(\tau)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
and
\#F_{\alpha^{(i)}}(\tau)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
. Define a
k
-sized window
\tau^{\prime}
over
\alpha
by
\tau^{\prime}=m\tau\cup\{h_{i}:1\leq i\leq m-1\}
for
h_{1}>m\tau(k-(m-1)-1)
,
h_{i+1}>h_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m-2
, and
h_{i}\equiv i\mod m
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Then
C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\cap C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=\emptyset
,
\#C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
, and
\#C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
. Therefore,

\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-4k\geq\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}4k-4(m-1)-4k\geq-4(m-1),

which is a contradiction since
m
is fixed.

Thus, exactly one residue of the singular decomposition of
\alpha
is aperiodic.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\alpha
is aperiodic, recurrent, and
\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty
, then the aperiodic residue from
Theorem
3.1
must be over two letters and for all
c\in\mathcal{A}
if
c
is not a letter in the aperiodic residue,
c
is singular for
\alpha
.

Proof:

Let
\alpha^{(p)}
be the aperiodic residue from
Theorem
3.1
,
0\leq p\leq m-1
. Suppose towards a contradiction that
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}\geq 3
. Since for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k
-sized window
\tau
over
\alpha^{(p)}
,
F_{\alpha^{(p)}}(\tau)\subset F_{\alpha}(m\tau)
, then
p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)\leq p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)<3k
. Thus by
Theorem
2.20
,

\alpha^{(p)}
must have a singular letter, call it
c
. Since all other residues are periodic and
c
appears in
\alpha^{(p)}
periodically,
c
must appear in
\alpha
periodically, and so
c
is singular for
\alpha
and thus cannot appear in
\alpha^{(p)}
, a contradiction. Thus
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}=2
.

Suppose
c\in\mathcal{A}
,
c\notin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}
. Then
c
only appears in residues which are periodic. Thus
c
appears periodically in
\alpha
, so
c
is singular for
\alpha
.

Clearly,
Theorem
3.1
and
Proposition
3.2
together prove
Theorem
1.1
in the case when
\alpha
is recurrent.

4
Non-Recurrent Case

This section is dedicated to proving
Theorem
1.1
in the non-recurrent case. For the entirety of this section, we assume that
\alpha
is a non-recurrent, aperiodic sequence,
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
, and
\liminf\limits\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty
.

Let
\beta
be an auxiliary word for
\alpha
. By
Theorem
2.21
, since
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)<3k\leq\ell k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Gamma
is disconnected. Let

be the decomposition cycle of the singular decomposition of
\alpha
with respect to

since the decomposition is non-trivial.

For each eventually periodic
\alpha^{(i)}
there exists an

such that
\sigma^{n_{i}}(\alpha^{(i)})
is periodic. For each aperiodic
\alpha^{(i)}
there exists an

appears in
\sigma^{n_{i}}(\alpha^{(i)})
infinitely often. Let

. Then for the sequence
\alpha^{\prime}=\sigma^{mt}(\alpha)
, all residues of
\alpha^{\prime}
are aperiodic or periodic, every letter appearing in a residue of
\alpha^{\prime}
appears in that residue infinitely often,
\alpha=w\alpha^{\prime}
for some finite word
w
, and
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=p_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}(k)+C^{\prime}
for some constant
C^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k
sufficiently large (since for any window, a finite prefix can only contribute a finite number of words). We further note that
\beta
is an auxiliary word for
\alpha^{\prime}
and the singular decomposition of
\alpha^{\prime}
is
\{\alpha^{\prime(i)}=\sigma^{t}(\alpha^{(i)}):0\leq i\leq m-1\}
. The graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of the residues of
\alpha^{\prime}
is such that

and

, and so

is also disconnected.

Lemma 4.1
.

Exactly one connected component of
\Gamma^{\prime}
contains aperiodic residues and all other connected components of
\Gamma^{\prime}
contain only singular residues.

Proof:

Since
\alpha^{\prime}
is aperiodic, at least one connected component of
\Gamma^{\prime}
contains residues which are aperiodic.

Suppose towards a contradiction that there are two disjoint connected components of
\Gamma^{\prime}
which contain aperiodic residues. Then there are two residues,
i\neq j
for which
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
and
\alpha^{\prime(j)}
are both aperiodic on disjoint alphabets. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\tau
be a maximal
k
-sized window over
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
. Then by
Theorem
2.8
,
\#F_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}(\tau)\geq 2k
. Furthermore,
F_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}(\tau)\cap F_{\alpha^{\prime(j)}}(\tau)=\emptyset
since the alphabets of
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
and
\alpha^{\prime(j)}
are disjoint, and by
Fact
2.10
,
\#F_{\alpha^{\prime(j)}}(\tau)\geq k+1
since
\alpha^{\prime(j)}
is aperiodic. Then
\#F_{\alpha^{\prime}}(m\tau)\geq 2k+k+1
, a contradiction.

Let
A\subset\{0,1,...,m-1\}
be the set of residues in the aperiodic connected component and
S=\{0,1,...,m-1\}\setminus A
be all other residues.

Let
i\in S
. Then
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is periodic, and so
\beta^{(i)}
is also periodic. Since the letters appearing in
\beta^{(i)}
only appear in residues from

, those letters must appear periodically in
\beta
and thus are singular for
\beta
. Therefore, since the letters are singular for
\beta
and periodic in

, they must be singular for
\alpha^{\prime}
, and for all

,
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is constant.

For the remainder of this section let
A
be the set of residues in the aperiodic connected component and
S
be the collection of all other residues.

Lemma 4.2
.

If more than two letters appear in a single aperiodic residue,
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
, then one of the letters must appear periodically within
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
.

Proof:

Suppose
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is an aperiodic residue for
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}\geq 3
.
Then
p_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}^{*}(k)\leq p_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}(k)<3k
for sufficiently large

.

If
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is recurrent, then by
Theorem
2.20
, it must have some singular letter, and by definition, this letter is periodic in
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
.

If
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is not recurrent, then by
Theorem
2.21
, the graph
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
of
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is disconnected, and by
Lemma
4.1
, only one connected component has aperiodic pieces and all other pieces are singular for

. Thus there is at least one letter which appears periodically in
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
c\in\mathcal{A}_{A}:=\bigcup_{i\in A}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}
, then there exists some residue
j
,
0\leq j\leq m-1
such that
c
appears aperiodically in
\alpha^{\prime(j)}
.

Proof:

Let
c\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
. If
c
appears periodically in each
\alpha^{\prime(j)}
,

then
c
would appear periodically in each

. Thus
c
would be singular for
\beta
, and since
c
is periodic in every residue of
\alpha^{\prime}
,
c
would be singular for
\alpha^{\prime}
, a contradiction.

Theorem 4.4
.

The alphabet of the aperiodic residues,
\mathcal{A}_{A}=\bigcup_{i\in A}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}
, has exactly two letters.

Proof:

Clearly
\#\mathcal{A}_{A}\geq 2
or else no residue from

would be aperiodic. Thus, suppose towards a contradiction that

.

Since
\beta
is contained in the orbit closure of
\alpha^{\prime}
,

. Thus, since
\beta
is recurrent,

either has the form outlined in the conclusion of

1.1
by Section 3, or

Let
a,b,
and

be distinct letters in

and let

.

Case I:
Suppose
\beta
has the form outlined in the conclusion of
Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose
\beta^{(p)}
,

, is the only aperiodic residue and is over the letters
\{a,b\}
, and all other residues of

Let
\tau
be a maximal
k
-sized window over
\alpha^{\prime(p)}
. Since

is in the orbit closure of
\alpha^{\prime}
,

, and since

2k

\tau

By

, since

,

, such that

If

Case II:
Suppose
\beta
is periodic.

Since every letter of

\alpha^{\prime}
and

, by

, there must be at least two residues of

which are aperiodic,
\alpha^{\prime(p)}

\alpha^{\prime(i)}

is singular for
\beta
. Then, without loss of generality, either

Let

Case IIa:
Suppose that

\tau

in

must appear in some residue of
\alpha^{\prime}

If there exists some

in in

.

There exists some decomposition of

into

Let

-sized window over

over
\alpha^{\prime}
by
\tau^{\prime}=m\tau\cup\{h_{i}:1\leq i\leq m-1\}

,

5
Strong Pattern Sturmian Sequences

In this section we will prove
Theorem
1.2
and
Theorem
1.3
. First,
Lemma
5.1
and its immediate corollary introduce the relationship between the maximal pattern complexity of a sequence and the maximal pattern complexity of its aperiodic residue, which is unique by
Theorem

.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)-3k=-\infty
and
\alpha^{(p)}
is the aperiodic residue from
Theorem

, then
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)+\ell-2
.

Proof:

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\tau
be a maximal
k
-sized window over
\alpha^{(p)}
. Consider the window
m\tau
over
\alpha
. Then
\#F_{\alpha}(m\tau)\geq\#F_{\alpha^{(p)}}(\tau)+\ell-2
since there is at least one word beginning with each letter of the alphabet. Thus
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq\#F_{\alpha}(m\tau)\geq p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)+\ell-2
.

An immediate corollary of
Lemma
5.1
is the following:

Corollary 5.2
.

If
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+C
for some
C\geq\ell-2
and
\alpha^{(p)}
is the only aperiodic residue,
0\leq p\leq m-1
, then
p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)\leq 2k+D
for some

and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma
5.3
sets the foundation for proving
Theorem
1.2
by showing that a nontrivial prefix of a non-recurrent sequence, as discussed in the beginning of Section 4, cannot occur when the maximal pattern complexity is restricted to
2k+\ell-2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2
for all

, then the aperiodic residue from

is over two letters,

and

, and for any

which is not equal to

,

is singular.

Proof:

If
\alpha
is recurrent, this is clearly true by
Theorem
3.1
and
Proposition
3.2
. Therefore, we will assume that
\alpha
is not recurrent. By the discussion at the beginning of Section 4, there is a word
w
such that
\alpha=w\alpha^{\prime}
, all residues of
\alpha^{\prime}
are aperiodic or periodic, and since
p_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}(k)\leq p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)
, by
Theorem
4.5
,
\alpha^{\prime}
is a sequence with exactly one aperiodic residue over two letters,
a
and
b
, and for any
c\in\mathcal{A}
,
c\neq a,b
,
c
is singular. Thus it suffices to show that
w
is trivial.

Assume, towards a contradiction, that
w
is non-trivial.

Define
x\in(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cup\{?\})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by
x_{i}=?
if
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}\subseteq\{a,b\}
and
x_{i}=\alpha^{\prime}_{i}
if
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}\not\subseteq\{a,b\}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
x
has least period
m
, or else
\alpha
could be decomposed with respect to
m_{0}<m
, and
\alpha=w^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}
where
w
is still non-trivial and
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
has the same structure as
\alpha^{\prime}
.

Let
\alpha^{\prime(p)}
,
0\leq p\leq m-1
, be the only aperiodic residue of
\alpha^{\prime}
.

Since
w
is non-trivial, there is some residue
i
such that either there exists some
c\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}
such that
c\notin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}\neq\{a,b\}
or
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}=\{a,b\}
,
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is periodic (potentially constant), and for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma^{t}(\alpha^{(i)})\neq\alpha^{(i)}
.

Consider the first case, where there exists some
c\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}
such that
c\notin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}\neq\{a,b\}
.

Let
k\geq 2
and let
\tau
be a maximal
k
-sized window over
\alpha^{\prime(p)}
. Then
F_{\alpha^{\prime}}(m\tau)=F_{\alpha^{\prime(p)}}(\tau)\cup\{d^{k}:d\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\text{ and }d\neq a,b\}
and so
\#F_{\alpha^{\prime}}(m\tau)\geq 2k+\ell-2
. We also note that
F_{\alpha^{\prime(p)}}(\tau)
only contains words over the letters
a
and
b
.

However, since there is some residue
i
for which there exists some
c\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}
such that
c\notin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{\prime(i)}}
, then either the
m\tau
-word
cd^{k-1}
for
c\notin\{a,b\}
,
cd^{k-1}
for
d\notin\{a,b\}
, or
cu
for
c\notin\{a,b\}
and
u\in F_{\alpha^{\prime(p)}}(\tau\setminus\{\tau(k-1)\})
, must also appear in
F_{\alpha}(m\tau)
, and so
\#F_{\alpha}(m\tau)\geq 2k+\ell-1
, a contradiction.

Now consider the second case, where
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i)}}=\{a,b\}
,
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
is periodic (potentially constant), and for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma^{t}(\alpha^{(i)})\neq\alpha^{(i)}
. We will further assume that the first case cannot occur, so constant residues in
\alpha^{\prime}
over letters other than
a
and
b
must also be constant in
\alpha
.

Let
q
be the least period of
\alpha^{\prime(i)}
. Since
m
is the least period of
x
, there exists some
j
,
0\leq j\leq m-1
, such that
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(i+j\mod m)}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p+j\mod m)}}=\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
p+j\not\equiv i\mod m
(or else use the distance
-j\mod m
instead).

Define a
q
-sized window
\tau
by
\tau=\{0,m,2m,...,(q-1)m\}
. Define an immediate extension
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau
by
\tau^{\prime}=\tau\cup\{h\}
for some
h>(q-1)m
and
h\equiv j\mod m
. Then
C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\cap C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
\{\sigma^{t}(\alpha^{\prime(i)})_{0}\sigma^{t}(\alpha^{\prime(i)})_{1}...\sigma^{t}(\alpha^{\prime(i)})_{q-1}:0\leq t\leq q-1\}\cup\{\alpha^{(i)}_{0}\alpha^{(i)}_{1}...\alpha^{(i)}_{q-1}\}\subset C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)
, so
\#C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\geq q+1
.

If
i+j\equiv p\mod m
then
\#C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq q+2
since at least one word from
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)
must have at least two extensions in
C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
or else
\alpha^{(p)}
would be eventually periodic. Furthermore,
\#C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq q+1
by
Fact
2.10
since
\alpha^{(p)}
is aperiodic. Since there are at least
\ell-2
residues which are constant over letters not equal to
a
or
b
, there are at least
\ell-2
more
\tau^{\prime}
-words. Thus,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\geq 2q+2+\ell-1
, a contradiction.

If
i+j\not\equiv p\mod m
, then
\#C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq q+1
,
\#C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq q+1
by
Fact
2.10
. Since
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\cap C_{p}(\tau,\alpha)=\emptyset
, either
\alpha^{(p-j\mod m)}=c^{\infty}
or
\alpha^{(i-j\mod m)}=c^{\infty}
for some
c\notin\{a,b\}
. Thus, either
\#C_{i-j\mod m}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 2
or
\#C_{p-j\mod m}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 2
. Then there are at least
\ell-3

\tau^{\prime}
-words over the residues which are constant over letters not equal to
a
,
b
, or
c
. Thus,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\geq 2q+2+\ell-1
, a contradiction.

Thus,
w
must be trivial.

We will now prove
Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of​

Theorem
1.2
:
Suppose that
\alpha
is an aperiodic sequence such that
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
and
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

By
Lemma
5.3
, exactly one residue is aperiodic over a two letter alphabet
\{a,b\}
. Let
\alpha^{(p)}
,
0\leq p\leq m-1
, be the aperiodic residue. Also by
Lemma
5.3
, for all
c\in\mathcal{A}
such that
c\notin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}
,
c
is singular for
\alpha
.

By
Lemma
5.1
, since
2k+\ell-2=p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)+\ell-2
, then
p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)\leq 2k
. Since
\alpha^{(p)}
is aperiodic, by
Theorem
2.8
,
p_{\alpha^{(p)}}^{*}(k)\geq 2k
, and so the aperiodic residue must have maximal pattern complexity
2k
, and is a pattern Sturmian over two letters.

Since each letter not witnessed by
\alpha^{(p)}
is singular, any residue on letters other than those in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}
are constant. Thus it suffices to show that there is no residue
\alpha^{(j)}
,
j\neq p
, which is nonconstant periodic over
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}
.

Suppose that there is some residue
j
,
0\leq j\leq m-1
and
j\neq p
, such that
\alpha^{(j)}
is nonconstant periodic over
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha^{(p)}}=\{a,b\}
. Let
k>m
,
\tau
be a maximal
(k-(m-1))
-sized window over
\alpha^{(p)}
, and define a
k
-sized window
\tau^{\prime}
over
\alpha
by
\tau^{\prime}=m\tau\cup\{h_{i}:1\leq i\leq m-1\}
for
h_{1}>m\tau(k-(m-1)-1)
,
h_{i+1}>h_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m-2
, and
h_{i}\equiv i\mod m
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Then
\#C_{p}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 2k-2(m-1)
. Since
\alpha^{(j)}
is nonconstant and
\alpha^{(p)}
is aperiodic, in every
C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
there are at least three distinct words classified by the letters appearing in the parts of the window centered over
\alpha^{(p)}
and
\alpha^{(j)}
: two distinct words with an
a
or
b
in the
\alpha^{(j)}
slot, and a third word since if the letter appearing in the
\alpha^{(j)}
slot always forced the letter appearing in the
\alpha^{(p)}
slot, then
\alpha^{(p)}
would be periodic (either constant, equal to
\alpha^{(j)}
, or the bit flip of
\alpha^{(j)}
). Therefore,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\geq 2k-2(m-1)+3(m-1)=2k+m-1
. Furthermore,
m\geq\ell
since
\alpha^{(p)}
and
\alpha^{(j)}
only use the letters
a
and
b
, and there are at least
\ell-2
other constant residues over the letters not equal to
a
or
b
. Therefore,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\geq 2k+\ell-1
, a contradiction.

The next part of this section is dedicated to proving
Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of​

Theorem
1.3
:
Suppose
\alpha
is aperiodic,
\#\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\ell\geq 3
, and there exists some
m\geq 2
such that the decomposition of
\alpha
with respect to
m
has one residue which is a pattern Sturmian over two letters and all other residues are constant.

Let
\alpha^{(p)}
,
0\leq p\leq m-1
, be the aperiodic pattern Sturmian over two letters.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\tau
be any
k
-sized window. Define
\tau_{i}=\{\tau(j):j\equiv i\mod m\}
for all
0\leq i\leq m-1
. Then
\tau=\tau_{0}\sqcup\tau_{1}\sqcup...\sqcup\tau_{m-1}
.

Suppose that
I=\{i:0\leq i\leq m-1\text{ and }\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset\}
.

Since any word in
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)
is completely determined by the letters of
\alpha^{(p)}
which appear in it,
\#C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\leq 2(\#\tau_{p-i\mod m})
.

Furthermore,
\#\left(\bigcup_{i\notin I}C_{p-i\mod m}(\tau,\alpha)\right)\leq m-1-\#I
since for all
i\notin I
,
C_{p-i\mod m}(\tau,\alpha)
is a set of words which only see constant sequences, so each
C_{p-i\mod m}(\tau,\alpha)
can contribute at most one word.

Therefore, for all
k
-sized windows
\tau
,

\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\leq\sum_{i\in I}2(\#\tau_{i})+\#\left(\bigcup_{i\notin I}C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\right)\leq 2k+m-1-\#I\leq 2k+m-2.

Thus
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\leq 2k+m-2
. By an application of
Theorem
2.9
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k+1)\geq p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)+2
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and so for sufficiently large
k
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+C
for some
\ell-2\leq C\leq m-2
.

6
Examples

This section introduces some relevant examples.
Example
6.1
demonstrates the existence of strong pattern Sturmian sequences over any finite alphabet.

Example 6.1
.

We will define a sequence
\alpha\in\{0,1,...,\ell-1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
which has maximal pattern complexity
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2
.

Let
x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a pattern Sturmian sequence over two letters. Define
\alpha\in\{0,1,...,\ell-1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by

\alpha_{(\ell-1)i+j}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if }j\equiv 0\mod\ell-1\\
j+1&\text{if }j\not\equiv 0\mod\ell-1.\end{cases}

The singular decomposition cycle of
\alpha
is
\ell-1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\tau
be any
k
-sized window. Define
\tau_{i}=\{\tau(j):j\equiv i\mod m\}
for all
0\leq i\leq m-1
. Then
\tau=\tau_{0}\sqcup\tau_{1}\sqcup...\sqcup\tau_{m-1}
.

Suppose that
I=\{i:0\leq i\leq\ell-2\text{ and }\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset\}
.

Since any two residues have disjoint alphabets, for all
0\leq i<j\leq\ell-2
,
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\cap C_{j}(\tau,\alpha)=\emptyset
.

Furthermore,
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\leq\max\{2(\#\tau_{\ell-1-i}),1\}
, and so
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}\#C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\leq 2k+(\ell-1-\#I)\leq 2k+\ell-2
since
\#I\geq 1
.

Since this is true for any
k
-sized window
\tau
, we have that
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\leq 2k+\ell-2
and since by
Theorem
2.11
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\geq 2k+\ell-2
, for this sequence,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+\ell-2
.

Example
6.2
is a sequence which has the structure outlined in
Theorem
1.3
but has maximal pattern complexity strictly greater than
2k+\ell-2
demonstrating that the converse of
Theorem
1.2
is not true.

Example 6.2
.

We will define a sequence
\alpha\in\{0,1,2,3\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
which is a pattern Sturmian sequence zippered with constant sequences, but has maximal pattern complexity
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=2k+C
for
C>\ell-2
.

Let
x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a pattern Sturmian over two letters. Define
\alpha
by

\alpha_{5i+j}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if }j\equiv 0\mod 5\\
2&\text{if }j\equiv 1,4\mod 5\\
3&\text{if }j\equiv 2,3\mod 5.\end{cases}

Let
\tau
be a maximal window for
x
of size
k-1
. Define
\tau^{\prime}
by
\tau^{\prime}(i)=m\tau(i)
for all
0\leq i\leq k-2
and
\tau^{\prime}(k-1)=h
for some
h\equiv 1\mod 5
and
h>m\tau(k-2)
.

Then
\#C_{0}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=2(k-1)
since
\alpha^{(0)}
is a pattern Sturmian sequence over two letters and
\tau
is a maximal window for
\alpha^{(0)}
. Furthermore,
\#C_{4}(\tau,\alpha)=2
since
C_{4}(\tau,\alpha)=\{2^{k-1}0,2^{k-1}1\}
, and
\#C_{1}(\tau,\alpha)=\#C_{2}(\tau,\alpha)=\#C_{3}(\tau,\alpha)=1
since
C_{1}(\tau,\alpha)=\{2^{k-1}3\}
,
C_{2}(\tau,\alpha)=\{3^{k}\}
, and
C_{3}(\tau,\alpha)=\{3^{k-1}2\}
. Clearly
\{C_{i}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha):0\leq i\leq 4\}
are pairwise disjoint. Thus,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})=2k+3>2k+(4-2)=2k+\ell-2
for
\ell=4
.

Example
6.3
is a uniformly recurrent sequence with two aperiodic residues which has complexity
4k
, showing that the bound in
Theorem
1.1
cannot be substantially improved below
4k
.

Example 6.3
.

We will create a sequence which has complexity
4k
and is two recurrent pattern Sturmian sequences zippered together with a constant sequence.

Let
x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the Fibonacci substitution defined by
0\to 01
and
1\to 0
, a Sturmian and therefore pattern Sturmian sequence, which is also uniformly recurrent.

Define
\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by

\alpha_{3i+j}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if }j\equiv 0,1\mod 3\\
2&\text{if }j\equiv 2\mod 3.\end{cases}

First, we must show that
\alpha
is uniformly recurrent. Let
L>0
and let
R_{L}(\alpha)=\{M\in\mathbb{N}:\alpha_{0}...\alpha_{L-1}=\alpha_{M}...\alpha_{M+L-1}\}
. Let
L^{\prime}=\lceil\frac{L}{3}\rceil
and define
R_{L}^{\prime}(x)=\{M\in\mathbb{N}:x_{0}...x_{L^{\prime}-1}=x_{M}...x_{M+L^{\prime}-1}\}
. Since
x
is uniformly recurrent,
R_{L^{\prime}}(x)=\{M_{1},M_{2},...\}
is syndetic and for any
M_{i}
,
x_{0}x_{1}...x_{L^{\prime}-1}=x_{M_{i}}x_{M_{i}+1}...x_{M_{i}+L^{\prime}-1}
. Thus,
x_{0}x_{0}2x_{1}x_{1}2...x_{L^{\prime}-1}x_{L^{\prime}-1}2=x_{M_{i}}x_{M_{i}}2x_{M_{i}+1}x_{M_{i}+1}2...x_{M_{i}+L^{\prime}-1}x_{M_{i}+L^{\prime}-1}2
and so
R_{L}(\alpha)=\{3M_{1},3M_{2},...\}
is syndetic and thus
\alpha
is a uniformly recurrent sequence.

Now we will show that
\alpha
has maximal pattern complexity
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=4k
for all
k\geq 2
.

First, we show that for any
k
-sized window
\tau
over
\alpha
,
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\leq 4k
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\tau
be any
k
-sized window over
\alpha
. If for all
0\leq i\leq k-1
,
\tau(i)\equiv 0\mod 3
(ie
\tau
only sees one residue at a time), then
C_{0}(\tau,\alpha)=C_{1}(\tau,\alpha)
since
\alpha^{(0)}=\alpha^{(1)}=x
, and
\#C_{0}(\tau,\alpha)\leq 2k
since
x
is a pattern Sturmian sequence. Furthermore,
C_{2}(\tau,\alpha)=\{2^{k}\}
. Thus,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\leq 2k+1
.

Suppose now that
\tau=\tau_{1}\sqcup\tau_{2}\sqcup\tau_{3}
for
\tau_{i}=\{\tau(j):\tau(j)\equiv i\mod 3\}
and there exists
i\neq j
such that
\#\tau_{i},\#\tau_{j}>0
. Then, for all
0\leq i<j\leq 2
,
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\cap C_{j}(\tau,\alpha)=\emptyset
since the position of the
2
s uniquely determines to which set
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)
a
\tau
-word belongs.

Then
\#C_{0}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)\leq 2(\#\tau_{0})+2(\#\tau_{1})
,
\#C_{1}\leq 2(\#\tau_{0})+2(\#\tau_{2})
and
\#C_{2}\leq 2(\#\tau_{1})+2(\#\tau_{2})
. Therefore

\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\leq 2(\#\tau_{0})+2(\#\tau_{1})+2(\#\tau_{0})+2(\#\tau_{2})+2(\#\tau_{1})+2(\#\tau_{2})=4k

Thus for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any
k
-sized window
\tau
,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau)\leq 4k
, and so
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)\leq 4k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we will show that for all
k\geq 2
there exists some window
\tau^{\prime}
such that
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})=4k
.

Let
\tau
be a maximal
k
-sized window for
x
such that the immediate restriction of
\tau
is a maximal
(k-1)
-sized window. Define
\tau^{\prime}
by
\tau^{\prime}(i)=3\tau(i)
for all
0\leq i\leq k-2
and
\tau^{\prime}(k-1)=3\tau(k-1)+1
.

For all
0\leq i<j\leq 2
,
C_{i}(\tau,\alpha)\cap C_{j}(\tau,\alpha)=\emptyset
since words in
C_{0}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)
are of the form
w_{0}
for
w_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{k}
, words in
C_{1}
are of the form
w_{1}2
for
w_{1}\in\{0,1\}^{k-1}
, and words in
C_{2}
are of the form
2^{k-1}w_{3}
for
w_{3}\in\{0,1\}
.

Then
\#C_{0}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=2k
since
C_{0}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=F_{x}(\tau)
,
\#C_{1}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=2k-2
since
C_{1}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=\{w_{1}2:w_{1}\in F_{x}(\tau\setminus\{\tau(k-1)\})\}
, and
\#C_{2}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=2
since
C_{2}(\tau^{\prime},\alpha)=\{2^{k-1}0,2^{k-1}1\}
.

Thus,
\#F_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})=4k
and so
p_{\alpha}^{*}(k)=4k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

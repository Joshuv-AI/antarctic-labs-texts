Optimize a chain convenience store location prediction model by using MTS-machine learning methodology
Authors: Tsung-Yin Ou, Hsin-Pin Fu, Mei-Zhen Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the past, convenience store location selection required substantial time, cost and effort for field research, and the location selection decision was often limited by the subjective experience of the decision-maker. The rapid development of machine learning (ML) in recent years has enabled many studies of model training. This study introduces a novel hybrid approach integrating Mahalanobis–Taguchi System (MTS) for variable reduction with ML algorithms to address the challenges of large-scale, multi-variable retail location selection. The proposed model reduces computational requirements and supports data-driven decision-making for convenience store chains. To achieve this, a hybrid approach combining the Mahalanobis–Taguchi System (MTS) and machine learning algorithms—XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), and support vector machine (SVM)—were employed to develop a location prediction model for chain convenience stores. Using real-world location data from Taiwanese convenience stores, MTS analysis mainly performed features selection, reducing the number of variables from nine to five. The research results show that the prediction ability of the MTS-XGBoost algorithm can be accurately maintained at more than 75% under different training set proportions, and it has more accurate prediction results than MTS-RF and MTS-SVM. This approach enables more efficient and accurate store location decisions, potentially transforming strategic planning in the retail sector.Keywords: Convenience store, Location selection, Mahalanobis–Taguchi system (MTS), XGBoost, Random forest (RF), SVM, Features selectionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

From 2019 to 2023, the number of convenience stores in Taiwan grew at annual rates of 3.4% to 5.5%. By the end of 2023, Taiwan had 13,706 convenience stores, an average of one for every 1,703 people. Taiwan’s density of convenience stores is second only to South Korea’s1. Ever-changing market conditions and fierce competition in the retail industry mean wise decision-making about opening new stores is crucial. Failure to occupy good business locations not only wastes capital costs but seriously impedes expansion plans. A poor location has both strategic and long-term effects, reducing the facility’s profitability and operability for an extended period of time given the conditions of the market, environment and population change2.

Convenience store location selection is complex because it is affected by many factors, such as demographic and socioeconomic characteristics, availability and cost of space, business ecosystems, competition, customer behavior and preferences, legal and fiscal factors, accessibility, availability of supplies, and climate and environmental factors3. Previous studies of differentiation in the competitive market structure of physical retail formats mostly focused on relative location and spatial competition4, finding that an accessible location and large store size are associated with decreased likelihood of store closure. Even in an increasingly digital world in which online shopping is burgeoning, location is crucial to the survival of physical stores5.

The convenience store site selection process requires substantial time, cost, and effort to conduct research and collect relevant data for evaluation. The rapid development of machine learning (ML) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies and algorithms in recent years has boosted research on location prediction6–8. However, ML methods require substantial data, in turn increasing cost and the computing power required. In practical applications, large amounts of data and many variables may create noise and decrease accuracy. Zouhri et al.9 assessed the effectiveness of dimensionality reduction on the interpretability of machine learning-based detection systems which achieved 99.99% accuracy with a reduction from 80 to 6 features. In this study, a model designed to predict the success of new convenience stores was established by reducing variables and applying ML. Convenience store location selection often involves various interacting nonlinear variables. Recent studies have highlighted the robustness of the Mahalanobis–Taguchi System (MTS) in addressing complex, nonlinear problems. Specifically, MTS has been shown to perform effectively in nonlinear multiclass classification10, high-dimensional small-sample analysis11, optimized feature selection12, the identification and screening of critical variables13, and abnormal observation analysis14. A comprehensive review further confirms its broad applicability across diverse contexts15. Building on this foundation, our study employs MTS as a method for variable screening and deletion, leveraging its strength in handling multiple nonlinear variables while preserving interpretability.

Case data were validated using XGBoost16, Random Forest (RF)17, and Support Vector Machine (SVM)18–20, which are well suited for retail location prediction due to their complementary strengths. XGBoost leverages gradient boosting with regularization and parallelization to deliver efficient, high-accuracy predictions, while RF reduces variance through ensemble learning and provides interpretable feature importance measures useful for managerial decisions. SVM, by maximizing classification margins and employing kernel functions, effectively captures nonlinear patterns and performs robustly with moderate sample sizes. Collectively, these algorithms are recognized for their ability to model complex, high-dimensional data with resistance to overfitting, strong interpretability, and computational efficiency, ensuring both reliable predictive accuracy and actionable insights for location decision-making.

This study makes twofold contributions. Practically, it equips convenience store operators with a systematic, data-driven tool that reduces reliance on subjective judgment, lowers evaluation costs and time, and enhances prediction accuracy by identifying key factors such as visibility, competitiveness, and residential density. Academically, it advances retail location analytics by integrating the Mahalanobis–Taguchi System with XGBoost, a hybrid approach that addresses noise and dimensionality challenges in real-world datasets while preserving accuracy and interpretability. Using real convenience store data, the study verifies the model’s effectiveness for nonlinear, multi-variable decision-making, thereby extending the applicability of MTS to a novel retail context.

Literature review

Convenience store location selection

Technologies for location selection analysis have existed for more than 50 years, yet most traditional retailers have historically relied on intuition, experience, and “common sense” rather than systematic models21. While such heuristic approaches are practical and low-cost, they lack rigor and are prone to subjective bias, resulting in inconsistent or suboptimal site decisions. With the advancement of analytical methods, researchers began adopting quantitative models. For instance, Lin et al.22 applied a spatial model to examine agglomeration among convenience store chains, analyzing how inter-store distance affects revenue. Although spatial models effectively capture geographic competition, they typically oversimplify complex consumer behaviors and external market dynamics. Similarly, Lienbacher et al.23 measured the distances between customers’ residential addresses, suppliers, and supermarkets, and found strong associations with supermarket location decisions. While distance-based models highlight the importance of accessibility, they neglect nonlinear interactions between demographic, economic, and competitive variables that often drive retail performance. Some scholars extended the scope of location analysis to resource-constrained environments. For example, Song et al.24 applied integer programming to optimize the placement of electric vehicle charging stations, while25 used data envelopment analysis to improve logistics center siting. These optimization-based methods are rigorous and computationally efficient; however, their applicability to convenience store site selection is limited because they treat location as a purely operational problem, without incorporating consumer demand heterogeneity or retail-specific competitive pressures.

Recent studies have integrated big data and data mining techniques to enhance retail location decisions. Formánek and Sokol26 proposed a methodology that combines linear mixed-effects models with cluster analysis to assess and plan store locations. While such hybrid statistical approaches improve segmentation and pattern detection, they often remain sensitive to noise and require large, clean datasets that are rarely available in practice. Other studies explored machine learning techniques such as neural networks, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), and random forests, which demonstrated strong performance on error metrics like RMSE and MAPE27. Nevertheless, these models tend to operate as “black boxes,” providing high accuracy but limited interpretability, which constrains their usefulness for managerial decision-making. Further, Brei et al.28 introduced a novel approach leveraging satellite imagery and ML to predict retail performance. Although innovative, image-based methods require extensive data preprocessing. Finally, some authors have argued for broader adoption of artificial intelligence in retail site selection29,30. While these studies underscore the potential of AI, they often present conceptual arguments or exploratory applications without addressing the challenges of data redundancy, variable noise, and the balance between predictive accuracy and managerial interpretability.

Prior research demonstrates diverse methodological advances but also reveals important limitations: spatial and optimization models often oversimplify consumer–market dynamics, while ML and AI techniques, despite their predictive power, struggle with noise reduction, interpretability, and transferability across contexts. These shortcomings highlight the need for approaches that are both computationally efficient and practically interpretable.

The Mahalanobis–Taguchi system

The MTS is a multivariate diagnostic prediction method that combines quality engineering, Mahalanobis distance (MD), signal-to-noise (SN) ratio, and orthogonal array (OA) methods. The main purpose of MTS is to identify and screen variables that contribute to abnormality, thereby enhancing the accuracy of classification and diagnosis31. Compared with traditional dimensionality reduction methods, MTS does not rely on distributional assumptions, making it suitable for diverse and complex datasets. However, its sensitivity to abnormal data and reliance on carefully constructed reference groups remain potential limitations.

Several studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of MTS in different domains. For instance, Zhang et al.32 highlighted its role in simplifying analysis and improving efficiency in site selection tasks. While the study illustrates MTS’s ability to streamline decision-making, it offers limited discussion of how the method performs under conditions of noisy, high-dimensional retail data. Similarly, Sikder et al.33 proposed a synergistic MTS-based framework for process quality control in manufacturing. This work confirmed MTS’s robustness in industrial applications, yet its focus on controlled environments limits generalizability to service industries such as retail. Wang et al.34 demonstrated that MTS, as a big data classification and reduction method, could support fault diagnosis and maintenance modeling without assuming data distributions. This highlights MTS’s versatility, though the study overlooked challenges of interpretability in managerial contexts. Similarly, Wang et al.35 showed that optimized MTS improves classification for imbalanced datasets, Mao et al.36 applied MTS to hypertension prediction, and Dashdondov and Kim37 applied MTS to evaluate EV battery consistency with strong results. While these works confirm MTS’s adaptability to medical and imbalance problems, their findings are highly domain-specific and less transferable to consumer-oriented sectors.

In summary, while MTS has proven effective in fields such as manufacturing and healthcare, prior applications are largely limited to controlled settings and lack integration with modern machine learning. This study addresses these gaps by combining MTS with advanced classifiers to deliver interpretable and practical insights for convenience store location decisions.

Research positioning and expected contributions

Over the past decades, research on retail location selection has evolved from classical spatial and optimization approaches to more advanced data-driven methods. Traditional studies often employed spatial competition models to capture agglomeration and accessibility3,26 or relied on optimization techniques such as integer programming and data envelopment analysis24,25. While insightful, these methods struggle with nonlinear interactions among multiple variables, a hallmark of modern retail contexts. More recently, machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) have expanded the methodological toolkit. For example, Lu et al.27 applied ML algorithms for retail store screening, and28 used satellite imagery with ML to predict performance. Despite their predictive power, such studies often depend on large datasets and face issues of redundancy and noise, limiting interpretability and practical application9. This study addresses these gaps by introducing a hybrid approach that integrates the Mahalanobis–Taguchi System (MTS) with advanced ML classifiers (XGBoost, Random Forest, SVM). MTS offers interpretable variable screening via signal-to-noise ratios, retaining factors with genuine managerial significance32,36. This is vital for decision-making, where transparency of key factors—such as visibility, supermarket competitiveness, and residential density—is as important as predictive accuracy. Machine learning algorithms

Machine learning is a subfield of AI and is commonly used in data mining. It involves applying algorithms to parse data, learn from it, and make decisions based on what is learned40. AI plays a crucial role in both front-end and back-end processes41. In the former area, AI is employed for recommendation systems and in-store customer experience management. When considering back-end processes, AI improves inventory optimization, price optimization, and the anticipation of customer demand, benefiting retailers’ planning decisions6. AI allows retailers to operate more efficiently.

XGBoost

XGBoost is an ML algorithm with high classification accuracy42–44. In the context of this study, one of the reasons for opting for XGBoost is its advanced optimization capability. It offers a fast and scalable tree-boosting system equipped with parallel processing, pruning, regularization, and missing-value handling. XGBoost has been successfully applied in diverse scientific domains to effectively address anomaly detection challenges45,46. Due to its low computational cost, the XGBoost algorithm can be used for hyperparameter tuning in order to find the best hyperparameter combination47. Different studies contribute to depression classification tasks or diabetes prediction and diagnosis with several machine learning algorithms; among these classifiers, the performance of XGBoost is found to be the best48,49. In this stage, the measurement scale of the reduced model was confirmed. Discussion and conclusions

In the case study, the MTS was used to analyze nine influencing factors of convenience store location selection to optimize the location selection process. The factors were ranked, in descending order of influence, as follows: visibility (H) > supermarket competitiveness (C) > store location (G) > convenience store competitiveness (B) > number of residents (F) > main customer type (E) > store area (I) > traffic volume (D) > business district type (A). Entrepreneurs and convenience store chains can use this ranking to improve store location decisions, or rely on the reduced set of five factors identified as most important in this study (visibility, supermarket competitiveness, store location, convenience store competitiveness, number of residents), improving the efficiency of store location selection. The prediction model of new store location selection established in this study can reduce interference from subjective factors.

From a strategic perspective, this ranking helps retailers prioritize investments in highly visible sites and systematically evaluate competitive intensity before considering secondary criteria such as store area or traffic flow. By focusing on the top-ranked factors, firms can reduce data collection costs, accelerate market entry, and align expansion strategies with consumer demand, thereby minimizing risks associated with subjective judgment or trial-and-error decision-making.

This study incorporates machine learning algorithms and verifies the effectiveness of MTS-XGBoost, MTS-RF, and MTS-SVM in the convenience store location selection process. Results show that after variable screening and reduction by MTS-XGBoost, the accuracy can be improved by up to 20.22%. When the proportion of training data is 75%, 70% or 67%, the accuracy of the classification model reaches 76.25%, 76.21% and 75.63% respectively. Obviously, after MTS performed variable screening, the predictive ability of the model did not decrease, but was steadily improved.

The MTS performs well when dealing with numerous variables, and can effectively identify variables with insignificant impact on the prediction target. It optimizes the store location selection process and resource allocation, saves time in searching and analysis, reduces the cost of data collection and processing, and minimizes economic losses due to improper store location selection. The method offers substantial benefits for convenience store developers.

Limitations and future development

Although the MTS method performed well in this study of convenience store location selection in Taiwan, its sensitivity to abnormal values and its stability under extreme market conditions require further investigation. On the basis of the results presented herein, the following research directions are suggested.

Cross-regional application. Expand the scope of research to other countries and markets. Variations in shopping culture and habits mean research results for different regions must be compared to test the applicability and stability of the MTS method under diverse conditions. In markets with different retail density, mobility patterns, or regulatory contexts, key variables may shift, so the model’s accuracy and rankings require region-specific calibration.

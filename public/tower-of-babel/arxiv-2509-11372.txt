On a class of thin obstacle-type problems for the bi-Laplacian operator
Donatella Danielli, Giovanni Gravina

Abstract
. This paper investigates the regularity of solutions and structural properties of the free boundary for a class of fourth-order elliptic problems with Neumann-type boundary conditions. The singular and degenerate elliptic operators studied naturally emerge from the extension procedure for higher-order fractional powers of the Laplacian, while the choice of non-linearity considered encompasses two-phase boundary obstacle problems as a special case. After establishing local regularity properties of solutions, Almgren- and Monneau-type monotonicity formulas are derived and utilized to carry out a blow-up analysis and prove a stratification result for the free boundary.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the regularity of solutions and some structural properties of the (thin) free boundary for weak solutions of the fourth-order boundary value problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{b}^{2}u=&0&\text{ in }B_{1}^{+},\\
u=&g&\text{ on }S_{1}^{+},\\
\partial_{\nu}u=&\partial_{\nu}g&\text{ on }S_{1}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}u=&0&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}\Delta_{b}u=&f(\cdot,u)&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime},\end{array}\right.

where
b\in(-1,1)
,
\Delta_{b}
denotes the elliptic operator

(1.2)

\Delta_{b}w\coloneqq y^{-b}\di(y^{b}\nabla w)=\frac{b}{y}w_{y}+\Delta w,

and

(1.3)

\partial_{y}^{b}w(x,0)\coloneqq\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{b}\partial_{y}w(x,y)

is the conormal
interior
derivative. Here
B_{r}\coloneqq\{X=(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R},\text{ and }|X|<r\}
. Unless otherwise stated, throughout the paper we assume that
n\geq 2
. Moreover, we set
B_{r}^{+}\coloneqq B_{r}\cap\{y>0\}
and use
S_{r}^{+}
and
B_{r}^{\prime}
to denote the lateral and the flat portions of
\partial B_{r}^{+}
, respectively. We mention here that
g
is a given, sufficiently regular boundary datum; the precise assumptions on
g
are given below in (
H.1
), and are motivated by the existence theory for weak solutions to (
1.1
) (see
3.3
). Due its technical nature, the definition of weak solutions is given below in
Definition

3.1
(see also
3.2
). Finally, the nonlinearity
f
is assumed to satisfy regularity and growth conditions that are compatible with the choice

(1.4)

f(x,u)\coloneqq\lambda_{-}(u^{-})^{p-1}-\lambda_{+}(u^{+})^{p-1},

where
\lambda_{\pm}>0
,
p\geq 1
, and
u^{+}\coloneqq\max\{u,0\}
and
u^{-}\coloneqq\max\{-u,0\}
denote the positive part and the negative part of
u
, respectively. In particular, a class of two-phase thin obstacle problems can be seen as particular cases of our analysis. This motivates and justifies the assumptions on
f
that we make throughout the paper (see, for example, (
H.2
) and (
H.3
)).

1.1.
Motivation and previous works

In the seminal paper
[
9
]
, Caffarelli and Silvestre gave a characterization of the fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\coloneqq C(n,s)\operatorname{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(z)}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dz,\qquad s\in(0,1),

as the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for a suitable extension of
u
in the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. One of the key insights used to establish this result is the equivalence between the
H^{s}
-seminorm and the Dirichlet energy for the weighted Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+};y^{b})
,
b=1-2s
, thus justifying the interest in recent years in the study of elliptic problems driven by the operator
\Delta_{b}
. For a comprehensive treatment of the fractional Laplacian see, for example,
[
1
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
34
]
, and the references therein. While the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension has been employed in a variety of settings, we highlight here some applications that are more directly related to the scope of our paper. A common feature of several of the works described below is the use of Almgren- and Monneau-type monotonicity formulas, made possible by the local character of the extended problem. For instance, in
[
21
]
, Almgren’s frequency formula was applied to characterize the asymptotic behavior of solutions to a fractional Laplace equation with a Hardy-type potential, and to establish the strong unique continuation property. In the context of free boundary problems involving the fractional Laplacian, this approach was introduced in the foundational works
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
30
]
; see also
[
11
]
, where the extension was used to study a one-phase free boundary problem for
(-\Delta)^{s}
. In
[
5
]
, the authors investigated a two-phase problem with a thin free boundary, which can be viewed as a localized version of the classical two-phase free boundary problem for the half-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{1/2}
(i.e.,
b=0
). This result was later extended to all
s\in(0,1)
(i.e.,
b\in(-1,1)
) in
[
3
]
. Moreover, in
[
4
]
, the non-local analogue of the classical two-phase obstacle problem (or two-phase membrane problem) was studied.

We conclude this brief and by no means exhaustive overview of second-order, extended problems by mentioning the work
[
16
]
, where Almgren- and Monneau-type monotonicity formulas, together with a blow-up analysis, were employed to provide a classification of the possible vanishing orders for weak solutions to

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{b}u=&0&\text{ in }B_{1}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}u=&\lambda_{+}(u^{+})^{p-1}-\lambda_{-}(u^{-})^{p-1}&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, they proved a stratification result for the nodal set, thus extending previous results of the first author and Jain for the case
b=0
(see
[
15
]
).

Observe that when
f
is given as in (
1.4
), (
1.1
) can be interpreted as the fourth-order counterpart to (
1.5
). Moreover, (
1.1
) continues to bear a connection with a lower-dimensional, fractional problem in view of Yang’s generalization of the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension for general non-integer orders of the fractional Laplacians (see
[
41
]
, and also
[
13
]
). To be precise, when
1<s<2
, Yang’s result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 3.1 in
[
41
]
)
.

For
1<s<2
, set
b\coloneqq 3-2s
and let
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+};y^{b})
be a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{b}^{2}u=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\\
u=&u_{0}&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}u=&0&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then we have that

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}(x)=C(n,b)\partial_{y}^{b}\Delta_{b}u(x,0).

The higher-order extension in
1.1
has sparked significant interest and has led to numerous investigations of related problems in recent years. For example, in
[
23

This was achieved by establishing an Almgren-type monotonicity formula and through a careful classification of blow-up profiles. A similar analysis was carried out by the same authors in
[

, where

is a given function in

. A more detailed discussion of the techniques used in
[

]
, along with a comparison to the strategy developed in this paper for addressing a Neumann coupling term of the form (

. We also mention
[

) with

) and

. Additionally, they obtained results on the structure of the free boundary

for

and

. Since a more in-depth comparison of our results with

For more details on the (thick) obstacle problem for the bi-Laplacian, we refer the reader to the works of Frehse (see

1.2.
Statement of the main results

Our first main result concerns the regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
). A key step in our proof is an application of a Moser-Trudinger iterative scheme for a reformulation of (
1.1
) as a system of second-order equations for
u
and
v\coloneqq\Delta_{b}u
(see
4.3
). This allows us to show an improvement in integrability for
u,v
, and their traces on
B_{R}^{\prime}
(see
4.5
), and the result is then combined (when
b\neq 0
) with elliptic regularity theory for operators with singular or degenerate weights in the Muckenhoupt class
A_{2}
to establish the regularity of solutions, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\colon B_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Carathéodory function and assume that there exists a constant
C
such that

|f(x,\zeta)|\leq C(1+|\zeta|^{p-1})

for
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
, where
p\in[1,p^{\#})
and
p^{\#}>2
is an explicitly computable constant that depends only on
n
and
b

(
see (
H.4
) for the precise definition of
p^{\#}
)
. Then, if
u
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
and
v\coloneqq\Delta_{b}u
, for all
r<1
we have that:

(i)

If
b\geq 0
, then
(u,v)\in C^{2,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})\times C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
\alpha\in(0,1-b)
;

(ii)

If
b<0
, then
(u,v)\in C^{3,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})\times C^{1,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
\alpha\in(0,-b)
.

Additionally, assume that
f
is locally Lipschitz-continuous in the sense that for all
M>0
there exists a constant
C
such that the inequality

|f(x_{1},\zeta_{1})-f(x_{2},\zeta_{2})|\leq C(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)

holds for all
x_{i}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\zeta_{i}|\leq M
,
i=1,2
. Then, for all
r<1
we have that:

(i)

If
b\geq 0
, then
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
\alpha\in(0,1-b)
;

(ii)

If
b<0
, then
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
\alpha\in(0,-b)
.

Here
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}
is used to denote the horizontal components of the gradient.

Finally, for all
b\in(-1,1)
, there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\partial_{y}^{b}v\in C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}^{+})
. In particular, if
b=0
we have that
(u,v)\in C^{3,\beta}(B_{r}^{+})\times C^{1,\beta}(B_{r}^{+})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
.

For more information on the role played by the bound on the growth condition imposed by
p^{\#}
, we refer the reader to the discussion in
4.4
.

After establishing the regularity of solutions in
Section

4
, the remaining sections of the paper focus on the study of the free boundary. For the case of linear or super-linear growth (
p\geq 2
) and non-degenerate weights (
b\leq 0
), we investigate the asymptotic behavior of solutions by carrying out a blow-up analysis. One of our main contributions is the proof of a monotonicity formula for the classical Almgren frequency function

N_{0}^{X_{0}}(r,u,v)\coloneqq\frac{r\int_{B_{r}^{+}(X_{0})}y^{b}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})\,dX}{\int_{S_{r}^{+}(X_{0})}y^{b}(u^{2}+v^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}},

where
v
is again used as a placeholder for
\Delta_{b}u
and
X_{0}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
(see
5.6
). We remark here that the proof of the frequency formula is non-trivial due to the presence of the non-linear coupling in the Neumann-type boundary condition
\partial_{y}^{b}v=f(\cdot,u)
. Our proof draws inspiration from analogous results in previous investigations (see, for example,
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
). A more detailed discussion of the analytical challenges and the techniques used is provided at the beginning of
Section

5
.

With Almgren’s frequency formula at our disposal, we follow the pathway outlined in
[
30
]
. To be precise, after a preliminary blow-up analysis of the so-called Almgren rescalings of
u
and
v
(see
6.1
), we leverage Weiss- and Monneau-type monotonicity formulas to prove the uniqueness of blow-up solutions. Our findings are summarized in the following theorem, where we use
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
to denote the space of
\mu
-homogeneous polynomials that are
b
-harmonic and symmetric with respect to
\{y=0\}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f\colon B_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz-continuous function and assume that there exists a constant
C
such that

|f(x,\zeta)|\leq C|\zeta|^{p-1}

for all
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
, where
p\in[2,p^{\#})
and
p^{\#}
is given as in (
H.4
). Moreover, assume that
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}f
and
\partial_{\zeta}f
are Carathéodory functions with the property that for every
M>0
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}f(x,\zeta)|\leq C|\zeta|\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|\partial_{\zeta}f(x,\zeta)|\leq C

for
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\zeta|\leq M
. Finally, let
u
be a non-trivial weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
, set
v\coloneqq\Delta_{b}u
, and assume that
b\leq 0
. Then, for every
X_{0}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
there exists
\mu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\}
such that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}N_{0}^{X_{0}}(r,u,v)=\mu.

Additionally, if
\mu<\infty
, there exist
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{u(X_{0}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}\to\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\quad\frac{v(X_{0}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\to\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\qquad\text{ in }H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b}),

\displaystyle\frac{u(X_{0}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}\to\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\quad\frac{v(X_{0}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\to\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\qquad\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha}(B_{R}^{+}),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and all
R>0
as
r\to 0^{+}
. Moreover, the polynomials
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
and
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
cannot both vanish identically.

Finally, the free boundary

F(u)\coloneqq\left(\partial\{u>0\}\cup\partial\{u<0\}\right)\cap B_{1}^{\prime}

is analyzed by dividing it into two components: the regular part
R(u)
, which inherits the regularity of the solution, and the singular part
S(u)
. Thanks to a by now standard argument based on the continuous dependence of blow-up solutions with respect to the singular point
X_{0}
and Whitney’s extension theorem, for
n=2,3
we prove that the subset of
S(u)
consisting of points with frequency
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
is contained in the countable union of
C^{1}
regular manifolds of dimensions
d=0,1,\dots,n-1
. For
n\geq 4
, the result holds for frequencies that are sufficiently small (see
8.4
).

1.3.
Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we introduce our notations and recall well-known results such as Poincaré, trace, and Sobolev-type inequalities for weighted Sobolev spaces, as well as a Rellich-type identity. Additionally, we collect results on the regularity of solutions to singular or degenerate elliptic equations that will be useful throughout the paper. In
Section

3
, we give the definition of weak solution and formulate assumptions under which existence of solutions can be established via variational methods (see
3.3
). The regularity of weak solutions is addressed in
Section

4
. Our main result in this section is the proof of
1.2
. With this at hand, the rest of the paper is devoted to the study of the free boundary. To be precise, in
Section

5
we establish the almost-monotonicity of the frequency functional, while in
Section

6
we begin our investigation of blow-up solutions by proving the existence of Almgren-type blow-ups. In
Section

7
, we derive monotonicity formulas of Monneau- and Weiss-type that allow us to prove the non-degeneracy of solutions. This is used later in the section to prove existence and uniqueness of homogenous blow-up solutions, thus concluding the proof of
1.3
. Finally, in
Section

8
we investigate the structure of the free boundary.

2.
Preliminary results

For the convenience of the reader, in this section we collect some of the definitions and tools used throughout the paper.

2.1.
Weighted Sobolev Spaces

The treatment of (
1.1
) is facilitated by the introduction of Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces with weight
\omega(X)\coloneqq y^{b}
,
b\in(-1,1)
. To be precise, for a given open, bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with Lipschitz boundary, we define

L^{2}(\Omega;y^{b})\coloneqq\{w:|y|^{b/2}w\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

and

H^{k}(\Omega;y^{b})\coloneqq\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega;y^{b}):\partial^{\alpha}w\in L^{2}(\Omega;y^{b})\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+1}\text{ with }|\alpha|\leq k\}.

Other function spaces are defined analogously. For more information, we refer the reader to the monograph
[
33
]
.

Observe that the assumption
b\in(-1,1)
guarantees that
|y|^{b}
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{2}
, that is, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
we have that

\left(\fint_{B}|y|^{b}\,dX\right)\left(\fint_{B}|y|^{-b}\,dX\right)\leq C.

Moreover, we remark that the weight is singular when
b<0
and degenerate for
b>0
.

This subsection collects widely known results such as Poincaré, trace, and Sobolev-type inequalities for the space
H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})
. We conclude the subsection with a Rellich-type identity, which will be instrumental for the proof of the frequency formula later in
Section

5
. The identity is obtained under certain regularity assumptions that are standard in the framework we consider and it can thus be verified for our solutions (see, in particular,
2.7
and
4.9
below).

Lemma 2.1

(Poincaré-type inequality)
.

For
w\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})
we have

\frac{n+b}{r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,dX\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}+\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dX.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward adaptation of a well-known argument, and is included here for the reader’s convenience. Recalling that
X\coloneqq(x,y)
, we have that

\displaystyle(n+1)\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,dX

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}^{+}}(\di(y^{b}w^{2}X)-\nabla(y^{b}w^{2})\cdot X)\,dX

\displaystyle=r\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}-\int_{B_{r}^{+}}(2y^{b}w\nabla w\cdot X+by^{b}w^{2})\,dX

\displaystyle\leq r\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}+\int_{B_{r}^{+}}((1-b)y^{b}w^{2}+r^{2}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2})\,dX.

The desired estimate follows by rearranging the terms in the previous inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Trace operators)
.

There exist compact trace operators

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}

\displaystyle\colon H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})\to L^{2}(S_{r}^{+};y^{b}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}

\displaystyle\colon H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})\to L^{2}(B_{r}^{\prime}).

Moreover,

\operatorname{Tr}\colon H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})\to H^{(1-b)/2}(B_{r}^{\prime})

is linear, continuous, and onto.

For a proof of
2.2
, we refer to
[
21
]
and Proposition 2.1 in
[
31
]
. In order to ease notation, in the following we continue to use
w
in place of
\operatorname{Tr}w
whenever
w\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})
.

Lemma 2.3

(Trace inequality)
.

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
r>0
and all
w\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})
, we have that

\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}w^{2}\,dx\leq C\left(r^{1-b}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dX+r^{-b}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right).

For a proof of
2.3
, see Lemma 2.5 in
[
21
]
.

Lemma 2.4

(Sobolev-type inequalities)
.

There exist constants
S=S(n,b)
and
S^{\prime}(n,b)
such that for all
w\in H^{1}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})
, we have that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}|w|^{2^{*}_{b}}\,dx\right)^{2/2^{*}_{b}}

\displaystyle\leq S^{\prime}\left(\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dX+\int_{S_{1}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|w|^{2^{**}_{b}}\,dX\right)^{2/2^{**}_{b}}

\displaystyle\leq S\left(\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dX+\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}w^{2}\,dX\right).

Here
2^{*}_{b}
and
2^{**}_{b}
are the fractional Sobolev exponent, defined via

2^{*}_{b}\coloneqq\frac{2n}{n-1+b}

and

2^{**}_{b}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\frac{2(n+1+b)}{n-1+b}&\text{ if }0<b<1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}&\text{ if }-1<b\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

For a proof of (
2.1
), we refer the reader to Lemma 2.6 in
[
21
]
, whereas (
2.2
) follows from Theorem 19.10 in
[
36
]
. We also remark that versions of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) continue to hold in
B_{r}^{+}
in view of a straightforward scaling argument.

In the following, we let

H^{2}_{x}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})\coloneqq\left\{w\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b}):\partial_{x_{i}}w\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b}),i=1,\dots,n\right\}.

Lemma 2.5

(Rellich identity)
.

Let
w\in H^{2}_{x}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})
be such that
\partial_{y}^{b}w\in H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{-b})
(see (
1.3
) for the definition of
\partial_{y}^{b}w
). Then

(2.3)

r\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}-2\left(\nabla w\cdot\frac{X}{r}\right)^{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}=(n+b-1)\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dX\\
-2\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}(\nabla w\cdot X)\Delta_{b}w\,dX-2\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}(\nabla_{x}^{\prime}w\cdot x)\partial_{y}^{b}w\,dx

for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in(0,R)
.

Proof.

Observe that the all the terms on the right-hand side of (
2.3
) are well-defined in view of the regularity assumptions on
w
. Notice also that, by the coarea formula,

\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\left||\nabla w|^{2}-2\left(\nabla w\cdot\frac{X}{|X|}\right)^{2}\right|\,dX=\int_{0}^{R}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\left||\nabla w|^{2}-2\left(\nabla w\cdot\frac{X}{r}\right)^{2}\right|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,dr.

Consequently, if we set

d(r)\coloneqq\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}-2\left(\nabla w\cdot\frac{X}{r}\right)^{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

we obtain that
d\in L^{1}(0,R)
. This implies that the left-hand side of (
2.3
) is well-defined for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in(0,R)
.

By a standard density argument, it is enough to establish (
2.3
) for
w\in C^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+})
. Observe that

\displaystyle\di(Xy^{b}|\nabla w|^{2})

\displaystyle=X\cdot\nabla(y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2})+(n+1)y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle=2y^{b}\sum_{i,k}X_{i}(\partial_{ik}w)(\partial_{k}w)+(n+b+1)y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\di((\nabla w\cdot X)y^{b}\nabla w)

\displaystyle=\nabla(\nabla w\cdot X)\cdot y^{b}\nabla w+y^{b}(\nabla w\cdot X)\Delta_{b}w

\displaystyle=y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}+y^{b}\sum_{i,k}X_{i}(\partial_{ik}w)(\partial_{k}w)+y^{b}(\nabla w\cdot X)\Delta_{b}w.

Combining the two previous identities, we obtain that

\di(Xy^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}-2(\nabla w\cdot X)y^{b}\nabla w)=(n+b-1)y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}-2y^{b}(\nabla w\cdot X)\Delta_{b}w.

From this, the desired identity follows by an application of the divergence theorem in
B_{r}^{+}\cap\{y>\tau\}
and by subsequently letting
\tau\to 0^{+}
.
∎

3.
Existence of weak solutions

We begin by introducing the assumptions utilized throughout the section. Following this, we give the definition of a weak solution to (
1.1
) and proceed to establish existence by employing the direct method in the calculus of variations.

In the following we let, for
\Delta_{b}w
as in (
1.2
),

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{w\in H^{1}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b}):\Delta_{b}w\in L^{2}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})\}

and set

\|w\|_{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\left(|w|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}+|\Delta_{b}w|^{2}\right)\,dX\right)^{1/2}.

Let
p\geq 1
. Next, we specify the assumptions on the boundary datum
g
and the nonlinearity
f
.

(H.1)

Assume that
g\in\mathcal{H}\cap L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})
satisfies
\partial_{y}^{b}g=0

B_{1}^{\prime}
.

(H.2)

Assume that
f\colon B_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Carathéodory and satisfies

|f(x,\zeta)|\leq C(1+|\zeta|^{p-1})

for
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
.

(H.3)

Let

F(x,\zeta)\coloneqq-2\int_{0}^{\zeta}f(x,\sigma)d\sigma.

Assume that there is a positive constant
c
such that

F(x,\zeta)\geq c|\zeta|^{p}

for
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
u\in\mathcal{H}\cap L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})
is a weak solution of

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{b}^{2}u=&0&\text{ in }B_{1}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}u=&0&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}\Delta_{b}u=&f(\cdot,u)&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime},\end{array}\right.

if

(3.2)

\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\Delta_{b}u\Delta_{b}\phi\,dX=\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\phi\,dx

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0,S_{1}^{+}}\cap L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})
, where

\mathcal{H}_{0,S_{1}^{+}}\coloneqq\overline{\left\{C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{1}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{1}^{+}}):\partial_{y}^{b}\phi=0\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime}\right\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}}.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

Observe that the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) is well-defined for all admissible test functions. Indeed, in view of (
H.2
), an application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\left|\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\phi\,dx\right|\leq C\left(1+\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})}^{p-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})}.

(ii)

We say that
u
solves (
1.1
) if in addition to being a solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
, it satisfies
u=g
and
\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}g
on
S_{1}^{+}
in the sense of traces.

Next, we consider the associated energy

(3.3)

\mathcal{J}(w)\coloneqq\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\Delta_{b}w|^{2}\,dX+\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}F(x,w)\,dx,

defined over the class

(3.4)

\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\{w\in\mathcal{H}:w=g\text{ on }S_{1}^{+},\partial_{\nu}w=\partial_{\nu}g\text{ on }S_{1}^{+},\text{ and }\partial_{y}^{b}w=0\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime}\}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Under the assumptions (
H.1
)–(
H.3
), let
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{A}
be given as in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively. Then
\mathcal{J}
admits a global minimizer
u\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover,
u
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
and
3.2

(ii)
.

Proof.

The proof follows by standard variational methods. We include here the details for the reader’s convenience.

Step 1:
Observe that (
H.1
) implies that
g\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, using the fact that
F(x,\zeta)\leq C(1+|\zeta|^{p})
(see (
H.2
) and (
H.3
)), we can also conclude that
\mathcal{J}(g)<\infty
. Let
\{w_{j}\}_{j}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an infimizing sequence. Then, for every
j
, by (
2.1
) we have that

(3.5)

(n+b)\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|w_{j}-w_{1}|^{2}\,dX\leq\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla(w_{j}-w_{1})|^{2}\,dX.

By the divergence theorem and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla(w_{j}-w_{1})|^{2}\,dX

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}(w_{j}-w_{1})\Delta_{b}(w_{j}-w_{1})\,dX

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|w_{j}-w_{1}|^{2}\,dX\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\Delta_{b}(w_{j}-w_{1})|^{2}\,dX\right)^{1/2}.

Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we conclude that

\|w_{j}-w_{1}\|_{H^{1}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})}^{2}\leq C(n,b)\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|\Delta_{b}(w_{j}-w_{1})|^{2}\,dX\leq C(n,b)\mathcal{J}(g),

where the last inequality follows by recalling that
F
is nonnegative and by noticing that, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{J}(w_{j})\leq\mathcal{J}(g)
for all
j
. Consequently, there exists a constant
C=C(n,b)
such that

\|w_{j}-w_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C\mathcal{J}(g)^{1/2}.

By standard compactness arguments together with
2.2
, eventually extracting a subsequence (which we do not relabel), we can find
u\in\mathcal{A}
such that
w_{j}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})
,
\Delta_{b}w_{j}\rightharpoonup\Delta_{b}u
in
L^{2}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})
, and
w_{j}\to u
in
L^{2}(B_{1}^{\prime})
and pointwise a.e. on
B_{1}^{\prime}
. In particular, we also obtain that
F(\cdot,w_{j}(\cdot))\to F(\cdot,u(\cdot))
pointwise a.e. on
B_{1}^{\prime}
. Since by Fatou’s lemma
\mathcal{J}
is weakly lower-semicontinuous with respect to the combination of these notions of convergence (see (

)), this shows that

is a global minimizer for
\mathcal{J}
over
\mathcal{A}
.

Step 2:
We now proceed to show that
u
is a weak solution to (
1.1
). To this end, observe that by (
H.3
) we have that

c\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})}^{p}\leq\mathcal{J}(u)<\infty,

thus proving that
u\in L^{p}(B_{1}^{\prime})
.
Finally, as one can readily check, for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{1}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{1}^{+}})
with
\partial_{y}^{b}\phi=0
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
, we have that

. For
\varepsilon>0
, the minimality of

implies that

(3.7)

0\leq\frac{\mathcal{J}(u+\varepsilon\phi)-\mathcal{J}(u)}{\varepsilon}=\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}(2\Delta_{b}u\Delta_{b}\phi+\varepsilon|\Delta_{b}\phi|^{2})\,dX+\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}\frac{F(x,u+\varepsilon\phi)-F(x,u)}{\varepsilon}\,dx.

By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, letting

in the previous inequality shows that

0\leq\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\Delta_{b}u\Delta_{b}\phi\,dX-\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\phi\,dx.

To conclude the proof, it is enough to observe that repeating the argument with

yields the opposite inequality.
∎

We remark that if in addition we have that

is convex for

-a.e.

, then

is strictly convex over the closed, convex set

. This, in turn, implies that the minimizer of

is unique. Notice that when

is given as in (

), one has that

which is indeed convex with respect to

.

Regularity of solutions

This section is dedicated to proving regularity results for

Lemma 4.1
.

For every

As one can readily check, the existence of a weak solution follows from a similar (but simpler) argument to that in the previous section; therefore, we omit the details. The regularity of

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a weak solution to (

Proof.

The proof uses arguments that are well-known to the experts (see, for example, Theorem 2.5 in
[

]
and Proposition 2.4 in

). The details are included here for the reader’s convenience.

Step 1:
We begin by considering a smooth cut-off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
such that
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{r}
and
\partial_{y}^{b}\eta=0
, and a function
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}\cap C^{2,\alpha}(B_{R}^{+})

on
S_{1}^{+}
, where

\eta\Psi
is an admissible test function for (

) for any such function

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}w\Delta_{b}\Psi\,dX

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}(2\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\Psi+\Psi\Delta_{b}\eta)\Delta_{b}u\,dX+\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\eta\Psi\,dx.

Next, we claim that

is the only solution to (

. Indeed, if

are two such solutions, then

Step 2:
To conclude, we observe that, for fixed

Then, an application of the Lions–Lax–Milgram theorem yields the existence of a function

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (

) in the sense of Definition
3.1
, and let
r<1
. Then
v\coloneqq\Delta_{b}u
is a weak solution to

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{L}_{b}(v)=&0&\text{ in }B_{r}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}v=&f(\cdot,u)&\text{ on }B_{r}^{\prime}\end{array}\right.

in the sense that

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi\,dX+\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\phi\,dx=0

for all
\phi\in H^{1}_{0,S_{r}^{+}}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})\cap L^{p}(B_{r}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r})
be such that
\partial_{y}^{b}\phi=0
on
B_{r}^{\prime}
. Then
\phi
is an admissible test function according to
Definition

3.1
, and therefore the divergence theorem (together with
4.2
) implies that

(4.3)

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi\,dX+\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\phi\,dx=0.

To conclude, we need to show that (
4.3
) continues to hold without the additional condition
\partial_{y}^{b}\phi=0
on
B_{r}^{\prime}
. To this end, let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r})
and define

\phi_{\varepsilon}(X)\coloneqq\phi(X)-y\eta\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)\partial_{y}\phi(X),

where
\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
is a smooth cut-off function that is compactly supported in
(-2,2)
and such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
(-1,1)
. Then
\{\phi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r}^{+})
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, and direct computations show that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\to\phi
in
H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Moreover, as one can readily check, on
B_{r}^{\prime}
we have that
\phi_{\varepsilon}=\phi
and
\partial_{y}^{b}\phi_{\varepsilon}=0
. Consequently, by (
4.3
) we obtain that

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dX+\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}f(x,u)\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0.

Finally, letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
yields the desired result.
∎

Next, we present a proposition that establishes the
L^{q}
-regularity theory for both
u
and
\Delta_{b}u
. This is the first step in our bootstrap process, and will enable us to apply the results of Subsection
2.2
. To proceed, we introduce the following structural assumption.

(H.4)

Let
p
be as in (
H.2
). Assume that

1\leq p<p^{\#}\coloneqq\frac{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}}{4}+1,

where
2^{*}_{b}
and
2^{**}_{b}
denote the fractional Sobolev exponents defined in
2.4
.

Remark 4.4
.

Before proceeding further, we comment on the assumption (
H.4
). Similar constraints are standard in the literature on regularity theory for comparable problems (see, for example, Corollary 2.10 in
[
31
]
); these conditions are generally dictated by integrability requirements on the specific test functions used in the proofs. In our case, as will become evident from the proof of
4.5
below, assumption (
H.4
) allows us to select a number
\beta_{0}
such that

2<\beta_{0}\leq\gamma_{0}\coloneqq\frac{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}}{2(p-1)},\qquad p>1.

The upper bound
\beta_{0}\leq\gamma_{0}
allows us to obtain uniform estimates with respect to the truncation we consider (see Step 2 in the proof of
4.5
). The lower bound
\beta_{0}>2
is crucial to our ability to iterate the argument (see Step 3 in the proof of
4.5
). It is also worth noting that values of
p
in
[1,2]
are permissible for all choices of
n
and
b
. Additionally, we remark that when
b=0
, the condition simplifies to

p<\frac{n(n+1)}{(n-1)^{2}}+1.

In particular, for
b=0
, the range
p\in[1,3]
is admissible for all physically relevant dimensions up to and including
n=4
. In view of (
H.2
), this implies that our regularity results apply to a range of exponents
p
that correspond to both sublinear and superlinear growth conditions for
f
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
and let
v\coloneqq\Delta_{b}u
. Assume that
f
satisfies (
H.2
) and (
H.4
). Then, for every
q\in[1,\infty)
and every
0<r<R<1
there exists a constant
C=C(n,b,q,R,r)
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}\right),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}\right).

Proof.

The proof adapts the approach of Proposition 7.6 in
[
23
]
, which itself draws inspiration from Theorem 2.3 in
[
7
]
. Our presentation highlights the key differences introduced by the nonlinearity
f
.

Observe that, in view of
4.3
, for every
R<1
, the pair
(u,v)
is a weak solution for the coupled system

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{L}_{b}(u)=&y^{b}v&\text{ in }B_{R}^{+},\\
\mathcal{L}_{b}(v)=&0&\text{ in }B_{R}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}u=&0&\text{ on }B_{R}^{\prime},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}v=&f(\cdot,u)&\text{ on }B_{R}^{\prime}.\end{array}\right.

We also notice that if
p=1
then by (
H.2
) we have that
\partial_{y}^{b}v\in L^{\infty}(B_{R}^{\prime})
. Therefore, in this case one can directly apply the results of Subsection
2.2
(see
4.6
below). For this reason, in the following we assume without loss that
p>1
.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1:
(
Improving the integrability of
u
) Let
\eta_{0,u}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{R}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{R}^{+}};[0,1])
be a cut-off function such that
\eta_{0,u}\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{r_{0}}^{+}}
for some
r_{0}\in(r,R)
. For
M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{M}\coloneqq\min\{|u|,M\}
and define

(4.7)

\phi_{0,u,M}(X)\coloneqq\eta_{0,u}^{2}(X)u_{M}(X)^{2^{**}_{b}-2}u(X).

Observe that
\phi_{0,u,M}
is an admissible test function for the Neumann-type equation satisfied by
u
(see
Definition

2.6
). In particular, testing with
\phi_{0,u,M}
yields the identity

(4.8)

\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\eta_{0,u}^{2}u_{M}^{2^{**}_{b}-2}u\right)\,dX=-\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}v\left(\eta_{0,u}^{2}u_{M}^{2^{**}_{b}-2}u\right)\,dX.

Direct computations (see also Lemma 9.1 in
[
24
]
) show that, if we set

K(\tau)\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{4}{4+\tau}\right\},

then

(4.9)

K(2^{**}_{b})\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\left|\nabla\left(\eta_{0,u}u_{M}^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)/2}u\right)\right|^{2}\,dX\leq\underbrace{\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\eta_{0,u}^{2}u_{M}^{2^{**}_{b}-2}u\right)\,dX}_{I}\\
+\left(2+K(2^{**}_{b})\frac{2^{**}_{b}+2}{2}\right)\underbrace{\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla\eta_{0,u}|^{2}u_{M}^{2^{**}_{b}-2}u^{2}\,dX}_{II}.

In view of (
4.8
), using Young’s inequality and the fact that
u_{M}\leq|u|
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{**}_{b}}\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\eta_{0,u}^{2}|v|^{2^{**}_{b}}\,dX+\frac{2^{**}_{b}-1}{2^{**}_{b}}\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\eta_{0,u}^{2}u_{M}^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)\frac{2^{**}_{b}}{2^{**}_{b}-1}}|u|^{\frac{2^{**}_{b}}{2^{**}_{b}-1}}\,dX

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{**}_{b}}\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\eta_{0,u}^{2}|v|^{2^{**}_{b}}\,dX+\frac{2^{**}_{b}-1}{2^{**}_{b}}\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\eta_{0,u}^{2}|u|^{2^{**}_{b}}\,dX.

Moreover, we have that

(4.11)

|II|\leq\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla\eta_{0,u}|^{2}|u|^{2^{**}_{b}}\,dX.

Combining (
4.9
) with the estimates in (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) shows that

(4.12)

\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}\left|\nabla\left(\eta_{0,u}u_{M}^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)/2}u\right)\right|^{2}\,dX\leq C(n,b,\eta_{0,u})\int_{B_{R}^{+}}y^{b}(|u|^{2^{**}_{b}}+|v|^{2^{**}_{b}})\,dX.

Since
u,v\in L^{2^{**}_{b}}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})
by (
2.2
), letting
M\to\infty
in (
4.12
) and using Fatou’s lemma, we obtain that
\nabla(\eta_{0,u}|u|^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)/2}u)\in L^{2}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})
. Consequently,
2.1
implies that
\eta_{0,u}|u|^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)/2}u\in H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})
and a further application of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) yields that
\eta_{0,u}|u|^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)/2}u\in L^{2^{**}_{b}}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})
and
\eta_{0,u}|u|^{(2^{**}_{b}-2)/2}u\in L^{2^{*}_{b}}(B_{R}^{\prime})
. Recalling that
\eta_{0,u}\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{r_{0}}^{+}}
we can then conclude that

(4.13)

u\in L^{(2^{**}_{b})^{2}/2}(B_{r_{0}}^{+};y^{b})\cap L^{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}/2}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime}),

and furthermore that

\|u\|_{L^{(2^{**}_{b})^{2}/2}(B_{r_{0}}^{+};y^{b})}+\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}/2}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}\leq C(n,b,R,r_{0})\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}\right),

where the constant
C
depends on
R
and
r_{0}
through
\eta_{0,u}
and a suitable rescaling of the constants
S
and
S^{\prime}
in
2.4
.

Step 2:
(
Improving the integrability of
v
) Let
r_{0}\in(r,R)
be given as in the previous step and let
\eta_{0,v}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{r_{0}}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{r_{0}}^{+}};[0,1])
be a cut-off function such that
\eta_{0,v}\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{\rho_{0}}^{+}}
for some
\rho_{0}\in(r,r_{0})
. Observe that in view of (
H.4
), we can find
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.14)

p\leq\frac{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}}{2(2+\varepsilon)}+1.

Similarly to the previous step, for
M\in\mathbb{N}
we let
v_{M}\coloneqq\min\{|v|,M\}
and define

(4.15)

\phi_{0,v,M}(X)\coloneqq\eta_{0,v}^{2}(X)v_{M}(X)^{\beta_{0}-2}v(X),

where

(4.16)

\beta_{0}\coloneqq\min\{2^{*}_{b},2^{**}_{b},2+\varepsilon\},

where
\varepsilon
is given as in (
4.14
). Next, we claim that
\phi_{0,v,M}
is an admissible test function for the Neumann problem satisfied by
v
(see (
4.2
)). Since by assumption
p>1
, if we set

(4.17)

\gamma_{0}\coloneqq\frac{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}}{2(p-1)},

then by (
H.2
) and (
4.13
) we have
f(\cdot,u(\cdot))\in L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})
with

(4.18)

\|f(\cdot,u)\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}^{\gamma_{0}}\leq C\left(1+\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}/2}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}^{2^{*}_{b}\cdot 2^{**}_{b}/2}\right).

Therefore, to prove the claim, it is enough to verify that
v\in L^{\gamma_{0}^{\prime}}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})
(here we use
′
to denote the Hölder conjugate). By (

), the desired condition follows provided that
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\leq 2^{*}_{b}
, which in turn is equivalent to

p\leq 2^{**}_{b}\left(\frac{2^{*}_{b}-1}{2}\right)+1.

Observe that this condition holds true for all values of

as in (
H.4
) and therefore does not pose any further restriction.
Arguing as in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we obtain that

(4.19)

K(\beta_{0})\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}\left|\nabla\left(\eta_{0,v}v_{M}^{(\beta_{0}-2)/2}v\right)\right|^{2}\,dX\leq-\underbrace{\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{\prime}}\eta_{0,v}^{2}f(x,u)v_{M}^{\beta_{0}-2}v\,dx}_{III}\\
+\left(2+K(\beta_{0})\frac{\beta_{0}+2}{2}\right)\underbrace{\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla\eta_{0,v}|^{2}v_{M}^{\beta_{0}-2}v^{2}\,dX}_{IV}.

Similarly to above, we readily obtain that

(4.20)

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{\prime}}\eta_{0,v}^{2}|f(x,u)|^{\beta_{0}}\,dx+\frac{\beta_{0}-1}{\beta_{0}}\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{\prime}}\eta_{0,v}^{2}|v|^{\beta_{0}}\,dx,

(4.21)

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}|v|^{\beta_{0}}|\nabla\eta_{0,v}|^{2}\,dX.

Crucially, by (
4.14
) and (
4.16
), one has
\beta_{0}\leq 2+\varepsilon\leq\gamma_{0}
. Using this fact and combining the estimates in (
4.18
), (
4.19
), (
4.20
), and (
4.21
) we can argue as in (
4.13
) and conclude that
\eta_{0,v}|v|^{(\beta_{0}-2)/2}v\in H^{1}(B_{r_{0}}^{+};y^{b})
. Moreover, recalling that
\eta_{0,v}\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{\rho_{0}}^{+}}
, we obtain that

(4.22)

v\in L^{\beta_{0}2^{**}_{b}/2}(B_{\rho_{0}}^{+};y^{b})\cap L^{\beta_{0}2^{*}_{b}/2}(B_{\rho_{0}}^{\prime}),

with

\|v\|_{L^{\beta_{0}2^{**}_{b}/2}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v\|_{L^{\beta_{0}2^{*}_{b}/2}(B_{r}^{\prime})}\leq C(n,b,R,r_{0},\rho_{0})\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}\right),

where the constant
C
depends on
R
,
r_{0}
, and
\rho_{0}
through
\eta_{0,u}
,
\eta_{0,v}
, and a suitable modification of the constants
S
and
S^{\prime}
in
2.4
.

Step 3:
(
The iterative argument
) The strategy employed above can be used to iteratively improve the integrability of
u
and
v
. To this end, set
\alpha_{0}\coloneqq 2^{**}_{b}
, let
\beta_{0}
and
\gamma_{0}
be given as in (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), respectively, and for

define

\alpha_{k}\coloneqq\frac{2^{**}_{b}\beta_{k-1}}{2},\qquad\beta_{k}\coloneqq\beta_{0}\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}\right)^{k},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\gamma_{k}\coloneqq\frac{2^{*}_{b}\alpha_{k}}{2}.

Observe that
\alpha_{k}\geq\beta_{k}
for all

and that
\beta_{k}\to\infty
as

, since
\beta_{0}>2
. For
q
as in the statement, let
K\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\beta_{K}\geq q
, set
\rho_{-1}\coloneqq R
, and for

define

\rho_{k}\coloneqq R-\frac{k+1}{K+1}(R-r)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad r_{k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho_{k-1}-\rho_{k}\right).

We can then define cut-off functions
\eta_{k,u}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{\rho_{k-1}}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{\rho_{k-1}}^{+}};[0,1])
and
\eta_{k,v}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{r_{k}}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{r_{k}}^{+}};[0,1])
such that
\eta_{k,u}\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{r_{k}}^{+}}
and
\eta_{k,v}\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{\rho_{k}}^{+}}
. With these at hand, one can reason as in the previous two steps by considering

\displaystyle\phi_{k,u,M}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\eta_{k,u}^{2}(X)u_{M}(X)^{\alpha_{k}-2}u(X),

\displaystyle\phi_{k,v,M}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\eta_{k,v}^{2}(X)v_{M}(X)^{\beta_{k}-2}v(X)

as test functions for the equations satisfied by

, respectively. To be precise, for

In turn, similarly to Step 2, we have that

and therefore we obtain that

Since (
4.4
) and (

The next proposition shows that additional regularity assumptions on
f
yield an improvement on the regularity of
v
. To be precise, we introduce the following structural assumption.

(H.5)

f\colon B_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz-continuous in the sense that for all
M>0
there exists a constant
C
such that

|f(x_{1},\zeta_{1})-f(x_{2},\zeta_{2})|\leq C(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)

hold for all
x_{i}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\zeta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\zeta_{i}|\leq M
,
i=1,2
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
and let
v\coloneqq\Delta_{b}u
. Assume that
f
satisfies (
H.2
), (
H.4
), and (
H.5
).
Then the following hold:

(i)

Assume that
b\geq 0
. Then for all
0<r<R<1
and all
\alpha\in(0,1-b)
there exists a constant
C=C(n,b,R,r,\alpha)
such that

\|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+1+\|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}^{\prime})}\right).

(ii)

Assume that
b<0
. Then for all
0<r<R<1
and all
\alpha\in(0,-b)
there exists a constant
C=C(n,b,R,r,\alpha)
such that

\|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+1+\|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}^{\prime})}\right).

Moreover, there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\partial_{y}^{b}v\in C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}^{+})
.

Proof.

Let
r_{1}\coloneqq(r+R)/2
and observe that if for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
with
|\xi|
sufficiently small we define the function

v^{\xi}(X)\coloneqq\frac{v(x+\xi,y)-v(X)}{|\xi|},

then
v^{\xi}
is a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{L}_{b}(v^{\xi})=&0&\text{ in }B_{r_{1}}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}v^{\xi}=&f^{\xi}&\text{ on }B_{r_{1}}^{\prime},\end{array}\right.

where

f^{\xi}(x)\coloneqq\frac{f(x+\xi,u(x+\xi,0))-f(x,u(x,0))}{|\xi|}.

Notice also that, by (
H.5
), there exists a constant
C
(potentially depending on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}
, but independent of
\xi
) such that

|f^{\xi}(x)|\leq C\left(1+\frac{|u(x+\xi,0)-u(x,0)|}{|\xi|}\right).

Consequently, for all
q\in[1,\infty)
we have that

(4.23)

\|f^{\xi}\|_{L^{q}(B_{r_{1}}^{\prime})}^{q}\leq C\left(1+\int_{B_{r_{1}}^{\prime}}\left|\frac{u(x+\xi,0)-u(x,0)}{|\xi|}\right|^{q}\,dx\right)\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}^{\prime})}^{q}\right).

In particular, we are in a position to apply
2.8
and conclude that if
b\geq 0
then
v^{\xi}\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})
with

\displaystyle\|v^{\xi}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|v^{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r_{1}}^{+};y^{b})}+\|f^{\xi}\|_{L^{q}(B_{r_{1}}^{\prime})}\right)

(4.24)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|v\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})}+1+\|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}^{\prime})}\right)

for all
\alpha\in(0,1-b)
, where in the last step we have used (
4.23
) and a classical estimate for the difference quotient of Sobolev functions. Observe that the inequality obtained in (
4.24
) is uniform with respect to
\xi
. Therefore, the Ascoli–Arzelá Theorem can then be used to show that
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
\alpha\in(0,1-b)

Remark 4.10
.

We conclude the section with some comments on the regularity of solutions and the techniques used.

First, we recall that the standard pathway to proving Hölder regularity for solutions to
stable
obstacle problems for the Laplacian (or its fractional counterparts) revolves around Morrey’s Dirichlet growth theorem (see, for example, Theorem 3.5.2 in
[
35
]
). A key step in obtaining the gradient estimate required to apply Morrey’s theorem is a Caccioppoli inequality, typically derived by either showing that
u^{\pm}
are subharmonic (or
b
-subharmonic, respectively), or by directly leveraging the fact that the quantities
f(u)u^{+}
and
f(u)u^{-}
have the correct sign (hence the distinction between stable and unstable cases). For more details on how these strategies have been successfully employed in previous works, we refer the reader to Section 3 of
[
5
]
, Sections 4 and 5 of
[
4
]
, Section 3 of
[
15
]
, and Section 2 of
[
16
]
.

In the present case, this approach does not seem to be viable due to the different structure of our fourth-order problem. Indeed, using any suitable modification of
v
as a test function leads to a boundary term involving
f(\cdot,u)v
, which has no known sign. Similar difficulties arise when approaching the problem from an energy perspective, noting that
v
is a minimizer of

\mathcal{K}(w)\coloneqq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dX+2\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}f(x,u)w\,dx

over the class

\mathcal{A}(r,v)\coloneqq\{w\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+};y^{b}):w=v\text{ on }S_{r}^{+}\}.

We also mention that while we relied on a similar concept of stability in the form of (
H.3
) for our existence result (see
3.3
), the assumption is not required for any of the results in this section.

Finally, it is worth noting that not only the approach described above cannot be directly applied, but subharmonicity may generally fail, as shown by the following one-dimensional example: let
b=0
,
p=2
,
\lambda_{-}=\lambda_{+}=1
, and set
u(x)\coloneqq-x^{3}+6
. Then
u
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}u^{(iv)}=&0,\\
u^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=&-6=\lambda_{-}u^{-}(0)-\lambda_{+}u^{+}(0),\\
u^{\prime}(0)=&0.\end{array}\right.

Moreover
u>0
in
(0,1)
, but
u^{\prime\prime}(x)=-6x<0
, thus showing that
u
is superharmonic.

5.
Almgren’s frequency formula

Almgren’s frequency functions, defined as the local ratio of energy over mass, have been extensively used to study the asymptotic behavior of solutions to a wide range of problems. In the following we will consider the
classical
frequency function, defined via

(5.1)

N_{0}(r,u,v)\coloneqq\frac{rD_{0}(r,u,v)}{H(r,u,v)},

where

(5.2)

D_{0}(r,u,v)\coloneqq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})\,dX

and

(5.3)

H(r,u,v)\coloneqq\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}(u^{2}+v^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

as well as the
perturbed
frequency function

N(r,u,v)\coloneqq\frac{rD(r,u,v)}{H(r,u,v)},

where

(5.4)

D(r,u,v)\coloneqq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+u\Delta_{b}u)\,dX+\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}(\partial_{y}^{b}v)v\,dx.

The latter was introduced in
[
22
]
, where the authors carried out a classification of blow-up profiles and used it to prove a unique continuation result for solutions to the fourth order problem
\Delta u=v
,
\Delta v=0
, with
\partial_{y}u=0
and Neumann boundary coupling given by
\partial_{y}v=hu
on the thin space. Here
h
denotes a given function in
C^{1}(B_{R}^{\prime})
.

As explained in
[
22
]
, the proof of the monotonicity argument for
N
is rather delicate due to the presence of the term

(5.5)

\int_{\partial B_{r}^{\prime}}huv\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

in the expression for
\tfrac{d}{dr}D(r,u,v)
. In
[
22
]
, estimates for this “boundary of the boundary” term are obtained via careful applications of the divergence theorem which ultimately allow one to recast it in terms of bulk and boundary terms that can be controlled (see Lemma 2.12 in
[
22
]
).

Although the techniques developed in
[
22
]
for the case
f(\cdot,u)=hu
suggest potential generalizations for instances where
h
is not necessarily differentiable, the lack of differentiability in the second variable for the prototypical nonlinearities considered in our study of obstacle-type problems necessitates a different approach. For this reason, in the following we obtain a quasi-monotonicity result for the Almgren functional
N_{0}
(see
5.6
below). It is worth noting that our proof continues to involve the modified energy
D(r,u,v)
, as it naturally arises in the expression for
H^{\prime}(r,u,v)
(see
5.5
below). However, by considering the classical Dirichlet energy
D_{0}(r,u,v)
we are able to obtain the desired estimates without ever having to consider derivatives of
D(r,u,v)
, thus avoiding some of the difficulties described above.

Remark 5.1
.

Our work, although related to
[
14
]
, addresses different aspects beyond just the extension to more general elliptic operators. Here a word of caution is necessary:

(i)

The nonlinearity considered in

is

[
14
]
the problematic term in (

Thus, when

,

is well-defined and can be estimated in terms of norms of the solution. Notably, the proof of Theorem 4.1 in

]
requires

(ii)

Furthermore, in

]
the authors obtain results for

. These, however, rely on the additional assumption that

on
\{u=0\}
(see Remark 2.9 in
[

]
) as a way to prevent problematic terms involving the integral

over the set

from playing a role in the analysis (see, for example, Equation (4.9) in

). Observe that this difficulty was not present in

For these reasons, the techniques developed in this section (and more in general, the main results of this paper) focus on the case

and differ from some of the arguments in

]
even when

Throughout the rest, we assume that

Lemma 5.3

Lemma 5.4

Arguing by contradiction, assume that

-\frac{2}{H(r)}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}(X\cdot\nabla u)v\,dX\geq-C(N_{0}(r)+r^{2}).

Next, consider the field

G(X)\coloneqq(xv_{y}(X)f(x,u(X)),-(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v(X))f(x,u(X)))

and notice that the divergence theorem applied to
G
yields the identity

(5.20)

\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)f(x,u)\,dx=\frac{1}{r}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}(y(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)-|x|^{2}v_{y})f(x,u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\\
+\int_{B_{r}^{+}}[nf(x,u)v_{y}+(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}f(x,u))v_{y}+(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u)\partial_{\zeta}f(x,u)v_{y}-(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)\partial_{\zeta}f(x,u)u_{y}]\,dX.

We proceed by estimating the terms on the right-hand side of (
5.20
) separately. In the following estimates we rely on the fact that
1\leq y^{b}
for
b\leq 0
. To begin, observe that arguing as in (
5.8
) and applying Young’s inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{r}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}(y(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)-|x|^{2}v_{y})f(x,u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cr\int_{S_{r}^{+}}(|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v|+|v_{y}|)|u|^{p-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\left(r|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{u^{2}}{r}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

where for
p>2
the constant
C
in the last inequality depends also on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}^{p-2}
. Consequently, in view of (
5.14
), for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r<1
we have that

(5.21)

\left|\frac{1}{r}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}(y(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)-|x|^{2}v_{y})f(x,u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right|\leq C(r^{2}D_{0}^{\prime}(r)+H(r)).

The second integral on the right-hand side of (
5.20
) can be estimated in a similar fashion. Indeed, by (
H.5′
) we have that for every
R<1
there exists a constant
C>0
, possibly depending on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}^{+})}
, such that

|\nabla_{x}^{\prime}f(x,u(X))|\leq C|u(X)|\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|\partial_{\zeta}f(x,u(X))|\leq C

holds for
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
-a.e.
X\in B_{R}^{+}
. Then, for all
r<R<1
we have that

(5.22)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{r}^{+}}nf(x,u)v_{y}\,dX\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(rD_{0}(r)+H(r)),

(5.23)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{r}^{+}}(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u)\partial_{\zeta}f(x,u)v_{y}-(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)\partial_{\zeta}f(x,u)u_{y})\,dX\right|

\displaystyle\leq CrD_{0}(r),

(5.24)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{r}^{+}}(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}f(x,u))v_{y}\,dX\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cr(D_{0}(r)+rH(r)).

Combining the estimate in (
5.21
) with those in (
5.22
), (
5.23
), and (
5.24
), and recalling the identity in (
5.20
) we have that

(5.25)

-\frac{2}{H(r)}\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)f(x,u)\,dx\geq-C\left(\frac{r^{2}D_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}+1+N_{0}(r)\right)

holds for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r<r_{0}
. Furthermore, by
5.2
, the remaining term on the right-hand side of (
5.18
) can be bounded from below as follows:

(5.26)

\frac{2rP(r)D(r)}{H(r)^{2}}\geq-\frac{2r|P(r)|D_{0}(r)}{H(r)^{2}}\geq-Cr^{-b}(N_{0}(r)^{2}+N_{0}(r)).

Finally, substituting (
5.19
), (
5.25
), and (
5.26
) into (
5.18
) shows that

(5.27)

N_{0}^{\prime}(r)\geq-C\left(1+N_{0}(r)+N_{0}(r)^{2}+\frac{r^{2}D_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}\right)

holds for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r<r_{0}
.

Step 3:
In view of (
5.16
) and (
5.13
), for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r<r_{0}
such that
N_{0}^{\prime}(r)\leq 0
, we have that

\frac{rD_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}\leq(n+b-1)\frac{D_{0}(r)}{H(r)}+\frac{2rD_{0}(r)D(r)}{H(r)^{2}}.

This, together with
5.2
, implies that for every such
r
we have

\displaystyle\frac{rD_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}

\displaystyle\leq(n+b-1)\frac{D_{0}(r)}{H(r)}+\frac{2rD_{0}(r)^{2}}{H(r)^{2}}+\frac{Cr^{1-b}D_{0}(r)(rD_{0}(r)+H(r))}{H(r)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(n+b-1+Cr^{1-b})\frac{D_{0}(r)}{H(r)}+(2+Cr^{1-b})\frac{rD_{0}(r)^{2}}{H(r)^{2}},

and therefore we have that

(5.28)

\frac{r^{2}D_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}\leq C(N_{0}(r)+N_{0}(r)^{2}).

Substituting (
5.28
) into (
5.27
) yields

(5.29)

\frac{N_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{1+N_{0}(r)+N_{0}(r)^{2}}\geq-C.

We remark that while (
5.29
) was obtained under the assumption that
N_{0}^{\prime}(r)\leq 0
, the estimate remains true also when
N_{0}^{\prime}(r)>0
. Hence we conclude that (
5.29
) holds for almost all
r\in(0,r_{0})
. Next, observe that (
5.29
) implies

(5.30)

\frac{N_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{(1+N_{0}(r))^{2}}\geq-C.

Integrating (
5.30
) over
(r_{1},r_{2})\subset(0,r_{0})
yields

C(r_{1}-r_{2})\leq\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{N_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{(1+N_{0}(r))^{2}}\,dr=\frac{1}{1+N_{0}(r_{1})}-\frac{1}{1+N_{0}(r_{2})},

which can be rewritten as

(5.31)

-\frac{1}{1+N_{0}(r_{1})}+Cr_{1}\leq-\frac{1}{1+N_{0}(r_{2})}+Cr_{2}.

Observe that (
5.31
) readily implies that
N_{0}
admits a nonnegative limit as
r\to 0^{+}
; therefore, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Notably, nearly the entire proof of
5.6
remains valid for
b>0
, with the exception of the estimates in (
5.20
). In essence, since it does not seem possible to bound the term

\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}(x\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}v)\partial_{y}^{b}v\,dx

in terms of
D_{0}(r)
and
H(r)
, we used our boundary conditions to replace
y^{b}\partial_{y}v
with
f(\cdot,u)
on
B_{r}^{\prime}
and proceeded by bounding the resulting integral in terms of Sobolev norms of
f(\cdot,u)
in
B_{r}^{+}
. However, after this substitution, it is unclear whether (or how) the degenerate weight
y^{b}
, for
b>0
, can be reintroduced in the estimates. Achieving this would likely require a different approach.

The rest of the analysis is facilitated by the introduction of an opportunely rescaled version of the function
H
, namely

(5.32)

h(r)\coloneqq\frac{H(r)}{r^{n+b}}=\frac{1}{r^{n+b}}\int_{S_{r}^{+}}y^{b}(u^{2}+v^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

The following result is inspired by its counterparts in previous investigations (see, for example, Lemma 2.15 in
[
22
]
).

Lemma 5.8
.

Under the assumptions of
5.6
, let
h
be as in (
5.32
) and
\mu
as in (
5.15
). Assume that
\mu<\infty
. Then the following hold:

(i)

for every
0<r<R<r_{0}
, we have that

r^{-2\mu}h(r)\leq R^{-2\mu}h(R)e^{C(R-r)},

where
C
is a constant that depends on on
\mu
and on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}^{p-2}
(when
p>2
). In particular,
h(r)=\mathcal{O}(r^{2\mu})
as
r\to 0^{+}
.

(ii)

For every
\delta>0
there exists
r_{\delta}>0
such that for every
0<r<R<r_{\delta}
, we have

R^{-2\mu}h(R)\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\delta}\leq r^{-2\mu}h(r).

In particular, there exists a constant
C=C(\delta)
such that
h(r)\geq C(\delta)r^{2\mu+\delta}
for all
0<r<r_{\delta}
.

Proof.

Observe that, in view of (
5.13
), for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r<1
we have that

h^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2D(r)}{r^{n+b}}.

Moreover, since for every such
r<r_{0}
we have that
H(r)>0
(see
5.4
), direct computations show that

(5.33)

\frac{d}{dr}\log(r^{-2\mu}h(r))=\frac{2}{r}\left(N_{0}(r)-\mu+\frac{rP(r)}{H(r)}\right).

We now proceed by estimating the right-hand of (
5.33
), thus obtaining differential inequalities that can be integrated. To this end, observe that by
5.6
, for every
r\in(0,r_{0})
we have that

(5.34)

N_{0}(r)-\mu\geq-\frac{Cr(1+\mu)^{2}}{1+Cr(1+\mu)}.

Combining (
5.33
) with (
5.34
) and by an application of
5.2
we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\log(r^{-2\mu}h(r))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{r}\left(N_{0}(r)-\mu-Cr^{1-b}(N_{0}(r)+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{r}\left((1-Cr^{1-b})N_{0}(r)-(1-Cr^{1-b})\mu-Cr^{1-b}(1+\mu)\right)

(5.35)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{r}\left(-\frac{(1-Cr^{1-b})Cr(1+\mu)^{2}}{1+Cr(1+\mu)}-Cr^{1-b}(1+\mu)\right).

Hence, we conclude that

\frac{d}{dr}\log(r^{-2\mu}h(r))\geq-C

for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r<r_{0}
, where
C
is a constant that depends on both the frequency
\mu
and on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}^{p-2}
when
p>2
. Integrating this inequality over
(r,R)\subset(0,r_{0})
yields

r^{-2\mu}h(r)\leq R^{-2\mu}h(R)e^{C(R-r)}.

To prove the second part of the statement, observe that for every
\delta>0
there exists
r_{\delta}
such that
N_{0}(r)-\mu\leq\delta/4
for all
r<r_{\delta}
. Consequently, we obtain that

\frac{d}{dr}\log(r^{-2\mu}h(r))\leq\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{\delta}{4}+Cr^{1-b}(\delta+\mu+1)\right).

Hence, possibly replacing
r_{\delta}
with a smaller number, we conclude that

\frac{d}{dr}\log(r^{-2\mu}h(r))\leq\frac{\delta}{r}.

As above, integrating this inequality over
(r,R)\subset(0,r_{\delta})
yields

R^{-2\mu}h(R)\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\delta}\leq r^{-2\mu}h(r).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Under the assumptions of
5.8
, there exists a constant
C
such that for every
r<R
we have that

\max\left\{\inf_{S_{r}^{+}}|u|,\inf_{S_{r}^{+}}|v|\right\}\leq Cr^{\mu}.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, assume that there exist a sequence
r_{j}\to 0^{+}
and constants
C_{j}\to\infty
such that

\max\left\{\inf_{S_{r_{j}}^{+}}|u|,\inf_{S_{r_{j}}^{+}}|v|\right\}\geq C_{j}r_{j}^{\mu}.

Then we have

r_{j}^{-2\mu}h(r_{j})\geq\frac{1}{r_{j}^{n+b+2\mu}}\int_{S_{r_{j}}^{+}}y^{b}\left(\max\left\{\inf_{S_{r_{j}}^{+}}|u|,\inf_{S_{r_{j}}^{+}}|v|\right\}\right)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\geq C(n,b)C_{j}^{2}.

Letting
j\to\infty
gives a contradiction with
5.8

(i)
.
∎

We conclude the section by showing that a version of statement
(i)
in
5.8
continues to hold even when the frequency is infinite. This result plays an important role later in our study of the free boundary (see, in particular, the proof of
8.2
).

Lemma 5.10
.

Under the assumptions of
5.6
, let
h
be as in (
5.32
) and
\mu
as in (
5.15
). Assume that
\mu=\infty
. Then, for every
M>0
there exists
r_{M}>0
such that for every
0<r<R<r_{M}
, we have

r^{-2M}h(r)\leq R^{-2M}h(R)e^{C(R-r)}

Here
C
is a constant that possibly depends on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{0}}^{\prime})}^{p-2}
when
p>2
.

Proof.

Let
C
be as in
5.2
. Then, for every
M>0
there exists
r_{M}>0
such that

(5.36)

(1-Cr^{1-b})N_{0}(r)\geq M

for all
0<r<r_{M}
. Then in view of (
5.36
), arguing as in the proof of
5.8
and by eventually replacing

with a smaller number (see in particular (

holds for

.

.
∎

6.
Almgren’s blow-up solutions

We recall that, in view of the local character of the analysis we employed, the conclusions of the previous section continue to hold for (sufficiently small) balls centered at
(x_{0},0)
, for any
x_{0}\in B^{\prime}_{1}
. Without loss of generality, throughout this section we continue to assume that
x_{0}=0
.

Let
h
be given as in (
5.32
) and, for
X\in B_{1}^{+}
, define the Almgren’s rescalings

(6.1)

u_{r}(X)\coloneqq\frac{u(rX)}{h(r)^{1/2}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad v_{r}(X)\coloneqq\frac{v(rX)}{h(r)^{1/2}}.

In the following we always assume that
r
is sufficiently small, so that if
u
and
v
are not identically zero, then
h(r)>0
(see
5.4
).

The main result of this section is the study of blow-up profiles for
\{u_{r}\}_{r}
and
\{v_{r}\}_{r}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
. Let
b\leq 0
, and let
N_{0}
be given as in (
5.1
) and
\mu
as in (
5.15
). Suppose
f
satisfies (
H.2′
), (
H.4′
), and (
H.5′
). Assume that
u
is not identically zero, and let the rescaled families
\{u_{r}\}_{r}
and
\{v_{r}\}_{r}\subset H^{1}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})
be defined as in (
6.1
). Finally, assume that
\mu<\infty
and fix
R>0
. Then for every sequence
r_{j}\to 0^{+}
there exist a subsequence (which we do not relabel) and functions
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b})
such that

(6.2)

\displaystyle u_{r_{j}}\to\tilde{u},\quad v_{r_{j}}

\displaystyle\to\tilde{v}\qquad\text{ in }H^{1}(B_{R}^{+};y^{b}),

(6.3)

\displaystyle u_{r_{j}}\to\tilde{u},\quad v_{r_{j}}

\displaystyle\to\tilde{v}\qquad\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha}(B_{R}^{+}),

as
j\to\infty
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Moreover,
\mu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
are homogeneous polynomial of degree
\mu
that cannot simultaneously be identically equal to zero and satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{L}_{b}(\tilde{u})=&0&\text{ in }B_{R}^{+},\\
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\tilde{v})=&0&\text{ in }B_{R}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}\tilde{u}=&0&\text{ on }B_{R}^{\prime},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}\tilde{v}=&0&\text{ on }B_{R}^{\prime}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1:
Fix
R_{0}>\min\{1,R\}
and assume that
r>0
is sufficiently small so that
rR_{0}<\min\{r_{0},r_{1}\}
, where
r_{0}
and
r_{1}
are given as in
5.4
and
5.8

(ii)
, respectively. In particular, we can assume that
H(rR_{0})>0
, that
N_{0}(rR_{0},u,v)\leq\mu+1
, and that

(6.4)

\frac{h(rR_{0})}{h(r)}\leq R_{0}^{2\mu+1}.

Next, by a change of variables and (
6.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{rR_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})\,dX

\displaystyle=r^{n-1+b}h(r)\int_{B_{R_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}(|\nabla u_{r}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{r}|^{2})\,dX,

\displaystyle\int_{S_{rR_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}(u^{2}+v^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=r^{n+b}h(r)\int_{S_{R_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}(u_{r}^{2}+v_{r}^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Consequently, we have that

(6.5)

N_{0}(rR_{0},u,v)=\frac{rR_{0}D_{0}(rR_{0},u,v)}{H(rR_{0},u,v)}=\frac{R_{0}D_{0}(R_{0},u_{r},v_{r})}{H(R_{0},u_{r},v_{r})}=N_{0}(R_{0},u_{r},v_{r}),

and moreover, by (
6.4
), it follows that

(6.6)

H(R_{0},u_{r},v_{r})=R_{0}^{n+b}\frac{h(rR_{0})}{h(r)}\leq R_{0}^{n+b+2\mu+1}.

Combining (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), we conclude that

(6.7)

\int_{B_{R_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}(|\nabla u_{r}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{r}|^{2})\,dX=\frac{1}{R_{0}}N_{0}(rR_{0},u,v)H(R_{0},u_{r},v_{r})\leq(\mu+1)R_{0}^{n+b+2\mu}.

Additionally, by
2.1
, together with (
6.6
) and (
6.7
), we obtain

\int_{B_{R_{0}}^{+}}y^{b}(u_{r}^{2}+v_{r}^{2})\,dX\leq C(n,b,\mu)R_{0}^{n+b+2\mu+2}.

This shows that the families
\{u_{r}\}_{r}
and
\{v_{r}\}_{r}
are bounded in
H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})
, provided that
r
is sufficiently small. In particular, for every sequence
\{r_{j}\}_{j}
,
j\geq 2
, such that
r_{j}\to 0^{+}
, there exist a subsequence (which we do not relabel) and functions
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})
such that
u_{r_{j}}\to\tilde{u}
and
v_{r_{j}}\to\tilde{v}
strongly in
L^{2}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})
(see, for example,
[
32
]
), strongly in
L^{2}(S_{1}^{+};y^{b})
(see
2.2
), and weakly in
H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})
. Throughout the remainder of the proof, we continue to work with this subsequence; however, we choose to omit the subscript
j
to keep the notation as simple as possible. In particular, the convergence of the traces obtained above implies that

(6.8)

\int_{S_{1}^{+}}y^{b}(\tilde{u}^{2}+\tilde{v}^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{S_{1}^{+}}y^{b}(u_{r}^{2}+v_{r}^{2})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}=1,

thus proving that
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}
are not both identically zero.

Step 2:
We now improve the convergence obtained in the previous step. To this end, notice that
u_{r}
and
v_{r}
are weak solutions to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{b}u_{r}=&r^{2}v_{r}&\text{ in }B_{R_{0}}^{+},\\
\Delta_{b}v_{r}=&0&\text{ in }B_{R_{0}}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}u_{r}=&0&\text{ on }B_{R_{0}}^{\prime},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}v_{r}=&\tilde{f}(\cdot,u_{r})&\text{ on }B_{R_{0}}^{\prime},\end{array}\right.

where

\tilde{f}(x,u_{r}(x,0))\coloneqq\frac{r^{1-b}}{h(r)^{1/2}}f(rx,h(r)^{1/2}u_{r}(x,0)).

Observe that by (
H.2′
),
5.8

(i)
, and using that by assumption
p\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C
(independent of
r
) such that

(6.9)

|\tilde{f}(x,u_{r}(x,0))|\leq Cr^{1-b}|u_{r}(x,0)|^{p-1}.

Furthermore, by following the circle of ideas in the proof of
4.5
, for every
q\in[1,\infty)
and every
R_{1}\in(R,R_{0})
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,b,q,R_{0},R_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{r}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R_{1}}^{+};y^{b})}+\|u_{r}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R_{1}}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{r}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v_{r}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})}\right),

\displaystyle\|v_{r}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R_{1}}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v_{r}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R_{1}}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{r}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})}+\|v_{r}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{0}}^{+};y^{b})}\right).

In view of (
6.9
), combining these estimates with the results of the previous step we find that for every
q\in[1,\infty)
there exists a constant
C=C(n,b,q,R_{0},R_{1},\mu)
such that

(6.10)

\|v_{r}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R_{1}}^{+})}+\|\tilde{f}(\cdot,u_{r}(\cdot,0))\|_{L^{q}(B_{R_{1}}^{\prime})}\leq C.

Now, if
b<0
and
q
is chosen to be sufficiently large, (
6.10
) allows us to apply
2.8
and
2.10

(iii)
to conclude that
\{u_{r}\}_{r}
and
\{v_{r}\}_{r}
are bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(B_{R})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Similarly, if
b=0
, fix
R_{2}\in(R,R_{1})
. Then
2.8
and
2.10

(iii)
can be applied to show that
\{v_{r}\}_{r}
is bounded in
C^{0,\alpha}(B_{R_{2}}^{+})
and that
\{u_{r}\}_{r}
is bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(B_{R_{2}}^{+})
, respectively. To proceed, arguing as in the proof of
4.7
, we further deduce that
\{v_{r}\}_{r}
is also bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(B_{R}^{+})
. Thus, (
6.2
) and (
6.3

Step 3:
Finally, observe that by Corollary

(i)

Consider the Weiss-type functional

Lemma 7.1

Proof.

\displaystyle W_{\mu}(r,u,v)

\displaystyle D_{0}(r,w_{1}+p,w_{2}+q)

8.
The free boundary: regularity and structural properties

In this section we undertake the study of the free boundary for non-trivial solutions to (
3.1
). We begin by recalling that in the context of this paper, the free boundary is the interface that separates the two phases
\{u>0\}
and
\{u<0\}
in the thin space. To be precise, the free boundary is defined as

F(u)\coloneqq\left(\partial\{u>0\}\cup\partial\{u<0\}\right)\cap B_{1}^{\prime};

its properties are classically analyzed by splitting
F(u)
into two disjoint subsets, namely

R(u)\coloneqq\{X\in F(u):\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u(X)\neq 0\}

and

S(u)\coloneqq F(u)\setminus R(u).

We will refer to these subsets as the
regular
and the
singular
part of

, respectively.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
. Then, in a neighborhood of any point in
R(u)
, the free boundary
F(u)
coincides with the graph of a
C^{3,\alpha}
function if
b\leq 0
and a
C^{2,\alpha}
function if
b>0
.

Proof.

Let
(x,0)\in R(u)
be given. Then, by definition of

there exists

such that
\partial_{x_{i}}u(x,0)\neq 0
. Without loss of generality, assume that
i=n
. But then, by the Implicit Function Theorem, there exist open sets
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
V\subset\mathbb{R}
with
x\in U\times V
and a function
\psi\colon U\to V
such that

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}))=x,

and with the property that

F(u)\cap(U\times V\times\{0\})=\{(\hat{x},\psi(\hat{x}),0):\hat{x}\in U\}.

In particular,
F(u)
is locally described by the graph of
\psi
. Since
\psi
inherits the regularity of

, the conclusion follows from
4.6
and
4.8
.
∎

The remainder of the section focuses on the study of the singular part. Given

We recall that

5.6
; moreover, if the limit is finite, then it must be a non-negative integer by
6.1
. We then define

S_{\mu}(u)=\{X_{0}\in S(u):N_{0}^{X_{0}}(0^{+})=\mu\},\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\}.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
u

3.1

(or equivalently, that
N_{0}^{\overline{X}}(0^{+})=\mu
). Arguing by contradiction, assume that
N_{0}^{\overline{X}}(0^{+})=\overline{\mu}<\mu
. Then, by
7.4
, we get that for all
r
sufficiently small

cr^{2\overline{\mu}}\leq h^{\overline{X}}(r).

However, this is in contradiction with (
8.3
). Similarly, if
\mu<\overline{\mu}<\infty
, then it follows from
5.8

(i)
(see also (
7.15
)) that

h^{\overline{X}}(r)\leq Cr^{2\overline{\mu}},

and we have again reached a contradiction with (
8.3
). Finally, if
\overline{\mu}=\infty
, fix
M>\mu
; then by an application of
5.10
we find a constant
C
such that

h^{\overline{X}}(r)\leq Cr^{2M}

holds for every
r
sufficiently small. Reasoning as above, we reach the desired conclusion.
∎

For
X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u)
,
\mu<\infty
, the
homogeneous rescalings
of
u
and
v
at
X_{0}
(defined analogously to (
7.12
)) are given by

u_{\mu,r}^{X_{0}}(X)\coloneqq\frac{u(X_{0}+rX)}{r^{\mu}},\qquad v_{\mu,r}^{X_{0}}(X)\coloneqq\frac{v(X_{0}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}.

Similarly, we use
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
and
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
to denote the corresponding blow-up limits (see
7.6
).
Finally, the Monneau functional centered at
X_{0}
is defined as

M_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(r,u,v,p,q)\coloneqq M_{\mu}(r,u(X_{0}+\cdot),v(X_{0}+\cdot),p(\cdot),q(\cdot)).

With these definitions at hand, we proceed to prove that the polynomials
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
and
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
depend continuously on
X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u)
.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of
Definition

3.1
. Let
b\leq 0
,
\mu
as in (
5.15
),
n=2,3
, and suppose
f
satisfies (
H.2′
), (
H.4′
), and (
H.5′
). Assume that
u
is not identically zero. Then, for every
\mu<\infty
, we have that the map
X_{0}\mapsto(\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})
is continuous from
S_{\mu}(u)
to
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\times\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. Moreover, for every compact
K\subset S_{\mu}(u)

\|u_{\mu,r}^{X_{0}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}+\|v_{\mu,r}^{X_{0}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\to 0

as
r\to 0^{+}
, uniformly with respect to
X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u)
. In particular, there exists a modulus of continuity
\sigma_{K}
such that

\displaystyle|u(X)-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(X-X_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{K}(|X-X_{0}|)|X-X_{0}|^{\mu},

\displaystyle|v(X)-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(X-X_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{K}(|X-X_{0}|)|X-X_{0}|^{\mu},

for all
X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u)
and all
X\in\overline{B_{1}^{+}}\setminus\overline{S_{1}^{+}}
. Finally, the result continues to hold for
n\geq 4
, provided that
\mu<4/(n-3)
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1:
Let
\varepsilon>0
be given and fix
X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u)
. Recalling that
M_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(0^{+},u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})=0
(see, for example, (
7.16
)), we can find
r_{\varepsilon}=r_{\varepsilon}(X_{0})
such that

M_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(r,u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})<\varepsilon

for all
r\leq(0,r_{\varepsilon}]
. Using the fact that
u
and
v
are Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
, there exists
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}(X_{0},\mu,\alpha)
such that for every
\overline{X}\in S_{\mu}(u)
with
|\overline{X}-X_{0}|<\rho_{\varepsilon}
, we have that

\displaystyle M_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(r_{\varepsilon},u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})<3\varepsilon.

By
7.3
, assuming without loss that
r_{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon
, we have that

(8.4)

M_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(r,u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})\leq M_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(r_{\varepsilon},u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})+C(r_{\varepsilon}-r)\leq C\varepsilon

holds for all
r\in(0,r_{\varepsilon})
. In particular, this implies that

(8.5)

\int_{S_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\left((\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})^{2}+(\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})^{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}M_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(r,u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})\leq C\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, this proves the first part of the statement.

Step 2:
Next, let
K
be a compact subset of
S_{\mu}(u)
and fix
X_{0}\in K
. We claim that if
r_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}
are chosen as in the previous step, then there exists a constant
C
such that for
r\in(0,r_{\varepsilon})
and every
\overline{X}
with
|\overline{X}-X_{0}|<\rho_{\varepsilon}
we have that

(8.6)

\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\left((u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}})^{2}+(v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}})^{2}\right)\,dX\leq C\varepsilon.

Indeed, by the coarea formula together with a change of variables, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\left((u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}})^{2}+(v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}})^{2}\right)\,dX

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S_{\rho}^{+}}y^{b}\left[\left(\frac{u(\overline{X}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(X)\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{v(\overline{X}+rX)}{r^{\mu}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(X)\right)^{2}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{n+b+2\mu}}\int_{S_{\rho r}^{+}}y^{b}\left[(u(\overline{X}+X)-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(X))^{2}+(v(\overline{X}+X)-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(X))^{2}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}d\rho

(8.7)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}2\rho^{n+b+2\mu}\left(M_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}(\rho r,u,v,\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}},\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})+\int_{S_{1}^{+}}y^{b}\left((\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})^{2}+(\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}})^{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)\,d\rho.

As one can readily check, (
8.6
) follows by substituting (
8.4
) and (
8.5
) into (
8.7
). Next, observe that for any
\overline{X}=(\bar{x},0)\in S_{\mu}(u)
we have that

(8.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{b}\left(u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\right)=&r^{2}v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}&\text{ in }B_{1}^{+},\\
\Delta_{b}\left(v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\right)=&0&\text{ in }B_{1}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}\left(u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\right)=&0&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime},\\
\partial_{y}^{b}\left(v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\right)=&\bar{f}(\cdot,u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}})&\text{ on }B_{1}^{\prime},\end{array}\right.

where

\bar{f}(x,u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}(x,0))\coloneqq r^{1-b-\mu}f(\bar{x}+rx,r^{\mu}u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}(x,0)).

For any
q>(n+1)/2
, an application of
2.10
yields the existence of a positive constant
C
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

(8.9)

\|u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{3/4}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})}+\|r^{2}v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})}\right).

By inspection in the proof of
5.8

(i)
, we can find
C
such that

h^{\overline{X}}(r)\leq Cr^{2\mu}

for all
\overline{X}\in S_{\mu}(u)\in\overline{B_{R}^{+}}
,
R<1
. In turn, we have that

\displaystyle\|r^{2}v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1}^{+};y^{b})}^{q}

\displaystyle=r^{2}\int_{B_{1}^{+}}y^{b}|v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}|^{q}\,dX

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{r^{n+1+b+q\mu}}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{S_{\rho}^{+}}y^{b}|v(\overline{X}+X)|^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cr^{2}}{r^{n+1+b+q\mu}}\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{n+b}h^{\overline{X}}(\rho)\,d\rho

(8.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cr^{2}}{r^{n+1+b+q\mu}}\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{n+b+2\mu}\,d\rho\leq Cr^{2-\mu(q-2)},

where
C
is a constant that depends on
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}^{+})}^{q-2}
when
q>2
. Observe that the right-hand side of (
8.10
) can be made arbitrarily small, provided that
2-\mu(q-2)>0
. In particular, if
n=2
then
q=2
has all the required properties and the desired result readily follows, while if
n=3
, we can choose

q\coloneqq 2+\frac{1}{1+\mu}>2.

Finally, if
n\geq 4
, since by assumption we have that
\mu<4/(n-3)
, then we can find
q\in((2+2\mu)/2,(n+1)/2)
. This shows that there exists
C=C(\varepsilon)
, independent of
\overline{X}
and
r
such that
C(\varepsilon)\to 0^{+}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
and with the property that

(8.11)

\|u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{+})}\leq C(\varepsilon)

for all
r\in(0,r_{\varepsilon})
.
Similarly, applying
2.8
for the equation satisfied by
v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}
(see (
8.8
)), we obtain that

(8.12)

\|v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|v_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{3/4}^{+};y^{b})}+\|\bar{f}(\cdot,u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{\prime})}\right).

Notice also that by (
H.2′
) there exists a constant
C
(independent of
r
and
\overline{X}
) such that

(8.13)

|\bar{f}(x,u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}(x,0))|\leq Cr^{1-b+\mu(p-2)}|u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}(x,0)|^{p-1}.

In particular, we have that

(8.14)

\|\bar{f}(\cdot,u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{\prime})}\leq Cr^{1-b+\mu(p-2)}\Big(\|u_{\mu,r}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{\prime})}\\
+\|\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{\overline{X}}-\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{\prime})}+\|\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{\prime})}\Big)^{p-1}.

Observe that the first term on the right-hand side of (
8.14
) is bounded by (
8.11
), and that the second term in controlled by (
8.5
) in view of the fact that all norms are equivalent on the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
; finally, the last term is bounded by a constant that depends only on
X_{0}
.

Since
K
is compact, the desired result follows by a covering argument. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we define the dimension of the singular set
S_{\mu}(u)
at a point
X_{0}
as

(8.15)

d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\coloneqq\min\left\{d_{u,\mu}^{X_{0}},d_{v,\mu}^{X_{0}}\right\},

where

d_{u,\mu}^{X_{0}}\coloneqq\operatorname{dim}\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(x,0)=0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

and

d_{v,\mu}^{X_{0}}\coloneqq\operatorname{dim}\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}^{\prime}\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(x,0)=0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

Theorem 8.4
.

Under the assumptions of
8.3
, let

(8.16)

S_{\mu}^{d}(u)\coloneqq\{X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u):d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}=d\}.

Then for every
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\leq n-1
. Moreover, for every
d\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
, the set
S_{\mu}^{d}(u)
is contained in a countable union of
d
-dimensional
C^{1}
manifolds.

Proof.

Observe that since
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, we must have that
u\equiv v\equiv 0
on
S_{\mu}(u)
. We begin by proving that
d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\leq n-1
. To this end, arguing by contradiction, assume that
d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}=n
. In particular, this assumption implies that
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
and
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
vanish identically on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
. Consequently, the polynomials
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
and
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
must also vanish identically on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
. Applying Lemma 2.1.2 in
[
30
]
, we deduce that
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
and
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
are identically zero in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which contradicts
7.6
.

Next, notice that by
8.3
we have that

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(X-X_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma(|X-X_{0}|)|X-X_{0}|^{\mu},

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(X-X_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma(|X-X_{0}|)|X-X_{0}|^{\mu}

for all
X,X_{0}\in S_{\mu}(u)
. Assume first that
d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}=d_{u,\mu}^{X_{0}}
and observe that, if this is the case, then
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
is necessarily non-trivial. The desired result then readily follows by arguing as in the proof of Theorem 1.3.8 in
[
30
]
. On the other hand, if
d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}=d_{v,\mu}^{X_{0}}
, then can repeat the same argument for the non-trivial polynomial
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

We conclude the section with remarks and open problems.

(i)

It is worth noting that the structure of the set
S_{1}(u)
for the fourth-order problem under consideration may differ significantly from its second-order counterpart, where points with frequency
\mu=1
are all regular. Indeed, in our case, we must still consider the scenario where
\mu=1
and
\nabla_{x}^{\prime}u(X_{0})=0
, thereby proving that
\tilde{u}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}\equiv 0
. This, in turn, implies that
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
cannot vanish identically. Since
\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}
is independent of
y
due to its regularity, we can find
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\tilde{v}_{\mu}^{X_{0}}(X)=a\cdot x.

(ii)

In view of
8.4
, it remains to analyze the structure of the singular sets
S_{0}(u)
and
S_{\infty}(u)
. Observe that if
\mu=0
, we have
d_{\mu}^{X_{0}}=n
. Therefore,
S_{0}(u)=S_{0}^{n}(u)
is trivially contained in a manifold of dimension
n
. Therefore, while
8.4
in a sense continues to hold also for
\mu=0
, it does not provide any valuable information on the structure of
S_{0}(u)
.
Similarly, whether the value
\mu=\infty
is admissible remains unclear, and the analysis of
S_{\infty}(u)
is entirely open, likely requiring very different techniques.

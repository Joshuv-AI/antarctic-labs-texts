Let’s chat (GPT) about adolescent idiopathic scoliosis: accuracy and reliability of chat responses to frequently asked questions
Authors: Esra Giray, Merve Damla Korkmaz, Ozge Gulsum Illeez, Nalan Capan, Evrim Karadag Saygi, Ayse Resa Aydin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients increasingly seek online medical information, with artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots like ChatGPT emerging as potential resources for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS); however, their accuracy and reliability need assessment. This study aimed to evaluate the accuracy and reliability of ChatGPT, an AI model, in answering questions related to AIS.MethodsSixty-four questions across four categories (general information, diagnosis and screening, treatment and follow-up, and quality of life [QoL]) were adapted from FAQs on professional association websites, the SOSORT consensus article, and QoL questionnaires. Two reviewers rated ChatGPT’s responses on a scale from 1 (correct and comprehensive) to 4 (completely incorrect). Descriptive statistics were calculated to demonstrate the percentages of responses per score as well as the percentages of scores across categories. Each question was entered twice to assess reliability and response similarity. The percentage of responses that differed when the same query was entered twice into the system was calculated. The Cohen’s Kappa statistic was utilized to assess the level of agreement between the two reviewers.ResultsOf all the responses, 53.1% were rated as “correct and comprehensive,” while 34.4% were rated as “correct but not comprehensive.” ChatGPT performed best in the QoL category, with 13 out of 15 (86.7%) responses rated as correct. The second-best performance was in the diagnosis and screening category, with 7 out of 13 (53.8%) correct responses, followed by the general information category, with 9 out of 17 (52.9%) correct responses. The lowest performance was in the treatment and follow-up category, with 5 out of 19 (26.3%) correct responses. Consistency in ChatGPT’s responses when questions were entered twice was 76.6%. Agreement between the reviewers’ scores was excellent, as indicated by Cohen’s Kappa statistic (Kappa: 0.82, 95% CI: 0.59 to 1.04; p = 0.0001).ConclusionsChatGPT demonstrated strong accuracy in addressing questions related to QoL in AIS, but its accuracy in treatment-related areas remains insufficient. Therefore, patients and parents are advised to consult medical professionals rather than rely solely on AI-generated information for AIS treatment and management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09315-2.

Background

Scoliosis is a three-dimensional structural deformity of the spine, diagnosed if the Cobb angle is ≥ 10° [1]. Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is the most prevalent form of scoliosis, starting in early puberty and affecting 1–3% of children in the at-risk population of those aged 10–16 years, with young women being more commonly affected. It can lead to reduced quality of life, mobility issues, cardiopulmonary impairment, and back pain in adulthood [2–4]. In parallel with the increasing digital engagement of both adolescents and their caregivers, particularly in seeking health-related information, artificial intelligence (AI)–powered language models have emerged as widely used tools. Platforms such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT, Google’s Gemini, and China-based DeepSeek are capable of generating coherent, human-like responses to medical queries. These tools are frequently utilized by adolescents and middle-aged adults, including parents searching for guidance regarding their children’s health. Given the high prevalence of AIS during adolescence and the digital habits of these populations, such AI tools are expected to become key sources of scoliosis-related information and support.

ChatGPT, developed by OpenAI, is an advanced generative language model based on deep learning. Launched on November 30, 2022, it became publicly accessible to anyone with a free account [5]. Within 64 days of its release, it reached 100 million users and quickly gained recognition for its ability to produce near-human-quality text on a wide range of topics [6]. Despite its popularity, ChatGPT was not specifically designed for healthcare, and its suitability for providing accurate and consistent medical information remains under investigation.

The use of AI models in medicine has been expanding. One recent study demonstrated that ChatGPT achieved a passing score on questions modeled after the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE), which assesses the ability to apply medical knowledge and concepts in clinical scenarios typical of the licensure process for physicians in the United States [7]. Additionally, ChatGPT has shown promising results in addressing frequently asked questions on cardiovascular disease, liver cancer, cervical cancer, and bariatric surgery [5, 8–10]. In the field of spine disorders, a recent study by Lambert et al. [11] evaluated ChatGPT’s responses to a small set of AIS-related questions, primarily focused on surgical treatments. However, there is a lack of research examining both the accuracy and consistency of ChatGPT’s answers across the full spectrum of AIS-related topics. However, no studies have yet examined ChatGPT’s consistency together with its accuracy in answering AIS-related questions. As a large language model, ChatGPT has the potential to respond to patient queries about AIS, but its accuracy and reliability must be evaluated to ensure it is a trustworthy resource. This study aimed to measure the accuracy and reliability of ChatGPT in answering frequently asked questions related to general information, diagnosis and screening, treatment and follow-up, and quality of life (QoL) concerning AIS.

Methods

Study design

The study was designed as an observational, prospective study and was reported in accordance with the STROBE guidelines for observational studies [12]. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Clinical trial number: not applicable.

Question curation/data source

We collected the questions by following a methodology similar to previous studies investigating the accuracy of ChatGPT in medical decision-making and referred to the protocol described by Hermann et al. [9] in our methods. Questions were obtained from the Frequently Asked Questions pages from the Italian Scientific Spine Institute (ISICO) website and the Scoliosis Research Society (SRS)’s professional society handbook for patients [13, 14]. Also, additional frequently asked questions were searched and identified from several web sources [15, 16]. In developing quality of life-related questions, we referenced established and widely recognized instruments, including the Scoliosis Research Society-22 (SRS-22), Brace Questionnaire, and Italian Spine Youth Quality of Life (ISYQoL) questionnaires. These instruments were chosen for their frequent citation in the literature and broad application across both surgical and nonsurgical treatment approaches, capturing quality of life across all treatment stages, including surgery, bracing, and observation, in both clinical and research settings [17–20]. Questions were reviewed, chosen, formulated, and authorized by all the authors for consideration in the study. Duplicate questions from sources were eliminated (Fig. 1). Questions requiring subjective or personalized responses were not converted to a generic language format. Instead, they were intentionally phrased in a conversational tone and written in the first person to resemble how users—typically adolescents with AIS or their caregivers—might input their queries into the ChatGPT interface [9, 10]. A total of 64 questions were then subcategorized into four groups: (1) General information (n = 17), (2) Diagnosis and screening (n = 13), (3) Treatment and follow-up (n = 19), and (4) Quality of life (n = 15) (Table 1).

ChatGPT

The questions were entered into ChatGPT version 3.5; we did not assess the ChatGPT Plus subscription option, which utilizes version 4.0, as it was not fully free, and it is anticipated that patients also may not have access to the full version. The model was not specifically trained on AIS or similar specialized medical conditions to ensure that the queries reflect real-world use of the AI system, such as when a teenager or parent enters questions into ChatGPT. This approach was chosen to ensure that the queries reflect real-world interactions with the AI system, providing a more realistic assessment of its performance in answering questions related to AIS [9].

Response generation

Each question was independently entered into a new chat window in ChatGPT’s GPT-3.5 model or a new tab, and its answer was recorded in a table format. Responses are recorded as Response 1 and Response 2. The similarity of responses is assessed to assess the reliability of these two responses.

Question grading

To assess the accuracy of ChatGPT responses, the data was exported and sent to two reviewers to evaluate the precision of the responses. They independently reviewed and scored responses by ChatGPT. We utilized the scoring system outlined in previously published ChatGPT literature as follows [5, 9, 10]:

We followed the method outlined by Hermann et al. [9], considering the responses in response 1 for scoring purposes. Only the first response was graded if the responses were similar in accuracy (Supplementary file 1). Finally, scores for each question were compared and discussed, and if the initial two numeric scores did not match, the answers were reviewed by an additional reviewer to resolve the discrepancy. If agreement on the numeric score was not reached among at least two reviewers after the third reviewer, the answer was then reviewed by a fourth reviewer. Each question was assigned the final numeric score agreed upon by at least two reviewers. All reviewers were blinded to each other’s scores.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS for Windows, version 20.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). To explore the accuracy of ChatGPT responses, descriptive statistics were calculated to demonstrate the percentages of responses per score as well as the percentages of scores across categories. To assess the reliability of ChatGPT’s responses, the percentage of responses that differed when the same query was entered twice into the system was calculated. The Cohen’s Kappa statistic was utilized to assess the level of agreement between the two reviewers. Cohen’s κ values are interpreted as follows: κ between 0.0 and 0.40 indicates poor agreement, κ = 0.41 to 0.60 indicates moderate agreement, κ = 0.61 to 0.80 indicates substantial agreement, and κ = 0.81 to 0.99 indicates almost perfect agreement [21]. A significance level of P < 0.05 was used for all analyses.

Results

ChatGPT provided correct and comprehensive answers to 34 (53.1%) questions, correct but not comprehensive answers to 22 (34.4%) questions, some correct and some incorrect answers to 6 (9.4%) questions, and completely incorrect answers to 2 (3.1%) questions (Fig. 2). The score breakdown by question category is shown in Fig. 3. ChatGPT responses to QoL questions had the highest proportion of correct and comprehensive answers (86.7%), while treatment and follow-up questions had the lowest (26.3%) (Fig. 3). ChatGPT showed the poorest performance in the general information and treatment and follow-up categories, with the highest proportions of incorrect responses in the general information (17.7%) and treatment and follow-up (21.1%) categories. Consistency was observed in 76.6% (49 out of 64) of responses when the same questions were entered twice. The Kappa statistic indicated almost perfect agreement between reviewers, with a Kappa value of 0.82 (95% CI: 0.59–1.04).

Discussion

According to the study’s results, ChatGPT demonstrated the highest accuracy in responding to questions related to the QoL of patients with AIS. In contrast, the lowest accuracy rates were observed in treatment, follow-up, and general information categories. These findings suggest that while AI technology, such as ChatGPT, can be a valuable tool for providing information on the impact of scoliosis on patients’ lives, its accuracy in treatment-related domains remains limited. Agreement among reviewers was excellent, and responses to repeated ChatGPT queries were substantially consistent, indicating that ChatGPT is reasonably reliable.

As a result of analyzing the answers given to the questions, it has been noted that in some cases, ChatGPT’s answers did not specifically address AIS but included information relevant to syndromic, neuromuscular, and congenital scoliosis without differentiation. For example, when asked about factors affecting progression, it mentioned that conditions like Marfan syndrome and neuromuscular diseases could also influence progression.

ChatGPT stated that in some mild cases, scoliosis curves might improve on their own. In the treatment of spinal curvature, a step-by-step approach is recommended based on the severity of the curvature. Accordingly, observation and scoliosis-specific exercises are suggested for mild curvatures, bracing and scoliosis-specific exercises for moderate curvatures, and bracing or surgical treatment for severe curvatures. Many factors affect the progression of the curvature, including positive family history, flattening of physiological thoracic kyphosis, an angle of trunk rotation exceeding 10 degrees, laxity of skin and joints, and growth spurts. Therefore, the risk of progression increases in the growing spine. However, according to scoliosis guidelines, there is no evidence that spinal curvature can regress without any intervention [1, 14, 22].

Although ChatGPT performs well in answering frequently asked questions, it is important to recognize that understanding the emotional experiences of patients is crucial [23]. These aspects, deeply personal and unique to each individual, may not be fully captured through AI interactions alone. A healthcare professional, with their expertise and experience, can always provide more accurate information and guidance, ensuring personalized care and addressing the nuances that AI cannot fully grasp.

According to guidelines, observation, scoliosis-specific exercises, and bracing are the treatment methods used in the conservative treatment of scoliosis and are preferred according to the degree of curvature. In addition, it is known that other treatment methods such as chiropractic treatment, manual therapy, and physical therapy modalities have no proven effect in the treatment of scoliosis [1]. According to the results of the current study, when the conservative treatment of scoliosis was asked to ChatGPT, the term ‘exercise’ was used instead of specific exercises, and treatment methods such as functional electrical stimulation, neuromuscular electrical stimulation, yoga, pilates, and chiropractic were also recommended. This suggests that the ChatGPT is also reporting methods that have not been fully proven to be effective as a treatment. However, when asked how many years the brace treatment will continue, no clear information was given about the duration of treatment.

Based on the SOSORT consensus, the goal of the treatment of AIS is mostly aesthetics (80%) [1]. However, it was observed that ChatGPT never mentioned aesthetics as the goal of treatment.

When asked whether braces are superior to each other, ChatGPT could not give a clear answer. According to the SOSORT guideline [1], “it is not possible to state with certainty which brace is better than the other” because “not all these studies are directly comparable because there are differences in the eligibility criteria and the main endpoint used to define the results.”

The ambiguity in some of ChatGPT’s responses—such as when asked whether one type of brace is superior to another—may reflect not only limitations of the model but also inconsistencies in the current evidence base and clinical guidelines [1]. For instance, the SOSORT guideline explicitly states that “it is not possible to state with certainty which brace is better than the other” due to differences in study designs, eligibility criteria, and outcome definitions [1]. Similarly, there is variation in scoliosis screening recommendations among organizations: while the Scoliosis Research Society (SRS) supports early screening [24], the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) does not currently recommend routine screening for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis due to insufficient evidence of benefit [25]. Furthermore, the role of scoliosis-specific exercises is emphasized by SOSORT, but this emphasis may not be consistently reflected in other treatment protocols. These inconsistencies in expert opinion and clinical practice guidelines likely contribute to the uncertainty observed in AI-generated responses.

In addition to these, as a positive aspect, ChatGPT has stated that patients should be followed up by their doctors when providing treatment recommendations.

In a study by Chalhoub et al. [26] evaluating the potential of ChatGPT as a substitute for spine surgeons in diagnosing and treating spine diseases, it was reported that patients with spondylolisthesis, stenosis, and scoliosis were misdiagnosed at a rate of 22%. Additionally, 53% of the patients received poor treatment recommendations. Similarly, in the present study, only 26.3% of ChatGPT’s responses to questions related to treatment and follow-up were correct. In a recent study by Lambert et al. [11], the authors examined ChatGPT’s responses to frequently asked questions, focusing primarily on surgical treatments for AIS. They found that ChatGPT often provided incorrect answers, particularly in differentiating and accurately identifying surgical options for AIS. Notably, the authors limited their evaluation to only 10 questions and did not repeat questions to assess response consistency. However, in real-world settings, different individuals may independently ask similar questions, making both the consistency and accuracy of ChatGPT’s responses essential. This highlights the need to evaluate ChatGPT’s reliability in providing consistent and accurate information across a variety of queries and users. Based on these findings, it can be concluded that ChatGPT is not effective in recommending treatment for scoliosis. The performance of ChatGPT in the diagnosis and treatment of many diseases in the health field has been evaluated, and it has been reported that although it is a powerful language model in general, it cannot replace the experience of trained medical professionals. Chang [27] stated that using AI in diagnosis and treatment algorithms can be a risk of not knowing to whom the responsibility will be attributed in case of possible misdiagnosis and malpractice. In the present study, ChatGPT answered 53.8% of the questions about diagnosis and screening and 52.9% of the questions about general information about scoliosis correctly. Even in these two categories with the highest correct response rates, almost half of the questions were answered correctly, which may indicate that ChatGPT’s responses are not yet a reliable substitute for the expertise of experienced medical professionals.

Importantly, ChatGPT was not specifically trained on AIS or similar specialized medical conditions. The general training approach used ensures that the queries reflect real-world interactions with the AI system but may limit the model’s performance on highly specialized queries. The general training of ChatGPT may impact its accuracy in providing detailed and specialized information about AIS, especially in areas such as treatment and follow-up, which require more specific knowledge. Future research could involve training AI models on domain-specific datasets to enhance their performance and reliability in specialized areas. This would improve the model’s ability to deliver accurate and relevant information for conditions like AIS.

The significance of this study lies in its being the first to evaluate both the accuracy and consistency of AI in answering questions related to AIS through a comprehensive assessment. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, the study assessed ChatGPT’s ability to generate systematic information without additional prompts or corrections. It did not evaluate whether different prompts or corrections would alter ChatGPT’s responses. Additionally, the study focused on responses generated by ChatGPT version 3.5. It is worth noting that while this version is accessible to everyone free of charge, the findings may not be generalizable to other versions, such as ChatGPT Plus (version 4.0), which may offer enhanced capabilities.

In conclusion, ChatGPT correctly answered questions related to the QoL of AIS patients but performed less accurately for general information, diagnosis and screening, treatment, and follow-up. This highly popular large language model needs further development and physician consultation before it can be recommended as a patient resource, especially for providing information on the diagnosis and treatment of AIS.

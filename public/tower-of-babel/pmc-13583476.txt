Super-selective distal splenic artery embolization-assisted laparoscopic partial splenectomy for a benign splenic cyst: a case report
Authors: Tze Hee (Zac) Tay, Doruk Seyfi, Muddassir Rashid, Ramesh Damodaran Prabha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSplenic cysts are uncommon, and contemporary management increasingly favors minimally invasive, spleen-preserving approaches. Partial splenectomy provides definitive treatment but may be limited by intraoperative bleeding risk. Selective distal splenic artery embolization (SAE) can reduce arterial inflow, create a parenchymal demarcation plane, and facilitate safer resection. We report the case of an 18-year-old male patient with a recurrent symptomatic splenic cyst who underwent super-selective distal SAE followed by laparoscopic partial splenectomy the following day. Embolization produced a clear revascularized upper-pole segment, enabling precise transection while preserving the remaining spleen. Estimated blood loss was 150 mL, recovery was uneventful, and histopathology confirmed a benign squamous cyst. Follow-up imaging at 3 months demonstrated preserved splenic tissue and no recurrence, with full return to contact sports. To our knowledge, laparoscopic partial splenectomy performed after selective distal SAE for a benign splenic cyst has not previously been described and may expand spleen-preserving options in carefully selected patients.Keywords: Splenectomy, Splenic artery, Spleen

INTRODUCTION

Splenic cysts are rare lesions, occurring in only 0.07%–0.13% of autopsies [1] and representing a small minority of splenic pathology overall [2]. They are often incidental, and intervention is considered for symptomatic, enlarging, or complicated lesions. Contemporary management favors minimally invasive splenic-preservation techniques, which are increasingly being used for benign disease to maintain immune function [3]. Laparoscopic fenestration (deroofing) is feasible for peripheral lesions, but historical series reported high recurrence rates after laparoscopic unroofing/excision [4]. In contrast, partial splenectomy offers definitive resection with a parenchymal margin while preserving viable residual spleen, yet adoption of this practice is limited by the perceived bleeding risk and technical difficulty [3].

Preoperative selective splenic artery embolization (SAE) can reduce arterial inflow, delineate an ischemic demarcation plane, and facilitate a blood-sparing parenchymal transection. This hybrid interventional radiology (IR)–surgical strategy has precedent in noncystic splenic pathology and more recently has been described for cystic disease on a robotic platform with indocyanine green (ICG) perfusion guidance [5,6]. We present a case of super-selective distal SAE followed by laparoscopic partial splenectomy for a large symptomatic splenic cyst, achieving safe resection with splenic preservation.

CASE REPORT

We describe an 18-year-old male patient who sustained blunt abdominal trauma. A computed tomography (CT) scan demonstrated splenomegaly with a large cystic structure (10 cm) in the upper splenic pole containing hyperdense material, concerning for a contained hematoma (Fig. 1A). He was managed conservatively in the intensive care unit and discharged without transfusion and advised to avoid contact sports. He had no prior medical or surgical history.

(A) Initial computed tomography (CT) abdomen portal venous phase at presentation, showing a 10-cm splenic cyst with hyperdense material. (B) Interval CT abdomen portal venous phase 1 month after initial presentation, showing a 12-cm splenic cyst. (C) CT abdomen portal venous phase 1 year later after two separate sessions of sclerotherapy, showing a 5.6-cm splenic cyst.

He was reviewed in the outpatient setting one month after his initial presentation and has intermittent left upper abdominal pain with progression of cyst size to 12 cm on an interval CT scan (Fig. 1B). Over the next year, he underwent two sessions of percutaneous sclerotherapy with alcohol instillation for persistent cyst. Subsequent CT (Fig. 1C) showed interval reduction in cyst dimensions (5.6 cm). Owing to persistent symptoms, desire to return to contact sports, and a refractory splenic cyst, super-selective distal SAE followed by laparoscopic partial splenectomy was undertaken.

Angioembolization was performed one day before surgery. Access was obtained with a right femoral artery puncture and 4-French sheath access. Splenic artery was cannulated with a 4-French Cobra 2 catheter and an angiogram was performed (Fig. 2A). Concurrent CT angiography localized arterial supply to the upper pole, including the short gastric arteries and the superior branch of the splenic artery with visible demarcation plane (Fig. 2B). Super-selective cannulation of the short gastric arteries was embolized with 3- and 4-mm embolization coils, , and the superior branch of the splenic artery was embolized with absorbable gelatin sponge and 6-mm embolization coil. The final angiogram showed preserved perfusion of the lower spleen (Fig. 2C), with approximately 39% of the spleen revascularized.

(B) Concurrent computed tomography angiography localizing arterial supply to the upper pole, including the short gastric arteries and the superior branch of the splenic artery with a visible demarcation plane. The patient went to the operating theatre the following day and was positioned in a lazy right lateral position. A 12-mm infraumbilical Hasson port was utilized to establish pneumoperitoneum and an additional 12-mm port (right paramedian for camera) and two 5-mm ports (right upper quadrant and left upper quadrant) were inserted. A well-demarcated revascularized upper-pole segment was visualized, guiding the resection. Lienorenal and gastrosplenic ligaments were partially taken down with the Thunderbeat energy device (Olympus). Selective ligation of short gastric vessels was performed with Ligaclips (Teleflex). The splenic hilum was identified and preserved. Partial splenectomy was performed using the Thunderbeat energy device and Ligaclips with the cyst intact and unruptured. Hemostasis was ensured and Floseal (Baxter) was applied to the cut surface of the spleen. A 19-French Blake drain (Ethicon) was placed in the perisplenic space and hilum. Bilateral transversus abdominis plane blocks were performed with 40 mL of 0.2% ropivacaine. Estimated blood loss was 150 mL and the operative time was 3 hours. There was no transfusion requirement. The patient was admitted to the high dependency unit for observation overnight and was stepped down to the ward on postoperative day (POD) 1. Oxycodone patient-controlled analgesia was used for analgesia on POD 1 and ceased on POD 2, transitioning to regular paracetamol and celecoxib, and oral oxycodone as required. Drain amylase was negative on POD 2 and the drain was removed. He was discharged on POD 4. Histopathology confirmed a benign squamous cyst. A 3-month postoperative CT scan (Fig. 3) showed no recurrence or complications. At follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic, had returned to full contact sports at 4 months, and demonstrated preserved splenic tissue on imaging without infectious complications.

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, there are no prior reports describing a purely laparoscopic partial splenectomy performed after super-selective distal SAE specifically for a benign splenic cyst. The closest prior report is a robotic technique article published in 2025 that standardized preoperative selective distal embolization followed by ICG-guided robotic partial splenectomy in two cyst cases but did not involve a laparoscopic platform [5]. Earlier literature demonstrates that preoperative selective embolization can enable partial splenectomy for other pathologies (e.g., hamartoma) in open surgery, supporting the physiologic rationale but not addressing cysts or laparoscopy [7].

Although laparoscopic fenestration was considered, the risk of recurrence was significant given the cyst’s parenchymal involvement and prior failed sclerotherapy. Total splenectomy was avoided because of the lifelong risk of overwhelming postsplenectomy infection, particularly in a young patient. A hybrid strategy using super-selective distal embolization followed by partial splenectomy provided a blood-sparing, definitive solution while preserving functional splenic tissue. Distal, super-selective embolization was selected rather than proximal embolization to create a well-defined demarcation plane while preserving perfusion to the lower pole, thereby facilitating a safe parenchymal transection.

This case therefore advances the field by showing that embolization-assisted splenic preservation is feasible with laparoscopy, potentially broadening access beyond robotic centers. In practical terms, super-selective distal SAE simplified dissection, provided a clear avascular demarcation of the transection plane, and facilitated hemostasis, while preserving residual splenic perfusion and function, achieving the key aims of organ-preserving surgery for benign disease [3,5,7].

Beyond the technical success, this case highlights the importance of spleen-preserving surgery in restoring quality of life for young, active patients. This patient’s central goal was to safely return to contact sports, which had been restricted due to the ongoing risk of cyst rupture and hemorrhage. Definitive minimally invasive management allowed him to resume these activities without limitation, providing meaningful improvement in function and lifestyle.

This outcome also underscores the value of a state-of-the-art multidisciplinary service. The coordinated expertise of IR capable of super-selective embolization, high-resolution CT angiographic planning, and advanced minimally invasive surgical capability was essential in delivering a safe hybrid approach that preserved splenic function while achieving definitive cure.

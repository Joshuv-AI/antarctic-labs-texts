New Model for Coal Gas Diffusion Based on the Fractal Tree-Like Bifurcation Network Structure
Authors: Jing Han, Zhen Liu, He Yang, Muyao Zhu, Jialu Lv
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the nonstationary diffusion and transport law of gas in coal, an innovative fractal diffusion model based on fractal theory and a treelike bifurcation network under different diffusion modes were built. In addition, the quantitative relationships among the diffusion coefficient, temperature, pressure, and structural parameters were determined. The model considers the effect of pore tortuosity and connectivity on gas diffusion, which renders it more realistic than the previously presented single-pore diffusion models. Moreover, each parameter in the model has a definite physical meaning and does not contain any empirical constants. The applicability of the new model was verified by experimental data and other model. Subsequently, a sensitivity analysis of the gas diffusion coefficient was performed to study the effect of the microstructural parameters on gas diffusion. Finally, the dispersion degree of the diffusion coefficients at different temperatures and pressures was analyzed to determine the main influencing factors of gas diffusion at different diffusion stages in coal and study their evolution. The results show that the gas diffusion state is more sensitive to pressure variations. The diffusion behavior of gases in coal-based porous media is more influenced by the temperature and pressure at the beginning of the diffusion process.

Introduction

Coalbed methane (CBM) in coal deposits or coal seams is one of the three major types of unconventional natural gas resources. However, the significantly low characteristics (i.e., the low reservoir pressure, gas saturation, and permeability) of coal reservoirs in China1 result in an unsatisfactory CBM production capacity and hinders its efficient exploitation in China. The transport of CBM from the inner part of the reservoir to the exterior requires three stages: desorption on the inner surface of the gas, diffusion within the pore space, and seepage within the fracture.2 Because diffusion takes the longest time, it determines the CBM production rate. The most basic and widely used theoretical model in the study of gas diffusion mechanisms in the energy field is Fick’s law; it was proposed by the German scientist Fick in 1855 to describe the linear law between the diffusive material flux and concentration gradient under steady- and unsteady-state conditions. When scholars study the diffusion law in porous media with different pore structures, they mostly use Fick’s law and introduce the respective pore structure, temperature, pressure, and other parameters to optimize it. The most widely used models for gas are the single-pore diffusion model proposed by Oxford University scholar Crank in 1956 and the later presented double-pore diffusion model. Liu et al. proposed a variable diffusion coefficient nonisothermal model by improving the classical single-pore diffusion model.3 Liu and Lin established a time-dependent diffusion model based on time-dependent diffusivity and the classical single-pore diffusion model, which classified the diffusion coefficients of coal particles into instantaneous variable diffusion coefficients and residual diffusion coefficients.4 The assumptions on the pore structure of porous media are relatively single and ideal in both the single-pore diffusion model and the double-pore diffusion model, and it is difficult to reflect the influence of the morphology and interconnectivity of the pore channels on gas diffusion.

Numerous studies have shown that the morphology and features of pores and fractures in coal bodies show significant self-similarity at different scales; such fractal characteristics affect the physical properties of coal bodies, such as permeability and porosity.5 Fractal theory has been widely used for the transportation of fluids in porous media. Xiao et al. established the relationship between the K–C equation and the permeability of porous media based on fractal theory.6,7 In addition, fractal theory has been applied to the capillary flow and diffusion of electrolytes in porous media.8,9 Tree-like bifurcation networks were first applied to the study of blood transportation systems. Muray proposed the famous Murray’s law in the 1920s after continuous research and theoretical summarization.10 The law is regarded as the cornerstone of the tree-like bifurcation network structure system. Nowadays, tree-like bifurcation network structures have been widely used in the field of characterization of porous media. Zhang and Xiao et al. studied the heat conduction of asymmetric and damaged tree-like bifurcation networks embedded in porous media.11,12 Liu et al., based on the tree-like bifurcation network structure, found that the permeability of the porous media was related to the damaged position and the number of damaged tubes at the damaged position and established a new permeability model.13 Liu et al. used a tree-like bifurcation network to characterize the internal fracture network morphology of the coal body and established a seepage model for the fracture network of the water-injected coal body.14 Jin, Zhou, and He et al. used gas adsorption experiments to determine the fractal characteristics of the pore structures of coal bodies and analyze the relationship among the fractal dimensions of pores.15−17 Furthermore, Lu et al. summarized the fractal characteristics of the adsorption pore of coal bodies and systematically investigated the applicability of different types of fractal models to characterize coal body pore structures at different scales.18 On this basis, several scholars have studied the influence of the pore structure on the adsorption and diffusion processes from different perspectives by combining adsorption and desorption experiments. For instance, Wood used W&L and FHH methods to derive the fractal dimensions of structures in porous media from adsorption isotherms from low-pressure nitrogen adsorption experiments.19 Based on fractal theory, Zhang and Liu investigated the effects of different fractal dimensions on gas adsorption and diffusion processes with nitrogen adsorption experiments.20,21

In summary, the diffusion characteristics of gas in a coal body are closely related to its pore structure, and factors such as the pore shape, connectivity, and bending degree can significantly affect the gas diffusion process. In addition, combining the fractal theory with the tree-like bifurcation network can better characterize the microstructural features of a coal body. A theoretical model that accurately reflects the fractal pore structure of porous media is urgently needed to describe gas diffusion and transport in coal quantitatively. Based on fractal theory, we constructed a fractal diffusion model based on the tree-like bifurcation network structure model. We compared and analyzed the gas diffusion coefficients from theoretical analyses and experiments to provide theoretical guidance for the subsequent investigation of the gas diffusion mechanism and the effect of each influencing factor on gas diffusion in coal.

To validate the model, the structural parameters must be determined to calculate the model. The remaining parameters can be determined directly or indirectly with nitrogen adsorption experiments, except for the assumptions for model construction. According to eqs 32 and 33, the common influencing factors of the fractal diffusion model of the tree-like bifurcation network under different diffusion modes are the number of bifurcations n, number of bifurcation levels m, primary pore length l0, pore length ratio γ, pore diameter ratio β, maximum pore diameter d0max, and minimum pore diameter d0min. The different influencing factors are the pore network’s structural distribution fractal dimension Df, tortuous fractal dimension DT, temperature T, and pressure P. The pore length ratio γ and pore diameter ratio β of the dendritic network structure are the basic structural parameters, as shown in eqs 6 and 7; they are considered constants in this model. Xu et al. introduced the asymmetry degree δ to define the diameter ratio27

the asymmetry degree of the tree-like bifurcation networks ranges from 0 to 1.0. When the number of bifurcations is n = 2 at each level of the tree-like bifurcation network, when δ = 1.0, the two bifurcation subtubes of the same bifurcation level have identical diameters, and the tree-like bifurcation network is symmetric; when δ = 0, one of the subtubes at the same level is much larger than the other one. According to the range of the asymmetry degree δ, the range of the diameter ratio β is 0–2–1/3. Regarding the length ratio γ, since there is no large amount of experimental data that can be used to determine it, it is usually assumed that the length ratio is equal to the diameter ratio.28,29 Consequently, γ = β, and the range of values is 0–2–1/3 in this work.

The model assumes that all tree-like bifurcation networks penetrate the model. Hence, the size range of the coal grains can be approximated to the range of the linear length L0 of tree-like bifurcation networks, i.e., 180–250 μm. According to Equation 19, l0 is 49.5–68.8 μm.

The maximum mother pipe diameter d0max and minimum mother pipe diameter d0min are the pore ranges in which diffusion occurs. Because the gas is considered to diffuse for 100 nm in the pore, the maximum mother pipe diameter d0max is 100 nm. The minimum mother pipe diameter is chosen such that the tree-like bifurcation network has sufficient space for bifurcations and that the gas can still enter the pore after a bifurcation. According to the diameter of the gas molecules, the minimum pore diameter that the gas can access is 0.33 nm,30 so the minimum mother tube diameter is d0min ≥ 1.32 nm according to the diameter ratio.

Calculation of the Knudsen Number under Different Diffusion Conditions

The motion state of gas within the pores of the coal body is mainly influenced by the pore diameter d and molecular free range l of the gas molecules; according to molecular motion theory,33 the relationship between the free range l of gas molecules and pore diameter d can be expressed based on the Knudsen number Kn

by calculation of the Knudsen number, the gas diffusion modes in coal can be determined. When the Knudsen number Kn < 0.1, the pore diameter of the coal body exceeds the average free range of gas molecules, and the main gas diffusion mode is bulk diffusion; when the Knudsen number Kn > 10, the average free range of gas molecules is much larger than the pore diameter of the coal body, and the main gas diffusion mode is Knudsen diffusion; when the Knudsen number 0.1 < Kn < 10, the motion state of the gas molecules is controlled by both diffusion modes (i.e., transition diffusion). Experimental Equipment

We developed an experimental high-precision gas desorption–diffusion system, which mainly consists of a vacuum degassing unit, a high-pressure gas injection unit, a constant temperature adsorption–desorption unit, and a desorption monitoring unit. The vacuum degassing unit consists of a vacuum unit and related piping and valves, which can be used to degas the coal sample before the experiment; the high-pressure gas injection unit consists of a high-pressure gas cylinder, high-pressure piping, a pressure-reducing tank, and an intelligent digital display pressure gauge, which can realize the set pressure in the coal sample tank. The constant-temperature adsorption–desorption unit is composed of a stainless-steel sample tank and water bath, which can create a constant pressure and temperature environment; the desorption monitoring unit is mainly composed of a micro gas flow meter and an intelligent display. The initial flow rate of the micro gas flow meter is very small; it can be used to monitor the instantaneous flow rate of the gas during the desorption–diffusion process. The intelligent display is connected to a gas flow meter, which can display gas flow changes in real time and store gas flow data to facilitate subsequent data processing. Experimental Scheme and Procedure

The gas desorption–diffusion experiments were performed at different adsorption equilibrium pressures (1, 2, 3, and 4 MPa) at a constant temperature (25 °C) and at different temperatures (25, 35, 45, 55, and 65 °C) at a constant pressure (2.0 MPa). Because of the influence of different initial adsorption amounts on the diffusion coefficient, the constant pressure group was studied at an identical initial adsorption temperature (25 °C). The specific experimental procedure was as follows.

By comparing the gas desorption curves of the two coal samples in Figure 6, it was found that the limiting desorption quantity of both coal samples increased with the increase in temperature, but the magnitude of the increase was slightly different. For the coal sample #1-DLT, the increase of the ultimate desorption quantity is larger in the temperature range of 25–45 °C, and the increase of the ultimate desorption quantity is smaller in the temperature range of 45 –65 °C. The increase in the ultimate desorption quantity of #2-LL coal samples gradually increased in the temperature range of 25–55 °C, and the increase in the ultimate desorption quantity decreased in the temperature range of 55–65 °C. On the other hand, the overall limiting desorption quantity of #2-LL was higher than that of the #1-DLT coal samples, and from the desorption curves, the trailing characteristic of the #2-LL coal samples was more obvious, which was closely related to the degree of development of the pore structure of the coal samples.

From the shape of the curves in Figure 7, the gas desorption quantity of coal particles showed strong trailing characteristics along the time direction; the larger the adsorption balance pressure, the faster the desorption speed was in the early stage, and the larger the cumulative desorption quantity, the more drastic the decay of gas desorption speed was, and the longer the trailing curves were in the time direction. Comparing the desorption curves of the two samples, it was found that with the increase of adsorption balance pressure, the ultimate desorption quantity of the two samples increased, and the increase was similar. But under the same adsorption balance pressure, the ultimate gas desorption quantity of the #2-LL sample was always larger than the #1-DLT sample. Results and Discussion

Comparison of Experimental and Theoretical Diffusion Coefficients

The diffusion coefficient in the single spherical particle model established by Liu et al. is related to temperature, gas pressure, porosity, and pore tortuosity (Liu model).3 The reliability of the new model was verified by comparing the experimental data and the Liu model under different temperature and pressure conditions. The theoretical and experimental diffusion coefficients of the two coal samples for different temperatures and pressures are compared in Figures 8 and 9, respectively.

Comparison of the Experimental and Theory Diffusion Coefficients of two coal samples at different temperatures.

Comparison of experimental and theoretical diffusion coefficients of two coal samples at different pressures.

According to Figure 8, the theoretical and experimental diffusion coefficients of the two coal samples increase with an increasing temperature. Thus, the increasing temperature accelerates gas diffusion. Regarding the increase in the theoretical diffusion coefficients of the two coal samples, the increase in the diffusion coefficient of the #2-LL coal sample with better pore development is greater than that of the #1-DLT coal sample with poorer pore development. Hence, although the increase in the temperature can promote gas diffusion, the respective increases in the diffusion coefficients vary with the degree of pore development of coal bodies. For coal bodies with better pore development, the increase in the gas diffusion coefficient with increasing temperature is more evident.

According to Figure 9, in the low pressure range, both theoretical and experimental diffusion coefficients of the two coal samples decrease significantly with increasing pressure, thereby indicating that the negative effect of the pressure on the gas diffusion process is dominant in the low pressure range; with a further increase in pressure, the two diffusion coefficients start to exhibit different trends. In addition, the decrease in the two diffusion coefficients slows down with further increasing pressure in the #1-DLT coal sample. By contrast, in the #2-LL coal sample, with further increasing pressure, the decrease in Dpefft decreases, whereas Da increases again. The reason for this trend may be that the pore space of a coal body changes dynamically with the pressure during the experiment. The theoretical model, which simulates the structural parameters, cannot represent the change in the calculation results better. This can be further verified by introducing pore structural parameters that change with pressure in the future.

Overall, the theoretical diffusion coefficients of the new model are slightly smaller than the experimental diffusion coefficients. In addition, possibly because the Liu model does not take into account pore connectivity and microstructural parameters, the trend of the new model curve is more consistent with the experimental data than the Liu model. It proves that the new model has better applicability for the prediction of the diffusion coefficient. Thus, the tree-like bifurcation network gas diffusion coefficient model is applicable and scientifically valid.

Analysis of the Evolution of Influencing Factors during Gas Diffusion in Coal

Sensitivity Analysis of the Gas Diffusion Coefficient

In order to avoid the problem of being limited by the range of coal samples used, we investigated the degree of influence of each parameter on the theoretical diffusion coefficient of the model. Based on the above model, a quantitative analysis of the factors affecting the diffusion coefficient of the porous media was performed by setting the baseline parameters and the range of parameter variation. First, the range of values of each parameter was determined, and the control variable method was used to take a certain number of points within the range of values of the factors to be studied, and the other factors were taken as baseline values and substituted into the model. The settings of each parameter for the sensitivity analysis are shown in Table 6. The theoretical diffusion coefficients with respect to the pore diameter ratio of the two coal samples with different bifurcation numbers are presented in Figure 10.

Theoretical diffusion coefficient Dpefft with respect to the diameter ratio at different bifurcation numbers.

According to Figure 10, at a constant number of bifurcations at each level, the diffusion coefficients of the two coal samples increase substantially with increasing β when β < 0.7. When β > 0.7, the increase in the diffusion coefficient slows down until it becomes almost unaffected by the changing diameter ratio. This is because when the number of bifurcations n is fixed, β determines the ratio of the effective cross-sectional area of adjacent pores; when β is small and increases, the effective cross-sectional area of the bifurcation network decreases with an increasing number of bifurcation stages. That is, at the same bifurcation level, the larger β is, the larger the effective cross-sectional area of the bifurcation network is, the larger the gas diffusion space is, and the smaller the diffusion resistance is; therefore, the gas diffusion coefficient increases rapidly. For β > 0.7, the effective cross-sectional area of the bifurcation network and the cross-sectional area of the primary pore gradually approach each other as the bifurcation number increases. Because the change is smaller, the increase in the diffusion coefficient tends to slow down. At a constant diameter ratio, the diffusion coefficients of two coal samples increase with an increasing number of bifurcations. This is because the larger bifurcation number represents a better-developed tree-like bifurcation network. Moreover, the effective cross-sectional area of the pore channel is larger at the same bifurcation level, and the diffusion space is larger, which promotes gas flow in the bifurcation network.

Since the characteristic length of the whole tree-like bifurcation network needs to be calculated from l0 with eq 19, the effects of the primary pore length l0 and the number of bifurcation stages m on gas diffusion can be considered separately. Figure 11 presents the theoretical diffusion coefficients with respect to the primary pore length l0 of two coal samples with different bifurcation levels m.

Theoretical diffusion coefficient Dpefft with respect to length of primary channel for different bifurcation series.

According to Figure 11, Dpefft decreases with an increasing number of bifurcation stages in the bifurcation network. This is because an increase in the number of bifurcation stages m means that the complexity of the bifurcation network, gas flow path, and diffusion resistance of the gas are increasing; these characteristics hinder gas diffusion in the bifurcation network. At a constant total bifurcation number, the gas diffusion coefficient decreases with increasing l0; this is due to the fact that the larger l0 is, the longer the total diffusion path of the gas and the greater the diffusion resistance. These trends do not promote gas diffusion at a constant bifurcation number m and pore length ratio γ.

Analysis of Main Influences on Gas Diffusion during Different Stages

To investigate further the degree of influence of temperature and pressure on the gas diffusion coefficient and determine the main influencing factors on gas diffusion at different diffusion stages, we calculated the mean diffusion coefficient of the different diffusion stages and the diffusion difference at different temperatures and pressures. We are based on the experimental data obtained from the gas desorption-diffusion experiments and divide the diffusion process into initial diffusion (t ≤ 300 s) and mid to late diffusion (t > 300 s) according to the characteristics of the change in the slope of the desorption curve. The schematic in Figure 12 presents the dispersion degree of the gas diffusion coefficient.

Dispersion degree and apparent deviation of the gas diffusion coefficient.

Overall, according to Figure 12, in the experimental and theoretical results, the standard deviation of the gas diffusion coefficients with respect to the pressure at each diffusion stage exceeds that of the gas diffusion coefficients with respect to the temperature. That is, the dispersion of the gas diffusion coefficients relative to the mean value with increasing pressure is larger than that with increasing temperature. This indicates that the gas diffusion state is more sensitive to pressure changes than to temperature changes. Therefore, pressure is the main influencing factor in gas diffusion in coal. The reason may be that the structure of the coal body is very sensitive to pressure;34 pressure changes affect the gas movement and diffusion space of gas in the coal and change the gas diffusion paths. The superposition of the two effects makes pressure the dominant influencing factor for gas diffusion inside a coal body.

The degree of influence of temperature and pressure on the diffusion process depends on the diffusion stage. Figure 13 presents the diffusion standard deviation of the different diffusion stages under different conditions; it clearly presents the dynamic influence of temperature and pressure changes on gas diffusion in coal.

Diffusion standard deviation at different diffusion stages.

According to Figure 13a, the standard deviation of the diffusion coefficients in the initial stage of both coal samples with respect to the pressure is greater than the standard deviation of the diffusion coefficients in the mid to late stage. This indicates that the diffusion behavior of gas within a coal-based porous medium is more influenced by pressure during the initial diffusion stage; the effect of pressure variations on gas diffusion in coal becomes weaker as the diffusion process proceeds; during the initial diffusion stage, the gas mainly diffuses through medium and large pores, whereas in the mid to late stage, diffusion mainly occurs in the form of gas that escapes from micropores inside the coal body. The researche has shown that the compressibility of medium and large pores is greater and that the stress sensitivity is higher than that of micro pores.35 This is also confirmed by the fact that the diffusion standard deviation of the #2-LL coal sample is larger than that of the #1-DLT coal sample during initial and mid to late diffusion. This is because the #2-LL sample has better pore development and more diffusion space, which renders it more sensitive to pressure changes.

In Figure 13b, which compares different diffusion stages, the standard deviation of the initial diffusion process of the two coal samples is greater than the standard deviation of the diffusion coefficients in the mid to late stages; thus, the effect of temperature changes on gas diffusion in coal becomes weaker as diffusion proceeds. At the same diffusion stage, the standard deviation of the diffusion coefficient of the #2-LL coal sample with respect to temperature exceeds that of the #1-DLT coal sample. Evidently, the gas diffusion state in the #2-LL coal sample is more influenced by the temperature than that of the #1-DLT coal sample. Consequently, the temperature has a greater effect on gas diffusion in coal bodies, with better pore development.

The model considers the effect of pore tortuosity and connectivity on gas diffusion, which renders it more realistic than the previously presented single-pore diffusion model; moreover, each parameter in the model has a definite physical meaning and does not contain any empirical constants.

Hemoglobin suresnes combined with α0-thalassemia: Diagnostic challenges and insights from trio-based whole exome sequencing
Authors: Hung-Lin Liu, Wan-Ting Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHemoglobin Suresnes (Hb Suresnes) is a rare hemoglobin variant caused by a substitution of arginine to histidine at position 141 (p.Arg142His) in the COOH terminus of the α-globin chain. This structural change alters the oxygen affinity of hemoglobin and leads to erythrocytosis. We report the first genetically confirmed case of Hb Suresnes co-inherited with α0-thalassemia (--/αα) in an East Asian patient, diagnosed via trio whole exome sequencing (WES). A 33-year-old male presented with asymptomatic erythrocytosis, microcytosis, and a family history of elevated red cell indices. Under genetic analysis with WES, we identified a heterozygous Hb Suresnes variant [NM_000558.3(HBA1): c.425G > A (p.Arg142His)] inherited from his father, alongside α0-thalassemia (--/αα) inherited from his mother. The overlapping hematologic phenotype initially mimicked polycythemia vera, prompting empirical therapeutic phlebotomy. This case highlights the diagnostic pitfall of misclassifying hereditary erythrocytosis as polycythemia vera and emphasizes the role of WES in resolving the complexity of coexisting hemoglobinopathies, ensuring accurate diagnosis and appropriate management.Keywords: Hb suresnes, α0-thalassemia, Co-inherited hemoglobinopathy, Whole exome sequencing, Case report

Introduction

Hemoglobin Suresnes (Hb Suresnes) is a rare hemoglobin variant with a replacement of arginine 141 to a histidine residue at the COOH terminus of the α chains [1, 2]. This variant affects the electrostatic interactions and oxygen-linked anion binding that result in a high affinity for oxygen, a decreased Bohr effect, reduced cooperativity in oxygen binding and oxygen delivery to tissues [3]. This condition frequently results in erythrocytosis, which may remain asymptomatic in many individuals but can also be associated with symptoms, particularly when occurring alongside other hemoglobinopathies such as thalassemia [4].

Hb Suresnes was first identified in 1976 among members of a French family [1]. The reported Hb levels ranged from 15.1 to 16.5 mg/dL with a normal mean corpuscular volume (MCV). While this variant has been previously identified in individuals of Caucasian, African American, and Costa Rican (Spanish-Indian) descent [5, 6], Hb Suresnes has not been previously reported in East Asia. Here, we present a case of Hb Suresnes/α0-thalassemia (--/αα) and uncover genetic alterations through trio-whole exome sequencing (WES).

Patient presentation

A 33-year-old man had been well until abnormal complete blood count (CBC) results were detected by a health examination. Laboratory data revealed erythrocytosis (9.51 × 106/uL), with a hemoglobin level of 18.7 g/dL and a MCV of 59.4 fL. The white blood cell count and platelet count were within normal limits. He is a non-smoker with no history of cardiopulmonary disorders. The family history revealed erythrocytosis in his father and younger sister.

The patient’s mother had MCV less than 80 fL, RBC counts slightly higher than normal, and normal hemoglobin profile, suggestive of α-thalassemia. The patient’s father exhibited normocytic erythrocytosis with increase of hematocrit and hemoglobin concentration but erythropoietin (EPO) levels in normal range. The patient appeared microcytic erythrocytosis with increased hematocrit and hemoglobin levels but low mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH). WES was performed using a NovaSeq 6000 intstrument (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), with Illumina’s DRAGEN Bio-IT Platform (v4.3) for the second analysis and Geneyx Analysis platform (v6.1) for the third analysis. The peripheral blood revealed a variant of Hb Suresnes [NM_000558.3(HBA1): c.425G > A (p.Arg142His)] in the patient and his father (Table 1). A heterozygous 30.8-kb deletion was detected in the patient’s mother that leads to deletion of the HBA1 and HBA2 genes in cis (--/αα) variant. The patients inherited a 30.8-kb deletion in alpha-globins (--/αα) from the mother and Hb Suresnes from the father. It seemed most likely that the patient’s relatively high red blood cell count, hematocrit and hemoglobin for his condition could be attributed to the compound heterozygous Hb Suresnes and α0-thalassemia (--/αα). This unique genetic combination produced a phenotype distinct from that associated with either Hb Suresnes or α0-thalassemia (--/αα) alone, characterized by microcytic erythrocytosis with profoundly elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit levels.

Prior to the confirmation of the genetic diagnosis, the patient underwent therapeutic phlebotomy empirically, with approximately 250 mL of blood removed every three months to manage erythrocytosis. Following the establishment of the diagnosis, the management strategy shifted to routine monitoring of hemogram parameters and metabolic profiles.

Discussion

The Hb Suresnes is often characterized in the literature as the substitution of arginine with histidine at position 141 of the α2 globin chain [5, 6]. In contrast, our case demonstrated an identical amino acid substitution at position 142 of the α1-globin chain. This discrepancy stems from the use of distinct reference numbering systems and analytical methodologies. Under the Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS) guidelines, which include the initiator methionine as position 1, the variant is designated at residue 142 [7]. Furthermore, the near-identical sequences of HBA1 and HBA2 contribute significant challenges for accurate gene-specific assignment using WES. In this case, the patient exhibited the Hb Suresnes variant on one allele and a cis deletion of both HBA1 and HBA2 genes on the other allele. These genetic defects resulted in the absence of intact α-globin chain production, leading to secondary erythrocytosis that mimicked polycythemia vera (PV).

Differentiating between secondary erythrocytosis and PV is clinically crucial for guiding appropriate management. While the JAK2 mutation is used for diagnosing PV, the serum erythropoietin (EPO) level may serve as a key marker for distinction. In PV, erythropoiesis occurs independently of EPO, resulting in typically low or undetectable EPO levels [8]. Our patient presented with asymptomatic erythrocytosis accompanied by a normal EPO level, aligning with findings from other studies of secondary erythrocytosis [9]. Additionally, Iron storage levels can aid in differentiating causes of erythrocytosis. In polycythemia vera (PV), iron deficiency often results from chronic red blood cell overproduction or therapeutic phlebotomy. In contrast, secondary erythrocytosis may also show reduced iron stores due to increased red cell mass demand [10]. In the present case, decreased iron storage was likely due to therapeutic phlebotomy performed prior to diagnosis to control elevated hematocrit. This case highlights the diagnostic challenge arising from the coexistence of Hb Suresnes and α0-thalassemia. The resulting microcytic erythrocytosis, elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit, and reduced iron stores closely mimic polycythemia vera, especially following therapeutic phlebotomy. In the absence of molecular testing, this overlap may lead to misdiagnosis and inappropriate management.

Therapeutic phlebotomy is central to PV management, aiming to lower hematocrit and reduce thrombotic risk. In secondary erythrocytosis, it is used more selectively—mainly for symptomatic hyper-viscosity or markedly elevated hematocrit [9]. While it offers short-term relief, addressing the underlying cause of erythropoiesis remains key. Thus, accurate differentiation between PV and secondary erythrocytosis is crucial for appropriate treatment.

Another hemoglobinopathy, Hb Legnano [α141(HC3) Arg→Leu], affects the same codon in the α-globin chain as Hb Suresnes but results from a distinct nucleotide substitution. In both variants, disruption of the salt bridge between α141 Arg and α126 Asp destabilizes the T-state and shifts the equilibrium toward the high-affinity R-state, thereby increasing oxygen affinity while reducing cooperativity and the Bohr effect [11]. Beyond Hb Legnano, prior reports of Hb Luton and Hb Crete coexisting with thalassemia further underscore the clinical impact of high-affinity hemoglobin variants. In such cases, reduced production of normal hemoglobin amplifies the relative proportion of the high-affinity variant, exacerbating erythrocytosis and increasing the risk of hyper-viscosity-related complications [4, 12]. In conclusion, we report the first East Asian patient with co-inherited Hb Suresnes and α0-thalassemia (--/αα) identified by trio WES. This case expands the limited literature on Hb Suresnes and highlights the diagnostic pitfall of hereditary erythrocytosis mimicking polycythemia vera. Accurate molecular diagnosis is crucial to prevent unnecessary interventions such as therapeutic phlebotomy and guide appropriate long-term management.

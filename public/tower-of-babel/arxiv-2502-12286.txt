Rational Capability in Concurrent Games Thanks: This is an extended version of the same title paper that will appear in AAMAS 2025. This version contains technical appendixes with proof details that, for space reasons, do not appear in the AAMAS 2025 version.
Yinfeng Li, Emiliano Lorini, Munyque Mittelmann

Abstract
. We extend concurrent game structures (CGSs) with a simple notion of preference over computations and define a minimal notion of rationality for agents based on the concept of dominance. We use this notion to interpret a CL and an ATL languages that extend the basic CL and ATL languages with modalities for rational capability, namely, a coalition’s capability to rationally enforce a given property. For each of these languages, we provide results about the complexity of satisfiability checking and model checking as well as about axiomatization.



1.
Introduction

The field of
logics for multi-agent systems
has been very active
in the last twenty years.
Different logics
have been proposed
and their proof-theoretic,
complexity
and algorithmic
aspects
for satisfiability
and model checking studied
in detail.
The list of logics in this area is long.
It includes
alternating-time temporal logic
(
ATL
)
(
Alur
et al. 2002
;
Goranko and van
Drimmelen 2006
)
,
its “next”-fragment
called
coalition logic
(
CL
)
(
Pauly 2002
;
Goranko 2001
)
,
the logic of agency
STIT

(
Broersen
et al. 2006
;
Boudou and Lorini 2018
)
, and
the more expressive strategy logic
(
SL
)
(
Mogavero
et al. 2014
;
Chatterjee et al. 2007
)
.
A widely used
semantics for interpreting
these logics is based on concurrent game structures (CGSs),
transition systems
in which state-transitions are labeled by joint actions of agents.
A CGS allows us to represent the repeated interaction between multiple agents
in a natural way as well as their choices and strategies.
It is similar
to the game-theoretic concept of dynamic
game
in which players move sequentially or repeatedly.
But an element that is missing from CGSs compared
to dynamic games is the preference of the agents.
Indeed, most logics for multi-agent
systems
including

ATL
,

CL
,

SL

and

STIT
abstract away
from the agents’ preferences
as they are only interested in representing
and reasoning about
the

game form
, namely, the way an outcome
is determined based on the agents’
concurrent choices over time.

In this paper we extend
CGSs with a basic concept of preference.
This is in order to have a semantics that allows us to represent a game in its entirety, capturing both its aspects (the game form and the agents’ preferences), and consequently to reason about rational
choices and strategies
in the game.
Specifically, we introduce a new class
of structures called CGS with preferences
(CGSP) that includes one preference
ordering for each agent at each state in the underlying CGS.
An agent’s preference at a given state is relative to
the set of computations (or histories) starting at this state.
We consider an interesting subclass
of CGSP with
stable preferences

in which agents’ preferences do not change over time. This reminds
the notion of
time consistency
of preferences studied in economics,
in opposition to time inconsistency
(
Frederik et al. 2002
)
.
We employ CGSP
to interpret
two novel
languages

R-ATL

and

R-CL
(
ATL
/
CL
with minimal rationality)
that extend the basic
ATL
and

CL
languages with
modal operators for

rational
capability, namely,
a coalition’s capability to
enforce a given outcome by choosing a

rational
strategy.
The notion of rationality
that we use to define these operators
is based on strong dominance:
the collective strategy of a coalition is rational insofar as the individual strategies that compose it are not strongly dominated.
It is a minimal notion of rationality since it does not require the agent to reason about what others will choose. It simply requires an agent not to play a strategy that is beaten by another
of its strategies regardless of what the others choose.
In
(
Horty 2001
)
, it is
shown that this minimal dominance-based requirement of rationality
is particularly suitable for defining the deontic notion of obligation, namely, what an agent or coalition
ought to do.
The general idea of refining the capability operators of ATL by restricting quantification to the agents’ rational strategies is shared with
Bulling et al.

(
Bulling
et al. 2008
)
.
But unlike us, they do not extend CGSs
with an explicit notion of preference. In their semantics
sets of
plausible/rational strategies
can be only referred to via
atomic plausibility terms (constants) whose interpretation is “hardwired”
in the model.
A similar idea can also be found in
(
Lorini and Sartor 2016
)

in which
rational

STIT
(“seeing to it that”)
modalities are introduced.

For each of the languages we introduce, results about the complexity of satisfiability checking and model checking as well as about axiomatization
are provided.
In particular, the following are the main results of the paper:

•

tree-like model property for
R-ATL
;

•

polynomial embeddings
of
R-ATL

into

ATL

under the stable preference assumption,
and
of
R-CL

into

CL

both
under the stable preference assumption
and with no assumption;

•

thanks to the embeddings,
tight complexity results
of satisfiability checking
for
R-ATL

and

R-CL
;

•

a sound and complete axiomatization
for the logic

R-CL
;

•

a model checking algorithm
for
R-ATL
for the class of concurrent game structures with short-sighted preferences.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
,
we discuss related work.
In Section
3
,
we present the semantic foundation
of our framework.
Then,
in Section
4
,
we introduce the languages of

R-ATL

and

R-CL
.
Section
5

is devoted
to the tree-like model property,
the embeddings
and the complexity results
for our logics. In Section
6

we deal with axiomatization, while in
Section
7

we move to model checking.
Detailed proofs are given in
Appendixes A-H.

2.
Related work

Several works have studied logics for reasoning about preferences, without considering the strategic and temporal dimensions. In particular,
van Benthem and
Liu 2007

(
van Benthem and
Liu 2007
)
proposed a dynamic logic of knowledge update and preference upgrade, where incoming suggestions or commands change the preference relations.
Lorini 2021

(
Lorini 2021
)
presented a general logical framework for reasoning about agents’ cognitive
attitudes, which captures concepts of knowledge, belief, desire, and preference.

Grossi
et al. 2022

(
Grossi
et al. 2022
)
investigated four different semantics for conditional logics based on preference relations over alternatives. The semantics differ in the way of selecting the most preferred alternative, which includes maximality, optimality, unmatchedness, and acceptability. Maximality is related to the rationality concept we consider in this paper. Maximal alternatives are those not strictly dispreferred to any other.

Two of the most important developments in logics for strategic reasoning are ATL
(
Alur
et al. 2002
)
and SL
(
Mogavero
et al. 2014
)
.
Unlike ATL, SL can express complex solution concepts (such as dominant strategy equilibrium) and thus capture some notions of rationality.
However, in both logics, agents’ preferences are not modeled intrinsically, instead, their goals can be represented as Boolean formulas.
A way to incorporate preferences in those logics is to include atomic propositions stating that the utility of an agent is greater than or equal to a given value
(
Baltag 2002
)
, which requires an exhaustive enumeration for each relevant utility threshold.
The extensions of ATL and SL with quantitative semantics
(
Jamroga et al. 2024
;
Bouyer et al. 2023
)
generalize fuzzy temporal logics and capture quantitative goals. This approach has been recently used to represent agents’ utilities in mechanism design
(
Maubert et al. 2021
;
Mittelmann et al. 2022
)
.

The dominance relation among strategies has been considered alongside specifications in temporal logics
(
Aminof
et al. 2021a
;
Aminof et al. 2021b
)
. These works provide algorithms for synthesizing best-effort strategies, which are maximal in the dominance order, in the sense that they achieve the agent goal against a maximal set of environment specifications.

Rationality in concurrent games is typically associated with agents’ knowledge and preferences. Know-How Logic with the Intelligence
(
Naumov and Yuan 2021
)
captures rational agents’ capabilities that depend on the intelligence information about the opponents’ actions. The interplay between agents’ preferences and their knowledge was described in
(
Naumov and
Ovchinnikova 2023
)
.
A sound, complete, and decidable logical system expressing higher-order preferences to the other agents was given in
(
Jiang and Naumov 2024
)
. However, none of these three papers address the connection between rational agents’ capabilities and their preferences.

Our work is also related to the research on rational verification and synthesis. The first is the problem of checking whether a temporal goal is satisfied in some or all game-theoretic equilibria of a CGS
(
Abate
et al. 2021
;
Gutierrez et al. 2023
)
. Rational synthesis consists in the automated construction of such a model
(
Fisman
et al. 2010
;
Condurache et al. 2016
)
.
Different types of agent objectives have been considered, including Boolean temporal specifications
(
Gutierrez et al. 2019
)
, mean payoff
(
Gutierrez et al. 2024
)
, and lexicographical preferences
(
Gutierrez et al. 2017
)

While being able to analyze multi-agent systems with respect to solution concepts, both rational verification and model-checking SL specifications face high complexity issues.
In particular, key decision problems for rational verification with temporal specifications are known to be
2Exptime
-complete
(
Gutierrez et al. 2023
)
and model-checking SL is non-elementary for memoryful agents
(
Mogavero
et al. 2014
)
.

ATL with plausibility
(
Bulling
et al. 2008
)
allows the specification of sets of
rational strategy profiles, and reason about agents’ play if the agents can only play these strategy profiles. The approach considers plausibility terms, which are mapped to a set of strategy profiles.
The logic includes formulas of the form
(\text{set-pl}\omega)\varphi
, meaning that “assuming that the set of rational strategy profiles is defined in terms of the plausibility terms
\omega
, then, it is
plausible to expect that
\varphi
holds”. This idea was extended in
(
Bulling and
Jamroga 2009
)
to a variant of SL for imperfect information games.
However, as emphasized in the introduction,
Bulling et al. do not
represent
agents’
preferences in their semantics.
This is a crucial
difference between their work and ours.
Our main focus is
on extending CGSs
with preferences,
studying the dynamic properties
of agents’ preferences in concurrent games,
and defining a logic
of rational capability with the help
of the semantics
combining CGSs with preferences.

3.
Semantics

In this section,
we first define the basic
elements of the semantics:
the notions
of concurrent game structure (CGS),
computation
and strategy.
Then, we
extend a CGS
with preferences
and use the resulting
structure to define the notion
of dominated strategy.

3.1.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a countable set of atomic propositions
and
\mathbb{AGT}=\{1,\ldots,n\}

A coalition is a (possibly empty)

from
\mathbb{AGT}
.
Coalitions are denoted by

C,C^{\prime},\ldots

The following definition introduces

Definition 0 (CGS).

A concurrent game structure (CGS)

with

•

•

•

such that
for every

and

:

(C1)

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
is deterministic (
collective choice determinism
),
1

1

\mathcal{R}

\forall w,v,u\in W,\text{ if }w\mathcal{R}v\text{ and }w\mathcal{R}u\text{ then }v=u
.

(C2)

if
\delta(1)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(w),\ldots,\delta(n)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(w)

(

),

(C3)

\forall w\in W,\exists v\in W\text{ s.t. }w\mathcal{R}v

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{i}(w)

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathbb{ACT}:\exists\delta\in\mathbb{JACT}\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(w)\neq\emptyset\text{ and }\delta(i)=a\}.

The previous definition slightly differs
from the usual definition of

(

(

In particular a

is a
multi-relational
structure,
alias

the kind of structure traditionally
used in modal
logic.
Every joint action
is associated
to a binary relation
over states satisfying certain properties,
while
in the usual
semantics for

ATL

and

SL

a transition function
is used that
maps a state
and a joint action executable
at this state to a successor state.
The two variants are interdefinable.
We use the multi-relational variant of CGS
since it
is particularly convenient for proving the model-theoretic
and proof-theoretic results
in the rest of the paper.

The relation

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}

with
\delta\in\mathbb{JACT}

is used to identify the set of states

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(w)=\{v\in W:w\mathcal{R}_{\delta}v\}

that are reachable from
state
w

when the agents collectively choose
joint action

\delta

at state
w
,
that is,
when every agent
i

chooses
the individual component
\delta(i)

at state
w
.

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(w)=\emptyset

means that the joint action
\delta
cannot
be collectively chosen by the agents
at state
w
.
The set

\mathcal{C}_{i}(w)

in the previous definition
corresponds
to
agent
i

at state
w

the set of actions
that agent
i

can choose at state
w

(or agent
i

at
w
). Note that an agent’s choice
set may vary from one state to another,
i.e.,
it might be the case that

\mathcal{C}_{i}(w)\neq\mathcal{C}_{i}(v)

if
w\neq v
.
Constraint C1 captures

collective choice determinism
:
the outcome
of a collective choice of all agents is uniquely determined.
Constraint C2 corresponds to the

independence of choices
assumption:
if agent
1

can individually choose action

\delta(1)
,
agent
2

can individually choose action

\delta(2)
,…,
agent
n

can individually choose action

\delta(n)
,
then the agents can collectively choose joint action

\delta
. More intuitively, this means that agents can never be deprived of choices due to the choices made by other agents.
Constraint C3 corresponds to the

neverending interaction
assumption:
every state in a CGS has

at least one successor
,
where the successor of a given state
is a state
which is reachable
from
the former via
a collective choice
of all agents.

For notational
convenience,
in the rest
of the paper,
sometimes
use the
abbreviation

\mathit{TRel}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathtt{def}}}{{=}}\ (\mathcal{R}_{\delta})_{\delta\in\mathbb{JACT}}

to indicate a profile
of transition relations,
and write

M=(W,\mathbb{ACT},\mathit{TRel},\mathcal{V})

instead
of
M=\big(W,\mathbb{ACT},(\mathcal{R}_{\delta})_{\delta\in\mathbb{JACT}},\mathcal{V}\big)

for a CGS.

3.2.
Adding preferences

In this section,
we extend the notion

1

Definition 0 (CGS with preferences).

Let

M=(W,\mathbb{ACT},\allowbreak\mathit{TRel},\mathcal{V})

be a CGS. A preference structure for
M

is a tuple
\Omega_{M}=(\preceq_{i,w})_{i\in\mathbb{AGT},w\in W}

where, for every
i\in\mathbb{AGT}
and
w\in W
,

\preceq_{i,w}

is total preorder over
\mathit{Comp}_{M,w}
.
We call the pair
(M,\Omega_{M})

a CGS with preferences (CGSP).
As usual, we write

\lambda^{\prime}\prec_{i,w}\lambda

if

\lambda^{\prime}\preceq_{i,w}\lambda

and

\lambda\not\preceq_{i,w}\lambda^{\prime}
.

We say that the CGSP

(M,\Omega_{M})
has stable preferences
if
the following condition holds:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{SP})\ \forall w,v\in W,\forall\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathit{Comp}_{M,v},

\displaystyle\text{ if }w\mathcal{R}v

\displaystyle\text{ then }\big(\lambda^{\prime}\preceq_{i,v}\lambda\text{ iff }w\lambda^{\prime}\preceq_{i,w}w\lambda\big).

Constraint
\mathbf{SP}

for stable preferences captures the fact
that an agent’s preference is stable over time:
an agent prefers a computation
\lambda
to a computation
\lambda^{\prime}

starting at the same world
v

if and only if it prefers the precursor of
\lambda

(i.e.,
w\lambda
)
to the precursor of
\lambda^{\prime}

(i.e.,
w\lambda^{\prime}
)
at each
predecessor
w
of
v
.

). Branches represent (groups of) computations. Transitions are labeled by the action taken by
v_{1}
and
*
denotes any action. Self-loops indicate computations where the state is repeated. Grey states indicate computations with an infinite suffix that repeats on the same state. Labels in the form under the grey states represent the preference relations
\preceq_{v_{1},w_{0}}
and
\preceq_{v_{2},w_{0}}
of the agents
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively. Computations labeled with
+_{i}
are strictly preferred to
=_{i}
by agent
v_{i}
, and
=_{i}
are strictly preferred to
-_{i}
by agent
v_{i}
(where
i=\{1,2\}
).

The strategies in which the agent performs
Move
in the initial state are not dominated, because it may lead to the state where he crossed or to a collision.
On the other hand, the strategy in which the agent waits (action
Skip
) when only the other agent has crossed is dominated by the strategy in which he moves whenever agent
v_{2}
has crossed.

The following definition introduces
the notion
of dominated strategy,
the essential constituent
of minimal rationality
for agents.

Definition 0 (Dominated strategies).

Let
P=(M,\Omega_{M})
be a CGSP
with
M=(W,\mathbb{ACT},\mathit{TRel},\mathcal{V})

a CGS and
\Omega_{M}=(\preceq_{i,w})_{i\in\mathbb{AGT},w\in W}

a preference structure for
M
,

i\in\mathbb{AGT}
,
w\in W
,
and
\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}},\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}^{\prime}\in\mathit{Str}_{M}^{\{i\}}
.
We say that at world
w

agent
i
’s strategy
\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}^{\prime}

dominates agent
i
’s strategy
\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}

iff

\displaystyle\forall\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{AGT}\setminus{\{i\}}}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathit{Str}_{M}^{\mathbb{AGT}\setminus{\{i\}}},\lambda^{M{,}w{,}\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{AGT}\setminus{\{i\}}}^{\prime\prime}}\prec_{i,w}\lambda^{M{,}w{,}\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{AGT}\setminus{\{i\}}}^{\prime\prime}}.

Agent
i
’s strategy
\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}

is said to be dominated at

if
there exists another strategy
\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}^{\prime}

of

which dominates
\mathcal{F}_{\{i\}}
at
w
.
Agent
i
’s set of dominated strategies
at

is denoted by

4.
Language

The language of
R-ATL

(
ATL
with
minimal rationality
), denoted
by
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
,
is defined by the following grammar:

\varphi,\psi

::=

p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\varphi\mid\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\varphi\mid\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\varphi\mid\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}\varphi\mid\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi),

where
p

ranges over
\mathbb{P}

and
C
ranges over
2^{\mathbb{AGT}}
.
The other Boolean connectives
and constructs

\vee,\rightarrow,\leftrightarrow,\top,\bot

are defined as abbreviations in the usual way.

On the one hand,
formulas

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\varphi
,

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\varphi

and

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

capture the notion of strategic capability.
They
have the usual

ATL
readings:

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\varphi

has to be read
“coalition
C
has
a strategy at its disposal
which guarantees that

\varphi
is going to be true in the next state”,
while

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\varphi

has to be read
“coalition
C
has
a strategy at its disposal
which guarantees that

\varphi
will always be true”.
Finally,

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

has to be read
“coalition
C
has
a strategy at its disposal
which guarantees that

\varphi
will be true until
\psi
is true”.
On the other hand,
formulas

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\varphi
,

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}\varphi

and

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

capture the notion
of
rational
strategic capability:

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\varphi

has to be read
“coalition
C
has
a
rational
strategy at its disposal
which guarantees that

\varphi
is going to be true in the next state”,

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}\varphi

has to be read
“coalition
C
has
a
rational
strategy at its disposal
which guarantees that

\varphi
will always be true”.
Finally,

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

has to be read
“coalition
C
has
a
rational
strategy at its disposal
which guarantees that

\varphi
will be true until
\psi
is true”.

Formulas of the language

\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})

are evaluated relative to a pair

(P,w)

with

P=(M,\Omega_{M})

a CGSP,

M=(W,\mathbb{ACT},\allowbreak\mathit{TRel},\mathcal{V})

a CGS,

\Omega_{M}

a preference structure for
M

and
w\in W
, as follows:

\displaystyle(P,w)\models p

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle p\in\mathcal{V}\big(\lambda(0)\big),

\displaystyle(P,w)\models\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\varphi

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\mathcal{F}_{C}\in\mathit{Str}^{C}_{M}\text{ s.t. }\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{O}(w,\mathcal{F}_{C}),

\displaystyle\big(P,\lambda(1)\big)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle(P,w)\models\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\varphi

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\mathcal{F}_{C}\in\mathit{Str}^{C}_{M}\text{ s.t. }\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{O}(w,\mathcal{F}_{C}),

\displaystyle\forall k>0,\big(P,\lambda(k)\big)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle(P,w)\models\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\mathcal{F}_{C}\in\mathit{Str}^{C}_{M}\text{ s.t. }\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{O}(w,\mathcal{F}_{C}),

\displaystyle\exists k>0\text{ s.t. }\big(P,\lambda(k)\big)\models\psi\text{ and }

\displaystyle\forall h\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\},\big(P,\lambda(h)\big)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle(P,w)\models\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\varphi

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\mathcal{F}_{C}\in\mathit{Str}^{C}_{M}\text{ s.t. }\forall i\in C,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{\{i\}}\not\in\mathit{Dom}_{M,w}^{i}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{O}(w,\mathcal{F}_{C}),\big(P,\lambda(1)\big)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle(P,w)\models\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}\varphi

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\mathcal{F}_{C}\in\mathit{Str}^{C}_{M}\text{ s.t. }\forall i\in C,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{\{i\}}\not\in\mathit{Dom}_{M,w}^{i}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{O}(w,\mathcal{F}_{C}),

\displaystyle\forall k>0,\big(P,\lambda(k)\big)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle(P,w)\models\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\mathcal{F}_{C}\in\mathit{Str}^{C}_{M}\text{ s.t. }\forall i\in C,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{\{i\}}\not\in\mathit{Dom}_{M,w}^{i}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{O}(w,\mathcal{F}_{C}),

\displaystyle\exists k>0\text{ s.t. }\big(P,\lambda(k)\big)\models\psi\text{ and }

\displaystyle\forall h\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\},\big(P,\lambda(h)\big)\models\varphi,

where
\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{\{i\}}

is the restriction
of function

\mathcal{F}_{C}

to
\{i\}\subseteq C
.
Note that the difference between

operators
and the

strategic capability

lies in the restriction
to non-dominated (minimally rational)

While the

over

of the coalition

(i.e.,

),
their rational

of the coalition

components are not dominated
(i.e.,
\forall i\in C,\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{\{i\}}\not\in\mathit{Dom}_{M,w}^{i}
).

The following fragment
defines
the language of

(

with

\varphi,\psi

::=

where
p

ranges over
\mathbb{P}

and
C
ranges over

.

The languages

of

and
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})

•

•

\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})

with no formulas

Example 0 (Crossing road (cont.)).

Returning to our example, it is easy to check that

5.
Tree-like model property and embedding

In this section we first state
the tree-like model property for the language
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.
Thanks to it, we will provide
a polynomial embedding
of the
R-ATL
-language
into the

ATL
-language
which also offers a polynomial embedding of
the

R-CL
-language
into the

CL
-language.
Thanks to the embedding
we will be able
to provide tight complexity results
for satisfiability checking for the
two languages
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})

and

\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.

5.1.
Tree-like model property

Let

\mathcal{R}^{*}
,
\mathcal{R}^{-}
and
\mathcal{R}^{+}

be, respectively, the reflexive and transitive closure,
the inverse, and the transitive closure of

\mathcal{R}=\bigcup_{\delta\in\mathbb{JACT}}\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
.

Definition 0.

Let
M=(W,\mathbb{ACT},\mathit{TRel},\mathcal{V})

be a CGS.
We say that:

•

M
has a unique root iff
there is a unique
w_{0}\in W
(called the
root
),
such that, for every
v\in W
,
w_{0}\mathcal{R}^{*}v
;

•

M
has unique predecessors iff
for every
v\neq w_{0}
, the cardinality of
\mathcal{R}^{-}(v)
is at most one;

•

M
has no cycles iff
\mathcal{R}^{+}
is irreflexive;

•

M
is tree-like iff it has a unique root,
unique predecessors and no cycles;

•

M

is joint action disjoint iff for every
w\in W

and for every
\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{JACT}
,
if
\delta\neq\delta^{\prime}

then
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(w)\cap\mathcal{R}_{\delta^{\prime}}(w)=\emptyset
.

The
property of
“having stable preferences”
defined in Definition
7

and the
properties of “having unique root”,
“having unique predecessors”,
“having no cycles”,
“being tree-like”
and “being joint action disjoint”
defined in Definition
1

are abbreviated
\mathit{sp},\mathit{ur},\mathit{up},\mathit{nc},\allowbreak\mathit{tr}
and
\mathit{ad}
.
The properties defined in Definition
1

naturally extend to CGSPs: the CGSP

P=(M,\Omega_{M})
satisfies one of these properties
if the underlying CGS
M

satisfies it.
For every
X\subseteq\{\mathit{sp},\mathit{ur},\mathit{up},\mathit{nc},\mathit{tr},\allowbreak\mathit{ad}\}
,
the class of CGS satisfying the properties in
X

is denoted by
\mathbf{CP}^{X}
.
By
\mathbf{CP}^{\emptyset}
, we denote the class of all CGSP.

The following Lemma

2

is a tree-like model property for the language
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.
The proof of the lemma
is given in Appendix A in the supplementary material.
The proof relies on a three-step transformation. First, we transform
a CGSP into a CGSP with joint action disjointness by constructing
one copy of a state for each possible joint action. Second, we transform
the resulting CGSP with joint action disjointness into a CGSP with joint action disjointness, unique
predecessor and no cycles. This second transformation associates every state
of the original CGSP to a finite path. Third, we generate the submodel
from the point of evaluation of the original model
to guarantee unique rootness.

Lemma 0.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.
Then,

•

\varphi

is satisfiable
for the class
\mathbf{CP}^{\emptyset}

iff
\varphi

is satisfiable
for the class
\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{tr},\mathit{ad}\}}
,

•

\varphi

is satisfiable
for the class
\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp}\}}

iff
\varphi

is satisfiable
for the class
\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp},\mathit{tr},\mathit{ad}\}}
.

5.2.
Embedding

Let us consider
the following translation

\mathit{tr}:\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{ATL}}(\mathbb{P}^{+},\mathbb{AGT})

with
\mathbb{P}^{+}=\mathbb{P}\cup\big\{\mathit{rat}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{AGT}\big\}
:

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(p)

\displaystyle=p,

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(\neg\varphi)

\displaystyle=\neg\mathit{tr}(\varphi),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(\varphi\wedge\psi)

\displaystyle=\mathit{tr}(\varphi)\wedge\mathit{tr}(\psi),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\mathit{tr}(\varphi),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\mathit{tr}(\varphi),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}\big(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)\big)

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\big(\mathit{tr}(\varphi)\ \mathsf{U}\ \mathit{tr}(\psi)\big),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\big(\mathit{rat}_{C}\wedge\mathit{tr}(\varphi)\big),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\big(\mathit{rat}_{C}\wedge\mathit{tr}(\varphi)\big),

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}\big(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}(\varphi\ \mathsf{U}\ \psi)\big)

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\big((\mathit{rat}_{C}\wedge\mathit{tr}(\varphi))\ \mathsf{U}\ (\mathit{rat}_{C}\wedge\mathit{tr}(\psi))\big),

with
\mathit{rat}_{C}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathtt{def}}}{{=}}\ \bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{AGT}}\mathit{rat}_{i}

and the special atomic formula

\mathit{rat}_{i}

standing for “agent
i

is rational”.

The idea of the translation
is to transform
a rational capability
operator
into its
ordinary capability
counterpart using the special
atomic formulas

\mathit{rat}_{i}
. Specifically, the fact that
a coalition
C

has a rational strategy to ensure a given outcome
is translated into the fact that
the coalition
C

has a strategy
to force the outcome
by ensuring that all its members are rational.
As the following theorem highlights,
satisfiability
of
R-ATL
-formulas
is
reducible to
satisfiability
of
ATL
-formulas
using
the translation

\mathit{tr}
.
The proof of the theorem
is given in Appendix B in the supplementary material.
The proof relies on a non-trivial
construction which
transforms
a tree-like CGSP into a new tree-like CGSP
in which an atomic formula of type
\mathit{rat}_{i}

matches
the computations that are generated by a
non-dominated strategy of agent
i
.
The assumption of stable preferences is essential
to guarantee that this matching exists.

Theorem 3.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.
Then,

\varphi

is satisfiable for the class

\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp}\}}

iff
\big(\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{AGT}}\langle\!\langle\{i\}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\mathit{rat}_{i}\big)\wedge\mathit{tr}(\varphi)

is satisfiable for the class

\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp}\}}
.

As the following theorem highlights,
if we restrict to the language

\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})

the translation
\mathit{tr}

also
provides
an embedding for the general
class

\mathbf{CP}
.
The proof of Theorem
4

is a straightforward
adaptation
of the proof of Theorem
3
.
Instead of matching
an atomic formula

\mathit{rat}_{i}

with a computation,
for every state in a tree-like CGSP
we match
\mathit{rat}_{i}
with
a successor
of this state along a computation generated by a
non-dominated strategy of agent
i
.
The assumption of stable preferences
is no longer required
since the translation
of formula
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\varphi

only refers
to the truth values
of atoms
\mathit{rat}_{i}

in the next state.

Theorem 4.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.
Then,

\varphi

is satisfiable for the class

\mathbf{CP}

iff
\big(\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{AGT}}\langle\!\langle\{i\}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\mathit{rat}_{i}\big)\wedge\mathit{tr}(\varphi)

is satisfiable for the class

\mathbf{CP}
.

The following complexity result
is a direct corollary of Theorems
3
and
4
,
the fact that the size of
\mathit{tr}(\varphi)

is polynomial
in the size of the input formula
\varphi

and the fact that satisfiability checking
for
ATL

is
Exptime
-complete
(
van Drimmelen 2003
)

and
satisfiability checking
for
CL

is
Pspace
-complete
(
Pauly 2002
)
.

Corollary 0.

Checking satisfiability of
formulas in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
relative to the class
\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp}\}}

is
Exptime
-complete.
It is
Pspace
-complete
relative to
both classes

\mathbf{CP}

and
\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp}\}}
,
when
restricting to the fragment
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.

Before concluding this section,
we would like to highlight the fact that the translation

\mathit{tr}

from
the language
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
to
the language
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P}^{+},\mathbb{AGT})

is adequate
for the stable preference
semantics
only. It does not
work for the general
class
\mathbf{CP}
.
To see this,
it is sufficient to observe
that, on the one hand,
the following formula
is valid
for the general
class

\mathbf{CP}
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{C,p}=_{\mathit{def}}\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}(\mathit{rat}_{C}\wedge p)\to\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}(\mathit{rat}_{C}\wedge p).

Indeed,

\varphi_{C,p}

is a basic
validity
of
ATL
.
Moreover,
we have

\displaystyle\mathit{tr}\big(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\to\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\big)=\varphi_{C,p}.

But, on the other hand,
the formula

\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\to\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p

is not valid
for the
class

\mathbf{CP}
,
which is the same thing
as saying that

\neg\big(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\to\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\big)

is satisfiable for
\mathbf{CP}
.
A counter-model
for this formula
is given in Appendix C.

Thus, there is no analog
of Theorem
3

for the class

\mathbf{CP}

since there exists a formula

\varphi

(i.e.,
\neg\big(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\to\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}p\big)
)
which
is satisfiable for

\mathbf{CP}

and, at the same time,

\big(\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{AGT}}\langle\!\langle\{i\}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{G}\mathit{rat}_{i}\big)\wedge\mathit{tr}(\varphi)

is not satisfiable for

\mathbf{CP}

since
\neg\mathit{tr}(\varphi)

(i.e.,
\neg\neg\varphi_{C,p}

which is equivalent to
\varphi_{C,p}
)
is valid
for

\mathbf{CP}
.

Definition 0 (Literal).

-literal

and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
.

The following definition introduces

R-CL

Definition 0 (Standard
R-CL
disjunction).

A formula of type

\displaystyle\chi\vee

\displaystyle\big((\bigwedge_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\langle\!\langle C_{x}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\psi_{x}\wedge\bigwedge_{x\in\mathbb{X}^{\mathit{rat}}}\langle\!\langle C_{x}\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\psi_{x})\to

\displaystyle(\bigvee_{y\in\mathbb{Y}}\langle\!\langle C_{y}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\psi_{y}\vee\bigvee_{y\in\mathbb{Y}^{\mathit{rat}}}\langle\!\langle C_{y}\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\psi_{y})\big)

and

The following is a normal form

It is proved in a way analogous to the normal form lemma for propositional logic.

Lemma 0 (Normal form).

Any formula

\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})

is equivalent to a conjunction of standard

than the modal

Let

\displaystyle\chi\vee

\displaystyle\big((\bigwedge_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\langle\!\langle C_{x}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\psi_{x}\wedge\bigwedge_{x\in\mathbb{X}^{\mathit{rat}}}\langle\!\langle C_{x}\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\psi_{x})\to

\displaystyle(\bigvee_{y\in\mathbb{Y}}\langle\!\langle C_{y}\rangle\!\rangle\mathsf{X}\psi_{y}\vee\bigvee_{y\in\mathbb{Y}^{\mathit{rat}}}\langle\!\langle C_{y}\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}\psi_{y})\big)

The following definition

Define

and

Let

be a standard

R-CL

is valid
then following

is satisfied:

•

\chi

is valid, or

•

there is

and

7.
Model checking

The global model checking problem for
R-ATL
consists of computing, for a given CGSP
P
, and a formula
\varphi
, all the states in which
\varphi
holds in
P
, formally
\{w\in W:(P,w)\models\varphi\}
.
In this section, we consider this problem relative
to a subclass of CGSP
in which preferences are short-sighted.

Definition 0 (Short-sighted preferences).

Let
(M,\Omega_{M})

be a CGSP with
M=(W,\mathbb{ACT},\allowbreak\mathit{TRel},\mathcal{V})

a CGS. We say that
M

has short-sighted preferences
if
the following condition holds:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{SSP})

\displaystyle\ \forall i\in\mathbb{AGT},\forall w\in W,\forall\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathit{Comp}_{M,w}\text{ if }\lambda(1)=\lambda^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle\text{ then }\lambda^{\prime}\approx_{i,w}\lambda,

where

\lambda^{\prime}\approx_{i,w}\lambda

iff

\lambda^{\prime}\preceq_{i,w}\lambda

and

\lambda\preceq_{i,w}\lambda^{\prime}
.

The short-sighted preference
condition means that an agent
is indifferent between computations that
are equal until the next state.
Notice that if a CGSP
M
has
both stable and short-sighted preferences
in the sense of Definitions

7

and

1

then the following holds:

\displaystyle\forall i\in\mathbb{AGT},\forall v\in W,\text{ if }v\in\mathcal{R}(w_{0})\text{ then }\forall\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathit{Comp}_{M,v},\lambda^{\prime}\approx_{i,v}\lambda.

This means that under stable and
short-sighted preferences only
the successor states of
the initial state
w_{0}

affect an agent’s preferences
since from the next state on an agent has complete indifference between computations.

The reason why we verify properties relative to CGSPs
with short-sighted preferences
is to have an efficient model-checking procedure. Indeed, verifying properties
with respect to the general class of CGSPs
would make model checking exponential since we would need to
compute dominance by alternating between
sets of strategies of exponential size (similar to
(
Aminof
et al. 2021a
)
).

The proof of Theorem
2
is given in Appendix H. The lower-bound follows from the model checking of
ATL

(
Alur
et al. 2002
)
.
For the upper bound, we first define agents’ preference relation in state
w
over the successors of
w
. This allows us to define the notion of agents’ dominated actions at a given state
w
. We then reinterpret the rational strategic modalities
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}
,
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}
, and
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\ \mathsf{U}\
over dominated actions instead of dominated strategies, which is equivalent for the case of GCSP with short-sighted preferences. Then, we extend the model-checking algorithm for
ATL
to include the modalities
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{X}
,
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\mathsf{G}
, and
\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathit{rat}}\ \mathsf{U}\
. The resulting algorithm, provided in Appendix H, runs in polynomial time.

Theorem 2.

The global model checking problem for
R-ATL
over GCSP with short-sighted preferences is
Ptime
-complete.

8.
Conclusion

We have proposed a novel
semantic
analysis of preferences
in concurrent games and used
our semantics based
on CGS with preferences
to define a new family
of
ATL

and
CL

languages distinguishing
the notion
of ordinary
capability
from
the notion
of
rational

capability.
We have provided
a variety
of proof-theoretic
and complexity
results for our languages
with an emphasis on both
satisfiability
checking and model checking.

Directions of future work are manifold.
Some proof-theoretic
aspects remain to be explored and complexity results
to be proved. Future work
will be devoted to i) axiomatizing the full logic

R-ATL
relative to class

\mathbf{CP}
and to class

\mathbf{CP}^{\{\mathit{sp}\}}
, ii) studying complexity
of satisfiability
checking
for the language
\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-ATL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
relative to the general
class
\mathbf{CP}
.
In our running example, the joint strategy in which both agents cross the road is not dominated. This is because preferences are defined for individual agents rather than coalitions.
Following previous work on group preference logic
(
Lorini 2011
)
and judgment aggregation
(
Grossi and
Pigozzi 2014
;
Baumeister
et al. 2017
)
, we plan to extend our framework with the notion
of group preferences resulting from the aggregation of individual preferences.
We also intend to
investigate levels of rationality, where agents assume minimal rationality of their opponents, allowing for iterated strong dominance
(
Bonanno 2008
;
Lorini 2013
)
.
Last but not least, we plan to consider an epistemic extension
of our semantics and languages
to be able to model concurrent games with imperfect information
and an agent’s knowledge of its rational capability.
Since model checking
ATL
with imperfect information and perfect recall is undecidable
(
Dima and Tiplea 2011
)
, we may consider models with public actions
(
Belardinelli et al. 2020
)
and hierarchical information
(
Berthon et al. 2021
)
.

Appendix G
Proof of Theorem

Proof.

Proving soundness is a
routine exercise.
We just show completeness
by an induction on the modal
degrees of formulas.
The
0
-modal degree case
follows from
completeness of classical propositional logic.
Let

\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
with modal degree greater than
0
. Assume
\varphi

is valid
and for any

\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textsf{R-CL}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{AGT})
, if
the modal degree of
\psi
is lower
than
the modal degree of

\varphi
and
\psi
is valid, then
\vdash\psi
. We want to show that

\vdash\varphi
.
By the normal-form lemma (Lemma
4
),
\varphi\leftrightarrow\bigwedge_{k\in K}\varphi_{k}

is valid, where
K
is a set of indices and
\varphi_{k}
is a standard

R-CL
disjunction whose modal degree is not greater than
\varphi
. Fix
k\in K
. Then
\varphi_{k}

is valid. By the downward validity lemma (Lemma
7
), the validity-reduction condition for

\varphi_{k}
holds. By inductive hypothesis, the
derivability-reduction condition
for
\varphi_{k}
holds. By
the upward derivability lemma (Lemma

),
\vdash\varphi_{k}
. Therefore,
\vdash\bigwedge_{k\in K}\varphi_{k}

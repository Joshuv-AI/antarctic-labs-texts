Surveying Professional Writers on AI: Limitations, Expectations, and Fears
Anastasiia Ivanova, Natalia Fedorova, Sergey Tilga, Ekaterina Artemova

Abstract
The rapid development of AI-driven tools, particularly large language models (LLMs), is reshaping professional writing. Still, key aspects of their adoption such as languages support, ethics, and long-term impact on writers voice and creativity remain underexplored. In this work, we conducted a questionnaire ( N = 301 N=301 ) and an interactive survey ( N = 36 N=36 ) targeting professional writers regularly using AI. We examined LLM-assisted writing practices across 25+ languages, ethical concerns, and user expectations. The findings of the survey demonstrate important insights, reflecting upon the importance of: LLMs adoption for non-English speakers; the degree of misinformation, domain and style adaptation; usability and key features of LLMs. These insights can guide further development, benefiting both writers and a broader user base.

Introduction

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have led to their adoption across various professional occupations. While research has explored the integration of AI chatbots in area such as research writing
(
liao2024llms
)
, software engineering (
stackoverflow2024
), or creative industries
(
guo2024pen
)
, little attention has been paid to their adoption in professional writing. The latter can include e.g. journalism, corporate blogging, or technical writing. Since recently, LLMs in the form of AI chatbots have found their place among specialized tools for enhancing productivity of the writers and quality of their grammar and style. Still, their efficacy in practice is underexplored.

In this study, we aim to analyze professional writers experience from using LLMs.
To this end, we conduct a large-scale survey of professional writers. As noted by
(
kellogg2018
)
, professional writing differs from general writing by requiring not only strong writing skills but also expertise in a specific genre or domain. Our survey focuses on non-fiction writers who are compensated for their work.

The survey consists of two parts. First, we collect data from a questionnaire (N=301 respondents from 49 countries; see
Figure 1
) to understand best practices in AI chatbots adopted by professional writers, including the differences between writing in English and other languages, the limitations and expectations, and ethical concerns. Second, we conduct an interactive survey (N=36), in which writers showcase their best practices with LLMs.

Based on the survey, we seek to answer the following research questions:
RQ1.
How do professional writers use AI chatbots?
RQ2.
How do AI chatbots impact professional writing tasks?
RQ3.
How useful are they in languages other than English?
RQ4.
Do they well-represent and respect cultural knowledge across different user backgrounds?
RQ5.
What are the main limitations writers face when using AI chatbots, and what improvements do they expect in the future?
RQ6.

What are the authors’ expectations and fears as to the impact of AI chatbots to their job security, text authorship rights and ethics in writing?

Our findings in relation to these research questions highlight several important insights.
However, a significant gap in performance across languages takes place, which makes multilingual users prefer English when interacting with AI chatbots.
This highlights ongoing challenges in AI’s linguistic versatility.
Moreover, the professional writing community is diverse, with varying opinions on key aspects of AI use.
Writers differ in their views on AI’s role in authorship rights, its cultural and linguistic representation, and its potential impact on the future of the writing profession.

Methodology

Participant selection
All participants in our study are verified writers on
Toloka
, a crowdsourcing platform for data labeling and high-quality annotation for downstream applications in generative AI. The writers engaged in
Toloka
primarily work with textual data and have undergone a selection process based on their professional background.
We selected questionnaire participants through a semi-automated process, choosing writers based on their proficiency in languages, professional writing experience, and academic qualifications in related disciplines such as journalism, linguistics, and philology. All participants received compensation at their established hourly rates for their
Toloka
work. They were informed that the results would be published in anonymized form.

Questionnaire design
Our questionnaire is inspired by prior research exploring sentiment towards AI in professional and language communities
(
liao2024llms
;
stackoverflow2024
;
guo2024pen
;
blaschke-etal-2024-dialect
)
. To better position the questions and multiple choice answers options, we initially conducted video interviews with five volunteer writers that consisted of open-ended questions about their prior experience and attitude toward AI chatbots. For maximum diversity, the volunteers were selected as three females and two males, aged between 25 and over 50, speaking Zulu, Brazilian Portuguese, Hindi, Filipino, and Afrikaans. Their educational backgrounds varied: three held degrees in media studies, one in philology and literature, and one in an unrelated field. Each participant had at least ten years of professional experience in writing/editing. Based on their feedback, we developed an initial draft of the questionnaire, which was later shared with an extended group of 10 professional writers for further refinement over the structure and wording.

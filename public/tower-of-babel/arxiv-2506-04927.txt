1.
Introduction

We deal with the solvability of
p(t)
-Laplacian Liénard periodic boundary value problems of type

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}+f(u)u^{\prime}+g(u)=h(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T),\end{array}\right.

where
p:[0,T]\to(1,\infty)
is continuous, with
p(0)=p(T)
and

\phi_{p(t)}(x)=|x|^{p(t)-2}x\qquad(t\in[0,T],\;x\in\mathbb{R}).

The functions
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous and
h\in C:=C([0,T];\mathbb{R})
. Setting
C^{1}:=C^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
and

C_{T}^{1}:=\{v\in C^{1}\ |\ v(0)-v(T)=0=v^{\prime}(0)-v^{\prime}(T)\},

a
solution
of (
1.1
) is defined as being a function
u:[0,T]\to(0,\infty)
with
u\in C_{T}^{1}
and
\phi_{p(\cdot)}(u(\cdot))\in C^{1}
, which satisfies the differential equation in (
1.1
) everywhere on
[0,T]
.

As
g
is only defined on
(0,\infty)
, the emphasis is on the case where
g
has a singularity at
0
. When

\lim_{x\to 0+}g(x)=+\infty,\;\lim_{x\to+\infty}g(x)=0,

the restoring force
g
is called
attractive
, and when

\lim_{x\to 0+}g(x)=-\infty,\;\lim_{x\to+\infty}g(x)=0,

g
is called
repulsive
.

In the classical case where
p(t)=2
for all
t\in[0,T]
, the history of such problems has been given in
[

.
The first existence results for the problem (

) that, if

(A)

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to 0+}[g(x)-\|h\|_{\infty}]>0,\;\limsup_{x\to+\infty}[g(x)-\overline{h}]<0,

or if

(R)

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to 0+}[g(x)+\min\{0,\overline{h}\}<0,\;\liminf_{x\to+\infty}[g(x)+\overline{h}]>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,dx=-\infty,\;\limsup_{x\to+\infty}g(x)<+\infty,

the problem

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}+f(u)u^{\prime}+g(u)=h(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\

has a positive solution. This easily implies that when

2.
Some auxiliary problems

Below, by
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
we denote the sup-norm on
C
, the spaces
C^{1}
and
C_{T}^{1}
will be considered with the norm

\|v\|_{1}=\|v\|_{\infty}+\|v^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, while the usual norm on
L^{r}:=L^{r}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{r}}
(
1\leq r\leq\infty
). By
B(0,\rho)
we denote the open ball of radius
\rho>0
in
C_{T}^{1}
and by
\overline{B}(0,\rho)
its closure. Also, we use the notations

p_{-}:=\min_{t\in[0,T]}p(t),\qquad p^{+}:=\max_{t\in[0,T]}p(t).

Consider the general periodic problem

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}=\ell(t,u,u^{\prime})\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T),\end{array}\right.

where
\ell:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function; recall this means:

(
i
) for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, the function
l(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous;

(
ii
) for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
the function
l(\cdot,x,y)
is measurable;

(
iii
) for each
\rho>0
there is some
\alpha_{\rho}\in L^{1}
such that

|l(t,x,y)|\leq\alpha_{\rho}(t),\;\mbox{ for a.e. }t\in[0,T]\mbox{ and all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ with }|x|,|y|\leq\rho.

Definition 2.1
.

By a
solution
of problem (
2.1
) we understand a function
u\in C^{1}_{T}
with
\phi_{p(\cdot)}(u(\cdot))
absolutely continuous, which satisfies the differential equation in (
2.1
) a.e. on
[0,T]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
\ell:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
u
is a solution of (
2.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
, then
\phi_{p(\cdot)}(u(\cdot))\in C^{1}
and
u
satisfies the differential equation in (
2.1
) everywhere on
[0,T]
– this actually means that
u
is solution in classical sense.

Below in this section, we adopt a strategy similar to the one from
[
14
, Section 6]
for
p(t)
a constant
p
. We make use of the following continuation theorem, which is an immediate consequence of
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an bounded open set in
C_{T}^{1}
such that the following conditions hold.

(H_{1})

For each
\lambda\in(0,1)
the problem

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}=\lambda\ell(t,u,u^{\prime})\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)\end{array}\right.

has no solution on
\partial\Omega
.

(H_{2})

The equation

\mathcal{L}(a):=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\ell(t,a,0)dt=0

has no solution on
\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}
.

(H_{3})

The Brouwer degree

deg_{B}[\mathcal{L},\Omega\cap\mathbb{R},0]\neq 0.

Then problem (
2.1
) has a solution in
\overline{\Omega}
.

It will be sometimes useful to write an element
v\in L^{1}
as
v(t)=\overline{v}+\tilde{v}(t),
with

\overline{v}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}v(t)dt,\ \ \left(\mbox{whence }\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{v}(t)dt=0\right).

Recall, according to the Sobolev inequality, that any absolutely continuous function
v
satisfies

(2.3)

\|\tilde{v}\|_{\infty}\leq\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}.

We first consider the problem

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}+\theta(u)u^{\prime}-\varepsilon u=e(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T),\end{array}\right.

where
\varepsilon>0
is a parameter,
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
e\in L^{1}
is with
\overline{e}=0.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any
\varepsilon^{*}>0
there exists
R:=R(\varepsilon^{*})>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*}]
, problem (
2.4
) has at least one solution in
B(0,R)
and any solution
u
of (
2.4
) belongs to
B(0,R)
and satisfies
\overline{u}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon^{*}>0
be fixed and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*}]
be arbitrarily chosen. Set

\Theta(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{s}\theta(\sigma)d\sigma ds\quad(x\in\mathbb{R})

and note that, if
w\in C^{1}
, then
(\Theta^{\prime}(w))^{\prime}=\theta(w)\,w^{\prime}
. We apply Theorem
2.3
with
\ell(t,x,y)=e(t)+\varepsilon x-\theta(x)y
. In this view, let us consider the family of problems

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}+\lambda(\Theta^{\prime}(u))^{\prime}-\lambda\varepsilon u=\lambda e(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)\end{array}\right.

and let
u(t)=\overline{u}+\tilde{u}(t)
be a solution of (
2.5
) for some
\lambda\in(0,1]
. Integrating
both members of the equation in (
2.5
) over
[0,T]
, taking into account the periodic boundary conditions, together with
p(0)=p(T)
and
\overline{e}=0
, it follows
\overline{u}=0
.

Multiplying by
u
the equation in (
2.5
) and integrating by parts over
[0,T]
, we obtain

-\int_{0}^{T}\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))u^{\prime}(t)dt-\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\Theta^{\prime}(u(t))u^{\prime}(t)dt-\lambda\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}u^{2}(t)dt=\lambda\int_{0}^{T}e(t)u(t)dt,

or,

\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\left(\Theta(u(t))\right)^{\prime}dt+\lambda\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}u^{2}(t)dt=-\lambda\int_{0}^{T}(\overline{e}+\tilde{e}(t))u(t)dt,

which gives

\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt+\lambda\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}u^{2}(t)dt=-\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{e}(t)u(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{T}|\tilde{e}(t)u(t)|dt\leq\|\tilde{e}\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{\infty}.

Hence, using Sobolev inequality (
2.3
), we derive

\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt\leq\|\tilde{e}\|_{L^{1}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}},

which yields

(2.6)

\int\limits_{[|u^{\prime}(t)|>1]}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}dt\leq\int\limits_{[|u^{\prime}(t)|>1]}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt\leq\|\tilde{e}\|_{L^{1}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}.

On the other hand, as

\displaystyle\int\limits_{[|u^{\prime}(t)|\leq 1]}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}dt\leq\int\limits_{[|u^{\prime}(t)|\leq 1]}dt\leq T

and using (
2.6
) and Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T+\|\tilde{e}\|_{L^{1}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T+\|\tilde{e}\|_{L^{1}}T^{\frac{p_{-}-1}{p_{-}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}}.

Consequently,

(2.7)

T^{\frac{p_{-}}{p_{-}-1}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p_{-}}}\leq R_{1},

where
R_{1}:=R_{1}(T,\tilde{e},p_{-})
is the (positive) solution of the equation

x^{p_{-}}-\|\tilde{e}\|_{L^{1}}T^{\frac{p_{-}-1}{p_{-}}}x-T=0.

From (
2.3
) and (
2.7
), one has that

(2.8)

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq R_{2},

with
R_{2}:=T^{\frac{p_{-}-1}{p_{-}}}R_{1}
.

Next, as
u
is a solution of (
2.5
), and since Rolle theorem implies the existence of
\tau\in(0,T)
such that
u^{\prime}(\tau)=0
, we have, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\left|\int_{\tau}^{t}[e(s)-(\Theta^{\prime}(u(s)))^{\prime}+\varepsilon u(s)]ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e\|_{L_{1}}+R_{2}\max_{|v|\leq R_{2}}|\theta(v)|+\varepsilon^{*}R_{2}T\leq R_{3},

where

R_{3}:=R_{3}(\varepsilon^{*})=\max\{\|e\|_{L_{1}}+R_{2}\max_{|v|\leq R_{2}}|\theta(v)|+\varepsilon^{*}R_{2}T,1\}.

Hence, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.9)

|u^{\prime}(t)|\leq R_{3}^{\frac{1}{p(t)-1}}\leq R_{3}^{\frac{1}{p_{-}-1}}:=R_{4}(\varepsilon^{*}).

This, together with (
2.8
), shows that
\|u\|_{1}<R:=R_{2}+R_{4}(\varepsilon^{*})+1
and condition
(H_{1})
in Theorem
2.3
is fulfilled with
\Omega:=B(0,R)
.

To check the remaining conditions of Theorem
2.3
, we see that

\mathcal{L}(a):=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(e(t)+\varepsilon a)dt=\varepsilon a

and
a=0
is the unique solution of
\mathcal{L}(a)=0
. As
\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}=\{-R,R\}
, clearly
(H_{2})
is satisfied. Also,
deg_{B}[\mathcal{L},\Omega\cap\mathbb{R},0]=1
, hence
(H_{3})
holds true.
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

For a result in line with Theorem
2.4
, but for a Liénard system with Dirichlet boundary conditions we refer the reader to

Let
\ell:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

the Nemytskii operator
associated to
\ell
; recall, this is

N_{\ell}(v)(t)=\ell(t,v(t),v^{\prime}(t)),\quad(v\in C_{T}^{1},\,t\in[0,T])

and it is known that

is continuous and sends bounded sets from

into equi-integrable sets in

. Also, defining

we have that

is completely continuous and

is continuous and maps equi-integrable sets in

into relatively compact sets in

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\ell:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is Carathéodory, then
there exists a continuous operator
\mathcal{K}:L^{1}\to C_{T}^{1}

into relatively compact sets in

, such that
u\in C^{1}_{T}
is a solution of problem (
2.1
) if and only if it is a fixed point of the completely continuous mapping

\mathcal{G}_{\ell}:C_{T}^{1}\to C_{T}^{1},\ \ v\mapsto Pv+QN_{\ell}(v)+\mathcal{K}N_{\ell}(v).

Proof.

See Section 3 in
[
4
]
.

The following result extends the existence part of Theorem
2.4
to the case where
\varepsilon=0
.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
e\in L^{1}
satisfies
\overline{e}=0
, then problem

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}+\theta(u)u^{\prime}=e(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)\end{array}\right.

has at least one solution
u
with
\overline{u}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\ell_{\infty},\,\ell_{n}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\ell_{\infty}(t,x,y)=e(t)-\theta(x)y,\quad\ell_{n}=\ell_{\infty}(t,x,y)+\frac{1}{n}x,\quad(n\in\mathbb{N},\ (t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}).

From Theorem
2.4
there exists
R=R(1)
such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}=\ell_{n}(t,u,u^{\prime})\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)\end{array}\right.

has at least one solution
u_{n}
in
\overline{B}(0,R)
with
\overline{u}_{n}=0
. For such a solution
u_{n}
, by Lemma
2.6
, one has

(2.11)

u_{n}=\mathcal{G}_{\ell_{n}}(u_{n})\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell_{n}}(\overline{B}(0,R))\ \ (n\in\mathbb{N}).

Since
N_{\ell_{\infty}}(\overline{B}(0,R))
is an equi-integrable set in
L^{1}
, there exists
\eta\in L^{1}
such that
\left|N_{\ell_{\infty}}(v)\right|\leq\eta
for all
v\in\overline{B}(0,R)
. It follows that
\left|N_{\ell_{n}}(v)\right|\leq\eta+R,
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in\overline{B}(0,R)
, which implies that the set

\mathcal{E}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}N_{\ell_{n}}(\overline{B}(0,R))

is equi-integrable in
L^{1}
. Then, using

\displaystyle u_{n}\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell_{n}}(\overline{B}(0,R))

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle P(\overline{B}(0,R))+QN_{\ell_{n}}(\overline{B}(0,R))+\mathcal{K}N_{\ell_{n}}(\overline{B}(0,R))

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle P(\overline{B}(0,R))+Q(\mathcal{E})+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{E})=:\widehat{\mathcal{E}},\quad(n\in\mathbb{N})

and that
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}
is relatively compact in
C^{1}_{T}
, we get the existence of a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}\subset\{u_{n}\}
and an
u\in C_{T}^{1}
so that
u_{n_{k}}\to u
in
C_{T}^{1}
, with
k\to\infty
.
As

N_{\ell_{n_{k}}}(u_{n_{k}})=N_{\ell_{\infty}}(u_{n_{k}})+\frac{1}{n_{k}}u_{n_{k}},

we infer that

N_{\ell_{n_{k}}}(u_{n_{k}})\to N_{\ell_{\infty}}(u),\;\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Then, from

u_{n_{k}}=\mathcal{G}_{\ell_{n_{k}}}(u_{n_{k}})=Pu_{n_{k}}+Q\left(N_{\ell_{n_{k}}}(u_{n_{k}})\right)+\mathcal{K}\left(N_{\ell_{n_{k}}}(u_{n_{k}})\right),\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

it follows

u=Pu+QN_{\ell_{\infty}}(u)+\mathcal{K}N_{\ell_{\infty}}(u)=\mathcal{G}_{\ell_{\infty}}(u),

i.e.
u
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{G}_{\ell_{\infty}}
, meaning that
u
is a solution for problem (
2.10
) – by Lemma
2.6
. The fact that
\overline{u}=0
is straightforward using that
\overline{u}_{n_{k}}=0
, (
k\in\mathbb{N}
), and the proof is complete.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
e\in L^{1}
be with
\overline{e}=0
. Then, for any
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, problem

(2.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}+\theta(c+u)u^{\prime}=e(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)\end{array}\right.

has at least one solution
u
with
\overline{u}=0
and, for such a solution
u
, it holds

(2.13)

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq K,

where
K=K(T,p_{-},e)
is a constant independent of
\theta
,
c
and
u
.

Proof.

The existence of a solution
u
for (
2.12
) with
\overline{u}=0
follows from Theorem
2.7
. To prove the existence of
K
in (
2.13
), let
c_{0}=c_{0}(T,p_{-})
be a constant so that

(2.14)

\int_{0}^{T}|v(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt\leq c_{0}\int_{0}^{T}|v^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt,\quad(v\in W^{1,p_{-}},\,\overline{v}=0);

such a constant is known to exist by Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality. Here, we have denoted by
W^{1,p_{-}}
the Sobolev space
W^{1,p_{-}}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, which is considered with its usual norm
\|v\|_{W^{1,p_{-}}}=\left(\|v\|_{L^{p_{-}}}^{p_{-}}+\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{p_{-}}}^{p_{-}}\right)^{1/p_{-}}
. By the continuity of the embedding
W^{1,p_{-}}\subset C
, there is another constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(T,p_{-})
so that

(2.15)

\|v\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}\|v\|_{W^{1,p_{-}}},\quad(v\in W^{1,p_{-}}).

Multiplying the equation in (
2.12
) by
u
and integrating over
[0,T]
, we deduce

-\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt=\int_{0}^{T}e(t)u(t)dt,

whence

\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt\leq\|e\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{\infty}.

Using this, we estimate

(2.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{[|u^{\prime}(t)|\leq 1]}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt+\int\limits_{[|u^{\prime}(t)|>1]}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T+\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)}dt\leq T+\|e\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{\infty}.

Then, from (
2.14
) with
v=u
, and (
2.16
), it follows

(2.17)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p_{-}}}^{p_{-}}=\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt+\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p_{-}}\ dt\leq(c_{0}+1)(T+\|e\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{\infty})

and from (
2.15
) with
v=u
and (
2.17
), we obtain

\|u\|_{\infty}^{p_{-}}\leq c_{1}^{p_{-}}(c_{0}+1)(T+\|e\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{\infty}),

which implies the existence of a constant
K=K(T,p_{-},e)>0
such that (
2.13
) holds true.
∎

3.
Lower and upper solutions

It is well known that the method of lower and upper solutions is an efficient tool in providing the existence and localization of solutions of various elliptic boundary value problems (see e.g. the survey paper
[
2
]
and the monograph
[
3
]
).
This will be a key ingredient in our approach for problem (
1.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

A
lower solution
(resp.
upper solution
) of problem (
1.1
) is a
C^{1}
-function
\alpha:[0,T]\to(0,\infty)
with
\phi_{p(\cdot)}(\alpha(\cdot))\in C^{1}
,
\alpha(0)=\alpha(T)
,
\alpha^{\prime}(0)\geq\alpha^{\prime}(T)
(resp.
\beta:[0,T]\to(0,\infty)
with
\phi_{p(\cdot)}(\beta(\cdot))\in C^{1}
,
\beta(0)=\beta(T)
,
\beta^{\prime}(0)\leq\beta^{\prime}(T)
) and

(\phi_{p(t)}(\alpha^{\prime}))^{\prime}+f(\alpha)\alpha^{\prime}+g(\alpha)\geq h(t),\quad(t\in[0,T]),

\left(\mbox{resp.}\ (\phi_{p(t)}(\beta^{\prime}))^{\prime}+f(\beta)\beta^{\prime}+g(\beta)\leq h(t),\quad(t\in[0,T])\right).

The following lemma can be proved by elementary arguments.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
and
\varphi:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. If there are
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}\in[0,T]
such that
\varphi(\tau_{0})\leq a
and
\varphi(\tau_{1})\geq b
, then there exist
t_{0},t_{1}\in[0,T]
with
\varphi(t_{0})=a
,
\varphi(t_{1})=b
and

\mbox{if }t_{0}<t_{1},\mbox{ then }\varphi(t)\in[a,b],\qquad(t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]),

respectively,

\mbox{if }t_{1}<t_{0},\mbox{ then }\varphi(t)\in[a,b],\qquad(t\in[t_{1},t_{0}]).

Theorem 3.3
.

If problem (
1.1
) has a lower solution
\alpha
and an upper solution
\beta
such that
\alpha(t)\leq\beta(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
, then it has a solution
u
with
\alpha(t)\leq u(t)\leq\beta(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
be the continuous function given by

\gamma(t,x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\beta(t),&\mbox{ if}&x>\beta(t),\\
x,&\mbox{ if}&\alpha(t)\leq x\leq\beta(t),\\
\alpha(t),&\mbox{ if}&x<\alpha(t)\end{array}\right.

and define
\ell^{*}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\ell^{*}(t,x,y)=h(t)-g(\gamma(t,x))-f(\gamma(t,x))y+x-\gamma(t,x).

Note that, as
\ell^{*}
is continuous, below the notion of solution will be understood in classical sense (see Remark
2.2
). We introduce the modified problem

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}=\ell^{*}(t,u,u^{\prime})\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)\end{array}\right.

and, in order to apply Theorem
2.3
, for
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we consider the homotopy problem associated with (
3.1
)

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}=\lambda\ell^{*}(t,u,u^{\prime})\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
u(0)-u(T)=0=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T).\end{array}\right.

Next, let
u\equiv u_{\lambda}
be a solution of problem (
3.2
) for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
. We choose
M_{1}>\|\beta\|_{\infty}
a constant such that

(3.3)

h(t)-g(\beta(t))+M_{1}-\beta(t)>0\ \mbox{ and }\ h(t)-g(\alpha(t))-M_{1}-\alpha(t)<0\quad(t\in[0,T])

and we show that
\|u\|_{\infty}<M_{1}
. Otherwise, suppose that there exists
t_{0}\in[0,T]
such that
u(t_{0})=\max_{t\in[0,T]}u(t)\geq M_{1}
or
u(t_{0})=\min_{t\in[0,T]}u(t)\leq-M_{1}
.
If
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
u(t_{0})\geq M_{1}
, then
u^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
and since
u
is a solution of (
3.2
), on account of
M_{1}>\|\beta\|_{\infty}
and (
3.3
), one has

\displaystyle(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}_{t=t_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\ell^{*}(t_{0},u(t_{0}),0)=\lambda(h(t_{0})-g(\beta(t_{0}))+u(t_{0})-\beta(t_{0}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda(h(t_{0})-g(\beta(t_{0}))+M_{1}-\beta(t_{0}))>0.

So, there exists a positive constant
\delta
such that
\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))
is strictly increasing on
(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta)
. Thus,

\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))>\phi_{p(t_{0})}(u^{\prime}(t_{0}))=0,\quad(t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta)),

which shows that
u^{\prime}(t)>0,\ t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta)
. Hence,
u
is strictly increasing on
(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta)
, contradicting
u(t_{0})=\max_{t\in[0,T]}u(t)
. In a similar way, if
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
u(t_{0})\leq-M_{1}
, since
-M_{1}<\alpha(t)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
and using the second inequality in (
3.3
), we obtain

(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}_{t=t_{0}}=\lambda\ell^{*}(t_{0},u(t_{0}),0)\leq\lambda(h(t_{0})-g(\alpha(t_{0}))-M_{1}-\alpha(t_{0}))<0,

which implies that there is
\delta>0
such that
u
is strictly decreasing on
(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta)
, i.e. again a contradiction with
u(t_{0})=\min_{t\in[0,T]}u(t)
.

If
t_{0}=0
or
t_{0}=T
and
u(0)=u(T)\geq M_{1}
, then
u^{\prime}(0)\leq 0
and
u^{\prime}(T)\geq 0
implying that
u^{\prime}(0)=0
and arguing as above we get a contradiction. The case when
t_{0}=0
or
t_{0}=T
under the assumption
u(0)=u(T)\leq-M_{1}
can be treated by similar arguments.

Next, we claim that there exists a constant
M_{2}>0
, such that
\|u^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<M_{2}
. To prove this, observe first that

(3.4)

|(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}|\leq|\ell^{*}(t,u,u^{\prime})|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}+c_{1}+c_{2}|u^{\prime}|+2M_{1}\leq c_{3}(1+|u^{\prime}|),\quad(t\in[0,T]),

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are positive constants depending on
M_{1}
. Since
u(0)=u(T)
, there is some
\tau_{0}\in[0,T]
such that
u^{\prime}(\tau_{0})=0
. Then, obviously one has

(3.5)

0=|u^{\prime}(\tau_{0})|^{p(\tau_{0})-1}<\left(\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1}.

We choose the constant
M_{2}>1
such that

\frac{M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}-\left(\displaystyle\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1}}{c_{3}}>T+2M_{1}

and suppose by contradiction that there is a point
\tau_{1}\in[0,T]
such that
|u^{\prime}(\tau_{1})|\geq M_{2}
, implying that

(3.6)

|u^{\prime}(\tau_{1})|^{p(\tau_{1})-1}\geq M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}.

In view of (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and Lemma
3.2
, there exist
t_{0},t_{1}\in[0,T]
with

|u^{\prime}(t_{0})|^{p(t_{0})-1}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1},\ \ |u^{\prime}(t_{1})|^{p(t_{1})-1}=M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}

and

\mbox{if }t_{0}<t_{1},\mbox{ then }|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)-1}\in\left[\left(\displaystyle\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1},M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}\right],\ \ (t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]),

respectively,

\mbox{if }t_{1}<t_{0},\mbox{ then }|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)-1}\in\left[\left(\displaystyle\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1},M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}\right],\ \ (t\in[t_{1},t_{0}]).

We discuss the case
t_{0}<t_{1}
; similar arguments work when
t_{1}<t_{0}
. Using the first change of variables formula, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle T+2M_{1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}-\left(\displaystyle\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1}}{c_{3}}=\int\limits_{\left(\frac{2M_{1}}{T}+1\right)^{p^{+}-1}}^{M_{2}^{p_{-}-1}}\frac{1}{c_{3}}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|u^{\prime}(t_{0})|^{p(t_{0})-1}}^{|u^{\prime}(t_{1})|^{p(t_{1})-1}}\frac{1}{c_{3}}\ ds=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\frac{(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{p(t)-1})^{\prime}}{c_{3}}\ dt.

Note that
u^{\prime}
does not vanish in
[t_{0},t_{1}]
. If
u^{\prime}(t)
is strictly positive on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
, then on account of (
3.7
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle T+2M_{1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\frac{(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}}{c_{3}}\ dt=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\frac{\lambda\ell^{*}(t,u,u^{\prime})}{c_{3}}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(1+u^{\prime}(t))dt\leq T+|u(t_{1})-u(t_{0})|<T+2M_{1},

i.e., a contradiction. Similarly, if
u^{\prime}(t)
is strictly negative on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
, one gets

\displaystyle T+2M_{1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\frac{-(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}))^{\prime}}{c_{3}}\ dt=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\frac{-\lambda\ell^{*}(t,u,u^{\prime})}{c_{3}}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(1-u^{\prime}(t))dt\leq T+|u(t_{0})-u(t_{1})|<T+2M_{1},

a contradiction, again.

Hence,
\|u\|_{1}<M_{0}
, where
M_{0}:=M_{1}+M_{2}
, and we shall apply Theorem
2.3
with
\Omega:=B(0,M_{0})
and
\ell=\ell^{*}
.
Clearly,
(H_{1})
in Theorem
2.3
is satisfied.
From (
3.3
), we have that
\ell^{*}(t,-M_{0},0)<0
and
\ell^{*}(t,M_{0},0)>0
for all
t\in[0,T]
, which yield

\mathcal{L}(-M_{0})=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\ell^{*}(t,-M_{0},0)dt<0,\ \ \ \mathcal{L}(M_{0})=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\ell^{*}(t,M_{0},0)dt>0

and, as
\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}=\{-M_{0},M_{0}\}
, hypothesis
(H_{2})
is fulfilled. Finally,
deg_{B}[\mathcal{L},\Omega\cap\mathbb{R},0]=1
, hence
(H_{3})
also holds true. Therefore, problem (
3.1
) has at least one solution in
\overline{\Omega}
.

We conclude the proof by showing that if
u
is a solution of (
3.1
), then
\alpha(t)\leq u(t)\leq\beta(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Suppose by contradiction that there is some
t_{0}\in[0,T]
such that

\max_{t\in[0,T]}(\alpha(t)-u(t))=\alpha(t_{0})-u(t_{0})>0.

If
t_{0}\in(0,T)
, then
\alpha^{\prime}(t_{0})=u^{\prime}(t_{0})
and there is a sequence
\{t_{k}\}
in
(t_{0},T)
converging to
t_{0}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}(t_{k})-u^{\prime}(t_{k})\leq 0
. As
\phi_{p(t)}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

is an increasing homeomorphism for all
t\in[0,T])
, this yields

\phi_{p(t_{k})}(\alpha^{\prime}(t_{k}))-\phi_{p(t_{0})}(\alpha^{\prime}(t_{0}))\leq\phi_{p(t_{k})}(u^{\prime}(t_{k}))-\phi_{p(t_{0})}(u^{\prime}(t_{0})),

implying that

\left(\phi_{p(t)}(\alpha^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=t_{0}}^{\prime}\leq\left(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=t_{0}}^{\prime}.

Thus, because
\alpha^{\prime}(t_{0})=u^{\prime}(t_{0})
and
\alpha
is a lower solution of (
1.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{p(t)}(\alpha^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=t_{0}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=t_{0}}^{\prime}=\ell^{*}(t_{0},u(t_{0}),u^{\prime}(t_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(t_{0})-g(\alpha(t_{0}))-f(\alpha(t_{0}))\alpha^{\prime}(t_{0})+u(t_{0})-\alpha(t_{0})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle h(t_{0})-g(\alpha(t_{0}))-f(\alpha(t_{0}))\alpha^{\prime}(t_{0})\leq\left(\phi_{p(t)}(\alpha^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=t_{0}}^{\prime},

a contradiction. If

\max_{t\in[0,T]}(\alpha(t)-u(t))=\alpha(0)-u(0)=\alpha(T)-u(T)>0,

then
\alpha^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(0)\leq 0
and
\alpha^{\prime}(T)-u^{\prime}(T)\geq 0
. Since
\alpha
is a lower solution of (
1.1
), one has
\alpha^{\prime}(0)\geq\alpha^{\prime}(T)
and using this, we get that
\alpha^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(0)=0=\alpha^{\prime}(T)-u^{\prime}(T)
. It follows
\phi_{p(0)}(\alpha^{\prime}(0))=\phi_{p(0)}(u^{\prime}(0))
. On the other hand,
\max_{t\in[0,T]}(\alpha(t)-u(t))=\alpha(0)-u(0)>0
implies, reasoning as above, that

\left(\phi_{p(t)}(\alpha^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=0}^{\prime}\leq\left(\phi_{p(t)}(u^{\prime}(t))\right)_{t=0}^{\prime}.

Using this inequality, together with
\alpha^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(0)
and the fact that
\alpha
is a lower solution of (
1.1
), we again proceed as above to obtain a contradiction. Consequently,
\alpha(t)\leq u(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Analogously, using that
\beta
is an upper solution of problem (
1.1
), it follows that
u(t)\leq\beta(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The variant of Theorem
3.3
with an increasing and odd homeomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
instead of
\phi_{p(t)}
, can be inferred as a particular case of
[
20
, Theorem 8.12]
.

4.
Main result

We are now in position to give the main result concerning problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

If there exists a constant
\alpha>0
with
g(\alpha)\geq\max_{[0,T]}h
and it holds

(4.1)

\limsup_{x\to+\infty}g(x)<\overline{h},

then problem (
1.1
) has at least one solution
u
with
u(t)\geq\alpha
, for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\alpha
is a lower solution of problem (
1.1
). On the other hand, from assumption (
4.1
), there exists a constant
\delta>\alpha
such that
g(x)<\overline{h}
, for all
x\geq\delta
. We define the continuous function
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(0),&\hbox{if $x<0$,}\\
f(x),&\hbox{if $x\geq 0$}\end{array}\right.

and for
c\in\mathbb{R}
let us consider the problem

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(v^{\prime}))^{\prime}+f_{0}(c+v)v^{\prime}=\tilde{h}(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
v(0)-v(T)=0=v^{\prime}(0)-v^{\prime}(T).\end{array}\right.

which is of type (
2.12
). Then, by Proposition
2.8
, there is a constant
K=K(T,p_{-},\tilde{h})>0
so that any solution
v
of (
4.2
) with
\overline{v}=0
satisfies
\|v\|_{\infty}\leq K
. Note that a solution of (
4.2
) is a classical one (see Remark
2.2
).

We choose
c_{\delta}=\delta+K
and let
v_{\delta}
with
\overline{v}_{\delta}=0
be a solution of problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{p(t)}(v^{\prime}))^{\prime}+f_{0}(c_{\delta}+v)v^{\prime}=\tilde{h}(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
v(0)-v(T)=0=v^{\prime}(0)-v^{\prime}(T).\end{array}\right.

One has that
\beta(t):=c_{\delta}+v_{\delta}(t)\geq c_{\delta}-K=\delta
and

(\phi_{p(t)}(\beta^{\prime}(t)))^{\prime}+f(\beta(t))\beta^{\prime}(t)+g(\beta(t))

=(\phi_{p(t)}(v_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)))^{\prime}+f_{0}(c_{\delta}+v_{\delta}(t))v_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)+g(c_{\delta}+v_{\delta}(t))<\tilde{h}(t)+\overline{h}=h(t)\quad(t\in[0,T]),

i.e.
\beta
is an upper solution for (
1.1
). Thus, as
\beta(t)\geq\alpha
for all
t\in[0,T]
, by Theorem
3.3
there exists
a solution
u
of problem (
1.1
) with
\alpha\leq u(t)\leq\beta(t)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If the continuous function
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfies

.

Example 4.4
.

If
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous,

,

and

, then problem

\\

The homotopy category of strongly flat modules
Javad Asadollahi and Somayeh Sadeghi

Abstract
. In this paper, we plan to build upon significant results by Amnon Neeman regarding the homotopy category of flat modules to study 𝕂 ⁡ ( S ​ SF ​ - ​ R ) {\mathbb{K}}({S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R) , the homotopy category of S S -strongly flat modules, where S S is a multiplicatively closed subset of a commutative ring R R . The category 𝕂 ⁡ ( S ​ SF ​ - ​ R ) {\mathbb{K}}({S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R) is an intermediate triangulated category that includes 𝕂 ⁡ ( Prj ​ - ​ R ) {\mathbb{K}}({\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R) , the homotopy category of projective R R -modules, which is always well generated by a result of Neeman, and is included in 𝕂 ⁡ ( Flat ​ - ​ R ) {\mathbb{K}}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R) , the homotopy category of flat R R -modules, which is well generated if and only if R R is perfect, by a result of Št́ovíček. We analyze corresponding inclusion functors and the existence of their adjoints. In this way, we provide a new, fully faithful embedding of the homotopy category of projectives to the homotopy category of S S -strongly flat modules. We introduce the notion of S S -almost well generated triangulated categories. If R R is an S S -almost perfect ring, 𝕂 ⁡ ( Flat ​ - ​ R ) {\mathbb{K}}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R) is S S -almost well generated. We show that the converse is true under certain conditions on the ring R R . We hope that this approach provides insights into the largely mysterious class of S S -strongly flat modules.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category. Let
\mathscr{X}
be the class of objects in
\mathscr{A}
. An
\mathscr{X}
-precover of an object

, is a morphism

with

, such that, for every

, the induced morphism
{\rm{Hom}}(X^{\prime},X)\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}(X^{\prime},M)\longrightarrow 0
is exact. If, further, for every

,

implies that

is an isomorphism, then

is an
\mathscr{X}
-cover. A class
\mathscr{X}
is precovering, resp. covering, if every object in
\mathscr{A}
has an
\mathscr{X}
-precover, resp.
\mathscr{X}
-cover. Dually, we can define the notions of
\mathscr{X}
-preenvelope,
\mathscr{X}
-envelope, preenveloping and enveloping classes.

Recall that a pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
of classes of objects of

is a cotorsion pair if
\mathscr{X}^{\perp_{1}}=\mathscr{Y}
and
{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{Y}=\mathscr{X}
, where orthogonal is taken with respect to
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}
.

A cotorsion pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is complete if for every object
M
in
\mathscr{A}
, there are short exact sequences

0\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0\ \ {\rm{and}}\ \ 0\rightarrow M\rightarrow Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow 0,

such that
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{X}
and
Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Y}.
This, in particular, implies that
\mathscr{X}
is a precovering class and
\mathscr{Y}
is a preenveloping class.

A cotorsion pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is hereditary if
\mathscr{X}
is closed under taking kernels of epimorphisms between objects of
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
is closed under taking cokernels of monomorphisms between objects of
\mathscr{Y}
.

5.

Recall that a full subcategory

Facts 5.1
.

Let

be a triangulated category and

be a thick subcategory of

. Then the following are equivalent.

has a right adjoint.

(ii)

such that

in

and

(iii)

The quotient functor

has a right adjoint.

(iv)

The composition
\mathscr{U}^{\perp}\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{U}
is an equivalence.

, Proposition 1.4]

Proposition 5.2
.

Let

-precover.

(ii)

Back to our case, let

6.

In this section, we define and study the notion of

-almost perfect ring, then
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
is

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a triangulated category with coproducts. Recall that
\mathscr{T}
is well generated if it has an

-small generators, for

. Such a set is called an

For a subcategory

of

-modules, we let

be the class of all direct summands of arbitrary direct sums of objects from
\mathscr{X}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an additive full subcategory of

-modules such that it is closed under coproducts. We say that
\mathbb{K}(\mathscr{X})
, the homotopy category of

\mathbb{K}({\rm{Add}}(\mathscr{X}\otimes_{R}R_{S}))
and
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Add}}(\mathscr{X}\otimes_{R}R/sR))
, for every

, are well generated.

.

R
be a commutative ring and

be a multiplicative subset.

(i)

Assume

, then

and

s\in S
,
{\mathbb{O}S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR\subset{\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R/sR
. Moreover, since
{S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\subset{\mathbb{O}S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R
there are inclusions

{S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S}\subset{\mathbb{O}S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S}\subset{\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R_{S},

and for every
s\in S
,

{S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR\subset{\mathbb{O}S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR\subset{\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R/sR.

Now
(ii)
implies that
{\rm{Add}}({\mathbb{O}S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S})={\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R_{S}
, and for every
s\in S
,

{\rm{Add}}({\mathbb{O}S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR)={\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R/sR.

Hence the homotopy categories of these subcategories are well generated.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
S\subset R
be a multiplicative subset. Consider the following statements.

(i)

R
is
S
-almost perfect.

(ii)

\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)=\mathbb{K}({S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R)
.

(iii)

\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
is
S
-almost well generated.

Then
(i)
is equivalent to
(ii)
and
(ii)
implies
(iii)
.

Proof.

(i)\Longleftrightarrow(ii)
. Ring
R
is
S
-almost perfect if and only if
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R={S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R
if and only if
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)=\mathbb{K}({S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R)
.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
. Since by Example
6.2

(ii)
,
\mathbb{K}({S\rm{SF}}\mbox{-}R)
is
S
-almost well generated, so is
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
.
∎

In the following we investigate special rings such that
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
being
S
-almost well generated implies that
R
is
S
-almost perfect.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
S\subset R
be a multiplicative subset.

(i)

\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S})=\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R_{S})
.

(ii)

Assume that for every
s\in S
, the ring
R/sR
is perfect. Then

\mathbb{K}({\rm{Add}}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR))=\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R/sR).

Proof.

(i)
We show that
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S}={\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R_{S}
, so the result follows.
Note that
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S}\subset{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R_{S}
. Now let
X
be an object in
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R_{S}
. Since
R_{S}
is a flat
R
-module, then
X
is a flat
R
-module. Moreover, since
X
is
R_{S}
-module, we have
X\simeq X\otimes_{R}R_{S}
. Hence
X
is an object in
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S}
and therefore
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R_{S}\subset{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S}
.

(ii)
Note that, for every
s\in S
,
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR\subset{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R/sR
. Now let
X\in{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R/sR
. Since for every
s\in S
,
R/sR
is perfect so we have
{\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R/sR={\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R/sR
. Then Example
6.2
(i)
implies that, for every
s\in S
,
{\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R/sR={\rm{Add}}({\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R\otimes_{R}R/sR)
, so
X
is in
{\rm{Add}}({\rm{Prj}\mbox{-}}R\otimes_{R}R/sR)\subset{\rm{Add}}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR)
.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of Krull dimension
1
and
S\subset R
be a multiplicative subset consisting of some nonzero-divisors in
R
. Then
R
is
S
-almost perfect if and only if
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
is
S
-almost well generated.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.3
, it is enough to show that if
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
is
S
-almost well generated, then
R
is
S
-almost perfect. Since
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
is
S
-almost well generated,
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R_{S})
and
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Add}}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R\otimes_{R}R/sR))
are well generated. Lemma
6.4

(i)
implies that
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R_{S})
is well generated. Hence by using
[
S
, Theorem 5.2]
,
R_{S}
is perfect. To complete the proof, we need to show that for every
s\in S
,
R/sR
is also perfect. To see this, we just note that the assumption on the ring
R
implies that the quotient ring
R/sR
, for every
s\in S
, is Artinian and hence is perfect.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

Assume that
\mathbb{K}({\rm{Flat}}\mbox{-}R)
is
S
-almost well generated. By the proof of Theorem
6.5
, we conclude that the ring
R_{S}
is always perfect. However, for arbitrary ring
R
, we do not know whether the quotient ring
R/sR
, for
s\in S
, is perfect.

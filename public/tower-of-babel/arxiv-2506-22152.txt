Multiple sign-changing and semi-nodal normalized solutions for a Gross-Pitaevskii type system on bounded domain: the L 2 L^{2} -supercritical case
Tianhao Liu, Linjie Song, Qiaoran Wu, Wenming Zou

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the existence of multiple sign-changing and semi-nodal normalized solutions for an m m -coupled elliptic system of the Gross-Pitaevskii type: { − Δ u j + λ j u j = ∑ k = 1 m β k ​ j u k 2 u j , u j ∈ H 0 1 ( Ω ) , ∫ Ω u j 2 d x = c j , j = 1 , 2 , ⋯ , m . \left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u_{j}+\lambda_{j}u_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\beta_{kj}u_{k}^{2}u_{j},\quad u_{j}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\ &\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{2}dx=c_{j},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,m.\end{aligned}\right. (0.1) Here, Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} ( N = 3 , 4 N=3,4 ) is a bounded domain. The constants β k ​ j ≠ 0 \beta_{kj}\neq 0 and c j > 0 c_{j}>0 are prescribed constants, while λ 1 , ⋯ , λ m \lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m} are unknown and appear as Lagrange multipliers. This is the first result in the literature on the existence and multiplicity of sign-changing and semi-nodal normalized solutions of couple Schrödinger system in all regimes of β k ​ j \beta_{kj} . The main tool which we use is a new skill of vector linking and this article attempts for the first time to use linking method to search for solutions of a coupled system. Particularly, to obtain semi-nodal normalized solutions, we introduce partial vector linking which is new up to our knowledge. Moreover, by investigating the limit process as c → = ( c 1 , … , c m ) → 0 → \vec{c}=\left(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\right)\to\vec{0} we obtain some bifurcation results. Note that when N = 4 N=4 , the system is of Sobolev critical. Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be either the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or a bounded regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The Schrödinger system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{j}=\Delta\Phi_{j}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\beta_{kj}\left\lvert\Phi_{k}\right\rvert^{2}\Phi_{j}&\quad\text{ for }\penalty\ x\in\Omega,\penalty\ \penalty\ t>0,\\
\Phi_{j}=\Phi_{j}(x,t)\in\mathbb{C},\quad\Phi_{j}(x,t)=0&\quad\text{ for }\penalty\ x\in\partial\Omega,\penalty\ \penalty\ t>0,\end{aligned}\quad j=1,2,\cdots,m,\right.

(1.1)

known in the literature as the Gross–Pitaevskii equations, has been intensively studied in recent years. Such system has applications in many physical models, especially in the Hartree–Fock theory for Bose–Einstein condensates with multiple states (see
[
2
,
31
,

,

,

Physically, the solutions

are the corresponding condensate amplitudes, the parameters
\beta_{jj}
and
\beta_{kj}(k\neq j)
are the intraspecies and interspecies scattering lengths, respectively. The sign of
\beta_{jj}
describes the interaction between particles of the same condensate:
\beta_{jj}>0
means attractive (or cooperative) interaction, while
\beta_{jj}<0
means repulsive (or competitive) interaction. Analogously,
\beta_{kj}(k\neq j)
describes the interaction between

In what follows, we always refer to
\beta_{jj}>0
for every

as the
focusing
case, and
\beta_{jj}<0
for every

case.

Problem (
1.1
) also arises in nonlinear optics, see
[
1
,
32
,
45
]

denotes the

component of beam in Kerr-like photorefractive media. The positive constant
\beta_{jj}
is for self-focusing in the

component of the beam, and the coupling

[

24

25

The ansatz
\Phi_{j}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{j}t}u_{j}(x)
,

, for standing wave solutions of system (

) leads to the following

-coupled nonlinear system of elliptic equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u_{j}+\lambda_{j}u_{j}=\mu_{j}u_{j}^{3}+\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{m}\beta_{kj}u_{k}^{2}u_{j}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&\penalty\ u_{j}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\quad j=1,2,\cdots,m,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu_{j}:=\beta_{jj}
for

.

There are two different research approaches and paths concerning the equation (
1.2
):
One direction is to fix the parameter
\lambda_{j}
for every

, which is referred to as

; on this line it has already achieved fruitful results. Another direction is to investigate solutions

to (

) that the

possesses a prescribed

-norm, which are commonly referred to as

. Recall that the

-norm of

is conserved in time for a solution

In spite of the clear physical relevance of solutions with prescribed

-norm, their
existence and properties are much less understood compared with solutions for fixed frequency problem. Therefor, this article focus on the system (
1.2
) with
the following constraint:

\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{2}dx=c_{j},\penalty\ \penalty\ j\in\left\{1,\cdots,m\right\}.

(1.3)

To better demonstrate our results, let’s first introduce a few concepts. Let
\vec{u}:=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m})
be a solution of (

). A solution

is called

if all of its components are nontrivial, that is,

Let

-constraints set:

In this way, the constraint variational method is an effective means and therefore the parameters

2
Vector link and the minimax level

2.1
Vector link

We introduce a new notion of (partial) vector link on product manifolds in an abstract setting. Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}
(
i\in\left\{1,\cdots,m\right\}
) stand for some
C^{2}
-Finsler manifolds, and let us define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(m)}:=\mathcal{M}_{1}\times\mathcal{M}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{M}_{m}.

Definition 2.1

(Abstract vector link)
.

Let
A
be a compact subset of
\mathcal{M}^{(m)}
with
D\subset A
. Then the set
A
is said to be linked to a subset
S\subset\mathcal{M}^{(m)}
, if
D\cap S=\emptyset
, and for each
h\in C(A,\mathcal{M}^{(m)})
such that
h|_{D}=\textbf{id}
, there holds

h(A)\cap S\neq\emptyset.

Definition 2.2

(Abstract partial vector link)
.

Let
d
be an integer with
1\leq d\leq m-1
. Define the projection map by

\displaystyle T_{d}:\mathcal{M}^{(m)}\to\mathcal{M}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{M}_{d},\quad\vec{u}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m})\mapsto(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d}).

Let
A
be a compact subset of
\mathcal{M}^{(m)}
with
D\times\left\{\vec{v}_{m-d}\right\}\subset A
where
D\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{M}_{d}
and
\vec{v}_{m-d}\in\mathcal{M}_{d+1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{M}_{m}
. Then the set
A
is said to be partially
d
-linked to a subset
S\subset\mathcal{M}^{(d)}
, if
D\cap S=\emptyset
, and for each
h\in C(A,\mathcal{M}^{(m)})
such that
h|_{D\times\left\{\vec{v}_{m-d}\right\}}=\textbf{id}
, there holds

T_{d}\circ h(A)\cap S\neq\emptyset.

Remark 2.3
.

There is no fundamental difference between Definition
2.1
and the usual notion of link in the literature, by seeing
\mathcal{M}^{(m)}
as a
C^{2}
-Finsler manifold; while Definition
2.2
, the partial vector link, is essentially new.

In what follows, we will use Definition
2.1
to construct minimax values that yield sign-changing normalized solutions for (
1.2
), and Definition
2.2
to derive minimax values that produce semi-nodal normalized solutions for (
1.2
).

2.2
The minimax level for sign-changing normalized solutions

Recall that the eigenvalues of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfy

0<\Lambda_{1}<\Lambda_{2}\leq\Lambda_{3}\leq\cdots.

(2.1)

Let
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the family of the unit eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}({\Omega})
with
\phi_{1}>0
. Then
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\overline{\text{span}\{\phi_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}}
. In our application later, we are particularly interested in the integer
k
such that
\Lambda_{k}<\Lambda_{k+1}
, of which there are infinitely many since
\Lambda_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
k
-dimensional subspace of
H_{k}
of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
H_{k}:={\text{span}\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{k}\}}
and its orthogonal complement as
H_{k}^{\bot}:=\overline{\text{span}\{\phi_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N},i\geq k+1\}}
. We then introduce the product spaces

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}:=\underbrace{H_{k}\times H_{k}\times\cdots\times H_{k}}_{m},\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\bot}:=\underbrace{H_{k}^{\bot}\times H_{k}^{\bot}\times\cdots\times H_{k}^{\bot}}_{m}.

(2.2)

Then, for all
k\geq 1
, all the components of fully nontrivial elements of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\bot}
are sign-changing.
Set

\mathbb{S}_{k}:=S_{\vec{c}}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{k},\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}:=S_{\vec{c}}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\bot},

(2.3)

then one can observe that
\mathbb{S}_{k}\cong\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}_{k}^{2}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}_{k}^{m}
, where

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{k}^{i}:=\bigg\{u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{i,j}\sqrt{c_{i}}\phi_{j}:\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{i,j}^{2}=1\bigg\},\quad i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}.

(2.4)

For each
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}
, we introduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{k+1}^{i}:=\bigg\{u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}t_{i,j}\sqrt{c_{i}}\phi_{j}:\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}t_{i,j}^{2}=1\penalty\ \text{ with }\penalty\ t_{i,k+1}\geq 0\bigg\},

(2.5)

and let

\mathbb{M}_{k+1}:=\mathbb{M}_{k+1}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{M}_{k+1}^{m}
. It follows that
\mathbb{S}_{k}\subset\mathbb{M}_{k+1}
since
\partial\mathbb{M}_{k+1}^{i}=\mathbb{S}_{k}^{i}
.

Let

\displaystyle S_{c_{i}}:=\bigg\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx=c_{i}\bigg\},\quad i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}.

Then
S_{\vec{c}}=S_{c_{1}}\times S_{c_{2}}\times\cdots\times S_{c_{m}}
.

The abstract definition
2.1
of vector link in the specific case where
\mathcal{M}_{i}=S_{c_{i}}
,
\mathcal{M}^{(m)}=S_{\vec{c}}
,
D=\partial\mathbb{M}_{k+1}
and
S=\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}
is stated as follows.

Definition 2.4

(Vector link)
.

A compact subset
A
of
S_{\vec{c}}
with
\partial\mathbb{M}_{k+1}\subset A
is said to be linked to
\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}
, if, for any continuous mapping
h\in C(A,{S_{\vec{c}}})
satisfying
h|_{\partial\mathbb{M}_{k+1}}=\textbf{id}
, there holds

h(A)\cap\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}\not=\emptyset.

Next we show how to construct minimax values at which sign-changing normalized solutions can be found. In this part, we will provide detailed arguments to prove that the minimax level we construct is finite and well defined. Further, we will estimate the minimax level and show that the linking sets have well value-separated property.

To obtain sign-changing solutions, as in many references such as
[
8
,
66
]
, we should use cones of positive and negative functions. Precisely, we define

\mathcal{P}_{i}:=\left\{\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})\in{\mathcal{H}}:u_{i}\geq 0\right\};\quad\mathcal{P}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}(\mathcal{P}_{i}\cup-{\mathcal{P}}_{i}).

(2.6)

Then all the elements outside
\mathcal{P}
are sign-changing. For
\delta>0
, we define
{\mathcal{P}}_{\delta}:=\left\{\vec{u}\in{\mathcal{H}}:\text{dist}(\vec{u},{\mathcal{P}})<\delta\right\}
, where

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\vec{u},{\mathcal{P}}):=\min\left\{\text{dist}(\vec{u},{\mathcal{P}}_{i}),\penalty\ \text{dist}(\vec{u},-{\mathcal{P}}_{i}):i=1,\ldots,m\right\},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\vec{u},\pm{\mathcal{P}}_{i}):=\inf\left\{\|\vec{u}-\vec{v}\|:\vec{v}\in\pm{\mathcal{P}}_{i}\right\}.

Note that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\delta}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\left(({\mathcal{P}}_{i})_{\delta}\cup(-{\mathcal{P}}_{i})_{\delta}\right),\quad(\pm{\mathcal{P}}_{i})_{\delta}=\left\{\vec{u}\in{\mathcal{H}}:\text{dist}(\vec{u},\pm{\mathcal{P}}_{i})<\delta\right\}.

Now we introduce the set
S^{*}(\delta)
by

\displaystyle S^{*}(\delta):=\mathcal{H}\backslash\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{\delta}}.

(2.8)

When
N=3,4
, the case is
L^{2}
-supercritical and the boundedness of Palais-Smale sequences becomes a challenging issue. To avoid this difficult, for
\vec{c}=\left(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{+}\right)^{m}
, we introduce the following bounded subset of
S_{\vec{c}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\rho}:=\left\{\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})\in S_{\vec{c}}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx<\rho\right\}.

(2.9)

In what follows, we define

I^{+}:=\left\{i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}:\mu_{i}>0\right\},\quad I^{-}:=\left\{i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}:\mu_{i}<0\right\},

(2.10)

and

J^{+}:=\left\{(i,j)\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}^{2}:\beta_{ij}>0,\penalty\ i\neq j\right\},\quad J^{-}:=\left\{(i,j)\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}^{2}:\beta_{ij}<0,\penalty\ i\neq j\right\}.

(2.11)

Throughout this paper, we use the notations

\mu_{\max}^{+}:=\max\left\{0,\penalty\ \mu_{i}:i\in I^{+}\right\}\geq 0,\quad\beta_{\max}^{+}:=\max\left\{0,\penalty\ \beta_{ij}:(i,j)\in J^{+}\right\}\geq 0,

(2.12)

\mu_{\min}^{-}:=\min\left\{0,\penalty\ \mu_{i}:i\in I^{-}\right\}\leq 0,\quad\beta_{\min}^{-}:=\min\left\{0,\penalty\ \beta_{ij}:(i,j)\in J^{-}\right\}\leq 0,

(2.13)

Take

0<\rho<\frac{2\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4}}{4\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)-3\left(\mu_{\min}^{-}+\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)}.

(2.14)

Then we have the following result.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\rho>0
satisfying (
2.14
), there is a constant
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\text{dist}(\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho},\mathcal{P})=\delta_{0}>0.

Proof.

By negation, we assume that

\text{dist}(\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho},\mathcal{P})=0.

Then we find
\{\vec{u}_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
and
\{\vec{p}_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}
such that
\|\vec{u}_{n}-\vec{p}_{n}\|\to 0
, and so
\|\vec{u}_{n}-\vec{p}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}\to 0
. From
\{\vec{u}_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
we see that
\{\vec{u}_{n}\}
is bounded in
{\mathcal{H}}
. Up to a subsequence, we may assume that
\vec{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup\vec{u}^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}
; moreover, we have
\vec{u}_{n}\to\vec{u}^{*}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Then we observe that
\vec{u}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{\bot}
and that
\vec{u}^{*}\in S_{\vec{c}}
, which implies that all components of
\vec{u}^{*}
are nontrivial and sign-changing. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\|\vec{u}^{*}-\vec{p}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\|\vec{u}^{*}-\vec{u}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}+\|\vec{u}_{n}-\vec{p}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}\to 0,

and it follows that
\|u^{*}_{i}-p_{n,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
for
i=1,\cdots,m
. From
\vec{p}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}
it follows that
p_{n,j}
is nonnegative or nonpositive for some
j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
. We have reached a contradiction, since
u^{*}_{j}
is sign-changing. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

M_{0}=M_{0}(\rho):=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho>0,

(2.15)

and

M_{1}=M_{1}(\rho):=M_{0}\rho.

(2.16)

Then we have
E(\vec{u})\geq M_{0}\|\vec{u}\|^{2}
for any
\vec{u}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}}
and
E(\vec{u})\geq M_{1}
for any
\vec{u}\in\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}

Proof.

For any

, by using (

), we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{4}dx\leq\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\sum_{i\in I^{+}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx\right)^{2}\leq\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx

(2.17)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1,j\neq i}^{m}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}u_{j}^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}u_{j}^{2}dx\leq\beta_{\max}^{+}\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx,

from which we get

\displaystyle E(\vec{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{4}dx-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1,j\neq i}^{m}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}u_{j}^{2}dx

(2.19)

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right]\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx=M_{0}\|\vec{u}\|^{2}.

Hence, for any
u\in\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
, we have
E(\vec{u})\geq M_{1}=M_{0}\rho
. This completes the proof.
∎

where

is the constant depending on

given in Lemma

, and define a minimax level

by

\displaystyle m_{\vec{c},\rho,k}^{\delta}:=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{L}_{\rho,k}}\sup_{\vec{u}\in A\cap S^{*}(\delta)}E(\vec{u}).

(2.21)

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
k
be a positive integer such that

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[2-\left(\mu_{\min}^{-}+\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right]\Lambda_{k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}<M_{1}(\rho)

(2.22)

and

\displaystyle{\left(\beta_{\max}^{+}-\mu_{\min}^{-}-\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho}\Lambda_{k+1}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}}+\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\Lambda_{k+1}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{c_{i}}^{2}

(2.23)

\displaystyle<\left[2-\left(\mu_{\min}^{-}+\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right](\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{k})\min_{1\leq i\leq m}c_{i}

Remark 2.8
.

Let
k
be a positive integer such that
\Lambda_{k}<\Lambda_{k+1}
, and for any
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{+}\right)^{m}

the relation (

Lemma 2.9
.

For any
\rho

\sup E(\mathbb{M}_{k+1})<M_{1}

Lemma 4.3

. Moreover, since
\mu_{i}>0

, up to a subsequence we get

\displaystyle w_{n,i}\to\left(-\Delta-\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{-}}\beta_{ij}(u_{j}^{*})^{2}\right)^{-1}\bigg(\mu_{i}(u_{i}^{*})^{3}+\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\beta_{ij}(u_{j}^{*})^{2}u_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{\vec{u}^{*},i}u_{i}^{*}\bigg)>0\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}),

where the last ”
>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\{|x\in\Omega:w_{n,i}(x)<0\}|=0
since
\Omega
is bounded, contradicting
\lim_{n\to\infty}\{|x\in\Omega:w_{n,i}(x)<0\}|>0
. This contradiction enables us take sufficiently small
K>0
if
\delta>0
is small enough. Observe that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{i}^{-}|^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigl\{\|\vec{u}-\vec{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}:\vec{v}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-1}\inf\bigl\{\|\vec{u}-\vec{v}\|:\vec{v}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\bigr\}=\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-1}\text{dist}(\vec{u},\mathcal{P}_{i}).

(4.9)

When

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{i}^{-}}u_{j}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K^{\frac{1}{6}}\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{i}^{-}}u_{j}^{6}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq K^{\frac{1}{6}}\mathcal{C}_{N,6}^{-2}\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx<K^{\frac{1}{6}}\mathcal{C}_{N,6}^{-2}\rho

where
\mathcal{C}_{N,6}
is the Sobolev best constant of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
defined by

\mathcal{C}_{N,6}:=\inf_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\cfrac{\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{6}dx\right)^{1/6}}.

Then, by (
4.8
) and (
4
) we have

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\vec{w},\mathcal{P}_{i})\leq\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\delta^{2}+K^{\frac{1}{6}}\mathcal{C}_{N,6}^{-2}\rho\beta_{\max}^{+}+\bar{\lambda}K^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\delta,

and therefore, for small
\delta>0

\vec{w}\in(\mathcal{P}_{i})_{\delta/2}
. When
N=4

\displaystyle\beta_{\max}^{+}\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{i}^{-}}u_{j}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\sum_{(i,j)\in J^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx\leq\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\rho<\frac{1}{2}.

Then, by (
4.8
) and (
4
) we have

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\vec{w},\mathcal{P})\leq\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\delta^{2}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-2}\rho+\bar{\lambda}K^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\delta,

and therefore, for small
\delta>0
, we conclude that
\vec{w}\in(\mathcal{P}_{i})_{\delta/2}
. Moreover, using (
4.8
) we get that
G(\vec{u})\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
for any
\vec{u}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
.
Similar argument works for the ”
-
” sign and we complete the proof.

Lemma 4.6

(Palais-Smale condition)
.

Assume that (
1.6
) holds and let
V
be defined in (
4.10
), and let
\{\vec{u}_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
where
\rho
satisfies (
2.14
) be such that

\displaystyle E(\vec{u}_{n})\to c<\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{ when }N=3,\\
\frac{1}{4}(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+})^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4},&\text{ when }N=4,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad V(\vec{u}_{n})\to 0\quad\text{strongly in }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Then, up to a subsequence, there exists
\vec{u}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}}\subset S_{\vec{c}}
such that
\vec{u}_{n}\to\vec{u}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
V(\vec{u})=0
.

Proof.

Observe that the sequence
\{\vec{u}_{n}\}=\{\left(u_{n,1},\ldots,u_{n,m}\right)\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
as it is contained in the bounded set
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}\subset S_{\vec{c}}
. Let
\lambda_{n,i}
be the corresponding Lagrange multiplier to
u_{n,i}
given in Proposition
4.1
. Then, it is straightforward to verify that the sequence
\left\{\lambda_{n,i}\right\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, up to subsequence, we may assume that there exist
\vec{u}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}\subset S_{\vec{c}}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle u_{n,i}\rightharpoonup u_{i}\quad\text{ weakly in }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(4.11)

\displaystyle u_{n,i}\rightharpoonup u_{i}\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega),

\displaystyle u_{n,i}\to u_{i}\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{r}(\Omega)\text{ for }2\leq r<2^{\ast},

\displaystyle u_{n,i}\to u_{i}\quad\text{ almost everywhere in }\Omega.

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,i}\to\lambda_{i}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R},

where
2^{*}=2N/(N-2)
.

For any
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}
, let
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}
be the Lagrange multiplier associated with
u_{i}
, then we claim that
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
. Indeed, for any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying that
\int_{\Omega}u_{i}vdx\neq 0
, then we can see from the fact
V(\vec{u}_{n})\to 0
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,i}\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}vdx=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{n,i}\cdot\nabla vdx+\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}^{3}vdx+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}u_{n,j}^{2}vdx+o_{n}(1).

(4.12)

Letting
n\to\infty
, we deduce from (
4.11
) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}vdx=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla vdx+\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{3}vdx+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}u_{j}^{2}vdx=\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}vdx,

(4.13)

which implies that
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
. Therefore, by using (
4.14
) and the fact
V(\vec{u}_{n})\to 0
again, it is easy to verify that
V(\vec{u})=0
.

It remains to prove that
\vec{u}_{n}\to\vec{u}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. When
N=3
, this is Sobolve subcritical case, and it is classical to prove that
\vec{u}_{n}\to\vec{u}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
by using the compact embedding of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
and of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
.

Next we consider the Sobolev critical case when
N=4
. In such case, the embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
is not compact, which is much more complicated. Let
\vec{\sigma}_{n}=\left(\sigma_{n,1},\ldots,\sigma_{n,m}\right)=\vec{u}_{n}-\vec{u}\in\mathcal{B}_{2\rho}
. Then we aim to prove
\vec{\sigma}_{n}\to 0
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
by using a contradiction argument. Without loss of generality, we may assume that there exists some
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}
, such that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,i}|^{2}dx\to l>0,\quad n\to\infty.

(4.14)

Recall that
V(\vec{u}_{n})\to 0
and
V(\vec{u})=0
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n,i}|^{2}dx+\lambda_{n,i}c_{i}=\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}^{4}dx+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}^{2}u_{n,j}^{2}dx+o_{n}(1),

(4.15)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}dx+\lambda_{i}c_{i}=\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{4}dx+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}u_{j}^{2}dx.

(4.16)

By using the Brézis-Lieb Lemma (see
[
19
]
for the case of one component and
[
23
, p.447]
for the case of two components), we have

\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}^{4}dx=\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,i}^{4}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{4}dx+o_{n}(1),\quad\int_{\Omega}u_{n,i}^{2}u_{n,j}^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,i}^{2}\sigma_{n,j}^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}u_{j}^{2}dx+o_{n}(1).

(4.17)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,i}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,i}^{4}dx+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,i}^{2}\sigma_{n,j}^{2}dx

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{i}\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,i}^{4}dx+\beta_{\max}^{+}\left(\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,i}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{n,j}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,i}|^{2}dx\right)^{2}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,i}|^{2}dx\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,j}|^{2}dx.

Observe that
\vec{\sigma}=\vec{u_{n}}-\vec{u}\in\mathcal{B}_{2\rho}
, and by letting
n\to\infty
, we have

l\geq(1-2\beta_{\max}^{+}\rho\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4})\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4}\geq\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4},

(4.19)

by the choice of
\rho
, see (
2.14
).
Then we have

\displaystyle E(\vec{\sigma}_{n})=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,i}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{n,i}|^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4}+o_{n}(1).

(4.20)

Moreover, since

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{i}\rvert^{2}dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{n,i}\rvert^{2}dx\leq\rho,

we deduce from Lemma
2.6
that

. Then by using (
4.17
) again, we have

\displaystyle E(\vec{u}_{n})=E(\vec{u})+E(\vec{\sigma}_{n})+o_{n}(1)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4}+o_{n}(1).

(4.21)

However, this is impossible since
E(\vec{u}_{n})\to c<\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}^{+}+\beta_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{4}
, and we get a contradiction. Therefore,
\vec{\sigma}_{n}\to 0
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and the proof is completed.
∎

constrained to

and find a contradiction with

\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}c_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq m}c_{i}\right)^{-1}<\hat{c}_{k},

(7.1)

so that we can take
\rho>0
with assumptions in Theorem
5.1
satisfied for
k-1
. Then, there exists a sign-changing solution
\vec{u}_{\vec{c}}
with Lagrange multipliers
\vec{\lambda}_{\vec{c}}
at level
m_{\vec{c},\rho,k-1}^{\delta}
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. For
i=1,\cdots,m
, let
v_{\vec{c},i}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{i}}}u_{\vec{c},i}
. In the following we further assume that there exists a constant
\overline{c}>1
such that

\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}c_{i}\right)\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq m}c_{i}\right)^{-1}\leq\overline{c}.

(7.2)

Step 1:

\{\vec{v}_{\vec{c}}\}
and
\{\vec{\lambda}_{\vec{c}}\}
are bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
respectively as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
.

By (
2.27
), (
2.28
) and Proposition
2.10
, we know

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})=m_{\vec{c},\rho,k-1}^{\delta}\leq\sup_{\mathbb{S}_{k}}E\leq\frac{1}{4}\left[2-\left(\mu_{\min}^{-}+\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right]\sum_{i=1}^{m}{c_{i}}\Lambda_{k}

and

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})\geq\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{M_{1}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{k-1}^{\bot}}E\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[\left(2-\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right)c_{i}\Lambda_{k}-\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\left(c_{i}\Lambda_{k}\right)^{2}\right].

By the choice of
\rho
(see Remark
2.8
), we know
\rho\to 0
as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
. This implies

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})=\Big(\frac{1}{2}+o_{\vec{c}}(1)\Big)\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\Lambda_{k}\quad\mbox{as}\ \vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}.

(7.3)

Using the Sobolev inequality, we have

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})\leq\frac{1}{4}\left[2-\left(\mu_{\min}^{-}+\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right]\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}|^{2}dx

and

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(2-\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}|^{2}dx\right)^{2}.

(7.4)

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx<\rho,

we obtain

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})=\Big(\frac{1}{2}+o_{\vec{c}}(1)\Big)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx\quad\mbox{as}\ \vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+},

which combining (
7.3
) implies

\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}}=\Lambda_{k}.

(7.5)

Therefore, by (
7.2
), we know

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla v_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{c_{i}}

is bounded, that is
\{\vec{v}_{\vec{c}}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

For
i=1,\cdots,m
, direct calculations show that
\vec{v}_{\vec{c}}
satisfies

-\Delta v_{\vec{c},i}+\lambda_{\vec{c},i}v_{\vec{c},i}=\mu_{i}c_{i}v_{\vec{c},i}^{3}+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}c_{j}v_{\vec{c},j}^{2}v_{\vec{c},i}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,

(7.6)

which implies
\{\lambda_{\vec{c},i}\}
is bounded, since
\lVert v_{\vec{c},i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.

Step 2:

For
i=1,\cdots,m
,
v_{\vec{c},i}
convergences strongly to some eigenfunction of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
-\lambda_{\vec{c},i}
convergences to the corresponding eigenvalue up to a subsequence as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
.

By Step 1, up to a subsequence we assume that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v_{\vec{c},i}\rightharpoonup v_{i}\quad\mbox{in}\ H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
&v_{\vec{c},i}\to v_{i}\quad\mbox{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega),\\
&v_{\vec{c},i}\to v_{i}\quad\mbox{almost everywhere in}\ \Omega,\\
&\lambda_{\vec{c},i}\to\lambda_{i}\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R},\end{aligned}

(7.7)

as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
. Since
\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
, we know
v_{i}\neq 0
. Obviously,

c_{i}\int_{\Omega}v_{\vec{c},i}^{4}dx\to 0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \sum_{j\neq i}c_{j}\int_{\Omega}v_{\vec{c},i}^{2}v_{\vec{c},j}^{2}dx\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\ \vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}.

Thus, from (
7.6
), we know
v_{\vec{c},i}\to v_{i}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
v_{i}
satisfies

-\Delta v_{i}+\lambda_{i}v_{i}=0\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega.

This shows that
-\lambda_{i}
is an eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
v_{i}
is the corresponding eigenfunction.

Step 3:
Completion.

Observe that
\lambda_{i}
in Step 2 depends on the sequence
\{\vec{c}\}_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}
. To complete the proof, it suffices to show that there exists
\{\vec{c}_{n}\}
such that (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) hold,
\vec{c}_{n}\to\vec{0}^{+}
, and
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{m}=\Lambda_{k}
. Arguing by contradiction, we suppose that such sequence
\{\vec{c}_{n}\}
does not exist. Let

T:=\left\{(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}>0,\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}=1,\penalty\ \left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}t_{i}\right)\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq m}t_{i}\right)^{-1}\leq\overline{c}\right\}.

(7.8)

For any
\vec{t}\in T
and
0<r<\hat{c}_{k}/\overline{c}
, we have that
\vec{c}=r\vec{t}
satisfies (
7.1
) and (
7.2
). Now we fix
\vec{t}=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})\in T
and take
\vec{c}=r\vec{t}
with
r\to 0
such that
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
. By Step 2, we assume that
-\lambda_{\vec{c},i}\to\Lambda_{j_{i}}
up to a subsequence as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
. By the assumption based on contradiction arguments, it is impossible that
\Lambda_{j_{1}}=\cdots=\Lambda_{j_{m}}=\Lambda_{k}
.
From (
7.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{c_{i}}

(7.9)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla v_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx

(7.10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\Lambda_{j_{i}}=\Lambda_{k},

(7.11)

that is

\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}(\Lambda_{k}-\Lambda_{j_{i}})=0.

This implies
(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})
is orthogonal to
(\Lambda_{k}-\Lambda_{j_{1}},\cdots,\Lambda_{k}-\Lambda_{j_{m}})
. Let
A
denote the set consisting all
(\Lambda_{k}-\Lambda_{j_{1}},\cdots,\Lambda_{k}-\Lambda_{j_{m}})
when
\vec{t}
varies in
T
. We have assumed that
\vec{0}\notin A
. Thus for each
\vec{a}\in A
, all vectors that orthogonal to
\vec{a}
form an
(m-1)
-dimensional hyperplane passing through the origin. It can be known that the intersection of this hyperplane and
T
is of
(m-2)
-dimension. Denote this intersection by
T_{\vec{a}}
, then dim
T_{\vec{a}}=m-2
and hence the
(m-1)
-dimensional measure of
T_{\vec{a}}
is zero. Since
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is countable,
A
is countable and hence the
(m-1)
-dimensional measure of
\cup_{\vec{a}\in A}T_{\vec{a}}
is zero. However,
T=\cup_{\vec{a}\in A}T_{\vec{a}}
and
T
has a positive
(m-1)
-dimensional measure. We have reached a contradiction and complete the proof.

∎

Next we consider
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=(\Lambda_{k_{1}},\cdots,\Lambda_{k_{m}})
when there exists
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
such that
k_{i}=1
. Rearranging
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}
if necessary, we assume that
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=(\Lambda_{k_{1}},\cdots,\Lambda_{k_{d}},\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{1})
with
k_{i}\geq 2
for
i\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
. The case
d=0
is allowed, which means that
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=(\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{1})
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Assume that (
1.6
) holds. Let
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=(\Lambda_{k_{1}},\cdots,\Lambda_{k_{d}},\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{1})
where
d
is an integer with
0\leq d\leq m-1
and
k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}
are positive integers no less than
2
. Then
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})
is a nontrivial bifurcation point of system (
1.2
).

Proof.

We first assume that
1\leq d\leq m-1
. Similar to the proof of Proposition
7.1
, we just provide the proof with
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{d}\geq 2
by using Theorem
6.1
.

Let
k=k_{1}=\cdots=k_{d}\geq 2
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\Lambda_{k-1}<\Lambda_{k}
. In this proof we always suppose for
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})\in(0,\infty)^{m}
that

\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}c_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq d}c_{i}\right)^{-1}<\hat{c}_{k,d},

(7.12)

so that we can take
\rho>0
with assumptions in Theorem
6.1
satisfied for
k-1
. Then there exists a
(d,m-d)
-semi-nodal normalized solution
\vec{u}_{\vec{c}}
of (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) with Lagrange multipliers
\vec{\lambda}_{\vec{c}}
at level
m_{\vec{c},\rho,k-1,d}^{\delta}
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. For
i=1,\cdots,m
, let
v_{\vec{c},i}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{i}}}u_{\vec{c},i}
. In the following we further assume that (
7.2
) holds true.

Step 1:

\{\vec{u}_{\vec{c}}\}
and
\{\vec{\lambda}_{\vec{c}}\}
are bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
respectively as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
.

By (
2.54
), (
2.55
), (
2.58
) and Proposition
2.16
, we know

\displaystyle E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})=m_{\vec{c},\rho,k-1,d}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbb{S}_{k,d}\times\left\{(\sqrt{c_{d+1}}\phi_{1},\cdots,\sqrt{c_{m}}\phi_{1})\right\}}E

(7.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left[2-\left(\mu_{\min}^{-}+\beta_{\min}^{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right]\left(\Lambda_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}+\Lambda_{1}\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}c_{i}\right),

(7.14)

and

\displaystyle E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}\cap\left(\mathbb{S}_{k-1,d}^{\bot}\times S_{\vec{c},m-d}\right)}E

(7.15)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[\left(2-\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right)c_{i}\Lambda_{k}-\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\left(c_{i}\Lambda_{k}\right)^{2}\right]

(7.16)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}\left[\left(2-\beta_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\rho\right)c_{i}\Lambda_{1}-\mu_{\max}^{+}\mathcal{C}_{N}^{-4}\left(c_{i}\Lambda_{1}\right)^{2}\right].

(7.17)

Similar to Step 1 in proving Proposition
7.1
, we have

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})=\Big(\frac{1}{2}+o_{\vec{c}}(1)\Big)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\Lambda_{k}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}c_{i}\Lambda_{1}\Big)\quad\mbox{as}\ \vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}.

(7.18)

Moreover,

E(\vec{u}_{\vec{c}})=\Big(\frac{1}{2}+o_{\vec{c}}(1)\Big)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx\quad\mbox{as}\ \vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}.

Therefore,

\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\Lambda_{k}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}c_{i}\Lambda_{1}}=1,

(7.19)

which combining (
7.2
) implies

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla v_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{c_{i}}

is bounded as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
, that is
\{\vec{v}_{\vec{c}}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

For
i=1,\cdots,m
, direct calculations show that
\vec{v}_{\vec{c}}
satisfies

-\Delta v_{\vec{c},i}+\lambda_{\vec{c},i}v_{\vec{c},i}=\mu_{i}c_{i}v_{\vec{c},i}^{3}+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}c_{j}v_{\vec{c},j}^{2}v_{\vec{c},i}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,

(7.20)

which implies
\{\lambda_{\vec{c},i}\}
is bounded, since
\lVert v_{\vec{c},i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.

Step 2:

Similar to Step 1 in the proof of Proposition
7.1
, for
i=1,\cdots,m
,
v_{\vec{c},i}
convergences strongly to some eigenfunction of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
-\lambda_{\vec{c},i}
convergences to corresponding eigenvalue up to a subsequence as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
. Moreover, for
i=d+1,\cdots,m
, since
v_{\vec{c},i}
is positive in
\Omega
, we know that
v_{\vec{c},i}
convergences strongly to first eigenfunction
\phi_{1}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
-\lambda_{\vec{c},i}
convergences to first eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}
.

Step 3:
Completion.

We can prove this step after a small modification of Step 3 in the proof of Proposition
7.1
. The definition of set
T
is modified as

T:=\left\{(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}>0,\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}=1,\penalty\ \left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}t_{i}\right)\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq d}t_{i}\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{k}}\overline{c}\right\}.

(7.21)

For any
\vec{t}\in T
and
0<r<\Lambda_{1}\hat{c}_{k,d}/\overline{c}
, we have that
\vec{c}=(rt_{1}/\Lambda_{k},\cdots,rt_{d}/\Lambda_{k},rt_{d+1}/\Lambda_{1},\cdots,rt_{m}/\Lambda_{1})
satisfies (
7.12
) and (
7.2
). Now we fix
\vec{t}=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})\in T
and take
\vec{c}=(rt_{1}/\Lambda_{k},\cdots,rt_{d}/\Lambda_{k},rt_{d+1}/\Lambda_{1},\cdots,rt_{m}/\Lambda_{1})
with
r\to 0
such that
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}
. By Step 2, we assume that
-\lambda_{\vec{c},i}\to\Lambda_{j_{i}}
up to a subsequence as
\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}
, and
j_{d+1}=\cdots j_{m}=1
. Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\Lambda_{k}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}c_{i}\Lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\vec{c}\to\vec{0}^{+}}\Big(\frac{1}{\Lambda_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}t_{i}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{c_{i}}+\frac{1}{\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}t_{i}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\vec{c},i}\rvert^{2}dx}{c_{i}}\Big),

(7.22)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}t_{i}\Lambda_{j_{i}}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{m}t_{i}=1,

(7.23)

that is

\sum_{i=1}^{d}t_{i}(\Lambda_{k}-\Lambda_{j_{1}})=0.

Then, we can choose special
(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})\in T
such that
\Lambda_{j_{1}}=\cdots=\Lambda_{j_{d}}=\Lambda_{k}
.

Finally, when
d=0
, our method proving Theorem
6.1
still works, in which case the normalized solutions that we obtain are positive. By Step 2 above, we know

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=(\underbrace{\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{1}}_{m})

is a nontrivial bifurcation point of system (

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let

\mathbb{B}:=\bigg\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\lambda_{i}\text{ is an eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ on $H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)$ for each }i\in\big\{1,\cdots,m\big\}\bigg\}.

On one hand, it is relatively standard to show that any nontrivial bifurcation point of system (
1.2
) is in
\mathbb{B}
. On the other hand, combining Proposition
7.1
and Proposition
7.2
we conclude that any point in
\mathbb{B}
is a nontrivial bifurcation point of system (
1.2
). Thus we complete the proof.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

We remark that bifurcation phenomenon from the nontrivial bifurcation point of system (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{B}
is of
m
-dimension. In fact, let
T
be defined in (
7.8
) or (
7.21
), which is of
(m-1)
-dimension after removing
Z
, a countable union of zero
\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
-measure sets. Then both

\left\{\vec{c}=r\vec{t}:0<r<\frac{\hat{c}_{k}}{\overline{c}},\penalty\ \vec{t}\in T\backslash Z\right\}

and

\left\{\vec{c}=r\left(\frac{t_{1}}{\Lambda_{k}},\cdots,\frac{t_{d}}{\Lambda_{k}},\frac{t_{d+1}}{\Lambda_{1}},\cdots,\frac{t_{m}}{\Lambda_{1}}\right):0<r<\frac{\Lambda_{1}\hat{c}_{k,d}}{\overline{c}},\penalty\ \vec{t}\in T\backslash Z\right\}

are
m
-dimensional.

7.2
Proof of Theorem
1.6

At the end of this article, we prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We merely prove that for any positive integer
1\leq d\leq m-1
, the set of
d
-semi-trivial bifurcation points of system (
1.2
) is exactly

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{B}_{d}:=

\displaystyle\bigg\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\exists i_{1}<\cdots<i_{d}\in\big\{1,\cdots,m\big\},\penalty\ \lambda_{i_{1}},\cdots,\lambda_{i_{d}}\text{ are eigenvalues of $-\Delta$ on }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\bigg\}.

Then it follows that the set of semi-trivial bifurcation points is

\bigg\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\exists i\in\big\{1,\cdots,m\big\}\text{ such that }\lambda_{i}\text{ is an eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ on }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\bigg\}.

One one hand, it is relatively standard to show that any
d
-semi-trivial bifurcation point of system (
1.2
) is in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
. On the other hand, let
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
. After rearranging
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}
, we assume that
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d}
are eigenvalues of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. By Theorem
1.5
, if
d\geq 2
there exists
\{(\lambda_{n,1},\cdots,\lambda_{n,d};u_{n,1},\cdots,u_{n,d})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\lambda_{n,i}\to\lambda_{i}
,
u_{n,i}\not\equiv 0
,
\|u_{n,i}\|\to 0
for
i=1,\cdots d
, and for each
n
,
(u_{n,1},\cdots,u_{n,d})
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{n,i}+\lambda_{n,i}u_{n,i}=\mu_{i}u_{n,i}^{3}+\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{d}\beta_{ij}u_{n,j}^{2}u_{n,i}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Such result also holds when
d=1
, in a single equation case. Then considering the sequence

\{(\lambda_{n,1},\cdots,\lambda_{n,d},\lambda_{d+1},\cdots,\lambda_{m};u_{n,1},\cdots,u_{n,d},0,\cdots,0)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}),

we know
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})
is a
d
-semi-trivial bifurcation point of system (
1.2
). The proof is complete.
∎

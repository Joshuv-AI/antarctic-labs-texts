Assessment of the efficiency of new regenerative gel and spray in experimental model systems
Authors: Natalia Bezdieniezhnykh, Oleksandra Lykhova, Maryna Borshchevska, Andriy Goy, Kyrylo Saulenko, Tamara Kozak, Dmytro Kukurudza, Yevgen Kruglov, Genadiy Borshchevskiy, Liubov Buchynska
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe problem of wound healing and the availability of effective and safe agents with regenerative properties is critically important today. The aim of the study was to develop an algorithm for evaluating products with regenerative potential using a set of in vitro and in vivo experimental models.MethodsIn vitro research included methods of working with cell culture. Studies were conducted using cell lines: HaCat, MAEС, 3T3-A31. In vivo research was conducted on Wistar rats with simulation of the pathological condition of a cut wound and the application of experimental samples and the following assessment of a set of indicators.ResultsAs a result of using a comprehensive approach to assess the effectiveness of agents with regenerative activity, it was shown that the spray and gel samples, the main component of which is collagen, with decamethoxine, particles of silver sulfadiazine, hyaluronic acid, and additionally a set of amino acids in a spray, were shown to cause a statistically significant increase in the number of live cells at certain concentrations in vitro and increase the speed of restoration of the damaged monolayer of endothelial cells. According to the results of the speed of healing of the skin in the area of ​​the simulated cut wound, a statistically significant increase in the speed of healing from 10 to 19% with the use of the spray and from 5 to 18% with the use of the gel in comparison with the animals of the control group (р≤0.05 and p ≤ 0.01). The evaluation of the results of the morphological analysis showed that with repeated application of the spray or gel, a significant reduction in the area of ​​the wound is observed already on the 8th day with faster healing compared to the control. Already from the 8th day, inflammatory changes and the area of ​​necrotic in the experimental groups significantly decreased.ConclusionA wound healing assessment model was developed and tested, which is convenient for monitoring the wound healing process and evaluating treatment outcomes. The efficacy and potential role in accelerating wound healing of new gel and spray products for external use was shown.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Gel and spray for external use, Effectiveness, Cell culture, Cut wound, Experimental animals

Introduction

The skin is the largest organ of the human body, performing an important barrier function to maintain internal balance, as well as protection from external factors, the constant influence of which makes the skin extremely susceptible to various types of damage [1–3]. A wound is a violation of the cellular and anatomical structure of a living tissue and can be caused by physical, chemical, thermal, and other factors that cause functional changes in a tissue or organ [4]. Restoration of the structure of damaged tissue is a complex process that includes migration, proliferation and differentiation of many types of cells, biomolecular interactions, synthesis of the extracellular matrix, etc. The interaction of a cell with the extracellular microenvironment is always bidirectional and dynamic, as cells can modulate the structure and composition of the extracellular matrix, which affects the morphology and behavior of cells during wound healing. The cellular microenvironment includes certain extracellular molecules, such as collagen, elastin, fibronectin, vitronectin, integrins, and laminins, which provide structural and biochemical support and attachment to surrounding cells [5, 6].

The healing process of an acute wound consists of successive phases that are regulated by certain interactions at the molecular, cellular and extracellular levels and is finalized by the closure of the wound within days or weeks and is schematically divided into three phases: inflammation, proliferation and remodeling. When the healing process does not take place properly chronic wounds are formed [5, 7], that in comparison with acute wounds are characterized by other phenotypic features, such as a decrease in the level of growth factors, limitation of mitotic activity, and violation of angiogenesis [8, 9].

Since today the problem of treating wounds caused by military actions is particularly acute, which requires the development of new means of treatment, let us consider certain nuances of this type of lesions. Medical care directly on the battlefield is mainly for blast injuries, which are often associated with severe tissue damage and can be challenging for both the patient and the medical personnel [10]. The most common injuries are damage to the musculoskeletal system (muscles, connective tissue, bones). Such injuries often limit the victim’s mobility, cause severe pain and, as a result, disability. At the same time, almost every injury received on the battlefield is accompanied by damage to the skin, which can be chemical or penetrating, often accompanied by a large burn, which in any case leads to the formation of scar tissue. The skin plays an important role in the proper healing of wounds in large areas where there is a risk of dehydration and infection. Thus, the development of new medicines and medical devices for the treatment of traumatic injuries is an extremely relevant task today, which we also addressed in this study. That is one of the main tasks of this work was to obtain medical products intended for the healing of wounds/burns with a complex action: detoxification (sorption), antimicrobial, regenerative, and anti-keloid. The developed gel and spray with potential regenerative properties must perform several functions: be an effective and safe means of treating wounds of various origins, be convenient to use: convenient form of application, reliable storage of the product, versatility of the product, which is provided by components with different mechanisms of action and indications for use.

The following well-known medical products were identified as analogs in the creation of medical products: PluroGel PSSD, GD MEDICAL AG, Switzerland; Kadefort spray powder, Pavia Pharmacies S.R.L., Italy; Wound healing spray Professional Dietetics Vulnamin, Italy. PluroGel PSSD Fire and Wound Gel is a topical treatment for burns, wounds and skin infections. The gel contains 1% silver sulfadiazine, which has antimicrobial properties to fight bacterial infections and also contains PluroGel technology, which provides a slow release of silver ions, promoting wound healing and tissue regeneration [11]. Kadefort is a powder composition that forms a protective film with an antimicrobial effect (barrier) and contributes to the creation of physiological conditions for the regeneration of damaged skin [12]. The barrier function is realized by the combination of kaolin and sodium hyaluronate since both ingredients have a specific effect: kaolin acts as an absorbent, which is important in the presence of exudate, and sodium hyaluronate acts as a polymer that forms a film and provides tissue hydration. Kadefort contains SСХ powder consisting of silicon dioxide, silver ions and chlorhexidine. Professional Dietetics Vulnamin wound healing spray is an interactive coating in the form of a liquid gel, aimed at promoting the formation of granulation tissue and tissue regeneration. It also facilitates wound cleansing with glutamine, proline and essential amino acids [13]. It is designed to promote the restoration of a moist microenvironment in particularly dry wounds while promoting the cleansing and granulation phase of untreated skin lesions.

All of the above products perform a function in wound healing and contain different components. We have combined several components in the new products to ensure its multifunctionality and a wider spectrum of action: in particular, a protein that is essential for tissue regeneration, an antiseptic and silver sulfadiazine for wound disinfection in the event of a potential infection, amino acids that will help increase cell number, and other auxiliary components. The main functional component of the new medical products gel and spray is collagen, which is known to be a component widely used in the treatment of wounds of various origins and plays a decisive role in stopping bleeding, reducing inflammation, promoting angiogenesis and stimulating tissue regeneration during wound healing [1, 14]. At the same time, collagen, which is also the dominant protein in the extracellular matrix, is used as a scaffold for the delivery and sustained release of other components that play a role in the healing process [15, 16].

The study of the pathophysiology of wound healing of various origins and the development of new tools and protocols for monitoring the healing process can certainly contribute to optimizing the treatment of patients [17]. At the same time, the experimental evaluation of any process is dependent on the use of experimental models in vitro and in vivo, including for the evaluation of wounds of various origins, the dynamics of their development, and the effectiveness of treatment. Several design issues must be considered in wound healing studies, including study objectives, wound type, sample characteristics and availability, cost, time frame, etc. In addition, the integration of different methods of wound assessment increases the reliability and validity of the results and provides a further understanding of the mechanisms involved in tissue repair [17–25]. Since one of the assessment methods is used, which does not always provide a complete answer, therefore, in this work, we used a comprehensive approach to evaluate the effectiveness of new (experimental) Spray and Gel products for external use with potential regenerative activity. At the same time, we combined in vivo and in vitro evaluation to establish the interchangeability of research methods for medicines and medical devices and the possibility of reducing the number of experimental animals in the future, which meets modern European requirements. And the introduction of such multifunctional medical products into clinical practice will be useful for improving the treatment of traumatic tissue injuries, in particular cut wounds, which is critically important today with such a rapid increase in traumatic injuries and limited activity of known medicines with wound healing properties.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Cell lines

HaCat (human keratinocytes), MAEС (mouse endothelial cells), 3T3-A31 (mouse fibroblasts). The study used cell lines of different histogenesis, which are components of the skin. Since the tested product is unlikely to be species-specific, the evaluation was performed on cells from different animals. All cell lines were obtained from the Bank of Cell Lines from Human and Animal Tissues of R.E.Kavetsky Institute of Experimental Pathology, Oncology, and Radiobiology of the NAS of Ukraine (Certificate No. AN 41 dated February 19, 2009). Cells were cultured in complete nutrient medium RPMI-1640 (L0496-500, Biowest, France)/ DMEM (L0102-500, Biowest, France) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (S181B-500, Biowest, France), 1x penicillin-streptomycin (L0022-100, Biowest, France) in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37ºС (CO2 incubator, Heal Force, China). After the cells formed a dense monolayer on the substrate (2–3 days of growth), they were reseeded, and the cells were dissociated using FBS with 1 mM Na-EDTA solution (Versen`s solution) (series No. 6, Vetline Agroscience, Ukraine). Cell counting was carried out using a Goryaev chamber (Voles, Ukraine) and trypan blue (A0668, Applichem, Germany). The number of cells was determined by the formula: A/80 × 2 = X*106 cells in a milliliter of medium, where A is the number of cells counted in the Goryaev chamber (in 5 large diagonal squares), x2 is the dilution factor.

The new medical product

Gel (Boosty gel Ag+, JSC Farmak, patent application number a202501304, 03/25/2025) (hereinafter referred to as “Gel”) for external use is a stable multicomponent colloidal solution of hydrogenated collagen in the form of an emulgel with particles of silver sulfadiazine in the form of a gel; the composition also includes hyaluronic acid, decamethoxine, poloxamer, silicon dioxide, hypromellose. The composition of the new medical product Spray (Boosty spray amino, JSC Farmak, patent application number a202501305, 03/25/2025) (hereinafter referred to as “Spray”) for wound healing contains collagen, decamethoxine, arginine, glycine, and L-aspartic acid. All components used in production are clinically proven materials for use.

Study of the viability of cells after their exposure to experimental samples (gel and spray for external use). Cells of the HaCat, MAEC and 3T3-A31 lines were seeded on 96-well plates in a complete nutrient medium (150 µl/well) at a concentration of 5 × 104/ml. After 24 h, test samples were introduced into the corresponding wells in a wide concentration range: in dilutions up to 1/256 for the spray and more than 1/5000 for the gel. The difference in dilutions was due to the consistency of the test products, in particular, the gel-like consistency of the gel made it impossible to study it in vitro at high concentrations (small dilutions), which was discovered at the pre-screening stages. Therefore, the initial dilutions for in vitro studies were chosen taking into account the consistency of the test samples, which would allow visualizing and evaluating the adequate response of the cells to the investigated gel and spray. The cells were incubated at a temperature of 37 °C in the presence of 5% CO2 for 72 h. The results were calculated visually and by staining the cells with crystal violet and MTT (3-[4,5-Dimetilthiazole-2-yl]-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide; Thiazolyl blue; A2231, Applichem, Germany). The results were recorded using a multiwell spectrophotometer at an excitation wavelength of 540 nm. The number of living cells was calculated according to the formula: X = (A 540 (experiment)/A540(control)) × 100%.

Cell staining methods

MTT. After cell incubation 10 µl of MTT (5 mg/ml) was added to each well of a 96-well plate and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere for 3 h. The appearance of formazan crystals was noted in the cells at the bottom of the wells and the plate was centrifuged. The supernatant was removed and 50 µl of DMSO was added to each well. The optical density (OD) of the contents of the wells was determined using a multiwell spectrophotometer (Labsystems Multiskan PLUS, Finland) at a wavelength of 540 nm.

Crystal violet (CV). After the incubation of the cells with the test samples, the culture medium was removed from the wells and 50 µl/well of dye (5 mg/ml in 70% methanol) was applied for 10 min. at room temperature. After that, the CV (405831000, ThermoScientific, USA) was washed and extracted from the cells using ethanol (100 µl to each well). The results of the experiment were taken into account using a multiwell spectrophotometer at a wavelength of 540 nm.

Analysis of the recovery rate of a damaged monolayer of cells in vitro by the scratch assay method. Analysis of the recovery rate of a damaged monolayer of cells in vitro by the scratch assay method. Cells (5 × 10 4 cells/well) were seeded in the wells of a 12-well plate in a complete nutrient medium and incubated at 37 °C in the presence of 5% CO 2 until the formation of a 100% monolayer. Sampler tips with a volume of up to 200 µl were used to create damage to the cell monolayer. After that, to remove cellular debris and level the edges of scratches, the nutrient medium was completely changed in all wells and test samples of spray and gel were introduced in dilutions: spray – 1/10, 1/50 and gel – 1/1000, 1/5000 for MAEC cells; spray – 1/20, 1/200 and gel – 1/1500, 1/10,000 for HaCaT cells; spray – 1/100, 1/500 and gel – 1/1500, 1/10,000 for 3T3-A31 cells. The concentration of the spray and gel was chosen according to preliminary data on the effect on cell viability and proliferative activity. The process of restoring the monolayer of cells in the damaged area was observed using a phase-contrast microscope Axiovert 25 (Carl Zeiss, Germany) and photographed using a digital camera. The first series of photos was taken immediately after the monolayer was damaged, and the subsequent ones were taken every 12 h up to 36 h (until complete recovery of the cell monolayer in the control). The area of monolayer damage was analyzed in the photo using an ImageJ program and the obtained values were expressed in pixels [26]. At least three images were analyzed for each group and time point.

Modeling of an cut wound in vivo. Duration of the study, dosage and scheme of application

The study design was constructed in such a way as to achieve the objectives of the experiment while using a minimum number of animals. Male Wistar rats were used − 39 animals, weighing 191.0 ± 10.0 g, aged 2-2.5 months. Before the experiment, the animals were healthy, with normal behavioral activity. The conditions of keeping the animals met the standards specified in the guidelines The Guide for Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (ILAR publication, 1996, National Academy Press, 1996). All procedures with experimental animals were reviewed and approved by the Commission on Bioethics of R.E.Kavetsky Institute of Experimental Pathology, Oncology, and Radiobiology of the NAS of Ukraine on compliance with the ethical principles of working with animals (Protocol No. 1 dated November 30, 2023). Environmental conditions: animals were housed in controlled environmental conditions (21–25 °C and 30–60% relative humidity). Identification of animals - by numbers of cages and by tags on animals. Before the study, the experimental animals were noted as healthy, with normal behavioral activity. All animal experiments were performed by ARRIVE guidelines and recommendations (study design, sample size, inclusion and exclusion criteria, randomization, blinded analysis, outcome assessment, and statistical methods). Wistar rats were chosen as the most relevant type of experimental animal for such an experiment: adult rats weigh 8–10 times more than mice, making them significantly larger and therefore easier to handle and perform surgical procedures. This advantage allows for the simulation of large wound areas, which is particularly useful for monitoring wound healing [27].

Modeling of a cut wound (CW) on experimental animals in vivo. Rats were depilated (on a 3.0 × 3.0 cm area of ​​skin on their backs) and anesthetized with Domitor (Orion pharma) with subsequent administration of the antidote Antimedin (Api-San) within 30 min after modeling the pathological condition. The active substance of Domitor is medetomidine hydrochloride, which belongs to the group of alpha-2-agonists. To achieve a sedative effect and analgesia, it is used in the following doses: 0.05–0.15 ml/kg. Dosages of the drug vary depending on the desired effect and individual characteristics. After that were made 3 pcs. (1.5–1.8 cm long at a distance of 0.5–0.7 cm) longitudinal parallel incisions of the skin and subcutaneous tissue (up to the fascia) with a sterile scalpel (Medicare). In this procedure was used a stencil to achieve standardization of the cut wound by the depth of the cut, the area of ​​the wound surface, and the length of the cuts. Animals were placed in individual cages and kept during the day. All animals were injected with an anesthetic drug. After 24 h, the tested samples were applied to the damaged area of ​​the skin.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the experimental samples during repeated dermal application to rats, 24 h after the simulation of CW, the tested samples of the gel and spray were applied to the area of ​​the damaged skin. Test samples of the gel and spray were applied without dilution in the original concentration. The samples were applied 2 times a day: gel – 200 mg single dose per animal (400 mg daily dose / per animal), spray – 300 µl single dose per animal (600 µl daily dose / per animal) for 14 days. No applications were applied to control animals, as the aim of the study was to evaluate the effectiveness of the new products with an untreated wound.

Experimental animals with CW were divided into 3 groups of 13 animals per group: control (№ 1–13), spray (№ 14–26), and gel (№ 27–39). Animals from each group were divided into 3 stages (time points) to observe the indicators in dynamics: 1st stage – 8 days from the beginning of application of experimental samples (5 animals were subjected to euthanasia - anesthetized with CO2), 2nd stage – 15 days from the beginning of application of experimental samples (5 animals were subjected euthanasia - anesthetized with CO2) and the 3rd stage − 28 days from the beginning of applying the test samples and 14 days after the last application of the gel and spray (3 animals were euthanized - anesthetized with CO2).

Clinical observations of the behavior and condition of the animals 30 min, 2, 6, and 24 h after the first application, and then daily throughout the study, the physiological indicators of the condition of the rats in the control and experimental groups were analyzed: locomotor and respiratory activity, the condition of fur, mucous membranes, behavioral reactions, functioning of the gastrointestinal tract.

Evaluation of the site of damage under the conditions of a CW modulation during the study of the effect of test samples. Every day after the modulation of CW during the entire period of the study, the condition of the skin in the area of ​​damage and the application of test samples of spray and gel were analyzed. Certain evaluation criteria have been developed to monitor the development and healing of an cut wound, namely: redness, swelling, length of incisions (wound area), and scab (presence). Every day, the damage area of ​​each experimental animal was assessed according to the developed criteria.

Morphological study of skin tissues of rats from the site of modulation of a pathological condition

Tissue samples were fixed in a 10% neutral buffered formalin solution. After fixation, the material was washed and dehydrated using alcohols of increasing concentration (50º − 96º). After that, the material was placed in a celloidin-castor mixture for 7 days at 37 ºС. Then the material was immersed in a mixture of ethyl alcohol (96º) and chloroform (1:1) for 30 min, and then in a mixture of chloroform and paraffin (1:1) for 45 min at 37ºС. Impregnation of tissues with liquid paraffin (Leica Geosystems, Germany) was carried out in three portions at a temperature of 56º. From each paraffin block, using a sled microtome (AMOS AMR400, Australia), several serial sections with a thickness of 4–5 μm were made, which were placed on chemically clean glass slides. For staining morphological preparations, the sections were deparaffinized in two portions of ortho-xylene followed by washing in ethyl alcohol. Staining was carried out by the classical method - hematoxylin-eosin.

Statistical analysis. Calculations of the average value of the studied indicators (M) and standard deviation (SD) were performed using the Excel 2016 software package. Statistical processing of the results obtained in vitro was carried out using the mathematical program of medical and biological statistics STATISTICA 6.0 using the Student’s t-test. Differences with a probability of at least 95% (p < 0.05) were considered reliable. The non-parametric Mann-Whitney U-test was used to assess the significance levels of the difference of the studied indicators between the control and experimental groups in vivo. Calculations were made using the STATISTICA 6.0 software package. Differences between groups were considered statistically significant if the obtained probability coefficient p was less than the α 0.05 significance level.

Results

The first stage of the study included the assessment of the effect of test samples of gel and spray for external use on the viability, proliferation and metabolic activity of cells in vitro. The analysis used cell lines recommended for preclinical evaluation of APIs, drugs, and medical devices, in particular: mouse endothelial cells (MAEC), mouse fibroblasts (3T3-A31) and human keratinocytes (HaCat). The study used cell lines, which are components of the skin: since the tested product is unlikely to be species-specific, the evaluation was performed on cells from different animals. By evaluating a wide range of concentrations of test samples (in dilutions from 1/16 to 1/12800 of the original product), it was shown that the spray causes a higher level of proliferative activity (increase in the number of living cells) compared to the gel. It was determined that the greatest and statistically significant increase in the number of living cells is observed when assessing endothelial cells (MAEC lines) from 10 to 35% (р≤0.05) in the range of studied dilutions of the spray from 1/16 to 1/256 (Fig. 1). At the same time, judging by the results of the MTT test, a statistically significant increase in the metabolic activity of cells is observed only when the spray is diluted 1/64 (Fig. 2). However, when evaluating the effect of the gel, human keratinocytes (HaCat line) were more sensitive: the number of live cells was statistically significantly higher from 10 to 17% (р≤0.05) in dilutions from 1/800 to 1/3200 compared to control cells (Fig. 3). The fact of a statistically significant increase in the metabolic (respiratory) activity of HaCat cells (according to the results of the MTT test) by 95% (р≤0.05) (Fig. 4) compared to the control cells turned out to be extremely important. It should be added that due to the viscous consistency of the experimental gel, its larger dilutions compared to the spray were studied for an adequate evaluation of the in vitro results. The different effects on cells of different histogenesis may indicate the potential for combined use of the gel and spray in subsequent wound treatment regimens in a certain sequence.

Figures for other groups are not presented here due to the lack of difference in cell culture micrographs when analyzed in control samples and after exposure to test agents. The assessment was carried out by monitoring the overgrowth of cells in the scratch over time: monitoring was carried out at several time points.

It was shown that at the observation point of 0 h there was no statistically significant difference between the sizes of the damaged monolayer area in the control and experimental groups. This confirms the correctness of the selected fields of view for this test. Comparative analysis of the investigated indicator 24 h after the injury revealed that in MAEС cells treated with spray and gel, the area of ​​damage to the cell monolayer was statistically significantly smaller by 36–63% and 78–89%, respectively, compared to the control group (Fig. 7).

An important stage of preclinical assessment, namely the effectiveness of test samples of gel and spray using experimental models in vivo, was the modeling of the pathological condition of the skin of Wistar rats - a CW. After that, the main indicators of the physiological state of rats were evaluated during the long-term (14 days) skin application of samples for therapeutic purposes; the course of the healing process under the influence of the new products was analyzed in comparison with the control group, and a histological analysis of the skin area at the site of CW and, accordingly, application of the gel and spray was performed.

As a result of the observation of the main indicators of the physiological state of experimental rats with CW during repeated skin applications of gel and spray for external use, no violations were noted during the entire observation period. The state of visible membranes, wool, locomotor and respiratory activity, and response to irritation - in all groups is normal.

To evaluate the direct effectiveness of the test samples of the spray or gel, measurements were taken of each incision in the dynamics of each animal, the area of ​​the wound surface was calculated (for each incision and the total − 3) and the rate of wound healing was estimated - % reduction in the area of ​​the wound surface in the dynamics (Table 1). All these indicators were further compared with the indicators of animals of the control group - with CW without treatment (Figs. 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12).

As can be seen from Table 1 and the figures presented the speed of healing of a damaged area of ​​skin in experimental rats after repeated application of experimental samples in the simulation of a CW, a statistically significant increase in the speed of healing (according to calculations of the change in the area of damage in %) from 10 to 19% (depending on days of observation) when using the spray and from 5 to 18% when using the gel in comparison with animals of the control group with simulated CW without treatment (р≤0.05 and р≤0.01).

Histological assessment of the skin of rats with modulated CW under the conditions of repeated application of spray or gel to the skin 8 days after the start of application of experimental samples (9 days after the CW modeling). Histological examination of the skin samples of rats from the CW of the control group revealed signs of the wound-healing process at various stages. In particular, damage to the skin was detected with the formation of a V-shaped gap lined with epithelium, which indicates the beginning of epithelization of the CW. At the same time, CWs with a lack of epithelization were also detected in the skin samples of rats of the control group - a fibrin clot with signs of inflammation was recorded between the edges of tissue damage (Fig. 13a, b). Histological analysis of the damaged area of ​​the skin of rats from the “Spray” group showed that the effect of the experimental sample caused faster and more complete healing of the cut wound in the studied animals. Already 9 days after modeling the cut wound, a significant reduction in its size was observed (Fig. 13c, d). At the same time, there is no swelling in the entire area of ​​damage and beyond it, there are moderate signs of inflammation. There were signs of an active process of epithelization of the damaged skin from the edges of the defect with the formation of granulation tissue. Also, in the samples of the damaged area of ​​the skin of the animals of this group, cuts were found, where the process of epithelization of the skin has almost ended and the restoration of the normal structure of the skin has begun. Also, 9 days after wound modeling, an increase in the number of hair follicles was observed as a source of epidermis, which is needed for wound healing (Fig. 14). After skin injury, epithelial stem cells are known to migrate from the follicle to support wound re-epithelialization, while mesenchymal cells from the hair follicle are mobilized to migrate into the wound bed and promote dermal repair [28].

In animals from the “Gel” group, it was shown that from the 9th day, inflammatory changes and tissue death significantly decreased (compared to control animals). Discussion

In vitro models, such as cell culture, scratch model, and skin explant culture, are designed for studies of the wound process, and preliminary results obtained from them will lead to the planning of further studies [5]. In addition, there is a high probability of finding certain mechanisms involved in the study of the activity of substances and APIs that are investigated using this method. Also, preliminary in vitro evaluation takes relatively little time for research. However, in vivo models remain the most predictive models for studying wound healing, allowing for a realistic representation of the wound environment, including different cell types, environmental signals, and paracrine interactions [30]. It must be remembered that the selected model should take into account such characteristics as the exact reproducibility of the lesion, the possibility for multiple studies, the ability to obtain several samples of biological material in dynamics, ease of handling and the time required to obtain results. The most common species are rats and mice. Although there are documented differences between the structure and physiology of rodents and human skin, wound-healing studies designed to account for these differences may provide valuable translational information [5, 31, 32]. The use of reliable wound assessment methods will contribute to knowledge about wound healing mechanisms and maximize efforts in potentially effective interventions to improve healing.

In this work, we achieved 2 main tasks: we developed a complex algorithm for evaluating the effectiveness of drugs and/or medical products with a potential regenerative effect using an integral approach involving in vitro and in vivo experimental models. And, accordingly, a direct evaluation of the effectiveness of new products - gel and spray medical products about their potential regenerative effect when used for therapeutic purposes on the wound surface of the skin - was carried out. Obtaining effective means for healing wounds of various natures, which at the same time are characterized by safety, is an extremely urgent task today, especially in the presence of active military operations and a large number of injuries to people. Moreover, an important aspect is the convenience of using such means for healing and their multifunctionality. These criteria were important for us when creating and testing new products (medical products - spray and gel for external use). Other wound-healing preparations of a similar profile do not always contain a complex in addition to materials for cell repair (such as collagen) with antiseptics/antibiotics and products for increasing cell number (amino acids as an example), which requires the use of other agents simultaneously for effective treatment of the wound process [33]. According to the authors, collagen-containing products have been shown to improve skin hydration, elasticity, and dermal density [34]. These effects are thought to be mediated by increased fibroblast activity, increased synthesis of extracellular matrix proteins such as elastin, and decreased expression of matrix metalloproteinases, which degrade native collagen. However, while in vitro and clinical studies have shown promising results, conclusive evidence from large randomized controlled trials remains limited [35, 36].

The results of the study of test samples in vitro showed that experimental samples of spray and gel cause a statistically significant increase in the number of living cells in certain concentrations. At the same time, the most significant effect is observed in the study on the model of endothelial cells that confirmed by data in in vivo studies, where an increase in blood vessels and their blood filling in the area of ​​damage was observed after the use of the newly tested products - spray and gel. This fact is extremely interesting and confirms the adequacy of our applied complex approach and correctly selected components of such an algorithm for the study of substances with regenerating properties. Also, the obtained indicators of an increase in the number of blood vessels under the influence of test agents may indicate their potential effect on neoangiogenesis, which is extremely important in tissue regeneration after damage [37]. It was established that both the spray and the gel in the studied concentrations increase the migration ability of endothelial cells and, accordingly, increase the speed of recovery of the damaged monolayer of MAEC cells. This is also confirmed by subsequent in vivo studies, which showed an intensification of the healing process of the damaged area of ​​the skin and an increase in the number of blood vessels after exposure to the test samples. The obtained facts support our idea of ​​the similarity of effects registration when using in vitro models and their comparison with in vivo, which in the future will potentially allow replacing certain stages of preclinical research and, accordingly, reducing the number of experimental animals under the conditions of relevance of the use of cell technologies.

Conclusion

An adequate and informative research algorithm was selected for the evaluation of the effectiveness of medicines / active pharmaceutical ingredients / medical products with potential regenerative activity. We developed and tested a model for assessing the course of wound healing, which proved to be convenient for monitoring the wound healing process and, accordingly, evaluating the treatment outcome. It is easily standardized, and the wound surface can be clearly measured, which will be very important for future similar studies, as it can be easily repeated in any other laboratory. We also simultaneously applied the method of assessing the effectiveness of the regenerative activity of test samples in vitro and in vivo in experimental models that contained certain similar components and showed an interesting similarity. Such observations can be used in the future when developing preclinical protocols for evaluating drugs and medical devices in order to reduce the number of experimental animals, provided that the use of cell models is relevant. Such an algorithm for evaluating substances with regenerative (wound-healing) activity is adequate and easily repeatable and, in our opinion, can be recommended for research in a similar direction. A comprehensive evaluation using experimental in vitro and in vivo models of new gel and spray products for external use showed their effectiveness and their potential role in accelerating wound healing. They can be used to treat traumatic skin injuries due to cuts and blast injuries after relevant clinical research. In addition, the use of products with similar content can be economically beneficial due to their multifunctionality and multi-component composition, as well as relatively inexpensive ingredients.

Limitations

This study has some limitations:

There were limitations in comparing the spray and gel in vitro on cells due to the thicker consistency of the gel, which limited its study at high concentrations in vitro. However, its consistency is extremely convenient for application in vivo to animals.

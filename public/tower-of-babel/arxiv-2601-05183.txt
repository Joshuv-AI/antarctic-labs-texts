Abstract
. We give an explicit construction of 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -gerbes over the differentiable stack [ G / G ] [G/G] , where G G is a compact and connected Lie group. Our construction provides a complete and detailed realization of results previously announced by Behrend–Xu–Zhang, using the description of gerbes over stacks as 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -central extensions of Lie groupoids. Moreover, we prove that the Dixmier–Douady class of the resulting gerbe coincides with the Alekseev–Malkin–Meinrenken equivariant 3-class; under the additional assumtion that G G is compact, simple, and simply connected, this class represents the generator of H G 3 ​ ( G , ℤ ) {\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G,\mathbb{Z}) .

1.
Introduction

Gerbes can be viewed as higher geometric analogues of line bundles, designed to encode topological information in degree three. From this perspective, gerbes provide a flexible framework for organizing higher-degree geometric data, with connections and curvings extending familiar notions from bundle theory. Among concrete models for gerbes, bundle gerbes introduced by Murray
[
21
]
provide a geometric realization on manifolds. Such structures appear prominently in geometric quantization, equivariant geometry, and the study of Wess–Zumino–Witten (WZW) type terms, and they also provide the natural geometric description of background

B
-fields in string theory
[
4
,
11
,
6
]
. Foundational work of Grothendieck and Giraud established that isomorphism classes of gerbes are classified by degree-three cohomology, highlighting their role as geometric representatives of cohomological invariants
[
14
]
.

A particularly important class of examples arises from the differentiable stack

[G/G]
, where

G
is compact, simple, simply connected, acting on itself by conjugation. Geometrically,

[G/G]
may be regarded as a refined quotient that encodes the conjugation symmetry of

G
in a way that remains well-behaved even when the ordinary quotient space

G/G
is singular. From this viewpoint,

[G/G]
provides a natural setting for studying objects that are invariant under conjugation, and it plays a central role in equivariant geometry. In particular, the cohomology of the stack

[G/G]
is naturally identified with the equivariant cohomology

{\rm H}^{\bullet}_{G}(G,\mathbb{Z})
. Within this framework, the Cartan 3-form on

G
, constructed from an invariant bilinear form on the Lie algebra, defines a canonical equivariant cohomology class, which serves as a fundamental example of a degree-three invariant associated to

G
. A central goal is to realize this canonical equivariant degree-three class as the Dixmier–Douady class of an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over
[G/G]
, commonly referred to as the basic gerbe.

The problem of constructing basic

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes over the stack

[G/G]
has been studied from several perspectives. One classical approach is based on Čech-type constructions, in which a gerbe is obtained by gluing local data. Using this method, Gawedzki and Reis constructed gerbes associated to the WZW model in the special case

G=SU(N)

[
11
]
. More generally, Meinrenken
[
20
]
constructed the basic

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over

[G/G]
for any compact, simple, simply connected Lie group

G
, and see
[
12
]
for further development.

A different, global approach was developed by Behrend and Xu, who showed that

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes over differentiable stacks can be described as

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extensions of Lie groupoids, up to Morita equivalence
[
3
]
. Within this framework, Behrend, Xu, and Zhang sketched a construction of the basic gerbe over

[G/G]
using groupoid methods
[
2
]
. However, a complete proof and a detailed geometric realization connecting this construction with the equivariant differential form picture were not provided. The goal of the present paper is to supply such a construction and to give a full, explicit proof that unifies these viewpoints.

For line bundles, the Chern class provides a topological invariant that can be computed from connection and curvature data. An analogous picture holds for

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes: their fundamental topological invariant is the Dixmier–Douady class, an integral cohomology class for differentiable stacks of degree three, which can be described using suitable connection-type data
[
3
,
21
,
15
]
. In this paper, we construct an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over

[G/G]
whose Dixmier–Douady class coincides with the canonical equivariant degree-three class in
{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a compact, simple and simply connecetd Lie group, and let
\alpha\in{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G,\mathbb{Z})
be the canonical generator. There exists an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids representing an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over the differentiable stack

[G/G]
, whose Dixmier-Douady class is
\alpha
.

The proof of Theorem A is based on symplectic reduction and the theory of symplectic groupoids. Starting from the Kac–Moody

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension

\widetilde{LG}\rightarrow LG
, we consider the trivialized cotangent bundle of

\widetilde{LG}
, which naturally carries both a symplectic groupoid structure and a Hamiltonian

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action. Performing symplectic reduction at a nontrivial regular value of the moment map yields a reduced symplectic groupoid with underlying space

LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The moment level set, together with this reduced groupoid, provide the desired

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension, and the reduced symplectic form plays the role of the curvature data for the associated gerbe.

To identify the resulting Dixmier–Douady class, we relate this reduced symplectic groupoid
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightrightarrows L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
to the Alekseev–Malkin–Meinrenken (AMM) quasi-symplectic groupoid
G\times G\rightrightarrows G

[
1
]
. Using the theory of
q-Hamiltonian spaces, we construct a Morita equivalence between these two groupoids and connect the reduced symplectic form with the AMM cocycle

\omega_{D}+\Omega
. This comparison implies that the gerbe obtained from symplectic reduction represents precisely the class
\alpha
. Notably, this Morita equivalence and the identification of cocycles do not rely on the existence of the Kac–Moody extension, nor on the simply connectedness of
G
, and can be established independently.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a compact, connected Lie group.

(1)

There is an
(LG\times LG)
-action on
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, making it a Hamiltonian
(LG\times LG)
-space. Moreover, this space is equivalent to the
q
-Hamiltonian
(G\times G)
-space
G\times G
, in the sense of
[
1
]
.

(2)

The symplectic groupoid
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
and the AMM quasi-symplectic groupoid
(G\times G,\omega_{D}+\Omega)
are Morita equivalent as quasi-symplectic groupoids.

As a by-product, we obtain a construction of symplectic groupoids integrating affine Poisson structures. Recall that an affine Poisson structure on the dual of a Lie algebra is obtained by twisting the Lie–Poisson structure by a Lie algebra
2
-cocycle, see for instance
[
18
]

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For any Lie algebra 2-cocycle
\lambda\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, consider the associated affine coadjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The corresponding action groupoid
\Gamma=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, together with the canonical 2-form with magnetic term
\omega_{\Gamma}\in\Omega^{2}(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, is a symplectic groupoid integrating the affine Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
determined by
\lambda
.

2.
Preliminaries

This section is an exposition of basic definitions and examples related to symplectic groupoids, Hamiltonian actions, and the de Rham cohomology of Lie groupoids, following the conventions outlined in
[
8
,
17
]
.

\bullet

Symplectic groupoids.

A groupoid is a small category in which every morphism admits an inverse. Concretely, it consists of two sets,
\mathcal{G}
(the arrows) and
M
(the objects), together with the following structure maps satisfying group-like axioms:

(1)

Source and target maps

\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t}:\mathcal{G}\to M
, assigning to each arrow its domain and codomain;

(2)

Unit map

\epsilon:M\to\mathcal{G}
, embedding objects as identity arrows;

(3)

Inverse map

\iota:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
, assigning to each arrow its inverse;

(4)

Composition map

\mathbf{m}:\mathcal{G}_{2}\to\mathcal{G}
, where

\mathcal{G}_{2}:=\left\{(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{s}(g)=\mathbf{t}(h)\right\},

denotes the set of
composable arrow pairs
.

A groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is a
Lie groupoid
if
\mathcal{G}
and
M
are smooth manifolds, all structure maps are smooth, and
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t}
are submersions.

Example 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Lie group acting on a manifold
M
by left actions. Then
G\times M
is a Lie groupoid over
M
with source map
\mathbf{s}(g,x)=x
, target map
\mathbf{t}(g,x)=g\cdot x
, and composition map
\mathbf{m}((g,x),(h,y))=(gh,y)
for
x,y\in M
and
g,h\in G
satisfying
h\cdot y=x.
This is called an action groupoid.

Given a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, denote by
{\rm pr}_{1},{\rm pr}_{2}:\mathcal{G}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
the canonical projections onto the first and second components, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie groupoid and
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
a symplectic form.
We say
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is a
symplectic groupoid
if
\omega
is multiplicative, that is,

{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega-\mathbf{m}^{*}\omega=0.

Remark 2.3
.

Any symplectic groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
uniquely determines a Poisson structure
\pi
on the base manifold
M
, so that the groupoid target map
\mathbf{t}:(\mathcal{G},\omega)\rightarrow(M,\pi)
is a symplectic realization.

Recall that for a smooth manifold
M
, the
Liouville form

\theta_{L}\in\Omega^{1}(T^{*}M)

is defined at each
\alpha\in T^{*}M
and
\xi\in T_{\alpha}T^{*}M

by

\theta_{L}(\xi)=\langle(p_{M})_{*}\xi,\alpha\rangle,

where
p_{M}:T^{*}M\rightarrow M
is the natural projection. The canonical 2-form

\omega_{\rm can}\in\Omega^{2}(T^{*}M)

is then given by
\omega_{\rm can}=-d\theta_{L}.
In what follows, we shall present a canonical example of a symplectic groupoid, serving as a fundamental illustration for the preceding theoretical discussions.

Example 2.4
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Consider the coadjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by
g\cdot\eta=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}^{*}\eta
, which satisfies
\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}^{*}\eta,v\rangle=\langle\eta,\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}v\rangle,
for any
v\in\mathfrak{g}
. This induces an action groupoid structure on
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with base space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}.
Define the diffeomorphism

\varphi:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow T^{*}G,\qquad(g,\xi)\mapsto L_{g^{-1}}^{*}\xi,

which trivializes
T^{*}G
using left translations. Let
\omega_{\rm can}\in\Omega^{2}(T^{*}G)
be the canonical symplectic form. Pulling back via
\varphi
, we obtain a closed 2-form
\omega:=-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can}
on
G
. For any
(v_{i},\xi_{i})\in\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong T_{(g,\eta)}(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}),
we have

-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can}\big((v_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},\xi_{2})\big)_{(g,\eta)}=\langle\xi_{1},v_{2}\rangle-\langle\xi_{2},v_{1}\rangle-\langle\eta,[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle.

One can readily verify that
(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*},-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can})
is indeed a symplectic groupoid. The induced Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the Lie-Poisson structure.

\bullet

Hamiltonian actions.

For any left action of a Lie group
G
on a smooth manifold
M
, we define its infinitesimal action as a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
, given by

\rho(v)|_{m}=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\exp(-tv)\cdot m,

for all
v\in\mathfrak{g}
and
m\in M
. Here,
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G
, and
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the space of smooth vector fields on
M.

Suppose
(M,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold, and
G
acts on
M
via symplectomorphisms (i.e., the action preserves the symplectic structure
\omega
). Let

\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
be the corresponding infinitesimal action. A
G
-equivariant smooth map
\mu:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is called a moment map if it satisfies
{\omega}^{\flat}(\rho(v))=d\mu^{v}
, for any
v\in\mathfrak{g}
. Here,
\mu^{v}\colon M\to\mathbb{R}

denotes the smooth function defined by
\mu^{v}(x)=\langle\mu(x),v\rangle
.
When such a moment map
\mu
exists, the triple

(M,\omega,\mu)
is referred to as a
Hamiltonian
G
-space
.

Example 2.5
.

Consider the
G
-action on
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, given by:

g\cdot(h,\xi):=(gh,\xi)\quad\forall g,h\in G,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

The corresponding infinitesimal action
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{X}(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is computed as:

\rho(u)_{(g,\xi)}:=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-tu)\cdot(g,\xi)=\left(-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}u,0\right)

for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
and
(g,\xi)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

For each
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, define the smooth function
f_{u}:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
by:

f_{u}(g,\xi):=\langle u,\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\xi\rangle,

where
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}
is the coadjoint representation of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the natural pairing between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
The
G
-equivariant moment map associated to this action is the target map (see Example
2.1
):

\mathbf{t}:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*},\quad(g,\xi)\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\xi.

To verify the moment map condition, we check that:

(-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can})^{-1}(df_{u})=\rho(u)=\left(-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}u,0\right),

where
(-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can})^{-1}:T^{*}(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})\to T(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denotes the bundle isomorphism induced by the symplectic structure
-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can}
. It is straightforward to confirm that the quadruple
(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*},-\varphi^{*}\omega_{\rm can},\cdot,\mathbf{t})
satisfies all axioms of a Hamiltonian
G
-space.

\bullet

Cohomology of Lie groupoids.

Associated with any Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, we recall the Čech cohomology groups
{\rm H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\Omega^{q})
and the de Rham cohomology groups
{\rm H}^{\bullet}_{\rm dR}(\mathcal{G})
.

For all
p\geq 0
, we define the manifold of composable sequence of
p
arrows as follows,

\mathcal{G}_{p}:=\{(g_{1},\cdots,g_{p})\in\underbrace{\mathcal{G}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{G}}_{p\penalty\ \text{times}}|\mathbf{s}(g_{i})=\mathbf{t}(g_{i+1}),\quad i=1,\cdots,p-1\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{G}_{0}=M
and
\mathcal{G}_{1}=\mathcal{G}.

For each
p\geq 2
and
0\leq i\leq p
, the face maps
\partial^{p}_{i}:\mathcal{G}_{p}\to\mathcal{G}_{p-1}
are defined piecewise as follows:

\partial^{p}_{i}(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{p})=\begin{cases}(g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{p}),&i=0,\\
(g_{1},\dots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\dots,g_{p}),&1\leq i\leq p-1,\\
(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{p-1}),&i=p.\end{cases}

For
p=1
, the face maps reduce to the structural maps of the groupoid:
\partial^{1}_{0}=\mathbf{s}
and
\partial^{1}_{1}=\mathbf{t}.
Collectively, the family
\{\mathcal{G}_{p},\partial^{p}_{i}\}_{p\geq 0,0\leq i\leq p}

forms a simplicial manifold.

For any
q\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, we define the differential

\partial:=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}(\partial_{i}^{p})^{*}\colon\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}_{p-1})\rightarrow\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}_{p}).

It is easy to check that
\partial^{2}=0
, which endows
(\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}_{\bullet}),\partial)
with the structure of a cochain complex. Its cohomology groups are precisely the Čech cohomology groups
{\rm H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\Omega^{q})
.

We further define the de Rham cohomology of the Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
via a double complex construction. The de Rham total complex is defined by
C_{\rm dR}^{n}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}_{p})
. The total differential
\delta:C_{\rm dR}^{n}(\mathcal{G})\rightarrow C_{\rm dR}^{n+1}(\mathcal{G})
is given by
\delta(\omega)=\partial\omega+(-1)^{p}d\omega
, for
\omega\in\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}_{p})\subset C_{\rm dR}^{p+q}(\mathcal{G})
. We thus obtain the de Rham cohomology of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
{\rm H}^{\bullet}_{\rm dR}(\mathcal{G})
.

Note that by the aforementioned degree conventions, any 2-form on
\mathcal{G}
is of degree 3 in the total de Rham complex. For any
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
and
\Omega\in\Omega^{3}(M)
,
\omega+\Omega
is a 3-cocycle in the total de Rham complex if and only if

(1)

d\Omega=0,\quad d\omega=\partial\Omega=\mathbf{s}^{*}\Omega-\mathbf{t}^{*}\Omega,

and

(2)

\partial\omega={\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega-\mathbf{m}^{*}\omega=0.

Finally, one can define sheaf cohomology for Lie groupoids, and see
[
3
]
for details. In particular, the de Rham complex of a Lie groupoid provides a fine resolution of the constant sheaf
\mathbb{R}
, and hence computes its sheaf cohomology. Accordingly, we may identify
{\rm H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{R})={\rm H}^{\bullet}_{\rm dR}(\mathcal{G})
.

Example 2.6
.

For any action groupoid
G\times M\rightrightarrows M
, the de Rham cohomology groups
{\rm H}^{\bullet}_{\rm dR}(G\times M)
coincide with the
G
-equivariant cohomology groups
{\rm H}^{\bullet}_{G}(M)
of
M
with respect to the
G
-action. This example illustrates the compatibility between groupoid cohomology and classical equivariant cohomology.

Example 2.7
.

If
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is a symplectic groupoid, then
\omega
is closed under the de Rham total differential, therefore determines a class in
{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm dR}(\mathcal{G})
.

3.
Symplectic groupoids of affine Poisson structures

In this section, we detail the construction of the symplectic groupoid for affine Poisson manifolds
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
using two methods. The first method provides a direct construction via integration of cocycles under the Van Est morphism. The second method, while requiring stronger assumptions (a group central extension), is essential as it naturally leads to the desired
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids that forms the basis of our gerbe construction.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\lambda\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a Lie algebra 2-cocycle, i.e.,
\lambda(u,[v,w])+\lambda(v,[w,u])+\lambda(w,[u,v])=0
, for any
u,v,w,\in\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\pi_{\lambda}
the
affine Poisson structure
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
associated with
\lambda
, whose Poisson bracket is given by

(3)

\{l_{v_{1}},l_{v_{2}}\}=l_{[v_{1},v_{2}]}+\lambda(v_{1},v_{2}),\quad\forall v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}.

Here
l_{v_{i}}

(i=1,2)
denotes the linear function on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by
l_{v_{i}}(\xi):=\langle\xi,v_{i}\rangle
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

We further define the map
\lambda^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the relation
\langle\lambda^{\flat}(u),v\rangle=\lambda(u,v)
, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.
It is obvious that
\lambda^{\flat}
satisfies

\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{v}\lambda^{\flat}(w)-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{w}\lambda^{\flat}(v)=\lambda^{\flat}([v,w]),\quad\forall v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

This implies that
\lambda^{\flat}
is a 1-cocycle in the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex for the coadjoint representation
\mathfrak{g}\to{\rm End}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, so its cohomology class
[\lambda^{\flat}]\in{\rm H}^{1}_{\operatorname{ad}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is well-defined.

3.1.
First approach: action groupoid for the affine action

Let
\Psi\colon{\rm H}_{\rm\operatorname{Ad}^{*}}^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})\to{\rm H}_{\rm\operatorname{ad}^{*}}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denote the Van Est morphism. On the cochain level,
\Psi
is defined by

\Psi(f)(u)=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}f(\exp(tu)),\quad f\in C^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*}),u\in\mathfrak{g}.

Suppose the map
\lambda^{\flat}
integrates to a
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
-valued Lie group 1-cocycle
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, that is,
\chi
satisfies

(4)

\chi(gh)=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\chi(h)+\chi(g),\quad\forall g,h\in G,

and
\Psi([\chi])=[\lambda^{\flat}]
.
A key result in Van Est theory states that if

G
is connected and simply connected, then the Van Est morphism
\Psi
is an isomorphism
[
9
]
. In this case,
\lambda^{\flat}
always admits an integration.

Following the conventions outlined in
[
18
]
, the
affine action
of group
G
on the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
associated with the 1-cocycle
\chi
is defined as follows:

(5)

g\cdot\xi=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\xi-\chi(g),

for all
g\in G
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
Let
\Gamma:=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denote the transformation groupoid corresponding to the action given by Equation (
5
).

Let
p_{G}:T^{*}G\rightarrow G
be the bundle projection, and
\lambda^{L}\in\Omega^{2}(G)
the left-invariant 2-form associated to
\lambda
. Then the
canonical form with magnetic term

[
18
]
is the 2-form
\omega_{M}\in\Omega^{2}(T^{*}G)
given by

\omega_{M}=\omega_{\rm can}+p_{G}^{*}\lambda^{L}.

Let
\varphi:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong T^{*}G
be the trivialization induced by left translations. Then the 2-form
\omega_{\Gamma}:=-\varphi^{*}\omega_{M}\in\Omega^{2}(\Gamma)
can be described by the following formula:

(6)

\omega_{\Gamma}((v_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},\xi_{2}))_{(g,\eta)}=\langle\xi_{1},v_{2}\rangle-\langle\xi_{2},v_{1}\rangle-\langle\eta,[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle-\lambda(v_{1},v_{2}),

for all
(v_{i},\xi_{i})\in\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong T_{(g,\eta)}\Gamma.
The main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then
(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})
is a symplectic groupoid integrating the affine Poisson structure
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that the Lie group 1-cocycle
\chi\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
integrates
\lambda^{\flat}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then the following identity holds:

\langle\chi(h),[v_{1},v_{2}]\rangle-\lambda(v_{1},v_{2})+\lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2})=0,

for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
h\in G.

Proof.

A straightforward computation yields the following:

\displaystyle\lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\langle\chi(h^{-1}\exp(tv_1)h),\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{*}\chi(\exp(tv_1)h)+\chi(h^{-1})\rangle,\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{*}\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-tv_1)}^{*}\chi(h)+\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{*}\chi(\exp(tv_1))+\chi(h^{-1}),\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=-\langle\chi(h),[v_{1},v_{2}]\rangle+\lambda(v_{1},v_{2}).

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The symplectic form
\omega_{\Gamma}
is multiplicative on
\Gamma
, that is,
{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}-\mathbf{m}_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}=0.

Proof.

We first establish an identification between

\Gamma_{2}
and
G\times G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
via the mapping

\big((g,\beta),(h,\eta)\big)\mapsto(g,h,\eta),\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ g,h\in G,\quad\beta,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Under this identification, the projection maps
{\rm pr}_{1},{\rm pr}_{2}\colon\Gamma_{2}\to\Gamma
and the groupoid multiplication
\textbf{m}_{\Gamma}\colon\Gamma_{2}\to\Gamma
take explicit forms:

{\rm pr}_{1}(g,h,\eta)=(g,h\cdot\eta),\quad{\rm pr}_{2}(g,h,\eta)=(h,\eta),\quad\textbf{m}_{\Gamma}(g,h,\eta)=(gh,\eta),

for all
g,h\in G
and
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

For any
(v,w,\xi)\in\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong T_{(g,h,\eta)}(G\times G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})
,
we compute the pushforward
{{\rm pr}_{1}}_{*}(v,w,\xi)

as follows:

\displaystyle{{\rm pr}_{1}}_{*}(v,w,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}{\rm pr}_{1}\big(g\exp(tv),h\exp(tw),\eta+t\xi\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\big(g\exp(tv),h\exp(tw)\cdot(\eta+t\xi)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(v,\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}h\exp(tw)\cdot\eta+{\rm Ad}_{h^{-1}}^{*}\xi\big)

\displaystyle=\big(v,\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}{\rm Ad}_{h^{-1}}^{*}({\rm Ad}_{\exp(-tw)}^{*}\eta-\chi(\exp(tw)))+\rm Ad_{h^{-1}}^{*}\xi\big)

\displaystyle=\big(v,\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{h^{-1}}(\xi-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{w}\eta-\lambda^{\flat}(w))\big).

Similarly, the pushforward of the projection map
{\rm pr}_{1}
and multiplication map
\mathbf{m}_{\Gamma}
are:

\displaystyle{{\rm pr}_{2}}_{*}(v,w,\xi)

\displaystyle=(w,\xi),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{m}_{\Gamma}}_{*}(v,w,\xi)

\displaystyle=(w+\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v,\xi).

Using the explicit formula for
\omega_{\Gamma}

in Equation (
6
), we compute the pullbacks of
\omega_{\Gamma}
along
{\rm pr}_{1},{\rm pr}_{2}
and
\mathbf{m}_{\Gamma}
for arbitrary tangent vectors
(v_{1},w_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2},\xi_{2})\in T_{(g,h,\eta)}(G\times G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. First, the pullback along
{\rm pr}_{1}
:

\displaystyle({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma})((v_{1},w_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2},\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}\rangle-\langle\eta,[w_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}]\rangle-\lambda(w_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2})

\displaystyle-\langle\xi_{2},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1}\rangle+\langle\eta,[w_{2},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1}]\rangle+\lambda(w_{2},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1})

\displaystyle-\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}[v_{1},v_{2}],\eta\rangle+\langle\chi(h),[v_{1},v_{2}]\rangle-\lambda(v_{1},v_{2}).

Next, the pullback along
{\rm pr}_{2}
:

\displaystyle({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma})((v_{1},w_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2},\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle w_{2},\xi_{1}\rangle-\langle w_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle-\langle[w_{1},w_{2}],\eta\rangle-\lambda(w_{1},w_{2}).

Finally, the pullback along

\textbf{m}_{\Gamma}
:

\displaystyle(\textbf{m}_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma})((v_{1},w_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2},\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle w_{2},\xi_{1}\rangle-\langle w_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle+\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2},\xi_{1}\rangle-\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle[w_{1},w_{2}],\eta\rangle-\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}[v_{1},v_{2}],\eta\rangle-\langle\eta,[w_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}]\rangle+\langle\eta,[w_{2},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1}]\rangle

\displaystyle-\lambda(w_{1},w_{2})-\lambda(w_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2})-\lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1},w_{2})-\lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2}).

By substituting these pullbacks into the combination
{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}-\textbf{m}_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}

and simplifying using Lemma
3.2
, we find that all cross terms cancel, leaving:

\displaystyle({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}-\textbf{m}_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma})((v_{1},w_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2},\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=\langle\chi(h),[v_{1},v_{2}]\rangle-\lambda(v_{1},v_{2})+\lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}v_{2})

\displaystyle=0.

So we conclude that
\omega_{\Gamma}
is multiplicative.

∎

To establish that
(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})
is the symplectic groupoid of
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
, a key requirement is that
\mathbf{t}_{\Gamma}:(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
constitutes a symplectic realization. Indeed, this is equivalent to verifying that
\mathbf{s}_{\Gamma}:(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}^{*},-\pi_{\lambda})
is a symplectic realization. A detailed proof of this result can be found in
[
18
]
; here, we present a concise sketch for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 3.4
.

The groupoid source map
\mathbf{s}_{\Gamma}:(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}^{*},-\pi_{\lambda})
is a symplectic realization.

Proof.

By the definition of
\omega_{\Gamma}
given in Equation (
6
), we have

\omega_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\mathbf{s}_{\Gamma}^{*}dl_{v})_{(g,\xi)}=(-v,-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{v}\xi-\lambda^{\flat}(v)),

for any
(g,\xi)\in\Gamma
and
v\in\mathfrak{g}.
Now, for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
, one can derive

\displaystyle\pi_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{s}_{\Gamma}^{*}dl_{v_{1}},\mathbf{s}_{\Gamma}^{*}dl_{v_{2}})_{(g,\xi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega_{\Gamma}\big((-v_{1},-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{v_{1}}\xi-\lambda^{\flat}(v_{1})),(-v_{2},-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{v_{2}}\xi-\lambda^{\flat}(v_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\xi,[v_{1},v_{2}]\rangle-\lambda(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\pi_{\lambda}(dl_{v_{1}},dl_{v_{2}})_{\xi}.

This directly implies that
(\mathbf{s}_{\Gamma})_{*}\pi_{\Gamma}=-\pi_{\lambda},
which is the desired conclusion.

∎

Combining Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
, we have established Theorem
3.1
.

3.2.
Second approach: reduction of cotangent bundle

Now we present an alternative approach for the construction of symplectic groupoid, adapting the method developed in
[
2
]
. The idea is to recognize the affine Poisson manifold
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
as a Poisson submanifold of some dual Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
equipped with the standard Lie-Poisson structure
\widetilde{\pi}_{\rm Lie}
. Then the symplectic groupoid
\Gamma
of
\pi_{\lambda}
can be constructed via symplectic reduction of the symplectic groupoid associated to
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\widetilde{\pi}_{\rm Lie})
. Notably, this method start with a stronger assumption, namely the existence of a group extension, but the construction yields naturally a central extension of Lie groupoids, which play an important role in later sections.

Any Lie algebra 2-cocycle
\lambda\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
determines a
\mathbb{R}
-central extension of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, as follows. Endow
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R}
with the Lie brackets

[(v_{1},r_{1}),(v_{2},r_{2})]_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}=([v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\lambda(v_{1},v_{2})),

for any
(v_{i},r_{i})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R}.
Then it fits into the short exact sequence

(7)

0\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow 0

where the second arrow denote the inclusion into
\mathbb{R}
-component of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, and the third arrow is the projection onto
\mathfrak{g}
. We can embed
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
via the map
j(\xi):=(\xi,1).

Then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
is a Poisson submanifold of
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\widetilde{\pi}_{\rm Lie})
.

Regarding the central extension of Lie algebras (
7
), let us recall the following result concerning its integration.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
22
]
, Theorem I.2)
.

Assume that
G
is a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, then there exists a Lie group
\widetilde{G}
with Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, and a central extension

(8)

1\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}^{1}\xrightarrow{i}\widetilde{G}\xrightarrow{p}G\xrightarrow{}1,

integrating the central extension (
7
).

From now on, we assume the existence of central extension (
8
). Next, we investigate the relation between the symplectic groupoid of
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
and
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\widetilde{\pi}_{\rm Lie})
. By Example
2.4
, the cotangent groupoid
(T^{*}\widetilde{G},-\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can})
is symplectic groupoid of
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\widetilde{\pi}_{\rm Lie})
. Let
\widetilde{\varphi}:\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\rightarrow T^{*}\widetilde{G}
be the diffeomorphism induced by left translations, then the groupoid
T^{*}\widetilde{G}
can be viewed as the transformation groupoid
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with respect to the coadjoint action of
\widetilde{G}
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. For simplicity, we denote the groupoid
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
as
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.

In view of the central extension (
8
),
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a Lie subgroup of
\widetilde{G}
, which induces a natural
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
\widetilde{G}
by left multiplication. This action lifts to an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\widetilde{G}
, thereby making
\widetilde{\Gamma}\cong T^{*}\widetilde{G}
a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-space. We now explicitly describe this action and its associated moment map on
\widetilde{\Gamma}.

The action map
\widetilde{F}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\widetilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\widetilde{\Gamma}
is given by
\widetilde{F}(s,(\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\eta}))=(s\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\eta}),
for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
(\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\eta})\in\widetilde{\Gamma}.
Let
{\rm pr}_{\mathbb{R}}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the projection. The moment map
\widetilde{\mu}:\widetilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by
\widetilde{\mu}={\rm pr}_{\mathbb{R}}\circ\widetilde{\mathbf{t}}
. Here
\widetilde{\mathbf{t}}
denote the target map of the groupoid
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}.

According to Example
2.5
,

(\widetilde{\Gamma},\widetilde{F},\widetilde{\mu},-\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can})
is a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-space.

Observe that
1
is a regular value of
\widetilde{\mu}
, and
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)=\widetilde{G}\times(\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\{1\})
. Since
\mathbb{S}^{1}
only act on the
\widetilde{G}
-component of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, it follows that the quotient space
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)/\mathbb{S}^{1}
can be expressed as
(\widetilde{G}/\mathbb{S}^{1})\times(\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\{1\})
, which is diffeomorphic to
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\Gamma
as smooth manifolds. Indeed, the groupoid structure on
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)/\mathbb{S}^{1}
also coincides with the transformation groupoid
{\Gamma}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The transformation groupoid
\widetilde{\Gamma}
has a natural structure of Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-space. At the regular value
1
of the moment map, it reduces to the groupoid
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\rm Ad}
and
\rm Ad
denote the adjoint representation of
\widetilde{G}
and
G
, respectively. Given the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension (
8
), the corresponding group 1-cocycle integrating
\lambda^{\flat}
is represented by a map
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, defined by the equation

\langle\chi(p(\widetilde{h})),v\rangle:=-{\rm pr}_{\mathbb{R}}\circ\widetilde{\operatorname{Ad}}_{\widetilde{h}^{-1}}(v,0),

for any
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{G},v\in\mathfrak{g}
.
From this definition, we derive a formula that elucidates the relation between the adjoint representations of
\widetilde{\rm Ad}
and
\rm Ad
. Specifically, for any
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{G}
and
(v,r)\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R}
, we have

\widetilde{\operatorname{Ad}}_{\widetilde{h}}(v,r)=(\operatorname{Ad}_{p(\widetilde{h})}v,r-\langle\chi(p(\widetilde{h})^{-1}),v\rangle).

Next, for any
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{G}
and
(\xi,t)\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{R},
the coadjoint action is given by

\widetilde{\operatorname{Ad}}^{*}_{\widetilde{h}^{-1}}(\xi,t)=(\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{p(\widetilde{h})^{-1}}\xi-t\chi(p(\widetilde{h})),t)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{R}.

By setting
t=1
, we focus on the restricted action of
\widetilde{G}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\{1\}
. This restricted action provides the groupoid structure on
\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which is isomorphic to
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)
. Upon taking the quotient by
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, this action transforms into the affine action as specified in Equation (
5
). Consequently, we conclude that
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)/\mathbb{S}^{1}
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
as Lie groupoids.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Based on the above discussions, given an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groups
\widetilde{G}
over
G
, one can get a
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids
\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
over
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. See Section
5.2
.

Next, we aim to demonstrate that the reduced symplectic form of
-\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}\in\Omega^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
coincides with
\omega_{\Gamma}
as in Equation (
6
). To this end, we define the projection
\widetilde{p}:\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)\rightarrow\Gamma
by
\widetilde{p}(\widetilde{h},(\eta,1))=(p(\widetilde{h}),\eta)
, and introduce the natural inclusion
\widetilde{j}:\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)\rightarrow\widetilde{\Gamma}
. The following result for symplectic reduction of cotangent bundle at regular value
1
is classical, and its proof can be found in
[
18
]
. We sketch a proof here for completion.

Proposition 3.8
.

On
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1),
the formula
-\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}=\widetilde{p}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}
holds, which implies that the symplectic form
-\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}\in\Omega^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
reduces to
\omega_{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma\cong\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)/\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Thus, the reduced symplectic groupoid is ismoorphic to the symplectic groupoid discussed in Section
3.1
.

Proof.

Since
T_{(\widetilde{g},(\eta,1))}\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)\cong\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, for any
(\widetilde{v}_{i},\xi_{i})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we compute by Equation (
6
) that

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}\big((\widetilde{v}_{1},\xi_{1}),(\widetilde{v}_{2},\xi_{2})\big)_{(\widetilde{g},(\eta,1))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\Gamma}((v_{1},\xi_{1}),(v_{2},\xi_{2}))_{(p(\widetilde{g}),\eta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1},v_{2}\rangle-\langle\xi_{2},v_{1}\rangle-\langle\eta,[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle-\lambda(v_{1},v_{2}).

Here
\widetilde{v}_{i}
is decomposed as
v_{i}+r_{i}
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}\big((\widetilde{v}_{1},\xi_{1}),(\widetilde{v}_{2},\xi_{2})\big)_{(\widetilde{g},(\eta,1))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}\big((\widetilde{v}_{1},(\xi_{1},0)),(\widetilde{v}_{2},(\xi_{2},0))\big)_{(\widetilde{g},(\eta,1))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\xi_{1},v_{2}\rangle+\langle\xi_{2},v_{1}\rangle+\langle[\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{v}_{2}]_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}},(\eta,1)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\xi_{1},v_{2}\rangle+\langle\xi_{2},v_{1}\rangle+\langle\eta,[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle+\lambda(v_{1},v_{2}).

Hence, the claim is established.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

In the work of Behrend-Xu-Zhang
[
2
]
, a slightly different approach was sketched for obtaining the symplectic form
\omega_{\Gamma}
. Namely, instead of constructing
\omega_{\Gamma}
directly as Equation (
6
), one can show that the form
\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{can}
on
R:=\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is basic with respect to the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle
R\rightarrow\Gamma.
Then one can go through the proof of Proposition
3.8
and conclude that the unique form one get on
\Gamma
is exactly
\omega_{\Gamma}
.

4.
The Case of Loop Lie algebras

In this section, we apply the construction presented in Section
3.1
to the case of loop Lie algebras. Furthermore, we delve into the relation between the symplectic groupoids obtained through this construction and the celebrated Alekseev-Malkin-Meinrenken (AMM) groupoid.

4.1.
Loop Groups and AMM groupoids

4.1.1.
The symplectic groupoid
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}

Throughout the whole section, we assume that
G
is a compact, connected Lie group, and let
\mathfrak{g}
be its Lie algebra. Following the notation introduced in
[
1
]
, we denote

LG:={\rm Map}(\mathbb{S}^{1},G)
to be the loop group of
G
, which consists of continuous loops in
G
of fixed sobolev space
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}.
The multiplication on
LG
is defined to be the pointwise multiplication. Its Lie algebra is given by
L\mathfrak{g}:=\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathfrak{g})
, which consists of loops in
\mathfrak{g}
with sobolev class
\alpha.

Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be a symmetric invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
, where invariance means
([u,v],w)=(u,[v,w]),
for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

We define
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as the space of
1
-forms
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathfrak{g})
, which is isomorphic to
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\otimes\mathfrak{g}.
Let
s
denote the coordinate on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Then each element of
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is of the form
v(s)ds
, where
v(s)
is a loop in
\mathfrak{g}
with Sobolev class
\alpha-1
. The natural pairing of
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
L\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\langle A,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(A,v),

which identifies
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as a subset of the topological dual
(L\mathfrak{g})^{*}.
Next, we define a map
\lambda:\wedge^{2}L\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(9)

\lambda(v_{1},v_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(v_{1},d{v_{2}}).

Since
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}d(v_{1},{v_{2}})=0
, the map
\lambda
is well-defined. Consequently,
\lambda
induces a map
\lambda^{\flat}:L\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, given by
\lambda^{\flat}(v)=-d{v}
.

Note that the Lie algebra structure on
L\mathfrak{g}
naturally induces an
L\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on

L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, defined by

v\cdot u(s)ds:=[v,u(s)]ds,

for all
v\in L\mathfrak{g}
and
u(s)ds\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Consequently, it is easy to verify that the map
\lambda^{\flat}
is a 1-cocycle, and thus determines a cohomology class

[\lambda^{\flat}]\in{\rm H}^{1}(L\mathfrak{g},L\mathfrak{g}^{*})
.
Then we can define the
affine Poisson structure
on
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
associated with
\lambda
, whose bracket is given by

(10)

\{l_{u},l_{v}\}(A):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}([u,v],A)+\lambda(u,v),

where
u,v\in L\mathfrak{g}
,
A\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
l_{u}
denote the linear function on
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by
l_{u}(A):=\langle u,A\rangle.

On the other hand,
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
also carries an
LG
-module structure defined by

\gamma\cdot u(s)ds:=(\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}u(s))ds,

for all
\gamma\in LG
and
u(s)ds\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. There is a Van Est morphism

\Psi\colon{\rm H}^{1}(LG,L\mathfrak{g}^{*})\longrightarrow{\rm H}^{1}(L\mathfrak{g},L\mathfrak{g}^{*})

defined at the cochain level by

\Psi(f)(v)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}f(\exp(tv)),

for all
v\in L\mathfrak{g}
and
f\in C^{1}(LG,L\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Although the Van Est map need not be an isomorphism in degree one for a general connected Lie group, the Lie algebra cocycle
\lambda^{\flat}
integrates explicitly in this case. Indeed, it is the image under the Van Est map of the group
1
-cocycle
\chi\colon LG\to L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by
\chi(\gamma)=-(d\gamma)\gamma^{-1}
. Hence, the Van Est integrability condition from Section
3.1
always holds for
\lambda^{\flat}
.

The loop group
LG
further acts on
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
via the gauge action, given by

(11)

\gamma\cdot A=\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}A+(d{\gamma})\gamma^{-1},

for any
\gamma\in LG
and
A\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Therefore, employing the same approach as discussed in Section
3.1
, we obtain a symplectic groupoid
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the transformation groupoid associated to the gauge action defined in Equation (
11
). For any tangent vector
(v_{i},A_{i})\in L\mathfrak{g}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong T_{(\gamma,A)}(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}),
we define a symplectic form

(12)

\displaystyle\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}((v_{1},A_{1}),(v_{2},A_{2}))_{(\gamma,A)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(A_{1},v_{2})-(A_{2},v_{1})-(A,[v_{1},v_{2}])-(v_{1},d{v_{2}}).

Then
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
is a symplectic groupoid.

Remark 4.2
.

When
G
is a compact, simply connected Lie group, then a group central extention of
LG
exists, and the method from Section
3.2
applies. In this case, we get an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids over
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and see Section
5.2
for further discussions.

4.1.2.
The AMM groupoid
G\times G

Next, we recall the AMM quasi-symplectic groupoid on
G\times G
, first introduced by Alekseev-Malkin-Meinrenken in
[
1
]
and further developed by Xu in
[
24
]
.

We begin with the definition of a quasi-symplectic groupoid. A
quasi-symplectic structure
on a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
over a base manifold
M
consists of a 2-form

4.2.
Relation of

In this section, we establish the relationship between the symplectic groupoid

4.2.1.
The holonomy map

Recall that for a principal bundle with a connection, the holonomy of a loop on the base manifold is defined as the parallel transport along that loop. In the special case of the trivial
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, the space of connections
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathfrak{g})
can be identified with
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. By picking the identity loop
{\rm id}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
and computing its holonomy, we obtain a map
\rm Hol:L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow G
, called
the holonomy map
.

Let
\Omega G\subset LG
be the set of loops based at the identity element

.

Lemma 4.7
.

[
1
]

The restricted action of
\Omega G
on
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
from Equation (

a principal
\Omega G
-bundle over

{\rm Hol}:L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow G

An equivalent, analytic description of the holonomy map is often useful. We identity
\mathbb{S}^{1}\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and denote by

the local coordinate.
Let
{\rm Hol}_{s}(A):[0,1]\rightarrow G
be the solution to the ODE
[

]

(14)

{\rm Hol}_{s}(A)^{-1}\frac{d}{ds}{\rm Hol}_{s}(A)=A(s),

{\rm Hol}_{0}(A)=e
. Then

is given by
\rm Hol(A)=\rm Hol_{1}(A)

A crucial property of the holonomy map is its compatibility with the

-action on

. Specifically, for any

, and

where

denotes the gauge action defined in Equation (

\Omega G
is a normal subgroup of

-quotient to the

coincides with the conjugation action of

on itself. This compatibility implies that the natural projection

is a Morita morphism, a key notion we recall below.

Recall that, for two Lie groupoids
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
and
\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows N
, a Lie groupoid morphism is a pair of maps
F:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
and

, that intertwines the groupoid structure maps. A Lie groupoid morphism

over

is called a
Morita morphism
, if

•

f
is a surjective submersion, and

•

The pullback groupoid

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}
.

Here, the arrow space of the pullback groupoid is the fiber product

, and the base space is
M
.
Two Lie groupoids

\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
are said to be
Morita equivalent
, if there exists a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{K}
together with Morita morphisms
\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{G}
-
\mathcal{H}
-bimodule structure on the base manifold of

, as an analogue for the Morita equivalence for algebras
[
24
]

Proposition 4.8

[

4.2.2.
The inversion map

Next, we recall the notion of inversion maps, and summarize the compatibility properties between the holonomy map and the inversion map. The use of the inversion maps follows from
[
19
]
.

Let
I:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
denote the map
I(s)=1-s.

Definition 4.9
.

The
inversion map

{\rm inv}:L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is defined by

{\rm inv}(A)(s)=-A(I(s))=-A(1-s),\quad\forall A\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\quad s\in[0,1].

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
A\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the holonomy of
{\rm inv}(A)
satisfies

{\rm Hol}_{s}({\rm inv}(A))={\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A).

In particular,
{\rm Hol}({\rm inv}(A))={\rm Hol}(A)^{-1}
.

Proof.

It suffices to check that
H(s):={\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A)
satisfies the ODE defined in Equation (
14
). Through direct computation, we obtain

\displaystyle H(s)^{-1}\frac{d}{ds}H(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[{\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A)]^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=s}[{\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-t}(A)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[{\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A)]^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=1-s}{\rm Hol}_{t}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A)^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=1-s}{\rm Hol}_{t}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A(1-s)={\rm inv}(A)(s).

∎

Lemma 4.11
.

For any
\gamma\in LG
and
A\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, in view of the action defined by Equation (
11
), we have

\gamma\cdot{\rm inv}(A)={\rm inv}((I^{*}\gamma)\cdot A).

Here,
I^{*}\gamma\in LG
denotes the pullback of

\gamma
by
I
, defined by
I^{*}\gamma(s)=\gamma(I(s))=\gamma(1-s).

Proof.

By the chain rule, we have
\frac{d}{dt}I^{*}\gamma=-I^{*}\frac{d}{dt}\gamma
, and thus
(\frac{d}{dt}I^{*}\gamma)(I^{*}\gamma)^{-1}=-I^{*}(\dot{\gamma}\gamma^{-1})
. Since
I^{2}
is the identity map, we get that
\dot{\gamma}\gamma^{-1}=-I^{*}(\frac{d}{dt}I^{*}\gamma)(I^{*}\gamma)^{-1}
. By definition of
{\rm inv}(A)=-I^{*}A
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma\cdot{\rm inv}(A)=\gamma\cdot(-I^{*}A)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}(-I^{*}A)+\dot{\gamma}{\gamma}^{-1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\rm inv}((I^{*}\gamma)\cdot A)

\displaystyle={\rm inv}(\operatorname{Ad}_{I^{*}\gamma}A+(\frac{d}{dt}I^{*}\gamma)(I^{*}\gamma)^{-1})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}I^{*}A-I^{*}(\frac{d}{dt}I^{*}\gamma)(I^{*}\gamma)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}(-I^{*}A)+\dot{\gamma}{\gamma}^{-1}.

Thus the result follows.
∎

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}{\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A_{t}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}{\rm Hol}_{1}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A_{t}){\rm Hol}_{1-s}(A)^{-1}{\rm Hol}_{1}(A))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Ad}_{{\rm Hol_{1}(A)^{-1}}}\overline{\theta}({\rm Hol}_{1-s}^{*}V).

Hence we have

{\rm inv}^{*}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta}=-\operatorname{Ad}_{{\rm Hol}(A)^{-1}}{\rm Hol}^{*}\bar{\theta}+\operatorname{Ad}_{{\rm Hol}(A)^{-1}}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}^{*}\bar{\theta}.

Note that
{\rm Hol}_{0}^{*}\bar{\theta}=0
and
({\rm Hol}^{*}\bar{\theta},{\rm Hol}^{*}\bar{\theta})=0.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have that

\int_{0}^{1}({\rm Hol}^{*}\bar{\theta},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}{\rm Hol}^{*}\bar{\theta})=0.

Then it follows from the definition of
\varpi
and
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariance of
(\cdot,\cdot)
that

\displaystyle{\rm inv}^{*}\varpi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}({\rm inv}^{*}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}{\rm inv}^{*}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta})ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(-{\rm Hol}^{*}\bar{\theta}+{\rm Hol}_{1-s}^{*}\bar{\theta},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}^{*}\bar{\theta})ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}({\rm Hol}_{1-s}^{*}\bar{\theta},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}{\rm Hol}_{1-s}^{*}\bar{\theta})ds

\displaystyle=-\varpi.

∎

In order to show Theorem
4.12
, we still need to check the following.

Lemma 4.18
.

For any
W_{1},W_{2}\in L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we have that

\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}((0,W_{1}),(0,W_{2}))=\sigma((0,W_{1}),(0,W_{2}))=0.

Proof.

It is clear that
\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}((0,W_{1}),(0,W_{2}))=0.
Also, we compute by Equation (
13
) that

f^{*}\omega_{D}\big((0,W_{1}),(0,W_{2})\big)_{(h,\eta)}=\omega_{D}\big((0,{\rm Hol}_{*}W_{1}),(0,{\rm Hol}_{*}W_{2})\big)_{(h(0),Hol(\eta))}=0.

Moreover, denote by
\varrho(h):L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
to be the action map for Equation (
11
), then we have by Lemma
4.17
that

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(\varpi,\varpi)((0,W_{1}),(0,W_{2}))_{(h,\eta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle inv}^{*}\varpi(W_{1},W_{2})+\varrho(h)^{*}\varpi(W_{1},W_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varpi(W_{1},W_{2})+\varrho(h)^{*}\varpi(W_{1},W_{2})

By Lemma A.1 of
[
1
]
, we have

(21)

\varrho(h)^{*}{\rm Hol}^{*}_{s}\bar{\theta}=\operatorname{Ad}_{h(0)}{\rm Hol}^{*}_{s}\bar{\theta}.

By the
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariance of
(\cdot,\cdot)
we have that

\displaystyle\varrho(h)^{*}\varpi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(\varrho(h)^{*}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta},\varrho(h)^{*}\frac{d}{ds}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta})ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(\operatorname{Ad}_{h(0)}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta},\operatorname{Ad}_{h(0)}\frac{d}{ds}{\rm Hol}_{s}^{*}\bar{\theta})ds=\varpi.

Therefore we get that
\Phi^{*}(\varpi,\varpi)=0
.
∎

It is clear that Part (2) of Theorem
4.12
follows from Lemma
4.16
and Lemma
4.18
. Finally, Part (3) is a direct consequence of Part(2).

4.3.2.
Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic groupoids

Now we shall apply Theorem
4.12
to obtain the proof for Theorem B. Recall that
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the action groupoid corresponds to the gauge action of
LG
on
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, while
D=G\times G
is the action groupoid of the conjugate action. Following Proposition
4.8
, the map
f:LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow D
given by
f(\gamma,A)=(\gamma(0),\rm{Hol}(A))
is a Morita morphism of Lie groupoids.

We show that
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
and
(D,\omega_{D}+\Omega)
are Morita equivalence as quasi-symplectic groupoids. Let
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\omega_{1}+\Omega_{1})
and
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime},\omega_{2}+\Omega_{2})
be two quasi-symplectic groupoids. Recall that the Lie groupoids
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime}
are Morita equivalent if there exists a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime\prime}
, together with Morita morphisms
f_{1}:\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and
f_{2}:\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime}
. We say
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\omega_{1}+\Omega_{1})
and
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime},\omega_{2}+\Omega_{2})
are
Morita equivalent as quasi-symplectic groupoids
, if
f_{1}^{*}[\omega_{1}+\Omega_{1}]=f_{2}^{*}[\omega_{2}+\Omega_{2}]
in
\rm H^{3}_{dR}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime\prime})
, and see
[
24
]
for details.

We first recall the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.19

(
[
1
]
)
.

For the 2-form
\varpi\in\Omega^{2}(L\mathfrak{g}^{*})
defined by Equation (
16
), we have that

d\varpi=-\rm{Hol}^{*}\Omega,

where
{\rm Hol}\colon L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to G
is the holonomy map and
\Omega\in\Omega^{3}(G)
is the invariant 3-form.

Combining Theorem
4.12
and Lemma
4.19
, we get the following result, which states that the Morita morphism
f
sends the groupoid cohomology class
[\omega_{D}+\Omega]
to
[\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}]
. The formula is first stated in
[
2
]
without a proof.

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
\delta
be the de Rham differential on the groupoid
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. We have that

(22)

\delta\varpi=\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}-f^{*}(\omega_{D}+\Omega).

Proof.

First, recall that the Morita morphism
f\colon LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to G\times G
is compatible with the base manifold map
{\rm Hol}\colon L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to G
. Then it follows that

f^{*}(\omega_{D}+\Omega)=f^{*}\omega_{D}+{\rm Hol}^{*}\Omega.

Next, by the definition of the groupoid de Rham differential
\delta
, we have that

\delta\varpi=\mathbf{s}^{*}\varpi-\mathbf{t}^{*}\varpi+d\varpi.

We may write the moment map
\Phi
as
\Phi=(\mathbf{t},{\rm inv}\circ\mathbf{s})
. Then by Lemma
4.17
we compute that

\Phi^{*}(\varpi,\varpi)=\mathbf{t}^{*}\varpi+(\rm{inv}\circ\mathbf{s})^{*}\varpi=\mathbf{t}^{*}\varpi-\mathbf{s}^{*}\varpi.

By Theorem
4.12
, the symplectic form
\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
satisfies the identity:

f^{*}\omega_{D}-\Phi^{*}(\varpi,\varpi)=\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
. Therefore, by the above equations and Lemma
4.19
,

\displaystyle\delta\varpi=-\Phi^{*}(\varpi,\varpi)+d\varpi

\displaystyle=\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}-f^{*}\omega_{D}-\rm{Hol}^{*}\Omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}-f^{*}(\omega_{D}+\Omega).

∎

Finally, we have the Morita equivalence between quasi-symplectic groupoids, which complete the proof of the following result, announced in
[
24
,
2
]
.

Theorem 4.21
.

The quasi-symplectic groupoids
(D,\omega_{D}+\Omega)
and
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
are Morita equivalent. The Morita morphism
f:LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow D
induces an isomorphism

f^{*}:{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm dR}(D)\cong{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G)\rightarrow{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm dR}(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}),

which sends the groupoid cohomology class
[\omega_{D}+\Omega]
to
[\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}]
.

Proof.

By Proposition 4.8 of
[
24
]
, since
f:LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow D
is a Morita morphism of Lie groupoids over the base map
{\rm Hol}\colon L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to G
, the quasi-symplectic groupoid
(D,\omega_{D}+\Omega)
is Morita equivalent to the pullback quasi-symplectic groupoid
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},f^{*}\omega_{D}+\rm{Hol}^{*}\Omega)
.

By Proposition 4.6 of
[
24
]
, Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic groupoids is invariant under adding or subtracting the groupoid de Rham differential of a 2-form. Specifically, the quasi-symplectic groupoid
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},f^{*}\omega_{D}+\rm{Hol}^{*}\Omega)
is Morita equivalent to
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},f^{*}\omega_{D}+\rm{Hol}^{*}\Omega+\delta\varpi)
. Combining this with the identity from Proposition
4.20
,
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},f^{*}\omega_{D}+\rm{Hol}^{*}\Omega+\delta\varpi)
coincides with the symplectic groupoid
(LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
.
∎

Remark 4.22
.

The above result finds applications in the study of prequantization
[
16
]
, and the unversal characteristic class of 2-group bundles, see Example 4.28 of
[
13
]
.

Remark 4.23
.

When
G
is compact, connected, and simply connected, the above result admit an elegant interpretation through the lens of moduli space of flat connections, see
[
1
,
19
]
. Our work extends the result to the non simply connected case.

5.
An
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over the stack
[G/G]

In this section, we discuss the stack-theoretic interpretation of our previous results, connecting the constructed symplectic groupoids and their extensions to equivariant cohomology classes. We begin by reviewing the definitions of stacks and gerbes, and then provide detailed explanations of how the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-extension of the groupoid
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightrightarrows L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
leads to the construction of an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over the differentiable stack
[G/G]
.

5.1.
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes and Dixmier-Douady classes

The notion of stacks is usually defined in an abstract way. For our context, we focus on the differentiable stacks, introduced by Behrend and Xu in
[
3
]
. Indeed,
differentiable stacks
are Lie groupoids up to Morita equivalence. For any Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, we use
[\mathcal{G}]
to denote the corresponding differentiable stack. Due to conventional usage in the literature, we adopt the notation
[G/G]
for the differentiable stack
[G\times G]
. For any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
and any Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, the sheaf cohomology of Lie groupoids
{\rm H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F})
is invariant under Morita equivalence. Thus, we take the cohomology group
{\rm H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F})
as the
sheaf cohomology of differentiable stack [
\mathcal{G}
]
, denoted as
{\rm H}^{\bullet}([\mathcal{G}],\mathcal{F})
.

An
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension
of a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is a groupoid
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M
satisfying a short exact sequence of Lie groupoids

\{e\}\times M\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times M\xrightarrow{\widetilde{i}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{p}}\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{}\{e\}\times M,

where
\widetilde{i}
and
\widetilde{p}
are groupoid morphisms over
\operatorname{id}_{M}
, and for any
\widetilde{g}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},r\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we have that
\widetilde{i}(\mathbf{t}(\widetilde{g}),r)\cdot\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{g}\cdot\widetilde{i}(\mathbf{s}(\widetilde{g}),r).

According to
[
3
]
, an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
over a differentiable stack
[\mathcal{G}]
can be viewed as an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extensions of Lie groupoids
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
, up to Morita morphism of central extensions. We shall use
[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}]
to denote this
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe.

Example 5.1
.

Given a central extension of Lie groups
1\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1
, we have that
\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids. Therefore,
[\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
is an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over the stack
[G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
.

Indeed,
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extensions give meanings to certain cohomology classes, due to the following deep result of Giraud.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
14
]
)
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the isomorphism classes of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes over a differentiable stack
[\mathcal{G}]
, and the cohomology group
{\rm H}^{2}([\mathcal{G}],\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

In particular, any central extension of Lie groupoids
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
defines a class
[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}]\in{\rm H}^{2}([\mathcal{G}],\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Such classes can be related to higher degree cohomology. Consider the exponential sequence of sheaves
0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\Omega^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow 0
. This sequence gives rise to a long exact sequence in cohomology, yielding a connecting morphism
{\rm H}^{2}([{\mathcal{G}}],\mathbb{S}^{1})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{3}([{\mathcal{G}}],\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
be an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids. Under the connecting morphism
{\rm H}^{2}([{\mathcal{G}}],\mathbb{S}^{1})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{3}([{\mathcal{G}}],\mathbb{Z})
, the image of the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}]
in
{\rm H}^{3}([{\mathcal{G}}],\mathbb{Z})
is called the
Dixmier-Douady class
of
[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}]
.

Remark 5.4
.

Note that for a compact Lie group
G
, the AMM groupoid
D=G\times G\rightrightarrows G
is a proper Lie groupoid, as the map
(s,t):D\to G\times G
is proper. This property is significant for the theory of gerbes, as it ensures the isomorphism
{\rm H}^{2}([G/G],\mathbb{S}^{1})\cong{\rm H}^{3}([G/G],\mathbb{Z})
, which means the Dixmier-Douady class truly classifies the
S^{1}
-gerbes over
D
(up to Morita equivalence).

Dixmier-Douady classes serve as higher analogues of Chern classes, representing a topological invariant that characterizes the extension.
Similar to how Chern classes are computed from curvature data, Dixmier-Douady classes for groupoid extensions can be related to curvature-like 3-forms. This essential connection is established by Behrend and Xu
[
3
]
. For an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids
\widetilde{p}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
, a
pseudo-connection
is defined as a pair
(\vartheta,B)
, where
\vartheta\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
is a connection 1-form for the associated principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle, and
B\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is a 2-form on the base manifold. The
pseudo-curvature
of this pseudo-connection is a specific 3-form
\eta+\omega+\Omega
, where
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{2})
,
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
, and
\Omega\in\Omega^{3}(M)
are derived from
\delta(\vartheta+B)=p^{*}(\eta+\omega+\Omega)\in C_{\rm dR}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
. Behrend and Xu proved the following fundamental result relating pseudo-curvature to the Dixmier-Douady class:

Theorem 5.5

(
[
3
]
)
.

Given any pseudo-connection
(\theta,B)
and its pseudo-curvature
\eta+\omega+\Omega
, the corresponding de Rham class
[\eta+\omega+\Omega]\in{\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}([\mathcal{G}])
is independent of the choice of pseudo-connection.

Under the canonical homomorphism
{\rm H}^{3}([\mathcal{G}],\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{3}([\mathcal{G}],\mathbb{R})={\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}([\mathcal{G}])
, the Dixmier-Douady class of
[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}]
maps to
[\eta+\omega+\Omega]
. In particular, the class
[\eta+\omega+\Omega]\in{\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}([\mathcal{G}])
is an integer class.

We may apply this framework to our construction. As established in Section
3.2
, given an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension
\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G
, our constructed symplectic groupoid
(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})
possesses a natural
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension
\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\Gamma
, which can be viewed as a principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle. The symplectic form
\omega_{\Gamma}
itself naturally arises from the symplectic reduction of the minus canonical form
-\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}:=\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. Let
\widetilde{j}:\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\widetilde{\Gamma}
be the inclusion map and
\theta_{L}\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
be the canonical Liouville form. According to Proposition
3.8
, it is clear that

d(\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\theta_{L})=-\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{\rm can}=\widetilde{p}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}.

Note that
\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\theta_{L}
is a connection 1-form for the principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle
\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\Gamma
. Consequently,
\widetilde{j}^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}\theta_{L}
is a pseudo-connection for our central extension, whose pseudo-curvature is precisely
\omega_{\Gamma}
.
Therefore, we conclude:

Proposition 5.6
.

The Dixmier-Douady class of the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
[\widetilde{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\Gamma]
maps to the de Rham class
[\omega_{\Gamma}]\in{\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}([\Gamma])
under the canonical homomorphism
{\rm H}^{3}([\Gamma],\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow{\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}([\Gamma])
.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem A

Now we return to the main theme of this paper, namely, constructing the basic
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over
[G/G]
. We shall construct explicitly cental extensions of Lie groupoids whose Dixmier-Douady classes coincides with the AMM class. Let
G
be a compact, connected Lie group, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

The differentiable stack
[G/G]
can be presented by both the action groupoids
G\times G
and
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}.
We shall pick
LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as the base for the central extensions, and the reason is as follows. The existence and classification of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extensions for a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}
are governed by a long exact sequence (Proposition 4.7 of
[
3
]
):

\displaystyle{\rm H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathbb{S}^{1})

\displaystyle\to\{\mathbb{S}^{1}\text{-central\penalty\ extensions \penalty\ of \penalty\ }\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}\}

\displaystyle\to{\rm H}^{2}([\mathcal{G}_{1}],\mathbb{S}^{1})\to{\rm H}^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathbb{S}^{1}).

In our case, we have
\mathcal{G}_{0}=L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. This choice is strategic because
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a contractible (infinite-dimensional) vector space, meaning its singular cohomology
{\rm H}^{k}(L\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
vanishes for
k>0
. This property simplifies the long exact sequence, ensuring that any
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over
[G/G]
can be represented by a corresponding
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of a Lie groupoid based at
L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

The loop Lie algebra cocycle in Equation (
9
) gives rise to a Lie algebra structure on the extended loop algebra
\widetilde{L\mathfrak{g}}:=L\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R},

[(v_{1},r_{1}),(v_{2},r_{2})]=([v_{1},v_{2}],\lambda(v_{1},v_{2})),

where
(v_{i},r_{i})\in\widetilde{L\mathfrak{g}}.

Then it gives a central extension of Lie algebras:

0\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\widetilde{L\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow L\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow 0.

If the central extension above to an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groups

(23)

1\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\widetilde{LG}\rightarrow LG\rightarrow 0,

then this is called the Kac-Moody extension. From now on, we assume that
G
is compact, connected, and the Kac-Moody extension for
LG
exists (for example, if
G
is simply connected
[
23
]
).

By the discussions in Section
3.2
, the space
\widetilde{LG}\times\widetilde{L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
with the canonical 2-form, constitute a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-space, with moment map
\widetilde{\mu}
. Here, following
[
19
]
we take the convention
\widetilde{L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}=L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{R}
. It is then clear that
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)=\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(1)/\mathbb{S}^{1}=LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. We thus obtain an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids

\{e\}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{i}}\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{p}}LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{}\{e\}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*},

which represents an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
[\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
over the stack
[LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}]=[G/G]
.

To study the Dixmier-Douady class of
[\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
, we may again take the Liouville 1-form on
\widetilde{LG}\times\widetilde{L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
, which pullback to a connection 1-form on
\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. By the discussion in Proposition
5.6
, we have that
\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
is the pseudo-curvature. We now conclude by Proposition
5.5
and Theorem
4.21
that

Theorem 5.7
.

The
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension
\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defines an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
[\widetilde{LG}\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
over the stack
[G/G]
. Its Dixmier-Douady class has image
[\omega_{LG\times L\mathfrak{g}^{*}}]=[\omega_{D}+\Omega]\in{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm dR}([G/G])
, under the canonical morphism
{\rm H}^{3}([G/G],\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow{\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}([G/G])
.

Finally, we explain why the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe constructed here is the basic gerbe. For any compact, simply connected simple Lie group
G
, it is well-known that the degree-3 integral equivariant cohomology group
{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. Under the inclusion
{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G,\mathbb{Z})\subset{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G)
, the image of the canonical generator of
\mathbb{Z}
is represented by the equivariant 3-form
\chi_{G}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
in the Cartan model, given by
\chi_{G}(\xi)=\Omega-\frac{1}{2}(\theta+\overline{\theta},\xi)
. An
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe is called basic
[
20
]
, if its Dixmier-Douady class is represented by
[\chi_{G}]
. In
[
5
]
, it is explicitly computed that, under the canonical isomorphism
{\rm H}^{3}_{G}(G)\cong{\rm H}_{\rm dR}^{3}(G\times G)
, the class
[\chi_{G}]
is identified with the AMM class. Since the Dixmier-Douady class of the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe from our construction coincides with the AMM class, we conclude that it generates the basic
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe.

A CR structure with blowing up solutions to the CR Yamabe problem
Claudio Afeltra, Andrea Pinamonti

Abstract
We prove the existence of a CR structure on S 3 S^{3} such that the set of solutions to the CR Yamabe problem is not compact and admits a blowing-up sequence. Such CR structure is built deforming the standard CR structure of S 3 S^{3} in the direction of the Rossi sphere CR structure on small balls, and the existence of the blowing-up sequence of solutions is proved through the Lyapunov-Schmidt method.



1
Introduction

The efforts to solve the Yamabe problem, that is the problem of finding a Riemannian metric conformally equivalent to a given one and with constant scalar curvature, have lead to huge developments in the field of geometric analysis, until the final part of the solution was completed by Richard Schoen in 1984, after previous contributions by Yamabe, Trudinger, Aubin and others (see
[
LP
]
for a survey of the history and proof of the problem).

After the solution of the problem, it was conjectured that the set of the solutions thereto is compact. After numerous partial results, the conjecture was proved to be true in dimension
n\leq 24
by Khuri, Marques and Schoen (see
[
KMS
]
), while surprisingly in higher dimension it was proved to be false by Brendle (for
n\geq 52
, see
[
B
]
) and Brendle and Marques (for
25\leq n\leq 51
, see
[
BM
]
).

The same problems can be posed in CR geometry.
CR geometry is the study of manifolds endowed with a certain geometric structure which arises in the study of real hypersurfaces of complex manifolds (see Section
2
for definitions). These manifolds carry a natural cotact structure, and the choice of a contact form determines a rich geometric structure, including a connection known as Tanaka-Webster connection, and a Riemannian metric.
Analogously to Riemannian geometry, the curvature tensor of the connection can be contracted twice with the metric giving rise to a scalar invariant known as Webster curvature.
Since the choice of a contact form is determined up to the multiplication by a nowhere zero function, it is natural to pose the so called CR Yamabe problem, that is the problem of finding a contact form such that the associated Webster curvature is constant.

This problem has striking similarities with the (Riemannian) Yamabe problem.
In particular the non conformally flat, high dimensional case was solved by Jerison and Lee in

[
JL1
]
and
[
JL3
]
by following the same strategy of the analogous Riemannian case; the remaining cases were solved by Gamara and Yacoub in
[
G
]
and
[
GY
]
using the method of critical points at infinity.

It is therefore natural to study the question of the compactness of the set of solutions to the CR Yamabe problem.
The study of this problem in Riemannian geometry uses blow-up analysis, which consists in taking a sequence which supposedly violates compactness and rescaling and dilating it around a local maximum in such a way to get a solution to the Yamabe problem in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. The analysis therefrom proceeds using the classification theorem by Caffarelly, Gidas and Spruck (see
[
CGS
]
).
Unfortunately the reproduction of this proof in CR geometry presents the obstacle that blowing up a CR manifold around a point one gets the Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{n}
, and for it there does not currently exist an analogous to the theorem of Caffarelly, Gidas and Spruck for
\mathbf{H}^{n}
.

In 2023, Catino, Li, Monticelli and Roncoroni proved such a classification theorem for
\mathbf{H}^{1}
(see
[
CLMR
]
). This allowed the first author of this work to prove a compactness theorem (see
[
A
]
).
With respect to the Riemannian case, such theorem had an additional hypothesis, that is that the pseudohermitian mass of the blow-up structure at every point is positive. The pseudohermitian mass is a quantity defined for asymptotically flat CR manifolds which is analogous to the ADM mass for asymptotically flat manifolds, and similarly appears in the expansion of the conformal sublaplacian; but unlike its Riemannian counterpart, it can be negative also for CR manifolds with positive Webster curvature, as was showed by an example by Cheng, Malchiodi and Yang in
[
CMY
]
.

In
[
A
]
the author raised the question of whether such hypothesis on the pseudohermitian mass was necessary in the compactness theorem proved therein.
In this article, we answer affermatively to the question and find a counterexample.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a CR structure on
S^{3}
, not equivalent to the standard one, such that the associated CR Yamabe equation

L_{J,\theta}u=2u^{3}

(where
L_{J,\theta}
is the conformal sublaplacian, defined in Section
2
) has a set of solutions
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
with
\max u_{k}\to\infty
.

In order to build this counterexample, we took inspiration by the strategy of Brendle in
[
B
]
for building an analogous counterexample for Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n\geq 52
.
The reason why the compactness theorem holds only in dimension
n\leq 24
is that a certain quadratic form arising from the blow-up analysis is positive definite in such dimensions, while this is not true for
n\geq 25
.
The starting point of Brendle’s proof was to deform the Euclidean metric of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
in a direction in which the quadratic form is negative.
Then he considered the manifold consisting of “bubbles” (that is, solutions to the Yamabe problem for the Euclidean metric) and applied the Lyapunov-Schmidt method, a method to find critical points of a perturbed functional starting from a manifold of critical points of the unperturbed functional.
Through delicate estimates, he proved that the existence of deformed metrics admitting arbitrarily large solutions to the Yamabe problem, and finally he build the required counterexample by modifying the Euclidean metric on the union of a sequence of disjoint balls
B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})
.

In our case, since the essential reason for the failure of the compactness theorem for three-dimensional CR manifolds is the fact that the pseudohermitian mass can be negative, our strategy is to deform the standard CR structure of
\mathbf{H}^{1}
in the direction of the only known counterexample to the CR positive mass theorem, which are known as Rossi spheres
S_{s}^{3}
.
In
[
CMY
]
it is proved that the pseudohermitian mass of their blow-up structure satisfies
m_{s}=-18\pi s^{2}+o(s^{2})
, and fine estimates for the associated CR Yamabe functional.
By making use of these results, we are able to study the perturbed functional restricted to an appropriate region of the manifold of bubbles and to build a CR structure with a blowing up sequence of solutions to the CR Yamabe problem.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we outline the basic definitions in CR geometry that we will need.
For an introduction to CR geometry we refer to
[
DT
]
.

A CR structure on a three-dimensional manifold
M
is a subbundle
\mathscr{H}
of
TM\otimes\mathbf{C}
such that
\mathscr{H}\cap\overline{\mathscr{H}}=0
.
The Levi distribution is
H(M)=\mathfrak{Re}(\mathscr{H}\oplus\overline{\mathscr{H}})
, and it carries the natural complex structure defined by
J(Z+\overline{Z})=i(Z-\overline{Z})
.
If
H(M)
is fixed, a CR structure can be equivalently defined as an complex structure
J
on it. This is the approach we will use to build the desired counterexample.

On an orientable CR manifold a contact form
\theta
such that
\left.\theta\right|_{H(M)}=0
can be chosen.
Since in this article such a form will be considered fixed and the main concern is the CR structure, we will generally omit the dependence of the various geometric objects by
\theta
.

The choice of a contact form on a CR manifold provides a rich geometry, in particular it allows to define a subriemannian metric
G_{\theta}
on
H(M)
and a connection
\nabla
called the Tanaka-Webster connection.
Contracting twice the curvature tensor of such connection with
G_{\theta}
allows to define a scalar invariant known as Webster curvature which is analogous to the scalar curvature in Riemannian geometry.

The contact form gives rise to the volume form
\theta\wedge d\theta
, which in this article will be mostly omitted in the integrals. Composing the divergence with respect to such volume form with the subriemannian gradient a second order subelliptic differential operator
\Delta_{J}
called the sublaplacian is defined.

Any other contact form for a given CR structure is of the form
u^{2}\theta
with
u\neq 0
everywhere.
The Webster curvature with respect to the contact form
\widetilde{\theta}=u^{2}\theta
is given by the formula

\widetilde{R}=u^{-3}(-4\Delta_{J}+R)u

and therefore, calling
L_{J}=-4\Delta_{J}+R
the conformal sublaplacian, the equation for the prescribed Webster curvature is

L_{J}u=2u^{3}.

(1)

The most important three-dimensional CR structure, playing the same role as
\mathbf{R}^{n}
in the category of Riemannian manifolds, is the Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{1}
.

It is defined as
\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{R}
with the group law
(z,t)\cdot(w,s)=(z+w,t+s+2\mathfrak{Im}(z\cdot\overline{w}))
, and it carries the left invariant CR structure generated by the left-invariant vector field

Z=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+i\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

and the contact structure

\theta=dt+i\left(zd\overline{z}-\overline{z}dz\right).

We call
J_{0}
the associated complex structure on
H(\mathbf{H}^{1})
.

\mathbf{H}^{1}
admits a family of CR and group automorphisms analogous to the dilations of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, defined by
\delta_{\lambda}(z,t)=(\lambda z,\lambda^{2}t)
, and called Heisenberg dilations.
The Korányi norm is defined as
|(z,t)|=\left(|z|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}
, and satisfies
|\delta_{\lambda}x|=\lambda|x|
.

The solutions to the CR Yamabe equation (
1
) on
\mathbf{H}^{1}
are

U(z,t)=\frac{c_{1}}{\left(t^{2}+(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

and the translations and dilations thereof,
u_{x,\lambda}=\lambda U\circ\delta_{\lambda}\circ L_{x}
(where
L_{x}(y)=x^{-1}y
).
We will use also the vector field
T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
(the Reeb vector field) and the generator of the dilations

\Xi=\left(zZ+\overline{z}Z\right)+2tT.

Let
X
be the Hilbert space

X=\{u\in L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbf{H}^{1})|\left\lVert\nabla_{J_{0}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\infty\}

with the scalar product

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{X}=\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}\nabla_{J_{0}}u\cdot\nabla_{J_{0}}v.

By standard functional analysis,
\Delta_{J_{0}}
is an isomorphism between
X
and the dual thereof
X^{\prime}
.

By the Sobolev inequality in the Heisenberg group

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X}
.

We also define the Heisenberg analogous of the
C^{2}
norm,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\Gamma^{2}}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert Zu\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\overline{Z}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert Z^{2}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\overline{Z}^{2}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert Z\overline{Z}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\overline{Z}Zu\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

2.1
Rossi spheres

The standard CR structure on
S^{2}
induced by the immersion in
\mathbf{C}^{2}
is generated by

W=\overline{w_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{1}}-\overline{w_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{2}}.

Then the Rossi sphere are defined as
S^{2}
with the contact form generated by
W+s\overline{W}
.

We recall that the Kelvin transform is defined as the function
F:S^{3}\setminus(0,-1)\to\mathbf{H}^{1}
given by

F(w_{1},w_{2})=\left(\frac{w_{1}}{1+w_{2}},\mathfrak{Re}\left(i\frac{1-w_{2}}{1+w_{2}}\right)\right)

whose inverse is

F^{-1}(z,t)=\left(\frac{2iz}{t+i(1+|z|^{2})},\frac{-t+i(1-|z|^{2})}{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}\right).

It is known that the push-forward through
F
of the standard CR structure on
S^{2}
is the Heisenberg CR structure. We want to compute the push-forward of the Rossi CR structure.

We have

dF\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{1}}\right)=\frac{1}{1+w_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{-t+i(1-|z|^{2})}{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=\frac{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}{2i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

and

dF\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{2}}\right)=-w_{1}\frac{1}{(1+w_{2})^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{2}i\frac{-(1+w_{2})-(1-w_{2})}{(1+w_{2})^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=-w_{1}\frac{1}{(1+w_{2})^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-i\frac{1}{(1+w_{2})^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=

=-\frac{2iz}{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}}{-4}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-i\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}}{-4}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=

=\frac{iz}{2}(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{i}{4}(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

and so

dF(W)=\frac{-t-i(1-|z|^{2})}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}dF\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{1}}\right)-\frac{-2i\overline{z}}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}dF\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{2}}\right)=

=\frac{-t-i(1-|z|^{2})}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}\frac{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}{2i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{2i\overline{z}}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}\left(\frac{iz}{2}(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{i}{4}(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)=

=\frac{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}\frac{i}{2}\left(t+i(1-|z|^{2})+2i|z|^{2}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{z}\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=

=\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}\frac{i}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{z}\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=

=\frac{i}{2}\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{2}}{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}Z=\frac{i}{2}\frac{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{3}}{t^{2}+(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}Z.

Therefore the push-forward of the Rossi sphere CR structure through
F
is generated by the vector field

Z^{s}=Z^{s}_{1}=Z+s\frac{(t-i(1+|z|^{2}))^{3}}{(t+i(1+|z|^{2}))^{3}}\overline{Z}=Z+s\varphi(z,t)\overline{Z}

where

\varphi(z,t)=\left(\frac{t-i(1+|z|^{2})}{t+i(1+|z|^{2})}\right)^{3}.

3
Lyapunov-Schmidt method

Let us define the four-dimensional submanifold of
X

\mathscr{M}=\left\{U_{x,\lambda}\;\middle|\;x\in\mathbf{H}^{1},\lambda\in(0,\infty)\right\}

and
\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}
the orthogonal to the tangent thereof at the point
U_{x,\lambda}
, that is

\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}=\operatorname{span}(Z_{1}U_{x,\lambda},Z_{\overline{1}}U_{x,\lambda},TU_{x,\lambda},\Xi U_{x,\lambda})^{\perp}=

=\left\{u\in X\;\middle|\;\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}\nabla_{J_{0}}u\nabla_{J_{0}}(Z_{1}U_{x,\lambda})=\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}\nabla_{J_{0}}u\nabla_{J_{0}}(Z_{\overline{1}}U_{x,\lambda})=\right.

\left.=\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}\nabla_{J_{0}}u\nabla_{J_{0}}(TU_{x,\lambda})=\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}\nabla_{J_{0}}u\nabla_{J_{0}}(\Xi U_{x,\lambda})=0\right\}

We notice that the CR Yamabe equation for
J
is the Euler-Lagrange equation for the functional

\mathscr{J}_{J}(u)=\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}uL_{J}u-\int_{\mathbf{H}^{1}}u^{4}

in the space
X
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
\alpha
such that if
J
is a CR structure on
\mathbf{H}^{1}
coinciding with
J_{0}
on
\mathbf{H}^{1}\setminus B_{1}(0)
and such that
\left\lVert J-J_{0}\right\rVert_{\Gamma^{2}}\leq\alpha
then for every
(x,\lambda)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}\times(0,\infty)
the operator
d^{2}\mathscr{J}_{J}(U_{x,\lambda})
is invertible on
\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}
, and the norms of
d^{2}\mathscr{J}_{J}(U_{x,\lambda})
and
\left(\left.d^{2}\mathscr{J}_{J}(U_{x,\lambda})\right|_{\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}}\right)^{-1}
are bounded independently by
x
,
\lambda
and
J
.

The proof of the above Proposition is standard in the Riemannian case (see
[
B
]
); in this case the proof is the same except that Lemma 5 from
[
MU
]
has to be used instead of the analogous result for
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, and the estimates in Propositions
A.1
and
A.2
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
\alpha
such that if
J
is a CR structure on
\mathbf{H}^{1}
coinciding with
J_{0}
on
\mathbf{H}^{1}\setminus B_{1}(0)
and such that
\left\lVert J-J_{0}\right\rVert_{\Gamma^{2}}\leq\alpha
then for every
(x,\lambda)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}\times(0,\infty)
there exists an unique
v_{x.\lambda}\in\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}
with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert\lesssim\alpha
such that

\pi_{\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}}\left(\nabla\mathscr{J}_{J}(U_{x,\lambda}+v_{x,\lambda})\right)=0.

Proof.

The equation to be solved can be written explicitely as

\pi_{\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}}\circ\Delta_{J_{0}}^{-1}\left(L_{J}(U_{x,\lambda}+v_{x,\lambda})-2(U_{x,\lambda}+v_{x,\lambda})^{3}\right)=0,

and therefore the solutions thereof are fixed points of the function

F(v)=(\pi_{\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}}\circ\Delta_{J_{0}}^{-1}\circ(L_{J}-6U_{x,\lambda}^{2}))^{-1}\pi_{\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}}\circ\Delta_{J_{0}}^{-1}\left(-6U_{x,\lambda}v^{2}-2v^{3}+L_{J}U_{x,\lambda}-2U_{x,\lambda}^{3}\right).

Using Proposition
3.1
, the fact that the adjoint of the immersion
X\to L^{4}
is
L^{\frac{4}{3}}\to X^{\prime}
, and Propositions
A.1
and
A.2
, for any
v\in\mathscr{E}_{x,\lambda}

\left\lVert F(v)\right\rVert_{X}\lesssim\left\lVert-6U_{x,\lambda}v^{2}-2v^{3}+L_{J}U_{x,\lambda}-2U_{x,\lambda}^{3}\right\rVert_{X^{\prime}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert-6U_{x,\lambda}v^{2}-2v^{3}+L_{J}U_{x,\lambda}-2U_{x,\lambda}^{3}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim\left\lVert U_{x,\lambda}v^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\left\lVert v^{3}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\left\lVert L_{J}U_{x,\lambda}-2U_{x,\lambda}^{3}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert U_{x,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\left\lVert v^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert v^{3}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\left\lVert L_{J}U_{x,\lambda}-L_{J_{0}}U_{x,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{3}+\left\lVert(\Delta_{J}-\Delta_{J_{0}})U_{x,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\left\lVert(R_{J}-R_{J_{0}})U_{x,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}^{3}+\alpha\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(\lambda\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda|x|)^{-4}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}+\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(\lambda\lambda(1+\lambda|x|)^{-3}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}+

+\left\lVert(R_{J}-R_{J_{0}})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert U_{x,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}^{3}+\alpha\lambda^{3}\left(\lambda^{-4}\int_{B_{\lambda}(0)}\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\frac{8}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}+\alpha\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{-4}\int_{B_{\lambda}(0)}(1+|x|)^{-4}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}+\alpha\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}^{3}+\alpha.

Therefore a ball of appropriate radius
\lesssim\alpha
is invariant for
F
.

On such a ball, with similar computations,

\left\lVert F(v)-F(w)\right\rVert_{X}\lesssim\left\lVert U_{x,\lambda}(v^{2}-w^{2})\right\rVert_{X^{\prime}}+\left\lVert(v^{3}-w^{3})\right\rVert_{X^{\prime}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert(v^{2}-w^{2})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert(v^{3}-w^{3})\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim

\lesssim\left\lVert v+w\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\left\lVert v-w\right\rVert_{L^{4}}+\left\lVert v^{2}+vw+w^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v-w\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\lesssim

\lesssim\alpha\left\lVert v-w\right\rVert_{X}+\alpha^{2}\left\lVert v-w\right\rVert_{X}

therefore
F
is a contraction thereon up to choosing
\alpha
small enough.
∎

Proposition 3.3

If

4
Construction of CR structures with large solutions

Let
x_{n}=(\frac{1}{n},0,0)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}
and let

sequences converging to zero and such that the balls

be a CR structure coinciding with

on

-\frac{1}{1-|f|^{2}}|\overline{Z}f|^{2}-\frac{\overline{f}^{2}}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}Zf\overline{Z}f-\frac{\overline{f}}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}(\overline{Z}f)^{2}-\frac{3-|f|^{2}}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}f\overline{Z}f\overline{Z}\overline{f}+

-\frac{f}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}(Z\overline{f})^{2}-\frac{1}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}\left(-|f|^{2}+3\right)\overline{f}ZfZ\overline{f}-\frac{1}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}\overline{f}^{3}(Zf)^{2}+

-\frac{1}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}\left|Z(|f|^{2})\right|^{2}+

-\frac{f^{2}}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}Z\overline{f}\overline{Z}\overline{f}-\frac{2|f|^{2}-|f|^{4}}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}|Zf|^{2}-\frac{f^{3}}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}(\overline{Z}\overline{f})^{2}+

-\frac{1}{(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}\left|Z\overline{f}+\overline{f}^{2}Zf+\overline{Z}(|f|^{2})\right|^{2}.

From this follows the thesis.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

There exist a constant
C
such that

\left|(\Delta_{\widetilde{J}}-\Delta_{J_{0}})u-\frac{1}{2}\left(f\overline{Z}^{2}u+\overline{f}Z^{2}u\right)-\frac{3}{4}\left(Z\overline{f}Zu+\overline{Z}f\overline{Z}u\right)\right|\lesssim

\leq C\left(|f|^{2}\Delta_{J_{0}}u+|f|^{3}|\nabla^{2}u|+|f|^{2}|\nabla f||\nabla u|\right).

Proof.

Using the notation of the proof of the former Proposition,

\Delta_{b}u=h^{1\overline{1}}(\widetilde{Z}\overline{\widetilde{Z}}+\overline{\widetilde{Z}}\widetilde{Z}-\omega_{1}^{1}(\overline{\widetilde{Z}})\widetilde{Z}u-\omega_{\overline{1}}^{\overline{1}}(\widetilde{Z})\overline{\widetilde{Z}}u)=

=h^{1\overline{1}}(Z+f\overline{Z})(\overline{Z}+h^{1\overline{1}}\overline{f}Z)u+(\overline{Z}+\overline{f}Z)(Z+f\overline{Z})u+

+h^{1\overline{1}}\frac{1}{1-|f|^{2}}\left(Z\overline{f}+\overline{f}^{2}Zf+\overline{Z}(|f|^{2})\right)(Z+f\overline{Z})u+

+h^{1\overline{1}}\frac{1}{1-|f|^{2}}\left(\overline{Z}f+f^{2}\overline{Z}\overline{f}+Z(|f|^{2})\right)(\overline{Z}+\overline{f}Z)u=

=h^{1\overline{1}}(Z\overline{Z}u+f\overline{Z}^{2}u+Z\overline{f}Zu+|f|^{2}\overline{Z}Zu)+

+h^{1\overline{1}}(\overline{Z}Zu+\overline{f}Z^{2}u+\overline{Z}f\overline{Z}u+|f|^{2}Z\overline{Z}u)+

+h^{1\overline{1}}\frac{1}{1-|f|^{2}}\left(Z\overline{f}+\overline{f}^{2}Zf+\overline{Z}(|f|^{2})\right)(Z+f\overline{Z})u+

+h^{1\overline{1}}\frac{1}{1-|f|^{2}}\left(\overline{Z}f+f^{2}\overline{Z}\overline{f}+Z(|f|^{2})\right)(\overline{Z}+\overline{f}Z)u=

=\frac{1}{1-|f|^{2}}((1+|f|^{2})\Delta_{J_{0}}u+f\overline{Z}^{2}u+\overline{f}Z^{2}u+Z\overline{f}Zu+\overline{Z}f\overline{Z}u)+

+\frac{1}{2(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}\left(Z\overline{f}+2\overline{f}^{2}Zf+2\overline{f}\overline{Z}f+f\overline{Z}\overline{f}+f|f|^{2}\overline{Z}\overline{f}+|f|^{2}Z\overline{f}\right)Zu+

+\frac{1}{2(1-|f|^{2})^{2}}\left(\overline{Z}f+2f^{2}\overline{Z}\overline{f}+2fZ\overline{f}+\overline{f}Zf+\overline{f}|f|^{2}Zf+|f|^{2}\overline{Z}f\right)\overline{Z}u.

From this the thesis follows.

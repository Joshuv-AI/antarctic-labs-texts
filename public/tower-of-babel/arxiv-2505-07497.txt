Generalized upper and lower Legendre conjugates for weight functions Thanks: This research was funded in whole or in part by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) 10.55776/PAT9445424
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We introduce and study new transformations between two functions satisfying some basic growth properties and generalize the known lower and upper Legendre conjugate (or envelope). We also investigate how these transformations modify recently defined growth indices for weight functions. A special but important and useful situation, to which the knowledge is then applied, is when considering associated weight functions which are expressed in terms of an underlying weight sequence. In this case these transformations precisely correspond to the point-wise product resp. point-wise division of the given sequences. Therefore, the new approach studied in this work illustrates the genuineness and importance and suggests applications for weighted spaces in different directions.

From an abstract point of view but also for concrete applications and when working with weighted structures one is interested in the following questions:

(I)

Let sequences
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
be given and write
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}:=(M_{p}N_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}:=\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
for the
point-wise product resp. point-wise quotient sequence.
How is then
\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}
,
\omega_{\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}}
related to
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
,
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
?

(II)

Study for more general resp. for a wider class of weight functions these analogous relations and transformations; more precisely one may ask:

Given functions
\sigma,\tau:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
, which function is then corresponding to the operation yielding
\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}
resp.
\omega_{\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}}
?

The content of this work is to answer and study
(I)
and
(II)
in detail: It turns out that taking the point-wise product is corresponding to

(1.1)

\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t):=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau(t/s)\},\;\;\;t\in[0,+\infty),

whereas the point-wise quotient is corresponding to

(1.2)

\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{\sigma(s)-\tau(s/t)\},\;\;\;t\in(0,+\infty).

The choice
\tau=\operatorname{id}
in (
1.1
) gives, up to inversion of the variable, the known
lower Legendre conjugate (or envelope) of
\sigma
and (
1.2
) for
\tau=\operatorname{id}
gives the
upper Legendre conjugate (or envelope) of
\sigma
;
see e.g.
[

, Sect. 2.5]
and the references therein and confirm formulas (
3.3
) and (
4.9

\check{\star}
and
\widehat{\star}

2.
Weight sequences, weight functions and associated functions

2.1.
Basic notation

We write
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{>0}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
,
\mathbb{R}_{>0}:=(0,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:=[0,+\infty)
. Occasionally, when treating non-standard situations we are using the conventions
0^{0}:=1
,
\frac{0}{0}:=0
,
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
,
+\infty-(+\infty)=0
,
0\cdot(-\infty):=0=:0\cdot(+\infty)
and
p\cdot(-\infty):=-\infty
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

2.2.
On weight sequences

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers. Moreover, let us set
\mu_{p}:=\frac{M_{p}}{M_{p-1}}
for
p\geq 1
and
\mu_{0}:=1
. Hence
\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{p}\cdots\mu_{1}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
; the case
p=0
gives the empty product.
\mathbf{M}
is called
normalized
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
.

Given
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}
, write
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
for the sequence obtained by
(M_{p}\cdot N_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
(point-wise product) and
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
for
\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
(point-wise quotient). The corresponding sequences of quotients are denoted and given by
\mu\cdot\nu
and
\frac{\mu}{\nu}
.

\mathbf{M}
is
log-convex
if

(2.1)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;M_{p}^{2}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1},

in the literature also denoted by
(M.1)
; see e.g
[
Komatsu73
]
.

For concrete applications and examples it is convenient to introduce the following class of sequences:

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;\mathbf{M}\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty\}.

Note:
For each
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
we have
\mu_{0}=1\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dots
and
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
. In particular,
\mathbf{M}
is non-decreasing and in fact there exists a one-to-one correspondence between sequences
\mathbf{M}
belonging to the set
\mathcal{LC}
and sequences
\mu_{0}=1\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dots
with
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
via setting
M_{p}:=\prod_{i=0}^{p}\mu_{i}
.

Next let us introduce relations between given sequences
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
:

(*)

Write
\mathbf{M}\leq\mathbf{N}
if
M_{p}\leq N_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

(*)

\mathbf{M}\hypertarget{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
if

\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty;

i.e. if
M_{p}\leq C^{p+1}N_{p}
for some
C\geq 1
and all
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

(*)

\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
are called
equivalent
if
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{M}
; i.e. if

(2.2)

0<\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty,

equivalently

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\frac{1}{C^{p+1}}N_{p}\leq M_{p}\leq C^{p+1}N_{p}.

(*)

Write
\mathbf{M}\hypertarget{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
if
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}=0
; i.e. if

(2.3)

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;C_{h}\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p}\leq C_{h}h^{p}N_{p}.

Obviously,
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
implies
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
, but
\vartriangleleft
neither is reflexive nor symmetric.

Remark 2.1
.

\preceq
and
\vartriangleleft
are crucial for the inclusion relations of the corresponding weighted spaces and, in particular, equivalent sequences yield the same weighted class. Therefore, in concrete applications it is no restriction to assume always
M_{0}=1
since otherwise
\mathbf{M}
can be replaced by
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
with
\widetilde{M}_{p}:=M_{p}/M_{0}
. Similarly, for each
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
one can find an equivalent sequence
\mathbf{N}
which is even normalized.

Finally, for any given log-convex sequence
\mathbf{M}
satisfying
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
there exists an equivalent sequence
\mathbf{N}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
; see
[
weightedentireinclusion1
, Rem. 2.5]
and
[
regularnew
, Prop. 3.5]
.

A prominent example are the
Gevrey sequences

\mathbf{G}^{s}:=(p!^{s})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
,
s>0
. Note that
\mathbf{G}^{s}
is equivalent to
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{s}
with
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{s}:=(p^{ps})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
by
Stirling’s formula.

We revisit some notions from
[
regularnew
, Sect. 2.5]
. Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
, then put

(2.4)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}:=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\right)^{1/p}=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p},

and

(2.5)

\mathbf{M}_{\inf}:=\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\right)^{1/p}.

Consequently:

(a)

\mathbf{M}_{\inf}\leq\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
,
(\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N})_{\inf}\geq\mathbf{M}_{\inf}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\inf}
and
(\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N})_{\iota}\geq\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
.

(b)

If
\mathbf{M}
is also log-convex then, as mentioned in
[
regularnew
, Sect.2.2]
, we get that
p\mapsto\left(\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\right)^{1/p}
is non-decreasing and

\mathbf{M}_{\inf}=\frac{M_{1}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{1},\hskip 15.0pt\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\right)^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty].

(c)

Note that
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
for a log-convex sequence corresponds to the “extreme (limiting) case”
M_{0}\geq 0
and
M_{p}=0
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
studied in
[
regularnew
, Sect. 4.1]
. However, formally this violates the (basic) assumption
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let now
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and remark that

(2.6)

0\leq\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\limsup_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}\leq+\infty,

and

0<\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}\Longleftrightarrow 0<\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p},

\limsup_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty\Longleftrightarrow\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty.

Next introduce the values

C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}:=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p},\hskip 15.0pt\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}:=\limsup_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}/M_{0}}{N_{p}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p},

and so

C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}=\inf\{C>0:\;\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\leq C^{p}\frac{N_{p}}{N_{0}},\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}\}.

We summarize:

(*)

C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\in(0,+\infty]
whereas
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\in[0,+\infty]
holds.

(*)

\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}=0
if and only if
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
.

(*)

\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
if and only if
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}<+\infty
resp. if and only if
C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}<+\infty
. Indeed,
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
amounts to have

(2.7)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\leq C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}^{p}\frac{N_{p}}{N_{0}},

and
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
is equivalent to

(2.8)

\forall\;h>\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\;\exists\;C_{h}>0\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p}\leq C_{h}h^{p}N_{p},

which should be compared with (
2.3
).

(*)

When
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\in(0,+\infty)
, i.e.
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
holds but
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{vartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
fails, then there exists some
D\geq 1
such that
C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\leq D\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}
. (This estimate is “meaningless/trivial” if
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}=+\infty=C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}
.)

(*)

When
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\in(0,+\infty)
, then by definition

(2.9)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\leq\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\mathbf{N}_{\iota}.

In general equality in (
2.9
) fails resp. is unclear: There might even exist some subsequence of integers
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p_{k}}/M_{0}}{N_{p_{k}}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p_{k}}<\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}
and such that
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p_{k}}}{M_{0}}\right)^{1/p_{k}}
.

(*)

Concerning the “extreme case”
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}=0
, i.e. if
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{vartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
, we remark: When
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]
, then necessarily
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
is valid.

Related to these observations and in view of the quotient sequence
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
we comment on the behavior of
p\mapsto\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{p}
appearing in
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
.

(i)

Even if both
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
are log-convex, then in general
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}

will not be log-convex anymore:
Consider, e.g.,
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{G}^{s_{1}}
,
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{G}^{s_{2}}
, with
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
, and so
\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}=p!^{s_{1}-s_{2}}
. The corresponding quotient sequence is given by
p^{s_{1}-s_{2}}
which is even decreasing and tending to
0
as
p\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
is
log-concave
which means that the estimate in (
2.1
) is reversed. On the other hand, clearly
\frac{\mathbf{G}^{s_{2}}}{\mathbf{G}^{s_{1}}}=\mathbf{G}^{s_{2}-s_{1}}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
.

(ii)

Let
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
be log-convex.

Then
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]
,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p}/N_{0})^{1/p}=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]
and concerning
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}
we have to distinguish:

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota},\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}=\frac{\mathbf{M}_{\iota}}{\mathbf{N}_{\iota}}\in(0,+\infty)
. Indeed, in this case
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
are equivalent.

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}=+\infty
. In this case
\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}
holds.

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
, then
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}=0
and
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
is valid.

(*)

The case
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
is not clear; here
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}\in[0,+\infty]
is possible and for the extreme cases see
(i)
before. But even the existence of
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}/N_{p}}{M_{0}/N_{0}}\right)^{1/p}
is not ensured: Indeed, it is not difficult to construct log-convex sequences
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
(with
M_{0}=1=N_{0}
) satisfying the “strongest possible oscillation”

(2.10)

0=\inf_{p\geq 1}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}<\sup_{p\geq 1}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}=+\infty.

So neither
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
nor
\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{M}
is valid, i.e.
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
are
not comparable,

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}
does not exist and
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}=0
.

For an explicit but involved example we refer to
[
GelfandShilovincl
, Sect. 3]
: From given
\mathbf{N}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
one can construct
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
such that (
2.10
) holds. (In
[
GelfandShilovincl
]
this question is related to the problem of non-triviality of Gelfand-Shilov-type spaces and so both
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
are required to have some more specific regularity properties.)

(iii)

Note that the growth of
p\mapsto\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}
is crucial for the inclusion relation of the corresponding weighted classes expressed in terms of
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
; concerning the ultradifferentiable setting we refer e.g. to
[
compositionpaper
, Prop. 2.12 & 4.6]
.

2.3.
Weight functions

We start by recalling the following definition; see
[
index
, Sect. 2.3 & 2.4]
.

Definition 2.2
.

\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called a
weight function
if

(*)

\omega
is non-decreasing and

(*)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
.

These growth requirements are sufficient in order to introduce and work with the crucial indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
; see Section
2.4
for details.

Let
\sigma,\tau:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be weight functions.

(*)

Write
\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
if

(2.11)

\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty.

(*)

\sigma
and
\tau
are called
equivalent,
denoted by
\sigma\hypertarget{sim}{\sim}\tau
, if
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma
.

(*)

Write
\sigma\hypertarget{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
if

(2.12)

\tau(t)=o(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty,

and note that
\vartriangleleft
neither is reflexive nor symmetric.

We are going to use the following notation for any (weight) function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
and arbitrary
\alpha>0
: set
\omega^{\iota}(t):=\omega(\frac{1}{t})
,
t>0
, and
\omega^{1/\alpha}(t):=\omega(t^{1/\alpha})
,
t\geq 0
. Note that
\omega^{1/\alpha}
is obtained by a power-substitution and it is again a weight function. Moreover,
(\omega^{\iota})^{1/\alpha}(t)=(\omega^{1/\alpha})^{\iota}(t)
for all
t>0
. Finally, let us write
\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}
for
t\mapsto t^{1/\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
arbitrary.

2.4.
The growth indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)

We briefly recall the definitions of the growth indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
; see
[
index
, Sect. 2.3
(6)
, Sect. 2.4
(7)
]
and the references in these sections. Let
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a weight function and
\gamma>0
. We say that
\omega
has property
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
if

(2.13)

\exists\;K>1:\;\;\;\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(K^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}<K.

If
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
holds for some
K>1
, then
(P_{\omega,\gamma^{\prime}})
is satisfied for all
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma
with the same
K
since
\omega
is non-decreasing. Moreover we can restrict to
\gamma>0
, because for
\gamma\leq 0
condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
is satisfied for any weight function
\omega
, again because
\omega
is non-decreasing and
K>1
. Then put

(2.14)

\gamma(\omega):=\sup\{\gamma>0:\;\;(P_{\omega,\gamma})\;\;\text{is satisfied}\},

and if none condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
(with
\gamma>0
) holds then set
\gamma(\omega):=0
.

Analogously, for
\gamma>0
we say that
\omega
has property
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
if

(2.15)

\exists\;A>1:\;\;\;\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(A^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}>A.

If
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
holds for some
A>1
, then
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma^{\prime}})
is satisfied for all
\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma
with the same
A
since
\omega
is non-decreasing. Moreover, we can restrict to
\gamma>0
because for
\gamma\leq 0
condition
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
is never satisfied for any weight function:
\omega
is assumed to be non-decreasing and
A>1
. Then set

(2.16)

\overline{\gamma}(\omega):=\inf\{\gamma>0:\;\;(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})\;\;\text{is satisfied}\}.

We obtain for any weight function
(0\leq)\gamma(\omega)\leq\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
; see again
[
index
, Sect. 2.3 & 2.4]
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function and consider the following two growth conditions appearing for weight functions in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor, see e.g.
[
BraunMeiseTaylor90
]
and
[
BonetMeiseMelikhov07
]
, and the following abbreviations have already been used in
[
dissertation
]
:

(\omega_{1})

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
; i.e.
\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(2t)\leq L\omega(t)+L
.

(\omega_{6})

\exists\;H\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;2\omega(Ht)\leq\omega(Ht)+H
.

\omega
has
(\omega_{1})
if and only if
\gamma(\omega)>0
, see
[
index
, Thm. 2.11, Rem. 2.12, Cor. 2.14]
, and
(\omega_{6})
holds if and only if
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)<+\infty
; see
[
index
, Thm. 2.16,
(7)
, Cor. 2.17]
.

Remark 2.4
.

For any
\alpha>0
we consider the weight function
\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}
and obtain

\gamma(\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha})=\overline{\gamma}(\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha})=\alpha.

Next we verify the following useful consequence.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function.

(i)

If (
2.13
) (i.e.
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
) is valid for some
K>1
, then for all
K^{\prime}\geq K
, too.

(ii)

If (
2.15
) (i.e.
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
) is valid for some
A>1
, then for all
A^{\prime}\geq A
, too.

Proof.

(i)
Note that the case
K^{\prime}=K^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, follows immediately by iteration; see also the proof of
[
index
, Cor. 2.14]
:

\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(K^{n\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}=\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\omega(K^{i\gamma}t)}{\omega(K^{(i-1)\gamma}t)}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(K^{i\gamma}t)}{\omega(K^{(i-1)\gamma}t)}<K^{n}.

In any case, by
[
index
, Thm. 2.11
(iii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
]
and taking in
(iii)
there
\alpha:=\gamma^{-1}
we have that
\omega
satisfies
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
if and only if
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\epsilon^{1/\gamma}\omega(t)}{\omega(\epsilon t)}=0
. Set
\eta:=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1/\gamma}}
and
s:=\epsilon t=\frac{t}{\eta^{\gamma}}
and hence the previous condition is equivalent to
\lim_{\eta\rightarrow+\infty}\limsup_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(\eta^{\gamma}s)}{\eta\omega(s)}=0
. Thus we can find some
\eta_{0}>1
such that for all
\eta\geq\eta_{0}
one has
\limsup_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(\eta^{\gamma}s)}{\eta\omega(s)}<1
and this verifies, in particular,
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
for each choice
K:=\eta\geq\eta_{0}
.

(ii)
Again, the case
A^{\prime}:=A^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, follows immediately by iteration:

\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(A^{n\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}=\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\omega(A^{i\gamma}t)}{\omega(A^{(i-1)\gamma}t)}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(A^{i\gamma}t)}{\omega(A^{(i-1)\gamma}t)}>A^{n}.

Similarly, by
[
index
, Thm. 2.16
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
]
and taking in
(ii)
there
\beta:=\gamma^{-1}
we have that
\omega
satisfies
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
if and only if
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\omega(kt)}{k^{1/\gamma}\omega(t)}=+\infty
. Take
\ell:=k^{1/\gamma}
and hence the previous condition is equivalent to
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(\ell^{\gamma}t)}{\ell\omega(t)}=+\infty
. Thus one can find some
\ell_{0}>1
such that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
we get
\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(\ell^{\gamma}t)}{\ell\omega(t)}>1
and this verifies
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
for each choice
A:=\ell\geq\ell_{0}
.
∎

2.5.
Associated function

For the following definition and the connection to the geometric construction of the
log-convex minorant
we refer to
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I]
and to the more recent work
[
regularnew
]
. Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given, then the
associated function

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\cup\{+\infty\}
is given as follows:

(2.17)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right),\qquad t\geq 0,

with the convention that
0^{0}:=1
. This ensures
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)\geq 0
for any
t\geq 0
since
\frac{t^{0}M_{0}}{M_{0}}=1
for all
t\geq 0
. (
2.17
) corresponds to
[
Komatsu73
,
(3.1)
]
and we immediately have that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is non-decreasing and satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty
.

Then recall
[
regularnew
, Lemma 2.2]
:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given.

(i)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
implies

\forall\;0\leq t\leq\mathbf{M}_{\inf}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0.

(ii)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
implies

\forall\;t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty.

(iii)

If
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
, then
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)<+\infty
for all
t\geq 0
.

Note:

(ii)
and
(iii)
together yield that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
is equivalent to
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)<+\infty
for any
t\geq 0
. In this case
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is a weight function according to Definition
2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex, hence
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]
, and we recall from
[
regularnew
]
(and
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I]
):

(a)

By the geometric approach presented and revisited in
[
regularnew
, Sect. 3 & 4]
, in particular when involving the relation with the
trace function
in
[
regularnew
,
(3.11)
]
, we know that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)<+\infty
for all
t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
.

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then
\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{\iota}}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)
is equal to the negative
y
-coordinate of the unique intersection point of the
y
-axis with a straight line being parallel to
t\mapsto t\cdot\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
; see
[
regularnew
, Sect. 4.2, Ex. 4.3, Sect. 5.8]
.

(b)

When
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then view of
(ii)
in Lemma
2.6
put
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty
for all
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
; see also
[
regularnew
, Sect. 4.2]
.

(c)

Next, let us recall that by
[
regularnew
, Sect. 3 & 4]
we have

(2.18)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p}=M_{0}\sup_{t\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))};

see also
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I, 1.4, 1.8]
and
[
Komatsu73
, Prop. 3.2]
. If
\mathbf{M}
is not log-convex, then (
2.18
) yields
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}
with
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}=(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
denoting the
log-convex minorant
of
\mathbf{M}
. Moreover, even if
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
by taking into account comment
(b)
and using the convention
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
, we can consider in (
2.18
) all
t\in(0,+\infty)
.

(d)

Finally, in
[
regularnew
, Sect. 4.1]
we have given (
2.18
) a meaning even if
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
: Here put

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0,\hskip 15.0pt\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty,\;\;\;\forall\;t>0.

Note that for any log-convex
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
we cannot have
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
and when
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
, then
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=0
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
which contradicts
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

For concrete applications, when one is (only) interested in the associated function w.l.o.g. one can assume that the defining sequence is log-convex; this holds since the geometric procedure gives, in particular, that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}=\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}}
.

Now, let us recall
[
regularnew
, Lemmas 2.4 & 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex and assume that
(\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=)\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
. Then

(2.19)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0,\;\text{for}\;t\in[0,\mu_{1}],\;\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)\;\text{for}\;t\in[\mu_{p},\mu_{p+1}],\;p\geq 1,

and when
(\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=)\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=:C<+\infty
, then (
2.19
) holds for all
t\in[0,C)
.

Note:
When taking into account (
2.19
) we see that for any log-convex
\mathbf{M}
satisfying
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
the function
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is a weight function in the sense of Definition
2.2
. In any case, Lemma
2.7
implies

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(\mathbf{M}_{\iota})=\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{\iota}}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t).

Remark 2.8
.

If
\mathbf{M}
is log-convex with
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=C<+\infty
, then there can exist
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\mu_{p}=C
for all
p\geq p_{0}
. In this case we say that the sequence of quotients eventually stabilizes and then
[\mu_{p},C)=\emptyset
for all
p\geq p_{0}
. However, (
2.19
) is again valid for all
t\in[0,\mu_{p_{0}})=[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
and

\forall\;p\geq p_{0}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(\mathbf{M}_{\iota})=\log\left(\frac{M_{0}\mu_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}}{M_{p_{0}}}\right)=\log\left(\frac{M_{0}\mu_{p}^{p}}{M_{p}}\right).

Example 2.9
.

For
s>0
consider the
Gevrey sequence
(of index
s
) defined by
\mathbf{G}^{s}=(p!^{s})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
and the corresponding quotients are given by
p^{s}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. We involve Lemma
2.7
and verify the known relation
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\operatorname{id}^{1/s}
(which immediately gives
\gamma(\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}})=\overline{\gamma}(\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}})=s
by Remark
2.4
).

First,
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}}(t)=0
for all
0\leq t\leq 1
and when
p^{s}\leq t\leq(p+1)^{s}
for some
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, then
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{t^{p}}{p!^{s}}\right)
which gives

\log\left(\frac{p^{sp}}{p!^{s}}\right)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}}(t)\leq\log\left(\frac{(p+1)^{sp}}{p!^{s}}\right).

Moreover,
\log\left(\frac{p^{sp}}{p!^{s}}\right)\geq\frac{p+1}{C}\Leftrightarrow p^{sp}\geq p!^{s}\exp(\frac{p+1}{C})
for some
C\geq 1
and all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
by
Stirling’s formula.
Similarly,
\log\left(\frac{(p+1)^{sp}}{p!^{s}}\right)\leq pD\Leftrightarrow(p+1)^{sp}\leq p!^{s}\exp(pD)
for some
D\geq 1
and all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Finally, note that
\frac{p+1}{C}\geq\frac{t^{1/s}}{C}
and
pD\leq t^{1/s}D
.

Let log-convex sequences
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}
be given and assume that
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
. Then
\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}\leq C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}^{p}\frac{N_{p}}{N_{0}}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
with
C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\in(0,+\infty)
, and so
\frac{N_{0}t^{p}}{N_{p}}\leq\frac{M_{0}(tC_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}})^{p}}{M_{p}}
for all
t\geq 0
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
is valid (recall (
2.7
)). By definition this gives

(2.20)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(tC_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}).

Naturally one should restrict in (
2.20
) to all
t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}(\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}})^{-1})
since in this case (
2.9
) ensures
t\in[0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
and hence finiteness for the left-hand side. However, even if either
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
or
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
formally the identity (
2.20
) is valid for any
t\geq 0
. Here, the word ”formally” refers to the fact that it does not matter when the right-hand side is equal to
+\infty
when using the conventions
t<+\infty
for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
and
+\infty=+\infty
; the same convention is used throughout the whole article:

(i)

The situation
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
and
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p})^{1/p}<+\infty
is excluded by relation
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
.

(ii)

If
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
, then
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
follows because
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
and by the above both sides are finite for any
t\geq 0
.

(iii)

If
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
, then necessarily
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
and so
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}=0
. The left-hand side is finite for any
t\geq 0
, whereas the right-hand side yields the value
+\infty
for all
t
satisfying
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}(C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}})^{-1}
. Thus, formally (
2.20
) holds again for all
t\geq 0
.

(iv)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota},\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then for
t>\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
the left-hand side is equal to
+\infty
but by (
2.9
) we get that in this case
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}(\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}})^{-1}\geq\mathbf{M}_{\iota}(C_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}})^{-1}
holds; therefore also the right-hand side is equal to
+\infty
and again (
2.20
) is formally valid for any
t\geq 0
. Note that here
\underline{C}_{\mathbf{M}\preceq\mathbf{N}}\in(0,+\infty)
.

3.
Generalized lower Legendre conjugate

We introduce and study now the first generalized conjugate defined on the set of all weight functions.

3.1.
Preliminaries

Let
\sigma
,
\tau
be given weight functions and put

(3.1)

\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t):=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau(t/s)\},\;\;\;t\in[0,+\infty).

Note:
By
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\tau(s)=+\infty
one can use the convention
\tau(+\infty)=+\infty
and thus consider in the definition all
s\geq 0
since
s=0
is then not effecting the value of
\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)
. We gather some immediate consequences:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma
,
\tau
be weight functions.

(a)

It holds that

(3.2)

\forall\;t,u\in[0,+\infty):\;\;\;\sigma\check{\star}\tau(tu)\leq\min\{\sigma(t)+\tau(u),\sigma(u)+\tau(t)\},

which gives
\tau,\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma\check{\star}\tau
;

(b)

\sigma\check{\star}\tau
is a weight function;

(c)

\check{\star}
is commutative.

Proof.

(a)
Obviously,
\sigma\check{\star}\tau(0)=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau(0)\}=\sigma(0)+\tau(0)(\geq 0)
since
\sigma
is non-decreasing. Thus the estimate is valid if
t=0
or
u=0
since both
\sigma
and
\tau
are non-decreasing. For
t,u>0
take
s=u
or
s=t
in (
3.1
) and hence (
3.2
) is verified.

Set (e.g.)
u=1
in (
3.2
) and since
\sigma,\tau
are weight functions one has
\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)=O(\tau(t))
and
\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
; i.e.
\tau,\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma\check{\star}\tau
.

(b)
By definition
\sigma\check{\star}\tau
is non-decreasing and also
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)=+\infty
since both
\sigma
and
\tau
are weight functions.
(a)
also gives that
0\leq\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)<+\infty
for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

(c)

\sigma\check{\star}\tau(0)=\tau\check{\star}\sigma(0)
holds by definition; see
(a)
. For
t>0
arbitrary and fixed we get with
u:=\frac{t}{s}
that

\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau(t/s)\}=\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma(t/u)+\tau(u)\}=\tau\check{\star}\sigma(t).

Consequently,
\check{\star}
is commutative.
∎

Example 3.2
.

Consider the weight function
\log_{+}(t):=\max\{0,\log(t)\}
for
t>0
and
\log_{+}(0):=0
; i.e.
\log_{+}(t)=0
if
t\in[0,1)
and
\log_{+}(t)=\log(t)
if
t\geq 1
.

We verify
\log_{+}\check{\star}\log_{+}=\log_{+}
and distinguish:

Let
t\in[0,1]
. Then for all
0<u\leq 1
one has
\log_{+}(u)+\log_{+}(t/u)=\log_{+}(t/u)
and
\log_{+}(t/u)=0
for all
u\geq t
. Summarizing, since both summands are non-negative and when choosing (e.g.)
u:=1
we get
\log_{+}\check{\star}\log_{+}(t)=0=\log_{+}(t)
.

Let
t>1
. Note that
u\mapsto\log_{+}(t/u)
is non-increasing on
(0,1]
and so the minimum of
u\mapsto\log_{+}(u)+\log_{+}(t/u)
is equal to
\log_{+}(t)=\log(t)
on
(0,1]
. For all
u\in(1,t)
one has
\log_{+}(u)+\log_{+}(t/u)=\log(u)+\log(t/u)=\log(t)
and finally, for all
u\in[t,+\infty)
, one has
\log_{+}(u)+\log_{+}(t/u)=\log(u)
which is non-decreasing on this interval and so the minimum is attained at
u=t
with the value
\log(t)
. Summarizing, for all values
t
under consideration the infimum in the definition yields the value
\log(t)=\log_{+}(t)
.

A special but important case is the following situation:

(3.3)

\sigma\check{\star}\operatorname{id}(t)=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+t/s\}=\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma(1/u)+tu\}=:(\sigma^{\iota})_{\star}(t),

with
h_{\star}(t):=\inf_{u>0}\{h(u)+tu\}
denoting the known
lower Legendre conjugate (or envelope);
see
[
index
, Sect. 2.5]
for explanations and citations. More generally, for any
\alpha>0
and
t\geq 0
one has

\sigma\check{\star}\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}(t)=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+(t/s)^{1/\alpha}\}=\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma\left(\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)^{\alpha}\right)+t^{1/\alpha}u\}=((\sigma^{\iota})^{\alpha})_{\star}(t^{1/\alpha})=(((\sigma^{\iota})^{\alpha})_{\star})^{1/\alpha}(t);

hence

(3.4)

\forall\;\alpha>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma\check{\star}\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}(t)=(((\sigma^{\iota})^{\alpha})_{\star})^{1/\alpha}(t).

Next we verify that
\check{\star}
preserves relations
\preceq
and
\vartriangleleft
between weight functions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma
,
\sigma_{1}
,
\tau
, and
\tau_{1}
be weight functions.

(i)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma_{1}
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau_{1}
, then
\sigma\check{\star}\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma_{1}\check{\star}\tau_{1}
is satisfied.

Consequently, if
\sigma\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\sigma_{1}
and
\tau\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\tau_{1}
, then
\sigma\check{\star}\tau\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\sigma_{1}\check{\star}\tau_{1}
.

(ii)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\sigma_{1}
and
\tau\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau_{1}
, then
\sigma\check{\star}\tau\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\sigma_{1}\check{\star}\tau_{1}
is satisfied.

Proof.

(i)
By assumption we can find
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 1
such that
\sigma_{1}(t)\leq C_{1}\sigma(t)+C_{1}
and
\tau_{1}(t)\leq C_{2}\tau(t)+C_{2}
for all
t\geq 0
and so for
C:=2\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
:

\forall\;t\geq 0\;\forall\;s>0:\;\;\;\sigma_{1}(s)+\tau_{1}(t/s)\leq C_{1}\sigma(s)+C_{2}\tau(t/s)+C_{1}+C_{2}\leq C(\sigma(s)+\tau(t/s)+1).

By taking the infimum over all
s>0
(see (
3.1
)) and since
\sigma\check{\star}\tau
is also a weight function we have verified
\sigma\check{\star}\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma_{1}\check{\star}\tau_{1}
.

(ii)
Here, for any
0<c<1
there exists
D_{c}\geq 1
such that
\sigma_{1}(t)\leq c\sigma(t)+D_{c}
and
\tau_{1}(t)\leq c\tau(t)+D_{c}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then
\sigma_{1}(s)+\tau_{1}(t/s)\leq c(\sigma(s)+\tau(t/s))+2D_{c}
for all
t\geq 0
and
s>0
and thus the conclusion follows.
∎

3.2.
Main results on the growth indices

The next main statement shows how
\check{\star}
is modifying the growth indices from Section
2.4
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\sigma
,
\tau
be weight functions.

(i)

If
\gamma(\sigma),\gamma(\tau)>0
, then

\gamma(\sigma)+\gamma(\tau)\leq\gamma(\sigma\check{\star}\tau).

(ii)

If
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma),\overline{\gamma}(\tau)<+\infty
, then

\overline{\gamma}(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)\leq\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)+\overline{\gamma}(\tau).

Proof.

(i)
Let
0<\gamma<\gamma(\sigma)
,
0<\delta<\gamma(\tau)
, then by definition we get

\exists\;K_{1}>1\;\exists\;\epsilon_{1}\in(0,1)\;\exists\;H_{1}>1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(K_{1}^{\gamma}t)\leq K_{1}^{1-\epsilon_{1}}\sigma(t)+H_{1},

and the analogous estimate holds for
\tau
with constants
K_{2},H_{2}>1
and some
\epsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
. By Lemma
2.5
we can assume w.l.o.g. that
K_{1}=K_{2}
and write
K
for this constant. We also set
H:=\max\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
and
\epsilon:=\min\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\}
and by commutativity, see
(c)
in Lemma
3.1
, we estimate as follows for all
t>0
:

\displaystyle\sigma\check{\star}\tau(K^{\gamma+\delta}t)

\displaystyle=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau((K^{\gamma+\delta}t)/s)\}=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau(K^{\gamma}(K^{\delta}t)/s)\}

\displaystyle\underbrace{=}_{u=K^{\delta}t/s}\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma(K^{\delta}t/u)+\tau(K^{\gamma}u)\}\leq\inf_{u>0}\{K^{1-\epsilon}\sigma(t/u)+K^{1-\epsilon}\tau(u)\}+2H

\displaystyle=K^{1-\epsilon}\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma(t/u)+\tau(u)\}+2H=K^{1-\epsilon}\tau\check{\star}\sigma(t)+2H=K^{1-\epsilon}\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)+2H.

By definition this verifies
(P_{\sigma\check{\star}\tau,\gamma+\delta})
and so
\gamma+\delta<\gamma(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)
by
[
index
, Thm. 2.11]
. Since
\gamma<\gamma(\sigma)
and
\delta<\gamma(\tau)
can be chosen arbitrary we have shown
\gamma(\sigma)+\gamma(\tau)\leq\gamma(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)
.

(ii)
Let
\gamma>\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)
and
\delta>\overline{\gamma}(\tau)
, then by definition

\exists\;A_{1}>1\;\exists\;\epsilon_{1}\in(0,1)\;\exists\;H_{1}\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(A_{1}^{\gamma}t)\geq A_{1}^{1+\epsilon_{1}}\sigma(t)-H_{1},

and

\exists\;A_{2}>1\;\exists\;\epsilon_{2}\in(0,1)\;\exists\;H_{2}\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(A_{2}^{\delta}t)\geq A_{2}^{1+\epsilon_{2}}\tau(t)-H_{2}.

Again, by Lemma
2.5
we can assume w.l.o.g. that
A_{1}=A_{2}
and simply write
A
for this constant. We set
H:=\max\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
,
\epsilon:=\min\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\}
and estimate as follows for all
t>0
:

\displaystyle\sigma\check{\star}\tau(A^{\gamma+\delta}t)

\displaystyle=\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma((A^{\delta}t)/u)+\tau(A^{\gamma}u)\}\geq\inf_{u>0}\{A^{1+\epsilon}\sigma(t/u)+A^{1+\epsilon}\tau(u)\}-2H

\displaystyle=A^{1+\epsilon}\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma(t/u)+\tau(u)\}-2H=A^{1+\epsilon}\tau\check{\star}\sigma(t)-2H=A^{1+\epsilon}\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t)-2H.

Therefore,
(\overline{P}_{\sigma\check{\star}\tau,\gamma+\delta})
is verified and in view of
[
index
, Thm. 2.16,
(7)
]
this implies
\gamma+\delta>\overline{\gamma}(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)
. Since
\gamma>\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)
and
\delta>\overline{\gamma}(\tau)
are arbitrary we have shown
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)+\overline{\gamma}(\tau)\geq\overline{\gamma}(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)
.
∎

Let us apply Theorem
3.4
to some special cases:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\sigma
,
\tau
be weight functions.

(i)

When
\gamma(\sigma)>0

\forall\;\alpha>0:\;\;\;\gamma(\sigma)+\alpha\leq\gamma((((\sigma^{\iota})^{\alpha})_{\star})^{1/\alpha})

(ii)

When

,
0<\gamma(\tau)=\overline{\gamma}(\tau)<+\infty
, then

\gamma(\sigma)+\gamma(\tau)=\gamma(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)=\overline{\gamma}(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)=\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)+\overline{\gamma}(\tau).

Note:

(i)
with

should be compared with
[

, Prop. 2.22]

(10)

\forall\;\alpha>0:\;\;\;\gamma(\sigma)+\alpha=\gamma((((\sigma^{\iota})^{\alpha})_{\star})^{1/\alpha})=\overline{\gamma}((((\sigma^{\iota})^{\alpha})_{\star})^{1/\alpha}),

thus, in particular, the following identity:

\forall\;\alpha,\beta>0:\;\;\;\alpha+\beta=\gamma(\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}\check{\star}\operatorname{id}^{1/\beta})=\overline{\gamma}(\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}\check{\star}\operatorname{id}^{1/\beta}).

Proof.

(i)
By using Remark
2.4
and Theorem

and

,

And then (

) yields the conclusion.

(ii)

.
∎

4.
Generalized upper Legendre conjugate

Now we proceed with the second conjugate; this approach is more technical.

4.1.
Preliminaries

Let
\sigma
,
\tau
be given weight functions and put

(4.1)

\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{\sigma(s)-\tau(s/t)\},\;\;\;t\in(0,+\infty).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\sigma
,
\tau
be weight functions. Then

(a)

\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau
is non-decreasing,
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)=+\infty
and

(4.2)

\forall\;t,u>0:\;\;\;\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(tu)\geq\max\{\sigma(t)-\tau(1/u),\sigma(u)-\tau(1/t)\},

which gives
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma
;

(b)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\sigma(s)-\tau(s)=\sigma(0)-\tau(0)
and put
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(0):=\sigma(0)-\tau(0)
.

Formally, the last equality could also be achieved by using the conventions
\frac{0}{0}=0
,
\frac{1}{0}=+\infty
,
\tau(+\infty)=+\infty
.

Proof.

(a)
Since
\tau
is non-decreasing also
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau
is non-decreasing and choose
s:=t
resp.
s:=u
in (
4.1
) to obtain (
4.2
). Choosing
u:=1
this estimate verifies
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)=+\infty
and
\sigma(t)=O(\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(b)
Since
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\tau(t)=+\infty
, for any fixed
s>0
there exists
t_{s}>0
(small) such that
\sigma(0)-\tau(0)>\sigma(s)-\tau(s/t)\Leftrightarrow\tau(s/t)>\sigma(s)-\sigma(0)+\tau(0)
for all
0<t\leq t_{s}
.

Note that obviously
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)\geq\sigma(0)-\tau(0)
for any
t>0
.
∎

However, in general it is not clear that
0\leq\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)<+\infty
for some/any
t\in[0,+\infty)
and so
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau
is
not automatically a weight function
according to Definition
2.2
. The next result is the first step to ensure that
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau
is a weight function:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be weight functions. In order to ensure
0\leq\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)
for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
it suffices to assume
\tau(0)=0
.

Proof.

Recall that
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)\geq\sigma(0)-\tau(0)
for any
t>0
and
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(0):=\sigma(0)-\tau(0)
; see
(b)
in Lemma
4.1
. If
\tau(0)=0
, then the conclusion follows since
\sigma(0)\geq 0
.
∎

4.2.
On the well-definedness of the generalized upper Legendre conjugate

This section is devoted to the the study that
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)<+\infty
for at least all sufficiently small
t
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be weight functions and let
t_{0}\in(0,+\infty]
. Consider the following assertions:

(i)

We have

(4.3)

\sup_{0<t<t_{0}}\limsup_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\tau(u)}<1.

(ii)

\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)<+\infty
holds for all
t\in[0,t_{0})
; i.e.

(4.4)

\forall\;t\in(0,t_{0})\;\exists\;D_{t}>0\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(s)-\tau(s/t)\leq D_{t}.

(iii)

We have

(4.5)

\sup_{0<t<t_{0}}\limsup_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\tau(u)}\leq 1.

Then
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
is valid.

(iv)

On the other hand, if

(4.6)

\exists\;t_{0}\in(0,+\infty):\;\;\;\limsup_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(t_{0}u)}{\tau(u)}>1,

then
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)=+\infty
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

For the quotients in the
\limsup
-expressions note that
\tau
is non-decreasing and
\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\tau(u)=+\infty
; so, in particular,
\tau(u)>0
for all
u
sufficiently large. On the other hand note that in general it is not clear if (
4.3
) and/or (
4.5
) is preserved under equivalence of weight functions.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
By assumption
\limsup_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\tau(u)}<1
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
, therefore

\exists\;\epsilon>0\;\forall\;t\in(0,t_{0})\;\exists\;u_{t}>0\;\forall\;u\geq u_{t}:\;\;\;\sigma(tu)\leq(1-\epsilon)\tau(u),

equivalently

\exists\;\epsilon>0\;\forall\;t\in(0,t_{0})\;\exists\;D_{t}>0\;\forall\;u\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(tu)\leq(1-\epsilon)\tau(u)+D_{t}.

Set
s:=tu
, then this estimate turns into

\exists\;\epsilon>0\;\forall\;t\in(0,t_{0})\;\exists\;D_{t}>0\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(s)\leq(1-\epsilon)\tau(s/t)+D_{t}<\tau(s/t)+D_{t},

hence by definition
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)\leq D_{t}<+\infty
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
follows immediately.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
In (
4.4
) put
u:=\frac{s}{t}
and so

\forall\;t\in(0,t_{0})\;\exists\;D_{t}>0\;\forall\;u\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(ut)\leq\tau(u)+D_{t}.

Thus (
4.5
) follows by taking into account
\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\tau(u)=+\infty
.

(iv)
Note that (
4.6
) implies
\limsup_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\tau(u)}>1
for all
t\geq t_{0}
and by assumption with
A:=\limsup_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(t_{0}u)}{\tau(u)}\in(1,+\infty]
we get

\exists\;\epsilon_{1}>0\;\forall\;\epsilon\in(\epsilon_{1},A-1)\;\exists\;u_{\epsilon}>0:\;\;\;\sigma(t_{0}u_{\epsilon})>(1+\epsilon)\tau(u_{\epsilon})>(1+\epsilon_{1})\tau(u_{\epsilon}).

Hence
\sigma(s_{\epsilon})-\tau(s_{\epsilon}/t_{0})>\epsilon_{1}\tau(s_{\epsilon}/t_{0})
with
s_{\epsilon}:=t_{0}u_{\epsilon}
which immediately gives
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t_{0})=+\infty
since the right-hand side of this estimate is unbounded as
s_{\epsilon}\rightarrow+\infty
.
∎

Note:

(i)

In general it is not clear if
(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Lemma
4.3
holds; for this note that (
4.5
) precisely means

\forall\;t\in(0,t_{0})\;\forall\;\epsilon>0\;\exists\;D_{t,\epsilon}>0\;\forall\;u\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(tu)\leq(1+\epsilon)\tau(u)+D_{t,\epsilon},

but which does not imply necessarily (
4.4
).

(ii)

The analogous proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Lemma
4.3
gives: If

(4.7)

\forall\;t>0:\;\;\;\liminf_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\tau(u)}>1

holds, then
0\leq\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)
for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
. However, (
4.7
) implies (
4.6
) for any
t_{0}\in(0,+\infty)
and so
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t)=+\infty
for any
t\in(0,+\infty)
.

Remark 4.4
.

Lemma

for

implies that, under appropriate (and mild) assumptions on

and

If

Moreover, for
\sigma\widehat{\star}\sigma
we have the following comments for which we use that

is non-decreasing:

(i)

The
\limsup
-expression satisfies the estimate in (

) for all

.

(

\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\sigma(u)}\geq 1
(for all large

(iv)

(v)

Similarly, let

5.
Generalized Legendre conjugates of associated weight functions

The aim in this section is to study in detail the above operations within the weight sequence setting; i.e. when
\sigma=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
and
\tau=\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
for weight sequences
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. In order to present a complete approach we treat non-standard situations and even allow
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and/or
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
.

First, note that the weight function
\log_{+}
from Examples
3.2
and
4.5
corresponds to the “exotic situation” when one allows resp. considers “sequences”
\mathbf{M}
such that
M_{p}=+\infty
for all sufficiently large
p
; see
[
solidassociatedweight
, Lemma 7.2]
. Indeed, this Lemma completely illustrates this particular and limiting example.

5.1.
Generalized lower Legendre conjugate

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}},\mathbf{N}=(N_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and let
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
,
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
denote the corresponding associated weight functions. Assume that
\mathbf{M}_{\iota},\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
and set

(5.1)

\mathcal{I}_{t}:=(t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota}),\;\;\;t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}),

with the conventions
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1}=0
if
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
, and
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
if either
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
or
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
. Then, analogously as in (
3.1
) let us introduce

(5.2)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t):=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)+\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)\},\;\;\;t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}).

In case
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
this precisely corresponds to (
3.1
). For this expression we refer also to
[
Boman98
,
(3.6)
]
. Indeed, in
[
Boman98
]

log-convex
sequences have been considered and so
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
,
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
follows but, however,
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
resp.
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
is not clear in general; see
(b)
in Section
2.2
.

Formally, if
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
resp.
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then in view of
(ii)
in Lemma
2.6
we are not dealing with weight functions in the sense of Definition
2.2
. We give an interpretation of (
5.2
) even in this (these) non-standard situation(s). In
[
Boman98
]
in (
5.2
) all
t>0
and
\inf_{s>0}
have been considered but
\mathcal{I}_{t}
seems to be the natural set in this context.

We comment on this in more detail and recall
(ii)
in Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.1
:

(i)

\mathcal{I}_{t}\neq\emptyset
is clear for
t=0
, for
t>0
when
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
(using
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
) and also otherwise since
t<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
.

For each
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
we have
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)<+\infty
because
s<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)<+\infty
because
\frac{t}{s}<\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
. Consequently,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)<+\infty
for all
t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
.

Clearly,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
is non-decreasing and one has

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)\}=\inf_{s\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}\{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)\}=0.

(ii)

We can consider in the infimum also the value
s=0
: Since
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
and by using the conventions
\frac{1}{0}=+\infty
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(+\infty)=+\infty
this will not change the value of
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)
. Note that
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(+\infty)=+\infty
is justified by continuity and
\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)=+\infty
if
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
and by
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)=+\infty
for all
u>\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
if
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
; recall
(b)
in Section
2.5
.

(iii)

Assume
t>0
and
\mathcal{I}_{t}\neq(0,+\infty)
, i.e.
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty

or

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
. Then for any
s\notin\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
we either have
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
, if
s>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
, or
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)=+\infty
, if
s<t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1}
. Thus formally we can even allow
s\in[0,+\infty)
, because if either
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
or
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)=+\infty
, this will not effect the definition of
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
.

(iv)

On the other hand, if
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty

and

\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
then fix
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
. For any
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
one has
\frac{t}{s}>\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
and
s>\frac{t}{\mathbf{N}_{\iota}}>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
and so
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)=+\infty
. And if
s\geq 0
with
s\notin\mathcal{I}_{t}
, and such values exist since
\mathcal{I}_{t}\neq(0,+\infty)
, then as before either
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
or
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)=+\infty
.

Summarizing, so far we have shown the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
be given with
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
,
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
. Then it holds that

(a)

\forall\;t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}):\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=\inf_{s\in[0,+\infty)}\{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)+\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)\};

(b)

\forall\;t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}):\;\;\;0\leq\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)<+\infty;

(c)

if
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
,
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then

\forall\;t\in(\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota},+\infty):\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty.

Next we verify
commutativity
in all cases.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
be given with
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
,
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
. Then

(5.3)

\forall\;t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}):\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=\omega_{\mathbf{N}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t).

Proof.

First, let
t=0
. Then, as seen before in
(i)
we get
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=\inf_{s\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}\{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)\}=0=\inf_{s\in(0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota})}\{\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(s)\}=\omega_{\mathbf{N}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)
.

So let
t>0
. We apply the substitution
u:=\frac{t}{s}
in (
5.2
) and distinguish:

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
, then
\mathcal{I}_{t}=(0,+\infty)
and as in
(c)
in Lemma
3.1
one has
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)+\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t/u)+\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)
for all
s>0
resp. for all
u>0
. Thus commutativity for all
t>0
holds.

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
, then for any
t>0
and
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}=(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
we get
u\in(t\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{-1},+\infty)
. This gives
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t/u)<+\infty
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)<+\infty
for all such
u
. Moreover,
(t\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{-1},+\infty)
corresponds to the natural interval under consideration for the definition of
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)
and so commutativity follows.

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then for any
t>0
and
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}=(t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},+\infty)
we get
u\in(0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
which gives
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t/u)<+\infty
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)<+\infty
for all such
u
and
(t\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{-1},\mathbf{N}_{\iota})=(0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
corresponds to the interval under consideration for defining
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)
. Consequently, commutativity follows in this case, too.

(*)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
. For any
t\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
and any
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}=(t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
we get
u\in(t\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{-1},\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
. This corresponds to the interval under consideration for
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t/u)<+\infty
,
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)<+\infty
for all such
u
. Therefore, commutativity is valid as well.

∎

5.2.
Non-standard cases for the generalized lower Legendre conjugate

Assume now that
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
or
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=0
and note that for log-convex sequences
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
this is excluded. But when allowing formally the log-convex sequence given by
M_{0}\geq 0
and
M_{p}=0
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, then
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
. This corresponds to the regularization approach studied in
[

, Sect. 4.1]
; see also
(d)
in Section
2.5
.

In order to treat this situation we use the conventions
\frac{1}{0}=+\infty
,
\frac{0}{0}=0
and
0=0\cdot(+\infty)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and assume that either
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
or
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=0
. Then

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0,\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty,\;\;\;\forall\;t>0.

Proof.

We distinguish between different situations:

(\ast)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
. If
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then
\mathcal{I}_{t}=\emptyset
for any
t>0
and we only consider
t=0
and get
\mathcal{I}_{0}=\{0\}
. The convention
\frac{0}{0}=0
gives
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0
. Formally, we can put
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty
for all
t>0
when considering only
s=0
in the infimum and this is then also justified by the conventions
t/0=+\infty
,
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(+\infty)=+\infty
and since
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
for all
s>0
; see
(d)
in Section
2.5
.

If
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
, then
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
gives
I_{t}=\{0\}
for any
t\geq 0
and so the conventions
\frac{0}{0}=0
and
\frac{1}{0}=+\infty
imply again
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty
for all
t>0
.

(\ast)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=0
. If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then for
t=0
the conventions
\frac{1}{0}=+\infty
and
0=0\cdot(+\infty)
give
\mathcal{I}_{0}=(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
and for any
t>0
the interval
\mathcal{I}_{t}
is empty when using
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1}=+\infty
. Therefore, again
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0
and we can put
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty
for all
t>0
which is justified by the fact that here
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)=+\infty
for all
s>0
if
t>0
is arbitrary but fixed.

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
, then for
t=0
one has
\mathcal{I}_{0}=(0,+\infty)
and for any
t>0
let us set
I_{t}=\{+\infty\}
. Hence
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0
and using
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(+\infty)=+\infty
and
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
gives
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty
for any
t>0
.

(\ast)

\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=0=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
. Then
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty=\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s)
for all
t,s>0
and
I_{0}=\{0\}
by the convention
0\cdot(+\infty)=0
whereas
I_{t}=\emptyset
for any
t>0
. Therefore,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0
(using
\frac{0}{0}=0
) and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)=+\infty
for all
t>0
when formally again only considering
s=0
; see the first case before.

∎

5.3.
Main result

In the next step, for log-convex sequences we establish the connection between
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}
; the proof basically repeats the arguments given in
[
Boman98
, Lemma 4]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex. Then

(5.4)

\forall\;t\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}):\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}(t)=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t).

Note:

(i)

If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then (
5.4
) can be extended to all
t\in[0,+\infty)
when we set both sides to be equal to
+\infty
if
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
. And for
t=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
we consider
\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}}\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}(t)
.

This is justified by
(ii)
in Lemma
2.6
applied to
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
and
(c)
in Lemma
5.1
.

(ii)

And by taking into account Lemma
5.3
this identity formally even holds if either
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=0
or
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=0
on whole
[0,+\infty)
: take on both sides for
t=0
the value
0
and
+\infty
for all
t>0
. On the other hand, the convention
0\cdot(+\infty)=0
used in the proof of Lemma
5.3
yields that (
5.4
) is only required to hold for
t=0
(and for this value having the equality
0=0
).

(iii)

Theorem
5.4
yields an alternative prove for the commutativity of
\check{\star}
.

(iv)

Recall that in
[
Boman98
, Lemma 4]
by the assumptions on the sequences one only has
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
. If now
\mathbf{M}_{\iota},\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then the part where it is claimed
“Fix an arbitrary
t>0
and choose
k
such that
t\in[\mu_{k}\nu_{k},\mu_{k+1}\nu_{k+1}]
”
is not satisfied for
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
; see
(b)
in Section
2.2
.

Proof.

First, since both
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
are log-convex we have that
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
is log-convex, so
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}>0
and, moreover,

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}\nu_{p}=(\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N})_{\iota}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}.

(a)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
; i.e.
\mathcal{I}_{t}=(0,+\infty)
for all
t\geq 0
. Then for any given
t\geq 0
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
s>0
we write

(5.5)

\frac{t^{p}M_{0}\cdot N_{0}}{M_{p}\cdot N_{p}}=\frac{s^{p}M_{0}}{M_{p}}\frac{(t/s)^{p}N_{0}}{N_{p}}.

Take in this expression the supremum over all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and the infimum over all
s>0
, hence by definition
\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}(t))\leq\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t))
for all
t\geq 0
.

For the converse, first consider
t\geq 0
such that
\mu_{p}\nu_{p}\leq t\leq\mu_{p+1}\nu_{p+1}
for some
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Then choose
\theta\in[0,1)
such that
t=(\mu_{p}\nu_{p})^{1-\theta}(\mu_{p+1}\nu_{p+1})^{\theta}
and set
s_{t}:=\mu_{p}^{1-\theta}\mu_{p+1}^{\theta}(>0)
. Hence
\mu_{p}\leq s_{t}\leq\mu_{p+1}
,
\frac{t}{s_{t}}=\nu_{p}^{1-\theta}\nu_{p+1}^{\theta}
and so
\nu_{p}\leq\frac{t}{s_{t}}\leq\nu_{p+1}
. In view of (
2.19
) we have

\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{t^{p}M_{0}N_{0}}{M_{p}N_{p}}\right)=\log\left(\frac{s_{t}^{p}M_{0}}{M_{p}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{(t/s_{t})^{p}N_{0}}{N_{p}}\right)=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s_{t})+\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t/s_{t}),

hence
\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}(t)\geq\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t)
is verified for all
t\geq\mu_{1}\nu_{1}
.

Second, for
0\leq t<\mu_{1}\nu_{1}
there exists
0<s_{t}<\mu_{1}
such that
\frac{t}{s_{t}}<\nu_{1}
. Consequently, again in view of (
2.19
), we obtain
\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}}(t)=0=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s_{t})=\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t/s_{t})
and so the desired estimate is established for all
t\geq 0
.

(b)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
; i.e.
\mathcal{I}_{t}=(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
for all
t\geq 0
. Then in (
5.5
) let
t\geq 0
and
s\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
and consider the supremum over all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and the infimum over all
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
. For the converse, we follow case
(a)
with
t\in[\mu_{1}\nu_{1},+\infty)
,
s_{t}\in[\mu_{1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota}]
resp.
t\in[0,\mu_{1}\nu_{1})
,
s_{t}\in(0,\mu_{1})
and use Lemma
2.7
.

(c)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
; i.e.
\mathcal{I}_{t}=(t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},+\infty)
for all
t\geq 0
. Then in (
5.5
) let
t\geq 0
and
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
and consider the supremum over all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and the infimum over all
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
. For the converse, we follow case
(a)
with
t\in[\mu_{1}\nu_{1},+\infty)
,
s_{t}\in[\mu_{1},+\infty)
and so
\frac{t}{s_{t}}\in[\nu_{1},\mathbf{N}_{\iota}]
and
s_{t}\in[t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},+\infty)
. When
t\in[0,\mu_{1}\nu_{1})
,
s_{t}\in(0,\mu_{1})
, then
\frac{t}{s_{t}}\in[0,\nu_{1})
and in both situations apply again Lemma
2.7
.

(d)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
and
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
; i.e.
\mathcal{I}_{t}=(t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
. Then in (
5.5
) let
0\leq t<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
and
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
and consider the supremum over all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and the infimum over all
s\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
. For the converse, follow case
(a)
and consider
t\in[\mu_{1}\nu_{1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\mathbf{N}_{\iota})
,
s_{t}\in[\mu_{1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota}]
and
\frac{t}{s_{t}}\in[\nu_{1},\mathbf{N}_{\iota}]
and so
s_{t}\in[t\mathbf{N}_{\iota}^{-1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota}]
. When
t\in[0,\mu_{1}\nu_{1})
and
s_{t}\in(0,\mu_{1})
, then
\frac{t}{s_{t}}\in[0,\nu_{1})
and in both cases use Lemma
2.7
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

For all sequences in the above proof for which

we have to consider the particular closed interval to which the corresponding sequence of quotients belongs: In this situation it is not excluded that the sequence of quotients eventually stabilizes. However, then Lemma

still can be applied by taking into account Remark

5.4.
Generalized upper Legendre conjugate

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given with associated weight functions
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
,
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
. In order to introduce
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\widehat{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
, first note that assumption
(A)
in Section
4
is clear because
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0
.

But concerning the fact that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\widehat{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
is well-defined, see Lemma

, additional relations have to be investigated and we prove a crucial technical result.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and assume that
\mathbf{N}
is log-convex. Then
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p})^{1/p}=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]
and the following are equivalent:

(i)

The associated weight functions satisfy

(5.6)

\forall\;t\geq 0\;\exists\;D_{t}\geq 1\;\forall\;u\in[0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota}):\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(tu)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)+D_{t}.

(ii)

\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}
holds.

Any of these equivalent assertions imply
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty
and if
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}<+\infty
, then formally in (
5.6
) one can even take any
u\geq 0
by
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)=+\infty
for all
u>\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
.

Note:
If
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=+\infty
, then (
5.6
) is precisely (
4.4
) with
t_{0}=+\infty
between
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
; see
(B)
in Section

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\widehat{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}
is well-defined if and only if
\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}
.

Proof.

Using different notation and when
\mathbf{N}_{\iota}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty

[
weightedentireinclusion2
, Lemma 4.2]
. First remark that log-convexity of
\mathbf{N}
implies
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(N_{p})^{1/p}=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
(
5.6

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty

and all

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(tu)=+\infty
and
\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)<+\infty

2.18
) implies for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>0

\displaystyle N_{p}

\displaystyle=N_{0}\sup_{u\in(0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota})}\frac{u^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u))}\leq N_{0}e^{D_{t}}\sup_{u\in(0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota})}\frac{u^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(tu))}

\displaystyle=N_{0}e^{D_{t}}\sup_{v\in(0,t\mathbf{N}_{\iota})}\frac{(v/t)^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(v))}\leq N_{0}e^{D_{t}}\sup_{v\in(0,+\infty)}\frac{(v/t)^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(v))}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{0}e^{D_{t}}}{M_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{p}M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}\leq\frac{N_{0}e^{D_{t}}}{M_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{p}M_{p}.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)

\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;C_{h}\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}\;\forall\;u\geq 0:\;\;\;\frac{M_{0}(u/h)^{p}}{M_{p}}\leq C_{h}\frac{M_{0}}{N_{0}}\frac{N_{0}u^{p}}{N_{p}},

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;C_{h}\geq 1\;\forall\;u\geq 0:\;\;\;\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(u/h))\leq\frac{C_{h}M_{0}}{N_{0}}\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)),

which implies the estimate in (
5.6

and note that naturally we restrict to

∎

Let

Note:
By

Proposition 5.8

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}

\mathbf{N}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]

The associated weight functions satisfy

\exists\;t_{0}>0\;\forall\;t\in[0,t_{0})\;\exists\;D_{t}\geq 1\;\forall\;u\in[0,\mathbf{N}_{\iota}):\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(tu)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(u)+D_{t}.

(ii)

\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{M}
holds.

Moreover,

2.9

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\geq\mathbf{N}_{\iota}(\underline{C}_{\mathbf{N}\preceq\mathbf{M}})^{-1}=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}t_{0}

Note:
In Proposition (
5.6

\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}

5.7

4.4

Proof.

\mathbf{N}_{\iota}\in(0,+\infty]

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
(
5.7

(0<)t_{0}\leq\frac{\mathbf{M}_{\iota}}{\mathbf{N}_{\iota}}

t

\displaystyle N_{p}

\displaystyle=N_{0}e^{D_{t}}\sup_{v\in(0,t\mathbf{N}_{\iota})}\frac{(v/t)^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(v))}\leq N_{0}e^{D_{t}}\sup_{v\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}\frac{(v/t)^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(v))}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{0}e^{D_{t}}}{M_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{p}M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}\leq\frac{N_{0}e^{D_{t}}}{M_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{p}M_{p}.

If instead of
(**)

(***)

\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{M}

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\widehat{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(0)=0,\hskip 15.0pt\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\widehat{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)-\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(s/t)\},\;\;\;t\in(0,(\underline{C}_{\mathbf{N}\preceq\mathbf{M}})^{-1}).

(*)

(*)

(***)

(**)

(*)

Taking

,

Proof.

First, concerning the equality

and
\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}

\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}_{\iota}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}=+\infty

For
t=0
in (
5.10
) one has the equality
0=0
and so consider from now on

\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\widehat{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}}(t))=\sup_{s\geq 0}\frac{\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}s^{p}M_{0}/M_{p}}{\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}(s/t)^{q}N_{0}/N_{q}}

\exp(\omega_{\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}}(t))=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{t^{\ell}M_{0}/N_{0}}{M_{\ell}/N_{\ell}}

For any

,
s\geq 0
and

Finally, for

When considering assumption

(c)

5.6.
Supplementary results

In this section we are studying how crucial and useful growth properties for weight sequences and relations between them are preserved under
(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})\mapsto\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
and
(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})\mapsto\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
. The relevance of this goal follows from the shown equalities in the main results Theorems
5.4
and
5.11
.

From now on, in this Section, let us assume that
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
which is only a subtle technical restriction when dealing with log-convex sequences satisfying
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=+\infty=\mathbf{N}_{\iota}
and not effecting the definition of weighted spaces; recall Remark
2.1
.

(i)

In
[
subaddlike
, Thm. 3.1]
it has been shown that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
satisfies
(\omega_{1})
(i.e.
\gamma(\omega_{\mathbf{M}})>0
) if and only if

(5.13)

\exists\;Q\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}:\;\;\;\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(M_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p})^{1/p}}>1.

(ii)

Concerning
(\omega_{6})
for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
(i.e.
\overline{\gamma}(\omega_{\mathbf{M}})<+\infty
) several equivalent reformulations exist; we refer to
[
modgrowthstrange
, Thm. 3.1]
and the citations given in this paper. Indeed,
(\omega_{6})
for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
holds if and only if
\mathbf{M}
satisfies
moderate growth (mg)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;p,q\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p+q}\leq C^{p+q}M_{p}M_{q},

equivalently

(5.14)

\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{2p}}{\mu_{p}}<+\infty.

In the literature (mg) is also denoted by (M.2); this notion is due to H. Komatsu in
[
Komatsu73
]
.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
be given.

(i)

If
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}
satisfy (
5.13
), then also
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
and if
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}
satisfy
(mg),
then also
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{M}
satisfies

(5.15)

\exists\;Q\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}:\;\;\;\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(M_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p})^{1/p}}=+\infty,

and
\mathbf{N}
satisfies
(mg),
then
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
has (
5.13
).

(iii)

If
\mathbf{M}
satisfies
(mg),

\mathbf{N}
satisfies (
5.13
) and
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
is log-convex, then
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
has
(mg).

Proof.

(i)
Concerning (
5.13
) note that if this condition holds for some
Q_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, then for all
Q\geq Q_{1}
because
p\mapsto(M_{p})^{1/p}
is non-decreasing by
M_{0}=1
and log-convexity. If now
\mathbf{M}
satisfies (
5.13
) with
Q_{1}
and
\mathbf{N}
with
Q_{2}
, then with
Q:=\max\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
one has

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(M_{Qp}\cdot N_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p}\cdot N_{p})^{1/p}}\geq\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(M_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p})^{1/p}}\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(N_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(N_{p})^{1/p}},

and the conclusion follows from this estimate.

Concerning (mg), everything is immediate by
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{2p}\nu_{2p}}{\mu_{p}\nu_{p}}\leq\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{2p}}{\mu_{p}}\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\nu_{2p}}{\nu_{p}}
and (
5.14
).

(ii)
Let (
5.15
) for
\mathbf{M}
be valid with some
Q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
(and hence for all
Q_{1}\geq Q
, too). Since
\mathbf{N}
satisfies (mg) one has, by iteration,

(5.16)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;Q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;N_{Qp}\leq C^{pQ(Q+1)/2-1}(N_{p})^{Q}\leq C^{2pQ^{2}}(N_{p})^{Q}.

By (
5.15
) for
\mathbf{M}
we can find
p_{0}
depending on
Q
and
C
such that for all
p\geq p_{0}
:

\frac{(M_{Qp}/N_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p}/N_{p})^{1/p}}\geq\frac{(M_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p})^{1/p}}\frac{1}{C^{2Q}}>1.

(iii)
Since
\mathbf{N}
satisfies (
5.13
) we get

\exists\;Q\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}\;\exists\;\epsilon>0\;\exists\;p_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;p\geq p_{\epsilon}:\;\;\;\frac{(N_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(N_{p})^{1/p}}\geq 1+\epsilon.

Hence, when applying (
5.16
) for
\mathbf{M}
to this
Q
we estimate by
\frac{(M_{Qp}/N_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p}/N_{p})^{1/p}}\leq C^{2Q}(1+\epsilon)^{-1}
for all
p\geq p_{\epsilon}
and when choosing a sufficiently large constant
C_{1}
this verifies
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{(M_{Qp}/N_{Qp})^{1/(Qp)}}{(M_{p}/N_{p})^{1/p}}\leq D
. By assumption
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
is log-convex and then the technical result
[
subaddlike
, Lemma 2.2
(ii)
]
applied to
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
yields the conclusion. (Indeed, formally in this result one shall assume that
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
but a switch to an equivalent and normalized sequence
\widetilde{\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
yields the conclusion since the boundedness of the above expression in the supremum and (mg) are preserved under equivalence.)
∎

Combining Lemma
5.12
with Theorems
5.4
and
5.11
immediately gives:

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
be given.

(i)

If
\gamma(\omega_{\mathbf{M}})>0
and
\gamma(\omega_{\mathbf{N}})>0
, then
\gamma(\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}})=\gamma(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}})>0
and if
\overline{\gamma}(\omega_{\mathbf{M}})<+\infty
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega_{\mathbf{N}})<+\infty
, then
\overline{\gamma}(\omega_{\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}})=\overline{\gamma}(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\check{\star}\omega_{\mathbf{N}})<+\infty
.

(ii)

Assume that
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
is log-convex and
[formula]

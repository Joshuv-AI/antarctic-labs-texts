Controlling quantum phases with step-like electric potentials in one-dimensional Hubbard systems
D. Arisa, R. M. Dos Santos, I. M. Carvalho, V. V. França*

Abstract
Quantum systems under electric potentials provide a powerful framework for uncovering and controlling novel quantum phases, especially in low-dimensional systems with strong correlations. In this work, we investigate quantum phase transitions induced by a step-like electric potential in a one-dimensional half-filled Hubbard chain. By analyzing i) tunneling energy and local doublon response, ii) charge and spin gaps, and iii) entanglement between the chain halves, we identify three distinct phases: Mott insulator, metal and band-like insulator. The metallic regime, characterized by the closing of both charge and spin gaps, is accompanied by an electric-potential dependence of kinetic energy and a quasi-periodic oscillatory behavior of local doublon response and entanglement. Although the metallic phase persists for different magnetizations, its extent in the phase diagram shrinks as spin polarization increases.

Introduction

A key feature of quantum systems is their pronounced sensitivity to external perturbations, such as electric potentials, as these enhance quantum fluctuations and lead to nontrivial emergent phenomena
Goychuk and Hänggi (2005)
;
Campisi et al. (2011)
. In systems described by the Hubbard model, which balances electron motion and repulsion, complex phenomena such as superfluid phases

;
Canella and França (2019)
;
Canella and França (2020)
;
Arisa and França (2020)
and Mott-Anderson transitions

;
Canella and França (2021)
emerge. Electric potentials can strongly affect these systems, producing undamped Bloch oscillations
Zheng (2021)
, driving them to non-thermal steady states
Joura et al. (2015)
, and even triggering transitions from metal to Mott insulator
Li et al. (2015)
;
Bellomia et al. (2024)
.

In one-dimensional (1D) systems, the reduced dimensionality amplifies quantum fluctuations, which significantly affect correlations and ordering

;
Giamarchi (2016)
. Also, the growing number of 1D materials

;

;

;

;

;

;
Wang et al. (2021)
;
Li et al. (2012b)
;
Alfieri et al. (2022)
makes them especially relevant for exploring quantum phase transitions (QPTs). Although two-site (dimer) Hubbard systems have long served as minimal models to explore the response to applied electrostatic potentials, including step-like profiles analysis
Balcerzak and Szałowski (2017)
;
Balcerzak and Szałowski (2018)
;
Villegas and Villagonzalo (2024)
;
Hasegawa (2005)
;
Hasegawa (2011)
, scaling up such systems introduces additional correlations

;

;

;
Grüner (1988)
;
Sanino et al. (2024)
;
Essler et al. (2005)
, arising from collective excitations, which cannot be captured by simple dimer model extrapolations.

Beyond dimer analyses, theoretical studies of extended 1D systems have shown that self-consistent step-like electrostatic potentials can generate metallic phases at the interface between Mott and band insulators
Oka and Nagaosa (2005)
;
Kancharla and Dagotto (2006)
. In contrast, a linear Wannier–Stark potential produces interfaces between commensurate and incommensurate density regions that remain insulating, with a finite local density-of-states gap
Boidi et al. (2024)
. Thus, while self-consistent steps yield metallic interfaces and linear potentials preserve insulating behavior, the case of fixed step-like potentials without self-consistency remains unresolved. This question is particularly relevant to ultracold-atom experiments, where optical lattices enable precise engineering of external potentials
Bloch et al. (2008)
;
Gross and Bloch (2017)
;
Schäfer et al. (2020)
, including state-dependent lattices capable of creating interfaces and step-like profiles
Gadway et al. (2010)
.

Here we investigate quantum phase transitions induced by a static step-like electrostatic potential in the many-site 1D Hubbard chains at half-filling. We identify three distinct phases: Mott insulator, metallic, and band insulator. The metallic phase is characterized by the vanishing of both charge and spin gaps, and the charge gap decays linearly with increasing system size, in contrast to previous studies where metallicity was confined to a finite-width interface region
Oka and Nagaosa (2005)
;
Kancharla and Dagotto (2006)
. The metallic phase is also marked by a field dependence of the kinetic energy and quasi-periodic oscillations in the local doublon response and entanglement. These quantities are then used to characterize the metallic phase in magnetized systems, since the gap is only well defined at zero magnetization. While the metallic phase persists across varying magnetizations, increased spin polarization progressively narrows its range in the phase diagram.

Fig.

practically has no effect on the maximum entanglement

S_{\max}
under spin polarization is also a robust signature that the metallic regime survives for

For the Mott and band-like insulators, entanglement reveals the localized nature of electronic states through its asymptotic behavior. For
V\gg U
, entanglement approaches zero, signaling the suppression of quantum correlations across the interface between the two halves of the chain. This is expected, as in this limit the system effectively behaves as if an infinite potential barrier forms at the interface, decoupling the halves. On the other hand, for
U\gg V
, since charge fluctuations are suppressed, finite entanglement originates solely from spin degrees of freedom, due to the superexchange interaction
Essler et al. (2005)
;
Giamarchi (2003)
, which mediates spin correlations across the interface and underlies the spin-density wave order.

4
Conclusion

In summary, we investigated quantum phase transitions induced by a step-like electric potential
V
in a 1D half-filled Hubbard model. The interplay between electronic interactions and the potential
V
defines distinct regimes in the system: Mott-insulator, metallic, and band-insulator phases. The metallic phase, marked by the closing of both charge and spin gaps, is accompanied by quasi-periodic oscillatory behavior in local doublon response and entanglement, due to quantum fluctuations enhanced at the interface. Although the metallic regime persists across different magnetizations, higher spin polarization reduces its width in the phase diagram, Fig.
3
. Future work could aim to explore how smoother interfaces affect the phase diagram. For instance, a linear Wannier–Stark potential
Boidi et al. (2024)
— which may be regarded as a smoother version of our step potential — has been shown to yield insulating domains at the interfaces, suggesting that smoothing may lead to qualitatively different scenarios. Other directions include extending the analysis to finite temperature and higher dimensionality, as well as proposing experimental setups for realizations in ultracold atoms or 1D materials.

Dissecting the temporal genetic networks programming soybean embryo development from embryonic morphogenesis to post-germination
Authors: Yen-Ching Wang, Wei-Hsun Hsieh, Liang-Peng Lin, Meng-Hsun He, Ya-Ting Jhan, Chu-Jun Huang, Junpeng Zhan, Ching-Chun Chang, Tzung-Fu Hsieh, Jer-Young Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractKey messageDesiccation-stage transcription factors perform similar functions, with early ones focused on desiccation tolerance and later ones on development. Gene networks governing late embryo development diverge between soybean and Arabidopsis.AbstractTo understand gene activities programming seed embryo development, we profiled the soybean embryo transcriptome across embryonic morphogenesis through post-germination. Transcriptomic landscapes across embryo development feature highly prevalent transcripts, categorized into early and late groups, with shared and distinct functions. During the mid-storage reserve accumulation stage, the upregulated genes are enriched with regulatory tasks at both the transcriptional and chromatin levels, including DNA methylation and chromatin remodeling. The epigenetic-related functions also dominate in the upregulated genes during germination, involving core histone variants and histone chaperones. Gene network analysis reveals both stage-specific modules and modules active across multiple stages. The desiccation-associated gene module integrates diverse transcription factors (TFs) that are sequentially active during different desiccation stages, transitioning from abiotic stress functions early on to developmental functions later. Two TFs, active during the early and mid-desiccation stages were functionally assessed in Arabidopsis overexpression lines to uncover their potential roles in desiccation processes. Interestingly, nearly half of the Arabidopsis orthologs of soybean TFs active in the desiccation-associated module are inactive during Arabidopsis desiccation. Our results reveal that chromatin and transcriptional regulation coordinate during mid-storage reserve accumulation, while distinct epigenetic mechanisms drive germination. Additionally, gene modules either perform stage-specific functions or are required across multiple stages, and gene networks during late embryogenesis diverge between soybean and Arabidopsis. Our studies provide new information on the biological processes and gene networks underlying development from embryonic morphogenesis to post-germination.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00299-024-03354-0.Keywords: Soybean, Embryo development, Gene regulatory networks

Introduction

Higher plant development can be divided into (i) embryonic development, starting from post-fertilization to initiate differentiation for axis and cotyledon formation, and (ii) post-germinative development, where differentiation recommences for adult growth (Goldberg et al. 1994; Becker et al. 2014). These two morphogenesis events are bridged by a period of developmental arrest, characterized by cessation of cell differentiation and division. During this period, several biological processes proceed sequentially for temporal transition. These include the accumulation of storage reserves, desiccation, dormancy, and germination (Fig. 1) (Le et al. 2010). In the reserve accumulation and desiccation phase, cotyledon cells undergo enlargement and endoreduplication. This process facilitates the rapid accumulation of storage reserves, including seed-storage proteins and oil, to serve as energy sources during germination and seedling growth (Chen et al. 2021). Additionally, various compounds, such as disaccharides, are produced and may perform specific functions to protect against reactive oxygen species (ROS), stabilize protein conformation, modulate membrane integrity, and increase cell wall flexibility (Leprince et al. 2017). Meanwhile, seeds acquire several important agricultural traits. These include desiccation tolerance and seed longevity, which is the capacity to remain viable for long periods (Leprince et al. 2017). Additionally, seeds develop seed vigor, which refers to germination rate, uniformity of seed germination, and tolerance to specific stresses in post-germination (Rajjou et al. 2012). Dormant seeds, the quiescent state, can survive in harsh environments and favorable conditions can break the dormancy for germination. Dormancy may result from exogenous factors (e.g., physical dormancy) or endogenous factors (e.g., physiological dormancy) (Baskin and Baskin 2021). Germination progresses through three phases: Phase I involves rehydration, Phase II reactivates primary metabolism (e.g., nutrient reserve mobilization), and Phase III loosens the cell wall for radical elongation that completes germination sensu stricto (Wang et al. 2015).

To understand the molecular basis of seed development, transcriptome profiling has been applied to study temporal changes in genome-wide gene activities. These studies focus on specific developmental aspects, such as early seed development to maturation (Belmonte et al. 2013; Hofmann et al. 2019; Jones and Vodkin 2013; Le et al. 2010; Severin et al. 2010), late maturation (Pereira Lima et al. 2017; Righetti et al. 2015; Verdier et al. 2013) or germination (Buitink et al. 2006; Dekkers et al. 2013; Howell et al. 2009; Narsai et al. 2011). Furthermore, most of these studies are conducted in the model plant Arabidopsis, which has a shorter desiccation period (about 2 days) compared to legumes (e.g., 20 days in soybean). This leads to a limited understanding of the molecular basis of seed desiccation and leaves a knowledge gap of gene activities and the interconnected gene regulatory networks programming the entire process from embryonic to post-germinative morphogenesis, especially in crops.

To fill the knowledge gap in genome-wide transcription activities from embryonic morphogenesis to post-germination in the crops, particularly during desiccation processes, we conducted an in-depth time course transcriptome analysis in soybean. Temporal mRNA populations can be clustered into two groups, delineated by stages featuring green and yellow cotyledons. Meanwhile, the transcriptomic profiles are characterized by highly abundant mRNAs, consisting of early and late expression groups with shared and different functions. Upregulated genes in the mid-storage reserve stage are enriched in functions of regulatory processes at the transcriptional and chromatin levels, including those involved in DNA methylation and chromatin remodeling. These upregulated epigenetic-related genes differ from those upregulated during germination, which include histone variants and histone chaperones. We inferred gene regulatory networks to identify the desiccation-associated gene module that operates across different desiccation stages. In the desiccation-associated module, functions related to desiccation tolerance are more abundant in TFs active during early desiccation, whereas developmental functions are more prevalent in TFs active during late desiccation. Two TFs, GmZAT18 and GmNAC099, which are upregulated in successive desiccation stages but have inactive orthologs in Arabidopsis during desiccation, were functionally assessed for their roles during desiccation. Furthermore, we examined the gene activities of Arabidopsis TFs that are orthologous to the active soybean TFs in the desiccation-associated module and found that 46% of these Arabidopsis orthologs are inactive during Arabidopsis desiccation. Our studies offer new insights into the molecular basis and gene networks driving development from embryonic to post-germinative morphogenesis in soybean.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Soybean plants [Glycine max (L.) cv. Williams 82] were grown at 22 °C under long-day conditions with a 16 h light to 8 h dark cycle in the greenhouse at Academia Sinica-Biotechnology Center in Southern Taiwan. Seeds before germination were staged based on length, weight, and embryonic characteristics, such as shape and color (Supplementary methods). Drawings of soybean seed embryos at all developmental stages used in our experiments are shown in Fig. 1a. ed, md, ld, and dry seeds had lengths of 10.0–11.0 mm, 9.0–10.0 mm, 8.0–9.0 mm, and 7.0–8.0 mm, respectively. In addition, ed, md, ld, and dry seeds weighed 200–300 mg, 200–300 mg, 150–200 mg, and 100–150 mg, respectively (Fig. 1b). The leave color, defoliation, stem color and pod color were also described in Supplementary methods. 3hai, 16hai and 27hai were collected 3 h, 16 h, 27 h after imbibition, respectively. Only embryos were harvested, frozen in liquid nitrogen, ground to a fine powder, and stored at −80 °C before RNA extraction. We collected three bio-replicates for each stage.

RNA-Seq library construction, sequencing, data processing, analysis, and the identification of coexpression modules

Total RNA was isolated from soybean embryos, using the PureLink Plant RNA Reagent (Thermo Scientific) and treated with RNase-free DNase I (QIAGEN). One ug of total RNA with a minimum RIN (RNA Integrity Number) value of 8 was employed for RNA-Seq library construction using the TruSeq Stranded Total RNA Library Prep Plant kit (Illumina). Hisat2 (Kim et al. 2019) was used to map sequenced reads to the soybean genome Wm82.a6.v1 (Espina et al. 2024). The read counts per gene were analyzed using HTSeq (Anders et al. 2015), followed by using DESeq2 (Love et al. 2014) to determine differentially expressed genes. The goseq package (Young et al. 2010) was used for soybean gene GO enrichment analysis with a cutoff FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg multiple testing correction). The WGCNA R package (Zhang and Horvath 2005) was employed to identify gene coexpression modules of highly correlated expressed genes using DESeq2 normalized data by following published methods (Zhan et al. 2015).

Arabidopsis growth conditions

The A. thaliana Columbia-0 (Col-0) ecotype seeds were sterilized by 50% (v/v) bleach with 0.1% (v/v) Triton X-100, and then washed four times with sterile water. After stratification at 4 °C in the dark for 3 days, seeds were sown on half-strength Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium containing 1% (w/v) sucrose, 0.8% (w/v) agar and incubated in a growth chamber. The growth chamber was controlled at 22°C, 150 μmol photons m−2 s−1, 60% relative humidity and 16 h light/8 h dark cycles.

Constructs and generation of transgenic plants

GmZAT18 and GmNAC099 were synthesized and ligated with pCAMBIA1302 vector to transfer to Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101 by floral-dip method. The seeds from transgenic plants were selected on half-strength MS agar medium with hygromycin till we obtained T3 lines.

Results

Transcriptome characteristics of developing and germinating soybean embryos

To understand the role of changes in transcript populations and levels that program soybean embryonic development from embryonic morphogenesis to post-germinative morphogenesis, we studied a detailed time course of the transcriptome across six developmental phases. These include 12 stages: (i) embryonic morphogenesis (cotyledon stage), (ii) storage reserve accumulation (early, mid-, and late maturation stage), (iii) desiccation (early, mid-, and late desiccation stage), (iv) dormancy, (v) germination (3hai, for germination phase I; 16hai for germination phase II; 27hai for germination III as radicle protrusion), and (vi) post-germinative morphogenesis (8-day seedling) (Fig. 1, Table 1). We performed RNA-seq using soybean embryos from desiccation throughout germination (Supplementary methods). Three biological replicates generated ~110 to 237 million read pairs per library, resulting in a high degree of reproducibility (Fig. S1, Table S1). We also retrieved embryo transcriptomes from the published dataset (GSE99571) (Jo et al. 2020), including stages in embryonic morphogenesis, storage reserve accumulation, and post-germination. We observed a strong correlation among replicates (Fig. S2). Together, RNA-Seq datasets used in our study exhibited high reproducibility, creating a comprehensive time course of embryonic developmental transcriptome for further analysis.

Distinct mRNA populations emerge from the late storage reserve accumulation stage through germination

To determine the temporal relationships of mRNA populations across different developmental stages, we conducted principal component analysis (PCA) and hierarchical clustering with correlation coefficients for all genes using FPKM (Fig. 2). Both analyses revealed strong similarities of the two group patterns. PCA analysis showed one group comprising stages before desiccation and after rehydration (from cot to mm and sdlg), while the other group consisted of stages undergoing desiccation and rehydration (Fig. 2a). Similarly, the clustering analysis dendrogram revealed two main clusters: one with green cotyledons and the other with yellow (Fig. 2b). The green cluster can be further divided into two sub-clusters: (i) before desiccation, including cot, em, and mm, and (ii) fully rehydrated, sdlg. The yellow cluster can be divided into two sub-clusters: (i) lm, 16hai and 27hai, and (ii) ed, md, ld, 3hai and dry. The two-major-cluster pattern signified substantial transcriptomic transitions during embryo development, possibly attributed to extensive programmed water loss, which morphologically begins in ed (Fig. 1b). Together, the results indicate temporally controlled transition processes from embryonic to post-germinative morphogenesis, marked by changes in the transcript population that primarily occur during desiccation to imbibition.

Transcriptomic landscapes during the transition from embryonic to post-germinative morphogenesis are characterized by highly prevalent mRNAs

In addition to the well-known transcripts encoding seed proteins (Goldberg et al. 1981), we sought to identify and characterize any other prevalent mRNAs during seed development. We uncovered highly abundant transcripts primarily from em to 16hai, revealing a diverse range of prevalence (Fig. 3a). For instance, in mm, 47% of the total mRNA molecules were transcribed from 11 genes, each producing at levels of > 10,000 FPKM, defined as superabundant genes (Fig. S3a). We identified 18 superabundant genes that were highly regulated with fluctuating transcript levels across development. Their expression patterns formed two groups: (i) the earlier superabundant gene group highly active from em to mm consisting of seed proteins, e.g., Kunitz trypsin inhibitor (KTi3), and (ii) the subsequent group, highly active from lm to 16hai, comprised proteins primarily associated with desiccation tolerance (Fig. S3a).

We next examined high prevalence genes, defined as genes with transcript levels > 1000 FPKM, and uncovered 127 high prevalence genes, representing 0.26% of total genes in the soybean genome—whose mRNA abundance increased in em and then dropped in 27hai (Fig. 3a). To understand the expression patterns of high prevalence genes, we applied unsupervised clustering analysis to reveal two major expression patterns similar to the superabundant genes: the first comprises 47 genes highly active from cot to lm (subgroup 1a, 39 genes) and during sdlg (a small subgroup 1b, eight genes), while the second includes 80 genes highly active from lm to 27hai (Fig. 3b). To characterize the major functions carried out by these two groups, we performed Gene Ontology (GO) analysis (FDR < 0.05) (Fig. 3c). We uncovered common dominant functions shared by the two major groups, including processes related to dormancy involved with ABA response, photosynthesis and response to abiotic stress. Additionally, we identified specific functions in each group, such as lipid storage in the first group and generation of precursor and energy in the second group. Interestingly, the “photosynthesis” function was enriched in subgroup 1b, where two genes were highly active from cot to em and then became highly prevalent again during sdlg. Meanwhile, other genes in the same subgroup 1b were not highly prevalent till sdlg. This may suggest various photosynthesis-related transcripts dominate from cot through sdlg. We further inspected the functions of lm-high-prevalence genes from the two major groups (Fig. S3b, Table S2). In group 1, the seven high-prevalence genes were primarily seed proteins. The functions of the remaining 37 lm-high-prevalence genes in the group 2 included desiccation tolerance (the largest category), as well as salvage and other functions. Therefore, lm exhibits a distinctive high-prevalence mRNA profile, spanning two groups, each with different major functions. Collectively, we identified a small set of specific genes highly transcribed during the transition, whose temporal expression patterns can be divided into early and late groups, carrying out distinct major functions in storage reserve accumulation and generation of precursor and energy, respectively, in addition to their shared functions such as desiccation tolerance.

em initiates transcription of genes related to storage reserves, desiccation, and dormancy

The accumulation of storage reserves in em may be triggered by sugar signals (Baier et al. 2004), as indicated by the enriched function “response to sucrose”, which led to the storage of lipids and carbohydrates, supported by the dominant functions such as lipid storage and starch biosynthesis observed in em. Additionally, seed proteins began to accumulate in protein storage vacuoles, as indicated by the significant functions of “protein targeting to vacuole” and “vacuole organization”, although mRNAs encoding seed proteins did not become superabundant until the following mm stage (Fig. 4, Fig. S3b). Meanwhile, diverse upregulated genes enriched in ABA response functions were consecutively active. These included enriched functions related to abiotic stress responses, e.g., "response to water deprivation", and dormancy, e.g., "seed dormancy process" containing dormancy-related genes ASG5 (Bassel et al. 2011) and HUB2 (Liu et al. 2007) (Table S4). Together, these results suggest that preparation for desiccation and dormancy begins in em as the embryo starts to accumulate storage reserves.

mm-upregulated genes are involved in both transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory events

The superabundant genes in mm may result from active transcriptional regulatory processes, as indicated by the mm-enriched function "regulation of transcription", which was also enriched in 16hai and sdlg. Most genes in this mm GO term were transcription factors (299 out of 424 genes), and these TF genes are enriched in epigenetic functions (e.g., “histone modification”) in addition to diverse developmental processes (Table S5), suggesting that TFs in mm may also target epigenetic-related genes. Additionally, epigenetic activities, such as "epigenetic regulation of gene expression" and “chromatin silencing” (Fig. 5a) were significant in the upregulated genes in mm, indicating epigenetic mechanisms are involved in modulating gene activities in mm. To understand what epigenetic events were active in mm, we manually annotated genes in the epigenetic-related GO terms in mm. We found most genes are associated with the two epigenetic hallmarks, chromatin and DNA methylation (Fig. 5b, Table S6a). The DNA methylation-related genes included (i) DNA demethylase (e.g., ROS1) and (ii) genes involved in de novo RNA-directed DNA methylation pathway (RdDM, e.g., IDN2). Chromatin-related genes contained three major groups: (i) chromatin writers, such as H3K27, H3K4 and H3K9 methyltransferase (e.g., SWN, SUVH1 and SUVR5, respectively); (ii) chromatin erasers, including histone deacetylase (e.g., SRT2), H3K27me3 demethylase (e.g., REF6) and H3K9 demethylase (e.g., IBM1); (iii) chromatin readers/remodelers, such as ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler (e.g., BRM) and transcriptional mediator (e.g., MOM1). The active chromatin-related function may contribute to seed development, as demonstrated by HUB2, which is in the mm-enriched GO term "seed dormancy process" and plays a role in seed dormancy through histone H2B monoubiquitination (Liu et al. 2007) (Table S4). Thus, in mm, gene activities are regulated at both the transcriptional and the epigenetic levels, potentially working synergistically to advance the developmental transition.

Epigenetic-related genes upregulated before and after desiccation and dormancy differ

During germination (3hai and 27hai) and post-germination, the epigenetic-related GO term “nucleosome assembly” became dominant which is distinct from the epigenetic-related GO terms in mm (Fig 5a). The upregulated genes in these GO terms were annotated as histone-core related proteins, such as histone chaperone, histone protein, and histone variants (e.g., HIS1-3) which are essential for developmental transitions (Jiang and Berger 2023) (Fig 5b, Table S6b). Intriguingly, there were more and different histone chaperone-related genes in 27hai compared to 3hai, (e.g., FAS1 and NRP2) (Fig. 5c, Table S6b), suggesting different histone variants are involved in different germination stages. Together, the key categories of epigenetic-related genes that dominate in mm are distinct from those during germination and post-germination.

lm activates diverse scavenge and protection systems at the transcription level before desiccation

The lm-stage embryos triggered internal changes through intercellular communication to transition from reserve accumulation to desiccation, highlighted by the significant function "cell communication" (Fig. 4). This included genes involved in transport, hormone signaling pathways (e.g., ABA), kinases, and drought response (Table S4). The lm embryos shut down the mitochondrial electron transport chain, signified by the upregulated gene ETFQO, an alternative substrate of the mitochondrial electron transport chain (Ishizaki et al. 2005). This occurred under the enriched function of "response to absence of light". Meanwhile, the lm embryo utilized an alternative energy source for acetyl-CoA production by using branched-chain amino acids (Gipson et al. 2017), as indicated by the enriched function of "leucine catabolic process”. The dominant function of “protein folding” began in lm, indicating the activation of macromolecule protection, while many functions related to salvage systems were also dominant in lm, together suggesting the preparation of lm embryos for the upcoming desiccation. For instance, the ROS response functions, including “response to hydrogen peroxide” and the antioxidant production function of “carotenoid biosynthetic process”, were active in mm. Furthermore, multifunctional molecules or pathways that contribute to desiccation tolerance were also upregulated in lm. For example, the Fe-S proteins, under the enriched function of “iron-sulfur cluster assembly”, play roles in electron transfer shutdown. They were also involved in disulfide reduction to maintain ROS homeostasis and dehydration (Johnson et al. 2005) (Fig. 4). Another example is the detoxification function of the “methylglyoxal (MG) catabolic process”, where MG signaling may confer desiccation tolerance (Hoque et al. 2016). These pathways, along with others, contribute to cascading the building up of the desiccation tolerance signals. For example, the “protein dephosphorylation” function, which includes protein phosphatase genes like ABI1 involved in ABA signal transduction, played a role in this process (Table S4). Together, in the late reserve accumulation stage, the embryo accumulates a variety of molecules to withstand the upcoming desiccation processes.

ed elicits the transcription of genes involved in diverse signal transduction pathways associated with desiccation tolerance

Embryos began shrinking in ed, underscored by the enrichment of cell wall remodeling functions. For instance, the “plant-type cell wall modification” function included genes involved in calcium-dependent cell wall loosening (e.g., ANN5) and remodeling biomechanical properties of the cell wall (e.g., pectin methylesterases, such as CWINV2) (Table S4). The "response to hypoxia” function became active in ed, indicating a response to the absence of photosynthetic oxygen supply due to photosystem shutdown (Rolletschek et al. 2005). In ed, the activation of scavenging of ROS-related functions persisted after lm, whereas the antioxidants and protective systems different from those in lm were invoked, such as the “flavonoid biosynthetic process” and the “postreplication repair” (Fig. 4, Table S3). Signal transduction function remained dominant, including small GTPase-mediated signal transduction and lipid signaling phosphatidylinositol (Table S4), which presumably contributes to the desiccation tolerance (Gasulla et al. 2013). Together, embryos in early desiccation employ various signal transduction pathways to trigger diverse cellular responses, such as continuing to activate different salvage and protection systems to cope with desiccation processes.

ld accumulates transcripts for utilization upon germination

In ld, functions related to salvage and protection continue to be dominant, such as “response to hydrogen peroxide” and “response to endoplasmic reticulum stress”, which involve protein restoration, modification, and transportation. Notably, several enriched functions in the mRNAs of ld and the subsequent dry stage illustrate preparation for biological processes during germination, including “protein import to nucleus” and “translation” in ld, and “transcription by RNA polymerase II” in dry. Furthermore, mRNAs related to “protein targeting to mitochondrion" in ld and “generation of precursor metabolites and energy” in dry were significantly accumulated. Together, the ld and dry stage embryos accumulate resources and transcripts that poise the seeds to quickly restart transcription and translation machinery and produce energy for germination.

16hai activates genes involved in signal transduction and various metabolic pathways associated with development

Signal transduction functions were significant, including the “second-messenger-mediated signaling” to receive extracellular signals for germination and the “response to sucrose” to boost seedling growth (Baier et al. 2004). Additionally, calcium plays dual roles as both a second messenger and a structural component in cell walls (Thor 2019), which aligns with the dominant functions of “calcium ion transport” and cell wall formation, such as “lignin metabolic process” (e.g., gene UGT72E1) and “cellulose biosynthetic process” (e.g., gene CSLB04) (Table S4). The key regulator COBRA, involved in directional cell elongation (Roudier et al. 2005), was upregulated in the dominant function of “polysaccharide biosynthetic process”. This upregulation was accompanied by the “cytoskeleton organization” function, working together to support radical expansion (Fig. 4, Table S3 and S4). Notably, several functions were enriched in 16hai, ahead of axis protrusion in 27hai. For instance, the light-responsive genes like CRY2 (Table S4), involved in photomorphogenesis (Ponnu et al. 2019), were upregulated under the GO function “response to light stimulus”. Additionally, the function of “glycine decarboxylation via the glycine cleavage system” was active to detoxify by-products of photosynthesis, which precedes the 27hai-enriched function of “photorespiration”. Functions related to various metabolite biosynthetic pathways were also enriched, including phytohormones (e.g., GA, ethylene, and BR), the mobilization of nutrition reserves (e.g., “proteasomal protein catabolic process” function), and energy production processes (e.g., the dominant functions of “glycolysis” and “gluconeogenesis”). These processes generated acetyl-CoA (i.e., the enriched “acetyl-CoA metabolic process” function), which then enters the TCA cycle for “ATP biosynthetic process”, as shown in the GO term. Meanwhile, many developmental programs (e.g., leaf and root development) commenced in 16hai and the same functions were also enriched in the subsequent 27hai and sdlg (Fig. 4). Together, genes involved in ATP production are activated sequentially, along with various signaling pathways, to support the initiation and progression of developmental processes.

27hai activates genes related to cell cycles, chloroplast functions, and mitochondrial functions associated with radial protrusion

In 27hai embryos, the active function of “mitotic G1 phase” (Fig. 4, Table S3) critical for cell determination, signified the resumption of the cell division (Ma et al. 2015). This had been halted during mm, as indicated by the enriched “cell cycle process” function. Functions related to chloroplast and mitochondrial function restoration were also enriched, such as “photosynthesis” and “aerobic respiration”. Meanwhile, various developmental programs continued to be activated. Together, 27hai embryos activate genes to resume mitochondrial respiration and chloroplast function to support rapid cell division and differentiation for seedling development.

Specific genes are preferentially active in distinct developmental stages

We determined genes that are either active exclusively or at elevated levels at one developmental stage as stage-specific up-regulated genes (Fig. S4), defined as genes up-regulated (DESeq2 with FDR < 0.05 and > 2-fold increase) in one stage when compared with all other stages in pairwise comparisons. We found that the stages with the highest numbers of stage-specific up-regulated genes and TFs are 27hai and the subsequent sdlg, while the fewest are observed during desiccation. Between the two morphogenesis stages, 4055 genes (8% of the genes in the soybean genome), including 374 TFs, were transcribed in a stage-specific manner. The results suggest that a small number of TFs, uniquely upregulated at specific stages, are likely involved in organizing unique gene circuits to perform distinct functions for driving seed development.

Identification of gene coexpression modules throughout seed embryo development

To characterize the sequential activation of gene regulatory processes throughout seed development, we identified co-expressed gene sets using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) on regulated genes (DESeq2 with FDR < 0.05 and > 2-fold change, across pairwise comparisons). A total of 41,891 out of 48,387 genes (~87% of the genes in the soybean genome) in the genome were organized into 16 coexpression modules (M1–M16) (Fig. 6a and b). We analyzed the Z-scores of genes within each module across developmental stages to reveal coordinated gene expression patterns within each module (Fig. S5). Subsequently, we performed a permutation test to demonstrate that the average topological overlap of the 16 modules is significantly higher than that of randomly sampled modules (p-value < 0.005; Table S7). Therefore, our analyses affirmed the reliability of the constructed regulatory modules. Together, embryo developmental processes are orchestrated by most genes in the soybean genome, which are quantitatively regulated over time and organized into co-expression groups to drive sequential biological processes essential for seed embryo development.

The gene modules are associated with temporal expression programs

To understand the relationships between gene modules and developmental stages, we calculated correlation coefficients to inspect module-stage associations (Fig. 6a). The 16 coexpression modules were divided into two groups. The first group, consisting of modules M1–M7, showed a relatively high correlation with stages from cot to lm. In contrast, the second group, comprising modules M8–M16, was associated with stages from lm through desiccation to sdlg. We then examined whether any module was associated with a specific stage. We found that ten modules were highly associated with a single stage, each with a correlation coefficient of at least 0.7 (M1, M2, M3, M4, M5, M7, M11, M12, M13 and M16). For instance, M4 is highly associated with mm (r = 0.96) suggesting functions conducted by genes interconnected in M4 are primarily operated in mm. In contrast, six modules showed similar correlation values between at least two stages, or one stage had a higher correlation than the others, with a correlation coefficient between 0.25 and 0.7 (M6, M8, M9, M10, M14 and M15). For example, M9 is active throughout desiccation stages (ed, md, and ld with r values 0.34, 0.42 and 0.59, respectively), indicating a continuum of desiccation processes and that the genes in this module perform functions that work across all three desiccation stages. Together, these results indicate that a module may perform crucial functions specific to one developmental stage or carry out shared functions needed across multiple stages.

The stage-associated modules are enriched with stage-specific up-regulated genes

We assessed the consistency between the assignment of genes to the coexpression modules and the stage-based patterns of mRNA accumulation by examining the overlap between these modules and the previously identified stage-specific up-regulated gene sets (Fig. 6c). The results revealed that most modules contain up-regulated genes specific to one stage. For instance, M1, M3, M4, M7, M9, M11, M13 and M16 each contained up-regulated genes from a single stage. However, M9 included specific up-regulated genes in md and ld. Similarly, M10 included dry-, and 3hai-specific up-regulated genes. The overlap between each module and the corresponding stage-specific up-regulated gene set was greater than expected by chance (hypergeometric test p-value < 0.01), indicating a specific subset of coexpressed genes within each module is associated with stage-specific functions. Together, these results suggest each stage is associated with one or more gene modules that trigger stage-specific gene regulatory processes and program the development within one stage.

Genes conducting specific functions are enriched in stage-associated modules

Genes within modules perform distinct functions that drive various biological processes and we employed a hypergeometric test to assess the relationship between temporal coexpression modules and genes linked to specific functions involved in the developmental transition from embryonic morphogenesis to post-germination (Fig. 6d, Table S8). We examined storage reserve genes and found that genes involved in fatty acid biosynthesis and seed protein production were significantly enriched in mm-associated module M4. High prevalence genes (> 1000 FPKM) were notably enriched in mm-associated M4, lm-associated M7 and M8, and ld-associated M9. Additionally, lm-associated M8 and ld-associated M9 were enriched with genes encoding late embryogenesis abundant (LEA) proteins or involved in desiccation (detailed methods for identifying these genes are described in the Supplementary methods). Longevity-associated genes were enriched in lm-associated M7 and dry-associated M10. Collectively, the results suggest that genes with specific functions are interconnected in gene modules, working together to drive developmental transitions.

Variations in major functions among sequentially activated TFs in the desiccation-associated gene module

We sampled transcriptomes from three sequential desiccation stages—ed, md, and ld—each with well-characterized seed morphology (see Supplementary methods). Our module-stage association analysis revealed that only one module, M9, is primarily associated with these desiccation stages (Fig. 6a). One possibility is that the overall similarity in expression patterns of regulated genes across the desiccation stages led to no distinguishable modules that meet the significant p-values and correlations used for the early, mid-, and late stages. The module M9 is linked to ed, md, and ld, and the correlation coefficients differ, suggesting that some genes in M9 have expression patterns that fluctuate across the consecutive desiccation stages. Module M9 was enriched with functions related to highly prevalent mRNAs, LEA proteins, and desiccation (Fig. 6d), which became active throughout the desiccation stages (Fig. S6a). Furthermore, we examined the activities of TF genes in module M9 by conducting a hierarchical clustering analysis on the expression patterns of 511 TFs in M9. The TFs in M9 were clustered into three groups (Fig. S6b). TF genes were activated consecutively from ed to ld, with the number of active TFs increasing from 53 in ed, to 97 in md, and reaching 361 in ld. This trend of increasing TF numbers during the progression of desiccation aligned with the rise in the r value from 0.34 to 0.42, and then to 0.59. GO analysis showed that there are shared major functions among the three TF groups, such as “response to water deprivation” between groups II and III (Fig. 7). Furthermore, distinct dominant functions were observed among the three TF groups, with group I being more related to abiotic stress and group III related to developmental processes. Together, some TFs are active throughout all stages of desiccation, performing essential functions needed at every desiccation stage. By contrast, other TFs have specific roles at individual stages, contributing to unique functions that drive desiccation progression or prepare the seed for developmental programs that will be invoked later during germination.

The majority of TFs activated early in the desiccation-associated gene module are involved in the abiotic stress response

We examined gene function by manually annotating 53 TF genes in group I, which were primarily active in ed (Fig. S6), to reveal that more than half were associated with abiotic stress response (Fig. 8a and b, Table S9). Remarkably, GmABI5, a key regulator contributing to desiccation tolerance, longevity, and dormancy (Zinsmeister et al. 2016), was among the M9-group I TFs (Fig. 8a). Additionally, genes involved in the biosynthetic pathway of the raffinose family of oligosaccharides (RFOs, associated with seed maturity) (Fig. S7), were also present in the same M9. This aligns with the fact that Arabidopsis ABI5 regulates the biosynthesis of RFOs (Zinsmeister et al. 2016). Among the 53 TFs in M9 group1, GmZAT18 (Glyma.13G133000) was unique in that it became highly active in ed followed by a precipitous decrease in ld (Fig. 8c, Fig. S8a). Interestingly, its Arabidopsis ortholog AtZAT18 (At3g53600) was not active from cot to desiccation stage (post-mature green, pmg) (Fig. S9a). To assess the potential role of GmZAT18 in soybean early desiccation, we created GmZAT18 overexpression (OE) lines in Arabidopsis. We did not observe obvious differences in the timing of seed desiccation between the GmZAT18 OE lines and WT. However, seeds from the two OE lines were smaller in length, width, and weight compared to WT seeds (Fig. S10a and b). Under normal conditions, the germination rates of radicle emergence and green cotyledon formation were similar between the OE lines and WT (Fig. 8d, Fig. S9b). In contrast, under osmotic stress, the germination rates for both radicle emergence and green cotyledons in the two OE lines were significantly higher than those of WT (Fig. 8d, linear mixed-effects model p < 0.01, see supplementary methods for statistical analysis, Fig. S9b). We examined the expression levels of GmZAT18 in the two OE lines and found that mRNA levels were relatively higher in line #18 compared to line #13 (Fig. S10c). The results demonstrate that GmZAT18 OE seeds had higher germination percentages than WT seeds under osmotic stress derived from mannitol (Fig. 8d, Fig. S9b), suggesting that GmZAT18 may contribute to conferring desiccation tolerance during the early desiccation stage in soybean.

Desiccation continuum in the desiccation-associated gene module

The 97 TFs in group II of the M9 module were primarily activated in md following group I TFs and function-enriched analysis suggested shared functions and distinct functions conducted by TFs between the two groups (Fig. 7 and 9a, Fig. S6). Among them, GmNAC099 (Glyma.13G243200) was unique for its high activity in md than any other stages, including sdlg (Fig. 9b, Fig. S8b and S11). Notably, its Arabidopsis ortholog At1g61110 was not active during desiccation (pmg) (Fig. S11). We generated Arabidopsis GmNAC099 OE lines to assess the gene functions. We did not observe a noticeable difference in the timing of seed desiccation between the GmNAC099 OE lines. However, when characterizing seed phenotypes, we found differences between the two OE lines compared to the WT: in general, seed length, width, and weight were larger than WT for OE line #5, while line #13 had smaller seeds than WT (Fig. S10a and b). Although the germination rates for radicle emergence and green cotyledons were similar between the OE lines and WT under normal conditions, the OE lines showed significantly higher germination rates under osmotic stress (Fig. 9c, Fig. 9d, linear mixed-effects model p < 0.01, see supplementary methods for statistical analysis). We analyzed the transcript levels of GmNAC099 in the two transformed lines and found that expression levels in line #13 were relatively higher than in line #5 (Fig. S10c). The results showed that GmNAC099 overexpression led to higher germination percentages in seeds subjected to osmotic stress derived from mannitol compared with WT seeds (Fig. 9c and d). The results suggest that GmNAC099 may be involved in the process of acquiring desiccation tolerance during the mid-desiccation stage. Together, our results showed different TFs are activated in succession to perform functions essential for acquiring desiccation tolerance and advancing desiccation processes, to ensure a desiccation continuum.

Expression patterns of soybean TFs in the desiccation-associated module are largely different from their Arabidopsis orthologs

The two functionally tested soybean TFs in desiccation-associated module M9 have their Arabidopsis orthologs that are not active during the Arabidopsis desiccation stage (Fig. S9a and S11). Therefore, we investigated the degree to which the expression patterns of M9 TFs differ from those of their Arabidopsis orthologs. In contrast to soybean TFs in M9, which are highly active during desiccation stages, their Arabidopsis orthologs showed different and diverse expression patterns that are active in various seed developmental stages in addition to the Arabidopsis desiccation stage (Fig. S6). We examined how many of these Arabidopsis orthologs are repressed during Arabidopsis desiccation stage. Among the 53 soybean TFs in M9 group I, 17% of their 35 Arabidopsis orthologs (6 TFs) were repressed in desiccation (pmg), including two that remained inactive or undetectable from the linear cotyledon stage to dry (Fig. S9a, Table S10a and b). In addition, 36% (21 of 58 genes) and 57% (81 of 142 genes) of the Arabidopsis TFs orthologous to the soybean TFs active in the soybean M9 TF groups II and III were inactive during the Arabidopsis desiccation (Fig. S11, Table S10a, c and d). The results reveal that during desiccation, while certain orthologous TFs between soybean and Arabidopsis are active in both species, a significant number of TF orthologs differ in their expression patterns between the two species. This expression disparity becomes more pronounced during late desiccation in soybean. Together, our data highlight significant divergence in the regulatory circuits guiding desiccation programs between soybean and Arabidopsis.

Discussion

We determined that various genes are sequentially activated across developmental stages to perform diverse functions, progressing embryonic to post-germinative morphogenesis in soybean. We leverage the longer desiccation period in soybean (about 20 days) compared to Arabidopsis (about 2 days) to thoroughly investigate the desiccation process. This allows us to analyze the process in early, mid-, and late desiccation stages, providing a comprehensive understanding of gene regulatory networks programming desiccation. Additionally, we found their Arabidopsis orthologs have different expression patterns. This study offers new insights into seed embryo development through various distinct morpho-physiological stages in crop seeds.

Genes involved in the desiccation process become active as early as the reserve accumulation stage

Embryos prepare for desiccation in em by activating genes involved in desiccation tolerance. Meanwhile, lipid and starch biosynthesis genes become active, followed by seed protein genes active in mm. Some genes involved in desiccation tolerance are upregulated in em, well before noticeable morphological changes, such as shrinking seed size, occur in ed. Desiccation is a harsh condition that may require embryos to gradually activate desiccation-tolerance genes early on, allowing them to slowly adjust their cell physiology in preparation for programmed water loss. Alternatively, some desiccation-tolerance genes may help cells adapt to the physiological changes caused by accumulating storage reserves, starting in em. These possibilities may not be mutually exclusive and require further study.

The transcript profile in lm is unique

The distinct functions of reserve accumulation and energy production are dominant in stages before and after lm, respectively, while both functions are dominant during lm (Fig. 3). Our module-stage analysis shows that modules M6, M7, and M8 are preferentially active in lm. Module M6 is also associated with mm but has a lower correlation coefficient (0.52) compared to lm (0.64); module M7 is highly associated with lm (r = 0.97); module M8 is also associated with ed but has a lower r value of 0.26 relative to lm (0.59) (Fig. 6). This suggests that the lm plays a dual role—one in accumulating storage reserves and another in preparing for large-scale desiccation processes. This detailed observation cannot be observed in Arabidopsis studies of transcription changes during embryo development due to the brief and overlapping periods for reserve accumulation and desiccation (Leprince et al. 2017). These new findings suggest several possible mechanisms for transcriptional regulation operating in these modules. One is that a major TF could trigger both gene networks M6 and M8 for storage reserve accumulation and desiccation. Alternatively, different upstream TFs may activate M6 and M8 in lm. The TFs and their cognate cis-elements that trigger the distinct biological processes remain to be determined.

Diverse transcription factors are activated sequentially during germination to conduct various developmental programs

Various functions enriched in sdlg become dominant in 27hai or even earlier in 16hai, suggesting the primary regulatory decisions in sdlg are made during germination through the sequential activation of diverse genes contributing to the same developmental processes. These results suggest that most seedling differentiation programs are activated de novo during germination, rather than being encoded in stored mRNAs in dry seeds, consistent with previous study (Comai and Harada 1990). Seedling differentiation programs may be driven by transcriptional cascades initiated by TFs whose mRNAs have accumulated in dry seeds. Further investigation is needed to examine the TFs involved in seedling differentiation, their upstream TFs, and whether the mRNAs of these TFs accumulate in dry seeds. Meanwhile, transcripts involved in epigenetic events also dominate in germination. How these regulatory layers cooperate and which genes in the soybean genome are controlled by these synergistic operations to program germination and seedling growth remain unanswered.

The gene regulatory networks governing desiccation in soybean and Arabidopsis may be divergent

The operation of module M9 significantly initiates and persists from early to mid- and late desiccation, implying a continuum of desiccation processes. GmZAT18 is primarily active in early desiccation stage and the OE lines in Arabidopsis show increased tolerance to osmotic stress, similar to Arabidopsis with overexpressed AtZAT18, initially characterized as abiotic stress-induced genes in somatic tissues (Yin et al. 2017). In soybean, GmZAT18 is induced by drought and heat stress in somatic tissues (Wang et al. 2018). Future research is needed to assess whether GmZAT18 and other TFs are regulated by the interaction of ABI5 and chromatin changes, similar to the case during germination, remains to be determined (Wang et al. 2021). The expression pattern of GmNAC099 is distinct from its Arabidopsis counterpart, which is silenced during Arabidopsis desiccation. Previous studies showed that GmNAC099 is more active in soybean root tissues in drought-tolerant cultivar than drought-sensitive one under both normal and drought-stress conditions (Thao et al. 2013). However, to the best of our knowledge, the drought-tolerance role in the Arabidopsis counterpart has not been demonstrated.

The GmZAT18 and GmNAC099 OE lines showed increased tolerance to osmotic stress derived from mannitol, but there was no significant difference in desiccation timing between the OE lines and the WT in Arabidopsis. The GmZAT18 transcript levels varied between the two OE lines. Both GmZAT18 OE lines produced smaller seeds than the WT, which may be due to a potential trade-off between plant growth and environmental stress response (Leisner et al. 2023). However, GmNAC099 OE line #5 produced larger seeds than the WT, while line #13 produced smaller seeds. Besides, the GmNAC099 transcript levels were lower in line #5 than in #13. The increased tolerance to osmotic stress in the GmZAT18 and GmNAC099 OE lines may be due to the accumulation of specific compounds like non-reducing sugars, antioxidants, or enzymes that protect cells from the harmful effects of water loss (Leprince et al. 2017; Saldanha et al. 2022). Further research is needed to identify the target genes of the overexpressed TFs, which could explain how they contribute to both enhanced osmotic stress tolerance and the variation in seed size.

Nearly half of the Arabidopsis TFs that are orthologous to the highly active soybean TFs integrated into the desiccation-associated module M9 are inactive during Arabidopsis desiccation. This suggests there may be differences in desiccation-associated regulatory networks between soybean and Arabidopsis. Additionally, the involvement of more TFs in desiccation processes could complicate gene interconnections and potentially enable soybeans to endure a longer desiccation period compared with Arabidopsis—about 20 days versus two days, respectively. Soybean has undergone at least two rounds of whole-genome duplication, resulting in its modern genome, which may explain the higher number of genes and TF genes than Arabidopsis (Schmutz et al. 2010). Additionally, duplicated genes in soybean could lead to subfunctionalization and/or neofunctionalization (Lin et al. 2010). These TF genes may be subject to environmental selection, differing from what occurred in Arabidopsis. As a result, these large numbers of TF genes may be activated sequentially over time, contributing to an extended desiccation period that enhances the plant's fitness to adapt to its environment. A key challenge in understanding desiccation processes is to unravel how genomic cis-regulatory elements and their transcription factors are integrated into gene regulatory networks that program desiccation.

A variety of genes involved in epigenetic events are active in mm and germination

DNA methylation levels in the CHH context are known to increase in maturation (Lin et al. 2017), which aligns with our finding that mm-upregulated genes are enriched for functions related to epigenetic events, including those involved in CHH methylation. Additionally, other epigenetic-related genes, such as those linked to chromatin remodelers and diverse histone modifications, are also upregulated. It is known that epigenetic mechanisms like histone modifications are involved in activating seed protein and fatty acid biosynthesis genes in Phaseolus and Arabidopsis seeds (Huang et al. 2022; Sundaram et al. 2013). What genes in the soybean genome are regulated directly at the epigenetic level during seed development remains to be determined. During germination, upregulated genes related to core histone proteins were dominant, including histone variants and histone chaperones that replace core histone proteins with variants. The functions of epigenetic events enriched in upregulated genes during germination are distinct from those in the mid-reserve accumulation stage, whose upregulated epigenetic-related genes conduct functions of DNA (de)methylation and chromatin remodeling. These results revealed by transcriptome datasets highlight the potential distinct epigenetic regulation mechanisms employed to control biological processes in embryos, whether they are preparing to shut down or undergoing high levels of cell division. It is known that epigenetic events may correlate with the cell cycle rate, such as H3.1, which is incorporated during the S-phase and remains at high levels in rapidly dividing cells signified by a changed H3.1/H3.3 ratio (Otero et al. 2016). In our analysis, we found that upregulated genes during germination include H3.1 (Table S6b). A major challenge for future research will be to uncover which genetic regulatory networks and epigenetic events interact, and how these factors work together to control seed development.

Conclusion

We profiled the temporal transcriptome of soybean embryos from embryonic morphogenesis through post-germination. Our enrichment analysis of upregulated genes indicates both transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory levels are active during the mid-reserve accumulation stage, with TFs targeting epigenetic-related genes and gene expression being modulated by epigenetic regulation, suggesting a coordinated interaction between these distinct regulatory levels. The enrichment analysis based on the RNA-seq data shows that chromatin events active during the mid-reserve accumulation stage involve DNA methylation and chromatin remodeling, while epigenetic events during germination involve core histone variants and histone chaperones. Gene co-expression network analysis shows that certain modules are restricted to specific developmental stages, highlighting distinct functions, whereas others extend across multiple stages, indicating functions that are crucial throughout several developmental stages. The desiccation-associated gene module incorporates various TFs that are sequentially active across different desiccation stages. While these TFs share similar primary functions, those related to abiotic stress responses are more prevalent during early desiccation, whereas developmental processes become more prominent during late desiccation. Two TFs are highly active during the early and mid-desiccation stages, respectively, while their Arabidopsis orthologs are inactive during Arabidopsis desiccation. To reveal the functions of these two TFs in soybean desiccation, they are overexpressed in Arabidopsis to uncover their potential roles in conferring desiccation tolerance. Furthermore, we found nearly half of the Arabidopsis TFs orthologous to highly active soybean TFs during soybean desiccation remain inactive during Arabidopsis desiccation. We conclude that the major epigenetic mechanisms involved in the seed maturation program and germination are different and that the gene networks that operate during late embryo development are divergent between soybean and Arabidopsis.

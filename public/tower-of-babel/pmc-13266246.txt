VISTA neutralization by immunization reprograms immunosuppression and augments vaccine efficacy in renal carcinoma
Authors: Jiawei Wang, Zhenzhen Wang, Wanting Zhao, Haomin Chen, Bowen Lu, Yingxiang Shao, Yanyan Zheng, Shanshan Liu, Meng Wang, Lin Fang, Huizhong Li, Praveen Neeli, Hui Tian, Gang Wang, Dafei Chai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRenal carcinoma remains a highly lethal malignancy, and tumor vaccine efficacy is frequently hampered by a profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. V-domain Ig suppressor of T-cell activation (VISTA), an inhibitory immune checkpoint enriched in myeloid cells and regulatory T cells (Tregs), represents a critical barrier to effective antitumor immunity. Targeting VISTA may therefore provide a promising strategy to overcome immune suppression and enhance tumor vaccine efficacy.MethodsRecombinant adenoviral vaccines encoding carbonic anhydrase IX (CAIX) and VISTA (Ad-CAIX and Ad-VISTA) were constructed and validated for efficient antigen expression. The antitumor efficacy of Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization was evaluated in subcutaneous, lung metastatic, orthotopic, and anti-programmed cell death protein 1-resistant renal carcinoma models. Vaccine-induced immune responses were assessed by flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, ELISA, cell proliferation assays, cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) assays, and in vivo immune cell depletion experiments.ResultsAd-VISTA immunization markedly potentiated the therapeutic efficacy of CAIX-targeted vaccination, resulting in significant tumor growth inhibition and prolonged survival across multiple renal carcinoma models. Mechanistically, VISTA-targeting remodeled the tumor microenvironment by enhancing the infiltration and activation of dendritic cells (DCs) and CD8+ T cells while reducing immunosuppressive myeloid cells and Tregs. Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization promoted the expansion and maturation of multiple DC subsets, characterized by increased expression of CD40, CD80, CD86, and major histocompatibility complex molecules, thereby facilitating efficient antigen presentation. VISTA immunization elicited robust antigen-specific antibody and neutralizing responses, restored CD8+ T-cell proliferation, and enhanced CTL-mediated tumor cell killing. Depletion of CD8+ T cells abrogated therapeutic efficacy of Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccine, establishing its essential role in tumor control. Furthermore, combined vaccination induced durable memory CD8+ T-cell responses capable of preventing tumor recurrence on rechallenge.ConclusionsThese results indicated that vaccine-induced VISTA blockade effectively amplifies DC-mediated CD8+ T-cell immunity and synergistically enhances tumor vaccine efficacy. Targeting VISTA represents a promising immunotherapeutic strategy for improving vaccine-based treatments in renal carcinoma.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Tumor vaccines targeting carbonic anhydrase IX (CAIX) are a promising strategy for renal carcinoma but are frequently limited by an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that impairs effective T-cell responses. V-domain Ig suppressor of T-cell activation (VISTA) is an inhibitory immune checkpoint enriched in myeloid cells and regulatory T cells that contributes to tumor immune suppression and resistance to checkpoint blockade. Adenoviral vaccine platforms can induce VISTA-neutralizing antibodies and potent CAIX-specific CD8+ T-cell immunity, enabling simultaneous targeting of tumor antigens and immunoregulatory pathways.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This study shows that adenoviral targeting of VISTA markedly enhances the efficacy of CAIX-based vaccination in renal carcinoma. VISTA-expressing adenovirus (Ad-VISTA) vaccination remodels the tumor microenvironment by promoting dendritic cell (DC) maturation and antigen presentation, thereby activating robust CD8+ T-cell responses. Co-immunization with Ad-VISTA/CAIX induces multifunctional and durable memory CD8+ T cells while reducing immunosuppressive myeloid cells and regulatory T cells. Notably, Ad-VISTA vaccination generates neutralizing antibodies against VISTA that restore CD8+ T-cell proliferation and cytotoxicity. The combined vaccine demonstrates potent antitumor activity across subcutaneous, orthotopic, lung metastatic, and anti-programmed cell death protein 1-resistant renal carcinoma models.

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY

This study identifies VISTA as a promising immunomodulatory target for improving tumor vaccine efficacy in renal carcinoma. By enhancing DC-mediated antigen presentation and relieving immune checkpoint-mediated suppression, adenoviral targeting of VISTA amplifies vaccine-induced antitumor immunity. These findings provide a framework for next-generation cancer vaccines that combine tumor antigens with immune checkpoint modulation and may help overcome resistance to immune checkpoint blockade.

Introduction

Renal carcinoma is a highly heterogeneous malignancy with steadily rising global incidence and limited therapeutic options for patients with advanced disease.13 Although immune checkpoint blockade, particularly targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), has improved clinical outcomes, a substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve durable responses due to profound immune suppression within the tumor microenvironment (TME).46 Tumor vaccines, which can prime robust and antigen-specific T-cell responses, represent a promising complementary strategy7 8; however, their clinical efficacy in renal carcinoma has been constrained by insufficient dendritic cell (DC) activation, poor antigen cross-presentation, and the presence of dominant myeloid-derived inhibitory pathways.9 10 Overcoming these barriers is essential to unlock the full therapeutic potential of tumor vaccines in renal carcinoma.

VISTA (V-domain Ig suppressor of T-cell activation), an immune checkpoint regulator enriched in myeloid cells and regulatory T cells (Tregs), has emerged as a central mediator of T-cell tolerance and an orchestrator of suppressive myeloid programming.11 12 Unlike PD-1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), VISTA is preferentially expressed on tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells and maintains immunosuppression even under inflammatory or interferon (IFN)-rich environments,13 14 making it a particularly challenging barrier for vaccine-induced immunity. Recent mechanistic studies have further revealed that VISTA is uniquely engaged under acidic conditions characteristic of the TME, where histidine-dependent interactions with PSGL-1 or VSIG-3 selectively suppress T-cell activation.1517 Antibodies engineered to disrupt these pH-dependent interactions can effectively restore antitumor immunity in vivo, underscoring the therapeutic promise of context-restricted VISTA blockade.18 19 Preclinical studies have shown that genetic or pharmacologic blockade of VISTA can reprogram the TME, enhance DC maturation, and restore CD8+ T-cell effector function.2022 However, whether VISTA inhibition can be efficiently achieved through immunization to synergize with tumor vaccines remains largely unexplored.

Carbonic anhydrase IX (CAIX), a hypoxia-induced membrane antigen highly expressed in renal carcinoma,23 is an attractive target for therapeutic vaccination due to its tumor specificity and immunogenicity.24 25 Nevertheless, CAIX-directed immune responses generated by vaccination alone often fail to overcome the suppressive myeloid milieu of renal carcinoma.26 27 Therefore, strategies capable of simultaneously relieving myeloid-driven immunoregulation are likely required to unlock the full potential of CAIX-based vaccination. In this context, immunization-driven generation of VISTA-neutralizing antibodies provides a particularly advantageous approach, as vaccine-induced endogenous and polyclonal VISTA blockade can achieve sustained suppression of this checkpoint while bypassing the dosing limitations and pharmacokinetic constraints associated with exogenously administered antibodies.

In this study, we developed adenoviral vectors encoding VISTA and CAIX to evaluate whether immunization-induced VISTA blockade can reprogram the TME and potentiate CAIX-targeted antitumor responses. We demonstrate that Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization enhances DC activation and antigen cross-presentation, promotes robust humoral and cellular immunity, and generates multifunctional and durable CD8+ T-cell responses capable of controlling both primary and metastatic renal carcinoma. Our findings reveal a previously unappreciated strategy in which vaccine-driven neutralization of an inhibitory myeloid checkpoint amplifies tumor antigen-specific immunity, offering a promising immunotherapeutic platform for renal carcinoma and potentially other solid tumors dominated by myeloid suppression.

Materials and methods

Animals and cell lines

Female BALB/c mice were procured from the Laboratory Animal Center of Xuzhou Medical University and maintained under specific-pathogen-free conditions, with controlled temperature and humidity. Mice aged 6–8 weeks were used at the onset of experiments. All animal procedures adhered to the institution’s guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals.

HEK293 cells were sourced from the American Type Culture Collection and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Gibco, 12800017) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; ExCell Bio, FSP500) and antibiotics (100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin; VICMED, VC2036). The murine renal carcinoma cell line Renca was purchased from Cobioer Biosciences (Nanjing, China) and maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium (Gibco, 31800022) supplemented with 10% FBS, antibiotics, 2 mM-L-glutamine (Sigma, G7513), 1 mM non-essential amino acid solution (Sigma, M7145), and 1 mM sodium pyruvate solution (Sigma, S8636). The hCAIX-Renca stable cell line was established as previously described in our laboratory.26 All cell lines were cultured at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2.

Plasmid construction and adenoviral vaccine preparation

The shuttle vector pCA13 with E1-deleted, Ad5 backbone vector pPE3, pCA13-hCAIX, and pcDNA3.1-mVISTA were maintained in our laboratory.25 Mouse VISTA complementary DNA was excised from the pcDNA3.1-mVISTA plasmid and subcloned into the pCA13 shuttle vector using HindIII (Takara, 1615) and XbaI (Takara, 1634) restriction sites. The resulting pCA13-mVISTA construct was co-transfected with the pPE3 adenoviral backbone into HEK293 cells to generate VISTA-expressing adenovirus (Ad-VISTA) through homologous recombination. Likewise, pCA13-hCAIX or the empty pCA13 vector was co-transfected with pPE3 into HEK293 cells to produce the CAIX-expressing adenovirus (Ad-CAIX) or control adenovirus (Ad-Ctrl). Viral plaques became visible in adenovirus-infected HEK293 cells cultured 10–14 days after transfection. Adenovirus purification was carried out using cesium chloride density gradient ultracentrifugation. Viral titers were determined in HEK293 cells by the dilution-plating method and expressed as plaque-forming units (PFU)/mL.

Adenoviral vaccine expression

For genomic DNA analysis, DNA was extracted from cell samples by repeated freeze-thaw cycles using the QIAGEN Genomic DNA Kit (TIANGEN, DP304-03). PCR amplification was performed using primers specific for CAIX or VISTA. The integrity and purity of the DNA were assessed by electrophoresis on 1% agarose gels at 100 V for 30 min, and gels were visualized under 312 nm using the gel imaging system (FireReader).

For flow cytometric analysis of adenoviral vaccine expression, 5×1 05 293 cells were seeded in each well of a 6-well plate and infected with Ad-VISTA or Ad-CAIX at a multiplicity of infection of 5. After 48 hours, cells were harvested and stained with Phycoerythrin (PE)-conjugated anti-mouse VISTA (BioLegend, 159603, RRID:AB_2876555) or anti-human CAIX (R&D Systems, FAB2188P, RRID:AB_2066531) antibodies for 30 min at room temperature. Protein expression was then evaluated by flow cytometry.

Animal models and vaccine treatment

For subcutaneous and lung metastasis models, mice were either implanted subcutaneously in the right flank with 2×105 hCAIX-Renca cells suspended in 100 µL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) on day 0 or injected intravenously via the tail vein with 5×105 cells. For the orthotopic renal model, mice were anesthetized, and 5×104 hCAIX-Renca cells suspended in 10 µL PBS containing 50% (v/v) growth factor-reduced Matrigel (Corning, 354234) were injected under the renal capsule. The incision was subsequently sutured.

For vaccination, subcutaneous tumor-bearing mice were randomly assigned to four groups and intramuscularly immunized with 3×108 PFU of Ad-Ctrl, Ad-VISTA, Ad-CAIX, or Ad-VISTA/CAIX on day 7 post-tumor inoculation. For combination vaccination, mice were administered 3×10⁸ PFU of Ad-VISTA together with 3×10⁸ PFU of Ad-CAIX per immunization. In the single-vaccine groups, an additional 3×10⁸ PFU of Ad-Ctrl was included to ensure that the total viral dose was equivalent across all treatment groups. Booster immunizations were administered on days 17 and 27. In combination experiments, mice additionally received anti-PD-1 (αPD-1) antibody (200 µg/mouse, Bio X cell, RRID:AB_10949053) treatment on days 14 and 21. Tumor growth was measured and tumor volume was calculated as V (mm3)=(length×width2/2. On day 42, tumors were excised, photographed, and weighed. Survival was recorded according to institutional ethical guidelines when tumor size reached the humane endpoint. Mice cured or tumor-bearing after Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination received a secondary tumor challenge on the contralateral left flank (2×105 hCAIX-Renca cells). Tumor growth and survival were monitored until day 70.

For the metastatic model, mice were vaccinated with the same doses as used in the subcutaneous tumor model on days 1, 11, and 21 post-tumor inoculation. On day 28, mice were sacrificed, lungs harvested, and metastatic nodules quantified. In the orthotopic tumor model, mice received three intramuscular vaccinations at the same dose on days 1, 11, and 21 following tumor implantation. On euthanasia, tumors were surgically excised and weighed, and the tumor inhibition rate was calculated relative to the mock group.

ELISA measurement of cytokines

Serum levels of interleukin (IL)-6 and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α in mice were measured using an ELISA kit (Invitrogen, BMS603-2, RRID:AB_2575650 and BMS607-3, RRID:AB_2575663) following the manufacturer’s protocols.

Preparation of single-cell suspension

Spleens were harvested and gently homogenized to release cells, which were subsequently filtered through a 70 µm nylon mesh to remove debris. Following centrifugation at 1,000 rpm for 5 min, red blood cells were lysed using ammonium-chloride-potassium lysis buffer. The remaining cells were collected and resuspended in RPMI 1640 medium to generate a single-cell suspension. Tumors were minced into small fragments and processed similarly to obtain single-cell suspensions. Tumor-infiltrating leukocytes were then isolated from the tumor cell suspensions using a 33.33% Percoll gradient (VicMed, VIC1555).

Flow cytometry analysis

For cell surface staining, cells were incubated for 1 hour at 4°C with the following fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies: PE-anti-mouse Cy7-CD45 (BioLegend, 103114, RRID:AB_312979), PE-anti-mouse CD3ε (BioLegend, 100308, RRID:AB_312673), PerCP-Cy5.5-anti-mouse CD4 (BioLegend, 116012, RRID:AB_2563023), PerCP-Cy5.5-anti-mouse CD8α (BioLegend, 100734, RRID:AB_2075238), FITC-anti-mouse CD49b (BioLegend, 108906, RRID:AB_313413), PerCP-anti-mouse F4/80 (BioLegend, 123126, RRID:AB_893483), FITC-anti-mouse CD11b (BioLegend, 101206, RRID:AB_312789), APC-anti-mouse CD11c (BioLegend, 117310, RRID:AB_313779), PE-anti-mouse CD103 (BioLegend, 110904, RRID:AB_2927994), PE-anti-mouse CD40 (BioLegend, 157506, RRID:AB_2860731), PE-anti-mouse CD80 (BioLegend, 104708, RRID:AB_313129), PE-anti-mouse CD86 (BioLegend, 159204, RRID:AB_2832568), PE-anti-mouse MHC-II (BioLegend, 107608, RRID:AB_313323), Alexa Fluor 647-anti-mouse FOXP3 (BioLegend, 126408, RRID:AB_1089115), APC/Cy7-Ly-6G/Ly-6C (BioLegend, 108424, RRID:AB_2137485), PE-anti-mouse CD44 (BioLegend, 103008, RRID:AB_312959), FITC-anti-mouse CD62L (BioLegend, 104406, RRID:AB_313093). Cells were subsequently washed two times with PBS and analyzed using an FACSCanto II flow cytometer (BD Biosciences).

For intracellular cytokine detection, lymphocytes were plated in 12-well plates at a density of 6×106 cells/mL and stimulated with 10 µg/mL purified hCAIX protein for 72 hours. During the final 5 hours, cells were treated with Ionomycin (500 ng/mL, Sigma-Aldrich, I9657), Phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) (50 ng/mL, Sigma-Aldrich, P1585), and Brefeldin A (5 µg/mL, eBioscience, 00–4506-51) at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator. Cells were then harvested, washed with PBS, and surface-stained with PerCP-Cy5.5-anti-mouse CD8α. Intracellular staining was performed using APC-anti-mouse IFN-γ (BioLegend, 505810, RRID:AB_315404), PE-anti-mouse IL-2 (BioLegend, 503808, RRID:AB_315302) and FITC-TNF-α (BioLegend, 506304, RRID:AB_315425). Flow cytometric acquisition was conducted on an FACSCanto II (BD Biosciences) using FACSDiva software, and data were analyzed with FlowJo software (TreeStar).

Antibody titer assay

Serum IgG levels were evaluated by ELISA. Recombinant VISTA or hCAIX protein (5 µg/mL) was coated onto 96-well plates by incubation at 4°C overnight. Plates were then blocked with PBS containing 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA). Diluted serum samples were applied to the wells and allowed to react for 1 hour at room temperature. Binding antibodies were detected using horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled anti-mouse IgG (Cell Signaling Technology, 7076, RRID:AB_330924). After adding the substrate and stopping the reaction, absorbance at 450 nm was recorded with a microplate reader. The antibody titer was defined as the greatest serum dilution that produced a signal at least two times that of the negative control.

Neutralization assay

ELISA plates were coated with purified recombinant VISTA (2 µg/mL, 100 µL/well) in PBS either overnight at 4°C or for 2 hours at 37°C. After washing with phosphate buffered saline with Tween-20 (PBST), wells were blocked with 5% BSA/PBS for 1 hour at room temperature. Serial dilutions of antibody serum from adenoviral vaccine-immunized mice were prepared in 1% BSA/PBS. Diluted samples were added to the coated wells and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. After a brief wash, biotinylated PSGL-1 or VSIG3 (0.5 µg/mL) was added for 1 hour, followed by PBST washes and incubation with streptavidin-HRP for 30 min. Plates were washed again, developed with 3, 3′,5, 5′-Tetramethylbenzidine (TMB), and the reaction was stopped and measured at 450 nm.

CD8+ T-cell proliferation

Lymphocytes were distributed into 48-well flat-bottom plates at 1×10⁶ cells per well and stimulated with IL-2 (100 U/mL) and hCAIX protein (10 µg/mL). Cultures were maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO₂ incubator for 5 days, with medium replenishment on day 3. Cell proliferation was quantified using the BeyoClick EdU Cell Proliferation Kit (Alexa Fluor 647; Beyotime). For EdU incorporation, cells were exposed to 10 µM EdU for 3 hours at 37°C, harvested, and subsequently labeled with PerCP-Cy5.5-anti-CD8α for 1 hour. After sequential fixation, permeabilization, and washing, a 70 µL click-reaction mixture was applied for 1 hour at room temperature. Samples were then washed three times with permeabilization buffer. The frequency of EdU+ cells within the CD8+ T-cell population was determined by flow cytometry.

Cytotoxic T lymphocyte killing assay

Lymphocytes were stimulated in RPMI 1640 supplemented with IL-2 (100 U/mL) and hCAIX protein (10 µg/mL) for 7 days at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Following stimulation, the cells were washed thoroughly and resuspended in fresh RPMI 1640 to serve as effector cells. Effector cells were then incubated with hCAIX-Renca target cells at an effector-to-target ratio of 50:1 in round-bottom 24-well plates for 48 hours under standard culture conditions (37°C, 5% CO₂). At the end of co-culture, cells were harvested and sequentially stained with PerCP-Cy5.5-anti-mouse CD8α and PE-anti-human CAIX prior to flow cytometric analysis. Cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) activity was quantified by determining the proportion of lysed tumor cells.

T-cell depletion

To achieve in vivo depletion of CD4+ or CD8+ T cells, mice received an intraperitoneal injection of 0.5 mg anti-mouse CD4 (Bio X cell, BE0119, RRID:AB_10950382) or CD8α (Bio X cell, BE0061, RRID:AB_1125541) monoclonal antibody 2 days before the initial vaccination. Additional doses were administered on days 5 and 12 to maintain depletion. The efficiency of CD4+ or CD8+ T-cell removal was assessed by flow cytometry.

ELISpot assay

IFN-γ-producing mouse T cells were assessed using a mouse IFN-γ single-color enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) kit (ImmunoSpot, Cat# SKU: mIFNgp-1M). Briefly, ELISpot plates were first activated with 70% ethanol, rinsed with PBS, and then coated with IFN-γ capture solution overnight at 4°C. After washing, the plates were incubated with CTL-Test Medium containing CAIX antigen at 37°C in a CO₂ incubator. Lymphocytes (5×10⁵ cells per well) were added and cultured under humidified conditions at 37°C for 3 days. After treatment with the IFN-γ detection antibody, enzymatic substrate was applied, leading to visible spot formation. Finally, the spots were imaged using an ImmunoSpot S6 Ultimate Reader (CTL), and the number of spot-forming cells was quantified with ImmunoSpot software.

H&E and immunohistochemistry staining

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor tissues were sectioned at 5 µm. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated through graded ethanol, and subjected to microwave-mediated antigen retrieval in 0.01 M sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% H₂O₂ for 20 min, followed by 2 hours blocking with 10% BSA. Sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with anti-mouse CD8 antibody (1:100, eBioscience, 14–0081-82, RRID:AB_467087), washed, and then incubated with goat anti-rat secondary antibody (Zhongshan Biotech, PV-9004) for 30 min at room temperature. Signals were developed using DAB (Zhongshan Biotech, ZLI-9018) and counterstained with hematoxylin. H&E staining of liver, kidney, and heart was performed per manufacturer instructions. Images were captured using a Nikon SCLIPSS TE2000-S microscope with ACT-1 software, and histopathological evaluation was conducted by a blinded pathologist.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism V.9.0. Data are presented as mean±SD. Comparisons between two groups were performed using a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test, whereas multiple group comparisons were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance. Survival curves were evaluated using the log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test. A p value<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical significance is indicated as follows: p<0.05, p<0.01, and p<0.001.

Results

VISTA immunization significantly enhances the antitumor efficacy of the Ad-CAIX vaccine in a subcutaneous tumor model

To deliver VISTA and the tumor antigen CAIX, we employed a replication-defective E1-deleted adenovirus serotype five platform, which has been widely validated for its safety and lack of replication in immunocompetent mouse muscle. Recombinant adenoviral vectors were generated by co-transfecting the shuttle plasmids pCA13-VISTA or pCA13-CAIX together with the adenoviral backbone pPE3 into HEK293 cells (figure 1A). Viral packaging was confirmed by the appearance of characteristic cytopathic plaques 10–14 days after transfection (figure 1B), and the presence of the transgenes was validated by PCR amplification from purified viral genomic DNA (figure 1C). To verify antigen expression, HEK293 cells were infected with Ad-VISTA or Ad-CAIX. Robust expression of VISTA and CAIX was detected in infected cells, whereas no signal was observed in the Ad-Ctrl group (figure 1D and E), confirming the high expression efficiency of Ad vectors.

We next evaluated the therapeutic activity of Ad-VISTA/CAIX in vivo. hRenca tumor-bearing mice received three intramuscular immunizations of Ad-VISTA/CAIX or control vaccine according to the schedule shown in figure 1F. Co-immunization with Ad-VISTA/CAIX resulted in markedly improved tumor control compared with Ad-CAIX alone, as demonstrated by significantly reduced tumor growth kinetics and final tumor volume (figure 1G and H). Consistently, tumor weight was substantially lower and the tumor inhibition rate significantly higher in the Ad-VISTA/CAIX group (figure 1I and J). To assess potential toxicity, we monitored body weight throughout treatment and observed no differences among groups, indicating that the vaccine regimen did not induce systemic toxicity (figure 1K). Serum IL-6 and TNF-α levels were transiently elevated on day 2 after immunization but returned to baseline by day 7 (figure 1L and M), suggesting an acute but self-limited innate immune activation typical of adenoviral vectors. Histopathological examination of major organs (heart, liver, kidney) revealed no tissue damage or inflammatory lesions (figure 1N), further supporting the safety of the vaccine. Collectively, these results indicate that VISTA immunization significantly potentiates the therapeutic efficacy of the Ad-CAIX vaccine while maintaining a favorable safety profile in vivo.

Targeting VISTA by immunization reshapes the tumor immune microenvironment and enhances the induction and maturation of DCs

To investigate how VISTA immunization augments the antitumor activity of the Ad-CAIX vaccine, we profiled immune cell populations in the spleen and tumor by flow cytometry. Compared with Ad-CAIX alone, Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization significantly increased the infiltration of CD3+ and CD8+ T cells, as well as DCs, in both spleen and tumor tissues (figure 2A and B). In contrast, the frequencies of CD4+ T cells, NK cells, and macrophages remained largely unchanged in both compartments. Notably, while splenic myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) were comparable between groups, intratumoral MDSCs and Tregs were markedly reduced following Ad-VISTA/CAIX treatment. These findings indicate that VISTA immunization effectively remodels the immune microenvironment enhancing effector immunity while alleviating immunosuppressive cell infiltration.

Given the essential role of DCs, particularly CD103+ and CD8+ DC subsets, in antigen cross-presentation and priming durable CD8+ T-cell immune responses,28 we next assessed DC abundance and activation status in vaccinated mice. Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization resulted in a robust expansion of total CD11b+CD11c+ DCs, CD103+CD11c+ DCs, and CD8+CD11c+ DCs in the spleen (figure 2C–2H). Furthermore, expression of key costimulatory and antigen-presenting molecules, including CD40, CD80, CD86, and major histocompatibility complex (MHC)-II, was significantly upregulated in DCs from the Ad-VISTA/CAIX group (figure 2I and J). Together, these results demonstrate that VISTA immunization enhances the immunogenicity of the Ad-CAIX vaccine by promoting DC expansion and maturation while simultaneously reducing immunosuppressive populations within the TME. This coordinated remodeling establishes a more favorable landscape for robust antitumor CD8+ T-cell responses.

Immunization-mediated blockade of VISTA promotes vaccine-induced antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell responses

Humoral immunity provides important support for CD8+ T cell-mediated tumor control, particularly when neutralizing antibodies counteract suppressive VISTA signaling. ELISA analysis demonstrated that immunization with Ad-VISTA, Ad-CAIX, or the combined Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccine each elicited robust antigen-specific antibody responses. Notably, antibody titers against VISTA or CAIX were comparable among the respective groups, indicating that Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization did not diminish antigen-specific humoral responses (figure 3A and B). Functional neutralization assays further revealed that sera from Ad-VISTA and Ad-VISTA/CAIX immunized mice effectively blocked VISTA binding to its ligands PSGL-1 and VSIG3 (figure 3C and D), confirming the generation of VISTA-neutralizing antibodies capable of disrupting its inhibitory axis.

To assess the antigen-specific cellular immunity induced by the combined vaccine, we quantified CD8+ T-cell proliferation, cytotoxicity, and Th1 cytokine production. On ex vivo stimulation with CAIX protein, splenic CD8+ T cells from Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunized mice displayed significantly enhanced proliferation compared with those from the Ad-CAIX group (figure 3E and F). In a tumor-cell killing assay, hCAIX-expressing target cells were efficiently eliminated by effector cells from the Ad-VISTA/CAIX group, resulting in substantially reduced tumor cell survival relative to Ad-CAIX alone (figure 3G and H), demonstrating superior CTL cytotoxic function. We next evaluated multifunctional cytokine responses by intracellular staining. The Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccine markedly increased the frequencies of IFN-γ+CD8+, IL-2+CD8+, and TNF-α+CD8+ T cells in spleen and tumor tissues compared with Ad-CAIX alone (figure 3I–L). These multifunctional CD8+ T cells are key drivers of effective antitumor immunity. Together, these results demonstrate that immunization-mediated VISTA blockade generates functional neutralizing antibodies and enhances vaccine-induced antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell proliferation, cytotoxicity, and effector function. This establishes VISTA immunization as a potent strategy to amplify tumor-specific CTL responses.

Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization induced durable antitumor immunity depends on memory CD8+ T-cell immune responses

Multifunctional CD8+ T cells, as key effectors of protective immunity, secrete multiple cytokines, including IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α, and are pivotal for mediating antitumor responses.29 30 To assess the induction of such cells, we evaluated the proportion of CD8+ T cells co-expressing two or three cytokines in splenic lymphocytes and tumor-infiltrating T cells from immunized mice. The frequencies of IFN-γ+TNF-α+CD8+, IFN-γ+IL-2+CD8+, IL-2+TNF-α+CD8+ and IFN-γ+IL-2+TNF-α+CD8+ T cells were markedly higher in the splenocytes of mice receiving Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunotherapy than in those treated with Ad-CAIX alone (figure 4A). Consistently, tumor-infiltrating T cells exhibited similar enhancements (figure 4B), indicating that the combination therapy robustly promotes antigen-specific multifunctional CD8+ T-cell responses. To determine whether these functional T cells were essential for the vaccine-induced antitumor effect, we performed in vivo T-cell depletion using anti-CD4 or anti-CD8 monoclonal antibodies. Depletion of CD4+ T cells did not significantly affect survival in Ad-VISTA/CAIX-treated mice (figure 4C). In contrast, CD8+ T-cell depletion resulted in a marked reduction in survival of mice bearing subcutaneous tumors and abrogated the antitumor efficacy of the vaccine (figure 4D). Flow cytometric analysis confirmed substantial decreases in the frequencies of CD8+ T cells and CD8+ DCs within both the spleen and TME in CD8-depleted mice (figure 4E and F). These findings underscore that Ad-VISTA/CAIX enhances antigen-specific multifunctional CD8+ T-cell responses, which are critical for its antitumor activity.

To evaluate the durability of the antitumor response induced by Ad-VISTA/CAIX, mice were rechallenged with tumors after initial therapy. Ad-VISTA/CAIX therapy reduces lung metastasis of tumor by amplifying antigen-specific multifunctional CD8+ T-cell immune responses

To further assess the therapeutic benefit of Ad-VISTA/CAIX, we employed a lung metastasis model in which mice were intramuscularly immunized with the indicated vaccines. Animals received three doses of the Ad-VISTA/CAIX regimen, and 35 days after tumor inoculation, they were euthanized and lung tissues were collected to visualize metastatic foci (figure 5A). Mice treated with Ad-VISTA/CAIX displayed a marked reduction in the number of lung metastases compared with those receiving Ad-CAIX alone (figure 5B and C). Consistently, the combination therapy significantly prolonged overall survival relative to Ad-CAIX treatment (figure 5D).

To elucidate the underlying immune mechanisms, we examined vaccine-induced CD8+ T-cell responses. Immunohistochemical staining and flow cytometric analysis revealed substantially increased infiltration of CD8+ T cells within metastatic lesions in the Ad-VISTA/CAIX group compared with Ad-CAIX and other controls (figure 5E and F). CD8+ T cells in the combination group also exhibited enhanced proliferative activity (figure 5G). ELISpot assays further demonstrated that Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization robustly elevated the number of IFN-γ-producing T cells relative to controls (figure 5H and I). We next evaluated multifunctional CD8+ T-cell responses in vitro. The Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccine significantly increased the frequencies of single-producing and poly-cytokine-producing CD8+ T cells, including IFN-γ+, IL-2+, TNF-α+, IFN-γ+TNF-α+, IFN-γ+IL-2+, IL-2+TNF-α+, and triple-positive IFN-γ+IL-2+TNF-α+CD8+ T cells, compared with control groups. Moreover, CTL assays showed that effector cells isolated from Ad-VISTA/CAIX-treated mice exhibited superior cytolytic activity against hCAIX-Renca target cells. Collectively, these results indicate that Ad-VISTA/CAIX potently suppresses lung metastasis by augmenting tumor-specific multifunctional CD8+ T-cell immunity.

Ad-VISTA/CAIX combination therapy elicits potent antitumor activity in an orthotopic renal cancer model

To determine whether the Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination strategy confers therapeutic efficacy against orthotopic renal tumors, we established a syngeneic orthotopic renal cancer model in BALB/c mice. Following implantation of hCAIX-Renca cells into the renal capsule, mice received intramuscular immunization with the indicated adenoviral vaccines. Tumor-bearing kidneys were collected on day 28 post-tumor inoculation, and tumor volume and weight were quantified (figure 6A). Mice treated with the Ad-VISTA/CAIX combination vaccine exhibited a marked reduction in renal tumor volume relative to mice treated with Ad-CAIX alone (figure 6B and C). Consistently, tumor weights were significantly decreased in the co-immunization group (figure 6D), and tumor inhibition rates were substantially higher in Ad-VISTA/CAIX-treated mice (figure 6E). These results demonstrate that combined Ad-VISTA and Ad-CAIX immunization robustly suppresses orthotopic renal tumor growth.

Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination enhances the therapeutic efficacy of αPD-1 by eliciting potent CD8+ T cell-mediated antitumor immunity

The clinical response rate to αPD-1 therapy in renal carcinoma remains suboptimal, and many patients fail to achieve durable benefit.31 We therefore examined whether Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination could enhance the antitumor efficacy of αPD-1 treatment. Mice were inoculated subcutaneously with hCAIX-Renca cells on day 0, followed by intraperitoneal administration of αPD-1 on days 14 and 21, and vaccination with Ad-VISTA/CAIX on days 7, 17, and 21 (figure 7A). By day 63, αPD-1 monotherapy resulted in only 20% survival, whereas Ad-VISTA/CAIX alone improved survival to 60%. Strikingly, the combination of Ad-VISTA/CAIX with αPD-1 achieved 100% survival, demonstrating a profound synergistic therapeutic effect (figure 7B). To investigate the immune mechanisms underlying this synergy, we analyzed tumor-infiltrating immune cells after treatment. Combination therapy markedly increased CD8+ T-cell infiltration while significantly reducing MDSCs and Tregs compared with either monotherapy (figure 7C). Assessment of effector function revealed a substantial elevation of IFN-γ+CD8+ T cells in tumors from the combination group (figure 7D and E). Consistent with this, ELISpot assays demonstrated a robust increase in CAIX-specific IFN-γ-secreting T cells following combination treatment (figure 7F). Furthermore, splenocytes isolated from mice receiving combined Ad-VISTA/CAIX and αPD-1 therapy exhibited significantly enhanced CD8+ T-cell proliferation on antigen stimulation (figure 7G), as well as stronger CTL activity against hCAIX-Renca target cells (figure 7H). Notably, the combination treatment also promoted the expansion of long-lived effector CD8+ T cells, indicating the establishment of durable systemic antitumor immunity. Together, these findings demonstrate that Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination effectively potentiates αPD-1 therapy, amplifying CD8+ T cell-mediated immune responses and generating long-term tumor-control activity in renal carcinoma.

Discussion

Several monoclonal antibodies targeting VISTA, including W0180,32 CI-899333 and HMBD-002,19 are currently undergoing investigation. These agents provide passive and epitope-specific checkpoint blockade. In contrast, Ad-VISTA vaccination represents an active immunization strategy that induces polyclonal anti-VISTA antibodies together with cellular immune responses. Polyclonal antibodies may recognize multiple functional epitopes, potentially limiting immune escape and enhancing blockade breadth. Moreover, vaccine-induced endogenous antibody production may provide more sustained immune modulation compared with repeated monoclonal antibody administration. Importantly, by combining VISTA targeting with CAIX-directed tumor antigen vaccination, this platform integrates checkpoint modulation with tumor-specific immune priming, offering a distinct immunotherapeutic paradigm. In this study, Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization effectively reprograms the TME, promotes robust DC activation, and elicits multifunctional CD8+ T-cell immunity capable of controlling renal carcinoma. These findings highlight a previously underappreciated avenue in which targeting an inhibitory myeloid checkpoint through vaccination can serve as a powerful immunomodulatory platform to augment tumor antigen-specific immune responses.

A central finding of our work is that Ad-VISTA/CAIX co-immunization expands and activates multiple DC subsets, including CD11c+, CD8+CD11c+, and CD103+CD11c+ populations. These DC subsets are essential for antigen cross-presentation and the initiation of CTL responses. The upregulation of CD40, CD80, CD86, and MHC molecules indicates that VISTA blockade enhances DC priming capacity and may relieve tonic suppression imposed by the VISTA-ligand axis within the TME. Previous studies have suggested that VISTA maintains myeloid quiescence and limits T-cell activation even under inflammatory conditions.14 34 35 Our findings extend this concept by demonstrating that active immunization can induce neutralizing antibodies that directly disrupt the VISTA pathway, thereby reshaping myeloid function and enabling vaccine-induced T-cell priming with greater magnitude and quality. Notably, VISTA is predominantly expressed on suppressive myeloid populations such as MDSCs, tumor-associated macrophages, and Tregs, whereas its expression on CD8+ T cells is relatively low and largely activation-dependent.35 36 Accordingly, vaccine-induced antibodies are more likely to engage these suppressive compartments rather than effector CD8+ T cells. Consistent with this notion, we did not observe a reduction in intratumoral CD8+ T-cell frequencies following Ad-VISTA vaccination. Instead, CD8+ T-cell infiltration and effector function were enhanced, supporting the idea that the dominant mechanism of action is functional blockade of VISTA-mediated immunosuppression rather than antibody-mediated depletion of VISTA+ cells. Functionally, the Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccine generated potent CAIX-specific humoral responses, a pronounced Th1-polarized cytokine environment, and multifunctional CD8+ T cells characterized by IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α. The requirement for CD8+ T cells that validated through depletion studies emphasizes the central role of cytotoxic lymphocytes in mediating tumor control in this setting. Notably, the vaccine also induced durable memory CD8+ T cells capable of rejecting tumor rechallenge, highlighting its potential to provide long-term tumor control.

The therapeutic efficacy of Ad-VISTA/CAIX in metastatic, orthotopic and αPD-1-resistant tumor models underscores the translational potential of this approach. Renal carcinoma is characterized by abundant MDSCs and Tregs cells, both of which express high levels of VISTA.37 38 Our data suggest that targeting VISTA may be especially advantageous in tumors dominated by these suppressive cell populations. Given the distinct and non-overlapping mechanisms of VISTA compared with PD-1 and CTLA-4 pathways, vaccine-induced VISTA blockade could serve as an attractive complement or alternative to existing immunotherapies, particularly in patients who exhibit limited responses to PD-1 inhibition.

CTLA-4-targeted therapy, exemplified by ipilimumab, enhances antitumor immunity primarily by promoting T-cell priming and expansion at the level of lymphoid organs and by depleting intratumoral regulatory T cells.39 In contrast, VISTA is predominantly expressed on MDSCs, tumor-associated macrophages, and Tregs within the TME, where it functions as a negative immune checkpoint maintaining peripheral tolerance and suppressing effector T-cell activity. Unlike the nivolumab or pembrolizumab pathway, which primarily regulates T-cell exhaustion in peripheral tissues, and CTLA-4, which acts mainly during T-cell priming, VISTA exerts non-redundant immunosuppressive effects within myeloid-rich TMEs. This distinction provides a strong mechanistic rationale for targeting VISTA, particularly in renal carcinoma, which is characterized by abundant MDSCs and Tregs with high VISTA expression.

In the current study, we focused on evaluating the therapeutic efficacy of Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination as a standalone strategy and in PD-1-resistant settings. We have not yet performed combination experiments with anti-CTLA-4 monoclonal antibody therapy. However, based on the complementary mechanisms of action, we think that combining vaccine-mediated VISTA targeting with CTLA-4 blockade represents an attractive therapeutic strategy. CTLA-4 inhibition may enhance initial T-cell priming, while vaccine-induced VISTA blockade may alleviate myeloid-mediated suppression within the TME, potentially resulting in synergistic antitumor immunity. However, CTLA-4 blockade is well known to be associated with significant immune-related adverse events, including severe colitis, dermatitis, and endocrinopathies,40 which can limit its clinical applicability in renal carcinoma and other solid tumors compared with PD-1-based strategies. Given these considerations, and because our study aims to develop a translationally feasible vaccine-based strategy, we prioritized evaluating Ad-VISTA/CAIX vaccination either as monotherapy or in PD-1-resistant settings rather than combining it with CTLA-4 blockade at this stage. Our rationale was to explore whether targeting VISTA could provide therapeutic benefit while potentially avoiding the higher systemic toxicity associated with CTLA-4 inhibition. Future studies will investigate whether optimized dosing or localized delivery can allow Ad-VISTA/CAIX to be safely combined with anti-CTLA-4, reducing the risk of immune-related adverse events.

Despite the promising findings, several limitations warrant consideration. The precise epitopes and functional mechanisms of VISTA-induced antibodies remain to be fully defined. Defining the dominant epitopes and binding affinities of these antibodies would further clarify the molecular basis of vaccine-induced VISTA blockade. Fc-dependent effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis may also contribute. Future studies incorporating macrophage depletion strategies (eg, clodronate liposomes or CSF1R inhibition) will be necessary to determine the relative contribution of Fc-mediated mechanisms to therapeutic efficacy. While CAIX is a well-established tumor antigen in renal carcinoma, antigen heterogeneity and loss could limit long-term vaccine efficacy in certain tumor contexts,41 suggesting that incorporation of additional tumor antigens or neoantigen strategies may broaden therapeutic coverage. Future single-cell and spatial analyses of the TME will further clarify how VISTA blockade reshapes immune populations. Given the non-redundant roles of VISTA and PD-1 pathways, combinatorial strategies and evaluation in other myeloid-rich tumors may help define the broader translational potential of this platform.

In summary, our study reveals that immunization-driven VISTA blockade synergizes with CAIX vaccination to generate robust DC activation, multifunctional CD8+ T-cell immunity, and durable tumor control in renal carcinoma. These findings establish a mechanistically distinct and highly effective immunotherapeutic strategy with broad potential for solid tumors characterized by myeloid-driven immune suppression.

Minimal A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras of endomorphisms: The case of d ​ ℤ d\mathbb{Z} -cluster tilting objects
Gustavo Jasso, Fernando Muro

Abstract
. The Derived Auslander–Iyama Corresponence, a recent result of the authors, provides a classification up to quasi-isomorphism of the derived endomorphism algebras of basic d ​ ℤ d\mathbb{Z} -cluster tilting objects in Hom \operatorname{Hom} -finite algebraic triangulated categories in terms of a small amount of algebraic data. In this note we highlight the role of minimal A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra structures in the proof of this result, as well as the crucial role of the enhanced A ∞ A_{\infty} -obstruction theory developed by the second-named author.

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{k}

Suppose also that

This question is known to have a negative answer. Indeed, let

A better question is then perhaps the following:

Can
\mathcal{T}
be reconstructed—as a triangulated category—from the graded
endomorphism algebra of the generator

Since we assume

•

•

The quasi-isomorphism class of the dg algebra

is not uniquely
determined by
\mathcal{T}
, as in principle it depends on a choice of enhancement

We shift the goalposts one last time and ask the following question instead:

Can
\mathcal{T}
be reconstructed—as a triangulated category—from the graded
endomorphism algebra of the generator
G\in\mathcal{T}

a minimal

However vague, a general answer the above question seems to be out of reach, at

1.
Hochschild cohomology of graded algebras

Hochschild cohomology of associative algebras was introduced by the eponymous
author in
[
Hoc45
]
, and its rich algebraic structure was first
investigated by Gerstehaber in
[
Ger63
]
. In this section we recall the basic
definitions in the case of graded algebras, where sign issues are slightly
more subtle. We adopt the sign conventions used in
[
Mur20
]
.

Let
\mathbf{\Lambda}
be a graded algebra.
3
3

3

For example, the cohomology of the
dg algebra of endomorphisms of a classical generator of a dg-enhanced
triangulated category.
The
Hochschild (cochain) complex of
\mathbf{\Lambda}

is the bigraded vector space with the components

\operatorname{C}^{p,q}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\otimes p},\mathbf{\Lambda}(q)),\qquad p\geq 0,\ q\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\mathbf{\Lambda}(q)^{i}=\mathbf{\Lambda}^{i+q}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the
\operatorname{Hom}

is taken in the category of graded vector spaces. It is convenient to introduce
some auxiliary notation in order to describe the differential and the additional
algebraic structure of the Hochschild complex.

Given Hochschild cochains

c_{1}\in\operatorname{C}^{p,q}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{2}\in\operatorname{C}^{s,t}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right),

we define, for
1\leq i\leq p
, their
(suspended) infinitesimal composition

c_{1}\bullet_{i}c_{2}\in\operatorname{C}^{p+s-1,q+t}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)

by the formula

(c_{1}\bullet_{i}c_{2})(x_{1},\dots,x_{p+q-1})\coloneqq\\
(-1)^{\maltese}c_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},c_{2}(x_{i},\cdots,x_{i+r-1}),x_{r},\cdots x_{p+q-1}),

where the sign is given by
4
4

4

The reader might be surprised that the sign
does not only involve the Koszul sign. The reason for this is that the use of
the so-called operadic suspension is implicit,
see
[
Mur16
, Definition 2.4]
for the definition with our preferred sign
conventions.

\textstyle\maltese\coloneqq(s-1)(p-i)+t\left(p-1+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|x_{j}|\right).

We also introduce the
pre-Lie product

\textstyle{c_{1}}\left\{c_{2}\right\}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{p}c_{1}\bullet_{i}c_{2}

the
Gerstenhaber bracket

[c_{1},c_{2}]\coloneqq{c_{1}}\left\{c_{2}\right\}-(-1)^{(p+q-1)(s+t-1)}{c_{2}}\left\{c_{1}\right\},

and the
cup product

{c_{1}}\cdot{c_{2}}\coloneqq(m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\bullet_{1}c_{1})\bullet_{p+1}c_{2},

where
m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\in\operatorname{C}^{2,0}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)
is the binary multiplication of the algebra
\mathbf{\Lambda}
.
Notice that the pre-Lie product and the Gerstenhaber bracket are bihomogeneous operations of bidegree

(-1,0)
, and the cup product is bihomogeneous of bidegree
(0,0)
.

The
Hochschild differential
is the bidegree
(1,0)
map

d_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\colon\operatorname{C}^{p,q}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\longrightarrow\operatorname{C}^{p+1,q}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right),\qquad x\longmapsto[m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}},x].

The cohomology

\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet,*}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\coloneqq\operatorname{H}^{\bullet,*}(\operatorname{C}^{\bullet,*}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right))

of the Hochschild complex is the
Hochschild cohomology of
\mathbf{\Lambda}
.

With respect to the total degree, the Hochschild cohomology of
\mathbf{\Lambda}
is a
Gerstenhaber algebra, that is it is a graded
commutative algebra which is endowed with a compatible shifted graded Lie algebra
structure. The graded commutative algebra structure is induced
by the cochain-level cup product, while the shifted Lie algebra structure is induced by the
Gerstenhaber bracket.
At the cohomological level, the cup product and the Gerstenhaber bracket satisfy the
Gerstenhaber
relation
,

[c_{1},{c_{2}}\cdot{c_{3}}]={[c_{1},c_{2}]}\cdot{c_{3}}+(-1)^{(p+q-1)(s+t)}{c_{2}}\cdot{[c_{1},c_{3}]},

where
c_{1}\in\operatorname{HH}^{p,q}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)
and
c_{2}\in\operatorname{HH}^{s,t}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)
. Moreover, if
\chark{\mathbf{k}}=2
then

\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet,*}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)
is equipped with the quadratic operation, called
Gerstenhaber square
, defined at the
cochain level by

\Sq(c)\coloneqq{c}\left\{c\right\}.

Notice that, if
c
has
bidegree
(p,q)
, then
\Sq(c)
has bidegree
(2p-1,2q)
. In general, the
Gerstenhaber square is not
\mathbf{k}
-linear nor it is an abelian group homomorphism.
Instead, it satisfies the following compatibility relations with respect to the
cup product and the Gerstenhaber bracket:

\displaystyle\Sq(c_{1}+c_{2})

\displaystyle=\Sq(c_{1})+\Sq(c_{2})+[c_{1},c_{2}],

\displaystyle\Sq(c_{1}\cdot c_{2})

\displaystyle=\Sq(c_{1})\cdot c_{2}^{2}+c_{1}\cdot[c_{1},c_{2}]\cdot c_{2}+c_{1}^{2}\cdot\Sq(c_{2}),

\displaystyle[\Sq(c_{1}),c_{2}]

\displaystyle=[c_{1},[c_{1},c_{2}]].

When
\chark{\mathbf{k}}\neq 2
, we can define the Gerstenhaber square in even total degrees by

\Sq(c)=\tfrac{1}{2}[c,c]
, and the above relations still hold.

1.1
.

The Hochschild cohomology of
\mathbf{\Lambda}
can be interpreted in terms of the
bigraded vector space of bimodule self-extensions of the diagonal

\mathbf{\Lambda}
-bimodule, that is there are isomorphisms of vector spaces

\operatorname{HH}^{p,q}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}^{e}}^{p,q}(\mathbf{\Lambda},\mathbf{\Lambda})=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}^{e}}^{p}(\mathbf{\Lambda},\mathbf{\Lambda}(q)),\qquad p\geq 0,\ q\in\mathbb{Z}.

Under these identifications, the cup product corresponds to the Yoneda
product, but the Gerstenhaber bracket has no easy interpretation in these
terms (see
[
Sch98
]
for an interpretation of the Gerstenhaber bracket in
terms of a ‘loop bracket’ of bimodule self-extensions, at least in the case of
ungraded algebras).

1.2
.

Together with its algebraic structure, the Hochschild complex is easily seen
to be functorial on
isomorphisms
of graded algebras.

Minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebras and universal Massey products

Although
1
is formulated in terms of dg algebras, parts of its proof
rely heavily on the closely-related theory of
A_{\infty}
-algebras. Standard
references for the latter include
[
Kel01
,
Kel02a
,
Kel02
,
LH03
]
. Our
presentation makes use of the algebraic structure on the Hochchild cohomology of
a graded algebra, but our sign conventions are precisely those used
in
[

]

Let
\mathbf{\Lambda}
be a graded algebra. A
minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra
structure
on
\mathbf{\Lambda}
is a sequence

\textstyle m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}=(m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}},m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}},\dots,m_{n}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}},\dots)\in\prod_{n\geq 2}\operatorname{C}^{n,2-n}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right),

where
m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\in\operatorname{C}^{2,0}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)
is the binary product of
\mathbf{\Lambda}
,
that satisfies the
Maurer–Cartan equation

0={m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}.

We call the pair
(\mathbf{\Lambda},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})
a
minimal

A_{\infty}
-algebra
. An
A_{\infty}
-morphism

f\colon(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}})\longrightarrow(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{2},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{2}})

between minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebras is a sequence homogeneous maps

f_{n}\colon\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow\mathbf{\Lambda}_{2},\qquad n\geq 1

of degree
1-n
that satisfy suitable equations that we do not need to recall
here; it suffices to mention that the
linear part

f_{1}
of
f
is a
morphism of associative algebras. We call
f
an
A_{\infty}
-isomorphism

if its linear part is an isomorphism, and a
gauge
A_{\infty}
-isomorphism

if it is the identity map (whence necessarily
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}=\mathbf{\Lambda}_{2}
).

2.1
.

For simplicity, we have omitted the definition of a general

A_{\infty}
-algebra, which is equipped with a degree
1
differential

m_{1}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}
and whose binary operation
m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}
is only
associative up to homotopy. There is also a companion notion of

A_{\infty}
-morphism, and one defines the notion

A_{\infty}
-
quasi-
isomorphism in terms of the linear part, which is
necessarily a morphism of complexes. The advantage of this generality is that
we may identify dg algebras with
A_{\infty}
-algebras such that
m_{n}=0

for
n\geq 3
, and consider
A_{\infty}
-morphisms between dg algebras and

A_{\infty}
-algebras.

2.2
.

Let
(\mathbf{\Lambda},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})
be a minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra. The
Maurer–Cartan equation corresponds to the infinite system of quadratic
equations

\textstyle 0=\sum_{p+s-1=n}{m_{p}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{s}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\},\qquad n\geq 3,

which is one way to write the
A_{\infty}
-equations
for an

A_{\infty}
-algebra. For
n=3
, the equation is

0={m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}

and corresponds to the associativity of the binary product. More generally,
the Maurer–Cartan equation expresses the fact that

consists of a family of ‘coherently associative’ higher operations.

Let
(\mathbf{\Lambda},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})
be a minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra. The

A_{\infty}
-equation for

yields

0={m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}+{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}=[m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}},m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}]=d_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}),

so that

is a Hochschild cocycle.
Its cohomology class

\{\!\!\{{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\}\!\!\}\in\operatorname{HH}^{3,-1}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)

is an invariant of the gauge
A_{\infty}
-isomorphism class of

.
Moreover, the
A_{\infty}
-equation for

yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle={m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{4}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}+{m_{4}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}+{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\left\{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\right\}

\displaystyle=[m_{2}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}},m_{4}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}]+\Sq(m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(m_{4}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})+\Sq(m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}),

so that

0=\Sq(\{\!\!\{{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\}\!\!\})\in\operatorname{HH}^{5,-2}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right).

Finally, set
\Lambda\coloneqq\mathbf{\Lambda}^{0}
and let

j\colon\Lambda\hookrightarrow\mathbf{\Lambda}
be the canonical inclusion of the
degree
0
part. The
restricted universal Massey product
is the class

\textstyle j^{*}\{\!\!\{{m_{3}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}\}\!\!\}\in\operatorname{HH}^{3,-1}\!\left(\Lambda,\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{\Lambda^{e}}^{3}(\Lambda,\mathbf{\Lambda}^{-1}),

where the

is computed in the category of ungraded

\Lambda

in
[
BKS04
]
(see
[
BD89
]

2.1.
Minimal models of dg algebras

Let
A
be a dg algebra. A
minimal model
for
A
is a minimal

A_{\infty}
-algebra
(\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(A),m^{A})
together with an
A_{\infty}

i\colon(\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(A),m^{A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}A

whose linear part

is a

\forall x\in\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(A),\quad[i_{1}(x)]=x.

By a well-known theorem of Kadeishvili
[

gauge
A_{\infty}
-isomorphism, we may define the universal Massey product of a dg
algebra
A
as that of any of its minimal models. The universal Massey product
is an invariant of the quasi-isomorphism class of

.

\displaystyle\longmapsto[\{\!\!\{{m_{3}^{f}}\}\!\!\},c].

(\mathbf{\Lambda},\{\!\!\{{m_{3}^{f}}\}\!\!\})

in

Next, we describe some other terms of the extended Bousfield–Kan
spectral sequence in a way that its usefulness in our context becomes more
apparent.

•

For

, the term

is the pointed set of
homotopy classes of morphisms of dg operads

that can be extended to
\mathbb{A}_{s+r+1}

and whose restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{s+1}
is homotopic to the restriction of

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}_{s+2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}_{s+r+1}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

f
to
\mathbb{A}_{s+2}

as above has the same restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{s+1}

considering the Milnor short exact sequence of pointed sets

where we remind the reader that

or, equivalently, the set of minimal

\mathbf{\Lambda}

There is a pointed bijection between the pointed set of homotopy classes

Finally, the following observation is also fundamental:

•

If we fix a minimal

-structure on

The above properties of the extended Bousfield–Kan spectral sequence are crucial to
the proof of
[
JKM22
, Theorem B]
, which is itself a key ingredient in the proof
of

. In a nutshell, this spectral sequence provides an
approach to studying the vanishing of obstructions to the existence and
uniqueness of minimal
A_{\infty}
-structures on a graded algebra.

4.1
.

Another question question that is usually dealt with when studying a spectral
sequence is that of convergence. Since in the limit
r\to\infty
of the
Bousfield–Kan spectral sequence and its extension agree, one knows that the
term
E_{\infty}^{s,t}
,
{t-s>0}
, contributes to the homotopy group

\pi_{t-s}(X_{\infty})
of the homotopy limit of the tower and to the set of
connected components
\pi_{0}(X_{\infty})
when
s=t
,
see
[
BK72
, Section IX.5]
. In the case of the space

X_{\infty}=\operatorname{Map}\!\left(\mathbb{A}_{\infty},\mathcal{E}\!\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}\right)\right)

of minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra structures on the underlying graded vector
space of
\mathbf{\Lambda}
, these are given as follows
[
Mur20
, Proposition 6.9]
:

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(X_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\text{minimal $A_{\infty}$-algebras with underlying graded vector space $\mathbf{\Lambda}$}

modulo gauge
A_{\infty}
-isomorphisms,

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(X_{\infty},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})

\displaystyle=\text{gauge $A_{\infty}$-automorphisms of the minimal $A_{\infty}$-algebra $(\mathbf{\Lambda},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})$}

modulo homotopies with identity linear part,

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(X_{\infty},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{HH}^{2-n}_{\geq 2}(B)\qquad n\geq 2,

where we write
B
for the minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathbf{\Lambda},m^{\mathbf{\Lambda}})
and

\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(B)
for its Hochschild cohomology,
see
[
Mur20
]
for definitions and explanations of the notation.

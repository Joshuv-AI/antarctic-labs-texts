ExoMol Line Lists – LXII: Ro-Vibrational Energy Levels and Line-Strengths for the Propadienediylidene ( C 3 {\mathrm{C}}_{3} ) in its Ground Electronic State
A. E. Lynas-Gray, O. L. Polyansky, J. Tennyson, S. N. Yurchenko and N. F. Zobov

Abstract
Improved opacities are needed for modelling the atmospheres and evolution of cool carbon-rich stars and extra-solar planets; in particular, contributions made by the astrophysically important propadienediylidene ( C 3 {\mathrm{C}}_{3} ) molecule need, at a minimum, to be determined using a line list which includes all significant transitions in the energy range of interest. We report variational calculations giving ro-vibrational energy levels and corresponding line-strengths for C 3 12 {}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3} , C 13 12 ​ C 12 ​ C {}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}} and C 12 12 ​ C 13 ​ C {}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}} . In the C 3 12 {}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3} case we obtain 2 166 503 ro-vibrational state energies ⩽ 20000 \leqslant 20000 cm − 1 {\rm cm}^{-1} for the electronic X ~ 1 ​ Σ g + \tilde{X}{\,}^{1}{\Sigma_{\rm g}}^{+} ground-state. Comparison with experiment indicates a maximum error of ± 0.03 \pm 0.03 cm − 1 {\rm cm}^{-1} in calculated positions of lines involving an upper state energy ⪅ 4000 \lessapprox 4000 cm − 1 {\rm cm}^{-1} . For lines with upper state energies ⪆ 4000 \gtrapprox 4000 cm − 1 {\rm cm}^{-1} to have comparable line-position accuracies, conical intersections would need to be accounted for in an adopted potential energy surface. Line lists and associated opacities are provided in the ExoMol Database ( http://www.exomol.com ).

Introduction

Interest in propadienediylidene (tricarbon, hereinafter
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
)
follows the demonstration by
Douglas (1951)
that the
molecular band near 4050 Å in cometary spectra may be
attributable to it. First observed by
Huggins (1881)
,
the  4050 Å band is also seen in carbon star spectra

(
McKellar, 1948
)
and work by

Douglas

allowed
Swings
et al. (1953)
to identify
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
as the carrier.
A detection of
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
is to be expected as

Honig
’s
(
1954
)

determination of the dissociation energy (the energy required to
break a C–C bond) of 160 kcal/mol (6.938 eV) led

Tsuji (1964)
to demonstrate that both

{\mathrm{C}}_{2}
and
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
achieve maximum number densities in conditions
prevailing in some carbon star atmospheres at temperatures

\lessapprox 2800
K.

Gausset et al. (1965)
reproduced the
{\rm C}_{3}
4050 Å band in
fluorescence and absorption using flash photolysis of diazomethane
mixed with an excess (100:1) of nitrogen. The electronic transition

\tilde{A}{\,}^{1}{\Pi}_{\rm u}-\tilde{X}{\,}^{1}{\Sigma}_{\rm g}^{+}

was found to produce the

{\rm C}_{3}
4050 Å band. Following their rotational analysis,

Gausset et al.
established through a vibrational
analysis that there are two low lying
\tilde{X}{\,}^{1}{\Sigma_{\rm g}}^{+}
levels
at 132
{\rm cm}^{-1}
and 286
{\rm cm}^{-1}
which have to be
respectively assigned as
2{\nu}_{2}
and
4{\nu}_{2}
, leading to the
discovery of the surprisingly low-order bending mode

{\nu}_{2}\approx 64\,{\rm cm}^{-1}
.

Giesen
et al. (2001)
exploited the
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
low-order bending mode
to establish its presence in the interstellar medium along the
line-of-sight towards Sgr B2(M). Reactions involving
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
in
the interstellar medium are understood to determine carbon chain
abundances in dark clouds
(
Loison et al., 2014
)
. Because
{\mathrm{C}}_{2}
and
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
are obvious building blocks for larger carbon molecules
such as
{\mathrm{C}}_{60}
,
Fan
et al. (2024)
located
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
in a
further twenty-seven lines-of-sight for follow-up observations.

Hébrard
et al. (2013)
revised
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
-hydrocarbon models of
Titan’s atmosphere, finding
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
,
{\mathrm{c}-}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}{\mathrm{H}}
and
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}{\mathrm{H}}_{3}
may be
detectable. Results from high dispersion optical spectroscopy

(
Rianço-Silva et al., 2024
)
are consistent with a
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
column
density of
10^{13}\,{\rm cm}^{2}
in the upper atmosphere of Titan.
An updated line list enabled
Lombardo
et al. (2019)
to detect
propadiene
(
{\mathrm{C}}{\mathrm{H}}_{2}{\mathrm{C}}{\mathrm{C}}{\mathrm{H}}_{2}
)
in the atmosphere of Titan using infrared high dispersion spectra;
their spectra could also be used to search for
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
were an
adequate line list available.

Clayton (1996)
reviewed R Corona Borealis (RCrB) stars whose atmospheric
compositions are 99% helium and 1% carbon (by numbers); their light
curves exhibit sharp and irregular decreases in brightness of roughly
6-8 magnitudes, the original magnitude being recovered only after a
slow increase in brightness which follows. Soot formation and
dissipation in RCrB atmospheres is understood to be the cause of the
characteristic RCrB light-curves and it was therefore of some interest
to note that laboratory studies of
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
show it to be the dominant
vapour species over graphite above
\approx 2000
K and identify it as a
potential precursor for soot formation in flames

(
Goulay et al., 2010
)
.
Kameswara Rao et al. (1990)
identify the
4050 Å
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
band in a RCrB spectrum obtained at maximum light
and find it to have disappeared as minimum light is approached,
suggesting that
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
is now absent and soot formation has taken
place.

Experiments by
Weltner
et al. (1964)
and

Weltner &
McLeod (1964)
respectively located the
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
ground state
asymmetric and symmetric stretches at 2040
{\rm cm}^{-1}

{\rm cm}^{-1}

{\mathrm{C}}_{3}

{\sim}5\,\mu{\rm m}

Treffers &

studied the vibrational

{\mathrm{C}}_{3}

{\sim}5\,\mu{\rm m}

tube furnace at 3100 K; they found a broad unresolved feature at

{\sim}2000\,{\rm cm}^{-1}

{\sim}370\,{\rm cm}^{-1}

{\mathrm{C}}_{3}

Note that this allows us to provide absolute transition intensities for all transitions that we consider,
something missing from all experimental studies reported up until now.

2
Ro-vibrational calculations

Yurchenko
et al. (2007)
published the variational methodology and associated program (TROVE) for general
calculations of ro-vibrational energies for an arbitrary small or medium-sized polyatomic. For triatomics, TROVE can use an exact kinetic energy operator (KEO) as developed by
Yurchenko &
Mellor (2020)
for quasi-linear molecules, adopted here for our
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
line list calculations using the PES and EDMF discussed below. We follow closely the calculation procedure described in detail by
Yurchenko et al. (2020)
, where TROVE was used to compute a ro-vibrational line list for CO
2
. The KEO is built using the bisector frame and valence coordinates
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
(bond lengths) and
\rho=\pi-\alpha
, where
\alpha
is a bond angle.

TROVE uses numerically constructed 1D primitive basis functions optimised for a given PES. The stretching basis functions
\phi_{v_{1}}(r_{1})
and
\phi_{v_{2}}(r_{2})
are generated using a Numerov-Cooley procedure
(
Noumerov, 1924
;
Cooley, 1961
)
, while the bending basis functions
\phi_{v_{3}}(\rho)
are obtained by solving the corresponding Schrödinger equation using associated Laguerre polynomials, see
Yurchenko &
Mellor (2020)
. For
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
, the basis set was limited by the following conditions

v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}\leq 56,

(1)

and
v_{1}\leq 30
,
v_{2}\leq 30
and
v_{3}\leq 56
.
Extra bending functions are needed to allow for the low frequency of the bending mode and the large range of angles (approaching 90
∘
) sampled
by the excited bending states we consider here.
The primitive basis functions are then further improved through a two-step contraction procedure. The final ro-vibrational basis functions are formed as symmetrically adapted products (see
Yurchenko et al. (2017)
for details)

\Phi_{n,K}^{J,\Gamma}=\Phi_{n}^{(J=0),\Gamma_{\rm vib}}|J,K,\tau\rangle,

(2)

where
\Phi_{n}^{(J=0),\Gamma_{\rm vib}}
is an eigenfunction of the vibrational (
J=0
) Schrödinger equation and
|J,K,\tau\rangle
is a symmetry adapted (Wang) rigid rotor wavefunction with
\tau
as its parity.
Here
n
corresponds to the maximal value of the rotational quantum number
k
(projection of the rotational angular momentum on the molecular axis
z
) in the ro-vibrational basis
n=10
.
In Eq. (
2
),
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{\rm vib}
are the total and vibrational basis symmetries, respectively. For the two symmetric isotopologues
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
and
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
, the C
2v
(M) molecular symmetry group
(
Bunker &
Jensen, 1998
)
is used to classify the ro-vibrational eigen-solutions spanning four irreducible representations
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.
For the asymmetric isotopologue
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
, the C
s
(M) molecular symmetry group is used for the final ro-vibrational states.
Internally, we employed the so-called artificial extended molecular symmetry group C
nv
(AEM)
(
Mellor
et al., 2021
;
Yurchenko
et al., 2024a
)
to classify the rotational and bending basis functions, which helped reduce the memory requirement.

Potential Energy and Dipole Moment Surfaces

Tennyson (2024)
extracted observed transition wavenumbers
within and between the
\tilde{X}{\,}^{1}{\Sigma_{\rm g}}^{+}

and
\tilde{A}{\,}^{1}{\Pi_{\rm u}}

{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
states from twenty-one
publications, subjecting these to a Measured Active
Rotational-Vibrational Energy Levels

(
Furtenbacher et al., 2007
;
Tennyson et al., 2024a
, MARVEL,)
analysis which yields 1887
empirical energy levels. We refined the

Schröder &
Sebald
PES using those

\tilde{X}{\,}^{1}{\Sigma_{\rm g}}^{+}
corrected energy
levels
Tennyson
lists in his table 3,
with the exception of those at 4081.9, 4199.9, 4333.9 and 4486.7
{\rm cm}^{-1}
for
which the energy uncertainties were comparatively high at
0.5
{\rm cm}^{-1}
.

PES refinement followed the procedure described in detail by
Yurchenko (2023)
and used for many ExoMol line list calculations, including the very recent works on H
2
CS
(
Mellor et al., 2023
)
, CH
4

(
Yurchenko et al., 2024b
)
, OCS
(
Owens
et al., 2024b
)
and N
2
O
(
Yurchenko
et al., 2024a
)
.
The equilibrium bond length and angle adopted were those
Schröder &
Sebald
use. Following
Yurchenko (2023)
, our refined PES was also constrained to the
Schröder &
Sebald
PES at geometries not represented by MARVEL corrected state energies used in the PES refinement.
For the refinements we selected 276 ro-vibrational MARVEL term values for
J=0-10
, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 with low uncertainties (
<0.1

{\rm cm}^{-1}
). These were reproduced with a root-mean-squares (rms) of 0.026
{\rm cm}^{-1}
.

The quality of the fit is illustrated in Table

, where we show TROVE predictions for 21 states compared to MARVEL energy levels for the

state

.

:
v_{1}
- symmetric stretch,
v_{2}
- bend,
\ell
- projection of
J
on to the bond angle bisector,

\sim
8800
{\rm cm}^{-1}

residuals have a mean of
-(3.2\pm 9.1)\times 10^{-3}

{\rm cm}^{-1}
.

Schröder &
Sebald (2016)
construct their electric dipole moment surface (EDMF) from 372 symmetry-unique nuclear configurations. Local transformation to the Eckart coordinate system yields parallel and perpendicular components. Least squares fits gave

Schröder &
Sebald
analytical expressions for the two components which we adopted for our line list calculations.

To summarise, our spectroscopic model consists of a refined PES and an
ab initio
DMS of C
3
, in its ground electronic state. We do not take into account any conical intersections or other couplings with other electronic states, see, e.g.
Rocha &
Varandas (2015)
, nor any pre-dissociative effects – C
3
has a high dissociation energy,
\sim 6.3
eV
(
Rocha &
Varandas, 2015
)
.

4
Line list Calculations

Table
2
lists TROVE input parameters selected,
as well as numbers of states and transitions calculated in each
case.
Adopted carbon atom masses were 11.996709 and 13.00006335 Da; these are nuclear masses, with
core valence electron masses added.
Transitions included in our line lists were limited to those for which the upper state energy is less than
20000\,

{\rm cm}^{-1}
and the lower state energy is less than
10000\,

{\rm cm}^{-1}
. A lower state energy maximum of 10000
{\rm cm}^{-1}
was selected in order to provide the sufficient temperature coverage for
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
absorption spectra simulations, while the upper energy thresholds is to maintain the line list completeness for the wavenumber range of 0–10000
{\rm cm}^{-1}
. Rotation quantum number
J
upper limits were matched to the lower state energy threshold, above which no further population of ro-vibrational states were considered.

State energies in
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
are slightly lower than corresponding state energies
in
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
, resulting in more being selected below a given maximum state energy,
leading to more transitions being calculated in the
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
case.
A much larger number of transitions calculated for the
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
isotopologue is a consequence of
more parity-allowed transitions in the case of an asymmetric molecule. Indeed, only even-parity
states (
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
) exist for
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
and
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
due to zero nuclear spin of
12
C
and the Pauli principle, while the asymmetric species
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
has no such restrictions
and all states are present.

The line lists are provided in the ExoMol format
(
Tennyson
et al., 2024b
)
, which consists of
a two file-type set, a states file and transitions files, augmented by a partition function.
The states file for
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
contains the ro-vibrational energy term values,
quantum numbers, life times, uncertainties and state degeneracies. Each state is
labeled with a state ID, a counting number. The structure of the
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
states file
is illustrated in Table
3
where we show an extract from the “AtLast” line
list for
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
. We note that while the quantum number labels given for levels labelled

Temperature-dependent partition functions were obtained as Boltzmann sums over all states in a line list:

Q(T)=\sum_{i}g^{\rm(ns)}(2J_{i}+1)e^{-c_{2}\tilde{E}_{i}/T},

where
\tilde{E}_{i}
is the energy term value for state
i
(cm
-1
) calculated with TROVE for the
\tilde{X}{\,}^{1}{\Sigma_{\rm g}}^{+}
electronic ground-state of
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
,
J_{i}
is the corresponding total angular momentum,
k
is the second radiation constant (cm/K),
g^{\rm(ns)}
is the state dependent nuclear spin degeneracy and
T
is the temperature (K). Since our partition functions only include states from the ground electronic states and therefore are not complete, especially at high temperatures, they
thus represent lower limits.

Note that ExoMol uses the HITRAN convention for partition functions which include the full nuclear spin degeneracy. The partition functions
Q(T)
for
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
,
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
and
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
were computed using temperatures in the range 1 to 5000 K in 1 K steps using ExoCross, which calculates
a finite sum over all calculated states following (for example) the approach which
Neale &
Tennyson (1995)
adopt. The resulting dependence of partition function with temperature was plotted in Figure
6
. Given energy maxima and the
J
-range selected (Table
2
), partition functions plotted in Figure
6

were fully converged.
Irwin
’s
(
1981
)
polynomial coefficients lead to a
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
partition function lower than our estimate by roughly one order of magnitude; this was anticipated as his polynomial coefficients are based on studies by
Pitzer &
Clementi (1959)
and
McBride et al. (1963)
, predating pioneering work by
Gausset et al. (1965)
who demonstrated the importance of the contribution from the low frequency bending mode. Since
12
C has zero nuclear spin, there is no difference for
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
, but for the
{}^{13}C
-containing isotopologues considered here, our partition functions will be a factor of two larger than standard astrophysical ones which neglect the nuclear spin factor.

Apart from the states, transition and partition function files, ExoMol line list
compilations contain opacities and line broadening parameters. Opacities in the form of
cross-sections and k-tables
(
Lacis &
Oinas, 1991
)

were calculated following the procedure
Chubb
et al. (2021)
describe,
adopting their temperature and pressure grid.
Molecular opacities for C
3
are provided in four files, each formatted for
the exoplanet atmospheric retrieval code identified in the file name:
ARCiS
(
Min, Michiel
et al., 2020
)
, TauREx
(
Al-Refaie et al., 2021
)
, NEMESIS
(
Irwin et al., 2008
)
and
petitRADTRANS
(
Molliére
et al., 2019
)
.

As
Blackwell et al. (1984, their figure 1)
show, an accurate
determination of the microturbulent velocity is required for a solar
abundance determination based on several absorption lines,
using precision oscillator strengths and a one-dimensional static model
stellar atmosphere in which radiation transfer occurs only along an
observer’s line-of-sight.

Asplund et al. (2000)
use more realistic dynamical model
atmospheres, in which three-dimensional radiation transfer is coupled
with the convective velocity field; they show that a
microturbulent velocity is an artefact of the static one-dimensional
model approximation and not needed. Retrieval codes for which
we have provided
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
opacities make no use of microturbulent
velocity and we have accordingly ignored it in our opacity table
calculations.

6
Comparison with Experiment

Martin-Drumel
et al. (2023, their figure 5)
present a Fourier
transform infrared spectrum of
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
in absorption from 1900 to
2100
{\rm cm}^{-1}
, deducing a 700 K rotational temperature and a Gaussian
line-shape of width 0.005
{\rm cm}^{-1}
as their experimental conditions.
Computed
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
transitions and state energies were used with ExoCross to synthesise the
Martin-Drumel
et al.
spectrum, assuming the
same temperature and Gaussian width. The comparison is shown in
Figure
7
: black is used for the

Martin-Drumel
et al.
spectrum and red for the spectrum
synthesised using
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
transitions and energies reported in the
present paper. The wavenumber range plotted in Figure
7

corresponds to the
v_{3}\simeq 2000

{\rm cm}^{-1}
broad absorption feature
plotted in Figs.
2
and
3
.

Figure 7:
The
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
experimental spectrum due to
Martin-Drumel
et al. (2023)
with the transmittance
compared to the transmittance computed (see text) for their experimental conditions using our
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
line list and plotted in the lower panel. The irregular spikes in both spectra correspond to local accidental coincidences of line positions. These responses are very sensitive to the accuracy of calculations as well as experimental resolutions and are very difficult to reproduce, which explains their appearance at different places.

A high resolution inspection of individual lines plotted in
Figure
7
showed a maximum difference between observed and calculated line-positions of 0.03
{\rm cm}^{-1}
, as expected given the formal least squares errors obtained using MARVEL energies for PES refinement described above. In most cases, calculated and observed line-positions agreed to better than 0.01
{\rm cm}^{-1}
as indicated by the state energy
comparison presented in Table
1
.
Our calculated line-intensities are also in good agreement with the observed relative values,
as may be seen in Figure
7
, showing the band structure to
have been correctly calculated. Our values can be used to place observations on an absolute scale.

7
Discussion

Treffers &
Gilra (1975)
showed experimentally that the

v_{3}\simeq 2000

{\rm cm}^{-1}
broad absorption feature completely dominates
the integrated
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
cross-section at 3100 K, with transitions
calculated in the present paper showing that this extends downwards to
temperatures of 300 K. The
v_{3}\simeq 2000

{\rm cm}^{-1}
band was therefore
found to completely dominate the infrared
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
opacity as

Gilra (1973)
suggests. As
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
transitions
computed in the present paper reproduce the
v_{3}\simeq 2000

{\rm cm}^{-1}
band
Martin-Drumel
et al. (2023)
observe, it was clear that our
line lists could be used to calculate the
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
contribution to
molecular opacity at temperatures less than
{\sim}5000
K; this limit
is a consequence of the fact that our lower state energies were
limited to states below 10000
{\rm cm}^{-1}
.

Marigo et al. (2022)
provide low-temperature gas
opacities. In the
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
case,
Marigo et al.

estimate this contribution using the line list by

Jørgensen et al. (1989)
. Our
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
line list is complete to
transition frequencies of 10000
{\rm cm}^{-1}
or less and our associated states
file complete for electronic ground state ro-vibrational energies less
than 20000
{\rm cm}^{-1}
; it is therefore an improvement on the earlier
line list by
Jørgensen et al.
, especially as our
calculated stretching frequencies are in very much better agreement with
experimental determinations. An immediate application of our line lists
for
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
and its
{}^{13}{\rm C}
-substituted isotopologues would be
to update
Marigo et al.
low-temperature gas
opacities. As part of this work, a similar update has been applied to the ExoMolOP Database
(
Chubb
et al., 2021
)
which did not include a
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
contribution.

Use of high-dispersion transit spectroscopy to detect and characterise
extra-solar planets, by
Guilluy
et al. (2019)
for example,
requires line lists for individual molecules having accurate
line-positions and relative intensities. Our
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
line list was
understood to be suitable for high-dispersion transit spectroscopy at
wavelengths longer than
\sim 2.5\,{\mu}{\mathrm{m}}

(
\sim 4000\,
{\rm cm}^{-1}
). Making our
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
line list
suitable for high-dispersion transit spectroscopy at shorter wavelengths
would be complicated by the presence of conical intersections as

Yarkony (1996)
,
Worth &
Cederbaum (2004)
and

Schuurman &
Stolow (2018)
review. Briefly, in the Born-Oppenheimer
approximation for molecular dynamics, nuclei move on potential energy
surfaces corresponding to associated electronic states; these may
intersect at a point in nuclear coordinates with the topology of a
double cone. While
Rocha &
Varandas (2018)
improve on the PES by

Rocha &
Varandas (2015)
and achieve an accurate representation in
the vicinity of the four conical intersections, they do not claim an
improvement for other geometries. Further PES development work will
therefore be needed.

As
Hinkle
et al. (1988)
note, the 4050 Å line cannot
be used to detect
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
in the interstellar medium and cool stars
through the lack of ultraviolet flux; they identify this molecule
in the circumstellar shell surrounding the obscured carbon star
IRC
+10216
using observed line positions from which molecular constants
were deduced. Detection of
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
, where it exits, is important as
chemical models of the environment are then constrained and our
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
line list should facilitate searches when infrared or radio data
are available. A kinematic analysis using lines of molecular species,
identified and for which number densities are known through a reliable
chemical model, establishes star formation modes as

Seo
et al. (2019)
demonstrate.

As it is understood from stellar evolution calculations

(
Sweigart et al., 1989
)
that
{}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
will be enriched
in stars ascending the giant and asymptotic giant branches due to dredge-up,
and
Giesen et al. (2020)
detect
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
and
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
in the
interstellar medium towards Sgr B2(M), future work to improve
our line lists for these isotopologues would seem to be worthwhile.
More laboratory work to create experimental line lists for

{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
and
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
would enable the MARVEL procedure to be applied;
PES refinement should follow for both cases, allowing improved
line lists to be calculated.

Lagarde
et al. (2024)
obtain
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}/^{13}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
abundance ratios for
seventy-one field red giants and identify cases where

{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}/^{13}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
\,\leqslant 10
. The relative contribution to stellar interior
opacity, and therefore to stellar structure, by isotopologues having
one or more
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
substituted by a
{}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
atom,
will become increasingly important as the
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}/^{13}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
ratio decreases.
Additional line lists for the remaining isotopologues
(
{}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
,
{}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}
and
{}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}^{13}{\mathrm{C}}
) could be computed should the need arise.

8
Conclusions

We have calculated new “AtLast” line lists for the electronic ground
state of the
{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
molecule and its two
{}^{13}{\rm C}
-substituted
isotopologues, known to be important in astrophysics. We have made the
transition, states and partition function files available through the
ExoMol database. Our line lists are applicable to studies of the
interstellar medium as well as cool star and planetary atmospheres.
In the
{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
case, the MARVEL procedure has provided experimental
accuracy in all comparisons; the other two line lists are not as
accurate, and improvements to be made in the future have been
identified.

Data Availability

As with other ExoMol line lists, our energy levels (states file),
line lists in the form of Einstein A-Coefficients (trans file),
calculated partition functions and opacities are available in the
ExoMol Database (
www.exomol.com
).
Our refined version of the
Schröder &
Sebald (2016)
PES for

{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
has been included in tabular form, along with a

FORTRAN90
subroutine for reading it,
as supplementary material.

Supplementary Material

Schröder &
Sebald (2016)
’s PES has been
refined using experimental energy levels through the MARVEL procedure.

Schröder &
Sebald (2016, their table 3)
provide non-redundant
parameters which, when used with their equation 3.1, defines the PES
they publish. Lines
4-61
of the file

C3_pes_refined.inp

list our revisions to their non-redundant parameters; in the preceding lines,
the number of data records, equilibrium bond-lengths in Å and the
equilibrium bond-angle supplement in degrees, have been provided.
With the additional file

C3_pes.f90

we have presented a
FORTRAN90
program which may be compiled
and used following

gfortran -o C3_pes.x C3_pes.f90
./C3_pes.x < C3_pes_refined.input

to compute potential energies in
{\rm cm}^{-1}
for the bond-angles and
bond-lengths listed in Lines
62-72
of the input file;

{}^{12}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}
potential energies listed in the same lines should
agree with those computed to the digit.

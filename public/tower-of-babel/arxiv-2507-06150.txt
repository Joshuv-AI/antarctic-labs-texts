Generic mean curvature flow with obstacles
Tim Laux, Keisuke Takasao

Abstract
. We study the obstacle problem associated to mean curvature flow. We add to the geometric vanishing-viscosity approximation of Evans and Spruck a singular perturbation that penalizes the violation of the constraint, and pass to the limit. The resulting level set formulation has unique solutions - up to fattening. Extending the work of Evans and Spruck and a work by Ullrich and one of the authors, we show that generic level sets of this flow are distributional solutions of the obstacle problem. Keywords: Mean curvature flow; obstacle problem; gradient flow; compensated compactness Mathematical Subject Classification (MSC20) : 53E10 (Primary); 35K65; 35A15; 35D30; 35D40



1.
Introduction and main results

Mean curvature flow is one of the most fundamental geometric evolution equations behind many phenomena in science and engineering.
In this paper, we are concerned with its associated obstacle problem, in which the flow is not allowed to penetrate certain regions.
In this paper, we construct solutions to the obstacle problem via Evans–Spruck’s geometric vanishing viscosity approach.
While this process naturally gives rise to the unique viscosity solution of the associated level set formulation, the main contribution of the present paper is a finer analysis revealing the evolution law for generic level sets of this solution:
Our main result shows that almost every level set is a BV solution to the obstacle problem for mean curvature flow.

There is a rich, but still growing stock of literature on the obstacle problem for mean curvature flow.
Almeida, Chambolle, and Novaga
[
ACN12
]
extend Chambolle’s version
[
Cha04
]
of the implicit time discretization of Almgren-Taylor-Wang
[
ATW93
]
and Luckhaus-Sturzenhecker
[
LS95
]
to the case of the obstacle problem and study its convergence in the planar case
d=2
.
Mercier and Novaga
[
MN15
]
studied the graphical case, proved short-time existence of classical solutions, long-time existence of viscosity solutions of the graphical mean curvature flow, and studied (subsequential) asymptotics for large times towards stationary solutions.
We also mention the PhD thesis of Logaritsch
[
Log16
]
in which the implicit time discretization in case of graphs with obstacles is studied in depth. Remarkably, in this case, one can prove the convergence of area and prove a variational inequality for the limit of this discretization.
Mercier
[
Mer14
]
and Ishii et al.
[
IKK17
]
constructed viscosity solutions for the obstacle problem without the assumption of being a graph.
In the long-term limit, the mean curvature flow with obstacle is expected to converge to the mean convex hull of the obstacle.
Spadaro
[
Spa20
]
used this idea to show an optimal regularity result for the mean convex hull via the long-time limit of the implicit time discretization of the obstacle mean curvature flow.
Based on a forced Allen–Cahn approximation, in dimensions
d=2,3
in
[
Tak21
]
, one of the authors constructed global weak solutions that satisfy a version of Brakke’s formulation away from the obstacles.
Together with Nik
[
NT25
]
, this dimensional restriction could be removed.
While these Brakke-type solutions describe the evolution away from the obstacles, they do not give any insight on the behavior at the contact with the obstacles.
Beside the viscosity solution, which relies on the comparison principle of two-phase mean curvature flow, we develop here the first weak solution concept that fully describes the solution to the obstacle problem.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain and let
\phi,\psi\in C^{1}(\Omega)
be functions
such that
\phi<\psi
in
\Omega
.
We want to construct and study level set solutions to mean curvature flow with obstacles
\phi
and
\psi
.
That means that the level set function should satisfy the constraint

(1)

\phi\leq u\leq\psi.

Away from the contact sets
\{u=\phi\}
and
\{u=\psi\}
, this formulation should encode the classical level set mean curvature flow

(2)

\partial_{t}u=\Big(\nabla\cdot\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\Big)|\nabla u|,

while on the contact sets, we need to encode the inequalities

(3)

\partial_{t}u\geq\Big(\nabla\cdot\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\Big)|\nabla u|\quad\text{on }\{u=\phi\}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}u\leq\Big(\nabla\cdot\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\Big)|\nabla u|\quad\text{on }\{u=\psi\}.

We construct our solutions via a modification of the geometric vanishing-viscosity approximation of Evans–Spruck
[
ES91
]
in a potential

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\Big(\nabla\cdot\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}}-V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\Big)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}},

where the potential

V_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\phi-u)_{+}^{4}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(u-\psi)_{+}^{4}

penalizes the violation of the constraint and a variant with exponent
2
instead of
4
was first proposed in the preprint
[
Mer14
]
. Here, for regularity reasons, we choose this higher power.

The main novelty of our work is a connection between this viscosity solution and distributional solutions in the spirit of
[
ES95
]
and
[
UL24
]
.
The latter solution concept is based on the gradient-flow structure of the mean curvature flow with obstacles instead of the comparison principle.
Also the vanishing viscosity approximation has a gradient-flow structure: Defining the energy

(4)

E_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}):=\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}}+\,V_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u})\,dx,

we compute, with
\delta E_{\varepsilon}
the first variation (or
L^{2}
gradient) of the energy:

(5)

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t))=\int_{\Omega}\delta E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}(\delta E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,dx\leq 0.

In particular, given well-prepared initial conditions, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\delta E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,dx\,dt<\infty.

The key estimate that we establish here is

(6)

\sup_{t>0}\int_{\Omega}|\delta E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})|\,dx<\infty.

This estimate is of a different flavor than the gradient-flow estimate above, as it does not include the area factor
\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}
and thus does not correspond to an estimate on each individual level set in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We first verify the existence of viscosity solutions.

Theorem 1.1

(Construction of viscosity solution)
.

Let
g\in C^{2}(\Omega)
,
\phi,\psi\in C^{1}(\Omega)
with
\phi\leq g\leq\psi
and let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the unique classical solution of (
16
).
Then
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
locally uniformly in
\Omega\times[0,\infty)
, where
u
is the unique viscosity solution of (
1
)–(
3
) in the sense of Definition
5.3
.

The proof can basically be found in the literature
[
Mer14
,
IKK17
]
. We include it for completeness in Appendix
5
.

Now we can state our main result. It states that generic level sets of this viscosity solution are BV solutions to the obstacle problem.
In particular, this is the first derivation of (generic) existence of BV solutions in the presence of obstacles.

Theorem 1.2

(Generic level sets are BV solutions)
.

Let
g,\phi,\psi
be well-prepared data in the sense of Definition
2.2
below and let
u
be the unique viscosity solution to (
1
)–(
3
).
Then, for a.e.
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the evolving super-level set
U(t)=\{u(\cdot,t)>\gamma\}
is a distributional solution to the mean curvature flow with obstacles
\Phi=\{\phi>\gamma\}
and
\Psi=\{\psi>\gamma\}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
below.

The proof of our main result, Theorem
1.2
, hinges on the key estimate (
6
), which is the basis for a compensated compactness argument that allows us to prove (an over the level sets averaged version of) the distributional equation

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}u\partial_{t}\zeta\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\zeta V|\nabla u|\,dx\,dt

for any
\zeta\in C_{c}^{1}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}V\nu\cdot X|\nabla u|\,dx\,dt\geq-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(I-\nu\otimes\nu)\colon\nabla X|\nabla u|\,dx\,dt

for any
X\in C_{c}^{1}((0,\infty)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying the constraints

X\cdot\nabla\phi\leq 0\quad\text{on }\{u=\phi\}\quad\text{and}\quad X\cdot\nabla\psi\geq 0\quad\text{on }\{u=\psi\}.

Then the statement for almost every level set follows from the relabeling property, and the co-area and layer-cake formulas.

2.
Main definitions and notation

For simplicity, we assume that
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, i.e., we impose periodic boundary conditions.
In the following, we introduce the main notation that will be used throughout the paper.

Our notion of BV solution is the following natural extension of the solution concept of Luckhaus and Sturzenhecker
[
LS95
]
.

Definition 2.1

(BV solution to mean curvature flow with obstacles)
.

Let
\Phi,\Psi\subset\Omega
be two open sets with
C^{1}
boundaries such that
\Phi\cap\Psi=\emptyset
.
A family of sets of finite perimeter
(U_{t})_{t\in(0,T)}
depending measurably on
t
is called a distributional solution to mean curvature flow with obstacles
\Phi
and
\Psi
and initial conditions
U_{0}
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

Constraints.

\Phi\subset U(t)\subset\Psi^{c}
for almost every
t\in(0,\infty)
,

(ii)

Velocity.

For
\Gamma_{t}:=\partial^{\ast}U_{t}
, it holds that

(7)

\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{t\in(0,\infty)}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{t})<\infty

and there exists an
(\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\llcorner{\Gamma_{t}})\otimes dt
-measurable function
V
with

(8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}V^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}dt<\infty

such that for any
\zeta\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,\infty))

we have

(9)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{U_{t}}\partial_{t}\zeta\,dxdt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\zeta V\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}dt-\int_{U_{0}}\zeta(\cdot,0)\,dx.

(iii)

Energy dissipation relation.
For a.e.
T^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
it holds

(10)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{T^{\prime}})+\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}V^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}dt\leq\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{0}),

where
\Gamma_{0}=\partial^{\ast}U_{0}
.

(iv)

Motion law.
We have

(11)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\left\{\left(I-\nu\otimes\nu\right)\colon\nabla X+V\nu\cdot X\right\}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}dt\geq 0

for any admissible test vector field
X\in C^{1}(\Omega\times[0,\infty);{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})
denotes the measure theoretic (outer) unit normal of
U_{t}
.
Here, a vector field
X
is called admissible if, for any
t\in(0,\infty)
,

(12)

X\cdot\nu_{\Phi}\geq 0\quad\text{on }\overline{\Gamma_{t}}\cap\partial\Phi\quad\text{and}\quad X\cdot\nu_{\Psi}\geq 0\quad\text{on }\overline{\Gamma_{t}}\cap\partial\Psi,

where
\nu_{\Phi}
and
\nu_{\Psi}
denote the exterior normal vectors to the obstacles
\Phi
and
\Psi
, respectively.

Note that our weak solution concept in Definition
2.1
encodes all relevant information of the evolution. Indeed, in case of the (expected) regularity
\partial U_{t}\in C^{1,1}
, we may integrate by parts in (
11
) to get

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}(H+V)X\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}dt\geq 0

for all test vector fields
X
satisfying (
12
),
which encodes
V=-H
on
\Gamma_{t}\setminus(\partial\Phi\cup\partial\Psi)
,
V\geq-H
on
\Gamma_{t}\cap\partial\Phi
, and
V\leq-H
on
\Gamma_{t}\cap\partial\Psi
.

Next, we state the precise definition of well-preparedness for our initial data and obstacles.

Definition 2.2

(Well-prepared data)
.

We call
\phi,\psi,g\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}

well-prepared data
if
\phi,\psi\in C^{1}(\Omega),g\in C^{2}(\Omega)
,

\phi<\psi\quad\text{and}\quad\phi\leq g\leq\psi\quad\text{in }\Omega,

and there exists
L,\ell<\infty
such that

(13)

\max_{x\in\Omega}|g(x)|\leq L,\quad\max_{x\in\Omega}\phi(x)\leq L,\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{x\in\Omega}\psi(x)\geq-L.

and the sets of critical points

\{x\in\Omega\mid\phi(x)\geq-(L+l)\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\phi(x)|=0\}

and

\{x\in\Omega\mid\psi(x)\leq L+l\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\psi(x)|=0\}

are finite.

Remark 2.3
.

The above assumptions for
\phi
and
\psi
are reasonable for our problem.
Let
U\subset(-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})^{d}
be an open set. Assume that
there exists
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
F:(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}\to(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}

F(x)=x
for any
x\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}\setminus(-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})^{d}

and

.
Then

has
C^{1}
-boundary
\partial U
.
Let
\tilde{\psi}:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}

be a

\tilde{\psi}(x)=\frac{128L}{3}(s^{2}-\frac{1}{64})
for any

\tilde{\psi}(s)=3L

\{x\in\Omega\mid\psi(x)\leq L+l\ \text{and}\ |\nabla\psi(x)|=0\}=\{0\}

Similarly, if
\{U^{i}_{+}\}_{i=1}^{N}

.

For
\varepsilon>0
and
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we define

(14)

|p|_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|p|^{2}}.

Let
g
be a (smooth) function on
\Omega

with

(15)

\phi(x)\leq g(x)\leq\psi(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Omega

and
u_{\varepsilon}
be a classical solution of

(16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\left(\delta_{ij}-\dfrac{\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=g(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f_{\varepsilon}=-V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
.
Here and throughout, we use Einstein’s summation convention.

Set
C_{0}:=\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}E_{\varepsilon}(g)
. Then it holds that
C_{0}<\infty

since
V_{\varepsilon}(g)\equiv 0
and
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|\,dx<\infty
.
In addition, by (
5
), we have

(17)

E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t))\leq E_{\varepsilon}(g)\leq C_{0}\quad\text{for any}\ \varepsilon\in(0,1)\ \text{and}\ t\geq 0.

Note that the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of (
16
) is the mean curvature flow of a graph in
(d+1)
dimensions in a potential when
\varepsilon=1
(see
[
EH89
]
for the mean curvature flow of a graph without potential).

Set

\nu_{\varepsilon}=(\nu_{\varepsilon,1},\dots,\nu_{\varepsilon,d}):=\frac{-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}},\qquad H_{\varepsilon}:=\mathrm{div}\,\nu_{\varepsilon}=-\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\right)=-\frac{1}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon},\quad f_{\varepsilon}:=-V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),

where the coefficients
a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}
are the following entries of a positive semi-definite matrix

a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}:=\delta_{ij}-\nu_{\varepsilon,i}\nu_{\varepsilon,j}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}^{2}}

for

.
With this notation, we can write (

\frac{\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}=-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}.

3.
Estimates, weak convergences

The proofs of this section extend those in
[
ES95
]
to the case of obstacle problems.

3.1.
L^{1}
-estimate for the mean curvature with the forcing term

We begin with the key estimate, which will allow us to carry out the desired compensated compactness argument.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that

(18)

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}|-H_{\varepsilon}(x,0)+f_{\varepsilon}(x,0)|\,dx<\infty.

Then we have

(19)

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1),t\geq 0}\int_{\Omega}|-H_{\varepsilon}(x,t)+f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|\,dx<\infty.

Proof.

We compute

(20)

-\partial_{t}\nu_{\varepsilon,i}=\frac{1}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{t,x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}\{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\}

and

(21)

-\partial_{t}H_{\varepsilon}=-\partial_{x_{i}}(\partial_{t}\nu_{\varepsilon,i})=\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}\{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\}\right).

Let
\eta:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth convex
function with
\eta(0)=0
.
Then we see that

(22)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\eta(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx&=\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})(-\partial_{t}H_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})(-\partial_{t}H_{\varepsilon})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}\,dx.\end{split}

Since
\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}=-V^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-V^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}
and
V^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{12}{\varepsilon}(s-\psi)_{+}^{2}+\frac{12}{\varepsilon}(\phi-s)_{+}^{2}\geq 0

and, since convexity of
\eta
and
\eta(0)=0
imply
\eta^{\prime}(s)s\geq 0
, we have
\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}\,dx\leq 0.

Thus

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\eta(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}\{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\}\right)\,dx\\
=&\,-\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{x_{i}}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}\{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\}\right)\,dx\\
=&\,-\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{x_{j}}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\\
&-\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{x_{i}}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\frac{a^{\varepsilon}_{ij}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\partial_{x_{j}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}\,dx.\end{split}

The first right-hand side integral is non-positive, again due to the convexity of
\eta
—now in form of
\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
—, and the fact that

(a^{\varepsilon}_{ij})_{ij}
is positive definite.
Integration in time yields

(23)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\eta(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\Big|_{t=T}\\
\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}\eta(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\Big|_{t=0}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime\prime}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})|\nabla(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})||-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\,dx,\end{split}

where

C_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sup_{x,t}\big|\nabla|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}\big|
.
For any
\delta>0
, we employ a smooth convex function
\eta_{\delta}:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,\infty)
such that

\eta_{\delta}(0)=0
,
\eta_{\delta}(s)\to|s|

uniformly as
\delta\to 0
,
0\leq\eta^{\prime\prime}_{\delta}(s)\leq\frac{C}{\delta}
, and

\mathrm{spt}\,\eta^{\prime\prime}_{\delta}\subset(-\delta,\delta)
.
Substituting
\eta=\eta_{\delta}
into (
23
),
we have

(24)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\delta}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\Big|_{t=T}\\
\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\delta}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\Big|_{t=0}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}C}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\{|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\leq\delta\}}|\nabla(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})||-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\,dx\\
\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\delta}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx\Big|_{t=0}+C_{\varepsilon}C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\{|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\leq\delta\}}|\nabla(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})|\,dx.\end{split}

Taking the limit
\delta\downarrow 0
, since
|\nabla(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})|=0

a.e. on
\{|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|=0\}
, we obtain

(25)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\,dx\Big|_{t=T}\leq\,\int_{\Omega}|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\,dx\Big|_{t=0},\end{split}

which is precisely our claim.
∎

For
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, we define the limit energy

E(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|\,dx,&\text{if}\ \ \{x\in\Omega\mid u(x)>\psi(x)\ \text{or}\ u(x)<\phi(x)\}=\emptyset,\\
\qquad+\infty,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Next, we show the simple fact that the approximate energies
E_{\varepsilon}
converge to

.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a positive sequence with
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. Then

E_{\varepsilon_{i}}

\Gamma
-converges to
E
in the uniform topology.
More precisely, the following hold:

(i)

If

u_{i}\to u
(locally) uniformly on
\Omega

as
i\to\infty
for

\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, then

E(u)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})

(ii)

It holds that

\limsup_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u)\leq E(u)\qquad\text{for any}\ u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).

Proof.

Argument for (
i
):
Assume that
u_{i}\to u
(locally) uniformly on
\Omega

as
i\to\infty
for

\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
By the lower semi-continuity of BV functions, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|\,dx\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|\,dx\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|_{\varepsilon_{i}}\,dx.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Omega}V_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})\,dx<\infty
. Then
one can easily check that

\{x\in\Omega\mid u(x)>\psi(x)\ \text{or}\ u(x)<\phi(x)\}=\emptyset.

Therefore
E(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|\,dx
and

E(u)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})
.
Similarly, if
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
with
E(u)=\infty
, then necessarily
\lim_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})=\infty
.

Argument for (
ii
):
For any
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
we may assume that
E(u)<\infty
.
Then
\phi(x)\leq u(x)\leq\psi(x)
for any
x\in\Omega
. Therefore
V_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u(x))=0
for any
x\in\Omega
and

\limsup_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u)=\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{\varepsilon_{i}}+V_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u)\,dx\leq\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|+\varepsilon\,dx=E(u),

where we used
|\Omega|=1
.
∎

The following proposition states the basic a priori estimates for our approximations
u_{\varepsilon}
. We define
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times[0,T]
for any
T>0
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
T>0
we have the following a priori estimates.

(i)

Uniform bounds:

\min\{\min_{x\in\Omega}\psi(x),\min_{x\in\Omega}g(x)\}\leq\min_{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\max_{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\max\{\max_{x\in\Omega}\phi(x),\max_{x\in\Omega}g(x)\}.

(ii)

Gradient bound:

\max_{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|\leq\max\{\max_{x\in\Omega}|\nabla\phi(x)|,\max_{x\in\Omega}|\nabla\psi(x)|,\max_{x\in\Omega}|\nabla g(x)|\}.

(iii)

Bound on time derivative:

\max_{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}}|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|\leq\max_{x\in\Omega}|\nabla^{2}g(x)|.

Proof.

Argument for (
i
).
We define the excess sets

\Omega_{T}^{+}:=\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mid u(x,t)>\phi(x)\},\qquad\Gamma^{+}:=\partial\Omega_{T}^{+}\setminus\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)\mid t=T\}.

Then
u_{\varepsilon}
is the sub-solution of the standard level set flow equation on
\Omega_{T}^{+}
, that is,

\frac{\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\leq\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\right)\qquad\text{on}\ \Omega_{T}^{+}.

Then the standard maximum principle implies

(26)

\max_{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega_{T}^{+}}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\max_{(x,t)\in\Gamma^{+}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\max\{\max_{x\in\Omega}\phi(x),\max_{x\in\Omega}g(x)\}.

In addition,
u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\phi(x)
for any
(x,t)\not\in\Omega_{T}^{+}
. By this and (
26
) we obtain the second inequality.
The rest of the proof can be shown in the same way.

Argument for (
ii
).
Let
k\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
and

\Omega_{T}^{k}:=\Omega_{T}\cap\{(x,t)\mid\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)>\max\{|\partial_{x_{k}}\psi(x)|,|\partial_{x_{k}}\phi(x)|\}\}
.
We compute

\partial_{x_{k}}f_{\varepsilon}=\frac{12}{\varepsilon}\{-((u-\psi)_{+})^{2}\partial_{x_{k}}(u_{\varepsilon}-\psi)+((\phi-u_{\varepsilon})_{+})^{2}\partial_{x_{k}}(\phi-u_{\varepsilon})\}.

Note that
\partial_{x_{k}}f_{\varepsilon}\leq 0
on
\Omega_{T}^{k}
.
Therefore we have

(27)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon}=&\,\partial_{x_{k}}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)+\partial_{x_{k}}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon})\\
\leq&\,\partial_{x_{k}}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)+f_{\varepsilon}\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\end{split}

holds on
\Omega_{T}^{k}
.
We compute

(28)

\begin{split}&\partial_{x_{k}}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)=\partial_{x_{k}}\left\{\left(\delta_{ij}-\dfrac{\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}\right\}\\
=&\,a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})-\frac{2\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}}{(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{2}}\left\{(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})-\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})\right\}.\end{split}

By (
27
) and (
28
),

\partial_{t}(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})\leq a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})+B\cdot\nabla(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon})\qquad\text{on}\ \Omega_{T}^{k},

for some vector-field
B
.
By the definition of
\Omega_{T}^{k}
,

\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\max_{x\in\Omega}\{|\partial_{x_{k}}\psi|,|\partial_{x_{k}}\phi|,|\partial_{x_{k}}g|\}
on the parabolic boundary of
\Omega_{T}^{k}
.
Hence
\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\max_{\Omega}\{|\partial_{x_{k}}\psi|,|\partial_{x_{k}}\phi|,|\partial_{x_{k}}g|\}
on
\overline{\Omega_{T}^{k}}
, by the maximum principle. Note that this inequality also holds on
\overline{\Omega_{T}}

since
\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leq\max_{x\in\Omega}\{|\partial_{x_{k}}\psi|,|\partial_{x_{k}}\phi|\}
if
(x,t)\not\in\Omega_{T}^{k}
.
Repeating the same argument, we obtain (
ii
).
Since
\partial_{t}\phi=\partial_{t}\psi\equiv 0
, we obtain (
iii
) similarly.
∎

For the convenience of the reader we briefly check the convexity of our integrand.

Lemma 3.4
.

Set
L(p,z,x)=\sqrt{|p|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}((z-\psi(x))_{+})^{4}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}((\phi(x)-z)_{+})^{4}
for
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the mapping
(p,z)\mapsto L(p,z,x)
is convex, that is,

L(p,z,x)+\nabla_{p}L(p,z,x)\cdot(q-p)+\partial_{z}L(p,z,x)(w-z)\leq L(q,w,x).

Proof.

D^{2}_{p}L=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|p|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}}(\delta_{ij}-q_{i}q_{j})_{ij}
, where
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{d})=\frac{p}{\sqrt{|p|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}}
.
Thus,
(D^{2}_{p}L)z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|p|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}}(z-(z\cdot q)q)
for any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Hence, any eigenvector has a positive eigenvalue. In addition,

\partial_{z}^{2}L=\frac{12}{\varepsilon}\{((z-\psi(x))_{+})^{2}+((\phi(x)-z)_{+})^{2}\geq 0
and
\partial_{p_{i},z}L=0
, any eigenvalue of
D^{2}_{p,z}L
is nonnegative.
Hence, the mapping is convex.
∎

The following crucial result strengthens the convergence of our approximation of the levelset solution and states that their energy converges to the energy of the limit. In other words, for each time, our approximations form a recovery sequence for their limit in the
\Gamma
-convergence.
The result hinges on the central estimate from Theorem
3.1
, and on the convexity of our energy from Lemma
3.4
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of (
16
) and
u
be the viscosity solution of (
1
)–
(
3
). Then there exist a subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0

(denoted by same index) and
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T])
such that

\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\qquad\text{weakly-}\ast\quad\text{in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\to u\quad\text{uniformly on}\ \Omega\times[0,T],\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}})=E(u),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

and

\phi(x)\leq u(x,t)\leq\psi(x)\qquad\text{for any}\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T].

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, there exists
C>0
such that

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\|u_{\varepsilon},\nabla u_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq C

holds. Hence there exist a subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0

(denoted by same index) and
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that

(29)

\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\quad\text{weakly-}\ast\quad\text{in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\to u\quad\text{uniformly on}\ \Omega\times[0,T]

by the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and the standard theory of the weak-
\ast
convergences.
We only need to show

\phi\leq u\leq\psi
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}})=E(u)
for this subsequence.

From the convexity, we have

(30)

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\eta)\geq&\,E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\cdot(\nabla\eta-\nabla u_{\varepsilon})+V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})(\eta-u_{\varepsilon})\,dx\\
=&\,E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})-\int_{\Omega}(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})(\eta-u_{\varepsilon})\,dx\end{split}

for the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
and for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Note that we may use
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
as the space of test function because
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
Fix
t\geq 0
and define a signed measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{t}

by
d\mu_{\varepsilon}^{t}:=(-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon})\,dx
. Then
\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}|\mu_{\varepsilon}^{t}|(\Omega)<\infty
by Theorem
3.1
.
Therefore there exist a signed measure
\mu^{t}

and a subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}^{t}\to 0

which depends on
t
such that

\int\eta\,d\mu_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}^{t}\to\int\eta\,d\mu^{t}\qquad\text{for any}\ \eta\in C(\Omega).

Note that we may use
C(\Omega)

as a function space for test functions since this space is separable.
Since
u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)
converges to
u(\cdot,t)
on
\Omega
uniformly and

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}|\mu_{\varepsilon}^{t}|(\Omega)<\infty
,
we have

\int_{\Omega}(-H_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}+f_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})(\eta-u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\eta-u)\,d\mu_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}^{t}+\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})\,d\mu_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}^{t}\to\int_{\Omega}(\eta-u)\,d\mu^{t}

as
i\to\infty
. From this, (
30
), and Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

E(\eta)\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}(\eta)\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})-\int_{\Omega}(\eta-u)\,d\mu^{t}

for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Letting
\eta\to u
in
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
we get

E(u)\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}).

Using Theorem
3.2
again, we obtain

\limsup_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})\leq E(u)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})
. Hence

\lim_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}})=E(u)
for some subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{i}^{t}\}
.
By repeating the same arguments, any subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{i_{j}}\}\subset\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
satisfies

\limsup_{j\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i_{j}}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i_{j}}})\leq E(u)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i_{j}}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i_{j}}})
.
This leads to the conclusion
\lim_{i\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}})=E(u)
for any
t\geq 0
.
By (
17
),
E(u)\leq C_{0}<\infty
for any
t\geq 0
, and hence we have
\phi\leq u\leq\psi
on
\Omega\times[0,T]
.
∎

3.2.
Convergence of the unit normal vectors

In this subsection, we suppress the dependence on time and assume that
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
with
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
satisfy
the following:

(A1)

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1),x\in\Omega}\{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|,|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\}<\infty.

(A2)

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\ \text{uniformly in}\ \Omega\ \ \text{and}\ \ \phi\leq u\leq\psi\ \text{in}\ \Omega.

(A3)

\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\quad\text{weakly-}\ast\ \text{in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(A4)

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|\,dx<\infty,

where
H_{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{div}\,\nu_{\varepsilon}=-\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}}\right)
and
f_{\varepsilon}=-V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})
.

We define the contact sets
\Omega_{+}:=\{x\in\Omega\mid u(x)=\psi(x)\}
and

\Omega_{-}:=\{x\in\Omega\mid u(x)=\phi(x)\}
. Since
u
is uniformly Lipschitz and
\inf_{x\in\Omega}\{\psi(x)-\phi(x)\}>0
,
we have
\mathrm{dist}\,(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})>0
.
Define
\Omega_{\pm}^{\delta}:=\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathrm{dist}\,(x,\Omega_{\pm})<\delta\}
for
\delta>0
. Then one can easily check that

\mathrm{dist}\,(\Omega_{+}^{\delta},\Omega_{-}^{\delta})>0

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
, and

(31)

\inf_{x\in(\Omega_{+}^{\delta})^{c}}\{\psi(x)-u(x)\}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\inf_{x\in(\Omega_{-}^{\delta})^{c}}\{u(x)-\phi(x)\}>0.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\delta>0

satisfy
\mathrm{dist}\,(\Omega_{+}^{\delta},\Omega_{-}^{\delta})>0
.
Then for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
we have

\{x\in\Omega\mid f_{\varepsilon}(x)<0\}\subset\Omega_{+}^{\delta}
and

\{x\in\Omega\mid f_{\varepsilon}(x)>0\}\subset\Omega_{-}^{\delta}
.

Proof.

We may assume
\mathrm{dist}\,(\Omega_{+}^{\delta},\Omega_{-}^{\delta})>0
. Since
u_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
on
\Omega
uniformly, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(32)

\inf_{x\in(\Omega_{+}^{\delta})^{c}}\{\psi(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\inf_{x\in(\Omega_{-}^{\delta})^{c}}\{u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\phi(x)\}>0,

where we used (
31
).
By (
32
) and

f_{\varepsilon}=\frac{4}{\varepsilon}(\phi-u_{\varepsilon})_{+}^{3}-\frac{4}{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-\psi)_{+}^{3},

if
x\in(\Omega_{+}^{\delta})^{c}
,

f_{\varepsilon}=\frac{4}{\varepsilon}(\phi-u_{\varepsilon})_{+}^{3}\geq 0
.
Hence
\{x\in\Omega\mid f_{\varepsilon}(x)<0\}\subset\Omega_{+}^{\delta}
.
The rest of the proof can be shown in the same way.
∎

The main result of this section and the main ingredient for Theorem
1.2
is the following strict convergence in
BV
. This follows from a compensated compactness argument based on the key estimate (
A4
), which in turn follows from Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Under the assumptions (
A1
)–(
A4
),
|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\rightharpoonup|\nabla u|
weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By the boundedness of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
, there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
and a Borel measure
\nu_{x}
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
such that

(33)

\nu_{x}(\Omega)=1,\qquad F(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}})\rightharpoonup\overline{F}\ \text{weakly-}\ast\ \text{in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega)

for any
F\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
, where

(34)

\overline{F}(x):=\int_{\Omega}F(\lambda)\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\qquad\text{for a.e.}\ x\in\Omega

(see
[
Eva90
, Theorem 11]
).
Since
|\nu_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq 1
for any
x\in\Omega
, by taking the subsequence,
we may assume that there exists
\nu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}\rightharpoonup\nu
weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. By using

\|\nu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\sup_{\eta\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ \|\eta\|_{L^{1}}=1}\left|\int_{\Omega}\nu\cdot\eta\,dx\right|,

we have
|\nu(x)|\leq 1
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.
From the boundedness of
|-H_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}|
, by taking the subsequence if necessary, we have

\int_{\Omega}(-H_{\varepsilon_{i}}+f_{\varepsilon_{i}})\eta\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\mu,\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C(\Omega)

for some signed measure
\mu
.
Then we compute that

(35)

\begin{split}&\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta\,dx=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(-H_{\varepsilon_{i}}+f_{\varepsilon_{i}})\eta+H_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta\,dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\mu-\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}\nu\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx,\qquad\forall\,\eta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).\end{split}

Thus the limit of
\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta\,dx
exists.
By Lemma
3.6
, we may choose
\eta\in C^{1}(\Omega)
such that
|\eta|\leq 1
,
\eta=-1
on
\Omega_{+}^{\delta}

and
\eta=1
on
\Omega_{-}^{\delta}
. Substituting this into (
35
), we obtain

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|f_{\varepsilon_{i}}|\,dx<\infty.

By using this and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\|u_{\varepsilon_{i}}-u\|_{\infty}=0
,
we have

(36)

\left|\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}}-u)\eta\,dx\right|\leq\|u_{\varepsilon_{i}}-u\|_{\infty}\|\eta\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|f_{\varepsilon_{i}}|\,dx\to 0\qquad\text{as}\ i\to\infty,\qquad\forall\,\eta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).

By (
35
) and (
36
), we obtain

(37)

\begin{split}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon_{i}}u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta\,dx=&\,\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{\varepsilon_{i}}-u)\eta\,dx+\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon_{i}}u\eta\,dx\\
=&\,\int_{\Omega}u\eta\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}\nu\cdot\nabla(u\eta)\,dx,\qquad\forall\,\eta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).\end{split}

By (
37
), for any
\eta\in C^{1}(\Omega)
we have

(38)

\begin{split}&\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta\,dx=-\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon_{i}}H_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta+u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\\
&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(-H_{\varepsilon_{i}}+f_{\varepsilon_{i}})u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta-f_{\varepsilon_{i}}u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\eta-u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}u\eta\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}u\eta\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega}\nu\cdot\nabla(u\eta)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}u\nu\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\nu\cdot\nabla u)\eta\,dx.\end{split}

Hence

(39)

\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\cdot\nu\quad\text{weakly-}\ast\ \text{in}\ L^{\infty}.

By this and

||\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|+\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon_{i}}|=\left|\frac{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|(\sqrt{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{i}^{2}}-|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|)}{\sqrt{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{i}^{2}}}\right|\leq\varepsilon_{i},

we have

(40)

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|\rightharpoonup-\nabla u\cdot\nu\quad\text{weakly-}\ast\ \text{in}\ L^{\infty}.

By (
33
) and (
34
),

(41)

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|\rightharpoonup\int_{\Omega}|\lambda|\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\quad\text{weakly-}\ast\ \text{in}\ L^{\infty}

and

(42)

\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\rightharpoonup\int_{\Omega}\lambda\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\quad\text{weakly-}\ast\ \text{in}\ L^{\infty},

by taking
F(\lambda)=|\lambda|
or
F(\lambda)=\lambda_{k}
for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})
. By the assumption

\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\rightharpoonup\nabla u
weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}
, we have

(43)

\nabla u(x)=\int_{\Omega}\lambda\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda),\qquad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

By this and (
40
), (
41
),

(44)

-\int_{\Omega}\lambda\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\cdot\nu(x)=-\nabla u(x)\cdot\nu(x)=\int_{\Omega}|\lambda|\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda),\qquad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

Hence

(45)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\lambda|\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)=\left|-\int_{\Omega}\lambda\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\cdot\nu(x)\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}\lambda\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\right||\nu(x)|\leq\int_{\Omega}|\lambda|\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\qquad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega,\end{split}

where we used
|\nu(x)|\leq 1
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Therefore for a.e.
x\in\Omega
,

(46)

\int_{\Omega}|\lambda|\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)=\left|\int_{\Omega}\lambda\,d\nu_{x}(\lambda)\right|=|\nabla u(x)|,\qquad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega,

where we used (
43
). By this and (
41
),

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{i}}|\rightharpoonup|\nabla u|
weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Note that this convergence holds for any subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
, hence
we obtain the statement.
∎

The following corollary shows that, thanks to the previous theorem, energy convergence implies that the potential energy vanishes in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=E(u)
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dx=0
.

Proof.

By the assumption and
\big||\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon}-|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\big|\leq\varepsilon
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|\,dx.

On the other hand, from Theorem
3.7
we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|\,dx.

Therefore
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dx=0
.
∎

The proof of the following theorem is exactly the same as that in

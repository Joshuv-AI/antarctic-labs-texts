On the representation of measurable and continuous dynamical systems by Lipschitz functions
Yonatan Gutman, Qiang Huo

Abstract




Abstract:

Two representations theorems are presented:

1.

Any Borel action of a second countable locally compact group
G
on a standard Borel space
X
admits an injective
G
-equivariant Borel map into the shift space of
1
-Lipschitz functions from
G
to the unit interval
\Lip_{1}(G)
.

2.

Any continuous action of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) on a metrizable compact space
X
admits an injective
G
-equivariant continuous map into
\Lip_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
if the fixed point set
\Fix(X,\mathbb{R}^{k})
embeds into
[0,1]
and
(X,\mathbb{R}^{k})
is
weakly locally free
, that is
\mathbb{R}^{k}
acts freely outside the
fixed point set.

The first theorem generalizes a theorem from 1973 by Eberlein for
\mathbb{R}
-flows. The second theorem generalizes a Lipschitz refinement of the Bebutov–Kakutani theorem proven by Gutman, Jin and Tsukamoto in 2019.

Keywords:
Topological models; Borel systems;
Eberlein’s theorem; Real flows; Multidimensional flows; Second countable locally compact groups; Lipschitz functions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group with a proper
right-invariant compatible metric

\rho:X\times X\to[0,\infty],\qquad\rho(x,y)=\inf\{d(g,e):gx=y\},

Proof.

By a theorem of Becker and Kechris
[
4
, Theorem 5.2.1]
we may assume w.l.o.g. that

is a Polish space and the action of

on

R_{r}=\{(x,y,g)\in X\times X\times\overline{B}_{r}(e)\,|\,gx=y\}.

Let
\pi_{X\times X}:X\times X\times G\rightarrow X\times X
be the projection on the first two coordinates and
\pi_{G}:X\times X\times G\rightarrow G
be the projection on the third coordinate. Let

is closed and the map

is upper semi-continuous and thus by Theorem
2.5
, Borel measurable. Indeed, fix
(x,y)\in\pi_{X\times X}(R_{r})
, and let

be open with

. Set

is closed, conclude

is upper semi-continuous.
Define the map

D_{r}:F(\overline{B}_{r}(e))\rightarrow[0,\infty],\,C\mapsto\inf_{g\in C}d(g,e)

Note that for every

D_{r}^{-1}((-\infty,t))=\{C\in F(\overline{B}_{r}(e)):C\cap B_{t}(e)\neq\emptyset\}.

where the RHS is an open set in the Vietoris topology. Conclude that

upper semi-continuous and thus Borel measurable.

For each

is Borel measurable. Extend

to
\tilde{D}_{r}:F_{\star}(\overline{B}_{r}(e))\to[0,\infty]

. Clearly

is Borel measurable.

Finally we note it holds for all

Thus

being the infimum over a countable collection of Borel functions is Borel.

We now verify the axioms of an extended metric.

Clearly
\rho(x,x)=0
for all
x\in X
. Let
x,y\in X
so that
\rho(x,y)=0
. By definition we may find a sequence
g_{n}\in G
so that
g_{n}\rightarrow e
and

. By the continuity of the action
x=y
.

To prove symmetry fix
x,y\in X
. Note that for all

. Thus it follows

.

To prove the triangle inequality

and
\rho(y,z)<\infty
. Thus we may find

with

gx=y
and

and

. Note

and therefore

\rho(x,z)\leq d(hg,e)\leq d(hg,g)+d(g,e)=d(h,e)+d(g,e)\leq\rho(y,z)+\rho(x,y)+2\epsilon.

Taking

yields the desired inequality.

Appendix C C
Some Facts about Lipschitz Functions

Lemma C.1
.

Let
f,g:[0,a]^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
\tau
-Lipschitz for some
\tau>0
, then
\max\{f,g\}:[0,a]^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\min\{f,g\}:[0,a]^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are
\tau
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

Denote
h(\mathbf{t})=\max\{f(\mathbf{t}),g(\mathbf{t})\}
. Note that for any
\mathbf{s}\in[0,a]^{k}
it holds

f(\mathbf{s})\leq f(\mathbf{t})+|f(\mathbf{s})-f(\mathbf{t})|\leq h(\mathbf{t})+\tau\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{t}\|_{2}.

Similarly
g(\mathbf{s})\leq h(\mathbf{t})+\tau\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{t}\|_{2}.

Thus

h(\mathbf{s})\leq h(\mathbf{t})+\tau\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{t}\|_{2}.

Reversing the roles of
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{t}
, one has
|h(\mathbf{s})-h(\mathbf{t})|\leq\tau\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{t}\|_{2}
as desired. Now notice
\min\{f,g\}=-\max\{-f,-g\}
to conclude the same result for the minimum function.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Let
(M,d)
be a metric space. Let
\tau^{\prime},\epsilon>0
and
0<\delta<1
. Let

and

2.

For all

\frac{d(a,t)}{d(A,E)}\leq\delta,\,\frac{|f(t)-f(a)|}{d(A,E)}\leq\epsilon.

(C.1)

Then for all

and

\frac{|f(t)-f(c)|}{d(t,c)}\leq\frac{\tau^{\prime}+\epsilon}{1-\delta}.

Proof.

Fix

. Let
a\in A
so that Equation (

) holds. We note
d(t,c)\geq|d(a,c)-d(t,a)|\geq d(a,c)(1-\frac{d(t,a)}{d(a,c)})\geq d(a,c)(1-\delta)
.
Thus

\frac{|f(t)-f(c)|}{d(t,c)}\leq\frac{|f(t)-f(a)|+|f(a)-f(c)|}{d(a,c)(1-\delta)}\leq\frac{\epsilon+\tau^{\prime}}{1-\delta}.

∎

Lemma C.3
.

Let

Proof.

By Rademacher’s Theorem (see e.g.

We conclude

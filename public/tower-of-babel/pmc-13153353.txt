Artificial intelligence in periodontal disease research: a bibliometric and visualized analysis of global research trends (2007–2025)
Authors: Wuyan Zhou, Xun Liu, Jinghong Yu, Yaoxu Li, Lin Cheng, Guanliang Wang, Fulin Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPeriodontal disease is one of the most common diseases in stomatology. With the continuous development of artificial intelligence (AI), its integration with periodontology is rapidly evolving. However, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis mapping this interdisciplinary field is currently lacking. We conducted a bibliometric analysis by retrieving publications related to AI and periodontal disease from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) for the period January 2007 to July 2025. Data processing and visualization were performed using R (Bibliometrix), VOSviewer, and CiteSpace. A total of 496 relevant articles (437 research papers; 59 reviews) were included. Annual publication output has shown sustained growth, particularly since 2021. China contributed the most publications (153 articles), followed by the United States. Among institutions, Pusan National University, South Korea (32 articles), and Saveetha Institute of Medical and Technical Science, India (31 articles) were the most productive. BMC Oral Health published the highest number of articles (n = 23). The co-authorship network involved 2,604 authors, with Pradeep Kumar Yadalam being the most prolific (15 articles). Co-citation analysis identified Orhan Kaan, Abu Patricia Angela R., and Falk Schwendicke as the most cited authors. Keyword analysis revealed “periodontitis,” “machine learning,” and “artificial intelligence” as core research foci, while burst detection indicated “progression” and “expression” as emerging thematic directions. This study provides a systematic overview of the research landscape, highlighting evolving trends, key contributors, and knowledge structure in AI applications for periodontal disease. The findings offer valuable insights to help dentists and researchers understand current applications, identify frontiers, and potentially guide the future clinical translation of AI technologies in periodontology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s44445-026-00160-0.Keywords: Periodontal disease, Artificial intelligence, Bibliometrics, Trends

Introduction

Global burden and definition of periodontal diseases

Periodontal diseases are a group of chronic infectious diseases characterized by gingival inflammation and alveolar bone resorption. They include various types such as gingivitis and periodontitis, and are one of the most common diseases in stomatology. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) "Global Oral Health Status Report" (2022), it is estimated that nearly 3.5 billion people suffer from oral diseases, among which periodontal diseases constitute a major portion. In 2021, the age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR) of severe periodontitis globally was 12,498.3 per 100,000 people, and more than 1 billion people worldwide were troubled by periodontal diseases (Wu et al. 2025). The harm of periodontal diseases is not limited to the local oral cavity but may also pose a serious threat to overall health (Hajishengallis and Chavakis 2021). Research has confirmed that there is a two-way influence between chronic periodontitis and systemic diseases such as diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and premature low birth weight infants (Liccardo et al. 2019; Stöhr et al. 2021; Enteghad et al. 2024; Genco et al. 2020). In addition, tooth loosening and loss caused by periodontal diseases will seriously affect the patient's masticatory function and quality of life, increasing the social medical burden (Jevdjevic and Listl 2025).

Diagnostic and therapeutic challenges in periodontology

Traditional diagnosis mainly relies on clinical examinations and imaging evaluations, but these methods have problems such as strong subjectivity, limited accuracy, and dependence on doctors' experience (Khanagar et al. 2021). In terms of treatment, the evaluation of the effectiveness of periodontal basic treatment and surgical treatment also lacks standardized objective indicators, making it difficult to predict treatment effects and develop personalized treatment plans. Recent guidelines from the European Federation of Periodontology (EFP) emphasize the need for evidence-based, personalized treatment protocols, which could be enhanced by AI-driven tools (Sanz et al. 2020). In addition, the long-term follow-up and maintenance of periodontal diseases rely on patient compliance, and the traditional management model makes it difficult to achieve dynamic monitoring and timely intervention (The Lancet 2023).

Artificial intelligence as a transformative tool

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to the theories, methods, and technologies that simulate human intelligence. Its core branches include Machine Learning (ML), Deep Learning (DL), Natural Language Processing, etc. (Haug and Drazen 2023) In recent years, the application of AI technology in the field of healthcare has made breakthrough progress and become the core driving force for promoting precision medicine and intelligent diagnosis and treatment (Rokaya et al. 2024; Haenlein and Kaplan 2019). With the in—depth penetration of AI technology in various medical fields, its cross—integration with periodontal disease research has become a new academic hotspot. The core logic of this cross—field is to use AI technology to solve the difficulties in the diagnosis and treatment of periodontal diseases, and at the same time, to drive the optimization and innovation of AI algorithms with the clinical needs of periodontal diseases (Bonny et al. 2023).

Rationale for bibliometric analysis

Although the application prospects of AI in periodontal disease research are promising, there is currently a lack of systematic bibliometric analysis in this field. Bibliometrics, proposed by Alan Pritchard in 1969, is a statistical method used to analyze literature information, identify development trends and research hotspots in specific fields (Ninkov et al. 2022). This method has been widely applied in multiple disciplines, including medical research (Hassan et al. 2021; Ahmad and Slots 2021; Shah et al. 2021). Conducting bibliometric analysis on publications, countries, institutions, journals, authors, and keywords helps to comprehensively understand the development history of this research field. This can objectively reveal the development trends, hotspot distribution, and knowledge structure of the field, providing data support for researchers.

Materials and methods

Data sources and search strategy

We performed a bibliometric study using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), retrieving English-language records published between 1 January 2007 and 20 July 2025 (date of search)。 Although bibliometric analyses are often conducted with both WoSCC and Scopus, WoSCC was selected for its extensive coverage of reputable, high-impact journals and its wide adoption by the scholarly community. (Darroudi et al. 2021; Zhiguo et al. 2023).

We used the following combination of search terms:

TS = [("periodontitis" OR "periodontal disease" OR "periodontal infection" OR "gingivitis" OR "periodontal diagnosis" OR "periodontal treatment") AND TS = ("artificial intelligence" OR "machine learning" OR "deep learning" OR "neural networks" OR "convolutional neural network" OR "natural language processing" OR "support vector machine" OR "random forest" OR "decision tree" OR "transfer learning")].

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Two independent reviewers screened the titles and abstracts of the retrieved records to determine eligibility based on the following criteria:

Inclusion criteria:

Studies focusing on the application of AI (including machine learning, deep learning, etc.) in periodontal disease diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, or mechanism research.

Original research articles and review articles published in English between 2007 and 2025.

Exclusion criteria:

Studies not related to periodontal diseases (e.g., dental caries, oral cancer).

Studies not involving AI methods.

Conference abstracts, letters, editorials, and non-peer-reviewed publications.

Any disagreements were resolved through discussion or by a third reviewer.

Record export and data management

All relevant records were exported in TXT and BIB formats under the project label "Complete Records and References". Data handling followed a reproducible pipeline in R.

Data cleaning and harmonization

Pre-processing comprised: (i) author name standardization (case, initials and variant spellings), (ii) keyword normalization (merging lexical variants/synonyms, (iii) deduplication across export formats, and (iv) manual relevance checks (titles/abstracts) to exclude false positives.Analytic environment and tools.

Analytic environment and tools

We used complementary software to characterize output, influence and structure:

R (v4.5.1) with bibliometric/bibliophagy (v4.1.3; http://www.bibliometrix.org) for descriptive indicators (annual output, sources, authorship, collaboration indices) and science mapping (Aria and Cuccurullo 2017). Biblioshiny (the web interface to bibliometrix) was used to generate word clouds (top 50 author keywords; font size proportional to frequency), collaboration maps and topic evolution plots, facilitating clear, clinician-facing interpretation of trends.

VOSviewer (v1.6.18) for co-authorship, country/institutional collaboration, co-citation and keyword co-occurrence networks (Eck and Waltman 2014).

CiteSpace (v6.1.R6) for burst detection, keyword clustering and timeline visualization (Synnestvedt et al. 2005).

Network construction and thresholds

Unless otherwise stated, default parameters were used. For VOSviewer-based maps, minimum inclusion thresholds were: 10 occurrences for authors, institutions, journals and keywords; 5 occurrences for countries; and 100 citations for documents. Node size reflects frequency or citations; edge thickness represents collaboration or co-occurrence strength. The distance reflects the strength of the connection between the nodes.

CiteSpace setting

Slice-by-slice networks were pruned and merged using the Pathfinder algorithm. Keyword clustering employed the G-index (k = 10) for node selection, followed by K-means clustering to delineate thematic groups. Timeline views were generated to depict the temporal evolution of clusters.

The burstness analysis of Keywords. The main parameter settings are as follows: Configure the detection model: f(x) = αe-αx, α₁/α₀ 2.0, αᵢ/αᵢ₋₁ 2.0, The Number of States: 2, γ [0–1]: 0.8, Minimum Duration: 1.

Discussion

Publication trends and stage characteristics

This study comprehensively analyzed the development trend of artificial intelligence in the field of periodontal disease research from 2007 to 2025 through bibliometric analysis, covering multi—dimensional information such as publication trends, cooperation networks, research hotspots and frontiers. It provided a quantitative basis for a deep understanding of this field and also revealed the research context and potential challenges behind it.

A total of 496 AI-related periodontal disease studies were retrieved, including 437 research papers and 59 reviews, laying a foundation for analysis. The number of publications has increased annually, divided into three chronological stages aligned with keyword burst analysis trends. In the stable period (2007–2012), research was exploratory, focusing on initial AI attempts in diagnosis, such as simple machine learning for analyzing limited periodontal images (Scivetti et al. 2007; Gomes-Filho et al. 2007; Lu et al. 2012). Concurrently (2009–2017), keywords like "biomarker" and "diversity" dominated, with traditional laboratory methods exploring microbial diversity and disease markers, accumulating basic data for AI applications (e.g., salivary inflammatory factors for model feature selection) (Fine et al. 2009; Carvalho et al. 2010; Fernandez-Botran et al. 2011). The slow growth period (2012–2021) saw expanded research scope including treatment effect prediction, though studies lacked large-scale clinical verification (Scher et al. 2012; Lee et al. 2018a, 2018b; Slavkin 2020). During 2017–2022, keywords "periodontiti", "prediction", and "adult" emerged, shifting focus to clinical applications like adult periodontitis diagnosis/prediction, exemplified by Lee et al.'s (2022) cited alveolar bone measurement method (Lee et al. 2022; Kabir et al. 2022). In the rapid growth period (2021–2025), publications exploded due to mature AI, big data, and policies. Research deepened into multimodal fusion and cross-center studies, with clinical-ready results like high-accuracy deep learning diagnostic models (Li et al. 2024). Correspondingly (2023–2025), "oral hygiene", "periapical lesion", and "expression" marked expansion into segmented scenarios (AI wearable monitoring) and deep mechanisms (gene expression analysis), though such studies need larger samples (Guo et al. 2020; Gan et al. 2024).

The analysis of authors’ production over time (Fig. 5B) reveals that the most productive authors, such as Falk Schwendicke, have sustained research output spanning the entire study period (2007–2025), demonstrating enduring engagement with the field. Their consistent contributions, often involving the development and validation of AI algorithms for diagnosis [e.g., (Yadalam et al. 2022a, 2022b; Brüllmann et al. 2006), have significantly advanced the research frontier.

The node association strength in the VOSviewer map reveals the interaction patterns of research topics, reflecting the current research hotspots and collaboration characteristics in the field. "Periodontitis", as a core node, has the closest associations with topics such as "diagnosis", "machine learning", and "microbiome", which is highly consistent with the current research status. Clinical diagnosis remains the core scenario of research, and AI—assisted diagnosis technologies (such as automatic image analysis) are current research hotspots. Meanwhile, the research on the association between the microbiome and periodontitis also maintains a high level of popularity. For example, a study published in the "Journal of Clinical Periodontology" in 2022 used machine learning to integrate imaging data and microbial data to construct a more accurate diagnostic model for periodontitis. It has a relatively high citation frequency, demonstrating the influence of multi—topic cross – research (Chang et al. 2020b).

The strong association (co-occurrence) among the keywords "artificial intelligence", "panoramic radiograph", and "convolutional neural networks" in the network analysis, as indicated by high total link strength values in VOSviewer, reflects the concentrated application of current AI technology in oral image analysis. Current research shows that panoramic radiographs and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) are the main types of images for AI analysis, and convolutional neural networks are the most commonly used algorithms. For example, the deep learning hybrid model proposed by Chang et al. (2020) for the diagnosis of periodontal bone loss in panoramic radiographs has been cited 67 times, becoming a benchmark study in this field, verifying the deep binding of technology and scenarios. (Angelov et al. 2024).

The association between "big data", "dental informatics" and "prediction" reveals the rise of data-driven research. The current research situation shows that the application of big data technology in the prediction of periodontal disease risk and the evaluation of treatment effects is gradually increasing. However, due to insufficient data standardization and privacy protection restrictions, large-scale multi-center data research is still rare. For example, most of the prediction models for periodontal disease progression based on electronic medical record data are single-center studies, and their generalization ability remains to be verified (Khubrani et al. 2025).

Compared with AI applications in caries detection or oral cancer diagnosis, periodontal AI research shows a stronger emphasis on image-based diagnosis and microbial integration, but lags in real-time monitoring and treatment robotics. This reflects the unique complexity of periodontal diseases, which require longitudinal tracking and multimodal data fusion.

Current challenges and future directions

At present, the application of artificial intelligence in periodontal disease research has entered the clinical verification stage from theoretical exploration, but large-scale clinical implementation is still scarce. Based on the analysis results in the previous text, we categorized the current status of the research at hand, and combined with hot keywords to look ahead to future research. Interdisciplinary cooperation is expected to be further deepened. Multidisciplinary teams integrating stomatology, artificial intelligence, biology, informatics, etc. are likely to be established, forming a collaborative innovation model of "clinical needs—technology research and development—mechanism research". Stomatologists raise clinical questions, AI experts develop solutions, and biologists analyze the mechanisms, jointly promoting the research progress.

A data sharing and standardization system could be gradually established. On the premise of protecting privacy, efforts will be made to promote the establishment of a national and even global periodontal disease database, and unified data standards and annotation specifications will be formulated to provide high—quality data support for AI research. International research institutions can jointly establish a periodontal disease AI research database to promote multi—center cooperative research.

The regulatory and ethical framework should also be continuously improved. Regulatory standards and approval processes for periodontal disease AI products will be formulated to standardize the application of the technology. At the same time, ethical issues in AI applications (such as data privacy and algorithm fairness) will be paid attention to ensure the sustainable development of the technology.

Limitations

There are several limitations in our research that need to be addressed. Firstly, due to software limitations, we only focused on the literature in the WOSCC database and did not include non—English literature, which may introduce publication bias and underrepresent research from non-English speaking regions. Additionally, while our search strategy combined controlled vocabulary and free-text keywords to maximize retrieval, it is possible that some pertinent publications employing alternative or emerging terminology were not captured. This reliance on a predefined keyword set may have influenced the completeness of the analyzed corpus. Secondly, the citation data is affected by time factors. Papers published earlier usually receive more citations than newly published ones. Therefore, some excellent papers may not receive due recognition because of their short publication time. Thirdly, there is a common phenomenon in the literature of citing review articles rather than original research, which may also lead to bias. During the literature search of this study, it was found that Periodontal Diseases, Peri—Implantitis, and Periapical Diseases are different diseases clinically. However, in the MeSH tree structure, Periodontal Diseases is the superordinate term of Peri—Implantitis and Periapical Diseases. The author believes that it may be because Periapical Diseases and Periodontal Diseases belong to the category of periodontal tissue structure lesions histologically, and the peri—implant tissue is functionally similar to the periodontal tissue of natural teeth. Therefore, in the literature index, MeSH regards it as an extended category of "Periodontal Diseases". We followed the classification principle of MeSH and did not delete the research on the above—mentioned diseases, which may have a certain impact on the results.

Conclusion

Through bibliometric analysis, this study systematically revealed the research status, hot topic evolution, and development trends in the field of "periodontal disease and artificial intelligence". Current research has transitioned from technology exploration to the clinical verification stage. AI has demonstrated great potential in aspects such as image diagnosis and treatment prediction. However, it still faces challenges such as insufficient technical generalization ability, data quality and sharing issues, and clinical translation obstacles. In the future, with the deepening of algorithm innovation, data integration, and interdisciplinary cooperation, artificial intelligence is likely to play a greater role in the precise diagnosis and treatment, full—process management, and mechanism research of periodontal diseases, potentially promoting the transformation of the field from empirical medicine to precision medicine, and from single—diagnosis and treatment to comprehensive health management. Building a collaborative and innovative research ecosystem and addressing bottleneck problems at the technical, data, and clinical levels are the keys to achieving this goal. This study provides a panoramic view of the field research, which helps researchers grasp the research direction and promotes the healthy development and clinical application of artificial intelligence in the field of periodontal diseases.

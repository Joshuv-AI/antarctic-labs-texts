Comment on a comparative study of retinal vein occlusion and serum 25-hydroxy Vitamin D at a tertiary care center
Authors: Pradeep Kumar Panigrahi
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We went through the article titled “A comparative study of retinal vein occlusion and serum 25-hydroxy Vitamin D at a tertiary care centre” by Bhattacharya et al.[1] We have a few observations for the authors. The study has been described as a prospective case–control study. For a study to be prospective in nature, the patients need to be checked multiple times throughout the course of the study. In the present study, data have been obtained from a single point of the time. The study design is a hospital-based comparative cross-sectional study. There are multiple known risk factors associated with retinal vein occlusion (RVO). Some of these risk factors include elderly age, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, a positive history of smoking, etc., It will be interesting to know how many patients with low serum Vitamin D levels had one or more of these risk factors. The mean values of blood sugar, blood pressure, blood lipids, etc., might have been statistically insignificant between the two groups, but it will be difficult to prove causation due to low Vitamin D levels in the presence of multiple risk factors in the same patient. Ideally, in the presence of multiple risk factors, the authors should have performed logistic regression analysis to determine if serum Vitamin D levels indeed play a role in the pathogenesis of RVO. The sample size is also too small to determine causation. In addition, elevated serum homocysteine levels have been associated with RVO. The authors should have included an assessment of serum homocysteine in all their patients.

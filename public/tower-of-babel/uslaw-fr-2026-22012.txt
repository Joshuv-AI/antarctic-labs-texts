Overview of This Information Collection

    Abstract: The Prevent All Cigarette Trafficking Act of 2009 (15 
U.S.C. 375-378, 18 U.S.C. 1716E) requires any person who sells, 
transfers, or ships for profit cigarettes or smokeless tobacco in 
interstate commerce to register with the Attorney General of the United 
States (delegated to ATF) and the state tax administrators of the 
states to which the person ships the tobacco products or in which the 
person advertises or offers them. Persons who sell these products must 
report sales into the jurisdictions, and if they engage in delivery 
sales, they must comply with state and local tax laws. In 2021, 
electronic nicotine delivery systems (ENDS) became subject to 
regulation under the PACT Act through the Preventing Online Sales of E-
Cigarettes to Children Act. This act expanded the definition of a 
`cigarette' within the PACT Act to include ``any electronic device 
that, through an aerosolized solution, delivers nicotine, flavor, or 
any other substance to the user inhaling from the device.'' Respondents 
use ATF Form 5070.1, Prevent All Cigarette Trafficking (PACT) Act 
Registration Form, to comply with the federal registration 
requirements. ATF uses the collected information to register applicants 
and to create and maintain a PACT Act registration database for 
registrants and a database for the unregistered/non-compliant delivery 
sellers. The non-compliant list is available to the attorneys general 
and tax administrators of each state, common carriers, and other 
persons the Attorney General deems appropriate.

Revisions to This Information Collection

    As part of this ICR renewal, ATF is making no program changes 
affecting this information collection, OMB 1140-0098. However, the 
number of registrants have increased (596 persons) since the last 
renewal in 2023, from 800 respondents to 1,396. In addition, the 
registration forms have been made electronically downloadable, 
fillable, signable, and submittable by email. This has resulted in a 
decrease in the amount of time it takes to complete the form, from 1 
hour to 30 minutes. The combined increase in respondents and decrease 
in time has resulted in a net decrease in burden hours, from 800 during 
the last renewal to 698 in 2026. ATF has also made minor plain writing 
edits to the form, and has merged the continuation sheet, formerly Form 
5070.1A, into Form 5070.1. Because this is a fully electronic form, 
there is no longer a need for a separate continuation sheet.

The relationships between structure and function of plant communities in the desert steppe
Authors: Shijie Lv, Zihan Wang, Baolong Yan, Hongmei Liu, Guodong Han, Zhongwu Wang, Zhiguo Li, Zhanwen Wang, Xiaohui Song, Saruul Kang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAssessing the relationships between spatial and temporal structures and functions of plant communities is an effective way to understand the changing dynamics of plant communities in specific environments. In this study, we investigated the response of structural and functional stabilities of plant communities to stocking rate in the desert steppe over a 16-year grazing period as the research background.MethodsWe used classical statistical methods to investigate the quantitative characteristics of plant communities over time (2014–2019) and space (2017–2019) at four stocking rates (control, CK, 0 sheep·ha–1·month–1; light grazing, LG, 0.15 sheep·ha–1·month–1; moderate grazing, MG, 0.30 sheep·ha–1·month–1; heavy grazing, HG, 0.45 sheep·ha–1·month–1) in the Stipa breviflora desert steppe of Inner Mongolia. We then examined the relationship between structural and functional stability of plant communities.ResultsOn the spatial scale, the structural stability of plant community was the highest in the LG treatment and the lowest in the MG treatment. The functional stability of plant community was the highest in the MG treatment and the lowest in the HG treatment. On the temporal scale, the structural stability of plant community was the highest in the MG treatment and the lowest in the LG treatment. The functional stability of plant community was the highest in the LG treatment and the lowest in the HG treatment. Affected by the stocking rate, the structural stability of plant community fluctuated more widely on the spatial scale and its functional stability varied more widely on the temporal scale. Nonetheless, the functional stability of the plant community is more responsive to the stocking rate.ConclusionsOur findings suggest that influenced by the disturbance of stocking rate, the structural stability of plant community is more significant than the functional stability in the desert grassland ecosystem, which lays a solid foundation for the study of ecosystem stability.Keywords: Stocking rate, Plant community, Temporal stability, Spatial stability, Desert steppe

Introduction

Ecological stability is influenced by the species diversity of the ecosystem, and higher species diversity of plant community is thought to have higher ecological stability [1]. Such that the species richness of plant community remains relatively constant and is not sensitive to the invasion of alien species [2–6]. However, the corresponding research results have shown that there is no correlation between species diversity and ecological stability, and even negative correlations [7–9]. Accompanied by the continuation of different research results, the maintenance of environmental stability is the result of multi-dimensional and multiple maintenance mechanisms [10, 11]. Such as, diversity insurance hypothesis [12], moderate interference hypothesis [13], resource limitation hypothesis [14], complex food web hypothesis [15–17], and dominant population hypothesis [18, 19], etc. The comprehensive research results revealed that different plant communities or ecosystems have distinct methods for maintaining stability. As a result, the study of grassland ecosystem stability has become one of the ecological hotpots in the past century.

Above-ground net primary productivity is an important ecological and economic index in the grassland plant communities [20]. It is a generally accepted approach to measure the functional stability of plant community by using above-ground net primary productivity or standing crops [1]. Researchers have emphasized the significance of species diversity in this process, but they have weakened or ignored the influence of species composition ratio and interactions among plant species [4, 9, 14]. It is well known that the proportion of species is a fundamental structural property of plant community [21, 22]. Since the complexity of plant community is higher than that of functional groups, populations and individual plants, it is challenging to find out the ecological basis or mechanism of functional maintenance by leaving the structure and discussing the function when researching the stability of plant community. However, the functional characteristics of individual species in plant community reflect the survival ability of species under environmental changes, focusing on standing crops, species composition and their proportion in plantcommunity [23, 24]. Therefore, the structure and function of plant communities are linked through the measurement of stability or changes in the degree of stability.

Grazing is one of the main ways of utilizing grassland. The stocking rate affects the composition and functionality of grassland plant communities. The grazing optimization hypothesis or the moderate disturbance hypothesis are supported by the increased species richness and aboveground production of plant communities at low stocking rates [25, 26]. More studies have been conducted to prove that moderate grazing is an effective way to balance the productivity and ecological benefits of grassland, and improve the productivity and biodiversity of various grassland ecosystems [27–29]. Nonetheless, some studies have shown the opposite conclusion. Due to the extension of grazing time, moderate grazing may exacerbate the magnitude of the decline in productivity and biodiversity in grassland ecosystems [30–33]. When the stocking rate increased to heavy grazing, the species composition and aboveground productivity of grassland plant communities decreased [34]. The cause could be because livestock forage on plant stems and leaves, resulting in decline and loss biodiversity and aboveground standing crops of plant communities [35, 36].

Precipitation is also an important factor influencing the productivity of plant community. It has been demonstrated that the productivity of meadow grassland decreases with decreasing temperature and precipitation [37]. Low and frequent rainfall plays an important role in improving the stability of desert steppe ecosystems [38, 39]. Plant community productivity increased with increasing the precipitation in temperate and alpine grasslands, while plant community productivity decreased with increasing the precipitation in tropical savannas [40]. The dual factors of grazing and precipitation also influence plant community productivity. For example, there is a parabolic relationship between plant community productivity and precipitation when the stocking rate is modest [41]. Under heavy grazing conditions, precipitation has less affects species composition and productivity of grassland plant communities [42]. Therefore, the sensitivity of grassland plant community to grazing is affected not only by stocking rate but also by grassland type and regional environmental conditions, which makes the changes in the structure and function of plant communities more complicated in the grassland-grazing system with regional characteristics.

The Stipa breviflora desert steppe is a transition zone from the grassland to the desert. It has the characteristics of regional transition, ecological fragility and sensitivity to disturbance [43]. This research carried out in the S. breviflora desert steppe. It was explained the relationship between structural and functional stabilities of plant communities from the perspectives in space and time, respectively. The following scientific questions were expected to be solved: (1) How does the structural and functional stabilities of plant communities change with the stocking rate increased? (2) Whether the structural stability can be united with the functional stability of plant communities?

Materials and methods

Study site

The study site is located in Siziwang Banner (41° 46′ 43.6″ N, 111° 53′ 41.7″ E, elevation 1450 m) at the comprehensive experiment and demonstration center of Inner Mongolia Academy of Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Sciences, China.

The study site has the mid-temperate continental monsoon climate zone. The annual average precipitation was 223.0 mm, and the precipitation was mainly concentrated from May to September each year, accounting for more than 80% of the total annual precipitation. According to previous studies by Lv et al. [44], 2014 is an average precipitation year, 2015 and 2017 are dry years, and 2016, 2018 and 2019 are humid years. The test area belongs to Stipa breviflora desert steppe, in which S. breviflora is the dominant species, and Cleistogenes songorica and Artemisia frigida are co-dominant species. The vegetation grass layer is low. Generally, the vegetation is relatively sparse with coverage of 15～20%, and the species composition is relatively poor. The soil is light chestnut soil [44].

Experimental design

The grazing experiment plots was established in 2004, and covered about 50 ha. The experiment was a random block design (see Fig. 1). The treatments were divided into three blocks randomly. Each block (each ca. 4.4 ha) contained four with four stocking rate treatments: no grazing, CK, 0 sheep·ha–1·month–1; light grazing, LG, 0.15 sheep·ha–1·month–1; moderate grazing, MG, 0.30 sheep·ha–1·month–1; heavy grazing, HG, 0.45 sheep·ha–1·month–1. The grazing numbers in CK, LG, MG and HG treatments were 0, 4, 8 and 12, respectively [43]. Grazing was carried out from June to November every year. The grazing management measures were to drive livestock into the grazing plot every day to let them eat freely, and return to the stables to drink water and salt in the evening. The grazing time was from 6:00 to 18:00.

Experimental design of grazing land

Vegetation sampling

To explore the relationship between the structure and function of the plant communities, we used two sampling methods: mechanical sampling in representative treatments and random sampling in different grazing treatments. Each sampling method represents the status of plant community under different stocking rates. The specific implementation methods are as follows.

Spatial quantitative characteristics of plant communities

From August 2017 to 2019, we selected a representative sampling area of 40 m × 40 m in one of the three repeat treatments of CK, LG, MG and HG treatments, which were similar in terms of topography and species composition. Four treatments were selected to represent the LG, MG, HG treatments in Block I and the CK treatment in Block II was selected in order to avoid edge effects because the CK treatment in Block I is located at the edge of the experiment site. In this representative sampling area, there were 77 quadrats of 50 cm×50 cm (Fig. 2). The height, coverage, and density of different plants and plant communities were measured separately.

Distribution of mechanical sampling points in the representative repeating treatment. Each black square represents a 50 cm×50 cm quadrat

Temporal quantitative characteristics of plant communities

From August 2014 to 2019, we randomly selected 10 quadrats of 1 m×1 m in three repeating treatments of CK, LG, MG, and HG treatments to determine the height, coverage, and density of different plants and plant communities. At the same time, the standing crop was clipped at the soil surface and taken back to the laboratory to dry in a 65 °C oven to constant weight (about 48 h) to obtain the standing crop of different plants and plant communities.

Structural stability index calculation

The Godron index was used as an index of structural stability of plant community [45]. The specific calculation method is as follows: the frequency values of different plant species are arranged in order from large to small, and the plant species correspond to their frequency. The frequency of plants is converted into relative frequency, which is gradually accumulated in the order of relative frequency from large to small. The sum of the plant species in the whole plant community is counted down, and gradually accumulated according to the order of plant species arrangement. The percentage of plant species corresponds to the cumulative relative frequency one by one, and the scatter plot is drawn. Each point is connected with a smooth curve, and a straight line is connected at the coordinates (0, 100) and (100, 0) of the two coordinate axes. The coordinates of the intersection with the curve are the required points. The obtained point coordinates were compared with the stable point coordinates (20,80) or 20/80 to determine the stability of plant community under each grazing treatment. This stability measure is called the structural stability index because it includes all plant populations and reflects the total species composition and structure of plant ommunities. Temporal (temporal structural stability index, TSS) and spatial structural stability (spatial structural stability index, SSS) calculations used this calculation method.

Data analysis

Stable point trend analysis

The data on the spatial scale from 2017 to 2019 as the number of repetitions to represent the spatial stability of plant community. In the structural stability, the cumulative reciprocal of species number, cumulative frequency of species occurrence, and structural stability index of plant communities under different stocking rates were listed in a table. The cumulative frequency of species number and cumulative frequency of species were plotted in a scatter plot, and the linear and nonlinear formula fitting was fitted to obtain the trend of the structural stability. In the functional stability, the mean of existing quantity, the standard deviation, and the functional stability index of plant communities under different stocking rates were listed in a table. The standing crop and standard deviation were plotted in a scatter plot, and the linear and nonlinear formula fitting was fitted to obtain the trend of the functional stability. All tables were completed in Excel 2019. All plotting and fitting processes were completed in Sigmaplot 14.0 (Systat Software Inc., San Jose, CA, USA).

The effect of stocking rate on structural stability and functional stability

The stocking rate, structural stability and functional stability indexes (calculated separately in time and space) were standardized (all divided by the maximum value), and then the standardized results of structural and functional stabilities were plotted in the same graph. The influence of stocking rate on structural and functional stabilities were compared, and the relationship between structural and functional stabilities were explained.

Discussion

Controversy and hypothesis of plant community stability

Plant community stability is divided into succession stability [3, 46] and disturbance stability [47–49]. The main feature of succession stability is the existence of dominant plant populations in the plant community, while disturbance stability emphasizes the potential ability of the plant community to maintain or return to a stable stage. Structural and functional stabilities are ultimately responsible for all forms of stability [50]. According to the relationship between the structure and function of the organism, as long as the plant community structure remains stable, its function won’t be greatly affected [21, 22]. Nonetheless, the primary meaning of stability for plant community is succession stability. The primary plant populations remain unchanged because they regulate the composition and activities of plant community [3, 44]. Although the structure and function of plant community fluctuate greatly, both structural and functional stabilities need to be carefully considered.

In the CK treatment, whether the structure or function of plant community, it was neither at the stable point with the highest degree of stability nor at the fluctuating point with the lowest degree of stability, indicating that the natural plant community of desert steppe is affected by climatic conditions. Although it reaches succession climax [6], its stability’s degree is only close to the stable point, leaving room for environmental changes and disturbances to ensure that the plant community remains relatively stable during years, seasons, and even disturbance conditions, which is in line with Clements’s plant community succession hypothesis [6].

In the LG treatment, the structural stability of plant community in space and time was the most unstable, but its functional stability was more stable than CK treatment. This demonstrated that structural and functional stabilities of plant community are not synchronized. Combined with the data in the calculation process, it was found that the number of species is the largest in the LG treatment. Consequently, it was agreed that the mild interference hypothesis did not necessarily enhance the plant community stability [51, 52], and then it was believed that the relationship between disturbance, functional diversity and stability [53]. Therefore, the stability maintenance of plant community in the LG treatment should be the result of functional diversity or functional redundancy [54, 55], rather than species diversity, and this mechanism dominates the functional stability of plant community in the LG treatment in time and space.

In the MG treatment, the structural stability of plant community was the most stable in space and time, and its functional stability was close to or higher than that of the CK treatment. This study showed moderate grazing (MG treatment) resulted in the most stable spatial structure of plant community (Table 1). Combining with the LG treatment, it could be seen that the structure of natural community fluctuates in response to the strength of the stocking rate, ensuring that the structure of plant community has a relative resistance and a certain range of changes. At the same time, the functional stability in time is generally less than that in space (Tables 2, 3 and 4), because the inter-annual precipitation variation has a greater effect on the desert steppe plant community. The threshold of plant community for structural stability has the range of variation between LG and MG treatment. It is evident that under the desert steppe, the natural plant community is not in the most stable or unstable state, leaving room for resisting interference in time or space. In the MG treatment, the structure and function of plant communities are comparatively stable. The Godron index reflects the ecological significance that 20% of the plant population occupies 80% of the resource of plant community [56]. Consequently, it is believed that the stability of plant community is a result of the combined influence of the dominant population and resource restriction in the MG treatment [14, 18, 19].

In the HG treatment, the structural and functional stabilities of plant community were unstablest in space and time. According to studies by Lv et al. [11], heavy grazing severely fragmented the Stipa breviflora plant population, weakening its anti-interference ability to the outside world. The proportion of the constructive species S. breviflora further increased in plant community (including density and standing crop), leading to the deviation of the Godron index (0.2, 0.8), making the structure and function of plant communities on the edge of disturbance. Although the plant community is still a stable state, this is the single dominating species’ control mechanism under disturbance maintenance. In addition to the species diversity of plant community, there is individual diversity within the plant population. This stable state is susceptible to changes in the disturbance intensity or the tolerance of a single dominant species, and seriously impacts the stability of plant community.

In general, the structural and functional stabilities of plant community changed as the stocking rate increased. Firstly, the structural and functional stabilities of natural community (CK treatment) are not the most closely aligned with the theoretical stability point and within the range of controllable fluctuations. Secondly, with the increasing stocking rate (interference factor), the theoretical points of structural and functional stabilities are not synchronous, the functional stability is achieved firstly, followed by structural stability, indicating that the structural stability is more resilient than its functional stability of plant community. Finally, the structural stability deviates from the stable point as the stocking rate increased, indicating that the structural stability of plant community changes and its functional stability continues to decline. At the same time, the plant community is relatively fragile. This change process is not only the succession process of plant community caused by disturbance but also the comprehensive embodiment of resistance and resilience. Therefore, in the process of plant community restoration, restoring structure is more important than restoring function.

The relationship between structural stability and functional stability

On the whole, whether in time or space, the structural stability of plant community obeys the sine curve distribution, and the model is y = a1sin(ωx + φ) + b1. The difference is that the variation range (ω value) on the spatial scale is greater than the temporal scale, potentially indicating the complex spatial niche survive for more species [57, 58]. The functional stability of plant community obeys the binary linear equation with a downward opening, and the model is y = a2 × 2 + b2x+c2. The difference is that the maximum value on the spatial scale is greater than the temporal scale, and the changing range of spatial scale is less than temporal scale, which means that the functional stability of plant community has higher resistance in space. It is evident that the structure and function of plant communities have a higher resistance. The reason is that high-intensity grassland use (high stocking rate) leads to the homogenization of soil resources [59], reducing the stability of plant community. Figure 6 illustrated that in the absence of grazing, c2 < b1, which further showing that the change of stocking rate initially impacts the structural stability, and the functional stability lags behind the structural stability of plant community.

Conclusion

Affected by the stocking rate, the fluctuation range of structural stability of plant community was larger on the spatial scale, and the variation of functional stability of plant community was larger on temporal scale. However, in both spatial and temporal scales, the functional stability of plant community is more sensitive to the response of stocking rate. Therefore, the structure and function of the natural plant communities (CK treatment) are not the closest to the theoretical stability point, but between the adjustable fluctuation range. The theoretical structural and functional stability points are not synchronized, and the structure determines the function of plant community. For vegetation restoration of plant community with severe livestock interference, restoring the structural stability is more important than the functional stability.

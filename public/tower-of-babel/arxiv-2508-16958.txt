The cut-off resolvent can grow arbitrarily fast in obstacle scattering Thanks: Submitted to the editors 23 August 2025.
Simon N. Chandler-Wilde, Siavash Sadeghi

Abstract
We consider time-harmonic acoustic scattering by a compact sound-soft obstacle Γ ⊂ ℝ n \Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} ( n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ) that has connected complement Ω := ℝ n ∖ Γ \Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma . This scattering problem is modelled by the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation Δ ​ u + k 2 ​ u = − f \Delta u+k^{2}u=-f in Ω \Omega , the boundary condition that u = 0 u=0 on ∂ Ω = ∂ Γ \partial\Omega=\partial\Gamma , and the standard Sommerfeld radiation condition. It is well-known that, if the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega is smooth, then the norm of the cut-off resolvent of the Laplacian, that maps the compactly supported inhomogeneous term f f to the solution u u restricted to some ball, grows at worst exponentially with k k . In this paper we show that, if no smoothness of Γ \Gamma is imposed, then the growth can be arbitrarily fast. Precisely, given some modestly increasing unbounded sequence 0 < k 1 < k 2 < … 0<k_{1}<k_{2}<\ldots and some arbitrarily rapidly increasing sequence 0 < a 1 < a 2 < … 0<a_{1}<a_{2}<\ldots , we construct a compact Γ \Gamma such that, for each j ∈ ℕ j\in\mathbb{N} , the norm of the cut-off resolvent at k = k j k=k_{j} is > a j >a_{j} .



keywords

Helmholtz equation, scattering, trapping, resolvent

Introduction

Given some compact set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) such that
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma
is connected, and some compactly supported source term
f\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{comp}}(\Omega)
, a classic problem in scattering theory is to seek
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
that is a solution of the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation

\Delta u+k^{2}u=-f,

(1)

in a weak sense in
\Omega
, and that satisfies the
Sommerfeld radiation condition

\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(x)-\mathrm{i}ku(x)=o\left(r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\right),\quad\text{ uniformly in }\frac{x}{r},\quad\mbox{ as }r:=\lvert x\rvert\rightarrow\infty.

(2)

In the above equations
k>0
is the given wavenumber, and the function space notations we use are recalled in §
2.1
.
It is well known that this scattering problem, which imposes on
\partial\Omega
a zero boundary condition on
u
through the requirement that
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
, is well-posed. Indeed, uniqueness is proved via Green’s theorem and Rellich’s lemma, and existence and continuous dependence on data is proved, e.g., by
a priori
estimates coupled with a limiting absorption argument (see, e.g.,
[
38
,
27
,
19
]
).

1.1
The question we address

It follows from the well-posedness of the above scattering problem that, given any
R>R_{\Gamma}:=\max_{x\in\Gamma}|x|
and any
k>0
, there exists some minimal
C_{k,R}>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C_{k,R}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})},

(3)

whenever
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset\Omega_{R}:=\{x\in\Omega:|x|<R\}
. This paper is concerned with answering the following question:

For fixed
R>R_{\Gamma}
, how fast can
C_{k,R}
grow as
k\to\infty
?

(4)

Let us make two comments regarding (
4
). First, note that this question is commonly equivalently formulated in terms of norms of the resolvent of the Laplacian. By the above well-posedness there is, for each
k>0
, a well-defined resolvent operator

R(k):=(-\Delta-k^{2})^{-1}:L^{2}_{\mathrm{comp}}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega),

(5)

that maps
f\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{comp}}(\Omega)
continuously to the solution
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
of the above scattering problem. For
R>R_{\Gamma}
let
\chi_{R}
denote the characteristic function of
\Omega_{R}
. Then, for
k>0
and
R>R_{\Gamma}
, the minimal
C_{k,R}
in (
3
) is precisely

C_{k,R}:=\|\chi_{R}R(k)\chi_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)},

(6)

the norm of the so-called
cut-off resolvent

\chi_{R}R(k)\chi_{R}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Second, a natural variant to (
4
) is to ask how the constant
C_{k,R}
changes if the
L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
norm of
u
in (
3
) is replaced by an
H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
norm, in particular by the natural wavenumber-explicit scaled norm
\|\cdot\|_{H_{k}^{1}(\Omega_{R})}
, defined, for
k>0
, by

\|v\|^{2}_{H_{k}^{1}(\Omega_{R})}=\||\nabla v|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}+k^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2},\quad v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R}).

(7)

In other words, what is the minimal
C_{k,R}^{\prime}>0
such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{k}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C^{\prime}_{k,R}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})},

(8)

whenever
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset\Omega_{R}
, i.e., what is

C^{\prime}_{k,R}:=\|\chi_{R}R(k)\chi_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{k}(\Omega_{R})}\;?

(9)

This question is equivalent to (
4
): the behaviour of
C_{k,R}^{\prime}
is evident once that of
C_{k,R}
is known (and vice versa). Clearly,
C_{k,R}^{\prime}\geq kC_{k,R}
. Moreover, a simple Green’s theorem argument, coupled with an estimate for a boundary integral that is well-known in dimension
n=2,3
, and that we establish for all
n\geq 2
in Lemma
A.1
in the appendix, establishes that

kC_{k,R}\leq C_{k,R}^{\prime}\leq\sqrt{2k^{2}C_{k,R}^{2}+C_{k,R}},\qquad k>0,\;\;R>R_{\Gamma}

(10)

(see §
2.2
below).

1.2
Discussion of previous work

There has been, over many decades, large research interest in (

[

In mild, so-called
hyperbolic trapping
(exemplified by the case where

,

C_{k,R}\leq C_{R}\frac{\log(2+k)}{k},\qquad k\geq k_{0},

(13)

for some

. Similarly in so-called
parabolic trapping
(exemplified by the case where
\Gamma

with

is only algebraically worse; the sharpest bound for the class of obstacles covered by

R>R_{\Gamma}

C_{k,R}\leq C_{R}k,\qquad k\geq k_{0},

By contrast, if
\Gamma
has an ellipse-shaped cavity (see Figure

can be achieved. Precisely, there exists an unbounded sequence of wavenumbers
0<k_{1}<k_{2}<\ldots
and an

such that, for all
R>R_{\Gamma}
,

C_{k_{j},R}\geq C_{R}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha k_{j}},\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,

(15)

for some
C_{R}>0
;

[

\Gamma=\overline{\Omega_{-}}

\Omega_{-}

\exp{(\alpha k_{j}^{q})}

as above and for some range of

5

(note that Popov proved

[

If one restricts attention to smooth scatterers, i.e.,
\Gamma=\overline{\Omega_{-}}

\Omega_{-}

(16)

We have noted above that, for a smooth scatterer, exponential growth with
k

C_{k,R}
can be achieved through some sequence in cases of strong, elliptic trapping. But it has been shown recently by Lafontaine et al.
[

that, for most wavenumbers, i.e., for all
k\geq k_{0}
outside some set
J
of arbitrarily small measure, growth is at worst algebraic in the case that
\Gamma=\overline{\Omega_{-}}
for some Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{-}
.

Theorem 1.3
(
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Let
\Omega_{-}
be a bounded Lipschitz open set such that
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma
is connected, where
\Gamma:=\overline{\Omega_{-}}
. Then, given
k_{0}>0
,
R>R_{\Gamma}
,
\delta>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C=C(k_{0},R,\delta,\varepsilon,n)
and a set

with Lebesgue measure

C_{k,R}\leq Ck^{5n/2+\varepsilon},\qquad k\in[k_{0},\infty)\setminus J.

1.3
Our main results

The main contribution of this paper is to show that, in contrast to the bounds (
11
), (
13
), (
14
), and (
16
) above, if one does not impose any smoothness or other geometrical constraint on
\Gamma
, then, in a sense we make precise, there is no constraint on growth, through some sequence, of the cut-off resolvent as a function of
k
.
The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose the sequences
(k_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are positive, increasing and unbounded, and that,
for some
c>0
,

k_{j}\geq c(j\log(j+\mathrm{e}))^{1/n}\log^{2}(\log(j+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}})),\qquad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(17)

Then there exists a compact
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma
connected, such that, for all
R>R_{\Gamma}=\max_{x\in\Gamma}|x|
it holds that
C_{k_{j},R}=\lVert\chi_{R}R(k_{j})\chi_{R}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq a_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

.

Suppose the sequences
(k_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

\ell_{j}:=\pi\sqrt{n}k_{j}^{-1}

j^{-1/n}k_{j}\geq\pi n^{1/2}\left(|\mathcal{O}_{\infty}|_{n}\right)^{-1/n},\qquad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(26)

This last inequality in fact implies (
21
); we have that
2(\Gamma(1+n/2))^{1/n}\leq\sqrt{\pi n}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with equality only for
n=1
, which follows by induction on
n
(for odd and even
n
separately).

The following remark explores the relationship between (i)-(iii) in the above theorem. Here and subsequently we use the notation

B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<R\},\qquad R>0.

Remark 1.8
.

(Conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
1.5
do not imply condition (iii)) It might be imagined that (i)-(ii) in Theorem
1.5
automatically imply that (iii) holds. This is not the case, as is shown by the following example, inspired by the “Swiss cheese sets” of Polking
[
29
]
(and see
[
7
, Example 8.3]
).

Let
\{q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a dense set of distinct points in
\partial B_{1}\setminus\{\pm e_{1},\ldots,\pm e_{n}\}
, where
e_{m}
denotes the unit vector in the
m
th coordinate direction. Let us describe a recursive placement of the cubes
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2},\ldots
in the open unit ball
B_{1}
, such that all the conditions of Theorem
1.5
except (iii) are satisfied. Set
\mathcal{O}_{1}:=\ell_{1}\mathcal{O}+t_{1}
, choosing
\ell_{1}>0
and
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset\overline{B_{1}}
and
B_{1/2}\cap\mathcal{O}_{1}=\emptyset
, and such that
\partial\mathcal{O}_{1}\cap\partial B_{1}=\{q_{1}\}
; this last requirement is possible since
q_{1}\neq\pm e_{m}
, for
m=1,\ldots,n
. Having chosen
\mathcal{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{j}
, for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\mathcal{O}_{j+1}:=\ell_{j+1}\mathcal{O}+t_{j+1}
, choosing
\ell_{j+1}>0
and
t_{j+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that
\ell_{j+1}<\ell_{j}
,
\mathcal{O}_{j+1}\subset\overline{B_{1}}
,
B_{1/2}\cap\mathcal{O}_{j+1}=\emptyset
, and
\partial\mathcal{O}_{j+1}\cap\partial B_{1}=\{q_{j+1}\}
, and such that
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{j+1}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}=\emptyset
,
i=1,\ldots,j
.

The sequence
(\mathcal{O}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of cubes formed by this construction clearly satisfies (i) and (ii) in Theorem
1.5
. In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{j}\subset B_{1}
and
B_{1/2}\cap\mathcal{O}_{\infty}=\emptyset
. Further, since
\partial\mathcal{O}_{j}\cap\partial B_{1}=\{q_{j}\}
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that
\Gamma_{j}\cap\partial B_{1}=\{q_{j}\}
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\Gamma_{j}:=\ell_{j}\Gamma^{\varepsilon_{j}}+t_{j}
, for some
\varepsilon_{j}\in(0,1)
(note that this definition for
\Gamma_{j}
implies that
\Gamma_{j}\subset\partial\mathcal{O}_{j}
, and that
\Gamma_{j}
contains all the corners of the cube
\mathcal{O}_{j}
). It follows that
\partial B_{1}\subset\Gamma:=\overline{\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{j}}\subset\overline{B_{1}}
and that
B_{1/2}\cap\Gamma=\emptyset
. Thus (iii) is not satisfied, i.e.,
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma
is not connected. Further, if
(k_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined by
k_{j}:=\pi\sqrt{n}\ell_{j}^{-1}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then
k_{j}
and
\ell_{j}
are related as in Theorem
1.5
and
(k_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a positive, increasing, unbounded sequence. If
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is some other positive, increasing, unbounded sequence and
\varepsilon_{j}
is defined by (
18
), for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then all the conditions of Theorem
1.5
are satisfied, except that (iii) does not hold.

The bound (
17
) implies all of (
21
), (
23
), (
24
), and (
26
), but is only logarithmically worse than the growth required by these constraints, indeed (
17
) no longer implies (
23
) if the
\log^{2}(\log(j+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}}))
factor in (
17
) is removed.
Nevertheless, our next theorem shows that, if
(k_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
17
) and is increasing, then it is possible to choose the translations
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
so that (i)-(iii) in Theorem
1.5
hold, so that
C_{k_{j},R}>a_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, establishing Theorem
1.4
. In a recent preprint (see
[
12
, §4]
), Chaumont-Frelet has proved, in the case
n=3
, a slightly less sharp result than the result below, showing, by a similar but more elaborate construction, that if, for some
c>0
and
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\ell_{j}\leq c/j^{1/3+\varepsilon}
, for

, then there exists
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that (i) and (ii) in Theorem

hold.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose that the sequence
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
and, for some

,

0<\ell_{j}\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{(j\log(j+\mathrm{e}))^{1/n}\log^{2}(\log(j+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}}))},\qquad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(27)

Then there exists a choice of the sequence of translations

such that the sequences
(\mathcal{O}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Gamma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, defined by

\mathcal{O}_{j}:=\ell_{j}\mathcal{O}+t_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma_{j}:=\ell_{j}\Gamma^{\varepsilon_{j}}+t_{j},\qquad j\in\mathbb{N},

satisfy (i)-(iii) of Theorem

The actual choice of the translations

required to achieve (i)-(iii) will be made clear in the proof of Theorem

. In Figure

, and

,

. In this example, so that the values of

1.4
Further questions

We note two further questions related to our main results. The first of these we address elsewhere, the second appears open.

Question 1

Theorem
1.4
tells us that
C_{k,R}
can increase arbitrarily fast through any mildly increasing sequence
(k_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
that satisfies (
17
) if our only requirement on
\Gamma
is that it is compact with connected complement. So does Theorem
1.3
still hold if its requirement that
\Gamma
be the closure of a bounded Lipschitz domain is relaxed to allow arbitrary compact
\Gamma
? In a paper in preparation
[
10
]
we answer this question in the affirmative, at the same time making a slight sharpening of the original proof, showing the following result.

Theorem 1.10
.

Suppose that
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is compact and
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma
is connected. Then, given
k_{0}>0
,
R>R_{\Gamma}
,
\delta>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C=C(k_{0},R,\delta,\varepsilon,n)
and a set
J\subset[k_{0},\infty)
with Lebesgue measure
|J|\leq\delta
, such that

C_{k,R}\leq Ck^{2n+\varepsilon},\qquad k\in[k_{0},\infty)\setminus J.

Our proof of this result uses the general
black-box
version,
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
, of Theorem
1.3
from
[
16
]
, refining the proof of that result by use of a sharper semiclassical maximum principle.

Question 2

Theorem
1.4
tells us that
C_{k,R}
can grow arbitrarily fast, through some sequence, as a function of
k
, if all we require is that
\Gamma
is compact with connected complement, whereas no worse than exponential growth is possible, by the bound (
16
) of
[
3
]
, if
\Gamma=\overline{\Omega_{-}}
, for some bounded
C^{\infty}
domain
\Omega_{-}
. So, if
\Gamma=\overline{\Omega_{-}}
and
\Omega_{-}
is not
C^{\infty}
but has less smoothness, e.g., is Lipschitz or
C^{0}
(as defined, e.g., in
[
19
]
), is there still a limit on how fast
C_{k,R}
can grow as
k\to\infty
, and is this limit still one of exponential growth?

Related to this question one can modify our construction of
\Gamma
in Theorems
1.5
and
1.9
, replacing
\mathcal{O}
and
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
by thickened versions,
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}:=(-1,2)^{n}
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\overline{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}}\setminus

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}\cup\{(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{$-1\leq x_{n}\leq 0$ and $0<x_{i}<\varepsilon$ for $i=1,...,n-1$}\}),

and defining each
\Omega_{j}
and
\Gamma_{j}
with
\mathcal{O}
replaced by
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
and
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
replaced by
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}
. Theorem
1.5
holds, as written, with this modification, and the construction of Theorem
1.9
follows through with trivial edits. Since the complement of
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}
is a connected Lipschitz domain, the boundary of the resultant connected domain
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Gamma
is locally the graph of a Lipschitz function at all
x
except the single point
x\in\Gamma\cap\partial\Omega
that is not in
\Gamma_{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
(cf. (
48
)). This demonstrates that it is enough for
\partial\Omega
to fail to be locally a Lipschitz graph at a single point for arbitrarily fast growth of
C_{k,R}
, through some sequence, as a function of
k
, to be achievable.

2.1
Function spaces

For definiteness, we note our (largely standard) function space notations. Given a domain (i.e., a non-empty open set)
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

H^{1}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\nabla u\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}\},

where
\nabla u
denotes the weak or distributional gradient. We recall that
H^{1}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
defined by

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

H^{1}(\Omega)
is also a Hilbert space when equipped with the (equivalent)
k
-dependent norm (
7
), for some
k>0
.
Let
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
denote the set of
C^{\infty}
functions compactly supported in
\Omega
and let

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

:=

\text{clos}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}(C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega))
, the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, and let
H^{1}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega):=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}
. We will also use Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Gamma)
, for
|s|\leq 1
, in the case that
\Gamma
is the bounded boundary of some Lipschitz domain, defined as in
[
19
]
or
[
6
]
.

We will use certain spaces of compactly supported and locally integrable functions. Let
L^{2}_{\mathrm{comp}}(\Omega)
denote the space of those functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with bounded support, a topological vector space (indeed an LF-space, e.g.,
[
35
]
) in which a sequence
(u_{n})
is convergent if it is convergent in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and, for some bounded
V\subset\Omega
,
\mathrm{supp}(u_{n})\subset V
for each
n
. Let
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
denote the set of functions on
\Omega
that satisfy
u|_{V}\in L^{2}(V)
for every bounded, measurable
V\subset\Omega
, a Fréchet space with the obvious topology (e.g.,
[
38
, p. 52]
) for which a sequence
(u_{n})\subset L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is convergent if
(u_{n}|_{V})
is convergent in
L^{2}(V)
for every bounded measurable
V\subset\Omega
. Let

\displaystyle H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega):\nabla u\in(L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))^{n}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\chi|_{\Omega}\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega),\forall\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

\displaystyle H_{0}^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega):\chi|_{\Omega}\,u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\forall\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

and

H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega):=\{u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Omega):\chi|_{\Omega}u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega),\forall\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

2.2
A weak formulation of the scattering problem

Key to our arguments will be a standard reformulation (e.g.,
[
26
, Theorem 2.6.6]
) of our scattering problem as a variational problem in
\Omega_{R}=\Omega\cap B_{R}
, for some
R>R_{\Gamma}=\max_{x\in\Gamma}|x|
. This variational formulation is set in the closed subspace
V_{R}\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
defined by

V_{R}:=\{v|_{\Omega_{R}}:v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\}.

(28)

Note that, where
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
is the solution to the scattering problem,
u_{R}:=u|_{\Omega_{R}}\in V_{R}
, for
R>R_{\Gamma}
.

For
R>R_{\Gamma}
and
k>0
let
\Gamma_{R}:=\partial B_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=R\}
and let
\gamma_{R}:V_{R}\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
denote the standard trace operator. In the appendix we recall that there is a well-defined Dirichlet to Neumann map
\mathrm{DtN}_{R}:H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\to H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
for the domain
G_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>R\}
exterior to
B_{R}
. This maps
\psi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
to the normal derivative
\partial_{r}w\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
of the unique solution
w\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(G_{R})
of the homogeneous Helmholtz equation in
G_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>R\}
that satisfies the radiation condition (
2
) and the Dirichlet boundary condition
\gamma_{R}^{+}w=\psi
, where
\gamma_{R}^{+}:H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(G_{R})\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
is the exterior trace operator. Note that if
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
satisfies the scattering problem, then
u|_{G_{R}}
satisfies this Dirichlet problem with
\psi=\gamma^{+}_{R}u=\gamma_{R}u
, so that

\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right|_{\Gamma_{R}}=\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\gamma_{R}u.

(29)

For
R>R_{\Gamma}
and
k>0
define the bounded sesquilinear form
a_{k,R}:V_{R}\times V_{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

a_{k,R}(v,w):=\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\overline{w}-k^{2}v\overline{w})\,\mathrm{d}x-\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\gamma_{R}v,\gamma_{R}w\rangle,\qquad v,w\in V_{R},

(30)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing on
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
.

To obtain the variational formulation we choose
R>R_{\Gamma}
so that
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset\Omega_{R}
.
We multiply (
1
), restricted to
\Omega_{R}
, by
\overline{v_{R}}
, the complex conjugate of some
v_{R}\in V_{R}
, and apply the divergence theorem. This leads, using (
29
), to the variational formulation that
u_{R}:=u|_{\Omega_{R}}\in V_{R}
satisfies

a_{k,R}(u_{R},v_{R})=G(v_{R}),\qquad\forall v_{R}\in V_{R},

(31)

where the continuous anti-linear functional
G:V_{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

G(v_{R}):=(f,v_{R})_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})},\qquad v_{R}\in V_{R},

and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}
is the standard inner product on
L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
. We have shown one part of the following standard result (e.g.,
[
26
]
,
[
33
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

If
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
satisfies the scattering problem then, for all
R>R_{\Gamma}
with
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset\Omega_{R}
,
u_{R}:=u|_{\Omega_{R}}\in V_{R}
satisfies the variational problem (
31
). Conversely, if, for some
R>R_{\Gamma}
with
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset\Omega_{R}
,
u_{R}\in V_{R}
satisfies (
31
) and we set
u(x):=u_{R}(x)
,
x\in\Omega_{R}
,
u(x):=w(x)
,
x\in G_{R}
, where
w
denotes the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem in
G_{R}
with data
\gamma^{+}_{R}w=\gamma_{R}u_{R}
, then
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}_{0}(\Delta,\Omega)
and satisfies the scattering problem.

This result, and that the scattering problem is well-posed, imply that the variational problem (
31
) has at most one solution for every
G\in V_{R}^{*}
, where
V_{R}^{*}
is the dual space of
V_{R}
. Existence follows by Fredholm theory by showing that
a_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the sum of coercive and compact sesquilinear forms (see, e.g., the discussion in
[
8
, §2.2]
). (We say that a sesquilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V_{R}
is compact if the associated operator
B:V_{R}\to V_{R}^{*}
, defined by
Bv(w):=b(v,w)
,
v,w\in V_{R}
, is compact.) We write
a_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)=\widetilde{a}_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)+b_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)
, where
b_{k,R}(v,w):=-2k^{2}(v,w)_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}
,
v,w\in V_{R}
, so that
b_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is compact by the compactness of the embedding of
V_{R}
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
. Further,

\mathrm{Re}\,\widetilde{a}_{k,R}(v,v)=\int_{\Omega_{R}}(|\nabla v|^{2}+k^{2}|v|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x-\mathrm{Re}\,\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\gamma_{R}v,\gamma_{R}v\rangle\geq\|v\|_{H^{1}_{k}(\Omega_{R})}^{2},

(32)

for all
v\in V_{R}
, by (
49
) in Lemma
A.1
, so that
\widetilde{a}_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive. We note that (
32
) and this decomposition of
a_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)
are standard for
n=2,3
(e.g.,
[
9
, p. 1441]
,
[
33
, Lemma 2.6]
). The inequality (
32
) may be stated for the first time here for
n>3
, since, to the best of our knowledge, the second inequality in (
49
) is not previously established except for
n=2,3
.

Let
\beta_{k,R}
denote the
inf-sup constant
of
a_{k,R}(\cdot,\cdot)
, defined as

\beta_{k,R}:=\inf_{0\neq v\in V_{R}}\sup_{0\neq w\in V_{R}}\frac{\lvert a_{k,R}(v,w)\rvert}{\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}_{k}(\Omega_{R})}\lVert w\rVert_{H^{1}_{k}(\Omega_{R})}}.

(33)

The well-posedness of (
31
) implies that
\beta_{k,R}>0
, for
k>0
,
R>R_{\Gamma}
(see, e.g.,
[
15
, Remark 2.20]
). Further, by
[
9
, Lemmas 3.3, 3.4]
(or see
[
11
, Lemma 5.1]
), we have

\beta_{k,R}^{-1}\leq 1+2kC_{k,R}^{\prime},\qquad k>0,\quad R>R_{\Gamma},

(34)

where
C_{k,R}^{\prime}
is the cut-off resolvent norm (
9
). (The proof in
[
9
, Lemmas 3.3, 3.4]
is for dimensions
n=2,3
, but carries over to all
n\geq 2
now (
49
) and (
32
) are available for
n>3
.) Further, if
u
is the solution of the scattering problem and
u_{R}:=u|_{\Omega_{R}}
, it follows from Theorem
2.1
and (
32
) that

\mathrm{Re}\,(f,u_{R})_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}=\mathrm{Re}\,a_{k,R}(u_{R},u_{R})\geq\|u_{R}\|_{H^{1}_{k}(\Omega)}^{2}-2k^{2}\|u_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2},\;\;k>0,\;\;R>R_{\Gamma}.

From this inequality we obtain, by Cauchy-Schwarz, that (
10
) holds. This, together with (
34
), implies in turn that

\beta_{k,R}^{-1}\leq 1+2k\sqrt{2k^{2}(C_{k,R})^{2}+C_{k,R}},\qquad k>0,\quad R>R_{\Gamma}.

(35)

This bound will be a key component in proving Theorem
1.5
below.

2.3
The multiplicative trace inequality in a cube

Our arguments, specifically our proof of Theorem
1.5
, need the following special case, with explicit constants, of the standard multiplicative trace inequality (e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 1.5.1.10, last formula on p. 41]
). In this lemma and its proof
\gamma:H^{1}(\mathcal{O})\to L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O})
is the standard trace operator.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a>0
,
\mathcal{O}:=(0,a)^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{O})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma\,v)\subset[0,a]^{n-1}\times\{0\}
. Then

\lVert\gamma\,v\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O})}^{2}\leq 2\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\leq k^{-1}\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{k}(\mathcal{O})},\quad k>0.

(36)

Proof.

The proof here adapts the argument in
[
2
]
, where an analogous bound is shown for the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(and cf.
[
13
]
).
Suppose that
v\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
. Using coordinates
x=(y,t)
, where
y\in[0,a]^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lvert v(y,0)\rvert^{2}=-\int_{0}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lvert v(y,t)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=-2\mathrm{Re}\,\bigg(\int_{0}^{a}\overline{v(y,t)}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg).

Since
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma\,v)\subset[0,a]^{n-1}\times\{0\}
, integrating the above identity over
[0,a]^{n-1}
gives that

\begin{split}\lVert\gamma\,v\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O})}^{2}&=-2\mathrm{Re}\,\bigg(\int_{[0,a]^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{a}\overline{v(y,t)}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\bigg)\\
&\leq 2\int_{\mathcal{O}}\lvert v(x)\rvert\lvert\nabla v(x)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}.\end{split}

The first inequality in (
36
) follows from the above inequality by the density of
C^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{O})
and the continuity of
\gamma
. Since

2k\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}=\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{k}(\mathcal{O})}-\left(k\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}-\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\right)^{2},

we obtain the second inequality.

We turn now to the proof of Theorem

, one of our main results. In the proof we obtain, for each

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be as in Theorem
1.5
,
R>R_{\Gamma}
, and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that the sidelength of the

\ell_{j}=\pi\sqrt{n}k_{j}^{-1}

t_{j}=0
so that
\mathcal{O}_{j}=\ell_{j}\mathcal{O}
,
and define the test function
u_{j}\in V_{R}
by

u_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sin\left(\frac{k_{j}}{\sqrt{n}}x_{i}\right),\hskip 5.69046ptx=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathcal{O}_{j},\\
0,\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\Omega_{R}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{j}.\end{cases}

(38)

Then the sesquilinear form (
30

u_{j}
as the first argument and

as the second, reduces to

Furthermore, by Green’s theorem and the fact that
(\Delta+k_{j}^{2})u_{j}=0

(40)

by Cauchy-Schwarz.
Now

\partial_{\nu}u_{j}(x)=-\frac{k_{j}}{\sqrt{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\sin\left(\frac{k_{j}}{\sqrt{n}}x_{i}\right),\quad x\in\partial\mathcal{O}_{j}\setminus\Gamma_{j}.

Explicitly computing the

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{\nu}u_{j}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{j}\setminus\Gamma_{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{4k_{j}}\bigg)^{n-3}\bigg(\frac{2k_{j}\ell_{j}\varepsilon_{j}}{\sqrt{n}}-\sin\left(\frac{2k_{j}\ell_{j}\varepsilon_{j}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\bigg)^{n-1}

\lVert u_{j}\rVert_{H_{k_{j}}^{1}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}=\frac{k_{j}^{2}\ell_{j}^{n}}{2^{n-1}}.

\displaystyle\beta_{k_{j},R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v\in V_{R}}\frac{|a_{k_{j},R}(u_{j},v)|}{\|u_{j}\|_{H^{1}_{k_{j}}(\mathcal{O}_{j})}\|v\|_{H^{1}_{k_{j}}(\mathcal{O}_{j})}}

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.9

We turn now to the proof of Theorem
1.9
, our final main result, i.e. to showing that conditions (i)-(iii) in Theorem
1.5
can be realised by a certain configuration of the sequence of cubes,
(\mathcal{O}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, under the mild assumption (

) on the rate of decrease of the sidelengths of these cubes. The proof of this theorem is constructive, providing a specification of the sequence of translations
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\ell_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be chosen as in the statement of the theorem. With
(\mathcal{O}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Gamma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
as defined in the theorem we need to choose a sequence of translations
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., an arrangement of the cubes
(\mathcal{O}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, so that (i)-(iii) in Theorem
1.5
are satisfied.
Working with coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, our methodology will be to arrange the cubes in an infinite sequence of layers parallel to the hyperplane
x_{n}=0
. To achieve this we choose, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the translation
t_{j}=(t_{j,1},\ldots,t_{j,n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
in a way we now specify.

Let
(d_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a decreasing, summable sequence of positive numbers. We will use, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the notations

I_{i}:=\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\mathcal{O}_{j}\mbox{ is in the $i$th layer}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{i}:=\min I_{i}.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lfloor s\rfloor
denotes the largest integer
\leq s
.

Place
\mathcal{O}_{1}
on the first level by itself, so that
I_{1}=\{1\}
and
B_{1}=1
, setting
t_{1}=0
. Then, separated from
\mathcal{O}_{1}
by the distance
d_{1}
in the
e_{n}
direction, place the next
\lfloor 2\log(2+\mathrm{e})\rfloor^{n-1}=3^{n-1}

I_{2}=\{2,\ldots,1+3^{n-1}\}
and
B_{2}=2
, setting

t_{j,n}:=-\ell_{B_{2}}-d_{1},\qquad j\in I_{2}.

In general, we place
\lfloor i\log(i+\mathrm{e})\rfloor^{n-1}
cubes on the
i
th level, so that

B_{i}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lfloor j\log(j+\mathrm{e})\rfloor^{n-1}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;I_{i}=\{B_{i},\ldots,B_{i}+\lfloor i\log(i+\mathrm{e})\rfloor^{n-1}-1\},\;\;i\in\mathbb{N}.

We define the
x_{n}
coordinates

of these layers recursively, setting

H_{1}:=0,\qquad H_{i+1}:=H_{i}-\ell_{B_{i+1}}-d_{i},\qquad i\in\mathbb{N},

so that

H_{i}=-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(\ell_{B_{j+1}}+d_{j}),\qquad i\in\mathbb{N},

and set

t_{j,n}:=H_{i},\qquad\mbox{for }j\in I_{i},\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

(46)

Note that, for
i\geq 2

B_{i}\geq\sum_{j=\lfloor i/2\rfloor}^{i-1}\lfloor j\log(j+\mathrm{e})\rfloor^{n-1}\geq\frac{i-2}{2}\big\lfloor\lfloor i/2\rfloor\log(\lfloor i/2\rfloor+\mathrm{e})\big\rfloor^{n-1}\sim\frac{i^{n}}{2^{n}}\log^{n-1}(i),

. Using (
27

\ell_{B_{i}}\leq\frac{c^{*}}{i\log(i)\log^{2}(\log(i))},\qquad\mbox{for all sufficiently large $i$}.

(47)

Thus

47
) and an application of the integral test.

The above partial construction has a number of important features. First, the set
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{j}

direction; if
x\in\mathcal{O}_{\infty}

H_{\infty}\leq x_{n}<\ell_{1}

is bounded, i.e. so that (i) and (ii) in Theorem

\Gamma:=\overline{\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{j}}

1.5

1.5
holds. Clearly,
\mathcal{O}_{j}\subset[0,\lfloor i\log(i+\mathrm{e})\rfloor h_{i}]^{n-1}\times[H_{\infty},\ell_{1}]

is bounded as long as

, as

\Gamma\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=H_{\infty}\}=\{H_{\infty}e_{n}\}

\Gamma=\{H_{\infty}e_{n}\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{j}.

Appendix A
Properties of Radiating Solutions

We show in this appendix, via spherical harmonic expansions, the properties of radiating solutions of the Helmholtz equation that are captured in Lemma
A.1
below. This lemma was previously proved, in dimensions

, using similar methods of argument, in
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
(and see
[
26
, Theorem 2.6.4]
for (
49
)).

The first of (
49
) can be proved alternatively (in any dimension) by a straightforward integration by parts argument. (An application of Green’s theorem shows that
\mathrm{Im}\,\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\bar{u}\partial_{r}u\,\mathrm{d}s

. Then taking the limit

) expresses that the energy flow is outgoing, and plays a key role in energy arguments via Green’s theorems. The second estimate in (
49
) has played an important role (in dimensions

) in establishing

estimates and positivity of sesquilinear forms (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
). We use it in this way (for general

) in our proof of (
32
) above.

The inequality (
50
) was established (for
n=2,3
) in
[
9
]
as a component in the proof (for
n=2,3
) of the cut-off resolvent estimate Theorem
1.2
, and has been an important component in the proofs of other similar estimates, e.g.,
[
34
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
11
, Theorem 1.10]
. The right condition on
\alpha
for (
50
) to hold in dimensions
n>3
is
a priori
not obvious; the condition
\alpha\geq\max(1,n-2)
in Lemma
A.1
reduces to the simpler condition
\alpha\geq 1
of
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
in dimensions
n=2,3
.
A slightly weaker version of (
50
), with the condition
\alpha\geq\max(1,n-2)
replaced by
\alpha\geq n-1
, is proved, in the case
n=2,3
, by an integration by parts argument using a so-called
Morawetz multiplier
, as
[
34
, Lemma 2]
or
[
11
, Lemma 2.4]
. The integration by parts proofs given in
[
34
]
and
[
11
]
for
\alpha=n-1
(the result holds for
\alpha>n-1
if it holds for
\alpha=n-1
, by (

n>3
. This is of note as it is the case
\alpha=n-1
that appears to be particularly important for applications of (
50
). For example, the case
\alpha=n-1
is enough for the proof of the cut-off resolvent estimates that are
[
9
, Lemma 3.5]
and
[
11
, Theorem 1.10]
and enough for the proof of wavenumber-independent coercivity in
[
34
, Theorem 3.2, §3.2]
.

In this lemma we use the notations

G_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>R\}
and
\Gamma_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=R\}
, introduced in §
2.2
,

\nabla_{T}
denotes the surface gradient on
\Gamma_{R}
and
\nabla_{S}
and
\Delta_{S}
denote the surface gradient and Laplace-Beltrami operator, respectively, on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\Gamma_{1}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
k>0
,
R>R_{0}>0
, and that
u\in C^{2}(G_{R_{0}})
satisfies
\Delta u+k^{2}u=0
in
G_{R_{0}}
and the Sommerfeld radiation condition (
2
). Then

\mathrm{Im}\,\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,\mathrm{d}s\geq 0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq 0,

(49)

and, for
\alpha\geq\max(1,n-2)
,

\alpha\,\mathrm{Re}\,\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,\mathrm{d}s+R\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\left(k^{2}|u|^{2}+\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right|^{2}-|\nabla_{T}u|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq 2kR\,\mathrm{Im}\,\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(50)

We shall prove this lemma via a reformulation in terms of Dirichlet to Neumann maps. For
R>0
consider the exterior Dirichlet problem in
G_{R}
of finding, given
\psi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, a
u\in H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(G_{R})
that satisfies
\Delta u+k^{2}u=0
in
G_{R}
, the radiation condition (
2
), and the boundary condition
\gamma^{+}_{R}u=\psi
, where
\gamma^{+}_{R}:H^{1,\mathrm{loc}}(G_{R})\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
is the trace operator. This problem is well-posed (e.g.,
[
19

), so that there is a well-defined map
\mathrm{DtN}_{R}:H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\to H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
that takes the Dirichlet data
\psi

\partial_{r}u
, the normal derivative of the solution

\Gamma_{R}
. This map
\mathrm{DtN}_{R}:H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\to H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
is continuous, indeed is also continuous as a mapping
\mathrm{DtN}_{R}:H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})
(see, e.g., the discussion in
[
32
, §2]
).

Suppose that
u
satisfies the conditions of the above lemma. Then, for
R>R_{0}
,
v:=u|_{G_{R}}
satisfies the above Dirichlet problem in
G_{R}
with boundary data

49
) can be written as

\mathrm{Im}\,\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi,\phi\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi,\phi\rangle\leq 0,

(51)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing on
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
that is an extension of the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}
on
L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})
,
and (
50
) can be written as

\displaystyle\alpha\,\mathrm{Re}\,\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi,\phi\rangle+R\left(k^{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}^{2}+\|\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}^{2}-\|\nabla_{T}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 43.05542pt\leq 2kR\,\mathrm{Im}\,\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi,\phi\rangle.

(52)

Thus Lemma
A.1
is implied by the following rephrased result.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that
R>0
and
k>0
. Then (
51
) holds for all
\phi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
and, if
\alpha\geq\max(1,n-2)

\phi\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{DtN}_{R}:H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\to H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
and
\mathrm{DtN}_{R}:H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})
are continuous, it is enough to prove that these inequalities hold for all
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
, for some
\mathcal{H}_{R}\subset H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})
that is dense in
H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})
(and so also in
H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
). We will choose a set
\mathcal{H}_{R}
defined in terms of spherical harmonics, so first recall relevant notations and results.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let
\mathcal{H}^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
denote the set of spherical harmonics of degree
m
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, as defined, e.g., in
[
19
, p. 252]
. Note (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 1.2.16]
and its proof for these details) that
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has the orthogonal decomposition

L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),

and the spherical harmonics are the eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{S}
; precisely, each
\mathcal{H}^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is a finite-dimensional eigenspace
corresponding to the eigenvalue
m(m+n-2)
. Thus every
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has an expansion

\phi=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2},

(53)

where
\phi_{m}
is the orthogonal projection of
u
from
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
to
\mathcal{H}^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Let
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
denote the set of spherical harmonics, by which we mean the set of those
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
that have a representation (
53
) with only finitely many terms, i.e. with
\phi_{m}=0
for all but finitely many
m
. Clearly,
\mathcal{H}
is a dense subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Let us show that
\mathcal{H}
is also dense in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.

Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{S}
denotes the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.
Recall (e.g.,
[
18
, Eq. (1.2.2)]
) that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi\Delta_{S}\psi\,\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\nabla_{S}\phi\cdot\nabla_{S}\psi\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\quad\phi\in H^{1}(S).

(54)

If
\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has the expansion (
53
) then, by (
54
), for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\nabla_{S}\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla_{S}\bar{\psi}_{j}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\Delta_{S}\phi_{i},\phi_{j})_{S}

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i(i+n-2)(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})_{S}=i(i+n-2)\|\phi_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\delta_{ij},

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. In particular,

\|\nabla_{S}\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}=m(m+n-2)\|\phi_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})},\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(56)

Further, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle m(m+n-2)\|\phi_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(m+n-2)\left|(\phi_{m},\phi)_{S}\right|=\left|(\Delta_{S}\phi_{m},\phi)_{S}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\nabla_{S}\phi_{m}\cdot\nabla_{S}\bar{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{S}\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\nabla_{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{m(m+n-2)}\,\|\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\nabla_{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})},

so that

\|\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq m^{-1}\|\nabla_{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})},\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}.

(57)

It follows from (
55
), (
56
), and (
57
)
that the series (
53
) also converges in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, so that
\mathcal{H}
is also dense in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, and that

\|\nabla_{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\nabla_{S}\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}=m(m+n-2)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.

(58)

For
\psi\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{R})
define
\widehat{\psi}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
by
\widehat{\psi}(\omega)=\psi(R\omega)
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and note that

\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\psi\,\mathrm{d}s=R^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\widehat{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\psi\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{R}).

(59)

Let
\mathcal{H}_{R}:=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{R}):\widehat{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}\}
. The density of
\mathcal{H}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
implies that
\mathcal{H}_{R}\subset H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})
is dense in
H^{1}(\Gamma_{R})
. We complete the proof of the lemma by showing that (
51
) and (
52
) hold for all
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
.

For
\nu\geq 0
let
J_{\nu}
and
Y_{\nu}
denote the usual Bessel functions of the first and second kinds of order
\nu
, and let
H_{\nu}^{(1)}:=J_{\nu}+\mathrm{i}Y_{\nu}
, so that
H_{\nu}^{(1)}
is the Hankel function of the first kind of order
\nu
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t>0
, let

j_{m}(t)=j_{m}(n,t):=\frac{J_{m+n/2-1}(t)}{t^{n/2-1}},\quad y_{m}(t)=y_{m}(n,t):=\frac{Y_{m+n/2-1}(t)}{t^{n/2-1}},

so that
j_{m}
and
y_{m}
are spherical Bessel functions of the first and second kinds. (Our notation is that of
[
19
]
or (for the case
n=3
)
[
26
,
28
]
, except that we omit a normalising factor
\sqrt{\pi/2}
.) For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t>0
define also the associated spherical Hankel function of the first kind,

h_{m}(t)=h_{m}(n,t):=j_{m}(n,t)+\mathrm{i}y_{m}(n,t)=\frac{H^{(1)}_{m+n/2-1}(t)}{t^{n/2-1}}.

It is convenient to also use the notations (e.g.,
[
28
, §10.18]
)

M_{\nu}(t):=|H_{\nu}^{(1)}(t)|,\quad N_{\nu}(t):=\left|{H_{\nu}^{(1)}}^{\prime}(t)\right|,\qquad t>0,\quad\nu\geq 0.

The arguments we make below use that
M_{\nu}
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
for
\nu\geq 0

[
37
, §13.74]
, so that also
|h_{m}|
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
If
\psi\in\mathcal{H}^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, then
\psi(x/|x|)h_{m}(n,k|x|)
is a solution of the Helmholtz equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
that satisfies the Sommerfeld radiation condition (see
[
19
, Lemma 9.3, p. 282]
).

Suppose
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
, so that
\widehat{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}
, and let
\phi_{m}
denote the component of
\widehat{\phi}
in
\mathcal{H}^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, noting that only finitely many of the
\phi_{m}
are non-zero. Then

\phi(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}(x/|x|),\qquad x\in\Gamma_{R},

(60)

so that

\nabla_{T}\phi(x)=R^{-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\nabla_{S}\phi_{m}(x/|x|),\qquad x\in\Gamma_{R}.

(61)

By inspection, the solution of the Dirichlet problem in
G_{R}
with boundary data
\phi
is, where
\rho:=kR
,

u(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}(x/|x|)h_{m}(k|x|)/h_{m}(\rho),\qquad x\in G_{R},

(62)

so that

\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi(x)=k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}(x/|x|)h^{\prime}_{m}(\rho)/h_{m}(\rho),\qquad x\in\Gamma_{R}.

(63)

From (
59
), (
60
), and (
63
) we see that, where
c_{m}:=\|\phi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}/|h_{m}(\rho)|^{2}
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi,\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{n-2}\rho\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}h^{\prime}_{m}(\rho)\overline{h_{m}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{n-2}\rho\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(|h_{m}(\rho)|^{2}\right)+\frac{2\mathrm{i}}{\pi\rho^{n-1}}\right),

where to obtain this last result we have use the Wronskian formula that
j_{m}(\rho)y^{\prime}_{m}(\rho)-y_{m}j^{\prime}_{m}(\rho)=2\rho^{1-n}/\pi
(e.g.,
[
19
, Exercise 9.3]
). This establishes (
51
) since
|h_{m}|
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.

Similarly, using (
59
), (
60
), (
61
), (
63
), and (
58
), we see that

\displaystyle R\left(k^{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}^{2}+\|\mathrm{DtN}_{R}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}^{2}-\|\nabla_{T}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{n-2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}\left((\rho^{2}-m(m+2-2))|h_{m}(\rho)|^{2}+\rho^{2}|h^{\prime}_{m}(\rho)|^{2}\right).

Thus (
52
) holds for all
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
if and only if
B_{m}(\rho)\leq 0
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\rho>0
, where

B_{m}(\rho):=(\rho^{2}-m(m+2-2))|h_{m}(\rho)|^{2}+\rho^{2}|h^{\prime}_{m}(\rho)|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\rho}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(|h_{m}(\rho)|^{2}\right)-\frac{4}{\pi}\rho^{3-n}.

(64)

To see that
B_{m}(\rho)\leq 0
if
\alpha\geq\max(1,n-2)
, completing the proof, let
\nu=m+n/2-1
,
p=1-n/2
, so that
m(m+n-2)=\nu^{2}-p^{2}
,
h_{m}(\rho)=\rho^{p}H_{\nu}^{(1)}(\rho)
,

\displaystyle h_{m}^{\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{p-1}(\rho{H_{\nu}^{(1)}}^{\prime}(\rho)+pH_{\nu}^{(1)}(\rho)),

\displaystyle\rho^{n}|h_{m}^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}N^{2}_{\nu}(\rho)+p^{2}M^{2}_{\nu}(\rho)+p\rho\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{\nu}^{2}(\rho)),

and

\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(|h_{m}(\rho)|^{2})=pM_{\nu}^{2}(\rho)+\frac{\rho}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M^{2}_{\nu}(\rho)).

Using these identities it follows that

\rho^{n-2}B_{m}(\rho)=(\rho^{2}-\nu^{2}+2p^{2}+\alpha p)M^{2}_{\nu}(\rho)+\rho^{2}N_{\nu}^{2}(\rho)+\left(p+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\rho\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{\nu}^{2}(\rho))-\frac{4\rho}{\pi},

for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\rho>0
. For
\nu\geq 0
and
t>0
, let

A_{\nu}(t):=M_{\nu}^{2}(t)(t^{2}-\nu^{2})+t^{2}N_{\nu}^{2}(t)-4t/\pi.

Then, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle\rho^{n-2}B_{m}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\nu}(\rho)+\frac{2-n+\alpha}{2}\left((2-n)M^{2}_{\nu}(\rho)+\rho\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{\nu}^{2}(\rho))\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A_{\nu}(\rho)+\frac{2-n+\alpha}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{\nu}^{2}(\rho)),

if
\alpha\geq n-2
. Now it is shown in
[
9
, Equation (2.7)]
that
A_{\nu}(t)\leq 0
, for
\nu\geq 1/2
and
t>0
. Since also
M_{\nu}
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, it follows that
B_{m}(\rho)\leq 0
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\rho>0
, if
\alpha\geq n-2
, as long as
n\geq 3
. In the case
n=2
,

B_{m}(\rho)=A_{m}(\rho)+\frac{\alpha\rho}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{m}^{2}(\rho)),

for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\rho>0
, so that
\alpha\geq n-2=0
implies that
B_{m}(\rho)\leq 0
for
\rho>0
if
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\alpha\geq 1
, then also

B_{0}(\rho)=A_{0}(\rho)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{0}^{2}(\rho))\leq A_{0}(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(M_{0}^{2}(\rho))\leq 0,

for
\rho>0
, see
[
9
, pp. 1436–1437]
.

Twisted torus knots with Horadam parameters
Brandy Doleshal, Brandy Doleshal

Abstract
. Sangyop Lee has done much work to determine the knot types of twisted torus knots, including classifying the twisted torus knots which are the unknot. Among the unknotted twisted torus knots are those of the form K ⁡ ( F n + 2 , F n , F n + 1 , − 1 ) K(F_{n+2},F_{n},F_{n+1},-1) , where F i F_{i} is the i i th Fibonacci number. Here, we consider twisted torus knots with parameters that are defined recursively, similarly to the Fibonacci sequence. We call these Horadam parameters , after the generalization of the Fibonacci sequence introduced by A.F. Horadam. Here, we provide families of twisted torus knots that generalize Lee’s work with Horadam parameters. Additionally, we provide lists of primitive/primitive and primitive/Seifert twisted torus knots and connect these lists to the Horadam twisted torus knots.

3.
Horadam Sequences

The Fibonacci sequence has been studied extensively and several generalizations of it have been established and studied. Here we use the generalization introduced by Horadam
[
7
]
and studied by countless others since.

Definition 1
.

An
(m,n;a,b)
-Horadam sequence
\mathcal{H}
is the sequence
\{H_{0},H_{1},H_{2}\ldots\}
where
H_{0}=m
,
H_{1}=n
and
H_{k}=aH_{k-2}+bH_{k-1}
for
k\geq 2
.

As an example, the Fibonacci sequence is the
(0,1;1,1)
-Horadam sequence, which we will denote by
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\ldots\}
. In this work, we focus mainly on the
(m,n;1,1)
-Horadam sequences, and we will shorten the notation to
(m,n)
-Horadam sequence, or
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
. In
[
7
]
,
m
and
n
are arbitrary integers, but in this work, we will at times need to restrict
m
and
n
to the positive integers that are relatively prime.

Here we state some lemmas proved by Horadam
[
6
]
and provide some results about Horadam sequences that will be useful for our knot-theoretic results.

Lemma 2

(Horadam
[
6
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is the
(m,n)
-Horadam sequence, then

H_{k}=mF_{k-1}+nF_{k}

for
k\geq 1
, where
F_{i}
is the
i
th Fibonacci number.

Using the previous lemma, Horadam
[
6
]
finds a relationship between any two terms in an
(m,n)
-Horadam sequence.

We let
s=m^{2}+mn-n^{2}
, which is the negative of Horadam’s
e

[
6
]
.

Lemma 3
.

For an
(m,n)
-Horadam sequence
\mathcal{H}
and for
k\geq 1
,

(1)

H_{k}^{2}+H_{k+1}H_{k}-H_{k+1}^{2}=(-1)^{k}s
,

(2)

\displaystyle H_{k}^{2}+H_{k}H_{k-1}-H_{k-1}^{2}=(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2\varepsilon_{k}s+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}H_{i}^{2}
, and

(3)

\displaystyle H_{k}^{2}+H_{k}H_{k-1}+H_{k-1}^{2}=(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2\varepsilon_{k}s+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}H_{i}^{2}+2H_{k-1}^{2}

where
\varepsilon_{k}=1
if
k
is even and
\varepsilon_{k}=0
if
k
is odd.

Proof.

Part 1 is given by Horadam
[
6
]
, up to shift in terms that results in a negative on the right side of the equation, accounting for the fact that
s=-e
.

We prove part 2 by induction. For the base case, we let
k=1
, so we have
H_{1}^{2}+H_{1}H_{0}-H_{0}^{2}=n^{2}+mn-m^{2}
, by the definition of
\mathcal{H}
. On the other hand
\varepsilon_{1}=0
because
1
is odd and
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{0}H_{i}^{2}
is an empty sum, so the right side of the equation is also
n^{2}+mn-m^{2}
. When
k=2
, we have
H_{2}^{2}+H_{2}H_{1}-H_{1}^{2}=(m+n)^{2}+n(m+n)-n^{2}=m^{2}+3mn+n^{2}
on the left of the equation, and
\displaystyle(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2\varepsilon_{2}s+2\sum_{i=1}^{1}H_{i}^{2}=n^{2}+mn-m^{2}+2m^{2}+2mn-2n^{2}+2n^{2}=m^{2}+3mn+n^{2}
on the right, as expected.

Let
s_{i}=H_{i}^{2}+H_{i}H_{i-1}-H_{i-1}^{2}
for
i\geq 1
, and suppose the statement is true for
s_{k-1}
for some
k\geq 2
. That is, our inductive hypothesis tells us that

\displaystyle s_{k-1}=(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2\varepsilon_{k-1}s+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}H_{i}^{2}.

Consider
s_{k}=H_{k}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k}-H_{k-1}^{2}
. Using the definition of
\mathcal{H}
, we have that
H_{k-1}=H_{k}-H_{k-2}
and
H_{k}=H_{k-1}+{H_{k-2}}
. Then we can rewrite
s_{k}
as
H_{k}^{2}+H_{k-1}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k-2}-(H_{k}-H_{k-2})^{2}=H_{k-1}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k-2}-H_{k-2}^{2}+2H_{k}H_{k-2}
. This expression is equal to
s_{k-1}+2H_{k}H_{k-2}.

Part 1 tells us that
H_{k}^{2}+H_{k+1}H_{k}-H_{k+1}^{2}=(-1)^{k}s
. Using the definition of
\mathcal{H}
, we then have that
H_{k}H_{k-2}=(-1)^{k-2}s+H_{k-1}^{2}
, so that
s_{k}=s_{k-1}+2(-1)^{k-2}s+2H_{k-1}^{2}
. Then, using the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle s_{k}

\displaystyle=(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2\varepsilon_{k-1}s+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}H_{i}^{2}+2(-1)^{k-2}s+2H_{k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2(\varepsilon_{k-1}+(-1)^{k-2})s+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}H_{i}^{2}.

If
k
is even, then
k-1
is odd and
\varepsilon_{k-1}+(-1)^{k-2}=1=\varepsilon_{k}
. If
k
is odd, then
k-1
is even and
\varepsilon_{k-1}+(-1)^{k-2}=0=\varepsilon_{k}
. Hence, the second part is proven.

The third part of the statement is obtained by adding
2H_{k-1}^{2}
to both sides of the equation in the previous part of the lemma.
∎

The previous lemma tells us that for all
k\geq 2
,
H_{k-1}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k}-H_{k}^{2}
is the same value, up to sign. In the next two lemmas, we show that for the very similar-looking
H_{k}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k}-H_{k-1}^{2}
forms an increasing sequence so that every value is different.

Lemma 4
.

For
m
and
n
positive, let
\mathcal{H}
be the
(m,n)
-Horadam sequence, and for
k\geq 2
, let
s_{k}=H_{k}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k}-H_{k-1}^{2}
and let
t_{k}=H_{k+1}^{2}+H_{k+1}H_{k}+H_{k}^{2}
. The sequences
\{s_{k}\}_{k\geq 2}
and
\{t_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are both increasing.

Proof.

In the proof of Lemma
3
, we showed that
s_{k}=s_{k-1}+2(-1)^{k-2}s+2H_{k-1}^{2}
. Rearranging, we see that
s_{k}-s_{k-1}=2(-1)^{k-2}s+2H_{k-1}^{2}
, so to show the sequence is increasing, it is enough to show that
(-1)^{k-2}s+H_{k-1}^{2}
is positive for all
k\geq 3
. That is, we want to show that
H_{k-1}^{2}>(-1)^{k-1}s
.

When
k
is even, this inequality is
H_{k-1}^{2}>n^{2}-mn-m^{2}
. Because
\mathcal{H}
is an increasing sequence for positive values of
m
and
n
,
H_{k-1}^{2}\geq H_{1}^{2}=n^{2}
. Since
m
and
n
are positive,
n^{2}-mn-m^{2}<n^{2}\leq H_{k-1}^{2}
.

When
k
is odd, the inequality becomes
H_{k-1}^{2}>m^{2}+mn-n^{2}
. Again,
\mathcal{H}
is an increasing sequence, so
H_{k-1}^{2}\geq H_{2}^{2}=m^{2}+2mn+n^{2}
, which is evidently greater than
m^{2}+mn-n^{2}
for positive values of
m
and
n
.

To show that
\{t_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is increasing, we use the recursion relation for
\mathcal{H}
to rewrite
t_{k}=H_{k+1}^{2}+H_{k+1}H_{k}+H_{k}^{2}
as
t_{k}=H^{2}_{k}+H_{k}H_{k-1}+H_{k-1}^{2}+2H_{k+1}H_{k}
, which we recognize as
t_{k}=t_{k-1}+2H_{k+1}H_{k}
. Then
t_{k}-t_{k-1}=2H_{k+1}H_{k}
, which is at least 2 for all
k\geq 2
, so
\{t_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is increasing.
∎

Remark 1
.

We note here that if
m<n
, the sequence
\{s_{k}\}
is increasing for
k\geq 1
as well since
H_{k-1}^{2}>H_{0}^{2}=m^{2}
and
m^{2}+mn-n^{2}<m^{2}
.

Lemma 5
.

For
m
and
n
positive, let
\mathcal{H}
be the
(m,n)
-Horadam sequence.

(1)

For
k\geq 2
, let
s_{k}=H_{k}^{2}+H_{k-1}H_{k}-H_{k-1}^{2}
. For all

, if

, then
s_{l}\neq s_{k}
. In fact,
|s_{l}-s_{k}|>1
.

(2)

For
k\geq 1
, let

. For all
l\geq 1
, if
l\neq k
, then
t_{l}\neq t_{k}
. In fact,
|t_{l}-t_{k}|>1
.

Proof.

The assertion that
s_{l}\neq s_{k}
for

is nearly an immediate result of Lemma
4
, with the exception of the case
l=1
. That is, we must show that
s_{1}\neq s_{k}
for
k\geq 2
. In Remark
1
, we noted that the

is increasing when
m<n
, so we need only show that
s_{1}\neq s_{k}
when

.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that

and there exists a

such that

. Then we have

(n^{2}+mn-m^{2})+2\varepsilon_{k}s+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}H_{i}^{2}=n^{2}+mn-m^{2}.

Simplifying this equation, we have

\varepsilon_{k}s=-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}H_{i}^{2}.

The right side of the equation is negative for
k\geq 2
, but
\varepsilon_{k}s=\varepsilon_{k}(m^{2}+mn-n^{2})
, which is at least zero because

.

Since
\{s_{k}\}_{k\geq 2}
is an increasing integer sequence, to show that the difference between any two terms is greater than 1, we need only show that the difference between consecutive terms is greater than 1.
As noted in the proof of Lemma
3
,
s_{k}-s_{k-1}=2((-1)^{k-2}s+H_{k-1}^{2})
for

. In the proof of Lemma

, we showed that
(-1)^{k-2}s+H_{k-1}^{2}
is positive for

, so

for these values of

To finish the proof, we must show that the absolute value of the difference between

and

is at least 2. Since

The assertion that

is similar to the proof for

, with the exception that

, which is always at least 2.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose

and
\gcd(m,n)=1
. Let
\{q_{l},\ldots,q_{1},q_{0}\}
and
\{r_{l},\ldots,r_{1},r_{0}\}
be the sequences of quotients and remainders, respectively, provided by the Euclidean algorithm for
m

(m,n)
-Horadam sequence
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a subsequence of a
(\pm 1,a)
-Horadam sequence
\mathcal{H}_{\pm 1,a}

a\geq 2
, if and only if
q_{i}=1
for
1\leq i\leq l
and
q_{0}\in\{1,2\}
.

Because a pair of numbers with the property given in Lemma

has the largest (
q_{0}=1
), or nearly the largest (
q_{0}=2
), possible number of equations in its Euclidean algorithm, we call
(m,n)
an
maximal pair

Proof.

First we prove that the given conditions guarantee that
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a subsequence of
\mathcal{H}_{\pm 1,a}
. Let the Euclidean algorithm for
m
and
n
be given by the sequence of equations

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=q_{l}m+r_{l}

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=q_{l-1}r_{l}+r_{l-1}

\displaystyle r_{l}

\displaystyle=q_{l-2}r_{l-1}+r_{l-2}

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=q_{0}r_{1}+r_{0}

Because

and

. If
q_{i}=1
for
0\leq i\leq l
, then we have the sequence
\mathcal{H}_{1,r_{1}}

H_{0}=r_{0}=1

. Then

and

, and we have that
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a subsequence of
\mathcal{H}_{1,r_{1}}
.

When

, then we consider the sequence
\mathcal{H}_{-1,r_{1}+1}=\{-1,r_{1}+1,r_{1},2r_{1}+1,(2r_{1}+1)+r_{1},\ldots\}=\{-1,r_{1}+1,r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\ldots\}

and

, and we have that
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a subsequence of
\mathcal{H}_{-1,r_{1}+1}
.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a subsequence of
\mathcal{H}_{1,a}

H_{j-1}=(q_{l}-1)H_{j}+r_{l}.

Because

H_{j}>H_{j-1}
. In order to satisfy this inequality, we must have

Now we turn our attention to the equation

Again,
\mathcal{H}_{1,a}

Consider the next equation from the Euclidean algorithm, we have

Using

.

We continue this process for each equation in the sequence of equations from the Euclidean algorithm, finding at each step that

q_{l-i+1}=1

Suppose instead that

is a subsequence of

n=H_{j+1}
for some
j\geq 1
. Once again we have
n=m+H_{j-1}
and
n=q_{l}m+r_{l}
, so

H_{j-1}=(q_{l}-1)H_{j}+r_{l}.

Because
a\geq 2
,
\mathcal{H}_{-1,a}
is very close to being an increasing sequence and
H_{j}>H_{j-1}
unless
j=2
, so the methods used when
\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a subsequence of
\mathcal{H}_{1,a}
need modification. We solve the equation for
r_{l}
to obtain

r_{l}=H_{j-1}-(q_{l}-1)H_{j}.

By Lemma
2
,
H_{j}=aF_{j}-F_{j-1}
and
H_{j-1}=aF_{j-1}-F_{j-2}
, where
F_{i}
is the
i
th Fibonacci number. Then we can rewrite
r_{l}
as

\displaystyle r_{l}

\displaystyle=aF_{j-1}-F_{j-2}-(q_{l}-1)(aF_{j}-F_{j-1})

\displaystyle=aF_{j-1}-F_{j-2}-(q_{l}-1)(a(F_{j-1}+F_{j-2})-F_{j-1})

\displaystyle=(a(2-q_{l})+q_{l}-1)F_{j-1}-(a(q_{l}-1)+1)F_{j-2}

Because
r_{l}>0
and
(a(q_{l}-1)+1)F_{j-2}\geq 0
, we have, at the very least, that
a(2-q_{l})+q_{l}-1>0
. Then

q_{l}-1>a(q_{l}-2).

We also know that
a\geq 2
, so

a(q_{l}-2)\geq 2q_{l}-4.

Combining these two inequalities, we find that
q_{l}<3
, so
q_{l}\in\{1,2\}
. If
q_{l}=2
, then
r_{l}=H_{j-1}-H_{j}\leq 1
, with equality if and only if
j=2
. Hence
j=2
and
l=0
, which means only
q_{0}
can be 2. If
q_{l}=1
, then
r_{l}=H_{j-1}
. As above, we can iterate this process so that, for
1\leq i\leq j-2
, at the
i
th step we have

H_{j-i}=(q_{l-i+1}-1)H_{j-i+1}+r_{l-i+1}.

Solving for
r_{l-i+1}
, using Lemma
2
and rearranging, we have

r_{l-i+1}=(a(2-q_{l-i+1})+q_{l-i+1}-1)F_{j-i}-(a(q_{l-i+1}-1)+1)F_{j-i+1}.

Similarly to above, we find that the coefficient of
F_{j-i}
,
a(2-q_{l-i+1})+q_{l-i+1}-1
, must be positive so that
q_{l-i+1}\in\{1,2\}
. If
q_{l-i+1}=2
, then

r_{l-i+1}=H_{j-i}-H_{j-i+1},

which can only be positive if
j=i+1
, and in that case
r_{l-i+1}=1=r_{0}
, so
q_{l-i+1}=q_{0}=2
. If
q_{l-i+1}=1
, then
r_{l-i+1}=H_{j-i}
, and we consider the next equation. To summarize, for each
i
with
1\leq i\leq j-2
, either the process terminates with
q_{0}=2
, or
q_{l-i+1}=1
and there are more equations to consider in the Euclidean algorithm for
m
and
n
. Up until this point, each step has been dependent on the fact that the Fibonacci sequence increases, but
F_{1}=F_{2}
, so we consider the case
i=j-1
separately.

Suppose
i=j-1
, so the equation becomes

H_{1}=(q_{l-j+2}-1)H_{2}+r_{l-j+2},

which we can rewrite as

r_{l-j+2}=a-(q_{l-j+2}-1)(a-1).

Because
r_{l-j+2}
is at least 1,
q_{l-j+2}\in\{1,2\}
. If
q_{l-j+2}=2
, then
r_{l-j+2}=1=r_{0}
so
l=j-2
and
q_{l-j+2}=2=q_{0}
. If
q_{l-j+2}=1
, then
r_{l-j+2}=H_{1}
. This means that the relevant equation of the Euclidean algorithm is

2a-1=(a-1)+a.

While this equation does hold, it cannot be an equation in the Euclidean algorithm for
m
and
n
because
a>a-1
. Hence
q_{l-j+2}=2=q_{0}
, and the process terminates. Thus the sequence
\{q_{l},\ldots q_{1},q_{0}\}
satisfies
q_{i}=1
for
1\leq i\leq l
and
q_{0}=2
.

∎

Lemma 7
.

Suppose
1<m<n
and
\gcd(m,n)=1
. Then
(m,n)
is a maximal pair if and only if any two consecutive terms of

form a maximal pair.

Proof.

Let
\{q_{l},\ldots,q_{0}\}
be the sequence of quotients for the Euclidean algorithm for

and
n

Consider two consecutive terms

and

, we know that the

and

for

and

K(H_{k+2},H_{k},H_{k+1},-1)
for
H_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
is a torus knot if and only if
(H_{k+2},H_{k},H_{k+1})
is of one of the forms
(aF_{b+3}-F_{b+2},aF_{b+1}-F_{b},aF_{b+2}-F_{b+1})
, with
a\geq 2
,
b\geq 1
and
(a,b)\neq(2,1)
, or
(aF_{b+1}+F_{b+2},aF_{b-1}+F_{b},aF_{b}+F_{b+1})
, with
a,b\geq 2
.

We have
(H_{k+2},H_{k},H_{k+1})
of the form
(aF_{b+3}-F_{b+2},aF_{b+1}-F_{b},aF_{b+2}-F_{b+1})
if and only if

H_{k}=G_{b}
and
H_{k+1}=G_{b+1}
for some
b\geq 1
where
G_{b},G_{b+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{-1,a}
. Since
G_{b},G_{b+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{-1,a}
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{G_{b},G_{b+1}}
is a subsequence of
\mathcal{H}_{-1,a}
, and Lemma
6
tells us that this is possible if and only if
(G_{b},G_{b+1})
is a maximal pair. Further because
(G_{b},G_{b+1})=(H_{k},H_{k+1})
,
(G_{b},G_{b+1})
is a maximal pair if and only if
(H_{k},H_{k+1})
is a maximal pair. Finally, because
H_{k},H_{k+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{m,n}
, Lemma
7
tells us that
(H_{k},H_{k+1})
is a maximal pair if and only if
(m,n)
is a maximal pair.

If
(H_{k+2},H_{k},H_{k+1})
is of the form
(aF_{b+1}+F_{b+2},aF_{b-1}+F_{b},aF_{b}+F_{b+1})
with
a,b\geq 2
, the proof is similar, except that we must first note that the
b
th term in
\mathcal{H}_{a,1}
is the same as
G_{b-1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,a+1}
. One can see this by using the definition of the Fibonacci sequence, along with Lemma
2
:
aF_{b-1}+F_{b}=aF_{b-1}+(F_{b-1}+F_{b-2})=F_{b-2}+(a+1)F_{b-1}
.

∎

Parts
2
and
3
of Proposition
1
is corroborated by Kadokami’s work
[
8
]
, as these knots can be drawn as braids in a way that shows they are equivalent to
b^{-}(H_{k-1},H_{k})
and
b^{+}(H_{k},H_{k+1})
, respectively. Kadokami does not reference these braids as twisted torus knots, however, so the characterization as twisted torus knots is novel.

5.
Primitive/primitive and primitive/Seifert twisted torus knots

As shown in Lemma
8
, all of the
(m,n;1,1)
-Horadam twisted torus knots are primitive/primitive. In this section, we provide a list of all of the primitive/primitive twisted torus knots and note that not all of the knots in this list can be seen immediately as Horadam twisted torus knots. Further, we list all of the twisted torus knots that are middle- or hyper-Seifert with respect to
H
and primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
, expanding and correcting work of Dean’s that listed the twisted torus knots that are middle-Seifert with respect to
H
and primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
p
and
q
be positive, coprime integers which are each at least 2 and let
r
be an integer with
2\leq r\leq p+q
. The primitive/primitive twisted torus knots are those of the form
K(p,q,r,\pm 1)
where
(p,q,r)
is a triple of the following type:

(1)

(p,q,p+q)
,

(2)

(p,q,p-q)
,

(3)

(2ij+i+j+\frac{1+\delta}{2},2j+1,2ij+i+j+\frac{1-\delta}{2})
, where
\delta=\pm 1
,
i\geq 0
,
j\geq 1
,

(4)

(3j+1+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2},2j+1,4j+2+\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and
j\geq 1
, or

(5)

(2jk+k+2\varepsilon,2j+1,2jk+k+\varepsilon)
where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
j\geq 1
,
k\geq 1
, and
(j,k,\varepsilon)\neq(1,1,-1)
.

Proof.

Dean
[
5
]
shows that a twisted torus knot
K(p,q,r,\pm 1)
is primitive/primitive if and only if
r\equiv\pm 1
or
\pm q\pmod{p}
and
r\equiv\pm 1
or
\pm p\pmod{q}
. We have four cases to consider.

(1)

r\equiv\pm q\pmod{p}
and
r\equiv\pm p\pmod{q}

(2)

r\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
and
r\equiv\pm p\pmod{q}

(3)

r\equiv\pm q\pmod{p}
and
r\equiv\pm 1\pmod{q}

(4)

r\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
and
r\equiv\pm 1\pmod{q}

Here, we assume that
p>q
, as Lemma
1
tells us that
K(p,q,r,\pm 1)
and
K(q,p,r,\pm 1)
are the same knot. We can recover the case where
q>p
.

In Case
1
, we have
r=kq+\varepsilon p=lp+\delta q
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\delta=\pm 1
, and
k
and
l
are nonzero integers. (Neither
k
nor
l
can be zero because
p
and
q
are relatively prime and
p,q\geq 2
.) Because
r\leq p+q
, we have the requirement
lp+\delta q\leq p+q
. Because
p>q
,
lp+\delta q>(l-1)p
. This means
(l-1)p<p+q
. Since
p+q<2p
, we have
l<3
. On the other hand,
r
is positive, so
p>q
tells us that
l\geq 1
. Then
l\in\{1,2\}
.

When
l=1
,
r=p+\delta q=kq+\varepsilon p
, so
(1-\varepsilon)p=(k-\delta)q
. If
\varepsilon=1
, then
k=\delta
and
p
and
q
are independent of each other. This gives us the set of triples
(p,q,p+\delta q)
, which are Types
1
and
2
from the statement of the theorem. If
\varepsilon=-1
, then
q=2
and
p=k-\delta
. In this case
r=k+\delta
, so we have the triple
(k-\delta,2,k+\delta)
, which is a special case of Type
1
or
2
, depending on the value of
\delta
.

When
l=2
,
r=2p+\delta q=kq+\varepsilon p
. The requirement that
r\leq p+q
forces
\delta=-1
, so we have
2p-q=kq+\varepsilon p
. Then
(2-\varepsilon)p=(k+1)q
. Since
2-\varepsilon\in\{1,3\}
and
q\geq 2
, we have
\varepsilon=-1
,
q=3
,
p=k+1
and
r=2k-1
. Revisiting the requirement that
r\leq p+q
, we have
2k-1\leq k+4
, so
k\leq 5
. Since
p>q
, we also have
k\geq 3
. This provides the three triples
(4,3,5)
,
(5,3,7)
, and
(6,3,9)
, but the triple
(6,3,9)
has
\gcd(p,q)=3
. The triples
(4,3,5)
and
(5,3,7)
are special cases of Type
4
with
j=1
.

In Case
2
,
r=kq+\varepsilon p=lp+\delta
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\delta=\pm 1
, and
k
and
l
are integers. The requirement that
r\geq 2
provides the restriction that
l
be a positive integer. Since
r\leq p+q
, we have
l\leq 2
, so that
l\in\{1,2\}
.

When
l=1
, we have
kq+\varepsilon p=p+\delta
, so
(1-\varepsilon)p=kq-\delta
. This forces
\varepsilon=-1
so that
2p=kq-\delta
and
k\geq 1
. Since
2p
is even, we have that
k
and
q
are both odd, so we let
k=2i+1
for some nonnegative integer
i
and
q=2j+1
for some positive integer
j
. Since
2p=kq-\delta
, we have
p=2ij+i+j+\frac{1-\delta}{2}
and
r=p+\delta=2ij+i+j+\frac{1+\delta}{2}
. This is Type
3
.

When
l=2
,
kq+\varepsilon p=2p+\delta
. We have
r=2p+\delta\leq p+q
, so
p\leq q-\delta
. Since
p>q
, this means
\delta=-1
,
p=q+1
, and
r=2q+1
. This produces the triple
(q+1,q,2q+1)
, a special case of Type
1
.

In Case
3
,
r=kq+\varepsilon=lp+\delta q
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\delta=\pm 1
, and
k
and
l
are integers. The requirement that
r\geq 2
provides the restriction that
l
be a nonnegative integer and
k\geq 1
. Since
r\leq p+q
, we have
l\leq 2
, so that
l\in\{0,1,2\}
.

When
l=0
, the two expressions for
r
reveal that
kq+\varepsilon=\delta q
, which is impossible, as it would require
q
to be 1, contradicting our assumptions. When
l=1
,
kq+\varepsilon=p+\delta q
. Then we have
p=(k-\delta)q+\varepsilon
and
r=kq+\varepsilon
. The triple
((k-\delta)q+\varepsilon,q,kq+\varepsilon)
is a special case of Type
1
or
2
, depending on the value of
\delta
.

When
l=2
,
r=kq+\varepsilon=2p+\delta q
. Since
r\leq p+q
, we have
\delta=-1
. Using the two expressions for
r
, we have
2p=(k+1)q+\varepsilon
. Then we know that
k+1
and
q
are both odd, so we write
k=2i
and
q=2j+1
for some positive integers
i
and
j
. Then
p=2ij+i+j+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}
and
r=4ij+2i+\varepsilon
. Since
r\leq p+q
, we need
4ij+2i+\varepsilon\leq 2ij+i+3j+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}+1
, which can be rearranged as
i(2j+1)\leq 3j+1+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}
. If
i\geq 2
,
i(2j+1)\geq 4j+4
, which is strictly greater than
3j+1+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}
for all values of
j
. Then
i=1
, and we have
p=3j+1+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}
and
r=4j+2+\varepsilon
. These are the triples that make up Type
4
.

In Case
4
,
r=kq+\varepsilon=lp+\delta
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\delta=\pm 1
, and
k
and
l
are integers. The requirement that
r\geq 2
provides the restriction that both
k
and
l
are positive. Since
r\leq p+q
, we have
l\leq 2
, so that
l\in\{1,2\}
.

When
\varepsilon=\delta
, we have
kq=lp
. Since
p
and
q
are relatively prime, this means
l=\mu q
and
k=\mu p
for some positive integer
\mu
. Then
r=\mu pq+\varepsilon
. Because
q\geq 2
, if
\mu\geq 2
or
q\geq 3
, then
r\geq 3p+\varepsilon>p+q
. Then
\mu=1
and
q=2
. The requirement that
r\leq p+q
can only hold then when
2p+\varepsilon\leq p+2
, which means
\varepsilon=-1
and
p=3
. Thus, we have the triple
(3,2,5)
, which is a special case of Type
1
.

Now suppose
\varepsilon=-\delta
, so
kq+\varepsilon=lp-\varepsilon
. When
l=1
,
p=kq+2\varepsilon
, and
r=kq+\varepsilon
. Because
p
has the same parity as
kq
, the requirement that
p
and
q
are relatively prime forces
q
to be odd. Then we can write
q=2j+1
for some positive integer
j
, and we have the examples of Type
5
.

When
l=2
,
r=2p-\varepsilon
. Since
r\leq p+q
and
p>q
, we must have that
\varepsilon=1
. Since
2p-1\leq p+q
, we have
p\leq q+1
. That is,
p=q+1
and
r=2q+1
, so we have the triple
(q+1,q,2q+1)
, which is a special case of Type
1
.

This completes the proof.
∎

The Horadam twisted torus knots can be described as being in the first two families of primitive/primitive twisted torus knots listed in Theorem
4
. Quick computations show that for the other three families listed in Theorem
4
,
r
is not equal to
p\pm q
, so the parameters are not Horadam parameters. However, since twisted torus knots can have multiple sets of parameters representing the same knot type (as demonstrated in Proposition
1
.(
1
)), it is possible that these knots could have a different set of Horadam parameters. We leave as an open question whether the set of all Horadam twisted torus knots is disjoint from the collection of all knots in the remaining three families.

Dean
[
5
]
classifies the types of Seifert knots as being hyper-, middle- and end-Seifert, and he claims a classification of the knots
K(p,q,r,\pm 1,1)
that are primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
and middle-Seifert with respect to
H
. Here, we expand and correct Dean’s work and talk about how the Horadam twisted torus knots do not generalize to this set.

Theorem 5
.

Let
p
and
q
be coprime integers with
p\geq 3
and
q\geq 2
. The twisted torus knots
K(p,q,r,m,\pm 1)
with
2\leq r\leq p+q
that are hyper-Seifert fibered with respect to
H
and primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
are those with
|m|>1
and
(p,q,r)
given by the same list of triples as in Theorem
4
.

Proof.

Dean
[
5
]
indicates that the hyper-Seifert fibered twisted torus knots are those that have the
|m|>1
and
r\equiv\pm 1
or
\pm q\pmod{p}
. In other words, Theorem
4
also lists the values
(p,q,r)
for which
K(p,q,r,m,\pm 1)
, with
|m|>1
, is a primitive/hyper-Seifert twisted torus knot.
∎

Next, we consider the list of knots that are middle-Seifert with respect to
H
and primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
, similar to Dean’s Theorem 4.1.

Theorem 6
.

Let
p
and
q
be coprime integers with
p\geq 3
and
q\geq 2
. The twisted torus knots
K(p,q,r,\pm 1)
with
2\leq r\leq p+q
that are middle-Seifert fibered with respect to
H
and primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
are given by the following triples
(p,q,r)
:

(1)

(p,q,p-kq)
where
2\leq k<p/q
,

(2)

(p,3,p+i)
where
i\in\{1,2\}
and
p\equiv i\pmod{3}
,

(3)

(i(2j+1)+j+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2},2j+1,(i+1)(2j+1)+\varepsilon)
where
i,j\geq 1
.

We find that in Dean’s Theorem 4.1, the inquality
2\leq\beta<p/q
was forgotten in four of the five cases listed there. Specifically, several cases in that proof consider the option that
q>p/2
, but in that case
p/q<2
, so there are no possible integral values for
\beta
. Hence, only Dean’s second case, listed first in Theorem
6
here, can occur with the hypotheses in Dean’s work.

In his work, Dean restricts to the case
r\leq\max(p,q)
. In the second and third families listed above in Theorem
6
,
r
is larger than
p
, so these cases expand Dean’s work beyond
r\leq\max(p,q)
.

Proof.

Dean
[
5
]
shows that a twisted torus knot
K(p,q,r,\pm 1)
is primitive with respect to
H^{\prime}
if and only if
r\equiv\pm 1
or
\pm p\pmod{q}
. Further, the twisted torus knot
K(p,q,r,\pm 1)
is middle-Seifert with respect to
H
when
r\equiv\pm\beta q\pmod{p}
, where
2\leq\beta<\frac{\max(p,q)}{\min(p,q)}
. We have two cases to consider.

(1)

r\equiv\pm p\pmod{q}
and
r\equiv\pm\beta q\pmod{p}

(2)

r\equiv\pm 1\pmod{q}
and
r\equiv\pm\beta q\pmod{p}

Lemma
1
tells us that we can assume that
p>q
, so as noted above, the inequality
2\leq\beta<p/q
forces
p>2q
.

Case
1
: The conditions of this case require that
r=kq+\varepsilon p
and
r=lp+\delta\beta q
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\delta=\pm 1
,
\displaystyle k\geq\left\lceil\frac{2-\varepsilon p}{q}\right\rceil
, and
l
is a nonzero integer satisfying
\displaystyle l\geq\left\lceil\frac{2-\delta\beta q}{p}\right\rceil
.

Using
r=kq+\varepsilon p=lp+\delta\beta q
and the bound on
r
, we have a lower bound on
lp
:
lp\geq 2-\delta\beta q
. Because
\delta=\pm 1
and
\beta
and
q
are positive, we can say that
2-\delta\beta q\geq 2-\beta q
. Further,
\beta<p/q
, so
2-\beta q>2-p
. Then we have a (fairly loose) lower bound
lp>2-p
. In particular, because
l\neq 0
, this tells us that
l\geq 1
. We know that
lp+\delta\beta q\leq p+q
, so
(\delta\beta-1)q\leq(1-l)p
. Since
1-l\leq 0
and
\beta,q\geq 2
, we have
\delta=-1
.

The equality
r=kq+\varepsilon p=lp-\beta q
tells us that
(k+\beta)q=(l-\varepsilon)p
. Since
p
and
q
are relatively prime,
l=mq+\varepsilon
and
k=mp-\beta
for some integer
m
. Because
l\geq 1
, either
(m,\varepsilon)=(0,1)
or
m\geq 1
. If
(m,\varepsilon)=(0,1)
, then
l=1
and
k=-\beta
, and we have
(p,q,r)=(p,q,p-|k|q)
, where
2\leq|k|<p/q
. These knots are the first family listed in our theorem, using the more familiar notation of Dean
[
5
]
.

If
m=1
and
\varepsilon=-1
,
l=q-1
and
k=p-\beta
. This gives
r=pq-\beta q-p\leq p+q
, which tells us that
p(q-2)\leq(\beta+1)q
. This inequality cannot hold when
q\geq 4
, because
2p\leq p(q-2)
, but
(\beta+1)q<p+q
This would require
p<q
, which contradicts our assumptions. When
q=2
,
r=p-2\beta
, so we have
(p,q,r)=(p,2,p-2\beta)
, where
2\leq\beta<p/2
. This is a special case of the knots listed in the first family of the theorem.

When
q=3
, we have
r=2p-3\beta
, where
2\leq\beta<p/3
. To maintain the requirement that
2p-3\beta\leq p+3
, we need
p\leq 3\beta+3
. On the other hand,
\beta<p/3
, so
3\beta
must be the element of
\{p-1,p-2,p-3\}
that is divisible by 3. Since
p
and
q=3
must be relatively prime,
3\beta
will be either
p-1
or
p-2
, depending on the congruence class of
p
modulo 3.

When
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
,
3\beta=p-1
, so
r=p+1
, and when
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,
3\beta=p-2
, so
r=p+2
. Then we have the triples
(p,3,p+1)
with
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
(p,3,p+2)
with
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
These knots constitute the second family listed in the theorem.

If
m\geq 1
and
\varepsilon=1
, we have
r=lp-\beta q=mpq+p-\beta q
. Because
\beta<p/q
,
p-\beta q>0
, so
r\geq mpq
. Then
r
is strictly greater than
p+q
, and no examples arise from this case.

If
m\geq 2
and
\varepsilon=-1
, then
kq-p=(mp-\beta)q-p
, which is strictly greater than
(2p-p/q)q-p=2p(q-1)
. The expression
2p(q-1)
is strictly greater than
p+q
for all allowable values of
p
and
q
, so no examples arise in this case.

Case
2
:
The conditions of this case require that
r=kq+\varepsilon
and
r=lp+\delta\beta q
, where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\delta=\pm 1
,
k\geq 1
, and
l
is a nonzero integer satisfying
\displaystyle l\geq\left\lceil\frac{2-\delta\beta q}{p}\right\rceil
.

First, we consider the lower bound on
l
. When
\delta=1
, because
\beta<p/q
,
\frac{2-\beta q}{p}>\frac{2-p}{p}
. We assume that
p\geq 3
, so
\frac{2-p}{p}
is greater than
-1
, which tells us that
\left\lceil\frac{2-\beta q}{p}\right\rceil\geq 0
, so the lower bound on
l
is nonnegative. When
\delta=-1
, we have
l\geq\left\lceil\frac{2+\beta q}{p}\right\rceil
. Because
2\leq\beta
and
2\leq q
,
\frac{2+\beta q}{p}\geq\frac{6}{p}
. Hence, we find that
l
will be bounded below by a positive number.

Since
r\leq p+q
, we have
kq+\varepsilon\leq p+q
, so we know
(k-1)q+\varepsilon\leq p
. Because
2\leq\beta<p/q
,
(k-\beta)q+\varepsilon<p
. Then if
\delta=1
, the equation
r=kq+\varepsilon=lp+\beta q
requires that
l
be negative. As noted above
l
cannot be negative, so we have
\delta=-1
.

Then
r=kq+\varepsilon=lp-\beta q
. This tells us that
lp=(k+\beta)q+\varepsilon
. We can rewrite the right side of this equation as
(k-1)q+\varepsilon+(\beta+1)q
, which we know is at most
p+(\beta+1)q
. Then, using the strict upper bound on
\beta
, we have
lp<2p+q
. That is,
l\leq 2
, and the possible values of
l
are 1 and 2.

When
l=1
, we have
r=kq+\varepsilon
and
p=(k+\beta)q+\varepsilon
, so we see that
r=p-\beta q
. This is a special case of an already-listed family of knots. When
l=2
, we have
2p=(k+\beta)q+\varepsilon
, so we see that
k+\beta
and
q
are both odd. Let
k+\beta=2i+1
and
q=2j+1
where
i,j\geq 1
. Then
p=i(2j+1)+j+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}
and
r=(2i+1-\beta)(2j+1)+\varepsilon
. The bounds on
\beta
guarantee that
2\leq\beta\leq i
because
\frac{p}{q}=i+\frac{j+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}}{2j+1}
, which is less than
i+1
. To maintain the requirement that
r\leq p+q
, then, we need
(2i+1-\beta)(2j+1)+\varepsilon\leq(i+1)(2j+1)+j+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}
. Reducing this inequality, we see that we need
(i-\beta)(2j+1)\leq j+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}

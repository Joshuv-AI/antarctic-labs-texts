Fractional Diffusion Bridges
Yuzuru Inahama

Abstract
Consider “stochastic differential equations” driven by fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H ∈ ( 1 / 4 , 1 ) H\in(1/4,1) . Their solutions are sometimes called fractional diffusion processes. The main purpose of this paper is conditioning these processes to reach a given terminal point. We call the conditioned processes fractional diffusion bridges. Our main tool for mathematically rigorous conditioning is quasi-sure analysis, which is a potential theoretic part of Malliavin calculus. We also prove a small-noise large deviation principle of Freidlin-Wentzell type for scaled fractional diffusion bridges under a mild ellipticity assumption on the coefficient vector fields. Keywords. rough path theory, Malliavin calculus, fractional Brownian motion, Watanabe distribution theory, quasi-sure analysis, large deviation principle. Mathematics subject classification. 60L20, 60H07, 60G22, 60F10,.

Introduction

Consider a diffusion process on the Euclidean space

{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
,
e\geq 1
, which is realized as (the law of)
a unique solution of the following stochastic differential equation (SDE) of Stratonovich type driven by the standard
d
-dimensional
Brownian motion
w=(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
:

dy_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y_{t})\circ dw^{j}_{t}+V_{0}(y_{t})dt,\qquad y_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

Here,
V_{j}~(0\leq j\leq d)
are sufficiently regular vector fields on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

and
T\in(0,\infty)
is the time horizon.
When we emphasize the initial value
a
, we write
y(t,a)

instead of
y_{t}
.
If the law of
(y(t,a))_{t\in[0,T]}
is denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{a}
,
then
\{\mathbb{Q}_{a}\mid a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}\}
becomes a diffusion process
associated with the generator
V_{0}+(1/2)\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}^{2}
,
which is a very important research object in probability theory.

In many problems in the theory of diffusion processes,
conditioning the process
(y(t,a))_{t\in[0,T]}
to reach
a given point
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
at the terminal time
T
is quite important and useful.
The conditioned process is called a diffusion bridge
(or pinned diffusion process) from
a
to
b
,
whose law will be denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}
.
Rigorous construction of a diffusion bridge is not so easy.
As far as the author knows, there are four methods:

(a)

Regular conditional probability given a random variable.
One defines
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}
as
\mathbb{Q}_{a}(\,\bullet\,|\Pi_{T}=b)
,
or equivalently, as
\mu(y(\,\cdot\,,a)\in\,\bullet\,|y(T,a)=b)
.
Here,
\mu
is the
d
-dimensional Wiener measure and

\Pi_{T}
stands for the evaluation map at the time
T
.

(b)

Define finite dimensional distributions of
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}

by using Chapman-Kolmogorov type formula
when the law of
y(t,a)
has a continuous density function

p(t,a,b)
for all
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
and
t>0
. (See the right hand side of
(
4.3
) below.)

(c)

When the law of
y(t,a)
has a
density function
p(t,a,b)
for all
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
and

p
is sufficiently regular as a function of
(t,a,b)
, one can obtain

\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}
as the law of the solution of the following SDE
whose drift is quite singular near time
T
:

d\hat{y}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(\hat{y}_{t})\circ dw^{j}_{t}+V_{0}(\hat{y}_{t})dt+(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{V}^{\top})(\hat{y}_{t})\nabla\log p(T-t,\hat{y}_{t},b)dt,\quad\hat{y}_{0}=a.

Here,
\nabla p(t,a,b)
stands for the gradient of
a\mapsto p(t,a,b)

and
\mathbf{V}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
is the diffusion matrix
associated with
\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq d}

(see at the beginning of
Section
5
for a precise definition).

(d)

Quasi-sure analysis, which is a potential theoretic part
of Malliavin calculus. This theory enables us to define
a Borel probability measure on a very thin subset of the Wiener space, which looks like a “pullback” of the diffusion bridge measure
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}
. One can obtain
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}

as the law of the Itô map (i.e. the solution map of the SDE)
under the pullback measure.

The main purpose of our present paper
is constructing bridge processes for solutions of “SDEs” driven by
fractional Brownian motion (FBM) with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/4,1)
.
These “SDEs” are understood
in the rough path sense when
H\in(1/4,1/2]

and in the Young sense when
H\in(1/2,1)
.
The solutions of these equations are sometimes called fractional diffusion processes.
When
H=1/2
, the solutions
coincide with those of Stratonovich SDEs.
Our main tool is quasi-sure analysis as in Item
(d)
above.
Recall that Malliavin calculus for those type of
stochastic systems is now well-developed.
We call these bridge processes fractional diffusion bridges.
We also prove a small-noise large deviation principle of
Freidlin-Wentzell type for these (scaled) fractional diffusion bridges
when the diffusion coefficients satisfy a suitable ellipticity condition.

Now we quickly explain why Items
(a)
–
(c)
are unavailable
for conditioning fractional diffusion processes.
Chapman-Kolmogorov formula as in
(b)
is in a sense
the Markov property itself.
The main reasons why the SDE in
(c)
produces a diffusion bridge
is Doob’s
h
-transform, which is a famous theorem
in the theory of Markov processes.
Since our processes are not Markov,
neither
(b)
nor
(c)
are available.
Though Method
(a)
is very general, it is merely defined for
almost all
b
with respect to
(\Pi_{T})_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}=y(T,a)_{*}\mu
.
Therefore, it is meaningless to discuss

\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}(\,\bullet\,|\Pi_{T}=b)
or

\mu(y(\,\cdot\,,a)\in\,\bullet\,|y(T,a)=b)
for a fixed
b

since the singleton
\{b\}

is usually of measure zero with respect to
y(T,a)_{*}\mu
.
(See Remark
4.8
for details.)
In contrast, a remarkable advantage
of using
(d)
is that we can define

\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H}
for every
a
and
b
with

p(T,a,b)>0
when
y(T,a)
is non-degenerate in the sense of Malliavin.
(In this paragraph,
(y(t,a))_{t\in[0,T]}
is the fractional diffusion process starting at
a
, whose law is denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}
.
The law of
(y(t,a))_{t\in[0,T]}
conditioned that

y(T,a)=b
is denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H}
.)

Remark 1.1
.

Our fractional diffusion processes/bridges do not satisfy
the Markov property (unless
H=1/2
).
Therefore, when we say fractional diffusion processes/bridges,
the term
diffusion
is not used as a terminology
from the theory of Markov processes,
but belongs to everyday vocabulary of English.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, many important facts on
Besov-type rough paths and Malliavin calculus are reviewed.
Those will be used in later sections.
In Section
3
we provide a quasi-sure refinement
of the canonical rough path lift for a certain class of Gaussian processes
including FBM with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/4,1/2]
.
We then prove the lift map is
\infty
-quasi-continuous.
Section
4
is a core part of this paper.
In this section, we condition a fractional diffusion process, that is,
a unique solution of a rough differential equation driven by
the canonical lift of FBM with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/4,1/2]
.
The conditioned processes (or measures)
obtained in this way are called fractional diffusion bridges.
In Sections
5
and
6

we state and prove a small-noise large deviation principle of
Freidlin-Wentzell type for scaled fractional diffusion bridges with
H\in(1/4,1/2]

under a certain
“everywhere ellipticity”” condition on the diffusion coefficient.
In Section
7
, we consider the case
H\in(1/2,1)

and show that all the results in Sections
4
–
6
still hold true. Note that this case is much simpler
because rough path theory is not needed to define fractional
diffusion processes. So, our explanations are a little bit quick.

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|X\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|X^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{1/i}

2.2
Review of Malliavin calculus and quasi-sure analysis

Let
({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
be an abstract Wiener space,
that is,
({\mathcal{W}},\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{W}}})
is a separable Banach space,
({\mathcal{H}},\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}})
is a separable Hilbert space,
{\mathcal{H}}
is a dense subspace of
{\mathcal{W}}
and the inclusion map is continuous, and
\mu
is a (necessarily unique) Borel probability measure
on

with the property that

{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
and
{\mathcal{H}}

Hence,
{\mathcal{W}}^{*}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}={\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}

A generic element of
{\mathcal{W}}

, while

is denoted by

or

We denote by

the family of centered Gaussian random variables
defined on
{\mathcal{W}}
indexed by
{\mathcal{H}}

(i.e. the homogeneous Wiener chaos of order
1
).
If
\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}

{\mathcal{W}}^{*}

then the extension coincides with the random
variable
\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle
.

{\mathcal{W}}^{*}

is define as the

-limit of

\{k_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is any sequence of
{\mathcal{H}}
such that

\langle k_{n},\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{*}
for all

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|k_{n}-k\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0

We also denote by
\tau_{k}\colon{\mathcal{W}}\to{\mathcal{W}}
the translation

\tau_{k}(w)=w+k
.
(For basic information on abstract Wiener spaces, see
[
34
,
16
]
among others.)

Now let us recall a few important properties of the Wiener chaos.
For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, denote by

\mathscr{C}_{m}
the
m
th homogeneous Wiener chaos
(see
[
34
, Chapter 1]
for example).
It is well-known that
\mathscr{C}_{0}
is the space of
constant functions
and
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\{\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle\mid k\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
.
The
m
th inhomogeneous Wiener chaos is denoted by

\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{m}:=\oplus_{i=0}^{m}\mathscr{C}_{i}
.
Heuristically,
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{m}
is the set of
all real-valued
“Gaussian polynomials” on
{\mathcal{W}}
of order at most
m
.
The most important fact is the orthogonal decomposition

L^{2}(\mu)=\oplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{C}_{i}
.
Moreover,
\mathscr{C}_{m}
is the eigenspace of
the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
on
L^{2}(\mu)
associated with the eigenvalue
-m
.
If each component of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-valued function
F=(F^{1},\ldots,F^{n})
on
{\mathcal{W}}

belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{m}
(resp.
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{m}
),
we say that

belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

(resp.
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

Restricted to each
\mathscr{C}_{m}~(m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
,
all the
L^{p}
-norms (
1<p<\infty
) are equivalent,
that is,
there exists a constant
C=C_{m,p}\geq 1
such that

C^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|F\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}},\qquad F\in\mathscr{C}_{m}.

This is a consequence of the hypercontractivity of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
semigroup. For details, see
[
34
, Theorem 2.14]
among others.

Most of the contents and the notation

Also,

We will use the following notation and facts in the main parts of this paper.

(a)

Sobolev spaces

(b)

(c)

Pullback

Remark 2.1
.

To use the integration by parts formula (
2.7
)–(
2.8
)
repeatedly, we set the notation.
For
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{k}
(

)
and
G\in{\mathbf{D}}_{\infty}
,
we define

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}(w;G)\in{\mathbf{D}}_{\infty}

in a recursive way as follows.
For
k=1
,

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}(w;G)=\Phi_{i_{1}}(w;G)

is given by (
2.8
) with
i=i_{1}
.
For
k\geq 2
, we set

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}(w;G):=\Phi_{i_{1}}\left(w;\Phi_{(i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})}(\,\cdot\,;G)\right).

Notice that this involves
D^{l}G~(0\leq l\leq k)
, but not
D^{k+1}G
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(\partial^{k}_{\mathbf{i}}T\circ F)G\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(T\circ F)\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}(\,\cdot\,;G)\bigr],

(2.12)

where we set
\partial^{k}_{\mathbf{i}}:=\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{k}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

In this remark, we explain the precise meaning of the continuity
of Watanabe’s composition map
T\mapsto T(F)=T\circ F

when
F\in{\bf D}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is non-degenerate.
It should be noted that
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is not
equipped with the usual topology of “pointwise” convergence.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define the
\mathscr{S}_{2k}
-norm
of
g\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by

\|g\|_{\mathscr{S}_{2k}}=\|(1+|\,\cdot\,|^{2}-\Delta/2)^{k}g\|_{\infty}
.
Here,
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the Schwartz space of
rapidly decreasing functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the usual sup-norm and
\Delta
is the Laplacian on

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Set
\mathscr{S}_{2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to be the closure of

\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with respect to this
\mathscr{S}_{2k}
-norm.
Then, it is known that

\{\mathscr{S}_{2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is non-increasing in
k

and
\cap_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathscr{S}_{2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathscr{S}_{-2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Moreover,

\mathscr{S}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the Banach space of
continuous functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which vanish at
\infty
.

Then, for every
k\in N
and
1<p<\infty
,

g\mapsto g(F)
extends to a bounded linear map from

\mathscr{S}_{-2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
{\bf D}_{p,-2k}
.
(See
[
17
, Theorem 9.1, pp. 378-379]
or
[
30
, Section 5.4]
.)
This continuity is the main reason why we can
define
T\circ F
for an
arbitrary
T\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

A prominent example of
T\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is Dirac’s delta function
\delta_{z}~(z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Here, we recall some basic properties of
Watanabe’s pullback of the delta functions
\delta_{z}(F)

when
F\in{\bf D}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is non-degenerate.

First, recall that, for sufficiently large constant
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the map

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ni z\mapsto\delta_{z}\in\mathscr{S}_{-2l}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is continuous and bounded
(see
[
17
, Lemma 9.1]
and its proof).
Then, by the continuity of Watanabe’s decomposition (see Remark
2.2
), the map

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ni z\mapsto\delta_{z}(F)\in{\bf D}_{p,-2l}

is bounded and continuous for every
p\in(1,\infty)
.
This in turn implies that the map

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ni z\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)G]\in{\mathbb{R}}

(2.13)

is bounded and continuous for every
G\in{\bf D}_{\infty}
.
(In fact, this function is known to belong to

\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, but this fact is not used in this paper.)
In particular, the density function

p_{F}(z):={\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)]
of the law of
F
under
\mu
is
continuous and bounded in
z
.

Furthermore, for every
\chi\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

\chi=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\chi(z)\delta_{z}\,dz\,\in\mathscr{S}_{-2l}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Here, the right hand side is a Bochner integral.
(Clearly,
z\mapsto|\chi(z)|\|\delta_{z}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{-2l}}
is integrable
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
dz
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.)
Then, it immediately follows that

{\mathbb{E}}[\chi(F)G]=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\chi(z){\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)G]\,dz,\qquad G\in{\bf D}_{\infty},\,\chi\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

(2.14)

Let
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let

\{\chi_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f(\hat{z})\chi_{n}(\hat{z})d\hat{z}=f(z)
for every

f\in C_{{\rm b}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
By the continuity in (
2.13
), we then have

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[\chi_{n}(F)G]={\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)G],\qquad G\in{\bf D}_{\infty}.

(2.15)

As one can easily guess, Watanabe’s pullback of delta functions are positive.
This fact is well-known and can be found in
[
30

.
Since this is of particular importance in this paper, we
provide a short proof, which is different from that in
[

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
F\in{\bf D}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is non-degenerate.
Then,
\delta_{z}(F)
is a positive Watanabe distribution for every
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Take any non-negative
G\in{\bf D}_{\infty}
and any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
It is sufficient to show that
{\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)G]\geq 0
.
Then, there exists

\{\chi_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
(i)
\chi_{n}
is non-negative for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and (ii)
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f(\hat{z})\chi_{n}(\hat{z})d\hat{z}=f(z)
for every
f\in C_{{\rm b}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Then, the lemma immediately follows from (
2.15
).
∎

Intuitively, it should be clear that the measure
corresponding to
\delta_{z}(F)

via Sugita’s theorem is concentrated on
F^{-1}(z)
.
The precise statement should be as in the following

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
F\in{\bf D}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is non-degenerate
and
{\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)]>0

Denote by
\nu_{z}
the finite Borel measure that corresponds to
the positve distributonion
\delta_{z}(F)
.
Then,

\nu_{z}(\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\mid\tilde{F}\neq z\})=(\nu_{z}\circ\tilde{F}^{-1})({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{z\})=0.

Here,
\tilde{F}
stands for an
\infty
-quasi-continuous modification of
F
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show the following: For every non-negative

\phi\in C_{{\rm K}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
whose support is away from
b
,
we have

0={\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{z}(F)\phi(F)]=\int_{{\mathcal{W}}}\phi(\tilde{F}(w))\nu_{z}(dw)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\phi(\hat{z})\,(\nu_{z}\circ\tilde{F}^{-1})(d\hat{z}).

(2.16)

We prove this by using (
2.15

Take an open neighborhood
O
of

which does not intersect
the support of
\phi
.
Then, there exists

\{\chi_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset C_{{\rm K}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
(i)
\chi_{n}
is non-negative for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(ii) the support of
\chi_{n}
is included in
O
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

and (iii)
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f(\hat{z})\chi_{n}(\hat{z})d\hat{z}=f(z)
for every
f\in C_{{\rm b}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Then, (
2.16
) immediately follows from (
2.15
).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In some of the books cited in this subsection (in particular
[
17
,

,

]
), results are formulated on a special Gaussian space.
However, almost most all of them

3
Quasi-sure refinement of
canonical rough path lift of Gaussian processes

Let
\xi=(\xi_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a real-valued
centered continuous Gaussian process.
We denote
the covariance of
\xi
by
R(s,t):={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{s}\xi_{t}]

(s,t\in[0,T])
.
The rectangular increment of
R
is defined by

\hat{R}\begin{pmatrix}s,\,t\\
u,\,v\end{pmatrix}:={\mathbb{E}}[(\xi_{t}-\xi_{s})(\xi_{v}-\xi_{u})]=R(t,v)-R(s,v)-R(t,u)+R(s,u)

for
s,t,u,v\in[0,T]
.
For
\rho\geq 1
and
[s,t]\times[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\subset[0,T]^{2}
,
the 2D
\rho
-variation norm of
\hat{R}
on
[s,t]\times[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]

is defined by

V_{\rho}(\hat{R},[s,t]\times[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]):=\sup_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left|\hat{R}\begin{pmatrix}t_{j-1},\,t_{j}\\
u_{k-1},\,u_{k}\end{pmatrix}\right|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho},

(3.1)

where the supremum runs over all the partitions

\pi=\{s=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=t\}
and

\pi^{\prime}=\{s^{\prime}=u_{0}<u_{1}<\cdots<u_{M}=t^{\prime}\}
.
We say that
R
has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

V_{\rho}(\hat{R},[s,t]^{2})\leq C(t-s)^{1/\rho}

holds for all
(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}
.
Note that if
\rho\leq\rho^{\prime}
and

R
has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation,
then
R
has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho^{\prime}
-variation.

Let
(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}=(w^{1}_{t},\ldots,w^{d}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be
a
d
-dimensional
centered continuous Gaussian process with independent components.
Since we consider a rough path lift,
we may assume
w_{0}=0
, a.s. without loss of generality.
We say that the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation
if the covariance of each component
w^{i}~(i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket)

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation.

We denote the law and the Cameron-Martin space of
(w_{t})

by
\mu
and
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.
The former is a probability measure on
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

and the latter is a linear subspace of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
If we set
\mathcal{W}:=\overline{\mathcal{H}}
, where the closure is taken in
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{H},\mu)
becomes an abstract Wiener space.
In what follows, we may and will assume that
(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is
canonically realized
(i.e. the coordinate process) on
(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{H},\mu)
.

For
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, set
\mathcal{P}_{l}=\{jT/2^{l}\mid j\in\llbracket 0,2^{l}\rrbracket\}

be the partition of
[0,T]
with equal length
T/2^{l}
.
For
w\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
w(l)
is the piecewise linear
approximation of
w
associated with
\mathcal{P}_{l}
, that is,

w(l)_{jT/2^{l}}=w_{jT/2^{l}}
for each
j\in\llbracket 0,2^{l}\rrbracket

and
w(l)
is linearly interpolated on each subinterval

[(j-1)T/2^{l},jT/2^{l}]~(j\in\llbracket 1,2^{l}\rrbracket)
.
For
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2]
and
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we set

W(l):=S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(w(l))
, which is
an
G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-valued random variable
under
\mu
.
We set

\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}:=\{w\in\mathcal{W}\mid\mbox{$\lim_{l\to\infty}W(l)$ exists in
$G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})$}\}

and

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}(w):=\begin{cases}\lim_{l\to\infty}W(l)&\mbox{(if $w\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}$),}\\
{\bf 0}&\mbox{(otherwise),}\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
{\bf 0}
stands for the zero rough path
(in some literarture, it is denoted by
\mathbf{1}
).
We view
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}
is a measurable map
from
\mathcal{W}
to
G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Also, for
(\alpha,m)
satisfying (
2.1
),
we set
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}
and

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}
in essentially the same way.
We will often write

W=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}
or

W=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}
without the parameters
of functional spaces for notational simplicity.
We have
{\mathcal{C}}^{1\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}
and

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}(w)=S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(w)

for every
w\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

since

\lim_{l\to\infty}\|w(l)-w\|_{\delta\textrm{-Hld}}=0
for every
\delta\in(0,1)
.
An analogous fact holds in the Besov case, too.

It is natural to ask whether
\{W(l)\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is convergent
in the geometric rough path space in an appropriate sense
or how large the subsets
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}

and
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}
are.
Concerning this, the following result is available
(see
[
11
, Theorem 15.42]
).
The limit

W:=\lim_{m\to\infty}W(m)
is called a canonical lift of
the Gaussian process
w=(w_{t})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation for
\rho\in[1,2)
.
Take any
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2]
with

2\rho<1/\alpha<\lfloor 2\rho\rfloor+1
.
Then, there exists a constant
\kappa\in(0,1)
with
the following property:
For every
q\in[1,\infty)
,
there exists a constant
C=C_{q}>0
independent of
l,l^{\prime}
such that

\|d_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(W(l),W(l^{\prime}))\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\kappa^{l\wedge l^{\prime}},\qquad l,l^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}

holds.
In particular,

W:=\lim_{l\to\infty}W(l)
exists a.s. (i.e.
\mu(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}})=1
)
and in
L^{q}
-sense for every
q\in[1,\infty)
.

Hence, under the assumptions of the above proposition, we have

\bigl\|\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}\bigr\|_{L^{q}}\vee\sup_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|\|W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}\bigr\|_{L^{q}}<\infty

for every
q\in[1,\infty)
and
i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor
.
It immediately follows that if
(\alpha,m)
satisfies

\frac{1}{\lfloor 2\rho\rfloor+1}<\alpha-\frac{1}{12m}<\alpha<\frac{1}{2\rho}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad m\in{\mathbb{N}},

(3.3)

then we have

\bigl\|\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}\bigr\|_{L^{q}}\vee\sup_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|\|W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}\bigr\|_{L^{q}}<\infty

(3.4)

for every
q\in[1,\infty)
and
i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Assume that the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation for
\rho\in[1,2)
.
Let
(\alpha,m)
satisfy (
3.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
\kappa\in(0,1)
with
the following property:
For every
q\in[1,\infty)
,
there exists a constant
C=C_{q}>0
independent of
l,l^{\prime}
such that

\|d_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}(W(l),W(l^{\prime}))\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\kappa^{l\wedge l^{\prime}},\qquad l,l^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}

holds.
In particular,

W:=\lim_{l\to\infty}W(l)
exists a.s.
(i.e.
\mu(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}})=1
)
and in
L^{q}
-sense for every
q\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

This inequality is immediate from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

In the following theorem and its corollary,
we provide main results of this section.
They claim that the canonical lift map for Gaussian processes
admits a quasi-sure refinement and, moreover,
is
\infty
-quasi-continuous.
The
\infty
-quasi-continuity will play an indispensable role
when we define fractional diffusion bridges in the next section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation for
\rho\in[1,2)
.
Let
(\alpha,m)
satisfy (
3.3
).
Then, the complement of

\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}
is a slim set.
Moreover,

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}\colon\mathcal{W}\to G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is
\infty
-quasi-continuous.

Proof.

In this proof
(\alpha,m)
is basically fixed.
For
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor
, we set

\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l}:=\left\{w\in\mathcal{W}\,\middle|\,\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m/i}>(l^{-2})^{12m/i}\right\}.

For every
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m/i}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{12m/i}

and

w\mapsto W(l)=S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(w(l))
is continuous
from
\mathcal{W}
to

G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Hence,
\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l}
is open in
{\mathcal{W}}
.

Pick
q\in(1,\infty)
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
arbitrarily.
By the Chebyshev-type inequality for the capacities
(see
[
29
, P. 90, Lemma 1.2.5]
), (
2.11
) and Corollary
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cap}_{q,k}(\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l})

\displaystyle\leq M_{q,k}l^{24m/i}\left\|\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m/i}\right\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,k}}

\displaystyle\leq M^{\prime}_{q,k}l^{24m/i}\left\|\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m/i}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq M^{\prime}_{q,k}C_{2}l^{24m/i}\kappa^{l}.

(3.5)

Here,
M_{q,k}
and
M^{\prime}_{q,k}
are positive constants
depending only on
(q,k)
and

C_{2}>0
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
are constants in Corollary
3.2
.
None of these four constants depend on
l
, which
implies that

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathrm{Cap}_{q,k}(\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l})<\infty.

Using the Borel-Cantelli lemma for the capacities (see
[
29
, P. 90, Corollary 1.2.4]
),
we can see from the above fact that

\mathrm{Cap}_{q,k}\left(\limsup_{l\to\infty}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l}\right)=0.

If
w\in\cap_{i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(\limsup_{l\to\infty}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l})^{c}
,
there exists
l_{0}(w)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}\leq l^{-2}\quad\mbox{for all $l\geq l_{0}(w)$ and $i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor$,}

from which it immediately follows that

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}<\infty\quad\mbox{for all $i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor$.}

Note that
the above condition implies that
\{W(l)\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is Cauchy in

G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Hence,

\cap_{i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(\limsup_{l\to\infty}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l})^{c}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}

and

\mathrm{Cap}_{q,k}((\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}})^{c})=0
.
Since
(q,k)
is arbitrary,
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}

is slim.

Next we show the
(q,k)
-quasi-continuity of

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be given.
We can easily see from (
3.5
) that
there exists
\ell(\varepsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\mathrm{Cap}_{q,k}\left(\cup_{i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}\cup_{l=\ell(\varepsilon)}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}\sum_{l=\ell(\varepsilon)}^{\infty}\mathrm{Cap}_{q,k}(\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l})<\varepsilon.

On the complement of the open subset

\cup_{i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}\cup_{l=\ell(\varepsilon)}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{l}
, we have

\|W(l+1)^{i}-W(l)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}\leq l^{-2}\quad\mbox{for all $l\geq\ell(\varepsilon)$ and $i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor$.}

This implies that the sequence
\{W(l)\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of continuous maps
from
{\mathcal{W}}
to
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

uniformly converges on the complement.
Therefore,
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}
is
also continuous restricted to the complement.
Thus, we have shown the
(q,k)
-quasi-continuity for every
(q,k)
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume that the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation for
\rho\in[1,2)
.
Take any
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2]
with

2\rho<1/\alpha<\lfloor 2\rho\rfloor+1
.
Then, the complement of

\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
is a slim set.
Moreover,

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}\colon\mathcal{W}\to G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is
\infty
-quasi-continuous.

Proof.

We can find
(\alpha^{\prime},m)
satisfying both (
3.3
)
and
\alpha^{\prime}-(12m)^{-1}\geq\alpha
.
With the Besov-Hölder embedding (
2.5
) at hand,
we just need to use Theorem
3.3
for
(\alpha^{\prime},m)
.
∎

Under the assumptions of Corollary
3.4
,

F\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}=F(W)
is
also
\infty
-quasi-continuous on
\mathcal{W}

if
F
is a continuous map from
G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to another topological space.
A prominent example of such an
F
is the Lyons-Itô map
associated with an RDE.

Let
V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
are vector fields on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

(which are here viewed as
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
) and
consider the following controlled ODE:

dy_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y_{t})dx^{j}_{t}+V_{0}(y_{t})dt,\qquad y_{0}=a

(3.6)

The initial value
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
is arbitrary, but fixed.
The super-index
j
on
dx^{j}
stands for the coordinate of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
not the level of rough path.
Here, we understand (
3.6
) as an RDE,
namely, a controlled ODE controlled by a geometric rough path
X
.
If a unique global solution
y
exists for
X
, we write
y:=\Phi_{a}(X)
.
(Since we only consider the first level of a solution,
any formulation of RDEs will do.)

If
V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket)
are of
{\rm Lip}^{\gamma}

and

V_{0}
is of
{\rm Lip}^{1+\delta}
for some
\gamma>\alpha^{-1}

and
\delta>0
,
then RDE (
3.6
) has a unique global solution

y=\Phi_{a}(X)
exists for every

X\in G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and, moreover,
the Lyons-Itô map

\Phi_{a}\colon G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
is continuous
(see
[
11
, Theorem 12.10 and Remark 12.7 (i)]
for example.)

Summarizing the above argument, we have the following corollary.
Note that
\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}=\Phi_{a}(W)

is the solution of (
3.6
) when the driving rough path
X
is
replaced by the Gaussian rough path

W=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(w)
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume that the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
\rho
-variation for
\rho\in[1,2)
.
Assume further that

V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket)
are of
{\rm Lip}^{\gamma}

for some
\gamma>2\rho

and
V_{0}
is of
{\rm Lip}^{1+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
.
Then, for every
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2]
with

2\rho<1/\alpha<\gamma\wedge(\lfloor 2\rho\rfloor+1)
,
the correspondence

w\mapsto\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}=\Phi(W)

is an
\infty
-quasi-continuous map
from
\mathcal{W}
to

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
.

Now we provide an example of Gaussian process that
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.3

and Corollary
3.4
.
Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\vec{H}=(H_{1},\ldots,H_{d})\in(0,1)^{d}
.
We set
\underline{H}=\min_{1\leq j\leq d}H_{j}
.
A
d
-dimensional continuous stochastic process

(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}=(w^{1}_{t},\ldots,w^{d}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
are
called a mixed FBM with
Hurst parameter
\vec{H}
if
all components are independent one-dimensional
centered Gaussian processes whose covariances are given by

\mathbb{E}[w^{j}_{s}w^{j}_{t}]=\frac{1}{2}(s^{2H_{j}}+t^{2H_{j}}-|t-s|^{2H_{j}}),\qquad s,t\in[0,T],\,j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket.

In other words,
w
is a collection of
independent one-dimensional FBM’s with the Hurst parameter
H_{j}
.
The law of this process is denoted by
\mu=\mu^{\vec{H}}
,
which is a centered Gaussian measure on
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
The associated Cameron-Martin space is
denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{\vec{H}}
. The triplet

({\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\mathcal{H}^{\vec{H}},\mu^{\vec{H}})
is an abstract Wiener space.
Obviously, the coordinate process on
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

is a standard realization of the mixed FBM with
the Hurst parameter
\vec{H}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
w=(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a mixed FBM with the Hurst parameter
\vec{H}
as above and assume that
\underline{H}\in(1/4,1)
.
Then, the covariance of
w

has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
(2\underline{H})^{-1}\vee 1
-variation.

Proof.

This claim reduces the one-dimensional case.
Hence, we may assume that
w
is a one-dimensional FBM
with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/4,1)
.
When
H\in(1/4,1/2]
, it is shown in
[
12
, Example 1]
that
the covariance
has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
(2H)^{-1}
-variation.
Therefore, it suffices to check that the covariance
has finite 2D Hölder-controlled
1
-variation when
H\in(1/2,1)
.
Recall that
w
is positively correlated in this case, i.e.,

{\mathbb{E}}[w^{1}_{u,v}w^{1}_{s,t}]\geq 0
if
u\leq v\leq s\leq t
.
Here, we set
w^{1}_{s,t}:=w_{t}-w_{s}
.

Let both
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be partitions of
[s,t]\subset[0,T]
.
Since the sum on the right hand side of (
3.1
)
satisfies sub-additivity when
\rho=1
,
the sum associated with
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
is dominated by
the sum associated with
(\pi\cup\pi^{\prime},\,\pi\cup\pi^{\prime})
.
Therefore, when we estimate
V_{1}(\hat{R},[s,t]^{2})
, we may only
consider the case
\pi=\pi^{\prime}
in (
3.1
).

Due to the positive correlation, it holds
for
\pi=\{s=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=t\}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\Bigl|{\mathbb{E}}[w^{1}_{t_{j-1},t_{j}}w^{1}_{t_{k-1},t_{k}}]\Bigr|=\Bigl|\sum_{k}{\mathbb{E}}[w^{1}_{t_{j-1},t_{j}}w^{1}_{t_{k-1},t_{k}}]\Bigr|\leq\Bigl|{\mathbb{E}}[w^{1}_{t_{j-1},t_{j}}w^{1}_{s,t}]\Bigr|

for every
j
.
Hence, it suffices to prove that there exists a positive constant

C=C_{H}
such that, for every
[u,v]\subset[s,t]
,

\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}[w^{1}_{u,v}w^{1}_{s,t}]\bigr|\leq C_{H}(v-u)

(3.7)

holds.

By straightforward computation, we have

\displaystyle 2\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}[w^{1}_{u,v}w^{1}_{s,t}]\bigr|

\displaystyle=|-(t-v)^{2H}-(u-s)^{2H}+(v-s)^{2H}+(t-u)^{2H}|

\displaystyle=\{(t-u)^{2H}-(t-v)^{2H}\}+\{(v-s)^{2H}-(u-s)^{2H}\}

\displaystyle=(v-u)\int_{0}^{1}2H\{t-v+\theta(v-u)\}^{2H-1}d\theta

\displaystyle\qquad+(v-u)\int_{0}^{1}2H\{u-s+\theta(v-u)\}^{2H-1}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 4HT^{2H-1}(v-u).

Here, we used the mean-value theorem and
2H>1
.
Thus, we obtained (
3.7
), which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4
Conditioning fractional diffusion processes

The main purpose of this section is defining
fractional diffusion bridges,
which is one of the two main results of this paper.
Throughout this section, we assume
H\in(1/4,1/2]
and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Denote by
\mu^{H}
the law of

d
-dimensional FBM with Hurst parameter
H
,
which is a centered Gaussian measure on
\mathcal{W}={\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
The coordinate process
w=(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
on
{\mathcal{W}}

is a canonical realization of FBM under
\mu^{H}
.
The Cameron-Martin space of
\mu^{H}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
As is well-known,
({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{H}}^{H},\mu^{H})
is an abstract Wiener space.

We study RDE (
3.6
) driven by the canonical lift

W:=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(w)
of FBM,
where
(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1}<\alpha<H
. It reads:

dy_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y_{t})dw^{j}_{t}+V_{0}(y_{t})dt,\qquad y_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

(4.1)

In other words,
y=\Phi_{a}(W)
.
We denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}
the law of this process
y
, that is,

\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}:=(\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}})_{*}\mu^{H}
.
Since
\Phi_{a}
takes values in the “little
\alpha
-Hölder space”

\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
,
this Borel probability measure sits on
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
.

4.1
Some properties of Cameron-Martin paths

In this subsection
we discuss the Cameron-Martin space
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
associated with
\mu^{H}
.
It is known that there is a continuous embedding

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{q\textrm{-var}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for any
q
with

\left(H+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1}<q<2.

(4.2)

More precisely, for every
q
as in (
4.2
),
there exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(q)>0

independent of
h
and
(s,t)

such that

\|h\|_{q\textrm{-var},[s,t]}\leq c_{1}\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H},\,(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}.

(4.3)

Here,
\|\cdot\|_{q\textrm{-var},[s,t]}
stands for the

q
-variation (semi)norm restricted to the subinterval
[s,t]
.
Note that
\tfrac{1}{q}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nearrow H

as
q\searrow(H+\tfrac{1}{2})^{-1}
.
(See
[
10
]
for the facts in this paragraph.)

By (
4.3
) and the general theory of Young integration,
there exists a constant
c_{2}=c_{2}(q)>0

independent of
h
and
(s,t)
such that

|S(h)^{i}_{s,t}|\leq c_{2}\|h\|_{q\textrm{-var},[s,t]}^{i}\leq c_{2}c_{1}^{i}\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{i}(t-s)^{i(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})},\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H},\,(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}

(4.4)

for all
i=1,2,3
.
This implies that

S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)
is an
\alpha
-Hölder weakly geometric
rough path for every
\alpha\in(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1},\,H)
.
By
[
11
, Theorem 8.22]
and slight adjustment of the
parameter
\alpha
,

S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)\in G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

for every
\alpha\in(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1},\,H)
.
By a standard argument,

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\ni h\mapsto S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)\in G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Now we consider the piecewise linear approximation of
a Cameron-Martin path.
Since
\lim_{l\to\infty}h(l)=h
in
(q+\delta)
-variation topology
for every sufficiently small
\delta>0
,
we have
\lim_{l\to\infty}S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h(l))=S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)
in
G\Omega_{1/\alpha\textrm{-var}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
However, whether

h\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
is not so obvious
though it looks natural heuristically.

For the pair of parameters
(\alpha,m)
, we
introduce the following condition:

m\in{\mathbb{N}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1}<\alpha-\frac{1}{12m}<\alpha<H

(4.5)

This condition will be frequently used in what follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let the notation be as above and assume (
4.5
).
Then, we have

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}
.
In particular,

\{S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h(l))\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to

S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}(h)

in
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

h\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}
,
that is,
\{S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h(l))\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Cauchy sequence
in
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
We only prove the case
1/4<H\leq 1/3
since the case
1/3<H\leq 1/2

is much easier.

In this proof, we use the following notation.
We write
H(l):=S_{3}(h(l))
and
W(l):=S_{3}(w(l))
.
Let
C>0
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
be constants
independent of
l
and
h
, which may vary from line to line.
We write

i,j,k\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
for coordinates of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
(These parameters do not stand for the level of a rough path.)

First, we calculate the first level paths.
For every
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we set

F^{1,i}_{l}(w):=\|W(l+1)^{1,i}-W(l)^{1,i}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m}=\iint_{\triangle_{T}}\frac{|W(l+1)^{1,i}_{s,t}-W(l)^{1,i}_{s,t}|^{12m}}{(t-s)^{1+12\alpha m}}dsdt.

Then,
F^{1,i}_{l}
is continuous in
w
and belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{12m}
.
By Corollary
3.2
, there exists
\kappa\in(0,1)

and
C>0
such that

\|F^{1,i}_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{l},\qquad l\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Noting that
D_{h}\{W(l+1)^{1,i}_{s,t}-W(l)^{1,i}_{s,t}\}=H(l+1)^{1,i}_{s,t}-H(l)^{1,i}_{s,t}
for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}

and
12m
is an even integer, we can easily see that

\displaystyle\langle D^{12m}F^{1,i}_{l}(w),\,h^{\otimes 12m}\rangle

\displaystyle=(D_{h})^{12m}F^{1,i}_{l}(w)

\displaystyle=C\iint_{\triangle_{T}}\frac{|H(l+1)^{1,i}_{s,t}-H(l)^{1,i}_{s,t}|^{12m}}{(t-s)^{1+12\alpha m}}dsdt

\displaystyle=C\|H(l+1)^{1,i}-H(l)^{1,i}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m}.

From (
2.11
) we can also see that

\displaystyle\|\langle D^{12m}F^{1,i}_{l}(w),\,h^{\otimes 12m}\rangle\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{12m}\|F^{1,i}_{l}\|_{{\bf D}_{2,12m}}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{12m}\|F^{1,i}_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{12m}\kappa^{l}

for every
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Thus, we have obtained

\|H(l+1)^{1,i}-H(l)^{1,i}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{12m}\kappa^{l},\qquad l\in{\mathbb{N}}.

This implies that the sequence
\{H(l)^{1}\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is Cauchy with respect to the
(\alpha,12m)
-Besov topology.

Next, we calculate the second level paths.
For every
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we set

F^{2,ij}_{l}(w):=\|W(l+1)^{2,ij}-W(l)^{2,ij}\|_{2\alpha,6m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{6m}=\iint_{\triangle_{T}}\frac{|W(l+1)^{2,ij}_{s,t}-W(l)^{2,ij}_{s,t}|^{6m}}{(t-s)^{1+12\alpha m}}dsdt.

Then,
F^{2,ij}_{l}
is continuous in
w
and belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{12m}
.
By Corollary
3.2
, there exists
\kappa\in(0,1)

and
C>0
such that

\|F^{2,ij}_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{l},\qquad l\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since
w\mapsto W(l)^{2,ij}_{s,t}
is a continuous quadratic
Wiener functional, we can easily see that

(D_{h})^{2}\{W(l+1)^{2,ij}_{s,t}-W(l)^{2,ij}_{s,t}\}=2\{H(l+1)^{2,ij}_{s,t}-H(l)^{2,ij}_{s,t}\},\qquad h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}.

By applying
(D_{h})^{12m}
to
F^{2,ij}_{l}
, we can obtain

\|H(l+1)^{2,ij}-H(l)^{2,ij}\|_{2\alpha,6m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{6m}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{12m}\kappa^{l},\qquad l\in{\mathbb{N}},

essentially in the same way as in the first level case.
Thus, the sequence
\{H(l)^{2}\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is Cauchy with respect to the
(2\alpha,6m)
-Besov topology.

Finally, we calculate the third level paths.
For every
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we set

F^{3,ijk}_{l}(w):=\|W(l+1)^{3,ijk}-W(l)^{3,ijk}\|_{3\alpha,4m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{4m}=\iint_{\triangle_{T}}\frac{|W(l+1)^{3,ijk}_{s,t}-W(l)^{3,ijk}_{s,t}|^{4m}}{(t-s)^{1+12\alpha m}}dsdt.

Then,
F^{3,ijk}_{l}
is continuous in
w
and belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{12m}
.
By Corollary
3.2
, there exists
\kappa\in(0,1)

and
C>0
such that

\|F^{3,ijk}_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{l},\qquad l\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In this case, we have

(D_{h})^{3}\{W(l+1)^{3,ijk}_{s,t}-W(l)^{3,ijk}_{s,t}\}=6\{H(l+1)^{3,ijk}_{s,t}-H(l)^{3,ijk}_{s,t}\},\qquad h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}.

By applying
(D_{h})^{12m}
to
F^{3,ijk}_{l}
, we can obtain

\|H(l+1)^{3,ijk}-H(l)^{3,ijk}\|_{3\alpha,4m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{4m}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{12m}\kappa^{l},\qquad l\in{\mathbb{N}},

essentially in the same way as in the first level case.
Thus, the sequence
\{H(l)^{3}\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is Cauchy with respect to the
(3\alpha,4m)
-Besov topology.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume
(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1}<\alpha<H
and
let the notation be as above.
Then, we have

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
.
In particular,

\{S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h(l))\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to

S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(h)

in
G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

Proof.

For any such
\alpha
, we can find
(\alpha^{\prime},m)

which satisfies (
4.5
) and

\alpha^{\prime}-(12m)^{-1}>\alpha
.
Then, the corollary follows immediately from Lemma

and

) .
∎

For
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
, consider the following controlled ODE
in the Young sense:

d\psi^{h}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(\psi^{h}_{t})dh_{t}+V_{0}(\psi^{h}_{t})dt,\qquad\psi^{h}_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

(4.6)

This is called the skeleton ODE associated with RDE (
4.1
).
(Note that this makes sense at least in the
q
-variational setting
since
q<2
.)
We write
\psi^{h}:=\Psi_{a}(h)
.
Then,
\Psi_{a}\colon{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{q\textrm{-var}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
is

Corollary 4.3
.

(1) Under the assumption of Lemma
4.1
,

\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}(h)=\Psi_{a}(h)

for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

(2) Under the assumption of Corollary
4.2
,
we have
\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(h)=\Psi_{a}(h)
for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

Proof.

We prove (2) only because we can prove (1) in the same way.
Let
q\in[1,2)
be as in (
4.2
) and
take
\delta>0
so small that
q+\delta<2
.
Since
\lim_{l\to\infty}h(l)=h
in
(q+\delta)
-variation topology,

\lim_{l\to\infty}\Psi_{a}(h(l))=\Psi_{a}(h)
in
(q+\delta)
-variation topology.
On the other hand,
\Psi_{a}(h(l))=\Phi_{a}\circ S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h(l))\to\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(h)
as

by
Corollary

.
Thus, we have obtained
\Phi_{a}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(h)=\Psi_{a}(h)
.
∎

Now, let us recall the Young translation.
Suppose that
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2]
and
q\in[1,2)

satisfy
\alpha+q^{-1}>1
.
Then, there is a unique continuous map

\mathcal{T}\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{q\textrm{-var}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times G\Omega_{1/\alpha\textrm{-var}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to G\Omega_{1/\alpha\textrm{-var}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with the following property:

\mathcal{T}_{k}(S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(x))=S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(\tau_{k}(x)),\qquad x,k\in{\mathcal{C}}^{q\textrm{-var}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Here, we set
\tau_{k}(x):=x+k
.
This map
\mathcal{T}
is actually locally Lipschitz continuous.
We call

\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)\in G\Omega_{1/\alpha\textrm{-var}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the Young translation of
X\in G\Omega_{1/\alpha\textrm{-var}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by

k\in{\mathcal{C}}^{q\textrm{-var}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
As is well-known,
each level of
\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)
can be written as
an iterated Young integral.

In the next lemma, which will ve used in Section
6
,
we will check that the Young translation still works
even if
{\mathcal{C}}^{q\textrm{-var}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
G\Omega_{1/\alpha\textrm{-var}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
are replaced by

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
and
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
respectively.
Before providing a precise statement of the lemma,
we introduce another condition on
(\alpha,m)
:

\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{12m}\right)+\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)>1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\alpha+\frac{2}{12m}<H.

(4.7)

Suppose that
H\in(1/4,1/2]
is given.
If we take
\alpha
sufficiently close to
H
and
take
m
sufficiently large (for that
\alpha
),
then
(\alpha,m)
satisfies both
(
4.5
) and (
4.7
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\alpha,m)
satisfy both (
4.5
)
and (
4.7
).
Then, the Young translation
\mathcal{T}
maps

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\times G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

onto
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Moreover, this map is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

We only prove the level 3 case (i.e.
1/4<H\leq 1/3
) since
the level 2 case (i.e.
1/3<H\leq 1/2
) is much easier.
We write
\beta:=\alpha-(12m)^{-1}
.
In this proof,
C_{j}~(j=1,2,\ldots)
are positive constants
which depend only on parameters of (rough) path spaces.

Take
q
as in (
4.2
) close enough to
(H+\tfrac{1}{2})^{-1}

so that

\beta+\frac{1}{q}>1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(i-1)\beta+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}>i\alpha\quad(i=1,2,3)

holds.
Note that
the first inequality above is the sufficient condition for Young integration.
Let

X=(X^{1},X^{2},X^{3})\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
By (
2.5
),
X\in G\Omega_{\beta\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
So, at least in the variational setting,
\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)
is well-defined
for
X
and
k\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
For simplicity,
we write
K=(K^{1},K^{2},K^{3})
for
S_{3}(k)

and
x_{t}:=X^{1}_{0,t}
.

It follows immediately from (
4.3
) and

(1/q)-(1/2)>\alpha
that

\|\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)^{1}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}=\|X^{1}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}+\|K^{1}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}\leq\|X^{1}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}+C_{1}\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}<\infty.

Next, we first estimate the second level path. Recall that

\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)^{2}_{s,t}=X^{2}_{s,t}+\int_{s}^{t}X^{1}_{s,u}\otimes dk_{u}+\int_{s}^{t}K^{1}_{s,u}\otimes dx_{u}+K^{2}_{s,t},

where the second and third terms on the right hand side
are Young integrals.
By the general theory of Young integration, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}X^{1}_{s,u}\otimes dk_{u}\right|+\left|\int_{s}^{t}K^{1}_{s,u}\otimes dx_{u}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|x\|_{1/\beta\textrm{-var},[s,t]}\|k\|_{q\textrm{-var},[s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\|X^{1}\|_{\beta\textrm{-Hld}}\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}(t-s)^{\beta+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}

for every
X
,
k
and
(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}
.
Note that
the Hölder exponent on the right hand side is larger than
2\alpha
.
As we have seen in (
4.4
),

\|K^{2}\|_{(2\alpha+\delta)\textrm{-Hld}}\leq C_{4}\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{2}
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
Hence, we have shown

\|\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)^{2}-X^{2}\|_{(2\alpha+\delta)\textrm{-Hld}}<\infty
,
which implies

\|\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)^{2}\|_{2\alpha,6m\textrm{-Bes}}<\infty
since

\|X^{2}\|_{2\alpha,6m\textrm{-Bes}}<\infty
by assumption.

Next we will show
\|\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)^{3}\|_{3\alpha,4m\textrm{-Bes}}<\infty
. We can easily see that
\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)^{3}
is written as sum
of eight terms.
By assumption,
\|X^{3}\|_{3\alpha,4m\textrm{-Bes}}<\infty
.
By the same argument as for
K^{2}
, we have

\|K^{3}\|_{3\alpha,4m\textrm{-Bes}}<\infty
.
If we set

A_{s,t}:=\int_{s\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}\leq t}\Bigl(dk_{t_{1}}\otimes dk_{t_{2}}\otimes dx_{t_{3}}+dk_{t_{1}}\otimes dx_{t_{2}}\otimes dk_{t_{3}}+dx_{t_{1}}\otimes dk_{t_{2}}\otimes dk_{t_{3}}\Bigr),

then we see from the general theory of Young integration that

|A_{s,t}|\leq C_{5}\|x\|_{1/\beta\textrm{-var},[s,t]}\|k\|^{2}_{q\textrm{-var},[s,t]}\leq C_{6}\|X^{1}\|_{\beta\textrm{-Hld}}\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{2}(t-s)^{\beta+2(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}

for every
X
,
k
and
(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}
.
The Hölder exponent on the right hand side is larger than
3\alpha
.
Hence,
\|A\|_{3\alpha,4m\textrm{-Bes}}<\infty
.

Set

B_{s,t}:=\int_{s\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}\leq t}dx_{t_{1}}\otimes dk_{t_{2}}\otimes dx_{t_{3}},\qquad D_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}X^{2}_{s,u}\otimes dk_{u}.

Then, we can see in the same way as above that

|B_{s,t}|\leq C_{7}\|x\|^{2}_{1/\beta\textrm{-var},[s,t]}\|k\|_{q\textrm{-var},[s,t]}\leq C_{8}\|X^{1}\|^{2}_{\beta\textrm{-Hld}}\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}(t-s)^{2\beta+(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}

for every
X
,
k
and
(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}
.
Since the
1/\beta
-variation norm of
u\mapsto X^{2}_{s,u}
on
[s,t]

is dominated by
C_{9}(\|X^{1}\|^{2}_{1/\beta\textrm{-var},[s,t]}+\|X^{2}\|_{1/(2\beta)\textrm{-var},[s,t]})
, we can see that

|D_{s,t}|\leq C_{10}(\|X^{1}\|^{2}_{\beta\textrm{-Hld}}+\|X^{2}\|_{2\beta\textrm{-Hld}})\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}(t-s)^{2\beta+(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}

for every
X
,
k
and
(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}
.

Take an arbitrary
rational partition
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T\}
and fix it.
The projection

z\mapsto(z_{t_{1}},\ldots,z_{t_{N-1}},z_{t_{N}})

from
{\mathcal{Y}}:=\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
to
({\mathbb{R}}^{e})^{N}

is denoted by
\Sigma
.
A generic element of
({\mathbb{R}}^{e})^{N}
is denoted by

{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1},x_{N})
.
One can easily see from the definition of regular conditional probability that

\Sigma_{*}\mathbb{V}_{a,b}=(\Sigma_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{a})\left(\,\cdot\,\middle|x_{N}=b\right)\qquad\mbox{for almost all $b$.}

Hence, we have only to calculate the right hand side.

As is well-known,
the finite dimensional distribution
\Sigma_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{a}

on
({\mathbb{R}}^{e})^{N}
is given by

\Sigma_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{a}(d{\bf x})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}p(t_{i}-t_{i-1},x_{i-1},x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{N}dx_{i}.

Here and below, we write
x_{0}:=a
.
From this, one can easily see that

\displaystyle(\Sigma_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{a})\left(\,\cdot\,\middle|x_{N}=b\right)

\displaystyle=p(T,a,b)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}p(t_{i}-t_{i-1},x_{i-1},x_{i})p(t_{N}-t_{N-1},x_{i-1},b)\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}dx_{i}\delta_{b}(dx_{N})

for almost all
b
with
p(T,a,b)>0
.
(Note that
\{b\mid p(T,a,b)=0\}
is a zero set with respect to
the law of
y(T,a)
, which equals
p(T,a,b)db
, and is
therefore irrelevant.)
Therefore,
for such
b
,
(\Sigma_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{a})\left(\,\cdot\,\middle|x_{N}=b\right)
satisfies (
4.3
)
with
\mathbb{U}_{a,b}
being replaced by this probability measure.

Since there are countably many rational partitions,
we have
\mathbb{U}_{a,b}=\mathbb{V}_{a,b}
for
almost all
b
. This completes the proof.
∎

Under the same condition of the above proposition,
we have the following corollary, which is the main aim of this subsection.

Corollary 4.12
.

For every
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
with
p(T,a,b)>0
, we have
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}=\mathbb{U}_{a,b}
.

Proof.

We use Proposition
4.11
for every fixed
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
.
We already know that the set of
b
satisfying

\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}=\mathbb{U}_{a,b}
is a dense subset of the
open set
\{b\mid p(T,a,b)>0\}
. For such
b
, we have

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}(z_{t_{i}})\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}(dz)

\displaystyle=p(T,a,b)^{-1}\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{e})^{N-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}g_{i}(x_{i})g_{N}(b)\prod_{i=1}^{N}p(t_{i}-t_{i-1},x_{i-1},x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}dx_{i}

(4.15)

with
x_{0}:=a
and
x_{N}:=b

for every rational partition and
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset C_{{\rm K}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
.
It suffices to show that both sides of (
4.3
) are
continuous in
b
on
\{b\mid p(T,a,b)>0\}
.

The left hand side of (
4.3
) equals

p(T,a,b)^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}g_{i}(y_{t_{i}})\delta_{b}(y_{T})\right]\,g_{N}(b).

Since
b\mapsto\delta_{b}(y_{T})\in{\bf D}_{p,-2l}
is continuous
when
l\in N
is large enough, the left hand side is continuous in
b
.
Since the map
(a,b)\mapsto p(t,a,b)
is continuous for every
t\in(0,T]

and the supports of
g_{i}
’s are compact, we can see from
Lebesgue’s bounded convergence theorem that the right hand side of (
4.3
) is also continuous in
b
.
∎

5
Large deviations for fractional diffusion bridges

In this section we prove a small noise large deviation principle
for scaled fractional diffusion bridges under a suitable
ellipticity condition on the coefficient vector fields.
We continue to assume
H\in(1/4,1/2]
,

(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1}<\alpha<H

and that the coefficient vector fields

V_{j}
are of
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}

(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
.
We basically work on the abstract Wiener space
({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{H}}^{H},\mu^{H})

and let
\beta\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
C^{2}
-function.
Typical examples of
\beta
are

\beta_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
and
\beta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{1/H}
.
We set
\lambda_{t}:=t
and denote by
{\mathbb{R}}\langle\lambda\rangle

be a one-dimensional linear subspace of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}})

spanned by
\lambda
.

For a small parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
we consider the following scaled RDE:

dy^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dw^{j}_{t}+\beta_{\varepsilon}V_{0}(y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt,\qquad y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

(5.1)

This can be viewed as a “scaled version” of RDE (
4.1
).
We sometime write
y^{\varepsilon}_{t}=y^{\varepsilon}(t,a)
.
The corresponding skeleton ODE is given as follows:
For
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
,

d\hat{\psi}^{h}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(\hat{\psi}^{h}_{t})dh_{t}+\beta_{0}V_{0}(\hat{\psi}^{h}_{t})dt,\qquad\hat{\psi}^{h}_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

(5.2)

This is very similar to ODE (
4.6
)
(the drift vector field
V_{0}
is replaced by
\beta_{0}V_{0}
).
We will write
\hat{\psi}^{h}=:\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)
.

Now we introduce the following notation.
We assume
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2]
for a while.
First let us recall the Young pairing
{\mathcal{P}}\colon G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times{\mathcal{C}}^{1\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}})\to G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})
.
It is defined to be a unique continuous map such that

\mathcal{P}(S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}(x),k)=S_{\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor}((x,k)),\qquad x\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),k\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}).

We denote by

\hat{\Phi}_{a}\colon G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})\to\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})

the Lyons-Itô map associated with the coefficients
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
.
(Unlike the preceding sections,
\hat{\Phi}_{a}
is
defined on the geometric rough path space over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, not
over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Note that
\hat{\Phi}_{a}(\mathcal{P}(X,\lambda))=\Phi_{a}(X)
.)

Assume
(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1}<\alpha<H
again,
Using these symbols, the solution of (
5.1
) can be written as

y^{\varepsilon}=\hat{\Phi}_{a}(\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon W,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))

with
W=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(w)
.
Note also that
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)=\hat{\Phi}_{a}(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(h),\beta_{0}\lambda))

for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

We say that the coefficient vector fields
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

of RDE (
5.1
) and of ODE (
5.1
)
satisfies the ellipticity condition
at
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
if
\{V_{1}(z),\ldots,V_{d}(z)\}
linearly spans
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
.
(Note that
V_{0}
is actually irrelevant.)
We say that
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
satisfies
the ellipticity condition on an open subset
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

if it satisfies the ellipticity condition at every
z\in U
.

We sometimes write
\mathbf{V}(z):=[V_{1}(z)|\cdots|V_{d}(z)]
,
which is an
e\times d
-matrix valued function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
.
(In this definition,
V_{j}
’s are viewed as
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
-valued functions
in an obvious way.)
It is well-known that

\mathbf{V}(z)\mathbf{V}(z)^{\top}
is invertible if and only if

\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
satisfies the ellipticity condition at
z
,
where
A^{\top}
stands for the transposed matrix of a matrix
A
.

From now on, we assume that
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
satisfies
the ellipticity condition at the starting point
a

of RDE (
5.1
) and of ODE (
5.2
).
Then,
y^{\varepsilon}_{t}
is known to be non-degenerate in the sense of
Malliavin for every
0<t\leq T
(see Remark
4.10
).
Likewise, thanks to the ellipticity assumption,

\hat{\Psi}_{a}(\,\cdot\,)_{t}
is non-degenerate
at every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
for every
0<t\leq T
.
Recall that
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(\,\cdot\,)_{t}\colon{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
is called
non-degenerate at
h
if its Fréchet derivative

D\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{t}\colon{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
at
h
is a surjective linear map.
This is equivalent to the invertibility of
the deterministic Malliavin
covariance of
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(\,\cdot\,)_{t}
at
h
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
be a connected open subset
on which
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
satisfies the ellipticity condition
and assume
a,b\in U
.
Then, there exists a
C^{\infty}
-path
\varphi\colon[0,T]\to U

such that
\varphi_{0}=a
and
\varphi_{T}=b
.
Set
h_{0}=0
and

h^{\prime}_{t}:=\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})^{\top}\{\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})^{\top}\}^{-1}[\varphi^{\prime}_{t}-\beta_{0}V_{0}(\varphi_{t})],\qquad t\in[0,T].

Then,
h\colon[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is also
C^{\infty}
and
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)=\varphi
.
Indeed,

\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})h^{\prime}_{t}=\varphi^{\prime}_{t}-\beta_{0}V_{0}(\varphi_{t})
.
Note that
C^{\infty}
-paths starting at
0
belong to
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
(See
[
5
, Lemma 6.2]
for example.
This fact still holds even when
1/2<H<1
.)
Hence, there exists
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
such that

\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)
connects
a
and
b
and
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(\,\cdot\,)_{T}
is non-degenerate at
h
.
Likewise, setting
h(\varepsilon)^{\prime}_{0}=0
and

h(\varepsilon)^{\prime}_{t}:=\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})^{\top}\{\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})\mathbf{V}(\varphi_{t})^{\top}\}^{-1}[\varphi^{\prime}_{t}-\beta_{\varepsilon}V_{0}(\varphi_{t})],\qquad t\in[0,T],

for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we have

d\varphi_{t}=\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(\varphi_{t})dh(\varepsilon)^{\prime}_{t}+\beta_{\varepsilon}V_{0}(\varphi_{t})dt,\qquad\varphi_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

This is “
\varepsilon
-dependent” skeleton ODE corresponds to (
5.1
).
The solution map (at time
T
) of the above ODE
is also non-degenerate at every point of
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}

since the coefficients still satisfy
the ellipticity condition at the starting point
a
.

In this case, as we have seen in Remark
4.9
,

\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(t,a))
is a positive Watanabe distribution
with
p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b):={\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(t,a))]>0
.
We denote by
\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
the
Borel probability measure on
{\mathcal{W}}
corresponding to

p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)^{-1}\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(t,a))
.
We denote its rough path lift by
\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}:=(\varepsilon\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}})_{*}\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
.
(It is worth recalling that
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}

is invariant under the dilation by
\varepsilon

and
\varepsilon\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(w)=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(\varepsilon w)
for all

w\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
.
Of course, an analogous fact holds in the Besov case, too.)

Set
\tilde{I}\colon G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to[0,\infty]
by

\tilde{I}(X)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}&(\mbox{if $X=S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)$ for some
$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}),\\
\infty&(\mbox{otherwise}).\end{cases}

Note that, for every
r\in[0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\{X\in G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mid\tilde{I}(X)\leq r\}

\displaystyle=\{S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\leq r$}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\cap\{X\in G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mid\hat{\Phi}_{a}(\mathcal{P}(X,\beta_{0}\lambda))_{T}=b\}.

We can easily see from
and the Schilder type LDP for fractional Brownian rough path
and Lyons’ continuity theorem
that the right hand side is compact.
Hence,
\tilde{I}
is good.
We also introduce another good rate function

I\colon G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle I(X)

\displaystyle=\tilde{I}(X)-\min\{\tilde{I}(X)\mid X\in G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\}

\displaystyle=\tilde{I}(X)-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\}.

The minimum on the right hand side is actually attained
since
\tilde{I}
good. Hence, the minimum of
I
is
0
.

Of course, essentially the same objects can be defined
in the Besov case, too.
(They are denoted by the same symbols.)
Let
(\alpha,m)
satisfy (
4.5
).
Then, the lifted measure

\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}:=(\varepsilon\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}})_{*}\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}

is introduced on the Besov rough path space

G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, too.
The good rate functions
\tilde{I}
and
I
can also be
defined on
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

in the same way as in the Hölder case.

Now we provide our main large deviation results.
The speed of these large deviations is
1/\varepsilon^{2}
.
The key is proving large deviations for the family of unnormalized measures

\{p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

on the Besov-type geometric rough path space, which is quite hard
and will be postponed to the next section.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
H\in(1/4,1/2]
.
We assume that
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}
-vector fields

V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
satisfy the following condition:
There exists a connected open subset
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

such that
a,b\in U
and
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

satisfies the ellipticity condition on
U
.
Then, the following three statements hold:

(1)
The following Varadhan type asymptotics holds:

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)=-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\}.

(2)
For every
\alpha
with
(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1}<\alpha<H
,

\{\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
satisfies a
large deviation principle
on
G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-}\rm{Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function
I
.
Moreover,
\{\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is exponentially tight
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

(3)
For every
(\alpha,m)
satisfying (
4.5
)
and (
4.7
),

\{\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
satisfies a large deviation principle
on
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
with good rate function
I
.
Moreover,
\{\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is exponentially tight
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Proof.

By the Besov-Hödler embedding (
2.5
),
Assertion (2) immediately follows from Assertion (3).
So, we only explain (1) and (3).

The large deviation principle for the
unnormalized
measures

\{\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
will be shown
in (
6.1
) and (
6.10
) below,
where
\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}:=p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
.
The exponential tightness will be shown in (
6.8
).
Moreover,
since the whole set
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is both open and closed, (
6.1
) and (
6.10
)
also imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{\varepsilon}(G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

\displaystyle=-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\}.

Thus, we have shown Assertion (1) and (3).
∎

We denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
the law of
y^{\varepsilon}

conditioned that
y^{\varepsilon}_{T}=b
, that is,
the law of
y^{\varepsilon}=\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(\varepsilon W,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))

under
\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
.
Since
y^{\varepsilon}=\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(\varepsilon W,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))

and the law of
\varepsilon W
under
\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
equals
\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
,
we have

\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}=[\hat{\Phi}_{a}\circ{\mathcal{P}}]_{*}(\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\otimes\delta_{\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda})
.

With Theorem
5.1
at hand,
we can easily obtain a large deviation principle for
the laws
\{\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon(0,1]}

of (scaled) fractional diffusion bridges as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.
This is one of our main results in this paper.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
H\in(1/4,1/2]
.
We assume that
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}
-vector fields

V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
satisfy the following condition:
There exists a connected open subset
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

such that
a,b\in U
and
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

satisfies the ellipticity condition on
U
.

Then, for every
\alpha
with
(\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1)^{-1}<\alpha<H
,

\{\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

satisfies a large deviation principle
on
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function
J
, where we set

\displaystyle J(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\xi=\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)$}\},

\displaystyle\qquad-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\},\qquad\xi\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{e}).

As usual,
\inf\emptyset=\infty
by convention.

Proof.

It is almost obvious that, as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
,

\{\delta_{\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is exponentially tight and
satisfies a large deviation principle
on
{\mathbb{R}}\langle\lambda\rangle
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function
L
, where
L(s\lambda):=0
if
s=\beta_{0}

and
L(s\lambda):=\infty
if
s\neq\beta_{0}
.

By this fact and Theorem
5.1
(2),

\{\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\otimes\delta_{\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

satisfies a large deviation principle
on
G\Omega_{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times{\mathbb{R}}\langle\lambda\rangle
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function
K
defined by

K(X,s\lambda):=\hat{I}(X)+L(s\lambda)
.

Since
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
is the law
of
\nu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\otimes\delta_{\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda}
under the
continuous map (i.e. the composition of the Young pairing and
\hat{\Phi}_{a}
),

\{\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

satisfies a large deviation principle
on
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function.
(This is due to the contraction principle. See
[
9
, Theorem 4.2.1]

for example.)
The explicit form of the rate function follows of
the contraction principle and the fact that

\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)=\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h),\beta_{0}\lambda))
for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

(1) & (3)

In this section we prove Theorem

(1) and (3).
We set
\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}=p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
, which corresponds to the positive Watanabe distribution

Similarly, we set

.
We mainly calculate these unnormalized finite measures.

Throughout this section, we will work

(3).
We will often write
W:=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}(w)
for simplicity.
Recall that

.
Precisely, we understand the right hand side in the following way
(we write

for simplicity):

•

Though

\varepsilon W

-valued,

•

Therefore,

6.1
Large deviation lower bound

In this subsection we will show that

\varliminf_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(O)\geq-\inf_{X\in O}\tilde{I}(X)

(6.1)

holds for every open subset

O
of
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Denote by
\iota^{*}\colon{\mathcal{W}}^{*}\hookrightarrow({\mathcal{H}}^{H})^{*}\cong{\mathcal{H}}^{H}

be the adjoint of the inclusion
\iota\colon{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}
.
By the Riesz identification
({\mathcal{H}}^{H})^{*}\cong{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
,
we view
\iota^{*}({\mathcal{W}}^{*})
as a dense subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
More concretely,

\iota^{*}({\mathcal{W}}^{*})=\{h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\mid\mbox{$\langle h,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}$ extends to an element of ${\mathcal{W}}^{*}$}\}
.

Take any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
with
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b
.
Since
D\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}
is non-degenerate,
i.e. a surjective linear map from
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
,
there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\iota^{*}({\mathcal{W}}^{*})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|h-h_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0
and
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h_{n})_{T}=b
for
all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

(See
[
20

By (a special case of) Lemma

\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h_{n})=S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)

(\alpha,12m)
-Besov topology.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{X\in O}I(X)

\displaystyle=\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{
$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$ and $S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)\in O$}\}

\displaystyle=\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|k\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{
$k\in\iota^{*}({\mathcal{W}}^{*})$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(k)_{T}=b$ and $S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(k)\in O$}\}.

For simplicity,
we write
K:=S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(k)
and set

B_{r}(X):=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}\left\{\hat{X}\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\middle|\|\hat{X}^{i}-X^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i}<r\right\}

for

and
X\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Note that
\{B_{r}(X)\}_{r\in(0,\infty)}
forms a

X
.
Similarly, thanks to Lemma
4.4
,

\{\mathcal{T}_{k}B_{r}(\mathbf{0})\}_{r\in(0,\infty)}

fundamental system of neighborhood around
K
.
(Here,
\mathbf{0}
stands for the zero rough path
and
\mathcal{T}_{k}
for the Young translation by
k
.)

Therefore,
in order to obtain (

), it suffices to show that

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\varliminf_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k}B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))\geq-\frac{1}{2}\|k\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}

(6.2)

for every
k\in\iota^{*}({\mathcal{W}}^{*})
with
\hat{\Psi}_{a}(k)_{T}=b
.
We will prove this lower estimate in the rest of this subsection.

Take
\alpha^{\prime}\in(\alpha,H)
sufficiently close to
H
.
Since the
\alpha^{\prime}
-Hölder topology is stronger than
the
(\alpha,12m)
-Besov norm, we have

\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}
.
By Corollary
3.4
and Cameron-Martin theorem,

(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}})^{c}
and

(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}-k)^{c}=(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}})^{c}-k
are both slim
for every
k\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
Hence,

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
,

3
3

3

Precisely speaking, the right hand side should be the composition
of
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
and the inclusion map

G\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-Hld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

quasi-surely.
Moreover, if
k\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
,

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(\,\cdot\,+k)=\mathcal{T}_{k}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
, quasi-surely.
(Consequently,

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}(\,\cdot\,+k)=\mathcal{T}_{k}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}

Indeed, if
w\in(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}})\cap(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}-k)
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(w+k)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}((w+k)(l))_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\mathcal{T}_{k(l)}S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(w(l))_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{k}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}(w)_{s,t},\qquad\qquad(s,t)\in\triangle_{T}.

The first equality is due to

.
The last equality is due to the Young translation
in the variation topologies because
(i)
w\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}
,
(ii)
\lim_{l\to\infty}\|k(l)-k\|_{q\textrm{-var}}=0
for
every

,
(iii) (we may assme that)
\alpha^{\prime}+1/q>1
.

Let
\chi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
be an even smooth function
with compact support such that

\chi(x)=1
if
|x|\leq 1/2
and
\chi(x)=0
if
|x|\geq 1
.
We write
\chi_{r}(x)=\chi(x/r)
for
r\in(0,\infty)
.
Clearly,

\mathbf{1}_{B_{r}(\mathbf{0})}(X)\geq\prod_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}\chi_{r}(\|X^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i}),\qquad X\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

For simplicity, we write
y^{\varepsilon}(t,a)=y^{\varepsilon}_{t}
.
We also write
W:=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}(w)
,
which is
\infty
-quasi-continuous in
w
as we have seen in Theorem
3.3
.

By Sugita’s theorem and the Cameron-Martin theorem
for the translation by
k/\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k}B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\left(\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\mid\varepsilon W\in\mathcal{T}_{k}B_{r}(\mathbf{0})\}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{W}}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{r}(\mathbf{0})}(\mathcal{T}_{-k}(\varepsilon W))\,\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(dw)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathcal{W}}}\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|\mathcal{T}_{-k}(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})\,\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(dw)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|\mathcal{T}_{-k}(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})\,\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})\,\delta_{b}(\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{e}}\delta_{0}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}-b}{\varepsilon}\right)\right].

(6.3)

Here, we set
\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}:=y^{\varepsilon}_{T}(\,\cdot\,+k/\varepsilon)
.
Note that if

\|\varepsilon W^{1}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m}\leq r
,
then

|\langle k,w\rangle/\varepsilon|\leq\|\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle\|_{{\mathcal{W}}^{*}}\|w\|_{{\mathcal{W}}}/\varepsilon\leq c\|\langle k,\,\cdot\,\rangle\|_{{\mathcal{W}}^{*}}\|W^{1}\|_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-Bes}}/\varepsilon\leq cr^{1/(12m)}/\varepsilon^{2}

for some constant
c>0
which depends only on
(\alpha,12m)
and
k
.
Applying Sugita’s theorem to the right hand side of (
6.1
),
we can easily see that

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k}B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{cr^{1/(12m)}}{\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{\|k\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{e}}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})\,\delta_{0}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}-b}{\varepsilon}\right)\right].

Therefore, in order to obtain (
6.2
), it suffices to show that

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})\,\delta_{0}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}-b}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\,\,\in(0,\infty)

(6.4)

for every fixed
r>0
.

First, we can easily see that as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\|(\varepsilon W)^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})=\prod_{i}\chi_{r}(\varepsilon^{12m}\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i})=1+O(\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{in ${\bf D}_{\infty}$}.

(6.5)

Next, it is known that

(\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}-b)/\varepsilon=\{y^{\varepsilon}_{T}(\,\cdot\,+k/\varepsilon)-\hat{\Psi}_{a}(k)_{T}\}/\varepsilon
is uniformly non-degenerate
in the sense of Malliavin.
(See
[
26
, Proposition 5.4]
.
Since we work in the elliptic setting, a direct proof is
not difficult in this case, however.)
It is also known that there exists

\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k)\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})

such that

y^{\varepsilon}_{T}(\,\cdot\,+k/\varepsilon)-\hat{\Psi}_{a}(k)_{T}=\varepsilon\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k)+O(\varepsilon^{2})\quad\mbox{in ${\bf D}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})$}

(6.6)

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.
(This is a consequence of the Taylor expansion of Lyons-Itô map.

\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k)
is the term of order 1 in the expansion.
This kind of expansion was already used in
[
26
]
, too.)
Moreover,
w\mapsto\zeta_{T}(w;k)
is a Gaussian random variable whose covariance matrix
coincides with the deterministic Malliavin covariance matrix of

h\mapsto\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}
at
k
, which is non-degenerate due to the
ellipticity assumption. (The mean may not vanish.)
Therefore the density function of
the law of
\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k)
is strictly positive
everywhere, in particular,
\mathbb{E}[\delta_{0}(\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k))]>0
.

Finally, we apply (a very special case of) the asymptotic theory for Watanabe distributions (see
[
17
, Section V-9]
).
By (
6.6
) and the uniform non-degeneracy, we have

\delta_{0}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}-b}{\varepsilon}\right)=\delta_{0}(\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k))+O(\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{in ${\bf D}_{-\infty}$}

(6.7)

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.
Combining this with (
6.5
), we see that the left hand side of
(
6.4
) equals
\mathbb{E}[\delta_{0}(\zeta_{T}(\,\cdot\,;k))]\in(0,\infty)
.
Thus, we have verified (
6.4
) and therefore
finished the proof of (
6.1
).

6.2
Exponential tightness

In this subsection we show that, under the condition of
Theorem
5.1
(3),
\{\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

is exponentially tight on

G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, that is,
for every
R\in[1,\infty)
there exists a precompact subset

K_{R}
of
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

satisfying that

\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(K_{R}^{c})\leq-R.

(6.8)

The key to prove this fact is that
the capacities of the complement of large
“homogeneous balls” admit Gaussian decay.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
q\in(1,\infty)
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exist positive constants
c
and
c^{\prime}
satisfying that

{\rm Cap}_{q,k}\left(\left\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\,\middle|\,\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq R\right\}\right)\leq c^{\prime}e^{-cR^{2}},\qquad 1\leq i\leq\lfloor 1/H\rfloor,1\leq R<\infty.

Here,
c
and
c^{\prime}
may depend on
q,k,\alpha,m
,
but not on
R
.

Proof.

From a Fernique type theorem for fractional Brownian rough path
with respect to
the Hölder rough path topology (see
[
11
, Theorem 15.33]
),
we can easily see that
there exist constants
c_{1},c_{1}^{\prime}>0
independent of
R
such that

\mu^{H}\left(\left\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\,\middle|\,\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq R\right\}\right)\leq c_{1}^{\prime}e^{-c_{1}R^{2}},\quad 1\leq i\leq\lfloor 1/H\rfloor,1\leq R<\infty.

Take a non-negative, non-decreasing, smooth function

\chi
such that

\chi\equiv 0
on
(-\infty,0]
and
\chi\equiv 1
on
[1,\infty)
.
All derivatives of
\chi
are bounded.
Set
G(w)=\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{12m/i}
.
Then,
G\in{\bf D}_{\infty}
and
\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)

is
\infty
-quasi-continuous.
Moreover,
\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)\geq 1
holds
on
the subset
\{\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq R\}
.
By
[
29
, P. 106, Theorem 4.4]
, we have

{\rm Cap}_{q,k}\bigl(\{\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq R\}\bigr)\leq\|\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,k}}.

Therefore, it suffices to estimate the Sobolev norm on the right hand side.

First we calculate the
L^{q}
-norm.
Since there exists a constant
r_{0}\geq 2
such that

R\geq r_{0}
implies
(R^{12m}-1)^{1/12m}\geq R/2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{H}\bigl(\{\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq(R^{12m}-1)^{1/12m}\}\bigr)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{H}\bigl(\{\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq R/2\}\bigr)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq(c_{1}^{\prime})^{1/q}e^{-(c_{1}/4q)R^{2}}

for all
R\geq r_{0}
.
By adjusting positive constants, we can easily show
that, for some constants
c_{2},c_{2}^{\prime}>0
,

\|\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)\|_{L^{q}}\leq c_{2}^{\prime}e^{-c_{2}R^{2}}
holds for all
R\geq 1
.

Since

D[\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)]=\chi^{\prime}(G-R^{12m}+1)DG
, we have

\displaystyle\|D[\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\bigl\|I_{\{G-R^{12m}+1\geq 0\}}\|DG\|_{{\cal H}^{H}}\bigr\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|DG\|_{L^{2q}}\mu^{H}\bigl(\{\|W^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}\geq(R^{12m}-1)^{1/12m}\}\bigr)^{1/2q}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|DG\|_{L^{2q}}\cdot(c_{1}^{\prime})^{1/2q}e^{-(c_{1}/8q)R^{2}}

for all
R\geq r_{0}
.
Hence, there are constants
c_{3},c_{3}^{\prime}>0
such that

\|D[\chi(G-R^{12m}+1)]\|_{L^{q}}\leq c_{3}^{\prime}e^{-c_{3}R^{2}}
holds for all
R\geq 1
.
From this and Meyer’s equivalence of the Sobolev norms,
there exists
c_{4},c_{4}^{\prime}>0
such that

\|\chi(G-R^{4m}+1)\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,1}}\leq c_{4}^{\prime}e^{-c_{4}R^{2}}

for all
R\geq 1
.

Repeating essentially the same argument,
we can estimate
D^{l}[\chi(G-R^{4m}+1)]
for any

l~(1\leq l\leq k)
to obtain the Gaussian decay of

\|\chi(G-R^{4m}+1)\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,k}}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists a constant
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
independent of
q
and
\varepsilon

such that

\|\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(T,a))\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,-2e}}=O(\varepsilon^{-\ell})\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\searrow 0$.}

for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

In this proof,
\ell_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
are certain constants
independent of
(q,\varepsilon)

and
c_{j}>0
are certain constants independent of
\varepsilon
(
j=1,2,\ldots
).
For simplicity, we denote by
\sigma_{\varepsilon}

the Malliavin covariance matrix of
y^{\varepsilon}(T,a):=y^{\varepsilon}_{T}
.
We also write
\gamma_{\varepsilon}:=(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{-1}
.

It is well-known that every
\|y^{\varepsilon}_{T}\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,k}}

is bounded in
\varepsilon
for every
(q,k)\in(1,\infty)\times{\mathbb{N}}
.
As was explained in Subsection
6.1
,

\{y^{\varepsilon}_{T}/\varepsilon\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is uniformly non-degenerate
in the sense of Malliavin calculus, which implies that

\|(\det\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\|_{L^{q}}=O(\varepsilon^{-2e})

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Hence, for some
\ell_{1}
, we have

\|\gamma_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}=O(\varepsilon^{-\ell_{1}})

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Set
A(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{e}\{(z^{j}-b^{j})\vee 0\}

for
z=(z^{1},\ldots,z^{e})
.
Then, we have

\partial_{1}^{2}\cdots\partial_{e}^{2}A=\delta_{b}

in the distributional sense.
Now we apply the multiple version of the
by parts formula (
2.12
) in Remark
2.1

with
F=y^{\varepsilon}_{T}
,
T=A
and
\mathbf{i}=(1,1,2,2,\ldots,e,e)
.
(see Remark
2.1
for the notation) to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}_{T})\,G\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[A(y^{\varepsilon}_{T})\,\Phi_{(1,1,\ldots,e,e)}(\,\cdot\,;G)\bigr],\qquad G\in{\mathbf{D}}_{\infty}.

It is easy to see that
\|A(y^{\varepsilon}_{T})\|_{L^{r}}
is bounded in
\varepsilon
for every
r\in(1,\infty)
.
For a given
q
, denote its conjugate exponent by
p
, i.e.
1/q+1/p=1
.
Then, there exists
\ell_{2}
and
c_{1}
such that

\|\Phi_{(1,1,\ldots,e,e)}(\,\cdot\,;G)\|_{L^{(p+1)/2}}\leq c_{1}\|G\|_{\mathbf{D}_{p,2e}}\varepsilon^{-\ell_{2}},\qquad G\in{\mathbf{D}}_{\infty},\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Combining these all, we have

\left|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}_{T})\,G\bigr]\right|\leq c_{2}\|G\|_{\mathbf{D}_{p,2e}}\varepsilon^{-\ell_{2}},\qquad G\in{\mathbf{D}}_{\infty},\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Since
{\mathbf{D}}_{\infty}
is dense in
\mathbf{D}_{p,2e}
,
this estimate proves the lemma.
∎

Now we show the exponential tightness (
6.8
).
For
R\geq 1
, set

K_{R}:=\left\{X\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\middle|\|X^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}^{1/i}<R\,\,\mbox{for all $i=1,\ldots,\lfloor 1/H\rfloor$}\right\}.

Clearly, this is a bounded subset.
By (
2.10
), Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(K_{R}^{c})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\mid\varepsilon W\in K_{R}^{c}\})

\displaystyle\leq\|\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(T,a))\|_{\mathbf{D}_{2,-2e}}{\rm Cap}_{2,2e}(\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\mid\varepsilon W\in K_{R}^{c}\})

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-\ell}e^{-cR^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1],R\geq 1.

Here,
c,c^{\prime},\ell
are positive constants
independent of
R
and
\varepsilon
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{\varepsilon}(K_{R}^{c})\leq-cR^{2}.

(6.9)

Since
cR^{2}
can be arbitrarily large, we have shown
(
6.8
) except the relative compactness of
K_{R}
.

For the relative compactness, we take
\alpha^{\prime}\in(\alpha,H)
.
When
\alpha^{\prime}
is sufficiently close to
\alpha
,

(\alpha^{\prime},12m)
still satisfies the assumption of
Theorem
5.1
(3) and the embedding

G\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime},12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is compact (i.e.
every bounded subset of the left set is a relatively compact
subset of the right set).
Therefore, using (
6.9
) for the

(\alpha^{\prime},12m)
-Besov topology finishes the proof of
the exponential tightness (
6.8
).

6.3
Large deviation upper bound

In this subsection we will show that

\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(F)\leq-\inf_{X\in F}\tilde{I}(X)

(6.10)

holds for every closed subset

F
of
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

In view of the exponential tightness (
6.8
),
the upper estimate
(
6.10
) immediately follows from the following inequality:

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(B_{r}(X))\leq-\tilde{I}(X)

(6.11)

for every
X\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
We will show (
6.11
) in Lemmas
6.3

and
6.4
below.

Now let us prove (
6.11
).

Lemma 6.3
.

If
\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(X,\beta_{0}\lambda))_{T}\neq b
,
then (
6.11
) holds.

Proof.

First, note that
since
w\mapsto\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(\varepsilon W,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))_{T}
is

\infty
-quasi continuous,

\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
is concentrated on the subset

\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}\mid\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(\varepsilon W,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))_{T}=b\}

(see Lemma
4.6
and its proof).
By the continuity, there exists
\delta,r>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
if
\hat{X}\in B_{r}(X)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, then

|\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(\hat{X},\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))_{T}-b|\geq\delta
.
Therefore, we have

\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(B_{r}(X))=\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon W\in B_{r}(X)\right)=0

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
This implies (
6.11
) since
\tilde{I}(X)=\infty
.
∎

Before proving the other case
\hat{\Phi}_{a}({\mathcal{P}}(X,\beta_{0}\lambda))=b
,
let us recall a Schilder type large deviation principle
for fractional Brownian rough path with respect to the

\alpha^{\prime}
-Hölder topology for every
\alpha^{\prime}<H
.
(See
[
11
, Theorem 15.55]
, in which
the expression of the rate function seems to contain a minor typo.)
By the embedding in (
2.5
),
the same large deviation principle still holds on
G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Due to the general fact in the large deviation theory
(see
[
9
, Theorem 4.1.18]
for instance), we have

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\mu^{H}(\{\varepsilon W\in B_{r}(X)\})\leq-I_{{\rm Sch}}(X)

(6.12)

for every
X\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
where we set

I_{{\rm Sch}}(X):=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}&(\mbox{if $X=S_{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(h)$ for some
$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$}),\\
\infty&(\mbox{otherwise}).\end{cases}

Lemma 6.4
.

It holds that

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(B_{r}(X))\leq-I_{{\rm Sch}}(X)

for every
X\in G\Omega_{\alpha,12m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

Take a non-negative, smooth, even function

\varphi
such that

\varphi\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
and
\varphi\equiv 0
on
[2,\infty)
.
Clearly,
\varphi
is compactly supported.
If we set

F^{\varepsilon}_{r}(w)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}\varphi\left(\|(\varepsilon W)^{i}-X^{i}\|_{i\alpha,12m/i\textrm{-Bes}}^{12m/i}/r\right),

then
F^{\varepsilon}_{r}\in{\bf D}_{\infty}
,
F^{\varepsilon}_{r}\geq 0
and

F^{\varepsilon}_{r}\equiv 1
on
B_{r}(X)
.
Moreover,
F^{\varepsilon}_{r}
is
\infty
-quasi-continuous in
w
.
For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1<p<\tilde{p}<\infty
,
there exist
positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}

which may depend on
k,p,\tilde{p},r
but not on
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle\|F^{\varepsilon}_{r}\|_{\mathbf{D}_{p,k}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|D^{j}F^{\varepsilon}_{r}\|_{L^{p}}\leq c_{2}\mu^{H}\left(\{\varepsilon W\in B_{2r}(X)\}\right)^{1/\tilde{p}}.

Using this, Sugita’s theorem and Lemma
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(B_{r}(X))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\left(\{\varepsilon W\in B_{r}(X)\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathcal{W}}}F^{\varepsilon}_{r}(w)\,\tilde{\mu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(dw)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}_{T})\,F^{\varepsilon}_{r}]

\displaystyle\leq\|\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(T,a))\|_{\mathbf{D}_{q,-2e}}\|F^{\varepsilon}_{r}\|_{\mathbf{D}_{p,2e}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\varepsilon^{-\ell}\mu^{H}\left(\{\varepsilon W\in B_{2r}(X)\}\right)^{1/\tilde{p}},

where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
and
\ell,c_{3}
are certain positive constants
independent of
\varepsilon
.
This inequality and (
6.12
) immediately imply that

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\tilde{\nu}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}(B_{r}(X))\leq-\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}I_{{\rm Sch}}(X).

Setting
\tilde{p}=2p-1
and then letting
p\searrow 1
,
we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

7
The Young case:
H>1/2

In this section we assume
H\in(1/2,1)
.
Denote by
\mu^{H}
the law of

d
-dimensional FBM with Hurst parameter
H
,
which is a Gaussian measure on

\mathcal{W}=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with
\alpha\in(1/2,H)
.
The coordinate process
w=(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
on
{\mathcal{W}}

is a canonical realization of FBM under
\mu^{H}
.
The Cameron-Martin space of
\mu^{H}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.
As is well-known,
({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{H}}^{H},\mu^{H})
is an abstract Wiener space.
Throughout this section
V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)

are assumed to be
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}
-vector fields on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
.

We consider the Young differential equation (YDE)
driven by FBM
w=(w_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
:

dy_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y_{t})dw^{j}_{t}+V_{0}(y_{t})dt,\qquad y_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

(7.1)

Under our condition on the coefficients,
this YDE is deterministic and has a unique solution for every fixed
w
.
We write
y=\Phi_{a}(w)
and view
\Phi
as a map from

\mathcal{W}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
.
It is known that
\Phi
is Fréchet-
C^{\infty}
(see
[
19
]
for example).
We denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}
the law of this process
y
, that is,

\mathbb{Q}_{a}^{H}:=(\Phi_{a})_{*}\mu^{H}
.
Since
\Phi_{a}
takes values in the “little
\alpha
-Hödler space”

\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
,
this Borel probability measure sits on
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
.

Under the
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}
-condition on the coefficients,

y_{t}\in{\bf D}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
for every
t\in[0,T]
.
(See
[
19
]
. See also
[
4
]
for the driftless case.
These proofs are different.)
Suppose that
y_{T}=y(T,a)
is non-degenerate
in the sense of Malliavin and
that
p(T,a,b):={\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{b}(y(T,a))]>0
.
In this case, a unique
Borel probability measure on
{\mathcal{W}}
corresponding to

p(T,a,b)^{-1}\delta_{b}(y(t,a))
is denoted by
\mu^{H}_{a,b}
.
We then set

\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{a,b}:=(\Phi_{a})_{*}\circ\mu^{H}_{a,b}
.
(When
p(T,a,b)=0
, we set
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{a,b}:=\delta_{\ell(a,b)}
,
where
\ell(a,b)_{t}:=a+(b-a)(t/T)
. )

As in the rough path case (i.e.
1/4<H\leq 1/2
),
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{a,b}
can be
regarded as (the law of) a bridge process.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
1/2<\alpha<H<1
.
Assume that

y_{T}=y(T,a)
is non-degenerate in the sense of Malliavin.
Then, the family

\{\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{a,b}(A)\mid A\in\mathcal{B}(\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})),\,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}\}

is (a version of) the regular conditional probability
of
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{a}
on

\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})

given the evaluation map at the time
T
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is much easier than that of Theorem
4.7
since the rough path lift map is not involved in the Young case.
So, we omit it.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Assume that
y_{T}=y(T,a)
is non-degenerate in the sense of Malliavin.
As in the case
H\in(1/4,1/2]
,

p(T,a,b)>0
if and only if there exists
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}

satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

\Phi_{a}(h)_{T}=b

(2)

D\Phi_{a}(h)_{T}
is a surjective linear map from

{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
.
Here,
D\Phi_{a}(h)_{T}
stands for the Fréchet derivative of

k\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\mapsto D\Phi_{a}(k)_{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
at
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

This equivalence can be checked as follows.
The map
w\mapsto\Phi_{a}(w)
is known to be
Fréchet-
C^{3}
whose derivatives of order
k~(0\leq k\leq 3)

are of at most polynomial growth, which
implies that this map is twice
{\mathcal{K}}
-regularly differentiable in
the sense of Aida-Kusuoka-Stroock
[
2
]
.
Then, the above equivalence is a special case of
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
.

In the following remark, we recall some results on the
non-degeneracy of the solution
(y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
of RDE (
3.6
).
In almost all problems concerning stochastic equations
driven by FBM,
the case
H>1/2
is much easier than the case
H\leq 1/2
.
On the Malliavin non-degeneracy, however,
the case
H>1/2
might be less known than the case
1/4<H\leq 1/2
.

Remark 7.3
.

Let
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
be the coefficient vector fields of YDE (
7.1
).
Here, they are viewed as first-order differential operators on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
.
We provide two known examples of sufficient condition
for
y_{t}=y(t,a)
to be non-degenerate in the sense of Malliavin
for all
t\in(0,T]
.

1.

\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
satisfies the ellipticity condition at
the starting point
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
(see
[
19
]
).

2.

V_{0}\equiv 0
and
\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq d}
satisfies Hörmander’s
bracket-generating condition at the starting point
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
(see
[
4
]
).

As far as the author knows, there is no published paper which proves
the Malliavin non-degeneracy
under the same bracket-generating condition on
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

as in Remark
4.10
.
However, this issue seems to have been solved by
a recent unpublished work
[
33
]
.

Now we turn to the large deviation principle.
For a small parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
we consider the following scaled YDE:

dy^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dw^{j}_{t}+\beta_{\varepsilon}V_{0}(y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt,\qquad y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}.

(7.2)

We sometime write
y^{\varepsilon}_{t}=y^{\varepsilon}(t,a)
.
Here,
\beta\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
{\rm Lip}^{\gamma}
-function for some
\gamma>1
.
(See Remark
5.3
.)
Typical examples of
\beta
are

\beta_{\varepsilon}=1
and
\beta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{1/H}
.

We denote by

\hat{\Phi}_{a}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})\to\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-Hld}}_{a}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})

the Young-Itô map (i.e. the solution map of YDE)
associated with the coefficients
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}
,
where
1/2<\alpha<H
.
Then,
y^{\varepsilon}=\hat{\Phi}_{a}((\varepsilon w,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))
.
The associated skeleton map is defined by

\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h):=\hat{\Phi}_{a}((h,\beta_{0}\lambda))
for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}
.

We assume that the coeffiecient vector fields

V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
are of
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}

satisfying the following condition:
There exists a connected open subset
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

such that
a,b\in U
and
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

satisfies the ellipticity condition on
U
.
Then, as we have seen in Remark
7.2
,

\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(t,a))
is a positive Watanabe distribution
with
p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b):={\mathbb{E}}[\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(t,a))]>0
.
We denote by
\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
the
Borel probability measure on
{\mathcal{W}}
corresponding to

p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)^{-1}\delta_{b}(y^{\varepsilon}(t,a))
.

Now we provide our main large deviation results in the Young case.
The speed of these large deviations is
1/\varepsilon^{2}
.
In this setting, the rate functions are defined by

\tilde{I}(w)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}\|w\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}&(\mbox{if $w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(w)_{T}=b$}),\\
\infty&(\mbox{otherwise}).\end{cases}

and

I(w)=\tilde{I}(w)-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\}.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
H\in(1/2,1)
.
We assume that
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}
-vector fields

V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
satisfy the following condition:
There exists a connected open subset
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

such that
a,b\in U
and
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

satisfies the ellipticity condition on
U
.
Then, the following two statements hold:

(1)
The following Varadhan-type asymptotics holds:

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log p^{\varepsilon}(T,a,b)=-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\}.

(2)
For every
\alpha\in(1/2,H)
,

\{\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
satisfies a
large deviation principle
on
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function
I
.
Moreover,
\{\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is exponentially tight
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

(3)
For every
(\alpha,m)\in(1/2,H)\times{\mathbb{N}}
with

\alpha-(2m)^{-1}>1/2
,

\{\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
satisfies a large deviation principle
on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,2m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
with good rate function
I
.
Moreover,
\{\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is exponentially tight
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Proof.

Due to the continuous embedding

{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,2m\textrm{-}{\rm Bes}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with

\gamma:=\alpha-(2m)^{-1}
, Assertion (2) immediately follows from
Assertion (3).

The proofs of Assertion (1) and (3) are essentially the same as
(actually much easier than) those of Theorem
5.1
(1) and (3). So we omit details. (See also Remark
5.3
)
∎

We denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
the law of
y^{\varepsilon}

conditioned that
y^{\varepsilon}_{T}=b
, that is,
the law of
y^{\varepsilon}=\hat{\Phi}_{a}((\varepsilon w,\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda))

under
\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}
.
Then, we have

\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}=[\hat{\Phi}_{a}]_{*}(\mu_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\otimes\delta_{\beta_{\varepsilon}\lambda})
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
H\in(1/2,1)
.
We assume that
C_{{\rm b}}^{\infty}
-vector fields

V_{j}~(j\in\llbracket 0,d\rrbracket)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
satisfy the following condition:
There exists a connected open subset
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}

such that
a,b\in U
and
\{V_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq d}

satisfies the ellipticity condition on
U
.

Then, for every
\alpha\in(1/2,H)
,

\{\mathbb{Q}_{a,b}^{H,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

satisfies a large deviation principle
on
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{e})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

with good rate function
J
, which is defined by

\displaystyle J(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\xi=\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)$}\},

\displaystyle\qquad-\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{H}}\mid\mbox{$h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{H}$ with $\hat{\Psi}_{a}(h)_{T}=b$}\},\qquad\xi\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\alpha\textrm{-}{\rm Hld}}_{a,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{e}).

As usual,
\inf\emptyset=\infty
by convention.

Proof.

Since the Young-Itô map
\hat{\Phi}_{a}
is continuous,
we can show this corollary from Theorem
7.4
(2)
in the same way as in Corollary
5.2
.
So, we omit details.
∎

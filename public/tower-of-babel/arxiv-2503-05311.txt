Upper tail bounds for irregular graphs
Anirban Basak, Shaibal Karmakar

Abstract
. We consider the upper tail large deviations of subgraph counts for irregular graphs H in 𝔾 ⁡ ( n , p ) \mathbb{G}(n,p) , the sparse Erdős-Rényi graph on n n vertices with edge connectivity probability p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) . For n − 1 / Δ ≪ p ≪ 1 n^{-1/\Delta}\ll p\ll 1 , where Δ \Delta is the maximum degree of H , we derive the upper tail large deviations for any irregular graph H . On the other hand, we show that for p p such that 1 ≪ n v H ​ p e H ≪ ( log ⁡ n ) α H ∗ / ( α H ∗ − 1 ) 1\ll n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\ll(\log n)^{\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}/\left(\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}-1\right)} , where v H v_{\textup{H}} and e H e_{\textup{H}} denote the number of vertices and edges of H , and α H ∗ \alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}} denotes the fractional independence number, the upper tail large deviations of the number of unlabelled copies of H in 𝔾 ⁡ ( n , p ) \mathbb{G}(n,p) is given by that of a sequence of Poisson random variables with diverging mean, for any strictly balanced graph H . Restricting to the r r -armed star graph we further prove a localized behavior in the intermediate range of p p (left open by the above two results) and show that the mean-field approximation is asymptotically tight for the logarithm of the upper tail probability. This work further identifies the typical structures of 𝔾 ⁡ ( n , p ) \mathbb{G}(n,p) conditioned on upper tail rare events in the localized regime.



1.
Introduction and main results

The classical large deviation theory traditionally deals with large deviations of linear functions of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. Probably the simplest non-trivial problem that falls outside realm of the classical theory is the large deviations of triangle counts in an Erdős-Rényi random graph, to be denoted by
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
, with vertex set
[\![n]\!]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and edge connectivity probability
p=p_{n}\in(0,1)
, where each pair of vertices is connected with a probability
p
independently of every other pair. In the last fifteen years there have been immense interest in studying large deviations of subgraph (in particular triangle) counts in Erdős-Rényi random graphs, both in the dense regime, i.e.
p\asymp 1
(see the monograph
[
10
]
and the references therein), and in the sparse regime, i.e.
p\ll 1
, e.g. see
[
3
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
(we refer the reader to Section
1.6
for all the notation).

Recently Harel, Mousset, and Samotij
[
16
]
showed that the speed and the rate function of upper tail of cliques (i.e. complete subgraphs) on
r
vertices in
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
undergoes a transition at the threshold
p^{({r-1})/{2}}\asymp n^{-1}(\log n)^{{1}/({r-2})}
. For
p^{({r-1})/{2}}\ll n^{-1}(\log n)^{{1}/({r-2})}
(and
p^{(r-1)/2}\gg n^{-1}
so that the expected number of cliques on
r
vertices diverges) the speed and the rate function are given by those for a sequence of Poisson random variables with diverging means, and thus that regime of
p
is rightly termed as the
Poisson regime
. In contrast, for
n^{-1}(\log n)^{{1}/({r-2})}\ll p^{({r-1})/{2}}\ll 1
the large deviation event is primarily due to the presence of some localized structures (of microscopic size) in the graph, and therefore that regime is termed as the
localized regime
.

It was conjectured in (an earlier version of) the work
[
16
]
that the transition between the localized and the Poisson regimes, for the upper tail of
all
connected regular subgraph counts, should occur at the threshold
p^{\Delta/2}\asymp n^{-1}(\log n)^{1/(v_{\textup{H}}-2)}
, where
\Delta
is the common degree of the regular graph
H
in context and
v_{\textup{H}}
denotes the number of vertices in
H
. In
[
16
]
the Poisson behavior was proved in the predicted Poisson regime, and the localized behavior for
p
such that
n^{-1/2-o(1)}\leq p^{\Delta/2}\ll 1
(and under the additional assumption that
H
is non bipartite in the extended range
n^{-1}(\log n)^{\Delta v_{\textup{H}}^{2}}\ll p\ll 1
).
The more recent work
[
6
]
extends the localized behavior for
all
regular graph in the sub regime left open by
[
16
]
. Therefore,
[
16
]
and
[
6
]
together settle the problem of upper tail large deviations of regular subgraph counts in sparse Erdős-Rényi graphs (albeit a couple of boundary cases). Let us also mention in passing that the speed and the rate function in the localized regime turns out to be the solution of an appropriate mean field variational problem. See Section
1.4
below for a further discussion on this.

In this short article we investigate whether for the upper tail of irregular subgraph (i.e. there exists at least two vertices with unequal degrees) counts in sparse Erdős-Rényi graphs a transition threshold between the localized and the Poisson regimes exists, and whether in the localized regime the large deviation probability can be expressed as the solution of some mean field variational problem (similar to the one mentioned in the regular setting).

1.1.
Localized regime I

We split the localized regime in two sub regimes. The rationale is that in the first sub regime the optimizer for the associated variational problem is a ‘planted’ one, namely the ‘planted hub’ in the irregular case. In contrast, in the second sub regime the optimizer is predicted to be ‘non-planted’ (meaning it can take value outside
\{p,1\}
; the rationale behind the nomenclature will be clear in Section
1.4
), as evidenced through Theorem
1.9
. For a graph
H
we write
e_{\textup{H}}
to denote the number of its edges. We use the notation
N(\textup{H},\textup{G})
to denote the number of
labelled
copies of
H
in
G
. In the graph theory literature, this quantity is often denoted by
inj(H, G)
(see
[
19
]
), the number of injective homomorphisms, or by
Emb(H,G)
(see
[
16
]
), the number of embeddings of
H
into
G
. Moreover,
N(\textup{H},\textup{G})
is equal to number of (unlabelled) copies of
H
in
G
multiplied by a factor of
|\textup{Aut}(H)|
, the number of automorphisms of
H
. Throughout the paper we will use the shorthand

\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\coloneqq\{N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}(n,p))\geqslant(1+\delta)n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Delta\geqslant 2
be an integer, and
H
be a connected, irregular graph with maximum degree
\Delta
. For every fixed
\delta>0
and all
p\in(0,1)
satisfying
n^{-1/\Delta}\ll p\ll 1
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right)}{n^{2}p^{\Delta}\log(1/p)}=\theta_{{\textup{H}}^{*}},

where
\theta_{{\textup{H}}^{*}}=\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}(\delta)
is the unique positive solution to the equation
P_{{\textup{H}}^{*}}(\theta)=1+\delta
and
P_{{\textup{H}}^{*}}
is the independence polynomial (see
[
8
, Definition 1.1]
) of the graph
{\textup{H}}^{*}
with
{\textup{H}}^{*}
being the induced subgraph of
H
on all vertices whose degree in
H
is
\Delta
.

Upper tail large deviations of general subgraph counts in
r
-uniform Erdős-Rényi random hypergraphs have been studied in
[
14
]
. Their result when translated to Erdős-Rényi random graphs (
r=2
) yield the upper tail large deviations for subgraph counts for any irregular subgraph
H
, with maximum degree
\Delta
, in the regime
n^{-1/\widetilde{\Delta}}\ll p\ll 1
, where
\widetilde{\Delta}=\Delta
if
H
is a star graph, and
\widetilde{\Delta}=\Delta+1
otherwise.
Thus Theorem
1.1
improves that result.

With some additional work we further obtain the typical structure of
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
conditioned on the atypical upper tail event
\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)
. To state the result we need a few notation. For any vertex
u
in a graph
G
we write
\deg_{\textup{G}}(u)
to denotes its degree. For
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
subsets of the vertex set of a graph
G
we write
\textup{G}([U_{1},U_{2}])
to denote the bipartite subgraph induced by edges with one endpoint in
U_{1}
and the other in
U_{2}
. Moreover, for any graph
\textup{G}^{\prime}
we write
e(\textup{G}^{\prime})
to denote its number of edges.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider the same setup as in Theorem
1.1
. Then for any fixed
\chi\in(0,1)
there exists some constant
c_{\chi}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{Hub}_{\chi}(\delta)\ \big|\ \textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right)\geq 1-\exp(-c_{\chi}r_{n,p}),

or all large
n
, where
r_{n,p}\coloneqq n^{2}p^{\Delta}\log(1/p)
and

(1.1)

\displaystyle\textup{Hub}_{\chi}(\delta)\coloneqq\big\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a set }

\displaystyle U\subseteq[\![n]\!]\textup{ s.t. }\textup{deg}_{\mathbb{G}(n,p)}(u)\geqslant(1-\chi)n\ \forall u\in U\textup{ and }

\displaystyle e({\mathbb{G}(n,p)}\left[U,[\![n]\!]\setminus U\right])\geqslant(1-\chi)\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}n^{2}p^{\Delta}\big\}.

Observe that Theorem
1.2
shows that conditioned on the upper tail event
\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)
the Erdős-Rényi random graph contains a subgraph that is ‘close’ to a complete bipartite graph (often termed as a hub in the literature) with probability approaching one. Note that Theorem
1.2
immediately implies Theorem
1.1
.

Although the primary focus of this paper is to study the upper tail problem for irregular graphs, the arguments employed in the proof of Theorem
1.2
can be extended to obtain the typical structure of
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
conditioned on the upper tail event of a regular graph. We include that result in this article for its potential future usage. To state it we need to introduce some notation.
For
U
a subset of the vertex set of
G
, we write
\textup{G}[U]
to denote the subgraph induced by the vertices in
U
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Delta\geqslant 2
be an integer, and
H
be a connected
\Delta
-regular graph. Fix
\delta>0
. Assume
p\in(0,1)
satisfy
p\ll 1
and
np^{\Delta/2}\gg(\log n)^{1/(v_{\textup{H}}-2)}
. For any fixed
\chi\in(0,1)
there exists some constant
c_{\chi}>0
such that the following hold.

(a)

If
np^{\Delta}\gg 1
then we have

(1.2)

\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{Clique}_{\chi}(\delta)\cup\textup{Hub}_{\chi}(\delta)\big|\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right)\geqslant 1-\exp(-c_{\chi}r_{n,p}),

for all large
n
, where

(1.3)

\displaystyle\textup{Clique}_{\chi}(\delta)\coloneqq\big\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a set }

\displaystyle U\subseteq[\![n]\!]\textup{ of size at least }(1-\chi)\delta^{1/v_{\textup{H}}}np^{\Delta/2}\textup{ and }

\displaystyle\min_{u\in U}\textup{deg}_{\mathbb{G}(n,p)[U]}(u)\geqslant(1-\chi)|U|\big\}.

Moreover, there exists some
\delta_{0}(\textup{H})
such that,
for all large
n
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{Hub}_{\chi}(\delta)\big|\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right){\mathds{1}}_{\delta>\delta_{0}(\textup{H})}+\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{Clique}_{\chi}(\delta)\big|\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right){\mathds{1}}_{\delta<\delta_{0}(\textup{H})}\leqslant\exp(-c_{\chi}r_{n,p}),

(b)

If
np^{\Delta}\ll 1
and
H
is non-bipartite then for all large
n
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{Clique}_{\chi}(\delta)\big|\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right)\geqslant 1-\exp(-c_{\chi}r_{n,p}).

Theorem
1.3
was proved in
[
16
]
for
\textup{H}=K_{r}
, the clique on
r
vertices. In the context of homomorphism densities, analogs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
were obtained in
[
13
]
(their result allows one to condition on the intersection of the upper tail events of multiple subgraphs) in the regime
p\gg n^{-1/(\Delta+1)}
, where
\Delta
is the maximal degree of the subgraphs under consideration. Theorem
1.3
is proved in Appendix
A
.

1.2.
Poisson regime

For any graph
J
a fractional independent set is a map
\alpha:V(\textup{J})\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\alpha(u)+\alpha(v)\leqslant 1
whenever
uv\in E(\textup{J})
. The
fractional independence number
of
J
, denoted by
\alpha^{*}_{J}
, is the largest value of
\sum_{v\in V(\textup{J})}\alpha(v)
among all fractional independent sets
\alpha
in
J
.
The next result obtains the upper tail large deviations of the number of copies of
H
for any
strictly balanced graph
(i.e.
e_{\textup{H}}/v_{H}>e_{\textup{J}}/v_{\textup{J}}
for any proper subgraph
J
of
H
). Since any regular graph is strictly balanced, it is a generalization of
[
16
, Theorem 1.6]
. Moreover, we believe that the range of
p
and the choice of
H
in the theorem below are optimal.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
H
be a strictly balanced graph. Fix
\delta>0
. For
p\in(0,1)
satisfying
1\ll n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\ll(\log n)^{\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}/\left(\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}-1\right)}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}\left(N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}(n,p))\geqslant(1+\delta)n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\right)}{n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}/\textup{Aut}({\textup{H}})}=(1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta,

where
\textup{Aut}(\textup{H})
is the number of bijective maps
\phi:V(\textup{H})\rightarrow V(\textup{H})
such that
(u,v)\in E(\textup{H})
if and only if
(\phi(u),\phi(v))\in E(\textup{H})
.

The assumption on
H
in Theorem
1.4
is necessary to get the Poisson behavior (even the speed) near the appearance threshold, e.g. in the regime
1\ll n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\ll(\log n)^{c_{\textup{H}}}
, where
c_{\textup{H}}>0
is some constant (see the counterexample in
[
21
, Theorem 1]
). On the other hand, if
n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\asymp 1
then it is well known that the random
N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}(n,p))
is asymptotically Poisson (see
[
18
, Theorem 1]
) for any strictly balanced graph
H
. For balanced graphs the asymptotic behavior of
N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}(n,p))
at the appearance threshold is more involved and the limit is not necessarily a Poisson random variable.

1.3.
Localized regime II

Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
leave open the range
n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\gg(\log n)^{\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}/\left(\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}-1\right)}
and
p\ll n^{-1/\Delta}
. The following result yields large deviations in that intermediate regime for
H=K_{1,r}
, the
r
-armed star graph, for
r\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
. Fix
\delta>0
. Then for
p\in(0,1)
satisfying
p\lesssim n^{-1/r}
and
n^{r+1}p^{r}\gg(\log n)^{r/(r-1)}
, we have

(1.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}\left(N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))\geqslant(1+\delta)n^{r+1}p^{r}\right)}{n^{1+1/r}p\log n}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{r}\delta^{1/r}\quad&\text{if $np^{r}\rightarrow 0$},\\
\frac{1}{r\rho^{1/r}}\left(\lfloor\delta\rho\rfloor+\{\delta\rho\}^{1/r}\right)&\text{if $np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty)$}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.6
.

The recent work
[
1
]
also studies the upper tail probability of the number of copies of
K_{1,r}
.A key to their proofs in
[
1
]
is the observation that
N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))
can be expressed as a sum of some function of the degrees of vertices in
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
. This essentially allowed them to study a functional of the degree sequence, which in turn is approximated by a collection of i.i.d. binomial random variables. Consequently, the problem reduces to analyzing the asymptotics of the corresponding log-probabilities (see
[
1
, Lemma 4.2]
). This approach also allowed them to consider the regime
n^{r+1}p^{r}=\Theta((\log n)^{r/(r-1)})
revealing a mixture of both Poisson and localization behaviour in that regime.

However, the approach of
[
1
]
does not seem to be amenable to address upper tail probabilities of subgraph counts for irregular graphs
\textup{H}\neq K_{1,r}
, even in the regime
p\gg n^{-1/\Delta}
, as considered in Theorem
1.1
.
Our proof follows a different route which is based on the machineries introduced in
[
16
,
6
]
. Furthermore, unlike Theorem
1.7
,
[
1
]
does not provide a description of the typical structure of the random graph conditioned on the presence of excess copies of
K_{1,r}
.

The next result derives the typical structure of
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
conditioned on the upper tail rare event of
K_{1,r}
in the setting of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
. Fix
\delta>0
. Assume
p\in(0,1)
, satisfies
n^{r+1}p^{r}\gg\left(\log n\right)^{r/(r-1)}
. For any fixed
\chi\in(0,1)
, there exists
c_{\chi}>0
such that the following hold.

(a)

If
np^{r}\ll 1
then

\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{High-degree}_{\chi}(\delta)\big|\textup{UT}_{K_{1,r}}(\delta)\right)\geqslant 1-\exp(-c_{\chi}\widetilde{r}_{n,p}),

for all large
n
, where
\widetilde{r}_{n,p}\coloneqq n^{1+1/r}p\log n
and

\textup{High-degree}_{\chi}(\delta)\coloneqq\left\{\max_{v\in[\![n]\!]}\deg_{\mathbb{G}(n,p)}(v)\geqslant(1-\chi)\delta^{1/r}n^{1+1/r}p\right\}.

(b)

If
np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty)
then

\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\textup{Hub}}_{\chi}(\delta)\big|\textup{UT}_{K_{1,r}}(\delta)\right)\geqslant 1-\exp(-c_{\chi}\widetilde{r}_{n,p}),

for all large
n
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textup{Hub}}_{\chi}(\delta)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bigg\{\exists U\subseteq[\![n]\!]\textup{ of size }\lfloor\delta\rho\rfloor\textup{ such that }\min_{u\in U}\deg_{\mathbb{G}(n,p)}(u)\geqslant(1-\chi)n

\displaystyle\textup{ and }\max_{v\in[\![n]\!]\setminus U}\deg_{\mathbb{G}(n,p)}(v)\geqslant(1-\chi)\{\delta\rho\}^{1/r}n\bigg\}.

Comparing Theorem
1.2
with Theorem
1.7
we see that the difference between the regimes
p\gg n^{-1/r}
and
p\asymp n^{-1/r}
is that in the former regime
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
, conditioned on the rare event
\textup{UT}_{K_{1,r}}(\delta)
, typically contains an almost complete bipartite graph of appropriate size, while in the latter regime it may additionally contain a vertex of high degree, depending on whether
\delta\rho\notin\mathbb{N}
or not. For
p\ll n^{-1/r}
(and
p\gg n^{-(1+1/r)}(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}
) the first scenario ceases to appear and in that regime
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
, conditioned on
\textup{UT}_{K_{1,r}}(\delta)
, typically contains a vertex of high degree.

Remark 1.8
.

In light of Theorem
1.7
(a) and
[
1
, Theorems 1.1 and Proposition 4.4]
, we expect that there exists a threshold
c_{r,\delta}\in(0,\infty)
such that for
c<c_{r,\delta}
, conditioned on
\textup{UT}_{K_{1,r}}(\delta)
, the random graph
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
would typically consist of approximately
\delta n^{r+1}p^{r}
labelled copies of
K_{1,r}
that are almost edge disjoint. On the other hand, if
c>c_{r,\delta}
, then the typical structure should exhibit a mixture behaviour: the event
\textup{High-degree}_{\chi}(\delta^{\prime})
holds for some
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
and
(\delta-\delta^{\prime})n^{r+1}p^{r}
labelled copies are almost edge disjoint.

1.4.
Naïve mean-field approximation

For a function
h:\{0,1\}^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
and the uniform measure
\mu
on
{\sf C}_{N}:=\{0,1\}^{N}
, the naïve mean-field variational problem provides an approximation for the
\log
-partition function

\log Z_{h}
, where
Z_{h}:=\int\exp(h)d\mu
.
There have been several works, in different settings, attempting to find sufficient conditions on
h(\cdot)
, e.g. appropriate
low-complexity
conditions on the (discrete) gradient of
h(\cdot)
, such that the mean-field approximation is asymptotically tight (see
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
20
]
).
A heuristic computation shows that the mean-field variational problem for the logarithm of the upper tail probability
\mu_{p}(f\geq(1+\delta)\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{p}}[f])
, where
f:\{0,1\}^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is some ‘nice’ function,
\delta>0
, and
\mu_{p}
is the product of
N
i.i.d.
\dBer(p)
measures, is formulated as below:

(1.5)

\Psi_{p,f}(\delta):=\inf\left\{I_{p}({\bm{\xi}}):{\bm{\xi}}\in[0,1]^{N}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\bm{\xi}}}[f]\geq(1+\delta)\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{p}}[f]\right\},

I_{p}({\bm{\xi}}):=\sum_{\upalpha=1}^{N}I_{p}(\xi_{\alpha}),\quad{\bm{\xi}}:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{N}),\quad\text{and}\quad I_{p}(x):=x\log\frac{x}{p}+(1-x)\log\frac{1-x}{1-p}\text{ for }x\in[0,1],

with the convention
0\log 0=0
and the probability measure
\mu_{\bm{\xi}}:=\otimes_{\upalpha=1}^{N}\dBer(\xi_{\upalpha})
.

Since, upon setting
N={n\choose 2}
, identifying each of the possible
{n\choose 2}
edges to
[\![N]\!]
, and letting
f
to be the number of copies of any subgraph in a graph on
n
vertices, the upper tail of
N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}(n,p))
falls under the framework described above and it has been of interest whether the log-probability of such events is well approximated by
\Psi_{p,\textup{H}}(\cdot)\coloneqq\Psi_{p,N(\textup{H},\cdot)}(\cdot)
.

In
[
8
]
, the authors computed a related variant of (
1.5
) in terms of
\textup{hom}(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}_{n})
, the number of homomorphisms of
H
in a graph on
n
vertices (see
[
8
, Section 1.2]
). For convenience, we introduce the notation
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\bm{\xi}}}[N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}_{n})]\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\bm{\xi}}}[N(\textup{H},\cdot)]
where
\mathbb{G}_{n}
denotes the simple unweighted random graph with edge distribution
\mu_{\bm{\xi}}
.

We next extend the notion of subgraph counts to weighted graphs. Let
\textup{G}_{n,{\bm{\xi}}}
be the weighted graph on vertex set
[\![n]\!]
with edge weights
(\xi_{i,j})_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}={\bm{\xi}}
.
We define
N(\textup{H},\textup{G}_{n,{\bm{\xi}}})
to be the following sum:

N(\textup{H},\textup{G}_{n,{\bm{\xi}}})=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi:V(\textup{H})\rightarrow[n]}}{{\text{injective}}}}\prod_{(u,v)\in E(\textup{H})}\xi_{\phi(u),\phi(v)}.

We record two useful observations. First,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\bm{\xi}}}[N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}_{n})]=N(\textup{H},\textup{G}_{n,{\bm{\xi}}})\leqslant\hom(\textup{H},\textup{G}_{n,{\bm{\xi}}})
.
Second, the optimizer
{\bm{\xi}}
which asymptotically minimizes the variational problem
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
also satisfies the inequality
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\bm{\xi}}}[N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}_{n})]\geqslant(1+\delta)n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}
, under the same setting of Theorem
1.1
.
Consequently, we have

(1.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Psi_{p,\textup{H}}(\delta)}{n^{2}p^{\Delta}\log(1/p)}=\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}.

Hence the mean-field approximation is asymptotically tight for the upper tail of any irregular subgraph count in the regime
n^{-1/\Delta}\ll p\ll 1
.

It is natural to seek whether such a mean-field approximation also holds under the setting of Theorem
1.5
. The following result shows that this is indeed the case for
H=K_{1,r}
. To ease notation, we write
\widehat{\Psi}_{p,r}(\delta)\coloneqq\Psi_{p,K_{1,r}}(\delta)
.

Theorem 1.9

(Mean-field approximation)
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
. Fix
\delta>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Assume

Emergence of non-planted optimizers

For
n^{-1/\Delta}\ll p\ll 1
, it was shown in
[
8
]
that
\Psi_{p,\textup{H}}
is optimized by some ‘planted’ hub of appropriate size, for any connected irregular graph
H
. Namely, the optimizer
{\bm{\xi}}^{*}=\{\xi_{i,j}^{*}\}_{i<j\in[\![n]\!]}
is of the form
\xi_{i,j}^{*}\in\{p,1\}
for all
i<j\in[\![n]\!]
. In this case the term ‘planted’ reflects that one can deterministically fix the edges of the hub (setting their weights to
1
), and the probability of observing this specific configuration already captures the correct large deviation speed and the rate function.

In contrast, in the regime
p\ll n^{-1/r}
and
n^{r+1}p^{r}\gg(\log n)^{r/(r-1)}
the optimizer
\widehat{\bm{\xi}}
of
\widehat{\Psi}_{p,r}
is a non-planted one, meaning that we no longer have
\widehat{\xi}_{i,j}\in\{p,1\}
(see (
4.2
)). This reflects a fundamentally different mechanism: the upper tail large deviations of
N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))
is driven by the presence of a vertex with high degree (as evidenced through Theorem
1.7
), rather than by a fixed deterministic structure. While this event constrains the number of edges in the one-neighbourhood of a vertex, it does not specify which edges are present. Consequently, the neighbourhood remains random, and the correct rate function is obtained by enforcing a large-degree event rather than fixing specific edges. Thus the optimizer turns out to be ‘non-planted’.

1.5.
Complete irregular bipartite graphs

Although we have a complete understanding of the upper tail large deviations of subgraph counts for any irregular connected
H
with maximum degree
\Delta
, in the regime
p\gg n^{-1/\Delta}
, beyond the case of star graphs, at the time of writing this paper, nothing is known in the range of
p
such that
(\log n)^{\alpha^{*}_{\textup{H}}/(\alpha_{\textup{H}}^{*}-1)}\ll n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}
and
p\ll n^{-1/\Delta}
. We believe that the arguments employed in the proof of Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
can be extended to analyse the upper tail large deviations of the number of copies of the
irregular complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
with
s<t
in the sparse regime
p\ll n^{-1/t}
. The upper tail behavior is expected to resemble that of
K_{1,r}
, with the dominant contribution arising from non-planted structures. Heuristically, the excess copies should be generated by a set of
s
vertices sharing an unusually large common neighborhood, of size at least
\frac{1}{s!}\,\delta^{1/t}\,n^{\frac{s}{t}+1}p^{s}
.
The large deviation speed predicted by this mechanism is consistent with the bounds in
[
17
]
.
The techniques developed for
K_{1,r}
in this paper, should extend to yield sharp asymptotics throughout the regime
n^{-1/t}\gg p\gg n^{-1/s-1/t}(\log n)^{O(1)}
.
However, reaching the optimal regime –namely, obtaining the correct exponent in the logarithmic factor– would require an appropriate extension of Lemma
3.13
.

Definition 2.3

Let

be a connected graph with maximum degree
\Delta
. Define

\displaystyle Q_{\textup{H}}\coloneqq\{\textup{J}:

\displaystyle\textup{ $\textup{J}$ is a non-empty subgraph of $\textup{H}$ without isolated vertices and }

\displaystyle\textup{admits a bipartition $V(\textup{J})=A\cup B$ such that $\textup{deg}_{\textup{J}}(a)=\Delta$ for all $a\in A$}\}.

In the anticipation of the use of Definition
2.3
in the proof of Theorem
1.3
we have allowed
H
to be regular there. In the rest of this section, unless mentioned otherwise, we will implicitly assume that
H
is irregular.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a connected, irregular graph with maximum degree
\Delta
.

(i)

Let
J
be a non-empty subgraph of
H
without isolated vertices. Then
\Delta(v_{\textup{J}}-\alpha^{*}_{\textup{J}})-e_{\textup{J}}\geqslant 0
,
and the equality holds if and only if
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
.

(ii)

Fix any
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
. Let
(a,b)\in E(\textup{J})
for some
a\in A
and
b\in B
. Define
\widehat{\textup{J}}
to be the subgraph obtained from
J
by deleting all edges incident to
\{a,b\}
. Then
\alpha^{*}_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}=|B|-1
.

The proof of Lemma
2.4
(i) uses an argument similar to that of
[
16
, Lemma 5.3]
. The proof of Lemma
2.4
(ii) uses the fact that the fractional independence number of a bipartite graph must be an integer.

Proof.

Assume that
J
is a non-empty subgraph of
H
without isolated vertices. By
[
16
, Lemma 5.2]

J
has a fractional independent set
\alpha:V(\textup{J})\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\alpha(v)\in\left\{0,\frac{1}{2},1\right\}
and
\sum_{v\in V(\textup{J})}\alpha(v)=\alpha^{*}_{\textup{J}}
. Then,

(2.7)

e_{\textup{J}}\leqslant\sum_{(u,v)\in E(\textup{J})}(2-\alpha(u)-\alpha(v))=\sum_{u\in V(\textup{J})}(1-\alpha(u))\text{deg}_{\textup{J}}(u)\leqslant\Delta\cdot\sum_{u\in V(\textup{J})}(1-\alpha(u))=\Delta\cdot(v_{\textup{J}}-\alpha^{*}_{\textup{J}}).

Suppose
\Delta(v_{\textup{J}}-\alpha^{*}_{\textup{J}})=e_{\textup{J}}
, then both inequalities in (
2.7
) are equalities. We must have
\alpha(u)+\alpha(v)=1
for every
(u,v)\in E(\textup{J})
and
\text{deg}_{\textup{J}}(u)=\Delta
whenever
\alpha(u)\neq 1
. Let
A=\alpha^{-1}(\{0\})
,
B=\alpha^{-1}(\{1\})
and
C=\alpha^{-1}(\{1/2\})
. Every vertex in
A\cup C
has degree
\Delta
. Each edge of
J
has either both endpoints in
C
or one endpoint in
A
and other endpoint in
B
. If
C\neq\phi
, then it induces a
\Delta
-regular subgraph of
H
and
H
being connected with maximum degree
\Delta
implies that
V(\textup{H})=C
, which contradicts that
H
is irregular. Thus
C=\phi
and hence
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
.

Conversely, if
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
set
\alpha(a)=0
for
a\in A
and
\alpha(b)=1
for
b\in B
. Such an
\alpha
is fractional independent set in
J
and we have
e_{\textup{J}}=\Delta(v_{\textup{J}}-\bar{\alpha})
, where
\bar{\alpha}\coloneqq\sum_{v}\alpha(v)
. This establishes part (i).

Turning to prove the second part we notice that (
2.7
) continues to hold even if
J
contains isolated vertices. This, in particular implies that
e_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}\leq\Delta(v_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}-\alpha^{*}_{\widehat{\textup{J}}})
. On the other hand,
e_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}=e_{\textup{J}}-\deg_{\textup{J}}(a)-\deg_{\textup{J}}(b)+1
and
e_{J}=\Delta|A|
. Combining these observations, as
a\in A
and
\deg_{\textup{J}}(b)\leq\Delta
, we find that

\alpha^{*}_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}\leq v_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}-e_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}/\Delta=|A|+|B|-2-\frac{\Delta(|A|-1)-(\deg_{\textup{J}}(b)-1)}{\Delta}<|B|.

Since
\widehat{\textup{J}}
is a bipartite graph it follows from
[
17
, Proposition A.3]
that
\alpha^{*}_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}
must be an integer and thus
\alpha^{*}_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}\leq|B|-1
. On the other hand, setting
\alpha(u)=0
for
u\in A\setminus\{a\}
and
\alpha(u)=1
for
B\setminus\{b\}
we see that
\alpha
is a fractional independent set of
\widehat{J}
for which
\bar{\alpha}=|B|-1
proving that
\alpha^{*}_{\widehat{\textup{J}}}=|B|-1
.
∎

Lemma 2.5

Lemma 2.6

Assume the same setup as in Lemma

Let

2.6
apply to any
\textup{G}\in\mathcal{I}^{*}_{m}
. This, in particular, implies that
|E_{\text{exc}}(\textup{G})|\leqslant m_{0}
and for every
e=(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})\setminus E_{\text{exc}}(\textup{G})
,

\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)+\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\frac{c_{0}}{\left(\log(1/p)\right)^{v_{H}/2}}\cdot n.

Define

B_{*}(\textup{G})\coloneqq\{v\in V(\textup{G}):\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant np^{\varepsilon}\}.

We claim that each edge
e=(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})\setminus E_{\text{exc}}(\textup{G})
has at least one endpoint in
B_{*}(\textup{G})
. Otherwise, as
p\ll 1

\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)+\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\leqslant 2np^{\varepsilon}\ll\frac{c_{0}}{\left(\log(1/p)\right)^{v_{H}/2}}\cdot n,

which is a contradiction. Another easy bound that we get is

2m\geqslant\sum_{v\in B_{*}(\textup{G})}\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant|B_{*}(\textup{G})|\cdot np^{\varepsilon}

and hence
|B_{*}(\textup{G})|\leqslant 2mn^{-1}p^{-\varepsilon}
. We now derive an upper bound on
|\mathcal{I}^{*}_{m}|
by counting the number of ways to construct a graph
\textup{G}\in\mathcal{I}^{*}_{m}
:

(1)

Choose some
m_{\text{exc}}\leqslant m_{0}
and then choose
m_{\text{exc}}
edges of
K_{n}
to form
E_{\text{exc}}(\textup{G})
.

(2)

Choose the set
B_{*}(\textup{G})
and then choose rest of
m-m_{\text{exc}}
edges from the set

\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\{(u,v)\in E(K_{n}):u\in B_{*}(\textup{G})\}.

The number of ways to choose
B_{*}(\textup{G})
is at most

{n\choose 2mn^{-1}p^{-\varepsilon}}\leqslant n^{2mn^{-1}p^{-\varepsilon}}\leqslant p^{-\varepsilon m},

where we have used
p\geqslant n^{-1/\Delta}
. We further have
|\mathfrak{B}|\leqslant|B_{*}(\textup{G})|\cdot n\leqslant 2mp^{-\varepsilon}
. Therefore, using
p\ll 1
yet again,

(2.12)

|\mathcal{I}^{*}_{m}|\leqslant p^{-\varepsilon m}\cdot\sum_{m_{\text{exc}}=0}^{m_{0}}{n^{2}\choose m_{\text{exc}}}\cdot{2mp^{-\varepsilon}\choose m-m_{\text{exc}}}\leqslant p^{-3\varepsilon m}\sum_{m_{\text{exc}}=0}^{m_{0}}{n^{2}\choose m_{\text{exc}}}.

Now using the inequality
\sum_{i=0}^{k}{m\choose i}\leqslant(me/k)^{k}
and the fact that
m_{0}\ll m_{\min}\leq m
we finally get

(2.13)

\sum_{m_{\text{exc}}=0}^{m_{0}}{n^{2}\choose m_{\text{exc}}}\leqslant\left(\frac{en^{2}}{m_{0}}\right)^{m_{0}}\leqslant p^{-2\varepsilon m}.

Plugging (
2.13
) in (
2.12
) gives
|\mathcal{I}^{*}_{m}|\leqslant p^{-5\varepsilon m}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We now turn to the prove Theorem
1.2
. To this end, for every
\varepsilon>0
define

\displaystyle\textup{Near-min}(\varepsilon)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\{\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}:\ \textup{G}\in\mathcal{I}^{*}\text{ and }e(\textup{G})\leqslant(1+\mathfrak{t}_{0}(\varepsilon))\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}n^{2}p^{\Delta}\},

where
\mathfrak{t}_{0}(\varepsilon)=\mathfrak{t}_{0}(\textup{H},\delta,\varepsilon)
is some constant, to be determined below, such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{t}_{0}(\varepsilon)=0
. By (
2.1
) and (
2.4
), and the continuity of the map
\delta\mapsto\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}(\delta)
, in the regime
n^{-1/\Delta}\ll p\ll 1
we get,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\exists\textup{G}\subseteq\mathbb{G}(n,p):\textup{G}\in\textup{Near-min}(\varepsilon)\right\}\big|\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)\right)\geqslant 1-(\mathbb{P}(\textup{UT}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)))^{\varepsilon/16},

for some appropriately chosen
\mathfrak{t}_{0}(\varepsilon)
. Hence, to complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
it remains to show that for any
\textup{G}\in\textup{Near-min}(\varepsilon)
there must exist
W\subseteq V(\textup{G})
such that

(2.14)

\min_{w\in W}\deg_{\textup{G}}(w)\geq(1-\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\varepsilon))n\qquad\text{ and }\qquad e(\textup{G}[W,V(\textup{G})\setminus W])\geqslant(\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}-\mathfrak{t}(\varepsilon))n^{2}p^{\Delta},

for some
\mathfrak{t}(\varepsilon)
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\varepsilon)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\max\{\mathfrak{t}(\varepsilon),\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\varepsilon)\}=0
.

The main tool is the following lemma. Before continuing, we recall from Definition
2.3
that every subgraph
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
admits a bipartition
V(J)=A\cup B
such that
\textup{deg}_{\textup{J}}(a)=\Delta
for all
a\in A
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
p\ll 1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
be such that
e(\textup{G})\lesssim n^{2}p^{\Delta}
.
There exists a constant
\widetilde{\eta}\coloneqq\widetilde{\eta}(\varepsilon,\textup{H})>0
such that for every
\eta\in(0,\widetilde{\eta})
with
U:=\{v\in V(\textup{G}):\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\eta n\}
and
V\coloneqq V(\textup{G})\setminus U
, we have

N_{U}(\textup{J},\textup{G}[U,V])\geqslant N(\textup{J},\textup{G})-\varepsilon n^{v_{\textup{J}}}p^{e_{\textup{J}}},\qquad\forall\ \textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}},

where
N_{U}(\textup{J},\textup{G}[U,V])
denotes the number of labelled copies of
J
in
\textup{G}[U,V]
such that the vertices of
A
are mapped to those in
U
.

Deferring the proof of Lemma
2.7
to later we now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For any
\textup{G}\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
we have

(2.15)

(\delta-2\varepsilon)n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\textup{G}}[N(\textup{H},\mathbb{G}(n,p))]-\mathbb{E}[N(H,\mathbb{G}(n,p))]\leqslant\sum_{\phi\neq\textup{J}\subseteq\textup{H}}N(\textup{J},\textup{G})\cdot n^{v_{\textup{H}}-v_{\textup{J}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}-e_{\textup{J}}},

where the sum is over nonempty subgraph
J
of
H
without any isolated vertices. By (
2.9
) and Lemma
2.7
we further obtain

(2.16)

\sum_{\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}}N_{U}(\textup{J},\textup{G}[U,V])\cdot n^{-v_{\textup{J}}}p^{-e_{\textup{J}}}\geqslant\delta-4\varepsilon.

We claim that

(2.17)

N_{U}(\textup{J},\textup{G}[U,V])\leqslant\ \left(e(\textup{G}[U,V])\right)^{|A|}\cdot n^{|B|-|A|}=\left(e(\textup{G}[U,V])\right)^{v_{\textup{J}}-\alpha_{\textup{J}}^{*}}\cdot n^{2\alpha_{\textup{J}}^{*}-v_{\textup{J}}},\qquad\forall\,J\in Q_{\textup{H}},

where we recall the definition of
Q_{\textup{H}}
from Definition
2.3
. To see (
2.17
) let
M
be a matching of
J
of size
|A|
. Each edge in the matching has at most
e(\textup{G}[U,V])
choices (restricted by the fact that
A
must be mapped to
U
). There are
n^{|B|-|A|}
many choices for the remaining
|B|-|A|
vertices in
B
. The equality in (
2.17
) follows once we note that
\alpha_{\textup{J}}^{*}=|B|
for all
J\in Q_{\textup{H}}
. Now using (
2.17
) in (
2.16
),

(2.18)

\delta-4\varepsilon\leqslant\sum_{\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}}\frac{N_{U}(\textup{J},\textup{G}[U,V])}{n^{v_{\textup{J}}}p^{e_{\textup{J}}}}\leqslant\sum_{\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}}\left(\frac{e(\textup{G}[U,V])}{n^{2}p^{\Delta}}\right)^{v_{\textup{J}}-\alpha_{\textup{J}}^{*}}=P_{\textup{H}^{*}}\left(\frac{e(\textup{G}[U,V])}{n^{2}p^{\Delta}}\right)-1,

where the last step follows from the fact that there is an one-to-one correspondence between independent sets in
\textup{H}^{*}
of size
k
and graphs
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
with
|A|=v_{\textup{J}}-\alpha^{*}_{\textup{J}}
.

Further, using
e(\textup{G}[U,V])\leqslant e(\textup{G})
and the monotonicity of
P_{\textup{H}^{*}}
, as
\textup{G}\in\textup{Near-min}(\varepsilon)
, we find that

(2.19)

P_{\textup{H}^{*}}\left(\frac{e(\textup{G}[U,V])}{n^{2}p^{\Delta}}\right)\leqslant 1+\delta+\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\varepsilon),

for some non-negative function
\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\cdot)=\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\cdot,\textup{H},\delta)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\varepsilon)=0
. Since the second inequality in (
2.18
) holds term-wise we conclude from (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), that

(2.20)

N_{U}(K_{1,\Delta},\textup{G}[U,V])\geqslant e(\textup{G}[U,V])\cdot n^{\Delta-1}-(\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\varepsilon)+4\varepsilon)n^{\Delta+1}p^{\Delta},

as
K_{1,\Delta}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
. Take
\gamma\coloneqq\sqrt{\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\varepsilon)+4\varepsilon}
. Let
W\coloneqq\{v\in U:\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant(1-\gamma)n\}\subseteq U
and set
\beta\coloneqq e(\textup{G}[W,V])/e(\textup{G}[U,V])
. Notice that

(2.21)

\displaystyle N_{U}(K_{1,\Delta},\textup{G}[U,V])=N_{W}(K_{1,\Delta},\textup{G}[W,V])+N_{U\setminus W}(K_{1,\Delta},\textup{G}[U\setminus W,V])

\displaystyle\leqslant e(\textup{G}[W,V])\cdot n^{\Delta-1}+e(\textup{G}[U\setminus W,V])\cdot\left((1-\gamma)n\right)^{\Delta-1}\leqslant\left(1-\gamma(1-\beta)\right)\cdot e(\textup{G}[U,V])\cdot n^{\Delta-1},

where the first inequality is obtained by observing that once we fix the image of any one edge of
K_{1,\Delta}
, the remaining
\Delta-1
many vertices can have at most
n^{\Delta-1}
or
((1-\gamma)n)^{\Delta-1}
choices depending on where the centre vertex of
K_{1,\Delta}
is mapped to
W
or
U\setminus W
respectively.

To conclude the proof we recall that
\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}
is the unique positive solution to
P_{\textup{H}^{*}}(\theta)=(1+\delta)
and use that
P_{\textup{H}^{*}}
is strictly increasing and continuous on
[0,\infty)
to deduce from (
2.18
) that

(2.22)

e\left(\textup{G}[U,V]\right)\geqslant(\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}-\mathfrak{t}_{2}(\varepsilon))n^{2}p^{\Delta},

for some non-negative function
\mathfrak{t}_{2}(\cdot)=\mathfrak{t}_{2}(\cdot,\textup{H},\delta)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{t}_{2}(\varepsilon)=0
.
Combining the lower and upper bounds in (
2.20
) with (
2.21
) and using (
2.22
) we conclude that

(2.23)

e(\textup{G}[W,V])\geqslant\left(\theta_{\textup{H}^{*}}-\mathfrak{t}_{2}(\varepsilon)-\sqrt{\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\varepsilon)+4\varepsilon}\right)n^{2}p^{\Delta}.

This yields (
2.14
) completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

It remains to prove Lemma

. It uses some results on
2
-matchings from
[
8

. A
2
-matching of a graph is simply a union of two matchings of it. Notice that for any

the set

is a vertex cover of

(a subset of vertices that intersects every edge) with the property that
\Delta|A|=e_{\textup{J}}

[
8

, for any
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
and any vertex

of

consisting of

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

Let
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
be such that
e(\textup{G})\leqslant Cn^{2}p^{\Delta}
for some constant
C<\infty
. Set
\eta>0
such that

(2.24)

\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{2v_{\textup{H}}\cdot(2C)^{2v_{\textup{H}}}}.

Define
U\coloneqq\{v\in V(\textup{G}):\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\eta n\}
and
V\coloneqq V(\textup{G})\setminus U
. Note that
|U|\leqslant 2e(\textup{G})/(\eta n)
.
Next for any
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}

(2.25)

N(\textup{J},\textup{G})-N_{U}(\textup{J},\textup{G}[U,V])=\Phi+\Psi,

where
\Phi
is the number of labelled copies of
J
in
G
such that at least one edge of
J
is mapped to some edge with both endpoints in
U
and
\Psi
is the number of labelled copies where at least one vertex in

is mapped to some vertex in

.

First we bound
\Phi
. Fix

such that

and

. Set

to be the number of copies of

in
G
such that the edge

is mapped to an edge with both end points in

. Let

\Phi\leqslant\sum_{(a,b)\in E(\textup{J})}\Phi_{a,b}\leqslant e_{\textup{J}}\cdot|U|^{2}\cdot\left(2e(\textup{G})\right)^{|A|-1}\cdot n^{|B|-|A|}\leqslant\frac{e_{\textup{J}}(2C)^{v_{\textup{H}}}}{\eta^{2}}\cdot n^{v_{\textup{J}}}p^{e_{\textup{J}}}\cdot p^{\Delta}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot n^{v_{\textup{J}}}p^{e_{\textup{J}}}.

We now turn to bound
\Psi
. Fix any

. Let

be the number of labelled copies of
J
in

of

such that the connected component of

\Psi_{a}\leqslant\psi_{a}\cdot\left(\prod_{i=2}^{s}N(M_{s},\textup{G})\right)\cdot n^{v_{\textup{J}}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}v_{M_{i}}},

(2.28)

\prod_{i=2}^{s}N(M_{s},\textup{G})\leqslant\prod_{i=2}^{s}(2e(\textup{G}))^{v_{M_{i}}-\alpha_{M_{i}}^{*}}\cdot n^{2\alpha_{M_{i}}-v_{M_{i}}}\leqslant(2C)^{v_{\textup{H}}}\cdot n^{\sum_{i=2}^{s}v_{M_{i}}}\cdot p^{(\Delta/2)\sum_{i=2}^{s}e_{M_{i}}},

3.
Proof of Theorems

1.5
and
1.7

As it is clear from the context, throughout this section we use
\textup{UT}(\delta)
instead of
\text{UT}_{K_{1,r}}(\delta)
. We begin with the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
(Lower Bound).

Using binomial tail bounds one can show that the
\log
-probability of the event that the degree of vertex
n
is greater than
(1+\varepsilon/4)\left\{\delta np^{r}\right\}^{1/r}\cdot n
is lower bounded by
-(1+\varepsilon/2)\left\{\delta np^{r}\right\}^{1/r}n\log n/r
, for all large
n
. On the other hand, it is straightforward to note that the
\log
-probability of the event that
K_{(1+\varepsilon/4)\lfloor\delta np^{r}\rfloor,n-1-(1+\varepsilon/4)\lfloor\delta np^{r}\rfloor}\subseteq\mathbb{G}(n,p)
is bounded below by
-(1+\varepsilon/2)\lfloor\delta\rho\rfloor n\log n/r
. Using a variance bound and Chebychev’s inequality one further finds that the number of copies of
K_{1,r}
in the rest of graph is at least
(1-\varepsilon\delta/8)n^{r+1}p^{r}
with probability bounded away from zero. Upon combining the last three observations we indeed get the lower bound in the regime of
p\in(0,1)
satisfying
n^{r+1}p^{r}\gg 1
and
np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in[0,\infty)
,

(3.1)

\log\mathbb{P}(\textup{UT}(\delta))\geqslant\begin{cases}-(1+\varepsilon)\delta^{1/r}\frac{1}{r}n^{1+1/r}p\log n\quad&\text{if $\rho=0$}\\
-(1+\varepsilon)\left(\left\lfloor\delta\rho\right\rfloor+\left\{\delta\rho\right\}^{1/r}\right)\frac{1}{r}n\log n&\text{if $\rho\in(0,\infty)$}.\end{cases}

We skip further details (cf.
[
1
, Section 5]
).
∎

We now move to the proof of the upper bound. This requires some definitions and preparatory results.

Definition 3.1

(Pre-seed graph)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be sufficiently small. Let
\overline{C}\coloneqq\overline{C}(\varepsilon,\delta)
be a sufficiently large constant. A graph
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
is said to be a pre-seed graph if the following holds:

(PS1)

\mathbb{E}_{\textup{G}}[N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))]\geqslant(1+\delta(1-\varepsilon))n^{r+1}p^{r}.

(PS2)

e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)
.

We first show that the probability of
\textup{UT}(\delta)
is bounded by the existence of pre-seed graphs in
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
. More precisely,

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
n^{r+1}p^{r}\gg 1
and
n^{1/r}p\rightarrow\rho\in[0,\infty)
.
For large enough
n
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{UT}}(\delta)\right)\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a pre-seed graph}\right).

Proof.

Using Lemma
[
16
, Lemma 3.7]
with
X=N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p)),d=r,\text{ and }l=\lceil\frac{\overline{C}}{r}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)\rceil
, for
n
sufficiently large we get

(3.2)

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\textup{UT}(\delta)\cap\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\text{ contains a pre-seed graph}\}^{c}\bigg)\leqslant\left(\frac{1+\delta(1-\varepsilon)}{1+\delta}\right)^{\frac{\overline{C}}{r}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)}.

Therefore taking
\overline{C}
sufficiently large (depending only on
\delta
and
\varepsilon
), as
p\lesssim n^{-1/r}
we deduce from (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) that

(3.3)

\mathbb{P}\big(\textup{UT}(\delta)\cap\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\text{ contains a pre-seed graph}\}^{c}\big)\leqslant(\mathbb{P}(\textup{UT}(\delta)))^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the next step we consider only those subgraphs for which the property
(\text{PS}1)
can be replaced by a simpler condition as given below.

Definition 3.3

(Seed graph)
.

A graph
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
is said to be a seed graph if the following holds:

(S1)

N(K_{1,r},\textup{G})\geqslant\delta(1-2\varepsilon)n^{r+1}p^{r}
,

(S2)

e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
n^{1+1/r}p\geqslant 1
. For large enough
n
, a pre-seed graph is a seed graph.

Proof.

As any subgraph of
K_{1,r}
without isolated vertices is isomorphic to
K_{1,s}
for some
s\in[\![r]\!]
, we begin by noting that

(3.4)

\mathbb{E}_{\textup{G}}\left[N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))\right]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))\right]+\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}{{r\choose s}}N(K_{1,s},\textup{G})n^{r-s}p^{r-s}+N(K_{1,r},\textup{G}),

for any
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
.
By
[
16
, Theorem 5.4]
and using the upper bound (PS2) for any
s\in[\![r-1]\!]
we find that

(3.5)

\displaystyle N(K_{1,s},\textup{G})n^{r-s}p^{r-s}\leqslant\left(2\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)\right)^{s}n^{r-s}p^{r-s}\leqslant\left(2\overline{C}\right)^{s}\frac{\left(\log(1/p)\right)^{s}}{n^{1-s/r}}n^{r+1}p^{r}=o(n^{r+1}p^{r}),

for any pre-seed graph
G
, where in the last step we used the assumption
n^{1+1/r}p\geqslant 1
.
As
\mathbb{E}[N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))]=(1+o(1))n^{r+1}p^{r}
, we now conclude from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) that any pre-seed graph
G
satisfies
N(K_{1,r},\textup{G})\geqslant(1-3\varepsilon)n^{r+1}p^{r}
.
∎

We then show that any seed graph must have a subgraph containing most of its copies of
K_{1,r}
such that each of the edges of the subgraph participates in a large number of copies of
K_{1,r}
as well. We call them
core graphs
. The definition of core graphs below is conceptually similar to that in
[
16
]
; however, the conditions (C1) and (C3) are simpler in our setting, as we have already identified the contributing terms in
\mathbb{E}_{\textup{G}}\left[N(K_{1,r},\mathbb{G}(n,p))\right]
.

Definition 3.5
.

A graph
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
is said to be a core graph if the following holds:

(C1)

N(K_{1,r},\textup{G})\geqslant\delta(1-3\varepsilon)n^{r+1}p^{r}
,

(C2)

e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)
,

and

(C3)

\min_{e\in E(\textup{G})}N(K_{1,r},\textup{G},e)\geqslant\delta\varepsilon n^{r+1}p^{r}/\left(\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)\right)
.

Once we have a seed graph
G
then we can remove its edges iteratively that participate in strictly less that
\delta\varepsilon n^{r+1}p^{r}/\left(\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)\right)
labelled copies of
K_{1,r}
to produce a subgraph
\textup{G}^{*}
so that condition (C3) is satisfied. Therefore
\textup{G}^{*}
is a core graph.
So, Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
along with the above discussion gives us the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume
n^{r+1}p^{r}\gg 1
and
n^{1/r}p\rightarrow\rho\in[0,\infty)
. For large enough
n
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{UT}}(\delta)\right)\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a core graph}\right).

Armed with Proposition
3.6
, the proof of the upper bound reduces to the following proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume
(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}\ll n^{1+1/r}p
. If
np^{r}\ll 1
, then for large enough
n
, we have that

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a core graph}\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-(1-\mathfrak{f}(\varepsilon))\frac{\delta^{1/r}}{r}n^{1+1/r}p\log n\right),

and if
np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty)
, then for large enough
n
, we have

(3.6)

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a core graph}\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-(1-\mathfrak{f}(\varepsilon))\left(\lfloor\delta\rho\rfloor+\left\{\delta\rho\right\}^{1/r}\right)\frac{1}{r}n\log n\right),

for some nonnegative function
\mathfrak{f}(\cdot)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{f}(\varepsilon)=0
.

The key idea is to show that the subgraph of a core graph induced by edges incident to vertices of low degree is bipartite, and then use a combinatorial argument to show that such a family of core graphs is entropically stable, in the sense that the logarithm of the number of such cores is negligible compared to the
\log
-probability cost of realizing a core. This ensures that a union bound over all such cores preserves not only the correct speed of decay but also the constant in front of it.
To make it precise, let
G
be a core graph and we consider the following set of low degree vertices

\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})\coloneqq\left\{v\in V(\textup{G}):\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\leqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right\}.

Let
\textup{G}_{\mathcal{W}}
be the subgraph induced by edges that are incident to some vertex in
\mathcal{W}
. We will show that there are no edges between vertices in
\mathcal{W}
. For this we need to obtain a lower bound on the product of degrees of the endpoints of edges in core graphs.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G
be a core graph. If
n^{1+1/r}p\geqslant 1
then for every edge
e=(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})

(3.7)

\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)\cdot\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\frac{\widetilde{c}_{0}n^{1+1/r}p}{\left(\log n\right)^{1/(r-1)}},

for some constant
\widetilde{c}_{0}\coloneqq\widetilde{c}_{0}(\varepsilon,\delta)>0
.

Proof.

Any copy of
K_{1,r}
that uses an edge
e=(u,v)
of graph
G
must have either
u
or
v
as its center vertex. Then one of the
r
edges of
K_{1,r}
is mapped to
e=(u,v)
and the rest
(r-1)
vertices are mapped to the neighbors of either
u
or
v
depending on which is the center vertex of the copy. Therefore,

(3.8)

\displaystyle N(K_{1,r},\textup{G},e)=r\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\left({\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)-i}\right)+r\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\left({\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)-i}\right)\leqslant r\cdot\left(\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)+\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\right)^{r-1}.

G
being a core graph satisfies condition (C3). Therefore, together with (
3.8
), we have

\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)+\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\left(\frac{\delta\varepsilon}{\overline{C}(r+1)}\right)^{1/(r-1)}\frac{n^{1+1/r}p}{\left(\log n\right)^{1/(r-1)}},

where we also used
n^{1+1/r}p\geqslant 1
. Observe that

\max\{\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u),\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left(\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)+\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\right)
and
\min\{\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u),\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\}\geqslant 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

For the lower bound on the product of degrees to be useful we will assume
n^{1+1/r}p\gg\left(\log n\right)^{1/(r-1)}
. Let
u\in\mathcal{W}\subset V(\textup{G})
and
(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})
, where
G
is a core graph. As
n^{1+1/r}p\gg(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}
, Lemma
3.8
implies that

(3.9)

\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\varepsilon\cdot\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)\cdot\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant\varepsilon\frac{\widetilde{c}_{0}n^{1+1/r}p}{(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}}\geqslant\frac{2}{\varepsilon},

for large enough
n
. This lower bound implies that
v\notin\mathcal{W}
. Hence
\textup{G}_{\mathcal{W}}
is bipartite.

In the next lemma using the above property of
\textup{G}_{\mathcal{W}}
we derive a bound on the number of core graphs in terms of its number of edges and number of vertices of small degree.

Lemma 3.9
.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and
r\geqslant 2
. Let
\mathcal{N}(\textbf{e},\textbf{w},\varepsilon)
be the number of core graphs with
e(\textup{G})=\textbf{e}
and
|\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})|=\textbf{w}
. Then for any
p\in(0,1)
satisfying
n^{-1-1/r}(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}\ll p\lesssim n^{-1/r}
and large enough
n
,

\mathcal{N}(\textbf{e},\textbf{w},\varepsilon)\leqslant{n\choose\textbf{w}}\cdot\exp\left(17r\varepsilon\textbf{e}\log(1/p)\right).

Proof.

The proof uses the same general strategy as that of
[
6
, Lemma 4.7]
, particularly the ideas used to handle the regime where
p
is not too sparse. We briefly outline the main approach. Let
G
be any core graph with
e(\textup{G})=\textbf{e}
and
|\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})|=\textbf{w}
. We start by splitting the vertices in
V(\textup{G})\setminus\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})
as follows: for each
k=1,\ldots,\lceil\log_{2}(\varepsilon n)\rceil+1
, define

V_{k}\coloneqq\{v\in V(\textup{G}):\deg_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant 2^{k-1}\cdot\varepsilon^{-1}\}.

This forms a nested sequence of subsets:
\phi=V_{\lceil\log_{2}(\varepsilon n)\rceil+1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{1}=V(\textup{G})\setminus\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})
.

Thanks to (
3.7
), for any edge
(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})
with
u\in V_{i_{0}-1}\setminus V_{i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}\geqslant 2
, we have
v\in V_{j_{0}}
, where
j_{0}\coloneqq j_{0}(i_{0})
is the smallest integer satisfying

2^{i_{0}+j_{0}-1}\varepsilon^{-2}\geqslant\frac{\widetilde{c}_{0}n^{1+1/r}}{(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}}.

Moreover, if
(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})
with
u\in\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})
, then
v\in V_{j_{*}}
, where
j_{*}
is the smallest integer satisfying

2^{j_{*}}\varepsilon^{-2}\geqslant\frac{\widetilde{c}_{0}n^{1+1/r}p}{(\log n)^{1/(r-1)}}.

These structural constraints allow us to bound the number of ways to construct a core
G
with
e(\textup{G})=\textbf{e}
and
|\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})|=\textbf{w}
by following the same argument used to obtain
[
6
, (4.20)–(4.22)]
. We omit the details, as the remaining computations are identical.
∎

We need another improvement of the bound on the number of copies of
K_{1,r}
in a graph
G
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume
t\geqslant 2
.
For every graph
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
,

N(K_{1,t},\textup{G})\leqslant\left(e(\textup{G})\right)^{t}.

Note that Lemma
3.10
improves
[
16
, Theorem 5.7]
by a factor of
2^{t}
. This will be essential in deriving the rate function of the upper tail event when
np^{r}\ll 1
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V(\textup{G})
, then the number of labeled copies of
K_{1,t}
with center at
v
is
\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}(\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)-i)
. Since two copies of
K_{1,t}
with different centers have to be distinct, we get the following result,

N(K_{1,t},\textup{G})=\sum_{v\in V(\textup{G})}\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\left(\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)-i\right)\leqslant\left(e(\textup{G})\right)^{t-2}\sum_{v\in\textup{G}}\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)(\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)-1)=\left(e(\textup{G})\right)^{t-2}N(K_{1,2},\textup{G}),

where in the inequality we used that
\text{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\leqslant e(\textup{G})
and in the last step we used the formula from the first equality with
t=2
. Therefore, it is enough to prove the lemma for
t=2
.
Let the adjacency matrix of
G
be denoted by
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
. Then

(3.10)

4\left(e(\textup{G})\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1},i_{2}}a_{i_{3},i_{4}}\geq\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=1}^{2}\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})\in A_{m_{1},m_{2}}}a_{i_{1},i_{2}}a_{i_{3},i_{4}},

where for
m_{1},m_{2}\in\{1,2\}
we set

A_{m_{1},m_{2}}\coloneqq\{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})\in[\![n]\!]^{4}:i_{m_{1}}=i_{m_{2}+2}\text{ and }\{i_{m}\}_{m=1}^{4}\setminus\{i_{m_{2}+2}\}\text{ are pairwise distinct}\}.

Observe that for any
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
as above we have
\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})\in A_{m_{1},m_{2}}}a_{i_{1},i_{2}}a_{i_{3},i_{4}}=N(K_{1,2},\textup{G})
. Hence, by (
3.10
) the claimed bound is immediate.
∎

To get the correct rate function when
np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty)
, we require a slightly more precise lower bound satisfied by the number of edges of a core graph.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G
be a core graph on
n
vertices. Assume
np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty)
. Then,

e(\textup{G})\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\left(\lfloor\delta\rho(1-5\varepsilon)\rfloor+\{\delta\rho(1-5\varepsilon)\}^{1/r}\right)\cdot n.

Proof.

Assume the contrary. Thus,
e(\textup{G})<C_{\delta,\rho}n\lesssim n^{2}p^{r}
, for some constant
C_{\delta,\rho}
depending only on
\delta
and
\rho
. Then, by
Lemma
2.7
, there exists a partition
V(\textup{G})=A\cup B
such that

(3.11)

N_{A}(K_{1,r},\textup{G}[A,B])\geqslant N(K_{1,r},\textup{G})-\delta\varepsilon n^{r+1}p^{r},

where

N_{A}(K_{1,r},\textup{G}[A,B])
is the number of labelled copies of
K_{1,r}
in
\textup{G}[A,B]
where the center vertex is mapped to a vertex in
A
.
Since
G
is a core graph, using condition (C1) in (
3.11
) and
[
16
, Lemma 5.14(i)]
we have

\left(\left\lfloor\frac{e\left(\textup{G}[A,B]\right)}{|B|}\right\rfloor+\left\{\frac{e\left(\textup{G}[A,B]\right)}{|B|}\right\}^{r}\right)\cdot|B|^{r}\geqslant N_{A}(K_{1,r},\textup{G}[A,B])\geqslant\delta(1-4\varepsilon)n^{r+1}p^{r}.

Further note that by definition of
A
in Lemma
2.7
, we have
|A|\lesssim e(\textup{G})/n\lesssim 1
. Therefore, using
(1-\varepsilon)n\leqslant|B|\leqslant n
and solving for
e(\textup{G}[A,B])
, we get

e(\textup{G}[A,B])\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\left(\left\lfloor\delta\rho(1-5\varepsilon)\right\rfloor+\left\{\delta\rho(1-5\varepsilon)\right\}^{1/r}\right)\cdot n,

which contradicts our assumption.
∎

We now finish the proof of the upper bound.

Proof of Proposition
3.7
.

By Lemma
3.10
for any core graph
G

e_{\min}\coloneqq\delta^{1/r}(1-3\varepsilon)^{1/r}n^{1+1/r}p\leqslant e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}n^{1+1/r}p\log(1/p)\eqqcolon e_{\max}.

As
\textup{G}_{\mathcal{W}}
is bipartite we also note that the set
\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})
can have at most
e(\textup{G})
elements.
Fix any
e_{\min}\leqslant\textbf{e}\leqslant e_{\max}
and
\textbf{w}\leqslant\textbf{e}
. We claim that

(3.12)

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\textup{G}\subset\mathbb{G}(n,p):\ e(\textup{G})=\textbf{e}\text{ and }|\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})|=\textbf{w}\right)\leqslant{n\choose\textbf{w}}\cdot p^{-17r\varepsilon\textbf{e}}\cdot p^{\textbf{e}}\\
\leqslant\exp\left(-(1-36r^{2}\varepsilon)(1-3\varepsilon)^{1/r}\frac{\delta^{1/r}}{r}n^{1+1/r}p\log n\right).

The first inequality is immediate from Lemma
3.9
. To prove the second inequality we split the range of
{\bm{w}}
and
{\bm{e}}
into two cases:
\textbf{w}\leqslant\varepsilon\textbf{e}
and
\textbf{w}\geqslant\varepsilon\textbf{e}
. In the first case, we have

(3.13)

{n\choose\textbf{w}}\cdot p^{(1-17r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}}\leqslant n^{\textbf{w}}\cdot p^{(1-17r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}}\leqslant n^{\varepsilon\textbf{e}}\cdot p^{(1-17r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}}\leqslant n^{-(1-19r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}/r},

where in the last step we used
p\lesssim n^{-1/r}
.
In the second case, using the lower bound
\textbf{w}\geqslant\varepsilon\textbf{e}\geqslant\varepsilon e_{\min}
we obtain that, for all large
n

{n\choose\textbf{w}}\cdot p^{(1-17r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}}\leqslant\left(\frac{en}{\textbf{w}}\right)^{\textbf{w}}\cdot p^{(1-17r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}}\leqslant\left(\frac{e}{\delta^{1/r}(1-3\varepsilon)^{1/r}}\right)^{\textbf{w}}\cdot n^{-\textbf{w}/r}\cdot p^{(1-17r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}-\textbf{w}}\leqslant n^{-\textbf{w}/r}\cdot p^{(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}-\textbf{w}},

where in the last step we have used
\textbf{w}\leqslant\textbf{e}
and
p\ll 1
.
To complete the proof of (
3.12
) we further split into two sub cases:
\textbf{w}\leqslant(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}
and
\textbf{w}\geqslant(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}
. If
\textbf{w}\leqslant(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}
, then using
p\lesssim n^{-1/r}
we get

(3.14)

n^{-\textbf{w}/r}\cdot p^{(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}-\textbf{w}}\leqslant n^{-(1-19r\varepsilon){\bm{e}}/r}.

On the other hand, if
\textbf{w}\geqslant(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}
, since we also have
\textbf{w}\leqslant\textbf{e}
, we get

(3.15)

n^{-\textbf{w}/r}\cdot p^{(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}-\textbf{w}}\leqslant n^{-(1-18r\varepsilon)\textbf{e}/r}\cdot p^{-18r\varepsilon\textbf{e}}\leqslant n^{-(1-36r^{2}\varepsilon)\textbf{e}/r},

where in the last step we used
n^{1+1/r}p\geqslant 1
.
Combining (
3.13
)-(
3.15
) along with
\textbf{e}\geqslant e_{\min}
we obtain the second inequality in (
3.12
).

Now, summing up both sides of (
3.12
) over allowable range of
e
and
w
, as
n^{1+1/r}p\gg 1
, we derive that

(3.16)

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{\textbf{e},\textbf{w}}\left\{\exists\textup{G}\subset\mathbb{G}(n,p):\ e(\textup{G})=\textbf{e},|\mathcal{W}(\textup{G})|=\textbf{w}\right\}\right)\\
\leqslant\exp\left(-(1-37r^{2}\varepsilon)(1-3\varepsilon)^{1/r}\frac{\delta^{1/r}}{r}n^{1+1/r}p\log n\right),

for all large
n
. See that this bound (
3.16
) is optimal if
\delta np^{r}<1
. Now, assume
np^{r}\rightarrow\rho\in[1,\infty)
. In this case, we use Lemma
3.11
to get that

e(\textup{G})\geqslant\widehat{e}_{\min}\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon)\left(\left\lfloor\delta\rho(1-5\varepsilon)\right\rfloor+\left\{\delta\rho(1-5\varepsilon)\right\}^{1/r}\right)\cdot n
.
Using the lower bound
\widehat{e}_{\min}
on the edges instead of
e_{\min}
and repeating the same steps as in the proof of (
3.16
) we derive (
3.6
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Next we turn our focus to proving Theorem
1.7
. We use the concept of
strong-core
graphs.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\overline{C}_{*}\coloneqq\overline{C}_{*}(\delta,\varepsilon,r)>0
be some constant, which we will fix later. We define a graph
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
to be a strong-core graph if the following holds:

(SC1)

N(K_{1,r},\textup{G})\geqslant\delta(1-4\varepsilon)n^{r+1}p^{r}
,

(SC2)

e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}_{*}n^{1+1/r}p
,

and

(SC3)

\min_{e\in E(\textup{G})}N(K_{1,r},\textup{G},e)\geqslant(\delta\varepsilon/\overline{C}_{*})\cdot(n^{1+1/r}p)^{r-1}
.

As will be seen in the lemma below the advantage of a strong-core graph over a core graph is that if
G
is strong-core graph then it has a further subgraph
\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}
which satisfies a strong upper and lower bound on product of degrees of adjacent vertices and contains almost the same number of labelled copies of
K_{1,r}
as in
G
. This will be essential to our proof.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
G
be a strong-core graph. The following hold:

(i)

There exists some constant
c_{0}\coloneqq c_{0}(\varepsilon,\delta,r)>0
such that for every edge
e=(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})

(3.17)

\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)\cdot\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\geqslant c_{0}n^{1+1/r}p.

(ii)

There exists a large constant
C_{0}\coloneqq C_{0}(\varepsilon,\delta,r)<\infty
such that the subgraph
\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}\subseteq\textup{G}
spanned by edges
e=(u,v)\in E(\textup{G})
for which

(3.18)

\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(u)\cdot\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\leqslant C_{0}n^{1+1/r}p,

satisfies

(3.19)

N(K_{1,r},\textup{G}_{\textup{low}})\geqslant\delta(1-5\varepsilon)n^{r+1}p^{r}.

Lemma
3.13
will be proved later. For now, we prove Theorem
1.7
by leveraging Lemma
3.13
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Fix
\chi\in(0,1)
.
Let
np^{r}\ll 1
. By (
3.3
) and arguing similarly as in the proof of Proposition
3.6

(3.20)

\mathbb{P}\left(\textup{UT}(\delta)\cap\left\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ does not contain a core graph}\right\}\right)\leqslant(\mathbb{P}(\textup{UT}(\delta)))^{2},

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Next observe that by repeating the same steps as in the proof of (
3.16
) with
e(\textup{G})\geqslant(1+Cr^{2}\varepsilon)\delta^{1/r}n^{1+1/r}p
, we obtain for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and some large absolute constant
C<\infty
,

(3.21)

\mathbb{P}\left(\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a core graph $\widetilde{\textup{G}}$, with }e(\widetilde{\textup{G}})\geqslant(1+Cr^{2}\varepsilon)\delta^{1/r}n^{1+1/r}p\}\right)\leqslant(\mathbb{P}(\textup{UT}(\delta)))^{1+2\varepsilon}.

Set
\overline{C}_{*}=(1+Cr^{2}\varepsilon)\delta^{1/r}
. We claim that any core graph
\widetilde{\textup{G}}
with
e(\widetilde{\textup{G}})\leqslant\overline{C}_{*}n^{1+1/r}p
contains a strong-core subgraph
\textup{G}\subset\widetilde{\textup{G}}
. To see this, we iteratively remove edges from a core graph
\widetilde{\textup{G}}
that participate in less than
(\delta\varepsilon/\overline{C}_{*})\cdot(n^{1+1/r}p)^{r-1}
copies of
K_{1,r}
. This process yields a subgraph
G
satisfying both (SC2) and (SC3). Property (SC1) follows directly by triangle inequality. Therefore, from (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) we have

(3.22)

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\mathbb{G}(n,p)\textup{ contains a strong-core graph G, with }e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}_{*}n^{1+1/r}p\right\}\Big|\textup{UT}(\delta)\right)\geqslant 1-(\mathbb{P}(\textup{UT}(\delta)))^{\varepsilon},

for any
\varepsilon>0
. To complete the proof we will show that any strong-core graph
G
with
e(\textup{G})\leqslant\overline{C}_{*}n^{1+1/r}p
has at least one vertex with degree at least
(1-\chi)n^{1+1/r}p
, upon choosing
\varepsilon>0
depending on
\chi
.

Turning to prove the above take
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
to be any strong-core graph. By Lemma
3.13
there exists a subgraph
\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}\subseteq\textup{G}
with properties (
3.17
),(
3.18
), and (
3.19
).
Now fix
\gamma\coloneqq C_{0}\left(2\overline{C}_{*}/\delta\varepsilon\right)^{1/(r-1)}
. Define

U\coloneqq\{v\in V(\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}):\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(v)\leqslant\gamma\}\text{ and }V\coloneqq\{v\in V(\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}):(u,v)\in E(\textup{G}_{\textup{low}})\textup{ for some }u\in U\}.

Observe that there cannot be any edge in
\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}
with both endpoints in
U
, as this would contradict (
3.17
) for sufficiently large
n
. Thus,
U\cap V=\phi
. Consequently, we obtain the following decomposition:

(3.23)

N(K_{1,r},\textup{G}_{\textup{low}})=N_{V}(K_{1,r},\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}[U,V])+\mathfrak{R},

where
\mathfrak{R}
is the number of labelled copies of
K_{1,r}
in
\textup{G}_{\textup{low}}
such that a leaf vertex of
K_{1,r}
is mapped to a vertex outside
U
. We aim to show that
\mathfrak{R}
is negligible.
To this end, note that if
(x,y)\in E(\textup{G}_{\textup{low}})
such that
x\notin U
, then by (
3.18

P_{\textup{H}}\left(\frac{e(G[U,V])}{n^{2}p^{\Delta}}\right)\geqslant 1+x\cdot(\delta-4\varepsilon).

Next by
[
16
, Theorem 5.4]
and the fact that
P_{\textup{H}}
is strictly increasing and continuous, we deduce from above

(A.7)

\begin{cases}e(\textup{G}[V])\geqslant\left((1-x)^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}\delta^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}/2-\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\varepsilon)\right)\cdot n^{2}p^{\Delta},\\
e(\textup{G}[U,V])\geqslant\left(\theta_{\textup{H}}(\delta x)-\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\varepsilon)\right)\cdot n^{2}p^{\Delta},\end{cases}

where
\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\cdot)\coloneqq\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\cdot,\textup{H},\delta)
is some non-negative function such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\varepsilon)=0
.
Since the map
x\mapsto\theta_{\textup{H}}(\delta x)+\left((1-x)\cdot\delta\right)^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}/2
is continuous and strictly concave in
[0,1]
, as
e(\textup{G})\leqslant(1+\mathfrak{s}_{0}(\varepsilon))\widetilde{\theta}_{\textup{H}}(\delta)n^{2}p^{\Delta}
, we may choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small so that (
A.7
) holds for
x\in[0,\mathfrak{s}_{2}(\varepsilon)]\cup[1-\mathfrak{s}_{2}(\varepsilon),1]
for some non-negative
\mathfrak{s}_{2}(\cdot)
with
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{s}_{2}(\varepsilon)=0
. Hence, enlarging
\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\cdot)
we may and will assume that (
A.7
) holds for some
x=x^{*}\in\{0,1\}
. Using this lower bound, observing that
e(\textup{G}[V])+e(\textup{G}[U,V])\leqslant e(\textup{G})
, and the upper bound on
e(\textup{G})
we obtain the following tight upper bounds:

(A.8)

\begin{cases}e(\textup{G}[V])\leqslant\left((1-x^{*})^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}\delta^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}/2+\mathfrak{s}_{0}(\varepsilon)+\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\varepsilon)\right)\cdot n^{2}p^{\Delta},\\
e(\textup{G}[U,V])\leqslant\left(\theta_{\textup{H}}(\delta x^{*})+\mathfrak{s}_{0}(\varepsilon)+\mathfrak{s}_{1}(\varepsilon)\right)\cdot n^{2}p^{\Delta}.\end{cases}

If
x^{*}=1
, we proceed as in proof of Theorem
1.2
and conclude that
W\subseteq V(\textup{G})
such that

\min_{w\in W}\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}}(w)\geqslant(1-\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\varepsilon))n\quad\text{and}\quad e(\textup{G}[W,V(\textup{G})\setminus W])\geqslant(\theta_{\textup{H}}-\mathfrak{t}(\varepsilon))n^{2}p^{\Delta},

for some non-negative
\mathfrak{t}(\varepsilon)
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\varepsilon)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\max\{\mathfrak{t}(\varepsilon),\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\varepsilon)\}=0
. On the other hand if
x^{*}=0
, Lemma
A.2
yields a subset
\widehat{V}
of
V
such that

|\widehat{V}|\geqslant(1-\mathfrak{g}(\varepsilon))\delta^{1/v_{\textup{H}}}\cdot np^{\Delta/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{v\in\widehat{V}}\textup{deg}_{\textup{G}[\widehat{V}]}(v)\geqslant(1-\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}(\varepsilon))|\widehat{V}|,

for some non-negative
\mathfrak{g}(\varepsilon)
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}(\varepsilon)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\max\{\mathfrak{g}(\varepsilon),\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}(\varepsilon)\}=0
. This yields (
1.2
).

To obtain (
1.3
) we simply note that there exists a unique
\delta_{0}(\textup{H})>0
such that
\theta_{\textup{H}}(\delta)<\delta^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}/2
if
\delta<\delta_{0}(\textup{H})
and
\theta_{\textup{H}}(\delta)>\delta^{2/v_{\textup{H}}}/2
if
\delta>\delta_{0}(\textup{H})
(cf.
[
8
, Eqn. (1.4)]
). Hence,
x^{*}=1
if
\delta<\delta_{0}(\textup{H})
and
x^{*}=0
if
\delta>\delta_{0}(\textup{H})
. This yields (
1.3
).
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

Let
\textup{G}\subseteq K_{n}
satisfy
e(\textup{G})\leqslant Cn^{2}p^{\Delta}
for some constant

.
Let
\widetilde{\eta}<1
be as in Lemma
2.7
. By Remark
2.8
the bound (
A.1
) holds for any
\textup{J}\in Q_{\textup{H}}\setminus\{\textup{H}\}
and any
\eta\in(0,\widetilde{\eta}]
.

Next suppose

is non-bipartite. Fix
a\in V(\textup{H})
and let
\textup{H}_{a}
denote the subgraph obtained by removing
a
and all edges incident to it. By
[
16
, Lemma 5.2]
we have
\alpha_{\textup{H}_{a}}^{*}\leqslant v_{\textup{H}}/2
. We claim
\alpha_{\textup{H}_{a}}^{*}<v_{\textup{H}}/2
. If not, then
\textup{H}_{a}\in Q_{\textup{H}}
(again by
[
16
, Lemma 5.2]
). In particular,
\textup{H}_{a}
is bipartite. However, since all neighbors of
a
in
H
has degree strictly less than
\Delta
in
\textup{H}_{a}
, reintroducing
a
and its adjacent edges would force

to be bipartite - a contradiction. Using this strict inequality, we bound the number of labelled copies of
H
where at least one vertex of
H
is mapped to
U=U(\widetilde{\eta})
,

N(\textup{H},\textup{G})-N(\textup{H},\textup{G}[V])\leqslant\sum_{a\in V(\textup{H})}|U|\cdot N(\textup{H}_{a},\textup{G})\leqslant\sum_{a\in V(\textup{H})}|U|\cdot(2e(\textup{G}))^{v_{\textup{H}_{a}}-\alpha_{\textup{H}_{a}}^{*}}\cdot n^{2\alpha_{\textup{H}_{a}}^{*}-v_{\textup{H}_{a}}}\leqslant\varepsilon n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}},

where in the last step we used
p\ll 1
.

We now turn to prove (
A.2
) when
H
is bipartite. Let
\widetilde{\Phi}
be the number of labelled copies of
H
in
G
where a pair of adjacent vertices of
H
is mapped to
U=U(\widetilde{\eta})
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
be the number of labelled copies of
H
where two neighbors of a vertex in
H
is mapped to
U
and
V=V(\widetilde{\eta})
, respectively. Arguing similarly as in (
2.26
) we find that
\widetilde{\Phi}\leqslant(\varepsilon/2)n^{v_{\textup{H}}}p^{e_{\textup{H}}}
. It remains to prove the same for
\widetilde{\Psi}
.

To this end, fix
a\in V(\textup{H})
and let
b
and
c
be two distinct neighbors of
a
. Let
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a,b,c}
be the number of labelled copies of
H
in
G
such that
b
is mapped to
U
and
c
is mapped to
V
. Consider a
2

M
formed by the union of two disjoint perfect matchings which contain the edges
(a,b)
and
(a,c)
respectively.
M
consists only of disjoint even cycles, say
C_{l},C_{l_{1}},\cdots,C_{l_{k}}
, with

containing
\{a,b,c\}
for some
l=l(\{a,b,c\})
determined by the triplet
\{a,b,c\}
. For

we define
\widetilde{\psi}_{a,b,c}(W,\widetilde{W})
to be the number of labelled copies of
C_{l}
in
G
such that
b
is mapped to
W
and
c
is mapped to
\widetilde{W}
. Thus,

(A.9)

\widetilde{\Psi}_{a,b,c}\leqslant\widetilde{\psi}_{a,b,c}(U,U^{c})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(C_{l_{i}},\textup{G})\leqslant\widetilde{\psi}_{a,b,c}(U,U^{c})\cdot(2Cn^{2}p^{\Delta})^{(v_{\textup{H}}-l_{1})/2}.

We will show that there exists a choice of

\eta<\widetilde{\eta}
such that for any
a\in V(\textup{H})
and any choice of neighbors
b
and
c
of
a
, with
l=l(\{a,b,c\})
as above,

\widetilde{\psi}_{a,b,c}(U({\eta}),U({\eta})^{c})\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}n^{l}p^{(\Delta l)/2}.

This together with (

) will yield the desired bound for
\widetilde{\Psi}
.
Define
s^{*}\coloneqq\lceil 2\Delta^{2}v_{\textup{H}}(2C)^{v_{\textup{H}}/2}/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil

i\in[\![s^{*}]\!]\cup\{0\}

|\mathcal{S}_{\{a,b,c\}}|<s^{*}/\Delta^{2}v_{\textup{H}}.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.

It follows from
[
16
, Lemma 5.1]
that the vertices of any
\Delta
-regular connected graph
H
can be covered by a collection
\mathcal{C}
of vertex disjoint edges and cycles. If
\mathcal{C}
contains no odd cycles, we may replace each cycle in
\mathcal{C}
with one of its perfect matching, thereby transforming
\mathcal{C}
into a perfect matching of
H
. Now, observe that any additional edge of
H
not in
\mathcal{C}
is adjacent to exactly two edges of
\mathcal{C}
. Consequently,
H
can be covered by a subgraph that is a disjoint union of
P_{4}
and a matching of size
(v_{\textup{H}}-4)/2
. Thus,

(A.12)

(1-\varepsilon)\cdot(2e(\textup{G}))^{v_{\textup{H}}/2}\leqslant N(\textup{H},\textup{G})\leqslant N(P_{4},\textup{G})\cdot(2e(\textup{G}))^{(v_{\textup{H}}-4)/2}.

The desired result now follows directly from
[
16
, Claim 5.12]
.

Next assume
\mathcal{C}
contains an odd cycle
C_{l}
for some
l\geqslant 3
. We then have the following inequality

(1-\varepsilon)\cdot(2e(\textup{G}))^{v_{\textup{H}}/2}\leqslant N(\textup{H},\textup{G})\leqslant N(C_{l},\textup{G})\cdot\prod_{\textup{J}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{C_{l}\}}N(\textup{J},\textup{G})\leqslant N(C_{l},\textup{G})\cdot(2e(\textup{G}))^{(v_{\textup{H}}-l)/2}.

This reduction shows that it is enough to establish Lemma
A.2
for
C_{l}
. Since the case
l=3
is already covered in
[
16
, Claim 5.13]
we may and will assume that
l\geqslant 5
. Next, for each edge
e\in E(\textup{G})
, let
c_{e}
be the number of unlabelled copies of
C_{l}
in
G
that contain the edge
e
. The proof of
[
16
, Lemma 5.5]
establishes the following key inequality:

N(C_{l},\textup{G})^{2}\leqslant 2e(\textup{G})\cdot\sum_{e\in E(\textup{G})}2c_{e}^{2}\leqslant 2e(\textup{G})\cdot\Lambda(C_{l}^{*}),

where
C_{l}^{*}
is the graph obtained by gluing two copies of

along an edge and
\Lambda(C_{l}^{*})
is the number of homomorphisms of
C_{l}^{*}
in

where one copy of
C_{l}
in

is mapped to distinct vertices. Now,

is upper bounded by

as

is covered by a matching of size

, and as

. The result now follows from
[

, Claim 5.12]
.

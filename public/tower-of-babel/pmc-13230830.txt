Simultaneous multi-controller fusion via genetic algorithm-optimized weighted summation for variable-speed wind turbine control
Authors: Aziz Chahbi, Mourad Yessef, Amine Amharech, Saad F Al-Gahtani, Z M S El-Barbary, Hatim Ameziane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough individual controllers have been frequently studied across several research fields, the synergistic combination of multiple control strategies is still mostly unexplored. This paper introduces and develops an advanced hybrid parallel controller (HPC) in which Proportional-integral (PI), Sliding Mode (SMC), and Backstepping (BSC) controllers operate concurrently and contribute simultaneously to the generation of the optimal signal command through weighted-summation. The motivation stems from an extensive concept that the suggested HPC architecture benefits from the complementary nature of these controllers, where each controller’s strengths compensate for the others’ weaknesses. PI offers a null steady-state error, SMC ensures excellent robustness and fast disturbance rejection, and BSC delivers Lyapunov-based stability for nonlinear dynamics. The HPC’s performance is evaluated using four manually configured weight settings designed to compare HPC’s performance to that of pure controllers and investigate the effect of weight distribution on HPC’s control behavior. Subsequently, a genetic algorithm (GA) is applied to determine the optimal weight distribution. Extensive Matlab/Simulink simulations have been carried out on a 1.5 MW wind turbine under different operating conditions. The results demonstrate that the developed HPC outperforms all pure controllers with an ITAE improvement ranging from 58 to 75.57%, while the GA-optimized HPC (HPC-GA) further outperforms all manually tuned configurations, showing more than 83% reduction in ITAE, with physically significant trends: SMC and BSC contributions increase with parameter changes, on the other hand PI contribution decreases until complete elimination at the most disturbing conditions, demonstrating that the GA serves as a discovery mechanism for HPC.Keywords: Wind turbine, Hybrid parallel controller, Genetic algorithm, System control, Intelligent controlSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

From manufacturing automation, aerospace navigation, and biomedical devices to energy conversion systems, control systems constitute the foundation of modern engineering, enabling precise regulation of complex dynamical processes1–4. The goal of any control strategy is to maintain the system’s output at a desired reference and keep it unchanged even in the presence of external disturbances, modeling uncertainties, or internal disturbances such as parametric variations through a feedback mechanism. Over the years, the control engineering community has developed an extensive repertoire of strategies with rich theoretical development and practical implementation; each characterized by a different design method and offering its own performance characteristics5,6. Because of their conceptual simplicity and well-known theoretical tuning methodologies, conventional Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers dominate industrial practice7,8. On the other hand, nonlinear techniques such as: Sliding Mode Control (SMC) and Backstepping Control (BSC) have been developed within rigorous mathematical frameworks to address systems for which linear approximations are inadequate9,10. Throughout the literature, a fundamental limitation has been confirmed that no single control strategy can simultaneously deliver all the required performance criteria. Despite the rich diversity of available approaches. Each controller has its own limitations across smoothness and robustness, overshoot reduction and response time, and theoretical calculation and real-world implementation11–13. While those limitations are controllable in slowly varying industrial processes, they become critically problematic in applications characterized by rapid dynamics, strong nonlinearities, and strong disturbances6.

In particular, the Wind Energy Conversion System (WECS) represents precisely such a challenging control environment. Over the past two decades, the number of installed wind power systems has grown exponentially around the world, rendering it one of the most promising sources of renewable energy. Based on the Global Wind Energy Council, the total wind power capacity worldwide exceeded 1173 GW by 202414, WWEA15), making it a crucial component of the global renewable energy portfolio. Despite this extraordinary progress, the fluctuating nature of wind resources continues to pose significant challenges for system operators seeking to maximize energy capture from installed capacity (Said et al., 2025). MPPT or Maximum Power Point Tracking is a crucial function in variable-speed wind turbine control16. Well-designed blades may achieve the maximum power coefficient (Cp_max = 0.593) according to the Betz limit when operating at optimal Tip-Speed Ratio (λopt). Measurement uncertainties or the selection of an inappropriate control strategy may cause deviations from this optimal operating point, leading to lower annual energy production and diminished economic returns17; Yessef et al.18,. Therefore, the WECS’s control presents a confluence of challenges that severely examine the efficiency of a given control strategy: as the defined relationship between λ and power coefficient (Cp) follows a highly nonlinear characteristic, the aerodynamic torque reveals a nonlinear cube dependence on wind speed, and the coupled nature between electrical and mechanical parts of Doubly-Fed Induction Generators (DFIG)6,19. In addition, parametric uncertainties, and variations coming from multiple sources, including tolerances in blade geometry, ice accumulation on rotor surfaces and changes in air density (ρ) with atmospheric conditions, while the combined turbine-generator inertia could fluctuate significantly across different configurations and operating conditions. Moreover, the wind speed itself represents a highly stochastic disturbance input characterized by rapid fluctuations across multiple periods, turbulent gusts happening within seconds, daily variations spanning hours and seasonal patterns extending over months. While grid-side perturbations propagate through the electrical subsystem to affect mechanical dynamics. This combination of nonlinearities, uncertainties, and continuous disturbances creates a control environment where the limitations of individual controllers become highly apparent14,20,21.

Proportional-Integral (PI), Sliding Mode Control (SMC), and Backstepping Control (BSC) are among the frequently used strategies for controlling WECS10,13,22,22,23. Each controller offers its own advantages, while simultaneously showing fundamental limitations that constrain their individual effectiveness. The PI controller is the most widely adopted strategy in commercial wind turbine applications due to its simplicity and low computational complexity. It requires only two parameters: an integral action to ensure perfect steady-state accuracy, and a proportional action to produce smooth control signals, leading to reduced mechanical stress. However, it is still unable to handle strong nonlinearities, valid only around specific operating points, due to its special design, fundamentally based on linearized system models24. Moreover, its high sensitivity to parametric variations may lead to degraded performance outside the nominal conditions, producing excessive overshoot or slow response time, causing weak disturbance rejection. In contrast, SMC is among the strongest robust controls that have attracted considerable research attention due to its excellent resilience to parameter variations and uncertainties9. This robustness property is reached once the system trajectory attains a predefined sliding surface, where the closed-loop dynamics become highly insensitive to matched disturbances and model uncertainties. Consequently, SMC ensures excellent disturbance rejection while achieving fast convergence, even in the lack of accurate knowledge of the system model25. On the other hand, the high-frequency switching generates the chattering phenomenon that produces important switching losses in power converters and reduces the lifetime of different components. Another perspective can be obtained using BSC, which provides a systematic approach to stabilize nonlinear systems through a recursive application of the Lyapunov stability theory26. This theory constructs virtual control inputs with associated Lyapunov function offering a formal guarantee of global stability27. In contrast to SMC, BSC yields smooth control signals without chattering since its approach does not include any discontinuous switching, and different from PI controller, it accommodates the natural DFIG’s nonlinearities by exploiting the actual system structure28. However, the main disadvantage of BSC is represented in its strong dependence on precise mathematical models, because the Lyapunov function definition necessitates highly accurate modeling of system dynamics. In addition, high-order systems require high computational resources, while standard BSC lacks an inherent mechanism for rejecting constant disturbances, potentially showing persistent steady-state error that PI’s integral action could eliminate. Furthermore, the parameter variations may degrade performance when they deviate outside their nominal values.

A thorough analysis of these three control strategies reveals a clear trend: each controller’s key strengths directly address the major limitations of the others. The PI controller offers perfect steady-state precision, which BSC and SMC lack. Whereas SMC and BSC deal with nonlinearities that highly degrade the performance of PI. SMC offers excellent robustness against uncertainties and parameter changes that make both PI and BSC unstable with poor performance, while PI and BSC produce a smooth control signal. SMC suffers from chattering; however, it effectively compensates for the high model dependency encountered in BSC and PI. The fundamental complementary relationship among these three control strategies implies that their judicious combination may yield superior performance exceeding that achievable by any individual strategy, a hypothesis that motivates our investigation of an advanced hybrid control architecture that integrates the complementary performance properties of these strategies within a single controller.

Although multiple strategies for combining several controllers have been investigated in the literature25,29–31, each has demonstrated specific limitations that restrict their ability to fully exploit the complementary nature of the used controllers. The single-controller limitation is preserved in cascaded architectures, in which controllers are arranged in series32. While this architecture operates well for systems with natural timescale separation, it may degrade stability or make the system unstable at loop interaction frequencies. Switching-based hybrid structures are hybrid parallel configurations designed to select at each moment the most appropriate controller among the available set of parallel controllers based on operating conditions33 or desired performance metrics. The switching mode of these structures introduces discontinuities that can excite unmodeled dynamics. Although an appropriate switching algorithm can ensure better selection of the appropriate controller, this structure inhibits the system from leveraging the advantages of the inactive controllers to compensate for the limitations of the selected controller. PI with SMC and fuzzy logic34; and SMC with fractional order as hybrid controller combinations have shown improved performance over pure controllers35. Yuan et al.36, proposed a two-dimensional iterative learning control combined with H∞ optimization and linear quadratic fault-tolerant design for uncertain batch processes, achieving strong robustness and disturbance rejection. Such approaches provide rigorous guarantees under uncertainty and fault conditions; however, they typically rely on complex formulations, high computational effort, and problem-specific structures. Moreover, combining multiple control laws may introduce conflicting control actions if not properly coordinated, potentially degrading performance or violating system constraints. The limitation to only two controllers necessarily leaves performance gaps where a third controller would prove advantageous. Most fundamentally, no previous architecture has explored the simultaneous contribution of three different controllers operating continuously to a combined control action through a weighted summation, as an advanced control structure that would enable the synergistic exploitation of complementary characteristics without switching and discontinuities.

This paper aims to develop a novel Hybrid Parallel Controller (HPC) architecture that addresses the previously confronted limitations through a fundamentally different control design. Rather than a single selection of one controller or arranging several controllers hierarchically, the suggested structure is characterized by the simultaneous operation allowing a continuous contribution of the three different controllers: PI, SMC, and BSC, in which each controller receives the same tracking error (speed error) and contributing to the final signal command (electromagnetic torque) through weighted summation. The HPC output is formulated as T*em = αPI ·Tem, PI + αSMC ·Tem, SMC + αBSC ·Tem, BSC. The unity sum constraint must be satisfied (αPI + αSMC + αBSC = 1) with 0 ≤ αi ≤ 1 to cover the complete range of possible configurations from pure implementations (αPI, αSMC, or αBSC = 1) when two weights are equal zero and the third weight equals one, through various blended combinations, to fully balanced multi-controller fusion. The objective of the unity-sum constraint: is to ensure appropriate magnitude scaling independently of the weight distribution; while the continuous contributions nature aims to eliminate discontinuities related to switching, which plague mode-selection approaches. The concurrent contributions of all controllers in this parallel structure allow each controller to compensate for the others’ limitations through synergistic interaction: SMC’s chattering phenomenon becomes mitigated when combined with smooth PI and BSC outputs, the BSC’s sensitivity becomes less critical when SMC compensates for modeling errors, the limited nonlinear aptitude of PI becomes insignificant when BSC and SMC handle nonlinear regions. The crucial consideration in multi-controller structures is the constraint satisfaction, mostly compared to MPC36 where constraints are explicitly enforced. In the proposed framework, the HPC output is formulated as a convex weighted combination of the three individual controllers. Each controller contributing concurrently to the resulting control action. The unity-sum condition (∑αi = 1, 0 ≤ αi ≤ 1) ensures that the weighted combination remains convex, thereby integrally avoiding constraint violations. Consequently, if each individual controller output is stable, the global HPC’s output remains also stable. In addition, mechanisms such as saturation can also be applied to ensure compliance with actuator constraints. While HPC framework differs from H∞ based design where constraints and robustness are surrounded within a unified optimization formulation, HPC delivers a reduced complexity, smooth control, and flexibility through the global optimized weighting coefficients using genetic algorithms (GA).

Controller parameters tuning and optimization constitute a crucial phase in control systems design, as the overall performance strongly depends on the appropriate configuration and controller gains selection. In conventional controllers, tuning is often performed thought analytical or heuristic techniques, such as: Ziegler Nichols or frequency-response-based methods, which are suited for linear systems and may fail to provide acceptable performance in the presence of nonlinearity and/or uncertainties. In this context of nonlinear controllers such as SMC and BSC, parameter calculation becomes more challenging due to the strong dependence on system dynamics and complex mathematical structure. Manual tuning and trial-and-error methods remain usually used but are time consuming and unable to guarantee optimal performance across varying operating conditions as emphasized in the literature. These restrictions become particularly critical in WECS, where strong nonlinearities, stochastic disturbances, and parameter variations necessitate adaptive and globally optimal tuning approaches. Consequently, intelligent optimization techniques, such as GA have been emerged as powerful tools allowing large parameter spaces exploration and identifying optimal solutions even without any information about the optimal solution or simplified assumptions. This motivates their implementation in the suggested HPC architecture to determine the optimal weighting coefficients.

Identifying the optimal weighting coefficients is a major challenge that this work tackles through genetic algorithm optimization, with the time-weighted absolute error integral (ITAE) as a single-objective fitness function. Instead of relying on engineering intuition or trial-and-error tuning, GA explores the entire space of possible weight configurations without any preconceived assumptions about optimal distributions, which allows it to discover configurations that human judgment would not be able to identify. The ITAE criterion was selected based on its desirable properties37. It is characterized by the time-weighting factor that provides minimal contribution in early transient errors while greatly penalizing late errors14, thus leading to the production of well-damped responses with minimal overshoot, a very important characteristic in WECS where oscillations may accelerate component wear38. Moreover, this HPC framework can be extended naturally to n parallel controllers beyond the specific three-controller implementation with the generalized formulation T*em = Σ αi ·Tem, i with Σ αi = 1, positioning the HPC architecture as a scalable methodology applicable to increasingly sophisticated multi-controller configurations incorporating Model Predictive Control (MPC), Fuzzy Logic Control (FLC), neural networks, or other advanced strategies.

Comparative analysis of control strategies

The WECS’s control presents several challenges due to nonlinearities, uncertainties, parameter variations, and strong wind fluctuations. To justify the proposed hybrid parallel framework, this section delivers a comprehensive analysis of the chosen controllers: PI, SMC, and BSC.

Advantages and limitations of PI controller

Due to its simplicity and ease of implementation, the PI controller ranks as one of the most widely used control strategies. With only two parameters (Kp and Ki), it requires low computational cost and offers a simple design process. The integral action ensures precise tracking by guaranteeing zero steady-state error for step reference inputs. Unlike SMC, PI produces smooth control signals without high-frequency oscillations, leading to reduced mechanical stress on actuators and power converters. However, the PI controller is still valid only around a specific operating point, since its design is based on a linearized representation of the real nonlinear system, making it unable to handle strong nonlinearities; it is also known by its strong sensitivity to parameter variations and uncertainties. Moreover, its dynamic response to rapid disturbances makes it inadequate for wind energy applications where wind speed varies continuously.

Advantages and limitations of SMC

SMC delivers exceptional robustness once the system trajectory achieves the sliding surface. For a well-defined sliding surface, the controller could exhibit complete invariance to matched uncertainties and external disturbances, which is known as the invariance principle. SMC does not need accurate system models, and can operate efficiently with only approximate system model. Its dynamic response has demonstrated superior resilience with faster convergence. However, a significant practical limitation is that the chattering phenomenon is frequently faced when SMC is implemented. This high-frequency switching generates oscillations that lead to electromagnetic interference, increased switching losses, accelerated wear of mechanical components, and strong audible noise. Various mitigation techniques can be applied, but they introduce trade-offs that degrade robustness properties.

BSC’s advantages and limitations

BSC offers a recursive design process that is particularly appropriate for nonlinear systems. A global asymptotic stability is ensured by the Lyapunov-based design approach. Therefore, BSC generates a smooth control signal, in contrast to SMC, which typically produces high-frequency chattering. Without requiring linearization, BSC methodology naturally accommodates system nonlinearities. However, the fundamental limitation of this approach is shown in its heavy dependence on accurate mathematical models. Sensitivity to parameter changes may degrade its effectiveness in practical applications. Unlike PI controller, which has the capacity to ensure perfect elimination of steady-state errors through integral action, BSC lacks an inherent mechanism for rejecting continuous disturbances. Moreover, it presents implementation challenges for high-order systems.

Justification for the hybrid parallel approach

The comprehensive analysis summarized in Tables 1 and 2 reveals fundamental complementarity of the three control strategies. Each controller possesses unique strengths that directly address the limitations of the others. Through synergistic interaction, the proposed HPC structure enables each controller to mitigate the limitations of the others. When SMC is integrated with the smooth outputs of PI and BSC components, the chattering phenomenon becomes diminished; the composite control of HPC benefits from the continuous contributions that naturally smooth the global output without losing robustness completely, rather than being completely dependent on the discontinuous sliding mode signal. The resilience of SMC component compensates for modeling inaccuracy; consequently, the sensitivity in BSC turns out to be less critical. The impact of SMC component can increase to maintain the overall performance when it is degraded due to the sensitivity of BSC to parameter variations. This self-compensating behavior appears automatically from the parallel configuration without requiring any explicit switching. The limited capability of PI to deal with nonlinearities becomes negligible when BSC and SMC components are used for nonlinear regions.

Manually tuned weight configurations

Prior to the design and evaluation of HPC-GA, four distinct HPCs were created based on technical judgment and control concepts. These models aim to satisfy two primary goals. The first goal is to elucidate how differing weight distributions fundamentally affect performance, highlighting their trade-offs in transient response quality, steady-state accuracy, and robustness against variations in parameters or external disturbances. Secondly, they provide a set of performance baselines to measure the effectiveness of the HPC-GA compared to manually tuned HPCs, thereby indicating the additional value gained from the GA optimization procedure.

HPC with balanced configuration: HPC-B

It represents the most regular fusion approach for the proposed HPC framework. In this specific configuration, the same weight was allocated to each control strategy (αPI = 0.333, αSMC = 0.333, αBSC = 0.334). This HPC-B will allow each controller to contribute equally to the final control signal based on the fundamental idea that no single controller dominates, while ensuring equal complementary abilities: PI’s high precision, SMC’s strong robustness, and the high stability of BSC. This balanced configuration offers several advantages; for example, there is no prior knowledge about optimal weight distribution, and it will be used as a reference baseline (0% improvement) against which all the other configurations will be compared and evaluated. However, it may lead to suboptimal performance when certain controller features are more necessary than others for a specific operating condition.

BSC-dominant configuration: HPC-BSC

The BSC-Dominant configuration assigns the majority weight (αBSC = 60%) to the Backstepping controller while maintaining equal minority contributions (αPI = αSMC = 20%) from the PI and SMC. This HPC-BSC is inspired by the theoretical benefits of BSC, where Lyapunov-based stability ensures excellent handling of nonlinearities and smooth control signal generation without chattering. The need for HPC-BSC is justified by multiple strong real physical model nonlinearities, such as those confronted in WECSs: the nonlinear relationship between wind speeds (V), tip speed ratio (λ), and power coefficient (Cp), as expressed in Eq. (4). Due to its recursive design procedure, BSC takes these nonlinear characteristics into account, which makes it theoretically well-suited as a main control component. While the 20% PI contribution conserves the integral action for steady-state high accuracy, a capability that standard BSC cannot provide, the other 20% SMC contribution delivers robustness against internal and external disturbances while reducing chattering to dominate the control signal (T*em). This configuration hypothesizes that stability and nonlinearity handling should be prioritized over pure SMC (robustness) or PI simplicity (high steady-state accuracy), especially for systems with a precise model.

SMC-dominant configuration: HPC-SMC

The SMC-Dominant configuration prioritizes sliding mode control (αSMC = 60%) with equal supporting contributions from PI and BSC (αPI = αBSC = 20%). The HPC-SMC emphasizes robustness as the primary control objective, suitable for scenarios in which parametric uncertainties, inaccurately modeled dynamics or external disturbances are expected to highly influence performance. The motivating factor behind the high contribution of SMC stems from its invariance property: the closed-loop dynamics become fully insensitive to uncertainties and parameter changes when the system trajectory achieves the sliding surface. SMC is still a crucial control strategy in WECSs where strong turbulence, DFIG parameter variations and grid disturbances form various disturbances. Although high-frequency chattering that degrade performance. The 20% PI contributes in reducing steady-state error through the integral action, while the 20% BSC can add more stability that pure single SMC cannot properly ensure. However, the high αSMC value may induce significant chattering, the inherent smoothness of the PI and BSC contributions can effectively compensate for this limitation, thus preserving overall control quality.

PI-assisted configuration: HPC-PI

The PI control strategy is still the most widely used control strategy in commercial wind power plants, just due to its easy understood behavior and available tuning procedures. HPC-PI is a wisdom from above design philosophy that prioritize industrial reliability of PI over academic optimization of both SMC and BSC. This setting augments the PI contribution to (αPI = 40%), and is larger than the equal contributions of SMC and BSC (αSMC = αBSC = 30%). While SMC and BSC have certain theoretical merits, there are complications in their practical applications: SMC demands chattering suppression, while BSC requires precise system models. All these restrictions accounted for the higher PI contribution as a most relevant factor. In this structure, the benefits of both nonlinear capabilities and robustness provided by SMC and BSC are utilized, along with some favorable qualities of PI controller: smooth control behavior; steady-state accuracy; achievable transient characteristics. This configuration is particularly suitable when controller simplicity and reliability are the balancing factors to cope with aggressive performance of robust nonlinear controllers.

Genetic algorithm optimization of HPC: HPC-GA

The most critical challenge for HPC is the determination of the optimal weighting coefficients (α*PI, α*SMC, and α*BSC), because of the complex nonlinear interactions between them. This limitation makes their determination impossible through the analytical optimization methods. Genetic Algorithms (GAs) can be used as an effective solution through their global search aptitude42,43,44.

Optimization problem formulation

The weighting optimization in this work is formulated as a minimization problem of the integral of time-weighted absolute error (ITAE) as a fitness function criterion:

The ITAE criterion was selected for its desirable properties: early transient errors contribute minimally due to the small time factor, while persistent steady-state errors are heavily penalized. This ensures the production of well-damped responses with minimal possible overshoot. By the unity sum constraint αPI + αSMC + αBSC = 1, the problem is reduced to only two variables:

Minimize J(αPI, αSMC) = ITAE

Subject to 0 ≤ αPI ≤ 1, 0 ≤ αSMC ≤ 1, αPI + αSMC ≤ 1

Where αBSC = 1 − αPI − αSMC

A complete exploration of the feasible search space becomes possible by the use of this formulation, from pure single-controller configurations to fully HPC architectures in which all the controllers contribute simultaneously.

Discussion on fitness function selection and multi-objective perspectives

Rationale for the single-objective ITAE criterion

The ITAE criterion was selected as a single-objective fitness function because its time-weighting factor t assigns minimal penalty to brief initial transients while heavily penalizing persistent steady-state errors, thereby promoting well-damped responses with minimal overshoot as a critical requirement in WECS where sustained oscillations may accelerate drivetrain fatigue. Furthermore, ITAE reduces the optimization to a two-variable problem in (αPI, αSMC), enabling convergence in approximately six generations as demonstrated in Sect. 6.2.2; and is a commonly adopted index term enabling direct comparison with the existing literature14,37.

Limitations of single-criterion optimization

Despite these advantages, optimizing only ITAE introduces a fundamental limitation: it directly quantifies the quality of generator speed tracking but provides no explicit measure of aerodynamic performance. In WECS operation, the ultimate control objective is maximum power extraction, which requires simultaneous and accurate tracking of the generator speed Ωg, the power coefficient Cp, and the tip-speed ratio λ. Since the relationship between these quantities is nonlinear as overseen by Eqs. (3) and (4), a controller that minimizes speed tracking error does not necessarily optimize aerodynamic efficiency. The ITAE criterion is therefore a necessary but not sufficient condition for globally optimal WECS performance.

Real-time performance analysis

The implementation of the proposed HPC-GA framework is structured into two distinct phases: an offline optimization phase and an online control phase:

The offline phase employs a Genetic Algorithm (GA) to determine the optimal weighting coefficients (α*PI, α*SMC, α*BSC) by evaluating a multi-objective fitness function through repeated simulations of the WECS model. For the considered configuration (25 generations and a population size of 50 individuals), the optimization process was completed in approximately 10 min on a standard Intel® Core™ i5-13600U processor. Since this phase is executed only once prior to controller deployment, its computational cost does not affect real-time operation.

In the online phase, the controller operates using fixed, pre-optimized weighting coefficients. It is important to emphasize that the internal parameters of the individual controllers (PI, SMC, and BSC) are also fixed and predetermined. Consequently, no optimization, adaptation, or parameter tuning is performed during runtime. The real-time control action is computed through two main steps: (i) evaluating the outputs of the individual controllers using their fixed parameters, and (ii) calculating their weighted combination using the optimized coefficients. Pure SMC (cyan traces in Figs. 5 and 6) provides exceptionally fast response capability due to its discontinuous switching action that drives the system state toward the sliding surface with high authority, and offers inherent robustness against matched disturbances and parameter variations. The speed responses in Fig. 5 reveal the characteristic sharp spikes at each step transition, representing the aggressive switching behavior of SMC. As quantified in Table 6, Pure SMC achieves response times of only 30, 85, and 140 ms across the three scenarios, significantly faster than Pure PI. The ITAE evolution in Fig. 6 shows Pure SMC (cyan) accumulating substantial error, reaching 11.23, 33.79, and 56.74 rad·s respectively, with ITAE degradation ranging from − 704% to −767%. The chattering issue is particularly problematic in power electronic applications, where it can excite unmodeled high-frequency dynamics and increase switching losses in the converters.

Pure BSC (dark red/maroon traces in Figs. 5 and 6) exhibits more appealing stability features, because of the systematic Lyapunov-based design method that ensures asymptotic stability. As seen in Fig. 6, Pure BSC shows smooth tracking that rarely overshoots (9.5% with the maximum; see Table 6) and settles very quickly, at around 4.5, 15 and 30 ms for higher inertia values, which is much faster than any pure controller. The ITAE plots presented in Fig. 6 validate BSC’s best overall performance with the final values of only 0.537 rad.s, 1.129 rad.s and 1.724 rad·s which are the lowest among all pure controller realizations. Table 6 summarizes the positive ITAE improvements for Pure BSC as 58.50%, 73.07%, and 75.57%. However, pure BSC itself cannot operate optimally under all operating conditions, as its recursive design method may not be able to find the optimal compromise between these contradictory control objectives (rapid response, excellent robustness, and disturbance rejection) without additional control actions, even though this approach allows them to be elegantly translated into mathematical formulas.

Hybrid parallel controller architecture

The hybrid parallel architecture fundamentally overcomes the individual controller limitations by synergistically combining all three controllers in a simultaneous operation mode. As demonstrated in Figs. 5 and 6, the hybrid configurations, including Balanced (blue curve), BSC-Dominant (orange curve), SMC-Dominant (yellow curve), and PI-Assisted (purple curve), exhibit substantially improved tracking performance compared to pure controllers. This parallel combination enables the exploitation of PI’s error elimination capability for precise steady-state tracking, SMC’s rapid disturbance rejection for handling sudden wind speed variations and grid perturbations, and BSC’s robust stability guarantees for maintaining system integrity across the entire operating envelope. This represents a paradigm shift from traditional cascade or switching-based hybrid schemes because all controllers contribute continuously to the final control action through weighted summation, allowing smooth transitions and avoiding the discontinuities associated with controller switching logic.

Comparison to other works

Table 9 contextualizes the proposed HPC-GA approach within the recent landscape of hybrid and advanced control strategies for WECS and the associated nonlinear systems, showing that while each of the compared architectures addresses specific performance aspects, no other architecture simultaneously achieves the comprehensive combination of stability guarantees, transient performance, computational ease, and architectural scalability demonstrated by the proposed framework. (Talibi et al.42,.) has confirmed the effectiveness of GA in control parameter tuning for wind turbine MPPT, but its approach remains limited to a single pure PI structure that inherits major sensitivity to parametric variations and nonlinearities, limitations that the suggested HPC architecture directly resolves through the simultaneous contributions of SMC and BSC operating in parallel with the PI component. (Masoud et al.34,.) overcome the limitations of single controllers by proposing a hybrid controller that combines fuzzy SMC and FOPID, which is further optimized using a deep reinforcement-learning selector. This approach provides high robustness and significantly reduces motion vibration. However, the practical implementation of this architecture is constrained by the computational complexity of DRL training, real-time fuzzy reasoning, and the selection mechanism itself. In contrast, the proposed HPC-GA converges in around six generations with a 50-individual population operating in an easily manageable two-dimensional weight space imposed by the unit sum constraint, offering a significantly easier-to-implement optimization pathway without sacrificing robustness. The switching architecture based on a selector (Taghavian et al.33,.), although theoretically proven to be optimal for monotonic constraint systems, is still based on a discontinuous switching logic that limits the active control action to a single controller at any given time. Thereby inhibiting the cross-compensation synergy that constitutes the fundamental advantage of our proposed HPC structure and causing transient disturbances at each switching event that can excite unmodeled dynamics and deteriorate the overall smoothness of the system. (Chahbi et al.43,.) provide a comprehensive comparative baseline by evaluating separately PI, SMC, and BSC controllers within the identical wind turbine-based speed control framework adopted in this study. They quantitatively confirm that each individual controller exhibits specific performance limitations, specifically slow response of the PI, SMC’s significant chattering and under-overshoot, and sensitivity and model dependency of BSC, which are collectively the main physical and theoretical motivations for the suggested HPC architecture. (Duong et al32.) demonstrated that cascade configurations, while structurally organized through a natural temporal separation of control loops, ultimately preserved the limitation of a single controller at each level and precluded the cross-compensation behavior enabled by the proposed parallel weighted summation HPC. This cascade configuration results in a high computation time for multi-loop MSBOA optimization in comparison to the fast convergence of GAs achieved in this study.

Across all compared architectures, the proposed HPC-GA uniquely and simultaneously delivers four properties that no single competing method achieves. First, HPC delivers formal Lyapunov-based stability guarantees inherited from the BSC component and rigorously preserved through the convex weighted combination as demonstrated in the stability theorem of Sect. 5.4. Second, near-zero overshoot of 0% with fast response times consistently ranging from 10 to 30 ms across all tested inertia scenarios from J = 100 to J = 500 kg·m². Third, a physically interpretable and theoretically consistent emergent weight distribution discovered autonomously by GA without any human bias, in which BSC and SMC contributions progressively increase with system nonlinearity and disturbances, while PI contribution diminishes until complete elimination under the most challenging conditions. Finally, a scalable n-controller generalization that extends naturally to additional control strategies, including MPC, FLC, and neural network-based methods without any architectural modification. These combined characteristics, supported by ITAE reductions of 70.87% to 83.73% over the balanced baseline and validated across ten independent GA runs with a coefficient of variation of only 5.37%, confirm that the proposed HPC-GA represents a meaningful, statistically robust, and verifiable advancement over all existing hybrid control approaches surveyed for WECS operating under different operating conditions.

Framework extensibility and future directions

Further implementations could combine additional control strategies, such as model predictive control (MPC) for constraint management, fuzzy logic control (FLC) for rule-based reasoning, adaptive control for evolving systems, H∞ control to ensure robustness, or neural network-based methods for data-driven control as shown in Figs. 10 and 11. The GA optimization architecture grows linearly with the number of controllers, as the (n − 1)-dimensional weight space remains feasible for modern evolutionary algorithms. This inherent scalability not only makes the hybrid parallel architecture an incremental improvement but also an approach that could be transformative and revolutionize control system design in many areas of engineering, from renewable energy to autonomous vehicles, industrial robotics to the aerospace sector, and wherever complex nonlinear dynamic systems require multi-objective optimization.

Conclusions

Contrary to existing hybrid control architectures that are limited to two controllers or sequential switching, this work developed a new parallel hybrid controller that enables the simultaneous implementation of three different control strategies: PI, SMC, and BSC, through a weighted summation architecture, providing a new approach that is fundamentally different from conventional cascaded or commutated architectures. The proposed HPC is designed to exploit the complementary advantages of the three strategies, with each controller actively compensating for the inherent limitations of the others. The superiority of the proposed HPC is confirmed by exhaustive MATLAB/Simulink simulations on a 1.5 MW wind turbine under different operating scenarios. While the fixed-weight configurations offered significant advantages over individual controllers, with ITAE improvements of up to 64.84%, GA-optimized HPC further improved performance by reducing ITAE by 70.87% to 83.73% compared to the balanced baseline, with zero overshoot and faster response times of 10 to 30 ms under all examined operating conditions. As well as the numerical improvements, the graphical analysis showed a very interesting physical trend: the BSC and SMC contributions gradually increase with mechanical inertia and disturbances, whereas the PI contribution decreases until it disappears completely under the most challenging conditions. This validates the proposed architectural design philosophy and confirms that metaheuristic optimization functions as a discovery mechanism rather than a conventional tuning procedure. The generalized n-controller formulation positions this framework as a scalable methodology applicable to diverse nonlinear engineering systems.

Dynamical electron–phonon vertex correction
Andrea Marini

Abstract
The dynamical screening of the electron–phonon vertex is caused by the retarded oscillations of the electronic charge following the electron–hole scattering with a phonon mode. This retardation induces a frequency dependence of the electron–phonon interaction. Model Hamiltonians and ab–initio approaches have instilled the idea that this retardation is, in most of the cases, negligible. In this work I demonstrate that the dynamical screening of the electron–phonon vertex cannot be neglected a priori . By using a perturbative expansion I introduce a controllable and physically sound method to evaluate and include dynamical screening effects. Based on the exact results of the homogeneous electron gas I propose a dynamical vertex correction function Γ e − p ​ ( ω ) \Gamma_{e-p}\left(\omega\right) designed to screen the commonly used adiabatic electron–phonon interaction. This function is expressed in terms of adiabatic quantities, that can be easily calculated and used to evaluate the strength of the dynamical corrections, even in realistic materials.

4
]
it has been demonstrated that the equilibrium limit of the phonon self–energy is a combination of the left and right self–energies.

In this limit the retarded phonon propagator satisfies the Dyson equation

D_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\left(t\right)=D^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\biggl(\delta_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)+\\
+\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}}\left(t^{\prime}-\overline{t}\right)-C^{ref}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}}\delta\left(t^{\prime}-\overline{t}\right)\right]D_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{2}}\left(\overline{t}\right)\biggr),

(22a)

with

\displaystyle\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\overrightarrow{\Pi}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\left(t\right)+\overleftarrow{\Pi}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\left(t\right)\right].

(22b)

By taking the equilibrium limit of
\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
it follows that

\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\left(t_{1}-t_{2}\right)=\\
\frac{1}{2}\left[g_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}{\mathbf{x}}_{3},t_{1}-t_{3}\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{2}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3},t_{3}-t_{2}\right)+\right.\\
\left.+\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{1}}\left(t_{1}-t_{3},{\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}{\mathbf{x}}_{2},t_{3}-t_{2}\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{2}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)\right]

(23)

Eq. (
23
) is a very powerful definition of the phonon self–energy. Indeed if
\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{2}}\left({\mathbf{x}}t\right)

is calculated exactly the left and right self–energies are identical and Eq. (
23
) reduces to one term. At the same time, however,
it gives us the freedom to approximate
\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}}
in such a way to break the symmetry of the individual left/right components still ensuring a fully
symmetric total self–energy.

The last ingredient we need to define explicitly is the inverse dynamical dielectric function
\epsilon^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(1,2\right)
that appears in Eq. (
18
). This is done
in Appendix
A
by introducing a combined index for bands pairs and momenta
{\mathbf{I}}_{i}\equiv\left(n_{i},m_{i},{\mathbf{k}}_{i}\right)
.

Any non–local operator
O\left(1,2\right)
can be represented in this basis by
using

O_{{\mathbf{I}}_{1}\overline{{\mathbf{I}}}_{2}}\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\int_{0}\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{1}{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\overline{u_{n_{1}{\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{q}}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}u_{m_{1}{\mathbf{k}}_{1}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)\\
\overline{u_{n_{2}{\mathbf{k}}_{2}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}u_{m_{2}{\mathbf{k}}_{2}-{\mathbf{q}}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)O\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}t_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}t_{2}\right).

(24)

The
\overline{{\mathbf{I}}}_{2}
in Eq. (
24
) appears to keep track of the change of order in the left and right pair of wave–functions.
It is simple algebra to demonstrate that, in the
\{{\mathbf{I}}_{i}\}
basis, the Fourier transformed of the equilibrium
\epsilon^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(1,2\right)
is

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}_{\mathbf{q}}^{-1}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{\underline{1}}+\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\underline{\underline{\chi}}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right),

(25)

with
\underline{\underline{\chi}}
the electronic response function matrix that solves the time–dependent Hartree equation

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{\chi}}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\left[\underline{\underline{1}}+\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\underline{\underline{\chi}}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\right].

(26)

Now, as both
\underline{\underline{\chi}}
and
\underline{\underline{\Pi}}
are matrices, in order to keep the formalism simple in view of the rest of the paper, I assume the phonon
self–energy and propagator to be diagonal
in the branch index. Thanks to this assumption we can transform the spatial integration’s in Eq. (
23
) into matrix multiplications:

\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{I}}_{1}{\mathbf{I}}_{2}}\left[g_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda,{\mathbf{I}}_{1}}\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}{\mathbf{I}}_{1}\overline{{\mathbf{I}}}_{2}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda,\overline{{\mathbf{I}}}_{2}}\left(\omega\right)+\right.\\
\left.+\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda,{\mathbf{I}}_{1}}\left(\omega\right)\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}{\mathbf{I}}_{1}\overline{{\mathbf{I}}}_{2}}\left(\omega\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda,\overline{{\mathbf{I}}}_{2}}\right]=\\
\frac{1}{2}\left[\underline{g}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}^{T}\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)+\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}^{T}\left(\omega\right)\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\underline{g}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right].

(27)

In Eq. (
27
) I have introduced a compact notation for Eq. (
21
), noticing that from

\displaystyle\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)\underline{g}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda},

(28)

it follows
\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}^{T}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{g}^{T}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1,T}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)
.

III.2
Quasi–phonon solution

Once we have the phonon self–energy we need a recipe to extract from the Dyson equation the final renormalized phonon width and energy.
In the case of electrons this procedure is known as quasi–particle approximation (QPA)
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
.

The goal of this section is to review the quasi–phonon approximation (QPHA), derived independently in Ref.
[
39
]
and Ref.
[
1
]
that,
in complete analogy to the electronic quasi–particle, will define both the phonon energy
and width. As it will be clear shortly the QPHA will impose any approximation to describe accurately both the real and imaginary part of the self–energy.

Let’s start from the retarded, frequency dependent, independent–particle phonon propagator

\displaystyle D^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\frac{\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}{\left(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+}\right)^{2}-\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}^{2}}.

(29)

In Eq. (
29
) does not appear the factor
2
at the numerator as a consequence of the
\sqrt{2}
appearing in Eq. (
10
).
In the diagonal case the Fourier transformed of Eq.(
22
a) is

\displaystyle D_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\frac{1}{\left(D^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right)^{-1}-\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)+C^{ref}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}.

(30)

At this point I formally introduce a QPHA representation of
\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
:

\displaystyle\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{QPHA}=\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}+\mathrm{i}{\rm Im}\left[\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]\omega+{\rm Re}\left[\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}},

(31a)

where

\displaystyle\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\frac{\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)-\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}}{\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}.

(31b)

In Eq. (
31
) I have used
\left.\left(\cdots\right)\right|_{s}
to represent the adiabatic limit of
\left(\cdots\right)
. I will use the same notation throughout the work.

Eq. (
31
) is formally very different from the QPA form of the electronic self–energy. Indeed in the QP the self–energy is expanded at the first order while
Eq. (
31
) needs a second order term. The reason of this difference traces back to the different frequency dependence of the self–energies.
The QPA is motivated by the fact that the poles of electron–electron interaction describe plasmonic excitations. In general plasmon excitations are,
energetically, very far from the single–particle levels. When the electron–electron interaction is mediated by phonons this argument does not hold anymore and,
indeed, it is possible to observe large deviations from the QPA
[
40
]
.

The case of the phonon self–energy is even more special. The
\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
frequency dependence, in the homogeneous electron gas, is dominated
[
12
]

by a fast rise of imaginary part which corresponds to a slower rise of the real part. This is the reason of the two terms in Eq. (
31
).

If we now use Eq. (
31
) in the Eq. (
30
) it is simple math to show that

\displaystyle\left.D_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{QPHA}=\frac{\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}{\left(\omega+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)^{2}-\left(\Omega^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)^{2}},

(32)

where

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\frac{1}{1-{\rm Re}\left[\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]},

(33a)

\displaystyle\gamma^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=-\frac{Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}{2}{\rm Im}\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)\right],

(33b)

\displaystyle\Omega^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\pm\sqrt{\left[Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}-C^{ref}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)-\left(\gamma^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)^{2}\right]}.

(33c)

From Eq. (
33
) we see that in the QPHA it appears a renormalization factor,
Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
, that is the phonon counterpart of the QPA renormalization factor.

The phonon on–the-mass shell (OMS)
[
5
]
approximation can be obtained from Eqs. (
32
)–(
33
) by assuming that
\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\ll 1
.
In this limit indeed

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\sim 1+{\rm Re}\left[\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right],

(34a)

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}\sim 1+\frac{{\rm Re}\left[\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]}{2}.

(34b)

From Eq. (
34
) it follows

\displaystyle\gamma^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\approx-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)\right]=\gamma^{OMS}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda},

(35a)

and

\Omega^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\approx\left(1+\frac{{\rm Re}\left[\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]}{2}\right)\times\\
\times\sqrt{\left(\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}-C^{ref}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}=\Omega^{OMS}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}.

(35b)

If we assume
\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}\approx C^{ref}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
Eq.(
33
c) and Eq.(
35
b) simplify to

\displaystyle\left(\Omega^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)^{2}=Z_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\omega^{2}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}-\left(\gamma^{QPHA}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)^{2},

(36a)

\displaystyle\Omega^{OMS}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+\frac{{\rm Re}\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right)\right]-\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}}{2}.

(36b)

Eqs. (
35
)–(
36
) make also clear that, if
\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
is large it is not possible to write the phonon width solely in terms of

{\rm Im}\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]
.
The OMS and QPHA approximations are compared in the case of the Homogeneous Electron gas in Section
VI.1
.

IV
The statically screened approximation

The actual evaluation of the full frequency dependence of
\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left({\mathbf{x}},\omega\right)
and
\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega,{\mathbf{x}}\right)
requires an enormous computational cost. The
reason is that the common applications of the e–p vertex require to use very large
{\mathbf{k}}
–point grids that make, in practice, numerically challenging to go beyond the
static approximation.

As discussed in the introduction the applications of model Hamiltonians
and the wide–spread use of DFPT
ab–initio
methods and codes,
have favored the use of a statically screened electron–phonon interaction:

\displaystyle\left.\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)\right|_{s}=\int\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{1}g_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)\epsilon^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}t_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}t_{1}^{+}\right),

(37a)

\displaystyle\left.\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)\right|_{s}=\int\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\epsilon^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}t_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}t_{1}^{+}\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right),

(37b)

with
t_{1}^{+}=t_{1}+0^{+}
.
Eq. (
37
) can be rewritten in matrix form by using the notation introduced in Section
III.1

\displaystyle\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\underline{g}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(t\right)=\underline{g}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s},

(38a)

\displaystyle\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(t\right)\underline{g}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s}\underline{g}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}.

(38b)

The discussion of how to use
\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}
neglecting the full dynamical form of the e–p vertex has inspired several of works that,
starting from Ref.
[
17
]
up to very recent Ref.
[
3
,
18
,
16
]
, have attempted to provide a rigorous basis to the static
screening assumption.
These works are based on two different approaches: the variational properties of the phonon self–energy
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
and the
down–folding from the Keldysh contour to the real time axis
[
16
]
.

In order to simplify the notation of this section I consider the homogeneous limit. This is particularly simple to
introduce in the present, momentum resolved, notation. Indeed it corresponds to neglect all microscopic spatial dependencies such that,
for example, in Eq. (
12
)
g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)\rightarrow g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
.

In this case Eq. (
27
) becomes

\displaystyle 2\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=g_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)+\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}.

(39)

I further assume
g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
so that
g_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
and
\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
.

By using Eq. (
39
) it is simple algebra to show that

\displaystyle\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\frac{g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}{1-V_{\mathbf{q}}\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)}.

(40)

If now we use Eq. (
40
) to write the phonon self–energy we get

\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\\
g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}^{2}\left[\frac{\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)-V_{\mathbf{q}}|\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)|^{2}}{|1-V_{\mathbf{q}}\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)|^{2}}\right].

(41)

From Eq. (
41
) it easily follows that

{\rm Im}\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right]=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}^{2}\left[\frac{{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)\right]}{|1-V_{\mathbf{q}}\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)|^{2}}\right]=\\
=|\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)|^{2}{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)\right],

(42)

Eq. (
42
) is, clearly, an exact rewriting of the
imaginary part
of the retarded phonon self–energy. The proof of Eq. (
40
) in the
out–of–equilibrium regime by down–folding the Keldysh expression to the real time axis can be found in Ref.
[
35
,
16
]
.
In Ref.
[
16
]
Stefanucci and Perfetto use Eq. (
42
) to demonstrate that the static limit can be taken without introducing double counting errors.
In Ref.
[
4
]
it was demonstrated, instead, that when the whole self–energy is considered, and not only its imaginary part, the statically screened
approximation can be obtained from a many–body diagrammatic expansion only at the price of including double counting terms.

The two works do not contradict each other as they represent two alternative ways of obtaining the statically screened limit of the
imaginary part
of the phonon
self–energy. Still the problem is how to give a precise estimation of the error introduced when we approximate

\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\sim\left.\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}
.
Here I also observe that, as discussed in Section
III.2
,
{\rm Im}\left[\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right]
is not enough to calculate the quasi–phonon solution of the
Dyson equation. The real part is also needed and, thus, we need an approach that treats real and imaginary parts on the same level.

IV.1
Variational properties of the phonon self–energy

The approach of Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
is in this direction, as the aim of these works is to use the variational freedom of the
self–energy (real and imaginary part) to find an expression that minimizes the error introduced by the static screening. The general idea is borrowed from
DFPT that is, indeed, a variational theory based on the extension of Hohenberg–Kohn theorem to density
perturbations
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. The DFPT variational concept
have been extended, in Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
, to the phonon
self–energy by using its (only formal
[
39
]
) analogy with the DFPT adiabatic dynamical matrix.

In the following I show how dynamical corrections emerge naturally from the variational
analysis of
\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
, in the same spirit of Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
.
I start by noticing that it is possible to define the phonon–induced perturbation of the electronic density,
\Delta n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{e}+\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}.

(43)

In Eq. (
43
)
\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{e}
is the purely electronic density and
\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}
its modification induced by the atomic perturbation
caused by an atomic shift along the normal mode
\left({\mathbf{q}}\lambda\right)
. From
linear–response we know that, if the e–p interaction is weak, we can approximate

\displaystyle\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{p}\left(\omega\right)\approx\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\chi_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}.

(44)

If we use Eq. (
44
) in Eq. (
39
) we get

\displaystyle\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}.

(45)

From Eq. (
45
) it easily follows that, within linear–response,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)}{\delta\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}}=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\delta_{{\mathbf{q}}{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}.

(46)

From Eq. (
46
) it is evident that, under the constrain given by Eq. (
44
) the linear variation of the self–energy with respect to the density variation
is not zero. Keeping this in mind Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
consider a more general functional of the
\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{p}\left(\omega\right)

and
\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)
obtained by observing that

\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\epsilon^{-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+V_{\mathbf{q}}\chi_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\\
g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+V_{\mathbf{q}}\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+V_{\mathbf{q}}\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}.

(47)

Let’s now add and remove
\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}V_{\mathbf{q}}
to Eq. (
39
)

\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)+\\
-\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}V_{\mathbf{q}}\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}.

(48)

At this point Eq. (
47
) is applied again on the first term on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
48
)

\displaystyle\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left[g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}V_{\mathbf{q}}\right]\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\left[g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+V_{\mathbf{q}}\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}\right]-\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}V_{\mathbf{q}}\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}.

(49)

Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
introduce, now, a new functional
\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)=\left[g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+\rho\left(\omega\right)V_{\mathbf{q}}\right]\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\left[g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}+V_{\mathbf{q}}\rho\left(\omega\right)\right]-\rho\left(\omega\right)V_{\mathbf{q}}\rho\left(\omega\right),

(50)

where, now,
\rho\left(\omega\right)\neq\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}
and, thus, it does not respect Eq. (
44
). It follows the
\mathcal{F}

is a functional of
\rho\left(\omega\right)
and, independently, of
\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)
. It easily follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\delta\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)}{\delta\rho\left(\omega\right)}\right|_{\rho\left(\omega\right)=\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}}=\left.V_{\mathbf{q}}\left[\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)-\rho\left(\omega\right)\right]\right|_{\rho\left(\omega\right)=\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{p}}=0,

(51a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{2}\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)}{\delta\rho^{2}\left(\omega\right)}=V_{\mathbf{q}}\left[\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)V_{\mathbf{q}}-1\right].

(51b)

From Eq. (
51
) it follows that, if we evaluate Eq. (
50
) at
\rho\left(\omega\right)\sim\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega=0\right)\right|_{p}
we get

\displaystyle\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)\sim\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{SS}+V_{\mathbf{q}}\left[\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)V_{\mathbf{q}}-1\right]\left[\rho\left(\omega\right)-\left.n_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega=0\right)\right|_{p}\right]^{2}.

(52)

In Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
Eq. (
52
) is used to state that, indeed, if we take a static approximation for the
phonon induced density the error we introduce in the self–energy is second order in the density error. In Eq. (
52
) we have introduced the
doubly statically–screened self–energy:

\displaystyle\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{SS}=g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left.\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}\left.\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s}+\left(\left.\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}\right)^{2}\Delta\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right).

(53)

The coefficient of the second order in Eq. (
52
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{2}\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)}{\delta\rho^{2}\left(\omega\right)}=-V_{\mathbf{q}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right),

(54)

where
\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)=1-V_{\mathbf{q}}\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)
. As
\chi^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\sim q^{2}
and
V_{\mathbf{q}}\sim\frac{1}{q^{2}}
it follows that

\lim_{{\mathbf{q}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{0}}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)=O\left(1\right)
and, consequently

\displaystyle\lim_{{\mathbf{q}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{0}}}\frac{\delta^{2}\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)}{\delta\rho^{2}\left(\omega\right)}=\infty.

(55)

We can further work out Eq.(
51
b) in order to better evaluate the coefficient of the divergence appearing in Eq. (
55
).
Indeed we observe that (see Eq. (
78
))
V_{\mathbf{q}}\sim\frac{g^{2}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}{\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}}
. This implies

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\rho,\chi^{0}\right]\left(\omega\right)}{\delta\rho^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\sim\frac{4\pi}{\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}q^{2}}\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{BB}.

(56)

In Eq. (
56
) I have introduced the bare–bare self–energy

\displaystyle\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{BB}=g^{2}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\chi^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right).

(57)

In general the screening (even if adiabatic) reduces the strength of
g_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}
. This means that we expect

\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{BB}\gg\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{SS}
.

Eqs. (
55
)–(
56
) demonstrate that, within the variational approach prposed by Ref.
[
17
,
3
,
18
]
, the second order coefficient
diverges when
{\mathbf{q}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{0}}
with a prefactor larger of the 0
th
order.
In practice, therefore, Eq. (
52
) cannot justify
a priori
the use of a doubly statically–screened self–energy. We need a more
reliable and accurate criterion that I am going to introduce in the next sections.

V
A dynamical perturbative expansion

In Ref.
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
C.Falter and collaborators have introduced a general renormalization theory of the electronic density reponse
that allows to separate the dielectric function into a part which contains the relevant degrees of freedom plus a renormalizing term. Here I follow the same
strategy to derive a perturbative expansion of
\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)
around its static limit.

In order to proceed we start from Eq. (
27
) written in the reference basis
{\mathbf{I}}_{i}\equiv\left(n_{i},m_{i},{\mathbf{k}}_{i}\right)
. We split the electronic response function as

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)=\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}+\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}\left(\omega\right).

(58)

In Eq. (
58
)
\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}=\underline{\underline{\chi}}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(t_{2}=t_{1}+0^{+}\right)
is the adiabatic electronic response function. We use, now, Eq. (
58
) to expand the
inverse dielectric matrix based on the identity

\displaystyle\left[\underline{\underline{A}}-\underline{\underline{B}}\right]^{-1}=\underline{\underline{A}}^{-1}+\underline{\underline{A}}^{-1}\underline{\underline{B}}\left[\underline{\underline{A}}-\underline{\underline{B}}\right]^{-1}.

(59)

Thanks to Eq. (
59
) we get

\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)=\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}+\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)=\\
\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}+\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}.

(60)

We can use now Eq. (
60
) to derive an equation of motion for
\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}}
starting from
\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}\right|_{s}
. Indeed from Eq. (

)
and Eq. (

\displaystyle\left[\underline{\underline{1}}-\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\right]\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s},

(61)

Eq. (
61
) demonstrates that it is possible to write a formal relation between the screened electron–phonon vertex and its
statically screened limit written. We can now define the dynamical electron–phonon vertex matrix
\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)

so that

\displaystyle\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s},

(62)

with

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left[\underline{\underline{1}}-\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\right]^{-1}.

(63)

Eq. (
63
) admits a
formal
Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\right]^{m},

(64)

with
\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
.
Eq. (
64
) is formally exact but meaningful only if the expansion terms are small enough. In practice this means that
it is possible to replace the full dynamical dependence with a statically screened interaction if and only if

\displaystyle\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\ll\underline{\underline{1}}.

(65)

Eq. (
65
) define the necessary conditions to use a statically screened electron–phonon interaction.

We can now use Eq. (
64
) to define the
n
–th order approximation to the phonon self–energy:

\overrightarrow{\Pi}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{g}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}^{T}\biggl\{\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)+\\
+\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\biggr\}\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s},

(66a)

and

\overleftarrow{\Pi}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}^{T}\biggl\{\left[\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right]^{T}\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}+\\
+\left[\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n-1}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right]^{T}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)\biggr\}\underline{g}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}.

(66b)

Thanks to Eq. (
66
) we can rewrite Eq. (
27
) as

\displaystyle\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left[\overrightarrow{\Pi}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)+\overleftarrow{\Pi}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right].

(67)

V.1
Adiabatic limit and the doubly statically screened approximation

From Eqs. (
62
)–(
64
) it follows that

\displaystyle\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}^{n=0}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\underline{\underline{\Gamma}}^{n=0}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}=\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{\pm{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}.

(68)

This means that at lowest order in the expansion, Eq.(
66
a) reduces to

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\Pi}^{n=0}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\left[\underline{g}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right]^{T}\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s}\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s}=\overleftarrow{\Pi}^{n=0}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}=\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}.

(69)

We see that Eq. (
69
) corresponds to the adiabatic and static limit of the phonon self–energy.

If we move now to the 1
st
order in
\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}_{\mathbf{q}}^{0}\left(\omega\right)
we obtain

\overrightarrow{\Pi}^{n=1}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}+\\
+\underline{g}^{T}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left[\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s}\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}+\underline{\underline{1}}\right]\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{g}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}

(70)

Now we observe that

\displaystyle\left[\underline{\underline{1}}+\left.\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\right|_{s}\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}\right]=\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}.

(71)

Eq. (
71
) can be verified order by order by expanding
\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}
in powers of
\underline{\underline{V}}_{\mathbf{q}}
. If we now use Eq. (
71
) in Eq. (
70
)
and use (see Eq. (
24
))
\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}=\left[\left.\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}\right|_{s}\right]^{T}
, we finally get

\overrightarrow{\Pi}^{n=1}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)=\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}+\left[\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{-{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}\right]^{T}\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\omega\right)\left.\underline{\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\right|_{s}=\\
=\left.\Pi_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)\right|_{SS}.

(72)

Eq. (
72
) demonstrates that the statically double screened
phonon self–energy corresponds to the first order in the Taylor expansion of
\overrightarrow{\Pi}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
in powers of

\Delta\underline{\underline{\chi}}^{0}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\omega\right)
.

Eq. (
72
) represents an alternative derivation of Eq. (
52
) with the crucial difference that Eq. (
66
) allows to calculate exactly all corrective
terms that, in Eq. (
52
), are embodied in, the potentially diverging, second order term. This divergence appears now explictly as the case where the
formal Taylor expansion that defines
\overleftarrow{\overrightarrow{\Pi}}^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}\lambda}\left(\omega\right)
does not converge.

What we need now is to evaluate the corrections to Eq. (
72
), via Eq. (
66
), to see if they are important and under which conditions.

VI
The three–dimensional homogeneous electron gas

Eq. (
66
) provides a formal and valid way to evaluate the validity of the statically screened approximation by calculating in practice the different orders
of the perturbative expansion. As this is very difficult to do in realistic materials and, in order to identify some general physical aspects of the problem, I
consider here the case of an exactly solvable model. This will allow us to estimate the importance of the perturbative terms of Eq. (
66
). The model is based
on the well known homogeneous electron gas (HEG). The HEG is used a basic tool in several textbooks
[
13

33

and works. The
reason is that it is possible to derive exact properties that can provide valuable insights in the physics of the electron–phonon problem.

The definition of phonons and electron–phonon interaction in the HEG is complicated by the fact that, commonly, the atoms are replaced with a positively
charged jell of atoms
[
33

. In this case phonons are introduced as sound waves of the ionic plasma whose
whose modes have a vanishing energy when
{\mathbf{q}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{0}}
. However in solids it is the static screening of the dynamical matrix
that produces vanishing phonon frequencies in the zero momentum limit. Thus a proper definition of phonons in the HEG must be derived by starting from a periodic array of atoms that, if the electrons are
assumed to be uniformly distributed, will produce just a single, bare, Drude–like atomic mode.

VII
Conclusions

Several conclusions can be drawn from the results presented in this work. Many years of literature, grounded on e–p models or indirect theoretical and
numerical results, have instilled the idea that the electron–phonon vertex can be replaced, in the Hamiltonian, with a statically screened expression. This
assumption is currently used in most of the calculations of physical phenomena caused by the electron–phonon interaction and it is also coded in many
ab–initio

codes.

In this work I have provided a mathematically accurate, and formally exact, way of including retardation effects via a dynamical vertex correction

\Gamma_{e-p}\left(\omega\right)
. The properties of this vertex function have been discussed in general and calculated in practice in an exactly solvable periodic
homogeneous electron gas. The model has been used to perform a detailed assessment of the performance of the different approximations corresponding to
different orders in the Taylor expansion of
\Gamma_{e-p}\left(\omega\right)
. The commonly used bare–screened and screened–screened approximations are obtained at the
lowest order of this perturbative expansion.

The results presented here unequivocally demonstrate what could be expected on the basis of physical intuition:
dynamical screening effects impose a combined description where the phonon scatters with a mixture of
single–particle and plasmonic excitations. If the energy of these excitations approaches the phonon frequency and, in general, the energy range spanned by the vertex
function, dynamical effects can be so large to be impossible to be described perturbatively. In this case any description based on statically screened vertexes
is bound to fail.

In order to have a tool to evaluate the importance of dynamical corrections,
I have proposed a simple form of
\Gamma_{e-p}\left(\omega\right)
written in terms of simple bare–screened and screened–screened calculations. Simulations that are possible
to do even
ab–initio
. This simplified vertex function can be used to evaluate the impact of the dynamical screening in realistic materials.

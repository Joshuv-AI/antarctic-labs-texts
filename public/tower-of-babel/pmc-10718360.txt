Modified Pedicled Internal Mammary Osteomyocutaneous Chimeric Flap for Salvage of Mandibular Reconstruction
Authors: Samantha D. Morin, Arvind U. Gowda, Kongkrit Chaiyasate
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Summary:The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap is a powerful option for head and neck reconstruction in patients not suitable for free tissue transfer. In this article, the senior author’s (K.C.) technique for flap elevation is described in the context of a patient with mandibular osteoradionecrosis resulting in severe crossbite and trismus after multiple failed attempts at reconstruction with free tissue transfer. The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap was chosen as it offered intraoral lining, extraoral soft tissue, and vascularized bone for mandibular reconstruction without requiring free tissue transfer. The flap dissection as well as the risks, benefits, and indications for this flap are described herein. The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap is a technically complex reconstructive option reserved for situations in which conventional methods have been exhausted. It offers an eloquent solution for patients who otherwise may have no options.

Takeaways

Question: What is an alternative option for head and neck reconstruction patients after multiple free flap failure, disease recurrence, osteoradionecrosis, or the vessel-depleted neck?

Findings: The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap offers a versatile salvage option for head and neck reconstruction.

Meaning: This flap offers an eloquent solution for patients who otherwise may have no options.

INTRODUCTION

Although free tissue transfer remains the gold standard for complex head and neck reconstruction, salvage options after multiple free flap failures, disease recurrence, or osteoradionecrosis (ORN) pose a difficult challenge.1,2 In this article, the use of a modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap is described to manage a patient presenting with ORN in the setting of multiple previously failed free flaps.

The patient was diagnosed with rhabdomyosarcoma of the soft palate with regional metastasis at age 12. He was treated with wide excision, adjuvant chemotherapy, neck dissection, and radiotherapy. Thirty years later, he developed radiation-induced squamous cell carcinoma of the maxilla requiring maxillectomy, bilateral neck dissections, and further radiotherapy. Numerous attempts at reconstruction failed, including a radial forearm flap, two anterior lateral thigh flaps, and a fibula flap. He presented to the senior author with osteoradionecrosis of the mandible, pathological fracture, extruding hardware, severe crossbite, trismus, and a frozen left neck.

Excision of the diseased mandible and soft tissues was planned, necessitating reconstruction with intraoral soft tissue, vascularized bone, and extraoral soft tissue. CT angiography of the neck showed moderate external carotid stenosis with no patent branches. Given the patient’s history of failed free tissue transfer and lack of recipient vessels, pedicled options providing all components required for reconstruction were considered.

The pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap was first described by Barreiro et al in 2017 for salvage after failed head and neck reconstruction.3 This technique was modified by the senior author to avoid median sternotomy while providing all necessary flap components. The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap was harvested through removal of the second through sixth costal cartilages to expose the internal mammary artery and veins. A skin paddle centered over the sixth rib was designed to be used for the intraoral defect and mandibular reconstruction. An intercostal branch from the internal mammary pedicle was dissected to this component of the flap. The oral muscosa defect was 5 × 6 cm, and the mandibular defect after excision of the diseased bone was 3.5 cm. The osteocutaneous portion of the flap was designed to match these dimensions. The vertical rectus abdominis myocutaneous segment was then dissected and isolated at the distal aspect of the pedicle. The flap was then rotated into the defect with care to avoid kinking or occluding internal mammary vessels. The buccal defect was then resurfaced with the skin paddle designed over the sixth rib, the rib was fixated to span the mandibular defect after establishment of occlusion, and the rectus abdominis myocutaneous segment was used to resurface the skin of the face and neck (Figs. 1–3). Several further procedures were performed to optimize the result in this patient, including debulking the rectus myocutaneous paddle, reconstructing the midface and lower lid with a paramedian forehead flap, and using an Abbe flap for upper lip reconstruction and a fascia lata sling for facilitating oral competence.

DISCUSSION

A need exists for innovative alternatives to conventional reconstruction in patients who have multiply recurrent or failed head and neck reconstruction with ORN. The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap offers a versatile option for patients with these complex reconstructive challenges. Indications for this flap include a lack of recipient vessels or adequate donor sites for free tissue transfer, and clotting disorders or other diseases that preclude microvascular surgery.

The pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap described by Barreiro el al in 2017 used a median sternotomy to access the internal mammary vessels.3 In the modified technique described here, median sternotomy is avoided by excising costal cartilage. Although this may be tedious, it facilitates identification and dissection of an intercostal branch supplying the osteocutanous component of the flap. It also facilitates visualization of the distal extent of the internal mammary artery where it becomes the superior epigastric artery and enters the rectus muscle. Through the parasternal window created by excision of costal cartilage, the arch or rotation into the head and neck region becomes straightforward for the multiple components of the flap, allowing for geometrical flexibility with inset.

Limitations of the described flap preclude its use as a primary option in head and neck reconstruction. Firstly, it requires tedious dissection, which extends operative time beyond that of conventional free flap reconstruction. Secondly, the donor site sacrifices a rectus muscle, and pedicle dissection destabilizes the chest, requiring prophylactic platting. Lastly, limited bone stock is available for mandibular reconstruction with use of rib. Workhorse flaps such as the free fibula still offer the best primary option for mandibular reconstruction. Specific indications for use of the modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap include previously failed attempts at free tissue transfer to the head and neck, salvage after failed reconstruction, clotting disorders that preclude microvascular surgery, and use in the vessel-depleted neck.

The modified pedicled internal mammary osteomyocutaneous chimeric flap offers an additional and reliable option for patients who may have exhausted alternatives. The malleable individual tissue components away from radiation fields and pedicle length to both the rectus myocutaneous and osteocutanous costal components of the flap permits a three-dimensional functional reconstruction in the head and neck without the need for microsurgical anastomosis.

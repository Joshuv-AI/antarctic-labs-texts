Entanglement produced by pairs of superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams
Authors: Menwuyelet Melaku, Deribe Hirpo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We examined a two-mode coherent and subharmonic light beam within a cavity that is coupled to a two-mode vacuum reservoir through a single port mirror. Utilizing the solutions of the quantum Langevin equations, we developed Q-functions for the two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams. Subsequently, we calculated the Q-function for the superposed two-mode light beams in question. By leveraging the relevant Q-functions for the superposed cavity light beams, we investigated the quadrature statistics and entanglement. To conduct our analysis, we focused on a quantum system characterized by Gaussian operators with a zero mean. We have identified that the superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams are in a squeezed state, with the squeezing occurring in the plus quadrature. Moreover, our analysis revealed that at steady state and at threshold, the superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic light beams exhibit a maximum squeezing of 25% below the two-mode vacuum-state level. We have also clearly illustrated, using continuous variable entanglement criteria, that the superposed two-mode cavity light beams are entangled at both steady-state and threshold, with the entanglement being manifested in the highly correlated squeezed photons, which show a 75% degree of entanglement. Our analysis, applying Cauchy-Schewarz, Hillary-Zubiary, and Logarithmic Negativity entanglement criteria, further confirmed that the superposed cavity light beams in question are entangled. The Logarithmic Negativity criteria validated the entanglement of the superposed light, indicating a 50% degree of entanglement.

Introduction

Quantum optics deals mainly with the quantum properties of the light generated by various optical systems such as lasers and with the effect of light on the dynamics of atoms. The quantum properties of light are largely determined by the states of the light mode, and the well-known quantum states of light are the number state, the chaotic state, the coherent state and the squeezed state1.

Of all states of light, the coherent states are the most important and arise frequently in quantum optics. Not only can they be an accurate representation of the light produced by a stabilized laser operating well above threshold, but many of the techniques for studying the properties of light rely on the properties of the coherent states2.

One of the most fundamentally interesting and intriguing phenomena associated with the composite quantum system is entanglement. In recent years, the topic of continuous-variable entanglement has received a significant amount of attention as it plays an important role in all branches of quantum information processing. The efficiency of quantum information schemes highly depends on the degree of entanglement3,4.

Entanglement is a physical phenomenon that occurs when pairs or groups of particles cannot be described independently; instead, a quantum state may be given for the system as a whole. Measurements of physical properties such as position, momentum, spin polarization, etc. performed on entangled particles are found to be appropriately correlated3–5.

The preparation and manipulation of these entangled states that have non-classical and non-local properties lead to better understanding of the basic quantum principles. In other words, it is a well-known fact that a quantum system is said to be entangled if it is not separable. That is, if the density operator for the combined state cannot be described as a combination of the product density operators of the constituents5–7.

A non-degenerate subharmonic generator at and above threshold has been theoretically predicted to be a source of light in an entangled state8–11. Recently, the experimental realization of the entanglement in two-mode subharmonic generator has been demonstared by Zhang et al12. They have found that a nondegenerate subharmonic generator can generate light in an entangled state employing the entanglement criteria for a bipartite continuous variable state12,13.

A detailed analysis of the quadrature squeezing and photon statistics of the light produced by a subharmonic generation has been done by a number of authors. It has been shown theoretically and subsequently confirmed experimentally that subharmonic generation produces a light that has a maximum of 50% squeezing below the coherent state level13–15.

Although Einstein, along with his colleagues Podolsky and Rosen (EPR), was the first to recognize the criterion for analyzing entanglement condition for a two-mode light beam16–19, a significant number of works have not been reported on the entanglement properties of a pair of superposed two-mode light beams. In this article, we aim to examine the statistical, squeezing, and entanglement characteristics of superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams. To achieve this, we utilize the quantum Langevin equations and operator dynamics to derive the Q-function for the two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light. Subsequently, we calculate the Q-function for the superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams. Utilizing the relevant Q-function, we investigate the squeezing and entanglement properties of the superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams.

In steady state (when time approaches infinity), the above equation takes the form66\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} (\Delta c_\pm )^2=2\mp \frac{2\gamma }{\kappa \pm 2\gamma }. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}A steady-state is the time-independent state that an open quantum system evolves towards and remains in, under constant driving and dissipation. Finding the steady state is crucial because it describes the long-term, stable operating conditions of devices like lasers, parametric oscillators, and atomic clocks.

In steady state, the quadrature variance no longer changes over time, which means that it is time-independent.

The equation discussed above indicates that the first term reflects the quadrature variance of the two-mode vacuum state. This suggests that the superposed two-mode system under examination is in a squeezed state, with squeezing taking place in the plus quadrature.

What are the main experimental limitations and noise sources?

Theoretical models often assume a perfect, isolated system: a single atom coupled infinitely strongly to a single mode of a lossless cavity, with no external influences. Real experiments are a constant battle against the environment to approximate these conditions. Real cavities are leaky; photons escape through imperfect mirrors or transmission lines.

Decoherence is the key limitation. Quantum superpositions and entanglement are sensitive and fade over time, defined by the cavity Photon Lifetime \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(T_{cavity} = 1/ \kappa )$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\kappa$$\end{document} signifies the rate of cavity decay. Photons can be lost through processes such as absorption, scattering, or transmission through imperfect mirrors. This limits the interaction time between the atom and photon.

In practice, executing cavity mode interactions presents significant technical challenges, and various sources of noise and imperfections need to be taken into account. These include photon loss and cavity decay (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\kappa$$\end{document}), as well as finite mirror reflectivity, subpar materials, and coupling to external modes. These factors restrict interaction time and coherent information transfer.

Conclusion

This paper presents an analysis of a superposed two-mode cavity light formed by two-mode coherent and subharmonic light beams. We studied the Q function, quadrature statistics (including quadrature variance and squeezing), and the entanglement criterion (Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality, Hillary Zubiary, Duan et al., and Logarithmic negativity). To perform our analysis, we considered a quantum system with Gaussian operators that have a zero mean. Our results show that the superposed two-mode coherent and subharmonic cavity light beams are in a squeezed state, with squeezing occurring in the plus quadrature. Moreover, our analysis demonstrates that at steady state and at threshold, these beams achieve a maximum squeezing of 25% below the two-mode vacuum-state level. The reduction of the squeezing value to half is caused by the presence of two-mode coherent light. It is understood that two-mode subharmonic light has a maximum squeezing capability of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$50\%$$\end{document}; when it is combined with two-mode coherent light, the squeezing is reduced by half. The impact of two-mode coherent light is not explicitly shown in an equation, but it can be easily discerned from the plots. We have also clearly demonstrated that superposed two-mode light beams are entangled at both steady-state and threshold, with the entanglement being observable in the highly correlated squeezed photons, which have a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$75\%$$\end{document} degree of entanglement. The logarithmic Negativity criterion has provided an entanglement degree of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$50\%$$\end{document}. In most criteria, the subharmonic parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma$$\end{document} and time, t, are observed to have a direct correlation with the increase in entanglement.

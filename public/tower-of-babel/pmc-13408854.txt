Optimizing multi-label image annotation: a hybrid CNN-Transformer deep learning approach
Authors: Chitrapriya Ningthoujam, Tejbanta Singh Chingtham, Dhruba Ningombam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMulti-label image annotation remains a challenging task due to highly imbalanced label distributions, co-occurring semantic concepts, and the presence of rare or noisy annotations. To address these challenges, we present ReSwinNet, a hybrid CNN–Transformer framework that integrates ResNet-50 for fine-grained spatial feature extraction with the Swin Transformer for capturing long-range dependencies through hierarchical self-attention. A token projection mechanism maps multi-scale CNN features directly into Swin-compatible representations, bypassing standard patch embedding to preserve fine-grained spatial context that conventional patch partitioning discards. A frequency-based label pruning strategy further reduces annotation noise and class imbalance by retaining only semantically stable labels, significantly improving training convergence and predictive focus. Experiments are conducted on two benchmark datasets under multiple label settings to ensure transparent and fair evaluation. Under the full 374-label space on Corel-5K, ReSwinNet achieves a macro F1 of 0.491, surpassing 7 of 10 compared state-of-the-art methods and recording the highest recall among all compared models. Under the leakage-free 65-label pruned setting, ReSwinNet achieves a macro F1 of 0.701 and mAP of 0.742, outperforming all compared methods under identical experimental conditions. On IAPRTC-12, ReSwinNet achieves a macro F1 of 0.596 and mAP of 0.629 under the 198-label pruned setting, representing an improvement of over 14 percentage points in mAP relative to the strongest baseline. These results confirm the effectiveness of spatially-aware token fusion combined with frequency-based label filtering for large-scale multi-label image annotation.Keywords: Multi-label, Optimization, Annotation, Imbalance, Thresholding, Swin, Hybrid, ResNet, CNN, Label pruningSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Multi-label image annotation is a fundamental problem in computer vision, where a single image can be associated with multiple semantic categories simultaneously. This task plays a vital role in applications such as image retrieval, scene understanding, content moderation, and medical diagnostics. Accurate annotation not only improves multimedia retrieval systems but also ensures reliable performance across diverse real-world scenarios. Unlike single-label classification, multi-label annotation introduces additional complexity due to the high dimensionality of the label space and dependencies among labels. A key challenge in this domain is label imbalance, where frequent labels dominate the training process while rare labels are underrepresented. This often leads to biased learning, overfitting to dominant categories, and degraded performance on infrequent labels1,2. Su et al.3developed a Visual Contextual Relationship Augmented Transformer (VCRAT) for image captioning, where self-attention-based contextual feature enhancement enhanced the representation of spatial and semantic correlations in images . Recent approaches attempt to address this imbalance through techniques such as data augmentation and resampling4, but these solutions either increase computational costs or fail to generalize across severely imbalanced datasets. This creates a significant barrier to deploying annotation systems in practical environments. Deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs), including ResNet5, are widely adopted for robust spatial feature extraction in annotation tasks6,7. More recently, Vision Transformers (ViTs) and hierarchical variants like the Swin Transformer8 capture long-range dependencies and fine-grained semantic relationships. However, balancing label distributions efficiently remains an open challenge. To address these challenges, we propose the ResNet-Swin Fusion Network (ReSwinNet) shown in Fig. 1 , a unified deep learning framework that integrates threshold-based label pruning with a hybrid CNN–Transformer architecture. Related work

Studies on automatic image tagging have evolved from initial non-deep techniques to sophisticated deep learning models. The background research can be generally divided into non-deep techniques, threshold-centric methods, deep learning strategies, and hybrid models.

Non-Deep Methods Early approaches employed metric learning and dictionary-based embeddings. For example, 2pKNN6 integrates metric learning with multi-label classification, while SLED7 introduces a semantic label-embedding dictionary to capture shared label combinations. Collaborative frameworks9 and sparse reconstruction methods such as 2PKNN-GSR10 further enhanced robustness against noisy or incomplete labels.

Threshold-Based Annotation Thresholding strategies have been widely adopted. CNN-AT11 proposed instance-specific adaptive thresholds within a ConvNet-driven binary relevance framework. Extensions in12,13 used neural ranking and nearest-neighbor reasoning to improve prediction. Earlier non-parametric methods relied on density estimation in the word–image space, but lacked scalability.

Deep Learning Approaches The adoption of CNNs led to major advances in feature learning. CNN-based architectures14,15, pre-trained models16, and hybrid CNN-RNN frameworks17 captured both label dependencies and image–label relevance. Post-processing refinements, such as label correction18,19, improved robustness. Descriptor-driven enhancements included dominant color descriptors20, edge-weighted descriptors21, and VGG-16 retrieval-based models22. Evolutionary methods such as MLFPSO23 optimized feature selection but incurred high computational costs.EAPT24 shows that pyramid-based attention can greatly enhance efficiency and precision in image processing tasks, achieving a balance between global context modeling and local detail retention.25 addresses how multi-label classifications can be achieved using clustering the space of labels to integrate global and local strategies.

Recent Advances and Hybrid Architectures Recent work has emphasized advanced architectures and hybrid frameworks. DMCKN26 incorporated geometric context via multi-order patch connections. A dynamic thresholding method based on Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) was introduced in27 to mitigate label dominance. Semi-supervised frameworks, such as adaptive weighted fusion with co-training28, enhanced robustness under partially labeled data. Similar to Zhang and Tian’s mutual-attention Transformer for anomaly detection29, which demonstrated that attention mechanisms can effectively fuse local and global features, our work also leverages Transformer architectures (Swin) to capture long-range dependencies for improved multi-label annotation, though applied to the challenge of label imbalance rather than anomaly detection. Hybrid CNN–Transformer models have gained attention for combining local and global feature representations. For example, ResNet–SLT–Word2Vec integration5 addressed the semantic gap, while Swin Transformer models8 have been adapted for multi-label annotation, including domain-specific tasks such as content-level annotation of construction imagery30. A more recent contribution, DMVMLC-VT31, addresses the problem of incomplete multi-view multi-label classification by combining autoencoder-based view representation with a completion module for missing views. It further employs contrastive fusion, pseudo-label enhancement, and a label balancing strategy to mitigate the effects of missing or skewed annotations. Graph-based approaches have also been investigated to capture label correlations. A recent research on multi-label semantic sharing in image-to-text retrieval32 utilizes graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to represent semantic connections between labels, consequently improving cross-modal retrieval efficiency. Although these methods effectively model label dependencies, they struggle to capture long-range visual context and may not directly resolve imbalance problems. In contrast, our proposed ReSwinNet utilizes CNN Transformer integration to seize both local and global visual characteristics, while incorporating label pruning to reduce the impact of infrequent or noisy labels.33introduced a unified learning framework that combines low-level and high-level attributes for multi-label identification of ships in intricate environments, showing that merging hierarchical representations improves distinguishing capability in various visual scenarios. Hybrid frameworks have also investigated feature fusion. For instance, a recent investigation into solid waste categorization34 utilizes multi-layer feature integration through the integration of features from EfficientNetB0 and InceptionV3 employing a HyperColumn method, fully connected layers and an enhancer classifier. Although these fusion techniques improve classification precision by utilizing complementary attributes,they are still restricted to CNN backbones and fixed concatenation methods While effective in handling view incompleteness and class imbalance, DMVMLC-VT primarily focuses on multi-view data integration rather than hybrid CNN–Transformer architectures. In contrast, the proposed ReSwinNet integrates statistical label pruning with CNN–Transformer fusion, explicitly addressing both label imbalance and feature integration within an end-to-end framework.

Proposed methodology

The proposed model addresses the challenges of multi-label image annotation by initially reducing the label space complexity and subsequently employing a hybrid deep learning framework for enhanced classification performance as shown in Fig. 2.

Flow of the proposed ResSwin Net model.

The methodology is structured into three core stages: (1) Label Pruning, where infrequent or redundant labels are eliminated to simplify the output space (2) Feature Extraction using ResNet-50, which captures high-level semantic features from the input images and (3) Annotation via Swin Transformer, which exploits hierarchical self-attention to model inter-label dependencies and produce the final multi-label predictions.

Label pruning

The infrequency of specific labels in a dataset can lead to several challenges in machine learning models. Models tend to overlearn frequent labels while neglecting rare ones, resulting in imbalanced learning and potential overfitting. This causes poor generalization on new images, particularly for sparse labels, leading to degraded precision and recall for these labels. As noted in prior works, this imbalance causes models to overfit on prevalent classes while neglecting infrequent ones, severely degrading recall and precision for underrepresented categories. Data preprocessing

For both datasets, frequency-based label pruning was applied to remove infrequent labels and reduce noise. For Corel-5K, the initial frequency-based pruning (Version 1) reduced the label vocabulary from 374 to 84 labels by retaining only those labels whose occurrence frequency met or exceeded the training set mean. A further leakage-free strategy was subsequently applied, computing frequency statistics exclusively on the training split, which reduced the retained label set from 84 to 65 labels (Version 2). For IAPRTC-12, the initial frequency-based pruning (Version 1) reduced the label vocabulary from 291 to 62 labels, retaining only those labels whose occurrence frequency met or exceeded the dataset mean of 281.94. Version 2 adopts a leakage-free strategy with a relaxed pruning threshold computed exclusively on the training set, retaining 198 labels to include categories previously discarded under the stricter Version 1 criterion, thereby enabling evaluation across a broader and more representative label space. It is noted that the direction of change between Version 1 and Version 2 differs between the two datasets: for Corel-5K, the retained label count decreases (84 to 65) due to leakage correction, whereas for IAPRTC-12 it increases (62 to 198) due to a relaxed threshold applied on training data only

To address class imbalance, images were subsampled such that the number of images per retained label approximated the computed average label frequency, ensuring balanced representation across classes and reducing the dominance of high-frequency labels. Specifically, the number of images sampled per label was proportional to its frequency in the dataset, subject to a minimum of 20 and a maximum of 150 samples per class, with a baseline target of 50 samples. This resulted in 3,668 unique training images and 499 test images for the Corel-5K Version 2 configuration, ensuring balanced class representation while preventing high-frequency labels from dominating the training distribution. For IAPRTC-12, the class distribution exhibited severe imbalance, with label frequencies ranging from as few as 1 to as many as 17 images per label. To accommodate this disparity, the sampling bounds were adjusted to a minimum of 50, a maximum of 800, and a baseline of 150 samples per class. This wider range allowed sufficient representation of rare labels while preventing dominant classes from overwhelming the training distribution, ensuring a more balanced learning signal across the 198 retained labels under Version 2. Further, to prevent data leakage, the dataset was first divided into training, validation, and test sets prior to any augmentation, as described in Algorithm 1. Each image was assigned exclusively to a single split, ensuring that no overlap existed across the partitions. Data augmentation was subsequently applied only to the training set. Consequently, no augmented variant of a training image could appear in either the validation or test sets. This strict separation ensures that the evaluation metrics accurately reflect the model’s generalisation capability on previously unseen data.

Threshold optimization

In the prediction stage, we adopted an adaptive thresholding strategy. Instead of applying a fixed threshold to the sigmoid output for all labels, we computed optimal thresholds for each label individually using validation data. These thresholds were selected to maximise the F1-score and the precision-recall balance, making the model more sensitive to both common and rare labels. Model outputs are produced as per-label probability scores via a sigmoid activation. Experimental results on Corel5K dataset

The proposed ReSwinNet was evaluated on the Corel5K dataset, a widely used benchmark for multi-label image annotation. The architecture processes each input image resized to through ResNet-50, extracting deep spatial features and producing an output feature map of size .

The experimental pipeline underwent a two-stage label space refinement to ensure rigorous and leakage-free evaluation, the results of which are summarised in Table 3. In the initial phase (Version 1), label pruning based on frequency thresholding was applied to the merged train and test pool, retaining 84 labels. However, this setting introduced a label selection leakage issue, as label frequencies were computed across both splits rather than exclusively on the training set. Consequently, Version 1 produced inflated performance metrics (P=0.98, R=0.94, F1=0.96, mAP=0.9249), which do not reflect true generalisation ability, as illustrated in Fig. 4. In the corrected leakage-free pipeline (Version 2), label frequencies were computed exclusively on the training set, reducing the retained label vocabulary to 65 labels. This correction resulted in a notable reduction in reported performance (F1=0.7008, mAP=0.7415), which more accurately reflects the model’s ability to generalise to unseen data. The substantial drop between Version 1 and Version 2 confirms that the earlier results were overly optimistic due to leakage. Image-level frequency-proportional sampling was further applied to ensure approximately equal representation per label, addressing the dataset’s inherent class imbalance. All subsequent experiments on Corel-5K are conducted exclusively under the leakage-free Version 2 setting.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed ReSwinNet, a hybrid deep learning architecture that integrates convolutional and transformer-based mechanisms for multi-label image annotation. By combining ResNet-50 for local spatial feature extraction with the Swin Transformer for capturing global contextual relationships, the model effectively bridges the strengths of both paradigms. A key contribution is the token projection mechanism that maps CNN features to transformer-compatible inputs for seamless fusion. Additionally, a label pruning strategy is employed as a pre-processing step to eliminate infrequent and noisy labels, thereby reducing annotation ambiguity and enhancing training stability. This not only improves model convergence but also boosts overall predictive performance by focusing on more semantically meaningful and frequent label categories.

Comprehensive experiments on the Corel-5K and IAPRTC-12 benchmarks demonstrate that ReSwinNet consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches across all evaluation metrics. Under the full 374-label space, ReSwinNet achieves a macro F1 of 0.491, surpassing 7 of 10 compared methods and recording the highest recall among all compared models. Under the leakage-free 65-label pruned setting, ReSwinNet achieves a macro F1 of 0.701 and mAP of 0.742, outperforming all compared methods under identical experimental conditions. On IAPRTC-12, ReSwinNet achieves over 14% higher mAP relative to the strongest baseline under the pruned 198-label setting, highlighting its robustness across datasets of varying scale and label complexity. The results affirm that integrating CNNs with hierarchical self-attention offers a powerful solution for complex multi-label image annotation tasks, particularly under frequency-filtered label settings where annotation noise is reduced and discriminative learning is more tractable. Future work will focus on hierarchical label taxonomy modelling to better capture semantic dependencies among co-occurring annotations, alongside exploring self-supervised pretraining for low-resource and open-vocabulary annotation settings.

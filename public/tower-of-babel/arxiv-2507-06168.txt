“Ideal” Topological Heavy Fermion Model in Two-dimensional Moiré Heterostructures with Type-II Band Alignment
Yunzhe Liu, Anoj Aryal, Dumitru Calugaru, Zhenyao Fang, Kaijie Yang, Haoyu Hu, Qimin Yan, B. Andrei Bernevig, Chao-xing Liu ∗

Abstract
Topological flat bands play an essential role in inducing exotic interacting physics, ranging from fractional Chern insulators to superconductivity, in moiré materials. When topological flat bands possess concentrated quantum geometry, a topological heavy fermion (THF) model was proposed as the starting point to describe the interacting moiré physics. In this work, we propose a design principle for realizing ”ideal” THF model, which can host an exact flat band with ”ideal quantum geometry”, namely the trace of Fubini-Study metric equals to the Berry curvature, in a class of two-dimensional moiré heterostructures with type-II band alignment. We first introduce a moiré Chern-band model to describe this system and show that topological flat bands can be realized in this model when the moiré superlattice potential is stronger than the type-II atomic band gap of the heterostructure. Next, we map this model into a THF model that consists of a localized orbital for ”f-electron” and a conducting band for ”c-electron”. We find that both the flatness and quantum geometry of the mini-bands in this THF model depend on the energy gap between c-electron and f-electron bands at Γ \Gamma which is experimentally controllable via external gate voltages. This tunability will allow us to realize an ”ideal” topological flat band with zero band-width and ”ideal quantum geometry” in this THF model. Our design strategy of topological flat bands is insensitive of twist angle. We also discuss possible material candidates for moiré heterostructures with type-II band alignment.

In this work, we tackle these challenges by proposing a design paradigm for realizing an ”ideal” THF model, in which the topological flat bands with ideal quantum geometry can be achieved by tuning external gate voltages, using moiré heterostructures composed of 2D materials with type-II band alignment. The design principle of the THF model in moiré heterostructures is illustrated in Fig.
1

1

1

1

Since conduction band electrons normally have a smaller effective mass compared to valence band electrons in many semiconducting compounds, we below assume the c-electron is from the conduction bands of part A while the f-electron is from the valence bands of part B. But it should be pointed out that it is also possible to interchange the roles of the conduction and valence bands, namely the c-electron is from the valence band of part B while the f-electron is from the conduction band of part A, when the moiré potential is imposed on part A. To be concrete, we here consider all the low-energy bands around
{\bf\Gamma}
in the atomic Brillouin zone (ABZ). In order to form topological flat bands with c- and f-electrons, two criteria are required: (1) the energy difference between the conduction band minimum (CBM) in part A and the valence band maximum (VBM) in part B at
{\bf\Gamma}
in ABZ is small (a few hundreds of meV) to form a type-II band alignment of the hetero-structure

;

For a small moiré potential in part B, we would expect the periodic bound states for f-electrons to form a trivial flat band. With increasing moiré potential strength, denoted as

\Delta_{0}>0
for both conduction and valence bands in Eq.(
7

In the momentum space, the
\delta
-function-like potential can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{m}}=\Delta_{0}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\alpha}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}}a_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}}^{\dagger}a_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}},

(8)

in the second quantization form, where
\mathbf{k}
is chosen within the first mBZ,
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}
are for any moiré reciprocal lattice vector,
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{G}_{M}
. We note that
\delta
-function potential couples a state at the momentum
\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}
to a state at
\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}
with an equal strength
\Delta_{0}
for any values of
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}
, and this long-range coupling in momentum space will lead to the divergence of the bound state energy under ideal 2D
\delta
-function potential (See SM Sec.C.1
SM
). Therefore, we introduce a momentum cutoff
G_{\Lambda}
,
|{\boldsymbol{g}}|,|{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}|\leq|G_{\Lambda}|
, for the
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime}
summations in the moiré reciprocal space for Eq.(
8
).

This problem for the
\delta
-function-like potential can be solved using the Green’s function formalism
Mahan 2013
, which is discussed in SM Sec.B.2
SM
. In the momentum space, the full Hamiltonian with the
\delta
-function-like morié potential can be written as

\displaystyle H_{mC}(\mathbf{k})=H_{0}(\mathbf{k})+N_{g}\Delta_{0}P_{0}

(9)

where
H_{0}(\mathbf{k})
is a block diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle H_{0}(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{C}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{1})&0&\dots&0\\
0&H_{C}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{2})&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&H_{C}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{N_{g}})\end{pmatrix}.

{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j}
is the moiré reciprocal lattice vectors within the momentum cut-off
G_{\Lambda}
(
j=1,2,...,N_{g}
), and
P_{0}=\left|\phi_{0}\right\rangle\left\langle\phi_{0}\right|\otimes\sigma_{0}
is the projection operator with
\left|\phi_{0}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{g}}}[1,1,...,1]^{T}
and
\sigma_{0}
is the 2-by-2 identity matrix. For this Hamiltonian, the trace of the Green’s function,
G=(E1_{2N_{g}\times 2N_{g}}-H_{mC}(\mathbf{k}))^{-1}
, can be derived as (See SM Sec.C.2
SM
)

\displaystyle Tr(G)=Tr_{\sigma}(\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{k},E))+Tr_{\sigma}\left(\Delta_{0}\sum_{j}\mathbf{g}^{0}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j},E)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}-\Delta_{0}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{k},E)}\mathbf{g}^{0}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j},E)\right),

(10)

where
Tr_{\sigma}
means the trace over 2-by-2
\sigma
matrix on the orbital basis
\alpha=1,2
,
\mathbf{g}^{0}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j},E)=(E\sigma_{0}-H_{C}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j}))^{-1}
is the free Green’s function of
H_{C}
and
\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{k},E)=\sum_{j}\mathbf{g}^{0}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j},E)
. The poles of
Tr(G)
determine the energy spectrum of
H_{mC}
(denoted as
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbf{k})
below).
For any eigen-energy
E_{\eta}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j})
of
H_{C}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j})
, one can prove the limit

\lim_{E\rightarrow E_{\eta}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j})}Tr(G)\approx-\frac{1}{\Delta_{0}}

(See SM Sec.C.2
SM
).
Therefore, the eigen-energy of
H_{C}(\mathbf{k}+{\boldsymbol{g}}_{j})
for any
j
is
not
the pole of
Tr(G)
. From Eq.(
10
), the poles of
Tr(G)
can only come from
\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}-\Delta_{0}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{k},E)}
and thus the eigen-energy spectrum
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbf{k})
of
H_{mC}
is determined by

\displaystyle Det(\sigma_{0}-\Delta_{0}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{k},\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbf{k})))=0.

(11)

III
The THF model

III.1
Mapping to THF model

The band inversion for moiré Chern-band model with either the first-shell approximation or periodic
\delta
-function-like potential can be captured by a THF model with the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}=\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|<\Lambda_{c}}\left[\alpha\mathbf{k}^{2}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta Ef^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}}+(\beta k_{+}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}}+h.c.)\right],

(13)

where
k_{\pm}=k_{x}\pm ik_{y}
, the annihilation operator
c_{\mathbf{k}}
is for c-electrons of CB1 while
f_{\mathbf{k}}
is for f-electrons of VB1. The parameters
\alpha,\Delta E,\beta
can be extracted from the moiré Chern-band model in Eq.(
1
) for both types of moiré potentials. Particularly, the expressions for the relation between
\alpha,\Delta E,\beta
in the THF model and Chern-band model parameters such as
M
and
\Delta_{0}
can be derived analytically for the periodic
\delta
-function-like potential, as shown below in Eq.(
14
). The
\alpha
term describes the dispersion of c-electrons, the
\Delta E
term describes the energy difference between the c-electron and f-electron at
\mathbf{\Gamma}
, and the
\beta
term describes the hybridization between c-electron and f-electron. We have set the energy of the conduction band bottom to be zero. The full THF model should also include the interaction terms
\hat{H}_{HF}=\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}+\hat{H}_{int}
with
\hat{H}_{int}
describing the screened Coulomb interaction previously derived in Ref.
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2024a
. We focus on the derivation of
\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}
in this section.

For the first-shell potential approximation of moiré potential, the mapping to the THF model can be constructed via the maximally localized Wannier function method
Marzari et al. 2012
;
Souza et al. 2001
, as detailed in SM Sec.B.2
SM
. To do that, we first construct the maximally localized Wannier function
|W^{(1)}_{\mathbf{R}}\rangle
for
J_{z}=3/2
orbital at the
1a
position, as shown in Fig.
2
(d), and its projection to the VB1 and CB1 is shown by the sizes of red dots in Fig.
2
(c). We also consider the wavefunction of the conduction band bottom at
\mathbf{\Gamma}
, denoted as
|\phi_{1,\mathbf{k}=0}\rangle
, and project the full Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{mC}(\mathbf{k})
into the subspace spanned by
|W^{(1)}_{\mathbf{R}}\rangle
and
|\phi_{1,\mathbf{k}=0}\rangle
, leading to the THF model
\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}
in Eq.(
13
) with the parameters
\alpha,\beta,\Delta E
extracted numerically in Eq.(S.B17) of SM Sec.B.2
SM
.

A similar procedure can be applied to the periodic
\delta
-function-like moiré potential to map the moiré Chern-band model to the THF model. Due to the simplicity of periodic
\delta
-function-like potential, we can solve all the parameters in the THF model via the Löwdin perturbation theory
Winkler 2003
, given by

\displaystyle\alpha=B+D;\Delta E=\mathcal{E}_{0}-M-\Delta_{0};\beta=\frac{AN_{v}}{\mathcal{E}_{0}+M},

(14)

where

is a normalization factor of the localized f-electron wavefunction
f^{\dagger}_{0}\left|0\right\rangle

and
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is the binding energy in Eq.(

depends on both

and
\Delta_{0}
, so it can be tuned by an external gate voltage experimentally. In Fig.
3

13
) with the parameters directly extracted from the perturbation theory in Eq.(
14
), and the dispersion fits well with the full numerical calculations for CB1 and VB1. The momentum-space Berry curvature distribution of the topological flat band in Fig.

\mathbf{\Gamma}

III.2
Topological Flat Band with Ideal Quantum Geometry in THF model

The THF Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}
in Eq.(
13
) can possess an exact flat band with ”ideal quantum geometry” when the condition
\Delta E=\beta^{2}/\alpha
is satisfied. To see that, we introduce
the mode operator

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{1,\mathbf{k}}=(\begin{array}[]{cc}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}&f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\end{array})u_{1,\mathbf{k}};\quad u_{1,\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha k_{+}\\
\beta\end{array}\right)

so that the Hamiltonian (
13
) becomes

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}=\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|<\Lambda_{c}}\left[E_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{1,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{1,\mathbf{k}}+(\Delta E-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha})f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}}\right],

(19)

where
E_{\mathbf{k}}=\alpha k^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}
. When
\Delta E=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}
, the second term in Eq.(
19
) vanishes so
\hat{H}_{HF}^{0}
only depends on a single mode operator
\gamma^{\dagger}_{1,\mathbf{k}}
, but not on the other mode

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{0,\mathbf{k}}=(\begin{array}[]{cc}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}&f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\end{array})u_{0,\mathbf{k}};\quad u_{0,\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\beta\\
\alpha k_{-}\end{array}\right)

which is orthogonal to
\gamma^{\dagger}_{1,\mathbf{k}}
. Thus, the solution of Eq.(
III.2
) corresponds to an exact flat band with zero energy (See Fig.
1
(e)).
Since the eigen-wavefunction in Eq.(
III.2
) only contains
k_{-}
except a normalization factor, this exact flat band realizes ”ideal quantum geometry” (See SM Sec.B.3
SM
)

\displaystyle Tr(g)=\Omega_{xy}=\frac{2\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}}{(\beta^{2}+\alpha^{2}k^{2})^{2}},

(24)

where
\Omega_{xy}=-2Im(\langle\partial_{k_{x}}u_{0,\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{k_{y}}u_{0,\mathbf{k}}\rangle)

is the Berry curvature and
g_{ij}=Re\left(\langle\partial_{k_{i}}u_{0,\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{k_{j}}u_{0,\mathbf{k}}\rangle-\langle\partial_{k_{i}}u_{0,\mathbf{k}}|u_{0,\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle u_{0,\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{k_{j}}u_{0,\mathbf{k}}\rangle\right)
(
i,j=x,y
)
is the Fubini-Study metric tensor of the flat band
Brody and Hughston 2001

We notice that
\Delta E
describes the energy difference between c-electrons and f-electrons at
\mathbf{\Gamma}
and is related to the atomic band gap
M
in Eq.(
14

1

away from 1, the bandwidth of the flat band increases and the ratio

departs from the ideal quantum geometry (
\chi=1
), as shown in Fig.
3
(c). The energy separation between the exact flat band (
\gamma_{0,\mathbf{k}}
) and the dispersive band (
\gamma_{1,\mathbf{k}}
) is
\Delta E=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}
at
{\bf\Gamma}
. We estimate
\Delta E\approx 40
meV with
\beta\approx
0.2 nm
\cdot
eV and
\alpha\approx
1 nm
2
\cdot
eV. Therefore, depending on the energy order of the Coulomb interaction energy

and in the heavy fermion regime when

V
Conclusion and Discussion

In this work, we propose a general scheme to realize the THF model with topological flat bands using 2D moiré heterostructures with type-II band alignment. The ability to continuously tune the atomic band gap
M
via external gate voltages will allow us to systematically explore different regimes of the THF model discussed in Ref.
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2024a
. The exact flat band with ”ideal quantum geometry” was first theoretically discussed for the tractable ”chiral” limit of the tBG model
Tarnopolsky et al. 2019
;
Ledwith et al. 2020
, in analog to the Laughlin wavefunctions of the fractional quantum Hall effect, for the realization of fractional Chern insulator phases. However, the chiral limit requires zero tunneling of the AA sites in the tBG model, while the ratio of the tunneling between the AA sites and AB/BA sites in realistic tBG is around
0.7\sim 0.8

Koshino et al. 2018
;
Carr et al. 2019
, far away from the chiral limit.
Our design principle of the ”ideal” THF model is achievable for a variety of 2D material heterojunctions and semiconductor hetero-structures, as exemplified by our model studies of Tl
2
Se
2
/Zn
2
Te
2
moiré heterojunction, and HgTe/CdTe quantum wells with patterned dielectric substrates (Sec.B, C and D in SM
SM
). A systematic search of 2D material hetero-structures with type-II band alignment is beyond the scope of this work. Although we focused on the materials with their low-energy bands around
{\bf\Gamma}
in the ABZ, this scheme of forming heterostructures with type-II band alignment is also possible for other 2D materials with low-energy bands around other high symmetry momenta, such as TMD
Tong et al. 2017
;
Zhu et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2024
. We note that heavy fermion physics has recently been revealed in TMD moiré systems, MoTe
2
/WSe
2

Zhao et al. 2023
, while its potential topological property
Guerci et al. 2024
remains unexplored in experiments.
The ”ideal” topological flat band found in our THF model makes our proposed platform appealing for exploring interacting topological physics such as fractional Chern insulator phase
Wang et al. 2021
, topological Kondo physics
Dzero et al. 2016
;
Dzero et al. 2010
;
Checkelsky et al. 2024
;
Guerci et al. 2024
;
Zhao et al. 2023
;
Neupane et al. 2013
, as well as superconductivity
Song and Bernevig 2022
;
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2024a
;
Stewart 1984
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Liu and Bernevig 2025
;
Wang et al. 2024
.
Narrow gap heterostructures with type-II band alignment also provide a platform to explore the physics of exciton insulators and exciton condensate
Ma et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2020
;
Gu et al. 2022
;
Nguyen et al. 2025
.

Abstract
. We show that locally bounded, local weak solutions to certain nonlocal, nonlinear diffusion equations modeled on the fractional porous media and fast diffusion equations given by ∂ t u + ( − Δ ) s ​ ( | u | m − 1 ​ u ) = 0 for 0 < s ⁡ < 1 and m > ​ 0 \displaystyle\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}(|u|^{m-1}u)=0\quad\mbox{ for }\quad 0<s<1\quad\text{and}\quad m>0 are locally Hölder continuous. We work with bounded, measurable kernels and provide the corresponding L l ​ o ​ c ∞ → C l ​ o ​ c 0 , α L^{\infty}_{loc}\rightarrow C^{0,\alpha}_{loc} De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory for the equation via a delicate analysis of the set of singularity/degeneracy in a geometry dictated by the solution itself and a careful analysis of far-off effects. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Problem

We are interested in studying the equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-L\phi(u)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T],

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 2
) is bounded and open,
T>0
, and the nonlinearity
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is of the following type

(1.2)

\displaystyle\phi(X)=|X|^{m-1}X\quad\mbox{ for }\quad m>0

and the operator
L
is modeled on the fractional Laplacian and given by the principal value integral

\displaystyle Lv(x,t)=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v(y,t)-v(x,t))K(x,y;t)\,dy

with
n\geq 2
for a kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
which is measurable and satisfies

(1.3)

\lambda|x-y|^{-n-2s}\leq K(x,y;t)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-n-2s}

for two fixed positive constants
\lambda,\Lambda>0
. Here and throughout the paper, we always assume that

\displaystyle s\in(0,1).

1.2.
The Context

The equation
Eq.

1.1
is modelled on the fractional diffusion equation (FDE) given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}(|u|^{m-1}u)=0\quad(s\in(0,1),m>0),

which is itself a nonlocal analogue of the classical diffusion equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta(|u|^{m-1}u)=0.

The nonlinear local diffusion equation finds applications in modelling density-dependent diffusive behaviours, such as plasma diffusion across magnetic fields
[
OD73
,
DGNP77
]
and phenomena described by scaling laws and self-similarity as detailed in the theory of the porous medium equation
[
Bar96
,
V0́7
]
. On the other hand, nonlocality, introduced via the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, is crucial to capture phenomena involving long-range interactions or anomalous diffusion, relevant in areas such as mechanics and physics involving constraints
[
Lio72
]
, stochastic hydrodynamic limits
[
GLP99
]
, and broader frameworks of nonlocal diffusion processes
[
Caf12
]
.

The nonlocal FDE has been a subject of extensive mathematical investigation. Foundational studies established the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the fractional porous medium equation
[
dPQRV11
,
dPQRV12
]
, explored existence and uniqueness for measure data
[
GMP15
]
, analysed distributional solutions
[
dTEJ17
]
, and investigated fundamental solutions and asymptotic behaviour like Barenblatt profiles
[
V1́4
]
, including optimal estimates for fast diffusion
[
VV15
]
. There have also been results demonstrating Hölder continuity for singular parabolic cases
[
KL11
]
using extension arguments, deriving quantitative local and global estimates
[
BV14
]
, establishing higher regularity and classical solutions
[
VdPQR17
]
, studying regularity for equations with rough kernels
[
dPQR16
]
or singular diffusion
[
dPQR18
]
for equations posed in the full space, analysing propagation effects like infinite speed
[
BFRO17
]
, investigating transformations of self-similar solutions
[
SdTV15
]
, and considering the Cauchy problem in bounded domains
[
BII23
]
. Numerical analysis of the equation has also progressed, with works developing robust numerical schemes based on semigroup theory
[
CdTGG20
]
and finite difference methods, including theoretical analysis and experimental validation
[
dTEJ19
,
dTEJ18
]
.

We are interested in studying local regularity along the lines of the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory. More specifically, we are interested in local Hölder bounds for locally bounded weak solutions to
Eq.

1.1
. In the linear setup when
m=1
, analogous regularity results were previously established: a priori estimates for integro-differential operators provided regularity for the elliptic case
[
Kas09
]
, and regularity theory, including Hölder continuity, was developed for the linear parabolic case
[
CCV11
]
. For nonlocal parabolic equations with degeneracy and singularity dependent on a fractional ’
gradient
’ term (like the fractional p-Laplacian), Hölder regularity was obtained in
[
APT
,
Lia24
]
. On the other hand, equation (
1.1
) exhibits singularity for the fast diffusion case (
m<1
) and degeneracy for the porous media case (
m>1
) - both directly at the level of the solution
u
itself.

1.3.
Main Ideas

The key issues in studying the local regularity of
Eq.

1.1
are:

1.

The singularity/degeneracy of the term
|u|^{m-1}
when
u\approx 0
.

2.

The nonlocal nature of the operator wherein far off values of the solution
u
can affect its regularity in a small open set.

3.

The absence of a De Giorgi isoperimetric inequality for fractional Sobolov spaces which is crucial in the local setup.

We note that even if the solution is assumed to be bounded, the singularity/degeneracy can still cause problems since the solution can be close to
0
. To deal with this, we separate our analysis into two phases - one where the solution is close to zero and one where it is away - and employ a variant of the switching radius argument. More precisely, we first study the solution close to zero and show that the equation forces a reduction in the supremum as we zoom in; further, the reduction continues as long as the solution stays close to zero. In particular, if the solution is always close to zero, then we are done. Thus we now switch to the case where the solution is away from zero in some cylinder and at this point, it turns out that we are almost in the linear setup from which we can conclude oscillation decay. Thus, the analysis boils down to studying the solution
u
in the set
\{u\approx 0\}
. We do this via suitably scaling the cylinders to match the singularity/degeneracy in terms of the solution itself i.e. we work in a geometry dictated by the solution. These ideas are not new, and our work draws much from previous works on the subject such as the study of
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity for (elliptic)
p-
Laplace equation
[
DiB83
]
and
C^{\alpha}
regularity for porous media systems
[
Lia21
]
; these ideas themselves, of course, go back to De Giorgi’s seminal work
[
DG57
]
.

For the non-locality, one could impose global boundedness but we are interested in local regularity, thus it is not an appropriate assumption to make. Furthermore, unlike the linear case, one cannot go from global boundedness to local boundedness with the addition of a source term due to the nonlinearity of
\phi
.

Finally, the De Giorgi isoperimetric inequality is an important tool in regularity theory for local equations; however, such an isoperimetric inequality in the fractional case necessarily comes with a jump term
[
APT23
, Theorem 4]
. In the absence of the inequality, one must usually resort to logarithmic estimates to control jumps, but such estimates are currently unavailable in the nonlocal nonlinear parabolic setup. To ameliorate the situation, we get a "good term" or the isoperimetric term in the energy estimate itself - the idea first appeared in
[
CCV11
]
; we make use of a variant as in
[
APT
]
. With the isoperimetric inequality at hand, we are left to deal with the non-locality of the operator itself. We do this via tail alternatives - which say that if the tail is not too large then we get the estimate we want. Finally, we need to make sure that the tails are in fact not too large and we are able to get this by zooming further and further away followed by delicately adding up the contributions.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\varphi\partial_{t}\llbracket u\rrbracket_{h}\,dx\,dt+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\llbracket(\phi(u)(y,t)-\phi(u)(x,t))K(x,y;t)\rrbracket_{h}(\varphi(y,t)-\varphi(x,t))\,dy\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\leq(\geq)\int_{\Omega}u(x,0)\cdot\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\frac{a}{h}}\varphi(x,a)\,da\,dx.\end{split}

Proof.

If we take the test function

to
Eq.

1.1
, then since

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\iint_{\Omega_{T}}u\partial_{t}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muh\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\,dx\,dt+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\llbracket(\phi(u)(y,t)-\phi(u)(x,t))K(x,y;t)\rrbracket_{h}(\varphi(y,t)-\varphi(x,t))\,dt\,dx\,dy\\

where in the above inequality ‘

3.2

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega_{T}}(\partial_{t}\llbracket u\rrbracket_{h})\varphi\,dx\,dt+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\llbracket(\phi(u)(y,t)-\phi(u)(x,t))K(x,y;t)\rrbracket_{h}(\varphi(y,t)-\varphi(x,t))\,dy\,dx\,dt\\

Remark 3.6

We justify the above remark as below. First of all, the following observation is necessary.

Lemma 3.7
.

Choose an open interval
I
an a compact set

such that

. Note that if

, then

\displaystyle|\phi(u)(x,t)-\phi(u)(y,t)|

\displaystyle=m|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\int_{0}^{1}|\xi u(x,t)+(1-\xi)u(y,t)|^{m-1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C(m)(|k|+M)^{m-1}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|

\displaystyle\eqsim(|k|+M)^{m-1}|(u(x,t)-k)_{+}-(u(y,t)-k)_{+}|,

where we write

.
In addition, we have that if

\displaystyle|(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{+}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{+}(y,t)|

\displaystyle=|\phi(u)(x,t)-\phi(k)|

\displaystyle=m|u(x,t)-k|\int_{0}^{1}|\xi u(x,t)+(1-\xi)k(y,t)|^{m-1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C(m)(|k|+M)^{m-1}|u(x,t)-k|

\displaystyle\eqsim(|k|+M)^{m-1}|(u(x,t)-k)_{+}-(u(y,t)-k)_{+}|.

Using this inequalities, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{K}\frac{|(u(x,t)-k)_{+}-(u(y,t)-k)_{+}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\leq C\int_{K}\int_{K}\frac{|\phi(u)(x,t)-\phi(u)(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}},

with the constant

now also depending on

; thus
(u-k)_{+}\in L^{2}(I;W^{s,2}(K))
.
∎

4.
De Giorgi Lemmas

For
R>0
,
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
Q=K_{R}(x_{0})\times(T_{1},T_{2}]\subset\Omega_{T}
. To incorporate the singularity/degeneracy of the equation
Eq.

1.1
in our analysis we need to work in a geometry intrinsic to the equation. We do this via working in cylinders which are scaled in space for the fast diffusion case and with cylinders scaled in time for the porous media case. The scaling factor will be chosen depending on
|u|
- the quantity responsible for the singularity/degeneracy.

For a scaling factor
\theta>0
we define

\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})+\mathfrak{Q}_{\rho}(\vartheta)=K_{\vartheta\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\rho^{2s},t_{0}]

and

\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\rho}(\theta)=K_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta\rho^{2s},t_{0}]

with
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\rho>0
. We omit the vertex
(x_{0},t_{0})
when it is clear from the context.

With
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\rho,\theta>0
, we work with a cylinder

\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\rho}(\theta)\subset Q\subset\Omega_{T}

and let
M>0
be a number such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle 2M\geq\esup_{Q}|u|.

Before giving the main result, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
Eq.

4.1
hold. With either

(4.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}:=\max\left\{u,M/4\right\}\quad\text{and a number}\quad k\in\left[M/2,2M\right]

or

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}:=\min\left\{u,-M/4\right\}\quad\text{and a number}\quad k\in\left[-2M,-M/2\right],

we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&M^{m-1}|(\widetilde{u}-k)_{\pm}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k)_{\pm}(y,t)|^{2}\\
&\quad\leq 4^{m}((\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{\pm}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{\pm}(y,t))((u-k)_{\pm}(x,t)-(u-k)_{\pm}(y,t)).\end{split}

Proof.

We only prove that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&M^{m-1}|(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}\\
&\quad\leq 4^{m}((\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t))((u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t))\end{split}

in case of
Eq.

4.2
, since the other three cases are proved similarly. By the symmetry of both sides of
Eq.

4.5
and the monotonicity of
\phi
, we can always assume

\displaystyle u(y,t)\geq u(x,t).

Now we divide the proof into three cases.

Case 1: When
u(x,t),u(y,t)\leq\frac{M}{4}
.
In this case,
\widetilde{u}(x,t)=\widetilde{u}(y,t)\equiv\frac{M}{4}
. Then we have

\displaystyle|(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(y,t)|\equiv 0

so that the left-hand side of
Eq.

4.5
is zero. Also, by
Eq.

3.11
in Remark
3.5
, the right-hand side of
Eq.

4.5
is nonnegative, thus
Eq.

4.5
is true.

Case 2: When
u(x,t),u(y,t)>\frac{M}{4}
.
In this case, we guarantee
u(x,t),u(y,t)>0
. Since
\frac{M}{4}<u(x,t)\leq 2M
,
\widetilde{u}(x,t)\equiv u(x,t)
, and
\widetilde{u}(y,t)\equiv u(y,t)
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle M^{m-1}|(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}\eqsim|u(x,t)|^{m-1}((u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t))^{2}

with the implicit constant
c=4^{m}
. Now we claim the following:

(4.7)

\displaystyle|u(x,t)|^{m-1}|(u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|\leq(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t).

To show the above claim, we divide the case into three subcases.

Subcase 1: When
u(x,t),u(y,t)>k
.
In this case, both sides of
Eq.

4.7
is zero, so
Eq.

4.7
is trivially true.

Subcase 2: When
u(y,t)>k
and
u(x,t)\leq k
.
Note that
|u(x,t)|\leq k
from
u(x,t)>0
. Then together with
(u-k)_{-}(y,t)\equiv(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t)\equiv 0
and
m\geq 1
, we estimate

\displaystyle|u(x,t)|^{m-1}|(u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|

\displaystyle=|u(x,t)|^{m-1}(k-u(x,t))

\displaystyle=k|u(x,t)|^{m-1}-\phi(u)(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(k)-\phi(u)(x,t)

\displaystyle=(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t).

Subcase 3: When
u(x,t),u(y,t)\leq k
.
In this case, recalling
u(y,t)\geq u(x,t)\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|u(x,t)|^{m-1}|(u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|

\displaystyle=|u(x,t)|^{m-1}(u(y,t)-u(x,t))

\displaystyle\leq\phi(u)(y,t)-\phi(u)(x,t)

\displaystyle=(\phi(k)-\phi(u)(x,t))-(\phi(k)-\phi(u)(y,t))

\displaystyle=(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t).

Considering
Subcase 1
–
Subcase 3
, we obtain
Eq.

4.7
. Now with
Eq.

4.6
, we get

\displaystyle M^{m-1}|(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m}|u(x,t)|^{m-1}|(u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m}((\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t))|(u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|

\displaystyle=4^{m}((\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t))((u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)),

where for the last equality we have used
(u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)\geq 0
since
u(x,t)\leq u(y,t)
.

Case 3: When
u(x,t)\leq\frac{M}{4}
and
u(y,t)>\frac{M}{4}
.
In this case, note that
u(x,t)<0
is possible. First, observe that

\displaystyle u(y,t)\equiv\widetilde{u}(y,t)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{u}(x,t)=\tfrac{M}{4}

and so

\displaystyle M^{m-1}|(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}=M^{m-1}|(k-\tfrac{M}{4})-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}.

Now we consider two subcases.

Subcase 1: When
u(y,t)\geq k
.
In this case, since
(u-k)_{-}(y,t)\equiv 0
and
k\geq\frac{M}{4}\geq u(x,t)
, together with
m\geq 1
we obtain

\displaystyle M^{m-1}|(k-\tfrac{M}{4})-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m}k^{m-1}(k-\tfrac{M}{4})(k-\tfrac{M}{4})

\displaystyle=4^{m}(\phi(k)-k^{m-1}\tfrac{M}{4})(k-\tfrac{M}{4})

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m}(\phi(k)-\phi(u)(x,t))(k-u(x,t))

\displaystyle=4^{m}((\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t))((u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)).

Subcase 2: When
\frac{M}{4}<u(y,t)<k
.
Then together with
u(x,t)\leq\frac{M}{4}
and
m\geq 1
, we estimate

\displaystyle M^{m-1}|(k-\tfrac{M}{4})-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=4^{m-1}(\tfrac{M}{4})^{m-1}(u(y,t)-\tfrac{M}{4})^{2}

\displaystyle=4^{m-1}\left((\tfrac{M}{4})^{m-1}u(y,t)-(\tfrac{M}{4})^{m-1}\tfrac{M}{4}\right)(u(y,t)-\tfrac{M}{4})

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m-1}\left(\phi(u)(y,t)-\phi(u)(x,t)\right)(u(y,t)-u(x,t))

\displaystyle=4^{m-1}((\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k))_{-}(y,t))((u-k)_{-}(x,t)-(u-k)_{-}(y,t)).

Considering
Subcase 1
and
Subcase 2
, we obtain
Eq.

4.5
in
Case 3
. Therefore, combining
Case 1
–
Case 3
, we get
Eq.

4.5
in any case.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For
m\geq 1
, let

be a local weak super(sub)-solution in

in the sense of Definition

\displaystyle\esup_{t\in(-\theta\rho^{2s},0)}\int_{K_{\rho}}\xi^{2}(u-k)_{-}^{2}(x,t)\,dx

For

\tau+\delta\theta\rho^{2s}=0
. For
i=0,1,\ldots
we set

\displaystyle k_{i}=2M-\frac{\mathfrak{f}M}{2}-\frac{\mathfrak{f}M}{2^{i+1}},\quad\rho_{i}=\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho}{2^{i+1}},\quad K_{i}=K_{\rho_{i}},\quad Q_{i}=K_{i}\times I_{i}=K_{i}\times(-\delta\theta\rho_{i}^{2s},0].

We note that on the set
\{u>k_{i}\}
we have

\displaystyle M<u\leq 2M.

We choose the cutoff function
\xi
such that
0\leq\xi\leq 1
,
\xi\equiv 1
on
Q_{i+1}
with its support away from
\partial_{P}Q_{i}
with

\displaystyle|\nabla\xi|\leq C\frac{2^{i}}{\rho}\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial_{t}\xi|\leq C\frac{4^{is}}{\delta\theta\rho^{2s}}.

Therefore, we may apply Lemma
3.4
to get

(5.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\esup_{t\in I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\xi^{2}(u-k_{i})^{2}_{+}\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\min\{\xi^{2}(x,t),\xi^{2}(y,t)\}\frac{((\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t))((u-k_{i})_{+}(x,t)-(u-k_{i})_{+}(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,dt\\
&\quad\leq\mathfrak{P}+\mathfrak{Q}+\mathfrak{R},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}=C\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\max\{|u|^{m-1}(x,t),|u|^{m-1}(y,t)\}\max\{(u-k_{i})_{+}^{2}(x,t),(u-k_{i})_{+}^{2}(y,t)\}\frac{|\xi(x,t)-\xi(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,dt,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}=C\int_{I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}(u-k_{i})_{+}^{2}|\partial_{t}\xi^{2}|\,dx\,dt,

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}=C\int_{I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\xi^{2}(u-k_{i})_{+}(x,t)\,dx\left(\underset{x\in\mbox{supp}(\xi(\cdot,t));t\in I_{i}}{\esup}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{K_{i}}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dt.

We choose the support of
\xi
appropriately as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
and estimate the terms similarly. We get

\displaystyle\underset{x\in\mbox{supp}(\xi(\cdot,t));t\in{I_{i}}}{\esup}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{K_{i}}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2^{i(n+2s)}\esup_{t\in(T_{1},T_{2}]}\int_{K_{R}\setminus K_{i}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(l))_{+}(y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy+2^{i(n+2s)}\esup_{t\in(T_{1},T_{2}]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K_{R}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(l))_{+}(y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy.

Recalling that
|u|\leq 2M
and the mean value theorem shows that

\displaystyle\esup_{t\in(T_{1},T_{2}]}\int_{K_{R}\setminus K_{i}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\leq\esup_{t\in(T_{1},T_{2}]}\int_{K_{R}\setminus K_{i}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(u-\tfrac{\mathfrak{f}M}{2}-\tfrac{\mathfrak{f}M}{2^{i+1}}))_{+}(y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\leq C\frac{\mathfrak{f}M}{\rho^{2s}}M^{m-1}\leq C\frac{\mathfrak{f}M^{m}}{\rho^{2s}}.

For the second term, we note that
\phi(k_{i})\geq 0
globally, and so we may estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K_{R}}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K_{R}}\frac{\phi(u)_{+}(y,t)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\leq\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{2s}\frac{\mathrm{Tail}^{m}(u_{+},Q)}{\rho^{2s}}.

Therefore enforcing

(5.28)

\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{2s}{m}}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{+},Q)\leq\mathfrak{f}M,

we get

\displaystyle\underset{x\in\mbox{supp}(\xi(\cdot,t));t\in I_{i}}{\esup}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\leq C2^{i(n+2s)}M^{m-1}\frac{\mathfrak{f}M}{\rho^{2s}}.

Hence, we find

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\leq CM^{m-1}\frac{2^{i(n+2s)}}{\rho^{2s}}(\mathfrak{f}M)^{2}|A_{i}|,

where
A_{i}=\{u>k_{i}\}
. Next we have,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}\leq C\frac{4^{is}}{\theta\delta\rho^{2s}}(\mathfrak{f}M)^{2}|A_{i}|,

Similarly, using
M<u\leq 2M
we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}\leq CM^{m-1}\frac{4^{i}}{\rho^{2s}}(\mathfrak{f}M)^{2}|A_{i}|.

Moreover, for the left-hand side of
Eq.

5.27
, using Lemma
4.1
we estimate

\displaystyle M^{m-1}\int_{I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\min\{\xi^{2}(x,t),\xi^{2}(y,t)\}\frac{|(\widetilde{u}-k_{i})_{+}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k_{i})_{+}(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\min\{\xi^{2}(x,t),\xi^{2}(y,t)\}\frac{((\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(x,t)-(\phi(u)-\phi(k_{i}))_{+}(y,t))((u-k_{i})_{+}(x,t)-(u-k_{i})_{+}(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,dt

using that
|u|\leq 2M
,
k_{i}\in[\tfrac{M}{2},2M]
and
m-1\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}:=\max\left\{u,M/4\right\}.

Putting together the above estimates we arrive at

\displaystyle\esup_{t\in I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\xi^{2}(\widetilde{u}-k_{i})^{2}_{+}\,dx+M^{m-1}\int_{I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}\min\{\xi^{2}(x,t),\xi^{2}(y,t)\}

\displaystyle\frac{|(\widetilde{u}-k_{i})_{+}(x,t)-(\widetilde{u}-k_{i})_{+}(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,dt

(5.29)

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathfrak{f}M)^{2}|A_{i}|\frac{2^{i(n+2)}}{\rho^{2s}}\left(2M^{m-1}+\frac{1}{\theta\delta}\right).

Now, we observe

(5.30)

\displaystyle\frac{(\mathfrak{f}M)^{2}}{4^{i+2}}|A_{i+1}|

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{Q_{i}}((\widetilde{u}-k_{i})\xi)_{+}^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{{Q}_{i}}((\widetilde{u}-{k}_{i})\xi)_{+}^{2\frac{n+2s}{n}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{n}{n+2s}}|A_{i}|^{\frac{2s}{n+2s}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{I_{i}}\int_{{K}_{i}}\int_{{K}_{i}}\frac{|((\widetilde{u}-{k}_{i})\xi)_{+}(x,t)-((\widetilde{u}-{k}_{i})\xi)_{+}(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,dt\right)^{\frac{n}{n+2s}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\esup_{t\in I_{i}}\int_{K_{i}}|((\widetilde{u}-{k}_{i})\xi)^{2}_{+}(x,t)|\,dx\right)^{\frac{2s}{n+2s}}|A_{i}|^{\frac{2s}{n+2s}}.

where we used the Sobolov embedding as in (
2.3
).

Arguing as in earlier proofs, we get

\displaystyle Y_{i+1}\leq Cb^{i}\left(\frac{M^{1-m}}{\theta\delta}\right)^{\frac{-2s}{n+2s}}\left(2+\frac{M^{1-m}}{\theta\delta}\right)Y_{i}^{1+\frac{2s}{n+2s}}\quad\text{for}\quad Y_{n}=\frac{|A_{n}|}{|Q_{n}|}.

Therefore

\displaystyle Y_{n}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{provided}\quad Y_{0}\leq C\frac{M^{1-m}}{\theta\delta}\left(2+\frac{M^{1-m}}{\theta\delta}\right)^{-\frac{n+2s}{2s}}.

Recalling that
\theta=M^{1-m}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{2s}{m}}\mathrm{Tail}(u;Q)\leq\pi^{\frac{1}{m}}M.

Thus with
\kappa
from Proposition
5.9
,

\displaystyle\esup_{Q_{\rho/2}(\nu\theta)}|u|\leq 2M(1-\kappa).

We now set

(6.3)

\displaystyle M_{0}=M,\quad M_{1}=(1-\kappa)M\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta_{1}=M_{1}^{1-m}.

Choose a
0<\lambda\leq c
such that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\frac{c}{2}\nu^{\frac{1}{2s}}(1-\kappa)^{\frac{m-1}{2s}}.

We set

(6.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}=\rho,\quad Q_{0}=Q_{\rho_{0}}(\theta_{0}),\quad\rho_{1}=\lambda\rho_{0}\quad\quad Q_{1}=Q_{\rho_{1}}(\theta_{1})

Then the choice of
\lambda
enforces
Q_{1}\subset Q_{\rho/2}(\nu\theta)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\esup_{Q_{1}}|u|\leq 2M_{1}.

Suppose that for
i=1,\ldots,l-1
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{i}=\lambda^{i}\rho_{0},\quad M_{i}=(1-\kappa)^{i}M_{0},\quad\theta_{i}=M_{i}^{1-m}\quad Q_{i}=Q_{\rho_{i}}(\theta_{i})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\esup_{Q_{i}}|u|\leq 2M_{i},

and we are close in zero in
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{l-1}
i.e. for every
i=1,\ldots,l-1
we have

\displaystyle|\{u\leq M_{i}\}\cap Q_{i}|>\nu|Q_{i}|\quad\text{and}\quad|\{u\geq-M\}\cap Q_{i}|>\nu|Q_{i}|.

We further restrict
\lambda
by

(6.6)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2s}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-\kappa)^{m}.

for the forthcoming computation. To get a reduction in
Q_{l}
we must now ensure that the far off effects are small. Indeed, by decomposing into successive annuli with respect to the radii
\rho_{i}
and using the bound on
u
in
Q_{i-1}
, we may compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}^{m}(u,Q_{i})

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{i}^{2s}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{M_{j-1}^{m}}{\rho_{j}^{2s}}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{i}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(1-\kappa)^{(i-j+1)(-m)}\lambda^{2s(i-j)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{T}}M_{i}^{m}.

where
C_{\mathfrak{T}}>1
is a universal constant. Thus we may satisfy the tail condition by further restricting
\lambda
by

(6.7)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2s}\leq\frac{\pi}{C_{\mathfrak{T}}}

Therefore, from (
6.4
),(
6.6
) and (
6.7
), choosing
\lambda>0
small enough so that

(6.8)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2s}\leq\min\left\{\frac{(1-\kappa)^{m}}{2},\frac{\pi}{C_{\mathfrak{T}}},\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^{2s}\nu(1-\kappa)^{m-1}.\right\},

we can find a sequence of cylinders
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{l}
such that if we are away from
0
in
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{l-1}
then for
j=1,\ldots,l
we have

\displaystyle\esup_{Q_{j}}|u|\leq 2M\left(\frac{\rho_{j}}{R}\right)^{\alpha_{0}}\quad\mbox{ for }\quad\alpha_{0}=\ln_{\lambda}(1-\kappa),

where
\rho_{j}=\lambda^{j}R
,
M_{j}=(1-\kappa)^{j}M
,
\theta_{j}=M_{j}^{1-m}
and
Q_{j}=Q_{\rho_{j}}(\theta_{j})
. Therefore, if we always stay close to
0
, then we are done - we have shown that solutions that are small are Hölder. We now proceed to study the case where the solutions are away from
0
.

6.1.2.
Away from
0
(
m\geq 1
)

Suppose that
l
is the first index such that

is away from
0
in
Q_{l}
. Then either

\displaystyle|\{u\leq M_{l}\}\cap Q_{l}|\leq\nu|Q_{l}|\quad\text{or}\quad|\{u\geq M_{l}\}\cap Q_{l}|\leq\nu|Q_{l}|

(6.9)

\mathrm{Tail}^{m}(u;Q_{l})\leq CM_{l}\left(1+\frac{\lambda^{2s}}{(1-\kappa)^{m}}+\cdots+\left(\frac{\lambda^{2s}}{(1-\kappa)^{m}}\right)^{l}\right)\leq 2CM_{l}.

Therefore by Lemma
4.2
and Remark
4.4
we have

\displaystyle|u|\geq\frac{1}{2}M_{l}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\quad Q_{\tfrac{\rho_{l}}{2}}(\theta_{l}).

By construction, we also have

\displaystyle\esup_{Q_{l}}|u|\leq 2M_{l}.

We now perform a change of variables as follows:

\displaystyle X=\frac{x}{\rho_{l}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad S=\frac{t}{\theta_{l}\rho_{l}^{2s}}

and define

\displaystyle U(X,S)=\frac{1}{M_{l}}u(\rho_{l}X,\theta_{l}\rho_{l}^{2s}S)\quad\mbox{ where }\quad(X,S)\in Q_{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then

(6.10)

\frac{1}{2}\leq|U|\leq 2\quad\mbox{ in }\quad Q_{1/2}.

and we observe that
U

(6.11)

\partial_{t}U+{L}\phi(U)=0\quad\text{in }Q_{1/2}.

Finally setting
W=\phi(U)
and using the bound (
6.10
) we find a
\beta
such that

\beta(W)\sim W

and

\partial_{t}\beta(W)-L^{\prime}W=0

where

K^{\prime}(X,Y)\sim|X-Y|^{-n-2s}.

We note that we now have ‘elliptic’ coefficients in time and a linear operator in space. It is well known how to handle ‘elliptic’ and more general degenerate coefficients in time
[
Par23
,
Par15
]
and so we may run linear arguments for the nonlocal part in the spirit of
[
CCV11
]
to get oscillation decay:

\eosc_{Q_{r}}W\leq Cr^{\alpha_{1}}

for universal constants
C>0
and
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
. Note that the usual estimate would come with a
|W|_{\infty;Q_{1/2}}
and a tail term on the right but we can estimate them from above by universal constants due to (
6.9
) and (
6.10
). Using (
6.10
) once again and going back to the original variables we get

\eosc_{Q_{r}(\theta_{l})}u\leq CM_{l}\left(\frac{r}{\rho_{l}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}

(with a different
C>0
and universal). Therefore combining the close to
0
and away from
0
estimates and using that
m>1
we arrive at

\eosc_{Q_{r}(\theta)}u\leq CM\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}

for
\alpha=\min\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\}
.

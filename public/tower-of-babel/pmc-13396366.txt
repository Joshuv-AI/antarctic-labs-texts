A V2X communication resource allocation method based on graph neural networks and deep reinforcement learning
Authors: Wenhong Yu, Xinran Yang, Shuo Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
High frequency vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication in the Internet of Vehicles (IoV) leads to severe spectrum collisions and limited system capacity. Meanwhile, safety information transmission requires V2V communication with high transmission success rate. This paper proposes a spectrum and power allocation method based on the integration of graph neural networks (GNNs) and dueling double deep-Q network (D3QN) reinforcement learning to address the above problems. Specifically, this method first constructs a graph with V2V communication links as nodes and the interference relationships between different V2V links as edges. Then, it utilizes GNN to extract low-dimensional features of graph nodes for characterizing the interference relationships between links. Finally, by using the learned low-dimensional features combined with local observations of V2V links, the Double Deep Q-Network mitigates Q-value overestimation by separating action selection from value evaluation, and decouples state value and action advantage through a dual-branch network structure. This design optimizes spectrum allocation and power selection, improves the information transmission success rate of V2V links, and reduces interference to vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) links. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed resource allocation method improves both the successful transmission rate of safety-critical messages over V2V links and the sum capacity of V2I links.

Introduction

With the continuous advancement of smart city construction, the strategic status of Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) technology in the field of intelligent transportation has become increasingly prominent. This technology includes two modes: V2I and V2V, which complement and cooperate with each other in intelligent transportation systems. V2V realizes safety applications through direct vehicle-to-vehicle interaction, which is flexible and real-time but lacks a global perspective; V2I obtains macroscopic road conditions via roadside facilities, featuring strong global performance yet relying on infrastructure coverage. In contrast, the V2X mode coordinatively unifies vehicle dynamic information and roadside sensing data, combining real-time security capabilities with global scheduling advantages. It overcomes the shortcomings of single-mode systems and achieves comprehensive information interaction and sharing among vehicles, roads, pedestrians, and clouds1–3. As the automotive industry accelerates its evolution toward cutting-edge technological frontiers such as autonomous driving, intelligent navigation, and self-parking, the core value and application prospects of V2X have become increasingly compelling. Nevertheless, V2X technology still faces critical bottlenecks in communication performance and system security that urgently demand resolution. In response to these challenges, both academia and industry have proposed a range of V2X solutions that optimize vehicular communication capabilities from various perspectives, with representative examples including Cellular V2X (C-V2X) and New Radio V2X (NR-V2X) developed under the Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) framework. However, the introduction of these emerging technologies has simultaneously given rise to new technical challenges4. To satisfy the wireless communication requirements inherent to V2X scenarios, resource allocation has emerged as one of the central research topics in vehicular network studies. Given that this problem is NP-hard in computational complexity, simultaneously guaranteeing the data transmission success rate of V2V links and the throughput requirements of V2I links in vehicular network environments presents considerable technical difficulty.

To address these resource allocation challenges, researchers have proposed a variety of approaches. Traditional resource allocation methods have typically been investigated through the lens of game theory, auction theory, and evolutionary algorithms. The work in5 investigated a wireless channel resource management method based on Device-to-Device (D2D) communication, where the latency and reliability requirements inherent to V2V communication were reformulated as optimization constraints, and the channel resource allocation problem was modeled as an optimization problem and solved using heuristic algorithms. Vehicular services were categorized into safety-oriented vehicle user equipment and non-safety-oriented vehicle user equipment according to access class, with the objective of maximizing the overall system throughput in6. An energy-sensitive spectrum sharing scheme was proposed in7 to mitigate interference between C-V2X users and users of the Vehicle Ad-hoc Network (VANET), and matching theory was used to investigate the problem of wireless resource allocation, resulting in a dynamic resource allocation algorithm for vehicle networks. A cross-layer adaptive resource allocation algorithm was proposed in8 for packet-based Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) systems.

In recent years, machine learning, and RL in particular, has been introduced into V2X resource allocation research owing to its capacity for autonomous learning and adaptation to complex environments9–14. A comprehensive survey on AI-enhanced cloud-edge-end collaborative networks was presented in15, systematically analyzing their architectures, key enabling technologies, and application scenarios, thereby providing an important research reference for intelligent resource allocation and collaborative optimization in vehicular network environments. The application of RL in the resource allocation domain was pioneered in16 by proposing a distributed decision-making scheme. DRL was applied in17 to investigate resource allocation in uplink Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) systems, designing a discretized multi-agent Deep Q-Network (DQN) structure to reduce output dimensionality while simultaneously incorporating Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) for continuous power allocation decisions. Both DQN and DDPG were applied in18 within an actor-critic framework, employing DQN for discrete channel selection and DDPG for continuous power selection, with the network’s adaptability to dynamically changing environments further enhanced through the incorporation of meta-reinforcement learning. A multi-feature traffic prediction method based on cellular network signaling information was proposed in19, which can provide data support and decision-making guidance for dynamic resource allocation and efficient scheduling in vehicular network scenarios. A heterogeneous graph was constructed in20, adopting a heterogeneous GNN-based learning strategy to furnish prior knowledge for channel and power selection in deep neural networks (DNNs), thereby reducing training complexity when environmental changes necessitate DNN retraining.

In summary, traditional methods suffer from several inherent limitations, including excessive dependence on Channel State Information (CSI), difficulty in achieving global optimality, and an inability to support end-to-end learning and adaptive decision-making. Furthermore, existing DRL-based approaches predominantly focus on improving network architectures to achieve more effective resource allocation strategies, while largely neglecting the consideration of information derived from local vehicular observations.

To achieve more efficient resource allocation and mitigate the adverse effects of limited and inaccurate local observation states of vehicles, this paper proposes a GNN-assisted DRL algorithm. Specifically, two key contributions are made: (1) To address the problem that vehicles can only observe local state information when autonomously selecting resources, a GNN is introduced to extract global topological features. (2) To establish an effective framework for resource allocation, GNN is integrated with D3QN, wherein each V2V link is represented as a node in the graph and treated as an independent agent within the D3QN framework.

Graph model

In this paper, to represent the interference relationships between links in a V2X network, the V2X network is modeled as an undirected weighted graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G=(V, \varepsilon )$$\end{document}. GNN is employed to extract interference features and provide low-dimensional global features for subsequent deep reinforcement learning decision-making.

Node feature aggregation mechanism

The role of GNN is to generate low-dimensional features for each node that capture global interference information through multi-round neighbor message passing and feature aggregation. In the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i-$$\end{document}th iteration, a node first aggregates the feature and edge weight information of all its neighbor nodes, then completes feature update by combining its own initial features with the results from the previous iteration. It introduces nonlinear fitting capability and adapts to the complex interference relationships in V2X scenarios. To balance feature extraction capability and computational latency, this paper sets the total number of GNN iteration rounds to 2 (i.e., a 2-layer GNN structure). After 2 iterations, the low-dimensional feature of node v is finally output.

In conclusion, the resource allocation problem investigated in this paper can be formulated in terms of two objectives: maximizing the aggregate capacity of all V2I links, and guaranteeing the transmission success probability of V2V link payloads. Resource allocation method based on GNN-D3QN

Spectrum and power allocation in V2X vehicular network scenarios is fundamentally a sequential decision-making problem in highly dynamic and uncertain environments. High-speed vehicle mobility, time-varying channel fading, and dynamically evolving network topologies render traditional model-based optimization methods inadequate, as they rely on idealized static channel state information and are incapable of tracking real-time environmental variations. Moreover, these methods suffer from severe the curse of dimensionality in large-scale, high-dimensional state-action spaces, making it difficult to reconcile the conflicting objectives of maximizing V2I throughput and ensuring highly reliable transmission for V2V safety-critical services. DRL integrates the feature extraction capability of DL with the sequential decision-making capability of RL. Without requiring explicit mathematical modeling of the complex V2X environment, DRL directly maps high-dimensional environmental states to optimal resource allocation actions, learns optimal policies through continuous interaction with the environment, and achieves long-term average performance optimization, making it well-suited to the dynamic nature and decision-making demands of vehicular networks.

The GNN-D3QN resource allocation algorithm is a joint optimization model designed to address V2X interference topology, incomplete local observations, and distributed decision-making requirements. Its core principle lies in leveraging the GNN to explicitly model the interference topology among V2V links, transforming high-dimensional local observations into low-dimensional features that incorporate global interference information, thereby resolving the decision bias introduced by conventional DRL approaches that rely solely on local observations. By combining the low-dimensional features extracted by the GNN with local observation information as the input to the D3QN, the algorithm exploits the D3QN’s decoupling of state value and action advantage to suppress Q-value overestimation and enhance distributed decision-making accuracy. This achieves a synergistic mechanism of GNN-based global perception and DRL-based distributed decision-making, jointly optimizing V2I capacity and V2V reliability.

RL is typically formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), which describes the interaction between an agent and the environment. The agent obtains state information from the environment, makes decisions according to a policy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi$$\end{document}, and then executes actions. In return, the environment provides rewards to the agent and updates the current state. Specifically, at each time step t, the agent observes the current state \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s_t$$\end{document} from the environment’s state space and selects an appropriate action \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$a_t$$\end{document} from the action space A based on the existing policy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi$$\end{document}, in order to allocate transmission channels and transmit power for V2V links. The policy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi$$\end{document} is determined by a state–action function, also referred to as the Q-function, denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Q(s_t, a_t)$$\end{document}. In DRL, DL is commonly used to approximate the Q-function so as to adapt to complex environmental dynamics. After the agent takes an action, the environment transitions to a new state \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s_{t+1}$$\end{document} and provides a reward \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R_t$$\end{document} that evaluates the quality of the selected action.

States

The state information mainly includes the vehicle’s observation of the environment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_v$$\end{document} and the low-dimensional features \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$h_v$$\end{document} extracted by the GNN model. We collect the previous channel and power selection information \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Y_{t-1}$$\end{document} of each agent’s neighbors, calculate the ratio \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O_t$$\end{document} of the remaining bits to the total bits required for transmission by the vehicle, and obtain the remaining transmission time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_t$$\end{document} under the delay constraint. Combining this information, the state observed by the agent is formulated as follows.14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} S_v^t = \left\{ h_v^t \Vert x_v^t \Vert Y_{t-1}^v \Vert O_v^t \Vert T_v^t \right\} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Actions

Based on the collected and observed state information, the D3QN network selects an action according to the policy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi$$\end{document}. Considering that the agent needs to jointly complete sub-channel and power allocation, this paper combines these two types of actions to form a unified action space. The action selected by the agent is mapped into two dimensions, corresponding to the decision directions of sub-channel selection and power selection, respectively. Assuming the agent performs this selection operation, its specific decomposition process is as follows:15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} a_{channel} = a_t\mod m \end{aligned}$$\end{document}16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} a_{power} = \left\lfloor \frac{a_t}{m} \right\rfloor \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where mod denotes the modulo operation, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lfloor \rfloor$$\end{document} represents division that rounds down to the nearest integer. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$a_{channel}$$\end{document} is the decomposed sub-channel selection action, while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$a_{power}$$\end{document} denotes the selection of power level.

Rewards

Considering the transmission success rate of safety information over V2V links and the transmission rate of V2I links, the reward function is as follows:17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} R_t = \lambda _p \sum _{m=1}^{M} C_m^{\textrm{V2I}}[m] + (1 - \lambda _p) \sum _{k=1}^{K} \xi _k[m] C_k^{\textrm{V2V}}[m]- \lambda _q (T_0 - T_t), k \in K \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _p$$\end{document} denotes the weight of the V2I link, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _q$$\end{document} denotes the weight of the utilized transmission time.\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_t$$\end{document} represents the remaining time, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_0$$\end{document} represents the transmission delay constraint.

D3QN network structure

We adopt a combination of Double DQN and Dueling DQN as our training and target networks, and the network structure is shown in Figure 2.Fig. 2D3QN Network.

D3QN Network.

The green component represents the fusion of local features with the low-dimensional features extracted by the Graph Neural Network. After the environmental state undergoes feature extraction through the deep neural network depicted in yellow, the resulting feature vector is fed into two parallel computational streams for bifurcated processing of decision-relevant information. The light blue computational stream focuses on state value estimation, characterizing the long-term expected return of the current environmental state itself. Its output is determined solely by the global state and is independent of the specific action taken by the agent, thereby providing a stable value baseline for overall decision-making. The light cyan computational stream is dedicated to computing the relative advantage of each candidate action, quantifying the differences among actions under the current state to highlight the contribution of effective actions to the overall return, thus providing a basis for action selection. This architecture decouples action advantage estimation from state value estimation, structurally alleviating the Q-value overestimation problem prevalent in conventional Q-learning and enhancing the accuracy of value assessment. Finally, in the red fusion module, the state value and action advantage are combined, with the mean advantage subtracted to achieve balanced calibration, yielding unbiased and stable action-value Q-values. The fusion formula is given as follows.18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} Q(s,a;\gamma ,\eta ,\chi ) = V(s;\theta ,\chi ) + \left( A(s,a;\gamma ,\eta ) - \frac{1}{|A|}\sum _{a'} A(s,a',\gamma ,\eta ) \right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma$$\end{document} denotes the parameters of the fully connected layers in the yellow module, while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\chi$$\end{document} represent the parameters of the two streams (green and blue, respectively). Where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V(s;\theta ,\chi )$$\end{document} denotes the state value function, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A(s,a;\gamma ,\eta )$$\end{document} denotes the action advantage function.

Algorithm process

The overall GNN-D3QN model consists of two layers. (1) GNN Layer: a two-layer graph convolutional network with an input dimension of 60 and an output dimension of 20, responsible for extracting global interference features. (2) D3QN Decision Layer: a three-layer fully connected network (300\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rightarrow$$\end{document}200\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rightarrow$$\end{document}120) that takes GNN-extracted features concatenated with local observations as input and outputs Q-values over a 60-dimensional action space. The operational workflow proceeds as follows. First, each V2V link onboard a vehicle generates local observations. Second, the GNN aggregates global interference information and outputs low-dimensional features. Third, the extracted features are fed into the D3QN, which produces channel and power allocation decisions. Fourth, the decisions are executed, rewards are received, and the parameters of both the GNN and D3QN are updated accordingly.Fig. 3Algorithm Schematic.

Algorithm Schematic.

Algorithm 1 supplements the algorithmic flowchart presented in Figure 3. During the training phase, a simulation environment is first constructed, with key parameters randomized, including vehicle speeds, initial positions and movement directions, channel fading characteristics of communication links, interference levels, and initial connection states. This paper adopts a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework: during the training phase, the GNN extracts global interference graph features and completes training in conjunction with a unified global reward function; during the execution phase, each V2V link independently performs decision-making relying on local observations and low-dimensional GNN-extracted features. Furthermore, an experience replay buffer continuously accumulates transition tuples, which are sampled in mini-batches during network updates to train the target network. Upon completion of training, the model is capable of generating efficient resource allocation strategies that satisfy the overall system requirements.

Simulation and analysis

Simulation environment

This study uses simulation settings consistent with16. Simulations are conducted in a single-cell system operating at a carrier frequency of 2 GHz, covering an area of 1,300 m \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document} 750 m, with the number of sub-channels set to 20. The simulation adheres to the Manhattan grid scenario described in 3GPP TR 36.885, encompassing both line-of-sight (LOS) and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) channel conditions. The spatial distribution of vehicles is modeled as a Poisson point process, with vehicles randomly distributed across each lane. A two-layer GNN model is employed in this work. The input feature dimension to the GNN is 60, and the extracted feature dimension is 20. The D3QN model adopts a three-layer neural network architecture, with 300, 200, and 120 neurons in each respective layer, and outputs the Q-values corresponding to a 60-dimensional action space. The ReLU activation function is applied between successive layers. The initial learning rate is set to 0.005, with a minimum value of 0.0001. The discount factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document} is set to 0.95, the replay buffer capacity is set to 100,000, and the mini-batch size is set to 64. Simulation results analysis

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed GNN-D3QN resource allocation method, this paper selects Ashraf, DQN, Double DQN, and Dueling DQN as baseline algorithms for comparison. The Ashraf method22 is one of the most representative traditional heuristic algorithms in the domain of V2V resource allocation for vehicular networks, which designs clustering and distributed resource allocation strategies based on inter-vehicle distance and interference relationships. It is widely adopted as a benchmark in V2X resource allocation research and represents the performance upper bound of non-learning-based optimization approaches. DQN serves as the most fundamental and classical baseline algorithm for deep reinforcement learning applied to V2X resource allocation, belonging to the same DRL framework as the proposed method, but without the Double and Dueling structural enhancements or the incorporation of GNN features. Double DQN addresses the Q-value overestimation problem inherent in DQN, yet does not incorporate GNN or the Dueling architecture. Its inclusion as a baseline enables verification of the performance gains in resource allocation stability achieved through the decoupling of the target network and value network. Dueling DQN decouples state value and action advantage via a dual-branch architecture, making it better suited for decision-making tasks in high-dimensional state spaces; however, it does not introduce GNN or global interference information. Its inclusion as a baseline allows for validation of the effectiveness of the value-advantage decomposition structure in V2X decision-making, while further highlighting the necessity of the joint GNN and D3QN design. Considering the stochastic disturbance characteristics caused by vehicle distribution, ten independent random simulation experiments are conducted in this paper. Since such random factors exert a limited impact on each performance metric, the average value of the experimental results is adopted as the final outcome. This approach ensures conciseness and clarity of the figures and tables, and highlights the comparison trends among different algorithms.

Summarize

This paper proposes a joint spectrum and power allocation scheme for vehicular networks based on GNN-D3QN. The graph neural network captures the global interference topology, while the dueling deep Q-network enables efficient distributed decision-making. Simulation results validate the effectiveness of the proposed scheme in improving the success probability of vehicle-to-vehicle communications and the capacity of vehicle-to-infrastructure communications. The current work considers a single-cell scenario; future research will explore extensions to multi-cell heterogeneous networks (HetNets) and the generalization from discrete power levels to continuous power control.

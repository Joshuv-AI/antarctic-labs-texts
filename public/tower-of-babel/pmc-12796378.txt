Red-billed blue magpie optimization for training feedforward neural networks
Authors: Jinzhong Zhang, Hongkai Li, Gang Zhang, Rui Chen, Tan Zhang, Anqi Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The input layer, hidden layer, and output layer constitute the feedforward neural networks (FNNs) with unidirectional propagation and no feedback loop connection. The FNNs exhibit pivotal properties of nonlinear fitting ability, flexible structure, multitasking adaptability, easy implementation, strong compatibility and composability. The FNNs can independently tackle classification and regression tasks and support the construction of complex networks. The red-billed blue magpie optimization (RBMO) is constructed on the collective collaborative exploration and sophisticated hunting proficiency of the red-billed blue magpies. The RBMO emulates searching for food sources, chasing and attacking prey, and hoarding superfluous food to balance extensive global exploration and meticulous local exploitation, and to ascertain the fittest solution. In this paper, the RBMO is proposed to train FNNs, the motivation is to measure the dissimilarity between anticipated and actual outputs, quantify the training efficiency and classification precision of the sample datasets, and fabricate the connection weights and biases. The RBMO exhibits the distinctive advantages of extensible adaptability and flexibility, accelerated convergence speed, heightened calculation accuracy, decreased control parameters, remarkable robustness and stability, and diminished computational complexity. Seventeen sample datasets are utilized to authenticate the applicability and practicality, the RBMO is compared with SFOA, FLO, APO, HEOA, EGO, BKA, WO, HO, IAO, NRBO, ETO and PO. The experimental results demonstrate that the RBMO can dynamically alternate between exploration and exploitation to strengthen stability and robustness, intensify training efficiency and classification precision, promote convergence speed and solution quality.

Mathematical model of feedforward neural networks

FNNs simulate the signal transmission mechanism of biological brain neurons and strictly adhere to hierarchical organization and unidirectional transmission. The input layer is responsible for receiving raw input data and feature signals, the dimensionality of the data features strictly determines the neuron scale. The hidden layer employs multi-layer nonlinear mapping to perform feature abstraction and transformation, and to regulate the network’s layer structure and neuron scale. The output layer exploits a linear activation function and neuron scale to actualize the final prediction result of classification probability or regression value. The ultimate goal of the FNN architecture rule is to construct an FNN model with low coding dimension, strong fitting ability, and high adaptation efficiency. Based on topology adaptation optimization, component collaboration performance, and coding matching efficiency, the RBMO can reduce the computational complexity of the search space, improve global detection coverage, reduce parameter-out-of-bounds overhead, accelerate local mining efficiency, balance FNN fitting and generalization, and advance prediction accuracy and optimization efficiency.

The train/test split strategy adopts scientific data splitting, strict isolation, and RBMO’s exploration, exploitation, and food storage optimization mechanisms to achieve depth adaptation. This strategy not only optimizes FNN weights/biases efficiently, but also avoids overfitting and ensures the objectivity of model generalization ability evaluation. The train split strategy is to calculate the individual fitness of the RBMO population (decoding parameters, FNN forward propagation, loss/accuracy calculation), and to drive parameter-iterative optimization. The sample size is sufficient and covers the core rules of the data, which provides effective feedback for RBMO’s global detection and local mining. The test split strategy is used to estimate the generalization performance of the final model separately and to produce objective indicators. This is strictly isolated and does not participate in any optimization steps, ensuring that the evaluation results reflect the model’s actual predictive ability.

The encoding of weights/biases transforms the parameter set of FNN into a continuous real vector that can be directly optimized by RBMO, materializes the deep adaptation of FNN parameter optimization and RBMO global search characteristics, and achieves higher prediction accuracy and stronger generalization ability. Therefore, the total space complexity of the RBMO for training FNNs is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N \times D + h \times (n + m))$$\end{document}.

Data preprocessing

The pivotal objective of data preprocessing for the RBMO in resolving the FNNs is to build a high-quality data set that supports the RBMO’s continuous optimization and FNN parameter learning requirements. This method employs data cleaning, feature coding, feature scaling, feature dimensionality reduction, data set division, and sample enhancement to eliminate data noise, unify the dimensional scale, control feature dimensionality, balance the sample distribution, and achieve a double boost in the RBMO optimization efficiency and FNN prediction accuracy. The adaptive RBMO is used to match the continuous, real-valued coding characteristics, unify the feature and parameter boundaries, reduce the input dimension and, consequently, the coding dimension, and avoid high-dimensional disasters that affect detection and mining efficiency. Adaptive FNN is used to eliminate dimensional differences, avoid weight-update imbalance, ensure data continuity, support continuous parameter learning, and control input dimensions to prevent overfitting. Data cleaning avoids NaN values or fitness calculation errors and stabilizes the RBMO iteration process. Feature coding converts non-numerical features to continuous values, which are then normalized to prevent dimension imbalance from interfering with RBMO search direction. Feature scaling is used to unify the features, connection weights, and biases, improve the effectiveness of the RBMO step-size update, and reduce the overhead of cross-border processing. Feature dimensionality reduction is used to control the input dimension, reduce the coding dimension, and alleviate the pressure on RBMO’s high-dimensional search. The data set is divided into a training set for RBMO fitness evaluation, a validation set for early stopping, and an objective evaluation set for model performance. The sample enhancement is implemented to increase the diversity of training sets, eliminate local optima, and stabilize fitness evaluation results.

Conclusion and future research

In this paper, the RBMO is proposed to train the FNNs, the motive is to break through the local optimal trap of traditional gradient descent method, accelerate the convergence rate of high-dimensional nonlinear issues, promote the generalization and robustness of complex task models, facilitate the integration of RBMO and neural networks, quantify the dissimilarity between prospective output and practical output, estimate training efficiency and classification accuracy of prediction sample datasets, and fabricate the connection weights and bias. The RBMO demonstrates strong collective, collaborative exploration and intelligent hunting proficiency, imitating the searching, chasing, attacking prey, and food storage behaviors of red-billed blue magpies. As a flexible and adaptable predator, the red-billed blue magpie primarily feeds on fruits, plants, insects, and small vertebrates, which typically move in groupuscules or clusters, jumping, ground-walking, and scouring trees for food. The RBMO exhibits the algorithmic characteristics of multi-strategy collaborative fusion, adaptive dynamic adjustment, elite retention and information sharing, scalability, hybrid potential, and easy implementation and application. The experimental results demonstrate that the convergence speed, solution precision, training efficiency, classification accuracy, stability and robustness of RBMO are superior to those of the SFOA, FLO, APO, HEOA, EGO, BKA, WO, HO, IAO, NRBO, ETO and PO according to seventeen sample datasets. The RBMO typically manipulates groupuscule and cluster scales to equalize global extensive exploration and local meticulous exploitation, avoid overfitting, reduce convergence fluctuations, and prevent gradient vanishing and combinatorial explosion, thereby ascertaining the fittest solution. The experimental results demonstrate that the RBMO employs relatively abundant predatory mechanisms to enhance detection efficiency, reduce computational complexity, consolidate adaptability and flexibility, improve training efficiency and classification accuracy, and accelerate convergence speed and solution precision.

The limitations of the RBMO for training the FNNs are summarized as follows: (1) Performance degradation in high-dimensional parameter space. High-dimensional spaces can lead to drawbacks such as dimensional disaster, search stagnation, local optimization, reduced convergence rate, loss of population diversity, and incomplete coverage of the solution space. (2) Dynamic imbalance between exploration and exploitation. The RBMO utilizes frequent information exchange and synchronous communication within the group collaboration to delay the overhead of parallelization implementation and to lag the convergence of synchronous and asynchronous communication. (3) Deficiency of task adaptation and constraint. The deep structure of FNNs can cause gradient disappearance or explosion. The RBMO cannot alleviate the attenuation or amplification effects of gradient transmission via parameter updates, thereby preventing adequate updating of the network’s bottom-layer parameters and ultimately affecting the FNN’s feature-learning ability.

The in-depth exploration and analysis of future research are summarized as follows: (1) We will analyze statistical significance analysis of performance metrics (e.g., classification accuracy or regression error) in detail to verify the effectiveness and feasibility of the RBMO. We will combine RBMO and machine learning to improve parameter estimation for the electro-hydraulic control system and introduce a nonlinear output-error model. (2) Strengthening and innovating the core mechanism. The RBMO will leverage an efficient hybrid intelligent collaborative optimization paradigm or structure-aware encoding scheme to avoid premature convergence, alleviate dimensional disasters, achieve collaborative evolution, maintain elite solutions, and preserve population diversity. (3) Tight integration of the deep learning paradigm. The RBMO will not only employ Gaussian regression processes and deep neural networks to calibrate proxy models regularly, intelligently evaluate training precision, effectively verify the fittest solutions, achieve seamless integration, check residual connections and attention mechanism components, and support serial and parallel computation. (4) Application exploration of cutting-edge and interdisciplinary fields. The RBMO will be applied to address automation, machine learning, neural architecture search, resource-constrained and special scenarios, and the adaptation of emerging computing paradigms.

A bibliometric analysis of extrachromosomal DNA in cancer over six decades
Authors: Qiqi Luo, Nan Zhou, Chuanfang Wu, Jinku Bao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractExtrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) is a critical driver of oncogene amplification and has significantly advanced the understanding of tumor heterogeneity. Although the biological mechanisms and clinical significance of ecDNA have been extensively studied, few articles have systematically summarized the historical development of this field. This study conducted a comprehensive retrospective analysis to map the evolutionary trajectory and research hotspots of ecDNA research in cancer from 1965 to 2025. We retrieved 3,328 publications from the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus. We analyzed the co-authorship coverage alongside co-citation and co-occurrence of regions, institutions, authors, and keywords using VOSviewer and CiteSpace. The United States and China acted as the predominant global contributors, accounting for 38.25% and 25.81% of publications, respectively. Keyword co-occurrence and burst detection analyses revealed the thematic evolution of the field. Early dominant terms such as "gene amplification" focused on copy number variation in the linear genome without in-depth structural exploration. Contemporary research has shifted to terms including "circular DNA", "chromatin topology", and "drug resistance". This evolution signifies a paradigm shift from linear genome investigations to epigenetic mechanisms and clinical applications. We also discussed development prospects and key challenges while proposing potential solutions to major obstacles. This study aims to provide novel perspectives for advancing ecDNA research and its clinical translation in precision oncology.Keywords: ecDNA, Cancer, Bibliometrics

Introduction

Cancer remains a formidable challenge in global public health. Its high incidence and mortality rates pose a significant threat to human health [1, 2]. Genomic instability is a hallmark of cancer development, where the abnormal amplification of oncogenes and therapy resistance genes constitutes a core mechanism driving tumor progression [3, 4].

In recent years, extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA), a distinct circular DNA molecule separate from chromosomal DNA, has emerged as a research focus due to its unique role in oncogene amplification [5–7]. These molecules range in size from 100 kb to several Mb and are often observed cytogenetically as paired structures called double minutes (DMs) [4]. ecDNA rapidly increases oncogene copy numbers during cell division through self-replication and uneven random distribution [8, 9]. Oncogenes on ecDNA exhibit transcriptional dominance compared to their chromosomal counterparts, producing higher RNA levels even after copy number normalization [2, 9, 10]. This advantage drives tumor heterogeneity and leads to therapy resistance [5, 11]. Clinical studies have revealed that ecDNA promotes tumor proliferation and invasion [2]. It also induces drug-resistant phenotypes by reshaping epigenetic regulatory networks, leading to markedly reduced patient survival rates [12]. Notably, the early emergence of ecDNA in pre-malignant lesions like Barrett's esophagus correlates strongly with elevated risks of malignant transformation, suggesting its involvement in tumor initiation [6, 13]. The continuous advancement of sequencing technologies, from early Sanger sequencing to modern single-cell multi-omics platforms, has significantly enhanced the detection accuracy and expanded the research dimensions of ecDNA [14–18]. These technological breakthroughs have shifted ecDNA research from structural characterization to the exploration of functional mechanisms and clinical translation.

Despite the established progression of ecDNA research, there is a lack of systematic quantitative analysis regarding knowledge evolution in this field. Bibliometrics combines quantitative methods and scientific knowledge mapping to analyze research trends [19]. It employs indicators like literature output, citation networks, and keyword co-occurrence to elucidate the dynamics between technological innovations and theoretical breakthroughs [20, 21]. Despite the significant growth of ecDNA-related publications, existing reviews predominantly focus on molecular mechanisms.

This study aims to construct a six-decade knowledge framework of ecDNA research through systematic bibliometric analyses. We seek to reveal the synergistic evolution between technological innovations and theoretical discoveries. Specifically, the objectives are: (1) to trace the progression of ecDNA research from cytogenetic observations to epigenetic regulatory mechanisms; (2) to examine how sequencing technologies, such as Sanger sequencing, NGS, and single-cell multi-omics, have transitioned the field from structural characterization to evolutionary dynamics; and (3) to analyze the heterogeneity of the global research landscape to propose future research priorities.

Material and methods

Research process

Figure 1 outlines the systematic search strategy and data analysis for this study. We retrieved relevant publications from two major databases to ensure comprehensive coverage: the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and Scopus. We consolidated and deduplicated the combined records using EndNote software. This process yielded the final dataset for analysis. We then analyzed this dataset with VOSviewer and CiteSpace to map the knowledge structure and collaboration networks [22, 23].

Flowchart of the study

Data collection

The Web of Science (WoS) is a leading academic database containing high-quality journals and comprehensive citation records [24]. Its integrated bibliometric tools (such as H-index calculation) make it a standard resource for research evaluation [25]. We conducted a systematic literature search using WoSCC and Scopus databases on December 8, 2025. These comprehensive databases ensure robust coverage of the field. The search period covered all publications from January 1, 1965, to December 8, 2025. The search strategy aimed to identify publications on ecDNA in cancer. We executed the query in the Topic (TS) field of the WoSCC database, which searches titles, abstracts, and keywords. The query was: TS = (ecDNA OR "extrachromosomal DNA" OR "double minute") AND TS = (cancer OR tumor* OR oncolog* OR neoplas*). This search yielded 2,920 initial records. Concurrently, we performed an equivalent search in the Scopus database using the Title-Abstract fields. The query was: TITLE-ABS(ecDNA OR "extrachromosomal DNA" OR "double minute") AND TITLE-ABS(cancer OR tumor* OR tumour* OR neoplas* OR oncolog*). The Scopus search retrieved 2,512 initial records. We exported the full records and cited references for all results.

We exported all retrieved records from both databases. We then merged and deduplicated the files using EndNote software. Duplicates were identified based on matching article titles and Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs). This process consolidated the literature into a final dataset of 3,328 unique publications. This dataset served as the foundation for all subsequent analyses. We calculated annual publication numbers using R software (version 4.4.3) and visualized temporal trends with the ggplot2 package [26, 27].

Data analysis

Countries/regions collaboration analysis

We constructed co-authorship networks of countries/regions using VOSviewer and CiteSpace to analyze global collaboration. In VOSviewer, the mapping process involved calculating a similarity matrix from co-occurrence data [22, 28, 29]. We applied association strength normalization to the edge weights. Countries or regions with fewer than 5 publications were excluded. The full counting method accounted for all co-occurrences. We used default parameters for the network layout but set attraction and repulsion values to 5 and 1, respectively, for optimal visualization. We applied an edge weight threshold of 300 to simplify the network. In CiteSpace, we conducted the analysis with annual time slices. The Pathfinder algorithm pruned redundant edges to simplify connections. Sliced network pruning identified significant collaborations. We displayed labels for entities ranked within the top 1%.

Institutions and authors collaboration analysis

In VOSviewer, we applied a minimum publication threshold of 10 articles for including institutions and authors. We used the full counting method to account for all co-occurrences. Association strength normalization was applied to edge weights. CiteSpace analysis of institutional collaboration networks used a g-index set to k = 15. The Pathfinder algorithm was applied for network pruning. In the author collaboration network, the g-index parameter was also set to k = 15, with no network pruning applied.

Keyword co-occurrence and burst detection

We constructed a keyword co-occurrence network using VOSviewer. Keywords were filtered by setting a threshold of at least 30 occurrences. The full counting method captured all co-occurrences. Edge weights were standardized using association strength normalization. We used CiteSpace for burst detection. The analysis period was divided into 1-year time slices. We retained the top 25 keywords with the highest burst strength within each time slice.

Citation co-occurrence analysis

Co-citation relationships were analyzed in CiteSpace with the node type set to "Cited Reference". The analysis was performed with the g-index set to 3. The Pathfinder algorithm pruned the network to reduce redundant links while preserving critical connections. All other parameters remained at their default values.

Results

Overview of the included literature

The search query identified 3,328 literature records from WoSCC and Scopus. These records consisted of 12 document types. Original research articles comprised 2,715 papers and accounted for 81.6% of the dataset. Review articles followed with 329 papers (9.9%). The remaining document types included meeting abstracts (98), editorial materials (49), conference papers (43), research notes (35), letters (19), book chapters (7), early access articles (11), corrections (3), retracted papers (14), and news items (5). We excluded retracted papers from the analysis. Full citation metadata and reference lists were retained to analyze collaboration patterns and research trends. Bibliometric indicators showed a total of 137,612 citations. There were 122,322 effective citations after excluding self-citations. The dataset had an h-index of 146 and an average of 41.35 citations per document.

Annual scientific output

Figure 2 illustrates that both publication output and citation counts in ecDNA cancer research have grown broadly with periodic fluctuations. Annual publications remained at minimal levels during the initial phase from 1965 to 1979. A period of steady growth occurred from 1980 to 1995. Subsequently, annual publication counts entered a plateau that lasted until around 2005. A broad expansion in both publications and citations commenced after 2005. Notably, citation accumulation accelerated substantially around 2019. This trend culminated in 9,638 citations and 245 publications in 2025. This trajectory reflects the evolution of the field from a niche topic to a high-impact area of cancer genomics.

Research hotspots and frontiers

Keyword co-occurrence analysis identifies active research areas. Analysis of 8,503 keywords in VOSviewer identified 134 terms meeting the threshold. Figure 6A reveals major thematic clusters. The green cluster focused on MDM2 and p53 interaction mechanisms. It comprised terms such as apoptosis, ubiquitination, activation, and cancer therapy. This cluster highlighted processes critical for cancer progression. The blue cluster included terms like gene expression, breast cancer, prognosis, and mutations. It highlighted clinical research on cancer subtypes and genetic alterations. The red cluster was anchored by "cancer" and contained "gene amplification", "therapy resistance", and "extrachromosomal circular DNA". This cluster emphasized genomic instability mechanisms contributing to tumor evolution.

CiteSpace analysis identified shifts in research focus through burst detection. Figure 6B shows the top 25 keywords with the strongest bursts. Early research from 1965 to 2000 emphasized gene amplification and molecular mechanisms. Dominant keywords included "gene amplification" and "wild-type p53". Mid-term studies from 2000 to 2018 transitioned towards omics technologies and featured keywords like "single nucleotide polymorphism". Recent investigations from 2018 to 2025 have prioritized epigenetic regulation and clinical challenges. Keywords are now dominated by "chromatin", "drug resistance", and "extrachromosomal circular DNA". This pattern reflects the shift from structural validation to functional exploration. The onset of these high-burst keywords aligns with the technological breakthrough of long-read sequencing in 2019.

Discussion

The six-decade trajectory of ecDNA research, as mapped by our bibliometric analysis, demonstrates that technological innovation drives the development of this field. Each methodological advance has created distinct publication phases, reshaped collaborative networks, and altered research priorities as seen in keyword evolution.

The initial phase from 1965 to the 1990s relied mainly on cytogenetics and electron microscopy [8, 34, 35]. These methods used morphological traits to confirm the existence of extrachromosomal elements [36]. However, they lacked the resolution to determine specific nucleotide sequences [37]. Consequently, annual publication volumes remained low. The bibliometric landscape focused on descriptive terms such as "double minute chromosomes". This limitation prevented researchers from linking ecDNA to broader cancer models [11]. As a result, keywords rarely co-occurred with core concepts like tumorigenesis.

The introduction of next-generation sequencing after 2006 paradoxically hindered ecDNA research by creating an analytical blind spot. This stagnation resulted from the topological incompatibility between circular DNA and standard linear reference genomes. Conventional alignment algorithms were designed to penalize read pairs that mapped to distant coordinates [2]. These anomalous signals represent ecDNA junctions but were routinely filtered out as sequencing noise [38]. This algorithmic bias caused circular DNA to be misclassified as linear amplifications in the literature [39]. Beyond technical constraints, this stagnation was reinforced by the prevailing 'linear genome' paradigm that dominated the post-Human Genome Project era. Research resources and funding were disproportionately allocated toward characterizing standard reference genomes and simple somatic mutations, which offered a more cost-effective and low-risk path for drug target discovery [40, 41]. Consequently, the scientific community prioritized high-throughput detection of linear alterations over the computationally expensive and complex reconstruction of non-canonical architectures. Bibliometric data from 2008 to 2017 reflects this exclusion. Research output skewed toward linear alterations and led to a surge in keywords related to "single nucleotide polymorphism". Reconstructing circular structures from short reads was computationally difficult compared to detecting point mutations [42]. These technical barriers marginalized complex structural variants for over a decade.

The field reached a decisive turning point in 2019 with the study by Paul Mischel and colleagues published [43]. This research resolved the structural blind spot by integrating electron microscopy, optical mapping, and whole-genome sequencing. This multi-modal approach definitively confirmed the circular structure of ecDNA [43]. It also revealed functional characteristics such as accessible chromatin and high oncogene expression. This study marked the transition from speculative observation to definitive structural resolution [43]. The impact of this breakthrough is visible in the post-2019 bibliometric data. The emergence of terms like "chromatin" and "drug resistance" as top burst keywords indicates a shift toward exploring functional consequences. Resolving the structural architecture established the physical basis for understanding how ecDNA drives tumor heterogeneity [2]. This confirms that integrating long-read capabilities and multi-omics verification was required to overcome linear genomic assumptions and unlock the modern era of ecDNA research [44, 45].

The global research landscape shows a functional distinction between the United States and China. While both dominate publication volume, the disparity in citation impact stems from the nature of their contributions. United States institutions have largely acted as innovators of foundational methodologies. Key computational algorithms for ecDNA topology were established primarily by US-based consortia [11, 18, 41, 46–48]. These methodological innovations generate high citations as they serve as universal tools. In contrast, researchers in China have excelled in the clinical validation of these paradigms [49–51]. This is particularly evident in cancer types prevalent in Asia, such as hepatocellular carcinoma and esophageal cancer [51]. This application-oriented strategy generates significant publication volume but typically yields fewer citations than source innovations. Additionally, the collaborative network in China remains centralized around domestic medical centers. This pattern limits the integration of Chinese clinical datasets into global genomic initiatives. Therefore, the citation gap reflects a structural division between method developers and downstream validators.

The structural analysis of the co-authorship network reveals a fragmentation that impacts scientific progress. The low network density of 0.1352 indicates a challenge of disciplinary barriers. ecDNA research requires integrating computational topology with chromatin biology and oncology. The sparse connectivity suggests these domains often operate in parallel rather than converging [52, 53]. This fragmentation creates a bottleneck for translation [54]. Decoding ecDNA-driven drug resistance requires simultaneous expertise in algorithms and wet-lab validation. The discrete clusters imply that methodological breakthroughs in one area may face delayed adoption in clinical studies due to weak transmission channels. Consequently, low network density acts as a systemic rate-limiting factor. Future progress depends on establishing strategic bridges between these specialized clusters to foster the cross-disciplinary fusion required for this complex field.

Limitations

In summary, this study maps the six-decade evolution of ecDNA research through a comprehensive bibliometric framework. Nevertheless, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, despite integrating both Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus, certain relevant publications indexed in other databases may not have been captured, potentially introducing selection bias. Second, and importantly, the merging of records from these two databases inevitably led to the loss of specific metadata fields due to inconsistent export formats. This loss of metadata may reduce the precision of co-citation and collaboration network analyses, and should be carefully considered when interpreting the network-based findings presented in this study. Future bibliometric analyses incorporating open-access databases such as PubMed or Dimensions could offer complementary and more inclusive perspectives.

Conclusion

Bibliometric analysis reveals a six-decade interplay between technological innovation and theoretical progress in ecDNA cancer research. Sequencing breakthroughs have systematically expanded research frontiers. The focus has shifted from identifying rare genomic aberrations to elucidating chromatin dynamics and therapeutic resistance networks. Regional analysis shows asymmetric contributions: the USA pioneered tools and mechanistic insights, while China emerged as a hub for clinical translation. The evolution of research priorities describes the central role of ecDNA in genomic instability and adaptive evolution [14, 15]. As the field moves from descriptive studies to mechanistic interventions, addressing translational bottlenecks enables proactive control of tumor evolution [16]. This outlines a roadmap for precision oncology that emphasizes the integration of computational biology and clinical medicine.

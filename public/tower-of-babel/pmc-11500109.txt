Quantitative comparison of the operating characteristics and engine performances of typical configurations of free-piston engine linear generator
Authors: Chendong Guo, Yahui Wang, Liang Tong, Hailong Deng, Huihua Feng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDue to their unique operating characteristics and high thermal efficiencies, free-piston engine linear generator (FPELG) has attracted research interest in recent years, thanks to the crankshaft absence. The FPELG has three typical structure configurations: single-cylinder single-piston, dual-cylinder dual-piston, and opposed-piston. These structure configurations have different benefits owing to their distinctive piston operating characteristics and engine performances. This paper analysed the piston operating characteristics and engine performances of the three typical FPELG structure configurations using a quantitative comparison method. The results revealed that the output power of the dual-cylinder dual-piston FPELG was the highest, at 3.7 kW. The opposed-piston FPELG exhibited the highest indicated thermal efficiency of 34%. In addition, the indicated power of FPELG was negatively correlated with the heat transfer area. The friction loss of each FPELG was < 5% of the overall energy consumption. Our study comprehensively analysed the various FPELG structure configurations and highlighted their advantages.Keywords: Free-piston engine linear generator, Typical structure configurations, Piston operating characteristics, Engine performances, Transfer heatSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In order to enhance the power density of power systems and improve the efficiency of converting thermal energy to electrical energy of energy conversion systems, attempts have been made to optimise existing energy conversion devices and explore new ones1–4. One of the current trending research topics is the free-piston engine linear generator (FPELG), a unique power system that converts thermal energy into electrical energy5–7. The FPELG directly combines the free-piston engine and the linear generator5,6. Its operational principle involves burning a mixture of fuel and air in the cylinder of the free-piston engine; after the combustion process, the gas drives the pistons and magnetic rod of the linear generator to move, and then the linear generator converts a portion of the kinetic energy of the magnetic rod into electrical energy5,8–10.

The operational principle highlights the significant differences in operational characteristics and engine performances between the FPELG and Conventional Alternative Engines (CAEs) with similar specifications. Existing literature indicates that the FPELG outperforms the CAEs in various aspects, such as thermal efficiency, power density, fewer transmission components, and a wider range of compatible fuels6,9,11. As a result, the FPELG has attracted significant attention as an innovative energy conversion solution5,12.

Based on the unique operational principle, there are three typical structure configurations of FPELG, namely the single-cylinder single-piston FPELG (SCSP-FPELG), dual-cylinder dual-piston FPELG (DCDP-FPELG) and opposed-piston FPELG (OP-FPELG). And the schematics representation of three typical structure configurations of FPELG are depicted in Fig. 11,7,9,11. These typical structure configurations have similar fundamental operating principles, in which the mixed gas from fuel and air is burned to power the piston throughout the expansion process and then generate electricity. However, they differ in the operating characteristics of the piston, particularly during the compression process5,10,12. For instance, the DCDP-FPELG has no rebound device since the power process occurs throughout the operating process, while for the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG, the rebound device is necessary to counterbalance the gas resistance force during the no-power process1,4. Furthermore, the opposing pistons of OP-FPELG require exact synchronisation for the entire operating process.

Schematic of three typical configurations of FPELG. 1—Sparking plug; 2—Engine piston; 3—Cylinder; 4—Fuel injector; 5—Scavenging port; 6—Control system; 7—External load; 8—Mover; 9—Stator; 10—Exhaust port; 11—Rebound piston; 12—Bounce chamber.

Each typical FPELG structure configuration has unique operating characteristics that result in different engine performances. Consequently, the typical structural configurations of FPELG have been extensively investigated1,13. A detailed summary of the current progress in research concerning the three typical structure configurations of FPELG is provided, which offers valuable insights into the most recent advancements and emerging trends in this area2.

SCSP-FPELG

The SCSP-FPELG consists of a linear generator, a free-piston engine, and a rebound device1. Prominent research institutes studying this structure configuration of FPELG structure include Newcastle University, the German Aerospace Center, Toyota Center R&D Labs Inc., the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and the Nanjing University of Science and Technology.

Since 2007, Roskilly group have revealed that the SCSP-FPELG under the compression-ignition combustion mode has superior fuel efficiency and less pollutant emissions than that of CAEs14,15. The decrease in heat transfer losses of SCSP-FPELG is ascribed to the higher piston velocity during the expansion process16,17. Furthermore, based on the simulation results, researchers have proposed a pseudo-derivative feedback control strategy well suited for the SCSP-FPELG. Haag group optimised the exchange process of SCSP-FPELG and found that a three-valve arrangement was the best option for the exchange process18. Moreover, the ratio of exhaust gas recirculation, controlled through adjustments in the intake valve opening and closing timing, influences the combustion characteristics19.

The Kazunari Moriya group compared the engine performances of SCSP-FPELG under different combustion modes. The simulation results indicated that the thermal efficiency of the free-piston engine was higher (42%) under the premixed-charge-compression-ignition (PCCI) combustion mode than that of the spark-ignition (SI) combustion mode while maintaining the same output power20. Moreover, a controller method for the continued steady operation of SCSP-FPELG has been proposed and successfully implemented20,21. Xu group investigated a four-stroke SCSP-FPELG with gasoline as the fuel. The authors reported an overall efficiency of the four-stroke SCSP-FPELG of 32%. The efficiency of SCSP-FPELG utilising the Miller cycle increased notably when compared with that of the Otto cycle22–26. Zhang group revealed that the SCSP-FPELG achieved an overall generation efficiency of 38.2%, producing an output power of 15 kW. The authors demonstrated that the operational parameters had a substantial impact on the engine performances of the system27–29.

Researchers have extensively studied the operational characteristics of the engine performances of SCSP-FPELGs when compared with CAEs. However, a quantitative evaluation comparing the performances of SCSP-FPELG with those of other FPLEGs is lacking.

DCDP-FPELG

The DCDP-FPELG comprises a linear generator and two free-piston engines1. The internationally renowned research organisations investigating the DCDP-FPELG include West Virginia University, Sandia National Laboratories, the free-piston energy converter (FPEC) initiative of the European Union, the Czech Technological University, Newcastle University, Ulsan University, Shanghai Jiaotong University, Chongqing Jiaotong University, and Beijing Institute of Technology.

A research team led by Clark observed that the DCDP-FPELG achieved a thermal efficiency exceeding 60% in the Homogeneous-Charge-Compression-Ignition (HCCI) combustion mode, outperforming compression-ignition combustion mode, as demonstrated by complex numerical simulation models30–34. Recent studies have investigated the combustion and heat release characteristics of DCDP-FPELGs equipped with spring-assisted mechanisms35,36. Van Blarigan group indicated that the theoretical thermal efficiency of DCDP-FPELG in the HCCI combustion mode, powered by hydrogen fuel, might reach 65%37. The combustion efficiency of DCDP-FPELG was enhanced by optimising the scavenging mechanism using Computational Fluid Dynamics model38,39.

The FPEC initiative, funded by the European Union, discovered that the movement pattern of FPEC piston did not adhere to a sine wave. In the HCCI combustion mode, the recorded effectiveness of FPEC reached 51%, with an output power of 23 kW40. Simultaneously, the straight generator exhibited extraordinary effectiveness and noteworthy production power upon implementing a longitudinal flux permanent magnet41–44. The PAVEL Deutsch group achieved a peak output power of 650 W. At the same time, the overall efficiency of the 50 cc DCDP-FPELG under the Spark-Ignition combustion mode was below 10%45–47.

Roskilly group demonstrated that in the two-stroke mode, the DCDP-FPELG ran at a notably increased frequency and a decreased peak gas pressure compared with the four-stroke mode48. During the starting phase, the peak pressures on both sides of free-piston engines exceed 11 bar with only a small gas pressure differential49. Lim group studied the DCDP-FPELG using propane as the fuel50. To guarantee a seamless transition between the various combustion modes, it is necessary to operate the DCDP-FPELG under specific parameters51. Xiao group demonstrated that various input parameters of DCDP-FPELG significantly affected the piston motion curves and the combustion and emission characteristics52,53.

Yuan group demonstrated that the combustion duration of DCDP-FPELG, using various fuels, decreased significantly as the hydrogen proportion increased54–56. Feng and Zuo investigated the engine performances of DCDP-FPELG using gasoline and diesel as fuels5,57–59. Examination of the simulated data showed that the post-combustion characteristics of DCDP-FPELG were superior to those of CAEs8,10,59. Experimental findings suggested that the diesel DCDP-FPELG achieved an efficiency of 38.5% and produced approximately 5.16 kW of output power60. Additionally, the DCDP-FPELG operating process was successfully implemented using the proposed control strategies for each working phase2,10,61,62.

The engine performances of DCDP-FPELG have been investigated using numerical models and experimental results, and feasible control strategies have been proposed. The results are essential for developing and optimising the FPELG to achieve high output power and indicated efficiency. However, no quantitative comparison has been made regarding the engine performances of DCDP-FPELG with alternative FPELG configurations.

OP-FPELG

The OP-FPELG consists of a free-piston engine with two pistons, two linear generators, and two rebound devices1. Leading research institutes worldwide, including Sandia National Laboratories, the German Aerospace Center, and Nanjing University of Sciences and Technology, have spearheaded the development of this technology.

Friedrich examined the engine performances of OP-FPELG utilising hydrogen as fuel in the HCCI combustion mode. The authors successfully implemented the passive synchronisation of pistons in the prototype. The experimental results indicated that the OP-FPELG demonstrated the performance range of 50–55%, achieving a peak efficiency of 60% under certain operational parameters. Nevertheless, the robustness of OP-FPELG prototype during the operating process was constrained63. Schneider and Xu analysed the transition mechanism of OP-FPELG between various combustion modes. The scavenging efficiency and combustion characteristics of OP-FPELG were optimised using multiple geometrical configurations. Additionally, a seamless transition from SI combustion mode to HCCI combustion mode was successfully implemented by adjusting the piston dead centre position64. Xu group demonstrated that the total efficiency of the gasoline OP-FPELG in the SI combustion mode could reach up to 42.5%, producing approximately 15 kW of electric power65. Additionally, the power density of the four-stroke OP-FPELG was higher and exhibited reduced mechanical vibration. This study focused on the engine performances of OP-FPELG under different combustion modes. Despite its short duration, the prototype operated effectively. Nonetheless, a quantitative analysis comparing the engine performances of OP-FPELG with those of other FPELG variants has not been conducted66.

Based on the preceding discussions, the operational characteristics of the piston and the engine performances of a particular structure configuration of FPELG have been the focus of several researcher groups. Nonetheless, a quantitative evaluation was not conducted on the operational characteristics of the piston and the engine performances of different structure configurations of FPELG. Consequently, the unique advantages of each FPELG structure configuration are not adequately manifested in diverse application procedures. The authors undertook a quantitative comparison of the operational characteristics of the piston and the overall engine performances of each structure configuration of FPELG. Furthermore, the energy flow distribution characteristics of each FPELG configuration are discussed to improve the overall engine performances, and the effects of the heat transfer area and friction on each structure configuration of FPELG were examined. The findings of the present study revealed the distinct benefits of each structure configuration of FPELG.

Results and discussion

Piston operating characteristics

For a quantitative comparison of the piston operating characteristics of FPELG, Fig. 6 displays the piston displacement curves of the three typical FPELG structure configurations. Initially, the DCDP-FPELG piston reached the top dead centre (TDC) ahead of the other two configurations. The operating principle of DCDP-FPELG involves alternating the combustion and expansion processes between two free-piston engines during the operation process. While one free-piston engine undergoes the compression process, the other supplies energy from combustion and expansion processes. Consequently, both free-piston engines were continuously engaged in combustion and expansion cycles during each DCDP-FPELG operation process.

Piston displacement curves of the three typical FPELG structure configurations.

Conversely, the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG obtained compression energy through the rebound devices. The energy produced in the FPELG by the fuel during the combustion and expansion processes exceeds that provided by the rebound device. Consequently, the accumulated energy of DCDP-FPELG in each working cycle was significantly higher than that of the other two typical configurations of FPELG during the steady operation process, resulting in a higher piston operating frequency of DCDP-FPELG. Second, the displacement curves of the pistons of SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG reached the TDC simultaneously in the first working cycle. However, the piston operating frequency of SCSP-FPELG was lower than that of OP-FPELG. OP-FPELG lacks a cylindrical head and has a smaller heat transfer area. Therefore, it experiences reduced heat transfer loss compared with the SCSP-FPELG. The heat transfer loss in each operating cycle of OP-FPELG was lower than that of SCSP-FPELG. In addition, the symmetrical piston operation characteristics of OP-FPELG are evident. Throughout the stable operation process, both pistons of OP-FPELG were compressed to the TDC during the compression process and moved in opposite directions during the expansion process, resulting in symmetrical displacement curves for the two pistons.

Figure 7 illustrates the piston velocity curves of the three typical FPELG structure configurations. Throughout the expansion process, the peak velocity of DCDP-FPELG reached 4.9 m/s, in contrast to the 5.3 m/s for the OP-FPELG and 5.1 m/s for the SCSP-FPELG. The peak velocity during the expansion process for the DCDP-FPELG was lower than those of OP-FPELG and SCSP-FPELG due to the increased momentum resistance from compression within the opposing side of the free-piston engine. The OP-FPELG and SCSP-FPELG utilised a rebound device to complete the working cycle, which required less momentum. Consequently, the peak expansion velocity was higher for the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG. Furthermore, peak velocities of 2.9 m/s and 3.1 m/s were observed in the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG, respectively. In contrast to the 4.9 m/s for the DCDP-FPELG during the compression process. The discrepancy arises from the energy source for the DCDP-FPELG, which originates from the combustion and heat release processes of FPELG. In contrast, the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG draw their energy from the rebound devices powered by the air spring.

Piston velocity curves of the three typical FPELG structure configurations.

The pressure within the air spring was considerably lower than that from the combustion and expansion processes of FPELG, as shown in Fig. 8. Consequently, the peak velocity of the piston during the compression process of DCDP-FPELG was faster than that of OP-FPELG and SCSP-FPELG. In the expansion process, the peak piston velocities of SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG were notably higher than those of DCDP-FPELG.

Gas pressure from the rebound device cylinder of FPELG.

Figure 9 illustrates the piston velocity-displacement curves for the three typical FPELG configurations, each FPELG configuration maintained a relatively stable state during the operation process. The energy conservation remains consistent between the initial energy input from the fuel and the final energy output of the linear generator, accounting for the friction and heat losses of FPELG. Nevertheless, the piston velocity-displacement curves of DCDP-FPELG deviated significantly from those of the other FPELG variants. This disparity can be attributed to the cyclical nature of the compression and expansion processes within the DCDP-FPELG, resulting in a piston velocity-displacement curve with central symmetry. By contrast, the compression process for both the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG draws energy from the air spring, which contains a gas pressure within the cylinder that is notably lower than the pressure generated during the expansion and combustion processes of FPELG. Therefore, the piston velocity-displacement curves of SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG lack central symmetry. Nonetheless, the resemblance between the piston velocity-displacement curves of SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG suggests similarity in structural dimensions and operational principles. The energy required for the compression process originates from the potential energy stored within the air springs during the expansion process, whereas the energy essential for the expansion process emanates from the liberated energy resulting from the fuel combustion within the engine cylinder. Therefore, the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG exhibited similar performance characteristics in terms of piston dynamics.

Piston velocity-displacement of the three typical FPELG structure configurations.

Engine performances

Figure 10 illustrates the P-V curves of the three FPELG configurations. Each structure configuration showed an isovolumetric combustion process. The peak in-cylinder pressures of DCDP-FPELG, SCSP-FPELG, and OP-FPELG were 42, 40, and 44 bar, respectively. Notably, the OP-FPELG exhibited the highest peak pressure inside the cylinder of the free-piston engine, which can be attributed to its unique structural features, i.e., the absence of the traditional cylindrical head. As a result, the cylinder features limited heat transfer areas to only the cylinder wall and the piston’s upper surface. The OP-FPELG had the lowest heat transfer losses for the same input energy and produced the highest cylinder pressure. A comparison of the engine performances of the three typical FPELG structure configurations is shown in Fig. 11. The indicated power of DCDP-FPELG, SCSP-FPELG, and OP-FPELG are 3.4, 1.7, and 3.6 kW, respectively. The DCDP-FPELG exhibited the highest indicated power among the three typical configurations of FPELG mainly because it had the highest frequency. Therefore, the DCDP-FPELG had excellent performance in electrical energy output.

Pressure-volume diagrams of the three typical FPELG configurations.

Performance parameters of the three typical FPELG configurations.

The engine performances of the three FPELG configurations were compared in this study by analysing the energy flow distribution, as presented in Fig. 12. The thermal efficiencies of DCDP-FPELG, SCSP-FPELG, and OP-FPELG were 32%, 31%, and 34%, respectively. The heat transfer loss of 26% for the OP-FPELG was lower than that of the other two typical configuration FPELGs (28%). The OP-FPELG did not have a cylindrical head but had a relatively small heat transfer area, thus minimising the heat transfer loss. It was further demonstrated that the heat transfer area directly affected the engine performances of FPELG. The fuel losses of the three typical FPELG configurations mean that the incomplete combustion of fuel during the combustion process, resulting in the loss during the heat release process, and the gas leakage in-cylinder of free-piston engine. In addition, the friction losses of FPELG mainly originated from the contact between the cylinder wall and piston ring and the friction inside the linear generator. These friction losses did not exceed 5% of the total energy of the three typical FPELG configurations.

Energy flow distribution of the three typical FPELG structure configurations.

Figure 13 shows the output voltages of the three typical configurations of FPELG. The SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG exhibited asymmetrical output voltage curves. The uneven output voltage pattern was closely related to the rebound devices employed in the SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG systems. During the operation process, the maximum piston velocities of SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG during the expansion process surpassed those during the compression process, leading to a more pronounced peak voltage in the former. The fluctuation in the output voltage of FPELG was directly influenced by the velocity at which the piston was operated.

Variation in heat transfer areas

The operational performances of three typical FPELG structure configurations were compared. The lower heat transfer losses of OP-FPELG owing to the lack of a cylinder head were highlighted. This section investigated the engine performances of the three typical configurations of FPELG with varying heat transfer areas. Utilising the heat-transfer properties of CAEs, the HOHENBERG function was applied to determine the heat transfer occurring in the cylinder head, cylinder wall, and piston top of FPELG. The FPELG exhibited a faster expansion process and slower compression process as the piston approached the TDC. Therefore, the piston remained near the TDC for a shorter duration. This section investigated the impact of varying the heat transfer area of FPELG within the range of − 20% to + 20%, as illustrated in Fig. 14. The findings revealed that alterations in the heat transfer area had minimal influence on the operational characteristics of the piston. Nevertheless, an increase in the heat transfer area corresponded to an increase in the heat transfer loss of FPELG. Specifically, the indicated power of DCDP-FPELG decreased from 3,870 to 3,663 W, that of SCSP-FPELG decreased from 1,750 to 1,728 W, and that of OP-FPELG decreased from 3,671 to 3,644 W. This suggests that the indicated power of each configuration of FPELG was negatively correlated with the heat-transfer area. The findings further illustrated that the range of fluctuations in the indicated power of OP-FPELG was narrower than those of the other two FPELG variants. Because the ratio of the heat transfer area to the volume of OP-FPELG combustion chamber is less than that of DCDP-FPELG and SCSP-FPELG. This different can be ascribed to the distinctive arrangement of the pistons, there is no heat transfer loss in the cylinder head of OP-FPELG. Furthermore, the reduced heat transfer area of OP-FPELG had a diminished influence on both the output power of the system and its indicated thermal efficiency. The results suggest that the performance sensitivity of FPELG to the heat transfer area decreased when the heat transfer area varied within a narrower range.

Indicated power of the three configuration FPELGs under various heat transfer areas.

Variation in friction

Owing to the lack of constraints from the crank linkage mechanism, the FPELG is influenced solely by the forces that dictate the movement of the pistons. Consequently, any variation in the forces applied on the pistons would affect both the operating characteristics of the pistons and energy conversion efficiency of FPELG. Based on the flow distribution characteristics of the three typical structure configurations, the system friction, as a key force applied on the pistons, directly affects the FPELG performances. This section explored the effect of system friction on the FPELG performances by increasing the friction coefficient from − 40 to 0%, as shown in Fig. 15. The impact of the system friction on the piston dynamics of FPELG was relatively minimal. When the friction coefficient was increased from − 40 to 0%, the peak friction of DCDP-FPELG increased from 60.98 N to 97.77 N, the peak friction of SCSP-FPELG increased from 68.36 N to 107.75 N, and the peak friction of OP-FPELG increased from 68.5 N to 108.92 N. Therefore, as the friction increases, the indicated power of FPELG decreases gradually, and there is no lateral force based on the structural features of FPELG. Thus, the energy loss owing to friction is relatively small. Furthermore, as the friction increased, the average velocity of the piston decreased, thereby lowering gas pressure within the cylinder. Consequently, the piston displacement and operational frequency decrease, affecting the indicated efficiency of FPELG. Ultimately, the increase in friction affected the engine performances of FPELG. Although the peak friction increase might have lowered the indicated power of FPELG, it is essential to highlight that the energy loss from the system friction remains minimal owing to the structural attributes of FPELG.

Indicated power of three typical FPELG structure configurations under various frictions.

Conclusions

This paper quantitatively compared the operating characteristics of the piston and the engine performances of the three typical FPELG configurations. Furthermore, the energy-flow distribution features of each structure configuration of FPELG were examined. The results are outlined as follows:

The DCDP-FPELG exhibited the highest piston operation frequency. The piston displacement curves of OP-FPELG were symmetrically distributed, allowing for the synchronous operation of the kinetic pistons on both sides. The OP-FPELG boasts an indicated thermal efficiency of 34%, the highest among all configurations. However, its indicated power fell short of that of DCDP-FPELG, standing at 3.7 kW. In contrast to the expansion process, the peak velocity of the piston during the compression process was low in both SCSP-FPELG and OP-FPELG.

The indicated power of each FPELG configuration was negatively correlated with the heat-transfer area. The variation range of OP-FPELG indicated that the power was smaller than that of the other two configurations of FPELG. Furthermore, when the heat-transfer area varied within a narrow range, the heat-transfer coefficient was a less sensitive parameter for the FPELG. Moreover, the indicated power of FPELG decreased gradually with an increase in friction.

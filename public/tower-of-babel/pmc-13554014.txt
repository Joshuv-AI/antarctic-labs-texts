Intelligent neuro computing paradigm for thermophoresis in carboxymethyl cellulose–water trihybrid nanoliquid with heat generation effects
Authors: Fawaz Alanazi, Ghada A. Alsawah, Achraf Ben Miled, Fuad Alsarari, Gulnar Hamidova Abdulhamid, Ilkhom Khaydarov, Ansar Abbas, Nidhal Ben Khedher
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This research uses thermophoretic particle deposition and a heat source to examine the effects of heat production on a trihybrid nanofluid based on carboxymethyl cellulose and water. A fundamental technique in electrical and aero solution engineering for transporting small particles over a heat gradient is thermophoretic particle deposition. This model is beneficial for increasing the efficacy and architecture of modern thermal management systems that rely on thermophoretic particle movement, like as industrial heat exchangers, electronic component cooling, and polymer processing with CMC-based fluid. The framework, which includes Stefan blowing, internal heat generation, and intelligent neuro-computing techniques, enables efficient control of nanoparticle distribution and heat transfer rates, resulting in predictive optimization and better performance in complex thermal processing and energy applications. With the advancement of associated technologies, the importance of artificial intelligence and machine learning has grown significantly. To address the mathematical formulation, this study trains a ML (machine learning) model based on artificial neural networks using the Bayesian-Regularized approach. The concentration profile decreases as the values of the thermophoretic parameters grow.

The relative contribution of thermal energy exchange between a heated surface and its surroundings is characterized by the dimensionless Stefan parameter, sometimes referred to as the Stefan number. Ludwig Boltzmann and Josef Stefan’s groundbreaking contributions to thermal radiation theory are connected to this parameter. When studying heat transport systems where radiative effects are not insignificant, it is essential. The relative impact of radiative heat transport vs conductive pathways is measured by the Stefan parameter. Smaller values of the Stefan number suggest that conduction is still the predominant mode, but larger values suggest that radiation controls the heat transfer process. Senbagaraja and De [21] used a porous spinning disc accounting for variable thermal conductivity along with thermal radiation impacts and Stefan blowing to investigate the parametric sensitivity of electro-osmotic EMHD tangent hyperbolic nanofluid dynamics. Alrehili [22] conducted a detailed examination of Casson nanofluid dynamics within a permeable medium, emphasizing the roles of non-uniform thermal generation, and Thompson–Troian slip mechanisms. Rana et al. [23] presented an examination of electromagnetic radiative nanofluids over a sheet, highlighting the functions of chemical reactivity, SB (Stefan blowing), and entropy generation. Maiti and Mukhopadhyay [24] analyze the result of SB, thermal radiation on nanofluid flow induced by an expanding disk.

These nanofluids offer superior thermal and physical characteristics as compared to regular ones. Many technical operations, such as nuclear reactor protection, powdered coal burners, depend on TPD in a fluid flow. The basic process is the result of various particle classes acting differently when exposed to a temperature gradient. This process greatly increases the minute particle deposition flow in the way of decreasing thermal, while it has no impact on large particles. Gas molecules in the cold zone typically move at a slow speed than those in the particle’s warm region. High-velocity particles clash with one another. The particles then acquire a velocity as a result of this momentum differential, commonly termed the thermophoretic flow. A thermal gradient applies the thermophoretic force to suspended particles. Anbuchezhian et al. [34] scrutinized magnetohydrodynamics laminar BL (boundary layer) flow through a permeable wedge with thermophoretic deposition under varying stream conditions Madhukesh et al. [35] described the thermos-phoretic particle deposition of nanofluids under mixed convection on an infinite porous plate utilizing simulation based on response surface technique and Crank Nicolson. [37] inspected the Oldroyd-B liquid flow over a spinning disc with thermophoresis. Tejaswini et al. [38] scrutinize the combined effect of thermophoresis on Howarth’s wavy permeable circular cylinder in stagnation point flow.

The transfer of heat or energy via electromagnetic waves is known as thermal radiation. Significant temperature variations between two media are the source of this. The effects of radiation are significant in both engineering and physics. The impacts of radiation heat transfer on different flow situations are crucial in the fields of high-temperature operations and space technology. Radiation effects are crucial for monitoring heat transfer in the polymer sector since heat-controlling components affect the final product’s quality. Additionally, sun radiation, nuclear power plants, airplanes, missiles, gas engines, and spacecraft all have significant radiative effects. Goud et al. [39] explored the heat transfer significance of MHD thermal radiation transient across a sheet using AE. Bilal et al. [40] presented a research on heat transport characteristics of HNF flow subjected to thermal radiation within the region bounded by two coaxial cylinders. Ahmed et al. [41] examined a detailed research on HNF via emphasizing the roles of space-dependent temperature radiation effects. The outcome of thermal radiation and impinging jet cooling on mixed generation of entropy and convection in a semicircular permeable channel are investigated by Halder et al. [42].

Solution methodology

The proposed IBRS-ANN uses the tansig (sigmoid) activation function and features a single hidden layer with 15 neurons. The dataset was split into 80% training and20% testing groups at random using a total of 1001 samples produced from the numerical solutions. An ANNs is a sophisticated computational framework modeled after the human brain’s interconnected neuronal structure. Its fundamental processing units, adaptive connectivity, scalar message exchange, and extensive inter-unit communication categorize it as a multiprocessor computer system. Actually, the ANNs is suitable for rainfall and discharge prediction since it deals an effective modeling technique. Layers of neurons make up an ANNs. The artificial neural networks comprise three layers: hidden, output and input. Data is received by the input layer, the hidden layers perform analysis and transformation, and the output layer produces predictions. Weights are the connecting lines that neurons use to communicate. The ANNs method section contains these details, and the ensuing regression, MSE, error histogram, absolute error, curve-fitting, and comparison tests authenticate the anticipated accuracy and dependability of the constructed network. Conclusions

This model is suitable to the innovation and improvement of modern thermal management systems where thermophoretic particle transport is important, such as industrial heat exchangers, electronic component cooling, and polymer processing using CMC-based fluid. The framework, which incorporates Stefan blowing, internal heat generation, and intelligent neuro-computing techniques, allows for efficient control of nanoparticle distribution and heat transfer rates, supporting predictive optimization and improved performance in complex thermal processing and energy-related applications.

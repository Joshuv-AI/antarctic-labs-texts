Volume as an index of a subalgebra Preprint: MIT-CTP/5897
Sam Leutheusser, Hong Liu

Abstract
We propose a new way to understand the volume of certain subregions in the bulk of AdS spacetime by relating it to an algebraic quantity known as the index of inclusion. This index heuristically measures the relative size of a subalgebra 𝒩 {{\mathcal{N}}} embedded within a larger algebra ℳ {{\mathcal{M}}} . According to subregion-subalgebra duality, bulk subregions are described by von Neumann algebras on the boundary. When a causally complete bulk subregion corresponds to the relative commutant 𝒩 ′ ∩ ℳ {{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}\cap{{\mathcal{M}}} —the set of operators in ℳ {{\mathcal{M}}} that commute with 𝒩 {{\mathcal{N}}} —of boundary subalgebras, we propose that the exponential of the volume of the maximal volume slice of the subregion equals the index of inclusion. This “volume-index” relation provides a new boundary explanation for the growth of interior volume in black holes, reframing it as a change in the relative size of operator algebras. It offers a complementary perspective on complexity growth from the Heisenberg picture, and has a variety of other applications, including quantifying the relative size of algebras dual to the entanglement wedge and the causal wedge of a boundary region, as well as quantifying the violation of additivity of operator algebras in the large N N limit. Finally, it may offer insights into the volume growth of de Sitter space through the changes in North and South pole observer algebras in time.

Two intersecting intervals for vacuum CFT

IV
Possible finite
N
interpretation of the index

IV.1
Further elaboration on the index formula

In equation (
4
), we proposed that the bulk volume can be used to obtain an index for boundary algebra inclusions. However, there are a number of issues that should be further clarified.

There are two ways of interpreting equation (
4
):

1.

It applies to algebras that are defined only in the large
N
limit.

2.

It applies to algebras that can be intrinsically defined at finite
N
, and gives the leading order behavior of the indices of such algebras in the large
N
limit. This would be analogous to the black hole entropy formula.

First consider interpretation 1 above, which appears natural as all the algebras in our earlier discussion have been
formulated in the large
N
limit. With these algebras being type III
1
, we can in principle define the Kosaki (or equivalently Longo) index for their inclusions. However, for the inclusions of under discussion, it is expected that the Kosaki indices are infinite
in the continuum limit.
12
12

12

In the continuum limit of the bulk EFT the algebra inclusions we are describing are split inclusions of type III
1
factors, for which the normal conditional expectation needed for a finite Kosaki index does not exist. See, for example, section 6 of
Faulkner:2024gst
for more details.

To have a finite index, we can consider introducing a
bulk cutoff
,
\epsilon
, to regularize
the algebras to type I, and accordingly the constant
C
in the exponent of (
4
) should have the form

C={c\over\epsilon^{a}l_{\rm AdS}^{b}}

(39)

where
c
is some dimensionless constant, and
a+b=d
. At this stage it is not completely clear what
a
should be.
One natural candidate is
a=d,\penalty\ b=0
, corresponding to a uniform lattice in all bulk directions. Another, holographically motivated, candidate is
a=d-1,\penalty\ b=1
, where the discretization of the bulk radial direction is related to the boundary time-resolution.

Now consider interpretation 2, which requires the existence of algebras that can be defined at finite
N
. So far, most algebras discussed in Sec.
II
and Sec.
III
—such as time-band algebras and causal wedge algebras—have only been defined precisely in the
N\to\infty
limit. Below we present an argument based on the generalized gravitational entropy that these algebras may also be defined at finite
N
and become type I. If they can indeed be defined at finite
N
, then it is natural for the constant
C
in (
4
) to have the form

C={c\over G_{N}l_{\rm AdS}}

(40)

where
c
is again some dimensionless number. Equation (
40
) may be also interpreted from (
39
) with
a=d-1
and
\epsilon=\ell_{p}
, the Planck length.

Below we present arguments for the existence of the algebras at finite
N
and (
39
) with
a=d-1
. The argument is somewhat speculative, but nevertheless suggestive.

IV.2
Algebras at finite
N
and generalized gravitational entropy

We now argue that the algebras discussed in Sec.
III
can be extended to finite
N
.
We will use the time-band algebra
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}
associated with a boundary time-band
I
in the vacuum sector as an illustration.
In the large
N
limit,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}
is dual to the bulk spherical Rindler region (see the red region of Fig.
4
Left)
{\bf B}=\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
, with
the identification
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathcal{Y}}_{I}
(
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is the bulk algebra in
{\mathfrak{b}}
).
Since
I
covers a full Cauchy slice on the boundary, a possible finite-
N
extension of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}
is
{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm CFT})
, the full boundary operator algebra. We now argue that there could exist a finite
N
extension of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}
that
is smaller than
{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm CFT})
.

For this purpose, consider first the bulk algebra
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
in the
G_{N}\to 0
limit, i.e. perturbatively in
G_{N}
.
It is type III
1
in the continuum, and an entropy cannot be defined.
Nevertheless, it is generally believed (see for example appendix A of
Bousso:2015mna
for a review) that the generalized gravitational entropy

S_{\rm gen}[{\mathfrak{b}}]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left({{\rm Area}(\partial{\mathfrak{b}})\over 4G_{N}(\epsilon)}+\widetilde{S}_{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\epsilon)\right)

(41)

exists. Here
\epsilon
is a bulk short-distance cutoff,
\widetilde{S}_{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\epsilon)
is the bulk entanglement entropy for
{\mathfrak{b}}
, and
G_{N}(\epsilon)
is the bare Newton constant (viewed as a coupling in the bulk effective field theory).

In the presence of a bulk cutoff
\epsilon
, the bulk algebra
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
turns into a type I algebra
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\epsilon}
, which now has an entropy
\widetilde{S}_{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\epsilon)
. The entropy
\widetilde{S}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\epsilon)
is divergent in the
\epsilon\to 0
limit, with the leading divergence of the form

\widetilde{S}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\epsilon)=f{{\rm Area}(\partial{\mathfrak{b}})\over\epsilon^{d-1}}+\cdots,

(42)

where
f
is some constant and
\cdots
denotes less divergent and finite terms in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
.
The specific form of the cutoff, e.g., a lattice regularization, is not important. The bare coupling
G_{N}(\epsilon)
is also divergent in the
\epsilon\to 0
limit.
Equation (
41

From subregion-subalgebra duality, we should have

\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\epsilon}={\mathcal{Y}}_{I}^{\epsilon}.

(43)

Namely, there exists a regularization on the boundary in the
N\to\infty
limit that turns
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}
into a type I algebra
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}^{\epsilon}
. While we do not currently know how to describe the regularization explicitly for a general boundary theory, the duality implies that it must exist. Note that at this stage
\epsilon
is independent of the renormalized value of
G_{N}
.

The existence of a well-defined limit for equation (
41
) can be interpreted as the existence of an extension of generalized gravitational entropy
S_{\rm gen}[{\mathfrak{b}}]
to finite
G_{N}
, just as in the case of black hole entropy. Here by finite
G_{N}
(or
N
), we always mean treating
G_{N}
(or
1/N
) non-perturbatively. It is then natural to interpret
S_{\rm gen}[{\mathfrak{b}}]
as the entropy for a finite
N
extension of
the time-band algebra
{\mathcal{Y}}_{I}^{\epsilon}
. After all, when
\epsilon\sim\ell_{p}
, one can no longer separate the area term in (
41
)
and the term (
42
) in
\widetilde{S}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\epsilon)
. We denote this sector-dependent finite-
N
extension as
{{\mathcal{B}}}_{I}
.
{{\mathcal{B}}}_{I}
cannot be the full boundary algebra
{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm CFT})
as
{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm CFT})
has zero entropy in the vacuum state.
Note that at finite
N
, the region
{\mathfrak{b}}
can no longer be sharply defined due to spacetime fluctuations.
What we are saying is that there is nevertheless a precisely-defined algebra.
We stress that the definition of
{{\mathcal{B}}}_{I}
is sector-dependent, as the physics associated with the bulk dual region
{\mathfrak{b}}
is different
in empty AdS and in a black hole geometry (or any bulk dual with a different geometry in
{\mathfrak{b}}
).

V
Algebraic probe of de Sitter volume growth

We now use our proposal (
4
) to describe a probe of the volume growth of de Sitter space formulated in terms of restricted algebras of observables associated to observers fixed at the North and South poles. We first review the static and Kruskal coordinate systems on de Sitter (dS) space. We then describe the relevant algebras of observables associated to observers travelling along the worldlines of the North and South poles. Finally, we apply our proposal and discuss how the growth of the volume of the region between the North and South cosmological horizons implies a particular behavior of the observers’ algebras.

V.1
Kruskal and Static Coordinates on dS
d+1

Consider
(d+1)

d+1

\mathbb{R}\times S_{d}

d+1
is covered by “Kruskal coordinates”

ds^{2}=-\frac{2dUdV}{(UV-1)^{2}}+\frac{(UV+1)^{2}}{(UV-1)^{2}}d\Omega_{d-1}^{2}\ ,

(44)

where

The coordinate system naturally associated to an observer at the North or South pole of the

d+1

(45)

The regions accessible to observers at the North and South poles are described by the above coordinates with

V.2
Algebras of North/South Pole Observables

Our algebraic probe of the volume growth of de Sitter is constructed by considering the algebras of observables accessible to an observer at the North or South pole during fixed ranges of their proper time. We denote the algebra of observables accessible to an observer at the North (South) pole during the proper-time-window
t\in({\mathfrak{t}},\infty)
by
{{\mathcal{A}}}_{N_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}
(
{{\mathcal{A}}}_{S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}
).
13
13

13

Note that we take both the proper time of the North and South observers to run forwards, so
t
runs in the opposite direction to the static time Killing vector in the region accessible from the South pole.

One finds that operations performed at the North pole at time
{\mathfrak{t}}
can only effect the region of de Sitter space with
V>e^{{\mathfrak{t}}}
. Similarly, operations performed at the South pole at time
{\mathfrak{t}}
can only effect the region of de Sitter space with
U>e^{{\mathfrak{t}}}
. By the time-like tube theorem one then has that
{{\mathcal{A}}}_{N_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}
is equivalent to algebra of all operations in the subregion

is equivalent to algebra of all operations in the subregion
\mathbf{S}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}=\{U>e^{{\mathfrak{t}}},\penalty\ V<0,\penalty\ -1<UV<1\}
. We denote the union of these two algebras by
{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\equiv{{\mathcal{A}}}_{N_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}\vee{{\mathcal{A}}}_{S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}

{{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
is dual to the region
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}=\{0<U<e^{{\mathfrak{t}}},\penalty\ 0<V<e^{{\mathfrak{t}}},\penalty\ UV<1\}

Our probe for the volume growth of de Sitter spacetime will be the growth of the “size” of the algebra

as
{\mathfrak{t}}
is increased. Note that
{{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
may be interpreted as the relative commutant for the inclusion
{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\subset{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{dS})

is the Hilbert space for quantum fields on the de Sitter background, suggesting that the “size” of
{{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
may be related to an index for this inclusion.

V.3
Applying our proposal to de Sitter

Our proposal is that an index for the inclusion
{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\subset{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{dS})
is given by the exponential of the volume of the maximal slice,
{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}},
of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
. Explicitly, our proposal is that

\left({{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{dS}):{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\right)=\exp\left(C\penalty\ {\rm Vol}({\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}})\right)\ ,

(46)

for some constant
C
.

Note that we have the inclusion
{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\subset{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}}
whenever
{\mathfrak{t}}>{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}
which implies the inclusion
{{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\supset{{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}}
, showing that the algebra
{{\mathcal{N}}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
does indeed grow as
{\mathfrak{t}}
is increased, reflecting the increase in
{\rm Vol}({\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}})
. Geometrically, we can think of the increase of
{\mathfrak{t}}
as an increase in the affine time of the ‘cuts’ of the future horizons for the North and South poles (respectively
U=0,\penalty\ V=e^{{\mathfrak{t}}}
and
U=e^{{\mathfrak{t}}},\penalty\ V=0
) that define the boundary of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
.

The discussion so far has been almost identical to the case of the eternal AdS black hole with the role of the Left/Right asymptotic boundary played by the South/North pole observer; however, the behavior of
{\rm Vol}({\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}})
is dramatically different in the de Sitter case. The important difference is that, despite the similarities between the future singularity of the eternal AdS black hole and future infinity of dS
d+1
when drawn on the Penrose diagram, the future singularity is a surface with volume shrinking to zero, while future infinity of dS
d+1
is a surface of volume increasing to infinity. The implies that maxmial volume surfaces are “repelled” from the future singularity of the eternal AdS black hole while they are instead “attracted” to future infinity in dS
d+1

Susskind:2021esx
. This gives rise to different behaviors of
{\rm Vol}({\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}})
in de Sitter versus the eternal AdS black hole, despite the fact that the algebraic probes for the “interior” region,
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}},
are defined in the exact same way (when we view the North/South observer as analogous to the Left/Right asymptotic boundary).

One particularly stark manifestation of this difference is that
{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
can be cutoff dependent
in dS
d+1
.
More explicitly, when
{\mathfrak{t}}>0
,
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
contains points at future infinity, meaning that a maximal volume slice,
{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}},
of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
becomes infinite for
{\mathfrak{t}}>0
.
We can regularize the infinity by cutting off dS at a “large time” by removing the part of the spacetime with
UV>1-\epsilon_{t}
for some small
\epsilon_{t}>0
. In the presence of this cutoff, the maximal volume slice of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}

for
{\mathfrak{t}}>0
runs along the cutoff hypersurface and becomes cutoff-dependent.
This is in contrast to the eternal AdS black hole case where no “near-singularity” cutoff was needed since maximal volume slices never approached the singularity.
The cutoff dependence here is reminiscent of the case for an index calculation dual to a subregion in AdS that intersects the boundary, where the bulk volume becomes divergent and requires the introduction of an IR cutoff near the AdS boundary.

For
{\mathfrak{t}}<0,
the maximal volume slice,
{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
, of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
in de Sitter is independent of any cutoff; however, for
d>1

l_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
, between the North and South horizon ‘cuts’ (at the same position on the
S_{d-1}

\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
.
14
14

14

No smooth and cutoff-independent geodesic connects these ‘cuts’ for
{\mathfrak{t}}>0

We will call

the ‘length’ of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
and view it as a proxy for
{\rm Vol}({\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}})

l_{{\mathfrak{t}}}=\arccos\left(1-2e^{2{\mathfrak{t}}}\right),\qquad{\mathfrak{t}}<0\ .

(47)

The ‘length’ of

has the following early time behavior

l_{{\mathfrak{t}}}=2e^{{\mathfrak{t}}}+\frac{e^{3{\mathfrak{t}}}}{3}+O(e^{5{\mathfrak{t}}}),\penalty\ {\mathfrak{t}}\to-\infty\ .

(48)

We see that the length of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
grows exponentially with the observers’ early proper time cutoff
{\mathfrak{t}}
as

The ‘length’ of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
has the following near-zero time behavior

l_{{\mathfrak{t}}}=\pi-2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{-t}+O((-t)^{3\over 2}),\penalty\ {\mathfrak{t}}\to 0^{-}\ .

(49)

Thus the ‘length’ of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
goes to a constant as

\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
approaches future infinity). For
{\mathfrak{t}}>0

\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
diverges as the “large-time” cutoff is removed.

With this behavior of the ‘length’ of
\mathbf{D}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
understood, we see that (

{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
into
{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm dS})

{\mathfrak{t}}<0

{{\mathcal{B}}}({{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm dS})
that are not contained in
{{\mathcal{N}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
is independent of any “large time” cutoff, whereas for
{\mathfrak{t}}>0
this number of operators becomes cutoff-dependent. It is interesting to note that this transition precisely occurs where signals sent from
{{\mathcal{A}}}_{S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}
and
{{\mathcal{A}}}_{N_{{\mathfrak{t}}}}
can no longer interact at any point in the future.

The divergence in the volume of maximal slices in dS has been interpreted as an exponential rate of complexity growth at late times
Susskind:2021esx
and has been called “hyperfast” complexity growth. It would be interesting to understand if our proposed discontinuous jump in algebra “size” can be connected to “hyperfast” complexity growth.

VI
Discussion

In this paper, we have proposed that the maximal volume slice of a bulk domain of dependence defines a new type of index for a corresponding boundary algebra inclusion. Heuristically, the exponential of the bulk volume measures the “size” of a relative commutant algebra on the boundary which characterizes the boundary algebra inclusion. We have discussed how this index shares some properties with the standard Kosaki index
kosakiIndex
, but is also not equivalent to this standard definition. We have also computed this holographic index in many cases of interest.

Applying our proposal to the Wheeler-de Witt patch of an eternal AdS black hole we have argued that the shrinking of a particular boundary algebra with the increase of the left and right boundary anchoring time is dual to the bulk volume growth. We then described a heuristic connection between algebra shrinking and boundary complexity growth. We discussed two possible interpretations of our index formula and argued that the well-definedness of the generalized entropy at finite
G_{N}
suggests that our index can be extended to the finite
N
setting; however, we do not have any explicit description of this extension. Finally, we applied our proposal to two antipodal observers in de Sitter space and discussed the algebraic consequences implied by the rapid growth of the volume of the region between the two observers’ future cosmological horizons.

Our proposal provides important insights into the boundary characterization of bulk subregions in holographic systems; however, there are still many aspects that should be better understood. The most pressing question raised by our proposal is: can we understand physics in bulk subregions at finite
G_{N}
through state-dependent type I boundary algebras? We have argued that such a description should exist and our proposal gives a conjecture for the size of these algebras, however an explicit description remains elusive.

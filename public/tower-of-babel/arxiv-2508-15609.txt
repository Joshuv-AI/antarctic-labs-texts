Sub-GeV Sterile Neutrino as a Probe of Neutrino Mass Generation in the Minimal Left-Right Symmetric Model
Gang Li, Ying-Ying Li, Sida Lu, Ye-Ling Zhou

Abstract
The minimal left-right symmetric model (mLRSM) provides an elegant and testable framework for addressing the origin of neutrino masses. We examine the constraints on the sub-GeV sterile neutrino in the type-II seesaw scenario of the mLRSM without left-right mixing, taking limits from collider searches, meson decays, supernovae, neutrinoless double beta ( 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta ) decay and cosmology. Specifically, we derive the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay constraints using the advanced effective field theory approach and up-to-date nuclear matrix element calculations. Besides, we update the SN1987A cooling bound with the state-of-the-art simulations, provide new constraints from the energy deposition in the supernova ejecta, and incorporate the stringent sterile neutrino lifetime upper limit τ ≲ 0.023 \tau\lesssim 0.023 s from the big bang nucleosynthesis. Our results identify the parameter region compatible with all current experimental and observational constraints, where the sterile neutrino mass lies between 700 MeV and 1 GeV and the right-handed W W boson mass is slightly below 20 TeV. This region is exclusively probed by the future tonne-scale 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay experiments, providing a unique window to test the mLRSM and the possible origin of neutrino masses.



I
Introduction

Neutrinos, among the most elusive particles, provide one of the clearest clues for physics beyond the standard model (BSM).
The genesis of their unnaturally small masses remains a major puzzle despite longstanding endeavors. One of the most elegant approaches addressing this problem is the seesaw mechanism
Minkowski 1977
;
Yanagida 1979
;
Gell-Mann et al. 1979
;
Mohapatra and Senjanovic 1980
;
Glashow 1980
.
With a Dirac mass of electroweak scale as a pivot, the masses of active neutrinos are naturally suppressed by the ultra-large right-handed (RH) Majorana neutrino masses.
Yet, this most naive scenario expects the RH neutrinos to be as heavy as
10^{15}
GeV, far beyond foreseeable experimental verifications.
More testable solutions emerge when embedding the seesaw mechanism within left-right symmetric models
Pati and Salam 1974
;
Mohapatra and Pati 1975a
;
Mohapatra and Pati 1975b
;
Senjanovic and Mohapatra 1975
;
Senjanovic 1979
. In particular, the minimal left-right symmetric model (mLRSM)
Mohapatra and Senjanovic 1980
;
Mohapatra and Senjanovic 1981
stands out for its notable predictability, and the possibilities of parity restoration and grand unification at high energy scales
Senjanovic 2011
.
Determining the origin of neutrino masses thus requires both verifying the possible Majorana nature of neutrinos and searching for additional RH particles, especially the RH neutrinos and gauge bosons.

As the most powerful particle generator ever, the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) can search for RH gauge bosons and neutrinos in the mLRSM thorough the well-known Keung-Senjanović process
Keung and Senjanovic 1983
, setting a lower bound on the mass of the RH
W
boson (
M_{W_{R}}
) to be about 6 TeV in general
Aad et al. 2023
;
Aad et al. 2019a
;
Tumasyan et al. 2022
.
Specifically, the same-sign lepton channel of the search can distinguish between Majorana and Dirac RH neutrinos.
Nevertheless, the sensitivity to
M_{W_{R}}
does not improve much at the high-luminosity LHC, as the production cross section decreases significantly with the increase of
M_{W_{R}}

Nemevšek et al. 2018
.
Instead, in certain parameter region, more stringent constraints are provided by other experiments and observations.
For RH neutrino with mass below GeV which is also dubbed as sterile neutrino, the focus of this work, it has been shown that future tonne-scale
0\nu\beta\beta
experiments
Abgrall et al. 2021
;
Adhikari et al. 2022
;
Agostini et al. 2023
could be sensitive to
M_{W_{R}}
as large as 25 TeV
de Vries et al. 2022
, which simultaneously provides the most sensitive and unambiguous probe of neutrinos’ Majorana nature.
Various other constraints on
M_{W_{R}}
also exist for different sub-GeV sterile neutrino mass ranges.
The SN1987A cooling bound has been estimated to exclude
M_{W_{R}}\lesssim 23
TeV for a sterile neutrino mass smaller than 10 MeV
Barbieri and Mohapatra 1989
.
Meson decays have also been recently shown to exclude
M_{W_{R}}\lesssim(4-19)
TeV for sterile neutrinos in the mass range
[50\penalty\ \text{MeV},1\penalty\ \text{GeV}]

Alves et al. 2024
.
Other weaker constraints on
M_{W_{R}}
in the mLRSM can be found in Ref.
Group 2025
.

In this work, we investigate and update the constraints on the sub-GeV sterile neutrino in the type-II seesaw scenario of the mLRSM without left-right mixing.
For both the current
0\nu\beta\beta
searches at KamLAND-Zen
Abe et al. 2023
and future tonne-scale experiments
Abgrall et al. 2021
;
Adhikari et al. 2022
;
Agostini et al. 2023
, we derive the constraints using the advanced effective field theory (EFT) approach and up-to-date nuclear matrix element (NME) calculations.
With the state-of-the-art simulations, we extend the applicability of the SN1987A cooling bound from sterile neutrino mass below 10 MeV to a larger mass range, and provide a new constraint by considering the energy deposition in the supernova ejecta.
We also examined other constraints, including those from the LHC, meson decays and cosmology.
As a result, we identify parameter region that remains compatible with all current experimental and observational constraints and can be uniquely probed by future
0\nu\beta\beta
decay experiments.

III.2
Supernova constraints

Supernovae (SNe) are ideal test grounds for the sub-GeV sterile neutrino and the underlying mLRSM, as their explosion dynamics and relevant observations may be significantly influenced by the light BSM particles.
Below we briefly explain how the SN constraints are drawn, leaving more calculation details in Appendix
C
.

The sterile neutrino
\nu_{4}
s are mainly produced in the core of the SN via
e^{-}+p\to\nu_{4}+n

3
3

3

The abundance of muons
Bollig et al. 2020

.
Note that the leading production channel in the type-I seesaw scenario,
\nu+N\to\nu_{4}+N

;
Chauhan et al. 2025

M_{D}

The other possible channel,
\nu+\nu\to\nu_{4}+\nu_{4}
mediated by the
Z_{R}
boson, turns out to be sub-leading.
For our calculations, we use the simulation SFHo-18.6 in Ref.
Bollig et al. 2020
performed by the Garching group for the profiles of all necessary thermodynamics quantities.

The sterile neutrinos propagate to the outside of the star through the envelope region after being produced, during which their flux get modified.
Their energy inevitably experiences a redshift induced by the stellar body’s gravitational potential.
More importantly, their flux intensity is dissipated during propagation, due to their own decay or the stellar envelope’s absorption through
\nu_{4}+n\to e^{-}+p
and
\nu_{4}+p\to e^{+}+n

These fail-to-escape sterile neutrinos deposit energy into the stellar envelope, increasing the total

;
Sung et al. 2019
;

The constraint on the total deposited energy is usually taken to be

e^{\pm}

\nu_{4}

10^{12}\,\text{g/cm}^{3}

Furthermore, in principle the two aforementioned absorption processes should also contribute to the energy deposition, as the final products are easily trapped in the ejecta.
Note that the absorption processes were not considered when SNe were used to constrain light BSM particles like dark photons or axion-like particles

;
Sung et al. 2019

Sterile neutrinos that escape the SN carry away energy, contributing an additional cooling channel for the star.
With the total budget of

III.3
Cosmological constraints

Stringent constraints on sterile neutrinos also arise from the modifications brought to the several crucial cosmic evolution eras, specifically the BBN and the cosmic microwave background (CMB).
Due to the relatively large interaction rate, the
\nu_{4}
in the mLRSM will inevitably thermalize during the early universe and then freeze out, see Appendix
D
for more details on
\nu_{4}
’s thermal history.
Meanwhile, the decay products of
\nu_{4}
may spoil the abundance of protons, neutrons or light elements like
4
He from the BBN through extra entropy injection, changing the cosmic expansion rate, or shifting the equilibrium of the reactions.
The sterile neutrinos are thus expected to decay either much earlier or much later than the time period relevant for the BBN, the former of which translates to an
upper
bound on
M_{W_{R}}
.
For
m_{\nu_{4}}>m_{\pi}
where the hadronic decay of
\nu_{4}
is available, the most stringent constraint is obtained from the injected pions’ shifting the
p\leftrightarrow n
conversion equilibrium, requiring the lifetime of sterile neutrinos
\tau_{\nu_{4}}\lesssim 0.023\text{ s}
4
4

4

Despite obtained from a 3+1 active-sterile neutrino mixing model, this lifetime constraint can be directly translated to the mLRSM, as both models are dominated by the same hadronic decay mode in this parameter region.
It is worth noting that a weaker condition

was employed in Refs.
Nemevsek et al. 2011
;
Nemevšek and Nesti 2023
.

Boyarsky et al. 2021
.
Though this BBN constraint becomes irrelevant for

\nu_{4}

For

From the model-building point of view, one possible approach to evade the cosmological constraint in this region is to introduce an additional completely invisible decay channel for

\nu_{4}+\nu_{4}\leftrightarrow\nu+\nu
.
The thermal averaged cross section can be roughly estimated as

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim N\dfrac{g^{4}s}{M_{V}^{4}}\,,\quad s\sim\text{max}(T^{2},m^{2}_{\nu_{4}})\,,

(46)

The freeze-out should happen at around
n\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim H
, where
n
corresponds to the number density of the SM particles for the former process and that of
\nu_{4}
for the latter, taking the values of
T^{3}/(\pi^{2})
and
m^{2}_{\nu_{4}}TK_{2}(m_{\nu_{4}}/T)/(2\pi^{3})
, respectively, with
K_{2}(z)
the modified Bessel function of the second kind.
However, it turns out that
n\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim H
is usually achieved when
T\gg m_{\nu_{4}}
, and the number density
n
for both processes are thus proportional to
T^{3}
.
With
\langle\sigma v\rangle
taking a value comparable to that in the interaction rate (
44
), we have the freeze-out temperature
T_{\rm fo}
to be around
T_{\rm dec}
.
The energy density ratio between
\nu_{4}
and the thermal bath at BBN is thus

\displaystyle\dfrac{\rho_{\nu_{4}}}{\rho_{\rm SM}}=\dfrac{m_{\nu_{4}}n_{\nu_{4}}}{\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}g_{\ast,{\rm BBN}}\,T^{4}_{\rm BBN}}=\dfrac{m_{\nu_{4}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}T^{3}_{\rm fo}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\rm BBN}}{T_{\rm fo}}\right)^{3}}{\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}g_{\ast,{\rm BBN}}\,T^{4}_{\rm BBN}}=\frac{30}{g_{\ast,{\rm BBN}}\pi^{4}}\dfrac{m_{\nu_{4}}}{T_{\rm BBN}}\gg 1\,,

(47)

meaning that a non-decaying
\nu_{4}
will also spoil BBN through the influence on the expansion rate.

W_{L}-W_{R}

We now discuss how the model and the corresponding constraints are changed at
\xi\neq 0

Two new decay channels become opened: the photonic decay
\nu_{4}\to\nu\gamma

\nu_{4}\to l^{+}l^{-}\nu

Bondarenko et al. 2018

The former arises from the induced neutrino transition dipole moment at one-loop level

The hadronic decay width (
32

(1-2\xi/(1+\xi^{2}))^{2}
, while is still the the most efficient channel at
m_{\nu_{4}}>m_{\pi}

The derivation of the
\gamma
-ray constraint is less direct compared with the cooling bound and the energy deposition bound due to its geometry.
Let the direction from the SN to the telescope be the polar axis,
\iota
the azimuthal angle of the escaped sterile neutrino’s propagation direction,
\alpha
the angle between the sterile neutrino’s propagation direction and it’s daughter photon’s. And denote

The time delay between the neutrino burst and the photon from the sterile neutrino’s decay is then

\displaystyle\delta t=x/\beta+L-d_{\rm NS}\,,

\beta

Since the parameter space of our interest always implies

\iota

\displaystyle\delta t\approx x(1/\beta-\cos\alpha)\approx x(1/\beta-\cos\iota)\,.

\displaystyle P_{\gamma}(\iota,x)=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\gamma^{2}(1-\beta\cos\alpha)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{m_{\nu_{4}}}{2\gamma(1+\beta\cos\alpha)}\,.

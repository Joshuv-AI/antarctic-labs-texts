Affirmative answer to the Question of Leroy and Matczuk on injectivity of endomorphisms of semiprime left Noetherian rings with large images
V. V. Bavula

Abstract
The class of semiprime left Goldie rings is a huge class of rings that contains many large subclasses of rings – semiprime left Noetherian rings, semiprime rings with Krull dimension, rings of differential operators on affine algebraic varieties and universal enveloping algebras of finite dimensional Lie algebras to name a few. In the paper, ‘Ring endomorphisms with large images,’ Glasg. Math. J. 55 (2013), no. 2, 381–390, A. Leroy and J. Matczuk posed the following question: If a ring endomorphism of a semiprime left Noetherian ring has a large image, must it be injective? The aim of the paper is to give an affirmative answer to the Question of Leroy and Matczuk and to prove the following more general results. Theorem. (Dichotomy) Each endomorphism of a semiprime left Goldie ring with large image is either a monomorphism or otherwise its kernel contains a regular element of the ring ( ⇔ \Leftrightarrow its kernel is an essential left ideal of the ring). In general, both cases are non-empty. Theorem. Every endomorphism with large image of a semiprime ring with Krull dimension is a monomorphism. Theorem. (Positive answer to the Question of Leroy and Matczuk) Every endomorphism with large image of a semiprime left Noetherian ring is a monomorphism. Key Words : Semiprime ring, semiprime left Goldie ring, left Noethrian ring, ring with Krull dimension, endomorphism with large image, monomorphism. Introduction

In the paper, module means a left module,
R

A left ideal of a ring is an

Recall that a ring

is a semisimple Artinian ring

a left ideal of a semiprime left Goldie ring is essential iff it contains a regular element of the ring

Theorem 1.1
.

(Dichotomy)
Each endomorphism of a semiprime left Goldie ring with large image is either a monomorphism or otherwise its kernel contains a regular element of the ring (
\Leftrightarrow
its kernel is an essential left ideal of the ring). In general, both cases are non-empty.

There are proper containments of the following classes of rings:

\displaystyle\{\rm semiprime\;left\;Goldie\;rings\}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\{\rm semiprime\;rings\;with\;Krull\;dimension\}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\{\rm semiprime\;left\;Noetherian\;rings\},

This follows from the following facts.
A semiprime ring with Krull dimension is a left order in a semisimple artinian ring,
[
2

[

Semiprime ring with Krull dimension need not be left Noetherian,
[

Semiprime left Goldie rings are precisely

Theorem 1.2

Every endomorphism with large image of a semiprime ring with Krull dimension is a monomorphism.

The example below shows that in general both cases in Theorem

4
, Theorem 1.12]
, shows that every large endomorphism of a semiprime left Goldie ring that respects regular elements is a monomorphism. Here we give a different and shorter proof.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
R
be a semiprime left Goldie ring and
\sigma\in{\rm End}(R)
be a large endomorphism. Suppose that
\sigma({\cal C}_{R})\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
then
\sigma
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\neq 0
, we seek a contradiction. On the one hand, by Theorem
2.2
.(3),

{\rm udim}(\sigma(R))={\rm udim}(R)-{\rm udim}({\rm ker}_{R}(\sigma))<{\rm udim}(R).

On the other hand, the endomorphism
\sigma
is large, i.e. the ring
\sigma(R)
contains an essential left ideal, say
I
, of the ring
R
. Therefore,

{\rm udim}(\sigma(R))\geq{\rm udim}({}_{\sigma(R)}I)\geq{\rm udim}({}_{R}I)={\rm udim}(R),

a contradiction.
∎

Large subrings of semiprime left Goldie rings, their regular elements and left quotient rings.
A subring
R^{\prime}
of a ring
R
is called a
large subring
if the ring
R^{\prime}
contains an essential left ideal of
R
.

For a large subring
R^{\prime}
of a ring
R
,
Theorem
2.4
gives explicit descriptions of the sets
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
and
Q_{l}(R^{\prime})
in terms of the sets
{\cal C}_{R}
and
Q_{l}(R)
. It also shows that all large subrings
R^{\prime}
of a ring
R
are semiprime left Goldie rings.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
R
be a semiprime left Goldie ring,
R^{\prime}
be a large subring of
R
that contains an essential left ideal
I

of
R
. Then:

1.

{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}={\cal C}_{R}\cap R^{\prime}
.

2.

For each element
c\in{\cal C}_{R}
, there is an element
c^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{R}
such that
c^{\prime}c\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
.

3.

{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R^{\prime},0)\cap{\rm Den}_{l}(R,0)
.

4.

Q_{l}(R^{\prime})={\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}^{-1}R^{\prime}={\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}^{-1}R=Q_{l}(R)
.

5.

The ring
R^{\prime}
is semiprime left Goldie ring.

6.

{\rm udim}(R^{\prime})={\rm udim}(R)
.

Proof.

1. The inclusion
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\supseteq{\cal C}_{R}\cap R^{\prime}
is obvious. It remains to show that the reverse inclusion holds. Given an element
c\in R
,
then
c\in{\cal C}_{R}
iff
c\in{}^{\prime}{\cal C}_{R}
(since
Q_{l}(R)
is a semisimple Artinian ring) iff the map
\cdot c:I\rightarrow R
,
a\mapsto ac
is injective (since the left ideal
I
of
R
essential). Suppose that
c^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
. Then the map
\cdot c^{\prime}:I\rightarrow R^{\prime}\subseteq R
,
a\mapsto ac
is injective. Therefore,
c^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{R}
, and so

{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}\cap R^{\prime}
.

2. Recall that the ring
R^{\prime}
contains an essential left ideal
I
of the ring
R
. Hence, for each element
c\in{\cal C}_{R}
, there is an element
c^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{R}
such that
c^{\prime}c\in I\subseteq R^{\prime}
. Clearly,
c^{\prime}c\in{\cal C}_{R}\cap R^{\prime}={\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
, by statement 1.

3. (i)
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R^{\prime},0))
: We have to show that

{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\in{\rm Ore}_{l}(R^{\prime})
, i.e. for any pair of elements

c^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
and
r^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}
, there are elements

c^{\prime}_{1}\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
and
r_{1}^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}
such that
c_{1}^{\prime}r^{\prime}=r^{\prime}_{1}c^{\prime}
. Since
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
(statement 1) and
{\cal C}_{R}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R,0)
, there are elements
c\in{\cal C}_{R}
and
r\in R
such that

cr^{\prime}=rc^{\prime}
.

Since the left ideal
I
of
R
is essential and the ring
R
is a semiprime left Goldie ring,
I\cap{\cal C}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. Therefore,
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}I=Q_{l}(R)
. Hence,
c_{1}r\in I
for some element
c_{1}\in{\cal C}_{R}
. By statement 2, for the element
c_{1}c\in{\cal C}_{R}
, there is an element
\xi\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
such that
\xi c_{1}c\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
. Clearly,

\xi c_{1}cr^{\prime}=\xi c_{1}rc^{\prime}.

So, it suffices to take
c_{1}^{\prime}=\xi c_{1}c\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
and
r_{1}^{\prime}=\xi\cdot c_{1}r\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}I\subseteq I\subseteq R^{\prime}
.

(ii)
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R,0)
. Since
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
(statement 1), it suffices to show that
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\in{\rm Ore}_{l}(R)
, i.e. for any pair of elements

c^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
and
r\in R
, there are elements

c^{\prime}_{1}\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
and
r_{1}\in R
such that
c_{1}^{\prime}r=r_{1}c^{\prime}
.
Since
{\cal C}_{R}\in{\rm Ore}_{l}(R)
,
cr=r^{\prime}c^{\prime}
for some elements
c\in{\cal C}_{R}
and
r^{\prime}\in R
. By statement 2,
\eta c\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
for some element
\eta\in{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}
. Now,

\eta c\cdot r=\eta r^{\prime}\cdot c^{\prime}.

So, it suffice to take
c_{1}^{\prime}=\eta c
and
r_{1}=\eta r^{\prime}
.

4. Recall that
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
(statement 1),
{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R,0)
and
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
. Therefore,

Q_{l}(R^{\prime})={\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}^{-1}R^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R^{\prime}}^{-1}R\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}R=Q_{l}(R).

By statement 2, we have the reverse inclusion
Q_{l}(R^{\prime})\supseteq Q_{l}(R)
. Therefore,
Q_{l}(R^{\prime})=Q_{l}(R)
.

5. By statement 4, the ring
Q_{l}(R^{\prime})=Q_{l}(R)
is a semisimple Artinian ring. By Goldie’s Theorem, the ring
R^{\prime}
is a semiprime left Goldie ring.

6. Statement 6 follows from statement 4:
{\rm udim}(R^{\prime})={\rm udim}(Q_{l}(R^{\prime}))={\rm udim}(Q_{l}(R))={\rm udim}(R)
.
∎

Suppose that
R
is a semiprime left Goldie ring and an endomorphism
\sigma\in{\rm End}(R)
has large image. Let
\pi:R\rightarrow\overline{R}:=R/{\rm ker}(\sigma)
,
r\mapsto\overline{r}:=r+{\rm ker}(\sigma)
. By the Homomorphism Theorem, the map
\overline{\sigma}:\overline{R}\rightarrow\sigma(R)
,
\overline{r}\mapsto\overline{\sigma}(\overline{r})=\sigma(r)
, is an isomorphism. In particular,

\overline{\sigma}({\cal C}_{\overline{R}})={\cal C}_{\sigma(R)}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;Q_{l}(\overline{R})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\sigma}}}{{\simeq}}Q_{l}(\sigma(R))\simeq Q_{l}(R),

(2)

by
[
4
, Proposition 1.9.(3)]
or Theorem
2.4
.(4).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
R
be a semiprime left Goldie ring and
\sigma
be an endomorphism of the ring
R
with large image.
So, there are two cases either
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}=\emptyset
or otherwise
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that the first case implies that the endomorphism
\sigma
is a monomorphism.

Suppose that
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}=\emptyset
. We have to show that
{\rm ker}(\sigma)=0
. The ring
Q_{l}(R)
is a semisimple Artinian ring. In particular, the ring
Q_{l}(R)
a left Noetherian ring. Therefore, for each ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, the left ideal
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\mathfrak{a}
of
Q_{l}(R)
is an
ideal
of
Q_{l}(R)
.
By applying the the exact functor
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}(-)
to the short exact sequence of left
R
-modules

0\rightarrow{\rm ker}(\sigma)\rightarrow R\rightarrow\overline{R}=R/{\rm ker}(\sigma)\rightarrow 0,

we obtain a short exact sequence of left
R
-modules

0\rightarrow{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}{\rm ker}(\sigma)\rightarrow{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}R=Q_{l}(R)\rightarrow{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\overline{R}={\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\Big(R/{\rm ker}(\sigma)\Big)\simeq{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}R/{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}{\rm ker}(\sigma)\rightarrow 0.

Since the left
Q_{l}(R)
-module
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}{\rm ker}(\sigma)
is an ideal of the semisimple Artinian algebra
Q_{l}(R)
, the algebra
Q_{l}(R)
is isomorphic to the direct product of semisimple Artinian rings

Q_{l}(R)\simeq{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}{\rm ker}(\sigma)\times{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\overline{R}.

Clearly,
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\overline{R}\simeq\pi({\cal C}_{R})^{-1}\overline{R}
and
\pi({\cal C}_{R})\in{\rm Den}_{l}(\overline{R},0)
. Since the algebra
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\overline{R}
is a semisimple Artinian ring, we must have
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\overline{R}\simeq Q_{l}(\overline{R})
. Then

{\rm udim}({\cal C}_{R}^{-1}{\rm ker}(\sigma))={\rm udim}(Q_{l}(R))-{\rm udim}({\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\overline{R})={\rm udim}(Q_{l}(R))-{\rm udim}(Q_{l}(R))=0.

Hence,
{\rm udim}({\rm ker}(\sigma))={\rm udim}({\cal C}_{R}^{-1}{\rm ker}(\sigma))=0
, i.e.
{\rm ker}(\sigma)=0
.
\Box

Case: The ring
R
is a semiprime ring with Krull dimension.
Recall that any semiprime ring with Krull dimension is a semiprime left Goldie ring. Let
{\cal K}
be the (left) Krull dimension of a ring or module.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
R
be a semiprime ring with Krull dimension and an endomorphism
\sigma\in{\rm End}(R)
has large image. Then
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))={\cal K}(R)
.

Proof.

(i)
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))\leq{\cal K}(R)
: The ring
\sigma(R)
is an epimorphic image of the ring
R
. Hence,
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))\leq{\cal K}(R)
.

(ii)
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))\geq{\cal K}(R)
:
By the assumption, the endomorphism
\sigma\in{\rm End}(R)
has large image. So, the kernel of
\sigma
contains an essential left ideal, say
I
, of the ring
R
.
It follows from the exact sequence of left
R
-modules

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow R\rightarrow\overline{R}=R/I\rightarrow 0

that

{\cal K}(R)=\sup\{{\cal K}(I),{\cal K}(R/I)\}.

The left ideal
I
of the semiprime left Goldie ring
R
contains a regular element, say
c\in{\cal C}_{R}
. Then

{\cal K}(R/I)\leq{\cal K}(R/Rc)<{\cal K}(R)
. Therefore,
{\cal K}(R)={\cal K}(I)
. Now,

{\cal K}(R)={\cal K}({}_{R}I)\leq{\cal K}({}_{\sigma(R)}I)\leq{\cal K}(\sigma(R)),

and the statement (ii) follows.

By the statements (i) and (ii),
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))={\cal K}(R)
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
R
be a semiprime ring with Krull dimension and an endomorphism
\sigma\in{\rm End}(R)
has large image. If
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{}^{\prime}{\cal C}_{R}\neq\emptyset
then
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))\leq{\cal K}(R)-1<{\cal K}(R)
.

Proof.

Fix an element
c\in{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{}^{\prime}{\cal C}_{R}
. Then
Rc\subseteq{\rm ker}(\sigma)
, and so

{\cal K}(\sigma(R))={\cal K}(R/{\rm ker}(\sigma))\leq{\cal K}(R/Rc)\leq{\cal K}(R)-1<{\cal K}(R).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Let
R
be a semiprime ring with Krull dimension and
\sigma
be an endomorphism of the ring
R
with large image. In particular, the ring
R
is a semiprime left Goldie ring.
By Theorem
1.1
, the endomorphism
\sigma
is either a monomorphism or otherwise
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. So, in order to prove Theorem
1.2
it suffices to show that

{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}=\emptyset.

Suppose that
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. Then, by Lemma
2.6
,
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))<{\cal K}(R)
. By Proposition
2.5
,
{\cal K}(\sigma(R))={\cal K}(R)
, a contradiction. Therefore,
{\rm ker}(\sigma)\cap{\cal C}_{R}\neq\emptyset
, as required.
\Box

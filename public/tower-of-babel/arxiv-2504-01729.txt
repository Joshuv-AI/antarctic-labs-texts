On the effect of the Coriolis force on the enstrophy cascade
Yuri Cacchiò, Amirali Hannani, Gigliola Staffilani

Abstract
We study the direct enstrophy cascade at small spatial scales in statistically stationary forced-dissipated 2D Navier-Stokes equations subject to the Coriolis force in the β \beta -plane approximation. We provide sufficient conditions inspired by Bedrossian et al 2020 ; Xie and Bühler 2018 to prove that at small scales, in the presence of the Coriolis force, the so-called third-order structure function’s asymptotics follows the third-order universal law of 2D turbulence without the Coriolis force. Our result indicates that at small scales, the enstrophy flux from larger to smaller scales is not affected by the Coriolis force, confirming experimental and numerical observations. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first mathematically rigorous study of the above equations.

1.1
Turbulence

Turbulence in stationary forced dissipated fluids in
3 dimensions
has a long history. In his seminal work, Kolmogorov
Kolmogorov 1941b
;
Kolmogorov 1941a
;
Kolmogorov 1941c
predicted the direct cascade of energy (cascade from small wave numbers to large wave numbers) under rather general assumptions. Quantitatively, the above cascade can be characterized using at least one of the following well-known quantities: ensemble average of the energy spectrum
k

E(k)\sim|k|\mathbb{E}(|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}),

(1.10)

where
\hat{u}(k)
is the Fourier transform of the velocity field, or ensemble average of the
n
point structure function defined as,

S_{n}(r):=\mathbb{E}(|\delta_{r}(u)|^{n}):=\mathbb{E}(|u(x+r)-u(x)|^{n}),

(1.11)

where
u
is the velocity field,
r
is the separating vector and the dependence on the spatial point
x
is suppressed by assuming homogeneity. Assuming the flow to be isotropic, the celebrated Kolmogorov’s
\frac{4}{5}
th law predicts the so called
third order structure function
to behave as

S_{3}^{\|}(r):=\mathbb{E}\left(\delta_{r}(u)\cdot\frac{r}{|r|}\right)^{3}\sim-\frac{4}{5}\varepsilon|r|,\ \ \ l_{\nu}(\nu)\ll|r|\ll l_{I},

(1.12)

where
l_{\nu}(\nu)
is the scale where dissipation happens,
l_{I}
is the scale where we inject energy,
\varepsilon
denotes the injected energy,
S_{3}^{\|}
is the third order
longitudinal
structure function and
\delta_{r}(u):=u(x+r)-u(x)
.

Kolmogorov’s argument relies on the fact that we have only energy conservation. In
2 dimensions
phenomenology of statistically stationary, forced-dissipated flow changes drastically as we have two conservation laws: energy and enstrophy. This leads to the more complicated
dual cascade
picture, first argued by Fjrtoft in
Fjrtoft 1953
, and later by Batchelor and Kraichnan
Batchelor 1969
;
Kraichnan 1967
. In this regime, we have inverse energy cascade: from small spatial scales (large wave numbers) to large spatial scales (small wave numbers), and direct enstrophy cascade from large spatial scales to small spatial scales. More explicitly, denote the dissipation, energy injection, and
friction scale by
l_{\nu}
,
l_{I}
, and
l_{\alpha}
respectively (
l_{\nu}(\nu)\ll l_{I}\ll l_{\alpha}(\alpha)
). The above dual cascade picture, predicts that energy is transferred from the injection scale to the friction scale and damped by friction (
inverse cascade
). On the other hand, enstrophy cascades from the injection scale to the dissipation scale and dissipates there by viscosity (
direct cascade
).

In two dimensions, the analogous of the
\frac{4}{5}
th law (
1.12
) has been realized only in 99’ by Bernard, Lindborg, and Yakhot
Bernard 1999
;
Lindborg 1999
;
Yakhot 1999
. The two-dimensional analogue is given in terms of the third-order structure functions assuming isotropy as follows (recall

l_{\nu}\ll l_{I}\ll l_{\alpha}
)

\displaystyle S_{3}^{\|}(r)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\delta_{r}u\cdot\frac{r}{|r|}\right)^{3}\sim\frac{1}{8}\eta|r|^{3},\ \ \ l_{\nu}\ll|r|\ll l_{I},

(1.13a)

\displaystyle S_{3}^{\|}(r)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\delta_{r}u\cdot\frac{r}{|r|}\right)^{3}\sim\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon|r|,\ \ \ l_{I}\ll|r|\ll l_{\alpha},

(1.13b)

where as before
\varepsilon
is the injected energy, and
\eta
is the injected enstrophy.

We can also write (
1.13a
) in terms of the mixed velocity-vorticity structure function as predicted by Eyink
Eyink 1996
,

S_{3}^{\|}(r)=\mathbb{E}\left(|\delta_{r}\omega|^{2}\delta_{r}u\cdot\frac{r}{|r|}\right)\sim-2\eta|r|,\ \ \ l_{\nu}\ll|r|\ll l_{I},

(1.14)

originally derived in
Yaglom 1949
for passive scalar
turbulence.

In
2D
, the energy spectrum is also predicted over the above-mentioned scales
Batchelor 1969
;
Kraichnan 1967

\displaystyle|k|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}\sim\varepsilon^{2/3}|k|^{-5/3},\ \ \ l^{-1}_{\alpha}\ll|k|\ll l^{-1}_{I},

(1.15a)

\displaystyle|k|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}\sim\eta^{2/3}|k|^{-3},\ \ \ \ l^{-1}_{I}\ll|k|\ll l^{-1}_{\nu}.

(1.15b)

As we stated, the above picture has been rather well understood in the physics community by theoretical, and numerical arguments, and has been confirmed by experiments
Bernard 1999
;
Boffetta 2007
;
Boffetta and Ecke 2012
;
Eyink 1996
;
Huang et al 2001
;
Kraichnan and Montgomery 1980
;
Lindborg 1999
;
Yakhot 1999
. However, there are few rigorous mathematical works in this direction

;
Bedrossian et al 2020
;
Papathanasiou 2021
;
Xie and Bühler 2018
. Most notably we should mention
Bedrossian et al 2019

Bedrossian et al 2020
where a very physically relevant and weak sufficient condition (cf. Section
1.4
) is provided for proving (
1.13a
)-(
1.13b
), (
1.14
) in 2D and (
1.12
) in 3D for the counterpart of equations (
1.1
)
without the Coriolis force
.

1.2
Result: Turbulence

Adding the Coriolis force changes the behavior of solutions of (
1.1
). Still we have two conservation laws: enstrophy and energy, which suggests that the dual cascade picture could persist. However, a priori it is not clear how the above laws (
1.12
), (
1.13a
)-(
1.13b
) and (
1.14
) are modified in this situation.
This problem was first derived by Rhines in
Rhines 1975
and argued later in
Chekhlov et al 1996
;
Constantinou et al 2014
;
Constantinou et al 2016
;
Cope 2020
;
Galperin et al 2008
;
Galperin et al 2006
;
Huang et al 2001
;
Salmon 1998
;
Vallis 2017
. In these works, theoretical arguments suggest that at large scales the behavior of the spectrum will change completely i.e. instead of (
1.15a
) we have

|k|\mathbb{E}|\hat{u}|\sim\beta^{2}|k|^{-5},

(1.16)

where
\beta
is the coefficient appearing in the beta plane approximation. On the other hand, numerical simulations and certain heuristics
Chekhlov et al 1996
;
Cope 2020
;
Galperin et al 2008
;
Huang et al 2001
;
Salmon 1998
;
Vallis 2017
suggest that at small scales we still have the same relations as (
1.13a
), (
1.14
) and (
1.15b
).
The main goal of this paper is to provide a (rather weak) sufficient condition (see discussion on the assumption (
2.10
) below) for proving the direct cascade of the enstrophy at small scales. In other words, we prove that at small scales, in the presence of the Coriolis force, (
1.13a
) and (
1.14
) are valid assuming (
2.10
), meaning that at small scales the Coriolis force is negligible for the behavior of the third order structure function.

1.2.1
Statement of the Result

More precisely, consider the 2D Navier-Stokes equations with additive white in time and colored in space noise with Coriolis force, in the
\beta
-plane approximation i.e. (
1.1
). In
Cacchiò et al 2024
we prove that the system (
1.1
) is well-posed and that these equations possess at least one stationary measure. By stationary we mean that the law of
u(\cdot)
coincides with the law of
u(\cdot+\tau)
for any
\tau\geq 0
. Denote the expectation w.r.t this stationary solution by
\mathbb{E}
. Then we assume an uniform bound of
\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}
in
\alpha
and
\nu
(cf. (
2.10
)), we refer to Section
1.4
for the motivation related to this assumption. Our main result stated in Theorem
2.1
, illustrates the following relations concerning the third order structure function at small scales (
l_{\nu}\ll l\ll l_{I}
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}\left|\delta_{ln}\omega\right|^{2}\delta_{ln}u\cdot ndxdn\sim-2\eta l,

(1.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\times I}|\delta_{ln}u|^{2}\delta_{ln}u\cdot n\ dxdn\sim\frac{1}{4}\eta l^{3},

(1.18)

where
\eta
is the injected enstrophy by the stochastic force (
1.7
),
\delta_{ln}u=u(x+nl)-u(x)
,
n
denotes the normal vector and
l
is a scalar,
\fint
is the normalized (w.r.t domain size) integration.

As expressed, the above asymptotics should be understood for small scales
l
with

l_{\nu}\ll l\ll l_{I}\ll l_{\alpha}
, in the inviscid limit (
\nu\to 0
). To make this more precise, first, we fix a scale
l\in[l_{\nu},l_{I}]
. In order to realize the limit
l\to 0
, we take the limit

\nu,\alpha\to 0
, notice that in this limit
l_{\nu}(\nu)\to 0
, and
l_{\alpha}(\alpha)\to\infty
. Only afterward, we take the limit
l_{I}\to 0
(cf. Theorem
2.1
).
On the other hand, in an ongoing project an inverse cascade of energy for statistically stationary solution of (
1.1
) at large scales is considered. Here we analyze the fact that the third order structure function behavior is only determined by the Coriolis force. More precisely, in an appropriate inertial range,
l_{\nu}\ll l_{I}\ll l\ll l_{\alpha}
, we expect

\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\times I}|\delta_{ln}u|^{2}\delta_{ln}u\cdot n\ dxdn\sim C\varepsilon\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{n}r^{m}\text{ with }n\geq 1\text{ and }m>1

(1.19)

as suggested by its spectral counterpart (
1.16
).

1.3
Sketch of the proof and main ideas

Let us briefly sketch the proof’s ideas and highlight the new difficulties arising in our setup.

To prove the cascade, we follow the ideas sketched in
Bedrossian et al 2019
;
Bedrossian et al 2020
;
Xie and Bühler 2018
. We follow the evolution of the two-point correlation function, and using stationarity we obtain the so called KHM (Karman-Howarth-Monin) relations
De Karman and Howarth 1938
;
Monin and Yaglom 2007
. Let us emphasize that because of the Coriolis force, we have a new term in our modified KHM (cf. Section
3
). The process of dealing with each terms, after taking the limits, in the KHM relation is morally similar to
Bedrossian et al 2020
at small scales.
Moreover, for treating these terms certain regularity properties are needed. These properties are straightforward in the case of
Bedrossian et al 2020
owing to the existing results in the literature, whereas in our case we cannot appeal to these results due to the presence of the new
\beta
term. Therefore we proved similar (in fact weaker but still sufficiently strong) regularity of these terms in
Cacchiò et al 2024
.
More importantly, in our case we need to deal with a new term corresponding to the Coriolis force in the KHM. This constitutes the main part of our analysis. By using the regularity results concerning the invariant measure’s support, we are able to Taylor expand these terms and prove that low-order contributions are miraculously zero, and higher orders are dominated by the effect of
\eta
at small scales.

1.4
Remarks

In this section we make some remarks concerning our result. First, let us explain the physical relevance of the uniform bound on
\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}
(cf. (
2.10
)).

By using the Itô formula (cf.
(
Kuksin and Shirikyan 2012
, Theorem 7.7.5)
) we obtain in
Cacchiò et al 2024
the following energy and enstrophy balance for stationary solution:
stationary energy balance

\alpha\mathbb{E}\norm{u}^{2}_{L^{2}}+\nu\mathbb{E}\norm{\nabla u}^{2}_{L^{2}}=\varepsilon,

(1.20)

stationary enstrophy balance

\alpha\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\mathbb{E}\norm{\nabla\omega}_{L^{2}}^{2}=\eta,

(1.21)

where
u
is a statistically stationary solution and
\mathbb{E}
is the stationary measure associated with
u
.
Taking advantage of the above balance relations we may derive from (
2.10
) the following limits:

\displaystyle\lim_{\nu\to 0}\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\left|\varepsilon-\alpha\mathbb{E}\norm{u}^{2}_{L^{2}}\right|=0,

(1.22)

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\sup_{\nu\in(0,1)}\left|\nu\mathbb{E}\norm{\nabla\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}-\eta\right|=0.

(1.23)

Physically, the above expressions mean that in the limit
\alpha,\nu\to 0
, all the injected energy by the stochastic force will be dissipated thanks to the drag
\alpha
at large scales. Moreover, all the enstrophy injected by this force will be dissipated at small scales by viscosity. Notice that this is aligned with the ”natural” anomalous dissipation assumption appearing in theory of turbulence
Frisch 1995
. This assumption states that Navier Stokes equations can balance the external energy independent of the Reynolds number thanks to the effect of the non-linearity.

As we mentioned, our work is inspired by
Bedrossian et al 2019
;
Bedrossian et al 2020
and also
Xie and Bühler 2018
. Our main contribution is to consider the effect of the planetary rotation (Coriolis force). We already explained the physical application of these equations and the difference with the case studied above. Mathematically, dealing with the new term poses several challenges as mentioned in Section
1.3
.

Let us mention that the averaging over the circle in order to deal with anisotropy is also discussed in
Arad et al 1999
;
Bedrossian et al 2019
;
Kurien and Sreenivasan 2001
;
Nie and Tanveer 1999
. However, there is a crucial difference in our case: our equations are intrinsically anisotropic because of the Coriolis force, and this average is essential to obtain a quantity only depending on the scale
l
.

From a physical point of view, it is not obvious that all the contribution of the Coriolis force on the dual cascade vanishes. In fact, in the
\beta
-plane approximation
f_{0}
represents the horizontal component of the rotation while
\beta
represents the variation of the rotation in relation to the curvature of the planets
Vallis 2017
. One might predict that at small scales the effect of curvature
\beta
is zero but we can not make a priori estimates regarding the other component
f_{0}
.
In contrast, it can be expected that at large scales the
\beta
contribution becomes relevant but still, we can not make a priori estimates on
f_{0}
.

However, for 2d incompressible flow,
f_{0}
has no impact on the dynamical evolution at all, then in Section
3
the new term derived in the KHM due to the Coriolis force, which we call
\varTheta
, depends only on
\beta
. Moreover, at small scales we show that the contribution of
\varTheta
on the direct enstrophy cascade is zero, confirming the above-mentioned heuristics as well as numerical observations
Chekhlov et al 1996
;
Constantinou et al 2014
;
Constantinou et al 2016
;
Cope 2020
;
Galperin et al 2008
;
Galperin et al 2006
;
Huang et al 2001
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we state the assumptions, certain terminology and the main results. We then derive in Section
3
the modified velocity/vorticity KHM relations related to the Coriolis force. In Section
4
we prove the main theorem which shows the direct cascade of enstrophy.

\frac{\overline{D}(l)}{l^{3}}=-\frac{4\nu}{l^{3}}\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(l)+\frac{4\alpha}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{\Gamma}(r)dr+\frac{4}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{\varTheta}(r)dr-\frac{4}{l^{4}}\int^{l}_{0}r\overline{a}(r)dr.

(4.19)

Step 1.
We first show that

\frac{4}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{a}(r)dr=\frac{2\varepsilon}{l^{2}}-\frac{\eta}{4}+o_{l\to 0}(1),

(4.20)

where
\overline{a}
is defined by

\overline{a}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\times I}e_{j}(x)\cdot e_{j}(x+rn)dx.

(4.21)

We Taylor expand the factor
e_{j}(x+rn)
,

e_{j}(x+rn)=e_{j}(x)+rn\cdot\nabla e_{j}(x)+\frac{1}{2}rn\cdot He_{j}(x)rn+o(r^{2})

(4.22)

where
He_{j}
is the Hessian matrix of
e_{j}
and
o
is the classic little
o
-notation, i.e., for two general functions
f(x),g(x)
, if
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0
, then
f(x)=o(g(x))
.

We observe that

\int_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}e_{j}\cdot\partial_{x^{i}}e_{j}dx=0.

(4.23)

Then,

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}e_{j}(x)\cdot e_{j}(x+rn

\displaystyle)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}e_{j}(x)\left(e_{j}(x)+rn\cdot\nabla e_{j}(x)+\frac{1}{2}rn\cdot He_{j}(x)rn+o(r^{2})\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,m=1}^{2}n^{i}n^{m}e_{j}(x)\cdot\partial_{x^{m}}\partial_{x^{i}}e_{j}(x)\ dx+o(r^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,m=1}^{2}n^{i}n^{m}\partial_{x^{m}}e_{j}(x)\cdot\partial_{x^{i}}e_{j}(x)\ dx+o(r^{3}).

Since,

\fint_{\mathbb{S}}n^{i}n^{m}\ dn=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{im},

(4.24)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}e_{j}(x)\cdot e_{j}(x+rn)dxdn

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,m}n^{i}n^{m}\partial_{x^{m}}e_{j}(x)\cdot\partial_{x^{i}}e_{j}(x)\ dxdn+o(r^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}-\frac{r^{2}}{4}|\nabla e_{j}(x)|^{2}\ dx+o(r^{3})

where

|\nabla e_{j}|^{2}=(\partial_{x^{1}}e_{j}^{1})^{2}+(\partial_{x^{2}}e_{j}^{2})^{2}+(\partial_{x^{1}}e_{j}^{2})^{2}+(\partial_{x^{2}}e_{j}^{1})^{2}.

By incompressibility we can rewrite the above equality as

|\nabla e_{j}|^{2}=(\partial_{x^{1}}e_{j}^{2})^{2}+(\partial_{x^{2}}e_{j}^{1})^{2}-2\partial_{x^{1}}e_{j}^{1}\partial_{x^{2}}e_{j}^{2}.

Using integration by parts we get,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}e_{j}(x)\cdot e_{j}(x+rn)dxdn

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\left(\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{r^{2}}{4}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|\nabla e_{j}(x)|^{2}\ dx\right)+o(r^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\left(\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{r^{2}}{4}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}(\partial_{x^{1}}e_{j}^{2})^{2}+(\partial_{x^{2}}e_{j}^{1})^{2}-2\partial_{x^{2}}e_{j}^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}e_{j}^{2}\ dx\right)+o(r^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\left(\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{r^{2}}{4}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|\nabla\crossproduct e_{j}(x)|^{2}\ dx\right)+o(r^{3})

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\overline{a}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{r^{2}}{8}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}|\nabla\crossproduct{e_{j}}|^{2}dx+o(r^{3})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon-\frac{r^{2}}{4}\eta+o(r^{3})

Multiplying by
r
and integrating we obtain (
4.20
).

Step 2.
We want to show for
l_{\nu}
satisfying
(
2.13
)

\lim_{l_{I}\to 0}\limsup_{\nu,\alpha\to 0}\sup_{l\in(l_{\nu},l_{I})}\left|\frac{4\alpha}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{\Gamma}(r)dr-\frac{2\varepsilon}{l^{2}}\right|=0.

(4.26)

We remark that in the above formula, the order in which the
\nu
and
\alpha
are taken to
0
does not matter.
We can write

\frac{4\alpha}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{\Gamma}(r)dr=\frac{4\alpha}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\left(\overline{\Gamma}(r)-\overline{\Gamma}(0)\right)dr+\frac{2\alpha}{l^{2}}\overline{\Gamma}(0)

Using the energy balance (
1.20
) we have

\alpha\overline{\Gamma}(0)=\varepsilon-\nu\mathbb{E}\norm{\nabla u}^{2}.

Noting that

\norm{\nabla u}^{2}_{L^{2}}=\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}},

(4.27)

and recalling the conditions (
2.10
) and (
2.13
), we get

\alpha\overline{\Gamma}(0)=\varepsilon-\nu\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}=\varepsilon+o(l_{\nu}^{2}).

Moreover, using a chain rule,

\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(l)=\sum_{i,j}\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}n^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}u^{j}(x+ln)u^{j}(x)\ dxdn

(4.28)

By (
4.23
) we observe that
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(0)=0
. Furthermore, taking a second derivative of
\overline{\Gamma}
and integrating by parts, we have

\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}(l)=\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}\sum_{i,m}n^{i}n^{m}\partial_{x^{i}}u^{j}(x+ln)\partial_{x^{m}}u^{j}(x)\ dxdn.

By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

|\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}(l)|\leq C\mathbb{E}\norm{\nabla u}^{2}_{L^{2}}=C\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Using Taylor expansion for
\overline{\Gamma}(l)
,

\overline{\Gamma}(l)\approx\overline{\Gamma}(0)+\underbrace{\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(0)}_{=0}\cdot l+\frac{1}{2}\sup\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}(l)\cdot l^{2}.

Then,

\left|\overline{\Gamma}(l)-\overline{\Gamma}(0)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{2}\sup\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}(l)\cdot l^{2}\leq Cl^{2}\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}},

(4.29)

and in particular

\frac{4\alpha}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\left|\overline{\Gamma}(r)-\overline{\Gamma}(0)\right|dr\leq C\alpha\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}\to 0,

(4.30)

when
\alpha\to 0
due to (
2.10
).

Step 3.
Analogous to
Bedrossian et al 2019
we have

\limsup_{\nu,\alpha\to 0}\sup_{l\in(l_{\nu},l_{I})}\frac{4\nu}{l^{3}}\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(l)=0.

(4.31)

To show the above limit, first observe that:

\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(l)-\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(0)=\int_{0}^{l}\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}(l^{\prime})dl^{\prime}.

(4.32)

However, in the previous step we already observed that
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(0)=0
, and

\sup_{l^{\prime}\in[0,l]}|\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}(l^{\prime})|\leq C\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}
. Therefore, we get:

|\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(l)|\leq lC\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}

(4.33)

This means

\frac{4\nu}{l^{3}}\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}(l)\lesssim\frac{\nu\mathbb{E}\norm{\omega}^{2}_{L^{2}}}{l^{2}}.

This gives us (
4.31
) thanks to (
2.10
) and (
2.13
).

Step 4.
We show that

\lim_{l_{I}\to 0}\limsup_{\nu,\alpha\to 0}\sup_{l\in[l_{\nu},l_{I}]}\left|\frac{4}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{\varTheta}(r)dr\right|=0.

(4.34)

By definition

\overline{\varTheta}(l):=\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}\beta ln^{2}\left(u^{2}(x)u^{1}(x+ln)-u^{1}(x)u^{2}(x+ln)\right)dxdn.

(4.35)

Let us consider

\mu(l):=\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}n^{2}\left(u^{2}(x)u^{1}(x+ln)-u^{1}(x)u^{2}(x+ln)\right)dxdn.

(4.36)

By definition,

\mu(0)=0.

(4.37)

Using a chain rule, we find

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(l)=\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}

\displaystyle n^{2}u^{2}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x+ln)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{1}(x+ln)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle n^{2}u^{1}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{2}(x+ln)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x+ln)\right)dxdn.

Then,

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(0)=\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}

\displaystyle n^{2}u^{2}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle n^{2}u^{1}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{2}(x)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x)\right)dxdn.

Using the incompressibility property, i.e.
\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x)+\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x)=0
, and integration by parts we have

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(0)=\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-n^{1}n^{2}u^{2}(x)\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x)}_{=0}+(n^{2})^{2}u^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{1}(x)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle n^{1}n^{2}u^{1}(x)\partial_{x^{1}}u^{2}(x)+\underbrace{(n^{2})^{2}u^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x)}_{=0}dxdn.

For the other two terms, after integration by parts, we conclude in the same way. In the end, we proved that

\mu^{\prime}(0)=0.

(4.38)

Now, we want to compute the second derivative of
\mu^{\prime\prime}
;

\displaystyle|\mu^{\prime\prime}(l)|=\Bigg|\mathbb{E}\fint_{\mathbb{S}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}\crossproduct I}

\displaystyle n^{1}n^{2}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{2}(x+ln)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x+ln)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(n^{2})^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{1}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{2}(x+ln)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x+ln)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle n^{1}n^{2}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{2}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x+ln)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{1}(x+ln)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(n^{2})^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{2}(x)\left(n^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}u^{1}(x+ln)+n^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}u^{1}(x+ln)\right)dxdn\Bigg|.

Using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
2.10
), we deduce,

|\mu^{\prime\prime}(l)|\leq C_{1}\mathbb{E}\norm{\nabla u}^{2}_{L^{2}}<\infty.

Since,

|\mu(l)|\leq|\mu(0)|+|\mu^{\prime}(0)l|+\sup|\mu^{\prime\prime}(l)|l^{2}\leq Cl^{2},

(4.39)

and thus

|\overline{\varTheta}(l)|\leq C|\beta l^{3}|,

(4.40)

we have,

\left|\frac{4}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}r\overline{\varTheta}(r)dr\right|\leq\frac{4}{l^{4}}\int_{0}^{l}C|\beta r^{4}|dr\leq C_{0}\frac{1}{l^{4}}l^{5}

(4.41)

that goes to 0 when
l\to 0
.

Joint attention GAN for medical report generation with clinical style preservation
Authors: Zuge Tang, Xiaoou Liu, Jun Bie, Liming Xu, Shijie Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Medical report generation, which aims to reduce workload of doctors and improve diagnosis efficiency, can be always concerned and significant. Currently, many medical report generation methods are proposed to automatically generate diagnosis report. However, it is challenging to generate semantically coherent paragraphs with accurate location and clinical language style. To address these, we, in this paper, propose novel medical report generation method based on generative adversarial networks with joint attention. Within it, an adversarial framework is designed to achieve high-quality medical report generation where generator generates diagnosis report meanwhile discriminator is used to distinguish authenticity and evaluator is utilized to preserve clinical style, respectively. During generation, joint attention is proposed to enhance and fuse visual and textual feature to capture high-order interactions among cross-modal features, which is desired for accurate location and filling visual-to-pathological gap. Then, language evaluator is introduced to preserve clinical language style and yield acceptable and readable report by clinicians. Besides, we propose gradient search-based Pareto optimal (GSPO) strategy for multi-objective GANs, which uses hard parameter sharing to determine multi-objective weight parameters and performs linear search along gradient direction to search Pareto optimal solution. Extensive experiments show that our method gains competitive performance comparing with other state-of-the-art medical report generation methods. Specifically, ours achieves average increments of 6.2% BLEU-2, 8.1% BLEU-3, 7.4% ROUGE and 13.1% MIRQI. Additionally, ours outperforms than other comparisons in clinical language style evaluation by radiologists.

Introduction

Medical report generation also called medical image captioning, which aims to automatically generate diagnosis descriptions with clinical sentences with given medical images1, is expected to reduce workload and improve diagnosis efficiency since accurate reasoning requires years of professional accumulation and training clinically2. Report generation can be simulated as seeing, focusing, and telling, which can be respectively behavioralized into feature representation, feature encoding, and report generation3,4. To this end, many methods for generating medical reports are proposed. Within it, deep generation-based generation achieves better results and is mainly focused in this paper.

Despite the great progress made by existing deep generation methods, it is still inadequate in (1) insufficient consideration of the visual-pathological gap and (2) weak evaluation of clinical language style. Most of the existing attention mechanisms mainly use simple region or gird features to transform visual units into linguistic features during language generation, which cannot accurately describe pathological location information and lacks the ability of complex cross-modal reasoning. As shown in Fig. 1, the results yielded by comparisons5,6 contain no location information. Then, almost all report generation methods lack clinical language evaluation, i.e., evaluations are performed by natural captioning indexes. It is well-known that diagnose report requires professional knowledge and has negation language style7,8. Related work

Deep medical report generation methods mainly contain feature representation, feature encoding and language generation modules. Within it, encoder-decoder, objects detections9,10 and attention mechanism are always involved. Recent overview articles on this issue include3,4, which report that the existing language generation models generally include Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), Long Short Term Memory (LSTM), Transformer and Generative Adversarial Nets (GAN). The related issues are summarized as follows.

RNN- and LSTM-based medical report generation

By incorporating memory property, RNN can hand the sequential data which is associated with the before and after inputs. To this end, some have adopted RNN into medical report generation. CORAL811 considers the Salient Alpha and time distributed variance regularization and builds multi-modal RNN to generate diagnose report. Then, combining with domain awareness and reinforcement learning, PromptMRG12 achieves automatic chest X-ray radiology report generation with considering both readability and clinical accuracy. Based on hierarchical RNN, CRHAN13 considers potential co-occurrence between common pathologies and pays insufficient attention to rare pathologies to achieve CT report generation.

LSTM whose core is to use the memory cell to encode the knowledge at each step of what inputs have been observed up to current step utilizes the input, forget and output gates to save and update the contextual information, which outperforms RNN in long-distance dependent. Combining the basic CNN model and LSTM, Rec-Att14 incorporates image and generated report in circular manner to enable next word generation with preservation of consistency among sentences. Then, Deepopht15 embeds the retinal disease identifier, clinical description generator and visual interpretation module into captioning system to generate ophthalmic disease diagnosis report. Then, considering that the current diagnose report mainly shows disease keywords and associated attributes, then DDL-GCN16 employs GCN17 to learn the specialized features and fit the finding in each disease.

The attention mechanism enable generation model to focus on main units or region and ignore secondary parts. On this line, Then, LHR-RFL18 is proposed to solve the inconsistent quality across difficult and easy samples by designing Linear Hybrid-Reward (LHR) and Reinforced Focal Learning (RFL) module. Rel-Net19 designs mixed-knowledge collaborative reasoning module, and fully considers medical terms to learn visual attention features to yield topic-oriented symptom and descriptions. Besides, TriNet6 encodes both deep visual and semantic embeddings, and fuses medical report embedding and medical subject headings for report generation. CAMANet20 uses multi-label classification module to predict local attributes and employs adaptive attention to control attention level when generating next word.

Transformer-based medical report generation

Transformer requires no notion of order for its inputs and incorporates relative positions and sizes of detected objects, which enables it to be suitable for bottom-up visual encoder. Thus, some have also incorporates it into diagnose report generation. Combining the skip connections and convolution block attention modules, METransformer21 exacts the visual features and employs memory driven Transformer22 to output the medical reports, and TSGET23 utilizes the two-stage global enhancement layers to facilitate Transformer to generate more reliable reports. To alleviate the serious data bias and over long sequence, AlignTrans24 is proposed to use align hierarchical attention to predict label and learn the multi-grained visual features of visual regions and disease tags, and utilize multi-grained Transformer to yield generation results. Similarly, WCL25 incorporates the weakly supervised contrastive loss to address that normal finds dominate the datasets to further generate informative sentences. Besides, CGFTrans26 uses visual attention to focus on different locations and employs the multi-level labels to guide generation. To relax dependency on paired data, KGAE27 which embeds pre-constructed knowledge graph, knowledge-driven encoder and knowledge-driven decoder projects medical images and reports into latent space to bridge visual and textual domains to generate reports in an unsupervised way.

In order to alleviate textual data bias, PPKED28 considers the prior medical knowledge and clinical experience and integrates PoKE, PrKE and MKD modules to generate clinical diagnose report. Then, X-REM29 learns fine-grained description of findings, and uses occurrences pattern to retrieve and normalize similar reports from report database to automatically generate chest X-ray radiology report. DMVF30 combines region- and grid-level features based to identify lesions and key areas, and align and enhance two type features flow to perform report generation.

Some have shown that cross-modal fusion improves generation accuracy. Then, XPRONET31 which contains three well-designed, fully differentiable and complementary modules makes deep fusion of visual and textual features and designs multi-label contrastive loss to promote generation. Analogously, SGF5 obtains domain knowledge from medical report to extract fined-grain visual features to comprehensively guide generation. PGT32 utilizes CNN and Transformer as encoder and decoder, respectively, and fuses visual features, language embeddings, and prior embeddings to generate accurate radiology reports. Considering intrinsic relationships between local pathological changes, RAMT33 adopts standard mean teacher paradigm to achieve the semi-supervised report generation with the fusion of graph embedding, semantic embedding and visual features.

GAN-based medical report generation

Previous works have shown that GAN where generator is trained to capture potential data manifold to generate report and discriminator is trained to distinguish authenticity has made much progress in medical imaging and process. Based on the neural symbolic learning34 and adversarial graph networks35, NSL36 embeds prior domain knowledge into GAN to perform semantic segmentation and symbolic logical reasoning to generate diagnosis report of spinal MRI image.

Although many methods have been proposed, there are still three challenges. Firstly, there is insufficient consideration about visual-to-pathological gap, which causes a lack of location information and limits capacity of complex multi-modal reasoning in diagnosis report generation. Then, almost all the existing medical report generation methods are evaluated by using natural language evaluator and the clinical language style evaluation is weak. Besides, there is no consideration about multi-objective optimization in deep report generation.

To overcome the above problems, we make the following efforts. We firstly use the designed joint attention to enhance and fuse visual and textual features to capture high-order feature interaction and transform visual units to pathological words. Then, we incorporate language style evaluator into adversarial framework to preserve clinical language style consistence between generated and clinical report. Moreover, we develop GSPO strategy which utilizes hard parameter sharing to determine multi-objective weight parameters and performs linear search along gradient direction to search Pareto optimal solution in multi-objective optimization. These three improvement constitute the proposed JAGAN.

The proposed method

In this section, we present formulation and framework of JAGAN. As shown in Fig. 2, clinical report R and medical image I will be firstly encoded into vector T and M, respectively. Subsequently, W and M will be input into visual feature enhancement (VFE), textual feature enhancement (TFE) and enhanced features fusion (EFF) module to achieve high-order interactions among visual and textual features to guide report generation in adversarial framework. Joint attention

Attention mechanism computes spatial attention weights so that it selectively focuses on salient target regions and guide subsequent sentence generation. However, almost all existing attention methods focus on features interactions within identical modality, e.g., spatial-channel, semantic-spatial and global-local, and ignore interaction among visual and textual units. Thus, we propose joint attention with enhanced features fusion (EFF) module to enhance and fuse visual and textual features to obtain high-order interactions among cross-modal features.

To capture consistent information between medical image and diagnose report, we design EFF module to enhance feature weight of consistent information meanwhile weaken feature weight of inconsistent information. Take medical image and diagnose report as input, we design joint attention associated with VFE, TFE and EFF module to enhance and fuse cross-modal features. Experimental results verify that joint attention yields high-order interactions of feature vectors and generate finer-grained feature representations to improve report generation.

Language style evaluator

The language style evaluator is designed to preserve clinical language style which describes diseases using negation fashion, e.g., no clear, free of, etc. Although some achieve high natural language scores, partial meanings and descriptions are inaccurate as shown in Figs. 3 and 4. We incorporate MIRQI40 which has been used for clinical language evaluation into evaluator to preserve clinical style. Gradient search-based pareto optimal strategy

It is noted that Eq. (26) includes multiple objects, and simple stochastic gradient method can be time-consuming and sub-optimal. To this end, we provide an efficient solution for multi-objective optimization to search optimal solutions44. Essentially, the total loss of JAGAN mainly contains generative, discriminative and evaluated loss, and it can be proved be non-convex with following simple formation35\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} {\mathbf{{L}}_{Total}} = {\alpha _1}{L_1} + {\alpha _2}{L_2} + ...{\alpha _n}{L_n} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbf{{L}} = \{ {L_1},{L_2},...,{L_n}\}$$\end{document} denotes the different losses and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\boldsymbol{\alpha } = \{ {\alpha _1},{\alpha _2},...,{\alpha _n}\}$$\end{document} denotes weights among losses. Then, we divide parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta$$\end{document} into shared parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta _s$$\end{document} and task-specific parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta _k$$\end{document}. Then, it can be formalized as multi-objective optimization by optimizing a set of conflicting objectives, i.e.,36\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} {\mathop {\mathop {\min }\limits _{{\theta ^s}}}\limits _{{\theta ^1},...,{\theta ^k}}} \sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _{^k}}{L_{^k}}({\theta _{^s}},{\theta _{^k}})} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where K is the number of objects. So far, the goal is to obtain global optimal solution of Eq. (36) which is NP-hard. Alternately, searching Pareto optimality which is defined as follows can be feasible44.

Definition 1

Pareto Optimality. For each object in multi-object optimization \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k(k \in K)$$\end{document}, if there are two sets of parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta = ({\theta _s},{\theta _k})$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\theta ^*} = (\theta _s^*,\theta _k^*)$$\end{document}, such that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L_k}({\theta _s},{\theta _k}) \ge {L_k}(\theta _s^*,\theta _k^*)$$\end{document} with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbf{{L}}({\theta _s},{\theta _k}) \ne \mathbf{{L}}(\theta _s^*,\theta _k^*)$$\end{document}. Then, the parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\theta ^*} = (\theta _s^*,\theta _t^*)$$\end{document} can be called Pareto optimality.

Obviously, When \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K=1$$\end{document}, there is no conflict, and the Pareto optimality is the optimal solution. When \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K=2$$\end{document}, adding KKT condition which is defined as Definition 2 to shared- and task-specific parameter to linearly search along the direction to obtain Pareto optimal solution.

Definition 2

KKT condition. If existing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\alpha _k} \ge 0$$\end{document} such that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}} = 1$$\end{document} with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}} {\nabla _{{\theta _s}}}{L_k}({\theta _s},{\theta _k}) = 0$$\end{document}. Then, we have \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\nabla _{{\theta _k}}}{L_k}({\theta _s},{\theta _k}) = 0$$\end{document} for each object \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k(k \in K)$$\end{document}.

Some44 have provided close solution when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K=2$$\end{document}. In our scene, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K>2$$\end{document} which is more general case in multi-objective optimization, and it is easy to conclude that it is very time-consuming to determine weight parameters by common cross-validation method and the time consumption of verification process increases almost exponentially as the number of targets increases. Besides, when parameter dimension is high, and the linear search in44 is easy to fall into local optimum.

Considering these issues, we propose to decompose multi-objective optimization into multiple two-objective optimizations, and then uses linear search along \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}{\nabla _\theta }}$$\end{document} direction to obtain Pareto optimal solution after determining the optimal weight parameters. The core is to multi-objective optimization decomposition, and to search shared and specific parameters along \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}{\nabla _{{\theta _s}}}}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}{\nabla _{{\theta _k}}}}$$\end{document} direction, respectively.

Theorem 1

When the partial derivative of the deep feature matrix to the shared parameter matrix is a full-rank matrix, and the weight parameter is determined, the Pareto optimal solution of multi-objective GANs is one of the follows:

Case 1: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}} {\nabla _{{\theta _s}}}{L_k}({\theta _s},{\theta _t}) = 0$$\end{document} and the current parameters, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$({\theta _s},{\theta _t})$$\end{document}, are Pareto Optimality;

Case 2: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum \limits _{k = 1}^K {{\alpha _k}} {\nabla _{{\theta _s}}}{L_k}({\theta _s},{\theta _t}) = 0$$\end{document} is the gradient direction, and all the loss will descent along this direction.

Case 1: If the gradient of Eq. Experiment and discussions

To verify the effectiveness of JAGAN, we conduct extensive evaluations on three baseline datasets to compare ours with several state-of-the-art report generation methods. Then, we compare the proposed multi-objective optimization with common-used cross-validation. Besides, some ablation investigations are conducted to analyze each component.

Datasets and experimental setting

IU X-Ray45 contains over 7,400 chest X-ray images which associated with paired 3,955 radiology reports. Following24,27, the entries without the findings will be excluded, which results in 6,471 images and 3,336 reports. Then, the reports will be pre-processed by tokenizing and converting to yield lower-cases and removing non-alpha tokens. At last, the top 1,200 words, which include over 99.0% word, are included in the vocabulary.

MIMIC-CXR46 is the large-scale dataset for medical report generation, and it contains over 227,835 imaging studies which are collected from 65,379 patients. Each imaging study includes one or more images, which results in 377,110 image and 227,835 reports in the dataset. Then, we adopt the official data split, which yields 368,960 image and 222,758 reports in the training set, 2,991 images and 1,808 reports in the validation set, and 5,159 images and 3,269 reports in the test set.

LGK30 mainly contains over 6,500 ultrasound image and corresponding reports. It generally describes three parts, including liver, gallbladder and kidney. Considering that there are many report phrases in the dataset, some normalizations such as data padding and language translation will be conducted. We split the each report into phases, which generates diagnose report vocabulary containing 210 words.

For IU-X-ray and LGK dataset, training, validation and testing set is set to be 7:1:224,27,30. During the training, the pre-trained Faster R-CNN47 is employed to extract the image features which will be input into the subsequent modules and output the 1024-dimension feature vectors, and all words will be normalized into one-hot vector.

Implementation details and evaluations

All experiments were conducted on Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold6148CPU with 20 cores and eight Tesla V100-SXM2 GPUs using same setting to ensure impartiality and objectivity. We firstly pre-training generator and discriminator with cross entropy, and update through Adam optimizer to gain preliminarily high CIDEr scores. Within it, the dimension of linear attention and decoding module are set to be 512 and 1024, respectively. Then, warm-up steps and initial learning rate are fixed to be 10,000 and 0.0005, respectively. Then, SCST strategy43 is adopted to further fine-tune the adversarial training and improve performance. During the fine-tune, the learning rate is set to be 0.00001 and all the networks will be updated with Adam optimizer alternately until gradient converges or pre-set iteration is reached.

To evaluate the generation performance, we adopt several common-used evaluations following previous works. Then, we present and compare some qualitative results by highlighting key word. Subsequently, score distribution evaluation is provided to comprehensively analyze performance without individual bias. Apart from the mentioned indexes, we also invite three chief physicians to evaluate language style.

Discussion and limitation

The proposed JAGAN achieves effective medical report generation and improves substantially comparing with others owning to three important perspectives: (1) joint attention is designed to obtain high-order cross-modal feature interactions, (2) language style evaluation is introduced to preserve clinical language style and yield acceptable report and (3) gradient search-based Pareto optimal strategy is proposed for multi-objective optimization to obtain accurate and optimal parameters. To investigate the effectiveness of each component in our JAGAN, and analyze the influence of computational complexity of the GSPO strategy, we have also conducted some ablation experiments on IU X-ray dataset. The quantitative and qualitative results with other state-of-the-art medical report generation or diagnose report generation competitors validate the superiority of the proposed JAGAN. However, there are still important issues in our method need to be solved. Firstly, we use fixed hand-crafted parser to extract disease words, negation, uncertainty and attributes. We will construct an automatic deep parser to extract word attribute to improve accuracy of positive disease keyword mentions47. Then, there is no prior experience of physicians to guide generation in the framework of JAGAN, which makes the training separate far from professional guidance48. Thus, we also plan to introduce prior and experience of chief physician to guide to generate accurate radiology report.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel medical diagnosis report generation method based on generative adversarial networks with joint attention and gradient-based search Pareo optimization strategy to achieve accurate report generation with clinical style preservation. Within it, joint attention associated with visual feature enhancement, textual feature enhancement and enhanced features fusion module is designed to enhance and fuse cross-modal features to yield high-order cross-modal interactions between medical images and diagnostic reports. Then, generator, discriminator and evaluator are incorporated into end-to-end framework where generator yields semantically and linguistically detectably indistinguishable diagnosis report while discriminator is designed to distinguish the generated and clinical report and the language evaluator is adopted to preserve clinical style to further improve readability. Besides, we design gradient-based search Pareo optimization strategy to search Pareto optimality to ensure trained model be optimal. Experimental results show that our proposed method gains competitive performance comparing with other recent state-of-the-art methods.

Exponents for Shared Randomness-Assisted Channel Simulation
Aadil Oufkir, Michael X. Cao, Hao-Chung Cheng, Mario Berta

Abstract
We determine the exact error and strong converse exponents of shared randomness-assisted channel simulation in worst case total-variation distance. Namely, we find that these exponents can be written as simple optimizations over the Rényi channel mutual information. Strikingly, and in stark contrast to channel coding, there are no critical rates, allowing a tight characterization for arbitrary rates below and above the simulation capacity. We derive our results by asymptotically expanding the meta-converse for channel simulation [Cao et al. , IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory (2024)], which corresponds to non-signaling assisted codes. We prove this to be asymptotically tight by employing the approximation algorithms from [Berta et al. , Proc. IEEE ISIT (2024)], which show how to round any non-signaling assisted strategy to a strategy that only uses shared randomness. Notably, this implies that any additional quantum entanglement-assistance does not change the error or the strong converse exponents.

Introduction

Channel simulation is a fundamental task in information theory, where the goal is to replicate the behavior of a noisy communication channel using alternative resources such as noiseless communication and shared randomness.
This task is pivotal in understanding the limits of communication systems and is one of the two extreme cases in the broader problem of channel interconversion (see, e.g.,
[
32
]
), with the other being Shannon’s channel coding problem
[
30
]
.
While the reverse Shannon theorem
[
3
,
2
]
characterized the asymptotic rate of communication cost required to simulate a noisy channel, recent research has shifted toward analyses in the finite blocklength regime, focusing on various deviation regimes
[
16
,
28
]
.
In particular, asymptotic communication rates in the small- and moderate-deviation regimes have been investigated in
[
5
]
.

In this paper, we extend the study of randomness-assisted channel simulation to the large deviation regime, where the communication rate
r
deviates from the channel capacity by some constant.
When the rate
r
exceeds the channel capacity, the distortion in the total variation distance (TVD) between the simulated and the target channels diminishes exponentially fast as the number of channels grows.
Conversely, when
r
is below capacity, the distortion tends to
1
exponentially.
In both cases, the rate of convergence can be characterized asymptotically by the Rényi information of the channel.
Specifically, we derive the error exponent and the strong converse exponent as follows (see Theorems
13
,
18
,
21
and
23
):

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha\geqslant 0}\;\alpha\cdot\left(r-I_{\alpha+1}(W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\right)

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\;(1-\alpha)\cdot\left(-r+I_{\alpha}(W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\right),

(2)

where
\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})
and
\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})
denote the minimal achievable distortions in TVD for simulating
W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n}
using a noiseless channel with alphabet size at most
\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor
with shared-randomness assistance and non-signaling assistance, respectively; and
I_{\alpha}(W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})
is the Rényi channel mutual information of order
\alpha
,
i.e.
,

I_{\alpha}(W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\coloneqq\adjustlimits{\sup}_{p_{\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}{\inf}_{q_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})}D_{\alpha}\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times q_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)

(3)

for all
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.
Here,
D_{\alpha}
is the Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
, defined as

D_{\alpha}\left(p\middle\|q\right)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)^{\alpha}q(x)^{1-\alpha}\right)\qquad\forall\alpha\geqslant 0.

(4)

Our results for randomness-assisted channel simulation are derived indirectly by first analyzing the exponents for channel simulation assisted by a stronger resource of the non-signaling correlation, then connecting the non-signaling and randomness-assisted scenarios via rounding techniques
[
4
]
.
We commence the analysis by establishing an elegant one-shot characterization for the non-signaling simulation error (Proposition
1
).
Then, the lower bound to the non-signaling error exponent is derived via Markov’s inequality, while the upper bound relies on the de Finetti reduction and the method of types.
The lower bound to the non-signaling strong converse exponent is based on the Chernoff bound, while the upper bound relies on the method of types and certain continuity arguments.
We note that in contrast to channel coding, the proofs of the converse of the error exponent and the achievability of the strong converse exponent for channel simulation are significantly more involved than the other directions.

Interestingly, the error exponent and the strong converse exponent remain unchanged whether the shared resources between the sender and receiver consist of classical randomness or a potent non-signaling resource.
Consequently, this invariance applies to any shared resources that exist “between” SR and NS, namely any shared mechanisms capable of producing shared randomness and that can be generated by non-signaling resources.
A specific example of this is unlimited shared entanglement in quantum theory.

In Section

In Section

Section

The study of the error exponent of some of the aforementioned tasks is available in the literature.

Channel Simulation and the Exact Distortion in TVD

In this section, we formally define the tasks of channel simulation and derive a one-shot expression of the minimal distortion in TVD for non-signaling channel simulation with constrained message size.
Given a channel
W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X})
, where
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are some finite sets, the goal of channel simulation is to construct another channel
\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X})

approximating

W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}
using a noiseless channel (
i.e.
, an identity channel) with a
small
alphabet size.
Naturally, one prefers a higher quality of the approximation.
More specifically, in this paper, we measure the distortion of the simulation in TVD and aim to minimize the following quantity as much as possible:

\displaystyle\epsilon=\left\lVert W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}-\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TVD}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\;\left\lVert W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(\cdot|x)-\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(\cdot|x)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TVD}}

(6)

where the TVD between two pmf
p
and
q
on
\mathcal{X}
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TVD}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert p(x)-q(x)\right\rvert.

(7)

On the other hand, one would also like to use as little resources as possible for the above task.
Specifically, we would like to minimize or restrict the size of the alphabet
M
of the identity channel
\mathrm{id}_{M}(\cdot|\cdot)\coloneqq\delta_{\cdot|\cdot}\in\mathcal{P}([M]|[M])
used in the construction of
\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}
.
Depending on the additional resources (other than the aforementioned
\mathrm{id}_{M}
),
\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}
is constructed differently as follows (see Figure
1
).

•

In the non-assisted scenario, we have the simple construction as
\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}\circ\mathrm{id}_{M}\circ\mathcal{E}
where
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{P}([M]|\mathcal{X})
,
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|[M])
are some uncorrelated encoder and decoder, respectively.

•

In the shared randomness-assisted scenario, we allow the encoder and the decoder to be coordinated by some shared random variable,
i.e.
,

\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\coloneqq\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}p_{\mathsf{S}}(s)\cdot\sum_{i,j\in[M]}\mathcal{D}(y|j,s)\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M}(j|i)\cdot\mathcal{E}(i|x,s)

(8)

where
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{P}([M]|\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{S})
,
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|[M]\times\mathcal{S})
.
Despite the fact that the shared random variable
\mathsf{S}
is also a type of communication resource, we do not constrain
\mathsf{S}
(
i.e.
,
p_{\mathsf{S}}
nor
\mathcal{S}
) in this paper.
Hence, such
\mathsf{S}
is often referred to as unconstrained shared randomness.

•

In the non-signaling-assisted scenario, we further allow the encoder and the decoder to take a form as a joint encoding-decoding map
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{IY}|\mathsf{XJ}}\in\mathcal{P}([M]\times\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}\times[M])

\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}
as

\widetilde{W}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)=\sum_{i,j\in[M]}\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{IY}|\mathsf{XJ}}(i,y|x,j)\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M}(j|i),

(9)

where we only restrict

Lemma 2

3
Error Exponent for Non-Signaling Channel Simulation

In the following, we are interested in the behavior of
\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}
for simulating asymptotically many copies of a channel given a fixed communication
rate

r>0
.
More precisely, we would like to study the exponent
-\frac{1}{n}\log{\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})}
as
n
tends to infinity.
Note that we will omit the second argument for
\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}
,
i.e.
,
W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n}
when it is clear from the context.
The first result of this section, Proposition

\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}
exponentially decays as
r>I_{1}(W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})
for any finite blocklength
n
.
Then, we will prove in Proposition

that the achievable error exponent is asymptotically tight.

3.1

Proposition 3

(Achievability for the Error Exponents)

Let

be a channel.
For all

Moreover, as
n
tends to infinity we have that

Remark 4
.

The established achievable error exponent

I_{\alpha}(W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})

Moreover, since

is continuous in

Proposition
3
then indicates that the non-signaling simulation error

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log{\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})},

(124)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log{\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})}

\displaystyle\geqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log{\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})}.

(125)

Proof.

Eq. (
124
) is an immediate implication of the first inequality of Theorem
19
.
To show (
125
), we apply the second inequality in Theorem
19
, and get

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor}\right)^{\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor}\right)\cdot\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})+\left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor}\right)^{\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor}

(126)

\displaystyle\leqslant\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right),

(127)

where we have used the fact that, for
n\geqslant\frac{1}{r}
,

\displaystyle\log\left\{\left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor}\right)^{\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor}\right\}

\displaystyle=\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor\cdot\log{\left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor}\right)}\leqslant\lfloor e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}\rfloor\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor}\right)

(128)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{e^{n\cdot(r+\delta)}-1}{e^{nr}}=-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}.

(129)

We further claim that for any positive sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\{\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right)\right)}

(130)

for any
r,\delta>0
.
We defer the proof of (
130
) to Appendix
C
.
Finally, (
125
) is the result of combining (
127
) and (
130
).
∎

Lemma
20
enables the following theorem.

Theorem 21
.

Let
W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X})
be a channel.
For all
r>0
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})=\sup_{\alpha\geqslant 0}\;\alpha\cdot\left(r-\adjustlimits{\sup}_{p_{\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}{\inf}_{q_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})}D_{\alpha+1}\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times q_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)\right).

Proof.

By (

\displaystyle\hskip 14.0pt\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log{\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\delta\in(0,r)}\sup_{\alpha\geqslant 0}\;\alpha\cdot\left((r-\delta)-\adjustlimits{\sup}_{p_{\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}{\inf}_{q_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})}D_{\alpha+1}\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times q_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)\right)

5.2
Strong Converse Exponent for Shared Randomness-Assisted Channel Simulation

By taking
M=M^{\prime}
in Theorem
19
, we have the following relationships between the deviations for channel simulation under the non-signaling and the shared randomness-assisted scenarios.

Lemma 22
.

Let
W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X})
be a channel.
For any
M\geqslant 1
, it holds that

1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(M,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\geqslant 1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(M,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\geqslant\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)\cdot\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(M,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\right).

(138)

Proof.

Let
M=M^{\prime}
in Theorem
19
, we have

1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(M,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\geqslant 1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(M,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\geqslant\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{M}\right)^{M}\right)\cdot\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(M,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\right).

(139)

This results in (
138
) by noting that
(1-\frac{1}{M})^{M}\leqslant\frac{1}{e}
for all
M\geqslant 1
.
∎

Theorem 23
.

Let
W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X})
be a channel.
For all
r>0
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right)=\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\;(1-\alpha)\cdot\left(-r+\adjustlimits{\sup}_{p_{\mathsf{X}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}{\inf}_{q_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})}D_{\alpha}\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times q_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)\right).

(140)

Proof.

By Theorem
18
, it suffices to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right),

(141)

which can be directly shown via Lemma
22
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right),

(142)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{SR}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)\cdot\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}})\right)\right)

(143)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\epsilon^{\mathrm{NS}}(\lfloor e^{nr}\rfloor,W_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}^{\otimes n})\right).\qed

(144)

6
Conclusion

In this study, we derived the error exponent and the strong converse exponent for channel simulation in the non-signaling and the shared randomness-assisted settings.
We expressed the exponents in terms of the Rényi divergences.
It should be noted that our expressions of exponents hold for all rates
r>0
,
i.e.
, there is no critical rate, despite the fact that the expressions only become nontrivial when
r
is above or below certain thresholds.
Our finding is quite surprising as compared to the error exponent of channel coding, in which the critical rate is involved
[
18
]
.
The method of types has proven to be a useful tool in analyzing the relevant terms in the non-signaling regimes.
Along with earlier findings on small- and moderate-deviation analysis, this research offers a comprehensive study of finite-blocklength analysis of the channel simulation task.
For future studies, it would be intriguing to investigate whether the methods and insights presented here can be generalized to address the unresolved issues in channel interconversion, especially, the corresponding error exponent and the strong converse exponent.

Appendix A
Classical Post-Selection Lemma with Integral Representation

Lemma

(Lemma
6
restated)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set.
There exists a probability measure
\nu
on the set of all pmfs on
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})

(145)

for every permutation invariant pmf
p_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}^{n})

Proof.

We use some elements from quantum information theory to construct the desired measure
\nu
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space with a basis

indexed by the set
\mathcal{A}
, and let
\mathsf{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{A}_{n}
be
n
quantum systems, each with the same state space
\mathcal{H}
.
For each type

(see Definition
7

\lvert\psi_{p}\rangle
to be the following pure state on the joint system
\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{p}\rangle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left\lvert\mathcal{T}_{n}(p)\right\rvert}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(p)}\lvert a_{1}\rangle\otimes\lvert a_{2}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes\lvert a_{n}\rangle.

(146)

Observe that
\lvert\psi_{p}\rangle
is invariant under any permutations of
\mathsf{A}_{1},\mathsf{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathsf{A}_{n}
,
i.e.
,
\lvert\psi_{p}\rangle
lies inside the symmetric subspace
\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
.
Since the pmf
p_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}
is permutation invariant, we can write

p_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}(\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n})=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{A})}\alpha_{p}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{n}(p)|}\mathbbm{1}\{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(p)\}}_{\eqqcolon\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n})}\qquad\forall\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}

(147)

for some pmf
\{\alpha_{p}\}_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{A})}

\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
.
Correspondingly, we define
\rho_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}
to be the following density operator on
\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{A})}\alpha_{p}\cdot\lvert\psi_{p}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{p}\rvert.

(148)

By construction,
\rho_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}
is supported on
\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
.
Thus, by
[
7
]
, it holds that

\rho_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}}\preccurlyeq\binom{n+\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert-1}{n}\cdot\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lvert\phi\rangle}\;\lvert\phi\rangle\!\langle\phi\rvert^{\otimes n}

(149)

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lvert\phi\rangle}

\mathcal{H}
.

Now, we apply the following quantum channel (CPTP map)

\mathcal{M}:\rho\mapsto\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rvert\rho\lvert\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rangle\,\lvert\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rangle\!\langle\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rvert

(150)

on both sides of the inequality (
149
).

148

146

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\text{LHS of~\eqref{eq:perm-inv-de-finetti:quantum}})

(151)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{A})}\alpha_{p}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(p)}\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathcal{T}_{n}(p)\right\rvert}\cdot\lvert\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rangle\!\langle\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rvert

(152)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{A})}\alpha_{p}\cdot\mathcal{U}_{p}=p_{\mathbf{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{n}},

(153)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\text{RHS of~\eqref{eq:perm-inv-de-finetti:quantum}})

\displaystyle=\binom{n+\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert-1}{n}\cdot\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lvert\phi\rangle}\;\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\left\lvert\left\langle\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\middle|\phi\right\rangle^{\otimes n}\right\rvert^{2}\cdot\lvert\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rangle\!\langle\mathbf{a}_{1}^{n}\rvert

(156)

where we have treated pmfs as diagonal density operators,

\mathcal{A}
for all pure state
\lvert\phi\rangle
.

\displaystyle\leqslant\xi\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert\cdot\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}:q(a)>0}\log{\frac{1}{q(a)}}+\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert\cdot\sup_{0\leqslant t_{0}\leqslant t_{1}\leqslant 1:t_{1}-t_{0}\leqslant\xi}\left\lvert t_{1}\log{t_{1}}-t_{0}\log{t_{0}}\right\rvert

(172)

\displaystyle\leqslant\xi\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert\cdot\log{\frac{1}{q_{\min}}}+\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert\cdot\xi\cdot\log{\frac{1}{\xi}}.\qed

Proof of Lemma

.

The proof utilizes the triangular inequality on absolute values and the property of the function
t\mapsto t\cdot\log{t}
in a similar way as in the proof of Lemma
12
.
To prove (
174
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip 13.3pt\left\lvert D\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times p_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)-D\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{x,y}p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\cdot\log{\frac{V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)}{p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}}-\sum_{x,y}p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\cdot\log{\frac{\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)}{\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}}\right\rvert

(176)

\displaystyle\ \begin{aligned} \leqslant\sum_{x,y}\left\lvert p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\cdot\log{\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\right)}-p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\cdot\log{\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\right)}\right\rvert\\
+\sum_{y}\left\lvert p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\log{p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}-\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\log{\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}\right\rvert\end{aligned}

(177)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{Y}\right\rvert\cdot\xi\cdot\log{\frac{1}{\xi}}+\sum_{y}\left\lvert p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\log{p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}-\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\log{\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}\right\rvert.

(178)

Note that

\displaystyle\left\lvert p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)-\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{x}p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)-p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x}\left\lvert p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)-p_{\mathsf{X}}(x)\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)\right\rvert

(181)

Hence,

\sum_{y}\left\lvert p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\log{p_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}-\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\log{\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert\mathcal{Y}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\cdot\xi\cdot\log{\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\cdot\xi}}.

(182)

Combining (
182
) with (
178
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert D\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times p_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)-D\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot\widetilde{V}_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{Y}\right\rvert\cdot\xi\cdot\log{\frac{1}{\xi}}+\left\lvert\mathcal{Y}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\cdot\xi\cdot\log{\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\cdot\xi}}=\text{RHS of~\eqref{eq:I:continuity:1:app}}.

\displaystyle\hskip 13.3pt\left\lvert D\left(p_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|p_{\mathsf{X}}\times p_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)-D\left(\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot V_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}\middle\|\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{X}}\times\widetilde{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)\right\rvert

Proof of Eq.

We restate (
130
) as the following lemma.

Lemma

(Eq. (
130
) restated)
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive sequence such that
\{\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent.
For any
r,\delta>0
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right)\right)}.

(
130
, repeated)

Proof.

Denote
L\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}
.
Let
\eta\in(0,\left\lvert L\right\rvert/2)
be picked arbitrarily.
There exists
N(\eta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L-\eta\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}\leqslant L+\eta
for all
n\geqslant N(\eta)
.
Furthermore, there must also exist some
N^{\prime}(\eta)
and
N^{\prime\prime}
such that
e^{n\cdot\delta}\geqslant-2n\cdot(L-\eta)
and
e^{-n\cdot r}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\cdot e^{n\cdot\delta}
for
n\geqslant N^{\prime}(\eta)
and
n\geqslant N^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.
Thus, for all
n\geqslant\max\{N(\eta),N^{\prime}(\eta),N^{\prime\prime}\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(n\cdot(L-\eta)\right)\right)}

(191)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(2\cdot a_{n}\right)}=\frac{1}{n}\log{a_{n}}+\frac{\log{2}}{n}

(192)

\displaystyle\leqslant L+\eta+\frac{\log{2}}{n}.

(193)

Let
n
tend to infinity, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right)\right)}\leqslant L+\eta.

(194)

Since the above holds for positive
\eta
that are arbitrarily close to
0
, it must hold that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right)\right)}\leqslant L.

(195)

On the other hand, since
\exp(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r})>0
, it is immediate that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left(a_{n}+\exp\left(-e^{n\cdot\delta}+e^{-n\cdot r}\right)\right)}\geqslant L.

(196)

Finally, (
130
) is the result of combining the above two inequalities.
∎

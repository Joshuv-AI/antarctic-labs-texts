Ferron-driven photoferroic hysteresis in van der Waals CuInP2S6
Authors: Sambhu Jana, Baolong Zhang, Sobhan Subhra Mishra, Thomas CaiWei Tan, Apoorva Chaturvedi, Edwin Hang Tong Teo, Ranjan Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractControlling ferroelectric polarization with light promises a frontier for ultrafast optoelectronics. Yet the fundamental mechanism by which transient photoexcitation produces persistent photocurrent retention has remained elusive. Here we demonstrate a quasiparticle state that mediates photoferroic retention in the van der Waals ferroelectric CuInP2S6. We identify ferrons, collective excitations of dipole-carrying lattice vibrations, as carriers of optically imprinted polarization states. Narrowband resonant ferron oscillations at 2.16 THz exhibit a butterfly-shaped hysteresis that mirrors the photocurrent hysteresis response, directly linking ferron dynamics to polarization-dependent photocurrent retention at room temperature. Above-bandgap photoexcitation drives ferron formation through displacive excitation of coherent lattice motion, enabling carrier-mediated reconfiguration of ferroelectric domains, while sub-bandgap excitation leaves the polarization unaltered. The analytical calculations quantitatively capture the symmetry-dependent photocurrent generation and the consequence of the ferron-mediated hysteresis. These results establish ferronics, the manipulation of ferroelectric order through ferron quasiparticles, as a distinct paradigm for optically controlled polarization and photocurrent retention in low-dimensional quantum materials.Subject terms: Nonlinear optics, Terahertz optics

Introduction

Controlling spontaneous polarization with light represents a frontier challenge in condensed matter physics, promising advances in ultrafast memory and neuromorphic computing technologies. Photo-ferroelectricity refers to the phenomenon in which the ferroelectric polarization state of a material is controlled by light irradiation, rather than by an applied electric field1,2. Unlike traditional electric-field-driven switching, photo-ferroelectricity exploits intrinsic light–matter interactions to achieve stable and reversible polarization control under zero-bias conditions3–5. This mechanism offers ample opportunities for ultrafast wireless read/write operations, as it can reduce switching times to the picosecond regime with minimal energy dissipation. A primary mechanism underpinning this phenomenon is the bulk photovoltaic effect (BPVE), a nonlinear optical process that produces steady-state photocurrents under ultrafast photoexcitation, without the need for external bias or built-in junctions6–8. Despite good theoretical understanding of BPVE-driven switching, the realization of a photoferroelectric memory, a persistent change in state sustained after optical excitation stops, remains a significant challenge.

The recent progress of low-dimensional van der Waals ferroelectrics (vdW), such as CuInP2S6 (CIPS), offers a promising material platform for optoferroelectric applications by combining facile exfoliation and integration, significant both in-plane and out-of-plane optical nonlinearity, and robust displacive ferroelectricity at room temperature9–11. One distinctive feature of such material is the presence of ferron, a quasiparticle representing a coherent, polarization-carrying soft phonon mode10,12–14. Unlike ordinary phonons, ferrons are directly linked to the ferroelectric order parameter and can be driven coherently either through stimulated Raman scattering or through the displacive type Raman scattering, depending on the photon energy of the pump pulse15,16. When a polar material is excited with photon energy hυ > Eg, the transient generation of photocarriers leads to the screening of the built-in electric field. However, the direct observation of ferron dynamics and their role in photoferroelectric switching remains largely unexplored. Terahertz (THz) emission spectroscopy provides a powerful, contact-free approach to probe these dynamics. The emitted THz waveform is proportional to the second time derivative of the macroscopic polarization17, thereby enabling a direct measurement of the polarization current. This capability allows a clear distinction between incoherent carrier responses and coherent ferron-driven oscillations, revealing the intrinsic polarization dynamics of the system. Experimentally, ferron excitation manifests as sharp THz emission peaks, in contrast to conventional phonon-related absorption dips seen in standard nonlinear crystals such as ZnTe18 or LiNbO₃19, emphasizing its distinct polarization-carrying nature. The resulting emission peak confirms that the optically excited mode carries a macroscopic dipole moment and contributes directly to the ferroelectric order parameter, distinguishing it from conventional optical phonons that do not couple strongly to polarization.

Here, we demonstrate and characterize ferron-mediated photoferroelectric memory in CIPS, addressing two critical, unresolved questions. First, while ferroelectric hysteresis is well described through external electric field tuning via electrodes, the realization of THz photoferroic retention (hysteresis) mediated by ferron oscillations and photocarrier-screened depolarization fields remains elusive and demands further investigation. Second, although BPVE is known to dominate the THz mechanism under above-bandgap excitation (with optical rectification (OR) prevailing below the bandgap), the feasibility of a higher-order BPVE mediated by two-photon absorption (TPA) at below-bandgap excitations in CIPS remains unexplored. This investigation into the nonlinear photoresponse and its connection to Ferron-driven hysteresis provides a novel pathway for manipulating electronic and polar orders, holding immense promise for the development of ultrafast photonic memory technologies.

Results and discussion

We employ THz emission spectroscopy to uncover the ultrafast, broadband, and giant BPVE and photoferroic memory effects in the van der Waals ferroelectric CIPS. As illustrated in Fig. 1a, femtosecond (fs) laser pulses are incident normally on the sample surface, generating THz radiation driven by the BPVE. Thin CIPS flakes with a thickness of approximately 1–2 μm are used in the experiments, and the x- and y-components of the emitted THz signal are resolved using two THz wire grid polarizers. A detailed description of the experimental design can be found in Supplementary Information, Note I. The side view (Fig. 1b) illustrates the stacking sequence and the presence of interlayer gaps9. The atomic structure comprises alternating layers stacked along the crystallographic c-axis, where each layer consists of [P2S6]4⁻ clusters coordinated by sulfur atoms, forming a network of edge-sharing octahedra. At room temperature, Cu ions are displaced either in the top or bottom of octahedral sites, close to the van der Waals, whereas In ions can be shifted downwards from the midplane of the layer20–22. This displacement leads to both in-plane (PIN), directed along the green arrow, and out-of-plane polarization (POOP), directed along the red arrow. Herein, we mainly focus on the in-plane polarization (PIN), as all measurements are performed with normal incidence in transmission mode using terahertz emission spectroscopy. The optoelectronic properties are characterized by absorption spectroscopy (Fig. 1c), revealing a sharp absorption edge corresponding to a direct bandgap of 2.52 eV, consistent with previous reports23 (see details in Supplementary Information, Note II).

The concept of ferrons and their link to the conventional soft phonons are graphically showcased in Fig. 1d, e. The ferron is a quasiparticle identified in displacive ferroelectrics that carries an electric dipole10,24,25. In CIPS, above the Curie temperature, the crystal remains centrosymmetric and exhibits paraelectric behavior. For T < Tc, symmetry is broken by the off-centering displacement of the Cu atoms, as indicated by the red arrow in Fig. 1b, leading to ferroelectric order. The corresponding lattice dynamics can be examined by Raman spectroscopy, which is sensitive to optical phonon modes in the crystal. As shown in Fig. 1f, Raman spectroscopy reveals several phonon peaks, among which the low-frequency mode near 73 cm−1 is of particular interest because it is associated with the Cu-sublattice vibration that directly drives the ferroelectricity in this material. Since the displacement of these same Cu atoms is the microscopic origin of the spontaneous polarization and the associated dipole moment, this mode is intrinsically coupled to the ferroelectric order parameter. We thus label this vibrational mode as the ferron mode. By contrast, the higher-frequency Raman-active phonons mainly originate from other lattice vibrations, and they may or may not directly couple to the ferroelectric order parameter and are not observed in our THz emission measurements.

To probe these low-energy dynamics, we employ THz emission spectroscopy, a technique exquisitely sensitive to ultrafast polarization changes. The mechanism of these ferroic THz emission is either impulsive stimulated Raman scattering (ISRS) or displacive excitation, depending on whether the pump photon energy lies below or above the bandgap, respectively26. In this section, our analysis focuses on the coherent oscillations in the time-domain signal. As depicted in Fig. 2a, the THz time signals exhibit long-range (~15 ps) oscillations with a modulating amplitude similar to the Higgs mode27. This modulation is characteristic of the ferroelectric polarization-coupled phonon oscillation and signifies the emergence of the ferron mode in the ferroelectric material when excited along the polar axis. Such behavior reflects the dynamic coupling between the soft optical phonon mode and the ferroelectric order parameter, where amplitude modulation analogous to a Higgs-like collective excitation manifests due to the coupling between lattice vibrations and polarization dynamics28. The significance of this ferron emission is particularly evident at low pump fluences, where broadband THz emission, typically associated with shift current or other ultrafast nonlinear optical processes, is strongly suppressed. Under these conditions, the ferron oscillation gives rise to a sharp, narrow band THz emission peak at 2.16 THz (Fig. 2b, yellow spectrum). The ferron frequency exhibits a slight blue shift with increasing fluence (Fig. 2b, black dotted line), indicating either a strengthened coupling between the soft phonon mode and the ferroelectric polarization or that photocarrier excitation reduces screening of the polar lattice distortion.

A comparative analysis of the fluence-dependent ferron amplitude under below- and above-bandgap excitation (Fig. 2c, d) reveals distinct saturation mechanisms. For below-bandgap excitation driven by impulsive stimulated Raman scattering, saturation is primarily a consequence of reaching the optical damage threshold of the material, where further increase in fluence risks irreversible multiphoton absorption or dielectric breakdown. In contrast, the saturation under above-bandgap excitation occurs at a significantly lower fluence and is governed by the screening of the ferroelectric depolarization field by a dense photocarrier plasma. This low-fluence saturation under above-bandgap conditions is a direct signature of a carrier-density-limited regime. The photogenerated electrons and holes rapidly screen the internal depolarization field that is essential for the ferroelectric order. This screening effectively flattens the potential energy landscape of the polar lattice, diminishing the driving force for the displacive excitation of the ferron mode and causing its amplitude to saturate. Despite these different saturation behaviors, the quality factor (Q-factor ~11 and 14) of the ferron mode remains consistently high under both excitation conditions. The details of the Q-factor calculation are provided in Supplementary Information, Note VI.

This analysis focuses on the large-amplitude burst (main pulse) in the THz time-domain signal observed under above-bandgap optical excitation (3.10 eV > Eg), where single-photon absorption (SPA) dominates. Time-resolved charge carrier dynamics, shown in Fig. 3a, confirm the efficient generation of real photo-carriers following such excitation. Furthermore, the THz peak-to-peak amplitude (P-P Amp.) exhibits a linear dependence on pump fluence (Fig. 3b), consistent with a second-order nonlinear process. Thus, second-order mechanisms such as photon drag, optical rectification (OR), and the linear bulk photovoltaic effect (LBPVE) must be systematically evaluated. First, we exclude the contribution of the photon drag effect to the THz emission in CIPS, as it arises from the direct transfer of photon momentum to charge carriers and is typically prominent in materials with high carrier mobility and concentration29. It generates a wavevector-dependent photocurrent that reverses polarity when the excitation direction is reversed. Experimentally, no phase reversal of the THz signal is detected upon front- and back-side illumination (Fig. 3c), inconsistent with photon drag. Furthermore, measurements are performed in transmission geometry under normal incidence, where in-plane momentum transfer is negligible, further ruling out the photon drag contribution30. Second, above bandgap excitation, OR is irrelevant as it is a χ(2) process that typically dominates only under below bandgap excitation. Thus, it is evident that the BPVE is the primary mechanism driving terahertz emission in CIPS under above-bandgap photoexcitation.

Since the BPVE depends on the pump polarization, we investigate the polarization-dependent shift current response in CIPS by measuring the transient electric field components (Ex(t) and Ey(t)) associated with the photocurrents as a function of laser polarization angle (γ), defined as the angle between the pump electric field and the PIN. As shown in Fig. 3d, g, both Ex and Ey exhibit pronounced periodic modulation to γ over a period (0°–360°), and the corresponding contour map of Ex(t,γ) and Ey(t,γ) further reveals a distinct two-fold γ-dependency. Furthermore, the THz time signals for Ex(t) and Ey(t) under identical p-polarized light at 0° and 180° reveal a significant time delay, as shown in Supplementary Fig. S3. These features appear to be associated with the non-volatility induced by the intrinsic in-plane polarization of ferroelectric CIPS. To deepen our understanding of ultrafast pump polarization-dependent ferroelectric switching and the resulting ferroic memory, we analyze the terahertz peak-to-valley amplitudes (P-V Amp.) of Ex and Ey across cyclic variations of γ. This analysis unambiguously demonstrates the ultrafast photocurrent-induced THz ferroelectric hysteresis response (Fig. 3e, h), analogous to the static ferroelectric polarization behavior. The dominance of the ferron spectra over the BPVE-driven THz spectra, as shown in the insets of Fig. 3f, i, further confirms that this nonvolatility originates from the ferron mode. This behavior can be understood in terms of ferron excitation under above-bandgap photoexcitation. In this regime, ferrons are primarily driven by displacive excitation mediated by ultrafast free-carrier dynamics. The intense optical pump generates a dense electron–hole plasma that rapidly screens the internal depolarization field, transiently perturbs the polarization state, and lowers the energy barrier for domain wall formation. This effect is more pronounced at low pump polarization angles than at higher angles. In our experiment, the ferroelectric polarization direction of CIPS is aligned with the 0° pump polarization angle (with respect to the laboratory frame of reference), and the ferron mode couples anisotropically with the incident light polarization. This anisotropic interaction modulates the dynamic response of the ferroelectric order parameter and can induce a remanent internal field upon reversal of the pump polarization angle.

To further investigate this material, we have performed the pump and polarization-dependent THz emission experiment under the below bandgap photoexcitation. In most conventional ferroelectric and perovskite materials, below-bandgap optical excitation (1.55 eV) typically leads to THz emission dominated by OR31,32. In such cases, the THz emission scales linearly with pump intensity (ETHz ∝ Ipump)33,34, consistent with the characteristics of a χ(2) process. In contrast, our measurements reveal that the THz peak-to-peak (P–P) amplitude in CIPS exhibits a nonlinear dependence, following a quadratic (ETHz∝Ipump2) trend up to a certain limit before, as illustrated in Fig. 4a. This nonlinear scaling is attributed to higher-order nonlinear optical processes, specifically indicating the involvement of TPA35 and a fourth-order χ(4) BPVE36. Unlike OR, which involves only virtual electronic transitions and does not generate real carriers37, the observed quadratic behavior signifies the ultrafast excitation of real charge carriers from the valence band (VB) to the conduction band under fs-laser excitation. For further evidence of real charge carrier generation in this process, we perform time-resolved charge carrier dynamics measurements, as illustrated in Fig. 4b through optical pump THz probe spectroscopy. These results provide direct evidence of real electronic transitions from the VB to the conduction band, corroborating the unique TPA-driven higher-order shift current mechanism responsible for the observed THz emission in CIPS.

The TPA-driven THz generation in non-centrosymmetric materials such as CIPS is described by a fourth-order nonlinear optical process, in which the nonlinear polarization PTPA is proportional to the fourth-order susceptibility tensor and the square of the incident electric field intensity. The indices i, j, k, l, and m are Cartesian components corresponding to the spatial directions of the fields and the current. The shift current, a characteristic of the BPVE in non-centrosymmetric materials, originates from the real-space displacement of electronic charge centers during interband optical transitions. In CIPS, this process becomes particularly prominent when the photon energy (ℏω) exceeds half of the direct bandgap energy of the material, at which point the dominant contribution to the shift current arises from transitions between the uppermost VB and the lowest conduction band (CB). Band structure calculations reveal that the VB in CIPS is primarily composed of Cu 3d and S 3s orbitals, while the CB is dominated by In 5s and P 3p orbitals26,39. This distinct orbital character, combined with the intrinsic lattice distortion, where the center of mass of the metal cations is displaced relative to the S anions, breaks inversion symmetry and leads to a pronounced shift in the electronic charge center upon photoexcitation. Such a noncentrosymmetric structure supports both SPA and TPA processes. The selection rules governing optical transitions in CIPS are dictated by the symmetry and parity of the involved electronic states40. For SPA, transitions are allowed when there is a change in orbital angular momentum of Δl = ±1, consistent with electric dipole selection rules. In contrast, TPA permits transitions with Δl = 0, ±2, enabling access to states that are forbidden under single-photon excitation.

The transition probability is governed by the momentum matrix element (Pcv), and the effective masses of the carriers further modulate the shift current response. The resulting real-space displacement between the charge centers of the CB and VB, denoted as Δrcv = rc – rv, directly contributes to the magnitude of the shift current. This mechanism can be attributed to the TPA-driven higher-order BPVE link with the asymmetric redistribution of charge density between the VB and CB (see details in Supplementary Information Note IV). Experimental evidence for this process in CIPS is provided by the quadratic dependence of the THz emission amplitude on laser fluence, as well as polarization-resolved and time-resolved measurements that confirm the involvement of real carrier transitions and the anisotropic nature of the nonlinear response.

Our experimental data reveal that the angular dependence of the THz emission fits well with the theoretically predicted behavior, as described in Fig. 4c, d. The THz peak-to-valley amplitude exhibits both positive and negative values, indicating that the reversal of the photocurrent direction originates from the switching of the ferroelectric polarization. This switching is driven by the higher-order shift current generated through TPA, a nonlinear optical process that produces a limited density of free carriers. These transient carriers partially screen the internal depolarization field. When the optical pump field exceeds this screening threshold, the ferroelectric polarization realigns with the pump electric field, reversing the photocurrent direction.

However, the THz peak-to-valley amplitudes show no discernible photoferroic hysteresis effect at sub-bandgap photoexcitation (Fig. 4e, f). The absence of photocurrent hysteresis stems directly from the underlying excitation mechanisms: (i) Impulsive stimulated Raman scattering (ISRS) drives ferron excitation via virtual photons, inducing shallow oscillations without free carriers or potential energy surface reshaping, yielding no retention across all polarizations. Unlike above-bandgap displacive processes, ISRS cannot substantially screen the internal depolarization field or modulate the ferroelectric order parameter, preventing transition from volatile oscillatory dynamics to persistent photocurrent retention. (ii) Additionally, sub-bandgap photoexcitation generates sparse free carriers through TPA, a higher-order nonlinear process dependent on anisotropic susceptibility tensors. Although THz peak amplitude may scale linearly in this regime, the carrier density remains too low for strong depolarization field screening, producing subtle polarization-selective memory-like retention at specific angles. Thus, while ISRS efficiently drives coherent ferrons, neither mechanism provides the sustained electronic screening needed for photoferroic hysteresis.

In conclusion, this study presents the experimental demonstration of the ultrafast photoferroic hysteresis effect in the van der Waals ferroelectric CIPS, directly driven by the ferron quasiparticles. Congruent hysteresis in ferron and BPVE-driven photocurrent amplitudes, along with the dominant contribution of the ferron mode in the THz spectra, provides clear evidence of the ferron-driven photocurrent retention. We experimentally and theoretically elucidate the nonlinear photoresponse of CIPS and uncover the detailed mechanism of ultrafast photocurrent generation. By harnessing optical excitation below the bandgap of CIPS, we reveal the quadratic scaling of the THz emission amplitude with pump fluence and observe ultrafast charge carrier dynamics, distinct signatures of a TPA-driven higher-order shift current under below-bandgap excitation. The distinct photocurrent generation and ferron excitation mechanisms under sub-bandgap excitation do not exhibit significant photocurrent hysteresis. These results establish ferronics as a distinct paradigm in light-matter interaction, where coherent quasiparticle excitations directly govern macroscopic ferroelectric order. By uniting photoferroic hysteresis with broadband bulk photovoltaic response, van der Waals ferroelectrics emerge as a compelling materials platform for integrated memory-photonics architectures, paving the way toward ultrafast, low-power, and optically reconfigurable electronics.

Methods

Synthesis of bulk single crystals

CIPS single crystals are synthesized using the chemical vapor transport (CVT) method. In this process, stoichiometric amounts of high-purity elemental copper, indium, phosphorus, and sulfur powders are thoroughly mixed and sealed in an evacuated quartz ampoule along with a small quantity of iodine, which serves as the transport agent. The ampoule is then placed in a two-zone furnace, with the source and growth zones maintained at 750 °C and 700 °C, respectively. The reaction is allowed to proceed for several days, enabling the growth of high-quality, regular-shaped single crystals. After the growth, crystals are extracted, and their phase purity and stoichiometry are confirmed by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) and Raman spectroscopy. The crystal structure in Fig. 1 is drawn using VESTA software.

Preparation of thin flakes

To obtain ultrathin CIPS flakes suitable for device fabrication, mechanical exfoliation is employed. The bulk CIPS single crystal is cleaved using adhesive tape (Scotch tape) to produce thin flakes, which are then transferred onto a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) substrate26. This method preserves the integrity of the van der Waals structure and minimizes contamination.

Raman spectroscopy

Raman spectroscopy measurements are performed to investigate the vibrational modes of the synthesized CIPS crystals. A 532 nm continuous-wave laser is used as the excitation source, with a laser power carefully controlled to avoid sample heating or damage. The measurement is carried out at room temperature under ambient conditions. The characteristic phonon modes observed in the Raman spectra confirm the layered crystal structure and high structural quality of CIPS.

Deformation cohomology of Nijenhuis algebras and applications to extensions, inducibility of automorphisms and homotopy algebras
Apurba Das

Abstract
. Our primary aim in this paper is to introduce and study the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator and of a Nijenhuis algebra. Our cohomology of a Nijenhuis algebra controls the simultaneous deformations of the underlying associative structure and the Nijenhuis operator. We interpret the second cohomology group as the space of all isomorphism classes of abelian extensions. Then we study the inducibility of a pair of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms in a given abelian extension and show that the corresponding obstruction can be seen as the image of a suitable Wells-type map. We also consider skeletal and strict 2 2 -term homotopy Nijenhuis algebras and characterize them by third cocycles of Nijenhuis algebras and crossed modules of Nijenhuis algebras, respectively. Finally, we introduce strict homotopy Nijenhuis operators on A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras and show that they induce N ​ S ∞ NS_{\infty} -algebras.

Keywords: Nijenhuis operators, Nijenhuis algebras, Cohomology, Deformations, Extensions, Homotopy Nijenhuis operators.

1.
Introduction

Finding the cohomology of an algebraic structure and applications to deformations and extensions are traditional problems initiated by Hochschild
[
20
]
and Gerstenhaber
[
16
]
for associative algebras. The same studies were subsequently generalized to the context of Lie algebras by Nijenhuis and Richardson
[
34
]
. Since then, cohomologies of various other algebraic structures were extensively studied and their applications to deformations were obtained. On the other hand, algebras are often equipped with additional compatible structures such as homomorphisms, derivations, representations etc. In such cases, it is also meaningful to consider simultaneous deformations (i.e. deformations of algebras and additional structures). In
[
17
]
Gerstenhaber and Schack first considered the cohomology and deformation theory of associative algebras endowed with homomorphisms. A further study on homomorphisms was also carried out using the minimal model of operads
[
14
]
and the method of derived brackets
[
15
]
. Similarly, the operadic treatment of algebras endowed with derivations is first considered by Loday in
[
30
]
.

In recent times, Rota-Baxter operators and their various cousins (such as relative Rota-Baxter operators, Rota-Baxter operators of any scalar weight
\lambda\in{\bf k}
, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, modified Rota-Baxter operators, averaging operators etc.) attract much interest due to their importance in the study of the classical Yang-Baxter equation, classical
r
-matrices, splitting of operads, pre-Lie algebras, (tri)dendriform algebras, infinitesimal bialgebras and quasisymmetric functions (see
[
18
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
19
]
and the references therein). Rota-Baxter operators and all their cousins can be characterized by their graphs as subalgebras of some bigger algebras. In the realm of the study of algebras equipped with additional structures, recently the cohomology and deformation theory of Rota-Baxter algebras are extensively studied. The first step toward such a study was given in
[
36
]
,
[
6
]
where the cohomology of a Rota-Baxter operator was defined. Subsequently, the authors in
[
24
]
,
[
9
]
construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements correspond to Rota-Baxter algebra structures. Then twisting by the Maurer-Cartan element, one obtains the controlling
L_{\infty}
-algebra for the corresponding Rota-Baxter algebra. The differential map of this controlling algebra is precisely the coboundary operator of the cochain complex defining the cohomology of the Rota-Baxter algebra. The same technique can be adapted to obtain the cohomology of algebras endowed with any of the above cousins of the Rota-Baxter operator. However, an exciting operator that appears in the linear deformation theory of algebraic structures
[
16
]
, integrable systems and tensor hierarchies in mathematical physics
[
23
]
, quantum bi-Hamiltonian systems
[
5
]
is the
Nijenhuis operator
.
A Nijenhuis operator on an associative algebra gives rise to a new associative algebra structure on the underlying space, called the deformed associative algebra. From the algebraic perspective, Nijenhuis operators also got a lot of attention. They were studied in a wide class of algebraic structures (see for instance
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

,

).
Unlike Rota-Baxter operators, it is important to remark that Nijenhuis operators cannot be characterized by their graphs as subalgebras of some bigger algebras. This makes the study of Nijenhuis operators a bit more intricate and therefore some results about Nijenhuis operators are not parallel to the study of Rota-Baxter operators. In
[
26
]
Leroux introduced the notion of an
NS-algebra
as the algebraic structure induced by a Nijenhuis operator (see also
[
25
]
). NS-algebras are a generalization of dendriform algebras and are closely related to twisted Rota-Baxter operators
[
37
]
. Recently, the present author has introduced the cohomology and deformation theory of an NS-algebra using the idea of nonsymmetric operads with multiplication
[
7
]
.

Our primary aim in this paper is to introduce and extensively study the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator (defined on an associative algebra) and also the cohomology of a Nijenhuis algebra. To begin with, we first recall the associative analogue of the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket considered recently by Baishya and the present author
[
3
]
. Since a Nijenhuis operator
N
is a Maurer-Cartan element for the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket, the operator
N
induces a cochain complex. We define the corresponding cohomology groups as the cohomology of the Nijenhuis operator
N
. It is important to note that, unlike the Rota-Baxter case, the cohomology of the Nijenhuis operator
N
cannot be expressed as the Hochschild cohomology of the deformed associative algebra. We find a homomorphism from the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator
N
to the cohomology of the induced NS-algebra. Consequently, we also obtain a homomorphism from the cohomology of
N
to the Hochschild cohomology of the deformed associative algebra. Next, we put our interest to define the cohomology of a Nijenhuis algebra. To do so, given a Nijenhuis algebra, we first obtain a homomorphism from the Hochschild cochain complex of the underlying associative algebra to the cochain complex induced by the Nijenhuis operator. The mapping cone corresponding to this homomorphism is defined to be the cochain complex associated with the Nijenhuis algebra. However, we are interested in a reduced version of this cochain complex to study deformations and abelian extensions of Nijenhuis algebras. We show that the cohomology groups thus obtained govern the deformations of the Nijenhuis algebra, i.e. simultaneous deformations of the underlying algebra and the Nijenhuis operator (cf. Theorem
4.1
, Theorem
4.2
). In the course of this paper, we explicitly define the cohomology of a Nijenhuis algebra with coefficients in a more general Nijenhuis bimodule. We observe that the second cohomology group with coefficients in a Nijenhuis bimodule parametrizes the isomorphism classes of all abelian extensions (cf. Theorem
4.3
).

Another interesting study related to the cohomology of an algebraic structure is the inducibility problem for a pair of automorphisms. Given an extension of abstract groups, C. Wells
[
39
]
first formulated an exact sequence connecting various automorphism groups and obtained a criterion for the inducibility of a pair of automorphisms. His study was further explored in various specific cases and generalized to other algebraic structures
[
21
]
,
[
4
]
. With Hazra and Mishra, the present author considered the extensions of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and studied the inducibility problem
[
32
]
. In particular, they defined the Wells map in the context of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras. They showed that a pair of Rota-Baxter Lie algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if its image under the Wells map vanishes identically. Therefore, it is natural to ask whether the inducibility problem can be answered in the context of Nijenhuis algebras. Given an abelian extension of Nijenhuis algebras, we first define a suitable Wells-type map
\mathcal{W}
and show that a pair of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if the pair is ‘compatible’ and its image under the map
\mathcal{W}
vanishes identically (cf. Theorem
5.3
).

On the other side, homotopy invariant algebraic structures play a prominent role in modern mathematical physics. Among others,
A_{\infty}
-algebras and
L_{\infty}
-algebras are the key ones (see, for example
[
22
]
,
[
13
]
). Our final aim in this paper is to define and study homotopy Nijenhuis operators on
A_{\infty}
-algebras. At first, we
consider homotopy Nijenhuis operators on

-algebras. A pair consisting of a

-term

-algebra and a homotopy Nijenhuis operator is called a

-term homotopy Nijenhuis algebra
. Particular attention are given to skeletal and strict

-term homotopy Nijenhuis algebras. We show that skeletal

-term homotopy Nijenhuis algebras can be characterized by third cocycles of Nijenhuis algebras. Then we introduce crossed modules of Nijenhuis algebras and show that they are equivalent to strict
2
-term homotopy Nijenhuis algebras. Next, we define the notion of a
strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator
on an arbitrary
A_{\infty}
-algebra. However, a generic homotopy Nijenhuis operator on an
A_{\infty}
-algebra is yet to be found. At last, we define the concept of an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra
(also called a

) and show that a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator induces an

-algebra structure. This generalizes the result of Leroux

]
that a Nijenhuis operator on an associative algebra gives rise to an NS-algebra.

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator
N
and obtain a homomorphism to the cohomology of the induced NS-algebra. Then using a byproduct of the Hochschild cochain complex of the underlying associative algebra and the cochain complex induced by the Nijenhuis operator, we define the cohomology of a Nijenhuis algebra in Section

-algebras.

2.
Nijenhuis operators and the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket

In this section, we first recall some examples and basic properties of Nijenhuis operators. In particular, given an associative algebra
A
, we describe the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket (that yields a graded Lie bracket) whose Maurer-Cartan elements correspond to Nijenhuis operators on
A
. Then we define and study the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator. In the end, we also obtain a homomorphism from the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator
N
to the cohomology of the induced NS-algebra. As a consequence, we connect the cohomology of the operator
N
to the Hochschild cohomology of the deformed associative algebra.

2.1 Definition
.

Let
A=(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra. A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a
Nijenhuis operator
on
A
if it satisfies

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot N(b)=N(N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b)),\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

2.2 Example
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be any associative algebra.

(i)

Then the identity map
\mathrm{Id}_{A}:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on
A
.

(ii)

For any element
x\in A
, the left multiplication map
l_{x}:A\rightarrow A,~\!a\mapsto x\cdot a
and the right multiplication map
r_{x}:A\rightarrow A,~\!a\mapsto a\cdot x
are both Nijenhuis operators on
A
. In particular, for any non-negative integer
k
, the map
N:{\bf k}[x]\rightarrow{\bf k}[x]
given by
N(x^{n})=x^{n+k}
is a Nijenhuis operator on
A={\bf k}[x].

(iii)

If
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on
A
then for any scalar
\lambda\in{\bf k}
, the map
\lambda N
is also a Nijenhuis operator on
A
.

2.3 Example
.

Let
A=(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra. An
A
-bimodule
is a vector space
M
endowed with two bilinear maps
\triangleright:A\times M\rightarrow M,~\!(a,u)\mapsto a\triangleright u
and
\triangleleft:M\times A\rightarrow M,~\!(u,a)\mapsto u\triangleleft a
that satisfy the following associative-like identities:

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\triangleright u=a\triangleright(b\triangleright u),\quad(a\triangleright u)\triangleleft b=a\triangleright(u\triangleleft b),\quad(u\triangleleft a)\triangleleft b=u\triangleleft(a\cdot b),\text{ for }a,b\in A\text{ and }u\in M.

Recall that, in this case, the direct sum
A\oplus M
carries an associative algebra structure (called the
semidirect product
) with multiplication

\displaystyle(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,v):=(a\cdot b~\!,~\!a\triangleright v+u\triangleleft b),\text{ for }(a,u),(b,v)\in A\oplus M.

(1)

(i)

For any linear map
d:A\rightarrow M
, its lift
\widetilde{d}:A\oplus M\rightarrow A\oplus M
defined by
\widetilde{d}(a,u)=(0,d(a))
is a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect product algebra
(A\oplus M,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!)
.

(ii)

A linear map
R:M\rightarrow A
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
(also called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
)
[
37
,
6
]
on
A
with respect to the
A
-bimodule
M
if it satisfies

\displaystyle R(u)\cdot R(v)=R(R(u)\triangleright v+u\triangleleft R(v)),\text{ for }u,v\in M.

It turns out that a linear map
R:M\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if its lift
\widetilde{R}:A\oplus M\rightarrow A\oplus M
defined by
\widetilde{R}(a,u)=(R(u),0)
is a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect product algebra
(A\oplus M,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!)
.

2.4 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
(M,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
be an
A
-bimodule. Suppose
R_{1},R_{2}:M\rightarrow A
are two relative Rota-Baxter operators that are compatible in the sense that their sum
R_{1}+R_{2}
is also a relative Rota-Baxter operator. Additionally, if
R_{2}
is invertible then
N=R_{1}R_{2}^{-1}
is a Nijenhuis operator on
A

[
27
]
.

2.5 Example
.

A
twilled associative algebra
is an associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
whose underlying vector space
A
has a direct sum decomposition
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
into two subalgebras. Let
p_{1}:A\rightarrow A
(resp.
p_{2}:A\rightarrow A
) be the projection map onto
A_{1}
(resp.
A_{2}
). Then
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are both Nijenhuis operators on
A
. In fact, any linear combination of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
is also a Nijenhuis operator on

2.6 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. A
free Nijenhuis algebra
over
A
is an associative algebra
F_{\mathrm{N}}(A)
with a Nijenhuis operator
\mathcal{N}:F_{\mathrm{N}}(A)\rightarrow F_{\mathrm{N}}(A)
and an algebra homomorphism

satisfying some universal property
[

. In the above reference, the authors have constructed the free Nijenhuis algebra over any associative algebra

.

2.7 Example
.

A
dendriform-Nijenhuis bialgebra

[
26
]
(see also
[
35
]
) is a triple
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\Delta)
consisting of an associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
and a coassociative coalgebra
(A,\Delta)
both defined on a same vector space satisfying additionally the following compatibility condition:

\displaystyle\Delta(a\cdot b)=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}\cdot b~\!+~\!a\cdot b_{(1)}\otimes b_{(2)}\!~-~\!a_{(1)}\cdot a_{(2)}\otimes b,\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

Here we use the Sweedler’s notation
\Delta(a)=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}
and
\Delta(b)=b_{(1)}\otimes b_{(2)}
to describe the coproducts. Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\Delta)
be a dendriform-Nijenhuis bialgebra. Consider the space
\mathrm{End}(A)
which has a natural associative algebra structure under the composition
\circ
. With this structure, the map

\displaystyle N:\mathrm{End}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)~~\text{ defined by }~~N(f)(a):=a_{(1)}\cdot f(a_{(2)}),\text{ for }f\in\mathrm{End}(A)\text{ and }a\in A

is a Nijenhuis operator on
(\mathrm{End}(A),\circ)
.

It is important to remark that Nijenhuis operators on an associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
are closely related to linear deformations of the algebra structure
[
16
]
(see also
[
27
]
). Indeed, if
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator then

\displaystyle a\cdot_{t}b:=a\cdot b+t~\!(N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b)),\text{ for }a,b\in A

is a linear deformation of the given associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
. However, this linear deformation is ‘trivial’ in the sense that it is equivalent to the undeformed one
a\cdot_{t}^{\prime}b=a\cdot b
.

Let
(A,\!~\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then the underlying vector space
A
inherits a new associative algebra structure with the (deformed) multiplication

\displaystyle a\cdot_{N}b:=N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

(2)

We often denote this deformed associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{N})
simply by
A_{N}
when no confusion arises. More generally, one has the following result
[
5
]
.

2.8 Proposition
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it.

(i)

Then for each
k\geq 0
, the map
N^{k}:A\rightarrow A
is also a Nijenhuis operator on the associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
.

(ii)

For any
k,l\geq 0
, the map
N^{l}:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the deformed associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot_{N^{k}}~\!)
. Moreover, the deformed algebras
(A,(~\!\cdot_{N^{k}})_{N^{l}})
and
(A,~\!\cdot_{N^{k+l}})
are the same.

In the following, we revise the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket
[
3
]
for a given associative algebra and study the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator. Let
A
be any vector space (not necessarily an associative algebra). For any
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m},A)
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
with
m,n\geq 1
, the
contraction
of
f
by
g
is denoted by
i_{g}f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m+n-1},A)
and it is defined by

\displaystyle(i_{g}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n-1}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{(i-1)(n-1)}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,a_{m+n-1}),

for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n-1}\in A
. Note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between associative algebra structures on the vector space
A
and elements
\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)
with
i_{\mu}\mu=0
.

Next, let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra with the corresponding multiplication map
\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)
, i.e.
\mu(a,b)=a\cdot b
, for all
a,b\in A
. Then the Hochschild coboundary operator of the associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
is the map
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},A)
, for
n\geq 0
, given by
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f:=(-1)^{n-1}~\!i_{f}\mu-i_{\mu}f
, for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1},

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A
. Further, there is an associative operation (called the
cup-product
operation)
\smile~\!~\!:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m},A)\times\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m+n},A)
given by

\displaystyle(f\smile g)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n}):=f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\cdot g(a_{m+1},\ldots,a_{m+n})

which yields a graded Lie bracket
[f,g]_{\smile}:=f\smile g~\!-~\!(-1)^{mn}~\!g\smile f
on the graded space
\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
. With all the above notations, the main result of
[
3
]
can be stated as follows.

2.9 Theorem
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra.

(i)

For any
m,n\geq 1
, define a bracket
[~,~]_{\mathsf{FN}}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m},A)\times\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m+n},A)
, called the
Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket
, by

\displaystyle[f,g]_{\mathsf{FN}}:=[f,g]_{\smile}~\!+~\!(-1)^{m}~\!i_{(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)}g~\!-~\!(-1)^{(m+1)n}~\!i_{(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}g)}f,

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes m},A)
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
.
Then
\big(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),[~,~]_{\mathsf{FN}}\big)
is a graded Lie algebra.

(ii)

A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
if and only if
N\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
\big(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),[~,~]_{\mathsf{FN}}\big)
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Note that the linear map
N
induces a map

\displaystyle d_{N}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},A)~~~\text{ given by }~~~d_{N}(f)=[N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}},\text{ for }f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A).

(3)

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle(d_{N}f)

\displaystyle(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=N(a_{1})\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})-(-1)^{n}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot N(a_{n+1})

(4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},N(a_{i})\cdot a_{i+1}+a_{i}\cdot N(a_{i+1})-N(a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1}),\ldots,a_{n+1})

\displaystyle-N\big(a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1}\big),

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A.
As
N
is a Nijenhuis operator (i.e.
[N,N]_{\mathsf{FN}}=0
), it turns out that
(d_{N})^{2}=0
. We can extend it to a map
d_{N}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
defined by

\displaystyle d_{N}(a)(b):=N(b)\cdot a-a\cdot N(b)-N(b\cdot a-a\cdot b),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

(5)

Then it also follows that
\{\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),d_{N}\}
is a cochain complex which we call the cochain complex associated with the Nijenhuis operator
N
. The corresponding cohomology groups are called the
cohomology groups
of the Nijenhuis operator
N
and are denoted by
H^{\bullet}(N)
.

2.10 Example
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be any associative algebra. Consider the identity map
\mathrm{Id}_{A}:A\rightarrow A
as a Nijenhuis operator on
A
. Then it follows from (
4
) and (
5
) that the map
d_{N=\mathrm{Id}_{A}}
is the zero map. As a consequence, we get that the cohomology groups of the Nijenhuis operator
N=\mathrm{Id}_{A}
are simply given by
H^{n}(\mathrm{Id}_{A})=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
, for all
n
. This shows that the cohomology of the identity map
\mathrm{Id}_{A}
(viewed as a Nijenhuis operator) is independent of the associative multiplication of
A
. This is not surprising as the identity map is a Nijenhuis operator for any associative multiplication.

2.11 Remark
.

It is important to note that the map
d_{N}
cannot be expressed as the Hochschild coboundary operator of the deformed associative algebra
A_{N}=(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!)
with coefficients in any bimodule. Equivalently, the cohomology of the Nijenhuis operator
N
is not the same as the Hochschild cohomology of the deformed associative algebra.

2.12 Remark
.

In
[
6
]
the present author has introduced the cohomology of a (relative) Rota-Baxter operator while studying its deformations. Let
R:M\rightarrow A
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator (see Example
2.3
(ii)). Then the cohomology of the operator
R
is given by the cohomology of the cochain complex
\{\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A),d_{R}\}
, where

\displaystyle d_{R}(a)(u):=~

\displaystyle R(u)\cdot a-a\cdot R(u)-R(u\triangleleft a-a\triangleright u),

\displaystyle(d_{R}f)(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1}):=~

\displaystyle R(u_{1})\cdot f(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n+1})-(-1)^{n}~\!f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\cdot R(u_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~\!f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},R(u_{i})\triangleright u_{i+1}+u_{i}\triangleleft R(u_{i+1}),\ldots,u_{n+1})

\displaystyle-R\big(u_{1}\triangleleft f(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}~\!f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\triangleright u_{n+1}\big),

for
a\in A
,
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A)
and
u,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1}\in M
. On the other hand, we may consider the (lift) Nijenhuis operator
\widetilde{R}:A\oplus M\rightarrow A\oplus M
(defined in Example
2.3
(ii)) on the semidirect product algebra and the cochain complex
\{\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}((A\oplus M)^{\otimes n},A\oplus M),d_{\widetilde{R}}\}
for this Nijenhuis operator. Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle d_{\widetilde{R}}\big(\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A)\big)\subset\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n+1},A),\text{ for all }n.

Hence
\{\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A),d_{\widetilde{R}}\}
is a subcomplex of
\{\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}((A\oplus M)^{\otimes n},A\oplus M),d_{\widetilde{R}}\}.

Moreover, while restricting to this subcomplex, one can observe that
d_{\widetilde{R}}=d_{R}
. This shows that the cochain complex of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
R
can be seen as a subcomplex of the cochain complex of the Nijenhuis operator
\widetilde{R}
.

Let
N
be a Nijenhuis operator on an associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
. Note that the differential
d_{N}
makes the triple
\big(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),[~,~]_{\mathsf{FN}},d_{N}\big)
into a differential graded Lie algebra (dgLa). In the following result, we observe that this dgLa controls the deformations of the Nijenhuis operator
N
.

2.13 Proposition
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then for any linear map
N^{\prime}:A\rightarrow A
, the sum
N+N^{\prime}:A\rightarrow A
is also a Nijenhuis operator if and only if
N^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the dgLa
\big(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),[~,~]_{\mathsf{FN}},d_{N}\big)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle[N+N^{\prime},N+N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}=~

\displaystyle[N,N]_{\mathsf{FN}}+[N,N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}+[N^{\prime},N]_{\mathsf{FN}}+[N^{\prime},N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle 2~\![N,N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}+[N^{\prime},N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}\quad(\because~[N,N]_{\mathsf{FN}}=0).

This shows that
[N+N^{\prime},N+N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}=0
if and only if
d_{N}(N^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}[N^{\prime},N^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{FN}}=0
. Hence the result follows.
∎

In the following, we first recall the cohomology of an NS-algebra and find a suitable homomorphism from the cohomology of a Nijenhuis operator
N
to the cohomology of the induced NS-algebra. As a consequence, we also get a homomorphism from the cohomology of
N
to the Hochschild cohomology of the deformed associative algebra
A_{N}=(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!)
.

2.14 Definition
.

[
26
]

An
NS-algebra

(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
is a vector space
A
endowed with three bilinear operations
\prec,\succ,\curlyvee:A\times A\rightarrow A
that satisfy the following set of identities:

\displaystyle(a\prec b)\prec c=~

\displaystyle a\prec(b\prec c+b\succ c+b\curlyvee c),

\displaystyle(a\succ b)\prec c=~

\displaystyle a\succ(b\prec c),

\displaystyle(a\prec b+a\succ b+a\curlyvee b)\succ c=~

\displaystyle a\succ(b\succ c),

\displaystyle(a\prec b+a\succ b+a\curlyvee b)\curlyvee c+(a\curlyvee b)\prec c=~

\displaystyle a\succ(b\curlyvee c)+a\curlyvee(b\prec c+b\succ c+b\curlyvee c),\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

2.15 Remark
.

In
[
29
]
Loday introduced the notion of a dendriform algebra in his study of the algebraic structure behind planar binary trees.
Note that an NS-algebra
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
for which
\curlyvee=0
is nothing but a dendriform algebra.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
is an NS-algebra. Then
A
can be given an associative algebra structure with the multiplication

\displaystyle a*b:=a\prec b+a\succ b+a\curlyvee b,\text{ for }a,b\in A.

The associative algebra
(A,~\!*~\!)
is said to be the
‘total associative algebra’
of the given NS-algebra. On the other hand, if
(A,~\cdot~\!)
is an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on it, then the vector space
A
inherits an NS-algebra structure with the operations

\displaystyle a\prec_{N}b:=a\cdot N(b),\quad a\succ_{N}b:=N(a)\cdot b~~~~\text{ and }~~~~a\curlyvee_{N}b:=-N(a\cdot b),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

The NS-algebra
(A,\prec_{N},\succ_{N},\curlyvee_{N})
is denoted by
A_{\mathrm{NS}}
and is said to be induced by the Nijenhuis operator
N
. Observe that the corresponding total associative algebra is precisely the deformed algebra
A_{N}=(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!)
given in (
2
).

Let
C_{n}
be the set of first
n
natural numbers. Since we will treat the elements of
C_{n}
as certain symbols, we write
C_{n}=\{[1],[2],\ldots,[n]\}
for conventional purposes. Let
A
be any vector space (not necessarily having any additional structure). Then for any
n\geq 1
, we define a linear space
\mathcal{O}_{A}(n)
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{A}(1):=\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{1}]\otimes A,A)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{O}_{A}(n)=\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{n+1}]\otimes A^{\otimes n},A),\text{ for }n\geq 2.

Given any
f\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(m)
,
g\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(n)
and
1\leq i\leq m
, we define an element (called the
partial composition
)
f\circ_{i}g\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(m+n-1)
by

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})

(6)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptf({\scriptstyle[r]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})&\text{ if }1\leq r\leq i-1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptf({\scriptstyle[i]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g({\scriptstyle[r-i+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})&\text{ if }i\leq r\leq i+n-1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptf({\scriptstyle[r-n+1]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1];a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})&\text{ if }{i+n\leq r\leq m+n-1},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptf({\scriptstyle[i]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g({\scriptstyle[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})&\\
~~+f({\scriptstyle[m+1]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})&\text{ if }r=m+n.\end{cases}

Then it has been shown in
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
that the collection of spaces
\{\mathcal{O}_{A}(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
endowed with the above partial compositions forms a nonsymmetric operad. This in particular implies that the shifted graded space
\mathcal{O}_{A}(\bullet+1)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{A}(n+1)
carries a graded Lie bracket

\displaystyle\llbracket f,g\rrbracket:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{(i-1)(n-1)}~\!f\circ_{i}g-(-1)^{(m-1)(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{(i-1)(m-1)}~\!g\circ_{i}f,

for
f\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(m)
and
g\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(n)
. Then an NS-algebra structure on the vector space
A
is equivalent to having a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
\big(\mathcal{O}_{A}(\bullet+1),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket\big)
. Explicitly, let the vector space
A
be endowed with three bilinear operations
\prec,\succ,\curlyvee:A\times A\rightarrow A
. Then we define an element
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(2)=\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{3}]\otimes A^{\otimes 2},A)
by

\displaystyle\pi([1];a,b)=a\prec b,\quad\pi([2];a,b)=a\succ b~~~~~\text{ and }~~~~~\pi([3];a,b)=a\curlyvee b,\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Then
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
is an NS-algebra if and only if
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(2)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
\big(\mathcal{O}_{A}(\bullet+1),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket\big)
. In this case, the Maurer-Cartan element
\pi
induces a map

\displaystyle\delta_{\pi}:\mathcal{O}_{A}(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{A}(n+1)~~~~\text{ given by }~~~~\delta_{\pi}(f):=(-1)^{n-1}~\!\llbracket\pi,f\rrbracket,\text{ for }f\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(n).

Then it follows that
\{\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{A}(n),\delta_{\pi}\}
is a cochain complex. The corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomologies of the NS-algebra
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
and they are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NS}}(A).

Next, let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then we have seen that
A_{\mathrm{NS}}=(A,\prec_{N},\succ_{N},\curlyvee_{N})
is an NS-algebra. Let
\pi_{N}\in\mathcal{O}_{A}(2)=\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{3}]\otimes A^{\otimes 2},A)
be the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
\big(\mathcal{O}_{A}(\bullet+1),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket\big)
. In other words,
\pi_{N}([1];a,b)=a\cdot N(b)
,
\pi_{N}([2];a,b)=N(a)\cdot b
and
\pi_{N}([3];a,b)=-N(a\cdot b)
, for
a,b\in A
. For each
n\geq 1
, we now define a map

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{A}(n+1)=\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{n+2}]\otimes A^{\otimes n+1},A)~~~~\text{ by }

\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\Theta_{n}(f)([r];~

\displaystyle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}):=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n+1}~\!a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})&\text{ if }r=1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\qquad 0&\text{ if }2\leq r\leq n,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptf(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})\cdot a_{n+1}&\text{ if }r=n+1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+i+1}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})&\text{ if }r=n+2,\end{cases}

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
,
[r]\in C_{n+2}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A
. Then it is easy to observe that
\Theta_{1}(N)=\pi_{N}.
Moreover, we have the following result.

2.16 Proposition
.

With the above notations, the collection of maps
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
describes a homomorphism from the reduced cochain complex
\{\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),d_{N}\}
of the Nijenhuis operator
N
to the shifted cochain complex
\{\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{A}(n+1),\delta_{\pi_{N}}\}
of the induced NS-algebra
A_{\mathrm{NS}}=(A,\prec_{N},\succ_{N},\curlyvee_{N})
. As a result, there is a homomorphism
\Theta_{\bullet}:H^{\bullet}(N)\rightarrow H^{\bullet+1}_{\mathrm{NS}}(A_{\mathrm{NS}})
between the corresponding cohomology groups.

Proof.

For any
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A
, we observe that

\displaystyle\llbracket\pi_{N},\Theta_{n}(f)\rrbracket([1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2})

\displaystyle=\pi_{N}\big([1];\Theta_{n}(f)([1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}),a_{n+2}\big)+(-1)^{n}~\!\pi_{N}\big([1];a_{1},\Theta_{n}(f)({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+2]};a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+2})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-(-1)^{n}~\!\Theta_{n}(f)\big([1];\pi_{N}([1];a_{1},a_{2}),a_{3},\ldots,a_{n+2}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-(-1)^{n}~\!\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}(-1)^{i-1}~\!\Theta_{n}(f)\big([1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\pi_{N}({\scriptstyle[1]+[2]+[3]};a_{i},a_{i+1}),\ldots,a_{n+2}\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}~\!a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})\cdot N(a_{n+2})+(-1)^{n}~\!a_{1}\cdot N\big((-1)^{n+1}(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+2})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+a_{1}\cdot N(a_{2})\cdot f(a_{3},\ldots,a_{n+2})+\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}(-1)^{i-1}~\!a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i}\cdot_{N}a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+2})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\cdot\big([N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}}(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+2})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}~\!(\Theta_{n+1}[N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}})([1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2}).

For any
2\leq r\leq n+1
, one can easily observe from the definitions that

\displaystyle\llbracket\pi_{N},\Theta_{n}(f)\rrbracket([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2})=0=(-1)^{n}~\!(\Theta_{n+1}[N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}})([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2}).

We also have

\displaystyle\llbracket\pi_{N},\Theta_{n}(f)\rrbracket([n+2];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2})

\displaystyle=\pi_{N}\big([2];\Theta_{n}(f)({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+2]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}),a_{n+2}\big)+(-1)^{n}~\!\pi_{N}\big([2];a_{1},\Theta_{n}(f)([n+1];a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+2})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-)^{i-1}~\!\Theta_{n}(f)\big([n+1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\pi_{N}({\scriptstyle[1]+[2]+[3]};a_{i},a_{i+1}),\ldots,a_{n+2}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\Theta_{n}(f)\big([n+1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\pi_{N}([2];a_{n+1},a_{n+2})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}~\!N\big((\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})\big)\cdot a_{n+2}+(-1)^{n}~\!N(a_{1})\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})\cdot a_{n+2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~\!f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i}\cdot_{N}a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})\cdot a_{n+2}-f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot N(a_{n+1})\cdot a_{n+2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\big([N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})\big)\cdot a_{n+2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}~\!(\Theta_{n+1}[N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}})([n+2];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2}).

Finally, it is also tedious but straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\llbracket\pi_{N},\Theta_{n}(f)\rrbracket([n+3];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2})=(-1)^{n}~\!(\Theta_{n+1}[N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}})([n+3];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2}).

Combining all these cases, we obtain that
\llbracket\pi_{N},\Theta_{n}(f)\rrbracket=(-1)^{n}~\!\Theta_{n+1}([N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}})
. Hence

\displaystyle(\delta_{\pi_{N}}\circ\Theta_{n})(f)=(-1)^{n}~\!\llbracket\pi_{N},\Theta_{n}(f)\rrbracket=\Theta_{n+1}[N,f]_{\mathsf{FN}}=(\Theta_{n+1}\circ d_{N})(f).

This concludes the proof.
∎

It has been shown in
[
7
, Theorem 5.4]
that there is a homomorphism from the cochain complex of an NS-algebra to the Hochschild cochain complex of the corresponding total associative algebra with coefficients in the adjoint bimodule. Combining this fact with Proposition
2.16
, we get the following.

2.17 Theorem
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then the collection of maps
\{\Psi_{n}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},A)\}_{n\geq 1}
given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}):=(\Theta_{n}(f))({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+2]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=(-1)^{n+1}~\!(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})

induces a homomorphism
\Psi_{\bullet}:H^{\bullet}(N)\rightarrow H^{\bullet+1}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A_{N};A_{N})
from the cohomology of the Nijenhuis operator
N
to the Hochschild cohomology of the deformed associative algebra
A_{N}
.

3.
Cohomology of Nijenhuis algebras

In this section, we introduce and study the cohomology of Nijenhuis algebras. Among others, we find a long exact sequence connecting the cohomology groups of a Nijenhuis algebra and the cohomologies of the underlying associative algebra and the Nijenhuis operator.

3.1 Definition
.

A
Nijenhuis algebra
is an associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
endowed with a distinguished Nijenhuis operator
N:A\rightarrow A
on it.

A Nijenhuis algebra as above is denoted by the pair
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
or simply by
(A,N)
when the associative multiplication of
A
is clear from the context.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
and
((A^{\prime},~\!\cdot^{\prime}~\!),N^{\prime})
be two Nijenhuis algebras. A
homomorphism
of Nijenhuis algebras from
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
to
((A^{\prime},~\!\cdot^{\prime}~\!),N^{\prime})
is an associative algebra morphism
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
satisfying
N^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ N
. Further, it is said to be an isomorphism of Nijenhuis algebras if
\varphi
is additionally bijective. Given a Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
, we denote the group of all Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms (self isomorphisms) of
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
by the notation
\mathrm{Aut}(A,N).

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be a Nijenhuis algebra. A
Nijenhuis bimodule
over
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
is given by a pair
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
consisting of an
A
-bimodule
(M,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
with a linear map
N_{M}:M\rightarrow M
satisfying

\displaystyle N(a)\triangleright N_{M}(u)=~

\displaystyle N_{M}\big(N(a)\triangleright u+a\triangleright N_{M}(u)-N_{M}(a\triangleright u)\big),

\displaystyle N_{M}(u)\triangleleft N(a)=~

\displaystyle N_{M}\big(N_{M}(u)\triangleleft a+u\triangleleft N(a)-N_{M}(u\triangleleft a)\big),

for all
a\in A
and
u\in M
.

3.2 Example
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be any Nijenhuis algebra. Then there is a natural Nijenhuis bimodule
((A,\triangleright_{\mathrm{ad}},\triangleleft_{\mathrm{ad}}),N)
over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
, where
a\triangleright_{\mathrm{ad}}b=a\triangleleft_{\mathrm{ad}}b=a\cdot b
(for
a,b\in A
) defines the adjoint
A
-bimodule structure on the vector space
A
. This is called the adjoint Nijenhuis bimodule.

3.3 Example
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be an associative algebra and
(M,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
be any
A
-bimodule.

(i)

Then
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),\mathrm{Id}_{M})
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),\mathrm{Id}_{A})
.

(ii)

For any
x\in A
, consider the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N=l_{x})
. Then it can be shown that
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M}=x\triangleright-)
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N=l_{x})
. Similarly, the pair
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M}=-\triangleleft x)
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N=r_{x})
.

3.4 Example
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be a Nijenhuis algebra and
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
be a Nijenhuis bimodule over it. Then for any
k\geq 0
, the pair
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),(N_{M})^{k})
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N^{k})
.

3.5 Example
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
and
((A^{\prime},~\!\cdot^{\prime}~\!),N^{\prime})
be two Nijenhuis algebras and
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
be a homomorphism of Nijenhuis algebras. Then
((A^{\prime},\triangleright,\triangleleft),N^{\prime})
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
, where
\triangleright:A\times A^{\prime}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
and
\triangleleft:A^{\prime}\times A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
are respectively given by
a\triangleright u=\varphi(a)\cdot^{\prime}u
and
u\triangleleft a=u\cdot^{\prime}\varphi(a)
, for
a\in A
and
u\in A^{\prime}
.

3.6 Example
.

(Lifting of Rota-Baxter bimodules) A
Rota-Baxter algebra
is a pair
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),R)
consisting of an associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
with a distinguished Rota-Baxter operator
R:A\rightarrow A
. Then we have seen that the map
R
lifts to a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect product algebra. In other words,
((A\oplus A,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!),\widetilde{R})
is a Nijenhuis algebra, where

\displaystyle(a,b)\cdot_{\ltimes}(c,d)=(a\cdot c~\!,~\!a\cdot d+b\cdot c)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\widetilde{R}(a,b)=(R(b),0),

for

4.
Deformations and abelian extensions of Nijenhuis algebras

In this section, we first study (formal and infinitesimal) deformations of Nijenhuis algebras. Among others, we show that the set of all equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations of a Nijenhuis algebra

We prove that the set of all isomorphism classes of such abelian extensions has a bijection with the second cohomology group of the Nijenhuis algebra with coefficients in the given Nijenhuis bimodule.

A
. That is,

\displaystyle A[[t]]=\big\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}t^{i}~\!|~\!a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i=0,1,2,\ldots\big\}.

Then
A[[t]]
is obviously a
{\bf k}[[t]]
-module. A
formal deformation
of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
consists of two formal sums

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=~

\displaystyle\mu_{0}+t\mu_{1}+t^{2}\mu_{2}+\cdots~\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)[[t]]~~~\text{ with }\mu_{0}=\mu,

\displaystyle N_{t}=~

\displaystyle N_{0}+tN_{1}+t^{2}N_{2}+\cdots~\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)[[t]]~~~\text{ with }N_{0}=N

that make
(A[[t]],\mu_{t})
into an associative algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[[t]]
, and the
{\bf k}[[t]]
-linear extension map
N_{t}:A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]]
is a Nijenhuis operator on the associative algebra
(A[[t]],\mu_{t})
. In other words,
((A[[t]],\mu_{t}),N_{t})
is a Nijenhuis algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[[t]]
. We denote a formal deformation as above simply by the Nijenhuis algebra
((A[[t]],\mu_{t}),N_{t})
over the ring
{\bf k}[[t]]
.

It follows that
((A[[t]],\mu_{t}),N_{t})
is a formal deformation of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
if and only if the following set of equations are hold: for any
p\geq 0
and
a,b,c\in A
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=p}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=p}\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c)),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=p}\mu_{i}(N_{j}(a),N_{k}(b))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=p}\big\{N_{i}\big(\mu_{j}(N_{k}(a),b)+\mu_{j}(a,N_{k}(b))-N_{k}(\mu_{j}(a,b))\big)\big\}.

Note that for
p=0
, both the above identities are trivially hold as
\mu_{0}=\mu
and
N_{0}=N
. However, for
p=1
, the above equations give rise to

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b)\cdot c+\mu_{1}(a\cdot b,c)=~

\displaystyle a\cdot\mu_{1}(b,c)+\mu_{1}(a,b\cdot c),

(14)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(N(a),N(b))+N_{1}(a)\cdot N(b)+N(a)\cdot N_{1}(b)=~

\displaystyle N_{1}(N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b))

(15)

\displaystyle+N\big(\mu_{1}(N(a),b)+\mu_{1}(a,N(b))

\displaystyle-N\mu_{1}(a,b)\big)+N(N_{1}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N_{1}(b)-N_{1}(a\cdot b)),

for all
a,b,c\in A
. The identity (
14
) simply means that
(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\mu_{1})(a,b,c)=0
while the identity (
15
) can be equivalently rephrased as
(d_{N}(N_{1})+\partial^{N}(\mu_{1}))(a,b)=0
. Combining these two observations, we get that

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(\mu_{1},N_{1})=\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mu_{1})~\!,~\!d_{N}(N_{1})+\partial^{N}(\mu_{1})\big)=0.

In other words,
(\mu_{1},N_{1})\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)=C^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N))
is a
2
-cocycle in the cochain complex of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
with coefficients in the adjoint Nijenhuis bimodule. The
2
-cocycle
(\mu_{1},N_{1})
is called the
infinitesimal
of the given formal deformation
((A[[t]],\mu_{t}),N_{t}).

Let
((A[[t]],\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\mu_{i}),N_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}N_{i})
and
((A[[t]],\mu^{\prime}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\mu^{\prime}_{i}),N^{\prime}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}N^{\prime}_{i})
be two formal deformations of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
. They are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a
{\bf k}[[t]]
-linear map
\varphi_{t}:A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]]
of the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{0}+t\varphi_{1}+t^{2}\varphi_{2}+\cdots~\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)[[t]]\quad(\text{with }\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}_{A})

that defines an isomorphism of Nijenhuis algebras from
((A[[t]],\mu_{t}),N_{t})
to
((A[[t]],\mu^{\prime}_{t}),N^{\prime}_{t})
. In other words, the following set of equations must hold: for any
p\geq 0
and
a,b\in A
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=p}\varphi_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))=\sum_{i+j+k=p}\mu_{i}^{\prime}(\varphi_{j}(a),\varphi_{k}(b))\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{i+j=p}\varphi_{i}\circ N_{j}=\sum_{i+j=p}N_{i}^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{j}.

For
p=0
, both the above identities are trivially hold as
\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}^{\prime}=\mu
,
N_{0}=N^{\prime}_{0}=N
and
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}_{A}
. However, for
p=1
, we get that

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b)-\mu_{1}^{\prime}(a,b)=~

\displaystyle a\cdot\varphi_{1}(b)-\varphi_{1}(a\cdot b)+\varphi_{1}(a)\cdot b=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\varphi_{1})(a,b),

\displaystyle N_{1}-N_{1}^{\prime}=~

\displaystyle N\circ\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{1}\circ N.

Combining these, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},N_{1})-(\mu^{\prime}_{1},N_{1}^{\prime})=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\varphi_{1})~\!,~\!-\partial^{N}(\varphi_{1}))=\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(\varphi_{1}).

As a conclusion, we have the following result.

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be a Nijenhuis algebra. Then the infinitesimal of any formal deformation of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
is a
2
-cocycle in the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N)),\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}\}
. Moreover, the cohomology class of the corresponding
2
-cocycle depends only on the equivalence class of the formal deformation.

Given a formal deformation of a Nijenhuis algebra, we have seen that the corresponding ‘infinitesimal’ is a
2
-cocycle. However, an arbitrary
2
-cocycle need not be the infinitesimal of some formal deformation. In the following, we consider the notion of ‘infinitesimal deformations’ of a Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
, and show that the set of all equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations has a bijection with the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N)).

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be a Nijenhuis algebra. As before, let
\mu
denote the associative multiplication of
A
. An
infinitesimal deformation
of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
consists of two parametrized sum
\mu_{t}=\mu+t\mu_{1}
and
N_{t}=N+tN_{1}
that makes
((A[t]/(t^{2}),\mu_{t}),N_{t})
into a Nijenhuis algebra over the local Artinian ring
{\bf k}[t]/(t^{2})
. Similar to the case of formal deformations, we say that two infinitesimal deformations
((A[t]/(t^{2}),\mu_{t}=\mu+t\mu_{1}),N_{t}=N+tN_{1})
and
((A[t]/(t^{2}),\mu^{\prime}_{t}=\mu+t\mu^{\prime}_{1}),N^{\prime}_{t}=N+tN^{\prime}_{1})
are
equivalent
if there exists a
{\bf k}[t]/(t^{2})
-linear map
\varphi_{t}:A[t]/(t^{2})\rightarrow A[t]/(t^{2})
of the form
\varphi_{t}=\mathrm{Id}_{A}+t\varphi_{1}
that defines an isomorphism of the above Nijenhuis algebras. Then we have the following result.

4.2 Theorem
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be a Nijenhuis algebra. Then there is a bijection

\displaystyle\bigg\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Set of all equivalence classes of infinitesimal }\\
\text{ deformations of }((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)\end{subarray}\bigg\}\quad\!\!\!\mathrel{\raise 0.94722pt\hbox{${\mathrel{\to}^{\Phi}_{\phantom{\Psi}}}$}\kern-11.63893pt\lower 0.94722pt\hbox{${\mathrel{\to}^{\phantom{\Phi}}_{\Psi}}$}}\quad\!\!\!\!H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N)).

Proof.

Let
\big((A[t]/(t^{2}),\mu_{t}=\mu+t\mu_{1}),N_{t}=N+tN_{1}\big)
be an infinitesimal deformation. Then one can easily check (similar to the formal deformation case) that
(\mu_{1},N_{1})
is a
2
-cocyle. Moreover, equivalent infinitesimal deformations give rise to cohomologous
2
-cocycles. Hence there is a well-defined map
\Phi
from the set of all equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations to the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N)).
On the other hand, we first observe that any
2
-cocycle
(\mu_{1},N_{1})
gives rise to an infinitesimal deformation
\big((A[t]/(t^{2}),\mu_{t}=\mu+t\mu_{1}),N_{t}=N+tN_{1}\big)
of the given Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
. Further, cohomologous
2
-cocycles correspond to equivalent infinitesimal deformations. This construction yields a map
\Psi
in the other direction. Finally, the maps
\Phi
and
\Psi
are inverses to each other. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Inducibility of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms

Given an abelian extension of a Nijenhuis algebra by a Nijenhuis bimodule, we study the inducibility of a pair of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms from an automorphism of the extension. To find an obstruction, we first define the Wells map in the context of Nijenhuis algebras. Our main result shows that a pair of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if the pair is ‘compatible’ and the image of this pair under the Wells map vanishes identically.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be a Nijenhuis algebra and
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
be a Nijenhuis bimodule over it. Suppose

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{((M,0),N_{M})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{((E,~\!\cdot_{E}~\!),N_{E})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

is a given abelian extension describing an element of
\mathcal{E}xt^{2}((A,N),(M,N_{M}))
, i.e. the induced
A
-bimodule structure on
M
coincides with the prescribed one. Let
\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
be the set of all Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(E,N_{E})
that satisfies
\varphi(M)\subset M
. Then
\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
is obviously a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(E,N_{E})
. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
. Then we naturally have
\varphi\big|_{M}\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})
. Next, for any section
s:A\rightarrow E
of the map
p
, we define a map
\overline{\varphi}:A\rightarrow A
by
\overline{\varphi}(a):=(p\varphi s)(a)
, for
a\in A.
It can be checked that the map
\overline{\varphi}
doesn’t depend on the choice of the section
s
. Further, the map
\overline{\varphi}
is a bijection on the set
A
. For any
a,b\in A
, we also have

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}(a\cdot b)=~

\displaystyle p\varphi(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)-\chi(a,b))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle p\varphi(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b))\quad(\because~\!\varphi(M)\subset M\text{ and }p\big|_{M}=0)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle p\varphi s(a)\cdot p\varphi s(b)=\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(b)

and additionally

\displaystyle(N\circ\overline{\varphi})(a)=Np\varphi s(a)=~

\displaystyle p\varphi N_{E}s(a)\quad(\because~\!Np=pN_{E}\text{ and }N_{E}\varphi=\varphi N_{E})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle p\varphi sN(a)\quad(\because~\!(N_{E}s-sN)(a)\in M,~\varphi(M)\subset M\text{ and }p\big|_{M}=0)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\overline{\varphi}\circ N)(a).

This shows that
\overline{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
is a Nijenhuis algebra automorphism. Hence we obtain a map

\displaystyle\tau:\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)~~~\text{ given by }~~~\tau(\varphi)=(\varphi\big|_{M},\overline{\varphi}),

for
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E}).
A pair of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
is said to be
inducible
if there exists a Nijenhuis algebra automorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
such that
\tau(\varphi)=(\beta,\alpha)
, i.e.
\varphi\big|_{M}=\beta
and
\overline{\varphi}=\alpha
. The inducibility problem then asks to find a necessary and sufficient condition under which a pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms is inducible.

5.1 Proposition
.

Let (
5
) be a given abelian extension. For a fixed section
s
, suppose the above abelian extension corresponds to the
2
-cocycle
(\chi,F)
. Then a pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if the following conditions hold:

(I)

\beta(a\triangleright u)=\alpha(a)\triangleright\beta(u)
and
\beta(u\triangleleft a)=\beta(u)\triangleleft\alpha(a)
, for
a\in A
and
u\in M
,

(II)

there exists a linear map
\lambda:A\rightarrow M
satisfying

\displaystyle\beta(\chi(a,b))-\chi(\alpha(a),\alpha(b))=~

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\triangleright\lambda(b)+\lambda(a)\triangleleft\alpha(b)-\lambda(a\cdot b),

(20)

\displaystyle\beta(F(a))-F(\alpha(a))=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\lambda(a))-\lambda(N(a)),\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

(21)

Proof.

Let
(\beta,\alpha)
be an inducible pair, i.e. there exists a Nijenhuis algebra automorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
such that
\varphi\big|_{M}=\beta
and
p\varphi s=\alpha
. For any
a\in A
and
u\in M
, we observe that

\displaystyle\beta(a\triangleright u)-\alpha(a)\triangleright\beta(u)=~

\displaystyle\beta(s(a)\cdot_{E}i(u))-s\alpha(a)\cdot_{E}i\beta(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi s(a)\cdot_{E}\varphi i(u)-s\alpha(a)\cdot_{E}i\beta(u)\quad(\because~\!\beta=\varphi|_{M})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a)\cdot_{E}i\beta(u)=0\quad(\because~\!(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a)\in\mathrm{ker~}p=\mathrm{im~}i\cong M).

In the same way, one can show that
\beta(u\triangleleft a)=\beta(u)\triangleleft\alpha(a)
. Hence the identities in (I) follow.

Next, we define a map
\lambda:A\rightarrow M
by
\lambda(a)=(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a)
, for
a\in A
. Then for any
a,b\in A
, we observe that

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\triangleright\lambda(b)+\lambda(a)\triangleleft\alpha(b)-\lambda(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=s\alpha(a)\cdot_{E}(\varphi s-s\alpha)(b)+(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a)\cdot_{E}s\alpha(b)-(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=\varphi s(a)\cdot_{E}(\varphi s-s\alpha)(b)+\varphi s(a)\cdot_{E}s\alpha(b)-s\alpha(a)\cdot_{E}s\alpha(b)-\varphi s(a\cdot b)+s(\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha(b))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad(\because~\!(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a)\in M\text{ and the multiplication of }M\text{ is trivial})

\displaystyle=\varphi s(a)\cdot_{E}\varphi s(b)-\varphi s(a\cdot b)-s\alpha(a)\cdot_{E}s\alpha(b)+s(\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha(b))\quad(\text{after cancellation and rearrangement})

\displaystyle=\beta(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)-s(a\cdot b))-\big(s\alpha(a)\cdot_{E}s\alpha(b)-s(\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha(b))\big)

\displaystyle=\beta(\chi(a,b))-\chi(\alpha(a),\alpha(b))

and

\displaystyle\beta(F(a))-F(\alpha(a))=~

\displaystyle\beta(N_{E}s(a)-sN(a))-(N_{E}s\alpha(a)-sN\alpha(a))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi N_{E}s(a)-\varphi sN(a)-N_{E}s\alpha(a)+sN\alpha(a)\quad(\because~\!\beta=\varphi|_{M})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{E}(\varphi s-s\alpha)(a)-(\varphi s-s\alpha)N(a)\quad(\because~\!\varphi N_{E}=N_{E}\varphi\text{ and }\alpha N=N\alpha)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\lambda(a))-\lambda(N(a))\quad(\because~\!N_{M}=N_{E}\big|_{M}).

Hence the one direction of the proof follows.

Conversely, let
(\beta,\alpha)
satisfy the conditions in (I) and (II). Since
s
is a section of the map
p
, any element
e\in E
can be uniquely written as
e=s(a)+u
, for some
a\in A
and
u\in M
. We now define a map
\varphi:E\rightarrow E
by

\displaystyle\varphi(e)=\varphi(s(a)+u)=s(\alpha(a))+(\beta(u)+\lambda(a)).

The map
\varphi
is clearly injective as
\varphi(e)=0
means that
s(\alpha(a))=0
and
\beta(u)+\lambda(a)=0
. Since
s,\alpha,\beta
are all injectives we get that
a=0
and
u=0
which in turn implies that
e=s(a)+u=0
. It is also surjective as any element
e=s(a)+u\in E
has a unique preimage
e^{\prime}=s(\alpha^{-1}(a))+(\beta^{-1}(u)-\beta^{-1}\lambda\alpha^{-1}(a))\in E
. This shows that
\varphi
is bijective. Next, we claim that
\varphi:E\rightarrow E
is a Nijenhuis algebra automorphism. For any two elements
e=s(a)+u
and
e^{\prime}=s(b)+v
from the vector space
E
, we observe that

\displaystyle\varphi(e)\cdot_{E}\varphi(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big(s(\alpha(a))+\beta(u)+\lambda(a)\big)\cdot_{E}\big(s(\alpha(b))+\beta(v)+\lambda(b)\big)

\displaystyle=s(\alpha(a))\cdot_{E}s(\alpha(b))+s(\alpha(a))\cdot_{E}\beta(v)+s(\alpha(a))\cdot_{E}\lambda(b)+\beta(u)\cdot_{E}s(\alpha(b))+\underbrace{\beta(u)\cdot_{E}\beta(v)}_{=0}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{\beta(u)\cdot_{E}\lambda(b)}_{=0}+\lambda(a)\cdot_{E}s(\alpha(b))+\underbrace{\lambda(a)\cdot_{E}\beta(v)}_{=0}+\underbrace{\lambda(a)\cdot_{E}\lambda(b)}_{=0}

\displaystyle=s(\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha(b))+\chi(\alpha(a),\alpha(b))+\alpha(a)\triangleright\beta(v)+\alpha(a)\triangleleft\lambda(b)+\beta(u)\triangleleft\alpha(b)+\lambda(a)\triangleleft\alpha(b)

\displaystyle=s(\alpha(a\cdot b))+\beta\big(\chi(a,b)+a\triangleright v+u\triangleleft b\big)+\lambda(a\cdot b)\quad(\text{by using (I) and }(\ref{eqn-8}))

\displaystyle=\varphi\big(s(a\cdot b)+\chi(a,b)+a\triangleright v+u\triangleleft b\big)\quad(\text{from the definition of }\varphi)

\displaystyle=\varphi\big(s(a\cdot b)+\chi(a,b)+s(a)\cdot_{E}v+u\cdot_{E}s(b)+u\cdot_{E}v\big)\quad(\because~\!u\cdot_{E}v=0)

\displaystyle=\varphi\big(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)+s(a)\cdot_{E}v+u\cdot_{E}s(b)+u\cdot_{E}v\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi\big((s(a)+u)\cdot_{E}(s(b)+v)\big)=\varphi(e\cdot_{E}e^{\prime}).

and

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ N_{E})(e)=~

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ N_{E})(s(a)+u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi\big(sN(a)+F(a)+N_{M}(u)\big)\quad(\because~\!N_{E}s-sN=F\text{ and }N_{M}=N_{E}\big|_{M})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle s(\alpha N(a))+\beta(F(a)+N_{M}(u))+\lambda(N(a))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{E}s(\alpha(a))+N_{M}\beta(u)+\big(\beta(F(a))-F(\alpha(a))+\lambda(N(a))\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad(\because~\!\alpha N=N\alpha,~\beta N_{M}=N_{M}\beta\text{ and }N_{E}s-sN=F)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{E}s(\alpha(a))+N_{M}\beta(u)+N_{M}(\lambda(a))\quad(\text{by }(\ref{eqn-9}))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{E}\big(s(\alpha(a))+\beta(u)+\lambda(a)\big)=(N_{E}\circ\varphi)(e).

This proves our claim that
\varphi
is a Nijenhuis algebra automorphism (i.e.
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(E,N_{E})
). Since
\varphi(M)\subset M
, we get that
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
. Finally, for any
a\in A
and
u\in M
,

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}(a)=~

\displaystyle p\varphi(s(a)+0)=p\big(s(\alpha(a))+\lambda(a)\big)=\alpha(a)\quad(\because~\!ps=\mathrm{Id}_{A}\text{ and }p\big|_{M}=0),

\displaystyle\varphi(u)=~

\displaystyle\varphi(s(0)+u)=\beta(u).

Hence
\tau(\varphi)=(\varphi\big|_{M},\overline{\varphi})=(\beta,\alpha)
which shows that the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is inducible.
∎

It is important to observe that the necessary and sufficient condition obtained in the above proposition depends on the section
s
. In the following, we aim to formulate a condition that doesn’t depend on the choice of any section. Let
s
be any section that induces the
2
-cocycle
(\chi,F)
corresponding to the given abelian extension. For any pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
, we define maps
\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},M)
and
F_{(\beta,\alpha)}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)
by

\displaystyle\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(a,b)=\beta\circ\chi(\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b))~~~~\text{ and }~~~~F_{(\beta,\alpha)}(a)=\beta\circ F(\alpha^{-1}(a)),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

With the above notations, we have the following.

5.2 Proposition
.

(i)

The pair
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})
is also a
2
-cocycle.

(ii)

Moreover, the cohomology class of the difference
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,F)
is independent of the choice of
s
.

Proof.

(i) Since
(\chi,F)
is a
2
-cocycle, the identities (
11
) and (
12
) are hold. In both of these identities, if we replace
a,b,c
by
\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c)
respectively, we obtain the corresponding identities for the pair
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})
. This shows that
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})
is a
2
-cocycle.

(ii) Let
s^{\prime}:A\rightarrow M
be any other section of the map
p
. If
(\chi^{\prime},F^{\prime})
is the
2
-cocycle corresponding to the abelian extension induced by the section
s^{\prime}
, then the
2
-cocycles
(\chi,F)
and
(\chi^{\prime},F^{\prime})
are cohomologous. In fact, we have
(\chi,F)-(\chi^{\prime},F^{\prime})=\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(g)
, where
g:=s-s^{\prime}
. Then it follows from the definitions of
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(\chi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)},F^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)})
that
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)},F^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)})=\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(\beta g\alpha^{-1})
. Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\big\{(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,F)\big\}-\big\{(\chi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)},F^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi^{\prime},F^{\prime})\big\}=\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(\beta g\alpha^{-1}-g)\text{ is a }2\text{-coboundary}.

Hence the result follows.
∎

With the help of the above notations, we will now define a set-map (which we refer as the
Wells map
in the context of Nijenhuis algebras)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}:\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))~~~\text{ by }~~~\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha)):=\big[(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,F)\big],

for
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
. Then it follows from the previous proposition that the Wells map doesn’t depend on the choice of the section
s
. In terms of the Wells map, Proposition
5.1
can be rephrased as follows.

5.3 Theorem
.

Let (
5
) be an abelian extension of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
by a Nijenhuis bimodule
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
. Then a pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
is inducible if and only if the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is ‘compatible’ in the sense that

\displaystyle\beta(a\triangleright u)=\alpha(a)\triangleright\beta(u)~~~\text{ and }~~~\beta(u\triangleleft a)=\beta(u)\triangleleft\alpha(a),\text{ for all }a\in A,u\in M

(22)

and the cohomology class
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
(\beta,\alpha)
be an inducible pair. Then by Proposition
5.1
, we get that the identities in (
22
) are hold. Also, there exists a linear map
\lambda:A\rightarrow M
satisfying (
20
) and (
21
). In these two identities, if we replace
a,b
respectively by
\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(a,b)-\chi(a,b)=~

\displaystyle a\triangleright\lambda\alpha^{-1}(b)+\lambda\alpha^{-1}(a)\triangleleft b-\lambda\alpha^{-1}(a\cdot b),

\displaystyle F_{(\beta,\alpha)}(a)-F(a)=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\lambda\alpha^{-1}(a))-\lambda\alpha^{-1}(N(a)).

This shows that
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,F)=\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(\lambda\alpha^{-1})
is a
2
-coboundary. Hence the cohomology class
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=[(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,F)]
is trivial.

Conversely, suppose the identities in (
22
) are hold and
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
. Let
s
be any section and
(\chi,F)
be the
2
-cocycle corresponding to the given abelian extension induced by the section
s
. Since
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
, the
2
-cocycles
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(\chi,F)
are cohomologous, say
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},F_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,F)=\delta_{\mathrm{NAlg}}(g)
, for some
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)
. Then it can be checked that the map
\lambda:=g\alpha:A\rightarrow M
satisfy the conditions (
20
) and (
21
). Hence by Proposition
5.1
, the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is inducible.
∎

Given an abelian extension of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
by the Nijenhuis bimodule
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
, we define

\displaystyle C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}=\{(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)~\!|~\begin{subarray}{c}\beta(a~\!\triangleright~\!u)~\!=~\!\alpha(a)~\!\triangleright~\!\beta(u),\\
\beta(u~\!\triangleleft~\!a)~\!=~\!\beta(u)~\!\triangleleft~\!\alpha(a),\end{subarray}\text{ for all }a\in A,u\in M\}.

The space
C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
is called the space of
compatible pairs
of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms. Then
C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
and it follows from the first part of the proof of Proposition
5.1
that the image of the map
\tau
lies inside
C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
, i.e.
\tau\big(\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})\big)\subset C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
. Moreover, by Theorem
5.3
, we get that a pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M,N_{M})\times\mathrm{Aut}(A,N)
of Nijenhuis algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if
(\beta,\alpha)\in C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
and
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
.

Like classical cases, here we show that the Wells map defined above fits into a short exact sequence.

5.4 Theorem
.

Let (
5
) be an abelian extension of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
by the Nijenhuis bimodule
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
. Then there is an exact sequence (called the
Wells exact sequence
)

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})\xrightarrow{\tau}C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M})),

(23)

where
\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(E,N_{E})~\!|~\!\tau(\varphi)=(\mathrm{Id}_{M},\mathrm{Id}_{A})\}.

Proof.

The sequence (
23
) is exact in the first place as the inclusion map
\iota:\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
is an injection. Next, to show that the sequence is exact in the second place, we first take an element
\varphi\in\mathrm{ker}(\tau)
. That is,
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
with the property that
\varphi\big|_{M}=\mathrm{Id}_{M}
and
\overline{\varphi}=\mathrm{Id}_{A}
. This shows that
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
. On the other hand,
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
implies that
\varphi\in\mathrm{ker}(\tau)
. Therefore, we get that
\mathrm{ker}(\tau)=\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})=\mathrm{im}(\iota)
.

Finally, we take an element
(\beta,\alpha)\in C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
with
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
. Then it follows from Theorem
5.3
that the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is inducible. Thus, there exists a Nijenhuis algebra automorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})
such that
\tau(\varphi)=(\beta,\alpha)
. This shows that
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{im}(\tau).
Conversely, if the pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{im}(\tau)
then by definition the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is inducible. Hence again by Theorem
5.3
, we get that
(\beta,\alpha)\in C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}
and
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
, i.e.
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{ker}(\mathcal{W})
. Hence
\mathrm{ker}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{im}(\tau)
which shows that the sequence is also exact in the third place. This concludes the proof.
∎

In the following, we show that the group
\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
that appears in the Wells exact sequence is isomorphic to the space
Z^{1}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))
of
1
-cocycles of the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
with coefficients in the Nijenhuis bimodule
((M,\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{M})
. More precisely, we have the following result.

5.5 Proposition
.

With the hypothesis of the above theorem, there is a group isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})~\!\cong~\!Z^{1}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}).

As a consequence, the group
\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
is abelian.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
. Then for any
a\in A
, we observe that
p((\varphi s-s)(a))=(p\varphi s-ps)(a)=0
which implies that
(\varphi s-s)(a)\in\mathrm{ker}(p)=\mathrm{im}(i)\cong M
. We now define a map

\widetilde{\varphi}:A\rightarrow M
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}(a):=(\varphi s-s)(a),\text{ for }a\in A.

For any
a,b\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}(a\cdot b)=~

\displaystyle\varphi s(a\cdot b)-s(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi\big(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)-\chi(a,b)\big)-\big(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)-\chi(a,b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi s(a)\cdot_{E}\varphi s(b)-s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)\quad(\because~\!\varphi\big|_{M}=\mathrm{Id}_{M})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\varphi}+s)(a)\cdot_{E}(\widetilde{\varphi}+s)(b)-s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)\quad(\because~\!\varphi s=\widetilde{\varphi}+s)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle s(a)\cdot_{E}\widetilde{\varphi}(b)+\widetilde{\varphi}(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)=a\triangleright\widetilde{\varphi}(b)+\widetilde{\varphi}(a)\triangleleft b

and

\displaystyle(N_{M}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}-\widetilde{\varphi}\circ N)(a)=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\widetilde{\varphi}(a))-(\varphi sN(a)-sN(a))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\widetilde{\varphi}(a))-\varphi(N_{E}s-F)(a)+(N_{E}s-F)(a)\quad(\because~\!F=N_{E}s-sN)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\widetilde{\varphi}(a))-N_{E}(\varphi s(a))+N_{E}s(a)\quad(\because~\!\varphi\big|_{M}=\mathrm{Id}_{M}\text{ and }\varphi N_{E}=N_{E}\varphi)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N_{M}(\widetilde{\varphi}(a))-N_{E}(\varphi s-s)(a)=N_{M}(\widetilde{\varphi}(a))-N_{M}(\widetilde{\varphi}(a))=0.

This shows that
\widetilde{\varphi}\in Z^{1}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))
. Finally, for any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi\psi}=\varphi\psi s-s=\varphi s-s+\varphi(\psi s-s)=\widetilde{\varphi}+\varphi\widetilde{\psi}=\widetilde{\varphi}+\widetilde{\psi}\quad(\because~\mathrm{im}(\widetilde{\psi})\subset M\text{ and }\varphi\big|_{M}=\mathrm{Id}_{M})

which implies that the map
\widetilde{(~)}:\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})\rightarrow Z^{1}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))
is a group homomorphism.

Next, we claim that the map
\widetilde{(~)}
is bijective. First, we take an element
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
with
\widetilde{\varphi}=0
. Then for any
e=s(a)+u\in E
, we observe that

\displaystyle\varphi(e)=\varphi(s(a)+u)=(\varphi s-s)(a)+s(a)+\varphi(u)=s(a)+u=e\quad(\because~\!\widetilde{\varphi}=0\text{ and }\varphi\big|_{M}=\mathrm{Id}_{M})

which implies that
\varphi=\mathrm{Id}_{E}
. This shows that the map
\widetilde{(~)}
is injective. Next, to show that it is surjective, we take an arbitrary element
d\in Z^{1}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))
. Hence
d:A\rightarrow M
is a linear map satisfying the usual derivation property and
N_{M}\circ d=d\circ N
. We now define a map
\underline{d}:E\rightarrow E
by

\displaystyle\underline{d}(e)=\underline{d}(s(a)+u):=s(a)+(u+d(a)),\text{ for }e=s(a)+u\in E.

Then it is easy to see that the map
\underline{d}
is bijective and satisfies additionally
\underline{d}(e\cdot_{E}e^{\prime})=e\cdot_{E}\underline{d}(e^{\prime})+\underline{d}(e)\cdot_{E}e^{\prime}
and
N_{E}\circ\underline{d}=\underline{d}\circ N_{E}
, for all
e,e^{\prime}\in E
. Hence
\underline{d}\in\mathrm{Aut}(E,N_{E})
. Moreover, for all
a\in A
,
u\in M
, we have

\displaystyle p\underline{d}s(a)=p(s(a)+d(a))=ps(a)=a~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\underline{d}(u)=\underline{d}(s(0)+u)=u.

Hence
\tau(\underline{d})=(\underline{d}\big|_{M},p\underline{d}s)=(\mathrm{Id}_{M},\mathrm{Id}_{A})
which in turn implies that
\underline{d}\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M,A}(E,N_{E})
. Finally,

\displaystyle\widetilde{(\underline{d})}(a)=(\underline{d}s-s)(a)=s(a)+d(a)-s(a)=d(a),\text{ for all }a\in A.

This shows that
\widetilde{(\underline{d})}=d
proving that the map
\widetilde{(~)}
is surjective. Hence the proof follows.
∎

5.6 Corollary
.

Given the above proposition, the Wells exact sequence can be alternatively read as an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow Z^{1}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M}))\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E,N_{E})\xrightarrow{\tau}C_{\triangleright,\triangleleft}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{NAlg}}((A,N);(M,N_{M})).

6.5 Definition
.

A
crossed module of Nijenhuis algebras
is a tuple
\big(((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N),((A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!),N_{1}),\varphi,\triangleright,\triangleleft\big)
consisting of Nijenhuis algebras
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
and
((A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!),N_{1})
together with a homomorphism
\varphi:A_{1}\rightarrow A
of Nijenhuis algebras and two bilinear maps
\triangleright:A\times A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}
and
\triangleleft:A_{1}\times A\rightarrow A_{1}
such that

(i)

((A_{1},\triangleright,\triangleleft),N_{1})
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over the Nijenhuis algebra
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
,

(ii)

the following compatibility conditions also hold:

\displaystyle a\triangleright(u\cdot_{1}v)=(a\triangleright u)\cdot_{1}v,\qquad(u\triangleleft a)\cdot_{1}v=u\cdot_{1}(a\triangleright v),\qquad(u\cdot_{1}v)\triangleleft a=u\cdot_{1}(v\triangleleft a),

(38)

\displaystyle\qquad\varphi(a\triangleright u)=a\cdot\varphi(u),\qquad\varphi(u\triangleleft a)=\varphi(u)\cdot a,\qquad\varphi(u)\triangleright v=u\cdot_{1}v=u\triangleleft\varphi(v),

(39)

for all
a\in A
and
u,v\in A_{1}
.

6.6 Example
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N)
be any Nijenhuis algebra. Then
\big(((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N),((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N),\varphi=\mathrm{Id}_{A},\triangleright_{\mathrm{ad}},\triangleleft_{\mathrm{ad}}\big)
is a crossed module of Nijenhuis algebras.

6.7 Example
.

Let
((A,~\!\cdot~\!),(A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}),\varphi,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
be a crossed module of associative algebras.

(i)

Then the tuple
\big(((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N=\mathrm{Id}_{A}),((A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}),N_{1}=\mathrm{Id}_{A_{1}}),\varphi,\triangleright,\triangleleft\big)
is obviously a crossed module of Nijenhuis algebras.

(ii)

For any
u\in A_{1}
, consider the left multiplications
l_{\varphi(u)}:A\rightarrow A
,
a\mapsto\varphi(u)\cdot a
and
l_{1,u}:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}
,
v\mapsto u\cdot_{1}v
. We have seen that they are Nijenhuis operators on the respective algebras. Then the tuple
(((A,~\!\cdot~\!),l_{\varphi(u)}),((A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}),l_{1,u}),\varphi,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
is a crossed module of Nijenhuis algebras.

6.8 Example
.

Let
\big(((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N),((A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!),N_{1}),\varphi,\triangleright,\triangleleft\big)
be any crossed module of Nijenhuis algebras. Then for any
k\geq 0
, the tuple
\big(((A,~\!\cdot~\!),N^{k}),((A_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!),N_{1}^{k}),\varphi,\triangleright,\triangleleft\big)
is also a crossed module of Nijenhuis algebras.

An

-algebra whose underlying graded vector space is concentrated in arity

With the above notations, a degree

[r]\in C_{k+1}

a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m+n=k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i(n+1)}~\!(\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n})([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=0,

(41)

where the partial compositions
\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n}
are the graded version of the operations defined in (
6
).

An
NS_{\infty}
-algebra whose underlying graded vector space is concentrated in arity
0
is nothing but an NS-algebra. On the other hand, an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
for which
\eta_{n}=0
, for
n\neq 2
, is nothing but a graded NS-algebra (i.e. an NS-algebra in the category of graded vector spaces). In
[
8
]
the present author has considered the explicit description of a
Dend_{\infty}
-algebra (strongly homotopy dendriform algebra). It turns out that an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
for which

\displaystyle\eta_{n}([n+1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=0,\text{ for all }n\geq 2\text{ and }a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}

is nothing but a
Dend_{\infty}
-algebra. Thus, an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra generalizes
Dend_{\infty}
-algebras.

6.13 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
be an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra. Then
(\mathcal{A},\{\overline{\eta}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle\overline{\eta}_{1}(a):=\eta_{1}([1];a)\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\eta}_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}):=\eta_{n}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),\text{ for }n\geq 2.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{A},\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
is an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra, the identities in (
41
) are hold. Hence for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and homogeneous elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m+n=k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i(n+1)}\sum_{r=1}^{k+1}~\!(\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n})([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=0.

By expanding the partial compositions (which are the graded version of the operations (
6
)), we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{m+n=k+1}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i(n+1)+n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}

\displaystyle\qquad~\!\eta_{m}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[m+1]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\eta_{n}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{k})=0.

This shows the desired result.
∎

6.14 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator on it. Then the pair
(A,\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
is an
NS_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle\eta_{n}([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{n})\big)&\text{ for }r=1,\ldots,n,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathcal{N}\big(\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{i},\ldots,a_{j},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{n})\big)\big)&\text{ for }r=n+1.\\
\qquad+\cdots+(-1)^{n-1}\mathcal{N}^{n-1}\big(\mu_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\big)\end{cases}

As a consequence,
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n,\mathcal{N}}\}_{n\geq 1})
is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra (called the deformed
A_{\infty}
-algebra), where
\mu_{1,\mathcal{N}}:=\mu_{1}
and for
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mu_{n,\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}):=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{n})\big)

\displaystyle~~~-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathcal{N}\big(\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{i},\ldots,a_{j},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{n})\big)\big)+\cdots+(-1)^{n-1}\mathcal{N}^{n-1}\big(\mu_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\big).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{N}:A\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator, it follows from (
40
) that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{n})\big)=\mathcal{N}\big(\eta_{n}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\big),\text{ for all }n.

On the other hand,
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{m+n=k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i(n+1)+n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\mu_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\mu_{n}(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{k})=0,

(42)

for all
k\geq 1
. In the identity (
42
), replace the tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
of homogeneous elements of
\mathcal{A}
by the tuple
(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{k}))
, for some
1\leq r\leq k
. For any fixed
m,n
and
i
, if
r\leq i-1
, then we get that

\displaystyle(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\mu_{m}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i-1}),\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{i}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i+n-1})\big),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\mu_{m}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i-1}),\mathcal{N}\big(\eta_{n}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1})\big),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\eta_{m}\big([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\eta_{n}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,a_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=(\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n})([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}).

On the other hand, if
i\leq r\leq i+n-1
then

\displaystyle(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\mu_{m}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i-1}),\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{i}),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i+n-1})\big),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\mu_{m}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i-1}),\eta_{n}([r-i+1];a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\eta_{m}\big([i];a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\eta_{n}([r-i+1];a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,a_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=(\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n})([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}).

Similarly, if
i+n\leq r\leq k
, we get that

\displaystyle(-1)^{n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\mu_{m}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i-1}),\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{i}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i+n-1})\big),\ldots,a_{r},\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=(\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n})([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}).

This implies that the
NS_{\infty}
-algebra identities (
41
) are hold for
[r]=[1],\ldots,[k]
. Finally, a straightforward but tedious computation (using the identity (
40
)) shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i(n+1)+n(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}~\!\bigg\{

\displaystyle\eta_{m}\big([i];a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\eta_{n}([n+1];a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,a_{k}\big)

\displaystyle+~

\displaystyle\eta_{m}\big([m+1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\eta_{n}({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n+1]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,a_{k}\big)\bigg\}=0

which imply that
\sum_{m+n=k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i(n+1)}~\!(\eta_{m}\circ_{i}\eta_{n})([k+1];a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=0
.
This verifies the identity (
41
) for
[r]=[k+1]
. Hence the proof of the first part follows.

The second part of the statement follows as a consequence of Proposition
6.13
.
∎

It is important to remark that a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator on an
A_{\infty}
-algebra is a generalization of Nijenhuis operators on
n
-ary algebras
[
28
]
. Motivated by a result from this reference, here we obtain the following.

6.15 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator.

(i)

Then for any
k\geq 0
, the map
\mathcal{N}^{k}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is also a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator on the
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
. Hence one obtains the deformed
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n,\mathcal{N}^{k}}\}_{n\geq 1})
.

(ii)

Further, for any
k,l\geq 0
, the map
\mathcal{N}^{l}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator on the deformed
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\{\mu_{n,\mathcal{N}^{k}}\}_{n\geq 1})
.

Proof.

(i) We will prove this result by using the mathematical induction on
k
. First, observe that the statement is true for
k=0,1
. Assume that the statement is true for some natural number
k
. Choose any homogeneous elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
\mathcal{N}^{k}
is a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator (by assumption), we observe that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}^{k}\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{n})\big)

(43)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathcal{N}^{2k}\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{i}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{j}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\cdots+(-1)^{n-1}\mathcal{N}^{nk}\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}(a_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{N}(a_{n})\big).

By repeatedly using the fact that
\mathcal{N}
is a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator and then cancellation of terms, the whole expression in (
43
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{k+1}\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{i},\ldots,\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{n})\big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathcal{N}^{2k+2}\mu_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{i},\ldots,a_{j},\ldots,\mathcal{N}^{k+1}(a_{n})\big)+\cdots+(-1)^{n-1}\mathcal{N}^{nk+n}\mu_{n}\big(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\big).

This shows that
\mathcal{N}^{k+1}
is a strict homotopy Nijenhuis operator (i.e. the statement is valid for
k+1
). Hence the result follows by the mathematical induction.

(ii) This can be proved by using the mathematical induction on
l
.
∎

Introduction to moduli spaces and Dirac geometry
Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. Let G G be a Lie group, with an invariant metric on its Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . Given a surface Σ \Sigma with boundary, and a collection of base points 𝒱 ⊆ Σ \mathcal{V}\subseteq\Sigma meeting every boundary component, the moduli space (representation variety) ℳ G ​ ( Σ , 𝒱 ) \mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}) carries a distinguished ‘quasi-symplectic’ 2-form. We shall explain the finite-dimensional construction of this 2-form and discuss its basic properties, using quasi-Hamiltonian techniques and Dirac geometry. This article is an extended version of lectures given at the summer school ’Poisson 2024’ at the Accademia Pontaniana in Napoli, July 2024.



1.
Introduction

This article is an extended version of lectures given at the summer school ’Poisson 2024’ at the Accademia Pontaniana in Napoli, July 2024.
The moduli spaces in its title are the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
of flat
G
-bundles over compact oriented surfaces
\Sigma
, with framings at a finite collection
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\Sigma
of base points, meeting every component of the boundary.
Equivalently, they are described as spaces of homomorphims from the fundamental groupoid into the Lie group
G
. If the Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
carries an invariant metric, then the moduli space acquires a distinguished 2-form
\omega
. For the case without boundary (and
\mathcal{V}=\emptyset
), this is the Atiyah-Bott symplectic form. When
\Sigma
has non-empty boundary, then this 2-form is neither closed or nondegenerate, but for
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\partial\Sigma
it is
quasi-symplectic
in the sense of quasi-Hamiltonian geometry.

My original plan for the lectures had been to divide the material into two parts. The first part would present a direct ‘low-tech’ construction of the 2-form via cutting and gluing of the surface. Most properties of the 2-form are direct consequences from the construction,
with the exception of ‘minimal degeneracy’ of the 2-form. This would then serve as a motivation for the second part, leading to an introduction to Dirac geometry as the proper framework for quasi-Hamiltonian spaces, with moduli spaces serving as the main examples.
In reality, this plan was too ambitious, and the lectures never actually reached the second part. Accordingly, Sections
2
–
5
of the present article are written in the style of lecture notes, replete with exercises. In later sections the presentation gradually morphs into a survey style, discussing material that was only hinted at in the lectures.

In more detail, the material is organized as follows. Section
2
begins with a rapid overview of moduli spaces of flat bundles over surfaces, and associated representation varieties. Section
3
defines the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
for any finite collection
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\Sigma
(meeting every component) as the space of homomorphisms from the fundamental groupoid

\Pi(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
into
G
. The space carries an action of
G^{\mathcal{V}}
, and taking holonomies along boundary edges
\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}
defines an equivariant map

\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to G^{\mathcal{E}}
. Starting in Section

4
, we assume that
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
carries an invariant metric. This determines a
Cartan 3-form

\eta\in\Omega^{3}(G)
and a related 2-form
\beta\in\Omega^{2}(G\times G)
. The main result of this section is the existence of a distinguished 2-form

\omega
on the space
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
, whose differential is the sum of pullbacks of
\eta
under the boundary holonomies, and whose contractions with the generators of the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-action are described explicitly. The 2-form admits a direct description for the case that
\Sigma
is a polyhedral region (an
n
-gon); the general case is reduced to this case via gluing diagrams. Using cutting and re-gluing, we prove that the 2-form does not depend on the choice of gluing diagram. Section
5
continues the discussion of cutting and gluing operations, examining in particular the gluing of two boundary circles of a surface. Among other things, this leads to a description of the 2-form for surfaces without boundary. Section
6
is devoted to Hamiltonian dynamics on the moduli spaces:
Every
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant function
f
on the moduli space determines a Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f}
(despite the fact that

\omega
is degenerate). For special choices of
f
, this leads to the so-called
Goldman flows
which we compute explicitly.
Section
7
shows that the moduli space for a cylinder is naturally a ‘quasi-symplectic groupoid’, where the groupoid multiplication is defined via gluing of cylinders. Finally, Section
8
gives an introduction to Dirac geometry, and supplies proofs of minimal degeneracy properties of the 2-form via a cross-section theorem for Dirac structures.

2.
Representation varieties

Let
G
be a Lie group, and
\Sigma
a compact, connected surface, without boundary.

The
representation variety
or
character variety
is the space

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0}),G)/G

of homomorphisms from the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0})
into the group
G
, up to conjugacy.
The definition of fundamental group involves the choice of a base point
x_{0}\in\Sigma
, but the quotient space no longer depends on this choice. The space
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
may also be regarded as the
moduli space of flat bundles
,

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\{\mbox{flat principal $G$-bundles}\ \ P\to\Sigma\}\big/\sim

where two such bundles are identified if they are related by
a
G
-equivariant diffeomorphism intertwining the flat connections and inducing the identity on the base. The correspondence is induced by the map taking the homomorphism
\kappa\colon\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0})\to G
to the associated principal bundle

P=\widetilde{\Sigma}\times_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0})}G.

In the opposite direction, given a flat
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
, one obtains
\kappa
as the parallel transport (holonomy)
of the connection, after choice of trivialization
P|_{x_{0}}\cong G
. Different choices of trivialization at
x_{0}
change
\kappa
by
G
-conjugacy.

Remark 2.1
.

If the group
G
is connected and simply connected, then every principal
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
is isomorphic to the trivial one, and
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
is the moduli space of flat connections on the trivial bundle. Equivalently, it is
the moduli space of Maurer-Cartan elements

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\Big\{A\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g})|\ {\mbox{d}}A+{\frac{1}{2}}[A,A]=0=0\Big\}/C^{\infty}(\Sigma,G).

where the group
C^{\infty}(\Sigma,G)
acts
by
g\cdot A=\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(A)-g^{*}\theta^{R}
. Here
\theta^{R}\in\Omega^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g})
is the right-invariant Maurer-Cartan form.
For general groups
G
, the space
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
may also include non-trivial flat bundles.

One obtains a concrete description of the moduli space by choosing generators of the fundamental group.
For a surface of genus
\mathsf{g}
, there are the
standard generators

\mathsf{a}_{1},\mathsf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{g},\mathsf{b}_{g}

with the relation
\mathsf{a}_{1}\mathsf{b}_{1}\mathsf{a}_{1}^{-1}\mathsf{b}_{1}^{-1}\cdots\mathsf{a}_{g}\mathsf{b}_{g}\mathsf{a}_{g}^{-1}\mathsf{b}_{g}^{-1}=1
.
It corresponds to a gluing diagram, depicted below for genus
\mathsf{g}=2
.

In terms of these generators, the moduli space is
identified with

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\Big\{(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{\mathsf{g}},b_{\mathsf{g}})\in G^{2\mathsf{g}}|\ \prod_{i=1}^{\mathsf{g}}a_{i}b_{i}a_{i}^{-1}b_{i}^{-1}=e\Big\}\big/G

where we interpret
a_{i},b_{i}\in G
as the holonomies along
\mathsf{a}_{i},\mathsf{b}_{i}\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0})
. One may also consider other gluing diagrams presenting the surface, corresponding to ‘non-standard’ generators. For example, gluing
opposite
sides of an 8-gon gives a
surface of genus
\mathsf{g}=2
; this gluing pattern corresponds to generators
\mathsf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{c}_{4}
with a relation

\mathsf{c}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{c}_{4}\mathsf{c}_{1}^{-1}\cdots\mathsf{c}_{4}^{-1}=1
.

The space
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
appears in many areas of mathematics and physics – far too many to properly review them here.
Let us just point out two major directions:

•

For
G=\operatorname{SU}(n)
, and a given choice of complex structure on the surface,
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
it is the moduli space of stable holomorphic vector bundles of rank
n
and degree
0
over
\Sigma
: this is the Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem
[
46
]
. In one direction, if
\kappa\colon\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{SU}(n)

is a homomorphism one obtains a holomorphic vector bundle

(\widetilde{\Sigma}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})/\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0})\to\Sigma

where
\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x_{0})
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
via
\kappa
. The theorem states that the holomorphic vector bundles obtained in this way are exactly the stable bundles of degree
0
; in particular, every
stable bundle arises in this way. The Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem has been vastly generalized to the
non-abelian Hodge correspondence
for the
Higgs moduli spaces
of Hitchin.

•

If
G
is connected but not simply connected, the isomorphism classes of principal
G
-bundles over
\Sigma
are labeled by
\pi_{1}(G,e)=\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the case of
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, and suppose
\Sigma
has negative
Euler characteristic
\chi(\Sigma)=2-2\mathsf{g}<0
. By the Milnor-Wood inequality
[
44
,
52
]
, the principal
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-bundle labeled by the integer
k
admits a flat connection if and only if
|k|\leq|\chi(\Sigma)|
.
Goldman
[
28
]
and Hitchin
[
31
]
proved that the component of
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
labeled by
k
is connected, and for
|k|=|\chi(\Sigma)|
coincides with the
Teichmüller space
of hyperbolic structures up to isotopy. (A sign change of
k
amounts to a change of orientation of the surface.) Hitchin’s discovery
[
32
]
of similar contractible components
(now called
Hitchin components
) for other split semisimple groups
G
such as
\operatorname{PSL}(n,\mathbb{R})
,
has led to the subject of
higher Teichmüller theory

[
14
,
25
]
. In particular, it was shown by Choi and Goldman

[
18
]
that the Hitchin component for
G=\operatorname{PSL}(3,\mathbb{R})
is the moduli space of convex
\mathbb{R}P(2)
-structures on
\Sigma
.

It is a classical fact that a nondegenerate
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}
-invariant symmetric bilinear form (‘metric’)
on the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)

determines a
symplectic structure
on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
. This symplectic structure was described by Atiyah-Bott
[
9
]
using a gauge theoretic approach (using infinite-dimensional symplectic reduction);
in special cases it was observed earlier (e.g., by Narasimhan
[
45
]
for the moduli space of stable bundles, and by Ahlfors
[
1
]
for Teichmüller space). In the first part of this article
we will explain a finite-dimensional construction, which
was developed in the 1990s starting with work of Goldman
[
26
]
, Karshon
[
34
]
, Weinstein
[
51
]
,
and Guruprasad-Huebschmann-Jeffrey-Weinstein
[
30
]
.
The approach below is based on the quasi-Hamiltonian techniques from Alekseev-Malkin-Meinrenken
[
4
]
,
with additional ideas due to Li-Bland and Ševera
[
37
,
48
]
. In this context, we mainly deal with moduli spaces associated to surfaces
with non-empty boundary
. The case without boundary is included later, via ‘reduction’. As applications, we explain a
computation of Goldman flows along these lines, as well as a construction of quasi-symplectic groupoids integrating the Cartan-Dirac structure (and its generalizations).

3.
The space
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

In order to develop tools of ‘cutting’ and ‘gluing’, it is convenient to generalize the setting to surfaces with possibly non-empty boundary and with more than one base point. This necessitates working with fundamental groupoids rather than just the fundamental group.

3.1.
Fundamental groupoids

Let
X
be a topological space with finitely many path components, and
Y\subseteq X
a finite subset of base points (‘vertices’), meeting all path components.
We denote by

\Pi(X,Y)\rightrightarrows Y

the
fundamental groupoid
. The set
\Pi(X,Y)
of arrows of this groupoid are homotopy classes of
maps of pairs
\gamma\colon(I,\partial I)\to(X,Y)
relative to
\partial I
, where
I=[0,1]
is the unit interval.
The source and target maps are

\mathsf{s}([\gamma])=\gamma(0),\ \ \mathsf{t}([\gamma])=\gamma(1),

and the groupoid multiplication is given by concatenation of paths
[\gamma^{\prime}]\circ[\gamma]=[\gamma^{\prime}*\gamma]
,
using the convention

(\gamma_{1}*\gamma_{2})(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{2}(2t)&0\leq t\leq{\frac{1}{2}},\\
\gamma_{1}(2t-1)&{\frac{1}{2}}\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

The groupoid inverse is given by pre-composition of paths with
t\mapsto 1-t
. The pairs
(X,Y)
form a category, with morphisms

f\colon(X,Y)\to(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
the continuous maps
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
f(Y)\subseteq Y^{\prime}
; the fundamental groupoid construction is a
covariant functor from this category into the category of groupoids. Note that
\Pi(f)\colon\Pi(X,Y)\to\Pi(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
depends only on the homotopy class of

f
relative to
Y
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a Lie group
G
, we define the
moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)
as the space of groupoid homomorphisms

\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(X,Y),G).

An element
\kappa\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)
assigns to every path
\gamma
with end points in
Y
a
holonomy

\kappa_{\gamma}\in G
, depending only on
[\gamma]
, in such a way that

\kappa_{\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2}}=\kappa_{\gamma_{1}}\kappa_{\gamma_{2}}.

The group
G^{Y}
of maps
h\colon Y\to G,\ y\mapsto h_{y}

acts on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)
by

(1)

(h\cdot\kappa)_{\gamma}=h_{\gamma(1)}\,\kappa_{\gamma}h_{\gamma(0)}^{-1}.

The construction of
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)
is covariant with respect to
G
and contravariant with respect to
(X,Y)
;
in particular, any morphism of pairs
f\colon(X,Y)\to(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
induces a map of moduli spaces,

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{G}(f)\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y),

depending only on the homotopy class of
f
relative to
Y
.
The map
\mathcal{M}_{G}(f)
is equivariant with respect to the pullback map
G^{Y^{\prime}}\to G^{Y}
.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the case that
f
is the identity map of
X^{\prime}=X
, but with
Y
a subset of
Y^{\prime}
. Then the map
(
2
) is just the quotient map for the action of
G^{Y^{\prime}-Y}\subseteq G^{Y^{\prime}}
:

(3)

\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)=\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y^{\prime})/G^{Y^{\prime}-Y}.

To see this choose, for each element of
Y^{\prime}-Y
, a path in
X
to an element of
Y
. Then every homomorphism
\kappa^{\prime}\colon\Pi(X,Y^{\prime})\to G
is uniquely determined by its restriction
\kappa\colon\Pi(X,Y)\to G
together with its values on the chosen paths. That is, the choice of paths identifies

\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)\times G^{Y^{\prime}-Y},

with the
G^{Y^{\prime}-Y}
-action given by right multiplication on the second factor.

This example indicates how to define
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)
if
Y
does not meet all components of
X
:
enlarge to a set
Y^{\prime}
that does meet all components, and use (
3
). In particular,
we define

\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)=\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,\emptyset).

Example 3.3
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a directed graph (quiver) with vertices
\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}
and oriented edges
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}
.
Then
\Pi(\Gamma,\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma})
is the
free groupoid
over the set of edges, and

(4)

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Gamma,\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma})=G^{\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}}.

Here the same notation is used for the abstract graph
\Gamma
and its geometric realization, taking a quotient of

\sqcup_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}}I
(one copy for each edge) by the relation determined by the source and target maps
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}\colon\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}
.

As a consequence, whenever
X
retracts onto a (geometric) graph
\Gamma
with
\mathcal{V}=Y
as its vertices, then

\Pi(X,Y)
is free, and so
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X,Y)
is a manifold diffeomorphic to an
\#\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}
-fold
product of
G
.

Surfaces with boundary

Let
\Sigma
be a compact oriented surface with boundary.
Let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\Sigma
a set of vertices.

.

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\Sigma,V),G)

with the action of
G^{\mathcal{V}}
. By (

), the choice of
\Gamma
, and of an orientation on

,
gives a

(5)

The resulting manifold structure on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
may be identified with the moduli space of flat principal

) over the base points,
P|_{\mathcal{V}}\cong\mathcal{V}\times G
.

G^{\mathcal{V}}
acts by change of framing. (Exercise

.)

f\colon(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})

\mathcal{M}_{G}(f)\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

3.3.
Boundary holonomies, mapping class group

From now on, we will usually make the following assumptions:

(A1)

Every component of
\Sigma
has non-empty boundary.

(A2)

The set
\mathcal{V}
of vertices meets every component of
\partial\Sigma
.

(A3)

The set
\mathcal{V}
is contained in
\partial\Sigma
.

Sometimes we will omit (A3), also allowing for a non-empty set
\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{int}}=\mathcal{V}\cap\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)

of ‘interior vertices’.
By assumption (A2), the vertex set divides the boundary into oriented boundary segments.
Their homotopy classes a subset

\mathcal{E}\subseteq\Pi(\partial\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cap\partial\Sigma),

called the
boundary edges
.
Hence,
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\partial\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cap\partial\Sigma)\cong G^{\mathcal{E}}
, and the inclusion

(\partial\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cap\partial\Sigma)\to(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
determines a
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-equivariant map

\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to G^{\mathcal{E}},

called the
boundary holonomies
.
In the theory to be developed below, the map
\Phi
will play the role of a momentum map (analogous to the momentum maps in symplectic geometry).

We define the
mapping class group

(6)

\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

to be the group of isotopy classes of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms
f\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
, preserving the
boundary
\partial\Sigma
and the set
\mathcal{V}
of base points. Note that
f
is not required to preserve the individual boundary components.
By functoriality, the map of pairs
f\colon(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

induces an
action

\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\circlearrowright\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}),\ [f]\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{G}(f^{-1})

on the moduli space. It combines with the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-action into an action of the semidirect product

(7)

G^{\mathcal{V}}\rtimes\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}),

and the map
\Phi
is equivariant for this action.

3.4.
Examples

We shall denote by
\Sigma_{g}^{r}
the connected, oriented surface of genus
g
with
r
boundary components.

Example 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}^{1}
be the disk, and suppose

consists of

points on the boundary.

Label the vertices clockwise by

\mathsf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{v}_{n}

\mathsf{e}_{i}

\mathsf{v}_{i+1}
to
\mathsf{v}_{i}
(with

\mathsf{e}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{e}_{n}=1
in
\Pi(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

The boundary holonomy map

realizes the moduli space as the submanifold

with

acting as

(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}).(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=(h_{1}a_{1}h_{2}^{-1},h_{2}a_{2}h_{3}^{-1},\ldots,h_{n}a_{n}h_{1}^{-1}).

The mapping class group
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
acts by cyclic permutation on

.

Example 3.7
.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}^{2}
be the cylinder (or annulus or 2-holed sphere), with one vertex on each boundary component.

Label the vertices by
\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}
. The fundamental groupoid

has a set of free generators

\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}
, where
\mathsf{a}
is the class of the oriented boundary loop at
\mathsf{v}_{1}
, and
\mathsf{b}
the class of a path from
\mathsf{v}_{1}
to
\mathsf{v}_{2}
.
Thus,

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\cong G^{2}

where
(a,b)\in G^{2}
are the holonomies along
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}
, respectively.
The action of
G^{\mathcal{V}}
reads as

(h_{1},h_{2}).(a,b)=(h_{1}ah_{1}^{-1},\ h_{2}ba_{1}^{-1}).

The holonomy around the second boundary component (based at
v_{2}
) is
ba^{-1}b^{-1}
, and so

\Phi(a,b)=\big(a,ba^{-1}b^{-1}\big).

For the mapping class group one finds

\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Here the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-generator interchanges the two boundary components; its action on the moduli space is

(a,b)\mapsto(ba^{-1}b^{-1},b^{-1}).

The generator of
\mathbb{Z}
acts by a ‘Dehn twist’ corresponding to a full turn of one of the
boundary components while leaving the other one fixed. The action on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\mathcal{V},\Sigma)
is

(a,b)\mapsto(a,ba)

Note that this operation preserves
\Phi
.

Example 3.8
.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}^{1}
be the 1-holed torus, and
\mathcal{V}
a single base point on its boundary.

As generators of the fundamental group(oid), we take lifts
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}\in\Pi(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
of standard generators of the fundamental group of the 2-torus

\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\cong G^{2},

As shown in
[

, the mapping class group is

the braid group on three strands: thus

has generators

with a single relation

The generators

S\colon(a,b)\mapsto(a,ba),\ \ T\colon(a,b)\mapsto(ab^{-1},b).

More generally, for

The group

4.
Two-forms on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

Throughout this section, we consider pairs
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
of a surface with a collection of vertices, satisfying (A1), (A2). We will show that a given metric on the Lie algebra of
G
determines a 2-form on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
, with nice properties. If (A3) is satisfied as well, this 2-form is ‘minimally degenerate’.

4.1.
The 2-form

We shall assume from now that the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)

comes equipped with an
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}
-invariant nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\cdot
, referred to as a

metric
. For example, if
G
is semi-simple we may take the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
. The metric determines
a
Cartan 3-form

(9)

\eta=\frac{1}{12}\theta^{L}\cdot[\theta^{L},\theta^{L}]\in\Omega^{3}(G)

(where
\theta^{L},\theta^{R}\in\Omega^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g})
are the Maurer-Cartan forms). Using that
\theta^{R}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\theta^{L}

we see that this 3-form is invariant under both left and right translations, and consequently (Exercise
4.1
) is closed:

{\mbox{d}}\eta=0.

For
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}=\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g})
, let
\xi_{\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})}

denote the generating vector field for the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
.
The holonomy along the edge
\mathsf{e}
transforms according to (cf. (
1
))

(10)

g\mapsto h_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}\ g\ h_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}^{-1}.

This shows that for each component
\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
of the map
\Phi
,

\xi_{\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})}\sim_{\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}}\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}^{L}-\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}^{R}

for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
, where the superscripts indicate left/right invariant vector fields,
and
where the tilde symbol signifies related vector fields.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
4
,
36
]

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies conditions (A1), (A2).
There is a canonically defined
2-form

\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})),

invariant under the action of

G^{\mathcal{V}}
and of the mapping class group
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
,
with the following properties:

(a)

{\mbox{d}}\omega=-\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\eta.

(b)

\iota(\xi_{\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})})\omega=-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}(\theta^{R}\cdot\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}+\theta^{L}\cdot\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})})
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
.

If condition (A3) is satisfied, then this 2-form also has the
minimal degeneracy
property

(c)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=\{0\}.

By
canonical
, we mean that the 2-form does not depend on additional choices; it is naturally associated with the
pair
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
. The proof of Theorem
4.1
will take up the remainder of this section. We start out by
providing some basic properties of the 3-form
\eta
(Subsection
4.2
). We then give an explicit description
of the 2-form
\omega
for the special case of an
n
-gon (Subsection
4.3
). General surfaces are
reduced to the case of
n
-gons via gluing patterns (Subsection
4.4
).

Proposition 4.2
.

If conditions (A1),(A2),(A3) are satisfied, then
\operatorname{ker}(\omega)
has the explicit description

(11)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)|_{\kappa}=\{\xi_{\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})}|_{\kappa}|\ \forall\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}\colon\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}+\operatorname{Ad}_{\Phi(\kappa)_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}}\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}=0\}.

Furthermore, there is a canonical isomorphism, for all
\kappa\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
,

(12)

\operatorname{ran}(T\Phi|_{\kappa})\cong\operatorname{ann}((\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}})_{\kappa})

where
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}})_{\kappa}
is the stabilizer algebra for the
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
-action at
\kappa
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\supseteq
in (
11
) follows from (
b
); the opposite inclusion is
best proved using Dirac geometry; see Proposition
8.2
below. The isomorphism
(
12
) is equivalent to the claim that
\operatorname{ann}(\operatorname{ran}(T\Phi|_{\kappa}))\cong(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}})_{\kappa}
. In fact, we will see
that this space of covectors is spanned by all

\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}g_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}(\theta^{R}\cdot\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}+\theta^{L}\cdot\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})})
with
\xi\in(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}})_{\kappa}
. Once again, the proof becomes more transparent using Dirac geometry,
where it follows from a general result, Proposition
8.3
below.
∎

Equation (
12
) shows, in particular, that the rank of
\Phi
at any given point
\kappa
equals
the codimension of the stabilizer of the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-action at that point. As a special case, we recover the following result of Goldman

[
26
, Section 3.7]
:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

be a Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric. For all
\mathsf{g}\geq 1
, the rank of the products-of-commutator-map

\Phi\colon G^{2\mathsf{g}}\to G,\ (a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{\mathsf{g}},b_{\mathsf{g}})\to\prod_{i=1}^{\mathsf{g}}a_{i}b_{i}a_{i}^{-1}b_{i}^{-1}

at a point
(a_{1},b_{1}\ldots,a_{\mathsf{g}},b_{\mathsf{g}})
equals the codimension of its stabilizer of
under conjugation.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{\mathsf{g}}^{1}
be the surfaces of genus
\mathsf{g}
with one boundary component, and let
\mathcal{V}=\{\mathsf{v}\}

consist of a single point on
\partial\Sigma
. Then

is just the momentum map for
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

the result follows from (

Note that the group unit is never a regular value of the product-of-commutators map; for example,
at

.

If

is non-simply connected,

can have several connected components, and it may happen that

has maximal rank on some of those components. For example, if

the map

4.2.
Properties of the Cartan 3-form

We shall consider the Cartan 3-form
\eta
alongside the following 2-form on
G\times G
,

(13)

\beta={\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\theta^{L}\cdot\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\theta^{R}.

Here
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}
are the two projections.

Proposition 4.5
.

(a)

The pullback of
\eta
under group inversion
\operatorname{Inv}\colon G\to G
satisfies

\operatorname{Inv}^{*}\eta=-\eta.

(b)

The pullback of
\eta
under group multiplication
\operatorname{Mult}\colon G\times G\to G
satisfies

\operatorname{Mult}^{*}\eta=\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\eta+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\eta-{\mbox{d}}\beta.

Proof.

This is a straightforward verification, using

\operatorname{Inv}^{*}\theta^{L}=-\theta^{R},\ \ \ \ \operatorname{Mult}^{*}\theta^{L}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{2}}^{-1}\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\theta^{L}+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\theta^{L}

(where we write elements of
G\times G
as
(g_{1},g_{2})
) and the Maurer-Cartan structure equation

{\mbox{d}}\theta^{L}+{\frac{1}{2}}[\theta^{L},\theta^{L}]=0
.
∎

The 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(G)
and 2-form
\beta\in\Omega^{2}(G\times G)
appear in the
context of moduli spaces of
G
-bundles in Chern-Simons theory and the Polyakov-Wiegman formula

[
47
]
; their significance for constructing the symplectic structure on moduli spaces of flat
G
-bundles
was recognized by Weinstein in
[
51
]
. These forms may be interpreted in terms of the Bott-Shulman double complex

[
13
,
33
]
for the simplicial realization of the classifying space
BG
. One has

\Omega^{i,j}(BG)=\bigoplus_{i+j=k}\Omega^{i}(G^{j})

with total differential
{\mbox{d}}+(-1)^{i}\delta
, where
d
is the de Rham differential (raising index
i
) and
\delta
is the
simplicial differential (raising index
j
), given on elements of bidegree
(i,j)
by
\delta=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i}^{*}

with

\partial_{i}(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{j})=\begin{cases}(g_{2},\ldots,g_{j})&i=0,\\
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{j})&0<i<j,\\
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{j-1})&i=j.\end{cases}

The sum
\eta+\beta
is a cocycle of total degree
4
for the total complex.
This amounts to the equations

{\mbox{d}}\eta=0,\ \ {\mbox{d}}\beta=\delta\eta,\ \ \delta\beta=0.

The cohomology class of
(\eta,\beta)
for the total differential is the 1st Pontrjagin class of the classifying bundle
EG\to BG
.

Remark 4.6
.

In shifted geometry parlance,
\eta+\beta
is a 2-shifted 2-form on the stack
G\rightrightarrows\operatorname{pt}
. For discussion, see e.g. Alvarez

[
7
]
.

Using Proposition
4.5
, one may check the following result, which will be useful in our construction of
the 2-form on the moduli space.

Proposition 4.7

(Ševera
[
48
]
)
.

Given any manifold
M
, the space

C^{\infty}(M,G)\times\Omega^{2}(M)

has a group structure with product

(\Phi_{1},\omega_{1})\bullet(\Phi_{2},\omega_{2})=\left(\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2},\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})^{*}\beta\right)

and inverse
(\Phi,\omega)^{-1}=(\Phi^{-1},-\omega)
.
The map

C^{\infty}(M,G)\times\Omega^{2}(M)\to\Omega^{3}(M),\ (\Phi,\omega)\mapsto{\mbox{d}}\omega-\Phi^{*}\eta

is a group homomorphism for this group structure.

We leave the proof as an exercise (see
Exercise
4.3
). We remark that the associativity of the product involves the property
\delta\beta=0
.

For
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, let
\xi^{L}
denote the left-invariant vector field generated by
\xi
, and
\xi^{R}
the
right-invariant vector field. The generating vector fields for the
G\times G
-action on
G
,
given by
(g^{\prime},g).a=g^{\prime}ag^{-1}
, are

(14)

(\xi^{\prime},\xi)_{G}=\xi^{L}-(\xi^{\prime})^{R}.

As already mentioned, the 3-form
\eta
is bi-invariant, hence its Lie derivative with respect to the vector field
(
14
) vanishes. Since
\eta
is closed, it follows that the contraction with this vector field is closed.
In fact, it is exact:

Proposition 4.8
.

The contractions of the 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(G)
with the generating vector fields of the
G\times G
-action are given by

\iota((\xi^{\prime},\xi)_{G})\eta=-{\mbox{d}}\Big({\frac{1}{2}}(\theta^{R}\cdot\xi^{\prime}+\theta^{L}\cdot\xi)\Big)

Proof.

From
\iota(\xi^{L})\theta^{L}=\xi=\iota(\xi^{R})\theta^{R}
one obtains
\iota(\xi^{L})\eta=\frac{1}{4}\xi\cdot[\theta^{L},\theta^{L}]
, and similarly
for
(\xi^{\prime})^{R}
. Now use the Maurer-Cartan equations.
∎

Proposition 4.9

There is a unique 2-form
\omega

(e,\omega)=(g_{1},0)\bullet\cdots\bullet(g_{n},0)

For existence, define
\omega
by Equation (

(g_{n},0)^{-1}=(g_{n}^{-1},0)

(g_{n},0)
, we obtain the alternative expression

(e,\omega)=(g_{n},0)\bullet(g_{1},0)\bullet\cdots\bullet(g_{n-1},0).

This shows that
\omega

\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma_{0}^{1},\mathcal{V})
. We next verify the properties (

), (

) and (

) from Theorem

(cf. Proposition

) to both sides of (

-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{*}\eta
. For (

Let us consider the action of the second factor:

h\cdot(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=(g_{1}h^{-1},hg_{2},g_{3},\ldots,g_{n}).

Its generating vector field is
(\xi^{L},-\xi^{R},0,\ldots,0)
.
By formula (

\omega=-(g_{1},g_{2})^{*}\beta+\omega_{1}

where

and the

\displaystyle\iota(\xi^{L},-\xi^{R},0,\ldots,0)\big((g_{1},g_{2})^{*}\beta\big)

A convenient reference for presentations of surfaces via gluing patterns is Thurston
[

Let

(17)

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{(k)}\subseteq\widehat{\Sigma}^{(k)}

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}=\sqcup_{k}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
be the set of boundary edges. A

with set of vertices

Geometrically, the boundary segments corresponding to paired edges are glued by orientation-reversing diffeomorphisms. Note that the
quotient map induces a bijection between the set
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
of free edges with the set
\mathcal{E}
of boundary edges of
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
.

Gluing patterns are conveniently described by ‘words’ (one word for each

), describing the relation arising from the
quotient map.

.

The 2-form

4.1

The interior vertices do not contribute to the 2-form, in the sense that the form on

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{int}})

Proof.

As shown in Section
4.3

\widehat{\omega}
satisfies Property (a) of Theorem

4.1

{\mbox{d}}\omega=\iota^{*}{\mbox{d}}\widehat{\omega}=-\iota^{*}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\eta.

For any pair of edges

\iota^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathsf{e}_{i}}=\iota^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathsf{e}_{j}}^{-1}

\operatorname{Inv}^{*}\eta=-\eta,

these terms will cancel.
It follows that the sum over edges in
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{E}

This gives Property (a) for
\omega

We next show independence of the choice of gluing pattern for
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})

as a quotient of a disjoint union (

of polygons. Given paired edges

from

with

Cutting

We have

\displaystyle(e,\widehat{\omega}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=(g_{1},0)\bullet\cdots\bullet(g_{n},0)

\displaystyle=(g_{i},0)\bullet\cdots\bullet(g_{n},0)\bullet(g_{1},0)\bullet\cdots\bullet(g_{i-1},0)

5.
More cutting and gluing

In the previous section, we constructed the 2-form on moduli spaces by cutting the surface into a disjoint union of polygons. It is also interesting to consider any finite number of cuts, without cutting all the way to polygons.

5.1.
Cutting along paths between vertices

Let us first describe single cuts. Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies (A1),(A2), and that
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
is obtained by cutting along an embedded path
\gamma
, with end points in
\mathcal{V}
and the rest of the path in the interior of
\Sigma
. The cut creates two new boundary edges:
\#\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\#\mathcal{E}+2
.

There are various cases, depending on whether the end points of
\gamma
are interior vertices or boundary vertices, and whether or not
\gamma
is a loop:

(a)

\gamma
is a loop based at a boundary vertex

(b)

\gamma
is a loop based at an interior vertex

(c)

\gamma
is a path between distinct boundary vertices

(d)

\gamma
is a path between a boundary vertex and an interior vertex

(e)

\gamma
is a path between distinct interior vertices

In cases (a) and (c) the cut created two new vertices, while
\chi(\Sigma)
increases by
1
.
See the picture below for a cut of type (a).

(The region inside the loop may contain handles and boundary components.) In cases (b) and (d) the cut creates one new vertex, while the Euler characteristic is preserved. See the picture below for a cut of type (b).

Finally, a cut of type (e) creates no new vertices, and the Euler characteristic decreases by
1
.
Observe that in all of the cases,

\#\mathcal{V}^{\prime}-\chi(\Sigma^{\prime})=\#\mathcal{V}-\chi(\Sigma)+1,

corresponding to one new generator of the fundamental groupoid created by the cut. Accordingly,
the quotient map
defines an embedding

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})

as a submanifold of codimension equal to
\dim G
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma^{1}_{1}
be the one-holed torus, with
\mathcal{V}
consisting of one vertex on its boundary.
Consider a cut (type (a)) along an embedded loop
\gamma
based at the vertex, winding once around the handle:

The cut surface is connected, with two boundary components, and has Euler characteristic
\chi(\Sigma^{\prime})=\chi(\Sigma)+1=0
. It is
thus an annulus
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma_{0}^{2}
.

The set

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
given by one vertex on one of the boundaries and two vertices on the other.

Example 5.2
.

Consider a similar example of
\Sigma=\Sigma^{1}_{1}
, with
\#\mathcal{V}=2
where one vertex is in the interior and one vertex on the boundary. Cut along a loop based at the interior vertex and winding around the handle. The result of this type (b) cut is a 3-holed sphere (pair of pants) with one vertex on each boundary component.

Suppose more generally that
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
is obtained from

(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
by iterated cuts of the type considered above. The quotient map
induces a map of fundamental groupoids, and hence a map of moduli spaces.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies (A1),(A2), and that
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
is obtained by
N
of cuts of the type considered above. Then the induced map

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})

is an embedding as a submanifold of codimension
N\dim G
,
equivariant for the inclusion
G^{\mathcal{V}}\hookrightarrow G^{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}
. The 2-form on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
is the pullback of that on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
under the embedding, and the boundary holonomy map

is given by restriction of

\Phi^{\prime}
followed by the projection

Cutting further, we arrive at a gluing diagram
(\widehat{\Sigma},\widehat{\mathcal{V}})
for

by omitting the edge identifications for the quotient map

.
The various claims are evident from this description. In particular,
the forms

on the two moduli spaces are obtained by pullback of

via embeddings

5.2.
Gluing equals reduction

Sometimes, it is desirable to cut surfaces along loops not containing any vertices.
To do so, simply add an interior vertex
\mathsf{v}
on the loop, and use the same method as before.

Suppose that
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies the assumptions (A1), (A2), and let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the surface obtained by cutting
\Sigma
along an embedded circle
C\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
with
C\cap\mathcal{V}=\emptyset
.

Conversely, letting
C_{\pm}\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
be resulting new boundary components, the surface
\Sigma
is recovered
as a quotient
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}/\sim

by gluing along
C_{\pm}
.

We may regard
\mathcal{V}
as a subset of
\Sigma^{\prime}
by taking the pre-image under the quotient map. The pair
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V})
does not satisfy (A2) since
\mathcal{V}
does not meet all boundary components. To fix this issue, choose a point
\mathsf{v}\in C
,
with pre-images
\mathsf{v}_{\pm}\in C_{\pm}
and let

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\mathcal{V}\sqcup\{\mathsf{v}_{+},\mathsf{v}_{-}\}.

Then
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
satisfies (A2). Note also that if
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies (A3) then so does
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
.

The set
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
of boundary edges of
\Sigma^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}\sqcup\{\mathsf{e}_{+},\mathsf{e}_{-}\}
where
\mathsf{e}_{\pm}
are defined by the
newly created boundaries
C_{\pm}
.
Let
\Phi_{\pm}=\Phi_{\mathsf{e}_{\pm}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\to G
denote the holonomies along these new boundary edges.
The two maps

(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\longrightarrow(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathsf{v}\})\longleftarrow(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})

induce an inclusion and projection

(20)

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longleftarrow}}\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathsf{v}\})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}),

and the 2-forms are related by

\iota^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\pi^{*}\omega.

We may think of this construction as a (quasi-)symplectic reduction:

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})/\kern-3.01385pt/G,

analogous to the ‘gluing equals reduction’ result for Hamiltonian loop group spaces

[
42
]
.
To make it more explicit, note that
the image of the inclusion
\iota
is the submanifold

Z=\{x|\ \Phi_{+}(x)=\Phi_{-}(x)^{-1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}).

The diagonal
G\subseteq G\times G
-action (where the two
G
’s correspond to
the two vertices
\mathsf{v}_{\pm}
) is identified with the
G
-action
on
\mathcal{M}_{G}((\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathsf{v}\})
corresponding to
\mathsf{v}
. As explained in Example
3.2
(see also Proposition
4.14
) this

-action is free and proper, and
\pi
is the corresponding quotient map.

.

A bit more generally, one can choose several points
\mathsf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{v}_{l}\in C

\mathsf{v}_{i,\pm}

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})/\kern-3.01385pt/G^{l}

where the right hand side is defined as the quotient of
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\sqcup\{\mathsf{v}_{i}|\ i=1,\ldots,l\})

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\big(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\times\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma_{0}^{2},\{\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}\})\big)/\kern-3.01385pt/G

That is, the moduli space of the cylinder serves as the identity under reduction. As we shall explain in Section
7
below, this property turns the moduli space of a cylinder into a
quasi-symplectic groupoid
.

5.3.
The symplectic structure for surfaces without boundary

As another example for the ‘gluing reduction principle’, consider the case that
the loop
C\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
is contractible.

Cutting along such a circle gives a disjoint union

\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\sqcup\Sigma_{0}^{1}

where
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
\Sigma
by removing the open disk bounded by
C
. Conversely, we may think of

\Sigma
as being obtained from
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
by “capping off” a boundary component. Since

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma_{0}^{1},\{\mathsf{v}\})
is just a point, we obtain

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime})/\kern-3.01385pt/G=\Phi_{C}^{-1}(e)/G

where
\Phi_{C}\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime})\to G
is the holonomy around
C
.
Note that the pair
(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime})
satisfies the three assumptions (A1),(A2),(A3) if and only if
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
does.
Consequently, in this case the 2-forms on the respective moduli spaces satisfy the minimal degeneracy property
(
c
).

This suggests a construction of the symplectic structure on the moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\emptyset)

for a compact, connected surface
\Sigma

without
boundary: Choose a loop
C\subseteq\Sigma

bounding a disk, as well as a base point
\mathsf{v}\in C
, and let
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
be the surface with boundary
C
obtained by removing the interior of the disk. Thus,
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\cong\Sigma_{\mathsf{g}}^{1}
where
\mathsf{g}
is the genus of the surface.
Let
\omega^{\prime\prime}
be the 2-form on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\{\mathsf{v}\})
, and let

\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\{\mathsf{v}\})\to G
be the holonomy around the boundary. (Using standard generators
of the fundamental group, this is the product-of-commutators map

\Phi\colon G^{2\mathsf{g}}\to G
.)
Then

Z=\Phi^{-1}(e)=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\{\mathsf{v}\})=\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\mathsf{v}),G),

and so

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=Z/G.

At this stage, we are faced with the problem that
e
is
not
a regular value of
\Phi
.
By Corollary
4.3
,
\Phi
has maximal rank exactly at those points
\kappa
for which the stabilizer under the
G
-action is discrete. The set of elements in
Z
having this property is a submanifold

\iota\colon Z_{\operatorname{reg}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\{\mathsf{v}\}).

The property (
a
) from Theorem
4.1
shows that the pullback
\iota^{*}\omega^{\prime\prime}
is
closed. By (
11
), the 2-form
\omega^{\prime\prime}
on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\{\mathsf{v}\})
is non-degenerate at all points
of
Z
, and by the momentum map property (
b
) the null foliation of

\iota^{*}\omega^{\prime\prime}
consists exactly of the orbit directions. Hence, if the
G
-action on
Z_{\operatorname{reg}}
is proper
(which is automatic if
G
is compact) then

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)_{\operatorname{reg}}=Z_{\operatorname{reg}}/G

has at worst orbifold singularities, and
\iota^{*}\omega^{\prime\prime}
descends to a symplectic 2-form
\omega
on this space.

Remark 5.8
.

If
G
is compact, one gets better control over the singularities.
In fact,
\Phi^{-1}(e)/G
can be realized as a
singular symplectic quotient in this case, and
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)
has the structure of a stratified symplectic space in the sense of Sjamaar-Lerman
[
49
]
.

5.4.
Lagrangian boundary conditions

In the previous section, we used reduction to arrive at 2-forms on moduli spaces that are actually symplectic, rather than just
quasi-symplectic. A different method, producing many important examples, was introduced by Ševera in
[
48
]
.

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies assumptions (A1),(A2),(A3). Suppose furthermore that we are given a collection
\underline{H}=\{H_{\mathsf{e}}\}

of closed Lagrangian Lie subgroups

H_{\mathsf{e}}\subseteq G,

one for each edge. (A subgroup
H\subseteq G
is called Lagrangian if its Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is Lagrangian, that is,
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{h}
.)

Proposition 5.9

(Ševera
[
48
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}=0
whenever
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e}^{\prime})=\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})
.
Then

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V};\underline{H})=\Phi^{-1}(\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}H_{\mathsf{e}})

is a smooth submanifold, and the pullback of the 2-form
\omega
to this submanifold is symplectic.

Proof.

We shall use the following fact: If
\phi_{1}\colon K_{1}\to K
and
\phi_{2}\colon K_{2}\to K
are two morphisms of Lie groups
such that
(T_{e}\phi_{1})(\mathfrak{k}_{1})+(T_{e}\phi_{2})(\mathfrak{k}_{2})=\mathfrak{k}
, then the maps
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are transverse, and the product map

\operatorname{Mult}_{K}\circ(\phi_{1}\times\phi_{2})\colon K_{1}\times K_{2}\to K

is a submersion.

The submanifold

(21)

Q=\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}H_{\mathsf{e}}\subseteq G^{\mathcal{E}}.

is a product
Q=\prod_{C}Q_{C}
over the set of boundary components
C\subseteq\partial\Sigma
, where

Q_{C}=\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{C}}H_{\mathsf{e}}\subseteq G^{\mathcal{E}}_{C}
is the product over the set
\mathcal{E}_{C}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
of edges
that are contained in
C
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{C}=\mathcal{V}\cap C
. The quotient map for the
G^{\mathcal{V}_{C}}
-action on

G^{\mathcal{E}_{C}}
may be identified with the product map
G^{\mathcal{E}_{C}}\to G
(defined after choice of an initial vertex in
\mathcal{V}_{C}
).
By the fact above, this quotient map remains a submersion when restricted to
Q_{C}
. It follows that
Q_{C}
is transverse to the
G^{\mathcal{V}_{C}}
-orbits, and hence
Q
is transverse to the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-orbits. Since
\Phi
is
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-equivariant, it follows that

Q
is transverse to the map
\Phi
, and hence
\Phi^{-1}(Q)
is a submanifold.

Let
\iota\colon\Phi^{-1}(Q)\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
be the inclusion map.
Since the subgroups
H_{\mathsf{e}}\subseteq G
are Lagrangian, the pullback of
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(G)
to these subgroups vanishes.
Hence, applying
\iota^{*}
to the identity
{\mbox{d}}\omega=-\sum_{\mathsf{e}}\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\eta
, we obtain

{\mbox{d}}\iota^{*}\omega=0.

The fact that
\iota^{*}\omega
is symplectic may be proved using the cross-section theorem
from Dirac geometry. We will give this argument in Section
8.8
.
∎

The argument that (
21
) is transverse to
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-orbits, and hence is transverse to
\Phi
,
works in the same way provided that every boundary component contains at least one vertex
\mathsf{v}
such that

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{g}
for
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e}^{\prime})=\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})
.

Remark 5.10
.

The assumption that the Lagrangian Lie subalgebras
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
correspond to
closed
subgroups is quite restrictive.
The proposition extends to the more general setting of just having integration to
immersed subgroups
, by defining

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V};\underline{H})
as a fiber product of
\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}H_{\mathsf{e}}
with
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
over

.

Example 5.11
.

6.
Poisson structures and Goldman flows

For a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, any function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
defines a
Hamiltonian vector field

X_{f}
by the equation

(22)

\iota(X_{f})\omega=-{\mbox{d}}f.

This vector field satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{X_{F}}\omega=0
; hence its flow preserves the symplectic structure.
The 2-form on our moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
is usually degenerate, hence vector fields satisfying
(
22
) may not exist, and are not unique in case they do. As we shall explain, these issues can be resolved
provided that
f
is
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant. For certain choices of
f
, the resulting Hamiltonian flows are the
Goldman flows
.

6.1.
Hamiltonian vector fields

Throughout, we shall make the assumptions (A1),(A2),(A3): All components of
\Sigma
have non-empty boundary, and the set

\mathcal{V}
is contained in the boundary and meets all components of the boundary. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
carries a
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant 2-form
\omega
and a
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-equivariant map (boundary holonomies)

\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to G^{\mathcal{E}},

satisfying the properties listed in Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}))
is
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant, there is a unique
Hamiltoian vector field

X_{f}
on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfying

\iota(X_{f})\omega=-{\mbox{d}}f

and such that the flow of
X_{f}
fixes the boundary holonomies. (That is,
X_{f}
is
\Phi
-related to
0
.)
These vector fields satisfy

\mathcal{L}(X_{f})\omega=0.

On the open subset where
\Phi
has maximal rank, the span of the Hamiltonian vector fields for invariant functions is exactly

\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)
.

This is a mild generalization of
[
4
, Proposition 4.6]
.
The proof is best done within the framework of Dirac geometry; see Proposition
8.5
below.
Using Hamiltonian vector fields, we obtain a Poisson bracket on the space
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}))^{G^{\mathcal{V}}}

of invariant functions, by the usual formula

\{f,g\}=\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}g=\omega(X_{f},X_{g}).

In this way, the moduli space for a surface with boundary

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})/G^{\mathcal{V}}

becomes canonically a Poisson manifold, possibly with singularities (since the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-action need not be free and proper).

Suppose
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
is obtained from
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
by cutting along an embedded circle
C\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
,
as in Section
5.2
. Thus

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})/\kern-3.01385pt/G\cong\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\sqcup\{\mathsf{v}\})/G.

There is a natural map

C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}))^{G^{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}}\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathsf{v}\}))^{G^{\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathsf{v}\}}}\cong C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}))^{G^{\mathcal{V}}}.

Proposition 6.2
.

The map on functions just described preserves Poisson brackets.

Proof.

Write
Z=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathsf{v}\})
, and let

\iota\colon Z\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
be the inclusion, and

\pi\colon Z\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
the projection. Let
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
be
G^{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}
-invariant function on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
, and
f,g
the resulting functions on

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
, thus
\pi^{*}f=\iota^{*}f^{\prime},\ \pi^{*}g=\iota^{*}g^{\prime}
.

Since the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f^{\prime}}
is
\Phi^{\prime}
-related to
0
, it
is tangent to the level sets of
\Phi^{\prime}
, and in particular is tangent to
Z
. By equivariance,
X_{f^{\prime}}|_{Z}
descends to a vector field on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
. This vector field is
\Phi
-related to zero, and its contraction with
\omega
equals
-{\mbox{d}}f
. It hence follows that
X_{f^{\prime}}|_{Z}\sim_{\pi}X_{f}
. Using this fact, it follows that

\iota^{*}\{f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\}=\iota^{*}X_{f^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})=\pi^{*}X_{f}(g)=\pi^{*}\{f,g\}.\qed

6.2.
Goldman flows

Interesting examples of invariant functions on the moduli space may be constructed from conjugation invariant functions

\varphi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
and loops
\alpha\colon S^{1}\to\Sigma
. To define these functions

(23)

\varphi_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to\mathbb{R},\ \

suppose first that
\alpha(0)=\alpha(1)=\mathsf{v}\in\mathcal{V}
. Letting
\mathsf{a}\in\Pi(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
be the (non-free) class
of
\alpha
in the fundamental groupoid, and
\operatorname{ev}_{\mathsf{a}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to G
the evaluation map
(holonomy along
\mathsf{a}
), we put

(24)

\varphi_{\alpha}=\varphi\circ\operatorname{ev}_{\mathsf{a}}.

It is clear that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant.

Lemma 6.3
.

The function
\varphi_{\alpha}
depends only on the
free
homotopy class of
\alpha
.

Proof.

Replacing
\mathsf{a}
with
\mathsf{a}^{\prime}=\mathsf{b}\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b}^{-1}
for some

\mathsf{b}\in\Pi(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
with
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{b})=\mathsf{v},\ \mathsf{t}(\mathsf{b})=\mathsf{v}^{\prime}
, the holonomy changes by conjugation,
hence
\varphi\circ\operatorname{ev}_{\mathsf{a}}=\varphi\circ\operatorname{ev}_{\mathsf{a}^{\prime}}
. ∎

Hence, the functions (
23
) are defined for arbitrary loops
\alpha
, not necessarily based at some point of
\mathcal{V}
.
By
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-equivariance, they descend to functions on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})/G^{\mathcal{V}}
.

In particular, the functions
\varphi_{\alpha}
are also defined for surfaces without boundary. For this case, the Poisson brackets
\{\varphi_{\alpha},\psi_{\beta}\}
of functions of this type
were calculated in the work of Goldman
[
27
]
, leading to the
Goldman bracket
on free homotopy classes of loops. See also
Massuyeau-Turaev
[
40
]
for the case with boundary.

We shall compute the Hamiltonian vector field of (
23
) in simple cases. For every conjugation invariant function
\varphi
,
let
\dot{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g})
be defined by

(25)

{\mbox{d}}\varphi=\theta^{L}\cdot\dot{\varphi}

Notice that
\operatorname{Ad}_{h}\dot{\varphi}(g)=\dot{\varphi}(hgh^{-1})
, hence we also have
{\mbox{d}}\varphi=\theta^{R}\cdot\dot{\varphi}
.
Using this notation, we have
{\mbox{d}}\varphi_{\alpha}=\operatorname{ev}_{\mathsf{a}}^{*}(\theta^{L}\cdot\dot{\varphi})
.

Example 6.4

(Moduli space of cylinder)
.

Consider the example
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=(\Sigma_{0}^{2},\{\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}\})
,
from Section

.
Using the notation given there, take
\alpha
to be the oriented boundary loop based at
\mathsf{v}_{1}
; thus
\operatorname{ev}_{\mathsf{a}}=\Phi_{1}

We claim that the Hamiltonian vector field

for

is given by

\iota(X_{f})a^{*}\theta^{L}=0,\ \ \iota(X_{f})c^{*}\theta^{L}=\dot{\varphi}(a),

with corresponding flow

(a,c)\mapsto(a,c\exp(-t\dot{\varphi}(a))).

To see this, note first that
X_{f}\sim_{\Phi}0
since the flow preserves both momentum map components
\Phi_{1}(a,c)=a,\ \Phi_{2}(a,c)=ca^{-1}c
.
Also, using the explicit formula for

(see Proposition
4.17
),

\displaystyle\iota(X_{f})\omega

\displaystyle=-{\frac{1}{2}}a^{*}(\theta^{L}+\theta^{R})\cdot\dot{\varphi}(a)-{\frac{1}{2}}\dot{\varphi}(a)\operatorname{Ad}_{a}c^{*}\theta^{L}+{\frac{1}{2}}c^{*}\theta^{L}\cdot\operatorname{Ad}_{a}\dot{\varphi}(a)

as desired. In terms of the

(h_{1},h_{2}).(a,c)=(\operatorname{Ad}_{h_{1}}a,h_{2}ch_{1}^{-1})

Generalizing this example, we have:

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose

satisfies (A1),(A2),(A3), and that
\alpha\colon[0,1]\to\partial\Sigma

Given an invariant function

\kappa_{t}=\exp(t\dot{\varphi}(\kappa(\mathsf{a})))\cdot\kappa

Letting

The curve

7.
The quasi-symplectic groupoid

As described in Example
5.7
, the moduli space of a cylinder,

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma_{0}^{2},\{\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}\})

serves as the identity under the gluing operation. We shall see that this moduli space carries the structure of
a
quasi-symplectic groupoid
, with the moduli spaces
for other surfaces (with one base point on each boundary) as
Hamiltonian spaces
.

7.1.
Quasi-symplectic groupoids

For background material on Lie groupoids, we refer to
[
21
,
39
]
. In a nutshell, a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{S}}\rightrightarrows M

consists of a manifold
{\mathcal{S}}
of arrows, a submanifold
M
of units, surjective submersions

\mathsf{s}.\mathsf{t}\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to M
and a multiplication map

\operatorname{Mult}_{\mathcal{S}}\colon{\mathcal{S}}^{(2)}\to{\mathcal{S}},\ (s_{1},s_{2})\mapsto s_{1}s_{2}

defined on the set
{\mathcal{S}}^{(2)}=\{(s_{1},s_{2})|\ \mathsf{s}(s_{1})=\mathsf{t}(s_{2})\}
of
composable arrows
. The axioms for a
Lie groupoid
say that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a category, with
M
as its objects and
{\mathcal{S}}
as its morphisms, such that

\operatorname{Mult}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the composition of morphisms and such that every morphism is invertible. A
(left) action

of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{S}}\rightrightarrows M
on a manifold
Q
along a map
\Phi\colon Q\to M
(sometimes called a momentum map)
is given by an action map

(27)

\mathcal{A}\colon{\mathcal{S}}\ _{\mathsf{s}}\!\!\times_{\Phi}Q\to Q,\ (s,q)\mapsto sq

(the subscripts indicate the fiber product),
with
\Phi(sq)=\mathsf{t}(s)
, satisfying the action properties
s_{1}(s_{2}q)=(s_{1}s_{2})q
, with

mq=q
for units
m\in M\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
. For example, every groupoid has a (unique) action on its space
M

of units along the identity map
\Phi=\operatorname{id}_{M}
; the action of
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
takes
m=\mathsf{s}(s)
to
sm=\mathsf{t}(s)
. This action on
M
restricts to an action on orbits
{\mathcal{S}}m=\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{s}^{-1}(m))
.

A 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
is called
multiplicative
if it satisfies

\operatorname{Mult}_{\mathcal{S}}^{*}\omega=\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\omega

where
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\colon{\mathcal{S}}^{(2)}\to{\mathcal{S}}
are the projections to the first and second factor. A
symplectic groupoid

is a Lie groupoid with a multiplicative symplectic 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
. A
Hamiltonian space
for a symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{S}},\omega_{\mathcal{S}})
is a symplectic manifold
(Q,\omega_{Q})
with a left-action of
{\mathcal{S}}
such that
the action map satisfies
\mathcal{A}^{*}\omega_{Q}=\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{Q}
. As it turns out, the orbits of
the action on units
M\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
acquire unique symplectic 2-forms for which they are Hamiltonian spaces.

Example 7.1
.

The basic example of a symplectic groupoid is the cotangent bundle of a Lie group,

{\mathcal{S}}=T^{*}G\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}

with its standard symplectic form.
Using left trivialization
T^{*}G\cong G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, with elements denoted
(c,\mu)
, the symplectic form is

(28)

\omega={\mbox{d}}\langle c^{*}\theta^{L},\mu\rangle=-\langle c^{*}\theta^{L},{\mbox{d}}\mu\rangle-{\frac{1}{2}}\langle[c^{*}\theta^{L},c^{*}\theta^{L}],\mu\rangle.

The groupoid structure is that of an action groupoid for the co-adjoint action: Its source and target map are

\mathsf{s}(c,\mu)=\mu,\ \mathsf{t}(c,\mu)=(\operatorname{Ad}_{c^{-1}})^{*}\mu
, and the groupoid multiplication reads as

(c_{1},\mu_{1})(c_{2},\mu_{2})=(c_{1}c_{2},\mu_{2}).

As shown by Mikami-Weinstein
[
43
]
, a Hamiltonian space for this symplectic groupoid is exactly the same as a Hamiltonian
G
-space in the sense of symplectic geometry, with
\Phi
as its momentum map. The symplectic structure
on the
G
-orbits
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{*}\subseteq T^{*}G
is the usual Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau 2-form.

Symplectic groupoids ‘integrate’ Poisson manifolds. Their Dirac-geometric generalizations are the quasi-symplectic groupoids.

Definition 7.2
.

[
16
,
53
]

A
quasi-symplectic groupoid
is a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{S}}\rightrightarrows M
with
\dim{\mathcal{S}}=2\dim M
, equipped with a
2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
and a 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(M)
such that

(a)

\omega
is multiplicative,

(b)

{\mbox{d}}\omega=\mathsf{t}^{*}\eta-\mathsf{s}^{*}\eta,\ \ {\mbox{d}}\eta=0
,

(c)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{s})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{t})=0
.

A
Hamiltonian space
for a quasi-symplectic groupoid is a manifold
Q
with a 2-form
\omega_{Q}
, together with an
{\mathcal{S}}
-action along
\Phi\colon Q\to M
, such that

(a)

\mathcal{A}^{*}\omega_{Q}=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathcal{S}}^{*}\omega+\operatorname{pr}_{Q}^{*}\omega_{Q}
,

(b)

{\mbox{d}}\omega_{Q}=-\Phi^{*}\eta
,

(c)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{Q})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=0
.

Here
\mathcal{A}
is the action map (
27
), and
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathcal{S}},\operatorname{pr}_{Q}
are the projections from the fiber product to the two factors.

Similar to the symplectic setting, the orbits
\mathcal{O}=\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{s}^{-1}(a))\subseteq M
of a quasi-symplectic groupoid

{\mathcal{S}}
(acting on its units
M
) are examples of Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{S}}
-spaces, with momentum map the inclusion. The 2-form
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}
is obtained by reduction: the pullback of
\omega
to
\mathsf{s}^{-1}(a)
descends to
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}
; the result does not depend on the
choice of
a
.

Remark 7.3
.

In
[
16
]
, the terminology
twisted pre-symplectic groupoid
was used. We prefer
quasi-symplectic
, since
\omega
does satisfy a nondegeneracy condition (property (c) above).
There is also a Dirac-geometric
formulation of quasi-symplectic groupoids without the use of 2-forms, see e.g.
[
35
]
.

7.2.
The moduli space of a cylinder

.

Let
C
be a compact, oriented 1-manifold (in other words, a finite collection of
circles), with a finite subset
\mathcal{V}\subseteq C
meeting each component of
C
.
Thus
\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V})\cong G^{\mathcal{E}}
. The moduli space of the cylinder
C\times I
, with vertices
\mathcal{V}\times\partial I
is a Lie groupoid

(29)

{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{M}_{G}(C\times I,\ \mathcal{V}\times\partial I)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V})

(see Exercise
3.1
), with source and target map induced by the two boundary inclusions of

(C,\mathcal{V})
into
(C\times I,\mathcal{V}\times\partial I)

cylinders.

The groupoid may be identified with the action groupoid:

{\mathcal{S}}\cong G^{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V}),

where the isomorphism is given by the source map together with the maps

{\mathcal{S}}\to G
, for
\mathsf{v}\in\mathcal{V}
, given by the holonomy of the path from
\mathsf{v}\times\{1\}
to
\mathsf{v}\times\{0\}
along
\mathsf{v}\times I
.

(Quasi-symplectic groupoids from moduli spaces)
.

.

(a)

The moduli space of

, is a

(b)

\omega
is multiplicative. Note that the description of the groupoid multiplication is parallel to the reduction operation from

It suffices to consider the case that

Enumerate the vertices counter-clockwise:

C

The groupoid multiplication involves two copies of

Remark 7.5
.

An important notion in the theory of Lie groupoids is
Morita equivalence
.
A Morita equivalence of Lie groupoids
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\rightrightarrows Q_{i},\ i=1,2
is given by a manifold
\mathcal{E}
, together with a left-action
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
along
\Phi_{1}\colon\mathcal{E}\to Q_{1}
and a right action of
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
along
\Phi_{2}\colon\mathcal{E}\to Q_{2}
, such that the two actions commute, both actions are principal actions, and
\Phi_{1}
is the quotient map for the

{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
-action while
\Phi_{2}
is the quotient map for the
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
-action. For a Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic
groupoids
[
53
]
, the manifold
\mathcal{E}
is equipped with a 2-form
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}
, in such a way that
\mathcal{E}
becomes a Hamiltonian space for
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{2}^{\operatorname{op}}
(where
\operatorname{op}
indicates the opposite groupoid obtained by reversing arrows, with 2-form
-\omega_{2}
). Momentum map theories for Morita equivalent quasi-symplectic groupoids are considered ‘essentially the same’. For more details and basic facts, see e.g.
[
5
, Appendix A]
.

In the moduli setting, if
C
is a compact oriented 1-manifold and
\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2}\subseteq C
are finite subsets meeting each component, then the quasi-symplectic groupoids
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
associated to
(C,\mathcal{V}_{1})
and
(C,\mathcal{V}_{2})
are Morita equivalent, with the
bimodule

{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\circlearrowright\mathcal{R}\circlearrowleft{\mathcal{S}}_{2}

given by
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{M}_{G}(C\times I,(\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\{0\})\sqcup(\mathcal{V}_{2}\times\{1\}))
.

7.3.
Orbits

As remarked after Definition
7.2
, the orbits of any quasi-symplectic groupoid
{\mathcal{S}}
are
Hamiltonian spaces for
{\mathcal{S}}
. In the case of
{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{M}_{G}(C\times I,\mathcal{V}\times\partial I)
, the groupoid is an action groupoid for the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V})\cong G^{\mathcal{E}}
. For a description of the orbits of this action, we may assume that

C
is connected.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{V}=\{\mathsf{v}\}
is a single point. Then
G^{\mathcal{E}}=G
with the usual conjugation action of
G^{\mathcal{V}}=G
, hence the orbits are conjugacy classes
\mathcal{O}=G.a\subseteq G
. The 2-form on
\mathcal{O}
, making it a quasi-Hamiltonian space
for the quasi-symplectic groupoid of
(C,\{\mathsf{v}\})
, may be computed from the formula for the 2-form on

{\mathcal{S}}
given in Proposition
4.17
: Pulling back to
\mathsf{s}^{-1}(a)
gives the 2-form

{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{L}\cdot\operatorname{Ad}_{a}\theta^{L}\in\Omega^{2}(G)
.
That is,

(\iota_{\mathsf{s}^{-1}(a)}^{*}\omega)(\xi^{L},\zeta^{L})=-{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{a}-\operatorname{Ad}_{a^{-1}})\xi\cdot\zeta.

It descends to the 2-form on
\mathcal{O}=G/G_{a}
, given on generating vector fields by the same expression,

\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(\xi_{\mathcal{O}},\zeta_{\mathcal{O}})|_{a}=-{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{a}-\operatorname{Ad}_{a^{-1}})\xi\cdot\zeta;

We may regard
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}
as a counterpart of the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau form on coadjoint orbits
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. It was first described in
[
30
]
, see also
[
4
, Section 3.1]
. Note that
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}
is the unique 2-form on the orbit satisfying the momentum map condition

\iota(\xi_{\mathcal{O}})\omega_{\mathcal{O}}=-\iota_{\mathcal{O}}^{*}(\theta^{L}+\theta^{R})\cdot\xi.

In the general case, if
\mathcal{V}
has
n\geq 1
elements, the orbits
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V})
of the groupoid
consist of
n
-tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
whose product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
lies in a fixed conjugacy class.
One may work out a formula for
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}
by pulling back the 2-form on
\mathcal{M}_{G}(C\times I,\mathcal{V}\times\partial I)

to source fibers.
The result is, for

\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),\ \zeta=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
, and any point
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in G^{\mathcal{E}}
,

\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(\xi_{\mathcal{O}},\zeta_{\mathcal{O}})|_{a}=-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\operatorname{Ad}_{a_{i}}-\operatorname{Ad}_{a_{i}^{-1}})\xi_{i+1}\cdot\zeta_{i}.

8.
Dirac geometry

Until now, we developed the theory of 2-forms on moduli spaces intrinsically, using gluing diagrams for surfaces. We did, however, postpone arguments having to do with the ‘minimal degeneracy’ property of the 2-form from Theorem
4.1
, since these
are better understood within the framework of Dirac geometry. We shall give these proofs now, after developing some foundational material.

8.1.
Definitions

The notion of a
Dirac structure
on a manifold was introduced by Courant and Weinstein
[
19
,
20
]
as a simultaneous generalization of Poisson structures and closed 2-forms. The basic idea is to describe bivector fields and 2-forms on a manifold
Q
in terms of their graphs. Fix a closed 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(Q)
, and denote

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q=TQ\oplus T^{*}Q

with the
Courant bracket

(also known as
Dorfman bracket
)
on its space of sections

[\![X_{1}+\mu_{1},X_{2}+\mu_{2}]\!]=[X_{1},X_{2}]+\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\mu_{2}-\iota_{X_{2}}{\mbox{d}}\mu_{1}+\iota_{X_{1}}\iota_{X_{2}}\eta

for vector fields
X_{i}
and 1-forms
\mu_{i}
. The bracket satisfies a version of the Jacobi identity:

[\![\sigma_{1},[\![\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}]\!]]\!]=[\![[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}]\!]]\!]+[\![\sigma_{2},[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3}]\!]]\!].

It is not skew-symmetric, but its symmetric part is an exact 1-form:

[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\!]+[\![\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1}]\!]={\mbox{d}}\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle.

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the symmetric bilinear form on
\ T_{\eta}Q
, given by

\langle X_{1}+\mu_{1},X_{2}+\mu_{2}\rangle=\iota_{X_{1}}\mu_{2}+\iota_{X_{2}}\mu_{1}.

A
Dirac structure
on
Q
is a subbundle
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
(with base
Q
) such that
A
is Lagrangian (i.e.,
A=A^{\perp}
), and such that its space of sections is closed under the Courant bracket. For every Dirac structure, the
Courant-Dorfman bracket restricts to a Lie bracket on
\Gamma(A)
, making
A
into a Lie algebroid with anchor

\mathsf{a}_{A}\colon A\to TQ
given by projection to the vector field part.

For
\eta=0
, one finds that a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TQ)
is a Poisson structure on
Q
if and only if the graph of the map

\pi^{\sharp}\colon T^{*}Q\to TQ
is a Dirac structure; similarly a 2-form
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{*}Q)
is closed if and only if the graph of
\omega^{\flat}\colon TQ\to T^{*}Q
is a Dirac structure.

The Dirac structures relevant to quasi-Hamiltonian geometry arise from the
\eta
-twisted Courant bracket on

{\mathbb{E}}=\mathbb{T}_{\eta}G
, where
G
is a Lie group with an invariant metric
\cdot
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(G)
the Cartan 3-form (
9
). It admits a trivialization
[
2
]

{\mathbb{E}}\cong G\times(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g})

by the map
s\colon\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma({\mathbb{E}})
,

(30)

s(\xi^{\prime},\xi)=\xi^{L}-(\xi^{\prime})^{R}+{\frac{1}{2}}(\theta^{L}\cdot\xi+\theta^{R}\cdot\xi^{\prime})\in\Gamma(\mathbb{T}G).

One may verify that
s
takes the Lie bracket and metric on
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
(regarded as constant sections) to the Courant bracket and metric
on
\Gamma({\mathbb{E}})
. Hence, every Lagrangian Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g})
determines a Dirac structure

A=G\times\mathfrak{l}
inside
{\mathbb{E}}
. In particular, the
Cartan-Dirac structure

A\subseteq{\mathbb{E}}

[
2
,
15
]

is the subbundle corresponding to the diagonal,
\mathfrak{l}=(\mathfrak{g})_{\Delta}
.

8.2.
Dirac morphisms, Hamiltonian spaces

Let
Q_{i},\ i=1,2
be manifolds with closed 3-forms
\eta_{i}
. A
Courant morphism

(31)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi\colon\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{1}}Q_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{2}}Q_{2}

is given by a smooth map
\Phi\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{2}
and a 2-form

\omega\in\Omega^{2}(Q_{1})
such that
\eta_{1}-\Phi^{*}\eta_{2}={\mbox{d}}\omega
. (If the 2-form
\omega
is zero, we use the
notation
\mathbb{T}\Phi
.) We think of the Courant morphism
as a
relation
, where
v_{1}+\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{1}}Q_{1}|_{q_{1}}
is related to

v_{2}+\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{2}}Q_{2}|_{q_{2}}
if and only if
q_{2}=\Phi(q_{1})
and

v_{2}=T\Phi|_{q_{1}}(v_{1}),\ \ \mu_{1}=(T\Phi|_{q_{1}})^{*}(\mu_{2})+\iota_{v_{1}}\omega.

We shall write

v_{1}+\mu_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi}v_{2}+\mu_{2}

for related elements.
Given another Courant morphism

\mathbb{T}_{\omega^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{2}}Q_{2}\dasharrow\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{3}}Q_{3}
, the composition of relations is given by

\mathbb{T}_{\omega^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime}\circ\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi=\mathbb{T}_{\omega+\Phi^{*}\omega^{\prime}}(\Phi^{\prime}\circ\Phi).

Given Dirac structures
A_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{i}}Q_{i}
, we say that (
31
) defines a (strong) Dirac morphism, also known as

morphism of Manin pairs

[
17
]
,

(32)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi\colon(\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{1}}Q_{1},A_{1})\dasharrow(\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{2}}Q_{2},A_{2}),

if for all
q_{2}=\Phi(q_{1})
, every element of
(A_{2})_{q_{2}}
is
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi
-related to a
unique
element of

(A_{1})_{q_{1}}
. By definition, a Dirac morphism defines a bundle map
\Phi^{*}A_{2}\to A_{1}
, or equivalently a
comorphism of vector bundles

A_{1}\dasharrow A_{2}
. In fact, it is a
comorphism of Lie algebroids
: the
pullback map on sections
\Gamma(A_{2})\to\Gamma(A_{1})
preserves Lie brackets.

As an important special case, we define

Definition 8.1
.

[
17
]

A
Hamiltonian space
for the Dirac structure
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
is a
manifold
M
together with a Dirac morphism

(33)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q,A).

We refer to
\Phi
as the
momentum map
of the Hamiltonian space. The Lie algebra morphism

\Gamma(A)\to\Gamma(TM),\ \sigma\mapsto\sigma_{M}

defines an
action of the Lie algebroid
A
on
M
along
\Phi
.

Conversely, given a manifold
M
with a 2-form
\omega
and a Lie algebroid action of
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
along a map
\Phi
,
the pair
(\Phi,\omega)
defines a Hamiltonian space if and only if
the following three conditions are satisfied:

(a)

{\mbox{d}}\omega=-\Phi^{*}\eta

(b)

\iota(\sigma_{M})\omega=-\Phi^{*}\alpha_{A}(\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in\Gamma(A)
,

(c)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=0
.

Here
\alpha_{A}(\sigma)\in\Omega^{1}(Q)
is the 1-form component of
\sigma\in\Gamma(A)
.
By (
b
), the kernel of
\omega
at
m
contains all
\sigma|_{M}|_{m}
such that
\alpha_{A}(\sigma)|_{m}=0
.
In fact, this is the entire kernel:

Proposition 8.2
.

The condition
\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=0
is equivalent to the following explicit description of the kernel,

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)|_{m}=\{\sigma_{M}|_{m}\colon\alpha_{A}(\sigma)|_{\Phi(m)}=0\},

for all
m\in M
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\operatorname{ker}(\omega)|_{m}
, with image
w=T\Phi(v)\in TQ|_{\Phi(m)}
. For all
\tau\in\Gamma(A)
we have

0=\omega(v,\tau_{M}|_{m})=\iota(v)\Phi^{*}\alpha_{A}(\tau)|_{m}=\iota(w)\alpha_{A}(\tau)|_{\Phi(m)}=\langle(w,0),\tau|_{\Phi(m)}\rangle.

This means that
(w,0)\in A_{\Phi(m)}^{\perp}=A_{\Phi(m)}
. Hence, there exists
\sigma\in\Gamma(A)
such that

\mathsf{a}_{A}(\sigma)|_{\Phi(m)}=w,\ \ \ \alpha_{A}(\sigma)|_{\Phi(m)}=0.

The difference

v-\sigma_{M}|_{m}
lies in
\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)
, and hence is zero by (
c
).
∎

For an action of a Lie algebroid
A
on a manifold
M
, the set of vector fields
\{\sigma_{M}|\ \sigma\in\Gamma(A)\}

are a locally finitely generated Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
They define a singular foliation on
M
(in the sense of Androulidakis-Skandalis
[
8
]
), with leaves the
A
-orbits. For
m\in M
, let
S_{m}=\{\sigma_{M}|_{m}|\ \sigma\in\Gamma(A)\}\subseteq TM|_{m}
be the tangent space to the orbit, and
\mathfrak{k}_{m}
the
stabilizer Lie algebra
, given by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{k}_{m}\longrightarrow A_{\Phi(m)}\longrightarrow S_{m}\longrightarrow 0.

The Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{k}_{m}
is inherited from the bracket on sections of
A
.

Proposition 8.3
.

Suppose
M
is a Hamiltonian
A
-space defined by a Dirac morphism (
33
).
For all
m\in M
we have

\operatorname{ann}(\operatorname{ran}(T\Phi|_{m}))=\mathfrak{k}_{m}.

where
\mathfrak{k}_{m}\subseteq A_{\Phi(m)}\cap T^{*}Q|_{\Phi(m)}
is regarded as a subspace of
T^{*}Q|_{\Phi(m)}
.
Similarly, the map
\omega^{\flat}\colon TM\to T^{*}M
restricts to
an isomorphism

\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi|_{m})\cong\operatorname{ann}(S_{m}).

Proof.

By definition of a Dirac morphism,
A_{\Phi(m)}
consists of all elements of
w+\nu\in\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q|_{\Phi(m)}
that are
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi
-related to
some
element of
TM|_{m}
. The subspace
\mathfrak{k}_{m}\subseteq A_{\Phi(m)}
consists of elements
that are related to
0\in TM|_{m}
. This means that
w=0
and
(T\Phi|_{m})^{*}\nu=0
, i.e.,

\nu\in\operatorname{ann}(\operatorname{ran}(T\Phi|_{m}))
.

For the second part, note that since
\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=0
, the map
\omega^{\flat}
restricts to an injection on
\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi|_{m})
. Since

\omega(\sigma_{M}|_{m},v)=\iota(v)\iota(\sigma_{M}|_{m})\omega=-\iota(v)\Phi^{*}\alpha_{A}(\sigma)|_{m},

the image
\omega^{\flat}(\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi|_{m}))
is contained in
\operatorname{ann}(S_{m})
. By dimension count (using the first part), this is an equality.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

This proposition generalizes a well-known fact for Hamiltonian group actions in symplectic geometry (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
): If
(M,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold with a Hamiltonian action of a Lie group
G
, with momentum map
\Phi\colon M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then

\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi|_{m})=T_{m}(G.m)^{\omega},\ \ \operatorname{ran}(T\Phi|_{m})=\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{g}_{m}),

where
\mathfrak{g}_{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
are the stabilizer algebras.

Hamiltonian spaces for the Cartan-Dirac structure (Section
8.1
) are the quasi-Hamiltonian spaces from
[
4
]
. More precisely, one obtains an equivalence with quasi-Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{g}
-spaces
. The integration to a
G
-action may be put in by hand, or more conceptually by the notion of Hamiltonian space for
the quasi-symplectic groupoid integrating the Cartan-Dirac structure
[
16
,
53
]
. The moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
are Hamiltonian space for a Dirac structure
on
G^{\mathcal{E}}
; this will be explained below.

8.3.
Admissible functions

The following result is due to Bursztyn, Iglesias-Ponte, and Ševera
[
17
, Section 3.4]
; in the case of quasi-Hamiltonian
G
-spaces it was proved in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 8.5
.

[
17
]

Let
M
be a Hamiltonian
A
-space defined by a Dirac morphism (
33
).
Suppose
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
is invariant under the
A
-action on
M
, with momentum map
\Phi
. Then there is a unique vector field

X_{f}
such that

\iota(X_{f})\omega=-{\mbox{d}}f,\ \ \ X_{f}\sim_{\Phi}0.

This vector field satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\omega=0.

Proof.

Invariance of
f
means that
{\mbox{d}}f|_{m}\in\operatorname{ann}(S_{m})
for all
m
.
By Property (
c
) of a Hamiltonian
A
-space, the vector bundle map

TM\to\Phi^{*}TQ\oplus T^{*}M,\ v\mapsto(T\Phi(v),-\iota_{v}\omega)

is injective. Proposition
8.3
shows that the elements
(0,{\mbox{d}}f|_{m})
lie in its range.
We hence obtain
X_{f}
as the pre-image of
(0,{\mbox{d}}f)
under that map.
The second claim follows by Cartan’s identity:

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\omega=\iota_{X_{f}}{\mbox{d}}\omega+{\mbox{d}}\iota_{X_{f}}\omega=-\iota_{X_{f}}\Phi^{*}\eta-{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{d}}f=0.

Here we used
X_{f}\sim_{\Phi}0
.
∎

In the terminology of Courant
[
19
]
, the function
f
is
admissible
. Note that the conditions on
X_{f}
mean that

(34)

X_{f}+{\mbox{d}}f\sim_{\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi}0.

Proposition 8.6
.

The formula

(35)

\{f,g\}=\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}g

defines a Poisson bracket on the space of
A
-invariant functions,
with

(36)

X_{\{f,g\}}=[X_{f},X_{g}].

Proof.

Given
A
-invariant functions
f,g
, the properties
X_{f}\sim_{\Phi}0,\ X_{g}\sim_{\Phi}0
imply

[X_{f},X_{g}]\sim_{\Phi}0
. Furthermore,

\iota([X_{f},X_{g}])\omega=\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\iota(X_{g})\omega-\iota(X_{g})\mathcal{L}(X_{f})\omega=-\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}dg=-{\mbox{d}}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}g)=-{\mbox{d}}\{f,g\}.

This proves (
36
). Skew symmetry of the bracket (
35
) follows from

\{f,g\}=-\iota(X_{f}){\mbox{d}}g=\iota(X_{f})\iota(X_{g})\omega
, and
the Jacobi identity follows by applying
(
36
) to a third
A
-invariant function.
∎

In general, the
A
-action on
M
may not be well-behaved, and hence the space of global
A
-invariant functions may be rather
small. However, consider the open subset

M_{\operatorname{reg}}=\{m\in M|\ \mathfrak{k}_{m}=0\}

on which the
A
-action is regular, i.e, where all leaves have maximal dimension
\operatorname{rank}(A)
. On this set, every point admits an open neighborhood
U
on which the foliation by
A
-orbits is fibrating.
Hence, we can consider Hamiltonian vector fields
X_{f}
for
local

A
-invariant functions
f\in C^{\infty}(U)
.
By Proposition
8.3
, the span of local Hamiltonian vector fields is exactly
\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)\subseteq TM
.
If the
A
-action on
M_{\operatorname{reg}}
is
globally
fibrating, the Poisson structure on
A
-invariant functions descends to a Poisson structure on the orbit space
M_{\operatorname{reg}}/\sim
with symplectic leaves given by the
images, under the quotient map, of intersections of
A
-orbits with the fibers of
\Phi
.

8.4.
Quasi-Poisson structures

Let
M
be a Hamiltonian
A
-space for a Dirac structure
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
.
The Poisson bracket on
A
-invariant functions may be extended to a
quasi-Poisson structure
on
all
functions, as follows. Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
be a Lagrangian
subbundle (not necessarily a Dirac structure) transverse to
A
. This defines a Lagrangian splitting

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q=A\oplus B.

The preimage
(\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi)^{-1}B\subseteq\mathbb{T}M
consists of all elements
v+\mu
such that
v+\mu\sim_{\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi}y
for some
y\in B
. It is a Lagrangian subbundle, which is transverse to
TM
. (Indeed,
v\in TM
with
v\sim_{\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi}y
would mean
y\in A
.) See, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 1.8]
.
It is hence the graph of a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}(TM))
. If
f
is a (local)
A
-invariant function, defining a Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f}
, then
X_{f}+{\mbox{d}}f\in(\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi)^{-1}B
by (
34
). Consequently,

X_{f}=\pi^{\sharp}({\mbox{d}}f)
. It follows that
\{f,g\}=\pi({\mbox{d}}f,{\mbox{d}}g)
for all (local)
A
-invariant functions. The construction of

\pi
depends on the choice of
B
, but in practice it often happens that there is a canonical choice.
We also remark that if
B
is integrable, i.e., a Dirac structure, then its pre-image under
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi
is again integrable, and hence is the graph of a
Poisson
structure.

8.5.
Cross sections

Suppose
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
is a Dirac structure. Given a smooth map
f\colon Q^{\prime}\hookrightarrow Q
, with the property that
f
is transverse to
the anchor map
\mathsf{a}_{A}\colon A\to TQ
, one obtains a
pullback Dirac structure

f^{!}A=(\mathbb{T}f)^{-1}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}

with respect to
\eta^{\prime}=f^{*}\eta_{Q}
. The bundle
f^{!}A
consists of all

v^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}Q^{\prime}
for which there exists
v+\mu\in A
with
v=f_{*}v^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime}=f^{*}\mu
.
We will mainly need this construction for the case of an embedding as a submanifold. Hamiltonian spaces for

A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
give rise to Hamiltonian spaces for the pullback. The following fact
is a Dirac-geometric analogue of the Symplectic Cross Section Theorem of Guillemin-Sternberg
[
29
]
.

Proposition 8.7

(Cross sections)
.

Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
be a Hamiltonian space for the Dirac structure
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta}Q
, and

\iota\colon Q^{\prime}\hookrightarrow Q

a submanifold transverse to the anchor
\mathsf{a}_{A}
(and hence also to the map
\Phi
). Let

M^{\prime}=\Phi^{-1}(Q^{\prime})
, and let
\omega^{\prime}
be the pullback of
\omega
and
\Phi^{\prime}
the restriction of
\Phi
.
Then
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
is a Hamiltonian space for the Dirac structure
A^{\prime}=\iota^{!}A\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\eta^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}
.

More generally, there is a Cross Section Theorem for abritrary Dirac morphisms. For the precise
statement (and its proof) see
[
41
, Theorem B.7]
. See
[
3
,
10
,
22
]
for related versions.

8.6.
Dirac structures associated with closed polygons

Let
C
be a compact oriented 1-manifold, with vertices
\mathcal{V}
and oriented edges
\mathcal{E}
. (In our application to moduli spaces for surfaces, this will be
C=\partial\Sigma
.) Thus
\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V})=G^{\mathcal{E}}
with the natural action of
G^{\mathcal{V}}
.
On
G^{\mathcal{E}}
, we have the closed 3-form
\eta^{\mathcal{E}}=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}g_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\eta
, and the corresponding standard
Courant algebroid
\mathbb{T}_{\eta^{\mathcal{E}}}\mathcal{M}_{G}(C,\mathcal{V})=(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}G)^{\mathcal{E}}
.
Equation (
30
) defines a trivialization

(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}G)^{\mathcal{E}}\cong G^{\mathcal{E}}\times(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}}.

Every Lagrangian Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}=(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}}
defines a Dirac structure
G^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{l}
. We will take

\mathfrak{l}
to be
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
, regarded as a Lie subalgebra by the inclusion

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}\to(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}}

taking the function
\mathsf{v}\mapsto\xi_{\mathsf{v}}
to the function
\mathsf{e}\mapsto(\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})},\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})})
.
Thus,
A=G^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
is spanned by sections

(37)

\sigma(\xi)=\sum_{\mathsf{e}}s^{\mathsf{e}}({\xi}_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})},{\xi}_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}),

where the superscript indicates that we put
s(\cdot,\cdot)
on the copy of
G
labeled by the edge
\mathsf{e}
.
The properties (
a
), (
b
), (
c
) of Theorem
4.1
may be rephrased as follows:

Proposition 8.8
.

For a pair
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfying (A1),(A2),(A3), the boundary holomomies define a
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-equivariant Dirac morphism

(38)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}),T\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}))\dasharrow((\mathbb{T}_{\eta}G)^{\mathcal{E}},A).

That is, the moduli space is a Hamiltonian space for this Dirac structure.

Recall that we had postponed the proof of property (
c
) of Theorem
4.1
, except for the case that

\Sigma
is a disjoint union of disks. We shall now use the Dirac-geometric formulation to complete the proof.

8.7.
Proof of minimal degeneracy

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})
satisfies (A1),(A2),(A3). In Subsection
4.4
, we constructed
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}))
as the pullback
\omega=\iota^{*}\widehat{\omega}
of the 2-form on

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\widehat{\Sigma},\widehat{\mathcal{V}})
, where
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a disjoint union of disks (polgons) obtained from
\Sigma
by cutting, and

\iota\colon\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{M}_{G}(\widehat{\Sigma},\widehat{\mathcal{V}})

is the inclusion. This inclusion may be regarded as a cross section, as follows: Let
\partial\widehat{\Sigma}
be the boundary of the cut surface, and
\partial\widehat{\Sigma}/\sim
its image under the quotient map
\widehat{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
. Thus,
\partial\widehat{\Sigma}/\!\!\sim
is an embedded graph in
\Sigma
, consisting of the boundary edges of
\Sigma
together with the paths
defining the cutting. The corresponding moduli space will be denoted by
Q
.
The maps
\partial\widehat{\Sigma}\to\partial\widehat{\Sigma}/\!\!\sim\,\,\leftarrow\partial\Sigma
induce maps

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\partial\widehat{\Sigma},\widehat{\mathcal{V}})=G^{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\longleftarrow}}\mathcal{M}_{G}(\partial\widehat{\Sigma}/\!\!\sim\,\,,\mathcal{V})=Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=G^{\mathcal{E}}.

The submanifold
Q\subseteq G^{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}
consists of maps
g\colon\widehat{\mathcal{E}}\to G
such that
g_{\mathsf{e}_{1}}=g_{\mathsf{e}_{2}}^{-1}
for every pair of ‘glued’ edges; the quotient map
\pi
omits components corresponding to glued edges. Observe that

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V})=j^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{G}(\widehat{\Sigma},\widehat{\mathcal{V}}))
.

Let
\widehat{A}\subseteq(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}G)^{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}
be the counterpart of
A
for the cut surface. Since the
2-form
\widehat{\omega}
for the cut surface is known to satisfy the properties of Theorem
4.1
, it defines a
Dirac morphism
\mathbb{T}_{\widehat{\omega}}\widehat{\Phi}
as in (
38
).

The anchor of
\widehat{A}
is transverse to the submanifold
Q
, due to the fact that
G^{\widehat{\mathcal{V}}}.Q=G^{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}
. Hence,
Proposition
8.7
gives a Dirac morphism

(39)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\widehat{\Phi}|_{j^{-1}(Q)})\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}),T\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma,\mathcal{V}))\dasharrow(\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{Q}}Q,j^{!}\widehat{A}),

The projection map

Proof.

The composition of maps

for all non-glued edges

and

\widehat{\xi}_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e}^{\prime})}=\widehat{\xi}_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})},\ \ \widehat{\xi}_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}=\widehat{\xi}_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e}^{\prime})}

8.8.
Lagrangian boundary conditions

By similar arguments, we can complete the proof that for a
collection of Lagrangian Lie subgroups
H_{\mathsf{e}}
attached to the edges,
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
5.9
. As explained in the proof of Proposition
5.9
,
the inclusion map
j\colon Q=\prod_{\mathsf{e}}H_{\mathsf{e}}\to G^{\mathcal{E}}
is transverse to the
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-orbits in
G^{\mathcal{E}}
. Hence, the Cross Section Theorem
8.7
applies, and gives a Dirac morphism

\mathbb{T}_{\iota^{*}\omega}(\Phi|_{\Phi^{-1}(Q)})\colon\ (\mathbb{T}\Phi^{-1}(Q),T\Phi^{-1}(Q))\dasharrow(\mathbb{T}Q,j^{!}(G^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}})).

Let
\pi\colon Q\to\operatorname{pt}
be the constant map to a point. We claim that
\mathbb{T}\pi
defines a Dirac morphism

\mathbb{T}\pi\colon(\mathbb{T}Q,j^{!}(G^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}))\dasharrow(0,0).

Suppose
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
is such that the image of
\delta(\xi)
under the anchor is tangent to
Q
, at some given

h\in Q
(with components
h_{\mathsf{e}}\in H_{\mathsf{e}}
).
This means that for all
\mathsf{e}
, the vector field
\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}^{L}-\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}^{R}
on
G
is tangent to
H_{\mathsf{e}}
. That is,

\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}-\operatorname{Ad}_{h_{\mathsf{e}}}\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}.

The
resulting section
j^{!}\delta(\xi)
of
j^{!}(G^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}})
satisfies
j^{!}\delta(\xi)\sim_{T\pi}0
if and only if
the 1-form part of
\delta(\xi)|_{h}
pulls back to
0
on
Q
. Equivalently, each

(\theta^{L}\cdot\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}+\theta^{R}\cdot\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})})|_{h}\in T^{*}G|_{h_{\mathsf{e}}}

pulls back to
0
on
H_{\mathsf{e}}
.
Since
H_{\mathsf{e}}
is Lagrangian, this gives the condition

\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})}+\operatorname{Ad}_{h_{\mathsf{e}}}\xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}.

Taken together, we arrive at the condition
\xi_{\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})},\ \xi_{\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e})}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}
for all
\mathsf{e}
. But since every vertex

\mathsf{v}
arises as
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{e})=\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{e}^{\prime})
for adjacent edges
\mathsf{e},\mathsf{e}^{\prime}
, and since
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}=0
by assumption, we conclude
\xi=0
. This shows that
\mathbb{T}\pi
is a Dirac morphism as desired. Letting
p=\pi\circ\Phi|_{\Phi^{-1}(Q)}\to\operatorname{pt}
be the projection
\Phi^{-1}(Q)\to\operatorname{pt}
, it follows that
\iota^{*}\omega
defines a Dirac morphism

\mathbb{T}_{\iota^{*}\omega}p\colon(\mathbb{T}\Phi^{-1}(Q),T\Phi^{-1}(Q))\dasharrow(0,0).

Equivalently,
\iota^{*}\omega
is symplectic.

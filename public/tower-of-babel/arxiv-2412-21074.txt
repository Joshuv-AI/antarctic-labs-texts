Relatively uniformly continuous semigroups on ordered vector spaces
Eduard Emelyanov, Nazife Erkurşun-Özcan, Svetlana Gorokhova 1 1 Sobolev Institute of Mathematics, Novosibirsk, Russia 2 2 Hacettepe University, Ankara, Turkey 3 3 Southern Mathematical Institute – the Affiliate of Vladikavkaz Scientific Center of RAN, Vladikavkaz, Russia

Abstract
We study relatively uniformly continuous operator semigroups on ordered vector spaces and extend several recent results obtained by M. Kramar Fijavž, M. Kandić, M. Kaplin, and J. Glück in the vector lattice setting to ordered vector spaces with generating cones.



Keywords:

ordered vector space, generating cone, relatively uniform convergence, operator semigroup, order continuous at zero

Introduction and preliminaries

Relatively uniformly continuous semigroups on vector lattices (VLs) were introduced and studied by Kramar Fijavž, Kandić, and Glück

[
10
,
9
,
8
]
in order to extend the theory of
C_{0}
-semigroups to the VL setting
(see also a recent paper
[
6
]
of the first author). It is rather natural and flexible to consider an abstract
Cauchy problem on a dense subspace of a Banach space (see
[
9
]
and references therein).
In a plenty of cases (e.g., a subspace of analytic functions), one may face even lack of the VL structure.
However, in various applications, underling PDEs describe processes in a compact area, and then
the space still possesses a structure of an ordered vector space (OVS) with a generating cone.
This motivates us to study how far the results of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
]

can be extended to OVSs.

Throughout the paper, vector spaces are real and operators are linear.
By
x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
we denote a decreasing net satisfying
\inf\limits_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}=0
.
An OVS
X
is said to have a
generating cone
if the span of its positive cone
X_{+}
is
X
, or
equivalently
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
. An element
u\in X_{+}
is said to be an
order unit
of
X

if
X=\bigcup\limits_{r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}r[-u,u]
. Clearly, if an OVS
X
possesses an order unit
then the positive cone
X_{+}
is generating.

Definition 1.1
.

A net
(x_{\alpha})
in an OVS
X

a)

order converges to
x\in X

(
briefly,
o
-converges to
x
or
x_{\alpha}\convo x
)

if there exists a net
g_{\beta}\downarrow 0
in
X
such that, for each
\beta
, there is

\alpha_{\beta}
with
\pm(x_{\alpha}-x)\leq g_{\beta}
for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{\beta}
.

b)

relative uniform converges to
x\in X

(
briefly,
ru
-converges to
x
)

if, for some
u\in X

(
a regulator of the convergence
)
, there exists an
increasing sequence
(\alpha_{n})
of indices
satisfying
\pm(x_{\alpha}-x)\leq\frac{1}{n}u
for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{n}
.
We abbreviate this by
x_{\alpha}\convr x(u)
or simply
x_{\alpha}\convr x
.

c)

ru
-Cauchy if the double net
(x_{\alpha^{\prime}}-x_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}})

ru
-converges to
0
.

Definition 1.2
.

A subset
C
of an OVS
X
is called

a)

ru
-closed if, for every net
(x_{\alpha})
in
C
,
x_{\alpha}\convr x
implies
x\in C
.

b)

ru
-dense if, for each
x\in X
there is a net
(x_{\alpha})
in
C
such that
x_{\alpha}\convr x
.

c)

ru
-complete in
X
if, for each
ru
-Cauchy net
(x_{\alpha})
in
C

with a regulator
u\in X
there exist
x\in C
and
w\in X
such that
x_{\alpha}\convr x(w)
.

As the
ru
-convergence is sequential (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Proposition 2.4]
),
nets in Definition
1.2
can be replaced by sequences.

The complements to
ru
-closed subsets of a VL
X
forms the (not necessary linear)
ru
-
topology
on
X

introduced by W.A.L. Luxemburg and L.C-Jr. Moore in
[
11
]
.
It is well known that
ru
-topology agrees with norm topology on a Banach lattices (BL)
(e.g., by
[
2
, Lemma 2.30]
). By
[
10
, Example 2.2]
,
the
ru
-topology on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
is not locally convex when
0<p<1
.

It should be noted that the similarly defined
ru
-
topology
on an OVS with a generating cone enjoy
all the properties of
ru
-
topology
on a VL listed in
[
11
, Section 4]

with a little clarification that the use of modulus like
|g|\leq f
need to be replaced by
\pm g\leq f
.

In the present paper, we focus at
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-parameter operator semigroups.
Namely, under a
semigroup
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on a vector space
X
we understand

-

a family of operators on
X
satisfying
T_{s+t}=T_{s}T_{t}
for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
T_{0}=I_{X}
, where
I_{X}
is the identity operator on
X
.

For unexplained terminology, notation, and basic results on OVSs, VLs,
and operator semigroups, we refer to
[

,
4
,
7
,

,

2
Relatively uniformly continuous at zero semigroups on ordered vector spaces

In order to deal with partial differential equations, whose boundary
conditions are facing lack of the common topological structure and
belong to just an Archimedean VL, Kandić and Kaplin introduced
an important concept of a relatively uniformly continuous semigroup on
a VL
[
9
, Definition 3.3]
. We extend their definition as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A semigroup
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on an OVS
X
is

a)

relatively uniformly continuous
(
or, a
ruc
semigroup
)
if
T_{s+h}x\convr T_{s}x
for every
x\in X
and
s\geq 0
as
h\to 0
.

b)

ruc
at zero, if
T_{h}x\convr x
as
h\downarrow 0
for each
x\in X
.

c)

order continuous if
T_{s+h}x\convo T_{s}x
for every
x\in X
and
s\geq 0
, as
h\to 0
.

d)

order continuous at zero if
T_{h}x\convo x
as
h\downarrow 0
for each
x\in X
.

e)

order bounded at zero if, for each
x\in X
there exists
\varepsilon_{x}>0
such that
the set
\{T_{h}x\}_{h\leq\varepsilon_{x}}
is order bounded.

It is worth noting that in Archimedean OVSs
ru
-convergence imply
o
-convergence, and hence each
ruc
(at zero) semigroup
on an Archimedean OVS is order continuous (at zero). There are two relatively easy cases when a semigroup
admits a localization of the property to be
ruc
/order continuous.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be a semigroup on an OVS
X
with a generating cone.

i)

Suppose
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is
ruc
at zero and there exists
\varepsilon>0
so that, for each
b\in X_{+}
,
there exists
v\in X_{+}
such that
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}T_{s}[-b,b]\subseteq[-v,v]
. Then
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is
ruc
.

ii)

Suppose
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is order continuous at zero and there exists
\varepsilon>0
so that, for each
x_{\alpha}\convo 0

there is a net
g_{\beta}\downarrow 0
such that, for each
\beta
there exists
\alpha_{\beta}
satisfying

\pm T_{s}x_{\alpha}\leq g_{\beta}
for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{\beta}
and
0\leq s\leq\varepsilon
. Then
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is order continuous.

Proof.

i)
Take arbitrary
x\in X
and
s_{0}>0
, and let
h\downarrow 0
. By the assumption,
(T_{h}x-x)\convr 0
.
By
[
5
, Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 3.2]
, for every
x_{\alpha}\convr 0
there exist
u\in X_{+}
and an increasing sequence

(\alpha_{n})
of indices with
\pm T_{s}x_{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{n}u
for

\alpha\geq\alpha_{n}
and
0\leq s\leq s_{0}
. In particular,
T_{s_{0}}
is
ru
-continuous. Thus,
T_{s_{0}+h}x-T_{s_{0}}x=T_{s_{0}}(T_{h}x-x)\convr 0
,
and hence the function
s\to T_{s}x
is right
ru
-continuous at
s_{0}
.

Since
(x-T_{h}x)\convr 0
, there exist
w\in X_{+}
and a decreasing sequence
(h_{n})
in
(0,s_{0}]

with
\pm T_{s}(x-T_{h}x)\leq\frac{1}{n}w
for
0<h\leq h_{n}
and
0\leq s\leq s_{0}
.
In particular,
T_{s_{0}-h}x-T_{s_{0}}x=T_{s_{0}-h}(x-T_{h}x)\convr 0
for
s_{0}\geq h\downarrow 0
,
and hence the function
s\to T_{s}x
is also left
ru
-continuous at
s_{0}
.

ii)
Take
x\in X
and
s_{0}>0
, and let
h\downarrow 0
. Then
(T_{h}x-x)\convo 0
.
By
[
5
, Proposition 3.3]
, for each
x_{\alpha}\convo 0
, there is a net
g_{\beta}\downarrow 0
such that,
for each
\beta
, there is
\alpha_{\beta}
satisfying
\pm T_{s}x_{\alpha}\leq g_{\beta}
for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{\beta}
and
0\leq s\leq s_{0}+1
.
In particular,
T_{s_{0}}
is order continuous. Therefore, we obtain
T_{s_{0}+h}x-T_{s_{0}}x=T_{s_{0}}(T_{h}x-x)\convo 0
,
and hence
s\to T_{s}x
is right order continuous at
s_{0}
.

Applying once more
[
5
, Proposition 3.3]
, find a net
q_{\beta}\downarrow 0
such that, for each
\beta
,
there is
h_{\beta}\in(0,s_{0}]
with
\pm T_{s}(x-T_{h}x)\leq q_{\beta}
for
0<h\leq h_{\beta}
and
0\leq s\leq s_{0}
.
In particular,
T_{s_{0}-h}x-T_{s_{0}}x=T_{s_{0}-h}(x-T_{h}x)\convo 0
for
s_{0}\geq h\downarrow 0
,
and hence
s\to T_{s}x
is also left order continuous at
s_{0}
.
∎

C_{0}
-semigroups need not to be
ruc
at zero
[
9
, Example 3.12]
.
In the reverse direction we have the following partial result.

Proposition 2.3
.

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
if one of the following conditions holds.

i)

X
is an OBS with a closed generating normal cone,
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}

is
ruc
at zero, and there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, for each
b\in X_{+}

the set
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}T_{h}[0,b]
is order bounded.

ii)

X
is a BL with order continuous norm and
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is an order continuous
at zero semigroup which consists of bounded operators.

Proof.

i)
It follows from
[
5
, Proposition 3.3(iv)]
that
\sup\limits_{0\leq h\leq M}\|T_{h}\|<\infty
for every
M\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Let
x\in X
and
h\downarrow 0
. Then
T_{h}x\convr x
because
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is
ruc
at zero. Normality of
X_{+}
ensures

T_{h}x\convr x\Longrightarrow\|T_{h}x-x\|\to 0
.

ii)
Let
x\in X
and
h\downarrow 0
.
Since the norm in
E
is order continuous, the rest follows from
T_{h}x\convo x\Longrightarrow\|T_{h}x-x\|\to 0
for all
x\in X
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be a positive semigroup on an OVS
X
with a generating cone. The following assertions are equivalent.

i)

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is order bounded at zero.

ii)

For every
x\in X
and
M\geq 0
the set
\{T_{h}x\}_{0\leq h\leq M}
is order bounded.

iii)

For every
x\in X_{+}
and
M\geq 0
the set
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq M}T_{s}[-x,x]
is order bounded.

iv)

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the set
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}T_{s}[-x,x]
is order bounded for every
x\in X_{+}
.

Proof.

i)\Longrightarrow ii)
Let
x\in X
and
M\geq 0
. By the assumption, there exist
a,b\in X
and
\varepsilon>0

satisfying
\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\subseteq[a,b]
. Since
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
,
\{T_{h}x\}_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\subseteq[-w,w]

for some
w\in X
. Then

\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq 2\varepsilon}=\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\bigcup T_{\varepsilon}\big(\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\big)\subseteq[-w-T_{\varepsilon}w,w+T_{\varepsilon}w],

and by repeating, for
n
such that
n\varepsilon\geq M
, we have

\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq M}\subseteq\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq n\varepsilon}\subseteq\left[-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{k\varepsilon}w,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{k\varepsilon}w\right].

ii)\Longrightarrow iii)
Let
x\in X_{+}
and
M\geq 0
.
Since
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
,
ii)
implies
\{T_{s}x\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\subseteq[-b,b]
for some
b\in X
.
Since
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is positive then
-b\leq-T_{s}x\leq T_{s}y\leq T_{s}x\leq b

for all
y\in[-x,x]
and
s\in[0,M]
, and hence
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq M}T_{s}[-x,x]\subseteq[-b,b]
.

iii)\Longrightarrow iv)
It is trivial.

iv)\Longrightarrow i)
Let
x\in X
. Find
\varepsilon>0
such that
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}T_{h}[-z,z]
is order bounded for every
z\in X_{+}
.
Since
X_{+}
is generating,
x\in[-z,z]
for some
z\in X
, and hence the set
\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}
is order bounded.
Since
x\in X
is arbitrary,
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is order bounded at zero.
∎

It is proved in
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
that, for each
ruc
at zero positive semigroup
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on a VL
X
,
the set
\{T_{s}x:0\leq s\leq M\}
is order bounded for every
M\geq 0
and
x\in X
.
By Proposition
2.4
, the same conclusion
holds for each order bounded at zero positive semigroup on an OVS with a generating cone.
In general, the assumption that the semigroup
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is positive is essential in Proposition
2.4
.
The next example shows that even a uniformly continuous
ruc
semigroup
(S_{s})_{s\geq 0}

on an order continuous Banach lattice
X
can be order bounded at zero yet
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}S_{s}[0,x]

is not order bounded for some
x\in X_{+}
and each
\varepsilon>0
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
E=\big(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty}\ell^{2^{n}}\big)_{0}
be the order continuous Banach lattice from the Krengel example

[
1
, Example 5.6]
. Consider the operator
T:E\to E
defined by
T(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)=({T_{1}}x_{1},{T_{2}}x_{2},\cdots)
,
where
T_{n}:\ell^{2^{n}}\to\ell^{2^{n}}
is invertable isometry from
[
1
, Example 5.6]
.
Clearly,
\|T\|=1
. However,
T
is not order bounded. Indeed, otherwise
|T|
would exists because
E
is Dedekind complete,
and then
|T|{\bf x}=(|{T_{1}}|x_{1},|{T_{2}}|x_{2},\cdots)
for every
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)\in E
. Take

x_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}e_{i}\in\ell^{2^{n}}\ \ \ \ \text{\rm and \ let}\ \ {\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)\in E.

[formula]

Denote by
M_{n}
an
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
matrix with all of its entries equal to
1
.
This leads to absurd, since

|T|{\bf x}=(|{T_{n}}|x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}|{T_{n}}|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}e_{i}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=

\left(\frac{1}{n}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}M_{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}e_{i}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}e_{i}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\notin E,

[formula]

because
\left\|\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}e_{i}\right)\right\|_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{2^{n}}}{n}\to\infty
.

Consider the order continuous Banach lattice
X=E\oplus E
, define a contraction
A:X\to X
by
A({\bf f},{\bf g})=(0,T{\bf f})
,
and set
S_{s}=e^{sA}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{l}A^{l}}{l!}
for
s\geq 0
.
(S_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is a uniformly continuous semigroup,
and
S_{s}=I_{X}+sA
for
s\geq 0
because
A^{2}=0
.
Then
(S_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is
ruc
since, for
({\bf f},{\bf g})\in X=E\oplus E
and
s_{0}\geq 0
,

S_{s_{0}+h}({\bf f},{\bf g})-S_{s_{0}}({\bf f},{\bf g})=(I_{X}+(s_{0}+h)A-(I_{X}+s_{0}A))({\bf f},{\bf g})=

hA({\bf f},{\bf g})=h(0,T{\bf f})\convr 0

with regulator
(0,T{\bf f})
as
s_{0}+h\geq 0
and
h\to 0
. As
ru
-converging nets are eventually order bounded,

(S_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is also order bounded at zero.

Note that the set
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}S_{s}[0,({\bf x},0)]
is not order bounded in
X
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
where
{\bf x}\in X
is as in
(1)
. Otherwise, the set
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}sA[0,({\bf x},0)]

would be order bounded in
E
for some
\varepsilon>0
, and hence
T[-{\bf x},{\bf x}]
must be order bounded in
E
. Then

(|{T_{n}}|x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in({T_{n}}[-x_{n},x_{n}])_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq T[-{\bf x},{\bf x}]
which is absurd because

(|{T_{n}}|x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\notin E
by
(2)
.

The following theorem generalizes
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
by dropping positivity of the semigroup in favor of the condition
that the semigroup contains infinitely many order bounded operators in every neighborhood of zero.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be a semigroup on an OVS
X
with a generating cone such that,
for some sequence
r_{k}\downarrow 0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
every
T_{r_{k}}
is order bounded.
Then the set
\{T_{s}x:0\leq s\leq M\}
is order bounded for every
M\geq 0
and
x\in X
if one of the following two conditions holds.

i)

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is
ruc
at zero.

ii)

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is order continuous at zero.

Proof.

Take arbitrary
M\geq 0
and
x\in X
.

i)
Since
T_{h}x\convr x
as
h\downarrow 0
, there exist
u\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
satisfying
\pm(T_{h}x-x)\leq u
for all
h\in[0,\varepsilon]
.
Pick any
r_{k_{\varepsilon}}\in(0,\varepsilon)
and take
n
with
nr_{k_{\varepsilon}}\geq M
.
Since
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
then
\pm x\leq w
for some
w\in X_{+}
.
Thus,
\pm T_{h}x\leq a_{1}:=u+w
for
0\leq h\leq\varepsilon
, and hence
\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}\subseteq[-a_{1},a_{1}]
.
Since
T_{r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}
is order bounded and
X_{+}
is generating,

\{T_{h}x:0\leq h\leq 2r_{k_{\varepsilon}}\}=T_{r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}\Big(\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}\Big)\subseteq T_{r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}[-a_{1},a_{1}]\subseteq[-a_{2},a_{2}]

for some
a_{2}\in X_{+}
. It follows
\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq{r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}}\subseteq[-(a_{1}+a_{2}),a_{1}+a_{2}]
.
Repeating n-times the argument, we obtain
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}\in X_{+}
with

\{T_{h}x:0\leq h\leq M\}\subseteq\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq n{r_{k_{\varepsilon}}}}\subseteq\left[-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right].

ii)
Take
M\geq 0
and
x\in X
. Since
T_{h}x\convo x
as
h\downarrow 0
, there exists a net
g_{\beta}\downarrow 0
in
X
,
such that for each
\beta
there is
k_{\beta}
with
\pm(T_{h}x-x)\leq g_{\beta}
for
0\leq h\leq r_{k_{\beta}}
.
Fix
\beta_{0}
and take
w\geq\pm x
. Then
\pm T_{h}x\leq a_{1}:=g_{\beta_{0}}+w
for
h\in[0,r_{k_{\beta_{0}}}]
, and hence

\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq s\leq r_{k_{\beta_{0}}}}\subseteq[-a_{1},a_{1}]
.
Let
nr_{k_{\beta_{0}}}\geq M
. Taking in account that
T_{r_{k_{\beta_{0}}}}
is order bounded and arguing as in the proof of
i)
,
we obtain that
\big\{T_{h}x\big\}_{0\leq h\leq M}\subseteq\left[-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right]

for some
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}\in X_{+}
.
∎

Later, in Theorem

An unusual family of supersingular curves of genus five
Dušan Dragutinović

Abstract
. We construct a family of smooth supersingular curves of genus 5 5 in characteristic 2 2 with several notable features: its dimension matches the expected dimension of any component of the supersingular locus in genus 5 5 , its members are non-hyperelliptic curves with non-trivial automorphism groups, and each curve in the family admits a double cover structure over both an elliptic curve and a genus- 2 2 curve. We also provide an explicit parametrization of this family.



1.
Introduction

An elliptic curve
E
defined over a field
k
of positive characteristic
p>0

\#E[p](\overline{k})=1
; that is, if the neutral element is the only
p
-torsion point of

\mathcal{J}_{C}\to E^{g}
defined over
\overline{k}
, where
\mathcal{J}_{C}
is the Jacobian of

and

is any supersingular elliptic curve over

. A famous result of van der Geer and van der Vlugt (
[
vdGvdV95
, Theorem 2.1]
) shows that for every

, there exists a smooth supersingular curve

of genus

defined over
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. In this article, we study the locus
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\mathrm{ss}}
of supersingular curves in the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of smooth genus-
g
curves over
k
in the case
g=5
and
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. For
g=4
, it was shown in
[
Dra24b
, Theorem 3.4]
that
\mathcal{M}_{4}^{\mathrm{ss}}
is pure of dimension
3
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.

Before presenting our results, we summarize a few properties relevant for our discussion:

•

In
[
vdGvdV95
, Proposition 4.4]
a positive-dimensional family
\mathcal{F}_{GV,g}
of supersingular curves

over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}

In particular,

.

•

If the supersingular locus

in
\mathcal{M}_{5}
is non-empty in characteristic
p
(which is true for

), any component of
\mathcal{M}_{5}^{\mathrm{ss}}
has dimension at least

, and the expected dimension of

equals

Namely, by

, the supersingular locus

, where

, and it would be exactly

•

By a conjecture of Oort (
[
EMO01

), given

, the generic point of every component of the supersingular locus

. So far, this conjecture was shown to be true for

, for

if

, and

if

for any

; see
[
Ibu20
]
,
[

]
,
[
Oor91
]

[
KYY21
]
,
[

,
[

. An analog of this conjecture for curves would state the following:
Assuming that the generic point of a component of the supersingular locus of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
corresponds to a non-hyperelliptic curve, its automorphism group is trivial.

Curves of genus

can be either hyperelliptic, trigonal, or non-hyperelliptic and non-trigonal, whose canonical model in

Using this classification, the data of all smooth curves of genus

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
5
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
whose canonical model in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
is given by
C=V(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
, where

(1.1)

\begin{cases}q_{1}=X^{2}+YZ,\\
q_{2}=T^{2}+ZT+XY,\\
q_{3}=U^{2}+UZ+b_{1}ZT+b_{2}XZ+b_{3}XT+b_{3}^{2}YT,\end{cases}

for some

. Then

is supersingular and satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(C)
;

(2)

There exists a double cover
C\to E
, where
E
is an elliptic curve;

(3)

There exists a double cover
C\to D
,
where
D
is a smooth curve of genus
2
.

(4)

The

-number of
C
equals

, where

Furthermore, let

denote the family which consists of the isomorphism classes of all curves

.

Note that

equals the expected dimension of the supersingular locus

and

. Furthermore, the generic point of

has a non-trivial automorphism group. Therefore, if the closure

of

turns out to be a component of

, it would provide a counterexample to the analog of Oort’s conjecture in the case

and

. While we were not able to decide this, we comment more on this possibility in Corollary

. Also, it is worth noting that

is a sublocus of the loci of double covers of both elliptic curves and curves of genus

.
Lastly, note that by
[

, Corollary 10.3]
, the generic point of every component of
\mathcal{S}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
has

-number
1
for any

and

, whereas the

-number of

equals

, resulting in another unusual property of

. We also present further discussion after proving this theorem in Section

.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Invariants of curves in positive characteristic

Let
K
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and let
\sigma:K\to K
the Frobenius of
K
. In the case when
K
is algebraically closed, we will denote it by
K=k
.
Furthermore, let
C
be a smooth curve over an algebraically closed field
k
of genus
g\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{J}_{C}
be its Jacobian, which is a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
g
over
k
.

2.1.1.
The
p
-rank and the
a
-number of a smooth curve

Let

(2.1)

F:H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})

be the
\sigma
-linear Frobenius operator on
C
.
The
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
of
C
, denoted by
f(C)
, is the semisimple rank of
F
, i.e.,

f(C)=\mathrm{rank}_{k}(F^{g}\mid_{H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})}),\quad 0\leq f(C)\leq g,

while the
a
-number of
C
, denoted by
a(C)
, is the number

a(C)=g-\mathrm{rank}_{k}(F\mid_{H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})}),\quad 0\leq a(C)\leq g.

Equivalently, one can define the
p
-rank of
C
as the number
0\leq f=f(C)\leq g
such that
{\#\mathcal{J}_{C}[p](k)=p^{f}}
. In this article, we will usually write

2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C)=f(C)

for the
2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
of a curve
C
defined over
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.

Given a matrix
M
, we denote by
M^{\sigma^{n}}
the matrix obtained from
M
by raising each of its entries to the
p^{n}
-th power, so if
M=(m_{ij})
, then
M^{\sigma^{n}}=(m_{ij}^{p^{n}})
,
n\geq 1
. In the case when
C
is a smooth curve, the matrix of the Frobenius operator (
2.1
) with respect to a fixed basis of
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
is called the Hasse-Witt matrix, denoted by
H=H_{C}
; the Hasse-Witt matrix of a smooth curve
C
is unique up to relation
H\sim SH(S^{\sigma})^{-1},
corresponding to the changes of bases of
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
induced by transition matrices
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{g}(k)
. If
H
is the Hasse-Witt matrix of
F
as in (
2.1
), then the operator
F^{n}=F\circ\ldots\circ F
is represented by the matrix

H\cdot H^{\sigma}\cdot\ldots\cdot H^{\sigma^{n}},n\geq 1.

In particular,

f(C)=\mathrm{rank}_{k}(H\cdot H^{\sigma}\cdot\ldots\cdot H^{\sigma^{g}})\quad\text{and}\quad a(C)=g-\mathrm{rank}_{k}(H).

2.1.2.
The Newton polygon of a smooth curve and supersingular curves

Let
\mathcal{J}_{C}[p^{\infty}]=\varinjlim\mathcal{J}_{C}[p^{n}]
be the
p
-divisible group of
\mathcal{J}_{C}
. By the Dieudonné-Manin classification
[
Man63
]
, there are certain
p
-divisible groups
G_{c,d}
for
c,d\geq 0
relatively prime integers and there is an isogeny of
p
-divisible groups

\mathcal{J}_{C}[p^{\infty}]\sim\bigoplus_{\lambda=\frac{d}{c+d}}G_{c,d},

for a unique choice of
slopes

\lambda
. If a slope
\lambda
appears in this decomposition with multiplicity
m
, then so does the slope
1-\lambda
. The Newton polygon of
C
is defined as the collection
\xi(C)
of those
\lambda
counted with multiplicities. We usually think of Newton polygon
\xi(C)
of
C
as the
connected union of finitely many line segments in the plane, which is lower convex and breaks at points with integer coordinates, starting at
(0,0)
and ending at
(2g,g)
. The slopes
\lambda
are the slopes of these line segments.

If the Newton polygon
\xi(C)
is a straight line starting at
(0,0)
and ending at
(2g,g)
, or altenatively if all slopes of
\xi(C)
equal
\frac{1}{2}
, we say that
C
is
supersingular
. Alternatively, it follows from
[
LO98
, 1.4]
that a smooth curve
C
of genus
g
over a field
K
is supersingular if and only if there exists an isogeny

\mathcal{J}_{C}\overset{\sim}{\to}E^{g}

over
k=\overline{K}
, where
E
is any supersingular elliptic curve over
k
.

The number of slopes
0
in
\xi(C)
equals the
p
-rank of
C
. Consequently, if
C
is supersingular, then
f(C)=0
. The reverse implication holds for
g=2
, but does not necessarily hold for
g\geq 3
.

2.1.3.
The purity of de Jong and Oort

By the purity theorem of de Jong and Oort, if a family
\mathcal{F}
of smooth curves of genus
g
with constant Newton polygon
\xi
contains in its closure in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
elements with a different Newton polygon, then this already occurs in codimension
1
. That is, there exists a family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
of dimension
\dim\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\dim\mathcal{F}-1
, whose generic Newton polygon is
\xi^{\prime}\neq\xi
, such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subsetneq\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. This result is well known and holds much more generally; for the precise statement, see
[
dJO00
, Theorem 4.1]
. A similar result also holds for the
p
-rank (and can in fact be seen as a consequence of de Jong–Oort purity for suitably chosen Newton polygons).

Given a perfect field

c_{-2}
denote the coefficients of
x_{2}^{-1}
and
x_{2}^{-2}
in
F_{2}
, respectively. Clearly,
c_{-1}^{2}=c_{-2}
, and therefore, the function field of
C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle
equals

\kappa(C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle)=k(x,y)/(y^{2}+y+F_{3}(x)),

where
x=x_{2}
,
y=y_{2}+c_{-1}x^{-1}
, and
F_{3}(x)\in k[x]
is a polynomial of degree
5
. We conclude that
C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle
is a smooth curve of genus
2
.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem and some remarks

We now prove Theorem
1.1
, the main result of this article, and provide some further discussion.
The formulation presented in Section
1
follows after relabeling
b_{1}=b_{8}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
5
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
whose canonical model in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
is given by
C=V(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
, where

(4.1)

\begin{cases}q_{1}=X^{2}+YZ,\\
q_{2}=XY+ZT+T^{2},\\
q_{3}=b_{2}XZ+b_{3}XT+b_{3}^{2}YT+b_{8}ZT+ZU+U^{2},\end{cases}

for some
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in k
,
b_{3}\neq 0
. Then
C
is supersingular and satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(C)
;

(2)

There exists a double cover
C\to E
, where
E
is an elliptic curve;

(3)

There exists a double cover
C\to D
,
where
D
is a smooth curve of genus
2
;

(4)

The
a
-number of
C
equals
a(C)=2
.

Furthermore, let
\mathcal{S}
denote the family which consists of the isomorphism classes of all curves
C
as in (
4.1
). Then
\dim\mathcal{S}=3
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
it follows that
2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C)=0
. By the second part of Proposition
3.3
, it follows that
\left\langle\iota\right\rangle\times\left\langle\eta\right\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(C)
, where
\iota:U\mapsto U+Z
and
\eta:T\mapsto T+Z
,
U\mapsto U+b_{3}X+g_{2}Z
are two automorphisms of order
2
, where
g_{2}\in k
is such that
g_{2}^{2}+g_{2}=b_{8}
. By Proposition
3.4
,
C\to C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle
is a double cover of an elliptic curve, while
C\to~C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle
and
C\to C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle
are two double covers of genus
2
curves.

Using the properties above, we can now prove that
C
is a supersingular curve. Consider the quotient maps

C\to C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle,\quad C\to C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle,\quad\text{and}\quad C\to C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle.

The pullbacks of
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle}
,
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}
, and
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle}
to
\mathcal{J}_{C}
are subvarieties of
\mathcal{J}_{C}
. The induced automorphism
\eta^{*}
acts trivially on the pullback of
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}
but not on the pullbacks of
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle}
(otherwise,
C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle
would have genus
2
) or
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle}
(by Proposition
3.4
).
Consequently, the intersection of the pullback of
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}
to
\mathcal{J}_{C}
with the pullbacks of
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle}
and
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle}
is
0
-dimensional. Similarly, the intersection of the pullbacks of
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle}
and
\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle}
to
\mathcal{J}_{C}
is
0
-dimensional.
Hence, there is an isogeny

(4.2)

\mathcal{J}_{C}\sim\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle}\oplus\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}\oplus\mathcal{J}_{C/\left\langle\iota\circ\eta\right\rangle}.

Finally, all the quotient curves are supersingular since they are of genus
g\in\{1,2\}
and have
2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}=0
, taking into account that
2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C)=0
. Hence,
C
is supersingular.

Let us prove the remaining claims. If we denote by
H
the Hasse-Witt matrix of
C
as in (
2.2
), it follows that
a(C)=5-\mathrm{rank}_{k}(H)=2
. Lastly, consider the family
\mathcal{S}
consisting of all isomorphism classes of curves
C
as in (
4.1
), for
b_{2},b_{8}\in k
,
b_{3}\in k^{*}
. As a consequence of the first part of Proposition
3.3
, it follows that
\dim\mathcal{S}=3
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In fact, we can avoid using the condition
{2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C)=0}
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. From the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we observe that
C/\langle\iota\rangle
is the elliptic curve defined by the equation
y^{2}+y=x^{3}
, which is the unique supersingular elliptic curve in characteristic
2
, up to isomorphism. Curves
C/\langle\eta\rangle
and
C/\langle\iota\circ\eta\rangle
are both hyperelliptic curves of genus
2
given by equations of the form

, where
F(x)\in k[x]
is a polynomial of degree
5
. Moreover, they are both supersingular since
[
SZ02
, Proposition 4.1]
implies that

2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C/\langle\eta\rangle)=2\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C/\langle\iota\circ\eta\rangle)=0.

Finally, the isogeny (
4.2
) shows that
C
itself is supersingular.

Remark 4.3
.

Another way to conclude that
a(C)=2
for curves
C
as in Theorem
1.1
is as follows. By
[
FL08
, Lemma 1.2]
, any such curve

admits an even theta characteristic

, i.e., a line bundle on

, where

is the canonical bundle, and such that

is a component of
\mathcal{M}_{5}^{\mathrm{ss}}
in

.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{5}
be the family of smooth curves of genus
5
over
k
whose canonical model in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
is given by
\{q_{1}=0,q_{2}=0,q_{3}=0\}
, where
q_{1}=X^{2}+YZ
,
{q_{2}=XY+ZT+T^{2}}
, and
q_{3}
as in (
3.3
), for some
b_{2},b_{3},b_{8}\in k
and
b_{6}\in k^{*}
, and let
\mathcal{S}
be the sublocus of
\mathcal{R}
defined by
b_{6}=b_{3}^{2}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{S}
is as in Theorem
4.1
). Then the following properties hold:

(1)

The generic point of the
4
-dimensional locus
\mathcal{R}
corresponds to a curve whose Newton polygon has slopes
\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}
, and
\frac{2}{3}
.

(2)

At least one of the following two holds:

•

There is a component
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{5}^{\mathrm{ss}}
in characteristic
2
with
\dim\Gamma\geq 4
; or

•

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{5}
is an intersection of
\mathcal{R}
and a
4
-dimensional locus whose generic Newton polygon has slopes
\frac{2}{5}
and
\frac{3}{5}
; in this case,
\mathcal{S}
is a component of the supersingular locus of
\mathcal{M}_{5}
with a non-trivial generic automorphism group.

Proof.

As we discussed in Proposition
3.2
and the first part of Proposition
3.3
,
\mathcal{R}
is irreducible and
\dim\mathcal{R}=4
. By Theorem
4.1
,
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
is a divisor whose generic Newton polygon is supersingular. Therefore, by the purity of de Jong-Oort (see Subsection
2.1.3
), the generic Newton polygon of
\mathcal{R}
will be the Newton polygon of any curve corresponding to an element of
\mathcal{R}-\mathcal{S}
. Consider
C
as in (

) with

and

. We compute

, which implies that the slopes of the Newton polygon of

\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}
; see, for example,
[
Dra24b
, Section 2]
.

Using de Jong–Oort purity again, we see that
\mathcal{R}
cannot be a component of the Newton polygon locus in
\mathcal{M}_{5}
with generic slopes
\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}
, since every such component must have dimension at least
5
. This follows from

Oor01
, Theorem 4.2]

.
Therefore, there is a component

\mathcal{S}

in

∎

Molecular mechanism of Yishen Qingzhuo oral liquid in treating chronic renal failure via the Nrf2/HO-1–mediated ferroptosis pathway
Authors: Aiping Zhao, Bishan Chen, Chu Lin, Weiming Wang, Hang Su, Yuliang Qiu, Wenjie Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the therapeutic effect and underlying mechanism of Yishen Qingzhuo oral liquid (YSQZ) in a rat model of chronic renal failure (CRF). The CRF model was established by 5/6 nephrectomy. Rats were randomly divided into the sham, CRF, and CRF + low/high-dose YSQZ groups (CRF+YSQZ-L/H). Based on the therapeutic effect, the optimal drug dosage was selected. Subsequently, a ferroptosis inducer (erastin) and Nrf2 inhibitor (ML385) were administered, and rats were further divided into the CRF, CRF+YSQZ, CRF+YSQZ+erastin, and CRF+YSQZ+ML385 groups. The CRF group exhibited impaired renal function, along with elevated urinary protein and ferrous ion levels, relative to those in the sham group. Oxidative stress markers were also dysregulated. Histopathological damage was severe in the CRF group, including increased iron deposition, reduced mitochondrial counts, decreased or even complete loss of mitochondrial cristae, and extensive rupture of the mitochondrial outer membrane, indicators of ferroptosis. The expression of Nrf2/HO-1 pathway-related proteins was decreased, whereas fibrosis-related proteins were upregulated. Whereas the CRF group exhibited marked renal impairment, the CRF+YSQZ-L and CRF+YSQZ-H groups displayed significant improvement in renal function. These treatment groups also exhibited reductions in renal pathological damage, iron deposition, and ferroptosis. The expression of proteins related to the Nrf2/HO-1 ferroptosis pathway was increased, whereas fibrosis-associated proteins were downregulated, with more pronounced effects observed in the high-dose group. However, these protective effects of YSQZ were reversed by co-treatment with erastin or ML385. YSQZ exerts therapeutic effects in rats with CRF, likely by inhibiting ferroptosis through activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway.Keywords: Yishen Qingzhuo oral liquid, chronic renal failure, ferroptosis, Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway, oxidative stress

Introduction

A recent epidemiological survey of 220 studies estimated that approximately 160 million adults in China have chronic kidney disease (CKD), representing the highest prevalence in Asia [1]. A cross-sectional survey of inpatients from tertiary hospitals found that CKD is associated with prolonged hospitalization and increased mortality [2]. CKD progression results in irreversible glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial fibrosis, ultimately leading to chronic renal failure (CRF). Once CRF advances to end-stage renal disease, renal replacement therapy becomes essential to sustain life, profoundly impairing quality of life and survival while imposing a heavy economic burden on families and society.

Current treatment options for CRF are limited, and many patients ultimately require dialysis. CRF is challenging to manage, and it carries a poor prognosis; however, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has displayed promise in delaying disease progression and improving outcomes. Yishen Qingzhuo oral liquid (YSQZ), an in-hospital preparation derived from a TCM formula, has demonstrated clinical efficacy in improving renal function. Our previous work suggested that this effect might be mediated through the activation of the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)/heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting oxidative stress [3,4].

Nrf2 is a crucial transcription factor that, upon activation, dissociates from its inhibitor Keap1 and translocates to the nucleus to upregulate antioxidant genes such as HO-1, enhancing cellular defense [5,6]. Beyond alleviating oxidative stress and renal damage [7], the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is a key regulator of ferroptosis, inhibiting this process by modulating iron metabolism and lipid peroxidation, thereby protecting against chronic kidney disease [8–11]. Several studies reported the occurrence of ferroptosis in 5/6 nephrectomy-induced CKD models and demonstrated that dysregulated iron metabolism is a major contributor to this process [12,13]. Among Nrf2’s diverse functions in kidney diseases, its regulation of oxidative stress is most closely associated with ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of cell death driven by lipid peroxidation [14,15].

Given the established role of Nrf2 as a promising but challenging potential therapeutic target in CRF and the close association between ferroptosis and Nrf2 signaling, this study investigated whether YSQZ exerts its renoprotective effects in a 5/6 nephrectomy rat model of CRF by modulating the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway to inhibit ferroptosis.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals

SPF male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (200 ± 20 g) were provided by Sprague-Dawley (Beijing) Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (license number: SCXK (Beijing) 2019-0010). All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the principles of Laboratory Animal Care and approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (No.: 2023169).

Experimental drug

YSQZ (Min Yao Zhi Zi: Z20150009) was prepared and provided by the Affiliated People’s Hospital of Fujian University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The formulation contains Semen Plantaginis, Radix Cyathulae, Radix et Rhizoma Rhei, Poria, Cortex Phellodendri, Herba Pyrolae chinensis, Cortex Moutan, Herba Taxilli, Rhizoma Dioscoreae, Fructus Corni, Rhizoma Anemarrhee, Radix Rehmanniae, and Herba Serissae.

Reagents and equipment

The following reagents and equipment were obtained from commercial suppliers: creatinine test kit (70127, Shandong Boco Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), MDA ELISA kit (E-EL-0060c, Wuhan Elabscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), glutathione kit (E-BC-K030-M, Wuhan Elabscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), ferrous ion colorimetric test kit (E-BC-K773-M, Wuhan Elabscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), hematoxylin (ZLI-9610, Beijing Zhongshan Jinqiao Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), eosin (G1100, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.), Masson trichrome (G1006, Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd.), Prussian blue stain kit (G1422, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.). Fully automatic biochemical analyzer (BK-600, Shandong Bo Ke Instrument Co., Ltd.), fluorescence microscope (CKX53, Olympus, Japan), transmission electron microscope (JEM-1400, JEOL Ltd.), and fully automatic chemiluminescence image analysis system (Tanon-5200, Shanghai Tianneng Technology Co., Ltd.).

Development of the rat model of CRF

After adaptive feeding for 1 week, SD rats were anesthetized via an intraperitoneal injection of 20% urethane solution (1.2 g/kg). One-third of the upper and inferior poles of the left kidney were excised in a fan-shaped manner, and the residual kidney was placed back into the abdomen. After 1 week, the right kidneys from the anesthetized rats were then excised. In the sham operation group, only the right and left kidney capsules were removed.

Grouping and drug administration

Animal experiment 1: SD rats were randomly divided into four groups (n = 10/group): sham, CRF, CRF+YSQZ-L, and CRF+YSQZ-H groups. The sham and CRF groups received distilled water (1 mL/100 g) by gavage. Starting 2 weeks after surgery, rats in the CRF+YQ-L/H group received YSQZ by gavage (3 mL/kg·day for the low-dose group and 6 mL/kg·day for the high-dose group) for 8 weeks.

The sham and CRF groups received distilled water (1 mL/100 g) by gavage. Starting 2 weeks after surgery, rats in the CRF+YQ-L/H group received YSQZ by gavage (3 mL/kg·day for the low-dose group and 6 mL/kg·day for the high-dose group) for 8 weeks.

Animal experiment 2: The drug dosage was selected according to the results of Animal Experiment 1. SD rats were randomly divided into four groups (n = 10/group): CRF, CRF+YSQZ, CRF+YSQZ+erastin, and CRF+YSQZ+ML385 groups. The CRF and CRF+YSQZ groups received the same treatment as described in Experiment 1. In the CRF+YSQZ+erastin group, erastin (10 mg/kg) was injected intraperitoneally concurrently with YSQZ treatment. In the CRF+YSQZ+ML385 group, ML385 (30 mg/kg) was injected intraperitoneally at the same time as YSQZ treatment. The doses of erastin and ML385 were referenced from previous studies [16,17].

SD rats were randomly divided into four groups (n = 10/group): CRF, CRF+YSQZ, CRF+YSQZ+erastin, and CRF+YSQZ+ML385 groups. The CRF and CRF+YSQZ groups received the same treatment as described in Experiment 1. In the CRF+YSQZ+erastin group, erastin (10 mg/kg) was injected intraperitoneally concurrently with YSQZ treatment. In the CRF+YSQZ+ML385 group, ML385 (30 mg/kg) was injected intraperitoneally at the same time as YSQZ treatment. The doses of erastin and ML385 were referenced from previous studies [16,17].

Specimen collection

After drug administration, all rats were placed in metabolic cages and fasted for 24 h. Urine was collected the following day. After anesthesia, blood was collected from the abdominal aorta. After standing for 30 min, the blood samples were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to obtain serum. After euthanasia, the left kidney tissues were collected from each rat. A portion of the kidney tissue was rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C. Another portion was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, and the remaining tissue was fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde solution.

Detection of biochemical indices

Serum and urine samples were collected (n = 10), and malondialdehyde (MDA) and glutathione (GSH) levels were measured according to the kit instructions. Blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine (SCr), and urine protein levels were measured using an automatic biochemical analyzer. Ferrous ion (Fe2+) levels were measured in kidney tissue according to the kit instructions.

Hematoxylin–eosin (HE) and Masson staining

Rat kidney tissue paraffin sections (n = 5) were baked, dewaxed, rehydrated, stained with hematoxylin for 3–5 min, rinsed with running water, differentiated with 1% hydrochloric acid alcohol, blued using bluing solution, and stained with eosin for 3–5 min. The sections were dehydrated, mounted, and observed under a microscope. Using Masson’s trichrome staining, and the collagen fiber area relative to the tissue area was measured, and semi-quantitative analysis was used to evaluate fibrosis. The ratio of the collagen fiber area to the total tissue area was calculated to assess renal interstitial fibrosis.

Iron content determination

Fe2+ levels in renal homogenates were quantified colorimetrically. Tissue samples were processed by homogenization in extraction buffer followed by centrifugation. A standard curve was prepared by diluting a commercial iron standard. Samples and standards were reacted with an equal volume of chromogenic agent, incubated at 37 °C, and centrifuged. The absorbance of the resultant Fe2+–probe complex in the supernatant was measured at 593 nm. Sample Fe2+ concentrations were determined via reference to the standard curve.

Prussian blue staining

Rat kidney tissue sections (n = 5) were placed in Perls’ working solution for 15–30 min, rinsed thoroughly with distilled water for 2–5 min, placed in nuclear fast red staining solution to lightly stain the nuclei, dehydrated, cleared, mounted with neutral gum, and observed under a microscope. Iron deposition was evaluated by calculating the ratio of the integrated optical density of positive staining to that of the analyzed tissue area.

Transmission electron microscopy

Rat kidney tissue was fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde for 2 h and then fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide for 2 h. The tissue was dehydrated at 4 °C. After acetone treatment, the tissue was embedded with epoxy resin, and after solidification, it was sliced using an ultramicrotome. Finally, the tissue was stained with 3% uranyl acetate and lead citrate and then observed under a transmission electron microscope.

Western blotting

Rat kidney tissue samples (n = 3) were homogenized in cell lysis buffer and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was collected, and the protein concentration was determined using a BCA protein assay kit. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 3% skim milk blocking solution for 1 h and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against Nrf2, HO-1, glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11), alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and collagen type I alpha 1 chain (COL1A1). After three washes, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 2 h. Then, the membranes were again washed three times. Finally, the membranes were soaked with enhanced chemiluminescence reagent and then visualized using an automated chemiluminescence imaging system to detect the expression of the target proteins. The antibodies used were mouse anti-β-actin (1:2,000, HC201, Beijing TransGen Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) (1:2,000, GB23301, Wuhan ServiceBio Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China), rabbit anti-Nrf2 (1:1,000, AF0639, Affinity Biosciences, Cincinnati, OH, USA), rabbit anti-HO-1 (1:1,000, bs-2075R, Beijing Bioson Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), rabbit anti-GPX4 (1:1,000, DF6701, Affinity Biosciences), rabbit anti-SLC7A11 (1:1,000, 26864-1-AP, Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA), rabbit anti-α-SMA (1:2,000, 14395-1-AP, Proteintech), and rabbit anti-COL1A1 (1:1,000, DF3555, Affinity Biosciences).

Statistical analysis

SPSS 27.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analysis, and GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad, Boston, MA, USA) was used to generate graphs. The quantitative data among multiple groups were tested for normality, and one-way ANOVA was used for comparisons. For post-hoc pairwise comparisons, the Bonferroni test was applied to data with homogeneous variance, whereas Tamhane’s T2 test was used for data with unequal variances. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that YSQZ ameliorates renal injury and dysfunction in a CRF rat model. Treatment with YSQZ reduced histopathological damage, inflammatory cell infiltration, fibrosis, oxidative stress, and iron deposition. Critically, the protective effects of YSQZ exhibited a clear dose dependency. This consistent dose–response relationship both strengthens the causal link between YSQZ treatment and the observed benefits and suggests that the underlying mechanisms are saturable and improvable.

Our findings strongly implicate the inhibition of ferroptosis as a key mechanism underlying YSQZ’s efficacy. Numerous studies revealed that ferroptosis plays an important role in the progression of kidney diseases [18–21]. Although CRF can lead to reduced iron bioavailability, renal cells remain vulnerable to iron overload. Iron deposition in kidney tissue can induce oxidative damage and pathological responses, disrupt cellular structural integrity and tissue homeostasis, and ultimately cause cell death [22]. Compared with other forms of cell death, ferroptosis is mainly characterized by iron overload and lipid peroxide accumulation [23]. GPX4, a member of the glutathione peroxidase family, regulates ferroptosis by catalyzing the reduction of lipid hydroperoxides to nontoxic lipid alcohols using GSH. Its inactivation leads to lipid peroxides accumulation [24]. SLC7A11, a protein that constitutes the glutamate–cystine antiporter, promotes the conversion of cystine to cysteine, which is involved in GSH synthesis. It is also an important regulator of ferroptosis [25]. Under transmission electron microscopy, we observed a decreased number of mitochondria and the reduction or complete disappearance of mitochondrial cristae in the CRF model group, along with widespread outer membrane rupture, indicating ferroptosis. Prussian blue staining revealed increased iron deposition, elevated Fe2+ levels, and significantly decreased expression of the ferroptosis-related proteins GPX4 and SLC7A11, all of which strongly indicate the occurrence of ferroptosis. Wang et al. [12] also reported ferroptosis in the same CRF animal model. YSQZ treatment effectively reversed these ferroptotic features in a dose-dependent manner.

Crucially, the protective effects of YSQZ were abolished by co-treatment with the ferroptosis inducer erastin or the Nrf2 inhibitor ML385, confirming the functional involvement of both ferroptosis and the Nrf2 pathway. The regulatory pathways of ferroptosis include the lipid metabolism, iron metabolism, and p62/Keap1/Nrf2 pathways. The pathways regulating GSH/GPX4 include the p53, AMPK/xc, and glutamine metabolism pathways [26]. Among these, Nrf2 is widely considered critical for regulating ferroptosis [27]. Our previous clinical and experimental studies confirmed that Nrf2 is a key target for drug therapy in CRF. In recent years, Nrf2-induced expression of HO-1 has been considered an important cellular anti-oxidative stress mechanisms [28]. In this study, we further explored the possible mechanism of ferroptosis in CRF by focusing on the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Although HO-1 is a classical downstream target of Nrf2, it might also be regulated by other transcription factors [4]. Furthermore, HO-1 expression appears to be influenced by the timing of sample collection following model establishment. In this study, the CRF model was maintained for approximately 8 weeks. Nrf2 and HO-1 expression was decreased in the established CRF model, whereas their expression was increased after drug intervention. After adding Nrf2 inhibitors, renal pathological damage and ferroptosis were aggravated. Correspondingly, the expression of the ferroptosis-related proteins, Nrf2 and HO-1, and levels of oxidative stress markers were all significantly altered. In other words, the Nrf2 inhibitor weakened the effect of YSQZ, and this effect was accompanied by the suppression of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway and the enhancement of ferroptosis. Therefore, we infer that the drug can inhibit ferroptosis by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Furthermore, similar results were observed upon the addition of the ferroptosis inducer erastin, which also affected the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Erastin is known to induce ferroptosis by inhibiting Nrf2/HO-1 signaling, a mechanism that has been validated in various models. A prior study reported that erastin induced ferroptosis in cervical cancer cells through the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway [29]. In a rat model of myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury, erastin suppressed the drug-induced upregulation of Nrf2 and HO-1 [30]. It is important to note that targeting NRF2 therapeutically is complex because of its pleiotropic roles, and previous clinical trials with NRF2 activators faced challenges related to efficacy and safety, highlighting the need for more nuanced therapeutic strategies.

It is noteworthy that HO-1 plays a complex dual role in the regulation of ferroptosis. On the one hand, HO-1 exacerbates intracellular iron overload during heme degradation, ultimately shifting to promote ferroptosis. HO-1 catalyzes the degradation of heme into free iron, bilirubin, and other byproducts. As reported in the literature, the HO-1 inhibitor zinc protoporphyrin IX significantly attenuated doxorubicin-induced myocardial injury in mice, suggesting that the free iron from heme degradation is involved in myocardial damage [31]. On the other hand, HO-1 exerts potent antioxidant effects through its catalytic products biliverdin/bilirubin and carbon monoxide, effectively scavenging lipid peroxides and thereby inhibiting the occurrence of ferroptosis. For example, HO-1 counteracted ferroptosis in a chronic kidney disease model induced by a high-fat, high-fructose diet feeding [32]. Similar findings were reported in adenine- and 5/6 nephrectomy-induced CRF rat models [33]. This has been demonstrated as a primary protective mechanism in various models, including chronic kidney disease [15,34,35].

Nrf2 activation occurs upstream as a prerequisite for the resurrection of GPX4 and SLC7A11 following YSQZ treatment. GPX4 is a key regulatory protein involved in ferroptosis. As a transcription factor, Nrf2 can upregulate GPX4 and protect cells from ferroptosis [36]. Nrf2 also promotes the expression of SLC7A11, which enhances intracellular GSH synthesis. However, when SLC7A11 is inhibited, GSH synthesis is impaired, GPX4 loses its ability to eliminate lipid peroxides, and ferroptosis is triggered [25]. In other models of kidney injury, ferroptosis was linked to Nrf2 and its downstream target proteins. For example, a study by Lin et al. [37] demonstrated that xanthine alleviated hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced injury in renal tubular epithelial cells, which might have been related to the upregulation of GPX4 and SLC7A11 and inhibition of ferroptosis. Similarly, Song et al. [13] found that activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway and increases in GPX4 expression could attenuate ferroptosis induced via unilateral ureteral obstruction and unilateral ischemia/reperfusion in renal tubular epithelial cells, whereas Nrf2 knockout abolished this protective effect. Furthermore, this is consistent with existing research confirming that the promoters of both the GPX4 and SLC7A11 genes contain antioxidant response elements, making them direct transcriptional targets of Nrf2 [38]. This study revealed that YSQZ exerts renal protective effects in a CRF model by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, with its core mechanism involving the inhibition of ferroptosis in renal tubular epithelial cells. Under CRF conditions, the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is suppressed, leading to decreased expression of the key downstream proteins GPX4 and SLC7A11, thereby triggering lipid peroxidation accumulation and activating ferroptosis. Following YSQZ intervention, Nrf2 is activated, and its nuclear translocation is promoted, resulting in HO-1 upregulation Additionally, ferroptosis is implicated as a pathogenic mechanism in ischemia/reperfusion- or doxorubicin-mediated cardiomyopathy. HO-1 expression increased in heart tissue following doxorubicin challenge, and this upregulation was associated with iron deposition in cardiac mitochondria [39]. The ultimate effect of HO-1 – whether pro-survival or pro-death – likely depends on the cellular context, the magnitude and duration of its induction, and the overall iron and redox homeostasis. Within the antioxidant context provided by YSQZ, the protective role of HO-1 predominates, synergistically enhancing the expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11, clearing lipid peroxides, and thereby effectively inhibiting ferroptosis and alleviating renal injury. In summary, the molecular pathway by which YSQZ ameliorates CRF can be summarized as activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 axis, upregulation of GPX4/SLC7A11, and inhibition of ferroptosis, ultimately resulting in renal protection (Figure S1).

In recent years, the therapeutic value of TCM in the treatment of CRF has received increasing attention from researchers. As an important approach to treating CRF, TCM formulas have the characteristics of multi-component, multi-target, and multi-pathway actions. However, their complex mechanisms for treating CRF remain incompletely understood. YSQZ has been used in clinical practice for many years. In our previous studies, we identified 35 active ingredients in YSQZ using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry. These active ingredients have potential value in the treatment of CRF. Many of them exert varying degrees of renoprotective effects, and their mechanisms might be related to oxidative stress and ferroptosis. For example, quercetin alleviates ferroptosis by reducing MDA and reactive oxygen species levels while increasing GSH levels, thereby attenuating renal damage [40]. Loganin has been demonstrated to improve renal function, reduce pathological damage, and inhibit oxidative stress and apoptosis. This process is accompanied by activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway [41]. In one study, paeoniflorin was found to alleviate renal injury by inhibiting SLC7A11-mediated ferroptosis [42]. Oleanolic acid has also been reported to alleviate oxidative stress and protect renal function by enhancing antioxidant enzyme activity [43]. It is noteworthy that other herbal constituents have also been reported to inhibit ferroptosis and alleviate renal injury by targeting the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis [44]. By contrast, our findings emphasize that YSQZ exerts a more comprehensive regulatory role by activating the core Nrf2/HO-1 signaling hub and potently upregulating its two key downstream effectors, namely GPX4 and SLC7A11, thereby demonstrating a multi-target anti-ferroptotic mechanism in chronic renal failure.

Our study had several limitations. This study suggests that the renoprotective effects of YSQZ are mediated through Nrf2 pathway activation, improving oxidative stress and iron metabolism imbalance. A limitation of this study was that activation of the Nrf2 pathway was primarily assessed by measuring total protein levels. Future studies would benefit from directly quantifying Nrf2 nuclear translocation and the expression of additional downstream targets such as NQO1 to provide more direct evidence of its functional activity. This work did not comprehensively assess alterations in other genes within the Nrf2-regulated network. Future research should further investigate the impact of YSQZ on downstream Nrf2 targets and use ferroptosis inhibitors to validate the underlying mechanisms. Although the Nrf2 inhibitor ML385 was used for mechanistic validation, the absence of a positive control group using an Nrf2 activator represents another gap that should be addressed in future studies. As a compound formulation, the specific active components of YSQZ remain unclear, necessitating further research using network pharmacology, serum pharmacochemistry, and pharmacodynamic evaluation to identify and validate the key components involved in its therapeutic effects. Building on the initial behavioral observations, subsequent work must include a comprehensive toxicological evaluation of YSQZ. Furthermore, mechanistic studies aimed at isolating its active components and identifying downstream ferroptosis targets through transcriptomics are essential. However, the specific compounds in YSQZ that inhibit ferroptosis and confer renoprotection remain unidentified and warrant further investigation. Future studies should analyze key iron-regulatory proteins (e.g., ferritin, ferroportin) to further elucidate the mechanism by which YSQZ modulates iron homeostasis. Additionally, to provide a more comprehensive and dynamic assessment of oxidative stress and redox balance, future studies could include measurements of the GSSG/GSH ratio along with other specific markers of oxidative damage or antioxidant capacity, such as oxidative damage by-products.

This study both deepens our understanding of the pharmacological mechanisms of YSQZ and provides a new theoretical basis for its clinical application. Given its well-defined dose-dependent renoprotective effects and multi-target regulation of the Nrf2/ferroptosis pathway, YSQZ holds promise for development as an adjunctive therapy to current standard treatments for chronic renal failure or as an early intervention strategy to delay its progression to end-stage renal disease, underscoring its significant translational medical value.

Conclusion

YSQZ exerted therapeutic effects in rats with CRF, and these effects were weakened by erastin and ML385. Thus, YSQZ might inhibit ferroptosis by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, thereby exerting a renoprotective effect. YSQZ demonstrates significant potential for use as an adjunctive therapy alongside standard care, and it could serve as an early intervention strategy to delay the progression to end-stage renal disease.

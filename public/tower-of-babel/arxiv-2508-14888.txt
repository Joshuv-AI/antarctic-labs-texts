GL n \textnormal{GL}_{n} large sieves and density estimates via positive semi-definiteness
Alexandru Pascadi, Jesse Thorner

Abstract
. Let 𝔉 n \mathfrak{F}_{n} be the set of unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} over a number field F F , and let 𝒮 ⊆ 𝔉 n \mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n} be a finite subset. Given π 0 ∈ 𝔉 n 0 \pi_{0}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n_{0}} , we establish large sieve inequalities for the families { L ⁡ ( s , π ) : π ∈ 𝒮 } \{L(s,\pi)\colon\pi\in\mathcal{S}\} and { L ⁡ ( s , π × π 0 ) : π ∈ 𝒮 } \{L(s,\pi\times\pi_{0})\colon\pi\in\mathcal{S}\} that, unlike previous results, are independent of progress towards the generalized Ramanujan conjecture and simultaneously handle the Dirichlet coefficients of L L , L − 1 L^{-1} , and log ⁡ L \log L . Our approach is based on the duality principle and is sharp in ranges that are complementary to large sieve inequalities based on trace formulæ. We apply our large sieve inequalities to establish several density estimates for families of L L -functions, counting potential violations of the generalized Riemann hypothesis and the generalized Ramanujan conjecture. In particular, we remove all restrictions in the log-free zero density estimate of Brumley, Thorner, and Zaman [ 7 , Theorem 1.3] for families of Rankin–Selberg L L -functions.

\theta=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}
, at which point the work of Luo, Rudnick, and Sarnak
[

]
ensures that the left-hand side of (

) equals zero. By combining our main technical result,
Theorem

5.1
, with the ideas in
[

]
, we achieve several refinements.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
F
be a number field,
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
,
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}
a finite subset, and

. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{density_{r}ange}(n,\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that if

and

, then

(1.14)

\#\{\pi\in\mathcal{S}\colon\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})|\geq\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{p}^{\theta}\}\ll_{n,[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{p}^{n}(D_{F}^{-n^{2}}Q^{2n})^{\frac{1-2\theta}{\max\{1,4\theta\}}+\varepsilon}.

Remark
.

An inspection of the proofs of
Theorems

1.6
and
5.1
will show that
With some care, one can replace
D_{F}^{-n^{2}}Q^{2n}
with
\max_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}
.

Compared to
[

removes the requirement that

, explicates the dependence on
\mathfrak{p}
on the right-hand side, eliminates the need for each

to be unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, and improves the exponent on the right-hand side of (

. Surprisingly, several of these refinements come together to produce a nontrivial upper bound on
|\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)|
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
F
be a number field. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{family}(n,\varepsilon)>0
such that if

, then
|\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)|\ll_{n,[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon}D_{F}^{-n^{2}}Q^{2n+\varepsilon}
.

Remark
.

It follows as an immediate corollary that if

, then

The bound on

in

Contrastingly, in this paper, the description of the ramified Euler factors goes into the proof of

Remark
.

As in

2.
Informal outline

Here we give an informal overview of our argument, ignoring various technical details, and emphasizing our key innovations. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
N,Q\geq 1
, and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}
,
\max_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}\leq Q
. By the duality principle (see
[
24
, p. 170]
), the bound for
C(N,\mathcal{S})
in
Theorem

1.1
is equivalent to the bound

(2.1)

\sup_{\|w\|_{2}=1}\sum_{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq N}\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\ll(NQ)^{o(1)}(N+Q^{n}|\mathcal{S}|).

We begin by describing previous approaches. Duke and Kowalski
[
12
, Section 4]
expanded the square in (
2.1
), swapped the order of summation, and applied Mellin inversion, thus obtaining

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq N}\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w_{\pi^{\prime}}}\sum_{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq x}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\overline{\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w(\pi^{\prime})}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{3-i\infty}^{3+i\infty}\Big(\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\overline{\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}}\Big)\frac{N^{s}}{s}ds.

It is implicit in their work that if
\theta
is the best bound towards GRC for each element of
\mathcal{S}
, then there exists a function
H(s,\pi,\pi^{\prime})
, holomorphic in the region
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>\tfrac{1}{2}+2\theta
, such that

\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\overline{\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}}=H(s,\pi,\pi^{\prime})L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}).

The basis for the results in
[
12
]
is that this factorization holds inside of the critical strip when
\theta<\tfrac{1}{4}
(and in particular, when GRC holds). Now, one pushes the contour as far to the left as progress towards GRC permits, bounding
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
in the critical strip using convexity.

It follows from recent work of Jiang
[
29
]
that
H(s,\pi,\pi^{\prime})
is actually holomorphic in the region
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>\tfrac{1}{2}+\theta
. Proceeding as in
[
12
]
, one arrives at (
1.7
). But as described earlier, even if
\theta=0
, (
1.7
) is inferior to the bound for
C(N,\mathcal{S})
in
Theorem

1.1
.

On the other hand, it follows from the work of Thorner and Zaman
[
46
, Section 4.2]
, which relies on the orthogonality of Schur polynomials evaluated at the sets of local roots
\{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\}
and
\{\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\}
, that the bound

(2.3)

\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\leq\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w}(\pi)\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}),\qquad(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}})=\mathcal{O}_{F}

holds without recourse to unproven progress towards GRC. Summing over all
\mathfrak{n}
with
\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq N
and applying Mellin inversion, one arrives at

(2.4)

\sum_{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq N}\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\Big|^{2}\leq\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w}(\pi)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{3-i\infty}^{3+i\infty}\frac{L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})}\frac{N^{s}}{s}ds.\hskip-2.84526pt

One then pushes the contour to the line
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(s)=(\log N)^{-1}
, bounding
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
using
Lemma

3.1
and the product over
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}
using the existing progress towards GRC in (
3.2
). It is this latter contribution that leads to the factor of
Q^{4\theta n^{2}}
in (
1.6
).

One of our key observations is that (
2.3
) actually holds for
all

\mathfrak{n}
:

(2.5)

\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\leq\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w}(\pi^{\prime})\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}).

To prove (
2.5
) in general, one must be more careful with how
\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})
is handled when
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}})\neq\mathcal{O}_{F}
. While an explicit combinatorial argument as in
[
46
]
becomes rather cumbersome and difficult to see, a linear-algebraic formulation inspired by
[
13
]
will lead to a fairly short proof. In fact, we can reformulate (
2.5
) as follows:

(2.6)

\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})-\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\overline{\lambda}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})\qquad\text{ form a \emph{positive semi-definite matrix} in }\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}.

In
[
13
, Proposition 4]
, Lichtman and Pascadi proved that
A_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}:=\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})
form a positive semi-definite matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}
(in the case
F=\mathbb{Q}
), which is a priori a weaker statement. However, applying this result to the family
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{S}\cup\{\mathbbm{1}\}
, where
\mathbbm{1}
denotes the trivial representation, we see that the
(|\mathcal{S}|+1)\times(|\mathcal{S}|+1)
block matrix

A^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}A&\vec{v}\\
\vec{v}^{*}&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{ is positive semi-definite},

where
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{S}}
is the vector with entries
\vec{v}_{\pi}:=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
.
It is a nice exercise to deduce that the matrix
A-\vec{v}\vec{v}^{*}
is positive semi-definite (either by matrix algebra or by spectral decomposition and Cauchy–Schwarz), which is precisely the content of (
2.6
).

By an extension of this argument, we can accommodate Rankin–Selberg convolutions with a given representation
\pi_{0}
; this leads to the bound

(2.7)

\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda_{\pi\times\pi_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\leq\lambda_{\pi_{0}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w}(\pi^{\prime})\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}),

and ultimately to a large sieve inequality for the Dirichlet coefficients of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions.
A key further step, inspired by
[
23
]
, is to extend the left-hand side to include Dirichlet coefficients
\lambda^{\circ}_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})
of other related Dirichlet series, such as
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})^{-1}
or
\log L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
. Although these coefficients do not form a positive semi-definite matrix, they turn out to have
\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}
as a “positive semi-definite cover”, in a sense that we introduce in
Section

4
. This leads to the general inequality

(2.8)

\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\lambda^{\circ}_{\pi\times\pi_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\leq\lambda_{\pi_{0}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}w(\pi)\overline{w}(\pi^{\prime})\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}).

The resulting large sieve inequality for
\lambda^{\circ}_{\pi\times\pi_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})
, given in (
5.1
), improves upon (
1.6
) by removing the coprimality restriction and eliminating the
Q^{4\theta n^{2}}
factor. Our proof of
Theorem

1.2
relies on inserting this result in the specific case where
\lambda^{\circ}_{\pi\times\pi_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})
equals the
\mathfrak{n}
-th Dirichlet coefficient
\mu_{\pi\times\pi_{0}}(\mathfrak{n})
of
L(s,\pi\times\pi_{0})^{-1}
into the zero detection arguments of Humphries and Thorner
[
23
, Section 5]
.

The application to the
log-free
zero density estimates in
Theorem

1.5
requires working with the Dirichlet coefficients of
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
. These are closely related to the coefficients of
\log L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
(for which the argument above applies), but differ by a logarithmic factor, which we cannot afford to lose. To prove a log-free zero density estimate for the family
\{L(s,\pi)\colon\pi\in\mathcal{S}\}
, Thorner and Zaman
[
46
]
inserted Selberg sieve weights into the large sieve (see
[
46
, Proof of Theorem 4.2]
) to handle the contribution of unramified prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
. Thorner and Zaman trivially estimated the contribution from
\{\mathfrak{p}^{k}\colon\textup{$\mathfrak{p}$ unramified, $k\geq 2$}\}
and
\{\mathfrak{p}^{k}\colon\textup{$\mathfrak{p}$ ramified, $k\geq 1$}\}
using the best unconditional progress towards GRC at ramified
[
41
]
and unramified
[
35
]
prime ideals. However, within the approach in
[
46
]
, the progress towards GRC in
[
35
,
41
]
is insufficient to handle the log-free zero density estimate for the family
\{L(s,\pi\times\pi_{0})\colon\pi\in\mathcal{S}\}
when
\pi_{0}
does not satisfy GRC. The need for GRC would be removed if each
\pi\in\mathcal{S}\cup\{\pi_{0}\}
were to satisfy

(2.9)

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\max_{j}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})|^{2r}}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{p}^{r(1+\varepsilon)}}\ll_{\varepsilon}\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}^{\varepsilon},

where
\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
ranges over the Satake parameters at
\mathfrak{p}
. This is Hypothesis 1.1 in the work of Brumley, Thorner, and Zaman
[
7
]
. This is (as of now) only known when
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
with
n\leq 4

[
5
]
.

To handle the family
\{L(s,\pi\times\pi_{0})\colon\pi\in\mathcal{S}\}
unconditionally, we prove a version of (
2.8
) with Selberg sieve weights inserted (see (
5.3
)), alleviating the need to handle the powers of ramified primes differently. To avoid a fatal logarithmic loss, we trivially estimate the prime powers
\mathfrak{p}^{k}
using pointwise bounds towards GRC when

. The smaller powers are handled using the Selberg sieve weights. These choices are inspired by the work of Soundararajan and Thorner
[
44
, Proof of Theorem 2.4]
, which addresses the case where
|\mathcal{S}|=1
. This leads to
Corollary

8.1
, which is our key ingredient towards the log-free zero density estimates in
Theorem

1.5
.

Once
Corollary

8.1
is proved, we apply it to the zero detection process for
L
-functions that uses Turán’s lower bound for power sums. So far, this seems to be the only method whereby one can prove log-free zero density estimates for automorphic or Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions without recourse to unproven progress towards GRC. Since the zero density estimates in
Theorem

1.2
(not log-free) have the best known exponents given their level of generality, we take the time here to numerically sharpen the rather large exponents that occur in
[
7
,
44
,
46
]
.

Finally, we deduce the density estimate for non-archimedean Langlands parameters in

1.6
as another corollary of

5.1
. Specifically, we apply
Theorem

5.1
for the coefficients of
\log L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
, and the sequence
a(\mathfrak{n})
supported on a single

. This improves the approach from
[

]
in two ways. First, our framework of positive semi-definite covers from

removes the ramification constraints at

3.
Properties of
L
-functions

We recall some standard facts about
L
-functions arising from automorphic representations and their Rankin–Selberg convolutions.

3.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient representation and
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
be the conductor of
\pi
. We express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth admissible representations of
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
, where
v
varies over places of
F
. When
v
is a non-archimedean place corresponding with a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, then the local
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})
is defined in terms of the Langlands parameters
A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=\{\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\alpha_{n,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\}
by

(3.1)

L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}.

If
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
, then
\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for all
j
. If
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
, then there might exist
j
such that
\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=0
. The standard
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
associated to
\pi
is

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}}.

The Euler product and Dirichlet series converge absolutely when
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>1
.

At each archimedean place
v
of
F
, there are
n
Langlands parameters
\mu_{j,\pi}(v)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

L(s,\pi_{\infty})=\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)),\qquad\Gamma_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$.}\end{cases}

By combining the work in
[
2
,
36
,
41
]
, we know that there exists

(3.2)

\theta_{n}\in\Big[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}\Big]

such that

(3.3)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})|\leq\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{p}^{\theta_{n}}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))\geq-\theta_{n}.

GRC asserts that in (
3.2
), one may take
\theta_{n}=0
. We have
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}=\mathfrak{q}_{\widetilde{\pi}}
, and for each
\mathfrak{p}
and each
v
, we have the equalities of sets
\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p})\}=\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})}\}
and
\{\mu_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\mu_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
.

Let
r_{\pi}
be the order of the pole of
L(s,\pi)
at
s=1
. The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(1-s))^{r_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})^{s/2}L(s,\pi)L(s,\pi_{\infty})

is entire of order 1. There exists a complex number
W(\pi)
of modulus 1 such that if
s\in\mathbb{C}
, then the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi})
holds. Let
d(v)=1
if
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
and
d(v)=2
if
F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
. The analytic conductor of
\pi

[
26
]
is given by

(3.4)

\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}(it)=D_{F}^{n}\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(3+|it+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)|^{d(v)}),\qquad\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}=\mathfrak{C}_{\pi}(0).

Since
\Lambda(s,\pi)
is entire of order 1, there exist complex numbers
a_{\pi}
and
b_{\pi}
such that

\Lambda(s,\pi)=e^{a_{\pi}+b_{\pi}s}\prod_{\Lambda(\rho,\pi)=0}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big)e^{s/\rho}.

The zeros
\rho
in the above Hadamard product are the nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi)
, and the zeros of
L(s,\pi)
that arise as poles of
s^{r_{\pi}}L(s,\pi_{\infty})
are the trivial zeros.

3.2.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. At each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}

4.
Positive semi-definite covers

In this section, we significantly extend the ideas in
[
13
]
on positive semi-definite families of
L
-functions. Our goal is to prove the inequality (
2.8
) for Rankin–Selberg coefficients, reiterated in
Proposition

4.7
—recall that such a result is implicit in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
when
\mathfrak{n}
has no ramified prime factors. Here, we treat ramified and unramified places on the same footing. To this end, we use a fairly general linear-algebraic language, although our arguments are ultimately combinatorial.

4.1.
Covers for matrices

We first introduce a generalization of the classical positive semi-definiteness property for matrices.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite set and
a,a^{+}\colon\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{C}
be functions (which can be viewed as matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}
). We say that
a^{+}(x,y)
is a
positive semi-definite cover
for
a(x,y)
if and only if there exist countably-many functions
u_{j}\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{C}
and complex numbers
d_{j}
with
|d_{j}|\leq 1
such that

(4.1)

a(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(y),\qquad\qquad a^{+}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(y),

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
, where the convergence is absolute.

Remark
.

In particular,
a^{+}(x,y)
is positive semi-definite if and only if it is a positive semi-definite cover for itself (or for the identically-zero matrix). Any Hermitian matrix has a positive semi-definite cover, obtained by replacing all eigenvalues in its spectral decomposition with their absolute values.

To motivate this definition, consider the following inequality.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite set and
a,a^{+}\colon\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{C}
be functions such that
a^{+}(x,y)
is a positive semi-definite cover for
a(x,y)
. If
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
are subsets and
v\colon\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathbb{C}
,
w\colon\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathbb{C}
are functions, then

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\
y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\end{subarray}}v(x)w(y)a(x,y)\Big|^{2}\leq\Big(\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}v(x)\overline{v}(x^{\prime})a^{+}(x,x^{\prime})\Big)\Big(\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}w(y)\overline{w}(y^{\prime})a^{+}(y^{\prime},y)\Big).

Proof.

Recall the notation in
Definition

4.1
. By expanding
a(x,y)
and
a^{+}(x,y)
as in (
4.1
), swapping sums, and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in
j
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\
y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\end{subarray}}v(x)w(y)a(x,y)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}v(x)u_{j}(x)\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}w(y)\overline{u}_{j}(y)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}v(x)u_{j}(x)\Big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big|\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}w(y)\overline{u}_{j}(y)\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}v(x)\overline{v}(x^{\prime})\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(x^{\prime})\Big)\Big(\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}w(y)\overline{w}(y^{\prime})\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\overline{u}_{j}(y)u_{j}(y^{\prime})\Big),

which simplifies to the desired bound.
∎

Definition

4.1
is also well-behaved, in the sense that positive semi-definite covers are stable under several operations which also preserve positive semi-definiteness.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. If
a^{+}(x,y)
(resp.
b^{+}(x,y)
) is a positive semi-definite cover of
a(x,y)
(resp.
b(x,y)
), then
|\lambda|a^{+}(x,y)
is a positive-definite cover of
\lambda a(x,y)
,
(a^{+}+b^{+})(x,y)
is a positive-definite cover of
(a+b)(x,y)
, and
(a^{+}\cdot b^{+})(x,y)
is a positive-definite cover of
(a^{+}\cdot b^{+})(x,y)
.

Proof.

Write
a(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(y)
and
a^{+}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(y)
, according to
Definition

4.1
. Similarly, write
b(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}v_{k}(x)\overline{v}_{k}(y)
and
b^{+}(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}v_{k}(x)\overline{v}_{k}(y)
. Per
Definition

4.1
, the bounds
|c_{j}|,|d_{k}|\leq 1
hold. The claims for scaling and addition are immediate. For multiplication, we have the expansion

a(x,y)\,b(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{j}d_{k}\ (u_{j}\cdot v_{k})(x)\ \overline{(u_{j}\cdot v_{k})(y)},

where
|c_{j}d_{k}|\leq 1
,
and
a^{+}(x,y)\,b^{+}(x,y)
similarly. We can then re-index to a single countable sum.
∎

4.2.
Covers for families of Dirichlet series

We now pass from matrices
a(x,y)
to families of Dirichlet series
A(s;x,y)
. The reader should keep in mind the case when
\mathcal{S}
is a family of automorphic representations, and the Dirichlet series

come from Rankin–Selberg convolutions.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
F
be a number field,
\mathcal{S}
be a finite set, and
(A(s;x,y))_{x,y\in\mathcal{S}}
,
(A^{+}(s;x,y))_{x,y\in\mathcal{S}}
be families of formal Dirichlet series with expansions

A(s;x,y)=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{a(\mathfrak{n};x,y)}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}},\qquad\quad A^{+}(s;x,y)=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{a^{+}(\mathfrak{n};x,y)}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}}.

We say that
A^{+}(s;x,y)
is a
positive semi-definite cover
for
A(s;x,y)
if for each
\mathfrak{n}
,
a^{+}(\mathfrak{n};x,y)
is a positive semi-definite cover for
a(\mathfrak{n};x,y)
(indexing over
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
). In other words, there exist functions
u_{j}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{C}
, complex numbers
d_{j}
with
|d_{j}|\leq 1
, and integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}_{j}
such that

A(s;x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}\frac{u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(y)}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}_{j}^{s}},\qquad\quad A^{+}(s;x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{u_{j}(x)\overline{u}_{j}(y)}{\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}_{j}^{s}},

where the convergence of the Dirichlet coefficient of each
\textnormal{N}\mathfrak{n}^{-s}
is absolute, pointwise in
x,y
. We say that
A^{+}(s;x,y)
is
positive semi-definite
if it is a positive semi-definite cover for
0
.

Fortunately, several natural operations on
L
-functions preserve positive semi-definite covers. The following lemma is a generalization of
[
13
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
If
A^{+}(s;x,y)
(resp.
B^{+}(s;x,y)
) is a positive semi-definite cover of
A(s;x,y)
(resp.
B^{+}(s;x,y)
), then the families

|z|A^{+}(s;x,y),\quad(A^{+}+B^{+})(s;x,y),\quad(A^{+}\cdot B^{+})(s;x,y),\quad\exp A^{+}(s;x,y)

are positive semi-definite covers, respectively, of

|z|A(s;x,y),\quad(A+B)(s;x,y),\quad(A\cdot B)(s;x,y),\quad\exp A(s;x,y)

If
C(s;x,y)
is positive semi-definite, then
\exp C(s;x,y)
is a positive semi-definite cover for
C(s;x,y)
.

Proof.

The claims about the covers of
zA(s;x,y)
,
(A+B)(s;x,y)
, and
(A\cdot B)(s;x,y)
follow from
Lemma

4.3
. For the exponentiation property, we use the expansions

\exp A(s;x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}A(s;x,y)^{k},\qquad\quad\exp A^{+}(s;x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}A^{+}(s;x,y)^{k}

with the previous properties (note that
A^{+}(s;x,y)^{k}
is a positive semi-definite cover for
A(s;x,y)^{k}
for each
k
). For the final claim, expand
\exp C(s;x,y)
as above and consider the term
k=1
.
∎

Finally, we apply these notions to Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{F}_{n}
is finite, then
(\log L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}))_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}
is positive semi-definite.

Proof.

This is a purely formal generalization of
[
13

to variable ranks
n
and general number fields.
When

(resp.,

(resp.,

\pi_{p},\ell,j

\log L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{j}(\pi)\overline{S}_{j}(\pi^{\prime})}{n_{j}^{s}},

where

.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{F}_{n}

L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})

v\colon\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathbb{C}
and

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\

In particular, if

(4.3)

\Big|\sum_{\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}w(\pi_{2})\lambda^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\leq\lambda^{+}_{\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1}}(\mathfrak{n})\sum_{\pi_{2},\pi_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}w(\pi_{2})\overline{w}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})\lambda^{+}_{\widetilde{\pi}_{2}^{\prime}\times\pi_{2}}(\mathfrak{n}).

∎

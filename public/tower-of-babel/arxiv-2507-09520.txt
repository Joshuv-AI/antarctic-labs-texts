Correlations in random cluster model at q = 1 q=1


Abstract
Let μ \mu be a measure that samples a subset of a finite ground set, and let 𝒜 e \mathcal{A}_{e} be the event that element e e is sampled. The measure μ \mu is negatively correlated if for any pair of elements e , f e,f one has μ ⁡ ( 𝒜 e ∩ 𝒜 f ) − μ ⁡ ( 𝒜 e ) ​ μ ​ ( 𝒜 f ) ≤ 0 \mu(\mathcal{A}_{e}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f})-\mu(\mathcal{A}_{e})\mu(\mathcal{A}_{f})\leq 0 . A measure is positively correlated if the direction of the inequality is reversed. For the random cluster model on graphs positive correlation between edges is known for q ≥ 1 q\geq 1 due to the FKG inequality, while the negative correlation is only conjectured for 0 ≤ q ≤ 1 0\leq q\leq 1 . The main result of this paper is to give a combinatorial formula for the difference in question at q = 1 q=1 . Previously, such a formula was known in the uniform spanning tree case, which is a limit of the random cluster model at q = 0 q=0 .

Introduction

An
increasing event

\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}

is a collection of subsets of
[n]
that are closed upward under containment, that is,
if
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
A\subseteq B\subseteq[n]
, then
B\in\mathcal{A}
.
Such an event depends only on some subset
of the ground set,
specifically on the union of the minimal elements of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to inclusion. A measure
\mu
on
2^{[n]}
is called
negatively associated
if it satisfies

\mu(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})-\mu(\mathcal{A})\mu(\mathcal{B})\leq 0

for any pair of increasing events
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
on
2^{[n]}
that depend on disjoint sets of elements of the ground set. Similarly,
\mu
is
positively associated
if it satisfies

\mu(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})-\mu(\mathcal{A})\mu(\mathcal{B})\geq 0

for any pair of increasing events
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
on
2^{[n]}
, without restrictions on the ground set.

The simplest example of an increasing event is the event
\mathcal{A}_{e}
consisting of all subsets of
[n]
containing specific element
e
. When a measure satisfies correct inequality
for any elements
e\not=f
of the ground set, we say that
\mu
is
negatively correlated
or
positively correlated
respectively.

The study of positive and negative association is a deep and beautiful subject. The case of positive association is easier due to a powerful of
FKG inequality
, due to Fortuin, Kastelyn, and Ginibre
[
FKG71
]
. Specifically, if a measure satisfies certain local
positive lattice condition
, positive association follows. There are many other related interesting questions and results in the theory of positive association, such as Berg-Kesten inequality
[
VDBK85
]
, Reimer inequality
[
Rei00
]
, etc. We direct the reader to
[
Gri18
, Chapter 4]
for a great introduction.

The case of negative association is harder, as no tool like FKG inequality is available. We direct the reader to the survey by Pemantle
[
Pem00
]
and to the work of Borcea, Brändén, and Liggett
[
BBL09
]
for rich sources of information on major ideas, theorems, and conjectures in it. Among the notable developments are the works of Wagner
[
Wag08
]
and Kahn and Neiman
[
KN10
]
, as well as recent papers of Brändén and Huh
[
BH20
]
and of Huh, Schröter, and Wang
[
HSW21
]
. The first of the latter introduces Lorenzian polynomials as a powerful tool allowing to prove versions of negative association.

The random cluster model, also known as Fortuin–Kasteleyn model
[
FK72
]
, is one of the fundamental models of statistical mechanics, see
[
Gri06
]
for a definitive source.
Negative association in the random cluster model was conjectured by Kahn
[
Kah00
]
and Grimmett–Winkler
[
GW04
]
. It is related to
uniform spanning tree
measure via certain limit at
q=0
. The uniform spanning tree measure is perhaps the most famous example of negative association, related to historical
Rayleigh monotonicity
property of electrical networks, see
[
FM92
,
Cho04
,
Wag08
,
CHLW08
]
, as well as
[
LP17
, Chapter 4]
for a modern exposition. Furthermore, one can also get
uniform spanning forest
and
uniform connected subgraph
measures by specializing random cluster model, for both of those negative association is conjectured and remains open
[
Har74
,
S+06
,
LL10
]
.

In the uniform spanning tree case an actual explicit formula is known
[
FM92
,
CHLW08
]
. It can be formulated as follows, see the discussion preceding Theorem
2.7
for the relevant notation.

Theorem

(
[
FM92
]
)
.

In the case of uniform spanning tree measure, for any graph
G
and edges
e,f
we have

\mu(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})-\mu(\mathcal{A})\mu(\mathcal{B})\sim\left(\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}}\mathbf{x}^{F}-\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}\mathbf{x}^{F}\right)^{2},

where
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
are collections of forests satisfying certain properties with respect to the edges
e
and
f
.

The main result of this paper is to give an explicit formula for the case
q=1
as follows, see Section
3
for relevant definitions and notation.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

For random cluster model at
q=1
for any graph
G
and edges
e,f
we have

\mu(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})-\mu(\mathcal{A})\mu(\mathcal{B})\sim\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\left(\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in B_{\beta,\gamma}}\mathbf{m}\right).

(1)

It is our hope that finding a common generalization of the two formulas can lead to resolving Kahn–Grimmett–Winkler conjecture in full generality. To this end we formulate
\alpha\beta\gamma
-ansatz conjecture, resolving which would generalize both of the above formulas, and would imply Kahn–Grimmett–-Winkler conjecture.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

we explain the necessary background on random cluster model and correlations in it. In Section

and in Section

Preliminaries

2.1
Background

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{e}:e\in E\}
be a set of positive real numbers indexed by
E
. Here we allow
G
to have parallel edges and loops. Let the state space be
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{E}
consisting of vectors
\omega=(\omega(e):e\in E)
. Let
\eta(\omega)=\{e\in E:\omega(e)=1\}
. This yields a correspondence between
\Omega
and subsets
F\subseteq E
given by
F=\eta(\omega)
. We will sometimes abuse the notation and denote
\eta(F)
for the subgraph of
G
induced by
F
. Let
F=\eta(\omega)
, we denote
\mathbf{x}^{\omega}:=\mathbf{x}^{F}:=\prod_{e\in F}x_{e}
, and let
k(\omega):=k(F)
be the number of connected components in
\eta(F)
.

Given two subsets
A,B\subseteq E
, consider event

\mathcal{J}_{A}^{B}=\{\omega\in\Omega:\omega(e)=1~\text{for all $e\in A$},\omega(e)=0~\text{for all $e\in B$}\}

and define the
random cluster model
measure

\mu(\mathcal{J}_{A}^{B})=\mathcal{T}_{A}^{B}=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{J}_{A}^{B}}\mathbf{x}^{\omega}q^{k(\omega)},

where
Z=\sum_{\omega}\mathbf{x}^{\omega}q^{k(\omega)}
is the partition function.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the measure for the random cluster model is usually written as

\phi_{p,q}(\omega)=\frac{1}{Z}q^{k(\omega)}\prod_{e\in E}p^{\omega(e)}(1-p)^{1-\omega(e)}.

One obtains this version from our version by setting
x_{e}=p/(1-p)
for each edge
e
, and canceling the common factor
\prod_{e\in E}(1-p)
.

Fixing two edges
e,f\in E
, we abuse the notation and write
ef,e,f
for
\{e,f\},\{e\},\{f\}
. For instance, we write
\mathcal{J}_{ef},\mathcal{J}^{ef},\mathcal{J}_{e}^{f},\mathcal{J}_{f}^{e}
for
\mathcal{J}_{\{e,f\}}
,
\mathcal{J}^{\{e,f\}}
,
\mathcal{J}_{\{e\}}^{\{f\}}
,
\mathcal{J}_{\{f\}}^{\{e\}}
, respectively. We say a probability measure
\mu
is
edge-negatively-associated
or
negatively correlated
if

\mu(\mathcal{J}_{e}\cap\mathcal{J}_{f})\leq\mu(\mathcal{J}_{e})\mu(\mathcal{J}_{f}),\quad\quad\text{for}~e,f\in E,e\neq f.

Conjecture 2.2

(
[
Kah00
,
GW04
]
)
.

The random cluster model is negatively correlated for
0\leq q\leq 1
.

With a little algebra, one can see that the condition for edge-negatively-association of random cluster model is equivalent to

\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef}\geq 0.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
E^{ef}
denote
E\backslash\{e,f\}
. One can easily see that there is a one-to-one correspondence between monomials in
\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}
and pairs of subset
(A,B)
of
E^{ef}
given by

(A,B)\mapsto q^{k_{1}(A,B)}\mathbf{x}^{A+e}\mathbf{x}^{B+f}\quad\text{where}\quad k_{1}(A,B)=k(A+e)+k(B+f).

We also have a similar correspondence with monomials in
\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef}
given by

(A,B)\mapsto q^{k_{2}(A,B)}\mathbf{x}^{A+e+f}\mathbf{x}^{B}\quad\text{where}\quad k_{2}(A,B)=k(A+e+f)+k(B).

Thus, when setting
q=1
, every monomial appears in
\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}
and
\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef}
with the same coefficient, and the difference is

.

It is easily seen in the Example
2.3
above for example, as after setting
q=1
the expression
x_{e}x_{f}x_{g}(q^{3}-q^{4})+x_{e}x_{f}x_{g}^{2}(q^{2}-q^{3})
turns into
0
.

By Factor theorem this means that
\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef}
is divisible by
1-q
. In addition, every monomial in this expression has a factor of
x_{e}x_{f}
. Thus, the difference
\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef}
is divisible by
x_{e}x_{f}(1-q)
, and thus for
0<q<1
it is natural to reformulate the conjecture as positivity of the expression

\mathcal{M}_{ef}(q):=(\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef})/x_{e}x_{f}(1-q)\geq 0.

It is useful to observe that each pair
(A,B)
of subsets in
E^{ef}
contributes
(q^{k_{1}(A,B)}-q^{k_{2}(A,B)})\mathbf{x}^{A+B}/(1-q)
to
\mathcal{M}_{ef}(q)
.

Example 2.5
.

For
G=K_{3}

\mathcal{M}_{ef}(q):=(\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef})/(1-q)=x_{g}q^{3}+x_{g}^{2}q^{2}.

Conjecture 2.6
.

For
0\leq q\leq 1
and for positive edgeweights
x_{e}
one has
\mathcal{M}_{ef}(q)\geq 0
.

Note that Conjecture
2.2
is trivial for

2.6
is not. In fact, the validity of Conjecture
2.6
follows, by taking

q>1
.

2.2
Comparison with Rayleigh monotonicity

It is possible to take the limit in the random cluster model to obtain Uniform Spanning Tree (UST) model. Specifically, one needs to take the limit so that
p,q\rightarrow 0
and
q/p\rightarrow 0
, see
[
Gri06
, Theorem 1.23]
.

Negative correlation for the UST model is a classical result equivalent to Rayleigh monotonicity in electrical networks, we refer the reader to
[
FM92
,
Cho04
,
CHLW08
,
Wag08
,
BBL09
,
LP17
]
for the proofs and the background. In our language, this result can be formulated as follows, where the limit and the specialization pick out the terms with the lowest power of
q
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
FM92
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

We have

(\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef})/q^{2}\mid_{q=0}\geq 0\Leftrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{M}_{ef}}{q^{2}}\mid_{q=0}\geq 0.

In fact,

\frac{\mathcal{T}_{e}^{f}\mathcal{T}_{f}^{e}-\mathcal{T}_{ef}\mathcal{T}^{ef}}{q^{2}x_{e}x_{f}}\mid_{q=0}=\left(\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}}\mathbf{x}^{F}-\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}\mathbf{x}^{F}\right)^{2},

where
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
are collections of forests satisfying certain properties with respect to the edges
e
and
f
.

In other words, in this case the negative correlation holds because the difference in question is a single square.

Example 2.8
.

In Example
2.3
we have

\frac{x_{e}x_{f}x_{g}(q^{3}-q^{4})+x_{e}x_{f}x_{g}^{2}(q^{2}-q^{3})}{q^{2}x_{e}x_{f}}\mid_{q=0}=x_{g}^{2}.

This example is sufficiently small that there is no subtraction inside the square on the right. However, already for the complete graph on four vertices the expression squared is not subtraction-free.

Lemma 4.1

For each monomial appearing in (

Proof.

4.2
The RHS

We now show a similar statement as Proposition

for the RHS. Consider a term

. This means that

Proposition 4.4
.

For each paracel
\gamma
, there is exactly one choice of
\beta
and
\mathbf{m}

\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{x}^{G^{ef}}

First, we show that there is exactly one choice of
\beta
. Since
\varnothing
is compatible with
\beta,\gamma
, every edge in
\beta
has to be a smoot for
\gamma
. Conversely, every edge that is a smoot for
\gamma
has to be in
\beta
. Indeed, if a smoot

is not in
\beta
, then
x_{g}
is in
\mathbf{m}
. This means that
g
is in some
\alpha
compatible with
\beta,\gamma
. This contradicts the requirement that
\gamma+\alpha
is a paracel.

Finally, we need to show that for each paracel
\gamma
and
\beta=\{\text{smoots in $\gamma$}\}
, there is always at least one way to split the remaining edges into disjoint
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
compatible with
\beta,\gamma
. Let
\Sigma
be the set of the remaining edges,

and

be the two connected components connected by

and

in
\eta(\gamma)
. Let

\alpha
and

We claim that both

. Every edge in

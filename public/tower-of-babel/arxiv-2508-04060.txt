Fourier transform and endoscopic transfer on real Lie algebras
Cheng Chen, Zhilin Luo

Abstract
. We prove that the endoscopic transfer on real Lie algebras commutes with the Fourier transform, using methods that are purely local.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 22E50 22E45; Secondary 20G20

1.
Introduction

In his seminal work (
[
Wal97
]
), J.-L. Waldspurger establishes a fundamental connection between smooth transfer and the Fourier transform on reductive Lie algebras and their endoscopic datum. Notably, the fundamental lemma for the unit element in the Lie algebra for almost all local places, now a theorem thanks to B.-C. Ngô in positive characteristic (
[
Ngô10
]
) and Waldspurger’s reduction to characteristic zero when the residual characteristic is sufficiently large (
[
Wal06
]
), implies both the existence of smooth transfer and its compatibility with the Fourier transform for Lie algebras over
p
-adic fields. The proof is of global nature and relies on the Lie algebra trace formula. Conversely, the work of D. Kazhdan and Y. Varshavsky (
[
KV12
, Remark 4.1.4]
) demonstrates that the compatibility between the Fourier transform and endoscopic transfer on Lie algebras implies the fundamental lemma for the unit element. D. Kazhdan and A. Polishchuk also delve into some variants of Waldspurger’s theorem (
[
KP03
]
).

The goal of the present paper is to establish the archimedean analogue of the theorem of Waldspurger using purely local methods. Over the complex field, this result is already known from Waldspurger (
[
Wal97
, §9]
), where the proof is likewise entirely local.

To state the theorems of this paper, we first fix our notation and conventions.

Notation and conventions

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{R}}
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We write
G
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
for
G({\mathbb{C}})
and
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
whenever no ambiguity arises.
Let
{\Gamma}={\mathrm{Gal}}_{{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}}
be the absolute Galois group of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))
(resp.
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))
) denote the space of Schwartz (resp. smooth and compactly supported) functions on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
is equipped with the Euclidean topology.

Fix a non-degenerate, conjugation-invariant, symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\psi(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
denote the Fourier kernel on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
\psi(x)=\exp(2\pi ix)
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Define

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}}(f)(X)=\int_{{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})}f(Y)\psi(\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})\,\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}Y,\quad f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})),

where
\,\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}Y
is the Haar measure on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
that is self-dual with respect to the Fourier transform. We equip
G({\mathbb{R}})
with the Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}g
induced from
\,\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}X
, so that the Jacobian of the exponential map is one at the identity.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
denote the regular semisimple locus of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))={\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})/G({\mathbb{R}})
be the set of
G({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy classes in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, and let
{\Sigma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))={\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})/G({\mathbb{C}})
be the set of
G({\mathbb{R}})
-stable conjugacy classes. Let
{\mathfrak{s}}_{G}:{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))\to{\Sigma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))
be the natural projection. For
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, let
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\in{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))
(resp.
{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{X}\in{\Sigma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))
) be its
G({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy (resp. stable conjugacy) class. Let
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
be the set of elliptic regular semi-simple elements, i.e., elements
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
whose centralizer of
X
in
G({\mathbb{R}})
is compact modulo the center of
G({\mathbb{R}})
.

For
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, let
G_{X}=T_{X}
be its centralizer in
G
. Consider the invariant orbital integral over the orbit
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
:

(1.0.1)

\mathrm{J}_{G}(X,f)={\mathrm{D}}^{G}(X)^{1/2}\int_{T_{X}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash G({\mathbb{R}})}f(g^{-1}Xg)\,\mathrm{d}\overline{g},\quad f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))

where
{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(X)=|\det(\mathrm{ad}X|_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}})|
is the absolute value of the Weyl discriminant of
X
. The integral (
1.0.1
) converges absolutely and defines a tempered distribution on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
(
[
Var77
, p.37, Lemma 2]
).

Let
\widehat{j}^{G}(X,\cdot)
be the Fourier transform of
\mathrm{J}_{G}(X,\cdot)
:

\mathrm{J}_{G}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}}(f))=\int_{{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})}f(Y)\widehat{j}^{G}(X,Y)\,\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}Y,\quad f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})).

Following
[
Wal97
, p.171]
, set
\widehat{i}^{G}(X,Y)={\mathrm{D}}^{G}(Y)^{1/2}\widehat{j}^{G}(X,Y)
. The distribution
(X,Y)\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})\mapsto\widehat{j}^{G}(X,Y)
is locally integrable and smooth on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
. Moreover,
(X,Y)\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})\mapsto\widehat{i}^{G}(X,Y)
is globally bounded (
[
Var77
, p.112, Proposition 9]
).

Endoscopic transfer

Let
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbb{R}})
be the set of tuples

{\mathrm{E}}=(G,G^{*},\varphi,H,s,\xi)

as in
[
Wal97
, §1.1]
, where
G,G^{*}
are reductive algebraic groups over
{\mathbb{R}}
with
G^{*}
quasi-split,
\varphi:G\to G^{*}
is an inner twist, and
(H,s,\xi)
is an endoscopic triple for
G^{*}
in the sense of
[
Kot84
, §7]
. Following
[
Wal97
, §2.3]
, one can define the transfer factor

{\Delta}(X_{H},X_{G})

for
(X_{H},X_{G})\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
where
{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
denotes the set of
G^{*}
-regular semisimple elements of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
, and
{\Delta}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the analogue of the Langlands-Shelstad transfer factor on Lie algebras (
[
LS87
]
).

For
f_{G}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))
and
X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, set

\mathrm{J}_{G,H}(X_{H},f_{G})=\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{G}}\in{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))}{\Delta}(X_{H},X_{G})\mathrm{J}_{G}(X_{G},f_{G}),

where the summation is taken over all
X_{G}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, modulo
G({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy, such that
{\Delta}(X_{H},X_{G})\neq 0
.

Similarly, for
f_{H}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}}))
and
{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{X_{H}}\in{\Sigma}({\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))
, set

\mathrm{J}_{H}^{\mathrm{st}}(X_{H},f_{H})=\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}_{H}}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{H}^{-1}({\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{X_{H}})}\mathrm{J}_{H}(X^{\prime}_{H},f_{H}),

where the summation is taken over the
H({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy classes contained in the stable conjugacy class of
X_{H}
.

Theorem 1.0.1

(Smooth transfer on Lie algebras)
.

For any
f_{G}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))

(
resp.
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))
)
, there exists
f_{H}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))

(
resp.
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}}))
)
, such that

\mathrm{J}_{G,H}(X_{H},f_{G})=\mathrm{J}^{\mathrm{st}}_{H}(X_{H},f_{H})

for any
X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
.

In the group setting, by the work of D. Shelstad (
[
She81
]
[
She82
]
), smooth transfer is known for
f_{G}\in{\mathcal{C}}(G({\mathbb{R}}))
and
f_{H}\in{\mathcal{C}}(H({\mathbb{R}}))
, where
{\mathcal{C}}(G({\mathbb{R}}))
denotes the space of Harish-Chandra Schwartz functions on
G({\mathbb{R}})
. This result result is extended to
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(G({\mathbb{R}}))
by A. Bouaziz (
[
Bou94b
]
). Using the same strategies as Bouaziz and Shelstad, and relying on Bouaziz’s spectral characterization of Lie algebra orbital integrals for both test and Schwartz functions (
[
Bou94a
]
), Theorem
1.0.1
follows immediately.

Fourier transform and endoscopic transfer are compatible

Building upon the work of
[
Wal95
, p.91]
and
[
Wal97
]
, for any
X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
,
we introduce the following two tempered distributions on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
, which are locally integrable and determined by their values on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Definition 1.0.2
.

For
(X_{H},X_{G})\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, set

\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},X_{G})

\displaystyle={\gamma}_{\psi}({\mathfrak{g}})\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{G}^{\prime}}\in{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))}{\Delta}(X_{H},X_{G}^{\prime})\widehat{i}^{G}(X_{G}^{\prime},X_{G}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathrm{D}}}_{G,H}(X_{H},X_{G})

\displaystyle={\gamma}_{\psi}({\mathfrak{h}})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{H}^{\prime}}\in{\mathfrak{s}}^{-1}_{H}({\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{X_{H}})\\
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{H}^{\prime\prime}}\in{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))\end{subarray}}w(X_{H}^{\prime\prime})^{-1}{\Delta}(X_{H}^{\prime\prime},X_{G})\widehat{i}^{H}(X_{H}^{\prime},X_{H}^{\prime\prime}),

where

•

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{G}^{\prime}}\in{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))
, i.e.,
X_{G}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
modulo
G({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy;

•

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{H}^{\prime}}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{H}^{-1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X_{H}}^{\mathrm{st}})
, i.e.,
X_{H}^{\prime}
runs over the
H({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy classes in the stable conjugacy class of
X_{H}
;

•

X^{\prime\prime}_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
modulo
H({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy;

•

w(X^{\prime\prime}_{H})=|{\mathfrak{s}}_{H}^{-1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime\prime}_{H}})|
, the number of
H({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy classes within the stable conjugacy class of
X^{\prime\prime}_{H}
;

•

{\gamma}_{\psi}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
{\gamma}_{\psi}({\mathfrak{h}})
are the Weil constants associated with
({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}})
, respectively
(
[
JL70
, §1]
)
. Here the non-degenerate conjugation-invariant symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}
on
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}})
is induced from
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
as in
[
Wal95
, VIII. 6]
.

We now state our main result, which can be viewed as the archimedean analogue of
[
Wal95
, Conjecture 2, p.91]
.

Theorem 1.0.3
.

With the above notation, for
X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
and any
X_{G}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, we have the identity

(1.0.2)

{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},X_{G})=\widetilde{{\mathrm{D}}}_{G,H}(X_{H},X_{G}).

Following the same argument as
[
Wal95
, Remarques. (10) p.92]
, Theorem
1.0.3
immediately implies the compatibility of Fourier transform with smooth transfer.

Corollary 1.0.4
.

With the above notation, let
f_{G}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}))
and
f_{H}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}}))
satisfy the smooth transfer condition

\mathrm{J}_{G,H}(X_{H},f_{G})=\mathrm{J}^{\mathrm{st}}_{H}(X_{H},f_{H}),\quad X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}).

Then

{\gamma}_{\psi}({\mathfrak{g}})\mathrm{J}_{G,H}(X_{H},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi,{\mathfrak{g}}}(f_{G}))={\gamma}_{\psi}({\mathfrak{h}})\mathrm{J}^{\mathrm{st}}_{H}(X_{H},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi,{\mathfrak{h}}}(f_{H})).

Over a
p
-adic field, Waldspurger shows that (
1.0.2
) for all pairs
(G,H)
implies the existence of the smooth transfer for test functions on Lie algebras (
[
Wal95
, Remarques (8) p.91]
). It would be interesting to know whether the same statement holds in the archimedean case.

We now briefly outline the proof of Theorem
1.0.3
:

•

First, following the approach of
[
Wal97
, §6]
,
one reduces the proof of (
1.0.2
) to the case in which the endoscopic triple
(H,s,\xi)
is elliptic and

X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}
;

•

Next, using a result of W. Rossmann
[
Ros78
, Corollary (15), p. 217]
, we verify the identity explicitly when
X_{G}
is also elliptic in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
;

•

Finally, we show that, as invariant tempered distributions on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
,
both
{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)
and
\widetilde{{\mathrm{D}}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)

are
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite and of elliptic type whenever

X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}
,
where
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is the symmetric algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
identified as the algebra of constant coefficient invariant differential operators on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
.
By
[
Var77
, Theorem 13, p. 116]
,
such distributions are uniquely determined by their values on the elliptic locus,
which completes the proof.

We emphasize that our proof is purely local,
whereas Waldspurger’s
p
-adic proof, which is significantly deeper, is global in nature,
relying on the fundamental lemma for the unit element at almost all local places.

Organization

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
,
we review the existence of endoscopic transfer for test functions and Schwartz functions on Lie algebras over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Subsection
2.1
recalls the notion of endoscopic datum.
Subsection
2.2
reviews the spectral characterization of orbital integrals and stable orbital integrals
for both test and Schwartz functions on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Subsection
2.3
states the existence of endoscopic transfer in this context.

In Section
3
,
we establish Theorem
1.0.3
, the real analogue of Waldspurger’s identity.
Subsection
3.1
treats the elliptic case,
where the endoscopic datum is elliptic,
X_{H}
is
G^{*}
-elliptic, and
X_{G}\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}
.
Subsection
3.2
recalls Harish-Chandra uniqueness theorem,
which asserts that any
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite invariant tempered distribution of elliptic type on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})

is uniquely determined by its values on the elliptic locus.
We then show that both
{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)
and
\widetilde{{\mathrm{D}}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)
satisfy these conditions,
thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.0.3
.

2.1.
Endoscopic datum

In this subsection, we briefly recall the definitions of endoscopic datum
and transfer factors on Lie algebras,
following
[
Wal97
, §2]
,
[

[

Endoscopic tuples

Let
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbb{R}})
be the set of tuples

{\mathrm{E}}=(G,G^{*},\varphi,H,s,\xi)
where:

(1)

\varphi\colon G\to G^{*}
is an inner twist between two reductive algebraic groups over
{\mathbb{R}}
,
with
G^{*}
the quasi-split pure inner form of
G
;

(2)

(H,s,\xi)
is an
endoscopic triple
(
[
Kot84
, §7]
), meaning that

•

H
is a quasi-split reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{R}}
;

•

\xi\colon{}^{L}H\to{}^{L}G
is an
L
-embedding from the
L
-group of
H
to that of
G
;

•

s\in Z_{\hat{H}}
, the center of the dual group
\hat{H}
, satisfies:

–

\xi(\hat{H})
is the connected component of the centralizer of
\xi(s)
in
\hat{G}
;

–

the image of
s
in
Z_{\hat{H}}/\xi^{-1}(Z_{\hat{G}})
is fixed by
\Gamma
;

–

its image
\bar{s}
in

\pi_{0}\big([Z_{\hat{H}}/\xi^{-1}(Z_{\hat{G}})]^{\Gamma}\big)

lies in the kernel of the natural map

\pi_{0}\big([Z_{\hat{H}}/\xi^{-1}(Z_{\hat{G}})]^{\Gamma}\big)\longrightarrow H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},Z_{\hat{G}}).

Fix
\Gamma
-stable Borel pairs

({\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{T}})\text{ of }G^{*},({\mathbb{B}}_{H},{\mathbb{T}}_{H})\text{ of }H,(\hat{{\mathbb{B}}},\hat{{\mathbb{T}}})\text{ of }\hat{G},\text{ and }(\hat{{\mathbb{B}}}_{H},\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}_{H})\text{ of }\hat{H}.

Since Borel pairs are unique up to conjugation,
we may assume that

\xi(\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}_{H})=\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}
and

\xi(\hat{{\mathbb{B}}}_{H})\subset\hat{{\mathbb{B}}}.
The restriction
\xi|_{\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}_{H}}
induces a dual isomorphism

\eta\colon{\mathbb{T}}_{H}\to{\mathbb{T}}

over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 2.1.1
.

Since
\hat{H}\subset\hat{G}
,
we have
W^{\hat{H}}\subset W^{\hat{G}}
,
where
W^{\hat{H}}
and
W^{\hat{G}}
are the absolute Weyl groups of
\hat{H}
and
\hat{G}
, respectively.
Hence, the isomorphism

\xi|_{\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}_{H}}\colon\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}_{H}\to\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}

is equivariant with respect to the actions of

W^{\hat{H}}\hookrightarrow W^{\hat{G}}
.
Passing to the dual side, and using the fact that

W^{H}\simeq W^{\hat{H}}\hookrightarrow W^{\hat{G}}\simeq W^{G}
,
we see that

\eta\colon{\mathbb{T}}_{H}\to{\mathbb{T}}

is also equivariant with respect to the actions of

W^{H}\hookrightarrow W^{G}
.

G^{*}
-regular elements

.

X_{H}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathrm{reg}}

-regular

h\in H

where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
is the Lie algebra of
{\mathbb{T}}_{H}
,
and
{\mathrm{Ad}}
denotes the adjoint action.

{\mathfrak{h}}_{G^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}

-regular elements in

.

.

2.2.
Orbital integrals and stable orbital integrals

In this subsection, we recall the spectral characterization of Lie algebra orbital integrals
and their stable variants over
{\mathbb{R}}
, following

[
Bou94a
,
Bou94b
]
and
[
She79a
]
.

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{R}}
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
When no confusion arises, we write
G=G({\mathbb{C}})
and
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Fix a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\Sigma({\mathfrak{t}})
be the set of roots of
{\mathfrak{t}}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
and let
\Sigma^{+}({\mathfrak{t}})\subset\Sigma({\mathfrak{t}})
be a choice of positive roots.
The Cartan involution on
G
induces a decomposition

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}^{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}^{-},

where
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\pm}
are the
\pm 1
-eigenspaces.

(2.2.1)

A root
\alpha\in\Sigma({\mathfrak{t}})
is called

if
\alpha|_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{+}}=0
.
Let
\Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})\subset\Sigma({\mathfrak{t}})
denote the set of imaginary roots.

For
\alpha\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})
, let
H_{\alpha}
be its coroot in
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Then
\overline{H_{\alpha}}=-H_{\alpha}
.
Let
X_{\alpha}
be a root vector associated to
\alpha
,
and choose
c\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
so that the root vector

X_{-\alpha}=c\overline{X_{\alpha}}

associated to
-\alpha
satisfies

[X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha}.

Consider the real
3
-dimensional subspace and its embedding

(2.2.2)

{\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha}\;+\;{\mathbb{R}}H_{\alpha}\;+\;{\mathbb{R}}X_{-\alpha}\ \hookrightarrow\ {\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}).

•

If
c>0
, then the embedding
(
2.2.2
) lifts to a homomorphism

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{R}}),

and we call
\alpha
a
noncompact root
.

•

If
c<0
, then the embedding
(
2.2.2
) lifts to a homomorphism

{\mathrm{S}}{\mathrm{U}}_{2}\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{R}}),

and we call
\alpha
a
compact root
.

Jump datum

Let
X_{0}\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
be a semisimple element such that
the derived algebra of its centralizer

{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{X_{0}}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}
be a
fundamental Cartan subalgebra
of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{X_{0}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
,
that is, under the Cartan decomposition

{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{X_{0}}={\mathfrak{k}}_{X_{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{X_{0}},

the real subspace
{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{X_{0}}

has maximal dimension among all Cartan subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{X_{0}}
.

Let
\pm\alpha
be the unique roots of
{\mathfrak{t}}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
that vanish on
X_{0}
.
Since
{\mathfrak{t}}
is fundamental, these roots satisfy

\pm\alpha\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})\quad\text{and are noncompact.}

Choose nonzero root vectors

corresponding to
\pm\alpha

[H_{\alpha},X_{\pm\alpha}]=\pm 2X_{\pm\alpha},\qquad[X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha},\qquad\overline{X_{\alpha}}=X_{-\alpha}.

{\mathfrak{g}}_{X_{0}}

Define

The mapping

(2.2.3)

\gamma\ \longmapsto\ \gamma\circ s^{-1}

induces a bijection

\Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})\cap\{\alpha\}^{\perp}\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}),

\{\alpha\}^{\perp}=\{\,\beta\in\Sigma({\mathfrak{t}})\mid\beta(H_{\alpha})=0\,\}.

Let
\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})\subset\Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})

be a chosen set of positive imaginary roots.
Under the map (

{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}

(2.2.4)

Following
[

is

With the notation above, a

Spectral characterization

Let
{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
be a
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant completely open neighborhood,
meaning that
{\mathcal{U}}
contains the semisimple part of the Jordan decomposition of every element in

.
Let

denote the subset of regular semisimple elements in

.

Following
[
Bou94a
]
, define
{\mathcal{O}}{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{U}})
(resp.
{\mathcal{O}}{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{U}})
)
as the space of
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant functions

\mathrm{J}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathcal{U}}_{\mathrm{reg}})

satisfying the following conditions:

I_{1}({\mathfrak{g}})

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
,

K^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}
a compact neighborhood,
and

{\mathfrak{t}}

Let
\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}
be the restriction of

to
{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
.
Then the function
\partial(u)\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}
is bounded on

K^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathcal{U}}\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
.

I_{2}({\mathfrak{g}})

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
,
and
\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})\subset\Sigma_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})

a system of positive imaginary roots.
Define

{\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\colon X\mapsto\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\frac{\alpha(X)}{|\alpha(X)|},\quad X\in{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathrm{reg}}.

Then
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}

extends smoothly to the complement of the imaginary noncompact roots
in
{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}
.

I_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})

Let
(X_{0},{\mathfrak{t}},\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}}),{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime},\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}),s)

be a jump datum (Definition
2.2.1
),
and
u\in{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{t}})
a constant-coefficient differential operator on
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\partial(u)\big({\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}\big)(X_{0}^{+})-\partial(u)\big({\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}\big)(X_{0}^{-})

\displaystyle=i\,d(X_{0})\,\partial(s\cdot u)\big({\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime})}\mathrm{J}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}}\big)(X_{0}),

where
d(X_{0})=2
or
1
depending on whether the reflection for
\alpha

can be realized in

. Here

\partial(u)\big({\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}\big)(X_{0}^{\pm})=\lim_{t\to\pm 0}\partial(u)\big({\mathfrak{a}}_{\Sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{im}}({\mathfrak{t}})}\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}\big)(X_{0}+itH_{\alpha}),

and for sufficiently small

,
we have
X_{0}\pm itH_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
,
so the above limit is well-defined.

I_{4}({\mathfrak{g}})

This condition specifies the growth behavior:

({\mathcal{C}})

For
\mathrm{J}\in{\mathcal{O}}{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{U}})
and any Cartan
{\mathfrak{t}}
,
the restriction
\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}
is compactly supported on
{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}
.

({\mathcal{S}})

For
\mathrm{J}\in{\mathcal{O}}{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{U}})
and any Cartan
{\mathfrak{t}}
,
the restriction
\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}}
lies in the Schwartz space

{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathrm{reg}})
,
with seminorms

\nu_{P,u}(\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}})=\sup_{X\in{\mathfrak{t}}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\Big|P(X)(\partial(u)\mathrm{J}_{\mathfrak{t}})(X)\Big|<\infty,

where

is a constant-coefficient differential operator on
{\mathfrak{t}}
,
and

is a polynomial function on
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

(
[

With the notation above, the map

(

In particular, for

3.2.
Harish-Chandra uniqueness theorem

In this subsection, we show that under the reduction (
3.1.1
), Proposition
3.1.1
suffices to deduce Theorem
3.0.1
for any
X_{G}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, with the aid of the Harish-Chandra uniqueness theorem, i.e.,
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite, invariant, tempered distributions on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
of
elliptic type
are uniquely determined by their values on the elliptic locus. By showing that both

\frac{{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)}{{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(\cdot)^{1/2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\widetilde{{\mathrm{D}}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)}{{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(\cdot)^{1/2}}

satisfy the hypotheses of the Harish-Chandra uniqueness theorem, we reduce the proof of Theorem
3.0.1
to Proposition
3.1.1
.

We begin by recalling the statement of the Harish-Chandra uniqueness theorem. Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{R}}
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Recall that
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is the algebra of constant coefficient differential operators on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
which acts naturally on the space of tempered distributions on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
. A tempered distribution on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
is said to be
invariant
if it is invariant under the conjugation action of
G({\mathbb{R}})
, and it is called an
eigendistribution
if it is an eigenvector for the action of
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, with eigencharacter given by a
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant polynomial.

The following lemma is due to Harish-Chandra (
[
Var77
, Part I, §7.1]
).

Lemma 3.2.1
.

For any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
, the tempered distribution
\widehat{j}^{G}(X,\cdot)
, which is the Fourier transform of
\mathrm{J}_{G}(X,\cdot)
, is a
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant tempered eigendistribution. That is, there exists a homomorphism

\chi^{G}_{[X]}:{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}

such that for all
z\in{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
,

\partial(z)\widehat{j}^{G}(X,\cdot)=\chi^{G}_{[X]}(z)\widehat{j}^{G}(X,\cdot).

Remark 3.2.2
.

From
[
Var77
, Part I, §7.1]
)
, after identifying
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
with the space of conjugation invariant polynomials on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
via a conjugation invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})
:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{C}})]

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\hat{z}

we have

\chi^{G}_{[X]}(z)=\hat{z}(iX).

By Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz, up to conjugation
\chi^{G}_{[X]}
is uniquely determined by
X
.

Following
[
Var77
, Theorem 13]
and
[
Var77
, p.118]
, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.2.3
.

A tempered, invariant,
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite distribution
\mu
on
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
is said to be of
elliptic type
if there exists a finite set of elliptic regular semi-simple elements

{\mathcal{X}}=\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}

such that

\partial(\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{X}})\mu=0,\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{X}}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}\ker(\chi^{G}_{[X_{j}]}).

In particular, for any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}
, the distribution
\widehat{j}^{G}(X,\cdot)
is of elliptic type.

The following result is due to Harish-Chandra (
[
Var77
, Theorem 13, p. 116]
).

Theorem 3.2.4
.

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
admits a elliptic Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}^{e}({\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\mu
be a tempered, invariant,
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite distribution of elliptic type, and let
\mathrm{F}_{\mu}
denote the analytic function on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
associated to
\mu
. Then

\mathrm{F}_{\mu}|_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{e}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu=0.

In particular, any finite linear combination of tempered, invariant eigendistributions of elliptic type is uniquely determined by its restriction to the elliptic locus.

To complete the proof of Theorem
3.0.1
under the reduction
(
3.1.1
), it remains, using
Theorem
3.2.4
together with
Proposition
3.1.1
, to establish the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2.5
.

Under the assumption (
3.1.1
), if
X_{H}
is
G^{*}
-elliptic, then both

\frac{{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)}{{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(\cdot)^{1/2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\widetilde{{\mathrm{D}}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)}{{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(\cdot)^{1/2}}

are tempered, invariant,
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite distributions of elliptic type.

Proof.

By definition,

\frac{{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},X_{G})}{{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(X_{G})^{1/2}}=\gamma_{\psi}({\mathfrak{g}})\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{G}^{\prime}}\in{\Gamma}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}}))}\Delta(X_{H},X_{G}^{\prime})\widehat{j}^{G}(X_{G}^{\prime},X_{G}).

Since
X_{H}
is
G^{*}
-elliptic, we have
X_{G}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{ell}}
whenever
\Delta(X_{H},X_{G}^{\prime})\neq 0
. It follows that

\frac{{\mathrm{D}}_{G,H}(X_{H},\cdot)}{{\mathrm{D}}^{G}(\cdot)^{1/2}}

is a finite linear combination of tempered, invariant eigendistributions of elliptic type. Hence, by Theorem
3.2.4
, it is itself of elliptic type.

It remains to show that

(3.2.1)

{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite distribution of elliptic type.

The

-invariance of (

) is clear. To verify temperedness, observe that

is globally bounded, as shown in

) defines a tempered distribution.

In order to show that (

) is
{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-finite and of elliptic type, we recall the framework of Harish-Chandra semi-simple descent, following

, §3.2]

Harish-Chandra semi-simple descent

Definition 3.2.6
.

An open subset
\omega\subset{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
is called
completely
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant
if

(1)

\omega
is
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant, and

(2)

for any
X\in\omega
, the semisimple part of
X
in its Jordan decomposition also lies in
\omega
.

Definition 3.2.7
.

Let
\omega\subset{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
be a completely
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant open subset.

•

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\omega)^{G}
denote the space of smooth
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant functions on
\omega
;

•

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\omega)^{G}
denote the space of
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant distributions on
\omega
.

Definition 3.2.8
.

Let
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}})
be a semisimple element.
An open subset
\omega_{X}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
is called
G
-good
if it is completely
G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant and the map

(3.2.2)

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\times^{G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})}G({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathbb{R}}),

\displaystyle(Y,g)

\displaystyle\longmapsto g^{-1}Yg,

induces an analytic isomorphism between

\omega_{X}\times^{G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})}G({\mathbb{R}})
and
\omega_{X}^{G}
,
where
\omega_{X}^{G}
denotes the image of the map (
3.2.2
). Here,
\omega_{X}\times^{G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})}G({\mathbb{R}})
is the quotient of

\omega_{X}\times G({\mathbb{R}})
by the
G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
-action

g_{X}\cdot(Y,g)=\big(g_{X}Yg_{X}^{-1},\,g_{X}g\big),\quad g_{X}\in G_{X}({\mathbb{R}}),\;(Y,g)\in\omega_{X}\times G({\mathbb{R}}).

From
[
BP20
, §3.2]
, an open subset

\omega_{X}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
is
G
-good if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

\omega_{X}
is completely
G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant;

•

For any
Y\in\omega_{X}
,

\eta^{G}_{X}(Y):=\big|\det\big(\mathrm{ad}(Y)\big)\big|_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}}\neq 0;

•

For any
g\in G({\mathbb{R}})
, the intersection

g^{-1}\omega_{X}g\cap\omega_{X}
is nonempty if and only if

g\in G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Let
\omega_{X}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
be a
G
-good open neighborhood of
X
, and set
\omega=\omega_{X}^{G}
.
Then
\omega
is completely
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant, since
\omega_{X}
is completely
G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant.
We have the following integration formula:

\int_{\omega}f(Y)\,dY\;=\;\int_{G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash G({\mathbb{R}})}\int_{\omega_{X}}f\big(g^{-1}Yg\big)\,\eta_{X}^{G}(Y)\,dY\,dg,\quad f\in L^{1}(\omega).

For every function
f
defined on
\omega
, let
f_{X,\omega_{X}}
be the function on
\omega_{X}
defined by

f_{X,\omega_{X}}(Y):=\eta_{X}^{G}(Y)^{1/2}\,f(Y).

Then the map
f\mapsto f_{X,\omega_{X}}
induces the following topological isomorphisms:

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\omega)^{G}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\omega_{X})^{G_{X}},\quad\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\omega_{\mathrm{reg}})^{G}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\omega_{X,{\mathrm{reg}}})^{G_{X}},

where
\omega_{\mathrm{reg}}=\omega\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\omega_{X,{\mathrm{reg}}}=\omega_{X}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{reg}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Similarly, we have an isomorphism of spaces of invariant distributions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\omega)^{G}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\omega_{X})^{G_{X}},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\longmapsto T_{X,\omega_{X}},

where for
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\omega)^{G}
, the distribution
T_{X,\omega_{X}}
is the unique
G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant distribution on
\omega_{X}
satisfying

\langle T,f\rangle=\int_{G_{X}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash G({\mathbb{R}})}\langle T_{X,\omega_{X}},\,({}^{g}f)_{X,\omega_{X}}\rangle\,dg,\quad f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\omega).

Moreover, if
f
is a locally integrable
G({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant function on
\omega
, then
f_{X,\omega_{X}}
is also locally integrable and

(T_{f})_{X,\omega_{X}}=T_{\,f_{X,\omega_{X}}},

where
T_{f}
and
T_{\,f_{X,\omega_{X}}}
denote the locally integrable distributions represented by
f
and
f_{X,\omega_{X}}
, respectively.

For an endoscopic tuple

{\mathrm{E}}=(G,G^{*},\varphi,H,s,\xi)\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbb{R}})
,
note that
\hat{G}
and
G
share the same absolute Weyl group, and
\hat{H}\subset\hat{G}
.
By Chevalley restriction theorem, after fixing an identification of maximal tori

H\supset T_{H}\simeq T_{G}\subset G

which is equivariant under the embedding of absolute Weyl groups

W^{H}\hookrightarrow W^{G}
as in Remark
2.1.1
,
we obtain a chain of embeddings of
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras:

{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})\;\simeq\;{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathfrak{t}}_{G}]^{W^{G}}\;\hookrightarrow\;{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}]^{W^{H}}\;\simeq\;{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{h}}).

Similarly, for any semisimple element
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
Chevalley restriction theorem provides a natural
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra embedding

{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{X}).

The following proposition is a consequence of
[
BP20
, Lemma 3.2.1]

Discovery of organelle-targeted active ingredients combination from Qifu decoction to treat heart failure via modulating cardiac mitochondria-lysosome dysfunction and contacts
Authors: Xin Ding, Qian Zhou, Ya Zhang, Junli Liang, Xuemei Su, Hongrui Ma, Ping Li, Jing Ma, Guangguo Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstract

Qifu decoction (QFD), composed of Astragalus mongholicus Bunge (Huangqi) and Aconitum carmichaelii Debeaux (Fuzi), is an efficient formula against heart failure (HF) [1], but the active ingredient combination (AIC) and therapeutic mechanism of QFD remain unclear. Both mitochondria and lysosomes are essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis [2], and cardiac mitochondria and lysosomes play the vital role of the development and progression of HF [3]. Preventing dysfunction of both organelles is the vital therapeutic strategy for HF. There are dozens of proteins localized to the mitochondrial and lysosomal membranes that control the transport of ions and substances across the membrane and are integral to mitochondrial and lysosomal function [4]. Mutations or aberrant expression of these proteins trigger a variety of disorders, making mitochondrial and lysosomal membranes attractive targets for the drug development of new anti-HF medication [5]. Hence, finding the bioactive compounds from QFD that interact with the mitochondrial and/or lysosomal membranes is promising for the treatment of HF. Moreover, the accurate profiling of the mitochondrial and lysosomal proteome without interference from cytoplasm and other organelles would be conducive to better dissect the underlying mechanisms.

For this purpose, three parts were designed and conducted. First, two novel organelles (mitochondria and lysosomes) membranes-coated magnetic nanoparticles (MM-MNPs and LM-MNPs) were designed to develop screening platform for the discovery of organelle-targeted active ingredients from QFD against HF, in which cardiac mitochondrial and lysosomal membranes fragments were separately immobilized onto epoxy functionalized magnetic poly(glycidyl methacrylate/divinyl benzene) (GMA/DVB) microsphere by covalent binding. Second, based on the adsorption characteristics, pharmacological verification and the compatibility theory of QFD, songorine (S) and neoline (N) from Fuzi, calycosin (C), and formononetin (F) (SNCF) from Huangqi were screened out and subsequently selected as representative AIC SNCF. Then the cardioprotective efficacies of SNCF were tested by in vitro and in vivo experiments. Third, the mechanisms of the SNCF were revealed by an integrated model of cardiac mitochondrial and lysosomal proteomics. The materials and methods are shown in the Supplementary data.

In the first part, the magnetic bead GMA/DVB-MNPs were first synthesized and characterized (Fig. S1). Then, the mitochondria and lysosome membranes were separately immobilized on the GMA/DVB-MNPs to obtain MM-MNPs and LM-MNPs through covalent reaction between the high-density epoxy groups on the microspheres and the amine groups on membrane phospholipids (Fig. 1A), in which the membrane proteins purity, immobilization condition, morphology, and magnetization curves were systematically assessed (Fig. S2). The adsorption selectivity, adsorption capacity, and capture conditions of the MM-MNPs and LM-MNPs were further evaluated by using a mitochondrial positive drug rotenone (mitochondrial electron transport chain complex I inhibitor), a lysosomal positive drug SG-094 (lysosomal two-pore channel protein 2 antagonist), and a negative drug tetracycline (Fig. S3). These results suggested that the mass transfer rates of rotenone onto MM-MNPs and SG-094 onto LM-MNPs were fast, which was beneficial to quickly capture bioactive ingredients in real sample applications. Moreover, 41 chemical compounds were identified in QFD (Fig. S4 and Table S1). Songorine, neoline, and talatisamine originating from Fuzi, and calycosin and formononetin originating from Huangqi, which exhibited stronger absorption capacity on the MM-MNPs or LM-MNPs, were screened out with the protective effects in vitro, whereas hypaconitine with stronger absorption capacity showed no protective effect (Figs. S5 and S6 and Table S1).

In the second part, songorine, neoline, calycosin and formononetin were selected as representative AIC SNCF against HF. Their concentrations in QFD were determined by ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-quadrupole time-of-flight tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q-TOFMS) (Fig. S7 and Tables S2 and S3). According to the concentration results, the composition mass ratio 1:3:3:3 for songorine, neoline, calycosin, and formononetin, which was nearly original proportion in QFD, was selected to establish SNCF combination for further experiments. The combined in vitro use of SNCF produced more pronounced cardioprotective effects on cell viability, apoptosis, reactive oxygen species (ROS), mitoROS, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels, mitochondrial membrane potential, and lysosomal membrane permeability than any components used individually, which supported the compatibility rationale of QFD for countering cardiac injury (Figs. S6 and S8). The cardioprotective effects of SNCF combination on myocardial infarction-induced HF were evaluated in male C57BL/6 mice with permanent ligation of the left anterior descending coronary artery. As expected, SNCF combination (10 and 25 mg/kg) attenuated HF on left ventricular function, enzymatic indices, infarction size, inflammatory cell infiltration, collagen deposition, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and apoptosis (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT) dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL) and apoptosis related proteins) (Figs. S9 and S10 and Table S4). Collectively, these results showed the potential of SNCF combination as a novel therapeutic strategy for improving cardiac function.

In the third part, an integrated mitochondrial and lysosomal proteomics approach was applied to acquire a precise understanding of the mechanism of SNCF in treating HF, which was without interference from cytoplasm and other organelles. To our knowledge, this is the first study of the combination of mitochondrial and lysosomal proteomics to explore the intervention mechanisms of traditional Chinese medicines (TCMs) against HF from two key cardiac subcellular organelles. Following the clues from the joint pathway analysis of proteomic results, we concluded that SNCF combination attenuated HF by regulating mitophagy and improving myocardial metabolism via tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK), and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) signaling pathway as well as cholesterol metabolism (Figs. S11−S13 and Table S5). The combination of transmission electron microscopy imaging and proteomics as well as Western blot analysis revealed that SNCF combination produced protective effects on the function and morphology of infarct heart through untethering mitochondria-lysosome contacts via TBC1 domain family, member 15/fission, mitochondrial 1/Ras-associated binding protein 7 (Tbc1d15/Fis1/Rab7) pathway (Fig. S14).

In conclusion, two novel MM-MNPs and LM-MNPs based on high-density epoxy functionalized magnetic poly nanoparticles were designed to capture subcellular organelle-targeted active ingredients from QFD with high selectivity and efficiency. Based on the adsorption characteristics, bioactivity evaluation and the compatibility theory, songorine, neoline, calycosin, and formononetin were identified as a representative AIC SNCF against HF. Furthermore, an integrated model of cardiac mitochondrial and lysosomal proteomics revealed that SNCF could alleviate HF by improving myocardial metabolism and regulating mitophagy as well as mitochondria-lysosome contacts (Fig. 1B). This work provided a scientific basis for SNCF combination to attenuate HF and could be regarded as a useful pilot trial exploring potential active ingredients and the mechanism of TCMs at the mitochondrial and lysosomal level. Crucially, the designed MM-MNPs- and LM-MNPs-assisted screening system provided a high-throughput screening strategy for mitochondria/lysosome-targeted compounds from natural products.

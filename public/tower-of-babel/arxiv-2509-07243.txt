Recent research on ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solutions of stationary Navier-Stokes equations
Li Li, Xukai Yan

Abstract
We make an exposition of recent research on ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solutions of the three-dimensional incompressible stationary Navier-Stokes equations with singular rays. We also discuss properties of such solutions that are axisymmetric with no swirl, and present graphs illustrating examples that exhibit various typical types of singular behavior.



1
Introduction

We study the three-dimensional incompressible stationary Navier-Stokes equations,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\\
&{\rm div\hskip 1.42271pt}u=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity vector,
p:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure and
\nu>0
is the viscosity constant. These equations are invariant under the scaling
u(x)\to\lambda u(\lambda x)
and
p(x)\to\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x)
for any
\lambda>0
. It is natural to study solutions which are invariant under this scaling.
For such solutions,
u
is
(-1)
-homogeneous and
p
is
(-2)
-homogeneous, and we call them
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions according to the homogeneity of
u
. In general, a function
f
is said to be
(-\alpha)
-homogeneous if
f(x)=\lambda^{\alpha}f(\lambda x)
for any
\lambda>0
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be the standard Euclidean coordinates, and
(r,\theta,\phi)
be the spherical coordinates, where
r
is the radial distance from the origin,
\theta\in[0,\pi]
is the polar angle measured from the positive
x_{3}
-axis to the vector, and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
is the meridian angle about the
x_{3}
-axis. A vector field
u
can be written as

u=u_{r}e_{r}+u_{\theta}e_{\theta}+u_{\phi}e_{\phi},

where

e_{r}=\left(\begin{matrix}\sin\theta\cos\phi\\
\sin\theta\sin\phi\\
\cos\theta\end{matrix}\right),\hskip 14.22636pte_{\theta}=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\theta\cos\phi\\
\cos\theta\sin\phi\\
-\sin\theta\end{matrix}\right),\hskip 14.22636pte_{\phi}=\left(\begin{matrix}-\sin\phi\\
\cos\phi\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

A vector field
u
is said to be
axisymmetric
if the components
u_{r}
,
u_{\theta}
and
u_{\phi}
are independent of
\phi
, and it is called
no-swirl
if
u_{\phi}\equiv 0
. For
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions
(u,p)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, (
1
) can be reduced to a system of partial differential equations for
(u,p)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. For any set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
, a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution
(u,p)
on
\Omega
is understood to have been extended to the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid x/|x|\in\Omega\}
so that
u
is
(-1)
-homogeneous and
p
is
(-2)
-homogeneous. We use this convention throughout the paper.

In 1944, Landau
[
22
]
discovered a 3-parameter family of explicit
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions to the stationary Navier-Stokes equations in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
, see also
[
49
]
and
[
50
]
. These solutions, now known as
Landau solutions
, are axisymmetric with no swirl and have exactly one point singularity at the origin. The expressions of Landau solutions
(u^{b},p^{b})
are given by

u^{b}=\frac{2}{r}(\frac{A^{2}-1}{(A-\cos\theta)^{2}}-1)e_{r}-\frac{2\sin\theta}{r(A-\cos\theta)}e_{\theta},\quad p^{b}=-\frac{4(A\cos\theta-1)}{r^{2}(A-\cos\theta)^{2}},

(2)

for some constant
A>1
. The net force associated with Landau solutions is a Dirac mass

f=b\delta_{0}
, where
b=(0,0,\beta)
and
\beta=16\pi(A+\frac{1}{2}A^{2}\ln\frac{A-1}{A+1}+\frac{4A}{3(A^{2}-1)})
.
Landau interpreted his solutions as a model of some jet discharged from the origin.

It is natural to ask whether there exist other
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) except Landau solutions. Tian and Xin proved in
[
52
]
that all nonzero
(-1)
-homogeneous, axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
must be Landau solutions. Šverák established the following result in 2006:

Theorem A

(
[
53
]
)
.

All
(-1)
-homogeneous nonzero solutions of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
are Landau solutions.

In the same paper, Šverák also proved that there is no nonzero
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of the incompressible stationary Navier-Stokes equations in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
for
n\geq 4
. In dimension two, he characterized all such solutions satisfying a zero flux condition. Homogeneous solutions of (
1
) have also been studied in various other works, see, e.g.
[
6
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
21
,
33
,
40
,
41
,
42
,
44
,
49
,
50
,
54
,
55
,
56
]
. There have also been works on homogeneous solutions of Euler’s equations, see
[
1
,
35
,
48
]
and the references therein.

A natural follow-up problem is to study
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) with possible finitely many singular rays. To this end, we consider solutions of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\})
where
P_{1},...,P_{m}
are finitely many points on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Such solutions are subject to less restrictive symmetry and regularity conditions, making related questions—such as their classification and stability—more challenging. Below are several central problems in the study of such solutions:

Questions:

1.

Existence and classification
: Let
m\geq 1
and
P_{1},...,P_{m}
be arbitrary points on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Does there exist any
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\})
? If yes, how can we classify such solutions?

2.

Singularity behavior
: What is the asymptotic behavior of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) near a singular point
P
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
?

3.

Vanishing viscosity limit
: What is the limiting behavior of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) as the viscosity
\nu\to 0
? How do they converge or not converge to solutions of Euler equations?

4.

Asymptotic stability
: Are
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions stable under perturbations? In particular, under what conditions such solutions are asymptotically stable, and in what norms or perturbation settings?

The classification of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
(Theorem
A
) in general does not extend to solutions with singularities on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. To study the above problems, a natural first step is to investigate solutions that are axisymmetric.

The existence and classification of
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
, where
S
is the south pole and
N
is the north pole, were studied in
[
25
]
-
[
27
]
.
In
[
25
]
, all such no-swirl solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
were classified, and
existence and nonexistence results of such solutions with nonzero swirl in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
were established. In
[
26
]
, a complete classification of all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
was provided. In
[
27
]
, existence and non-existence results for
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
with nonzero swirl were established.
Additionally,
[
25
]
derived the asymptotic expansions of all local
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) near a singular ray. In
[
30
]
, an optimal removable singularity result for
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions near a singular ray was established without assuming axisymmetry. The vanishing viscosity limit of
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
was analyzed in
[
28
]
.
Furthermore, the asymptotic stability of the least singular axisymmetric no-swirl solutions under

-perturbations was proved in
[
31
]
, while the asymptotic stability of Landau solutions under

-perturbations was established in
[

In this paper, we provide an exposition to recent developments in the study of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (

) with singular rays, along with make some remarks and discussions.
In Section

In Section

2.1
Existence and classification

The existence and classification of
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
were studied in
[
25
,
26

27

For these solutions, the system (

\mathbb{S}^{2}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu\frac{d^{2}u_{r}}{d\theta^{2}}+(\nu\cot\theta-u_{\theta})\frac{du_{r}}{d\theta}+u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2}+u_{\phi}^{2}+2p=0;\\
&\frac{d}{d\theta}(\frac{1}{2}u_{\theta}^{2}-\nu u_{r}+p)=\cot\theta u_{\phi}^{2};\\
&\nu\frac{d}{d\theta}(\frac{du_{\phi}}{d\theta}+\cot\theta u_{\phi})-u_{\theta}(\frac{du_{\phi}}{d\theta}+\cot\theta u_{\phi})=0;\\
&u_{r}+\frac{du_{\theta}}{d\theta}+\cot\theta u_{\theta}=0.\quad\quad\mbox{(divergence free condition)}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

If we further assume that

has no swirl, i.e.

, then (
1
) is reduced to

\nu(\frac{du_{\theta}}{d\theta}-\cot\theta u_{\theta})-\frac{1}{2}u_{\theta}^{2}=\frac{a\cos^{2}\theta+b\cos\theta+c}{\sin^{2}\theta}

(4)

for some constants
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
u_{r}
and

can be determined by

u_{r}=-\frac{du_{\theta}}{d\theta}-u_{\theta}\cot\theta,\quad p=\nu u_{r}-\frac{1}{2}u_{\theta}^{2}+\textrm{const},\quad\textrm{ on }\mathbb{S}^{2}.

(5)

By rescaling
\tilde{u}=u/\nu
, (
1
) is equivalent to the same equation of
\tilde{u}
with
\nu=1
. For convenience,
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
focused on the case
\nu=1
when studying the existence and classification of solutions. In this paper, we present all the results for general
\nu
to more clearly illustrate the behavior of
u
in terms of
\nu
.
In our study, the following change of variables and functions played an important role.

y:=\cos\theta,\quad U_{r}:=u_{r}\sin\theta,\quad U_{\theta}:=u_{\theta}\sin\theta,\quad U_{\phi}:=u_{\phi}\sin\theta.

(6)

Then (
1
) for
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions is converted to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu(1-y^{2})U_{\theta}^{\prime}+2\nu yU_{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}U_{\theta}^{2}+\int\int\int\frac{2U_{\phi}(s)U_{\phi}^{\prime}(s)}{1-s^{2}}dsdtdl=b_{1}y^{2}+b_{2}y+b_{3},\\
&\nu(1-y^{2})U_{\phi}^{\prime\prime}+U_{\theta}U_{\phi}^{\prime}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(7)

where
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
are some constants,
y\in(-1,1)
,
and the derivatives are in
y
. The divergence free condition is equivalent to

u_{r}=U^{\prime}_{\theta}(y).

This change of variables had been used by Serrin
[
44
]

2.1
, all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
1
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\}
are classified as the two parameter family
\{(u^{\tau,\sigma},p^{\tau,\sigma})\mid(\tau,\sigma)\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\}
.
In
[
25
]
, it was proved that for every
(\tau,\sigma)\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\cap\{\tau<3\nu\}
, there exists a one-parameter family of axisymmetric solutions with nonzero swirl in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
, emanating from
u^{\tau,\sigma}
in the
e_{\phi}
direction.
In contrast, for
(\tau,\sigma)\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\cap\{\tau>3\nu\}
, such solutions do not exist.
Nearby
\{(u^{\tau,\sigma},p^{\tau,\sigma})\mid(\tau,\sigma)\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\tau>3\nu\}
, there is no
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solution with nonzero swirl.

The existence result was established using perturbation methods. Define
b_{\tau,\sigma}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta}(u_{\theta})_{\tau,\sigma}dt
, and set

v_{\tau,\sigma}=\frac{\nu}{\sin\theta}\left(\int_{0}^{\theta}e^{-b_{\tau,\sigma}(t)}\sin tdt\right)e_{\phi}.

Then
v_{\tau,\sigma}
is a solution of the linearized equation of (
1
) at
(u^{\tau,\sigma},p^{\tau,\sigma})
.
We decompose
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}=\mathcal{I}_{\nu 1}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\nu 2}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\nu 3}
, where

\mathcal{I}_{\nu 1}=\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\cap\{\tau<2\nu,\sigma<\nu-\frac{\tau}{4}\},\mbox{ }\mathcal{I}_{\nu 2}=\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\cap\{\tau=2\nu,\sigma<\frac{\nu}{2}\},\mbox{ }\mathcal{I}_{\nu 3}=\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\cap\{\tau\geq 2\nu,\sigma=\frac{\tau}{4}\}.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
25
]
)
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of one of the four sets
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 1}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 2}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 3}\cap\{2\nu<\tau<3\nu\}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 3}\cap\{\tau=2\nu\}
, then there exist
\delta=\delta(K)>0
, and
(u,p)\in C^{\infty}(K\times(-\delta,\delta)\times(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\}))
such that for every
(\tau,\sigma,\beta)\in K\times(-\delta,\delta)
,
(u,p)(\tau,\sigma,\beta;\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
satisfies (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,x_{3})|x_{3}\leq 0\}
, with nonzero swirl if
\beta\neq 0
, and
||\left(\sin\frac{\theta+\pi}{2}\right)(u(\tau,\sigma,\beta)-u_{\tau,\sigma})||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
. Moreover,
\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}u(\tau,\sigma,\beta)|_{\beta=0}=v_{\tau,\sigma}
.

On the other hand, for
(\tau,\sigma)\in\mathcal{I}_{3}\cap\{\tau>3\nu\}
, there does not exist any sequence of solutions
\{u^{i}\}
of (
1
) in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
, with nonzero swirl, such that
||\left(\sin\frac{\theta+\pi}{2}\right)(u^{i}-u_{\tau,\sigma})||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.

This result in particular provided the first examples of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions with nonzero swirl of (
1
) and a singular ray exhibiting finite ordered singularity behavior, i.e.
\limsup_{|x|=1,x\to S}|u(x)||\textrm{dist}(x,S)|^{k}<\infty
for some
k>0
.
A more detailed and stronger version of Theorem
2.3
, including a uniqueness result, can be found in
[
25
]
(see Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.2 and Theorem 4.3 there).

The existence part of Theorem
2.3
was established using perturbation methods via implicit function theorems. The key step is to construct appropriate Banach spaces and operators, where the change of variables (
6
) and (
7
) was used. Three different sets of spaces are needed for
(\tau,\sigma)
in
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 1}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 2}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\nu 3}
, where the singular behaviors of
u_{\tau,\sigma}
are different.
In particular, for some
(\tau,\sigma)
on the boundary of
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
, the spaces involve logarithmic functions. The nonexistence part follows from analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the solutions near the singularity
S
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, see Theorem
2.4
for details.

Using a similar approach,
[
27
]
established the existence and nonexistence results for
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) with nonzero swirl in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{N,S\})
nearby the no-swirl solutions
(u^{c,\gamma},p^{c,\gamma})
. Detailed statements can be found in
[
27
]
.

2.1.3
Remarks on the existence of non-axisymmetric solutions

In this subsection, we make a brief discussion about the existence and nonexistence of non-axisymmetric
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) with singular rays.
Theorem
A
has ruled out the
existence of non-axisymmetric
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.
But what about solutions with singularities on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
?

Some special examples of non-axisymmetric
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions can be inferred from
[
53
]
, which
connects
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) to the conformal geometry on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
In
[
53
]
, it was proved that if
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
), then there exists some function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying

-\triangle_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi+2=2e^{\varphi}\quad\mbox{on }\mathbb{S}^{2},

(16)

such that
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Conversely, if
\varphi
is a solution to (
16
), then
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
is a solution to (
1
) after being extended to a
(-1)
-homogeneous vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
By the classical Liouville formula (see
[
34
,
8
]
), the solutions of (
16
) takes the form

\displaystyle\varphi=\ln\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}},

(17)

where
f
is a locally univalent meromorphic function and
z\in\mathbb{C}
is the stereographic projection of
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
This enables us to construct
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
), including non-axisymmetric ones, by selecting appropriate functions
f
.

Let
u
be a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
constructed via the Liouville formulas above. If
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, then the corresponding
f
must be of the form
f=az
for some constant
a
, and
u
is a Landau solution (see
[
53
]
). If
u
is axisymmetric but may have singularities on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, it was proved in
[
30
]
that
f=az^{\alpha}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Non-axisymmetric solutions can be constructed by choosing suitable forms of
f
, such as
f=ae^{bz}
, or
f=\int_{0}^{z}(t-z_{1})^{l_{1}}\cdots(t-z_{m})^{l_{m}-1}dt
, where the latter one generates solutions of (
1
) with multiple singularities on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Further investigation into the existence and nonexistence of other non-axisymmetric solutions is of interest. For instance, a natural question is whether nonaxisymmetric solutions exist nearby known axisymmetric solutions.

2.2
Asymptotic behavior of solutions near singular rays

To study
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) with finitely many singularities on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, it is helpful to first analyze the asymptotic behavior of solutions near an isolated singularity
P
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In particular, we focus on the asymptotic behavior and the classifications of solutions satisfying
u=O(1/{\rm dist}(x,P)^{k})
for some positive integer
k
. The asymptotic behavior of local
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in
B_{\delta}(S)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\}
, for any
\delta>0
, was analyzed in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
. In
[
30
]
, an optimal removable singularity result was established for
(-1)
-homogeneous solution
u\in C^{2}(B_{\delta}(S)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
. Below we give an exposition of these results and make some further discussion.

2.2.1
Asymptotic behavior of homogeneous axisymmetric solutions

In
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
, the asymptotic expansions of all local
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in
B_{\delta}(S)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\}
, for any
\delta>0
, were established. A summary of these results was provided in
[
30
]
, which is recalled below.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\delta>0
, and
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\})
be a
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solution of (1). Denote
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})
. Then
\tau:=\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|u_{\theta}
exists and is finite, and
u=O(1/\big||x^{\prime}|\ln|x^{\prime}|\big|^{2})
. Moreover,

(i)
If
\tau\geq 3\nu
, then
|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}
must be a constant, and
|x^{\prime}|u_{\theta}
and
u_{r}
must be real analytic functions in
1+\cos\theta
near
S
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

(ii)
If
2\nu<\tau<3\nu
, then either
|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}\equiv
constant, or
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|^{\tau/\nu-1}u_{\phi}
exists and is finite and not zero. Moreover,
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|^{2\tau/\nu-4}u_{r}
exists and is finite.

(iii)
If
\tau=2\nu
, then
\eta:=\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}(|x^{\prime}|u_{\theta}-2\nu)\ln|x^{\prime}|
exists and is
0
or
2\nu
.

- When
\eta=0
, then
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|^{\epsilon}u_{r}=0
for any
\epsilon>0
. Either
|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}
is a constant, or
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}
exists and is finite and not zero.

- When
\eta=2\nu
, then
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|^{2}\big|\ln|x^{\prime}|\big|^{2}u_{r}=-2\nu
, and
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}
exists and is finite.

(iv)
If
\tau<2\nu
and
\tau\not=0
, then
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}
and
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|^{\max\{\tau/\nu,0\}}u_{r}
both exist and are finite.

(v)
If
\tau=0
, then
\sigma:=\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|x^{\prime}|u_{\phi}
and

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}u_{r}/\ln|x^{\prime}|
both exist and are finite.
Moreover,
\tilde{u}:=u-\sigma/|x^{\prime}|e_{\phi}
is also a solution of (
1
), and

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|\tilde{u}|/\big|\ln|x^{\prime}|\big|
exists and is finite.

Note (i) implies that if
\tau\geq 3
and
u
is a
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solution in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S\})
, then
u_{\phi}\equiv 0
. This in particular provided the proof of the nonexistence part of Theorem
2.3
.

Similar results to those in Theorem
2.4
hold for solutions
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(N)\setminus\{N\})
. In an ongoing project
[
29
]
with Y.Y. Li, we have established the full asymptotic expansion for all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
1
) in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\}
. These expansions take the form of three types of infinite series for
\tau
in three different sets.
We have also proved the convergence of these series solutions and shown that all local
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
1
) in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\}
are completely classified by them.
We expect that similar results hold for axisymmetric solutions with nonzero swirl, while the expansions are more intricate. We plan to study this problem in the near future.

2.2.2
Removable singularity for
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}

The next step is to study the asymptotic behavior of general
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) near possible singular rays, without assuming axisymmetry.
In
[
30
]
, the following removable singularity problem was considered:
For local
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions (not necessarily axisymmetric) of (
1
) near a potential singularity on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, under what condition is the singularity removable?

There has been much study on the behavior of solutions of (
1
) near isolated singularities in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, see e.g.
[
9
,
45
,
47
,
46
,
7
,
19
,
20
,
37
,
38
,
53
]
.

The asymptotic behavior of axisymmetric solutions, as described in Theorem
2.4
, suggests that the least singular behavior of a solution near a singularity
S
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is of the order of
\ln{\rm dist}(x,S)
.
Therefore, a natural removable singularity condition is
u=o(\ln{\rm dist}(x,S))
. Denote
B_{\delta}(S):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid{\rm dist}(x,S)<\delta\}
for
\delta>0
.
The following result has been established in
[
30
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\delta>0
,

(u,p)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\})
be a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
). If

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}\frac{|u(x)|}{\ln{\rm dist}(x,S)}=0
, then
(u,p)
can be extended as a
C^{2}
function in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)
.

The above removable singularity result is optimal in the following sense: For any
\alpha>0
, there exists a
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solution
(u,p)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
of (
1
), such that
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|u(x)|/\ln{\rm dist}(x,S)=

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to N}|u(x)|/\ln{\rm dist}(x,N)=-\alpha
. Examples of such solutions can be found in
[
26
]
and
[
30
]
, see example
3.1
for a special case.

In view of Theorem
2.4
,
all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
are of the following three mutually exclusive types:

Type 1. Landau solutions, satisfying
\sup_{|x|=1}|u(x)|<\infty
;

Type 2. Solutions satisfying
0<\limsup_{|x|=1,x^{\prime}\to 0}|u(x)|/|\ln|x^{\prime}||<\infty
;

Type 3. Solutions satisfying
\limsup_{|x|=1,x^{\prime}\to 0}|x^{\prime}||u(x)|>0
.

Here we denote
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})
. Several examples of solutions with isolated singularities on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, along with discussions of their asymptotic behaviors, were provided in
[
30
]
and summarized below.

a.
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
The existence and classification of these solutions are discussed in Section
2.1
.
Among them, the only Type 2 solutions are a specific family of axisymmetric no-swirl solutions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
, given by
\{(u^{c,\gamma},p^{c,\gamma})\mid(c,\gamma)\in I_{\nu},c_{1}=c_{2}=0,c_{3}>-4\nu^{2},c_{3}\neq 0,\gamma^{-}<\gamma<\gamma^{+}\}
(see Section
2.1.1
for the definition of
u^{c,\gamma}
, and Example
3.1
for some special solutions). These solutions satisfy
\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to P}|u^{c,\gamma}|/\ln\textrm{dist}(x,P)=-2|c_{3}|
, where
P=S
or
N
. Landau solutions correspond to
\{(u^{c,\gamma},p^{c,\gamma})\mid c=0,\gamma^{-}<\gamma<\gamma^{+}\}
.
In
[
30
]
, it was shown that all other
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
are of Type 3.

b. Serrin’s solutions
In a pioneering work
[
44
]
concerning a representative model for tornado-like flows, Serrin studied
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\cap\{x_{3}>0\}
, with a singular ray along the positive
x_{3}
-axis and some boundary conditions on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
. Notably, the solutions formulated in
[
44
]
exhibit behavior distinct from Landau solutions. Near the north pole
N
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, these solutions satisfy
u_{\theta}=O(|x^{\prime}|\ln|x^{\prime}|)
,
u_{r}=O(\ln|x^{\prime}|)

and
0<\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to N}|x^{\prime}||u_{\phi}|<\infty
. In particular, Serrin’s solutions are of Type 3.

c. Solutions given by Liouville formulas

These solutions are discussed in Section
2.1.3
. Aside from Landau solutions, all examples obtained via the Liouville formulas are of Type 3.
There are no Type 2 solutions given by Liouville formulas.

d. Solutions that are also solutions of Euler’s equation
There is a family of
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
1
) that also satisfy the Euler’s equations. These solutions take the form
u_{\theta}=\frac{a\cos\theta+b}{\sin\theta}
,
u_{r}=a
,
u_{\phi}=0
,
p=-\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}+2ab\cos\theta}{2\sin^{2}\theta}
for some constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
These solutions are also of Type 3.

2.2.3
Further discussion on singular behavior

Consider general
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
), we raise the following questions.

Question 2.1
.

Let
\delta>0
,
P\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(P)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P\})
be a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
). What are the possible asymptotic behaviors of
u
near
P
?

Question 2.2
.

How can we classify
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (
1
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
that satisfy
u=O\left(|\textrm{dist}(x,S)|^{-k}\right)
for some integer
k\geq 1
?

By Theorem
A
(
[
53
]
),
all solutions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
are Landau solutions, which we refer to as Type 1 solutions. By the removable singularity result (Theorem
2.5
), for any
P\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution in
C^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(P)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P\})
satisfying
u=o(\ln\text{dist}(x,P))
can be smoothly extended across
P
. The next natural class of interest is the family of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions exhibiting Type 2 behavior, i.e. solutions satisfying
0<\limsup_{|x|=1,x^{\prime}\to 0}|u(x)|/|\ln|x^{\prime}||<\infty
.
A natural question is:
Does there exist a nonaxisymmetric
(-1)
-homogeneous solution exhibiting Type 2 behavior? Under what conditions must a Type 2 solution in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
be axisymmetric?

As shown in
[
30
]
, any Type 2 axisymmetric solution on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
must be no-swirl. The only Type 2 axisymmetric solutions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
are the family
\{u^{c,\gamma}\mid(c,\gamma)\in I_{\nu},c_{1}=c_{2}=0,c_{3}>-4\nu^{2},c_{3}\neq 0,\gamma^{-}<\gamma<\gamma^{+}\}
. In particular, there is no
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solution

u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P\})
of (
1
) satisfying
0<\limsup_{|x|=1,x\to P}|u(x)|/|\ln{\rm dist}(x,P)|<\infty
, where
P=S
or
N
.

If nonaxisymmetric solutions are considered, then the classification becomes much more involved. So far, the only nonaxisymmetric solutions we know are those constructed via Liouville formulas (see Section
2.1.3
).
These solutions are of the form
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
where
\varphi
is given by (
17
), generated from some meromorphic function
f
. Using this expression, it can be shown that such solutions do not exhibit Type 2 behavior.

2.3
Vanishing viscosity limit of homogeneous solutions

Let
\nu=0
in (
1
), then (
1
) reduces to the
3
D incompressible stationary Euler equations

v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla q=0,\quad{\rm div\hskip 1.42271pt}\textrm{ }v=0.

(18)

A fundamental question in fluid dynamics is whether solution of Navier-Stokes equations converge to the solutions of Euler equations as
\nu\to 0
.
Building on the classification of all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
1
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
,
[
28
]
studied the vanishing viscosity limit of sequences of these solutions.

The classification of all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of the Euler equations (
18
) were obtained in
[
52
]
.
Let
J_{0}
be defined by (
11
) with
\nu=0
, and
\partial^{\prime}J_{0}=(\partial J_{0}\cap\{c_{1}=0\textrm{ or }c_{2}=0\})\setminus\{0\}
, where
\partial J_{0}
denotes the boundary of
J_{0}
. Let
P_{c}(x)
be the second order polynomial given in (
10
).
Let
v^{\pm}_{c}=v^{\pm}_{c,r}\vec{e}_{r}+v^{\pm}_{c,\theta}\vec{e}_{\theta}
, where

v^{\pm}_{c,\theta}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2P_{c}(\cos\theta)}}{r\sin\theta},\quad v^{\pm}_{c,r}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\pm\frac{P_{c}^{\prime}(\cos\theta)}{r\sqrt{2P_{c}(\cos\theta)}},

and

q_{c}(r,\theta,\varphi)=-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}(P_{c}^{\prime\prime}(\cos\theta)+\frac{2P_{c}(\cos\theta)}{\sin^{2}\theta}).

Then
\{(v^{\pm}_{c},q_{c})\ |\ c\in\mathring{J}_{0}\cup\partial^{\prime}J_{0}\}
is the set of all
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of (
18
) in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
.

Let
c\in J_{0}
,
\nu_{k}\to 0
, and
(c_{k},\gamma_{k})\in I_{\nu_{k}}
be a sequence such that
c_{k}\to c
. The question is whether the corresponding
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions
(u_{\nu_{k}}^{c_{k},\gamma_{k}},p_{\nu_{k}}^{c_{k},\gamma_{k}})
(as identified in Theorem
2.2
) of (
1
) converge to one of the solutions
(v^{\pm}_{c},q^{\pm}_{c})
of (
18
) as
\nu_{k}\to 0
and
c_{k}\to c
.
For
c
and
c_{k}
in different parts of
J_{0}
and
J_{\nu_{k}}
, the limiting behaviors of
(u_{\nu_{k}}^{c_{k},\gamma_{k}},p_{\nu_{k}}^{c_{k},\gamma_{k}})
are different.
In particular, the behaviors of the upper and lower solutions

2.4
Asymptotic stability of homogeneous solutions

Consider the
3
D incompressible evolutionary
Navier-Stokes equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=f,&\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
{\rm div\hskip 1.42271pt}u=0,&\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\end{array}\right.

(19)

with initial velocity
u_{0}(x)
and external force
f=f(x,t)
.
When
f=0
, it is a classical result that there exists a weak solution to (
19
) satisfying
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=0
, demonstrating the asymptotic stability of the trivial solution
(see, e.g.,
[
4
,
15
,
18
,
23
,
24
,
36
,
43
,
51
]
). The asymptotic stability has also been studied for other steady states
[
5
,
6
,
16
,
17
,
3
]
. For Landau solutions, the external force takes the form
f=b\delta_{0}
for some constant
b
as mentioned in Section
1
.
In
[
16
]
, it was proved that small Landau solutions are asymptotically stable under
L^{2}
-perturbations. The
L^{p}
-stability of Landau solutions for
p\geq 3
has been studied by
[
33
,
56
]
.

Since the solutions obtained in
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
exhibit different behavior from Landau solutions, it is natural to explore their asymptotic stability or instability.
This question was studied in
[
31
]
for the class of
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) exhibiting Type 2 behavior. These solutions are the family
\{(u^{c,\gamma},p^{c,\gamma})\mid(c,\gamma)\in M_{\nu}\}
among the solutions classified in Theorem
2.2
, where
M_{\nu}:=\{(c,\gamma)\mid c_{1}=c_{2}=0,c_{3}>-4\nu^{2},\gamma^{-}<\gamma<\gamma^{+}\}
. Each of these solutions satisfies
u^{c,\gamma}=O(\ln|x^{\prime}|)
and
p^{c,\gamma}=O(\ln|x^{\prime}|)
as
x^{\prime}\to 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and they exhibit the least singular behavior among all
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions except Landau solutions.
The corresponding external force of such a solution is given by

f^{c,\gamma}=(4\pi c_{3}\ln|x_{3}|\partial_{x_{3}}\delta_{(0,0,x_{3})}-b^{c,\gamma}\delta_{0})e_{3}
, where
b^{c,\gamma}=2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\left(y|U_{\theta}^{\prime}|^{2}-\frac{2+y^{2}}{1-y^{2}}\nu U_{\theta}-\frac{y}{1-y^{2}}U_{\theta}^{2}\right)dy
.

For any fixed
(c,\gamma)\in M_{\nu}
,
let
u
be a solution of (
19
) with
f=f^{c,\gamma}
. Let

w=u-u^{c,\gamma}
,
\pi=p-p^{c,\gamma}
and
w_{0}=u_{0}-u^{c,\gamma}
.
Then
w
satisfies the perturbed system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{t}-\nu\Delta w+(w\cdot\nabla)w+(w\cdot\nabla)u^{c,\gamma}+(u^{c,\gamma}\cdot\nabla)w+\nabla\pi=0,&\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
{\rm div\hskip 1.42271pt}w=0,&\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x).&\end{array}\right.

(20)

Theorem 2.7

(
[
31
]
)
.

There exists some
\mu_{0}>0
, such that for any
c=(0,0,c_{3})
,
|(c,\gamma)|<\mu_{0}
and
w_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there exists a weak solution
w
of (
20
) in
\mathbf{X}:=L^{\infty}([0,\infty),L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,\infty),\dot{H}^{1}_{\sigma})
. Moreover,
w
is weakly continuous from
[0,\infty)
to
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and satisfies

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla\otimes w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}d\tau\leq\|w(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

for almost all
s\geq 0
, including
s=0
and all
t\geq s
.
Furthermore, any such weak solution satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=0
. If in addition
w_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
\frac{6}{5}<p<2
, then there exists some constant
C>0
, depending only on
(c,\gamma)
,
n,p
and
\|w_{0}\|_{p}
, such that
\|w(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}
, for all
t>0
.

Here
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
denote the space of all divergence free functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The proof of Theorem
2.7
relies on semigroup theory and follows the framework in
[
16
]
. The key step is to establish the energy estimates

|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla u^{c,\gamma})\cdot wdx|\leq K\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}
,
for some sufficiently small constant
K
, and any divergence free
v,w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
When
u^{c,\gamma}
is replaced by a small Landau solution
u^{b}
, for which
|\nabla u^{b}|=O(1/|x|^{2})
, the energy estimate follows from the classical Hardy’s inequality (see
[
16
]
).
However, the solutions
u^{c,\gamma}
are more singular,
which satisfy
|\nabla u^{c,\gamma}|=O(\frac{1}{|x||x^{\prime}|})
and Hardy’s inequality does not work in this context.
In
[
31
]
, the energy estimate was proved
by establishing a
new
weighted Hardy type inequality:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|w(x)|^{2}}{|x\|x^{\prime}|}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w(x)|^{2}\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{|x|}dx,\textrm{ for all }w\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(21)

where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},...,x_{n-1})
for dimensions
n\geq 2
, and
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
.
Note that (
21
) is stronger than the classical Hardy’s inequality (which is (
21
) with
|x^{\prime}|
replaced by
|x|
). This type of inequalities are of independent research interest.
In
[
32
]
, inequality (
21
) was further extended into a family of rather general anisotropic Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities.
The optimal constant and extreme functions have been studied recently by
[
2
,
13
]
.
The
L^{p}
-stability of Type 2 solutions
u^{c,\gamma}

has been studied in
[
57
]
for
p\geq 3
.

A natural next objective is to study the asymptotic stability of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions exhibiting Type 3 behavior. We begin with the least singular case, where
|\bar{u}|=O(1/|x^{\prime}|)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. These solutions are more singular than Type 2 solutions, and the stability proof in
[
31
]
does not apply. New approaches or analytical tools will be needed.
As a first step, we have identified the force of all the
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
in an ongoing project
[
29
]
.

-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions

of (
1

\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\{\theta_{0}<\theta<\theta_{1}\}

0\leq\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}\leq\pi

1
) is reduced to (
10
) of
U_{\theta}
, with
c=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(u,p)

if

3.1
Representation of
solutions

As mentioned in Section
2.1.1
, equation (
10
) is a Ricatti-type ODE and can be converted to a second-order linear ODE. Let
U_{\theta}
be a solution of (
10
) on some interval
(y_{0},y_{1})\subset(-1,1)
. Set
w(y):=e^{\int_{y_{0}}^{y}\frac{U_{\theta}(s)}{2\nu(1-s^{2})}ds}
, then
w\neq 0
on
(y_{0},y_{1})
and
U_{\theta}
can be expressed by

U_{\theta}=U_{\theta}[w]:=2\nu(1-y^{2})\frac{w^{\prime}(y)}{w(y)}.

(22)

By computation, (
10
) is equivalent to

2\nu^{2}(1-y^{2})^{2}w^{\prime\prime}(y)-(c_{1}(1-y)+c_{2}(1+y)+c_{3}(1-y^{2}))w(y)=0,

(23)

for
y_{0}<y<y_{1}
.
By standard ODE theory, (
23
) always have two linearly independent solution
w_{1},w_{2}\in C^{2}(-1,1)
, and its general solution is given by
w=C_{1}w_{1}+C_{2}w_{2}
for constants
C_{1},C_{2}
.

Let
\bar{y}\in(-1,1)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the initial value problem

\nu(1-y^{2})U_{\theta}^{\prime}+2\nu yU_{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}U_{\theta}^{2}=c_{1}(1-y)+c_{2}(1+y)+c_{3}(1-y^{2}),\quad U_{\theta}(\bar{y})=\gamma.

(24)

Let
w_{1},w_{2}
be a pair of fundamental solutions of (
23
). Denote

\mu:=\frac{\gamma}{2\nu(1-\bar{y}^{2})},\quad\textrm{ and }D:=-\frac{w_{2}^{\prime}(\bar{y})-\mu w_{2}(\bar{y})}{w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})-\mu w_{1}(\bar{y})}\textrm{ if }w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})\neq\mu w_{1}(\bar{y}).

(25)

Recall we have defined
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{1}^{\prime},\tau_{2}^{\prime}
in (
14
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\nu>0
,
c=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\bar{y}\in(-1,1)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exist some
y_{0},y_{1}
satisfying
-1\leq y_{0}<\bar{y}<y_{1}\leq 1
,
and a unique solution

of (
24

w(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Dw_{1}(y)+w_{2}(y),&\textrm{ if }w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})\neq\mu w_{1}(\bar{y}),\\
w_{1}(y),&\textrm{ if }w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})=\mu w_{1}(\bar{y}),\end{array}\right.

(26)

where
w\neq 0
on
(y_{0},y_{1})
,
w_{1},w_{2}
are two linearly independent fundamental
solutions of (
23
), and
\mu
and

are defined by (

10
) on some interval
(y_{0},y_{1})\subset(-1,1)

Proof.

Let
w
be defined by (
26
) and

be defined by (
22
). Note
w\in C^{\infty}(-1,1)
. We show that there exist some
\epsilon>0
, such that
U_{\theta}
is the unique solution of (

) in
(\bar{y}-\epsilon,\bar{y}+\epsilon)

We first show that there is some
\epsilon>0
such that
w\neq 0
in
(\bar{y}-\epsilon,\bar{y}+\epsilon)
, for which we only need to show

. Indeed, if
w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})\neq\mu w_{1}(\bar{y})

25

w(\bar{y})=Dw_{1}(\bar{y})+w_{2}(\bar{y})=\frac{w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})w_{2}(\bar{y})-w_{2}^{\prime}(\bar{y})w_{1}(\bar{y})}{w_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y})-\mu w_{1}(\bar{y})}.

As

are linearly independent solutions of (

When

If

is a fundamental solution of (

So

in both cases,
and there exists some
\epsilon>0
, such that
w\neq 0
and

given by (

satisfy (

satisfies the ODE in (

) in
(\bar{y}-\epsilon,\bar{y}+\epsilon)
. By the definition of
U_{\theta}
,

and

, we also have

by direct computation.
The uniqueness of the solution of (

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Proof.

We only prove (i). The proof of (ii) is similar. Let

be fixed and

be a constant.
By standard ODE theory, there exists a unique solution

of (

) on

on

defined by (

) is a solution of (

) on

, we have

Next, we prove the uniqueness of the solution of (

) in

such that

and Lemma

is given by (

) with some solution

depends only on

(27)

and

are linearly independent fundamental solutions of (

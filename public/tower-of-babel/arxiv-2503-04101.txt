Stabilizer Ranks, Barnes Wall Lattices and Magic Monotones
Amolak Ratan Kalra, Pulkit Sinha

Abstract
In 2024, Kliuchnikov and Schönnenbeck showed a connection between the Barnes Wall lattices, stabilizer states and Clifford operations. In this work, we study their results and relate them to the problem of lower bounding stabilizer ranks. We show the first quantitative lower bound on stabilizer fidelity as a function of stabilizer ranks, which reproduces the linear-by-log lower bound for χ δ ​ ( | H ⟩ ⊗ n ) \chi_{\delta}({\ket{H}^{\otimes n}}) , i.e., on the approximate stabilizer rank of | H ⟩ ⊗ n \ket{H}^{\otimes n} . In fact, we show that the lower bound holds even when the fidelity between the approximation and | H ⟩ ⊗ n \ket{H}^{\otimes n} is exponentially small, which is currently the best lower bound in this regime. This is shown via an intermediate result between stabilizer fidelity and stabilizer rank , which also gives us an Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ n / log ⁡ log ​ n ) \Omega(\log n/\log\log n) lower bound on the stabilizer rank of pseudorandom quantum states. Next, we define a new magic monotone for pure states, the Barnes Wall norm , and its corresponding approximate variant. We upper bound these monotones by the C ​ S CS -count of state preparation and also by the stabilizer ranks. In particular, the upper bound given by the C ​ S CS -count is tight, in the sense that we exhibit states that achieve the bound. Apart from these results, we give a Fidelity Amplification algorithm, which provides a trade-off between approximation error and the stabilizer rank. As a corollary, it gives us a way to compose approximate stabilizer decompositions into approximate decompositions of their tensor products. In particular this recovers the best known approximation for | H ⟩ ⊗ n {\ket{H}^{\otimes n}} . Furthermore we show that this best known approximation is effectively a Barnes Wall lattice approximation. It can also be shown that the Barnes wall norm of this approximation asymptotically matches the upper bound we present. Finally, we provide an alternative, elementary proof of the existence and density of product states with maximal stabilizer ranks, which was first proven by Lovitz and Steffan (2022), where they used results from algebraic geometry.

Introduction

Understanding the boundary between classical and quantum computation is a problem of vital importance in the field of quantum information. One way to approach this problem is via the lens of classical simulation of quantum circuits. In this arena, we attempt to quantify the cost of simulating different families of quantum circuits by giving explicit classical algorithms to simulate them and then study how the efficiency of these algorithms scales with the number of input qubits.
The first landmark result in this approach was given by Gottesman and Knill
[
1
]
, which showed that arbitrary quantum circuits using stabilizer states and measurements can be classically simulated efficiently (see also
[
2
]
). While these stabilizer circuits are not universal, they can be made to perform universal quantum computation by supplementing them with special non-stabilizer states called magic states.

In
[
3
]
, Bravyi, Smith and Smolin described a new algorithm to simulate these universal quantum circuits. They decomposed the magic states as linear combinations of a small number of stabilizer states, and then simulated the stabilizer circuits on this combination instead. The minimum number of such stabilizer states needed for a given magic state
\ket{\psi}
is referred to as the
stabilizer rank
of
\ket{\psi}
, denoted as
\chi(\ket{\psi})
. For such simulation algorithms,
\chi({\ket{\psi}})
is effectively the most important metric of efficiency or computational complexity. In particular, if we are able to efficiently find polynomially sized decompositions for magic states like
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
, then this has various complexity-theoretic implications such as
BQP=BPP
and even
P=NP

[
4
]
. Also, instead of finding an exact decomposition in terms of stabilizers, we can also hope to approximate the magic states with linear combination of stabilizer states instead. The corresponding rank in this case is called the
\delta
-approximate stabilizer rank, denoted as
\chi_{\delta}(\ket{\psi})
.

The problem of finding optimal bounds for
\chi(\ket{\psi})
and
\chi_{\delta}(\ket{\psi})
for tensor powers of magic states, termed the
stabilizer rank
problem, has received a lot of attention, with various works chipping away at both the upper and lower bounds, with most results focusing on the ranks of
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
or (equivalently)
\ket{T}^{\otimes n}
. Here,
\ket{H}=\cos\frac{\pi}{8}\ket{0}+\sin\frac{\pi}{8}\ket{1}
, and
\ket{T}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}+e^{i\pi/4}\ket{1})
.

For
\chi(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
, the first lower bound of
\Omega(\sqrt{n})
was given by Bravyi, Smith and Smolin in
[
3
]
, with a corresponding upper bound of
O(2^{n/2})
. In a series of works,
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, these lower and upper bounds were improved to
\Omega(\frac{n^{2}}{\mathrm{polylog}(n)})
and
O(2^{0.4n})
respectively.

For
\chi_{\delta}(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
, the best known lower and upper bounds are
\Omega(\frac{n^{2}}{\mathrm{polylog}(n)})

[
7
]
and
O(2^{0.23n})
[
5
]
for constant
\delta
.

In order to study the stabilizer rank, various other measures of stabilizer complexities have been introduced, including but not limited to: stabilizer fidelity, stabilizer extent and stabilizer alignment (see
[
5
]
), with emphasis on relating these to each other and as well as to stabilizer ranks
[
10

5
,
11
]
. In particular, we know that tensor products of magic states, such as
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
, have exponentially large stabilizer extent, and exponentially small stabilizer fidelity.

1.1
Overview of the Main Results

In a recent work, Kliuchnikov and Schönnenbeck
[
12
]
demonstrated that for
n
qubit Barnes Wall lattices, the automorphism group of the lattice, as well as the set of minimum length vectors, are upto phase exactly the Clifford unitaries and stabilizer states respectively. In our work, we study the problem of bounding stabilizer ranks and other notions of stabilizer complexities from the perspective of these recent results. Till now none of the techniques developed to study stabilizer ranks make use of this particular connection. Another reason to pursue this connections is that similar number theoretic techniques have been used to give synthesis algorithms for single qubits with close to optimal
T
-count
[
13
,
14
]
.

We first use such a lattice interpretation in Section
3
to prove the first quantitative lower bound on stabilizer fidelity based on the stabilizer rank. Such a bound was shown to exist by Mehraban and Tahmasbi
[
10
]
, but the result was only existential and did not give a concrete bound. We do this via an intermediate result between the stabilizer rank and stabilizer extent:

Theorem 1

(Upper bound on stabilizer extent)
.

Let
\ket{\phi}
be a pure state such that
\ket{\phi}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{i}\ket{s_{j}}
, where
\ket{s_{j}}
’s are all linearly independent stabilizer states,
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, we have that
\left\|\mathbf{c}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{e}\cdot(2k)^{\frac{2k+1}{2}}
.

This theorem is established using Minkowski’s theorem for lattices. At an intuitive level, it tells us that the determinant of the Gram matrix of lattice generators is large, if the minimal vector of the lattice is long. When we consider the lattice generated by the stabilizer states in the above theorem, since this determinant is large, we can show that the stabilizer extent is small.

Theorem 2

(Lower bound on stabilizer fidelity)
.

Let
\ket{\phi}
be a pure state with stabilizer fidelity
F_{S}
. For any state
\ket{\psi}
with
\chi(\psi)=k
, we have
\frac{|\braket{\phi|\psi}|}{\sqrt{F_{S}}}\leq\sqrt{e}(2k)^{\frac{2k+1}{2}}
. In particular, if
\frac{|\braket{\phi|\psi}|}{\sqrt{F_{S}}}=2^{\Omega(n)}
, then
k=\Omega\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)
.

This linear-by-log lower bound holds not only for the exact stabilizer rank for states with exponentially small stabilizer fidelity, such as
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
, but it holds even when the approximate stabilizer decomposition has only some exponentially small inner product with the said states.

Corollary 3

(Linear-over-log lower bound)
.

For any state
\ket{\psi}
with
F(\ket{\psi},\ket{H}^{\otimes n})\geq c^{n}
, for any constant
c>\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{8})
, we have that
\chi(\ket{\psi})=\Omega(\frac{n}{\log n})
.

Our lower bounds are the best in this regime, as none of the other results, including the quadratic lower bound in
[
7
]
, provide any non trivial lower bounds for this. Moreover, as observed in
[
10
]
, such a relation between stabilizer rank and fidelity helps imply a lower bound on stabilizer ranks for pseudorandom states using Theorem 1 from
[
15
]
.

Corollary 4
.

Given polynomially many copies of
\ket{\psi}
, we can distinguish, with high probability, the following two cases using a polynomial sized quantum circuit:

1.

\chi(\ket{\psi})=O\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)

2.

\ket{\psi}
is a Haar random unit vector.

This improves upon the
\omega(1)
lower bound provided in
[
10
]
. Note that the only observation that we used for this result was that the size of the minimal vector was a constant, i.e, we did not use the fact that the minimum is attained by the stabilizer states. To capture this notion of minimality of stabilizer states, in Section
4
, we defined a new measure on states in
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
, which we call the Barnes Wall norm.

Definition
.

The Barnes Wall norm of a state
\ket{\psi}
, denoted
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\psi})
, is the length of the smallest vector on the Barnes Wall lattice proportional to
\ket{\psi}
.

We also define the corresponding approximate variant of this measure, denoted as
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\ket{\psi})
. This norm enables us to argue about the stabilizer rank of magic states using properties exclusive to the Barnes Wall lattice in Theorem
7
. In particular, we show that if a state has high approximate Barnes Wall norm, then it also has high approximate stabilizer rank. It also turns out that these norms satisfy many of the properties that we expect from magic monotones:

Theorem 5

(Magic monotone properties)
.

The following properties hold for
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
:

(i)

\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
is invariant under the action of the Clifford Group.

(ii)

\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})\geq 1
, with equality only for stabilizer states.

(iii)

When
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi}),\penalty\ \mathcal{N}(\ket{\psi})<\infty
, we have
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi}\otimes{\ket{\psi}})=\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})\otimes\mathcal{N}(\ket{\psi})
. In particular,
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi}^{\otimes n})=\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})^{\otimes n}
.

(iv)

For all non-stabilizer
\ket{\phi}
,
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi}^{\otimes n})=2^{\Omega(n)}
.

(v)

\mathcal{N}
is non-increasing under uniform probability
1
-qubit Pauli measurements (post-selecting on any outcomes). If it indeed does decrease, it has to divide the original value.

(vi)

If the measurement does not have uniform probability, then we have the weaker constraint that
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})\geq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}(\ket{\psi})]
, where
\ket{\psi}
is the random post-measurement state.

(vii)

\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})
is either
\infty
or
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})
is of the form
\frac{c}{{(1+i)}^{n}}
where
c\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
.

The first two properties follow directly from the fact that stabilizer states are minimal vectors of the Barnes Wall lattice, and the Clifford group is the automorphism group of the lattice. The other properties of this norm can be shown using the dual of the Barnes Wall lattice. The dual is first used to give a characterization of finding the shortest vector on the lattice proportional to a given vector. This shortest vector for a given state is exactly the vector we use to measure the Barnes Wall norm
\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})
. Furthermore the divisibility properties of this norm can also be used to bound the
CS
count:

Theorem 6
.

Let
\ket{\phi}
be an
n
-qubit state which can be prepared exactly with
m

CS
gates and Clifford operations without any intermediate measurement then:

\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})\,\penalty\ |\penalty\ \,\mathcal{N}(\ket{CS})^{m}=2^{m}

If it does need intermediate measurements then we have the weaker result of

\mathcal{N}(\ket{\phi})\leq 2^{m}

Here
\ket{CS}
is the magic state
\ket{CS}=\frac{1}{2}(\ket{00}+\ket{01}+\ket{10}+i\ket{11})
.
We also relate
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\ket{\psi})
to the approximate stabilizer rank of
\ket{\psi}
, using a lattice approximation lemma:

Theorem 7
.

For any
\ket{\psi}\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
and
\delta,\delta_{0}>0
,

\mathcal{N}_{\delta+\delta_{0}}(\ket{\phi})\leq\frac{2\chi_{\delta}(\ket{\phi})}{\delta_{0}^{2}}.

It is immediately clear that approximating magic states with stabilizer decomposition is at most as hard as providing exact decompositions. However, it is not immediate that approximation doesn’t suddenly becomes hard after a certain threshold of error. That is, it is a possibility that there are some approximation constants
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
\chi_{\delta_{1}}(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
is polynomial in
n
, while
\chi_{\delta_{2}}(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
is exponential. In Section
5
, we show that such scenarios are not possible, via our Fidelity Amplification result. For this, define the
relative error
of pure state
\ket{\phi}
with respect to
\ket{\psi}
to be
\frac{1-F(\ket{\phi},\ket{\psi})}{F(\ket{\phi},\ket{\psi})}
, where
F(\ket{\phi},\ket{\psi})
is the fidelity of
\ket{\phi},\ket{\psi}
.

Theorem 8

(Fidelity amplification for
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
)
.

Given a stabilizer decomposition of
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
with relative error
\epsilon
and rank
k
, there exists another stabilizer decomposition with rank
O(\alpha k)
and relative error
\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}
.

In particular, for proving super-polynomial lower bounds for the approximate rank, this result shows that it suffices to show those for decompositions with polynomially small error. Further, this also says that any such lower bound will also apply to decompositions with polynomially high relative error, i.e, with polynomially small fidelity. We prove this result by starting with a stabilizer decomposition
\ket{\psi}
of
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
, and looking at its components along this direction and in the perpendicular direction. On applying an
H
gate with probability
\frac{1}{2}
independently to each qubit of
\ket{\psi}
to get a distribution over states
\ket{\phi}
, the expected relative error of
\frac{\ket{\psi}+\ket{\phi}}{2}
is half of its original value. As such, there is a stabilizer decomposition of rank
2k
with half the relative error. This result also lets us compose good approximate decompositions together to higher tensor powers:

Corollary 9
.

Given a
\delta
relative error rank
k
stabilizer decomposition for
\ket{H}^{\otimes m}
, and some
\alpha>1
, there is a rank
O((k(1+\delta))^{n})
constant relative error stabilizer decomposition for
\ket{H}^{\otimes mn}
.

Applying this result to
\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
retrieves the best known approximate stabilizer decomposition for
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
with rank
O(2^{0.23n})
first proved in
[
6
]
. In Section
5.1
we further show that the best known approximations for
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
are effectively lattice approximations which asymptotically match the bound in Theorem
7
.

Finally, in Section
6
we also provide an alternate proof for the density of product states with stabilizer rank exactly
2^{n}

Theorem 10
.

Product states with stabilizer rank exactly
2^{n}
form a dense and open subset of the set of product states.

This result had earlier been shown by Lovitz and Steffan
[
8
]
, where they had used techniques from algebraic geometry. In our work, we only use the vector space and metric space structure of subspaces spanned by stabilizer states to obtain the result, making these techniques more amenable to being extended to results about approximate stabilizer ranks. Note that any lower bound on the approximate stabilizer ranks of arbitrary product states implies the same lower bound (upto log factors) on
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
, as we can approximate any product state with
O(n\cdot\mathrm{polylog}(n))
many
T
-gates.

Recall that

Definition 44

Define relative error of

\frac{{\rm{Tr\penalty\ }}[(I-{{\ket{\phi}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{\phi}}})\,{{\ket{\phi^{\prime}}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{\phi^{\prime}}}}]}{{\rm{Tr\penalty\ }}[{{\ket{\phi}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{\phi}}}\,{{\ket{\phi^{\prime}}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{\phi^{\prime}}}}]}

Lemma 45
.

Given a rank
k

\frac{{\rm{Tr\penalty\ }}[(I-({{\ket{H}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{H}}})^{\otimes n})\,{{\ket{\psi}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{\psi}}}]}{{\rm{Tr\penalty\ }}[({{\ket{H}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{H}}})^{\otimes n}\,{{\ket{\psi}}\hskip-2.0pt{\bra{\psi}}}]}

In this section, we prove the existence of pure states with maximal ranks. The argument for openness and density of these states, i.e, the proof of Theorem

Consider the space

Observation 49

For any

B=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{V}_{k},B\subseteq A}A

Proof.

We know that

for some subcollection

(by definition of a

subspace). Now, clearly, for each such

. Now, for any
A\notin S

, we can add

A
on both sides.

∎

For any

\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{V}_{k},v\in A}A

Theorem 51
.

There is a product state for which the exact stabilizer rank is

6.1

In the proof Theorem
51

\ket{\phi}
to have maximal
\mathcal{W}_{k,\ket{\phi}}
among all product states. However, looking at the argument carefully, we can see that it only has to be maximal in a neighborhood. We use this observation to extend the proof to the following result:

Theorem 54
.

The set of product states with stabilizer rank
2^{n}
is open and dense in the set of product states.

Proof.

Fix
k=2^{n}-1
. For openness, we use a simple topological argument. Observe that each
V_{k}
space is closed in
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
, and thus so is their union.

Now, in
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
, the set of vectors which have stabilizer rank exactly
2^{n}
is
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}\setminus\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}A
, which is open in
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
. Since the topology on the product states is the subspace topology when considering the product states as a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
, the openness follows.

For density, fix any
\ket{\phi}
and
\epsilon
. Let the
\epsilon
ball around
\ket{\phi}
in the product states be
B_{i}
. If there any product state in
B_{i}
which has stabilizer rank
2^{n}
, then we are done.

Suppose this is not the case. Then, choose
\ket{\phi^{\prime}}
with the maximal
\mathcal{W}_{k,\ket{\phi^{\prime}}}
in
B_{1}
. Now, choose a ball
B_{2}
centered at
\ket{\phi^{\prime}}
such that
B_{2}\subseteq B_{1}
.

From this, we have that all states in
B_{2}
have stabilizer rank at most
k=2^{n}-1
, and that
\mathcal{W}_{k,\ket{\phi^{\prime}}}
is maximal in
B_{2}
. Then, by the argument in proof of Theorem
51
, we have that
k=2^{n}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Directions for Future work

Here, we outline some open problems that arise naturally due to our work, which we hope will help towards solving the stabilizer rank problem:

First, in the proof of Theorem
1
, we related the smallest eigenvalue of the Gram Matrix to the Stabilizer extent and the rank of the corresponding decomposition. Is it possible to use the structure of the lattice to bound more eigenvalues?

Problem 55
.

In proof of Theorem
1
, find more relations between eigenvalues and the extent.

If it were possible to do this, it would strengthen the results in Theorem
1
. Next, we believe trying to bound

is a viable strategy to understand
\chi_{\delta}(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
.

Problem 56
.

Find bounds on
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
.

In particular, lower bounds for this would imply corresponding lower bounds on
\chi_{\delta}(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})
.

Our Fidelity Amplification result, Theorem
8
, trades off stabilizer rank and relative error. Is it possible to create other such trade offs, such as between rank and number of qubits? For example, could we prove that existence of low stabilizer rank decompositions of
\ket{H}^{\otimes n}
imply even lower rank decompositions of
\ket{H}^{\otimes m}
for
m<n
?

Problem 57
.

Find analogues to Fidelity Amplification for other trade offs.

For the next problem, note that the existence of exact stabilizer rank
2^{n}
product states directly implies that there is some function
\delta:\mathbb{N}\to(0,1)
such that for each
n

\chi_{\delta(n)}(\ket{\psi})=2^{n}
for some product state
\ket{\psi}
. One can see this by a limiting argument. However, the limiting argument does not tell us what values of

work. So, a natural question would be to narrow this down.

Problem 58
.

Find examples of
\delta(n)
for which
\chi_{\delta(n)}(\ket{\psi})=2^{\Omega(n)}
for some product state
\ket{\psi}
.

Note that if the above holds for any

\chi(\ket{H}^{\otimes n})

T
gates.

Finally, it can be useful generalize the notions of the monotones
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
using the Barnes Wall lattices corresponding to
\mathbb{Z}[e^{i\pi/4}]
, as defined in
[
12
]
. This is because then there would be some vector proportional to

Note that simply generalizing the definition of

and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
to correspond to the usual euclidean lengths is not useful, as

To counteract this, instead of directly working with the
l_{2}
norm of the vectors, we can work with some norm function
F
applied to
\braket{\psi|\psi}
. The norm function defined in
[
12
]
is a candidate, but since it isn’t multiplicative, most of the arguments in Section
4
fail.

Next, we can consider the field norm for the extension

. Since it can be negative, we may further take the absolute value. Using this norm, almost all the arguments in Section

can be recreated, except, we have no knowledge about the minimal vectors with respect to this norm. It would still be the case that the stabilizer states have norm 1, but the arguments in
[
12
]
do not exclude the possibility of even smaller vectors with respect to this norm. In case there are indeed even smaller vectors, then this would lose any significance as a measure of magic.

.

Find a natural generalization of

and

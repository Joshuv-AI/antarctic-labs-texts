Yang Chen and Cheng Cheng

Abstract
. Conjugate phase retrieval considers the recovery of a function, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its phaseless measurements. In this paper, we explore the conjugate phase retrieval in a shift-invariant space generated by a Gaussian funciton. First, we show that the modulus function in the Gaussian shift-invariant space can be determined from the phaseless Hermite samples taken on a discrete sampling set. We then show that a function in the shift-invariant space generated by a Gaussian can be uniquely determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its phaseless Hermite samples on a discrete set. For the functions with finite coefficient sequences, we provide an explicit reconstruction procedure.



1.
Introduction

Shift-invariant spaces

(1.1)

V(\phi)=\{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}\phi(\cdot-k),c_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},

have been widely used in wavelet analysis and approximation theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Thakur proved that a real-valued bandlimited signal can be uniquely determined, up to a sign, from its magnitude samples with sampling rate more than twice Nyquist rate
[
33
]
. The bandlimited signals are living in the shift-invariant space
generated by a sinc function
\frac{\sin(\pi\cdot)}{\pi\cdot}
. As the sinc function has infinite support and slow decay, the shift-invariant space is more suitable for modeling signal with smoother spectrum, e.g. taking into account real acquisition and reconstruction devices, and it has been widely used in finite elements, approximation theory and wavelets
[
2
]
.
When the generator
\phi
is a compactly supported real-valued function, the authors in this paper and their collaborators used the non-separability to characterize the phase retrievability of the real-valued signals and showed that the nonseparable real signals in
V(\phi)
can be reconstructed, up to a sign, from their phaseless samples taken on a discrete set with finite sampling density
[
9
,
13
,
15
]
.
Recently, Gröchenig proved that all real-valued signals in a shift-invariant space generated by a Gaussian
\phi_{\lambda}:=e^{-\lambda\cdot^{2}}
can be determined, up to a sign, from their phaseless samples on a separated set with lower Beurling density greater than 2, and phase retrieval is impossible for a complex-valued signal in the such space
[
19
]
. Romero also considered the phaseless sampling and reconstruction of real-valued functions (also called sign retrieval) in a shift-invariant space generated by a totally positive function
[
32
]
.

Observe that in a complex shift-invariant space generated by a real-valued function
\phi
, a function
f\in V(\phi)
if and only if its conjugation
\bar{f}\in V(\phi)
, and they have the same magnitudes
|f(x)|,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Taking the trivial ambiguity caused by conjugation into account, conjugate phase retrieval considers the problem of determining a signal
f
in a conjugate invariant space
\mathcal{C}
, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its phaseless measurements
|\psi(f)|,\psi\in\Psi
, i.e.,

g\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f,\Psi}\ {\rm if\ and\ only\ if}\ g=zf\ {\rm or}\ g=z\bar{f}\ {\rm for\ some}\ z\in{\mathbb{T}},

where

\mathcal{M}_{f,\Psi}:=\{g\in\mathcal{C},|\psi(g)|=|\psi(f)|,\psi\in\Psi\},

\Psi
is a collection of linear functionals and
{\mathbb{T}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}},|z|=1\}
,

[
10
,
12
,
17
,
27
,
28
]
.
Here a linear space
\mathcal{C}
is said to be conjugate invariant if
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{C}
provided that
f\in\mathcal{C}
. Similar to the characterization of phase retrievability by non-separability in a real linear space
[
9
]
, a characterization of conjugate phase retrieval in a general conjugate invariant space has been given in
[
10
]
, also see
[
17
]
for a finite dimensional space from the real frame vectors and
[
12
]
for a complex shift-invariant space generated by a compactly supported real-valued function.

In infinite-dimensional spaces, such as the complex shift-invariant space generated by a gaussian, oversampling of the magnitudes alone may not be sufficient for the (conjugate) phaseless recovery of the signal
[
19
]
. The authors consider the (conjugate) phase retrieval of complex-valued signals from structured modulation
[
11
,
26
,
27
,
30
]
. In
[
26
]
, the authors consider the phase retrieval of complex signals from fractional Fourier transform, and in
[
28
]
, the authors show that any complex-valued bandlimited function
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the magnitudes measurements
|f(x)|,|f^{\prime}(x)|,x\in{\mathbb{R}},
on the whole real line.

Hermite sampling has been widely used in aircraft instrument communication and air traffic control simulation, it considers the determination of the signal from the samples and its derivatives samples on a discrete sampling set
[
11
,
12
,
21
,
27
,
28
]
. Inspired by these, we consider the conjugate phaseless sampling and reconstruction of functions in the shift-invariant space

(1.2)

V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}:=\{f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-\beta k)^{2}},\ \{c_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})\}

generated by a Gaussian
e^{-\lambda\cdot^{2}}
from its absolute values and the absolute values of its derivatives on a discrete set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, where the stepsize
\beta>0
and the parameter
\lambda>0
. That is the determination of a function
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the magnitude measurements

|f(\gamma)|,|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma,
taken on some discrete set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.

A set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is said to have the lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)
if

(1.3)

D^{-}(\Gamma):=\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\#\Gamma\cap[x-r,x+r]}{2r}.

In this paper, our main theorem stated in Theorem
3.1
shows that any function
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
with a mild assumption,
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the phaseless samples
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
, taken on a discrete set
\Gamma
with the lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)\geq 2\beta^{-1}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we show that two functions in the shift-invariant space
V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
have the same phaseless Hermite samples on some discrete set if and only if they have the same magnitudes on the whole line, see Theorem
2.1
. In Section
3
, we show that the functions in
V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
with certain decay can be conjugate phase retrieval from its phaseless Hermite samples on a sampling set with lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)\geq 2\beta^{-1}
, see Theorem
3.1
. At the end of this section, we also show that any function in the shift-invariant space generated by a Hermite function can be conjugate phase retrieval from its Hermite phaseless samples on a sampling set of positive measure when its coefficient sequence has certain polynomial decay.
Due to the exponential decay of the Gaussian function
\phi_{\lambda}
outside the interval
[-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}\lambda},\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}\lambda}]
, only finite terms contributes to the sum. In Section
4
, we consider the conjugate phase retrieval of the signals in
V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
with finite coefficient sequence, and give an algorithm to reconstruct the signals in
V^{\infty}_{1,1}
with finite duration from its Hermite phaseless samples taken on a discrete set of finite cardinality.
In Appendix
A
, we include the proof of some technique lemmas.

2.
Phaseless Hermite sampling in a shift-invariant space with Gaussian

Given a signal
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
in (
1.2
), let

(2.1)

{\mathcal{M}}_{f}:=\{g\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda},\ |g(x)|=|f(x)|{\rm\ and}\ |f^{\prime}(x)|=|g^{\prime}(x)|\ {\rm for\ all}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}

contain all functions
g\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
having the same Hermite magnitude measurements on the whole line
{\mathbb{R}}
as
f
has. The phaseless Hermite sampling and reconstruction problem considers whether there exists a discrete sampling set
\Gamma
such that

{\mathcal{M}}_{f,\Gamma}={\mathcal{M}}_{f},

where

(2.2)

{\mathcal{M}}_{f,\Gamma}=\{g\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda},\ |g(\gamma)|=|f(\gamma)|{\rm\ and}\ |f^{\prime}(\gamma)|=|g^{\prime}(\gamma)|\ {\rm for\ all}\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}.

In this section, we show that for any signal
f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-\beta k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
with the mild condition

(2.3)

\{kc_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}),

there exists the discrete sampling set
\Gamma
with the lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)>2\beta^{-1}
such that the equality

{\mathcal{M}}_{f}={\mathcal{M}}_{f,\Gamma}

holds, cf. Theorem
2.1
.

Without loss of generality, we set
\beta=1
as
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
if and only if
f\in V^{\infty}_{1,\beta^{2}\lambda}
and denote
V^{\infty}_{1,\lambda}:=V^{\infty}_{\beta=1,\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{1,\lambda}
and
\Gamma
be the sampling set with the lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)>2
in (
1.3
), and let
{\mathcal{M}}_{f,\Gamma}
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
be the sets defined as in (
2.2
) and (
2.1
) respectively. If (
2.3
) holds, then

M_{f,\Gamma}=M_{f}.

An equivalent statement to the above theorem is that the modulus function
|f(x)|,x\in{\mathbb{R}},
can be determined from the phaseless Hermite samples
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma,
taken on a discrete set
\Gamma
with finite density. To prove Theorem
2.1
, we recall some basic results in the shift-invariant space generated by a Gaussian.

Lemma 2.1
.

[Lemma 3,
[
19
]
]

If
f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-\beta k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
, then

(2.4)

|f|^{2}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}r_{m}e^{-2\lambda(\cdot-\frac{\beta m}{2})^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\frac{\beta}{2},2\lambda}

with the sequence
r\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
, where
r_{m}=\tilde{r}_{m}e^{\lambda m^{2}/2}
,
\tilde{r}_{m}=\tilde{c}\ast\bar{\tilde{c}}(m)
and
{\tilde{c}}_{m}=c_{m}e^{-\lambda m^{2}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The above theorem on the function
|f|^{2},f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
is crucial in the (conjugate) phase retrieval of a function in the complex shift-invariant space generated by a Gaussian.

Lemma 2.2
.

[Theorem 4.4,
[
20
]
]

Let
\lambda>0
and
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a separated set with lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)>\beta^{-1}
. Then for any function
f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(x-\beta k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
with
c\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
, there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

(2.5)

A\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)\leq\|c\|_{\infty}\leq B\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma).

From the above lemma, we know that for a sampling set, the coefficients
c
of the function
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
in (
1.2
) can be determined uniquely from the samples with lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)>\beta^{-1}
. The above sampling theorem for a shift-invariant space with a Gaussian is a main tool for our uniqueness.

Now we are ready for the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Denote
\tilde{c}_{k}=c_{k}e^{-\lambda k^{2}}
and
r_{k}=\tilde{c}\ast\bar{\tilde{c}}(k)e^{\lambda k^{2}/2}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, we know that the coefficients
r_{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, can be uniquely determined from
|f(\Gamma)|
as the modulus

(2.6)

|f(x)|^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}r_{k}e^{-2\lambda(x-k/2)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{2},2\lambda}.

Next we prove that
|f^{\prime}|
can be uniquely determined from
|f(\Gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\Gamma)|
.

Write
\omega(x):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}kc_{k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}
. By (
2.3
) and similar arguments for the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we have that

(2.7)

\omega\in V^{\infty}_{1,\lambda}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ |\omega|^{2}\in V^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{2},2\lambda}.

Observe that

f^{\prime}(x)=2\lambda\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(k-x)c_{k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}=2\lambda(\omega(x)-xf(x)).

By a direct calculation, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}}{4\lambda^{2}}\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743ptx^{2}|f(x)|^{2}+|\omega(x)|^{2}-2x\Re(\omega(x)\overline{f(x)})

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743ptx^{2}|f(x)|^{2}+|\omega(x)|^{2}-2x\Re(\sum_{k,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}kc_{k}\bar{c}_{j}e^{-\lambda((x-k)^{2}+(x-j)^{2})})

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743ptx^{2}|f(x)|^{2}+|\omega(x)|^{2}-x\sum_{k,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(k+j)c_{k}\bar{c}_{j}e^{-\lambda((x-k)^{2}+(x-j)^{2})}

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-7.22743ptx^{2}|f(x)|^{2}+|\omega(x)|^{2}-x\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}nr_{n}e^{-2\lambda(x-\frac{n}{2})^{2}}.

Then the evaluations of
|\omega|
on the discrete set
\Gamma

|\omega(\gamma)|^{2}=\frac{|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|^{2}}{4\lambda^{2}}+\gamma\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}nr_{n}e^{-2\lambda(\gamma-\frac{n}{2})^{2}}-\gamma^{2}|f(\gamma)|^{2},\gamma\in\Gamma,

can be obtained by the coefficients
r_{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and the phaseless Hermite samples
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Together with Lemma
2.2
and (
2.7
), we know that the function
|\omega|^{2}
can be uniquely determined. Hence
|f^{\prime}|
can be uniquely determined by (
2.8
).

Then for any signal
g\in M_{f,\Gamma}
, i.e.,
|g(\gamma)|=|f(\gamma)|
and
|g^{\prime}(\gamma)|=|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have
|g(x)|=|f(x)|
and
|g^{\prime}(x)|=|f^{\prime}(x)|
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and hence
g\in M_{f}
. This together with
{\mathcal{M}}_{f}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{f,\Gamma}
proves our conclusion.
∎

3.
Conjugate phaseless sampling and reconstruction

As
zf,z\overline{f}
have the same Hermite magnitude measurements on the whole line
{\mathbb{R}}
, we know that

\{zf,z\overline{f}\}\subset M_{f},

where
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
and
z,|z|=1
is some unimodular constant.
The conjugate phase retrieval considers whether the converse of the above inequality is true.
In this secton, we show that any function
f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(x-\beta k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
with
\{kc_{k}\}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
, can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the phaseless samples
\{|f(\gamma)|,|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma\}
, taken on a discrete set
\Gamma
with
D^{-}(\Gamma)\geq 2\beta^{-1}
, that is,

(3.1)

M_{f}=\{zf,z\overline{f}\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\lambda(x-k\beta)^{2}}
be a function in
V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}
satisfying (
2.3
), and
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a separated set with the lower Beurling density
D^{-}(\Gamma)>2\beta^{-1}
. Then
f

can be uniquely determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its phaseless Hermite samples
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Combined with Theorem
2.1
, the proof of Theorem
3.1
reduces to proving that the coefficient sequence
c
can be determined from
|f|^{2}
and
|f^{\prime}|^{2}
. In
[
19
]
, the authors show that the Fourier series of a sequence with Gaussian decay can be extended to an entire function of order
2
. For the convenience, we include the theorem in the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

[Lemma 2,
[
19
]
]

For a sequence
\{{\tilde{c}}_{k}=c_{k}e^{-\gamma k^{2}}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with some
c\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
\gamma>0
, the Fourier series
\hat{\tilde{c}}(\xi):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\tilde{c}}_{k}e^{2\pi ik\xi}
can be extended to an entire function
\tilde{C}(z)=\hat{\tilde{c}}(\xi+iy)
of order 2, that is,
|\tilde{C}(\xi+iy)|=O(e^{\pi^{2}y^{2}/\lambda})
.

Here an entire function
f
is said to be of order
\rho
if

\rho:=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\sup\frac{\log\log M_{f}(r)}{\log r}<\infty,

where
r>0
and
M_{f}(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|
.
Let
f
be an entire function of finite order and denote
Z_{f}
the multiset of its zeros, with
0
excluded and all zeros repeated according to their multiplicities.
Set the involution function of
f
by

f^{\natural}(z):=\overline{f(-\bar{z})}
and the involution multiset by
Z_{f}^{\natural}:=-\overline{Z_{f}}
which is a multiset formed by the reflection of the elements in
Z_{f}
about the imaginary axis. The involution function
f^{\natural}
of an entire function
f
is also an entire function of the same order as
f
has, and
Z_{f^{\natural}}=(Z_{f})^{\natural}
.
The following result on the conjugate phase retrieval of entire functions plays a crucial role in our proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f,g
be entire functions of finite order and satisfy

(3.2)

ff^{\natural}=gg^{\natural}{\rm\ and\ }f^{\prime}(f^{\prime})^{\natural}=g^{\prime}(g^{\prime})^{\natural}.

Then we have

f=\alpha g
or
f=\alpha g^{\natural}
, where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
is some unimodular constant.

The above result has been established in
[
28
, Theorem 1]
for entire functions where the involution function of the function
f
is defined by
f_{\ast}(z)=\overline{f(\bar{z})}
. Our proof is similar to the proof there. For the completeness of the paper, we provide a proof of Lemma
3.3
in Appendix
A
.

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem. To avoid the complicated calculation of parameters, we set
\beta=1
in the following proof.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
:

Let

f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{1,k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}

and

g(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{2,k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}\in V^{\infty}_{\beta,\lambda}

with coefficient sequences
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
satisfying (
2.3
) and
|f(\gamma)|=|g(\gamma)|,|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|=|g^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
on the sampling set
\Gamma
with
D^{-}(\Gamma)>2
. Set
\omega_{1}(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}d_{1,k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}
and
\omega_{2}(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}d_{2,k}e^{-\lambda(x-k)^{2}}
, where
d_{i,k}=kc_{i,k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}},i\in\{1,2\}
.

Denote
\tilde{c}_{i,k}=c_{i,k}e^{-\lambda k^{2}}
and
\tilde{d}_{i,k}=d_{i,k}e^{-\lambda k^{2}},i\in\{1,2\},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Set

r_{i,k}:=\tilde{c}_{i}\ast\bar{\tilde{c}}_{i}(k)e^{\lambda k^{2}/2}

and

s_{i,k}:=\tilde{d}_{i}\ast\bar{\tilde{d}}_{i}(k)e^{\lambda k^{2}/2}

for
i\in\{1,2\},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Following the similar arguments in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the coefficients
r_{1,k},s_{1,k}

can be uniquely determined by the phaseless samples
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
and
r_{2,k},s_{2,k}

can be uniquely determined by the phaseless samples
|g(\gamma)|
and
|g^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
. Moreover, we have

(3.3)

r_{1,k}=r_{2,k}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ s_{1,k}=s_{2,k}\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ \ k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

by Theorem
2.1
.

Next we show that the coefficient sequence
\{c_{i,k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}},i\in\{1,2\}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the sequences
\{r_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\{s_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Define

C_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\tilde{c}_{i,k}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz}\ {\rm\ and\ }\ D_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\tilde{d}_{i,k}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz},

and

R_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}r_{i,k}e^{-\lambda k^{2}/2}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz}\ {\rm\ and\ }\ S_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}s_{i,k}e^{-\lambda k^{2}/2}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz},i\in\{1,2\}.

From a direct calculation, we have

(3.4)

R_{i}(z)=C_{i}(z)C_{i}^{\natural}(z)\ {\rm\ and\ }\ S_{i}(z)=D_{i}(z)D_{i}^{\natural}(z)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}C_{i}^{\prime}(z)C_{i}^{\prime}(z)^{\natural},i\in\{1,2\}.

This together with (
3.3
), we have

(3.5)

C_{1}(z)C_{1}^{\natural}(z)=C_{2}(z)C_{2}^{\natural}(z)\ {\rm\ and\ }\ C_{1}^{\prime}(z)C_{1}^{\prime}(z)=C_{2}^{\prime}(z)C_{2}^{\prime}(z).

By (
2.3
) and Lemma
3.2
, the functions
C(z)
,
D(z)
and their involution functions

C^{\natural}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\overline{\tilde{c}_{k}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz}\ {\rm\ and\ }\ D^{\natural}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\overline{\tilde{d}_{k}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}C^{\prime}(z)^{\natural}

are entire functions of order 2.
Together with (
3.5
), Lemma
3.3
implies that
C_{1}=\alpha C_{2}
or
C_{1}^{\natural}=\alpha C_{2}^{\natural}
. By the linear independence of
\{e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}kz}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, the sequence
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
or
\{\bar{c}_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant, which completes our proof.
∎

3.1.
Shift-invariant spaces generated by a Hermite function

In this subsection, we study the conjugate phase retrieval in shift-invariant spaces generated by a Hermite function.
Let
h_{n},n\geq 0,
be Hermite functions defined by

(3.6)

h_{n}(x)=\zeta_{n}e^{\pi x^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}x^{n}}(e^{-2\pi x^{2}})=\zeta_{n}H_{n}(x)e^{-\pi x^{2}},

where
H_{n}(x)=e^{2\pi x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(e^{-2\pi x^{2}})
are the Hermite polynomials of order
n
and
\zeta_{n}
is the normalized constant.
For the case that
n=0

and

Proposition 3.4

Let

be the Hermite function (

be the shift-invariant space (

, it can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the magnitudes
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
, taken on a set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
of positive measure.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}h_{n}(x-k)
be a function in

with
\{c_{k}k^{n}\}\in\ell^{\infty}
. Denote

the space of polynomials with degrees no larger than
n
. For all
l\geq 0
, there exist polynomials
p_{l}
of degree
l
such that

\frac{d^{l}}{dx^{l}}e^{-\pi x^{2}}=p_{l}(x)e^{-\pi x^{2}}\ {\rm\ and}\ \ \overline{{\rm span}\{p_{l},0\leq l\leq n\}}={\mathcal{P}}_{n}.

Hence there exist real numbers
m_{l},0\leq l\leq n,
such that

\displaystyle f(x+iy)\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle=\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}H_{n}(x+yi-k)e^{-\pi(x-k+yi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\pi(x-k+yi)^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}m_{l}p_{l}(x+yi-k)

\displaystyle=\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{l=0}^{n}m_{l}(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}e^{-\pi(x-k+yi)^{2}})^{(l)}.

As the function

is analytic on the whole complex plane, its derivatives of order up to
n
are also entire. Thus the function

which is a finite combination of entire functions is also entire.

Observe that

\displaystyle f(x+iy)\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle=\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}H_{n}(x+yi-k)e^{-\pi(x-k+yi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}q_{l}(k)(x+yi)^{l}e^{-\pi(x-k+yi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{l=0}^{n}(x+yi)^{l}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}q_{l}(k)e^{-\pi(x-k+yi)^{2}},

where

are polynomials of degree

\displaystyle|f(x+iy)|\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\leq\hskip-3.61371pt

and hence

is an entire function of order 2.

As

\displaystyle|f(x)|^{2}=f(x)\overline{f(\bar{x})},x\in{\mathbb{R}},

the function

can be extended to an entire function of finite order on the complex plane. Similarly we have that

can be extended to entire function of finite order on the complex plane. Therefore the functions

and

can be uniquely determined from the samples taken on a set

can be conjugate phase retrieval from the phaseless samples
|f(\gamma)|
and
|f^{\prime}(\gamma)|,\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Proof of Lemma

To prove Lemma
3.3
, we recall the well-known Hadamard factorization theorem.

Lemma A.1
.

(
Hadamard Factorization Theorem
)

[
16
]
Let
f
be an entire function of finite order
\rho
. Then
f
has the unique factorization

f(z)=z^{m}e^{P(z)}\prod_{a\in Z_{f}}E_{[\rho]}(a,z),

where
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
P(z)
is a polynomial of degree at most
\rho
,
[\rho]
is the largest integer number not greater than
\rho
and

(A.1)

E_{[\rho]}(a,z)=\begin{cases}(1-\frac{z}{a})&{\rm\ if}\quad[\rho]=0,\\
(1-\frac{z}{a})e^{(\frac{z}{a}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{z^{2}}{a^{2}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{1}{[\rho]}(\frac{z}{a})^{[\rho]})}&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
f,g
be the entire functions of finite order satisfying (
3.2
) and denote
Z_{f}
and
Z_{g}
their zero multisets with
0
excluded respectively. We first prove that there exist entire functions
u,v
of finite order such that
f=uv
and
g=uv^{\natural}
. As
ff^{\natural}=gg^{\natural}
, the entire functions
f
and
g
are of the same order
\rho
.
By Lemma
A.1
, the functions
f
and
g
can be decomposed as

f(z)=z^{m}e^{P_{f}(z)}\prod_{a\in Z_{f}}E_{[\rho]}(a,z),

and

g(z)=z^{n}e^{P_{g}(z)}\prod_{a\in Z_{g}}E_{[\rho]}(a,z).

Then their involution functions

f^{\natural}(z)=(-1)^{m}z^{m}e^{P_{f}^{\natural}(z)}\prod_{a\in Z_{f}}E_{[\rho]}(-\bar{a},z),

and

g^{\natural}(z)=(-1)^{n}z^{n}e^{P_{g}^{\natural}(z)}\prod_{a\in Z_{g}}E_{[\rho]}(-\bar{a},z).

By
ff^{\natural}=gg^{\natural}
, we have

(A.2)

m=n,P_{f}+P_{f}^{\natural}=P_{g}+P_{g}^{\natural}{\rm\ and\ }Z_{f}\dot{\cup}Z_{f}^{\natural}=Z_{g}\dot{\cup}Z_{g}^{\natural}.

Let
Z_{0}=Z_{f}\dot{\cap}Z_{g}
be the multiset of the common elements in
Z_{f}
and
Z_{g}
with the multiplicities counted and
Z_{1}=Z_{f}\dot{\setminus}Z_{0}
and
Z_{2}=Z_{g}\dot{\setminus}Z_{0}
. By (
A.2
), we have
Z_{2}=Z_{1}^{\natural}
. Define

u(z)=z^{m}e^{\frac{P_{f}(z)+P_{g}(z)}{2}}\prod_{a\in Z_{0}}E_{[\rho]}(a,z){\rm\ and\ }v(z)=e^{\frac{P_{f}(z)-P_{g}(z)}{2}}\prod_{a\in Z_{1}}E_{[\rho]}(a,z).

Then we have

(A.3)

f=uv{\rm\ and\ }g=uv^{\natural}.

By
f^{\prime}(f^{\prime})^{\natural}=g^{\prime}(g^{\prime})^{\natural}

) shows that

(u^{\prime}u^{\natural}-u(u^{\prime})^{\natural})(v^{\prime}v^{\natural}-v(v^{\prime})^{\natural})=0.

It implies that

or

Quantum trajectories and Page-curve entanglement dynamics
Katha Ganguly, Preethi Gopalakrishnan, Atharva Naik, Bijay Kumar Agarwalla, Manas Kulkarni

Abstract
We consider time dynamics of entanglement entropy between a filled fermionic system and an empty reservoir. We consider scenarios (i) where the system is subjected to a dephasing mechanism and the reservoir is clean, thereby emulating expansion of effectively interacting fermions in vacuum, and (ii) where both the system and the reservoir are subjected to dephasing and thereby enabling us to address how the entanglement between the part of the effectively interacting system and its complement evolves in time. We consider two different kinds of quantum trajectory approaches, namely stochastic unitary unraveling and quantum state diffusion. For both protocols, we observe and characterize the full Page curve-like dynamics for the entanglement entropy. Depending on the protocol and the setup, we observe very distinct characteristics of the Page curve and the associated Page time and Page value. We also compute the number of fermions leaking to the reservoir and the associated current and shed light on their plausible connections with entanglement entropy. Our findings are expected to hold for a wide variety of generic interacting quantum systems.

\displaystyle C^{\xi}_{ij}(t)=\langle\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{c}_{j}(t)\rangle=[U(t)\,U^{\dagger}(t)]_{ji},

\displaystyle S_{\xi}(t)=-\sum_{k=1}^{L_{S}}\Big[\lambda_{k}\log_{2}\lambda_{k}+(1-\lambda_{k})\log_{2}(1-\lambda_{k})\Big],

Summary and Outlook.
– In this work, we thoroughly examine the entanglement entropy Page curve in an effectively interacting system following the quantum trajectory approach [recall Table.
1
]. Depending on the unraveling protocol and the setup, we observe distinct time dynamics for the growth and decay of EE. The SUU protocol always leads to diffusive growth (
\sqrt{t}
) for EE with a growth coefficient independent of system size. The QSD protocol due to local monitoring always leads to much slower growth of EE. The maximum attainable value of EE, i.e., the Page value, shows interesting system-size scaling (volume law, sub-volume law, area law) depending on the unraveling protocol. The subsequent temporal decay of the Page curve shows distinct behaviour between the two geometrical arrangements of probes. Remarkably, we find strong numerical evidence supporting the relation between the entropy production rate and particle current up to Page time.

It would be interesting to understand the
effect of disorder on the entanglement growth and combined effect of disorder and probes in various geometrical arrangements. Our setup is non-markovian as far as system-reservoir interaction is concerned. However, the dephasing mechanism was assumed to be Markovian. It will be interesting to investigate the effect of non-markovian dephasing mechanisms on the temporal behaviour of entanglement entropy.

\hat{H}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{L-1}h_{ij}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}_{j}.

Here,
g

we assign a different hopping strength
g_{c}
.
L_{P}
is the total number of probes i.e.,

\displaystyle L_{P}=\begin{cases}L_{S}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{Probe-Clean (PC)}\,\,{\rm case}\\
L\,\,\,\,\,\quad\text{Probe-Probe (PP)}\,\,{\rm case,}\end{cases}

(S3)

and
\gamma

is the full density matrix of the system and the reservoir.
The dephasing Lindblad equation with the jump operators being the local particle number
\hat{n}_{i}
given in Eq. (
S1

\rho(t)
, which is an ensemble average of pure states that evolves stochastically in time, as will be elaborated later.
Although this noise averaged description is well suited for calculating the expectation values of observables that depend linearly on
\rho(t)

S1
)

Different unraveling protocols may result in different values for the same nonlinear functional of
\rho(t)
. Thus, to study such nonlinear functional like entanglement entropy, which is the main focus of this work, it is paramount to discuss the details of the underlying protocol. Given that entanglement entropy is a valid and reliable measure of entanglement only for pure states, it is important to have a description of the mixed-state evolution in terms of pure states.
Theoretical descriptions of such pure state dynamics usually appear in terms of stochastic differential equations

S1
b for PC and PP setup, respectively]. The stochastic Hamiltonian is therefore given as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{T}(t)=\hat{H}+\sum_{i=1}^{L_{P}}\xi_{i}(t)\,\hat{n}_{i},

(S4)

where
\hat{H}
is the Hamiltonian of the full system of size
L
,
\hat{n}_{i}
is the local number operator. Recall that
L_{P}
is the total number of probes which is
L_{S}
in the PC case and
L
in the PP case.
\xi_{i}(t)
in Eq. (
S4
) is a Gaussian white noise with noise-noise correlation given by
\overline{\xi_{i}(t)\,\xi_{j}(t^{\prime})}=\gamma\,\delta_{ij}\,\delta(t-t^{\prime})
with
\gamma
being the dephasing noise strength. Starting with an arbitrary initial state, the time evolution of the system under the stochastic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{T}(t)
in Eq. (
S4
) can be written as,

\displaystyle|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\rangle\approx e^{-i\hat{H}dt-i\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi^{t}_{j}\,\hat{n}_{j}}|\psi_{\xi}(t)\rangle,

(S5)

where
d\xi^{t}_{j}
is the infinitesimal noise increment from time
t
to
t+dt
and it follows the Itó rule i.e.,
d\xi^{t}_{i}\,d\xi_{j}^{t}=\gamma\,dt\,\delta_{ij}
and
\overline{d\xi_{i}^{t}}=0
. The state
|\psi_{\xi}(t)\rangle
for a particular noise realization is called a quantum trajectory. Expanding the exponential in Eq. (
S5
) and keeping terms up to
O(dt)
, we obtain,

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\big\rangle=\big[\,\hat{\mathbb{I}}-i\hat{H}dt-i\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi_{j}^{t}\,\hat{n}_{j}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,\hat{n}_{j}\,dt\,\big]\,\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle,

(S6)

which can be recast as,

\displaystyle d\,\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\big[-i\hat{H}dt-i\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi_{j}^{t}\,\hat{n}_{j}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,\hat{n}_{j}\,dt\,\big]\,\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle.

(S7)

Eq. (
S7
) is the Stochastic Schr
\ddot{{\rm o}}
dinger Equation (SSE) under SUU protocol. For non-interacting systems, where the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
is quadratic, starting with a Gaussian initial state, the Gaussian nature of the state will be preserved under the evolution in Eq. (
S6
) for each quantum trajectory. The noise averaged dynamics
\overline{\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle\big\langle\psi_{\xi}(t)\big|}=\rho(t)
represents the Lindblad equation in Eq. (
S1
) which is however non-quadratic in the fundamental fermionic operators.

We will now discuss how to simulate quantum trajectories from Eq. (
S6
). It can be observed that any Gaussian state with a fixed number of fermions can be written as

\displaystyle\big|\psi\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{L}U_{jl}\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\,\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle.

(S8)

Here,
\big|0\big\rangle
corresponds to the vacuum state of the entire lattice, and
U
is a
L\times N
matrix with
N
being the total number of fermions in the system. It satisfies the isometry
U^{\dagger}\,U=\mathbb{I}_{N}
, which is required for the norm preservation of the state. Each column of the
U
matrix represents a single-particle state. For example, the
U
matrix for the domain-wall state, which is the initial state that we consider in our work, is given by

\displaystyle U(0)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&1&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0\end{bmatrix},

(S9)

Now, the time evolution by the SSE in Eq. (
S6
) can be written as,

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\big\rangle\approx e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle,

(S10)

where
\hat{K}_{t}
up to
O(dt)
is the following,

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{t}=-i\hat{H}dt-i\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi_{j}^{t}\,\hat{n}_{j}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,\hat{n}_{j}\,dt.

(S11)

Note that the state
\big|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\big\rangle
is the state of the full system, and its dimension is
{}^{L}C_{N}
, thereby making it computationally challenging. Note that
\hat{K}_{t}
in Eq. (
S11
) is quadratic in fermionic creation and annihilation operator, and hence, starting with a Gaussian state, the state at time
dt
follows from Eq. (
S8
) and (
S10
),

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(dt)\big\rangle=e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{L}U_{jl}\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\,\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle.

(S12)

Taking the term
e^{\hat{K}_{t}}
inside the product and the summation, we get

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(dt)\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{L}U_{jl}\,e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\,e^{-\hat{K}_{t}}\,e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle

(S13)

Using the fact that
\hat{K}_{t}|0\rangle=|0\rangle
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(dt)\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{L}U_{jl}\,e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\,e^{-\hat{K}_{t}}\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle.

(S14)

Using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, we get

\displaystyle e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\,e^{-\hat{K}_{t}}=\sum_{k,j}[Me^{-ihdt}]_{kj}\,\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger},

(S15)

where
M
is a
L\times L
matrix that takes the form

\displaystyle M={\rm diag}(e^{-id\xi^{0}_{1}+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,dt},\,\,e^{-id\xi^{0}_{2}+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,dt},\,\,\dots\,e^{-id\xi^{0}_{L_{P}}+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,dt},\,\,1,\dots 1,\,\,1,\dots\,1),

(S16)

where we recall that
L_{P}
is the number of probes.
d\xi_{i}^{0}
s are independent random Gaussian noise with mean zero and variance
\gamma\,dt
and
h
is the single particle
L\times L
matrix for the Hamiltonian of the system.
From Eq. (
S15
) and Eq. (
S14
), we obtain,

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(dt)\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{k,j=1}^{L}U_{jl}\,[Me^{-ihdt}]_{kj}\,\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger}\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle.

(S17)

Defining
U(dt)=Me^{-ihdt}\,U(0)
, Eq. (
S17
) can be finally written as

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(dt)\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{L}U_{kl}(dt)\,\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle.

(S18)

However, as the
M
matrix given in Eq. (
S16
) contain the factor
e^{\gamma dt}
in few entries, the
U(dt)
does not satisfy the isometry condition
U^{\dagger}(dt)U(dt)=\mathbb{I}_{N}
. Hence we perform a QR decomposition of the matrix
U(dt)
and redefine
U(dt)=Q
as obtained from QR decomposition.
Thus the evolution of the state
|\psi_{\xi}(dt)\rangle
can be obtained by the matrix
U(dt)
which is of smaller dimension (
L\times N
). These steps can be followed to generate a quantum trajectory under SUU protocol.
The evolution of
U
in a particular trajectory can be summarized as,

\displaystyle U(t+dt)=\begin{cases}{\rm diag}(e^{-id\xi^{t}_{1}}\,\,e^{-id\xi^{t}_{2}\,\,}\,\,e^{-id\xi^{t}_{3}\,\,}\dots e^{-id\xi^{t}_{L_{S}}\,\,}\,\,1\dots\,1\,\,1)\,e^{-ihdt}\,U(t),\,\,\,\,\quad{\rm(PC}\,\,{\rm case)}\\
{\rm diag}(e^{-id\xi^{t}_{1}}\,\,e^{-id\xi^{t}_{2}\,\,}\,\,e^{-id\xi^{t}_{3}\,\,}\dots\,\dots\,e^{-id\xi^{t}_{L}\,\,}\!\!)\,e^{-ihdt}\,U(t).\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad{\rm(PP}\,\,{\rm case)}\end{cases}

(S19)

We now derive an important connection between the
U(t)
matrix and the correlation matrix defined as
C_{ij}=\langle c^{\dagger}_{i}\,c_{j}\rangle_{t}
. As the number of fermions
N
is conserved throughout the dynamics, we can write the state in a trajectory at each instant of time as [obtained from Eq. (
S18
)]

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{L}U_{kl}(t)\,\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle,

(S20)

where
U^{\dagger}(t)U(t)=\mathbb{I}_{N}
for any time
t
. Each of the
N
columns of
U(t)
represents a single particle state which are orthogonal to each other, and they form an
incomplete
basis in the single particle Hilbert space since
N<L
. We can now elevate the dimension of
U(t)
from
L\times N
to
L\times L
and form a unitary matrix by adding the rest
L-N
number of single particle basis states. We denote the full matrix as
\mathbb{U}(t)
. Now, in terms of
\mathbb{U}(t)
, the state
\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle
is given as

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{L}\mathbb{U}_{kl}(t)\,\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle.

(S21)

Now we define another complete set of fermionic creation and annihilation operators as,

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathbb{U}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger},\,\,\,\,\,\quad\hat{b}_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathbb{U}^{*}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{c}_{j}.

(S22)

The operators
\hat{c}_{j}
and
\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}
are also related to these new operators
b_{k}
and
{b_{k}^{\dagger}}
respectively as,

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\mathbb{U}^{*}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger},\,\,\,\,\,\quad\hat{c}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\mathbb{U}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{b}_{k}.

(S23)

The state
\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle
in Eq. (
S21
) can also be written in terms of the operator
\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}
as,

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}\,\hat{b}_{2}^{\dagger}\,\hat{b}_{3}^{\dagger}\,\dots\,\hat{b}_{N}^{\dagger}\big|0\big\rangle

(S24)

Next we obtain the Correlation matrix
C^{\xi}_{ij}(t)=\big\langle\psi_{\xi}(t)\big|\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}_{j}\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle
as,

\displaystyle\big\langle\psi_{\xi}(t)\big|\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}_{j}\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\sum_{l,k=1}^{L}\mathbb{U}^{*}_{il}(t)\,\mathbb{U}_{jk}(t)\,\big\langle 0\big|\hat{b}_{N}\hat{b}_{N-1}\dots\,\hat{b}_{1}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{b}_{k})\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{2}\dots\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{N}\big|0\big\rangle.

(S25)

We observe that none of the terms corresponding to
l,k>N
will contribute to the summation. The terms with
l\neq k
will also not contribute to the summation. Hence only contribution comes from the terms
l=k<N
, thereby resulting in the following form of the correlation matrix
C^{\xi}_{ij}(t)
,

\displaystyle C_{ij}^{\xi}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mathbb{U}^{*}_{il}(t)\,\mathbb{U}_{jl}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}U^{*}_{il}(t)\,U_{jl}(t)=[U(t)U^{\dagger}(t)]_{ji}.

(S26)

Thus the correlation matrix has an elegant expression in terms of the
U
matrices defined in Eq. (
S19
).

\hat{n}_{i}

\gamma
and (b) as the Probe-Probe (PP) setup, where both the system and the reservoir sites are monitored. The hopping strength for both the system and the reservoir is

I.2
Quantum State Diffusion (QSD)

Another widely employed stochastic protocol that also mimics the same dephasing Lindblad dynamics, given in Eq. (
S1
), upon averaging, is the Quantum State Diffusion (QSD) protocol
[
95
,
96
,
97
,
98
,
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
]
. Practical implementation of this QSD protocol is possible in ultra-cold atom platforms
[
78
,
103
]
. The stochastic dynamics under this protocol describes the evolution of a quantum system subjected to continuous weak measurements
[
104
,
105
]
. Such measurements fall into the class of Positive Operator Valued Measurements (POVM) which are the generalization of projective measurements and it is represented as a set of positive operators
\{\hat{M}_{\mu}\}
satisfying
\sum_{\mu}\hat{M}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{M}_{\mu}=\mathbb{\hat{I}}
. Generally, such POVM can be performed by coupling the system with a detector and making projective measurement of the detector. In this subsection, we first derive the
\hat{M}_{\mu}
for QSD protocol associated with the Lindblad dynamics in Eq. (
S1
). We next obtain the SSE and further discuss the numerical simulation.

For the specific setup given in Fig.
S2
, the system is subjected to continuous weak monitoring of the local particle number
\hat{n}_{i}
at
L_{P}
number of lattice sites. To derive the SSE under the continuous monitoring,
we first discretize the time
t
into
n
number of intervals of size
dt
, assuming
n
is very large and
dt
is very small. Before every measurement, each of the detectors (indexed by
i
), which are used for the purpose of monitoring, is always prepared in a state

|\phi_{i}\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{i}\,\phi(x_{i})|x_{i}\rangle,

(S27)

where
x_{i}
corresponds to the position of the
i
-th detector. Here
\phi(x_{i})
is the wavefunction of the
i
-th detector which for the case of QSD type monitoring takes the form,

\displaystyle\phi(x_{i})=\Big(\frac{2\gamma\,dt}{\pi}\Big)^{1/4}e^{-\gamma\,dt\,x_{i}^{2}},

(S28)

where
\gamma
is the monitoring rate which is the same as the dephasing strength in the Lindblad equation in Eq. (
S1
).

The initial state of the full system is expressed in the local number operator basis and it is given as,

\displaystyle|\psi(0)\rangle=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots n_{L}}F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(0)\,|n_{1}\rangle|n_{2}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle,

(S29)

where
|n_{i}\rangle
corresponds to the eigenvector of local
\hat{n}_{i}
operator and
F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}
is,

\displaystyle F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}=\langle n_{1}\dots n_{L}|\psi(0)\rangle.

(S30)

We now write the joint state of the detectors and the system which belongs to the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\rm SD}=\mathcal{H}_{D_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D_{2}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D_{L_{P}}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S_{L_{P}}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S_{L_{P}+1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S_{L}}
where the subscript ‘
{\rm SD}
’ corresponds to the full system and all the detectors and
S_{i}
and
D_{i}
corresponds to
i
-th lattice site and
i
-th detector, respectively. Therefore the joint state takes the form,

\displaystyle|\Psi(0)\rangle=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots n_{L}}F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(0)\Big[(|\phi_{1}\rangle\otimes|n_{1}\rangle)\otimes(|\phi_{2}\rangle\otimes|n_{2}\rangle)\dots\otimes(|\phi_{L_{P}}\rangle\otimes|n_{L_{P}}\rangle)\Big]\otimes|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle.

(S31)

The ket
|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle
outside square bracket indicate that there are no detectors on those sites. Note that we have used
|\psi(0)\rangle
to represent the state in the system Hilbert space and
|\Psi(0)\rangle
to represent the state in the complete system-detector Hilbert space. To perform the continuous monitoring, we couple the system with the detector by an interaction Hamiltonian
H_{\rm int}
which is given as,

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}f(t)\,\hat{P}_{j}\otimes\hat{n}_{j},

(S32)

where
\hat{P}_{j}
is the momentum operator of the
j
-th detector and
\hat{n}_{j}
is the number operator of the
j
-th site.
f(t)
is the coupling impulse function which satisfy
\int_{0}^{dt}f(\tau)\,d\tau=1
. One such example of this coupling impulse function can be
f(t)=\sum_{n}\delta(t-n\,\tau_{c})

[
106
]
where the coupling time
\tau_{c}
lies within
dt/2<\tau_{c}<dt
.
Then, the total Hamiltonian of the system and the detector is given as

\displaystyle H_{T}(t)=\mathbb{\hat{H}}_{S}+f(t)\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,\hat{P}_{j}\otimes\hat{n}_{j},

(S33)

where
\mathbb{\hat{H}}_{S}
corresponds to the system Hamiltonian
H
given in Eq. (
S2
) and it is expressed in the joint Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SD}}
of the detectors and local sites by suitable use of identity operators. The corresponding unitary operator for evolving the setup from
t=0
to
t=dt
is then given by,

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(dt,0)=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{dt}d\tau\,H_{T}(\tau)}\approx\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{dt}f(\tau)\,d\tau\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\hat{P}_{j}\otimes\,\hat{n}_{j}}\,\,e^{-i\,\mathbb{\hat{H}_{S}}dt}=\Big(e^{-i\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\hat{n}_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e^{-i\hat{P}_{L_{P}}\otimes\,\hat{n}_{L_{P}}}\Big)e^{-i\,\mathbb{\hat{H}_{S}}dt}

(S34)

Here we have used the fact that
\int_{0}^{dt}f(\tau)d\tau=1
and Eq. (
S34
) is correct upto
O(dt)
. We first operate the unitary operator
e^{-i\mathbb{H}_{S}dt}
on the state
|\Psi(0)\rangle
given in Eq. (
S31
) and as it only has the system Hamiltonian part it will only change the
F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}
to
\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}
. Now operating the unitary operator in Eq. (
S34
) on the joint state of the detector and the system in Eq. (
S31
), we obtain,

\displaystyle|\Psi(dt)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\!\!\!\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)\Big(e^{-i\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\hat{n}_{1}}|\phi_{1}\rangle\otimes|n_{1}\rangle\Big)\otimes\dots\otimes\Big(e^{-i\hat{P}_{L_{P}}\otimes\hat{n}_{L_{P}}}|\phi_{L_{P}}\rangle\otimes|n_{L_{P}}\rangle\Big)\otimes|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle

(S35)

Using the fact that
e^{-i\hat{P}_{j}\otimes\hat{n}_{j}}|\phi_{j}\rangle\otimes|n_{j}\rangle=e^{-i\hat{P}_{1}n_{1}}|\phi_{j}\rangle\otimes|n_{j}\rangle
, we write,

\displaystyle|\Psi(dt)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\!\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)\Big(e^{-i\hat{P}_{1}n_{1}}|\phi_{1}\rangle\otimes|n_{1}\rangle\Big)\otimes\dots\otimes\Big(e^{-i\hat{P}_{L_{P}}n_{L_{P}}}|\phi_{L_{P}}\rangle\otimes|n_{L_{P}}\rangle\Big)\otimes|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle.

(S36)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)=\langle n_{1}\dots\ n_{L}|e^{-iHdt}|\psi(0)\rangle.

(S37)

Inserting the identity operator as a completeness relation for each of the detectors, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Psi(dt)\rangle=\!\!\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{1}|x_{1}\rangle\langle x_{1}|e^{-i\hat{P}_{1}n_{1}}|\phi_{1}\rangle\otimes|n_{1}\rangle\Big)\otimes\dots

\displaystyle\dots\otimes\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{L_{P}}|x_{L_{P}}\rangle\langle x_{L_{P}}|e^{-i\hat{P}_{L_{P}}n_{L_{P}}}|\phi_{L_{P}}\rangle\otimes|n_{L_{P}}\rangle\Big)\otimes|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle

(S38)

The monentum operator
\hat{P}_{j}
effectively boosts the wave-function of the
j
-th detector to yield,

\displaystyle|\Psi(dt)\rangle=\!\!\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{1}\phi(x_{1}-n_{1})|x_{1}\rangle\otimes|n_{1}\rangle\Big)\otimes\dots

\displaystyle\dots\otimes\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{L_{P}}\phi(x_{L_{P}}-n_{L_{P}})|x_{L_{P}}\rangle\otimes|n_{L_{P}}\rangle\Big)\otimes|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle

(S39)

Note that the combined system-detector state
|\Psi(dt)\rangle
in Eq. (
S39
) is completely entangled because of the presence of integration over the position variable.
Finally, we perform the projective measurement of the position operator on every detector. Mathematically it is performed by operating the projection operator

\displaystyle\mathbb{\hat{P}}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{L_{P}}}=\big(|\mu_{1}\rangle\langle\mu_{1}|\otimes\mathbb{\hat{I}}_{2}\big)\otimes\dots\otimes\big(|\mu_{L_{P}}\rangle\langle\mu_{L_{P}}|\otimes\mathbb{\hat{I}}_{2}\big)

(S40)

on the state
|\Psi(dt)\rangle
where
\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{L_{P}}\}
are the readings from the detectors. Thus the joint state of the system and the detector after every detector is measured is given by,

\displaystyle|\Psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\,\,\mathbb{\hat{P}}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{L_{P}}}\big|\Psi(dt)\big\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\!\!\!\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)

\displaystyle\big(\phi(\mu_{1}-n_{1})\dots\phi(\mu_{L_{P}}-n_{L_{P}})\big)\Big[|\mu_{1}\rangle\otimes|n_{1}\rangle\otimes\dots

\displaystyle\dots\otimes|\mu_{L_{P}}\rangle\otimes|n_{L_{P}}\rangle\Big]\otimes|n_{L_{P}+1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle.

(S41)

Here
\mathcal{N}
corresponds to the normalization factor of the state
|\Psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
which is given as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2}=\langle\Psi(dt)|\mathbb{\hat{P}}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{L_{P}}}|\Psi(dt)\rangle=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\!\!\big|\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)\big|^{2}\big|\phi(\mu_{1}-n_{1})\big|^{2}\dots\big|\phi(\mu_{L_{P}}-n_{L_{P}})\big|^{2}

(S42)

From Eq. (
S41
), we see that the system and the detector are not entangled. Therefore if we now take the inner product over the detector subspace
\langle\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{L_{P}}|\Psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
, we can obtain the state of the system i.e.,

\displaystyle|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\tilde{F}_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(dt)\big(\phi(\mu_{1}-n_{1})\dots\phi(\mu_{L_{P}}-n_{L_{P}})\big)|n_{1}\rangle\dots|n_{L}\rangle.

(S43)

Eq. (
S43
) is the state at
dt
after one measurement and it is conditioned with the outcome
\{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{L_{P}}\}
.

As mentioned before, such weak measurements on the system which are done by coupling the system with the detector and making projective measurements on the detector refer to a more general class of measurements called Positive Operator Valued Measurement (POVM). The POVM elements are
\hat{E}_{\mu}=\hat{M}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{M}_{\mu}
with
\mu=\{\mu_{i}\}
and these measurement operators
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
operates on the initial state of the system
|\psi(0)\rangle
and produces the state at time
dt
i.e.,
|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\}\rangle
. The form of the measurement operator
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
is,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{L}}\phi(\mu_{1}-n_{1})\dots\phi(\mu_{L_{P}}-n_{L_{P}})\,|n_{1}\dots n_{L}\rangle\langle n_{1}\dots n_{L}|\Bigg)e^{-i\hat{H}dt}

(S44)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\Big[\hat{M}_{\mu_{1}}(dt)\otimes\dots\hat{M}_{\mu_{L_{P}}}(dt)\Big]\otimes\mathbb{\hat{I}}_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{\hat{I}}_{2}\Bigg)e^{-i\hat{H}dt}.

(S45)

Note that the presence of
e^{-i\hat{H}dt}
in
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
is due to the fact that within the interval
dt
, the system is also evolving by the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
. Here all
\mathbb{\hat{I}}_{2}
in Eq. (
S45
) which are outside the square bracket correspond to the sites that do not have detectors. Each
\hat{M}_{\mu_{j}}
in Eq. (
S45
) takes the form,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\mu_{j}}(dt)=\sum_{n_{j}\in\{0,1\}}\phi(\mu_{j}-n_{j})|n_{j}\rangle\langle n_{j}|=c\sum_{n_{j}\in\{0,1\}}e^{-\gamma\,dt\,(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}|n_{j}\rangle\langle n_{j}|,

(S46)

where
c=(2\gamma\,dt/\pi)^{1/4}
. Recall that the wave-function
\phi(x_{j})
is given in Eq. (
S28
).
Each
\hat{M}_{\mu_{j}}
satisfies the relation
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu_{j}\,\hat{M}^{\dagger}_{\mu_{j}}\hat{M}_{\mu_{j}}=\mathbb{\hat{I}}_{2}
. From Eq. (
S46
), we now write the operator
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
as,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)=c^{L_{P}}e^{-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}e^{-i\hat{H}dt}\approx c^{L_{P}}e^{-i\hat{H}dt}e^{-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}

(S47)

where we have used the fact that
e^{-iAdt}\,e^{-iBdt}\approx e^{-iBdt}\,e^{-iAdt}+O(dt^{2})
because we are interested only upto
O(dt)
.
Thus the state at time
dt
in terms of the operator
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
can be approximated as,

\displaystyle|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{c}}\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)|\psi(0)\rangle\approx\frac{c^{L_{P}}}{\mathcal{N}_{c}}e^{-i\hat{H}dt}e^{-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\mu_{j}-\hat{n}_{j})^{2}}|\psi(0)\rangle,

(S48)

which is correct upto
O(dt)
. Recall that
|\psi(0)\rangle
is the initial state given in Eq. (
S29
). Here

\mathcal{N}_{c}
is the normalization factor for the state
|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
in Eq. (
S48
) which is given as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{c}=\langle\psi(0)|\hat{M}^{\dagger}_{\mu}(dt)\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)|\psi(0)\rangle,

(S49)

with
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
being,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)\approx c^{L_{P}}\,e^{-i\hat{H}dt}e^{-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\mu_{j}-\hat{n}_{j})^{2}}.

(S50)

Eq. (
S48
) is the conditional state
|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
at time
dt
conditioned on the measurement outcomes
\{\mu_{j}\}
. Next we calculate the probability of obtaining this conditional state
|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
given in Eq. (
S48
),

\displaystyle P(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{L_{P}};dt)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{c}^{2}=\langle\psi(0)|\hat{M}^{\dagger}_{\mu}(dt)\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)|\psi(0)\rangle.

(S51)

Using
|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
given in Eq. (
S48
), we get

\displaystyle P(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{L_{P}};dt)=\sum_{\{n_{j}\}}|F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(0)|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}.

(S52)

Eq. (
S48
) and (
S52
) are the main ingredients to construct the SSE. Repeating such conditional evolution through the operator
\hat{M}_{\mu}(dt)
with associated probability
P(\{\mu_{i}\};t)
in each interval
dt
, we can obtain the state
|\psi(dt,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
at any time
t
. Note that all these
\mu_{j}
are not independent random variables rather they are described by the joint probability distribution function
P(\{\mu_{i}\};t)
in Eq. (
S52
). From Eq. (
S52
), we get the distribution of each
\mu_{j}
as,

\displaystyle p(\mu_{j},dt)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu_{1}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu_{j-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu_{j+1}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu_{L_{P}}P(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{L_{P}};dt)

(S53)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{n_{j}\}}|F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(0)|^{2}\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}

(S54)

Now using Eq. (
S30
) we can re-express
|F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(0)|^{2}
which gives,

\displaystyle p(\mu_{j},dt)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{n_{j}\}}\langle\psi(0)|n_{1}\dots n_{L}\rangle\langle n_{1}\dots n_{L}|\psi(0)\rangle\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}

(S55)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{n_{j}\}}\langle\psi(0)|e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-\hat{n}_{j})^{2}}|n_{1}\dots n_{L}\rangle\langle n_{1}\dots n_{L}|\psi(0)\rangle\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(S56)

In the second step we moved the exponential inside
\langle\psi(0)|n_{1}\dots n_{L}\rangle
by converting
n_{j}
into an operator
\hat{n}_{j}
. Next, we use resolution of identity,
\hat{\mathbb{I}}=\sum_{\{n_{j}\}}|n_{1}\dots n_{L}\rangle\langle n_{1}\dots n_{L}|
to get,

\displaystyle p(\mu_{j},dt)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\psi(0)|e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-\hat{n}_{j})^{2}}|\psi(0)\rangle

(S57)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{0})^{2}},

(S58)

where
\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{0}=\langle\psi(0)|\hat{n}_{j}|\psi(0)\rangle
. As each
\mu_{j}
is a Gaussian stochastic variable, we can write it in terms of a random Gaussian noise as the following,

\displaystyle\mu_{j}=\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{0}+\frac{d\xi^{0}_{j}}{2\gamma dt},

(S59)

where all
d\xi_{j}^{0}
s are now independent random Gaussian noise with zero mean and variance
\gamma\,dt
and it satisfies the Itó rule
d\xi^{0}_{i}\,d\xi^{0}_{j}=\gamma\,dt\,\delta_{ij}
.
Thus using Eq. (
S59
) in Eq (
S48
), the state
|\psi(t,\{\mu_{i}\})\rangle
can be written in terms of independent noise
d\xi^{t}_{i}
as,

\displaystyle|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{L_{P}}}{\mathcal{N}_{c}}\exp{\Bigg(-i\hat{H}dt-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\Big(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t}-\frac{d\xi^{t}_{j}}{2\gamma dt}\Big)^{2}}\Bigg)|\psi_{\xi}(t)\rangle

(S60)

The subscript
\xi
is introduced to represent the role of noise. We recall that the normalization factor,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{c}^{2}=\sum_{\{n_{j}\}}|F_{n_{1}\dots n_{L}}(0)|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\Big(\frac{2\gamma dt}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2\gamma dt(\mu_{j}-n_{j})^{2}}=c^{2L_{P}}\,e^{-2\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\mu_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle)^{2}}=c^{2L_{P}}e^{-\frac{L_{P}}{2}},

(S61)

where we have used the expression of
\mu_{j}
from Eq. (
S59
).
Now expanding the square inside the exponential in Eq. (
S60
) and putting the form of
\mathcal{N}
, we obtain,

\displaystyle|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\rangle=\frac{c^{L_{P}}}{c^{L_{P}}e^{-\frac{L_{P}}{4}}}\exp\Big(-i\hat{H}dt-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})d\xi^{t}_{j}-\frac{L_{P}}{4}\Big)

(S62)

Finally, the state at time
t+dt
takes the form,

\displaystyle|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(-i\hat{H}dt-\gamma dt\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})d\xi^{t}_{j}\Big)|\psi_{\xi}(t)\rangle,

(S63)

Expanding the exponential in Eq. (
S63
) and keeping upto terms
O(dt)
, we get,

\displaystyle|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\rangle=\big[\hat{\mathbb{I}}-i\hat{H}dt+\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi_{j}^{t}\,(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})^{2}\,dt\,\big]\,\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle.

(S64)

Finally, we write the SSE under the QSD protocol from Eq. (
S64
),

\displaystyle d\,\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\big[-i\hat{H}dt+\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi_{j}^{t}\,(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})^{2}\,dt\,\big]\,\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle.

(S65)

Here recall that
d\xi_{i}^{t}
is the infinitesimal noise increment in time
dt
which also follows the Itó rule i.e.,
d\xi^{t}_{i}\,d\xi_{j}^{t}=\gamma dt\delta_{ij}
and
\overline{d\xi_{i}^{t}}=0
and
\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t}=\langle\psi_{\xi}(t)|\,\hat{n}_{j}\,|\psi_{\xi}(t)\rangle
. Note that the QSD protocol in Eq. (
S65
) is nonlinear in
|\psi_{\xi}(t)\rangle
due to the presence of
\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t}
in the evolution which is like the back action of measurement on the system. Such a nonlinearity is absent in the SUU protocol in Eq. (
S6
) as this protocol does not describe any physical measurement process. Next, we will discuss the numerical simulation of the SSE under QSD protocol given in Eq. (
S65
).
Similar to SUU protocol, the Gaussianity is still preserved throughout the evolution here as well if one starts with Gaussian initial state such as domain wall state (
|1\,\,1\,\,1\,\dots 1\,\,0\,\,0\dots 0\rangle
) which we had considered in our analysis. Similar to subsection
I.1
we define
\hat{K}_{t}
as,

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t+dt)\big\rangle\approx e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle,

(S66)

From Eq. (
S65
), we write the
\hat{K}_{t}
operator in this case,

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{t}

\displaystyle=-i\hat{H}dt+\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}d\xi_{j}^{t}\,(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\,(\hat{n}_{j}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t})^{2}\,dt

(S67)

\displaystyle=-i\hat{H}dt-\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t}\Big(d\xi^{t}_{j}\,+\frac{\gamma}{2}\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{t}\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{L_{P}}\hat{n}_{j}\Big(d\xi^{t}_{j}+\frac{\gamma}{2}(2\langle n_{j}\rangle_{t}-1)\Big).

(S68)

Hence we can write the state at any instant of time as,

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{\xi}(t)\big\rangle=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{L}U_{jk}(t)\,c_{j}^{\dagger}\,\Big)\,\big|0\big\rangle,

(S69)

where recall that
N
is the total number of particles and
L
is the total number of sites. The
U
matrix respects the isometry relation,
U^{\dagger}(t)U(t)=\mathbb{I}_{N}
. Following procedure similar to Eq. (
S12
),(
S13
) and (
S14
) in subsection
I.1
, we get

\displaystyle e^{\hat{K}_{t}}\,\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\,e^{-\hat{K}_{t}}=\sum_{k,j}[Me^{-ihdt}]_{kj}\,\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger},

(S70)

where the matrix
M
now takes the form

\displaystyle M={\rm diag}(e^{d\xi^{t}_{1}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\,(2\langle n_{1}\rangle_{t}\,-1)\,dt},\dots e^{d\xi^{t}_{L_{S}}+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,(2\langle n_{L_{S}}\rangle_{t}\,-1)\,dt},\,\,\,1,\dots\,1,\,\,1)

(S71)

Again following the procedure of Eq. (
S17
) and (
S18
) in subsection
I.1
we get the evolution of the
U(t)
in this case as,

\displaystyle U(t+dt)=\begin{cases}{\rm diag}(e^{d\xi^{t}_{1}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\,(2\langle n_{1}\rangle_{t}\,-1)\,dt},\dots e^{d\xi^{t}_{L_{S}}+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,(2\langle n_{L_{S}}\rangle_{t}\,-1)\,dt},\,\,\,1,\dots\,1,\,\,1)\,e^{-ihdt}\,U(t),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\quad{\rm(PC}\,\,{\rm case)}\\
{\rm diag}(e^{d\xi^{t}_{1}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\,(2\langle n_{1}\rangle_{t}\,-1)\,dt},\,\dots\,\dots\,e^{d\xi^{t}_{L}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\,(2\langle n_{L}\rangle_{t}\,-1)\,dt\,\,}\!\!)\,e^{-ihdt}\,U(t),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad{\rm(PP}\,\,{\rm case)}\end{cases}

(S72)

where
d\xi_{i}^{t}
s are independent random Gaussian noise with zero mean and variance
\gamma\,dt
and
h
is the single particle Hamiltonian of the system. Having obtained
U(t)
we can extract the correlation matrix
C_{ij}^{\xi}(t)=[U(t)U^{\dagger}(t)]_{ji}
, where the superscript
\xi
represents each quantum trajectory.

II
Dynamics of bipartite entanglement

In this section, we discuss the details of EE dynamics for bipartite setup. In such a scenario, instead of a full Page curve dynamics, one sees a growth in EE followed by a steady state saturation. It is important to note that this saturation value may not always match the Page value (
S_{P}
, the maximum value of EE in a Page curve). It is therefore interesting to understand the saturation dynamics of EE in a bipartite setup. Furthermore, it is important to analyze whether or not the Page value and the saturation value (
S_{\rm sat}
) show similar system size scaling behaviour. In Fig.
S3
, we present a scenario where the Page value and the saturation value of EE completely differ and more interestingly show very different system size scaling dependence. We consider the bipartite PC setup with domain wall initial condition and perform QSD unraveling. We set
\gamma=0.1
. Initially, the EE shows similar behaviour to the EE in the large reservoir case (see Fig.
2
b) i.e. starting from zero it shows a logarithmic growth until it reaches a maximum value. But contrary to the large reservoir case, in the bipartite case, the finite size effect kicks in. The particle front that flows ballistically in the empty reservoir, reflects off the boundary (due to the bipartite nature of the setup) and flows back to the system. Around this time scale there is also a sharp increase in the EE value as seen in Fig
S3
. It is followed by the saturation of EE value. It is clear that this saturation value is completely different than the Page value (see Fig.
2
b). Moreover, as seen in the inset of Fig
S3
, the EE in this bipartite setup clearly shows a sub-volume law scaling up to
L_{S}=160
for
\gamma=0.1
. In contrast, in Fig.
2
b inset, the Page value for system-reservoir setup shows a sub-volume to area law crossover with crossover seen at
L_{S}\approx 160
for the same
\gamma=0.1
value.

III
Initial condition dependence on entanglement entropy

The question of how our results of entanglement entropy depend on the details of the initial condition is an interesting question that requires a thorough investigation. In this direction, we use another initial condition which is a Neel state. More precisely, in our setup by Neel state we mean the following: the system sites are filled alternatingly whereas the reservoir is completely vacuum. In this section, we discuss the dynamics of EE in the PP case with QSD unraveling starting with a Neel state and with
\gamma=0.1
. As seen in Fig
S4
a, the Neel state initial condition also demonstrates Page curve dynamics. The rising part of EE shows a
\ln(\ln(t))
growth. Subsequently, the EE reaches the Page value and decays as a power-law. On comparing the PP case with the domain wall initial condition, as discussed in the main text (see Fig
3
b), the Neel state initial condition shows the same growth and decay in time for EE. In Fig
S4
b we plot the bipartite EE which also shows the
\ln(\ln(t))
growth and finally saturates to a system-size dependent value.

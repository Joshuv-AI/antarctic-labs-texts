Affinization of dendriform D \mathrm{D} -bialgebras, Lie bialgebras and solutions of classical Yang-Baxter equation
Bo Hou

Abstract
. In this paper, we mainly discuss how to use dendriform D \mathrm{D} -bialgebras to construct Lie bialgebras and the relationship between the solutions of their corresponding Yang-Baxter equations. We provide two methods for obtaining Lie algebras from dendriform algebras using the tensor product with perm algebras, one by means of associative algebras and the other by means of pre-Lie algebras. We elevate both approaches to the level of bialgebras and prove that the Lie bialgebraa obtained using these two approaches are the same. There is a correspondence between symmetric solutions of the dendriform Yang-Baxter equation in dendriform algebras and certain skew-symmetric solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the Lie algebras induced from the dendriform algebras. The connections between triangular bialgebra structures, 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators related to the solutions of these Yang-Baxter equations are discussed in detail. During the discussion, we also present a method for constructing infinite-dimensional antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra by using the affineization of dendriform D \mathrm{D} -bialgebras.

Introduction

A bialgebra structure on a given algebra structure is obtained as a coalgebra
structure together which gives the same algebra structure on the dual space with
a set of compatibility conditions between the products and coproducts.
Two famous examples of bialgebras are Lie bialgebras
[
13
]
and antisymmetric
infinitesimal (for short ASI) bialgebras
[
36
,
3

. Lie bialgebras can be viewed
as a linearization of Poisson-Lie groups. It were first introduced by Drinfeld in
the context of the theory of Yang-Baxter equations and quantum groups. Later it has
found applications in many other areas of mathematics and mathematical physics such as
the theory of Hopf algebra deformations of universal enveloping algebras
[
15
]
,
string topology and symplectic field theory
[
11
]
,
Goldman-Turaev theory of free loops in Riemann surfaces with boundaries
[
17
]
,
and so on. Infinitesimal bialgebras first appeared in the work of Joni and Rota to give
an algebraic framework for the calculus of divided differences
[
22
]
.
The antisymmetric version of infinitesimal bialgebras was introduced in
[
36
]

by Zhelyabin by using the name associative
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebra as an associative analog
of Lie bialgebra. Later this structure was studied systematically by Bai under
the name antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra in
[
3
]
.
The antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra can be characterized by the well-known
matched pair of associative algebras and a double construction of a Frobenius algebra.
In recent years, the bialgebra theories of various algebra structures have been
extensive developed, such as left-symmetric bialgebras (also called pre-Lie
bialgebras)
[
2

, Jordan bialgebras
[
36
]
, Novikov bialgebras
[

1.2.
Dendriform
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebras affinization construction of
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras.
An important construction of
infinite-dimensional Lie algebras is a process of affinization.
Balinsky and Novikov showed that the affinization of a Novikov algebra naturally
defines a Lie algebra, a property that in fact characterizes the Novikov algebra
[
6
]
.
Recently, Hong, Bai and Guo have introduced the notion of quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
Novikov algebra and shown that the tensor product of a finite-dimensional Novikov
bialgebra and a quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right Novikov algebra can be naturally endowed
with a completed Lie bialgebra. The converse of this result also holds when
the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right Novikov algebra is some special right Novikov algebra,
giving the desired characterization of the Novikov bialgebra that its affinization
is a Lie bialgebra
[
18
]
. The affinization of perm bialgebras by quadratic

\mathbb{Z}
-graded pre-Lie algebras is another example
[
26
]
. All these examples fit
into the interpretation of the Ginzburg-Kapranov operadic Koszul duality
[
16
]
.
Here, we give an affinization of dendriform
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebras which
is independent of operadic Koszul duality.

Roughly speaking, the affinization of a given algebra structure is to define
algebra structure on the vector space of Laurent polynomials and obtain another algebra
structure on the tensor product, which could resolve the given algebra structure in turn.
In this paper, we consider the affinization of dendriform
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebras.
We first show that the tensor product of a dendriform algebra and a special
perm algebra on the vector space of Laurent polynomials (which is constructed in

[
26
]
) being an associative algebra characterizes the dendriform algebra.
That is, we obtain associative algebras via affinization of dendriform algebras
by perm algebras, which gives an affinization characterization of dendriform algebras.
Second, we consider the dual version of the dendriform algebra affinization, for
dendriform coalgebras. We introduce the notion of completed coassociative coalgebras
and prove that there is a natural completed coassociative coalgebra structure on the
tensor product of a dendriform coalgebra and the completed perm coalgebra of Laurent
polynomials, a property that in fact characterizes the dendriform coalgebra.
Finally, we use the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra lift the affinization
characterizations of dendriform algebras and dendriform coalgebras to the level
of bialgebras.

2.
Preliminaries on pre-Lie algebras and dendriform algebras

In this section, we recall the notions of pre-Lie algebras, dendriform algebras,
bimodules over pre-Lie algebras and dendriform algebras, the
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
pre-Lie algebras and dendriform algebras. Starting from a dendriform
algebra, using the tensor product with a perm algebra, we present two methods for
obtaining a Lie algebra, and the Lie algebras obtained by these two methods are
consistent. Dendriform algebras were introduced by Loday.
First, we recall the definition of dendriform algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A
dendriform algebra

(D,\prec,\succ)
is a vector space
D
with two
bilinear operators
\prec,\succ:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
, such that

\displaystyle(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\prec d_{3}=d_{1}\prec(d_{2}\prec d_{3})+d_{1}\prec(d_{2}\succ d_{3}),

\displaystyle\qquad\quad(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\prec d_{3}=d_{1}\succ(d_{2}\prec d_{3}),

\displaystyle d_{1}\succ(d_{2}\succ d_{3})=(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\succ d_{3}+(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\succ d_{3},

for any
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in D
.

Recall that an
associative Rota-Baxter algebra
is an associative algebra
(A,\ast)

endowed with a linear operator
R:A\rightarrow A
subject to the following relation:

R(a_{1})\ast R(a_{2})=R\big(R(a_{1})\ast a_{2}+a_{1}\ast R(a_{2})\big),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

Example 2.2
.

(i)
Let

regular bimodule
over
(D,\prec,\succ)
. Let
(A,\ast)

be an associative algebra. Recall that the regular bimodule
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A})
of
(A,\ast)
is given by
\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{1}\ast a_{2}
and

\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{2}\ast a_{1}
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in D
.
It is easy to see that the regular bimodule
(D,\mathbf{l}_{D},\mathbf{r}_{D})
of the
sub-adjacent associative algebra
(D,\ast)
of
(D,\prec,\succ)
is also
induced by the regular bimodule
(D,\mathbf{l}_{\prec},\mathbf{r}_{\prec},\mathbf{l}_{\succ},\mathbf{r}_{\succ})
of
(D,\prec,\succ)
, i.e.,
\mathbf{l}_{D}=\mathbf{l}_{\prec}+\mathbf{l}_{\succ}

and
\mathbf{r}_{D}=\mathbf{r}_{\prec}+\mathbf{r}_{\succ}
.

Dendriform algebras are not only closely related to the associative algebras,
but also closely related to the pre-Lie algebras.

Definition 2.4
.

A
pre-Lie algebra
is a pair
(A,\diamond)
, where
A
is a vector space and

\diamond:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
is a bilinear operator such that,

a_{1}\diamond(a_{2}\diamond a_{3})-(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})\diamond a_{3}=a_{2}\diamond(a_{1}\diamond a_{3})-(a_{2}\diamond a_{1})\diamond a_{3},

for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
.

In
[
1
]
, Aguiar pointed out that every dendriform algebra has a pre-Lie
algebra structure.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
1
,
3
]
)
.

Let
(D,\prec,\succ)
be a dendriform algebra.
Define a bilinear operator
\diamond:D\otimes D\rightarrow D

d_{1}\diamond d_{2}=d_{1}\succ d_{2}-d_{2}\prec d_{1},

for any
d_{1},d_{2}\in D
. Then
(D,\diamond)
is a pre-Lie algebra,
called the
induced pre-Lie algebra
from
(D,\prec,\succ)
.

Recall the notion of bimodule over a pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a pre-Lie algebra. A
bimodule
over
(A,\diamond)
is a
triple
(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
, such that
V
is a vector space, and
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(V)
are linear maps satisfying

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})-\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})-\mathfrak{l}(a_{2}\diamond a_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})-\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})-\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a pre-Lie algebra. Define linear maps
\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(A)
by
\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{1}\diamond a_{2}
and

\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{2}\diamond a_{1}
respectively, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
Then
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A})
is a bimodule over
(A,\diamond)
,
which is called the
regular bimodule
over
(A,\diamond)
.

Given a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\diamond)
one may define a bracket on
A
by

[a_{1},a_{2}]=a_{1}\diamond a_{2}-a_{2}\diamond a_{1}
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
Then
(A,[-,-])
is a Lie algebra, which is called the sub-adjacent
Lie algebra of
(A,\diamond)
. Moreover, since the properad of pre-Lie algebras
and the the properad of perm algebras are Koszul dual, we can also get a Lie algebra
by the tensor product of a pre-Lie algebra and a perm algebra.

Definition 2.7
.

A
perm algebra
is a pair
(B,\cdot)
, where
B
is a vector space and

\cdot:B\otimes B\rightarrow B
is a bilinear operator such that,

b_{1}(b_{2}b_{3})=(b_{1}b_{2})b_{3}=(b_{2}b_{1})b_{3},

for any
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
, where
b_{1}b_{2}:=b_{1}\cdot b_{2}
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
33
,
26
]
)
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
(B,\cdot)
be a perm algebra.
Define a bracket
[-,-]:(A\otimes B)\otimes(A\otimes B)\rightarrow A\otimes B

[a_{1}\otimes b_{1},\;a_{2}\otimes b_{2}]=(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})-(a_{2}\diamond a_{1})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1}),

a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. Then
(A\otimes B,[-,-])

the induced Lie algebra
from
(A,\diamond)
by
(B,\cdot)
.

Similarly, we can get an associative algebra
by the tensor product of a dendriform algebra and a perm algebra.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(D,\prec,\succ)
be a dendriform algebra and
(B,\cdot)
be a perm algebra.
Define a bilinear operator

for any
d_{1},d_{2}\in D
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. Then
(D\otimes B,\ast)

is an associative algebra, called
the induced associative algebra
from

(D,\prec,\succ)
by
(B,\cdot)
.

Proof.

For any
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in D
and
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
,

\displaystyle\;((d_{1}\otimes b_{1})\ast(d_{2}\otimes b_{2}))\ast(d_{3}\otimes b_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\big((d_{1}\succ d_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})+(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1})\big)\ast(d_{3}\otimes b_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;((d_{1}\succ d_{2})\succ d_{3})\otimes((b_{1}b_{2})b_{3})+((d_{1}\succ d_{2})\prec d_{3})\otimes(b_{3}(b_{1}b_{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad+((d_{1}\prec d_{2})\succ d_{3})\otimes((b_{2}b_{1})b_{3})+((d_{1}\prec d_{2})\prec d_{3})\otimes(b_{3}(b_{2}b_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(d_{1}\otimes b_{1})\ast\big((d_{2}\succ d_{3})\otimes(b_{2}b_{3})+(d_{2}\prec d_{3})\otimes(b_{3}b_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(d_{1}\otimes b_{1})\ast((d_{2}\otimes b_{2})\ast(d_{3}\otimes b_{3})).

Thus,

∎

Moreover, note that each associative algebra
(A,\ast)
has a Lie algebra structure
by the commutator, i.e., the bracket
[-,-]:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
is given by

[a_{1},a_{2}]=a_{1}\diamond a_{2}-a_{2}\diamond a_{1}
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
This Lie algebra
(A,[-,-])
is called the sub-adjacent Lie algebra of
(A,\ast)
.
We have multiple methods to construct a Lie algebra from a dendriform algebra.
One approach is to use the tensor product with a perm algebra to obtain an associative
algebra, and then use commutator to obtain a Lie algebra; Another approach is to
first obtain a pre-Lie algebra, and then use the tensor product of a pre-Lie algebra
and a perm algebra to obtain the Lie algebra. In fact, the Lie algebras obtained
by these two methods are the same.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let

be a dendriform algebra and

be a perm algebra.
Then we have a commutative diagram:

(D,\prec,\succ)

a dendriform algebra

-\otimes B

(D\otimes B,\ast)

an associative algebra

(D,\diamond)

a pre-Lie algebra

-\otimes B

(D\otimes B,[-,-])

a Lie algebra

Proof.

On the one hand, by Proposition
2.9
, we get a Lie algebra structure
on

, where the bracket is given by

\displaystyle\;[d_{1}\otimes b_{1},\;d_{2}\otimes b_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(d_{1}\otimes b_{1})\ast(d_{2}\otimes b_{2})-(d_{2}\otimes b_{2})\ast(d_{1}\otimes b_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})+(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1})-(d_{2}\succ d_{1})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1})-(d_{2}\prec d_{1})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2}),

for any

.
On the other hand, by Propositions

and

we get a Lie algebra

\displaystyle\;[d_{1}\otimes b_{1},\;d_{2}\otimes b_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(d_{1}\diamond d_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})-(d_{2}\diamond d_{1})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})-(d_{2}\prec d_{1})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})-(d_{2}\succ d_{1})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1})+(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1}),

Example 2.11

Consider the 2-dimensional dendriform algebra

In this paper, we will prove that this commutative diagram is also correct at
the level of bialgebras.

3.
Quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras induced by pre-Lie bialgebras

In
[
26
]
, a Lie bialgebra structure on the tensor product of a pre-Lie bialgebra
and a quadratic perm algebra is given. In this section, we consider a special class
of Lie bialgebras, the quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras. We show that a quasi-triangular
pre-Lie bialgebra induces a quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra structure on the tensor product.

Recall that a
pre-Lie coalgebra
is a pair
(A,\vartheta)
, where
A
is a vector
space and
\vartheta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is a linear map satisfying

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\vartheta)\vartheta-(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes\vartheta)\vartheta=(\vartheta\otimes\mathrm{id})\vartheta-(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\vartheta\otimes\mathrm{id})\vartheta,

where
\tau:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the flip operator defined by

\tau(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}):=a_{2}\otimes a_{1}
for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
(A,\vartheta)
be a pre-Lie coalgebra.
If for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, the following compatibility conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle(\vartheta-\tau\vartheta)(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})-(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathrm{id})((\vartheta-\tau\vartheta)(a_{2}))

\displaystyle\quad-(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1}))((\vartheta-\tau\vartheta)(a_{2}))-(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2}))(\vartheta(a_{1}))+(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\tau(\vartheta(a_{1})))=0,

\displaystyle\vartheta(a_{1}\diamond a_{2}-a_{2}\diamond a_{1})-(\mathrm{id}\otimes(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})-\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})))(\vartheta(a_{1}))

\displaystyle\quad-(\mathrm{id}\otimes(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})-\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})))(\vartheta(a_{2}))-(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\vartheta(a_{2}))+(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\vartheta(a_{1}))=0,

then we call
(A,\diamond,\vartheta)
a
pre-Lie bialgebra
.

Recall that
Lie coalgebra

(\mathfrak{g},\delta)

is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
,
such that
\tau\delta=-\delta
and
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\delta)\delta-(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes\delta)\delta=(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta

A
Lie bialgebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\delta)
such that

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
is a Lie algebra,
(\mathfrak{g},\delta)
is a Lie coalgebra, and the following
compatibility condition holds:

\delta([g_{1},g_{2}])=(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1})\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1}))(\delta(g_{2}))-(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{2})\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{2}))(\delta(g_{1})),

where
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1})(g_{2})=[g_{1},g_{2}]
, for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(

.

is a perm coalgebra.

\mathrm{CYBE}

be a basis of

. Since
\omega(-,-)
is
antisymmetric nondegenerate, we get a basis

of

,
which is called the dual basis of

with respect to

\omega(-,-)

, where

\mathrm{PLYBE}

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
(B,\cdot,\omega)
be a
quadratic perm algebra, and
(A\otimes B,[-,-])
be the induced Lie algebra.
Suppose that
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
is a solution
of the
\mathrm{PLYBE}
in
(A,\diamond)
,
r-\tau(r)
is
(\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathrm{ad}_{A})
-invariant. Then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{r}=\sum_{i,j}(x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes(y_{i}\otimes f_{j})\in(A\otimes B)\otimes(A\otimes B)

is a solution of the
\mathrm{CYBE}
in
(A\otimes B,[-,-])
and
\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r})

is
\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}
-invariant, where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
is a basis of
B

and
\{f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}\}
is the dual basis of
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}

with respect to
\omega(-,-)
.

Proof.

First, for any
1\leq p,q\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\;[\widehat{r}_{12},\;\widehat{r}_{13}]+[\widehat{r}_{12},\;\widehat{r}_{23}]+[\widehat{r}_{13},\;\widehat{r}_{23}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\sum_{p,q}\big((x_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\big)\bullet\big((e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big)-\big((x_{j}\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\big)\bullet\big((e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big(x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{j}\big)\bullet\big(e_{p}\otimes(f_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{q}\big)-\big(x_{i}\otimes(x_{j}\diamond y_{i})\otimes y_{j}\big)\bullet\big(e_{p}\otimes(e_{q}f_{p})\otimes f_{q}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond y_{j})\big)\bullet\big(e_{p}\otimes e_{q}\otimes(f_{p}f_{q})\big)-\big(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{j}\diamond y_{i})\big)\bullet\big(e_{p}\otimes e_{q}\otimes(f_{q}f_{p})\big).

Moreover, for given
s,u,v\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{u}\otimes e_{v},\;\sum_{p,q}(e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\Big)

\displaystyle=\omega(e_{s},\;e_{u}e_{v}),

\displaystyle\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{u}\otimes e_{v},\;\sum_{p,q}(e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\Big)

\displaystyle=\omega(e_{s},\;e_{v}e_{u}),

\displaystyle\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{u}\otimes e_{v},\;\sum_{p,q}e_{p}\otimes(f_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{q}\Big)

\displaystyle=\omega(e_{s},\;e_{v}e_{u}-e_{u}e_{v}).

By the nondegeneracy of
\omega(-,-)
, we get

\sum_{p,q}e_{p}\otimes(f_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{q}=\sum_{p,q}\big((e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}-(e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big).

Similarly, we also have
\sum_{p,q}e_{p}\otimes e_{q}\otimes(f_{p}f_{q})=\sum_{p,q}\big((e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}-(e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big)
,

\sum_{p,q}e_{p}\otimes(e_{q}f_{p})\otimes f_{q}=\sum_{p,q}(e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}

\sum_{p,q}e_{p}\otimes e_{q}\otimes(f_{q}f_{p})=-\sum_{p,q}(e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}
.

\displaystyle\;[\widehat{r}_{12},\;\widehat{r}_{13}]+[\widehat{r}_{12},\;\widehat{r}_{23}]+[\widehat{r}_{13},\;\widehat{r}_{23}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\sum_{p,q}\Big((x_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond y_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{j}\diamond y_{i})\Big)\bullet\big((e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\Big((x_{j}\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}\otimes(x_{j}\diamond y_{i})\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond y_{j})\Big)\bullet\big((e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big).

Since
r-\tau(r)
is
(\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathrm{ad}_{A})
-invariant, we get

\mathbf{PL}_{r}=\sum_{i,j}\big((y_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{j}+x_{j}\otimes x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond y_{j})-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond y_{j})-y_{j}\otimes(x_{j}\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}\big)=\sum_{i,j}\Big((y_{j}\diamond y_{i})\otimes x_{j}\otimes x_{i}+y_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{j}\diamond x_{i})-y_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\diamond y_{j})-y_{j}\otimes(x_{j}\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}\Big)
, and so that

\displaystyle-(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathbf{PL}_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Big((x_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond x_{j})\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond y_{j})+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{j}\diamond y_{i})\Big),

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathbf{PL}_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Big(x_{j}\otimes x_{i}\otimes(y_{j}\diamond y_{i})+x_{j}\otimes(y_{j}\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\diamond y_{j})\otimes y_{i}-(x_{j}\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\Big).

Thus,
[\widehat{r}_{12},\;\widehat{r}_{13}]+[\widehat{r}_{12},\;\widehat{r}_{23}]+[\widehat{r}_{13},\;\widehat{r}_{23}]=0
, i.e.,
\widehat{r}
is a solution of
the
\mathrm{CYBE}
in
(A\otimes B,[-,-])
if

is a solution of the
\mathrm{PLYBE}
in
(A,\diamond)
.

Second, we show that
\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r})
is
\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}
-invariant.
For any
a\in A
,

,

, we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})+\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)(\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\Big((x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes[a\otimes e_{p},\;y_{i}\otimes f_{j}]+[a\otimes e_{p},\;x_{i}\otimes e_{j}]\otimes(y_{i}\otimes f_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(y_{i}\otimes f_{j})\otimes[a\otimes e_{p},\;x_{i}\otimes e_{j}]+[a\otimes e_{p},\;y_{i}\otimes f_{j}]\otimes(x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\Big(\big(x_{i}\otimes(a\diamond y_{i})\big)\bullet\big(e_{j}\otimes(e_{p}f_{j})\big)-\big(x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond a)\big)\bullet\big(e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}e_{p})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big((a\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}\big)\bullet\big((e_{p}e_{j})\otimes f_{j}\big)-\big((x_{i}\diamond a)\otimes y_{i}\big)\bullet\big((e_{j}e_{p})\otimes f_{j}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(y_{i}\otimes(a\diamond x_{i})\big)\bullet\big(f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}e_{j})\big)-\big(y_{i}\otimes(x_{i}\diamond a)\big)\bullet\big(f_{j}\otimes(e_{j}e_{p})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big((a\diamond y_{i})\otimes x_{i}\big)\bullet\big((e_{p}f_{j})\otimes e_{j}\big)-\big((y_{i}\diamond a)\otimes x_{i}\big)\bullet\big((f_{j}e_{p})\otimes e_{j}\big).

For given

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{t},\;\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}e_{p})\Big)=\omega(e_{s},\;e_{p}e_{t}-e_{t}e_{p}),

\sum_{j}(e_{p}e_{j})\otimes f_{j}=\sum_{j}(e_{j}e_{p})\otimes f_{j}+\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}e_{p})

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{j}(f_{j}e_{p})\otimes e_{j}=-\sum_{j}(e_{j}e_{p})\otimes f_{j},\qquad\quad\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{j}e_{p})=-\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}e_{p}),

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(e_{p}e_{j})\otimes f_{j}=-\sum_{j}(e_{p}f_{j})\otimes e_{j}=\sum_{j}-f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}e_{j})=\sum_{j}(e_{j}e_{p})\otimes f_{j}+\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}e_{p}).

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})+\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)(\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\Big(\Big(x_{i}\otimes(a\diamond y_{i})+(a\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}-y_{i}\otimes(a\diamond x_{i})-(a\diamond y_{i})\otimes x_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond a)+y_{i}\otimes(x_{i}\diamond a)\Big)\bullet\big(e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}e_{p})\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(x_{i}\otimes(a\diamond y_{i})+(a\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}-y_{i}\otimes(a\diamond x_{i})-(a\diamond y_{i})\otimes x_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-(x_{i}\diamond a)\otimes y_{i}+(y_{i}\diamond a)\otimes x_{i}\Big)\bullet\big((e_{p}e_{j})\otimes f_{j}\big)\Big).

\sum_{i}(a\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}+x_{i}\otimes(a\diamond y_{i})-x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond a)-(a\diamond y_{i})\otimes x_{i}-y_{i}\otimes(a\diamond x_{i})+y_{i}\otimes(x_{i}\diamond a)=0
for any

, we get

\big(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})+\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)(\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r}))=0
, i.e.,

\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r})
is
\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}
-invariant. The proof is finished.

Theorem 3.8
.

(A,\diamond,\vartheta)
be a pre-Lie bialgebra,
(B,\cdot,\omega)

be a quadratic perm algebra, and

\delta

3.1
) and
\widehat{r}

3.4
). Therefore, we have

(i)

(A,\diamond,\vartheta)

Proof.

Let
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}
. First, for any

\displaystyle\delta(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{(b)}\Big(((a\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i})\bullet(b_{(1)}\otimes b_{(2)})+(x_{i}\otimes(a\diamond y_{i}))\bullet(b_{(1)}\otimes b_{(2)})

4.
Infinite-dimensional ASI bialgebras vis affinization of dendriform
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebras

In this section, we recall the notion of a quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra, as
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra equipped with an invariant bilinear form. We show that
the tensor product of a finite-dimensional dendriform
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebra and a quadratic

\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra can be naturally endowed with a completed ASI bialgebra.
The converse of this result also holds when the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra
is special, giving the desired characterization of the dendriform
\mathrm{D}
-bialgebra
that its affinization is a completed ASI bialgebra.

4.1.
Affinization of dendriform algebras and dendriform coalgebras

To provide the affinization characterization of dendriform algebra, we first recall
the notion of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra.

Definition 4.1
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative algebra
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra
)
is an associative algebra
(A,\ast)
(resp. a perm algebra
(B,\cdot)
) with a
linear decomposition
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
(resp.
B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
) such that
each
A_{i}
(resp.
B_{i}
) is finite-dimensional and
A_{i}\ast A_{j}\subseteq A_{i+j}

(resp.
B_{i}\cdot B_{j}\subseteq B_{i+j}
) for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 4.2

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
B=\{f_{1}\partial_{1}+f_{2}\partial_{2}\mid f_{1},f_{2}\in\Bbbk[x_{1}^{\pm},x_{2}^{\pm}]\}
and define a binary operation
\cdot:B\otimes B\rightarrow B
by

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\partial_{s})\cdot(x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}\partial_{t}):=\delta_{s,1}x_{1}^{i_{1}+j_{1}+1}x_{2}^{i_{2}+j_{2}}\partial_{t}+\delta_{s,2}x_{1}^{i_{1}+j_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}+j_{2}+1}\partial_{t},

for any
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
(B,\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra with the linear decomposition

B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
, where

B_{i}=\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{2}f_{k}\partial_{k}\mid f_{k}\text{ is a homogeneous
polynomial with }\deg(f_{k})=i-1,\;k=1,2\Big\}

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By Proposition
2.9
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(D,\prec,\succ)
be a finite-dimensional dendriform algebra and

(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\cdot)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra.
Define a binary operation on
D\otimes B
by

(d_{1}\otimes b_{1})\ast(d_{2}\otimes b_{2})=(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})+(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\otimes(b_{2}b_{1}),

for any
d_{1},d_{2}\in D
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. Then
(D\otimes B,\ast)
is a

\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative algebra, which is called an
affine associative algebra

from
(D,\prec,\succ)
by
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\cdot)
.
Moreover, if
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\cdot)
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra given in
Example
4.2
, then
(D\otimes B,\ast)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative
algebra if and only if
(D,\prec,\succ)
is a dendriform algebra.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.9
,
(D\otimes B,\ast)
is an associative algebra.
Since
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\cdot)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded,
(D\otimes B,\ast)
is a

\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative algebra. If
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\cdot)
is the

\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra given in Example
4.2
, then we have

\displaystyle\Big((d_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast(d_{2}\otimes\partial_{2})\Big)\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})

\displaystyle=\Big((d_{1}\succ d_{2})\otimes x_{1}\partial_{2})+(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\otimes x_{2}\partial_{1}\Big)\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})

\displaystyle=((d_{1}\succ d_{2})\succ d_{3})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}+((d_{1}\succ d_{2})\prec d_{3})\otimes x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+((d_{1}\prec d_{2})\succ d_{3})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}+((d_{1}\prec d_{2})\prec d_{3})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1},

and

\displaystyle(d_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast\Big((d_{2}\otimes\partial_{2})\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=(d_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast\Big((d_{2}\succ d_{3})\otimes x_{2}\partial_{1}+(d_{2}\prec d_{3})\otimes x_{1}\partial_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=(d_{1}\succ(d_{2}\succ d_{3}))\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}+(d_{1}\prec(d_{2}\succ d_{3}))\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+(d_{1}\succ(d_{2}\prec d_{3}))\otimes x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}+(d_{1}\prec(d_{2}\prec d_{3}))\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}.

Comparing the coefficients of
x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}
, we get
(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\prec d_{3}=d_{1}\succ(d_{2}\prec d_{3})
. Similarly, comparing the coefficients of

x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}
in equations
((d_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast(d_{2}\otimes\partial_{1}))\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{2})=(d_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast((d_{2}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{2}))
and

((d_{1}\otimes\partial_{2})\ast(d_{2}\otimes\partial_{1}))\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})=(d_{1}\otimes\partial_{2})\ast((d_{2}\otimes\partial_{1})\ast(d_{3}\otimes\partial_{1}))
, we get
(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\prec d_{3}=d_{1}\prec(d_{2}\prec d_{3})+d_{1}\prec(d_{2}\succ d_{3})
and
d_{1}\succ(d_{2}\succ d_{3})=(d_{1}\prec d_{2})\succ d_{3}+(d_{1}\succ d_{2})\succ d_{3}
respectively.
Thus,
(D,\prec,\succ)
is a dendriform algebra. The proof is finished.
∎

Recall that a
dendriform coalgebra
is a triple
(D,\theta_{\prec},\theta_{\succ})
,
where
D
is a vector space and
\theta_{\prec},\theta_{\succ}:D\rightarrow D\otimes D

are linear maps such that the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad(\theta_{\prec}\otimes\mathrm{id})\theta_{\prec}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\theta_{\prec})\theta_{\prec}+(\mathrm{id}\otimes\theta_{\succ})\theta_{\prec},

\displaystyle(\theta_{\succ}\otimes\mathrm{id})\theta_{\prec}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\theta_{\prec})\theta_{\succ},\qquad\qquad(\mathrm{id}\otimes\theta_{\succ})\theta_{\succ}=(\theta_{\prec}\otimes\mathrm{id})\theta_{\succ}+(\theta_{\succ}\otimes\mathrm{id})\theta_{\succ}.

To carry out the dendriform coalgebra affinization, we need to extend the codomain
of the comultiplications
\theta_{\prec},\theta_{\succ}
to allow infinite sums.
Let
U=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{i}
and
V=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}
be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces.
We call the
completed tensor product
of
U
and
V
to be the vector space

U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V:=\prod_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{i}\otimes V_{j}.

If
U
and
V
are finite-dimensional, then
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is just the usual
tensor product
U\otimes V
. In general, an element in
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is an
infinite formal sum
\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{ij}
with
X_{ij}\in U_{i}\otimes V_{j}
.
So
X_{ij}=\sum_{\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}
for pure tensors

u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\in U_{i}\otimes V_{j}
with
\alpha
in a finite
index set. Thus a general term of
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is a possibly infinite sum

\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i\alpha}\otimes v_{j\alpha}
, where
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha
is
in a finite index set (which might depend on
i,j
). With these notations, for linear
maps
f:U\rightarrow U^{\prime}
and
g:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
, define

f\,\hat{\otimes}\,g:U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\rightarrow U^{\prime}\,\hat{\otimes}\,V^{\prime},\qquad\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{i,j,\alpha}f(u_{i,\alpha})\otimes g(v_{j,\alpha}).

Also the twist map
\tau
has its completion

\hat{\tau}:V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\rightarrow V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
,

\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{j,\alpha}\otimes u_{i,\alpha}
.
Moreover, we define a (completed) comultiplication to be a linear map

\Delta:V\rightarrow V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
,

\Delta(v):=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}
.
Then we have the well-defined map

(\Delta\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathrm{id})\Delta(v)=(\Delta\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathrm{id})\Big(\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}\Big):=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}\Delta(v_{1,i,\alpha})\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}\in V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V.

Definition 4.4
.

(i)

A
completed coassociative coalgebra
is a pair
(A,\Delta)
where

A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space and

\Delta:A\rightarrow A\,\hat{\otimes}\,A
is a linear map satisfying

(\Delta\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathrm{id})\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\,\hat{\otimes}\,\Delta)\Delta.

(ii)

A
completed perm coalgebra
is a pair
(B,\nu)
, where

B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space and
\nu:B\rightarrow B\,\hat{\otimes}\,B
is a linear map satisfying

(\nu\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathrm{id})\nu=(\mathrm{id}\,\hat{\otimes}\,\nu)\nu=(\tau\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathrm{id})(\nu\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathrm{id})\nu.

Example 4.5

(
[
26
]
)
.

Consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

\displaystyle\nu(x_{1}^{m}x_{2}^{n}\partial_{1})

\mathrm{AYBE}

is a basis of
B
and
\{f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}\}

Proof.

First, for any

\displaystyle\;\widehat{r}_{12}\ast\widehat{r}_{13}+\widehat{r}_{13}\ast\widehat{r}_{23}-\widehat{r}_{23}\ast\widehat{r}_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\sum_{p,q}\Big(((x_{i},e_{p})\ast(x_{j},e_{q}))\otimes(y_{i},f_{p})\otimes(y_{j},f_{q})+(x_{i},e_{p})\otimes(x_{j},e_{q})\otimes((y_{i},f_{p})\ast(y_{j},f_{q}))

\sum_{p,q}e_{q}\otimes e_{p}\otimes(f_{p}f_{q})=-\sum_{p,q}(e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}

.

\displaystyle\;\widehat{r}_{12}\ast\widehat{r}_{13}+\widehat{r}_{13}\ast\widehat{r}_{23}-\widehat{r}_{23}\ast\widehat{r}_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\sum_{p,q}\Big((x_{i}\succ x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\succ y_{j})-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\prec y_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\prec y_{j})\otimes y_{i}\Big)\bullet\big((e_{p}e_{q})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big((x_{i}\prec x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\succ y_{j})-x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\succ y_{j})\otimes y_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\prec y_{j})\otimes y_{i}\Big)\bullet\big((e_{q}e_{p})\otimes f_{p}\otimes f_{q}\big).

Since
r
is symmetric and
\mathbf{D}_{r}=0

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\Big((x_{i}\succ x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\succ y_{j})-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\prec y_{j})+x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\prec y_{j})\otimes y_{i}\Big)=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\Big((x_{i}\prec x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes(y_{i}\succ y_{j})-x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\succ y_{j})\otimes y_{i}-x_{j}\otimes(x_{i}\prec y_{j})\otimes y_{i}\Big)=0.

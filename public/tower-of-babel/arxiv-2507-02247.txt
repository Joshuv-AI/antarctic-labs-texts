Ill-posedness of the Euler equations and inviscid limit of the Navie-Stokes equations in Besov spaces
Jinlu Li, Xing Wu, and, Yanghai Yu

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem to the incompressible Euler and Navie-Stokes equations on the d-dimensional torus. Our aim of this paper is two fold. Firstly, we construct a new initial data and present a simple proof of the ill-posedness of the Euler equations in different senses: (1) the solution map of the Euler equations starting from u 0 u_{0} is discontinuous at t = 0 t=0 in B p , ∞ s B^{s}_{p,\infty} with s > 0 s>0 and 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , which covers the result obtained by Cheskidov and Shvydkoy in [ 10 , Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 138 (2010)] ; (2) the solution map of the Euler equations is not continuous as a map from B p , ∞ s B^{s}_{p,\infty} to L T ∞ ​ ( B p , ∞ s ) L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{p,\infty}) ; (3) the solution map of the Euler equations cannot be Hölder continuous in time variable in Besov spaces B p , r s B^{s}_{p,r} . Secondly, we prove the failure of the B p , ∞ s B^{s}_{p,\infty} -convergence in the inviscid limit of a family of solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations towards a solution of the Euler equations. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first one addressing the inviscid limit problem to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in the weak Besov space.



Keywords:
Euler equations; Navier-Stokes equations; Ill-posedness; Inviscid limit; Besov spaces

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d\geq 2

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\varepsilon\Delta u+\nabla P=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\mathrm{div\,}u=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}\setminus 2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d},\end{cases}

(NS)

where
\varepsilon>0
is the kinematic viscosity, the vector field
u(t,x):[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
stands for the velocity of the fluid, the quantity
P(t,x):[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denotes
the scalar pressure, and
\mathrm{div\,}u=0
means that the fluid is incompressible.

When the viscocity vanishes (
\varepsilon=0
), then the Navier-Stokes equations (
NS
) reduces to the Euler equations for ideal incompressible fluid

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\mathrm{div\,}u=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}\setminus 2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d},\end{cases}

(E)

We say that the Cauchy problem (
NS
) or (
E
) is Hadamard (locally) well-posed in a Banach space
X
if for any data
u_{0}\in X
there exists (at least for a short time)
T>0
and a unique solution in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
which depends continuously on the data. In particular, we say that the solution map is continuous if for any
u_{0}\in X
, there exists a neighborhood
B\subset X
of
u_{0}
such that
for every
u\in B
the map
u\mapsto U
from
B
to
\mathcal{C}([0,T];X)
is continuous, where
U

denotes the solution to (
NS
) or (
E
) with initial data
u_{0}
. For the well-posedness of (
E
) in Besov spaces we refer to see
[
1
, Theorem 7.1]
and
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The problem of the convergence of smooth viscous solutions of (

improved the result and proved the convergence in

resolved inviscid limit of Yudovich type solutions with only the assumption that the vorticity is bounded. In the case of two dimensional (torus or the whole space) and rough initial data, by taking greater advantage of vorticity formulation, more beautiful results were obtained quantitatively (see for example

In this paper, we consider the ill-posedness problem of the Euler equations and inviscid limit issue of the Navie-Stokes equations in Besov spaces.

For any
R>0
, from now on, we denote any bounded subset
U_{R}
in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by

U_{R}:=\left\{\phi\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\|\phi\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq R,\;\mathrm{div\,}\phi=0\right\}

and also

\displaystyle u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})=\text{\rm the solution map of}\,\eqref{e}\,\text{\rm with initial data}\,u_{0},

\displaystyle u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{0})=\text{\rm the solution map of}\,\eqref{ns}\,\text{\rm with initial data}\,u_{0}.

Our main results of this paper reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1

Let
d\geq 2
and
s>0
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. There exists a divergence-free vector
field
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

of the Euler equations (
E
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\left\|u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})-u_{0}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geq\eta_{0},

where
\eta_{0}
is some positive constant only dependent of
p
and
d
.

Remark 1.1

Theorem
1.1
demonstrates the ill-posedness of the Euler equations in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
. More precisely, there exists
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}
such that the corresponding solution to the Euler equations that starts from
u_{0}
does not converge back to
u_{0}
in the metric of
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
as time goes to zero.

Remark 1.2

Theorem
1.1
improves the result obtained by Cheskidov and Shvydkoy in
[
10
]
since the index
p>2
have be enlarged to
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We simplify their approach and present a short and direct proof by constructing explicitly travelling wave solutions, which does not depend on the nonlinear structure of the Euler equations. In fact, we can construct special solutions causing the ill-posedness. To see clearly this, for instance, let
s>0
and

v(x_{1}-t)=2^{-ns}\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}(x_{1}-t)\right),\quad v(x_{1})=2^{-ns}\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)

be two high frequency waves such that
n
is a larger number. In this case, both
\left\|v(x_{1}-t)\right\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
and
\left\|v(x_{1})\right\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
have positive lower and super bounds. However, notice that

v(x_{1}-t)=-v(x_{1}),\quad\text{if}\quad\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t=\pi,

the Besov norm of the difference, i.e.,
\left\|v(x_{1}-t)-v(x_{1})\right\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=2\left\|v(x_{1})\right\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
, still has a positive lower bound.

We recall the classical well-posedness theory for the Euler equations, as found in
[
1
]
, which is convenient for our purposes: Assume that
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
and
s>1+d/p
and
u_{0}\in B_{p,r}^{s}
satisfies
\mathord{{\rm div}}u_{0}=0
, then there exists a short time
T=T\left(u_{0}\right)
, such that the Euler equations (
E
) has a unique solution
u(t,x)\in C\left([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s}\right)
if
1\leq r<\infty
or
u(t,x)\in C_{w}\left([0,T];B_{p,\infty}^{s}\right)
(the subscript
w
indicates weak continuity in the time variable). Furthermore, we have the estimate

\|u(t)\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}}\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}}\quad\text{ for }0\leq t\leq T.

Our second result of this paper reads as follows:

Theorem 1.2

Let
d\geq 2
. Assume that
(s,p,r)
satisfying

\displaystyle s>\frac{d}{p}+1,(p,r)\in[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty)\quad\mathrm{or}\quad s=\frac{d}{p}+1,(p,r)\in[1,\infty]\times\{1\}.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists a divergence-free vector
field
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

of the Euler equations (
E
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\left\|u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})-u_{0}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}{t^{\alpha}}=+\infty.

Remark 1.3

We would like to mention that Theorem
1.2
is new. In fact, it is known that the solution
u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})
for (
E
) is continuous in time
in Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
r<\infty
while Theorem
1.2
furthermore indicates that the solution
u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})
for (
E
) cannot be Hölder continuous in time in the same Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Our third result of this paper reads as follows:

Theorem 1.3

Let
d\geq 2
and
s>1+d/p
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The solution map of the incompressible Euler equations is not
continuous as a map from
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
to
L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. More precisely, there exits initial data
u_{0},u^{n}_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u^{n}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=0

but the solution map satisfies that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\rm E}(t_{n},u^{n}_{0})-u^{\rm E}(t_{n},u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geq\eta_{0},\quad t_{n}\to 0^{+}\quad\text{as}\;\;n\to\infty,

with some positive constant
\eta_{0}
only dependent of
p
and
d
.

Remark 1.4

Although Theorem
1.3
also demonstrates the ill-posedness of the Euler equations in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
, we would like to emphasize that it is different from Theorem
1.1
. In fact, Theorem
1.3
indicates that the data-to-solution map of the incompressible Euler equations do not depend continuously on the initial data.

Our last result is the following.

Theorem 1.4

Let
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Assume that
s>1+d/p
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then a family of solution maps
(u_{0},\varepsilon)\mapsto u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{0})
to the Navier-Stokes equations (
NS
)
do not converge to the solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u^{\rm E}(t,u_{0})
of the Euler equations (
E
) in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
.
More precisely, there exists initial data
u_{0}\in U_{R}
such that

\liminf_{\varepsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}}\left\|u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t_{n},u_{0})-u^{\rm E}(t_{n},u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geq\eta_{0},

with some positive constant
\eta_{0}
only dependent of
p
and
d
.

Remark 1.5

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.4
is the first result in the weak Besov topology on the non-convergence of the solutions for the Navier-Stokes equations in the inviscid limit.

2
Preliminaries

We define the periodic Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
as

(\mathcal{F}u)(k)=\widehat{u}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{-\mathrm{i}x\cdot k}u(x)\mathrm{d}x.

and the inverse Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
as

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}u)(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}u(k)e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot k}.

We decompose
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
on the circle
\mathbb{T}^{d}
into Fourier series, i.e.

u(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{u}(k)e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot k}.

We are interested in solutions which take values in the Besov space
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Recall that one way to define this space requires a dyadic partition of unity. Given a smooth bump function
\chi
supported on the ball of radius
4/3
, and equal to 1 on the ball of radius
3/4
, we set
\varphi(\xi)=\chi(2^{-1}\xi)-\chi(\xi)
and
\varphi_{j}(\xi)=\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)
. then we deduce that
\varphi
satisfies that
{\rm{supp}}\;\varphi\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\right\}
and

\varphi(\xi)\equiv 1
for
4/3\leq|\xi|\leq 3/2
. Using this partition, we define the periodic dyadic blocks as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=0,\quad\text{ if }\quad j\leq-2,

\displaystyle\Delta_{-1}u=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\chi(\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi},

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{j}(\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi},\quad\text{ if }\quad j\geq 0.

The operators
\Delta_{j}
defined on the periodic domain share many properties with those
on the whole space(see
[
1
]
). In particular, we obtain the Littlewood-Paley decomposition of
u

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}\Delta_{j}u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

Definition 2.1

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is defined by

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\;\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}<\infty\right\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}\left(2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right)^{r}\right)^{1/r},

if
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}\left(2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right),

if
r=\infty
.

Let us complete this section by presenting two lemmas which will be used often in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1

Let
3\leq m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
-1\leq j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Delta_{j}\left[\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{m}x_{1}\right)\right]=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\quad j\neq m,\\
\cos(\frac{11}{8}2^{m}x_{1}),&\text{if}\quad j=m.\end{cases}

Proof.
We set
\lambda=\frac{11}{8}2^{m}
and
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda,0,\cdots,0)
for simplicity. Notice that
\varphi_{j}(k)
is symmetric, i.e.,
\varphi_{j}(k)=\varphi_{j}(|k|)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\left(\cos(\lambda x_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{j}(k)\left(\mathbf{1}_{\vec{\lambda}}(k)+\mathbf{1}_{-\vec{\lambda}}(k)\right)e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{j}(\ell)\left(\mathbf{1}_{\lambda}(\ell)+\mathbf{1}_{-\lambda}(\ell)\right)e^{\mathrm{i}x_{1}\ell}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{j}(\lambda)\cos(\lambda x_{1})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\quad j\neq m,\\
\cos(\lambda x_{1}),&\text{if}\quad j=m.\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{1}_{K}(x)
is the indicator function, taking a value of 1 if
x=K
and 0 otherwise.

Thus we obtain the desired result of Lemma
2.1
.

Remark 2.1

For large fixed
m
,
\varphi_{j}\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{m}x_{1}\right)
vanish except for
j=m
due to the support condition of
\varphi
and
\varphi(k)\equiv 1
for

Lemma 2.2

Let
1\ll n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\left\|\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x\right)\right\|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi])}=c_{0}:=\begin{cases}\left(2\int^{\pi}_{0}|\cos x|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/{p}},&\text{if}\quad p\in[1,\infty),\\

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Choice of initial data.
Let us fix an
s>0
and define the initial data

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=(1,f(x_{1}),0,\ldots,0),

(3.1)

where
f(x)
is a bounded real-valued periodic function of one variable of the following form

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}2^{-js}\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x\right).

(3.2)

It is not difficult to check that
\mathord{{\rm div}}u_{0}=0
and
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Construction of solution.
An fundamental observe is that the vector field

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\left(1,f\left(x_{1}-t\right),0,\ldots,0\right),

(3.3)

is an obvious periodic solution of the incompressible Euler equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=0,\quad\mathrm{div\,}u=0,

with
P=0
, i.e. this is a pressureless flow.

From (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)=\left(0,f\left(x_{1}-t\right)-f(x_{1}),0,\ldots,0\right),

From the explicit formula for
f(x)
in (
3.2
) and by direct computation, we see that

\displaystyle f(x_{1}-t)-f(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}2^{-js}\left[\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{a}_{j}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{b}_{j}(t)\right],

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{j}(t)=\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}t\right)-1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{b}_{j}(t)=\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}t\right).

(3.4)

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we have for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle 2^{ns}\Delta_{n}[f(x_{1}-t)-f(x_{1})]

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{a}_{n}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{b}_{n}(t).

Letting
\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t_{n}=\pi
, then one has
\mathbf{a}_{n}(t_{n})=-2
and
\mathbf{b}_{n}(t_{n})=0
.
Thus we have for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle\quad 2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}[f(x_{1}-t_{n})-f(x_{1})]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=2(2\pi)^{d-1}\left\|\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}.

From which and Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle\|u(t_{n},x)-u_{0}(x)\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|f(x_{1}-t_{n})-f(x_{1})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}[f(x_{1}-t_{n})-f(x_{1})]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=2c_{0}(2\pi)^{d-1}.

Notice that
t_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let us fix an
s>1+\frac{d}{p}
and define the initial data

u_{0}(x)=(1,g(x_{1}),0,\cdots,0),

where
g(x)
is a bounded real-valued period function of one variable of the following form

\displaystyle g(x)=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}j^{-2}2^{-js}\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x\right).

(4.1)

It is not difficult to check that
\mathord{{\rm div}}u_{0}=0
and
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for any
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
.

Let
t\geq 0
, we consider

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\left(1,g\left(x_{1}-t\right),0,\ldots,0\right),

which is a period function which also satisfies the incompressible Euler equations (
E
) with initial condition
u_{0}(x)=(1,g(x_{1}),0,\cdots,0)
.

Continue in a similar fashion we see that

\displaystyle u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)=\left(0,g\left(x_{1}-t\right)-g(x_{1}),0,\ldots,0\right),

and

\displaystyle g(x_{1}-t)-g(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}j^{-2}2^{-js}\left(\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{a_{j}}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{b}_{j}(t)\right),

where
\mathbf{a_{j}}(t)
and
\mathbf{b_{j}}(t)
are given by (
3.4
).

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we have for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{ns}\Delta_{n}[g(x_{1}-t)-g(x_{1})]

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{a}_{n}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{b}_{n}(t),

Letting
\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t_{n}=\pi
, then one has
\mathbf{a}_{n}(t_{n})=-2
and
\mathbf{b}_{n}(t_{n})=0
. Thus we have for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle\quad 2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}[g(x_{1}-t_{n})-g(x_{1})]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=2(2\pi)^{d-1}n^{-2}\left\|\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}.

From which and Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle t_{n}^{-\alpha}\|u(t_{n},x)-u_{0}(x)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=t_{n}^{-\alpha}\|g(x_{1}-t_{n})-g(x_{1})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq t_{n}^{-\alpha}2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}[g(x_{1}-t_{n})-g(x_{1})]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=2c_{0}(2\pi)^{d-1}t_{n}^{-\alpha}n^{-2}.

Notice that
t_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
t_{n}^{-\alpha}n^{-2}\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
\lambda_{n}=1+\frac{1}{n}
and
f(x)
be given by (
3.2
), we consider

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\left(1,f(x_{1}-t),0,\ldots,0\right),

\displaystyle u_{n}(t,x)=\left(\lambda_{n},f\left(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t\right),0,\ldots,0\right).

It is not difficult to verify that both
u(t,x)
and
u_{n}(t,x)
are periodic solutions to the Euler equations (
E
) with initial data
u_{0}=(1,f(x_{1}),0,\cdots,0)
and
u_{0,n}=(\lambda_{n},f(x_{1}),0,\cdots,0)
, respectively.

Difference of initial data.
Obviously, we see that

\displaystyle\|u_{0,n}-u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\frac{1}{n}\|1\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{n}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\;n\to\infty,

where we have used
\Delta_{j}1=0
if
j\neq-1
.

Difference of solutions.
Obviously,

u_{n}(t,x)-u(t,x)=\left(\frac{1}{n},f(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t)-f(x_{1}-t),0,\ldots,0\right).

Next we shall prove that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}(t,x)-u(t,x)\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\nrightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\;n\to\infty.

A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle f(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t)-f(x_{1}-t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}2^{-js}\left[\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{c}_{j}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{d}_{j}(t)\right],

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{j}(t)=\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}\lambda_{n}t\right)-\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}t\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{j}(t)=\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}\lambda_{n}t\right)-\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}t\right).

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we have for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle 2^{ns}\Delta_{n}[f(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t)-f(x_{1}-t)]

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{c}_{n}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{d}_{n}(t).

Letting
\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t_{n}=n\pi
, then one has
\mathbf{c}_{n}(t_{n})=\pm 2
and
\mathbf{d}_{n}(t_{n})=0
, which give that

\displaystyle\quad 2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}[f(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t_{n})-f(x_{1}-t_{n})]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=2(2\pi)^{d-1}\left\|\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}.

Thus, we have for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle\|u_{n}(t_{n},x)-u(t_{n},x)\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq\|f(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t_{n})-f(x_{1}-t_{n})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}-\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}[f(x_{1}-\lambda_{n}t)-f(x_{1}-t_{n})]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}-\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\geq 2c_{0}(2\pi)^{d-1}-\frac{1}{n}.

Letting
n\to\infty
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

6
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Choice of initial data.
Let us fix an
s
satisfying
s>1+d/p
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
and define the initial data

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=(1,f(x_{1}),0,\ldots,0),

where
f(x)
is given by (
3.2
).

Construction of solution to the Euler equations.

Let
t\geq 0
, we consider

\displaystyle u^{\rm E}(t,x)=\left(1,f(x_{1}-t),0,\ldots,0\right).

By the classical well-posedness theory, we know that
u^{\rm E}(t,x)
is a unique periodic solution of the Euler equations (
E
) with initial data
u_{0}(x)=\left(1,f(x_{1}),0,\ldots,0\right)
.

Construction of solution to the Navier-Stokes equations.

Assume that
f_{n}(t,x_{1})
solves the Cauchy Problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{n}+\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n}-\varepsilon_{n}\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}f_{n}=0,\quad t>0,\\
f_{n}(t=0,x_{1})=f(x_{1}).\end{cases}

(6.1)

Let
t\geq 0
, we consider

\displaystyle u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x)=(1,f_{n}(t,x_{1}),0,\ldots,0).

Due to (
6.1
), we know that
u^{\rm NS}_{n}(t,x)
is a unique periodic solution of the Navier-Stokes equations with initial data
u_{0}(x)=\left(1,f(x_{1}),0,\ldots,0\right)
.

It is easy to deduce that the Cauchy Problem (
6.1
) has a unique explicit solution

\displaystyle f_{n}(t,x_{1})=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}2^{-js}\left[\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}(x_{1}-t)\right)e^{-\varepsilon_{n}\frac{121}{64}2^{2j}t}\right].

We shall compare the solution of Euler equations with that of Navier-Stokes equations. Obviously,

u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t_{n},u_{0})-u^{\rm E}(t_{n},u_{0})=(0,f_{n}(t,x_{1})-f(x_{1}-t_{n}),0,\ldots,0),

and

\displaystyle f_{n}(t,x_{1})-f(x_{1}-t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=3}2^{-js}\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{j}(x_{1}-t)\right)\left[e^{-\varepsilon_{n}\frac{121}{64}2^{2j}t}-1\right].

By Lemma
2.1
, one has for some
n
large enough

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\left[f_{n}(t,x_{1})-f(x_{1}-t_{n})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2^{-ns}\left[\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{e}_{n}(t)+\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\mathbf{f}_{n}(t)\right]\mathbf{g}_{n}(t),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{n}(t)=\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t\right),\quad\mathbf{f}_{n}(t)=\sin\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t\right)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{n}(t)=e^{-\varepsilon_{n}\frac{121}{64}2^{2n}t}-1.

Letting
\frac{11}{8}2^{n}t_{n}=\pi
and
\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{8}{11\pi}2^{-n}
, which gives that

\left(\mathbf{e}_{n}(t_{n}),\;\mathbf{f}_{n}(t_{n}),\;\mathbf{g}_{n}(t_{n})\right)=(-1,\;0,\;e^{-1})

and thus

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\left[f_{n}(t_{n},x_{1})-f(x_{1}-t_{n})\right]

\displaystyle=(1-e^{-1})2^{-ns}\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right).

Combining the above, we have

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t_{n},x)-u^{\rm E}(t_{n},u_{0})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}\left(f_{n}(t_{n},x_{1})-f(x_{1}-t_{n})\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left(1-e^{-1}\right)\cdot\left\|\cos\left(\frac{11}{8}2^{n}x_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=c_{0}\left(1-e^{-1}\right)(2\pi)^{d-1}.

Taking the
\liminf
, we deduce that

\liminf_{\varepsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}}\left\|u^{\rm NS}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t_{n},u_{0})-u^{\rm E}(t_{n},u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\geq c_{0}(2\pi)^{d-1}.

Notice that
t_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

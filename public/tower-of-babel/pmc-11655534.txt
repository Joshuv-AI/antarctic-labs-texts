Chromosome-level genome assembly and annotation of the camouflage grouper (Epinephelus polyphekadion)
Authors: Haoyi Li, Yiqun Liu, Mingguang Mao, Yunxiang Mao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe camouflage grouper (Epinephelus polyphekadion) is an economically carnivorous fish widely distributed in the Indo-Pacific. However, no valid reference genome of E. polyphekadion severely hinders further research on biology and genomic breeding programs. Here, we presented a high-quality chromosome-level genome assembly of E. polyphekadion using PacBio sequencing and Hi-C technologies. The chromosome-level genome has a total length of 1.1 Gb with a contig N50 of 40.8 Mb. Approximately 99.4% of the assembly lengths were anchored to 24 pseudochromosomes. In total, 25,165 protein-coding genes were predicted, among which 24,634 genes (97.9%) were functionally annotated. The completeness of the assembly was estimated to be 99.1% using BUSCO. In summary, the first reference genome of E. polyphekadion in this study will not only provide pivotal genetic resources for further biological and evolutionary studies of groupers, but also benefit the development of genomic hybridization breeding in the artificial grouper industry.Subject terms: Comparative genomics, Genomics

Background & Summary

Groupers (Order Perciformes, Family Epinephelinae), are a biologically diverse group of carnivorous predators inhabiting in coral reef ecosystems, including more than 160 species in 16 genera1. They have become excellent marine species for extensive aquaculture due to their tender flesh, rich nutrition and high market demand2,3. In addition, most groupers are protogynous hermaphrodites and sex reversal by environmental factors occurs in grouper populations4. In recent years, the frequent outbreaks of viral diseases have brought significant economic losses to the grouper aquaculture industry, such as viral nervous necrosis, which causes moderate to high (10 to 100%) mortality rates in larval stages5–7. As such, groupers are a perfect model for investigating development, sex differentiation and disease prevention. The camouflage grouper (Epinephelus polyphekadion), belonging to the Epinephelus genus of the Epinephelidae family, is a marine fish with high nutritional value. This species is mainly distributed in the eastern center of Pacific and the western regions of Indian Ocean. Due to the overfishing and environmental deterioration, wild populations have become decreased and vulnerable. In recent years, artificial breeding technology for the camouflage grouper has become matured.

So far, genomic resources, i.e., giant grouper Epinephelus lanceolatus8, red-spotted grouper Epinephelus akaara9, leopard coral grouper Plectropomus leopardus10, humpback grouper Cromileptes altivelis11 and yellow grouper Epinephelus awoara12 have been published. However, only one study of mitochondrial genome of E. polyphekadion was published13 and other research mainly focused on serum biochemical indexes, growth patterns and performance of a hybrid grouper derived from brown-marbled grouper (E. fuscogutatus, ♀) × camouflage grouper (E. polyphekadion, ♂)14,15. Therefore, it was necessary to have a high-quality reference genome of E. polyphekadion, which will provide key insights into the genetic resources for grouper evolution and germplasm conservation.

At present, high integrity through single-molecule real-time (SMRT) sequencing technology and high-throughput chromatin conformation capture (Hi-C) technology are applied for genome assembly at the chromosome level widely16,17. In this study, we assembled the first chromosome-level genome of E. polyphekadion in a size of 1.1 Gb with contig N50 of 40.8 Mb. And BUSCO analysis showed that 99.1% of the final assembly was complete. In summary, this high-quality of chromosomal-level genome data will support for biological and ecological investigations and breeding programs in aquaculture.

Methods

Sample collection, library construction and sequencing

One adult camouflage grouper (E. polyphekadion) was sampled from Hainan Chenhai Aquatic Co., Ltd. (Lingshui), China. The samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for further analysis. Genomic DNA of E. polyphekadion was extracted from dorsal muscle tissue using a QIAGEN DNeasy Kit (QIAGEN,Shanghai, China). For the Illumina platform (NEB, USA), the short fragment library with an insertion size of 350 bp was generated using NEB Next® Ultra™ DNA Library Prep Kit. Subsequently, HiFi SMRTbell Libraries with an insert size of 20 kb were generated using the SMRTbell Template PrepKit 2.0 (PacBio, USA), and the library was sequenced in circular consensus sequencing(CCS) on the PacBio Sequel II platform. The Hi-C library was constructed following the standard protocol described previously with certain modifications18 and was sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq × platform. Approximately 41.95 Gb of PacBio data were generated.

RNA isolation was performed using RNAsimple Total RNA Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China). RNA-sequencing libraries for 10 tissues (spleen, brain, gill, skin, muscle, liver, heart, intestine, kidney and fin) were constructed using the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit following the manufacturer’s protocol and also sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq × platform. The raw data were filterd by fastp (v 0.21.0)19 software with parameters of “-g -q 5 -u 20 -n 15 -l 150 -z 6 -D”.

Genome size estimation, assembly and quality assessment

The genome size of E. polyphekadion was estimated by Jellyfish (v2.2.7) based on k-mer of 1720. The estimated genome size was 1,106.95 Mb, the heterozygosity rate was approximately 0.39%, and the repeated content was 44.64%. The genome was assembled by SOAPdenovo221 with k-mer of 41. Subsequently, the draft genome was corrected and re-assembled using HiFi long reads by Hifiasm (v0.11) with the parameters “-t 30 -D 10”22. To further obtain the chromosome-level genome, we applied ALLHiC (v0.9.8)23 pipeline to link the mapped contigs to 24 pseudochromosomes. Eventually, the 1.1 Gb chromosome-level genome of E. polyphekadion was assembled, with a contig N50 length of 40.8 Mb (Fig. 1a), and 99.40% of scaffolds were mapped to 24 chromosomes (Fig. 1b).

Repetitive sequence and noncoding RNA annotation

For repeat annotation, repetitive sequences were detected using a combined approach of homology alignment and de novo searches. Homologous sequences comparison was conducted using RepeatMasker (v4.1.0) and RepeatProteinMask based on Repbase database25. And de novo candidate data base of repetitive elements were constructed by LTR (v1.0.6)26, RepeatScout (v1.0.5)27 and RepeatModeler (v2.0.1)28 software. Additionally, tandem repeat sequences were predicted using the software TRF (v4.09). In this study, a total of 469.31 Mb of repetitive sequences were identified, accounting for 42.67% of the genome assembly (Table 2).

Protein coding gene prediction and functional annotation

A combination of homology-based prediction, de novo prediction, and transcriptome-based prediction was used to predict the protein-coding genes within E. polyphekadion genome. First, protein sequences from other fish, including Epinephelus fuscoguttatus, Epinephelus lanceolatus, Epinephelus moara, Danio rerio, Gasterosteus aculeatus and Takifugu rubripes, were aligned against E. polyphekadion genome using Augustus (v3.2.3)31, Glimmer (v3.0.4)32 and Genscan (v1.0)33 software. Next, solar software (v0.9.6) was used to conjoin with BLAST (v2.2.26) to hit34. GeneWise (v2.4.1)35 then used to predict the exact gene structure of the corresponding genomic region on each BLAST hit. Additionally, high-quality RNA-seq data were assembled by Trinity (v2.11.0)36, and the sequences were aligned against the E. polyphekadion genome to assemble spliced alignment37. Furthermore, RNA-seq reads were mapped to the E. polyphekadion genome using Tophat (v2.0.13) and then assembled into gene models by Cufflinks (v2.1.1)38. Finally, a non-redundant and more complete gene set was integrated via EvidenceModeler (EVM) (v1.1.1) using various gene models. Furthermore, the predicted protein-coding genes in E. polyphekadion genome were annotated based on public databases including SwissProt39, NR database, InterPro40 and KEGG pathway41. A total of 25,165 protein-coding genes were predicted (Table 4), of which 97.9% were functionally annotated (Fig. 2b). The lengths of average transcript and CDS were 20,019.24 and 1,655.14 bp, respectively (Fig. 2a).

Data Records

Illumina, PacBio and Hi-C data for E. polyphekadion genome have been deposited under the Sequence Read Archive SRP32259442. The genome assembly was deposited at GenBank with the accession number GCA_041411175.143. Besides, the genome annotation44 file was available in the figshare database.

Technical Validation

Evaluation of the assembled genome

The completeness and accuracy of the E. polyphekadion genome assembly were assessed using Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO, v4.1.2) with the parameters “-l vertebrata_odb10 -m genome - i” and Core Eukaryotic Genes Mapping Approach (CEGMA, v2.5)45. In addition, the Merqury software was used to assess the genome quality with consensus quality value (QV) of 49.2082. The BUSCO results demonstrated that assembly contained 99.1% complete sequences, among which 3,285 were single-copy and 39 were duplicated. Moreover, CEGMA evaluation revealed that 93.55% (232/248) core eukaryotic genes (CEGs) were identified. Collectively, these results indicating that the genome assembly of E. polyphekadion has a good and high completeness.

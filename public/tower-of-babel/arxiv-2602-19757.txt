Explicit Construction of Spherical 5 5 - and 7 7 -Designs
Ryutaro Misawa

Abstract
. This paper develops an explicit and implementable framework for constructing spherical designs by lifting point sets from tight fusion frames. By combining existing ingredients, we obtain, in every dimension, explicit spherical 5 5 -designs with | X | = 𝒪 ⁡ ( d 3 ) |X|=\mathcal{O}(d^{3}) . As a core component of the method, we give an explicit construction of simplex 3 3 -designs realized as orbits of the symmetric group. Using these simplex designs as input, we further construct spherical 7 7 -designs in arbitrary even dimensions; more precisely, for every even integer d ≥ 6 d\geq 6 we obtain spherical 7 7 -designs in dimension d d , and if d 2 − 1 \frac{d}{2}-1 is a prime power then the number of points is 𝒪 ⁡ ( d 6 ) \mathcal{O}(d^{6}) .



1.
Introduction

Spherical
t
-designs are fundamental objects appearing in approximation theory, algebraic combinatorics, and related areas (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
6
]
).
A finite subset
X
of the unit sphere

S^{d-1}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid||x||=1\}

is called a (weighted) spherical
t
-design if, for every function of total degree at most
t
, the (weighted) average of its values on
X
coincides with the integral with respect to the normalised surface measure
\sigma
.
When all weights are equal, we simply call it a spherical
t
-design; when arbitrary positive real weights are allowed, we call it a weighted spherical
t
-design.

Spherical
t
-designs were introduced by Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel
[
10
]
.
Let
N(d,t)
denote the minimum cardinality of a spherical
t
-design on
S^{d-1}
.
Using linear programming, Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel
[
10
]
obtained the following lower bound.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Fix natural numbers
d,t
. Then
N(d,t)
satisfies

(1.1)

N(d,t)\geq\begin{cases}\binom{d+\frac{t}{2}-1}{d-1}+\binom{d+\frac{t}{2}-2}{d-1}&\text{if }t\ \text{is even},\\[5.69054pt]
2\binom{d+\frac{t-1}{2}-1}{d-1}&\text{if }t\ \text{is odd}.\end{cases}

A spherical
t
-design on
S^{d-1}
achieving equality in (
1.1
) is called a
tight
t
-design
.
On
S^{1}
, it is known that a regular
(t+1)
-gon is a tight
t
-design (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
).
However, for
S^{d-1}
with
d\geq 3
, tight
t
-designs are extremely rare.
Bannai–Damerell
[
7
,
8
]
showed that the only possible values of
t
for which tight spherical
t
-designs may exist are

t\in\{1,2,3,4,5,7,11\}
.
Moreover, classification is complete for
t=1,2,3,11
, whereas the cases
t=4,5,7
have remained open for a long time (See
[
6
]
).

If
t
is fixed and
d\to\infty
, then the bound (
1.1
) suggests that a tight
t
-design, if it exists, should satisfy

|X|=\mathcal{O}\bigl(d^{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}\bigr).

Here and throughout, for functions
f(d)
and
g(d)
we write
g(d)=\mathcal{O}(f(d))
as
d\to\infty
if there exist constants
C>0
and
d_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

|g(d)|\leq C\,|f(d)|\qquad(\forall\,d\geq d_{0}).

When
t
is fixed, the constants
C
and
d_{0}
may depend on
t
.
Therefore, a natural goal is to obtain an
explicit construction that is close to this order-optimal behavior
.

Problem 1.2
.

Fix
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Give an explicit construction of spherical
t
-designs
X_{d}\subset S^{d-1}
such that

|X_{d}|=\mathcal{O}(d^{\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\rfloor}).

Since the cases
t=1,2,3
are already well understood from the viewpoint of explicit constructions, we focus on the simplest remaining cases, namely
t=4,5,7
, and summarize the current progress on Problem
1.2
.
A point to keep in mind is that, depending on the literature, statements are often made while mixing spherical
t
-designs and
weighted
spherical
t
-designs.
In particular, in numerical analysis, weighted designs are also called cubature formulas of strength
t
,
and equal-weight designs are sometimes called Chebyshev-type cubature formulas

[
9
,
24
,
22
]
.
Below, we restrict attention to families that exist in infinitely many dimensions.

(1)

t=4
and
t=5
.
Weighted spherical designs have long been studied from the viewpoint of numerical analysis.
In particular, Stroud
[
24
]
constructed many weighted designs for small values of
t
.
As a general framework yielding explicit constructions with fewer points in higher dimensions,
Victoir
[
25
]
proposed a method using orthogonal arrays, producing weighted spherical
5
-designs with
\mathcal{O}(d^{2})
points.
For equal weights, Bajnok
[
3
]
showed that for sufficiently large
N
one can construct spherical
5
-designs on
S^{d-1}
with
N
points.
Baladram
[
5
]
also gave an inductive construction using low-dimensional examples, still of exponential order.
Furthermore, Mohammadpour–Waldron
[
19
]
showed that, assuming the Zauner conjecture, one can construct a spherical
5
-design with
6d^{2}
points in even dimensions
d
.
Levenshtein
[
16
]
constructed a spherical
4
-design with
d(d+1)
points for
d=4^{m}
.
Kuperberg
[
15
]
constructed spherical
5
-designs with
\mathcal{O}(d^{2})
points for
d=2^{m}
.

(2)

t=7
.
Kuperberg
[
14
]
gave an explicit construction of weighted spherical
7
-designs on
S^{d-1}
for all
d
, and stated that the number of points is
\mathcal{O}(d^{4})
.
On the other hand, compared with the cases
t=4,5
, there is much less general theory for constructing spherical
7
-designs.
Sidelnikov
[
23
]
constructed examples of exponential order for
d=2^{m}
.
Later, Kuperberg
[
14
]
showed that, in dimensions
d
for which a Hadamard matrix of order
d
exists, one can construct spherical
7
-designs with
\mathcal{O}(d^{6})
points.

In this paper, we use the following construction scheme, originating in König
[
13
]
and Kuperberg
[
15
]
, and further developed in Okuda
[
21
]
, Lindblad
[
17
]
, and Misawa
[
18
]
.
For integers
d>k
, we choose finitely many
k
-dimensional subspaces
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and place suitable designs on the unit spheres
S(V)\simeq S^{k-1}
contained in each
V
.
By lifting these configurations to a point set on the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we obtain spherical designs.
As our main results, within this framework we give explicit constructions primarily of spherical
5
-designs and spherical
7
-designs.
Moreover, by focusing on the case
(d,k)=(2d^{\prime},2)
(
d^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
), we combine several related analogues—simplex designs, projective toric designs, and interval designs—and present the constructions in an explicit and implementable form.

In particular, we describe the simplex design, which forms the core component of our method.
In design theory, simplex designs are analogues of spherical designs on a simplex, reformulated in a systematic manner by Baladram
[
4
]
.
For a positive integer
d
, define the standard simplex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by

\Delta^{d-1}:=\Bigl\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \Bigm|\ \sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}=1,\ \ x_{i}\geq 0\ (1\leq i\leq d)\Bigr\}.

A finite subset
X\subset\Delta^{d-1}
is called a simplex
t
-design if, for every function
f(x)=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
of total degree at most
t
, the average of
f
over
X
agrees with the integral of
f
with respect to the normalized
(d-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure in
\Delta^{d-1}
. One can pose problems for simplex designs analogous to those for spherical designs, but the construction theory for simplex designs is far less developed.
Indeed, for
t=1
, the gravity point of
\Delta^{d-1}
gives a simplex
1
-design, and for
t=2
, Baladram
[
4
]
constructed simplex
2
-designs with
d
points for every
d
.
However, for
t\geq 3
, no explicit constructions are known in general.
In this paper, we construct simplex
3
-designs using the natural action of the symmetric group of degree
d
, denoted
S_{d}
.

We summarize the contributions of this paper as follows.

Contributions

(1)

Toward Problem
1.2
, by combining known constructions we give, for
t=4
and
t=5
and for
every

d
, explicit spherical
t
-designs with
|X|=\mathcal{O}(d^{3})
.

(2)

For
every

d
, We give an explicit construction of simplex
3
-designs.

(3)

For
t=7
, we provide constructions of spherical
7
-designs in arbitrary even dimensions; in particular, for
d=2q
with
q
a prime power, we obtain spherical
7
-designs on
S^{d-1}
with
|X|=\mathcal{O}(d^{6})
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we introduce the designs used in our constructions.
In Section 3 we construct spherical
5
-designs.
In Section 4 we construct simplex
3
-designs as orbits of the symmetric group of degree
d
, and then use them to construct spherical
7
-designs.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the notions of designs needed for our constructions and recall basic properties when necessary.
We begin with spherical designs, allowing positive weights. Let
\sigma
be the normalized surface measure on
S^{d-1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
t,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
A pair
(X,\lambda)
consisting of a finite set
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset S^{d-1}
and positive weights

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}>0
is called a
weighted spherical
t
-design
if for every function

f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
of total degree at most
t
,

\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x)\,d\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,f(x_{j}),

holds, where
\Lambda:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}
.

Remark 2.2
.

In Definition
2.1
,

(i)

If all weights are equal (i.e.,
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}
), then
(X,\lambda)
is a
spherical
t
-design
.

(ii)

Allowing general positive real weights
\lambda_{j}>0
leads to the notion of a weighted spherical
t
-design.

Below, as analogues of spherical designs, we define several related notions: interval designs, simplex designs, projective toric designs, and complex projective designs.

2.1.
Interval
t
-design

Definition 2.3

(
[
20
, Sec. 1]
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and set
w_{d}(x):=(1-x^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
on
[-1,1]
.
A finite set
X\subset[-1,1]
is an
interval
t
-design with respect to
w_{d}
if for every polynomial
f
with

\deg f\leq t
,

\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}}\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\,w_{d}(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}f(x),\qquad\alpha_{d}:=\int_{-1}^{1}w_{d}(x)\,dx.

Using the change of variables
u^{2}=t
in the definition of the beta function
B(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,dt
, we obtain

\int_{-1}^{1}u^{2m}(1-u^{2})^{d-1}\,du=B\!\left(m+\tfrac{1}{2},d\right)\qquad(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}).

Hence

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{2d}}\int_{-1}^{1}u^{2}(1-u^{2})^{d-1}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{B(\tfrac{3}{2},d)}{B(\tfrac{1}{2},d)}=\frac{1}{2d+1},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{2d}}\int_{-1}^{1}u^{4}(1-u^{2})^{d-1}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{B(\tfrac{5}{2},d)}{B(\tfrac{1}{2},d)}=\frac{3}{(2d+1)(2d+3)}.

2.2.
Simplex
t
-design

Let
\rho
be the normalized
(d-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure in
\Delta^{d-1}
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
4
, Eq. (1.1)]
)
.

A finite set
X\subset\Delta^{d-1}
is called a
simplex
t
-design
if

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}f(x)\,d\rho(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}f(x)

holds for every polynomial
f(x)=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
of total degree at most
t
.

The multivariate beta function is defined by

B(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}):=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}{\Gamma\!\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\Bigr)},\qquad(\alpha_{i}>0),

where the gamma function
\Gamma
is given by

\Gamma(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}\,dt,\qquad(\alpha>0).

By linearity, it suffices to verify Definition
2.4
for monomials

x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{k_{d}}
with
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}\leq t
.
Moreover, the integral representation of
B
over the simplex
(see
[
4
, Eq. (2.2)]
) yields the following moment formula.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
4
, Eq. (2.2) & Eq. (2.3)]
)
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Then for any nonnegative integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}
,

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots x_{d}^{k_{d}}\,d\rho(x)=(d-1)!\,B(k_{1}+1,\dots,k_{d}+1).

Corollary 2.6
.

For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}p_{2}(x)\,d\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{d+1},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}p_{3}(x)\,d\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{(d+1)(d+2)}.

Proof.

Since
p_{2}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}
and
\rho
is symmetric under coordinate permutations,

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}p_{2}(x)\,d\rho(x)=d\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}x_{1}^{2}\,d\rho(x).

Applying Proposition
2.5
with
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{d})=(2,0,\dots,0)
, we get

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}x_{1}^{2}\,d\rho(x)=(d-1)!\,B(3,1,\dots,1)=(d-1)!\,\frac{\Gamma(3)\Gamma(1)^{d-1}}{\Gamma(d+2)}=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}.

Hence (
2.3
) follows.

Similarly, since
p_{3}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{3}
,

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}p_{3}(x)\,d\rho(x)=d\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}x_{1}^{3}\,d\rho(x).

Applying Proposition
2.5
with
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{d})=(3,0,\dots,0)
, we get

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}x_{1}^{3}\,d\rho(x)=(d-1)!\,B(4,1,\dots,1)=(d-1)!\,\frac{\Gamma(4)\Gamma(1)^{d-1}}{\Gamma(d+3)}=\frac{6}{d(d+1)(d+2)}.

Therefore (
2.4
) holds.
∎

2.3.
Projective toric designs

In this subsection, following
[
12
, Sec. 2, Def. 2.4–2.5]
,
we define designs on the projective torus.
Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and consider the
d
-dimensional torus

{\mathrm{T}}^{d}:=({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}.

Let

{\mathrm{T}}:=\{\,(\theta,\dots,\theta)\in{\mathrm{T}}^{d}\mid\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\,\}

be the diagonal subgroup, and call the quotient

P({\mathrm{T}}^{d}):={\mathrm{T}}^{d}/{\mathrm{T}}

the
projective torus
.
Let
\mu
be the normalized Haar measure on
P({\mathrm{T}}^{d})
.
Next, we define monomials on the projective torus.
Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and set
I_{d}:=\{1,\dots,d\}
.
For
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s}),\,b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{s})\in I_{d}^{s}
and
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d})\in{\mathrm{T}}^{d}
, define

m_{a,b}(\varphi):=\exp\!\Bigl(i\sum_{k=1}^{s}(\varphi_{a_{k}}-\varphi_{b_{k}})\Bigr).

This function is invariant under diagonal translations
\varphi\mapsto\varphi+(\theta,\dots,\theta)
, and hence it is well-defined as a function on
P({\mathrm{T}}^{d})
(denoted by the same symbol
m_{a,b}
).

We then define the following function spaces:

\mathrm{Pol}_{t}\bigl(P({\mathrm{T}}^{d})\bigr):=\operatorname{span}\{\,m_{a,b}\mid 0\leq s\leq t,\ a,b\in I_{d}^{s}\,\}.

Definition 2.7

(
[
12
, Def. 2.5]
)
.

Let
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
A finite subset
X\subset P({\mathrm{T}}^{d})
is called a
projective toric
t
-design

if for every
f\in\mathrm{Pol}_{t}\bigl(P({\mathrm{T}}^{d})\bigr)
,

\int_{P({\mathrm{T}}^{d})}f(x)\,d\mu(x)=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}f(x)

holds.

Moreover, Iosue et al.
[
12
]
explicitly constructed projective toric
t
-designs with
\mathcal{O}(d^{t})
points for every
d
when
t
is fixed:

Proposition 2.8

(
[
12
, Sec. 3.1]
)
.

Fix
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(1)

If
d-1
is a prime power, then there exists a projective toric
t
-design

X\subset P(\mathrm{T}^{d})
that is isomorphic to the cyclic group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{|X|}
, whose order is given by

|X|=\frac{(d-1)^{t+1}-1}{d-2}=1+(d-1)+\cdots+(d-1)^{t}.

(2)

For a general
d
, let
m\geq d
be the smallest integer such that
m-1
is a prime power.
Then there exists a projective toric
t
-design
X\subset P(\mathrm{T}^{d})
satisfying

|X|=\frac{(m-1)^{t+1}-1}{m-2}.

In particular, if
t
is fixed, then
|X|=\mathcal{O}(d^{t})
.

2.4.
Complex projective
t
-design

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let

\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}:=\{V\leq{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\mid\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}V=1\}

be the complex projective space endowed with the normalized Fubini–Study measure
\mu_{\mathrm{FS}}
.
For each
k\geq 0
, set

\mathrm{Hom}_{k,k}({\mathbb{C}}^{d}):=\Bigl\{\,P(z,\overline{z})\in{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\dots,z_{d},\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{d}]\ \Bigm|\ \deg_{z}P=\deg_{\overline{z}}P=k\Bigr\},

the space of bihomogeneous polynomials of bidegree
(k,k)
.
For
P\in\mathrm{Hom}_{k,k}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})
and
[x]\in\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}
, choose a unit representative
x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and define

P([x]):=P(x,\overline{x}).

This value is independent of the choice of a unit representative, hence well-defined.
Define

\mathrm{Pol}_{t}(\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}):=\operatorname{span}\Bigl\{\,P|_{\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}}\ \Bigm|\ P\in\mathrm{Hom}_{k,k}({\mathbb{C}}^{d}),\ 0\leq k\leq t\Bigr\}.

Definition 2.9

(
[
11
]
)
.

A finite set
X\subset\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}
is called a
complex projective
t
-design
if for every
f\in\mathrm{Pol}_{t}(\mathbb{CP}^{d-1})
,

\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}}f(x)\,d\mu_{\mathrm{FS}}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}f(x)

holds.

Moreover, Kuperberg
[
15
]
and Iosue et al.
[
12
]
showed that a
t
-design on
\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}

can be obtained from a simplex
t
-design and a projective toric
t
-design:

Proposition 2.10

(
[
12
, Sec. 4.1]
)
.

Let
Y
be a
t
-design on the simplex
\Delta^{d-1}
, and let
X
be a
t
-design on the projective torus
P(\mathrm{T}^{d})
.
Consider the map

\pi:\ \Delta^{d-1}\times P(\mathrm{T}^{d})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{d-1},\qquad(p,[\varphi])\longmapsto\Bigl[\,\sum_{n=1}^{d}\sqrt{p_{n}}\,e^{i\varphi_{n}}|n\rangle\,\Bigr].

Then

Z:=\pi(Y\times X)

is a
t
-design on
\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}
.
Here
\{|n\rangle\}_{n=1}^{d}
denotes the standard orthonormal basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
.

2.5.
Tight
t
-fusion frames

Finally, we define tight
t
-fusion frames, which serve as inputs to the lifting construction, and state the corresponding lifting theorem.
Let
d,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
1\leq k\leq d
, and let

G_{k,d}:=\{\,V\leq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid\dim V=k\,\}

denote the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Definition 2.11
.

[
2
, Def. 4.1]

Let
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset G_{k,d}
be a finite set and let
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be positive weights.
For each
j
, let
P_{V_{j}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
V_{j}
.
If there exist constants
A,B>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

A\,\|x\|^{2t}\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{j}\,\|P_{V_{j}}x\|^{2t}\ \leq\ B\,\|x\|^{2t},

then
\{(V_{j},\omega_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
is called a
t
-fusion frame
.
If, in particular,
A=B
, then it is called a
weighted tight
t
-fusion frame
.
In the equal-weight case, we call
\{(V_{j},\omega_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
a
tight
t
-fusion frame
(
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
) and often omit the weights, simply writing
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
.

We can obtain a spherical
(2t+1)
-design on
S^{d-1}
from a tight
t
-fusion frame on
G_{k,d}
and a spherical
(2t+1)
-design on
S^{k-1}
.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
18
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

Let
t,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset G_{k,d}
be a finite set with positive weights
\{\omega_{V}\}_{V\in\mathcal{F}}
, and assume that

(\mathcal{F},\{\omega_{V}\})
is a weighted tight
t
-fusion frame.
For each
V\in\mathcal{F}
, let
(Y_{V},\{\lambda_{V,z}\}_{z\in Y_{V}})
be a weighted spherical
s
-design on
S(V)
, and assume that

\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}

is independent of
V
.
Define a weighted finite set
(Z,w)
on
S^{d-1}
as follows:

Z:=\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{F}}Y_{V}\subset S^{d-1},\qquad w(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\mathcal{F}\\
z\in Y_{V}\end{subarray}}\omega_{V}\,\lambda_{V,z}\quad(z\in Z).

Then
(Z,w)
is a weighted spherical
r
-design on
S^{d-1}
with

r=\min\{s,\,2t+1\}.

In particular, in the equal-weight case we obtain the following statement.

Corollary 2.13

(

For each non-negative integer

It is well known that every symmetric polynomial in

can be expressed as a polynomial in

.

The following lemma reduces the verification of the simplex
3
-design property for

-invariant sets to checking only

and

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that a finite set

is
S_{d}
-invariant.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is a simplex
3
-design.

(2)

\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}p_{2}(x)=\frac{2}{d+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}p_{3}(x)=\frac{6}{(d+1)(d+2)}.

Proof.

Assume (1).
Applying Definition
2.4
with
t=3
to
f=p_{2}
and
f=p_{3}
, and using
(
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we obtain (2).
Thus it remains to prove (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).
For any polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
of degree
\leq 3
, define

f^{\mathrm{sym}}(x):=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}f(\sigma x).

Since
X
is
S_{d}
-invariant, we have

\sum_{x\in X}f(x)=\sum_{x\in X}f^{\mathrm{sym}}(x).

Moreover, the right-hand side of Proposition
2.5
is invariant under permutations of
(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
.
Hence, for any

and any polynomial

\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}f(\sigma x)\,d\rho=\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}f(x)\,d\rho,

Consequently, to prove that

is a simplex

-design, it suffices to show that for every symmetric polynomial

,

\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}g(x)=\int_{\Delta^{d-1}}g\,d\rho.

If

3
-design.

(iii)

4.1.
Thinning by a
3
-transitive subgroup

Let

\Omega_{k}:=\Bigl\{\,(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\in I_{d}^{k}\ \Bigm|\ i_{r}\neq i_{s}\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq r<s\leq k\Bigr\}.

Then

|\Omega_{k}|=(d)_{k}:=d(d-1)\cdots(d-k+1).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
and
G\leq S_{d}
be
k
-transitive on
I_{d}
.
Then for any function
\Phi:\Omega_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and any
\mathbf{i}\in\Omega_{k}
,

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\Phi(g^{-1}\mathbf{i})=\frac{1}{(d)_{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\Omega_{k}}\Phi(\mathbf{j}).

Proof.

Immediate.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
d\geq 3
.
Assume that
x\in\Delta^{d-1}
and the
S_{d}
-orbit
X:=S_{d}\cdot x
is a simplex
3
-design.
Let
G\leq S_{d}
be a
3
-transitive subgroup on
I_{d}
and set
Y:=G\cdot x
.
Then
Y
is a simplex
3
-design.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}]
of degree
\leq 3
,

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}f(g\cdot y)=\frac{1}{|S_{d}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}f(\sigma\cdot y).

By linearity, we may assume that
f=m
is a monomial of degree
\leq 3
.
Then

m(y)=\prod_{t=1}^{k}y_{i_{t}}^{a_{t}},\quad\text{for some }k\in I_{3},\ \mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\in\Omega_{k},\ a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\text{ with }\sum_{t=1}^{k}a_{t}\leq 3.

Define
\Phi:\Omega_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi(j_{1},\dots,j_{k}):=\prod_{t=1}^{k}x_{j_{t}}^{a_{t}}.

For
g\in S_{d}
, since
(g\cdot x)_{i_{t}}=x_{g^{-1}(i_{t})}
, we have
m(g\cdot x)=\Phi(g^{-1}\mathbf{i})
.
As
G
is
3
-transitive and
k\leq 3
, it is
k
-transitive, hence Lemma
4.5
gives

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}m(g\cdot x)=\frac{1}{(d)_{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\Omega_{k}}\Phi(\mathbf{j}).

Similarly, applying Lemma
4.5
to
S_{d}
which is also
k
-transitive, we obtain the same right-hand side, and hence

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}m(g\cdot x)=\frac{1}{|S_{d}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}m(\sigma\cdot x).

This proves the desired identity for all
f
of degree
\leq 3
, and hence
Y
is a simplex
3
-design.
∎

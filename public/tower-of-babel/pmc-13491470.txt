Silicon-Based Highly c‑Axis-Oriented Strontium Barium Niobate Films with Large Pockels Coefficients
Authors: Jiangfeng Yuan, Jun Zheng, Sen Liang, Peng Rui, Hui Yu, Hui Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To develop a new generation of high-performance silicon-based electro-optic modulators, it is necessary to find an electro-optic crystal with a simple fabrication process, stable chemical properties, and large Pockels coefficients, such as strontium barium niobate (Sr x Ba1–x Nb2O6, SBN). In this work, potassium-doped Sr0.75Ba0.25Nb2O6 and sodium-doped Sr0.75Ba0.25Nb2O6 buffers were fabricated on SiO2 via the sol–gel method, followed by the deposition of Sr0.75Ba0.25Nb2O6 (SBN75) films using radio frequency magnetron sputtering (RFMS). This sequential processing enabled the realization of SBN75 films exhibiting a c-axis preferential orientation. Lattice mismatch is not a limiting factor in this approach. Furthermore, the extracted Pockels coefficient r 33 of highly c-axis-oriented SBN75 films reached a maximum value of 431.3 pm/V, which is 10 times larger than that in lithium niobate (LN). The aforementioned findings make SBN75 a promising candidate for compact and high-speed electro-optic modulators.

Introduction

Owing to the centrosymmetric structure of silicon crystal, conventional silicon-based electro-optic modulators cannot exploit the linear electro-optic effect and instead rely primarily on the Kerr effect, thermo-optic effect, and plasma dispersion effect for modulation. , Among these mechanisms, the change in refractive index caused by the Kerr effect is comparatively modest, while the thermo-optic effect suffers from an intrinsically slow thermal response. , In contrast, the plasma dispersion effect offers a superior modulation efficiency and bandwidth. However, it inevitably introduces additional optical absorption loss due to the change in carrier concentration, , rendering it less attractive for low-loss photonic integrated circuits.

To date, lithium niobate (LN) remains the most extensively studied electro-optic material for integrated modulators − while it also has outstanding ferroelectric, , piezoelectric, , and nonlinear optical , properties. Its well-established commercial production of high-quality single crystals constitutes a decisive advantage over other electro-optic crystals. In 2018, a research group at Harvard University first demonstrated a Mach–Zehnder electro-optic modulator fabricated on an LN-on-insulator (LNOI) platform. The device operated at voltages (V π L = 2.8 V·cm) compatible with complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) technology, supported data rates up to 210 Gb/s, and exhibited on-chip optical propagation loss below 0.5 dB. Nevertheless, LN’s relatively low Pockels coefficients fundamentally constrain further miniaturization of LN-based modulators (the largest Pockels coefficient in LN is r 33 = 31 pm/V ).

In addition to LN, some ferroelectric crystals have attracted researchers’ attention, showing great potential in the field of high-speed electro-optic modulation in recent years. − For example, the theoretical value of r 51 for barium titanate (BTO) crystal with a perovskite structure can reach more than 900 pm/V, − which means that electro-optical modulators based on BTO may achieve lower half-wave voltage or a more compact footprint compared with LN-based devices. , A key advantage of BTO is its ability to achieve high-quality epitaxial growth using a few-nanometer-thick strontium titanate (STO) buffer on SiO2 without wafer bonding. , This BTO-on-insulator technique is currently being used commercially. Furthermore, significant progress has been realized in the performance of BTO-based electro-optic modulators. From 2023 to 2025, , a Swiss research team was dedicated to propose and develop a novel high-potential plasmonic BTO-on-SiN platform for next-generation high-speed, low-loss modulators. The resulting modulator showed >100 GHz electro-optical bandwidths and achieved symbol rates of up to 256 GBd. Lead zirconate titanate (PZT) also has demonstrated significant advances in electro-optic modulation technology. , In 2015, highly textured PZT films were fabricated by spin-coating lanthanide-based films as buffers on Si substrates using the chemical solution deposition (CSD) process. The PZT films showed strong (100) preferential orientation and had large effective Pockels coefficients ranging from 110 to 240 pm/V with different crystallinity, which makes them an ideal candidate for silicon-based active integrated nanophotonic devices. However, the application of the CSD process to the PZT functional layers poses challenges for precise control of the structure size of devices. And excessive repetition of spin-coating cycles can accumulate process-induced errors and exacerbate structural defects, resulting in elevated devices’ optical loss. Beyond ferroelectric crystals, certain organic polymers and epsilon-near-zero (ENZ) materials have also emerged as new candidates for electro-optic modulation. , However, the former lacks sufficient chemical stability, and the latter incurs substantial optical propagation loss near the operating wavelength. Both of them have shortcomings that are difficult to address in the short term.

Considering the problems mentioned above, it is necessary to find a new type of electro-optic crystal with a simple fabrication process, stable chemical properties, and large Pockels coefficients. Strontium barium niobate (Sr x Ba1–x Nb2O6, SBN) crystals are multifunctional materials that exhibit strong electro-optic, nonlinear optical, and ferroelectric properties, along with pronounced photorefractive and pyroelectric responses. − The Pockels coefficient r 33 of single-crystal Sr0.75Ba0.25Nb2O6 (SBN75) can reach about 1350 pm/V in theory. However, achieving large-area, high-quality epitaxial or preferentially oriented silicon-based SBN75 films remains highly challenging, which has once hindered the development of SBN75-based devices.

In our prior study, template layers synthesized via the sol–gel method, using the potassium-doped SBN precursor solution, were found to promote c-axis-oriented growth of subsequently deposited SBN films. The highest crystallinity in the SBN films was achieved when the K+/Nb5+ molar ratio in the solution was 2:3. However, that work did not systematically investigate the role of such template layers as buffers for preferred orientation control of SBN75 films. In this paper, a series of buffers were fabricated on SiO2 using a sol–gel method with an alkali metal ion-doped (K+ and Na+) SBN75 precursor solution. Subsequent radio frequency magnetron sputtering (RFMS) deposition of SBN75 films on these buffers yielded SBN75 exhibiting a strong c-axis preferential orientation. Given that SiO2 is a widely used cladding material in optoelectronic device fabrication, the substantial refractive index contrast (>0.5) between SBN75 and SiO2 facilitates the monolithic integration of SBN75 on insulator (SBNOI) photonic platforms, thereby eliminating the need for wafer bonding, analogous to the aforementioned BTO-based approaches. Additionally, the Pockels coefficient r 33 of highly crystalline SBN75 films was experimentally determined, reaching a maximum value of 431.3 pm/V, which is 10 times larger than that in LN. The aforementioned findings make SBN75 a promising candidate for applications in the field of electro-optic modulation.

Results and Discussion

The Effect of Buffers on the Growth of SBN75 Films

Our prior work demonstrated that when the K+/Nb5+ molar ratio in the potassium-doped SBN precursor solution was 2:3, the buffer yielded the most favorable outcomes in terms of the crystallinity of SBN films on Si substrates. (The potassium-doped SBN75 buffer with the K+/Nb5+ molar ratio of 2:3 is denoted KSBN.)

Similarly, Na+ doping (chemically analogous to K+ doping due to the similar group valence configuration) was also implemented and yielded comparable enhancement in c-axis preferred orientation and crystallinity of SBN75 films. Sodium-doped SBN75 buffers using the corresponding precursor solution with different Na+/Nb5+ molar ratios spanning from 2:1 to 1:5 were fabricated on Si substrates. Then, SBN75 films were deposited on the buffered Si substrates and an unbuffered Si substrate.

XRD patterns in Figure S1 reveal that all of the above SBN75 films, regardless of whether a buffer is employed, exhibit polycrystalline microstructures. Notably, when the Na+/Nb5+ molar ratio is varied between 1:2 and 1:5, the SBN75 films display pronounced (001) and (002) preferred orientations, as evidenced by enhanced diffraction intensity and reduced full width at half-maximum (fwhm) values in Figure S1h,i. A systematic comparison of the XRD data across all samples further indicates that the SBN75 film synthesized at the Na+/Nb5+ molar ratio of 1:4 achieves the highest crystallinity. (The sodium-doped SBN75 buffer with the Na+/Nb5+ molar ratio of 1:4 is denoted as NaSBN.)

Building upon the above findings, the experimental scope was extended to quantitatively assess the buffers’ promoting effect on c-axis preferred orientation in SBN75 films. Three representative samples with multilayer stacksMgO/SBN75, Si/KSBN/SBN75 (sample 1 in Table ) and Si/NaSBN/SBN75were fabricated for this purpose. The MgO substrate was selected owing to the minimal lattice mismatch (<1.2%) between SBN and MgO, which enables epitaxial growth (described as (100) SBN // (310) MgO) of SBN75 films. (The growth conditions of SBN75 films on MgO and Si substrates have been optimized separately in prior studies. Nevertheless, the optimal growth conditions on both substrates are identical under the limitations of the current sputtering equipment.)

A further noteworthy point is that, relative to the NaSBN buffer, the SBN75 film deposited on the KSBN buffer has superior crystallinity due to its enhanced diffraction intensity, reduced fwhm values of the (001) and (002) peaks, and fewer other orientation diffraction peaks.

To elucidate the reasons for the different preferred orientation behaviors observed in SBN75 films grown on KSBN buffers versus NaSBN buffers, samples comprising each buffer deposited on Si substrates were fabricated for interfacial characterization. Secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) and cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were employed to probe elemental distribution and structural evolution in the depth direction. To facilitate the observation of the structural characteristics of the samples, the thicknesses of the KSBN and NaSBN buffers were appropriately increased.

As shown by the SIMS depth profiles and the corresponding TEM images in Figure a,b,d,e, regions A and C with a stable elemental composition in the depth direction correspond to the SBN75 layer and the Si substrate, respectively. Region B contains all of the six ionic species. The concentration of K+ or Na+ exhibits an initial increase, followed by a subsequent decrease, whereas the Si4+ content gradually rises. This ionic distribution profile correlates with the transition from the KSBN or NaSBN buffer to the native and thermally grown SiO2 layers formed on the Si surface. Notably, SIMS depth profiling reveals limited diffusion of K+ and Na+ in both the SBN75 film and the SiO2 layer. A comparison of SIMS analyses in Figure a,d suggests that a more pronounced Na+ signal within both the SBN75 layer and the SiO2 layer is observed relative to K+. This enhanced distribution of Na+ is consistent with its higher diffusion coefficient in SiO2 and its smaller ionic radius. If the introduction of doped ions indeed promotes the c-axis-oriented growth of SBN75 films, then the lower Na+ doping concentration required to achieve comparable (001) and (002) preferential orientations, relative to K+, may be attributed to the stronger diffusivity.

The Key Factors in the Mechanism of the Buffers Promoting the Growth of SBN75 Films

The aforementioned experiments have confirmed that the buffers promote c-axis-oriented growth of SBN75 films. However, the key factors responsible for this outcome remain unclear. For this reason, we designed two controlled experiments targeting critical variables in the fabrication process of the buffers, thereby enabling systematic identification of the influential factors. Control group 1 consists of samples 1 and 2 with corresponding multilayer stacks Si/KSBN/SBN75 and Si/SBN75. The SBN75 film deposited directly on the Si substrate (sample 2) exhibits poor crystallinity with very low diffraction intensity and an increased fwhm value of the (001) peak, relative to that grown on the buffered Si substrate (sample 1). It is clear that potassium ions enhance the structural ordering of the buffer, thereby promoting favorable in situ growth of the SBN75 film.

SiO2 Layer Contributes to the Preferential c-Axis-Oriented Growth of SBN75 Film

Previous studies have predominantly focused on the intrinsic properties of buffers. However, the potential influences of extrinsic factors on SBN75 growth remain underexplored. Considering that the Si substrate has native and thermally grown SiO2 thin layers on the surface, the buffer of sample 1 is essentially fabricated on SiO2. So the presence or absence of the SiO2 layer may also modulate the c-axis preferential orientation of the SBN75 film.

To test the validity of the aforementioned hypothesis, samples 3, 4, and 5 in control group 2 with multilayer stacks Si/TiN/SBN75, Si/TiN/KSBN/SBN75, and Si/TiN/SiO2/KSBN/SBN75 were fabricated. Titanium nitride (TiN) is employed to inhibit interdiffusion between the substrate and the overlying film. TiN, SiO2, and KSBN layers form the composite buffers in samples 4 and 5. Among the three samples, the SBN75 film grown directly on TiN (sample 3) exhibits the lowest diffraction intensity and the largest fwhm value of the (001) peak, indicating the poorest crystalline quality. The introduction of the KSBN buffer (sample 4) leads to a moderate improvement in the crystallinity of the SBN75 film. However, the resulting SBN75 film still falls significantly short of highly c-axis-oriented crystallization. When a thin SiO2 layer was deposited on the TiN film prior to buffer formation (sample 5), the sputtered SBN75 film demonstrates a marked enhancement in crystallinity, as evidenced by the highest diffraction intensity and the narrowest fwhm value of the (001) peak. These observations collectively indicate that the presence of the SiO2 layer is essential for achieving c-axis-oriented growth of the SBN75 film.

In addition to the two control groups, we deposited an SBN75 film (sample 6) on a KSBN-buffered silica substrate. On the basis of previous findings, this sample yielded the SBN75 film exhibiting strong c-axis orientation as expected. Fused silica offers both a substantial refractive index contrast relative to that of SBN75 and utility as an optical cladding material. The successful realization of a highly c-axis-oriented SBN75 film on a silica substrate means that SBN75-based waveguide-type integrated electro-optic modulators employing SiO2 as the lower cladding layer offer advantages for future device design and fabrication.

It is noteworthy that the same experiments were undertaken to identify the key factors within the NaSBN buffer promoting the c-axis preferred orientation of the SBN75 film. Those studies yielded conclusions similar to those derived from KSBN-based analyses.

It has been established that alkali metal ion doping and the presence of the SiO2 layer both contribute to c-axis-oriented crystallization for the SBN75 film. Notably, c-axis alignment exhibits a distinct sensitivity to doped ions (optimal orientation is achieved only within a specific compositional window) and the SiO2 layer (presence is sufficient). The growth mechanism remains incompletely understood. Plausible contributing factors include thermal migration and diffusion of doped ions as well as interfacial reactions between silicon and oxygen at the Si/SiO2 interface. A comprehensive mechanistic understanding will require the integration of first-principles calculations with more controlled variable experimental investigations in future studies.

The extracted Pockels coefficients of r 13 and r 33 of the SBN75 film are 22.6 pm/V and 431.3 pm/V for the KSBN buffer and 19.1 pm/V and 387.1 pm/V for the NaSBN buffer. Furthermore, the measured r 33 value of 431.3 pm/V for silicon-based SBN75 films underscores its great application potential for integrated electro-optic modulators. Provided that SBN75 films exhibit c-axis-oriented growth, TiN or heavily doped Si is viable as the bottom electrode for SBN75-based waveguides. This multilayer stack is supported by prior experimental results. c-Axis preferential orientation of SBN75 films on SiO2 yields a high index contrast of >0.5, thereby enabling tight optical mode confinement and direct monolithic integration with the SBNOI photonic platforms. Applying the driving voltage in the c-axis direction facilitates full utilization of the largest electro-optic coefficient of r 33, leading to enhanced modulation efficiency or a reduced device footprint. Building upon the aforementioned design principle, we aim to develop a Mach–Zehnder interferometer and present preliminary simulation results that validate its operational feasibility. Conclusions

In this study, the sol–gel method was employed to synthesize KSBN and NaSBN buffers. Using these newly developed buffers, we successfully achieved the magnetron sputtering deposition of highly c-axis-oriented SBN75 films on Si and silica substrates without considering the issue of lattice mismatch. In particular, the c-axis preferential orientation of SBN75 films on SiO2 is critical for enabling the in situ growth of SBN75 that satisfies the waveguide refractive index matching requirement and for facilitating the monolithic integration of SBNOI photonic platforms. Furthermore, the extracted Pockels coefficients r 13 and r 33 of the SBN75 films are 22.6 pm/V and 431.3 pm/V for the KSBN buffer and 19.1 pm/V and 387.1 pm/V for the NaSBN buffer. These values substantially exceed the corresponding Pockels coefficients of LN and demonstrate the feasibility of SBN75 films for developing a new generation of compact, high-speed electro-optic modulators.

Experimental Section

Fabrication of the Buffers

The sol–gel method was applied to fabricate KSBN or NaSBN buffer, owing to its capacity to yield a thin film with excellent compositional stability and tunability. The buffer is sufficiently thin (<30 nm) such that only one or two spin-coating cycles are required to fabricate a high-quality film.

KSBN and NaSBN buffers were synthesized on different substrates via the same sol–gel process. Under a dry argon atmosphere, Sr metal, Ba metal, Niobium Ethoxide, and Potassium (or Sodium) Hydroxide were reacted with excess 2-methoxyethanol at 120 °C, respectively. Following the complete reaction, Sr, Ba, Nb, and K (or Na) alkoxides were obtained through fractional distillation at 160 °C. The mixture of four kinds of metal alkoxides in a certain ratio at 85 °C formed the KSBN (or NaSBN) precursor solution. The molarity of the solution was 0.05 M. Then the precursor solution was spin-coated on different substrates (at 500 rpm for 6 s and 3000 rpm for 30 s) and heat-treated in a rapid thermal annealing chamber (RTP) with a two-step heating process (ramping rate of 6 °C/s, 350 °C for 3 min, 1000 °C for 1 min) to crystallize the KSBN (or NaSBN) film. The procedure was repeated until the desired thickness was obtained. (The schematic process flow for buffer fabrication is presented in Figure S6.)

Deposition of the SBN75 Films

The SBN75 films were deposited by RF magnetron sputtering using an SBN75 target. This approach facilitates the fabrication of a thicker SBN75 layer while maintaining compositional homogeneity.

The molar ratio of target’s raw materials was SrO:BaO:Nb2O5 = 3:1:4. The sputtering system mainly includes magnetron sputtering sources (Kurt J. Lesker), an RF power supply (CESAR Power Generator, Advanced Energy), and a thin-film deposition monitor (SQM-160, Inficon). Prior to deposition, the pressure of the magnetron sputtering growth chamber was pumped below 4 × 10–4 Pa. During deposition, we fixed the substrate temperature, RF sputtering power, and working pressure at 600 °C, 300 W, and 0.2 Pa, respectively, with the argon and oxygen flow rates of 35 and 10 sccm.

Deposition of the TiN and SiO2 Films

The TiN and SiO2 films shown in Figure c were deposited by RF-reactive magnetron sputtering using Ti and Si targets, respectively. This approach enables high-efficiency and high-quality film formation.

Prior to deposition, the pressure of the magnetron sputtering growth chamber was pumped below 4 × 10–4 Pa. For TiN film growth, the substrate temperature, RF sputtering power, and working pressure were maintained at 600 °C, 200 W, and 0.3 Pa, respectively, with a nitrogen flow rate of 20 sccm. For SiO2 film growth, the substrate temperature, RF sputtering power, and working pressure were maintained at 300 °C, 100 W, and 0.4 Pa, respectively, with argon and oxygen flow rates of 40 and 4 sccm.

Material Characterization

The crystallinity of the films was analyzed by an X-ray Diffractometer (X-pert Powder) with CuKα radiation. (The diffractograms were recorded for 2θ angles between 20° and 60°, with a step size of 0.02° and a time per step of 36.5 s.) The fwhm values were obtained from a coupled 2θ scan. The TEM slices of specimens were prepared using a Focused Ion Beam System (Scios 2 HiVac). TEM analyses were performed using a Spherical Aberration-Corrected Electron Microscope (HF5000). The elemental distribution of the films was obtained using a Time-of-Flight Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometer (TOF-SIMS V).

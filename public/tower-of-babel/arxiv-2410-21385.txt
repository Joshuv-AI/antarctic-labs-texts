Thermal Gravitational Waves During Reheating
Nicolás Bernal, and Yong Xu

Abstract
In this work, we revisit the generation of stochastic gravitational waves (GWs) from interactions in the thermal plasma. We extend the existing literature by incorporating the reheating phase into the thermal history. Our results show that the amplitude of the GW spectrum can be significantly enhanced because the temperature during reheating can be much higher than the reheating temperature. Furthermore, since the temperature evolution during reheating differs from that of free radiation, the peak frequency of the spectrum can also shift. Additionally, the morphology of the spectrum can present characteristic features. We also compute the contribution of the integrated GW spectrum to the effective number of neutrino species, Δ ​ N eff \Delta N_{\text{eff}} , which can be substantially larger.

The Set-up

We begin with the evolution of the GW energy density
\rho_{\text{GW}}
, which is governed by the Boltzmann equation

\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{dt}+4\,H\,\rho_{\text{GW}}=\gamma\,,

(1)

where
H
denotes the Hubble parameter,
t
the cosmic time, and
\gamma
corresponds to the graviton production rate density from the SM thermal bath. It takes a form
[
8

9

\gamma(T)=\frac{4\,T^{4}}{M_{P}^{2}}\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\hat{\eta}(T,\hat{k})=\frac{2\,T^{4}}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}}\int dk\,k^{2}\,\hat{\eta}(T,\hat{k}),

(2)

with
M_{P}\simeq 2.4\times 10^{18}

\hat{k}\equiv k/T
defined as a function of the graviton momentum

\frac{d\gamma}{dk}(T,\hat{k})=\frac{2\,T^{6}}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}}\,\hat{k}^{2}\,\hat{\eta}(T,\hat{k}).

(3)

The source term
\gamma

[
8

9

\hat{\eta}(T,\hat{k})\simeq\begin{cases}\frac{\bar{\eta}}{g_{1}^{4}(T)\,\ln\left[\frac{5}{\hat{m}_{1}(T)}\right]}&\text{ for }\hat{k}\lesssim\alpha_{1}^{2}\,,\\
\hat{\eta}_{\text{HTL}}(T,\hat{k})+\hat{\eta}_{2}(T,\hat{k})&\text{ for }\hat{k}\gtrsim\max(\hat{m}_{n})\,,\end{cases}

(4)

where
\bar{\eta}
is
[
46

\bar{\eta}=\zeta(5)^{2}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{12}{\pi}\right)^{5}\frac{3}{2(1232+9\pi^{2})}\simeq 15.51\,.

(5)

We note that the first piece in Eq. (

[

\hat{\eta}_{\text{HTL}}(T,\hat{k})=\frac{\hat{k}}{16\pi\,(e^{\hat{k}}-1)}\sum_{n=1}^{3}N_{n}\,\hat{m}_{n}^{2}(T)\,\log\left[1+\frac{4\,\hat{k}^{2}}{\hat{m}_{n}^{2}(T)}\right].

(6)

The other terms in the second part of Eq. (
4
) is
[
9
]

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{2}(T,\hat{k})

\displaystyle\equiv\left(3g_{2}^{2}+12g_{3}^{2}\right)\eta_{gg}(\hat{k})+\left(g_{1}^{2}+3g_{2}^{2}\right)\eta_{sg}(\hat{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\left(5g_{1}^{2}+9g_{2}^{2}+24g_{3}^{2}\right)\eta_{fg}(\hat{k})+\left(3|y_{t}|^{2}+3|y_{b}|^{2}+|y_{\tau}|^{2}\right)\eta_{sf}(\hat{k})\,,

arising from infrared-finite loop integrals involving only gauge fields
\eta_{gg}
, scalars and gauge fields
\eta_{sg}
, fermions and gauge fields
\eta_{fg}
, and scalars and fermions

\gamma(T)\simeq\mathcal{C}\,\frac{T^{7}}{M_{P}^{2}}\,,

3
Parametrizing Cosmic Reheating

The detailed dynamics of the reheating phase remains uncertain, but is typically assumed to be driven by the decay or annihilation of a scalar field
\phi
, such as the inflaton, characterized by an effective equation-of-state parameter
\omega
. This implies that the energy density of the scalar field scales as
\rho_{\phi}(a)\propto a^{-3(1+\omega)}
during reheating. To reheat the Universe, energy must be transferred from
\phi
to the SM radiation, with a corresponding energy density given by

\rho_{R}(T)\equiv\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}\,g_{\star}(T)\,T^{4},

(10)

where where
g_{\star}(T)
is the effective number of relativistic degrees of freedom contributing to the radiation energy. The reheating temperature
T_{\text{rh}}
is defined as the temperature at which the radiation energy density is equal to the inflaton energy density,
\rho_{\phi}(a_{\text{rh}})=\rho_{R}(a_{\text{rh}})=3\,H_{\text{rh}}^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}
, where
a_{\text{rh}}
is the scale factor at
T=T_{\text{rh}}
, and
H_{\text{rh}}\equiv H(a_{\text{rh}})
is the Hubble expansion rate at that time. It follows that the Hubble rate is
[
47
,
48
]

H(a)=H_{\text{rh}}\times\begin{cases}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3(1+\omega)}{2}}&\text{ for }a\leq a_{\text{rh}},\\
\left(\frac{g_{\star}(T)}{g_{\star}(T_{\text{rh}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{rh}})}{g_{\star s}(T)}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{2}&\text{ for }a_{\text{rh}}\leq a\,,\end{cases}

(11)

where
g_{\star s}(T)
denotes the effective number of relativistic degrees of freedom contributing to the entropy density

s(T)=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{45}\,g_{\star s}(T)\,T^{3}.

(12)

Additionally, the evolution of the SM temperature during reheating is parametrized as
T(a)\propto a^{-\alpha}
, and therefore
[
47
,
48
]

T(a)=T_{\text{rh}}\times\begin{cases}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\alpha}&\text{ for }a_{\text{ini}}\leq a\leq a_{\text{rh}},\\
\left(\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{rh}})}{g_{\star s}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}&\text{ for }a_{\text{rh}}\leq a\,,\end{cases}

(13)

where
T_{\text{ini}}\equiv T(a_{\text{ini}})=T_{\text{rh}}\times(a_{\text{rh}}/a_{\text{ini}})^{\alpha}
. After reheating, the Universe transitions to a radiation-dominated phase, where
H(a)\propto a^{-2}
and
T(a)\propto a^{-1}
, as expected.

Here, we offer a few comments about the way reheating is parametrized. The most popular reheating scenario corresponds to a heavy inflaton with an energy density that scales as non-relativistic matter (
\omega=0
), which perturbatively decays into a couple of SM particles, giving rise to a scaling of the temperature of
\alpha=3/8

[
22
]
. This scenario can be realized in viable inflationary models such as Starobinsky inflation
[
17
]
or polynomial inflation
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
. However, other scenarios are also possible. For example, within the framework of the
\alpha
-attractor inflation model
[
53
,
54
]
, it is possible that the inflaton oscillates at the bottom of a monomial potential
V(\phi)\propto\phi^{n}
with
n\geq 2
during reheating, with a corresponding equation-of-state parameter
\omega=(n-2)/(n+2)

[
55
]
. Furthermore, the value of
\alpha
depends on the mechanism by which the inflaton energy is transferred to the radiation, specifically the nature of the inflaton-matter couplings
[
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
]
. For example, when the inflaton decays into bosons, one has
\alpha=3/(2(n+2))
, and when it decays into fermions with
n<7
,
\alpha=3(n-1)/(2(n+2))
. In the case of fermionic inflaton decay and
n>7
, one has
\alpha=1
. When considering inflaton annihilation into bosons through contact interactions,
\alpha=9/(2(n+2))
for
n\geq 3
. In the case of reheating via
s
-channel annihilation mediated by a light scalar, resonant effects can lead to
\alpha=3\,(7-2n)/(2(n+2))
and
\alpha=3\,(5-n)/(2(n+2))
for bosonic and fermionic final states, respectively
[
59
]
. If the mediator is massive, one has
\alpha=1
for inflaton annihilations into a pair of fermions
[
59
]
. Moreover, it is also possible to have a constant temperature during reheating with
\alpha=0

[
56
,
60
,
61
,
62
]
. If the inflaton energy density is diluted faster than free radiation, that is, if
\omega>1/3
, it is not necessary for the inflaton to decay or annihilate away, and then one can have
\alpha=1
, as in the case of kination
[
63
,
64
]
. Finally, it is possible to have
\alpha>1
, in particular
\alpha=2
for non-relativistic particles in kinetic equilibrium.

\displaystyle\simeq\Delta N_{\text{eff}}^{\text{RD}}\,\frac{2}{14\alpha-11-3\omega}\left(\frac{T_{\text{ini}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{14\alpha-11-3\omega}{2\alpha}}\left[1-\left(\frac{T_{\text{rh}}}{T_{\text{ini}}}\right)^{\frac{14\alpha-11-3\omega}{2\alpha}}\right].

(22)

It is interesting to note that
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}^{\text{RH}}
features a power-law enhancement
(T_{\text{ini}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{\frac{14\alpha-11-3\omega}{2\alpha}}
with respect to the contribution during radiation domination for
14\alpha-11-3\omega>0
. This arises from the fact that during reheating, the temperature can be much higher than the reheating temperature
T_{\text{rh}}
.

Alternatively, for the special case where
14\alpha-11-3\omega=0
, we find

\displaystyle\Delta N_{\text{eff}}=\Delta N_{\text{eff}}^{\text{RH}}

\displaystyle=\frac{360\,\mathcal{C}}{7\,\alpha\,\pi^{3}}\left(\frac{11}{4}\,\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{\Delta N_{\text{eff}}})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{rh}})}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}\sqrt{\frac{10}{g_{\star}(T_{\text{rh}})}}\,\frac{T_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}}\,\ln\frac{T_{\text{ini}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Delta N_{\text{eff}}^{\text{RD}}\,\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\ln\left(\frac{T_{\text{ini}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right),

(23)

with features a logarithmic boost
\ln(T_{\text{ini}}/T_{\text{rh}})
with respect to the contribution during radiation domination. Referring to Fig.

, the dashed blue line corresponds to
14\alpha-11-3\omega=0
. Above, at, and below this line,
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
features a power-law, logarithmic, or order-one enhancement, respectively.

5
Gravitational Wave Spectrum

In the previous section, we computed the integrated GW spectrum and its contribution to
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
. Now we will analyze the differential spectrum. The primordial GW spectrum at present,
\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)
, per logarithmic frequency
f
, is defined as

\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)\equiv\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\,\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln f}=\Omega_{\gamma}^{0}\,\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{d\ln k}\,,

(25)

where the frequency relates with the momentum as
k=2\pi\,f
,
\rho_{c}
corresponds to the critical energy density of the Universe, and
\Omega_{\gamma}^{0}\,h^{2}\simeq 2.47\times 10^{-5}
is the photon abundance at present
[
74
]
.
Our next task is to obtain the differential spectrum
\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{d\ln k}
. To incorporate the reheating phase, it is convenient to rewrite Eq. (
1
) as

\frac{d}{da}\left[a^{4}\,\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln k}\right]=\frac{a^{3}}{H}\,k\,\frac{d\gamma}{dk}(T,\hat{k})\,,

(26)

with which we find that the differential GW energy density at
a_{\text{fin}}
is

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln k_{\text{fin}}}(a_{\text{fin}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{\text{fin}}^{4}}\Bigg[\int_{a_{\text{ini}}}^{a_{\text{rh}}}da\,\frac{a^{3}\,k(a)}{H(a)}\,\frac{d\gamma}{dk}\left(T(a),\hat{k}(a)\right)+\int_{a_{\text{rh}}}^{a_{\text{fin}}}da\,\frac{a^{3}\,k(a)}{H(a)}\,\frac{d\gamma}{dk}\left(T(a),\hat{k}(a)\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{k_{\text{rh}}}{a_{\text{fin}}^{4}\,a_{\text{rh}}\,H_{\text{rh}}}\Bigg[\int_{a_{\text{ini}}}^{a_{\text{rh}}}da\,a^{4}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{1-3\omega}{2}}\frac{d\gamma}{dk}\left(T_{\text{rh}}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\alpha},\hat{k}_{\text{rh}}\left(\frac{a}{a_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\int_{a_{\text{rh}}}^{a_{\text{fin}}}da\,a^{4}\,\frac{d\gamma}{dk}\left(T_{\text{rh}}\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a},\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{rh}}^{4}}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}\,H_{\text{rh}}\,a_{\text{fin}}}\Bigg[\int_{a_{\text{ini}}}^{a_{\text{rh}}}da\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{8\alpha-3\omega-3}{2}}\hat{\eta}\left(\hat{k}_{\text{rh}}\left(\frac{a}{a_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})\int_{a_{\text{rh}}}^{a_{\text{fin}}}da\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{2}\Bigg],

(27)

by using Eqs. (
11
) and (
13
), and noticing that the graviton momenta always scales as
k(a)\propto 1/a
regardless of the behavior of the background. The first term inside the square brackets represents the contribution from the reheating phase, while the second is the contribution from the radiation era. In the second part, the running of the couplings (and thus the temperature dependence of the source term) has been neglected. In the next sections, the two terms will be independently analyzed.

5.1
After Reheating

We first focus on the contribution after reheating, in the radiation-dominated phase, namely the second term in Eq. (
27
). We find that

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}}(a_{\text{fin}})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{rh}}^{4}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}\,H_{\text{rh}}\,a_{\text{fin}}}\int_{a_{\text{rh}}}^{a_{\text{fin}}}da\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{2\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{rh}}^{4}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}\,H_{\text{rh}}}\,\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a_{\text{fin}}}\left[1-\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a_{\text{fin}}}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{fin}}^{4}\,T_{\text{rh}}^{3}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}\,H_{\text{rh}}}\left[1-\frac{T_{\text{fin}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right]\simeq\frac{6}{\pi^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{10}{g_{\star}(T_{\text{rh}})}}\,\frac{T_{\text{fin}}^{4}\,T_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}}\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})\,,

(28)

with which we find that at present

\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}}(a_{0})=\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}}(a_{\text{fin}})\left(\frac{a_{\text{fin}}}{a_{0}}\right)^{4}\simeq\frac{6}{\pi^{3}}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{fin}})}\right]^{\frac{4}{3}}\sqrt{\frac{10}{g_{\star}(T_{\text{rh}})}}\,\frac{T_{0}^{4}\,T_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}}\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})\,.

(29)

Using Eq. (
25
), we obtain the GW spectrum from the radiation-dominated era

\Omega^{\text{RD}}_{\text{GW}}(f)=\Omega^{0}_{\gamma}\,\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{d\ln\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}}(a_{0})\simeq\Omega^{0}_{\gamma}\,\frac{18}{\pi^{5}}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{fin}})}\right]^{\frac{4}{3}}\sqrt{\frac{10}{g_{\star}(T_{\text{rh}})}}\,\frac{10}{g_{\star}(T_{0})}\,\frac{T_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}}\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k}_{\text{fin}})\,,

(30)

which is linearly proportional to
T_{\text{rh}}

[
8
,
9
]
.
The frequency
f
relates to
\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}
as

\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}=\frac{k_{\text{fin}}}{T_{\text{fin}}}=\frac{k_{0}}{T_{0}}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{fin}})}{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}\right]^{1/3}=\frac{2\pi\,f}{T_{0}}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{fin}})}{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}\right]^{1/3},

(31)

with which we find the peak frequency is located around

f_{\text{peak}}\simeq\frac{3.92}{2\pi}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{fin}})}\right]^{1/3}T_{0}\simeq 74~\text{GHz}\,,

(32)

independently of the reheating temperature. We note that the factor 3.92 corresponds to the position of the maximum of the function
\hat{k}\,\frac{d\gamma}{d\hat{k}}\sim\hat{k}^{3}\,\hat{\eta}(\hat{k})\sim\hat{k}^{4}/(e^{\hat{k}}-1)
in Eq. (
26

in Eq. (

5.2
During Reheating

We now focus on the contribution to the GW spectrum during reheating, namely, the first term of Eq. (
27
), which is

\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln k_{\text{fin}}}(a_{\text{fin}})\simeq\frac{2\,\hat{k}_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{fin}}^{3}\,T_{\text{rh}}^{4}}{\pi^{2}\,M_{P}^{2}\,H_{\text{rh}}\,a_{\text{fin}}}\int_{a_{\text{ini}}}^{a_{\text{rh}}}da\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{8\alpha-3\omega-3}{2}}\hat{\eta}\left(\hat{k}_{\text{rh}}\left(\frac{a}{a_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\right).

(33)

Introducing the dimensionless variable
x\equiv a/a_{\text{rh}}
, and comparing with Eq. (
28
), one has that

\Omega^{\text{RH}}_{\text{GW}}(f)\simeq\Omega^{\text{RD}}_{\text{GW}}(f)\times\left[\frac{1}{\hat{\eta}^{\text{RD}}(\hat{k}_{\text{rh}})}\int_{a_{\text{ini}}/a_{\text{rh}}}^{1}dx\,\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\frac{8\alpha-3\omega-3}{2}}\,\hat{\eta}\left(\hat{k}_{\text{rh}}\,x^{\alpha-1}\right)\right],

(34)

where the term within the square bracket corresponds to a potential enhancement, which depends on
T_{\text{ini}}/T_{\text{rh}}
(via the integration limit
a_{\text{ini}}/a_{\text{rh}}
),
\alpha
, and
\omega
. For instantaneous reheating, the term within the square brackets approaches 1, and there is no enhancement. We also note that the bulk of the contribution occurs when
x\ll 1
, as expected, since graviton production is a UV-dominated freeze-in process.

During reheating, the temperature scales as
T(a)\propto a^{-\alpha}
, which can differ from the scaling of the graviton momentum,
k\propto a^{-1}
. As a result, the analytical integration of the source term becomes, in general, very challenging for
\alpha\neq 1
.
7
7

7

The analytical solution can be written in terms of a difference of incomplete gamma functions, not particularly enlightening for ordinary humans.
For the special case with
\alpha=1
, we find

\displaystyle\Omega^{\text{RH}}_{\text{GW}}(f)

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega^{\text{RD}}_{\text{GW}}(f)\times\int_{a_{\text{ini}}/a_{\text{rh}}}^{1}dx\,x^{\frac{3\omega-5}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{\text{RD}}_{\text{GW}}(f)\times\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3(1-\omega)}\left[\left(\frac{T_{\text{ini}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{3(1-\omega)}{2}}-1\right]&\text{for $\omega\neq 1$}\,,\\
\log\left(\frac{T_{\text{ini}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)&\text{for $\omega=1$}\,,\end{cases}

(35)

which features a power-law enhancement
\sim(T_{\text{ini}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{3(1-\omega)/2}
compared to the pure radiation case for
\omega\neq 1
. We also note that in this case where
\alpha=1
, the location of the peak is the same as in the radiation-dominated scenario.

6
Conclusions

In this study, we revisited the production of the stochastic gravitational wave (GW) background generated through graviton emission from interactions of standard model (SM) particles in the thermal plasma. The GW spectrum produced during the radiation-dominated era peaks at a frequency of approximately
80
GHz, with an amplitude that scales linearly with the reheating temperature.

We extended existing results in the literature by incorporating the cosmic reheating phase in the thermal history of the Universe. Since graviton production is suppressed by powers of the Planck scale, it exhibits a UV freeze-in behavior, implying that it mainly occurs at the highest temperatures reached by the bath, typically during the reheating era. Being agnostic about the evolution of the background during reheating, we conveniently parametrize the evolution of the SM temperature and the Hubble expansion rate as a function of the cosmic scale factor
a
as
T(a)\propto a^{-\alpha}
and
H(a)\propto a^{-3(1+\omega)/2}
, respectively. This general parametrization allows us to explore various reheating scenarios.

Compared to the case of pure radiation domination, there is a significant enhancement in GW production, with typically a strong dependence on the ratio of the maximum temperature to the reheating temperature. The boost in the total energy density in the form of GWs can be quantified by its contribution to the effective number of neutrinos
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
, as shown in Eq. (
22
) and Eq. (
23
). We find that for
11-14\alpha+3\omega>0
,
=0
or
<0
, there is a power law, logarithmic, or order-one enhancement, respectively. The result is summarized in Fig.
3
, where we demonstrate that the enhancement brings the
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
contribution from the GW spectrum closer to the sensitivity of future experiments, even in the case of very low reheating temperatures.

The enhancement also manifests itself in the differential GW spectrum. In the case where
11-14\alpha+3\omega>0
, we find that the GW spectrum exhibits a power-law enhancement, as shown in Eq. (
35
) and Fig.
5
. Furthermore, beyond the overall boost in amplitude, the GW spectrum produced during reheating can peak at different frequencies when
\alpha\neq 1
, as demonstrated in Fig.
6
. Moreover, we observe novel characteristic features, with a broader spectrum emerging, as illustrated in Fig.
7
.

In summary, this work presents a thorough exploration of GW production from the thermal plasma, taking the reheating phase into account. We have highlighted new features of the GW spectrum compared to the pure radiation domination case.

Questionnaire responses dataset on scientific literacy as a global citizenship-related competence among preservice teachers in Vietnam
Authors: Ut Phuong Dang, Lien Phuong Lai, Quynh Viet Pham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset contains questionnaire responses on preservice teachers’ self-reported scientific literacy-related competencies, applications, perceived importance, enhancement measures, and determinants in Vietnam. The dataset comprises 807 anonymized and complete responses collected in May 2025 through an online Vietnamese-language questionnaire administered via Google Forms. It includes 42 five-point Likert-scale items and six background variables covering gender, year of study, academic performance, teaching practicum experience, perceived necessity of scientific literacy, and readiness to apply scientific literacy. The questionnaire items are organized into five scientific literacy-related dimensions: global scientific literacy competence, application of scientific literacy competence, perceived importance of scientific literacy competence, suggested measures for enhancing scientific awareness, and determinants of scientific literacy competence. The data repository contains the respondent-level dataset, a codebook, the original Vietnamese questionnaire, an English translation, and a README file. These materials may be reused for descriptive analysis, subgroup comparison, reliability assessment, exploratory factor re-examination, scale refinement, instrument adaptation, and cross-context comparison in teacher education and science education research.Keywords: Teacher education, Science education, Educational measurement, Questionnaire instrument, Item-level responses, Cross-context comparison

Background

Scientific literacy is widely recognised as an important competence for engaging with scientific knowledge and socioscientific issues such as climate change, public health, and sustainability [[3], [4], [5], [6], [7]]. In this data article, scientific literacy is operationalized as a multidimensional and context-sensitive competence related to the understanding, use, evaluation, and communication of scientific knowledge in socially situated contexts. This operationalization extends beyond factual science knowledge to include application to socioscientific issues, ethical and sustainability-oriented reasoning, and global citizenship-related dispositions relevant to science teacher education. Accordingly, the dataset should be understood as documenting preservice teachers’ self-reported scientific literacy-related perceptions and competencies rather than direct performance-based measures of scientific literacy. This operationalization is consistent with integrated and transformative views of scientific literacy that emphasize socioscientific reasoning, ethical reflection, sustainability, and action-oriented citizenship [8]. In teacher education and science education research, questionnaire-based datasets can provide structured documentation of preservice teachers’ perceptions of scientific literacy-related competencies, their pedagogical application, and the contextual conditions associated with their development [[9], [10], [11], [12]]. However, openly accessible respondent-level datasets on this topic remain limited, particularly in non-Western teacher education contexts. This article describes a dataset of anonymized questionnaire responses from preservice teachers in Vietnam and provides the accompanying files needed for reuse, including the codebook, bilingual questionnaire materials, and README documentation. The dataset may support descriptive reuse, subgroup comparison, reliability assessment, scale-oriented re-examination, and cross-context adaptation in science education and teacher education research.

Data Description

The dataset contains anonymized responses from 807 preservice teachers enrolled in teacher education programmes in Vietnam. All responses are complete cases (N = 807) with no missing values. The data are organised into two groups of variables.

The first group contains six background variables: gender, year of study, academic performance, teaching practicum experience, perceived necessity of scientific literacy, and readiness to apply scientific literacy. The repository is organised into five main files. The first file, raw_dataset_preservice_teachers_vietnam.xlsx, includes anonymized respondent-level data for all 807 participants, covering both background variables and responses to the 42 questionnaire items. The second file, codebook_scientific_literacy_dataset.xlsx, documents variable names, item labels, response coding, and category definitions. The third file, questionnaire_vietnamese.pdf, provides the original survey instrument used for data collection. The fourth file, questionnaire_english_translation.pdf, contains an English translation of the questionnaire to support international reuse and cross-context adaptation. The fifth file, README.pdf, summarises the dataset structure, file contents, coding conventions, and recommended reuse procedures. Together, these files enable direct reuse of the dataset for descriptive analysis, subgroup comparison, reliability assessment, scale refinement, and cross-context adaptation in teacher education and science education research.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Instrument development

The dataset was generated using a structured questionnaire developed to document preservice teachers’ self-reported scientific literacy-related competencies, applications, perceived importance, enhancement measures, and determinants in relation to global citizenship-oriented science education. In this dataset, scientific literacy is operationalized as a multidimensional and context-sensitive competence involving the understanding, use, evaluation, and communication of scientific knowledge in socially situated contexts. This operationalization includes science-related reasoning and application, awareness of socioscientific and sustainability issues, ethical responsibility, and global citizenship-related dispositions relevant to science teacher education.

Questionnaire items were derived and adapted from scientific literacy and global citizenship frameworks in science education [[3], [4], [5], [6], [7]], with reference to prior empirical work on global scientific literacy measurement [2,1]. The items were conceptually adapted rather than directly reproduced. The adaptation process involved selecting content areas relevant to preservice teacher education, revising item wording for the Vietnamese context, and organizing the final questionnaire into five scientific literacy-related dimensions for dataset documentation and reuse.

The instrument should therefore be understood as a self-report questionnaire on perceived scientific literacy-related competencies and orientations, not as a direct performance-based test of scientific literacy. Some items combine closely related aspects of scientific literacy, such as scientific reasoning, sustainability, ethics, and global citizenship, to reflect the contextual and integrated nature of science teacher education. This design choice should be considered when reusing the dataset, particularly for researchers interested in further scale refinement or item-level psychometric analysis.

The questionnaire was administered in Vietnamese. The original instrument uses the term “năng lực tư duy khoa học”, which is reported in English as “scientific literacy” in the dataset, codebook, and accompanying materials to ensure terminological consistency across files and facilitate international reuse. The final instrument consisted of 42 closed-ended items measured on five-point Likert scales, together with six background variables.

Participants and data collection

Data were collected in May 2025 through an online questionnaire administered using Google Forms. The survey targeted preservice teachers enrolled in teacher education programmes at Vietnamese universities and was distributed through lecturer networks and institutional communication channels.

Participation was voluntary. Before responding, participants were informed about the purpose of the data collection, the anonymous treatment of responses, and their right to decline participation. Only respondents who provided informed consent were allowed to proceed.

A total of 807 valid responses were retained in the final dataset. The dataset includes respondent background information on gender, year of study, academic performance, teaching practicum experience, perceived necessity of scientific literacy, and readiness to apply scientific literacy.

Data processing and coding

Responses were exported from Google Forms and screened prior to inclusion in the final dataset. Data cleaning focused on completeness, consistency of response coding, and removal of any potentially identifying information. All retained responses were complete cases and were anonymized before storage and sharing.

The dataset was organised in spreadsheet format, and a separate codebook was prepared to document variable names, item labels, response options, and coding rules. Likert-type items were coded from 1 to 5, with higher values indicating higher levels of endorsement. Descriptive statistics were generated using IBM SPSS Statistics to support dataset documentation and facilitate reuse.

Internal consistency was examined using Cronbach’s alpha for each dimension. Because several alpha coefficients were high, they were interpreted together with exploratory component analysis rather than as standalone evidence of measurement quality. An exploratory principal component analysis with Varimax rotation and Kaiser normalization was conducted on the 42 Likert-scale items to examine the dimensional organization of the questionnaire. Principal component analysis was used as a descriptive dimension-reduction procedure to summarize the empirical organization of the item responses for dataset documentation and reuse, rather than to claim confirmatory construct validation. The Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin value indicated strong sampling adequacy (KMO = 0.974), and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant (χ² = 42,975.374, df = 861, p < .001), supporting the factorability of the correlation matrix. A five-component solution was retained on the basis of eigenvalues greater than 1, factor interpretability, and consistency with the intended questionnaire structure. The retained components explained 81.04% of the total variance. Table 3 summarizes the rotated component solution by reporting the corresponding questionnaire dimension, number of items, and absolute primary component loading range. The absolute primary component loadings ranged from 0.657 to 0.896. All items showed their highest loading on the component corresponding to their intended questionnaire dimension, although some secondary loadings suggested conceptual overlap among closely related dimensions. These results support the use of the dataset for descriptive and measurement-oriented reuse, while also allowing future researchers to re-examine item redundancy, dimensionality, and scale refinement using the shared respondent-level data.

Analytical use and reuse potential

The dataset was prepared for secondary use in descriptive analysis, subgroup comparison, reliability assessment, exploratory factor re-examination, scale refinement, and instrument adaptation. Because the repository includes the respondent-level dataset, bilingual questionnaire materials, and a detailed codebook, the data may also support comparative and cross-context use in science education, teacher education, and educational measurement. Future users may also examine item redundancy, shorten scales, or test alternative dimensional structures using the shared item-level responses.

Limitations

This dataset has several limitations related to sampling and response structure. First, although the respondents were drawn from multiple teacher education institutions in Vietnam, the sample was not designed to be nationally representative. Second, the dataset is strongly skewed toward female respondents, reflecting the composition of many teacher education cohorts and limiting subgroup comparison by gender. Third, the data were collected through self-reported questionnaire responses at a single point in time. Accordingly, the dataset is more appropriate for descriptive, comparative, and measurement-oriented reuse than for causal interpretation. Even with these constraints, the dataset provides a structured set of item-level responses that may support reuse in teacher education and science education research.

In addition, some questionnaire items combine closely related aspects of scientific literacy, sustainability, ethics, and global citizenship within single statements. The high internal consistency coefficients may therefore partly reflect conceptual overlap among items, and future reuse of the dataset may include item-level refinement, shortening of scales, or further validation in other contexts.

Ethics Statement

Participants were informed about the objectives of the data collection, the voluntary nature of participation, and the anonymous handling of responses before completing the questionnaire. Informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data submission. No personally identifiable information is included in the shared dataset. The data collection involving human participants was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was carried out under an institutional research project approved by VNU University of Education, Vietnam National University, Hanoi (Decision No. 1225/QĐ-ĐHGD).

Spatial cube complexes
Adrien Abgrall

Abstract
. We provide a new geometric characterization for the spine of untwisted outer space of a right-angled Artin group, constructed by Charney, Stambaugh, and Vogtmann. We realize the spine as the natural simplicial complex associated with the category of spatial cube complexes , a new class of locally CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \mathrm{CAT}(0) cube complexes with a simple definition in terms of hyperplane collapses.



1.
Introduction

\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes have been introduced by Gromov (
[
11
]
) as a combinatorial model for non-positive curvature. Since then, the study of groups acting by isometries on
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, or discrete wallspaces (
[
15
]
,
[
12
]
) has become an important topic in geometric group theory. Notably, the work of Haglund and Wise on
special cube complexes
(
[
13
]
) has had far-reaching consequences, emphasizing the strong links between
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes and right-angled Artin groups. These groups form a natural class of finitely presented groups stable under free product and direct product, containing both finite-rank free and free abelian groups. They are the fundamental groups of a class of special cube complexes called the
Salvetti complexes
.

One of the first known results about automorphism groups of right-angled Artin groups is due to Laurence (
[
14
]
) and Servatius (
[
16
]
). They found a finite generating set for the group
\mathsf{Out}(A)
of outer automorphisms of any right-angled Artin group
A
, closely resembling the Nielsen generators of
\mathsf{Out}(F_{n})
, the outer automorphism group of a free group. This generating set can contain
twists
: automorphisms related to transvections in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})=\mathsf{Out}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. However, the
untwisted subgroup

\mathcal{U}(A)\leq\mathsf{Out}(A)
, the subgroup generated by all the Laurence-Servatius generators except the twists, appears very similar to
\mathsf{Out}(F_{n})
. It is worth noting that in many right-angled Artin groups, such as free groups themselves,
\mathcal{U}(A)=\mathsf{Out}(A)
, and that, in general, the untwisted subgroup was characterized by Fioravanti (
[
9
]
) as the group of automorphisms preserving coarsely a natural median structure on
A
.

Drawing on this similarity, and the definition due to Culler and Vogtmann (
[
7
]
) of the outer space for free groups, Charney, Stambaugh, and Vogtmann (
[
5
]
) defined a
spine of untwisted outer space
for any right-angled Artin group
A
. This spine is a contractible finite-dimensional simplicial complex
K
endowed with a properly discontinuous and cocompact action of
\mathcal{U}(A)
by isometries. It provides an interesting geometric model for the group and allows for explicit computation of homological invariants. The quotient of
K
by a torsion-free subgroup of
\mathcal{U}(A)
of finite index is a classifying space for this subgroup, recovering the fact that
\mathcal{U}(A)
is finitely presented (which was originally due to Day in
[
8
]
, where
\mathcal{U}(A)
is designated as the subgroup generated by
long-range automorphisms
).

Like for the Culler-Vogtmann spine,
K
is defined as a set of actions of
A
on a certain class of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, and
\mathcal{U}(A)
acts by precomposition with the action. This particular class of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes is described in
[
5
]
as obtained from the universal cover of the Salvetti complex by a
blow-up
procedure. The definition is entirely explicit, but highly technical.

In this paper, we provide a new description of the Charney-Stambaugh-Vogtmann spine of untwisted outer space
K
. This description is based on combinatorial properties of hyperplane collapses in
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, which appear naturally from the viewpoint of groups acting on discrete wallspaces.

Definition 1.1

(see Definitions
3.1
and
3.3
)
.

Let
G
be act by combinatorial isometries on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Z
, inducing an action of
G
on the set
\mathfrak{W}
of hyperplanes of
Z
. The
collapse
of a
G
-invariant family
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{W}
of hyperplanes of
Z
is a
G
-equivariant cellular map of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes with domain
Z
obtained by collapsing to a point each edge dual to a hyperplane of
\mathfrak{H}
. This has a simple interpretation in terms of wallspace maps, corresponding to Caprace and Sageev’s
restriction quotients
(
[
3
]
, see Definition
2.2
and Lemma
3.2
).

When
G
acts without cube inversions, a
strong collapse
is the collapse
c\colon Z\to Y
of a
G
-invariant family of hyperplanes
\mathfrak{H}
satisfying any of the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

For every
H\in\mathfrak{H}
, and every
g\in G\setminus Stab(H)
, some hyperplane separating
H
from
gH
is not contained in
\mathfrak{H}

(2)

For every
H\in\mathfrak{H}
with carrier
K
, the subcomplex
c(K)\subseteq Y
is disjoint from its distinct
G
-translates

(3)

For every
H\in\mathfrak{H}
with carrier
K
, the immersion
c(K)/Stab(H)\to Y/G
is an embedding.

When
G
acts freely on
Z
and
Y
, we describe a further equivalent condition in the quotient as follows: Parallel edges of
Z/G
lying inside the same vertex preimage of the quotient map
Z/G\to Y/G
are parallel in that vertex preimage (Lemma
3.7
).

This new property of collapses, resembling part of the definition of special cube complexes, appears to be the right tool to characterize the Charney-Stambaugh-Vogtmann blow-ups geometrically as
spatial cube complexes
, defined as follows.

Definition 1.2

(see Definitions
5.4
,
5.12
,
5.13
)
.

Let
A
be a right-angled Artin group acting freely and cocompactly on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Z
. Let
\mathbb{S}
be the associated Salvetti complex, with a standard action of
A
on its universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
. The action of
A
on
Z
is:

•

Salvetti-like
when the quotient
Z/A
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{S}
;

•

cospatial
when
Z/A
cannot be "unsubdivided" (in a sense made precise in Definition
5.1
), and for every hyperplane
H
of
Z
, there exists a strong collapse with domain
Z
, with Salvetti-like range, and that does not collapse
H
;

•

untwisted
, or
coarse-median-preserving
, when there exists vertices
x\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
,
z\in Z
and a constant
N
such that for
a,b,c\in A
, the unique
g\in A
such that
gx=\mu_{\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}}(ax,bx,cx)
satisfies
d_{Z}(gz,\mu_{Z}(az,bz,cz))\leq N
, where
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}}
and
\mu_{Z}
are the medians on the
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
Z
respectively.

A
spatial cube complex
is the quotient of a cospatial action of
A
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
be the category with cospatial untwisted actions of
A
as objects and strong collapses as arrows, considered modulo
A
-equivariant combinatorial isomorphism. This category is naturally associated to a simplicial complex
\mathcal{N}
(its nerve), where
0
-cells correspond to objects,
1
-cells to arrows,
k
-cells to sequences of
k
composable arrows.

We are then able to prove the following reinterpretation.

Theorem
5.14
.

The group
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on untwisted cospatial actions by precomposition. It acts on
\mathcal{N}
by combinatorial isomorphisms.

There exists an
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
-equivariant, order-preserving, combinatorial isomorphism between the spine of untwisted outer space
K_{\Gamma}
and
\mathcal{N}
.

We also prove that every hyperplane collapse
c
between spatial cube complexes for
A
is the quotient of a strong collapse (Corollary
5.8
). Hence, any equivariant hyperplane collapse between objects of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
defines an arrow of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
.

To briefly outline the contents of this paper, in Section 2 we recall useful properties of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes and give a short account of the construction of the Charney-Stambaugh-Vogtmann spine. In Section 3, we define and relate several properties of hyperplane collapses in
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes with a group action. In Section 4, we investigate further properties of such collapses when the domain or the range is chosen among the Charney-Stambaugh-Vogtmann spine. Finally in Section 5, we use previously defined strong collapses to define the category of spatial cube complexes and prove the main theorem.

2.
Background

2.1.
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes

\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes
are famous examples of metric cell complexes coming from work of Gromov (
[
11
]
) and Sageev (
[
15
]
). They have many equivalent characterizations as
median graphs
(
[
6
]
) or
discrete wallspaces
(
[
12
]
). We only recall some of their main features and refer the reader to
[
17
]
,
[
10
]
for a more thorough approach.

Definition 2.1
.

A
cube complex
is a cell complex obtained by gluing together Euclidean cubes (isometric to
[0,1]^{n}
for various integers
n
) isometrically along subcubes. It is endowed with a piecewise Euclidean length metric extending the standard metric on each cube. A
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
(resp.
locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
) is a cube complex where the piecewise Euclidean metric is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
(resp.
locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
). Being locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
is equivalent to satisfying
Gromov’s link condition
, a combinatorial condition on vertex links (see
[
2
]
, Chapter II, Theorem 5.5). The
0
-skeleton of a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
is endowed with a different metric, induced by the graph metric on the
1
-skeleton
X^{(1)}
. It is this
combinatorial metric
that we will consider, denoted
d
.

When
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, for each triple of vertices
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in X^{(0)}
there exists a unique
median

\mu(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in X^{(0)}
with the following property: for
i\neq j
,
d(x_{i},x_{j})=d(x_{1},\mu(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}))+d(\mu(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),x_{j})
.

When
X
is locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, say two edges
e,f
of
X
are
parallel
when there exists a sequence of edges
e=e_{0},\dots,e_{n}=f
such that for
1\leq i\leq n
,
e_{i-1}
and
e_{i}
are opposite edges in a square of
X
. This is an equivalence relation on edges whose equivalence classes are called the
hyperplanes
of
X
. An edge
e
and a hyperplane
H
are
dual
if
e\in H
. The
carrier
(resp.
open carrier
) of
H
is the union of closed cells (resp. open cells) of
X
whose closure contains an edge dual to
H
. It is a connected subcomplex (resp. subspace) of
X
. Two hyperplanes of
X
are
transverse
if their open carriers intersect, or equivalently if they have dual edges that span a square in
X
.

When
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, for every hyperplane
H
with open carrier
C
,
X\setminus C
has two connected components called the
halfspaces of
H
, which are subcomplexes of
X
. Hyperplanes are transverse if and only if their halfspaces pairwise intersect, i.e. removing both open carriers separates
X
into four connected components. We will say a quadruple of vertices of
X
witnesses the transversality of the two hyperplanes if it contains one point per such component. For
A,B
non-empty subspaces of
X
, the
separator

\mathrm{Sep}(A\mid B)
is the set of hyperplanes
H
such that
A
is contained in one halfspaces of
H
and
B
is contained in the other. We will sometimes use this notation with
A
or
B
being a hyperplane instead of a subspace. The definition remains the same taking as a subspace the carrier of the hyperplane.

Definition 2.2

(Sageev’s construction, restriction quotients)
.

A
discrete wallspace
is a set
E
together with a set
\mathcal{W}
of partitions of
E
in two parts, such that two points of
E
are always in the same part of all but finitely many partitions in
\mathcal{W}
. When
Z
is a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex,
Z^{(0)}
with the set of partitions given by halfspaces of hyperplanes is a discrete wallspace. Conversely for any wallspace
(E,\mathcal{W})
the following construction defines a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X(E,\mathcal{W})
:

•

Each vertex of
X(E,\mathcal{W})
is the choice of one part in each partition such that the chosen parts pairwise intersect and every vertex of
E
belongs to all but finitely many of the chosen parts.

•

Two distinct vertices of
X(E,\mathcal{W})
are linked by an edge if and only if they have the same chosen parts for all partitions except one.

•

2^{k}
distinct vertices of
X(E,\mathcal{W})
are the vertices of a
k
-cube if and only if they have the same chosen parts for all partitions except
k
. Each possible choice of parts for these
k
partitions corresponds to one of the vertices.

The set of hyperplanes of
X(E,\mathcal{W})
canonically identifies with
\mathcal{W}
. Starting with a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Z
with set of hyperplanes
\mathfrak{W}
, the
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X(Z,\mathfrak{W})
canonically identifies with
Z
.

Let
Z
be a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex with set of hyperplanes
\mathfrak{W}
and let
\mathfrak{W}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{W}
. Then
(Z^{(0)},\mathfrak{W}^{\prime})
is a discrete wallspace and the map
id_{Z^{(0)}}\colon(Z^{(0)},\mathfrak{W})\to(Z^{(0)},\mathfrak{W}^{\prime})
induces a cellular map
Z\simeq X(Z^{(0)},\mathfrak{W})\to X(Z^{(0)},\mathfrak{W}^{\prime})
of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, called the
restriction quotient of
Z
associated with
\mathfrak{W}^{\prime}
. The map is defined on vertices by mapping a choice of parts made for all partitions in
\mathfrak{W}
to the same choice of parts for the subset of partitions
\mathfrak{W}^{\prime}
. Adjacent vertices in
Z
are mapped to equal or adjacent vertices in
X(Z^{(0)},\mathfrak{W}^{\prime})
, and the map extends affinely to
k
-cubes.

Sageev’s construction was introduced in
[
15
]
, and restriction quotients were introduced by Caprace and Sageev in
[
3
]
. The following lemmas highlight the relationship between hyperplanes and edge paths in
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes.

Lemma 2.3
.

In a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex, two edges dual to the same hyperplane and incident at a vertex are equal. Two edges dual to transverse hyperplanes and incident at a vertex span a square at that vertex. Moreover, every embedded edge cycle is of even length at least
4
, and every embedded edge cycle of length
4
bounds a square.

The first part of this result is a consequence of Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.6 in
[
17
]
. Any edge cycle crosses every hyperplane an even number of times, hence is of even length. Thus, there are no embeddeded edge cycles of length
2
, and in every embedded cycle of length
4
, pairs opposite edges belong to two distinct parallelism classes. The four vertices of the cycle witness the transversality of the two hyperplanes involved, and the first part of the lemma asserts that two consecutive edges in this cycle span a square. The two remaining edges of the cycle are parallel to the two remaining sides of the square and incident to them, thus equal.

A straightforward induction gives the following corollary

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
H
be a hyperplane in a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
and
S
a subcomplex of the carrier of
H
, contained in one halfspace of
H
. Then
S
spans a product with an edge dual to
H
. In other words, there exists a cellular map
[0,1]\times S\to X
injective on open cells mapping
\{0\}\times S
to
S
and
[0,1]\times\{v\}
to an edge dual to
H
for each vertex
v
of
S
.

Lemma 2.5
.

In a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex, an edge path is geodesic if and only if it crosses no hyperplane twice. In particular, the distance between two vertices is equal to the cardinality of their separator.

The first part of this result is Lemma 3.9 in
[
17
]
. It implies that a geodesic path crosses a hyperplane if and only if its endpoints belong to distinct halfspaces of the hyperplane. The second part follows.

Definition 2.6

(Convexity, local convexity)
.

When
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, a subcomplex
Y\subseteq X
is
convex
if it is full (every cube of
X
with all vertices in
Y
is in
Y
) and geodesically convex (every geodesic edge path in
X
between vertices of
Y
is contained in
Y
). Geodesic convexity is equivalent to the inclusion
\mu(Y^{(0)},Y^{(0)},X^{(0)})\subseteq Y^{(0)}
. When
Y
is convex,
Y
is itself
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
and the inclusion map is an isometric embedding. Carriers and halfspaces of hyperplanes in
X
are convex. A subcomplex is convex if and only if it is an intersection of halfspaces (the empty intersection corresponding to
X
itself).

Likewise, when
X
is locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
a subcomplex
Y\subseteq X
is
locally convex
if all the connected components of the preimage of
Y
in the universal cover of
X
are convex. When
Y
is locally convex,
Y
is itself locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
and the inclusion map is locally isometric and
\pi_{1}
-injective.

Lemma 2.7

(Helly property for convex subcomplexes)
.

In a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex, if finitely many convex subcomplexes pairwise intersect, they intersect globally.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the number of subcomplexes. If
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
pairwise intersect, let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
belong to each of the three intersections. The median
\mu(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
belongs to
A_{1}
because two of the points do and
A_{1}
is convex. Symmetrically it belongs to
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
, proving the global intersection is non-empty.

If
A_{1},A_{2},\dots A_{n}
pairwise intersect, they intersect triplewise by the previous case, thus
A_{1}\cap A_{n},A_{2}\cap A_{n},\dots,A_{n-1}\cap A_{n}
are pairwise intersecting convex subcomplexes. Applying the induction hypothesis, the
A_{i}
intersect globally.
∎

Definition 2.8

(Special cube complex, see
[
13
]
,
A-special
, Definition 3.2 or
[
17
]
, Definition 4.2)
.

Let
G
be a group acting freely on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
by isometries. This action is
cospecial
when it satisfies the following assumptions.

•

(2-sidedness) If
g\in G
stabilizes a hyperplane of
X
,
g
does not exchange its halfspaces.

•

(no self-intersection) For
g\in G
and
H
a hyperplane of
X
,
H
and
gH
are not transverse.

•

(no direct self-osculation) For
g\in G
and
H
a hyperplane of
X
with halfspaces
H^{+},H^{-}
, if the carriers of
H
and
gH
intersect, then
H^{+}
and
gH^{+}
intersect, and
H^{-}
and
gH^{-}
intersect.

•

(no inter-osculation) For
g\in G
and
H,K
transverse hyperplanes of
X
, if the carriers of
gH
and
K
intersect, then
gH
and
K
are transverse.

A
special cube complex
is the quotient of a cospecial action. It is a locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex.

2.2.
Untwisted outer space for right-angled Artin groups

Right-angled Artin groups are well-known example of finitely presented groups. We only recall some of their properties, a more detailed account can be found in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a non-empty finite simple graph. The corresponding
right-angled Artin group
is the group
A_{\Gamma}
given by the following presentation.

A_{\Gamma}=\left\langle V\mid[v_{i},v_{j}]\;\forall\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E\right\rangle

Standard generators
of
A_{\Gamma}
are the elements of
V
. Let
V^{\pm}=V\sqcup V^{-1}\subset A_{\Gamma}
. For
x\in V^{\pm}
the
link of
x
is the set
lk(x)
defined by the intersection of
V^{\pm}\setminus\{x,x^{-1}\}
with the centralizer of
x
. When
x\in V
, elements of
lk(x)
are the vertices of
\Gamma
adjacent to
x
and their inverses.

The
Salvetti complex

\mathbb{S}
associated with
A_{\Gamma}
is the subcomplex of the torus
(S^{1})^{V}
whose cubes correspond one-to-one to the cliques of
\Gamma
. It is, up to isomorphism, the only special cube complex with one vertex with fundamental group isomorphic to
A_{\Gamma}
. Hyperplanes of
\mathbb{S}
have a canonical bijective labeling by
V
. Two hyperplanes are transverse if and only if their labels are adjacent in
\Gamma
.

In this paper, we are interested in the outer automorphism group
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
and more precisely to its
untwisted subgroup

\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})\leq\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
. A definition can be found in
[
5
]
, Section 2.2, but we quote the following characterization due to Fioravanti.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
9
]
, Proposition A)
.

An outer automorphism
[\varphi]\in\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
is untwisted if and only if for some (any) representative
\varphi\in\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
, the standard action of
A_{\Gamma}
on the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
, denoted
(g,x)\mapsto g\cdot x
, and the action given by
(g,x)\mapsto\varphi(g)\cdot x
induce the same coarse median structure on
A_{\Gamma}
via pulling back the median on the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
.

The group
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on the
spine of untwisted outer space
,
K_{\Gamma}
, which was constructed in
[
5
]
, and of which we will provide another construction. Below, we recall the main features of the construction in
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.11

(Whitehead partition)
.

Consider partitions
\mathbf{P}=(\{P,P^{*}\},L)
of
V^{\pm}
into three parts:
V^{\pm}=P\sqcup P^{*}\sqcup L
, with
P
and
P^{*}
non-empty. One distinguished part,
L
, is called the
link
of the partition, and the other parts

are the
sides
of the partition. We identify each partition
(P,P^{*},L)
with its
opposite

(P^{*},P,L)
. The following notation will be useful:

\displaystyle lk(\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=L

\displaystyle single(\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\pm}\mid x\in P,\,x^{-1}\in P^{*}\text{ or }x\in P^{*},\,x^{-1}\in P\}

\displaystyle double(P)

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\pm}\mid x,x^{-1}\in P\}

\displaystyle double(P^{*})

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\pm}\mid x,x^{-1}\in P^{*}\}

A
based Whitehead partition

(\mathbf{P},b)
is a partition
\mathbf{P}
of the form above, together with a
basepoint

b\in V^{\pm}
, satisfying the following hypotheses:

•

b\in P
,
b^{-1}\in P^{*}
, and
L=lk(b)
.

•

If
x\in P
and
x^{-1}\in P^{*}
,

(we say that
\mathbf{P}

splits

x
).

•

If
x\in P
and
y\in P^{*}
are not inverses,
x
and
y
do not commute.

•

P
and
P^{*}
have at least two elements each.

A
Whitehead partition
is a partition
\mathbf{P}
of the form above such that there exists
b\in V^{\pm}
making
(\mathbf{P},b)
a based Whitehead partition. Such a
b
is not unique in general. A Whitehead partition is entirely determined by any basepoint and one side.

A Whitehead partition
\mathbf{P}
is
adjacent
to a standard generator

when some (any) basepoint of
\mathbf{P}
is adjacent to
v
in
\Gamma
. Likewise, two Whitehead partitions
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are
adjacent
when some (any) basepoints for
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are adjacent in
\Gamma
. The partitions
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are

if they are adjacent or if exactly one of
P\cap Q,P\cap Q^{*},P^{*}\cap Q,P^{*}\cap Q^{*}
is empty (see
[

Definition 2.12

(Spine of untwisted outer space)

Let

Given

(see

Lemma 2.14

In a blow-up

Every blow-up

Definition 3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex with hyperplane set
\mathfrak{W}
. Let
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{W}
and let

be the collapse of
\mathfrak{H}
. The following hold:

(1)

c
is surjective, and cube preimages are convex.

(2)

preserves medians.

(3)

c
maps each

-cube of

onto a
k
-cube of

, with

. Up to isometries, the restriction of

to such an

-cube corresponds to the restriction
[0,1]^{n}\to[0,1]^{k}
of the orthogonal projection
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
with kernel spanned by the basis vectors dual to hyperplanes of
\mathfrak{H}
.

(4)

c
is the quotient map corresponding to the equivalence relation induced by the orthogonal projections on cubes defined above.

(5)

Assume a group
G
acts on

by combinatorial isometries and
\mathfrak{H}
is

-invariant. Then there is a natural action of
G
on

making

equivariant. If

acts cocompactly on

, then

acts cocompactly on

.

Proof.

(1)
Recall that a

-cube

in

corresponds to a choice of

pairwise transverse hyperplanes

together with a choice of halfspaces for all hyperplanes in
\mathfrak{W}\setminus(\mathfrak{H}\cup\{H_{1},\dots,H_{k}\})
such that every vertex of
Z
lies in all but finitely many of the chosen halfspaces, every two chosen halfspaces intersect, and every chosen halfspace intersects with both halfspaces of every
H_{i}
. Note that the preimage
c^{-1}(C)
is the intersection of all such chosen halfspaces, hence is convex. It remains to prove that it is non-empty.

Let
C^{\prime}\in Z
be a
k
-cube dual to
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
. The existence of
C^{\prime}
is guaranteed by the fact that

are pairwise transverse, and for each

,

is entirely contained in a halfspace of

. Choose further

such that the (finite) number of chosen halfspaces for hyperplanes in

that do not contain

is minimal. Assume the existence of

whose chosen halfspace does not contain

. Note that in that case, both halfspaces of

intersect both halfspaces of each of the

are pairwise transverse. Taking such

only contains hyperplanes

whose chosen halfspace contains

. Such a hyperplane

cannot exist as its chosen halfspace would not intersect the chosen halfspace for

, i.e. there exists an edge

and incident to a vertex of

. Since

is transverse to all the

,

by Lemma

lies in the chosen halfspace of every

, proving surjectivity.

(2)
By Lemma
2.5
,

maps geodesic edge paths to geodesic edge paths. Thus, for every vertices

of

lies on geodesics joining

.

(3)
Let

be an

.

be a maximal subcube that is not dual to any hyperplane of

, with dimension

. Then

by

is mapped to a single vertex. Recall that

is affine on

. This means that

, and

is injective on

. Moreover,

coincides with the orthogonal projection described in the statement on all vertices of

equals that orthogonal projection.

(4)
We prove that for

such that for all

and the orthogonal projection from

to the same point. If there exists such a sequence

, let

(5)
The action of

Recall that an isometry of a

cube complex is a

if it stabilizes some (finite-dimensional) cube without fixing it pointwise. It is a

Definition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group acting by combinatorial isometries without cube inversions on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Z
. Let
\mathfrak{W}
denote the set of hyperplanes of
Z
with the induced action of
G
, and let
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{W}
be
G
-invariant.
We say that
\mathfrak{H}
is:

•

weakly collapsible
if for every vertex
z
of
Z
, and every
g\in G\setminus Stab(z)
, the separator
\mathrm{Sep}(z\mid gz)
is not contained in
\mathfrak{H}
;

•

strongly collapsible
if for every
H\in\mathfrak{H}
, and every
g\in G\setminus Stab(H)
, the separator
\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid gH)
is not contained in
\mathfrak{H}
.

A
strong (resp. weak) collapse map
is the collapse of a strongly (resp. weakly) collapsible
G
-invariant family of hyperplanes.

We will often implicitly consider collapse maps modulo
G
-equivariant combinatorial isomorphisms of their domain and range. Given a collapse (resp. weak collapse, strong collapse)
c\colon Z\to Y
of a
G
-invariant family
\mathfrak{H}
, we will often refer to induced map of quotients
Z/G\to Y/G
as the collapse (resp. weak collapse, strong collapse) of the quotient family
\mathfrak{H}/G
.

Remark 3.4
.

The weak collapse condition is equivalent to asking that
Stab(c(z))\subseteq Stab(z)
for every vertex
z
of
Z
. The reverse inclusion is always true by equivariance of
c
. In particular, when
G
acts freely on
Z
,
c
is a weak collapse if and only if
G
acts freely on
Y
, if and only if
c
induces a homotopy equivalence
Z/G\to Y/G
(see Lemma
3.6
).

The strong collapse condition is equivalent to asking that for every hyperplane
H\in\mathfrak{H}
with carrier
K
,
c(K)
is disjoint from its distinct
G
-translates in
Y
. It is also equivalent to asking that the immersion
c(K)/Stab(c(K))\to Y/G
is an embedding.

When
G
acts freely, it is clear from Lemma
3.2
that the map of quotients
Z/G\to Y/G
induced by
c
is the same as the collapse defined in
[
5
]
(Definitions 4.1 and 4.3) when the family
\mathfrak{H}/G
has compatible carriers (i.e. when
\mathfrak{H}
is weakly collapsible and carriers of hyperplanes of
\mathfrak{H}/G
decompose as products in
Z/G
, one factor being the dual edge).

Having compatible carriers in the quotient
Z/G
implies being weakly collapsible, but the converse does not hold (the weak collapse condition does not require that carriers embed). When
G
does not invert hyperplanes of
\mathfrak{H}
(i.e. the corresponding quotient hyperplanes in
Z/G
are two-sided), being strongly collapsible implies having compatible carriers in the quotient (see Lemma
3.5
), but the converse does not hold either: consider for example a family of two parallel hyperplanes in the first subdivision of a single square with two opposite vertices identified.

Note that our main interest will be free, cospecial actions, which have no cube inversions or hyperplane inversions.

In Lemma
3.7
, we will give a characterization of strong collapses in the quotient. First, we need to establish some properties of collapse maps.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
act on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Z
without cube inversions. Let
\mathfrak{H}
be a strongly collapsible family of hyperplanes in

. Then
\mathfrak{H}
is weakly collapsible.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z^{(0)}
,

and assume that
\mathrm{Sep}(z\mid gz)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
, yet

. Let
H
be dual to the first edge of a geodesic edge path

to

. Then

. Therefore

by the strong collapse assumption. Since

is geodesic,

and

are not in the same halfspace of

, thus

exchanges the halfspaces of

. By convexity,

lies in the carrier of

entirely. If

is a single edge, this edge is a cube inverted by

, a contradiction. Otherwise, there exists another geodesic path

in the carrier of

with the same endpoints as

. Proceeding inductively in the

-invariant carrier of

, we get that all hyperplanes in

are pairwise transverse, and stabilized by

with exchanged halfspaces. Thus

and the union of geodesic edge paths joining

to

is then the

inverted by

, a contradiction.
∎

. Since

(2)
This is clear since both actions are without cube inversions and have the same vertex stabilizers by the weak collapse assumption.

and vertex preimages of

to the same vertex. Therefore, their images

in

in

∎

Lemma 3.9

(Strong collapses are quasi-isometries)
.

Let

4.
Collapses between blow-ups

In this section we prove that the collapse of a tree-like families of hyperplanes in a blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
is an example of strong collapse (see Definition
2.12
). By Lemma
3.8
, the same holds for any sub-family of a tree-like family. Conversely, any collapse whose domain and range are both blow-ups of the Salvetti
\mathbb{S}
is the collapse of a sub-family of a tree-like family. In particular, collapses between blow-ups of
\mathbb{S}
are strong.

Lemma 4.1

(Tree-like collapses are strong collapses)
.

Let
\mathbf{\Pi}
be a collection of pairwise compatible Whitehead partitions and
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
a tree-like family of hyperplanes in the blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
. Let
\mathfrak{H}
be the set of all lifts of hyperplanes of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
to the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}}
endowed with the free cocompact action of
G=\pi_{1}\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\simeq A_{\Gamma}
. Then
\mathfrak{H}
is strongly collapsible, with strong collapse map lifting the tree-like collapse
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\to\mathbb{S}
of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Proof.

By
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.11,
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
has compatible carriers. In particular,
\mathfrak{H}
is weakly collapsible, inducing the tree-like collapse
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\to\mathbb{S}
of
\overline{H}
in the quotient.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{H},g\in G
and assume
\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid gH)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
. Let
x,y
be vertices of the carrier of
H
, in the same halfspace of
H
, such that
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gy)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
. Let
p
be a geodesic edge path joining
x
and
y
, remaining in the carrier of
H
by convexity, and let
q
be its projection in the quotient
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
. The path
q
remains in the carrier of the projected hyperplane
\overline{H}
and never crosses
\overline{H}
. Let
l
denote the label of
\overline{H}
in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
. By construction of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, for every edge
e
of
q
labelled by a standard generator
v\in V
, there exists an edge path
q_{e}
with the same endpoints as
e
such that every label of an edge of
q_{e}
is a partition
\mathbf{P}
with
v\in single(\mathbf{P})
. In particular, since
l\in lk(v)
,
l\in lk(\mathbf{P})
, meaning that the path
q_{e}
remains in the carrier of
\overline{H}
. Let
q^{\prime}
be the path obtained from
q
by replacing each such edge
e
with the corresponding path
q_{e}
. This path
q^{\prime}
has the same endpoints as
q
, still lies in the carrier of
\overline{H}
, and all of its edges are labelled by partitions.

We make the following claim: For every edge
f
labelled by some partition
\mathbf{P}
with
l\in lk(\mathbf{P})
, there exists a path
q^{\prime}_{f}
with the same endpoints as
f
such that the following holds: Every edge of
q^{\prime}_{f}
is either dual to a hyperplane of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
transverse to
\overline{H}
, or is labelled by a partition
\mathbf{Q}
with
l\in lk(\mathbf{P})\subsetneq lk(\mathbf{Q})
. In particular,
q^{\prime}_{f}
remains in the carrier of
\overline{H}
. Indeed, let
\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{\Pi}
denote the set of partitions that have the same link as
\mathbf{P}
, and let
\overline{f}
be the projection of
f
via the collapse
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\to\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}}
. By definition of a tree-like family of hyperplanes, there exists a path
\gamma
joining the endpoints of
\overline{f}
in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}}
such that every edge of
\gamma
is dual to a hyperplane projected from
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
, whose label has the same link as
\mathbf{P}
. Define
q^{\prime}_{f}
as the shortest path in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
with the same endpoints as
f
that projects to
\gamma
in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi^{\prime}}}
. Every edge of
q^{\prime}_{f}
is either dual to a hyperplane of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
transverse to
\overline{H}
, or labelled by a partition
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbf{\Pi}\setminus\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
. Assume
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbf{\Pi}\setminus\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
appears as the label of an edge of
q^{\prime}_{f}
. The cycle
q^{\prime}_{f}f
is a shortest cycle lifting
\gamma\overline{f}
to
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
and has an edge labelled
\mathbf{Q}
. By construction of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, some edge of
q^{\prime}_{f}f
is labelled by a vertex
v\in single(\mathbf{Q})
. In particular,
lk(v)\subseteq lk(\mathbf{Q})
. Since
v
is not the label of any hyperplane of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
by definition,
v
is the label of an edge of
\gamma\overline{f}
. In particular,
lk(v)=lk(\mathbf{P})
. This proves that
lk(\mathbf{P})\subseteq lk(\mathbf{Q})
and equality cannot hold since
\mathbf{Q}\notin\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
. This proves the claim.

Let
f
be an edge of
q^{\prime}
which is not dual to a hyperplane of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
. Choose such an
f
whose label has a link of minimum size. Now use the claim to replace
f
by the corresponding path
q^{\prime}_{f}
(remaining in the carrier of
\overline{H}
), and iterate this process inductively. The process terminates because at each step, either the number of edges labelled by partitions with link of minimal size decreases, or the minimal size increases (that size being bounded). The path
q^{\prime\prime}
on which the process terminates has the same endpoints as
q^{\prime}
and
q
, is still contained in the carrier of
\overline{H}
and crosses only hyperplanes of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
. The cycle
q^{-1}q^{\prime\prime}
lifts to a path
p^{-1}p^{\prime\prime}
in the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}}
starting from

, with other endpoint
g^{\prime}y
for some
g^{\prime}\in G
.

Finally,
\mathrm{Sep}(g^{\prime}y\mid gy)\subseteq\mathrm{Sep}(g^{\prime}y\mid x)\cup\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gy)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
. By the weak collapse assumption,
gy=g^{\prime}y
. This point, an endpoint of
p^{\prime\prime}
, belongs to the carrier of
H
. The oriented edges dual to
H
containing
y
and
gy
project to parallel oriented edges incident to the same vertex of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
with the same orientation. By construction of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, they must be the same edge in the quotient, hence
g\in Stab(H)
. Since
g\in G
and
H\in\mathfrak{H}
were arbitrary,
\mathfrak{H}
is strongly collapsible.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Collapses between blow-ups are tree-like)
.

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
be a family of hyperplanes in a blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
. Assume that the range of the collapse of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
is isomorphic to a blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}}
(of the same Salvetti complex
\mathbb{S}
). Then
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
is contained in a tree-like family of hyperplanes of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
.

The assumption that the range is isomorphic to a blow-up of
\mathbb{S}
is necessary. Indeed, in the single blow-up of
\left\langle a,b,c\mid[a,b]=1\right\rangle
corresponding to the partition
\{a,c\}\sqcup\{a^{-1},c^{-1}\}\sqcup\{b,b^{-1}\}
with basepoint
a
, the family containing only the hyperplane dual to
c
is weakly collapsible but not tree-like.

Proof.

The collapse of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
lifts to the collapse of some
A_{\Gamma}
-invariant family
\mathfrak{H}
between universal covers. Since this collapse is equivariant and both actions are free, it is a weak collapse. The composition
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\to\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{S}
of the collapse of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
and the canonical collapse of hyperplanes labelled
\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence. Thus, up to adjoining
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
with all hyperplanes of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
mapping to partition-labelled hyperplanes in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}}
, assume
\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
is empty. The family
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
still lifts to a weakly collapsible family, and the range of its collapse is isomorphic to the Salvetti complex
\mathbb{S}
. The goal is to apply Theorem
2.13
to prove that
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
is tree-like.

By the weak collapse assumption, every edge cycle in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
dual to hyperplanes of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
only is nullhomotopic. It only remains to check that hyperplanes of
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
are carrier retracts in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, i.e. have carriers that embed as a product with the dual edge. Assume
H\in\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
is not a carrier retract. By specialness of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, there exists
\gamma
a closed edge path in the carrier of
H
that crosses
H
exactly once. By the weak collapse assumption, no edge dual to
H
is a loop, hence the label
l
of
H
is either a partition of
\mathbf{\Pi}
or split by some partition of
\mathbf{\Pi}
. Let
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbf{\Pi}
be the partition either equal to
l
or splitting
l
and note that
lk(l)\subseteq lk(\mathbf{P})
. The image
H^{\prime}
of
H
in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
under the collapse of
\mathbf{\Pi}\setminus\mathbf{P}
is still not dual to any loops. The image
\gamma^{\prime}
of
\gamma
under this collapse is a closed path in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
. Yet, all the edges of
\gamma^{\prime}
except the unique edge dual to
H^{\prime}
have labels in
lk(l)\subseteq lk(\mathbf{P})
. By construction of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
, these edges are loops, and
\gamma^{\prime}
cannot be closed, a contradiction.
∎

5.
Spatial cube complexes

The graphs involved in the construction of Culler-Vogtmann’s outer space for free groups are assumed not to contain vertices of valence
2
, i.e. are maximally unsubdivided. We describe an analogue using hyperplanes.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
. The
subdivision
of the standard
n
-cube
[0,1]^{n}
along the hyperplane given by the first coordinate is the product cube complex
[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n-1}
where the first factor has vertex set
\{0,1/2,1\}
and the second factor is the standard
n-1
-cube. It comes with a cellular homeomorphism from the standard
n
-cube given by the identity map of underlying sets (but its inverse is not cellular).

Let
X
be a locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex and
H
a two-sided, non-self-intersecting hyperplane of
X
. The
subdivision of
X
along
H
is the locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X^{\prime}
obtained by applying the subdivision procedure described above to each cube of
X
containing one edge dual to
H
, along the hyperplane
H
. There is a cellular homeomorphism
X\to X^{\prime}
, which is piecewise affine. The complex
X^{\prime}
is locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
. Any edge dual of
X
dual to
H
is mapped to a path of length
2
in
X^{\prime}
. The two hyperplanes dual to this path are the
images of
H
in
X^{\prime}
. They are distinct because
H
is two-sided.

Finally, let
X
be any locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex. A pair
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
of distinct hyperplanes of
X
is
redundant
if there exists a locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Y
with a two-sided, non-self-intersecting hyperplane
H
, and an isomorphism between
X
and the subdivision
Y^{\prime}
of
Y
along
H
, mapping
H_{1},H_{2}
to the images of
H
in
Y^{\prime}
. Note that redundant hyperplanes are non-transverse, and have the same set of transverse hyperplanes.

Remark 5.2
.

If
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
is a redundant pair of non-self-osculating hyperplanes in a locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
, it is clear from the definition and Lemma
3.7
that the family of lifts of
H_{1}
(resp. of
H_{2}
) to the universal cover is strongly collapsible. Letting
X\to X_{1}
,
X\to X_{2}
be the corresponding collapses in the quotient, there is an isomorphism
X_{1}\to X_{2}
such that the composition
X\to X_{1}\to X_{2}
is homotopic to the collapse
X\to X_{2}
.

Moreover, it is clear from the definition and the description in Lemma
3.2
that in the range of the collapse of any family of hyperplanes in
X
disjoint from
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
, the pair of images of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
is still redundant.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{\Pi}
be a family of pairwise compatible Whitehead partitions. The corresponding blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
of
\mathbb{S}
has no redundant pair of hyperplanes.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{\Pi}
is empty, the result is obvious since
\mathbb{S}
has only one vertex. If
\mathbf{\Pi}=\{\mathbf{P}\}
is a single Whitehead partition, the single blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
has no redundant pair either. Indeed, since
P
and
P^{*}
have at least two elements each, both vertices of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
have at least three incident edges that pairwise do not span squares (one edge labelled
\mathbf{P}
, one edge labelled by a basepoint
b
of
\mathbf{P}
and one edge labelled by an element of
P\cup P^{*}\setminus\{b,b^{-1}\}
).

If
\mathbf{\Pi}=\{\mathbf{P,Q}\}
, every pair distinct from the pair
\{H_{\mathbf{P}},H_{\mathbf{Q}}\}
of hyperplanes labelled
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
is preserved in one of the collapses
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
,
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{Q}}
, therefore not redundant by Remark

. If

are transverse, they do not have the same set of transverse hyperplanes, therefore are not redundant. If
H_{\mathbf{P}},H_{\mathbf{Q}}
are not transverse,
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are not adjacent. Hence, exactly one of the four quadrants, say

is empty. It is then easily checked that all three vertices of
\mathbb{S}^{\{\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Q}\}}
have at least three incident edges that pairwise do not span squares: for the vertex corresponding to

(resp.

), take edges labelled by
\mathbf{P}
, a basepoint

of
\mathbf{P}
, and an element of

(resp.
\mathbf{Q}
, a basepoint

of
\mathbf{Q}
and an element of

). Finally, for the vertex corresponding to

, take edges labelled by
\mathbf{P}
,
\mathbf{Q}
and an element of

. This proves that
\{H_{\mathbf{P}},H_{\mathbf{Q}}\}
is not redundant.

Finally, any pair of hyperplanes
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
in any blow-up

is preserved in the range of the collapse of all hyperplanes different from

∎

Definition 5.4
.

Consider a free cocompact action of
A_{\Gamma}
on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
Z
by combinatorial isometries. This action is:

•

Salvetti-like
if the quotient
Z/A_{\Gamma}
is combinatorially isomorphic to the Salvetti complex
\mathbb{S}
, or equivalently if the action is transitive on vertices and cospecial;

•

blow-up-like
if
Z/A_{\Gamma}
is combinatorially isomorphic to some blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, where
\mathbf{\Pi}
is a family of pairwise compatible Whitehead partitions of
V^{\pm}
;

•

cospatial
if
Z/A_{\Gamma}
has no redundant pair of hyperplanes, and for every hyperplane
H
of
Z
, there exists a strong collapse map with Salvetti-like range that does not collapse
H
.

A
spatial cube complex
is the quotient of a cospatial action.

Remark 5.5
.

Let
\mathbf{\Pi}
be a family of pairwise compatible Whitehead partitions, and consider the blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
of
\mathbb{S}
. For every partition
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbf{\Pi}
, there exists a basepoint

for
\mathbf{P}
making the family

tree-like. Lemma

guarantees that tree-like families are strongly collapsible, and by
[

]
, Theorem 4.11, the range of such a collapse is combinatorially isomorphic to the Salvetti complex

. Moreover,

has no redundant pair of hyperplanes by Lemma

. Therefore, every such blow-up is spatial. Proposition

will prove the converse.

Lemma 5.6

Every spatial cube complex is special.

Proposition 5.7
.

. In particular, every cospatial action of

is blow-up-like.

Let
\widetilde{c}\colon\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{S}
be a strong collapse with
\widetilde{X}
cospatial and
\widetilde{S}
Salvetti-like. Let

, choose an identification

and consider the induced collapse in the quotient

. Let

be the unique vertex preimage, containing all vertices of

. Every hyperplane of

has an orientation and label in

. For each hyperplane

of

dual to an edge of

, define a Whitehead partition

By Lemma

,

induces a unique hyperplane of the

. Orient the halfspaces of that hyperplane in

,

. Every element

of

•

If every oriented edge dual to

terminates in

.

•

If every oriented edge dual to
H_{a}

.

•

.

This defines a partition

as

.

First, we make the following observation: Let

be any edge of

starting in
H^{-}
and terminating in

, with dual hyperplane

. We claim that every hyperplane transverse to

is transverse to

. If

is in

,

must be dual to

is not in

, let

be a geodesic edge path in

. Note that in particular,

.

,

, and

. By definition of

, any oriented edge dual to

and terminates in

. Moreover,

is transverse to

are transverse. Therefore, by the observation,

is transverse to

, hence

. This proves that

, the second assumption of the definition of a Whitehead partition.

Next, note that

if and only if

and

are transverse, in particular

is symmetric. Indeed, if

, there exist two oriented edges
e^{+},e^{-}
dual to
H_{a}
with

terminating in
H^{+}
and
e^{-}
in

. Up to taking

and

maximally close in the carrier of

, they are opposite edges in a square. Let

be the edge of that square joining the terminal endpoints of

and

. Its dual hyperplane

is transverse to

. By the observation,

must be transverse to

. The reverse implication is clear.

Furthermore, note that if some edge dual to
H_{a}
has endpoints on opposite sides of

,

and

are not transverse by the observation, hence

. Therefore,

and

Now assume that

,

We need to prove the existence of a basepoint with link

. By assumption,

where

is combinatorially isomorphic to

and

is not collapsed. Let

and

. Some oriented edge of

. By Lemma

(1.)
some side of

, say

of

, or

,

, and

of

be the (two-sided) hyperplane corresponding to

. This means that

in

, since one halfspace of

\mathrm{Sep}(x_{1}\mid y_{1})

In a special cube complex, a length

. Finally, hyperplanes labelled

and

Finally, by definition, any cospatial action admits at least one strong collapse to a Salvetti-like action. The previous argument proves then that every cospatial action is blow-up-like.

Theorem

, Lemmas

and

, and Proposition

yield the following corollaries.

Corollary 5.8
.

Any equivariant collapse between cospatial actions of
A_{\Gamma}
is strong.

Corollary 5.9
.

Any marking-preserving collapse between spatial cube complexes is isomorphic to the collapse of a family of hyperplanes labelled by partitions in some blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
of
\mathbb{S}
.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
be a tree-like family of hyperplanes in a blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
. There exists a family
\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
of pairwise compatible Whitehead partitions and relabeling of the hyperplanes giving an isomorphism
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\to\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}}
mapping
\overline{\mathfrak{H}}
to
\mathbf{\Pi}^{\prime}
.

The latter corollary was used implicitly in
[
1
]
, Definition 2.16.

In view of Remark
5.2
, it could be possible in general to have a locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
marked by
A_{\Gamma}
with two distinct marking-preserving strong collapses with ranges isomorphic as marked complexes. However, the following lemma proves that it is not the case for spatial cube complexes.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(X,m)
be a marked spatial cube complex. Let
c_{1}\colon(X,m)\to(Y_{1},m_{1})
,
c_{2}\colon(X,m)\to(Y_{2},m_{2})
be marking-preserving collapse maps with spatial range, collapsing distinct families of hyperplanes of
X
. Then, there is no marking-preserving isomorphism
(Y_{1},m_{1})\to(Y_{2},m_{2})
.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that there exists a marking-preserving isomorphism
\iota\colon(Y_{2},m_{2})\simeq(Y_{1},m_{1})
. By Corollary
5.9
, there exists an isomorphism between
X
and some blow-up
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
of
\mathbb{S}
such that all hyperplanes collapsed by
c_{1}
are labelled by partitions. Let
d\colon(Y_{1},m_{1})\to(\mathbb{S},m_{3})
be a marking preserving collapse corresponding to the collapse of all remaining hyperplanes labelled by partitions. Up to replacing
c_{1}
by
d\circ c_{1}
and
c_{2}
by
d\circ\iota\circ c_{2}
, assume
Y_{1}=\mathbb{S}
. Thus,
c_{1}\colon\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}\to\mathbb{S}
is the standard collapse. Up to replacing
X
with the range of the collapse of all hyperplanes collapsed by
c_{2}
labelled by partitions (with a marking making all collapses involved marking-preserving), assume
c_{2}
only collapses hyperplanes labelled by generators. The set
\mathfrak{H}
of hyperplanes collapsed by
c_{2}
forms a tree-like family by Theorem
2.13
. By assumption,
\iota_{*}\circ(c_{2})_{*}\circ(c_{1})_{*}^{-1}
is an inner automorphism of
\pi_{1}Y_{1}
. In particular, for every edge cycle
\gamma
in
X
, shortest edge cycles in the homotopy classes of
c_{1}\circ\gamma
and
c_{2}\circ\gamma
have the same length. Note that by Lemma
3.2
,
(2.)
, if
\gamma
is shortest in its homotopy class, so are
c_{1}\circ\gamma
and
c_{2}\circ\gamma
. Let
C\subset\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
be the
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
subcomplex given by the only vertex preimage under
c_{1}
.

Let
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbf{\Pi}
and let
H
be the corresponding hyperplane of
C
. Let
b\in single(\mathbf{P})
, and let
\gamma
be a shortest edge cycle in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
projecting via
c_{1}
to the edge loop of
Y_{1}=\mathbb{S}
labelled by
b
:
\gamma
has one edge labelled
b
and all of its remaining edges are labelled by partitions. Since
c_{2}\circ\gamma
must have only one edge,
|\gamma|=2
. Hence,
c_{2}
must collapse the edge labelled
b
, and the endpoints of the edge labelled
b
in
\gamma
are only separated by
H
in
C
. By construction of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, this means that
b
is not split by any partition in
\mathbf{\Pi}
other than
\mathbf{P}
.

Now assume

contains
b^{\prime}\notin\{b,b^{-1}\}
, with, say
b,b^{\prime}\in P
. Let
\gamma^{\prime}
be a shortest edge cycle in
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
projecting via
c_{1}
to the length
2
edge cycle of
Y_{1}=\mathbb{S}
labelled by
bb^{\prime}
:
\gamma^{\prime}
has one edge
e
labelled
b
, one edge
e^{\prime}
labelled
b^{\prime}
, and all of its remaining edges are labelled by partitions. Write
\gamma^{\prime}=epe^{\prime}p^{\prime}
, with
p,p^{\prime}
only labelled by partitions. Since
c_{2}\circ\gamma^{\prime}
must be of length
2
,
|p|+|p^{\prime}|=2
. Since the terminal endpoint of

and the initial endpoint of
e^{\prime}
are on opposite halfspaces of
H
in
C
,
p
has at least one edge labelled
\mathbf{P}
. Symmetrically,
p^{\prime}
has at least one edge labelled
\mathbf{P}
. Hence both
p
and
p^{\prime}
are single edges labelled
\mathbf{P}
. However, by the argument above,
b
and
b^{\prime}
are not split by any partition other than
\mathbf{P}
. Thus, the two endpoints of

are separated only by

in

, and the same holds for the two endpoints of

. Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
,

joins the endpoints of

and
p^{\prime}
joins the endpoints of

. Hence,
e^{-1}e^{\prime}
forms a closed length

to a path labelled
b^{-1}b^{\prime}

\mathbb{S}

to the trivial path. This contradicts the fact that

and

are homotopy equivalences. Thus,

contains only two elements: the unique basepoint

\mathbf{\Pi}
have pairwise distinct unique basepoints. Since

Then, since

Definition 5.12

Let

act freely and cocompactly on a

on the universal cover

. The action of

is

, when there exists vertices

,

such that for

, the unique

such that

satisfies

. This is equivalent to asking that both actions of

on
Z
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
induce the same coarse median structure on
A_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the category whose objects are cospatial untwisted actions of
A_{\Gamma}
and whose arrows are given by equivariant collapse maps, or, equivalently, given by strong collapse maps, thanks to Corollary

.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
be the category of cospatial untwisted actions of
A_{\Gamma}
and equivariant collapse maps, considered modulo
A_{\Gamma}
-equivariant isomorphism (the

of
\mathcal{C}
in the categorical sense). Equivalently, it is the category of spatial cube complexes with coarse-median-preserving markings, and marking-preserving hyperplane collapses, considered modulo marking-preserving isomorphism. By Proposition
5.7
, Lemma
5.11
and the finiteness of the set of blow-ups of
\mathbb{S}
,
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
has countably many objects, finitely many arrows with fixed domain (resp. range), and at most one arrow with fixed domain and range.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the

of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
i.e. the simplicial complex having one
0
-cell per object of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
, one
1
-cell per non-identity arrow of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
, and more generally one
k

k
composable non-identity arrows of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
. Equivalently, it is the simplicial complex of the poset of cospatial actions of
A_{\Gamma}
(up to
A_{\Gamma}
-equivariant combinatorial isomorphism) ordered by equivariant collapse maps.

Theorem 5.14
.

The group
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on untwisted cospatial actions by precomposition. It acts on
\mathcal{N}
by combinatorial isomorphisms.

There exists an
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
-equivariant, order-preserving, combinatorial isomorphism between the spine of untwisted outer space
K_{\Gamma}
and
\mathcal{N}
.

In particular, using the results of
[
5
]
,
\mathcal{N}
is finite-dimensional and contractible, and the action of
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
on
\mathcal{N}
is properly discontinuous and cocompact.

Proof.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
act on
Z
cospatially, and consider a strong collapse
c\colon Z\to S
to a Salvetti-like action. Since
Z
has finitely many orbits of hyperplanes,
c
is quasi-isometric and preserves medians (Lemmas
3.2
and
3.9
). Thus, the action on
S
is untwisted if and only if the action on
Z
is.

Moreover, fixing a combinatorial isomorphism
\iota\colon S/A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{S}
and a vertex
x\in S
induces a map
A_{\Gamma}\to A_{\Gamma}\cdot x\to\pi_{1}(S/A_{\Gamma})\to\pi_{1}\mathbb{S}=A_{\Gamma}
which is an automorphism
\varphi
of
A_{\Gamma}
. The action of
A_{\Gamma}
on
S
is then equivariantly isomorphic to the standard action of
A_{\Gamma}
on
\mathbb{S}
precomposed with
\varphi
. By Theorem
2.10
, the action on
S
is untwisted if and only if
[\varphi]\in\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Then, letting
[\psi]\in\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
, precomposing the action on
S
by
\psi
is equivariantly isomorphic to precomposing the action on
\mathbb{S}
by
\psi\circ\varphi
. Finally, the actions on
Z
and
S
precomposed by
\psi
still differ by the equivariant strong collapse
c
. This proves that the action on
Z
precomposed by
\psi
is untwisted if and only if the original action on
Z
was untwisted. This action is clearly cospatial and does not depend on the representative of
[\psi]
, up to equivariant automorphism of
Z
. Therefore,
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on the vertices of
\mathcal{N}
and the action is adjacency-preserving, i.e. extends to an action on
\mathcal{N}
by combinatorial isomorphisms.

By Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, Proposition
5.7
, and Remark
5.5
, an action of
A_{\Gamma}
is blow-up-like if and only if it is cospatial, and a map between cospatial actions is an equivariant collapse if and only if it lifts the collapse of a subfamily of a tree-like family of hyperplanes in a blow-up of
\mathbb{S}
. Finally, it is clear that untwisted markings on the Salvetti complex in the sense of
[
5
]
correspond to precompositions of the action on
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
by elements of
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
, i.e. untwisted Salvetti-like actions. This proves that the poset defining

is
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
-equivariantly isomorphic to the poset defining

.
∎

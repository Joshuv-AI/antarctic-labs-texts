Imaging nuclear shape through anisotropic and radial flow in high-energy heavy-ion collisions
The STAR Collaboration

Abstract
Most atomic nuclei exhibit ellipsoidal shapes characterized by quadrupole deformation β 2 \beta_{2} and triaxiality γ \gamma , and sometimes even a pear-like octupole deformation β 3 \beta_{3} . The STAR experiment introduced a new “imaging-by-smashing” technique Abdulhamid et al. 2024 ; Jia 2025 to image the nuclear global shape by colliding nuclei at ultra-relativistic speeds and analyzing outgoing debris. Features of nuclear shape manifest in collective observables like anisotropic flow v n v_{n} and radial flow via mean transverse momentum [ p T ] [p_{\mathrm{T}}] . We present new measurements of the variances of v n v_{n} ( n = 2 n=2 , 3, and 4) and [ p T ] [p_{\mathrm{T}}] , and the covariance of v n 2 v_{n}^{2} with [ p T ] [p_{\mathrm{T}}] , in collisions of highly deformed 238 U and nearly spherical 197 Au. Ratios of these observables between these two systems effectively suppress common final-state effects, isolating the strong impact of uranium’s deformation. Comparing results with state-of-the-art hydrodynamic model calculations, we extract β 2 ​ U \beta_{2\mathrm{U}} and γ U \gamma_{\mathrm{U}} values consistent with those deduced from low-energy nuclear structure measurements. Measurements of v 3 v_{3} and its correlation with [ p T ] [p_{\mathrm{T}}] also provide the first experimental suggestion of a possible octupole deformation for 238 U. These findings provide significant support for using high-energy collisions to explore nuclear shapes on femtosecond timescales, with broad implications for both nuclear structure and quark–gluon plasma studies.



I
Introduction

High-energy heavy-ion collisions, conducted at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) and the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), have been used to produce and study the quark-gluon plasma (QGP). This deconfined state of matter undergoes a spacetime evolution that is well-described by relativistic hydrodynamics
Busza et al. 2018
. The hydrodynamic expansion translates initial spatial anisotropies and size variations within the QGP into measurable patterns in the azimuthal and radial distributions of outgoing particles in the momentum space, observed as anisotropic
Ollitrault 1992
;
Ackermann et al. 2001
and radial flow
Schnedermann et al. 1993
;
Abelev et al. 2009

While these observables contain vital information about the transport properties of the QGP, their interpretation is complicated by inherent uncertainties in the initial conditions (IC) – the spatial distribution of energy density before the hydrodynamic evolution. The IC cannot be calculated directly from first principles; they must be inferred alongside QGP properties using hydrodynamic models, which introduces significant uncertainties in both
Bernhard et al. 2016
;
Everett et al. 2021
;
Nijs et al. 2021
. Consequently, it is important to develop methods to disentangle the influence of IC from final-state effects.

Quadrupole deformation also influences
c_{2}\{4\}

\displaystyle c_{2}\{4\}=-c_{1}-d_{1}\beta_{2}^{4}\;.

(8)

where

and

are positive, centrality-dependent coefficients. The liquid-drop model calculation predicts
d_{1}=2/7b_{1}^{2}

Jia 2022b
. Previous STAR measurements suggest that

is larger than

in the 0–5% most central collisions
STAR Collaboration 2015
.

For higher-order flow harmonics (

,4), large statistical uncertainties in

and
c_{4}\{4\}
preclude measuring
\rho_{3}^{\mathrm{pcc}}
and
\rho_{4}^{\mathrm{pcc}}
. Thus, the simpler normalization of Eq. (
5
) is used. For
n=2
in central collisions and generally for
n=3
and 4,
|c_{n}\{4\}|\ll c_{n}\{2\}^{2}
, so
\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(v_{n}^{2})}\approx\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
and
\rho_{n}^{\mathrm{pcc}}\approx\rho_{n}
. However, in non-central collisions,
\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(v_{2}^{2})}<\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
, leading to
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc}}>\rho_{2}
. In peripheral collisions, their differences are more affected by non-flow.

Previous studies.
Jia 2022b
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
found
\rho_{2}
nearly centrality-independent for spherical nuclei, making it ideal for detecting quadrupole deformation. This normalization also benefits from the simple dependence of
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
on
\beta_{2}
(Eq. (

)). In contrast,
\mbox{var}(v_{2}^{2})
has a more complex form:

\displaystyle\mbox{var}(v_{2}^{2})=(a_{1}+b_{1}\beta_{2}^{2})^{2}-c_{1}-d_{1}\beta_{2}^{4}

(9)

where
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
are from Eq. (
2
), and
c_{1}
and

are from Eq. (

).

All observables are computed for four

, 0.2–3 GeV/
c
, 0.5–2 GeV/
c
, and 0.5–3 GeV/
c
. Studying

. Furthermore, the hydrodynamic response may vary with

II.3
Influence of non-flow correlations

Non-flow refers to correlations among a few particles from a common source (e.g., resonance decays, jets) that are uncorrelated with the initial geometry. Most non-flow sources are short-range (near-side), but dijet fragmentation can cause wider
\eta
correlations (away-side). The non-flow contribution generally increases with
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, decreases for higher-order cumulants, and decreases with
\eta
gaps between particles. However, the
\eta
-gap dependence can also arise from longitudinal fluctuations.

Near-side non-flow is largely suppressed by the subevent method, which requires a large
\eta
gap. Remaining away-side non-flow can be estimated assuming independent sources, where non-flow in
n
-particle cumulants (
\mathcal{O}
) scales with charged particle multiplicity as
1/\mbox{$N_{\mathrm{ch}}$}^{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{nf}}\approx\frac{\left[\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle\mbox{$N_{\mathrm{ch}}$}^{n-1}\right]_{\mathrm{peri}}}{\mbox{$N_{\mathrm{ch}}$}^{n-1}}\;,

(10)

where “peri” denotes the
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
range corresponding to the 90–100% centrality bin. While widely used in small collision systems at RHIC and the LHC
Chatrchyan et al. 2013
;
Abelev et al. 2013
;
Aad et al. 2014
;
Adare et al. 2013
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2023
, this method may not be appropriate for large systems where jet quenching strongly modifies away-side correlations.

Following Ref.
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
, non-flow is estimated by: 1) the difference between the standard method and subevent methods, and 2) the
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
-scaling method (Eq. (
10
)). The latter is applied for
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
, where medium effects redistribute non-flow azimuthally rather than suppress them. This approach isn’t suitable for
\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle
, where medium effects should always suppress non-flow.

The subevent requirement reduces the signal in the most peripheral bin for
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle
, and
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
. Differences between standard and subevent methods are used to estimate residual non-flow in the subevent method. These differences, propagated to ratios between U+U and Au+Au, are 1.1%, 3.5%, and 11% for
R_{v_{2}^{2}}
,
R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}
, and
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
, respectively.

For non-flow estimation based on the
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
-scaling method, we assume the entire signals in the 80–100% centrality two-subevent results are non-flow, then estimate the non-flow fraction using Eq. (
10
). This is used for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
. In 0–5% most central collisions, estimated non-flow is about 6% for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
and about 1.4% for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
. Propagated to ratios, these are 2.8% for
R_{v_{2}^{2}}
and 2.5% for
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
.

Non-flow systematic uncertainties are taken as the larger of the two estimates for
R_{v_{2}^{2}}
and
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
, while for
R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}
, the difference between standard and subevent methods is used. Total non-flow uncertainties in the 0–5% centrality are 2.8%, 3.5% and 11% for
R_{v_{2}^{2}}
,
R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}
and
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
, respectively.

Non-flow correlations are physical processes, not instrumental effects. Their estimation often relies on model assumptions. Rather than being corrected for in the data, non-flow physics should be incorporated into theoretical models used for comparison. This is standard practice for flow measurements in large collision systems at RHIC and the LHC. Most models do not yet correctly describe the non-flow correlations (e.g., including contributions from resonance decays but not jet quenching). This should be taken into account when comparing measurements to models.

In this paper, non-flow systematics are treated as model-dependent uncertainties and are incorporated for quantitative extraction of nuclear shape parameters in Section
IV.2
, but are otherwise not included in data plots.

III.1
General trends and the signatures of deformation

To isolate the effects of nuclear deformation, we compare results from collisions of highly-deformed
238
U with those of nearly-spherical
197
Au, as a function of centrality. The 20% difference in mass number between these nuclei is expected to have a minor influence on the hydrodynamic response
Mäntysaari et al. 2024

Figure

Ratios between the two systems

While normalized observables like

selection. A more robust approach involves direct ratios of the same observable between U+U and Au+Au (Eq. (

A challenge in this analysis is that observables are dominated by baseline contributions from spherical nuclei (
a_{n}
terms in Eqs. (
2
)), which are strong functions of impact parameter and increase rapidly towards more peripheral collisions. In contrast, deformation-related terms (
b_{n}

;
Jia 2022b
. Therefore, the ratios and their centrality dependencies discussed above largely reflect impact parameter dependencies.

To remove the
a_{n}
terms, which have similar values in U+U and Au+Au, we calculate baseline-subtracted ratios
Jia 2022b
:

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}-\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}}{(\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}-\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}})\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}-\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}}}\;,

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}-\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(v_{2}^{2})_{\mathrm{U}}-\mathrm{var}(v_{2}^{2})_{\mathrm{Au}}}\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}-\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}}}.

(11)

This difference
\Delta\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{U}}-\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Au}}
cancels most spherical baseline contributions while isolating the deformation component:

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{b_{3}\left(\cos(3\gamma_{\mathrm{U}})\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}^{3}-\cos(3\gamma_{\mathrm{Au}})\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}^{3}\right)}{b_{1}\sqrt{b_{2}}(\beta_{2{\mathrm{U}}}^{2}-\beta_{2{\mathrm{Au}}}^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\overset{\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}\rightarrow 0}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}-\frac{b_{3}}{b_{1}\sqrt{b_{2}}}\cos(3\gamma_{\mathrm{U}})\;.

(12)

The second line shows that in the large
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
limit and ignoring
\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}
,
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}
depends only on uranium’s triaxiality. A liquid-drop model estimates the coefficients
-\frac{b_{3}}{b_{1}\sqrt{b_{2}}}=-\sqrt{10}/7

Jia 2022b
. In this limit,
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}=-0.76
assuming
(\beta_{2\mathrm{U}},\gamma_{\mathrm{U}})=(0.28,0^{\circ})
and
(\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}},\gamma_{\mathrm{Au}})=(0.14,45^{\circ})
.

The subtraction method is valid when the baseline difference is much smaller than the deformation contribution. This holds for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
over a wide centrality range, but only a limited range for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
(0–5%),
\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(v_{2}^{2})}
(0–10%), and
\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle
(0–20%), see Fig.
7
.

The interpretation of
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}
is more complex due to a more involved form for
\mathrm{var}(v_{2}^{2})
derived from Eq. (

). Assuming identical
a_{1}
,

,
c_{1}
and
d_{1}=2/7b_{1}^{2}
for the two collision systems:

\displaystyle\Delta\mathrm{var}(v_{2}^{2})=b_{1}^{2}(\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}^{2}-\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}^{2})(2a_{1}/b_{1}+5/7(\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}^{2}+\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}^{2}))\;,

(13)

we obtain:

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{b_{3}\left(\cos(3\gamma_{\mathrm{U}})\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}^{3}-\cos(3\gamma_{\mathrm{Au}})\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}^{3}\right)}{b_{1}\sqrt{b_{2}(2a_{1}/b_{1}+5/7(\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}^{2}+\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}^{2}))}(\beta_{2{\mathrm{U}}}^{2}-\beta_{2{\mathrm{Au}}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\overset{\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}\rightarrow 0}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}-\frac{b_{3}\beta_{2{\mathrm{U}}}}{b_{1}\sqrt{b_{2}(2a_{1}/b_{1}+5/7\beta_{2{\mathrm{U}}}^{2})}}\cos(3\gamma_{\mathrm{U}})\;.

(14)

The
a_{1}/b_{1}
term, affected by the spherical baseline, reduces the magnitude of
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}
relative to
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}

\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}
can be obtained over a wider centrality range because the centrality interval over which

7

Model comparisons

IV.1
Comparisons with initial-state and hydrodynamic models for
v_{n}
–
p_{\mathrm{T}}
correlations

The correlation between
v_{n}
and
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
arises from hydrodynamic responses to correlated fluctuations between elliptic eccentricity,
\varepsilon_{2}
, and the inverse of the transverse area,
d_{\perp}
. Most flow observables are influenced by both initial-state geometry and final-state QGP evolution.

Initial state model.
Models that consider only the initial state, such as the Glauber model, rely on linear response relations:
v_{n}\propto\varepsilon_{n}
and
\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}/p_{\mathrm{T}}\propto\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp}
. These relations yield estimators for
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc}}
in terms of eccentricity and
d_{\perp}

Schenke et al. 2020a
;
Bozek and Mehrabpour 2020
:

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right\rangle\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp})^{2}\right\rangle}}

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(\varepsilon_{2}^{2})}\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp})^{2}\right\rangle}}\;.

(15)

Similarly, ratios between U+U and Au+Au, mimicking
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
or
R_{\rho_{2}}
(Eq. (
3
)), as well as the subtracted forms to mimic
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}
and
\rho_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}
, can be formed.

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{2}^{\mathrm{sub}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta\left\langle\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp}\right\rangle}{\Delta\left\langle\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right\rangle\sqrt{\Delta\left\langle(\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp})^{2}\right\rangle}}

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{2}^{\mathrm{pcc,sub}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta\left\langle\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{\Delta\mathrm{var}(\varepsilon_{2}^{2})}\sqrt{\Delta\left\langle(\delta d_{\perp}/d_{\perp})^{2}\right\rangle}}\;.

(16)

Here,
\Delta\mathcal{O}
represents the difference of an observable
\mathcal{O}
between U+U and Au+Au collisions. For this study, we use the standard quark Glauber model
Loizides 2016
with deformation parameters from Ref.
Jia 2022b
. These normalized quantities serve as estimators for the corresponding ratios of final-state observables and do not capture final-state effects in the data (Fig.
11
).

IV.2
Quantitative constraints on Uranium deformation parameters

Having explored nuclear deformation’s influence on flow observables and established that U+U to Au+Au ratios exhibit the clearest signatures of deformation, we now perform a more quantitative, simultaneous extraction of
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}
. This is accomplished by using three observables
R_{v_{2}^{2}}
,
R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}
, and
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
together. If we ignore the modest deformation in the Au nucleus, Eq. (
2
) suggests:

\displaystyle R_{v_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{a_{\rm 1,U}}{a_{\rm 1,Au}}\left(1+\frac{b_{1}}{a_{1}}\beta_{\rm 2}^{2}\right)_{\rm U}\;,

\displaystyle R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{a_{\rm 2,U}}{a_{\rm 2,Au}}\left(1+\frac{b_{2}}{a_{2}}\beta_{\rm 2}^{2}\right)_{\rm U}\;,

\displaystyle R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{a_{\rm 3,U}}{a_{\rm 3,Au}}\left(1-\frac{b_{3}}{a_{3}}\beta_{\rm 2}^{3}\cos(3\gamma)\right)_{\rm U}\;

(17)

In the absence of deformation, these ratios approach
a_{n,\rm U}/a_{n,\rm Au}
, which are less than unity as
a_{n}
are dominated by random fluctuations and are larger in Au+Au collisions. Following Ref.
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
, we consider additional uncertainties for these ratios:

•

Non-flow estimate:
Non-flow estimates, representing physical correlations, are included in the measured data ratios as model-dependent contributions (see Section
II.3
).

•

Impact of viscosity:
Varying QGP shear and bulk viscosities by
\pm 50
% in IP-Glasma+MUSIC shows that while individual flow observables can change significantly, their ratios in U+U and Au+Au remain relatively stable (top panels of Fig.
27
). Half the maximum variation is included in the model uncertainty.

•

Impact of other structure parameters:
To derive uncertainties on
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}
, other Woods–Saxon inputs in Table
1
are varied: 1) Au deformation parameters are varied within
\beta_{2\mathrm{Au}}\approx 0.12
–0.14 and
\gamma_{\mathrm{Au}}\approx 37
–
53^{\circ}
according to Refs.

;

from 0 to 0.09. 3) The assumed nuclear radius
R_{0}
and nuclear skin

d_{\mathrm{min}}
are also varied. The impact of these variations on ratios of flow observables is shown in the bottom panels of Fig.

Overall, the extracted deformation parameters align with low-energy nuclear measurements under the rigid-rotor assumption

V
Summary and outlook

The correlation between elliptic flow (
v_{2}
) and event-by-event average transverse momentum (
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
) in high-energy heavy-ion collisions offers a unique sensitivity to the shape of the colliding nuclei, notably their quadrupole deformation (
\beta_{2}
) and triaxiality (
\gamma
). This sensitivity is robustly demonstrated by comparing collisions of strong prolate-deformed
238
U nuclei with weakly oblate-deformed
197
Au nuclei, focusing on three observables:
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle
, and
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
.

We construct a normalized quantity,
\rho_{2}=\frac{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle\sqrt{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}}
, which reduces sensitivity to final-state expansion dynamics. In 0–7% ultra-central U+U collisions,
\rho_{2}
exhibits a steep transition from positive to negative values, whereas it stays nearly constant in Au+Au collisions. Further suppression of residual final-state effects is achieved by using the ratio
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}=\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}/\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}
. This ratio shows a sharp decrease in central collisions, nearly independent of the correlation methods or
p_{\mathrm{T}}
selections. This provides direct evidence from the high-energy regime that
238
U possesses strong prolate deformation (large
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
) and small traxiality (
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}\sim 0^{\circ}
). Independent evidence for large
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
also comes from the strong rise of
R_{v_{2}^{2}}=\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}/\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}
and
R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}=\left\langle((\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}/\left\langle((\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}
in ultra-central collisions. In contrast, observables associated with higher-order harmonics (
v_{3}
and
v_{4}
), which are less affected by quadruple deformation, show much smaller differences between U+U and Au+Au systems.

The
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
data were compared with both a Glauber model, which accounts for the influence of nuclear deformation on initial collision conditions, and a state-of-the-art hydrodynamic model simulation (IP-Glasma+MUSIC), which incorporates final-state effects. Both models employ the Woods-Saxon parameterization for nuclear deformation. These models quantitatively reproduce the centrality dependence of
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
when
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}=0.28
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}=0^{\circ}
. In ultra-central collisions, where deformation effects are dominant, the IP-Glasma+MUSIC model yields
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}\approx 0.27
–0.30, while the Glauber model supports a triaxiality value of
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}\lesssim 15^{\circ}
.

For a more precise extraction of the uranium shape, we performed a joint fit of the hydrodynamic model to both
R_{(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}
data (mainly constraining
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
) and
R_{v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}
data (sensitive to both
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}
).By accounting for variations in medium evolution and initial-state parameters, and incorporating non-flow effects in the data, we determine
\beta_{2\mathrm{U}}=0.300\pm 0.016
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{U}}=8.3\pm 4.7^{\circ}
. These values are broadly consistent with low-energy estimates under the rigid-rotor assumption. This marks a robust extraction of the nuclear ground-state shape purely from high-energy collisions.

Observables involving higher-order flow harmonics, such as
\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle v_{4}^{2}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle v_{4}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
, are also sensitive to octopole
\beta_{3}
and hexadecapole
\beta_{4}
deformations. Our findings that
R_{v_{3}^{2}}=\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}/\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}>1
and
R_{v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}}=\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}/\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Au}}<1
in ultra-central collisions are consistent with the presence of a modest
\beta_{3\mathrm{U}}\sim 0.08
–0.09
Zhang et al. 2025
. This represents the first experimental evidence for octupole collectivity of
238
U using high-energy heavy-ion collisions. While a similar analysis was performed for
\beta_{4\mathrm{U}}
, the uncertainties in analogous ratios involving
\left\langle v_{4}^{2}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle v_{4}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
were too large for a definite statement.

The success of imaging nuclear shapes via high-energy heavy-ion collisions raises questions about the relationship between these short-timescale measurements (collision crossing time is
\lesssim 0.1
fm/
c
) and low-energy methods, which probe much longer timescales. It also raises issues about possible partonic modifications within highly boosted nuclei. Addressing these aspects is crucial to advance our understanding of the elusive initial-state stage of heavy-ion collisions. Further studies, including various hydrodynamic modeling frameworks and explicit treatment of longitudinal decorrelations, are necessary to refine this imaging technique even further.

Crucially, the performance of this method should be calibrated by comparing other pairs of isobar or isobar-like collision systems
Jia 2025
. Such calibration is vital for probing nuclear structure at high energies and for imposing stricter constraints on the QGP’s initial conditions, dynamics, and properties. With proper calibration, the high-energy “imaging-by-smashing” method opens a new frontier for studying the collective, many-body structure of atomic nuclei across the nuclide chart and various energy scales.

Many applications of this method are possible
Jia et al. 2024a
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
: 1) Probing octupole and hexadecapole deformations through precise measurements of high-order flow harmonics in central collisions (Results of
\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
indeed suggest presence of octupole deformation in Uranium nucleus
Zhang et al. 2025
). 2) Accessing the nuclear shape of odd-mass nuclei through comparative measurement with neighboring even-even nuclei, overcoming challenges in the
\gamma
-spectroscopy. 3) Using multi-particle correlations
Aaboud et al. 2020
;
Acharya et al. 2021
;
Sirunyan et al. 2020
to distinguish between average deformation and shape fluctuations
Dimri et al. 2023
, aiding in the understanding of shape-coexistence phenomena
Heyde and Wood 2011
. 4) Constraining the radial structure of nuclei, including the neutron skin
Li et al. 2020
, via ratios of bulk observables. These goals can be achieved by effectively leveraging collider facilities to collide carefully selected isobar species.

Classification of time series using information granules for efficient detection of unmanned aerial vehicles faults
Authors: Adam Kiersztyn, Paweł Karczmarek, Rafał Stegierski, Arkadiusz Syta, Bartłomiej Ambrożkiewicz, Kamil Jonak, Andrzej Koszewnik, Daniel Ołdziej, Paweł Dzienis, Kristina Bazienė, Justinas Gargasas, Patrycja Miazek, Konrad Smoliński
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractClassification of time series data is of significant importance across various domains. In particular, an important issue is to ensure the proper operation of vehicles such as drones. In this study, we propose a novel approach that utilizes the paradigm of information granules for time series classification. The proposed methodology transforms consecutive time windows of the original vibration signal into information granules, which are compact statistical summaries describing the signal dynamics within each segment. These granules serve as the input for machine learning classifiers and provide a more interpretable and noise-robust representation of the data. The proposed method is tested on data collected during the operation of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), demonstrating its effectiveness. Experimental results indicate that the classification accuracy significantly improves when using information granules compared to raw data. In this study, the classification task involves distinguishing five classes corresponding to different rotor fault levels (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% faulty rotors). This allows for precise identification of UAV operational health. In the case of the Tree Ensemble method, classification accuracy increased from 57.5% (raw data) to 100% (using information granules from four combined time windows), highlighting the substantial enhancement achieved through the proposed approach. Additionally, the study explores the impact of different time window lengths and training set sizes on classification performance, providing insights into optimizing the proposed method for practical applications.Keywords: Time series classification, Information granules, Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), Machine learning, Fault detectionSubject terms: Aerospace engineering, Computer science, Scientific data

Introduction

Classification of time series data is a problem widely discussed in the scientific community. Due to the presence of time series in various areas of life, it remains a highly important and actively researched topic. There are many approaches to classifying time series. In recent years, techniques based on machine learning have gained prominence due to their effectiveness, accessibility, and ease of implementation.

Especially in the last decade, numerous studies have been devoted to time series classification. Most of them proposed deep neural networks. In the work1, the authors used recurrent networks to divide the input time series into different classes. The convolutional network framework was used, among others, in2. The latest considerations regarding deep neural networks focus, among others, on the issues of transfer learning3, densely knowledge-aware networks4, graph-based models5, multi-feature based network6, and many other approaches. The interested reader can find detailed literature reviews in the works7 or8. In addition to methods based on neural networks, more classical approaches are being developed, such as those related to dynamic time warping (DTW)9, DTW and clustering10, Z-time algorithm11, fuzzy cognitive maps12,13, fuzzy rules14, hybrid fuzzy-deep learning approaches15 or, finally, granular descriptions of time series16.

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have gained broad acceptance in civilian and commercial contexts, including emergency response, maritime exploration, farming, traffic oversight, building, and supply chain management17–21. In terms of fault identification, examining small UAVs offers clear benefits, such as reduced risk, shorter inspection time, and higher cost efficiency. Recently, there has been an increasing interest in using vibration-based techniques for fault detection in helicopters22,23. For instance, researchers have successfully applied vibroacoustic techniques to detect faults in the gear transmission systems of military helicopters24. Similarly, vibration signals have been employed to diagnose faults in planetary gear mechanisms25. Recognizing the operational similarities between multirotor UAVs and helicopters, recent studies have proposed tailored improvements to diagnostic models for multirotor UAVs26. This research contributes to enhancing the reliability and safety of UAV operations.

Analyzing vibroacoustic signals plays a crucial role in detecting drone faults27,28, serving as a non-intrusive and efficient diagnostic approach. By monitoring changes in the characteristics of these signals in both the time and frequency domains, it becomes possible to identify irregularities or deviations that may indicate potential faults or malfunctions, including propeller damage, engine defects, or instability of the launch platform. The application of advanced sensors and signal processing techniques enables real-time monitoring of UAV conditions, supports proactive maintenance, and reduces the likelihood of critical failures. Fault detection based on vibroacoustic signals enhances the reliability, safety, and overall performance of drones, contributing to the continued development of UAV technology and its diverse applications, ranging from surveillance and logistics to environmental monitoring.

Machine learning (ML) algorithms play a significant role in diagnosing unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) by leveraging both short- and long-term correlations in signals to detect and predict potential faults29. Through advanced analysis of sensor data, machine learning algorithms can identify irregularities in UAV operations, aiding in the early detection and prevention of mechanical or operational issues30,31. By discerning patterns that are not immediately apparent in the data, these algorithms can also support predictive maintenance32, indicating emerging problems and thereby reducing downtime while improving overall reliability. The integration of machine learning with UAV diagnostics enables more efficient and proactive maintenance strategies, enhancing the dependability and operational safety of unmanned aircraft. The data used in this approach are obtained from discretely sampled and denoised analog signals corresponding to the voltage and current inputs of four UAV actuators. Another study focuses on diagnosing faults in brushless DC (BLDC) motors of UAVs33. Also, fuzzy techniques have recently been used effectively to detect the UAV faults, see, for instance,34. These include neuro-fuzzy systems35, fuzzy logic and data fusion approaches34, and, finally, type-2 fuzzy sets36. The concept of information granules16 has been applied in various domains to handle imprecision and uncertainty37–41. Despite its potential, applications in UAV diagnostics remain scarce. Compared with existing UAV fault detection techniques, which often rely on handcrafted features or deep learning models trained on raw vibration or IMU data42–46, the proposed method introduces an intermediate representation based on information granules. This representation captures the temporal behavior of vibration signals within multiple overlapping time windows, enabling efficient feature extraction, lower noise sensitivity, and improved interpretability. Consequently, the proposed approach provides a novel balance between data-driven learning and model transparency, extending beyond conventional classification schemes based on raw data.

Our proposed approach is based on the information granules paradigm. Time series analysis involves transforming a time window of a given length into an information granule. Then, based on the appropriately constructed information granules, classification is performed using various machine learning approaches. Each information granule is constructed using classical statistical measures describing the distribution of the analyzed variables. The proposed solution has been thoroughly tested in a series of comprehensive experiments.

The proposed approach based on information granules introduces a novel representation of time series that captures both local and global signal dynamics. By employing statistical descriptors of time windows, the method effectively reduces noise and enhances interpretability when compared with traditional feature extraction or frequency-domain approaches. Moreover, it offers lower computational cost, which makes it suitable for implementation in UAV diagnostic systems. However, the current version of the method has been evaluated in offline mode and has not yet been adapted for real-time processing. Its performance also depends on the type and placement of the piezoelectric sensors, which may influence the quality of the acquired signals. These limitations define natural directions for future research, including integration with edge computing platforms and validation across different UAV types and mission profiles. With such extensions, the proposed approach could become a universal and efficient tool for UAV fault detection.

Compared with existing UAV fault detection techniques, the proposed approach introduces a distinct and interpretable representation of vibration signals. Traditional methods often rely on the direct analysis of raw vibration or IMU data, where features are handcrafted in the time or frequency domains (e.g., FFT-based descriptors, spectral energy, or cepstral coefficients). Other approaches employ deep learning architectures such as convolutional or recurrent neural networks that automatically learn features from raw sensor streams. While effective, these methods typically require large datasets, high computational power, and they provide limited interpretability of the learned internal representations.

In contrast, our method introduces an intermediate level of abstraction by transforming consecutive time windows of the signal into compact statistical summaries, referred to as information granules. This step captures both the local and global dynamics of UAV vibrations and, at the same time, reduces noise sensitivity and computational cost. As a result, the proposed approach combines the transparency and efficiency of classical feature-based techniques with the accuracy typically associated with modern machine learning models. This conceptual balance between interpretability and data-driven learning constitutes the main novelty of the present work.

Compared with other UAV fault detection methods, the proposed approach offers several important advantages. Many existing studies rely on handcrafted features extracted directly from vibration or IMU data using Fourier or wavelet transforms, or they employ deep learning architectures trained on large volumes of raw data. Although these techniques can achieve high accuracy, they typically require extensive preprocessing, considerable computational resources, and large annotated datasets. Our method introduces an intermediate representation layer based on information granules, which compresses the data while preserving essential temporal characteristics. This representation is simple, interpretable, and computationally efficient, allowing for high accuracy even with moderate dataset sizes. In contrast to deep neural models, which often operate as black boxes, the proposed approach provides a transparent and lightweight framework suitable for real-time diagnostic applications.

Experimental setup

The research object was an 8-armed UAV equipped with 8 brushless direct current (BLDC) propulsion motors (see Fig. 1, each coupled directly to a 15x5.5 propeller and controlled individually by the Pixhawk Cube autopilot with a 50 Hz Pulse-Width Modulation (PWM) signal transmitted by the Hobbywing 40A electronic speed controller (ESC). The drone platform was constructed from carbon composites (central plate) and aluminum (arms). The power source consisted of a nominal battery consisting of 6 lithium-polymer electric cells with a combined voltage of 22.2V. The measurement included two MicroFiber Composite (MFC) 8528P2 piezoelectric sensors mounted on one of the drone’s arms in two positions: lateral and upper, as illustrated in Fig. 2. On the same arm, the PWM signal was varied to activate the motor with 5 different levels, using an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU), which allowed the simulation of propulsion unit faults at varying severity levels. The weight of the octocopter in a ready-to-fly state, i.e., with control and measurement equipment (200g) and battery (1470g), was 7125g (see Table 1).

The experimental procedure was carried out using a suspended octocopter UAV operating in flight. The desired rotor speeds were established using an RC transmitter and a Pixhawk autopilot, which generated appropriate 5V PWM signals for the eight BLDC motors. Once the hover state was achieved, a DC-DC solid-state relay and a waveform generator controlled by a laptop introduced a PWM signal with varying duty cycles to simulate different damage levels on motor DA1. A decrease in the PWM duty cycle increased the severity of the simulated fault (e.g., a 25% duty cycle simulated a 75% fault). This process lasted approximately 5–6 seconds, after which the UAV was safely landed. Each damage level was tested in four additional repetitions to ensure robustness. The mechanical state of the UAV arm was monitored using two MFC8528 piezoelectric sensors, while an IMU sensor separately recorded vibration accelerations (not analyzed in this study). Voltage signals from the piezo sensors were captured via a USB-6531 data acquisition board mounted on the UAV.

The dataset of 52,430 samples was generated by performing repeated flight tests for each simulated fault level in a controlled environment. Each of the five classes (representing different levels of simulated rotor faults: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%) included 10,486 individual samples. The tests were conducted using a consistent experimental setup, ensuring repeatability and data quality. While the fault levels were simulated by adjusting the PWM duty cycle (as described in our methodology), the flight tests themselves involved real UAV operation, capturing the natural variability of vibrations and dynamic conditions inherent in drone missions. This approach ensures that the dataset reflects both realistic operational dynamics and well-controlled experimental conditions. The tests were performed with the UAV operating in hover flight, not in a stationary mode on the ground. The simulated rotor faults were introduced dynamically during flight. Using Waveforms software and an Analog Discovery 2 DAQ card connected to a laptop, a PWM signal with reduced duty cycle was injected into one of the phases of motor DA1 through a DC-DC solid-state relay. A decrease in the PWM duty cycle (e.g., 25%) simulated a higher fault severity (e.g., 75% fault). Each damage level was tested for 5−6 seconds and repeated five times to ensure data robustness. The drone was then safely landed using the RC transmitter. During this process, vibration signals were captured from two MFC8528 piezoelectric sensors mounted laterally and vertically on the affected arm. The IMU recorded accelerations but was not used for PWM injection or analysis. The data acquisition system operated with a sampling frequency of 10kHz, and each observation corresponds to approximately 10 seconds of flight. In total, the dataset contains 52,430 labeled samples obtained from five repeated flights per damage level. All signals were denoised and normalized before feature extraction to eliminate noise and amplitude bias, ensuring consistency and reproducibility across all measurements.

Results of numerical experiments

Baseline classification on raw data

The performance of the proposed, innovative time series classification method was tested on data describing the operation of drones. Based on the readings of the selected two parameters, the percentage of rotors operating correctly is determined. Membership in one of five categories is verified. For each of the five categories, 10,486 observations were determined to form a time series. Each time, 10 seconds of drone operation were recorded. The total number of observations was 52,430. A simple graphical comparison of the results for two extremely different classes (see Figs. 4 and 5) allows us to determine the existence of significant differences between the readings. Therefore, it seems possible to classify the time series.

Visualization of signals when all engines are operational.

Visualization of signals in case all engines are faulty.

It may seem that a straightforward classification could be performed based on the reading at a given moment in time. In order to verify this hypothesis, typical classification techniques available in the KNIME Analytics Platform were used47. Among the available techniques, based on previous experience48, the following classification methods were selected: Decision Tree (DT)49, Naive Bayes Classifier (NB)50, MultiLayer Preceptron (MLP)51, Gradient Bosseted Tree (GBT)52, Fuzzy Rule Classifier (FR)53, Random Forest (RF)54, Tree Ensamble (TE)55.

A brief description of the considered classifiers is provided below. All models were implemented using the default settings available in the KNIME Analytic Platform. The Decision Tree (DT) algorithm builds a hierarchical structure in which each internal node splits the data based on the value of a selected feature. Despite its simplicity and interpretability, the DT model is prone to overfitting, especially in the presence of noisy input data. The Naive Bayes (NB) classifier applies Bayes’ theorem under the assumption of feature independence. It is efficient and effective for certain types of data, but performs suboptimally when correlations between input variables are strong.

The MultiLayer Perceptron (MLP) is a type of feedforward neural network capable of modeling nonlinear relationships. It consists of at least one hidden layer and uses backpropagation to adjust weights. Although powerful, its effectiveness depends on the structure of the network and training configuration. The Gradient Boosted Tree (GBT) is an ensemble technique in which each new tree attempts to correct the errors made by the previous ones. It is robust to overfitting and well-suited for structured data.

The Fuzzy Rule (FR) classifier builds a set of fuzzy if then rules derived from the data, which allows for smooth decision boundaries. It is particularly useful when dealing with uncertainty and imprecise data. The Random Forest (RF) classifier constructs multiple decision trees trained on bootstrapped subsets and performs classification by majority voting. Its ensemble nature ensures high robustness and good generalization.

The Tree Ensemble (TE) method represents a generic ensemble of decision trees, combining bagging and boosting strategies. In our experiments, this method achieved the highest classification performance, benefiting from multiple perspectives introduced by combined statistical descriptors from different time windows. The available data were randomly divided into a training set and a test set. 90% of the samples were assigned to the training set. Classification using information granules

To understand the differences in classification performance across the seven machine learning models, we analyzed the characteristics of each algorithm. Ensemble-based methods such as Random Forest, Gradient Boosted Trees, and Tree Ensemble consistently outperformed others due to their robust handling of variance and effective use of multiple decision trees. These models benefit from internal feature selection and aggregation, which helps stabilize results and improve generalization. In contrast, simpler models like Naive Bayes and Fuzzy Rule classifiers showed lower performance, likely because they lack the capacity to capture complex nonlinear interactions within the aggregated features.

This poor performance of raw signal classification can be attributed to several factors. First, the raw sensor signals exhibit significant noise and fluctuations, which can obscure relevant patterns and reduce class separability. Second, single-moment readings do not capture the temporal dynamics of UAV behavior, which are crucial for accurately distinguishing fault conditions. Finally, raw signals inherently lack the statistical aggregation present in information granules, further reducing their descriptive power. These factors highlight the advantage of our proposed approach, which aggregates temporal data and enhances class separability.

Therefore, it seems reasonable to carry out the classification based on the proposed method using information granules for time windows. The selection of time-windows lengths (K=25, 75, 125, 250) was initially guided by heuristic considerations informed by our experience with UAV flight dynamics and data sampling characteristics. These window lengths were chosen to represent a range of temporal granularities, capturing both short-term fluctuations and longer-term patterns in the signals. Following this heuristic selection, we performed extensive experiments to validate the effectiveness of these windows, as shown in Table 4 and Fig. 8. Although a formal hyperparameter search (e.g., grid or Bayesian optimization) was not employed in this study, our results across multiple window lengths and their combinations demonstrate that these choices are appropriate for robust classification performance. Due to the nature of the analyzed data and the sampling frequency, the initial length of the time window was initially set at K = 25. After replacing the raw data with information granules, the classification presented in Fig. 7 was obtained.

This procedure allowed us to assess the sensitivity of the proposed approach to data partitioning and to confirm whether the very high classification accuracy remained consistent across multiple repetitions.

For every experiment, standard performance measures such as precision, recall, F1-score, accuracy, and the Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) were calculated. Influence of training set size

The superior performance of the Tree Ensemble method can be attributed to its ensemble nature, which leverages multiple decision trees to reduce variance and improve generalization. This structure inherently minimizes the risk of overfitting, even in the presence of high-dimensional feature spaces generated by information granules. The random selection of features and bootstrapped samples within the ensemble further contributes to robust and stable predictions. Our extensive use of 10-fold independent testing and varying training set sizes also supports the validity of these results, reducing the chance that near-perfect accuracy arises purely from overfitting.

Conclusions

Summary of results

The proposed time-series classification method utilizing information granules presents highly promising results for fault detection in UAVs. By converting time windows into information granules derived from statistical descriptors, the approach significantly enhances classification accuracy compared to directly using raw sensor data. Experimental results clearly demonstrate that employing information granules leads to significant improvements in classification performance across various classical machine learning techniques.

The effectiveness in terms of the accuracy measure of the method was evaluated using data from UAV operations, where the goal was to determine the percentage of rotors operating correctly. The method’s performance was compared with traditional classification approaches using raw data. The results show that classification based on single readings from raw data yielded unsatisfactory accuracy levels, with significant confusion between different fault categories.

However, when applying the proposed method with information granules, classification accuracy increased substantially across all tested classification techniques. Notably, the effectiveness of classification increased with the length of the time window used to construct information granules. Combining information from multiple time windows further enhanced classification accuracy, indicating the method’s robustness and scalability.

Moreover, the size of the training set was found to impact classification performance, with larger training sets leading to higher accuracy levels. Even with a modest training set size, the proposed method achieves impressive classification accuracy, underscoring its potential for practical applications.

Limitations of the current study

Despite the promising performance of the proposed approach, several implementation challenges and limitations should be acknowledged. The current implementation was validated offline on a specific UAV platform under controlled laboratory conditions, and it has not been yet adapted for real-time onboard processing. Furthermore, the method’s effectiveness depends on the type and placement of piezoelectric sensors, which may influence the acquired signal characteristics. Future work will therefore focus on extending the method toward real-time implementation on embedded or edge computing platforms, integrating additional sensing modalities (e.g., IMU, acoustic, or current signals), and validating the approach across different UAV types and flight missions. These efforts will contribute to developing a versatile and reliable diagnostic framework for practical UAV applications.

Future research directions

Overall, the results suggest that leveraging the information granules paradigm for time series classification holds promise for enhancing fault detection and improving the reliability and safety of UAV operations.

Further research could explore optimization strategies and real-world deployment scenarios to fully exploit the method’s capabilities in UAV maintenance and monitoring systems. Moreover, it is worth incorporating the information granules into the neural network-based approaches. Also, one of the interesting ways of future development paths is to combine various methods using simple and fast techniques of aggregation of classification results. It may be helpful to work with a few fast methods, which can be less effective individually, but may produce good results when aggregated into one model.

From the practical point of view, the proposed approach can also be integrated into real-time UAV monitoring or onboard diagnostic systems. The transformation of vibration signals into statistical information granules involves only simple arithmetic operations (mean and standard deviation), which makes the process computationally efficient and well-suited for real-time or near-real-time execution. The classification stage, based on tree ensemble methods, is equally lightweight and can operate with very low latency even on embedded edge devices. These properties make it possible to implement the proposed framework on compact UAV hardware equipped with controllers or edge-computing modules such as Raspberry Pi, STM32, or NVIDIA Jetson. Furthermore, the structure of the algorithm allows for easy parallelization and extension to multiple sensors or modalities, enabling scalability without substantial computational overhead. In future implementations, the framework could be directly integrated with the UAV’s flight controller or onboard health management system to support autonomous fault detection and adaptive control strategies in real missions. This shows that the method is not only accurate but also practical and scalable for onboard deployment in real-world UAV applications.

Future research directions include extending the proposed approach by integrating information granules with deep learning techniques, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or transformer-based architectures. This could combine the benefits of granular statistical summaries with powerful sequence modeling capabilities. Moreover, incorporating more sophisticated granulation methods (e.g., fuzzy or rough sets) could further improve the descriptive power of the time series representations. Another promising avenue is the development of an ensemble of fast classifiers to leverage the complementary strengths of simpler models. In addition, validating the method on a wider range of UAV platforms and including more complex, real-world mission scenarios would help to generalize the findings and ensure robust deployment in diverse environments. Finally, online or real-time implementations of the proposed classification method would enable immediate UAV fault diagnosis and intervention in dynamic operational settings. Future work will focus on integrating the proposed granulation-based representation with deep learning architectures, such as convolutional and recurrent neural networks. A promising direction includes the use of attention-based models (e.g., Transformers), which may benefit from the reduced input dimensionality provided by granules. Moreover, hyperparameter optimization techniques (e.g., grid or Bayesian search) will be applied systematically to further refine window lengths and classifier settings. These extensions aim to validate the method in real-time scenarios and broaden its applicability across diverse UAV platforms and fault types.

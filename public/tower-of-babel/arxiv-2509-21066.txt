A Riemannian Variational and Spectral Framework for High-Dimensional Sphere Packing: Barrier–Dynamics Reconciliation, Periodic Rigidity, and Discrete-Time Guarantees
Faruk Alpay, Hamdi Alakkad

Abstract
We reconcile a barrier-based geometric model of sphere packings with a provably convergent discrete-time dynamics and provide full technical development of key theorems. Specifically, we (i) introduce a C 2 C^{2} interior barrier potential U ν U_{\nu} that is compatible with a strict-feasibility safeguard and has Lipschitz gradient on the iterates’ domain; (ii) correct and formalize the discrete update previously denoted as Eq. (12); (iii) prove barrier-to-KKT consistency and state (and prove) a corollary explaining the role of the quadratic term in U ν U_{\nu} (Section 4 ); (iv) prove that strict prestress stability implies periodic infinitesimal rigidity (Section 5 ); (v) establish discrete-time Lyapunov descent and projected descent (with feasibility projection integrated into the proof) and local linear convergence for the Spectral-Projected Interior Trajectory method ( Spit ) under explicit, non-circular step-size/damping rules (Section 6 ); (vi) clarify B B -updates and give an ℰ \mathcal{E} -nonexpansive joint solver (Sec. 6.1 – 6.1 ); and (vii) include minimal schematic illustrations and a reproducibility stub (Sec. 7 ). We emphasize theoretical rigor and mathematical proofs; empirical evaluation is deferred.

Configuration, Constraints, and
C^{2}
Interior Barrier

Let
\Lambda
be a full-rank lattice with basis
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and volume
V(\Lambda)=|\det B|

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}
be sphere centers modulo

For
i<j
and
t\in\Lambda

s_{ij,t}(x,B):=\|x_{i}-x_{j}-t\|^{2}-4\qquad(\text{feasible iff }s_{ij,t}\geq 0).

(1)

We minimize
V(B)
subject to
s_{ij,t}\geq 0

Standing assumptions and gauge (global, non-circular)

(A1)

Gauge fixing.
We work modulo translations/rotations by fixing
\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}=0
and treating rigidity modulo the space
\mathsf{T}=\{(u,A):u_{i}=c+\Omega x_{i},\ A=\Omega,\ \Omega^{\top}=-\Omega,\ c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

(A2)

Finite shift set.
There exists
R<\infty

such that if a pair contributes to
U_{\nu}
, then

with

on the safeguarded domain.

(A3)

Cell nondegeneracy.
There are constants

with

\underline{\sigma}\,\mathrm{Id}\preceq B^{\top}B\preceq\overline{\sigma}\,\mathrm{Id}
on the feasible set.

(A4)

Strict interior initialization.
The initial
(x^{0},B^{0})

6.1

(A5)

Existence for barrier subproblems.

\Phi_{\nu}
in (

(A6)

Regularity at the limit.
At any limit point
(x^{\star},B^{\star})
of
\Phi_{\nu_{k}}

\nu_{k}\downarrow 0

(A7)

Connectivity (spectral).
When spectral quantities are used, the contact graph is connected.

2.1
Interior barrier compatible with dynamics

Fix parameters
\nu>0
(barrier strength) and
\delta\in(0,1)
(safety margin). Define a
C^{2}

interior
barrier on the strict-feasibility slab

U_{\nu}(x,B):=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{S}}\phi_{\nu}\bigl(s_{ij,t}(x,B)\bigr),\qquad\phi_{\nu}(s):=-\nu\log s+\frac{\nu}{2\delta}\,(s-\delta)^{2}\quad(s>0).

(2)

The term
-\nu\log s
enforces strict feasibility; the quadratic term makes
U_{\nu}\in C^{2}
and strongly convex near
s=\delta
.

(i,j,t)

\|x_{i}-x_{j}-t\|\leq R

L_{\text{lip}}=L_{\text{lip}}(\nu,\delta,S,R)
such that

\|\nabla U_{\nu}(\xi)-\nabla U_{\nu}(\zeta)\|\leq L_{\text{lip}}\|\xi-\zeta\|

Barrier Consistency and Interior Variant

Consider the log-barrier family on the strict interior:

\Phi_{\nu}(x,B)=V(B)-\nu\sum_{i<j}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{S}}\log s_{ij,t}(x,B).

(4)

Theorem 4.1

(Log-barrier
\Rightarrow
KKT)
.

Suppose
\nu_{k}\downarrow 0
and minimizers
(x_{\nu_{k}},B_{\nu_{k}})\to(x^{\star},B^{\star})
exist, and LICQ holds for the active set
\mathcal{A}
at
(x^{\star},B^{\star})
. Then
(x^{\star},B^{\star})
satisfies the KKT conditions for
\min V(B)
s.t.
s_{ij,t}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Deferred to Appendix
A.2
.
∎

Corollary 4.2

(Interior
U_{\nu}
has the same KKT limit)
.

Let
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(x,B):=V(B)+U_{\nu}(x,B)
, and let
(x_{\nu},B_{\nu})
be a minimizer of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\nu}
. Under (A2)–(A6), any accumulation point
(x^{\star},B^{\star})
of
\{(x_{\nu},B_{\nu})\}
as
\nu\downarrow 0
satisfies the KKT conditions of the original problem. In particular, with

\mu^{(\nu)}_{ij,t}\ :=\ -\phi_{\nu}^{\prime}\!\bigl(s_{ij,t}(x_{\nu},B_{\nu})\bigr)\ =\ \frac{\nu}{s_{ij,t}(x_{\nu},B_{\nu})}\ -\ \nu\,\frac{s_{ij,t}(x_{\nu},B_{\nu})-\delta}{\delta},

the sequence
\mu^{(\nu)}\rightharpoonup\mu\geq 0
(after extraction), and the limiting multipliers satisfy KKT.

Proof.

Set
s_{i}=s_{ij,t}(x_{\nu},B_{\nu})
to simplify notation and write
\phi^{\prime}=\phi_{\nu}^{\prime}
. First-order optimality for
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\nu}
reads

\nabla_{B}V(B_{\nu})\ +\ \sum_{i}\phi^{\prime}(s_{i})\,\nabla_{B}s_{i}\ =\ 0,\qquad\sum_{i}\phi^{\prime}(s_{i})\,\nabla_{x}s_{i}\ =\ 0.

Define
\mu_{i}^{(\nu)}:=-\phi^{\prime}(s_{i})=\nu/s_{i}-\nu(s_{i}-\delta)/\delta
. Then the above is equivalent to

\nabla_{B}V(B_{\nu})\ -\ \sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{(\nu)}\,\nabla_{B}s_{i}\ =\ 0,\qquad\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{(\nu)}\,\nabla_{x}s_{i}\ =\ 0.

Partition indices into active-at-the-limit
\mathcal{A}
and inactive-at-the-limit
\mathcal{I}
: by continuity, there exists
\sigma>0
such that
s_{i}\geq\sigma
for
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
\nu
small enough, hence
\mu_{i}^{(\nu)}\to 0
for
i\in\mathcal{I}
. For
i\in\mathcal{A}
, boundedness of
\{\mu^{(\nu)}\}
follows from LICQ: introduce

\mathsf{J}(x,B):=\bigl[\,\{\nabla_{x}s_{i}(x,B)\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\ \ \ \ \{\nabla_{B}s_{i}(x,B)\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\,\bigr].

LICQ implies
\sigma_{\min}(\mathsf{J}(x^{\star},B^{\star}))>0
, hence in a neighborhood
\|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\mu_{i}^{(\nu)}[\nabla_{x}s_{i};\nabla_{B}s_{i}]\|\geq\underline{\sigma}\,\|\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{(\nu)}\|
. Taking norms and using boundedness of
\nabla V,\nabla s_{i}
yields
\|\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{(\nu)}\|\leq C
for some
C
independent of
\nu
. Extract a convergent subsequence
\mu^{(\nu)}\to\mu\geq 0
; pass to the limit to obtain KKT stationarity and complementarity (inactive
\Rightarrow

\mu_{i}=0
, active
\Rightarrow

s_{i}\to 0
).
∎

5
Periodic Rigidity

Notation and trivial motions (disambiguation).

On the active set
\mathcal{A}
, write
r_{ij,t}=x_{i}-x_{j}-t
and
n_{ij,t}=r_{ij,t}/\|r_{ij,t}\|
.
We
define

A\ :=\ \dot{B}\,B^{-1}\quad\text{(cell velocity/strain rate; dimensionless)}.

A periodic infinitesimal motion is a pair
(u,A)
with
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfying

r_{ij,t}^{\top}\bigl(u_{i}-u_{j}-A\,r_{ij,t}\bigr)=0\qquad\forall(i,j,t)\in\mathcal{A}.

(5)

Trivial (Euclidean) motions form the subspace

\mathsf{T}\ :=\ \bigl\{\,(u,A):\ u_{i}=c+\Omega x_{i},\ A=\Omega,\ \Omega^{\top}=-\Omega,\ c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\bigr\}.

Definition 5.1

(Prestress and stress energy)
.

An
equilibrium stress
is a set
\omega=\{\omega_{ij,t}\}_{\mathcal{A}}
with
\omega_{ij,t}=\omega_{ji,-t}
. Its quadratic form on motions is

Q_{\omega}(u,A)\ :=\ \sum_{(i,j,t)\in\mathcal{A}}\omega_{ij,t}\,\bigl(n_{ij,t}^{\top}(u_{i}-u_{j}-A\,r_{ij,t})\bigr)^{2}.

(6)

We say the framework is
strictly prestress stable
if there exists an equilibrium stress
\omega
with
Q_{\omega}
positive definite on the subspace
\{(u,A):\eqref{eq:inf}\text{ holds}\}/\mathsf{T}
.

Theorem 5.2

(Prestress stability
\Rightarrow
periodic rigidity)
.

If the framework is strictly prestress stable, then it is periodically infinitesimally rigid; i.e., any
(u,A)
satisfying (
5
) is trivial modulo
\mathsf{T}
.

Proof.

By strict prestress stability,
Q_{\omega}
is positive definite on the nontrivial motion subspace; hence the only motion satisfying (
5
) with
Q_{\omega}=0
is trivial (modulo
\mathsf{T}
). See
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.
∎

Remark 5.3

(KKT multipliers versus strict prestress stability)
.

At a KKT point with strictly positive contact multipliers
\mu_{ij,t}>0
on
\mathcal{A}
,
\omega=\mu
is an equilibrium stress. However, positivity of multipliers alone does not imply
Q_{\omega}\succ 0
on nontrivial motions; certification requires a constrained spectral check or SOSC-type curvature that rules out nontrivial flats.

Discrete-Time Dynamics and the
Spit
method

We evolve
x
(and optionally
B
) by a damped velocity-Verlet scheme on the interior barrier
U_{\nu}
.
We refer to the resulting algorithm as the
Spectral-Projected Interior Trajectory
method (
Spit
): a barrier-driven dynamics with feasibility projection and optional spectral nudges.

Let
\eta>0
be damping and
\Delta t>0
a time step.
The corrected update is:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} v^{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=v^{k}-\frac{\eta\Delta t}{2}\,v^{k}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\,\nabla U_{\nu}(x^{k},B^{k}),\\
x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=x^{k}+\Delta t\,v^{k+\frac{1}{2}},\\
v^{k+1}&=\left(1-\frac{\eta\Delta t}{2}\right)v^{k+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\,\nabla U_{\nu}(x^{k+\frac{1}{2}},B^{k}),\\
x^{k+1}&=x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{(before projection)}.\end{aligned}}

(12)

Assumption 6.1

(Strict-feasibility safeguard)
.

There exists
\delta\in(0,1)
and a finite neighbor set
\mathcal{S}
such that all iterates satisfy
s_{ij,t}(x^{k},B^{k})\geq\delta
for all
(i,j,t)
used in
U_{\nu}
; if needed, we project minimally along contact normals to enforce
s\geq\delta
after each step.

Assumption 6.2

(Local smoothness)
.

On the safeguarded set,
\|\nabla U_{\nu}(x,B)-\nabla U_{\nu}(y,B)\|\leq L_{\text{lip}}\|x-y\|
.

Proposition 6.3

(Discrete (unprojected) Lyapunov descent)
.

Under Assumptions
6.1
–
6.2
, if

0<\eta\Delta t<2\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{\text{lip}}\,\Delta t^{2}\ \leq\ \tfrac{1}{2},

(7)

then with

\mathcal{E}^{k}:=U_{\nu}(x^{k},B^{k})+\tfrac{1}{2}\|v^{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2},\qquad\gamma=\tfrac{1}{\Delta t^{2}}-\tfrac{L_{\text{lip}}}{2}\ \ (\geq 0\text{ by }\eqref{eq:explicit-bounds}),

(8)

we have
\mathcal{E}(x^{k+1},v^{k+1})\leq\mathcal{E}(x^{k},v^{k})

before
feasibility projection.

6.1
Feasibility projection: energy bounds and an
\mathcal{E}
-nonexpansive variant

Let
(x,v,B)
denote the tentative state after (

(x^{+},B^{+})

A general bound.

Define the composite gradient

\bar{g}_{x}(x,B):=\nabla_{x}U_{\nu}(x,B)+\gamma\,(x-x^{k-1}).

(9)

For any spatial displacement
d_{x}

\mathcal{E}(x+d_{x},v,B)-\mathcal{E}(x,v,B)\ \leq\ \left\langle\bar{g}_{x}(x,B),d_{x}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{\text{lip}}+\gamma}{2}\,\|d_{x}\|^{2}.

(10)

\mathcal{E}
-nonexpansive projection (x-only).

Let
C_{\delta}:=\{y:\ s_{ij,t}(y,B)\geq\delta\ \forall(i,j,t)\}

Project onto the linearized feasible set

x^{+}\ \in\ \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{y\in\tilde{C}_{\delta}(x,B)}M_{x,B}(y),\quad\tilde{C}_{\delta}(x,B):=\bigl\{y:\ s_{ij,t}(x,B)+\left\langle\nabla_{x}s_{ij,t}(x,B),y-x\right\rangle\ \geq\ \delta\ \ \forall(i,j,t)\bigr\}.

(11)

Then

\mathcal{E}(x^{+},v,B)\ \leq\ M_{x,B}(x^{+})+\tfrac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}\ \leq\ M_{x,B}(x)+\tfrac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}\ =\ \mathcal{E}(x,v,B).

(12)

\mathcal{E}
-nonexpansive

When updating

8
Limitations and Counterpoints

We emphasize mathematical guarantees and do not report measured experiments in this manuscript. Spectral indicators stabilize dynamics but do not certify geometric optimality; barrier modeling is analytical and must be paired with feasibility safeguards; discrete guarantees are local and require smoothness on a strict-feasibility neighborhood.

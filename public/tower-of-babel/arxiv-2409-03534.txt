Extended symmetry of higher Painlevé equations of even periodicity and their rational solutions
H. Aratyn, J.F. Gomes, G.V. Lobo, A.H. Zimerman

Abstract
The structure of extended affine Weyl symmetry group of higher Painleve equations of N N periodicity depends on whether N N is even or odd. We find that for even N N , the symmetry group A ^ N − 1 ( 1 ) {\widehat{A}}^{(1)}_{N-1} contains the conventional Bäcklund transformations s j , j = 1 , … , N s_{j},j=1,{\ldots},N , the group of automorphisms consisting of cycling permutations but also reflections on a periodic circle of N N points, which is a novel feature uncovered in this paper. The presence of reflection automorphisms is connected to existence of degenerated solutions and for N = 4 N=4 we explicitly show how the reflection automorphisms around even points cause degeneracy of a class of rational solutions obtained on the orbit of translation operators of A ^ 3 ( 1 ) {\widehat{A}}^{(1)}_{3} . We obtain the closed expressions for solutions and their degenerated counterparts in terms of determinants of Kummer polynomials.

2
Dressing chain derivation of Painlevé equations for even
periodicity

We start with a formalism of the dressing chain equations of even periodicity.
The conventional definition of a dressing chain of
N
periodicity is

(j_{n}+j_{n+1})_{z}=-j_{n}^{2}+j_{n+1}^{2}+\alpha_{n},\;\;n=1,{\ldots},N,\quad j_{N+i}=j_{i}\,,

(2.1)

however for even
N
this expression requires, for consistency,
an imposition of a quadratic constraint that can be introduced as
modification of the dressing chain
formulation. Such modification was proposed on basis of Dirac reduction
method in
[
6
]
, where the authors put forward
a system of dressing chain equations
of even periodicity defined as :

(j_{i}+j_{i+1})_{z}=-j_{i}^{2}+j_{i+1}^{2}+\alpha_{i}+(-1)^{i+1}\frac{(j_{i}+j_{i+1})}{\Phi}\Psi,\;\,i=1,2,{\ldots},N,\quad j_{N+i}=j_{i}\,,

(2.2)

where

\Psi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k+1}\left(j_{k}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}\right),\qquad\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}j_{k}=z\,.

(2.3)

Remarkably, the dressing chain equations (
2.2
) can be rewritten
entirely in terms of
f_{i}=j_{i}+j_{i+1}
without any references
to
j_{i}
. It needs to be
emphasized that such elimination of
j_{i}
variables while expressing
the dressing chain equations in terms of
f_{i}
requires inserting the
definition of
\Psi
from (
2.3
) into equations (
2.2
).
This is because the relation
f_{i}=j_{i}+j_{i+1}
is not invertible
for even
N
. In contrast when
N
is taken to be odd
such relation is invertible as illustrated by example of
N=3
with the
inverse relation given by:

j_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(f_{1}-f_{2}+f_{3}),\;\;j_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(f_{2}-f_{3}+f_{1}),\;\;j_{3}=\frac{1}{2}(f_{3}-f_{1}+f_{2})\,,

and after inserting these relations back into equations (
2.1
)
one obtains the symmetric Painlevé IV equations
f_{i,z}=f_{i}(f_{i+1}-f_{i-1})+\alpha_{i},i=1,2,3
.

In the case of equations (
2.2
)
introducing new variables

g_{i}(x)=\frac{f_{i}}{z}=\frac{j_{i}+j_{i+1}}{z},\quad x=-z^{2}/2\,,

(2.4)

we are able to derive
the
N
even Painlevé equations (
1.1
),
where
g_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}
satisfy conditions (
1.2
).
The equations (
1.1
)
agree with higher Noumi-Yamada equations

[
21
]
with
A^{(1)}_{l}
symmetry for
l=2n+1
,
which were originally written
in terms of variable
z
. One of the advantages of using
x
is that
it makes it possible to uncover
a new symmetry structure of
N
automorphisms. These automorphisms
preserve the
A^{(1)}_{N-1}
Weyl symetry group of Bäcklund
transformations of equations (
1.1
) and will explain
presence of denegeneracy among rational solutions.
In case of equations with
N=4
we obtain a regular
Painlevé V equation
directly from (
1.1
) as Painlevé V equation
is conventionally expressed using the variable
x
(see below the equation
(
4.2
) for the derivation).

3
Symmetries of higher Painlevé equations for even
N

Equations (
1.1
) are invariant under the
extended affine
A^{(1)}_{N-1}
Weyl group of
Bäcklund transformations,
s_{i}
and an automorphism
\pi
:

\begin{split}s_{i}&:g_{i\pm 1}\to g_{i\pm 1}\mp\frac{\gamma_{i}}{g_{i}}\,,\;\;\;g_{i}\to g_{i},\;\;\;g_{j}\to g_{j},\,j\neq i,i\pm 1,\\
&\gamma_{i}\to-\gamma_{i},\;\;\gamma_{i\pm 1}\to\gamma_{i\pm 1}+\gamma_{i},\;\;\gamma_{j}\to\gamma_{j},\,j\neq i,i\pm 1,\;\;i=1,{\ldots},N\,,\\
\pi&:\pi(g_{i})=g_{i+1},\;\pi(\gamma_{i})=\gamma_{i+1},\end{split}

(3.1)

where for convenience we introduced the parameters

\gamma_{i}=\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2x}

with the normalization
\sum_{i}^{N}\gamma_{i}=1/x
that corresponds to
normalization
\sum_{i}^{N}\alpha_{i}=2
for the parameters
\alpha_{i}
.

In addition to the
extended affine Weyl group of Bäcklund transformations
the equations (
1.1
) are invariant under
automorphisms that are generated by reflections around the fixed point
n
on a periodic circle
1,2,,3,{\ldots},N+1,N+2,{\ldots}
with a periodic condition
k=k+N
for any point
k
.
These automorphisms also map
x\to-x
.
We denote these automorphisms as
P^{N}_{n}
but will drop the
upper script
N
,
when it will be obvious from the
context and will use only the
P_{n}
symbol. We distinguish between
two types of automorphisms
depending on whether

n
is even or odd. Accordingly, the two classes of reflections are :

1.

Reflections around even points
n=2i
denoted by

P_{2i},i=0,1,{\ldots},\frac{N}{2}

that act as follows:

P_{2i}(k)=2i-k,\quad k=0,1,2,3,4,5,..,\quad k+N=k

(3.2)

on a periodic line of points with a period of
N
.
Each reflection is equivalent to
a single transposition that interchanges

k
with
2i-k
.
There are always two fixed points, namely,

i
and
i+\frac{N}{2}
that according to

P_{2i}(i)=2i-i=i,\;\;\;P_{2i}(i+\frac{N}{2})=2i-i-\frac{N}{2}=i-\frac{N}{2}\sim i+\frac{N}{2},

that are being transformed into themselves. The symbol
\sim
is
to indicate that the periodic condition
k=k+N
was used.
The remaining
N-2
points fall into

N/2-1
(even/even and odd/odd) pairs that are mapped into each other
under even reflections.

2.

Reflections around odd points
n=2i+1
denoted by

P_{2i+1},i=0,1,{\ldots},\frac{N}{2}-1

that act through

P_{2i+1}(k)=2i+1-k,\quad k=0,1,2,3,4,5,..,\quad k+N=k

(3.3)

on a periodic line of points
k=1,2,3,4,{\ldots}
with a period of
N
.
Each of these reflections is equivalent to transpositions
of
N/2
pairs of even/odd points that are being interchanged
into each other.

All these reflections obviously square to one:

P_{n}^{2}=1\,.

All the above reflections naturally induce transformations of
\alpha_{k},g_{k}

through:

\begin{split}{\widehat{P}}_{n}(g_{k}(x))&=g_{P_{n}(k)}(-x)=g_{n-k}(-x),\\
{\widehat{P}}_{n}(\alpha_{k})&=\alpha_{P_{n}(k)}=\alpha_{n-k},\;\;n=2i,2i+1\end{split}

(3.4)

that keep equations (
1.1
) invariant when also
x\to-x
.
The straightforward although formal way to prove the invariance of
the Painlevé
equations (
1.1
) is to introduce transformations :

{\widehat{P}}_{n}(j_{k})=\sqrt{-1}j_{n-k+1},\;\;{\widehat{P}}_{n}(\alpha_{k})=\alpha_{n-k},\;\;{\widehat{P}}_{n}(z)=\sqrt{-1}z\,.

(3.5)

of the dressing equations (
2.2
)
that generate transformations (
3.4
) together
with
x\to-x
. It follows from these definitions that

{\widehat{P}}_{n}(\Psi)=(-1)^{n}\Psi,\quad{\widehat{P}}_{n}(\Phi)=\Phi,

when the use is made of periodic conditions.
As a result the dressing chain equations (
2.2
)
remain invariant under the transformations (
3.5
). This in
turn proves invariance of equations (
1.1
) under
automorphisms (
3.4
) that are generated by reflections on a periodic circle.
Note that the imaginary factor present in definition (
3.5
)
goes away when the transformation is applied on quantities

x
and
g_{k}(x)
.

We find from the definitions of reflections the following relations
for obtaining the automorphisms
\pi
and its powers out of products of
reflections
{\widehat{P}}_{n}
:

\pi={\widehat{P}}_{n+1}{\widehat{P}}_{n},\;\pi^{-1}={\widehat{P}}_{n}{\widehat{P}}_{n+1},\,\pi^{2}={\widehat{P}}_{n+2}{\widehat{P}}_{n},\;\pi^{-2}={\widehat{P}}_{n}{\widehat{P}}_{n+2},\;\;n=2i,2i+1\,.

(3.6)

More generally we find the following identities

{\widehat{P}}_{n_{1}}{\widehat{P}}_{n_{2}}=\pi^{n_{1}-n_{2}},\quad{\widehat{P}}_{n_{1}}\pi^{n_{2}}={\widehat{P}}_{n_{1}-n_{2}}\,,

(3.7)

which show that reflections and
\pi
automorphisms close under
multiplications and form the group with identity
1=\pi^{0}={\widehat{P}}_{n}^{2}
. This structure is consistent with the reflection
automorphisms transforming
x\to-x
, despite the fact that
\pi

is not acting on
x
.
We will refer to reflections and
\pi
and its powers
as group of
automorphisms with cyclic permutations

\{1,\pi,\pi^{2},{\ldots},\pi^{N-1}\}
forming a subgroup of these
automorphisms.

Even/odd reflections commute among themselves

[P_{2i},P_{2j}]=0,\;\quad[P_{2i+1},P_{2j+1}]=0,\;\;i\neq j

while we have the following conjugation transformations:

\begin{split}P_{2i+1}P_{2i}P_{2i+1}&=P_{2i+2}\;\;,\;\;P_{2i+1}P_{2i}P_{2i+3}=P_{2i}\\
P_{2i}P_{2i+1}P_{2i}&=P_{2i+1}\;\;,\;\;P_{2i}P_{2i+1}P_{2i+2}=P_{2i-1}\end{split}

(3.8)

for the mixed even/odd reflections.

Additional important identities are :

{\widehat{P}}_{n}\pi{\widehat{P}}_{n}=\pi^{-1},\;\;\;{\widehat{P}}_{n}\pi^{-1}{\widehat{P}}_{n}=\pi,\;n=2i,2i+1\,.

(3.9)

It is important that reflections preserve the Bäcklund symmetries
through the following conjugation formula:

{\widehat{P}}_{n}s_{m}{\widehat{P}}_{n}\,=\,s_{P_{n}(m)},\;n=2i,2i+1,\,n,m=1,{\ldots},N

(3.10)

where
P_{n}(m)
is the point obtained from
m

by acting with the reflection
P_{n}
.

The above situation differs from the one which is encountered in
the case of odd
N
. First, in such case the corresponding
Painlevé equations remain expressed in terms of the variable
z
that
causes a presence of imaginary factors when dealing with
the transformation defined in (
3.5
).
Furthermore the “even” and “odd” reflections are no longer
clearly distinguishable.
For example, for
N=3
trying to apply the above definitions of reflections will
result in a similar action for the “even” reflection:

P_{2}(1)=1,\;P_{2}(2)=3,\;P_{2}(3)=2\,,

as for the “odd” reflections:

P_{1}(1)=3,\;P_{2}(2)=2,\;P_{2}(3)=1,\;P_{3}(1)=1,\;P_{3}(2)=1,\;P_{3}(3)=3\,.

We notice in all three cases the existence of
one fixed point with the remaining two points being
transposed into each other. Although such reflections are not inducing
directly any symmetry of the
symmetric Painlevé IV equations (without introducing a transformation
of the variable
z
involving imaginary factor), a similar but
albeit more general automorphisms have
been recently discussed in
[
5
]
.

4.3

Conjugation of Bäcklund transformations by

\displaystyle{\widehat{P}}_{2}T_{2}^{-1}{\widehat{P}}_{2}

\displaystyle=T_{1},\;\;{\widehat{P}}_{2}T_{4}{\widehat{P}}_{2}=T_{3}^{-1}\,,

(4.19)

\displaystyle{\widehat{P}}_{0}T_{2}^{-1}{\widehat{P}}_{0}

\displaystyle=T_{3},\;\;{\widehat{P}}_{0}T_{4}{\widehat{P}}_{0}=T_{1}^{-1}\,.

(4.20)

The transformations of translation operators by odd reflections are as

{\widehat{P}}_{1}T_{2}^{-1}{\widehat{P}}_{1}=T_{4},\quad{\widehat{P}}_{1}T_{4}{\widehat{P}}_{1}=T_{2}^{-1}\,,

(4.21)

{\widehat{P}}_{1}

for

5.1
The

The

(

1.

the

by

2.

the
m=0

The
T_{4}

\mathbb{T}(0,m;\mathsf{a})=T_{4}^{m}|F=1,\,G=0\rangle_{\alpha_{\mathsf{a}}}=\big(F_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})\,,G_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})=0\big)\,,

is governed by the recurrence relation

F_{m}=1+\frac{xm}{xF_{m-1}+\mathsf{a}/2-m}\,,

(5.1)

obtained from the
T_{4}
transformation rule

\begin{split}T_{4}(F)&=1-G+(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{4})/(F-\gamma_{4}/(1-G))\,,\\
T_{4}(G)&=F-\gamma_{4}/(1-G)+(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{4})/(G-(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{4})/(F-\gamma_{4}/(1-G)))\,.\end{split}

(5.2)

The above recurrence relation for
F_{m}

has a solution :

F_{m}=\frac{N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a}+2)}{N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})}\,,

(5.3)

in terms of Kummer polynomials
N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})
:

N_{n}(x,\mathsf{a}+2)=2^{n}x^{n}(-x)^{-\mathsf{a}/2}U(-\frac{\mathsf{a}}{2},-\frac{\mathsf{a}}{2}+n+1,x)\,,

(5.4)

where

U(b,b+n+1,x)=x^{-b}\sum_{s=0}^{s=n}\binom{k}{s}(b)_{s}x^{-s}\,,

(5.5)

is a Kummer polynomial
U(a,b,x)
in
x
of degree
n
when

a-b+1=-n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Generally
U(a,b,x)

solves Kummer’s equation:

x\frac{d^{2}w}{d^{2}x}+(b-x)\frac{dw}{dx}-aw=0\,.

Starting with
N_{0}(x,\mathsf{a})=1
and applying the first of the basic two
recurrence relations:

\displaystyle N_{m+1}(x,\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=2xN_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})+(\mathsf{a}-2)N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a}-2),

(5.6)

\displaystyle 2mN_{m-1}(x,\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a}+2)-N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})=\frac{dN_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})}{dx}\quad m=0,1,2,{\ldots},

(5.7)

that can be derived from relation (
5.4
), one first obtains
N_{1}(x,a)=2x+a-2
and eventually arrives at
a general expression:

N_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})=\sum_{p=0}^{p=m}\binom{m}{p}(2x)^{p}(\mathsf{a}-2)(\mathsf{a}-4)\cdots(\mathsf{a}-2(m-p))\,.

(5.8)

For the
T_{2}^{-1}

\mathbb{T}(k,0;\mathsf{a})=T_{2}^{-k}|F=1,\,G=0\rangle_{\alpha_{\mathsf{a}}}\,,

(5.9)

we obtain from expression for the action by

:

T_{2}^{-1}(F,G)=\left(G-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{F}+\frac{1/x-\gamma_{2}}{1-F+\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{4}}{1-G+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{F}}},1-F+\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{4}}{1-G+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{F}}\right)\,,

(5.10)

a chain of transformations that results
in a recurrence relation:

G^{k}=\frac{2k}{2x(1-G^{k-1})+(\mathsf{a}+2k-2)}\,.

G_{k}=1-\frac{R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)}{R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})}=\frac{2kR_{k-1}(x,\mathsf{a})}{R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})}\,,

(5.12)

in terms of Kummer polynomials:

\begin{split}&R_{n}(x,\mathsf{a})=\sum_{p=0}^{p=n}\binom{n}{p}(2x)^{p}\mathsf{a}(\mathsf{a}+2)(\mathsf{a}+4)\cdots(\mathsf{a}a+2(n-p-1))\\
&=2^{n}x^{n}x^{\mathsf{a}/2}U(\frac{\mathsf{a}}{2},\frac{\mathsf{a}}{2}+n+1,x)\,,\end{split}

(5.13)

The above expression can alternatively be obtained
from the initial condition
R_{0}(x,\mathsf{a})=1
by
applying the recursion relation (
5.14
) from the two basic
recursive relations satisfied by polynomials
R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})
:

\displaystyle R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=2xR_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})+\mathsf{a}R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}+2)\,,

(5.14)

\displaystyle 2kR_{k-1}(x,\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})-R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)=\frac{dR_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})}{dx}\,.

(5.15)

Polynomials denoted by
N_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})
for the
T_{4}
-orbit and
R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})
for the
T_{2}
-orbit, are both of order
k
, and are
related to each other through relation:

R_{k}(-x,\mathsf{a})=(-1)^{k}N_{k}(x,2-\mathsf{a})\,.

(5.16)

that is consistent with
P_{1}
reflection that induces
x\to-x,\;F\rightarrow 1-G,\;G\rightarrow 1-F
and
\alpha_{1}\leftrightarrow\alpha_{4},\;\alpha_{2}\leftrightarrow\alpha_{3}
. For
\alpha_{i}
given in relation (

)
this is equivalent to

{\widehat{P}}_{1}\;:\;k\leftrightarrow m,\;\mathsf{a}\leftrightarrow 2-\mathsf{a},\;x\leftrightarrow-x\,.

(5.17)

5.2
A General Case of
T_{4}^{m}T_{2}^{-k}

orbit and the generalized higher-type Kummer polynomials

We now consider the full orbit given as in (
4.12
) by :

T_{2}^{-k}T_{4}^{m}|F=1,\,G=0\rangle_{\alpha_{\mathsf{a}}}=\big(F^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a}),\quad G^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})\big),\;\;\;k,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,.

Here
F^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a}),\,G^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})
are solutions to
equations (
4.2
)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dx}F^{k}_{m}&=F^{k}_{m}(F^{k}_{m}-1)(2G^{k}_{m}-1)-\frac{\mathsf{a}+2k-2m}{2x}F^{k}_{m}+\frac{\mathsf{a}+2k}{2x}\,,\\
\frac{d}{dx}G^{k}_{m}&=-G^{k}_{m}(G^{k}_{m}-1)(2F^{k}_{m}-1)+\frac{\mathsf{a}+2k-2m-2}{2x}G^{k}_{m}+\frac{-2k}{2x}\,,\end{split}

(5.18)

with parameters given in (
4.3
).

See also the reference
[
10
]
for use of the
translation operators to obtain solution of Painlevé V equation in
terms of Laguerre polynomials and
[
19
]
for a more recent discussion.

Here we are able to find the Kummer polynomial representation for both

F^{k}_{m}
and
G^{k}_{m}
as given in the main result of this section by the
following expression:

\begin{split}F^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})&=\frac{R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R_{m(k+1)}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})}{R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})R_{m(k+1)}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)}\,,\\
G^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})&=2k\frac{R_{m(k+1)}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R^{(m)}_{(m+1)(k-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})}{R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R_{(m+1)k}^{(m)}(x,\mathsf{a})},\;\;m,k=0,1,2,..\,.\end{split}

(5.19)

The symbols
R_{n}^{(0)}(x,\mathsf{a})=R_{n}(x,\mathsf{a})
are regular Kummer polynomials of the
n
-th order
defined in relation (
5.13
).
The symbols
R_{n}^{(m)}(x,\mathsf{a})
are generalized Kummer
polynomials of the
n
-th order of
the
m
-th type.
They are obtained from Kummer polynomials by a set of recurrence
relations :

\begin{split}R^{(m-2)}_{(m-1)(k+1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R^{(m)}_{(m+1)k}(x,\mathsf{a})&=\frac{1}{2m}\big[R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R_{m(k+1)}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})\\
&-R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})R_{m(k+1)}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)\big]\,,\end{split}

(5.20)

that
are valid for
m=1,2,3,{\ldots}
and
k=0,1,{\ldots}
with initial
variables being

R^{(-1)}_{0}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)=1
and
R^{(0)}_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})=R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})
.
The master recurrence
relation (
5.20
) gives rise
to the
determinant expressions for the
mk
order and the
m-1
-type
Kummer-like polynomials
R^{(m-1)}_{mk}(x,\mathsf{a})
.
Their determinant expressions are obtained by employing
the Desnanot-Jacobi identity:

|M|\times|M_{1,n}^{1,n}|=|M_{n}^{n}|\times|M_{1}^{1}|-|M_{1}^{n}|\times|M_{n}^{1}|

(5.21)

which is valid for any a
(k+1)\times(k+1)
matrix
M
.
The notation in identity (
5.21
) is such that

M_{i}^{j}
is a matrix
M
with

j
-th row and
i
-th column removed.
When the Desnanot-Jacobi identity is applied on the above
recurrence relation (
5.20
)
(up to a constant
C_{m-1}
determined below)
one finds
mk
order and the
m-1
-type
Kummer-like polynomial
R^{(m-1)}_{mk}(x,\mathsf{a})
being the determinant
of the
m\times m
matrix
M
with matrix elements to be given by:

R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})=C_{m-1}|M|\,,\quad M_{ij}=R_{k+i-1}(x,\mathsf{a}-2(m-j)),\quad i,j=1,{\ldots},m,

(5.22)

where
R_{n}(x,b)
are the original Kummer polynomials (
5.13
)
and the constants satisfy the relation:

C_{m}=\frac{1}{2m}\frac{C_{m-1}^{2}}{C_{m-2}},\quad m=2,3,4,{\ldots}\,.

(5.23)

It follows for the first few cases that

C_{0}=1,\;C_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\;C_{2}=\frac{1}{2\cdot 2}(\frac{1}{2})^{2}=\frac{1}{16}\,.

Generally, we find from relation (
5.23

C_{m}=\frac{1}{2^{m(m+1)/2}}\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{1}{(m-j)^{j+1}},\quad m=2,3,4,{\ldots}

(5.24)

The value of
C_{m}
is such that the identity
R_{0}^{(m)}(x,a)=1,m=0,1,2,{\ldots}
holds as follows from the above determinant expressions.

From the determinant expression (
5.22
) we find as a special
example
R_{2k}^{(1)}(x,\mathsf{a})
to be given by:

R_{2k}^{(1)}(x,\mathsf{a})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix}R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})\\
R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a})\end{vmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(R_{2}(x,\mathsf{a})R_{1}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)-R_{1}(x,\mathsf{a})R_{2}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)\bigr)\,.

One can check explicitly that

(5.26)

Other basic examples of
R_{mk}^{(m-1)}

\displaystyle R_{3k}^{(2)}(x,\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\begin{vmatrix}R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-4)&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})\\

(5.27)

\displaystyle R_{4k}^{(3)}(x,\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(\frac{1}{16})^{2}\begin{vmatrix}R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 3)&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 2)&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})\\
R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 3)&R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 2)&R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a})\\
R_{k+2}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 3)&R_{k+2}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 2)&R_{k+2}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k+2}(x,\mathsf{a})\\
R_{k+3}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 3)&R_{k+3}(x,\mathsf{a}-2\cdot 2)&R_{k+3}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)&R_{k+3}(x,\mathsf{a})\end{vmatrix}\,.

(5.28)

It follows from the above determinant expressions
that Kummer polynomials
of the

-th kind
and of the

R_{n}^{(p)}(x,\mathsf{a})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(2x)^{k}\left(\mathsf{a}-a_{1}^{(n,k,p)}\right){\ldots}\left(\mathsf{a}-a_{n-k}^{(n,k,p)}\right),\quad p\geq 0,n>0,\;\;R_{0}^{(p)}(x,\mathsf{a})=1\,,

and thus fully defined
by roots

Using equation :
dR_{n}(x,\mathsf{a})/dx=R_{n}(x,\mathsf{a})-R_{n}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)

\begin{split}R_{n}(x,\mathsf{a}-6)&=R_{n}(x,\mathsf{a})-3R_{n}^{\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})+3R_{n}^{\prime\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})-R_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x,\mathsf{a}),\\

and accordingly the determinant in equation (

) can be
rewritten as Wronskian of Kummer polynomials:

R_{4k}^{(3)}(x,\mathsf{a})=\frac{1}{3}(\frac{1}{16})^{2}\begin{vmatrix}R_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})&R_{k}^{\prime\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})&R_{k}^{\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})&R_{k}(x,\mathsf{a})\\
R_{k+1}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})&R_{k+1}^{\prime\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})&R_{k+1}^{\prime}(x,\mathsf{a})&R_{k+1}(x,\mathsf{a})\\

(5.30)

with all polynomials now taken at the same point
\mathsf{a}
.

There also exists a dual master recurrence relation:

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2k}\left(R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R_{(m+1)k}^{(m)}(x,\mathsf{a})-R_{mk}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})R_{k(m+1)}^{(m)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)\right)\\
&=R_{m(k+1)}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2)R^{(m)}_{(k-1)(m+1)}(x,\mathsf{a})\,.\end{split}

(5.31)

Applying the duality relation

R_{km}^{(m-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}-2){=}N_{km}^{(k-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})\,,

(5.32)

that exchanges

and
\mathsf{a}\to\mathsf{a}-2

In view of the duality relations (

\begin{split}F^{k}_{m}(x,\mathsf{a})&=\frac{N_{mk}^{(k-1)}(x,\mathsf{a})N_{m(k+1)}^{(k)}(x,\mathsf{a}+2)}{N_{mk}^{(k-1)}(x,\mathsf{a}+2)N_{m(k+1)}^{(k)}(x,\mathsf{a})}\,,\\

(5.37)

6
Even
{\widehat{P}}_{2},{\widehat{P}}_{4}

General remark that applies to automorphisms
{\widehat{P}}_{4},{\widehat{P}}_{2},{\widehat{P}}_{3}
is that
implementing them requires enforcing the constant

to be an
integer, chosen here to be

6.1
Action of the
{\widehat{P}}_{2}
automorphism

For
{\widehat{P}}_{2}
the transformation

\alpha_{2}\leftrightarrow\alpha_{4}
for
\alpha_{2}=-2k
and

\alpha_{4}=2-\mathsf{a}+2m=2(1-l+m)
is equivalent to
that of

l\to 1+m+k,\;\;\;k\to l-1-m,

for

.

Accordingly
{\widehat{P}}_{2}
transformations are such
that

\begin{split}{\widehat{P}}_{2}(F_{m}^{k}(x,l))&=F_{m}^{l-1-m}(-x,1+m+k)\\

and are expected to be solutions to equations
(
4.2
) with the same parameters as
solutions
F_{m}^{k}(x,\mathsf{a}),1-G_{m}^{k}(x,\mathsf{a})
. Are these functions equal to
the original solutions
F_{m}^{k}(x,\mathsf{a}),1-G_{m}^{k}(x,\mathsf{a})
or are they
degenerated solutions is
the main question. We will fully
answer it in the subsection
6.3
but first we
will discuss how it works on an explicit example:

Example 6.1
.

Here we calculate a solution
T_{2}^{-k}T_{4}^{m}|F=1,\,G=0\rangle_{\alpha_{\mathsf{a}=4}}

6.3

{\widehat{P}}_{2}

{\widehat{P}}_{4}

We now present a general discussion

\alpha_{i}

The result will follow if we are able to show the following equalities:

\displaystyle{\widehat{P}}_{2}T_{2}^{-k_{3}}T_{4}^{m_{3}}|g_{1}=1,g_{2}=0\rangle_{a_{3}}

\displaystyle{=}\pi s_{1}T_{2}^{-k_{1}}T_{4}^{m_{1}}|g_{1}=1,g_{2}=0\rangle_{a_{1}}\,,

(6.11)

for some values of
k_{i},m_{i}
and
a_{i}

[
20

(
6.10
) and (
6.11
)

6.10
) holds and
multiply the equation (
6.10

{\widehat{P}}_{2}

\pi={\widehat{P}}_{2}{\widehat{P}}_{1}

{\widehat{P}}_{1}s_{1}{\widehat{P}}_{1}=s_{4}

Multiplying both sides by
s_{4}

\begin{split}\pi^{-1}s_{4}{\widehat{P}}_{2}T_{2}^{-k_{3}}T_{4}^{m_{3}}|g_{1}=1,g_{2}=0\rangle_{a_{3}}&={\widehat{P}}_{1}{\widehat{P}}_{2}{\widehat{P}}_{1}s_{1}T_{2}^{-k_{1}}T_{4}^{m_{1}}|g_{1}=1,g_{2}=0\rangle_{a_{1}}\\

9
Summary and conclusions

The even periodicity higher Painlevé equations possess extended symmetry structure
when compared with the odd periodicity counterparts. The additional reflection automorphisms that
we have introduced in this work split on reflection around even and odd points and subsequently play different roles
in determining rational solutions. The reflections around even points generate new solutions while maintaining the parameters of Painlevé equations unchanged thus they are directly engaged in generating degeneracy. The reflections around odd points act as duality transformations that maintain the value of solutions although formally changing its appearance by mapping into the dual formulation. The duality property plays crucial role in determining the structure of solutions and provide a useful framework for merging solutions of simple orbits of one single translation operator into orbits created by actions of two independent translation operators. Developing these formalism embed higher number of translation operators will be crucial for complete characterization of more complex solutions including Umemura solutions, We palne to complete proofs for N byond N =4. It would be important to establish theory of discrete Painleve structures that are naturally e emerging from use of translation operators
Proofs given for
N=4
and arguments presented by looking for particular solutions will be established for higher N ¿4.

On raw moments of the binomial distribution
Kalle Leppälä

Abstract
We study the k k :th raw moment of a variable R R following the binomial distribution B ​ ( n , p ) \text{B}(n,p) , where n / k → β > 0 n/k\rightarrow\beta>0 . It is known that 𝔼 ⁡ ( R k ) \mathbb{E}(R^{k}) is bounded both from below and from above by functions of the form k k ​ Ψ k k^{k}\Psi^{k} . We solve the asymptotically optimal value of Ψ \Psi as a function of p p and β \beta .

In each of the three instances the simple bounds leave us wanting for a more precise description of the asymptotic behaviour of the raw moment

General results

Theorem 1
.

Suppose

. Then

\mathbb{E}(R^{k})=k^{k}(\Psi+o(1))^{k}\,,

where

\Psi=\frac{\beta^{\beta}(e^{\chi}-1)^{\tau}}{\tau^{\tau}(\beta-\tau)^{\beta-\tau}\chi}\,,

(4)

\chi=\frac{1}{\beta}+W_{0}\Big(\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}(1-p)}{\beta p}\Big)\,,\quad\tau=\frac{\beta}{1-p}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\beta W_{0}\Big(e^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}(1-p)/(\beta p)\Big)+1}-p\Bigg)\,,

and
W_{0}
stands for the principal branch of the Lambert function.

Proof of the asymptote

To deal with the values of
j
in (
2
) close to extremities, we shall need the unimodality of the sequence of coefficients. In his article about unimodality for the Stirling numbers of the second kind
[
5
]
, Dobson points out an earlier unpublished proof by D. Klarner showing logarithmic concavity, a stronger property that implies unimodality when the sequence is also positive. Here as well this approach turns out to be fruitful: the desired result is a trivial corollary of Klaner’s inequality (
5
), which we also prove for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2

(Klaner)
.

For all
2\leq j\leq k-1
,

j\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}\geq(j+1)\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}\,.

(5)

Consequently, the sequences

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}\,,\quad\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}(n)_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}(n)_{j}p^{j}\,,

where
1\leq j\leq\min\{k,n\}
, are each logarithmically concave and as positive sequences thereby also unimodal.

Proof.

When
k=3
, the inequality (
5
) is valid as
2\cdot 3/1\geq 3\cdot 1/3
. We make an induction hypothesis and proceed to show it for
k+1
. Using the recurrence
[
9
]

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j}=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j - 1}+j\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}

and the induction hypothesis, we obtain

\displaystyle j\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j-1}}

\displaystyle=j\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}+j\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-2}+(j-1)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}=j\frac{1+j\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}{\tfrac{j-1}{j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}+j-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{j}{j-1}\cdot\frac{1+j\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}{\tfrac{1}{j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}+1}\geq\frac{j+1}{j}\cdot\frac{1+j\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}{\tfrac{1}{j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}+1}

\displaystyle=(j+1)\frac{1+(j+1)\tfrac{j}{j+1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}/\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}+j}=(j+1)\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}+(j+1)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}+j\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}

\displaystyle=(j+1)\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j}}\,.

Thus, (
5
) is valid by induction. Because
j+1\geq j
we get

\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}\geq\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}\,,

in other words, Stirling numbers of the second kind form a logarithmically concave sequence with respect to
j
. Now also

\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}(n)_{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}(n)_{j-1}}=(n-j+1)\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}\geq(n-j)\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}=\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}(n)_{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}(n)_{j}}

and

\hskip-0.85358pt\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}(n)_{j}p^{\,j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}(n)_{j-1}p^{\,j-1}}=(n\hskip-0.28453pt-\hskip-0.28453ptj\hskip-0.28453pt+\hskip-0.28453pt1)p\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j-1}}\geq(n\hskip-0.28453pt-\hskip-0.28453ptj)p\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}}=\frac{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j+1}(n)_{j+1}p^{\,j+1}}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}(n)_{j}p^{\,j}}\,.\qed

We can now prove the main result Theorem
1
. When

, the coefficients in (

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Temme
[
10
]
,
[
11
, chapter 34]
uses a modified saddle point method to approximate the Stirling numbers of the second kind with

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}=\binom{k}{j}\left(\frac{k-j}{e}\right)^{k-j}\frac{(e^{\chi}-1)^{j}}{\chi^{k}}\sqrt{\frac{k-j}{k(\chi-k/j+1)}}(1+o(1))\,,

(6)

where
\chi
is the real and positive saddle point of the function

\phi(x)=-k\log x+j\log(e^{x}-1)\,.

Differentiating yields

\phi^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{k}{x}+\frac{je^{x}}{e^{x}-1}\,,

and so

\frac{k}{\chi}=\frac{je^{\chi}}{e^{\chi}-1}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad-\frac{k}{j}e^{-\frac{k}{j}}=\Big(\chi-\frac{k}{j}\Big)e^{\chi-\frac{k}{j}}\,.

(7)

Because
-1/e<-ke^{-k/j}/j<0
, equation (
7
) has two real solutions corresponding to the two real branches of the Lambert function. The branch
W_{-1}
gives the invalid solution
\chi=0
, so we should express the positive real saddle point
\chi
using the principal branch
W_{0}
as

\chi=W_{0}\Big(-\frac{k}{j}e^{-\frac{k}{j}}\Big)+\frac{k}{j}\,.

With this we can work out

\sqrt{\frac{k-j}{k(\chi-k/j+1)}}\leq 1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad-\frac{j}{k}\leq W_{0}\Big(-\frac{k}{j}e^{-\frac{k}{j}}\Big)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad-\frac{j}{k}e^{-\frac{j}{k}}\leq-\frac{k}{j}e^{-\frac{k}{j}}\,.

The last inequality is easily verified for
1\leq j\leq k
, thus the square root factor in (
6
) is bounded between zero and one.

The convergence of
o(1)
to zero as
k
tends to infinity in (
6
) is uniform with respect to
j
as long as
\delta k<j<(1-\delta)k
, where
\delta
is any positive constant

[
3
]
, see
[
4
]

for a proof in a more general context of
r
-associated Stirling numbers of the second kind. A recent preprint
[
2

of Canfiel, Helton and Hughes actually demonstrates uniform convergence without restrictions, but in our case the extremities of

,
\delta<\tau<1-\delta

An effective version
k^{k}/e^{k-1}\leq k!\leq k^{k+1}/e^{k-1}

7

\log\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}\right)=(1-\tau)k\log k+\big(-\tau\log\tau-(1-\tau)+\tau\log(e^{\chi}-1)-\log\chi\big)k+o(k)\,.

The convergence in

remains uniform with respect to

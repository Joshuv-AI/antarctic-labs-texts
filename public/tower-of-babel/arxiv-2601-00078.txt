Double-Pumped Kerr Parametric Amplifier Beyond the Gain-Bandwidth Limit
Nicolas Zapata, Najmeh Etehadi Abari, Mitchell Field, Patrick Winkel, Simon Geisert, Soeren Ihssen, Anja Metelmann, Ioan M. Pop

Abstract
Superconducting standing-wave parametric amplifiers are crucial for the readout of microwave quantum devices. Despite significant improvements in recent years, the need to operate near an instability point imposes a fundamental constraint: the instantaneous bandwidth decreases with increasing amplifier gain. Here we show that it is possible to obtain parametric amplification without instability by using two simultaneous drives that activate phase-preserving gain and frequency conversion. Realized in a granular aluminum dimer with Kerr nonlinearity, our method demonstrates a sixfold bandwidth increase at 20 dB gain, surpasses the conventional gain–bandwidth scaling up to 25 dB, and remains near the quantum limit.

In summary, we have demonstrated a standing-wave parametric amplifier with a nonconventional gain–bandwidth scaling, realized by double-pumping a granular aluminum Bose–Hubbard dimer.
Unlike previous implementations, our approach relies solely on four-wave mixing stemming from grAl Kerr nonlinearity, offering greater integration flexibility by eliminating the need to engineer pure three-wave mixing processes in the device.
The device achieves a factor of six bandwidth broadening for a maximum gain of 20 dB, with improved GBW scaling up to 25 dB, and it maintains added noise levels near the quantum limit. The large amplification–deamplification ratio observed in phase-sensitive mode, also highlights the promise of these amplifiers for generating both single and two-mode vacuum squeezing. These features could be investigated more efficiently by designing a two-port device with direct access to the hybridized quadratures, which would allow us to probe the path entanglement of the emitted light. Furthermore, the concept of a multi-pumped parametric amplifier could be extended by adding a third mode and applying six pump tones, which has been predicted to also provide nonreciprocity
35
;
36
.

Due to the hopping interaction

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\pm\sqrt{\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}^{2}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}^{2}+2\left(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}^{2}+\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}}^{2}\right)-\frac{(\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}+\Delta_{\mathrm{b}}^{2})}{2}+\frac{\epsilon\epsilon}{2}},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\pm,-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\pm\sqrt{\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}^{2}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}^{2}+2\left(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}^{2}+\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}}^{2}\right)-\frac{(\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}+\Delta_{\mathrm{b}}^{2})}{2}-\frac{\epsilon\epsilon}{2}},

where

\displaystyle\epsilon\epsilon

\displaystyle\equiv\Big\{\Big[(\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}-\Delta_{\mathrm{b}}^{2})-4\left(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}^{2}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}}^{2}\right)\Big]^{2}+4\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}^{2}\Big[(\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{b}})^{2}

\displaystyle-4(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}})^{2}\Big]-4\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}^{2}\Big[(\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{b}})^{2}-4(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}+\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+32\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}\>\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}\left(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}\>\Delta_{\mathrm{b}}+\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}}\>\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}\right)\Big\}^{1/2}.

(36)

When
\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{S}}
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}=-\Delta_{\mathrm{b}}=-2\Lambda_{\mathrm{S}}
, and by defining the cooperativity of each coupling parameter as
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}=4|\Lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}/\kappa^{2}
with
\alpha\in\{\mathrm{TMS},\mathrm{BS},\mathrm{S}\}
, the eigenvalues can be further simplified as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\pm,\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left(-1\pm\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}}\right).

(37)

As discussed in the main text, our analysis focuses on two characteristic operating points: the exceptional point (EP) and the Bogoliubov point (BP), where the system remains dynamically stable. The EP arises when when
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}
, whereas the BP is defined by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}
-
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}
= -1, corresponding to equal magnitudes of the real and imaginary components of the eigenvalues. As shown in
Fig.

2
(a) of the main text, moving away from the instability region and approaching the BP leads to a broader BW. Further increasing
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}
beyond the BP, results in normal-mode splitting which manifests as a splitting of the gain profiles.

A.2.3
Predicted gain and added noise

To extract the gain and added noise of the amplifiers, we employ the input–output formalism
17
in the frequency domain

\hat{\vec{X}}_{\mathrm{out}}(\omega)=\hat{\vec{X}}_{\mathrm{in}}(\omega)+\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{ext}}\>\hat{\vec{X}}(\omega),

(38)

where
\hat{\vec{X}}(\omega)=\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\omega)\>\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{xp}}\>\hat{\vec{X}}_{\mathrm{in}}(\omega)
, and
\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{xp}}
. Therefore, the scattering matrix, which connects the output and input quadratures through
\hat{\vec{X}}_{\mathrm{out}}(\omega)=\mathbf{S}(\omega)\>\hat{\vec{X}}_{\mathrm{in}}(\omega)
, is given by

\mathbf{S}(\omega)=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{xp}}\>\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\omega)\>\mathbf{K_{\mathrm{xp}}}+\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{4}}}.

(39)

For symmetric couplings of the grAl resonators, the scattering matrix at zero frequency can be expressed in the simplified form

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}[0]=\begin{pmatrix}s_{\mathrm{11}}&0&0&s_{\mathrm{14}}\\
s_{\mathrm{21}}&s_{\mathrm{11}}&s_{\mathrm{23}}&s_{\mathrm{24}}\\
s_{\mathrm{24}}&s_{\mathrm{14}}&s_{\mathrm{11}}&s_{\mathrm{34}}\\
s_{\mathrm{23}}&0&0&s_{\mathrm{11}}\end{pmatrix},

(40)

with

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{11}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}{1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}},

(41a)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{14}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}}-\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}\right)}{1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}},

(41b)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{23}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}\right)}{1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}},

(41c)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{24}}

\displaystyle=\frac{16\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{S}}}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}}{\left(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}\right)^{2}},

(41d)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{21}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{S}}}\,\left(1+\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}\right)^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}\right)^{2}},

(41e)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{34}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{S}}}\,\left(1+\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}}-\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}\right)^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}\right)^{2}}.

(41f)

The maximum gain of the amplifier at zero frequency is then given by

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{0}}=\Big|s_{\mathrm{21}}(\omega=0)\Big|^{2}=\Big|\frac{8\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{S}}}\,\left(1+\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}}\right)^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BS}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}\right)^{2}}\Big|^{2}.

(42)

In
Fig.

6
(a), the zero-frequency gain is shown as a function of the BS coupling for both the EP and BP, assuming
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{S}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}/2
. The BP configuration exhibits a broader bandwidth at 20 dB gain, which is obtained at the expense of increased BS cooperativity.

We calculate the added noise at both EP and BP using the symmetrized noise spectral density.

\bar{S}_{\mathrm{F_{\textit{i}},F_{\textit{i}}}}^{\mathrm{(out)}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\Omega}{2\pi}\>\left\langle\left\{\hat{F}_{\mathrm{\textit{i},out}}(\omega),\hat{F}_{\mathrm{\textit{i},out}}(\Omega)\right\}\right\rangle,

(43)

where
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{\textit{i},out}}=\{\hat{X}_{\mathrm{\textit{i},out}},\hat{P}_{\mathrm{\textit{i},out}}\}
with
i=\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}
. The amplifier added noise photons are then calculated using

\bar{n}_{\mathrm{add}}(\omega)=\frac{\bar{S}^{\mathrm{(out)}}_{\mathrm{\hat{P}_{\mathrm{a}},\hat{X}_{\mathrm{a}}}}(\omega)}{G(\omega)}-\frac{1}{2},

(44)

where
G(\omega)=|s_{\mathrm{21}}(\omega)|^{2}
. In
Fig.

6
(b), we show the calculated added noise photons associated with the quadrature
\hat{P}_{\mathrm{a,out}}
for the amplifier operating at both the EP and BP. The added noise for other quadratures is expected to exhibit a similar trend. For both the EP and BP, our calculations predict the realization of a broadband quantum-limited amplifier. Notably, the BP amplifier sustains a lower added-noise level over a broader frequency range.

A.2.4
Dynamics in presence of a single pump

Under single pump operation, the system Hamiltonian in the hybridized basis becomes

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{H}}/\hbar

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}}\Big\{\tilde{\omega}_{i}\>\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i}+\Big(\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\textit{i}}}}\>\mathrm{e}^{-2i\omega_{\mathrm{g}}t}\>\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c}\Big)\Big\}

(45)

\displaystyle+\Big(\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}\>\mathrm{e}^{-2i\omega_{\mathrm{g}}t}\>\hat{c}_{a}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{b}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c}\Big)+\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}\left(\hat{c}_{a}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{b}+\hat{c}_{b}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{a}\right),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\mathrm{a}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathrm{a}}+2\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{L}}\,|\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+2\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{R}}\,|\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}|^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\mathrm{b}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathrm{b}}+2\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{L}}\,|\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}|^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+2\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{R}}\,|\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,

and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{a}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{L}}\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{R}}\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{S_{\mathrm{b}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{L}}\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{R}}\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{L}}\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}-\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{R}}\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}\right)\sin 2\theta,

(48)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{L}}|\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}|^{2}-\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{R}}|\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}|^{2}\right)\sin 2\theta.

(49)

In the frame rotating at
\omega_{\mathrm{g}}
, the Hamiltonian in the hybridized basis takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{H}}/\hbar

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}}\Big\{\Delta_{i}\>\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i}-\Big(\Lambda_{S_{i}}\>\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c}\Big)\Big\}

(50)

\displaystyle+\Big(\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}\>\hat{c}_{\mathrm{a}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\mathrm{b}}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c}\Big)+\Lambda_{\mathrm{BS}}\left(\hat{c}_{\mathrm{a}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\mathrm{b}}+\hat{c}_{\mathrm{b}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\mathrm{a}}\right),

where
\Delta_{i}=\omega_{\mathrm{g}}-\tilde{\omega}_{i}
and
i=\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}
.
For a nondegenerate amplifier, the pump frequency satisfies
2\omega_{\mathrm{g}}=\tilde{\omega}_{\mathrm{a}}+\tilde{\omega}_{\mathrm{a}}
. In this case, the dominant interaction terms correspond to the TMS coupling, allowing the system dynamics to be reduced to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{H}}/\hbar\approx-\Delta_{\mathrm{a}}\>\hat{c}_{\mathrm{a}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\mathrm{a}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{b}}\>\hat{c}_{\mathrm{b}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\mathrm{b}}+\left(\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}\>\hat{c}_{\mathrm{a}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\mathrm{b}}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),

(51)

which, in the quadrature representation, takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{H}}/\hbar\approx-\sum_{i=\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{2}\left(\hat{X}_{i}^{2}+\hat{P}_{i}^{2}\right)+\Lambda_{\mathrm{TMS}}\left(\hat{X}_{\mathrm{a}}\hat{X}_{\mathrm{b}}-\hat{P}_{\mathrm{a}}\hat{P}_{\mathrm{b}}\right).

(52)

Therefore, for the single-pump configuration, the system operates as a phase-preserving amplifier with the maximum gain at zero frequency, which is given by

\displaystyle G_{0}=\left|\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}+1}{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}-1}\right|^{2}.

(53)

According to this relation, the highest amplification is achieved as
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}\rightarrow 1
. However, this condition is limited by the dynamical stability requirement
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{TMS}}<1
(see the denominator of
Eq.

53
), which imposes the GBW scaling in the single-pumped grAlPA.

Measurement setup

Appendix G
Dynamic Range

In Figure
12
(a) we show saturation power measurements of the grAlPA for low-power gain
G\mathrm{{}_{0}}\approx
20 dB. When driven by a single pump, the amplifier exhibits a nonmonotonic saturation curve for increasing probe powers
P\mathrm{{}_{s}}
. This effect, colloquially referred as shark-fin
50
, originates from operating the grAlPA in a multistable regime. In this situation, we define two characteristic powers: the conventional
G\mathrm{{}_{0}}
–1 dB compression point, and the
G\mathrm{{}_{0}}
+1 dB compression point, which quantifies the power threshold above which transitions between metastable states occur
50
. We measure -1 dB compression points close to -106 dBm for the single-pumped case (similar to previously reported values
60
) and -126 dBm for the double-pumped case, respectively. Strikingly, we find that the single-pumped +1 dB point and the double-pumped –1 dB point lie within the same range. As shown in
Fig.

12
(b), we observe a similar behavior across different gain levels, indicating that the dynamic range under two-pump operation is currently limited by the multistability of the amplifier, and could be improved in future designs.

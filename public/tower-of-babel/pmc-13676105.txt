Perception of obesity among adults as health-related priority in Saudi Arabia. A cross-sectional survey among Riyadh primary healthcare centers attendees
Authors: Mohammed Saad Alqahtani, Badr F. Al-Khateeb, Musab Umair, Raed Aldahash, Awad Alshahrani, Ashraf El-Metwally
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore perception of obesity as a health priority among primary healthcare attendees in Riyadh.DesignA cross-sectional study was conducted between March and July 2023.SiteForty-eight randomly selected primary health care (PHC) centers in Riyadh.ParticipantsA total of 14,239 adults were recruited through multistage cluster sampling and administered an electronic questionnaire.Main measurementsThe primary outcome was the participants’ perception of obesity as a health priority (Yes/No). Independent variables included sociodemographic characteristics (age, gender, education), behavioral factors (smoking, activity, diet), and clinical history (diabetes, hypertension, heart disease).ResultsObesity was perceived as a significant public health issue within their communities by 71.7% (n = 10,207) of the respondents. Multivariable logistic regression showed that age ≥50 (adjusted odds ratio (AOR) 1.27; 95% CI: 1.15–1.39, P < .001), male gender (AOR 1.14; 95% CI: 1.06–1.23, P = .001), and no health insurance coverage (AOR 1.21; 95% CI: 1.12–1.32, P < .001) predicted greater prioritization of obesity. Clinically, diabetes (AOR 1.23, P = .006), hypercholesterolemia (AOR 1.31, P = .003), and heart disease (AOR 1.38, P = .02) were significant independent predictors.ConclusionAlthough there is significant community awareness about obesity, perception is strongly dependent on age, gender, and the presence or absence of symptomatic co-morbidities. Therefore, public health campaigns need to capitalize on this established awareness to promote behavior modification, especially among young and insured individuals who often overlook obesity as serious medical condition.

Introduction

Obesity has become a major global public health concern in recent times, with prevalence rates rising across all geographical and socioeconomic boundaries.1 On a global scale, approximately one-third of the adult population suffers from obesity, accounting for a major share of the non-communicable disease (NCD) burden, most notably including type 2 diabetes mellitus, cardiac disorders, and cancer.2 Within the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA), the prevalence of obesity has similarly escalated, resulting in substantial clinical and economic consequences.3, 4 The reasons contributing to the rise in obesity burden include rapid urbanization, physical inactivity, and consumption of energy-dense diets.5, 6

The importance of tackling the issue of obesity in Saudi Arabia is highlighted by the Saudi Vision 2030 and the Health Sector Transformation Program.7 The focus of these two national plans is on preventing diseases and improving public health status to create a “vibrant society.”8, 9 An essential component of these public health efforts is decreasing the prevalence of obesity to minimize the financial strain on the healthcare system, driven both by direct medical costs and the diminished productivity.10, 11 To successfully implement these public health policies, a unified primary healthcare (PHC) system that addresses both obesity and its determinants is needed.

However, despite the widespread clinical recognition of obesity as a chronic disease, there remains a significant knowledge gap regarding its perception as a public health priority among the Saudi population. Existing literature has primarily focused on the presence of comorbidities or stigmatization associated with obesity, revealing a marked gap between actual health risks and public awareness. Research indicates that an unusually high number of obese individuals in KSA underestimate their weight status or fail to prioritize it relative to other medical conditions. Furthermore, while previous investigations have explored weight awareness in various regions of the Kingdom, comprehensive data characterizing public perception in the capital city of Riyadh remains lacking.

Assessing the alignment between public awareness and national healthcare priorities remains important for the success of these public health initiatives. Given that PHC centers serve as the first point of contact with patients, assessing patient perceptions of obesity in this setting is crucial for shaping future evidence-based health promotion programs and clinical interventions. Inadequate public recognition of this issue may compromise the success of future clinical interventions due to non-adherence to treatment recommendations. Thus, this study aims to investigate the perception of obesity as a health issue among 14,000 adults visiting primary healthcare centers in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Ultimately, these findings aim to guide policymakers in making evidence-based decisions withing the framework of the Health Sector Transformation Program.

Methods

Study design and sampling technique

Cross-sectional study was conducted in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, from March to July 2023 to assess the perception of obesity as a health priority among patients attending primary healthcare (PHC) centers. A multistage cluster sampling approach was applied to ensure a representative urban sample. The first stage involved selecting 48 PHC centers randomly from the comprehensive list of 103 PHCs available in Riyadh. In the second stage, a consecutive sampling method was employed to recruit participants from the waiting rooms of the selected centers during multiple shifts.

Study sample and eligibility criteria

The target population of this study consisted of adult patients attending PHC centers. To isolate general public perception from clinical opinion, all healthcare providers and staff working at the centers were excluded from the study. Participants were recruited from waiting rooms during multiple shifts; all 14,239 eligible individuals who were approached agreed to participate and completed the questionnaire, resulting in a 100% response rate.

Data collection tool: validation and reliability

Data were collected using an electronic questionnaire consisting of validated English-language health perception scales. The survey was linguistically validated through a rigorous process of forward translation into Arabic and subsequent back-translation into English.

To ensure comprehensiveness of the survey, a pilot study was conducted by selecting 100 patients in the Hail region (another geographical region was implemented to avoid contamination of the sample). Additionally, a focus group of 20 key informants evaluated the questionnaire to insure its completeness. Internal consistency was assessed by retesting with another pilot sample of 100 participants, resulting in a correlation coefficient of 0.83. Additionally, face validity was confirmed by a panel of experts in public health and epidemiology.

Data collection procedure

Participants completed the questionnaire on tablet devices, with trained data collectors available to assist in case of any difficulty. The questionnaire consisted of four main sections:1.Sociodemographic: Age, gender, marital status (categorized as married vs. not married), education level, and employment.2.Behavioral factors: Smoking status, physical activity levels, and dietary habits (e.g., fast-food consumption).3.Clinical history: Presence of chronic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease.4.Perceptions of obesity: These questions were designed to assess the participants’ views on obesity as a medical and public health priority.

Ethical considerations

Strict ethical protocols were applied during the study. The research goals were thoroughly communicated to all eligible participants, and their informed written consent was acquired before entering the data into the database. Participants had no obligation to participate and remained anonymous throughout the process.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables, including gender, education level, and the presence of comorbidities, were summarized using frequencies and percentages. The continuous variable of age was assessed for normality using histograms and reported as mean (SD).

The primary outcome assessed was the participants’ perception of obesity as a health priority (Yes/No). Chi-square tests were used in the bivariate analysis to evaluate the association between independent variables (sociodemographic and behavioral factors) and the primary outcome. Factors demonstrating statistically significance were subsequently incorporated into a multivariable logistic regression model. Adjusted odds ratio (AOR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were derived, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05. All statistical analyses were carried out using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0.

Health status and lifestyle factors

Overall, the population reported highly satisfactory health statuses, with 33.7% reporting “excellent” health and 35.6% “very good” health. Only 2.1% rated their health as “poor.” The majority of the study participants (75.7%) had no private health insurance coverage, suggesting strong reliance on the public healthcare facilities. A total of 71.7% (n = 10,207) of the sample population viewed adult obesity as a critical issue within their communities (Table 1).

While participants generally reported high levels of health satisfaction, nearly one-third were regular smokers and over one-third reported physical inactivity. Chronic comorbidities such as diabetes and hypertension were present in the cohort, though self-reported obesity was relatively low (Table 1).

Multivariable analysis identified older age (≥50 years) as the most significant predictor, with these individuals exhibiting 27% higher odds of prioritizing obesity (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] 1.27; 95% CI: 1.15–1.39; P < 0.001). Lower educational attainment and a lack of private health insurance coverage also emerged as significant predictors for prioritizing obesity. Likewise, participants without health insurance coverage were more likely to prioritize obesity as a health issue than their insured counterparts (AOR 1.21; 95% CI: 1.12–1.32; P < 0.001). Marital status, while significant in the initial bivariate analyses, did not remain a significant independent predictor in the final multivariable logistic regression model (AOR 1.01; P = 0.83).

Behavioral risk factors and co-morbidities associated with adult obesity perception among Saudi residents

In addition to sociodemographic factors, behavioral patterns and the presence of comorbidities were significantly associated with the prioritization of obesity as a health issue (Table 3). Lifestyle choices strongly influenced perception; specifically, smoking and fast-food consumption were associated with a twofold increase in the odds of prioritizing obesity. Moreover, active individuals were 1.65 times more likely to rate obesity as a health priority compared to physically inactive individuals (P < 0.001). Specific comorbidities also demonstrated significant associations with the outcome. Participants diagnosed with diabetes (AOR 1.23; P = 0.006), hypercholesterolemia (AOR 1.31; P = 0.003) or heart disease (AOR 1.38; P = 0.02) all demonstrated greater probability of viewing obesity as a health priority. Discussion

This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the perception of obesity as a health priority among a large cohort of primary health care (PHC) attendees in Riyadh. As the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia undergoes a rapid epidemiological transition toward non-communicable diseases (NCDs), public awareness remains a cornerstone of the Health Sector Transformation Program. Our results reveal a significant convergence between public awareness and the national health policy goals outlined in Saudi Vision 2030 and the Health Sector Transformation Program. By identifying key demographic and behavioral predictors, this study provides a framework for aligning public perception with the urgent need to address the rising prevalence and substantial clinical and economic consequences of obesity in the Kingdom.

A notable finding of this study is that 71.7% of the respondents recognize obesity as a serious health threat in their communities. This high level of awareness is a positive indicator for public health authorities, indicating the successful dissemination and public reception of information related to obesity on the other hand, an interesting paradox arises from the comparison between this high community awareness with the individual clinical status. While approximately three-quarters of the participants regarded obesity as a major community issue, the prevalence of self-reported obesity within the cohort was low. This suggests a ‘distanced perception’, where individuals recognize obesity as a societal threat but fail to internalize it as a personal risk.12 This gap is particularly concerning given that recent modeling studies in Saudi Arabia emphasize that even modest weight loss can significantly reduce the long-term burden of chronic diseases, yet our findings suggest that personal prioritization remains low until secondary comorbidities emerge.

Our multivariable analysis revealed several independent variables associated with these perceptions. One strong independent variable affecting the prioritization of obesity was advanced age (≥50 years). This can be attributed to the fact that increasing age comes with health problems.13 Many elderly individuals have witnessed friends and relatives suffer from obesity-related complications such as impaired mobility and cardiovascular events.14, 15 In contrast, younger individuals may view obesity primarily as an aesthetic or social concern,16 rather than as a health issue with insidious, long-term clinical consequences.17

The identification of male sex as significant independent predictor of prioritizing obesity warrants further contextualizing within Saudi culture. Traditionally, studies of the region have indicated that females encounter more difficulties when engaging in physical activities and may equate a higher BMI with vitality.18, 19, 20 The result of this study suggests that there might be increased health literacy among males, or, conversely, a persistent traditional normalization of elevated BMI levels among females.

Interestingly, a lower educational level correlated with higher obesity prioritization in our study. While contrary to traditional health literacy models, where more education is associated with improved health literacy, this association likely reflects local healthcare utilization patterns. Lack of health insurance was another strong predictor of obesity prioritization. Uninsured and less-educated individuals rely predominantly on public PHC facilities, receiving greater direct exposure to the preventative health strategies recently implemented to minimize the financial strain on the public healthcare system.21, 22 This increased exposure likely explains why these groups, who interact more frequently with public health messaging, demonstrate higher levels of obesity prioritization compared to their privately insured counterparts.

Our findings also highlight significant positive associations between obesity prioritization and specific lifestyle behaviors, notably smoking, fast food consumption, and physical activity. In the case of smokers and fast-food consumers, higher prioritization may be attributed to the fact that people with such habits may have a heightened awareness of their personal risk of developing obesity and associated metabolic conditions.23, 24

From a clinical perspective, the presence of a strong association between obesity and conditions such as diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, and heart disease delineates the role of obesity as a driver of metabolic dysfunction. Obesity in Saudi Arabia is considered a benign “lifestyle condition” until it manifests in secondary clinical diagnoses. Public prioritization of obesity appears to surge only after it actively compromises cardiovascular health or lipid profiles.25

It should be noted that the findings must be contextualized within the broader framework of the Saudi Vision 2030 strategy. The high level of public concern demonstrates that the public awareness programs have been successful. At the same time, Riyadh's obesogenic environment with few walking opportunities and a high density of fast-food outlets create barriers that directly contradict these public health objectives.

Strengths and limitations

The primary merits of this study include its large-scale cohort (n = 14,239) and multistage cluster sampling methodology, providing robust statistical power and strong generalizability to adult inhabitants of Riyadh. Furthermore, the use of an extensively tested tool with a high reliability coefficient (0.83) strengthens the data's validity.

However, these results must be interpreted in light of several limitations. Firstly, being a cross-sectional study, causal relationships cannot be drawn between the identified predictors and obesity perception. Secondly, all clinical diagnoses used in this study are based on respondents’ accounts, introducing the potential for recall and underreporting bias. Finally, our findings are specific to urban public health care attendees and may not be generalizable to rural or privately insured populations.

Conclusion and implications

Overall, it can be concluded that obesity is viewed as a critical public health issue among primary healthcare attendees in Riyadh. However, this perception is strongly associated with specific sociodemographic and clinical factors, including age, gender, educational background, and comorbidity status. Despite high community-level awareness, a significant gap exists between recognizing obesity as a societal threat and being proactive for personal prevention. To effectively bridge this gap, future public health interventions must be tailored to address the needs of younger populations and privately insured individuals, who currently demonstrate lower levels of obesity prioritization.

These findings yield two primary implications for public health policy and future health interventions. First, given the high baseline level of obesity awareness, future interventions must shift focus from educational campaigns to facilitating actionable behavioral change. Public health strategies should prioritize targeting and eliminating the structural barriers that impede healthy lifestyle adoption.

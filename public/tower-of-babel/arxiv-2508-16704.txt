Stability analysis of Euler’s elastica ring using harmonic balance
Muhammad Sami Siddiqui

Abstract
The stability analysis of elastic rings subjected to various loading conditions is examined, focusing on stable and unstable configurations. The harmonic balance method is employed to investigate the stability range under different loading conditions. This method provides improved accuracy in results and yields a smooth, stable range of values for all modes of symmetry. Additionally, the bifurcation analysis technique of linearization is utilized to identify unstable regions, and it demonstrates good agreement with the results obtained using the harmonic balance method.



Keywords: Euler’s elastica ring; Harmonic balance method; Post buckling; Stability analysis; Linearization.

Model and Governing Equation

Figure 1
:
Elastic ring model with geometric description

We consider a two-dimensional, thin, inextensible, uniform circular elastic ring in its equilibrium configuration. The ring deforms under the application of a uniform loading parameter
p
, which represents a constant inward pressure or compression. The curve
\Lambda
describing the ring is parametrized by the arc length
s
, and its position vector is denoted as
r(s)=(x_{1}(s),x_{2}(s))
, where
x_{1}(s)
and
x_{2}(s)
are Cartesian coordinates in a Euclidean plane.

The coordinates
x_{1}(s)
and
x_{2}(s)
can be expressed as integrals involving the angle
\vartheta(s)
, which represents the angle between the tangent to the curve and the x-axis:

\displaystyle x_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=\int\cos(\vartheta(s))ds,\quad x_{2}(s)=\int\sin(\vartheta(s))ds,\quad 0\leq s\leq 2\pi.

The angle
\vartheta(s)
is given by:

\displaystyle\vartheta(s)=s+\int_{0}^{s}\nu(s)ds,

where
\nu(s)
represents the curvature difference between the deformed and undeformed ring.

The Euler elastica model, derived using force and moment balance formulation, is given by:

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime\prime}(s)+\mu\nu(s)-\beta+\frac{3}{2}\nu(s)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu(s)^{3}=0,

(1)

where
\mu
and
\beta
are parameters related to the loading parameter
p
, with
p=\mu+\beta-1
.

To ensure the closure of the ring, the following conditions must be satisfied:

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}(0)=\nu^{\prime}\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)=0,\quad\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n}}\nu(s)ds=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\nu(-s)=\nu(s),\quad\nu\left(s-\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)=\nu(s),

(3)

where
n
represents the number of-fold symmetry of the loading conditions.

For more details on the formulation, refer to
[
21
]
and
[
8
]

3
Method: Harmonic Balance

The harmonic balance method
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
is employed to solve Eq. (
1
). This method proposes a solution in the form of a Fourier series and determines the coefficients by balancing harmonics. The general expression for the Fourier series is given by:

\displaystyle\nu(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(A_{k}\cos{(nks)}+B_{k}\sin{(nks)}).

To obtain the zeroth-order harmonic balance approximation for mod
n
, we start with an initial guess solution
\nu_{0}(s)=A\cos{(ns)}
, which satisfies the conditions (
2
) and (
3
). Substituting this into Eq. (
1
), we have:

\displaystyle(A\cos{(ns)})^{\prime\prime}+\mu(A\cos{(ns)})-\beta+\frac{3}{2}(A\cos{(ns)})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(A\cos{(ns)})^{3}=0.

(4)

By simplifying and balancing the harmonics in Eq. (
4
), we solve for the required parameters, resulting in the zeroth-order approximation terms:

\displaystyle\nu_{0}(s)=A\cos{(ns)},\quad\mu_{0}(A)=\frac{1}{8}\left(8n^{2}-3A^{2}\right),\quad\beta_{0}(A)=\frac{3}{4}A^{2}.

For higher-order approximations, an iterative procedure is used with the following expression:

\displaystyle\nu_{k}(s)=\nu_{k-1}(s)+\delta\nu_{k-1}(s),\quad\mu_{k}=\mu_{k-1}+\delta\mu_{k-1},\quad\beta_{k}=\beta_{k-1}+\delta\beta_{k-1},

(5)

where
k
represents the order of approximation, and
\delta\square
denotes the incremental operator.

The first approximation terms
v_{1}
,
\mu_{1}
, and
\beta_{1}
are calculated using a similar procedure:

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(3A^{3}-6A^{2}+8An^{n}-32An^{2}\right)}{M}\cos(ns)-\frac{6A^{2}}{M}\cos(2ns),

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{9A^{4}+24A^{2}\left(n^{n}-5n^{2}-3\right)+48A\left(n^{n}+n^{2}\right)-64n^{2}\left(n^{n}-4n^{2}\right)}{8\left(M\right)},

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{6A^{2}\left(n^{n}-4n^{2}\right)}{M},

where
M=3A^{2}-12A+8n^{n}-32n^{2}
. A similar procedure is followed for higher-order approximations.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have computed the stability ranges of the Euler elastica problem using the harmonic balance method. The obtained stability diagrams are smooth and extend to larger loading values compared to numerical solutions. The diagrams vary with the applied symmetric loading mod
n
, starting from a stable closed shape to a contact shape
p_{c}
, and then transitioning to a self-intersecting shape and an unstable region with irregular shapes.

The harmonic balance method proves to be effective in computing stability diagrams for all loading conditions
n
. It demonstrates good agreement with the bifurcation theory of linearization, as the computed unstable ranges of the parameters
(\mu,\beta)
align with the unstable regions in the harmonic balance results.

In conclusion, the harmonic balance method provides accurate stability calculations for the Euler elastica, yielding stable equilibrium shapes over an extended range of the loading parameter
p
. It also aligns well with the bifurcation theory of linearization, indicating the unstable regions in the parameter space

On the Mechanisms of Collaborative Learning in VAE Recommenders
Tung-Long Vuong, Julien Monteil, Hien Dang, Volodymyr Vaskovych, Trung le, Vu Nguyen

Abstract
Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are a powerful alternative to matrix factorization for recommendation. A common technique in VAE-based collaborative filtering (CF) consists in applying binary input masking to user interaction vectors, which improves performance but remains underexplored theoretically. In this work, we analyze how collaboration arises in VAE‑based CF and show it is governed by latent proximity : we derive a latent sharing radius that informs when an SGD update on one user strictly reduces the loss on another user, with influence decaying as the latent Wasserstein distance increases. We further study the induced geometry: with clean inputs, VAE‑based CF primarily exploits local collaboration between input‑similar users and under‑utilizes global collaboration between far‑but‑related users. We compare two mechanisms that encourage global mixing and characterize their trade‑offs: ① β \beta ‑KL regularization directly tightens the information bottleneck, promoting posterior overlap but risking representational collapse if too large; ② input masking induces stochastic geometric contractions and expansions, which can bring distant users onto the same latent neighborhood but also introduce neighborhood drift. To preserve user identity while enabling global consistency, we propose an anchor regularizer that aligns user posteriors with item embeddings, stabilizing users under masking and facilitating signal sharing across related items. Our analyses are validated on the Netflix, MovieLens-20M, and Million Song datasets. We also successfully deployed our proposed algorithm on an Amazon streaming platform following a successful online experiment.

Our work aims to fill this gap with a comprehensive study the effect of input noise in VAE-based CF. The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We conduct an in-depth analysis of the collaboration mechanism in VAE-based collaborative filtering models and reveal that ① collaboration in VAE-CF is fundamentally governed by latent proximity; ② VAE-CF with clean inputs primarily leverages local collaboration and fails to utilize global collaborative signals when input distances are large; ③ Both

\beta

A common solution to alleviate collapse is to use a more expressive prior (often multi-modal, e.g., mixtures, VampPrior, or flow-based priors). However, an expressive prior also weakens the global pairwise contraction effect compared to a simple prior such as the standard normal prior. Specifically, with a Gaussian prior, the KL term pulls all user posteriors toward a common zero-centered basin, so user pairs have a higher chance of becoming latently close, strengthening sharing at a fixed
\beta

Input masking (data‑level).

Smaller values of
\rho

However, masking affects the geometry of the latent space in a more stochastic manner than the uniform contraction produced by increasing
\beta

For an individual user, each new mask realization slightly shifts the latent posterior, so the user’s nearest-neighbor set fluctuates from batch to batch. Over training, these shifts accumulate, producing what we refer to as
neighborhood drift
: the local structure of the user’s neighborhood wanders, and the gradients shared among nearby users become noisy and inconsistent.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we analyzed how collaboration emerges in VAE–CF and showed that it is fundamentally governed by

latent
proximity: SGD updates are shared within a data‑dependent
sharing radius
, clean
inputs bias the model toward
local
collaboration, and global signals can be induced by either
the
\beta
–KL/prior pathway (near‑uniform contraction of latent distances, with collapse risk if
over‑used) or by input masking (stochastic neighborhood mixing with potential drift). Guided by these insights, we introduced
Personalized Item Alignment
(PIA), a training‑only
regularizer that attaches learnable item anchors and softly pulls masked encodings toward each user’s
anchor centroid. PIA preserves instance information, stabilizes the geometry under masking, and
promotes
semantically grounded
global mixing without adding test‑time overhead. Empirically,
PIA improves over vanilla VAE–CF on standard benchmarks and in an A/B test on a large‑scale
streaming platform, with ablations across user‑activity strata and latent‑space visualizations
corroborating the theory.

Related Works

Motivated by our theoretical analysis, both
\beta
-weighted KL regularization and input masking can promote
global
collaboration, albeit with different trade-offs. Prior work has largely focused on controlling the KL term, primarily along two lines: (i) scheduling the
\beta

\beta
-scheduling.
Liang et al.
(
17
)

\beta

\mathrm{KL}\!\left(q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})\,\|\,p(\mathbf{z})\right)

(
20
)
propose gradually increasing this weight over training to mitigate posterior collapse.

Flexible priors and architectures.
Several works replace the standard normal prior with richer alternatives to better match the data. Examples include VampPrior and its hierarchical variants (e.g., HVamp)
(
39
;
13
)
, as well as implicit or learned priors
(
43
)

(
34

(
12

(

.

To our knowledge, ours is the first work to systematically analyze the collaboration mechanisms in VAE-based CF, showing that both
\beta

global
collaboration.

In contrast to prior works
, guided by our theoretical analysis, we propose a regularization scheme that addresses the issues induced by input masking, mitigating the loss of local collaboration while preserving its benefits for global alignment.

VAE models for collaborative filtering (CF) applications typically employ shallow network architectures as a means of alleviating the issue of posterior collapse. However, this limitation reduces the model’s representational capacity and restricts posterior flexibility. In this constrained setting, we find that PIA becomes less effective i.e., its regularization offers little performance gain. This is reflected by the nDCG results in Table
6
.

When increasing the depth of the encoder, the benefits of PIA become more pronounced, as shown in Table
6
by comparing Deeper-Encoder with and without alignment. However, Deeper-Encoder models also exhibit a greater tendency toward posterior collapse
3
, which ultimately undermines overall recommendation quality (see Table
6
, comparing Shallow-Encoder and Deeper-Encoder).
These findings underscore a critical trade-off in VAE-based CF models: while increased posterior flexibility—achieved through deeper architectures—enhances the effectiveness of personalized item alignment, it also intensifies the risk of posterior collapse, which in turn can degrade recommendation performance. Conversely, shallow architectures mitigate collapse but yield inflexible posteriors, limiting the benefits of alignment. Thus, the utility of alignment is closely tied to the model’s capacity to learn expressive yet stable latent representations.

To address this issue, we propose to enhance flexible posteriors while avoiding posterior collapse by using
normalizing flows
(NF)
38
;
15
;
29
with deeper architectures. Normalizing flows builds flexible posterior distributions utilizing a sequence of
T
invertible transformations to transform the original posterior
q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
into a flexible posterior
q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{T}|\mathbf{x})
as follows,

\mathbf{z}_{0}\sim q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{0}|\mathbf{x}),\quad\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{f}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t-1})\quad\forall\>t=1,\ldots T,

(11)

where
\mathbf{f}_{t}
’s are trainable, invertible-transformations, such as invertible linear-time transformation
29
or inverse autoregressive network
15
.

We then apply the KL regularization in the transformed latent space
\mathcal{Z}_{T}
, i.e.,
\text{KL}\bigl(q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{T}|\mathbf{x})\|p(\mathbf{z})\bigr)
, rather than in the original latent space
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
. This design enables the posterior in the original latent space
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
to remain flexible and better capture variations across different inputs. Owing to the invertibility of the transformations, the latent variables in the transformed space
\mathcal{Z}_{T}
retain identifiability, even under strong regularization. This leads to a more robust and structured latent representation in VAE-based CF when using normalizing flows as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE-NF}}(\mathbf{x};\theta,\phi,f_{1},...,f_{T})=-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}_{T}\sim q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{T}|\mathbf{x}_{u})}\bigl[\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z}_{T})\bigr]+\beta D_{\text{KL}}\bigl(q_{\phi}(_{T}|\mathbf{x})\parallel p(\mathbf{z})\bigr)

(12)

where the sampling
\mathbf{z}\sim q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{T}|\mathbf{x}_{u})
is performed as follows:
\mathbf{b}\sim\operatorname{Ber}(\bm{\alpha})\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{h}=\mathbf{x}\odot\mathbf{b}\rightarrow\text{with }\bm{\mu},\bm{\sigma}^{2}=q_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h}),\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},(\bm{1})\rightarrow\mathbf{z}_{0}=\bm{\mu}+\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbf{z}_{T}=f_{T}\circ\circ\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{z}_{0})
and the probability density function of the last transformation can be computed as:

\log q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{T}|\mathbf{x})=\log q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{0}|\mathbf{x})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\det\left|\frac{d\mathbf{z}_{t}}{d\mathbf{z}_{t-1}}\right|.

(13)

The effectiveness of the Shallow (IAF) Encoder posterior is shown from Table
6
, where Shallow (IAF) Encoder not only achieves stronger base performance but also demonstrates clear improvements when alignment is applied.

For shorthand, write

A first-order Taylor expansion gives

(14)

\displaystyle\|g_{v}-g_{u}\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{q_{v}}\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{v},\mathbf{z})-\mathbb{E}_{q_{u}}\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{u},\mathbf{z})\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{q_{v}}\big[\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{u},\mathbf{z})-\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{v},\mathbf{z})\big]\Big\|}_{=\Delta_{x}(u,v)}\;+\;\underbrace{\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{q_{v}}\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{u},\mathbf{z})-\mathbb{E}_{q_{u}}\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{u},\mathbf{z})\Big\|}_{\leq\,L_{\theta z}\,W_{1}(q_{v},q_{u})}.

‑Lipschitz in

Combining the bounds and substituting into Eq. (

\eta

j\in H
, exactly one of
(x_{1j},x_{2j})
equals
1
. After masking, the post-mask difference at
j
equals
1
iff the unique
1
is kept, with probability
\rho
. Hence

Y:=\sum_{j\in H}\mathbf{1}\{\text{post-mask difference at }j=1\}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(h,\rho).

For
j\in S
, both entries are
1
. The post-mask difference equals
|b_{1j}-b_{2j}|
, which is
1
iff the masks disagree; this happens with probability

\Pr(b_{1j}\neq b_{2j})=\rho(1-\rho)+(1-\rho)\rho=2\rho(1-\rho)
.
Thus

Z:=\sum_{j\in S}|b_{1j}-b_{2j}|\sim\mathrm{Binomial}\!\big(s,\,2\rho(1-\rho)\big).

Independence of masks across coordinates implies
Y\perp Z
, and the masked distance decomposes as

D^{\prime}=\|\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}\|_{1}\;=\;Y+Z.

Contraction.

Because
D^{\prime}
is integer-valued,
D^{\prime}<\delta
is equivalent to
D^{\prime}\leq T_{\delta}
.
Consider the event
\mathcal{E}:=\{Z=0\}\cap\{Y\leq T_{\delta}\}
. On
\mathcal{E}
we have
D^{\prime}=Y+Z\leq T_{\delta}
, hence

\Pr[D^{\prime}<\delta]\;\geq\;\Pr[\mathcal{E}]=\Pr[Z=0]\Pr[Y\leq T_{\delta}]

by independence of
Y
and
Z
.
Now
\Pr[Z=0]=(\Pr[b_{1j}=b_{2j}])^{s}=(\rho^{2}+(1-\rho)^{2})^{s}
, and

\Pr[Y\leq T_{\delta}]=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{h,T_{\delta}\}}\binom{h}{k}\rho^{k}(1-\rho)^{h-k}.

Multiplying the two factors yields Eq. (
27
).

Expansion.

Using
D^{\prime}=Y+Z
with
Y\perp Z
and
U_{\delta}=\lceil\delta\rceil
,

\Pr[D^{\prime}\geq\delta]=\Pr[Y+Z\geq U_{\delta}]=\sum_{m=0}^{h}\Pr[Y=m]\;\Pr[Z\geq U_{\delta}-m]

=\sum_{m=0}^{h}\binom{h}{m}\rho^{m}(1-\rho)^{h-m}\sum_{k=\max\{U_{\delta}-m,0\}}^{s}\binom{s}{k}\big(2\rho(1-\rho)\big)^{k}\big(1-2\rho(1-\rho)\big)^{s-k},

.

Finally, since
D^{\prime}=Y+Z\geq Z
,

\Pr[D^{\prime}\geq\delta]\;\geq\;\Pr[Z\geq U_{\delta}]=\sum_{k=U_{\delta}}^{s}\binom{s}{k}\big(2\rho(1-\rho)\big)^{k}\big(1-2\rho(1-\rho)\big)^{s-k},

which gives Eq. (
28
). This completes the proof.
∎

E.4
proof of Proposition
3.1

Proposition E.5
.

Assume the encoder posterior is diagonal-Gaussian,

q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_{h})=\mathcal{N}\!\big(\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h}),\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\sigma}_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{h}))\big)
. Let the item centroid be
\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}:=\frac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{i}
, then

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}_{h},\mathbf{x};\phi,E)=\underbrace{\big\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|^{2}}_{\text{align mean to item centroid}}\;+\;\underbrace{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})}_{\text{variance shrinkage}}\;+\;\text{const}(\mathbf{x},E),

(29)

where
\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\sigma}_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{h}))
and

\mathrm{const}(\mathbf{x},E)=\tfrac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\|\mathbf{e}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
.

Proof.

Write
q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_{h})=\mathcal{N}\!\big(\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h}),\,\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big)
with

\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\sigma}_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{h}))
, we have:

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{e}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\big\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\mathbf{e}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h}).

Averaging over
i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}
yields

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}_{h},\mathbf{x};\phi,E)=\frac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\big\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\mathbf{e}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h}).

Given the item centroid be
\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}:=\frac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{i}
, then, we obtain

\frac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\big\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\mathbf{e}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\big\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\|\mathbf{e}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Combining the last two displays gives

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}_{h},\mathbf{x};\phi,E)=\big\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})+\underbrace{\Big(\tfrac{1}{|S_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sum_{i\in S_{\mathbf{x}}}\|\mathbf{e}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)}_{\mathrm{const}(\mathbf{x},E)},

which is exactly Eq. (
29
).
∎

E.5
proof of Proposition
3.2

Proposition E.6
.

Fix
\mathbf{x}
and its neighborhood in which the ELBO objective
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}}(\mathbf{x};\theta,\phi;\,\mathbf{x}_{h})
, defined in Eq. (
1
), written as a function of the encoder mean
\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, admits a quadratic approximation
with mask-independent curvature

H\succeq mI,\qquad\|H\|_{2}\leq L\qquad(0<m\leq L<\infty).

Adding
\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}\|\bm{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
to this objective yields an effective Hessian
H_{\mathrm{e}ff}=H+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}I
.
Let
\bm{\mu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{h})
be the unregularized minimizer over masks and
\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}(\mathbf{x}_{h})
the minimizer with alignment. Then with
\tau=\Big(\tfrac{L}{L+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\Big)
, we obtain the following inequalities

\displaystyle\big\|\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big]\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \tau^{2}\,\big\|\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\bm{\mu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big]\big\|_{2},

(30)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big]

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \tau^{2}\,\operatorname{tr}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\bm{\mu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big],

(31)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\ \|\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\ \big]

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \tau\ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\ \|\bm{\mu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\ \big].

(32)

Moreover, if
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\bm{\mu}^{(0)}]
commutes with
H
(e.g., they are simultaneously diagonalizable),
then the Löwner-order contraction

\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big]\ \preceq\ \tau^{2}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\bm{\mu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\big]

holds.

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{x}
and a mask
\mathbf{b}
, and let
F_{\mathbf{b}}(\bm{\mu})
denote the (unregularized) mask-conditioned denoising objective as a function of the
encoder mean
\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(all other quantities
\mathbf{x}
, the masked input

\mathbf{x}_{h}
, decoder parameters, are held fixed).

Choose a reference point
\tilde{\bm{\mu}}
in a neighborhood where
F_{\mathbf{b}}

admits a quadratic approximation with
mask-independent
curvature matrix
H
, and assume

H\succeq mI,\qquad\|H\|_{2}\leq L,\qquad 0<m\leq L<\infty.

Equivalently, we approximate the Hessian uniformly across masks by

\nabla^{2}F_{\mathbf{b}}(\tilde{\bm{\mu}})\approx H
.

Quadratic surrogate.

Define the quadratic model of
F_{\mathbf{b}}
around
\tilde{\bm{\mu}}
by

\displaystyle J_{\mathbf{b}}(\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle:=F_{\mathbf{b}}(\tilde{\bm{\mu}})+\nabla F_{\mathbf{b}}(\tilde{\bm{\mu}})^{\top}(\bm{\mu}-\tilde{\bm{\mu}})+\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\bm{\mu}-\tilde{\bm{\mu}})^{\top}H\,(\bm{\mu}-\tilde{\bm{\mu}}).

(33)

Let

\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{b}}:=\nabla F_{\mathbf{b}}(\tilde{\bm{\mu}}),\qquad\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}:=\tilde{\bm{\mu}}-H^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{b}}.

Completing the square yields an equivalent form

\displaystyle J_{\mathbf{b}}(\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle=c_{\mathbf{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\bm{\mu}-\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}})^{\top}H\,(\bm{\mu}-\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}),

(34)

where the (mask-dependent) constant

c_{\mathbf{b}}:=F_{\mathbf{b}}(\tilde{\bm{\mu}})-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\top}H^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{b}}

is independent of
\bm{\mu}
.

In particular, the unique minimizer of
J_{\mathbf{b}}
is
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}
:

\arg\min_{\bm{\mu}}J_{\mathbf{b}}(\bm{\mu})=\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}.

If
F_{\mathbf{b}}
is exactly quadratic with curvature
H
in this neighborhood, then

\bm{\mu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{h})=\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}
; otherwise,

\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}
is the minimizer of the local quadratic approximation to
F_{\mathbf{b}}
.

Adding the alignment penalty
\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}\|\bm{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
gives the
first–order condition

(H+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}I)\,\bm{\mu}=H\,\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}.

Define

M:=(H+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}I)^{-1}H,\qquad\tau:=\frac{L}{L+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\in(0,1).

Then the aligned minimizer is the affine
shrinkage
of
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}
toward
\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}
:

\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}\;=\;M\,\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}+(I-M)\,\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}.

(35)

Spectral bounds on
M
.

Diagonalize
H=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}
with
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{i})
,
m\leq\lambda_{i}\leq L
.
Then

M=Q\,\mathrm{diag}\!\Big(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\Big)\,Q^{\top}.

Let
\alpha_{i}:=\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}})\in(0,1)
.
It follows that

0\preceq M\preceq I,\qquad\|M\|_{2}=\max_{i}\alpha_{i}=\frac{\lambda_{\max}(H)}{\lambda_{\max}(H)+2\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\;\leq\;\tau,\qquad\|M^{2}\|_{2}=\|M\|_{2}^{2}\leq\tau^{2},

(36)

and, eigenwise,
\alpha_{i}^{2}\leq\tau\,\alpha_{i}
, hence

M^{2}\;\preceq\;\tau\,M\;\preceq\;\tau\,I.

(37)

Variance contraction (operator norm).

Since
\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}
is mask–independent, Eq. (
35
) gives

\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\!\big[\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}\big]=M\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\!\big[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}\big]\,M.

(38)

Taking spectral norms and using submultiplicativity,

\big\|\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}]\big\|_{2}\;\leq\;\|M\|_{2}^{2}\,\big\|\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]\big\|_{2}\;\leq\;\tau^{2}\,\big\|\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]\big\|_{2},

which is Eq. (
30
) upon noting
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\bm{\mu}^{(0)}]
.

Variance contraction (trace).

From Eq. (
38
),

\operatorname{tr}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}\!\big[\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}\big]=\operatorname{tr}\!\Big(\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]\,M^{2}\Big)\;\leq\;\|M^{2}\|_{2}\,\operatorname{tr}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]\;\leq\;\tau^{2}\,\operatorname{tr}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}],

where the inequality uses
M^{2}\preceq\|M^{2}\|_{2}I
and the fact that for
A,B\succeq 0
,

\operatorname{tr}(AB)\leq\|B\|_{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(A)
. This yields Eq. (
31
).

Löwner-order contraction under commutation
(
9
)
.

If
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]
commutes with
H
, then it commutes with
M
.
In the common eigenbasis, write
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]=Q\,\mathrm{diag}(v_{i})\,Q^{\top}
with
v_{i}\geq 0
.
Then

M\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}]\,M=Q\,\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{i}^{2}v_{i})\,Q^{\top}\;\preceq\;Q\,\mathrm{diag}(\tau^{2}v_{i})\,Q^{\top}=\tau^{2}\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{b}}[\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}],

proving the Löwner-order bound.

Mean–drift contraction.

From Eq. (
35
),

\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}=M(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}),

hence

\displaystyle\|\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}})^{\top}M^{2}(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}})^{\top}(\tau I)(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}})\qquad\text{(by Eq. (\ref{eq:M2-less-tauM})) }

\displaystyle=\tau\,\|\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{b}}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Taking
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{b}}
gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\|\bm{\mu}^{(\mathrm{A})}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\;\leq\;\tau\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{b}}\big[\|\bm{\mu}^{(0)}-\bar{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\big],

which is Eq. (
32
).

∎

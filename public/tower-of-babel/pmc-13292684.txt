A highly efficient fluorescence-based virus neutralizing test for fowl adenoviruses associated with hepatitis-hydropericardium syndrome and inclusion body hepatitis
Authors: Baiyu Wang, Qilong Qiao, Minghe Xu, Ning Li, Panpan Yang, Yanfang Cong, Dongdong Yang, Jianli Li, Kexiang Yu, Jun Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFowl adenoviruses (FAdVs), specifically FAdV-4 associated with hepatitis-hydropericardium syndrome (HHS) and FAdV-8a, FAdV-8b, and FAdV-11 associated with inclusion body hepatitis (IBH), pose significant threats to the global poultry industry. The current gold standard for detecting neutralizing antibodies against these viruses is the conventional virus neutralization test (VNT), which relies on the subjective visual assessment of cytopathic effects. This method is time-consuming, low-throughput, and prone to inter-observer variability, highlighting an urgent need for a rapid, objective, and high-throughput alternative. Leveraging reverse genetics systems, we constructed recombinant reporter viruses for FAdV-4, FAdV-8a, FAdV-8b, and FAdV-11, each expressing the green fluorescent protein ZsGreen. These fluorescent-tagged viruses were used to develop a rapid fluorescence-based virus neutralization test (F-VNT). The test quantifies neutralization titers based on the reduction of green fluorescence in infected cell cultures, allowing for an objective and automated readout. Notably, this new test delivers results within 24 hours post-infection, significantly faster than the conventional VNT. Additionally, the established method was used to investigate cross-neutralization of anti-FAdV-8a, -8b, -11, and -4 sera against heterologous viruses. The results showed high cross-neutralization between FAdV-8a and -8b. Anti-FAdV-11 antibodies exhibited weak neutralization against FAdV-8a and -8b, while anti-FAdV-4 antibodies had no neutralizing effect on FAdV-8a, -8b, or -11. The developed F-VNT provides a rapid, accurate, and high-throughput platform for serological evaluation of antibodies against FAdVs associated with HHS and IBH. This method represents a substantial improvement over traditional VNT and is well-suited for large-scale seroepidemiological studies and vaccine efficacy evaluations in the poultry industry. This study also provides theoretical guidance for antigen selection in developing multivalent vaccines against HHS and IBH.Keywords: Fowl adenovirus, Hepatitis-hydropericardium syndrome, Inclusion body hepatitis, Neutralizing antibody test, Reverse genetic system

Introduction

Fowl adenoviruses (FAdVs) are important pathogens responsible for substantial economic losses in poultry farming worldwide. Based on genomic sequences and serum cross-neutralization tests, FAdVs are currently classified into five species (FAdV-A to FAdV-E) and further divided into 12 serotypes (FAdV-1 to FAdV-8a, and FAdV-8b to FAdV-11) (Benkő et al., 2022). Among the various FAdV serotypes, FAdV-4 is the primary causative agent of hepatitis-hydropericardium syndrome (HHS), a disease characterized by hydropericardium, hepatitis, and high mortality (Schachner et al., 2018, 2021). Meanwhile, FAdV-8a, FAdV-8b, and FAdV-11 are frequently implicated in inclusion body hepatitis (IBH), an acute hepatic disease characterized by the presence of intranuclear inclusion bodies in hepatocytes (Steer-Cope et al., 2017; Şahindokuyucu et al., 2020). Both IBH and HHS are characterized by rapid onset and high mortality in young birds. Different vaccine platforms, including inactivated vaccines (Kim et al., 2014; Meng et al., 2019), subunit vaccines (Shah et al., 2012; Ruan et al., 2018; Wang and Yuan, 2025), and recombinant viral vector vaccines (Wang et al., 2022; Song et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025), have been developed for the prevention and control of HHS and IBH. Nevertheless, there is a lack of high-throughput serological or cell‑based assays capable of efficiently evaluating the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of these vaccine candidates.

Given the increasing incidence of co-infections with FAdVs and the varying efficacy of existing vaccines against different serotypes (Stoute et al., 2025), effective surveillance of these viruses and evaluation of vaccine-induced immunity rely heavily on the detection of serotype-specific neutralizing antibodies (NAbs). Several serological methods can be employed for the detection of NAbs, such as the virus neutralization test (VNT) (Feichtner et al., 2018; Rocha et al., 2023). The conventional VNT remains the most widely recognized gold standard method for NAb detection and evaluation of vaccine protection. However, the conventional VNT has several inherent limitations. Its endpoint determination is based on the subjective microscopic observation of virus-induced cytopathic effect (CPE), which can lead to inconsistent interpretations between different technicians. Furthermore, the conventional VNT is a labor-intensive and time-consuming process, often requiring 3 to 7 days for clear CPE to develop. These constraints make it unsuitable for high-throughput testing, which is increasingly demanded by modern poultry production systems for rapid diagnosis and large-scale monitoring (Schachner et al., 2018).

To address these challenges, reporter viruses expressing fluorescent or luminescent proteins have emerged as powerful tools for developing rapid and quantitative neutralization tests. The objective of this study was to develop and validate a highly efficient fluorescence-based virus neutralization test (F-VNT) for the major HHS- and IBH-associated FAdVs. Utilizing the FAdV reverse genetics systems constructed previously, we generated recombinant FAdV-4, FAdV-8a, FAdV-8b, and FAdV-11 viruses expressing the ZsGreen fluorescent protein as reporter viruses. This report described the application of these reporter viruses in a rapid, objective, and high-throughput F-VNT.

Materials and methods

Cells, viruses, E. coli strains and plasmids

For virus propagation, Leghorn Male Hepatocellular (LMH) cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and incubated at 37 °C under 5% CO2.

FAdV-4 strain CH/HNJZ/2015 (GenBank accession no. KU558760) was previously isolated by our team from chickens with HHS in Henan Province, China (Zhang et al., 2018). FAdV-8b strain CH/SDQD/2021 (GenBank accession no. OQ225144) and FAdV-8a strain SDQD/2023 (GenBank accession no. OR901942) were both isolated in our laboratory from livers of birds affected by IBH in Shandong Province, China, in 2021 and 2023, respectively (Qiao et al., 2024c). FAdV-11 strain FJSW/2021 (GenBank accession no. OK336458) was isolated from livers of IBH-infected birds in Fujian Province, China, in 2021 (Qiao et al., 2024a).

The reverse genetics platforms for FAdV-4, FAdV-8b and FAdV-11 have been described in our earlier publications (Zhang et al., 2018; Qiao et al., 2024a; c), while the reverse genetics platform for FAdV-8a was developed following an analogous strategy. The reporter viruses rFAdV-8b-ZsGreen and rFAdV-11-ZsGreen were constructed using established reverse genetics platforms for FAdV-8b and FAdV-11, respectively. In both constructs, the ORF11 region was replaced with the gene encoding Zoanthus sp. green fluorescent protein (ZsGreen), as previously described in our published work (Qiao et al., 2024b; c).

Additionally, the E. coli strains GB05-dir (ccdB-sensitive) and GBred-gyrA462 (ccdB-resistant), along with the plasmid pR6K-Amp-ccdB containing an ampicillin-ccdB selection cassette, were generously provided by Prof. Hailong Wang of Shandong University.

Construction of infectious clones for recombinant reporter viruses

Applying a two-step seamless DNA recombineering technique, the recombinant reporter viruses rFAdV-4-ZsGreen and rFAdV-8a-ZsGreen were generated using corresponding reverse genetics platforms (Wang et al., 2014). A ZsGreen expression cassette comprising a CMV promoter at the 5′ end and a BGH polyadenylation signal at the 3′ end was introduced into the FAdV-4 genome at its natural 1966-bp deletion site. For FAdV-8a, the same cassette was used to replace its ORF11 region.

First, the 1966-bp deletion site of FAdV-4 or the ORF11 region of FAdV-8a was inserted or replaced with the Amp-ccdB selection cassette in the E. coli GBred-gyrA462. Using primers designed with homologous arms targeting the flanking regions of the selected sites, along with SwaI restriction sites and sequences specific to the Amp‑ccdB expression cassette (Table 1), the Amp‑ccdB expression cassette was PCR‑amplified from the template plasmid pR6K‑amp‑ccdB. Following purification via gel extraction, the amplified cassette and the respective infectious clone (p15A-cm-FAdV-4 or p15A-cm-FAdV-8a) were co‑electroporated into E. coli GBred‑gyrA462 for homologous recombination mediated by Redα/β. Positive clones were selected by chloramphenicol and ampicillin, yielding the intermediate constructs p15A‑cm‑FAdV‑4‑1966/Amp‑ccdB and p15A‑cm‑FAdV‑8a‑ΔORF11/Amp‑ccdB. Subsequently, the Amp‑ccdB cassette in each construct was replaced with a ZsGreen expression cassette. To achieve this, the above intermediate plasmids were digested with SwaI to excise the Amp‑ccdB cassette. Meanwhile, the ZsGreen expression cassette was amplified using primers containing homologous arms corresponding to the intended insertion sites (Table 1). The purified ZsGreen amplicon and the SwaI‑linearized vector were assembled using the NEBuilder® HiFi DNA Assembly kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol, and the assembly mixture was then electroporated into GB05‑dir. Transformants were selected with chloramphenicol and counter‑selected against the ccdB toxin, resulting in the final plasmids: p15A‑cm‑FAdV‑4‑1966/ZsGreen and p15A‑cm‑FAdV‑8a‑ΔORF11/ZsGreen. Both constructs were verified by EcoRI restriction digestion and sequencing.

Rescue and characterization of recombinant reporter viruses

The rescue of each recombinant virus was performed by transfecting LMH cells with 2 µg of the respective full-length infectious clone, using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer's protocol. Following transfection, the cells were monitored daily for signs of cytopathic effect (CPE) and fluorescent signals. At 4 days post-transfection when obvious CPE and fluorescence were observed, the viruses were harvested and verified by PCR and sequencing. The resultant reporter viruses were designated as rFAdV‑4‑ZsGreen and rFAdV‑8a-ZsGreen. The genetic stability of the recombinant viruses was confirmed by 20 serial passages and genome sequencing. The growth kinetics of the recombinant viruses were evaluated by inoculating LMH cells with each recombinant reporter virus and the corresponding parental virus; the viruses were harvested at 24, 48, 72, 96, 108, and 120 hpi for TCID50 measurement.

Preparation of antisera against FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11

To prepare chicken antisera specific for FAdV-4, -8a, -8b and -11, SPF chickens were immunized with the corresponding inactivated vaccine. All animal experiments were conducted under the approval of the Animal Care and Use Committee of Henan Agricultural University. The parental FAdV-4, -8a, -8b and -11 were concentrated to 106 TCID50/100 μL, inactivated with formaldehyde in 0.1% final concentration, and emulsified with an equal volume of ADJ 501 (W/O/W) adjuvant (Zhengzhou Adjuvant Biotech Co., Ltd., China). For the establishment of F-VNT, a total of 40 antisera (10 per serotype, FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11) were collected from SPF chickens at multiple time points during the four-week period following immunization with inactivated homologous virus vaccines. All samples were heat‑inactivated at 56 °C for 30 min. These antisera, which exhibited a broad range of neutralizing antibody titers, were subsequently used for correlation analysis between the conventional VNT and the newly established F-VNT.

Establishment of the fluorescence-based neutralization test

Optimization of experimental conditions for the F-VNT

To identify the optimal reporter virus dose for the F-VNT, each recombinant FAdV was diluted in 50 μL of maintenance medium (DMEM/F12 medium containing 2% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin) to achieve doses corresponding to 100, 200, 300, and 400 TCID50. Each dilution was combined with an equal volume of either FAdV-negative control serum or its homologous serotype-specific positive antiserum. After incubation at 37°C for 1 h, the virus-serum mixtures were used to inoculate LMH cell monolayers in 96-well plates. Each well was then supplemented with the maintenance medium to a final volume of 200 μL. The plates were subsequently incubated at 37°C under 5% CO2. Cell monolayers were examined under a fluorescence microscope at 12-hour intervals up to 36 hours post-infection (hpi) to monitor green fluorescence. For each serotype, the optimal challenge dose and readout time were defined as the lowest virus dose and earliest timepoint at which widespread, distinct fluorescence was observed in wells containing negative serum, while wells containing homologous positive serum showed a complete absence of fluorescence. This selected dose and readout time provided a clear and consistent differentiation between neutralized and non-neutralized samples in subsequent neutralization assays. The fluorescent-based NAb titer was defined as the highest serum dilution that resulted in a 100% reduction in fluorescence compared to the virus control wells.

Assessment of the specificity of the F-VNT

To evaluate the specificity of the established F-VNT, the cross-neutralizing activity of antisera against other common avian pathogens was examined. Specifically, positive sera for avian influenza virus (H9N2 AIV FJSW strain), Newcastle disease virus (NDV genotype VII YN1106 strain), infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV TL strain), and infectious laryngotracheitis virus (ILTV WG strain) were prepared in the laboratory by immunizing SPF chickens with the corresponding vaccine prepared in lab. F-VNT was performed to test their ability to neutralize rFAdV-4-ZsGreen, rFAdV‑8a-ZsGreen, rFAdV-8b-ZsGreen, and rFAdV-11-ZsGreen. All antisera were heat-inactivated and filtered prior to the assay, and then mixed with the determined optimal dosage of each recombinant fluorescent reporter virus. After incubation at 37 °C for 1 h, the virus–serum mixtures were added to LMH cells seeded in 96-well plates, and the cells were observed for the fluorescent signals at 12 h intervals until 36 hpi.

Detection of cross-neutralization activities of anti-FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11 sera against homologous and heterologous serotypes

Antisera generated in SPF chickens against the four FAdV serotypes were examined for neutralizing activity against both their respective homologous virus and the heterologous viruses corresponding to the remaining three serotypes to evaluate the cross-reactive NAb responses. Each antiserum was heat-inactivated and underwent two-fold serial dilutions from 21 to 220 using DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 1% antibiotics to a final volume of 50 μL. An equal volume of the predetermined optimal virus dose was added to each serum dilution. After mixing, the virus-serum complexes were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h for neutralization and then transferred onto LMH cell monolayers in 96-well plates. Plates were incubated at 37 °C under 5% CO2 and examined for fluorescent signals at 24 hpi. The neutralizing antibody titer was recorded as the reciprocal of the highest serum dilution that fully suppressed fluorescence induced by the reporter virus. Antigenic relatedness (R) between viral serotypes was calculated according to the Archetti and Horsfall formula, R = (r1 × r2)1/2, where r1 denotes the titer ratio of heterologous neutralization (antiserum to virus A vs. virus B) to homologous neutralization (antiserum to virus A vs. virus A), and r2 represents the corresponding inverse ratio (antiserum to virus B vs. virus A relative to its homologous titer). An R value approaching 1 reflects a high degree of antigenic similarity between the two viruses.

Comparison with the conventional VNT

Serotype-specific antisera with varying antibody titers were prepared in the laboratory against FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11. A total of 40 antisera (10 per serotype) were used to evaluate the correlation between the novel F-VNT and the conventional VNT. In the conventional VNT, sera were heat-inactivated and subjected to two-fold serial dilution before being incubated with 200 TCID50 of the parental strains FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11 at 37°C for 1 h. The mixture was then applied to LMH cell monolayers. After 2 h of virus adsorption, the inoculum was aspirated and replaced with maintenance medium. Cells were examined for CPE under a light microscope at 96 hpi. The neutralization titer, defined as the reciprocal of the highest serum dilution that prevented CPE in 50% of the inoculated wells, was determined using the Reed-Muench method. For the established F-VNT, the same antisera were similarly diluted, mixed with the recombinant reporter corresponding viruses, and assayed as described previously. The agreement between NAb titers obtained from the two methods was analyzed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient.

Data analysis

The correlation between titers obtained from the F-VNT and the conventional VNT was analyzed by Pearson's correlation coefficient. Statistical evaluations were conducted in GraphPad Prism (version 10.0). For comparisons involving multiple groups, one‑way ANOVA was applied, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant and p > 0.05 reported as not significant (ns). For each experiment, results from three independent experiments were summarized as the mean ± standard deviation.

Results

Characterization of recombinant reporter viruses

To establish a fluorescence-based VNT for FAdVs of different serotypes, recombinant fluorescent reporter viruses were constructed using a seamless DNA recombineering technique and FAdV reverse genetics platforms. For FAdV-4, a ZsGreen expression cassette was inserted into its natural 1966-bp deletion region; for FAdV-8a, -8b, and -11, the ZsGreen cassette was used to replace the ORF11 gene (Fig. 1a). The corresponding recombinant viruses, rFAdV-4-ZsGreen, rFAdV‑8a-ZsGreen, rFAdV-8b-ZsGreen, and rFAdV-11-ZsGreen, were successfully rescued in LMH cells, as confirmed by the detection of CPE and fluorescence signals, PCR amplification, and sequencing. The reporter viruses replicated efficiently in LMH cell cultures and expressed strong green fluorescence as early as 12 hpi, with peak intensity observed at 24 hpi (Fig. 1b). The fluorescence was easily detectable and provided a clear distinction between infected and uninfected cells. Growth kinetic profiles of the reporter viruses were comparable to those of their respective parental strains (Fig. 1c). The genetic stability of the reporter viruses was verified through 20 serial passages in LMH cells, followed by genome sequencing, which confirmed the consistent expression of the inserted ZsGreen gene.

(B) Rescue and fluorescence validation of the recombinant viruses. Representative fluorescence microscopy images confirmed successful virus rescue and robust ZsGreen expression (Scale bar = 100 μm). (C) Replication kinetics of the recombinant reporter viruses compared to their respective parental strains.

Establishment of the novel F-VNT

Using the constructed recombinant reporter viruses of FAdVs, a novel F-VNT was established. The optimal viral dose for the assay was determined through a preliminary assay. The reporter viruses, rFAdV-4-ZsGreen, rFAdV‑8a-ZsGreen, rFAdV-8b-ZsGreen, and rFAdV-11-ZsGreen were diluted to graded doses (ranging from 100 to 400 TCID50 per well) and separately incubated with equal volumes of corresponding chicken antisera against FAdV-4, -8a, -8b and -11, as well as with negative control chicken serum. After incubation, each virus‑serum mixture was added to LMH cell monolayers in 96‑well plates, and green fluorescence was observed at multiple post‑infection time points (12, 24, and 36 hpi). The results confirmed that positive antisera effectively neutralized the homologous reporter virus, whereas negative serum showed no neutralizing activity. Based on the criteria that optimal conditions should yield strong, distinct green fluorescence in negative serum controls while maintaining clear neutralization endpoints in positive sera, the viral dose of 200 TCID50 per well and the readout time of 24 hpi were selected as the standardized parameters for the F‑VNT (Fig. 2, Fig. 3). Although the four recombinant viruses showed different growth kinetics, 200 TCID50 was identified as the optimal dose for all serotypes, as it consistently provided clear fluorescence in negative controls and complete neutralization by homologous antisera at 24 hpi. Furthermore, while fluorescence was detectable as early as 12 hpi, the signal intensity and uniformity at 24 hpi with 200 TCID50 provided the most reliable and reproducible endpoint for neutralization assessment, with a clear distinction between neutralized and non-neutralized wells.

Using the optimized F‑VNT, the NAb titers of the homologous antisera against each reporter virus were determined. As shown in Fig. 4, the Log2 NAb titers for FAdV‑4, FAdV‑8a, FAdV‑8b, and FAdV‑11 antisera against their respective reporter viruses were 13, 13, 14, and 14, respectively. These results indicate that the generated antisera possess high and serotype‑specific neutralizing activity, and the F‑VNT can effectively quantify NAb levels with a wide dynamic range.

The specificity of the newly developed F-VNT was validated by testing antisera against other common avian pathogens: H9N2 AIV FJSW strain, NDV genotype VII YN1106 strain, IBDV TL strain, and ILTV WG strain. None of these heterologous antisera exhibited any detectable neutralizing activity against the FAdV fluorescent reporter viruses, which confirmed the specificity and reliability of the F-VNT in distinguishing FAdV-specific neutralization activities (Fig. 5).

Cross-neutralization activity of anti-FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11 sera against heterologous serotypes

Utilizing the established F‑VNT, the cross‑neutralizing antibody titers of antisera raised against FAdV‑4, ‑8a, ‑8b, and ‑11 were measured to assess cross‑reactive antibody responses across different FAdV serotypes. The antigenic relatedness among different serotypes was summarized in Table 2. Notably, the antibodies against FAdV-4 showed no neutralizing effect on FAdV-8a, -8b, or -11 (Fig. 6a). Antisera against FAdV-8a and FAdV-8b exhibited a relatively high degree of cross-neutralization activity, suggesting significant antigenic cross-reactivity between these two serotypes (Fig. 6b and 6c). The FAdV-11 antisera demonstrated some neutralization activity against FAdV-8a and -8b; however, the cross-neutralization titers were relatively low (Fig. 6d).

Correlation analysis between the novel F-VNT and the conventional VNT

Correlation analysis was performed using 10 chicken antisera prepared in the laboratory against each of the four parental viruses, FAdV-4, -8a, -8b and -11. The NAb titers measured by the novel F-VNT showed a strong positive correlation with those obtained from the conventional VNT (Fig. 7). These results demonstrated a high level of agreement between the two methods and supported the use of the novel F-VNT as a practical alternative to the conventional VNT for measuring NAb titers in clinical samples from chickens.

Correlation analysis between the novel F-VNT and the conventional VNT. Neutralizing antibody titers in ten chicken antisera against each parental FAdV serotype were measured in parallel by both assays. The conventional VNT employed the wild-type viruses (FAdV-4, -8a, -8b, and -11), whereas the novel F-VNT used the corresponding recombinant reporter viruses. The correlation analysis revealed a strong linear agreement between the two methods, with coefficients of determination (R2) of 0.966 for FAdV-4 (A), 0.979 for FAdV-8a (B), 0.941 for FAdV-8b (C), and 0.95 for FAdV-11 (D).

Discussion

The global circulation of FAdV-4 causing HHS and FAdV-8a, -8b, and -11 causing IBH represents a persistent and economically significant threat to poultry industries worldwide (Cizmecigil et al., 2020; Hosseini et al., 2020; Feng et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Islam et al., 2026). These diseases contribute to substantial economic losses due to mortality, reduced productivity, and costs associated with control measures. Therefore, the development of a sensitive and specific serological assay for monitoring FAdV serotype distribution, understanding spatiotemporal dynamics of the diseases, and assessing flock immunity status is crucial.

In this study, we successfully developed a novel fluorescence-based neutralization test for the key FAdV serotypes causing HHS and IBH. This test was based on the use of recombinant reporter viruses engineered to express the ZsGreen fluorescent protein. These reporter viruses were constructed by leveraging the FAdV reverse genetics platforms established in our previous works. These platforms enabled seamless and precise DNA recombineering of the parental FAdV strains, allowing for the generation of the desired recombinant viruses within one week. The resulting reporter viruses exhibited growth kinetics comparable to their respective parental strains and demonstrated genetic stability across multiple passages, confirming their suitability as reliable and consistent tools for the subsequent neutralization assays.

Building directly upon the reporter viruses, we established a robust and efficient F-VNT. The F-VNT enables real-time, high-throughput detection of neutralizing antibodies, eliminating the subjective interpretation required for scoring cytopathic effects in conventional assays. This method allows for the clear determination of NAb titers within 24 hours. The readout is based on a distinct fluorescence pattern: wells without neutralizing antibodies display widespread, bright green fluorescence, whereas wells containing neutralizing antibodies show a clear, dose-dependent reduction in fluorescent signal. By shortening the test duration from several days to 24 hours, the F-VNT facilitates a significantly faster response time, which is critical for efficient diagnostic applications and timely vaccine evaluation in field applications. Compared to a previous study that used 10,000-20,000 TCID50 of a FA4-EGFP reporter virus to detect neutralizing antibodies against FAdV-4 (Guo et al., 2022), our F-VNT requires only 200 TCID50 per well. This difference is attributed to the brighter fluorescence of ZsGreen driven by a CMV promoter, allowing reliable detection at lower virus doses. Furthermore, the reliance on fluorescence detection removes the inter-observer variability in the conventional VNT, leading to more reproducible and reliable results. The strong correlation between NAb titers measured by the F-VNT and those obtained using the conventional VNT confirms the reliability and accuracy of this new method. Moreover, the high specificity demonstrated through testing antisera against other common avian pathogens reinforces that the F-VNT is highly specific to FAdVs and minimizes the risk of cross-reactivity in field applications.

Using the constructed novel F-VNT, a cross-neutralization analysis was performed using antisera against FAdV-4, -8a, -8b and -11. Distinct patterns of antigenic relatedness among the four FAdV serotypes were observed. While homologous neutralization titers were consistently high, heterologous neutralization was limited, indicating significant antigenic divergence. Specifically, FAdV-4 antiserum showed no neutralizing activity against the other three serotypes, and conversely, antisera against FAdV-8a, -8b, and -11 could not neutralize FAdV-4. A partial cross-neutralization was observed between FAdV-8a and FAdV-8b. Furthermore, a low level of cross-neutralization was detected among FAdV-8a, -8b, and -11. These results underscored the strong serotype specificity of neutralizing antibodies and highlighted the critical need for developing multivalent vaccines to provide broad protection against the major pathogenic FAdV serotypes.

In summary, we have established a highly efficient, rapid, and high-throughput fluorescence-based virus neutralization test for FAdV-4, FAdV-8a, FAdV-8b, and FAdV-11. This test addresses the critical limitations of the conventional VNT and provides the poultry industry with a powerful tool for rapid serodiagnosis and monitoring of vaccine-induced immunity against HHS and IBH.

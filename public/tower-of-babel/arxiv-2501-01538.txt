Bounds and Optimal Results for the Total Irregularity Measure
Akbar Ali, Darko Dimitrov, Tamás Réti, Abeer M. Albalahi, Amjad E. Hamza

Abstract
A (molecular) graph in which all vertices have the same degree is known as a regular graph. According to Gutman, Hansen, and Mélot [ J. Chem. Inf. Model. 45 (2005) 222–230], it is of interest to measure the irregularity of nonregular molecular graphs both for descriptive purposes and for QSAR/QSPR studies. The graph invariants that can be used to measure the irregularity of graphs are referred to as irregularity measures. One of the well-studied irregularity measures is the “total irregularity” measure, which was introduced about a decade ago. Bounds and optimization problems for this measure have already been extensively studied. A considerable number of existing results (concerning this measure) also hold for molecular graphs; particularly, the ones regarding lower bounds and minimum values of the mentioned measure. The primary objective of the present review article is to collect the existing bounds and optimal results concerning the total irregularity measure. Several open problems related to the existing results on the total irregularity measure are also given.

Introduction

All graphs considered in the present article are simple unless otherwise stated (for instance, the graphs considered in Theorem
34

[

(and

,

]
, respectively).

Irregularity in graphs typically refers to the properties of graphs that deviate from certain regular patterns; for instance, see the book
[
10
]
.
Generally, an irregularity measure of a graph
G
is a numerical quantity used to assess the extent to which
G
deviates from being regular. Particularly, a numerical quantity
IM
associated with a connected graph
G
is said to be an irregularity measure of
G
if it satisfies the following condition:
IM(G)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
G
is regular; for example, see
[
13
]
.
Irregularity measures may be significant in dealing with certain problems raised in several fields, including network analysis and chemistry; for example, see
[
24
,
34
,
42
,
56
,
33
]
.

There exist many irregularity measures in the literature; for instance, see the papers
[
56
,
29
,
41
,
53
,
5
,
28
]
and the survey
[
15
]
. In the present article, we are concerned with the total irregularity, which is an irregularity measure introduced around a decade ago by Abdo, Brandt, and Dimitrov in
[
4
]
. The total irregularity of a graph
G
is denoted by
\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)
and is defined as

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset V}|d_{x}-d_{y}|,

where
V
is the vertex set of
G
and
d_{x}
denotes the degree of the vertex
x\in V
. The total irregularity is an extended version of Albertson’s well-known irregularity measure
[
7
]
, defined as

\,{\rm irr}(G)=\sum_{xy\in E}|d_{x}-d_{y}|,

where
E
is the edge set of
G
. It needs to be noted
[
20
]
that

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)=irr(G)+irr\left(\overline{G}\right),

(1)

where
\overline{G}
is the complement of
G
; it is defined as a graph with the same vertex set as
G
has, while two vertices in
\overline{G}
are adjacent if and only if they are nonadjacent in
G
.
We also note that for any (nonregular) disconnected graph
G
, we have
\,{\rm irr}(G)=0
if and only if every component of
G
is regular. Conversely,
\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)=0
holds if and only if
G
is regular, regardless of whether
G
is connected.

Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers such that
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\neq 0
. The Gini coefficient
\zeta
(also known as Gini index, named after Gini
[
40
]
) for
X
can be written (see
[
57
]
) as

\zeta(X)=\frac{1}{2n\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|=1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{2}{n\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}ix_{i}\,.

(2)

If
G
is an
n
-order graph with size
m\geq 1
and degree sequence
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
, such that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
, then by using (
2
)
the Gini index for
G
is defined (for example, see
[
32
,
14
,
30
]
) as follows:

\zeta(G)=\frac{1}{4mn}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{i}-d_{j}|=1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{i=1}^{n}id_{i}\,.

(3)

Thus,
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
of
G
can also be rewritten as

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)=2nm\cdot\zeta(G)=2(n+1)m-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}id_{i}\,,

where
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
.

The main purpose of the present review article is to collect existing bounds and optimal results on
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
. The remaining part of the paper is organized as follows. The next section provides most of the notations and definitions used in the subsequent sections. Section
3
consists of two subsections: the first one is devoted to the results on the greatest value of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
over different graph classes while the second one is about its least values. Section
4
also is divided into two subsections: the first one gives lower bounds on
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
while the second one provides its upper bounds.
Several open problems related to existing results on
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
are given in Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide most of the notations and definitions used in the subsequent sections.

By an
n
-order graph, we mean a graph of order
n
. A nontrivial graph is any
n
-order graph with
n\geq 2
. By a molecular graph, we mean a connected graph of the maximum degree not more than
4
. The
n
-order star, path, and complete graphs are denoted by
S_{n}
,
P_{n}
, and
K_{n}
, respectively. A graph that is not regular is known as a nonregular graph. A pendent vertex is a vertex with degree 1. A vertex with a degree greater than 2 is referred to as a branching vertex. The degree set of a graph
G
is the set of all distinct elements of the degree sequence of
G
.

A nontrivial path
S
in a tree
T
is said to be a segment of
T
if the end vertices of
S
have degrees different from 2 in
T
and every other vertex (if exists) of
S
have degree
2
in
T
.

The cyclomatic number of an
n
-order connected graph with size
m
is the number
m-n+1
. By an
n
-order unicyclic, or bicyclic, or tricyclic graph, we mean a connected
n
-order graph with the cyclomatic number 1, or 2, or 3, respectively.

The distance
d(x,y)
between two vertices
x
and
y
of a graph is the length of a shortest path between them. The eccentricity of a vertex
x
in a graph with vertex set
V
is denoted by
\epsilon_{x}
and is defined as
\epsilon_{x}=\max_{u\in V}d(x,u)
.
The diameter of a graph
G
is the greatest number among the eccentricities of all vertices of
G
.
The non-self-centrality number of a connected graph
G
is denoted as
N(G)
and is defined
[
61
]
as

N(G)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq V}|\epsilon_{x}-\epsilon_{y}|.

Following
[
11
]
, we recall the definitions of a polyomino system and related concepts as follows: “A polyomino system is a connected geometric figure obtained by concatenating congruent regular squares side to side in a plane in such a way that the figure divides
the plane into one infinite (external) region and a number of finite (internal) regions,
and all internal regions must be congruent regular squares. In a polyomino system,
two squares are said to be adjacent if they share a side. The characteristic graph of a given polyomino system consists of vertices corresponding
to squares of the system; two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding
squares are adjacent. A polyomino system whose characteristic graph is the path
graph is called a polyomino chain. In a polyomino chain, a square having one (respectively two) neighboring
square(s) is called a terminal (respectively, nonterminal) square. Any polyomino chain
can be represented by a graph, in which the edges represent the sides of a polyomino chain while the vertices correspond to the points where two sides of a square meet. In
what follows, by a polyomino chain, we always mean the graph corresponding to the
polyomino chain. In a polyomino chain, a nonterminal square having a vertex of degree 2
is known as a kink. A linear polyomino chain is the one, without kinks. A zigzag polyomino chain is the one, consisting of only kinks and/or terminal squares.”

4
Bounds on the Total Irregularity

This section is divided into two subsections: the first one gives lower bounds on
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
while the second one provides its upper bounds. Before going towards the first subsection of the present section, we remark here that we can derive in many cases bounds concerning
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
from the existing bounds of
\,{\rm irr}
; for instance, for a graph
G
of size
m
, the chain of inequalities

\sqrt{\sigma(G)}\leq\,{\rm irr}(G)\leq\sqrt{m\cdot\sigma(G)},

holds (see
[
54
]
) with either of the equalities if and only if
G
is regular, where
\sigma(G)
is the sigma index
[
36
]
of
G
(see also
[
8
,
43
,
26
,
51
]
) defined as

\sigma(G)=\sum_{xy\in E}(d_{x}-d_{y})^{2};

consequently, for an
n
-order graph
G
of size
m
,
by Equation (
1
), we have

\sqrt{\sigma(G)}+\sqrt{\sigma\left(\overline{G}\right)}\leq\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\leq\sqrt{m\cdot\sigma(G)}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-m\right)\sigma\left(\overline{G}\right)},

with either of the equalities if and only if
G
is regular.

4.1
Lower bounds on the total irregularity

We start this section with the remark that the inequality
\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\geq\,{\rm irr}(G)
holds for every graph
G
, because of the definitions of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
and

.

Let
n_{s}
denote the number of vertices of a graph
G
having degree
s

Theorem 19
.

[
16
]

If
G
is an
n
-order nonregular graph, then

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\geq n_{s}(n-n_{s}),

where
n_{s}\neq 0
with
1\leq s\leq n-1
.

The first Zagreb index  —  introduced in
[
44
]
(see its most-recent surveys
[
22
,
12
]
)  —  for a graph
G
is denoted by
Z_{1}(G)
and is defined as

Z_{1}(G)=\sum_{x\in V}(d_{x})^{2}.

Theorem 20
.

[
38
]

Let
G
be a nonregular
n
-order graph with maximum degree
\Delta
, size
m
and minimum degree
\delta
. Then,

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\geq\frac{n\cdot Z_{1}(G)-4m^{2}}{\Delta-\delta},

with equality if and only if the degree set of
G
is
\{\delta,\Delta\}
.
Also,

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}\Delta(\Delta-1)+2mn(2\Delta-1)-4m^{2}}{\Delta-\delta},

with the equality if and only if the degree set of
G
is
\{\Delta-1,\Delta\}
. Furthermore,

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\geq\frac{2mn(2\delta+1)-4m^{2}-n^{2}\delta(\delta+1)}{\Delta-\delta},

with equality if and only if the degree set of
G
is
\{\delta,\delta+1\}
.

A graph in which all vertices have the same eccentricity is known as a self-centered graph. By a non-self-centered graph, we mean a graph that is not self-centered.

Let
K_{n}(k)
denote the
n
-order graph constructed from the complete graph

by dropping
k
pairwise nonadjacent edge(s), where

and

. Take
\mathcal{G}_{n}=\{K_{n}(k):1\leq k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}
.

Theorem 21
.

[
62
]

If
G
is an
n
-order connected non-self-centered graph with diameter

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\geq N(G)

Theorem 22
.

[

.

[

If

4.2
Upper bounds on the total irregularity

In this section, we state the existing upper bounds on
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
.

Theorem 32
.

[
27
]

For every connected
n
-order graph
G
, the following inequality holds:

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\cdot irr(G).

(6)

The equality in (
6
) is attained for infinitely many values of
n
.

The inequality given in Theorem
32
can be improved if we restrict ourselves to trees.

Theorem 33
.

[
27
]

For a nontrivial
n
-order tree
T
, the following inequality holds:

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(T)\leq(n-2)\,irr(T),

with equality if and only if
T
is the path graph
P_{n}
.

Theorem 34
.

[
30
]

For an
n
-order graph
G
, possibly containing loops, with
m
edges, the following inequality holds:

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(G)\leq 2(n-1)m.

(7)

If
G
contains
n-1
vertices of degree
0
and one vertex incident with
m
loops then the equality in (
7
) is attained.

The Cartesian product
G_{1}\square G_{2}
of two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is the graph with the vertex set
V(G_{1})\times V(G_{2})
, where two vertices
(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(b_{1},b_{2})
of
G_{1}\square G_{2}
are adjacent if and only if either
a_{1}b_{1}
is an edge in
G_{1}
and
a_{2}=b_{2}
, or
a_{1}=b_{1}
and
a_{2}b_{2}
is an edge in
G_{2}
.

Theorem 35
.

[
62
]

If
T
is an
n
-order tree with diameter
d
and maximum degree

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(T)<N(T)\quad\text{and}\quad\,{\rm irr}_{t}(T\square K_{2})<N(T\square K_{2}),

where

,

is the non-self-centrality number of

.

[

\,{\rm irr}_{t}(P_{n}^{*})<N(P_{n}^{*})\quad\text{and}\quad\,{\rm irr}_{t}((P_{n}^{*})^{*})<N((P_{n}^{*})^{*}),

5
Open Problems

Although numerous open problems concerning
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
can be posed, the problems presented here are closely related to existing results on
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
.

Open problems and conjectures concerning the closely related irregularity measures,
\sigma
-irregularity and
\sigma_{t}
-irregularity, will be omitted here. For some of these problems and conjectures, we refer the reader to
[
26
,
28
,
35
,
50
]
.

The problem of finding graphs with the extremum values of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
among all fixed-order trees with a given number of segments or branching vertices was addressed in
[
68
]
; in the case of the maximum value of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
, the obtained graphs are not molecular ones. This observation suggests the following problem:

Problem 41
.

Characterize graphs attaining the maximum value of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
in the class all fixed-order molecular trees with a given number of (i) segments or (ii) branching vertices.

Theorem
4
provides all graphs possessing the maximum value of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
over the class of all fixed-order connected graphs; one may think of establish a similar result by fixing the size instead of order, which yields the next problem.

Problem 42
.

Characterize graphs attaining the maximum value of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
over each of the following graph classes: (i) class of all connected graphs with a given size, (ii) class of all molecular graphs with a given size.

It is natural to ask establishing a maximal version of Theorem
12
; or, at least establishing the mentioned version about the first three maximum values of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
. Thus, we pose the following problem related Theorem
12
:

Problem 43
.

Characterize graphs achieving the first three maximum values of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
among all fixed-order molecular graphs with a given cyclomatic number.

Keeping in mind Theorem
13
and existing literature, we pose the next problem.

Problem 44
.

Characterize graphs achieving the first three maximum values of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
among all fixed-order connected graphs with a given cyclomatic number (greater than
6
).

Although Theorems
27
and
28
provide a partial solution to Problem
26
, and Theorem
38
gives a partial solution to Problem
37
, both of these problems are still generally open.

We end this paper by posing the minimal version of Problem
42
as follows:

Problem 45
.

Characterize graphs attaining the minimum value of
\,{\rm irr}_{t}
over each of the graph classes given in Problem
42
.

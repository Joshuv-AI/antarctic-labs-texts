Novel possible symmetries of S S -matrix generated by ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} -graded Lie superalgebras
Ren Ito, Akio Nago

Abstract
In this paper, we explore the ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} -graded Lie (super)algebras as novel possible generators of symmetries of S S -matrix. As the results, we demonstrate that a ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} -graded extension of the supersymmetric algebra can be a symmetry of S S -matrix. Furthermore, it turns out that a ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} -graded Lie algebra appears as internal symmetries. They are natural extensions of Coleman-Mandula theorem and Haag-Łopuszański-Sohnius theorem, which are the no-go theorems for generators of symmetries of S S -matrix.

Introduction

Since the introduction of
SU(4)

[
1
,
2
]
.
The quark model introduced in the Standard Model is a model using
SU(3)

[
3

.
It successfully describes the relationship among hadrons with the same spin.
Furthermore, if we consider the
SU(6)
symmetry for quark flavor and spin, we can embed hadrons with different spins in the same representation.

However, such
SU(6)
symmetry that unify and describe different spins are approximate symmetries that exist in the non-relativistic limit, and despite some success, all attempts to generalize them to relativistic quantum theory have failed
[
4
]
.
Indeed, it was shown by Coleman and Mandula in 1967 that such symmetries are forbidden in relativistic quantum field theory (QFT), known as the Coleman-Mandula theorem
[
5
]
.
The theorem says that all possible symmetries of
S
-matrix generated by Lie algebra are only Poincaré symmetry and internal symmetry such that its generators commute with Poincaré algebra.
This prevents us to unify Poincaré symmetry and internal symmetries into a single multiplet.
The reason for the strong restriction of this theorem is that the symmetries allowed in the S-matrix of QFT were searched only in the framework of Lie algebras.
If we want to avoid the Coleman-Mandula theorem, it is natural to explore symmetries generated by algebras beyond the Lie algebra.

In searching for symmetries that do not conflict with the Coleman-Mandula theorem, Haag, Łopsuzański and Sohnius extended the algebra to Lie superalgebra, so that the symmetry generators are closed not only for comutation relations but also for anti-commutation relations
[
7
]

This showed that supersymmetry is the only possible symmetry as an extension of Poincare symmetry, which is known as the Haag-Łopsuzański-Sohnius theorem.
As its result, if we consider the Lie superalgebra as symmetry generators of the
S
-matrix, supersymmetry is allowed in QFT.

Preliminaries

2.1
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded Lie superalgebra

We recall the definition of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA
[
8
,
9
]
.

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
refers to the
n
-fold direct product of the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
, namely
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\cdots
(repeated
n
times).

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded vector space (over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
), which is the direct sum of homogeneous vector subspaces labeled by an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{\vec{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{a}}.

(2.1)

An element of
\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{a}}
is said to have the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-grading
\vec{a}
.

Definition 2
.

We define the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-Lie bracket by

\llbracket A,B\rrbracket=AB-(-1)^{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}BA,

(2.2)

where
A\in\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{a}},\ B\in\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}
, and
\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}
is the standard scalar product of
n
-dimensional vectors.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded vector space is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded Lie superalgebra if

\llbracket A,B\rrbracket\in\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{a}+\vec{b}}
and the Jacobi identity is satisfied:

\displaystyle\llbracket A,\llbracket B,C\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket A,B\rrbracket,C\rrbracket+(-1)^{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}\llbracket B,\llbracket A,C\rrbracket\rrbracket.

(2.3)

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA can be regarded as an extended Lie algebra because the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-Lie bracket is a commutator (anti-commutator) for
\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}
is even (odd). Actually when
n=0
, a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA is equivalent to an ordinary Lie algebras. Furthermore, when
n=1
, it corresponds to the standard LSA, which consists of a bosonic sector (
0
-grading) and a fermionic sector (
1
-grading). It is known that for
n\geq 2
, a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA also generates a symmetry.

2.2
Coleman-Mandula theorem

In this subsection, we briefly review the Coleman-Mandula theorem, which is known as the no-go theorem for symmetries in QFT
[
6
,
5
]
.
The theorem states that
“The continious symmetries of the
S
-matrix in QFT can only be generated by a Lie algebra consisting of the translation generator
P_{\mu}
, the homogeneous Lorentz transformation generator
M_{\mu\nu}
, and the internal symmetry generator
B_{l}
”.
Namely they discussed possible symmetries of the
S
-matrix generated by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA’s with
n=0
and proved Poincaré symmetry and internal symmetry were only allowed in QFT.
To consider the avoidance of this theorem, we look at the proof of this theorem
[
6
,
2
]
.
They used the following resonable assumptions in their proof,

1.

The
S
-matrix is defined in relativistic local field theory,

2.

The finite number of particle types with masses lighter than an arbitrary mass
M>0
is finite,

3.

The amplitudes for elastic two-body scattering are analytic functions of the scattering angle at almost all energies and angles.

The symmetry generator here means a Hermitian operator on the Hilbert space which is commutative with the
S
-matrix and acts on multi particle states as well as single particle ones.
Note that we work in the momentum space so that any matrix element of the group generator is a distribution in momentum space.

First, we consider all generators which commute with
P_{\mu}
.
Any compact Lie algebra can be decomposed into a direct sum of a semi-simple part
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{S}}
and an Abelian part
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{A}}
(Levi-Decomposition).
Since
P_{\mu}
commute with itself,
P_{\mu}
is in Abelian part:
P_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{A}}
.
Thus, we consider the generators
B_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{S}}
.
We transform the commutation relation between
P_{\mu}
and
B_{\alpha}
by Lorentz transformation

\displaystyle[U(\Lambda)B_{\alpha}U^{-1}(\Lambda),{\Lambda^{\nu}}_{\mu}P_{\nu}]=0

(2.4)

where
U(\Lambda)
is the unitary operator on Hilbert space and represents the Lorentz transformation.
Since
{\Lambda^{\nu}}_{\mu}
is regular,
U(\Lambda)B_{\alpha}U^{-1}(\Lambda)
must be a linear combination of
B_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle U(\Lambda)B_{\alpha}U^{-1}(\Lambda)={D(\Lambda)^{\beta}}_{\alpha}B_{\beta},

(2.5)

where
D(\Lambda)
is the representation matrix of Lorentz group.
Since the metric of semi-simple part
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{S}}
is positive definite,
D(\Lambda)
must be an identity matrix for
B_{\alpha}
to satisfy the same algebraic relation after Lorentz transformation.
Noting that the Lorentz group is not compact, the symmetry generated by
B_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{S}}
is independent of Lorentz symmetry.
The
B_{\alpha}
is independent of the momentum and acts as an identity matrix with respect to the spin, which is the generator of internal symmetry
B_{l}
.

Next, we consider all symmetry generators which do not commute with
P_{\mu}
.
All symmetry generators
G_{\alpha}
act on single particle states as follows

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}\ket{n ,p}=\sum_{n^{\prime}}\int\differential^{4}p^{\prime}\left(K_{\alpha}(p^{\prime},p)\right)_{n^{\prime},n}\ket{n^\prime,p^\prime},

(2.6)

where
n^{\prime},n
denote the spin and particle type.
The kernel
K_{\alpha}(p^{\prime},p)
will be zero if both
p
and
p^{\prime}
are not on the mass hyperboloid.
Furthermore, the kernel
K(p^{\prime},p)
is zero for all
p\neq p^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(p^{\prime},p)=K_{\alpha}(p)\delta^{4}(p-p^{\prime}).

(2.7)

From the second assumption, since the mass of the particle takes only discrete values,
G_{\alpha}
commute with the mass square operator.
Thus, the commutation relation of
P_{\mu}
and
G_{\alpha}
satisfies

\displaystyle[P^{\mu},G_{\alpha}]=a_{\alpha}^{\mu\nu}P_{\nu},

(2.8)

where
a_{\alpha}^{\mu\nu}
is anti-symmetric with respect to
\mu,\nu
.
It follows that
G_{\alpha}
, which does not commute with
P_{\mu}
, is the Lorentz generator

\displaystyle[P_{\mu},M_{\nu\rho}]=i(g_{\nu\mu}P_{\rho}-g_{\rho\mu}P_{\nu}).

(2.9)

Therefore, the symmetries of the
S
-matrix in QFT are only the Poincaré and internal symmetries.

2.3
Haag-Łopuszański-Sohnius theorem

In this subsection, we briefly review the Haag-Łopuszański-Sohnius theorem
[
7
]
, which extends the Coleman-Mandula theorem.
In (
2.6
),
G_{\alpha}
can be separated into two different types; bosonic type for same statistics
n^{\prime},n
, or fermionic type for different statistics
n^{\prime},n
.
All bosonic type generators are fully analyzed in the Coleman-Mandula theorem.
A complete basis of bosonic type generators are given by the
P_{\mu}
,
M_{\mu\nu}
and
B^{l}
.
On the other hand, the fermionic type generator is not mentioned in the Coleman-Mandula theorem because of the assumption that the all generators consist of Lie algebra.
They extended the Lie algebra to include anti-commutation relations in order to consider the fermionic type generators.
This theorem says that,
“Poincaré symmetry can only be extended to supersymmetry”

Now, let us see how this supersymmetry avoids the Coleman-Mandula theorem.
Note that

However, the proof in the previous section concludes that the possible

Therefore, it is enough to consider fermionic generator

Let

Since

The irreducible representation of the Lorentz group is specified by two spinors, which we write as

In general, because fermionic generators can have an odd number of spin indices, we denote the generator of

Now consider the anti-commutation relation between

Since it is producted from two fermionic generators, it must be bosonic and belong to

The bosonic generators are defined by the Coleman-Mandula theorem and are either

It follows that

\displaystyle=\sum_{L}u_{l,KL}Q^{10}_{\alpha\,L},\qquad

\displaystyle\{Q^{10}_{\alpha\,K},B_{l}^{11}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{L}v_{l,KL}Q^{01}_{\alpha\,L},

\displaystyle[Q^{01}_{\alpha\,K},\bar{Q}^{10}_{\dot{\alpha}\,L}]

\displaystyle=0.

(3.7)

We compute
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\sum_{l}a_{l,KL}\bar{s}_{l,KL}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\big(a_{l,KL}s_{l,NM}+a_{l,NL}s_{l,KM}\big)

\displaystyle\sum_{M}\big(s_{l,LM}a_{m,KM}-s_{l,KM}a_{m,LM}\big)

From these conditions, introducing the generator
Z^{11}_{KL}:=\sum_{l}c_{l,ML}B^{11}_{l}\in\mathfrak{g}_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle[Q^{01}_{\alpha\,K},\,Q^{10}_{\beta\,L}]

\displaystyle=0,

.

S
-matrix:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{(0,0)}

\displaystyle\ni P_{\mu},M_{\mu\nu},B^{00}_{l},Z^{(1)}_{KL},\bar{Z}^{(1)}_{KL},Z^{(2)}_{KL},\bar{Z}^{(2)}_{KL},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle\ni B^{11}_{l},Z^{11}_{KL},\bar{Z}^{11}_{KL},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{(0,1)}

\displaystyle\ni Q_{\alpha\,K}^{01},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,K}^{01},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{(1,0)}

\displaystyle\ni Q_{\alpha\,K}^{10},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,K}^{10}.

(3.13)

Therefore the following theorem holds.

Theorem 1
.

There exits the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded extension of supersymmetric algebra which is given by the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded LSA
\langle P_{\mu},Z^{(1)}_{KL},\bar{Z}^{(1)}_{KL},Z^{(2)}_{KL},\bar{Z}^{(2)}_{KL},Z^{11}_{KL},\bar{Z}^{11}_{KL},Q_{\alpha\,K}^{01},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,K}^{01},Q_{\alpha\,K}^{10},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,K}^{10}\rangle
. We call this
\mathcal{N}=(K|L)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-supersymmetric algebra, where
K
and
L
are the number of the
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
-graded supercharges.

Specifically, if we set the number of each supercharges
Q_{\alpha\,K}^{01},Q_{\alpha\,L}^{10}
to
K=L=1
, it corresponds to the
\mathcal{N}=2\ \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-supersymmetric algebra, which was introduced by Bruce
[
22
]
.
In the present case of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-grading, the internal symmetries are generated by ordinary Lie algebra
B^{00}_{l}
and
B^{11}_{l}
. This does not seem to be a big difference from the ordinary supersymmetry. However we will see that internal symmetries are generated by a graded Lie algebra when
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded (
n\geq 3
) extensions of supersymmetry are considered.

3.2
Possible
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded generators of symmetries

The discussion for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded LSA in the previous section can also be applied to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA. We define two subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
:

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})_{\text{ev.}}

\displaystyle:=\{\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\ |\ \vec{b}\cdot\vec{b}=0\mod 2\}

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})_{\text{od.}}

\displaystyle:=\{\vec{f}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\ |\ \vec{f}\cdot\vec{f}=1\mod 2\},

(3.14)

and the subalgebras of (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{\vec{b}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}\quad(\vec{b}\neq\vec{0}),\qquad\mathfrak{h}_{\vec{f}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{f}},

(3.15)

where
\vec{0}
is the identity element of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
.
Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\vec{b}}
is a Lie algebra, while
\mathfrak{h}_{\vec{f}}
is a LSA. This shows that we can obtain generators by applying the Coleman-Mandula theorem to
\mathfrak{h}_{\vec{b}}
and the Haag-Łopuszański-Sohnius theorem to
\mathfrak{h}_{\vec{f}}
. It can be seen that the following generators of the
S
-matrix are allowed:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{0}}

\displaystyle\ni P_{\mu},M_{\mu\nu},B^{\vec{0}}_{l},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}

\displaystyle\ni B^{\vec{b}}_{l},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{f}}

\displaystyle\ni Q_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,K}^{\vec{f}},

(3.16)

and the algebraic relations become

\displaystyle\llbracket Q_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,L}^{\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}^{\mu}P_{\mu}\delta_{KL}\delta^{\vec{f}\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},\qquad

\displaystyle\llbracket Q_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}},Q_{\beta\,L}^{\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{l}a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{l,KL}B^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}_{l},

\displaystyle\llbracket Q^{\vec{f}}_{\alpha\,L},B_{l}^{\vec{b}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=s^{(\vec{f},\vec{b})}_{l,LM}Q^{\vec{b}+\vec{f}}_{\alpha\,M},

\displaystyle\llbracket B_{l}^{\vec{b}},B_{m}^{\vec{b}\,^{\prime}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=i\sum_{k}h^{(\vec{b},\vec{b}\,^{\prime})}_{lmk}B_{k}^{\vec{b}+\vec{b}\,^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\llbracket\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}\,K}^{\vec{f}},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}\,L}^{\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}\varepsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\sum_{l}

\displaystyle\bar{a}^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{l,KL}B^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}_{l},

(3.17)

where
a,\,s
, and
h
are structure constants with the
\vec{0}
-grading, and
a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{l,KL}=(-1)^{\vec{f}\cdot\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}a^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{f})}_{l,LK}
and
h^{(\vec{b},\vec{b}\,^{\prime})}_{lmk}=-(-1)^{\vec{b}\cdot\vec{b}\,^{\prime}}h^{(\vec{b}\,^{\prime},\vec{b})}_{mlk}
.

In the same way as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-extension, we obtain the conditions for structure constants from the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-Jacobi identities (
2.3
). They are obtained from
(A,B,C)=(Q,\bar{Q},B)
:

\displaystyle s_{l,LM}^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\bar{s}_{l,ML}^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime})},

(3.18)

from
(A,B,C)=(Q,Q,\bar{Q})
:

\displaystyle\sum_{l}a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{l,LM}\bar{s}^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime\prime},\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{l,NK}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.19)

from
(A,B,C)=(Q,Q,Q)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{l}a^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{f}\,^{\prime\prime})}_{l,LM}s^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime\prime})}_{l,KN}-(-1)^{\vec{f}\cdot\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}\sum_{l}a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime\prime})}_{l,KM}s^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime\prime})}_{l,LN}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.20)

and from
(A,B,C)=(Q,Q,B)
:

\displaystyle s^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{b})}_{l,LM}a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime}+\vec{b})}_{m,KM}+(-1)^{\vec{f}\cdot\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}s^{(\vec{f},\vec{b})}_{l,KM}a^{(\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{f}+\vec{b})}_{m,LM}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}ih^{(\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime},\vec{b})}_{klm}a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{k,KL}.

(3.21)

Using these conditions and setting
Z_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}:=\sum_{l}a^{(\vec{f},\vec{f}\,^{\prime})}_{l,KL}B^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}_{l}
, then it satisfies

\displaystyle\llbracket Z_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},\,T\rrbracket=\llbracket\bar{Z}_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},\,T\rrbracket=0,\quad T\in\{P_{\mu},Z_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},\bar{Z}_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},Q_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}},\bar{Q}_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}}\},

(3.22)

where
Z_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}=(-1)^{\vec{f}\cdot\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}Z_{LK}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}}
.

Therefore the following theorem holds.

Theorem 2
.

There exits the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded extension of supersymmetric algebra which is given by the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA
\langle P_{\mu},Z_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},\bar{Z}_{KL}^{\vec{f}+\vec{f}\,^{\prime}},Q_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}},\bar{Q}_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}}\rangle
.

This algebra contains
K_{i}
supercharges
Q_{\alpha\,K_{i}}^{\vec{f}}
in each subspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{f}}
and nontrivial centers in each subspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}
. We call this
\mathcal{N}=(K_{1}|K_{2}|...|K_{2^{n-1}})

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-supersymmetric algebra.
Similarly to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-case, if we set the number of each
Q_{\alpha,K}^{\vec{f}}
to
K_{i}=1
, it corresponds to the
\mathcal{N}=2^{n-1}\ \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-supersymmetric algebra, which was introduced by Bruce
[
22
]
.

3.3
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-Graded internal symmetries

In this section, we focus on the generators of internal symmetry with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-grading
B_{l}^{\vec{b}}
. The discussion in the previous section says there can exist
B_{l}^{\vec{b}}
in each subspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}
and they satisfy

\displaystyle\llbracket B_{l}^{\vec{b}},B_{m}^{\vec{b}\,^{\prime}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=i\sum_{k}h^{(\vec{b},\vec{b}\,^{\prime})}_{lmk}B_{k}^{\vec{b}+\vec{b}\,^{\prime}}.

(
3.17
)

This means the set of
B_{l}^{\vec{b}}
is a
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})_{\text{ev.}}
-graded LSA. Especially, if we set
n=2k+1
, we can prove that this algebra is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2k}
-graded Lie algebra, which is another type of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded extension of Lie algebra introduced by Rittenberg and Wyler
[
8
,
9
]
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{LA}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-graded vector space (
2.1
) and
\vec{\alpha}=(\vec{\alpha}_{1},\vec{\alpha_{2}},...,\vec{\alpha}_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
where
\vec{\alpha}_{i}=(\alpha_{2i-1},\alpha_{2i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{LA}
is called
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-graded Lie algebra if
\mathfrak{g}_{LA}
has the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-Lie bracket defined by

\displaystyle[A,B\}:=AB-(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\vec{\alpha}_{i}\times\vec{\beta}_{i}|}BA,\quad A\in\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{\alpha}},\ B\in\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{\beta}},

(3.23)

and
[A,B\}
satisfies

\displaystyle[A,B\}

\displaystyle\in\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{\alpha}+\vec{\beta}},

\displaystyle[A,[B,C\}\}

\displaystyle=[[A,B\},C\}+(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\vec{\alpha}_{i}\times\vec{\beta}_{i}|}[B[A,C\}\}

(3.24)

Theorem 3
.

The
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n+1})_{\text{ev.}}
-graded LSA

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\text{ev.}}:=\bigoplus_{\vec{b}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n+1})_{\text{ev.}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}

(3.25)

is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{LA}
.

Proof.

Let
f
be an injective map
f:\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}\to\vec{a}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n+1})_{\text{ev.}}
, defined by

\displaystyle\vec{a}=\left(\vec{\alpha}_{1},\vec{\alpha}_{2}+|\vec{\alpha}_{1}|(1,1),\cdots\vec{\alpha}_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}|\vec{\alpha}_{k}|(1,1),\cdots,\vec{\alpha}_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|\vec{\alpha}_{k}|(1,1),\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\vec{\alpha}_{k}|\right)

where
|\vec{a}_{k}|^{2}:=a_{2k-1}^{2}+a_{2k}^{2}
, and it is equal to
|\vec{a}_{k}|=a_{2k-1}+a_{2k}\mod 2
.

Since
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{LA}
and
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n+1})_{\text{ev.}}
-graded LSA
\mathfrak{g}_{\text{ev.}}
have the same number of the graded subspaces, the only difference of them is the Lie bracket, which comes from
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\vec{\alpha}_{i}\times\vec{\beta}_{i}|
in (
3.23
) and
\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}
in (
2.2
). However, taking into account that computations are performed in modulo 2, we have straightforwardly

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\vec{\alpha}_{i}\times\vec{\beta}_{i}|=\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}.

(3.26)

Therefore the map
f
is an isomorphism between
\mathfrak{g}_{LA}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\text{ev.}}
.
∎

From this theorem, we can redefine
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-graded Lie algebra (Definition
3
).

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded LSA in Definition
2
. We define
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n-1}
-graded Lie algebra as the subalgebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\text{ev.}}:=\bigoplus_{\vec{b}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})_{\text{ev.}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{b}}\subset\mathfrak{g}.

(3.27)

This is a more general definition, which means we can define not only
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n}
-graded Lie algebras, but also
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2n+1}
-graded Lie algebras. Finally, for the internal symmetry, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 4
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra is allowed as the generators of the internal symmetry of S-matrix.

This is very interesting result because we can consider new types of gauge symmetries in relativistic QFT.
Specifically,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-extension of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
has already been introduced in
[
9
]
and some representation of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
was investigated in
[
60
]
. These will be helpful to construct
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
gauge field theory.

4
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we explored novel possible generators of symmetries of
S
-matrix in order to apply
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded Lie superalgebra to relativistic quantum field theory. As a result, we found
\mathcal{N}=(K_{1}|K_{2}|\cdots|K_{2^{n-1}})

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded supersymmetry is the only allowed extension of supersymmetric algebra as the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded extension. Specifically, if we set the number of each
Q_{\alpha\,K}^{\vec{f}}
’s to
K_{i}=1
, they correspond to the
\mathcal{N}=2^{n-1}\ \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-supersymmetric algebra, which was introduced by Bruce
[
22
]
. However we cannot determine the number of supercharges purely from algebraic restrictions. This should be determined by the relationship between the degrees of freedom of particles within a model under consideration.

Nontrivial results of the present paper are summarized as follows.

1.

It is possible to construct
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded supersymmetric relativistic QFT.

2.

Gauge symmetry is not necessary generated by a Lie algebra, but by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra.

Thus an important future work is to construct a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
gauge field theory.

Finally, in this paper, we have only considered the case of massive particles.
According to
[
5
,
7
]
, conformal generators can exist in the case of massless particles.
Therefore if we explore novel generators in the massless case, we could obtain more nontrivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-graded conformal generators.

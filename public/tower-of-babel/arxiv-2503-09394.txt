Bidirectional Prototype-Reward co-Evolution for Test-Time Adaptation of Vision-Language Models
Xiaozhen Qiao, Peng Huang, Jiakang Yuan, Xianda Guo, Bowen Ye, Zhe Sun, Xuelong Li

Abstract
Test-time adaptation (TTA) is crucial in maintaining Vision Language Models(VLMs)’ performance when facing real-world distribution shifts, particularly when the source data or target labels are inaccessible. Existing TTA methods rely on CLIP’s output probability distribution for feature evaluation, which can introduce biases under domain shifts. This misalignment may cause features to be misclassified due to text priors or incorrect textual associations. To address these limitations, we propose B idirectional P rototype- R eward co- E volution ( BPRE ), a novel TTA framework for VLMs that integrates feature quality assessment with prototype evolution through a synergistic feedback loop. BPRE first employs a Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module to evaluate feature quality and guide prototype refinement precisely. The continuous refinement of prototype quality through Prototype-Reward Interactive Evolution will subsequently enhance the computation of more robust Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Scores. Through the bidirectional interaction, the precision of rewards and the evolution of prototypes mutually reinforce each other, forming a self-evolving cycle. Extensive experiments are conducted across 15 diverse recognition datasets encompassing natural distribution shifts and cross-dataset generalization scenarios. Results demonstrate that BPRE consistently achieves superior average performance compared to state-of-the-art methods across different model architectures, such as ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16. By emphasizing comprehensive feature evaluation and bidirectional knowledge refinement, BPRE advances VLM generalization capabilities, offering a new perspective on TTA.

Recent advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs)
[
2
,
38
,
18
]
have opened new opportunities to integrate human language into various computer vision tasks.
Despite the unprecedented zero-shot generalization capability achieved by VLMs such as CLIP
[
23
]
, existing VLMs suffer from a large performance drop when encountering domain shifts, which is due to the inconsistent data distribution between the source domain and the target domain. However, in real-world applications, the inevitable domain discrepancies caused by dynamic and ever-changing environments make domain adaptation of VLMs an urgent problem.

To address this issue, some researchers have developed efficient adaptation methods
[
40
,
45
,
3
]
that fine-tune the VLMs using limited target domain samples. However, the requirement for target domain annotations remains a critical bottleneck, especially in dynamic environments with frequent distribution shifts
[
24
,
36
]
. Recently, several studies have explored the test-time adaptation (TTA) for VLMs, aiming to adaptively optimize the model at a low cost during the test phase. Shu
et al.

[
25
]
introduced test-time prompt tuning (TPT) that optimizes prompts for individual test samples to enhance out-of-distribution generalization. This framework was enhanced by DiffTPT
[
8
]
through diffusion-based augmentations, while Karmanov
et al.

[
14
]
proposed a training-free approach using dynamic visual caches. More recently, Zhang
et al.

[
37
]
developed a dual prototype evolving strategy that accumulates task-specific knowledge from both visual and textual modalities during testing.

Overall, most of the existing TTA works
[
40
,
14
,
37
]
have highlighted the importance of utilizing historical feature information to accumulate task-specific knowledge for each class by Caches and Prototypes. This approach aims to effectively construct accurate and discriminative representations of target classes, which are crucial for enhancing the model’s robustness to distribution shifts, as well as improving its efficiency and performance during the test-time phase.
Current methods
[
14
,
37
]
primarily rely on CLIP’s output probability distribution, derived from the fusion of Text Features and Image Features, to evaluate feature quality, inherently using the text modality as a reference. However, as shown in Fig.
2
, distribution shifts during testing may cause the text encoder to misalign with the semantic space of the image modality, leading to biases in feature selection and evaluation. On one hand, text modality priors cause the model to favor features that strongly align with pre-trained textual descriptions, even when they lack sufficient visual information, while visually rich samples with weaker textual representations may be misclassified (Fig.
2
(a)). On the other hand, misalignment of textual descriptions can introduce feature evaluation biases, where CLIP may incorrectly associate generalized textual descriptions (
e.g.
,“picture”) with certain image features, leading the model to mistakenly consider them high-quality samples while overlooking their true semantics, thereby affecting feature selection accuracy (Fig.
2
(b)).

Based on these insights, we propose a novel test-time VLM adaptation framework:
B
idirectional
P
rototype-
R
eward co-
E
volution (BPRE), which iteratively refines task-specific knowledge, as illustrated in Fig.
3
. Unlike existing approaches, BPRE introduces the Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module, an evaluation method that integrates intrinsic Image Feature properties. By leveraging visual information while incorporating Semantic Similarity and Feature Diversity as key criteria, this module provides a more comprehensive assessment of Image Feature quality, reducing excessive dependence on the text modality. Semantic Similarity ensures selected features align with the task’s semantic space, mitigating errors from textual biases, while Feature Diversity enhances coverage across varied data distributions, preventing overfitting and improving prototype selection during test-time adaptation. Additionally, the Prototype-Reward Interactive Evolution module adaptively updates historical features and Prototypes using Reward scores. This bidirectional reinforcement loop allows refined prototypes to enhance reward estimation, forming a self-reinforcing optimization process that boosts robustness against noise and facilitates interpretable cross-modal learning, yielding more discriminative and stable representations.

The test-time generalization of BPRE is evaluated across 15 diverse recognition datasets under natural distribution shifts and cross-dataset generalization. As shown in Fig.

In summary, our contributions can be listed as:

•

We introduce the Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module, integrating Similarity, Confidence, and Diversity Reward Scores for comprehensive feature evaluation, improving prototype selection.

•

We propose the Prototype-Reward Interactive Evolution Module, establishing a bidirectional reinforcement mechanism where reward scores guide prototype evolution, and refined prototypes enhance reward estimation, improving model robustness.

•

Experiments across 15 diverse image recognition datasets confirm the competitive generalization performance of BPRE.

Method

As shown in Fig.

3.1
Preliminary

Zero-Shot CLIP.
CLIP
[
23
]
consists of dual encoders: a visual encoder
\mathcal{E}_{v}(\cdot)
and a text encoder
\mathcal{E}_{t}(\cdot)
, which project images and text into a shared
d
-dimensional embedding space. CLIP performs zero-shot prediction for a
C
-class classification task by measuring similarities between image and text embeddings. Given a test image
X_{\mathtt{test}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{test}}
and class-specific text descriptions
{\mathcal{T}_{c}}
, the prediction process can be formulated as:

\displaystyle f_{v}=\mathcal{E}_{v}(X_{\mathtt{test}}),\quad f_{t_{c}}=\mathcal{E}_{t}(\mathcal{T}_{c})

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{CLIP}(y=y_{c}|X_{\mathtt{test}})=\frac{\exp(\mathrm{sim}(f_{t_{c}},f_{v})/\tau)}{\sum_{t^{\prime}}\exp(\mathrm{sim}(f_{t^{\prime}},f_{v})/\tau)},

(1)

where
\mathrm{sim}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes cosine similarity and
\tau
is the temperature parameter.

Test-Time Prompt Tuning.
TPT
[
25
]
improves CLIP’s zero-shot generalization by learning adaptive prompts from test samples. For each test image
X_{\mathtt{test}}
, TPT generates
N
augmented views
{\mathcal{A}_{n}(X_{\mathtt{test}})}_{n=1}^{N}
and aggregates their predictions based on confidence. Specifically, TPT selects predictions with entropy below threshold
t
and computes their average within the top
\rho
-percentile:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{TPT}}(X_{\mathtt{test}})=\frac{1}{\rho N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\,\mathbf{1}\Big[\mathcal{H}\big(\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{CLIP}}(\mathcal{A}_{n}(X_{\mathtt{test}}))\big)\leq t\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{CLIP}}(\mathcal{A}_{n}(X_{\mathtt{test}})),

(2)

where
\mathcal{H}(p)=-\sum_{i=1}^{C}p_{i}\log p_{i}
is the prediction entropy. The prompt is optimized to minimize the entropy of the final prediction:
\min\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{TPT}}(X_{\mathtt{test}}))
.

3.2
Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward

To effectively assess samples’ contributions to model performance and guide feature learning, we introduce a Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module, which dynamically assigns quality scores by integrating multiple characteristics.

Similarity Reward Score.

In cross-modal learning, semantic consistency between sample features and category prototypes serves as a crucial indicator for sample quality evaluation. To this end, we design a similarity-based reward
R_{sim}
that measures the alignment between sample features and prototype representations:

R_{sim}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}}\cos(\frac{f_{i}}{\|f_{i}\|_{2}},\frac{p_{k}}{\|p_{k}\|_{2}}),

(3)

where
f_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the feature vector of sample
x_{i}
, and
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{K}\}
denotes the set of category prototypes, with each
p_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
representing the prototype of category
k
. The function
\cos(\cdot,\cdot)
computes the cosine similarity between the normalized feature vector
f_{i}
and prototype
p_{k}
, while
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the
L_{2}
-norm for normalization. This similarity reward enhances feature discriminability by enforcing semantic consistency and aligning prototype evolution with category semantics.

Confidence Reward Score.

To assess the model’s prediction confidence, we define a confidence reward
R_{conf}
based on the entropy of the predicted probability distribution. Utilizing temperature-scaled normalization,
R_{conf}
is derived from CLIP’s predicted probability distribution
\mathbf{P}_{CLIP}
:

R_{conf}=1-\frac{-\sum_{i=1}^{C}\mathbb{P}_{CLIP}(X_{test})\log\mathbb{P}_{CLIP}(X_{test})}{\log C},

(4)

where
C
is the number of categories. This entropy-based confidence reward prioritizes high-certainty predictions, enhancing model reliability while fostering more robust feature representations.

Diversity Reward Score.

To prevent representation collapse and encourage diverse feature exploration, we introduce a diversity reward
R_{div}
based on historical feature comparisons. We maintain a fixed-size feature memory queue
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{M}\}
, where each
h_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents the average feature of a past batch, and
M
is the queue size. For a given sample feature
f_{i}
, its novelty is measured by the maximum cosine similarity with historical features:

S_{max}=\max_{h_{j}\in\mathcal{H}}\cos(\frac{f_{i}}{\|f_{i}\|_{2}},\frac{h_{j}}{\|h_{j}\|_{2}}),

(5)

The diversity reward is then defined as:

R_{div}=1-S_{max}.

(6)

This mechanism enhances feature diversity while maintaining discriminability, improving the model’s generalization across downstream tasks.

By integrating the proposed reward components, we define an adaptive comprehensive reward function that evaluates sample quality while guiding prototype evolution:

R=\lambda_{sim}R_{sim}+\lambda_{conf}R_{conf}+\lambda_{div}R_{div},

(7)

where
\lambda_{sim}
,
\lambda_{conf}
, and
\lambda_{div}
are hyperparameters that balance the contributions of similarity reward
R_{sim}
, confidence reward
R_{conf}
, and diversity reward
R_{div}
, respectively.

R_{final}=R_{min}+(R-R_{min})*\eta_{\text{warm}},

(8)

where
R_{min}
is the minimum reward threshold, and
\eta_{\text{warm}}
is a warmup factor. Initially, when
\eta_{\text{warm}}
is small,
R_{final}
remains close to
R_{min}
, stabilizing early adaptation. As
\eta_{\text{warm}}
increases,
R_{final}
gradually approaches
R
, enhancing adaptation while mitigating fluctuations.

This framework employs a comprehensive reward mechanism to optimize cross-modal transfer learning. By integrating similarity, confidence, and diversity metrics, it dynamically evaluates feature representations. The adaptive reward system, guided by a warmup phase and historical feature tracking, ensures stable learning signals while facilitating prototype evolution, effectively bridging reward estimation and prototype learning.

3.3
Prototype-Reward Interactive Evolution

Building on the Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module, we propose a dynamic prototype evolution mechanism that utilizes reward-guided learning to refine category-specific prototypes. By weighting feature contributions based on their computed rewards, this approach adaptively enhances prototype representations, fostering a more robust and discriminative feature space for cross-modal recognition.

Prototype Representation and Initialization.

For a classification task with
C
categories, we maintain a set of prototype representations in a
d
-dimensional image feature space:
v_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. These prototypes act as learnable category-level representations, capturing essential class characteristics.

Figure 4
:
(a-d) t-SNE
[
29
]
visualizations of image features in the priority queues demonstrate that as additional samples are incorporated, class-specific features become more tightly clustered on the Food101
[
1
]
dataset. (e) As the number of input samples grows, the reward score computed by the Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module increases.

At the start of training, prototypes are initialized using category-specific statistics. The visual prototype
v_{c}^{(0)}
for category
c
is set as the mean feature representation of all samples in that category:

v_{c}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{c}|}\sum_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{c}}f_{v},

(9)

where
\mathcal{D}_{c}
denotes the set of samples belonging to category
c
.

Quality-Aware Visual Prototype Evolution.

Leveraging the comprehensive quality assessment derived from our multi-dimensional reward mechanism, we propose a quality-aware momentum update strategy for dynamic prototype evolution. This mechanism strategically incorporates sample contributions by utilizing their reward scores
R
as quality indicators, thereby ensuring that samples with higher reward values exert greater influence on the prototype evolution process. Specifically, for category
c
, the visual prototype update at time step
t
is formulated as:

v_{c}^{(t+1)}=m\cdot v_{c}^{(t)}+(1-m)\cdot\sum_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{c}}w_{i}^{(t)}\cdot f_{v},

(10)

where
m\in[0,1]
is a momentum coefficient that stabilizes the update process by controlling the balance between historical information and new observations. The adaptive weight
w_{i}^{(t)}
for each sample is computed through temperature-scaled softmax normalization:

w_{i}^{(t)}=\frac{\exp(R_{final}/\tau)}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{D}_{c}}\exp(R_{final}/\tau)}.

(11)

where
R_{i}
is the comprehensive quality score derived from our multi-dimensional reward mechanism, and
\tau
is a temperature parameter that controls the sharpness of the weight distribution.

Overall, the dual promotion mechanism creates a dynamic feedback loop between reward estimation and prototype evolution, facilitating their mutual enhancement. The mechanism operates through two complementary processes: (1) high-fidelity prototypes serve as anchor points for precise reward assessment, and (2) refined reward signals guide targeted prototype updates. As illustrated in Fig.
4
, increasing the number of iterative samples results in tighter clustering of class-specific features, with a corresponding rise in reward scores from the Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module. This iterative interaction progressively enhances the framework’s ability to model fine-grained visual-semantic relationships and adapt to intra-class variations. By co-evolving prototypes and rewards, the mechanism improves robustness against noisy data and fosters interpretable cross-modal feature learning, ultimately producing more discriminative and generalizable representations for cross-modal recognition.

4.2
Implementation Details

We adopt PyTorch as the deep learning framework and utilize CLIP with ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 backbones as the visual encoders. Following TPT, each test image is augmented into 64 views through random resized cropping (63 augmented + 1 original). The Dynamic Prototype Evolution employs a momentum-based update mechanism (momentum=0.9) with an Multi-Dimensional Quality-Aware Reward Module. For the prototype evolution function, we set temperature

\tau=0.01
. The cache mechanism maintains a queue of size 3 per class, with a normalized entropy threshold of 0.1 for prototype updates. During testing, the reward calculator undergoes a 1000-step warmup period with a minimum reward threshold of 0.1, while the prototype manager performs weighted feature averaging every 10 iterations based on computed rewards.
All experiments are conducted on either a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 or A100 GPU. For feature space visualization, we utilize t-SNE with perplexity 30 to project the prototype features into 2D space.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we propose BPRE, a novel framework for test-time adaptation of VLMs that integrates bidirectional prototype evolution with multi-dimensional reward assessment. By incorporating Semantic Similarity and Feature Diversity, BPRE enables more effective feature selection while mitigating text modality biases. The Prototype-Reward Interactive Evolution module further establishes a self-reinforcing cycle, where refined prototypes enhance reward estimation, and improved rewards guide prototype updates, forming a continuous optimization process. Extensive experiments across diverse datasets confirm BPRE’s effectiveness in handling domain shifts while maintaining computational efficiency, making it a promising solution for real-world adaptation scenarios.

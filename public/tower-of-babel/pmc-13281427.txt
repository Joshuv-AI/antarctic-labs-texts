Advancing healthy ageing in Greece: the WHO ICOPE protocol within the JA PreventNCD framework
Authors: Theodoula Adamakidou, Alexandra Koreli, Neda Tavassoli, Chrysoula Tsiou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe World Health Organization’s Integrated Care for Older People (ICOPE) program is an evidence-based and user-friendly approach that supports healthy aging. Despite its demonstrated value, most healthcare professionals remain unfamiliar with its application in clinical settings. Structured training in the ICOPE approach could significantly enhance its adoption and contribute to promoting healthy aging. This study developed within the framework of the European project JA PreventNCD and aims to describe the protocol of a strategy for the implementation of ICOPE framework in Greece.MethodsThis protocol describes a prospective longitudinal study integrating both educational and research components. During the Implementation Phase – Theoretical Education, approximately 75 healthcare professionals will be recruited to complete training in the ICOPE approach. In the subsequent Implementation Phase – Practical Training and Research, trained professionals will recruit at least 200 older adults aged ≥ 60 years with a Katz Index ≥ 5. They will conduct ICOPE Step 1 screenings and Step 2 assessments, followed by individualized care planning and referrals. Older adults will be assessed twice annually over a 24-month period. Trainees will serve as field researchers, recording assessment data and uploading it to a specifically developed digital platform that supports standardized data entry, longitudinal monitoring, and pseudonymized data export for analysis.Expected outcomesThe study is expected to enhance healthcare professionals’ competencies in early detection of intrinsic capacity and functional decline and promote consistent use of person-centred assessment frameworks in primary healthcare. Biannual monitoring will generate the first national dataset on intrinsic capacity trajectories among Greek older adults. Findings will inform the feasibility, acceptability, and sustainability of ICOPE in Greece and provide evidence to support future scale-up and policy adoption.ConclusionThis protocol represents the first structured effort to embed the WHO ICOPE model within the Greek healthcare system. By integrating professional training, community-based assessment, and digital documentation, the initiative aims to strengthen person-centred care, prevent dependency, and align national ageing policies with international standards.

Introduction

As populations age globally, the healthy ageing approach emphasizes not merely longer life, but the preservation of functional ability, participation and well-being in later years. Within this framework, interventions targeting older adults are increasingly focused on promoting health, independence and quality of life rather than solely treating disease. Early intervention and the adoption of a holistic, person-centred approach can reduce the risk of decline, enhance resilience, and support older individuals in remaining active contributors to their communities [1, 2].

In 2017, the WHO launched the Integrated Care for Older People (ICOPE) strategy to address the complex challenges of population ageing [3]. This initiative promotes healthy ageing by enhancing functional ability and intrinsic capacity (IC), which is a key predictor of health outcomes and refers to physical and mental abilities shaped by individual and environmental factors [4]. In 2019, the WHO released a practical handbook with screening tools and interventions to identify IC decline, guide care planning and support caregivers through 5 steps [5, 6]. This approach emphasizes community-based implementation by multidisciplinary teams and follows five steps: 1) Screening for intrinsic capacity decline, 2) In-depth person-centred assessment, 3) developing the personalized care plan, and 4) referral pathway and monitoring, and 5) community’s engagement and caregivers support [5].

Several countries have adopted ICOPE, reporting improved health outcomes and confirming its feasibility across diverse healthcare settings [7–10]. In France, over 27,000 older adults have been screened systematically for IC decline, demonstrating integration into routine practice [7, 11]. The tool effectively identifies individuals at risk of functional decline and disability, facilitating timely referrals to specialists for appropriate intervention [7–9, 11, 12]. The ICOPE approach has been associated with high levels of beneficiary satisfaction [9, 11] and adherence [7], and the strong person-centered focus [12]. Its success is further supported by the active involvement of nurses and the use of digital monitoring tools [11] under a user-friendly design [12]. Research demonstrated ICOPE acceptability in regions with fragmented healthcare systems [9]. Though, insufficient staff training and poor integration across levels of care has been identified by some researchers as a key barrier for its implementation [10].

Greece has one of the highest proportions of older adults in the European Union, with individuals aged 65 and over comprising 23.3% of the population [13]. However, while life expectancy at age 65 is about 20 years for both women and men, a substantial share of these years is lived with significant activity limitations [14]. Greece’s health system remains hospital-centered and physician-oriented [15]. Although primary healthcare (PHC) provides preventive services and multidisciplinary teams, fragmentation, limited referral pathways, underfunding, and systemic resistance to reform, still hinder its ability to manage chronic and lifestyle-related conditions [16]. These challenges, alongside inadequate geriatric services [17], underscore the urgency of implementing the ICOPE approach.

Building on the framework of implementation science, which aims to bridge the gap between research and real-world practice [18], this study responds to the WHO’s call for country-specific ICOPE pilot implementations and the recognized need for staff training (WHO, 2022) [19]. It presents the “Proposition of a Strategy to Implement the WHO ICOPE Program”—a national initiative designed to integrate ICOPE into the Greek healthcare system. The protocol outlines a concise and targeted training model that incorporates mentoring and promotes nationwide engagement, tailored to the specific needs and contextual realities of the Greek population.

This initiative is conducted within the framework of the European JA PreventNCD project, which aims to reduce the burden of non-communicable diseases (NCDs) through integrated prevention strategies [20, 21]. The study integrates health literacy interventions to strengthen healthcare professionals’ competencies and screening activities to enhance older adults’ access to care.

At the start of the JA PreventNCD project, during the preparation phase, the Greek team familiarized themselves with the WHO ICOPE framework and obtained certification in its implementation after completing an intensive training program delivered by the French project leaders. Subsequently, the team developed a structured ICOPE training program for healthcare professionals in Greece and will be implemented during the two-year implementation phase. Participants and recruitment

Healthcare professionals

The purposive sample of approximately 75 healthcare professionals will include nurses, physicians, social workers, physiotherapists, psychologists and occupational therapists, working in PHC across Greece. Inclusion criteria will be professional experience in PHC; current employment in PHC; postgraduate studies related to PHC disciplines and the willingness to participate in the training program.

Recruitment for the training program will be publicly announced via social media and professional networks. There will be no financial burden for trainees, since the training falls within the JA PreventNCD project, and no monetary compensation will be provided.

Older adults

A purposive sample of at least 200 community-dwelling older adults living independently in their own homes, will be recruited nationwide. Inclusion criteria will include: (a) age ≥ 60 years; (b) independent functional status (Katz Index ≥ 5); (c) living independently in a private household. Older adults will be recruited through the personal and social networks of participating healthcare professionals, typically including parents/neighbors/siblings/friends. Patients under the care of the participating healthcare professionals will not be recruited, to avoid any pre-existing professional relationship. Each professional required at least five individuals to enroll.

This study represents a pilot implementation of the ICOPE framework within the JA PreventNCD project, primarily aiming to train healthcare professionals and assess the feasibility of ICOPE implementation in Greece. As a feasibility study, a pragmatic sampling approach will be adopted, and the study does not aim to ensure statistical representativeness with respect to age, sex, or other sociodemographic characteristics. Accordingly, both sample sizes were determined based on feasibility criteria and were informed by the French ICOPE implementation experience, which provided realistic expectations regarding recruitment capacity and workflow demands within a pilot context.

Measures

Clinical and sociodemographic data

Data collected from healthcare professionals’ group will include demographic and professional characteristics such as age, gender, specialty, current position, and workplace location (city/town).

Data collected from older adults in Step1 will include sociodemographic characteristics (age, gender, educational level, marital status, number of children, living conditions, working and financial status), health behaviors (alcohol/tobacco use) and Katz Index score. Additional data will be collected in Step 2, including vaccination history, height, weight, BMI, recent weight changes, the presence of sleep disturbances, incontinence, and use of formal/informal support structures received by the individual.

It should be noticed that data collection is restricted to the standardized ICOPE assessment framework. Variables such as chronic diseases, medication use, lifestyle factors, mental health status, and social engagement are captured within the intrinsic capacity domains (e.g., vitality, mobility, cognition, psychological well-being, and functional ability) and are documented to inform individualized care pathways. They are therefore systematically collected, although not presented as standalone variables in the protocol.

Regarding trainees’ evaluation, those who will fail in the first online evaluation test of the theoretical part, a second opportunity for re-examination will be given. Participants who successfully complete both theoretical education and practical training will receive the Certificate.

Concerning the training program evaluation, trainees will evaluate its quality and value at the end of the theoretical part and at the end of the entire program (September 2027).

Following the release of the 2nd edition of the ICOPE approach [23], Steps 1 and 2 will be updated accordingly within the training program. Specifically, at the educational level, trainees will be informed about all modifications introduced in the updated edition of the ICOPE framework. At the level of data collection and recording within the digital platform, only those changes that do not compromise the longitudinal comparability of the data will be implemented.

Data collection

The study is scheduled to begin in September 2025 and conclude in September 2027. Healthcare professionals who satisfied the inclusion criteria will be entered to Implementation phase-theoretical education. After successfully completing the theoretical component, each healthcare professional will recruit and assess in person five older adults from their personal or social networks during the practical training. Concurrently, over this 24-month period, the trained healthcare professionals will act as field researchers, collecting and recording data from the assessments.

In the Implementation Phase-Practical Training and Research older adults will be assessed by healthcare professionals with Step 1, which screens six domains: cognition, mobility, nutrition/vitality, vision, hearing, and mood. Any decline detected in a domain will trigger an in-depth evaluation (Step 2) where diseases and risk factors and physical and social environment will be assessed. Then a personalized care plan will be developed, and the participants will be referred to appropriate healthcare professionals (e.g., family doctor, ophthalmologist, neurologist) and will receive personalized counseling under Step 3 to promote healthy behaviors, including social participation, use of community resources, balanced nutrition, and regular physical activity. All participants will be reassessed with Step 1 every six months over a two-year period to monitor progress and adjust care based on previous findings and recommendations. Data from the Step 1 and Step 2 assessments will be documented for each participant.

Building upon the structured biannual ICOPE evaluation process described above, the study procedures do not replace routine PHC services. Participants will continue to receive usual PHC throughout the study period. In the event of an urgent or acute condition, or a perceived deterioration in health status between scheduled ICOPE assessments, older adults will be advised to contact their PHC provider or appropriate health services for clinical evaluation and management according to standard care procedures. The individualized ICOPE prevention and care plan may be revised as clinically indicated during follow-up.

Digital ICOPE application: development and functionality for the study

A digital application has been developed to support the study, with iterative user testing to ensure usability, functionality, and alignment with study objectives. Designed by a member of the research team, it facilitates the systematic documentation of Step 1 and Step 2 assessments by healthcare professionals.

Upon login, users select the relevant ICOPE step. Results of Step 1 are presented in a color-coded summary (red for impairments, green for normal), with previous assessments available for comparison. Real-time data entry replaces paper forms, improving efficiency and accuracy. Step 2 assessments are initially completed on paper and later questionnaires’ scores are entered into the application. All data can be exported in Excel format for statistical analysis.

Ethics

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the UNIWA (Approval Number: 37792—15/04/2025). The training program and methodology were approved by the KEDIVIM Committee of UNIWA. The European Commission has reviewed the overall project proposal, and the Publication Board of the JA PreventNCD has reviewed and accepted the publication proposal for this paper. The final paper is the sole responsibility of the authors.

First, health care professionals, prior to their involvement in the study will consent to act in the capacity of researchers during their practical training, and in this role, will fully comply with all ethical and professional standards governing research involving human subjects. Informed consent will be obtained from both groups of participants. They will be informed of their voluntary participation, their right to withdraw at any time without consequences, data pseudonymization procedures, and the exclusive of their data for the purposes of this study.

The management of paper-based forms and digital data will comply with EU GDPR regulations. The principal investigator will securely maintain the re-identification key linking personal identifiers to study codes. Data will be retained in accordance with ethics committee regulations and will be securely destroyed after the mandated retention period.

Concerning the digital application, data will be stored securely in a MariaDB database on okeanos.grnet.gr, with restricted physical and network access. The digital application design will ensure that all connections are protected by a VPN and a firewall, and access is limited to authenticated users with unique credentials. Each user can only view his/her own data, with access control through authentication and filtering. All assessments will be linked to users and timestamps to ensure traceability. Only data necessary for the study objectives will be collected and analyzed.

Data processing, dissemination, sustainability, and policy impact

The Greek research team will analyze the data and submit descriptive results to the project’s lead team in France, Final phase (Figs. 1, 2). This study will focus on collecting specific general indicators and the impact of ICOPE on dependency prevention; however, the economic, social, and medical outcomes will require further investigation.

The study’s communication and dissemination strategy are designed to maximize visibility and impact. Findings will be presented through oral and poster presentations at national and international scientific conferences, while the study protocol and results will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed international scientific journals to reach the wider academic and professional community.

To promote sustainability, the educational materials, digital tools, and study protocols will be maintained and made accessible for future use in the healthcare system. Lessons learned and best practices from the study will be incorporated into ongoing professional development programs and shared with relevant stakeholders. Based on the data produced, specific policy recommendations will be developed and proposed to the competent Greek authorities, aiming to shape future national strategies for dependency prevention and the promotion of healthy and active ageing in the country.

Expected outcomes and statistical analysis

The study is anticipated to produce outcomes across three main domains: a) training, b) older adult health outcomes, and c) the feasibility of ICOPE implementation within the Greek healthcare setting.

Regarding the training component, healthcare professionals are expected to develop competence in applying the ICOPE tool, thereby enhancing their familiarity, skills and confidence in assessing older adults in clinical practice.

Regarding older adult outcomes, the systematic biannual assessment of intrinsic capacity is expected to facilitate early identification of functional decline and support timely, personalized interventions aimed at preventing dependency and improving quality of life. Specifically, the study will assess the prevalence of declines across intrinsic capacity domains at baseline and their changes over the two-year follow-up period. Additionally, the proportion of participants requiring personalized care plans and referrals will be recorded.

Concerning implementation outcomes, data analysis is expected to provide new evidence on integrated care of older adults in Greece and to contribute to broader European and national healthy aging initiative. Implementation indicators will include feasibility measures (e.g., recruitment and completion rates), acceptability (e.g., participant and professional feedback), adherence to the scheduled assessments, and fidelity to the protocol.

With regard to the statistical analysis plan, descriptive statistics will be used to summarize participant characteristics, training outcomes, and intrinsic capacity measures at each time point. In addition, longitudinal approaches will be applied to examine changes over time across repeated measurements. All analyses will be performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), and statistical significance will be set at p ≤ 0.05.

At this point in time, all preparatory procedures have been completed (Fig. 1), and the first assessment (M1) and registration of all participating older adults are currently underway. The program is progressing as planned, and data collection is expected to be completed by September 2027.

Discussion

This protocol is part of the European project JA PreventNCD and the Social inequalities work package (WP7). It was developed by academic and research staff certified in the ICOPE framework by the Gérontopôle of TUH. The study outlines a pilot initiative to develop and implement a training program for early detection of frailty in older adults. Its primary objective is to introduce the ICOPE guidelines [3, 5] into the Greek healthcare context through a structured, multidisciplinary training program. To date, no published evidence supports ICOPE implementation in Greece. This protocol aims to train approximately 75 healthcare professionals and promote the framework’s integration into national healthcare practice.

Key strengths

Given the limited adoption of the ICOPE framework in Greece, this study highlights the innovation and added value of the proposed protocol. Adapting the tool to the cultural and operational characteristics of Greece’s healthcare system [15] is essential to ensure its acceptability and effectiveness in real-world settings.

Τhe training program is delivered through the KEDIVIM [24] of the UNIWA which operates as an independent academic unit. The KEDIVIM was selected to ensure institutional credibility and wide professional reach, as public university programs in Greece are widely trusted [25]. The program’s attractiveness lies in its official academic status, free participation, and focus on a relevant and timely professional competency [26].

The strengths of this protocol lie in its threefold focus: (a) remote training of healthcare professionals, (b) biannual systematic assessment of community-dwelling older adults, and (c) structured documentation of assessment data.

The first strength lies in the online delivery of the program, enables participation by professionals in remote or underserved areas who often have limited access to training opportunities but serve populations with a high proportion of older adults. This approach promotes educational equity and strengthens capacity in regions where the need for geriatric care is greatest. Additionally, the protocol acknowledges that effective assessment and management of older adults’ health requires a collaborative framework that integrates expertise across disciplines and extends beyond the traditional clinical perspective [27]. The training program emphasizes person-centered assessment, integrated care planning, and preventive practices within PHC. This focus aligns with international priorities for healthy ageing [28, 29] and addresses the recognized need to enhance Greek professionals’ competencies in identifying and managing frailty [26]. Furthermore, trainees are expected to cultivate a sense of professional responsibility toward ICOPE recipients, as the practical component involves periodic assessments and ongoing engagement with participants [30].

The protocol’s second objective is the biannual assessment of older adults over a two-year period, with each health professional completing five evaluations. For older adults, ICOPE offers a structured screening framework that facilitates the early detection of functional decline and the implementation of timely preventive interventions, thereby supporting healthy ageing and improving quality of life [5]. Repeated assessments provide valuable insight into functional trajectories, generating data to inform evidence-based care policies. The effectiveness of these interventions, however, depends on consistent implementation, institutional support, and integration with health and social care services. Recruitment of older adults will be carried out through each professional’s existing social network—a strategy intended to enhance access, promote adherence to follow-up, and reflect real-world clinical practice. This process is designed to place minimal burden on both professionals and participants.

The study’s third objective is the systematic documentation and analysis of assessment data for research purposes. A comprehensive dataset will be collected from older adults across diverse regions, generating evidence on the feasibility and sustainability of the training program and informing the potential integration of the ICOPE model into the Greek healthcare system. A key innovation of this study is the dual role of participating healthcare professionals, who serve both as trainees and researchers.

Additional strengths of the program include a concise, targeted theoretical training phase and structured mentoring during the practical component [31], both of which contribute to its overall effectiveness. Knowledge certification will be achieved through a remote multiple-choice examination, which is designed to serve as an educational tool rather than solely an evaluative measure.

The limitations of the study include the use of a purposive sampling approach, the potential influence of social desirability bias on participation, and possible deviations from strict clinical adherence to the ICOPE protocol due to the pilot nature of the research. Nevertheless, the study is expected to provide a foundational framework for the broader integration of the ICOPE tool into Greece’s PHC system and to contribute meaningfully to the ongoing transformation of care for the ageing population.

Challenges

Given the systemic and structural weaknesses of PHC in Greece [32, 33], the introduction of a new tool such as ICOPE presents a significant challenge. Its adoption may initially encounter skepticism, while structural barriers within the healthcare system could further hinder implementation [32, 34]. The key challenge, therefore, lies in ensuring the sustainability of the intervention through effective integration into PHC and demonstrating its practical value. Data generated from this small-scale study will help build the case for the formal adoption of ICOPE within the national healthcare system. State-supported incentives are recommended to promote routine use—similar to the experimental approach adopted in France, where healthcare professionals are compensated per assessment—an experimental model that could facilitate broader uptake in Greece [35].

The implementation of the ICOPE approach in Greece, within the framework of this project, aligns closely with the core principles of implementation science, which emphasize context-specific adaptation, stakeholder engagement, and continuous evaluation to ensure the effective translation of evidence into practice [18]. The study’s design—integrating professional training, real-world application, and systematic evaluation—embodies an iterative process of continuous learning and improvement central to implementation science. This methodology not only supports the successful integration of ICOPE into routine clinical practice but also generates critical evidence to inform future scale-up efforts and policy development for healthy ageing in Greece.

In the next phase, we plan to relaunch the ICOPE training program by opening new cohorts through public calls. With prior experience and established infrastructure, including an e-Class educational platform and a digital data recording system, we are well-positioned to support the program’s practical implementation.

This study carries certain risks, particularly uncertainty regarding healthcare professionals’ engagement, as participation depends primarily on intrinsic motivation and opportunities for professional development. To inform future training cycles, we will evaluate how well the program aligns with participants’ needs, preferences, and capacities. Potential low participation will be addressed by leveraging professionals’ interest in self-development. Although similar programs have been successfully implemented in countries such as France [11], the limited adoption of holistic, person-centered care within Greek PHC system presents challenges. These may include inconsistent familiarity with ICOPE, limited training, and weak integration into routine clinical practice.

Another key challenge involves expanding the digital platform to support large-scale data collection for national implementation. Ensuring long-term sustainability may require dedicated funding, policy support, or integration into structured professional development programs.

Conclusion

The implementation of the ICOPE approach in the Greek population through an interprofessional training program represents a critical first step toward strengthening person-centered care for older adults and promoting healthy ageing. This study is expected to demonstrate the feasibility, sustainability, and effectiveness of systematically monitoring intrinsic capacity in alignment with WHO standards.

The approach—integrating professional training, regular assessments of older adults, and personalized care plans with appropriate service linkage—can serve as a model for replication in other local or national health interventions. It highlights the importance of prevention, community-based care, and interprofessional collaboration as cornerstones of effective, integrated geriatric practice.

As the study focuses on protocol implementation, the data generated will be essential for validating the program’s effectiveness and relevance. These findings will inform the future systematic integration of the ICOPE approach into PHC, supporting training programs that enhance older adults’ quality of life and address fragmented care. Strengthening interprofessional collaboration through educational interventions may act as a catalyst for transforming the healthcare system into one that is more person-centered and functionally responsive to the needs of older adults.

Nevertheless, large-scale, long-term research will be required to fully evaluate the intervention’s impact on dependency, healthcare costs, and quality of life outcomes. Successful implementation of this study may provide a foundation for institutional reforms and the formal incorporation of ICOPE interventions into the Greece’s PHC system.

Refinement of the L 2 L^{2} -decay estimate of solutions to nonlinear Schrödinger equations with attractive-dissipative nonlinearity
Naoyasu Kita, Hayato Miyazaki, Takuya Sato

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the L 2 L^{2} -decay estimate of solutions to nonlinear dissipative Schrödinger equations with power-type nonlinearity of the order p p . It is known that the sign of the real part of the dissipation coefficient affects the long-time behavior of solutions, when neither size restriction on the initial data nor strong dissipative condition is imposed. In that case, if the sign is negative, then Gerelmaa, the first and third author [ 7 ] obtained the L 2 L^{2} -decay estimate under the restriction p ⩽ 1 + 1 / d p\leqslant 1+1/d . In this paper, we relax the restriction to p ⩽ 1 + 4 / ( 3 ​ d ) p\leqslant 1+4/(3d) by refining an energy-type estimate. Furthermore, when p < 1 + 4 / ( 3 ​ d ) p<1+4/(3d) , using an iteration argument, the best available decay rate is established, as given by Hayashi, Li and Naumkin [ 11 ] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

In this paper, we consider a nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(NLS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=\lambda|u|^{p-1}u,&(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

where
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
u=u(t,x)
is a
\mathbb{C}
-valued unknown function,
u_{0}=u_{0}(x)
is a given function,

p>1
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
One imposes a
dissipative condition

\displaystyle\real\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\quad\imaginary\lambda<0.

In the case
\real\lambda<0
, the condition is called an
attractive-dissipative condition
; otherwise it is said to be a
repulsive-dissipative condition
.
The equation (
NLS
) appears as a relevant model in various physical phenomena, such as nonlinear optics, Bose-Einstein condensates and so on.
For instance, in the optical fiber engineering,

\real\lambda
corresponds to the magnitude of the nonlinear Kerr effect, and
\imaginary\lambda
implies the magnitude
of dissipation due to nonlinear Ohm’s law (see e.g.
[
1
]
).
The dissipative condition is firstly introduced by Shimomura
[
24
]
on the study of the long-time behavior of solutions to (
NLS
).
The condition implies that the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution monotonically decreases as
t\rightarrow\infty
, because
the solution satisfies

(1.1)

\displaystyle\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2}=\norm{u_{0}}_{2}^{2}+\imaginary\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\norm{u(s)}_{p+1}^{p+1}\,ds

for any
t\geqslant 0
.
The aim of this paper is to investigate the time decay rate of the
L^{2}
-norm of solutions to (
NLS
)
without the size restriction on the initial data in any dimensions.

For the case
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, the equation (
NLS
) possesses a Hamiltonian structure.
In particular, the
L^{2}
-norm is conserved for
t
.
In that case, if
p\geqslant 1+2/d
,
then it is known that (
NLS
) admits a global solution that decays in time on the order
-d/2
in
L^{\infty}
,
provided that the data
u_{0}
is small in an appropriate weighted Sobolev space (e.g.,
[
5
,
9
,
12
]
).
The decay rate is the same as that of linear solutions.
In contrast, to the best of our knowledge, when
p<1+2/d
, there are no results regarding
the long-time behavior of solutions to (
NLS
) under
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

When
\imaginary\lambda\neq 0
, the long-time behavior of the solution exhibits different aspects.
As the identity (
1.1
) shows,
if
\imaginary\lambda>0
, then the
L^{2}
-solution blows up in finite time (e.g.,
[
17
,
3
]
).
In the case
\imaginary\lambda<0
, we shall first review some known results under small data.
For
d\leqslant 3
, when
p
is sufficiently close to
1+2/d
with
p\leqslant 1+2/d
, the long-time behavior of the solutions
to (
NLS
) was identified by Shimomura
[
24
]
, and the first author and Shimomura
[
15
]
.
Specifically, they determined the decay rate of the solution as

\norm{u(t)}_{\infty}\lesssim\begin{cases}(t\log t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}&\text{if}\ p=1+2/d,\\
t^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}&\text{if}\ p<1+2/d,\end{cases}

and proved that the solution possesses
\norm{u(t)}_{2}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
In the case
p>1+2/d
,
the third author
[
22
]
showed that the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution does not decay in time and has a lower bound.
These results demonstrate that the long-time behavior of solutions to (
NLS
) under the dissipative condition
is different from that under the non-dissipative condition
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

The first attempt to study the time decay of the
L^{2}
-norm of solutions without the size restriction on the data
was conducted by the first author and Shimomura
[
16
]
.
They showed that in
d=1
, under the strong dispersive condition

(1.2)

\displaystyle(p-1)|\lambda|\leqslant(p+1)|\imaginary\lambda|,\quad\imaginary\lambda<0,

when
p\leqslant 1+2/d
is close to
1+2/d
,
the solution satisfies
\|u(t)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
The condition (
1.2
) was firstly introduced by Liskevich and Perelmuter
[
18
]
to study the analyticity
of some semigroup (cf. Okazawa and Yokota
[
21
]
).
Eventually, assuming the condition (
1.2
), Hayashi, Li and Naumkin
[
11
]
identified the time decay rate
of the
L^{2}
norm of the solution under the initial data
u_{0}\in\Sigma\coloneqq H^{1}\cap\mathcal{F}H^{1}
as follows:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\norm{u(t)}_{2}\lesssim\begin{cases}(\log t)^{-\frac{d}{d+2}}&\text{if}\ p=1+2/d,\\
t^{-\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-1\right)}&\text{if}\ p<1+2/d.\end{cases}

In
[
19
,
14
]
,
the optimality of the decay rate of (
1.3
) for
d=1
is discussed under
u_{0}\in\Sigma
, and
they suggested that the factor
d/(d+2)
in the decay rate of (
1.3
) could potentially be refined to
d/2
.
However, the best available rate for any
\Sigma
-solutions is in (
1.3
).
Another direction for the time decay of the
L^{2}
-norm of solutions is to investigate the relation
between the regularity of solutions and the decay rate.
The direction is treated in Ogawa and the third author
[
20
]
(see also
[
22
,
23
,
22
]
).
It is also indicated that the time decay rate depends on the weighted conditions of the initial data.
In fact, for
d\geqslant 1
, Cazenave and Naumkin
[
4
]
, and Cazenave, Han and Naumkin
[
2
]

showed that the decay rate of the
L^{2}
norm of the solution depends on the weight
N
,
under specific initial data
u_{0}
involved the quadratic phase oscillation
e^{ib|x|^{2}}
with
b\ll 1

satisfying
\inf\expectationvalue{x}^{N}|u_{0}(x)|>0
.

The above results on the
L^{2}
-decay of the solution, without imposing any size restriction on the data,
require the strong dissipative condition (
1.2
), except for
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
.
In this paper, when
p\leqslant 1+2/d
, we identify the time decay rate of the
L^{2}
norm of the solution
without condition (
1.2
) and the size restriction on the initial data
u_{0}
merely in
\Sigma
,
which is a natural space as the behavior of the solution is considered.
In our setting, the sign of
\real\lambda
affects the behavior of the solution.
In fact, when
p\leqslant 1+2/d
, Gerelmaa, the first and third author
[
8
]
discussed the
L^{2}
-decay estimate
of the solution to (
NLS
) under the repulsive dissipative condition
\real\lambda>0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
,
and showed that the decay rate coincides with (
1.3
).
On the other hand, under the attractive dissipative condition
\real\lambda<0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
, Gerelmaa,
the first and third author
[
7
]
obtained the following:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
1<p\leqslant 1+1/d
.
Assume
\real\lambda<0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.
Given
u_{0}\in\Sigma
with
\norm{u_{0}}_{\ \Sigma}\neq 0
,
(
NLS
) admits a unique global solution
u\in C([0,\infty);\Sigma)
.
If
p=1+1/d
, then it holds that

\norm{ u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(\log(1+t))^{-\frac{d}{(d+2)(p-1)}}

for any
t\geqslant 1
.
When
1<p<1+1/d
, the estimate

\norm{ u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-\frac{3-d(p-1)}{2-d(p-1)}\right)}

is valid for any
t\geqslant 0
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the decay rate of the
L^{2}
-norm is slower than (
1.3
).
Further, the more restriction
p\leqslant 1+1/d
is required.
In this paper, we relax the restriction to
p\leqslant 1+4/(3d)
,
and establish the same decay rate as (
1.3
) when
p<1+4/(3d)
.

1.2.
Main result

Based on the Duhamel principle, we give the definition of solutions to (
NLS
) as follows:

Definition 1.2

(Solution)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval and fix
t_{0}\in I
.
We say a function
u\colon I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a solution to (
NLS
) on
I
if
u\in C(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

satisfies

u(t)=U(t-t_{0})u(t_{0})-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}U(t-s)F(u(s))\,ds

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
t\in I
, where
U(t)=e^{\frac{it\Delta}{2}}

and
F(u)=\lambda|u|^{p-1}u
.

The main result in this paper
is as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p\leqslant 1+4/(3d)
.
Assume
\real\lambda<0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.
Take
u_{0}\in\Sigma
with
\norm{u_{0}}_{\ \Sigma}\neq 0
.
Then (
NLS
) admits a unique global solution
u\in C([0,\infty);\Sigma)
.
Moreover, if
p=1+4/(3d)
, then it holds that

\norm{ u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(\log(1+t))^{-\frac{3d}{2(d+2)}}

for any
t\geqslant 1
.
When
1<p<1+4/(3d)
, the estimate

\norm{ u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-1\right)}

is valid for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Remark 1.4
.

The restriction
p\leqslant 1+4/(3d)
is imposed in Proposition
3.1
,
due to the uniform growth estimate
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim t^{1/2}
given in Proposition
2.2
.
If
1+4/(3d)<p\leqslant 1+2/d
, it remains an open problem to establish the decay estimate of the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution under the attractive-dissipative condition
\real\lambda<0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
,
without imposing any size restriction on the initial data.

Remark 1.5
.

Our proof is also applicable to the case of the repulsive-dissipative condition
\real\lambda>0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

The strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on the argument in
[
17
,
8
]
.
Applying the standard energy-type method to (
NLS
), Hölder’s inequality leads to

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2}\lesssim-\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{\beta}\norm{u(t)}_{q}^{p+1-\beta}

for some
\beta>p+1
, where
q=1
if
d=1
and
q=2(d+1)/(d+2)
if
d\geqslant 2
.
The key to obtain the desired decay estimate is to extract as much of the time decay factor as possible from the right-hand side of (
1.4
).
To this end, we apply the following scale-invariant inequality, which can be regarded as the dual of Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality:

\norm{u}_{q}\lesssim\norm{u}_{2}^{1-d\alpha}\norm{x u}_{2}^{d\alpha},\quad\alpha\coloneqq\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}.

This inequality implies that it suffices to obtain a uniform-in-time estimate of
\norm{xu(t)}_{2}
, in order to get the desired
L^{2}
-decay estimate.
We remark that the norm
\norm{xu(t)}_{2}
grows over time in general and
the growth rate depends on that of
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}
. When
\real\lambda\geqslant 0
,
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}

is uniformly bounded in time and hence
\norm{x u(t)}_{2}\lesssim t
holds for large time.
The uniform estimate yields the best available rate in (
1.3
).
In contrast,
when
\real\lambda<0
,
the norm
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}
may grow over time, leading to a higher growth for
\norm{xu(t)}_{2}
.
This, in turn, worsens the decay rate of the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution.

In Theorem
1.1
of
[
8
]
, the restriction
p\leqslant 1+1/d
arises from the use
of the uniform estimate
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2-d(p-1)}}
for large time.
We shall show the refined estimate
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim t^{1/2}
and improve upon the restriction.
To get this refined estimate,
one develops the energy-type estimate

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))\lesssim\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{\frac{2(4p-d(p-1))}{4-d(p-1)}},\quad E(u(t))\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{p+1}\norm{u(t)}_{p+1}^{p+1},

by picking up dissipative effect from the nonlinearity.
Further, in the case
p<1+4/(3d)
,
combining the refined
L^{2}
-decay estimate with (
1.5
) leads to improving
the growth rate of the uniform estimate for
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}
, thereby refining the decay rate of
\norm{u(t)}_{2}
.
By repeating the argument, we can obtain the uniform bound of
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}
and reach
to the decay rate in (
1.3
) as given by
[
11
]
.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we give some results which play an important role throughout this paper.
Let us first recall Strichartz’ estimates. A pair
(p,q)
is said to be admissible if

2\leqslant q,r\leqslant\infty,\quad\frac{2}{q}=d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right),\quad(d,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty).

Lemma 2.1

(Strichartz’ estimate, e.g.,
[
25
,
10
,
27
,
13
]
)
.

Let
(q,r)
and
(\widetilde{q},\widetilde{r})
be admissible pairs.
For any interval
I\ni 0
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\norm{U(t) f}_{L^{q}(I;L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{0}\norm{ f}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\norm{\int_0^t U(t-s) F(s)\, ds}_{L^{q}(I;L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{0}\norm{ F}_{L^{\widetilde{q}^{\prime}}(I;L^{\widetilde{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

where
C_{0}>0
is a certain constant not depending on
I
, and
\rho^{\prime}
is the dual exponent of
\rho\geqslant 1
.

Let us define
J(t)=x+it\nabla
for any
t\geqslant 0
, which is
connected to the Galilean symmetry of the equation (
NLS
).
The operator possesses the property

J(t)=\mathcal{M}(t)it\nabla\mathcal{M}(-t)=U(t)xU(-t),\quad\mathcal{M}(t)\coloneqq e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{2t}}.

Let us prove the existence of a unique global
H^{1}
-solution with
J(t)u\in L^{2}

and the uniform-in-time estimate for
\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}
.
In particular, when
\real\lambda<0
, the growth rate of the estimate is refined, compared to the previous work
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1<p\leqslant 1+4/d
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.
Suppose
u_{0}\in\Sigma
.
Then (
NLS
) has a unique global solution
u\in C([0,\infty);H^{1})
with
J(t)u\in C([0,\infty);L^{2})
. The solution exhibits

(2.3)

\displaystyle\norm{u(t)}_{2}\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\norm{u_{0}}_{2}

for any
t\geqslant 0
.
Moreover, if
\real\lambda\geqslant 0
, there exists
M(u_{0})>0
not depending on

(2.4)

\displaystyle\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}\leqslant{}

\displaystyle M(u_{0})

for any

.
If
\real\lambda<0
,
the solution possesses

(2.5)

\displaystyle\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}

.

Proof.

The existence of the solution has been already proven by several authors, but we give a sketch of the proof for reader’s convenience.
Let us introduce the complete metric space

\displaystyle X_{T}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\left\{u\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{q_{0}}(0,T;L^{r_{0}})\mid\norm{ u}_{X_{T}}\leqslant 2C_{0}\norm{u_{0}}_{2}\right\},

\displaystyle\norm{ u}_{X_{T}}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\norm{ u}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}+\norm{ u}_{L^{q_{0}}(0,T;L^{r_{0}})},\quad d(u,v)\coloneqq\norm{u-v}_{X_{T}},

where
r_{0}=p+1
,
2/q_{0}=d/2-d/r_{0}
and
C_{0}>0
is a certain constant given by Strichartz’ estimate.
In what follows, we simply write
L^{q}(0,T;L^{r})
as
L^{q}_{T}L^{r}
.
The standard well-posedness theory implies that there exist
T>0
and a unique solution
u\in X_{T}

to (

) with

for any admissible pair
(q,r)
(for details, see
[
26
]
).
Thanks to
\imaginary\lambda<0
,
multiplying (
NLS
) by
\overline{u}
, taking the imaginary part in the both side and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we reach to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2}=\imaginary\lambda\norm{u(t)}_{p+1}^{p+1}\,dx\leqslant 0,

which yields
\norm{u(t)}_{2}\leqslant\norm{u_{0}}_{2}
for any
t\in[0,T]
.
Then the
L^{2}
-solution

can be extended globally in time.
Further, since
p<1+4/d
, in view of
u_{0}\in\Sigma
, by means of
J(t)=\mathcal{M}(t)it\nabla\mathcal{M}(-t)
,
we easily verifies
\nabla u
,
J(\cdot)u\in C([0,\infty);L^{2})\cap L^{q}_{loc}((0,\infty);L^{r})

from the persistence of regularity of solutions via the standard bootstrap argument involving Strichartz’ estimate.

Let us move on to prove (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
Multiplying (
NLS
) by
\overline{\partial_{t}u}
, taking the real part in the both side and integrating over

,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))=2\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t)|^{p+1}\imaginary(u(t)\overline{\partial_{t}u(t)})\,dx,

where
\lambda_{1}=\real\lambda
,
\lambda_{2}=\imaginary\lambda<0
and

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{p+1}\norm{u(t)}_{p+1}^{p+1}.

Using (
NLS
) again, one estimates

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))={}&\frac{\lambda_{2}(p+1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\lambda_{2}(p-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p-3}\real\left(u^{2}\overline{\nabla u}^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&{}+2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\norm{u}_{2p}^{2p}\\
\leqslant{}&\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\norm{u}_{2p}^{2p}.\end{aligned}

When
\lambda_{1}\geqslant 0
, (
2.6
) yields
E(u(t))\leqslant E(u_{0})
for any
t\geqslant 0
and thus (
2.4
) is valid.
The case
\lambda_{1}<0
is more delicate.
Set
f=|u|^{\frac{p+1}{2}}
. Note that
\norm{ \nabla|u|}\leqslant\norm{ \nabla u}
.
By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Young’s inequality,
we see from (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda_{2}}{p+1}\norm{ \nabla f}_{2}^{2}+2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\norm{f}_{\frac{4p}{p+1}}^{\frac{4p}{p+1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle-\frac{4|\lambda_{2}|}{(p+1)^{2}}\norm{ \nabla f}_{2}^{2}+C\norm{u}_{2}^{2p\left(1-\theta\right)}\norm{ \nabla f}_{2}^{\frac{4p}{p+1}\theta}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2p\kappa\left(1-\theta\right)},

where

\theta=\frac{d(p-1)(p+1)}{p\left(4+d(p-1)\right)}\in(0,1),\quad\kappa=\frac{4+d(p-1)}{4-d(p-1)}>1,

since
p<1+4/d
.
This implies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))\lesssim\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{\frac{2(4p-d(p-1))}{4-d(p-1)}}.

Since
\norm{u(t)}_{2}\leqslant\norm{u_{0}}_{2}
, (
2.7
) yields (
2.5
).
This completes the proof.
∎

To prove the main result, we employ the following uniform-in-time estimate for
\norm{xu(t)}_{2}
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
1<p<1+4/d
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.
Let
u
be a global solution given by Proposition
2.2
.
Then the solution satisfies
u\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{F}H^{1})
.
Moreover if
\real\lambda\geqslant 0
, then the estimate

\norm{x u(t)}_{2}\lesssim 1+t

holds for any
t\geqslant 0
. If
\real\lambda<0
, the solutions satisfies

\norm{x u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}}

for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Remark 2.4
.

When
\real\lambda\geqslant 0
, the uniform estimate in Lemma
2.3
was obtained in previous works
[
17
]
and
[
8
]

using the operator
J(t)=x+it\nabla
.
We directly establish the estimate
via a virial-type argument, which provides a simpler proof compared to the previous works.

Proof.

Noting
J(t)=x+it\nabla
, it follows that

\displaystyle\norm{x (u(t) - u(t_{0}) )}_{2}\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\norm{J(t) u(t) - J(t_{0}) u(t_{0})}_{2}+|t-t_{0}|\norm{\nabla u(t_{0})}_{2}+|t|\norm{u(t) - u(t_{0})}_{2}\rightarrow 0

as
t\rightarrow t_{0}
, which implies
u\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{F}H^{1})
.
Secondly, let us only treat the case
\real\lambda<0
because the other case is similar.
Multiplying (
NLS
) by
|x|^{2}\overline{u}
, taking the imaginary part in the both side and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\norm{ x u(t)}_{2}^{2}\leqslant\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{u}x\cdot\nabla u\,dx.

Using (
2.8
) and (
2.5
), one deduces from Hölder’s inequality that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\norm{x u(t)}_{2}\leqslant\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies the desired estimate. Thus the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

More precisely, we need the regularization argument to verify (
2.7
) and (
2.9
),
because the equation (
NLS
) makes sense only in
H^{-1}
, and
\partial_{t}u
,
|x|^{2}u\not\in H^{1}
.
(
2.7
) can be justified by the persistence of regularity of solutions, together with the standard density argument.
As for the justification of (
2.9
), we refer the reader to Appendix
A
.

The following scale-invariant inequality, which can be regarded as the dual of Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality, plays a crucial role in proving the main result.
The first author applied this inequality in
[
17
]
to obtain an
L^{2}
-decay estimate for (
NLS
).

Lemma 2.6

(Scale invariant inequality)
.

Let
q\in[1,2)
if
d=1
and
q\in(2d/(d+2),2)
if
d\geqslant 2
.
Set
\alpha=1/q-1/2
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then it holds that

\norm{ f}_{q}\lesssim\norm{ f}_{2}^{1-\frac{d\alpha}{m}}\norm{|x|^{m} f}_{2}^{\frac{d\alpha}{m}}.

Proof.

The case
\norm{f}_{2}=0
is obvious; otherwise, by the scaling and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\norm{ f}_{q}\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{d}{q}}\norm{(1+ |y|^{m})^{-1}}_{2q/(2-q)}\norm{ (1+|y|^{m}) f( \lambda\cdot)}_{2}\lesssim\lambda^{d\alpha}\left(\norm{ f}_{2}+\lambda^{-m}\norm{|x|^{m} f}_{2}\right),

from which the assertion follows, taking
\lambda=\norm{|x|^{m} f}_{2}/\norm{ f}_{2}
.
The proof is completed.
∎

3.
Proof of the main result

As an initial step to prove Theorem
1.3
, we shall show the following
L^{2}
-decay estimate.
The proposition yields the result of the case
p=1+4/(3d)
in Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1<p\leqslant 1+4/(3d)
.
Assume
\real\lambda<0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.
Given
u_{0}\in\Sigma
with
\norm{u_{0}}_{\Sigma}\neq 0
, let
u
be a global solution given by Proposition
2.2
.
If
p=1+4/(3d)
, then it holds that

\norm{ u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(\log(1+t))^{-\frac{3d}{2(d+2)}}

for any
t\geqslant 1
.
When
1<p<1+4/(3d)
, the estimate

\norm{ u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-\frac{3}{2}\right)}

is valid for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We shall follow the argument in
[
17
,
8
]
.
Multiplying (
NLS
) by
\overline{u}
, taking the imaginary part in the both side and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we see from
\imaginary\lambda<0
and Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2}=\imaginary\lambda\norm{ u(t)}_{p+1}^{p+1}\leqslant-|\imaginary\lambda|\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{\beta}\norm{u(t)}_{q}^{p+1-\beta},

where
q=1
if
d=1
and
q=2(d+1)/(d+2)
if
d\geqslant 2
, and
\beta>p+1
is defined by

\beta\alpha=\frac{p+1}{q}-1,\quad\alpha=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}.

By means of Lemma
2.6
, thanks to Lemma
2.3
, one has

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle-\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{p+1+\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}\norm{x u(t)}_{2}^{\frac{d(1-p)}{2}}\lesssim-(1+t)^{-\frac{3d(p-1)}{4}}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{p+1+\frac{d(p-1)}{2}},

which yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{-\frac{(d+2)(p-1)}{2}}\right)\gtrsim(1+t)^{-\frac{3d(p-1)}{4}}

for any
t\geqslant 0
.
Noting (
2.3
) and
p\leqslant 1+4/(3d)
, integrating the above for
t
, we establish the desired estimate.
∎

In the case
p<1+4/(3d)
, we will employ an iteration argument to update the decay rate of
\norm{u(t)}_{2}
to the rate given by
[
11
]
.
Let us consider the recurrence relation

(3.2)

\displaystyle a_{n+1}=ra_{n}+q

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} r&=\frac{d\left(4p-d(p-1)\right)}{(d+2)\left(4-d(p-1)\right)}=1-\frac{2\left(4-3d(p-1)\right)}{(d+2)\left(4-d(p-1)\right)},\\
q&=\frac{1}{2}-r\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-1\right)=-\frac{\left(4-3d(p-1)\right)\left(4-(d-2)(p-1)\right)}{2(d+2)(p-1)\left(4-d(p-1)\right)}.\end{aligned}

The following is valid:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
1<p<1+4/(3d)
. Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence satisfying (
3.2
) with
a_{1}=1/2
.
Then there exists
\alpha<0
such that
\alpha=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}
.

Proof.

The relation (
3.2
) rewrites
a_{n+1}-\alpha=r\left(a_{n}-\alpha\right)
with
\alpha=q/(1-r)
.
Then
a_{n}=\alpha+r^{n-1}(a_{1}-\alpha)
holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thanks to
1<p<1+4/(3d)
, we have
r\in(0,1)
and
q<0
from a direct calculation.
Hence it is concluded that
a_{n}\rightarrow\alpha<0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
as desired.
∎

With Lemma
3.2
in place, we can establish the time uniform bound of
\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}
.

Proposition 3.3

(Iteration argument)
.

Let
1<p<1+4/(3d)
.
Assume
\real\lambda<0
and
\imaginary\lambda<0
.
Take
u_{0}\in\Sigma
with
\norm{u_{0}}_{\ \Sigma}\neq 0
.
Let
u
be a global solution given by Proposition
3.1
.
Then (
2.4
) holds for any

.

Proof.

We see from (
2.5
) and Proposition
3.1
that the solution satisfies

\norm{ u(t)}_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-(a_{1}+1)\right)},\qquad\norm{ \nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{a_{1}},

where
a_{1}=1/2
.
Substituting the first estimate in the above into (
2.7
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{2}{d(p-1)}-(a_{1}+1)\right)\frac{2(4p-d(p-1))}{4-d(p-1)}}.

for any

.
From a use of Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Young’s inequality, this implies

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}\lesssim 1+(1+t)^{ra_{1}+q}\lesssim(1+t)^{a_{2}},

where

are defined as in (

) and

fulfills (

).
Plugging the above into (

\displaystyle\norm{x u(t)}_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{a_{2}+1}

for any

Hence it follows from (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{2}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle-\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{p+1+\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}\norm{x u(t)}_{2}^{\frac{d(1-p)}{2}}\lesssim-(1+t)^{-\frac{d(p-1)}{2}(a_{2}+1)}\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{p+1+\frac{d(p-1)}{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\norm{u(t)}_{2}^{-\frac{(d+2)(p-1)}{2}}\right)\gtrsim(1+t)^{-\frac{d(p-1)}{2}(a_{2}+1)}

By iterating the above argument, we see from Lemma

that there exists

.
Hence, denoting the smallest

such that

∎

Appendix A
Justification of the derivation to (
2.9
) in Lemma
2.3

In this appendix, we shall justify the derivation of (
2.9
) by the regularization argument (e.g.,
[
6
, Proof of Lemma 6.5.2]
).
Set

f_{\varepsilon}(t)=\norm{e^{-\varepsilon|x|^{2}}|x|u(t)}_{2}^{2}

for any
t\geqslant 0
and
\varepsilon>0
.
by the integration by part, we see from (
NLS
) and
\imaginary\lambda<0
that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\nabla\left(e^{-2\varepsilon|x|^{2}}|x|^{2}\overline{u(t)}\right)\cdot\nabla u(t)+2\lambda e^{-\varepsilon|x|^{2}}|x|^{2}|u(t)|^{p+1}\right\}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\imaginary\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varepsilon|x|^{2}}\left(2-4\varepsilon|x|^{2}\right)e^{-\varepsilon|x|^{2}}x\overline{u(t)}\cdot\nabla u(t)\,dx.

Since
e^{-\varepsilon|x|^{2}}\left(2-4\varepsilon|x|^{2}\right)
is bounded in
x
and
\varepsilon
, Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality gives us

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)\leqslant\norm{\nabla u(t)}_{2}\sqrt{f_{\varepsilon}(t)}.

Integrating the above from
0
to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(t)\leqslant\norm{x u_{0}}_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\norm{\nabla u((s))}_{2}\sqrt{f_{\varepsilon}(s)}\,ds.

Hence, Gronwall’s inequality leads to

\displaystyle\sqrt{f_{\varepsilon}(t)}\leqslant\norm{xu_{0}}_{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\norm{\nabla u(s)}_{2}\,ds.

Thanks to Fatou’s lemma, we have
\norm{x u(t)}_{2}^{2}\leqslant f_{\varepsilon}(t)
for any
t
. Then

\displaystyle\norm{x u(t)}_{2}\leqslant\norm{xu_{0}}_{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\norm{\nabla u(s)}_{2}\,ds

holds. The estimate is nothing but (
2.9
).
∎

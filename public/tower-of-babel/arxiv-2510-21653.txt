Quantum Difference Equations for Grassmannians
Xingyu Cheng, Reese Lance, Nikhil Nagabandi, Andrey Smirnov

Abstract
We consider quantum difference equation (QDE) for equivariant quantum K-theory of the Grassmannian. In this paper we obtain a solution to the QDE and use the solution to asymptotically derive the Bethe ansatz equations. In the limit, we obtain similar results for the cohomological analogue. For both cases, we describe the nonequivariant solutions as well. As an application, we identify the quantum K-theory ring of Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) \mathrm{Gr}(k,n) with a quantum 5 vertex XXZ integrable spin chain.

\Psi(qz)\mathcal{O}(-1)=M(z)\Psi(z).

40

\mathrm{L}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}A(x)&B(x)\\

The main space of study is
\textrm{Gr}(k,n)

k

A partition

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}\geq 0

k
-

of

The

Then move vertically until there is a top edge in the diagram. Repeat this procedure and stop when the top right corner of the rectangle is reached (if at the ceiling of the rectangle, move east). The path should have
n

1,\dots,n
, starting at the bottom left corner. The labels on the vertical steps form the
k

k

2.1
Cohomology

Consider the complete flag
F_{\bullet}=(0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}=\C^{n})
. The Schubert variety corresponding to a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{k})
is

X_{\lambda}(F_{\bullet})=\{V\in X\mid\dim(V\cap F_{k+i-\lambda_{i}})\geq i\text{ for all $i\in[k]$}\},

where
[k]:=\{1,\dots,k\}
. The class of the Schubert variety is denoted by
\sigma_{\lambda}
, and does not depend on
F_{\bullet}
. The cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
(with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients) has a basis given by the Schubert classes
\sigma_{\lambda}
for partitions
\lambda
for partitions
\lambda
in the
k\times(n-k)
box. This basis will be referred to as the

The quantum cohomology of the Grassmannian
\textrm{Gr}(k,n)
is an algebra, denoted as
\textrm{QH}^{*}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))
. As a module,
\textrm{QH}^{*}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))=\textrm{H}^{*}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[z]
, where
z
is the quantum parameter. The product structure of
\textrm{QH}^{*}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))
is given by a sum over partitions
\nu
and degrees

:

\sigma_{\lambda}*\sigma_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu,d\geq 0}z^{d}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\sigma_{\nu}

(5)

where the coefficients

are given by certain genus 0 Gromov-Witten invariants (see
[
Ber97
]
and
[
Buc03
]

We will be mainly concerned with the equivariant versions of the above. The ring structure of

is defined very similarly to the usual quantum cohomology, except we replace

with certain genus 0 3-point equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants (see

]

will be an algebra over
\textrm{H}^{*}_{T}(\textrm{pt})=\mathbb{Z}[u_{1},\dots,u_{n}]
.
\textrm{H}^{*}_{T}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))
has a basis given by the equivariant versions of Schubert classes, which we also denote by

2.2
K-theory

To define the equivariant quantum K-theory ring for Grassmannians, we follow the treatment of
[
Mih
]
,
[
BM11
]
,
[

Denote the representation ring of

The

\mathrm{K}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))=\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Rep}_{T}

The determinant tautological subbundle

The

\lambda

The qde for the

3.1
The operator
M^{C}(z)

Let
M^{C}(z):=-c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(-1))*
, where
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(-1))*
is the operator of quantum multiplication by
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(-1))
. Note
M^{C}
coincides with
\sigma_{1}*
. The formula for this operator is a special case of the Pieri rule (
[
Mih06
]
) for
\sigma_{1}*
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})
be a partition in the
k\times(n-k)
rectangle and
r=\{r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\}
be the corresponding
k

M^{C}(z)\sigma_{\mu}=\sum_{\lambda}\sigma_{\lambda}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{r_{i}}\right)\sigma_{\mu}+z\sum_{\nu}\sigma_{\nu},

where
\lambda
ranges over all partitions such that
|\lambda|=|\mu|+1
and

n-k\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}\geq\mu_{k},

and
\nu

|\nu|=|\mu|+1-n

\mu_{1}-1\geq\nu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}-1\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{k}-1\geq\nu_{k}\geq 0.

With
M^{C}(z)

R

3.2
Partition functions for cohomology

We find the
R
matrix from a transition function of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as described in
[
OS22
]
. First consider the torus
\mathbb{C}_{a}^{\times}
acting on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as

[x:y]\to[ax:y]=[x:a^{-1}y].

We have the fixed points
p_{1}=[1:0]=0
and
p_{2}=[0:1]=\infty
. We assign the ordering and weights as:

p_{1}
p_{2}
{<}
-u
u

Then we have attracting sets
Attr_{+}(p_{1})=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
Attr_{+}(p_{2})=p_{2}
. From this we have the positive attracting matrix defined with restrictions as
T_{ij}^{+}=Attr_{+}(p_{j})|_{p_{i}}
:

T^{+}=\begin{pmatrix}Attr_{+}(p_{1})|_{p_{1}}&Attr_{+}(p_{2})|_{p_{1}}\\
Attr_{+}(p_{1})|_{p_{2}}&Attr_{+}(p_{2})|_{p_{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&u\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

Then consider the same diagram with the opposite order:

p_{1}
p_{2}
{>}
-u
u

Then we have
Attr_{-}(p_{1})=p_{1}
and
Attr_{-}(p_{2})=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. From this we have

T^{-}=\begin{pmatrix}Attr_{-}(p_{1})|_{p_{1}}&Attr_{-}(p_{2})|_{p_{1}}\\
Attr_{-}(p_{1})|_{p_{2}}&Attr_{-}(p_{2})|_{p_{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-u&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(13)

Then we compute using (
12
) and (
13
) to get

(T^{-})^{-1}\cdot T^{+}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&u\end{pmatrix}.

(14)

This forms the middle block of our
R

R(u)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&1&u&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

This satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation

R_{a1}(u_{1}-x)R_{a2}(u_{2}-x)R_{12}(u_{2}-u_{1})=R_{12}(u_{2}-u_{1})R_{a2}(u_{2}-x)R_{a1}(u_{1}-x).

As written,

denotes the auxiliary space and each Lax operator acts on the spaces of the subscript. For instance,
R_{a1}:\mathbb{C}_{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{2}\to\mathbb{C}_{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{2}
acts as

With these weights, the partition function can be obtained via lattice models. This was done in

]
, and is the same as the factorial Schur polynomial.

Proposition 3.2
.

The partition function for
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{T}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))
coincides with the factorial Schur polynomials:

s_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{u})=\frac{\det\left((x_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{u})^{\lambda_{j}+k-j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})},

where
(x\,|\,\boldsymbol{u})^{b}=(x-u_{1})\cdots(x-u_{b})
is the falling factorial.

3.3
\textrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\textrm{Gr}(1,n))
case: solution to qde

We prove the solution to the qde

\epsilon z{\partial}_{z}\psi(z)^{1n}=M(z)^{C}\psi(z)^{1n}
, using products of Gamma functions to construct the explicit form of the solution. Recall the identity
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The solution the qde for
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(1,n))
is as follows: choose a partition
\lambda
in the
1\times(n-1)
rectangle. Then the solution
\psi_{\lambda}^{1n}
is a class in
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(1,n))
of the form:

\psi_{\lambda}({x},\boldsymbol{u},z)^{1n}=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}s_{\lambda}(x+d\epsilon)\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}

(15)

Proof.

Out of the possible partitions, we have two cases. For convenience we will omit writing
x
and
\boldsymbol{u}
in
\psi(x,\boldsymbol{u},z)
for the proof.

Case 1:

\lambda\neq(n-1)

Recall that with Proposition
3.1
,
M(z)^{C}s_{\lambda}=u_{r_{1}}s_{\lambda}+s_{\lambda+1}
, where
r
is the corresponding
1
-subset to
\lambda
(in this case
\lambda_{1}+1=r_{1}
). Then

M(z)^{C}\psi_{\lambda}(z)^{1n}=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{r_{1}}s_{\lambda}(x+d\epsilon)+s_{\lambda+1}(x+d\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}.

With this, the difference of both sides is

\displaystyle\epsilon z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\psi_{\lambda}(z)^{1n}-M(z)^{C}\psi_{\lambda}(z)^{1n}

\displaystyle=\epsilon z\cdot\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/\epsilon+d)\,s_{\lambda}(x+d\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-1}{\epsilon^{-nd}}

(16)

\displaystyle-z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{r_{1}}s_{\lambda}(x+d\epsilon)+s_{\lambda+1}(x+d\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x+d\epsilon-u_{r_{1}})s_{\lambda}(x+d\epsilon)-s_{\lambda+1}(x+d\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}.

(17)

By the factorial Schur polynomial formula (
6
), we have
s_{\lambda}(x)=\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}}(x-u_{m})
. From this, the numerator of (
17
) simplifies as

\displaystyle(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{r_{1}})s_{\lambda}(x\!+\!d\epsilon)\ -\

\displaystyle s_{\lambda+1}(x\!+\!d\epsilon)=(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{r_{1}})\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}}(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{m})-\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}+1}(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{m})

\displaystyle=(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{r_{1}})\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}}(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{m})-(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{\lambda_{1}+1})\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}}(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{m})

\displaystyle=(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{r_{1}})\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}}(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{m})-(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{r_{1}})\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{1}}(x\!+\!d\epsilon\!-\!u_{m})

\displaystyle=0,

which results in (
17
) reducing to 0.

Case 2:

\lambda=(n-1)

The beginning is similar to case 1, but note
M(z)^{C}s_{n-1}=u_{n}s_{\lambda}+z
, and we will show the difference of both sides telescopes to 0. Observe

\displaystyle\epsilon z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\psi_{\lambda}(z)^{1n}-M(z)^{C}\psi_{\lambda}(z)^{1n}

\displaystyle=\epsilon z\cdot\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/\epsilon+d)\,s_{n-1}(x+d\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-1}{\epsilon^{-nd}}-z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}s_{n-1}(x+d\epsilon)+z}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x+d\epsilon-u_{n})s_{n-1}(x+d\epsilon)-z}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}.

(18)

We have from the factorial Schur polynomial formula (
6
) that

\displaystyle(x+d\epsilon-u_{n})s_{n-1}(x+d\epsilon)

\displaystyle=(x+d\epsilon-u_{n})\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}(x+d\epsilon-u_{m})

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{n}(x+d\epsilon-u_{m}).

Note that when
d=0
, restricting
x
to any fixed point (i.e.
x=u_{i}
for any
i\in[n]
) makes this product 0. By equivariant localization of the ring, this quantity is equivalent to 0 in this ring. Using this, we further split (
18
) into two terms and eliminate the
d=0
term of the first sum:

\displaystyle z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x+d\epsilon-u_{n})s_{n-1}(x+d\epsilon)-z}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}=

\displaystyle=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{m=1}^{n}(x+d\epsilon-u_{m})}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}-z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{m=1}^{n}(x+d\epsilon-u_{m})}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}-z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}.

(19)

Using the shift identity of the Gamma function, we have

\frac{(x+d\epsilon-u_{i})}{\Gamma(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{i}}{\epsilon}+1)}=\frac{\epsilon}{\Gamma(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{i}}{\epsilon})}.

Then the first term of (
19
) changes:

\displaystyle=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon^{n}}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}-z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle=z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}\right)}\left(\frac{z^{d}}{\epsilon^{n(d-1)}}\right)-z^{\frac{x}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}+d-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z^{d+1}}{\epsilon^{nd}}\right).

Re-indexing the first sum with shift
d\to d+1
results in the difference of two identical sums, completing the proof.
∎

3.4
\textrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))
case: solution to qde via geometric Satake

By
[
CV24
]
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}[z]
-modules

\vartheta_{k,n}:\bigwedge^{k}_{\mathbb{Z}[z]}\mathrm{H}^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})\to\mathrm{H}_{T}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)).

Let
\sigma^{\mathbb{P}}_{p}
denote a class in the cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
and
\sigma^{G}_{\lambda}
denote a Schubert class in the cohomology of the Grassmannian. For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, the isomorphism
\vartheta_{k,n}
takes

\sigma^{\mathbb{P}}_{\lambda_{k}}\wedge\sigma^{\mathbb{P}}_{\lambda_{k-1}+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\sigma^{\mathbb{P}}_{\lambda_{1}+k-1}\mapsto\sigma^{G}_{\lambda}.

By solving the qde for
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, we get solutions that live in
\mathrm{QH}_{T}^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
. If we take the
k
-th wedge power and then map by
\vartheta_{k,n}
,
[
CV24
]
showed that the result is now a solution to the qde for
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
. Using this Satake correspondence, we are able to construct the solution to the qde in
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
from copies of the qde solution for
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(1,n))
.

Denote by
\boldsymbol{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
and
\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{d}\epsilon:=(x_{1}+d_{1}\epsilon,\dots,x_{k}+d_{k}\epsilon)
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The solution the qde for
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
is as follows: choose a partition
\lambda
in the
k\times(n-k)
rectangle. Then the solution
\psi_{\lambda}
is a class in
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
taking the form:

\psi_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{x}},\boldsymbol{u},z)=C\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\frac{\prod\limits_{b,c=1}^{k}\left(\frac{x_{c}-x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-d_{b}+1\right)}{\prod\limits_{b=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}s_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{d}\epsilon)\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}

(20)

where
C=((-1)^{k-1}z)^{(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}\prod_{b,c=1}^{k}\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1\right)^{-1}
The sum over
\boldsymbol{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
means to sum over
d_{b}\geq 0
for each
b\in[k]
, and
|\boldsymbol{d}|=d_{1}+\cdots d_{k}
.

In order to prove this Theorem we first need the following Lemma:

Lemma 3.5
.

\prod\limits_{b=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{c=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c})-(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1)}=(-1)^{(k+1)|\boldsymbol{d}|}\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon}+d_{i})-(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j})}{\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}}

(21)

Proof.

First note that in the left side’s double product, the
b=c
terms are 1, so we may write

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{b=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{c=1}^{k}

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c})-(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1)}=

\displaystyle=\left(\prod\limits_{b<c}\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c})-(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1)}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{b>c}\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c})-(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{b<c}\left(\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c})-(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b})-(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+1)}\right).

(22)

Now pick
\xi,\eta\in[k]
. Note that using the identity
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
we find each term of the product can be written as

\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{\xi}}{\epsilon}+d_{\xi})-(\frac{x_{\eta}}{\epsilon}+d_{\eta})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{\xi}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{\eta}}{\epsilon}+1)}\frac{\Gamma((\frac{x_{\eta}}{\epsilon}+d_{\eta})-(\frac{x_{\xi}}{\epsilon}+d_{\xi})+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{x_{\eta}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{\xi}}{\epsilon}+1)}=\left(\frac{\frac{x_{\xi}-x_{\eta}}{\epsilon}+d_{\xi}-d_{\eta}}{\frac{x_{\xi}-x_{\eta}}{\epsilon}}\right)(-1)^{d_{\xi}-d_{\eta}}.

Note that
\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{d_{i}-d_{j}}=(-1)^{(k+1)|\boldsymbol{d}|}
, so we use the this and last line to write (
22
) as

(-1)^{(k+1)|\boldsymbol{d}|}\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon}+d_{i})-(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j})}{\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}},

completing the proof.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Using the Satake Correspondence from
[
CV24
]
, the solution
\psi(z)
is a wedge product of solutions
\psi^{1n}
, with a shift of
z
and dividing by the Vandermonde determinant. Written as a determinant we have

\psi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{u},z)=\frac{\det\left[\psi^{1n}_{\lambda_{i}+k-i}\left(x_{j},\boldsymbol{u},(-1)^{k-1}z\right)\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

(23)

We write this in our desired form via algebraic manipulation. First we rewrite the numerator. Using the definition of determinant, the numerator of (
23
) is

=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\psi^{1n}_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}\left(x_{j},(-1)^{k-1}z\right).

(24)

By Proposition
3.3
, we write (
24
) as

=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(((-1)^{k-1}z)^{\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}}\sum_{d_{j}=0}\frac{s_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d_{j}}\right).

(25)

Let
\widetilde{C}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}((-1)^{k-1}z)^{x_{j}/\epsilon}=((-1)^{k-1}z)^{(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k})/\epsilon}

25

\boldsymbol{d}

\widetilde{C}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{s_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d_{j}}\right).

We’ll switch the sums and distribute the product to write this as

\widetilde{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{s_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d_{j}}\right).

(27)

From
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left({(-1)^{k-1}z}/{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{d_{j}}=\left({(-1)^{k-1}z}/{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}

\widetilde{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{s_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\right)\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}.

Since

s_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x)=\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x-u_{m}).

With this, (

\widetilde{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\frac{\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon-u_{m})}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}.

(29)

Observe by manipulating the numerator, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{m=1}^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon-u_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon\,|\,\boldsymbol{u})^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-{\sigma(j)}}

\displaystyle=\det\left[(x_{j}+d_{j}\epsilon\,|\,\boldsymbol{u})^{\lambda_{i}+k-{i}}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq k}

\displaystyle=s_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{d}\epsilon)\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{i}+d_{i}\epsilon-x_{j}-d_{j}\epsilon).

With this manipulation, we write (

) as

\widetilde{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\frac{s_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{d}\epsilon)\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{i}+d_{i}\epsilon-x_{j}-d_{j}\epsilon)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}.

(30)

Now that the numerator has been rewritten, we will divide back the denominator. Using (
30
), we write (
23
) as

\widetilde{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}}\frac{s_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{d}\epsilon)\,}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(x_{i}+d_{i}\epsilon-x_{j}-d_{j}\epsilon)}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z}{\epsilon^{n}}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}.

(31)

Note that Lemma
3.5
gives us

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(x_{i}+d_{i}\epsilon-x_{j}-d_{j}\epsilon)}{x_{i}-x_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon}+d_{i}-\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}-d_{j})}{\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(k-1)|\boldsymbol{d}|}\prod_{b,c=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-d_{b}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}.

Plugging this manipulation back into (

) and simplifying we may cancel the

terms. In addition let
C=\widetilde{C}\prod_{b,c=1}^{k}\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+1\right)^{-1}
, Then (

3.5
Bethe equations and Bethe vectors

Using the solution to the qde for

, we asymptotically can derive the Bethe equations. These coincide with those derived from the Bethe Ansatz (see Section 4.2 of
[
GK17
]
).

Proposition 3.6
.

For
\mathrm{QH}^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
there are
k
Bethe equations: One for each
j\in[k]
given by

(x_{j}-u_{1})\cdots(x_{j}-u_{n})=(-1)^{k-1}z.

Proof.

First note that as
\epsilon\to 0
we have
\partial_{x}\ln\Gamma(x/\epsilon)=\Gamma^{\prime}(x/\epsilon)/\Gamma(x/\epsilon)\approx\ln(x/\epsilon)=\ln(x)-\ln(\epsilon)
.

Let
\varphi
denote the integrand solution except the factorial Schur polynomial and part of
C
:

\varphi:=\frac{\prod_{b,c=1}^{k}{\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-d_{b}+1\right)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|+(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{|\boldsymbol{d}|n}}\right).

The saddle point equations are determined by
x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln(\varphi)=0
for
j\in[k]
.

For
j\in[k]
the left side is

\displaystyle x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\left(\frac{\prod_{b\neq c}{\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-d_{b}+1\right)}}{\prod_{b=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)}\left(\frac{z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|+(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{|\boldsymbol{d}|n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\sum_{b\neq c}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-d_{b}+1\right)-x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\sum_{b=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}+d_{b}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)+

\displaystyle\hskip 289.07999pt+x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\left(\frac{z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|+(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{|\boldsymbol{d}|n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\neq j}^{k}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-d_{b}+1\right)+\sum_{c\neq j}^{k}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}-d_{j}+1\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)+x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\left(\frac{z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|+(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{|\boldsymbol{d}|n}}\right).

Note as
\epsilon\to 0
, we have
\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}-d_{b}+1\right)\approx\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}-\frac{x_{b}}{\epsilon}\right)\approx\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln(\frac{x_j-x_b}{\epsilon})
. Similarly,
\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{c}}{\epsilon}+d_{c}-\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}-d_{j}+1\right)\approx-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln(\frac{x_c-x_j}{\epsilon})
. For the third sum, we have that
\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}+d_{j}-\frac{u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}+1\right)\approx\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{x_{j}-u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}\right)\approx\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\left(\frac{x_{j}-u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}\right)
. Then replacing each into above we have

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}\left(\sum_{b\neq j}^{k}\ln\left(\frac{x_{j}-x_{b}}{\epsilon}\right)-\sum_{c\neq j}^{k}\ln\left(\frac{x_{c}-x_{j}}{\epsilon}\right)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ln\left(\frac{x_{j}-u_{\ell}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)+

\displaystyle x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\left(\frac{z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|+(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{|\boldsymbol{d}|n}}\right).

We have the first two sums cancel to
\ln(-1)^{k-1}
, and we can condense the third sum as
\ln(\prod(x_j-u_\ell))
. For the last term we have

x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\left(\frac{z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|+(x_{1}+\cdots x_{k})/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{|\boldsymbol{d}|n}}\right)=x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\sum_{b=1}^{k}\ln\frac{z^{(x_{b}/\epsilon+d_{b})}}{\epsilon^{nd_{b}}}=x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln\frac{z^{(x_{j}/\epsilon+d_{j})}}{\epsilon^{nd_{j}}},

which is asymptotically equivalent to
x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\ln{z^{(x_j/\epsilon)}}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Since
\partial_{x_{j}}\ln{z^{(x_j/\epsilon)}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln z
, our expression above becomes

\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}\left(\ln(-1)^{k-1}-\ln\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}(x_{j}-u_{\ell})\right)+\ln z\right).

(33)

Setting this equal to 0, eliminating
\frac{x_{j}}{\epsilon}
, and taking the exponential of both sides we obtain

\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}(x_{j}-u_{\ell})=(-1)^{k-1}z,

completing the proof.
∎

The roots of this equation plugged into the factorial Schur polynomial give the eigenvalues of
M(z)^{C}

we construct the off shell Bethe Vector as

\begin{pmatrix}s_{\lambda^{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}\,|\,\boldsymbol{u})\\
s_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}\,|\,\boldsymbol{u})\\
\vdots\\
s_{\lambda^{\binom{n}{k}}}(\boldsymbol{x}\,|\,\boldsymbol{u}).\end{pmatrix}.

Plugging in solutions of the Bethe equations for
\boldsymbol{x}
give the eigenvectors of
M(z)
.

Lemma 4.5

Let

Consider the product

The

\left(\prod_{b<c}\frac{\left(\frac{x_{b}}{x_{c}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\frac{x_{b}q^{d_{b}}}{x_{c}q^{d_{c}}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}\right)\left(\prod_{b>c}\frac{\left(\frac{x_{b}}{x_{c}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\frac{x_{b}q^{d_{b}}}{x_{c}q^{d_{c}}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}\right)=\left(\prod_{b<c}\frac{\left(\frac{x_{b}}{x_{c}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\frac{x_{b}q^{d_{b}}}{x_{c}q^{d_{c}}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}\frac{\left(\frac{x_{c}}{x_{b}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\frac{x_{c}q^{d_{c}}}{x_{b}q^{d_{b}}}q,q\right)_{\infty}}\right)

4.4
Bethe equations via saddle point and Bethe vectors

Proposition 4.7
.

At
q\to 1
the saddle point of the integral (
42
) corresponds to the
k
Bethe equations

z\prod\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\ \{j\}}\frac{-x_{j}}{x_{i}}=\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{x_{j}}{u_{\ell}}\right),

(56)

for
j\in[k]
.

Proof.

In the solution (
42
), note
z^{d_{i}}
can be written as

z^{d_{i}}=z^{\frac{\ln x_{i}q^{d_{i}}}{\ln q}}z^{\frac{-\ln x_{i}}{\ln q}}.

Rewrite each
z^{d_{i}}
of
z^{|\boldsymbol{d}|}
as this.
Let
\varphi
denote the inside of the sum except for the double Grothendieck polynomials and each
z^{\frac{-\ln x_{i}}{\ln q}}
(keep the numerator). The saddle point is defined by the equations
x_{j}\partial_{j}\ln(\varphi)=0
for
j\in[k]
. First we use the following Lemma:

Lemma 4.8
.

Take limit
q\to 1
. Asymptotically, we have

x\frac{\partial\ln((x,q)_\infty)}{\partial x}=\frac{-\ln(1-x)}{\ln(q)}+o(\ln(q)).

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\ln(x,q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\ln\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ln(1 - x q^m)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{(xq^{m})^{b}}{b}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{b}}{b}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{bm}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{b}}{b(1-q^{b})}

\displaystyle\approx-\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{b}}{-\,b^{2}\ln q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln q}\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{b}}{b^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x)}{\ln q},

where the approximation
q^{b}=e^{b\ln q}\approx 1+b\ln q
was used since
q\to 1
. By taking derivatives we obtain our desired result.
∎

Also note that as
q\to 1
, we have
x_{i}q^{d_{i}}\approx x_{i}
. Then observe for

\displaystyle x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\ln(\varphi)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\ln\left[\prod\limits_{b,c=1}^{k}(x_{j}/x_{c},q)_{\infty}\;\prod_{b=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}(x_{b}/u_{\ell},q)_{\infty}^{-1}\;\prod\limits_{c=1}^{k}z^{\frac{\ln x_{c}}{\ln q}}\right]

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ln q}\left(\sum_{c\neq j}-\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{x_{c}}\Bigr)\;+\;\sum_{b\neq j}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{b}}{x_{j}}\Bigr)\;+\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{u_{\ell}}\Bigr)\;-\;\ln z\right)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\sum_{c\neq j}-\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{x_{c}}\Bigr)\;+\;\sum_{b\neq j}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{b}}{x_{j}}\Bigr)\;+\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{u_{\ell}}\Bigr)\;-\;\ln z

\displaystyle=0.

Then by exponentiating both sides we obtain:

\displaystyle\exp\Biggl[-\sum_{c\neq j}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{x_{c}}\Bigr)+\sum_{b\neq j}^{k}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{b}}{x_{j}}\Bigr)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ln\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{u_{\ell}}\Bigr)\Biggr]

\displaystyle=z

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{b\neq j}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{b}}{x_{j}}\Bigr)\;\;\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{u_{\ell}}\Bigr)}{\displaystyle\prod_{c\neq j}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x_{j}}{x_{c}}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle=z

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{x_{j}}{u_{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=z\prod\limits_{b\in\{1..k\}\setminus\ j}\frac{-x_{j}}{x_{b}}.

∎

The solutions to the Bethe equations give eigenvalues of
M(z)
. Since the representative for

is

The off shell Bethe Vector is

\begin{pmatrix}G_{\lambda^{1}}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{u})\\
G_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{u})\\
\vdots\\
G_{\lambda^{\binom{n}{k}}}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{u}).\end{pmatrix}.

For each Bethe equation, substituting its roots for
\boldsymbol{x}
gives an eigenvector

\textrm{QK}(\textrm{Gr}(k,n))

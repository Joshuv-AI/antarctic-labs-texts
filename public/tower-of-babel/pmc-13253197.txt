Magnetic field-assisted thawing enhances quality attributes, microstructure, and sensory properties of glutinous rice Daifuku
Authors: Mengzi Nie, Yue Zhang, Yu Tian, Jiaxin Li, Enhong Gao, Li-Tao Tong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFreezing and thawing significantly affect the texture and flavor of glutinous rice-based desserts like Daifuku. This study investigated the impact of magnetic field-assisted thawing (MFT) on Daifuku quality, comparing it with conventional refrigerated thawing (RFT) under identical freezing conditions. Daifuku samples were frozen at −20 °C in a pulsed magnetic field (6 mT, 0.8 Hz) and thawed at 5 °C under various MFT conditions (static and pulsed fields). Results showed that MFT, particularly at 4 mT and 0.6 Hz, shortened thawing time by 18.6%, preserved texture and aroma. MFT inhibited ice recrystallization, maintained starch-protein network integrity, improved water distribution by limiting bound water migration and promoted uniform pore distribution. Compared to RFT, MFT-treated samples exhibited 80% reduced chewiness, 18% increased resilience and improved viscoelastic properties. Sensory evaluation revealed that MFT-treated samples resembled fresh Daifuku. These findings confirm MFT as an effective and economical thawing strategy for glutinous rice products.Keywords: Glutinous rice products, Magnetic field-assisted thawing, Pulsed magnetic field, Ice crystal, Quality retention

Introduction

Daifuku is a popular dessert that features an elastic outer layer made from glutinous rice flour and an inner filling such as cream, bean paste, and jam, which requires freezing to maintain freshness and extend shelf life during distribution (Tian et al., 2025). The subsequent thawing process is critical, as it plays a key role in determining the final product quality, both under household conditions and in the food industry. Traditional thawing methods, such as refrigerated, room-temperature, and water thawing, are limited by slow heat transfer. This prolongs the time spent in the phase transition zone, thereby increasing the risk of ice recrystallisation. Due to its high amylopectin content, Daifuku's gel network is particularly sensitive during thawing, making it more prone to the formation of large ice crystals that disrupt the starch-protein network, thereby significantly reducing sensory acceptability and commercial value (Chang et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024).

To address these limitations, emerging non-thermal and advanced mild-heat thawing techniques, such as ultrasound, high-pressure, pulsed electric fields, ohmic, and radio frequency, have been explored (Bereda, 2025; Mahmood et al., 2024). These methods can accelerate thawing rates or reduce moisture loss, but they may introduce mechanical or thermal stress, leading to adverse physical or chemical alterations in the product (Safwa et al., 2024). Furthermore, the requirement for specialized equipment often poses challenges for industrial-scale application (Cai et al., 2019; Mahmood et al., 2024; Safwa et al., 2024). Consequently, for frozen Daifuku and associated products, a more gentle and energy-efficient approach is preferable (Jia et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2025).

Magnetic field treatment is a novel and promising non-thermal technique that can regulate hydrodynamics and ice crystallization during freeze-thaw cycles. Studies suggest that magnetic field exposure can shorten the duration water remains in the phase transition zone (approximately −1 °C to −5 °C), where ice crystallization is most active (Jha et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2025). This effect promotes the formation of finer, more uniform crystals and reduces the amount of water susceptible to freezing, ultimately improving the texture and sensory properties of starch-based foods after freeze-thaw cycles (Li et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025a). The underlying mechanisms involve distinct actions of different field types. Static fields influence the orientation of dipoles and the organization of hydrogen bonds, while pulsed fields periodically disrupt the alignment of water molecules and temporarily reorganize hydrogen bonds. This dynamic effect increases the dissipation of energy at the ice-water interface, prevents the formation of new crystals, and stabilizes the ordered domains within the starch gel. Together, these mechanisms reduce ice recrystallisation and help to maintain the integrity of the gel network (Shafiei et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025). Additionally, although magnetic field treatment requires a specialized refrigerator, it demands less equipment compared to methods that require complex or expensive devices.

Although magnetic fields have been applied to rice-based matrices, comprehensive investigations targeting the thawing stage of glutinous rice confections remain limited. In particular, studies on Daifuku-like products that compare static fields (a magnetic field with a constant intensity without variation in its strength) versus pulsed fields (a magnetic field that alternates between positive and negative intensities at specific frequencies) and link thawing kinetics to microstructure, water distribution, rheology, texture, and sensory attributes are still scarce (He et al., 2023b; Jin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023). Due to high amylopectin content, limited amylose/amylopectin-lipid complexes and thin-skinned structure of Daifuku, it may exhibit distinct thawing behavior and failure modes.

Therefore, this study would evaluate the effects of static and pulsed magnetic fields on the thawing performance and quality of frozen Daifuku. Commercial Daifuku samples were used, which were chosen to reflect typical market formulations and to ensure that the research is aligned with industry practices, where product formulations are generally consistent. The focus of this study was to evaluate the impact of magnetic-field-assisted thawing (MFT) on Daifuku's texture and sensory properties, rather than modifying the formulation itself. The samples were pre-frozen under controlled conditions, and then thawed using conventional refrigerated thawing (RFT) or MFT of various intensities and pulse frequencies. We evaluated the physicochemical properties, including thawing curves, microstructure, molecular structure, moisture distribution, rheology, texture, and sensory attributes. The aim is to integrate the results of apparent indicators, explore the possible mechanisms underlying MFT, and to provide practical guidance on improving industrial thawing strategies for frozen rice products.

Materials and methods

Materials

All Daifuku samples were supplied by Hefei Quanji Food Co., Ltd. (Hefei, Anhui Province, China). The main ingredients of Daifuku included glutinous rice flour (total starch content: 85.08 ± 0.23%, amylose content: 6.12 ± 0.04%), whipping cream, drinking water, white granulated sugar, vegetable oil, salt, and trehalose. Trehalose, commonly used in the food industry, was added to retard starch retrogradation, which may improve texture stability and prolong shelf life. Although trehalose may influence starch retrogradation, its role during MFT was not the primary focus of this study, and its impact on the thawing process was considered secondary to the main variable under investigation. The study employed commercially prepared Daifuku from a single production batch to avoid inconsistencies in freezing behavior arising from morphology differences in self-prepared products. All other reagents were analytical grade.

Static and pulsed magnetic-field assisted thawing of Daifuku

Commercial Daifukus (25 ± 0.5 g each) were divided into seven treatment groups, five pieces per group, and individually vacuum-sealed. The samples were first equilibrated at 5 °C for 6 h in a refrigerator to standardize the initial temperature, and then frozen in a pulsed magnetic field chamber at −20 °C with an intensity of 6 mT and a frequency of 0.8 Hz using a Magnetic Field Freezer (MFI 10-ProS, INDUC Scientific, China) until fully frozen. Our previous research demonstrated that these freezing conditions have minimal impact on Daifuku's quality. Therefore, Daifuku was frozen under these conditions. Subsequently, they were thawed at 5 °C under RFT and MFT, respectively. For the MFT treatments, static and pulsed magnetic fields with varying intensities were applied. Specifically, the frozen samples were exposed to static magnetic fields of 2, 4, and 6 mT. Based on the thawing-rate measurements, the optimal magnetic field intensity was identified. Preliminary results indicated that the appearance and textural properties of thawed Daifuku at 0.8 Hz showed no significant improvement compared with 0.6 Hz. Therefore, pulsed magnetic field oscillation frequency was set to 0.2, 0.4, and 0.6 Hz to evaluate its effects on the textural quality of the Daifuku. All samples were placed at the identical position within the Magnetic Field Freezer during the MFT treatments. A portion of the thawed Daifuku skin samples was freeze-dried for subsequent microstructure and molecular structure analyses, and part of the freeze-dried samples was further ground and passed through a 100-mesh sieve as required. The remaining thawed samples were directly used for morphology analysis, moisture distribution, rheological measurements, texture analysis and sensory evaluation without freeze-drying.

Thawing temperature profile

Thawing curves were recorded with a digital temperature logger (LogET 5TE, Jiangsu Jingchuang Electric Co., Ltd., Jiangsu Province, China). A T-type thermocouple probe, connected to a computer, was inserted into the geometric center of each Daifuku sample, and the displayed values were recorded as the core temperature. Data were collected at 30 s intervals.

Morphological changes and microstructural analysis

The microstructure of the outer surface of thawed Daifuku was examined following the method of Marta et al. (2025) with appropriate modifications. After gold sputter-coating, the freeze-dried samples were observed using a scanning electron microscope (SEM; Hitachi S-570, Hitachi, Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) operated at an accelerating voltage of 10 kV, and images were captured at 500× magnification. For cross-sectional morphology and dimensional analysis, each thawed Daifuku was cut through its geometric center, and its length, width, and height were precisely measured.

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) analysis of thawed Daifuku skin

For FTIR measurements, 2 mg of freeze-dried Daifuku skin sample was thoroughly ground and mixed with 200 mg of oven-dried KBr powder and pressed into a pellet. Spectra were collected on a Tensor 27 FTIR spectrometer equipped with a DLATGS detector (Bruker Optics, Germany) over the range of 4000–400 cm−1, with 64 scans at a resolution of 4 cm−1. Baseline correction was performed automatically in the 1200–800 cm−1 region using OMNIC 8.0 software, and spectra were then deconvoluted with a half-bandwidth of 19 cm−1 and an enhancement factor of 1.9. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate following the method of Bhavadharani and Gurumoorthi (2025).

X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis of thawed Daifuku skin

The crystalline characteristics of thawed Daifuku skin were analyzed using an X-ray powder diffractometer (Bruker D8 Advance, Germany). Before measurement, all freeze-dried and powdered samples were equilibrated to a uniform moisture content of approximately 10% by storing them at 30 °C for about 12 h in a sealed chamber containing saturated lithium chloride solution. Diffraction patterns were collected according to the method of Yertai et al. (2025) with minor modifications. The instrument was operated with Cu–Kα radiation (λ = 0.1542 nm) at 40 kV and 40 mA, using a step size of 0.02° and a scanning rate of 10°·min−1 over a 2θ range of 5–45°.

Freezable water content of thawed Daifuku skin

The freezable water content was determined by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC; DSC 8000, Perkin Elmer, Norwalk, CT, USA) following the method of Tian et al. (2025). Approximately 5 mg of sample from the geometric center of the thawed Daifuku skin was sealed in an aluminum pan and immediately placed in the DSC. High-purity nitrogen (70 mL·min−1) served as the purge gas, with an empty pan as the reference. The temperature program involved cooling from 20 °C to −40 °C at 10 °C·min−1, holding at −20 °C for 5 min, and then heating back to 20 °C at the same rate.

Moisture distribution in thawed Daifuku skin

The moisture distribution of thawed Daifuku skin was analyzed using low-field nuclear magnetic resonance (LF-NMR) spectroscopy equipped with an imaging module (NMI20-030H-I, Niumag Electronic Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai China). The transverse relaxation time (T₂) was determined using the Carr–Purcell–Meiboom–Gill (CPMG) pulse sequence. Instrument parameters were set as follows: spectrometer frequency (SF) 21 MHz, echo time (TE) 0.1 ms, number of echoes (NECH) 2000, and number of scans (NS) 64. Each group of samples was analyzed in triplicate, and mean values were used for statistical analysis.

Rheological analysis of thawed Daifuku skin

The rheological behavior of thawed Daifuku skin was evaluated following the method of Bao et al. (2025) with minor modifications. Tests were performed on a rotational rheometer (Physica MCR 301, Anton Paar GmbH, Austria) equipped with a parallel-plate geometry (40 mm diameter, 1 mm gap). A strain sweep from 0.01% to 10% was first conducted to determine the linear viscoelastic region, after which a frequency sweep (0.1–10 Hz) was carried out at a fixed strain of 0.1%. Key rheological parameters were recorded in real time to characterize the viscoelastic properties of the samples.

Texture analysis and penetration properties of thawed Daifuku

Texture profile analysis (TPA) of the entire thawed Daifuku was performed using a texture analyzer (TA-XT Plus Texture Analyzer, Stable Micro Systems, Godalming, UK). The experimental procedure was adapted from the method established by Dong et al. (2024) with minor modifications. Specifically, a P/2 cylindrical probe (2 mm in diameter) was used for penetration/puncture tests and the maximum penetration strength of the entire thawed Daifuku was recorded. A P/50 cylindrical probe (50 mm in diameter) was used for compression tests. Instrument parameters were set as follows: pre-test speed 2.0 mm·s−1, test speed 1.0 mm·s−1, post-test speed 1.0 mm·s−1, trigger force 5.0 g, and compression strain 50%. Additionally, the two ends of the thawed Daifuku skin (cut to 60 mm × 30 mm) were fixed in tensile grips, ensuring that the sample could freely stretch in the vertical direction. The test speed was set to 2 mm/s based on the sample's viscoelasticity. The strain height was recorded as the maximum length that the sample could stretch before breaking. All measurements were conducted at room temperature, and each sample was tested in at least five replicates to ensure the reliability of the data.

Sensory analysis of thawed Daifuku

A consumer panel of 30 well-trained volunteers (approximately 70% female and 30% male, aged 20–50 years) evaluated the sensory attributes of thawed Daifuku using a 9-point hedonic scale (1 = dislike extremely, 9 = like extremely). All participants had basic knowledge of food sensory evaluation and formal training in assessing Daifuku quality. Seven attributes—appearance, surface color, stickiness and elasticity, aroma, chewiness, flavor, and overall acceptability, were scored, and the mean panel scores were calculated for statistical analysis. The procedure followed the method of Tian et al. (2025) with minor modifications. The sensory evaluation procedures carried out in this study were fully compliant with local ethical regulations and were approved by the Ethics Committee of IFST-CAAS (Institute of Food Science and Technology, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences). The authors affirm that participants' rights and confidentiality were rigorously maintained throughout the study. To ensure ethical standards, participation was voluntary, study objectives and potential risks were clearly communicated, written informed consent was obtained from all participants, disclosure of participant information without explicit permission was prohibited, and participants were allowed to withdraw at any point. A comprehensive consent form detailing the sensory evaluation protocol has been provided in the supplementary material.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Experimental data were subjected to one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and significant differences among treatment means were evaluated using Duncan's multiple range test at a significance level of P < 0.05. Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Data visualization was conducted with Origin 2021 (Origin Lab Corp., Northampton, MA, USA).

Results and discussion

Thawing curves

The temperature–time profiles of Daifuku subjected to RFT and MFT were presented in Fig. 1. All samples were frozen under identical conditions (−20 °C, 6 mT, 0.8 Hz) to ensure comparable initial states, and the core temperature was recorded continuously from −18 °C to 5 °C. The thawing process consists of three stages: preheating, phase transition, and heating (Kaur & Annapure, 2024)

Thawing curves of Daifuku during conventional thawing and magnetic field-assisted thawing at various magnetic field intensities.

The RFT group required the longest thawing time (approximately 70 min) to rise from −18 °C to 5 °C. Static magnetic fields of 2, 4, and 6 mT shortened the total thawing time to about 58–60 min, with no significant differences among these static field treatments. Similar trends have been reported in glutinous rice dumplings, where stronger fields provided no further acceleration once sufficient water-dipole alignment was achieved (Li et al., 2023). The 4 mT treatment was selected for subsequent experiments as it provided rapid thawing similar to 6 mT without the additional energy cost of higher field strengths.

Pulsed magnetic field further accelerated thawing: frequencies of 0.2 and 0.4 Hz reduced thawing time to roughly 57–59 min, and at 0.6 Hz achieved the fastest thawing (approximately 53 min), representing an improvement of about 18% compared with RFT. This faster thawing efficiency was mainly attributed to improved heat transfer and accelerated phase transition, which shortened the exposure time of samples within the critical temperature zone where moisture migration typically occurs (Kong et al., 2024). Rapid thawing is beneficial for product quality because extended residence in the phase-transition zone promotes moisture migration and starch retrogradation, leading to surface roughness and flavor loss (Liu et al., 2023; Qiao & Peng, 2024). Thus, pulsed magnetic fields at 4 mT with frequencies, especially at 0.6 Hz, offered a high-efficiency thawing strategy for Daifuku.

Macroscopic appearance and microstructure

The morphological characteristics of Daifuku after different thawing treatments are shown in Table 1. Representative top and side views with SEM micrographs are presented in Fig. 2, in which the applied field strength and pulsed frequency are specified directly in the label (e.g., MFT-4mT &0.2 Hz indicates pulsed magnetic field-assisted thawing at 4 mT and 0.2 Hz). RFT caused the greatest dimensional change. Compared with fresh Daifuku, the RFT samples showed significant reductions in length (4.81 cm) and height (2.43 cm), while the aspect ratio (length/width ≈ 1.0) indicated uniform horizontal contraction but pronounced loss of height. MFT effectively mitigated this deformation. Static fields of 2–6 mT resulted in significantly higher sample heights (up to 2.74 cm at 4 mT) relative to RFT, and pulsed magnetic fields at 4 mT with frequencies of 0.2–0.6 Hz maintained lengths and widths comparable to fresh Daifuku while maintaining heights around 2.58–2.63 cm. These dimensional data are consistent with the visual appearance in Fig. 2, where RFT-treated samples exhibited a yellowish surface and obvious flattening, whereas samples subjected to MFT retained a more rounded and upright profile with only slight central indentation. As shown in Fig. 2(B), the irregular and enlarged pores observed in the RFT samples likely resulted from localized mechanical stress and uneven water redistribution during recrystallization, which weakened the interfacial bonding within the starch–protein matrix (Wei et al., 2025). In contrast, MFT treatments markedly reduced the number and size of voids, and pulsed magnetic fields offered the greatest structural protection. Under the conditions of 4 mT and 0.6 Hz, the microstructure most closely resembled that of fresh Daifuku, with fewer depressions and a more cohesive gel network, indicating that ice-crystal coalescence and recrystallization were effectively suppressed (Jiang et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2024). These results indicated that MFT could mitigate structural damage compared with conventional RFT. By limiting the excessive growth of ice crystals, it helped preserve the starch–protein network and the water-holding capacity of the glutinous rice-based skin, thereby retaining the soft and elastic texture typical of freshly prepared Daifuku (He et al., 2024).

XRD analysis

The XRD patterns of Daifuku skin powders after different thawing treatments are presented in Fig. 3(A). All samples were fully gelatinized before freezing, therefore the native A-type crystallinity of glutinous rice starch was almost completely disrupted (Tian et al., 2025). Relative crystallinity (RC) differed significantly among treatments (P < 0.05). The RFT group showed the lowest RC (3.19% ± 0.27%), indicating severe loss of long-range order. Static magnetic field-assisted thawing (2–6 mT) resulted in significantly higher RC values (6.06%–7.14%), representing increases of roughly 90%–120% over RFT (P < 0.05). Applying a pulsed magnetic field at 4 mT further enhanced structural ordering, reaching 9.33% ± 0.21% at 0.4 Hz and 9.51% ± 0.78% at 0.6 Hz. All pulsed treatments were significantly higher than the static field treatments (P < 0.05) but did not differ from each other. The decrease in long-range order under RFT may be related to water migration and subsequent rearrangement of hydrogen-bonded structures during thawing, rather than direct mechanical disruption by ice crystals. Magnetic fields appeared to stabilize these molecular interactions by modulating hydrogen-bond dynamics (Yun et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). Although all treated samples remained far below the crystallinity of native rice starch, MFT effectively alleviated the loss of long-range order compared with conventional RFT, consistent with previous observations in other starch-based systems (He et al., 2023a).

FTIR analysis

The short-range molecular ordering of starch and the secondary structure of proteins in Daifuku skin after different thawing treatments were analyzed by FTIR spectroscopy and deconvolution of the amide I band (Fig. 3(B) and Table 2). The ratio of absorbance at 1047 cm−1 to 1022 cm−1 (R1047/1022), an indicator of the short-range double-helix order of starch, increased markedly in all thawed samples compared with fresh Daifuku, reflecting the retrogradation and molecular reorganization of starch during storage and thawing (Zhang et al., 2023). RFT produced the highest R1047/1022 value (1.9061 ± 0.0014), consistent with pronounced retrogradation. MFT slowed this increase: static fields of 4 mT and 6 mT yielded ratios around 1.61, approximately 15% lower than RFT (P < 0.05), while the pulsed treatment at 4 mT & 0.6 Hz further reduced the ratio to 1.5662 ± 0.0072, a decrease of nearly 18% relative to RFT. These results indicated that the magnetic fields, particularly when combined with pulses, suppressed the recrystallization process of starch, thereby mitigating the hardening of texture.

In addition to starch structural changes, the secondary structure of proteins also responded to magnetic-field-assisted thawing, showing a similar trend of structural stabilization. Compared with fresh samples, all thawed groups showed lower α-helix content and higher proportions of β-sheet and β-turn structures, signifying loss of ordered helical motifs and increased molecular unfolding (Li et al., 2025b). However, MFT-treated samples exhibited a milder decline in α-helix content, ranging from 16% to 21%, with the highest retention (21.36%) observed at 4 mT and 0.6 Hz. The moderation of α-helix loss suggests that magnetic fields limit the formation and growth of large ice crystals, thereby reducing disruption of the protein–starch network and preserving hydrogen bonding within protein molecules (He et al., 2024). Magnetic fields have similarly protected protein conformation in wheat dough by modulating hydrogen bonding and preventing excessive protein aggregation, resulting in a softer, more elastic structure (He et al., 2023; Mioduszewski & Cieplak, 2021).

Water state and distribution

The water state distribution of the Daifuku skin after different thawing treatments is shown in Fig. 3(C), and the thermal transition parameters obtained from DSC are summarized in Table 3. Moisture in the gel matrix can be classified as tightly bound water (T₂₁), immobilized water (T₂₂), and free water (T₂₃). To quantitatively analyze the distribution of moisture in different binding states, the peak area normalization method was adopted. A21, A22, and A23 represent the ratios of the peak areas of T21, T22, and T23 to the total peak area. In more complex systems such as sweet dumplings and wheat dough, the bound water content is large, which contributes to greater variation in the observed results (He et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). Fresh Daifuku contained 25.85% A21, 70.91% A22, and 3.24% A23. RFT markedly reduced the proportion of bound water to 20.34%, a 21% decrease relative to the fresh control (P < 0.05), while semi-bound water increased to 78.54%, indicating substantial migration of tightly bound water into less restricted states. MFT significantly alleviated this shift. Static fields of 2–6 mT limited the loss of A₂₁ to about 22.8%, and pulsed magnetic fields at 4 mT further raised the bound-water fraction compared with RFT, demonstrating that magnetic treatment suppressed the conversion of bound to immobilized water and slowed moisture redistribution during freeze–thaw cycling (Wang et al., 2024).

DSC measurements provided complementary evidence. In the RFT-treated samples, the enthalpy of melting (ΔH) increased from 45.33 J·g−1 in fresh Daifuku to 64.38 J·g−1, and the freezable-water content rose from 31.66% to 50.73%, indicating that a greater fraction of water became freezable as the gel network weakened and tightly bound water was released (Otsuka et al., 2017). MFT reduced both parameters in a field-strength-dependent manner. At 4 mT and 0.6 Hz, freezable water decreased to 34.36%, ΔH dropped to 52.32 J·g−1, and the peak melting temperature declined by about 1.4 °C compared with RFT (P < 0.05). These reductions reflected tighter water–matrix interactions and fewer large ice crystals, consistent with the higher proportion of bound water observed by LF-NMR.

Overall, the LF-NMR and DSC results demonstrated that MFT effectively restrained the migration of tightly bound water into more mobile states, thereby lowering freezable water and melting enthalpy (Guo et al., 2025). By stabilizing hydrogen bonding within localized water clusters and the starch–protein gel network, magnetic fields reduced ice-crystal recrystallization and helped maintain the structural integrity and textural quality of the Daifuku skin during thawing.

Rheological properties

The dynamic rheological behavior of Daifuku skin after different thawing treatments is shown in Fig. 3(D and E). For all samples, the storage modulus (G′) exceeded the loss modulus (G″) across the entire frequency range of 0.1–10 Hz, and tan δ remained above 0.2, indicating a predominantly elastic response and the typical viscoelastic, gel-like nature of glutinous rice skin (Wang et al., 2026). With increasing frequency, G′ and G″ both increased, but the increase rate of the RFT sample was markedly lower than that of the MFT samples. The lower G′ values observed for RFT may be associated with the reduced junction density and the increased internal friction within the gel network, as the large ice crystals disrupted the continuity of the viscoelastic phase. MFT maintained a higher degree of molecular entanglement, leading to greater energy storage capacity during deformation (Tao et al., 2025).

Additionally, MFT significantly reduced the decline in viscoelastic properties. For the full range of frequencies tested, a static magnetic field of 2–6 mT resulted in higher G′ and G″ values than RFT. Specifically, the 4 mT pulsed field (0.2–0.6 Hz) provided the greatest enhancement, reaching G′ values of around 4000 Pa at 10 Hz and RFT values of approximately 3200 Pa. Compared to RFT, the MFT samples exhibited lower tanδ values, which further confirmed that the magnetic field could preserve the elastic properties of the gel matrix (Jin et al., 2024). These results indicated that MFT preserved the soft, elastic texture of Daifuku skin and enhanced structural stability during processing, thereby protecting the starch-protein network, and it was also hypothesized to suppress the recrystallization of ice crystals.

Textural properties

Both texture (Table 4) and penetration measurements (Table 1) indicated the differences in physical properties of Daifuku skin after various thawing processes. Compared to fresh Daifuku samples, RFT exhibited significantly increased hardness values (from 144.32 g to 697.58 g) and chewiness values (from 128.84 g to 779.65 g), as well as obvious changes in elasticity and adhesion. The increase in hardness of RFT samples is possibly due to the combined action of phase transformation stress and starch retrogradation, which causes the corresponding microstructure to reduce elasticity (Qiao & Peng, 2024). However, MFT effectively prevented these negative effects. In static magnetic field treatment (2–6 mT), there was a decrease in hardness values of the sample by over 50% compared to RFT, while cohesiveness and elasticity mostly remained unchanged. Among these, it was found that the 4 mT pulsed magnetic field produced the most beneficial results. At 0.6 Hz, the hardness of the Daifuku skin reached 310.27 g and exhibited an 18% increase in elasticity and an approximately 80% reduction in chewiness compared to the RFT group (P < 0.05). All freeze-thawed samples showed negative adhesion values, reflecting the tensile force needed to separate the probe. It is notable that the MFT group showed only a slight decline, demonstrating greater surface cohesion and water retention capacity.

In order to evaluate the toughness and fracture resistance of Daifuku skin, which cannot be fully reflected by TPA parameters, penetration testing was used. When compared with fresh Daifuku (107.81 g, 136.88 mm), the RFT sample revealed a lower penetration force (102.16 g) and a reduced deformation capacity (138.38 mm). Therefore, it can be concluded that the conventional freeze-thaw cycles caused the surface become relatively more fragile and prone to cracking. For the MFT group, the penetration results exhibited values similar to those of the fresh control (approximately 106–108 g), along with slight increase in strain and height values, which suggested enhanced elasticity and rupture resistance in the glutinous rice skin. As shown in Table 1, the pulsed MFT at 4 mT and 0.4–0.6 Hz achieved the highest penetration intensity and strain, reflecting a soft and elastic gel network. Therefore, these results demonstrated that MFT effectively reduced starch recrystallisation and preserved the integrity of the starch-protein network, thereby decreasing surface hardening and increasing elasticity compared to conventional thawing.

Sensory evaluation

The Fig. 3(F) showed the sensory evaluation of Daifuku after different thawing treatments. There were significant differences between the various treatments. RFT had the lowest score for most attributes, particularly appearance, chewiness, and overall acceptability. It also demonstrated the rough surface, increased hardness value, and loss of moisture observed in the texture and microstructure. Furthermore, MFT improved the sensory attributes. The appearance, color, and viscoelasticity of samples treated under the static magnetic field of 6 mT were better than those treated under 2 mT or 4 mT (P < 0.05). It indicated that the Daifuku had improved surface roughness and enhanced chewiness. The highest overall score was achieved by the pulsed magnetic field at 0.6 Hz and 4 mT, with the chewiness, flavor, and acceptability approaching those of fresh Daifuku (with an average acceptability score of around 6.5 on a 9-point scale). As can be seen in Fig. 2, the pulsed MFT group displayed a more continuous gel network, which correlates with superior flavor and aroma retention. This network prevented the loss of volatile compounds, restrained key lipid oxidation and protected Maillard reaction compounds, helping to preserve the characteristic aroma of glutinous rice (Hu et al., 2024). Overall, these findings confirmed that MFT, particularly at 4 mT and 0.6 Hz, could preserve the ideal appearance, texture, and flavor of Daifuku. Notably, the increased acceptance from consumers suggested that the magnetic field can significantly improve the commercial quality of freeze-thawed glutinous rice products.

Integrated quality assessment and mechanism

Based on the detailed data of the physicochemical parameters mentioned above, a comprehensive analysis was conducted on the significant differences between MFT and RFT. As shown in Fig. 4(A), there presented the thawing mechanism of Daifuku, with two different treatments: conventional thawing (RFT) and MFT. Under RFT conditions, heat transfer within the critical phase transition zone of the Daifuku sample is slower, resulting in a prolonged residence time. This promotes the growth and aggregation of large ice crystals, which easily disrupt the structure of the starch-protein network (Kumar et al., 2020). Specifically, the RFT group showed higher freeze-thaw water content and lower viscoelastic modulus, as well as significant loss of elasticity and cohesiveness and poorer sensory scores, which was consistent with the results of Li et al. (2023).

At the same time, MFT produced an entirely different thawing pattern. The magnetic field accelerated the transfer of heat within the samples, causing the ice core to melt more quickly and shortening the phase transition period (Li, Sun, et al., 2025). Through SEM observation, Daifuku skin exhibited a more uniform and dense gel network structure with smaller ice crystal sizes under the influence of the magnetic field, indicating that ice recrystallization was inhibited (Zhao et al., 2025). What's more, the crust structure of Daifuku was kept compact and elastic owing to the fact that it retained more bound water (Li et al., 2023).

There are also significant differences between static and pulsed magnetic fields. Compared to RFT, static fields of 2–6 mT improved thawing quality, but the improvement was limited. By contrast, a 4 mT pulsed field at 0.4–0.6 Hz produced better results for almost all parameters, which is consistent with the results of other studies (Jin et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024). This was because the pulsed field periodically disrupts the alignment of water dipoles, which in turn promoted transient hydrogen bond rearrangements that facilitate energy redistribution at the ice-water interface (He et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). This dynamic effect further sped up the melting of ice crystals and decreased their propensity to combine into larger structures (Li, Sun, et al., 2025). The repeated reorientation of water molecules also helped maintain a stable hydrogen-bond network within the starch-protein gel, thereby inhibiting short-term starch retrogradation and protecting protein secondary structures.

Additionally, the correlation heatmap (Fig. 4(B)) revealed significant relationships between quality parameters. Hardness, elasticity, and resilience showed strong positive correlations, suggesting that the structural integrity maintained through MFT is closely linked to improvements in texture. This is consistent with the observation that MFT preserved the starch-protein network and limited the breakdown of gel structure during thawing. In contrast, springiness and gumminess exhibited negative correlations with hardness, further confirming that the softer and more elastic Daifuku produced under MFT treatment maintained a desirable balance of texture attributes. Moisture parameters, such as freezable water (FW) and unfreeable water (UW), were positively correlated with hardness and gumminess (r > 0.8), suggesting that water retention was crucial for texture and quality. These results supported by both structural and rheological analyses, and highlighted the critical role of MFT in preserving Daifuku's overall quality during the thawing process. By reducing the mechanical stress caused by large ice crystals and ensuring better water distribution, MFT enhanced both the microstructure and macroscopic texture of Daifuku, leading to a final product with characteristics similar to fresh Daifuku.

Conclusion

In the present study, MFT was found to be an efficient and quality-ensuring thawing approach that involves stabilizing hydrogen bonding interactions through the use of magnetic fields. It maintained the starch-protein gel network and preserved the structure of bound water by suppressing ice recrystallization and regulating water migration. Compared to conventional thawing methods, the combined effect of these molecular and microstructural changes resulted in enhanced elasticity, reduced hardness, and improved sensory qualities. Among all treatments, the pulsed magnetic field at 4 mT and 0.6 Hz provided the optimal balance between thawing efficiency and product quality. This research underscores the potential for MFT to be applied to other traditional and modern food products, offering a more efficient and gentle thawing method for frozen foods. Overall, MFT offered a practical and scalable strategy for maintaining the softness and cohesiveness of glutinous rice products like Daifuku during industrial thawing and distribution processes. Further research is needed to explore the applicability of MFT in other starch-based or frozen food systems and to evaluate its potential effects on starch digestibility and glycemic response.

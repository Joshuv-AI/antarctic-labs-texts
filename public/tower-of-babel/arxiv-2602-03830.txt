1
Introduction

A classic theme of group theory research is the generation property of groups. The structure of a group can be described using its generators. There have been numerous research on generators of specific groups. For example, it is delivered in undergraduate group theory courses that symmetric groups can be generated with two elements. Using the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, it is proved that every finite non-abelian simple group is
2
-generated.

One of the interesting generation problems is the growth rate of the minimum number of generators of the direct product
G^{n}
where
G
is a finite group. Wiegold shows in
[
16
]
that the number of required generators grows linearly when
G
is an imperfect group and it grows logarithmically when
G
is perfect. Apart from direct products, another way to construct groups is via wreath products. In this paper, we will investigate the minimum number of generators of iterated wreath products.

Bhattacharjee
[
1
]
investigates the generation property of iterated wreath product of alternating groups constructed via the natural action. She proves that the probability that such products can be generated by two random elements is positive. This shows that iterated wreath products of alternating groups are
2
-generated. Quick
[
12
]
generalises this result and shows that the same result stills holds for iterated wreath products of non-abelian simple groups constructed from any faithful transitive action of the factors. Bondarenko
[
2
]
considers iterated wreath products of transitive permutation groups with uniformly bounded number of generators. He establishes that the number of generators of the product involving
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
is bounded if and only if the number of generators of the abelian direct product
G_{1}/G_{1}^{\prime}\times G_{2}/G_{2}^{\prime}\times\dots\times G_{n}/G_{n}^{\prime}
is bounded as well.

Recently, the author of this paper
[
9
]
considers the generators required for the iterated wreath products of symmetric groups, alternating groups or cyclic groups. In particular, it is established that the minimum number of generators of iterated wreath products of symmetric groups and alternating groups is the maximum of
2
and the number of symmetric groups involved. The method used relies on Lucchini’s work
[
10
]
. Lucchini determines the number of generators required for the wreath product
A\operatorname{wr}S_{n}
of an abelian group
A
constructed via the natural action of the symmetric group of degree
n
. This is then adapted for the wreath product
A\operatorname{wr}A_{n}
constructed via the natural action of the alternating group of degree
n
in
[
9
]
. In
[
10
]
, Lucchini also establishes the minimum number of generators for the wreath product
A\operatorname{wr}G
constructed via the regular action of a simple group
G
. We will use this result as part of our argument and generalise it to apply to the case that
G
is an almost simple group.

In this article, we will establish the precise number of generators required for an iterated wreath product constructed from the regular action of almost simple groups. If we take the factors involved to be simple groups, then we recover the observation that iterated wreath products constructed via the regular action of non-abelian simple groups are
2
-generated.

To state the theorem, we recall some basic notations. For a group
G
, we write
d(G)
for the minimum number of generators for
G
. For a finite abelian group
A
and a prime number
p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
, we write
d_{p}(A)
for the minimum number of generators for the Sylow-
p
subgroup of
A
. Because of the Fundamental Theorem of Finite Abelian Groups,
d(A)=\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}}d_{p}(A)
. We will establish the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, …,
G_{k}
be a sequence of almost simple groups. Let
S
be the socle of
G_{1}.
Let

W=G_{k}\operatorname{wr}G_{k-1}\operatorname{wr}\dots\operatorname{wr}G_{1}

be the iterated wreath product constructed via the regular action of each factor. Set

A=(G_{k}/G_{k}^{\prime})\times(G_{k-1}/G_{k-1}^{\prime})\times\dots\times(G_{2}/G_{2}^{\prime}).

Then

d(W)=\max_{p}(d(A\times G_{1}),d(A)+1,d_{p}(A)+2),

where
p
ranges over the set of prime numbers dividing both
\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
.

We finish this introduction by giving the structure of this paper. In Section
2
, we recall basic definition that we will use. We will also introduce the results that our conclusions depend on, particularly the ones from
[
10
]
. In Section
3
, using the results in
[
10
]
and also extending them to cover a more general case, we will determine the minimum number of generators of the wreath product of an abelian group by an almost simple group constructed via the regular action. We will then provide the main proof of the theorem in the final section. We will prove it by showing that
d(W)=d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
and determining
d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
, where
W,A,G_{1}
are as defined in Theorem
1.1
(see Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
respectively).

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the notations that we will use and summarize the
facts from previous work that we rely upon. We first start with the
definition of wreath products and its associated notation.

Let
G
and
H
be permutation groups on the finite sets
X
and
Y
,
respectively. Let
B=\prod_{y\in Y}G_{y}
be the direct product
of copies of
G
indexed by the set
Y
. We shall write
b=(g_{y})=(g_{y})_{y\in Y}
for an element of
B
expressed as a
sequence of elements from
G
indexed by
Y
. We define an action
of
H
on
B
by permuting the entries of its elements in the same way
that
H
acts on
Y
:

(g_{y})^{h}=(g_{yh^{-1}})

for
b=(g_{y})\in B
and
h\in H
. The (permutational) wreath
product
W=G\operatorname{wr}_{Y}H
of
G
by
H
is the semidirect product
W=B\rtimes H
constructed via this action. In this article, we shall
construct wreath products from the regular action of
H
. Consequently, we shall omit the
subscript on the wreath product and simply write
W=G\operatorname{wr}H
for the
wreath product of
G
by
H
constructed via the regular action of the
permutation group
H
.

The wreath product
W=G\operatorname{wr}H
has a natural action on the Cartesian
product
X\times Y
given by

(x,y)^{g}=(x^{g_{y}},y^{h})

for
(x,y)\in X\times Y
and
g=(g_{y})h\in W
. As a
consequence, if
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, …,
G_{k}
is a sequence of
permutation groups acting regularly on the sets
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
,
…,
X_{k}
, respectively, then we may iterate the wreath product
construction exploiting this induced action. Thus we recursively
define
W_{1}=G_{1}
acting upon the set
X_{1}
and then, for
2\leqslant i\leqslant k
, having
defined
W_{i-1}
with an action on
X_{i-1}\times\dots\times X_{1}
, we define
W_{i}=G_{i}\operatorname{wr}W_{i-1}
with its action on

X_{i}\times X_{i-1}\times\dots\times X_{1}
.

Let
d(G)
be the minimum number of generators of a finite group
G
. We have the following important result.

Theorem 2.1
(Lucchini, Menegazzo
[
11
]
).

If
G
is a non-cyclic finite group with a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
, then
d(G)=\max(2,d(G/N))
.

Given a group
G
, we say that an additively written abelian group
M
upon which
G
acts via automorphisms is a
G
-module
. We can consider a
G
-module
M
as a module for the group ring
\mathbb{Z}G
. If such
M
is isomorphic to an abelian chief factor of the group
G
as a
G
-module, then it is an elementary abelian
p
-group for some prime
p
and therefore can also be viewed as a module for the group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We define
I_{G}
to be the kernel of the natural map
\mathbb{Z}G\to\mathbb{Z}
induced by mapping every element of
G
to
1
, which is also called the
augmentation ideal
of the group ring
\mathbb{Z}G
. We denote by
d(I_{G})
the minimum number of generators of
I_{G}
as a
G
-module.

Similar to Lucchini’s earlier work
[
10
]
on generation of wreath products, in order to obtain the number of generators of a group
G
, we shall use the
presentation rank

\operatorname{pr}(G)
. It is a non-negative integer by definition in
[
14
]
, but this also follows from Lemma
2.2
below. Our work relies on the following results involving
\operatorname{pr}(G)
. Lemma
2.2
below characterise
\operatorname{pr}(G)
and links
d(G)
to
d(I_{G})
, the minimum number of generators of the augmentation ideal of
G
as a
G
-module.

Lemma 2.2
(Roggenkamp
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Let
G
be a finite group. Then

\operatorname{pr}(G)=d(G)-d(I_{G}).

Lemma 2.3
(Gruenberg
[
6
, (B.ii)]
).

Let
G
be a finite group. If
\operatorname{pr}(G)>0
and
N
is a soluble normal subgroup of
G
, then
d(G)=d(G/N)
.

Our work relies upon the results in Lucchini’s work
[
10
]
. In the following, we will recall the notations required and introduce useful conclusions.

A chief factor of a finite group
G
is the quotient
H/K
of normal subgroups
H
and
K
with
K<H
such that there is no normal subgroup
N
of
G
satisfying
K<N<H
. For a chief factor
X=H/K
and a normal subgroup
N
of
G
, we say
X
is
contained
in
N
if
K<H\leqslant N
. We will also say
X=H/K
is
contained
in
G/N
if
N\leqslant K<H\leqslant G
. We will say that
H/K
is a
complemented
chief factor if there exists a subgroup
C
such that
G=HC
and
H\cap C=K
; that is, when
C/K
is a complement to the subgroup
H/K
in the quotient group
G/K
. If an abelian chief factor
H/K
is complemented, then we can find a maximal subgroup to be the complement
C
. Given a
G
-module
M
, we define
\delta_{G}(M)
to be the number of abelian chief factors of
G
isomorphic to
M
as a
G
-module and complemented in a chief series of
G
. Using Theorem 2.1 in
[
7
]
, it follows that
\delta_{G}(M)
does not depends on choices of chief series of
G
and hence is an invariant of
G
.

For a finite group
G
and an additively written
G
-module
A
, the first cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,A)
is equal to the quotient of the group
\mathrm{Der}(G,A)
of derivations
G\to A
by the group
\mathrm{Inn}(G,A)
of inner derivations (see, for example,
[
13
, Chapter 11]
). A derivation
\delta\colon G\to A
is a function from the group
G
to the additive module
A
such that for
g,h\in G
,

(gh)^{\delta}=(g^{\delta})^{h}+h^{\delta},

while an inner derivation
\delta
is a derivation such that there exists
a\in A
for any
g\in G
,

g^{\delta}=a-a^{g}.

The results developed by Lucchini in
[
10
]
relies on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,M)
, where
M
is a
G
-module.

For an irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module
M
, Schur’s Lemma tells us that
\operatorname{End}(M)
is a division ring containing multiplication scalers. As
\operatorname{End}(M)
is finite, then it is a field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The action of
\operatorname{End}(M)
on
M
induces an action of
\operatorname{End}(M)
on the group of derivations
\mathrm{Der}(G,M)
. Therefore, we can view
M
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,M)
as vector spaces over
\operatorname{End}(M)
. In this context, Lucchini introduces the following parameters:

\displaystyle r_{G}(M)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\operatorname{End}(M)}M

\displaystyle s_{G}(M)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\operatorname{End}(M)}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,M)

\displaystyle h_{G}(M)

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{s_{G}(M)-1}{r_{G}(M)}\right\rfloor+2.

(1)

The first two parameters are the exponents of the orders of
M
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,M)
when expressed as powers of the order of
\operatorname{End}(M)
. When we use these parameters, we will show that for the
G
-modules that we are interested in,
\operatorname{End}(M)
contains only the scalers. Thus
r_{G}(M)
is the dimension of
M
.

Finally, we collect useful results from
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
in the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)

(Lucchini
[
10
, 1.2]
)

Let
M
be an irreducible
G
-module for a finite group
G
.
Then

s_{G}(M)=\delta_{G}(M)+\dim_{\operatorname{End}(M)}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G/\mathrm{C}_{G}(M),M).

(ii)

(Lucchini
[
10
, 1.3]
)

Let
M
be an irreducible
G
-module. Then

\dim_{\operatorname{End}(M)}H^{1}(G/C_{G}(M),M)<r_{G}(M).

(iii)

(Stammbach
[
15
, Theorem A]
)

Given a prime integer
p
, a finite group
G
is
p
-soluble if and only if
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G/C_{G}(M),M)=0
for all irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules
M
.

(iv)

(Lucchini
[
10
, Proposition 1]
)

Let
H
be a finite group and
G
be a transitive permutation group of degree
n
, then

\displaystyle d(I_{H\operatorname{wr}G})=\max\left(d(I_{(H/H^{\prime})\operatorname{wr}G}),\left\lfloor\frac{d(I_{H})-2}{n}\right\rfloor+2\right).

(v)

(Lucchini
[
10
, Theorem 2]
)

If
H
is a finite soluble group and
G
is a transitive permutation group of degree
n
, then

d(H\operatorname{wr}G)=\max\left(d\left(\frac{H}{H^{\prime}}\operatorname{wr}G\right),\left\lfloor\frac{d(H)-2}{n}\right\rfloor+2\right).

(vi)

(Lucchini
[
10
, Theorem 4]
)

Let
A\operatorname{wr}G
be the wreath product of a finite abelian group
A
by a finite group
G
constructed via the regular action of
G
. Set

\rho_{p}=\max_{M}h_{G}(M)+d_{p}(A)

where
M
ranges over the set of non-trivial irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules, with
\rho_{p}=0
if every irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules is trivial. Then

d(A\operatorname{wr}G)=\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}}(d(A\times G),\rho_{p}).

In this paper, we are interested in the case that
G
is an almost simple group with socle
S
. Then in the third part of the lemma above, recall that
G
is
p
-soluble if and only if
p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
.

3
Wreath products of an abelian group by an almost simple group of Lie type

In Lucchini
[
10
, Theorem 4]
(see Lemma
2.4
(vi)
), a formula is given for
d(A\operatorname{wr}G)
, where
A
is a finite abelian group and the wreath product is constructed via the regular action of
G
. We will use this result to investigate the case when
G
is an almost simple group of Lie type in this section. The formula relies on the maximum of
h_{G}(M)
among non-trivial
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules for a fixed prime
p
. By Equation (
1
) and Lemma
2.4
(i)
, we can use
\delta_{G}(M)
to determine this maximum. We will devote the first half of this section to
\delta_{G}(M)
. Establishing this, we will then give the formula for
d(A\operatorname{wr}G)
.

Throughout this section, let
S
be a simple group of Lie type and
G
be an almost simple group with socle
S
. We denote by
\operatorname{Out}(S)
the outer automorphism group of
S
, and by
\operatorname{Outdiag}(S)
the outer diagonal automorphism group of
S
.

Consider the normal series

\mathbf{1}<S\leqslant G,

and refine it to the following chief series:

\mathbf{1}=Y_{0}<Y_{1}=S<Y_{2}<\dots<Y_{n}=G.

The chief factors of
G
isomorphic to
G
-modules necessarily occurs as chief factors of
G/S
. As we are only interested in abelian chief factors of
G
, then in the following lemma, in order to establish
\delta_{G}(M)
, we will focus on chief factors of
G/S
. Note that the group
G/S
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Out}(S)
, and Theorem 2.5.12(b) in
[
5
]
shows that

\operatorname{Out}(S)=\operatorname{Outdiag}(S)\rtimes(\Phi_{S}\Gamma_{S}).

(2)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type, and let
G
be an almost simple group with socle
S
. Suppose that
M
is a non-trivial
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module for some prime
p
. Then

\delta_{G}(M)\leqslant 1.

Furthermore, in each of the following three cases,
\delta_{G}(M)=0
:

(i)

S=D_{4}(q)
for some prime power
q
and
p\neq 2,3
;

(ii)

S=D_{2m}(q)
, for some
m\geqslant 3
and some prime power
q
;

(iii)

S\neq D_{2m}(q)
for any
m,q
and
p
does not divide
\mathopen{|}\operatorname{Outdiag}(S)\cap(G/S)\mathclose{|}
.

Proof:
.

In order to prove
\delta_{G}(M)\leqslant 1
, it suffices to show that these chief factors are pairwise not
G
-isomorphic. In the following, we will split into three cases according to
S
:
S=D_{4}(q)
for some prime power
q
,
S=D_{2m}(q)
for some
m\geqslant 3
and prime power
q
and
S\neq D_{2m}(q)
for any
m
and
q
.

Suppose that
S=D_{4}(q)
. Then, according to
[
8
, Chapter 2.6]
,
\operatorname{Out}(S)=E\times F
, where
F
is cyclic and
E
is isomorpic to a subgroup of
S_{4}
. Suppose that
G/S
is nilpotent, so
[G/S,G/S]\leqslant\Phi(G/S)
. As all non-trivial chief factors must be contained in
[G/S,G/S]
, they are inside
\Phi(G/S)
. However, abelian chief factors inside the Frattini group is not complemented, and therefore,
\delta_{G}(M)=0
. Now assume that
G/S
is not nilpotent, whence
E
is
S_{3}
,
A_{4}
or
S_{4}
. Then
[G/S,G/S]
is a subgroup of
C_{3}
,
C_{2}^{2}
or
S_{3}
. A non-trivial chief factor must have size bigger than
2
, and hence non-trivial chief factors in
[G/S,G/S]
have pairwise different orders. The conclusion follows. Moreover, if
p\neq 2,3
, then
M
is not
G
-isomorphic to non-trivial chief factors in
[G/S,G/S]
.

Now assume that
S=D_{2m}(q)
for some
m\geqslant 3
and some prime power
q
. In this case, using
[
8
, Chapter 2.6]
again,
\operatorname{Out}(S)=E\times F
, where
F
is cyclic and
E
is either abelian or isomorphic to
D_{8}
. Thus,
G/S
is nilpotent. By the argumet above,
\delta_{G}(M)=0
.

Finally, assume that
S\neq D_{2m}(q)
for any
m
and
q
. Theorem 2.5.12 in
[
5
]
tells us that
\Phi_{S}\Gamma_{S}
in Equation (
2
) is abelian. Thus non-trivial abelian chief factors of
G
must be contained in
N=(G/S)\cap\operatorname{Outdiag}(S)
, and
N
is cyclic according to
[
3
, Table 5]
. Furthermore, as
\Phi(N)\leqslant\Phi(G)
and
\Phi(N)
is normal in
G
, non-trivial complemented abelian chief factors of
G
must appear between
\Phi(N)
and
N
. Let
H_{X}=X_{2}/X_{1}
and
H_{Y}=Y_{2}/Y_{1}
be two abelian complemented chief factors of
G/S
such that
\Phi(N)\leqslant X_{1}<X_{2}\leqslant Y_{1}<Y_{2}\leqslant N
. Note that any prime square does not divide
\mathopen{|}N/\Phi(N)\mathclose{|}
. Then
\mathopen{|}H_{1}\mathclose{|}\neq\mathopen{|}H_{2}\mathclose{|}
, whence they are not
G
-isomorphic. Moreover, if
p
does not divide
\mathopen{|}N\mathclose{|}
, then
M
is not isomorphic to abelian chief factors contained in
N
.

\square

Now that we have determined
\delta_{G}(M)
, we will establish
\max_{M}h_{G}(M)
, where
M
ranges over the set of non-trivial irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules. We will use Lemma

(iii)
to obtain
\max_{M}h_{G}(M)
based on whether

is

-soluble.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type and
G
be an almost simple group with socle
S
. Let

be a prime number. Then

\displaystyle\max_{M}h_{G}(M)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}$},\\
2&\text{if $p\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}$}.\end{cases}

Proof:
.

We assume that
p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
. Then
G
is
p
-soluble. Then Lemma
2.4
(iii)
yields that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G/C_{G}(M),M)=0
for all irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules
M
. Hence we deduce from Lemma
2.4
(i)
that
s_{G}(M)=\delta_{G}(M)
. Substituting this into Equation (
1
) gives

h_{G}(M)=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta_{G}(M)-1}{r_{G}(M)}\right\rfloor+2.

As
p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
, then
p\neq 2,3
when
S=D_{4}(q)
according to the order of
D_{4}(q)
given in
[
3
, Table 6]
. On the other hand, when
S\neq D_{2m}(q)
, It follows from Theorem 2.5.12(c) in
[
5
]
that
p\nmid\mathopen{|}\operatorname{Outdiag}(S)\mathclose{|}
when
p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
. Lemma
3.1
then yields that
\delta_{G}(M)=0
for all non-trivial irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules when
p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
. Then
\max_{M}h_{G}(M)=1
.

Now assume that
p\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
. Lemma
3.1
gives
\delta_{G}(M)\leqslant 1
for all non-trivial irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules. We obtain from Lemma
2.4
(i)
and
2.4
(ii)
that

\displaystyle s_{G}(M)

\displaystyle=\delta_{G}(M)+\dim_{\operatorname{End}(M)}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G/\mathrm{C}_{G}(M),M)

\displaystyle<1+r_{G}(M).

Substituting this into Equation (
1
) gives

h_{G}(M)=\left\lfloor\frac{s_{G}(M)-1}{r_{G}(M)}\right\rfloor+2<3.

On the other hand, as
p\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
, then
G
is not
p
-soluble and there is a non-trivial
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module
M_{0}
such that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G/\mathrm{C}_{G}(M_{0}),M_{0})
is not trivial by Lemma
2.4
(iii)
. Thus we deduce that
\dim_{\operatorname{End}(M_{0})}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G/\mathrm{C}_{G}(M_{0}),M_{0})\geqslant 1
and hence
s_{G}(M_{0})\geqslant\delta_{G}(M_{0})+1
by Lemma
2.4
(i)
. Replacing
s_{G}(M_{0})
in Equation (
1
) for
M_{0}
with this inequality obtains that

h_{G}(M_{0})\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{\delta_{G}(M_{0})}{r_{G}(M_{0})}\right\rfloor+2\geqslant 2.

We then conclude that
\max h_{G}(M)=2
for all non-trivial irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules
M
.

\square

Now let
S
be a non-abelian simple group of Lie type, let
G
be any almost simple group with socle
S
and let
A
be a finite abelian group. Let
W=A\operatorname{wr}G
be the wreath product of the abelian group
A
by the almost simple group
G
constructed via the regular action. We will use Lemma
2.4
(vi)
to establish
d(W)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type and
G
be an almost simple group with socle
S
. Let
A
be a finite abelian group and set
W=A\operatorname{wr}G
to be the wreath product of the abelian group by the almost simple group with respect to the regular action. Then

d(W)=\max_{q}(d(A\times G),d(A)+1,d_{q}(A)+2),

where
q
ranges over the set of the prime numbers dividing
\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
.

Proof:
.

Using Lemma
2.4
(vi)
, it suffices to show that

\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}}\rho_{p}=\max_{q}(d(A)+1,d_{q}(A)+2),

(3)

where
q
ranges over the set of the prime numbers dividing
\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
and

\rho_{p}=\max_{M}h_{G}(M)+d_{p}(A).

Lemma
3.2
tells us that
\rho_{p}\geqslant 1+d_{p}(A)
for all
p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
. Hence
\rho_{p}\geqslant 1+d(A)
. Lemma
3.2
also shows that, for any prime number
p
dividing
\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
,
\rho_{p}=2+d_{p}(A)
. Thus

\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}}\rho_{p}\geqslant\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|},p\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}}\rho_{p}=\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|},p\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}}2+d_{p}(A).

Then

\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}}\rho_{p}\geqslant\max_{q}(d(A)+1,d_{q}(A)+2).

On the other hand, for prime numbers
p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
p\nmid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that
d(A)+1\geqslant\rho_{p}
. When
p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
p\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
, we deduce from Lemma
3.2
that

\max_{q\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|},q\mid\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}}d_{q}(A)+2\geqslant\rho_{p}.

We then obtain that

\max_{p\mid\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}}\rho_{p}\leqslant\max_{q}(d(A)+1,d_{q}(A)+2).

We conclude that Equation (
3
) holds.

\square

4
Proof of main theorem

We will now establish the main theorem. Accordingly, let
k\geqslant 2
, and let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{k}
be almost simple groups. Define

W=G_{k}\operatorname{wr}G_{k-1}\operatorname{wr}\dots\operatorname{wr}G_{1}

to be the wreath product constructed via the regular action of each factor. We also define

H=G_{k}\operatorname{wr}G_{k-1}\operatorname{wr}\dots\operatorname{wr}G_{2}

and set
A=H/H^{\prime}
to be the abelianization of
H
; that is,

A=(G_{k}/G_{k}^{\prime})\times(G_{k-1}/G_{k-1}^{\prime})\times\dots\times(G_{2}/G_{2}^{\prime}).

Combining Lemma
3.3
with results in
[
10
]
establishes
d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S
be the socle of
G_{1}
. The minimum number of generators of the wreath product
A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}
is given by

d(A\operatorname{wr}G)=\max_{q}(d(A\times G),d(A)+1,d_{q}(A)+2),

where
q
ranges over the set of prime numbers dividing
\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
and
\mathopen{|}S\mathclose{|}
.

Proof:
.

The case when
S
is a simple group of Lie type is given in Lemma
3.3
. If
S
is an alternating group, then
\operatorname{Out}(S)
is abelian. According to
[
3
, Table 1]
,
\operatorname{Out}(S)
is also abelian when
S
is one of the sporadic groups. It follows that the abelian chief factors of
G
are all trivial in the case that
S
is an alternating simple group or one of the sporadic simple groups. Then for any non-trivial
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module
M
,
\delta_{G}(M)=0
. In this case, the formula is a direct consequence of
[
10
, Corollary 5]
.

\square

The key step of the main theorem is the following lemma, which reduces
d(W)
to
d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
W
and
A
be as defined above. Then

d(W)=d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}).

(4)

Proof:
.

We first observe that
A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}
is a homomorphic image of
W=H\operatorname{wr}G_{1}
, and so
d(W)\geqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
. We will prove the reverse inequality in the rest of the proof.

Set
L=G_{k-1}\operatorname{wr}G_{k-2}\operatorname{wr}\dots\operatorname{wr}G_{1}
acting on
\Gamma=G_{k-1}\times\dots\times G_{1}
such that
W=G_{k}\operatorname{wr}_{\Gamma}L
. Denote the socle of
G_{k}
by
F
. As the subgroup
N=F^{\Gamma}
of the base group
G_{k}^{\Gamma}
is the unique minimal normal subgroup of
W
, then Theorem
2.1
yields

d(W)=d(W/N)=d((G_{k}/F)\operatorname{wr}L).

It suffices to prove that
d((G_{k}/F)\operatorname{wr}L)\leqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
, and we will use induction in the following.

Suppose that
k=2
, and then
W/N=(G_{2}/F)\operatorname{wr}G_{1}
. Since
G_{2}/F
is soluble, Lemma
2.4
(v)
yields that

\displaystyle d((G_{2}/F)\operatorname{wr}G_{1})

\displaystyle=\max\left(d(((G_{2}/F)/(G_{2}/F)^{\prime})\operatorname{wr}G_{1}),\left\lfloor\frac{d(G_{2}/F)-2}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}),\left\lfloor\frac{d(G_{2}/F)-2}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\right).

As
2\leqslant d(G_{1}),d(G_{2})\leqslant 3
by
[
4
, Corollary]
, then

\left\lfloor\frac{d(G_{2}/F)-2}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\leqslant\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\leqslant d(G_{1})\leqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}).

(5)

We therefore establish that
d((G_{2}/F)\operatorname{wr}G_{1})=d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
.

Now assume that
k>2
and that the lemma holds for any iterated wreath product of almost simple groups with fewer than
k
factors. We shall prove the lemma by splitting into two cases according to the presentation rank of
\bar{W}=(G_{k}/F)\operatorname{wr}L
.

Suppose first that
\operatorname{pr}(\bar{W})=0
. Set
\bar{H}=(G_{k}/F)\operatorname{wr}G_{k-1}\operatorname{wr}\dots\operatorname{wr}G_{2}
such that
\bar{W}=\bar{H}\operatorname{wr}G_{1}
, and observe that
\bar{H}/\bar{H}^{\prime}\cong A
. Then Lemma
2.2
gives
d(\bar{W})=d(I_{\bar{W}})
. Substituting this into Lemma
2.4
(iv)
yields

\displaystyle d(\bar{W})

\displaystyle=\max\left(d(I_{A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}}),\left\lfloor\frac{d(I_{\bar{H}})-2}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left(d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}),\left\lfloor\frac{d(\bar{H})-2}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\right).

(6)

Set
B=G_{k}/G_{k}^{\prime}\times\dots\times G_{3}/G_{3}^{\prime}
so that
d(H)=d(B\operatorname{wr}G_{2})
by the induction hypothesis. Using the fact that
d(G_{2})\leqslant 3
from
[
11
, Corollary]
, we deduce that

d(\bar{H})\leqslant d(H)\leqslant d(B)+d(G_{2})\leqslant d(A)+3.

Lemma
4.1
gives that
d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})\geqslant d(A)+1
, and so

\left\lfloor\frac{d(\bar{H})-2}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\leqslant\left\lfloor\frac{d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})}{\mathopen{|}G_{1}\mathclose{|}}\right\rfloor+2\leqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1}).

Then
d(\bar{W})\leqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
by Equation (
6
), and hence we have established Equation (
4
).

Suppose now that
\operatorname{pr}(\bar{W})>0
. As
\bar{W}=(G_{k}/F)\operatorname{wr}L
and
G_{k}/F
is soluble, then it follows from Lemma
2.3
that
d(\bar{W})=d(L)
. Set
C=(G_{k-1}/G_{k-1}^{\prime})\times\dots\times(G_{2}/G_{2}^{\prime})
. Then according to the induction hypothesis,
d(L)\leqslant d(C\operatorname{wr}G_{1})\leqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
. We conclude that
d(\bar{W})\leqslant d(A\operatorname{wr}G_{1})
regardless of
\operatorname{pr}(\bar{W})
and the proof is completed.

\square

Combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
gives the main theorem of this paper.

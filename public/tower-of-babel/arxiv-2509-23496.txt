Crossing probabilities in geometric inhomogeneous random graphs
Emmanuel Jacob, Céline Kerriou, Amitai Linker and Peter Mörters

Abstract




Abstract:
In a geometric inhomogeneous random graph vertices are given by the points of a Poisson process and are equipped with independent weights following a heavy tailed distribution. Any pair of distinct vertices is independently forming an edge with a probability decaying as a function of the product of the weights divided by the distance of the vertices. For this continuum percolation model we study the crossing probabilities of annuli, i.e. the probabilities that there exist paths starting inside a ball and ending outside a larger concentric ball with increasing inner and outer radii.
Depending on the radii,
the power-law exponent of the degree distribution and the decay of the probability of long edges, we identify regimes where the crossing probabilities by a path are equivalent to the crossing probabilities by one or by two edges.
We also identify the escape probabilities from balls with strong centre, i.e. the asymptotics of the probability that there exists a path starting from a vertex with a given weight leaving a centred ball as radius and weight are going to infinity.
As a corollary we get the subcritical one-arm exponents characterising the decay of the probability that a typical point is in a component not contained in a centred ball whose radius goes to infinity.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Motivation

In general continuum percolation models, vertices are given by the points of a homogeneous Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

and edges are formed randomly according to some translation invariant probability law leading to finite expected vertex degrees.
Phase transitions in these models express themselves in a sudden qualitatively change of the
graph topology when the intensity of the underlying point process crosses a positive and finite critical value. This change of behaviour arises not only in the dichotomy of existence of an infinite, dense connected component for supercritical densities versus only finite components for subcritical densities, but also in various other forms - such as the existence or absence of a path comprising many different edges, or the existence or absence of a path
crossing an annulus of large width. The latter is of particular interest because of its local and quantitative nature. If the crossing probability of an annulus with fixed ratio of inner and outer radius goes to zero as the width goes to infinity, then this fact can be used to initiate renormalization arguments, directly showing many features characterising subcritical behaviour - such as finite expected component size and exponential decay of the probability that there is a path of length
n
starting at a typical point, see Gouéré and Théret
[
Gou08
,
GT19
]
. We hence speak
of the
quantitatively subcritical phase
of the percolation model.

In the classical continuum percolation model of Gilbert
[
Gil61
]
vertices are connected by an edge if their distance lies below a fixed threshold value. In this model all phase transitions occur at the same critical intensity and the transitions are sharp. In particular,
for subcritical densities the probability that there is a path from a point inside to a point outside of an annulus decays exponentially in the width of the annulus, while
for supercritical densities the number of vertex-disjoint paths crossing the annulus grows at least like a power of the width
[
Tan93
,
FH25
]
, see
[
AV23
]
for related analytic results. At criticality, crossing probabilities in two-dimensional percolation have rich relations to conformal field theory and stochastic Loewner evolution and have been central to progress in percolation theory in the last twenty years, see for example
[
Car92
,
Sch01
]
for lattice models and
[
MM12
]
for nonrigorous results on continuum percolation.

For continuum percolation models with sufficiently heavy-tailed edge length distribution or with a power-law degree distribution one cannot expect exponential decay of the annulus crossing probabilities for subcritical densities. In the former case
with only polynomially decaying probability a single edge can cross the annulus and in the latter case a single strong vertex inside the annulus can be adjacent to an edge connecting it to the
outside, or vice versa. Therefore crossings using only one edge give polynomial lower bounds for the subcritical crossing probabilities. In
[
DCRT20
]
Duminil-Copin et al study the Poisson-Boolean model, a percolation model which has
power-law degree distributions.
Under a moment assumption they show that for subcritical densities, the crossing probability of an annulus with fixed ratio of inner and outer radius is asymptotically equivalent to the probability that a single edge crosses the annulus. See also the recent work of Dembin and Tassion
[
DT22
]
, where the moment condition is relaxed.

In this paper we consider a different long-range percolation model often called the
geometric inhomogeneous random graph
. In this model, vertices are placed according to a homogeneous Poisson point process in space and each vertex carries an independent weight. Given vertices and weights, edges are created independently between vertices with a probability given as a function
depending on the product of the weights of the adjacent vertices and their distance in space. Choosing heavy-tailed weight distributions and a
slowly decreasing profile leads to a power-law degree distribution and heavy-tailed edge length distribution.
We study this model in the quantitatively subcritical phase.

Recent work of Jacob et al
[
JJL25
]
gives criteria for existence and absence of a quantitatively subcritical phase. It turns out that when the probability that an annulus with proportional inner and outer radius is crossed by a single edge does not go to zero, this is not enough to guarantee the presence of an infinite component. In this case there is a subcritical, but no quantitatively subcritical phase. Conversely, if there is a quantitatively subcritical phase, the probability that such annuli are crossed by a single edge goes to zero with a rate that does not depend on the intensity of the underlying Poisson process. In this case the natural conjecture that the subcritical and quantitatively subcritical phase agree is still an open problem, which we do not address here.

Here we focus on the quantitatively subcritical phase and give
up-to-constants matching bounds for the crossing probabilities
of annuli with outer radius growing faster than the inner radius. These up-to-constants bounds are of polynomial order in the radii and do not depend on the intensity of the underlying Poisson process
as long as it is quantitatively subcritical.
In particular we show that, if the tail of the long edge probability is heavy relative to the tail of the degree distribution, the order of the crossing probability is that of the crossing by a single edge, but if it is light, then
it depends on the
rate of divergence of the inner radius compared to the outer radius whether we see the one-edge or two-edge asymptotics. Moreover, depending on the power-law exponent of the degree distributions the order of the crossing probabilities may or may not depend on the tail of the long edge probabilities. We also obtain up to constants bounds for the escape probabilities from balls with a strong centre,
i.e. the probability that there exists a path from a vertex to the outside of a ball around that vertex as the weight of the centre and the radius of the ball go to infinity. As before we see different parameter regimes in which paths with one or two edges determine the escape probabilities. Finally, as a corollary of our main results, we get the subcritical one-arm exponents, which describe the polynomial rate at which the probability that a typical point is in a component not contained in a centred ball goes to zero as the radius goes to infinity. Again, these exponents do not depend on the underlying intensity as long as it is quantitatively subcritical.

1.2.
Background and description of the model

We consider the
geometric inhomogeneous random graph

[
BKL19
]
, also known as weight-dependent random connection model
[
GLM21
,
GHMM22
]
or kernel-based spatial random graph
[
JKM24
,
JKM25
]
with factor kernel, or as continuum scale-free percolation model
[
DvdHH13
,
DW19
]
in the literature.
In this model, vertices are given by a homogeneous Poisson point process
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1]
with intensity
\lambda>0
, which represents the density of vertices in the model. We interpret a point
\mathbf{x}=(x,u)\in\mathcal{P}
of this Poisson point process as having position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and mark
u
uniform in
(0,1]
. Whenever we want to emphasize the role of
\lambda
we denote the underlying probability measure by
\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}
.

The random graph
\mathscr{G}=(\mathcal{P},E)
has vertex set
\mathcal{P}
and, given the vertex set, the edge set
E
is defined such that for every pair of vertices
(x,u),(y,v)
we sample the edge
((x,u),(y,v))
, denoted by
(x,u)\sim(y,v)
, independently with probability

\displaystyle\rho(|y-x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma}),

where
0\leq\gamma<1

is a parameter regulating the inhomogeneity of the model and
\rho\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is a non-increasing
profile function
satisfying

\displaystyle 0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(|x|^{d})\,dx<\infty.

In this paper, we consider both hard and soft profile functions. More precisely, in the hard model we choose
\rho
of the form
\rho=1_{[0,r_{0}]}
, for some positive constant
r_{0}
, in which case an edge
(x,u)\sim(y,v)
exists if and only if

|y-x|^{d}\leq r_{0}u^{-\gamma}v^{-\gamma}.

In the soft model, we choose
\rho
such that there are constants
0<{\mathtt{c}},{\mathtt{C}}<\infty
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\mathtt{c}}x^{-\delta}\leq\rho(x)\leq{\mathtt{C}}x^{-\delta}\quad\text{ for all }x\geq 1,

for a
decay exponent

\delta>1
.
We characterise the hard model by formally setting the decay exponent to
\delta=\infty
. In the case
\gamma=0
we formally set
1/\gamma=\infty
and only consider the soft model, which then becomes the continuum long-range percolation or random connection model
[
Pen91
,
BM93
]
. Although our results are also new in this case, our main focus in this paper is on the case
\gamma>0
, where the model is inhomogeneous with a degree distribution of power-law type with exponent
\tau=1+\frac{1}{\gamma}
.
An alternative interpretation of the very same model is that every vertex
(x,u)
gets an independent Pareto-distributed weight
W_{x}=u^{-\gamma}
and vertices in location
x,y
are connected with probability

\rho(\!\!

\frac{|x-y|^{d}}{W_{x}W_{y}}

\!\!)
.

We write
B(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
to denote the open ball of radius
r>0
centred at
x
and
B(x,r)^{{\sf c}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)
to denote its complement.
Given two disjoint sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we write

A\longleftrightarrow B
if there exists a path with endpoints in
A
, resp.
B
.
Here and throughout the paper paths are assumed to be self-avoiding.
Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
we write

A^{{}_{\,\overset{k-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B,

if there is such a path with at most
k
edges. In particular,
B(x,r)^{{}_{\overset{1-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(x,r^{\prime})^{\sf c}
means that exists an edge with endpoints in
B(x,r)
and
B(x,r^{\prime})^{{\sf c}}
.
Using this notation we define the probabilities of crossing an annulus,

\displaystyle\theta_{r}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{r}(\lambda)=\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}\big(B(0,r)\longleftrightarrow B(0,2r)^{\sf c}\big),

\displaystyle\pi_{r}

\displaystyle:=\pi_{r}(\lambda)=\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}\big(B(0,r)^{{}_{\overset{1-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(0,2r)^{\sf c}\big),

for
r>0
. For notational simplicity, we will write the dependence in
\lambda
only when necessary. Next, we define the critical parameter

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{c}:=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0,\lim_{r\to\infty}\theta_{r}(\lambda)=0\}.

The regime
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
is the
strongly
or
quantitatively subcritical phase
when, loosely speaking, looking at large scale (i.e. a ball with radius of order
r
) it is unlikely that there exists a component of macroscopic size (i.e. a component of diameter larger than
r
) in this ball.
Of course,
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
is
no larger
than the classical critical parameter

\displaystyle\lambda_{c}:=\sup\big\{\lambda\geq 0:\,\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}\big(B(0,1)\longleftrightarrow B(0,r)^{\sf c}\big)=0\big\}.

In
[
JJL25
, Section 2]
the authors consider a general model that contains our model when considering the same parameters
\delta
and
\gamma
as we do, and
\alpha=\gamma
as their third parameter. We now summarize their results in our context. They show in Theorem 2.1 that the behaviour of
\theta
is governed by an exponent
\zeta
that we get from their Proposition 2.3:

(1)

If
\delta>2
and
\gamma<1/2
, then

\displaystyle\zeta=\max\left\{2-\delta,2-\frac{1}{\gamma}\right\}<0
.

(2)

If
\delta<2
or
\gamma>1/2
, then

\displaystyle\zeta=\max\left\{2-\delta,\frac{2\gamma-1}{2\gamma-1/\delta}\right\}>0.

(3)

Otherwise, namely if
\delta=2
and
\gamma\leq 1/2
, or
\gamma=1/2
and
\delta\geq 2
, then
\zeta=0
.

In the case
\zeta>0
, we have
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}=0
and there is no quantitatively subcritical phase. We will show in Lemma
3.1
that in our model this also applies if

\zeta=0
, i.e. when

\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
or
\delta=2
. In the case
\zeta<0
, we have
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}>0
, still from their Theorem 2.1, as well as

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\theta_{r}}{\log r}=d\zeta,\quad\text{ for all }0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}.

By carefully looking at their proof, we can see that the limsup is a true limit, and thus

\theta_{r}=r^{d\zeta(1+o(1))}\text{ as }r\to\infty,\quad\text{ for all }0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}.

In this paper, we are interested in the quantitatively subcritical phase, so we always assume that
\delta>2
and
\gamma<1/2
in order to have
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}>0
. Moreover, we will mainly study the regime
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
. In this context, our aim is to provide more precise bounds on the crossing probabilities, not only of an annulus with outer and inner radius of a fixed ratio, as in the definition of
\theta_{r}
, but also of annuli where the ratio between outer and inner radius goes to infinity. For such annuli we ask whether crossings by one, two or perhaps a larger number of edges are still asymptotically equally probable as crossings with arbitrarily long paths.

Notation

We reserve the word
domain
for Borel subsets

(1.2)

\displaystyle D\subseteq\mathcal{D}:=\{(u,r,R)\in(0,1]\times[2,\infty)\times[4,\infty)\colon R\geq 2r\}.

Domains are characterised by stating the additional restrictions on
(u,r,R)\in\mathcal{D}
.
If there are no additional restrictions on a variable or a variable is not used in the given context we may omit it from the notation, writing for example
(r,R)\in\{r>R^{\epsilon}\}
if

(r,R)\in[2,\infty)\times[4,\infty)
satisfy
R/2\geq r>R^{\epsilon}
and the value of
u\in(0,1]
plays no role. We define
D^{{\sf c}}
to be
\mathcal{D}\setminus D
.
Given two functions
f,g\colon D\to\mathbb{R}
defined on a domain
D
, we write

\displaystyle f(u,r,R)

\displaystyle\lesssim g(u,r,R)\text{ on }D,

if there exist
C,R_{0}>1
such that
f(u,r,R)\leq Cg(u,r,R)
for all
(u,r,R)\in D
with
R\geq R_{0}
. We write

\displaystyle f(u,r,R)

\displaystyle\asymp g(u,r,R)\text{ on }D,

if both
f(u,r,R)\lesssim g(u,r,R)
and
g(u,r,R)\lesssim f(u,r,R)
on
D
. If no domain
D
is mentioned the equivalence is supposed to hold on all of
\mathcal{D}
.

1.3.
Main results

Recall that we assume that
\delta>2
and
\gamma<1/2
, so that there exists a quantitatively subcritical phase, namely
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}>0
. In this context, as mentioned above, for any
\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
we have the crossing probability decay

\theta_{r}=r^{d\zeta(1+o(1))}\text{ as }r\to\infty,

with
\zeta
defined as in Section
1.2
. In
[
JJL25
]
, this result is obtained as a consequence of a similar result for the one-edge crossing probability
\pi_{r}
. By a slight refinement of their argument, we obtain the following result, which we prove in Section
2.3
.

Proposition 1.1
.

In the quantitatively subcritical phase
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
, we have

\theta_{r}\asymp\pi_{r}.

Remark 1.2
.

Instead of
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
the proof only uses the (potentially weaker) assumption that
\theta_{r}(\lambda)\to 0
as
r\nearrow\infty
. Under this assumption the precise bounds for
\pi_{r}
given in Section
3

give the up-to-constants asymptotics of
\theta_{r}
. The sharpness of the phase transition arises from the fact that for
\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
the crossing probability
\theta_{r}
decays polynomially with a rate not depending on
\lambda
, whereas for
\lambda>\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
it remains of order one.

Our main interest is to consider thicker annuli, so we generalize the definition of
\theta_{r}
to

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}:=\mathbb{P}(B(0,r)\longleftrightarrow\hbox{}B(0,R)^{\sf c}),

and that of
\pi_{r}
to

\displaystyle\pi_{r,R}:=\mathbb{P}(B(0,r)^{{}_{\overset{1-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(0,R)^{\sf c}),

for any
R\geq r>0
. Note that
\pi_{{r}}=\pi_{r,2r}
.
We also define the two-edge crossing probability, that is the probability of having a crossing path with at most two edges, by

\displaystyle\pi^{(2)}_{r,R}:=\mathbb{P}(B(0,r)^{{}_{\overset{2-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\hbox{}B(0,R)^{\sf c}).

While, for all
\lambda>0
, we have

(1.3)

\pi_{r,2r}^{(2)}\asymp\pi_{r,2r},

the analogous statement for thicker annuli does not necessarily hold if the inner radius
r
is of smaller order than the outer radius
R
. Bounding
\theta_{r,R}
therefore involves the study of the two-edge crossing events. Further, we also state bounds for the probability of having a path from one fixed point to the outside of a ball of radius
R
.
Given a point
\mathbf{x}=(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1]
, we define the rooted graph
(\mathscr{G},\mathbf{x})
by first constructing
\mathscr{G}
, and then, if
\mathbf{x}\notin\mathscr{G}
, adding
\mathbf{x}
to the vertex set and sampling its adjacent edges as usual. Given a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
\mathbf{x}\longleftrightarrow A
if there exists a finite-length path in
(\mathscr{G},\mathbf{x})
from the root
\mathbf{x}
to a vertex in
A\times(0,1]
.
We write

\mathbf{x}^{{}_{\,\overset{k-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}A,

if there is such a path with at most
k
edges.

For
u\in(0,1]
we define the
escape probabilities
from a ball with radius
R>0
as

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}((0,u)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}),

\displaystyle\pi_{(u),R}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}((0,u)^{{}_{\overset{1-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(0,R)^{\sf c}),

\displaystyle\pi^{(2)}_{(u),R}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}((0,u)^{{}_{\overset{2-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(0,R)^{\sf c}).

The idea behind these definitions is twofold: On the one hand, by averaging
\theta_{(u),R}
over a uniform random mark
u

we get the escape probabilities from a ball centred in a typical point. On the other hand, by evaluating
\theta_{(u),R}
for small deterministic values of
u
we check the effect of a strong vertex planted at the origin on the connectivity at scale
R
. This will be the essential tool to get lower bounds on the subcritical one-arm exponents in Theorem
4
.

The following result, which is our main theorem,
shows that in the quantitatively subcritical phase
two
edges
cross wide annuli or escape from balls with a strong centre with the same asymptotic probability as arbitrary paths. Recall that
\delta>2,\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
are necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a quantitatively subcritical phase.

Theorem 1
.

For the geometric inhomogeneous random graph with
0\leq\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
and either a hard profile and
\gamma\not=0
or a soft profile with
\delta>2
and with intensity
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
, we have for all
\epsilon>0
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}

\displaystyle\asymp\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\{r\geq R^{\epsilon}\},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}

\displaystyle\asymp\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\{u\leq R^{-\epsilon}\}.

Remark 1.3
.

In the quantitatively subcritical phase, two-edge paths asymptotically yield the same crossing probabilities as paths with an arbitrary number of edges, irrespective of the relative growth rates of the inner and outer radii or the weight of the central vertex. In this phase the crossing probabilities
\theta_{r,R}
decay polynomially with an order not depending on
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
, whereas in the supercritical phase
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
the
\theta_{r,R}
are all of order one.

Remark 1.4
.

Recall that Proposition
1.1
states that in the quantitatively subcritical phase we have
\theta_{r,2r}\asymp\pi_{r,2r}
, which already gives our result for that particular choice of
R
, since a trivial inclusion of events implies
\pi_{r,R}\leq\pi_{{}_{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}\leq\theta_{r,R}
,
thus giving
\theta_{r,2r}\asymp\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{{}^{r,2r}}
. Conversely, we can recover Proposition
1.1
from our result by using (
1.3
).

We find explicit up-to-constant bounds for the one and two-edge crossing probabilities, which combined with Theorem
1
gives a full picture showing when either
one or two edges suffice to cross wide annuli with the same asymptotic probability as arbitrary paths.

Theorem 2

(Annulus crossing probabilities)
.

For
0\leq\gamma<\frac{1}{2}

and

0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c},
we have, for
all
0<\epsilon<1
, that:

(1)

If
2<\delta<1/\gamma-1
, then

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}

\displaystyle\asymp\pi_{r,R}\asymp r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}\qquad\text{ on }\{r\geq R^{\epsilon}\}.

(2)

If
1/\gamma-1<\delta<1/\gamma
, then

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}

\displaystyle\asymp\begin{cases}\pi_{r,R}\asymp r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}&\text{ on }\{r\geq R^{1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\},\\
\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\asymp r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})}&\text{ on }\{R^{\epsilon}\leq r\leq R^{1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\}.\\
\end{cases}

(3)

If
\delta>1/\gamma
or if
\delta=\infty
, then

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}

\displaystyle\asymp\begin{cases}\pi_{r,R}\asymp r^{d}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\log R&\text{ on }\{r\geq R(\log R)^{\frac{-1}{d(1-\gamma)}}\},\\
\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\asymp r^{d\gamma}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\gamma)}&\text{ on }\{R^{\epsilon}\leq r\leq R(\log R)^{\frac{-1}{d(1-\gamma)}}\}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.5
.

The probability of crossing the annulus by an arbitrary path is of the same order as crossing it with one edge as soon as the annulus is not too thick for this strategy. If
\delta<{1}/{\gamma}
the crossover of strategies occurs
at
r\asymp R^{1+\delta-{1}/{\gamma}}
. Hence there is no crossover in regime
(1)
when
the profile has the heaviest tail and one edge always crosses with the same order of probability as an arbitrary path. The intermediate regime
(2)
however splits into two parts depending on the
thickness of the annulus. If it is too thick two edges cross with the same order as an arbitrary path, but one does not. In the light tail regime
(3)
,
which includes the hard profile, the crossover occurs for much thinner annuli, at
r\asymp R(\log R)^{{-1}/{d(1-\gamma)}}
. Then the order of the annulus crossing probability does not depend on the profile tail index
\delta
.
We always assume that the inner radius grows at least
polynomially with the outer radius.

Remark 1.6
.

For the asymptotic crossing probabilities in the boundary cases
\delta\gamma=1-\gamma
and
\delta\gamma=1
see Section
3
, more precisely Corollary
3.4
.

Theorem 3

(Escape probabilities from balls with strong centre)
.

For
0\leq\gamma<\frac{1}{2}

and

0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c},
we have, for all
\epsilon>0
, that

(1)

If
2<\delta<1/\gamma-1
, then

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}\asymp\pi_{(u),R}\asymp\begin{cases}u^{-\gamma\delta}R^{d(1-\delta)}&\text{ on }\{R^{d(\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\leq u\leq R^{-\epsilon}\},\\
1&\text{ on }\{u\leq R^{d(\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\}.\\
\end{cases}

(2)

If
1/\gamma-1<\delta<1/\gamma
, then

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}\asymp\begin{cases}\pi^{(2)}_{(u),R}\asymp u^{-\gamma}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})}\phantom{IIJ}\text{ on }\{R^{-\frac{d(1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma})}{\gamma\delta}}\leq u\leq R^{-\epsilon}\},\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\pi_{(u),R}\asymp\begin{cases}u^{-\gamma\delta}R^{d(1-\delta)}\phantom{XX}&\text{ on }\{R^{d(\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\leq u\leq\smash{R^{-\frac{d(1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma})}{\gamma\delta}}}\},\\

(3)

If

or if

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}\asymp\begin{cases}\pi^{(2)}_{(u),R}\asymp u^{-\gamma}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\gamma)}\phantom{XJ}\text{ on }\{R^{-d}\leq u\leq R^{-\epsilon}\},\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\pi_{(u),R}\asymp\begin{cases}u^{-1}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\phantom{XX}&\text{ on }\{R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\leq u\leq R^{-d}\},\\
1&\text{ on }\{u\leq R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\}.\\
\end{cases}\end{cases}

Remark 1.7
.

As in Remark
1.6
the boundary cases
\delta\gamma=1-\gamma
and
\delta\gamma=1
are detailed in Corollary
3.4

of Section
3
.

Although in Theorem
3
we plant unusually strong vertices at the origin, the result enables us to give lower bounds on the probability that the connected component of a typical point has a radius larger than
R
about its centre. To make sense of the notion of a typical point we use the Palm theory of marked Poisson processes, which allows us to pick a typical vertex from the graph and shift it to the origin.

Theorem 4

(Subcritical one-arm exponents)
.

For
0\leq\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
and

0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c},
as
R\nearrow\infty
,

\frac{1}{d\log R}\log\mathbb{P}^{*}((0,U)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c})\longrightarrow\begin{cases}{1-\delta}&\text{if $2<\delta<1/\gamma-1$,}\\
(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})&\text{if $1/\gamma-1\leq\delta<1/\gamma$,}\\
(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\gamma)&\text{if $\delta\geq 1/\gamma$ or $\delta=\infty$,}\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{P}^{*}
is the Palm measure at the origin associated with the marked Poisson point process on which our graph is defined, and
(0,U)
is the vertex located at the origin under
\mathbb{P}^{*}
.

Remark 1.8
.

Equivalent ways to define
\mathbb{P}^{*}
are (i) sample a standard uniform
U
and add the point
(0,U)
to the vertex set, (ii) pick a point at random from all points located in
B(0,n)
, shift it to the origin and take a limit of the probability as
n\to\infty
, (iii) condition the Poisson process on having a point located at the origin. To do so, condition the Poisson process on having a point
with location in
B(0,\varepsilon)
, and let
\varepsilon
go to 0. For details see
[
LP17a
]
.

Remark 1.9
.

The event that the connected component of a typical point has a radius larger than
R
about its centre has polynomially decaying probability and the rate does not depend on
\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
.
It is easy to see that the same result also holds for the diameter of a typical component. The result also holds under the (potentially weaker) assumption
\theta_{R}(\lambda)\to 0
and therefore
under this assumption the one-arm exponents extend to
\lambda=\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
.

Remark 1.10
.

In fact, we prove a sharper lower bound since

\mathbb{P}^{*}((0,U)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c})\geq\mathbb{P}^{*}((0,U)\overset{2-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}B(0,R)^{\sf c})\asymp\pi_{(1),R}^{(2)},

with asymptotics provided in Table
1
. We also conjecture that Theorem
1
holds for
\epsilon=0
, in which case we would get

\mathbb{P}^{*}((0,U)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c})\asymp\pi_{{}^{(1),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}.

Remark 1.11
.

Jahnel et al.
[
JLO24
]
identify the one-arm exponents for the soft Boolean model for sufficiently small
\lambda>0
. In a comparable parametrisation they obtain

\frac{1}{d\log R}\log\mathbb{P}^{*}((0,U)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c})\longrightarrow\begin{cases}{1-\delta}&\text{if $1<\delta\leq 1/\gamma-1$,}\\
(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})&\text{if $1/(\gamma\wedge(1-\gamma))-1<\delta$.}\\

Interestingly, the exponents agree for

but for

Their method of proof also differs considerably from ours.

Preliminary considerations

2.1.
Continuity and monotonicity of crossing probabilities

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
R>0
the map
(0,R)\to(0,\infty),r\mapsto\theta_{r,R}
is strictly increasing and the map

(0,1]\to(0,\infty),u\mapsto\theta_{(u),R}
is strictly decreasing, the maps
\{0<r<R\}\to(0,\infty),(r,R)\mapsto\theta_{r,R}
and
(u,R)\mapsto\theta_{(u),R}
are continuous.

Proof.

For
r<r^{\prime}<R
the event
\{B(0,r)\leftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\}
implies

\{B(0,r^{\prime})\leftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\}
giving monotonicity. It is strict as with positive probability the latter holds while
B(0,r)\cap\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
. Similarly, under a straightforward coupling
for
u<u^{\prime}
the event
\{(0,u^{\prime})\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\}
implies

\{(0,u)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\}
. The monotonicity is strict, as with positive probability there is exactly one edge adjacent to the origin for
u
but none for
u^{\prime}
.
Observe that for
r^{\prime}\leq r\leq r^{\prime\prime}\leq R^{\prime\prime}\leq R\leq R^{\prime}
the event
\{B(0,r^{\prime\prime})\leftrightarrow B(0,R^{\prime\prime})^{\sf c}\}\setminus\{B(0,r^{\prime})\leftrightarrow B(0,R^{\prime})^{\sf c}\}
implies that there is a vertex in

B(0,r^{\prime\prime})\setminus B(0,r^{\prime})
or in
B(0,R^{\prime})\setminus B(0,R^{\prime\prime})
. The probability of this event goes to zero as
r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}\to r
and
R^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}\to R
, and hence
0\leq\theta_{r^{\prime\prime},\,R^{\prime\prime}}-\theta_{r^{\prime},\,R^{\prime}}\to 0
. This implies continuity as
\theta_{r^{\prime},R^{\prime}}\leq\theta_{r,R}\leq\theta_{r^{\prime\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}.
Continuity of
\theta_{(u),R}
is proved following similar steps, using a straightforward coupling for the graphs with different values of
u
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

All statements of the lemma also hold for
\pi_{r,R}
and
\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{{}^{r,R}}
in place of
\theta_{r,R}
, and
\pi_{(u),R}
and
\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{{}^{(u),R}}
in place of
\theta_{(u),R}
.

2.2.
Scaling finite-crossing events

Many of the events we consider involve only a finite number of vertices and edges in the graph.
In geometric inhomogeneous random graphs the asymptotic order of probability of such events is typically not changed if we modify the intensity
\lambda
of the Poisson point process, or the scale at which the event is considered.

Definition 2.3
.

An event
E
is called a
finite-crossing event
for a graph
\mathscr{G}
with vertex set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]
if there exists
k\geq 1
, and a Borel subset
A\subset(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1])^{k}

such that

E=\big\{\exists(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{k})\in V^{k}\cap A,\forall 1\leq i\leq k-1,\,\mathbf{x}_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{i+1}\text{ or }\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\sim\mathbf{x}_{i+1}\big\}

We also use this definition for rooted graphs

(\mathscr{G},\mathbf{x}_{0})
adding the requirement
\mathbf{x}_{0}\sim\mathbf{x}_{1}

to the definition of
E
. Given
k
and
A
we say
E
is the finite-crossing event associated with
(k,A)
.

The set

A
is called
downward-closed
if, for all
((x_{1},u_{1}),\ldots,(x_{k},u_{k}))\in A
and
u_{1}^{\prime}\leq u_{1},u_{2}^{\prime}\leq u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}^{\prime}\leq u_{k}
it follows that
((x_{1},u_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,(x_{k},u_{k}^{\prime}))\in A
. The event
E
is called downward-closed if
A
is.

Note that the definition of finite-crossing events is broad enough to include events of the form
A^{{}_{\,\overset{k-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B
and
(x,u)^{{}_{\,\overset{k-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}A
, the latter using the notion for the graph with root
(x,u)
, and that both events are downward-closed. However, the events
A\longleftrightarrow B
and
(x,u)\longleftrightarrow A
are not finite-crossing events, since these consider paths of arbitrary length. The important feature of this definition is that a finite-crossing event
E
associated with

(k,A)
depends only on
k
vertices and at most
k-1
edges observed between these vertices, thus allowing us to control the impact of rescaling
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Let
A\subset(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1])^{k}
be a Borel set as before, and
c>0
. We define
cA
as

cA:=\{(cx,u)\colon(x,u)\in A\},

that is, the set defined by rescaling by
c
the location of all the points in
A
. If
E
is the finite-crossing event associated with
(k,A)
and
c>0
, we write
cE
to denote the finite-crossing event associated with

(k,cA)
. In the special case where
E
is defined on
(\mathscr{G},(x,u))
,
cE
is defined on
(\mathscr{G},(cx,u))
, that is, we also rescale the location of the root.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
and let
E
be a finite-crossing event associated with some
(k,A)
. Then, for any decay exponent
1<\delta\leq\infty
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle p^{k}\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{(p\lambda)}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}(E),

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\tfrac{\mathtt{c}}{\mathtt{C}}p^{d\delta})^{k-1}\mathbb{P}^{(p^{-d}\lambda)}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}(p^{-1}E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{(p^{-d}\lambda)}(E),

where the left-hand side in (
2.2
) uses
\mathtt{c},\mathtt{C}
from (
1.1
) for the soft profile and is set equal to zero for the hard profile. If
E
is downward closed and
\gamma>0
, we also have

(2.3)

\mathbb{P}^{(p^{d(1/\gamma-1)}\lambda)}(E)\leq\mathbb{P}^{(\lambda)}(p^{-1}E).

Proof.

We first give the complete proof for the case where
E
is an event on the unrooted graph
\mathscr{G}
, and then describe the necessary modifications to extend it to
(\mathscr{G},(x_{0},u_{0}))
. As we will consider several values of
\lambda
, we write
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
for the graph obtained for a fixed value of
\lambda
. The inequalities in (
2.1
) follow from the observation that the graph
\mathscr{G}_{p\lambda}
can be obtained by first constructing the graph
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
, and then performing independent Bernoulli percolation with retention parameter
p
on its vertices. In this construction, if
E
holds on
\mathscr{G}_{p\lambda}
, then it must also hold on
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
. Conversely, any sequence of vertices satisfying
E
in
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
is also present in
\mathscr{G}_{p\lambda}
with probability at least
p^{k}
.

For the proof of (
2.2
)
we define a coupling of
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
and
\mathscr{G}_{p^{-d}\lambda}
. Fix a realization of
\mathscr{G}_{p^{-d}\lambda}
and define an intermediate graph
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
by moving each vertex
(x,u)\in\mathscr{G}_{p^{-d}\lambda}
to a new location
(p^{-1}x,u)
, while preserving all the edges. Construct
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
by performing independent edge percolation on
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
, with each edge
\{(x,u),(y,v)\}
being retained with probability

\frac{\rho\left(p^{-d}|y-x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma}\right)}{\rho\left(|y-x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma}\right)}\geq\frac{\mathtt{c}}{\mathtt{C}}p^{d\delta},

where the inequality follows from (
1.1
) in the case of a soft profile. It can be readily checked that this alternative construction of
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
gives the same law as the original one. From this construction, we deduce that
E
occurs in
\mathscr{G}_{p^{-d}\lambda}
if and only if
p^{-1}E
occurs in
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
, and the result then follows from the same argument as in the proof of (
2.1
).

The proof of (
2.3
) follows, again, from a coupling of
\mathscr{G}_{p^{d(1/\gamma-1)}\lambda}
and
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
. The construction is a bit more involved than the one used for (
2.2
).

(1)

Fix a realization of
\mathscr{G}_{p^{d(1/\gamma-1)}\lambda}
and define an intermediate graph
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
by moving each vertex
(x,u)\in\mathscr{G}_{p^{d(1/\gamma-1)}\lambda}
to
(p^{-1}x,p^{d/\gamma}u)
, while preserving all the edges.

(2)

For every pair of vertices
\{(p^{-1}x,p^{d/\gamma}u),(p^{-1}y,p^{d/\gamma}v)\}
in
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
not initially adjacent, we resample the edge with probability

\frac{\rho\left(p^{d}|y-x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma}\right)-\rho\left(|y-x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma}\right)}{1-\rho\left(|y-x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma}\right)}.

This probability is nonnegative by our assumptions on
\rho
.

(3)

Finally, we construct
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
by adding to the vertex set of
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
the points of a Poisson point process
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[p^{d/\gamma},1)
with intensity
\lambda
, and sampling edges between vertices in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and those of
\widetilde{\mathscr{G}}
as before.

It can be verified that this construction yields a sample of
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
. Suppose that
E
is a downward closed finite-crossing event, and that
(x_{1},u_{1}),\ldots,(x_{k},u_{k})
is a sequence of vertices in
\mathscr{G}_{p^{d(1/\gamma-1)}\lambda}
satisfying
E
. Then the sequence
(p^{-1}x_{1},p^{d/\gamma}u_{1}),\ldots,(p^{-1}x_{k},p^{d/\gamma}u_{k})
obtained after Step (1)
satisfies
p^{-1}E
in
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
, so from this coupling we conclude (
2.3
).

The proof of the lemma for events
E
defined on
(\mathscr{G},(x_{0},u_{0}))
is analogous; however, special treatment is needed for the root. In the proof of (
2.2
), the root is always kept during site percolation. In the proof of (
2.1
), we keep its adjacent edges. In the proof of (
2.3
), the root is relocated to
(p^{-1}x_{0},u_{0})
instead of
(p^{-1}x_{0},p^{d/\gamma}u_{0})
, and no edges are added adjacent to it in Step
(2)
. With these considerations, the proof carries over analogously.
∎

The next lemma states that linear rescaling of the inner or outer radii does not affect the order of crossing and escape probabilities.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0\leq\gamma<1
and
1<\delta\leq\infty
and assume
\gamma=0
and
\delta=\infty

(2.5)

\displaystyle c(a)\pi^{(2)}_{(u),R}\leq

\displaystyle\pi^{(2)}_{(u),aR}\leq C(a)\pi^{(2)}_{(u),R}.

Moreover, for every

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle c(a,b,\varepsilon)\pi_{r,R}\leq

\displaystyle\pi_{ar,bR}\leq C(a,b,\varepsilon)\pi_{r,R},

(2.7)

\displaystyle c(a,b,\varepsilon)\pi^{(2)}_{r,R}\leq

\displaystyle\pi^{(2)}_{ar,bR}\leq C(a,b,\varepsilon)\pi^{(2)}_{r,R}.

Combining (

) and (

2.3.
Proof of Proposition
1.1

The proof of Proposition
1.1
is essentially hidden in the findings of
[
JJL25
]
.
In
[
JJL25
, Definition 1.4]
, the authors introduce the event

\mathcal{L}(r,c):=\big\{\exists\ \mathbf{x}\sim\mathbf{y}\colon|x|<r,|x-y|>cr\big\},

and prove in (15) that

\theta_{r}\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(2r,1/20))+C_{1}\theta_{r/10}^{2},

for some constant
C_{1}
depending only on the dimension, and in particular independent of
r
. This
renormalization inequality is at the heart of the definition and study of the quantitatively subcritical phase
\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
. Indeed, if
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(2r,1/20))
tends to 0 as
r
tends to infinity, a property which does not depend on the value of
\lambda>0
by (
2.1
), and if
\theta_{r}
is sufficiently small for some value of
r
, then
\theta_{r}
will automatically decay to 0 with decay being controlled by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(2r,1/20))
.
More precisely,
[
JJL25
]
shows that in the case
\zeta<0
, for any given
\alpha>0
and
c>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(\alpha r,c))=r^{d\zeta(1+o(1))}.

Then they use the renormalization inequality to show first that
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}>0
, and moreover that for any
\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
the term
\theta_{r}
must also decay as
r^{d\zeta(1+o(1))}.

In particular, the term
C_{1}\theta_{r/10}^{2}
is negligible, and we obtain the upper bound

\theta_{r}\lesssim\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(2r,1/20)).

It is elementary that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(r,c))\asymp\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(r^{\prime},c^{\prime}))
if
cr=c^{\prime}r^{\prime}
. Indeed, in the two events we look for edges of same length in balls of slightly different sizes and we can include the smaller ball in the larger ball and the larger in a fixed number of smaller balls. We can thus continue this upper bound with

\theta_{r}\lesssim\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(r/30,3))\leq\pi_{r/30}.

where the last inequality follows by inclusion of events. By (
2.8
), we also have

\pi_{r/30}\asymp\pi_{r}
and combining this with the elementary inequality
\pi_{r}\leq\theta_{r}
, we deduce
\theta_{r}\asymp\pi_{r}\asymp\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(r,1))
.

3.1.
Statement of the one and two-edge crossing probabilities

We first argue that if

Lemma 3.1
.

If

.

From now on we assume that
\delta>2
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
such that a quantitatively subcritical phase exists. The following proposition summarizes the main results of this section.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

3.2.
Proof of the one and two-edge crossing probability bounds.

In this section we prove the results stated in Section
3.1

except for Lemma
3.6
, which will be
proved in Section
3.3
.
The probability
\mathbb{P}_{(x,u)}
and expectation
\mathbb{E}_{(x,u)}
refer to the vertex set
\mathcal{P}
with the point
(x,u)
added. We include the case
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
and
\delta=2
until further notice.

Bounds on
\pi_{(u),R}

These bounds are the most straightforward.
Under
\mathbb{P}_{(0,u)}

we write
M_{R}
for the number of connections of the origin to vertices in
B(0,R)^{\sf c}
. We then have
\pi_{(u),R}=\mathbb{P}_{(0,u)}(M_{R}>0).
Note that by construction,
M_{R}
is Poisson distributed; therefore
\mathbb{P}_{(0,u)}(M_{R}>0)\asymp\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]\wedge 1
and hence it is enough to compute the latter. By the construction of the graph and Campbell’s formula
[
LP17b
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]=\lambda\int_{B(0,R)^{\sf c}}\int_{0}^{1}\rho(|x|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x.

In the case
\gamma=0
(which excludes the case
\delta=\infty
) we obtain

(3.7)

\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]=\lambda\int_{B(0,R)^{\sf c}}\rho(|x|^{d})\mathrm{d}x\asymp R^{d(1-\delta)},

while in the case
\gamma>0
we can use use the change of variables
|x|=z
and
w=uvz^{d/\gamma}
to obtain

\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]\asymp\int_{R}^{\infty}u^{-1}z^{-1+d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\int_{0}^{uz^{d/\gamma}}\rho(w^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}z.

To study this integral, suppose first that
u\leq R^{-d/\gamma}
and observe that since
\rho\asymp 1
on
(0,1]
,
we have

\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]\gtrsim\int_{R}^{u^{-\gamma/d}}u^{-1}z^{-1+d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\int_{0}^{uz^{d/\gamma}}\rho(w^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}z\asymp\int_{R}^{u^{-\gamma/d}}z^{d-1}\mathrm{d}z\gtrsim 1,

hence
\pi_{(u),R}\asymp 1
. Suppose next that
u\geq R^{-d/\gamma}
, so that for any
z\geq R
,

\int_{0}^{uz^{d/\gamma}}\rho(w^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}w\asymp\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\gamma\delta>1,\\
1+\log(uz^{d\delta})&\text{ if }\gamma\delta=1,\\
(uz^{d/\gamma})^{1-\gamma\delta}&\text{ if }\gamma\delta<1.\end{cases}

Note that this is also valid for the case
\delta=\infty
. Replacing this expression into our previous computation of
\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]
yields

(3.8)

\mathbb{E}_{(0,u)}[M_{R}]\wedge 1\asymp\begin{cases}u^{-1}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\wedge 1&\text{ if }\gamma\delta>1,\\
u^{-1}R^{d(1-\delta)}(1+\log_{+}(uR^{d\delta}))\wedge 1&\text{ if }\gamma\delta=1,\\
u^{-\gamma\delta}R^{d(1-\delta)}\wedge 1&\text{ if }\gamma\delta<1.\end{cases}

Observe that when
\gamma=0
, equation (
3.8
) reduces to (
3.7
). Setting
u=1
in (
3.8
) yields the asymptotic behaviour of
g(R)
, and replacing this expression in (
3.8
) leads to (
3.1
). These computations are straightforward, except for a minor issue in the case
\gamma\delta=1
, the function
g(R)
includes a factor of
\log(R)
, while the corresponding factor in (
3.8
) is
1+\log_{+}(uR^{d\delta})
. This discrepancy is irrelevant when
u\leq R^{d(1-\delta)}
, since in that regime we have
\pi_{(u),R}\asymp 1
. On the other hand, if
u\geq R^{d(1-\delta)}
, then
R^{d}\leq uR^{d\delta}\leq R^{d\delta}
, so we indeed obtain
1+\log_{+}(uR^{d\delta})\asymp\log(R)
, as required.

Bounds on
\pi_{r,(x,u)}

To obtain bounds for
\pi_{r,(x,u)}
we follow the same program as the one in the previous section, by writing
M_{R}^{\prime}
for the number of connections of
(x,u)
to vertices in
B(0,r)
, so that
\mathbb{P}_{(x,u)}(M_{R}^{\prime}>0)\asymp\mathbb{E}_{(x,u)}[M_{R}^{\prime}]\wedge 1
. Using again Campbell’s formula we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(x,u)}[M_{R}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{B(0,r)}\int_{0}^{1}\rho(|x-y|^{d}(uv)^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{B(0,r)}\int_{0}^{1}\rho(R^{d}(uv)^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}y\asymp r^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\rho(R^{d}(uv)^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}v,

where we have used that
2r\leq R
on any domain (as defined in (
1.2
)), so any vertex
(y,v)\in B(0,r)\times[0,1]
satisfies
|x-y|\asymp R
. The remaining calculations are analogous to the ones given in the previous section, so we omit the details.

Bounds on
\pi_{r,R}

For given
r
and
R\geq 2r,
we observe that any direct connection from
B(0,r)
to
B(0,R)^{\sf c}
is of length at least
R/2
. Therefore, we can bound
\pi_{r,R}
by the expected number of vertices in
B(0,r)
incident to such a long edge, namely

\pi_{r,R}\leq{\mathrm{Vol}}(B(0,r))\int_{0}^{1}\pi_{(u),R/2}\,\mathrm{d}u\asymp\begin{cases}r^{d}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\log R&\text{ if }\gamma\delta>1,\\
r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}(\log R)^{2}&\text{ if }\gamma\delta=1,\\
r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}&\text{ if }\gamma\delta<1.\end{cases}

To obtain matching lower bounds in the case
0\leq\gamma\delta<1
we work conditionally on the locations and weights of all vertices in
B(0,r)
and in
B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)
. With probability of order one, we find order
r^{d}
such vertices in the first set, and order
R^{d}
such vertices in the second set. Now each of the
r^{d}R^{d}
possible connections hold independently with probability of order
R^{-d\delta}
, even in the worst case scenario where the vertices have weights one and are at distance
2R+r
apart, so indeed we can find a connection with probability of order
r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}
.
This analysis covers the particular case where
\delta=2
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, giving a lower bound of order
(r/R)^{d}
which is of order
1
on
\{R=2r\}
, thus proving Lemma
3.1
.

In the case
1<\gamma\delta\leq\infty
we bound
\pi_{r,R}
from below by the probability of the event where the strongest vertex in
B(0,r)
is connected to the strongest vertex in
B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)
. Call
U
and
V
their respective marks, and observe that they are connected, conditionally on
U
and
V
with probability of order
(UV)^{-\gamma\delta}R^{-d\delta}\wedge 1
which is of order
1
as soon as
UV<R^{-d/\gamma}
. To obtain a lower bound for the probability of this latter event, let
N_{r}
and
N_{R}
denote the number of vertices in
B(0,r)
and
B(0,R)^{{\sf c}}
, respectively. Since these variables are Poisson distributed with mean
\mathbb{E}N_{r}\asymp r^{d}
and
\mathbb{E}N_{R}\asymp R^{d}
, with probability of order one we have
cr^{d}<N_{r}<Cr^{d}
and
cR^{d}<N_{R}<CR^{d}
for some constants
c
and
C
. Assume that this event holds, and further restrict the condition
UV<R^{-d/\gamma}
to

\{r^{d}R^{-d/\gamma}\leq V\leq R^{-d},\,R^{-d/\gamma}\leq U\leq R^{-d/\gamma}/V\}.

It can be checked that from the bounds of
N_{r}
and
N_{R}
, on this set
U
and
V
have density of order
r^{d}R^{d}
, so we arrive at

\mathbb{P}(UV<R^{-d/\gamma})\gtrsim\int_{r^{d}R^{-d/\gamma}}^{R^{-d}}\int_{R^{-d/\gamma}}^{R^{-d/\gamma}/v}r^{d}R^{d}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\asymp r^{d}R^{d(1-1/\gamma)}\log(R^{1/\gamma-1}/r),

which matches the upper bound in the case
\gamma\delta>1
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, since
\log(R^{1/\gamma-1}/r)\asymp\log R
. Observe that if
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
this expression becomes
(r/R)^{d}\log(R/r)
, which is of constant order on
\{R=2r\}
, thus proving Lemma
3.1
in the case
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
.

In the case
\gamma\delta=1
we still consider the strongest vertices in
B(0,r)
and in
B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)
, which are connected with conditional probability of order
(UV)^{-1}R^{-d\delta}
, however, working on the event
\{UV<R^{-2d}\}
does not give a matching lower bound anymore. Instead, we compute
\mathbb{E}[(UV)^{-1}R^{-d\delta}]
which, when restricted to the event

\{R^{-d(\frac{\delta-1}{2})}r^{-d/2}\leq U\leq r^{-d}\}\cap\{R^{-d(\frac{\delta+1}{2})}r^{d/2}\leq V\leq R^{-d}\},

gives a lower bound of order

R^{-d\delta}r^{d}R^{d}\log(R^{\delta-1}/r)^{2}.

Since the case
\delta=2
was already treated, we can assume that
\delta>2
, so the bound becomes of order
r^{d}R^{-d(\delta-1)}(\log R)^{2}
, thus matching the upper bound.

Note that this analysis not only completes the proof of (
3.2
), but also of Lemma

, which shows that for
\delta=2
or
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
there is no subcritical quantitative phase. We will henceforth assume that
\delta>2
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.

on
\{u\leq(\log R)^{-\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}}\}
so on this domain we obtain a matching lower bound by simply neglecting the term
\mathbb{P}_{(0,u)}(N^{\prime}_{R}>0)
in (
3.9
). To obtain a matching lower bound on
\{u\geq(\log R)^{-\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}}\}
we abbreviate
z_{0}=(u^{-\gamma\delta}R^{d(\delta-1)})^{\frac{1}{d\delta}}/2
and define the set

A_{(u),R}:=\{(x,v):R^{\frac{1}{2\delta}}\leq|x|\leq z_{0},\text{ and }\,R^{\frac{d(1-\delta)}{\gamma\delta}}\leq v\leq u^{-1}|x|^{-\frac{d}{\gamma}}\}.

Observe that Vol
(A_{(u),R})\asymp u^{-1}R^{-d/2}
. As
u\geq(\log R)^{-\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}}
we get that
u^{-1}R^{-d/2}\lesssim 1
, and hence
\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{P}\cap A_{(u),R}|=1)\asymp\text{Vol}(A_{(u),R})
. We work on this event and denote by
(X,V)
the unique vertex in
A_{(u),R}
, whose location is uniformly distributed within this set. Conditional on the location of
(X,V)
, we have

\pi_{(V),2R}\asymp V^{-\gamma\delta}R^{d(1-\delta)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}((0,u)\sim(X,V))\asymp 1.

Since
-\gamma\delta=\gamma-1
, the unconditional probability of
N^{\prime}_{R}>0
is then lower bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(N_{R}^{\prime}>0\big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\big(|\mathcal{P}\cap A_{(u),R}|=1\big)\iint_{A_{(u),R}}\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(A_{(u),R})}\mathbb{P}((0,u)\sim(x,v))\pi_{(v),2R}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\asymp\iint_{A_{(u),R}}v^{\gamma-1}R^{d(1-\delta)}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x\asymp u^{-\gamma}R^{d(1-\delta)}\log R,

as wanted.

Bounds on
\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}

These bounds are relatively similar to the bounds of
\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}
, so we treat them more briefly. We provide asymptotics available on the domain
\{R\geq 8r\}
, while we use Lemma
2.5
to generalize the result to the whole
\mathcal{D}
.
In order to have a two-edge connection from
B(0,r)
to
B(0,R)^{\sf c}
, we must either have a direct connection from
B(0,2r)
to
B(0,R/2)^{\sf c}
, or we can find an intermediate vertex
x\in B(0,R/2)\setminus B(0,2r)
, connected via a direct edge to both
B(0,r)
and
B(0,R)^{\sf c}
. Writing
N_{R}
for the number of such intermediate vertices, we then have

\displaystyle\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\leq\pi_{2r,R/2}+\mathbb{P}(N_{R}>0)\lesssim\pi_{r,R}+\mathbb{E}[N_{R}],

using Lemma
2.5
and
R\geq 8r
to obtain the second line. Besides, we have

(3.13)

\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\geq\pi_{r,R}+\mathbb{P}(N^{\prime}_{R}>0),

where now

N^{\prime}_{R}=\#\{\mathbf{x}=(x,v)\in\mathcal{P},\exists\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{P},|z|<r,|x|<R,|y-x|\geq 2R,\mathbf{z}\sim\mathbf{x}\sim\mathbf{y}\}.

Using Mecke’s formula and observing that the connection to
B(0,R)^{\sf c}
must be of length at least
R/2
and of course different from the connection to
B(0,r)
, we have

\mathbb{E}[N_{R}]\asymp\lambda\int_{B(0,R/2)\setminus B(0,2r)}\int_{0}^{1}\pi_{r,(x,v)}\pi_{(v),R/2}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x.

The computation of this integral is similar to the one from the previous subsection, but requires considering additional cases, so we leave the details to the reader. We obtain here that

(3.14)

\mathbb{E}[N_{R}]\asymp\begin{cases}r^{d\gamma}R^{d(1-\gamma)(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}&\text{ if }\gamma\delta>1,\\
r^{d\gamma}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})}(\log R)^{1-\gamma}(\log r)^{\gamma}&\text{ if }\gamma\delta=1,\\
r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})}&\mbox{ if }1>\gamma\delta>1-\gamma,\\
r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}R^{d(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})(1-\frac{1}{\delta})}\log(R/r)&\mbox{ if }\gamma\delta=1-\gamma,\\
r^{d(\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma})}R^{d(1-\delta)}&\mbox{ if }1-\gamma>\gamma\delta>1/2,\\
r^{d(2-\delta)}R^{d(1-\delta)}\log r&\mbox{ if }\gamma\delta=1/2,\\
r^{d(2-\delta)}R^{d(1-\delta)}&\mbox{ if }1/2>\gamma\delta.\end{cases}

From this computation, we conclude that
\pi_{r,R}+\mathbb{E}[N_{R}]
coincides with the expression in (
3.4
) whenever
\delta\geq 1/\gamma-1
. In the borderline case
\delta=1/\gamma-1
, the claimed upper bound follows by bounding the
\log(R/r)
term in
\mathbb{E}[N_{R}]
by
\log R
. Finally, when
\delta<1/\gamma-1
, the bound follows from the fact that the expressions obtained for
\mathbb{E}[N_{R}]
are of smaller order than
\pi_{r,R}\asymp r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}
.

We now turn to the lower bounds for
\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}
, starting with the observation that in the case
\delta<1/\gamma-1
the two-edge and the one-edge crossing probabilities coincide, so the lower bounds are obtained by simply ignoring the term
\mathbb{P}(N^{\prime}_{R}>0)
in (
3.13
). When
\delta>1/\gamma-1
, we obtain matching lower bounds by following an analogous approach to that in the previous section, namely, by constructing a set
A_{r,R}
such that any vertex placed within it is simultaneously connected to
B(0,r)
and to
B(0,R)^{{\sf c}}
with probability of order one. Depending on the relation between
\gamma
and
\delta
, the set
A_{r,R}
is defined as

\begin{cases}B(0,r^{\gamma}R^{1-\gamma})\setminus B(0,r)\times(0,R^{d(1-1/\gamma)})&\text{on }\{r\leq R(\log R)^{-\frac{1}{d(1-\gamma)}}\}\\
&\text{if }\delta>\frac{1}{\gamma},\\
B\big(0,r^{\gamma}R^{1-\gamma}(\frac{\log r}{\log R})^{\gamma/d}\big)\setminus B(0,r)\times(0,R^{d(1-\delta)}\log R)&\text{on }\{r\leq R(\log R)^{-\frac{1}{d(1-\gamma)}}\}\\
&\text{if }\delta=\frac{1}{\gamma},\\
\left(B\big(0,r^{\frac{1}{\delta}}R^{1-\frac{1}{\delta}}\big)\setminus B(0,r)\right)\times\big(0,R^{d(\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\big)&\text{on }\{r\leq R^{1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\}\\
&\text{if }\frac{1}{\gamma}-1<\delta<\frac{1}{\gamma}.\end{cases}

Finally, the case
\delta=1/\gamma-1
only needs to be analyzed on the domain
\{r^{d}\leq(\log R)^{\frac{\delta}{\delta-1}}\}
, using the same approach as in the previous section for this specific case. This time we consider the set

A_{r,R}:=\{(x,v):r\leq|x|\leq(\tfrac{r}{R})^{\frac{1}{\delta}}R,\text{ and }\,R^{d(1-\delta)}\leq v^{\gamma\delta}\leq r^{d}|x|^{-d\delta}\},

and observe that Vol
(A_{r,R})\asymp r^{d(\gamma\delta-\delta+1)/(\gamma\delta)}
. Since
\gamma=\frac{1}{\delta+1}
in this case, it follows that the exponent is negative, and hence
r^{d(\gamma\delta-\delta+1)/(\gamma\delta)}\lesssim 1
, so
\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{P}\cap A_{r,R}|=1)\asymp\text{Vol}(A_{r,R})
. As in the previous section, we denote by
(X,V)
the unique vertex in
A_{r,R}
, whose location is uniformly distributed within this set. Conditional on
(X,V)
, we have

\pi_{(V),2R}\asymp V^{-\gamma\delta}R^{d(1-\delta)}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{r,(X,V)}\asymp 1,

so the unconditional probability of
N^{\prime}_{R}>0
is then lower bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(N_{R}^{\prime}>0\big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\big(|\mathcal{P}\cap A_{r,R}|=1\big)\iint_{A_{(u),R}}\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(A_{r,R})}\pi_{r,(x,v)}\pi_{(v),2R}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\asymp\iint_{A_{r,R}}v^{\gamma-1}R^{d(1-\delta)}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x\asymp r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}R^{d(1-\delta)}\log R,

thus giving the desired lower bound.

3.3.
Proof of Lemma
3.6

We begin the proof of (
3.5
) by recalling from (
3.3
) that
\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{{}^{(u),R}}\asymp\pi_{(u),R}+(u^{-\gamma}h(R)\wedge 1)
. Looking at the expressions (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) and their computation already made in the bound of
\pi_{{}^{(u),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
, we deduce both

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\big((0,u)\sim(x,v)\big)\,\pi_{(v),R}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\asymp u^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}v^{-\gamma}\,\pi_{(v),R}\,\mathrm{d}v\asymp u^{-\gamma}h(R)

\displaystyle\lesssim\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}\phantom{fhojrtt}\text{ on }\{u^{-\gamma}h(R)\leq 1\},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\big((0,u)\sim(x,v)\big)\,\big(v^{-\gamma}h(R)\wedge 1\big)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\asymp u^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}v^{-\gamma}\,\big(v^{-\gamma}h(R)\wedge 1\big)\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim u^{-\gamma}h(R)\int_{0}^{1}v^{-2\gamma}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}\phantom{fhojrtt}\text{ on }\{u^{-\gamma}h(R)\leq 1\}.

Combining the two bounds completes the proof of (
3.5
). For the proof of (
3.6
) we once again use that
\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{{}^{(u),R}}\asymp\pi_{(u),R}+(u^{-\gamma}h(R)\wedge 1)
and recall from (
3.14
) that

\int_{B(0,R/2)\setminus B(0,2r)}\int_{0}^{1}\pi_{r,(x,v)}\pi_{(v),R}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\asymp\mathbb{E}N_{R}\lesssim\pi_{r,R}^{(2)},

where

N_{R}=\{(x,v)\in\mathcal{P}\colon x\in B(0,R/2)\setminus B(0,2r)\text{ with }B(0,r)\sim(x,v)\sim B(x,R)\}.

It remains to prove that

(3.15)

\int_{B(0,R/2)\setminus B(0,2r)}\int_{0}^{1}\pi_{r,(x,v)}(v^{-\gamma}h(R)\wedge 1)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\pi_{r,R}^{(2)},

which we do by considering different cases for
\gamma
and
\delta
.

\bullet
If
\delta<\frac{1}{\gamma}-1
we have

\pi^{(2)}_{(v),R}\asymp\pi_{(v),R}
and
(
3.6
) is proved.

\bullet
If
\frac{1}{\gamma}-1\leq\delta<\frac{1}{\gamma}
we have

\displaystyle\pi_{r,(x,v)}

\displaystyle\asymp v^{-\gamma\delta}r^{d}|x|^{-d\delta}\wedge 1,\quad\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\asymp\pi_{r,R}+r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}h(R),

so using the change of variables
z=v^{\frac{\gamma}{d}}r^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}x
we can bound the left-hand side of (
3.15
) by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}(v^{-\gamma\delta}r^{d}|x|^{-d\delta}\wedge 1)v^{-\gamma}h(R)\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\asymp r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}h(R)\int_{0}^{1}v^{-2\gamma}\mathrm{d}v\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|z|^{-d\delta}\wedge 1)\mathrm{d}z\asymp r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}h(R).

Since
r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}h(R)\lesssim\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}
, (
3.6
) is proved.

\bullet
If
\delta=\frac{1}{\gamma}
we have

\displaystyle\pi_{r,(x,v)}

\displaystyle\asymp v^{-\gamma\delta}r^{d}|x|^{-d\delta}(1+\log_{+}(v|x|^{d\delta}))\wedge 1,\quad\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\asymp\pi_{r,R}+r^{d\gamma}(\log r)^{\gamma}h(R).

The proof of (
3.6
) is obtained by applying the same change of variables as in the previous case. The upper bound follows from analogous computations and the observation that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|z|^{-d\delta}(1+\log_{+}(r^{d}|z|^{d\delta}))\wedge 1)\mathrm{d}z\asymp(\log r)^{\gamma}.

\bullet
If
\delta>\frac{1}{\gamma}
we have

\displaystyle\pi_{r,(x,v)}

\displaystyle\asymp v^{-1}r^{d}|x|^{-d/\gamma}\wedge 1,\quad\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\asymp\pi_{r,R}+r^{d\gamma}h(R),

and the proof follows by applying the change of variables
z=v^{\frac{\gamma}{d}}r^{-\gamma}x
and performing the same computations from the previous cases, which we omit.

4.
Bootstrapping: The key argument

In this section we provide the key ingredient behind our main result: the upper bounds in Theorem
1
. In Section
4.1
we observe that from Proposition
1.1
we get upper bounds on the annulus crossing and escape probabilities, which imply the bounds in Theorem
1
on a domain where
r
is within a constant multiple of
R
and
u
is small. In Section
4.2
, we present the crucial geometric argument, Proposition
4.7
, which
allows us to iteratively improve upper bounds, such as those in Section
4.1
. In Section
4.3
, we use this as a bootstrap step to recursively enlarge the domain on which Theorem
1
holds. The proof will be completed
in Section
5
when we use the calculations of Section
3
to show that
any domain of the form
\{r\geq R^{\varepsilon}\}
, resp.
\{u\leq R^{-\varepsilon}\}
, is covered by the domains generated in finitely many steps of this procedure.

4.1.
The easy bounds

First, note the trivial lower bounds

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}\geq\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\theta_{r,R}\geq\pi_{r,R}^{(2)},

which hold since restricting paths to have a certain number of edges decreases their probability. These lower bounds hold for all choices of
\delta>2
and
0\leq\gamma<1/2
, directly implying the lower bounds in Theorem
1
. The rest of this section focuses on proving non-trivial upper bounds.

We recall that while the computations of Section
3
were available for all
\lambda>0
, we now assume that we are in the quantitatively subcritical phase, that is
0<\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
. In particular, in this case we can make use of Proposition
1.1
.
Recalling also (
1.3
), this proposition is the statement of (
1.4
) in Theorem
1
, but restricted to the domain
\{\tfrac{R}{2}=r\}
, namely

(4.1)

\theta_{r,R}\asymp\pi_{{}^{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}\text{ on }\{r=\tfrac{R}{2}\}.

Lemma
2.5
allows to extend (
4.1
) to a larger domain, namely

(4.2)

\theta_{r,R}\asymp\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\text{ on }D_{0},

where

(4.3)

D_{0}:=\{\tfrac{R}{32}\leq r\leq\tfrac{R}{2}\}.

To see this, recall that
\theta_{r,R}
and
\pi_{r,R}
are increasing in
r
. Thus, on
D_{0}
, Proposition
1.1
and Lemma
2.5
give

\theta_{r,R}\leq\theta_{R/2,R}\asymp\pi_{R/2,R}\asymp\pi_{R/32,R}\leq\pi_{r,R}\leq\pi_{{}^{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
.
Note that using the same arguments we could extend (
4.1
) to domains of the form
\{cR\leq r\leq\frac{R}{2}\}

with
c\in(0,1/2)
any constant. Here and in what follows
c=1/32
is a convenient but arbitrary choice.

We now turn our attention to
\theta_{(u),R}
, for which there is no result analogous to Proposition
1.1
. The next lemma shows that this proposition can also be used to obtain a version of (
1.5
) on an explicitly defined domain.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have, for all
0<u<1
and
R\geq 4,

(4.4)

\theta_{(u),R}\leq\pi_{(u),R/2}+\theta_{R/2,R}.

Consequently, we have

(4.5)

\theta_{(u),R}\asymp\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}\text{ on }\widetilde{D}_{0},

where the domain
\widetilde{D}_{0}
is defined as

(4.6)

\widetilde{D}_{0}:=\begin{cases}\{u^{-1}\geq R^{d}\log R\}&\text{ if }\delta\geq\frac{1}{\gamma},\\
\{u^{-\gamma\delta}\geq R^{d}\}&\text{ if }\frac{1}{\gamma}>\delta.\end{cases}

Remark 4.2
.

Using the asymptotic expressions given in Proposition
3.2
, it can be
checked that
\widetilde{D}_{0}
is defined precisely such that

\displaystyle\pi_{R}\lesssim\pi_{{}^{(u),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\widetilde{D}_{0}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\pi_{{}^{(u),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}\lesssim\pi_{R}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\widetilde{D}_{0}^{\sf c},

where we recall that
\widetilde{D}_{0}^{\sf c}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{0}
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Rewriting
\{(0,u)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{{\sf c}}\}
in terms of
\{(0,u)^{{}_{\overset{1-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(0,R/2)^{{\sf c}}\}
and its complement, we obtain the inclusion

\displaystyle\{(0,u)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{{\sf c}}\}\subseteq\{(0,u)^{{}_{\overset{1-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}}}B(0,R/2)^{{\sf c}}\}\,\cup\,\{B(0,R/2)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{{\sf c}}\},

which gives the upper bound (

). Using Proposition
1.1
gives
\theta_{(u),R}\lesssim\pi_{(u),R/2}+\pi_{R}
, so (

) follows from the trivial bound
\pi_{(u),R}\leq\pi_{{}^{(u),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
and Remark
4.2
.
∎

Note that in the previous lemma, (

) holds for all
0<u<1
and

, and that by monotonicity,

holds for any

. By combining these inequalities with Proposition

, we conclude this section with the following upper bounds, which hold on all

\theta_{(u),R}\lesssim\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}+\pi_{R}\quad\text{ and }\quad\theta_{r,R}\lesssim\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}+\pi_{R}.

4.2.
The geometric argument

In this section, we use a geometric argument to obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\colon(4,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
R\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{R}
be a map satisfying
\mathcal{E}_{R}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{2R}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}

(4.10)

(4.12)

.

In the previous section we showed that (

4.3.
The bootstrap argument

In Section
4.1
we have shown that

\displaystyle\theta_{r,R}

\displaystyle\asymp\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}\text{ on }D_{0},

\displaystyle\theta_{(u),R}

\displaystyle\asymp\pi_{(u),R}^{(2)}\text{ on }\widetilde{D}_{0},

where
D_{0}
and
\widetilde{D}_{0}
are defined in (
4.2
), respectively (
4.6
). The idea behind the bootstrap argument lies in recursively applying the geometric argument given in Proposition
4.3
to show that these bounds also hold on increasingly larger domains
D_{1},\widetilde{D}_{1}
;
D_{2},\widetilde{D}_{2}
;
\ldots
.
We then show in Lemma
5.1
that, given
\epsilon>0
as in the statement of Theorem
1
, we can choose a large (but finite)

such that
D_{k}
contains
\{r\geq R^{\epsilon}\}
and
\widetilde{D}_{k}
contains
\{u\leq R^{-\epsilon}\}
, proving Theorem
1
. Our argument relies on obtaining bounds for
\theta_{r,R}
and
\theta_{(u),R}
simultaneously.

Note that the bounds obtained for
\theta_{(u),R}
in Proposition
4.3

require choosing the radius
r
appropriately within the range
[2,R/32]
. It is natural to choose a value
s=s(u,R)
such that

\pi_{{}^{s,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
and
\pi_{{}^{(u),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
are comparable. Given that we eventually prove Theorem
1
, this can heuristically be viewed as requiring the event of escaping the ball
B(0,R)
to be as likely when starting a path from a single (possibly strong) vertex
(0,u)
as from a ball
B(0,s(u,R)).
We thus define

(4.28)

s(u,R):=\inf\{s\in[2,R/32],\ \pi_{s,R}^{(2)}\geq\pi^{(2)}_{(u),R}\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=R/32
. Because the infimum

may be attained at boundary points of the interval

we cannot guarantee that
\pi_{{}^{s,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}=\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{{}^{(u),R}}
. However, Lemma
4.9
below shows that the quantities are always comparable and gives the asymptotic value of the function

.
Before stating this lemma, we
introduce the following notation. Recall
\mathcal{D}
from (

, we write

(4.29)

\displaystyle f(u,r,R)\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\lesssim}g(u,r,R)\text{ on }D,

if there exists

such that

and

on

g(u,r,R)\,\smash{\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\lesssim}}\,f(u,r,R)
on
D
.

Note that if

and

then

.

From

\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\lesssim}

follows using the same steps as in the case

\bullet
In the case

Lemma 4.10
.

For any

the following holds,

(4.36)

\displaystyle r_{k}(R)\underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty.

(4.37)

\displaystyle\pi_{{}^{r_{k+1}(R),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}=\pi_{r_{k+1}(R)}\pi_{{}^{r_{k}(R),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}\;\text{ for all large $R$}.

(4.38)

\displaystyle r_{k}(R)\lesssim r_{k}(2R).

Proof.

We begin by showing (
4.36
) by induction. The case

r_{0}(R)=R/32
. Next, suppose that
r_{k}(R)
tends to infinity as

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}}{\pi_{r_{k}(R),R}^{(2)}}=0.

We write out the proof only for the case
\delta>1/\gamma
as the other cases can be shown similarly. The bounds (
3.4
) and (

) on
\pi_{{}^{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
and
\pi_{r,R}
yield

\displaystyle\pi_{r,R}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\asymp r^{d}\log(R)g(R)+r^{d\gamma}h(R),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\pi_{r_{k}(R),R}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\asymp(r_{k}(R))^{d}\log(R)g(R)+(r_{k}(R))^{d\gamma}h(R).

Thus,
\pi_{{}^{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}/\pi_{{}^{r_{k}(R),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}\lesssim(r/r_{k}(R))^{d}+(r/r_{k}(R))^{d\gamma}

r_{k}(R)

4.39
) follows.
Hence, for large

\pi_{{}^{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}<\pi_{r}\pi_{{}^{r_{k}(R),R}}^{{}_{(2)}}.

By definition of

The equality (

once we ensure that

Finally, we show (
4.38

k=0

r_{0}(R)=R/32
. Suppose now that
r_{k}(R)\lesssim r_{k}(2R)
, and let

For such

) considering only the case

\displaystyle\frac{\pi_{{}^{r^{\prime},R}}^{{}_{(2)}}}{\pi_{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\frac{C^{d}(r_{{k+1}}(2R))^{d}\log(R)g(R)+C^{d\gamma}(r_{{k+1}}(2R))^{d\gamma}h(R)}{C^{d(2-\frac{1}{\gamma})}(r_{{k+1}}(2R))^{d(2-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\log(r_{{k+1}}(2R))}

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}c_{2}C^{2d(\frac{1}{\gamma}-1)}\frac{\pi_{{}^{r_{k+1}(2R),2R}}^{{}_{(2)}}}{\pi_{r_{k+1}(2R)}},

where we used

Combining this inequality with the previous one we obtain, for sufficiently large

Proposition 4.11

Let

Proof of the theorems

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1
.

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
1
, we must verify that, for sufficiently large
k
, the domains
D_{k}
and
\widetilde{D}_{k}
contain the desired domains of the theorem. This is established in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
R_{0}\,{>4}
such that

\displaystyle D_{k}\cap\{R>R_{0}\}

\displaystyle\supset\{\tfrac{R}{2}\geq r\geq R^{\epsilon}\}\cap\{R>R_{0}\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{k}\cap\{R>R_{0}\}

\displaystyle\supset\{R^{-\epsilon}\geq u\}\cap\{R>R_{0}\}.

Theorem
1
follows by combining Proposition
4.11
, Lemma
5.1
choosing
k
sufficiently large.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

We show that the sequence
(r_{k}(R))_{k\geq 0}
defined by (
4.35
) satisfies

(5.1)

\displaystyle r_{k}(R)\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}R^{\alpha_{k}},

where
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a sequence of positive reals depending on
\gamma
and
\delta
, that satisfies
\alpha_{0}=1
and
\alpha_{k}\to 0
, and where we recall the notation

\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}
from (
4.29
).

The result for
D_{k}\cap\{R\geq R_{0}\}
then follows from the above as (
5.1
) implies that there exists constants
C>0
and
a\geq 0
such that, for sufficiently large
R
and
k
,

(5.2)

r_{k}(R)\geq CR^{\alpha_{k}}(\log R)^{a}\geq R^{\epsilon},

since
\epsilon>0
is fixed. To obtain the result for
\widetilde{D}_{k}\cap\{R\geq R_{0}\}
, we can deduce

\widetilde{D}_{k}\cap\{R\geq R_{0}\}=\{u\leq u_{k}(R),\,R\geq R_{0}\}

analogously to (
4.43
), where
u_{k}
is defined in (
4.42
), and satisfies
s(u_{k}(R),R)=r_{k}(R)
for
R
large. The result then follows from Lemma
4.9
(
4.32
) together with (
5.2
). We divide the proof of (
5.1
) according to the values of the model parameters
\gamma
and
\delta
, moving from the easier to the more involved cases.

\bullet

Case
2<\delta\leq\tfrac{1}{\gamma}-1:

Consider first the case
\delta<\tfrac{1}{\gamma}-1
and recall from Proposition
3.2
the asymptotics

(5.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{r}

\displaystyle\asymp r^{d(2-\delta)},\qquad\pi_{r,R}^{{}_{(2)}}\asymp r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\pi_{r_{k+1}(R),R}^{{}_{(2)}}

\displaystyle\asymp r_{k+1}(R)^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\pi_{r_{k+1}(R)}\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{r_{k}(R),R}\asymp r_{k+1}(R)^{d(2-\delta)}r_{k}(R)^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}.

Combining the above with Lemma
4.10
(
4.37
) yields
r_{k+1}(R)\asymp r_{k}(R)^{1/(\delta-1)}
and by recursively applying this, we obtain

r_{k}(R)\asymp r_{0}(R)^{1/(\delta-1)^{k}}\asymp R^{1/(\delta-1)^{k}}.

Hence
\alpha_{k}=1/{(\delta-1)^{k}}\to 0.

For
\delta=\tfrac{1}{\gamma}-1
, recall from Corollary
3.4
that the asymptotics (
5.1
) stay valid on the domain

\{(\log R)^{\frac{\delta}{\delta-1}}\leq r^{d}\},

from which we deduce again the same asymptotics for the sequence
(r_{k}(R))_{k\geq 0}
.

\bullet

Case
\delta\geq\tfrac{1}{\gamma}:

The precise asymptotics for
\pi_{r}
and
\pi_{{}^{r,R}}^{{}_{(2)}}
in Proposition
3.2
are more involved in this case. But ignoring precise log factors, we can summarize these as

\pi_{r}\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}r^{d(2-\frac{1}{\gamma})},\qquad\pi_{r,R}^{{}_{(2)}}\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}r^{d\gamma}R^{d(2-\gamma-\tfrac{1}{\gamma})}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\pi_{r_{k+1}(R),R}^{{}_{(2)}}

\displaystyle\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}r_{k+1}(R)^{d\gamma}R^{d(2-\gamma-1/\gamma)},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\pi_{r_{k+1}(R)}\pi_{r_{k}(R),R}^{{}_{(2)}}

\displaystyle\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}r_{k+1}(R)^{d(2-\frac{1}{\gamma})}r_{k}(R)^{d\gamma}R^{d(2-\gamma-\frac{1}{\gamma})}.

Similarly to the first case, we combine the above with Lemma
4.10
to obtain

\displaystyle r_{k+1}(R)\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}r_{k}^{\left(\gamma/(1-\gamma)\right)^{2}}\underset{{}^{{}_{\log}}}{\asymp}R^{\alpha_{k}},\text{ with $\alpha_{k}=\big(\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\big)^{2k}$. }

As

\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}<1

\alpha_{k}\to 0
, concluding this case.

\bullet

Case
2\vee(\tfrac{1}{\gamma}-1)<\delta<\tfrac{1}{\gamma}:

Proposition

now provides the asymptotics

\displaystyle\pi_{r}

\displaystyle\asymp r^{d(2-\delta)}

\displaystyle\pi_{r,R}^{{}_{(2)}}

\displaystyle\asymp\begin{cases}r^{\frac{d}{\delta}}R^{d(1+\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}-\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{\delta})}&\text{ on }\{r\leq R^{1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\},\\
r^{d}R^{d(1-\delta)}&\text{ on }\{R^{1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\leq r\leq R/2\}.\end{cases}

We use this to obtain recursively
r_{k}(R)\asymp R^{\alpha_{k}}
, however the recursive definition of the sequence
(\alpha_{k})
now differs when this exponent crosses the value

More precisely,

\blacktriangleright

For
k=1,\ldots,k_{0}
, with
k_{0}:=\max\big\{k\geq 0\colon\ \frac{1}{(\delta-1)^{k}}\geq 1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}\big\},
we have

\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{(\delta-1)^{k}}.

\blacktriangleright

Then
\alpha_{k_{0}+1}\in(0,\ 1+\delta-\frac{1}{\gamma}).

\blacktriangleright

Finally for

\alpha_{k+1}=\frac{\alpha_{k}}{(\delta-1)^{2}}=\frac{\alpha_{k_{0}+1}}{(\delta-1)^{2(k-k_{0})}}.

5.3.
Proof of Theorem

We assume that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{*}\big((0,U)\overset{2-\text{edge}}{\longleftrightarrow}B(0,R)^{\sf c}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\pi_{(u),R}^{{}_{(2)}}\mathrm{d}u\asymp\pi_{(1),R}^{{}_{(2)}}=h(R).

We deduce the lower bounds in Theorem

from Table

For the upper bound we use

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{*}\big((0,U)\longleftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{R^{-\epsilon}}\mathbb{P}\big((0,u)\leftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\big)\,du+\mathbb{P}\big((0,R^{-\epsilon})\leftrightarrow B(0,R)^{\sf c}\big)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{0}^{R^{-\epsilon}}\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{(u),R}\,du+\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{(R^{-\epsilon}),R}.

Using that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{-\epsilon}}\pi_{(u),R}^{{}_{(2)}}\,du

\pi^{{}_{(2)}}_{(R^{-\epsilon}),R}\lesssim\pi_{(R^{-\epsilon}),R}+R^{\epsilon\gamma}h(R)\wedge 1,\text{ and }\pi_{(R^{-\epsilon}),R}\lesssim R^{\epsilon}g(R),

An attention-based transfer learning model for diagnosing subluxation in temporomandibular joint panoramic radiographs
Authors: Yasin Sancar, Isil Karabey Aksakalli, Ozlem Sokmen, Hande Barin, Kubra Torenek-Agirman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractArtificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning (DL) techniques have great potential to accelerate diagnostic processes, increase accuracy, and support clinical decision-making in healthcare. In this study, we propose a transfer learning-based approach–one of the DL techniques–to improve subluxation (SL) detection in temporomandibular joint panoramic radiography (TMJ-PR) images. For this purpose, we prepared and publicly released a dataset comprising 3,425 annotated TMJ-PR images to encourage reproducibility and further research in this domain. Several transfer learning models including MobileNet, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3, Xception, EfficientNetV2B0, InceptionResNetV2, and DenseNet201 were trained and evaluated using a 5-fold cross-validation method. By integrating a self-attention mechanism into the DenseNet201 model which achieved the highest baseline performance across all metrics, the proposed attention-based version yielded further improvements, achieving an accuracy of 90.7%, precision of 90.7%, recall of 90.7%, specificity of 89.4%, and F1-score of 90.7%. The results indicate that the proposed model achieves superior F1-score performance compared to all baseline models with relative improvements ranging from +2.40% (vs. DenseNet201) to +14.26% (vs. EfficientNetV2B0). The findings demonstrate that the proposed model not only improves subluxation detection performance but also offers a promising foundation for integration into a clinical decision support system (CDSS), enhancing early diagnosis and treatment planning using low-cost TMJ-PR images. The publicly shared dataset further supports transparency and reproducibility in future medical AI research.Subject terms: Dental diseases, Oral diseases

Introduction

Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) subluxation (SL) (hypermobility) is a dislocation disorder characterized by hyperextension of the condyle during maximum mouth opening, with spontaneous reduction of the condyle head1. The normal anatomy of the joint allows a relatively smooth movement of the condyle downward and along the articular eminence. In the case of SL, the maximum degree of rotational motion of the disc is reached before the maximum translation of the condyle. At maximum opening of the mouth, the final stage of translational movement occurs with a sudden jump with an accompanying sound as the condyle slides over the apex of the articular eminence1,2. When the mouth is closed, the condyle spontaneously returns to the temporal fossa. If left untreated, recurrent dislocations are seen and clinical complaints occur3,4. Patients usually present to the dentist with complaints of mandibular deviation (usually in unilateral cases), difficulty in chewing, headache, tenderness in the masticatory muscle area and pain and voice in the TMJ area. For etiology, Pradhan et al.5 identified six categories of predisposing factors: congenital weakness of the joint ligaments, iatrogenic factors, trauma, drug-induced, physiologic (yawning, vomiting, etc.) and systemic factors (epilepsy, arthritis, etc.). Badel et al.6 reported that it is usually caused by the predisposition of bony condylar surfaces and weakness of ligament and musculature. They stated that TMJs in which the functional posterior surface of the articular eminence is relatively short and steep, while the anterior surface is longer and has a less steep slope are more prone to SL. Conservative treatments such as jaw exercises, splinting, medication and physiotherapy methods are available in the treatment, followed by elimination of the cause, if any. Surgical treatment options are generally preferred in cases where conservative methods are insufficient5.

Various imaging modalities such as cone beam computed tomography (CBCT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), direct radiography techniques, arthrography are used to examine the TMJ and surrounding structures7. To diagnose condylar hypermobility accurately, it is essential to select an appropriate imaging modality that accounts for the dynamic function of the TMJ. This necessitates acquiring images of the joint in both closed-mouth and open-mouth positions. Schüller’s transcranial projections, once commonly used for TMJ evaluation, are no longer the preferred radiographic technique due to their relatively high radiation exposure. Today, Temporomandibular joint panoramic radiographies (TMJ-PRs) are widely used, representing the classical method of choice for X-ray diagnosis of TMJ hyperextension6. These images obtained with many orthopantomography (OPG) devices, provide four lateral TMJ projection images for each TMJ in both closed and open jaw positions8. While TMJ-PR offers the advantages of low cost, low radiation dose, fast acquisition time and accessibility, evaluations are often subjective. Even in the presence of SL, it is not clear how often these radiographs influence clinician decision-making and shape patient care9,10. This prompts the consideration of whether an artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted system could serve as a more objective and reliable diagnostic tool for the detection of SL. A review of the literature on AI applications in TMJ research reveals studies focuses on TMJ osteoarthritis (TMJ-OA) detection from CBCT images10–13, TMJ segmentation from CBCT, TMJ-OA detection from OPG14 and MRIimages15, as well as the identification of disc displacements using MRI, OPG, and TMJ-PR images16. There are also AI studies17,18 based on clinical findings of Temporomandibular joint Disorders (TMD). However, a review of the existing literature reveals a lack of studies focused on the detection of SL using AI.

Transfer learning has become a cornerstone in medical imaging studies where datasets are inherently limited. Instead of training a deep model from scratch, pre-trained architectures (e.g., MobileNet, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3, Xception, EfficientNetV2B0, InceptionResNetV2, and DenseNet201) enable the transfer of generalized image features, thereby reducing overfitting and accelerating convergence on domain-specific tasks such as TMJ subluxation detection. This approach is especially valuable in radiographic diagnosis, where subtle structural differences must be captured from a relatively small dataset. Consequently, our study systematically evaluates multiple transfer learning backbones to validate their suitability for TMJ panoramic radiographs and subsequently enhances the best-performing model with a self-attention mechanism.

In our study, we propose two main hypotheses for the discrimination of SL and Normal images from TMJ-PR images: (i) transfer learning based Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) can detect TMJ subluxation on panoramic radiographs and (ii) adding an attention mechanism to the transfer learning architecture can provide a clinically significant performance improvement. In line with these hypotheses, firstly, the performance of CNN and various transfer learning models in detecting SL and Normal images in TMJ-PR images was evaluated, and then the effect of this contribution on classification performance was investigated by adding the self-attention mechanism to the DenseNet201 architecture, which is the most successful model among the baseline models. This approach seeks to address the current gap in the literature regarding the discrimination of SL and Normal TMJ-PR images and lay the groundwork for future research in this area.

Results

In this study, CNN and transfer learning models such as MobileNet19, ResNet50V220, InceptionV321, Xception22, EfficientNetV2B023, InceptionResNetV224 and DenseNet20125 models are implemented on the TMJ-PR dataset. The dataset consists of a total of The dataset consists of a total of 3,425 TMJ-PR images, 1,478 Normal and 1,947 SL images (57% SL). To ensure a reliable and generalized evaluation, 5-fold cross-validation is employed. In each fold, training is carried out with early stopping to avoid overfitting. This means that training was stopped if the validation loss does not improve for a predefined number of epochs within a fold. After early stopping, the model state corresponding to the lowest validation loss in each fold is retained, and evaluation metrics are computed based on the corresponding validation predictions. Moreover, if the validation loss value does not decrease for two epochs for each model, the learning rate is reduced by (10%). Final performance results are then averaged across all folds. All models are compared in terms of metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, specificity and F1-score using 5-fold cross validation. This comparison is performed to comprehensively evaluate the classification performance of the models, and it is aimed to increase the accuracy of the model by adding the attention mechanism to the best performing model among the transfer learning models. All experiments in the study are performed on a PC configured with an Intel Core i9-9960X processor (16 cores, 32 threads) and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2070 Super GPU (8 GB VRAM), with 32 GB RAM. When the performance of all models is analyzed in Table 1, it is observed that the highest classification performance is achieved by the proposed attention-based DenseNet201 model, which attains 90.7% accuracy, 90.8% precision, 90.7% recall, 89.4% specificity, and 90.7% F1-score. These results clearly demonstrate that integrating a self-attention mechanism into the DenseNet201 backbone leads to a consistent and meaningful improvement across all evaluation metrics.

Among the baseline CNN trained from scratch and pre-trained transfer learning models, DenseNet201 stands out as the best-performing model, reaching 89.1% accuracy, 89.2% precision, 89.1% recall, 87.3% specificity, and 89.1% F1-score. This confirms its strong ability to distinguish between subluxation (SL) and normal cases without additional architectural modifications. The second-best performing model is ResNet50V2, which achieves 87.9% accuracy, 87.9% precision and recall, and an F1-score of 87.8%, showing balanced classification performance. Similarly, InceptionV3 and Xception deliver competitive results with F1-scores of 87.2% and 87.3%, respectively. On the other hand, EfficientNetV2B0 exhibits the lower performance among the pre-trained models, achieving 80.6% accuracy, 80.7% precision, 80.6% recall, 78.2% specificity, and 80.6% F1-score. However, the baseline CNN model, trained from scratch without leveraging prior knowledge, achieved the lowest performance with 66.71% accuracy, indicating its limited ability to generalize from a relatively small dataset and capture the complex patterns in medical images. In contrast, transfer learning methods such as ResNet50V2, DenseNet201, and InceptionResNetV2 significantly improved performance by utilizing weights pretrained on large-scale datasets like ImageNet, which already encode rich feature representations such as edges, textures, and shapes. This prior knowledge allowed the models to adapt more effectively to the target task, achieving accuracies above 87% and highlighting the substantial benefits of transfer learning, particularly when data availability is limited.

To further validate the observed performance improvement, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test was conducted to compare the proposed attention-based model with the baseline DenseNet201 model across the 5-fold results. The test yielded a p-value of p = 0.0625, indicating a trend toward statistical significance. While this value slightly exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold, it still supports the consistency and reliability of the proposed model’s improvement, especially when considered alongside the high anatomical plausibility of the Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM)26 visualizations. These findings suggest that, beyond numerical gains, the integration of an attention mechanism into an already powerful architecture like DenseNet201 offers a clinically meaningful advantage for decision support in the detection of subluxation using TMJ radiographs.

As a result of the experiments, a new approach for SL detection was developed based on the DenseNet201 architecture, which achieved the highest performance in terms of all metrics, and the self-attention mechanism was integrated into this architecture, allowing the model to focus more on subluxated regions. The results show that this integration has very positive effects on the classification performance. The proposed model achieves the highest performance in all metrics with 90.7% accuracy, 90.7% precision, 90.7% recall and 90.7% F1-score values. In particular, the F1-Score indicates that the proposed model achieves a high level of performance by effectively balancing precision and recall in the classification of positive cases. Moreover, the specificity value of 89.4%, which surpasses that of all baseline models, demonstrates the model’s strong capability in correctly identifying negative cases.

Discussion

In this study, a transfer learning based classification model integrated with attention mechanism, customized for the detection of SL presence in TMJ-PR, is developed. As a result of the comparisons with transfer learning based models, it is revealed that the proposed DenseNet201 based and self-attention supported architecture shows superior performance in all evaluation metrics. The model’s 90.7% accuracy, 90.7% precision, 90.7% recall, 89.4% specificity and 90.7% F1-score values reveal that not only positive (SL) classes but also negative (N) classes can be separated with high accuracy compared to the baseline transfer learning models. These findings provide evidence supporting the two main hypotheses proposed in this study. The first hypothesis that transfer learning-based CNNs can detect subluxation in TMJ panoramic radiographs is validated by the superior performance of the DenseNet201 model among the baseline models. The second hypothesis that performance improvement will be achieved with the integration of the attention mechanism is supported by the fact that the attention-based DenseNet201 model gives the highest performance in all evaluation metrics. Hereby, this architectural improvement provided not only statistical but also clinically significant improvement.

When AI-based studies for the diagnosis and prognosis of TMJ-OA are examined, it is seen that many AI-based models such as ResNetV214, Resnet152 and EfficientNetB727, R-CNN, VGG1628, YOLOv513, U-Net29, DiscNet, U-Net and SegNet-TMJ15 are generally performed. Similar to the ensemble learning approach used in the multi-class segmentation study proposed by Yoon et al.30, significant improvements in classification performance were achieved thanks to an approach that combines attention mechanism and model diversity. In this proposed approach, in addition to capturing dense local information with the DenseNet architecture, the feature extraction capacity of the model is increased with self-attention layers focusing on contextual relationships at the global level. This enables the capture of subtle structural differences that are critical in the detection of SL, especially in the TMJ. In addition, the automatic segmentation-based preprocessing (auto cropping) technique used minimizes the effect of noisy regions in the dataset, allowing the model to focus only on diagnostically significant regions. This approach is similar to the steps of Yoon et al.30 such as ROI (Region of Interest) extraction and contrast enhancement for MRI images, and supports the effect of a preprocessing strategy that increases the generalization capacity of the model. The results obtained from the proposed model show that TMJ-PR can be used effectively for both self-attention integration at the architectural level and for complex tasks such as SL detection on radiographic images. Furthermore, the findings show that the performance improvement of the self-attention mechanism integrated into the DenseNet201 model enabled the model to focus on anatomically relevant structures rather than background noise, and that this mechanism increased interpretability. In TMJ-PRs, where subluxation clues are characterized by slight spatial shifts relative to the condylar joint height, self-attention facilitates global context modeling between distant receptive fields that traditional convolutional kernels may overlook. The capacity to dynamically reweight spatial dependencies enables the network to highlight diagnostically meaningful relationships between bilateral joint regions, increasing robustness against patient positioning and radiographic contrast variations. Therefore, even if there is no statistically significant difference, incorporating the attention mechanism into the deep learning model has been shown to both facilitate the interpretability of Grad-CAM visualizations and increase the model’s practical applicability in real clinical settings. Therefore, the main contributions of this study are threefold: (i) the release of the first publicly available dataset for TMJ subluxation detection, (ii) the systematic benchmarking of multiple transfer learning architectures to establish robust baselines, and (iii) the integration of a novel self-attention mechanism into DenseNet201, which improved F1-score by up to 14.2% and provided clinically meaningful interpretability. The proposed approach has the potential to be integrated as a decision support system, especially for physicians who are new to clinical practice or have little experience. In addition, objectifying and standardizing SL diagnosis through low-cost and widely used TMJ-PR can provide clinical contributions in terms of accelerating diagnostic processes and early guidance for treatment.

Our study highlights the critical role of transfer learning in medical imaging tasks where dataset size is inherently limited. By leveraging pre-trained CNNs trained on large-scale natural image datasets, our approach benefits from generalized feature representations that accelerate convergence and mitigate overfitting risks. The comparative evaluation of multiple transfer learning backbones (MobileNet, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3, Xception, EfficientNetV2B0, InceptionResNetV2, and DenseNet201) confirmed the superiority of DenseNet201 in capturing subtle structural differences in TMJ panoramic radiographs. Without transfer learning, training a deep CNN from scratch would not only require substantially more annotated data but also result in weaker generalization to unseen samples. Thus, transfer learning with the self-attention mechanism further enhances performance by directing the model to clinically relevant regions. Moreover, the objectification and standardization of SL diagnosis through low-cost and widely available TMJ-PR may provide clinical benefits by accelerating diagnostic processes and facilitating early referral for treatment when supported by the patient’s clinical findings.

In the literature, SL has been described not only as a pathological phenomenon but also as an anatomical variation. Gupta et al.31 emphasized that SL can also be observed in individuals without clinical symptoms and, unless supported by clinical findings, should not be regarded as a pathological condition on its own. In the present study, the classification was based solely on radiographic images, which represents an important limitation. Due to the retrospective design, clinical examination and functional movement findings could not be included. Nevertheless, since the main objective of our study was to investigate whether AI can distinguish movements of the TMJ, the practical applicability of the results obtained requires future prospective studies that integrate clinical data to reach a definitive diagnosis.

The limitations of the study include that a classification based on only two classes (Normal and SL) was made, the model was not tested in multiple centers, and the applicability of the model to images obtained with different devices was not tested. However, integrating the attention mechanism into the model increases the classification performance, but also increases the architectural complexity and training time of the model. This may limit the applicability of the model, especially in environments with limited hardware resources. Second limitation is that only mouth-open images are cropped and included in the cross-validation sets in two parts, right and left, instead of the entire image. As another limitation of this study, cross-validation was performed on an image-wise rather than a patient-wise basis. This may have led, to a limited extent, to the inclusion of images from the same patient in different folds and, consequently, to a potential patient-level data leakage. Although this is unlikely to have significantly affected the overall performance trend, further studies employing patient-wise data splitting are needed to obtain more conclusive results.

Moreover, all models were initialized from ImageNet-pretrained weights. While widely adopted, natural-image pretraining can be suboptimal for radiographic texture statistics (domain shift). We therefore acknowledge this as a limitation. We hypothesize that medical-domain pretraining (e.g., radiology-focused corpora) could (i) improve sensitivity to subtle condylar-eminence relationships, (ii) reduce data requirements, and (iii) yield more stable convergence for TMJ-PR.

In future studies, it is aimed to hold the fine-tuning protocol constant and vary only the initialization (ImageNet vs.medical-domain pretraining) to quantify potential gains. This comparison complements our current contributions and aligns with best practices for transfer-learning in medical imaging. A system can be developed with high accuracy and sensitivity, including hypomobile TMJ images directly from the TMJ-PR image and marking the SL with explainable AI. Furthermore, it is aimed to train DL models with expanded datasets that include multi-class condyle morphology, all joint movements, and differential diagnoses other than SL, such as TMJ-OA, and to ensure cross-validation on different imaging systems.

Materials and methods

Study participants and data collection

In this study, TMJ-PR images obtained using a Promax device (Planmeca, Helsinki, Finland) and a Kodak 9000 2D device (Carestream Health, Inc., New York, USA) from patients who visited the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Radiology, Faculty of Dentistry, Atatürk University, between January 2016 and January 2025 were used. A standard imaging protocol was applied according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In this protocol, the Frankfort horizontal plane was kept parallel to the floor, and the vertical line was aligned parallel to the sagittal plane. For the closed-mouth position, patients were instructed to bite the bite block while keeping their heads stable. For the open-mouth position, patients were instructed to open their mouths as wide as comfortably possible without changing the head position. A total of 4,270 images obtained with this protocol were anonymized and saved in JPEG format. Exclusion criteria included patients younger than 18 years of age, films with artifacts, radiographs with partial or complete exclusion of the condylar head, radiographs with hypomobility, and images with joint deformity. Gender differences were not taken into account when selecting images.

Data pre-processing

TMJ-PR images were evaluated by two oral diagnosis and maxillofacial radiology (ODMFR) experts with at least ten years of professional experience in the field of condylar mobility in the maximum open mouth position. As a result of this evaluation, the mobility of the condyles was divided into three categories: hypomobility, normal mobility, and (mild or marked) hypermobility (SL). Images with hypomobility (both sides hypomobile or one side normal and the other hypomobile) and images containing artifacts were excluded from the study. The remaining 2,135 images were divided into two main groups as Normal and SL. The images with mouth closed position in the images were removed by automatic cropping. This division process was developed using a segmentation method based on white vertical spaces on the image. This method allowed the separation of the four components contained in an image. As a result, two images belonging to the left and right sides were obtained for each patient from the images in the open mouth position. The total of 4270 cropped images obtained were relabeled as Normal and SL by ODMFR experts in order to correctly group the non-bilateral cases (one side normal, the other side subluxated). 845 cropped images, which could not be agreed upon and could negatively affect the decision-making process of the model due to the superposition of the structures belonging to the opposite TMJ and could not clearly show the articular eminence-condyle relationship, were excluded from the study. The overall dataset consists of 1,947 SL and 1,478 Normal images (57% SL). Figure 3 presents the four-part TMJ-PR images before auto-cropping.

Temporomandibular joint panoramic radiography (TMJ-PR) performed on the orthopantomography device (Planmeca Promax, Helsinki, Finland): right closed (1), right open (2), left open (3), left closed (4) mouth.

After the auto-cropping process, a total of 3,425 images, where the joint surfaces on which the model will be worked on can be clearly observed, were first converted from color to grayscale using the OpenCVSharp library. Then, these images were converted to binary format. A fixed threshold value (color code: 220) was used for the binary imaging process, thus ensuring that only bright (white) regions were highlighted. In the resulting binary image, the total pixel values in each column were calculated and the whiteness intensity was analyzed on the vertical axis. Using these totals, the positions of the vertical white lines were determined and these points were used as segmentation boundaries. The presence of white lines was determined when the total pixel value in the relevant column remained below a certain threshold (e.g. 98%), in proportion to the product of the maximum brightness value of 255 and the image height. The original image was divided into four subcomponents using the obtained split points. However, thanks to the control, only the images with four valid split points were processed. After segmentation, each part was recorded independently; in this context, only the middle two parts (2nd and 3rd) were recorded. This approach was preferred especially to separate and analyze the discrete structures (subluxated and normal blocks) in the image. The definitions of these structures are given below6:

Normal: At maximum mouth opening, the condyle has reached the lowest part of the articular eminence.

Proposed attention-based DenseNet201 model

In this study, a DL-based transfer learning model enhanced with intensified feature extraction and attention mechanism is proposed, aiming at SL detection in TMJ panoramic radiographs.

The proposed model consists of four main components, as shown in Fig. 5: preprocessing, feature extraction, attention mechanism and classification. The preprocessing method among these components is explained in detail in the materials and methods section. For feature extraction, the performance of various transfer learning models was evaluated. All CNN backbones (MobileNet, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3, Xception, EfficientNetV2B0, InceptionResNetV2, and DenseNet201) were initialized with ImageNet-pretrained weights and then fine-tuned on our TMJ-PR dataset. The fine-tuning process allowed the transfer of general low- and mid-level image features learned from natural images to the medical imaging domain, improving convergence and reducing overfitting risks given the limited dataset size.

Architecture of the Proposed Attention-based DensetNet201 Model.

Among the transfer learning models, the pre-trained DenseNet201 architecture showed the highest accuracy rate, therefore it was preferred to build the proposed method. DenseNet201 enables features to be transferred more efficiently thanks to densely connected convolutional blocks and at the same time enables the model to work more stably by reducing the gradient fading problem. The model consists of four DenseNet blocks and transition layers placed between these blocks. In the proposed approach, the pool3_relu output in the third DenseNet block is given as input to the self-attention mechanism and combined with the output obtained from the conv5_block32_concat layers in the fourth DenseNet block, and then transferred to the classification layer. The self-attention module, one of the most critical components of the model, focuses on important regions and ensures that these regions are given weight. Two separate attention vectors are obtained by applying 1×1 convolution layers on the feature maps (x). After these vectors are normalized with the softmax function, they are multiplied to form an attention map. The resulting attention map is multiplied with the original feature map to obtain re-weighted features, allowing the model to focus on regions associated with SL.

Fine-tuning policy (unfreezing) for transfer learning

All convolutional backbones (MobileNet, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3, Xception, EfficientNetV2B0, InceptionResNetV2, DenseNet201) were fine-tuned with a full-network unfreeze strategy from the first epoch; it means that all layers were set trainable. stage-wise alternatives (e.g., unfreezing only the last stage or the last N layers) were considered during protocol design, but the reported results were obtained with the full-network policy to maximize plasticity and allow low-level filters to adapt to the radiographic domain. Training hyperparameters for all models (optimizer, learning-rate schedule, batch size, early stopping) are described in detail in the Subsection “Training configuration details”. The attention-enhanced DenseNet201 model uses identical training settings and inserts the attention block between the pool3_relu and conv5_block32_concat feature stages to fuse global contextual cues with high-level representations.

Training configuration details

To ensure full reproducibility, all fine-tuned transfer learning backbones (MobileNet, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3, Xception, EfficientNetV2B0, InceptionResNetV2, and DenseNet201) were trained under a standardized configuration. Each model was implemented in TensorFlow/Keras using ImageNet-pretrained weights and fine-tuned on the TMJ-PR dataset with an input size of . The Adam optimizer was employed with an initial learning rate of , , and . The loss function was categorical cross-entropy, and accuracy was used as a primary evaluation metric.

Training was conducted for a maximum of 50 epochs with a batch size of 8. An early stopping strategy (patience = 5 epochs) was applied to restore the best model weights once the validation loss stopped improving. In addition, a ReduceLROnPlateau learning-rate scheduler decreased the learning rate by a factor of 0.1 if the validation loss did not improve for three consecutive epochs, with a minimum learning rate of .

Data augmentation included horizontal flipping and random shifts to enhance generalization. All models were trained and validated using 5-fold cross-validation, and the final results were reported as mean ± standard deviation across folds.

For the proposed self-attention–based DenseNet201 model, the same configuration was maintained, with the self-attention block inserted between the pool3_relu and conv5_block32_concat layers. The attention mechanism followed a query–key–value formulation with softmax normalization to emphasize globally relevant features.

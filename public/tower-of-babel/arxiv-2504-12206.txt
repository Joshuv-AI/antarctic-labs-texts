On the classification of finite GK-dimensional pre-Nichols algebras and quasi-quantum groups
Yuping Yang

Abstract
. We prove that every pre-Nichols algebra of a nondiagonal object in the twisted Yetter-Drinfeld category 𝒴 𝕜 ​ G 𝕜 ​ G ​ 𝒟 Φ {{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}} has infinite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, where G G is a finite abelian group and Φ \Phi is a 3 3 -cocycle on G G . 1.
introduction

This work contributes to the classification of infinite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras of finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension (abbreviated as GKdim), along with their corresponding pre-Nichols algebras.
Throughout, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic zero.

Over the past two decades, the lifting method introduced by Andruskiewitsch and Schneider
[
3
]
has led to significant advances in the classification of finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras, see
[
4
,
6
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
and the references therein. Notably, Heckenberger
[
15
]
developed the theory of Weyl groupoids and arithmetic root systems, which has since become an essential tool in the classification program for pointed Hopf algebras. More recently, Andruskiewitsch, Angiono, Heckenberger, and their collaborators have extended this classification effort to pointed Hopf algebras with finite GKdim over abelian groups
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
8
]
. A central objective in this research direction is the classification of finite GK-dimensional pre-Nichols algebras in the Yetter-Drinfeld module category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}}
, where
G
is an abelian group.

Recall that a pre-Nichols algebra of a braided vector space
V
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded connected braided Hopf algebra
\mathcal{P}=\oplus_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{P}_{i}
which is generated by
\mathcal{P}_{1}=V
. Let
T(V)
and
B(V)
denote the tensor algebra and the Nichols algebra of
V
respectively, and let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a pre-Nichols algebra of
V
. Then there are two canonical surjective homomorphisms of graded braided Hopf algebras:

T(V)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{P}(V)\twoheadrightarrow B(V).

Thus, a natural strategy for classifying finite GK-dimensional pre-Nichols algebras in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}}}
is as follows. First, classify all finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras. Then, for each such Nichols algebra, determine all finite GK-dimensional pre-Nichols algebras that cover it.

The study of pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras, or dually basic quasi-Hopf algebras, is motivated by the theory of pointed tensor categories
[
13
]
. In
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
, Etingof and Gelaki started the classification of finite dimensional basic quasi-Hopf algebras. Angiono
[
5
]
classified finite dimensional basic quasi-Hopf algebras over cyclic groups with orders not divided by small prime numbers. In
[
23
,
24
]
, Huang, Liu and Ye presented a classification of finite dimensional basic quasi-Hopf algebras via representation type. In a series of works
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
26
]
, the author of the present paper and his collaborators classified finite dimensional coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras over abelian groups, and partially proved a generation conjecture
[
13
, Conjecture 5.11.10]
of pointed finite tensor categories. However, until now there are barely any classification results of infinite dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras which are not Hopf algebras.

In this paper, we initiate the study of infinite dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras with finite GKdim. Adopting a quasi-version of the lifting method, we focus primarily on Nichols algebras and pre-Nichols algebras in the twisted Yetter-Drinfeld category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
, where
G
is a finite abelian group and
\Phi
is a
3
-cocycle on
G
. It is worth noting that Angiono and García Iglesias
[
8
]
proved that a Nichols algebra of diagonal type in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}}}
has finite GKdim if and only if its root system is finite. Using the the reduction method of
[
21
,
22
]
, this result can be generalized to Nichols algebras of diagonal type in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
. Hence, the principal objective of this paper is to treat the case of Nichols algebras of nondiagonal type.

The novelty of this work lies primarily in the following two aspects. First, we investigate the homogenous subalgebra
A(W)
generated by
W
inside a graded pre-Nichols algebra
P(V)
, and establish a sufficient condition on
W
for
A(W)
itself to be a pre-Nichols algebra. By utilizing this construction, we derive the key inequalities:

\operatorname{GKdim}(B(W))\leq\operatorname{GKdim}(A(W))\leq\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)).

Consequently, we are able to employ arithmetic root systems to study
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(W))
and, via the above inequalities, to obtain estimates for
\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V))
.
Second, we introduce the concept of minimal nondiagonal objects in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
and furnish a detailed structural characterization of such objects. This desription allows us to compute the GKdim of their associated Nichols algebras, ultimately proving that every Nichols algebra of nondiagonal type in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
has infinite GKdim.

Now we present the main result of this paper. The following is our first main theorem, which provides a classification of finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras of simple objects in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a simple twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module with
\operatorname{dim}(V)\geq 2
, and the
G
-degree of
V
is
g
. Then
B(V)
has finite GKdim if and only if
V
is one of the following types:

(T1)

g\triangleright v=v
for all
v\in V.

(T2)

g\triangleright v=-v
for all
v\in V.

(T3)

g\triangleright v=\zeta_{3}v
for all
v\in V
and
\operatorname{dim}(V)=2
, where
\zeta_{3}
is a primitive third root of unity.

If
V
is of type (T1), then
B(V)\cong S(V)
and
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\operatorname{dim}(V)
. For types (T2) and (T3), it was shown in
[
26
]
that
B(V)
is finite dimensional, and hence
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=0
.

Theorem
1.1
provides a key preliminary step for studying finite GK-dimensional pre-Nichols algebras in the twisted Yetter-Drinfeld category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. According to this theorem, it is enough to consider pre-Nichols algebras of objects of the form
\oplus_{1\leq i\leq\theta}V_{i}
, where each
V_{i}
is of type (T1)-(T3). Our second main theorem concerns the GKdim of pre-Nichols algebras of nondiagonal type.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
8.3
)
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
be a nondiagonal object. Then every pre-Nichols algebra of
V
has infinite GKdim.

Using this result and applying the method developed in our earlier work
[
21
,
22
]
, we can readily classify all finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
8.4
)
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
be a finite-dimensional object. Then
B(V)
has finite GKdim if and only if it is of diagonal type and the corresponding root system is finite, that is, an arithmetic root system.

Via bosonization, this theorem also yields a classification of finite GK-dimensional coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras over finite abelian groups that are generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements, see Corollary
8.5
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall necessary definitions and facts about twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules, pre-Nichols algebras, generalized root systems and Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. Section 3 is devoted to graded pre-Nichols algebras and their homogenous subalgebras. In Sections 4, we classify finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras of diagonal type in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
, and prove Theorem
1.1
. Sections 5-7 are devoted to the study of Nichols algebras of minimal nondiagonal objects. Finally, in Section 8, we prove Theorems
1.2
-
1.3
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some necessary notions and basic facts about twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules, pre-Nichols algebras, generalized root systems and GK-dimension. Throughout, let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let
\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{Z}_{m}

2.1.
Twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module

Let
G
be a finite group. It is well known that the group algebra
\mathbbm{k}G
is a Hopf algebra with
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\;S(g)=g^{-1}
and

\varepsilon(g)=1
for any
g\in G
. Let
\Phi
be a normalized
3
-cocycle on
G
, that is a function
\Phi:G\times G\times G\to\mathbbm{k}^{*}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(ef,g,h)\Phi(e,f,gh)=\Phi(e,f,g)\Phi(e,fg,h)\Phi(f,g,h),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Phi(f,1,g)=1

for all
e,f,g,h\in G
. The linear extension of
\Phi
to
(\mathbbm{k}G)^{\otimes 3}
, also denote by
\Phi
, becomes a
convolution-invertible map. Let
\alpha,\beta\colon\mathbbm{k}G\to\mathbbm{k}
be two linear functions defined by

\alpha(g):=\varepsilon(g)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(g):=\frac{1}{\Phi(g,g^{-1},g)},\quad\forall g\in G.

Then
kG
together with these
\Phi,\ \alpha
and
\beta

makes a coquasi-Hopf algebra, which will be written as
(\mathbbm{k}G,\Phi)
in what follows. In order to distinguish the Yetter-Drinfeld modules of the group algebra
\mathbbm{k}G
, the Yetter-Drinfeld modules of
(\mathbbm{k}G,\Phi)
are called twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules when
\Phi
is not trivial. The category of twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules of
(\mathbbm{k}G,\Phi)
will be denoted by
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
.

Next we recall the construction of
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
when
G
is a finite abelian group. For each
g\in G
, let
\Phi_{g}:G\times G\to\mathbbm{k}^{*}
be the function:

(2.3)

{\Phi}_{g}(x,y)=\frac{\Phi(g,x,y)\Phi(x,y,g)}{\Phi(x,g,y)},\quad\forall x,y\in G.

By direct computation one can show that
{\Phi}_{g}
is a 2-cocycle on
G
. The construction of
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
can be summarized as follows, see
[
25
]
for detailed computations.

Definition 2.1
.

An object in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
is a
G
-graded vector space
V=\oplus_{g\in G}V_{g}
such that each
V_{g}
is a projective

G
-representation with respect to the 2-cocycle
{\Phi}_{g},
namely for any
e,f\in G,v\in V_{g}
we have

(2.4)

e\triangleright(f\triangleright v)={\Phi}_{g}(e,f)(ef)\triangleright v.

The comodule structure
\rho:V\to kG\otimes V
is given by

(2.5)

\rho(v)=g\otimes v,\ \ v\in V_{g},\ \ g\in G.

The module structure on the tensor product
V_{g}\otimes V_{h}
is determined by

(2.6)

e\triangleright(X\otimes Y)={\Phi}_{e}(g,h)(e\triangleright X)\otimes(e\triangleright Y),\ X\in V_{g},\ Y\in V_{h}.

The associativity and the braiding constraints of
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
are given respectively by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\ a_{V_{e},V_{f},V_{g}}((X\otimes Y)\otimes Z)=\Phi(e,f,g)^{-1}X\otimes(Y\otimes Z),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X\otimes Y)=(e\triangleright Y)\otimes X

for all
X\in V_{e},\ Y\in V_{f},\ Z\in V_{g}.

Moreover, suppose
V_{g}
is
(G,{\Phi}_{g})
-representation,
V_{h}
is a
(G,{\Phi}_{h})
-representation, then
V_{g}\otimes V_{h}
is a
(G,{\Phi}_{gh})
-representation. The dual object
V_{g}^{*}
of
V_{g}
is a
(G,{\Phi}_{g^{-1}})
-representation and
(V_{g}^{*})^{*}=V_{g}
, see
[
26
, Proposition 2.5]
for details. Suppose
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
is a simple twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module, then there exists an element
g\in G
such that
V=V_{g}
, and we say that the
G
-degree of
V
is
g
and denote
g_{V}=g
. Moreover, for each nonzero element
X\in V=V_{g}
, we denote
g_{X}=g
. Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}
be a direct sum of simple objects in
{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
,
g_{i}
the
G
-degree of
V_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\theta
. Then the subgroup
G_{V}:=\langle g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{\theta}\rangle
will be called the
support group
of

. According to (
2.8
), it is clear that the braiding of

is determined by the action of
G_{V}
.

Let
(V,\triangleright,\rho)\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and let

be a
2
-cochain on

. Then there is a new action
\triangleright_{J}
of

determined by

(2.9)

g\triangleright_{J}X=\frac{J(g,x)}{J(x,g)}g\triangleright X,

where

and

is a homogenous element with
G
-degree

. We denote

Moreover there is a tensor equivalence

which takes

and

\varphi_{2}(U,V)\colon\ (U\otimes V)^{J}\to U^{J}\otimes V^{J},\ \ Y\otimes Z\mapsto J(y,z)^{-1}Y\otimes Z

Lemma 2.2

2.3.
Nichols algebras and pre-Nichols algebras

Nichols algebras and pre-Nichols algebras can be defined for general braided vector spaces, see
[
17
]
. For the purpose of this paper, we only introduce these definitions for objects in the category
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional object in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}.
By
T(V)
we denote the tensor algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
generated freely by
V.
As linear space
T(V)
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
, where
V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
means

\underbrace{(\cdots((}_{n-1}V\otimes V)\otimes V)\cdots\otimes V).
This induces a natural
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded structure on
T(V).
Furthermore,
T(V)
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded braided Hopf algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
by declaring that every nonzero element in
V
is primitive.

A pre-Nichols algebra of
V
is a braided Hopf algebra
\mathcal{P}(V)
obtained as the quotient of
T(V)
by an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-homogenous Hopf ideal
I\subset\bigoplus_{n\geq 2}V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{P}(V)
is a connected
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded braided Hopf algebra generated by
V
. Let
I(V)
be the maximal
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-homogenous Hopf ideal contained in
\bigoplus_{n\geq 2}V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
. The Nichols algebra
B(V)
of
V
is defined as the quotient Hopf algebra
T(V)/I(V)
. Thus
B(V)
is a particular pre-Nichols algebra, and there exist braided Hopf algebra epimorphisms

T(V)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{P}(V)\twoheadrightarrow B(V),

Where the restrictions of both epimorphisms to degree one are
\operatorname{id}_{V}
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of
\theta
simple objects. Any pre-Nichols algebra
\mathcal{P}(V)
of
V
is said to be of rank
\theta
, and we write
\operatorname{Rank}(\mathcal{P}(V))=\theta
.
Let
g_{i}=g_{V_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq\theta
. If the set
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{\theta}\}
freely generates
G
, i.e.,
G\cong\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle g_{\theta}\rangle
, then
V
and the associated pre-Nichols algebras are called of
standard form
.

For each braided Hopf algebra
H
in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
and a
2
-cochain
J
on
G
, one can define a new braided Hopf algebra
H^{J}
in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi\ast\partial J}
whose multiplication
\circ
is given by

(2.16)

X\circ Y=J(x,y)XY

for all homogenous elements
X,Y\in H,
where
x=g_{X}
and
y=g_{Y}
denote
G
-degrees of
X
and
Y
respectively. In particular, let
(V,\triangleright,\rho)\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a pre-Nichols algebra of
V
. Then

\mathcal{P}(V)^{J}
is also an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded braided Hopf algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi\ast\partial J}
, generated by
V^{J}=(V,\triangleright_{J},\rho)
. This leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a pre-Nichols algebra of
V\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and let
J
be a
2
-cochain on
G
. Then
\mathcal{P}(V)^{J}
is a pre-Nichols algebra of
V^{J}\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi\ast\partial J}}
.

Adopting the same terminology as in the theory of coquasi-Hopf algebras, we say that
\mathcal{P}(V)
and
\mathcal{P}(V)^{J}
are twist equivalent, or
\mathcal{P}(V)^{J}
is a twisting of
\mathcal{P}(V)
. A special case of Lemma
2.6
was given in
[
21
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
21
, Lemma 2.12]

The twisting
B(V)^{J}
of
B(V)
is a Nichols algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi\ast\partial J}
and
B(V)^{J}\cong B(V^{J})
.

2.4.
Dynkin diagram and root system

Arithmetic root systems are invariants for Nichols algebras of diagonal type with certain finiteness property. A complete classification of arithmetic root systems, established by Heckenberger
[
16
]
, plays a crucial role in the classification program of pointed Hopf algebras with finitely many PBW generators.

Suppose
B(V)
is a Nichols algebra of diagonal type in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}.
Fix a diagonal basis
\{X_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}
of
V
. As shown in
[
21
]
,
B(V)
has a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded braided Hopf algebra structure with
\operatorname{deg}(X_{i})=e_{i},1\leq i\leq n
, where
\{e_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}
is a canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}.
Moreover,
B(V)
admits a PBW basis whose generators are homogenous with respect to this
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading. Following
[
15
]
, we denote by
\triangle^{+}(B(V))
the set of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-degrees of these PBW generators, counted with multiplicities, and define the root system of
B(V)
as

\bigtriangleup(B(V)):=\bigtriangleup^{+}(B(V))\bigcup-\bigtriangleup^{+}(B(V)).

When
\bigtriangleup(B(V))
is finite, it coincides with an arithmetic root system.

To each Nichols algebra of diagonal type one can also associated a diagram.
Let
V
be a diagonal object in
{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
with a diagonal basis
\{X_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}
and braiding
\mathcal{R}
.
The structure constants of
B(V)
are
\{q_{ij}|1\leq i,j\leq n\}
satisfying (
2.15
). The generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is defined as an undirected graph as follows:

1)

There is a bijecton
\phi
from the index set
I=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
to the set of vertices of
\mathcal{D}(V)
.

2)

The vertex
\phi(i)
is labelled by
q_{ii}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

3)

For
1\leq i,j\leq n,
the number
n_{ij}
of edges between
\phi(i)
and
\phi(j)
is either
0
or
1.
If
i=j
or
q_{ij}q_{ji}=1
, then
n_{ij}=0
. Otherwise
n_{ij}=1
and the unique edge is labelled by
\widetilde{q_{ij}}=q_{ij}q_{ji}
for all

1\leq i<j\leq n.

A Nichols algebra of diagonal type always admits a generalized Dynkin diagram, and this diagram is independent of the choice of diagonal basis for
V
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module of diagonal type. The generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is said to be of finite type if
\bigtriangleup(B(V))
is finite.

Let
J
be a
2
-cochain on
G
and let
V^{J}
be the twisting of
V
by
J
. Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{J}
the braiding of
V^{J}
and by
\{\overline{q_{ij}}|1\leq i,j\leq n\}
be the corresponding structure constants. Using (
2.9
) we obtain

\mathcal{R}_{J}(X_{i}\otimes X_{j})=g_{i}\triangleright_{J}X_{j}\otimes X_{i}=\frac{J(g_{i},g_{j})}{J(g_{j},g_{i})}g_{i}\triangleright X_{j}\otimes X_{i}=\frac{J(g_{i},g_{j})}{J(g_{j},g_{i})}q_{ij}X_{j}\otimes X_{i},

where
g_{i}
is the
G
-degree of
X_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Hence
\overline{q_{ij}}=\frac{J(g_{i},g_{j})}{J(g_{j},g_{i})}q_{ij}
, which yields

\overline{q_{ii}}=q_{ii},\ \ \overline{q_{ij}}\overline{q_{ji}}=q_{ij}q_{ji},\ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Consequently,
\mathcal{D}(V)
and
\mathcal{D}(V^{J})
are the same generalized Dynkin diagram. Moreover, by (
2.16
) the twisting preserves the
Z^{n}
-degree of every
Z^{n}
-homogenous element of
B(V)
. Therefore we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a Nichols algebra of diagonal type and let
J
be a
2
-cochain on
G
. Then
B(V)
and its twisting
B(V^{J})
share the same root system and the same generalized Dynkin diagram.

2.5.
On GK-dimension

Let
A
be a finitely generated algebra, and let
V
be a finite-dimensional generating subspace of
A
. Set
A_{n}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq n}V^{i}
. The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension (GK-dimension or GKdim for short) of
A
is defined by

(2.17)

\operatorname{GKdim}(A)=\overline{\operatorname{lim}}_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{log}_{n}{\operatorname{dim}(A_{n})}.

The value of GK-dimension does not depend on the choice of finite-dimensional generating subspace. If
A
is not finitely generated, its GK-dimension is defined as

(2.18)

\operatorname{GKdim}(A)=\operatorname{sup}\{\operatorname{GKdim}(B)\ |\ B\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{a}\ \mathrm{finitely}\ \mathrm{generated}\ \mathrm{subalgebra}\ \mathrm{of}\ A\}.

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a pre-Nichols algebra of
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\mathcal{P}(V)_{n}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq n}V^{i}\subset\mathcal{P}(V),\ \ \ \ B(V)_{n}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq n}V^{i}\subset B(V).

Since the canonical epimorphism from
\mathcal{P}(V)
onto
B(V)
is
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded, we have
\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{P}(V)_{n})\geq\operatorname{dim}(B(V)_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consequently

(2.19)

\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V))\geq\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V)).

Furthermore, if
B\subset\mathcal{P}(V)
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded subalgebra, then it is clear that

(2.20)

\operatorname{GKdim}(B)\leq\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)).

Let
J
be a
2
-cochain on
G
. From (
2.16
) we obtain
(\mathcal{P}(V)^{J})_{n}\subset\mathcal{P}(V)_{n}
as linear spaces for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

(2.21)

\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)^{J})\leq\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)).

On the other hand, Applying (
2.21
) again gives

(2.22)

\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V))=\operatorname{GKdim}(((\mathcal{P}(V)^{J})^{J^{-1}})\leq\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)^{J}).

Thus we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a pre-Nichols algebra of
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, and let
J
be a
2
-cochain on
G
. Then

\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)^{J})=\operatorname{GKdim}(\mathcal{P}(V)).

3.
Graded pre-Nichols algebras

In this section, we explore the structural properties of graded pre-Nichols algebras in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, with a particular emphasis on the associated based groups and homogenous subalgebras.
Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}
be a direct sum of simple objects in the category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. Recall that a pre-Nichols algebra
\mathcal{P}(V)
is said to be
graded
if it is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded braided Hopf algebra such that
\operatorname{deg}(V_{i})=e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\theta
, where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\theta}\}
denotes the standard basis of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
.

3.1.
Based groups

Let
H
be a braided Hopf algebra in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
. We call
G
the based group of
H
, and denote by
G_{H}
be the support group of
H
. For any
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and a pre-Nichols algebra
\mathcal{P}(V)
, it is clear that
G_{\mathcal{P}(V)}=G_{V}
. A useful technique in the study of Nichols algebras, introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
, is to realize a given Nichols algebra in different twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module categories. This method can be extended naturally to graded pre-Nichols algebras.

Let
H
and
M
be braided Hopf algebras in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}F}^{\mathbbm{k}F}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
respectively, where
F
is also a finite abelian group and
\Psi
is a
3
-cocycle on
F
. We say that
H
and
M
are isomorphic if there exists a linear isomorphism
f:H\to M
that is both an algebra homomorphism and a coalgebra homomorphism.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H
be a braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and let
F
be a subgroup of
G
such that
G_{H}\subset F
. Then
H
is also a braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}F}^{\mathbbm{k}F}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
, where
\Psi=\Phi_{F}
is the restriction of
\Phi
to
F
. In particular,
H
is a braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{H}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{H}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{H}}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:H\to\mathbbm{k}G\otimes H
be the comodule structure map. Since
\rho(H)\subset\mathbbm{k}G_{H}\otimes H\subset\mathbbm{k}F\otimes H
, the space
H
inherits an
F
-comodule structure, and thus
H=\oplus_{g\in F}H_{g}
. Take
X\in H_{g}
and
e,f\in F
. Using (
2.4
) we obtain

e\triangleright(f\triangleright X)={\Phi}_{g}(e,f)ef\triangleright X={\Psi}_{g}(e,f)ef\triangleright X.

Hence each
H_{g}
is an
(F,\Psi_{g})
-projective representation, and consequently
H
is a twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module in the category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}F}^{\mathbbm{k}F}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
.

It remains to show that
H
is a braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}F}^{\mathbbm{k}F}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
. Let
m
and
\bigtriangleup
denote the product and coproduct of
H
, respectively. Since
m
and
\bigtriangleup
are morphisms in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, for all
g\in F\subset G
and
X,Y\in H
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle g\triangleright m(X\otimes Y)=m(g\triangleright(X\otimes Y)),

(3.2)

\displaystyle X_{1}Y_{1}\otimes m(X_{2}\otimes Y_{2})=m(XY)_{1}\otimes m(XY)_{2},

where we use the Sweedler notation
\rho(X)=X_{1}\otimes X_{2}
for all
X\in H
. Because
X_{1}Y_{1},m(XY)_{1}\in G_{H}\subset F
, the identities (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) imply that
m
is a morphism in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}F}^{\mathbbm{k}F}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
. A similar argument shows that
\bigtriangleup
is also a morphism in the same category. Thus
H
is indeed a braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}F}^{\mathbbm{k}F}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
.
∎

We now present an alternative method for changing the based group of a graded pre-Nichols algebra. Let
\mathbbm{G}
be a finite abelian group and let
\pi:\mathbbm{G}\to G
be a group epimorphism. Define the
3
-cocycle
\pi^{*}{\Phi}
on
\mathbbm{G}
by

\pi^{*}{\Phi}(e,f,g)=\Phi(\pi(e),\pi(f),\pi(g)),\ \forall e,f,g\in\mathbbm{G}.

If
\mathcal{P}(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is graded, we shall show that
\mathcal{P}(V)
can also be realized in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbbm{G}
be a finite abelian group and let
\pi:\mathbbm{G}\to G
be a group epimorphism that admits a section
\iota:G\to\mathbbm{G}
, i.e.,
\pi\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_{G}
. For an object
(V,\triangleright,\rho)
in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, define

(3.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}=(\iota\otimes\operatorname{id})\rho,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{g}\ \widetilde{\triangleright}X=\pi(\mathbbm{g})\triangleright X.

Then
\widetilde{V}=(V,\widetilde{\triangleright},\widetilde{\rho})
is a twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module over
(\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G},\pi^{*}\Phi)
. Moreover, if
V
is simple, so is
\widetilde{V}
.

Proof.

Since
V
is a
G
-graded, we may write
V=\bigoplus_{g\in G}V_{g}
. Then
V
inherits an
G
-grading by setting

V_{\mathbbm{g}}=\begin{cases}V_{g}&\ \mathrm{if}\ \mathbbm{g}=\iota(g)\ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{some}\ g\in G,\\
\ 0,&\ \mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

To show that
\widetilde{V}
belongs to
(\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G},\pi^{*}\Phi)
, it suffices to verify that each homogenous component
{V}_{\iota(g)}
is a projective
\mathbbm{G}
-representation with respect to
2-cocycle
{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}_{\iota(g)}
. Take
e,f\in\mathbbm{G}
and
v\in{V}_{\iota(g)}
, we have

\displaystyle e\ \widetilde{\triangleright}(f\ \widetilde{\triangleright}v)=\pi(e)\triangleright(\pi(f)\triangleright v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{g}(\pi(e),\pi(f))\pi(ef)\triangleright v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(g,\pi(e),\pi(f))\Phi(\pi(e),\pi(f),g)}{\Phi(\pi(e),g,\pi(f))}ef\ \widetilde{\triangleright}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(\pi\circ\iota(g),\pi(e),\pi(f))\Phi(\pi(e),\pi(f),\pi\circ\iota(g))}{\Phi(\pi(e),\pi\circ\iota(g),\pi(f))}ef\ \widetilde{\triangleright}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{*}\Phi(\iota(g),e,f)\pi^{*}\Phi(e,f,\iota(g))}{\pi^{*}\Phi(e,\iota(g),f)}ef\ \widetilde{\triangleright}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\pi^{*}\Phi}_{\iota(g)}(e,f)ef\ \widetilde{\triangleright}v.

Thus
{V}_{\iota(g)}
is indeed a projective
\mathbbm{G}
-representation with respect to
{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}_{\iota(g)}
, and consequently
\widetilde{V}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
.

Now suppose
V
is simple. If
\widetilde{V}
is not simple, it would contain a nontrivial subobject
\widetilde{U}
. Because
\widetilde{V}=\bigoplus_{\mathbbm{g}\in\mathbbm{G}}V_{\mathbbm{g}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}V_{\iota(g)}
, we have
\widetilde{U}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}U_{\iota(g)}
with
U_{\iota(g)}\subset V_{\iota(g)}
. Define
U=\bigoplus_{g\in G}U_{g}
, where
U_{g}=U_{\iota(g)}
as vector spaces. Then
U
becomes an object in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
by defining
g\triangleright u=\iota(g)\widetilde{\triangleright}\ u
for all
g\in G
and
u\in U
. It is clear that
U
is a nontrivial subobject of
V
, which is a contradiction since
V
is simple. Thus
\widetilde{V}
is also simple.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbbm{G}
be a finite abelian group and let
\pi:\mathbbm{G}\to G
be a group epimorphism that admits a section
\iota:G\to\mathbbm{G}
. Then every graded pre-Nichols algebra
\mathcal{P}(V)
in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is isomorphic to a graded pre-Nichols algebra in the category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}\Phi}}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{V}
be the twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
obtained from
V
via (
3.3
)-(
3.4
). Denote by
T(V)
and
T(\widetilde{V})
the tensor algebras of
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
and of
\widetilde{V}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
, respectively. As vector spaces,
\widetilde{V}=V
, hence
T(V)=T(\widetilde{V})
and the identity map
\epsilon:T(V)\to T(\widetilde{V})
is an algebra isomorphism, because both tensor algebras are freely generated by the same underlying vector space.

We now show that
\epsilon
is also a coalgebra morphism, and therefore an isomorphism of braided Hopf algebras. Write
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}
as a direct sum of simple objects. By Lemma
3.2
,

\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\widetilde{V}_{\theta}
is also a direct sum of simple objects. Clearly,
T(V)
and
T(\widetilde{V})
are both
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded with
\operatorname{deg}(V_{i})=\operatorname{deg}(\widetilde{V}_{i})=e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\theta
. For a homogenous element
X\in T(V)
of degree
\operatorname{deg}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\theta}k_{i}e_{i}
, denote by
g_{X}
its
G
-degree in
T(V)
and by
\mathbbm{g}_{X}
its
\mathbbm{G}
-degree in
T(\widetilde{V})
. Then

(3.5)

g_{X}=g_{1}^{k_{1}}g_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots g_{\theta}^{k_{\theta}},\ \ \mathbbm{g}_{X}=\iota(g_{1})^{k_{1}}\iota(g_{2})^{k_{2}}\cdots\iota(g_{\theta})^{k_{\theta}}

and
\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{X})=g_{X}.

Let
\Delta
and
\widetilde{\Delta}
be the coproducts of
T(V)
and
T(\widetilde{V})
respectively. We will prove that

(3.6)

(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta=\widetilde{\Delta}\circ\epsilon

by induction on the lengths of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-homogenous elements. For
X\in V
, both
\Delta(X)
and
\widetilde{\Delta}(X)
equal to
X\otimes 1+1\otimes X
, thus the claim holds for generators.

Assume the statement is true for all elements of length
n\geq 1
. Take a nomogenous element
X\in V^{n+1}
. We may write
X=\sum_{i\in I}X_{i}Y_{i}
with
X_{i}\in V
and
Y_{i}\in V^{n}
homogenous. Using Sweedler’s notation
\Delta(Y_{i})=Y_{i1}+Y_{i2}
and the induction hypothesis, we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}&(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta(X_{i}Y_{i})\\
&=(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)(X_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes X_{i})(Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2})\\
&=(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)[\Phi(g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i1}},g_{Y_{i2}})X_{i}Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2}+\frac{\Phi(g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i1}},g_{Y_{i2}})}{\Phi(g_{Y_{i1}},g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i2}})}g_{X_{i}}\triangleright Y_{i1}\otimes X_{i}Y_{i2}]\\
&=\Phi(g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i1}},g_{Y_{i2}})X_{i}Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2}+\frac{\Phi(g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i1}},g_{Y_{i2}})}{\Phi(g_{Y_{i1}},g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i2}})}g_{X_{i}}\triangleright Y_{i1}\otimes X_{i}Y_{i2},\end{split}

and

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\Delta}\circ\epsilon(X_{i}Y_{i})=\widetilde{\Delta}(\epsilon(X_{i})\epsilon(Y_{i}))\\
&=(X_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes X_{i})(Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2})\\
&=\pi^{*}\Phi(\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}},\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i1}},\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i2}})X_{i}Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\frac{\pi^{*}\Phi(\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}},\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i1}},\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i2}})}{\pi^{*}\Phi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i1}},\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}},\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i2}})}\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}}\widetilde{\triangleright}\ Y_{i1}\otimes X_{i}Y_{i2}\\
&=\Phi(\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}}),\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i1}}),\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i2}}))X_{i}Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\frac{\Phi(\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}}),\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i1}}),\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i2}}))}{\Phi(\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i1}}),\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}}),\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{Y_{i2}}))}\mathbbm{g}_{X_{i}}\widetilde{\triangleright}\ Y_{i1}\otimes X_{i}Y_{i2}\\
&=\Phi(g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i1}},g_{Y_{i2}})X_{i}Y_{i1}\otimes Y_{i2}+\frac{\Phi(g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i1}},g_{Y_{i2}})}{\Phi(g_{Y_{i1}},g_{X_{i}},g_{Y_{i2}})}g_{X_{i}}\triangleright Y_{i1}\otimes X_{i}Y_{i2}.\end{split}

Thus
(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta(X_{i}Y_{i})=\widetilde{\Delta}\circ\epsilon(X_{i}Y_{i})
for each
i\in I
, whence
(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta(X)=\widetilde{\Delta}\circ\epsilon(X)
.
This shows that
\epsilon
is a coalgebra morphism, and consequently an isomorphism of braided Hopf algebras between
T(V)
and
T(\widetilde{V})
.

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{P}(V)
is isomorphic to a quotient of
T(\widetilde{V})
. Let
I
be the braided Hopf ideal of
T(V)
such that
\mathcal{P}(V)=T(V)/I
. Because
\mathcal{P}(V)
is graded,
I
must be
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded. Set
\widetilde{I}=\epsilon(I)
. It is a braided Hopf ideal of
T(\widetilde{V})
, hence
T(\widetilde{V})/\widetilde{I}
is a braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}(\Phi)}}
. Clearly,
the map
\epsilon
induces an isomorphism from
\mathcal{P}(V)=T(V)/I
to
T(\widetilde{V})/\widetilde{I}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}(\Phi)}}
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Recall that for each finite abelian group
G
, there exists an abelian group
\widehat{G}
and a canonical group epimorphism
\pi:\widehat{G}\to G
defined by (
2.14
). With these notations we have

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a graded pre-Nichols algebra of diagonal type in the category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. Then
\mathcal{P}(V)
is twist equivalent to a graded pre-Nichols algebra of diagonal type in the ordinary Yetter-Drinfeld module category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}\mathcal{YD}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathcal{P}(V)
is already a pre-Nichols algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}}
. Applying Lemma
3.3
, we see that
\mathcal{P}(V)
is isomorphic to a graded pre-Nichols algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}\Phi_{G_{V}}}}
. Now, because
\mathcal{P}(V)
is of diagonal type, Lemma
2.5
implies that
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
. Hence, by Proposition
2.3
, the pulled-back cocycle
\pi^{*}\Phi_{G_{V}}
is a
3
-coboundary on
\widehat{G_{V}}
. Consequently, there exists a
2
-cochain
J
on
\widehat{G_{V}}
such that
\partial J=\pi^{*}\Phi_{G_{V}}
. Finally, Lemma
2.6
shows that the twisting
\mathcal{P}(V)^{J^{-1}}
is a graded pre-Nichols algebra in the ordinary Yetter-Drinfeld category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}\mathcal{YD}}
.
∎

3.2.
Homogenous subalgebras

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a graded pre-Nichols algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. For any homogenous subobject
I
of
\mathcal{P}(V)
, we denote by
\operatorname{Deg}(I)
the set of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-degrees of nonzero homogenous elements of
I
. The main result of this subsection is as follows.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a graded pre-Nichols algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
with counit
\epsilon
, and let
A
be an homogenous subalgebra of
\mathcal{P}(V)
. Assume that there exist homogenous subobjects
M,N\subset\ker\epsilon
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\operatorname{Deg}(A)\cap\operatorname{Deg}(M)=\emptyset
and
\operatorname{Deg}(A)\cap\operatorname{Deg}(N)=\emptyset
;

(b)

M
and
N
are
A
-bimodules under the product of
\mathcal{P}(V)
, and
M^{2}\subset M,N^{2}\subset N
;

(c)

\Delta(A)\subset A\otimes A+M\otimes N
.

Then
A
is a braided bialgebra with a new comultiplication
\widetilde{\Delta}=P\circ\Delta
, where

P:A\otimes A+M\otimes N\to A\otimes A

is the canonical projection.

Proof.

First, we show that
\widetilde{\Delta}
is coassociative. By condition (c), we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(A)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle(A\otimes A)\otimes A+(M\otimes N)\otimes A+(P(V)\otimes P(V))\otimes N.

(3.10)

\displaystyle(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(A)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle A\otimes(A\otimes A)+A\otimes(M\otimes N)+M\otimes(P(V)\otimes P(V)).

By condition (a), the right hand sides of (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) are direct sums. For a homogenous element
x\in A
, we can write

(3.11)

(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(x)=(\widetilde{\Delta}\otimes\operatorname{id})\widetilde{\Delta}(x)+(x^{\prime}_{1}\otimes x^{\prime}_{2})\otimes x^{\prime}_{3}+(x^{\prime\prime}_{1}\otimes x^{\prime\prime}_{2})\otimes x^{\prime\prime}_{3}

with
(x^{\prime}_{1}\otimes x^{\prime}_{2})\otimes x^{\prime}_{3}\in(M\otimes N)\otimes A
and
(x^{\prime\prime}_{1}\otimes x^{\prime\prime}_{2})\otimes x^{\prime\prime}_{3}\in(P(V)\otimes P(V))\otimes N
.
Similarly,

(3.12)

(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(x)=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\widetilde{\Delta})\widetilde{\Delta}(x)+Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes(Y^{\prime}_{2}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{3})+Y^{\prime\prime}_{1}\otimes(Y^{\prime\prime}_{2}\otimes Y^{\prime\prime}_{3}),

where
Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes(Y^{\prime}_{2}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{3})\in A\otimes(M\otimes N)
and
Y^{\prime\prime}_{1}\otimes(Y^{\prime\prime}_{2}\otimes Y^{\prime\prime}_{3})\in M\otimes(P(V)\otimes P(V))
.
Let
a
denote the associator of the category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. From the coassociativity of
\Delta
we have

a\circ(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(x)=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(x).

Substituting the two decompositions (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), and considering the
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-degrees of the three summands in each decomposition, we obtain

a\circ(\widetilde{\Delta}\otimes\operatorname{id})\widetilde{\Delta}(x)=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\widetilde{\Delta})\widetilde{\Delta}(x).

Hence
\widetilde{\Delta}
is coassociative.

Next we verify that
\widetilde{\Delta}
is an algebra homomorphism. For
X,Y\in A
, we write

\begin{split}\Delta(X)&=\widetilde{\Delta}(X)+X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2},\\
\Delta(Y)&=\widetilde{\Delta}(Y)+Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{2}\end{split}

with
X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2},Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{2}\in M\otimes N
. Then we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Delta}(XY)&=P\circ\Delta(XY)\\
&=P\big((\widetilde{\Delta}(X)+X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2})(\widetilde{\Delta}(Y)+Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{2})\big)\\
&=P\big(\widetilde{\Delta}(X)\widetilde{\Delta}(Y)+\widetilde{\Delta}(X)(Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{2})+(X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2})\widetilde{\Delta}(Y)+(X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2})(Y^{\prime}_{1}\otimes Y^{\prime}_{2})\big)\\
&=\widetilde{\Delta}(X)(\widetilde{\Delta}(Y).\end{split}

Here the fourth identity follows from the condition (b). Thus
\widetilde{\Delta}
is an algebra homomorphism.

Finally, we check the counit condition, that is
(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})\widetilde{\Delta}=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon)\widetilde{\Delta}=\operatorname{id}
. For each
X\in A
, we write
\Delta(X)=\widetilde{\Delta}(X)+X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2}
with
X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2}\in M\otimes N
. So
(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})(X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2})=0
since
M,N\subset\ker\epsilon
. Consequently,

(3.13)

\begin{split}(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})\widetilde{\Delta}(X)&=(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})(\widetilde{\Delta}(X)+X^{\prime}_{1}\otimes X^{\prime}_{2})\\
&=(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(X)\\
&=X.\end{split}

A symmetric computation gives
(\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon)\widetilde{\Delta}(X)=X
. Hence we get
(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})\widetilde{\Delta}=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon)\widetilde{\Delta}=\operatorname{id}
.

We complete the proof of the proposition.
∎

Using Proposition
3.6
, we can now derive an interesting and important result. We need the following two lemmas. The first generalizes
[
17
, Corollary 5.2.12]
and is proved by a similar method.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
H=\oplus_{n\geq 0}H_{n}
be a connected graded braided bialgebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
that is generated by
H_{1}
. Then
H
is a braided Hopf algebra.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
H
admits an antipode. Let
\eta
and
\varepsilon
be the unit and counit of
H
respectively, and let

\mathcal{S}=\sum_{l\geq 0}(\eta\varepsilon-\operatorname{id})^{*l},

where
*
denotes the convolution product and we set
(\eta\varepsilon-\operatorname{id})^{*0}=\eta\varepsilon
.
Let
1
be the identity element of
H
. Since
(\eta\varepsilon-\operatorname{id})(1)=0
, for any homogenous element
X\in H_{l}
we have
(\eta\varepsilon-\operatorname{id})^{*m}(X)=0
whenever
m>l
. Hence
\mathcal{S}(X)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}(\eta\varepsilon-\operatorname{id})^{*i}(X)
is a finite sum, and
\mathcal{S}
is well defined linear endomorphism of
H
. Set
f=\eta\varepsilon-\operatorname{id}
, we have

\begin{split}(\operatorname{id}*\mathcal{S})(X)&=((\eta\varepsilon-f)*\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}f^{*i})(X)\\
&=\Big(\eta\varepsilon({X_{1}})-f({X_{1}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{0\leq i\leq l}f^{*i}({X_{2}})\Big)\\
&=\sum_{0\leq i\leq l}f^{*i}({X})-\sum_{0\leq i\leq l}f^{*i+1}({X})\\
&=\eta\varepsilon(X).\end{split}

Here the last equality uses the fact that
f^{*l+1}({X})=0
. Thus
\operatorname{id}*\mathcal{S}=\eta\varepsilon
. A symmetric argument gives
\mathcal{S}*\operatorname{id}=\eta\varepsilon
. Therefore
\mathcal{S}
is an antipode for
H
, and consequently
H
is a braided Hopf algebra.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of simple objects with
\theta\geq 3
, and let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a graded pre-Nichols algebra of
V
with
\operatorname{deg}(V_{i})=e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\theta
. Let
U=\operatorname{ad}_{V_{i}}(\operatorname{ad}_{V_{j}}(V_{k}))\subset\mathcal{P}(V)
, where the indices
i,j,k\in\{1,2,\ldots,\theta\}
are pairwise distinct. If
U\neq 0
, then for every nonzero element
\mathbbm{X}\in U
the coproduct takes the form

\Delta(\mathbbm{X})=1\otimes\mathbbm{X}+\mathbbm{X}\otimes 1+\sum_{l\in\mathcal{J}}{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2},

where for all
l\in\mathcal{J}
(if
\mathcal{J}\neq\emptyset
) we have

(3.14)

\operatorname{deg}({{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1})\in\{e_{i},e_{j},e_{i}+e_{j}\},\ \ \ \operatorname{deg}({{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2})\in\{e_{i}+e_{k},e_{j}+e_{k},e_{k}\}.

Proof.

Assume the
G
-degree of
V_{l}
is
g_{l}\in G
for
1\leq l\leq\theta
. Without loss of generality, write
\mathbbm{X}=\operatorname{ad}_{X}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z))
with
X\in V_{i},Y\in V_{j},Z\in V_{k}
. Direct calculation yields

(3.15)

\begin{split}\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z))&=\Delta(YZ-g_{j}\triangleright ZY)\\
&=(Y\otimes 1+1\otimes Y)(Z\otimes 1+1\otimes Z)\\
&\ \ \ \ -(g_{j}\triangleright Z\otimes 1+1\otimes g_{j}\triangleright Z)(Y\otimes 1+1\otimes Y)\\
&=\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)+Y\otimes Z-g_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{j}\triangleright Z,\end{split}

and

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\Delta(g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z))=g_{i}\triangleright\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z))\\
&=g_{i}\triangleright(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)+Y\otimes Z-g_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{j}\triangleright Z)\\
&=g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\otimes 1+1\otimes g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\\
&\ \ \ +\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})g_{i}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{i}\triangleright Z-\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})g_{i}\triangleright(g_{k}\triangleright Y)\otimes g_{i}\triangleright(g_{j}\triangleright Z)\\
&=g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\otimes 1+1\otimes g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)+\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})g_{i}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{i}\triangleright Z\\
&\ \ \ -\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})\Phi_{g_{j}}(g_{i},g_{k})\Phi_{g_{k}}(g_{i},g_{j})(g_{i}g_{k})\triangleright Y\otimes(g_{i}g_{j})\triangleright Z.\end{split}

Consequently, we have

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)))\\
&=\Delta(X\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)-g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)X)\\
&=(X\otimes 1+1\otimes X)\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times\big[\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)+Y\otimes Z-g_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{j}\triangleright Z\big]\\
&\ \ -\big[g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\otimes 1+1\otimes g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)+\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})g_{i}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{i}\triangleright Z\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})\Phi_{g_{j}}(g_{i},g_{k})\Phi_{g_{k}}(g_{i},g_{j})(g_{i}g_{k})\triangleright Y\otimes(g_{i}g_{j})\triangleright Z\big]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times(X\otimes 1+1\otimes X)\\
&=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z))+\operatorname{ad}_{X}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z))\otimes 1\\
&\ \ +\big[X\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)-(g_{j}g_{k})\triangleright X\otimes g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)\big]\\
&\ \ +\big[\Phi(g_{i},g_{j},g_{k})\Phi^{-1}(g_{j},g_{i},g_{k})g_{i}\triangleright Y\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X}Z\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\Phi(g_{i},g_{k},g_{j})g_{i}g_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X}(g_{j}\triangleright Z)\big]\\
&\ \ +\Phi(g_{i},g_{j},g_{k})\big[XY\otimes Z-Xg_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{j}\triangleright Z-g_{i}\triangleright Yg_{k}\triangleright X\otimes g_{i}\triangleright Z\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\Phi(g_{j},g_{i},g_{k})\Phi(g_{k},g_{i},g_{j})g_{i}g_{k}\triangleright Yg_{k}\triangleright X\otimes g_{i}g_{j}\triangleright Z\big].\end{split}

Define

(3.18)

\displaystyle{{}^{1}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{1}\mathbbm{X}}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z)-(g_{j}g_{k})\triangleright X\otimes g_{i}\triangleright\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(Z),

(3.19)

\begin{split}{{}^{2}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{2}\mathbbm{X}}_{2}&\ \ =\ \ \Phi(g_{i},g_{j},g_{k})\Phi^{-1}(g_{j},g_{i},g_{k})g_{i}\triangleright Y\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X}Z\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ -\Phi(g_{i},g_{k},g_{j})g_{i}g_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X}(g_{j}\triangleright Z),\end{split}

and

(3.20)

\begin{split}&{{}^{3}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{3}\mathbbm{X}}_{2}\\
&=\Phi(g_{i},g_{j},g_{k})\big[XY\otimes Z-Xg_{k}\triangleright Y\otimes g_{j}\triangleright Z-g_{i}\triangleright Yg_{k}\triangleright X\otimes g_{i}\triangleright Z\\
&\ \ \ \ +\Phi(g_{j},g_{i},g_{k})\Phi(g_{k},g_{i},g_{j})g_{i}g_{k}\triangleright Yg_{k}\triangleright X\otimes g_{i}g_{j}\triangleright Z\big].\end{split}

Set
\mathcal{J}=\{l|{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2}\neq 0,1\leq l\leq 3\}
. Then

\Delta(\mathbbm{X})=1\otimes\mathbbm{X}+\mathbbm{X}\otimes 1+\sum_{l\in\mathcal{J}}{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2},

and whenever
\mathcal{J}\neq\emptyset
it is clear that
{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}
and
{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2}
satisfy condition (
3.14
).
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of simple objects with
\theta\geq 3
, and let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a graded pre-Nichols algebra of
V
with
\operatorname{deg}(V_{i})=e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\theta
. Define
W=\operatorname{ad}_{V_{i}}(\operatorname{ad}_{V_{j}}(V_{k}))+\operatorname{ad}_{V_{j}}(\operatorname{ad}_{V_{i}}(V_{k}))\subset\mathcal{P}(V)
, where the indices
i,j,k\in\{1,2,\ldots,\theta\}
are pairwise distinct, and let
A(W)
be the subalgebra of
\mathcal{P}(V)
generated by
W
. If
W
is nonzero, then
A(W)
is a pre-Nichols algebra of
W
.

Proof.

Suppose that
W\neq 0
, then
\operatorname{Deg}(W)=\{e_{i}+e_{j}+e_{k}\}
. Let
\mathbbm{X}\in W
be a nonzero element. By Lemma
3.8
we have

(3.21)

\Delta(\mathbbm{X})=1\otimes\mathbbm{X}+\mathbbm{X}\otimes 1+\sum_{l\in\mathcal{J}}{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1}\otimes{{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2},

where
\operatorname{deg}({{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{1})\in\{e_{i},e_{j},e_{i}+e_{j}\}
and
\operatorname{deg}({{}^{l}\mathbbm{X}}_{2})\in\{e_{i}+e_{k},e_{j}+e_{k},e_{k}\}
for each
l\in\mathcal{J}
if
\mathcal{J}\neq\emptyset
.

Define

\begin{split}&S=\{k(e_{i}+e_{j}+e_{k})+ae_{i}+be_{j}|k,a,b\in\mathbb{N}_{0},a+b\neq 0\},\\
&T=\{k(e_{i}+e_{j}+e_{k})+ae_{i}+be_{j}+ce_{k}|k,a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}_{0},ab=0,c>0,a+b\leq c\}.\end{split}

Clearly, both
S
and
T
are additively closed, and
\{e_{i},e_{j},e_{i}+e_{j}\}\subset S
,
\{e_{i}+e_{k},e_{j}+e_{k},e_{k}\}\subset T
. Now, set

(3.22)

\begin{split}&M=\{X\in\mathcal{P}(V)|\operatorname{deg}(X)\in S\}\\
&N=\{X\in\mathcal{P}(V)|\operatorname{deg}(X)\in T\}.\end{split}

Because
S
and
T
are closed under addition, we have
M^{2}\subset M
and
N^{2}\subset N
. Since

\operatorname{Deg}(A(W))=\{n(e_{i}+e_{j}+e_{k})|n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

it is immediate that
\operatorname{Deg}(A(W))\cap\operatorname{Deg}(M)=\emptyset,Deg(A(W))\cap\operatorname{Deg}(N)=\emptyset.

Moreover, because
\operatorname{Deg}(A(W))+S\subset S
and
\operatorname{Deg}(A(W))+T\subset T
, the spaces
M
and
N
are
A(W)
-bimodules under the product of
\mathcal{P}(V)
.

We now show that

\Delta(A(W))\subset A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N.

From
3.21
, we already have
\Delta(W)\subset A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N
. Assume inductively that
\Delta(W^{i})\subset A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then we have

\begin{split}\Delta(W^{n})&=\Delta(W)\Delta(W^{n-1})\subset(A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N)(A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N)\\
&\subset(A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N).\end{split}

Since
A(W)
is generated by
W
, it follows that
\Delta(A(W))\subset A(W)\otimes A(W)+M\otimes N
.

Applying Proposition
3.6
, we obtain that
A(W)
becomes a braided bialgebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
under the new comultiplication
\widetilde{\Delta}=P\circ\Delta
, where
P:A\otimes A+M\otimes N\to A\otimes A
is the canonical projection. Finally,
A(W)
is connected graded braided bialgebra generated by
W
, and every nonzero element in
W
is primitive under
\widetilde{\Delta}
. Thus, by Lemma
3.7
,
A(W)
is a pre-Nichols algebras.
∎

4.
Nichols algebras of diagonal type

In this section, we classify finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras of diagonal type in the category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
. We also provide a classification of finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras arising from simple objects. These results serve as a foundation for the further study of finite GK-dimensional pre-Nichols algebras.

Recall that an ordinary braided vector space is a pair
(V,c)
, where
V
is a vector space and
c\in GL(V\otimes V)

satisfies the braid equation:

(4.1)

(\operatorname{id}\otimes c)(c\otimes\operatorname{id})(\operatorname{id}\otimes c)=(c\otimes\operatorname{id})(\operatorname{id}\otimes c)(c\otimes\operatorname{id}).

For an object
V
in the category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
, its braiding
c
does not generally satisfy (
4.1
). Instead, it fulfills the following twisted analogue:

(4.2)

\begin{split}&(\operatorname{id}\otimes c)\circ a_{V,V,V}\circ(c\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ a^{-1}_{V,V,V}\circ(\operatorname{id}\otimes c)\circ a_{V,V,V}\\
&=a_{V,V,V}\circ(c\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ a^{-1}_{V,V,V}\circ(\operatorname{id}\otimes c)\circ a_{V,V,V}\circ(c\otimes\operatorname{id}),\end{split}

where
a
denotes the associativity constraint of
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
, determined by the
3
-cocycle
\Phi
(see definition
2.1
). Consequently, when
\Phi
is nontrivial, an object
V
in the category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
is not an ordinary braided vector space.

In
[
8
]
, Angiono and García Iglesias classified all finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras of ordinary braided vector spaces of diagonal type.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
8
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional braided vector space of diagonal type. Then
B(V)
is finite GK-dimensional if and only if the corresponding root system is finite, that is, an arithmetic root system.

Applying the theorem, we obtain a classification of finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras of diagonal type in the category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a finite-dimensional object of diagonal type. Then
B(V)
has finite GKdim if and only if the corresponding root system is finite, i.e., it coincides with an arithmetic root system.

Proof.

Let
G_{V}
be the support group of
V
. Because
V
is of diagonal type, Lemma
2.5
implies that
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
. By Lemma
3.1
,
B(V)
is a Nichols algebra in

{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
. Let
\pi:\widehat{G_{V}}\to G_{V}
be the canonical homomorphism defined in (
2.14
). According to Corollary
3.5
,
B(V)
is twist equivalent to a Nichols algebra
B(V^{J})
in the ordinary Yetter-Drinfeld category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}\mathcal{YD}
, where
J
is a
2
-cochain on
\widehat{G_{V}}
satisfying
\partial J=(\pi^{*}\Phi_{G_{V}})^{-1}
.

Assume that
B(V)
has finite GKdim. Then, by Lemma
2.10
,
B(V^{J})
also has finite GKdim. Therefore Theorem
4.1
implies that the root system
\bigtriangleup(B(V^{J}))
is finite. Since twisting does not change the root system by Proposition
2.9
, we have
\bigtriangleup(B(V))=\bigtriangleup(B(V^{J}))
, consequently
\bigtriangleup(B(V))
is finite. Conversely, if
\bigtriangleup(B(V))
is finite, then
\bigtriangleup(B(V^{J}))
is finite as well, and Theorem
4.1
yields that
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V^{J}))
is finite. Since twisting does not change GKdim,
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))
is also finite. This complete the proof.
∎

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a finite-dimensional object of diagonal type. Recall that the generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is said to be of finite type when the root system
\bigtriangleup(B(V))
is finite. An immediate consequence of Theorem
4.2
is the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a finite-dimensional object of diagonal type such that
B(V)
has finite GKdim. Then
\mathcal{D}(V)
is of finite type.

In
[
16
]
, Heckenberger gave a complete list of generalized Dynkin diagrams of finite type for Nichols algebras of diagonal type in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbbm{G}}\mathcal{YD}
, where
\mathbbm{G}
is an abelian group. In what follows, we denote by
\mathfrak{D}
the set of generalized Dynkin diagrams of finite type that appear in that list.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a finite-dimensional object of diagonal type. If the generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is of finite type, then
\mathcal{D}(V)
belongs to
\mathfrak{D}
.

Proof.

Because
V
is of diagonal type, Proposition
3.6
show that
B(V)
is twist equivalent to a Nichols algebra
B(V^{J})
in the ordinary Yetter-Drinfeld module category
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}\mathcal{YD}}
.
Assume that
\mathcal{D}(V)
is of finite type, then
\Delta(B(V))
is finite. By Proposition
2.9
, the root system remains unchanged under twisting, so
\Delta(B(V^{J}))
is also finite. Hence
\mathcal{D}(V^{J})
is of finite type, and therefore belongs to
\mathfrak{D}
. Applying Proposition
2.9
once more, the diagrams
\mathcal{D}(V)
and
\mathcal{D}(V^{J})
coincide. Thus
\mathcal{D}(V)
belongs to
\mathfrak{D}
.
∎

A generalized Dynkin diagram is called an
n
-cycle if it has vertices
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and two vertices
i,j
are connected precisely when
|i-j|=1
or
\{i,j\}=\{1,n\}
. The following corollary plays an important role in the study of GKdim of Nichols algebras.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be an object of diagonal type. If the generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
contains an
n
-cycle with
n\geq 4
, then
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{D}(V)
contains an
n
-cycle with
n\geq 4
. Comparing with the set
\mathfrak{D}
of generalized Dynkin diagrams of finite type listed in
[
16
]
, we see that
\mathcal{D}(V)
does not belong to
\mathfrak{D}
, hence it is not of finite type. By Corollary
4.3
, we obtain
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.
∎

Since every simple object in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is necessarily of diagonal type, we can apply Theorem
4.2
to classify all finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras of simple objects.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a simple object with
\operatorname{dim}(V)\geq 2
, and let
g=g_{V}
. Then
B(V)
has finite GKdim if and only if
V
is one of the following types:

(T1)

g\triangleright v=v
for all
v\in V
.

(T2)

g\triangleright v=-v
for all
v\in V
.

(T3)

g\triangleright v=\zeta_{3}v
for all
v\in V
and
\operatorname{dim}(V)=2
, where
\zeta_{3}
is a primitive third root of unity.

Proof.

Since
V
is simple, the action of its
G
-degree element
g
is given by
g\triangleright v=\zeta v
for all
v\in V
, where
\zeta\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
is a scalar. Let
n=\operatorname{dim}(V)
and fix a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of
V
. If
\zeta=1
, then
B(V)
is isomorphic to the symmetric algebra
S(V)
, whence
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\operatorname{dim}(V)
. If
\zeta=-1
, then
B(V)=T(V)/I
with

I=\langle X_{i}X_{j}+X_{j}X_{i},X_{i}^{2}|1\leq i\neq j\leq n\rangle.

So
B(V)
is a finite dimensional Nichols algebra and hence
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=0
.

Now assume that
\zeta\neq\pm 1
. If
n\geq 3
, then the generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
contains an
n
-cycle whose vertices are labelled by
\zeta
and whose edges are labelled by
\zeta^{2}
. Comparing with the set
\mathfrak{D}
of generalized Dynkin diagrams of finite type, we see that such a diagram does not appear in
\mathfrak{D}
. Therefore it is not of finite type, and consequently
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.

If
n=2
, the diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
has the form

{\rule[-12.8037pt]{0.0pt}{34.14319pt}\begin{picture}(0.0,3.0)\put(1.0,2.0){\circle{2.0}}\put(2.0,2.0){\line{1}{0}{10.0}}\put(13.0,2.0){\circle{2.0}}\put(1.0,5.0){\makebox[0.0pt]{\scriptsize$\zeta$}}\put(7.0,4.0){\makebox[0.0pt]{\scriptsize$\zeta^{2}$}}\put(13.0,5.0){\makebox[0.0pt]{\scriptsize$\zeta$}}\end{picture}}\quad.

A direct inspection of the list
\mathfrak{D}
shows that the only diagram of this shape that is of finite type is

{\rule[-12.8037pt]{0.0pt}{34.14319pt}\begin{picture}(0.0,3.0)\put(1.0,2.0){\circle{2.0}}\put(2.0,2.0){\line{1}{0}{10.0}}\put(13.0,2.0){\circle{2.0}}\put(1.0,5.0){\makebox[0.0pt]{\scriptsize$\zeta_{3}$}}\put(7.0,4.0){\makebox[0.0pt]{\scriptsize$\zeta_{3}^{-1}$}}\put(13.0,5.0){\makebox[0.0pt]{\scriptsize$\zeta_{3}$}}\end{picture}}\quad,

where
\zeta_{3}
is a primitive third root of unity. In this case,
\mathcal{D}(V)
is of Cartan type
A_{2}
with parameter
q=\zeta_{3}
, the Nichols algebra
B(V)
is finite-dimensional and thus
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=0
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a simple object with
\operatorname{dim}(V)\geq 2
. Then
B(V)
is finite-dimensional precisely when
V
is of type (T2) or (T3). This result has already been established in
[
26
]
.

5.
Minimal nondiagonal objects

In this section, we initiate the study of pre-Nichols algebras of nondiagonal type, where the so-called minimal nondiagonal objects play a central role. Recall that an object
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is said to be minimal nondiagonal if it is nondiagonal and every nonzero proper subobject of
V
is diagonal. We shall first analyze the Yetter-Drinfeld module structure of minimal nondiagonal objects. Afterward, we prove that if
V
is a minimal nondiagonal object satisfying
G_{V}=G
and
\operatorname{dim}(V)\geq 9
, then
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of
\theta
simple objects. If
\theta=1
or
2
, the support group of
V
is necessarily cyclic or a direct product of two cyclic groups, this forces
V
to be of diagonal type by Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.5
. Hence, if
V
is nondiagonal, we must have
\theta\geq 3
. This observation yields the following statement.

Lemma 5.1
.

Every nondiagonal object in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
must be a direct sum of at least
3
simple objects.

We now turn to the structure of minimal nondiagonal objects. The following result will be needed.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
26
, Lemma 4.5]

Suppose that the set
\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{\theta}\}
generates the group
G
and that

\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})=\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{k},g_{j})

for all
g_{i},g_{j},g_{k}\in\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{\theta}\},
then
\Phi
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of simple objects. If
V
is nondiagonal, then there exist indices
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\in\{1,2,\ldots,\theta\}
such that the subobject
V_{i_{1}}\oplus V_{i_{2}}\oplus V_{i_{3}}
is nondiagonal.

Proof.

Assume, to the contrary, that
V_{i_{1}}\oplus V_{i_{2}}\oplus V_{i_{3}}
is diagonal for every triple
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\in\{1,2,\ldots,\theta\}
. Choose bases
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}\}
,
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}\}
,
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}\}
of
V_{i_{1}},V_{i_{2}}
,
V_{i_{3}}
, respectively , such that their union is a diagonal basis of
V_{i_{1}}\oplus V_{i_{2}}\oplus V_{i_{3}}
. Let
g_{i}=g_{V_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq\theta
. For each
1\leq k\leq r
we have

g_{i_{2}}\triangleright X_{k}=p_{k}X_{k},\ \ g_{i_{3}}\triangleright X_{k}=q_{k}X_{k}

for certain constants
p_{k},q_{k}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. Then

\displaystyle\Phi_{g_{i_{1}}}(g_{i_{2}},g_{i_{3}})g_{i_{2}}g_{i_{3}}\triangleright X_{k}=g_{i_{2}}\triangleright(g_{i_{3}}\triangleright X_{k})=p_{k}q_{k}X_{k},

\displaystyle\Phi_{g_{i_{1}}}(g_{i_{3}},g_{i_{2}})g_{i_{2}}g_{i_{3}}\triangleright X_{k}=g_{i_{3}}\triangleright(g_{i_{2}}\triangleright X_{k})=p_{k}q_{k}X_{k}.

Thus
\Phi_{g_{i_{1}}}(g_{i_{2}},g_{i_{3}})=\Phi_{g_{i_{1}}}(g_{i_{3}},g_{i_{2}})
. Since this equality holds for all triples of indices, Lemma
5.2
implies that
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
. By Lemma
2.5
, the whole object
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}
would be diagonal, which is a contradiction to the hypothesis that
V
is nondiagonal.
∎

We can now give a precise characterisation of minimal nondiagonal objects.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of
\theta
simple objects. Then
V
is a minimal nondiagonal object if and only if
\theta=3
and the restricted
3
-cocycle
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is nonabelian on the support group
G_{V}
.

Proof.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\theta}
is a direct sum of simple objects. Assume
V
is a minimal nondiagonal object. Lemma
5.3
implies that
\theta\leq 3
, while Lemma
5.1
gives
\theta\geq 3
. Hence
\theta=3
and we can write
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
. By Lemma
2.5
,
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
since
V
is nondiagonal.

Conversely, suppose
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
is a direct sum of three simple objects and
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
. Then Lemma
2.5
implies that
V
is nondiagonal. Moreover, by Lemma
5.1
, every nonzero proper subobject of
V
must be diagonal. Therefore
V
is minimal nondiagonal.
∎

The following corollary follows directly from Lemma
5.3
and Proposition
5.4
.

Corollary 5.5
.

(1)

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a minimal nondiagonal object with
G_{V}=G
. Then
G
can be generated by three elements.

(2)

Every nondiagonal object in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
contains at least one minimal nondiagonal subobject.

In our earlier work
[
22
]
, we studied the structure of twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
that decompose into a direct sum of three simple objects and satisfy
G_{V}=G
. This study essentially characterizes the structure of minimal nondiagonal objects.

Proposition 5.6
.

[
22
, Lemma 3.5, Proposition 3.6]

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of simple objects with
G_{V}=G
. Denote
g_{i}=g_{V_{i}}\in G
and
m_{i}=|g_{i}|
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\operatorname{dim}(V_{1})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{2})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{3})=n
, where
n
is the order of
\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}
.

(2)

If
V
is nondiagonal, then
n>1
, and for a fixed
i\in\{1,2,3\}
there exists a basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
of
V_{i}
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle g_{i}\triangleright X_{l}=\alpha_{i}X_{l},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n;

(5.2)

\displaystyle g_{j}\triangleright X_{l}=\beta_{i}(\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{k})}{{\Phi}_{g_{i}}(g_{k},g_{j})})^{l-1}X_{l},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n;

(5.3)

\displaystyle g_{k}\triangleright X_{l}=X_{l+1},\ g_{k}\triangleright X_{n}=\gamma_{i}X_{1},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n-1.

Here
j\neq k\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}
, and the scalars
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
satisfy

(5.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{m_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m_{i}-1}\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{i},g_{i}^{l}),

(5.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{i}^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m_{j}-1}\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{j},g_{j}^{l}),

(5.6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}^{\frac{m_{k}}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m_{k}-1}\Phi_{g_{i}}(g_{k},g_{k}^{l}).

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
V
be a minimal nondiagonal object in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
satisfying
G_{V}=G
. Then
\operatorname{dim}(V)\geq 6
and each simple subobject of
V
has dimension
\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{dim}(V)
.

It is worth noting that in Proposition
5.6
, the dimensions of
V_{1},V_{2}
and
V_{3}
are also equal to the order of either
\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{3},g_{1})}{{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{1},g_{3})}
or
\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{1},g_{2})}{{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{2},g_{1})}
. This equivalence follows from the following identities (see
[
22
, Lemma 3.4]
):

(5.7)

\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}=\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{3},g_{1})}{{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{1},g_{3})}=\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{1},g_{2})}{{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{2},g_{1})}.

Based on Corollary
5.7
, we can introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group that can be generated by
3
-elements, and let
\Phi
be a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G
. For each integer
n\geq 2
, denote
\mathcal{F}_{n}
be the set of minimal nondiagonal objects
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
such that
G_{V}=G
and
\operatorname{dim}(V)=3n
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. If for some
i\in\{1,2,3\}
the simple object
V_{i}
is not of the types (T1)-(T3) in Theorem
4.6
, then
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V_{i}))=\infty
and hence also
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
, because
B(V_{i})
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded subalgebra of
B(V)
. Therefore, in the following we shall only consider the case in which
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
V_{3}
are all of types (T1)-(T3).

For the rest of this paper, let
\mathcal{F}^{T}_{n}\subset\mathcal{F}_{n}
denote the subset

(5.8)

\mathcal{F}^{T}_{n}=\{V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}|\ V_{i}\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{of}\ \mathrm{type}\ (T1),(T2)\ \mathrm{or}\ (T3),1\leq i\leq 3\}.

For each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, we further set

(5.9)

\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(Ti)}=\{V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}|V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\ \mathrm{are}\ \mathrm{all}\ \mathrm{of}\ \mathrm{type}\ (Ti)\}.

Throughout the remainder of this section, we assume that
G
can be generated by
3
-elements and that
\Phi
is a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G
. We now prove that if
V
is a minimal nondiagonal object satisfying
G_{V}=G
and
\operatorname{dim}(V)\geq 9
, then
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Assume
n\geq 3
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}\neq\emptyset
. Then
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
for all
V\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, and denote
g_{i}=g_{V_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. By the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, we have

G=G_{V}=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
and
n=|\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}|\geq 3.

We shall prove that
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}))=\infty
, which forces
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
because
B(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded subalgebra of
B(V)
.

Set
m_{i}=|g_{i}|
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. According to Proposition
5.6
, there exists a basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
of
V_{1}
such that

(5.10)

\displaystyle g_{1}\triangleright X_{l}=\alpha_{1}X_{l},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n;

(5.11)

\displaystyle g_{2}\triangleright X_{l}=\beta_{1}(\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})})^{l-1}X_{l},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n;

(5.12)

\displaystyle g_{3}\triangleright X_{l}=X_{l+1},\ g_{3}\triangleright X_{n}=\gamma_{1}X_{1},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n-1.

Here
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
satisfy

\alpha_{1}^{m_{1}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m_{1}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{1},g_{1}^{l}),\ \beta_{1}^{m_{2}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m_{2}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{2}^{l}),\ \gamma_{1}^{\frac{m_{3}}{n}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m_{3}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{3}^{l}).

Similarly, there exists a basis
\{Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
of
V_{2}
such that

(5.13)

\displaystyle g_{2}\triangleright Y_{l}=\alpha_{2}Y_{l},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n;

(5.14)

\displaystyle g_{1}\triangleright Y_{l}=\beta_{2}(\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{1},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{3},g_{1})})^{l-1}Y_{l},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n;

(5.15)

\displaystyle g_{3}\triangleright Y_{l}=Y_{l+1},\ g_{3}\triangleright Y_{n}=\gamma_{2}Y_{1},\ \ 1\leq l\leq n-1.

Here
\alpha_{2},\beta_{2},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
satisfy

\alpha_{2}^{m_{2}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m_{2}-1}\Phi_{g_{2}}(g_{2},g_{2}^{l}),\ \beta_{2}^{m_{1}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m_{1}-1}\Phi_{g_{2}}(g_{1},g_{1}^{l}),\ \gamma_{2}^{\frac{m_{3}}{n}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m_{3}-1}\Phi_{g_{2}}(g_{3},g_{3}^{l}).

From (
5.10
)-(
5.11
) and (
5.13
)-(
5.14
), the set
\{X_{1},\ldots X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
forms a diagonal basis of
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
.
Let
q=\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}
and denote by
\mathcal{R}
the braiding on
V
. Then for all
1\leq s,t\leq n
we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X_{s}\otimes Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\beta_{2}q^{t-1}Y_{t}\otimes X_{s},

(5.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(Y_{t}\otimes X_{s})

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}q^{s-1}X_{s}\otimes Y_{t}.

Consequently, in the generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
, the vertices corresponding to
X_{s}
and
Y_{t}
are connected precisely when

\beta_{1}\beta_{2}q^{s+t-2}\neq 1.

Assume that
\beta_{1}\beta_{2}
is not an
n
-th root of unity. Then the vertices corresponding to
X_{s}
and
Y_{t}
are connected for all
1\leq s,t\leq n
. Hence
\mathcal{D}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
contains a
2n
-cycle, Corollary
4.5
forces
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}))=\infty
.

We now assume that
\beta_{1}\beta_{2}
is an
n
-th root of unity. If
n=3
, then
|q|=3
, so there exists a number
0\leq k<3
such that
\beta_{1}\beta_{2}=q^{k}
. In this case, vertices corresponding to
X_{s}
and
Y_{t}
are connected if and only if

k+s+t-2\neq 0\ (\operatorname{mod}3).

Since
k\in\{0,1,2\}
, define
Y^{\prime}_{1}=Y_{3-k}
,

Y^{\prime}_{2}=\begin{cases}Y_{1},&\ \mathrm{if}\ k=0,\\
Y_{4-k},&\ \mathrm{if}\ k=1,2\end{cases}

and

Y^{\prime}_{3}=\begin{cases}Y_{2-k},&\ \mathrm{if}\ k=0,1,\\
Y_{3},&\ \mathrm{if}\ k=2.\end{cases}

Clearly
\{Y^{\prime}_{1},Y^{\prime}_{2},Y^{\prime}_{3}\}=\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3}\}
is a basis of
V_{2}
.
Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
be the vertices of
\mathcal{D}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
corresponding to
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
, and
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
those corresponding to
Y^{\prime}_{1},Y^{\prime}_{2},Y^{\prime}_{3}
. A direct verification shows that
a_{1}
is connected to
b_{1}
and
b_{3}
,
a_{2}
to
b_{3}
and
b_{2}
, and
a_{3}
to
b_{2}
and
b_{1}
. Therefore
\mathcal{D}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
contains a
6
-cycle, whence
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}))=\infty
by Corollary
4.5
.

Finally, suppose
n>3
. Let
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
be the integer satisfying
\beta_{1}\beta_{2}=q^{k}
. Then the vertices corresponding to
X_{s}
and
Y_{t}
are connected exactly when

k+s+t-2\neq 0\ (\operatorname{mod}n).

Define

Y^{\prime\prime}_{1}=\begin{cases}Y_{n-k+2},&\ \mathrm{if}\ 2<k<n,\\
Y_{2-k},&\ \mathrm{if}\ 0\leq k\leq 2,\end{cases}

and

Y^{\prime\prime}_{2}=\begin{cases}Y_{n-k+3},&\ \mathrm{if}\ 3<k<n,\\
Y_{3-k},&\ \mathrm{if}\ 0\leq k\leq 3.\end{cases}

Let
a_{1},a_{2}
be the vertices of
\mathcal{D}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
corresponding to
X_{1},X_{2}
, and
b_{1},b_{2}
those corresponding to
Y^{\prime\prime}_{1},Y^{\prime\prime}_{2}
. One easily check that both
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are connected to
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
. Thus

\mathcal{D}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
contains a
4
-cycle, we also obtain
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}))=\infty
by Corollary
4.5
.
∎

Compared with the families
\mathcal{F}_{n}
for
n\geq 3
, the study of GK-dimension for Nichols algebras generated by objects in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
poses greater challenges. This problem will be addressed in the next two sections.

6.
Nichols algebras of objects in
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{(T2)}

Throughout this section, let
G
be a finite abelian group that can be generated by
3
-elements, endowed with a nonabelian
3
-cocycle
\Phi
, and assume that
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{(T2)}
is nonempty.

6.1.
A standard form

In this subsection, we take an object
V\in\mathcal{F}_{2}^{(T2)}
in standard form (see Subsection 2.3 for the definition). Write
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
as a direct sum of simple objects, and let
g_{i}=g_{V_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Thus
V_{1},V_{2}
,
V_{3}
are all of type (T2), and because
V
is in standard form, we have

G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\langle g_{2}\rangle\times\langle g_{3}\rangle.

Set
m_{i}=|g_{i}|
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. The main result of this subsection is the following.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a
3
-cocycle on
G
of the form

(6.1)

\Phi(g_{1}^{i_{1}}g_{2}^{i_{2}}g_{3}^{i_{3}},g_{1}^{j_{1}}g_{2}^{j_{2}}g_{3}^{j_{3}},g_{1}^{k_{1}}g_{2}^{k_{2}}g_{3}^{k_{3}})=\zeta_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}^{ci_{1}j_{2}k_{3}},

where
1\leq c<(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
,

, and
0\leq i_{3},j_{3},k_{3}<m_{3}
. Then we have
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty.

We shall prove the proposition through a sequence of lemmas. Throughout the remainder of this subsection, the
3
-cocycle
\Phi
is assumed to be of the form (
6.1
).

According to Proposition
5.6
, the object
V_{1}
has a basis
\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
satisfying

(6.2)

\displaystyle g_{1}\triangleright X_{i}=-X_{i},\ i=1,2,

(6.3)

\displaystyle g_{2}\triangleright X_{1}=\beta_{1}X_{1},g_{2}\triangleright X_{2}=-\beta_{1}X_{2},

(6.4)

\displaystyle g_{3}\triangleright X_{1}=X_{2},g_{3}\triangleright X_{2}=\gamma_{1}X_{1},

with constants
\beta_{1},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
subject to
\beta_{1}^{m_{2}}=1,\ \gamma_{1}^{\frac{m_{3}}{2}}=1
. Here (
6.2
) follows from the fact that
V_{1}
is of type (T2).

Similarly,
V_{2}
has a basis
\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
for which

(6.5)

\displaystyle g_{2}\triangleright Y_{i}=-Y_{i},\ i=1,2,

(6.6)

\displaystyle g_{1}\triangleright Y_{1}=\beta_{2}Y_{1},g_{1}\triangleright Y_{2}=-\beta_{2}Y_{2},

(6.7)

\displaystyle g_{3}\triangleright Y_{1}=Y_{2},g_{3}\triangleright Y_{2}=\gamma_{2}Y_{1},

with
\beta_{2},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
satisfying
\beta_{2}^{m_{1}}=1,\ \gamma_{2}^{\frac{m_{3}}{2}}=1
.

Finally,
V_{3}
has a basis
\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\}
such that

(6.8)

\displaystyle g_{3}\triangleright Z_{i}=-Z_{i},\ i=1,2,

(6.9)

\displaystyle g_{2}\triangleright Z_{1}=\beta_{3}Z_{1},g_{2}\triangleright Z_{2}=-\beta_{3}Z_{2},

(6.10)

\displaystyle g_{1}\triangleright Z_{1}=Z_{2},g_{1}\triangleright Z_{2}=\gamma_{3}Z_{1},

where
\beta_{3},\gamma_{3}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
are constants with
\beta_{3}^{m_{2}}=1,\gamma_{3}^{\frac{m_{1}}{2}}=1.

-cycle

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that

(6.13)

\beta_{1}\beta_{2}=1,\ \beta_{3}\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}=\pm 1,\ {\gamma_{1}\gamma_{3}}=1,

then
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty.

Proof.

The proof will be organized in four steps. We note that
B(V)
is
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
-graded with
\operatorname{deg}(V_{i})=e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, where
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
is a standard basis of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
.

Step 1
. We prove that

(6.14)

\Phi(g_{1}^{i_{1}}g_{2}^{i_{2}}g_{3}^{i_{3}},g_{1}^{j_{1}}g_{2}^{j_{2}}g_{3}^{j_{3}},g_{1}^{k_{1}}g_{2}^{k_{2}}g_{3}^{k_{3}})=(-1)^{i_{1}j_{2}k_{3}}.

From the explicit formula (
6.1
), a direct calculation yields

\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}=\frac{\Phi(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\Phi(g_{2},g_{3},g_{1})\Phi(g_{3},g_{1},g_{2})}{\Phi(g_{3},g_{1},g_{2})\Phi(g_{1},g_{3},g_{2})\Phi(g_{3},g_{2},g_{1})}=\Phi(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}).

On the other hand, because
\operatorname{dim}(V_{1})=|\frac{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}|=2
, we have
|\Phi(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})|=2
.
Hence

\Phi(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})=\zeta_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}^{c}=-1.

Therefore the
3
-cocycle
\Phi
necessarily takes the form (
6.14
).

Step 2
. We show that the subspaces
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{2}),\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{3})
and
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{2}}(V_{3})
of
B(V)
are all
2
-dimensional.

First, we compute the coproduct of elements in
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{2}}(V_{3})
. Using (
3.15
) we obtain

(6.15)

\begin{split}\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1}))=&\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+Y_{1}\otimes Z_{1}-g_{3}\triangleright Y_{1}\otimes g_{2}\triangleright Z_{1}\\
=&1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})\otimes 1+(Y_{1}-\beta_{3}Y_{2})\otimes Z_{1}.\end{split}

Similarly,

(6.16)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})+\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})\otimes 1+(Y_{1}+\beta_{3}Y_{2})\otimes Z_{2},

(6.17)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1})\otimes 1+(Y_{2}-\beta_{3}\gamma_{2}Y_{1})\otimes Z_{1},

(6.18)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2})+\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2})\otimes 1+(Y_{2}+\beta_{3}\gamma_{2}Y_{1})\otimes Z_{2}.

From
\beta_{3}^{2}\gamma_{2}=1
(by (
6.13
)) and equations (
6.15
) and (
6.17
), we obtain

(6.19)

\begin{split}&\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1}))\\
&=(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1}))\otimes 1+1\otimes(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1})).\end{split}

If
\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1})\neq 0
, it would be a nonzero primitive element of length
2
, which contradicts the definition of a Nichols algebra. Hence

(6.20)

\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})+\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{1})=0.

From (
6.16
) and (
6.18
), we similarly obtain

(6.21)

\begin{split}&\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})-\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2}))\\
&=(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})-\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2}))\otimes 1+1\otimes(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})-\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2})),\end{split}

and consequently

(6.22)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})-\beta_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(Z_{2})=0.

Therefore, by (
6.20
) and (
6.22
), the set
\{\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1}),\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})\}
forms a basis of
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{2}}(V_{3})
.

Next we consider
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{2})
. Analogous to (
6.15
)-(
6.18
) we obtain

(6.23)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{1}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{1})\otimes 1,

(6.24)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{2}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{2})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{2})\otimes 1+2X_{1}\otimes Y_{2},

(6.25)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{1}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{1})\otimes 1+2X_{2}\otimes Y_{1},

(6.26)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{2}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{2})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{2})\otimes 1.

Equations (
6.23
) and (
6.26
) force

(6.27)

\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{1})=0,\ \operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{2})=0.

Consequently
\{\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Y_{2}),\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Y_{1})\}
is a basis of
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{2})
.

Finally, we determine a basis of
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{3})
. Using (
3.15
) we have

(6.28)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{1}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{1})\otimes 1+X_{1}\otimes Z_{1}-X_{2}\otimes Z_{2},

(6.29)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2})\otimes 1+X_{1}\otimes Z_{2}-\gamma_{3}X_{2}\otimes Z_{1},

(6.30)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{1}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{1})\otimes 1+X_{2}\otimes Z_{1}-\gamma_{1}X_{1}\otimes Z_{2},

(6.31)

\displaystyle\Delta(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{2}))=1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{2})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{2})\otimes 1-X_{1}\otimes Z_{1}+X_{2}\otimes Z_{2}.

Because
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{3}=1
by (
6.13
), the last four identities imply that

(6.32)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{1})+\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{2})=0,

(6.33)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2})+\gamma_{3}\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(Z_{1})=0.

Hence
\{\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{1}),\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2})\}
is a basis of
\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{3})
.

Step 3.
Set
W=\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(\operatorname{ad}_{V_{2}}(V_{3}))+\operatorname{ad}_{V_{2}}(\operatorname{ad}_{V_{1}}(V_{3}))
. We will show that the four elements

\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{1})),\operatorname{ad}_{X_{2}}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(Z_{2})),\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{1}}(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2})),\operatorname{ad}_{Y_{2}}(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{1}}(Z_{2}))

are nonzero and linearly independent in

Proposition 7.4
.

For every

, we have
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(U))=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
U\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
. We can decompose

U=U_{1}\oplus U_{2}\oplus U_{3}
of simple objects. If
U\notin\mathcal{F}_{2}^{T}
, then for some

, the simple object

is not of type (T1), (T2) or (T3). Consequently,
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(U_{i}))=\infty
, this forces
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(U))=\infty
. On the other hand, if

, we also have

by Propositions

Corollary 7.5
.

Let

be a minimal nondiagonal object such that

8.
Classification

In this section, we prove that every pre-Nichols algebra of a nondiagonal object in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
has infinite GKdim. As applications, we obtain a classification of all finite-dimensional objects in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
whose Nichols algebras have finite GKdim.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a minimal nondiagonal object. By Lemma
3.1
,
V
is naturally an object in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
. It may happen, however, that
V
is no longer a minimal nondiagonal object in the latter category when
G_{V}\neq G
. We need the following lemma to address this situation.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a minimal nondiagonal object. Then there exists a minimal nondiagonal object
V^{\prime}
in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
such that
V^{\prime}\subset V
as objects in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
and
G_{V^{\prime}}=G_{V}
.

Proof.

Write
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
as a direct sum of simple objects in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, and set
g_{i}=g_{V_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then
G_{V}=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
. Because
V
is nondiagonal, Lemma
2.5
implies that
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
. For each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, choose a simple object
V^{\prime}_{i}\subset V_{i}
in the category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
and let
V^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{1}\oplus V^{\prime}_{2}\oplus V^{\prime}_{3}
. It is clear that
G_{V^{\prime}}=G_{V}
. Since
V^{\prime}
is a direct sum of
3
-simple objects and
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}=G_{V^{\prime}}
, Proposition
5.4
shows that
V^{\prime}
is a minimal nondiagonal object in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a minimal nondiagonal object. Then every pre-Nichols algebra of
V
has infinite GKdim.

Proof.

Because there exists a canonical projection from every pre-Nichols algebra
\mathcal{P}(V)
onto
B(V)
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
B(V)
is also a Nichols algebra in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}
. According to Lemma
8.1
, there exists a minimal nondiagonal object
V^{\prime}\subset V
in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}}
with
G_{V^{\prime}}=G_{V}
. By Corollary
7.5
, we have
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V^{\prime}))=\infty
. Since
B(V^{\prime})
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded subalgebra of
B(V)
, we conclude that
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.
∎

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
V\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
be a nondiagonal object. Then every pre-Nichols algebra of
V
has infinite GKdim.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
. Because
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is nondiagonal, Corollary
5.5
guarantees the existence of a minimal nondiagonal subobject
V^{\prime}\subset V
. By Proposition
8.2
, we have
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V^{\prime}))=\infty
. Consequently, we obtain
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))=\infty
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we classify all finite GK-dimensional Nichols algebras in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a finite-dimensional object. Then
B(V)
has finite GKdim if and only if it is of diagonal type and the corresponding root system is finite, namely, an arithmetic root system.

Proof.

Assume
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))<\infty
. By Theorem
8.3
,
B(V)
must be of diagonal type. Then Theorem
4.2
forces the root system
\Delta(B(V))
to be finite. Conversely, if
V
is diagonal and the corresponding root system is finite, by Theorem
4.2
again we obtain
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))<\infty
.
∎

This classification leads to a structural description of finite GK-dimensional coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras over finite abelian groups that are generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements.

Corollary 8.5
.

Let
H
be a coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebra over
G
generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements. If
H
is finitely generated and
\operatorname{GKdim}(H)<\infty
, then

H\cong B(V)\#\mathbbm{k}G

for some
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
whose generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is of finite type, where
\Phi
is a
3
-cocycle on
G
determined by the associator of
H
.

Proof.

Because
H
is coradically graded, it decomposes as a bosonization
H\cong R\#\mathbbm{k}G
, where
R=\oplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}
is a coradically graded connected braided Hopf algebra in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}{\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}}
, and
\Phi
is the
3
-cocycle on
G
determined by the associator of
H
. Let
P(R)
denote the subspace of primitive elements of
R
. Since
R
is coradically graded, we have
P(R)=R_{1}
. Furthermore, the assumption that
H
is generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements implies that
R
is generated by
P(R)=R_{1}
. Therefore,
R
is a pre-Nichols algebra of
R_{1}
such that
P(R)=R_{1}
, this forces
R
to be the Nichols algebra
B(R_{1})
.

Now let
V=R_{1}
, we obtain
H\cong B(V)\#\mathbbm{k}G
. If
H
is finitely generated and
\operatorname{GKdim}(H)<\infty
, then
V
must be finite dimensional and
\operatorname{GKdim}(B(V))<\infty
. By Theorem
8.4
,
B(V)
is of diagonal type and its root system is finite, hence the generalized Dynkin diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is of finite type.
∎

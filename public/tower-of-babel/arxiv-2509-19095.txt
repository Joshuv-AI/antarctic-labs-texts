Exact Lagrangian fillings of twist-spun torus links
Vincent Chen, Patton Galloway, James Hughes, Luciana Wei

Abstract
. We construct exact Lagrangian fillings of Legendrian torus links Λ ⁡ ( k , n − k ) \Lambda(k,n-k) that are fixed by a Legendrian loop that acts by 2 ​ π ​ ℓ / n 2\pi\ell/n rotation. Using these rotationally symmetric fillings, we produce fillings of the corresponding Legendrian twist-spun tori. Our construction is combinatorial in nature, relating symmetric weakly separated collections and plabic graphs to symmetric Legendrian weaves via the T-shift procedure of Casals, Le, Sherman-Bennett, and Weng. The main technical ingredient in this process is a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of maximal weakly separated collections of k k -element subsets of { 1 , … , n } \{1,\dots,n\} that are fixed by addition of ℓ \ell modulo n n .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context

The classification of exact Lagrangian fillings of Legendrian links is an important problem in low-dimensional contact and symplectic topology. While the unknot and the Hopf link are the only two Legendrians for which there exist a complete classification of fillings
[
EP96
,
Tho25
]
, the last decade has seen a significant advancement in our understanding of constructing and distinguishing fillings
[
EHK16
,
Pan17
,
TZ18
,
CN22
,
CSC24
]
. Many of the most recent works related to this problem, including
[
STWZ19
,
CG22
,
GSW24
,
ABL22
,
Hug23
,
CW24
]
, are closely related to the theory of cluster algebras. In particular,
[
Cas21
, Conjecture 5.1]
describes a conjectural ADE-type classification, giving a 1-1 correspondence between exact Lagrangian fillings of braid positive Legendrians and cluster seeds of corresponding cluster algebras. This is accompanied by
[
Cas21
, Conjecture 5.4]
, which posits a BCFG-type classification of fillings of Legendrian links admitting certain symmetries.

In
[
HR25
]
, the third author and Agniva Roy expanded this conjectural correspondence between symmetric fillings and cluster seeds to include exact Lagrangian fillings of a particular family of Legendrian surfaces known as Legendrian twist-spun tori. These twist-spun tori are first studied in
[
EK08
]
, and they are constructed as the mapping tori of a Legendrian loop of a Legendrian link, i.e., a Legendrian isotopy starting and ending at the same front diagram. While Legendrian loops appear in other contexts—most notably the first construction of infinite families of exact Lagrangian fillings in
[
CG22
]
—the twist-spun tori constructed from them give an alternative interpretation for
[
Cas21
, Conjecture 5.4]
through an adaptation of the cluster structures of
[
CW24
]
to this higher dimensional context.

From a cluster-theoretic perspective, twist-spinning corresponds to a folding operation on a cluster algebra analogous to folding Dynkin diagrams in classical Lie theory. Beyond the finite-type case, one particularly rich source of foldings arises from the action of the cyclic shift automorphism
\rho
on the Grassmannian
\text{Gr}(k,n)
, studied by Chris Fraser in
[
Fra20b
]
; see also
[
Kau24
]
. In loc. cit., Fraser gives a conjectural generalized cluster structure on (the distinguished component of) the cyclic symmetry locus
\text{Gr}(k,n)^{\rho^{\ell}}
, which the third author used to distinguish a collection of fillings of twist-spuns of
(2,n)
-torus links in
[
HR25
]
.

2.
Contact-geometric background

We begin with the necessary background on Legendrian links and their exact Lagrangian fillings.
The standard contact structure
\xi_{st}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the 2-plane field given as the kernel of the 1-form
\alpha_{\text{st}}=dz-ydx
. A link
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{st})
is Legendrian if
\Lambda
is everywhere tangent to
\xi_{st}
. As
\Lambda
can be assumed to avoid a point, we can equivalently consider Legendrians
\Lambda
contained in the contact 3-sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{3},\xi_{st})

[
Gei08
, Section 3.2]
. We consider Legendrian links up to Legendrian isotopy, i.e. ambient isotopy through a family of Legendrians.
In this work, we will depict a Legendrian link
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{st})
solely via the front projection
\Pi:(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by
\Pi(x,y,z)=(x,z)
.

The symplectization
\text{Symp}(M,\ker(\alpha))
of a contact manifold
(M,\ker(\alpha))
is the symplectic manifold
(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times M,d(e^{t}\alpha))
. Given two Legendrian links
\Lambda_{-},\Lambda_{+}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
, an exact Lagrangian cobordism
L\subseteq\text{Symp}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\ker(\alpha_{\text{st}}))
from
\Lambda_{-}
to
\Lambda_{+}
is a cobordism
\Sigma
such that there exists some
T>0
satisfying the following:

(1)

d(e^{t}\alpha_{\text{st}})|_{\Sigma}=0

(2)

\Sigma\cap((-\infty,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})=(-\infty,T]\times\Lambda_{-}

(3)

\Sigma\cap([T,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})=[T,\infty)\times\Lambda_{+}

(4)

e^{t}\alpha_{\text{st}}|_{\Sigma}=df
for some function
f:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
that is constant on
(-\infty,T]\times\Lambda_{-}
and
[T,\infty)\times\Lambda_{+}
.

An
exact Lagrangian filling
of the Legendrian link
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
is an exact Lagrangian cobordism
L
from
\emptyset
to
\Lambda
that is embedded in
\text{Symp}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\ker(\alpha_{\text{st}}))
. Equivalently, we consider
L
to be embedded in the symplectic 4-ball with boundary
\partial L
contained in contact
(\mathbb{S}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
.

2.1.
Legendrian twist-spun tori

Let
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
be a Legendrian link and

Legendrian loop
of

. That is,
\varphi_{t}
is a Legendrian isotopy satisfying
\Lambda=\varphi_{0}(\Lambda)=\varphi_{1}(\Lambda)
. For

with

\mathbb{R}_{z}\times T^{*}\mathbb{R}_{x\geq 0}\times T^{*}S^{1}\xhookrightarrow{}(\mathbb{R}^{5},\xi_{\text{st}})

[

, Section 1.3]

Definition 2.1
.

\varphi_{t}

Given a twist-spun

2.2.
Legendrian weaves

We now describe Legendrian weaves, a construction of Casals and Zaslow that can be used to produce exact Lagrangian fillings of a Legendrian link
[
CZ22
]
. The key idea of their construction is to combinatorially encode a
Legendrian
surface
\mathfrak{w}
in the 1-jet space
J^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{2})=T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{z}
by the singularities of its front projection in
\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{z}
. The Lagrangian projection
\pi:T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{Z}\to T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2}
of
\mathfrak{w}
then yields an exact Lagrangian surface in
T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

More explicitly, we construct a filling of
\Lambda
by first describing a local model for a Legendrian surface
\mathfrak{w}
in
J^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{2})=T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{z}
. We equip
T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2}
with the symplectic form
d(e^{r}\alpha)
where
\ker(\alpha)=\ker(dy_{1}-y_{2}d\theta)
is the standard contact structure on
J^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D}^{2})
and
r
is the radial coordinate. This choice of symplectic form ensures that the flow of
the Liouville vector field defined by the 1-form
e^{r}\alpha
is transverse to
J^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}
, thought of as the cotangent fibers along the boundary of the 0-section. The Lagrangian projection of
\mathfrak{w}
is then a Lagrangian surface in
(T^{*}\mathbb{D}^{2},d(e^{r}\alpha))
. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{w}\subseteq(J^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{2}),\ker(dz-e^{r}\alpha))
is a Legendrian, we immediately obtain the function
z:\pi(\mathfrak{w})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
dz=e^{r}\alpha|_{\pi(\mathfrak{w})}
, demonstrating that
\pi(\mathfrak{w})
is exact.

The boundary of
\pi(\mathfrak{w})
is taken to be a positive braid
\beta
in
J^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
so that we may regard it as a Legendrian link in a contact neighborhood of
\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}
. As the 0-section of
J^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is Legendrian isotopic to a max-tb standard Legendrian unknot, we can take
\partial\pi(\mathfrak{w})
to equivalently be the standard satellite of the standard Legendrian unknot. Diagramatically, this implies that the braid
\beta
in
J^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
can be given as the
(-1)
-framed closure of
\beta
in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
.

Local models for these singularities of fronts are classified by work of Arnold

(1)

.

The

The immersion points of a Lagrangian projection of a weave surface

Recall that for

Given

]
)

A maximal weakly separated collection

.

3.2.
Plabic graphs and plabic tilings

We now introduce plabic graphs and plabic tilings.
Plabic graphs are planar bicolored graphs originally introduced in
[
Pos06
]
that have a rich combinatorial structure tied to cluster algebras and positroid varieties. We follow the same conventions as
[
CLSBW23
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{n}
be the unit disk with
n
marked points on the boundary
A
plabic graph

G

\mathbb{D}_{n}
is a planar graph embedded in
\mathbb{D}_{n}
such that:

(1)

each marked point of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
is a boundary vertex of
G

(2)

each internal (non-boundary) vertex of

(3)

each boundary vertex is adjacent to a unique internal vertex of
G

The faces of a plabic graph

are the connected components of

\mathcal{F}(G)

3.3.
Grassmannians and the cyclic shift automorphism

Let
\text{Gr}(k,n)
denote the space of complex
k
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\widetilde{\text{Gr}}(k,n)
the corresponding affine cone. Given a point
X\in\text{Gr}(k,n)
, thought of as the row span of a
k\times n
matrix, the Plücker embedding of
\text{Gr}(k,n)
describes
X
by its
k\times k
minors. We denote by
\Delta_{I}
with
I\in{[n]\choose k}
the corresponding Plücker function. By
[
Sco06
]
, the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\text{Gr}}(k,n)]
is a cluster algebra with Plücker coordinates
\Delta_{I}
appearing as a subset of the cluster variables. The reader unfamiliar with cluster algebras can consult
[
FWZ20
]
for a detailed introduction, though we largely avoid any technicalities related to them in this work.

If we view the elements of a weakly separated collection as indices of Plücker coordinates, then the combinatorics of plabic graphs give ways to understand the cluster structure on
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\text{Gr}}(k,n)]
. In particular, we can relate the notion of rotational symmetry of a plabic graph or plabic tiling to a particular operation on
\widetilde{\text{Gr}}(k,n)
known as the cyclic shift automorphism. This automorphism, commonly denoted by
\rho
, acts on the Grassmannian
\widetilde{\text{Gr}}(k,n)
by shifting the indices of Plücker coordinates:
\Delta_{I}\mapsto\Delta_{I+_{n}1}
.
The action preserves the cluster structure, and is therefore a cluster automorphism
[
Fra20a
]
.

In our context, under the identification of weakly separated collections with Plücker coordinates, the cyclic shift acts by
I\mapsto I+_{n}1
for
I\in D
a
k
-element set. As the boundary vertices of a plabic tiling (hence the boundary faces of a plabic graph) are labeled by
k
-element consecutive sets, this has the effect of rotating the plabic tiling (hence the dual plabic graph) by
2\pi/n
. Thus, a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric weakly separated collection fixed corresponds to a plabic graph with
2\pi\ell/n
rotational symmetry.

3.4.
T-shift of plabic graphs

We now give a recipe for constructing a Legendrian weave from a trivalent plabic graph following
[
CLSBW23
]
. Note that for plabic graphs without lollipops, one can apply the moves in Definition
3.6
to show that any plabic graph is move equivalent to a trivalent plabic graph; see e.g.
[
FWZ25
, Lemma 7.4.2]
.

Definition 3.10
.

Given a trivalent plabic graph
G
, we define the
\bm{T}
-shift

G^{\downarrow}
to be the trivalent plabic graph constructed via the following steps:

(1)

For each marked point
i
in
\partial\mathbb{D}
, add a new marked point
i^{\prime}
slightly counterclockwise of
i
.

(2)

Replace every internal black trivalent vertex with a white trivalent vertex.

(3)

Place a black vertex
u
in each face of
G
. For each white vertex
v
added in Step 2 that appears on the boundary of the face, draw an edge connecting it to
u
. For every
u
in a boundary face, add an edge from the bordering marked point
i^{\prime}
to
u
.

(4)

Delete every black vertex of degree 2. For every black vertex of degree
d\geq 3
, arbitrarily expand it into a trivalent tree with
d
leaves.

We refer to the rank of
G
as the size of a face label
I\in\mathcal{F}(G)
. Note that this quantity is well-defined, as
\mathcal{F}(G)\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
.

Proposition 3.11

(Proposition 3.3,
[
CLSBW23
]
)
.

If
G
is a reduced trivalent plabic graph, then
G^{\downarrow}
is also a reduced trivalent plabic graph with rank one less than that of
G
.

We now describe how to use repeated iteration of T-shift to produce a Legendrian weave. Fixing

with
\mathcal{F}(G)\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
, we denote by
G_{1}=G^{\downarrow},G_{2},\dots,G_{k-1}
the plabic graphs obtained from repeatedly applying T-shift to
G
. Label each
G_{i}
by
\sigma_{i}
and denote their union by
\mathfrak{w}(G)=\cup_{i=1}^{k-1}G_{i}
.

Proposition 3.12

(Proposition 3.7,
[
CLSBW23
]
)
.

Given a plabic graph
G
with face labels
\mathcal{F}(G)\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
, the graph
\mathfrak{w}(G)
is a
k
-graph encoding a Legendrian weave embedded in
J^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
with boundary braid
(\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{k-1})^{n}
.

See Figure
7
for the T-shift construction applied to our running example of a
\rho^{3}
-symmetric weakly separated collection

.

4.
Constructing fillings of twist-spun torus links

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
assuming the existence of a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collections, which we then construct in Section

. We start by verifying that
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric weakly separated collections yield rotationally symmetric plabic tilings, and hence, rotationally symmetric Legendrian weaves.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
D\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
be a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection. The plabic tiling
\Sigma(D)
has
2\pi\ell/{n}
rotational symmetry and is fixed by the action of
\rho^{\ell}
.

Proof.

For a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection
D
, we have that for any
I\in D,
the
k
-element set
I+_{n}\ell
is also in
D
. By Definition
3.9
, the vertices of
\Sigma(D)
are given by
v_{S}=\sum_{i\in S}v_{i}
where
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the vertices of a regular
n
-gon centered at the origin. Therefore, for any vertex
v_{I}
of
\Sigma(D)
, we must have that the point
v_{I+_{n}\ell}=\rho^{\ell}\cdot v_{I}
is also a vertex of
\Sigma(D)
. Similarly,
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetry of

implies that the
\rho^{\ell}
orbits of the edges and faces of
\Sigma(D)
also lie in
\Sigma(D)
.

∎

For a maximal weakly separated collection
D
, we denote by

the reduced plabic graph dual to the tiling
\Sigma(D)
. By
[
Hug24
, Lemma 5.8]
the induced action of the Kálmán loop
\psi
on
\mathfrak{M}(\Lambda(k,n-k)
is identical to the action of the cyclic shift on
\text{Gr}(k,n)
and corresponds to a

rotation of any weave filling

of
\Lambda(k,n-k)
. We now show that

-symmetry of

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection

, the Legendrian weave
\mathfrak{w}(G_{D})
is Legendrian isotopic relative to the boundary to
\psi^{\ell}\cdot\mathfrak{w}(G_{D})
.

Proof.

Let
D\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
be a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection. By Lemma

,
\Sigma(D)
is fixed by the

rotation induced by the action of

, hence its dual plabic tiling

must be as well. Following Proposition

, we obtain an embedded Legendrian weave

from the plabic graph

. To verify symmetry of

of degree

∎

Remark 4.3
.

While our proof of Theorem

below only requires Legendrian weaves fixed by

Using the Legendrian weave described in Lemma

5.
Symmetric weakly separated collections.

In this section, we establish our necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection. We start by showing that our condition is necessary by studying the fixed points of plabic tilings under rotation. We then give an algorithm generalizing
[
PTZ20
, Theorem 1.6]
that constructs a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection given input data satisfying our condition.

5.1.
Necessary condition

Throughout this section, we assume that
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
. We can do so without loss of generality because, for any maximal weakly separated collection
D\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
, the weakly separated collection
D^{\prime}\subseteq{[n]\choose n-k}
given by
D^{\prime}=\{[n]\backslash A|A\in D\}
produces an isomorphic plabic tiling. The following lemma appears in
[
PTZ20
]
as a crucial technical ingredient in the proof of their necessary condition for the existence of a
\rho^{k}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection. We include a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 5.1

(Lemma 2.2,
[
PTZ20
]
)
.

For
I\in{[n]\choose k}
, if
I=I+_{n}\ell
, then
d|k
.

Proof.

Consider the map
\phi:\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
given by
\phi(a)=a+\ell\pmod{n}
. The order of
\phi
is
d
, as this is the smallest integer such that
d\ell\equiv 0\pmod{n}
, and every element of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
lies in a
\phi
-orbit of size
d
. Since
I
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, and
I=I+_{n}\ell
, it follows that
I
is a union of
\phi
-orbits. Since each
\phi
-orbit has size
d
, the cardinality
|I|=k
must be a multiple of
d
. Thus,
d|k
.
∎

Our necessary condition now follows as a generalization of
[
PTZ20
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
D\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
is a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection, then
k\equiv c\pmod{d}
, for
c\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

Proof.

Let
D\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
be a
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collection, and let
\Sigma(D)
be the associated plabic tiling. From the
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetry of
D
we know that the plabic tiling
\Sigma(D)
is fixed by
2\pi\ell/n
rotation, and therefore
\rho^{\ell}\cdot\Sigma(D)=\Sigma(D)
. Consider the center of
\Sigma(D)
, which is the unique fixed point of
\rho^{\ell}
. There are three cases that can happen:

(1)

If the center is a vertex
v_{I}
, then
I=I+_{n}\ell
. By
Lemma

5.1
, we have
d\mid k
, and thus
k\equiv 0\pmod{d}
.

(2)

If the center lies on an edge of
\Sigma(D)
, then we must have
d=2
. However, if this edge is fixed by a rotation through an angle of
\pi
, then
\rho^{\ell}
must send white cliques to white cliques, and black cliques to black cliques. In order for this to happen, then the two faces on either side of the edge fixed by
\rho^{\ell}
must be the same color. But we cannot have this because white and black cliques must alternate. Thus this case cannot occur.

(3)

If the center lies in the interior of a face on the plabic tiling
\Sigma(D)
, then the face
F
and its label
K
must be fixed by the
\rho^{\ell}
action. Since
F
corresponds to either a black or a white clique,
K
is either size
k-1
or
k+1
.
By
Lemma

5.1
,
d\mid|K|=k\pm 1
. Since
|K|
is either
k+1
or
k-1
, we get
that
k\equiv\pm 1\pmod{d}
.

Taking all three cases, we have
k\equiv c\pmod{d}
for
c\in\{-1,0,1\}
, which completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Generating a set when
n=d\ell

We now turn to generating
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collections. In
[
PTZ20
, Section 5]
, the authors detail an algorithm for generating
\rho^{\ell}
-symmetric maximal weakly separated collections when

. Here we give a generalization that covers the

. See Subsection

for a modification of the algorithm described below in the case where

(1)

Assume

and write
k=dr+c
for some

. For each

by

\displaystyle\overline{a}=\{a+i\ell\mid 0\leq i\leq d-1\}.

(2)

Fix an arbitrary total order on these orbits: say
\overline{a_{1}}<\overline{a_{2}}<\dots<\overline{a_{\ell}}
. Denote the complement of the union of the first
s-1

a_{i}
in

by

\displaystyle P_{s}=[n]\backslash\bigcup_{i=1}^{s-1}\overline{a_{i}}.

(3)

For

, define the successor function
S_{P_{s}}(x_{i})=x_{i+1}
for

, and

. In other words,

is a bijection from

to itself that defines a cyclic ordering on

.

(4)

For an additional parameter

we modify

in the following manner:

\displaystyle P_{s,h}

\displaystyle=P_{s}\backslash\{a_{s}+h\ell,\dots,a_{s}+(d-1)\ell\}

. For

. Denote the corresponding interval of consecutive elements of

(5)

Define

-orbit representatives by

and denote the corresponding

\displaystyle L_{s}=\{I+_{n}x\ell\mid I\in B_{s},x\in[d]\}

(6)

Finally, we obtain a candidate weakly separated collection from the union of all

\displaystyle D=\bigcup_{s=1}^{\ell-r+1}L_{s}

We now give an alternative informal process for generating the sets

that the reader may find slightly more intuitive. Note that this process allows for elements in the same

. We assert without proof that the two processes yield the same weakly separated collection

for a fixed

and total ordering.

After performing Steps (1) – (3) exactly as given above, we proceed as follows:

(1)

For a given

I_{1}
of
P_{s}
of size

ending in

. If no such interval exists, then
B_{s}
is empty. Otherwise, append
I_{1}
to

.

(2)

Check if there are elements in

to the right of
a_{s}
that are also in

. Remove the rightmost such element, if it exists, and append

to the right. Continue until no such elements remain, adding the resulting

to

each time an element is replaced.

(3)

If
I_{j}
does not contain any elements in the congruence class
\overline{a_{s}}
to the right of
a_{s}
, remove the leftmost element of
I_{j}
. Each time an element is removed, if the removed element is in
\overline{a_{s}}
, append the first missing successor to
a_{s}
in
S_{P_{s}}
that is not in
I_{j}\cup\overline{a_{s}}
to the right. Otherwise, append the first successor to
a_{s}
in
S_{P_{s}}
not in
I_{j}
. Add the resulting set

to
B_{s}
.

(4)

Lemma 5.5

Let

for

are not all distinct only when

Since

Lemma 5.6
.

For all
s<\ell-r
,
|B_{s}|=k
. Furthermore, if
k=dr-1
or
k=dr
,
|B_{\ell-r}|=k
.

Proof.

Let

. We wish to enumerate distinct intervals

appearing in
B_{s}
. Since
|P_{s}|=dt,
there is a bijection

uniquely defined by

and

where the cyclic ordering on

is given by
S_{[dt]}(x)=x+_{n}1

. To avoid double counting any particular

to represent, for a fixed

such that

th^{*}\in I(i,h^{*})
. Then
th^{*}-i+1
is the size of the interval
[i,th^{*}]
, so it is the minimum size of

for a fixed

must have size

. Rewriting the inequality, we define

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}=\left\lfloor\frac{k+i-1}{t}\right\rfloor\geq h

as the number of valid and distinct intervals

for our fixed

, every

distinct values of

yields the total number of distinct intervals, which is also equivalent to the cardinality of

\displaystyle|B_{s}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left\lfloor\frac{k+i-1}{t}\right\rfloor.

Let

, and

. We then have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left\lfloor\frac{k+i-1}{t}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{at+b+i-1}{t}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=at+\left\lfloor\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{b+i-1}{t}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=at+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\begin{cases}0&i<t-b\\
1&i\geq t-b\end{cases}

\displaystyle=at+b.

Therefore, in order to describe when

, it suffices to determine when

. Recall that

, so that for any

. Additionally, if we assume that

, then

and

. Similarly, for

, we have

, and

.
Thus, for all

,

. Moreover, for both

and

, we have

as well.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

If

.

Let

\displaystyle|B_{\ell-r}|

∎

\displaystyle=\left|C\backslash\bigcup\limits_{s=1}^{\ell-g}L_{s}\right|

Now define

by

and

when

as

Flow polytopes for extensions of bipartite graphs Thanks: The authors thank Alejandro Morales and Martha Yip for helpful conversations.
Benjamin Braun, Kaitlin Bruegge, Robert Davis, Derek Hanely

Abstract
. The space of unit flows on a finite acyclic directed graph is a lattice polytope called the flow polytope of the graph. Given a bipartite graph G G with minimum degree at least two, we construct two associated acyclic directed graphs: the extension of G G and the almost-degree-whiskered graph of G G . We prove that the normalized volume of the flow polytope for the extension of G G is equal to the number of matchings in the almost-degree-whiskered graph of G G . Further, we refine this result by proving that the Ehrhart h ∗ h^{*} -polynomial of the flow polytope for the extension of G G is equal to the unsigned matching polynomial of the almost-degree-whiskered graph of G G .

A perennial problem regarding flow polytopes is to compute and understand their volumes, where in this work we consider the

It is well-known that the normalized volume of a lattice polytope

The Lidskii formula

In the case of the flow polytopes considered in this paper, the Lidskii formula collapses to a single summand, and gives the volume as the number of integer points in an associated flow polytope defined by a different netflow vector.
In some cases this makes the determination of the volume easier, but not always.
While there are some lovely combinatorial models known for computing flow polytope volumes

, there remains a need to better understand the behavior of these volumes, especially as the volume relates to the combinatorics of the underlying DAG.

Motivated by this goal, given a bipartite graph

In this work, we prove that the normalized volume of the flow polytope

2.3.
Ehrhart Theory

A

is the convex hull of finitely many points in

We say

The

The

E(P;z):=\sum_{t\geq 0}i(P;t)z^{t}=\frac{h_{0}^{*}+h_{1}^{*}z+\cdots+h_{d}^{*}z^{d}}{(1-z)^{\dim(P)+1}}\,.

The fact that this is a rational function is due to Ehrhart

.
The coefficients

are known to be nonnegative integers

.
The polynomial

(Ehrhart)

of

An important property of a lattice polytope

Theorem 2.14

If a lattice polytope

The fact that

is a single value immediately implies that there is at most one

will be greater than

•

The conditions on

In this case, one of the following situations must hold:

(if it exists), while

.

\alpha_{1,i}\beta_{i,j}\gamma_{j,2}
, then
a_{i}\geq j
and
a_{j}\leq i
, and further if both
a_{i}=j
and
a_{j}=i
, then
a_{i,j}=-
.
Fourth and finally, note that if
S
contains
\alpha_{1,i}\beta_{i,j}\gamma_{j,1}
, then
a_{i}\geq j
and
a_{j}\geq i
.
Thus, there exists upper and lower bounds on each
a_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n+m\}
.
Further, observe that in the second and third cases above, conditions on the signs of
a_{i,j}
are forced by the routes in
S
when
a_{i}=j
and
a_{j}=i
.

We next define a clique vector
\mathbf{a}^{S}
and prove that it satisfies
\phi(\mathbf{a}^{S})=C_{max}(S)
with all the corresponding properties of the hypotheses of Lemma
4.1
.
For each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, set
a^{S}_{i}:=\min_{S}(i)
.
For each
j\in\{n+1,n+2,\ldots,n+m\}
, set
a^{S}_{j}:=\max_{S}(j)
.
If we further have

i=\mathrm{max}_{S}(j)\text{ and }j=\mathrm{min}_{S}(i)\,,

then
a^{S}_{i,j}:=+
.

Now suppose that
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
is a clique vector such that
S\subseteq\phi(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
.
Then we know for every
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n+m\}
we have

\mathrm{min}_{S}(i)\leq a^{\prime}_{i}\leq\mathrm{max}_{S}(i)\,.

Further, we know that for every
j
such that
\mathrm{min}_{S}(i)<j<\mathrm{max}_{S}(i)
, there is no route in
S
supported on the edge
\beta_{i,j}
, as this would lead to a contradiction to the max and min values of
a_{i}
via the argument given in the first paragraph above.
Therefore, we are freely able to adjust the entries of
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
as described in the four conditions of Proposition
4.4
.
By iteratively decreasing the values
a^{\prime}_{i}
and increasing the values
a^{\prime}_{j}
, applying conditions
(3)
and
(4)
as needed during this process, a sequence of clique vectors is produced that terminates at
\mathbf{a}^{S}
.
There are no routes in
S
that cause conflicts with any of the maximal cliques corresponding to these clique vectors, as there are no routes to conflict with along those edges.
This produces the desired sequence of cover relations in the framing lattice.
Since each of these saturated chains in the framing lattice terminates at
\phi(\mathbf{a}^{S})
, this completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
G=G(H)
and let
F
be the canonical bipartite framing for
G
.
Given a clique
C\in\mathscr{L}_{G,F}
, we have

\mathrm{cov}_{\mathscr{L}_{G,F}}(C)=|\psi\circ\phi^{-1}(C)|\,,

i.e., the number of cliques covering
C
is equal to the number of edges in the matching in
W(H)
corresponding to
C
.

Proof.

Let
\phi(\mathbf{a})
be a clique with corresponding matching
\psi(\mathbf{a})
of
W(H)
.
We will prove this corollary by showing how each cover relation listed in Proposition
4.4
can be associated to performing a unique “action” on a specific edge of
\psi(\mathbf{a})
.

Suppose
iw_{i,j}\in\psi(\mathbf{a})
for some
i=1,\dots,n
.
There are two possibilities:
a_{i}=j
and
a_{j}\neq i
, or
a_{i}=j
,
a_{j}=i
, and
a_{i,j}=+
.
First, if
a_{j}\neq i
, then let
j^{\prime}
be the largest neighbor of
i
that is less than
j
.
If
j^{\prime}w_{i,j^{\prime}}
is an edge of
\psi(\mathbf{a})
as well, then let
\psi(\mathbf{b})
be the matching that deletes
iw_{i,j}
and
j^{\prime}w_{i,j^{\prime}}
and adds
ij^{\prime}
.
Then
\phi(\mathbf{a})\lessdot\phi(\mathbf{b})
since
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
satisfy Proposition
4.4

(1)
.
If
j^{\prime}w_{i,j^{\prime}}
is not an edge in
\psi(\mathbf{a})
, then replace
iw_{i,j}
with
iw_{i,j^{\prime}}
if possible; if not, delete
iw_{i,j}
.
If
iw_{i,j^{\prime}}
exists, then the resulting matching,
\psi(\mathbf{b})
, has a corresponding clique
\phi(\mathbf{b})
covering
\phi(\mathbf{a})
since
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
again satisfy Proposition
4.4

(1)
.
The only way
iw_{i,j^{\prime}}
cannot exist is if
j^{\prime}
and
j
are the lowest-valued neighbors of
i
.
Then

\phi(\mathbf{a})\lessdot\phi(\mathbf{b})=(\phi\circ\psi^{-1})(\psi(\mathbf{a})\setminus iw_{i,j})

because
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
once more satisfy Proposition
4.4

(1)
.

Now, suppose
a_{j}=i
and
a_{i,j}=+
.
There is then exactly one way to produce a clique covering
\phi(\mathbf{a})
whose corresponding matching modifies
iw_{i,j}
: replace
a_{i,j}=+
with
-
and, if possible, replace
iw_{i,j}
with
iw_{i,j^{\prime}}
where
j^{\prime}
is the largest neighbor of
i
that is smaller than
j
; otherwise, delete
iw_{i,j}
.
Then the clique vectors associated to
\psi(\mathbf{a})
and the resulting matching satisfy Proposition
4.4

(4)
.

We have shown thus far that if
\psi(\mathbf{a})
contains an edge
iw_{i,j}
, then there is exactly one way to associate a clique
\phi(\mathbf{b})
covering
\phi(\mathbf{a})
by performing an action on
iw_{i,j}
.
An analogous argument holds when considering edges of the form
jw_{j,i}
for
j\in\{n+1,\dots,n+m\}
.
In this case, we use Proposition
4.4

(2)
or
(4)
.
Note that if an action on
iw_{i,j}
results in deleting an edge
j^{\prime}w_{j^{\prime},i}
, then performing an action on
j^{\prime}w_{j^{\prime},i}
cannot result in deleting
iw_{i,j}
, so there is indeed just one action associated to each edge.

Finally, if
ij\in\psi(\mathbf{a})
, then
a_{i}=j
,
a_{j}=i
, and
a_{i,j}=-
.
By Proposition
4.4

(3)
, the clique
\phi(\mathbf{a})
is covered by the matching corresponding to the clique vector that replaces
a_{i,j}=-
with
+
.

By examining each of the conditions listed in Proposition
4.4
, one may verify that each of the cover relations describes exactly one of the above actions on an edge of
\psi(\mathbf{a})
.
The conclusion of the corollary follows.
∎

The following corollary is directly implied by Corollary
4.7
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
G=G(H)
and let
F
be the canonical bipartite framing for
G
.
The number of cliques in
\mathscr{L}_{G,F}
that are covered by
k
other cliques is the number of
k
-matchings in
W(H)
.

We conclude this work with the proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the DKK triangulation of
\mathcal{F}_{1}(G)
induced from
\mathscr{L}_{G,F}
, and let
\mathcal{T}^{\star}
be the half-open triangulation described in Lemma
4.1
.
By Corollary
4.3
,

h^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(G);z)=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal{T}^{\star}}z^{\mathrm{cov}(\Delta)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n+m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta\in\mathcal{T}^{\star}\\
\mathrm{cov}(\Delta)=k\end{subarray}}z^{k}.

By Corollary
4.8
, the coefficient of
z^{k}
will be the number of matchings of
W(H)
of size
k
, completing the proof.
∎

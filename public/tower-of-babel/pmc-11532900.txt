Surgical resection of a metastatic para-aortic lymph node deposit of renal mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma – A case report

Introduction

Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma (MTSCC) is an uncommon and slow-growing subtype of renal cell carcinoma (RCC), with low malignant potential [1]. Histologically, the tumours are composed of bland cuboidal and spindle cells along with intercellular mucin within the tubules [1]. Due to the low prevalence of this subtype of RCC, follow-up data is limited and therefore the true biological nature of this tumour is not fully understood.

Of all cases reported in the literature on MTSCC, very few have been related to metastatic deposits. Of those that report metastatic disease, the majority have had a sarcomatoid component evident. Kubota et al. [2], reported 2 cases of successful resection of metastatic MTSCC with a sarcomatoid element present, both patients died due to their disease at 5 years and 10 years. These cases, were initially diagnosed as papillary RCC and were re-analysed. Mikami et al. [3], reported a successful laparoscopic lymphadenectomy for a patient 7 years following initial radical nephrectomy with MTSCC, similarly histological re-evaluation was used to re-classify initial lesion from papillary RCC to MTSCC.

Larkin et al. [4], reported the first successful treatment of a patient with multiple metastatic deposits of biopsy proven MTSCC with sunitinib.

Here, we present a case of recurrent para-aortic lymph node metastatic MTSCC treated with surgical excision.

Methods

This case has been reported in line with the Surgical Case Report (SCARE) guidelines [5].

Case presentation

In 2018, a 56 year old male patient who was otherwise fit and well presented with an incidental finding of a 6 cm left centrally located kidney mass on a Computer Tomography (CT) imaging investigation (See Images 1 & 2) performed to assess diverticular disease. There was also concern about a perihilar lymph node. This was completely asymptomatic and baseline routine bloods were normal. Following discussion at a multidisciplinary team meeting, the differential diagnosis was a renal cell carcinoma (RCC) or a transitional cell carcinoma (TCC) with an RCC thought to be a more likely histological diagnosis. A decision was made to perform a left laparoscopic radical nephrectomy and excision of the perihilar para-aortic lymph node at the time.

Coronal and axial sections of CT performed showing an incidental finding of a 6 cm left centrally located kidney mass (red arrow). (For interpretation of the references to color in this figure legend, the reader is referred to the web version of this article.)

Histology revealed a mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma of the kidney, invading into renal sinus fat but resection margins clear. The lymph node histology confirmed metastatic carcinoma, pT3N1. Post-operative course was uneventful and baseline eGFR was 52. A follow up surveillance CT thorax, abdomen and pelvis (CT TAP) at 6 months displayed a further 3 cm left para-aortic lymph node (See Images 3 & 4).

Follow up CT TAP at 6 months. Coronal & axial views showing recurrent left para-aortic metastatic lymph node deposit of MTSCC (red arrow). (For interpretation of the references to color in this figure legend, the reader is referred to the web version of this article.)

The CT also showed a large retrosternal goitre for which he underwent a total thyroidectomy and sternotomy prior to excision of his left para-aortic lesion. Histology of his thyroid revealed a T1 follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma. Following recovery from his thyroidectomy, the para-aortic lesion was then excised. Histologically, it demonstrated a metastatic mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma (See Histology slides 1A-F). The patient recovered well post-operatively.

Histology

The haematoxylin and eosin slides demonstrate a well circumscribed tumour that is composed of tightly packed tubules with admixed spindle cells. (See Histology slides 1A-C) This tumour is positive for AMACR, vimentin and focally for CD10. Other markers including PAX8, AE1/3, CK7 and CK20 are negative. These features are in keeping with a mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma (MTSCC).

Follow up

One year later, the patient developed further metastatic disease with a 1.7 cm deposit anterior to the left psoas muscle. He was commenced on Cabozantinib daily. His disease remains stable at 3 years follow up and he remains on cabozantinib.

Discussion

Mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma is described as slow growing and relatively indolent in nature. Given its rarity, histological confirmation can be challenging, with some reports of an initial diagnosis of papillary RCC prior to being re-analysed [2,3]. Furthermore, long term outcomes and follow up on this tumour is not available in literature. Of all cases reported in the literature on mucinous tubular and spindle cell carcinoma, very few have been related to metastatic deposits, adding to the rarity of our patient case report. Our case further varies from the literature in that the sarcomatoid element was not evident. The infrequent nature and minimal literature available on this further adds to the complexity in the management of this patient. Excision in its' entirety is an important factor in limiting the progression of this indolent carcinoma [3].

Larkin et al. [4], have shown that tyrosine kinase inhibitors are a suitable treatment option for multiple metastatic deposits. Our case report shows that for metastatic disease, ongoing treatment with cabozantinib allows the patient to have a good quality of life while keeping the disease stable.

Conclusion

Further histological and clinical longterm follow up is required in order to better understand the nature of this subtype of RCC. A surgical approach to excision of metastatic deposits is a safe and effective method of treating these patients. Excision in its entirety is the most important factor in limiting its local progression. Our case however, illustrates a usually aggressive form of this rare cancer.

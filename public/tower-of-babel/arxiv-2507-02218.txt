A geometric model for the non- τ \tau -rigid modules of type D ~ n \widetilde{D}_{n}
Blake Jackson

Abstract
. We give a geometric model for the non- τ \tau -rigid modules over acyclic path algebras of type D ~ n \widetilde{D}_{n} . Similar models have been provided for module categories over path algebras of types A n , D n , A_{n},D_{n}, and A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} as well as the τ \tau -rigid modules of type D ~ n \widetilde{D}_{n} . A major draw of these geometric models is the “intersection-dimension formulas” they often come with. These formulas give an equality between the intersection number of the curves representing the modules in the geometric model and the dimension of the extension spaces between the two modules. This formula allows us to calculate the homological data between two modules combinatorially. Since there are infinitely many distinct homogeneous stable tubes in the regular component of the Auslander-Reiten quiver of type D ~ n \widetilde{D}_{n} , all of which are disjoint, our geometric data requires an extra decoration on the admissible edges in our geometric model to prevent intersections between curves corresponding to modules in distinct stable tubes of the Auslander-Reiten quiver.

The main feature of these geometric models regarding the module category is the so-called intersection-dimension formula for the modules over these algebras.
Modules in the geometric model are represented by some set of admissible decorated curves in their respective surface.
The intersection-dimension formula relates the geometric data (intersection number of curves) with the homological data of the modules they represent (dimension of the first extension spaces between the two modules).
The former is a straightforward calculation after the model is given; the latter can be highly nontrivial, especially for path algebras over quivers of affine Dynkin type (for example, quivers of type
\widetilde{D}_{n}

This paper continues this tradition with an intersection-dimension formula for the non-

Definition 2.2

Let

Quivers were introduced by Gabriel
[
12
]

[
10

11

While this paper is less concerned with cluster theory, developments in the field of cluster algebras have led to significant contributions in other areas of mathematics, such as the representation theory of algebras.
This paper is concerned primarily with quivers of type

It is also worth noting that mutation acts as an involution on quivers (and clusters

This means that for a quiver

Definition 2.3
.

Let

be a finite, connected quiver and

be an algebraically closed field.
Then

is the

If

.
If

Example 2.4
.

Perhaps the first example we should consider is the path algebra of type
A_{2}
.
In this case, the quiver is given by

Q=A_{2}=\quad
1
2
\alpha

Then
\mathbf{k}Q=\mathbf{k}A_{2}
has vector space basis
\{e_{1},\alpha,e_{2}\}
, where
e_{i}
is the lazy or constant path at vertex
i
.
If we write our paths right-to-left, the multiplication between basis elements is

\alpha e_{1}=\alpha,\quad e_{2}\alpha=\alpha,\quad e_{1}^{2}=e_{1},\quad e_{2}^{2}=e_{2},

and all other products are zero.
Therefore, our path algebra is isomorphic to the following upper triangular matrix algebra:

\mathbf{k}Q=\mathbf{k}A_{2}\cong\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{e_{1}}&\lambda_{\alpha}\\
0&\lambda_{e_{2}}\end{bmatrix}\ :\ \lambda_{c}\in\mathbf{k}\text{ for }c\text{ a path}\right\}

We care about path algebras over quivers because of the following classical result from the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras.

Theorem 2.5
.

Every finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field is Morita equivalent to a quotient of a path algebra over some quiver
Q
.

Moreover, if the algebra is hereditary, we can drop the “a quotient of” condition.
A ring or an algebra is said to be

if all submodules of projective modules are projective.
Hereditary algebras are algebras with global dimension 1.
This already tells us a great deal about the homological properties of these algebras: if
\mathcal{A}
is a hereditary algebra, then
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)=0
for
i>1
and any
M,N\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
.
This restricts the study of extensions to
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
in hereditary algebras.
Since the path algebras we are interested in (acyclic type
\widetilde{D}_{n}
) are hereditary, this motivates the right-hand side of Theorem
A
.

Definition 3.1

Let

If

If

M\oplus M^{\prime}=\left(M_{i}\oplus M_{i}^{\prime},\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{\alpha}&0\\

Theorem 3.2

(
[
22

Let

Most modern work dealing with the representation theory of hereditary algebras

Our goal by the end of this section is to understand the global and local structure of the category

The objects of this category are isoclasses of indecomposable (left)

Definition 3.3

Let

With the irreducible morphisms as arrows and the isoclasses of indecomposable modules as objects,

One major implication of being a Krull-Schmidt category is that we can define the Auslander-Reiten quiver of

To this end, we define the Auslander-Reiten translation of a non-projective indecomposable module.

The projective objects in

Theorem 3.10

Let

Then there are isomorphisms

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M,N)\cong D\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\tau^{-1}N,M)\cong D\overline{\operatorname{Hom}}(N,\tau M).

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M,N)\cong D\operatorname{Hom}(\tau^{-1}N,M)\cong D\operatorname{Hom}(N,\tau M).

The most immediate corollary of Theorem

Corollary 3.11

Let

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M,N)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}(\tau^{-1}N,M)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}(N,\tau M).

5.2.
The Category of Admissible Edges

Now that
E^{\times}_{\lambda}
has been described and we have the notion of an elementary move, we can define the second category that plays a major role in Theorem
A
.

Definition 5.6
.

A
mesh relation
is an equality between certain sequences of elementary moves.
To be precise, for
(\gamma,\kappa,\lambda)\in E^{\times}_{\lambda}
, the mesh relation is

m_{(\gamma,\kappa,\lambda)}=\sum_{\alpha}(\alpha)\alpha

where the sum is over all elementary moves
\alpha:(\gamma,\kappa,\lambda)\mapsto(\lambda_{i},\kappa_{i},\lambda)
and
(\alpha)
is the elementary move
(\alpha):(\lambda_{i},\kappa_{i},\lambda)\mapsto\rho(\gamma,\kappa,\lambda)
which is guaranteed to exist for
(\gamma,\kappa,\lambda)\notin PTE(\mathbf{T})
.
The
category of admissible edges

\mathcal{E(\mathbf{T})}
is the category with objects being homotopy classes of admissible edges.
The space of morphisms from
(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1})
to
(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})
is a quotient of the vector space spanned by elementary moves
(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})\mapsto(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1})
by the subspace generated by all the mesh relations.

Remark 5.7
.

It is crucial to note that the morphisms in
\mathcal{E(\mathbf{T})}
go in the
opposite
direction of the elementary moves.
Also, note that the category of admissible edges has 3 components: the preprojective component
\mathcal{P(\mathbf{T})}
with a definite “beginning” starting with the projective tagged edges, the preinjective component
\mathcal{Q(\mathbf{T})}
with a definite “end” consisting of the injective tagged edges, and the regular component
\mathcal{R(\mathbf{T})}
which itself is made of infinitely many stable tubes.
A pictorial representation of the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the module category can be found in Figure
1
, and the same picture works for describing the edge category.

It shouldn’t be much of a surprise that
\rho
can be thought of as a right-to-left translation in
\mathcal{E(\mathbf{T})}
.
Also, by the definitions of the elementary move and
\rho
, we can see that there will be three types of meshes in this category.
The following lemma makes clear the structure of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{T})
.

Lemma 5.8
.

\mathcal{R(\mathbf{T})}
is made up of connected components called stable tubes.
The tubes are made of admissible edges that have a finite orbit under
\rho
.
There are two stable tubes of rank 2, one of rank
(n-2)
, and infinitely many of rank 1.
The stable tubes of rank 2 are composed of admissible edges with both endpoints in the punctures, i.e.,
\gamma(0),\gamma(1)\in\mathbf{P}
.
The difference between the edges in each of the tubes of rank 2 is the tagging (and coloring); in one stable tube of rank 2, we have
\kappa(0)=\kappa(1)
for all admissible edges, and in the other we have
\kappa(0)\neq\kappa(1)
.
The edges in the tube of rank
(n-2)
cut out a twice-punctured polygon, and the rank of this tube is the same as the number of marked points in
\mathbf{M}
.
Finally, there are infinitely many stable tubes of rank 1 that are made up of edges with both endpoints in the punctures and who are stable under
\rho
.

6.
Equivalence of Categories

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be an acyclic triangulation of a twice-punctured disk with
(n-2)
marked boundary points corresponding to an acyclic quiver
Q^{\mathbf{T}}
of type
\widetilde{D}_{n}
.
There is an equivalence of categories

\varphi:\Gamma(\operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}})\to\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{T})

such that
\varphi\circ\tau=\rho\circ\varphi
.

Proof.

To prove the Theorem, we show that both categories have the same “basic objects” and that
\varphi
preserves irreducible morphisms.
The basic objects are the projective objects, the injective objects, and the objects at the mouth of the stable tubes.
Fix an acyclic quiver
Q
of type
\widetilde{D}_{n}
.
This corresponds to a unique triangulation
\mathbf{T}
of a twice-punctured disk with
(n-2)
marked boundary points where each edge
i\in\mathbf{T}
corresponds to a vertex
i\in Q^{\mathbf{T}}_{0}
.
Let
\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}}
be the path algebra of this quiver.

The projective (resp. injective) admissible edges are the
n
unique admissible edges obtained by applying
\rho^{-1}
(resp.
\rho
) to each edge in the triangulation
\mathbf{T}
.
Specifically, the admissible edge corresponding to
P(j)
(resp.
I(j)
) is obtained by applying
\rho^{-1}
(resp.
\rho
) to the edge in
\mathbf{T}
labeled
j
.
All of these edges will have the color
\lambda=-\infty
.

Since the dimension vector of the modules at the mouths of the stable tubes of rank
r>1
uniquely determines the isomorphism class of indecomposable representations, the corresponding admissible edges are uniquely determined by their intersections with the edges of the triangulation.
The admissible edges corresponding to the modules at the mouth of the stable tubes of rank 2 correspond to taggings and colorings of
\gamma_{0}
, the untagged edge connecting the punctures
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.
One stable tube of rank 2 contains admissible edges
(\gamma_{0},\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1})
and
(\gamma_{0},1-\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1})
where
\kappa_{1}(0)=\kappa_{1}(1)
and the other stable tube of rank 2 contains admissible edges
(\gamma_{0},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})
and
(\gamma_{0},1-\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})
where
\kappa_{2}(0)\neq\kappa_{2}(1)
.
Also,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\{0,1\}
with
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
.

The admissible edges at the mouth of the stable tube of rank
(n-2)
are the ones with both endpoints in the boundary that do not intersect
\gamma_{0}
.
The corresponding isomorphism classes of indecomposable representations are uniquely determined by their dimension vectors, which are given by the intersection data of the admissible edge with the edges in the triangulation.
The color of the edges in the stable tube of rank
(n-2)
is
\lambda=\infty
.

Finally, we come to the homogeneous stable tubes (those of rank 1).
These tubes have a unique module at their mouth
M_{\lambda}
(see Example
3.14
).
The dimension vector for this module is the imaginary root
\delta=(1,1,2,2,\ldots,2,1,1)
of the root system of type
\widetilde{D}_{n}
.
Each choice of
\lambda\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
(
\lambda\neq 0,1,\infty
) gives a unique isomorphism class of indecomposable representations which sits at the mouth of some tube of rank 1.
The corresponding admissible edge at the mouth of the stable tube of rank 1 is the curve labeled
\gamma
in Figure
9
with the color
\lambda
.

Therefore,
\varphi
maps the basic objects of
\Gamma(\operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}})
to the basic objects of
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{T})
.
By construction, there is an irreducible morphism
M\to N
in
\operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}}
if and only if there is an elementary move
(\gamma_{N},\kappa_{N},\lambda_{N})\mapsto(\gamma_{M},\kappa_{M},\lambda_{M})
, and hence an arrow
(\gamma_{M},\kappa_{M},\lambda_{M})\to(\gamma_{N},\kappa_{N},\lambda_{N})
in
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{T})
.

Since
\varphi
as described above is invertible, we get our desired result.
∎

The equivalence of categories identifies a homotopy class of an admissible edge with an isomorphism class of an indecomposable module
M=M(\gamma,\kappa,\lambda)
.
By observing the intersections of a curve with the triangulation and comparing this with the dimension vector of the module it represents, the following result is immediate.

Let

The Intersection-Dimension Formula

Lemma 7.1
.

Without loss of generality, let
(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{T})
and
(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda)\in E^{\times}_{\lambda}
where
\mathbf{T}
is an acyclic triangulation of a twice-punctured
(n-2)
-gon
\mathbf{S}
and let
M(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty)=M_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}})
and
M(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})=M_{2}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}})
be the corresponding modules under the equivalence
\varphi^{-1}
.
Then

\operatorname{Int}((\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty),(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda))=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{1},M_{2})+\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{2},M_{1}).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.13
, we know that any non-zero homomorphism must begin in the preprojective component.
If
M_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}})
as well, assume without loss of generality that
M_{1}
is to the left of
M_{2}
.
First note that, for an indecomposable module
M
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}(P(j),M)\cong M_{j}

where
M_{j}
is the vector space at vertex
j
in the indecomposable quiver representation
M
.
Since
\operatorname{Int}(-,-)
,
\operatorname{Hom}(-,-)
, and
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(-,-)
are invariant under
\tau
and
\rho
for non-projective modules over a path algebra of type
\widetilde{D}_{n}
, we can apply these translations to both objects until
(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty)\in\mathbf{T}
.
Say
\rho^{n+1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty)=j\in\mathbf{T}
so that
\tau^{n}M_{1}=P(j)
.
Then, if
e_{j}
is the unit vector with a
1
in the
j^{th}
position and zeros elsewhere and
\tau^{n+1}M_{2}\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Int}((\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty),(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Int}(\rho^{n+1}(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},-\infty),\rho^{n+1}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Int}(j,\rho^{n+1}(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\underline{dim}}(\tau^{n+1}M_{2})\cdot e_{j}=(\tau^{n+1}M_{2})_{j}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}(P(j),\tau^{n+1}M_{2})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}(\tau^{-n}P(j),\tau^{-n}\tau^{n+1}M_{2})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}(M_{1},\tau M_{2})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{2},M_{1})

where the last inequality follows from the Auslander-Reiten formulas.
Moreover, by our assumption that
M_{1}
is to the left of
M_{2}
, we have that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{1},M_{2})=0
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1}),(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{T})
where
\mathbf{T}
is an acyclic triangulation of a twice-punctured
(n-2)
-gon
\mathbf{S}
and let
M(\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1})=M_{1},M(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2})=M_{2}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k}Q^{\mathbf{T}})
be the corresponding modules under the equivalence

.
Then

\operatorname{Int}((\gamma_{1},\kappa_{1},\lambda_{1}),(\gamma_{2},\kappa_{2},\lambda_{2}))=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{1},M_{2})+\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{2},M_{1}).

Proof.

If
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
, then the intersection number is zero.
Also, the two modules

and
M_{2}
live in disjoint stable tubes, and therefore there are no nonzero homomorphisms between

M_{2}

If

, then the two modules live in the same stable tube.
Since each indecomposable object in a given stable tube is uniquely determined by its composition series (i.e., they are uniserial) with respect to the indecomposable modules at the mouth of the tube, calculation of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
is particularly easy (see
[
23

).
In particular,
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(-,-)
can be calculated completely combinatorially within a given stable tube, and
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{1},M_{2})=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M_{2},M_{1})
by symmetry.
Also, by construction (and visual inspection), the intersection number between two admissible edges in the same stable tube follows exactly the same pattern as their corresponding modules.
∎

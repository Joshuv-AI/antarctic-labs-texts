Torsion of elliptic curves with rational j j -invariant over the maximal elementary abelian 2-extension of ℚ \QQ
Lucas Hamada

Abstract
. In this paper, we classify the possible torsion subgroup structures of elliptic curves defined over the compositum of all quadratic extensions of the rational number field, whose j j -invariant is a rational number not equal to 0 or 1728.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11G05; Secondary 12F05.

1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\QQ
. B. Mazur proved in
[
21
]
that the torsion subgroup
E(\QQ)_{\tors}
is isomorphic to one of the following 15 groups:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle/\penalty N_{1}\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle N_{1}=1,\;\ldots,10,12,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2N_{2}\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle N_{2}=1,\;\ldots,4.

Since then, there have been proven analogues for several other classes of elliptic curves. Let
\Phi(d)
denote the set of all isomorphism classes of torsion subgroups
E(K)_{\tors}
, where
K
runs through all extensions of
\QQ
of degree
d
and

runs through all elliptic curves defined over

[

, and M. Kenku and F. Momose in
[

]
described

. The classification of

[
14
]
, and M. Derickx, A. Etropolski, J. Morrow, M. van Hoeij, and D. Zureick-Brown in
[
7
]
.

Similarly, let
\Phi_{\QQ}(d)
denote the set of all isomorphism classes of torsion subgroups

, where

runs through all elliptic curves defined over
\QQ
and

runs through all extensions of
\QQ
of degree

. F. Najman determined

\Phi_{\QQ}(3)

[

. E. González-Jiménez determined

in

[

, along with M. Chou in

[

]
and Y. Fujita
[

,

Let

be an elliptic curve defined over

. Then, the torsion subgroup

is isomorphic to one of the following groups

\displaystyle\ZZ

\displaystyle/N_{1}\ZZ,

\displaystyle N_{1}=1,3,5,7,9,15,

\displaystyle\ZZ/2\ZZ

A natural next step in the study of elliptic curves is to classify their torsion structures, focusing on classes closely related to elliptic curves defined over
\QQ
. This paper focuses on elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant, which are quadratic twists of elliptic curves defined over
\QQ

[

classified, up to isomorphism, the torsion subgroups of elliptic curves

1.1
for elliptic curves with rational

-invariants. Specifically, we prove the following theorem:

(Main Theorem)
.

Let

be an elliptic curve defined over
\QQ(2^{\infty})
satisfying

is isomorphic to one of the following groups

\displaystyle\ZZ

\displaystyle/N_{1}\ZZ,

\displaystyle N_{1}=1,3,5,7,9,13,15,

\displaystyle\ZZ/2\ZZ

\displaystyle\oplus\ZZ/2N_{2}\ZZ,

\displaystyle N_{2}=1,\;\ldots,6,8,

\displaystyle\ZZ/3\ZZ

\displaystyle\oplus\ZZ/3\ZZ,

\displaystyle\ZZ/4\ZZ

\displaystyle\ZZ/6\ZZ

Unfortunately, stronger results are required for the proof of Theorem
1.2
. A useful method for further restricting the possibilities of the extension degree of the field of definition of a torsion point is the concept of cyclic
n
-isogeny.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a field
K
and let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
. We say that
E
admits a (cyclic)
n
-isogeny over
K
if there exists an elliptic curve
\widetilde{E}/K
and an isogeny
\phi:E\to\widetilde{E}
defined over
K
, such that the kernel of
\phi
,
\ker(\phi)
, is a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
n
that is invariant under the action of
\gal(\overline{K}/K)
.

Remark 2.3
.

In the preceding definition, when
K=\QQ
, we simply refer to an
n
-isogeny over
\QQ
as an
n
-isogeny, omitting the phrase
over
\QQ
for simplicity.

In this paper, we frequently rely on the following well-known equivalence, which allows us to interpret the existence of a cyclic
n
-isogeny over
K
in terms of the Galois-invariant cyclic subgroups of
E(\overline{K})_{\tors}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Equivalent condition for cyclic isogeny)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a field
K
of characteristic zero. Then
E
admits a cyclic
n
-isogeny over
K
if and only if there exists a Galois extension
\widetilde{K}
of
K
and a cyclic subgroup
C_{n}\subset E(\widetilde{K})_{\tors}
of order
n
, such that
C_{n}
is invariant under the action of
\gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
.

For elliptic curves defined over
\QQ
, we have a complete list of the possible
n
-isogenies.

Theorem 2.5

(B. Mazur
[
22
]
. See
[
20
]
for a complete list of references)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\QQ
. If

has an

-isogeny over
\QQ
, then

or

. If

does not have complex multiplication, then

.

M. Chou established a straightforward criterion for an elliptic curve to possess an

Lemma 2.6

(M. Chou. Adapted from the proof of Proposition 4.1 in
[

]
)
.

Let

be a Galois extension of
\QQ
, and let

Proof..

This follows from the fact that

is a

.
∎

Using the concept of cyclic isogeny, we now prove two lemmas that provide additional restrictions on the extension degree of the field of definition of torsion points of elliptic curves defined over
\QQ
. The first lemma follows directly from the definition of cyclic isogeny.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\QQ
, and
K
a Galois extension of
\QQ
. If
P_{n}\in E(K)
is a point of order n such that the subgroup
\left<P_{n}\right>
generated by
P_{n}
is
\gal(K/\QQ)
-invariant, then
[\QQ(P_{n}):\QQ]
divides
\varphi(n)
.

Proof..

Consider the homomorphism induced by the action of
\gal(K/\QQ)
on
\left<P_{n}\right>
:

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\gal(K/\QQ)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left(\ZZ/n\ZZ\right)^{\times}

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\longmapsto a_{\sigma},

where
P_{n}^{\sigma}=a_{\sigma}P_{n}
. By the isomorphism theorem, we observe that
\gal(\QQ(P_{n})/\QQ)\cong\gal(K/\QQ)/\Ker(\Phi)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\left(\ZZ/n\ZZ\right)^{\times}
. Therefore,
[\QQ(P_{n}):\QQ]=\#\gal(\QQ(P_{n})/\QQ)
divides
\varphi(n)=\#(\ZZ/n\ZZ)^{\times}
.
∎

The second lemma provides a stronger result for the case where the elliptic curve
E
has a
p^{n-1}
-isogeny of a particular form.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\QQ
, and let
K
be a finite Galois extension of
\QQ
such that
E(K)[p^{n}]=\left<P_{p^{n}},Q_{p}\right>\cong(\ZZ/p^{n}\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/p\ZZ)
, where
p
is a prime number,
n
is a positive integer,
P_{p^{n}}\in E(K)[p^{n}]\setminus E(\QQ)
is a point of order
p^{n}
, and
Q_{p}\in E(\QQ)[p]
is a point of order
p
. If the extension degree
[K:\QQ]
is coprime to
p
, then
[\QQ(P_{p^{n}}):\QQ]
divides
p-1
.

Proof..

Consider the following map:

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\gal(K/\QQ)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left(\ZZ/p^{n}\ZZ\right)^{\times}

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\longmapsto a_{\sigma},

where
P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma}=a_{\sigma}P_{p^{n}}+b_{\sigma}Q_{p}
. We claim that
\Phi
is a well-defined group homomorphism. First, we show that
a_{\sigma}
is indeed coprime to
p
. Suppose, for contradiction, that
a_{\sigma}
is divisible by
p
. There are two cases to consider:

(i)

If
n=1
, then
P_{p}^{\sigma}=b_{\sigma}Q_{p}
, which implies that
P_{p}\in E(\QQ)
. However, this contradicts the assumption that
P_{p}\notin E(\QQ)
.

(ii)

If
n\geq 2
, then
P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma}
, hence
P_{p^{n}}
, would not have order
p^{n}
, which contradicts the definition of
P_{p^{n}}
.

Thus,
a_{\sigma}
must be coprime to
p
.

Next, we verify that
\Phi
is a group homomorphism. Let
\sigma,\tau\in\gal(K/\QQ)
. Then,

P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma\tau}=(P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma})^{\tau}=(a_{\sigma}P_{p^{n}}+b_{\sigma}Q_{p})^{\tau}=a_{\tau}a_{\sigma}P_{p^{n}}+(a_{\sigma}b_{\tau}+b_{\sigma})Q_{p}.

Thus,
a_{\sigma\tau}=a_{\sigma}a_{\tau}
, showing that
\Phi
is a group homomorphism.

Now, consider the kernel of
\Phi
:

\Ker(\Phi)=\left\{\sigma\in\gal(K/\QQ)\;|\;P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma}=P_{p^{n}}+b_{\sigma}Q_{p}\right\}.

Let
\sigma\in\Ker(\Phi)
. Composing the action of
\sigma
for
\order(\sigma)
times, we get

P_{p^{n}}=P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma^{\order(\sigma)}}=P_{p^{n}}+\order(\sigma)b_{\sigma}Q_{p}.

Therefore,
[\order(\sigma)b_{\sigma}]Q_{p}=\OO
, meaning that
\order(\sigma)b_{\sigma}
must be divisible by
p
. Since
\order(\sigma)
is coprime to
p
, we conclude that
b_{\sigma}
must be divisible by
p
. Hence the kernel of
\Phi
is given by

\Ker(\Phi)=\left\{\sigma\in\gal(K/\QQ)\;|\;P_{p^{n}}^{\sigma}=P_{p^{n}}\right\}.

Finally, by Galois theory, we have

\gal(\QQ(P_{p^{n}})/\QQ)\cong\gal(K/\QQ)/\Ker(\Phi),

which, by the isomorphism theorem, is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\left(\ZZ/p^{n}\ZZ\right)^{\times}
. Since
\varphi(p^{n})=p^{n-1}(p-1)
and
[K:\QQ]
is coprime to
p
, we conclude that
[\QQ(P_{p^{n}}):\QQ]
divides
p-1
.
∎

3.
Elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant

In the present section, we briefly review the theory of elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant and prove some necessary results for the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero. We know that every elliptic curve
E
defined over
K
can be represented by the form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B\;\;\;\;(A,B\in K).

Suppose its
j
-invariant
j(E)
satisfies
j(E)\in\QQ\setminus\{0,1728\}
, and define
E^{\prime}
to be the elliptic curve

(1)

E^{\prime}\colon y^{2}=x^{3}-\frac{27j(E)}{j(E)-1728}x+\frac{54j(E)}{j(E)-1728}.

Observe that
E^{\prime}
is defined over
\QQ
and satisfies
j(E^{\prime})=j(E)
. For simplicity, denote the coefficients of
E^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneq-\frac{27j(E)}{j(E)-1728},

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneq\frac{54j(E)}{j(E)-1728}.

Since
j(E)=j(E^{\prime})
is not 0 or 1728, by the definition of the
j
-invariant, all these coefficients
A,B,A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are non-zero. It is known (see J. H. Silverman
[
24
]
) that
E^{\prime}
is a quadratic twist of
E
by
D=AB^{\prime}/BA^{\prime}\in K
, that is

E^{\prime}=E^{D}:\;y^{2}=x^{3}+AD^{2}x+BD^{3},

and there exists an isomorphism
\phi
, defined over the field
L_{K,D}\coloneq K(\sqrt{D})
, as follows:

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi\colon E&\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}E^{\prime}\\
(x,y)&\longmapsto\left(xD,yD\sqrt{D}\right).\end{split}

Remark 3.1
.

The field
L_{K,D}
depends only on the choice of the equation for the elliptic curve
E
.

We use the above notation for the rest of this section.

Notation 3.2
.

From now, if
E
is an elliptic curve defined over a field
K
of characteristic zero, satisfying
j(E)\in\QQ\setminus\{0,1728\}
, then
E^{\prime}
is the elliptic curve defined by the formula in (
1
),
D
is the element of
K
such that
E^{\prime}=E^{D}
,
L=L_{K,D}=K(\sqrt{D})
is the field of definition of the isomorphism
\phi
, as defined in (
2
), and
\widehat{L}
is the Galois closure of
L
over
\QQ
, in case
L/\QQ
is an algebraic extension.

From the discussion above, we get a simple lemma about the image by
\phi
of points of finite order of
E
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
E(K)
has a point
P_{n}
of order
n
, then its image
\phi(P_{n})
in
E^{\prime}(L)
belongs to
E^{\prime}(K)
if and only if
\sqrt{D}\in K
, that is
L=K
, or
n=2
.

Proof..

Let
P_{n}=(x,y)\in E(K)
. Its image
\phi(P)=(xD,yD\sqrt{D})
belongs to
E^{\prime}(K)
if and only if
yD\sqrt{D}\in K
, which is equivalent to
\sqrt{D}\in K
or
y=0
. The latter case means that
P_{n}=(x,0)
has order
n=2
.
∎

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to relate the properties of elliptic curves defined over the field
\QQ(2^{\infty})
with
j
-invariant in
\QQ\setminus\{0,1728\}
, to properties of elliptic curves defined over
\QQ
.

The most general theorem of this nature is the following:

Theorem 3.4

(E. González-Jimenéz and J. M. Tornero
[

, Theorem 3)
.

Let

be an elliptic curve over a field

a non-square element of

a quadratic twist of

\Psi\circ\overline{\Psi}=[2]\times[2]

Moreover,
\Ker(\Psi)\subset E(L)[2]

\coker(\Psi)
and

are equal to 2.

Finally, for

In Section

is Galois. For this we need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be an elliptic curve defined over
\QQ
,

an extesion of
\QQ
and
\widehat{L}
its Galois closure over
\QQ
. For every

\displaystyle\nu:E(L)

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}E(L^{\sigma})

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\sigma(x),\sigma(y)),

is the image of

by

Proof..

Since

is defined over
\QQ
, the point

belongs to

∎

Lemma 3.7

Lemma 3.8

4.
Technical lemmas from group theory

As a final preparation for the proof of Theorem
1.2
, in this section, we present two necessary lemmas on groups.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
G
is a group with normal subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
where
H_{1}
has order
2
,
G/H_{1}
has exponent
2
, and
G/H_{2}\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ
, then
G
is abelian.

Proof..

Since both the quotient groups
G/H_{1}
and
G/H_{2}
are abelian, the commutator subgroup
[G,G]
is contained in
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
, implying that
[G,G]
has order 1 or 2. Now, if
[G,G]
has order 2, that is,
H_{1}=[G,G]\subseteq H_{2}
, then, by the isomorphism theorem, we obtain

(G/H_{1})/(H_{2}/H_{1})\cong G/H_{2}\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ,

which is not possible, since
G/H_{1}
only has points of order 2. Therefore,
[G,G]=\left\{e\right\}
and
G
is abelian.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If
G
is a finite group of order
2^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
, having normal subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
such that
G/H_{1}\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^{\oplus(n-2)}
and
G/H_{2}\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ
, respectively, then every subgroup of
H_{1}
is normal in
G
.

Proof..

We need to prove this for a non-abelian group
G
. By the same argument in the proof of the last lemma, it follows that
H_{1}\not\subseteq H_{2}
and
[G,G]\subseteq H_{1}\cap H_{2}
. Since
G
is non-abelian we conclude that
[G,G]
has order 2. If
H_{1}\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ
, then its unique non-trivial subgroup is
[G,G]
which we know that is a normal subgroup of
G
. From now on, suppose
H_{1}\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/2\ZZ)
. First, we observe that every element
x\in G
has order dividing 4. Indeed, since

G/H_{1}\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^{\oplus(n-2)},

we have
x^{2}\in H_{1}
, which implies
x^{4}=e
. In particular, it follows that
G^{2}
is a subset of
H_{1}
and since we can prove, using the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, that

(6)

G/[G,G]\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^{\oplus(n-3)}\oplus\ZZ/4\ZZ,

we conclude that
G^{2}\neq[G,G]
, otherwise
G^{2}
would be a normal subgroup of
G
implying that
G/G^{2}
is a 2-elementary abelian group, contradicting the isomorphism (
6
). Since
G^{2}
has order at least 2, we have
H_{1}=G^{2}[G,G]
.

Finally, we claim that both
[G,G]
and
G^{2}
are subsets of
\Z(G)
, the center of
G
, concluding that
H_{1}\subseteq\Z(G)
, and every subgroup of
H_{1}
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Let
[G,G]=\{e,a\}
. Since
[G,G]
is a normal subgroup of
G
, for every element
x\in G
we have
x^{-1}ax=a
, where it follows that
[G,G]\subseteq\Z(G)
.

Now, let
y^{2}\in G^{2}
. For every
x\in G
we have three possibilities:

(i)

If
[x,y]=e
, then
x
commutes with
y
and, in particular, with
y^{2}
.

(ii)

If
[y^{-1},x]=e
, then
x
commutes with
y^{-1}
and, in particular, with
y^{-2}=y^{2}
.

(iii)

If neither (i) nor (ii) holds, that is, if
[x,y]\neq e
and
[y^{-1},x]\neq e
, then, since
[G,G]
has order 2,

[x,y]=[y^{-1},x].

By some calculations, we get

[x,y^{2}]=[x,y]^{2}=e,

showing that
x
commutes with
y^{2}
.

Since
y^{2}\in G^{2}
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that
G^{2}\subseteq\Z(G)
.
∎

\QQ(2^{\infty})=\QQ(\{\sqrt{m}\colon m\in\ZZ\})

Let

is not a Galois extension, and its Galois closure

satisfies

. Then, there is no intermediate field

of the extension

such that

is a quartic cyclic extension of

Proof..

Suppose there exists a quartic cyclic extension

of

\widehat{L}
. Let

be such that
\widehat{L}=\QQ(2^{\infty})(\alpha)
,

and

, respectively. Let
\alpha^{(i)}
(

,

over
\QQ
and let

\displaystyle\alpha^{(i)}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{(i)}+a_{1}^{(i)}\alpha+a_{2}^{(i)}\alpha^{2}+a_{3}^{(i)}\alpha^{3}\;\;(a_{0}^{(i)},a_{1}^{(i)},a_{2}^{(i)},a_{3}^{(i)}\in\QQ(2^{\infty}),\;i\in I)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=b_{0}+b_{1}\alpha+b_{2}\alpha^{2}+b_{3}\alpha^{3}\;\;(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in\QQ(2^{\infty}))

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{1}\alpha+c_{2}\alpha^{2}+c_{3}\alpha^{3}\;\;(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\QQ(2^{\infty}))

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle=x^{4}+d_{3}x^{3}+d_{2}x^{2}+d_{1}x+d_{0}\;\;(d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\QQ(2^{\infty}))

be, respectively, the representation of
\alpha^{(i)}
,
\beta
and
\gamma
in the
\QQ(2^{\infty})
-basis

of
\widehat{L}

and its extension

. Since

if and only if

. Together with the definition of
\QQ(2^{\infty})
, this yields the following unique property of 2-torsion points.

Lemma 5.6
.

If
E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))[2]\neq\{\mathcal{O}\}
, then
E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))[2]=E[2]
and
E^{\prime}(\QQ)[2]\neq\{\mathcal{O}\}
.

Proof..

Recall that
E^{\prime}
has the form

E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}\;\;(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\in\QQ).

A point
P=(x,y)\in E^{\prime}(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
has order 2 if and only if
y=0
and
x
is a root of the cubic polynomial
x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}
. If
E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))[2]\neq\{\mathcal{O}\}
, then, by Lemma
3.3
,
E^{\prime}(\QQ(2^{\infty}))[2]\neq\{\mathcal{O}\}
and
x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}
has a root in
\QQ(2^{\infty})
. If
x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}
is irreducible in
\QQ[x]
then the extension degree of the field of definition of the point of order 2 in
E^{\prime}(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
is 3, which is not possible since
\QQ(2^{\infty})
contains no subfield of degree 3 over
\QQ
. This implies that
x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}
has a root in
\QQ
and the other roots also belong to
\QQ(2^{\infty})
. Therefore,
E^{\prime}(\QQ(2^{\infty}))[2]=E^{\prime}[2]
, and cosequently, by Lemma
3.3
,
E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))[2]=E[2]
.
∎

This lemma allows us to use the following theorem from Knapp
[
17
]
.

Theorem 5.7

(Knapp
[
17
]
, Theorem 4.2)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic not equal to 2 or 3, and let

be an elliptic curve defined over
K
given by

with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in K
. For

, there exists a
K
-rational point

such that
[2]Q=P
if and only if
x-\alpha,x-\beta,x-\gamma
are all squares in
K
. Moreover, if we fix each sign of
\sqrt{x-\alpha}
,
\sqrt{x-\beta}
and
\sqrt{x-\gamma}
, then

equals one of the following:

\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\beta}\pm\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\gamma}\pm\sqrt{x-\beta}\sqrt{x-\gamma}+x

or

-\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\beta}\pm\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\gamma}\mp\sqrt{x-\beta}\sqrt{x-\gamma}+x.

The first case we need to verify is when

contains all the points of order 2.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
has a point of order
4
. If
E^{\prime}[2]\subseteq E^{\prime}(\QQ)
then
E^{\prime}[4]\subseteq E^{\prime}(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
and

. In particular,

is isomorphic to one of the groups in Theorem
1.1
and

satisfies the conclusion of Proposition

Proof..

The hypothesis is equivalent to

E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}=(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)(x-\gamma)\;\;\;(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\QQ).

This implies that
\sqrt{\alpha-\beta},\sqrt{\alpha-\gamma}
, and
\sqrt{\beta-\gamma}
are in
\QQ(2^{\infty})
. By Theorem
5.7
,

contains

and, by Lemma

.
∎

From this point onward, we consider the case

When

Using this notation we can prove the following two lemmas:

Lemma 5.10
.

If
E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
has a point of order

and

, then
\widehat{L}
is contained in
\QQ(\D_{4}^{\infty})
, the compositum of all Galois extensions

with Galois group

isomorphic to

Let

its image in

. By Lemma

. By Theorem

, we know that either one of the following conditions is satisfied (using Notation

•

\sqrt{a-b\sqrt{n}}

Hence, either

Lemma 5.11
.

If
(\ZZ/8\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/8\ZZ)\subseteq E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
and
E^{\prime}(\QQ)[2]\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ
, then
L=\QQ(2^{\infty})
.

Proof..

Suppose that
L\neq\QQ(2^{\infty})
. By Lemma
3.7
, we know that
L/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension, and by the proof of Lemma
5.10
, we have

L=\QQ(2^{\infty})(\sqrt{\sqrt{n}})=\QQ(2^{\infty})(\sqrt{a+b\sqrt{n}}).

Since
E^{\prime}[4]\subseteq E^{\prime}(L)
, Theorem
5.7
implies that the

-coordinates of the points of order 4 in

are one of the following:

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}n},

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{a+b\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{2b\sqrt{n}}+(a+b\sqrt{n}),

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{a-b\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{-2b\sqrt{n}}+(a-b\sqrt{n}).

Since

has 12 points of order 4, all six possibilities listed above indeed occur as
x
-coordinates of some point of order 4. Using these

-coordinates, the assumption that

, and Theorem
5.7
, we obtain that

\sqrt{\sqrt{a+b\sqrt{n}}}\sqrt{\sqrt{2b}}

belongs to

. Let

\beta\coloneq\sqrt{a+b\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{2b},

and observe that

,

derived above, we obtain

∎

With the results from the lemmas in this subsection, along with the lists of Theorems

Lemma 5.12
.

E(\QQ(2^{\infty}))
has no point of order

.

Proof..

Suppose that

and

Now, when

is a Galois extension we have three possibilities:

is isomorphic to

Together, Lemmas

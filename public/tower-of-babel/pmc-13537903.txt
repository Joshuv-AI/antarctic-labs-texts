Repeated Immunization Drives Coordinated Ag Presentation Associated With B- and CD8+ T-Cell Responses in Liver-Stage Malaria
Authors: Nikunj Tandel, Rajeev K. Tyagi
Published: 2026

The limited efficacy of subunit malaria vaccines targeting the liver stage (LS) remains a major obstacle to inducing durable and functionally effective immune responses against this intracellular pathogen (1). Although the LS represents a critical bottleneck in the Plasmodium life cycle and a stage at which sterile protection can be achieved, vaccine strategies targeting LS Ags have so far generated only sub-optimal short-lived immunity (2). The protein-based vaccine formulations elicit poor immunity to the LS stage infection (1). The latter is due to the inefficient induction of CD8+ T cell response essentially required to eliminate the parasitized hepatocytes.

These responses depend on antigen-presenting cells (APCs), with dendritic cells (DCs) long regarded as the principal mediators of cross-presentation of exogenous Ags to MHC class I molecule (34). However, Ag acquisition and presentation may involve a more dynamic and context-dependent network (5), particularly during repeated Ag exposure (6).

These considerations have important implications for adjuvant design, since adjuvants can shape not only the magnitude but also the quality of immunity by modulating Ag availability, APC activation, and the inflammatory milieu (7). In this context, poly(I:C) and R848 are of particular interest for LS malaria vaccine development. Poly(I:C), a synthetic analog of double-stranded RNA, activates TLR3 and induces type I IFN-dominated responses (8), whereas R848 stimulates TLR7/8 signaling and promotes pro-inflammatory cytokine production along with APC activation (9).

Our recent studies using both model Ag (ovalbumin) and LS malaria Ags suggested that poly(I:C) and R848 could enhance Ag-specific Ab responses, improve Ab maturation, and support long-lasting humoral immunity (1011). These findings indicate robust germinal center activity and highlight the capability of these adjuvants to shape both the magnitude and quality of the immune responses (10). Altogether, our observation suggest that repeated immunization could influence Ag processing and presentation to support integrated humoral and cellular immunity.

Within this framework, B cells may contribute to Ag presentation beyond their established role in Ab production (121314). Although DCs remain central to cross-presentation, B cell associated recognition and Ab-mediated processes may modulate Ag uptake, processing, and presentation during repeated immunization (15). Therefore, repeated immunization with malaria LS Ags may alter Ag-presentation dynamics. The repeated exposure, distinct from the natural mosquito-bite exposure, may help link humoral responses to the CD8+ T cell priming.

B cell expansion, affinity maturation, and immune complex formation may enhance Ag capture and Fc receptor-mediated uptake by APCs such as DCs and macrophages (Fig. 1) (16). This may, in turn, support MHC class-I-restricted presentation and CD8+ T cell priming. Overall, these observations advocated that repeated immunization may favor B cell mediated Ag presentation to increase the CD8+ T cell priming.

There have been a few questions remains unanswered; whether naïve and/or memory B cells can directly contribute to the cross-presentation in malaria vaccination, how B cell receptor-mediated uptake influences this process, and whether specific B-cell subsets are responsible for shaping CD8+ T cell responses. We believe addressing these scientific questions shall support the proposed model.

In essence, poly(I:C) and R848 illustrate how adjuvants may amplify immune response and answer in shaping the Ag presentation context in which humoral and cellular immunity are coordinated. Although poly(I:C) and R848 are highlighted here as illustrative adjuvant candidates, other adjuvant(s) or their combination may elicit humoral and cell-mediated immune responses. If B cells play a direct or indirect role in CD8+ T cell activation, malaria subunit vaccines could be designed to include B cell epitopes that also contain CD4+ and CD8+ T cell epitopes. The latter may help overcome the current immune threshold and support the induction of sterilizing immunity. This conceptual framework supports the development of next-generation conferring long-lasting protection.

Picard Groups in Equivariant Algebra and Stable Homotopy Theory
Jesse Keyes and Jordan Sawdy

Abstract
. Traditionally, homotopy groups in G G -equivariant stable homotopy theory have been graded over RO ⁡ ( G ) \RO(G) , the real representation ring of G G . It is arguably more natural to grade homotopical structures over the Picard group of the equivariant stable homotopy category. Though there is a canonical map of abelian groups RO ⁡ ( G ) → Pic ⁡ ( Ho ⁡ ( 𝒮 ​ p G ) ) \RO(G)\rightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})) relating the two, this map is neither injective or surjective in general. Fausk, Lewis, and May give an algebraic expression of Pic ⁡ ( Ho ⁡ ( 𝒮 ​ p G ) ) \Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})) in terms of the Picard group of the Burnside ring A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) , and this work suggests a folklore isomorphism between Pic ⁡ ( A ⁡ ( G ) ) \Pic(A(G)) and Pic ⁡ ( ℳ ​ ack G ) \Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}) . We prove the existence of this folklore isomorphism in the setting of finite groups, then leverage our analysis to prove a classification of invertible Mackey functors in the setting of finite abelian groups. As a consequence, we furnish a classification of invertible A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) -modules again for G G a finite abelian group.



1.
Introduction

Constructing the
G
-equivariant stable homotopy category,
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
, involves inverting the real representation spheres associated to
G
with respect to the smash product of
G
-spaces. Consequently, we can consider the homotopy of any object in
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
as
\RO(G)
-graded. Historically, the structure of
RO(G)
-graded homotopy groups has been used in homological settings when a theory of Poincaré duality is desired.
It is also known that
RO(G)
-graded homotopy groups detect weak equivalences for
G=C_{2}
, so often the choice to compute these groups is made to forego computing homotopy Mackey functors. On the other hand,
\mathbb{Z}
-graded homotopy Mackey functors detect weak equivalences by definition.
There is yet another group over which we can grade homotopy groups or Mackey functors: the Picard group of
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
. Grading homotopy groups or Mackey functors over the Picard group of
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
is justified formally in that no choice is made in what invertible objects (up to isomorphism) we choose to grade homotopical structures over.

Essentially, two choices are being made. The first choice is whether to regard
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
as enriched in abelian groups or in
G
-Mackey functors, though these are related via change of enrichment along the lax monoidal functor
\ev_{G/G}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathscr{A}\textnormal{b}}
. The second choice comes from a more general formalism discussed throughout
[
3
]
: given any additive (or
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
-enriched) closed symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
, one can define a multi-graded abelian group (respectively,
G
-Mackey functor)
\pi_{*}(X)
for any
X\in\mathscr{C}
. Importantly, the grading here can be over any subgroup
A\leq\Pic(\mathscr{C})
, where
\Pic(\mathscr{C})
denotes the Picard group of
\mathscr{C}
. From this, it is clear that grading over
\Pic(\mathscr{C})
is a canonical choice.

In
[
4
, Theorem 0.1]
, Fausk, Lewis, and May give an algebraic description of
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
as an extension of
\Pic(A(G))
by some free abelian group, where
A(G)
is the Burnside ring for
G
. In particular, they prove that there is an embedding

\Pic(A(G))\coloneqq\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})\cong\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{\pi_{0}(\mathbb{S}_{G})})\hookrightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

whose cokernel is given by some free abelian group. Further,
\Pic(A(G))
is computed by tom Dieck and Petrie; see the discussion following
[
13
, Proposition 11.3]
. If one regards
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
as enriched in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
, the work of
[
4
]
raises the question of whether an embedding

\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\cong\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{\underline{A}})\cong\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{\underline{\pi}_{0}(\mathbb{S}_{G})})\hookrightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

with the same free cokernel exists. Such an embedding would imply an isomorphism between
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
and
\Pic(A(G))
. It is folklore that there exists an isomorphism
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\rightarrow\Pic(A(G))
induced by evaluating invertible Mackey functors at level
{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}
.

1.1.
Outline of Results

The first goal of this article is to give a precise account of the folklore statement above: for
G
a finite group, we build an isomorphism
\Pic(A(G))\to\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
. This analysis goes through
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
along the same lines as
[
4
]
. We define a full symmetric monoidal subcategory,
k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
, of
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
consisting of
G
-Mackey functors which satisfy a finiteness condition analogous to that in
[
4
]
for Künneth objects.

Theorem A
.

There is a split exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\rightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\xrightarrow{\Dim}\mathbb{Z}^{r}\rightarrow 0

in which the image of
\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
in
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
is precisely the isomorphism classes of invertible Künneth objects in
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
. In particular, we learn that
\Pic(A(G))
is isomorphic to
\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
.

To obtain an isomorphism
\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\cong\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
, we show that the kernel of the dimension function above contains
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
.

Theorem B
.

There exists an embedding
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\hookrightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
whose image is contained in the kernel of the dimension function. Consequently, the inclusion
k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\subseteq{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
induces an isomorphism on Picard groups.

Following this identification of
\Pic(A(G))
with
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
, we build explicit representatives for the isomorphism classes of invertible Mackey functors, and our approach is largely inspired by that of
[
1
, Definition 4.5]
. This classification furnishes that of invertible
A(G)
-modules, recovering the computation of
\Pic(A(G))
given by
[
13
]
.

Theorem C
.

There is an isomorphism

T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}\coloneqq\prod_{H\leq G}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:H]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}\rightarrow\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})

given by sending
\alpha\in T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}
to the corresponding twisted Burnside Mackey functor. That is, every invertible Mackey functor is isomorphic to a twisted Burnside Mackey functor.

The main technical hurdle in justifying the folklore isomorphism
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\rightarrow\Pic(A(G))
is that the functor
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\to{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
is not strong symmetric monoidal, so it need not send invertible objects to invertible objects in general. However, this can be rectified by restricting the domain, as shown in the following result:

Theorem D
.

The functor
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
given by evaluation at
{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}
is a strong symmetric monoidal equivalence of categories. That is, it induces an isomorphism

\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\cong\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})\cong\Pic(A(G)).

We note that Künneth objects in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
coincide with dualizable objects (i.e., finitely generated projective modules).

Corollary
.

The map

T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}\rightarrow\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\xrightarrow{\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}}\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})\cong\Pic(A(G))

sending
\alpha\in T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}
to the corresponding twisted Burnside module is an isomorphism. That is, every invertible
A(G)
-module is isomorphic to a twisted Burnside module.

2.
Picard Groups

The Picard group is a fundamental invariant associated to symmetric monoidal categories, consisting of the isomorphism classes of invertible objects. We begin by recalling the notion of Picard groups. See
[
8
, Chapter XI]
for an account of symmetric monoidal categories and strong symmetric monoidal functors.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
be a symmetric monoidal category. The
Picard group
of
\mathscr{C}
, denoted
\Pic(\mathscr{C})
, is the abelian group consisting of isomorphism classes of invertible objects with respect to
\otimes
. An object
X\in\mathscr{C}
is
invertible, or
\otimes
-invertible,
if there exists an object
Y\in\mathscr{C}
with
X\otimes Y\cong\mathbbm{1}
. The group operation in
\Pic(\mathscr{C})
is induced by
\otimes
.

The full signature of a symmetric monoidal category includes more than we require in
Definition

2.1
, namely the structure of associators, unitors, etc. We suppress this data in the signatures of symmetric monoidal categories throughout and refer to this structure only if explicitly necessary.

Remark 2.2
.

An object
X\in(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
is
\otimes
-invertible if and only if the endofunctor
(-)\otimes X
is an automorphism of
\mathscr{C}
.

As the Picard group is defined in terms of the monoidal structure, one would expect that
\Pic
is functorial for strong symmetric monoidal functors as these functors are precisely those which preserve the structure of symmetric monoidal categories.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given a strong symmetric monoidal functor
F\colon\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}
between symmetric monoidal categories, there is an induced map of abelian groups
F\colon\Pic(\mathscr{C})\rightarrow\Pic(D)
.

Proof.

Strong symmetric monoidality of a functor implies the preservation of invertibility for objects.
∎

We will see an application of this fact in the context of equivariant algebra, where several change of groups functors are strong symmetric monoidal functors.

A class of examples immediately arise from commutative monoids in symmetric monoidal categories. We will denote the category of commutative monoids in a symmetric monoidal category,
\mathscr{C}
, by
\textnormal{C}\mathcal{M}\textnormal{on}(\mathscr{C})
. If
\mathscr{C}
is cocomplete and the functor
R\otimes(-)
preserves coequalizers for
R\in\textnormal{C}\mathcal{M}\textnormal{on}(\mathscr{C})
, then
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{R}
inherits a symmetric monoidal structure via the usual construction using coequalizers. We will denote this symmetric monoidal product by
(-)\otimes_{R}(-)
. For details, see
[
7
, Lemma 2.2.2]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
be a cocomplete, symmetric monoidal category and
R
be a commutative monoid in
\mathscr{C}
such that
R\otimes(-)
preserves coequalizers. The
Picard group
of
R
, denoted
\Pic(R)
, is the Picard group of the symmetric monoidal category
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{R}
of modules over
R
equipped with
\otimes_{R}
.

For example, one could consider
R\in{\textnormal{C}\mathscr{R}\textnormal{ing}}\simeq\textnormal{C}\mathcal{M}\textnormal{on}({\mathscr{A}\textnormal{b}})
or
\underline{R}\in{\mathscr{G}\textnormal{reen}}_{G}\simeq\textnormal{C}\mathcal{M}\textnormal{on}({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
, where
{\mathscr{G}\textnormal{reen}}_{G}
is the symmetric monoidal category of
G
-Green functors. In the case that

, we have
\Pic(R)\coloneqq\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{R})\cong\Pic(\mathscr{C})
, since the unit in any symmetric monoidal category is uniquely a commutative monoid and every object in

is canonically a module over the unit.

Remark 2.5
.

[

The Picard groups arising from commutative rings in this way can be thought of algebro-geometrically as a higher analog of the group of units. Let

\displaystyle R^{\times}

\displaystyle\cong

Example 2.6
.

The following symmetric monoidal categories have trivial Picard groups:
({\mathscr{S}\textnormal{et}},\times,\{*\})
,
({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{\mathbb{Z}},\otimes,\mathbb{Z})
,

The Picard group of the stable homotopy category is isomorphic to

Definition 3.1

Equivalently, a Mackey functor

•

•

3.2.
Change of Groups

Throughout this section, we let
G
denote a finite group. Further, let
H\leq G
and
N\trianglelefteq G
with
Q={}^{G}\!/_{\!N}
. There are several adjunctions relating the category of
G
-Mackey functors to the categories of
Q
-Mackey functors and
H
-Mackey functors which allow us to inductively approach a computation of
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
. We begin by recalling the usual induction-restriction adjunction.

Proposition 3.11
.

There is an adjunction

{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{H},
\perp
\uparrow_{H}^{G}
\downarrow_{H}^{G}

where
\uparrow_{H}^{G}(\underline{M})({}^{G}\!/_{\!J})\coloneqq\underline{M}(\res_{H}^{G}({}^{G}\!/_{\!J}))
and
\downarrow_{H}^{G}(\underline{N})({}^{H}\!/_{\!K})\coloneqq\underline{N}({}^{G}\!/_{\!K})
for
\underline{M}\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{H}
and
\underline{N}\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
. The restrictions, transfers, and conjugations in both
\uparrow_{H}^{G}\underline{M}
and
\downarrow_{H}^{G}\underline{N}
are induced by those in
\underline{M}
and
\underline{N}
, respectively. We will refer to
\uparrow_{H}^{G}
and
\downarrow_{H}^{G}
as the induction and restriction of Mackey functors, respectively.

Proof.

The induction and restriction of Mackey functors are obtained by pre-composing with the restriction and induction functors on Burnside categories, respectively, and these functors on Burnside categories are appropriately adjoint so as to induce the desired adjunction.
∎

Example 3.12
.

The induction of
\mathbb{Z}\in{\mathscr{A}\textnormal{b}}\simeq{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{e}
to the cyclic group of order two,
C_{2}
, has the following Lewis diagram:

\mathbb{Z}
\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}].
\Delta
\nabla

Here,
\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}]
is the free abelian group with generators given by the elements of
C_{2}
and
C_{2}
-action given by multiplication in
C_{2}
. Then,
\Delta
is the diagonal map which picks out the sum of the basis elements and
\nabla
is the “fold” map which sends every basis element to
1
.

This examples demonstrates the more general failure of
\uparrow_{H}^{G}
to be strong symmetric monoidal for
H\lneq G
as it does not preserve the unit up to isomorphism. However,
\downarrow_{H}^{G}
is always a strong symmetric monoidal functor. In particular, this implies that the restriction of Mackey functors induces a map

\downarrow_{H}^{G}\colon\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\rightarrow\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{H}).

as in
Proposition

2.3
.

The restriction of Mackey functors also has a right adjoint,
coinduction
, which agrees with induction. This generalizes the same phenomenon we see in representations of finite groups. We now turn our view towards a few adjunctions which witness the close relationship between
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
and
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{Q}
. The change of groups between
G
and its various quotients behaves much more nicely concerning invertibility.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
q^{*}\colon\mathscr{B}_{Q}^{op}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{G}^{op}
denote the functor between Burnside categories given by viewing
Q
-sets and spans of
Q
-sets as
G
-sets and spans of
G
-sets, respectively, via pulling back along the quotient map
q\colon G\twoheadrightarrow Q
. We get a functor
\qRes_{Q}^{G}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{Q}
given by precomposition with
q^{*}
which we will refer to as
restriction along the quotient from
G
to
Q
. This functor is denoted by
\beta^{!}
in
[
11
, Section 5]
and goes by the name “deflation” in various places throughout the literature.

Conceptually, the restriction along a quotient
G\twoheadrightarrow{}^{G}\!/_{\!N}
identifies the subgroups of
{}^{G}\!/_{\!N}
with those in
G
containing
N
and simply forgets the data of
\underline{M}
for subgroups not containing
\underline{N}
. We find the following visualization of the restriction along a quotient useful:

Heuristic
.

\underline{M}\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
to
\qRes_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!N}}^{G}\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!N}}
can be viewed as follows:

\underline{M}=
\bullet
N
\bullet
G
\bullet
{}^{N}\!/_{\!N}
\bullet
{}^{G}\!/_{\!N}

We suppress the Weyl actions in the above pictures for aesthetic reasons, but note that the actions of various Weyl groups in

follow from the isomorphisms
{}^{H}\!/_{\!J}\cong{}^{{}^{H}\!/_{\!N}}\!/_{\!{}^{J}\!/_{\!N}}
for
N\leq J\leq H\leq G
.

The restriction of Mackey functors along a quotient map has a strong symmetric monoidal left adjoint which we will denote by
\qInd_{Q}^{G}
.

Definition 3.14
.

The
induction of Mackey functors along a quotient map

q\colon G\twoheadrightarrow Q
is the functor

\qInd_{Q}^{G}(-)\coloneqq\Lan_{q^{*}}(-)\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{Q}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}.

This functor goes by the name of
\beta_{!}
in
[
11
, Section 5]
. We use the term “induction along a quotient” in light of this functor being left adjoint to restriction along a quotient as in
3.16
.

Proposition 3.15
.

The induction of Mackey functors along a quotient map is a strong symmetric monoidal functor
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{Q}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
.

Proof.

Since
q^{*}
is a product preserving functor, the left Kan extension of a Mackey functor along
q^{*}
is an product preserving functor
\mathscr{B}_{G}^{op}\rightarrow{\mathscr{A}\textnormal{b}}
, hence a Mackey functor. Left Kan extending along
q^{*}
is a symmetric monoidal functor for the box product as the box product is the Day convolution product of the Cartesian product with the tensor product of abelian groups and
q^{*}
is product preserving. That is,
\qInd_{Q}^{G}
is strong symmetric monoidal via commuting left Kan extensions.
∎

Proposition 3.16
.

There is an adjunction

{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{Q}
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
\perp
\qInd_{Q}^{G}
\qRes_{Q}^{G}

Proof.

This follows from defining
\qInd_{Q}^{G}
as the left Kan extension along
q^{*}
, while
\qRes_{Q}^{G}
is defined as precomposition with
q^{*}
.
∎

We also consider the
inflation of
Q
-Mackey functors along the quotient map
q
. This is a construction that produces
G
-Mackey functors and is closely related to the induction along a quotient map via a left inverse of the functor
q^{*}
.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\Fix_{N}\colon\mathscr{B}_{G}^{op}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{Q}^{op}
denote the functor given on
G
-sets and spans of
G
-sets by taking
N
-fixed points.

Proposition 3.18
.

The functor
\Fix_{N}
gives a left inverse of
q^{*}
.

Proof.

The
N
-fixed points of a
Q
-set
T
viewed as a
G
-set by pulling back along the quotient map are given by
T
.
∎

Definition 3.19
.

The

from

\Inf_{Q}^{G}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{Q}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}

Again, we describe the inflation functor as identifying the subgroups of

\underline{M}

.

The

There is an adjunction

{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}

\perp
\Phi^{N}
\Inf_{Q}^{G}

Proof.

Again,
\Phi^{N}
is defined by left Kan extending along

.

The
N
-geometric fixed points functor is strong symmetric monoidal.

Proof.

The left Kan extension along a product and coproduct preserving functor between Burnside categories gives a strong symmetric monoidal functor on Mackey functors. It suffices to check that

4.
The Picard Group of
G
-Spectra

4.1.
Künneth Objects and Dualizable Objects

Throughout this section,
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
will denote a closed symmetric monoidal category. We begin by recalling some finiteness conditions related to dualizability, then specialize to the (equivariant) algebraic setting of the category of modules over some sufficiently nice commutative monoid object. For more details concerning these notions, see
[
4
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

An object
X\in\mathscr{C}
is
dualizable
if there exists an object
Y\in\mathscr{C}
and maps

(1)

\eta\colon\mathbbm{1}\rightarrow X\otimes Y

(2)

\varepsilon\colon Y\otimes X\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}

called the
unit
and
counit
, respectively, which together satisfy the
triangle identities:

(1)

({\id_{X}}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ(\eta\otimes{\id_{X}})=\id_{X}

(2)

(\varepsilon\otimes{\id_{Y}})\circ({\id_{Y}}\otimes\eta)=\id_{Y}
.

Such an object
Y
is called a
dual of
X
, and it is unique up to a unique isomorphism preserving units and counits.

This definition is valid more generally in any symmetric monoidal category. However, in
closed
symmetric monoidal categories, it is particularly well-behaved: if
X
is dualizable, then it has a canonical choice of dual given by
DX\coloneqq[X,\mathbbm{1}]
, as well as a canonical counit given by the evaluation map
[X,\mathbbm{1}]\otimes X\to\mathbbm{1}
. In other words,
X
is dualizable if and only if there is a unit map
\eta\colon\mathbbm{1}\to X\otimes DX
which satisfies the triangle identities with the evaluation map.

Definition 4.2
.

A dualizable object
X
in a symmetric monoidal category is called a
Künneth object
if it is a retract of a finite copower of the unit object. That is,
X
is a retract of
\coprod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}
for some natural number
n
.

In the category of modules over a commutative ring, Künneth is equivalent to dualizable, which is equivalent to finitely generated and projective. However, this is not the case in general. For example, the Mackey functors
\uparrow_{H}^{G}\underline{A}\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
for
H\lneq G
are dualizable (i.e., finitely generated and projective), but they are not Künneth objects.

From here, we specialize to the context of additive symmetric monoidal categories. Moreover, we borrow notation from
[
4
]
, letting
k\mathscr{C}\subseteq d\mathscr{C}
denote the full subcategories of Künneth objects and dualizable objects in
\mathscr{C}
, respectively. These are additive symmetric monoidal subcategories of
\mathscr{C}
with strong symmetric monoidal inclusions
k\mathscr{C}\hookrightarrow d\mathscr{C}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
. Consequently, we set
\Iso(k\mathscr{C})
and
\Iso(d\mathscr{C})
to be the semirings of isomorphism classes of Künneth objects and dualizable objects, respectively, under
\oplus
and
\otimes
.

Lastly, we note that invertible objects are dualizable by
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
, and an inverse of an invertible object serves as a canonical choice of dual. One can see, in general, that invertibility does not imply that an object is a Künneth object. For example,
\Sigma^{\infty}S^{1}
is an invertible spectrum which is not a retract of
\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}
for any
n
.

4.2.
An Algebraic Description of
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

Throughout,
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
will denote the
G
-equivariant stable homotopy category for
G
a finite group. We regard
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
as a symmetric monoidal category equipped with its usual smash product and unit given by the sphere
G
-spectrum
\mathbb{S}_{G}\coloneqq\Sigma_{G}^{\infty}S^{0}
. In
[
4
]
, Fausk, Lewis, and May give an exact sequence involving the Picard group of
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{g})
. In fact, they prove the existence a more general embedding theorem for Picard groups:

Theorem 4.3
.

[
4
, Theorem 0.2]

Let
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
be a stable homotopy category. There is an embedding
\Pic(\End(\mathbbm{1}))\hookrightarrow\Pic(\mathscr{C})
, where
\End(\mathbbm{1})
is the ring of endomorphisms of
\mathbbm{1}
. The objects in the image of this embedding are the invertible objects which are retracts of finite coproducts of copies of
\mathbbm{1}
(i.e. invertible Künneth objects).

Specializing to
\mathscr{C}=\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
, they go on to extend this embedding to an exact sequence:

Theorem 4.4
.

[
4
, Theorem 0.1]

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. There is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Pic(A(G))\rightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow C(G),

where
C(G)
is the additive group of integer-valued, continuous functions on the space of closed subgroups of
G
.

In the case that
G
is a finite group, it immediately follows that the Picard group of the equivariant stable homotopy category decomposes as a direct sum
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}\oplus\Pic(A(G))
for some finite
r
. The quotient
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))/\Pic(A(G))
is identified with
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, where
r
is the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
. See the beginning of
[
1
, Section 3]
for a discussion of this fact which follows from
[
15
, Theorem III.5.4]
.

At this point, the obstruction to understanding
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
abstractly is to understand
\Pic(A(G))
, and tom Dieck-Petrie continue to provide.

Theorem 4.5
.

[
12
, (3.33)]
[
14
, Theorem 5]

For a finite group
G
, there is an isomorphism

\Pic(A(G))\cong\prod\limits_{(H)\in{}^{\Sub(G)}\!/_{\!\text{conj}}}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:H]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\},

where
{}^{\Sub(G)}\!/_{\!conj}
is the set of conjugacy classes of subgroups of
G
.

We obtain an additive description of
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
in the case that
G
is a finite group:

Theorem 4.6
.

[
4
,
12
,
15
]

For a finite group
G
, the Picard group of the
G
-equivariant stable homotopy category decomposes as a sum

\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}\oplus\prod\limits_{(H)\in{}^{\Sub(G)}\!/_{\!\text{conj}}}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:H]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\},

where
r
is the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
.

4.3.
An Equivariant Algebraic Description of
\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

The equivariant stable homotopy category is certainly an additive stable homotopy category. Consequently, the formalism of
[
4
]
applies to
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
as we have seen. However,
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
can be equipped with a richer structure: a
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
-enrichment. In order to recast the results of
[
4
]
with a view towards this
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
-enrichment, we first give a refined stratification of levels of dualizability in
G
-spectra.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
X\in\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
be a dualizable object. We say that
X
is
weakly Künneth
if it is a retract of a finite wedge of induced spheres. That is,
X
is a retract of
\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}({}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}{}_{+}}\wedge\mathbb{S}_{G})
for some natural number
n
.

It is immediately clear from the definition that Künneth objects are weakly Künneth objects: simply set
H_{i}=G
for all
i
. We have the symmetric monoidal full subcategory
w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
of weakly Künneth objects and strong symmetric monoidal inclusions

k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})\subseteq w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})\subseteq d\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}).

Similarly,
\Iso(w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
denotes the semiring of weakly Künneth objects under
\vee
and
\wedge
. We begin our analysis with a result analogous to
[
4
, Proposition 1.2]
:

Proposition 4.8
.

A dualizable object
X\in\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
is weakly Künneth if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

\underline{\pi}_{0}(DX)\boxtimes\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)\rightarrow\underline{\pi}_{0}(DX\wedge X)
is an isomorphism.

(2)

\varphi\colon\underline{\pi}^{0}(Y)\boxtimes\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)\cong\underline{\pi}_{0}(DY)\boxtimes\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)\rightarrow\underline{\pi}_{0}(DY\wedge X)\cong\pi_{0}([Y,X])
is an isomorphism for all dualizable objects
Y
.

(3)

\varphi\colon\underline{\pi}_{0}(Y)\boxtimes\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)\rightarrow\underline{\pi}_{0}(Y\wedge X)
is an isomorphism for all dualizable objects
Y
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 1.2]
.
∎

The above proposition generalizes
[
4
, Proposition 1.2]
in two ways: we take
\pi_{0}
as valued in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
, but we also replace Künneth with weakly Künneth. This is because Künneth objects do not coincide with dualizable objects in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
. In particular, if
X
is a Künneth object in
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
, then
\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)
is a Künneth object in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
. On the other hand, if
X
is a weakly Künneth object in
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
, then
\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)
is a dualizable Mackey functor.

Theorem 4.9
.

For
G
a finite group, the map
\underline{\pi}_{0}\colon\Iso(w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow\Iso(dMack_{G})
is a isomorphism of semirings. In particular, taking units gives an isomorphism

\underline{\pi}_{0}\colon\Pic(w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow\Pic(d{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})=\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}).

Proof.

See the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 1.5]
, in which weakly Künneth corresponds to finitely generated and projective.
∎

Moreover, we have analogous results for Künneth objects. By definition,
\underline{\pi}_{0}
of a Künneth object in
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
is a Künneth object in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
. Similarly, we get a map
\underline{\pi}_{0}\colon\Iso(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow\Iso(kMack_{G})
of semirings.

Theorem 4.10
.

For
G
a finite group, the map
\underline{\pi}_{0}\colon\Iso(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow\Iso(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
is an isomorphism of semirings. In particular, taking units gives an isomorphism

\underline{\pi}_{0}\colon\Pic(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}).

Proof.

Again, the proof is analogous to
[
4
, Proposition 1.5]
.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

We have the following commutative square:

\Pic(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
\Pic(w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
\Pic(d{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
\underline{\pi}_{0}
\underline{\pi}_{0}

It isn’t remarkable that this diagram commutes; we simply emphasize that the bottom horizontal map
need not
be an isomorphism. If one instead considers
A(G)
-modules, the following diagram commutes:

\Pic(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
\Pic(w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})
\Pic(d{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})
\cong
\pi_{0}
\pi_{0}

This comparison tells us that, a priori, considering
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
as
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
-enriched gives a richer analysis of invertible
G
-spectra. However, we will see in
Corollary

4.17
that the bottom left map is an isomorphism for finite groups.

4.4.
Building Invertible
G
-Spectra

As the preceding theorems suggest,
[
4
]
gives a construction for invertible Künneth objects in
\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
coming from
\Pic(A(G))
. Moreover, this construction is inverse to the map induced by
\pi_{0}
. We give a somewhat different construction here whose input is
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
G
be a finite group. There are embeddings

kE_{(-)}\colon\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\hookrightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

and

E_{(-)}\colon\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\hookrightarrow\Pic(\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

whose images are the isomorphism classes of invertible Künneth and weakly Künneth objects in
G
-spectra, respectively.

Proof.

Throughout, we denote
\uparrow_{H_{i}}^{G}\underline{A}
by
\underline{A}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}
. Let
\underline{M}
be an invertible Mackey functor. Since invertible objects are dualizable, there exists a retraction

with section
s\colon\underline{M}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{i}\underline{A}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}
. This yields an idempotent

s\circ r\in\End\left(\bigoplus_{i}\underline{A}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}\right).

One can check that localizing
\bigoplus_{i}\underline{A}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}
at this idempotent realizes
\underline{M}
. That is,

\underline{M}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\underline{A}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}[e_{r}^{-1}].

Moreover, our idempotent lifts to an idempotent

e_{r}\in\End\left(\bigvee_{i}{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}_{+}\wedge\mathbb{S}_{G}\right).

This follows from identifying
\underline{\pi}_{0}(\vee_{i}{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}_{+}\wedge\mathbb{S}_{G})
with
\oplus_{i}\underline{A}_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}
. Localizing this wedge of spheres at
e_{r}
yields a weakly Künneth (hence, connective)
G
-spectrum

E_{\underline{M}}\coloneqq\left(\bigvee_{i}{{}^{G}\!/_{\!H_{i}}}_{+}\wedge\mathbb{S}_{G}\right)[e_{r}^{-1}].

To see that
E_{\underline{M}}
is invertible, we first observe that the homotopy of
E_{\underline{M}}
is given by
\underline{M}\boxtimes\pi_{*}(\mathbb{S}_{G})
by construction. More generally, the homotopy of
E_{\underline{M}}\wedge X
for any
X\in\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G})
is given by

\underline{M}\boxtimes\underline{\pi}_{*}(X).

One can leverage this description to show that
E_{\underline{M}}
is independent of the choice of
\underline{M}
, retraction, and section. Indeed, if a different retraction or section is chosen, then one can build a map which induces a natural isomorphism of equivariant homology theories. An isomorphism of invertible Mackey functors will similarly yield a natural isomorphism of equivariant homology theories. In particular, we see that
E_{\underline{M}}
is invertible with inverse given by
E_{\underline{M}^{-1}}
.

We have constructed a set-theoretic section

E_{(-)}\colon\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\xrightarrow{\cong}\Pic(w\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))

of
\underline{\pi}_{0}
, which is a group isomorphism by
Theorem

4.9
. Consequently,
E_{(-)}
is multiplicative by virtue of being the inverse of a group isomorphism. The analogous construction for invertible Künneth Mackey functors yields an embedding
kE_{(-)}\colon\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\rightarrow\Pic(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))
which is inverse to
\underline{\pi}_{0}\colon\Pic(k\Ho(\mathscr{S}\textnormal{p}^{G}))\rightarrow\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
.
∎

5.
A Classification of Invertible Mackey Functors and
A(G)
-Modules

In the case that
G=C_{n}
, a cyclic group of order
n
, there is already a classification of invertible Mackey functors given in
[
1
, Proposition 4.6]
. It is shown that every invertible
C_{n}
-Mackey functor is isomorphic to some “twisted Burnside Mackey functor”. Here, we generalize this classification to all finite abelian groups with an approach heavily inspired by
[
1
]
. In particular, we build explicit representatives for the classes in
\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
and
\Pic(A(G))
. The
A(G)
-modules we construct will have underlying group given by
A(G)
but come equipped with a module structure that is not induced by the ring structure on
A(G)
. For this reason, we call such a module a “twisted Burnside module”. Similarly, we will refer to the Mackey functors constructed here as “twisted Burnside Mackey functors”.

5.1.
Twisted Burnside Mackey Functors

The objects constructed here are obtained by scaling the restriction maps in the Burnside Mackey functor, and we refer to the scalings as “twists”. We start with the object that parameterizes these so-called “twists”.

Definition 5.1
.

The
Burnside twists of
G
, denoted
T_{G}
, is defined by
T_{G}=\prod_{H\leq G}\mathbb{Z}
.
We will denote elements of
T_{G}
with lowercase Latin letters, such as
a
,
b
,
c
, and so on. For any such
a\in T_{G}
and subgroup
H
of
G
, we write
a_{H}
for the image of

under the projection from
T_{G}
onto its
H
-factor.

Here,
T_{G}
is meant to be the product of commutative monoids under multiplication and as such inherits the structure of a commutative monoid via pointwise multiplication.

Definition 5.2
.

Given a Burnside twist
a\in T_{G}
and a subgroup
H
of
G
, the
H
-restriction factor
of
a
, denoted
r_{H}^{a}
, is the integer

r_{H}^{a}=\prod_{K\geq H}a_{K}.

Note that
r_{G}=a_{G}
and that
r_{H}
divides
r_{J}
for any subgroup

of
H
. When no confusion will arise, we write

as

.

Definition 5.3
.

Given a Burnside twist

, the
a
-twisted Burnside Mackey functor

\underline{A}^{a}
is level-wise equal to
\underline{A}
(i.e.,
\underline{A}^{a}({}^{G}\!/_{\!H})=A(H)
for every subgroup

of

\underline{A}
. However, the restrictions in
\underline{A}^{a}
are scaled from those in
\underline{A}
using the restriction factors of

as follows: for any generator
{}^{H}\!/_{\!J}
in

\res^{a}_{H,K}({}^{H}\!/_{\!J})=\frac{r_{J\cap K}}{r_{J}}\cdot\res^{H}_{K}({}^{H}\!/_{\!J})=\frac{r_{J\cap K}}{r_{J}}\left|{}^{H}\!/_{\!JK}\right|\cdot{}^{K}\!/_{\!J\cap K}.

We use

to denote the restriction from
H
to

in
\underline{A}^{a}

\underline{A}^{a}=\underline{A}
. In this case, we use
\res_{H}^{K}
as above.

Remark 5.4

In the case that

satisfies

for all proper subgroups

of

\underline{A}^{a}
is

to

Proposition 5.5
.

The
a
-twisted Burnside Mackey functor
\underline{A}^{a}
is indeed a Mackey functor for any twist
a\in T_{G}
.

Proof.

First, note that transfers compose appropriately as they are the same as in
\underline{A}
. To see that the restrictions compose appropriately, let
J\leq K\leq H\leq G
and
L\leq H
. Then,

\displaystyle\res^{a}_{K,J}\left(\res^{a}_{H,K}({}^{H}\!/_{\!L})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\res^{a}_{K,J}\left(\frac{r_{K\cap L}}{r_{L}}\cdot|{}^{H}\!/_{\!KL}|\cdot{}^{K}\!/_{\!K\cap L}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{K\cap L}}{r_{L}}\cdot|{}^{H}\!/_{\!KL}|\cdot\res^{a}_{K,J}\left({}^{K}\!/_{\!K\cap L}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{K\cap L}}{r_{L}}\cdot|{}^{H}\!/_{\!KL}|\cdot\frac{r_{J\cap K\cap L}}{r_{K\cap L}}\cdot|{}^{K}\!/_{\!J(K\cap L)}|\cdot{}^{J}\!/_{\!J\cap K\cap L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{J\cap K\cap L}}{r_{L}}\cdot|{}^{H}\!/_{\!KL}|\cdot|{}^{K}\!/_{\!J(K\cap L)}|\cdot{}^{J}\!/_{\!J\cap K\cap L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{J\cap L}}{r_{L}}\cdot|H|\cdot\frac{|K\cap L|}{|K|\cdot|L|}\cdot|K|\cdot\frac{|J\cap K\cap L|}{|J|\cdot|K\cap L|}\cdot{}^{J}\!/_{\!J\cap L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{J\cap L}}{r_{L}}|H|\cdot\frac{|J\cap L|}{|J|\cdot|L|}\cdot{}^{J}\!/_{\!J\cap L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{J\cap L}}{r_{L}}\cdot|{}^{H}\!/_{\!JL}|\cdot{}^{J}\!/_{\!J\cap L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\res^{a}_{H,J}({}^{H}\!/_{\!L}).

The verification of the double coset formula is similarly straightforward. We conclude that
\underline{A}^{a}
is a Mackey functor.
∎

.

Before we proceed with the classification, we record a useful lemma concerning the image of
\underline{A}^{(-)}
in the kernel of geometric fixed points.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\overline{a}\in\prod_{J\ngeq N}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:J]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}
and
a\in T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}
obtained by extending
\overline{a}
by 1’s. For any
\underline{P}\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
, we have an isomorphism

\Hom(\underline{A}^{a},\underline{P})\cong\left\{\gamma\in\underline{P}({}^{G}\!/_{\!G})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{J\ngeq N}\underline{P}({}^{G}\!/_{\!J})^{W_{G}(J)}\mid\res_{J}^{K}\gamma_{K}=\frac{r_{J}^{a}}{r_{K}^{a}}\gamma_{J}\text{ for $J\leq K$}\right\}

Proof.

Combining
Lemma

5.7
with the observation that we have
\res_{H}^{G}\gamma_{G}=r_{H}^{\overline{a}}\gamma_{H}=\gamma_{H}
for
H\geq N
yields the isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 5.16
.

For
G
a finite abelian group, the map
\underline{A}^{(-)}\colon\prod_{J\leq G}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:J]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}\rightarrow\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
is injective.

Proof.

Let
a\in\prod_{H\leq G}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:H]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}
and suppose
\varphi\colon\underline{A}^{a}\to\underline{A}
is an isomorphism. If
G=e
, the theorem holds trivially. So, we suppose
|G|=n>1
and induct on the number of prime factors of
n
(counted with multiplicity). For the base case,
n=p
is prime, so
G=C_{p}
. The only possibly non-trivial component of the twist
a
is
a_{e}
in this case. However, invertibility of
\varphi
and commutativity with transfers implies that
\varphi_{C_{p}}({}^{C_{p}}\!/_{\!C_{p}})=n\cdot{}^{C_{p}}\!/_{\!e}\pm{}^{C_{p}}\!/_{\!C_{p}}
for some integer
n
. Commutativity with restrictions then implies
\pm a_{e}=np\pm 1
, so
a_{e}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{[C_{p}:e]}
. Thus,
\underline{A}^{(-)}
is injective in this case.

If
n
has more than one prime factor, let
N\leq G
be any non-trivial proper subgroup and define

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{J}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{J}&J\geq N\\
1&\text{ else}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\overline{a}_{J}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{J}&J\ngeq N\\
1&\text{ else}\end{cases}.

It follows that
\underline{A}^{a}
decomposes as
\underline{A}^{\hat{a}}\boxtimes\underline{A}^{\overline{a}}
. Further,
Proposition

5.12
shows that
\underline{A}^{\hat{a}}
is
\qInd_{Q}^{G}(\underline{A}^{\alpha})
, where
{\alpha}_{{}^{J}\!/_{\!N}}=a_{J}
for
J\geq N
. It is also straightforward to check that
\Phi^{N}\underline{A}^{\overline{a}}
is isomorphic to
\underline{A}
. Now,
Corollary

3.27
and injectivity of
\qInd_{Q}^{G}
imply that both
\underline{A}^{\alpha}
and
\underline{A}^{\overline{a}}
are trivial. Since
|N|
and
|Q|
are proper divisors of
|G|
, they have fewer prime factors, so our induction hypothesis yields

a_{N}={\alpha}_{{}^{N}\!/_{\!N}}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{[Q:e]}.

But
[Q:e]=[G:N]
, so we conclude that
\underline{A}^{(-)}
is injective.
∎

Corollary 5.17
.

For
G
a finite abelian group, the embedding

\underline{A}^{(-)}\colon T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}\rightarrow\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})

is an isomorphism. That is, every invertible Mackey functor is isomorphic to some twisted Burnside Mackey functor.

Proof.

The cardinality of
T_{G}^{\mathrm{inv}}
is finite and equal to that of
\Pic(A(G))\cong\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})
.
∎

5.3.
Twisted Burnside Modules

Using the twisted Burnside Mackey functors we have constructed, we can produce explicit representatives of the classes in
\Pic(A(G))
via an adjunction relating modules over the Burnside ring to Mackey functors.

Definition 5.18
.

Given an
A(G)
-module
M
, we can build a Mackey functor
\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}M
which is defined at level
{}^{G}\!/_{\!H}
to be
A(H)\otimes_{A(G)}M
. The restrictions and transfers in
\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}M
are given by tensoring the identity on
M
with the restrictions and transfers in
\underline{A}
, respectively.

This tensoring construction has a right adjoint which is given by evaluating Mackey functors at
{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}
.

Definition 5.19
.

There is an evaluation functor
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
given on objects by
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}(\underline{M})\coloneqq\underline{M}({}^{G}\!/_{\!G})
. We view
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\underline{M}
as an
A(G)
-module via

{}^{G}\!/_{\!H}\cdot m=\tr_{H}^{G}\res_{H}^{G}m

for any
m\in\underline{M}({}^{G}\!/_{\!G})
.

Proposition 5.21
.

The functor
\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}(-)\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
is strong symmetric monoidal.

Proof.

We proceed by the Yoneda Lemma. Let
M,N\in{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
and
\underline{P}
be an arbitrary Mackey functor. We have

\displaystyle\Hom(\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}(M\otimes_{A(G)}N),\underline{P})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Hom(M\otimes_{A(G)}N,\underline{P}({}^{G}\!/_{\!G}))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Hom(M,\Hom(N,\underline{P}({}^{G}\!/_{\!G})))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Hom(M,\underline{\Hom}(\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}N,\underline{P})({}^{G}\!/_{\!G}))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Hom(\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}M,\underline{\Hom}(\underline{A}\otimes N,\underline{P}))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Hom((\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}M)\boxtimes(\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}N),\underline{P}).

∎

Definition 5.22
.

For
a\in T_{G}
, the
a
-twisted Burnside module
, denoted
A(G)^{a}
, is the (left)
A(G)
-module given by
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\underline{A}^{a}
. Unpacking the module structure gives an explicit formula for the action of
A(G)
on
A(G)^{a}
:

{}^{G}\!/_{\!H}\cdot{}^{G}\!/_{\!J}=\tr_{H}^{G}\res^{a}_{G,H}{}^{G}\!/_{\!J}=\frac{r_{J\cap K}}{r_{H}}\cdot[G:JK]\cdot{}^{J}\!/_{\!J\cap K}.

Warning
.

The functor
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
is not a strong symmetric monoidal functor. This fails already when
G=C_{2}
: let
F\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma}
denote the fixed points Mackey functor associated to the
C_{2}
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma}
. Then,
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}F\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma}=0
and one can check via Lewis’ description of the box product that
F\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma}\boxtimes F\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma}
is isomorphic to
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
, the dual constant Mackey functor at
\mathbb{Z}
. Notably, the evaluation of
\mathbb{Z}^{*}
at
{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}
is non-zero.

The failure of
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}
to be strong symmetric monoidal means that we cannot deduce that
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}
descends to a map on Picard groups. In particular, we cannot conclude immediately that twisted Burnside modules are invertible. It turns out that restricting our attention to Künneth Mackey functors greatly remedies this problem, and the first step in this restricted analysis is an instantiation of another result of
[
4
]
.

Proposition 5.23
.

The functor
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
restricts to a strong symmetric monoidal functor

\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}.

Proof.

In
[
4
, Proposition 1.2(iii)]
, consider Künneth objects in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
and note that
\pi_{0}
translates to
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}
.
∎

Similarly, it is easy to verify the following:

Proposition 5.24
.

The functor
\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}(-)
preserves Künneth objects, hence it restricts to a strong symmetric monoidal functor

k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}\rightarrow k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}.

Proof.

Since
\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}(-)
is an additive, strong symmetric monoidal functor, we have

\underline{A}\otimes_{A(G)}\left(\bigoplus_{i}A(G)\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i}\underline{A}.

Further,
\underline{A}\otimes(-)
preserves retractions.
∎

Proposition 5.25
.

The functor
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}\rightarrow k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}
is a symmetric monoidal equivalence of categories. Consequently, it gives an isomorphism of Picard groups

\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}\colon\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\rightarrow\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})\cong\Pic(A(G)).

Proof.

To see that
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}
is fully faithful, we observe that Künneth objects in
{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G}
are finitely generated as Mackey functors at
\underline{M}({}^{G}\!/_{\!G})
. This implies that maps out of a Künneth Mackey functor are uniquely determined by their evaluation at
{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}
. On the other hand,
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}
is essentially surjective by virtue of having a right inverse as in
Proposition

5.20
.
∎

As twisted
A(G)
-modules are defined precisely as the
({}^{G}\!/_{\!G})
-evaluation of twisted Burnside Mackey functors,
Corollary

5.17
furnishes a classification of invertible
A(G)
-modules through the previous results.

Theorem 5.26
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. The map

\prod_{H\leq G}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:H]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}\xrightarrow{\underline{A}^{(-)}}\Pic(k{\mathscr{M}\textnormal{ack}}_{G})\xrightarrow{\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}}\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)})

is an isomorphism with composite given by

A(G)^{(-)}\colon\prod_{H\leq G}\left({}^{\mathbb{Z}}\!/_{\![G:H]}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}\rightarrow\Pic({\mathscr{M}\textnormal{od}}_{A(G)}).

That is, every invertible
A(G)
-module is isomorphic to a twisted Burnside module.

Proof.

\underline{A}^{(-)}
is an isomorphism by
Corollary

5.17
and
\ev_{{}^{G}\!/_{\!G}}
induces an isomorphism on Picard groups by
5.25
. The composite is as stated by definition.
∎

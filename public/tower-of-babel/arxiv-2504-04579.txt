From Continual Learning to SGD and Back: Better Rates for Continual Linear Models
Itay Evron, Ran Levinstein, Matan Schliserman, Uri Sherman, Tomer Koren, Daniel Soudry, Nathan Srebro

Abstract
We study the common continual learning setup where an overparameterized model is sequentially fitted to a set of jointly realizable tasks. We analyze forgetting, defined as the loss on previously seen tasks, after k k iterations. For continual linear models, we prove that fitting a task is equivalent to a single stochastic gradient descent (SGD) step on a modified objective. We develop novel last-iterate SGD upper bounds in the realizable least squares setup and leverage them to derive new results for continual learning. Focusing on random orderings over T T tasks, we establish universal forgetting rates, whereas existing rates depend on problem dimensionality or complexity and become prohibitive in highly overparameterized regimes. In continual regression with replacement, we improve the best existing rate from 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( d − r ¯ ) / k ) \mathcal{O}((d-\bar{r})/k) to 𝒪 ⁡ ( min ⁡ ( 1 / k 4 , d − r ¯ / k , T ​ r ¯ / k ) ) \mathcal{O}(\min(1/\sqrt[4]{k},\sqrt{d-\bar{r}}/k,\sqrt{T\bar{r}}/k)) ,where d d is the dimensionality and r ¯ \bar{r} the average task rank. Furthermore, we establish the first rate for random task orderings without replacement. The resulting rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( min ⁡ ( 1 / T 4 , ( d − r ¯ ) / T ) ) \mathcal{O}(\min(1/\sqrt[4]{T},\,(d-\bar{r})/T)) shows that randomization alone, without task repetition, prevents catastrophic forgetting in sufficiently long task sequences. Finally, we prove a matching 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k 4 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt[4]{k}) forgetting rate for continual linear classification on separable data. Our universal rates extend to broader methods, such as block Kaczmarz and POCS, illuminating their loss convergence under i.i.d. and single-pass orderings.

Introduction

In continual learning (CL), tasks are presented sequentially, one at a time.
The goal is for the learner to adapt to the current task—
e.g.,
by fine-tuning using gradient-based algorithms—while retaining knowledge from previous tasks.
A central challenge in this setting is termed

catastrophic forgetting
, where expertise from earlier tasks is lost when adapting to newer ones.
Forgetting is influenced by factors such as task similarity and overparameterization
(
Goldfarb et al., 2024
)
,
and is also related to trade-offs like the plasticity-stability dilemma
(
Mermillod et al., 2013
)
.
CL is becoming increasingly important with the rise of foundation models, where retraining is prohibitively expensive and data from prior tasks is often unavailable,
e.g.,
due to privacy or data retention constraints.

Previous work has shown, both analytically
(
Evron et al., 2022
;
Evron et al., 2023
;
Kong et al., 2023
;
Jung et al., 2025
;
Cai and Diakonikolas, 2025
, e.g.,)
and empirically
(
Lesort et al., 2023
;
Hemati et al., 2024
)
, that forgetting diminishes when tasks are ordered randomly or cyclically.
Task orderings can be explored from multiple perspectives:
as a strategy to mitigate forgetting (
e.g.,
by actively ordering an agent’s learning environments); as a naturally occurring phenomenon, such as periodic trends ine-commerce; or as a means to model popular CL benchmarks, such as randomly split datasets.

Our work focuses on a widely studied analytical setting---realizable continual linear regression,

Evron et al. (2022)

This raises a fundamental question, critical in highly overparameterized regimes:

Does worst-case forgetting necessarily scale with dimensionality, and if so, is the dependence indeed linear?

To this end, we bridge continual learning and last-iterate stochastic gradient descent (SGD) analysis.
We revisit an established connection between continual linear regression and the Kaczmarz method for solving systems of linear equations
(
Kaczmarz, 1937
;
Evron et al., 2022

.
Given rank-

Extending to general rank, we prove that learning an

Motivated by this, we prove convergence rates for the last iterate of fixed-step-size SGD that, crucially, hold for a broad range of step sizes not covered by prior work

Specifically, prior results either hold only for the average iterate

.
We overcome this challenge by carefully refining analysis techniques for SGD
(
Srebro et al., 2010b

Shamir and Zhang, 2013

to accommodate a wider range of step sizes, including the stepwise-optimal one.

Applying our last-iterate analysis to continual regression, we tighten the existing forgetting rate and establish the first dimension-independent rate

Furthermore, we provide the first rate for random task orderings

Our results also yield novel rates for the related Kaczmarz and NLMS methods.

Summary of Contributions.

To summarize, our main contributions in this paper are:

•

We establish new reductions from continual linear models to SGD with a rather large, “stepwise-optimal” step size, generalizing results from prior work—limited to rank-

•

We provide novel last-iterate SGD analysis for a realizable least squares setup, yielding the first informative rates for fixed step sizes large enough to support the reductions to continual learning.

•

Our main results are improved loss and forgetting rates in both continual linear regression and classification

Definition 4

(Forgetting of Training Data)
.

Let

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{\tau}(k)\right]>0

Remark 5

(Forgetting vs. Regret)
.

While regret and forgetting are related, they can differ significantly
(
Evron et al., 2022
)
.
Regret is a key quantity in online learning,
defined in our setting as

\frac{1}{2k}{\sum}_{t=1}^{k}\|{\mathbf{X}_{\tau(t)}\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{y}_{\tau(t)}}\|^{2}

That is, it measures the suboptimality of each iterate on the

In contrast, forgetting evaluates an iterate’s performance across

We further define the average training loss to easily connect with

Definition 6

(Training Loss)
.

The training loss of any vector
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{m=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{m=1}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}_{m}}\right\|^{2}\,.

We bound both the forgetting and the loss, leveraging a key property—expected (in-sample) forgetting can be upper bounded using expected training loss across all tasks.
Specifically,

\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[F_{\tau}(k)]\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{w}_{k-1}\right)\right]+\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{k}

(Recall that

Our proof, given in App.

(similar to our loss) by analyzing a linear map.
Unlike ours, the polynomial rates they derive involve matrix properties related to the condition number.

Proof Idea.

We rewrite the Kaczmarz update (
Scheme

2
) in a recursive form of the differences,
i.e.,
\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}={\mathbf{P}}_{\tau(t)}\left({\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right)
for a suitable projection matrix
{\mathbf{P}}_{\tau(t)}
.
We define the linear map
Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{m=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}
to capture the evolution of the difference’s second moments, enabling sharp analysis of the expected loss in terms of
Q

Using properties of
Q
, norm inequalities, and the spectral mapping theorem, we establish a fast
\mathcal{O}\left({1/k}\right)
rate with explicit dependence on
T
,
d
, and
\bar{r}
.

Remark 9

(The
\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}\!R^{2}
Scaling Term)
.

All the rates we derive contain a multiplicative factor of
\|\mathbf{w}_{\star}\|^{2}R^{2}
,
a generally unavoidable scaling term in linear regression.
Prior work on continual learning has either normalized it away implicitly—e.g., by
assuming

(

explicitly, as we do

The rate in

involves additional problem
parameters, i.e.,

\bar{r}

4.3

Discussion

Our work established reductions from continual linear regression and classification
to “stepwise-optimal” SGD.
This enabled the development of analytic tools for last-iterate SGD schemes, leading to significantly improved and even universal rates for random orderings in continual learning.
Our main results are summarized in
Tables

1
,
2
and
3
.

Much of the related work has been covered throughout the paper.
A further discussion of related work can be found in
App.

A
.
Here, we briefly highlight additional aspects of our work.

Random Continual Benchmarks.

Many popular continual benchmarks in deep learning implicitly assume a random ordering, such as the permuted MNIST benchmark
(
Kirkpatrick et al., 2017
)
.
Our paper shows that in sufficiently long task sequences,
random ordering is enough to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and the training loss goes to zero, even in the worst case.
In accordance with our results,

Lesort et al. (2023)
examined a random CL benchmark—in which a subset of
classes
is randomly sampled in each task—and observed that forgetting diminishes as more tasks are sampled, even while training with standard SGD (without any modifications to mitigate forgetting).
This suggests that random orderings may contaminate continual learning benchmarks, making it harder to isolate the algorithmic effects being tested.
Furthermore, real-world tasks often change gradually, not adhering to random orderings.
Such “gradually evolving” datasets might be more challenging and relevant as continual benchmarks.

Connections to the Kaczmarz Method.

In
Section

3.1
we revisited known connections between continual regression and the Kaczmarz method
(
Evron et al., 2022
)
.
We broadened this connection in
Section

3.2
, bridging the
block
Kaczmarz method and “stepwise-optimal” SGD, thus applying our novel SGD bounds to the Kaczmarz method.
Using Kaczmarz terminology, given a system
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
consisting of
T
blocks of an average rank
\bar{r}
where

\mathbf{A}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{{n_{m}}\times d},\mathbf{b}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
,
our rates from
Section

4
can be summarized as

\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\big[\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{m=1}^{T}\big\|{\mathbf{A}_{m}\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{b}_{m}}\big\|^{2}\big]=\mathcal{O}\big({\min\big({k^{-1/4},\frac{1}{k}\sqrt{d-\bar{r}},\frac{1}{k}\sqrt{T\bar{r}}}\big)}\big)

for random orderings with replacement and

\mathcal{O}\big({\min\big({k^{-1/4},\frac{1}{k}\left({d-\bar{r}}\right)}\big)}\big)

without replacement.
Note that we bounded the
loss
, rather than the “error”
\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}
,
thus enabling the derivation of rates independent of quantities like the condition number that can make convergence arbitrarily slow.

Non-uniform Sampling.

The seminal work of
Strohmer and Vershynin (2009)
proposed a Kaczmarz variant that samples rows with probability proportional to their squared norm.
Our approach also accommodates non-uniform sampling, including norm-based ones.
Specifically, Claim
G.1
tightens
Theorem

10
by employing norm-weighted
block
sampling, thereby replacing the dependence on the maximum row norm
R
with the average
\bar{R}
.

From Training to Generalization Results.

The rates derived in our paper apply to the forgetting and loss on the training sets (
Definitions

4
and
6
).
These rates can be extended to the generalization loss at the cost of an additive
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
term (where
N
is the number of samples per task) via uniform-convergence arguments—or even an additive
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
term using the more refined Rademacher bounds for linear models from
Srebro et al. (2010a)
.

Future and Follow-up Work.

We narrowed the gap between existing lower and upper worst-case bounds for random orderings in continual linear regression (see
Table

1
).
However, a considerable gap remains between

\Omega(1/k)
and
\mathcal{O}({1/k^{1/4}})
, largely stemming from our proof technique in
Lemma

14
.
Our argument follows the approach of
Shamir and Zhang (2013)
, originally developed to control the last SGD iterate in the convex, non-smooth regime.
When instantiated in our (smooth, realizable) setting, this approach introduces an exponential dependence on
\eta\beta
.
Because of this exponential sensitivity, employing coarse inequalities (
e.g.,
bounding a negative term by
0
) can be costly: even small constant-factor losses may effectively change the power of
T
appearing in the final bound.

Following our reductions (
Sections

3
and
6
), improved rates for “stepwise-optimal” SGD rates would immediately refine the bounds for continual linear regression and classification.
Indeed, a follow-up work by
Attia et al. (2025)
departs from
Shamir and Zhang (2013)
and builds on a different technique proposed by
Zamani and Glineur (2023)
.
Using a more refined analysis, they establish an improved rate for SGD and, combined with our reductions, obtain a tighter upper bound of
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{k})
for continual linear regression (and classification).
Finding the exact worst-case complexity between
\Omega(1/k)
and
\mathcal{O}({1/\sqrt{k}})
remains an open question.

Finally, we note that our analytical flow relies on
Reduction

1
, which in turn assumes that the continual
Scheme

1
learns each task
to convergence
.
Nonetheless, a concurrent follow-up by
Levinstein et al. (2025)
follows a similar overall analytical flow, despite studying more practical continual learning schemes that do not learn to convergence—namely, those using
\ell_{2}
regularization or finite step budgets.
Similarly to us, they reduce learning an entire task to a single gradient step and apply last-iterate SGD analysis to obtain convergence rates.

Most of the related work is already discussed in the main body of the paper.
Here, we elaborate on several interesting connections that remain open.

Last-iterate Guarantees for SGD.

For the general (non-realizable) smooth stochastic setup, the recent work of
Liu and Zhou (2024a)
was the first (and only, to our knowledge) to provide upper bounds on the convergence rate of the last SGD iterate. While their bounds are applicable in the realizable setting, they require non-constant step sizes to obtain non-trivial convergence, and are therefore not useful for our purposes (see
Table

3
).
Our analysis technique in
Section

5
borrows from the work of
Shamir and Zhang (2013, also mentioned in Table 3)
which, in fact, belongs to the comparatively-richer line of work on
the non-smooth setting
(
Shamir and Zhang, 2013
;
Jain et al., 2019
;
Zamani and Glineur, 2023
;
Liu and Zhou, 2024a
)
.
Notably, SGD in a stochastic non-realizable (either smooth or non-smooth) setup requires uniformly bounded noise assumptions, and generally cannot accommodate a constant step size independent of the optimization horizon.

With versus Without Replacement in Kaczmarz Methods.

Our results in
Section

4
establish universal bounds for random orderings, both with and without replacement.
Both the with- and without-replacement variants converge linearly towards the minimum-norm solution
\mathbf{w}_{\star}

(
Gower and Richtárik, 2015
;
Han and Xie, 2024
)
, but as we explained in
Section

7
, the rates can be arbitrarily slow.

Recht and Ré (2012a)
formulated a noncommutative analog of the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality that, if true, could have shown that without-replacement orderings lead to faster loss convergence than with-replacement orderings in Kaczmarz methods, and consequently in continual linear regression.
Years later,
Lai and Lim (2020)
proved that this inequality does not hold in general
(
De Sa, 2020
, see also)
.
Moreover, as in other areas, empirical studies found that row shuffling followed by cyclic orderings performs as well as i.i.d. orderings
(
Oswald and Zhou, 2015
)
.
This naturally connects to interesting observations and open questions regarding various forms of shuffled SGD
(
Bottou, 2009
;
Yun et al., 2021
)
.
Our rates are similar for both with- and without-replacement orderings (up to small constants), meaning they do not indicate a clear advantage for either. However, we believe they are far from tight, leaving interesting open questions in this direction.

Connections to Normalized Least Mean Squares.

The NLMS algorithm is a classical adaptive filtering method.
In its simplest version
(
Slock, 1993
)
, the method perfectly fits a single—usually noisy—random sample at a time, using the same update rule as the Kaczmarz method (and thus, as our continual
Scheme

1
in a rank-
1
case).
There also exists a more complex version of this method, which uses more samples per update
(
Sankaran and Beex, 2000
)
.
Both papers give strong
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
MSE rates in the noiseless setting (matching our realizable setting).
However, they assume a very limited data model, where the sampled vectors are either orthogonal or identical up-to-scaling.
Under such conditions,
Evron et al. (2022)
showed that there is no forgetting (of previously learned tasks), implying that the MSE decays as the number of tasks still unseen at time
k
.

Alternative Continual Schemes That Do Not Forget.

​​
Schemes such as Recursive Least Squares (RLS; see
Chong and Zak 2004, Chapter 12.2
) and its block variant BRMP
(
Zhuang et al., 2021
)
provide analytical alternatives to the block Kaczmarz method (
Scheme

2
).
These methods effectively avoid forgetting by maintaining an
\mathcal{O}(d^{2})
matrix.
See also Proposition 5.5 in
Evron et al. (2023)
.

Importantly, we study a continual scheme (
Scheme

1
) that closely characterizes common training practices.
In particular, training with (S)GD to convergence coincides with the analytical updates of the (block) Kaczmarz method (
Scheme

2
), making the latter illustrative of most gradient-based continual learning approaches.
Understanding continual gradient-based algorithms in linear models is especially relevant given the linear dynamics of deep neural networks in the NTK regime
(
Jacot et al., 2018
)
.
These regimes are typically highly overparameterized, yet they remain covered by our analysis and by the dimensionality-independent rates we derive for the naturally forgetful but
memoryless

Scheme

1
.

\forall m\in\left[{T}\right],\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Upper bound:

\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\leq R^{2}f_{m}(\mathbf{w})\triangleq\max_{m^{\prime}\in\left[{T}\right]}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m^{\prime}}}\right\|^{2}f_{m}
.

Gradient:

\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f_{m}(\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}\left({\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right)=\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{y}_{m}

1

\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbf{w})=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}_{m}}\right\|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star})}\right\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m}}\right\|^{2}}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star})}\right\|^{2}\leq R^{2}f(\mathbf{w})\,.

\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f_{m}(\mathbf{w})=\left(\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}\right)^{\top}{\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star})=\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star})=\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{m}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{+}\mathbf{y}_{m}\,.

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\bigg[\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{t=1}^{k}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{\tau(t)}\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{y}_{\tau(t)}}\right\|^{2}\bigg]\leq\frac{3\min\left({\sqrt{d-\bar{r}},\sqrt{T\bar{r}}}\right)}{2\left({k-2}\right)}\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\|^{2}R^{2}\,,

where
\bar{r}\triangleq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{m\in[T]}\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{X}_{m})
.
(Recall that
{R\triangleq\max_{m\in[T]}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{m}}\right\|}
.)

Here, we prove the main result, followed by auxiliary corollaries and lemmas in
Section

C.1
.

Proof Idea.

We rewrite the Kaczmarz update (
Scheme

2
) in a recursive form of the differences,
i.e.,
\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}={\mathbf{P}}_{\tau(t)}\left({\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right)
, with a suitable projection matrix
{\mathbf{P}}_{\tau(t)}
.
We define the linear map
Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{m=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}
to capture the evolution of the difference’s second moments, enabling sharp analysis of the expected loss in terms of
Q
.
Using properties of
Q
, norm inequalities, and the spectral mapping theorem, we establish a fast
\mathcal{O}\left({1/k}\right)
rate with explicit dependence on
T
,
d
, and
\bar{r}
.

We analyze the randomized block Kaczmarz algorithm for solving the linear system
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{y}
, where the matrix and vector are partitioned into blocks as follows:

\mathbf{X}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{X}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{X}_{T}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{y}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{y}_{T}\end{pmatrix}\,.

By defining
\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}
and exploiting the recursive form of
Eq.

3
from the proof of
Lemma

B.1
, we obtain
\mathbf{z}_{t}={\mathbf{P}}_{\tau(t)}\mathbf{z}_{t-1}
. Note that
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{0}_{d}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}=-\mathbf{w}_{\star}
.

Now, define the linear map
Q:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

as

\displaystyle Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{m\sim\text{Unif}\left({\left[{T}\right]}\right)}\left[{{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}}\right]=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{m=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}.

(5)

This map plays a central role in our analysis and has been studied in similar forms in prior work
(
Guo et al., 2022
)
.
Note that
{\mathbf{P}}_{m}
is an orthogonal projection,
i.e.,
symmetric and idempotent. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\!\left[{\mathbf{z}_{t+1}\mathbf{z}_{t+1}^{\top}}\right]

\displaystyle\!=\!\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{m,\tau}\!\left[{{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}^{\top}}\right]\!=\!\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{m,\tau}\!\left[{{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{m}}\right]\!=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{m}\left[{{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}}\right]{\mathbf{P}}_{m}}\right]\!=Q\left[{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}}\right]}\right]\!.

It follows that

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}}\right]=Q^{t}\left[{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\mathbf{z}_{0}\mathbf{z}_{0}^{\top}}\right]}\right]=Q^{t}\left[{\mathbf{z}_{0}\mathbf{z}_{0}^{\top}}\right]=Q^{t}\left[{\left({\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right)\left({\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right)^{\top}}\right]=Q^{t}\left[{\mathbf{w}_{\star}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}}\right]\,,

where
Q^{t}

denotes
t
applications of
Q
.
The map
Q
captures the evolution of the error’s second-moment under Kaczmarz updates, offering a tractable approach to analyzing the algorithm’s convergence.

The expected loss at step
t
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[{\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbf{w}_{t}}\right)}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{y}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{i}\left({\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right)}\right\|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{z}_{t}}\right\|^{2}}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\frac{1}{2T}\left\|{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{z}_{t}}\right\|^{2}}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\frac{1}{2T}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{z}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\operatorname{tr}\left({\frac{1}{2T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}}\right)}\right]=\operatorname{tr}\left({\frac{1}{2T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau}\left[{\mathbf{z}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}}\right]}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({\frac{1}{2T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}Q^{t}\left[{\mathbf{w}_{\star}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}}\right]}\right).

We are now ready to derive the final bound.
From
Lemma

C.7
, we have

\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right].

Additionally, by Corollary
C.5
,
Q^{k}\left[{\mathbf{w}_{\star}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}}\right]
is symmetric and positive semidefinite (PSD). We also note that
\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
is symmetric PSD.
The key insight from Lemma
C.7
, combined with the trace product inequality (Lemma
C.6
), is that it allows the expected loss to be expressed using a polynomial in
Q
. This reformulation simplifies the convergence analysis by reducing it to examining the spectral properties of
Q
.
Invoking the trace product inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[{\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbf{w}_{k}}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({\frac{1}{2T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}Q^{t}\left[{\mathbf{w}_{\star}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}}\right]}\right)\leq\frac{R^{2}}{2}\operatorname{tr}\left({\left({\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right)Q^{k}\left[{\mathbf{w}_{\star}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}}\right]}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lem:Q trace}}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{2}\operatorname{tr}\left({Q^{k}\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right]\mathbf{w}_{\star}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}}\right)=\frac{R^{2}}{2}\mathbf{w}_{\star}^{\top}Q^{k}\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right]\mathbf{w}_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2}\left\|{Q^{k}\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right]}\right\|_{2}=\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2}\left\|{\big({Q^{k}\left({I-Q}\right)}\big)\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2}\left\|{\big({Q^{k-1}\left({I-Q}\right)}\big)Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2}\left\|{\big({Q^{k-1}\left({I-Q}\right)}\big)Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{operator norm}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2}\left\|{Q^{k-1}\left({I-Q}\right)}\right\|\cdot\left\|{Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Lemmas}\\
\text{\ref{cor:polynomial_Q_spectrum},\ \ref{cor:dimensionality_bound}}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2e\left({k-1}\right)}\min\left({\sqrt{T\bar{r}},\sqrt{d-\bar{r}}}\right)\,.

To clarify, the operator norm of a linear map
H
is defined as
\left\|{H}\right\|=\sup_{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},\left\|{\mathbf{A}}\right\|_{F}=1}\left\|{H\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]}\right\|_{F}
. The reason for switching from the spectral norm to the Frobenius norm is to enable the use of the spectral mapping theorem to bound the operator norm of
Q^{k-1}\left({I-Q}\right)
, applicable only for inner-product-based norms.
We complete the proof by bounding the forgetting using the training loss (
Lemma

B.1
).
That is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[F_{\tau}(k)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\bigg[\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{t=1}^{k}\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{\tau(t)}\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{y}_{\tau(t)}}\right\|^{2}\bigg]\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[{\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbf{w}_{k-1}}\right)}\right]+\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}R^{2}}{2\left({k-2}\right)}\min\left({\sqrt{T\bar{r}},\sqrt{d-\bar{r}}}\right)\,.

\jmlrQED

C.1
Key Properties and Auxiliary Lemmas

Definition C.1

(Positive Map)
.

A positive map
H:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a linear
map that maps PSD matrices to PSD matrices. Formally, if
\mathbf{0}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, then
\mathbf{0}\preccurlyeq H\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]
.

Definition C.2

(Symmetric Map)
.

A symmetric map
H:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a linear
map that maps symmetric matrices to symmetric matrices. Formally, if
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, then
H\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]=H\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]^{\top}
.

Corollary C.3
.

Q
, defined in
Eq.

5
, is a positive map.

Let
\mathbf{0}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then, for all
i\in\left[{T}\right]
,
\mathbf{0}\preccurlyeq{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
. Meaning
Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]
is PSD as a convex combination of PSD matrices.

\jmlrQED

Corollary C.4
.

Q
is a symmetric map. Moreover, for all
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, it satisfies
Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]^{\top}=Q\left[{\mathbf{A}^{\top}}\right]
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then,

\displaystyle Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]^{\top}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left({{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}\right)^{\top}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}^{\top}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}=Q\left[{\mathbf{A}^{\top}}\right].

\jmlrQED

Corollary C.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, the iterated application of the map
Q
, denoted
Q^{n}
, is a positive symmetric map.

For
n=1
, given by
Corollaries

C.3
and
C.4
.
For
n>1
, this follows trivially by induction.

\jmlrQED

Lemma C.6

(Trace Product Inequality)
.

Let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric PSD matrices such that
\mathbf{A}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{B}
.
Then,
\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}}\right)\leq\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}}\right)
.

Since
\mathbf{0}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}^{\top}
, it has a square symmetric PSD root
\mathbf{C}^{1/2}
.
Given that
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
are symmetric and
\mathbf{A}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{B}
,
it follows that
\mathbf{C}^{1/2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}^{1/2}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{C}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}^{1/2}

(from
Horn and Johnson, 2012
, Theorem 7.7.2.a)
.
Applying the cyclic property of the trace and using the fact that for symmetric matrices ordered in the Löwner sense, their traces are also ordered
(
Horn and Johnson, 2012
, Corollary 7.7.4.d)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}^{1/2}\mathbf{C}^{1/2}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{C}^{1/2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}^{1/2}}\right)\leq\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{C}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}^{1/2}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}}\right).

\jmlrQED

Lemma C.7
.

Let
R=\max_{i\in\left[{T}\right]}{\left\|{\mathbf{X}_{i}}\right\|}
. Then,
\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]

We perform SVD on each
\mathbf{X}_{i}=\mathbf{U}_{i}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\top}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{i}=\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{V}_{i}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\top}

On the other hand:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]=\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left({\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}}\right)\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}\left({\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}+\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Im}\left({\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}}\right)\\
\subseteq\text{Im}\left({\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right)\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}-\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}-\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}+\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{+}\mathbf{X}_{i}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{V}_{i}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\top}.

Now consider the difference:

\displaystyle\left({\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right)-\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{V}_{i}\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\top}.

We know that
\frac{1}{R}\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}}\right)_{j,j}\in[0,1]
.
We analyze two cases for each diagonal entry:

•

If
\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}}\right)_{j,j}=0
, then
\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}}\right)_{j,j}=0
.

•

Otherwise,
\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}}\right)_{j,j}=1
, and
\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}}\right)_{j,j}\leq 1
, which gives
\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}}\right)_{j,j}\geq 0
.

Thus,

\mathbf{0}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{V}_{i}\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\top}\,.

Averaging over all
i
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbf{V}_{i}\left({{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{+}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{i}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\top}=\left({\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right)-\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{2}T}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}-Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]\,.

\jmlrQED

Lemma C.8
.

Let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Then,
\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}Q^{n}\left[{\mathbf{B}}\right]}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({Q^{n}\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]\mathbf{B}}\right)
.

From the definition of
Q
(
Eq.

5
),

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}Q^{n}\left[{\mathbf{B}}\right]}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}\frac{1}{T^{n}}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\mathbf{B}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{linearity}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}=1}^{T}\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\mathbf{B}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{cyclic property}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}=1}^{T}\operatorname{tr}\left({{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\mathbf{B}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{linearity}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({\left({\frac{1}{T^{n}}\sum_{j_{1}\ldots,j_{n}=1}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{1}}\cdots{\mathbf{P}}_{j_{n}}}\right)\mathbf{B}}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({Q^{n}\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]\mathbf{B}}\right)\,.

\jmlrQED

Proposition C.9
.

Q
is self adjoint.

Let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then,

\displaystyle\langle Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right],\mathbf{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]^{\top}\mathbf{B}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{B}^{\top}Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Lemma~\ref{lem:Q trace}}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({Q\left[{\mathbf{B}^{\top}}\right]\mathbf{A}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{cor:Q-is-symmetric}}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({Q\left[{\mathbf{B}}\right]^{\top}\mathbf{A}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{A}^{\top}Q\left[{\mathbf{B}}\right]}\right)=\langle\mathbf{A},Q\left[{\mathbf{B}}\right]\rangle\,.

\jmlrQED

Proposition C.10
.

The spectrum of
Q
is contained in the interval
[0,1]
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then, by definition,

\displaystyle\langle Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right],\mathbf{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left({Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right]^{\top}\mathbf{A}}\right)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\operatorname{tr}\left({{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{idempotence,}\\
\text{cyclic property}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\operatorname{tr}\left({{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\|{{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}\right\|_{F}^{2}\geq 0\,.

Since each
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
is an orthogonal projection, its spectral norm satisfies
\left\|{{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}\right\|_{2}=1
.
Applying the operator inequality
\left\|{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Y}}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|{\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{2}\left\|{\mathbf{Y}}\right\|_{F}
twice, we obtain

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\|{{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}\right\|_{2}^{4}\left\|{\mathbf{A}}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left\|{\mathbf{A}}\right\|_{F}^{2}\,.

Thus, for any
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,

0\leq\langle Q\left[{\mathbf{A}}\right],\mathbf{A}\rangle\leq\left\|{\mathbf{A}}\right\|_{F}^{2}\,.

From the Rayleigh quotient characterization of eigenvalues, this implies that every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
Q
satisfies
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
,
i.e.,

\sigma(Q)\subset[0,1]\,
.

\jmlrQED

Lemma C.11
.

\left\|{Q^{n}\left({I-Q}\right)}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{en},\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

By
Proposition

C.9
,
Q
is self adjoint. Thus, we can apply the spectral mapping theorem to the polynomial
x\mapsto x^{n}\left({1-x}\right)
. The eigenvalues of
Q^{n}\left({I-Q}\right)
are of the form
\lambda^{n}\left({1-\lambda}\right)
, where
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
Q
. From
Proposition

C.10
, we know that
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right]
.
Using an algebraic property of
\lambda^{n}\left({1-\lambda}\right)
for
\lambda\in\left[{0,1}\right]
, we conclude that
\lambda^{n}\left({1-\lambda}\right)\in\left[0,\frac{1}{en}\right]
.

Therefore,
\left\|{Q^{n}\left({I-Q}\right)}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{en}
.

\jmlrQED

Lemma C.12
.

\left\|{Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{F}\leq\min\left({\sqrt{T\bar{r}},\sqrt{d-\bar{r}}}\right)
.

We first bound
\left\|{Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{F}
using the operator norm bound on
Q
(
Proposition

C.10
):

\displaystyle\left\|{Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left\|{Q}\right\|}_{\leq 1}\cdot\left\|{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{F}=\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}\left({\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right)}=\sqrt{T\bar{r}}\,.

Next, we use a pseudo-inverse property—that
\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{I}
—and the positivity of
Q
to show,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq Q\left[{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]

\displaystyle Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq Q\left[{\mathbf{I}}\right]

\displaystyle\left\|{Q\left[{\mathbf{X}^{+}\mathbf{X}}\right]}\right\|_{F}

Appendix E
Proofs of Last-Iterate SGD Bounds (
Section

5
)

In this section we provide proofs and full technical details of our upper bounds for least squares SGD.
We begin by recording a few elementary well-known facts, which can be found in e.g.,
Bubeck (2015)
.
We provide proof for completeness.

Lemma E.1

(Fundamental regret inequality for gradient descent)
.

Let
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\eta>0
, and suppose
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}-\eta\mathbf{g}_{t}
for all
t
, where
\mathbf{g}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{g}_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are arbitrary vectors. Then for any
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds that:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{w}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}})\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|^{2}.

Observe,

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}-2\eta\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{w}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}})+\eta^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{g}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{w}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\eta}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{g}_{t}}\right\|^{2}.

Summing the above over
t=0,\ldots,T
and telescoping the sum leads to,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{w}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{g}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{g}_{t}}\right\|^{2},

which completes the proof.

\jmlrQED

Lemma E.2

(Descent lemma)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be
\beta
-smooth for
\beta>0
, and suppose
\min_{\mathbf{w}}f(\mathbf{w})\in\mathbb{R}
is attained.
Then, for any
\eta>0
,
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have for
\mathbf{w}^{+}=\mathbf{w}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{w})
:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}^{+})\leq f(\mathbf{w})-\eta\left(1-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}.

Furthermore, for any
\mathbf{w}_{\star}\in\argmin_{\mathbf{w}}f(\mathbf{w})
, it holds that:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}\leq 2\beta\left(f(\mathbf{w})-f(\mathbf{w}_{\star})\right).

Observe, by
\beta
-smoothness:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{w})+\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\cdot(\mathbf{w}^{+}-\mathbf{w})+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{w}^{+}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{w})-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\cdot\nabla f(\mathbf{w})+\frac{\beta}{2}\eta^{2}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{w})-\eta\left(1-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\|^{2},

which proves the first claim.
For the second claim, apply the above inequality with
\eta=1/\beta
, which gives

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta\left(f(\mathbf{w})-f(\mathbf{w}^{+})\right).

The second claim now follows by using the fact that

f(\mathbf{w}_{\star})\leq f(\mathbf{w}^{+})
.

\jmlrQED

Lemma E.3
.

\mathbf{A}
and vector
\mathbf{b}

\mathbf{w}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

f(\mathbf{w}_{\star})=0

\displaystyle 2f(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\nabla f(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star})\,,

and
\gamma>0

\displaystyle(2-\gamma)f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{1}{\gamma}f(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\leq\nabla f(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z})\,.

For any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}_{\star}=\mathbf{b}

\displaystyle\nabla f(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\big\langle\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}),\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}),\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}),\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=2f(\mathbf{w})-\big\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{b},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{b}\big\rangle.

Plugging in

and the first claim follows.
For the second claim, note that by Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\nabla f(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\geq 2f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{b}}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{b}}\right\|^{2}=(2-\gamma)f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{1}{\gamma}f(\mathbf{z})\,.

\jmlrQED

, the following holds:
For any
{i_{0},\ldots i_{t-1}\in\mathcal{I}^{t}},i\in\mathcal{I}
,

\Pr(i_{\tau_{1}}=i|i_{0},\ldots,i_{t-1})=\Pr(i_{\tau_{2}}=i|i_{0},\ldots,i_{t-1})
.
Then, for any initialization
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
with-replacement SGD (
Eq.

2
) with step-size
\eta<2/\beta
, holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(\mathbf{w}_{T},i_{T})\leq(eT)^{\eta\beta\left(1-\eta\beta/4\right)}\mathbb{E}\left[\tfrac{1}{T+1}{\sum}_{t=0}^{T}f(\mathbf{w}_{t};i_{t})\right],

where the expectation is taken with respect to
i_{0},\ldots,i_{T}
sampled from
\mathcal{P}
.

Denote
f_{t}(\mathbf{w})\!\triangleq\!f(\mathbf{w};i_{t}),\,\mathbf{g}_{t}\!\triangleq\!\nabla f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})
, and observe that
by
Lemma

E.1
,

\forall\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t\leq T
(w.p.
1
):

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{g}_{t},\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T-k}-\mathbf{z}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\left\|{\mathbf{g}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Descent \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lem:gd_descent}}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T-k}-\mathbf{z}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\eta\beta\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{\star})

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T-k}-\mathbf{z}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\eta\beta\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f_{t}(\mathbf{z})+f_{t}(\mathbf{z})-f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{\star})\,.

By
Lemma

E.3
, this implies for any
\gamma>0
:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-\eta\beta\right)f_{t}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\!\Big({(2-\gamma)f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\frac{1}{\gamma}f_{t}(\mathbf{z})}\Big)+\eta\beta\!\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{z})-f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lem:sqloss_gradineq}}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{g}_{t},\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle+\eta\beta\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{z})-f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{above}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T-k}-\mathbf{z}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\eta\beta\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{z})-\underbrace{f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{\star})}_{=0}

\displaystyle\implies(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)\!\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\!f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T-k}-\mathbf{z}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{z})\,.

Now, set
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{w}_{T-k}

and take expectations to obtain:

\displaystyle(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 0+\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{T-k})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\big({k+1}\big)\gamma(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{T-k})\,.

Defining
S_{k}\triangleq\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})
,
implies that

\displaystyle(k+1)S_{k}-kS_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\sum_{t=T-k+1}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})=f_{T-k}(\mathbf{w}_{T-k})\,,

and
by the assumption on the distribution
\mathcal{P}
it follows that

\mathbb{E}f_{T-k}(\mathbf{w}_{T-k})=\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{T-k})
for any
t\geq T-k
.

Thus, combined with our previous display,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}S_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\big({k+1}\big)\gamma(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{T-k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\big({k+1}\big)\gamma(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)}\sum_{t=T-k}^{T}\Big({(k+1)\mathbb{E}S_{k}-k\mathbb{E}S_{k-1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)}\Big({(k+1)\mathbb{E}S_{k}-k\mathbb{E}S_{k-1}}\Big)\,.

Rearranging,
denoting
\displaystyle c\triangleq\gamma(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)
,
and requiring
c\in(0,1)
,
we get

\displaystyle\frac{k}{c}\mathbb{E}S_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\frac{k+1}{c}-1}\right)\mathbb{E}S_{k}

\displaystyle\iff\mathbb{E}S_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k+1-c}{k}\mathbb{E}S_{k}

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{E}f_{T}(\mathbf{w}_{T})=\mathbb{E}S_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{T}\left({1+\frac{1-c}{k}}\right)\mathbb{E}S_{T}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}1+x\leq e^{x},\forall x\geq 0\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left({\sum_{k=1}^{T}\frac{1-c}{k}}\right)\mathbb{E}S_{T}

\displaystyle=\exp\left({\left({1-c}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{T}\frac{1}{k}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}S_{T}\leq\exp\big({\left({1-c}\right)\left({1+\log T}\right)}\big)\mathbb{E}S_{T}

\displaystyle=\left({eT}\right)^{1-c}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]\,.

(6)

Now, getting the “best” rate requires maximizing
c=\gamma(2-\gamma-\eta\beta)
.
To this end, we choose
\gamma=1-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}
,
which implies

c=\big({1-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}}\big)^{2}

(under the
\eta<\frac{2}{\beta}
condition, we now have both
\gamma>0
and
c\in(0,1)
as required above).
Then,

1-c=\eta\beta\big({1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}}\big)
, and we finally get the required

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{T}(\mathbf{w}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq\left({eT}\right)^{\eta\beta\big({1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}}\big)}\cdot\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\,.

\jmlrQED

E.2
Extending the SGD Bounds to Without Replacement Orderings

Here, we extend
Theorem

12
to a
without
-replacement setting.
Specifically, we consider gradient descent under a random
permutation
of the
T
tasks.
That is, for some initialization
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, step size
\eta>0
, and
\pi_{t}\sim\textrm{Unif}(\mathcal{I})
sampled without replacement,

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{t+1}\leftarrow\mathbf{w}_{t}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})\,,

(7)

where
f(\mathbf{w};i)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{b}_{i}}\right\|^{2}
as defined in
Setup

1
.
Our main result is given below.

Theorem E.4
.

Last-Iterate Bound for Realizable Regression Without Replacement

Consider the
\beta
-smooth, realizable
Setup

1
.
Define for all
T\!\geq\!2
,

\hat{f}_{0:T}(\mathbf{w})\!\triangleq\!\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}f(\mathbf{w};\pi_{t})
.
Then, without-replacement SGD (Eq. (
7
)) with step-size
\eta<{2}/{\beta}
, holds:

\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\hat{f}_{0:T}(\mathbf{w}_{T})\leq\frac{eD^{2}}{\eta(2-\eta\beta)T^{1-\eta\beta\left(1-\eta\beta/4\right)}}+\frac{4\beta^{2}\eta D^{2}}{T}\,,\quad\forall T=2,\dots,n-1\,,

where
D\triangleq\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|
.
In particular,
for
\eta=\frac{1}{\beta\log T}

yields

{\frac{14\beta D^{2}\log T}{T}}

and

\eta=\frac{1}{\beta}

yields

\frac{7\beta D^{2}}{\sqrt[4]{T}}
.

The proof, given next, is based on the algorithmic stability of SGD
(
Bousquet and Elisseeff, 2002
;
Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2010
;
Hardt et al., 2016
)
, and more specifically, on a variant of stability, suitable for without replacement sampling
(
Sherman et al., 2021
;
Koren et al., 2022
)
.

The proof of our theorem follows by a combination of
Lemmas

E.5
and
E.6
.
The first, stated below, establishes a bound on the expected “next sample” loss and follows immediately by combining
Lemmas

14
and
13

(notice that

\eta<\frac{2}{\beta}\Longrightarrow\exp\big({\eta\beta\big({1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}}\big)}\big)\mapsto\exp\big({z\big({1-\frac{z}{4}}\big)}\big)
for
z\in(0,2)
,
which is monotonic increasing and upper bounded by

e
).

Lemma E.5
.

For any step-size
\eta<2/\beta
and initialization
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, without-replacement SGD
Eq.

7
satisfies, for all
1\leq T\leq n-1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{T};\pi_{T})

\displaystyle\leq e^{\eta\beta\left(1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}\right)}T^{\eta\beta\left(1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}\right)}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})\right]\leq\frac{e\cdot\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta(2-\eta\beta)T^{1-\eta\beta\left(1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}\right)}}\,.

Next, we consider the “empirical loss” objective. Given any permutation
\pi\in\mathcal{I}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{I}
, define:

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{0:t}(\mathbf{w})\triangleq\frac{1}{t+1}\sum_{i=0}^{t}f(\mathbf{w};\pi_{i}).

In the without-replacement setup, our optimization objective is the expected empirical loss
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\hat{f}_{0:t}(\mathbf{w})
, which, when
t=n
, satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\hat{f}_{0:t}(\mathbf{w})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\bar{f}(\mathbf{w})
.
Our second lemma (given next) bounds the expected empirical loss w.r.t. the next sample loss. This is the crux of extending our with-replacement upper bound to the without-replacement setup.

Lemma E.6
.

For without-replacement SGD
Eq.

7
with step size
\eta\leq 2/\beta
, for all
1\leq T\leq n
, we have that the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\hat{f}_{0:T}(\mathbf{w}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{T};\pi_{T})+\frac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{T+1}.

The proof of
Lemma

E.6
builds on an algorithmic stability argument similar to that given in
Lei and Ying (2020)
, combined with the without-replacement stability framework proposed by
Sherman et al. (2021)
.
Before turning to the proof given in the next subsection, we quickly prove
Theorem

E.4
.

By
Lemmas

E.6
and
E.5
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\hat{f}_{0:T}(\mathbf{w}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{T};\pi_{T})+\tfrac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{T+1}\leq\tfrac{e\cdot\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{\eta(2-\eta\beta)}T^{\eta\beta\left(1-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}\right)-1}+\tfrac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{T+1}.

The result for
\eta=\frac{1}{\beta}
is straightforward.
To see the result for
\eta=\frac{1}{\beta\log T}
,
notice that in this case,

\displaystyle\tfrac{eD^{2}T^{\eta\beta\left(1-\eta\beta/4\right)-1}}{\eta(2-\eta\beta)}=\frac{e\beta D^{2}\log T}{T(2\!-\!\frac{1}{\log T})}T^{\frac{1}{\log T}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\log T}\right)}=\tfrac{\beta D^{2}\log T}{T}\frac{\exp\left(2-\frac{1}{4\log T}\right)}{2-\frac{1}{\log T}}\leq\frac{10\beta D^{2}\log T}{T}.

\jmlrQED

E.2.1
Proving
Lemma

E.6

Notation.

We first add a few definitions central to our analysis.
Given a permutation
\pi\in\mathcal{I}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{I}
, denote:

\displaystyle\pi(j\leftrightarrow k)

\displaystyle\triangleq\pi\text{ after swapping the }j\textsuperscript{th}\text{ and }k\textsuperscript{th}\text{ coordinates},

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{\pi}

\displaystyle\triangleq\text{The iterate of SGD on step }\tau\text{ when run on permutation }\pi.

Most commonly, we will use the following special case of the above:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)}

\displaystyle\triangleq\text{The iterate of SGD on step }\tau\text{ when run on }\pi(i\leftrightarrow t).

When clear from context, we omit
\pi
from the superscript and simply write
\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}
.
Concretely, these definitions imply
\mathbf{w}_{0}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}\triangleq\mathbf{w}_{0}
, and
\forall i,t,\tau\in\mathcal{I}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{\tau+1}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\eta\nabla f\Big({\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{\tau}}\Big)\,.

We have the following important relation, to be used later in the proof.

Lemma E.7
.

For all
i,t,\tau\in\mathcal{I},i\leq\tau\leq t
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau};\pi_{i})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{i})\,.

The proof follows from observing that the random variables
{f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau};\pi_{i})}
and
f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{i})
are distributed identically (the indices
\pi_{i},\pi_{t}
are exchangeable).
Formally, let
\Pi(\mathcal{I})\triangleq\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{I}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{I}\right\}
be the set of all permutations over
\mathcal{I}
, and observe

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{i})=\frac{1}{|\Pi(\mathcal{I})|}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{I})}f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{i})\,.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau},\pi_{i})=\frac{1}{|\Pi(\mathcal{I})|}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{I})}f(\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{\pi};\pi_{i})\,.

Hence, since there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\pi
and
\pi(\tau\leftrightarrow i)
, in particular,

\displaystyle\left\{\pi\mid\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{I})\right\}=\left\{\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)\mid\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{I})\right\},

the result follows.

\jmlrQED

Our next lemma, originally given in
Sherman et al. (2021, Lemma 2 therein)
, can be thought of as a without-replacement version of the well known stability
\iff
generalization argument of the with-replacement sampling case
(
Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2010
;
Hardt et al., 2016
)
.

Lemma E.8
.

The iterates of without-replacement SGD
Eq.

7
, satisfy for all
t
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi_{t})\right]

We have, by definition of
\hat{f}_{0:t-1}
and
Lemma

E.7
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{i})\right]=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi_{t})\right],

where the last equality is immediate since by definition,
\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{i}=\pi_{t}
.
The claim now follows by linearity of expectation.

\jmlrQED

We are now ready to prove our main lemma.
We note that the proof shares some features with that of the with-replacement case (
Lemma

F.2
).

We prove the theorem for every
t
.
Any
\beta
-smooth realizable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
holds that

\displaystyle\left|h(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})-h(\mathbf{w})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\nabla h(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w})\right|+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Young's ineq.}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\|{\nabla h(\mathbf{w})}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w}}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h(\mathbf{w})+\beta\left\|{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w}}\right\|^{2}\,.

(8)

Hence, by
Lemma

E.8
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi_{t})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Jensen}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left|{f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)};\pi_{t})}\right|

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Eq.~(\ref{eq:h-bound})}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})+\beta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{t},\pi_{t})+\frac{\beta}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{t}}\right\|^{2}.

(9)

Next, we bound
\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{t}}\right\|^{2}
.
For any
0\leq\tau\leq t-1
,
we denote
f_{\tau}\triangleq f(\cdot;\pi_{\tau})
, and
f_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}\triangleq f(\cdot;\pi(i\leftrightarrow t)_{\tau})
. Observe that for any
\tau
such that
\tau\neq i
, we have
f_{\tau}=f_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}
, thus, by the non-expansiveness of gradient steps in the convex and
\beta
-smooth regime when
\eta\leq 2/\beta
(see Lemma 3.6 in
Hardt et al., 2016
):

\displaystyle\tau\leq i

\displaystyle\implies\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{\tau}}\right\|=0,

\displaystyle i<\tau

\displaystyle\implies\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\tau+1}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{\tau+1}}\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{i+1}}\right\|^{2}.

Further,

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{i+1}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\eta\nabla f_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)})-\left({\mathbf{w}_{i}-\eta\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\right)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}=\mathbf{w}_{i}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta^{2}\left\|{\nabla f_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Jensen}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta^{2}\left\|{\nabla f_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)})}\right\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\left\|{\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\beta\eta^{2}f_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)})+4\beta\eta^{2}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\,,

and by
Lemma

E.7

\mathbb{E}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})=\mathbb{E}f_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)})
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{i+1}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\beta\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\,.

Now,

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i\leftrightarrow t)}-\mathbf{w}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(8\beta^{2}\eta^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\right],

which, when combined with
9
yields:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})+\left(8\beta^{2}\eta^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\right].

Finally, by the regret bound given in
Lemma

13
,

\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\leq\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{2\eta(2-\eta\beta)}
,
and therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})-\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})+\frac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{(2-\eta\beta)t}

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{E}\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})+\frac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{(2-\eta\beta)t}.

Finally, since
\hat{f}_{0:t}=\frac{t}{t+1}\hat{f}_{0:t-1}+\frac{1}{t+1}f_{t}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hat{f}_{0:t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})=\frac{t}{t+1}\mathbb{E}\hat{f}_{0:t-1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})+\frac{1}{t+1}\mathbb{E}f_{t}(\mathbf{w}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2t+1}{t+1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f(\mathbf{w}_{t};\pi_{t})+\frac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{(2-\eta\beta)(t+1)},

which completes the proof.

\jmlrQED

arbitrary (nonempty) closed convex sets
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{T}
,
initial point
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and ordering
\tau
.
Define

.
Then,

(i)

f_{m}
is convex and
1
-smooth.

(ii)

The POCS update is equivalent to an SGD step:

\mathbf{w}_{t}=\bm{\Pi}_{\tau(t)}(\mathbf{w}_{t-1})=\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f_{\tau(t)}(\mathbf{w}_{t-1})
.

First, by Theorem 1.5.5 in

is continuously differentiable and for every

Plugging in

into an appropriate SGD step, we get

\mathbf{w}_{t}=\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f_{\tau(t)}(\mathbf{w}_{t-1})=\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\left(\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\bm{\Pi}_{\tau(t)}(\mathbf{w}_{t-1})\right)=\bm{\Pi}_{\tau(t)}(\mathbf{w}_{t-1})\,,

and the second part of the lemma follows.
In addition,
\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we prove convexity by using a projection inequality (also from Theorem 1.5.5 in
Facchinei and Pang, 2003
). That is,

\displaystyle f_{m}(\mathbf{x})-f_{m}(\mathbf{w})-\langle\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}-\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}-\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 35.00005pt+\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w}),\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\rangle+\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}-\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\rangle+0+\|\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{w}+\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}\geq 0\,.

For the
1
-smoothness,

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{w})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathbf{x}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{x})-\left({\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}(\mathbf{w})}\right)}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}_{m})(\mathbf{x})-(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}_{m})(\mathbf{w})}\right\|\leq\left\|{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}}\right\|\,,

where we used the non-expansiveness of
\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}_{m}

(
Bauschke et al., 2011
, Propositions 4.2, 4.8 in)
.

Lemma F.1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a nonempty closed and convex set, and

f(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})}\right\|^{2}
.
Then, we have for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma>0

\displaystyle(2-\gamma)f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{1}{\gamma}f(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\leq\nabla f(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z})\,.

In addition, for any
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{K}
we have

\displaystyle 2f(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle\leq\nabla f(\mathbf{w})^{\top}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{u})\,.

We already established that
\nabla f(\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})
.
Combining this with simple algebra, we obtain,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{z})\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{z}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{z})\right\rangle\,.

By Theorem 1.5.5 (b) in
Facchinei and Pang (2003)
,
we have that

\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{z})\right\rangle\geq 0\,,

and finally we get,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 2f(\mathbf{w})-\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{z}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{z})\right\rangle\,.

Plugging in
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{u}
, the second term vanishes
(since
\mathbf{u}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{0}
)
and the second claim follows.

For the first claim, note that by Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2f(\mathbf{w})-\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{z}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{z})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 2f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{w}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{w})}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|{\mathbf{z}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{z})}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2f(\mathbf{w})-\gamma f(\mathbf{w})-\frac{1}{\gamma}f(\mathbf{z})\,.

\jmlrQED

Lemma F.2
.

Consider with-replacement SGD
Eq.

2
with step size
\eta\leq 2/\beta
, and define, for every
0\leq T
,
\hat{f}_{0:T}(\mathbf{w})\triangleq\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}f(\mathbf{w};i_{t})
.
For all
1\leq T
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hat{f}_{0:T-1}(\mathbf{w}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\bar{f}(\mathbf{w}_{T})+\frac{4\beta^{2}\eta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{\star}}\right\|^{2}}{T}\,.

Our proof here mostly follows the proof of
Lemma

E.6
.
Recall that from Eq. (
8
), any
\beta
-smooth realizable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
holds that

\left|h(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})-h(\mathbf{w})\right|\leq h(\mathbf{w})+\beta\left\|{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w}}\right\|^{2}
.
Denote
f_{t}\triangleq f(\cdot;i_{t})
for all
t\in\left\{0,.\hskip 0.8pt\!.\hskip 0.8pt\!.,T\right\}
.
Now, by the standard stability
\iff
generalization argument
(
Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2010
;
Hardt et al., 2016
)
, and denoting by
\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i)}
the SGD iterate after
\tau
steps on the training set where the
i
th
example was resampled as
j_{i}
:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{f}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-\hat{f}_{0:T-1}(\mathbf{w}_{T})\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\Big|{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{j_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{T};j_{i})-f(\mathbf{w}_{T}^{(i)};j_{i})\right]}\Big|

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Jensen; Eq.~(\ref{eq:h-bound})}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{T};j_{i})+\beta\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{T}}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bar{f}(\mathbf{w}_{T})+\frac{\beta}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{T}}\right\|^{2}.

Next, we bound
\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{T}}\right\|^{2}
. By the non-expansiveness of gradient steps in the convex and
\beta
-smooth regime when
\eta\leq 2/\beta

(
Hardt et al., 2016
, see Lemma 3.6 in)
:

\displaystyle\tau\leq i

\displaystyle\implies\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{\tau}}\right\|=0,

\displaystyle i<\tau

\displaystyle\implies\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{\tau+1}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{\tau+1}}\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{i+1}}\right\|^{2}.

Further,

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{i+1}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)}-\eta\nabla f_{j_{i}}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)})-\left({\mathbf{w}_{i}-\eta\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\right)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)}=\mathbf{w}_{i}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta^{2}\left\|{\nabla f_{j_{i}}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Jensen}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta^{2}\big\|{\nabla f_{j_{i}}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)})}\big\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\big\|{\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\text{smoothness,}\\
\text{non-negativity}\end{subarray}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\beta\eta^{2}f_{j_{i}}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)})+4\beta\eta^{2}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\,.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{T}}\right\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{i+1}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\beta\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}f_{j_{i}}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(i)})+4\beta\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})=8\beta\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\,.

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{f}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-\hat{f}_{0:T-1}(\mathbf{w}_{T})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bar{f}(\mathbf{w}_{T})+\frac{\beta}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{w}_{T}^{(i)}-\mathbf{w}_{T}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bar{f}(\mathbf{w}_{T})+{8\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}f_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\right].

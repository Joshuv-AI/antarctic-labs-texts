Illuminating sequential freeze-in dark matter with dark photon signal at the CERN SHiP experiment
Xinyue Yin, Sibo Zheng

Abstract
Single-field freeze-in dark matter barely leaves observable footprints in dark matter direct detection, collider or fixed-target experiments, which can be altered in the two-field context. In this work, we consider sequential freeze-in dark matter through signals of dark photon mediator with a mass range of m A ′ ∼ 10 − 2 − 10 m_{A^{\prime}}\sim 10^{-2}-10 GeV covered by the proposed SHiP experiment. We show that the dark charge is fixed to be e ′ ∼ 1.3 × 10 − 12 e^{\prime}\sim 1.3\times 10^{-12} and the mixing parameter is restricted to 10 − 11 ≤ ϵ < 10 − 8 − 10 − 7.5 10^{-11}\leq\epsilon<10^{-8}-10^{-7.5} , as a result of the out-of-equilibrium condition of dark photon and the observed relic abundance of dark matter. Within this ϵ \epsilon region, the 5(15)-year data of proton bremsstrahlung process for the dark photon, assuming vector meson (dipole) dominance, excludes ϵ ≥ 10 − 8.5 ​ ( 10 − 7.9 ) \epsilon\geq 10^{-8.5}(10^{-7.9}) at 90% confidence level, implying only a narrow region of ϵ \epsilon close to ∼ 10 − 11 \sim 10^{-11} left for alternative tests.



I
Introduction

Dark matter (DM) is a key ingredient of standard cosmology.
Its particle nature is still unknown.
If DM couples to the Standard Model (SM) sector with a coupling strength being near unity,
it is able to keep thermal equilibrium with the SM thermal bath,
and obtains the observed relic abundance
Planck:2018vyg
via so-called freeze-out mechanism.
Different from the freeze-out mechanism,
freeze-in mechanism
Hall:2009bx
or a mixture of them
Chu:2011be
,
specialized for the DM coupling strength being tiny,
has gained a renewed interest recently due to null experimental results on the freeze-out DM scenarios.

Well-known single-field examples of freeze-in DM include millicharged particle
Holdom:1985ag
;
Feldman:2007wj
,
sterile neutrino
Asaka:2005cn
;
Becker:2018rve
;
Datta:2021elq
, scalar
McDonald:2001vt
,
and axion-like particle
Xu:2024cof
.
Such freeze-in DM barely leaves observable footprints in collider or DM detection experiments,
except that only few of them are in the reaches of cosmic ray experiments such as Ly
\alpha
or 21-cm signals within limited parameter regions
Xu:2024uas
.

Beyond the single-field situation, two-field freeze-in DM model can be in the scopes of collider, fixed target/beam dump or DM detection experiments,
as illustrated by earlier studies
Hessler:2016kwm
;
Ghosh:2017vhe
;
Calibbi:2018fqf
;
Belanger:2018sti
;
Yin:2024sle
on a fermion or scalar mediator communicating between the DM and SM sector.
The main reason is that coupling strength of the mediator to the SM sector is not necessarily as small as the DM coupling to the mediator.

Inspired by the aforementioned studies,
we consider freeze-in DM through a massive dark photon mediator;
see e.g.,
Fabbrichesi:2020wbt
for a recent review.
Notably, this two-field freeze-in DM model differs from a millicharged DM via massless dark photon
Kovetz:2018zan
;
Liu:2019knx
and
a massive dark photon itself being the DM
Nelson:2011sf
;
Arias:2012az
;
Graham:2015rva
;
Nakai:2020cfw
.
We will investigate how to probe this freeze-in DM via the dark photon signals at the CERN SHiP
Alekhin:2015byh
;
SHiP:2015vad
;
SHiP:2025ows
, a proposed proton dump experiment which provides access to new freeze-in DM parameter regions not yet explored.

II.2
Sequential freeze-in

Now we discuss the DM freeze-in production to address the observed DM relic abundance
\Omega_{\chi}h^{2}=0.12\pm 0.001

Planck:2018vyg
.
If
\epsilon>\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}\sim 10^{-8}

Hambye:2019dwd
,
where
\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}
is the threshold value required by thermal equilibrium,
the decay of thermalized dark photon into a DM pair is the main DM production process,
leading to
e^{\prime}\sim 10^{-11}
. In contrast, if
\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}
,
the heavy dark photons are generated from the inverse decay
\psi\bar{\psi}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
where
\psi
denotes electrically charged SM particles,
and subsequently decay to DM particles with
e^{\prime}\leq\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}
,
which is called as sequential freeze-in DM

The Boltzmann equations describing the sequential freeze-in process are given by

\displaystyle\dot{n}_{A^{\prime}}+3Hn_{A^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi}\int d\Pi_{A^{\prime}}d\Pi_{\psi}d\Pi_{\bar{\psi}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{A^{\prime}}-p_{\psi}-p_{\bar{\psi}})

(3)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\mid M\mid^{2}_{A^{\prime}\rightarrow\psi\bar{\psi}}\left(f^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\psi}f^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\bar{\psi}}-f_{A^{\prime}}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{n}_{\chi}+3Hn_{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Pi_{A^{\prime}}d\Pi_{\chi}d\Pi_{\bar{\chi}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{A^{\prime}}-p_{\chi}-p_{\bar{\chi}})

(4)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\mid M\mid^{2}_{A^{\prime}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}f_{A^{\prime}},

Dark photon signal at the SHiP Experiment

Production

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\omega_{\chi A^{\prime}}(z_{2},p_{2\perp}^{2})dz_{2}dp_{2\perp}^{2}d\sigma_{pp\to\chi\bar{\chi}},

III.2
Decay

The heavy dark photon decays into lepton pair

For the lepton decay channels, the partial decay width is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(A^{\prime}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\alpha\epsilon^{2}m_{A^{\prime}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\ell}^{2}}{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}}\left(1+\frac{2m_{\ell}^{2}}{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}\right),

Regarding the hadron decay channels, the partial decay width reads as

\displaystyle\Gamma(A^{\prime}\to\mathrm{hadrons})=\Gamma(A^{\prime}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})R,

(7)

where

\displaystyle R=\frac{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathrm{hadrons})}{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})},

(8)

For the DM decay channels, the partial decay width is

\displaystyle\Gamma(A^{\prime}\to\chi\bar{\chi})=\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{D}m_{A^{\prime}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}}\left(1+\frac{2m_{\chi}^{2}}{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}\right),

IV
Discussion

Massive dark photon mediated DM,
being active within the region of
\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}
,
has been frequently mentioned in the literature.
However, the precise information on such DM is missing.
This study has filled the gap by pointing out the concrete parameter space of sequential freeze-in DM which
corresponds to
e^{\prime}\sim 1.3\times 10^{-12}
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{DM}}\leq\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}
,
with
\epsilon_{DM}\sim 10^{-11}
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}\sim 10^{-8}-10^{-7.5}
for the dark photon mass
m_{A^{\prime}}\sim 10^{-2}-10
GeV, as seen in Fig.
1
.

Besides, we have shown that a large portion of the
\epsilon
region allowed is in the reach of the proposed CERN SHiP experiment.
Concretely speaking, the 5(15)-year observations of the Pbrem production process for the dark photon,
assuming VMD (dipole dominance),
exclude
\epsilon\geq 10^{-8.5}(10^{-7.9})
at 90% CL, as shown by the red (blue) plot in Fig.
3
.
Only a narrow region of
\epsilon
close to
\sim 10^{-11}
is left for alternative tests.

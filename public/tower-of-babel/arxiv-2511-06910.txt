Profile of a Touch-Down solution to a nonlocal MEMS model with critical parameters
Maissâ Boughrara

Abstract
This work investigates a mathematical model arising in the study of MEMS devices, described by the following parabolic equation on [ 0 , T ) × Ω [0,T)\times\Omega : ∂ t v = Δ ​ v + λ ( 1 − v ) 2 ​ ( 1 + γ ​ ∫ Ω 1 1 − v ​ 𝑑 x ) 2 , 0 ≤ v ≤ 1 , \partial_{t}v=\Delta v+\frac{\lambda}{(1-v)^{2}\left(1+\gamma\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-v}\,dx\right)^{2}},\qquad 0\leq v\leq 1, where Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} is a bounded domain and λ , γ > 0 \lambda,\gamma>0 . We construct a solution with a prescribed profile, which quenches in finite time T T at exactly one interior point a ∈ Ω a\in\Omega . Moreover, we are able to provide an asymptotic description of the quenching profile. We reformulate the problem as a blow-up problem to utilize the techniques employed in [ 15 ] , [ 5 ] and [ 3 ] . The proof proceeds through two principal steps: a reduction to a finite-dimensional dynamical system and a classical topological argument employing index theory. The main challenge lies in managing the nonlocal integral term, which generates an additional gradient term when the problem is transformed into the blow-up framework.



Keywords:
MEMS, touch-down phenomenon, quenching solution, asymptotic profile, nonlocal partial differential equations.

Introduction

Micro-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS) are electronic devices that enable the translation of a mechanical signal, such as a sound wave, into an electrical signal (see the book by Kavallaris and Suzuki
[
12
]
). This system is usually very small, between 1 and 100 micrometers. MEMS devices are essential in modern technology such as telecommunications, biomedical engineering, space exploration, etc.

Roughly speaking, a MEMS device consists in a rigid plate and a flexible membrane, both connected to a current source and a capacitor.

One model that describes the behavior of such device is a hyperbolic PDE (see Guo and Souplet
[
9
]
as well as Kavallaris and Suzuki
[
12
]
) given by the following:

\begin{cases}&\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{tt}v+\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+\displaystyle\frac{f(x,t)}{(1-v)^{2}\left(1+\gamma\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-v}dx\right)^{2}},\ x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
&v(x,t)=0,x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
&v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\varepsilon
is the ratio of the interaction due to the inertial and damping terms,
v\in[0,1]
is the deflection of the membrane,
f(x,t)>
0 is the varying dielectric permittivity of the elastic plate, and
\Omega
is a regular domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and is the shape of the elastic plate. Note that in this model, the rigid plate is located at position
v\equiv 0
. In fact, assuming the dumping term is high, we may take
\varepsilon=0
, and reduce to standing for the following parabolic equation:

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{(1-v)^{2}\left(1+\gamma\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-v}dx\right)^{2}},\ x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
&v(x,t)=0,x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
&v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),x\in\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Note that we take here
f\equiv\lambda>0
. Up to replacing
v(x,t)
by
v(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}x,\lambda t)
, we may assume that
\lambda=1
. In fact, we will consider the following generalization of the problem (
1.2
).

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-v)^{\bar{p}}\left(1+\gamma\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(1-v)^{r}}dx\right)^{q}},\ x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
&v(x,t)=0,x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
&v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\bar{p},q,r>0
and
\Omega
is a regular domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

This problem arises in the study of the dynamic deflection of the elastic membrane. During normal operation of the device, the membrane does not touch the rigid plate, i.e.
v(x,t)<1
for all
x\in\Omega,t\geq 0
. If ever
v(x_{0},t_{0})=1
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega

, this means that the membrane touches the rigid plate and the MEMS device is damaged for good. Such phenomenon is called ”Touch-down”. As mathematicians, we would like to understand this phenomenon to avoid it and to get more reliable devices. From a mathematical point of view, Touch-down happens if there exists
T<+\infty
and

\underset{t\rightarrow T}{\lim\inf}[\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}1-v(x,t)]=0.

(1.4)

Moreover, we say that
a\in\Omega
is a Touch-down point if and only if there exist a sequence
(a_{n},t_{n})\in\Omega\times(0,T)
such that

(a_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow(a,T)\text{ and }v(a_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow 1,\text{ as }n\rightarrow+\infty.

This Touch-down phenomenon can be interpreted as a form of finite-time quenching, where a physical quantity reaches zero in finite time. In this case, that quantity is the gap between the membrane and the rigid plate, given by
1-v
.

In this work, we construct a solution which quenches in finite time called
T
only and only at one point

and give its quenching profile. Many have studies the quenching problem for this model. We may cite

1-v(x,t)\sim(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(p-1+\frac{(p+1)^{2}}{4p}\frac{|x|^{2}}{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\text{ as }t\rightarrow T.

(1.5)

However, the existence of a quenching solution specifically in the critical regime has not yet been established and remains an open question.

1.2
Setting of the problem and strategy

We recall our goal in this work. We want to construct a solution for (

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-2\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u+1}+\displaystyle\theta(1+u)^{p},\\

&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

,

(1.18)

We emphasize that equation (

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-a\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u+1}+\displaystyle\theta(1+u)^{p}.

This equation coincides with the one studied in

•

A formal approach, where we understand the dynamics of equation (

•

A rigorous approach, where we linearize the equation around the candidate for the profile and show the existence of a solution converging to

Using the spectral informations about the linearized operator, we first handle the projection of the negative part of the spectral, which happens to be infinite dimensional, with finite codimension. This way, we reduce the question to a finite dimensional problem, which is solved thanks to a topological argument from the degree theory.

\tilde{W}_{0}\sim\frac{\beta\kappa}{(p-1)s}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ and}\hskip 14.22636pt\tilde{W}_{2}\sim\frac{\gamma}{s},

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. We obtain that the ODE system is satisfied if

\gamma=-\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{4(p-2)}.

and we recall that
p=\bar{p}+2>2
. Therefore, the ODE is solved by

\begin{split}&\tilde{W}_{0}=\frac{\beta\kappa}{(p-1)s}+o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right),\\
&\tilde{W}_{2}=-\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{4(p-2)s}+o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right).\end{split}

(2.25)

Thus,

\begin{split}W&=\kappa-\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{4(p-2)}\frac{(|y|^{2}-2N)}{s}+\frac{\beta\kappa}{(p-1)s}+o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\\
&=\kappa-\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{4(p-2)}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}-\frac{\kappa N(1+\beta)}{(p-2)s}+\frac{\beta\kappa}{(p-1)s}+o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\end{split}

(2.26)

in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and also uniformly on compact sets by standard parabolic regularity. However, we need to go further in space and not just in compact sets. Since (
2.5
) is similar to the semilinear heat equation, we are motivated to seek for a blow-up solution of the form

W(y,s)=\varphi_{b}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\text{"lower perturbation"},

where

\varphi_{b}(z)=\left(p-1+b|z|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

(2.27)

for some
b>0
. Using a Taylor expansion, we have that

\varphi_{b}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)=\kappa-\frac{b\kappa}{(p-1)^{2}}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}+O\left(\frac{|y|^{4}}{s^{2}}\right).

(2.28)

Matching this expansion with (
2.26
) with the one just obtained, we get

b=\frac{(p-1)^{2}(1+\beta)}{4(p-2)}.

(2.29)

Thus, we deduce

W(y,s)\sim\varphi(y,s),

where

\varphi(y,s)=\varphi_{b}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{a}{s},

(2.30)

and

a=\frac{\kappa N(1+\beta)}{2(p-2)}+\frac{\beta\kappa}{(p-1)}.

(2.31)

This formal result is adapted from
[
3
]
and
[
17
]
, where in the latter the authors obtained the

estimate

We assume that (
2.32
) hold. Thus, we derive

(2.33)

|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq\frac{C(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}-\frac{1}{2}}\theta(t)^{-{\frac{1}{p-1}}}}{1+\sqrt{|\log(T-t)|}},

(2.34)

for all

and
x\in P_{1}(t)

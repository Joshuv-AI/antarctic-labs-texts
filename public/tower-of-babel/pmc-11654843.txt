Hypertension Induced by Hypoxia‐Inducible Factor Prolyl Hydroxylase Inhibitors in Treating Anemia in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease: A Mini‐Review
Authors: Wei Zhang, Yan Li, Ji‐Guang Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHypoxia‐inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase (HIF‐PH) inhibitors are a new class of agents for the treatment of anemia in chronic kidney disease (CKD). Unlike traditional treatments such as erythropoiesis‐stimulating agents (ESAs), HIF‐PH inhibitors are orally administered drugs and may increase endogenous erythropoietin and improve iron homeostasis. However, a significant concern is their possible side effect on blood pressure. The current mini‐review summarizes the data of 26 randomized controlled (placebo or ESAs) trials on six different HIF‐PH inhibitors with regard to their potential influence on blood pressure and hypertension in the management of anemia in CKD. Overall, the use of HIF‐PH inhibitors was associated with a higher risk of hypertension than placebo (pooled risk ratio 1.36, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.16–1.59), but a lower risk of hypertension than ESA treatment (pooled risk ratio 0.92, 95% CI 0.86–0.98), especially in CKD patients not undergoing dialysis (pooled risk ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.73–0.98). This review highlights the importance of blood pressure monitoring during the treatment of HIF‐PH inhibitors, especially out‐of‐office blood pressure measurement.

Introduction

Renal anemia is a common complication of chronic kidney disease (CKD) and has been found to be associated with reduced quality of life and increased mortality [1]. Current treatment generally includes blood transfusions and the use of erythropoiesis‐stimulating agents (ESAs), typically epoetin and darbepoetin, along with iron supplementation [2]. A recent advance is the potential alternative use of the oral hypoxia‐inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase (HIF‐PH) inhibitors [3], which stimulate endogenous erythropoietin (EPO) production by stabilizing the HIF‐α subunit and allowing the dimerization of the HIF α and β subunits and stimulation of genes involved in the protection against hypoxia, including the EPO gene [4, 5].

Several previous phase 2 and 3 clinical trials have shown a superior effect of various HIF‐PH inhibitors compared to placebo and similar efficacy compared to ESAs [6, 7]. In a meta‐analysis including a total of 30 studies comprising 13 146 patients, HIF‐PH inhibitors treatment showed a slight increase in hemoglobin (Hb) levels (weighted mean differences 0.13, p = 0.001) compared with ESAs [6]. Evaluation of the safety of various HIF‐PH inhibitors, however, is insufficient [8]. A particular case is the increase in blood pressure or incidence of hypertension during treatment with these drugs. HIF‐α subunits are oxygen labile and, under normal oxygen tension, oxidized by prolyl hydroxylases. Inhibitors of prolyl hydroxylases can stabilize HIFs, simulate hypoxia, and promote erythropoiesis. Animal studies suggest that HIF stabilizers may induce hypertension by provoking calcification and augmenting chronic intermittent hypoxia as occurs in sleep apnea [9]. Chronic intermittent hypoxia through HIF signaling in the carotid artery is thought to provoke systemic hypertension.

Literature Search

We searched the English literature up to March 1, 2023 for randomized controlled clinical trials that investigated the efficacy and safety of HIF‐PH inhibitors. The searched databases included PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. The relevant text words and medical subject headings included all spellings of “Prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor”, “PHD inhibitor”, “hypoxia‐inducible factor stabilizer”, “HIF stabilizer”, “Roxadustat”, “FG‐4592”, “Vadadustat”, “AKB‐6548”, “Daprodustat”, “GSK127883”, “Molidustat”, “BAY 85–3934”, “Enarodustat”, “JTZ‐951”, “DS‐1093a”, “Desidustat”, and “anemia OR anaemia”.

In total, 87 articles were identified. After the removal of duplicates and screening abstracts and references of the identified articles, 41 full‐length articles were assessed. From these assessed articles, 15 (36.6%) were removed, because of outcomes not of interest (n = 6) or incomplete data (n = 9). Finally, a total of 26 studies were included in the present review (Figure 1). We first describe the results of these individual trials, and then perform a pooled analysis on the risk of hypertension.

Individual Drugs

Roxadustat

The most reported HIF‐PH inhibitor is roxadustat (FG‐4592) from FibroGen (San Francisco, CA, USA), Astellas (Tokyo, Japan), and AstraZeneca (London, UK, Table 1). In a phase 2 double‐blind trial in 91 patients who were not dialysis‐dependent [10], Hb increase ≥1 g/dL from baseline was achieved in 80.0% of patients in the low‐dose (1.1–1.75 mg/kg) group and 87.1% in the high‐dose (1.50–2.25 mg/kg) group, versus 23.3% in the placebo arm. Four roxadustat‐treated but no placebo‐treated patients reported having hypertension as AE.

In a further phase 3 trial in Chinese patients with CKD and not undergoing dialysis [11], those in the roxadustat group had a higher mean change from baseline in the Hb level than those in the placebo group after 8 weeks of follow‐up (1.9 vs. −0.4). Hypertension occurred in six patients in the roxadustat group (6%) and two patients in the placebo group (4%). In a European trial in patients with anemia (n = 597) [12], 79.2% of patients in the roxadustat treatment group were responders compared with 9.9% in the placebo group during the first 24 weeks of treatment without patients having received rescue therapy. Hypertension occurred in 87 patients in the roxadustat group (22.3%) and 28 patients in the placebo group (13.8%). The ANDES (A study of roxadustat for the treatment of anemia in participants with chronic kidney disease and not receiving dialysis) study investigated in 922 patients with stage 3–5 CKD and not on the dialysis efficacy and tolerability of roxadustat [13]. In the roxadustat (n = 611) and placebo (n = 306) groups, the mean Hb change from baseline over weeks 28–52 was significantly greater for roxadustat versus placebo, with a least‐square mean difference of 1.85 g/dL (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.74–1.97). Hypertension as AE was reported in 15.5% (95/611) of roxadustat treated patients and 8.8% (27/306) of patients on placebo. In another phase 3 study in non‐dialysis dependent CKD patients [14], the mean change in Hb from baseline was 1.75 g/dL with roxadustat versus 0.40 g/dL with placebo. A common AE with both roxadustat and placebo was hypertension (11.5% vs. 9.1%, respectively).

In 87 Chinese patients with anemia of CKD and on dialysis [10], 59.1%, 88.9%, and 100% of the low‐, medium‐, and high‐dose patients maintained their Hb levels after 5‐ and 6‐week treatment versus 50% of the epoetin alfa‐treated patients (Table 2). Three roxadustat‐treated and one epoetin alfa‐treated subjects reported having hypertension as AE. In a further phase 3 trial in 305 Chinese patients with CKD and undergoing dialysis [15], roxadustat led to a numerically greater mean change in the Hb level from baseline to week 23 through week 27 than epoetin alfa (0.7 vs. 0.5) and was statistically noninferior to epoetin alfa (difference 0.2; 95% CI [−0.02 to 0.5]). Hypertension occurred in 25 patients in the roxadustat group (12.3%) and 16 patients in the placebo group (16.0%). The SIERRAS (Study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of roxadustat in the treatment of anemia in participants with end‐stage renal disease on stable dialysis) study was a phase 3 trial enrolled 741 adults on dialysis or end‐stage renal disease receiving ESAs for anemia [16], and roxadustat was noninferior to epoetin alfa (least squares mean difference 0.48 [95% CI 0.37–0.59]). One of the most frequently reported AEs was hypertension, occurring in 16.8% of patients in the roxadustat group and 12.7% of patients in the epoetin alfa group. In PYRENEES (A phase 3, randomized, open‐label, active‐controlled study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of roxadustat in the maintenance treatment of anemia in end stage renal disease patients on stable dialysis) study (n = 836) [17], the least square mean treatment differences for the Hb change from baseline to weeks 28–36 and from baseline to weeks 28–52 were 0.235 and 0.171 g/dL, respectively, demonstrating non‐inferiority of roxadustat to ESA. The AE of hypertension (17.9% vs. 18.8%) was reported similarly in the roxadustat and ESA groups.

Daprodustat

Daprodustat (GSK‐1278863) is a HIF‐PH inhibitor from GlaxoSmithKline (London, UK). In a phase 2 study in patients with anemia on hemodialysis [18], 103 patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1:1:1 ratio to receive daprodustat 10, 15, 25, or 30 mg or placebo three times weekly over 29 days. The results showed a dose‐dependent increase in Hb with three times weekly daprodustat. One AE of hypertensive crisis occurred in the daprodustat 15 mg group. The ASCEND‐NHQ (anemia studies in CKD: Erythropoiesis via a novel prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor daprodustat in non‐dialysis participants evaluating Hb and quality of life) trial evaluated the effects of daprodustat on Hb in a phase 3 study in patients with non‐dialysis dependent CKD [19]. Among 614 randomized patients, the adjusted mean treatment difference in Hb was significant (1.40 g/dL, 95% CI 1.23–1.56). The most frequent AE was the worsening of hypertension (10% [31/307] and 8% [26/307] in the daprodustat and placebo groups, respectively).

In 217 Japanese patients not on dialysis [20], the mean Hb levels during 40–52 weeks of treatment were 12.0 g/dL in the daprodustat group and 11.9 g/dL for epoetin beta pegol, with a between‐group difference of 0.1 g/dL. There was no meaningful difference in the frequencies of hypertension as AE (3% [4/149] and 5% [8/150] in the daprodustat and epoetin groups, respectively). The ASCEND‐ND (anemia studies in chronic kidney disease: Erythropoiesis via a novel prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor daprodustat‐non‐dialysis) study compared daprodustat with darbepoetin alfa in 3872 patients with CKD not undergoing dialysis [21]. The mean difference in the Hb levels between treatment groups was 0.08 g/dL (95% CI 0.03–0.13). Worsening of hypertension was reported in 344 of 1937 patients in the daprodustat group (17.8%) and 363 of 1933 patients in the darbepoetin alfa group (18.8%).

In a phase 2, placebo‐controlled study in 97 Japanese patients on hemodialysis and with anemia [22], dose‐dependent increases in the mean Hb changes were observed after 4‐week administration of daprodustat 4–10 mg. Two non‐serious AEs of hypertension were observed in the daprodustat group. The ASCEND‐D (anemia studies in chronic kidney disease: Erythropoiesis via a novel prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor daprodustat‐dialysis) study compared daprodustat with ESAs (epoetin or darbepoetin alfa) in 2956 patients with CKD and undergoing dialysis [23]. The mean difference in the Hb levels between the two treatment groups was 0.18 g/dL (95% CI 0.12–0.24). Worsening of hypertension was reported in 293 of 1482 patients in the daprodustat group (19.8%) and 302 of 1474 patients in the darbepoetin alfa group (20.5%). The ASCEND‐ID (anemia studies in chronic kidney disease: Erythropoiesis via a novel prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor daprodustat‐in incident dialysis) study investigated in 312 CKD patients who started dialysis within 90 days before randomization the efficacy and safety of daprodustat versus darbepoetin alfa in treating anemia of CKD [24]. The mean Hb concentration was 10.5 g/dL for daprodustat and 10.6 g/dL for darbepoetin alfa, with an adjusted mean treatment difference of −0.10 g/dL (95% CI −0.34 to 0.14). Worsening of hypertension was the most reported AE, which occurred in 38 patients (24%) in the daprodustat group versus 29 (19%) in the darbepoetin alfa group.

Vadadustat

Vadadustat from Akebia (AKB‐6548, Cambridge, USA) recently has completed the phase 3 stage of development for the treatment of anemia secondary to CKD. In a phase 2b, multicenter, double‐blind, randomized, parallel‐group, placebo‐controlled study in 210 participants with non‐dialysis dependent CKD [25], a successful Hb level response, defined as either mean Hb levels in g/dL or an increase in Hb level of at least 1.2 g/dL from baseline, was achieved in a greater percentage of vadadustat‐treated patients than those on placebo (54.9% vs. 10.3%; p < 0.0001). Hypertension was reported as an AE in 8.0% of vadadustat‐treated patients (11 of 138) and 2.8% of those on placebo (two of 72). In a phase 2 trial [26], 111 patients with CKD‐induced anemia received once‐daily vadadustat (150, 300, or 600 mg) or placebo for 6 weeks. Significant dose‐dependent increases in the mean Hb values were observed at week 6 in all vadadustat groups versus placebo (placebo and vadadustat 150, 300, and 600 mg: −0.47, 0.43, 1.13, and 1.62 in non‐dialysis dependent CKD and −1.48, 0.28, 0.08, and 0.41 in dialysis‐dependent CKD, respectively). Five participants (13.5%) in the vadadustat groups and none in the placebo group had hypertension as AEs.

In a phase 3 trial, a total of 3476 patients with non‐dialysis dependent CKD were randomized to vadadustat (n = 1739) and darbepoetin alfa (n = 1732) [27]. The mean between‐group differences in the change in the Hb concentration at week 24 through week 36 were 0.05 g/dL (95% CI −0.04 to 0.15) in previously ESA‐untreated patients and −0.01 g/dL in previously ESA‐treated patients. Hypertension was reported as an AE in 16.0% of vadadustat‐treated patients (279 of 1739) and 18.5% of those treated with darbepoetin alfa (320 of 1732). Similarly, in 151 and 153 Japanese participants received vadadustat and darbepoetin alfa, respectively [28], the mean between‐group difference was −0.26 g/dL (95% CI −0.50 to −0.02). Two patients in the vadadustat group and 11 in the darbepoetin alfa group reported hypertension. In a phase 3 trial of 3923 patients with anemia and dialysis‐dependent CKD [29], the mean difference between the vadadustat and darbepoetin alfa groups in the change in Hb concentration were −0.31 and −0.17 g/dL in patients with incident and prevalent dialysis‐dependent CKD, respectively. Hypertension was reported as an AE in 11.1% of vadadustat‐treated patients (216 of 1947) and 13.7% of those treated with darbepoetin alfa (268 of 1955).

Enarodustat

Enarodustat (JTZ‐951) is being developed as an orally available drug to promote hematopoiesis. In two phase 2 trials in anemia patients with CKD not on dialysis and on hemodialysis, respectively, the change in Hb from baseline was greater with the increasing dose of enarodustat and over 65.1% of patients maintained their Hb level within the target range at the end of treatment [30, 31]. Thirteen and one patient, respectively, in the enarodustat treatment groups of these two trials reported hypertension as AE. The incidence of hypertension‐related AEs in the placebo groups were not reported in either of the two studies.

In a phase 3 trial in 193 anemic patients with CKD not requiring dialysis, the mean Hb level was 10.96 g/dL in the enarodustat group and 10.87 g/dL in the darbepoetin alfa group, with a between‐group difference of 0.09 g/dL [32]. Five (4.7%) patients in the enarodustat group and five (4.6%) in the darbepoetin alfa group reported hypertension or increased blood pressure as AEs. In a phase 3 trial in 172 hemodialysis patients [33], the mean Hb level was 10.73 g/dL in the enarodustat group and 10.85 g/dL in the darbepoetin alfa group, with a between‐group difference of 0.12 g/dL. Four (4.6%) patients in the enarodustat group and two (2.3%) in the darbepoetin alfa group reported hypertension or increased blood pressure as AEs.

Molidustat

Molidustat (BAY 85–3934) is currently being developed by Bayer Healthcare (Wuppertal, Germany). In the daily oral treatment increasing endogenous EPO (DIALOGUE) 1 trial [34], a 16‐week, phase 2b, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled study of once‐ and twice‐daily administration of different fixed dosages of molidustat in anemic ESA‐naive patients with non‐dialysis dependent CKD, 101 patients were randomly assigned to molidustat and 20 patients to placebo [34]. Molidustat treatment was associated with an estimated increase in mean Hb levels of 1.4–2.0 g/dL. The incidence of hypertension‐related AEs was lower for molidustat (10%) than for placebo (25%).

Efficacy was also demonstrated in patients not on dialysis in DIALOGUE 2 (n = 124), in which treatment was switched from darbepoetin to molidustat or continued with darbepoetin. All molidustat dose arms were able to maintain Hb with the prespecified target range of 10.0–12.0 g/dL. Hypertension, however, occurred slightly more often in the molidustat group (15%), compared to the darbepoetin group (13%). In the DIALOGUE 4 trial in patients on dialysis (n = 199), only molidustat starting doses of 75 and 150 mg once daily maintained Hb levels within the target range after switching from epoetin. The incidence of hypertension‐related AE was lower for molidustat (11%) than for epoetin (19%).

Desidustat

Desidustat is currently in the phase 3 stage of development by Cadila Healthcare (ZYAN1, Gujarat, India) [35]. In a phase 2, placebo‐controlled study in 117 non‐dialysis dependent CKD patients with anemia in India, the desidustat arms displayed a mean Hb increase of 1.57, 2.22, and 2.92 g/dL in the 100, 150, and 200 mg treatment arms, respectively, compared with 0.46 g/dL in the placebo arm, after 6 weeks of treatment. No AEs of hypertension were reported during the trial.

Pooled Analysis on the Risk of Hypertension

HIF‐PH Inhibitors Versus Placebo

Twelve trials of HIF‐PH inhibitors versus placebo for anemia in CKD reported hypertensive AEs (5800 participants). The pooled results indicated a significant difference in the risk of hypertension between the HIF‐PH inhibitors and placebo (risk ratio [RR], 1.36 [95% CI 1.16–1.59]). Subgroup analyses for each of the HIF‐PH inhibitors showed that roxadustat was associated with a significantly higher risk of hypertension when compared with placebo (RR 1.43 [95% CI 1.20–1.70]), while others (daprodustat, vadadustat, and molidustat) yielded no statistically significant difference (Figure 2).

Meta‐analysis results of HIF‐PH inhibitors versus placebo for hypertension. CI, confidence interval; HIF‐PH, hypoxia‐inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase; RR, risk ratio.

HIF‐PH Inhibitors Versus Epoetin

Sixteen trials of the HIF‐PH inhibitors versus ESAs reported hypertension events (17 897 participants), with the pooled results showing a lower risk of hypertension with the HIF‐PH inhibitors than ESAs (RR 0.92 [95% CI 0.86–0.98]).

Six trials reported hypertension events in CKD patients who were not on dialysis (9613 participants, Figure 3). The pooled results indicated a significant difference in the risk of hypertension between the HIF‐PH inhibitors and ESA (RR 0.90 [95% CI 0.81–0.99]). Subgroup analyses for each of the HIF‐PH inhibitors (daprodustat, vadadustat, molidustat, and enarodustat) revealed no statistically significant difference in the incidence of hypertension as AE except for vadadustat (RR 0.85 [95% CI 0.73–0.98]).

Meta‐analysis results of HIF‐PH inhibitors versus erythropoiesis‐stimulating agent for hypertension in patients not undergoing dialysis. CI, confidence interval; HIF‐PH, hypoxia‐inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase; RR, risk ratio.

Ten trials reported hypertension events in CKD patients who were on either hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis (8284 participants, Figure 4). The meta‐analysis of these trials indicated no statistically significant difference in the risk of hypertension between the HIF‐PH inhibitors and ESA (RR 0.93 [95% CI 0.85–1.03]). Subgroup analyses for each of the HIF‐PH inhibitors (roxadustat, daprodustat, vadadustat, molidustat, and enarodustat) revealed no statistically significant difference in the incidence of hypertension as AE except for vadadustat (RR 0.81 [95% CI 0.68–0.96]).

Discussion

HIF‐PH inhibitors have consistently shown non‐inferiority compared with ESAs in improving and maintaining Hb levels. Our study indicated that HIF‐PH inhibitors are associated with a higher risk of hypertension than placebo, but a lower risk than ESAs treatment. The mechanisms for the increased risk of hypertension associated with the use of HIF‐PH inhibitors remain under investigation. It is well known that the EPO therapy increases blood pressure and the risk of hypertension [36]. In a review including six trials comparing EPO to placebo/no EPO, a statistically significant doubling of the relative risk for hypertensive adverse events (unadjusted percentages are 13.6% for placebo/no EPO and 30.3% for EPO) as compared with placebo is evident. The mechanisms of EPO‐induced hypertension are incompletely understood. Besides, the direct effects of the prostanoids, endothelins, and nitric oxide pathways, EPO administration is also associated with increased responsiveness to catecholamines and angiotensin II and mitigation of hypoxia‐induced vasodilatation responses.

On the other hand, the HIF stabilizers stimulate EPO genes and many other genes responsible for angiogenesis, tumor growth, cell proliferation, and metabolism. In animal studies, hypoxia induces inorganic phosphorus‐induced vascular smooth muscle calcification, which might contribute to arterial stiffness and then hypertension. Also, chronic intermittent hypoxia through HIF signaling in the carotid artery is thought to provoke systemic hypertension [37]. However, relative to EPOs, the HIF stabilizers may be less pressor than the direct use of EPOs at a similar treatment effect on anemia, because a number of genes within the HIF‐mediated transcriptional cascade are involved in vasomotor control [38], and as evidenced in the pooled analysis of randomized controlled trials that compared these two different therapies.

Limitations

Our study should be interpreted within the context of several limitations. First, in the comparison between HIF‐PH inhibitors and placebo, the observed increased risk of hypertension in HIF‐PH inhibitors was mainly driven by the subgroup analysis of Roxadustat, which was the most reported. Second, the study duration of these trials varied from 4 to 104 weeks, which could also affect the results of this meta‐analysis. Third, there is limited data regarding long‐term cardiovascular outcomes of patients with the treatment of HIF‐PH inhibitors. Last, no trials have been specifically designed to evaluate the effect on blood pressure of HIF‐PH inhibitors.

Conclusions and Perspectives

The HIF‐PH inhibitors are emerging drugs for the treatment of anemia secondary to CKD, which might have a significant influence on anemia treatment. The use of these drugs is associated with a higher risk of hypertension than placebo, but a lower risk of hypertension than the ESA treatment, especially in CKD patients who were not undergoing dialysis. Nonetheless, blood pressure might have to be closely monitored in future research on the HIF‐PH inhibitors. It should be of note that current evidence is mostly based on office blood pressure measurements. Out‐of‐office blood pressure measurement, such as 24‐h ambulatory and home blood pressure monitoring, could help better clarify the association between HIF‐PH inhibitors and blood pressure in CKD patients.

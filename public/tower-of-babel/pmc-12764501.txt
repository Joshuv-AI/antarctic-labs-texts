Intensified pegylated-asparaginase in consolidation therapy improved outcome of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation for adult T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia
Authors: Junjie Chen, Jia Li, Zhixiang Wang, Zicong Huang, Jieping Lin, Jiawang Ou, Xiuli Xu, Bingqing Tang, Chenhao Ding, Zihong Cai, Ren Lin, Xuan Li, Qifa Liu, Hongsheng Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractResearch on the optimal consolidation therapy for adult T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) remains limited, and how to integrate pediatric-inspired chemotherapy with allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (SCT) is less addressed. Based on retrospective analysis indicating that 4 doses of pegylated-asparaginase (PEG-Asp) prior to SCT were optimal for survival, we designed a PEG-Asp-intensified pediatric-inspired regimen, PDT-ALL-2016, for adult T-ALL (NCT03564704). In this protocol, SCT administered following 4 or 5 doses of PEG-Asp. The impact of PEG-Asp on transplantation outcomes was evaluated in two cohorts: the prospective PDT-ALL-2016 cohort (2016.1-2021.12, N = 63) and the retrospective adult regimen cohort (2008.1-2015.12, N = 61). With intensified PEG-Asp consolidation, the prospective cohort exhibited superior survival compared to the retrospective cohort. Furthermore, within the prospective cohort, patients who received non-truncated PEG-Asp demonstrated better survival than those who received a truncated dose. Then, the entire cohort was stratified into high-dose (4–5 doses, N = 45), medium-dose (2–3 doses; N = 33) and low-dose (0–1 doses; N = 46) groups, based on the number of PEG-Asp doses administrated pre-SCT. The high-dose group showed superior survival compared to the other two groups in both the entire cohort and in subgroup analysis. Our findings indicate that high-dose PEG-Asp is associated with improved outcome in adult T-ALL undergoing SCT.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06576-8.Keywords: T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, Asparaginase, Minimal residual disease

Introduction

T cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) accounts for approximately 20–25% of adult ALL cases [1]. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (SCT) has long been considered the cornerstone of treatment for adult ALL when treated with standard adult protocols [1, 2]. However, the introduction of pediatric-inspired regimens has significantly improved survival rates for adult T-ALL [3–7]. Both allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and pediatric-inspired chemotherapy regimens can reduce the relapse rate of adult ALL to approximately 40%. Nevertheless, the optimal integration of SCT with pediatric-inspired chemotherapy in T-ALL to further improve survival remains underexplored.

Depletion of asparagine by administration of the asparaginase (Asp) has been a critical component of the pediatric-inspired regimen [8–10]. We retrospectively observed that post-SCT survival in adult ALL was associated with the cumulative dose of pegylated-asparaginase (PEG-Asp), with 4 doses demonstrating the best survival. Based on this findings, we designed PDT-ALL-2016 pediatric-inspired protocol (NCT03564704), in which SCT is performed after patients receive four or five doses of PEG-Asp during chemotherapy.

In this study, we demonstrate that high-dose PEG-Asp administration prior to SCT significantly improves survival, effectively combining SCT with pediatric-inspired regimen for adult T-ALL patients.

Methods

Patients and pre-HSCT chemotherapy

This study enrolled 124 consecutive subjects diagnosed with T-ALL who underwent HSCT from 2008.1 to 2021.12, with outcomes updated in 2023.1. Data were collected retrospectively for patients managed between 2008.1 and 2015.12 in adult cohort, and prospectively during 2016.1 to 2021.12 in PDT-ALL-2016 cohort. Informed consent was obtained from all the patients. This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Board of Nanfang Hospital, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

The retrospective cohort included patients before 2015.12. In this cohort, patients were treated with adult protocol, including VDLP (Vincristine, Daunorubicin or Idarubicin, PEG-Asp, and Prednisone) or VIP (Vincristine, Daunorubicin or Idarubicin, and Prednisone) as an induction therapy, followed by 3 cycles of Hyper-CVAD A/B consolidation [11] and subsequent SCT. Variable doses of PEG-Asp was added into the consolidation, according to doctors’ decision and donors’ preparation.

The prospective PDT-ALL-2016 protocol was used from 2016.1, this protocol had been previously reported [12–17]. SCT was recommended for all patients diagnosed with T-ALL. The timing of SCT was scheduled after 2 phases and 3 blocks of PEG-Asp-containing induction and consolidation therapies (total of 4 or 5 doses PEG-Asp was administrated pre-SCT), the detailed protocol was attached in the supplemental data. We uniformly employ the long-acting PEG-Asp as first-line treatment to reduce the risk of allergic reactions, improve patient compliance, and maximize the total PEG-Asp dosage. We utilize a Tolerated Dosage (TD-Asp) titration approach: the initial dose starts at 2000 U/m². If the recovery time from coagulation disorders exceeds two weeks, the subsequent dose is reduced by 20%. This process is repeated iteratively until the dose at which coagulation disorders resolve within two weeks is identified as the tolerated dose. Additionally, the maximum single dose of asparaginase for any individual is capped at 3750 IU/m2.

The risk classification in this study was reported previously [18]. High-risk features included: (1) white blood cell count (WBC) count ≥ 100 × 109/L; (2) complex karyotype (3) T-cell precursor (ETP) immunophenotype; (4) central nervous system leukemia (CNSL) at diagnosis.

CR was defined as disappearance of leukemic blasts in the peripheral blood and < 5% blasts in the bone marrow (BM) aspirate, recovery of blood cell count, and lack of extramedullary disease. MRD evaluation took place prior to consolidation (day 45) and before SCT. MRD assessment was determined by 8-color flow cytometry according to the standardized EuroFlow methodology as previously described [19, 20], cutoff values of MRD-positive were defined as 0.01%. bone marrow samples were used for the monitoring of MRD.

Transplant modalities

Patients received SCT from human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-matched sibling donor (MSD), unrelated-donor (MUD) or haploidentical related donor (HID). Donor selection was based on patients’ biological characteristics and patients or guardians consent [21]. TBI/Cy (total body irradiation: 4.5 Gy/d on days − 5 and − 4 from a linear accelerator with lung and lens shielding, cyclophosphamide: 60 mg/kg once daily on days − 3 to − 2) was the conditioning regimens as previously reported [14]. Acute and chronic graft-versus-host diseases (GVHD) were diagnosed and graded according to previously reported criteria. Cyclosporine A (CsA) combined with methotrexate (MTX) were used for GVHD prophylaxis in patients with MSD transplant. CsA + MTX + antithymocyte globulin and mycophenolate were used in patients undergoing MUD or HID SCT [22].

Statistical methods

Associations between categorical variables were assessed by a Fisher exact test, while the Wilcoxon two sample test was performed to test for differences between continuous variables. Overall survival (OS) was measured from the date of transplantation to the date of death or last follow-up. Leukemia-free survival (LFS) was measured from the date of transplantation to the date of event occurred or at the last follow-up. Relapse or death by any cause were considered as events in the LFS analysis. Cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR) was calculated from the date of transplantation to the date of relapse, considering non-relapse mortality (NRM) as a competing event. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to compare survival. Probabilities of NRM and CIR were generated using cumulative incidence estimates to account for competing risks and compared by Gray’s test. To adjust for differences in baseline characteristics, Cox proportional hazards regression was used.

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author (hanson_tcm@126.com) upon reasonable request.

Effect of pretransplant PEG-Asp dose for survival in retrospective cohort and prospective cohort

Based on retrospective observation, we found that clinical outcomes for adult ALL (both B and T-ALL) improved with increasing doses of PEG-Asp administered during pre-SCT consolidation. A trend toward the best survival was observed with 4 doses of PEG-Asp, compared to lower doses (Figure S1). In 2016, we designed the PDT-ALL-2016 protocol, in which SCT was performed following 2 phases and 3 blocks of PEG-Asp-containing induction and consolidation therapies (the use of PEG-Asp in phase 2 induction was determined by MRD response and risk classification).

In T-ALL, intensified PEG-Asp treatment in the prospective cohort resulted in superior survival outcomes compared to the retrospective cohort. A significantly higher proportion of patients in the prospective cohort achieved MRD-negative status prior to transplantation (74.6% vs. 62.5%, Table 1). The 5-year OS rates was 69.3% (58.1–82.6%) in the prospective cohort, versus 46.3% (35.1–61.1%) in the retrospective cohort (p =.001). The 5-year LFS rates was 61.8% (55.0-69.4%) versus 39.0% (33.5–45.3%) (p =.001), in prospective and retrospective cohorts, respectively (Fig. 2AB). The 5-year CIR was 17.1% (8.5–28.1%) vs. 42.3% (29.2–57.1%), and the NRM had no statistical difference between two cohorts (Fig. 2CD).

Effect of intensified versus truncated PEG-Asp dose prior to SCT in PDT-ALL-2016 cohort

To assess the effect of intensified PEG-Asp administration in the PDT-ALL-2016 prospective cohort, we compared survival outcomes between patients who received 4 or 5 full doses of PEG-Asp (non-truncated) and those who received truncated doses due to intolerable toxicity. In the PDT-ALL-2016 cohort, high-dose PEG-Asp still showed better survival. The 5-year OS was 78.2% (66.−91.9%) in the non-truncated dose group, compared to 52.1% (33.2–81.6%) in the truncated dose group. The 5-year LFS was 78.2% (66.5–91.9%) in the non-truncated dose group, compared to 25.1% (25.1–72.9%) in the truncated dose group (Fig. 2 EF).

Outcome of high-dose PEG-Asp versus medium- and low-dose in entire cohort

To further assess the impact of high-dose PEG-Asp on survival, we analyzed outcomes across the entire T-ALL cohort. The OS and LFS were superior in 4–5 doses PEG-Asp group (N = 45; 36.2%; high-dose group), followed by the 3, 2, 1 and 0 doses group (Figure S2), survival were similar between 2 and 3 doses (N = 33;26.6%; defined as medium-dose), and 0–1 dose (N = 46; 37.2%; defined as low-dose). Patient characteristics in the three groups are summarized in Table 1, with baseline characteristics balanced across groups.

The clinical outcomes were superior in high-dose group (Fig. 3). The 5-year OS rates were 79.2% (67.9–92.3%) for high-dose group, 63.4% (48.9–83.2%) for the medium-dose group, and 35.3% (23.6–52.8%) for the low-dose group (p <.001). The 5-year LFS were 68.2% (53.3–87.2%) for the high-dose group, 50.3% (34.3–73.8%) for the medium-dose group, and 26.0% (16.0-42.4%) for the low-dose group (p <.001). The 5-year CIR was 13.4% (3.3–30.7%) for the high-dose group, 40.5% (21.3–59.0%) for the medium-dose group, and 58.0% (42.8–71.6%) for the low-dose group (p <.001; Fig. 3 C). The 5-year NRM were similar across the three groups, with rates of 18.5% (8.5–31.5%), 9.1% (2.3–21.9%), and 15.2% (6.6–27.2%) for the high-, medium-, and low-dose groups, respectively (p =.514; Fig. 3D).

Toxicity

Table S1 and Table S2 shows the induction and consolidation cycles related toxicities according to adult regimen cohort versus PDT-ALL-2016 cohort, or low-, medium- and high-dose groups. Infections were the most common toxicities. During the induction therapy, grade 3 to 4 infections occurred in 31 (50.8%) patients in the adult regimen cohort and 30 (47.6%) in the PDT-ALL-2016 cohort, while elevated amylase/lipase (any grade) was observed in 4 (6.6%) and 7 (11.1%) patients, respectively. Acute pancreatitis developed in 2 (3.3%) and 2 (3.2%) cases across the two cohorts. During the consolidation therapy phase, grade 3 to 4 infections were observed in 38 (62.3%) patients in the adult regimen cohort and 36 (57.1%) in the PDT-ALL-2016 cohort. Pancreatic enzyme elevation (any grade) occurred in 3 (4.9%) and 9 (14.3%) patients, respectively. Acute pancreatitis was documented in 3 (4.9%) patients receiving the adult regimen, whereas no cases were reported in the PDT-ALL-2016 cohort.

Meanwhile, during the induction therapy phase, grade 3 to 4 infections occurred in 21 (45.7%), 20 (60.6%), and 20 (44.4%) patients in the high-, medium-, and low-dose PEG-Asp groups, respectively. Pancreatic enzyme elevation (any grade) was observed in 6 (13%), 5 (15.2%), and 0 (0%) patients across the three dose groups. Acute pancreatitis developed in 1 (2.2%) patient in the high-dose group, 3 (9.1%) in the medium-dose group, and 0 (0%) in the low-dose group. It is particularly noteworthy that adverse events such as elevated pancreatic enzymes, thrombosis, and pancreatitis may lead to the withholding of PEG-Asp during the corresponding chemotherapy block. Consequently, the incidence of such adverse events in the high-dose group was 0.

Subgroup analysis

To further investigate the role of high-dose PEG-Asp pretransplant, subgroup analysis were conducted. As shown in Fig. 4 and Table S3. High-dose PEG-Asp showed superior survival in all subsets, except pre-SCT MRD-positive subset. For ETP ALL, the 5-year OS was 79.2% (67.9–92.3%), 63.4% (48.9–82.3%) and 35.3% (23.6–52.8%) in high-, medium-, and low-dose groups, respectively. The 5-year OS of non-ETP T-ALL was 82.6% (68.4–99.6%), 65.0% (47.1–89.6%), and 37.7% (23.7–59.8%), respectively. Of note, in MRD-negative subsets, high-dose PEG-Asp was also associated with better 5-year OS and lower relapse rates. The 5-year OS was 85.2% (74.0-98.1%), 68.1% (51.2–90.7%), 51.6% (33.2–80.4%) in high-, medium-, and low-dose groups, respectively.

Discussion

For adult T-ALL, SCT remains the most effective anti-leukemic therapy, reducing relapse rates to 30–40% [1, 2, 23, 24]. With the adoption of pediatric-inspired chemotherapy, the survival of adult T-ALL has improved significantly, achieving relapse rates comparable to those observed in patients undergoing allo-HSCT [6, 25, 26]. However, how to combine SCT and pediatric-inspired regimen to further improve survival for adult T-ALL is less addressed. Herein, we showed that presented that intensified PEG-Asp administration prior to SCT is strongly associated with improved outcomes.

Depletion of asparagine through Asp is a key component of chemotherapy for ALL, and its significance has been well established in pediatric T-ALL [8, 10]. In adult T-ALL, several studies have demonstrated that pediatric-inspired regimens containing L-Asp are associated with superior survival outcomes [5, 27]. Notably, PEG-Asp has shown enhanced anti-leukemic effects compared to the native enzyme [28, 29]. However, research on the optimal consolidation therapy for adult T-ALL remains limited. Previous study had demonstrated that exposure to L-Asp longer than 20 weeks showed improved outcomes compared with shorter exposure [30, 31]. Therefore, in PDT-ALL-2016 protocol, SCT was scheduled after the administration of 4 to 5 doses of PEG-Asp.

Relapse after SCT is the primary cause of treatment failure in ALL. Many studies have confirmed that MRD status prior to SCT is the most powerful predictor of survival in adult ALL [32, 33]. Most MRD turning positive occurs within the first 3 months post-SCT [34], and relapse manifest within 9 months after HSCT [35, 36]. This time window coincident with time required for immune reconstitution to exert graft versus leukemia (GVL) effect (3 months for cellular immunity, and 12 months for full immune reconstitution [37–39]). The PDT-ALL-2016 protocol emphasizes the optimization of pre-SCT chemotherapy through intensified PEG-Asp-containing consolidation to achieve deep remission as far as possible, prevent early relapse, and allow sufficient time for immune system reconstitution to fully exert the GVL effect.

The critical role of PEG-Asp as a cornerstone of pediatric-inspired regimens has been extensively studied. However, the optimal pre-transplant doses of PEG-Asp remains controversial for patients undergoing SCT. This study therefore focuses on investigating the quantitative optimization of PEG-Asp administration prior to transplantation. In this study, intensified PEG-Asp consolidation led to a higher proportion of patients achieving MRD negativity prior to SCT, which may partially explain the improved survival in prospective cohort and high-dose PEG-Asp group. In fact, for those who achieved MRD-negative status, more sensitive techniques, such as next-generation sequencing-based MRD, may detect residual leukemic cells [40]. We speculate that intensified PEG-Asp administration not only increased the proportion of patients achieving MRD negativity before SCT but also achieved a deeper MRD response. This hypothesis is supported by subgroup analysis, which demonstrated that in the MRD-negative pre-SCT subset, the high-dose PEG-Asp group exhibited superior OS, LFS, and CIR compared to the medium-dose and low-dose groups. Our previous study, the PASS-ALL trial, suggested that the PDT-ALL-2016 protocol may induce durable or deeper MRD responses (d/d MRD) in HR-ALL. In that study, as consolidation treatment, PDT-ALL-2016 regimen resulted in significant longer time-to-positive MRD than adult protocol [15].

We compared toxicities between the two cohorts during both induction and consolidation phases. The results demonstrated that high-dose PEG-Asp exhibited an overall manageable safety profile. Consistent with most regimens, infections and elevated liver enzymes remained the most common adverse events [6, 41]. However, the reported incidence of PEG-Asp-specific toxicities, such as pancreatitis and thrombosis, varies significantly across studies [4, 42–44]. In our cohort, these toxicities occurred at relatively low and controllable rates. It is particularly noteworthy that adverse events such as elevated pancreatic enzymes, thrombosis, and pancreatitis may lead to the withholding of PEG-Asp during the corresponding chemotherapy block. Consequently, the incidence of such adverse events in the high-dose group was 0.

Limitations of this study include the extended time interval between the adult regimen cohort (2008–2015) and the PDT-ALL-2016 pediatric-inspired cohort (2016–2022). The evolution of transplantation modality (e.g., increasing use of haploidentical donors) during this period may have introduced confounding bias in inter-cohort comparisons. To address this, we incorporated transplantation modality as a covariate in multivariate analysis, which confirmed high-dose PEG-Asp as an independent prognostic factor. An additional consideration is that the high-dose PEG-Asp group predominantly comprised patients from the PDT-ALL-2016 cohort, whereas medium/low-dose groups were derived from the adult regimen and PDT-ALL-2016 cohorts. Consequently, the superior survival observed with high-dose therapy could theoretically be attributed to protocol differences rather than PEG-Asp dose itself. To mitigate this confounding effect and isolate the therapeutic impact of high-dose PEG-Asp, we performed an intra-cohort comparison within the PDT-ALL-2016 cohort, evaluating outcomes between patients receiving full 4 to 5 doses versus those administered 0 to 3 doses. This analysis demonstrated significantly improved survival among patients complete high-dose PEG-Asp therapy, thereby substantiating its efficacy while minimizing cohort-derived bias.

In summary, our study demonstrates that in adult T-ALL patients undergoing transplantation, adequate consolidation therapy (three cycles) and sufficient asparaginase exposure prior to transplantation are essential. High-dose asparaginase (four doses) reduces post-transplant relapse rates, exhibits manageable toxicity, and does not increase NRM, thereby enabling more patients to achieve superior long-term survival. Furthermore, our subgroup analysis revealed that even among patients who achieved pre-transplant MRD negativity, those receiving high-dose asparaginase had better survival outcomes compared to those with lower asparaginase exposure. This suggests that high-dose asparaginase may induce deeper and more durable MRD responses.

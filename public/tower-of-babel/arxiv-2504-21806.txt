The embedding space of a Hopf link
Rachael Boyd, Corey Bregman

Abstract
. We study the unparametrised smooth embedding space of a Hopf link in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , and prove that it is is homotopy equivalent to the closed 3-manifold S 3 / ℚ 8 S^{3}/\mathbb{Q}_{8} . As an intermediate step in the proof, we show that the inclusion of the subspace of round embeddings is a homotopy equivalence. We provide analogous results for the unparametrised smooth embedding space of a Hopf link in S 3 S^{3} , which we show is homotopy equivalent to ℝ ​ P 2 × ℝ ​ P 2 \mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2} .

1.
Introduction

Let
H\colon S^{1}\sqcup S^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the smooth 2-component link whose image is the union of the unit circle centered at the origin in the
xy
-plane and the unit circle centered at
(1,0,0)
in the
xz
-plane. The isotopy class of
H
gives the simplest example of a nontrivial 2-component link, and is known as the
Hopf link
. We study the homotopy type of the unparametrised smooth embedding space of the Hopf link in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i.e. the connected component of

\emb(S^{1}\sqcup S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})/\Diff(S^{1}\sqcup S^{1})

containing
H
. Denote this space by
\mathcal{E}(H)
.
Our main result shows that
\mathcal{E}(H)
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex and provides an explicit finite CW model. Regarding
S^{3}
as the Lie group
\SU(2)
of unit quaternions, let
\mathbb{Q}_{8}
denote the subgroup
\langle\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\rangle
and let
S^{3}/\mathbb{Q}_{8}
be the prism-manifold quotient. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

The embedding space
\mathcal{E}(H)
is homotopy equivalent to
S^{3}/\mathbb{Q}_{8}.

The corresponding
round
embedding space, which we denote
\mathcal{R}(H)
, is the subspace of
\mathcal{E}(H)
for which each component is a round circle lying in an affine hyperplane
\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. To prove Theorem
A
, we first reduce to studying this subspace.

Theorem B
.

The inclusion map
\mathcal{R}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}(H)
is a homotopy equivalence.

In the final section of the paper we prove analogous results for the unparametrised embedding space of a Hopf link in
S^{3}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
. In this setting, the inclusion of a round subspace is also a homotopy equivalence, as we show in Theorem
5.2
, and has the homotopy type of an explicit finite manifold.

Theorem C
.

The embedding space
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}.

Recall that Hatcher
[
Hat83
, Appendix]
showed that the unparametrised embedding space of an unknot in
S^{3}
is homotopy equivalent to the space of great circles in
S^{3}
, which can be identified with the Grassmannian
\Gr_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
.
To prove Theorem
C
, we show that
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
is homotopy equivalent to a quotient of
\Gr_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
by the involution which exchanges
V
and
V^{\perp}
. It is interesting that, up to homotopy, the space of Hopf links is naturally a quotient of the space of unknots in this way.

Damiani and Kamada computed the fundamental group of
\mathcal{R}(H)
to be the quaternion group
\mathbb{Q}_{8}

[
DK19
]
, and we independently obtain that the (smooth)
motion group

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{E}(H))
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}_{8}
. This agrees with Goldsmith’s computation of the
topological motion group

\pi_{1}(\Homeo(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\Homeo(\mathbb{R}^{3},L))
of torus links
[
Gol82
]
where we view
H
as the torus link
T(2,2)
. We therefore conclude that the smooth and topological motion groups are the same. By
[
BB25
, Corollary B]
the motion group in
S^{3}
agrees with the motion group in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

In
[
BH13
]
, Brendle–Hatcher studied the unparametrised embedding space
\mathcal{E}(U_{n})
, where
U_{n}
is the
n
-component unlink in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. They also prove that the inclusion of the subspace
\mathcal{R}(U_{n})
of round embeddings is a homotopy equivalence. As a consequence, one knows that
\mathcal{E}(U_{n})
has the homotopy type of a finite-dimensional manifold. Our work is inspired by this, in the setting of a single Hopf link.

In previous work we study the homotopy type of the unparametrised embedding space
\mathcal{E}(L)
of a
split link

L
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
BB25
]
. We show that
\mathcal{E}(L)
is homotopy equivalent to a space with extra structure, and thus provide an iterated semi-direct product formula for the motion group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{E}(L))
. When
L
is a union of unknot and Hopf link components, the results of
[
BB25
]
are combined with Theorem
B
to derive a formula for the motion group, given in
[
BB25
, Example 4.12]
.

Organisation

In Section

we set up definitions and notation, and collect results on the homotopy type of some embedding spaces for later use. We prove Theorem
B
in Section
3
and Theorem
A
in Section
4
. In Section

we consider the space of Hopf links in

and prove the two analogous theorems.

2.
Setting up notation and preliminary lemmas

In this section we establish notation and define the spaces which we will work with throughout the paper. We then collect some important results concerning the topology of embedding spaces that we will need.

2.1.
Definitions and notation

Let
\emb(M,N)
be the space of smooth embeddings of
M
into
N
, with the

topology, and let

be the subspace of smooth embeddings of
M
into

that restrict to a fixed embedding on
\partial M

\partial M
is a pointwise fixed subset of

. All embeddings we consider will be proper, i.e. map boundaries to boundaries and interiors to interiors.

Let

equipped with the

topology. For

let

be the subgroup of diffeomorphisms of

which restrict to the identity map on
M
, and let
\Diff(N,M)
be the subgroup of diffeomorphisms of
N
which fix

setwise, i.e.

. If

has boundary, let
\Diff_{\partial}(M)=\Diff_{\partial M}(M)
.

.

The basic model for the Hopf link is the embedding

\displaystyle\text{Component }1:

\displaystyle z=0

\displaystyle\text{Component }2:

\displaystyle(x-1)^{2}+z^{2}=1,

\displaystyle y=0.

. We will refer to any link that is isotopic to

.

Let

Definition 2.3
.

A
round unknot
is an unparametrised embedding

\phi\in\emb(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})/\Diff(S^{1})

that determines a Euclidean circle in an affine hyperplane

. A

is
\rho\in\mathcal{E}(H)
such that each component of
\rho
is a round unknot. Let
\mathcal{R}(H)
be the subspace of
\mathcal{E}(H)
consisting of round Hopf links, and call this the

.

Observe that every round unknot bounds a canonical flat disc
D^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The canonical discs spanned by the two components of a round Hopf link
\rho\in\mathcal{R}(H)
intersect in a straight arc, with one endpoint on each unknot component.
We call this (unoriented) arc the
arc of intersection
of the Hopf link, and denote it
\arc(\rho)
. It is shown in blue in Figure
1
.

The embedding
H
from Example
2.1
is round. Hence
H
will also serve as a basepoint for
\mathcal{R}(H)
. The link
H
also has the following additional properties which we record here.

•

Each component of
H
has radius 1.

•

The canonical discs spanned by the components of
H
are perpendicular.

•

The endpoints of the arc of intersection

are the center points of the canonical disks spanned by the two components.

2.2.
Rounding unknots

The following lemma is equivalent to the Smale conjecture (see (15) in the Appendix to
[
Hat83
]
) and will be used to make the components of Hopf links round.

Lemma 2.4
.

The space
\mathcal{R}(U)
of round embeddings of the unknot in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is homotopy equivalent to the space
\mathcal{E}(U)
of smooth embeddings.

2.3.
Straightening unknotted arcs

Let
B^{3}
be the closed 3-ball. An equator divides
\partial B^{3}
into two open hemispheres
S^{2}_{+}
and
S^{2}_{-}
. Consider the space

\emb_{U}((I,\{1\},\{0\}),(B^{3},S^{2}_{+},S^{2}_{-}))

of proper embeddings of
I=[0,1]
into
B^{3}
which send
\{1\}
to
S^{2}_{+}
and
\{0\}
into
S^{2}_{-}
, where the subscript
U
indicates that the embeddings are isotopic to an unknotted arc.

Lemma 2.5
.

The space of embeddings
\emb_{U}((I,\{1\},\{0\}),(B^{3},S^{2}_{+},S^{2}_{-}))
is contractible.

Proof.

The forgetful map
\emb_{U}((I,\{1\},\{0\}),(B^{3},S^{2}_{+},S^{2}_{-}))\rightarrow S^{2}_{+}\times S^{2}_{-}
which records the image of the endpoints is a fibration. By a statement equivalent to the Smale conjecture the fiber is contractible (see (6) in the Appendix to
[
Hat83
]
). Since
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{2}_{-}
is contractible, we conclude
\emb_{U}((I,\{1\},\{0\}),(B^{3},S^{2}_{+},S^{2}_{-}))
is also contractible.
∎

2.4.
Contractibility of collars

Let
M
be a smooth compact manifold with boundary and let
F\subset\partial M
be a compact codimension 0 submanifold (possibly with boundary).

Definition 2.6
.

A
collar of
F
is an embedding of
\chi\colon F\times I\rightarrow M
such that
\chi(F\times\{0\})=F\subset\partial M
and
\chi(F\times(0,1])\subset\Int(M)
. Denote by
\coll(F)
the space of collars of

.

When
\partial F\neq\emptyset
, we regard

as a manifold with corners. On the other hand, if
\partial F=\emptyset
(and hence

is a component of

), then the above notion of collar agrees with the usual one. The following result is due to Cerf
[
Cer61
, 5.2.1, Corollaire 1]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
F\subset\partial M
be a codimension 0 compact submanifold. The space of collars
\coll(F)
is contractible.

3.
Relating the round and smooth embedding spaces

In this section we prove our intermediate theorem:

.

The inclusion map
\mathcal{R}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}(H)
is a homotopy equivalence.

Our proof takes inspiration from Hatcher’s work
[
Hat76
,
Hat99
]
. Since
\mathcal{E}(H)
is a Fréchet manifold (see
[
BF81
,
Mic80
]
) and thus has the homotopy type of a CW complex
[
Mil59
,
Pal66
]
, it is enough to prove that the inclusion
\mathcal{R}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}(H)
induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. Equivalently, we must show that the relative homotopy groups
\pi_{k}(\mathcal{E}(H),\mathcal{R}(H))
vanish for all
k\geq 0
.

3.1.
Notation and first reductions

Since the proof will occupy the entire section we first set up some notation used throughout the proof, and describe a preliminary step. To this end, suppose
f\colon(D^{k},\partial D^{k})\to(\mathcal{E}(H),\mathcal{R}(H))
is a
D^{k}
-parametrised family of Hopf links representing a relative homotopy class
[f]\in\pi_{k}(\mathcal{E}(H),\mathcal{R}(H))
.

•

The standard round Hopf link
H
of Example
2.1
is the basepoint for both
\mathcal{E}(H)
and
\mathcal{R}(H)
, so for the basepoint
t_{0}\in\partial D^{k}=S^{k-1}
,
f(t_{0})=H
.

•

We make a choice of labelling of the link components at the basepoint and denote them
L_{1}=L_{1}(t_{0})
and
L_{2}=L_{2}(t_{0})
. Since
D^{k}
is contractible this yields a continuous choice of labelling
L_{1}(t)
and
L_{2}(t)
of the link
f(t)=(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t))
for each
t\in D^{k}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The map
f\colon(D^{k},\partial D^{k})\to(\mathcal{E}(H),\mathcal{R}(H))
is homotopic rel
\partial D^{k}
to a map such that
L_{1}(t)
is round for all
t\in D^{k}
.

Proof.

The restriction
L_{1}(t)
of
f
to the first component is a
D^{k}
-family of unknots representing a relative homotopy class in
\pi_{k}(\mathcal{E}(U),\mathcal{R}(U))
. By Lemma
2.4
, this relative homotopy group is trivial, so we can homotope
L_{1}(t)
rel
\partial D^{k}
into
\mathcal{R}(U)
. Extend this homotopy by isotopy extension to the second component
L_{2}(t)
. Note that
L_{2}(t)
remains round and unchanged for
t\in S^{k-1}
, since the homotopy restricts to the identity on
\partial D^{k}
, and thus we can assume that the isotopy extension acts as the identity.
∎

4.
Computing the homotopy type

In this section, we prove our main result: that

by the quaternion group

\mathbb{R}^{3}

•

•

•

5.
Hopf links in
S^{3}

In this section we briefly discuss the translation of the above results to embeddings of Hopf links in
S^{3}
. Let
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
denote the space of smooth, unparametrised embeddings of a Hopf link in
S^{3}
. In this case our basepoint is the embedding consisting of the two great circles

H=\{(x,y,0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\mid x^{2}+y^{2}=1\}\cup\{(0,0,z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\mid z^{2}+w^{2}=1\}.

Definition 5.1
.

Recall that a great circle of
S^{3}
is the intersection of
S^{3}
with a 2-plane in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
Let
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
be the subspace of
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
where each component is a great circle, and the two components lie in orthogonal subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We will refer to a point of
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
as a
great Hopf link
.

The Smale conjecture (see (7) in
[
Hat83
, Appendix]
) is also equivalent to the assertion that the unparametrised embedding space of an unknot in
S^{3}
is homotopy equivalent to the space of great circles. Using this fact as our starting point, an argument analogous to that of Section
3
yields the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

The inclusion
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

The proof that the relative homotopy groups
\pi_{k}(\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3}),\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3}))
are trivial follows a very similar procedure to the proof of Theorem
B
, where we showed that the relative homotopy groups
\pi_{k}(\mathcal{E}(H),\mathcal{R}(H))
are trivial. We highlight in the bullet points below the key inputs we use in the
S^{3}
case, but the general set-up and the most technical part of the proof follows the proof of Theorem
B
without change.

•

We first use (7) in
[
Hat83
, Appendix]
to homotope the link component
L_{1}(t)
to be a great circle for all
t\in D^{k}
.

•

The round component
L_{1}(t)
spans a family of flat discs in
S^{3}
. Make a choice of one of these discs to be
D_{1}(t)
– this can be done continuously over
D^{k}
.

•

To define an ellipsoid, note that there is a unique great circle perpendicular to the disc
D_{1}(t)
. We define the ellipsoid
E_{h}
to have NP and SP on this great circle, at distance
h
from
D_{1}(t)
, and choose the maximum height of the ellipsoid to be such that the upper and lower hemispheres of the ellipsoid are disjoint.

•

The proof to homotope
D_{2}(t)
continuously over
D^{k}
such that it intersects
D_{1}(t)
at its centrepoint is identical to that in the proof of Theorem
B
, except for the arc through the NP and SP of the ellipsoid we choose at one of the intermediate stages. In the
S^{3}
setting, we homotope the arc in
E_{i}
to the arc passing through the NP and SP which agrees with the segment of the great circle perpendicular to
D_{1}(t)
. This property will then pass to ellipsoids of smaller height, which we need to make the homotopy continuous over
D^{k}
.

•

At this point instead of shrinking
L_{1}
(which would mean it does not remain a great circle) we can consider the complement of the ball
B_{i}
bounded by the ellipsoid
E_{i}
in
S^{3}
, which is also a ball with boundary
E_{i}
. We can homotope the arc of
L_{2}(t)
lying in this ball to the arc segment given by the the great circle perpendicular to
D_{1}(t)
. This can once again be done continuously over the
D^{k}
since the great circle is the same for ellipsoids of all heights, and this completes the proof.∎

We now show that
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
has the homotopy type of a compact manifold, and thus determine the homotopy type of
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
as well.

.

The embedding space
\mathcal{E}(H,S^{3})
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.2
it is enough to show that the subspace
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. Since each 2-plane in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
has a unique orthogonal complementary 2-plane, each great circle
\gamma
determines a
unique
great circle
\gamma^{\perp}
linking
\gamma
exactly once and lying in the orthogonal complement of the plane containing
\gamma
. Accordingly, the space of oriented, labeled, great Hopf links in
S^{3}
with linking number
+1
can be identified with the oriented Grassmannian

\Gr_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=\SO(4)/(\SO(2)\times\SO(2)).

Regarding
\SO(4)
as orthonormal bases with determinant
+1
, we see that

(i)

\SO(4)
acts transitively on the space of oriented, labeled, great Hopf links.

(ii)

The identity coset corresponds to the basepoint
H
.

As in Section
4
, to pass from oriented, labeled, great Hopf links to
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
, we must further quotient by the action of
\langle\alpha,s\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
where
\alpha
acts by flipping over both link components, and
s
acts by exchanging the link components. Accordingly, we see that taking the quotient by
\alpha
yields
\Gr_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, the space of unoriented 2-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Quotienting further by
s
then identifies each 2-plane
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
with its orthogonal complement
V^{\perp}
. Thus
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
is the space of unordered pairs
\{V,V^{\perp}\}
where
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
is 2-dimensional, or equivalently the space of all orthogonal splittings
\mathbb{R}^{4}=V\oplus V^{\perp}
.

To see that
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
is
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, we first argue that
\Gr_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, closely following the exposition in
[
BFV25
]
. Regarding
\mathbb{R}^{4}\cong\mathbb{H}
as the quaternions, the standard inner product is given by
\langle x,y\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(x\bar{y}+y\bar{x}).
The function
\mu(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(x\bar{y}-y\bar{x})
defines a map from
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
to the subset of pure imaginary quaternions, since

\overline{\mu(x,y)}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{(x\bar{y}-y\bar{x})}=\frac{1}{2}(y\bar{x}-x\bar{y})=-\mu(x,y).

Thus, when
x\perp y
it follows from the inner product formula that
\mu(x,y)=y\bar{x}=-x\bar{y}.

If, moreover,
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
are orthogonal unit quaternions, then
\mu(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
is a purely imaginary unit quaternion. Similarly, the function
\nu(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{x}y-\bar{y}x)
defines a map from
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
to the subset of pure imaginary quaternions, and sends pairs of orthonormal quaternions to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Let
V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\mid x\perp y\}
be the Stiefel manifold of 2-frames in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and consider the map
\xi\colon V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\to\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
given by
\xi(x,y)=(\mu(x,y),\nu(x,y)).
Since
\mu,\nu
are clearly equivariant with respect to the
\SO(2)
action given by right multiplication by
e^{i\theta}
, we obtain a quotient map
\bar{\xi}\colon V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})/\SO(2)\cong\Gr^{+}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.
It is not difficult to see that
\bar{\xi}
is a homeomorphism; see
[
BFV25
]
for details.

Therefore, to compute the quotient
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
, we need only determine how
\alpha
and
s
act on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We may regard
\alpha,s
as acting on
\SO(4)
by right multiplication by the two matrices,

\alpha=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}1&0&0&0\\
0&-1&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&-1&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right),~s=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&1\\
\hline\cr 1&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&0\end{array}\right),

hence the induced action on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
is by isometries with respect to the standard product metric. If
(x,y)\in V_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
span a 2-plane, then
\alpha(x,y)=(x,-y)
. Thus
\mu(x,-y)=-\mu(x,y)
and
\nu(x,-y)=-\nu(x,y)
, so
\xi(\alpha(x,y))=-\xi(x,y)
. It follows that
\alpha
acts on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
by the antipodal map
-I
in both coordinates. Thus we conclude
\Gr_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=\Gr_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{4})/\langle\alpha\rangle\cong\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}/\langle(-I,-I)\rangle
. According to
[
Hil02
, Ch. 12.2]
, the only group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
that acts freely by isometries on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
and contains
(-I,-I)
is

\langle(I,I),(-I,I),(I,-I),(-I,-I)\rangle.

Since
\langle\alpha,s\rangle
acts freely, we conclude that
\mathcal{R}(H,S^{3})
is
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Theorem

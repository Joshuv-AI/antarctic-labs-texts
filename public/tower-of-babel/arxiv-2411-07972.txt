A Zero-Knowledge PCP Theorem
Tom Gur, Jack O’Connor, Nicholas Spooner

Abstract
We show that for every polynomial q ∗ q^{*} there exist polynomial-size, constant-query, non-adaptive PCPs for 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} which are perfect zero knowledge against (adaptive) adversaries making at most q ∗ q^{*} queries to the proof. In addition, we construct exponential-size constant-query PCPs for 𝖭𝖤𝖷𝖯 \mathsf{NEXP} with perfect zero knowledge against any polynomial-time adversary. This improves upon both a recent construction of perfect zero-knowledge PCPs for # ​ 𝖯 \#\mathsf{P} (STOC 2024) and the seminal work of Kilian, Petrank and Tardos (STOC 1997).



1
Introduction

The PCP theorem
[
AS98
,
ALMSS92
]
states that for any language in
\mathsf{NP}
, there exists a polynomial-size proof that can be checked probabilistically by reading only a constant number of bits from the proof; or, succinctly,

\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{PCP}[\log n,1]\;,

where
\mathsf{PCP}[r,q]
is the class of all languages that admit a PCP verifier that uses
O(r)
random bits and reads
O(q)
bits of the proof (note that logarithmic randomness complexity implies polynomial proof length).

While PCPs originated from the study of zero-knowledge proofs
[
GMR89
]
, there seems to be an intrinsic tension between the two notions: PCPs achieve locality by encoding NP witnesses in a manner that
spreads
global information throughout the proof, whereas zero-knowledge proofs aim to
hide
all information except for the validity of the statement. Moreover, PCPs are fundamentally non-interactive objects, whereas interaction is often crucial for zero knowledge.

Indeed, one must take care in even
defining
zero knowledge PCPs (ZK-PCPs), as it is impossible to achieve non-trivial zero knowledge against a malicious verifier that reads the entire proof. In their seminal work on ZK-PCPs, Kilian, Petrank and Tardos
[
KPT97
]
identify two regimes of interest:

(a)

Polynomial-size
PCPs that are zero-knowledge against a verifier that makes at most a
fixed
polynomial number of queries
q^{*}
to the PCP. In this regime, they construct ZK-PCPs for NP with polylogarithmic query complexity. We refer to
q^{*}
as the “query bound”.

(b)

Exponential-size
PCPs that are zero-knowledge against
any polynomial-time
verifier. In this regime, they construct ZK-PCPs for NEXP with polynomial query complexity.

However, these constructions fall short of a “zero-knowledge PCP theorem” in a fundamental way: the query complexity is polylogarithmic, as opposed to
O(1)
, which is a characteristic property of the PCP theorem.

A major obstacle to achieving
O(1)
query complexity via the
[
KPT97
]
approach is that the technique used to obtain zero knowledge leads to an inherently
adaptive
honest verifier (i.e., which makes multiple rounds of queries to the proof). Known query-reduction methods apply only to non-adaptive PCPs. Aside from its theoretical interest, non-adaptivity is crucial for some applications
[
IWY16
]
. Finally, these constructions only achieve statistical zero knowledge (SZK-PCP) and not perfect zero knowledge (PZK-PCP).

Building on techniques developed in
[
BCFGRS17
,
CFGS18
,
CCGOS23
]
, a recent work
[
GOS24
]
constructed exponential-size PZK-PCPs with polynomially many non-adaptive queries for
\#\mathsf{P}
. Our first result shows that such PZK-PCPs exist for
\mathsf{NEXP}
, with constant query complexity.

Theorem 1
.

There exist PCPs for
\mathsf{NEXP}
of exponential length with a non-adaptive verifier that reads
O(1)
bits of the proof, which are perfect zero-knowledge against any efficient adversary.

Our second result “scales down” the above to obtain polynomial-size non-adaptive PZK-PCPs for
\mathsf{NP}
with constant query complexity.

However, the strategy above strongly relies on the

of the ZK-IPCP. Indeed, observe that if we were to “unroll” this interaction into a PCP, we would simply write down all of
\hat{A}

1

without

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\{0,1\}^{r}\\

\hat{A}

\{0,1\}^{s}

\sum_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\{0,1\}^{r}\\

since the product expression “zeroes out” any term of the sum for which some
a_{i}\neq\hat{A}(b_{i})

Next, observe that for any

\mathbb{F}

2

\prod_{i=1}^{3}(\hat{A}(b_{i})-(1-a_{i}))=\prod_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\left(C(b_{i},c)-\frac{1-a_{i}}{2^{k}}\right)=\sum_{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(C(b_{i},c_{i})-\frac{1-a_{i}}{2^{k}}\right)~.

Taken together, we see that checking (
1

\sum_{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\sum_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\{0,1\}^{r}\\

which can be proven in zero knowledge using the
[
GOS24
]
ZK-PCPP for sumcheck. In particular, the PCPP simulator requires only polynomially many evaluations of the summand to simulate polynomially many queries to the proof. By setting
k=\omega(\log n)
we can simulate those evaluations by lazily simulating
C
as a uniformly random polynomial (via an algorithm of
[
BCFGRS17
]
), and then evaluating the summand directly. The query complexity of the verifier is
\mathrm{poly}(n)
.

Our result for
\mathsf{NP}
is obtained in a similar way, scaling the parameters appropriately. In particular, for a given adversary query bound
q^{*}
, we can set
k=O(\log q^{*})
. The query complexity of the verifier is then
\mathrm{poly}(\log n,\log q^{*})
. We also point out that the honest prover in our construction for
\mathsf{NP}
is efficient, given a valid witness as input.

Proof composition and zero knowledge.
In order to obtain constant query ZK-PCPs for
\mathsf{NP}
, we would like to apply the proof composition paradigm
[
BGHSV06
]
to our ZK-PCPs. This involves composing a robust outer PCP with an inner PCP of proximity to obtain a PCP which inherits the randomness complexity of the former and query complexity of the latter. To do this, we first need to strengthen our ZK-PCPs to satisfy
robust
soundness (i.e., the local view of the verifier must be far from an accepting view, with high probability). Then we show that proof composition
preserves
the zero knowledge of the outer PCP. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first composition theorem for ZK-PCPs.

Our first step is to obtain robust ZK-PCPs for
\mathsf{NP}
with polylogarithmic query complexity. While the PZK-PCP of
[
GOS24
]
(upon which our PZK-PCP builds) is an algebraic construction, it does
not
have constant robust soundness as written. We present a modification of the
[
GOS24
]
PCP for sumcheck, following the “query bundling” approach of
[
BGHSV06
]
, that has constant robust soundness.

The key challenge is to show that this modification preserves zero knowledge. In fact, we will show that a much more general class of “local” transformations preserve zero knowledge. This class includes not only query bundling, but also the subsequent steps of alphabet reduction and proof composition. To capture this class formally, we define a new notion,
locally computable proofs
.

Definition 2

(Locally computable algorithms (informal; see
Definition

3.1
))
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{0}
be randomized algorithms. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
\ell
-locally computable
from
\mathcal{A}_{0}
if there exists an efficient, deterministic oracle algorithm
f
making at most
\ell
queries to its oracle such that, for every input
x
, the following two distributions are identically distributed:

\mathcal{A}(x),\quad(f^{\pi_{0}}~|~\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{A}_{0}(x)).

We show that if the PCP prover algorithm
\mathcal{P}
is
\ell
-locally computable from a zero-knowledge PCP prover
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, then provided
\ell
is asymptotically smaller than the query bound on the ZK-PCP,
\mathcal{P}
inherits the zero knowledge guarantee of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Intuitively, this is true because if a proof
\pi
is locally computable from a proof
\pi_{0}
, and
\pi_{0}
is zero knowledge, then we can apply
f
to the simulator for
\pi_{0}
to obtain a simulator for
\pi
. This notion is surprisingly versatile, and allows us to prove zero knowledge in an array of distinct settings:

•

Robustification of
[
GOS24
]

We show that our modified

GOS24

Alphabet reduction.
Recall that alphabet reduction allows us to transform a robust PCP over a large alphabet into a boolean PCP, while maintaining robustness. It is performed by encoding each symbol of the PCP with a good error correcting code. More formally, if
\pi
is a distribution over robust PCP proofs over the alphabet
\{0,1\}^{a}
for some
a\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathsf{ECC}\colon\{0,1\}^{a}\to\{0,1\}^{b}
is a systematic binary error-correcting code of constant relative distance and rate, then we can obtain a robust boolean PCP by defining a new proof
\tau(\alpha)\ {:=}\ \mathsf{ECC}(\pi(\alpha))
for every proof index
\alpha
, and writing
\tau
over the alphabet
\{0,1\}
. Then
\tau
is
1
-locally computable from
\pi
by the following function:

f^{\pi}(\alpha,i)=\mathsf{ECC}(\pi(\alpha))_{i},

where
i\in[b]
. We note that prior work on alphabet reduction for ZKPCPs
[
HVW22
]
required a much more complex construction because their ZKPCP achieves only a quadratic gap between the honest verifier’s query complexity and the query bound.

•

Proof composition.
Recall that composition of an outer PCP system
(\mathcal{P}_{\text{out}},\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}})
for
\mathcal{L}
with an inner PCP of proximity
(\mathcal{P}_{\text{in}},\mathcal{V}_{\text{in}})
for circuit evaluation proceeds as follows. Let
\pi_{\text{out}}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{\text{out}}
. For every choice
r\in\{0,1\}^{r_{\text{out}}}
of
\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}}

\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}}

Q(r)
and an “inner proof”
\pi_{r}\ {:=}\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{in}}(\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}},\pi_{\text{out}}|_{Q(r)})

\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}}
performed its verification of
\pi_{\text{out}}
using randomness

\ell_{\text{out}}

\pi_{\text{out}}

\ell_{\text{out}}
is the query complexity of
\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}}

(\pi_{\text{out}},(\pi_{r})_{r\in\{0,1\}^{r_{\text{out}}}})

Hence the composed proof is
\ell_{\text{out}}
-locally computable from
\pi_{\text{out}}

f^{\pi_{\text{out}}}(\mathcal{O},i)=\begin{cases}\pi_{\text{out}}(i)~&\text{if}~\mathcal{O}=\pi_{\text{out}}\\
\mathcal{P}_{\text{in}}(\mathcal{V}_{\text{out}},\pi_{\text{out}}|_{Q(r)})_{i}~&\text{if}~\mathcal{O}=\pi_{r},~\text{for some}~r\in\{0,1\}^{r_{\text{out}}}\end{cases}.

Note that the composed PCP is locally computable from the outer PCP, so only the outer PCP needs to be zero knowledge to ensure the composed PCP is zero knowledge. Therefore we can employ the existing (non-ZK) PCP of proximity for circuit evaluation of
[
BGHSV06
]
as the inner PCPP.

1.2
Open problems

This work shows that for any polynomial
q^{*}
, any language in NP has a polynomial-sized proof that can be probabilistically checked by probing only
O(1)
bits, but where any set of
q^{*}
bits carries no information about the witness. This can be viewed a zero-knowledge PCP theorem that matches parameters of the original PCP theorem
[
AS98
,
ALMSS92
]
. Since then, stronger versions of the PCP theorem have been shown. It is tempting to ask whether zero-knowledge PCPs can match the strongest constructions of standard PCPs.

In particular, one of the most immediate open questions is whether it is possible to obtain ZK-PCPs with nearly-linear length. Optimising the proof length of PCPs received much attention for decades after the first proof of the PCP theorem, where the current state of the art achieves quasilinear proof length
[
BS08
,
Din07
]
. We ask whether the same can be obtained for zero-knowledge PCPs.

Open Problem 1

(Short ZK-PCPs)
.

Do there exist
O(1)
-query ZK-PCPs for
\mathsf{NP}
with proof length
\tilde{O}(n)
?

We remark that our algebraic zero-knowledge techniques are based on Reed-Muller arithmetisation and the sumcheck protocol, whereas (non-ZK) constructions of quasilinear length PCPs are based on Reed-Solomon arithmetisation and combinatorial gap amplification, hence new ideas are necessary for such strengthening of our theorem.

Even more ambitiously, one could ask whether it is possible to transform any construction of a (non-ZK) PCP to a ZK-PCP while preserving its parameters.

Open Problem 2

(PCP to ZK-PCP transformation)
.

Is there a black-box transformation that imbues a PCP construction with zero knowledge?

We note that prior to
[
GOS24
]
, all works on ZK-PCPs (see below) followed this approach, but those transformations either introduce adaptivity or only achieve weak ZK guarantees, and none preserve query complexity. Similiar transformations are known, e.g., for multi-prover interactive proofs
[
BGKW88
]
and their quantum analogues
[
GSY19
,
MS24
]
whereas in other models, such as in zero-knowledge streaming interactive proofs
[
CDGH23
]
, we have a zero knowledge sumcheck protocol but no generic transformation is known.

We remark that our techniques make whitebox use of the structure of the
[
BFLS91
]
PCP, and rely strongly on the
[
GOS24
]
ZK-PCP for sumcheck; hence, obtaining a generic transformation would require new ideas.

The quantum PCP conjecture.
Finally, we highlight a connection between zero-knowledge PCPs and one of the most imporant open problems in quantum complexity theory: the quantum PCP (QPCP) conjecture.

Most classical constructions of PCPs rely on an encoding of the
\mathsf{NP}
witness via a locally-testable and (relaxed) locally-decodable code
[
BGHSV06
,
GRR20
]
. Even though there is growing evidence that good quantum LTCs may exist
[
AE15
,
LLZ22
,
ABN23
,
DLV24
]
, quantum codes
cannot
be locally decodable due to the no-cloning theorem. This is one of the main barriers towards applying algebraic and coding-theoretic techniques to QPCPs.

Quantum codes are fundamentally tied to zero knowledge. It is a well-known fact that the erasure of a subset of qubits of a codeword is correctable if and only if the reduced density matrix on that subset is independent of the encoded state. In other words, roughly speaking, a quantum code has good distance if and only if it satisfies a quantum analogue of the PZK property for PCPs. Thus PZK-PCPs are perhaps the closest classical analogue of QPCPs, and studying them may help to shed light on the QPCP conjecture.

1.3
Related work

The first zero-knowledge PCPs appeared in the work of Kilian, Petrank and Tardos
[
KPT97
]
. Later works
[
IMS12
,
ISVW13
,
IW14
,
IMSX15
]
simplified this construction, and extended it to PCPs of proximity and the closely related notion of zero-knowledge locally testable codes (LTCs). These constructions rely on an adaptive honest verifier, and hence it is unclear how to use proof composition to improve their parameters.
[
IMS12
,
IMSX15
]
showed that PCPs which are zero knowledge against any efficient adversary and where the proof oracle is described by a polynomial-sized circuit exist only for languages in
\mathsf{SZK}
.

Another line of work, motivated by cryptographic applications, focuses on obtaining SZK-PCPs for NP with a
non-adaptive
honest verifier from leakage resilience. These results come with caveats, achieving either a weaker notion of zero knowledge known as
witness indistinguishability

[
IWY16
]
, or simulation against adversaries making only quadratically many more queries than the honest verifier
[
HVW22
]
. See
[
Wei22
]
for a survey of this line of work.

In related models that allow for interaction, zero-knowledge proofs are easier to construct. We know that
\mathsf{PZK\text{-}MIP}=\mathsf{MIP}
(
=\mathsf{NEXP}
)
[
BGKW88
]
, where
\mathsf{MIP}
is the class of languages with a multi-prover interactive proofs. The quantum analogue of this result,
\mathsf{PZK\text{-}MIP}^{*}=\mathsf{MIP}^{*}
(
=\mathsf{RE}
), is also known to hold
[
CFGS22
,
GSY19
,
MS24
]
. The constructions in this work draw inspiration from a similar result for interactive PCPs (IPCPs)
[
KR08
]
, an interactive generalisation of PCPs (and special case of IOPs):
\mathsf{PZK\text{-}IPCP}=\mathsf{IPCP}=\mathsf{NEXP}

[

,

]
.

2.1
Coding theory

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. A
code

C
is a subset
C\subseteq\Sigma^{\ell}
. Given two strings
x,y\in\Sigma^{n}
, we denote the relative Hamming distance between
x
and
y
by
\Delta(x,y)\ {:=}\ \left|\{i\in[n]:x_{i}\neq y_{i}\}\right|/n
. We say that a vector
x
is
\varepsilon
-far from a set
S\subseteq\Sigma^{n}
if
\min_{y\in S}\Delta(x,y)\geq\varepsilon
.

Reed–Muller codes.
The Reed–Muller (RM) code is the code consisting of evaluations of multivariate low-degree polynomials over a finite field. Given a finite field
\mathbb{F}
, and positive integers
m
and
d
we denote by
\mathrm{RM}[\mathbb{F},m,d]
the linear code consisting of evaluations of
m
-variate polynomials over
\mathbb{F}
of total degree at most
d
.

2.2
PCPs

Definition 2.1

(PCP)
.

A
probabilistically checkable proof (PCP)
for a relation
\mathcal{R}
consists of a prover
\mathcal{P}
and a PPT verifier
\mathcal{V}
such that the following holds.

1.

Completeness.
For every
(x,w)\in\mathcal{R}
,

\Pr_{\pi\leftarrow\mathcal{P}(x,w)}[\mathcal{V}^{\pi}(x)=1]=1.

2.

Soundness.
For every
x\notin\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{R})
and every oracle
\pi^{*}
,

\Pr[\mathcal{V}^{\pi^{*}}(x)=1]\leq\frac{1}{2}.

We say that a PCP is
efficient
if
\mathcal{P}(x,w)
can be computed efficiently for any
(x,w)\in\mathcal{R}
.

We formally define PCPs for relations (rather than langauges) to allow us to discuss efficient PCP provers in the
\mathsf{NP}
setting.

Definition 2.2

(PCPP)
.

For
\delta\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,1]
, a
probabilistically checkable proof of proximity (PCPP)
for a pair language
\mathcal{L}
with proximity parameter
\delta
consists of a prover
\mathcal{P}
and a verifier
\mathcal{V}
such that the following holds for every pair of strings
(x,y)
.

1.

Completeness.
If
(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}
,

\Pr_{\pi\leftarrow\mathcal{P}(x,y)}[\mathcal{V}^{(y,\pi)}(x)=1]=1.

2.

Soundness.
If
y
is
\delta
-far from the set
\mathcal{L}[x]\ {:=}\ \{y:(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}\}
, then for every oracle
\pi^{*}
,

\Pr[\mathcal{V}^{(y,\pi^{*})}(x)=1]\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Definition 2.3

(Non-adaptive PCP verifiers)
.

A
non-adaptive PCP verifier
is an algorithm of the form
\mathcal{V}^{\pi}(x;\mu)=D(x,\pi|_{Q(x;\mu)};\mu)
, where
D\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\times\Sigma^{q}\times\{0,1\}^{r(n)}\to\{0,1\}
is the
decision algorithm
, and
Q\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{r(n)}\to\text{Dom}(\pi)^{q}
is the
query algorithm
.

Definition 2.4

(Accepting view of a PCP verifier)
.

For non-adaptive
q
-query PCP verifiers of the form
\mathcal{V}^{\pi}(x;\mu)=D(x,\pi|_{Q(x;\mu)};\mu)
, where
D\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\times\Sigma^{q}\times\{0,1\}^{r(n)}\to\{0,1\}
, and
Q\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{r(n)}\to\text{Dom}(\pi)^{q}
, we define, for each choice of randomness
\mu\in\{0,1\}^{r(n)}
the
set of accepting views of
\mathcal{V}
to be:

\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x;\mu))\ {:=}\ \{a\in\Sigma^{q}:D(x,a;\mu)=1\}.

Definition 2.5

(Accepting view of a PCP of proximity verifier)
.

For non-adaptive
q
-query PCP of proximity verifiers of the form
\mathcal{V}^{(y,\pi)}(x;\mu)=D(x,(y,\pi)|_{Q(x;\mu)};\mu)
, where
D\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\times\Sigma^{q}\times\{0,1\}^{r(n)}\to\{0,1\}
, and
Q\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{r(n)}\to\text{Dom}((y,\pi))^{q}
, we define, for each choice of randomness
\mu\in\{0,1\}^{r(n)}
, the
set of accepting views of
\mathcal{V}
to be:

\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x;\mu))\ {:=}\ \{a\in\Sigma^{q}:D(x,a;\mu)=1\}.

In both of the above definitions when it is clear from context we will omit
\mu
and write
\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x))
.

Definition 2.6

(Robust soundness)
.

A non-adaptive PCP for a relation
\mathcal{R}
has
robustness
\rho
if for every
x\notin\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{R})
, it holds that, for every oracle
\pi^{*}
,

\Pr_{\mu}\left[\Delta(\pi^{*}|_{Q(x)},\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x;\mu)))\leq\rho\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}.

A non-adpative PCP of proximity for a language
\mathcal{L}
has
robustness
\rho
if for every
(x,y)
such that
y
is
\delta
-far from
\mathcal{L}[x]
, it holds that, for every oracle
\pi^{*}
,

\Pr_{\mu}\left[\Delta((y,\pi^{*})|_{Q(x)},\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x;\mu)))\leq\rho\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Definition 2.7
.

For
\rho\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,1]
, a PCP has
expected robustness
\rho
if for every
x\notin\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{R})
, we have for every oracle
\pi^{*}

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu}\left[\Delta(\pi^{*}|_{Q(x)},\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x;\mu)))\right]\geq\rho(|x|).

A PCP of proximity has
expected robustness

if for every
(x,y)
such that
y
is
\delta
-far from
\mathcal{L}[x]
, it holds that, for every oracle
\pi^{*}
,

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu}\left[\Delta((y,\pi^{*})|_{Q(x)},\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}(x;\mu)))\right]\geq\rho(|x|).

The following proposition relating robustness and expected robustness appears as Proposition 2.10 in
[
BGHSV06
]
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
BGHSV06
]
)
.

If a PCPP has expected robustness

2.3
Zero-knowledge PCPs

Definition 2.9

(View)
.

For a PCP
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
and a (possibly malicious) verifier
\mathcal{V}^{*}

\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{P}}(x,w)
denotes the view of
\mathcal{V}^{*}
with input

\pi\leftarrow\mathcal{P}(x,w)

\mathcal{V}^{*}

\mathcal{V}^{*}

\pi

Definition 2.10

(Perfect zero-knowledge PCP)
.

We say that a PCP system

for a relation

is

q^{*}
-query-bounded verifier

\mathsf{Sim}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
(the simulator), such that for every
(x,w)\in\mathcal{R}
,
\mathsf{Sim}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}(x)
is distributed identically to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{P}}(x,w)

All of our constructions satisfy the stronger notion of

Definition 2.11

(Black-box perfect zero-knowledge PCP)
.

We say that a PCP system
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
for a relation
\mathcal{R}
is

if there exists a PPT algorithm
\mathsf{Sim}
(the simulator), such that for every (possibly malicious) adaptive
q^{*}
-query-bounded verifier
\mathcal{V}^{*}
, and for every
(x,w)\in\mathcal{R}
,
\mathsf{Sim}^{\mathcal{V}^{*}}(x)
is distributed identically to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{P}}(x,w)

We define the complexity class
\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}
and its robust version.

Definition 2.12

(
\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP},\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}
)
.

For functions

, we define the complexity class
\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{q^{*},\Sigma}[r(n),q(n)]
to be the set of all languages that admit perfect zero-knowledge PCPs written over the alphabet
\Sigma
with randomness complexity

and query bound

.

We define the complexity class

\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}[r,q]\ {:=}\ \bigcap_{q^{*}}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{q^{*},\{0,1\}}[r+\log q^{*},q]

where the intersection ranges over all functions

that are bounded by

for some polynomial

.

That is,
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}[r,q]
if for any query bound

(that is at most exponential in

),
\mathcal{L}
has a perfect zero-knowledge PCP of length

over the boolean alphabet with query complexity

.

We also define zero-knowledge PCPs of proximity.

(View of PCP of proximity)

For a PCP of proximity

\mathcal{V}^{*}

\mathcal{V}^{*}
’s queries to
(y,\pi)
.

(Black-box perfect zero-knowledge PCP of proximity)
.

We say that a PCP of proximity system

for a pair language
\mathcal{L}
is

if there exists an algorithm

-query-bounded verifier

3
Zero-knowledge proof composition

In this section, we will prove that PCP proof composition (as in
[
BGHSV06
]
) preserves zero knowledge. Towards this end, we introduce the notion of
locally computable proofs
, which are proofs in which each symbol can be deterministically and efficiently computed from a local view of another proof. For example, in proof composition, the each inner proof is locally computable from the outer (zero-knowledge) proof.

In
Section

3.1
, we formally define locally computable proofs and show that if a PCP is locally computable from a PZK-PCP, then the locally computable PCP also exhibits perfect zero knowledge. In
Section

3.2
, we show how to convert a PZK-PCP over an arbitrary alphabet into a boolean PZK-PCP. In
Section

3.3
, we show that proof composition preserves perfect zero knowledge. Specifically, if the outer PCP of the composition is perfect zero knowledge, then the composed PCP will also be perfect zero knowledge.

3.1
Locally computable proofs

We introduce the notion of
\ell
-locally computable proofs. This is a new notion that we consider to be of independent interest and will be useful towards showing that query bundling, alphabet reduction and proof composition preserve zero knowledge.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{0}
be randomized algorithms, and let
\ell\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
\ell
-locally computable
from
\mathcal{A}_{0}
on
C\subseteq\{0,1\}^{*}
if there exists an efficient, deterministic oracle algorithm
f
making at most
\ell
queries to its oracle such that, for every
x\in C
, the following two distributions are identical:

\mathcal{A}(x);\qquad(f^{\pi_{0}}~|~\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{A}_{0}(x)).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
be a PZK-PCP for a relation
\mathcal{R}
with query bound
q^{*}
, and let
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
be a PCP for
\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathcal{P}
is
\ell
-locally computable from
\mathcal{P}_{0}
on
\mathcal{L}
. Then
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
is perfect zero knowledge with query bound
q^{*}/\ell
.

While we state and prove
Lemma

3.2
for the case of PCPs, the analysis straightforwardly extends to the case of PCPs of proximity.

The intuition behind
Lemma

3.2
is that if
\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{0}
is zero knowledge, then since any local view of
\pi_{0}
is efficiently simulatable and
\pi\leftarrow\mathcal{P}
is computable from a local view of
\pi_{0}
, we can combine these two functionalities to obtain a simulator for
\mathcal{P}
. More formally, let
\mathcal{V}^{*}
be a (possibly adaptive) malicious verifier of
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
. Our goal is to construct a simulator for the view of
\mathcal{V}^{*}
and our strategy for doing so consists of two parts. In
Construction

3.3
, we construct a hybrid simulator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
for
\mathcal{V}^{*}
, which is given oracle access to the (zero-knowledge) proof
\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{0}
. This hybrid simulator uses the local computability of
\pi\leftarrow\mathcal{P}
and its oracle access to
\pi_{0}
to simulate
\mathcal{V}^{*}
’s view of
\pi
.
Construction

3.4
is our simulator for
\mathcal{V}^{*}
. As
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
is itself a malicious verifier of the proof
\pi_{0}
, we can invoke the zero-knowledge property of
\pi_{0}
to simulate the view of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
. Our simulator for
\mathcal{V}^{*}
works by running
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
and answering its queries to
\pi_{0}
using the simulator for
\pi_{0}
.

Construction 3.3
.

A hybrid simulator for any malicious verifier
\mathcal{V}^{*}
of the PCP
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
, a PCP which is
\ell
-locally computable from
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
by a function
f
. Receives oracle access to
\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{0}
.

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}^{\pi_{0}}(x,r)
:

1.

Initialise an empty list
T
.

2.

Run
\mathcal{V}^{*}
on random coins
r
. Every time
\mathcal{V}^{*}
makes a query
\alpha
to
\pi
, compute
\beta\ {:=}\ f^{\pi_{0}}(\alpha)
, add
(\alpha,\beta)
to
T
and feed
\beta
to
\mathcal{V}^{*}
as the response from
\pi
.

3.

Output
(r,T)
.

Construction 3.4
.

A perfect zero-knowledge simulator for a PCP system
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
which is
\ell
-locally computable from a PZK-PCP
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
. Let
\overline{\mathsf{Sim}}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
denote the simulator for
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
for a malicious verifier
\mathcal{V}^{*}
.

\mathsf{Sim}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}(x)
:

1.

Run
\overline{\mathsf{Sim}}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}}(x)
(that is, simulate the view of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
(
Construction

3.3
)) to obtain a query-answer set
T_{0}
for
\pi_{0}
and random coins
r_{0}
.

2.

Run
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}(x,r)
(

3.3
) using
T_{0}
to answer its queries to
\pi_{0}
to obtain
(r,T)
.

3.

Output
(r,T)
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.2
.

We show that
Construction

3.4
is a perfect zero-knowledge simulator for
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
. We first analyse
Construction

3.3
, and show that its output is identically distributed to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{P}}
. Then we analyse
Construction

3.4
, and show that provided
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
does not break the query bound on
\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{0}
, we can simulate its view of
\pi_{0}
. Combining these two facts yields the result.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{*}
be an arbitrary (possibly adaptive) malicious verifier of
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
, and let
\pi
and
\pi_{0}
be random variables denoting the output of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{0}
respectively. Consider the hybrid simulator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}^{\pi_{0}}
defined in
Construction

3.3
. As
\mathcal{P}
is
\ell
-locally computable by
f
, we have that
\pi
is identically distributed to
(f^{\pi_{0}}(\alpha))_{\alpha\in\text{Dom}(\pi)}
. Thus the output of
Construction

3.3
is distributed identically to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{P}}
.

Now we analyse
Construction

3.4
. As
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
is a PZK-PCP, and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
is a verifier of
\pi_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{0}
, there exists a simulator
\overline{\mathsf{Sim}}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}}
for
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}^{\pi_{0}}
, whose output is identically distributed to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}},\mathcal{P}_{0}}(x,w)
provided that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
makes at most
q^{*}
queries to
\pi_{0}
. Note that for every query made by
\mathcal{V}^{*}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}
makes at most
\ell
queries to
\pi_{0}
. Thus, the output of
\overline{\mathsf{Sim}}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}}
is identically distributed to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}},\mathcal{P}_{0}}(x,w)
provided that
\mathcal{V}^{*}
makes at most
q^{*}/\ell
queries. Therefore, as
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}(x)
is receiving answers to its queries to
\pi_{0}
identically distributed to the real proof, by the first part, its output is identically distributed to
\mathsf{View}_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{P}}
.
∎

3.2
Alphabet reduction of PZK-PCPs

In this section, we use the notion of
\ell
-locally computable proofs to show that standard alphabet reduction techniques preserve zero knowledge. In particular, we will show this for the alphabet reduction construction in
[
BGHSV06
, Lemma 2.13]
. For the sake of clarity, we include a self-contained presentation of their construction below (
Construction

3.6
).

Lemma 3.5

(Zero-knowledge alphabet reduction)
.

If a language
\mathcal{L}
has a PZK-PCP over the alphabet
\{0,1\}^{a}
, query complexity
q
, randomness complexity
r
, query bound
q^{*}
, decision complexity
d
and robust-soundness error
s
with robustness parameter
\rho
, then
\mathcal{L}
has a boolean PZK-PCP with query complexity
O(a\cdot q)
, randomness complexity
r
, query bound
q^{*}
, decision complexity
d+O(a\cdot q)
and robust-soundness error
s
with robustness parameter
\Omega(\rho)
.

Following
[
BGHSV06
]
, the construction relies on the existence of good error-correcting codes, which have constant relative distance and rate. To obtain the desired decision complexity, a code which is also computable by linear-sized circuits is used, such as
[
Spi96
]
.

Construction 3.6
.

A boolean PZK-PCP for the language
\mathcal{L}
, given a PZK-PCP
(\mathcal{P}_{a},\mathcal{V}_{a})
over the alphabet
\{0,1\}^{a}
for
\mathcal{L}
, where the verifier
\mathcal{V}_{a}
has decision algorithm
D_{a}
and query algorithm
Q_{a}
. Let
\mathsf{ECC}\colon\{0,1\}^{a}\to\{0,1\}^{b}
where
b=O(a)
be a systematic binary error-correcting code of constant relative minimum distance, computable by an explicit circuit of size
O(a)
.

Proof:

1.

Run
\mathcal{P}_{a}(x)
to obtain a proof
\pi_{a}
.

2.

Define the proof oracle
\tau
, by
\tau(\gamma)\ {:=}\ \mathsf{ECC}(\pi_{a}(\gamma))
, for all
\gamma\in\text{Dom}(\pi_{a})
.

3.

Output
(\pi_{a},\tau
).

Verifier:

1.

Compute the query set
Q\ {:=}\ Q_{a}(x)\subseteq\text{Dom}(\pi_{a})
.

2.

Run
D_{a}(x,\pi_{a}|_{Q})
and reject if it rejects.

3.

For each
\gamma\in Q
, compute
\mathsf{ECC}(\pi_{a}(\gamma))
, query
\tau(\gamma)
and reject if
\mathsf{ECC}(\pi_{a}(\gamma))\neq\tau(\gamma)
.

4.

If all of the above checks pass, then accept.

Proof of
Lemma

3.5
.

As
Construction

3.6
is entirely unchanged from
[
BGHSV06
]
, a proof of completeness, soundness and all of the parameters (apart from the query bound) can be found in Lemma 2.13 of
[
BGHSV06
]
. Here we argue zero knowledge.

We show zero knowledge by demonstrating that
\mathcal{P}
, as defined in
Construction

3.6
, is
1
-locally computable from the PZK-PCP
\mathcal{P}_{a}
, which has query bound
q^{*}
. Hence, by
Lemma

3.2
, the PCP
\mathcal{P}
inherits its zero-knowledge property from
\mathcal{P}_{a}
. Towards this end, observe that
\mathcal{P}
is
1
-locally computable by the function
f\colon\text{Dom}(\pi)\to\{0,1\}^{b}
, defined as

4
Robust PCPPs for polynomial summation

In this section, we construct a PCP of proximity for the
\mathsf{Sum}
pair language, defined below, which achieves constant

Definition 4.1
.

We define the language
\mathsf{Sum}
to be the set of all pairs
((\mathbb{F},1^{m},1^{d},H,\gamma),F)

where
\mathbb{F}
is a finite field,
m,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}
, and

F\in\mathrm{RM}[\mathbb{F},m,d]
with
\sum_{\vec{b}\in H^{m}}F(\vec{b})=\gamma
.

Note that
d
and

are specified in unary, so that the verifier and simulator for our PCPP can run in time
\mathrm{poly}(\log|\mathbb{F}|,m,d,|H|)
. Below, for readability, we will write explicit inputs of
\mathsf{Sum}
as
(\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma)
; this should be understood as
(\mathbb{F},1^{m},1^{d},H,\gamma)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field, and let
m,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}
and
\delta>0
be such that
\delta>\frac{md}{|\mathbb{F}|}
and
d\geq|H|+1
. Then
Construction

4.3
is a robust PCP of proximity for
\mathsf{Sum}
, over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
, with proximity parameter
\delta
and robustness parameter
\rho=\Omega(\delta)
. The verifier makes
O(|\mathbb{F}|)
-many queries to
F
and
|\mathbb{F}|
-many queries to
\pi
. The proof length is
|\mathbb{F}|^{m-1}
.

While the robust PCP of
[
BGHSV06
]
uses (a variant of) sumcheck, to the best of our knowledge
Lemma

4.2
has not previously been shown explicitly.
Our proof here is substantially simpler than that of
[
BGHSV06
]
, in part because the structure of our PCP permits a simpler “bundling” of symbols. We will build on this construction when constructing robust PZK-PCPPs for
\mathsf{Sum}
, in
Section

5
.

Construction 4.3
.

A robust PCP of proximity
\mathrm{RSC}
for
\mathsf{Sum}
with proximity parameter
\delta>0
over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
. Both parties receive the explicit input
(\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma,\delta)
, and oracle access to the evaluation table of the implicit input
F\colon\mathbb{F}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}
.

Proof:

1.

For each
i\in[m-1]
, define the
i
-th sumcheck polynomial to be the
i
-variate polynomial
g_{i}(\vec{X})\in\mathbb{F}^{\leq d}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{i}]
given by

g_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})\ {:=}\ \sum_{\vec{b}\in H^{m-i}}F(x_{1},\dots,x_{i},\vec{b}).

2.

Define
\pi\colon\mathbb{F}^{m-1}\to\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
by

\pi(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)\ {:=}\ (g_{1}(\alpha),g_{2}(c_{1},\alpha),\dots,g_{m-1}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)),

for every
(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)\in\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
.

3.

Output
\pi
.

Verifier:

1.

Sample
\vec{c}\ {:=}\ (c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1})\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
uniformly at random.

2.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
, query
\pi(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)\ {:=}\ (g_{1}(\alpha),g_{2}(c_{1},\alpha),\dots,g_{m-1}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha))
.

3.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
, query
F(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1},\alpha)
.

4.

For each
i\in[m-1]
, reject if
g_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},X)
is not a polynomial of degree at most
d
.

5.

Check that

\sum_{b\in H}g_{1}(b)=\gamma.

6.

For each
i\in[m-2]
, check that

\sum_{b\in H}g_{i+1}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i},b)=g_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i}).

7.

Check that

\sum_{b\in H}F(\vec{c},b)=g_{m-1}(\vec{c}).

8.

Perform a robust low total degree test (
Theorem

2.16
) on
F
, with proximity parameter
\delta_{\text{RM}}=\min(\delta,1/5)
, and reject if the test fails.

9.

If none of the above checks fail, then accept.

We now prove that
Construction

4.3
is robust, but first outline our strategy for doing so. Conceptually speaking,
Construction

4.3
consists of two tests: the sumcheck tests in
Steps

4
to
7
(which includes a check that the proof itself has low-degree structure), and the low-degree test on the input oracle in
Step

8
. The low-degree test is indeed robust (by
Theorem

2.16
), so the key technical component in proving the robustness of
Construction

4.3
is
Lemma

4.4
, which asserts that the sumcheck tests (
Steps

4
to
7
) reject robustly, under the assumption the input oracle is close to a low-degree polynomial. Since the verifier makes roughly the same number of queries when performing both of these tests, this implies the overall construction is robust.

Lemma

4.4
holds due to the assumed low-degree structure of
F
and our specific choice of bundling. If the view of the sumcheck verifier is rejecting, then, since both the proof
\pi
and the input oracle
F
are close to low degree, at least one of
F,g_{1},\dots,g_{m-1}
needs to be modified to equal a different polynomial. By the Schwartz-Zippel lemma, this requires a substantial fraction of modifications. Our bundling strategy ensures that these modifications form a substantial fraction of the view of the verifier. We now proceed with a formal proof.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\tilde{F}\colon\mathbb{F}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}
be
\delta_{\Sigma}
-far from
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]
, but
\delta_{\text{RM}}
-close to
\mathrm{RM}[\mathbb{F},m,d]
for some
\frac{md}{|\mathbb{F}|}<\delta_{\text{RM}}\leq\delta_{\Sigma}
and
d\geq|H|+1
. Then for all proofs
\pi^{*}
,

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta\left((\pi^{*}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\tilde{F}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}}),~\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V})\right)\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}\min\left(\delta_{\text{RM}},1-4\delta_{\text{RM}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\hat{F}\in\mathbb{F}^{\leq d}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
be the some polynomial of total degree at most
d
which is
\delta_{\text{RM}}
-close to
\tilde{F}
, as guaranteed by the hypothesis. Note that by the condition that
\tilde{F}
is
\delta_{\Sigma}
-far from
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]
and
\delta_{\text{RM}}\leq\delta_{\Sigma}
, it must be that
\sum_{\vec{b}\in H^{m}}\hat{F}(\vec{b})\neq\gamma
. Before proceeding, we remind the reader of some notation: for any
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1})\in\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
, and any
i\in[m-1]
, we denote
\vec{c}_{i}\ {:=}\ (c_{1},\dots,c_{i})
. Also, for a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}^{\leq d}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{i}]
, we denote the restriction of
f
to the axis-parallel line defined by
\vec{c}_{i-1}
by
f|_{\vec{c}_{i-1}}(X)\ {:=}\ f(\vec{c}_{i-1},X)
.

First, fix some
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1})\in\mathbb{F}^{m-1}
, and suppose that for some
i\in[m-1]
,
g_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},X)
is
\delta_{\text{RM}}
-far from every univariate polynomial of degree at most
d
. Then since the verifier inspects each
g_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},X)
at every point in
\mathbb{F}
and rejects if
g_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},X)
is not a polynomial of degree at most
d
(
Step

4
), at least a
\delta_{\text{RM}}
-fraction of symbols of
\pi^{*}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}}
would need to be modified in order for the verifier to accept. Thus
(\pi^{*}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\tilde{F}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}})
has distance at least
\delta_{\text{RM}}/2
to any accepting view.

Hence we can proceed under the assumption that for all
i\in[m-1]
,
g_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},X)
is
\delta_{\text{RM}}
-close to some univariate polynomial
\hat{g}_{i}(X)\in\mathbb{F}^{\leq d}[X]
. Let
E
be the event that either

\sum_{b\in H}\hat{g}_{1}(b)\neq\gamma,

or

\sum_{b\in H}\hat{g}_{i+1}(b)\neq\hat{g}_{i}(c_{i})

for some
i\in[m-2]
, or

\sum_{b\in H}\hat{F}(\vec{c},b)\neq\hat{g}_{m-1}(c_{m-1}).

Note that since
\sum_{\vec{b}\in H^{m}}\hat{F}(\vec{b})\neq\gamma
, by the soundness of sumcheck,
\Pr_{\vec{c}}[\neg E]\leq md/|\mathbb{F}|
.

Conditioned on
E
, in order for the verifier to accept, one of
g_{1}(X),g_{2}|_{\vec{c}_{1}}(X),\dots,g_{m-1}|_{\vec{c}_{m-2}}(X),\tilde{F}|_{\vec{c}}(X)
must be modified to equal a degree
d
polynomial other than
\hat{g}_{1}(X),\hat{g}_{2}|_{\vec{c}_{1}}(X),\dots,\hat{g}_{m-1}|_{\vec{c}_{m-2}}(X),\hat{F}|_{\vec{c}}(X)
respectively. This would require modifying
g_{i}|_{\vec{c}_{i-1}}(X)
, for some
i\in[m-1]
, in at least a
1-d/|\mathbb{F}|-\Delta(g_{i}|_{\vec{c}_{i-1}},\hat{g}_{i}|_{\vec{c}_{i-1}})\geq 1-d/|\mathbb{F}|-\delta_{\text{RM}}
fraction of points, or modifying
\tilde{F}_{\vec{c}}(X)
in at least a
1-d/|\mathbb{F}|-\Delta(\tilde{F}|_{\vec{c}},\hat{F}|_{\vec{c}})
fraction of points. Thus, the verifier’s view of the proof would require modification in at least a
\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{d}{|\mathbb{F}|}-\max(\delta_{\text{RM}},\Delta(\tilde{F}|_{\vec{c}},\hat{F}|_{\vec{c}})))
fraction of points.

Thus the expected distance of
(\pi^{*}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\tilde{F}(c_{1},\dots,c_{m-1},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}})
to an accepting view is at least

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[1-\frac{d}{|\mathbb{F}|}-\max(\delta_{\text{RM}},\Delta(\tilde{F}|_{\vec{c}},\hat{F}|_{\vec{c}}))\right]-\Pr_{\vec{c}}[\neg E]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{d}{|\mathbb{F}|}-\delta_{\text{RM}}\right)-\Pr_{\vec{c}}[\neg E]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{d}{|\mathbb{F}|}-\delta_{\text{RM}}\right)-\frac{md}{|\mathbb{F}|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-4\delta_{\text{RM}}),

where we have used that

\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\tilde{F}|_{\vec{c}},\hat{F}|_{\vec{c}}))]\leq\delta_{\text{RM}}
and that

\frac{md}{|\mathbb{F}|}\leq\delta_{\text{RM}}
by hypothesis.
∎

Now we employ
Lemma

4.4
to prove that
Construction

4.3
is robust, following the approach we outlined above.

Proof of
Lemma

4.2
.

Completeness is straightforward; we prove robust soundness and efficiency.

Robust soundness.
Suppose that
\tilde{F}
is
\delta
-far from
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]
. First, if
F
is
\delta_{\text{RM}}
-far from
\mathrm{RM}[\mathbb{F},m,d]
, then by
Theorem

, the expected distance of the view of the low degree test (

8
) from any accepting view is
\Omega(\delta)
(since

). As the low-degree test makes

queries to

makes
|\mathbb{F}|
queries to each of

and
\pi
, the queries made by the low-degree test form a constant fraction of the queries made overall by the verifier. Hence the expected distance of the view of the verifier from any accepting view is also
\Omega(\delta)
.

Second, if
F
is
\delta
-far from
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]
but

-close to

.

By

, for an appropriate choice of constant

,

has robustness

.

) makes

queries to

, and the rest of

makes
|\mathbb{F}|
queries to each of

and

.

verifiers regardless of their complexity).

Construction

5.2
closely follows
[
GOS24
]
, with only a few modifications to achieve constant robustness. We provide a brief high-level description of the
[
GOS24
]
construction, and then present our construction in detail. We refer the reader to
[
GOS24
]
for further exposition.

The
[
GOS24
]
ZKPCP for
\mathsf{Sum}
is a (standard) sumcheck PCPP for a “masked” version of the polynomial
F
; specifically, a sumcheck PCPP for the claim

\sum_{\vec{a}\in H^{m}}F(\vec{a})+\underbrace{Q(\vec{a})-Q(\vec{a}_{\mathsf{rev}})}_{\text{antisymmetric}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{H}(a_{i})T_{i}(\vec{a})}_{\text{nullstellensatz}}=\gamma

(2)

where

•

Q
is a uniformly random polynomial in
\mathbb{F}^{\leq\vec{d}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
;

•

Z_{H}\ {:=}\ \prod_{a\in H}(X-a)
is the nonzero univariate polynomial of minimal degree that is zero on
H
; and

•

T_{i}
, for each
i\in[m]
, is a uniformly random polynomial in
\mathbb{F}^{\leq\vec{d}_{i}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
, where
\vec{d}_{i}
taking value
d-|H|
in the
i
-th coordinate and
d
in all other coordinates.

The polynomials
Q,T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
are provided in the proof. It is not difficult to see that, for any functions
Q,T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
, (
2
) is true if and only if
\sum_{\vec{a}\in H^{m}}F(\vec{a})=\gamma
.

The mask polynomials make the PCPP zero knowledge. The purpose of the “antisymmetric” term
Q(\vec{X})-Q(\vec{X}_{\mathsf{rev}})
is to hide the values of intermediate sums in the sumcheck proof, which are
\#\mathsf{P}
-hard to compute, while summing to zero over
H^{m}
to maintain soundness.
The “nullstellensatz” term computes, by the combinatorial nullstellensatz
[
Alo99
]
, a uniformly random polynomial
N
such that
N(\vec{h})=0
for all
\vec{h}\in H
. By adding this term, we retain the antisymmetric structure of the mask on
H^{m}
(necessary for soundness) while maximising the randomness outside of
H^{m}
(necessary for simulation).

We are now ready to present our construction.

Construction 5.2
.

A robust PZK-PCP of proximity for
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]
with proximity parameter
\delta>0
over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
. Both parties receive common input
(\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma,\delta)
, and oracle access to the evaluation table of
F\colon\mathbb{F}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}

Proof:

1.

Sample a polynomial

uniformly at random, where
\vec{d}=(d,\ldots,d)

2.

For each

uniformly at random, where
\vec{d}_{i}=(d,\dots,d-|H|,\dots,d)

d

Define

\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
.

Define

(

(rather than

6.

Output

, written over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
.

Verifier:

1.

Emulate the robust sumcheck verifier
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
(
Construction

4.3
) on input
F+R
and proof
\pi_{\Sigma}
. To query the input

, query

,
\pi_{P}(\vec{\alpha})\ {:=}\ (Q(\vec{\alpha}),T_{1}(\vec{\alpha}),\dots,T_{m}(\vec{\alpha}))
and
\pi_{P}(\vec{\alpha}_{\mathsf{rev}})\ {:=}\ (Q(\vec{\alpha}_{\mathsf{rev}}),T_{1}(\vec{\alpha}_{\mathsf{rev}}),\dots,T_{m}(\vec{\alpha}_{\mathsf{rev}}))

rejects.

Perform a robust low-degree test (

2.16

3.

Perform a robust vector-valued low-degree test on

(

2.16

and degree parameter

Accept if and only if none of the above tests rejected.

To prove the robustness of our construction, we show a technical lemma (see

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\tilde{F}\colon\mathbb{F}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}
be
\delta_{\Sigma}
-far from
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]

\delta_{\text{RM}}

\mathrm{RM}[\mathbb{F},m,d]

\delta_{\Sigma}>\delta_{\text{RM}}\geq\frac{md}{|\mathbb{F}|}

\delta_{\text{RM}}<1/5

(\pi^{*}_{\Sigma},\pi^{*}_{P})
,

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta\left((\pi^{*}_{\Sigma}(\vec{c}_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\pi^{*}_{P}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\pi^{*}_{P}(\alpha,\vec{c}_{\mathsf{rev}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\tilde{F}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}}),~\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V})\right)\right]=\Omega(\delta_{\text{RM}}).

Here we view
(\pi^{*}_{\Sigma}(\vec{c}_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\pi^{*}_{P}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\pi^{*}_{P}(\alpha,\vec{c}_{\mathsf{rev}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\tilde{F}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}})\in(\mathbb{F}^{m+1})^{4\mathbb{F}}
as a string over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
, of length
4|\mathbb{F}|
, via some natural embedding of
\mathbb{F}
into
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
. The metric
\Delta
refers to Hamming distance over
\mathbb{F}^{m}
.

Proof.

First, suppose that
\pi^{*}_{P}
is
\varepsilon_{P}
-far (where
\varepsilon_{P}\ {:=}\ \frac{1}{8}\delta_{\text{RM}}
, see
Step

3
), with respect to the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
, from
\mathrm{RM}^{m+1}[\mathbb{F},m,m\cdot d]
. In this case, then by the robustness of the vector-valued low-degree test (

\Omega(\varepsilon_{P})=\Omega(\delta_{\text{RM}})
to any accepting view. Since this test forms a constant fraction of the overall view of the verifier, the expected distance of the view of the verifier from any accepting view would be
\Omega(\delta_{\text{RM}})
.

Hence we can assume that
\pi^{*}_{P}
is
\varepsilon_{P}
-close, with respect to the alphabet
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
, to some vector of

and note that by definition of

, where this distance is with respect to
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
. Lastly, we denote

’s view of the “corrected” proof

by

\hat{\nu}(\vec{c})\ {:=}\ (\pi_{\Sigma}(\vec{c}_{m-2},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\hat{F}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\\
\pi_{\hat{P}}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\pi_{\hat{P}}(\alpha,\vec{c}_{\mathsf{rev}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}})~.

We show next that the verifier’s view of the corrected proof

is far (in expectation) from any accepting view. We accomplish this step by appealing to the robustness of the sumcheck PCPP (

4.3
), and by arguing that
\mathcal{V}_{1}
inherits this robustness. Then as the view of the actual proof

is close (in expectation) to the view of the corrected proof

must be far (in expectation) from any accepting view.

By
Lemma

4.4
, if
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
has oracle access to the evaluation table of a function

, which is
\delta_{\Sigma}
-far from
\mathsf{Sum}[\mathbb{F},m,d,H,\gamma]

\pi_{\Sigma}

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta(\nu_{\Sigma}(\vec{c}),\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}))\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}\min\left(\delta_{\text{RM}},1-4\delta_{\text{RM}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\text{RM}}~,

(3)

where the final equality holds since

. Now consider the verifier
\mathcal{V}_{1}

F
and
\pi_{P}
to run
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
on input oracle

and proof
\pi_{\Sigma}

\mathcal{V}_{1}

Next, we upper bound the expected distance of

and

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta(\pi_{\hat{P}}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}},\pi_{P}(\vec{c},\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}})\right]\leq\varepsilon_{P}=\frac{1}{8}\delta_{\text{RM}}.

Since

and

are substrings of

Finally, we put everything together to obtain the result:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta\left(\nu(\vec{c}),\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}_{1})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\left|\Delta\left(\hat{\nu}(\vec{c}),\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}_{1})\right)-\Delta\left(\nu(\vec{c}),\hat{\nu}(\vec{c})\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta\left(\hat{\nu}(\vec{c}),\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal{V}_{1})\right)\right]-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\vec{c}\leftarrow\mathbb{F}^{m-1}}\left[\Delta\left(\nu(\vec{c}),\hat{\nu}(\vec{c})\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}\delta_{\text{RM}}-\frac{1}{16}\delta_{\text{RM}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\delta_{\text{RM}},

where the first inequality is a consequence of the reverse triangle inequality, and the second is due to linearity of expectation and the fact that the
\mathbb{E}|X|\geq|\mathbb{E}[X]|
for any random variable

∎

6
Robust PZK-PCPs for NP and NEXP

In this section we show how to build robust PZK-PCPs for NP and NEXP using a robust PZK-PCPP for sumcheck as a building block.

Definition 6.1

(Oracle 3-SAT)
.

Let
B\colon\{0,1\}^{r+3s+3}\to\{0,1\}
be a 3-CNF. We say that
B
is
implicitly satisfiable
if there exists
A\in\{0,1\}^{s}\to\{0,1\}
such that for all
z\in\{0,1\}^{r},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in\{0,1\}^{s}
,
B(z,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},A(b_{1}),A(b_{2}),A(b_{3}))=1
. Let
\mathsf{Oracle}\textsf{-}\mathsf{3SAT}
be the language of implicitly satisfiable 3-CNFs.

Theorem 6.2

(Cook-Levin)
.

For any
T(n)
-time nondeterministic Turing machine
M
,
T=\Omega(n)
, there is a polynomial-time reduction
R_{M}
such that for any input
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,

R_{M}(x,T)\in\mathsf{Oracle}\textsf{-}\mathsf{3SAT}\Leftrightarrow\exists w,M(x,w)=1~.

Moreover,
R_{M}(x,T)
is a formula in
O(\log T(n))
variables of size
\mathrm{poly}(n,\log T(n))
.

Theorem 6.3
.

The following inclusions hold:

(i)

for any
q^{*}\leq 2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
,
\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{q^{*},\Sigma(n)}[\log n+\log q^{*},\mathrm{poly}(\log n+\log q^{*})]
, where
|\Sigma(n)|=\mathrm{poly}(n,q^{*})
, and

(ii)

for any
q^{*}\leq 2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
,
\mathsf{NEXP}\subseteq\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{q^{*},\Sigma(n)}[\mathrm{poly}(n)+\log q^{*},\mathrm{poly}(n)]
, where
|\Sigma(n)|=\mathrm{poly}(2^{n},q^{*})
.

Proof.

Fix a language
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{NTIME}(T)
, where for
(i)
,
T=\mathrm{poly}(n)
, and for
(ii)
,
T=2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic
2
of size to be fixed,

a subfield of
\mathbb{F}
of size
\Theta(\log T)
. We will fix an arithmetisation
\hat{B}
of

over
\mathbb{F}
; i.e., for all
c\in\{0,1\}^{r+3s+3}
,
\hat{B}(c)=1-B(c)
. The PCP constructions which prove
(i)
and
(ii)
differ only in the choice of
\hat{B}
. For
(i)
, note that since
r+3s+3=O(\log n)
, the evaluation table of

is computable in polynomial time. Hence we choose
\hat{B}
to be the unique multilinear extension of
\hat{B}
; this can be evaluated in polynomial time, and the total degree
d_{B}
of
\hat{B}
is
O(\log n)
. For
(ii)
, we will take
\hat{B}
to be the function computed by the arithmetic formula corresponding to the boolean formula

; this has total degree
d_{B}=O(|B|)=\mathrm{poly}(n)
. In both cases we choose
\mathbb{F}
so that
d_{B}\ll|\mathbb{F}|=\mathrm{poly}(\log T)
.

Let
m_{1}\ {:=}\ r/\log|H|
,
m_{2}\ {:=}\ s/\log|H|
. Let
\gamma_{1}\colon H^{m_{1}}\to\{0,1\}^{r}
,
\gamma_{2}\ {:=}\ H^{m_{2}}\to\{0,1\}^{s}
be the lexicographic orderings of the elements of

respectively. By
[

, Claim 4.2]
, for

the unique minimal-degree extension
\hat{\gamma}_{i}
of
\gamma_{i}
has individual degree

and can be evaluated in time
\mathrm{poly}(|H|,m_{i},\log|\mathbb{F}|)
.

For a polynomial
\hat{A}\colon\mathbb{F}^{m_{2}}\to\mathbb{F}
, we define

g_{\hat{A}}(z,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\ {:=}\ \hat{B}(\gamma_{1}(z),\gamma_{2}(b_{1}),\gamma_{2}(b_{2}),\gamma_{2}(b_{3}),a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{3}(\hat{A}(b_{i})+a_{i}-1)~.

Next, for a polynomial
\hat{C}\colon\mathbb{F}^{m_{2}+k}\to\mathbb{F}

PZK-PCP for NP and NEXP with constant query complexity

In this section, we combine our zero-knowledge proof composition theorem and our randomness-efficient, robust PZK-PCPs for
\mathsf{NP}
and
\mathsf{NEXP}
to obtain constant-query PZK-PCPs for
\mathsf{NP}
and
\mathsf{NEXP}

Theorem 7.1
.

The following inclusions hold:

(i)

\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}[\log n,1]
, and

(ii)

\mathsf{NEXP}\subseteq\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}[\mathrm{poly}(n),1]
.

Moreover, when
q^{*}
is polynomial,
\mathsf{NP}
has efficient PZK-PCPs with constant query complexity and query bound
q^{*}
.

Proof.

Part (i).
We prove this via a chain of inclusions as follows.

We start by performing alphabet reduction on the robust PZK-PCP for
\mathsf{NP}
we constructed in
Theorem

6.3
. Let
q^{*}\leq 2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
be an arbitrary query bound; for simplicity, we will assume without loss of generality that
q^{*}\geq n
. By part (i) of
Theorem

6.3
, for any
\tilde{q}\leq 2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
,

\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{\tilde{q},\Sigma(n)}[\log n+\log\tilde{q},\mathrm{poly}(\log n+\log\tilde{q})],

where
|\Sigma(n)|=\mathrm{poly}(n,\tilde{q})
. This can be viewed as a PCP over the alphabet
\{0,1\}^{a}
, where
a\ {:=}\ \log|\Sigma(n)|=O(\log n+\log\tilde{q})
. Therefore, we can apply alphabet reduction (
Lemma

3.5
) to obtain

\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{\tilde{q},\Sigma(n)}[\log n+\log\tilde{q},\mathrm{poly}(\log n+\log\tilde{q})]\subseteq\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{\tilde{q},\{0,1\}}[\log n+\log\tilde{q},\mathrm{poly}(\log n+\log\tilde{q})].

Next, we perform proof composition to obtain constant query complexity. Let
Q(n)=\mathrm{poly}(\log n+\log\tilde{q})
be the query complexity of the boolean
\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}
and set
\tilde{q}(n)
equal to a polynomial in

and

large enough to ensure that
\tilde{q}(n)/Q(n)\geq q^{*}(n)
for all

;

suffices for any

.

3.11
,
\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{\tilde{q},\{0,1\}}[r,q]\subseteq\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{\tilde{q}/q,\{0,1\}}[r+\log n,1]
. Thus, we have that

\mathsf{r}\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{\tilde{q},\{0,1\}}[\log n+\log\tilde{q},\mathrm{poly}(\log n+\log\tilde{q})]\subseteq\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}_{q^{*},\{0,1\}}[\log n+\log q^{*},1].

was arbitrary,
\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{PZK}\text{-}\mathsf{PCP}[\log n,1]
.

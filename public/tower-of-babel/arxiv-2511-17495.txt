Classification of analytic SO o ​ ( p , q ) \text{SO}^{o}(p,q) -actions on closed ( p + q − 1 ) (p+q-1) -dimensional manifolds I : p , q ≥ 3 p,q\geq 3
Spyridon Lentas

Abstract
This paper provides a classification of analytic actions of the semi-orthogonal group SO ∘ ​ ( p , q ) \text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q) , for p , q ≥ 3 p,q\geq 3 , on closed, connected ( p + q − 1 ) (p+q-1) -dimensional manifolds. Adapting Uchida’s construction of SO ∘ ​ ( p , q ) \text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q) actions on S p + q − 1 \text{S}^{p+q-1} , we explicitly construct analytic actions of SO ∘ ​ ( p , q ) \text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q) on S p × S q − 1 \text{S}^{p}\times\text{S}^{q-1} and S p − 1 × S q \text{S}^{p-1}\times\text{S}^{q} , as well as actions on SO ∘ ​ ( p , q ) × P S 1 \text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)\times_{P}\text{S}^{1} , where P P is a maximal parabolic subgroup of SO ∘ ​ ( p , q ) \text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q) . The main result demonstrates that any analytic SO ∘ ​ ( p , q ) \text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q) action on a closed, connected ( p + q − 1 ) (p+q-1) -dimensional manifold is covered by one of the constructed actions.

Introduction

Consider the Lie group
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
, for
p,q\geq 3
. We are interested in analytic actions of
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
on closed, connected manifolds. When the dimension of the manifold is
<p+q-2

\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)

p+q-2

, where

is a maximal parabolic subgroup, which is isomorphic to the stabiliser of an isotropic line in the standard representation of
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
on
\mathbb{R}^{p+q}
with a scalar product of signature
(p,q)
. This paper contains results from the author’s PhD dissertation, see
[
8
]
. We classify analytic
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
actions on closed, connected manifolds of dimension
p+q-1
. More specifically, we get a classification up to analytic isomorphism for the analytic
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
actions on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
,
\text{S}^{p}\times\text{S}^{q-1}
,
\text{S}^{p-1}\times\text{S}^{q}
and
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)\times_{P_{\text{null}}}\text{S}^{1}
, where a maximal compact subgroup acts in a standard way, and then we show that an arbitrary manifold of dimension
p+q-1

\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
action is
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
-equivariantly covered by one of these spaces. Here,
P_{\text{null}}
acts on
\text{S}^{1}
via an analytic flow, see Section
4.1
for the relevant definitions.
Theorem
1.1

.

1.1
Background and Motivation

In
[
15
]
, Uchida studied smooth actions of
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
with the extra assumption that the action of a maximal compact subgroup on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
is the standard orthogonal one. He showed that such actions are in one to one correspondence with pairs
(\Phi,f)
where
\Phi
is a smooth flow on
\text{S}^{1}
and
f:\text{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{P}^{1}
is smooth, and
\Phi
and
f
satisfy certain conditions. We will briefly recall Uchida’s construction in Section
2
. Here,
\text{S}^{1}
is diffeomorphic to the fixed point set of
H
, which is a principal isotropy group with codimension 1 orbits for the action of the maximal compact subgroup. The function
f
encodes the isotropy algebra at a point in
\text{S}^{1}
for the given action of
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
, see Lemma
2.2
and Remark
4
. Note that Uchida’s result gives a correspondence between smooth
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,q)
actions and pairs
(\Phi,f)
, but only classifies them up to homeomorphism.

Such a pair
(\Phi,f)
was first introduced by Asoh in
[
2
]
in order to study actions of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
\text{S}^{3}
. It was then modified by Uchida in
[
15
]
to study the actions mentioned above and modified even further by Mukoyama in
[
9
]
to study smooth
\text{Sp}(2,\mathbb{R})
actions on
\text{S}^{4}
. Uchida in
[
16
]
used Mukoyama’s approach to study smooth
\text{SO}^{\circ}(p,2)
actions on
\text{S}^{p+1}
, as well as
\text{Sp}(p,q)
actions on
\text{S}^{4p+4q-1}
in
[
17
]
. Mukoyama also studied smooth
\text{SU}(p,q)
actions on
\text{S}^{2p+2q-1}
and
\mathbb{R}\text{P}^{p+q-1}
in
[
10
]
. On the other hand, assuming analyticity, Schneider classified
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
actions on 2-dimensional compact manifolds in
[
11
]
. We note that
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\text{Sp}(2,\mathbb{R})
are locally isomorphic to
\text{SO}^{\circ}(1,2)
,
\text{SO}^{\circ}(1,3)
and
\text{SO}^{\circ}(2,3)
respectively.

In a different direction, Uchida classified analytic
\text{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
actions on
\text{S}^{n}
in
[
13
]
. Later, both the analytic and the smooth
\text{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
actions on
n
-dimensional closed manifolds were classified by Fisher and Melnick in
[
4
]
. Son, in
[
12
]
, extended the result in the analytic case to dimensions
m
, where
n\leq m\leq 2n-3
. She also classified the smooth actions in these dimensions when the action if fixed point free.

Remark 4

Recall that
\mathscr{F}
is the fixed point set of

For the actions that we consider, since there is no point on

gives an analytic flow

on

. Abusing the notation, we will write

Remark 5

By straightforward matrix multiplications, we see that

and

(A2)

\displaystyle f(z)=0\Leftrightarrow z=(\pm e_{p+1},\epsilon_{1})

Lemma 2.3
.

(i)

Suppose
\Phi_{\theta}
is an induced basic
j_{1}
-flow on
\mathscr{S}
, see Definition

. Then,
f_{\Phi}
, see Remark

, is analytic, and

and

can be extended to

so that they satisfy relations

.

(ii)

Suppose
\Phi_{\theta}
and

are an analytic flow and function respectively on

such that they satisfy relations

. Then,
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta}\big|_{\mathscr{S}}
is an induced basic
j_{1}
-flow on
\mathscr{S}
and
\displaystyle f_{\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}}=f
.

Proof.

(i)

Suppose
\Phi_{\theta}
is an induced basic
j_{1}
-flow on
\mathscr{S}
. Firstly, we prove that
f_{\Phi}
is analytic on
\mathscr{S}
. Observe that,
f_{\Phi}
is evidently analytic on the orbits of
(\pm e_{p+1},\epsilon_{1})
. To prove the analyticity at a fixed point of
\Phi_{\theta}
, say
z_{1}
, we can use Poincaré’s theorem, see
[
1
, Section 22]
, to linearise the flow around
z_{1}
. The theorem implies that there exists a change of coordinates around
z_{1}
such that, after using a chart centered at
z_{1}
, the flow has the form
\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta}(x)=x\,e^{tJ_{\Phi}(z_{1})}
, where
J_{\Phi}
is the Jacobian of
\Phi_{\theta}
at
z_{1}
, which is nonzero. Then, let
y
be a point in the domain in which the above form of
\Phi_{\theta}
is valid. Note that, by (A3),
f_{\Phi}
takes the form
\displaystyle f_{\Phi}\left(\Phi_{\theta}(y)\right)=\frac{f(y)+\text{tanh}(\theta)}{1+f(y)\text{tanh}(\theta)}
.
Let
x=\Phi_{\theta}(y)
. Since
\Phi_{\theta}(y)=y\,e^{tJ_{\Phi}(z_{1})}
, we can solve for
\theta
and get
\displaystyle\theta=\frac{1}{J_{\Phi}(z_{1})}\,\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)
. Then, a straightforward calculation shows that
\displaystyle\text{tanh}(\theta)=\frac{1-\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{2/J_{\Phi}(z_{1})}}{1+\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{2/J_{\Phi}(z_{1})}}
. But the right hand side of this equality is an analytic function in a neighbourhood of
x=0
. Hence,
f_{\Phi}
is analytic at
z_{1}
. Similarly, it is shown that
f_{\Phi}
is analytic at the other fixed point of
\Phi_{\theta}
as well. Subsequently, we extend
\Phi_{\theta}
to all of
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{S}\bigcup j_{2}\star\mathscr{S}
, by defining for
z\in j_{2}\star\mathscr{S}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Phi_{\theta}(z):=j_{2}\star\left(\Phi_{-\theta}\left(j_{2}\star z\right)\right)
. Similarly, we extend
f_{\Phi}
on
j_{2}\star\mathscr{S}
by defining
f_{\Phi}(z):=-f_{\Phi}(j_{2}\star z)
. Clearly,
\Phi_{\theta}
and
f_{\Phi}
satisfy the relations (A1)-(A4). Evidently,
\Phi_{\theta}
and
f_{\Phi}
are analytic on
\mathscr{F}
.

(ii)

Suppose
\Phi_{\theta}
and
f
are an analytic flow and function such that they satisfy relations (A1)-(A4). The fact that the fixed points of
\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
are not
(\pm e_{p+1},\epsilon_{1})
and the relation
\displaystyle j_{1}\star\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(z)=\Phi_{-\theta}^{\prime}(j_{1}\star z)
are immediate. The flow
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
generates a vector field
X
on
\mathscr{S}
. If
\partial_{\mathscr{S}}
is a basic vector field of
\mathscr{S}
, the we can write
X=g\cdot\partial_{\mathscr{S}}
, where
g
is an analytic function on
\mathscr{S}
. Then, property (A3) for
z=(e_{p+1},\epsilon_{1})
shows that
f
satisfies the following differential equation

g\,\partial_{\mathscr{S}}\left(f\right)=1-f^{2}

(7)

Note that this equation is valid at the fixed points of
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
also, since at those points
g=0
and
f=\pm 1
. The last equality follows from (A3) by taking
\theta\rightarrow\pm\infty
. Let
z_{1}
be one of the fixed points of
\Phi_{\theta}
for which
f(z_{1})=1
. We can pick a chart centered at
z_{1}
, where (
7
) takes the form

g\cdot f^{\prime}=1-f^{2}

(8)

The functions in (
8
) are to be understood as composed with the chart, but we still use the same letters. Since
f
is an analytic function on
\mathscr{S}
and since it is not constantly equal to 1, it cannot be constantly equal to 1 around
z_{1}
. That means that the order of vanishing of

in (
8
) at

cannot be infinite. By order of vanishing we mean the smallest number

such that
(1-f)^{(n)}(0)=f^{(n)}(0)\neq 0
, but
(1-f)^{(k)}(0)=0
for
0\leq k<n
, where
(1-f)^{(0)}=1-f
. But then, differentiating equation (
8
)
(n+1)
-times and evaluating at
0
, we get that
g^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{2}{n}

Of course,
g^{\prime}(0)
is equal to the Jacobian of
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
at
z_{1}
. By (A1), we get that

, hence we see that at the other fixed point of
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
, which is

, we have that the Jacobian of
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
is
J_{\Phi}\left(j_{1}(z_{1})\right)=\frac{2}{n}
. Then, by Lemma

,
\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}
is an induced basic
j_{1}
-flow.

2.3
The Uchida construction

As it was mentioned in the Introduction, in
[
15
]
, Uchida studied smooth actions of
G
on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
extending the standard orthogonal action of
K
.

Remark 7
.

Uchida showed that such actions are in one to one correspondence with pairs
(\Phi,\tilde{f})
where
\Phi
is a smooth flow on
\text{S}^{1}
and
\tilde{f}:\text{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{P}^{1}
is smooth, and
\phi
and
\tilde{f}
satisfy:

(i)

j_{i}\star\Phi_{\theta}(z)=\Phi_{-\theta}(j_{i}\star z)

(i=1,2)

(ii)

\tilde{f}(z)=[a:b]\Rightarrow\tilde{f}(j_{i}\star z)=[a:-b]

(i=1,2)

(iii)

\tilde{f}(z)=[a:b]\Rightarrow\tilde{f}\left(\Phi_{\theta}(z)\right)=\left[a\,\text{cosh}(\theta)+b\,\text{sinh}(\theta):a\,\text{sinh}(\theta)+b\,\text{cosh}(\theta)\right]

(iv)

\tilde{f}(z)=[0:1]\Leftrightarrow z=e_{p+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{f}(z)=[1:0]\Leftrightarrow z=e_{1}

where,
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
act on
\text{S}^{1}
as the reflections with respect to the
y
-axis and
x
-axis respectively, see
[
15
, Theorem]
. We will refer to the above conditions as
Uchida conditions
.

The correspondence roughly goes as follows. Given a
G
action on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
, let
\mathscr{F}
be the fixed point set of
H
. Then,
\mathscr{F}
is diffeomorphic to
\text{S}^{1}
. The pair
(\Phi,\tilde{f})
is obtained via
\mathscr{M}(p,q)
and Lemma
2.2
respectively. For the reverse direction, suppose a pair
(\Phi,\tilde{f})
satisfying the Uchida conditions is given. We can assume that
(\Phi,\tilde{f})
are a flow and a function on
\mathscr{F}
, since
\mathscr{F}
is diffeomorphic to
\text{S}^{1}
. For
z\in\mathscr{F}
with
\tilde{f}(z)=[a:b]
, let
U^{\prime}(z)=(H_{[a:b]})^{\circ}
. Then, by equation (
3
),
G=K\,\mathscr{M}(p,q)\,U^{\prime}(z)
for any
z\in\mathscr{F}
. Therefore, an action of
G
on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
such that
K
acts in the standard way is defined in the following way: Let
v\in\text{S}^{p+q-1}
and
g\in G
. There exist
z\in\text{S}^{1}
and
k\in K
such that
k\star z=v
. Using (
5
), write
gk=k_{1}\,m(\theta)\,u
. Then, the action is defined as
g\star v:=k_{1}\star\Phi_{\theta}(z)
, where on the right hand side the action of
k_{1}
is the standard orthogonal one.

Let
\mathscr{S}_{3}=\{\alpha\,e_{1}+\beta\,e_{p+1}:\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=1\}\subseteq\text{S}^{p+q-1}
. Then
\mathscr{S}_{3}=\mathscr{F}
for

actions on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}
extending the orthogonal
K

Definition 2.3
.

Assume
\Phi_{\theta}
is a nontrivial analytic flow on
\textup{S}^{1}
. We will say that

is a

•

, none of which are the points

or
\pm e_{2}

•

\textup{J}_{i}\Phi_{\theta}(z)=\Phi_{-\theta}(\text{J}_{i}z)

, where

•

The Jacobian of

, where

.

Note that the second condition in the above definition implies that if

is a fixed point of

, then the rest of the fixed points are

, for

, and
\text{J}_{1}\text{J}_{2}\star z_{1}

at

is

-2/n
at

,

. Similarly to Definition
2.2
, we can define

(j_{1},j_{2})
-flows
on
\mathscr{S}_{3}
. Furthermore, similarly to Lemma

it can be shown that starting from an induced basic
(j_{1},j_{2})
-flow on

we can get a pair

. Consequently, by the results in
[

]
, we get an analytic

\text{S}^{p+q-1}
extending the orthogonal
K
action. We will refer to an action of

on
\text{S}^{p+q-1}

:

Lemma 3.7

Suppose

\mathscr{O}^{p}

\mathscr{O}^{q}

\mathscr{O}^{p}\simeq\textup{SO}(4)\big/\textup{U}_{2}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\mathscr{O}^{q}\simeq\textup{SO}(4)\big/\textup{U}_{2}

Then, the action is not locally effective. In particular, it cannot extend to an action of
\textup{SO}^{\circ}(4,4)

Proof.

Assume it is locally effective. We will get a contradiction by looking at the isotropy representation of
\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2}
at

\text{T}_{x}\mathscr{O}^{p}\oplus\text{T}_{x}\mathscr{O}^{q}

\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2}

\text{T}_{x}M=\text{T}_{x}\mathscr{O}^{p}\oplus\text{T}_{x}\mathscr{O}^{q}\oplus W

is also invariant under the action of
\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2}
. Now, since
\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2}
acts by isometries, the isotropy representation gives us a map from
\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2}
into block matrices of the form
\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}A&&\\
&B&\\
&&C\end{bmatrix}
, where
A,B\in\text{O}_{2}
and
C\in\text{O}_{3}
. However, dim
(\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2})=8
, while dim
(\text{O}_{2}\times\text{O}_{2}\times\text{O}_{3})=5
. This means that the map above must have nontrivial kernel, i.e. there exists a subgroup
H
of
\text{U}_{2}\times\text{U}_{2}
with dimension at least 1, that acts trivially on
\text{T}_{x}M

Cohomological equation for geodesic flows on flat surfaces
Giovanni Forni, Nelson Moll

Abstract
. We prove the existence of solutions of the cohomological equation for the geodesic flow on the unit tangent bundle of a compact flat surface with finitely many cone points. We also prove the ergodicity of the holonomy foliation for surfaces with non-rational holonomy, and the cohomology-free property of the horizontal foliated Laplacian under a simultaneous Diophantine condition.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we will prove the existence of solutions of the cohomological equation for the geodesic flow on the unit tangent bundle of a compact flat surface with finitely many cone points. Our approach is based on harmonic analysis, with the aim of adapting

[
3
]
and
[
4
]
from the case of translation surfaces to that of flat surfaces with non-rational holonomy. We prove, under an irrationality condition on the cone angles, that the horizontal (holonomy) foliation is ergodic and derive by a Cheeger-type argument bounds on the eigenvalues of the horizontal foliated Laplacian. These bounds are proved under a simultaneous Diophantine condition on the angles of the cone points. As an immediate consequence we prove that the foliated Laplacian is hypoelliptic with range of codimension one. These results will allow us to find solutions of the cohomological equation for the geodesic flow on the surface. Distributional solutions exist for all sufficiently smooth data of zero average, while smooth solutions exist for data in the kernel (perpendicular) of the infinite dimensional space of invariant distributions. In other terms,
the flat geodesic flow is stable in the sense of A. Katok

]
,
[

]
.

Let the pair
(S,R)
be a flat surface together with a flat metric
R
that has finitely many cone points at the set
\Sigma=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{\sigma}\}\subset S
. We will consider the geodesic flow of
R
, which is euclidean away from each cone point.
The unit tangent bundle
M:=T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
over the complement of the set of cone points has an infinitesimal homogeneous structure
given by vector fields
\{X,Y,\Theta\}
satisfying the commutation relations

[X,Y]=0\,,\quad[\Theta,X]=Y\,,\quad[\Theta,Y]=-X\,.

The flow
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}^{X}
is the geodesic flow of the flat metric
R
, which acts on the tangent bundle
TS
by the formula
\phi_{t}^{X}(v)=\gamma_{v}(t)
, where
\gamma_{v}(t)
is the unit tangent vector to the geodesic with initial condition equal to
v\in TS
. The flow
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}^{Y}
denotes the orthogonal geodesic flow,
which acts on the tangent bundle
TS
by the formula
\phi_{t}^{X}(v)^{\perp}=\gamma_{v^{\perp}}(t)
, where
\gamma_{v^{\perp}}(t)
is the unit tangent vector to the geodesic with initial condition equal to the perpendicular vector
v^{\perp}
to
v\in TS
in the positive direction. Because of the presence of cone points, the flows
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}^{X}
and
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}^{Y}
are defined on the complement of a countable union of hypersurfaces (given by all unit tangent vectors generating geodesics which end in cone points).
The flow
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
is the flow of rotations in the fiber of
T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
which is everywhere defined and smooth on
T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
.

Let
\{\hat{X},\hat{Y},\hat{\Theta}\}
the system of
1

\{X,Y,\Theta\}
. We have the structural equations

d\hat{X}=-\hat{\Theta}\wedge\hat{Y},\quad d\hat{Y}=\hat{\Theta}\wedge\hat{X}\quad\text{ and }\quad d\hat{\Theta}=0\,.

Let
\text{vol}_{M}=\hat{X}\wedge\hat{Y}\wedge\hat{\Theta}
denote the volume
form on
M:=T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
. Since by the above formulas

(d\circ\imath_{X})\text{vol}_{M}=(d\circ\imath_{Y})\text{vol}_{M}=(d\circ\imath_{\Theta})\text{vol}_{M}=0\,,

it follows that the flows
\phi^{X}_{\mathbb{R}}

\phi^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
are
volume preserving, that is, in terms of the Lie derivatives with respect to the generators,

\mathcal{L}_{X}\text{vol}_{M}=\mathcal{L}_{Y}\text{vol}_{M}=\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}\text{vol}_{M}=0\,.

We introduce a simultaneous Diophantine condition on the holonomy of a flat surface. Let

A crucial step in the analysis of the cohomological equation for translation
flows is the following cohomology-free result for the partial (foliated)
Laplacian

H=-(X^{2}+Y^{2}).

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose the holonomy of the flat surface
S
with conical singularities at
\Sigma
is simultaneously Diophantine of exponent
\gamma>0
. For function
f
on
M
with
L^{2}
bounded
\Theta

and with
\int_{M}fd\text{vol}_{M}=0

to the cohomological equation

Hu=f.

We recall that the
cohomological equation
for a flow on a manifold

can be written in terms

Xu=f\,.

It is immediate that
X
-invariant distributions
, that is, distributional
solutions

of the equation

of the cohomological equation. In fact,
if for a given smooth function

such that

, then for every

D(f)=D(Xu)=(XD)(u)=0\,.

The

of a flow (in the sense of A. Katok
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
) means that

-invariant distributions are a complete set of obstructions for the existence of a smooth

The main results of this paper establish the stability of the geodesic flow of a flat surface with simultaneously Diophantine holonomy, as well as the existence of an infinite dimensional space of distributional obstruction, given by invariant distributions. These results (see Theorems

,
5.4

Theorem 1.6
.

For any
\gamma>0

such that following holds.
Suppose that the holonomy of

satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. Then

•

for any function

of zero average with square integrable derivatives up to order

of the cohomological equation

;

•

if in addition

belongs to the kernel of all

-invariant

The space of all

2.
Structure of the Paper

Our approach to proving existence of distributional
and smooth solutions of the cohomological equation
is based on
a priori
estimates (see Theorem
5.2
). These in turn
are based on the harmonic analysis on flat surfaces
recalled in section
3
, in particular
Lemma
3.3
, and on a priori bounds
for the partial (horizontal) Laplacian (see Theorem
4.6
). The analysis of the horizontal
Laplacian is based on lower bounds
on eigenvalues of the partial (horizontal) Laplacian,
derived from a Cheeger-type estimate (see Theorem

4.3
).

The paper is organized as follows. In section
3

we establish basic results on geometry and analysis on flat surfaces with conical points. In particular we introduce convenient Sobolev spaces (which distinguish between horizontal and vertical smoothness) in subsection
3.4
,
and recall the creation/annihilation properties of foliated Cauchy-Riemann operators in subsection
3.5
.

In section
4
we prove results on the dynamics and analytical properties of the horizontal (holonomy)
2
-dimensional foliation. In subsection
4.1

we prove that the horizontal foliation is ergodic.
The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of results
on the analysis of the horizontal foliated Laplacian. We prove
in subsection
4.2
that the horizontal Laplacian is globally hypoelliptic (cohomology free). This result is based
on Cheger-type lower bounds on its eigenvalues.
Finally, in section
5
we complete the analysis of the cohomological equation for the geodeic flow. In subsection
5.1
we give a proof of existence of distributional and smooth solutions and in subsection
5.2
we describe the structure of the space
of distributional obtructions to the existence of smooth solutions (invariant distributions).

3.
Geometry and Analysis on Flat Surfaces

Let
(S,R)
denote a flat surface
S
with a finite set
\Sigma\subset S
of conical singularities.

3.1.
Local homogeneous structure

Let
\{X,Y,\Theta\}
denote the frame composed of the generator
X
of the
geodesic flow
, of the generator
Y
of the
orthogonal geodesic flow
and of the generator
\Theta
of the
rotations in the fibers
of
M=T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
with respect to the flat metric
R
on
S
.
We recall the commutation relations

(2)

[X,Y]=0\,,\quad[\Theta,X]=Y\,,\quad[\Theta,Y]=-X\,.

Important auxiliary operators are the foliated
Cauchy-Riemann operators

\partial^{\pm}=X\pm iY
. By the main commutation relation we derive the following commutation identities for the Cauchy-Riemann operators

(3)

[\partial^{+},\partial^{-}]=0\,,\quad[\Theta,\partial^{\pm}]=\mp i\partial^{\pm}\,.

3.2.
A Fourier decomposition

Since
\Theta
integrates to a circle action on
M
, it induces a splitting
of the space
L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
:

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

(4)

E_{k}=\{f\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M}):f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta}=e^{ik\theta}f\,,\text{ for all }\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following orthogonal splitting holds:

L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{k}\,.

Proof.

We recall the argument for the convenience of the reader.
Let
\phi^{\Theta}_{t}
be the flow given by rotation of a vector
v\in M:=T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
. For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the projection
\pi_{k}:L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})\to L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
by

(5)

\pi_{k}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}(f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta})d\theta\,.

It can be verified that, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\pi_{k}
is the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace
E_{k}
. In fact, for all
r>0
by change of variables and periodicity, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{k}(f)\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}(f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta+r})d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ikr}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{r}^{2\pi+r}e^{-ik\theta}(f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta})d\theta=e^{ikr}\pi_{k}(f)\,.

In addition, it is immediate that, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\pi_{k}^{2}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}(\pi_{k}(f)\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta})d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\pi_{k}(f)d\theta=\pi_{k}(f)\,.

and that for Since for
h\not=k\in\mathbb{Z}

\pi_{h}\pi_{k}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}(\pi_{h}(f)\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta})d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-i(k-h)\theta}fd\theta=0\,.

Since by orthogonality

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\pi_{k}(f)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

and by Fourier series expansion for periodic functions on the real line we have that, for all
f\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
and for almost all
(x,\xi)\in M
,

f(x,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{k}(f)(x,\xi)\,,

we conclude that
\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{k}=\text{Id}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}
.
∎

3.3.
Local coordinates

The smooth structure on
M
induced by the flat metric with conical singularities can be understood in local coordinates.
At a regular point there exists a (locally defined) complex canonical coordinate
z=x+iy
on
S
such that the metric
R
is euclidean:

R=\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

As a consequence, the unit tangent bundle is locally trivial with local coordinates
(z,\theta)
and we have the following local formulas

X=\cos\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\,,\quad Y=-\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\cos\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\,,\quad\Theta=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}

with invariant volume form
\text{vol}_{M}=dx\wedge dy\wedge d\theta
.

At a conical point of angle
2\pi(\alpha+1)
, there exists a (locally defined) complex canonical coordinate
z=x+iy
on
S
such that the metric
R

has the expression

(6)

R=|z|^{\alpha}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

As a consequence, the unit tangent bundle is not locally trivial and has local coordinates
(z,\theta)
with the following identification

(7)

(e^{2\pi i}z,\theta)\equiv(z,\theta+2\pi\alpha)\,.

With respect to the above coordinates we have the following local formulas

\partial^{+}=e^{-i\theta}\bar{z}^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bar{z}}}\,,\quad\partial^{-}=e^{i\theta}z^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\,,\quad\Theta=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}

and, as a consequence,

(8)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{+}+\partial^{-}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}(e^{-i\theta}\bar{z}^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bar{z}}}+e^{i\theta}z^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z})\,,

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{+}-\partial^{-}}{2i}=\frac{1}{2i}(e^{-i\theta}\bar{z}^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bar{z}}}-e^{i\theta}z^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z})\,,

with invariant volume form
\text{vol}_{M}=|z|^{2\alpha}dx\wedge dy\wedge d\theta
.

We introduce Sobolev spaces on
M
below. First we introduce the space
of smooth functions on the unit tangent bundle of a flat surface with
conical points.

Definition 3.2
.

The natural space
C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
of smooth functions on the unit tangent bundle of a surface
S
with cone singularities at
\Sigma
is defined as follows. A function
f\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
if it restricts to a smooth function of the canonical local coordinates
(z,\theta)
in the unit tangent bundle of a neighborhood of any regular point and if it restricts to a smooth
function of the variables

(z,\bar{z},e^{-i\theta}z^{\alpha},e^{i\theta}\bar{z}^{\alpha})

in the unit tangent bundle of a neighborhood of any conical point of angle

2\pi(\alpha+1)
.

The above definition is motivated by the condition that the space

C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
be the common domain of all iterates of the operators

\partial^{+}
,
\partial^{-}
,
\Theta
or equivalently
X
,
Y
,
\Theta
.

3.4.
Sobolev spaces

For
(r,s)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, we define the norm

(9)

|f|_{r,s}=\Big(\sum\limits_{i+j\leq r}\sum\limits_{l\leq s}\|X^{i}Y^{j}\Theta^{l}f\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

We then define the
L^{2}
Sobolev space
H^{r,s}(M)
on
M
as the completion with respect to the norm
|\cdot|_{r,s}
of the space
C_{\Sigma}^{\infty}(M)
of smooth functions. We also define
H^{-r,-s}(M)
to be the dual space to
H^{r,s}(M)
.

We note that for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists
C_{r,s}>0
such that, for all
f\in H^{r,s}(M)
we have

(10)

C_{r,s}^{-2}|f|_{r,s}^{2}\,\leq\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+n^{2s})|\pi_{n}(f)|_{r,0}^{2}\,\leq\,C_{r,s}^{2}|f|_{r,s}^{2}

The Lie derivative operators
X
and
Y
, as well as the Cauchy-Riemann
operators
\partial^{\pm}
are defined on the common domain
H^{1,0}(M)
.
Since the flows
\phi^{X}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}
are
volume preserving, the Lie derivative operators
X
and
Y
are skew-symmetric on

H^{1,0}(M)
. Since the set of the singular trajectories of
\phi^{X}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}
are countable unions
of codimension one submanifolds and have therefore zero volume, the operators

X
and
Y
are essentially skew-adjoint
[
7
]
. As for the Cauchy-Riemann operators,
it follows that the adjoint of
\partial^{\pm}
on the common domain
H^{1,0}(M)
is an extension of
-\partial^{\mp}
on the same domain.

The Cauchy-Riemann operators satisfy the following crucial
identity.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
v\in H^{1,0}(M)
we have

\|\partial^{+}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{-}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|Xv\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|Yv\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,.

Proof.

By the definition of the space
H^{1,0}(M)
, it
suffices to prove the statement for smooth functions
v\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
and conclude by a density argument.

For
v\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
, we have the commutation identity
XYv=YXv
, hence

\langle Yv,Xv\rangle=-\langle XYv,v\rangle=-\langle YXv,v\rangle=\langle Xv,Yv\rangle\,.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\pm}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

\displaystyle=\langle(X\pm iY)v,(X\pm iY)v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Xv,Xv\rangle+\langle Yv,Yv\rangle\pm i(\langle Yv,Xv\rangle-\langle Xv,Yv\rangle)

\displaystyle=\|Xv\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|Yv\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,,

as stated.
∎

Since the flow
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
leaves invariant the plane generated by

\{X,Y\}
, we derive the following result on the orthogonal projections onto the eigenspaces of
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
r\in\mathbb{N}
and for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the image of the
Sobolev space
H^{r,0}(M)
under the projection operator
\pi_{n}:L^{2}(M,\text{\rm vol}_{M})\to E_{n}
is contained in the space
H^{r,0}(M)\cap E_{n}
and
we have the following estimates. For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
C_{r}>0
such that for all
f\in H^{r,0}(M)
we have

|\pi_{n}(f)|_{r,0}\leq C_{r}|f|_{r,0}\,.

Proof.

We recall that

\pi_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-int}f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{t}dt\,.

Since
(\phi^{\Theta}_{t})_{\ast}(X)=(\cos t)X+(\sin t)Y
and
(\phi^{\Theta}_{t})_{\ast}(Y)=(-\sin t)X+(\cos t)Y
by the commutation relations, it follows that (in the weak sense)

\displaystyle X\pi_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-int}[(\cos t)X+(\sin t)Y]f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{t}dt\,,

\displaystyle Y\pi_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-int}[(-\sin t)X+(\cos t)Y]f\circ\phi^{\Theta}_{t}dt\,.

It follows that for
f\in H^{1,0}(M)
we have that
\pi_{n}(f)\in H^{1,0}(M)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that, for all
f\in H^{1,0}(M)
, we have

|\pi_{n}(f)|_{1,0}\leq C_{1}|f|_{1,0}\,.

The statement can be proved similarly, and we leave the argument to the reader.
∎

3.5.
Creation and annihilation

By the commutation relations (
3
), the Cauchy-Riemann
operators are operators of creation and annihilation for the spectral
decomposition of the rotation. In fact we have

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the Cauchy-Riemann operators
\partial^{\pm}:E_{n}\cap H^{1,0}(M)\to E_{n-1}
and
\partial^{-}:E_{n}\cap H^{1,0}(M)\to E_{n+1}
are well-defined and bounded.

Proof.

Let
f\in E_{n}\cap H^{1,0}(M)
then
\partial^{\pm}f\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
and by definition of the Sobolev spaces

\|\partial^{\pm}f\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq|f|_{1,0}\,.

By the commutation relations we get (taking the derivative with respect to
\Theta
in weak sense)

\Theta(\partial^{\pm}f)=[\Theta,\partial^{\pm}]f+\partial^{\pm}\Theta f\\
=\mp i\partial^{\pm}f+in\partial^{\pm}\Theta f=i(n\mp 1)\partial^{\pm}f\,,

hence
\partial^{\pm}f\in E_{n\mp 1}
, as claimed.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\pm}
denote the kernels of
\partial^{\pm}
in

L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
, or in other terms the kernels of the
adjoint operators
(\partial^{\mp})^{\ast}
(which are extensions
of
-\partial^{\pm}
respectively).

The space
\mathcal{M}^{+}
is the space of (foliated) meromorphic
functions and similarly
\mathcal{M}^{-}
is the space of (foliated) anti-meromorphic functions. We have the well-known descriptions:

Lemma 3.6
.

The space
\mathcal{M}^{\pm}
decomposes as as orthogonal sum of its components in the eigenspaces of the rotation:

\mathcal{M}^{\pm}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{M}^{\pm}\cap E_{n}).

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the space
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{n}:=\mathcal{M}^{\pm}\cap E_{n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-}_{n}:=\mathcal{M}^{-}_{n}\cap E_{n}
are in bijective correspondence with the space of holomorphic and, respectively, anti-holomorphic
n
-differentials.

Proof.

For every
m^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{\pm}
we have
the decomposition

m^{\pm}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{n}(m^{\pm})\,.

By Lemma
3.5
it follows that
\pi_{n}(m^{\pm})\in\mathcal{M}^{\pm}
, since for all
v\in H^{1,0}(M)
,

\langle\pi_{n}(m^{\pm}),\partial^{\mp}v\rangle=\langle m^{\pm},\pi_{n}(\partial^{\mp}v)\rangle=\langle m^{\pm},\partial^{\mp}(\pi_{n\pm 1}(v))\rangle=0\,.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let now
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{n}
denote the space of holomorphic and, respectively, anti-holomorphic
n
-differentials, defined
as holomorphic and, respectively, anti-holomorphic sections
of the
n
-th power of the canonical bundle of the Riemann surface
S
. There exist maps
\imath_{n}^{\pm}:\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{n}\to\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{n}
defined as follows: for all
d^{\pm}_{n}\in\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{n}
,

\imath_{n}^{\pm}(d^{\pm}_{n})(x,\xi)=d^{\pm}_{n}(x)(\xi)\,,\quad\text{for all }(x,\xi)\in T_{1}(S)\,.

It can be verified by a local calculation that,since the
differentials
d^{+}_{n}
and
d^{-}_{n}
are respectively holomorphic
and anti-holomorphic, the functions
\imath_{n}^{+}(d^{+}_{n})
and

\imath_{n}^{-}(d^{-}_{n})
belong to the space
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and

\mathcal{M}^{-}
respectively. In addition, since
d^{\pm}_{n}
are
(smooth)
n
-differentials, the functions
\imath_{n}^{\pm}(d^{\pm}_{n})
are square integrable and belong to the space

E_{n}
. Finally, it can be verified that given any function

m^{\pm}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{n}
, there exists a unique
n
-differentials
d^{\pm}_{n}
such that

d^{\pm}_{n}(x)(\xi)=m^{\pm}_{n}(x,\xi)\,,\quad\text{for all }(x,\xi)\in T_{1}(S)\,,

hence the maps
\imath_{n}^{\pm}:\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{n}\to\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{n}
are isomorphisms.
∎

4.
The horizontal (holonomy) foliation

In this section we examine the dynamical properties of the horizontal
foliation, including its ergodicity for surfaces with non-rational holonomy
and the cohomological equation for the foliated Laplacian under Diophantine
conditions on the holonomy representation. Bounds on the inverse of the
foliated Laplacian will be relevant in the study of the cohomological equation
for the flat geodesic flow.

4.1.
Ergodicity

We recall that according to Definition
1.1
the horizontal (holonomy) foliation
\mathcal{F}_{H}
has leaves tangent to the integrable distribution
\{X,Y\}\subset TM
. We have the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that the holonomy representation
\pi_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
is non-rational. Then any function
u\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
constant along the leaves of the horizontal foliation
\mathcal{F}_{H}
is constant, hence the foliation

\mathcal{F}_{H}
is ergodic.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
be any function constant along the leaves of
the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{H}
. Since each leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{H}
is invariant under the flows
\phi^{X}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}
it follows that
in the weak
L^{2}
sense we have

Xu=Yu=0\,,\quad\text{ hence }\quad\partial^{\pm}u=0\,,

Now expand
u=\sum\limits_{n}u_{n}
in terms of the eigenspaces of
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
. We claim that the function
u_{n}
is constant
along the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{H}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In fact, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and for any
v\in H^{1,0}(M)
, since

\partial^{\pm}:E_{n}\cap H^{1,0}(M)\to E_{n\mp 1}
by Lemma
3.5
and
\pi_{n\pm 1}(v)\in H^{1,0}(M)
by Lemma
3.4
, we have

\langle u_{n},\partial^{\pm}v\rangle=\langle\pi_{n}(u),\partial^{\pm}v\rangle=\langle u,\pi_{n}(\partial^{\pm}v)\rangle=\langle u,\partial^{\pm}\pi_{n\mp 1}(v)\rangle=0\,.

We finally claim that under the hypotheses that the holonomy representation
is non-rational, then
u_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
is constant and
u_{n}=0
for all

n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

In fact, since the holonomy representation is not rational, for any point

x\in S\setminus\Sigma
there exists a closed path
\gamma:[0,1]\to S\setminus\Sigma
such that
\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=x
and such that the
holonomy map corresponding to
\gamma
(on the fiber
M_{x}
) is an irrational
rotation. For any
(x,\xi)\in M_{x}
, let then
\hat{\gamma}_{\xi}:[0,1]\to M
denote the lift of
\gamma
defined by the parallel transport of
\xi\in M_{x}
along

\gamma
. There exists
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
2\pi\theta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

\hat{\gamma}_{\xi}(1)=\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta}(\hat{\gamma}_{\xi}(0))=\phi^{\Theta}_{\theta}(x,\xi)\,.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
u_{n}
is constant along the leaves of the
foliation
\mathcal{F}_{H}
, hence constant along the path
\hat{\gamma}_{\xi}
and
since
u_{n}\in E_{n}
we have

e^{2\pi in\theta}u_{n}(x,\xi)=u_{n}(\hat{\gamma}_{\xi}(1))=u_{n}(\hat{\gamma}_{\xi}(0))=u_{n}(x,\xi)\,.

For all
n\not=0
, since
2\pi\theta
is irrational, it follows that

u_{n}(x,\xi)=0
, and as a consequence, since
(x,\xi)
was any given point in
M
,
we conclude that
u_{n}\equiv 0
.

For
n=0
, we have that
u_{0}
, as a
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant function
is the pull-back of a function on
S
. Since
u_{0}
is constant along leaves of

\mathcal{F}_{H}
which project onto
S
by local diffeomorphisms, it follows that

u_{0}
is locally constant, hence constant.
∎

As a consequence of the ergodicity of the holonomy foliation we
prove that the flat geodesic flow and its rotations have no non-constant regular invariant function.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose that the holonomy representation
\pi_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
is non-rational.
Let
v\in H^{1,0}(M)
be any function such that
there exists
\theta\in S^{1}
such that
X_{\theta}v=0
. Then
v

is constant along the holonomy foliation, hence it is constant.

Proof.

We have

X_{\theta}v=e^{-i\theta}\partial^{+}v+e^{i\theta}\partial^{-}v=0\,.

Following Lemma
3.4
let us write
v=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{n}(v)
as a series of its projections
\pi_{n}(v)
onto
E_{n}\cap H^{1,0}(M)
. By Lemma
3.5
and by the above identity
X_{\theta}v=0
is equivalent to the sequence
of identities

e^{-i\theta}\partial^{+}v_{n+1}=-e^{i\theta}\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\,,\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

By Lemma
3.3
it follows that, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{+}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{+}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{+}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{-}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

However, since
\partial^{+}v
and
\partial^{-}v\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
, we have that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\partial^{+}v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\partial^{-}v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq|v|^{2}_{1,0}<+\infty\,,

which by the recursion in formula (
11
) implies
that

\partial^{+}v_{n}=\partial^{-}v_{n}=0\,,\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

We have thus prove that
\partial^{+}v=\partial^{-}v=0
, or,
in other terms
Xv=Yv=0
, that is,
v
is invariant along
the holonomy foliation, hence by Theorem
4.1
it is constant.
∎

4.2.
A Cheeger-type bound

In this subsection we prove a lower bound on the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet quadratic form of the foliated Laplacian
H=-(X^{2}+Y^{2})
.

We note that since the operator
H
commutes with the flow
\phi^{\Theta}_{\mathbb{R}}
it also commutes with the Laplacian
\Delta=-(H+\Theta^{2})
. It follows that all these operators can be simultaneously diagonalized with eigenfunctions in the Sobolev space
H^{1,\infty}(M)
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\lambda_{n}(H)
be the least eigenvalue of the Dirichlet quadratic form of
H
on
E_{n}
. We have the following

Theorem 4.3
.

If the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)
satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponents
\gamma>0
(see Definition
1.4
), then there is a constant
C>0
such that, for all

n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

\lambda_{n}(H)\geq\frac{C}{n^{2\gamma}}.

The proof of this result is based on Cheeger’s proof of a lower bound for the least eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Since
H
is not the Laplacian of a Riemannian metric, we consider the family of elliptic operators

\Delta_{\epsilon}=H-\epsilon^{2}\Theta^{2}\,.

For all
\epsilon>0
the operators
\Delta_{\epsilon}
are the Laplace-Beltrami operator for a metric on
M
with conical singularities along finite many circles.

For every
\epsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, since the Laplace operators
\Delta_{\epsilon}
and
H
commute (and commute with the Lie derivative operator
\Theta
) we have that

(12)

\lambda_{n}(H)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{n}(\epsilon)\,.

We will prove below a Cheeger-type bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the
Dirichlet for of
\Delta_{\epsilon}
on the subspace
E_{n}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}(M)
denote the set of all separating hypersurfaces in

M
which are
n
-covers of
S\setminus\Sigma
under the canonical projection
of
M=T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
onto
S\setminus\Sigma
(in the sense that the
restriction of the projection to the hypersurface is onto with fibers of cardinality equal to
n\not=0
).

Let
\text{Area}_{\epsilon}
and
\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}
denote the
2
-dimensional
volume (area) and the
3
-dimensional volume of the metric
g_{\epsilon}
on
M
(defined by the condition that
\{X,Y,\epsilon\Theta\}
is a orthonormal frame of
TM
) whose Laplace-Beltrami operator is
\Delta_{\epsilon}
.

For every
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(M)
, let
M^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}
the components of
M\setminus{\mathcal{S}}
. Let then

(13)

h_{n}(\epsilon):=\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(M)}\dfrac{\text{Area}_{\epsilon}({\mathcal{S}})}{\min\{\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}(M^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}),\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}(M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}})\}}.

We prove below the following lower bound:

Theorem 4.4
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, the least Friederichs eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}(\epsilon)
of the Dirichlet form of the operator
\Delta_{\epsilon}
on
M
restricted to
E_{n}
is bounded below as

\lambda_{n}(\epsilon)\geq\left(\frac{h_{n}(\epsilon)}{2}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

The proof follows Cheeger’s argument in
[
2
]
.
Let
\nabla_{\epsilon}
denote the gradient of the Riemannian metric

g_{\epsilon}
on
M
and let
\text{vol}_{\epsilon}
denote its volume element. By the variational principle

\lambda_{n}(\epsilon)=\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M,\mathbb{R})\cap(E_{n}\oplus E_{-n})}\frac{\|\nabla_{\epsilon}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}}{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}}\,.

Since
f\in E_{n}
with
n\not=0
, then
f
has zero average on
M
. For
all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}_{f}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{(x,\xi)\in M|f(x,\xi)>\lambda\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{-}_{f}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{(x,\xi)\in M|f(x,\xi)<\lambda\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{(x,\xi)\in M|f(x,\xi)=\lambda\}\,.

We note that since
f\in E_{n}\oplus E_{-n}
(and it is real-valued) all of its level
\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda)
in
M
set are
n
-covers of the surface
S\setminus\Sigma
and by the Sard theorem they are
2
-dimensional manifolds for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since
M
is connected and
f
has zero average, there exists
\lambda_{0}

such that
\text{vol}_{\epsilon}(\Omega^{+}_{f}(\lambda_{0}))=\text{vol}_{\epsilon}(\Omega^{-}_{f}(\lambda_{0}))
and by the co-area formula we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\epsilon}(f-\lambda_{0})^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{+}_{f}(\lambda_{0}),\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}=2\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}A_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda))(\lambda-\lambda_{0})d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq 2\inf_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}\frac{A_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda))}{\text{vol}_{\epsilon}(\Omega^{+}_{f}(\lambda))}\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}\text{vol}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda))(\lambda-\lambda_{0})d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq h_{n}(\epsilon)\|f-\lambda_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{+}_{f}(\lambda_{0}),\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\epsilon}(f-\lambda_{0})^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{-}_{f}(\lambda_{0}),\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}=2\int_{-\infty}^{\lambda_{0}}A_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda))(\lambda_{0}-\lambda)d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq 2\inf_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}\frac{A_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda))}{\text{vol}_{\epsilon}(\Omega^{-}_{f}(\lambda))}\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}\text{vol}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{f}(\lambda))(\lambda_{0}-\lambda)d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq h_{n}(\epsilon)\|f-\lambda_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{-}_{f}(\lambda_{0}),\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}\,.

By the above abounds and by the Hölder inequality it follows that

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla_{\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}\|f-\lambda_{0}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla_{\epsilon}(f-\lambda_{0})^{2}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\geq h_{n}(\epsilon)\|f-\lambda_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}

hence finally, since
f
has zero average,

\|\nabla_{\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}^{2}\geq\frac{h_{n}(\epsilon)^{2}}{4}\|f-\lambda_{0}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}^{2}\geq\frac{h_{n}(\epsilon)^{2}}{4}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{\epsilon})}^{2}

∎

We now need to bound
h_{n}(\epsilon)
from below. Let
\{\gamma_{1},\dots\gamma_{d}\}
denote a basis of
\pi_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
, and for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, let
2\pi\theta_{i}
denote the angle of the rotation of the holonomy along
\gamma_{i}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

There exists
c>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\epsilon>0
the Cheeger-type constant
h_{n}(\epsilon)

satisfies the lower bound

h_{n}(\epsilon)\geq c\sum_{i=1}^{d}d_{\mathbb{R}}(n\theta_{i},\mathbb{Z})\,.

Proof.

The area form of the metric
g_{\epsilon}
, defined by the orthonormality condition on
\{X,Y,\epsilon\Theta\}
, is

(14)

dA_{\epsilon}:=\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}|\hat{Y}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}|\hat{X}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}+|\hat{X}\wedge\hat{Y}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

and its volume is
dV_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-1}dV
, hence for all
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle h_{n}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(M)}\dfrac{\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Big(|\hat{Y}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}+|\hat{X}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}}{\min\{\text{Vol}(M^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}),\text{Vol}(M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}})\}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(M)}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Big(|\hat{Y}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}+|\hat{X}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
we consider a topological annulus
A_{i}
in
S\setminus\Sigma
which is a tubular neighborhood of the loop
\gamma_{i}
. Let us assume that the holonomy along
\gamma_{i}
is a rotation with angle an irrational multiple of
2\pi
. Then for every surface
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(M)
, the inverse image of
A_{i}
in
\mathcal{S}
(under the restriction to
\mathcal{S}
of the canonical
projection) is an annulus
\hat{A}_{i}\subset\mathcal{S}
, which is an
n
-cover of the annulus
A_{i}
. Let
\hat{\gamma}_{i}
a core curve of the annulus
\hat{A}_{i}
which projects onto
\gamma_{i}
.
Let
\hat{I}_{i}
denote an arc transverse to
\hat{\gamma}_{i}

such that
\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i}
is simply connected.

The boundary of
\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i}
is the union
of
(\partial\hat{A}_{i})\setminus(\hat{I}_{i}\cap\partial\hat{A}_{i})
and of the arcs

\hat{I}_{i}^{\pm}
which both map to
\hat{I}_{i}
under the inclusion
of
\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i}
into
M
.
It can be proved that there exist a closed
1
-form
\hat{V}
such that
\int_{\hat{I}_{i}}\hat{V}>0
which in addition is horizontal, that is
\hat{V}(\Theta)=0
, and a function
\theta:\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\hat{\Theta}=d\theta
on
\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i}
and
\int_{\partial\hat{A}_{i}}\theta\hat{V}=0
.

We note that for any pair of points

p^{\pm}\in\hat{I}^{\pm}_{i}
such that
p^{\pm}
have the same image
in
\hat{I}_{i}\subset M
under the inclusion map, we have that

\theta(p^{+})-\theta(p^{-})=\pm 2\pi n\theta_{i}
modulo
2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. We then have that there exists a constant
c_{i}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{A}_{i}}\Big(|\hat{Y}\wedge\hat{\Theta}|^{2}+|\hat{X}\wedge

\displaystyle\hat{\Theta}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\geq c_{i}\Big|\int_{\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i}}\hat{V}\wedge d\theta\Big|=c_{i}\Big|\int_{\partial(\hat{A}_{i}\setminus\hat{I}_{i})}\theta\hat{V}\Big|

\displaystyle=c_{i}\Big|\int_{\hat{I}^{+}_{i}}\theta\hat{V}-\int_{\hat{I}^{-}_{i}}\theta\hat{V}\Big|=2\pi c_{i}d_{\mathbb{R}}(n\theta_{i},\mathbb{Z})\int_{\hat{I}_{i}}\hat{V}\,.

The statement of the lemma follows since the above inequality
holds for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

The
theorem follows from the limit in formula (
12
), from the Cheeger-type lower bound for the eigenvalues in Theorem
4.4
, from the lower bound on the Cheeger constant in Theorem
4.5
and from the Definition
1.4
of simultaneously Diophantine holonomy.

∎

As a corollary of the Cheeger constant estimate, we can show that the partial Laplacian
H
has solutions
Hu=f
if
f
has zero average and is sufficiently regular in the fiber direction.

Theorem 4.6
.

If the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)
satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
(see Definition
1.4
), then for any zero-average function
f\in H^{r,s}(M)
with
r\geq 0
and
s\geq 2\gamma
, there is a unique zero-average solution
u\in H^{r,s-2\gamma}(M)
to the equation

Hu=f.

In addition, for every
r\geq 0
and
s\geq 2\gamma
there exists a constant
C_{r,s}>0
such that we have

|u|_{r,s-2\gamma}\leq C_{r,s}|f|_{r,s}\,.

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\{\phi_{n,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a orthonormal basis of eigenfunction of
H
on the space
E_{n}
and let
\{\lambda_{n,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the corresponding sequence of eigenvalues such that

H\phi_{n,k}=\lambda_{n,k}\phi_{n,k}\,.

Let us assume that
\phi_{0,0}\equiv 1\in E_{0}
. We can then write, for any
f\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
,

f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k>0}f_{n,k}\phi_{n,k}\,.

We note that by the definition of the Sobolev norms, for
every
r,s\geq 0
there exists a constant
K_{r,s}>0
such that,
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
k\geq 0
we have

K_{r,s}^{-1}(1+n^{2})^{s}|\phi_{n,k}|^{2}_{r,0}\,\leq\,|\phi_{n,k}|^{2}_{r,s}\,\leq\,K_{r,s}(1+n^{2})^{s}|\phi_{n,k}|^{2}_{r,0}

The unique formal solution
u
of zero average is then given by the formula

u:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k>0}f_{n,k}\lambda_{n,k}^{-1}\phi_{n,k}\,.

We note that since the operator
H
on
E_{0}
is unitarily equivalent to the (non-negative) flat Laplacian on
S
(which is elliptic and therefore has a spectral gap), it follows that there exists a constant
\lambda_{S}>0
such that

\lambda_{0,k}\geq\lambda_{S}>0\,,\quad\text{ for all }k>0\,.

By the hypothesis of simultaneously Diophantine holonomy and
by Theorem
4.3
we derive that there exist
constants
C_{s}
and
C^{\prime}_{s}
(also depending on
(S,R)
) such that

\displaystyle|u|^{2}_{r,s-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k>0}(1+n^{2})^{s-2\gamma}|f_{n,k}|^{2}|\lambda_{n,k}|^{-2}|\phi_{n,k}|^{2}_{r,0}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k>0}(1+n^{2})^{s-2\gamma}|f_{n,k}|^{2}|(1+n^{2})^{2\gamma}|\phi_{n,k}|^{2}_{r,0}\leq C^{\prime}_{s}|f|^{2}_{r,s}\,.

∎

5.
The cohomological equation

In this section we finally derive our main results on solutions
of the cohomological equation for the flat geodesic flow and
its rotations.

5.1.
Distributional and Smooth Solutions

In this subsection we prove a priori bounds for the Lie derivative operator
X
of the flat geodesic flow on smooth functions and derive a result on distributional and
smooth solutions of the cohomological equation.

The following a priori estimate for the foliated Cauchy-Riemann
derivatives in terms of the geodesic flow derivative holds.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
r\geq 0
and every
s>s^{\prime}+3/2
there exists a constant
C_{r,s,s^{\prime}}>0
such that the following holds.
For any
v\in H^{r+1,0}(M)
such
Xv\in H^{r,s}(M)
we have
that
\partial^{\pm}v\in H^{r,s^{\prime}}(M)
and

|\partial^{\pm}v|_{r,s^{\prime}}\leq C_{r,s,s^{\prime}}|Xv|_{r,s}\,.

Proof.

The equation
Xv=f
, for
v\in H^{1,0}(M)
, is equivalent to the sequence of equations

(15)

\partial^{+}v_{n+1}+\partial^{-}v_{n-1}=2f_{n}\,,\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

By taking into account that by Lemma
3.3

we have
\|\partial^{+}v\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{-}v\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}
for all
v\in H^{1,0}(M)
, it follows from the identity (
15
) that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|\partial^{+}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

\displaystyle=2\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|\partial^{-}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,,

hence by induction

\|\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|f_{n+2k}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|\partial^{-}v_{n+2K+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,.

Since
\partial^{-}v\in L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})
as
v\in H^{1,0}(M)

and

\|\partial^{-}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\partial^{-}v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,,

it follows that
\|\partial^{-}v_{n+2K+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\to 0
as
K\in\mathbb{N}
diverges. We therefore have the estimate

(16)

\|\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|f_{n+2k}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{+}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|\partial^{-}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

\displaystyle=2\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}+\|\partial^{+}v_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\,,

hence

(17)

\|\partial^{+}v_{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|f_{n-2k}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}

Let us assume that
f\in H^{0,s}(M)
. It follows that

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq|f|_{0,s}(1+n^{2})^{-s/2}\,,

hence by estimates (
16
) for
n\geq 0
and
(
17
) for
n\leq 0
we derive that, for
all
s>1
there exists a constant
C_{s}>0
such that

\|\partial^{+}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{-}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq C_{s}|f|_{0,s}(1+n^{2})^{-(s-1)/2}\,.

so that, for
s>3/2
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}_{s}>0
such that

\|\partial^{+}v\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}=\|\partial^{-}v\|_{L^{2}(M,\text{vol}_{M})}\leq C^{\prime}_{s}|f|_{0,s}\,.

In fact, more generally for every
s^{\prime}\geq 0
and for
s>s^{\prime}+3/2
there exists a constant
C_{s,s^{\prime}}>0
such that

|\partial^{+}v|_{0,s^{\prime}}=|\partial^{-}v|_{0,s^{\prime}}\leq C_{s,s^{\prime}}|f|_{0,s}\,.

Since
[H,X]=0
on
H^{3,0}(M)
, it follows that under the
assumption that
v\in H^{2k+1,0}(M)
we have

X(H^{k}v)=H^{k}(Xv)=H^{k}(f)

so that, if
f\in H^{2k,s}(M)
with
s>s^{\prime}+3/2
, we have

|H^{k}(\partial^{\pm}v)|_{0,s^{\prime}}=|\partial^{\pm}H^{k}(v)|_{0,s^{\prime}}\leq C_{s,s^{\prime}}|f|_{2k,s}\,,

which immediately implies the conclusion for
r=2k
even.
The statement for odd
r=2k+1
follows by interpolation.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let us assume that the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)
satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. Given
r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\geq 0
and for every
r>r^{\prime}+1
and
s>s^{\prime}+2\gamma+3/2
, there exists a constant
C:=C(r,r^{\prime}s,s^{\prime},\gamma)>0
such that, for all zero average functions
v\in H^{r,0}(M)
with
Xv\in H^{r,s}(M)
we have that

(18)

|v|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\leq C|Xv|_{r,s}\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
we have

|\partial^{\pm}v|_{r^{\prime}+1,s^{\prime}+2\gamma}\leq C_{r,s,s^{\prime}}|Xv|_{r,s}\,,

hence

|Hv|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}+2\gamma}\leq C_{r,s,s^{\prime}}|Xv|_{r,s}\,.

By Theorem
4.6
under the assumption that

v
has zero average we have, for any
r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\geq 0
we derive the estimate

|v|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\leq C_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|Hv|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}+2\gamma}\leq C_{r,s,s^{\prime}}C_{r,s,s^{\prime}+2\gamma}|Xv|_{r,s}\,,

as stated.
∎

For all
r,s\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)\subset H^{-r,-s}(M)
denote the space of all
X
-invariant distributions:

\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M):=\{D\in H^{-r,-s}(M)|XD=0\}\,.

We recall that the distributional equation
XD=0
for a distribution
D\in H^{-r,-s}(M)
is by definition equivalent to the condition

D(Xv)=0\,,\quad\text{ for all }v\in H^{r+1,s}(M)\,.

We prove below our main theorem about distributional and smooth solutions of the cohomological equation.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let us assume that the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)

satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. Given
r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\geq 0
and for every
r>r^{\prime}+1
and
s>s^{\prime}+2\gamma+3/2
, there exists a constant
C:=C(r,r^{\prime}s,s^{\prime},\gamma)>0
such that, for any distribution
F\in H^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}(M)
vanishing on constant functions there exists a solution
U\in H^{-r,-s}(M)
of the cohomological equation
XU=F
such that

(19)

|U|_{-r,-s}\leq C|F|_{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}\,.

Proof.

Given a distribution
F\in H^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}(M)
vanishing on constant function, we can formally define a solution
U\in H^{-r,-s}(M)
on the range of the Lie derivative operator by the formula

\langle U,Xv\rangle=-\langle F,v\rangle\,,\quad\text{ for all }v\in H^{r+1,s}(M)\,.

By Theorem
5.2
we have, for all
v\in H^{r+1,s}(M)
,

|\langle U,Xv\rangle|=|\langle F,v\rangle|\leq|F|_{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}|v|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\leq C_{r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|F|_{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}|Xv|_{r,s}\,,

hence the formal solution
U
extends by continuity (since the Sobolev space
H^{r,s}(M)
is complete) to a bounded linear
functional on the closed subspace

(20)

R_{r,s}(X):=\overline{\{f\in H^{r,s}(M)|f=Xv\text{ with }v\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)\}}^{H^{r,s}(M)}\,,

and to a bounded linear functional on
H^{r,s}(M)
defined
as
0
on the orthogonal complement
R_{r,s}(X)^{\perp}\subset H^{r,s}(M)
.
It follows by its definition that the distribution

U\in H^{-r,-s}(M)
is a solution of the cohomological equation
XU=F
and satisfies the stated bound.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let us assume that the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)
satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. Given
r>r^{\prime}+1\geq 2
and
s>s^{\prime}+3/2+2\gamma\geq 3/2+2\gamma
, there exists a constant
C_{r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}>0

such that the following holds. For every
f\in H^{r,s}(M)

such that
f\in\text{\rm Ker}(\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M))
, there
exists a unique zero average solution
u\in H^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(M)
of the cohomological equation
Xu=f
which satisfies the estimate

|u|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\leq C_{r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|f|_{r,s}\,.

Proof.

Let
R_{r,s}(X)\subset H^{r,s}(M)
the closure of the range of the Lie derivative operator
X
as in formula (
20
), that is,

R_{r,s}(X):=\overline{\{f\in H^{r,s}(M)|f=Xv\text{ with }v\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)\}}^{H^{r,s}(M)}\,.

We claim that the subspace
R_{r,s}(X)
is equal to the
joint kernel
\text{\rm Ker}(\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M))
of all
X
-invariant distributions in the dual space
H^{-r,-s}(M)
.
In fact, on the one hand if
D\in\text{\rm Ker}(\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M))
, then by definition
D(Xv)=0
for all
v\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
, hence
D
vanishes on
R_{r,s}(X)

since
D
is a continuous functional on
H^{r,s}(M)
. On the
other hand, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, the closed subspace
R_{r,s}(X)
is equal to the joint kernel of all functionals

D\in H^{-r,-s}(M)
which vanish on it. However, any functional
vanishing on
R_{r,s}(X)
, vanishes on all functions of the form

Xv
with
v\in H^{r+1,s}(M)
, hence it is an
X
-invariant distribution. The claim is therefore proved.

Finally, we prove that for all
f\in R_{r,s}(X)
, there exists
a solution
u\in H^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(M)
satisfying the stated estimates.
In fact, since
f\in R_{r,s}(X)
there exists a sequence
\{f_{k}\}
, such that
f_{k}=Xv_{k}
with
v_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{\Sigma}(M)
of zero average, which converges to
f
in
H^{r,s}(M)
.

By Theorem
5.2
it follows that, for all

k,h\in\mathbb{N}
,

|v_{k}|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\leq C|f_{k}|_{r,s}\quad\text{ and }\quad|v_{k}-v_{h}|_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\leq C|f_{k}-f_{h}|_{r,s}\,,

so that, since
\{f_{k}\}
is convergent, hence a Cauchy sequence in
H^{r,s}(M)
, then
\{v_{k}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(M)
. By completeness the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
converges
to a function
u\in H^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(M)
which is a solution of the
cohomological equation
Xu=f
and satisfies the stated bound
by continuity.

Finally, we conclude with the observation that since
r^{\prime}\geq 1

and
s^{\prime}\geq 0
, by Corollary
4.2
the zero-average solution of the cohomological equation in
H^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(M)

is unique. The same conclusion can also be derived from the
a priori estimate proved in Theorem
5.2
.
∎

5.2.
Invariant distributions

In this sections we prove some results on the space of invariant distributions for the geodesic flow, in particular we prove that
it has countable dimension and codimension.

The space of invariant distributions is related to the space
of foliated meromorphic (or anti-meromorphic) functions through the following constructions.

Let us assume that, for a given meromorphic functions
m
on
M
(square-integrable or otherwise taken as a distribution), the
cohomological equation
XU=m
has a distributional solution.
Then the distribution
D=\partial^{+}U
is an
X
-invariant
distribution (possibly equal to the zero distribution).

In this section we make this outline precise. We begin by
a description of the spaces of foliated meromorphic and anti-meromorphic distributions.

For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

E_{n}^{-r}=\{D\in H^{-r,0}(M)|\Theta(D)=inD\}\,.

Theorem 5.5
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the subspaces
\mathcal{M}^{+,r}_{n}\subset E^{-r}_{n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-,r}_{-n}\subset E^{-r}_{-n}
of foliated meromorphic and anti-meromorphic distributions are in bijective correspondence respectively
with the space of meromorphic
n
-differentials on
S
and
the space of anti-meromorphic
n
-differentials on
S
with order of zero or pole
k_{p}
at any conical point
p\in\Sigma
of parameter
\alpha_{p}
, and angle
2\pi(\alpha_{p}+1)
, satisfying the inequalities

k_{p}>(n-1)\alpha_{p}-r-1\,.

Proof.

An
n
-differential
d_{n}
is a section of the

n
-th power of the canonical bundle
T^{\ast}(S)
of the Riemann surface
S
. It induces a function on
M=T_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
by the formula

(21)

f_{n}(x,v)=d_{n}(x)(v)\,,\quad\text{ for all }(x,v)\in M\,.

The function
f_{n}
is well-defined on
M
, it is foliated
meromorphic (anti-meromorphic) on
M
if
d_{n}
is meromorphic
(anti-meromorphic) and there exists
r\geq 0
such that
f_{n}
belongs to
E_{n}^{-r}
since
d_{n}
is an
n
-differential, hence

\Theta f_{n}=inf_{n}\,.

Conversely, given a foliated meromorphic (anti-meromorphic)
function
f_{n}
on
M
which verifies the above differential
equation, the identity in formula (
21
) uniquely
defines a meromorphic (anti-meromorphic)
n
-differential
d_{n}
.

Clearly any meromorphic or anti-meromorphic
n
-differential induces
a foliated meromorphic, respectively anti-meromorphic, function on
M
. We then investigate the relation between the integrability properties of the induced function
and the order of zero or pole of the differential.

Let
p\in\Sigma
be a conical point of total angle
2\pi(\alpha+1)
. We recall that the metric on
S
near
p

has the expression
|z^{\alpha}dz|
(with respect
to a canonical coordinate). Let
d_{n}=d_{n}(z)dz^{n}
be a meromorphic differential with a zero or pole of order
k\in\mathbb{Z}
at
p
(
k>0
for a zero and
k<0
for a pole). The function
f_{n}
has the form

f_{n}(z,\theta)=d_{n}(z)(z^{-\alpha}e^{i\theta})^{n}

hence by integrating on a disk centered at
p
in polar coordinate we can verify that
f_{n}
is locally square integrable iff

\int_{0}^{1}r^{2k}r^{-2n\alpha}r^{2\alpha+1}dr<+\infty\,.

that is, iff

k>(n-1)\alpha-1\,.

We conclude that the above inequality for all
p\in\Sigma
is the necessary and sufficient condition for the function
f_{n}

to belong to the space
\mathcal{M}^{+,0}_{n}
of square-integrable foliated meromorphic eigenfunctions of
\Theta
with eigenvalue
in
.

A similar set of inequalities for all
p\in\Sigma
is the necessary and sufficient condition for the function
f_{n}
induced by an anti-meromorphic
n
-differential
d_{n}

to belong to the space
\mathcal{M}^{-,0}_{-n}
of square-integrable foliated anti-meromorphic eigenfunctions of
\Theta
with eigenvalue
-in
.

We recall that the Cauchy-Riemann operators are given
in a local canonical coordinate near a cone point of
angle
2\pi(\alpha+1)
by the formulas

\partial^{+}=e^{-i\theta}\bar{z}^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}\,,\quad\partial^{-}=e^{i\theta}z^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\,.

Let
f_{n-1}
be the foliated meromorphic function on
M
given by a meromorphic
(n-1)
-differential
d_{n-1}
. We then have

\displaystyle\partial^{-}f_{n-1}(z,\theta)

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta}z^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Big(d_{n-1}(z)(z^{-\alpha}e^{i\theta})^{n-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial d_{n-1}}{\partial z}(z)(z^{-\alpha}e^{i\theta})^{n}-(n-1)\alpha d_{n-1}(z)z^{-1}(z^{-\alpha}e^{i\theta})^{n}\,,

which is the foliated holomorphic function induced by the meromorphic
n
-differential

(22)

d_{n}=\Big(\frac{\partial d_{n-1}}{\partial z}(z)-(n-1)\alpha d_{n-1}(z)z^{-1}\Big)dz^{n}\,,

hence the (non-negative) order of pole is changed by
-1
.
A similar calculation can be carried out for the foliated
anti-meromorphic functions (or it can be derived by complex
conjugation).

Since the the range of the map

d_{n-1}(z)\to\partial d_{n-1}(z)/\partial z-(n-1)\alpha d_{n-1}(z)z^{-1}

on the space of Laurent series consists of all Laurent series (with no term of order
(n-1)\alpha-1
in case
(n-1)\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
), it follows that, locally near
p\in\Sigma
, all functions induced by a meromorphic
n
-differential with order of zero
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, with no term of order
(n-1)\alpha-1
in case
(n-1)\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, are
\partial^{-}
derivatives of functions induced by a meromorphic
(n-1)
-differential with order of zero
k+1
. In case
(n-1)\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, the term
of the Laurent series of order
(n-1)\alpha-1
is given by the
identity

z^{(n-1)\alpha-1}=\frac{\partial d_{n-1}}{\partial z}(z)-n\alpha d_{n-1}(z)z^{-1}\quad\text{for }d_{n-1}(z)=z^{(n-1)\alpha}\log|z|\,.

The differential
z^{(n-1)\alpha}\log|z|dz^{n-1}
(which is (neither meromorphic nor anti-meromorphic) gives a square integrable function, hence

z^{(n-1)\alpha-1}dz^{n}
gives a function in
\mathcal{M}^{+,-1}
.

Similar statements hold for anti-meromorphic functions induced by anti-meromorphic
n
-differentials.

We conclude that the necessary and sufficient condition for the function
f_{n}
induced by a meromorphic
n
-differential
d_{n}
to belong to the space
\mathcal{M}^{+,r}_{n}
of foliated meromorphic functions in
E^{-r}_{n}
are given by the
set of inequalities

k_{p}>(n-1)\alpha_{p}-r-1\,,\quad\text{ for all }p\in\Sigma.

A similar set of inequalities for all
p\in\Sigma
is the necessary and sufficient condition for the function
f_{n}
induced by an anti-meromorphic
n
-differential
to belong to the space
\mathcal{M}^{-,r}_{-n}
of foliated anti-meromorphic functions in
E^{-r}_{-n}
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

For every flat surface
(S,R)
with conical singularities, there exist infinite (countable) sets
Z^{\pm}_{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that

\mathcal{M}^{\pm,0}_{n}\not=\{0\}\,,\quad\text{ for all }n\in Z^{\pm}_{S}\,.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
, given any integral
vector
(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\sigma}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k_{i}=n(2g-2)\,,

the moduli space of meromorphic
n
-differentials having
\sigma
zeros or poles of orders
(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})
has dimension
2g-2+\sigma
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma})
denote the parameters
of the cone angles of
(S,R)
.
By the ergodic properties of toral translations, for every
\epsilon>0
there exists an infinite subset
Z_{S}(\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that, for all
n\in Z_{S}(\epsilon)
,

\min_{(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\sigma}}|(n-1)(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma})-(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})|<\epsilon\,.

Let
(k^{\prime}_{1},\dots,k^{\prime}_{\sigma})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\sigma}
be an integer
vector which realizes the above minimum. It follows that

|\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k^{\prime}_{i}-(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}\alpha_{\sigma}|=|\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k^{\prime}_{i}-(n-1)(2g-2)|<\sigma\epsilon\,.

For
\sigma\epsilon<1
we derive that

\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k^{\prime}_{i}=(n-1)(2g-2)\,.

For
g\geq 2
, since
2g-2\geq 0
, it follows that there exists
(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k_{i}=n(2g-2)
and, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,\sigma\}
,

k_{i}\geq k^{\prime}_{i}>(n-1)\alpha_{i}-\epsilon>n-1)\alpha_{i}-1\,.

It then follows from Theorem
5.5
(with
r=0
) that, for all
n\in Z(\epsilon)
, the spaces
\mathcal{M}^{+,0}_{n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-,0}_{-n}
have positive dimension. In fact, by the Riemann-Roch theorem, for
g\geq 2
and
n>1
the space of
n
-differentials with orders of zeros given by the vector
(k^{\prime}_{1},\dots,k^{\prime}_{\sigma})
(or higher) is at least

(2n-1)(g-1)-(n-1)(2g-2)=g-1\,.

In the case
g=1
, the surface has no conical singularities and
has integral holonomy, therefore all the spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{n}
have complex dimension
1
(the only homolomorphic or anti-holomorphic functions on complex tori are constant).

In the case
g=0
(and then
\sigma\geq 3
), by a explicit computation, or by the Riemann-Roch theorem, the space of
n
-differentials with zeros or poles of order
(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})
has dimension

-2n-\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k_{i}+1\,.

Given the ergodic properties of toral translation, since

\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}[(n-1)\alpha_{i}-1]=-2n+2-\sigma\,,

there exists an infinite set
Z_{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that, for each
n\in Z_{S}
there exists
(k_{1},\dots,k_{\sigma})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\sigma}
with
k_{i}>(n-1)\alpha_{i}-1
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,\sigma\}
and

-2n+2-\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}[(n-1)\alpha_{i}-1]<\sum_{i=1}^{\sigma}k_{i}=-2n+3-\sigma\,.

It follows that for
n\in Z_{S}
the dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{+,0}_{n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-,0}_{-n}
is at least

-2n-(2n+3-\sigma)+1=\sigma-3+1>0\,.

The argument is complete.
∎

By Theorem
5.3
we derive, as a special case, the
existence of distributional solutions of the cohomological equation with data given by foliated meromorphic or anti-meromorphic distributions vanishing on constant functions.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let us assume that the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)

satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. Given
r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\geq 0
and for every
r>r^{\prime}+1
and
s>s^{\prime}+2\gamma+3/2
, there exists a constant
C:=C(r,r^{\prime}s,s^{\prime},\gamma)>0
such that, for all meromorphic, respectively anti-meromorphic, distribution
m^{\pm}\in H^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}(M)
, vanishing on constant functions there exists a solution
U^{\pm}\in H^{-r,-s}(M)
of the cohomological equation
XU^{\pm}=m^{\pm}
such that

(23)

|U^{\pm}|_{-r,-s}\leq C|m^{\pm}|_{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}\,.

The construction of
X
-invariant distributions proceeds as follows (in analogy with the case of translation surfaces
[
3
]
). We define the following linear operators

\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
from the space of meromorphic distributions to the space
\mathcal{I}_{X}(M)
of invariant distributions.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},\pm}_{0}\subset H^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}(M)
denote
the subspace of meromorphic, respectively anti-meromorphic,
distributions vanishing to constant functions. Let

\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)\subset H^{-r,-s}(M)
be the subspace
of all
X
-invariant distributions.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let us assume that the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)

satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. Let
r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\geq 0
and for every
r>r^{\prime}+2
and
s>s^{\prime}+2\gamma+3/2
. There exist bounded linear maps
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}:\mathcal{M}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},\pm}_{0}\to\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)\subset H^{-r,-s}(M)
, defined as
follows: for every
m^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},\pm}_{0}
there
exists a unique
U^{\pm}\in H^{-(r-1),-s}(M)
, orthogonal to
\mathcal{I}^{r-1,s}_{X}(M)
, such that

XU^{\pm}=m^{\pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{D}^{\pm}(m^{\pm})=\partial^{\pm}U^{\pm}\,.

For
r^{\prime\prime}>r+2
and
s^{\prime\prime}>s+2\gamma
the maps
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
on the spaces
\mathcal{M}^{r^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime\prime},\pm}
are onto the subspace
\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)_{0}\subset\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)
of
X
-invariant distributions vanishing on constant functions.

Proof.

Let
m^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime},\pm}
a meromorphic/anti-meromorphic, distribution vanishing on constant functions. Under the condition of Corollary
5.7
there exists a solution
U^{\pm}\in H^{-(r-1),-s}(M)
of the cohomological equation
XU^{\pm}=m^{\pm}
.

The distribution
D^{\pm}:=\partial U^{\pm}\in H^{-r,-s}(M)

is
X
-invariant since (by commutation)

XD^{\pm}=X\partial U^{\pm}=\partial^{\pm}(XU^{\pm})=\partial^{\pm}m^{\pm}=0\,.

We observe that the solution of the cohomological equation

XU^{\pm}=m^{\pm}
is not uniquely defined, but defined only
up to invariant distributions in the space
H^{-(r-1),-s}(M)
.
However, we can consider the unique solution orthogonal in
the Hilbert space
H^{-(r-1),-s}(M)
to the subspace
\mathcal{I}_{X}^{r-1,s}(M)\subset H^{-(r-1),-s}(M)
of all
X
-invariant distributions. Such a solution minimizes the norm among all
solutions, therefore by the estimate in Corollary
5.7
, we have

|D^{\pm}|_{-r,-s}=|\partial^{\pm}U^{\pm}|_{-(r-1),-s}\leq|U^{\pm}|_{-(r-1),-s}\leq C|m^{\pm}|_{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}\,,

hence the operator (which is easily seen to be linear) is bounded,
as stated.

It remains to prove that the maps
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
are onto
the subspace
\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)_{0}
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)_{0}
. We first prove that
the equations
\partial^{\pm}U^{\pm}=D
have distributional solutions. In fact, by Theorem
4.6
, for all zero-average functions
v\in H^{r+2,s+2\gamma}(M)
we have

|\langle D,v\rangle|\leq|D|_{-r,-s}|v|_{r,s}\leq C_{r,s}|Hv|_{r,s+2\gamma}\leq C_{r,s}|\partial^{\pm}v|_{r+1,s+2\gamma}\,,

hence we can define a distribution
U^{\pm}
by

\langle U,\partial^{\pm}v\rangle=-\langle D,v\rangle\,,\quad\text{ for all }v\in\in H^{r+2,s+2\gamma}(M)\,,

the extended to the whole space
H^{r+1,s+2\gamma}(M)
first by
continuity for the extension to the closure of the range of the foliated Cauchy-Riemann operator in
H^{r+1,s+2\gamma}(M)
, then by orthogonality (as zero on the orthogonal complement).
We have thus constructed a solution
U^{\pm}\in H^{-(r+1),-(s+2\gamma)}(M)
of the equation
\partial^{\pm}U^{\pm}=D
. Let then

m^{\pm}=XU^{\pm}\in H^{-(r+2),-(s+2\gamma)}(M)\,.

We claim that
m^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{r+2,s+2\gamma,\pm}_{0}
and that
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}(m^{\pm})=D
. In fact,

\partial^{\pm}m^{\pm}=\partial^{\pm}(XU^{\pm})=X(\partial^{\pm}U^{\pm})=XD=0\,,

hence
m^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{r+2,s+2\gamma,\pm}
and vanish on constant functions since
m^{\pm}
are derivatives.
Finally, for any
r^{\prime\prime}>r+2
and
s^{\prime\prime}>s+2\gamma
, the distribution
m^{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}^{r^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime\prime},\pm}_{0}
and

\mathcal{D}^{\pm}(m^{\pm})=D
by the definition of the maps.
The argument is thus complete.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Let us assume that the holonomy of the flat surface
(S,R)

satisfies a simultaneous Diophantine condition of exponent
\gamma>0
. For all
r>2
and
s>3/2+\gamma
, the space
\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}\subset H^{-r,-s}(M)
of
X
-invariant distributions has countable dimension.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the kernel of the maps

\mathcal{D}^{\pm}:\mathcal{M}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},\pm}_{0}\to\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}(M)\subset H^{-r,-s}(M)

have countable codimension.
Let
m^{\pm}\in\text{Ker}(\mathcal{D}^{\pm})\cap H^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}(M)
. By the definition of the maps
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
, there exists a distribution
U^{\pm}\in H^{-(r-1),-s}(M)
such that
\partial^{\pm}U^{\pm}=0
and
XU^{\pm}=m^{\pm}
. It follows that

U^{\pm}
is a foliated meromorphic/ant-meromorphic function and

m^{\pm}=XU^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{+}+\partial^{-})U^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mp}U^{\pm}\,.

In summary if
m^{\pm}\in\text{Ker}(\mathcal{D}^{\pm})\cap H^{-r^{\prime},-s^{\prime}}(M)
, then
m^{\pm}\in\partial^{\mp}(\mathcal{M}^{(r-1),s,\pm})
. It then suffices to prove that the range of Cauchy-Riemann operators in the spaces of all foliated meromorphic/anti-meromorphic distributions has countable codimension. As we have remarked above (see formula (
22
)) on all the spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\pm,r}_{n}
of meromorphic/anti-meromorphic distributions which belong the distributional eigenspace
E^{-r}_{n}
of the operator
\Theta
, the Cauchy-Riemann operators lower by one the order of zero of the corresponding
n
-meromorphic/anti-meromorphic differential. Therefore, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
r_{n}>0
such that the
n
-meromorphic/anti-meromorphic differential of maximal total order of zero gives a foliated meromorphic/anti-meromorphic distribution
m^{\pm}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}^{\pm,r_{n}}_{n}
which does not belong to the range of the Cauchy-Riemann operator
\partial^{\mp}
, hence the corresponding invariant distributions

D^{\pm}_{n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\pm}(m^{\pm}_{n})
are non-zero. The set of distributions
\{D^{\pm}_{n}\}
is linearly independent, since otherwise there would be a linear combination of the set

\{m^{\pm}_{n}\}
which belongs to the range of the Cauchy-Riemann operator
\partial^{\mp}
. Since the Cauchy-Riemann operators respect the spectral decomposition of
\Theta
, it would follow
that each distribution
m^{\pm}_{n}
in the linear combination belongs to the range of the Cauchy-Riemann operator
\partial^{\mp}
, in contradiction with the assumption that it has
maximal order of zero.

The above argument proves that the space
\mathcal{I}_{X}
of all

X
-invariant distributions has countable dimension. The proof
that there exists
r,s>0
such that the space
\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}
of
finite order

X
-invariant distributions has
countable dimension is based on Corollary
5.6
.
In fact, by that corollary there exist infinite sets
\mathbb{Z}^{\pm}_{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathcal{M}^{\pm,0}_{n}\not=\{0\}
.
By Theorem
5.8
, the
X
-invariant distributions in
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}(\mathcal{M}^{\pm,0}_{n})
belong to the dual
Sobolev space
H^{-r,-s}(M)
for all
r>2
and
s>2\gamma+3/2
,
hence
\mathcal{I}^{r,s}_{X}
has countable dimension.
∎

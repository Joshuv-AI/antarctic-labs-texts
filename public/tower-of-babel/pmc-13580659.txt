Clinical and Patient-Reported Outcomes of Premium Intraocular Lenses in Glaucoma Patients Undergoing Combined Cataract and Minimally Invasive Glaucoma Surgery
Authors: Jack Wolsky, Christianah Jemiyo, Michael Greenwood
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPremium intraocular lenses (IOLs), including multifocal, extended depth-of-focus (EDOF), and light adjustable lenses (LALs) are used to reduce spectacle dependence after cataract surgery. However, their use in glaucoma patients remains controversial due to concerns that reduced contrast sensitivity and pre-existing visual field loss limits functional benefits from these optics. Increasing use of minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) at the time of cataract surgery has made combined refractive and glaucoma management more common, but data on real world outcomes remains limited.MethodsThis single-center, IRB-approved retrospective observational study with cross-sectional survey analysis included glaucoma patients who underwent bilateral cataract surgery with implantation of multifocal, EDOF, or LAL IOLs with concurrent MIGS from 2020 to 2025. Postoperative visual outcomes, intraocular pressure (IOP), glaucoma medication burden, spectacle dependence, dysphotopsias, and patient satisfaction were assessed. Longitudinal outcomes were analyzed using mixed-effects models adjusted for baseline glaucoma severity (mean deviation). Satisfaction was evaluated using proportional odds logistic regression adjusting for age and baseline disease severity.ResultsSeventy-nine patients (Multifocal n=43, EDOF n=24, LAL n=12) were included. All groups demonstrated significant postoperative improvement in distance visual acuity and reduction in IOP and glaucoma medication burden. Adjusted IOP trajectories and most recent IOP were similar between groups. Distance visual acuity outcomes were similar between groups after adjustment for baseline glaucoma severity. Near visual acuity favored multifocal IOLs, while LAL demonstrated the highest spectacle dependence. Dysphotopsias were more frequent with multifocal lenses and lowest in LAL. Satisfaction was high across all groups, with predicted probabilities of being “very” or “somewhat satisfied” of 94% (multifocal), 88% (EDOF), and 87% (LAL).ConclusionsIn appropriately selected glaucoma patients undergoing cataract surgery with MIGS, multifocal, EDOF, and LAL IOLs provide comparable visual outcomes, excellent IOP control, and high satisfaction. Differences in spectacle dependence and dysphotopsia support individualized IOL selection.Keywords: glaucoma, intraocular lens, patient-reported outcomes, spectacle independence, visual acuity

Introduction

In recent years, advanced intraocular lens (IOL) technologies including multifocal, extended depth-of-focus (EDOF), and light-adjustable lenses (LALs) have gained popularity due to their ability to reduce spectacle dependence following cataract surgery. These IOLs attempt to provide patients with a wider range of uncorrected vision, which improves quality of life by decreasing their reliance on glasses for distance, intermediate, and near tasks.1,2 However, this visual freedom can come with trade-offs. Multifocal and EDOF IOLs have been associated with decreased contrast sensitivity, reduced performance in low-light situations, and dysphotopsias such as glare and haloes, which negatively affect patient satisfaction following surgery.3–5

These risks raise specific concerns when considering advanced IOLs for patients with glaucoma, a progressive optic neuropathy characterized by retinal ganglion cell loss and corresponding visual field deficits.6 Glaucoma can independently affect contrast sensitivity and visual function, leading to concerns that presbyopia-correcting IOLs could increase these deficits and cause additional dissatisfaction or visual discomfort.7–9 Because of this, many surgeons have traditionally considered glaucoma a relative contraindication for presbyopia-correcting lenses, despite a growing number of studies that suggest patients may benefit from them.10,11

Additionally, the emergence of minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS) has enabled safe and effective intraocular pressure (IOP) reduction at the time of cataract surgery. Because of this, it is more common to combine glaucoma and refractive goals in a single procedure.12,13 Currently, there is limited data on real-world visual outcomes, spectacle dependence, and subjective satisfaction among glaucoma patients who undergo cataract surgery and simultaneous MIGS and are implanted with bilateral presbyopia-correcting IOLs.

The purpose of this study was to evaluate both objective and subjective visual outcomes in glaucoma patients who received bilateral implantation of multifocal, EDOF, or LAL IOLs during cataract surgery with concurrent MIGS procedures. Through a combination of retrospective chart review and a cross-sectional patient-reported outcome survey, we assessed postoperative visual acuity, spectacle dependence, presence of dysphotopsias, and overall satisfaction in this unique patient population. We hypothesize that with appropriate patient selection, glaucoma patients can achieve high satisfaction and spectacle independence with presbyopia-correcting IOLs.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This was a single-center, IRB-approved, retrospective observational study with a cross-sectional survey component. This study was conducted at Vance Thompson Vision, West Fargo, ND. Institutional Review Board approval was obtained prior to data collection, and the study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Participants

Patients who underwent bilateral cataract extraction with implantation of premium intraocular lenses (IOLs) including multifocal, extended depth-of-focus (EDOF), or light adjustable lenses (LALs) between 2020 and 2025 were eligible for inclusion. All patients underwent a minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) procedure at the time of surgery.

Inclusion criteria were age ≥ 18 years, diagnosis of glaucoma, bilateral implantation of the same premium IOL platform, and at least 3 months postoperative prior to administration of the survey.

Patients were excluded if they had significant ocular comorbidities expected to substantially limit postoperative visual outcomes or interfere with analysis, which included retinal detachment, advanced macular pathology, or severe optic neuropathy not attributed to glaucoma.

Although primary analysis was focused on patients receiving bilateral implantation of the same IOL platform, a small subset of patients received toric and non-toric variants of the same lens platform between eyes. These patients were retained to reflect real-world clinical practice. Additionally, the primary outcomes emphasized patient-reported satisfaction and functional vision rather than refractive precision alone. Inclusion of these cases therefore was not expected to significantly alter satisfaction outcomes.

Among 120 eligible patients identified through chart review, 87 completed the telephone survey. Eight patients were subsequently excluded because of incomplete or conflicting clinical data, resulting in a final study cohort of 79 patients, including 43 multifocal, 24 EDOF, and 12 LAL recipients.

Intraocular Lenses

Three premium IOL categories were evaluated: multifocal, extended depth-of-focus (EDOF), and light adjustable lenses (LALs). Surgical Technique

All surgeries included in this study were performed by one experienced cataract surgeon at a single private practice institution in West Fargo, North Dakota. The choice of IOL was determined preoperatively through shared decision-making between the surgeon and patient based on visual goals, glaucoma severity, ocular characteristics, and tolerance for potential dysphotopsias. Because IOL selection was not randomized, this reflects real-world clinical practice but may introduce selection bias.

Standard phacoemulsification cataract extraction was performed in all cases followed by in-the-bag implantation of the selected IOL. Concurrent MIGS procedures were performed based on surgeon discretion and glaucoma severity. These included G2 iStent inject, iStent infinite, Kahook Dual Blade (KDB), OMNI canaloplasty, Streamline, endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation (ECP), and Via360 procedures.

IOL power selection was targeted towards emmetropia when clinically appropriate. Corneal refractive enhancement with LASIK or PRK was offered to patients with residual postoperative refractive error. Any refractive enhancement procedures occurred more than 2 weeks before survey administration.

Data Collection

Charts were reviewed to gather demographic, preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative clinical data. Demographic variables included age, sex, and surgery date. Preoperative ocular variables included glaucoma severity metrics, visual field index (VFI), mean deviation (MD), cup-to-disc ratio, pachymetry, corneal hysteresis (ORA), baseline intraocular pressure (IOP), and number of glaucoma medications. Glaucoma severity was classified using the ICD-10-based severity classification documented in the medical record as mild, moderate, or severe. Additional variables included previous LASIK or PRK prior to surgery, IOL brand and model, concurrent MIGS procedures, postoperative YAG capsulotomy, and postoperative refractive enhancements.

Postoperative measurements were collected at postoperative day 1, week 1, month 1, month 3, month 6, and year 1 when available. Distance visual acuity (DVSC), near visual acuity (NVSC), IOP, and glaucoma medication burden were recorded at each visit.

Patient Survey

Eligible patients were contacted by telephone between July 7 and July 14, 2025, and invited to complete an 11-question structured survey regarding postoperative visual experience. All participants were at least 3 months postoperative at the time of survey administration.

The survey evaluated overall satisfaction with vision, glare and halo frequency and bother, spectacle dependence during common daily visual activities, and willingness to choose the same IOL again. Visual activities included daytime driving, nighttime driving, computer use, mobile phone use, television viewing, and reading small print.

Overall satisfaction was assessed using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “very satisfied” to “very dissatisfied”. Glare frequency and glare bother were similarly assessed using 5-point scales. Spectacle dependence for each activity was graded from “never” to “all the time”, with an additional option indicating the activity was not performed. Responses regarding willingness to choose the same IOL again were recorded as “yes”, “no”, or “maybe”. Survey responses were linked to corresponding clinical data for integrated analysis.

The survey was developed based on commonly assessed patient-reported outcome domains in cataract and refractive surgery, including satisfaction, dysphotopsias, and spectacle independence. Although the survey was not formally validated, the content was designed to reflect clinically relevant postoperative visual experiences routinely assessed in clinical practice. The complete survey is provided in Supplementary Appendix 1.

Ethics Approval and Informed Consent

This study was reviewed and approved by the University of North Dakota Institutional Review Board (IRB Project Number: IRB0006738; Review Level: Expedited, Categories 5 and 7). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Given the minimal-risk design and telephone-based survey methodology, the University of North Dakota Institutional Review Board approved the use of verbal informed consent for the survey component of the study. Verbal informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data collection. Participants were provided with a standardized script explaining the purpose of the study, the voluntary nature of participation, the estimated time commitment, and the potential risks and benefits. Participants were informed that their responses would remain confidential and that they could decline to answer questions or withdraw at any time without penalty. Contact information for the study team and the Institutional Review Board was provided.

Verbal consent was confirmed prior to survey initiation and documented by the study team by recording the date, time, and initials of the individual obtaining consent.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses followed standardized data cleaning and variable harmonization procedures. Raw data were imported from Excel, variable names were standardized, multiple representations of missingness were normalized to NA, and invalid subject identifiers were excluded. Binary clinical variables were recoded as interpretive factors, and patient-reported outcomes were converted to ordered categorical factors preserving clinical meaning.

Descriptive statistics were reported as means (SD) for continuous variables and frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Group differences between Multifocal, EDOF, and LAL IOLs were assessed using Kruskal–Wallis rank-sum tests for continuous variables and Pearson’s Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables where appropriate.

Longitudinal outcomes including intraocular pressure (IOP), distance visual acuity (DVSC), and glaucoma medication burden were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models with random intercepts to account for repeated measurements over time. Baseline glaucoma severity, measured by mean deviation (MD), was included as an adjustment covariate in all models. Estimated marginal means were calculated to generate adjusted outcome trajectories by IOL group.

Visual and clinical outcomes were displayed using boxplots, longitudinal line plots, stacked bar charts, and correlation heatmaps. Relationships among continuous clinical variables were evaluated using Spearman correlation analysis.

Model diagnostics included residual-versus-fitted plots to assess homoscedasticity and Q–Q plots to assess normality of residuals. Mild heteroskedasticity and tail deviations were considered acceptable given the bounded nature of logMAR and biological variability of IOP.

Patient-reported satisfaction outcomes were additionally analyzed using proportional odds logistic regression adjusting for age and baseline glaucoma severity. Predicted probabilities of being “very satisfied” or “somewhat satisfied” were generated by IOL group.

Missing data patterns were formally assessed prior to analysis. Variables with greater than 70% missingness were excluded from inferential modeling. No direct imputation was performed because of the retrospective observational design. Available-case analyses were performed at each postoperative time point, with the number of eyes contributing to each analysis reported by IOL group in Supplementary Table 1.

All statistical tests performed using two-sided testing with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05.

No formal a priori sample size or power calculation was performed because of the retrospective observational design. Emphasis was placed on reporting effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals for regression-based associations to the magnitude and precision of key findings.

Patient-Reported Outcomes

Frequency of glare and haloes was highest in the Multifocal group, intermediate in the EDOF group, and lowest in the LAL group. Multifocal patients were most likely to report being at least somewhat bothered by these dysphotopsias, followed by LAL patients, with EDOF patients least likely to report being somewhat bothered (Figure 9). Discussion

This study provides a clinical and patient-reported evaluation of premium intraocular lens implantation in glaucoma patients and integrates both unadjusted and adjusted longitudinal outcome analyses. Our findings demonstrate that all three premium IOL platforms achieve substantial visual improvement, durable IOP reduction, and high patient satisfaction when implanted concurrently with glaucoma surgery.

Longitudinal mixed-effects modeling confirmed a clear postoperative reduction in IOP across all IOL types, with early postoperative variability followed by long-term stabilization. Although LAL demonstrated the lowest adjusted IOP at 1 year, this likely reflects sample size differences rather than a true lens-specific pressure advantage. The consistency of IOP control across IOL types reinforces that postoperative IOP is primarily driven by the glaucoma procedure and cataract extraction rather than IOL optical design.

Distance visual acuity improved rapidly in all groups within the first postoperative week. After adjusting for baseline glaucoma severity, all groups achieved comparable long-term DVSC. LAL showed a slight early lag but demonstrated excellent adjusted outcomes by one month. These findings suggest that preoperative visual field loss rather than IOL type is the dominant determinant of early postoperative distance acuity.

Near and intermediate performance demonstrated expected optical trade-offs. Multifocal lenses provided superior non-distance vision, while LAL demonstrated the greatest need for spectacle correction. This finding is consistent with the design of the LAL, which enables postoperative refractive optimization instead of relying on diffractive or multifocal optical splitting. Prior studies have shown that non-diffractive and monofocal-based presbyopia correcting lenses tend to reduce dysphotopsias and preserve contrast sensitivity at the expense of reduced spectacle independence compared to multifocal or trifocal systems.20,23,25 Dysphotopsia burden followed expected patterns, with Multifocal lenses demonstrating higher glare frequency, followed by EDOF, with LAL patients experiencing the fewest dysphotopsias. When clinically meaningful bother by these dysphotopsias was considered, 47% of Multifocal patients, 42% of LAL patients, and 29% of EDOF patients reported being at least somewhat bothered. This demonstrates the importance of patient preference and education during lens selection.

Patient-reported satisfaction remained uniformly high after adjustment for age and glaucoma severity. The adjusted probability of being very or somewhat satisfied exceeded 85% in all groups. While Multifocal lenses demonstrated the highest predicted satisfaction, differences were not clinically meaningful.

Collectively, these findings support the safe use of premium IOLs in well-selected glaucoma patients and reinforce that premium IOL selection should be individualized based on visual priorities, tolerance for dysphotopsia, and desire for spectacle independence. Further research may explore differences between glaucoma and non-glaucoma patients when evaluating for visual outcomes, spectacle dependence, dysphotopsias, and patient satisfaction.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design may limit causal inference. Second, lens choice was not randomized, instead resulting from shared decision-making between the surgeon and patients, which may introduce selection bias. Third, no formal a priori power calculation was completed, and the sample size may have limited the ability to detect smaller differences, particularly in non-significant trends and subgroup analyses. Fourth, incomplete long-term follow-up resulted in missing postoperative data at later visits, although mixed-effects modeling was used to mitigate the impact of missingness without direct imputation. Fifth, postoperative interventions such as YAG capsulotomy and laser enhancement may contribute to variability in outcomes; however, this reflects common clinical management patterns and was accounted for in the analysis where appropriate. Finally, although the survey instrument was created based on clinically relevant domains routinely assessed in cataract and refractive practice, it was not formally validated.

Conclusion

After adjustment for baseline glaucoma severity and patient age, Multifocal, EDOF, and Light Adjustable lenses all provide excellent postoperative visual outcomes, durable IOP control, and high patient satisfaction in glaucoma patients undergoing combined cataract and glaucoma surgery. Multifocal IOLs maximize near visual performance at the expense of increased dysphotopsias, whereas LAL minimizes the frequency of glare and haloes but requires greater spectacle dependence. These data support expanded, individualized use of premium IOL technologies in appropriately selected glaucoma patients.

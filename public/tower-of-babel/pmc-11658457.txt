Asymmetrically PEGylated and amphipathic heptamethine indocyanine dyes potentiate radiotherapy of renal cell carcinoma via mitochondrial targeting
Authors: Zifei Wu, Xie Huang, Chuan Wu, Yan Zhou, Mingquan Gao, Shenglin Luo, Qiang Xiang, Weidong Wang, Rong Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Enhancing the sensitivity of radiotherapy (RT) towards renal cell carcinoma (RCC) remains a challenge because RCC is a radioresistant tumor. In this work, we design and report asymmetrically Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)ylated and amphipathic heptamethine indocyanine dyes for efficient radiosensitization of RCC treatment. They were synthesized by modifying different lengths of PEG chains as hydrophilic moieties on one N-alkyl chain of a mitochondria-targeting heptamethine indocyanine dye (IR-808), and a radiosensitizer 2-nitroimidazole (NM) as a hydrophobic moiety on another N-alkyl chain. The PEG modification significantly improved water solubility, decreased the intermolecular π–π large aggregates, thereby enhanced renal excretion. The asymmetrical and amphipathic modification enhanced the preferential accumulation in renal tumors through self-assembly into small-size nanoparticles in aqueous environment. Radiosensitization was further improved by preferential accumulation in renal tumor cells and their mitochondria as mitochondria play a crucial role in rapid cancer cell growth, metastasis, and RT resistance. Additionally, the modification also increased the abilities of fluorescence emission and photostability, which is meaningful for imaging-guided precise RCC RT. Therefore, our findings may present a theranostic radiosensitizer for renal tumor-targeted imaging and radiosensitization.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12951-024-03012-3.

Introduction

Globally, renal cell carcinoma (RCC) affects nearly 300,000 individuals annually, resulting in over 100,000 deaths each year. Radiotherapy (RT) is the mainstay treatment for patients with RCC, particularly those ineligible for surgery. However, RCC is a radioresistant tumor that requires a continuous high dosage of radiation exposure, inevitably increasing the risk of damage to healthy tissues. Hence, enhancing the sensitivity and selectivity of RCC in RT is vital [1–3]. Radiosensitizers are a class of molecules or materials that can sensitize cancer cells to RT. Their primary mechanisms include the following: (I) inhibiting the radiation-induced repair of DNA damage or increasing the degree of DNA damage; (II) disrupting the cell cycle and organelle function to improve cytotoxicity; and (III) inhibiting the expression of radiation-resistant genes or promoting the expression of radiation-sensitive genes [4]. Nitroimidazoles are a promising class of oxygen-mimetic radiosensitizers that can mitigate hypoxia-induced radioresistance [5]. The electron-affinic nitro group in the imidazole structure makes it vulnerable to oxygen species radicals induced via ionizing radiation. The resulting radical adducts have much higher reactivity to DNA, leading to DNA strand breaks, self-repair failure, and programmed cell death [6–8]. However, nitroimidazole derivatives often exhibit high uptake and metabolic ratio by livers following administration because of poor water solubility. Consequently, the in vivo drug distribution of nitroimidazoles accumulated in kidneys is much low, limiting the therapeutic outcome [9–11].

Polyethylene glycol (PEG) is a polymer with remarkable water solubility and good biocompatibility [12], and its modification can extend blood circulation time, alter drug pharmacokinetics, and enhance drug bioavailability [13, 14]. PEG modification is a hot research topic in drug design and development [15–17]. Various PEGylated agents are currently used in clinical practice, including PEGylated doxorubicin liposomes, PEGylated irinotecan liposomes, PEG-asparaginase, and PEG-antihemophilic factor [18]. Particularly in the context of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, there has been a significant increase in the approval of PEGylated vaccines for marketing purposes [19, 20]. PEGylation has been extensively investigated for the development of anticancer drugs due to its ability to modify physicochemical properties and reduce cytotoxicity, and thereby promote therapeutic efficacy [21, 22]. Moreover, recent reports suggest that PEGylation markedly promotes drug clearance via the renal metabolic pathway, increasing the likelihood of the accumulation of anticancer drugs and their prototypes in RCC cancer cells [23, 24].

Mitochondria are vital subcellular organelles that play a central role in energy production and cell survival. In particular, tumor cell mitochondria have been identified as closely related to cancer metastasis and RT resistance. Thus, the specific delivery of nitroimidazole radiosensitizers to cancer cell mitochondria can significantly improve the RT effect. Recently, a mitochondria-targeting nitroimidazole, 808-NM2, has been identified to preferentially accumulate in tumor cells and effectively inhibit breast tumor growth synergistically via chemotherapy and RT [25]. 808-NM2 was prepared by chemically conjugating nitroimidazoles with a mitochondria-targeting heptamethine cyanine dye (IR-808) which has also been extensively evidenced with tumor-preferential accumulation [26–28]. Although 808-NM2 has the potential to be a radiosensitizer, its application for RCC is hindered by its strong hydrophobicity and low renal accumulation.

In this study, the mitochondria-targeted 808-NM2 was modified with different molecular weights of PEG (PEG-550 and PEG-2000), aiming to improve water solubility, enhance RCC accumulation, and radiosensitization. The structures of the newly designed PEG-808-NM2 molecules (PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2) are shown in Scheme 1. Unlike 808-NM2, one side chain of PEG-808-NM2 is a long hydrophilic PEG linker, whereas the other side chain is a hydrophobic ending with 2-nitroimidazole. In our design, their amphiphilic and asymmetrical structures are anticipated not only to improve water solubility but also to facilitate self-assembly in aqueous solution to form nanoparticles with small sizes. Recent studies have acknowledged that the size of nanoparticles below 200 nm is more prone to kidney excretion [29]. Experimental section

Chemicals and materials

2-Nitroimidazole, 1,6-dibromohexane, 2,3,3-trimethylindole, PEG monomethyl ether (PEG-550, PEG-2000), methylene chloride, and methanol were purchased from Adamas. Ethanol, n-hexane, ethyl acetate, toluene, n-butanol, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and sodium acetate were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. N,N-dimethylformamide was obtained from Aladdin. All reagents were used directly without further purification. The synthesis of PEG550-808-NM2, PEG2000-808-NM2, and 808-NM2 are described in detail in Supplementary Information.

Characterization

PEG550-808-NM2, PEG2000-808-NM2, and 808-NM2 were structurally characterized using 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy (Agilent 600 MHz NMR spectrometer) and mass spectrometry (Bruker Ultraflextreme MALDI-TOF system). These three compounds were accurately weighed, dissolved in DMSO to form a reserve solution of 10 mg·mL−1, and stored at − 20 °C for subsequent use. The ultraviolet (UV) absorption spectra were measured using a UV–Visible NIR spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, UV-3600), and the fluorescence emission spectra were recorded using a fluorescence spectrometer (Lumina Fluorescence Spectrometer, Thermo Fisher, USA). The line graphs were plotted using Origin 2019 software.

Cell culture

The normal human kidney-2 (HK-2, #CRL-2190) cell line and Renal Carcinoma cell line (RENCA, #CRL-2947) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection. Following the manufacturer’s recommendation, cells were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, #10099141C) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (HyClone, #SV30010) in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Cell proliferation imaging was captured using a fluorescence microscope (DM3000, Leica, Germany).

Subcellular localization

To determine the intracellular distribution of PEGylated 808-NM2 in RENCA cells, a costaining experiment was conducted using commercially available Mito-Tracker (Invitrogen, #7512). RENCA cells were seeded in a confocal dish for 24 h, incubated with PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2 for 6 h, and stained with Mito-Tracker Red CMXROs (1:5000). After 30 min incubation, they were washed multiple times with PBS and resuspended in fresh media. Images were captured using confocal laser scanning microscopy.

DNA damage assay

RENCA cells were inoculated in 6-well flat-bottom plates (2 × 105 cells per well) and incubated for 24 h. Subsequently, 0.5 μg·mL−1 of 808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2 were added to the plates and incubated for 6 h. The cells were washed with RPMI-1640, followed by 6 Gy X-ray irradiation, and incubation for 24 h. After digestion and centrifugation, cell concentration was adjusted to 106–107 cells·mL−1 using a comet assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng, #G010-1-1) following the manufacturer’s protocol, including plate preparation, cell lysis, DNA base deconvolution, single-cell electrophoresis, result observation, and data processing.

Detection of reactive oxygen species

RENCA cells were seeded on 6-well plates (2 × 105 cells·mL−1) and incubated for 24 h. Subsequently, 0.5 μg·mL−1 PEG2000-808-NM2, 0.5 μg·mL−1 808-NM2, and RPMI-1640 were separately added into two plates and incubated for 12 h. A reactive oxygen species (ROS) fluorescence probe (2′, 7′-dichlorofluorescein diacetate, DCFH-DA, Beyotime, #S0033S) was diluted in serum-free medium at 1:1000, resulting in a final concentration of 10 μM·L−1. Following removal of the cell culture medium, the cells were supplemented with 1 mL of DCFH-DA, incubated for 30 min, and washed thrice with RPMI 1640. The three groups were exposed to 6 Gy X-ray irradiation and relocated for observation using a conventional fluorescence microscope.

RENCA cells were cultured, frequently observed, and the experiment was stopped when visible colonies of clones could be observed. The cells were washed twice with PBS and fixed with 5 mL of 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 15 min. Western blot

1 × 106 cells were seeded in 6-well plate and incubated overnight. They were treated with different treatments. After that, cells were washed with PBS and the pellets were lysed in a buffer containing Triton X-100 and protease inhibitors. Proteins were recovered and separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. Then, proteins were transferred to a PVDF membrane and blocked with 5% fat-free milk. The primary antibodies (dilution) were incubated overnight at 4 °C, as follows: Caspase 3 (CST, 1/1000), Bax (CST, 1/1000), Bcl-2 (Proteintech, 1/1000), followed by secondary antibody incubation. Finally, the proteins were detected using ECL reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Animal experiments

Female BALB/c mice (18–20 g, 7 weeks old) were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Center of Third Military Medical University (Chongqing, China). All animal experimental protocols received approval from the Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Third Military Medical University (Approval No. AMUMEC20210301).

Tumor model and targeting imaging

Renal carcinoma tumors in mice were established by subcutaneously injecting 5 × 106 RENCA cells near the hind limbs of mice. The mice were housed in filter-topped cages and provided sterile food and water in a specific pathogen-free facility. Fourteen days after the establishment of the tumor-bearing mouse model, BALB/c mice were selected for in vivo tumor-targeting imaging, with the tumor volume at approximately 100 mm3. Furthermore, 808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2 were injected into the tail vein at a concentration of 5 mg·kg−1 and a volume of 200 μL (five mice in each group). NIR fluorescence imaging of in vivo or ex vivo tumor tissues was conducted using the Pearl® Trilogy system (Gene Co., Ltd.) at different times (0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 12, 24, 36, 48, and 72 h) following drug administration.

In vivo radiotherapy

In addition, renal carcinoma tumors in mice were established by subcutaneously injecting 5 × 106 RENCA cells near their hind limbs. Statistical analysis

All statistical data were analyzed using Origin (Version 2019, Origin Lab, USA) and GraphPad Prism (Version 9.0, La Jolla, USA) software. Unless otherwise noted, the data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation with at least three independent experiments. A one-way analysis of variance and a t-test were used to test the differences. The cell survival fraction was plotted using a nonlinear regression multitarget single-hitting model. *p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were considered highly significant.

Results and discussion

Chemical synthesis and characterization of PEG-808-NM2

To improve water solubility and enhance the renal cancer accumulation of 808-NM2, we designed and synthesized PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2 by modifying different lengths of PEG moieties (PEG-550 or PEG-2000) on one side of the N-alkyl chain of 808-NM2 [25]. However, obtaining their PEGlyated derivatives through direct chemical modification is impossible, based on the structure of 808-NM2. Therefore, a de novo synthetic route was designed utilizing 2-nitroimidazole and PEG as starting materials. As shown in Fig. 1, the nitroimidazole-modified intermediate 1b and PEG-modified intermediates 1f/1 g were synthesized via bromination and substitution, respectively. Finally, two PEGlyated 808-NM2 compounds (PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2) were obtained using two condensation reactions, which were similar to the synthesis of 808-NM2. The structures of these compounds were characterized using 1H NMR spectroscopy, as described in Supporting Information (Figures S1-S3). Moreover, mass spectra were also measured, and it showed a normal distribution with a difference of 44 Da between each unit, indicating the typical characteristic of PEGylation (Figure S4, Supporting Information) [33]. Moreover, superimposed 1H NMR spectra of compounds PEG2000, 1b and PEG2000-808-NM2 demonstrated that the newly synthesized compound PEG2000-808-NM2 possessed the characteristic peaks of PEG2000 and indole quaternary ammonium salt (Figure S5, Supporting Information). NIR optical properties and water solubility of PEG-808-NM2

To evaluate the photochemical properties of PEG550-808-NM2/PEG2000-808-NM2, we conducted ultraviolet (UV) absorption and fluorescence emission experiments. First, the concentrations of PEG550-808-NM2, PEG2000-808-NM2, and 808-NM2 were adjusted to 10 μg·mL−1 in methanol (MeOH) and PBS, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2A, compared with 808-NM2, PEGylated compounds (PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2) exhibited an enhanced NIR absorption in PBS, indicating improved dispersion in aqueous solution. In contrast, 808-NM2 showed the highest absorption intensity in methanol, which is attributed to its strong hydrophobicity (Figures S6-S7, Supporting Information). Further evidence was presented to reveal the relationship between absorbance and hydrophilicity. As shown in Fig. 2B, at concentrations of 1.25 μg·mL−1 and below, 808-NM2 exhibited a higher degree of dispersion in PBS, as evidenced by the presence of relatively higher monomer peaks (M-peaks) [34]. However, with increasing concentrations, the formation of H-type dimers and subsequent dimer peaks (D-peaks) rapidly increased due to π–π interactions. The introduction of PEG550 in PEG550-808-NM2 enhanced the molecular hydrophilicity and reduced intermolecular aggregation at high concentrations. However, notably, PEG550-808-NM2 still tended to aggregate at higher concentrations, which manifested as constantly strengthening D-peaks (Fig. 2C). Impressively, PEG2000-808-NM2 exhibited the highest hydrophilicity in PBS. Figure 2D demonstrates that at both low and high concentrations, the M-peak consistently surpassed the D-peak and exhibited a sharp peak, which might attribute to the longer PEG chain [34]. To assess fluorescence features, their fluorescence emission in different solvents were also determined. As shown in Fig. 2E, PEG2000-808-NM2 showed the strongest fluorescence emission in aqueous solution (PBS). This result was also confirmed by acquiring NIR images with a small animal NIR imaging system (Figures S8-S9, Supporting Information). According to Table 1, PEG2000-808-NM2 also exhibited the larger stokes shift, higher extinction coefficient and stronger fluorescent quantum yield, compared to 808-NM2 and PEG550-808-NM2. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that PEG2000-808-NM2 with a longer PEG chain formed self-assembly spherical nanoparticles with a diameter of approximately 50 nm which was less than hydrodynamic size (220 nm) after dispersing in water for 24 h (Fig. 2F). These phenomena might be ascribed to the effect of aggregation, which led to the hydrodynamic size being larger than that measured by TEM. Besides, zeta potential was also measured to be 30.6 mV (Figure S10, Supporting Information). However, PEG550-808-NM2 could not form nanoparticles under the same conditions, suggesting that the longer PEG chain possesses superior self-assembly capability and generates smaller nanoparticles in an aqueous solution (Figures S11-S13, Supporting Information). This characteristic could promote preferential accumulation in renal cancer cells through the enhanced permeation and retention (EPR) effect.

Self-assembly molecular dynamics simulation of PEG-808-NM2

To elucidate the self-assembly mechanism of PEG2000-808-NM2, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted in an aqueous environment. Each simulation box contains five independent PEG2000-808-NM2 molecules and then performs a MD simulation for 100 ns. The molecular structure (Fig. 3A) was captured per 10 ns as a representative conformation in the PEG2000-808-NM2 simulation system. As shown in Fig. 3B, PEG2000-808-NM2 molecules rapidly self-assembled to form a nanocluster at 40 ns and became stable at 50 ns. The resulting nanocluster remained unchanged for the subsequent 50 ns except its position varied.Fig. 3The supramolecular assembly process of PEG2000-808-NM2 in water by MD simulations

The supramolecular assembly process of PEG2000-808-NM2 in water by MD simulations

Notably, hydrogen bonds between PEG-808-NM2 and water were further simulated and found that the number of hydrogen bonds gradually reduced in the first 50 ns of the simulation and tended to keep stable after longer simulation times (Figure S14, Supporting Information). The findings indicate a complex system was gradually formed because of self-assembly, which is consistent to previous reports [35]. Furthermore, during the whole MD simulations process, the density, potential, pressure, and temperature kept stabilized, suggesting a successful and credible self-assembly progress (Figure S15, Supporting Information). According to previous literature, [36, 37] the self-assembly of PEG-808-NM2 may be facilitated by non-covalent hydrophobic interactions and π-π stacking, attributing by 808-NM2 with a hydrophobic polymethine link and two indocyanine rings, and by a long hydrophilic PEG unit, respectively.

The findings demonstrated that the uptake levels of PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2 were significantly greater in RENCA cells than in HK-2 cells (p < 0.01; Fig. 4A–C). To further investigate the intracellular distribution of PEGylated 808-NM2 in RENCA cells, a costaining experiment was conducted using commercially available Mito-Tracker. The results showed a significant overlap between the red fluorescence of PEGylated 808-NM2 and the green fluorescence of Mito-Tracker (Fig. 4D), indicating good accumulation of PEGylated 808-NM2 in mitochondria. Furthermore, analysis conducted using Image J software revealed high Pearson correlation coefficients and Manders colocalization coefficients for both PEG550-808-NM2 and PEG2000-808-NM2 with Mito-Tracker. Specifically, PEG550-808-NM2 exhibited a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.94, whereas PEG2000-808-NM2 exhibited a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.95. These high values indicate that the synthesized compounds retain good mitochondrial targeting.

Furthermore, we evaluated effect of hypoxic conditions on the uptake of PEG2000-808-NM2. The results showed that hypoxic conditions increased the uptake of PEG2000-808-NM2 in RENCA cells compared with normal oxygen conditions (Fig. 4E–F). This finding implies a considerable elevation in tumor uptake within hypoxic environments when nitroimidazole moieties are present, revealing a mechanism that primarily accumulates in hypoxic tumors. Nitroimidazole represents a widely investigated hypoxic radiosensitizer [38–40]. The eletrophilicity of nitro group is engineered to interact with the DNA free radicals generated by ionizing radiation in a similar way to mimic oxygen. The generation of such adducts leads to the breakage of DNA strands, stabilizing the damage and impeding the cells from accomplishing their self-repair processes, thereby hastening cell death [25]. In addition, under hypoxic conditions, nitroimidazole undergoes reduction reaction to generate active hydroxylamine. This active hydroxylamine then binds to cellular macromolecules including DNA and proteins, consequently entrapping the drug within the hypoxic cell [41]. This characteristic leads to increased uptake of nitroimidazole-modified PEG2000-808-NM2 under hypoxic conditions.

To quantitatively analyze the radiosensitization effect, we utilized GraphPad Prism 7 software to calculate several commonly used parameters that reflected this effect (Fig. 6B). Based on these curves, the SER of PEG2000-808-NM2 was calculated to be 1.40. Typically, a higher SER indicates increased sensitivity to irradiation following treatment. The SER of paclitaxel, a clinically available radiosensitizer, was 1.32 in lung cancer cells [42]. Therefore, the radiosensitization effect of PEG2000-808-NM2 was higher than that of paclitaxel.

In DNA damage assays, higher values of TDNA %, tail moment (TM), and olive tail moment (OTM) indicate more severe radiation damage to cells. In Fig. 6C–F, compared with other treatment groups, the TDNA %, TM, and OTM values were significantly higher in the PEG2000-808-NM2 radiation group (p < 0.01). The above results verified that PEG2000-808-NM2 significantly enhanced radiation-induced DNA damage, underscoring its potent radiosensitization effect in vitro.

Imaging of tumor targeting and tissue distribution

Given its excellent NIR fluorescent properties, the cancer-targeting capability of PEG2000-808-NM2 was evaluated in RENCA-bearing tumor xenografts (Fig. 7A). In vivo NIR imaging showed that high fluorescence signals were observed in tumors 24 h after PEG2000-808-NM2 injection, indicating preferential tumor accumulation and NIR fluorescence imaging properties. Notably, higher fluorescence intensity and prolonged circulation time were observed in the PEG2000-808-NM2 group compared with the 808-NM2 group. The fluorescence intensities of the main organs and tumors were calculated after the mice being eliminated (Fig. 7B). The fluorescence tumor-to-muscle and kidney-to-muscle ratios in the PEG2000-808-NM2 group increased over time and were significantly higher than those in the 808-NM2 group (Fig. 7C–D and Figure S20, Supporting Information). These findings confirm that PEG2000-808-NM2 preferentially accumulates in tumors and is subsequently cleared through renal metabolism. Radiosensitization of PEG2000-808-NM2 in vivo

We investigated the use of PEG2000-808-NM2 as a radiosensitizer for in vivo tumor therapy, considering its significant alleviation of hypoxia and enhanced RT effects observed in vitro. Based on the data from our in vivo therapy experiments, we observed that the growth of tumors in mice treated with PEG2000-808-NM2 (20 mg·kg−1) combined with X-ray irradiation (6 Gy) was significantly inhibited (Fig. 8A–D). Meanwhile, PEG2000-808-NM2 (20 mg·kg−1)-mediated RT resulted in prolonged suppression of tumor growth and a higher survival rate compared with the other treatment groups (Fig. 8A). The body weight of mice in the various treatment groups showed no significant change compared with the control group (Fig. 7B). Pathological verification of therapeutic efficacy was conducted using hematoxylin and eosin staining following different treatments. Compared to the control group, H&E and TUNEL staining of tumor slices from mice in group of PEG2000-808-NM2 (20 mg·kg−1) with X-ray irradiation (6 Gy) exhibited apparent cell apoptosis and necrosis (Fig. 8E and Figure S21). Moreover, no significant inflammatory response or organ damage was observed in the main organs of the mice (Figure S22, Supporting Information). Due to substantial accumulation of PEG2000-808-NM2 in the liver and lungs, the potential toxic effects on liver toxicity (ALT, AST and ALP levels) and pulmonary toxicity (inflammatory cytokine levels) were evaluated. There were no significant changes in these parameters, further supporting the safety of PEG2000-808-NM2 (Tables S1–S2, Supporting Information). Hemolysis analysis demonstrated excellent hemocompatibility of PEG2000-808-NM2, even at relatively high concentrations, (Figures S23-S24, Supporting Information). Conclusion

In this work, PEGylated heptamethine indocyanine dyes with nitroimidazole moieties were successfully synthesized and evaluated for the sensitization of RCC to RT. The results showed that the asymmetric and amphipathic modification endowed PEG2000-808-NM2 self-assembly abilities to form water-soluble nanoparticles in aqueous environments, prolonged the circulation time in vivo and enhanced its preferential accumulation in renal tumors. The highly efficient accumulation in renal tumors is partly attributed to improved renal excretion because PEG2000-808-NM2 may have higher probability to be retained by renal tumors, which are often surrounded by abundant and immature vessels. The SER of PEG2000-808-NM2 was calculated to be 1.40, surpassing that of paclitaxel, a clinically available radiosensitizer. This efficient radiosensitization potentially occurs due to its preferential accumulation in renal tumor cells and their mitochondria, known for their involvement in rapid cancer cell growth and RT resistance. Additionally, PEG modification significantly increased the fluorescence intensity, providing a platform for imaging-guided precise RT. In summary, PEG2000-808-NM2 exhibited preferential renal tumor accumulation, NIR fluorescence imaging capability, and efficient radiosensitization for RCC. This multifunctional theranostic radiosensitizer holds promise for personalized treatment in RCC and other solid tumors.

An Operadic Generalization of the Gerstenhaber-Shack Theorem
Andy Yu

Abstract
A simplicial cochain complex can be derived from a locally small poset by taking the nerve of the poset viewed as a category. We show that the simplicial cochain complex and a relative Hochschild cochain complex of the incidence algebra of the poset are isomorphic as operads with multiplications. This result implies that the hG-algebras derived from those operads are isomorphic, which is a generalization of the Gerstenhaber-Shack theorem. The isomorphism also induces a differential graded Lie algebra isomorphism, which we use to compute the moduli space of formal deformations of the incidence algebra.

. That is, for
f,g\in G[1]
,
[f,g]:=f\circ g-(-1)^{|f||g|}g\circ f
is a Lie bracket. Since the bracket is completely construc determinedrace operation, the differential graded Lie algebras (DGLA) derived from isomorphic operads with multiplications are isomorphic, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

\Phi:(C^{\bullet}(P;R)[1],;k-],d)\rightarrow(C^{\bullet}(RP,S;RPk[1],[k,-],d)
is a DGLA isomorphism. ∎

sectionModuli Space of Deformations of Incidence Algebra

The Maurer-Cartan space
\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
, also known as the nerve of a DGLA
\mathfrak{g}
, is invariant under quasi-isomorphisms up to homotopy equivalence
[
Cos09
]
,
[
Get09
]
. We introduce the Maurer-Cartan space and use it compute the formal deformations of the incidence algebra
kP
using the big Witt vectors
W
. We show the moduli space of formal deformations of
kP
,
\pi_{0}(\MC_{\bullet}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]]))
, is isomorphic
H^{2}(P;W)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Omega_{\bullet}
be the simplicial differential graded commutative algebra (DGCA) defined by

\Omega_{n}:=\frac{R[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n},dt_{0},\ldots,dt_{n}]}{\big(\sum t_{i}=1,\sum dt_{i}=0\big)},\qquad\deg(t_{i})=0,\deg(dt_{i})=1.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a DGLA over
k
. Let the
Maurer-Cartan set
of
\mathfrak{g}
be defined by

\MC(\mathfrak{g}):=\{a\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}\mid da+\frac{1}{2}[a,a]=0\}.

The equation
da+\frac{1}{2}[a,a]=0
is called the
Maurer-Cartan equation
. Let us assume that
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
is a pro-nilpotent Lie algebra over
k
. We associate with it the group
\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{0})
of formal symbols

\exp(x)
,
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, with multiplication given by the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula
[
KS02
, Section 1.4]
. The group
\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{0})
is called the
gauge transformation group
and has a natural action on
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
. See more discussions at
[
Man22
, Chapter 6]
and
[
GM88
, Section 2]
.

The
derived
(
Maurer-Cartan
)
moduli space
, or
nerve
, of
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}):=\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{R}\Omega_{\bullet}).

Let

\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g}):=\pi_{0}(\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}))

be the
(
Maurer-Cartan
)
moduli space
, which can be identified with the orbit space
\MC(\mathfrak{g})/\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{0})

[
Get09
]
. This is viewed here as a pointed set with base point 0.

Definition 2.7
.

A
formal deformation
on an associative algebra
A
is an associative
k[[\lambda]]
-linear map
F\colon A[[\lambda]]\otimes_{k[[\lambda]]}A[[\lambda]]\rightarrow A[[\lambda]]
of the form

F=m_{A}+\lambda F_{1}+\lambda^{2}F_{2}+\dots,

where
m_{A}:A\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow A
is the original product on
A
and, for each
n\geq 1
,
F_{n}\colon A\otimes_{R}A\to A
is an
k
-linear map, extended by
\lambda
-linearity, such that and
F(1,a)=F(a,1)=a
for all
a\in A
.
Two deformations
F
,
F^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there is an
k[[\lambda]]
-algebra isomorphism
f\colon(A[[\lambda]],F^{\prime})\to(A[[\lambda]],F)
of the form
f=\id_{A}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}f_{n}\lambda^{n}
.

Any formal deformation of the incidence algebra
A=kP
may be written as

F=m_{kP}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}F_{n}\lambda^{n}\in C^{2}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]].

(4)

with
F_{n}\in C^{2}(kP;kP)
.
Then the associativity condition on
F
may be written as

F(F(a,b),c)=F(a,F(b,c))\qquad\text{for all $a,b,c\in kP$}.

This is equivalent to the condition that
F\in\mathcal{E}nd_{kP}(2)[[\lambda]]
is an operad multiplication:

F\circ F=0\quad\text{or, equivalently,}\quad[F,F]=0,

(5)

where the circle product and bracket are extended from
C^{2}(kP;kP)
to
C^{2}(kP;\linebreak kP)[[\lambda]]
by
\lambda
-linearity.
Since the Hochschild differential may be expressed through the Gerstenhaber bracket:
d=[m_{A},-]
, the formal deformation
F
corresponds to a formal solution
\tilde{F}\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}F_{n}\lambda^{n}
of the Maurer-Cartan equation

d\tilde{F}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{F},\tilde{F}]=0.

The following correspondence has been known
[
Mar12
, ch. 4]
.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mbox{Equivalence classes of}\\
\mbox{formal deformations of }kP\end{array}\right\}\cong\mathscr{MC}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]]).

(6)

The next lemma shows that a formal deformation
F
of
kP
implies the existence of an equivalent deformation
F^{\prime}
such that
F_{n}^{\prime}\in C^{2}(kP,S;kP)
for
n>0
.

Lemma 2.8
.

There is a homotopy equivalence of derived Maurer-Cartan spaces

\MC_{\bullet}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]])\simeq\MC_{\bullet}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP,S;kP))[[\lambda]]).

Proof.

The inclusion
i:C^{\bullet}(kP,S;kP)\hookrightarrow C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)
is a DGLA quasi-isomorphism
[
GS88
, page 137]
. Then
i\otimes\id_{\lambda R[[\lambda]]}:\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP,S;kP)[[\lambda]]\hookrightarrow\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]]
is also a DGLA quasi-isomorphism. The ring
\lambda k[[\lambda]]=\varprojlim_{n}\lambda R[\lambda]/\lambda^{n}
is pro-nilpotent, so the DGLAs
\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP,S;kP)[[\lambda]]
and
\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]]
are also pro-nilpotent.
Then the standard results of the homotopy equivalence of the nerves for quasi-isomorphic pro-nilpotent DGLAs, explicitly

[
Cos09
, Lemma 5.3.1]
, see also
[
Get09
, Proposition 4.9]
, implies the statement of the lemma. ∎

Corollary
2.5
and Lemma
2.8
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 2.9
.

There is a homotopy equivalence of derived Maurer-Cartan spaces
\MC_{\bullet}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(kP;kP)[[\lambda]])\simeq\MC_{\bullet}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(P;k))[[\lambda]])
. ∎

We would like to conclude with the identification of the Maurer-Cartan moduli space
\mathscr{MC}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(P;k)[[\lambda]])
.

Corollary
2.9
implies any solution of the Maurer-Cartan equation in the DGLA
\lambda C^{\bullet}(P;k)[[\lambda]]

can be identified with an element

\omega=m_{\mathrm{S}}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\omega_{n}\lambda^{n}\in C^{2}(P;k)[[\lambda]]=C^{2}(P;k[[\lambda]])

(7)

that satisfies
5
. That is,
\tilde{\omega}:=\omega-m_{\mathrm{S}}
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation

d\tilde{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\omega},\tilde{\omega}]=0.

(8)

By
\lambda
-linearity, we have for
\omega
and
\rho\in C^{\bullet}(P;k)[[\lambda]]
,

[\omega,\rho]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\sum_{p+q=n}[\omega_{p},\rho_{q}]\lambda^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad d\omega:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}(d\omega_{n})\lambda^{n}.

Collecting the coefficients in equation (
8
) by each power of
\lambda
, we get the collection of equations

d\omega_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p+q=n}[\omega_{p},\omega_{q}]=0,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(9)

Since all the
\omega_{n}
’s are 2-cochains,
[\omega_{p},\omega_{q}]=\omega_{p}\circ\omega_{q}-(-1)^{|\omega_{p}||\omega_{q}|}\omega_{q}\circ\omega_{p}=\omega_{p}\circ\omega_{q}+\omega_{q}\circ\omega_{p}
. We rearrange the terms in the sum and obtain

d\omega_{n}+\sum_{p+q=n}\omega_{p}\circ\omega_{q}=0.

(10)

Let
W
be the abelian multiplicative group of the big Witt ring
1_{R}+\lambda k[[\lambda]]
. Then every
\omega\in C^{2}(P;W)
is of the same form (
7
). Then
d\omega=1_{W}
if and only if
(d_{0}\omega)(d_{2}\omega)=(d_{1}\omega)(d_{3}\omega)
. Term by term, this equation is equivalent to the collection of equations

d_{0}\omega_{n}+d_{2}\omega_{n}+\sum_{p+q=n}(d_{0}\omega_{p})(d_{2}\omega_{q})=d_{1}\omega_{n}+d_{3}\omega_{n}+\sum_{p+q=n}(d_{1}\omega_{p})(d_{3}\omega_{q}).

(11)

The terms involving
\omega_{n}
form the differential
d\omega_{n}
in
C^{\bullet}(P;R)
. Moving everything to the left-hand side, we obtain

d\omega_{n}+\sum_{p+q=n}(d_{0}\omega_{p})(d_{2}\omega_{q})-(d_{1}\omega_{p})(d_{3}\omega_{q})=d\omega_{n}+\sum_{p+q=n}\omega_{p}\circ\omega_{q}=0,

(12)

which is the same as (
10
). Thus, we see that the associativity equation (
5
) for
\omega
as in (
7
) is equivalent to
d\omega=1_{W}
for the same
\omega
considered as a cochain in
C^{2}(P;W)
. So we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

An element
\omega\in\lambda C^{2}(P;k)[[\lambda]]
is an MC element
if and only if
\omega\in Z^{2}(P;W)
. ∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Two
Maurer-Cartan elements
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}\in\lambda C^{2}(P;k)[[\lambda]]
are equivalent if and only if they are cohomologous in
C^{2}(P;W)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are equivalent. Then there is
\varphi\in C^{1}(P;W)
such that
\varphi(\omega^{\prime})=\omega(\varphi\otimes\varphi)
. Rewriting this equation with the operad notation we obtain
(d_{1}\varphi)\omega_{q}=(d_{0}\varphi)(d_{1}\varphi)\omega^{\prime}
, where
d_{i}
’s are face maps. Rearranging the terms, we get
\omega=(d\varphi)\omega^{\prime}
, which means
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are cohomologous. Mirroring the process yeilds the proof for the reverse direction.
∎

Theorem 2.12
.

The Maurer-Cartan moduli space
\mathscr{MC}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(P;k)[[\lambda]])
is isomorphic to the cohomology group
H^{2}(P;W)
of the poset
P
with coefficients in the group of big Witt vectors:

\mathscr{MC}(\lambda C^{\bullet}(P;k)[[\lambda]])\cong H^{2}(P;W).

Proof.

Lemmas
2.10
and
2.11
imply the statement.
∎

.

Corollary 2.13

The set of equivalence classes of formal deformations of the incidence algebra

may be naturally identified with the cohomology group

Proof.

In view of the identification (

), we need to present a natural isomorphism

which follows from Theorem
2.12

2.9
.
∎

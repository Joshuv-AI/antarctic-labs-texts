L 2 L^{2} -contraction and asymptotic stability of large shock for scalar viscous conservation laws
Alexis F. Vasseur, Yi Wang, Jian Zhang

Abstract




Abstract
: We investigate
L^{2}
-contraction and time-asymptotic stability of large shock for scalar viscous conservation laws with polynomial flux. For the strictly convex flux
f(u)=u^{p}
with
2\leq p\leq 4
, we can prove
L^{2}
-contraction and time-asymptotic stability of arbitrarily large viscous shock profile in
H^{1}
-framework by using
a
-contraction method with time-dependent shift and suitable weight function. Additionally, if the initial perturbation belongs to
L^{1}
, then
L^{2}
time-asymptotic decay rate
t^{-\frac{1}{4}}
can be obtained.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with
L^{2}
-contraction and time-asymptotic stability of arbitrarily large shock for the following scalar viscous conservation laws with polynomial flux:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=u_{xx},\quad f(u)=u^{p},\ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\[2.84526pt]
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\\[2.84526pt]
\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}u_{0}(x)=u_{\pm}.\end{array}\right.

where
u_{0}(x)
is the given initial data and
u_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
are the prescribed far-field states. We focus on the case that the asymptotic state of the solution to (
1.1
) is the viscous shock wave. Therefore, it is assumed that
p>1
such that
f(u)=u^{p}
is strictly convex for
u>0
, and that

(1.2)

0<u_{+}<u_{-}.

Remark that for the special case
p=2,
that is, the classical Burgers equation,
f^{\prime\prime}(u)=2
and
f(u)=u^{2}
is always strictly convex for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
, and then we only need to assume that
u_{+}<u_{-}
.

It can be expected that the large-time asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is determined by the following viscous shock profile
U(x-st)

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-sU^{\prime}+f(U)^{\prime}=U^{\prime\prime},\quad^{\prime}=\frac{d}{d\xi},\quad\xi=x-st,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle U(\pm\infty)=u_{\pm},\end{array}\right.

where
s
is the shock speed determined by the Rankine-Hugoniot condition:

(1.4)

s=\frac{f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}.

Integrating (
1.3
) over
(\pm\infty,\xi]
, we can get the following first order ODE

(1.5)

\displaystyle U^{\prime}

\displaystyle=h(U):=-s(U-u_{\pm})+f(U)-f(u_{\pm})

\displaystyle=(U-u_{\pm})\left[\frac{f(U)-f(u_{\pm})}{U-u_{\pm}}-\frac{f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}\right]<0.

Note that the strict convexity of the flux
f(u)
implies the above decreasing monotonicity of the viscous shock profile
U(\xi)
. Moreover, the existence of the viscous shock profile
U(\xi)
to (
1.3
) is standard and it is unique up to any constant translation.

The stability of viscous shock wave for conservation laws has been extensively studied since the pioneer works of Hopf
[
6
]
and Il’in-Oleinik
[
9
]
for one-dimensional (1D) scalar equation. In 1976, Sattinger
[
26
]
introduced a semigroup approach to establish the stability of viscous shock waves to 1D parabolic equations, including (
1.1
), in certain weighted spaces. Then Matsumura-Nishihara
[
24
]
and Goodman
[
4
]
independently proved the time-asymptotic stability of viscous shock profile for 1D isentropic Navier-Stokes equations and viscous conservation laws with artificial viscosity respectively, under the zero mass conditions such that the anti-derivative method can be applied. Meanwhile, Nishihara
[
25
]
proved the point-wise stability of viscous shock to 1D Burgers equation by virtue of the Hopf-Cole transformation and Kawashima-Matsumura
[
18
]
further obtained the convergence rate for the viscous shock to scalar equation by weighted energy method and the stability of viscous shock to both 1D full Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations and the discrete Broadwell model system. Note that in all the above mentioned time-asymptotic results for viscous shock to the system case, the crucial zero mass conditions are imposed to the initial perturbations such that the anti-derivative variables can be well-defined. Then Liu
[
21
]
, Szepessy-Xin
[
28
]
and Liu-Zeng
[
22
]
removed the zero mass conditions in
[
24
,
4
,
18
]
by introducing the constant shift on the viscous shock and the coupled diffusion waves in the transverse characteristic fields. And Mascia-Zumbrun
[
23
]
established the spectral stability of viscous shocks for the 1D compressible Navier-Stokes system, relaxing the zero mass conditions to a slightly weaker spectral condition. More recently, Kang-Vasseur-Wang
[
16
,
17
]
proved the generic Riemann solutions (containing viscous shock wave, rarefaction wave, and even viscous contact wave) to both barotropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations
[
17
]
and full compressible Navier-Stokes(-Fourier) equations
[
16
]
with the help of
a
-contraction method for the stability of viscous shock wave with time-dependent shift invented in
[
13
]
. Remark that both
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
solve the long-standing open problems for the time-asymptotic stability of the composite waves of Riemann profiles.
In addition, Freistuhler-Serre proved the
L^{1}
-stability of viscous shock waves
[
19
]
(see also Serre
[
27
]
) and Kenig-Merle proved
L^{p}
-stability(
1\leq p\leq+\infty
) of viscous shock waves
[
3
]
to scalar equation. For multi-dimensional case, Goodman
[
5
]
proved nonlinear stability of planar shock profile for viscous scalar conservation laws by using the anti-derivative techniques and the shift function depending on both the time and the transverse spatial variables. Then Humpherys–Lyng–Zumbrun
[
8
]
proved the spectral
stability of the planar viscous Navier-Stokes shock by the numerical Evans-function method in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and one can refer to the survey paper by Zumbrun
[
30
]
for the related results and the references therein. Very recently, Wang-Wang
[
29
]
proved the time-asymptotic stability of planar weak shock profile to 3D barotropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
under general
H^{2}
-initial perturbations by using
a
-contraction method.

On the other hand, for scalar conservation laws with or without viscosity, Kružkov
[
20
]
proved the famous
L^{1}
-contraction stability in quite general case. However, Kružkov’s theory is not valid for
L^{p}
-contraction
(\forall p>1)
, in particular, in the physical
L^{2}
-norm.
With the time-dependent shift
\mathbf{X}(t)
and possible weight function
a
, Kang-Vasseur prove
L^{2}
-contraction of arbitrarily large shock to both 1D general inviscid system of hyperbolic conservation laws
[
12
]
and 1D viscous Burgers equation
[
13
]
, and the latter result is extended by Kang
[
20
]
to scalar viscous conservation laws with general strictly convex flux, provided that the viscous shock wave strength was sufficiently small. Then Kang-Vasseur-Wang
[
15
]
prove
L^{2}
-contraction of large planar shock to scalar multi-dimensional viscous conservation laws by a special transformation and Kang-Vasseur
[
14
]
obtain
L^{2}
-contraction of small shock to 1D compressible barotropic Navier-Stokes equations. Kang-Oh
[
11
]
obtained
L^{2}
decay for large perturbations of weak viscous shock for multi-dimensional Burgers equation. Note that
a
-contraction theory has been well established at the inviscid level uniformly in the shock amplitude
[
12
]
, and at the viscous level only for small shocks
[
13
]
even with small perturbations, except Kang-Vasseur’s work
[
13
]
on specific Burgers’ equation for arbitrarily large shock. In fact, in the context of the viscous model,
a
-contraction results in the large shock setting (even in the scalar case) remain largely open.
Very recently, Blochas-Cheng
[
1
]
showed that the
a
-contraction property fails for large shock to certain kinds of viscous conservation laws, which showcases a “viscous
destabilization” effect in the sense that the
a
-contraction property is verified for the inviscid model for arbitrarily shock, but can fail for the viscous one, and raised the open question of whether this
a
-contraction property of large shock holds for scalar viscous conservation laws with polynomial fluxes.

In the present paper, we aim to address the question in
[
1
]
under small
H^{1}
framework around the shock profile and further prove the time-asymptotic stability of this large shock with the time decay rate. Based on
a
-contraction method with suitably chosen time-dependent shift and the weight function
a
, crucially depending on the shock wave strength, we can establish
L^{2}
-contraction for any large shock to (
1.1
) with strictly convex fluxes of the polynomial form
f(u)=u^{p}
(
2\leq p\leq 4
) and suitably small
H^{1}
perturbations, and further prove
L^{\infty}
time-asymptotic stability of this large shock profile with the time decay rate if the initial
H^{1}
-perturbation additionally lies in
L^{1}
. In fact, our proof is motivated by the recent work of Huang-Wang-Zhang
[
7
]
for the time-asymptotic stability of composite wave of large viscous Oleinik shock and rarefaction wave for the cubic non-convex scalar viscous conservation laws.

Precisely, our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any given
u_{\pm}
satisfying (
1.2
), let
U(\xi)
be viscous shock wave defined in (
1.3
). Then there exists a positive constant
\epsilon^{*}
such that if the initial data
u_{0}
satisfies

(1.6)

\Big\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\Big\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}<\epsilon^{*},

for any initial shock location
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with
p\in[2,4]
admits a unique global-in-time classical solution
u
. Moreover, there exists an absolutely continuous shift
\mathbf{X}(t)
(defined in (
2.5
)) and a smooth weight function
a(U(\xi)):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
(defined in (
2.4
)) such that the following
L^{2}
-contraction of arbitrarily large viscous shock holds

(1.7)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\big(U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))\big)|u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))|^{2}dx\leq 0,\quad\forall t>0.

Moreover, the time-asymptotic stability of large viscous shock holds

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t)\Big|=0,

with

(1.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}|\dot{\mathbf{X}}(t)|=0.

In addition, if
u_{0}(x)-U(x-x_{0})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have the following
L^{2}
-time decay rate

(1.10)

\|u(t,\cdot)-U(\cdot-st-\mathbf{X}(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C_{*}\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{\displaystyle 1+C_{*}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}},

where
C_{*}:=C\big[1+\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\big]
.

Remark 1.1.
Since the weight function
a(U(\xi))
is bounded from below and above,
L^{2}
-contraction (
1.7
) implies the uniform
L^{2}
-stability

(1.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))|^{2}dx\leq C^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{0}(x)-U(x-x_{0})|^{2}dx,\quad\forall t>0,

where positive constant
C^{*}=C^{*}(u_{+},u_{-},p)
is independent of the time
t>0
.

Remark 1.2.
The shift
{\mathbf{X}}(t)
is proved to satisfy the time-asymptotic behavior (
1.9
), which implies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathbf{X}(t)}{t}=0,

that is, the shift
\mathbf{X}(t)
grows at most sub-linearly with respect to the time
t
. Therefore, the shifted viscous shock wave
U\big(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))
keeps the original traveling wave profile time-asymptotically.

Notations.
Throughout this paper, several positive generic uniform-in-time constants are denoted by
C
.
Denote
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq p\leq+\infty)
and
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as the usual Lebesgue space and Sobolev space in
\mathbb{R}
with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{p}d\xi\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ 1\leq p<+\infty,\quad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}:={\rm ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|.

and

\|f\|:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\ \ \ \|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}:=(\|f\|^{2}+\|f_{\xi}\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and the shift function
\mathbf{X}(t)
, we denote

f^{\pm\mathbf{X}}(t,\xi):=f(t,\xi\pm\mathbf{X}(t)).

2.
Preliminaries and main result

In this section, we start with the construction of weight function
a(U(\xi))
and shift function
\mathbf{X}(t)
. Then, we present the local existence of the solution and the uniform-in-time a priori estimates, whose proofs are given in the subsequent sections. Finally, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
by the continuity arguments.

For convenience, we rewrite the equation (
1.1
) through the coordinates transformation
(t,x)\rightarrow(t,\xi=x-st)
, and then
u(t,\xi):=u(t,\xi+st)=u(t,x)
satisfies

(2.1)

u_{t}-su_{\xi}+f(u)_{\xi}=u_{\xi\xi}.

And
U^{-\mathbf{X}}:=U(\xi-\mathbf{X}(t))
satisfies

(2.2)

U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{t}+\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}-sU^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+f(U^{-\mathbf{X}})_{\xi}=U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi\xi}.

Then the perturbation
\phi(t,\xi):=u(t,\xi)-U(\xi-\mathbf{X}(t))
satisfies

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{t}-s\phi_{\xi}-\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+[f(\phi+U^{-\mathbf{X}})-f(U^{-\mathbf{X}})]_{\xi}=\phi_{\xi\xi},\\[8.53581pt]
\phi(0,\xi)=\phi_{0}(\xi):=u_{0}(\xi)-U(\xi-x_{0}),\end{array}\right.

for any initial shock location
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the sequel, we always assume that
p\in[2,4]
for the polynomial flux
f(u)=u^{p}
.

2.1.
Construction of weight function

Define the weight function
a(U(\xi))
as

(2.4)

a(U(\xi)):=\frac{(u_{-}-u_{+})h(U)}{(U-u_{+})(U-u_{-})}=\frac{f(U)-f(u_{-})}{U-u_{-}}-\frac{f(U)-f(u_{+})}{U-u_{+}},

It is easy to check that
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
a(U(\xi))>c>0
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, that is,
U\in(u_{+},u_{-})
, and
\|a(U(\xi))\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C
. Notice that for the Burgers equation case
(p=2)
, the above weight function
a=u_{-}-u_{+}
is exactly the shock wave strength, which means that there is no need for the weight and is consistent with the classical result
[
13
]
for Burgers equation.

2.2.
Construction of shift function

Define the shift function
\mathbf{X}(t)
as the solution to the following ODE:

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{X}}(t)=-\frac{4}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U^{-\mathbf{X}}(\xi))U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}(\xi)\phi(t,\xi)d\xi,\\[11.38109pt]
\mathbf{X}(0)=x_{0},\end{array}\right.

where the initial shock location
x_{0}
can be chosen arbitrarily with or without zero mass condition.

Denote

\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\xi):=\phi(t,\xi+\mathbf{X}(t))=u(t,\xi+\mathbf{X}(t))-U(\xi).

Then the perturbation equation (
2.3
) can be rewritten as

(2.6)

\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{t}-\dot{\mathbf{X}}(t)(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+U_{\xi})-s\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+\big[f(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}+U)-f(U)\big]_{\xi}=\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi\xi}.

By (
2.5
), we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{X}}(t)=-\frac{4}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U(\xi))U_{\xi}(\xi)\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\xi)d\xi.

Substituting (
2.7
) into (
2.6
) gives that

(2.8)

\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{t}+\frac{4(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+U_{\xi})}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U)U_{\xi}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}d\xi-s\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+\big[f(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}+U)-f(U)\big]_{\xi}=\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi\xi},

with the initial condition

(2.9)

\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t=0,\xi)=u_{0}(\xi+x_{0})-U(\xi):=\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{0}(\xi).

Note that both the non-local equation (
2.8
) for
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}
and the initial value
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{0}(\xi)
in (
2.9
) are independent of the definition of the shift function
\mathbf{X}(t),
even though we still write as
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}
.

We reformulate the Cauchy problem at general initial time
\tau\geq 0
, that is,

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{t}+\frac{4(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+U_{\xi})}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U)U_{\xi}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}d\xi-s\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}+\big[f(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}+U)-f(U)\big]_{\xi}=\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi\xi},\quad t>\tau,\\[14.22636pt]
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t=\tau,\xi):=\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\tau}(\xi).\end{array}\right.

The local existence of the strong solution
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}\in C([\tau,\tau+t_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(\tau,\tau+t_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
to the scalar nonlocal equation (
2.8
) can be stated as follows, whose proof will be given in Appendix.

Proposition 2.1
.

(Local existence) For any
M>0
, there exists a positive constant
t_{0}=t_{0}(M)
which is independent of
\tau
such that if
\|\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M
, then the Cauchy problem (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) has a unique strong solution
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\xi)
on the time interval
[\tau,\tau+t_{0}]
satisfying

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\xi)\in C([\tau,\tau+t_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(\tau,\tau+t_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau+t_{0}]}\|\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2M.\end{array}\right.

Then we can determine a unique shift function
\mathbf{X}(t)
by ODE (
2.7
) and the local solution
\phi(t,\xi):=u(t,\xi)-U(\xi-\mathbf{X}(t))
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(A priori estimates) For any given
u_{\pm}
satisfying (
1.2
), there exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
, such that if the Cauchy problem (
2.3
) has a solution
\phi\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
for some time
T>0
with

\mathcal{N}(T):=\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\epsilon_{0},

then there exists a uniform-in-time positive constant
C_{0}
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{\xi}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\phi}^{2}|U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}|d\xi d\tau\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}|\mathbf{\dot{X}}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau\leq C_{0}\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

and

(2.13)

|\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)|\leq C_{0}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

The proof of Proposition
2.2
will be given in next section. Based on Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, we can prove Theorem
1.1
as follows.

2.3.
The continuity argument

By the continuity argument, we can extend the local solution to the global one for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
as follows. Define

\epsilon^{*}:=\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{2}}{64C_{0}}}\right\},\qquad M:=\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},

where
\epsilon_{0}
and
C_{0}
are given in Proposition
2.2
. By (
1.6
),
\|\phi_{0}^{\mathbf{X}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}<\epsilon^{*}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4}(=M)
, and using the local existence result in Proposition
2.1
, there exists a positive constant
T_{0}=T_{0}(M)
such that a unique solution on
[0,T_{0}]
satisfing
\|\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}
for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
.
Note that
\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\phi^{\mathbf{X}}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}
for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
.
Especially, since
\mathbf{X}(t)
is absolutely continuous and
\phi^{\mathbf{X}}\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1}{(\mathbb{R})})
, we have
\phi\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1}{(\mathbb{R})})
. Consider the maximal existence time:

T_{M}:=\sup\left\{t>0\Big|\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\|\phi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\epsilon_{0}\right\}.

If
T_{M}<\infty
, then the continuity argument implies that
\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,T_{M}]}\|\phi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\epsilon_{0}
. By Proposition
2.2
, it holds that

\sup_{\tau\in[0,T_{M}]}\|\phi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{C_{0}\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{8},

which contradicts the fact
\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,T_{M}]}\|\phi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\epsilon_{0}
.
Therefore,

T_{M}=\infty.

By Proposition
3.1
, we have

(2.14)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\big(U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))\big)|u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))|^{2}dx\leq 0,\forall t>0,

which verifies
L^{2}
-contraction in Theorem
1.1
.
By using Proposition
2.2
again, we have

(2.15)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\phi_{\xi}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}d\tau+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\phi}^{2}|U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}|d\xi d\tau\leq C_{0}\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})},\end{array}

and

(2.16)

|\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)|\leq C\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\ \ \ \forall t>0.

2.4.
Time-asymptotic behaviors

Now we justify the time-asymptotic behaviors (
1.8
) and (
1.9
). First we set

\displaystyle g(t):=\|\phi_{\xi}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}.

From (
2.15
), it is obvious that

g(t)\in L^{1}(0,+\infty).

Next we show that

g^{\prime}(t)\in L^{1}(0,+\infty).

By (
3.36
)-(
3.38
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi_{\xi}\|^{2}\Big|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Big(\|\phi_{\xi}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+|\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\phi}^{2}|U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}|d\xi\Big)dt<+\infty.

Hence

(2.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}g(t)=\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|\phi_{\xi}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}=0.

By Sobolev inequality, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sqrt{2}\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{\xi}(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

By (
2.16
) and (
2.18
), it holds that

(2.19)

|\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)|\leq C\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\rightarrow 0,\ \ \ {\rm as}\ \ \ t\rightarrow+\infty.

2.5.
Decay estimate

Besides (
1.6
), if
u_{0}(x)-U(x-x_{0})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we first show that
\mathbf{X}(t)-x_{0}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Rewrite
u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))
as follows:

(2.20)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))=&\displaystyle\underbrace{u(t,x)-U(x-st-x_{0})}_{I_{1}(t,x)}\\
&\displaystyle+\underbrace{U(x-st-x_{0})-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))}_{I_{2}(t,x)}.\\
\end{array}

By
L^{1}
-contraction for solutions to the scalar viscous conservation laws
[
27
]
, we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle\|I_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Inspired by
[
13
]
, we denote
\tau=\tau(t):=\mathbf{X}(t)-x_{0}
and

\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|U(\xi-\tau)-U(\xi)|^{2}d\xi:=R(\tau).

Then we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial{R}}{\partial\tau}(\tau)&\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}[U(\xi-\tau)-U(\xi)]\frac{\partial U(\xi-\tau)}{\partial\xi}d\xi\\[8.53581pt]
&\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\xi}^{\xi-\tau}\frac{\partial U(y)}{\partial y}dy\frac{\partial U(\xi-\tau)}{\partial\xi}d\xi\\[11.38109pt]
&\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{x}^{x+\tau}(U)^{\prime}(y)(U)^{\prime}(x)dydx,\end{array}

which is strictly positive for
\tau>0
.
Furthermore, if
\tau>1
, then we have

\frac{\partial{R}}{\partial\tau}(\tau)\geq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{x}^{x+1}(U)^{\prime}(y)(U)^{\prime}(x)dydx:=\beta>0.

Hence, for
\tau>1

R(\tau)\geq R(1)+\beta(\tau-1)\geq\beta(\tau-1),

and then it holds that

1<\tau\leq\frac{R(\tau)}{\beta}+1.

Since
\displaystyle R(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|U(\xi-\tau)-U(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|U(\xi)-U(\xi+\tau)|^{2}d\xi=R(-\tau)
, that is,
R(\tau)
is an even function with respect to
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\forall\tau<-1,

R(\tau)\geq R(1)+\beta(-\tau-1)=R(1)+\beta(|\tau|-1)\geq\beta(|\tau|-1).

On the other hand, it is obvious that for
\tau\in[-1,1]
,

R(\tau)\geq 0>\beta(|\tau|-1).

Therefore, we have
\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

|\tau|\leq\frac{R(\tau)}{\beta}+1,

where
\displaystyle\beta:=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{x}^{x+1}(U)^{\prime}(y)(U)^{\prime}(x)dydx>0.

Equivalently, it holds that
\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

(2.22)

|\mathbf{X}(t)-x_{0}|\leq\frac{\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{\beta}+1.

Using the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\geq a^{2}-2|ab|
and (
2.15
), (
2.21
), we can get

(2.23)

\displaystyle\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|I_{1}(t,x)||I_{2}(t,x)|dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(I_{1}(t,x)+I_{2}(t,x)\big)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|I_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)-U(x-st-\mathbf{X}(t))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=2\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|I_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}(t,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2|u_{-}-u_{+}|\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+C_{0}\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

where in the last inequality we use the fact that
\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq|u_{-}-u_{+}|
.
By (
2.22
) and (
2.23
), we get that
\mathbf{X}(t)-x_{0}
is uniformly bounded and satisfies

(2.24)

|\mathbf{X}(t)-x_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\Big(2|u_{-}-u_{+}|\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+C_{0}\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\Big)+1.

Since
U(x)
is a monotone decreasing function, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=sgn(\tau(t))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(U(\xi-\tau(t))-U(\xi)\big)d\xi

\displaystyle=sgn(\tau(t))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{-\tau(t)}\partial_{y}U(\xi+y)dyd\xi

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{|\tau(t)|}\partial_{y}U(\xi+y)dyd\xi.

Then, using Fubini’s theorem to get

(2.25)

\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=|\mathbf{X}(t)-x_{0}||u_{-}-u_{+}|.

By (
2.21
), (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), we have

(2.26)

\displaystyle\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\|(I_{1}+I_{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|I_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|I_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{1},

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle C_{1}=\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+|u_{-}-u_{+}|\Big[\frac{1}{\beta}\Big(\displaystyle 2|u_{-}-u_{+}|\|u_{0}(\cdot)-U(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 241.84842pt+C_{0}\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\Big)+1\Big].\end{array}

Then Gagliardo-Nirenberg intepolation inequality shows that

\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{2}{3}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|\partial_{x}\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{3}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

By (
2.26
), we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{3}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|\partial_{x}\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{2}\|\partial_{x}\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

From the proof of Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
(see (
3.26
)) and (
3.34
), for positive constant
C
we have

(2.28)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(Ca+1)(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}d\xi\leq-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi})^{2}d\xi

Since
0<c<a^{-\mathbf{X}}<C
for positive constants
c
and
C
, by (
2.27
) and (
2.28
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi(t,\cdot)\sqrt{Ca^{-\mathbf{X}}+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq-2\|\partial_{x}\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2}{(C_{2})^{2}}\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|^{6}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq-C_{3}\|\phi(t,\cdot)\sqrt{Ca^{-\mathbf{X}}+1}\|^{6}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

which implies the decay estimate

(2.29)

\|\phi(t,\cdot)\sqrt{Ca^{-\mathbf{X}}+1}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{\|\phi_{0}\sqrt{Ca^{-\mathbf{X}}+1}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{1+2C_{3}t\|\phi_{0}\sqrt{Ca^{-\mathbf{X}}+1}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}.

By (
2.29
) and the inequality
2(a+b)^{\frac{1}{4}}\geq a^{\frac{1}{4}}+b^{\frac{1}{4}}
, we have

(2.30)

\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C_{*}\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{1+C_{*}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}},

for some uniform-in-time positive constant
C_{*}
. Thus we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Uniform-in-time a priori estimates

In this section, we prove Proposition
2.2
for the uniform-in-time a priori estimates. To do this, we assume that the Cauchy problem (

) has a solution
\phi\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
for some constant

L^{2}

as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{1}>0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}ad\xi\leq 0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}\big|{\phi}^{2}d\xi d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}|\mathbf{\dot{X}}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau\leq C\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2},

.

In order to prove Proposition

, we need to use a Poincar
{\acute{\rm e}}
type inequality and weighted energy method. Thus, we start with a Poincar
{\acute{\rm e}}
type inequality.

Lemma 3.1
.

(

14
]

satisfying

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}(y)dy-\Big(\int_{0}^{1}f(y)dy\Big)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}(y)|^{2}dy.

For simplicity, we denote
a^{-\mathbf{X}}:=a(U^{-\mathbf{X}}(\xi))=a\big(U(\xi-\mathbf{X}(t))\big)

Multiplying the equation

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}a^{-\mathbf{X}}\right]_{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)\phi^{2}(a^{-\mathbf{X}})_{\xi}-\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)U^{-\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}\phi a^{-\mathbf{X}}=\phi_{\xi\xi}\phi a^{-\mathbf{X}}.

Integrating (

with respect to

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}ad\xi\right]_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}\left[\frac{s}{2}a_{\xi}-\frac{1}{2}a_{\xi\xi}-\frac{a_{\xi}}{2}f^{\prime}(U)+\frac{af^{\prime\prime}(U)}{2}U_{\xi}\right]d\xi

\displaystyle+\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}a_{\xi}d\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}aU_{\xi}d\xi\right]

where we have used the fact

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{-\mathbf{X}}\phi[f(\phi+U^{-\mathbf{X}})-f(U^{-\mathbf{X}})]_{\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\phi^{\mathbf{X}}[f(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}+U)-f(U)]_{\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}aU_{\xi}[f(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}+U)-f(U)-f^{\prime}(U)\phi^{\mathbf{X}}]d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}U_{\xi}\Big[\frac{af^{\prime\prime}(U)}{2}-\frac{a^{\prime}f^{\prime}(U)}{2}\Big]d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}}O(1)(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{3}U_{\xi}d\xi.

Let

by the inverse function theorem. For any fixed

In order to use the weighted Poincar
{\acute{\rm e}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}aU_{\xi}d\xi=2\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}a\frac{U_{\xi}}{u_{+}-u_{-}}d\xi\Big)^{2}=2\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\psi(t,y)dy\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{0}^{1}\psi^{2}(t,y)dy-\int_{0}^{1}|\psi_{y}(t,y)|^{2}y(1-y)dy.

Furthermore, we have

(3.10)

and

(3.11)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\psi_{y}(t,y)|^{2}y(1-y)dy

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u_{+}-u_{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}a)_{\xi}\big]^{2}\frac{(U-u_{-})(u_{+}-U)}{U_{\xi}}d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{[(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}a)_{\xi}]^{2}}{a}d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi})^{2}d\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(a_{\xi\xi}-\frac{a_{\xi}^{2}}{a}\Big)(\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}d\xi.

By (

(3.12)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\mathbf{\dot{X}}(t)}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\mathbf{X}}aU_{\xi}d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\big(\phi^{\mathbf{X}}_{\xi}\big)^{2}d\xi\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\frac{2a^{2}U_{\xi}}{u_{+}-u_{-}}+a_{\xi\xi}-\frac{a_{\xi}^{2}}{a}\right](\phi^{\mathbf{X}})^{2}d\xi.

By (
2.4

a_{\xi}=a^{\prime}(U)U_{\xi}

Note that

(3.14)

and

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist positive constants

\forall U\in[u_{+},u_{-}],

Proof.

By (

(3.17)

\displaystyle g(u_{+})

\displaystyle=2a(u_{+})\Big[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u_{+})}{4}+\frac{a(u_{+})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{2a(u_{+})}{u_{-}-u_{+}}\Big[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u_{+})(u_{-}-u_{+})}{4}+f^{\prime}(u_{+})-\frac{f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}\Big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a(u_{+})}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\Big[f(u_{-})-f(u_{+})-f^{\prime}(u_{+})(u_{-}-u_{+})-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u_{+})}{4}(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a(u_{+})}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\Big[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u_{+})}{4}(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)}{6}(u_{-}-u_{+})^{3}\Big]<0,

where

On the other hand, by (

a(u_{-})=f^{\prime}(u_{-})-s=f^{\prime}(u_{-})-\frac{f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}>0,

then we have

\displaystyle g(u_{-})

\displaystyle=2a(u_{-})\Big[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u_{-})}{4}+\frac{a(u_{-})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}\Big]

\displaystyle=2a(u_{-})\left[u_{-}^{p-2}\Big(\frac{p^{2}-5p+4}{4}+\frac{(2-p)u_{+}}{u_{-}-u_{+}}+\frac{u_{+}^{2}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\Big)-\frac{u_{+}^{p}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2a(u_{-})\left[u_{-}^{p-2}\Big(\frac{(2-p)u_{+}}{u_{-}-u_{+}}+\frac{u_{+}^{2}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\Big)-\frac{u_{+}^{p}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2a(u_{-})}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}\underbrace{\Big[(2-p)u_{+}(u_{-}-u_{+})u_{-}^{p-2}+u_{+}^{2}\big(u_{-}^{p-2}-u_{+}^{p-2}\big)\Big]}_{:=l_{1}(u_{-},u_{+})}<0,

where we have used the fact that
\forall p\in[2,4],\forall 0<u_{+}<u_{-},

Next, we claim that

(3.19)

(3.20)

3.21

\forall p\in[2,4]

\forall U\in(u_{+},u_{-}),

(3.24)

\forall p\in[2,4]
,

(3.25)

\displaystyle=2\left[h+\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(U)(U-u_{-})(u_{+}-U)}{4}\right]^{2}>0,\quad\forall U\in(u_{+},u_{-}).

Hence, by (
3.15
), we proved the claim (
3.19
). From (

g(u_{\pm})<0
. By the properties of continuous function over closed interval, there exists a positive constant

∎

Thus, by (

(4.21)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}[f(\phi_{2}+U)-f(\phi_{1}+U)]_{\xi}d\xi dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|^{2}dt+C(M)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\big(\|\Phi\|^{2}+\|\Phi_{\xi}\|^{2}\big)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|^{2}dt+C(M)t_{0}\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}(\|\Phi\|^{2}+\|\Phi_{\xi}\|^{2}).

Then we can compute that

(4.22)

\displaystyle\left|4\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}U_{\xi}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}d\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U)U_{\xi}\Phi d\xi dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|^{2}+Ct_{0}^{2}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|^{2}+Ct_{0}^{2}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

(4.23)

\displaystyle\left|4\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi_{2\xi}\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}d\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U)U_{\xi}\Phi d\xi dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|\|\phi_{2\xi}\|\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|^{2}dt+C(M)t_{0}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

and

(4.24)

\displaystyle\left|4\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Phi_{\xi}\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}}{(u_{-}-u_{+})^{2}}d\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(U)U_{\xi}\phi_{1}d\xi dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\|\Phi_{\xi}\|\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}dt

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi\xi}\|^{2}dt+C(M)t_{0}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Substituting (
4.21
)-(
4.24
) into (
4.20
) and then taking
t_{0}
suitably smaller than before if needed, we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\xi}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

By (
4.19
) and (
4.25
), we have

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\tilde{\Phi}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{2}{3}\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+t_{0}}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Hence, the mapping
\mathcal{T}:Y_{\tau,t_{0}}^{2M}\to Y_{\tau,t_{0}}^{2M}
is a contraction mapping in
C([\tau,\tau+t_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
for suitably small
t_{0}=t_{0}(M)>0
.
Therefore, there exist a suitably small
t_{0}=t_{0}(M)>0
and a unique fixed point
\phi\in Y_{\tau,t_{0}}^{2M}\subset C([\tau,\tau+t_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
for the mapping
\mathcal{T}
, that is,

\phi(t,\xi)=\mathcal{T}\phi(t,\xi).

Then we finish the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

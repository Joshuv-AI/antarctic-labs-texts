Wigner Cat Phases: A finely tunable system for exploring the transition to quantum chaos Publication type: Paper
M. Süzen 1 \orcid 0000-0002-9460-7297

Abstract
The transition to chaos for quantum dynamics is quantified via a finely tunable mixed random matrix ensemble. The mixed Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (mGOE) forms a pedagogically accessible family of systems in simulating Many-Body Localization (MBL) transitions. It can be tuned from chaotic to localized and heavy-tailed localized phases in a continuous fashion, providing an opportunity to explore new phases. We numerically study how the spectral properties of mGOE evolve during these transitions. Characterization of transition to quantum chaos is computed and analyzed via empirical spectral density, nearest-neighbor spacing, and adjacent gap ratios with statistical uncertainty quantifications that strengthens the robustness of evidence of transitions. The transition is identified as Wigner Cat Phases , because of the shape of empirical spectral densities, which depens on the tuneable parameter. These simulated phases in mGOE appear to be an ideal tool to study Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH) and its related transitions, representing a family of physical systems under different localisation and disorder strengths.

Quantum thermalization transition to localisation

A definite quantum state
|\Psi(t)\rangle
can evolve over time given its
eigenstates
|\alpha\rangle
and eigenenergies
E_{\alpha}
in an exponential
fashion with a corresponding observable linear operator
A
,

|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}C_{\alpha}\exp(-iE_{\alpha}t)|\alpha\rangle

where
\sum_{\alpha}|C_{\alpha}|^{2}=1
, a normalised state. How this system achieves
thermal equilibrium, observable reaches to an equilibrium value given temperature
T_{\alpha}

is shown
[
15
,
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
]

by two conditions, so-called
Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis
(ETH):

1.

Expectation value
\langle\alpha|A|\alpha\rangle
is a smooth function of
eigenenergies
\Phi(E_{\alpha})
implying individual eigenstates possess thermal properties,

\langle A\rangle_{T_{\alpha}}=\Phi(E_{\alpha})
, independent of initial values.

2.

Given transition from
\alpha
to
\beta
, diagonal elements of

\langle\alpha|A|\beta\rangle
should be much larger than off-diagonal elements.
This is important as to recover thermal properties for individual eigenstates.

This is also related to
quantum ergodicity
because ensemble average

\langle A\rangle
is equal to time average of
A
if thermalization is achieved. This
outstanding and insightful description of quantum thermalization due to
Deutsch-Srednicki

provided a basis for showing that, quantum system whereby their classical counterparts are
chaotic in Lyapunov sense satisfies these two conditions of ETH.

3
Invariant Mixing Ensembles: Tuning for Localisation

The primary invariant ensemble that represents a quantum chaotic system as as proposed
by BGS conjecture is Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE). Its numerical
construction was clearly demonstrated by the seminal paper of
Edelman-Rao

[
22
]
, among other generalised ensembles. Here, we concentrate on only
GOE. A given matrix
G_{1}
having entries drawn from Gaussian distribution with
mean
\mu=0
and standard deviation
\sigma
. We can sample GOE and obtain a member
of ensemble
A^{GOE}(N)
with the following algebraic equality
[
22
]
,a

NxN
square matrix,

A^{GOE}(N)=\frac{1}{2}(G_{1}(N)+G_{1}^{T}(N))

,

G_{1}^{T}(N)
is the transpose matrix. The sampled matrix
A^{GOE}(N)
has the following properties.

1.

Diagonals with the following distribution:
diag(A^{GOE})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma)
,

2.

Offdiagonals with the following distributions:
offdiag(A^{GOE})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma/2)
.

If we draw
M
matrices, we call the resulting set
GOE
of size
M
and with square matrix
sizes of
N
, denoted by
GOE(M,N)
. By doing this we can estimate bootstrapped confidence
intervals on all computed properties
[
54
,
14
]
. This kind of confidence
intervals is not usually practiced in RMT studies but in our context it is important for
investigations of the mixed ensembles due to nature of numerical generation of mixed ensembles.

A mixed ensemble, we mix the matrix sizes in the given ensemble of size
M
. A mixed
GOE

denoted by
mGOE
can be characterized by one more parameter degree of mixture
\mu
, hence

mGOE(M,N,\mu)
. In the limiting case
\mu=1.0
mixed ensemble is equivalent to
its conventional counterpart, such that
GOE(M,N)=mGOE(M,N,\mu=1.0)
.

Numerical construction of
mGOE
or any mixed ensemble of the corresponding canonical
random matrix ensemble follows the following recipe.

1.

There will be
M
different size random matrices drawned from
GOE(n_{i})
.

2.

The number of matrices of size
n_{i}
is denoted by
m_{i}
with the
constrain
M=\sum_{i}m_{i}
.

3.

The degree of mixture
\mu
can be intepreted as success probability in a Binomial process,
that satisfied
M=\sum_{i}m_{i}
and limiting case
\mu=1.0

exactly
.

4.

The set of
n_{i}
then can be identified by
M
Binomial trials with the following
parametrisation
Binomial(\mu,N)
.

5.

Then we drawn matrices from conventional GOE,
M
times
GOE(n_{i})
, whereby size of
matrices comes from the Binomial process.

6.

In the large
M
limit,
N\cdot\mu=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i}n_{i}
should hold.

For example, in building
mGOE(M,N,\mu)
, a simple possible case for
M=10
for
N=1000

at
\mu=0.8
a possible set of draws from Binomial process can be the following
Binomial(1000,0.8)
,

[809,813,809,797,808,789,795,810,807,797]
are the sizes
n_{i}
of matrices we should
generate from
GOE
. Mean of the sizes is
800.1
, in the large
M
limit this should be exactly
800
.

3.1
Spectral Periodicity: Degenarcy and inducing localisation

The above recipe to generate mixed ensemble will generate many different length spectra. It is
important to align spectra of different size matrices. For this purpose, we apply
periodic boundary conditions on the resulting spectra. The matrix with size
n_{i}
will produce

n_{i}
eigenvalues. Periodicity dictates repeated eigenvalues up to the base size
N
for
mGOE, recall the parameters of the ensemble
mGOE(N,M,\mu)
. This leads to degeneracy
in the energy levels.

In investigating localisation phenomenon, MBL, intuitively the degree of mixture
\mu
plays a
role of tunable parameter that drives the representative quantum system of
mGOE
to
localisation away from thermalization at
\mu=1.0
due to expressed degeneracy and randomness in
generating the mixed ensemble via Binomial draws. Effect of such
directed randomness
is
also observed in deep learning
[
50
]
.

The theoretical justification of this behaviour lies in mismatched spectral analysis
depending on the matrix sizes. This can be imagined via average behaviour of different
spectral distribution. Hence, this generates a defect in spectral density
in sampling
mGOE
, driving the system to localisation with decreasing
\mu
. From a physical
stand point, equivalently this is a kind of inducing a disorder in generating mixed ensemble,
amplified by the spectral periodicity and variation of sizes in mixture. This is not a
finite-size effect, rather
conjectured
to be an inherent property of the mixed ensemble.

3.2
Self-consistent spectral unfolding

Unfolding the spectra aimed at removing local fluctuations and making them locally flat
on average. It is shown in the literature that computation of spectral nearest-neighbor
spacings are sensitive to eigenvalue outliers
[
1
]
. For this reason, we used
interquartile range of eigenvalues without any loss of distributional quality,
i.e., perfect agreement with the theoretical distributions in the full thermalization
GOE case.

A self-consistent procedure of using multiple different polynomial of a given degree and
select the degree such that resulting mean fluctuations are closest to one. Even though
adjacent gap ratio is insensitive to unfolding, we used the folded spectrum for all analysis.

Numerical Experiments

Numerical investigation of how tuning mGOE gives rise to a deviation from
limiting value
\mu=1.0

corresponding representative quantum system’s dynamics. Decreasing
\mu
parameter
represents a more localized states, i.e., MBL. We use matrix order of

with ensemble sample size of

The generated dataset using the formulation of mGOE along different

to MBL. In supporting the transition, we analyze how spectral,
nearest-neighbour spacings and adjacent gap ratio densities evolve as a function
of the tuning parameter. Additionally, we track mean adjacent gap ratio over tuned
parameter to see under which tuning the computed values matches with
full ETH and non-chaotic dynamics.

5
Conclusion

Pedagogically easy to understand ensemble of random matrices is introduced for simulation
of localization transition to quantum chaos. Contributions of such mixed ensemble are three
fold: (1) advances the notion of matrix ensemble to arbitrarily shaped observable matrices,
such as complex networks with varying size sub-components or quantum system as in this work,
(2) provides physically plausable toy model that is easy to simulate the transition to
quantum chaos with an arbitrarily precise fine tunable parameter for pedagogical and
research purposes, and (3) we demonstrated that MBL phases with heavy-tailed nearest-neighbour
spacings are possible that still yields mean adjacency-gap value of an integrable system,
our results providing spectral data albeit simulated for a new MBL phase, i.e., heavy-tailed
localized.

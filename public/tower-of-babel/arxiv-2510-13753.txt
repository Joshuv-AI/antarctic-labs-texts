Strong solution for polymeric fluid-structure interaction with small initial acceleration
Prince Romeo Mensah

Abstract
. We consider the problem of a 3D-3D-2D mutually coupled solute-solvent-structure three-states system. This describes the interaction of a flexible structure with a polymeric fluid of classical Oldroyd-B type without centre-of-mass diffusion. We construct a unique higher-order regularity notion of a strong solution for the system by decoupling the solute from the solvent-structure subsystem, solving the decoupled system individually, and glueing the solutions through a fixed-point argument. As a requirement for the construction, we rely on a maximal regularity result for the Stokes problem on moving domains with non-trivial boundary conditions; a result that is also of independent interest.

As mentioned earlier, the three-states analysis of polymeric fluid-structure interaction problems is a recent program started in
[
3

Analyzing such a system, therefore, require fundamental techniques from two previously distinct areas of continuum mechanics: the analysis of polymeric fluids and the study of fluid-structure interaction problems. In the initial work
[
3
]
, the solute subcomponent is modelled on the mesoscopic scale leading to a Fokker–Planck equation for the (mesoscopic) description of the polymer distribution. The more general Koiter shell is also used to model the structure and the existence of a weak solution to the underlying system is constructed. When the same structure equation (
1.6
) is used instead of the Koiter shell, a unique strong solution to the mixed mesoscopic-macroscopic model is then constructed in
[
4
]

In the 2D-2D-1D setting for the fully macroscopic problem, weak and strong solutions are constructed in
[
19
]
. A important feature making this possible is a dissipation term in the solute subcomponent which is not available in our current setting. In fact, the absence of this term is a crucial reason why we are unable to construct a weak solution is this work. Having constructed this two main class of solutions, we perform certain singular limit arguments for this dissipative system in our other works
[
17
,
18
]
. In
[
18
]
, we show that
any family of strong solutions that is parametrized by the center-of-mass diffusion coefficient converges, as the coefficient goes to zero, to a weak solution of a corotational polymer
fluid-structure interaction system without center-of-mass diffusion but whose polymer number density and extra stress tensor are essentially bounded. As a consequence, a weak-strong uniqueness result is obtained that says that the weak solution of the latter is unique in the class of the strong solution of the former as the center-of-mass diffusion vanishes.

A similar singular limit argument is also performed in
[
17
]
except that the limit system is now purely non-dissipative. As such, the damping term in the solute is eliminated together with the center-of-mass diffusion. Weak-strong uniqueness is also shown to be true and the rate at which the solution to the original dissipative system decays exponentially in time to the equilibrium solution is also given.

Coming back to our current work for the 3D-3D-2D problem without diffusion in the solute, we aim to construct relatively high regular solutions that satisfy the whole system pointwise almost everywhere. Beyond the difficulty posed by the lack of diffusion, there are other reasons why constructing less regular and more generalised solution appear to be beyond reach. One such reason is the hyperbolic structure exhibited by the solute. Several systems with this structure, including our own, that are posed in a
d

Another challenge emanates from the very 3D-3D-2D setting where several optimal interpolation that one obtains in the 2D-2D-1D setting is lost. This is particularly critical for the solute subsystem where the 3D Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality applied to the terms
(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}
in the convected derivative yield undesirable estimates when working in lower regularity spaces.

1.1.
Plan

The strategy to solving Theorem
1.4
involves splitting the problem into a solvent-structure subproblem and a solute subproblem. A fixed-point argument for composite maps is then used to glue the solutions of these subproblems together.
A stated in Theorem
1.2
, we have recently constructed the required notion of a strong solution for the first subproblem. To combine this solution to the solution of the second subproblem, however, we require non-trivial higher order estimates beyond the natural energy estimate. This raises an issue of maximal regularity for the Stokes problem on a moving domain with non-trivial boundary conditions; a result that is currently unavailable in the literature. For this reason, we devote an intermediate section, Section
3
, to the proof of this result. The main result is given by Theorem
3.1
below and it extend the recent breakthrough
[
1
]
for the same moving boundary Stokes problem but with the trivial boundary condition. By combining these maximal regularity results with elliptic estimates for the pressure, we are able to derive our desired estimate for the solvent-structure subproblem under a smallness condition on the initial acceleration.

Due to the lack of dissipation in the solute subproblem, we find it difficult to use a Galerkin method to construct a solution for the solute subproblem. We, therefore, resort to the method of characteristics. To apply this to our equation, however, we apply a regularity preserving transformation to rectify the disorder in the convected derivative term
\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}
. By ‘disorder’, we simply mean that we ideally wanted the terms in the product swapped. Since matrix products are not commutative, we transform the whole tensor-valued equation for the solute subproblem into a vector-valued equation which allow us to apply the aforementioned method of characteristics while preserving regularity.

Having constructed solutions to the two subproblems, we then apply Banach fixed-point arguments to get a local strong solutions to the fully coupled system. We mention in passing that the high-regularity required for our solution makes it difficult, if at all possible, to hope to extend the local solution for the full system globally (assuming no degeneracies even occur in the shell) even with just a small initial acceleration.

\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. However, because the analysis at the rigid part is essentially trivial when compared to the fixed part, we shall identify the whole of
\partial\Omega
with
\omega
and endow it with periodic boundary conditions. Let
I:=(0,T)
represent a time interval for a given constant
T>0
. The time-dependent displacement of the structure is given by
\eta:\overline{I}\times\omega\rightarrow(-L,L)
where
L>0
is a fixed length of the tubular neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
given by

\displaystyle S_{L}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L\}.

For some
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we now assume that
\partial\Omega
is parametrized by an injective mapping
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{k}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}\neq 0
and small Lipschitz bound, i.e. there exists a strictly positive constant
\epsilon<1
such that
\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\omega)}\leq\epsilon
.
The set
\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
, given by

\displaystyle\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}=\big\{\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}:=\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})\eta(t,\mathbf{y})\,:\,t\in I,\mathbf{y}\in\omega\big\}.

then represents the boundary of the flexible (moving) domain at any instant of time
t\in I
and the vector
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})
is the outward unit normal at the point
\mathbf{y}\in\omega
.
We also let
\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})

be the corresponding outward normal of
\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
at the spacetime point
\mathbf{y}\in\omega
and
t\in I
. Then for
L>0
sufficiently small, we note that
\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})
is close to
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})
and
\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}
is close to
\bm{\varphi}
. As a result, it follows that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{1}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}\times\partial_{y_{2}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}\neq\bm{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})\neq 0

for
\mathbf{y}\in\omega
and
t\in I
. Thus, in particular, there is no loss of strict positivity of the Jacobian determinant provided that
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I;W^{1,\infty}(\omega))}<L
.

For the interior points, we transform the reference domain
\Omega
into a time-dependent moving domain
\Omega_{\eta(t)}
whose state at time
t\in\overline{I}
is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\eta(t)}=\big\{\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x}):\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\big\}.

Here,

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\eta(t,\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\quad\text{elsewhere }\end{cases}

is the Hanzawa transform with inverse
\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta(t)}
and where for a point
\mathbf{x}
in the neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
, the vector
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))
is the outward unit normal at the point
\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathbf{y}\in\omega}|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y})|
. Also,
s(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}-\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})))\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a cut-off function that is
\phi\equiv 0
in the neighbourhood of
-L
and
\phi\equiv 1
in the neighbourhood of
0
. Note that
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))+\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))[s(\mathbf{x})+\eta(t,\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))]&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\quad\text{elsewhere. }\end{cases}

We also note that for the boundary point
\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y})\in\partial\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have that

\displaystyle\big(\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\circ\bm{\varphi}^{-1}\big)(\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y}))=\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}(\mathbf{y})=\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{n}\eta\in\partial\Omega_{\eta}.

Therefore,
\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\circ\bm{\varphi}^{-1}:\partial\Omega\rightarrow\partial\Omega_{\eta}
correspond to the Hanzawa transform
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\overline{\Omega_{\eta}}
restricted to the boundary, i.e.,
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\circ\bm{\varphi}^{-1}
. Consequently, in particular,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\circ\bm{\varphi}

\displaystyle\quad\text{on }I\times\omega.

Furthermore, the transform
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}
and its inverse

\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}=\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta}
satisfy the following properties, see
[
2
,
6
]
for details.
If for some
\ell,R>0
, we assume that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}+\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<\ell<L\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<R

holds, then for any
s>0
,
\varrho,p\in[1,\infty]
and for any
\eta,\zeta\in B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\omega)
(where
B^{s}_{\varrho,p}
is a Besov space), we have that the estimates

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}+\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}\lesssim 1+\|\eta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}-\bm{\Psi}_{\zeta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}+\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}-\bm{\Psi}_{\zeta}^{-1}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}\lesssim\|\eta-\zeta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)}

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)},\qquad\eta\in W^{1,1}(I;B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega))

holds uniformly in time with the hidden constants depending only on the reference geometry, on
L-\ell
and
R
.

3.
Maximal regularity theory

A central result we shall use in our construction of a solution, which is of independent interest, is the extension of the classical maximal regularity theory for the Stoke system to moving domains with nontrivial boundary conditions. This is a new result and build on the recent work
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
for moving domains with homogeneous boundary conditions. More precisely, we consider the following system

(3.1)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathbf{f}

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{ on }I\times\omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}

and prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta)

be a dataset such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega_{\eta}),\qquad\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot)\in W^{3,2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{\star}(\cdot)\in W^{1,2}(\omega),\qquad\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),

\displaystyle\text{with }\mathbf{u}_{0}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{\star}\mathbf{n}\text{ on }\omega.

Suppose further that

\displaystyle\eta\in W^{2,2}(I;L^{2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I;W^{1,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap L^{\infty}(I;W^{3,2}(\omega))

and there exists constants
\ell,\kappa_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}\leq\ell<L\qquad\inf_{I\times\omega}|\partial_{1}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\times\partial_{2}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}|\geq\kappa_{0}.

Then there exist a unique solution to (
3.1
) such that the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\int_{I}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\||\partial_{t}\eta|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{I}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\partial_{t}\eta\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\,|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

hold with a constant depending on
\eta_{0}
.

Theorem
3.1
extend the following
maximal regularity result
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
for the Stokes system in a moving domain but with zero boundary condition.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},\eta_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta)

be a dataset such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega_{\eta}),\qquad\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot)\in W^{3,2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)\in W^{1,2}_{0,\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),

\displaystyle\text{with }\mathbf{u}_{0}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta_{0}}=\bm{0}\text{ on }\omega.

Suppose further that

\displaystyle\eta\in C(\overline{I};B_{\varrho,2}^{\theta}(\omega))\cap C(\overline{I};W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap C(\overline{I};C^{1}(\omega))\cap W^{1,3}(I;W^{3,2}(\omega))

with
\varrho\geq 4
and
\theta\in(\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{3}{\varrho}]
, and there exists constants
\ell,\kappa_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}\leq\ell<L\qquad\inf_{I\times\omega}|\partial_{1}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\times\partial_{2}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}|\geq\kappa_{0}.

Then there exist a unique solution to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathbf{f}

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\text{ on }I\times\omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}

such that

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that the restrictive choice of
W^{2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}
for the initial condition
\mathbf{u}_{0}
in
[
2
,
Theorem 3.1]
is just a simplification to avoid the usual but complicated choice of
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in(L^{p},W^{2,p})_{1-\frac{1}{r},r}\cap L^{p}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}
that is required for the theorem to hold true. Here,
(L^{p},W^{2,p})_{1-\frac{1}{r},r}=B^{2(1-1/r)}_{p,r}
is an interpolation space. In the particular case where

in

, Theorem 3.1]
, a careful inspection of the proof shows that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{0,\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})
is sufficient for the proof as expected since

when

.

The key to the proof of Theorem

is the combination of Theorem

with the following proposition (see

Proposition 3.4
.

For a given
\eta\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{1,2}(\omega))
with
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}\leq\ell<L
, there are linear operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{\eta}:L^{1}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}:\{\xi\in L^{1}(I;W^{1,1}(\omega)):\,\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)=0\}\rightarrow L^{1}(I;W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})),

such that the tuple
({\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)),\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))
satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))\cap L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta})),

\displaystyle\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I;L^{2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}({\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)))\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})),

\displaystyle({\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\xi)))\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\mathbf{n},\quad\text{ on }I\times\omega,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\xi))(t,\mathbf{x})=0\text{ for }(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times(\Omega\setminus S_{\ell})

provided that we have
\xi\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I;L^{2}(\omega))
.
In particular, we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{2,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{2,p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\xi\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\,|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\partial_{t}\xi\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\xi\,\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\xi\,\partial_{t}\eta\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

for any
p\in(1,\infty),q\in[1,\infty]
.

Remark 3.5
.

The operator
\mathscr{K}_{\eta}
is a

If
\xi

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Proposition
3.4
, there exist an extension

\Phi:={\mathscr{F}}^{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta\mathbf{n})

such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\Phi=0

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\Phi\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=\partial_{t}\eta\,\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on }\overline{I}\times\omega

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{2}\eta\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\||\partial_{t}\eta|^{2}\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\mathbf{n}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\,\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}(\partial_{t}\eta\mathbf{n})\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\,|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Recall from Section
2.1
that
\mathbf{n}
is a smooth unit vector.

Now set
\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{u}-\Phi
so that (
3.1
) is equivalent to

(3.3)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{w}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathbf{f}-\partial_{t}\Phi+\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\Phi

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{w}=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\text{ on }I\times\omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}-\Phi(0)

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}.

If we now apply Theorem
3.2
to (
3.3
), we obtain a unique solution satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+

\displaystyle\|p\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

and by using the inequality

\displaystyle|x-y|^{2}\geq(|x|-|y|)^{2}=|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}-2|x|\,|y|,

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-2\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\,\mathrm{d}t

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{I}

\displaystyle\big(\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\int_{I}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

\Phi

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}+

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\delta

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+c(\delta)\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
\delta>0
. We can now choose
\delta
small and absorb this
\delta

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

However, by (

),

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\||\partial_{t}\eta|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\,\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{I}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\partial_{t}\eta\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\,|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

hold. This finishes the proof.
∎

In analogy with the standard maximal regularity result for the Stokes system on fixed domains, one can also obtain higher spatial regularity for our moving domain. For the purpose of our work, we are only interested in improving the spatial regularity by two factor of differentiability. For this, we now set
\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}:=\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\circ\bm{\varphi}
so that the restriction of
\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}
to the boundary
\omega
is
\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}
, recall (
2.2
). If we take the partial derivatives
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}
of
\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}
, then by the chain rule

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}(\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\Phi}_{\eta})

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{i}}\bm{\Phi}_{\eta})^{\top}(D^{2}\mathbf{u})\circ\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}+D\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{\Phi}_{\eta}

with corresponding trace

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}(\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta})

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y_{i}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta})^{\top}(D^{2}\mathbf{u})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\partial_{y_{j}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}+D\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta})^{-1}\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{y_{j}}\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{y_{j}}\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{t}\eta+\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}\partial_{t}\eta.

and
\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}
on

. However, since

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta})=D\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\partial_{t}\eta)\otimes\mathbf{n}+(\partial_{t}\eta)\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{n}

on

(3.4)

\displaystyle(D^{2}\mathbf{u})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\otimes\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}}{|\partial_{x_{i}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}|^{2}|\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}|^{2}}\otimes\big\{

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{y_{j}}\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{y_{j}}\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{t}\eta+\mathbf{n}\,\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}\partial_{t}\eta

\displaystyle-((\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\partial_{t}\eta)\otimes\mathbf{n}+(\partial_{t}\eta)\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{n})(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta})^{-1}\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\big\}.

Since the Stokes equation is linear, it follows that the doubly differentiated Stokes equation for

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}:=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\mathbf{u},\qquad q:=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}p,\qquad\mathbf{g}:=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\mathbf{f}

solve

(3.5)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}q+\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta}

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }I\times\Omega_{\eta}

\displaystyle(D^{2}\mathbf{u})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle\text{ on }I\times\omega

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}

where
\mathbb{H}
is the right-hand side of (

). We now recall that since
\bm{\varphi}
and

are smooth vectors and

, all functions of

and

in the definition of

above are uniformly bounded in

if

.

If we now apply Theorem

to (

) and summing over all

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta)

be a dataset such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})),\qquad\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot)\in W^{5,2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{\star}(\cdot)\in W^{3,2}(\omega),\qquad\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)\in W^{3,2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),

\displaystyle\text{with }\mathbf{u}_{0}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{\star}\text{ on }\omega.

Suppose further that

\displaystyle\eta\in W^{2,2}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I;W^{3,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;W^{4,2}(\omega))\cap L^{\infty}(I;W^{5,2}(\omega)).

and there exists constants

Then there exist a unique solution to (

) such that

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{3,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\int_{I}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\eta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

4.
Strong solutions to subproblems

The initial step in our strategy for actually constructing a solution involves solving the solvent-structure subproblem and the solute subproblem independently of each other. After that, in a subsequent section, we will use a fixed-point argument to get a local solution to the fully coupled system.

4.1.
The solvent-structure subproblem

In the following, for a given stress tensor
\underline{\mathbb{T}}
, a given pair of body forces
\mathbf{f}
and
g
, we wish to derive suitable estimates for the unique strong solution of the following solvent-structure system of equations

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{u}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathbf{f}+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\underline{\mathbb{T}},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\eta-\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta+\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta=g-(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{n}_{\eta})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,|\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta})|,

defined on
I\times\Omega_{\eta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
(with (
4.3
) defined on

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}+(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}-p\mathbb{I}+\underline{\mathbb{T}}.

We then complement (
4.1
)–(
4.3
) with the following initial and interface conditions

(4.4)

\displaystyle\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot),\qquad\partial_{t}\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{\star}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\omega,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}.

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\omega.

1.2
. The estimate we wish to derive is required in a subsequent section where we deal with the fully coupled solute-solvent-structure problem. We state this in the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

For the dataset
(\mathbf{f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\underline{\mathbb{T}})
satisfying (

(4.7)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\big(\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\eta_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\epsilon

,
let

\displaystyle\sup_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon

where the last inequality follows from (

In order to obtain higher regularity estimates, we have to take the time derivative of our original solvent-structure subsystem. (4.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}=0,

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{3,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\eta_{0}\|_{W^{5,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|g(0)\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}(0)\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{f}(0)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{3}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\epsilon\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\epsilon\sup_{I_{*}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}.

If we now make the highest spatial derivative term in (
4.3
) the subject and use trace theorem, we obtain

(4.29)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{*}}\|\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{3}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|g\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

By combining (
4.27
)-(
4.29
) with (
4.26
) and absorbing
\epsilon
-terms into the left-hand side, we conclude that

(4.30)

\displaystyle\sup_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|p\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{2}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}p\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{3}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{3}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}:=

\displaystyle c\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{3,2}(\omega)}^{2}+c\|\eta_{0}\|_{W^{5,2}(\omega)}^{2}+c\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}

\displaystyle+c\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}(0)\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+c\|\mathbf{f}(0)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+c\|g(0)\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+c\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|g\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+c\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Finally, due to the continuity of the following embedding

\displaystyle L^{2}(I_{*};W^{6,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I_{*};W^{4,2}(\omega))\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(I_{*};W^{5,2}(\omega)),

we can also bound
\eta
in the latter space in terms of
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We now have all the required estimates for the fluid-structure subproblem so we proceed to the construction of a strong solution for the solute subproblem. Afterwards, we will use a fixed-point argument to close the full system.

4.2.
The solute subproblem

For a known flexible domain
\Omega_{\zeta}
and a known solenoidal vector field
\mathbf{v}
, we aim in this section to construct a strong solution of the following subproblem:

(4.31)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}=(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}+2\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})-2\mathbb{T}

on
I\times\Omega_{\zeta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
subject to the following initial condition

(4.32)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(0,\cdot)=\mathbb{T}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\zeta(0)}.

Let us start with a precise definition of what we mean by a strong solution.

Definition 4.2
.

Assume that
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{v},\zeta)
satisfies

(4.33)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big),

\displaystyle\zeta\in W^{3,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{6,2}(\omega)\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\zeta}=(\partial_{t}\zeta)\mathbf{n}\quad\text{on }I\times\omega,\quad\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L.

We call

\mathbb{T}

a
strong solution
of (
4.31
)-(
4.32
) with dataset
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{v},\zeta)
if

(a)

\mathbb{T}\in W^{1,\infty}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big)
;

(b)

Equation (
4.31
) is satisfied a.e. in
I\times\Omega_{\zeta}
.

We now formulate our result on the existence of a unique strong solution of (
4.31
)-(
4.32
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{v},\zeta)
satisfy (
4.33
).
Then there is a unique strong solution
\mathbb{T}
of (
4.31
)-(
4.32
), in the sense of Definition
4.2
,
such that

(4.34)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\Big(\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(1+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\bigg)e^{c\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}

holds.

Proof.

First of all, we note that given the regularity of the input
\zeta
, the embedding

\displaystyle W^{3,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{6,2}(\omega)\big)\hookrightarrow C^{1,\beta}(I\times\omega)

is continuous for all
\beta<\tfrac{1}{2}
, and so
\partial\Omega_{\zeta}
is of class
C^{1,\beta}
. The shell is thus, better than Lipschitz.

We wish to apply the method of characteristic to (
4.31
)-(
4.32
). Because the term
\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}
is not in the right form and we are unable to rewrite it as
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{T}
for some matrix,
\mathbb{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, we instead transform the matrix
\mathbb{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
into a vector
\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}
by way of the mapping

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&T_{12}&T_{13}\\
T_{21}&T_{22}&T_{23}\\
T_{31}&T_{32}&T_{33}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto(T_{11},T_{12},T_{13},T_{21},T_{22},T_{23},T_{31},T_{32}T_{33})^{\top}.

In so doing, the mapping
\mathbb{T}\mapsto\mathbf{T}
transforms (
4.31
)-(
4.32
) into the vector-valued system

(4.35)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{T}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{T}=(\mathbb{W}-2\mathbb{I})\mathbf{T}+\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega_{\zeta},

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(0,\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{T}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\zeta(0)}

which is in a desired form.
Here, we use the fact that the tensor
[(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}]\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
transforms to a vector
\mathbb{W}\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}

where
\mathbb{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{9\times 9}
is the block matrix given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{W}_{11}&\mathbb{W}_{12}&\mathbb{W}_{13}\\
\mathbb{W}_{21}&\mathbb{W}_{22}&\mathbb{W}_{23}\\
\mathbb{W}_{31}&\mathbb{W}_{32}&\mathbb{W}_{33}\end{pmatrix}_{9\times 9}

with sub entries

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}2\partial_{1}v_{1}&\partial_{1}v_{2}&\partial_{1}v_{3}\\
\partial_{2}v_{1}&\partial_{1}v_{1}+\partial_{2}v_{2}&\partial_{2}v_{3}\\
\partial_{3}v_{1}&\partial_{3}v_{2}&\partial_{1}v_{1}+\partial_{3}v_{3}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}v_{2}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{1}v_{2}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{1}v_{2}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{13}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}v_{3}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{1}v_{3}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{1}v_{3}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{21}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2}v_{1}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{2}v_{1}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{2}v_{1}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{22}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}v_{1}+\partial_{2}v_{2}&\partial_{1}v_{2}&\partial_{1}v_{3}\\
\partial_{2}v_{1}&2\partial_{2}v_{2}&\partial_{2}v_{3}\\
\partial_{3}v_{1}&\partial_{3}v_{2}&\partial_{2}v_{2}+\partial_{3}v_{3}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{23}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2}v_{3}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{2}v_{3}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{2}v_{3}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{31}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{3}v_{1}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{3}v_{1}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{3}v_{1}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{32}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{3}v_{2}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{3}v_{2}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{3}v_{2}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{33}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}v_{1}+\partial_{3}v_{3}&\partial_{1}v_{2}&\partial_{1}v_{3}\\
\partial_{2}v_{1}&\partial_{2}v_{2}+\partial_{3}v_{3}&\partial_{2}v_{3}\\
\partial_{3}v_{1}&\partial_{3}v_{2}&2\partial_{3}v_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

Also, the symmetric tensor
2\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
transforms to a vector
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}

given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}=(2\partial_{1}v_{1},\partial_{1}v_{2}+\partial_{2}v_{1},\partial_{1}v_{3}+\partial_{3}v_{1},\partial_{1}v_{2}+\partial_{2}v_{1},2\partial_{2}v_{2},\partial_{2}v_{3}+\partial_{3}v_{2},\partial_{1}v_{3}+\partial_{3}v_{1},\partial_{2}v_{3}+\partial_{3}v_{2},2\partial_{3}v_{3})^{\top}.

With these definitions, we directly obtain the following equivalent norm relations

(4.36)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\sim\|\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{\zeta})},

(4.37)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{W}\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\sim\|\mathbb{W}\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\sim\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{s+1,p}(\Omega_{\zeta})}.

for any
s\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
.
We now apply the method of characteristics to (
4.35
). Here, we note that
due to the kinematically compatible condition

\mathbf{v}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\zeta}=(\partial_{t}\zeta)\mathbf{n}
at the boundary, a characteristic curve stays in the moving domain for all times.
Thus, we define
\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x})
to be the unique solution (follows from Picard–Lindelöf) to the equation:

(4.38)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x})}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=\mathbf{v}(t,\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x})),

(4.39)

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}(0,\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}

so that
\mathbf{T}
solves

(4.40)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{T}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=\big(\mathbb{W}(t,\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x}))-2\mathbb{I}\big)\mathbf{T}(t,\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x}))+\mathbf{W}(t,\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega_{\zeta},

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(0,\bm{\Phi}(0,\mathbf{x}))=\mathbf{T}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\zeta(0)}.

More precisely, the solution to (
4.40
) is obtained by employing the integrating factor

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\big(2\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{W}(t^{\prime},\bm{\Phi}(t^{\prime},\mathbf{x}))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}

leading to

(4.41)

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(t,\mathbf{x})=\frac{\mathbf{T}_{0}(\mathbf{x})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}(2\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{W}(s,\bm{\Phi}(s,\mathbf{x})))\,\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{W}(t^{\prime},\bm{\Phi}(t^{\prime},\mathbf{x}))\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}(2\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{W}(t^{\prime},\bm{\Phi}(t^{\prime},\mathbf{x})))\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}}.

Note that uniqueness of the solution (
4.41
) is a direct consequence of the linearity of (
4.40
) since the difference of any two solution will satisfy the easier homogeneous version of (
4.40
) with

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}(t,\bm{\Phi}(t,\mathbf{x}))\equiv 0,\qquad\mathbf{T}_{0}(\mathbf{x})=0.

Now observe that if
\mathbf{v}\in L^{1}(I;W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))
, then by
(
4.37
),
\mathbb{W}\in L^{1}(I;W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))
. Thus, we can use Jensen’s inequality and convexity of the exponential function to conclude that

\displaystyle\|e^{\pm\int_{0}^{t}(2\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{W}(t^{\prime},\bm{\Phi}(t^{\prime},\mathbf{x})))\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\lesssim 1

for
\mathbf{v}\in L^{1}(I;W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))
.
Consequently, since
W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})
is a Banach algebra, we obtain from (
4.41
) that

(4.42)

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}\in C(\overline{I};W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))

provided
\mathbf{T}_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta_{0}})
and from (
4.42
) and (
4.36
), we finally obtain the existence of the unique strong solution

(4.43)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}\in C(\overline{I};W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})).

For the estimate (
4.34
), we consider all multi-indexes satisfying
|\alpha|\leq 3
and apply
\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}
to (
4.31
). This result in

(4.44)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}+(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T})(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}+2\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle-2\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}+R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}

where

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}((\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}),

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T})(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}),

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T})-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}((\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{T})

are commutators which can be estimated using the commmutator estimate
[
10
, (2.3)]
to obtain

\displaystyle\|R_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})},

\displaystyle\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|R_{3}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}.

Thus, if we now test (
4.44
) with
\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}
and use Reynold’s transport theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

If we sum the estimate above over all
|\alpha|\leq 3
, we obtain by Grönwall’s lemma that

\displaystyle\sup_{I}\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\lesssim\Big(\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)e^{c\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}.

By returning to the original equation (
4.31
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{I}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\Big(1+\sup_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\Big)+\sup_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(1+\sup_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\Big)e^{c\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}+\sup_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(1+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\bigg)e^{c\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

where we have used the continuous embedding

\displaystyle L^{2}(I;W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))\cap W^{1,2}(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))\hookrightarrow W^{2/3,2}(I;W^{7/2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(I;W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))

in the last step. Combining the two supremum estimates above yield

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\Big(\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\Big)\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(1+\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{c\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}

which can further be bounded by the desired simplified right-hand side given in
(
4.34
). This completes the proof.
∎

5.
The fully coupled system

To construct a local-in-time solution for the fully coupled system, we resort to a fixed-point argument. The existence of such a fixed-point will follow from closedness and contraction properties for a suitable map. More precisely, to show the closedness property, we consider the mapping
\mathtt{T}=\mathtt{T}_{1}\circ\mathtt{T}_{2}
with

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}(\underline{\mathbb{T}})=\mathbb{T},\qquad\mathtt{T}_{2}(\underline{\mathbb{T}})=(\eta,\mathbf{u},p),\qquad\mathtt{T}_{1}(\eta,\mathbf{u},p)=\mathbb{T}

defined on the space

\displaystyle X_{\eta}

\displaystyle:=L^{\infty}(I_{*};W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta}))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I_{*};W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})),

equipped with its canonical norm
\|\cdot\|_{X_{\eta}}
and where
I_{*}
is the local time yet to be determined.
We now consider the subset

\displaystyle B_{R}:=\big\{\underline{\mathbb{T}}\in X_{\eta}\,:\,\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}\leq R^{2}\big\}.

and show that
\mathtt{T}:X_{\eta}\rightarrow X_{\eta}
maps
B_{R}
into
B_{R}
, i.e., for any
\underline{\mathbb{T}}\in B_{R}
, we have that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}=\|\mathtt{T}(\underline{\mathbb{T}})\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}=\|\mathtt{T}_{1}\circ\mathtt{T}_{2}(\underline{\mathbb{T}})\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}=\|\mathtt{T}_{1}(\eta,\mathbf{u},p)\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}\leq R^{2}.

Indeed, for

\displaystyle(\eta,\mathbf{u})\in

\displaystyle W^{1,\infty}\big(I_{*};W^{3,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I_{*};W^{5,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{3,2}\big(I_{*};L^{2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{2,2}\big(I_{*};W^{2,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I_{*};W^{6,2}(\omega)\big)

\displaystyle\times W^{2,2}\big(I_{*};L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap W^{1,2}\big(I_{*};W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I_{*};W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big),

let
\mathbb{T}
be the unique strong solution of (
4.31
)-(
4.32
) with dataset
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u},\eta)
as shown in Theorem
4.3
.
As shown in Theorem
4.3
, the tensor
\mathbb{T}
will satisfy

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I_{*}}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(1+\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I_{*}}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)e^{c\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}.

On the other hand, for
\underline{\mathbb{T}}\in B_{R}
, let
(\eta,\mathbf{u},p)
be the unique strong solution of (
4.1
)–(
4.6
) with data
(\mathbf{f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\underline{\mathbb{T}})
as shown in Theorem
1.2
. Then by Proposition
4.1
, we can control

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I_{*}}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

in terms of

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{data})+\int_{I_{*}}\Big(\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{data})

\displaystyle:=\|\eta_{\star}\|_{W^{3,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\eta_{0}\|_{W^{5,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}(0)\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{f}(0)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|g\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

However, by using Hölder’s inequality in time, we obtain

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{*}}\Big(\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim T_{*}\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}.

We also note that for
R>0
large enough and
T_{*}
very small (say
T_{*}<\tfrac{1}{R^{2}}
), we can use Hölder inequality in time and (
5.3
)-(
5.4
) to obtain

(5.5)

\displaystyle e^{c\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{cT_{*}^{1/2}\sqrt{\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{cT_{*}^{1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{data})+T_{*}\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{cT_{*}^{1/2}\sqrt{c+T_{*}R^{2}}}.

If we now combine all of (
5.2
)-(
5.5
), then for
R>0
large enough and
T_{*}
very small (say
T_{*}<\tfrac{1}{R^{2}}
),
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}\|_{X_{\eta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+T_{*}R^{2}\Big)\Big(c+T_{*}R^{2}\Big)e^{cT_{*}^{1/2}\sqrt{c+T_{*}R^{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+1\Big)\Big(c+1\Big)e^{cR^{-1}\sqrt{c+1}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}e^{c_{2}R^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq R^{2}

for
R\ln({R^{2}}/{c_{1}})\geq c_{2}
. Thus,
\mathtt{T}:X_{\eta}\rightarrow X_{\eta}
maps
B_{R}
into
B_{R}
as desired in (
5.1
).

Now, for the contraction property, we consider the superset
Y_{\eta}\supseteq X_{\eta}
given by

\displaystyle Y_{\eta}

\displaystyle:=L^{\infty}(I_{*};L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})),

and equipped with its canonical norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y_{\eta}}
, and show that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathtt{T}(\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{2})\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{2})\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}

hold for any pair of strong solutions
\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{i}\in X_{\eta_{1}}
,
i=1,2
for the solute subproblem (
4.31
)-(
4.32
)
with dataset
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{i},\eta_{i})
,
i=1,2
, respectively. To show (
5.6
), we note that since the fluid domain depends on the deformation of the shell, we have to transform one solution, say
\mathbb{T}_{2}
, to the domain of
\mathbb{T}_{1}
to get a difference estimate that is well-defined on a single domain. For this, we set
\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}=\mathbb{T}_{2}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}
,
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}=\mathbf{u}_{2}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}
and obtain (see
[
19
]
for further details)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})^{\top}+2\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})-2\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\mathbb{H}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2})

defined on
{I_{*}}\times\Omega_{\eta_{1}}
where

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2})=

\displaystyle(1-J_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}})\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}-J_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}^{-1}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}(\mathbb{B}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}-\mathbb{I})(\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\mathbb{I})+(\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\mathbb{I})(\mathbb{B}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}-\mathbb{I})^{\top}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})^{\top}

\displaystyle+\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}(\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{B}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}})+2(1-J_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}})\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}.

We now consider the difference

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{12}:=\mathbb{T}_{1}-\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2},\quad\mathbf{u}_{12}=\mathbf{u}_{1}-\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\quad\eta_{12}=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}

and find that
\mathbb{T}_{12}
solves

(5.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}_{12}+\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}_{12}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbb{T}_{12}+\mathbb{T}_{12}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{1})^{\top}+2\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12})-2\mathbb{T}_{12}

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12})^{\top}-\mathbf{u}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}-\mathbb{H}_{-\eta_{12}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2})

on
{I_{*}}\times\Omega_{\eta_{1}}
with identically zero initial condition.
If we now test (
5.7
) with
\mathbb{T}_{12}
, then for
t\in{I_{*}}
, we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+2\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{1}||\mathbb{T}_{12}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+2\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}||\mathbb{T}_{12}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}\big[\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12})^{\top}\big]:\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}:\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}\mathbb{H}_{-\eta_{12}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}):\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=:K_{1}+\ldots+K_{5}

Firstly, by using the high regularity of the extra stress tensor and the small acceleration estimate (
4.12
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

Next, we note that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

hold for any
\delta>0
and similarly,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(K_{3}+K_{4})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

for any
\delta>0
. To deal with
K_{5}
we first expand it as follows

(5.11)

\displaystyle K_{5}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}(1-J_{-\eta_{12}})\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}:\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}(1-J_{-\eta_{12}})\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}:\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}J_{-\eta_{12}}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta_{12}}^{-1}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta_{12}}:\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}(\mathbb{B}_{-\eta_{12}}-\mathbb{I})(\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\mathbb{I}):\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}(\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}+\mathbb{I})(\mathbb{B}_{-\eta_{12}}-\mathbb{I})^{\top}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})^{\top}:\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\eta_{1}}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}(\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{B}_{-\eta_{12}}):\mathbb{T}_{12}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle K_{5}^{1}+\ldots+K_{5}^{6}.

Then we have by interpolation

\displaystyle K_{5}^{1}+K_{5}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,4}(\omega)}\big(\|\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}+\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\big)\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+c(\delta)\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big(\|\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}+\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\big).

Next, we have

\displaystyle K_{5}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+c(\delta)\|\partial_{t}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}.

as well as

\displaystyle K_{5}^{4}+K_{5}^{5}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,4}(\omega)}(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}+1)\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+c(\delta)\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+1).

Similarly,

\displaystyle K_{5}^{6}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,4}(\omega)}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+c(\delta)\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}

Thus,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{5}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\leq

\displaystyle\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big(\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+1\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

If we combine the estimates
K_{1},\ldots,K_{5}
and absorb the
\delta
-terms (by choosing
\delta
very small) into the left-hand side,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}_{12}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

for all
t\in{I_{*}}
so that by Grönwall’s lemma and the fact that
\mathbf{u}_{1}
is a strong solution,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I_{*}}\|\mathbb{T}_{12}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\lesssim\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Now, following from Theorem
1.2
, let consider two strong solutions
(\eta_{i},\mathbf{u}_{i},p_{i})
,
i=1,2
of (
4.1
)–(
4.6
) with data
(\mathbf{f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{i})
, respectively. For

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{12}:=\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{1}-\underline{\overline{\mathbb{T}}}_{2},\quad\mathbf{u}_{12}=\mathbf{u}_{1}-\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\quad\eta_{12}=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},

where
\underline{\overline{\mathbb{T}}}_{2}:=\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{2}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}}
, it follows from
[
6
, Remark 5.2]
(c.f. (
4.8
)) that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in{I_{*}}}\|\eta_{12}\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta_{12}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{*}}\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{12}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim T_{*}\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{12}\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}.

Inserting into (
5.13
) then yields

(5.14)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cT_{*}\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{12}\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}.

If we choose
T_{*}<\tfrac{1}{2c}
,
then we conclude from (
5.14
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}_{12}\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\underline{\mathbb{T}}_{12}\|_{Y_{\eta_{1}}}^{2}.

The existence of the desired fixed point now follows and finishes the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.4
.

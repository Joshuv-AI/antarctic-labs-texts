Risk factors for post-PCI restenosis in patients with coronary heart disease and the development and validation of a nomogram prediction model
Authors: Xin Jin, Zhengliang Dong, Dongsheng Chai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to analyze risk factors for in-stent restenosis (ISR) following percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in patients with coronary heart disease and develop and validate a nomogram prediction model. Clinical data were retrospectively collected from 1135 patients with coronary heart disease who underwent PCI at our hospital between January 2015 and December 2024. The occurrence of ISR within 1 year after PCI was defined as the outcome event. Using R software, the patients were randomly divided into a modeling group (795 cases) and a validation group (340 cases) in a 7:3 ratio. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent risk factors for ISR, and a nomogram prediction model was constructed based on the results of the multivariate analysis. The model’s discriminatory ability was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic curves and the area under the curve (AUC). Model calibration and clinical utility were assessed using calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA). Internal validation was conducted using Bootstrap resampling combined with 10-fold cross-validation. A total of 61 patients developed ISR, with an incidence rate of 5.37%, including 43 cases in the modeling group and 18 cases in the validation group. Results of multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that diabetes (odds ratio [OR] = 2.578, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.143–3.528), multivessel disease (OR = 2.361, 95% CI: 1.689–3.490), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) ≥ 1.8 mmol/L (OR = 1.462, 95% CI: 1.290–2.160), total stent length ≥ 30 mm (OR = 3.142, 95% CI: 2.322–4.657), and minimum stent diameter < 3.0 mm (OR = 3.633, 95% CI: 2.588–5.102) were identified as independent risk factors for post-PCI ISR (all P < .05). The AUC of the model was 0.833 in the modeling group and 0.822 in the validation group, indicating that the model demonstrated good discriminatory ability in both groups. The calibration curve and DCA results demonstrate that the model has good clinical utility. Post-PCI ISR is associated with multiple factors. The nomogram demonstrated good predictive performance in the present cohort and may assist in the early identification of patients at high risk of post-PCI ISR. Further external validation is required before broader clinical application.

1. Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is one of the major cardiovascular diseases that seriously threatens human health worldwide, with its incidence and mortality rates showing an upward trend year by year.[1] Due to its advantages of minimal invasiveness, rapid recovery, and proven efficacy, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has become a key therapeutic approach for patients with CAD, particularly those with coronary artery stenosis.[2,3] With the continuous advancement of interventional techniques and the widespread use of drug-eluting stents (DES), both the short-term and long-term outcomes for patients following PCI have improved significantly. However, in-stent restenosis (ISR) remains one of the major issues affecting the long-term efficacy of PCI.[4,5]

ISR refers to the recurrence of vascular stenosis within or at the margins of the stent following PCI. Its pathogenesis is complex, involving multiple pathophysiological processes such as intimal hyperplasia, inflammatory responses, endothelial dysfunction, and vascular remodeling.[6,7] Previous studies[8] have shown that even with the widespread use of second-generation DES, the incidence of ISR remains approximately 5 to 10%. This not only increases the risk of repeat revascularization but can also lead to recurrent angina, acute coronary syndrome, and even cardiovascular death, severely impacting patients’ quality of life and long-term prognosis. Therefore, the early identification of high-risk patients for post-PCI ISR and the implementation of targeted interventions are of significant clinical importance for improving patient outcomes.

Currently, there are numerous studies on ISR risk factors both domestically and internationally,[9–11] and it is generally accepted that diabetes, hyperlipidemia, small vessel disease, long lesions, and complex coronary lesions are closely associated with the occurrence of ISR. However, there remain certain discrepancies in results across different studies, and most studies are limited to the analysis of risk factors, lacking individualized predictive tools based on comprehensive multivariate assessment. Furthermore, due to differences in patient baseline characteristics, lesion types, and interventional treatment strategies across regions, establishing an ISR risk prediction model applicable to patients at our center holds practical significance.

As a visual tool widely used in clinical predictive research in recent years, the nomogram can integrate multiple independent risk factors to quantitatively predict an individual’s risk of event occurrence. It offers advantages such as ease of use, high intuitiveness, and significant clinical value. Currently, nomograms have been widely applied in fields such as oncology and cardiovascular disease; however, relevant research on ISR following PCI remains relatively limited.[12,13]

Therefore, this study conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical data from patients with coronary heart disease at our hospital following PCI to identify independent risk factors for ISR. Based on multivariable logistic regression analysis, a nomogram prediction model was constructed and internally validated, with the aim of providing a reference for the early identification and clinical intervention of high-risk patients for post-PCI ISR.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data sources and collection

This study is a single-center retrospective cohort study. Data were obtained from our hospital’s electronic medical record system and coronary intervention database, with cases enrolled between January 2015 and December 2024. Uniform inclusion and exclusion criteria, along with standardized data collection procedures, were applied throughout the study to minimize selection and information biases. All case data were independently screened and extracted by 2 researchers; in cases of disagreement, a senior physician reviewed and adjudicated the data to ensure accuracy and completeness.

The study design and implementation adhered to the STROBE guidelines for observational studies and the TRIPOD guidelines for clinical prediction model reporting. As this study involved a retrospective analysis of anonymized medical records, all patient-identifiable information was de-identified, eliminating the need for additional invasive procedures and the risk of privacy breaches. The study protocol complied with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was reviewed and approved by the hospital’s ethics committee, with patient informed consent waived.

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: Patients meeting diagnostic criteria for coronary heart disease who underwent PCI; implantation of at least one coronary stent; completion of post-procedural coronary angiography or coronary CTA follow-up; complete clinical data, including general information, laboratory parameters, coronary lesion characteristics, and PCI-related data; age ≥ 18 years. Exclusion criteria: Patients with severe heart failure (NYHA Class IV) or severe arrhythmias; patients with severe hepatic or renal insufficiency or malignant tumors; patients with acute infectious diseases, autoimmune diseases, or hematological disorders; patients with a history of coronary artery bypass grafting; incomplete follow-up data or lost to follow-up; patients with psychiatric disorders or poor compliance who were unable to complete standardized follow-up; patients with poor-quality imaging data that did not allow for a clear determination of ISR.

2.3. Collection of relevant variables

Relevant clinical data from the perioperative period of PCI in patients with coronary heart disease were collected. General demographic information, data on underlying conditions, and laboratory parameters were based on the results of the first examination conducted prior to PCI after hospital admission; characteristics of coronary artery lesions and PCI-related data were derived from coronary angiography and surgical records. To facilitate statistical analysis and clinical application, variables in this study were dichotomized according to commonly used clinical standards, as follows:

General demographics: gender (male/female); age (≥65 years/<65 years); body mass index (BMI) (≥24 kg/m2/<24 kg/m2); smoking history (yes/no); alcohol consumption history (yes/no).

Here, smoking history is defined as past or current continuous smoking for ≥6 months; alcohol consumption history is defined as regular alcohol consumption ≥1 time per week for more than 6 months.

Underlying medical conditions: Hypertension (yes/no); diabetes (yes/no); hyperlipidemia (yes/no); history of myocardial infarction (yes/no).

Here, hypertension is defined as a previous confirmed diagnosis of hypertension or long-term use of antihypertensive medication; diabetes is defined as a previous confirmed diagnosis of diabetes or long-term treatment with antidiabetic medication; hyperlipidemia is defined as a previous confirmed diagnosis of hyperlipidemia or long-term use of lipid-lowering medication.

Laboratory parameters: low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) (≥1.8 mmol/L/<1.8 mmol/L); serum creatinine (≥110 μmol/L/<110 μmol/L); C-reactive protein (CRP) (≥10 mg/L/<10 mg/L).

Specifically, 1.8 mmol/L serves as the cutoff value for determining whether LDL-C meets the target; serum creatinine is used to reflect renal function; and CRP is used to assess systemic inflammation levels.

Characteristics of coronary artery lesions: number of affected vessels (multivessel disease/single-vessel disease); bifurcation lesions (present/absent); calcified lesions (present/absent).

Specifically, multivessel disease is defined as stenotic lesions in ≥2 major coronary arteries; bifurcation disease is defined as coronary artery lesions involving the bifurcation of major vessels; and calcified lesions are determined based on coronary angiography results.

PCI-related data: total stent length (≥30 mm/<30 mm); minimum stent diameter (<3 mm/≥3 mm).

Here, total stent length refers to the sum of the lengths of all stents implanted during a single PCI procedure; minimum stent diameter refers to the smallest diameter among the implanted stents.

2.4. Definition of outcomes

In this study, the occurrence of ISR within 1 year after PCI was used as the outcome variable. ISR is defined as a degree of stenosis ≥ 50% within the stent or within 5 mm of the stent margin, as indicated by coronary angiography or coronary CTA during the 1-year post-PCI follow-up period.

All patients received standardized medical therapy in accordance with coronary heart disease guidelines, including dual antiplatelet therapy, lipid-lowering therapy, and other basic treatments. Postoperatively, coronary angiography or coronary CTA was performed periodically as clinically indicated, with imaging results independently interpreted by 2 experienced interventional cardiologists; in cases of disagreement, a senior physician conducted a further review and confirmation.

Based on follow-up results, patients were classified into ISR and non-ISR groups, and risk factors associated with ISR after PCI were further analyzed.

2.5. Statistical analysis

To thoroughly explore predictive models for ISR following PCI in patients with CAD, this study employed a stratified random sampling strategy. The collected clinical data were divided into a modeling group (70%) and a validation group (30%) in a 7:3 ratio using R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria; version 4.2.1). Data from the modeling group were used for model construction and preliminary validation, while data from the validation group were used to independently assess the model’s generalization ability.

Prior to modeling, all candidate predictor variables underwent data preprocessing and multicollinearity diagnosis. The overall missing data rate in this study was <5%; cases with missing data for core study variables were directly excluded, and only samples with complete data were included in the analysis. Sample size was determined based on the events-per-variable criterion for clinical predictive models (events-per-variable ≥ 10). The number of outcome events in this study was sufficient and met the requirements for predictive model construction. Variance inflation factor was used to diagnose multicollinearity; all variables had variance inflation factor values <5, indicating no significant multicollinearity and meeting the requirements for binary logistic regression modeling.

First, univariate analysis of the study data was performed using SPSS 27.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk). Categorical data were presented as counts (%) and compared between groups using the chi-square (χ2) test; univariate logistic regression analysis was used to assess the association between each clinical variable and the occurrence of post-PCI ISR. A cutoff of P < .10 was set as the screening criterion, and statistically significant variables were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent risk factors for post-PCI ISR. In the multivariate analysis, a P-value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Based on the results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis, a nomogram prediction model was constructed using R software to visually demonstrate the contribution of each independent risk factor to the risk of post-PCI ISR. Furthermore, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted separately for the modeling and validation groups, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess the model’s discriminatory ability. The closer the AUC value is to 1, the better the model’s predictive performance.

To further evaluate the model’s fit, a calibration curve was plotted to assess the consistency between the model’s predicted probability and the actual occurrence probability. If the calibration curve approaches the ideal diagonal line, it indicates that the model has good calibration ability. Concurrently, decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to evaluate the model’s clinical utility, with its clinical value determined by comparing the net benefit of the model at different threshold probabilities.

Furthermore, to enhance the stability and reliability of the model validation results, this study employed Bootstrap resampling (1000 times) combined with 10-fold cross-validation for internal validation. Finally, by integrating the results of the ROC curve, calibration curve, and DCA, the model’s discriminatory ability, calibration capability, and clinical utility were comprehensively evaluated.

3. Results

3.1. General characteristics

The patient selection process for this study is shown in Figure 1. From January 2015 to December 2024, a total of 1342 patients with coronary heart disease who underwent PCI were screened. Following the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 1135 patients were ultimately enrolled. Among them, 61 cases of in-stent restenosis (ISR) occurred post-PCI, with an incidence rate of 5.37%. After randomization in a 7:3 ratio, 795 patients were assigned to the modeling group and 340 to the validation group. In the modeling group, 43 patients experienced ISR, while 18 in the validation group did so.

3.3. Construction and validation of the nomogram prediction model

Based on the independent risk factors identified through multivariate logistic regression analysis – including diabetes, multivessel disease, LDL-C ≥ 1.8 mmol/L, total stent length ≥ 30 mm, and minimum stent diameter < 3.0 mm – a nomogram prediction model for ISR after PCI was constructed (Fig. 2). By assigning corresponding scores to each independent risk factor, this model enables individualized prediction of the risk of post-PCI ISR, providing clinicians with a simple and intuitive risk assessment tool.

Nomogram prediction model for ISR after PCI. ISR = in-stent restenosis, PCI = percutaneous coronary intervention.

ROC curves were further plotted in the development and validation groups to evaluate the model’s predictive performance. The results of the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test showed χ2 = 6.842 (P = .527) in the modeling group and χ2 = 7.116 (P = .503) in the validation group, indicating that the model fits well and that the predicted results are highly consistent with the observed outcomes. Analysis of the ROC curves revealed an AUC of 0.833 for the modeling group and 0.822 for the validation group (Fig. 3A and B), indicating that the model possesses good discriminatory ability and can effectively distinguish between high-risk and low-risk patients for ISR following PCI.

Calibration curves of the nomogram model for predicting ISR after PCI in the modeling group (C) and validation group (D). Decision-making curves (DCA) for the nomogram model predicting post-PCI ISR in the modeling group (E) and validation group (F). PCI = percutaneous coronary intervention.

To further enhance the reliability of the model validation results, this study employed bootstrap resampling combined with 10-fold cross-validation for internal validation. The results showed that the model’s AUC was 0.826, with a sensitivity of 79.1% and a specificity of 78.4%, indicating that the model possesses good stability and generalization ability.

Additionally, to verify the consistency between the risk predicted by the nomogram and the actual incidence of ISR, calibration curves were further plotted (Fig. 3C and D). The results showed that the predicted curve closely approximated the ideal diagonal line, indicating that the model possesses good calibration. DCA (Fig. 3E and F) revealed that the model demonstrated high net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities, suggesting its significant clinical utility.

4. Discussion

As a key treatment for CAD, PCI effectively alleviates coronary stenosis, relieves symptoms of myocardial ischemia, and reduces the risk of cardiovascular events. With the widespread use of DES, the short- and long-term outcomes of patients following PCI have improved significantly. However, ISR remains one of the major issues affecting the long-term efficacy of PCI.[14] ISR not only increases the risk of repeat revascularization but also leads to recurrent angina, acute coronary syndrome, and even severe cardiovascular events, significantly impacting patients’ quality of life and prognosis.[15] Therefore, early identification of high-risk patients for ISR and the implementation of targeted interventions are of great clinical significance.

This study conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical data from 1135 patients with coronary heart disease who underwent PCI. The results showed that the incidence of ISR after PCI was 5.37%, which is generally consistent with the 5 to 10% reported in previous studies from the DES era, suggesting that the findings of this study possess a certain degree of reliability and clinical representativeness. Through univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses, this study further identified diabetes, LDL-C ≥ 1.8 mmol/L, multivessel disease, total stent length ≥ 30 mm, and minimum stent diameter < 3.0 mm as independent risk factors for post-PCI ISR. A nomogram prediction model was constructed based on these variables. The model’s AUC values in the training and validation groups were 0.833 and 0.822, respectively, indicating good discriminatory ability and stability. Furthermore, the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, calibration curves, and DCA results further confirmed the model’s good fit and clinical utility.

Diabetes was identified as one of the key risk factors in this study. Previous studies[16,17] have shown that prolonged hyperglycemia in diabetic patients can lead to impaired vascular endothelial function, persistent activation of the inflammatory response, and abnormal proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells, thereby promoting neointimal formation and vascular remodeling and increasing the risk of ISR. Furthermore, diabetic patients often present with more severe diffuse CAD and microvascular dysfunction, which further increases the incidence of adverse events following PCI.[18] The results of this study suggest that diabetic patients have a significantly elevated risk of ISR, consistent with previous findings. Therefore, for PCI patients with diabetes, greater emphasis should be placed on perioperative glycemic control, standardized antidiabetic therapy, and long-term follow-up to reduce the risk of ISR.

Elevated LDL-C levels are also a major risk factor for ISR. Numerous studies[19–21] have confirmed that elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol promotes the progression of atherosclerosis and local inflammatory responses, accelerating intimal hyperplasia. In this study, an LDL-C level of ≥1.8 mmol/L was used as the cutoff; the results showed that patients who did not achieve LDL-C control targets had a significantly increased risk of ISR. This suggests that intensive lipid-lowering therapy following PCI is crucial for reducing the incidence of ISR. Current guidelines recommend that high-risk patients with coronary heart disease actively undergo high-intensity statin therapy and maintain LDL-C at the lowest possible levels to improve long-term prognosis. In recent years, the use of novel lipid-lowering agents, such as PCSK9 inhibitors, has also provided new therapeutic approaches for further reducing the risk of ISR.[22]

This study also found that multivessel disease is a significant risk factor for ISR following PCI. Multivessel disease typically indicates a more severe degree of coronary atherosclerosis, a broader extent of lesions, and more complex vascular conditions.[23,24] Such patients often require the implantation of more stents or longer stents, and the interventional procedure is more complex; consequently, they are more prone to vascular injury and intimal hyperplasia, ultimately increasing the risk of ISR. Previous studies[25,26] have also indicated that complex coronary lesions are closely associated with adverse events following PCI, and the results of this study further support this view. Therefore, for patients with multivessel disease, the complexity of the lesions should be thoroughly assessed prior to PCI, and a more individualized interventional treatment strategy should be formulated.

Stent-related factors also play a significant role in the development of ISR. The results of this study indicate that total stent length ≥ 30 mm and minimum stent diameter < 3.0 mm are both important independent risk factors for ISR. Among these, small-vessel lesions are considered one of the most classic risk factors for ISR.[27] The smaller the vessel diameter, the more pronounced the impact of neointimal hyperplasia of the same degree on the luminal area, making restenosis more likely to occur.[28] Furthermore, longer stents imply a greater extent of vascular injury and a longer area of foreign body exposure, which can induce more pronounced inflammatory responses and intimal hyperplasia, thereby increasing the risk of ISR.[29] Therefore, during PCI, stent selection and implantation strategies should be optimized as much as possible to reduce the use of unnecessarily long stents and to ensure adequate lesion pretreatment, thereby lowering the risk of ISR. Concurrently, the widespread application of intravascular imaging technologies, such as intravascular ultrasound and optical coherence tomography, can further enhance the precision of stent implantation, thereby improving long-term PCI outcomes.[30]

In recent years, nomograms have been widely adopted in clinical predictive modeling research due to their advantages of visualizability, individualization, and ease of use. In this study, a nomogram prediction model was constructed based on the results of multivariate logistic regression analysis, and the model was evaluated using ROC curves, calibration curves, and DCA. The results demonstrated that the model had good discrimination and calibration and showed potential clinical utility in the present cohort, suggesting that it may assist clinicians in identifying patients at high risk of ISR following PCI. Compared with traditional single-risk-factor assessment, nomograms can comprehensively quantify risk by integrating multiple clinical variables, which aligns more closely with the development direction of precision medicine.

Furthermore, this study employed a combined training and validation group approach for internal validation and further evaluated model stability through Bootstrap resampling combined with 10-fold cross-validation. The results supported the internal predictive performance and stability of the model. However, because the model was developed and validated using data from a single center, its generalizability and clinical applicability require further evaluation through external validation and prospective studies.

Of course, this study still has certain limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective study, it may be subject to selection bias and information bias. Second, some potential confounding factors – such as postoperative medication adherence, stent type, post-stent dilation status, and dynamic changes in postoperative inflammatory levels – were not included in the analysis, which may have influenced the results. Furthermore, this study only conducted internal validation; external validation across multiple centers is still lacking, and the model’s generalizability requires further research to confirm. Future large-scale, multicenter, prospective studies are needed to further optimize the ISR prediction model and enhance its clinical applicability.

5. Conclusion

In-stent restenosis (ISR) following PCI is associated with multiple factors. Diabetes, LDL-C ≥ 1.8 mmol/L, multivessel disease, total stent length ≥ 30 mm, and minimum stent diameter < 3.0 mm were independently associated with post-PCI ISR in the present cohort. The nomogram developed using these factors demonstrated good discrimination, calibration, and potential clinical utility in the present cohort and may assist clinicians in the early identification of patients at high risk of ISR. Further multicenter prospective studies and external validation are required before the model can be applied in broader clinical settings.

Abstract
. We show that the mutation of τ \tau -exceptional sequences over cyclic Nakayama algebras Λ \Lambda where all projectives have length exactly | Λ | |\Lambda| respects the braid group relations.



1.
Introduction

Exceptional sequences for finite dimensional algebras were first studied in
[
Cra93
]
and
[
Rin94
]
. Crawley-Boevey showed that there is a transitive braid group action on complete exceptional sequences for hereditary algebras over algebraically closed fields, and Ringel generalized this to arbitrary hereditary artin algebras. For non-hereditary algebras, however, exceptional sequences are not as well-behaved and complete exceptional sequences might not exist. Using
\tau
-tilting theory, Buan and Marsh proposed in
[
BM21
]
a generalization of exceptional sequences for finite dimensional algebras by introducing
\tau
-exceptional sequences
, which coincide with exceptional sequences for hereditary algebras. However, it was only recently that a mutation operation on these
\tau
-exceptional sequences was suggested: In
[
BHM24
]
, Buan, Hanson and Marsh define a mutation on
\tau
-exceptional sequences for general finite-dimensional algebras, which coincides with the classical mutation for hereditary algebras. Further, Nonis shows in
[
Non25
]
that the mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences over algebras on the form
R\otimes kQ
, for
Q
an acyclic quiver and
R
a finite dimensional commutative local
k
-algebra, also coincides exactly with the mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences over
kQ
, and therefore that this mutation respects the braid group relations.

In this paper, we continue the hunt for algebras such that the mutation on
\tau
-exceptional sequences over them gives a braid group action. In particular we utilize the properties of mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences over Nakayama algebras developed in
[
BKT25
]
to connect the work of Crawley-Boevey
[
Cra93
]
and Ringel
[
Rin94
]
with the work of Buan, Marsh and Hanson
[
BHM24
]
by proving the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

4.14
)
.

Let
C_{n}
be the Nakayama algebra given as a quotient of the
n
-cycle path algebra over a field
k
, modulo the ideal generated by paths of length
n
. Then mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences over
C_{n}
respects the braid group relations.

The paper is structured as follows: We first recall the basics of
\tau
-tilting theory including
\tau
-exceptional sequences, then discuss mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences generally before working with a specific class of Nakayama algebras to obtain the result above.

2.
Background

All algebras are in this paper assumed to be basic, finite dimensional algebras over a field
k
. Given an algebra
\Lambda
, we denote by
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
the category of finite-dimensional left
\Lambda
-modules. We assume all modules to be basic, and denote by
|M|
and
\ell(M)
the number of indecomposable direct summands and the length of a module
M
respectively. We let
\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
denote the subcategory of projective
\Lambda
-modules. We reserve the constant
n
to refer to the rank of an algebra
\Lambda
.

Given a module
M
in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
, we denote by
\mathsf{add}M
the subcategory of modules which are direct sums of direct summands of
M
. We denote by
\mathsf{Gen}M
the subcategory of modules
N
such that there exists an epimorphism in
\Hom(\mathsf{add}M,N)
. We also have the left and right
perpendicular subcategories

M^{\perp}=\{X\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda\mid\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,X)=0\}
and
{}^{\perp}M=\{X\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda\mid\Hom_{\Lambda}(X,M)=0\}
.

Given a subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
and a
\Lambda
-module
M
, a
left
\mathcal{C}
-approximation
of
M
is a map
f\colon M\to X
where
X\in\mathcal{X}
such that any map
g\colon M\to X^{\prime}
for
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
factors through
f
, i.e, there exists an endomorphism
h\colon X\to X
such that
h\circ f=g
. Right approximations are defined dually. An approximation is
minimal
if the map is minimal in the usual sense.

2.1.
\tau
-tilting theory

Closely following
[
AIR14
]
, we recall the basics of
\tau
-tilting theory.

Definition 2.1
.

[
AIR14
, Def. 0.1]

A module
M
is called
\tau
-rigid
if
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\tau M)=0
, where
\tau
denotes the Auslander-Reiten translate in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
. A
\tau
-rigid module
M
with
|M|=n
is called a
\tau
-tilting module.

Given a
\tau
-rigid module
M
, one might ask which modules
X
are compatible with
M
in the sense that
M\oplus X
is
\tau
-rigid. We are in particular interested in two dual notions of such compatible modules, namely those that maximize
\mathsf{Gen}(M\oplus X)
and those that minimize it.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a
\tau
-rigid module. Let
X\notin\mathsf{add}M
be an indecomposable module such that
X\oplus M
is
\tau
-rigid.

•

We say that an indecomposable module
X
is a direct summand of the
Bongartz complement

\mathrm{B}(M)
of
M
if and only if
X
is not generated by any
\tau
-rigid module
M\oplus N
unless
N
has
X
as a summand.

•

We say that an indecomposable module
X
is a direct summand of the
co-Bongartz complement

\mathrm{C}(M)
of
M
if and only if
X
is generated by
M
.

Remark 2.3
.

By
[
Dem+23
, Theorem 4.4]
the above definition is equivalent to the definition of Bongartz completions appearing in
[
AIR14
, Thm. 2]
.

If
|M|=r
then
|\mathrm{B}(M)|=n-r
and
|\mathrm{C}(M)|\leq n-r
. In particular,
M\oplus\mathrm{C}(M)
need not be
\tau
-tilting over
\Lambda
but will always be a
\tau
-tilting module over
\Lambda/\Lambda e\Lambda
for some idempotent
e
. Some authors introduce non-module (shifted projective) summands to appear in the co-Bongartz complement that convey equivalent information to the idempotent
e
appearing above. We refer the reader to
[
AIR14
, Thm. 2.10 and 2.18]
,
[
Dem+23
, Sec. 4]
and
[
BKT25
, Sec. 2 and 4]
for details.

Given a
\tau
-rigid module
M
, the category
J(M)=M^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau M
is called the
\tau
-perpendicular category
of
M

[
Jas15
, Def. 3.3]
. Jasso
[
Jas15
]
showed that
J(M)\cong\mathsf{mod}\Gamma
for some algebra
\Gamma
of rank
n-|M|
and that there is an intimate relationship between the
\tau
-tilting theory of
\Lambda
and that of
\Gamma
.

Using the work of Jasso, Buan and Marsh
[
BM21
]
generalized the notion of exceptional sequences for hereditary algebras to arbitrary finite dimensional algebras.

Definition 2.4
.

[
BM21
, Def. 1.3]

A sequence of
\Lambda
-modules
(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{t})
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence
in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
if

(1)

A_{t}
is
\tau
-rigid indecomposable in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
, and

(2)

if
t>1
, then
(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{t-1})
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
J(A_{t})
.

We let
\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
denote the set of
\tau
-exceptional sequences over
\Lambda
and
\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
the set of
complete

\tau
-exceptional sequences, that is, those of length
n=|\Lambda|
.

It is important to consider that
\tau
depends on the category in which it is being computed, so when talking about
\tau
-exceptional sequences we always need to keep in mind which ambient category we are in. If, for example,
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
, then
(A_{1},A_{2})
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
J(A_{3})
. However,
(A_{1},A_{2})
need not be a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
.

We make use of subscripts (and sometimes superscripts) when we want to make explicit in which subcategory a computation is taking place, so we could write that
A_{2}
is
\tau_{J(A_{3})}
-rigid or that
A_{1}
is
\tau_{J_{A_{3}}(A_{2})}
-rigid. This last example demonstrates the practicality of recursively extending the definition of
\tau
-perpendicular categories. Following
[
BHM24
]
, we thus make the following definition.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{t})
be a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in some given ambient module category. Then we define

J(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{t})=\begin{cases}J(A_{1})&\text{if $t=1$}\\
J_{J(A_{2},A_{3},\dots,A_{t})}(A_{1})&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Note that complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences always exist: For an arbitrary module category
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
any projective module is
\tau
-rigid. In particular, we can always extend any incomplete
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{t})
to a longer
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(P,B_{1},\dots,B_{t})
by picking
P
to be an indecomposable projective in
J(B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{t})
.

Remark 2.6
.

There is a more general notion of
signed

\tau
-exceptional sequences. For brevity, we here skip this definition and refer the interested reader to
[
BM21
]
,
[
BHM24
]
and
[
BKT25
]
for details on signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences especially in the context of mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences.

The following theorem gives a useful uniqueness-property of complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences, namely that if we know

\tau
-exceptional sequence, then the remaining coordinate is uniquely determined.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
HT24
, Theorem 8]

Let
A=(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n})
and
B=(B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{n})
be full
\tau
-exceptional sequences such that
A_{i}=B_{i}
for all
1\leq i<j
and all
j<i\leq n
for some integer

. Then
A_{j}=B_{j}
.

The following corollary will be useful to us.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
(X,C)
be a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. Then
X
is uniquely determined by
C
and
J(X,C)
.

Proof.

Extend
(X,C)
to a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n-2},X,C)
using the projective modules determined by

. Then
X
must be uniquely determined by
Theorem

2.7
.
∎

There is an isomorphism between
\tau
-exceptional sequences and certain
ordered

\tau
-rigid modules.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{t}
be a module with
M_{i}
indecomposable for all
i
. Given a module
M
along with a given ordered decomposition, we call
M
an
ordered
module.

Let
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{t}
be an ordered
\tau
-rigid module. We say that
M
is
TF-ordered
if
M_{i}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(\bigoplus_{j>i}M_{j})
.

Note that a
\tau
-rigid module

may be ordered in
|M|!
distinct ways. By abuse of notation, we use the direct sum notation as usual with the caveat that we care about the order of the direct summands. We denote by
\mathsf{TF}\text{-}\Lambda
the set of TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid
\Lambda
-modules.

Example 2.10
.

Let
\Lambda
be a rank
2
algebra with indecomposable projective modules
P(1)
and
P(2)
. Then
P(1)\oplus P(2)
and
P(2)\oplus P(1)
are two distinct TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid modules.

For a
\tau
-rigid module
M
, we denote by
f_{M}(X)=X/t_{M}(X)
the co-kernel of the trace of
M
in
X
. Given a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module
M\oplus N
, we then have that
f_{N}(M)
is
\tau
-rigid in
J(N)
.

Remark 2.11
.

For a
\tau
-rigid module
M
, we have that
(\mathsf{Gen}M,M^{\perp})
is a torsion pair, inducing for any module

a short exact sequence
t_{M}(X)\xhookrightarrow{}X\twoheadrightarrow f_{M}(X)
. This illustrates one of many connections between
\tau
-tilting theory and torsion theory, and we refer the reader to
[
AIR14
]
and
[
Dem+23
]
for more information.

Proposition 2.12
.

[
BM21
, Prop. 5.6]

Let
M
be a
\tau
-rigid module. Then
f_{M}
gives a bijection between indecomposable
\tau
-rigid modules

such that
N\oplus M\in\mathsf{TF}\text{-}\Lambda
, and indecomposable
\tau_{J(M)}
-rigid modules.

It follows that for
M\oplus N\in\mathsf{TF}\text{-}\Lambda
with

indecomposable then
(f_{N}(M),N)
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. Indeed we have the following stronger result linking TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid modules and
\tau
-exceptional sequences.

Theorem 2.13
.

[
MT20
, Theorem 5.1]
[
BM21
, Thm. 5.4]

There is a bijection
\Psi\colon\mathsf{TF}\text{-}\Lambda\to\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
defined recursively by

\Psi(M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{t})=(\Psi_{J(M_{t})}(f_{M_{t}}(M_{1}),f_{M_{t}}(M_{2}),\dots,f_{M_{t}}(M_{t-1})),M_{t})

where we get rid of unnecessary parenthesis by identifying
((A),B)=(A,B)
.

Notice that the rightmost coordinate of a
\tau
-exceptional sequence is the same module as the rightmost summand of the corresponding TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module. In particular, for a
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(B,C)

\Psi^{-1}(B,C)=f^{-1}_{C}(B)\oplus C
.

2.2.
Mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences

Buan, Marsh and Hanson define in
[
BHM24
]
a
mutation
of
\tau
-exceptional sequences, generalizing the mutation of exceptional sequences as defined by Crawley-Boevey in
[
Cra93
]
. In this paper, we restrict to considering the mutation for
\tau
-tilting finite algebras
; algebras with a finite number of
\tau
-rigid modules up to isomorphism. Let now
\Lambda
be a
\tau
-tilting finite algebra and
(B,C)
a
\tau
-exceptional sequence.

Definition 2.14
.

[
BHM24
, Def. 3.11a]

We say that a
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(B,C)
is left regular if
C\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
or
C\notin\mathsf{add}\mathrm{B}(f^{-1}_{C}(B))
.

Definition 2.15
.

[
BHM24
, Def.-Prop. 4.3]

Let
(B,C)
be a left regular
\tau
-exceptional sequence. Let

B^{\prime}=\begin{cases}B&\text{if $C$ is projective}\\
f^{-1}_{C}(B)&\text{else}\end{cases}.

Then we define the left mutation of
(B,C)
as the
\tau
-exceptional sequence

\varphi(B,C)=(X,B^{\prime})

where
X
is the unique module such that
(X,B^{\prime})
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence and
J(B,C)=J(X,B^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.16
.

The module
X
in the above definition is unique by
Corollary

2.8
if it exists. In
[
BHM24
, Def.-Prop. 4.3]
, an explicit formula for
X
is given.

For
\tau
-tilting finite algebras,
\tau
-exceptional sequences
(B,C)
which are not left regular still always have a well-defined mutation
\varphi(B,C)
. We do not here give the general formula for irregular mutation, and instead refer the reader to see
[
BHM24
, Sec. 3.3]
and
[
BKT25
, Def. 3.23]
for details on mutation in the irregular case.

Mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences of length
>2
is defined in two steps. First, note that since
J(B,C)=J(\varphi(B,C))
for a
\tau
-exceptional sequence, if
(A,B,C)
is a
\tau
-exceptional sequence then so is
(A,\varphi(B,C))
. Secondly, note that for a
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(A,B,C)
, the sequence
(A,B)
is
\tau
-exceptional in
J(C)
. Let now
A=(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{t})
be an arbitrary
\tau
-exceptional sequence. Following
[
BHM24
, Def. 5.2]
, we set

\varphi_{i}(A)=(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{i-1},\varphi^{J(A_{i+2},A_{i+3},\dots,A_{t})}(A_{i},A_{i+1}),A_{i+2},\dots,A_{t})

for
1\leq i\leq t-1
. The superscript on

indicates which subcategory the computation is done in.

2.3.
Mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences over Nakayama algebras

Let
T=B\oplus C
be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module over a Nakayama algebra
\Lambda
. Then
\Psi(T)
gives a
\tau
-exceptional sequence which may be mutated, and in
[
BKT25
]
explicit formulas for the TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module
T^{\prime}
such that
\Psi(T^{\prime})=\varphi(\Psi(T))
are given. Let
\overline{\varphi}(T)=\Psi^{-1}(\varphi(\Psi(T)))
.

To compute
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)
for Nakayama algebras, we split into different cases as follows.

(TF-1)

C\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
;

(TF-1a)

and
\Hom(C,B)=0
;

(TF-1b)

and
\Hom(C,B)\neq 0
;

(TF-3)

C\not\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(B)\oplus\mathrm{C}(B))
;

(TF-2)

C\in\mathsf{add}\mathrm{C}(B)
;

(TF-2a)

and
B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
;

(TF-2b)

and
B\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
;

(TF-4)

C\in\mathsf{add}\mathrm{B}(B)\setminus\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
.

TF-4 captures the irregular case.

Theorem 2.17
.

[
BKT25
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
\Lambda
be a Nakayama algebra and let

be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module. Then the left mutation of TF-ordered modules is given by

\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=\begin{cases}C\oplus B&\text{ in Case TF-1a and Case TF-3,}\\
B\oplus f_{C}B&\text{ in Case TF-1b and Case TF-4,}\\
\rad^{k}B\oplus B&\text{ in Case TF-2a,}\\
P(\rad^{k}B)\oplus B&\text{ in Case TF-2b,}\\
\end{cases}

where
C\cong B/\rad^{k}B
for some

P(\rad^{k}B)\to\rad^{k}B
is the projective cover of
\rad^{k}B
.

3.
Mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences as group actions

Let
\Lambda
be an arbitrary
\tau
-tilting finite algebra and recall that
\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
denotes the set of complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences over
\Lambda
. Since
\Lambda
is assumed to be
\tau
-tilting finite,
\varphi_{i}(A)
(left mutation) and
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(A)
(right mutation) is well-defined for all
A\in\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
and
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
. Letting
\mathsf{F}_{n-1}
be the free group with generators
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{n-1}\}
, we have a left
\mathsf{F}_{n-1}
action on
\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
given by setting
\rho_{i}\cdot A=\varphi_{i}(A)
.

Definition 3.1
.

By the braid group relations on
n
strands we mean the relations B1 and B2 below, where we assume
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
:

B1:

\rho_{i}\rho_{j}=\rho_{j}\rho_{i}
for
|i-j|\geq 2
.

B2:

\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}=\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1}
for
i\leq n-2
.

The braid group
B_{n}
is defined as the group given by the generators
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{n-1}\}
and the braid group relations B1
\cup
B1.

Proposition 3.2
.

The relations B1 are always respected by the canonical
\mathsf{F}_{n-1}
-action on
\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
in the sense that
\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\cdot A=\rho_{j}\rho_{i}\cdot A
for any
A\in\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
and
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
where
|i-j|\geq 2
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
i+1<j
. Let
A=(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n})
be a complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence over
\Lambda
. To compute
\varphi_{k}(A)
we need two pieces of information: The
\tau
-exceptional sequence
A_{(k,k+1)}=(A_{k},A_{k+1})
and the ambient category
J(A_{k+2},\dots,A_{n})
.

We need to check that
\varphi_{i}(\varphi_{j}(A))
agrees with
\varphi_{j}(\varphi_{i}(A))
in coordinates
i,i+1
and
j,j+1
as all other coordinates will be left unchanged with respect to
A
.

We have

\varphi_{j}(\varphi_{i}(A))_{(i,i+1)}=\varphi_{i}(A)_{(i,i+1)}=\varphi_{i}(\varphi_{j}(A))_{(i,i+1)}

where the first equality follows from noting that
\varphi_{j}
leaves coordinates
i,i+1
unchanged, and the second equality comes from the fact that

J(A_{i+1},\dots,A_{j},A_{j+1},\dots,A_{n})=J(A_{i+1},\dots,\varphi_{j}(A)_{j},\varphi_{j}(A)_{j+1},\dots,A_{n}).

Further, we have

\varphi_{j}(\varphi_{i}(A))_{(j,j+1)}=\varphi_{j}(A)_{(j,j+1)}=\varphi_{i}(\varphi_{j}(A))_{(j,j+1)}

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\varphi_{i}
leaves coordinates
j,j+1
unchanged and the first equality follows from noting that
\varphi_{i}(A)_{k}=A_{k}
for all
k\geq i+2
.
∎

In the remainder of this section we will discuss a sufficient (but not necessary) criterion for the B2 relations to be respected by the mutation action on complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences.

Definition 3.3
.

We say that a class
\mathcal{A}
of algebras is closed under Jasso reductions if for any
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{A}
and
X
any
\tau
-rigid module over
\Lambda
, we have that
J(X)
is equivalent to
\mathsf{mod}\Gamma
for some
\Gamma
in
\mathcal{A}
.

The following lemmas will be useful.

Lemma 3.4
.

A class of algebras
\mathcal{A}
is closed under Jasso reductions if and only if it is closed under indecomposable Jasso reductions, that is requiring that if
X
is
\tau
-rigid indecomposable in
\Lambda\in\mathcal{A}
, then
J(X)\cong\mathsf{mod}\Gamma
for some
\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Clearly being closed under Jasso reductions imply being closed under indecomposable Jasso reductions.

Assume that

-rigid
\Lambda

\Lambda

Lemma 3.5
.

-exceptional sequence in a disconnected module category of which

Proposition 3.6
.

Consider the two criteria below for an algebra
\Lambda
.

M1:

For any
\tau
-exceptional sequence
(B,C)
we have that
\varphi(B,C)=(?,B)
.

M2:

For any indecomposable
\tau
-rigid module

over

-exceptional both in

\tau
-exceptional sequences respects the braid group relations.

Proof.

Let
(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n})
be any complete
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
\mathsf{c}\text{-}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{exc}\Lambda
for some
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{A}
. We can assume that

.

Let

and consider the sequence

in

. Since

\tau

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}(A_{i},A_{i+1},A_{i+2})

(2)

(3)

\displaystyle=(A^{\star\star}_{i+2},A^{\star}_{i+1},A_{i})

(By M1 in

(1)

\ell^{>t}(M^{t}_{l+1})=\ell^{>t}(M^{t}_{l})+1
.

If
Y\notin J(X)
and
Y
is generated by some module in the cyclic component of
J(X)
, then
Y\notin X^{\perp}
and so
\ell(X)\geq\ell^{>x}(Y)>0
. So for any module
M
in the cyclic component of
J(X)
generating
Y
, we have
\ell^{>x}(M)\geq\ell(X)+1
. Note that
M
is of minimal length among the modules in the cyclic component of
J(X)
generating
Y
exactly when
\ell^{>x}(M)=\ell(X)+1
.

We can now conclude. Since
V^{\prime}
generates
V\notin J(X)
and
V^{\prime}
lies in the cyclic component of
J(X)
, we have
\ell^{>x}(V^{\prime})>0
. We also have that
V^{\prime}
injects into
U^{\prime}
, so
\ell^{>x}(V)=\ell^{>x}(U^{\prime})=\ell(X)+1
. Thus
V^{\prime}
is the module in the cyclic component
J(X)
of minimal length generating
V
, as wanted.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
M,N
be indecomposable modules in
J(X)
for
X
an indecomposable
C_{n}
-module. Then
\Hom(M,\tau N)=0
if and only if
\Hom(M,\tau_{J(X)}(N))=0
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.8
,
\Hom(M,\tau N)=0
implies
\Hom(M,\tau_{J(X)}N)=0
. We will now prove the converse by splitting into three cases depending on which component of
J(X)\cong\mathsf{mod}(A_{i}\times C_{j})
the modules
M
and
N
lie in. We call
\mathsf{mod}A_{i}
the hereditary component of
J(X)
, and
\mathsf{mod}C_{j}
the cyclic component of
J(X)
.

Note first that if
X
is projective, then we have no non-zero cyclic component, and so
M
and
N
both lie in the hereditary component. In the cases where there is a non-zero cyclic component, we can assume
X
to be non-projective.

(1)

Assume first that
M
lies in the hereditary component of
J(X)
. Note that there are no maps from
X
to any module in the hereditary component of
J(X)
, because the top of
X
does not appear in any of the modules
\rad^{1}X,\rad^{2}X,\dots
which by
Lemma

4.6
are the relative projectives in the hereditary component of
J(X)
. Thus by
Lemma

4.7
all modules generated by
M
in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
are indeed modules in
J(X)
, so
\mathsf{Gen}M\cap J(X)=\mathsf{Gen}M
and therefore
\Ext^{1}_{C_{n}}(N,\mathsf{Gen}_{C_{n}}M\cap J(X))=\Ext^{1}_{C_{n}}(N,\mathsf{Gen}_{C_{n}}M)
which by
Lemma

4.8
gives us our wanted conclusion.

(2)

Assume now that
M
lies in the cyclic component and
N
in the hereditary component of
J(X)
. If
\tau N\in J(X)
then
\tau N
is also in the hereditary component: Observe that since
J(X)
is closed under extensions,
\Ext^{1}_{C_{n}}(N,Y)=0
for any
Y
in the cyclic component of
J(X)
. Now, as
\Ext_{C_{N}}^{1}(N,\tau N)\neq 0
we get that
\tau N
cannot lie in the cyclic component of
J(X)
.
We therefore in this case have
\Hom(M,\tau N)=0
since
M
and
\tau N
again lie in different components of
J(X)
. Assume now that
\tau N\notin J(X)
.

If
N
is a quotient of
\rad^{i}X
then
\tau N
is a quotient of
\rad^{i}\tau X
by
Proposition

4.2
(3). Note that every proper quotient of
\rad^{i}\tau X
lies in
J(X)
: If
\rad^{i}\tau X=M^{t}_{l}
then
M^{t}_{l-1}=\rad^{i+1}X
. From
\tau N\notin J(X)
, we then get that
N=\rad^{i}X
.

Thus
\tau N
has an inclusion into
\tau X
. But then any nonzero map in
\Hom(M,\tau N)
would induce a nonzero map
M\to\tau X
, which we cannot have since
M\in J(X)
. Therefore, we also have
\Hom(M,\tau N)=0
if
\tau N\notin J(X)
.

(3)

If we are not in the cases dealt with above,
M
and
N
both lie in the cyclic component of
J(X)
. Assume that
\Ext^{1}_{C_{n}}(N,\mathsf{Gen}M)\neq 0
. Let

0\to M^{\prime}\xhookrightarrow{f}E\overset{g}{\twoheadrightarrow}N\to 0

be a non-split short exact sequence with
M^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Gen}M
. We will construct a non-split short exact sequence showing that
\Ext^{1}_{C_{n}}(N,\mathsf{Gen}_{\Lambda}M\cap J(X))\neq 0
.

By
Proposition

4.2
(2), there is an indecomposable direct summand
U
of
E
such that the co-restriction of
f
to
U
is an injection and an indecomposable direct summand
V
of
E
such that the restriction of
g
to
V
is an epimorphism. As the sequence is non-split, we have
\ell(U)>\ell(M^{\prime})
and
\ell(V)>\ell(N)
. Thus

\ell(U)+\ell(V)>\ell(M^{\prime})+\ell(N)=\ell(E)

so
U
and
V
cannot be distinct indecomposable direct summands of
E
, and so we must have
U=V
.

By
Lemma

4.6
, the projectives in the cyclic component of
J(X)
are projective in
\mod C_{n}
. The projective cover of
N
lies in the cyclic component of
J(X)
and must coincide with the projective cover of
U
, so
U
is generated by some module in
J(X)
.

Let
U^{\prime}
be the module of minimum length in
J(X)
generating
U
. If
U=M^{t}_{l}
, then
U^{\prime}=M^{t}_{l+i}
for some integer
i
. Let now
M^{\prime\prime}
be the indecomposable module with the same top as
M^{\prime}
but of length
i
more, that is
M^{\prime\prime}=M^{\text{top}(M^{\prime})}_{\ell(M^{\prime})+i}
. We obtain a short exact, non-split sequence

0\to M^{\prime\prime}\xhookrightarrow{f^{\prime}}U^{\prime}\oplus E/U\overset{g^{\prime}}{\twoheadrightarrow}N\to 0

where
f^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}
are the unique maps that are non-zero on each (co)-restriction. To complete the proof, it is sufficient to show that
M^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{Gen}M\cap J(X)
. By
Lemma

4.9
(setting
V^{\prime}=M^{\prime\prime}
and
V=M^{\prime})
, we get that
M^{\prime\prime}
is the module of least length in the cyclic component of
J(X)
generating
M^{\prime}
, so
M^{\prime\prime}
is generated by
M
.

∎

From the above lemma it follows that any module in
J(X)\subseteq\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
is relatively
\tau
-rigid in
J(X)
if and only if it is
\tau
-rigid in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(B,C)
be a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
J(X)
. Then
(B,C)
is also a
\tau
-exceptional sequence in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
and
\Psi^{-1}(B,C)={\Psi^{-1}_{J(X)}}(B,C)
.

Proof.

Let
{\Psi^{-1}_{J(X)}}(B,C)=B^{\prime}\oplus C
. Since
B^{\prime}\oplus C
is
\tau_{J(X)}
-rigid, it is also
\tau
-rigid by
Lemma

4.10
. Thus we compute
\Psi(B^{\prime}\oplus C)=(f_{C}(B^{\prime}),C)={\Psi_{J(X)}}(B^{\prime}\oplus C)
, where the last equality follows from noting that
J(X)
is a full subcategory.
∎

We now prove the final technical result necessary to establish the main result of the paper.

Proposition 4.12
.

Property M2 in
Proposition

3.6
holds for any algebra on the form
C_{n}
: Given an indecomposable
C_{n}
-module
X
and a
\tau_{J(X)}
-exceptional sequence
(B^{\prime},C)=\Psi(B\oplus C)
, we have

\varphi^{J(X)}(B^{\prime},C)=\varphi(B^{\prime},C).

Proof.

We give a case-by-case proof depending on how
B
and
C
as modules in
J(X)
embed into
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
. Largely relying on the formulas in
Theorem

2.17
, we show in each situation that
\overline{\varphi}^{J(X)}(B\oplus C)=\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)
, implying that
\varphi^{J(X)}(B^{\prime},C)=\varphi(B^{\prime},C)
.

(1)

Assume first that
B
and
C
lie in different components of
J(X)
. Then
\overline{\varphi}^{J(X)}(B\oplus C)=C\oplus B
by
Lemma

3.5
. We now compute the mutation in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

If
C
is projective in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
, we are in case TF-1a and so
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=C\oplus B
as wanted. Assume that
C
is not projective in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
. We wish to show that we are in case TF-3, namely that
C
is neither in the Bongartz nor co-Bongartz complement of
B
. Since
C
lies in a different component of
J(X)
, we have
\Hom_{C_{n}}(C,B)=0=\Hom_{C_{n}}(B,C)
. Note that non-projective summands of the Bongartz complement of
B
have maps into
B
, and summands of the co-Bongartz complement of
B
have maps coming from
B
. Therefore, if
C
is not projective we are in case TF-3 and so
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=C\oplus B
as wanted.

(2)

Assume now that
B
and
C
both lie in the cyclic component of
J(X)
. Since
J(X)
is full and since taking projective cover of a module (which appears in case TF-2b) in the cyclic component of
J(X)
coincides with taking projective cover of the module in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
, it is sufficient to show that the TF-ordered module
B\oplus C
lies in the same case (TF1-4) in
J(X)
and in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

(TF-1)

Since
C
lies in the cyclic component of
J(X)
, it is
C
is projective in
J(X)
if and only if it is projective in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
, so we are in TF-1a (b) in
J(X)
if and only if we are in TF-1a (b) in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

(TF-2)

If
C
is generated by
B
then we are in case TF-2 in both
J(X)
and in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
. Since being projective in the cyclic component of
J(X)
implies being projective in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
, we are in case TF2-a (b) in
J(X)
exactly when we are in case TF-2a (b) in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

(TF-4)

Notice that the non-projective summands of the Bongartz complement of
B
in
J(X)
are the modules on the form
\rad^{i}B
who also lie in
J(X)
. Thus we are in TF-4 in
J(X)
exactly when we are in TF-4 in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

(TF-3)

If we are not in any of the above cases, we must be in case TF-3, both in
J(X)
and in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

(3)

Assume that
B
and
C
both lie in the hereditary component of
J(X)
. Note that the projectives in this subcategory are exactly the modules on the form
\rad^{i}X
for
i\geq 1
. In particular, none of these are projective in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
, which we must take into consideration in TF-2b. We must also keep in mind that taking (co-)Bongartz complements differ in
J(X)
and in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

For each case in
J(X)
, we now identify the corresponding case in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
and compare the computations of
\overline{\varphi}
.

(TF-1a)

If we are in Case TF-1a in
J(X)
, then
C
is not on the form
\rad^{i}B
and so we must be in case TF-3 in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
. Computing
\overline{\varphi}
is given by the same formula for Case TF-1a and TF-3.

(TF-1b)

If we are in Case TF-1b in
J(X)
, then by
\tau
-rigidity and TF-ordering of
B\oplus C
we must have
C\cong\rad^{i}B
and so we are in case TF-4 in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
. Computing
\overline{\varphi}
is given by the same formula for Case TF-1b and TF-4.

(TF-2a)

If we are in case TF-2a in
J(X)
, then
C\cong B/\rad^{i}B
for some
i\geq 1
and so we are also in case TF-2a in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

(TF-2b)

If we are in case TF-2b in
J(X)
then note first that
P_{J(X)}(\rad^{k}B)=\rad^{k}B
since we are in a hereditary subcategory of
J(X)
, so
\overline{\varphi}^{J(X)}(B\oplus C)=\rad^{k}B\oplus B
. In
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
, we would be in case TF-2a, since no indecomposable module in the hereditary component of
J(X)
is projective in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
. We thus get
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=\rad^{k}B\oplus B
as wanted.

(TF-3)

Observe that the modules
\rad^{i}B
(
B/\rad^{i}B
) are exactly the summands of the (co) Bongartz complement of
B
taken in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
that also lie in the hereditary component of
J(X)
, and that these summands also appear in the (co) Bongartz complement of
B
in
J(X)
. Thus, if we are in TF-3 in
J(X)
, then we are in TF-3 in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
too.

(TF-4)

If we are in case TF-4 in
J(X)
then
B
is not projective in
J(X)
and so
C=\rad^{i}B
for some
i\geq 1
and so we are in case TF-4 also in
\mathsf{mod}C_{n}
.

∎

Proposition 4.13
.

Property M2 in
Proposition

3.6
holds for any algebra in
\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be an algebra in
\mathcal{N}
and let
X
be indecomposable
\tau
-rigid over
\Lambda
.

We can only have
\varphi^{J(X)}(B,C)\neq\varphi(B,C)
if
B
,
C
and
X
all lie in the same component of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
: If
B
and
C
lie in different components, then
\varphi(B,C)=(C,B)=\varphi^{J(X)}(B,C)
. If
B
and
C
lie in the same component but
X
lies in a different component then considering the reduction
J(X)
does not influence the mutation so
\varphi(B,C)=\varphi^{J(X)}(B,C)
.

Assume therefore that
B
,
C
and
X
lie in the same component
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
. Either we have
\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{mod}C_{i}
or
\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{mod}A_{i}
for some integer
i
. In the first case,
Proposition

4.12
shows that
\varphi^{J(X)}(B,C)=\varphi(B,C)
. In the second case, we are in a hereditary algebra where the property always holds by
Proposition

3.7
.
∎

, in particular for any cyclic Nakayama algebra on the form

strands.

Since

4.5
and

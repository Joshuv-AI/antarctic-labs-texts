Enhanced polariton interaction in the presence of disorder
Matthew Prest 1,2, Cassandra Imperato 1, Oleg L. Berman 1,3, David W. Snoke 4, Klaus Ziegler 3,5

Abstract
We consider the interaction between exciton-polaritons in a semiconductor quantum well, embedded in a microcavity, in the presence of disorder. The disorder acts on the excitons in the semiconductor quantum well. We have calculated the exciton and polariton self-energies and the exciton and polariton energy dispersion relations in the presence of disorder. Our results demonstrate that disorder increases the polariton-polariton interaction.



I
Introduction

Absorption of a photon by a semiconductor leads to the creation of an electron in the conduction band and a positive charge, i.e., ”hole”, in the valence band. This electron-hole pair can form a bound state called an ”exciton”
Snoke 2020
;
Moskalenko and Snoke 2000
. The Bose-Einstein condensation and superfluidity of such excitons are expected to exist at experimentally observed exciton densities at temperatures much higher than for the BEC of alkali atoms
Moskalenko and Snoke 2000
. A direct exciton is a two-dimensional (2D) exciton, formed as a bound state by an electron and a hole in a single semiconductor quantum well, while an indirect exciton is formed by the bound state of an electron and a hole in neighboring quantum wells. Excitons can be created when the material absorbs photons and can decay by emitting photons. When a suitable material for the occurrence of excitons is put inside an optical microcavity, linear superposition between photons and excitons can be found
Snoke and Keeling 2017
. Such a quasi-particle is known as an exciton-polariton.

Many theoretical and experimental studies have identified Bose coherent effects of 2D excitonic polaritons in a quantum well embedded in a semiconductor microcavity
Littlewood 2007
;
Carusotto and Ciuti 2013
;
Snoke 2020
;
Snoke and Keeling 2017
. To obtain polaritons, two Bragg mirrors are placed opposite each other in order to form a microcavity, and a quantum well is embedded within the cavity at the antinodes of the confined optical mode. The resonant interaction between a direct exciton in a quantum well and a microcavity photon results in the Rabi splitting of the excitation spectrum. Two polariton branches appear in the spectrum due to the resonant exciton-photon coupling. The lower polariton branch of the spectrum has a minimum at zero momentum. The effective mass of the lower polariton is extremely small. These lower polaritons form a 2D weakly interacting Bose gas. The extremely light mass of these bosonic quasiparticles at experimentally achievable excitonic densities results in a relatively high critical temperature for superfluidity. The critical temperature is relatively high because the 2D thermal de Broglie wavelength is inversely proportional to the mass of the quasiparticle, and this wavelength becomes comparable to the distance between the bosons. BEC and superfluidity of exciton-polaritons have been observed in a microcavity
Carusotto and Ciuti 2013
;
Snoke 2020
;
Peng et al. 2022
. The various applications of microcavity polaritons for optoelectronics and nanophotonics have recently been developed
Snoke and Keeling 2017
.

The influence of disorder on polariton-polariton interaction was studied experimentally in Ref.
Snoke et al. 2023
. In this paper we develop the theory of polariton-polariton interaction in the presence of disorder, acting on the excitons. We have obtained the exciton and polariton self-energies and exciton and polariton dispersion relations in the presence of disorder,acting on the excitons. We have obtained the exciton self-energy in the coherent potential approximation (CPA). We have obtained the polariton-polariton interaction by employing the Lindhard screening model for a Bose gas
Snoke et al. 2023
. We have demonstrated that the polariton-polariton interaction is enhanced due to disorder.

II
Disordered Excitons

We consider a polariton gas in the presence of disorder, where the electron is interacting with a potential
U_{e}=\alpha_{e}U(\vec{r})
, and a hole is interacting with the potential
U_{h}=-\alpha_{h}U(\vec{r})
. Disorder is presented by an uncorrelated white noise potential
U(\vec{r})
with

\displaystyle\langle U(\vec{r})U(\vec{r}`)\rangle=D\delta^{d}(\vec{r}-\vec{r}`)\text{, and }\langle U(\vec{r})\rangle=0.

(1)

A derivation of the self-energy
Q(\vec{k})
of the polariton in
d=2
with disorder strength
D
, and
m_{e}\neq m_{h}
can be based on the self-consistent CPA,
given by the CPA equation
Gevorkyan and Lozovik 1985

\displaystyle Q(\vec{k})=\frac{-1}{2}\int G^{0}(\vec{q})B(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|)\frac{d^{2}q}{4\pi^{2}},

(2)

where
G^{0}
is the associated Green’s function of the exciton gas. Thus, we get from the proper choice of
G^{0}
and
B
the equation (cf. discussion in Ref.
Gevorkyan and Lozovik 1985
)

\displaystyle Q(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}q}{E-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2M}+Q(q)+i\epsilon}\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}}{m_{h}}\big(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+

\displaystyle\frac{4M^{2}}{a^{2}m_{e}^{2}}\big)^{-3/2}-\frac{\alpha_{h}}{m_{e}}\big(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+\frac{4M^{2}}{a^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\big)^{-3/2}\bigg]^{2}.

(3)

where
\epsilon>0
was introduced to avoid the poles of the
Green’s function.
First we note that this equation is rotational invariant, which reduces the CPA equation to a

1D
integral equation of the form

\displaystyle Q(k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2DM^{4}}{m_{e}m_{h}\pi a^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{qdq}{E-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2M}+Q(q)+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}}{m_{h}}\bigg(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+\frac{4M^{2}}{a^{2}m_{e}^{2}}\bigg)^{-3/2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{h}}{m_{e}}\bigg(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+\frac{4M^{2}}{a^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\bigg)^{-3/2}\bigg]^{2},

(4)

where
\alpha_{e}
,
\alpha_{h}
are inversely proportional to their respective masses

\displaystyle\alpha_{e}=\frac{m^{\prime}m_{0}}{m_{e}},\ \ \alpha_{h}=\frac{m^{\prime}m_{0}}{m_{h}}

(5)

with
m_{0}
the rest mass of the electron in vacuum and
m^{\prime}
is a tunable parameter. The type of equation (
II
) is also known as a non-linear Hammerstein Equation
Dolph 1949
with the general form

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\int_{a}^{b}K(x,s)F(s,\varphi(s))ds.

(6)

For a numerical solution of this equation we employ the Picard Iteration approach
Kelley 1995

with a damping parameter
\omega
:

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}(k)=(1-\omega)Q_{n}(k)+\omega\int K(q,k)\cdot F(q,Q_{n}(q))dq.

(7)

To examine the feasibility of solving this equation numerically, we plot the integrand weight
K(k,q)
in
Fig.
1
. As illustrated, the most significant contribution that will affect
Q(k)

occurs near the origin, and a large, finite cutoff for
q
will be sufficient.

K(k,q)
for
m^{\prime}=1/3
over a domain of
k
and
q
up to
4\times 10^{5}cm^{-1}
, illustrating convergence to
0
for large values of
q
.

III
Lower Polariton Dispersion

Polaritons emerge through the coupling of the excitons to cavity photons. This results in a linear superposition of excitons with the cavity photons. Here we assume that disorder does not affect
the photons. Therefore, we consider disordered excitons, following the results of the previous section, and couple them directly to the photons, which leads to a linear superposition of disordered excitons and photons. Then the dispersion relation for the lower polariton
Snoke et al. 2023
is given by

\displaystyle E_{LP}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(E_{ph}(\vec{k})+E_{ex}(\vec{k})-

\displaystyle\sqrt{[E_{ph}(\vec{k})-E_{ex}(\vec{k})]^{2}+4\Omega^{2}}),

(8)

where
\Omega
is the Rabi splitting and

\displaystyle E_{ex}(\vec{k})=E_{band}-E_{bind}+\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2M}-Q({\vec{k}}).

(9)

Additionally, the photon energy is determined by

\displaystyle E_{ph}=\sqrt{\bigg(\frac{\hbar ck}{n}\bigg)^{2}+k_{0}^{2}},

(10)

where
n
is the cavity refractive index and
k_{0}
is set to the exciton energy at
k=0
such that the detuning is zero.

IV
Polariton-Polariton Interaction Strength

To calculate the effective interaction strength between the polaritons at finite temperature we use the Matsubara formalism that was outlined in
Snoke 2020
. To this end, we first calculate the Hopfield coefficients
Hopfield 1958

\displaystyle X_{P}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\big(\frac{\Omega}{E_{LP}-E_{ph}}\big)^{2}}}

(11)

\displaystyle C_{P}=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{1+\big(\frac{E_{LP}-E_{ph}}{\Omega}\big)^{2}}}.

(12)

Next, the mean-field polariton-polariton interaction strength
g

without correlations can be written as
Snoke et al. 2023

\displaystyle g=g_{ex}|X_{P}|^{4},

(13)

where we use
g_{ex}=12\hskip 2.84526pt\mu\text{eV}\mu\text{m}^{2}

Snoke et al. 2023
. Finally, we calculate the polarization bubble
\Pi^{0}
, using the Matsubara finite-temperature formalism for 2D bosons
Snoke 2020
;
Snoke et al. 2023
;
Fetter and Walecka 2012

\displaystyle\Pi^{0}=-\frac{AM_{\text{pol}}}{\pi\hbar^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{-\mu/k_{B}T}-1},

(14)

where
M_{\mathrm{pol}}
is the effective polariton mass. This is
plugged into the polariton-polariton interaction strength taking
into account the correlation effects
g^{\prime}
following the
procedure in Ref.
Snoke et al. 2023

and Ref.
Fetter and Walecka 2012
to give

\displaystyle g^{\prime}\approx g\frac{1-\frac{g}{A}\Pi^{0}}{1-2\frac{g}{A}\Pi^{0}}.

(15)

This approximation holds for
\frac{g}{A}\Pi^{0}<<1/2
which is valid in the low density, non-condensate regime that we examine here.
In this case, the polariton-polariton interaction strength
with the correlation effects
g^{\prime}
given by Eq. (
15
) will be
approximately 5% larger than the mean-field polariton-polariton
interaction strength
g
given by Eq. (
13
).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M_{\text{pol}}}=\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{d^{2}Re(E_{LP})}{dk^{2}}\Bigg]_{k=0}.

After inserting (

20K

Because our lower polariton dispersion is isotropic and using the particle number

VII
Conclusion

Our calculations are the first to our knowledge to include disorder as a self-energy term in the exciton dispersion, and indicate an increasing interaction strength between polaritons in the presence of disorder. While this increase nominally contributes to a closer predicted value to experiment, it remains substantially lower due to the input value of
g_{ex}
. Future consideration possibly including multiple interacting quantum wells may be needed to better replicate experimental results.

, and

\displaystyle B(\vec{R}_{1},\vec{R_{2}})=D\int d^{d}r\bigg[\alpha_{e}\bigg(\frac{M}{m_{h}}\bigg)^{d}\bigg|\varphi_{0}\bigg((\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}})\frac{M}{m_{h}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\bigg|\varphi_{0}\bigg((\vec{r}-\vec{R}_{2})\frac{m}{m_{h}}\bigg|^{2}-\alpha_{h}\bigg(\frac{m}{m_{e}}\bigg)^{d}\bigg\rvert\,\varphi_{0}\bigg((\vec{R}_{2}-\vec{r})\frac{M}{m_{e}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\bigg].

Taking a translation such that

implies,

\displaystyle B(\vec{R_{1}})=D\int d^{2}r\bigg[\alpha_{e}\bigg(\frac{M}{m_{h}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg|\varphi_{0}\bigg(\frac{M}{m_{h}}(\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}})\bigg)\bigg|^{2}-

\displaystyle\bigg.\alpha_{h}\bigg(\frac{M}{m_{e}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg|\varphi_{0}\bigg((\vec{R_{1}}-\vec{r})\frac{M}{m_{e}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\bigg]\times\bigg[\alpha_{e}\bigg(\frac{M}{m_{h}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg|\varphi_{0}(\vec{r}\frac{M}{m_{h}})\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{h}\bigg(\frac{M}{m_{e}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg|\varphi_{0}(\vec{r}\frac{M}{m_{e}})\bigg|^{2}\bigg].

(23)

Using the
2D
ground state wave function

\displaystyle|\varphi_{0}(r)|^{2}=e^{-2r/a}\pi a^{2},

(24)

and defining the following constants,
\beta_{h}=2M/am_{h}

, we arrive at the following form,

\displaystyle B(\vec{R_{1}})=\frac{DM^{2}}{\pi a^{2}}\int d^{2}r\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}^{2}}{m_{h}^{4}}e^{-\beta_{h}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|}e^{-\beta_{h}r}-

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{m_{h}^{2}m_{e}^{2}}e^{-\beta_{h}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|}e^{-\beta_{e}r}-\frac{\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{m_{h}^{2}m_{e}^{2}}e^{-\beta_{e}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|}e^{-\beta_{h}r}+

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{h}^{2}}{m_{e}^{4}}e^{-\beta_{e}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|}e^{-\beta_{e}r}\bigg].

(25)

We then can break apart

into the following four integrals,

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{e}^{2}}{\pi a^{2}m_{h}^{4}}\int d^{2}r\hskip 2.84526pte^{-\beta_{e}r}e^{-\beta_{e}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|},

(26)

\displaystyle I_{2}=-\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{\pi a^{2}m_{e}^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\int d^{2}r\hskip 2.84526pte^{-\beta_{e}r}e^{-\beta_{h}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|},

(27)

\displaystyle I_{3}=-\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{\pi a^{2}m_{e}^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\int d^{2}r\hskip 2.84526pte^{-\beta_{h}r}e^{-\beta_{e}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|},

(28)

\displaystyle I_{4}=\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{h}^{2}}{\pi a^{2}m_{e}^{4}}\int d^{2}r\hskip 2.84526pte^{-\beta_{h}r}e^{-\beta_{h}|\vec{r}-\vec{R_{1}}|}.

We can define the following functions so that each

can be expressed as a convolution,
f_{e}(\vec{r})=e^{-\beta_{e}r}
and
f_{h}(\vec{r})=e^{-\beta_{h}r}
. Our integrals can now be expressed as follows

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{e}^{2}}{\pi a^{2}m_{h}^{4}}\big[f_{e}*f_{e}\big](\vec{R_{1}}),

(30)

\displaystyle I_{2}=-\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{\pi a^{2}m_{e}^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\big[f_{e}*f_{h}\big](\vec{R_{1}}),

(31)

\displaystyle I_{3}=-\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{\pi a^{2}m_{e}^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\big[f_{h}*f_{e}\big](\vec{R_{1}}),

(32)

\displaystyle I_{4}=\frac{DM^{2}\alpha_{h}^{2}}{\pi a^{2}m_{e}^{4}}\big[f_{h}*f_{h}\big](\vec{R_{1}}).

(33)

Now taking the Fourier transforms of
f_{e}
and
f_{h}

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{e}(k)

\displaystyle=\int e^{i\vec{k}.\vec{r}}e^{-\beta_{e}r}rdrd\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-\beta_{e}r}dr\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{ikr\cos\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-\beta_{r}}2\pi J_{0}(kr)dr,

(34)

where

is the Bessel Function of the first kind. Now note that for a Laplace transform with
s>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[J_{0}(kr)]=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sr}J_{0}(kr)dr=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+k^{2}}}.

(35)

We can introduce a derivative operator in
s
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sr}J_{0}(kr)dr\bigg]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{ds}\bigg[e^{-sr}\bigg]J_{0}(kr)dr

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-sr}J_{0}(kr)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+k^{2}}}.

Calculating the derivative and substituting back in for

\displaystyle\implies\tilde{f}_{e}(k)=\frac{2\pi\beta_{e}}{(k^{2}+\beta_{e}^{2})^{3/2}},\tilde{f}_{h}(k)=\frac{2\pi\beta_{h}}{(k^{2}+\beta_{e}^{2})^{3/2}}.

Using that the convolution is equal to the inverse Fourier transform of the product in Fourier space gives us

\displaystyle B(\vec{k})=\frac{16\pi DM^{4}}{m_{e}m_{h}a^{4}}\bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{e}}{m_{h}}(k^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{-3/2}-

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{h}}{m_{e}}(k^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{-3/2}\bigg]^{2}.

(38)

Which gives us

\displaystyle B(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{16\pi DM^{4}}{m_{e}m_{h}a^{4}}\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}}{m_{h}}(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{-3/2}

\displaystyle Q(\vec{k})=\frac{-1}{2}\int G^{0}(\vec{q})B(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|)\frac{d^{2}q}{4\pi^{2}},

Where

\displaystyle Q(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}q}{E-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2M}+Q(q)}\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}}{m_{h}}\big(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+

\displaystyle\frac{4M^{2}}{a^{2}m_{e}^{2}}\big)^{-3/2}-\frac{\alpha_{h}}{m_{e}}\big(|\vec{k}-\vec{q}|^{2}+\frac{4M^{2}}{a^{2}m_{h}^{2}}\big)^{-3/2}\bigg]^{2}.

\displaystyle Q(k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2DM^{4}}{m_{e}m_{h}\pi a^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{qdq}{E-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2M}+Q(q)}

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(k^{2}+q^{2}-2kq\cos\theta+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{3}}-

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(k^{2}+q^{2}-2kq\cos\theta+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{h}^{2}}{m_{e}^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(k^{2}+q^{2}-2kq\cos\theta+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{3}}\bigg].

(43)

We can solve two of the angular integral terms analytically, first defining

\displaystyle I_{5}=\frac{\alpha_{e}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(k^{2}+q^{2}-2kq\cos\theta+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{3}}.

(44)

Next we can consider the following similar integral

\displaystyle I_{6}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{x}{a-b\cos x}.

(45)

We can use a tangent half-angle substitution
t=\tan x/2
for the case
|a|>|b|
.

\displaystyle\implies I_{6}=\int_{\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2dt}{a+b+(a-b)t^{2}}.

(46)

Which evaluates to

\displaystyle I_{6}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}}}.

(47)

Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}\bigg[\frac{1}{a-b\cos x}\bigg]=\frac{-1}{(a+b\cos x)^{2}}.

(48)

We can use the ”Feynman Trick” to switch the order of operators,

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{dx}{(a+b\cos x)^{2}}=\frac{-d}{da}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{dx}{(a+b\cos x)},

(49)

\displaystyle=\frac{-d}{da}\bigg[\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}}}\bigg].

(50)

We can repeat this trick a second time to get

\displaystyle I_{5}=\frac{\pi[2(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{2}+4k^{2}q^{2}]}{[(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{2}-4k^{2}q^{2}]^{5/2}}.

(51)

This form can be used to solve two of the three angular integrals analytically. The remaining cross term instead must be approximated numerically, this term is defined as

\displaystyle I_{7}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(k^{2}+q^{2}-2kq\cos\theta+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(k^{2}+q^{2}-2kq\cos\theta+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{3/2}}.

(52)

Although no analytic solution for
I_{7}
can be found, it is straightforward to approximate numerically. Using this term we are left with a nested numerical integration problem,

\displaystyle Q(k)=\frac{-4DM^{4}}{m_{e}m_{h}a^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{qdq}{E-\hbar^{2}q^{2}/2M+Q(q)+i\epsilon}\times

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{e}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\frac{(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{2}+2k^{2}q^{2}}{[(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{2}-4k^{2}q^{2}]^{5/2}}+\frac{\alpha_{h}^{2}}{m_{e}^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\frac{(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{2}+2k^{2}q^{2}}{[(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{2}-4k^{2}q^{2}]^{5/2}}-\frac{\alpha_{e}\alpha_{h}}{m_{e}m_{h}\pi}I_{7}\bigg].

(53)

With
\alpha_{e}
,
\alpha_{h}
the relative disorder sensitivities, inversely proportional to their respective masses,

\displaystyle\alpha_{e}=\frac{m^{\prime}}{m_{e}},

(54)

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}=\frac{m^{\prime}}{m_{h}},

(55)

with
m^{\prime}
approximately equal to the rest mass of the electron in vacuum. Our Hammerstein equation now becomes

\displaystyle Q(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{-4DM^{4}m^{\prime 2}}{m_{e}^{3}m_{h}^{3}a^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{qdq}{E-\hbar^{2}q^{2}/2M+Q(q)+i\epsilon}

(56)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\frac{(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{2}+2k^{2}q^{2}}{[(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{e}^{2})^{2}-4k^{2}q^{2}]^{5/2}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{2}+2k^{2}q^{2}}{[(k^{2}+q^{2}+4M^{2}/a^{2}m_{h}^{2})^{2}-4k^{2}q^{2}]^{5/2}}-\frac{1}{\pi}I_{7}\bigg].

Our resulting equation is a non-linear Hammerstein Equation
Dolph 1949
, of the form

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\int_{a}^{b}K(x,s)F(s,\varphi(s))ds.

(57)

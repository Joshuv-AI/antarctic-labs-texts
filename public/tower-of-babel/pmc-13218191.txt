Efficacy and safety of Doxycycline versus Macrolides for Mycoplasma pneumoniae INfectiOn in Children (DOMINO): a protocol for a multicentre, randomised, open-label, superiority trial

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is a major cause of community-acquired pneumonia in children. In East Asia, the prevalence of macrolide-resistant MP (MRMP) has surged, leading to treatment failures and prolonged illness. While doxycycline is an effective alternative, its use in young children has historically been limited due to concerns about tooth discolouration. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of doxycycline compared with azithromycin as a first-line treatment for children with pneumonia suspected of MRMP infection.Methods and analysisThis is a multicentre, randomised, open-label, parallel-group superiority trial conducted at 14 tertiary hospitals in South Korea. A total of 208 children (aged 3–17 years) with pneumonia and confirmed or suspected MP infection will be randomised 1:1 to receive either doxycycline (4 mg/kg/day in two divided doses for 7–14 days) or azithromycin (10 mg/kg on day 1, then 5 mg/kg on days 2–5) (online supplemental tables 1 and 2). Randomisation will be stratified by age (3–7 years vs 8–17 years). A standardised ‘rescue therapy’ protocol ensures patient safety by allowing control group patients to switch to doxycycline if no clinical improvement is observed within 48–72 hours. The primary outcome is the defervescence rate within 72 hours after randomisation. Secondary outcomes include treatment failure rate, length of hospital stay, symptom duration and adverse events. Safety assessment will specifically include tooth discolouration evaluation at Day 28, focused on children aged <8 years. Efficacy will be analysed using the intention-to-treat principle.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Institutional Review Boards (IRB) of all participating centres. Written informed consent will be obtained from parents or legal guardians, and assent will be obtained from children aged 7 years and older. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations.Trial registration numberNCT07306234.Keywords: PAEDIATRICS, Pulmonary Disease, Antibiotics, Community child health

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is a leading cause of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in school-aged children.1 Although the disease is often self-limiting, appropriate antibiotic therapy is crucial to shorten the duration of symptoms and prevent progression.2 Macrolides have traditionally been the first-line treatment of choice; however, the global emergence of macrolide-resistant MP (MRMP) has become a critical public health concern, particularly with the pathogen’s resurgence following the COVID-19 pandemic.3 4

The prevalence of MRMP is exceptionally high in East Asia. Recent surveillance data indicate resistance rates exceeding 80–90% in the Republic of Korea, China and Japan.5 6 Infections caused by MRMP are significantly associated with refractory clinical courses, including prolonged fever, increased risk of treatment failure and longer hospitalisation compared with macrolide-sensitive infections.7 8

Current clinical guidelines recommend switching to second-line antibiotics, such as tetracyclines or fluoroquinolones, if there is no clinical improvement within 48–72 hours of initial macrolide therapy.9,11 However, the use of these alternative agents in young children has historically been limited due to safety concerns. Tetracyclines have historically been contraindicated in children under 8 years of age due to the risk of permanent tooth discolouration and enamel hypoplasia.12 Although recent evidence suggests that doxycycline has a lower calcium-binding capacity than older tetracyclines and may not induce staining in short courses (≤21 days), clinicians often adhere to a standard ‘watch and wait’ approach with macrolides.13 14 Consequently, young children with MRMP frequently experience delayed effective treatment.

The DOMINO (Doxycycline vs Macrolides for Mycoplasma pneumoniae INfectiOn in Children) study is a multicentre, randomised, open-label superiority trial designed to investigate whether first-line treatment with doxycycline is superior to azithromycin in achieving clinical improvement in children aged 3–17 years with pneumonia suspected of MRMP infection.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The DOMINO study is a multicentre, randomised, open-label, parallel-group, superiority clinical trial. The study will be conducted at 14 university-affiliated tertiary hospitals across the Republic of Korea. Participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive either doxycycline (Intervention group) or azithromycin (Control group). Children and adolescents aged 3–17 years presenting with CAP will be screened for eligibility (figure 1 and table 1). The planned enrolment period is from July 2026 to June 2028, with final follow-up expected by August 2028. As of the current submission, enrolment has not yet begun; the study is in the pre-enrolment preparatory phase. Recruitment and enrolment

Participants will be recruited from children presenting to the outpatient clinics, emergency departments or inpatient wards of the 14 participating hospitals. Treating physicians at each site will screen children aged 3–17 years who present with CAP for eligibility. Potential participants will be identified through routine clinical assessments during MP outbreak periods. Research coordinators at each site will assist with screening and enrolment. Parents or legal guardians will be approached by the study team, provided with information about the trial and given adequate time to consider participation before informed consent is obtained. No financial incentives will be offered; however, transportation reimbursement will be provided. All study-related medications and assessments will be provided at no cost to the participants.

Randomisation and blinding

Eligible participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to either the Doxycycline group (Intervention) or the Azithromycin group (Control). The randomisation sequence was generated by an independent biostatistician who is not involved in participant enrolment or outcome assessment. Stratified block randomisation with variable block sizes (block sizes of 4 and 6, randomly permuted) was used to reduce predictability of allocation. The Interactive Web Response System was programmed and managed by an independent contract research organisation. To ensure balanced allocation and minimise bias due to age-related safety concerns or pharmacokinetics, randomisation will be stratified by age group (3–7 years vs 8–17 years) using a stratified block randomisation method.

Due to the distinct differences in formulation (tablets/capsules vs suspension) and dosing schedules (7–14 days vs 5 days) between the two drugs, this study uses an open-label design. However, to minimise detection bias associated with unblinded assessment, the primary outcome is based on objective endpoints (body temperature <38.0°C) and standardised assessment tools.

Intervention and control groups’ condition

Intervention group (Doxycycline): For participants weighing >45 kg, oral doxycycline 100 mg will be administered two times per day for the entire duration of treatment (7–14 days). For those weighing ≤45 kg, the dose will be 4 mg/kg/day (maximum 200 mg/day) divided into two doses (two times per day). The standard treatment duration is 7 days, which may be extended up to 14 days based on clinical response.

Sample size calculation

Based on previous retrospective data, the 72-hour defervescence rate was assumed to be 80% for the Doxycycline group and conservatively set at 50% for the Azithromycin group.15 To detect this difference with 99% power and a two-sided significance level of 0.05, 88 subjects are required per group. Allowing for a 15% dropout rate, the total target sample size is determined to be 208 participants (104 per group).

The assumed defervescence rates (80% vs 50%) were based on a previous retrospective study, which showed rates of 85.7% and 41.2% for doxycycline and macrolides, respectively, among confirmed MRMP cases.15 The 99% power accounts for potential dilution from including clinically suspected cases. With 104 participants per group, the study also provides approximately 80% power to detect a 20% absolute difference at α=0.05.

Statistical analysis

The ITT set includes all randomised participants, regardless of whether they received the assigned treatment or completed the study. Participants will be analysed according to the treatment group to which they were randomised. The difference in defervescence rates between groups will be analysed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. For the primary outcome, missing data will be imputed as ‘failure’ (non-responder imputation). Non-responder imputation was selected as the primary missing data strategy because it is conservative in a superiority trial setting, consistent with ICH E9(R1) guidelines. Based on the operational infrastructure of the 14 participating centres, we expect the rate of missing primary outcome data to be below 10%. As a sensitivity analysis, multiple imputation using chained equations will also be performed under the missing-at-random assumption to assess the robustness of the primary results. Secondary analyses will include per-protocol (PP) analysis and safety set analysis. Time-to-event outcomes will be analysed using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and the log-rank test. For time-to-defervescence analysis, participants in the control group who switch to rescue doxycycline will be censored at the time of rescue therapy initiation. A secondary analysis will also be performed without censoring, treating the event time as the actual time to defervescence regardless of rescue therapy, to assess the sensitivity of results to this analytical choice.

A prespecified sensitivity analysis will be conducted in the subgroup of participants with microbiologically confirmed MRMP infection (based on retrospective 23S rRNA mutation analysis). An additional sensitivity analysis will be performed in participants with PCR-confirmed MP infection, regardless of resistance status. These analyses will help evaluate whether any dilution of the treatment effect occurred due to the inclusion of clinically suspected cases.

As a sensitivity analysis, we will evaluate an alternative definition of treatment success that excludes rescue therapy switching. In this analysis, control group participants who switch to rescue doxycycline but achieve defervescence within the 72-hour window from the original randomisation will still be classified as treatment failures. An additional PP analysis will assess defervescence among participants who completed their assigned treatment without modification.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not directly involved in the study design. However, the protocol was developed to address significant unmet medical needs—prolonged fever and risk of complications in children with MRMP—identified by paediatricians and patients’ families. Results will be shared with participants and the public through lay summaries on study completion.

Ethics and dissemination

This study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The protocol has been approved by the Central Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Korea Academy of Medicine (approval no. CIRB 2025–006). Written informed consent will be obtained from parents or legal guardians, and assent will be obtained from children aged 7 years and older after providing age-appropriate information. An example of the participant consent form is provided in online supplemental file 2. To protect participant privacy, all collected data will be pseudonymised and managed in a secure electronic database.

An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB), comprising paediatric infectious disease specialists and a biostatistician, will review cumulative safety data and interim results every 6 months to ensure participant safety and trial integrity. No formal interim efficacy or futility analysis is planned. An unplanned interim analysis may be considered only if the DSMB identifies a significant safety signal that warrants re-evaluation of the risk-benefit balance. In addition to ethical oversight, this study protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety of the Republic of Korea (Investigational New Drug Application approval no. 103079).

The results of this study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international conferences. Lay summaries of the findings will also be shared with participants and the public through the study’s website. The trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT07306234), and with the Clinical Research Information Service (identifier: PRE20260125-001).

Discussion

The DOMINO trial aims to provide high-quality evidence for using doxycycline as a primary treatment for MP pneumonia in the era of high macrolide resistance.

The main strength of our study is its multicentre, randomised design, which specifically includes younger children who have often been excluded from such trials due to historical safety concerns. By prospectively monitoring tooth discolouration, we hope to provide important safety data that can update clinical practice. The primary limitation is the open-label nature of the trial; however, we chose this to reflect real-world clinical settings and used objective temperature measurements to ensure the reliability of our results.

For patients and their families, this study could lead to faster recovery and shorter hospital stays by identifying a more effective first-line antibiotic. For healthcare professionals and researchers, our findings could inform clinical guidelines and provide a clear protocol for managing resistant infections in children. Ultimately, we expect this trial to reduce the clinical burden of MRMP in paediatric populations.

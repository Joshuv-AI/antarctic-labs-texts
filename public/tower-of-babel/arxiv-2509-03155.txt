Abstract
. A collection of miscellaneous continuous, semi-discrete, and discrete integrable systems can be associated with each integrable evolution equation of the KdV type. We give them for the Schwarz-KdV equation and generalize to the vector case. The existence of these vector generalizations is a non-trivial experimental fact, any mathematical explanation of which is not yet known.



1.
Introduction

Using the symmetry approach to classification of integrable equations
(see
[
1
,

,
3
]
),
in the papers
[
4
,

all scalar integrable evolution equations of the form

(1.1)

u_{t}=u_{xxx}+f(u,u_{x},u_{xx})

that have infinite sequence of local conservation laws, were listed.

A generalization of this approach to the case of systems of the form

(1.2)

\boldsymbol{u}_{t}=\boldsymbol{u}_{xxx}+f_{2}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{xx}+f_{1}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{x}+f_{0}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}

where the coefficients
f_{i}
depend on scalar products between the vectors
{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}}_{x},{\boldsymbol{u}}_{xx}
, was proposed in
[
9
]
.
Some lists of integrable equations (
1.2
) were obtained in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

One of the most beautiful examples of such integrable systems is the so called vector Schwarz-KdV equation
[
6
]
:

(1.3)

\boldsymbol{u}_{t}=\boldsymbol{u}_{xxx}-3\frac{(\boldsymbol{u}_{x},\boldsymbol{u}_{xx})}{(\boldsymbol{u}_{x},\boldsymbol{u}_{x})}\boldsymbol{u}_{xx}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{(\boldsymbol{u}_{xx},\boldsymbol{u}_{xx})}{(\boldsymbol{u}_{x},\boldsymbol{u}_{x})}\boldsymbol{u}_{x},

where
\boldsymbol{u}(x,t)
is a vector, which belongs to a
N
-dimensional vector space
V
equiped with a scalar product
(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}=\partial\boldsymbol{u}/\partial t,\boldsymbol{u}_{i}=\partial^{i}\boldsymbol{u}/\partial x^{i}
. In the component form it is a system of
N
evolution equations invariant with respect to the orthogonal group.

Any integrable equation (
1.1
) generates several associated differential, difference-differential and fully discrete integrabe equations
[
18
,
19
]
. In Section
2
we decribe these integrable models in the case of the scalar Schwarz-KdV equation

(1.4)

u_{t}=u_{xxx}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{u_{xx}^{2}}{u_{x}}.

The main question, which we are discussing in this paper, is whether there exists such a variety of associated integrable vector equations for each vector equation (
1.2
). The answer is not a priori obvious. We will show that this is the case for a vector equation (
1.3
). While in the scalar case the search for related integrable systems is reduced to rather simple algorithmizable calculations, in the vector case the calculations become complex and sometimes require non-trivial tricks.

2.
Scalar case

Let us demonstrate the collection of associated integrable systems for the scalar Schwarz-KdV equation (
1.4
).

2.1.
Bäklund transformation.

We assume that each function
u_{n}(x,t),\,n\in Z
satisfies equation (
1.4
). Then the formula

(2.1)

(u_{n+1})_{x}=\alpha\frac{(u_{n+1}-u_{n})^{2}}{(u_{n})_{x}},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{C}

defines an auto-Bäklund transformation
[
15
]
for (
1.4
). This semi-discrete chain is integrable model itself. In the paper
[
8
]
such chains related to equations (
1.1
) are called
dressing.

To obtain formula (
2.1
), we write

(2.2)

(u_{n+1})_{x}=Q\Big((u_{n})_{x},u_{n},u_{n+1}\Big),

differentiate it by
t
, and elliminate all
t
-derivatives using (
1.4
) and all
x
-derivatives of
u_{n+1}
in virtue of (
2.2
).
The remaining variables
u_{n+1},u_{n},(u_{n})_{x},\dots,(u_{n})_{xxxx}
are regarded as independent jet variables. Splitting with respect to
(u_{n})_{xx},(u_{n})_{xxx},(u_{n})_{xxxx}
, we obtain a overdetermined system of non-linear PDEs for
Q\Big((u_{n})_{x},u_{n},u_{n+1}\Big)
. This can be solved quite easily. The chain (
2.1
) corresponds to the "most interesting" solution of this system. The existence of other solutions can be explained by the fact that equation (
1.4
) is invariant with respect to the Möbius transformations

\tilde{u}=\frac{au+b}{cu+d},\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}.

2.2.
Volterra type chain.

The pair of equations (
1.4
) and (
2.1
) are compatible with the following chain of Volterra type

*

Such integrable chains were classified in
[
13

.
:

(2.3)

(u_{n})_{z}=\beta\frac{(u_{n+1}-u_{n})(u_{n-1}-u_{n})}{u_{n+1}-u_{n-1}},\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{C}.

Compatibility provides the existence of solutions
u(n,x,t,z)
satisfying (
1.4
).

The chain (
2.1
) can be regarded as a hyperbolic equation, where the variable
x
is continuous and
n
is discrete. Usual hyperbolic integrable equation
u_{xy}=F
has integrable evolution symmetries with respect to both variables
x
and
y
. The equation (
2.1
) has the
x
-symmetry (
1.4
) and
n
-symmetry (
2.3
).

2.3.
Negative flow.

Now, let us consider the relation (
2.1
), it’s shift

(u_{n})_{x}=\alpha\frac{(u_{n}-u_{n-1})^{2}}{(u_{n-1})_{x}},

and (
2.3
) together with its
x
-derivative. Expressing
(u_{n+1})_{x},(u_{n-1})_{x},u_{n+1},u_{n-1}
in terms of
u_{n},(u_{n})_{x},(u_{n})_{z},(u_{n})_{zx}

from these four equations and differentiating (
2.3
) in

(2.4)

u_{zxx}=\frac{u_{zx}^{2}-\beta^{2}u_{x}^{2}}{2u_{z}}+\frac{u_{xx}u_{zx}}{u_{x}}+2\alpha u_{z},

where
u=u_{n}.
This equation is a negative flow for (
1.4
). Moreover, a recursion operator for (
1.4
) can be extracted from (
2.4
), see
[
18
,
19
]
.

2.4.
System of NLS type.

Equation (
1.4
) is an
x
-symmetry for (
2.4
). But equation (
2.4
) turns out to be really integrable and therefore has
z
-symmetries (cf.
[
17
]
) of the form

u_{\tau}=F_{1}(u,u_{x},u_{z},u_{zx},u_{zz},..),\qquad(u_{x})_{\tau}=F_{2}(u,u_{x},u_{z},u_{zx},u_{zz},..).

Computation shows that the simplest
z
-symmetry has the form

F_{1}=-\frac{u_{zzx}u_{z}}{u_{x}}+\frac{u_{zz}u_{zx}}{u_{x}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{u_{z}u_{zx}^{2}}{u_{x}^{2}},\qquad F_{2}=(F_{1})_{x}.

Denoting
u_{x}
by
v
, we obtain the following second order system:

(2.5)

u_{\tau}=-\frac{v_{zz}u_{z}}{v}+\frac{v_{z}u_{zz}}{v}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{u_{z}v_{z}^{2}}{v^{2}},\qquad v_{\tau}=-\frac{v_{zz}v_{z}}{v}+\frac{u_{zz}}{vu_{z}}\left(v_{z}^{2}-\beta^{2}v^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{v_{z}^{3}}{v^{2}}+\beta^{2}v_{z}.

The differential substitution

\tau=\beta^{-1}y+\frac{1}{2}z,\qquad u_{z}=e^{\sqrt{\beta}u^{\prime}-\sqrt{\beta}v^{\prime}/2}\Big(\beta e^{\sqrt{\beta}v^{\prime}}+(e^{\sqrt{\beta}v^{\prime}})_{z}\Big),\qquad v=e^{\sqrt{\beta}v^{\prime}}

reduces (
2.5
) to the potential derivative NS-system

u_{y}=u_{zz}+v_{z}u_{z}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\beta v_{z},\qquad v_{y}=-v_{zz}+u_{z}v_{z}^{2}-\beta u_{z}.

Note that it is integrable
[
7
]
(a special case of the system w3 ) for any constant coefficients of
v_{z}
and
u_{z}
.

If desired, we can continue to create a net of integrable
systems generated by the Schwarz-KdV equation.
In particular, for the system

(2.6)

u_{y}=u_{zz}+v_{z}u_{z}^{2}+k_{1}v_{z},\qquad v_{y}=-v_{zz}+u_{z}v_{z}^{2}+k_{2}u_{z}.

one can find the following Bäklund transformation:

\displaystyle U_{z}

\displaystyle=fu_{z}-(1+f^{-1})f_{v},\qquad\quad V_{z}=f^{-1}(v_{z}-(1+f^{-1})f_{u}),

where the function
f(u,v,U,V)
is defined by the compatible system of ODEs

f_{U}=f_{u},\ f_{V}=f_{v},\ f_{vv}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{1}(f-1)f^{2}-(f+3)f_{v}^{2}}{f(f+1)},\ f_{uu}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{2}(f-1)f^{2}-(f+3)f_{u}^{2}}{f(f+1)},

which is semi-discrete system for two fields
u
and
v
. However, in this paper we do not aim to describe in some sense a complete web of integrable systems that can be obtained from the Schwarz-KdV equation.

2.5.
Superposition formula

We assume now that functions

u(x,t,z_{1},\dots,z_{k},n_{1},\dots n_{k})

satisfy equation (
1.4
), equation (
2.1
) with different parameters
\alpha_{i}
and equation (
2.3
) with different parameters
\beta_{i}
. Let us denote by
T_{i}
the shift
n_{i}\mapsto n_{i+1}
. Then

T_{i}(u_{x})=\alpha_{i}\frac{\big(T_{i}(u)-u\big)^{2}}{u_{x}}.

Consider the relations

u_{x}T_{i}(u_{x})=\alpha_{i}\big(T_{i}(u)-u\big)^{2},\qquad T_{j}(u_{x})T_{i}T_{j}(u_{x})=\alpha_{i}\big(T_{i}T_{j}(u)-T_{j}(u)\big)^{2},

u_{x}T_{j}(u_{x})=\alpha_{j}\big(T_{j}(u)-u\big)^{2},\qquad T_{i}(u_{x})T_{j}T_{i}(u_{x})=\alpha_{j}\big(T_{j}T_{i}(u)-T_{i}(u)\big)^{2}.

Eliminating
x
-derivatives from these equations and
using that
T_{i}T_{j}(u)=T_{j}T_{i}(u)
, we give rise to the relation

\alpha_{j}^{2}\Big(T_{i}T_{j}(u)-T_{i}(u)\Big)^{2}\Big(T_{j}(u)-u\Big)^{2}=\alpha_{i}^{2}\Big(T_{i}T_{j}(u)-T_{j}(u)\Big)^{2}\Big(T_{i}(u)-u\Big)^{2},

Factorizing this, we obtain two candidates for the superposition formula. It can be verified that only relation

(2.7)

\alpha_{j}\Big(T_{i}T_{j}(u)-T_{i}(u)\Big)\Big(T_{j}(u)-u\Big)=\alpha_{i}\Big(T_{i}T_{j}(u)-T_{j}(u)\Big)\Big(T_{i}(u)-u\Big),

is invariant with respect to the
x
-derivation. This superposition formula for the equation (
1.4
) can be represented by the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}T_{i}(u)&@>{\alpha_{j}}>{}>&T_{i}T_{j}(u)\\
@A{\alpha_{i}}A{}A&&@A{}A{\alpha_{i}}A\\
u&@>{}>{\alpha_{j}}>&T_{j}(u)\end{CD}\,.

It is well known
[
16
]
that this integrable discrete equation satisfies the
3d
-consistency condition.

2.6.
3-D consistent hyperbolic system
[
18
]
.

Let us extend the system

(2.8)

u_{z_{i}xx}=\frac{u_{z_{i}x}^{2}-\beta_{i}^{2}u_{x}^{2}}{2u_{z_{i}}}+\frac{u_{xx}u_{z_{i}x}}{u_{x}}+2\alpha_{i}u_{z_{i}},\qquad i=1,\dots,k

of equations (
2.4
) by

(2.9)

u_{z_{i}z_{j}}=\frac{\alpha_{i}u_{z_{i}}u_{xz_{j}}-\alpha_{j}u_{z_{j}}u_{xz_{i}}}{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})u_{x}},\qquad i\neq j,\quad i,j=1,\dots,k.

One can verify that the system (
2.8
), (
2.9
) is compatible:

\Big(u_{z_{i}xx}\Big)_{z_{j}}=\Big(u_{z_{j}xx}\Big)_{z_{i}}=\Big(u_{z_{j}z_{i}}\Big)_{xx},\qquad\Big(u_{z_{i}z_{j}}\Big)_{z_{k}}=\Big(u_{z_{j}z_{k}}\Big)_{z_{i}}=\Big(u_{z_{k}z_{i}}\Big)_{z_{j}},

where the derivatives are calculated in virtue of (
2.8
), (
2.9
). This means that there exists a common solution
u(x,z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
of this system. For given equation (
2.8
) equations (
2.9
) were found from the compatibility conditions by some non-trivial computation. It is interesting to note that (
2.9
) is compatible on its own, i.e. without using consequences of system (
2.8
).

Some of equations from Sections 2.1-2.6 are well known. The others were found by more or less simple calculations by the algorithms described in the Adler’s papers.
In contrast with this, finding similar objects for a vector Schwarz-KdV equation is not yet algorithmizable, requires various non-trivial tricks, and is sometimes extremely time and memory-consuming.

3.
Around vector Schwarz-KdV equation

In this section, for aesthetic reasons, instead of a single variable
\boldsymbol{u}
with subscripts, we use various bold characters. The transition to index notation in the vector formulas is easily performed by comparing with the corresponding formulas from Section 2, taking into account that scalar formulas should coincide with vector ones if
N=1.

3.1.
Bäklund transformation.

For vector equations (
1.2
) the Bäklund transformations we are dealing with have the form

\boldsymbol{u}_{x}=K_{1}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}+K_{2}\boldsymbol{u}+K_{3}\boldsymbol{v},

were
K_{i}
are some functions of the six scalar products of vector variables
\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}
. In the paper
[
10
]
, the following Bäklund transformation for the vector Schwarz-KdF equation (
1.3
) was found:

(3.1)

\boldsymbol{u}_{x}=\frac{\mu}{\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})-\frac{\mu}{2\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}.

It is easy to verify that (
3.1
) coinсides with (
2.1
) if
N=1
,

\boldsymbol{u}\to u_{n+1},\boldsymbol{v}\to u_{n},\mu=2\alpha
.

3.2.
Volterra type chain.

The vector Volterra type chain has the form

(3.2)

\boldsymbol{v}_{z}=Q_{1}\boldsymbol{u}+Q_{2}\boldsymbol{v}+Q_{3}\boldsymbol{w},

where the coefficients
Q_{i}
are functions of all scalar products between the vectors
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}
. This chain has to be compatible with equation (
1.3
) for
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}
, and
\boldsymbol{w}
modulo equation (
3.1
) taking together with

(3.3)

\boldsymbol{w}_{x}=\frac{\mu}{\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v})-\frac{\mu}{2\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}.

The compatibility condition

(3.4)

(Q_{1}\boldsymbol{u}+Q_{2}\boldsymbol{v}+Q_{3}\boldsymbol{w})_{t}=\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{xxx}-3\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\boldsymbol{v}_{xx})}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}\boldsymbol{v}_{xx}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{xx},\boldsymbol{v}_{xx})}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}\right)_{z}

involves vectors
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w},
whose
x
-derivatives are related by (
3.1
) and (
3.3
). It is easy to see that the vector
\boldsymbol{v}_{xxx}
in the relation (
3.4
) cancels out, and
we can take

(3.5)

\boldsymbol{u},\quad\boldsymbol{v},\quad\boldsymbol{w},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{xx}

for independent vector jet variables. Finding the scalar products of relations (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) with independent vectors, we obtain a set of algebraic relations between scalar products of vectors (
3.5
). Each of these scalar products can be expressed in terms of products between vectors (
3.5
). The latter products we regard as independent.

Splitting (
3.4
) with respect to the indpendent vectors, we obtain a system of five PDEs for functions
Q_{i}
. Each of these PDEs can be splitted with respect to the independent scalar products that depend on
x
-derivatives.
Solving the resulting system of linear PDEs for the functions
Q_{i}
, each of which depends on six variables, we have found the following vector Volterra type chain

(3.6)

\boldsymbol{v}_{z}=\frac{(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})-(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v})}{(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{w})^{2}}.

It can be verified that (
3.6
) is compatible with (
3.1
).
If
N=1
, then (
3.6
) coincides with (
2.3
) after the replacement

\{\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}\}\to\{u_{n},u_{n-1},u_{n+1}\}
.
Under additional assumption
|\boldsymbol{v}|=|\boldsymbol{u}|=|\boldsymbol{w}|=1
this integrable vector Volterra type chain was found in
[
14
]
(see formula
V_{3}
) .

3.3.
Negative flow.

Let us assume that equations (
3.6
), (
3.1
), and (
3.3

(3.7)

\boldsymbol{v}_{zxx}=a_{1}\boldsymbol{v}_{zx}+a_{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{xx}+a_{3}\boldsymbol{v}_{z}+a_{4}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}+a_{5}\boldsymbol{v},

(3.8)

\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{xx},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\quad\boldsymbol{v}.

It is clear that
\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{zxx}

\boldsymbol{u},\quad\boldsymbol{v},\quad\boldsymbol{w},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{xx}.

Using these relations, we can express

The main problem is that the coefficients in this relation depend on the scalar products
of vectors (
3.9

(3.10)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle a_{1}=\Big(\ln(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\Big)_{x},\qquad a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\ln\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}\Big)_{z},\qquad a_{5}=0,\\[17.07164pt]
\displaystyle a_{3}=\mu-\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{xx})}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}-\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})^{2}}{2(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})^{2}}-\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{z})^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}{2(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{z}^{2}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{zx}^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}{2(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})^{2}}+\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})(\boldsymbol{v}_{xx},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}{2\boldsymbol{v}_{z}^{2}},\\[17.07164pt]
\displaystyle a_{4}=\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{z})^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\boldsymbol{v}_{z}^{2}}-\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{xx},\boldsymbol{v}_{z})(\boldsymbol{v}_{zx},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\boldsymbol{v}_{x}^{2}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{zx}^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}-\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})}{\boldsymbol{v}_{z}^{2}}.\end{array}

In the scalar limit
N=1
formula (

2.4

\boldsymbol{v}\to u,\mu=2\alpha

3.4.
System of NLS type.

The simplest
z

(3.11)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{z}^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{u}_{z},\boldsymbol{u}_{x})}\boldsymbol{u}_{zzx}+\frac{(\boldsymbol{u}_{z},\boldsymbol{u}_{zx})}{(\boldsymbol{u}_{z},\boldsymbol{u}_{x})}\boldsymbol{u}_{zz}-\boldsymbol{u}_{z}^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{(\boldsymbol{u}_{z},\boldsymbol{u}_{x})}\Big)_{z}\boldsymbol{u}_{zx}+

\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}=F_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{z},\boldsymbol{u}_{zz},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{zz}),\qquad\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}=F_{2}(\boldsymbol{u}_{z},\boldsymbol{u}_{zz},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{z},\boldsymbol{v}_{zz}),

The explicit form of the system (

\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime 2}=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime 2}=1

(3.13)

3.6.
Vector superposition formula.

Assume that the following identities hold:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{x}

\displaystyle=\Phi(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\mu),\quad\boldsymbol{w}_{x}=\Phi(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\nu),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{s}_{x}

\displaystyle=\Phi(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{x},\nu),\quad\boldsymbol{s}_{x}=\Phi(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{w}_{x},\mu),

where
\Phi(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}_{x},\mu)
denotes the right hand side of the Bäklund transformation (
3.1
).

By the vector formula of superposition we mean a relation of the form

(3.15)

\boldsymbol{s}=k_{1}\boldsymbol{u}+k_{2}\boldsymbol{v}+k_{3}\boldsymbol{w},

where the coefficients
k_{i}
depend on the scalar products of the vectors
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{w}
.

Eliminating the vectors
\boldsymbol{u}_{x},\boldsymbol{w}_{x}
and
\boldsymbol{s}_{x}
from system (
3.14
), we obtain a relation of the form

(3.16)

(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\boldsymbol{A}+(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\boldsymbol{B}+(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{v}_{x})\boldsymbol{C}+a\boldsymbol{v}_{x}=0,

where the vectors
\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{C}
and the scalar
a
do not depend on

\boldsymbol{v}_{x}
.

It is natural to assume (this was suggested by V. Adler) that this relation is an identity with respect to
\boldsymbol{v}_{x}
. This means that
a=0
and that, replacing

\boldsymbol{v}_{x}
by
\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{s}
, and
\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{s},
we obtain three vector relations, which do not depend on the derivative
\boldsymbol{v}_{x}
. Their scalar products by the vector variables, together with the relation
a=0,
give us an algebraic system for the scalar products
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{s}),(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{s}),(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{s}),(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{s})
.

Non-trivial calculations show that this overdetermined system has two solutions. One of them leads to the superposition formula
[
10
]

\boldsymbol{s}=\boldsymbol{v}+(\mu-\nu)\frac{\nu(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})^{2}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})-\mu(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})^{2}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})}{\big(\mu(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})-\nu(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})\big)^{2}}.

The second solution corresponds to the replacement
\mu\mapsto-\mu.

The superposition formula can be rewritten as

\mu\frac{\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}}{(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}}-\nu\frac{\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}}{(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}}=(\mu-\nu)\frac{\boldsymbol{s}-\boldsymbol{v}}{(\boldsymbol{s}-\boldsymbol{v})^{2}}.

It this form it is a vector analog of the "cross-ratio"   model
[
16
]
. In the scalar limit the latter formula reduces to (
2.7
).

Rotational stability of magnetic field in rotating quark-gluon plasma
Aritra Das, Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
Relaxation of the magnetic field in rigidly rotating quark-gluon plasma is studied. It is shown that the infrared modes satisfying k < | m | ​ Ω k<|m|\Omega and k < | m ± 1 | ​ Ω k<|m\pm 1|\Omega , where integer m m is the projection of the orbital angular momentum along the rotating axis and Ω \Omega is the angular velocity, are unstable. The instability onset time and the magnetic field growth rate are computed for a standard initial profile of the magnetic field. Given the present phenomenological values of Ω \Omega and electrical conductivity σ \sigma the instability is not expected to be a significant factor in the field’s time evolution.

II
Evolution equation of magnetic field

We proceed to derive the equations that govern the time-evolution of magnetic field. Taking the curl of (
1
) and using (
5
) and (
4
) we derive

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\bm{B}=\sigma\bm{\nabla}\times(\bm{v}\times\bm{B})\,.

(6)

The last term on the right-hand-side of (
6
) can be transformed as

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}\times\left(\bm{v}\times\bm{B}\right)|_{i}=\left(\bm{\Omega}\times\bm{B}\right)_{i}-\bm{\Omega}\cdot(\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla})B_{i}\,,

(7)

where we used
{\bm{v}}={\bm{\Omega}}\times{\bm{r}}
with a constant angular velocity
{\bm{\Omega}}
.
Therefore, the evolution equation of magnetic field reads

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\bm{B}=\sigma\left[\left(\bm{\Omega}\times\bm{B}\right)-\bm{\Omega}\cdot(\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla})\bm{B}\right].

(8)

Assuming that
\bm{\Omega}=\Omega\bm{\hat{z}}
we obtain the following set of equations

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}\right)B_{x}=\sigma\Omega\left[B_{y}-\left(\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla}\right)_{z}B_{x}\right],

(9)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}\right)B_{y}=\sigma\Omega\left[-B_{x}-\left(\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla}\right)_{z}B_{y}\right],

(10)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}\right)B_{z}=-\sigma\Omega\left(\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla}\right)_{z}B_{z}.

(11)

Defining
\tilde{B}=B_{x}+iB_{y}
and employing Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) we derive

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}+\sigma\Omega(x\partial_{y}-y\partial_{x})+i\sigma\Omega\right]\tilde{B}=0\,.

(12)

Once
\tilde{B}
is computed, its real and imaginary parts yield the transverse components
B_{x}
and
B_{y}
. We can write (
11
) for the
z
-component in a similar form:

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}+\sigma\Omega(x\partial_{y}-y\partial_{x})\right]B_{z}=0.

(13)

The problem of solving Eqs (
12
) and (
13
) consists in computing the eigenfunctions of the differential operator

\displaystyle L=\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}+\sigma\Omega(x\partial_{y}-y\partial_{x})+\kappa i\sigma\Omega\,,

(14)

with
\kappa=0,\pm 1
.

III
The eigenmodes of magnetic field

To solve Eqs (
12
) and (
13
) it is natural to employ cylindrical coordinates
(\rho,\phi,z)
:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{\rho}^{2}-\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\phi}^{2}+\sigma\partial_{t}+\sigma\Omega\partial_{\phi}+i\kappa\sigma\Omega\right)B(t,{\bm{r}})=0\,,

(15)

where
B
stands for either
B_{z}
,
\tilde{B}
or
\tilde{B}^{*}
for
\kappa=0,1,-1
respectively. Separating variables we write

\displaystyle B(t,\bm{r})=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dk_{\perp}\ k_{\perp}e^{i(m\phi+k_{z}z)}J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})\,,

(16)

where
\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,{\bm{k}})
are amplitudes constrained by the initial conditions. Plugging (
16
) into (
15
) and employing the Bessel equation

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}-\frac{m^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)=-k_{\perp}^{2}J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\,

(17)

we obtain the equation that governs the time-evolution of the amplitudes:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})+\sigma\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})+\left[k^{2}+i\sigma(m+\kappa)\Omega\right]\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})=0,

(18)

where
|\bm{k}|^{2}=k^{2}=k_{\perp}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}
. We are seeking a solution in the form
\mathcal{B}\sim e^{-i\omega t}
which yields
the dispersion relation:

\displaystyle\omega^{2}+i\sigma\omega-k^{2}-i\sigma(m+\kappa)\Omega=0\,.

(19)

It admits two solutions

\displaystyle\omega^{\pm}_{m,\kappa}(k)=-i\frac{\sigma}{2}\pm\widetilde{\omega}_{m,\kappa}(k)=-i\frac{\sigma}{2}\pm\sqrt{k^{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}+i\sigma(m+\kappa)\Omega}\,.

(20)

The general solution to (
18
) is then

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m,\kappa}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})=a^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k_{\perp},k_{z})^{-i\omega^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k)t}+a^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k_{\perp},k_{z})e^{-i\omega^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k)t}\,.

(21)

The coefficients
a^{\pm}_{m}(k_{\perp},k_{z})
are determined by the initial conditions at
t=0
:

\displaystyle a^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k_{\perp},k_{z})=\frac{i\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{m,\kappa}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})-\omega^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k)\mathcal{B}_{m,\kappa}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})}{\omega^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k)-\omega^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k)}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle a^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k_{\perp},k_{z})=-\frac{i\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{m,\kappa}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})-\omega^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k)\mathcal{B}_{m,\kappa}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})}{\omega^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k)-\omega^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k)}\,.

(23)

The coefficients
\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})
and
\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})
are extracted from the initial magnetic field and its derivative as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\,e^{-ik_{z}z}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho\,J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-im\phi}B(0,{\bm{r}})

(24)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\,e^{-ik_{z}z}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho\,J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-im\phi}\dot{B}(0,{\bm{r}})

(25)

The expression of
\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})
can be simplified and written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}t}\left\{\left[\cos\left[\widetilde{\omega}_{m,\kappa}(k)t\right]+\frac{\sigma}{2}\frac{\sin\left[\widetilde{\omega}_{m,\kappa}(k)t\right]}{\widetilde{\omega}_{m,\kappa}(k)}\right]\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})+\frac{\sin\left[\widetilde{\omega}_{m,\kappa}(k)t\right]}{\widetilde{\omega}_{m,\kappa}(k)}\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})\right\}\,.

(26)

IV
The instability condition

Information about the stability of the magnetic field in the rigidly rotating plasma is encoded in eigen-frequencies
\omega^{\pm}_{m}(k)
. In particular, the instability is indicated by the emergence of the positive imaginary part.
The imaginary part of the modes reads:

\displaystyle\im\omega_{m,\kappa}^{\pm}(k)=-\frac{\sigma}{2}\pm\frac{\text{sgn}(m+\kappa)}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\sqrt{\left(k^{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}+(m+\kappa)^{2}\sigma^{2}\Omega^{2}}-\left(k^{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\right)\right]^{1/2},

(27)

where
sgn
stands for the sign function. The instability of the field is signified by the singularities of the causal Green’s function in the upper-half of the complex
\omega
-plane, which is indicated by the positive values of the imaginary part of the eigen-frequencies. Since the term inside the square root of the second term in (
27
) is positive we are seeking a solution to the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\im\omega^{+}_{m,\kappa}(k)>0,&m+\kappa>0,\\
\im\omega^{-}_{m,\kappa}(k)>0,&m+\kappa<0\,.\end{cases}

(28)

These inequalities are satisfied by the modes with

\displaystyle k<|m+\kappa|\,\Omega\,.

(29)

It is remarkable that the instability condition (
29
) does not depend on electrical conductivity
\sigma
. Nevertheless the logarithmic rate of the field increase
\partial_{t}\log B
, given by
\im\omega_{m,\kappa}^{\pm}
, clearly depends on it.

The eigen-frequencies
\omega^{\pm}_{m,\kappa}
depend on two parameters
k
and
m
which run over all possible values constrained only by the initial conditions. Examination of Eq. (
27
) indicates that
\im\omega^{\pm}_{m,\kappa}
is increasing function of
m
at fixed
k
.
At large
m
, it grows in proportion to
\sqrt{m}
. On the other hand, at fixed
m
and large
k
, it behaves like

\displaystyle\im\,\omega_{m,\kappa}^{\pm}(k)\approx-\frac{\sigma}{2}\left(1\mp\frac{(m+\kappa)\Omega}{k}\right)\,,\quad|m|\Omega\ll k\,.

(30)

Evidently, at large enough
k
both modes cross into the lower half-plane of complex
\omega
. Thus, the largest
\im\omega_{m,\kappa}^{\pm}
is achieved at smaller values
k
and larger values of
m
as shown in Figs.
1
and
2
.

V
Evolution of magnetic field in rotating quark-gluon plasma

In the previous section, we concluded that the magnetic field in rigidly rotating plasma is unstable. Whether this instability has a phenomenological significance hinges on the particular values of the parameters
\sigma
,
\Omega
and the initial field profile. In this section, we investigate the time-evolution of the magnetic field in a system approximating the quark-gluon plasma. The initial magnetic field is taken to be

\displaystyle\bm{B}(0,\bm{r})=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{B_{0}}{2}\left\{f(x,y-vt,z)+f(x,y+vt,z)\right\}\bm{\hat{z}}\,,

(31)

where

\displaystyle f(x,y,z)=\exp\left[-\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\Delta_{x}^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{\Delta_{y}^{2}}+\frac{z^{2}}{\Delta_{z}^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(32)

It is a superposition of two waves moving with velocity
v
along the light-cones
y-vt
and
y+vt
, where
y
is the heavy-ion collision axis. In this model
\dot{{\bm{B}}}(0,{\bm{r}})=0
. Since the average magnetic field points along the
z
-axis, we will focus only on computing the time-evolution of
B_{z}
. The instability of other components has a very similar form. Thus, throughout this section we set
\kappa=0
. The amplitudes
\mathcal{B}_{m}
are obtained by inverting (
16
) and using (
31

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\,e^{-ik_{z}z}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-im\phi}B_{0}\hat{{\bm{z}}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{\rho^{2}\cos^{2}\phi}{\Delta_{x}^{2}}+\frac{\rho^{2}\sin^{2}\phi}{\Delta_{y}^{2}}+\frac{z^{2}}{\Delta_{z}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}B_{0}\hat{{\bm{z}}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\Delta_{x}\Delta_{y}\Delta_{z}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\left[\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}}{2}(\Delta_{x}^{2}+\Delta_{y}^{2})+k_{z}^{2}\Delta_{z}^{2}\right]\right)I_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}}{8}(\Delta_{x}^{2}-\Delta_{y}^{2})\right),&\text{ even }m,\\
0,&\text{ odd }m\end{cases}

(33)

where
I
stands for the modified Bessel function. The integrals are performed in the Appendix
A

\dot{\bm{B}}(0,\bm{r})=0
and Eq. (
25

\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})=0

satisfy the equation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{*}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})=(-1)^{m}\mathcal{B}_{-m}(t,k_{\perp},-k_{z}),

(34)

which can be used to represent (

\displaystyle B(t,\bm{r})=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dk_{\perp}\ k_{\perp}J_{0}(k_{\perp}\rho)\cos(k_{z}z)\mathcal{B}_{0}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})

\displaystyle+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dk_{\perp}\ k_{\perp}J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\,\text{Re}[e^{i(m\phi+k_{z}z)}\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})]\,.

(35)

The fact that
\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,{\bm{k}})
is even in
k_{z}
for all
m

\displaystyle B(t,\bm{r})=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dk_{\perp}\ k_{\perp}J_{0}(k_{\perp}\rho)\cos(k_{z}z)\mathcal{B}_{0}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})

\displaystyle+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dk_{\perp}\ k_{\perp}J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)\cos(k_{z}z)\,\text{Re}[e^{im\phi}\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})].

VI
Discussion and Summary

We investigated the stability of magnetic field in uniformly rotating plasma and determined that soft modes with
k<|m+\kappa|\Omega
, where
\kappa=\pm 1,0
are unstable regardless of the electric conductivity
\sigma
. However, the rate of growth, given by the imaginary part of frequency, does depend on
\sigma
. It is interesting to note that the modes with
k<|m|\Omega
are superradiant
[
33
,
34
]
.

Our analysis assumed that rotation is non-relativistic, meaning that the
\Omega r\ll 1
. This allowed us to neglect the effects at the light-cylinder. This approximation holds well in practical applications.

The contribution of the valence charge
q
moving with velocity
{\bm{v}}
along the
y
-axis can be calculated by substituting
{\bm{J}}^{\prime}=q{\bm{v}}\delta(y-vt)\delta({\bm{b}})
into (
1
), where
{\bm{b}}
is the position vector in the
xz
plane. Since the velocity is ultra-relativistic,
(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2})B
can be neglected compared to
(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2})B

[
16
,
12
]
which results in the dispersion relation that has no singularities in the upper-half plane of the complex
\omega
-plane. Thus, any contribution to the instability due to the valence charges is suppressed by at least one power of
1/\gamma^{2}
.

In quark-gluon plasma produced in heavy-ion collisions, the instability develops only at unrealistically late times or high
\Omega
’s due to small electrical conductivity.

Appendix A
Calculation of
\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})

In this section, we perform the integration in Eq. (
33
), to find the amplitudes
\mathcal{B}_{m}(t,k_{\perp},k_{z})
. We first perform the
\phi
-integration followed by the integration over
\rho
. The integral over
z
can be performed separately resulting in

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\,e^{-ik_{z}z}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{\Delta_{z}^{2}}}=\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{z}\exp\left(-\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{4}\Delta_{z}^{2}\right)\,.

(39)

For the
\phi
-integral we write

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\phi}{\Delta_{x}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{\Delta_{y}^{2}}\right)=\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\Delta_{+}}+\frac{1}{\Delta_{-}}\cos(2\phi)\right]\,.

(40)

This leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}(0,k_{\perp},k_{z})

\displaystyle=B_{0}\bm{\hat{z}}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{z}e^{-\frac{1}{4}k_{z}^{2}\Delta_{z}^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho J_{m}(k_{\perp}\rho)e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\Delta_{+}}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-im\phi}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\Delta_{-}}\cos(2\phi)}

(41)

In the last line we end up with
\phi
integral of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n}(\alpha)=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-in\phi}e^{-\alpha\cos(2\phi)}\ ,

(42)

where
\alpha=\rho^{2}/(2\Delta_{-})
.
To do this integration, we make a change of variable
\phi^{\prime}=2(\phi-\pi)
. Then

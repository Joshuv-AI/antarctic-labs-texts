First-principles molecular-level study of phosphonate-induced electronic modulation and visible-light response in a tungsten–oxo cluster
Authors: Carlos N. Kabengele, Giresse N. Kasiama, Clément L. Inkoto, Damien S-T. Tshibangu, Koto-Te-Nyiwa Ngbolua, Pius T. Mpiana, Dorothée D. Tshilanda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Understanding how local coordination environments influence the electronic structure of tungsten-based hybrid systems is essential for the rational design of visible-light-responsive functional materials. In this work, we present a first-principles investigation of the electronic and optical properties of a phosphonate-coordinated tungsten-oxo model using a finite-cluster approach. A minimal W–phosphonate unit was adopted to capture the essential local features of the W–O–P motif while remaining computationally tractable for hybrid-functional and excited-state calculations. Ground-state calculations at the PBE level show that the occupied frontier states are predominantly ligand-centered, mainly derived from O 2p orbitals, whereas the low-lying unoccupied states are largely localized on W 5d orbitals, indicating a ligand-to-metal charge-transfer (LMCT)-like character. Hybrid HSE06 calculations yield a frontier energy separation of 2.44 eV and enable vacuum-referenced alignment of the frontier levels relative to the H⁺/H₂ and O₂/H₂O redox potentials. The resulting level positions provide qualitative redox-relevant molecular descriptors and suggest frontier-level arrangements potentially relevant to photoinduced charge-transfer processes. However, these descriptors should not be interpreted as direct evidence of photocatalytic HER or OER activity. Time-dependent DFT calculations using the CAM-B3LYP range-separated hybrid functional further reveal pronounced visible-light absorption, with an intense band centered at approximately 545 nm assigned to LMCT-like excitations. Overall, these results indicate that phosphonate coordination can modulate both the frontier electronic structure and the optical response of tungsten-oxo motifs, providing molecular-level insight into ligand-induced electronic modulation in phosphonate-functionalized tungsten-based materials.

Introduction

The development of photocatalytic materials capable of efficiently harvesting visible light remains a major challenge for applications such as environmental remediation and solar-to-chemical energy conversion. For a material to be relevant to photocatalytic processes under solar irradiation, it should ideally combine strong visible-light absorption, efficient separation of photogenerated charge carriers, and electronic energy levels that are thermodynamically compatible with the targeted redox half-reactions1. In practice, achieving these requirements simultaneously remains difficult, which has motivated the rational design of advanced semiconductor systems through strategies such as band-gap engineering, heterostructure construction, and controlled defect modulation2. Among the various materials explored, tungsten-based systems, particularly tungsten trioxide (WO₃), have attracted considerable attention because of their high chemical stability, semiconducting character, and strong oxidative capability under illumination. However, the photocatalytic performance of WO₃ remains intrinsically limited by several factors, including incomplete utilization of the solar spectrum, rapid recombination of photogenerated charge carriers, and energy-level positions that are not always optimally aligned with the redox potentials of water3–6. One promising strategy to address these limitations is to tailor the local coordination environment of tungsten centers through strongly coordinating ligands. In this respect, phosphonate groups are especially attractive because of their strong affinity for high-valent metal centers, their rich coordination versatility, and their ability to stabilize hybrid metal–oxygen architectures. Accordingly, phosphonate-based metal–organic frameworks and related hybrid systems have shown enhanced structural stability and tunable electronic properties, making them appealing platforms for photocatalysis. Nevertheless, despite these advances, a detailed atomic-scale understanding of how phosphonate coordination affects the local electronic structure and redox-relevant electronic descriptors of tungsten-based systems remains limited7–9. In this context, density functional theory (DFT) provides a powerful framework for establishing relationships between local coordination environments and the resulting electronic and optical properties. Semi-local generalized gradient approximation (GGA) functionals, such as Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE), are widely used to analyze orbital composition and charge redistribution in hybrid systems. However, PBE is well known to underestimate frontier energy separations, which can affect the quantitative description of optical and redox-related properties. Hybrid functionals such as HSE06 partially overcome this limitation by incorporating a fraction of exact exchange, thereby providing a more reliable description of the HOMO–LUMO gap and of vacuum-referenced frontier-level alignment10–13. In the present work, we investigate the electronic structure and optical response of a phosphonate-coordinated tungsten-oxo model using a first-principles cluster approach. A minimal WO₃–phosphonate unit was adopted in order to isolate the essential local features of the W–O–P coordination motif while keeping hybrid-DFT and excited-state calculations computationally tractable. This model is not intended to reproduce a complete periodic tungsten phosphonate framework; rather, it is designed to capture the local electronic consequences of phosphonate coordination around a tungsten-oxo center. On this basis, the ground-state electronic structure is analyzed at the PBE level through total and projected densities of states, frontier molecular orbitals, and charge distribution. HSE06 calculations are then used to refine the frontier energy separation and to align the occupied and unoccupied frontier levels with respect to the vacuum scale. Finally, time-dependent DFT calculations are employed to evaluate the optical absorption profile and to identify the dominant visible-light excitations. Overall, this study aims to provide a molecular-level understanding of how phosphonate coordination modifies the local electronic structure and visible-light response of a tungsten-oxo motif. Beyond the specific model considered here, the results provide molecular-level insight into ligand-induced electronic modulation in tungsten-based hybrid systems and may help inform future periodic and interfacial studies of visible-light-responsive phosphonate-functionalized materials.

Computational details

Structural model and geometry optimization

All calculations were performed within the framework of density functional theory (DFT). The molecular model considered in this work consists of a phosphonate-coordinated tungsten-oxo cluster designed to capture the essential local features of the W–O–P coordination environment. The optimized model contains 47 atoms and has the chemical composition C8H23O10P4W2. This finite cluster was not intended to reproduce a complete periodic tungsten phosphonate framework, but rather to isolate the local electronic effects of phosphonate coordination around a tungsten-oxo center while maintaining computational tractability for hybrid-functional and excited-state calculations. The initial geometry was built using the Avogadro molecular editor. To represent the system as an isolated cluster and minimize spurious interactions between periodic images, the structure was placed in a cubic supercell with a lattice parameter of 25 Å. Periodic boundary conditions were retained only as a computational convenience, while the large vacuum region ensured negligible image–image interactions. Full structural relaxation was carried out until the residual forces on all atoms were below 10⁻³ Ry Bohr⁻¹.

Ground-state electronic structure calculations

Ground-state DFT calculations were carried out using the Quantum ESPRESSO package (version 7.4.1). The interaction between valence electrons and ionic cores was described using norm-conserving ONCV pseudopotentials. Geometry optimization and the initial electronic-structure analysis were performed with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange–correlation functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA)10. A kinetic-energy cutoff of 120 Ry was used for the plane-wave basis set. Owing to the large supercell and the finite nature of the cluster model, Brillouin-zone sampling was restricted to the Γ point. Unless otherwise stated, all calculations were performed for a closed-shell system with total charge 0 and spin multiplicity 1. The convergence threshold for the self-consistent field cycle was set to 10⁻⁷. Additional unoccupied states were included in the non-self-consistent calculations in order to properly describe the low-lying acceptor orbitals relevant to the frontier electronic structure.

Density of states, frontier orbitals, and charge analysis

The total density of states (DOS) and projected density of states (PDOS) were obtained from non-self-consistent calculations including an increased number of empty states. Because the present system is a finite molecular cluster, the DOS should be interpreted as a broadened representation of discrete molecular orbital levels rather than as a true electronic band structure of a periodic solid. A Gaussian broadening of 0.05 eV was used to generate the DOS curves. Frontier molecular orbitals were analyzed in order to identify the spatial distribution and orbital character of the occupied and unoccupied frontier states. Orbital and charge-density isosurfaces were visualized using VESTA. Atomic charge redistribution was evaluated using Löwdin population analysis.

Hybrid-functional calculations and vacuum-level alignment

To obtain a more reliable description of the frontier energy separation, additional calculations were performed using the screened hybrid HSE06 exchange–correlation functional11,13,14. For the finite cluster considered here, the resulting occupied and unoccupied frontier energies were interpreted as molecular electronic descriptors rather than as true band edges of an extended semiconductor. To place these frontier levels on an absolute energy scale, the electrostatic potential in the supercell was analyzed and the vacuum reference was extracted from the asymptotic plateau region far from the cluster. The energies of the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied frontier levels were then aligned with respect to this vacuum reference. This vacuum-referenced frontier-level analysis was used as a qualitative descriptor for discussing redox-relevant electronic tendencies, while recognizing that it does not replace a full periodic band-edge analysis.

Excited-state and optical properties calculations

Because the W–phosphonate system is modeled here as an isolated cluster, its optical response was investigated using a molecular excited-state approach. Time-dependent DFT (TD-DFT) calculations were therefore carried out with the ORCA package (version 6.1.1) using the CAM-B3LYP range-separated hybrid functional and the def2-TZVP basis set. A total of 52 excited states were computed in order to simulate the UV–Vis absorption spectrum. The spectrum was generated using a Gaussian broadening of 0.05 eV. The dominant electronic excitations were analyzed on the basis of their orbital composition and oscillator strengths. In the notation used throughout this work, transitions such as 73a → 80a denote excitations from the 73rd occupied alpha molecular orbital to the 80th alpha molecular orbital. CAM-B3LYP was selected because its Coulomb-attenuating formulation provides a more appropriate treatment of charge-transfer excitations than conventional B3LYP, which is particularly relevant for ligand-to-metal charge-transfer transitions in the present W–phosphonate model. Previous studies have shown that range-separated and long-range-corrected functionals can substantially improve the description of charge-transfer and optoelectronic excitations in TD-DFT, especially when the correct asymptotic treatment of exchange is important15–17. Nevertheless, the absolute excitation energies should still be interpreted with caution, because charge-transfer states can remain sensitive to the amount and range separation of exact exchange used in TD-DFT. Therefore, the calculated spectrum is discussed mainly in terms of relative spectral features, oscillator strengths, and excitation character rather than as a definitive quantitative benchmark of excitation energies.

Results

Structural Model and Local Coordination Environment

Figure 1 shows the optimized geometry of the isolated W–phosphonate cluster considered in this work. The model was designed to represent the local W–O–P coordination motif rather than a fully periodic tungsten phosphonate framework. In this sense, the emphasis of the present structural analysis is placed on local coordination chemistry and metal–ligand bonding rather than on long-range crystallographic organization.

Fig. 1Optimized geometry of the W-phosphonate system.

Optimized geometry of the W-phosphonate system.

The optimized structure reveals that phosphonate oxygen atoms coordinate to the tungsten center and generate well-defined W–O–P linkages. The calculated W–O bond lengths associated with the phosphonate-coordinated oxygen atoms are 2.033 and 2.066 Å, whereas the terminal W = O bonds are shorter, consistent with their stronger multiple-bond character. The corresponding P–O distances, ranging from 1.573 to 1.590 Å, are also compatible with phosphonate oxygen participation in metal coordination. In addition, the W–O–P angles of 102.35° and 104.16° fall within a chemically reasonable range for phosphonate-bridged coordination environments18. These structural parameters indicate that the selected cluster provides a chemically reasonable local coordination motif suitable for probing the electronic consequences of phosphonate binding around a tungsten–oxo center. However, this geometry optimization does not establish the thermodynamic stability or experimental accessibility of a corresponding extended material.

Electronic structure at the PBE level

Total and projected density of states (DOS/PDOS)

The total density of states (DOS) and the projected density of states (PDOS) were calculated based on the optimized geometry at PBE level; results are presented in Fig. 2 below. The Fermi level is set to 0 eV and indicated by the dashed vertical line.

Fig. 2PBE-calculated total and projected density of states.

PBE-calculated total and projected density of states.

The ground-state electronic structure of the optimized W–phosphonate cluster was first analyzed at the PBE level using total and projected densities of states, as shown in Fig. 2. Because the system is finite, the calculated DOS represents a broadened distribution of discrete molecular orbital energies rather than true continuous energy bands. Accordingly, the electronic gap discussed here should be interpreted as a frontier molecular energy separation derived from the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied states. The occupied frontier region is dominated primarily by O 2p states, with a smaller contribution from P-derived orbitals, indicating that the highest occupied states are largely ligand-centered. In contrast, the lowest unoccupied region is mainly composed of W 5d states, showing that the low-lying acceptor orbitals are predominantly metal-centered. This orbital arrangement suggests that low-energy electronic excitation involves charge transfer from ligand-based occupied orbitals to tungsten-centered unoccupied states. At the PBE level, the frontier energy separation obtained from the occupied and unoccupied edge states is 1.54 eV. This value should not be interpreted as a bulk semiconductor frontier gap, but rather as a HOMO–LUMO-like gap for the finite cluster model. The apparent smoothness of the DOS originates from the broadening applied for visualization and does not imply metallic behavior. The underlying discrete orbital spectrum retains a finite occupied–unoccupied separation.

Frontier electronic states and charge distribution

To further clarify the nature of the frontier states, the spatial distributions of the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied frontier orbitals were examined, as shown in Fig. 3.

The highest occupied orbital is mainly localized over phosphonate oxygen atoms and neighboring ligand fragments, consistent with a ligand-centered donor character. By contrast, the lowest unoccupied orbital is largely concentrated around the tungsten center, with strong W 5d character, indicating a metal-centered acceptor state. This donor–acceptor spatial separation is consistent with a ligand-to-metal charge-transfer (LMCT)-like excitation pattern and suggests spatial electron–hole separation at the molecular orbital level19–22. While some degree of metal–ligand hybridization is naturally present, the dominant occupied-to-unoccupied redistribution supports an electron-density shift from the phosphonate/oxo ligand environment toward the tungsten center upon excitation.

HSE06 frontier gap and vacuum-referenced level alignment

As expected, the PBE functional provides a qualitative description of orbital character but underestimates the occupied–unoccupied frontier energy separation. For this reason, additional calculations were carried out using the HSE06 hybrid functional in order to obtain a more reliable frontier gap and a more robust positioning of the occupied and unoccupied frontier levels relative to vacuum. At the HSE06 level, the frontier energy separation increases from 1.54 eV to 2.44 eV. This substantial increase is consistent with the known tendency of semi-local GGA functionals to underestimate electronic gaps in oxide and hybrid metal–ligand systems24. Although the present model is a finite cluster and therefore does not possess true valence and conduction bands in the solid-state sense, the HSE06 result still provides a more realistic estimate of the energy spacing between donor- and acceptor-type frontier states.

To place these frontier levels on an absolute energy scale, the electrostatic potential in the supercell was analyzed and the vacuum level was extracted from the asymptotic plateau region, as illustrated in Fig. 4.

Previous hybrid DFT studies on pristine (WO₃) have reported band gaps typically in the range of ~ 2.5–2.8 eV, depending on the crystal phase and computational methodology. These values are consistently higher than the HOMO–LUMO gap obtained for the present W–phosphonate model, suggesting that phosphonate coordination may contribute to a reduced frontier-level separation and a visible-region optical response within the present molecular model9,25–27. In a closely related study, Peeples et al.28 demonstrated that the electronic structure of phosphonate-based MOFs can be strongly modulated by the coordination environment of the metal nodes. In the Cu-phosphonate framework TUB1, the coexistence of square-planar and trigonal-bipyramidal Cu centers was shown to induce a significant reduction of the band gap. Optical measurements revealed visible-light band gaps of 2.4 eV (indirect) and 2.7 eV (direct), while DFT calculations highlighted a pronounced spin-dependent electronic structure. In particular, the lowest unoccupied states were found to be predominantly localized on the square-planar Cu sites, which effectively narrow the overall band gap compared to the trigonal-bipyramidal Cu environments. Nevertheless, more generally, previous studies on d⁰ transition-metal-based MOFs and coordination frameworks have consistently shown that hybrid functional calculations yield band gaps in the visible range, commonly between ~ 1.8 and 2.5 eV, placing the present results well within the established literature trends. For instance, in the phosphonate MOF TUB75 studied by Siemensmeyer et al., (2020) the experimental UV-Vis gap was ~ 1.4 eV and the DFT-calculated band gap was 1.77 eV, aligning well with the band gap range observed in the present work29. Similarly, Syzgantseva et al. (2019) demonstrated through hybrid HSE06 calculations that d⁰ transition-metal–based MOFs exhibit band gaps typically ranging between ~ 1.8 and 2.4 eV depending on the metal node and linker chemistry, in good agreement with the HSE06 frontier gap reported here30. The vacuum-referenced frontier-level positions derived from HSE06 calculations provide a qualitative molecular descriptor of redox-relevant electronic tendency in the W–phosphonate cluster.

The LUMO (− 3.95 eV) lies above the H⁺/H₂ reduction potential (− 4.44 eV), suggesting a qualitative energetic tendency for electron transfer toward proton-reduction-related processes at the molecular descriptor level. Meanwhile, the HOMO (− 6.389 eV) is located below the O₂/H₂O oxidation potential (− 5.67 eV), indicating that the oxidative side is less straightforward to interpret within the present finite-cluster model. Because these levels are derived from an isolated cluster and do not include solvent, pH, surface/interface effects, or reaction intermediates, they should not be interpreted as quantitative band-edge positions or as direct evidence of photocatalytic HER/OER activity.

In this context, the HSE06 frontier-gap correction and vacuum-referenced alignment suggest that the W–phosphonate cluster possesses electronic descriptors consistent with visible-light-responsive molecular systems. However, these descriptors should not be interpreted as quantitative evidence of HER or OER activity, because reaction free energies, adsorption intermediates, solvent/interface effects, and kinetic barriers were not considered.

Optical properties and visible-light absorption

The optical response of the W–phosphonate cluster was investigated by TD-DFT, and the simulated UV–Vis absorption spectrum is shown in Fig. 5. Because the present system is a finite molecular model, the calculated spectrum is discussed in terms of discrete excited states and their oscillator strengths rather than in terms of interband optical transitions in a periodic solid.

Fig. 5Calculated UV–vis absorption spectrum of the phosphonate-functionalized WO₃ cluster obtained from TD-DFT calculations.

Calculated UV–vis absorption spectrum of the phosphonate-functionalized WO₃ cluster obtained from TD-DFT calculations.

The computed spectrum shows a clear extension of the absorption profile into the visible region, with an intense band centered at approximately 545 nm (2.27 eV). This transition is associated with a relatively high oscillator strength (f = 0.149), indicating that it is optically allowed and likely to dominate the visible absorption response of the model system. Additional intense features are also present at higher energies in the ultraviolet region. A detailed excited-state analysis indicates that the visible band arises predominantly from transitions involving several occupied ligand-centered orbitals and a small set of low-lying tungsten-centered acceptor orbitals. In particular, the most intense visible excitation (STATE 12) is mainly composed of 73a → 80a (0.396) and 71a → 80a (0.282) contributions, with smaller additional components from 75a → 81a (0.123) and 72a → 81a (0.077). Here, the notation 73a → 80a denotes an excitation from the 73rd occupied alpha molecular orbital to the 80th alpha molecular orbital. The recurrent involvement of the same low-lying acceptor orbitals indicates that photoexcited electrons are preferentially transferred toward tungsten-centered unoccupied states. These results support the assignment of the visible absorption as predominantly LMCT-like in character, in agreement with the frontier-orbital and PDOS analyses discussed above.

To go beyond the frontier-orbital-based interpretation and provide an excited-state-specific validation of the LMCT assignment, natural transition orbital analysis was carried out for the dominant visible TD-DFT excitation at approximately 545 nm, as illustrated in Fig. 6.

As shown in Fig. 6, the hole NTO is mainly distributed over the oxo/phosphonate ligand environment, whereas the corresponding electron NTO shows a stronger contribution around the tungsten–oxo region. This electron–hole redistribution provides a direct excited-state description of the visible transition and supports the assignment of the 545 nm absorption band to a predominantly ligand-to-metal charge-transfer excitation. Therefore, the NTO analysis provides additional excited-state-level support for the LMCT-like character inferred from the PDOS, frontier-orbital composition, and TD-DFT transition analysis.

Such a donor-to-metal transfer pattern is consistent with previous theoretical descriptions of low-energy charge-transfer excitations in phosphonate-containing and tungsten-based hybrid systems21,26,29,30. It is also worth noting that the most intense visible absorption band at 2.27 eV does not coincide exactly with the HSE06 frontier gap of 2.44 eV. This is not unexpected: the frontier gap reflects the energy separation between the occupied and lowest unoccupied frontier states, whereas the most intense optical absorption corresponds to an optically allowed excited-state transition with significant oscillator strength. In finite systems, the lowest-energy electronic excitation does not necessarily give rise to the most intense absorption feature. Therefore, the difference between the HSE06 frontier gap and the dominant TD-DFT absorption maximum is physically reasonable. Taken together, the TD-DFT results support the view that phosphonate coordination promotes low-energy charge-transfer excitations that extend the calculated optical response of the tungsten–oxo motif into the visible region. This finding further suggests that ligand coordination may provide a useful molecular strategy for modulating the light-absorption properties of tungsten-based hybrid systems.

Scope and limitations of the finite-cluster model

The present finite-cluster model was designed to provide molecular-level insight into how phosphonate coordination modifies the local electronic structure and optical response of a tungsten–oxo motif. This approach is appropriate for analyzing local orbital redistribution, frontier-level modulation, and the character of molecular excited states. In particular, it allows the W–O–P coordination environment to be isolated from the complexity of extended frameworks, thereby making hybrid-functional and excited-state calculations computationally tractable.

At the same time, the model is not intended to provide a complete description of photocatalytic activity in an extended material. It does not include long-range periodicity, band dispersion, dielectric screening, aqueous solvation, pH-dependent protonation, solid–liquid interfacial effects, charge-carrier dynamics, reaction intermediates, or kinetic barriers. Therefore, the vacuum-referenced frontier-level alignment and TD-DFT absorption analysis should be interpreted as qualitative electronic and optical descriptors rather than as direct proof of photocatalytic performance. A complete mechanistic assessment would require periodic slab or framework models, explicit solvent/interface effects, adsorption and reaction free energies, and kinetic-barrier calculations. Within this scope, the present study provides a local molecular basis for understanding phosphonate-induced electronic modulation in tungsten–oxo systems and can guide future periodic and interfacial studies.

Conclusion

In this work, a first-principles cluster model was employed to investigate how phosphonate coordination modifies the local electronic structure and optical response of a tungsten-oxo motif. The ground-state electronic analysis shows that the occupied frontier states are mainly ligand-centered, whereas the low-lying unoccupied states are predominantly tungsten-centered, giving rise to a donor–acceptor arrangement consistent with ligand-to-metal charge-transfer excitations. Hybrid HSE06 calculations provide a more reliable estimate of the frontier energy separation and suggest that phosphonate coordination modulates the vacuum-referenced frontier electronic levels of the system. CAM-B3LYP TD-DFT calculations further reveal a pronounced visible-light absorption band centered near 545 nm, which is assigned to an LMCT-like excitation from ligand-based occupied orbitals toward tungsten-centered acceptor orbitals. Taken together, these results indicate that phosphonate coordination can serve as a molecular strategy for modulating both the frontier electronic structure and the optical response of tungsten-oxo units. The present study should be understood as a molecular-level analysis of a local W–O–P coordination motif rather than as a full description of an extended periodic photocatalyst. Accordingly, the vacuum-referenced frontier-level alignment discussed here provides qualitative insight into redox-relevant electronic tendencies, but does not replace a complete periodic frontier-level analysis. Even with this limitation, the results offer useful molecular-level guidance for future periodic, interfacial, and mechanistic studies of phosphonate-functionalized tungsten-based hybrid materials with potentially enhanced visible-light response.

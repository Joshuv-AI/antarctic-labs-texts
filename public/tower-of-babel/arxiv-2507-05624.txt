Abstract
. Multimodal emotion and intent recognition is essential for automated human-computer interaction, It aims to analyze users’ speech, text, and visual information to predict their emotions or intent. One of the significant challenges is that missing modalities due to sensor malfunctions or incomplete data. Traditional methods that attempt to reconstruct missing information often suffer from over-coupling and imprecise generation processes, leading to suboptimal outcomes. To address these issues, we introduce an Attention-based Diffusion model for Missing Modalities feature Completion (ADMC). Our framework independently trains feature extraction networks for each modality, preserving their unique characteristics and avoiding over-coupling. The Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN) generates missing modality features that closely align with authentic multimodal distribution, enhancing performance across all missing-modality scenarios. Moreover, ADN’s cross-modal generation offers improved recognition even in full-modality contexts. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art results on the IEMOCAP and MIntRec benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in both missing and complete modality scenarios.



1.
Introduction

Multimodal emotion and intent recognition
(
EmoCaps
;
modeling
;
ref5
;
ref6
)
is pivotal for automated human-computer interaction, as it seeks to interpret and identify human emotions and intentions through various modalities such as speech content, voice tones, and facial expressions. However, real-world applications often face the challenge of missing modalities due to incomplete data collection or sensor malfunctions, including speech recognition errors, user silence, or visual occlusion, as shown in Fig.
1
. These issues significantly complicate multimodal recognition.

To tackle missing modalities, several existing studies
(
adversarial
;
contrastive
;
retrieval
;
CRA
;
MCTN
;
MMIN
;
TATE
;
EMMR
;
IMDer
)
focus on leveraging available modalities to reconstruct the missing information, thus mitigating the adverse effects on model performance caused by modality loss.
Approaches such as those in
(
MCTN
;
TransM
)
, Pham et al. and Wang et al. utilize modality translation to create a unified representation, but this requires separate reconstruction models for each missing modality scenario, leading to increased costs and inconvenience.
To address this, Zhao et al.
(
MMIN
)
proposed a unified framework capable of handling all missing modalities within a single model. This framework, known as the Missing Modality Imagination Network (MMIN), employs cascade residual auto-encoders
(
CRA
)
and cycle consistency learning to generate the missing modalities. Building upon MMIN, Zuo et al.
(
IF-MMIN
)
enhance model performance by introducing modality-invariant features to bridge modality gaps.

Figure 1
.
In real-world human-computer interaction, modality missingness frequently occurs due to factors such as speech recognition errors, user silence, or visual occlusion. These issues significantly impact the performance of multimodal recognition and pose challenges to the effective fusion of multimodal information.

Despite advancements, significant challenges persist.
The above methods often attempt to recover the multimodal joint representation by jointly training feature extraction and classification networks.
However, this approach can lead to over-coupling, causing a sharp decline in performance when modalities are missing. Even with completed modalities, the performance may fall short compared to simple zero padding, which is unsatisfactory. Moreover, these methods often lack a precise generation process, making it difficult to produce missing modality features that closely align with authentic multimodal feature distribution, leading to minimal model improvements.

In this paper, we propose an Attention-based Diffusion model for Missing Modalities feature Completion framework (ADMC). To prevent over-coupling between features and the classification network, we independently train feature extraction networks for each modality through single-modality classification, preserving their unique characteristics. Subsequently, we introduce an Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN) to generate missing modality features that closely adhere to the authentic multimodal feature distribution. ADN operates in the latent space of full-modality features, training a denoising autoencoder with a self-attention mechanism to capture inter-modal dependencies. In the presence of missing modalities, the module completes them through a sequence of denoising steps. Finally, to ensure optimal performance of available modalities even when some are missing, we train separate multimodal feature fusion networks (MF) for each missing modality scenario. MF employs a self-attention mechanism to calculate the weights for each modality and aggregates them accordingly. Interestingly, we find that even in full-modality scenarios, using ADN for cross-modal generation can provide supplemental information, enhancing the final recognition. Thus, our method excels in both missing and full-modality scenarios, achieving state-of-the-art results on the IEMOCAP and MIntRec benchmarks.

Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We introduce a novel ADMC framework for missing modality completion that integrates independently trained feature extraction networks and an attention-based diffusion model.

•

We propose an Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN) for capturing inter-modal dependencies and generating accurate missing modality features.

•

Our method excels in both missing and full-modality scenarios, showcasing strong practical applicability.

•

We evaluated our approach on the IEMOCAP and MIntRec datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results.

RELATED WORK

2.1.
Incomplete Multimodal Learning

To address the issue of missing modalities, simple strategies like filling missing modality values with zeros
(
padding
)
or averages
(
padding1
)
. However, these methods lack supervision information, leading to suboptimal performance. Low-rank imputation methods attempt to recover missing data by restoring the low-rank nature of complete multimodal data. For instance, Fan et al.
(
low-rank1
)
minimized the tensor tubal rank to handle various missing modalities. Liang et al.
(
low-rank
)
incorporated non-linear functions to capture complex correlations in tensor rank minimization. However, these methods may overlook the complementarity between heterogeneous modalities, resulting in suboptimal results.

Given the powerful feature representation capabilities of deep learning methods, they have shown remarkable performance in reconstructing missing modalities. Early approaches include Autoencoders (AE)
(
AE
)
, Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN)
(
GAN
)
, and Variational Autoencoders (VAE)
(
VAE
)
. Recently, Tran et al.
(
CRA
)
proposed a Cascade Residual Autoencoder (CRA) to reconstruct missing modality data. Zuo et al.
(
IF-MMIN
)
and Zhao et al.
(
MMIN
)
employed CRA and cycle consistency learning to learn joint multimodal representations for addressing missing modality problems, gaining significant attention due to their superior performance. Yuan et al.
(
Transformer-based
)
and zeng et al.
(
TATE
)
utilized Transformer architectures to integrate cross-modal information and capture inter-modal dependencies, achieving outstanding results in missing modality completion tasks.
Additionally, Lian et al.
(
GCNet
)
introduced Graph Neural Networks (GNN) to capture speaker and temporal dependencies, effectively enabling the reconstruction of missing modalities.
These methods have significantly advanced research in the field of incomplete multimodal learning.

2.2.
Diffusion Model

Diffusion models were first applied in the field of image processing.
Ho et al.
(
DDPM
)
significantly improved the quality of generated samples by simplifying the training objective (directly predicting noise) and optimizing model parameterization. Compared to other generative models, such as GANs and VAEs, these improvements demonstrated a more stable training process. Building on this foundation, Song et al.
(
SBGM
)
proposed a unified framework for diffusion models by integrating score matching with stochastic differential equations (SDE), addressing the inefficiency of traditional diffusion models. These advancements have established a solid theoretical foundation and practical methods for high-efficiency diffusion models.

With the further development of diffusion models, their conditional generation capabilities have been progressively explored and optimized. Dhariwal et al.
(
Classifier
)
and Ho et al.
(
Classifier-free
)
leveraged the gradients of pre-trained classifiers or directly trained generative models to achieve conditional control, laying the groundwork for conditional diffusion.
Conditional diffusion models can also guide the generation process by integrating textual descriptions or other modalities. For example, Zhong et al.
(
zhong
)
combined pre-trained language models with diffusion models to generate high-quality images that align closely with textual descriptions. Similarly, Saharia et al.
(
palette
)
applied diffusion models to conditional image generation, demonstrating remarkable performance across various image transformation tasks.
Furthermore, Liu et al.
(
audioldm
)
implemented diffusion processes in latent space for text-to-audio cross-modal generation, significantly reducing computational complexity while preserving generation quality.
These advancements have further expanded the application scenarios of diffusion models.

3.
Methodology

The overall framework of our method is shown in Fig.
2
. It analyzes a video segment, which includes acoustic
A
, visual
V
, and textual
T
data, to predict its emotion or intent category
y
.
During the training phase, the model is provided with full-modalities as input, aiming to independently trains Feature Extraction Networks (ITFN) and the Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN).
In the testing phase, the model accepts either partial or full-modalities. The pre-trained ITFN and ADN are utilized to perform missing modality completion or enhancement tasks.

The proposed method consists of three stages. First, modality-specific feature extraction networks are independently trained to obtain visual (
F_{v}
), acoustic (
F_{a}
), and textual (
F_{t}
) features (Fig.
2
(a)).
Next, the parameters of these networks (
Enc_{v}
for visual,
Enc_{a}
for acoustic, and
Enc_{t}
for textual) are fixed, and an Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN) is trained to generate missing modality features (Fig.
2
(b)).
Finally, with both the feature extraction networks and ADN frozen, a Multimodal Fusion (MF) classification network is trained on the complete set of modality features to produce the final classification results (Fig.
2
(c)).

3.1.
Modality-specific Feature Extraction

We follow the feature extraction approach described in
(
MMIN
;
IF-MMIN
;
mintrec
)
, extracting visual
F_{v}
, audio
F_{a}
, and text
F_{t}
utterance-level representations from the raw datas.

To preserve the unique characteristics of each modality, the method uses networks trained on single-modality classification to extract features, with early stopping applied to prevent overfitting, as shown in Fig.
2
(a).
The parameters of these feature extraction networks are fixed during subsequent tasks. For ease of processing, these features are stacked to form a multimodal feature representation
F^{0}_{tva}
, which serves as the input for the following modules.

3.2.
Attention-based Diffusion Networks (ADN)

To achieve a precise generation process, the method uses an Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN). The ADN consists of two processes: forward and reverse.

In the forward process, multimodal features

\epsilon

increases, the original information is progressively lost, approaching a noise distribution, as defined in Eq.

\epsilon^{\prime}
at each time step, as defined in Eq.
2
. The ADN network is trained by minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between
\epsilon
and
\epsilon^{\prime}
, as shown in Fig.
2
(b).

3.3.
Modality Completion and Enhancement

To achieve strong performance under both missing modality and full-modality scenarios, we introduce two methods: Missing Modality Completion Recognition (MMCR) and MultiModal Enhancement Recognition (MMER), as shown in Fig.

In the MMCR, we replace the missing features with Gaussian noise
\epsilon
and use the trained ADN to generate the missing modality features.
This process ensures the completeness of multimodal data, enhancing the model’s robustness across various missing modality scenarios.

In the MMER, where the input consists of full-modality features, we use the ADN for cross-modal generation (e.g., using

and

These generated features

Experimental setup

4.1.
Dataset

We evaluated our proposed method on two benchmark multimodal datasets: IEMOCAP
(
IEMOCAP
)
and MIntRec
(
mintrec
)
. IEMOCAP is an Interactive Emotional dataset with four emotional labels, while MIntRec is a multimodal intent recognition dataset collected from life TV dramas, featuring twenty intent labels. Specifically, In the IEMOCAP dataset, the frame-level features are 130-dim OpenSMILE features (IS13 ComParE)
(
OpenSMILE
)
, 342-dim Denseface features from a pretrained DenseNetet
(
DenseNet
)
, and 1024-dim BERT-large embeddings
(
Bert
)
. For the MIntRec dataset, the frame-level features are 768-dim wav2vec 2.0 features
(
wav2vec2
)
, 256-dim Faster R-CNN features from a pretrained ResNet-50
(
mintrec
)
, and 768-dim BERT embeddings
(
Bert
)
.

Similar to prior works
(
MMIN
;
IF-MMIN
)
,
we use specific encoders
Enc
for each modality.
For
Enc_{a}
, we use an LSTM
(
LSTM
)
network to capture temporal information from sequential frame-level acoustic features, followed by max-pooling to generate utterance-level acoustic embeddings.
For
Enc_{v}
, a similar approach to
Enc_{a}
is applied on frame-level visual features, extracting utterance-level visual embeddings.
For
Enc_{t}
, we employ a TextCNN
(
kim2014
)
to process sequential word-level textual features, resulting in utterance-level textual embeddings.

4.2.
Implementation details

The hidden size for encoders
Enc_{a}
and
Enc_{v}
is set to 256, resulting in a 256-dim output for each modality feature.
In the ITFN module, it consists of a uniquely designed
Enc
and a 2-layer fully connected classification network.
In the ADN module, the self-attention network has 4 encoder layers with 8 attention heads and a hidden size of 1024.
The diffusion model uses noise scheduling parameters from
\beta_{s}=0.0003
to
\beta_{e}=0.06
over
N=1000
steps.
In the MF module, the self-attention network has 2 layers with 4 attention heads, a hidden size of 1024, and a dropout rate of 0.3.
The classifier consists of 2 fully connected layers with a hidden size of 128.
For training, we use the Adam optimizer with a dynamic learning rate, starting at 0.0001, and apply the Lambda LR scheduling method for learning rate updates.
The best model is selected based on its performance on the validation set, and its results are subsequently reported on the testing set to ensure robust evaluation.
All models are implemented with Pytorch deep learning toolkit and run on an RTX 4090 GPU.

Metrics

The performance of the proposed model is evaluated using Weighted Accuracy (WA) and Unweighted Accuracy (UA), as shown in Eq.

WA accounts for class imbalances by assigning higher weights to less frequent classes, ensuring that the model’s performance on rare classes is emphasized. UA calculates the average accuracy across all classes without considering class frequencies, providing a straightforward measure of overall performance.

5.2.
Comparative Study

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of our method, we compare it with previous state-of-the-art approaches and report weighted accuracy (WA) and unweighted accuracy (UA), as shown in Tables
1
and
2
.
On the IEMOCAP dataset, our results significantly outperform previous approaches across all modality-missing scenarios, with an average performance improvement of 6.5%.
Similarly, on the MIntRec dataset, our method sustained these advantages, achieving an average performance improvement of 9.4%.
These results demonstrate the robustness and generalization of our method in missing modality completion tasks.

Specifically, compared to autoencoder-based methods (AE, CRA), translation-based methods (MCTN, MMIN) typically yield better performance. The modality-to-modality transformations in these methods help capture inter-modality dependencies and generate features for missing modalities. However, MMIN and IF-MMIN consistently underperform ADMC in single-modality input scenarios. This performance gap arises primarily from their joint training strategies, which lead to strong coupling between the feature extraction networks of different modalities. When certain modalities are missing, this coupling causes a significant drop in model performance.
In contrast, ADMC utilizes independently trained feature extraction networks (ITFN), which effectively reduce inter-modality coupling. This approach allows each available modality to fully express its representational power, even when some modalities are missing. Additionally, ADMC employs Attention-based Diffusion Networks (ADN) to capture inter-modality dependencies and generate missing modality features that closely align with the authentic multimodal feature distribution, thereby significantly enhancing the overall performance of the model.

5.3.
Comparison of Experimental Results under Different Missing Rate

In previous methods (such as TransM and TATE), researchers conducted binary classification tasks under various missing rates (ranging from 0 to 0.5), using a probabilistic approach to randomly drop one or two modalities.
We conducted similar experiments on the IEMOCAP dataset. Our approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baseline methods across all missing rates, with a notable performance improvement of approximately 12.45% at a missing rate of 0.5.
As shown in Table
3
, as the missing rate increases, the F1 score and accuracy (ACC) of all methods decrease. However, our method demonstrates superior stability, with the smallest performance drop and minimal fluctuation even at higher missing rates.
This stability is attributed to the decoupling of the feature extraction network in our model, which effectively mitigates the impact of missing modalities on the overall model’s performance.

Ablation Study

To validate the contributions of the ITFN and ADN modules, we conducted ablation experiments, as shown in Table
1
and Table
2
.
The experimental results demonstrate that the ITFN module and the ADN can significantly enhance model performance, and removing either one would result in a performance decline.

Specifically,
1) w/o ITFN
: This setting utilizes jointly trained feature extraction networks while employing ADN to generate missing modality features.
Experimental results indicate that this method generally outperforms existing approaches, demonstrating that ADN effectively capture inter-modal dependencies and reconstructs missing modality features from available ones.
However, due to the instability of jointly trained networks, performance fluctuations may occur in certain cases, potentially leading to degraded model performance.

2) w/o ADN
: This setting employs ITFN for independent feature extraction and fills missing modality features with zeros, without performing modality completion.
Experimental results show that this method outperforms existing approaches across all missing modality scenarios, highlighting the importance of preventing excessive coupling between feature extraction networks in modality completion tasks.
Compared to joint training, ITFN preserves the discriminative power of available modalities, ensuring strong classification performance.
However, due to the absence of the ADN, the model struggles to effectively exploit cross-modal information, resulting in a decline in classification accuracy under high missing-rate scenarios.
In summary, ITFN and ADN are complementary, and their combination achieves the best overall performance.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose an Attention-based Diffusion Model for Missing modalities feature Completion (ADMC), which integrates independently trained feature extraction networks and an Attention-based Diffusion Network (ADN).
Our approach preserves the distinct characteristics of each modality while generating missing modality features that closely adhere to the authentic multimodal feature distribution.
Extensive experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance.

Nevertheless, our method has certain limitations, such as the lack of loss propagation between the diffusion model and the classification network, which may restrict overall model performance.
In future work, we will address the issues and explore broader multimodal applications, including autonomous driving, user profiling, and other scenarios involving missing modalities.

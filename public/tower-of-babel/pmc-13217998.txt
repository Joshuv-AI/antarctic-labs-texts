Defining psychological giftedness: a reflexive thematic analysis of experts’ and counsellors’ perspectives
Authors: Nurul Suzaina Joli, Fonny Dameaty Hutagalung, Hutkemri Zulnaidi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTraditional definitions of giftedness have primarily focused on cognitive ability and academic performance, often overlooking the internal psychological dimensions of gifted individuals. This study addresses this gap by exploring the concept of psychological giftedness as an integrative framework that brings together cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural capacities, particularly in relation to developmental challenges such as asynchronous development.MethodsUsing a qualitative design, semi-structured interviews were conducted with four experts in gifted education and five counsellors working with gifted individuals. The data were analysed using reflexive thematic analysis (RTA).ResultsThree interconnected themes were identified: (1) psychological giftedness as holistic human development beyond academic achievement; (2) the synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains; and (3) psychological giftedness as a dynamic capacity for contextual adaptability and internalized resilience. Based on these findings, psychological giftedness is defined as a dynamic and synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains. It is a continuous developmental trajectory that enables gifted individuals to achieve contextual adaptability and internalized resilience, allowing them to navigate their unique developmental phases (such as asynchronous development) and flourish across diverse life contexts.ConclusionBeyond mere academic aptitude, this construct encompasses deep self-awareness, interpersonal competence, and adaptive self-regulation, which collectively manifest as internalized resilience. Ultimately, psychological giftedness fosters the development of well-rounded, resilient, and highly socioemotionally competent individuals who can navigate challenges and flourish across diverse and transitioning life contexts. Introduction

Gifted and talented (G&T) individuals are often identified through their advanced cognitive abilities, typically measured using intelligence quotient (IQ) assessments [1]. However, this traditional approach has been increasingly critiqued for its narrow focus on intellectual functioning, often overlooking the broader psychological and emotional dimensions of giftedness. Contemporary scholarship has recently emphasized that the experiences of gifted individuals involve complex psychological and socioemotional dimensions that extend beyond mere cognitive ability [2]. One of the main psychological challenges faced by gifted individuals is asynchronous development, where their intellectual growth far exceeds their emotional and social maturity [3]. While these individuals may perform exceptionally well in academic settings, many struggles in regulating their emotions, managing behaviours, and interacting socially [3]. This developmental imbalance increases their vulnerability to emotional distress, anxiety, and other psychological challenges, including a tendency toward social withdrawal [4].

Existing identification tools such as IQ tests and achievement scores do not adequately capture these psychological complexities. Scholars argue that important traits such as emotional maturity, adaptability, and metacognitive ability are often overlooked [5, 6]. This gap reveals the urgent need to adopt a more holistic model of assessment that integrates both cognitive and non-cognitive domains [7, 8]. Recent meta-analytical evidence suggests that psychological characteristics, such as overexcitabilities and emotional intensity, are closely associated with giftedness and must be understood within broader developmental frameworks [9]. Furthermore, recent systematic reviews emphasize that the internal well-being of gifted individuals is often more fragile than their academic performance suggests, necessitating a more integrated psychological approach to identification [10]. While theoretical contributions such as Renzulli’s Three-Ring Conception [11], Gagné’s Differentiated Model [12], and Gardner’s Theory of Multiple Intelligences [13] acknowledge the role of non-cognitive traits, contemporary models continue to call for a more explicit articulation of the integration of cognitive, emotional, and behavioural capacities [14].

Research shows that gifted individuals are often more emotionally sensitive, socially aware, and behaviourally complex than their peers [15, 16]. Such findings highlight the need for a holistic construct that defines the psychological capacities of gifted individuals. This study introduces psychological giftedness as a novel construct reflecting the dynamic and synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains. Unlike traditional models that treat these domains as separate entities, this construct emphasizes how gifted individuals leverage their advanced cognitive architecture including metacognition and critical thinking to manage their unique developmental phases and foster resilience. By drawing on insights from both experts in gifted education and counsellors, this study aims to define psychological giftedness as a dynamic framework encompassing contextual adaptability and internalized resilience, providing a foundation for future self-assessment tools. Reflexive thematic analysis was used to systematically analyse and interpret participants’ perspectives, ensuring a deep exploration of the subjective and developmental nature of this construct [17, 18].

Methodology

Design

This study adopted a qualitative design to capture in-depth perspectives from experts and counsellors on the psychological dimensions of giftedness. Reflexive thematic analysis (RTA) was selected, following Braun and Clarke’s six-phase framework [17, 18]. RTA was chosen because it allows for flexibility in capturing participants’ nuanced perspectives while recognizing the active role of the researcher in knowledge construction.

Researcher positionality

In accordance with the principles of RTA, the researchers acknowledge their positionality and its potential influence on the research process. The lead researcher is an academic and practitioner with extensive experience in gifted education within the Malaysian national context. This ‘insider’ status (emic perspective) facilitated a deeper rapport with the participants, as the researcher shared a common professional language and a nuanced understanding of the local gifted education landscape. To ensure trustworthiness and mitigate personal bias, findings were subjected to critical peer debriefing among the research team to ensure interpretations remained grounded in the data rather than prior institutional expectations.

Participants

Nine participants were purposively selected for this study, comprising four experts in gifted education and five practicing counsellors from a specialized institution for gifted individuals. The selection process followed a non-probability purposive sampling technique to ensure that participants possessed the specific professional insights required to define the psychological dimensions of giftedness. Recruitment was conducted through professional networks and institutional referrals within the Malaysian gifted education community. The selection of experts was predicated on their foundational expertise in Educational Psychology and Child Development, which provided the necessary theoretical and clinical basis for their subsequent specialization in Gifted and Talented Psychology (as identified in Table 1). This dual expertise is critical; their 18 to 31 years of cumulative experience reflects a career trajectory that began with broader psychological and developmental research and evolved into a specialized focus on giftedness as the field formalized in Malaysia. While formal national gifted programs were institutionalized after 2006, these experts had already established extensive careers in psychological services and teacher education, allowing them to provide a vital longitudinal perspective on the psychological maturation of high-potential learners across different eras of the Malaysian education system. Furthermore, the high level of academic qualification among the participants with three experts holding Doctoral degrees (PhDs) and the majority of counsellors holding Master’s degrees ensures that the insights gathered were grounded in both advanced theoretical knowledge and extensive clinical experience. The participating counsellors possessed between 5 and 17 years of professional experience in educational and counselling services specifically tailored for gifted populations. Data collection

Individual semi-structured interviews were conducted between December 2024 and March 2025, with each session lasting between 45 and 90 min. All interviews were audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim; subsequently, pseudonyms were assigned to participants to ensure strict confidentiality. To complement the interview data, field notes were maintained to capture contextual nuances and document the researcher’s reflexive observations throughout the process.

Instruments

The primary instrument in this study was the researcher, serving as the qualitative data collector through a semi-structured interview protocol. This interview guide was specifically developed for the study, informed by a comprehensive literature review on giftedness and consultations with domain experts. To ensure content validity, the protocol underwent expert validation by five specialists prior to field implementation. The English version of the interview guide is available as Supplementary File 1.

Language and translation

Interviews were conducted and transcribed in the participants’ native language (Bahasa Melayu) to preserve the cultural and semantic richness of the qualitative data. Primary data analysis was performed on the original transcripts to maintain nuanced meanings. For international publication, illustrative quotations and the final thematic structure were translated into English. To ensure conceptual equivalence, a collaborative translation approach was adopted: the lead researcher performed the initial translation, which was then cross-verified by a bilingual co-researcher to preserve the original psychological intent and contextual essence of the participants’ narratives.

Procedures

All procedures in this study were conducted in accordance with established qualitative research standards. Prior to data collection, formal ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Research Ethics Committee. Informed consent was secured from all participants, with rigorous measures implemented to ensure confidentiality, anonymity, and voluntary participation. The study was executed in two distinct phases:

Phase 1: preparation

A semi-structured interview protocol was developed, grounded in a review of literature within gifted education and psychology. To bridge theoretical and practical dimensions of psychological giftedness, the protocol incorporated insights from experts with extensive experience in the field. For content validity, the instrument underwent a formal review by five specialists prior to field implementation.

Phase 2: data collection

Individual semi-structured interviews were conducted between December 2024 and March 2025. Each session, lasting between 45 and 90 min, was audio-recorded and subsequently transcribed verbatim. To safeguard participant identity, pseudonyms were assigned, while field notes were maintained to document contextual nuances and researcher reflexivity. All procedures involving human participants strictly adhered to the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments.

Data analysis and rigor

Data analysis followed the six-phase framework of Reflexive Thematic Analysis (RTA) as outlined by Braun and Clarke [17, 18]. An inductive approach was employed, allowing codes and themes to emerge organically from the data rather than being retrofitted into pre-existing theoretical frameworks. To ensure the integrity of the findings, several measures were implemented. First, manual coding was prioritized to facilitate deep interpretive engagement and immersion, avoiding the distancing effects of software automation. Second, a systematic audit trail and analytic memos were integrated into the coding matrices to document the evolution of codes and decision-making processes. Third, peer debriefing was conducted through iterative discussions with the research team and a qualitative methodology expert to refine thematic boundaries. Finally, researcher reflexivity remained central; the researcher’s positionality as both an academic and practitioner was explicitly acknowledged, using reflexive insights to “bracket” prior assumptions and ensure themes remained deeply grounded in the participants’ lived experiences.

Result

The reflexive thematic analysis (RTA) of interviews with four experts and five counsellors revealed psychological giftedness as a dynamic, “whole-person” trait rather than just a high IQ score or a straight-A report card. It’s a shift from seeing giftedness as something a student does (performance) to something a student is (being) (Table 2).

Narrative findings

Theme 1: psychological giftedness as holistic human development beyond academic achievement

Participants consistently emphasized that psychological giftedness must be understood as a humanistic maturation process that extends far beyond academic achievement. This theme reflects a shift from viewing giftedness as a measure of performance to viewing it as a state of being. Expert 1 underscored this perspective by highlighting the importance of fostering gifted individuals as “human beings, not robots,” arguing that psychological giftedness should fundamentally integrate emotional stability, social competence, and positive personal attributes alongside academic excellence. This suggests that the construct serves as a necessary corrective measure against the often-dehumanizing pressure of academic-centric identification systems, repositioning the gifted learner as a well-rounded individual rather than a mere vessel for academic success. Rather than viewing giftedness as a static rank of superiority, participants described psychological giftedness as a continuous and dynamic developmental trajectory. This perspective acknowledges that giftedness involves a unique internal intensity that requires ongoing psychological maturation. Counsellor 5 reinforced this notion, stating that “psychological giftedness is holistic intelligence that goes beyond academics, encompassing cognitive, emotional, social, and behavioural aspects.” This narrative suggests that the ‘whole person’ approach is essential to managing the complexities of giftedness. By framing psychological giftedness as a dynamic capacity for growth, participants argue that it provides a framework for students to balance their intellectual capabilities with the emotional maturity needed to navigate life’s challenges. Ultimately, this holistic development is seen as the foundation for nurturing emotionally stable and socially adept individuals who can thrive across diverse life contexts.

Theme 2: synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains

A strong consensus emerged among participants that psychological giftedness is not defined by isolated traits but by the synergistic integration of three core domains of functioning. Expert 2 articulated this tripartite view by stating that the construct encompasses “cognitive intelligence, emotional intelligence, and behavioural regulation,” suggesting that psychological giftedness is an active interplay between how a student thinks, feels, and acts. Participants emphasized that these domains are interdependent, where high-level cognitive processes serve as the regulatory foundation for socioemotional and behavioural maturity.

Theme 3: psychological giftedness as a dynamic capacity for contextual adaptability and internalized resilience

The final theme characterizes psychological giftedness as a dynamic capacity for contextual adaptability and internalized resilience, representing the functional manifestation of the integrated domains. Unlike traditional views that treat giftedness as a static trait, participants described it as an active psychological strength that enables learners to navigate the unique vulnerabilities associated with high intellectual potential. Expert 4 emphasized this by stating that students with psychological giftedness possess the internal tools to “if a student has psychological giftedness, they can manage their vulnerabilities and cope with stress,” suggesting that this construct acts as a protective buffer against the pressures of their environment. Central to this theme is the concept of proactive coping and stress management. Gifted individuals often face intense internal and external pressures, such as high parental expectations or personal perfectionism. However, psychological giftedness allows them to intellectually analyze and manage these stressors effectively. Counsellor 2 reinforced this, noting that a high IQ is ““it is not useful if a student has a high IQ but low EQ; they must know how to cope with stress effectively”. This capacity for proactive coping ensures that the individual’s cognitive brilliance is not overshadowed by emotional distress, but rather used as a resource for self-regulation. Furthermore, this capacity is manifested as contextual adaptability across diverse life transitions. Participants highlighted that psychological giftedness enables students to adjust their social and emotional responses as they move through different environments, such as from home to boarding school or university. Counsellor 1 observed that these students “understand themselves so they can adapt in any situation,” underscoring the flexibility inherent in the construct. This adaptability is driven by internalized resilience, which serves as a dynamic psychological strength. It empowers gifted individuals to face criticism and challenges while continuing to “achieve the life they aspire to,” ultimately moving beyond mere survival toward a state of long-term psychological flourishing. Discussion

The present study introduced and conceptualized psychological giftedness as a multidimensional construct integrating cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains. Critically, this construct is not proposed to replace well-established behavioural science concepts such as resilience or well-being. Instead, it serves as a specialized framework to understand how these processes are uniquely mediated by the high cognitive complexity and asynchronous development often found in gifted individuals.

Psychological giftedness as holistic human development beyond academic achievement

The emphasis on holistic human development reflects participants’ concern that current practices remain overly focused on academic outcomes while neglecting emotional and personal growth. This aligns with critiques of IQ-based identification, described as reductionist in capturing the full spectrum of gifted individuals’ needs [7, 15]. The findings suggest that psychological giftedness involves a humanistic maturation that moves beyond mere cognitive assessment to include the complex social and emotional experiences of the individual [2]. Unlike traditional views, our model positions this as a continuous developmental trajectory [14, 16] requiring the active cultivation of emotional stability alongside intellect. By nurturing the “whole person,” the framework acknowledges that giftedness is a lived experience where cognitive potential is only one part of a larger, evolving human system [2, 3]. Consequently, psychological giftedness serves as the necessary conceptual foundation to ensure exceptional potential is supported by the resources required for optimal human flourishing [8, 14].

Synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains

The second theme underscores the multidimensionality of the construct, emphasizing the synergistic integration of domains. While cognitive features like metacognition are hallmarks of giftedness [6, 11], this study extends the view by integrating them with socioemotional capacities (e.g., deep self-awareness) and behavioural regulation. This is supported by evidence from Olszewski-Kubilius et al. [9], suggesting that high potential often co-exists with heightened emotional sensitivity (overexcitabilities). Rather than treating these as separate entities, our findings suggest that psychological giftedness represents a functional state where metacognitive awareness is actively harnessed to regulate socioemotional intensity [9, 14]. This resonates with Fodor et al. [10], who highlight that well-being is contingent upon the successful integration of emotional and cognitive worlds. Thus, psychological giftedness is only effectively expressed when cognitive brilliance is complemented by behavioural regulation and adaptive traits, allowing the individual to flourish in complex environments.

Dynamic capacity for contextual adaptability and internalized resilience

The final theme highlights contextual adaptability and internalized resilience as defining functional features. While resilience is often viewed as an external protective factor, this study positions it as an internalized resilience an integral, dynamic psychological strength. Participants described this as a proactive coping mechanism to manage stress and navigate societal expectations. Contextual adaptability, meanwhile, was understood as the capacity to thrive across diverse life transitions, such as transition from secondary school to university. Recent scholarship supports this; Fodor et al. [10] and Preckel et al. [14] argue that gifted frameworks must account for how individuals adapt their potential to meet environmental demands. By framing resilience not merely as an outcome but as an internalized core strength, our study aligns with Rinn’s [2] call to understand the lived, functional experiences of giftedness. Unlike deterministic traits, this dynamic capacity can be nurtured, ensuring that inherent intensities are managed through self-regulation to prevent underachievement and ensure long-term success [3, 9, 14].

Summary definition of psychological giftedness

Based on the synthesis of thematic findings, this study proposes a refined conceptual definition of psychological giftedness:

"Psychological giftedness is defined as a dynamic and synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains. It is a continuous developmental trajectory that enables gifted individuals to achieve contextual adaptability and internalized resilience, allowing them to navigate their unique developmental phases (such as asynchronous development) and flourish across diverse life contexts"

Implications of the study

The findings of this study offer several critical implications for the field of gifted education, particularly in the Malaysian context and beyond:

Holistic educational support and “whole-person” development

The study encourages educators, counsellors, and school administrators to adopt a broader, humanistic understanding of giftedness. This shift is crucial for developing support structures that move beyond “academic coaching” toward nurturing the whole individual. By recognizing the synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional and behavioural domains, schools can implement interventions that specifically address asynchronous development. Such support should focus on humanistic maturation, ensuring that students are not merely academic performers but individuals with stable emotional foundations.

Policy and identification reform

The findings underscore the need for policymakers to revisit existing identification criteria for gifted programs, which traditionally rely heavily on high-IQ or standardized test scores. A revised framework incorporating psychological giftedness can ensure more equitable assessment procedures. This allows for the identification of students who possess exceptional socioemotional or metacognitive traits such as deep self-awareness and proactive coping which are often overlooked in traditional screenings, yet are vital for long-term success.

Development of specialized assessment instruments

The expert-derived themes and the proposed definition offer a robust theoretical foundation for the development of new psychometric instruments. Unlike general well-being scales, these tools can specifically target the functional integration of psychological domains in gifted populations. Future instruments could measure internalized resilience and contextual adaptability, addressing a critical gap in current assessment toolkits that often fail to capture the dynamic psychological strengths of gifted individuals.

Self-assessment and empowerment

The conceptualization of psychological giftedness provides a foundation for developing self-assessment tools. Such instruments would enable gifted individuals to better understand their own cognitive-emotional landscapes, fostering self-advocacy and proactive mental health management. By reframing their inherent intensities not as vulnerabilities but as manageable and adaptive strengths, students are empowered to navigate their continuous developmental trajectory with greater confidence and agency.

Limitations

This study presents several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the research employed a purposive sample of nine participants, consisting of Malaysian experts and school counsellors. While this sample provided significant “information power” a concept central to qualitative excellence due to the participants’ extensive veteran experiences, the findings are reflective of the Malaysian educational landscape and may not capture the full diversity of global perspectives.

Second, the context-specific nature of the participants’ interpretations means that the findings require further cross-cultural validation before being generalized to different cultural or educational systems. Although the proposed framework of psychological giftedness is theoretically robust, its practical manifestation may vary across different societal values. Third, in line with the Reflexive Thematic Analysis (RTA) framework, the interpretation of data is inherently shaped by the researchers’ positionality. While rigorous steps were taken to ensure trustworthiness and credibility, the findings should be viewed as contextually grounded insights rather than universally generalizable claims. Furthermore, as noted during the editorial process, these results represent a preliminary conceptual framework. While it establishes a robust, synergistic definition of giftedness, its application remains within a specific geographic context. Future research should prioritize broader empirical testing and quantitative validation of the proposed domains cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural to solidify its standing in the global scholarship of gifted education.

Recommendations for future research

To build upon the conceptual foundation established in this study and to ensure the practical utility of the proposed framework, the following recommendations are suggested for future research:

Psychometric operationalization and instrument development

A critical next step is to translate the identified qualitative themes cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains into a validated quantitative measurement. Future research should prioritize the development of a Self-Assessment Instrument of Psychological Giftedness. This involves generating a robust item pool based on expert and student perspectives, followed by rigorous validation processes, including Content Validity Index (CVI) evaluation and pilot testing to ensure reliability.

Factor analytical validation (EFA and CFA)

To solidify the structural validity of the psychological giftedness construct, future studies should employ advanced statistical techniques such as Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA). These analyses are essential to confirm whether the three-dimensional synergistic model comprising the 11 sub-themes identified in this study is statistically stable and replicable across larger populations of gifted and talented students.

Longitudinal monitoring of developmental trajectories

It is recommended that future research adopt a longitudinal approach using the newly developed self-assessment tool. Tracking how a student’s self-perceived psychological giftedness evolves as they navigate diverse life transitions from secondary school to higher education will provide invaluable data on the stability and dynamism of the construct over time, particularly in relation to their continuous developmental trajectory.

Predictive validity and underachievement prevention

Future studies should investigate the relationship between scores on the psychological giftedness scale and long-term academic and personal success. Research could focus on how internalized resilience and proactive coping (as measured by the instrument) act as mediators in preventing underachievement and fostering holistic well-being among gifted individuals.

Cross-cultural and linguistic adaptation

While this study provides a robust Malaysian-based framework, future research should explore the cross-cultural adaptation of the instrument. Testing the model in different linguistic and cultural settings would determine if the humanistic maturation of giftedness is perceived similarly across global educational landscapes.

Conclusion

This study introduces a refined and holistic conceptualization of psychological giftedness, shifting the focus from purely cognitive metrics to a more comprehensive, multidimensional framework. By synthesizing expert perspectives, the study defines psychological giftedness as a dynamic and synergistic integration of cognitive, socioemotional, and behavioural domains. It is a continuous developmental trajectory that enables gifted individuals to achieve contextual adaptability and internalized resilience, allowing them to navigate their unique developmental phases (such as asynchronous development) and flourish across diverse life contexts. The findings highlight that giftedness is not a static trait but as a continuous and dynamic developmental trajectory.

The establishment of this conceptual framework serves as a critical prerequisite for the development of specialized assessment tools. It provides the theoretical rigor necessary to move beyond traditional IQ-based identification toward a more equitable and holistic approach. Ultimately, this study underscores the importance of nurturing the “whole person,” ensuring that the exceptional potential of gifted individuals is supported by the psychological resources required for lifelong flourishing. By positioning internalized resilience and proactive coping at the core of the construct, this framework offers educators, counsellors, and policymakers a robust foundation to better identify, support, and empower gifted individuals in Malaysia and beyond.

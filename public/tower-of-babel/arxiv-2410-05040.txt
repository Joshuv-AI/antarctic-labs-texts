A nodally bound-preserving discontinuous Galerkin method for the drift-diffusion equation
Gabriel R. Barrenechea, Tristan Pryer, Alex Trenam

Abstract
In this work, we introduce and analyse discontinuous Galerkin (dG) methods for the drift-diffusion model. We explore two dG formulations: a classical interior penalty approach and a nodally bound-preserving method. Whilst the interior penalty method demonstrates well-posedness and convergence, it fails to guarantee non-negativity of the solution. To address this deficit, which is often important to ensure in applications, we employ a positivity-preserving method based on a convex subset formulation, ensuring the non-negativity of the solution at the Lagrange nodes. We validate our findings by summarising extensive numerical experiments, highlighting the novelty and effectiveness of our approach in handling the complexities of charge carrier transport.

Introduction

Charge carrier transport is a physical process concerned with the
interaction of mobile charge carriers in the presence of an electric
field. Modelling this process is of interest in many applications
including battery electrolytes, fuel cells, ion membrane channels,
plasma physics, semi-conductor devices, etc. (see
e.g.
Jü 09
,
wWZCX 12
for details). The typical scale of
applications means that molecular dynamics approaches are often
computationally impractical, despite offering the most detailed
physical description. Continuum models are a common alternative, and
include the Poisson-Nernst-Planck (PNP) system, also known as the
drift-diffusion equations (or Van Roosbroeck equations in
semi-conductor literature).

In this work we introduce and analyse a new discontinuous Galerkin
(dG) method for the drift-diffusion model:

\partial_{t}u=\operatorname{div}\!\left(\nabla u+u\nabla\psi\right),

(1)

where
\psi
is a prescribed electric potential and
u
the
particle concentration. We are interested in this as a prototypical example of an
electrolyte model that extends to the classical (normalised) two-species PNP equations

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho&=\operatorname{div}\!\left(\nabla\rho+\rho\nabla\psi\right)\\
\partial_{t}\nu&=\operatorname{div}\!\left(\nabla\nu-\nu\nabla\psi\right)\\
-\operatorname{div}\!\left(\varepsilon\nabla\psi\right)&=\rho-\nu+f,\end{split}

(2)

where
\rho
and
\nu
, respectively, represent positively and negatively
charged particle concentrations, and the electric potential
\psi
is now a variable
Ner 89
,
Pla 90
,
DH 23
. The electric permitivity is
\varepsilon>0
, and
f\geq 0
is the background fixed charge density.

The drift-diffusion equation (
1
) is an advection-diffusion equation, where the advection is the gradient of the electric potential. There is a rich body of literature concerning numerical methods (finite difference, finite volume, finite element, etc.) for advection-diffusion equations
Roo 08
,
HV 03
. In view of our application to concentrations (which must remain positive to make sense physically), we highlight the recent review of finite element methods respecting the discrete maximum principle for this problem
BJK 24
. The presence of boundary and interior layers present a particular challenge due to the propensity of typical finite element solutions to display spurious oscillations around (pseudo-)discontinuities. In many cases these oscillations cause the solution to become negative.

Maintaining the positivity of concentration variables in the PNP system (
2
) is not only desirable for physical interpretations but is integral to the stability of the solution and the satisfaction of an associated energy decay
Gaj 85
. Boundary layers, known physically as electrical double layers, can occur close to charged surfaces due to an exponential decay in electric potential
Bag 05
. The associated large values of
\nabla\psi
lead to locally convection-dominated flow, an effect which is only exacerbated with coupling of additional equations governing fluid flow, such as in the case of the Navier–Stokes–PNP system
CI 19
.

A piecewise linear continuous Galerkin approximation of
(
2
) is shown in
PS 09
to satisfy an
M-matrix property, which ensures the satisfaction of a discrete
maximum principle. With dG methods there is no such guarantee, even
for an interior penalty discretisation of pure diffusion

BJK 24
. For the steady-state drift-diffusion
scheme discretised with conforming piecewise linear finite elements,
in
BMP 89
different monotone finite element methods were
proposed, all of them sharing the aim of rewriting the popular
Sharfetter-Gummel finite volume scheme in a finite element way. The
analysis of the methods is carried out by rewriting the problem as a
mixed formulation. Some other methods, such as the dG schemes of

LW 17
and
LWYY 22
, make use of positivity-preserving
limiters to deal with this problem. Other approaches involve making a
judicious change of variables to guarantee the positivity of the
concentrations. Examples include the logarithmic change of variables
used in
MXL 16
,
FX 22
and the the Slotboom transformation
utilised in
Slo 73
,
XCLZ 13
. The cost of making such
substitutions is the introduction of further non-linearities to the
already-non-linear system. In this paper we consider dG methods,
which are well-suited to advection-dominated regimes due to the
additional stability they offer.

With the PNP system in mind, we examine in this paper two methods for
solving (
1
). The first is a classical dG
formulation of the problem, and the second is a method where
positivity of the concentration is hard-coded into the finite element
space. For the classical methodology, under both the natural advection-diffusion assumption
\Delta\psi\leq 0
and, alternatively, a time step restriction, we are able to show
well-posedness and conduct a convergence analysis, however, the
methodology does not satisfy a positivity preservation property. That
is, there is no guarantee that the solution does not become negative
within the domain.

The second method aims to rectify this. The main idea
behind the positivity enforcement is to utilise the methodology from

BGPV 24
,
ABP 24
, where we define a closed convex
subset of the standard dG space. Using this we formulate the problem
as a variational inequality at each timestep. This guarantees that the
dG solution is positive at the Lagrange nodes of each element. We show the problem is
well-posed and examine some of the qualitative properties of the
solution. In our numerical experiments we then show that the second
method preserves nodal postivity in the context of the PNP system.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: in
§
2
we fix some basic notation and discuss the model
problem its properties; a temporal semi-discretisation is studied in §
3
to transform the parabolic problem into a sequence of elliptic problems; in §
4
and §
5
we then introduce a dG spatial discretisation and a nodally bound-preserving extension; §
6
is devoted to demonstrating the properties possessed by these discretisations through numerical experiments; and finally, §
7
contains
some concluding remarks.

2
The drift-diffusion equation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\leq 3
be a bounded, polytopal
domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. Throughout this work we denote the
standard Lebesgue spaces by
L^{p}\!\left(\Omega\right)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
, with corresponding norms

\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}\!\left(\omega\right)}
. The
L^{2}
inner product over
\omega
is denoted
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\omega}
, where the subscript is omitted when
\omega=\Omega
. We introduce the Sobolev spaces

[
Eva 10
,
RR 04
, c.f.]

W^{m,p}\!\left(\Omega\right):=\left\{w\in L^{p}\!\left(\Omega\right):\;\mathrm{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}w\in L^{p}\!\left(\Omega\right),\text{ for }\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|\leq m\right\},

(3)

which are equipped with norms and semi-norms

\displaystyle\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{W^{m,p}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}:=\sum_{\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|\leq m}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\mathrm{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{p}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\text{ and }\left|w\right|_{W^{m,p}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}=\sum_{\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|=m}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\mathrm{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{p}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2},

(4)

respectively, where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{d}\}
is a
multi-index,
\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
and
derivatives
\mathrm{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are understood in a weak sense.
We identify the Hilbertian Sobolev spaces and norms by the notation
H^{m}\!\left(\Omega\right):=W^{m,2}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, and a zero subscript (e.g.
H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
) indicates vanishing trace on
\partial\Omega
.

We consider the drift-diffusion equation, an advection-diffusion
problem where the advection is the gradient of a potential
\psi=\psi\!\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)
. In the context of electrolytes, this
potential can be understood physically as the global electric
potential, and
\nabla\psi
is the electric field. In this viewpoint
the solution variable
u=u\!\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)

represents the density of some charged particles, with an initial
concentration profile given by
u_{0}
. For simplicity of
presentation, we suppose homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on

\partial\Omega\times\left(0,T\right]
, however, our results extend in a
straightforward fashion to more general Dirichlet conditions (see

ABP 24
,
BGPV 24
). We explore such numerical
examples in Section
6
. Therefore, we seek
u

satisfying

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u=\operatorname{div}\!\left(\nabla u+u\nabla\psi\right)&\text{ in }\Omega\times\left(0,T\right],\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times\left(0,T\right],\\
u=u_{0}&\text{ on }\Omega\times\left\{0\right\}.\end{split}

(5)

Introducing the bilinear forms
a:H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)\times H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
b:L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\times H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle a\!\left(w,v\right)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

(6)

\displaystyle b\!\left(w,v\right)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\!\left({w\nabla\psi}\right)\cdot{\nabla v}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

(7)

the initial-boundary value problem (
5
) can be written weakly as follows. Given
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and initial data
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, seek
u\!\left(t\right)\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, for almost every
t\in\left(0,T\right]
, such that

\left\langle\partial_{t}u,v\right\rangle+a\!\left(u,v\right)+b\!\left(u,v\right)=0\quad\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right).

(8)

Remark 2.1
.

The regularity assumption
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
is strong; however, much of the analysis can still be conducted under weaker assumptions. In particular,
\psi\in W^{2,p}\!\left(\Omega\right)
for
p>d
ensures that
\nabla\psi\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, which is sufficient to prove most of the results presented herein. To avoid technical diversions, we focus on the more regular case in this work.

Standard existence and uniqueness results for advection-diffusion equations (see
[
Eva 10
,
RR 04
, c.f.]
) apply to establish the well-posedness of (
8
).

Lemma 2.2

(PDE well-posedness)
.

Let
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. If
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, then there exists a unique solution
u(t)\in L^{2}\!\left(\left(0,T\right];H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)\right)\cap H^{1}\!\left(\left(0,T\right];H^{-1}\!\left(\Omega\right)\right)
to the weak IBVP
(
8
).

The problem (
8
) satisfies a stability result and a parabolic maximum principle, which we now detail. The preservation of these properties at the discrete level is the focus of the following sections. We begin with a useful lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(Energy identity)
.

Let
w\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and

\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. Then

a\!\left(w,w\right)+b\!\left(w,w\right)=\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Delta\psi,w^{2}\right\rangle.

(9)

Proof.

Using the definition of
b\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle b\!\left(w,w\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle w\nabla\psi,\nabla w\right\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\operatorname{div}\!\left(w\nabla\psi\right),w\right\rangle.

(11)

Using the product rule and rearranging we obtain

b\!\left(w,w\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Delta\psi,w^{2}\right\rangle.

(12)

The result then follows from the definition of
a\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Stability)
.

For almost every
t\in\left(0,T\right]
, let

u\!\left(t\right)\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
solve
(
8
), with
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and

0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right]=-\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Delta\psi,u^{2}\right\rangle.

(13)

The right hand side of the above equality is non-positive if
\Delta\psi\leq 0
.

Proof.

Choosing
v=u
in equation (
8
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert\!\right\rvert^{2}\right]=\left\langle\partial_{t}u,u\right\rangle=-a\!\left(u,u\right)-b\!\left(u,u\right),

(14)

and applying Lemma
2.3
completes the proof.
∎

The following result is a direct application of the parabolic maximum principle applied to the drift-diffusion equation (
5
), under the assumption
\Delta\psi\leq 0
.

Lemma 2.5

(Parabolic Maximum Principle
[
RR 04
, Theorem 4.26]
)
.

Let
u\in C\!\left(\left(0,T\right];H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)\right)
be a solution of the initial-boundary value problem (
5
) with
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. Suppose
\Delta\psi\leq 0
in
\Omega
. Then, for almost every
t\in\left(0,T\right]
, the solution satisfies the maximum principle

0\leq\inf_{\Omega}u_{0}\leq\inf_{\Omega}u\!\left(t\right)\leq\sup_{\Omega}u\!\left(t\right)\leq\sup_{\Omega}u_{0}.

(15)

3
A temporal semi-discretisation

In this section we examine an implicit backward Euler discretisation
of the problem (
8
) and some of the properties
inherited by this discretisation. We semi-discretise in time, and
hence the parabolic problem is transformed into a sequence of elliptic
problems.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the set of discrete time steps

\left\{t^{0},\ldots,t^{N}\right\}
, with
0=t^{0}<t^{1}<\ldots<t^{N}=T
and denote the time step size by
\tau
. A variable
w
at
time step
t^{n}
is denoted
w^{n}:=w\left(t^{n}\right)
. Our arguments
work with variable
\tau
, but for simplicity of presentation we
keep it fixed.

With the above notations (
5
) can be presented as a sequence of elliptic
problems. Let
u^{0}:=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, with
u_{0}\geq 0
, and

\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. For
n=1,2,\dots,N
find

u^{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
such that

\mathscr{A}(u^{n},v):=\left\langle u^{n},v\right\rangle+\tau\!\left({a(u^{n},v)+b(u^{n},v)}\right)=\left\langle u^{n-1},v\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>v\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right).

(16)

We now show that if
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, i.e., under the same
assumption as Lemma
2.2
, then
(
16
) is well-posed. In addition, there is a time step
condition which, if satisfied, guarantees well-posedness for any

\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. The proof is based on the following
coercivity and boundedness results regarding
\mathscr{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 3.1

(Semi-discrete coercivity)
.

Let
w\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. If
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, then

\mathscr{A}(w,w)\geq\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.

(17)

Alternatively, removing the assumption of
\Delta\psi
, suppose
instead that

\tau<\frac{4}{C_{\operatorname{Sob}}^{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{3}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}}.

(18)

where
C_{\operatorname{Sob}}
is the constant associated with the Sobolev embedding
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, which holds for
d\leq 3
. Then

\mathscr{A}(w,w)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.

(19)

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathscr{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(w,w)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau a\!\left(w,w\right)+\tau b\!\left(w,w\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}-\frac{\tau}{2}\left\langle\Delta\psi,w^{2}\right\rangle.

(21)

If
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, then (
17
)
follows. Otherwise, we invoke the Sobolev embedding

\operatorname{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, for
d\leq 3
,
and use Hölder’s inequality to see that

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\left\langle\Delta\psi,w^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{3}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{6}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\operatorname{Sob}}\frac{\tau}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{3}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}.

(23)

Young’s inequality then gives

\frac{\tau}{2}\left\langle\Delta\psi,w^{2}\right\rangle\leq C_{\operatorname{Sob}}^{2}\frac{\tau^{2}}{8}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{3}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2},

(24)

and therefore

\mathscr{A}(w,w)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left(1-C_{\operatorname{Sob}}^{2}\frac{\tau}{8}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{3}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.

(25)

The result in (
19
) is then a consequence of the time step restriction (
18
).
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Boundedness of
\mathscr{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
)
.

Let
w,v\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. Then

\mathscr{A}(w,v)\leq\left(1+C_{P}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\left(\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\left\lvert\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(26)

where
C_{P}
is the Poincaré constant.

Proof.

Applying the Hölder and Poincaré inequalities to the definition of
\mathscr{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
gives

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(w,v)

\displaystyle=\left\langle w,v\right\rangle+\tau a\!\left(w,v\right)+\tau b\!\left(w,v\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}+\tau\left(1+C_{P}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{P}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\left(\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla w\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}\right).

(29)

and using the discrete Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then yields the result.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.1
and Lemma

3.2
, the Lax-Milgram Lemma then yields the
following result:

Corollary 3.3

(Existence and uniqueness of semi-discrete solution)
.

Under the conditions of Lemma

3.1
, for
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, there exists a unique
u^{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)

solving (
16
).

The temporal semi-discretisation (
16
) satisfies the
following stability and maximum principle results, which are
semi-discrete analogues of the ones presented in Lemmata

2.4
and

2.5
, respectively.

Lemma 3.4

(Semi-discrete stability)
.

Let the conditions of Lemma
3.1
be satisfied, with
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, and let
u^{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
solve (
16
), for
n=1,2,\ldots,N
. Then

\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u^{n-1}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}-\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla u^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.

(30)

Proof.

From (
16
) and Lemma
3.1
we have

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla u^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\leq\mathscr{A}(u^{n},u^{n})=\left\langle u^{n-1},u^{n}\right\rangle,

(31)

and the result follows by application of the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Maximum Principle for the Semi-Discrete Problem)
.

Suppose
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and
\Delta\psi\leq 0
in
\Omega
. Let
\left\{u^{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N}
be the sequence of solutions to the semi-discrete problem (
16
) with initial condition
u^{0}=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, where
u_{0}\geq 0
. Then the following maximum principle holds:

0\leq\inf_{\Omega}u^{0}\leq\inf_{\Omega}u^{n}\leq\sup_{\Omega}u^{n}\leq\sup_{\Omega}u^{0},\quad\text{for all }n=1,2,\dots,N.

(32)

Proof.

We prove the non-negativity of
u^{n}
by induction on
n
. For the
base case,
n=0
, the initial condition
u^{0}=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
is given and, by assumption,
u_{0}\geq 0
. Now,
assume inductively that
u^{n-1}\geq 0
for some
n\geq 1
. We
want to show that
u^{n}\geq 0
.

Let us begin by defining the positive and negative parts of
u^{n}
by
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{+}:=\max\!\left({u^{n},0}\right)
and
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{-}:=u^{n}-\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{+}
, respectively, noting that
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{+},\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{-}\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
since
u^{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
.
Then the mutually-disjoint support of
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{+}
and
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{-}
implies that

\left\langle u^{n},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rangle=\left\langle\left(u^{n}\right)_{+}+\left(u^{n}\right)_{-},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rangle=\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.

(33)

Choosing
v=\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}
in
(
16
) then leads to

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}=\left\langle u^{n-1},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rangle-\tau\left(a\!\left(u^{n},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right)+b\!\left(u^{n},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right)\right).

(34)

It follows from the inductive hypothesis that

\left\langle u^{n-1},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rangle\leq 0,

(35)

and using again the mutually-disjoint
support of
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{+}
and
\!\left({u^{n}}\right)_{-}
then gives

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\leq-\tau\!\left({a\!\left(\left(u^{n}\right)_{-},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right)+b\!\left(\left(u^{n}\right)_{-},\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right)}\right).

(36)

If
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, then Lemma
2.3
implies that

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}=0,

(37)

and so
\left(u^{n}\right)_{-}\equiv 0
. In other words
u^{n}\geq 0
.
The upper bound can be shown in a similar fashion taking
v=\left(u^{n}-\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u^{0}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)_{+}
.
∎

4
A discontinuous Galerkin method

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a regular subdivision of
\Omega
into disjoint
simplicial or box-type (quadrilateral/hexahedral) elements
K
. We
assume that the subdivision
\mathcal{T}
is shape-regular, that

\bar{\Omega}=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\bar{K})
, and that the codimension-one elemental
faces are straight planar segments; these will be, henceforth,
referred to as
facets
. We shall denote the
union of all
interior
facets associated with

\mathcal{T}
, not including the boundary, by
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}
.

For
0\leq p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a
K\in\mathcal{T}
we denote the
set of polynomials of total degree at most
p
by
\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)
which
allows us to define the discontinuous Galerkin finite element space

\mathbb{V}_{p}:=\left\{w_{h}\in L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right):\;w_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)\quad\>\forall\>K\in\mathcal{T},\text{ and with vanishing trace on }\partial\Omega\right\}.

(38)

Remark 4.1
.

Our motivation for imposing the boundary conditions strongly is the pointwise nature of the contraints we shall apply in Section
5
. It is then straightforward to extend the method to non-homogeneous conditions. We believe the nodally bound-preserving method is compatible with discretisations with weakly-enforced boundary conditions, however, this remains the focus of active research.

Let
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
K_{1}
upwind from
K_{2}
, be two
elements sharing a facet
e\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}
. For a function

w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we define the jump and average operators over
e
by

\displaystyle\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket_{e}:=w|_{K_{1}}\mathbf{n}_{K_{1}}+w|_{K_{2}}\mathbf{n}_{K_{2}},

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor_{e}:=w|_{K_{1}}-w|_{K_{2}},

\displaystyle\left\{\!\!\left\{w\right\}\!\!\right\}_{e}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(w\rvert_{K_{1}}+w\rvert_{K_{2}}\right),

(39)

respectively. We will usually omit the subscript
e
. Let
h_{K}:=\text{diam}(K)
and
h\in\mathbb{V}_{0}
to be the piecewise constant meshsize function such that
h|_{K}=h_{K}
.

Now we define the discontinuous Galerkin method. Let
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
,
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, and
\Pi_{h}:L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{p}
be the
L^{2}
projection. For
u_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\!\left(u_{0}\right)
and each
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, find
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
such that

\mathscr{A}_{h}(u_{h}^{n},v_{h}):=\left\langle u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right\rangle+\tau\!\left({a_{h}(u_{h}^{n},v_{h})+b_{h}(u_{h}^{n},v_{h})}\right)=\left\langle u^{n-1}_{h},v_{h}\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p},

(40)

where, for
w,v\in H^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
and
\sigma,\mu>0
,

a_{h}(w,v):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\!\left({\int_{K}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}}\right)-\int_{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}}\!\left({\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\{\!\!\left\{\nabla v\right\}\!\!\right\}+\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\{\!\!\left\{\nabla w\right\}\!\!\right\}-\frac{\sigma}{h}\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

(41)

and

b_{h}(w,v):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\!\left({\int_{K}w\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}}\right)-\int_{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}}\!\left({\left(\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}\right)\left\{\!\!\left\{w\right\}\!\!\right\}\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor-\frac{\mu}{2}\lvert\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}\rvert\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(42)

The bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the symmetric interior penalty (SIP) discretisation of the diffusive term, which is coercive, assuming
\sigma
is chosen large enough, on
\mathbb{V}_{p}
under the norm

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w_{h}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{\operatorname{sip}}^{2}:=\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\nabla\!_{h}w_{h}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{h}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\left\llbracket w_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}\right)}^{2},

(43)

where
\sigma
depends on the polynomial degree
p
and geometric
features of the mesh
CGH 14
.

Lemma 4.2

(SIP coercivity, e.g.
[
DE 12
, Lemma 4.12]
,
[
CDGH 17
, Lemma 27]
)
.

Let
w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
. There exists
\widehat{\sigma}>0
and
C_{C}^{\operatorname{sip}}>0
, such that if
\sigma\geq\widehat{\sigma}
, then

a_{h}(w_{h},w_{h})\geq C_{C}^{\operatorname{sip}}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w_{h}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{\operatorname{sip}}^{2}.

(44)

The bilinear form
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is an upwinding discretisation of the advective term, where the classical upwinding method is recovered when
\mu=1
. A natural notion of error when considering the problem (
40
) is the energy norm defined by

\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}:=\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w_{h}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert w_{h}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{\operatorname{sip}}^{2}+\frac{\tau\mu}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\lvert\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}\rvert^{1/2}\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor w_{h}\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}\right)}^{2},

(45)

and the well-posedness of the discrete problem is a consequence of the
following coercivity result for the bilinear form
\mathscr{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the proof of which follows the same lines as
[
DE 12
, Lemma 4.59]
.

Lemma 4.3

(Discrete coercivity)
.

Let
w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
and
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. With
C_{C}^{\operatorname{sip}}
and
C_{\operatorname{Sob}}^{K}
defined as in Lemma
4.2
. If
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, then
\mathscr{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive on
\mathbb{V}_{p}
, with

\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h},w_{h})\geq C_{C}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2},\quad\text{ where }C_{C}:=\min\!\left(1,C_{C}^{\operatorname{sip}}\right).

(46)

Otherwise, if
\Delta\psi\nleq 0
, then
\mathscr{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive on
\mathbb{V}_{p}
provided
\tau<2\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{-1}
, we have

\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h},w_{h})\geq C_{C}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2},\quad\text{ where }C_{C}:=\min\!\left(1-\frac{\tau}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\Delta\psi\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)},C_{C}^{\operatorname{sip}}\right).

(47)

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, and let the assumptions of Lemma
4.3
be satisfied. Then there exists a unique
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
solving (
40
), for each
n=1,2,\ldots,N
.

The discrete solution to (
40
) enjoys the satisfaction of the following stability result.

Lemma 4.5

(Discrete stability)
.

Let the conditions of Lemma
4.3
be satisfied. For
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, let
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
be the solution to (
40
), with
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{h}^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{h}^{n-1}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}-C_{C}^{\operatorname{sip}}\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{h}^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{\operatorname{sip}}^{2}-\frac{\tau\mu}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\lvert\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}\rvert^{1/2}\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor u_{h}^{n}\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}\right)}^{2}

(48)

\displaystyle-\tau\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\!\left({-\frac{1}{2}\int_{K}\!\left({u_{h}^{n}}\right)^{2}\Delta\psi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}}\right),

and the sequence
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{h}^{n}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}
is monotonically decreasing if
\Delta\psi\leq 0
.

Proof.

Integrating (
42
) by parts, for all
w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
, we find that

b_{h}(w_{h},w_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\!\left({-\frac{1}{2}\int_{K}w_{h}^{2}\Delta\psi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}}\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\lvert\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}\rvert^{1/2}\left\lfloor\!\!\left\lfloor w_{h}\right\rfloor\!\!\right\rfloor\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}\right)}^{2}.

(49)

Choosing
v_{h}=u_{h}
in (
40
), we obtain

\mathscr{A}_{h}(u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n})=\left\langle u_{h}^{n-1},u_{h}^{n}\right\rangle.

(50)

and then using Lemma
4.2
, the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities, and (
49
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.6

(Non-conforming DMP)
.

In the conforming setting, at least with
\Delta\psi\leq 0
, we
can use lumping to ensure the discrete problem forms an M-matrix at
the algebraic level
Tho 06
which guarantees a
discrete maximum principle. In the non-conforming setting things are
less clear due to the coupling between elements through the jump
terms. It is unclear whether a DMP for a SIP discretisation can be
proven for a simple diffusion equation, see
[
BJK 24
, §9.4]
for a further discussion and references.

4.6
A priori error analysis

In this section we show an a priori error bound in the energy norm for
the discretisation (
40
). The error analysis is based on
the following lemmata, see also
DGP 20
, where we
make use of the stronger norm

\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}^{2}:=\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}+\tau\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\lvert\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}\rvert^{1/2}\left\{\!\!\left\{w_{h}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{int}}\right)}^{2}.

(51)

Lemma 4.7

(Boundedness of
\mathscr{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
)
.

Let
w_{h},v_{h}\in H^{2}\!\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
. There exists
C_{B}>0
, such that

\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})\leq C_{B}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}\left|\!\left|\!\left|v_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|.

(52)

Lemma 4.8

(Consistency of
\mathscr{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
)
.

Let
u^{n}\in H^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\cap H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
solve (
16
), and let
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
solve (
40
). Then

\mathscr{A}_{h}(u^{n}-u_{h}^{n},v_{h})=\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}.

(53)

Proof.

Let
A:H^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\longrightarrow L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)
be the operator associated with the bilinear form
\mathscr{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
. That is, for
w\in H^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)
,

\left\langle Aw,v\right\rangle=\mathscr{A}(w,v)\quad\>\forall\>v\in H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right).

(54)

The strong form of the temporal semi-discretisation (
16
) then reads: for
u^{0}:=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, with
u_{0}\geq 0
, and
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, seek
u^{n}\in H^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\cap H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
such that

\left\langle Au^{n},v\right\rangle=\left\langle u^{n-1},v\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>v\in L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right).

(55)

Also, let
A_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{p}\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{p}
be the operator associated with the bilinear form
\mathscr{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e. for
w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
,

\left\langle A_{h}w_{h},v_{h}\right\rangle=\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})\quad\>\forall\>v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}.

(56)

As a result of the consistency of the discrete operator
A_{h}
, and using (
55
), we then have

\mathscr{A}_{h}(u^{n},v_{h})=\left\langle A_{h}u^{n},v_{h}\right\rangle=\left\langle Au^{n},v_{h}\right\rangle=\left\langle u^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}.

(57)

The result then follows by taking the difference of equations (
57
) and (
40
).
∎

Lemma 4.9

(A Strang-type lemma)
.

For
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, let
u^{n}\in H^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\cap H^{1}_{0}\!\left(\Omega\right)
solve (
16
) and
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
solve (
40
). Also, let
C_{C}
and
C_{B}
be defined as in Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.7
, respectively. Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.3
, we have

\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{C_{B}}{C_{C}}\right)\inf_{w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}}\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}+\frac{1}{C_{C}}\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}}\frac{\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle}{\left|\!\left|\!\left|v_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|}.

(58)

Proof.

Let
w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
. The triangle inequality gives

\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|\leq\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|+\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|,

(59)

and we now control the second term on the right-hand side. Using first Lemma
4.3
and then Lemma
4.8
, it follows that

\displaystyle C_{C}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h}-u_{h}^{n},w_{h}-u_{h}^{n})

(60)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h}-u^{n},w_{h}-u_{h}^{n})+\mathscr{A}_{h}(u^{n}-u_{h}^{n},w_{h}-u_{h}^{n})

(61)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{h}(w_{h}-u^{n},w_{h}-u_{h}^{n})+\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right\rangle.

(62)

Lemma
4.7
then gives

\displaystyle C_{C}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|\!\left({C_{B}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}+\frac{\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right\rangle}{\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|}}\right)

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|\left(C_{B}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}+\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}}\frac{\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle}{\left|\!\left|\!\left|v_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|}\right),

(64)

and dividing by
C_{C}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|
results in

\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|\leq\frac{C_{B}}{C_{C}}\left|\!\left|\!\left|w_{h}-u^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}+\frac{1}{C_{C}}\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}}\frac{\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle}{\left|\!\left|\!\left|v_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|}.

(65)

Returning to (
59
), we thus have

\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right|\!\right|\!\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{C_{B}}{C_{C}}\right)\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}+\frac{1}{C_{C}}\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}}\frac{\left\langle u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle}{\left|\!\left|\!\left|v_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|},

(66)

and choosing
w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
to minimise
\left|\!\left|\!\left|u^{n}-w_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{*}
completes the proof.
∎

As highlighted in Remark

, whereby we
seek solutions that respect the bounds at, but not necessarily between,
element nodes. Figure

demonstrates the concept
for piecewise linear and piecewise quadratic polynomials in one

p=1
the solution satisfies the
bounds
globally
, and not just at the nodes.

Let us begin by defining the closed convex subset
\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{p}
by restricting finite element functions at the Lagrange nodes of each element, denoted
\mathcal{L}_{K}
for all
K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, to be between zero and the supremal value of the initial condition:

\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}:=\left\{w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}:0\leq w_{h}\!\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\right)\leq\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{0}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)},\text{ for all }\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{K},\text{ for all }K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}\right\}.

(67)

Remark 5.1
.

The use of the upper limit
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{0}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}
is rather coarse and does not provide control as the solution decays towards zero. Given that the problem at each time step satisfies an elliptic maximum principle, one could instead consider a family of convex sets where the upper limit is given by
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u^{n-1}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}
, providing sharper bounds as the solution evolves. At the discrete level
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert u_{h}^{n-1}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)}
could be used, with implementations requiring only an upper limit update step to be added in the solving routine. Time-dependence in the definition of the convex set is also considered in the companion paper
ABP 25
.

We then pose the following method, where we write
\!\left({u_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}
to distinguish the nodally bound-preserving (or
constrained
) solution from the
unconstrained
solution
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
to (
40
).

Given
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and initial data
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, so
u_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\!\left(u_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
, seek
\!\left({u_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}
, for
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, such that

\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left({\!\left({u_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+},v_{h}-\!\left({u_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+}}\right)\geq\left\langle\!\left({u_{h}^{n-1}}\right)^{+},v_{h}-\!\left({u_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+}\right\rangle\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}.

(68)

Remark 5.2
.

A discrete approximation to the initial condition in
\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}
can be obtained using a bound-preserving operator
\Pi^{+}_{h}:L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{p}^{+}
. If
u_{0}
is smooth enough to admit point values we may use the Lagrange interpolant. If
u_{0}
is less smooth then other constructions are available (see e.g.

5.4
Higher-order time discretisations

Higher-order time discretisations can also be posed whilst retaining the structure preservation properties. For example, an unconstrained second-order Crank-Nicolson method would read as follows. Given
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
and
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\!\left(\Omega\right)
, so
u_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\!\left(u_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
, seek
u_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}
, for

, such that

\left\langle u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right\rangle+\tau a_{h}\left(\frac{u_{h}^{n}+u_{h}^{n-1}}{2},v_{h}\right)+\tau b_{h}\left(\frac{u_{h}^{n}+u_{h}^{n-1}}{2},v_{h}\right)=\left\langle u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{p}.

(71)

Then the following stability result, which is an analogue of Lemma
4.5
holds.

.

Let the conditions of Lemma
4.3
be satisfied, and let

solve (

), for

. Then

which is monotonically decreasing if
\Delta\psi\leq 0
.

Proof.

Choosing

A corresponding nodally bound-preserving Crank-Nicolson discretisation can be posed and the related analogue to Lemma
5.4
proceeds in a similar fashion to the backward Euler case.

Remark 5.6
.

The methods presented herein can be combined with other higher-order dissipative-type time discretisations, such as dG-in-time approaches

In the final example we demonstrate the efficacy of our method as an approach to discretising the coupled Poisson-Nernst-Planck system (

\beta_{h}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)

(88)

\displaystyle\varepsilon a_{h}\!\left(\psi_{h}^{n},\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\!\left({\rho_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+}-\!\left({\nu_{h}^{n}}\right)^{+}+f,\Phi\right\rangle\quad\>\forall\>\Phi\in\mathbb{V}_{p}.

(89)

Concluding remarks

The dG methods introduced and analysed in this work are shown to converge optimally in the energy norm, and the nodally bound-preserving method respects a discrete analogy of the maximum principle enjoyed by the drift-diffusion equation. Both discretisations also satisfy an energy dissipation law under the same assumptions on the data as the PDE. Although we focus on a backward Euler time discretisation, we also demonstrate structure preservation for a Crank–Nicolson scheme and discuss other higher-order-in-time methods. The presentation of the nodally bound-preserving method in a variational inequality framework simplifies the treatment of the analysis, and the implementation is achieved via a straightforward iterative projection method at each time step. We summarise with extensive numerical experiments, which demonstrate the just-described properties and the effectiveness of the methods. Finally, with a view towards future work, we consider an extension to the coupled Poisson-Nernst-Planck system, showing the robustness of the bound preservation in this case.

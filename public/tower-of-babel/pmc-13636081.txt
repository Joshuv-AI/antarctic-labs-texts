Sand-grinding integrated with coatings for preparation of hydrophobic surface based on Miao silver
Authors: Hongyun Zhang, Jian Li, Jinfu Pan, Qiuyan Mo, Yongjun Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWater-proofing, anti-corrosion and anti-tarnishing requirement is currently the key to improvement of Miao silver industry. Inspired by superhydrophobic surface (SHS), we managed to use it as a protecting layer to isolate from air, water vapor, liquid electrolyte possibly around with silver ware; meanwhile also give a guide for other mental productions with the same requirement. Considering micro/nanostructure of lotus leaf, water strider’s leg, butterfly wings together surface wax substance, especially for butterfly wings’ scale-like macrostructure with semi-cylinder-like nanostructure on it, a biomimetic semi-cylinder was theoretically put forward to simulate such composite structure. Followed by it, experimentally using sand-grinding integrated with SiO2-doped fluorinated modified silane coatings to obtain an SHS with water contact angle (CA) of 162° and CA hysteresis of 4°, micro/nanostructure obtained by coarse/fine sand-grinding in same direction respectively for simulation of butterfly wings’ scale structure (macro size) and cylinder structure (nano size); coatings mainly consist of fluorinated modified silane and nano SiO2 grain from doping; thus structure and coating design having some of independent.Our study emphasizes three aspects below:1.Theoretically, how to simulate micro/nano structure of butterfly wings by modeling based on the natural and artificial SHS, thus a semi-cylinder model was proposed.2.Experimentally, how to prepare such an SHS similar to the butterfly wings’ composite structure; thus method of sand-grinding integrated with coatings was configured.3.How to establish the relationship between microstructure and the roughness, solid fraction, contact angle, adhesion work and spreading coefficient based on the already formed composite wetting state.Keywords: Biomimetic, Micro/nano–structure, Doping, Composite wetting state, Roughness, Solid fraction

Background

Silver products, widely used in Miao ethnic clothing and vessels, are prone to corrosion and discoloration, seriously affecting the bright appearance. In electronic industry, the discoloration of silver also leads to increase of contact resistance of silver parts resulting in that the weldability and conductivity gets worse with it [1,2]. Therefore, how to prevent and slow down surface corrosion and discoloration of silver, and develop new anti-corrosion and anti-discoloration technology is a challenge to improve the application value of silver [3,4], especially for improvement of Miao silver jewelry and development of the local industry. The already studies indicate that corrosion and discoloration of the silver are affected by multi-factors as oxide (oxygen, nitrogen, chlorine), sulfide, residual salt, temperature, humidity, electromagnetic wave and so on (external factors) [3]; as well as properties of silver itself, coating performance, and process conditions (internal factors) [5].

The practical used Miao silver in ethnic clothing and vessels is mainly based on material of brass, white copper, or red copper instead of really pure silver. Freshly made Miao silver products are bright and white but easy to be oxidized and fade due to exposed to the air and no protection coatings. Therefore, how to reduce or prevent the oxidation and discoloration of silver ornaments requires further improvement in surface processing techniques, especially for the ability to collect and then repel the sweat [3,6,7]. Based on some success cases of anti-corrosion and discoloration by using the SHS [[8], [9], [10]], we also managed to use similar method to solve the problem of corrosion and discoloration for Miao silver to some extent.

Traditionally speaking, artificial SHS to simulate the organic superhydrophobic surfaces (lotus leaf, butterfly swing and water strider) is mainly based on two steps: firstly creating a surface with high roughness in micro/nano scale, then coating a hydrophobic materials with low surface energy [[11], [12], [13]]. We conducted this study to design an SHS with a micro-nano composite structure similar to the surface of water strider foot and butterfly wings by sandpaper-grinding the white copper and doping insoluble SiO2 nanoparticles into a fluorinated modified silane coating, and finally achieved an SHS with a certain degree of corrosion resistance. Thus both the sanding analogous to metal wire drawing (up-down mode) and the doping into coatings (down-up mode), obtaining an interface synergy effect (interaction of micro/nanostructure and chemical compositions), as a low cost and practical technique, are main experiment method different from other studies, and firstly applied to the Miao silver ornaments. Obviously, despite of our basic experiment method not breaking through the above traditional line but with own distinctive features in concrete technique style. Meanwhile, such method with general application property can be promoted to other requirements, i.e., silver contact materials for reduction of resistance, silver alloy solder, water repellence, seawater desalination, anti-biofouling, anti-fogging, antibacterial coatings and anti-icing of solid surfaces,oil-water separation and salt-tolerant evaporation[10,[14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]].

Proposing a semi-cylinder model

Considering practical requirement of Miao silver or others, water-proof, anti-tarnishing/corrosion, and use of sand-grinding as an economic technology, we try to group them and create a superhydrophobic effect by designing a new method.

Experimental basis for constructing the SHS

So far, because of the superhydrophobic phenomena from lotus leaf, taro leaf, water strider leg, butterfly wings in nature, strong curiosity drives people to study its secret. Accompanied with development of observation technique of microstructure, especially inventions of Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) and Environmental Scanning Electron Microscope (ESEM), man have ability to observe the microstructure of materials. Finally, SEM and ESEM demonstrated the reason of “lotus effect” [20,21]. Taking a lotus leaf for example, as a representative of plant with hydrophobicity, on the surface of lotus leaf, there are innumerable frustum-shaped prisms in micrometer size (with an average diameter, spacing, and height of 8–10 µm, 10–12 µm, and 12–15 µm, respectively), and countless nanoscale protrusions (with an average height of 150 nm) being densely distributed on each prism and between them; meanwhile, the surface of these protrusions is covered with a hydrophobic waxy layer (Figs. 1(a)) [21]. Similarly, as a representative of animal with ability of water repellence, the surface of the water strider foot has countless bristle with an average length of 40–50 µm, and each bristle is covered with several nanoscale grooves (with length of >1 µm, and width of about 150 nm (Figs. 1(b) and (c))[20]. In addition, the surface of butterfly wings also has a scale-like micrometer structure (length of 100 µm, width of about 30 µm), on which the nanoscale cylindrical structure with length in micrometer, diameter about 1 µm; the column is also distributed with grooves and linear stripes between grooves with width 100 nm as the third level structure (Figs. 1(d) and (e)) [22]. Sum up, these organism structure enjoy a common characteristic: micro/nano double scale composite and structure fractal, the first-level structure with micrometer level ranging from 10 to 40 µm, and the second-level structure with nanometer level of about 100 nm. Although the above three microstructures differ greatly from one another in morphology, they all acquire e superior water repellence ability. Enlightened by such micro/nano structure, large number of artificial SHS’s progressively got to be fabricated. Just right, we are familiar with use of sand paper; butterfly wings’ micro-morphology ignites our creative line [22].

Bionic modeling and line of preparation

Considering convenience of preparation, mainly referring the micro-morphology of butterfly wings and water striders' legs (Figs. 1(b)-(e)), a quasi-hemispherical composite structure (Figs. 1(f), (g)) has been theoretically designed based on the cylindrical grooves on butterfly wings, the steel hair bundles of water striders' legs. The hydrophobic coatings along with nano-granular in it (Fig. 1(g)) are similar to the wax substance with low surface free energy on the lotus leaf. The line connecting two intersection points of each nanoscale semicircle with microscale semicircle in cross-section is precisely the diameter of the nano-semicircle.

Step-by-step protocol:

Miao silver (copper sheet) selected as the substrate to simulate the two-tiered structures of butterfly wings and water striders' legs, as well as the nano-protrusions on lotus leaf surfaces by metal-drawing (or etching from up to down)[23]. For the first tier, a micrometer-scale structure was formed by coarsely grinding the copper sheet surface with 100-mesh coarse sandpaper to simulate the groove on butterfly wings and water striders’ legs; for the second tier, nano-scale structures were built on the mesoscale structure using 1200-mesh fine sandpaper to create a nano-semispherical structure on the mesoscale hemispherical structure. Additionally, immersing mutually insoluble nano-hydrophobic particles in liquid hydrophobic coatings can improve the hierarchical effects of micro-nano composite structures as shown in Fig. 1(g). Then, when applying suspension to the roughened surface of the components, a synergy effect for hydrophobicity appears by combining the silane, as a low-surface-energy material, with the aforementioned hierarchical structures [24]. Establish analytical relationship between microstructure and the property parameters based on the already formed composite wetting state

To our knowledge, it is micro/nano-structure that creates the superhydrophobic effect. Therefore, series of property parameters, i.e., roughness, solid fraction, contact angle, adhesion work and spreading coefficient, must be associated with microstructure parameters, such as radius of semi-cylinder model, coatings chemical compositions (approximately represented by intrinsic CA on the ideal smooth surface).

About this transition, it is no doubt there has been practical significance for surface to waterproof, also the purpose of this study. Actually, its realization suffer from multi-factors, i.e., morphology, surface energy, temperature and humidity, vibration of water droplet and purity, especially roughness [26]. Therefore, corresponding critical condition for transition from non-composite to composite wetting state is not absolutely. Only the outside environment and materials of solid-liquid interface given, corresponding critical condition is still certain, i.e., under certain outside environment and materials, such critical condition depend mainly on roughness of solid surface, and critical roughness change with hydrophobicity of materials, more hydrophobic, lower critical roughness. We once obtained 1.8 or so of value based on two-step microstructure and 120° of intrinsic CA based on equal adhesion work by linking non-composite with composite wetting state; earlier value of 1.75 in 1964 from Rulon E. Johnson Jr. and Robert H. Dettre’s research based on equal apparent CA using Wenzel equation and Cassie-Baxter equation [26,28]. Higher roughness can inhibit the formation of non-composite wetting state. Therefore this study specially highlights the roughness of surface by designing composite micro/nano structure, satisfying the condition of transition from non-composite to composite wetting state. Significantly, the roughness of surface model from computation is 4.93, being far higher than the critical value, no doubt that formation of composite wetting state.

The computation results largely satisfy with the actual hydrophobic state of the lotus leaf and water stride’s leg. Furthermore, we found that the radius (r) of nano-semi-cylinder has key effect to the surface property once the composite wetting state is formed. However, both radius directly and comparably affect the roughness and solid fraction respectively (Fig. 6(a1) and 6(a2)). More detailed discussion may refer to our previous study [26].

Especially note that both radius of R and r only used for configuration of microstructure respective in micro-size and nano-size, its change in size will not influence morphology of coatings, namely, the morphology keeps unchanged, also the roughness of 4.93 at least from theoretically modeling. Certainly, there exist differences in the morphology experimentally due to sand-grinding differences in direction and force.

Property test

Contact angle test

Direct property test mainly includes water CA, CAH (by means of dragging using needle of syringer instead of sessile drop method) obtained by SDC-350. However, the roughness (aim at area), solid fraction, adhesion work and spreading coefficient belong to compound quantities, only by indirectly calculating and computing software attached by SDC-350 itself. Test data are as Table 1 shows [25].

Syringe dragging test

Take sample 15 sheet (2 cm × 4 cm), roughen one-half using the No.8 method, and then brush it with suspension three times; the other half remains unchanged. Then drop 10 µl of water on each half, insert the needle tip to drag the water droplets (Fig. 3(h)). Capture a specific dragging state, and measure the CA at the front and back edges simultaneously, which are approximately advancing/receding CA, of which their difference is the CA hysteresis (shorten for CAH next). Therefore, this study does not use Sessile Drop (injection/absorption) method to measure the advancing/receding CA.

Aging and anti-corrosion test

Set normal temperature and indoor environment, place sample 8 in such condition for 30 days, the CA and CAH almost do not suffer from changing, indicating that the environment under normal outdoor air conditions will not have a significant impact on the coatings, and the coatings are durable to aging (Fig. 5(a), (b), (e), (f)).

Whether having anti-corrosion ability or not, referring to Chinese standards GB/1763–89 (test Method for Chemical Resistant Properties of Paint Coatings), firstly use mixture of wax and rosin with ratio of 1:1 to edge-banding, then soak 2/3 of sample 8 (white copper substrate+coatings) and 1/2 of sample 19(95 Ag substrate+coatings) into 3% salt solution for 20 days. The tested CAs fall down to 138° from 161° (Fig. 5(e)-(g)), to 152° from 154° (Fig. 5(k)-(l)), respectively. The hydrophobic performance decrease greatly for white substrate, but lightly slow for 95Ag substrate owe to weak chemical activity of Ag. Both surface coatings show signs of swelling, suggesting that the corrosion resistance to salt solution is poor and temporarily can not meet the practical requirements, needs to be further optimized.

Further copper sulfate drop test approves above results. After 19 h contact with copper sulphate, 4 samples: sample 19, as prepared 95Ag, sample 15, as prepared white copper do not show obvious corrosion signs only leaving blue crystal due to all water being evaporated. After cleaning, retest water CA with 155°, 68°, 145°, 80°, only lightly decrease in CA, demonstrating coatings and substrates with certain anti-corrosion capabilities (Fig. 5(m) and 5(n)), at least for short time contact, because the chemical activity of silver is less than that of copper.

On account of weak anti-corrosion ability, impedance between coatings and conductive substrate (white copper and 95Ag) also shows minor change, i.e. before immersing tends to ∞, later shows some limit values, even zero value because of coatings possibly not being uniform in thick thus corroded in some point, leading to short circuit.

Data analysis

Select 10 pieces of samples for direct or indirect testing, test content includes roughness, solid fraction, CA together hysterisis (Table 1), suface/intersurface tension, adhesion work and spread coefficient, SEM observation and Infrared spectrum (Fig. 6).

Roughness together solid fraction determines theoretical and experiment results

According to Eqs. (1) and (13), the area roughness and solid fraction in possible composite wetting state are as Fig. 6(a1), (a2) indicate. Computation results of the roughness demonstrate its increase with radius of micro semi-cylinder, and inversely with radius of nano semi-cylinder. When higher area roughness (larger than 1.8) satisfy condition for forming composite wetting state, solid-liquid interface possibly enter into composite wetting state instead of non-composite wetting state, thus gaining large CA, less CAH and adhesion work together spreading coefficient (Fig. 6(b2)-(e2)). Correspondingly, experiment test largely accords with computation, i.e., with increase in arithmetic mean roughness of the substrate from sample 1 to 8, after coating fluorinated modified silane, water CA also increase with it from 64° to 131°. If coating suspension (obtained by doping SiO2 into fluorinated modified silane) on the substrate of sample 8, water CA continues to increase up to 162°, advancing/receding CA respectively up to 165°/161°, adhesion work of 3.627 mJ (Table 1). Yet the computation results, based on the composite wetting state, are that water CA change from 160° to 170°, CAH of 3° or so, adhesion work of 1.5 mJ or so (Fig. 6(d2)). Simulation and experiment results largely agree with each other, which prove validity of the study line.

Differences in chemical compositions and micro-morphology result in different surface property

Despite that the microstructure of surface is the key to the superhydrophobic phenomena, chemical compositions (infrared spectrum in Fig. 7(a)-(c)) and micromorphology (SEM in Fig. 7(d)-(f)) also cannot be ignored; different affiliated absorption peak and SEM structure reflect the changes in surface chemical compositions and micromorphology, respectively. Compared with the as-prepared white copper (infrared spectrum showing main absorption peaks at 575.84 cm−1, 1044.31 cm−1, affiliated absorption peak at 780.97 cm−1, 1535.13cm−1, 1623.39cm−1, 1678.16cm−1, Fig. 7(a)), the fluorinated modified silane (infrared spectrum showing main absorption peak at 800.46 cm−1, 1727.25 cm−1, affiliated absorption peak at 566.78 cm−1, 742.75cm−1, 954.73cm−1, 1678.16cm−1, 2959.65 cm−1, Fig. 7(b)), nano SiO2 particles (infrared spectrum showing absorption peak at 802.49 cm−1, 1069.47 cm−1, affiliated absorption peak at 950.82 cm−1, Fig. 7(c)), as low surface energy materials, concurrently play the pivotal role in improving roughness and reducing surface energy of the as-prepared white copper in the composite structure. The SEM of sample 8 (Fig. 8(c)) shows the distribution of nano SiO2 particles on the cylindrical groove (Fig. 8(b)). Similarly, from the as-prepared white copper (69°)→ roughened (64°) →coating silver (86°) →coating nanofluorinated modified silane (136°) →coating silica suspension (162°), the CA increases significantly and the CAH decreases sharply. As surface arithmetic mean roughness changes (Table 1) and different infrared spectrum indicates composition change (Fig. 7(a)-(c)), both surface roughening and low surface energy coatings synchronously affect on the waterproofing of the silver-coated film (Fig. 5(i)-(j)).

As supplement, we selected 95Ag for further verification (sample 19 in Table 1), the method for roughening surface is same to sample 8, and series of property parameters are also presented in Table 1, of which change in the CA from 75°(as-prepared silver), 72° (being roughened), 135° (coated nanofluorinated modified silane), to 154°(coated silica suspension), indicating improvement of the hydrophobicity step by step. Meanwhile, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) also test the existence of fluorinated modified silane and SiO2 (Fig. 8), demonstrating the positive effect of hydrophobic materials and microstructure in superhydrophobicity. Although the base materials are different with white copper (Fig. 8(a) showing characteristic peaks in XPS images, i.e., copper, nickel, fluorine, silicon, carbon, oxygen) and 95Ag (Fig. 8(b) showing characteristic peaks in XPS images, i.e., silver and fluorine, silicon, carbon, oxygen), the coating materials is the same of suspension, thus presenting similarity in micro-morphology of coatings (Fig. 8(c) and (d) of SEM images).

Sample 2, as roughened Miao silver by sandpaper, enjoy water CA of 64°, decreased by 5° from 69° (Sample 1), getting more hydrophilic, which Wenzel Equation reveals. Followed by coating hydrophobic materials, fluorinated modified silane or SiO2-fluorinated modified silane suspension, water CA increases obviously, from hydrophilic to hydrophobic, compositions interacting with microstructure, indicating their synergy. Therefore, difference in microstructure and compositions from sample 1 to 10 as well as, directly results in difference in water CA, thus CAH, solid-liquid interface tension, adhesion work and spreading coefficient. Based on their change rule, we can find main factors, quantifying relationships and related proof.

Method validation

The results obtained include theoretical computation and corresponding experiment test, of which the main property parameters are CA together CAH, roughness and micro-morphology. Validation of preparation and theoretical modeling is indicated as the following.

Firstly, the consistent of preparation with modeling get to be confirmed, implying the reasonability of bio-simulation. From appearance of model, it is totally inclined to like the microstructure of butterfly’s swings. Experimentally, by coarse/fine sand-grinding, signs of grinding in microscale are used to simulate the first-step large semicircle microstructure with diameter of structure unit in 135 μm or so, and the second-step small semicircle microstructure with diameter of structure unit in 15±0.3μm(Fig. 8(c) together inset). Referring to the ratio of micro/nano scale of 100 from lotus leaves, we set this ratio from 50 to 100 and keep the morphology of surface with large microsemicircle encircled by small microsemicircle along the semi-circular path in cross section as substrate, followed by nano SiO2 grain covering it (Fig. 8(c) and (d) flower-shaped or chorionic villous structure). Therefore the morphology of surface model will not change with R/r of ratio; the consistence of model and preparation gets to be verified. Although the preparation, with our simple method, is hard to satisfy the requirement of theoretical modeling, even possibly having obvious difference between them, fortunately, the test results are largely close to computation, taking sample 8, its initial arithmetic mean roughness (one-dimension linearity) from sandpaper-grinding is about 1.868 (Table 1), if transferred to area roughness used for Wenzel equation, 3.49 or so; after coating suspension, such roughness value being over 4 is possible; the obtained CA together CAH of 162°and 4° (165°minus 161°) respectively (Fig. 5(e)); corresponding computation results are: given the roughness between 4.85 to 4.94, CA minimum of 166°, CAH from 1° to 3.5°; having no obvious difference between them, namely, quantitatively fitting. Using software carried by CA measurement instrument (SDC-350) to indirectly measure the adhesion work, the result shows us that, with increase of the CA, the tension of solid-liquid interface also increases with it, implying obvious phase separation; inversely, the wa together spreading coefficient of Sl/s decreases (Table 1), indicating inhibition of wetting. Meanwhile, the computation gives the same result as well (Fig. 6(b2)-(e2)). All in all, both experiment and computation fit with each other, quantitatively or qualitatively, despite that the experiment technique is simple and economic.

Secondly, experiment method designed by us has self speciality, integrating the traditional etching with coating, but further developing, winning the success in fabrication of SHS, especially based on Miao silver. Currently the academic community uniformly thinks that the SHS are typically fabricated in one of two ways: (i) creating roughness on an inherently hydrophobic surface; or (ii) modifying a rough surface with a low surface energy coating [12,13]. Compared with already used chemical etching and coatings technique, i.e., spin/spray/dip/ coating [[33], [34], [35], [36], [37]], our creation mainly presents three aspects (advantage):

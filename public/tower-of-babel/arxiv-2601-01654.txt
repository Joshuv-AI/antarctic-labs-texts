The p p -curvature of quantum connections for Calabi–Yau threefolds Thanks: The first-named author is supported by NSF DMS-2404843.
Shaoyun Bai, Jae Hee Lee

Abstract
. We show that for closed Calabi–Yau manifolds of real dimension 6 6 , the quantum Steenrod operations on divisor classes agree with the p p -curvature of the quantum connection.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of main result

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold satisfying the Calabi–Yau condition
c_{1}(X)=0\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. For such
(X,\omega)
, genus
0
Gromov–Witten invariants constructed from moduli spaces of genus
0
pseudoholomorphic maps with at least three marked points are defined over
\mathbb{Z}

[
RT95
,
MS12
]
, which in turn admits well-defined mod
p
reduction, where
p
is a prime number. Denote by
\Lambda
the Novikov ring associated to
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, whose elements are series

(1.1)

\sum_{A\in\mathrm{im}(\pi_{2}(X)\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})),\ \int_{A}\omega\geq 0}c_{A}q^{A},\quad\quad\quad c_{A}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

such that for any fixed
E\geq 0
, there are only finitely
c_{A}\neq 0
for all
A\in\mathrm{im}(\pi_{2}(X)\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}))
satisfying
\int_{A}\omega\leq E
. Then, given a class
b\in\mathrm{im}(H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}))
, the three-pointed genus
0
Gromov–Witten invariants give rise to an endomorphism known as the (small)
quantum product
with
b
,

(1.2)

\mathbb{M}_{b}:H^{*}(M;\Lambda)\to H^{*}(M;\Lambda)

characterized by that for any class
a_{1},a_{2}\in H^{*}(M;\Lambda)
, we have

(1.3)

(\mathbb{M}_{b}(a_{1}),a_{2})=\sum_{A\in\mathrm{im}(\pi_{2}(X)\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}))}q^{A}\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{0,3,A},

where
\langle-\rangle_{0,3,A}
denotes the mod
p
Gromov–Witten correlator.

There is a natural lift of the operator
\mathbb{M}_{b}
to the
\mu_{p}
-equivariant quantum cohomology

(1.4)

QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}):=H^{*}(X;\Lambda)[\![t,\theta]\!],

where
\mu_{p}
is the cyclic group of order
p
acting trivially on
X
with group cohomology
H^{*}(B\mu_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![t,\theta]\!]
such that
|t|=2
and
|\theta|=1
. To this end, one notes that a class
b\in\mathrm{im}(H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}))
induces an operator

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{b}:\Lambda

\displaystyle\to\Lambda

\displaystyle\sum_{A}c_{A}q^{A}

\displaystyle\to\sum_{A}c_{A}(b\cdot A)q^{A}

where
(b\cdot A)
is the pairing between
H^{2}(X)
and
H_{2}(X)
. Then we can define the
quantum connection

(1.6)

\nabla_{b}:=t\partial_{b}+\mathbb{M}_{b}:QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}),

in which the operators
\partial_{b}
and
\mathbb{M}_{b}
are extended linearly in the variables
t
and
\theta
as the natural lift of the operator
\mathbb{M}_{b}
. This is the characteristic
p
version of the Dubrovin–Givental connection
[
Dub98
,
Giv95
]
, where we also replace the
S^{1}
-equivariant loop parameter by the discretized version arising from the order
p
cyclic subgroup of
S^{1}
. Associativity of the quantum product implies the flatness of quantum connection
\nabla
.

In a field of characteristic
p
, the following fundamental operator known as the
p
-curvature
of
\nabla
along
b
,

(1.7)

\psi_{b}:=\nabla_{b}^{p}-t^{p-1}\nabla_{b}:QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}),

is
\Lambda
-linear (cf.
[
Kat70
, Section 5]
) (perhaps surprisingly, since
\psi_{b}
involves iterative compositions of the connection
\nabla_{b}
). From its definition alone, it seems difficult to prescribe a direct geometric interpretation of the operator
\psi_{b}
.

Initially proposed by Fukaya
[
Fuk97
]
and studied in detail by Seidel–Wilkins
[
Wil20
,
SW22
]
, the quantum
D
-module
QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
carries distinguished endomorphisms, the
quantum Steenrod operations

(1.8)

Q\Sigma_{b}:QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})

which are defined for any
b\in H^{*}(X;\Lambda)
using
\mu_{p}
-equivariant Gromov–Witten type invariants. The operator
Q\Sigma_{b}
is a quantum deformation of the total Steenrod operation, in the sense that
Q\Sigma_{b}|_{q=0}=St_{p}(b)\smile
where
St_{p}(b)
is the
p
-th total Steenrod power applied to
b
. A detailed review of the construction of
Q\Sigma_{b}
is provided in Section
2.4
.

The main theorem of this paper identifies the
p
-curvature of quantum connection and the quantum Steenrod operation for Calabi–Yau threefolds, verifying a conjecture proposed by the second-named author in this setting. On the one hand, it provides an enumerative interpretation of the
p
-curvature of the quantum connection using simply-described moduli spaces; on the other hand, it provides a full computation of the quantum Steenrod operations which is very difficult in general.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a symplectic Calabi–Yau manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X=6
, and
b\in\mathrm{im}(H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}))
a degree
2
cohomology class. Then

(1.9)

Q\Sigma_{b}=\psi_{b},

that is the quantum Steenrod operation for
b
and the
p
-curvature endomorphism for
b
are equal.

The first result of this kind was established for certain equivariant symplectic resolutions in
[
Lee23
]
using extra endomorphisms of the quantum
D
-module provided by the shift operators (see also
[
BL25a
]
for some additional examples along the same lines of ideas). A similar result for closed monotone symplectic manifolds has been announced by Pomerleano–Seidel, whose weaker version, which asserts that the difference
Q\Sigma_{b}-\psi_{b}
is nilpotent, appears in
[
Che24a
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

The only
2
-dimensional Calabi–Yau closed symplectic manifold is the
2
-torus. In this case,
Q\Sigma_{b}=\psi_{b}
holds tautologically due to absence of instanton corrections and degree reasons. A similar observation applies to Calabi–Yau Kähler surfaces, i.e., hyperKähler surfaces. Indeed, there are no non-constant
J
-holomorphic curves for a generic integrable complex structure
J
in the hyperKähler twistor
S^{2}
-family (see, e.g.,
[
SV19
]
). Therefore, for
b\in\mathrm{im}(H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}))
, the quantum Steenrod operation
Q\Sigma_{b}
is reduced to the total Steenrod operation, which is the Artin–Schreier expression
St_{p}(b)=b^{p}-t^{p-1}b
. This agrees with
\psi_{b}
by a straightforward computation. For general symplectic
4
-manifolds, the argument of this paper does not seem to apply.
∎

Remark 1.3
.

The methods in this paper can used to deduce some extensions of Theorem
1.1
to higher dimensions: see Section
4.6
for a brief discussion. We are content with the threefold situation due to the cleanness of the statement.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

Our main theorem broadly falls under the slogan “
p
-curvature = quantum power operation” in the following sense. Gromov–Witten theory admits generalizations where the coefficients can be other complex-oriented generalized cohomology theories. For such generalizations, there are analogues of quantum connections; in particular, for complex
K
-theory, this is known as the quantum
q
-difference connection, cf.
[
GL03
,
Oko17
]
, which admits a
q
-analog of the notion of
p
-curvature. On the other hand, one can define enumerative invariants from the moduli spaces inducing the quantum Steenrod operations for other coefficients, leading to quantum power operations in general. From this point of view, the authors’ earlier work
[
BL25b
]
proves “
p
-curvature = quantum power operation” for complex
K
-theory, though based on moduli spaces of quasimaps instead of stable maps. We refer the reader to
loc.cit.
for a discussion on the relation between the mod
p
cohomological and
K
-theoretic “
p
-curvature = quantum power operation” conjecture, which is relevant for the current result.
∎

1.2.
Contexts for the result

After stating our main theorem precisely, we provide two contexts into which
1.1
fits. One is mirror symmetry in the mod
p
setting, the other is the computation of quantum Steenrod operations.

1.2.1.
Arithmetic aspects of mirror symmetry

In the pioneering work
[
CdlOGP91
]
, Candelas, de la Ossa, Green, and Parkes brought ideas from mirror symmetry into concrete mathematical predictions of enumerative geometry of the quintic
3
-fold. The physics formulation was turned into the rigorous mathematical statement that the generating series of certain
3
-pointed Gromov–Witten invariants of the quintic
3
-fold matches with a particular period integral of the quintic mirror family under the mirror map by Givental
[
Giv96
]
and Lian–Liu–Yau
[
LLY97
]
. These two generating series are closely related to the quantum connection and the Gauss–Manin connection; see
[
CK99
]
for an introduction. In fact, one can derive the aforementioned agreement of generating series from an isomorphism of variations of Hodge structures (cf.
[
Mor93
]
), in which the connections play an explicit role. Using this language, the mathematical study of mirror symmetry has been generalized to large classes of compact Calabi–Yau manifolds.

Even though mirror symmetry is usually concerned with varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, the objects in the formulation have their characteristic
p
counterparts where
p
is a prime number. For us, on the
B
-side, one can define the Gauss–Manin connection for smooth families of varieties over any field
k

[
KO68
]
. When
k
is of characteristic
p
, the Gauss–Manin connection, which is a flat connection over a field of positive characteristic, has its associated
p
-curvature. The
p
-curvature is a fundamental invariant obstructing the existence of formal gauge transformations which trivialize the connection in characteristic
p
. It is the crucial piece appearing in deep conjectures concerning the existence of a full set of algebraic flat sections of a given flat connection with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-coefficients, known as the Grothendieck–Katz
p
-curvature conjecture. In particular, for Gauss–Manin connections, Katz investigated their
p
-curvatures to establish the
p
-curvature conjecture for Picard–Fuchs equations in the seminal work
[
Kat72
]
.

Under mirror symmetry,
1.1
can be viewed as a direct enumerative-geometric understanding of the
p
-curvature of the flat connection associated to
A
-side variation of Hodge structures, defined from the mod
p
reduction of quantum connection. When the Hodge-theoretic mirror symmetry holds over characteristic
p
, upon determining the mirror map,
1.1
leads to explicit descriptions of the
p
-curvature of the Gauss–Manin connection of the
B
-side family in terms of the enumerative geometry of the
A
-side Calabi–Yau manifold. In fact, the computation can be read off from genus
0
BPS states in the setting of
3
-folds, which can be computed from genus
0
Gromov–Witten invariants: see the discussion in the next subsection. One may then study arithmetic information of the
B
-side Gauss–Manin connection based on powerful
A
-side localization computations under the mirror symmetry correspondence.

1.2.2.
Computation of quantum Steenrod operation

The other Gromov–Witten type invariant in
1.1
, quantum Steenrod operation, is notoriously hard to compute, partly due to lack of virtual localization techniques in the mod
p
setting. The most powerful result for computing
Q\Sigma_{b}
for general semipositive symplectic manifolds is the covariant constancy property by Seidel–Wilkins
[
SW22
]
, which asserts that the endomorphism
Q\Sigma_{b}:QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
commutes with the quantum connection. Consequently, for any fixed
p
, one can determine the low degree parts of
Q\Sigma_{b}
from the fundamental solution to the quantum differential equation and the total Steenrod operation. Other than the result we prove, all previous (rarely full) computations of the quantum Steenrod operations were for a very special class of non-compact Calabi–Yau manifolds
[
Lee25
,
Lee23
]
or monotone symplectic manifolds with simple Fukaya categories
[
Che24b
]
.

In view of the previous limitations, in contrast,
1.1
provides a
full
computation of the quantum Steenrod operations for all closed Calabi–Yau threefolds. Furthermore, a byproduct of the proof is that
1.1
clarifies the enumerative content of the quantum Steenrod operations: the information in quantum Steenrod operations that are not
a priori
determined by classical Steenrod operations and ordinary Gromov–Witten theory are exactly the contributions of multiple covers of embedded genus
0
curves. We refer the reader to Remark
3.5
for a more precise discussion.

1.3.
Proof method

Unlike the proof of previous results, which either relies on leveraging extra symmetries of quantum
D
-modules or special algebraic features of the quantum connection in the monotone setting, our proof of
1.1
builds on salient geometric features of curve counting in Calabi–Yau threefolds discovered in Ionel–Parker’s seminal proof of the Gopakumar–Vafa integrality conjecture for Calabi–Yau
3
-folds
[
IP18
]
.

More precisely,
1.1
is inferred using the
cluster formalism
of
[
IP18
]
, the structure theorem about the moduli space of
J
-holomorphic curves in Calabi–Yau manifolds. A cluster is a collection of
J
-holomorphic curves close to a given
embedded

J
-holomorphic curve called the core, and the moduli space of
J
-holomorphic maps admits a decomposition into clusters, which define closed-and-open components of the moduli space thereby providing geometric decompositions of Gromov–Witten generating series according to the images of
J
-holomorphic maps. Via an isotopy argument, the upshot is that the theorem can be reduced to the case of the
elementary clusters
where the neighborhood of a curve is that of a
local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-geometry
, that is to the case where
X
is the Calabi–Yau 3-fold given as the total space of the bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In this setting, the claim can be inferred from the work
[
Lee25
, Section 3]
due to the second-named author; see
3.3
for an exposition. As a new input, we introduce modifications of the cluster formalism which incorporate incidence constraints at marked points on curves and
C^{\infty}
-small inhomogeneous perturbations, necessary to define the structure constants of the operations we consider.

The proof indicates that combined with the structure theorem of
[
IP18
]
, the result implies that the
p
-curvature of the quantum connection is governed by the virtual counts of embedded curves in Calabi–Yau manifolds, which are the Gopakumar–Vafa invariants in the genus
0
setting (the BPS invariants). Under the Gopakumar–Vafa formula, one can compute genus
0
BPS invariants from genus
0
Gromov–Witten invariants.This provides simple algorithms for computing the
p
-curvature of quantum connection from Gromov–Witten invariants.

Remark 1.5
.

Ionel–Parker’s cluster formalism is also used in
[
DIW21
]
to prove the Gopakumar–Vafa finiteness conjecture. It also provides important inspiration to Pardon’s recent important progress on the MNOP conjecture
[
Par25
]
.
∎

Remark 1.6
.

Rezchikov has proposed an alternative approach to
1.1
away from finitely many primes for Calabi–Yau manifolds certain assumptions on its Fukaya category by appealing to deep structures of Fukaya categories and open-closed string maps, in particular the existence of a non-commutative Cartier isomorphism. It would be interesting to compare this approach with our method.
∎

1.4.
Further discussions

1.4.1.
Q\Sigma_{b}=\psi_{b}
for general semi-positive targets

One noteworthy aspect of our proof is that the argument crucially relies on the Calabi–Yau assumption and the setting of threefolds. On the other hand, the announced proof of “
Q\Sigma_{b}=\psi_{b}
” for monotone symplectic manifolds by Pomerleano–Seidel crucially depends on monotonicity. See also Section
4.6
for a discussion on difficulties of generalizations to higher dimensional Calabi–Yau manifolds. To further understand the relation between the
p
-curvature of quantum connection and quantum Steenrod operations for general semi-positive manifolds, further new ideas may be required. Following Remark
1.4
, one possibility is to clarify the relation between
K
-theoretic and cohomological Gromov–Witten invariants so that one can leverage the nice properties of
K
-theoretic counts; see
[
BL25b
, Section 5]
for a proposal along these lines.

1.4.2.
Quantum Steenrod operations from virtual counts

In
[
BX22
]
, the first-named author and Xu defined integral Gromov–Witten type invariants for general closed symplectic manifolds, which lead to the construction of quantum Steenrod operations for general target manifolds in
[
BX26
]
. Furthermore, it is shown in
[
BX26
]
that the definition of
Q\Sigma_{b}
in
loc.cit.
, which relies on the FOP perturbation method in
[
BX22
]
, agrees with the definition in
[
SW22
]
when the target is semi-positive. It would be interesting to investigate “
Q\Sigma_{b}=\psi_{b}
” in this most general setting. Also, an interesting open question is to develop a
\mu_{p}
-localization formalism for the FOP invariants, therefore further clarifying the enumerative meaning of degree
dp
contributions in
Q\Sigma_{b}
.

1.4.3.
Arithmetic properties of quantum connection

1.1
is broadly part of the recent progress in our understanding of the structure of the quantum connection rendered as
arithmetic
objects. In a similar vein, the recent important progress towards the exponential type conjecture for quantum connections of Fano varieties
[
Che24a
]
(see also
[
PS23
]
) relies on mod
p
reductions of the mixed characteristic version of quantum connection. Another related work is
[
BPS25
]
, which studies overconvergent Frobenius structures of the
p
-adic version of quantum
D
-modules. These works constitute an emerging systematic study of arithmetic properties of quantum connection, guided by the expectation that the mod
p
or
p
-adic versions of the quantum connections should carry rich structures that are parallel to the structures that the mod
p
and
p
-adic Gauss–Manin connections carry in the algebraic setting. It would be of great interest to investigate further enumerative interpretations of arithmetic objects and seek implications.

1.5.
Organization of the paper

In Section 2, we recall the definition and properties of the quantum connection, its
p
-curvature, and the quantum Steenrod operations. Our exposition only slightly differs from existing ones in that we use mod
p
pseudocycles to implement the incidence constraints. In Section 3, we prove the claim for the local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, by appealing to the previous computation in this case from
[
Lee25
]
. In Section 4, we reduce the claim to the case of local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by invoking the cluster formalism.

2.
Quantum operations

In this section, we briefly review the construction of the two operators in concern, the quantum Steenrod operations and the
p
-curvature of the quantum connection for a semipositive symplectic manifold
X
. There are several existing expositions of the material here in the literature, including
[
Wil20
,
SW22
,
Lee23
]
, which differ in the ways that the incidence constraints for defining Gromov–Witten type invariants are implemented. In this paper, we use
mod
p
pseudocycles
as the geometric objects representing (Poincaré dual of) classes in
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

2.1.
p
-pseudocycles

Fix a prime
p
. The purpose of this subsection is to introduce the notion of
p
-pseudocycles, defined in
[
Wil
, Appendix A]
. We closely follow the exposition in
[
BPX24
, Appendix A]
, and refer the reader there for details.

p
-pseudocycles are generalizations of the more familiar notion of (integral) pseudocycles, which geometrically represent classes in
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for a smooth oriented manifold
X
. The
p
-pseudocycles allows one to similarly represent classes in
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
which do not necessarily arise from
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The intersection theory of
p
-pseudocycles will be used below and Gromov–Witten invariants valued in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to define operations on quantum cohomology with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coefficients.

Let
X
be a smooth manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth map
f:W\to X
from a
k
-dimensional oriented manifold with boundary
W
is called an (oriented
k
-dimensional)
p
-pseudocycle
if it satisfies the following properties.

(1)

f(W)
is precompact in
X
.

(2)

The dimension of the
\Omega
-set of
f
,

(2.1)

\Omega_{f}:=\bigcap_{K\subseteq W\mathrm{compact}}\overline{f(W\setminus K)},

is at most
k-2
.

(3)

There is a smooth map
g:V\to X
and an oriented
p
-fold covering
\partial W\to V
such that
f|_{\partial W}
is the pullback of
g
by the covering.

Properties (1) and (2) define the usual notion of an (integral) pseudocycle when
\partial W=0

[
MS12
, Section 6.5]
,
[
Zin08
]
. Property (3) allows an existence of codimension 1 boundary as long as it is a
p
-fold cover, and it allows
p
-cycles to represent classes in
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
with non-vanishing Bockstein (i.e. those chains that are cycles in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
because their boundaries are
p
-fold multiples). It is considered vacuous if
\partial W=0
(as an oriented manifold).

Definition 2.2
.

Two (oriented
k
-dimensional)
p
-pseudocycles
f_{0}:W_{0}\to X
and
f_{1}:W_{1}\to X
are
(p-)
cobordant
if there exists a smooth map
\hat{f}:\hat{W}\to X
from a
(k+1)
-dimensional oriented manifold with boundary
\hat{W}
such that

(1)

\hat{f}(\hat{W})
is precompact in
X
.

(2)

\dim\Omega_{\hat{f}}\leq k-1
.

(3)

There is an oriented diffeomorphism
\partial\hat{W}\cong-\mathrm{Int}W_{0}\sqcup\mathrm{Int}W_{1}\sqcup W_{h}
such that
\hat{f}|_{\mathrm{Int}W_{i}}=f_{i}|_{\mathrm{Int}W_{i}}
and
\hat{f}|_{W_{h}}
is a pullback of some map
g:V\to X
by an oriented
p
-fold covering
W_{h}\to V
.

The set of bordism classes of
p
-pseudocycles are denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{k}^{(p)}(X)
. Using disjoint union as addition and orientation reversal as inverses,
\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{H}_{k}^{(p)}(X)
forms a graded abelian group. It is a
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module since
p
times any
p
-pseudocycle is cobordant to the empty set by the trivial cylinder.

If we denote
\mathcal{H}_{*}(X)
the abelian group of (integral) pseudocycle bordisms, there is an abelian group map
\mathcal{H}_{*}(X)\to\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)
by considering a pseudocycle as a
p
-pseudocycle. Recall that Zinger
[
Zin08
]
constructs a natural isomorphism

(2.2)

\Phi_{*}:H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathcal{H}_{*}(X).

Later we use
p
-pseudocycles to implement Gromov–Witten invariants with incidence constraints, hence we need intersection theory of
p
-pseudocycles:

Proposition 2.3
.

There is a well-defined intersection pairing

(2.3)

\cap:\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)\to\mathbb{F}_{p}.

The intersection pairing is the (signed) count of intersection points when
p
-pseudocycles of complementary dimensions meet transversally. The count is only well-defined modulo
p
on bordism classes due to codimension
1
boundary that are
p
-fold covers. For details, we refer the reader to
[
BPX24
, Appendix A]
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
BPX24
, Theorem A.3]
)
.

There is a natural map

(2.4)

\Phi_{*}^{(p)}:H_{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)

satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

The following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}_{*}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{*}^{(p)}}

(2)

If a homology class
A\in H_{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is represented by a smooth cycle
f:W\to X
that is a
p
-pseudocycle, then
\Phi_{*}^{(p)}(a)
is represented by
f
.

(3)

The map
\Phi_{*}^{(p)}
intertwines the Poincaré intersection pairing on
H_{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
with the intersection pairing on
\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)
.

In particular, a
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-homology class naturally gives rise to a (bordism class of)
p
-pseudocycles. For convenience, we introduce the following notation:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a closed oriented manifold. Let
a\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and denote its Poincaré dual homology class by
\mathrm{PD}[a]\in H_{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then

(2.5)

\Phi(a):=\Phi_{*}^{(p)}(\mathrm{PD}[a])\in\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X)

is the (bordism class of) a
p
-pseudocycle representing the cohomology class
a\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

By a slight abuse of notation, we may denote by
\Phi(a)
some
p
-pseudocycle representing the bordism class
\Phi(a)
.

2.2.
Quantum product using
p
-pseudocycles

Fix a connected closed symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
and a compatible almost complex structure
J
. Furthermore impose that the symplectic manifold satisfies the
Calabi–Yau assumption
that
c_{1}(X)=0
. The results of this section can be posed in greater generality (cf.
[
MS12
, Section 6.1]
) by using semipositive targets, but the slightly stronger assumption of (spherical) weak monotonicity is needed to show the regularity of the moduli spaces for quantum Steenrod operations in general. All Calabi–Yau manifolds satisfy such positivity assumptions.

To define quantum cohomology, we fix the Novikov ring. Let
\mathbb{k}
be a commutative ring. We consider symbols of the form
q^{A}
referred to as quantum parameters. The index
A
ranges over classes in
A\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
in the image of the Hurewicz homomorphism
\pi_{2}(X)\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for which
\int_{A}\omega\geq 0
. The Novikov ring is defined to be

(2.6)

\Lambda=\Lambda_{\mathbb{k}}:=\left\{\lambda=\sum_{A}c_{A}q^{A}:c_{A}\in\mathbb{k}\right\}

such that for each bound
E
, the set
\{A:\int_{A}\omega_{X}\leq E,c_{A}\neq 0\}
is finite. In particular, the expression
\sum_{A}c_{A}q^{A}
itself may involve an infinite sum.

We fix a prime
p
and let
\mathbb{k}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The cohomology algebra with Novikov coefficients
H^{*}(X;\Lambda)
admits the (small) quantum product
\star_{q}
using the three-pointed Gromov–Witten invariants and the Poincaré pairing
(\cdot,\cdot):H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\otimes H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
. One can extend the Poincaré pairing to
H^{*}(X;\Lambda)
by first extending
q^{A}
-linearly for a finite truncation
\Lambda_{\leq E}\subseteq\Lambda
given a bound
\int_{A}\omega\leq E
as a map
(\cdot,\cdot):H^{*}(X;\Lambda_{\leq E})^{\otimes 2}\to\Lambda_{\leq 2E}
and taking the limit.

Fix the curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with its fundamental class
[\mathbb{P}^{1}]\in H_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1};\mathbb{Z})
, and consider the three-pointed genus
0
Gromov–Witten moduli space of maps satisfying the
J
-holomorphic curve equation (with source a parametrized copy of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
)

(2.7)

\mathcal{M}_{A}=\mathcal{M}_{0,3}(X,J,A):=\{u:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X:\overline{\partial}_{J}u=0,u_{*}[\mathbb{P}^{1}]=A\}.

For a
C^{\infty}
-generic choice of
J
, it can be arranged that all
simple
maps (those that do not factor through a nontrivial branched covering
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
) are transversely cut out. Hence, the moduli space of simple maps is a manifold of dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{A}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X+2c_{1}(A)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X
, which is equipped with a natural orientation as the linearized
\overline{\partial}_{J}
-operator can be deformed to be
\mathbb{C}
-linear.

The moduli space admits the stable map compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}
, and since the source curve of the maps
u:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
is parametrized, there are evaluation maps

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}=\mathrm{ev}_{0}\times\mathrm{ev}_{\infty}\times\mathrm{ev}_{1}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}

\displaystyle\to X\times X\times X

(2.9)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto(u(0),u(\infty),u(1)).

It is standard that, under our Calabi–Yau assumption, the evaluation map
\mathrm{ev}
restricted to the simple maps in
\mathcal{M}_{A}
form an integral pseudocycle in
X\times X\times X
, whose bordism class is denoted by

(2.10)

\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}]\in\mathcal{H}_{*}(X\times X\times X)\cong H_{*}(X\times X\times X;\mathbb{Z}).

We may consider this integral pseudocycle as a
p
-pseudocycle in
X\times X\times X
. Its (
p
-)bordism class is independent of the choice of
J
by a standard cobordism argument.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},b\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. The
quantum product correlator
is the
3
-pointed Gromov-Witten invariant

(2.11)

\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{A}:=\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}]\cap\Phi(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes b)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

where
\cap
denotes the intersection pairing of
p
-pseudocycles in
X\times X\times X
.

One can easily extend the correlator
q^{A}
-multi-linearly to insert classes from
H^{*}(X;\Lambda)
. We define the quantum product by using the quantum product correlators as structure constants:

Definition 2.7
.

On
H^{*}(X;\Lambda)
, the (small) quantum product
\star:=\star_{q}:H^{*}(X;\Lambda)\otimes H^{*}(X;\Lambda)\to H^{*}(X;\Lambda)
is the bilinear operation defined by

(2.12)

\left(b\star_{q}a_{1},a_{2}\right)=\sum_{A}\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{A}q^{A}\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Poincaré pairing. It is well-defined (in the sense that the right hand side is genuinely an element of
\Lambda
) by Gromov compactness. The operation
\star
defines a graded-commutative and associative product on
H^{*}(X;\Lambda)

[
MS12
, Chapter 9]
. We denote this ring as the
quantum cohomology
,

(2.13)

QH^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}):=\left(H^{*}(X;\Lambda),\star_{q}\right).

It is useful to consider the quantum cohomology not just as a ring, but as a collection of
\Lambda
-linear operators indexed by cohomology classes
b\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
:

(2.14)

\mathbb{M}_{b}:=b\ \star_{q}\in\mathrm{End}(H^{*}(X;\Lambda)).

For later use, we describe the following alternative characterization of this operator via correspondences. Let
b\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a cohomology class represented by a
p
-pseudocycle
\Phi(b):B\to X
. Take the fiber product

(2.15)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,J,A)_{b}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,J,A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ev}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi(b)}

and consider the evaluation map

(2.16)

\mathrm{ev}_{0}\times\mathrm{ev}_{\infty}:{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,J,A)_{b}}\to X\times X

whose restriction to the simple locus defines an integral pseudocycle if the
p
-pseudocycle representative for
b
is chosen generically, hence a homology class in
H_{*}(X\times X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathcal{H}^{(p)}_{*}(X\times X)
. Denote this homology class by
\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A,b}]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

The operator
\mathbb{M}_{b}
is induced by the correspondence
\mathrm{ev}_{0}\times\mathrm{ev}_{\infty}:{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,J,A)_{b}}\to X\times X
.

Proof.

Denote the two projections from
X\times X
by
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:X\times X\to X
, and similarly let
\pi_{12}:X\times X\times X\to X\times X
to be the projection to first two factors. It suffices to show that

(2.17)

(\pi_{2})_{*}\left(\pi_{1}^{*}a_{1}\cap\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A,b}]\right)\cap a_{2}=\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{A}.

The desired result follows from

(2.18)

\displaystyle(\pi_{2})_{*}\left(\pi_{1}^{*}a_{1}\cap\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A,b}]\right)\cap a_{2}

\displaystyle=(\pi_{2})_{*}\left(\pi_{1}^{*}a_{1}\cap\pi_{2}^{*}a_{2}\cap\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A,b}]\right)

(2.19)

\displaystyle=(\pi_{2})_{*}\left((a_{1}\otimes a_{2})\cap\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A,b}]\right)

(2.20)

\displaystyle=(\pi_{2})_{*}\left((\pi_{12})_{*}(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes b\cap\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}_{A}}])\right)

(2.21)

\displaystyle=(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{12})_{*}\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{A}

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{A}.

∎

2.3.
Quantum connection and
p
-curvature

The
quantum connection
or Dubrovin–Givental connection
[
Dub98
,
Giv95
,
Sei18
]
is the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant lift of the operators
\mathbb{M}_{b}
. In this paper, we consider the subgroup
\mu_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{m}
, the group of complex
p
-th roots of unity, in our constructions.

Lemma 2.9
.

The group cohomology of
\mu_{p}
with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coefficients

(2.23)

H^{*}(B\mu_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![t,\theta]\!]

is a graded polynomial algebra on two formal variables with degrees
|t|=2
and
|\theta|=1
. If
p=2
, these generators are subject to the relation
t=\theta^{2}
, and if
p>2
,
t\theta=\theta t
and
\theta^{2}=0
.

Remark 2.10
.

The notation
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![t,\theta]\!]
denotes
graded
completion, and as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra the ring is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![t,\theta]\!]\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[t,\theta]
(the direct sum of completed gradeds).
∎

Definition 2.11
.

The
\mu_{p}
-equivariant quantum cohomology is the graded
\Lambda
-module defined as

(2.24)

QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}):=H^{*}(X;\Lambda)[\![t,\theta]\!]

where
|t|=2
and
|\theta|=1
.

Here, we remark that there is no
\mu_{p}
-action on the target symplectic manifold
X
; the action of
\mu_{p}
should be considered as a
discretized loop rotation
on the source curve
C
for
J
-holomorphic maps
u:C\to X
.

There is no natural ring structure on
QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
(the naive
t,\theta
-linear extension of the quantum product is not geometrically meaningful). However, it still carries an action of certain connection-type operators.

Definition 2.12
.

Fix
b\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. The
Novikov derivative
is the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear derivation

(2.25)

\displaystyle\partial_{b}:\Lambda

\displaystyle\to\Lambda

(2.26)

\displaystyle\sum c_{A}q^{A}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum c_{A}(b\cdot A)q^{A}

where
(b\cdot A)
is the pairing between
b\in H^{2}(X)
and
A\in H_{2}(X)
.

The Novikov derivative behaves like a logarithmic derivative, and can be extended
t,\theta
-linearly to
\Lambda[\![t,\theta]\!]
.

Definition 2.13
.

The
quantum connection
is the collection of operators
\nabla_{b}:QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
indexed by
b\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
defined as

(2.27)

\nabla_{b}:=t\partial_{b}+\mathbb{M}_{b}

where
\mathbb{M}_{b}
is the quantum product by
b
considered as a
\Lambda
-linear endomorphism of
QH^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
(extended
t,\theta
-linearly).

The quantum connection is reminiscent of
\lambda
-connections
of Deligne from nonabelian Hodge theory (see e.g.
[
Sim97
]
), where the role of
\lambda
is played by the equivariant parameter
t
in our construction. In particular, the quantum connection lifts the quantum product to
\mu_{p}
-equivariant cohomology in the sense that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QH^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QH^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla_{b}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{M}_{b}}

where the vertical maps specialize
t=\theta=0
, forgetting the
\mu_{p}
-equivariance.

In characteristic
p
, the
p
-th iterate
\partial_{b}^{p}
is again a derivation (it acts on
q^{A}\in\Lambda
the same way as
\partial_{b}
). Therefore it is natural to ask whether or not the association
\partial_{b}\mapsto\nabla_{b}
preserves such
p
-th power operations.

Definition 2.14
.

The
p
-curvature
of the quantum connection is the endomorphism of
QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
defined by

(2.28)

\psi_{b}:=\nabla_{b}^{p}-t^{p-1}\nabla_{b}.

A remarkable fact is that the
p
-curvature is a
\Lambda
-linear
endomorphism of
QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
even though
\nabla_{b}
is certainly not as it satisfies the Leibniz rule. The general proof of linearity can be found in
[
Kat70
, Section 5]
.

2.4.
Quantum Steenrod operations

In this subsection we define the quantum Steenrod operations using
p
-pseudocycles to implement the incidence constraints. Due to technical difficulties with strata with
\mathbb{Z}/p
-isotropy in the moduli space of
J
-holomorphic maps, the moduli spaces are defined using inhomogeneous perturbations. Other expositions of the construction can be found in
[
SW22
,
Lee25
,
Lee23
]
.

2.4.1.
Model of equivariant cohomology

Constructions of quantum Steenrod operations involve the rotational symmetry group
\mu_{p}
of the source curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The model of the classifying space
B\mu_{p}
is as follows. We take
E\mu_{p}=S^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
as the unit sphere on which
\mu_{p}
acts by simultaneous multiplication
\sigma
of all complex coordinates. We use the cell decomposition of
S^{\infty}

[
SW22
, Section 2a]
with exactly
p
many
i
-cells
\Delta^{i},\sigma\Delta^{i},\dots,\sigma^{p-1}\Delta^{i}
for each dimension
i\geq 0
, forming a free
\mu_{p}
-orbit. The
i
-cell
\Delta^{i}
has a natural compactification to
\overline{\Delta}_{i}
as a manifold with stratified boundary, whose top boundary strata are given by

(2.29)

\displaystyle\partial\overline{\Delta}_{i}=\begin{cases}\bigcup_{j=0}^{p-1}\sigma^{j}\Delta^{i-1}&i\mbox{ even}\\
\sigma\Delta_{i-1}-\Delta_{i-1}&i\mbox{ odd}\end{cases}.

The images of
\Delta^{i}
under the quotient
E\mu_{p}\to B\mu_{p}=E\mu_{p}/\mu_{p}
induces a CW-structure on
B\mu_{p}
with unique
i
-cell for every
i\geq 0
. With
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coefficients, the quotient images of
\overline{\Delta}^{i}
become cycles and they additively generate
H_{*}(B\mu_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

The additive generators
\theta\in H^{1}(B\mu_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
t\in H^{2}(B\mu_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})
from
Lemma

2.9
correspond to those classes satisfying

(2.30)

\langle\theta,\Delta^{1}\rangle=1,\quad\langle t,\Delta^{2}\rangle=-1.

We denote the preferred additive generator of
H^{*}(B\mu_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})
in degree
i
with the following notation:

(2.31)

(t,\theta)^{i}=\begin{cases}t^{i/2}&i\mbox{ even }\\
t^{(i-1)/2}\theta&i\mbox{ odd }\end{cases}.

2.4.2.
Parametrized moduli spaces

For the target symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
, fix a compatible almost complex structure
J
. An equivariant inhomogeneous term is a choice of a
J
-complex antilinear bundle homomorphism

(2.32)

\nu^{eq}\in C^{\infty}(E\mu_{p}\times_{\mu_{p}}\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X;\Omega^{0,1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\boxtimes TX).

where
\mu_{p}
acts on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by rotation action (denoted
\sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
). Equivalently,
\nu^{eq}
can be considered as a collection of inhomogeneous terms
\nu^{eq}_{w}
(in the usual sense) parametrized by
w\in E\mu_{p}
satisfying the equivariance condition

(2.33)

\nu_{\sigma_{E}(w),z,x}=\nu_{w,\sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(z),x}\circ D\sigma_{z}:T\mathbb{P}^{1}_{z}\to TX_{x}.

Assume that
\nu^{eq}
is supported away from the neighborhoods of distinguished marked points
z_{0}=0,z_{j}=\zeta^{j},z_{\infty}=\infty
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}^{eq}_{A}
the moduli space of solutions to the parametrized problem

(2.34)

\mathcal{M}^{eq}_{A}=\mathcal{M}_{0,p+2}^{eq}(X,J,\nu,A):=\left\{(u:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X,w\in E\mu_{p}):u_{*}[\mathbb{P}^{1}]=A,\ (\overline{\partial}_{J}u)_{z}=\nu^{eq}_{w,z,u(z)}\right\}.

The moduli space admits the stable map compactification (fiberwise under the projection
\mathcal{M}^{eq}_{A}\to E\mu_{p}
), and carries an evaluation map

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}^{eq}:\

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{eq}_{A}\to X\times X\times(E\mu_{p}\times X^{p})

(2.36)

\displaystyle(u,w)\mapsto(u(0);u(\infty);(w,u(z_{1}),\dots,u(z_{p})))

which is
\mu_{p}
-equivariant with respect to the
\mu_{p}
-action is given by
(u,w)\mapsto(\sigma_{E}(w),u\circ\sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
on
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{eq}
and cyclic permutation on
E\mu_{p}\times X^{p}
. Provided the boundedness of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{eq}_{A}
, by Gromov compactness the evaluation map is covered by
E\mu_{p}
-parametrized versions of simple stable maps
[
MS12
, Chapter 6]
.

2.4.3.
Quantum Steenrod operators

Fix
i\geq 0
and denote by
\overline{\Delta}_{i}\subseteq E\mu_{p}
the natural compactification of
\Delta_{i}
as a manifold with boundary. By a slight abuse of notation, we also denote the inclusion of the quotient image of
\overline{\Delta}_{i}
under
E\mu_{p}\to B\mu_{p}
into
B\mu_{p}
as
\overline{\Delta}_{i}\hookrightarrow B\mu_{p}
(this is a closed manifold if
i
is odd). Consider the moduli space obtained from the fiber product,

(2.37)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}^{eq,i}:=\begin{cases}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}^{eq}\times_{E\mu_{p}}\overline{\Delta}^{i}&i\mbox{ even}\\
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}^{eq}/\mu_{p}\times_{B\mu_{p}}\overline{\Delta}^{i}&i\mbox{ odd}\end{cases}

which carries an evaluation map

(2.38)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}^{eq,i}:\

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{eq,i}_{A}\to X\times X\times X^{p}

(2.39)

\displaystyle(u,w)\mapsto(u(0);u(\infty);(u(z_{1}),\dots,u(z_{p}))).

Lemma 2.15
.

For a generic choice of
J
and
\nu^{eq}
, the map
\mathrm{ev}^{eq,i}
restricted to the open locus
\mathcal{M}^{eq,i}_{A}\to X\times X\times X^{p}
defines a
p
-pseudocycle of dimension
i+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X+2c_{1}(A)=i+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X
.

Proof.

For
i
odd, the open locus fibers over the closed submanifold
\overline{\Delta}_{i}\subseteq B\mu_{p}
. The Omega-limit set of the evaluation map consists of the image of (
B\mu_{p}
-parametrized versions of) the simple stable maps, which is codimension at least
2
. Therefore, the evaluation map defines an integral pseudocycle. For
i
even,
\overline{\Delta}_{i}
is a manifold with boundary whose codimension
1
boundary
\partial\overline{\Delta}_{i}=\mu_{p}\cdot\left(\bigcup_{k<i}\Delta^{i}\right)
is given as a free
\mu_{p}
-orbit, and therefore admits a
p
-fold covering
\partial\overline{\Delta}_{i}\to\partial\overline{\Delta}_{i}/\mu_{p}
. The Omega-limit set of the evaluation map still consists of the image of (
E\mu_{p}
-parametrized versions of) simple stable maps, which is of codimension at least
2
.
∎

We denote the corresponding
p
-pseudocycle as
\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{eq,i}_{A}]
, which is independent of the choice of
J
and
\nu^{eq}
up to bordism by standard cobordism arguments.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},b\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. The
i
-th quantum Steenrod correlator
is the number

(2.40)

\langle a_{1},a_{2},b^{\otimes p}\rangle_{A}^{eq,i}:=\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{A}^{eq,i}]\cap\Phi(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes b^{\otimes p})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

where
\cap
denotes the intersection pairing of
p
-pseudocycles in
X\times X\times X^{p}
.

The equivariant inhomogeneous term
\nu^{eq}
can be (
C^{\infty}
-)generically chosen so that the intersection with the
p
-pseudocycle representatives of
\Phi(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes b^{\otimes p})\in\mathcal{H}_{*}^{(p)}(X\times X\times X^{p})
is transverse. Equivalently, the transversality requirement can be achieved by choosing generic
p
-pseudocycle representatives.

Definition 2.17
.

Fix
b\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. On
QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
, the
quantum Steenrod operator

Q\Sigma_{b}:QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH_{\mu_{p}}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
for
b
is the
\Lambda[\![t,\theta]\!]
-linear operator defined by the structure constants

(2.41)

(Q\Sigma_{b}(a_{1}),a_{2})=\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{A}(-1)^{\dagger}\langle a_{1},a_{2},b^{\otimes p}\rangle_{A}^{eq,i}q^{A}(t,\theta)^{i}\in\Lambda[\![t,\theta]\!]

for signs

(2.42)

\dagger=\begin{cases}|b||a_{1}|&i\mbox{ even}\\
|b||a_{1}|+|b|+|a_{1}|&i\mbox{ odd}\end{cases}

and the Poincaré pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Remark 2.18
.

The signs
(-1)^{\dagger}
are largely invisible, since in this paper we will focus on equator insertions
b\in H^{2}(X)
of even degree, and the series rarely has a nonzero term for
i
odd, cf.
4.22
.
∎

Remark 2.19
.

(Simplification in the Calabi–Yau setting). Fix a class
b\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Note that

(2.43)

Q\Sigma_{b}:QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})

is a degree
p|b|=2p
endomorphism of graded modules. Hence, the highest degree of the equivariant parameters
(t,\theta)^{i}
that can appear in a structure constant
(Q\Sigma_{b}(a_{1}),a_{2})\in\Lambda[\![t,\theta]\!]
is bounded above by

(2.44)

p|b|+|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X-2c_{1}(A)\leq 2p+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X.

In particular, to define the quantum Steenrod operator
Q\Sigma_{b}
it suffices to consider a finite-dimensional approximation of
E\mu_{p}=S^{\infty}
, for large
E\mu_{p}^{\mathrm{fin}}:=S^{2N+1}\subseteq S^{\infty}
and
\mu_{p}
-equivariant inhomogeneous term parametrized over
E\mu_{p}^{\mathrm{fin}}
. In particular, the inhomogeneous term
\nu^{eq}
can be chosen to be parametrized over a
compact
manifold.
∎

3.
Local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
geometry

In this section, we review the geometry of a local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in a Calabi–Yau 3-fold and verify the “local version” of the main theorem. Using the cluster formalism, the verification for this local situation will be used to deduce the claim for general Calabi–Yau 3-folds.

3.1.
Local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and its small quantum connection

We consider the geometry of a local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which is the noncompact Calabi–Yau
3
-fold

(3.1)

Y=\mathrm{Tot}\left(\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\right),

the total space of the rank 2 vector bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
, together with its integrable complex structure and the Kähler form given by its description as a small resolution of the quadric cone
\{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{4}
. In particular, the zero section
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a Kähler submanifold of
Y
.

By maximum principle, all non-constant
J
-holomorphic spheres in
Y
must map into the zero section
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Hence, as a set, the moduli space of
J
-holomorphic spheres into
Y
is isomorphic to the moduli space of
J
-holomorphic spheres into
\mathbb{P}^{1}
; however, their obstruction theories differ (the former carries an obstruction bundle arising from the normal directions, see
[
Voi96
,
Lee25
]
.)

3.1.1.
Quantum operations on non-compact targets

Since
Y
is non-compact, the discussion of the quantum product and the quantum Steenrod operations as Novikov-linear endomorphisms of
H^{*}(Y):=H^{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z})/\mathrm{Torsion}
requires a modification. We illustrate the basic issue in the case of the quantum product. Recall that the (small) quantum product is defined using
J
-holomorphic genus
0
spheres with
3
-marked points together with its evaluation maps,

(3.2)

\mathrm{ev}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(Y;J)\to Y\times Y\times Y

which defines a pseudocycle
\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3,A}]\in H_{*}(Y^{3};\mathbb{Z})
assuming sufficient positivity of the target
Y
. The corresponding 3-pointed Gromov–Witten invariants

(3.3)

\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{0,3,A}=\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3,A}]\cap(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes b)\in H_{0}(Y^{3};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}

for cohomology classes
a_{1},a_{2},b\in H^{*}(Y)
of degree
|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|+|b|=\mathrm{vdim}_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3,A}
are then used to define the structure constants of the small quantum product as

(3.4)

b\star_{q}a_{1}=\sum_{A\in H_{2}(Y;\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{a_{2}}\langle a_{1},a_{2},b\rangle_{0,3,A}\ q^{A}\ a_{2}^{\vee}

where the sum
\sum_{a_{2}}
ranges over a chosen basis
\{a_{2}\}
of
H^{*}(Y)
. Here
a_{2}^{\vee}
is the dual cohomology class under the unimodular Poincaré pairing
(\cdot,\cdot):H^{*}(Y)\otimes H^{*}(Y)\to\mathbb{Z}
, inducing the dualizing map
a_{2}^{\vee}\in H^{*}(Y)^{\vee}\cong H^{*}(Y)
. In other words, the operation
b\star_{q}-
is most naturally defined as a map
H^{*}(Y)\to H^{*}(Y)^{\vee}
, and we post-compose with the linear isomorphism
H^{*}(Y)^{\vee}\cong H^{*}(Y)
induced by the Poincaré pairing to consider the quantum product as an endomorphism of
H^{*}(Y)
.

In the case of non-compact target
Y
, we modify this using the compactly supported cohomology
H^{*}(Y)^{\vee}\cong H^{*}_{c}(Y)
, and the (purely quantum part of) an operation defined by Gromov–Witten theory is naturally considered as a map
H^{*}(Y)\to H^{*}_{c}(Y)
.

3.1.2.
Quantum connection of local
\mathbb{P}^{1}

In light of the discussion above, we consider both the cohomology and the compactly supported cohomology of local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
Y=\mathrm{Tot}\left(\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\right).

The cohomology of

is rank
2
; indeed,
H^{*}(Y)\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
by the deformation retract to the zero section. It is generated by two classes

and
b=[F]\in H^{2}(Y)
, Poincaré dual to the fundamental class (codimension
0
) and the fiber of

(codimension
2
) respectively.

The compactly supported cohomology of

is again rank
2
; by integration along fibers,
H^{*-4}_{c}(Y)\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. It is generated by two classes
C=[\mathbb{P}^{1}]\in H^{4}_{c}(Y)
and
P=[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{6}_{c}(Y)
, Poincaré dual to the cycle of the zero section (codimension
4
) and a point in
Y
(codimension
6
) respectively.

The degrees of
J
-holomorphic spheres in
Y
are indexed by
H_{2}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. The expected dimension of the corresponding moduli space of genus
0
,
3
-pointed
J
-holomorphic spheres of degree
A
is

(3.5)

\mathrm{vdim}_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3,A}(Y;J)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}Y+2c_{1}(A)=6

as
c_{1}=0
. Hence, Gromov–Witten invariants are only non-trivial for three insertions given by
H^{2}
-classes. The computation of the corresponding quantum product is due to Voisin:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Voi96
]
)
.

Let
Y
be the local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
Calabi–Yau
3
-fold and choose the basis
\langle 1,b,C,P\rangle\in H^{*}(Y)\oplus H^{*}_{c}(Y)
. The matrix of the quantum product by
b\in H^{2}(Y)
, the Poincaré dual of the fiber class, is given in this basis by

(3.6)

b\ \star_{q}-=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0\\
0&\frac{q}{1-q}&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\end{pmatrix}:H^{*}(Y)\oplus H^{*}_{c}(Y)\to H^{*}(Y)\oplus H^{*}_{c}(X).

Proof.

The quantum product by
b\in H^{2}(Y)
is an operation which raises cohomological degree by
|b|=2
, where the degree is well-defined as a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading as
c_{1}(Y)=0
. As our basis elements are chosen to be in degrees
0,2,4,6
, the corresponding matrix can only have non-zero entries on the leading subdiagonal. The classical terms (
q=0
) follow from the fact that (i) the fiber and the whole space intersects along the fiber, and (ii) the fiber and the zero section intersects at a single point. The quantum terms follow from the fundamental computation of Voisin
[
Voi96
, Theorem 1.1]
that
\langle b,b,b\rangle_{0,3,A}=1
for all
A=k[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
,
k\geq 1
, leading to the sum

(3.7)

\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle b,b,b\rangle_{0,3,k[\mathbb{P}^{1}]}\ q^{k}=q+q^{2}+q^{3}+\cdots=\frac{q}{1-q}.

Note that this computation is equivalent to the Aspinwall–Morrison formula
\langle\rangle_{0,0,k[\mathbb{P}^{1}]}=\frac{1}{k^{3}}
by the divisor axiom.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The matrix
B=b\ \star_{q}
of the quantum product by the fiber class is nilpotent,
B^{4}=0
.
More generally, any word of length
\geq 4
in the matrix
B
and its derivatives
B^{(j)}:=(q\partial_{q})^{j}B
must be zero.

The above corollary is already immediate by noting that
B
is an endomorphism of cohomological degree
2
acting on a vector space with bounded degrees.

3.2.
The local computation

Here, we revisit the computation in
[
Lee25
, Section 4]
for the quantum Steenrod operation of
Y
and verify that it is equivalent to our key local computation that the quantum Steenrod operation is equal to the
p
-curvature of the quantum connection.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Lee25
, Corollary 4.16]
)
.

Let
Y=\mathrm{Tot}(\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\to\mathbb{P}^{1})
, and consider a divisor class
b\in H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{F}_{p}).
Then

(3.8)

Q\Sigma_{b}=\psi_{b}\in\mathrm{End}(H^{*}(Y)\oplus H^{*}_{c}(Y)).

Here, we consider the (
t
-)connection given by
\nabla_{b}=tq\partial_{q}+B
and its
p
-curvature

(3.9)

\psi_{b}:=\nabla_{b}^{p}-t^{p-1}\nabla_{b}.

Recall that
t
is a degree
2
variable so that
\nabla_{b}
is a derivation of
H^{*}(Y;\Lambda)
of degree
2
. Denote by
B^{(j)}:=(q\partial_{q})^{j}B
the (logarithmic) derivatives of the matrix
B
.

First we compute the leading term of the
p
-curvature, which is a rather general statement.

Lemma 3.4
.

The
p
-curvature
\psi_{b}
is a linear endomorphism of degree
2p
. In its expansion as a polynomial in
t
, we have the leading term

(3.10)

\psi_{b}=t^{p-1}(B^{(p-1)}-B)+\left(\mbox{terms of lower order in }t\right).

Proof.

The linearity of
p
-curvature (despite its expression in terms of
\nabla_{b}
which is not linear in the Novikov variables) is standard, as can be found in e.g.
[
Kat70
, Section 5]
. From the definition
\psi_{b}:=\nabla_{b}^{p}-t^{p-1}\nabla_{b}
and the fact that
\nabla_{b}
is of degree
2
, it is clear that the
p
-curvature is of degree
2p
, and hence can be expressed as a matrix-coefficient polynomial in
t
of degree bounded by
p
:

(3.11)

\psi_{b}=t^{p}C_{0}+t^{p-1}C_{1}+\left(\mbox{terms of lower order in }t\right).

Again from definition, it is clear that
C_{0}=0
. It remains to show that
C_{1}=B^{(p-1)}-B
. It suffices to show that the
t^{p-1}
-term in the expansion of
\nabla_{b}^{p}=(tq\partial_{q}+B)^{p}
is equal to
B^{(p-1)}
. The
t^{p-1}
-term in the expansion is in turn given by

(3.12)

t^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(q\partial_{q})^{p-k}B(q\partial_{q})^{k-1}.

The Leibniz rule
[q\partial_{q},B^{(k)}]=B^{(k+1)}
implies that each term in the sum can be written as

(3.13)

(q\partial_{q})^{p-k}B(q\partial_{q})^{k-1}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-k}\binom{p-k}{i}B^{(i)}(q\partial_{q})^{p-k-i}\right)(q\partial_{q})^{k-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-k}\binom{p-k}{i}B^{(i)}(q\partial_{q})^{p-1-i}.

Now consider

(3.14)

\displaystyle C_{1}+B

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(q\partial_{q})^{p-k}B(q\partial_{q})^{k-1}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-k}\binom{p-k}{i}B^{(i)}(q\partial_{q})^{p-1-i}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p-i}\binom{p-k}{i}B^{(i)}(q\partial_{q})^{p-1-i}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{i+1}B^{(i)}(q\partial_{q})^{p-1-i}

(3.18)

\displaystyle=B^{(p-1)}.

This is the desired result. The fourth equality follows from
\sum_{k=1}^{p-i}\binom{p-k}{i}=\binom{p}{i+1}
(Pascal’s triangle).
∎

We may now give the proof of
3.3
, which is a re-interpretation of
[
Lee25
, Corollary 4.16]
.

Proof of
3.3
.

First consider the case where
b=[F]\in H^{2}(Y)
is the class Poincaré dual to the fiber of
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
(the generator of
H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{1})
). We consider the quantum connection

(3.19)

\nabla_{b}:=tq\partial_{q}+B=tq\partial_{q}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0\\
0&\frac{q}{1-q}&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\end{pmatrix}

in the direction of the fiber class
b\in H^{2}(Y)
, and the corresponding
p
-curvature

(3.20)

\psi_{b}:=\nabla_{b}^{p}-t^{p-1}\nabla_{b}.

By
Lemma

3.4
, the leading term of the
p
is given by

(3.21)

\psi_{b}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}t^{p-k}C_{k}=t^{p-1}\left(B^{(p-1)}-B\right)+t^{p-2}C_{2}+t^{p-3}C_{3}+\cdots.

From
3.2
, we know that
C_{j}=0
for all
j\geq 4
. From covariant constancy
[\nabla_{b},\psi_{b}]=0
, it follows that

(3.22)

(tq\partial_{q})\psi_{b}=t^{p}C_{1}^{(1)}+t^{p-1}C_{2}^{(1)}+t^{p-2}C_{3}^{(1)}

is equal to

(3.23)

[\psi_{b},B]=t^{p-1}[C_{1},B]+t^{p-2}[C_{2},B]+t^{p-3}[C_{3},B].

This yields a system of equations which determine
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
by comparing the coefficients of the
t
-terms. The end result of the computation is that

(3.24)

\psi_{b}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
-t^{p-1}&0&0&0\\
t^{p-2}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}d^{p-2}q^{d}&-t^{p-1}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}q^{pd}&0&0\\
-t^{p-3}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}2d^{p-3}q^{d}&-t^{p-2}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}d^{p-2}q^{d}&-t^{p-1}&0\end{pmatrix}

Here, to simplify

, we used the fact that
d^{p-1}\equiv 1
mod
p
for all
d\not\equiv 0
. Moreover, note that all entries are in given in terms of iterated logarithmic derivatives of the function
f(q)=\frac{q}{1-q}
. In particular, they are all rational functions.

To conclude the proof, we simply remark that this is exactly the same result as the computation of
Q\Sigma_{b}
from
[
Lee25
, Corollary 4.16]
(in
loc. cit.
the convention uses
-t
in place of our
t
, leading to the difference in signs for
t^{p-2}
term).

For more general classes of the form
b=k[F]\in H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{F}_{p})
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the claim follows from the special case above because both operators
Q\Sigma_{b}
and
\psi_{b}
are Frobenius-linear in the entry
b
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The computation shows that there are terms in

counting Gromov–Witten invariants in degree
p\cdot d[\mathbb{P}^{1}]\in H_{2}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
; they arise in the

-term of

in the form

. The above proof that

“explains” the source of these terms, as they arise as the leading term in the

-fold multiple covers
of the underlying to the simple curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\subseteq Y
mapping to the zero section of

. Hence, we arrive at the enumerative interpretation that, for Calabi–Yau 3-folds, the (

-terms of) quantum Steenrod operations count the multiple covers of the embedded
J
-holomorphic curves. It could be interesting to compare this result with the FOP counts from the strata with
\mathbb{Z}/p
-isotropy.
∎

4.

In this section, we prove

]
when

is a Calabi–Yau 3-fold of real dimension

•

Fix an energy bound

of the moduli space of genus

-holomorphic curves in

•

The structure constants of the quantum Steenrod and the

•

For

•

By the cluster isotopy theorem, adapted to our situation, the theorem is reduced to the case of elementary clusters
up to

For the discussions prior the proof of Theorem

, we will be focused on the computation and structural properties of the enumerative invariants from

4.1.
Cluster decomposition of the moduli space

We first review the cluster formalism to describe the structure of the moduli space of genus

Definition 4.1

Fix a real number

4.1.1.

Lemma 4.6

-holomorphic map

-holomorphic section of

-holomorphic maps. Let

-small

Since

4.3.
Contributions from elementary clusters

In the previous section, we showed that both the
p
-curvature endomorphism
\psi_{b}
and the quantum Steenrod operator
Q\Sigma_{b}
(more precisely,
E
-truncations thereof) admit a cluster decomposition. Here we show that the cluster components of the structure constants agree for a particular type of a cluster that can be identified with the local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
geometry.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Calabi–Yau manifold of real dimension
6
.

Definition 4.14

(
[
IP18
, Definition 3.1]
,
[
DIW21
, Definition 3.42]
)
.

A cluster
(\mathcal{U},J,C)
is called
elementary
if

(1)

The core curve
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
with its
J
-holomorphic embedding
u:C\to X
has a normal bundle
u^{*}TX/TC=:N_{C}\cong\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)
as almost complex manifolds. In particular, the linearization of
u:C\to X
uniquely descends to a
normal operator

D_{N}:\Gamma(NC)\to\Gamma(\Omega^{0,1}_{C}\otimes NC)
.

(2)

The only non-trivial
J
-holomorphic maps into an
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
C
are multiple covers of the embedding
C\hookrightarrow X
.

(3)

For any such multiple cover
\rho:\widetilde{C}\to C
, the pullback
\rho^{*}D_{N}
is injective.

The normal operator
D_{N}:\Gamma(NC)\to\Gamma(\Omega^{0,1}_{C}\otimes NC)
is the restriction of the linearization
D_{u}
to the normal directions, see
[
IP18
, (4.15)]
or
[
Lee25
, Section 3.2]
. Property (3) is called the
super-rigidity
of the core curve
C
; it means that there are no (first-order) normal perturbations of the curve, even for any of its multiple covers. It moreover implies that the core
C
must be transversely cut out.

Our situation is simpler than that of
[
IP18
,
DIW21
]
in the sense that we only consider rational curves:

Lemma 4.15
.

There exists an elementary cluster
(\mathcal{U},J,C)
whose core curve is
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, such that
\mathcal{U}\subseteq X
is isomorphic as complex manifolds to the total space
\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\to C
. In particular,
J
can be chosen to be integrable near
C
.

Proof.

Fix
J
to be an
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
such that all simple maps are transversely cut out, and let

be a genus
0
embedded
J
-holomorphic curve. Then

and the normal bundle

\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}(b)
as complex vector bundles. We deform the almost complex structure near

so that it agrees with the natural holomorphic structure on
N_{C}
transported from the total space of
\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}(b)
. Now note that

since

. Moreover,

since

is regular with index

and therefore should not have any

-holomorphic deformations; hence,

∎

4.4.
Cluster isotopy theorem

The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following statement.

Proposition 4.18
.

Fix

and cohomology classes
a_{1},a_{2}\in QH^{*}_{\mu_{p}}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. For two

-clusters

and

such that

\displaystyle c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\pm c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}+\sum_{k}c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}

\displaystyle c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\pm c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}+\sum_{k}c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}

where the core curves

for some

.

To prove Proposition

, we follow Ionel–Parker’s cluster isotopy method
[

, Section 7]
, which was also used in
[

, Section 3.3]
. We will focus on explaining how to adapt their argument to our setting and refer the reader to the original reference of the technical details.

To this end, we introduce some notations. To simplify the notations, given two

and
(\mathcal{U}_{1},J_{1},C_{1})
with

, we write

(4.50)

c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}\approx c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}

if

(4.51)

c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}=c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}+\sum_{k}c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}

where
\{(\mathcal{U}_{k},J_{k},C_{k})\}
consists of
E
-clusters such that
[C_{i}]=d_{i}[C]
for some
d_{i}\geq 2
. We write

(4.52)

c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}\approx c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}

for a similar meaning for

-curvature.

Denote by
\mathcal{J}(X)
the space of
\omega
-compatible almost complex structures on

, equipped with

-topology. Write

(4.53)

\mathcal{M}(X)^{\mathrm{emb}}:=\{(J,[u])\ |\ J\in\mathcal{J}(X),\ u:S^{2}\to X\ \text{embdded $J$-holomorphic sphere}\},

i.e., the universal moduli space of

genus
0

-holomorphic curves, where

stands for the classes represented by

up to reparametrization.
\mathcal{M}(X)^{\mathrm{emb}}
is an infinite-dimensional smooth manifold. We equip

Given any stable

.

\displaystyle c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{s}}

\displaystyle\approx c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}}+c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}},

\displaystyle c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{s}}

\displaystyle\approx c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}}+\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}},

by
4.10
and
4.13
. Therefore, the computations that
c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{s}}\approx 0
and
c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{s}}\approx 0
for
\mathrm{sign}(s)=-\sigma
show the Lemma holds.
∎

Finally, we need the following lemma which is concerned with isotopies of almost complex structures keeping the core curve pseudoholomorphic. This is needed for comparing cluster contributions with the ones from the standard (elementary) cluster. To set up the stage, we introduce some further notations. Given an embedded closed
2
-dimensional submanifold

, write
\mathcal{J}_{C}\subset\mathcal{J}(X)
is subset consisting of all

J
-holomorphic. Recall the definition of

proceeding Lemma

.

.

Let
(J_{t})_{-1\leq t\leq 1}
be a
C^{1}
-path in
\mathcal{J}_{C}\cap\mathcal{J}(X)
that is contained in
\mathcal{J}^{E}_{isol}

\mathrm{pr}_{\mathcal{J}}:\mathcal{M}(X)^{\mathrm{emb}}_{A}\to\mathcal{J}(X)

(4.62)

c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}\approx-c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{-}},\quad\quad\quad c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}\approx-c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{-}}.

Proof.

Again, the most important technical input is the Kuranishi model discussed in

We consider the space

4.5.
Final proof

We explain how to deduce
1.1
using the technical results above.

Proof of
1.1
.

Let
b\in\mathrm{im}(H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}))
. Then the total Steenrod power of
b\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is given by

(4.63)

\mathrm{St}(b)=b^{p}-t^{p-1}b\in H^{2p}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})[\![t,\theta]\!],

since the Bockstein
\beta
vanishes on
b
. Since
\mathrm{St}(b)\smile=Q\Sigma_{b}|_{q^{A}=0}
under the projection
(-)|_{q^{A}=0}:\Lambda\to\Lambda_{\leq 0}
by the general property of the quantum Steenrod operations
[
Lee25
, Proposition 2.21]
, and
(b^{p}-t^{p-1}b)\smile=\psi_{b}|_{q^{A}=0}
, we conclude that the classical terms of the two operations agree,
Q\Sigma_{b}|_{q^{A}=0}=\psi_{b}|_{q^{A}=0}
. It suffices to consider the non-classical terms (i.e. terms with
q^{A}
with
\omega(A)>0
) in both operations.

Fix an energy bound
E>0
, an almost complex structure
J\in\mathcal{J}(X)^{E}
and a finite cluster decomposition
\mathcal{C}(X)^{E}=\bigcup_{\ell}\mathcal{C}(X)^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}
. It suffices to show that for a fixed cluster
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}
, there is an equality

(4.64)

c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}=c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}\in\Lambda_{\leq E}

for all
i
; then we can take
E\to\infty
.

By cluster decomposition, the monomial terms in
q^{A}
in both structure constants
c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}=c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}
have the form
A=k[C_{\ell}]
for some
k\geq 1
.

Now fix
J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}(X)^{E}
such that the core curve
C_{\ell}
is
J^{\prime}
-holomorphic and moreover the cluster
(\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{elem}},J^{\prime},C_{\ell})
is an elementary cluster; this can be achieved by taking the elementary cluster around
C_{\ell}
and perturbing it away from
C_{\ell}
to achieve the transversality condition
J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}(X)^{E}
(cf.
[
IP18
, Theorem 7.1]
. By the cluster isotopy theorem (Proposition
4.18
), we have

(4.65)

\displaystyle c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\pm c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}}+\sum_{k}c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}

\displaystyle c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\pm c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}}+\sum_{k}c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}

where
(\mathcal{U}_{k},J_{k},C_{k})
are
E
-clusters with
[C_{k}]=d_{k}[C_{\ell}]\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for
d_{k}\geq 2
. By the local computation (Proposition
4.16
) for elementary clusters, we know that

(4.66)

c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}}=c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}}.

For each of the clusters
(\mathcal{U}_{k},J_{k},C_{k})
in the error terms
c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}
and
c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}
, we may now apply the cluster isotopy theorem again to deform these clusters to elementary clusters at the cost of introducing new clusters with core curves with homology classes
d[C_{k}]
for
d>2
. This deformation process also fixes the sign ambiguity in (
4.65
), as both signs are determined by the orientation sign of the underlying embedded curve (cf.
[
IP18
, Equation (7.3)]
). After finitely many iterations, the homology classes of the core curves in the error terms have energy
\omega(N[C_{\ell}])>E
for large
N\gg 0
, implying that the corresponding
E
-truncations of the structure constants in
\Lambda_{\leq E}
vanish. Therefore

(4.67)

c_{i}\langle Q\Sigma_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}=c_{i}\langle\psi_{b};a_{1},a_{2}\rangle^{E}\in\Lambda_{\leq E}

for a fixed
E
, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.22
.

Note that the part of the proof that uses the cluster formalism shows that the
non-classical terms
of the two operations agrees. A priori, the classical term (the coefficient of
q^{0}
) may differ if
b\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a torsion class which does not lift to
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, since

(4.68)

\mathrm{St}(b)=b^{p}-t^{p-1}b+t^{p-2}\theta(\beta b)

has the term from the Bockstein
\beta b\in H^{3}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
which does not contribute to the (classical part of) the
p
-curvature.

Assuming
\pi_{1}(X)=1
(which is true for all manifolds with a Calabi–Yau metric whose global holonomy is contained in
\mathrm{SU}(n)
),
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
has no torsion, so the issue does not arise.

Even if we take a torsion class
b\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
, the difference between the two operators only occurs in the classical term in low degrees. Indeed, the Bockstein class
\beta b\in H^{3}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
always admits an integral lift
\beta_{\mathbb{Z}}b\in H^{3}(X;\mathbb{Z})
from the connecting homomorphism associated to the short exact sequence
\mathbb{Z}\overset{\cdot p}{\to}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
of abelian groups, and this integral lift is always
p
-torsion. In particular, the ordinary Gromov–Witten invariants with
\beta_{\mathbb{Z}}b
as insertions must vanish. Hence, covariant constancy
[
SW22
]
implies that even if the classical Steenrod operation
\mathrm{St}(b)
has the Bockstein term (carrying
\theta
), there will be no such term in the quantum parts.
∎

4.6.
Calabi–Yau
n
-folds

We would like to conclude with an explanation of the key difficulty in generalizing our proof to higher-dimensional Calabi–Yau manifolds.

The difficulty in proving the statement of
1.1
is that there is no analog of
4.10
, that is we can no longer show that the
p
-curvature endomorphism
\psi_{b}
and its structure constants satisfy a cluster decomposition. In the
3
-fold case, we crucially used that the embedded images of the simple rational curves are isolated for a generic choice of
J\in\mathcal{J}(X)^{E}
, implying that the
compositions
of operations arising from curve counts satisfy a cluster decomposition (
Lemma

4.12
). In higher dimensions, this is no longer true, as the expected dimension of the space of embedded curves is
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X-6>0
and there could in general be curves representing different homology classes intersecting with each other. The counts of such intersecting curves are closely related to “meeting invariants” in
[
KP08
]
, and they in general account for the subtleties in the enumerative geometry of Calabi–Yau manifolds beyond complex dimension 3 (see
[
KP08
, Section 0.6]
).

Note that
[
IP18
, Section 9.2]
, the higher-dimensional analogue of the Gopakumar–Vafa formulas using the cluster formalism is discussed. The idea is to introduce additional incidence constraints
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\in H^{*}(X)
to the
J
-holomorphic curves, so that the index of the curves stays
0
: this can be achieved for constraints satisfying the dimension constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\gamma_{i}|=\dim X-6
. Indeed, the cluster decomposition, and the related transversality results, apply in general to index
0
curves. However, running this strategy to our situation produces a statement that
bulk-deformed
analogues of
Q\Sigma_{b}
and
\psi_{b}
with bulk-deformation given by
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
agree, which is a different statement from
1.1
.

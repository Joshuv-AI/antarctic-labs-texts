Mg alloyed InP and InN as candidate materials for UV photodetectors with possible solar cell and photocatalysis applications
Authors: Boshra Kiani Sadr, Aliasghar Shokri, Fatemeh Shirvani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the present work, the optoelectronic properties of InP and InN alloys doped with Mg through substitution of indium atoms (with an Mg concentration of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x = 0.25$$\end{document}) have been investigated using density functional theory within the generalized gradient approximation. To assess the experimental feasibility of these alloys, their structural, thermodynamic, and dynamical stability were examined through cohesive energy, formation enthalpies, and phonon calculations. The calculated electronic density of states revealed band gaps of 1.27 and 0.55 eV for InP and InN, respectively. Moreover, in the Mg-doped alloys, the Fermi level crosses the electronic states, suggesting a metallic-like behavior. In addition, a gap-like separation is observed within the conduction band for both systems, indicating a redistribution of unoccupied electronic states after alloying. Introduction

Optoelectronic materials have attracted significant attention due to their wide range of applications in modern technologies1–5. These applications include solar cells, night vision systems, medical imaging devices, and smartphones, among others. A variety of well-known optoelectronic materials, such as GaN, GaAs, graphene, InN, Pb-based compounds, SiC, CdTe, and InP, have been extensively studied using both experimental and theoretical approaches6–30. In recent years, increasing environmental concerns and the high production cost of some conventional materials have motivated researchers to explore alternative compositions. In this context, alloying with environmentally friendly and cost-effective elements such as Mg, Si, Zn, Sn, and Ge has emerged as a promising strategy to tailor material properties and enhance performance31–42.

Sabah et al. 43 performed a density functional theory study on the optical properties of GaAs, InAs, GaN, and InN under applied pressures of 0, 6, and 14 GPa. Their findings revealed that increasing pressure leads to a noticeable enhancement in the intensity of the absorption coefficient peaks for all investigated materials. Specifically, the main absorption peaks were located around 6.3 eV for GaAs and InAs, while for GaN and InN they appeared at higher photon energies in the range of 10–12 eV. In addition, a clear pressure-induced widening of the band gap was observed. The band gap values increased from 1.538 to 2.9682 eV for GaAs, from 0.544 to 1.958 eV for InAs, from 2.742 to 3.192 eV for GaN, and from 0.458 to 0.825 eV for InN as the pressure increased from 0 to 14 GPa.

Bendahah et al. 44 studied Sc-doped GaN with different compositions using DFT and SCAPS-1D simulations. They reported band gaps in the range of 1.7–2.7 eV along with a power conversion efficiency of about 14.5% and a high fill factor of 86%.

Belhachi 29 investigated the electronic properties of Eu- and Er-doped Al\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_{0.25}$$\end{document}Ga\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_{0.75}$$\end{document}N using density functional theory within the local density approximation, taking spin polarization into account. Their results indicated semiconducting band gaps of 1.97 and 2.24 eV for Eu- and Er-doped systems, respectively.

Yang et al. 45 developed an InGaAs/InP heterostructure photodiode that achieved bandwidths of 20, 15, and 15.5 GHz at wavelengths of 850, 1310, and 1550 nm (Infrared range), respectively, demonstrating strong potential for optical sensing applications.

Chu et al. 46 examined the absorption properties of InN nanowires in the infrared (IR) region and reported a high absorption coefficient of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1.4 \times 10^{6}$$\end{document} m\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{-1}$$\end{document} at 1550 nm, along with enhanced responsivity compared to InGaAs-based systems.

Al-Bediry et al. 47 employed DFT method to investigate the optical properties of tetragonal Si\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_{0.67}$$\end{document}Ge\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_{0.33}$$\end{document} alloy. Their results indicated an indirect band gap of 1.4 eV. Furthermore, the calculated plasmon energies were 16.58 eV and 16.91 eV along the x- and z-directions, respectively, while the corresponding refractive indices were found to be 3.38 and 3.31.

Rizwan et al. 48 explored the optical properties of Mg-alloyed CaTiO\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_{3}$$\end{document} using first-principles calculations. Their findings showed a slight increase in the band gap from 1.84 to 1.92 eV, accompanied by an enhancement of the refractive index from 2.0 to 2.4. In addition, the absorption edge was observed to shift toward higher photon energies, from 2.1 to 2.5 eV.

To the best of our knowledge, a systematic first-principles study of Mg-alloyed InP and InN with a fixed composition of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x=0.25$$\end{document}, combining structural stability, electronic structure, and a comprehensive set of optical properties, has not been reported so far. The present results provide new insights into the optoelectronic behavior of these systems and suggest their potential relevance for applications in ultraviolet photodetectors, solar energy conversion, photocatalysis, and plasmonic-related technologies.

In particular, Mg is expected to act as an acceptor dopant when substituting In atoms due to its lower valence electron count compared to indium. This substitution can induce hole doping, shift the Fermi level toward the valence band, and modify the electronic states near the band edges, thereby tuning the optical absorption edge and overall optoelectronic behavior of the host materials.

In this work, motivated by the growing demand for cost-effective and environmentally friendly materials, Mg atoms are introduced into InP and InN by partially substituting In sites in order to examine their optoelectronic properties within the framework of density functional theory using the generalized gradient approximation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: first, the computational methodology is described; next, the electronic properties, including total and partial density of states, are presented; subsequently, the optical properties, such as the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function, optical conductivity, energy loss function, absorption coefficient, and reflectivity, are analyzed; and finally, the main conclusions are summarized.

Computational details

In this study, the cubic structures of InP (with space group of F-43m) and InN (with space group of Fm-3m) have been considered. Although the thermodynamically stable phase of InN is the wurtzite structure (P6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_3$$\end{document}mc), the cubic (zinc-blende) phase has also been reported under specific growth conditions and is of considerable interest due to its structural compatibility with other III–V cubic semiconductors49–51. Therefore, the cubic phase of InN is adopted in this work to enable a consistent comparison with InP and to explore its optoelectronic properties in a metastable but technologically meaningful configuration. One Mg atom is substituted for an In atom in each structure, corresponding to an alloy composition of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x = 0.25$$\end{document}. Each structure contains a total of 8 atoms, consisting of 4 In atoms and 4 P or N atoms (see Fig. 1a–d). All calculations in this work were carried out within the framework of DFT as implemented in the Quantum ESPRESSO package. The exchange–correlation effects were described using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the GGA approximation52–54. The interaction between valence electrons and ionic cores was treated using norm-conserving pseudopotentials. Self-consistent field (SCF) calculations were performed to obtain the ground-state electronic structure. Structural optimization was carried out using variable-cell relaxation (vc-relax), where both lattice parameters and atomic positions were fully relaxed by minimizing the total energy and atomic forces. The convergence thresholds were set to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{-5}$$\end{document} eV for total energy and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{-4}$$\end{document} eV/Å for forces. A Monkhorst–Pack k-point mesh of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$8 \times 8 \times 8$$\end{document} was used for Brillouin zone sampling, and the plane-wave cutoff energy was set to 50 Ry. For the electronic DOS calculations, a denser k-point mesh of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$20 \times 20 \times 20$$\end{document} was employed to ensure accurate resolution. To further assess the role of electron correlation effects associated with the In d-orbitals, additional test calculations were performed using the DFT+U approach. However, no significant changes in the electronic band gaps were observed compared to standard GGA results. Therefore, and considering the absence of strongly localized d electrons in the studied systems, the DFT+U corrections were not included in the final analysis. Phonon calculations were performed using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) to evaluate the dynamical stability of the structures55. Calculated bond lengths (in Å) for pristine and Mg-alloyed InP and InN.

The calculated bond lengths provide insight into the nature of chemical bonding and lattice stiffness in the studied systems. As shown in Table 1, the In-N bond length (2.29 Å) is significantly shorter than the In-P bond length (2.57 Å), indicating stronger interatomic interactions in the nitride-based system. This behavior is consistent with the higher electronegativity of nitrogen compared to phosphorus, suggesting that InN possesses a stronger ionic character, whereas InP exhibits relatively more covalent bonding. Therefore, both compounds can be classified as having mixed ionic-covalent bonding, with InN being more ionic in nature. From the perspective of lattice stiffness, shorter bond lengths generally correspond to stronger bonding and higher rigidity. Accordingly, the shorter bonds in InN imply a stiffer lattice compared to InP, which is also consistent with the larger cohesive energy values obtained for the nitride system. Upon Mg incorporation, only minor changes in bond lengths are observed. In the phosphide system, the In-P bond length remains unchanged (2.57 Å), while the Mg-P bond is slightly shorter (2.54 Å), indicating a small local strengthening of bonds around Mg atoms. Similarly, in the nitride system, both In-N and Mg-N bond lengths slightly decrease to 2.27 Å, suggesting a marginal increase in bonding strength and local lattice stiffness. Overall, these results indicate that Mg alloying does not drastically alter the bonding nature but induces subtle local modifications, which can influence the mechanical rigidity and bonding characteristics of the systems.

Since Mg has a different valence electron configuration compared to In, its incorporation can introduce additional electronic states near the Fermi level, potentially leading to band gap tuning or even a transition from semiconducting to metallic-like behavior. Such modifications are anticipated to influence the optical response, particularly in terms of absorption coefficient, dielectric function, and optical conductivity. An enhancement in absorption in the ultraviolet region may improve the performance of UV photodetectors, while suitable band gap values in the pristine compounds can support solar energy conversion and photocatalytic activity. In contrast, the emergence of metallic-like characteristics in the Mg-alloyed systems may enable plasmonic behavior, which is beneficial for plasmon-based optoelectronic and sensing applications.

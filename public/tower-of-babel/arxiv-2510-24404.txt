Skyrmion-vortex pairing from duality
Shantonu Mukherjee

Abstract
An interaction between ferromagnetic and superconducting order, to be realized in a 2d ferromagnetic superconductor, is proposed obeying necessary symmetry principles. This interaction allow us to formulate a duality, similar to the Boson-vortex duality in 2+1 dimensional superfluid. In the dual theory Skyrmion and vortex excitations interact with each other via an emergent gauge field. The static interaction potential is attractive for a Skyrmion and a vortex with opposite topological charges. This interaction can lead to formation of bound pairs of the mentioned topological excitations.



I
Introduction

The coexistence of ferromagnetism and superconductivity, though seemingly counterintuitive, has been experimentally realized in various material systems owing to recent advances in experimental techniques
Oner et al. 2020
;
Jin et al. 2006
;
Kakani and Upadhyaya 1987
;
Zhu et al. 2016
. This coexistence phase provides a fertile ground for exploring rich physical phenomena emerging from the interplay between topological excitations such as superconducting vortices and magnetic Skyrmions. Among the most intriguing directions of current research is the realization of hybrid or composite topological entities formed by the coupling of superconducting vortices and magnetic Skyrmions at superconductor–ferromagnet interfaces
Baumard et al. 2019
;
Dahir et al. 2019
;
Hals et al. 2016
;
Andriyakhina and Burmistrov 2021
;
Dahir et al. 2020
;
Petrović et al. 2021
as well as within ferromagnetic superconducting bulks
Mukherjee and Lahiri 2025
. In these studies, the internal structure of the composite objects and their mutual interactions have been analyzed through collective field descriptions, often by solving the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations. While that approach captures the microscopic details faithfully, it often obscures an intuitive understanding of the effective macroscopic dynamics of these collective excitations.

To gain such physical intuition, it is convenient to adopt an effective point-particle like description of these mesoscopic topological entities. In this framework, the dynamics of each composite object can be tracked via the motion of its center, enabling one to employ the language of classical particle mechanics. This description allows a natural characterization of local interactions between these emergent particles. One systematic way to construct such an effective point-particle description is through duality transformations that map collective field configurations to particle degrees of freedom with emergent local interactions.

A well-known example of this correspondence is the particle-vortex duality in (2+1)-dimensional field theories
Peskin 1978
;
Dasgupta and Halperin 1981
. This duality plays a central role in the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition
Kosterlitz and Thouless 1973
and is typically discussed in the context of the planar X–Y model–an effective low-energy description for two-dimensional superfluids or spin systems
Savit 1980
. The particle-vortex duality maps vortex excitations into point particles interacting via an effective logarithmic potential. Such interactions lead to bound vortex-antivortex pairs forming a confined phase, while the BKT transition describes a transition to a deconfined phase in which isolated vortices emerge. Moreover, in the dual formulation, the vortex dynamics is characterized in terms of the motion of its core, which provides a powerful tool to study vortex motion and response under external perturbations such as electromagnetic fields.
Constructing an analogous duality in the present context would yield an equally powerful framework for analyzing the dynamics of Skyrmion-vortex bound states
.

In this work, we begin with a theoretical model akin to that introduced in a preceding study
Mukherjee and Lahiri 2025
. The model consists of a nonlinear sigma model coupled to the Landau–Ginzburg theory of superconductivity through a spin–magnetic-field interaction (SMFI) term, augmented by an additional direct coupling term (discussed in Section
III
). The inclusion of this direct coupling term is pivotal for formulating a dual theory. As will be demonstrated, the dual formulation features an emergent gauge field mediating the interaction between Skyrmion and vortex excitations. Depending on the field configuration, this interaction can be attractive for Skyrmion-antivortex or anti-Skyrmion-vortex pairs, leading to the formation of bound states. Notably, this direct coupling, essential for realizing the dual mapping, has not been explored previously in the context of magnetic systems to our knowledge, making the duality developed here novel. (For comparison, see
Lee and Kane 1990
for an analogous duality in fermionic systems.) The dual theory provides a natural framework to study the coupled dynamics of Skyrmion-vortex bound states via coupled Thiele-type equations and offers a theoretical avenue to explore BKT-like unbinding transitions of these composite excitations–an exciting new direction in the field.

II
Revisiting the CP
1
representation of spin system

Two component complex spinors form a 2d complex (
\mathbb{C}
) Hilbert space. However, for any given state
\ket{\psi}
there will be states
\ket{\psi^\prime}=\lambda\ket{\psi}
which will differ by a complex phase factor. These states form a equivalent class of states. Thus the Hilbert space is a collection of such equivalent classes or
rays
. The space formed by these physical states or rays is called a projective Hilbert space or
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
(Quotient space
(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{C}^{*}
). For Normalized spinors
|z|^{2}=1
, the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
space become the Bloch sphere. This is because
|z|^{2}=1
defines a 3-sphere, while the space of equivalent states connected by
U(1)
transformations forms a circle leading to a quotient space
S^{3}/U(1)
which is topologically equivalent to
S^{2}
. Thus space of normalized 2d rays forms a 2-sphere known as Bloch sphere.

The fluctuation in a spin system is expressed in terms of the normalized spin vector
\hat{n}
. The spin states expressed by these vectors lie on a 2-sphere which is again the Bloch sphere. This induces a map between normalized spin vector
\hat{n}
and
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
spinor expressed by the following relation

\hat{n}=z^{\dagger}\bm{\sigma}z,\,\,\,\,z^{\dagger}z=1.

(1)

The 2d spinor
z
can be chosen as

z=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta/2)\\
e^{i\phi}sin(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix}

(2)

Let us now see how various terms in the action of an ferromagnetic system changes in this new representation. We first write down the general action for the unit spin field for such systems

S_{FM}=\int d^{3}x\left(\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\hat{n})\cdot\partial_{t}\hat{n}-\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}\left(\mathbf{\gradient}n^{i}\right)^{2}-\mu n^{i}\varepsilon^{ijk}\partial^{j}A^{k}\right)

(3)

Here the first term is the geometric phase that a
dynamical spin gains while tracing a path in spin space.
This term arises in the following way

\displaystyle L=i\bra{\hat{n}}\partial_{t}\ket{\hat{n}}-H=i\bra{\hat{n}}\partial_{n^{i}}\ket{\hat{n}}\frac{\partial n^{i}}{\partial t}-H

(4)

One can recognize that the first term in the second line of Eq (
4
) to be the berry connection
\bm{\mathcal{A}}=i\bra{\hat{n}}\gradient_{\hat{n}}\ket{\hat{n}}
. Presence of this geometric phase in a dynamical spin action can be rigorously derived using spin coherent states
Han 2017
. It can be readily verified that in the new
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation this geometric phase term takes the following form

\bm{\mathcal{A}}\cdot\partial_{t}\hat{n}=i\bra{\hat{n}}\gradient_{\hat{n}}\ket{\hat{n}}\cdot\partial_{t}\hat{n}=S(\cos\theta-1)\dot{\phi}=iz^{\dagger}\partial_{t}z,

(5)

where in the second line we have obtained the expression using spin coherent states for
\ket{\hat{n}}

Han 2017
. Now we take a look at the second term of Eq (
3
). This term is called a non-linear sigma term which captures the fluctuation of the spin field
\hat{n}
. The constant
\rho_{M}
is the spin stiffness which
determine the energy cost for spin variation
. In the new
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation this term will have the following form

\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}\left(\mathbf{\gradient}n^{i}\right)^{2}=2\rho_{M}\left((\gradient z^{\dagger})\cdot(\gradient z)-(z^{\dagger}\gradient z)\cdot(\gradient z^{\dagger}z)\right),

(6)

where we have used the identity
\sigma^{i}_{ab}\sigma^{i}_{\alpha\beta}=2\delta_{a\beta}\delta_{b\alpha}-\delta_{ab}\delta_{\alpha\beta}
and
z^{\dagger}z=1
. The term
z^{\dagger}\gradient z
or more generally
a_{\mu}=-iz^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}z
behaves as a gauge field under the transformation
z\rightarrow e^{i\chi}z
:

z\rightarrow e^{i\chi}z,\quad a_{\mu}\rightarrow a_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\chi.

(7)

Due to this gauge degree of freedom the Eq (
6
) can be written by defining a covariant derivative for
z
as

\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}\left(\mathbf{\gradient}n^{i}\right)^{2}=2\rho_{M}|D_{\mu}z|^{2},\quad D_{\mu}=\left(\partial_{\mu}-ia_{\mu}\right).

(8)

Thus we see that
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation brings out a gauge degree of freedom in the effective theory. This will help us further in thinking of new terms that can be present in the theory as we shall see in the next section. In this context let us also discuss one other term in this representation that will be useful in formulation of the duality. This term is the topological current density or the
Skyrmion current density
and is expressed as

J^{s}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\varepsilon^{ijk}n^{i}\partial^{\beta}n^{j}\partial^{\gamma}n^{k},\quad i,\,j,\,k\in(1,2,3),\quad\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma\in(0,1,2).

(9)

Using the relation in Eq (
1
) into the above definition one can show that the Skyrmion current density
J^{s}_{\mu}
takes the following form

J^{s}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\partial^{\beta}a^{\gamma}.

(10)

This form lets us think of the Skyrmion current as an effective magnetic field (
\bm{\tilde{B}}
) and effective electric field (
\bm{\tilde{E}}
)

J^{s}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\partial_{x}a_{y}-\partial_{y}a_{x},\,\,\partial_{y}a_{t}-\partial_{t}a_{y},\,\,\partial_{t}a_{x}-\partial_{x}a_{t})=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\tilde{B}_{z},\,\,-\tilde{E}_{y},\,\,\tilde{E}_{x}).

(11)

Due to this form, the conservation law of this topological current transforms to Faraday’s Law

\partial_{\mu}J^{s\mu}=-\partial_{t}\tilde{B}^{z}+(-\partial_{x}\tilde{E}_{y}+\partial_{y}\tilde{E}_{x})=0.

(12)

III
Skyrmion-vortex Pairing from duality

In this work our system of choice is a 2d system hosting both S-wave superconducting and ferromagnetic order which coexist and interact with each other. This system represents a 2d ferromagnetic superconductor thin film. An effective theory for such a system can be expressed by the following Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{SC}+\mathcal{L}_{FM}+\mathcal{L}_{I},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{SC}=\frac{\rho_{s}}{2m}(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A})^{2}+f_{0}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{t}\Phi-qA_{0}).

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{FM}=\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\hat{n})\cdot\partial_{t}\hat{n}-\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}\left(\mathbf{\gradient}n^{i}\right)^{2}-\mu n^{i}\varepsilon^{ijk}\partial^{j}A^{k}.

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{I}=-\lambda\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos\theta)\mathbf{\gradient}\phi\cdot(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A}).

(16)

The first part of the total Lagrangian expresses the phase fluctuation of the superconducting order parameter
Lee and Fisher 1991
keeping the amplitude fixed (
\Phi=e^{i\chi}
) while second part expresses the spin fluctuation of the ferromagnet
Read and Sachdev 1995
. The last part
\mathcal{L}_{I}
expresses an interaction between the two coexisting phases. The angles
\theta,\phi
defines the components of the unit spin field
\hat{n}\,(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\,\cos\theta)
which describes the spin coherent states on the Bloch sphere in the spin space. As the non-linear sigma model is invariant under infinetesimal global SO(3) rotation of the spin field
\hat{n}
, this new interaction should also obey this symmetry. It can indeed be shown to be true as follows. Under a global SO(3) rotation the spin field changes as
\theta\rightarrow\theta+\delta\theta,\quad\phi\rightarrow\phi+\delta\phi
. This changes the interaction term as

\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(\theta+\delta\theta))\mathbf{\gradient}(\phi+\delta\phi)\cdot(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A}).

(17)

For small and constant changes
\delta\theta,\,\delta\phi
we have

\cos(\theta+ \delta\theta)\simeq\cos\theta,\quad\mathbf{\gradient}(\phi+\delta\phi)=\mathbf{\gradient}\phi

(18)

Thus we see that the above statement is true. This term will also remain invariant under emergent gauge transformation in the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation as we shall see. In the following we shall adopt the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation of the spin field and shall formulate a duality which would lead to a confining phase of Vortex and Skyrmion. Using the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation of various relevant term developed in Sec-
II
the above Lagrangian takes the following form

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\rho_{s}}{2m}(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A})^{2}+f_{0}(\partial_{0}\chi-qA_{0}-\frac{1}{f_{0}}a_{0})-\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}4(|D_{\mu}z|^{2})+\lambda\bm{a}\cdot(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A}),

(19)

We note that the emergent gauge field
a_{\mu}
has appeared in the second, third and the last term. Previously the theory had the symmetry under rotation of the spin field which in this new representation translates to the invariance of the Lagrangian under global SU(2) transformation of the spinor field
z
. Also, due to appearance of the the gauge field
a_{\mu}
a new gauge invariance under the transformation
\mathbf{a}\rightarrow\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{\gradient}\xi
emerges. This is manifestly true for the third term, where as in the last term this invariance is satisfied as the associated current
\mathbf{J}=(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A})
is the conserved current for the superconductor. To proceed towards the formulation of the duality we first combine the first and the last term in Eq (
19
) by the completion of square as follows

\frac{\rho_{s}}{2m}(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A})^{2}+\lambda\mathbf{a}\cdot(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A})=\frac{\rho_{s}}{2m}\left(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A}-\frac{2m\lambda}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{a}\right)^{2}-\frac{2m\lambda^{2}}{\rho_{s}}|\mathbf{a}|^{2}.

(20)

This leads to the following form of the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\rho_{s}}{2m}\left(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A}-\frac{2m\lambda}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{a}\right)^{2}+f_{0}\left(\partial_{0}\chi-qA_{0}-\frac{1}{f_{0}}a_{0}\right)-4\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}|\mathbf{\gradient}z|^{2}-\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2\rho_{s}}\right)|\mathbf{a}|^{2}\right).

(21)

To derive the dual model let us first linearize the first term in the above Lagrangian by introducing a auxiliary field
f_{i}
through a Hubbard Stratonovich (H-S) transformation as follows

\int\mathcal{D}f_{i}\exp[i\int d^{4}x[-f_{i}f_{i}-\sqrt{\frac{2\rho_{s}}{m}}\mathbf{f}\cdot\left(\Phi^{*}\mathbf{\gradient}\Phi-q\bm{A}-\frac{2m\lambda}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{a}\right)]]

(22)

Thus the total Lagrangian thus obtained is given by

\mathcal{L}=-f_{i}f_{i}-\sqrt{\frac{2\rho_{s}}{m}}\mathbf{f}\cdot\left(\mathbf{\gradient}\chi-q\bm{A}-\frac{2m\lambda}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{a}\right)+f_{0}\left(\partial_{0}\chi-qA_{0}-\frac{1}{f_{0}}a_{0}\right)-4\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}|\mathbf{\gradient}z|^{2}-\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2\rho_{s}}\right)|\mathbf{a}|^{2}\right).

(23)

To proceed We first absorb the factor
\sqrt{\frac{2\rho_{s}}{m}}
into a redefinition of the auxiliary field
f_{i}\rightarrow J_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{2\rho_{s}}{m}}f_{i},\,f_{0}\rightarrow J_{0}=f_{0}
. This will lead us to the following Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{m}{2\rho_{s}}J_{\mu}J^{\mu}+J_{\mu}\left(\partial^{\mu}\chi-qA^{\mu}-\tilde{a}^{\mu}\right)-4\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}|\mathbf{\gradient}z|^{2}-\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2\rho_{s}}\right)|\mathbf{a}|^{2}\right),

(24)

where we have redefined the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
gauge fields as
\tilde{a}_{i}=\dfrac{2m\lambda}{\rho_{s}}a_{i},\,\tilde{a}_{0}=\dfrac{1}{f_{0}}a_{0}
and also have added a term
f_{0}^{2}
for the sake of completeness. This allowed us to write the first two terms using relativistic notation which will help us in obtaining the dual model. We now write the phase
\chi
dividing it into small scale fluctuation (
\chi_{r}
) and a singular part(
\chi_{s}
) which defines the mesoscopic excitation of the superconductor called vortices
Mukherjee and Lahiri 2020
. With this we write the above Lagrangian as

\mathcal{L}=\frac{m}{2\rho_{s}}J_{\mu}J^{\mu}-\partial^{\mu}J_{\mu}\chi_{r}+J_{\mu}\left(\partial^{\mu}\chi_{s}-qA^{\mu}-\tilde{a}^{\mu}\right)-4\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}|\mathbf{\gradient}z|^{2}-\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2\rho_{s}}\right)|\mathbf{a}|^{2}\right).

(25)

Now let us integrate out
\chi_{r}
to get the following identity

\partial^{\mu}J_{\mu}=0\implies J_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\partial^{\nu}b^{\rho},

(26)

where
b^{\mu}
is an emergent gauge field. This will lead to the following form of the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{m}{8\rho_{s}}(\partial_{\mu}b_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}b_{\mu})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\partial^{\nu}b^{\rho}\left(\partial^{\mu}\chi_{s}-qA^{\mu}-\tilde{a}^{\mu}\right)-4\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}|\mathbf{\gradient}z|^{2}-\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2\rho_{s}}\right)|\mathbf{a}|^{2}\right)

(27)

Next we write down the vortex current and the Skyrmion current (Sec-
II
) in the following way

\displaystyle J^{v}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\partial^{\nu}\partial^{\rho}\chi_{s},\,\,\,J^{s}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\partial^{\nu}\tilde{a}^{\rho}.

(28)

Putting these definitions into above Lagrangian we get

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{m}{8\rho_{s}}(\partial_{\mu}b_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}b_{\mu})^{2}+b^{\mu}\left(J^{v}_{\mu}-\frac{q}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta}\partial^{\alpha}A^{\beta}-J^{s}_{\mu}\right)-4\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}|\mathbf{\gradient}z|^{2}-\left(\frac{\rho_{M}}{2}-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2\rho_{s}}\right)|\mathbf{a}|^{2}\right)

(29)

The above dual action suggest that vortices (
J^{v}_{0}
) and Skyrmions (
J^{s}_{0}
) interact with each other via an emergent gauge field
b_{\mu}
. However, there is a mixed Chern-Simons term involving
b_{\mu}
and
A_{\mu}
. It is well known that such a term would generate an effective mass for any of the gauge fields involved when the other one is integrated out
Deser et al. 1982
;
Allen et al. 1991
;
Mukherjee and Lahiri 2020
. Due to this topological mass mechanism at work the emergent gauge field
b_{\mu}
will gain a mass due to the quantum effects of dynamical electromagnetic gauge field
A_{\mu}
leading to a screened interaction between Skyrmion and vortex excitations

V(|\bm{R}_{v}-\bm{R}_{s}|)\sim\frac{2\rho_{s}N_{v}N_{S}\lambda}{m}K_{0}\left(\tilde{m}|\bm{R}_{v}-\bm{R}_{s}|\right).

(30)

This interaction potential will be negative for
\lambda<0
and opposite charges of vortex (
N_{v}
) and Skyrmion (
N_{s}
). Due to this screened coulomb potential a vortex and an anti-Skyrmion or an anti-vortex and a Skyrmion will form a pair with a size
<\frac{1}{\tilde{m}}
, where
\tilde{m}=\sqrt{\frac{q^{2}\rho_{s}}{m}}
is the effective photon mass.

IV
Discussion and Future Work:

At this end a few points need to be discussed.

•

We first note that our model in the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
representation is invariant under the gauge transformation
a_{\mu}\rightarrow a_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\chi
, while in the dual model in Eq (
29
) the mass term for
\bm{a}
seems to violate this gauge invariance. However, this is only apparently true. This is because, for the sake of formulating the duality we had added and subtracted a suitable term in Eq (
20
) as a part of the method of completion of square. Thus even at this level (Eq (
20
)) the gauge invariance is manifestly present. But due to the H-S transformation this gauge invariance becomes hidden. As this H-S transformation is an exact transformation one can easily get back the original theory by integrating out the auxiliary field
f_{i}
. Thus the gauge invariance of the dual model is only apparently broken.

•

We also observe that the interaction potential arising from the dual model (Eq (
29
)) has the following behavior in different distance ranges

\displaystyle V(r)\sim-\ln(r/\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda_s)\quad\text{for}\quad r<<\lambda_{s}

(31)

\displaystyle V(r)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi\lambda_{s}}{2r}}\exp\left((-r/\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda_{s})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r>>\lambda_{s}

(32)

where the constant
\lambda_{s}=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\tilde{m}
is the penetration depth of the superconducting order. Thus within the range
r<\lambda_{s}
the attractive interaction is logarithmically diverging leading to a strongly bound state, while outside this range the attraction fades away. Also we see that the strength of the interaction depends linearly on
\rho_{s}
which means that as the superconducting order becomes weak, i.e.
\rho_{s}\rightarrow 0
, the interaction strength also becomes small.

•

The possibility of formation of anti-Skyrmion-vortex bound state suggested by the dual model of Eq (
29
) leads us to think of BKT like physics in the current scenario. As like BKT scenario, there can two phases- one is characterized by non-zero density of anti-Skyrmion-vortex pairs, while in the other phase the pair density will become zero. If such a phase transition indeed exist that would be a novel scenario in the condensed matter literature. Investigation into this direction is ongoing and will be reported in future.

•

The other very interesting direction of future study could be to analyze the dynamics of the bound pairs. The collective dynamics of a magnetic texture is captured by Thiele equation
Thiele 1973
which can be derived from Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation by considering the dynamics of the centre of the topological object. Whereas the dynamics of the vortices in a superconductor is captured by the Bardeen-Stephen equation (or Nozières-Vinen equation)
Bardeen and Stephen 1965
;
Nozières and Vinen 1966
. In presence of the binding interaction derived in the present work these equations will be coupled differential equation which can be solved for the bound states. The most interesting aspect of such collective motion will be to observe a Skyrmion Hall effect generated purely due to vortex motion. Also, topological-Hall like transport may also be changed due to such bound states. These directions will be investigated in future.

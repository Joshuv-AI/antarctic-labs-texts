Identifying potential ligand-receptor interactions by integrating LSTM network and the attention mechanism for cell-cell communication prediction
Authors: Yingwei Deng, Min Chen, Pengfei Gao, Ruogu Luo, Zejun Li, Yuhua Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCell-cell communication (CCC) mediated by ligand-receptor (L-R) interactions is fundamental to deciphering tissue development and disease mechanisms. While single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has advanced this field, existing computational methods for inferring CCC often suffer from limitations such as dependence on static databases and a failure to capture the sequential dependency of amino acids within proteins, which restricts their generalizability and predictive accuracy. Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to develop a robust computational framework capable of identifying potential L-R interactions directly from protein sequence data, thereby overcoming the reliance on static databases and enabling the discovery of novel signaling pairs.MethodsTo achieve this objective, we introduce CellAL, a deep learning-based framework for predicting potential interacting L-R pairs and decoding cellular communication. The CellAL pipeline consists of two main stages: (1) L-R Pair Identification, which extracts sequence features using BioTriangle, selects informative features via XGBoost to reduce dimensionality, and classifies interactions using a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network integrated with an attention mechanism specifically designed to capture long-range sequence dependencies that characterize structural binding affinities; and (2) CCC Inference, which filters identified pairs using scRNA-seq data and quantifies crosstalk intensity through a comprehensive scoring strategy that combines expression thresholding, expression product, and specific expression metrics.ResultsPerformance evaluations on four standard L-R interaction datasets demonstrated that CellAL significantly surpassed classical protein-protein interaction prediction methods and achieved competitive performance against state-of-the-art ensemble models, achieving the highest AUPR values on three datasets. The identified To achieve L-R pairs showed a high degree of overlap with existing databases such as CellChat and Connectome. Furthermore, when applied to human melanoma scRNA-seq data, CellAL successfully inferred critical signaling networks, revealing strong bidirectional crosstalk between melanoma cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), macrophages, and endothelial cells. These findings were consistent with results from three other representative CCC prediction tools.ConclusionsCellAL effectively overcomes the limitations of database dependence by leveraging sequence-level biochemical modeling to predict structural L-R interactions. By integrating deep learning predictions with transcriptomic data, CellAL provides a robust and valuable tool for dissecting complex CCC networks at single-cell resolution, particularly within the tumor microenvironment.

Introduction

In multicellular organisms, cells coordinate their activities through communication between various cell types. Cell-cell communication (CCC) is fundamental to perform the biological functions of cells, tissues, organs, and systems [1-3]. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) provides unprecedented resolution and promotes our understanding of intracellular and intercellular heterogeneity [4, 5]. We can accurately infer rare cell subpopulations [6], construct complex gene-gene regulatory networks [7], investigate drug response [8–11], and decipher cellular crosstalk across communicating cell types as well as under diseased and homeostatic conditions based on scRNA-seq data [12].

In vivo experiments have advanced the development of cellular signaling pathway analysis. However, these wet-laboratory methods are time-consuming, laborious, and expensive. Consequently, artificial intelligence techniques have been increasingly accumulated to study CCC from scRNA-seq data. To facilitate this, comprehensive resources such as CellCommuNet have been established, curating hundreds of datasets to explore communication differences between disease and normal states [13]. CCC is driven by various mediators including protein-mediated interactions, metabolite-mediated interactions, and host–microbiome interactions [14]. However, ligand–receptor interactions (LRIs)—where ligands in one cell bind to cognate receptors on other cells—are dominant mediators for cellular communications analysis [15]. Recently, this scope has expanded to include non-peptide ligands (NPLs) modeled by tools like MetaLigand [16], and extracellular vesicle-mediated miRNA communication inferred by miRTalk [17], highlighting the complexity of intercellular dynamics.

Recently, many artificial intelligence tools have been designed for CCC inference through LRIs using scRNA-seq data. For example, CellPhoneDB [18] and ICELLNET [19] evaluated the product of Ligand and Receptor (L-R) expressions. The widely used CellChat tool has recently evolved to CellChat v2, incorporating an expanded database, interactive visualization, and enhanced functionalities for comparing communication across different biological conditions [20, 21]. SingleCellSignalR [22] computed a p-value for each LRI through a score null distribution when deciphering statistically significant and cell-type–specific signaling. CellChat [21], CellPhoneDB [18], and ICELLNET [19] recapitulated known LRIs based on the multi subunit structures of L-R pairs. CellEnBoost [23], CellDialog [2] and CellGDnG [24] decoded potential LRIs and further inferred CCC by combining scRNA-seq data.

In addition to the above LRI analysis tools, several tools both analyze LRIs and investigate downstream signal pathways. to better understand regulatory mechanisms. For example, to analyze gene expressions from multiple facets, PyMINEr [25] built autocrine and paracrine signaling networks. More recently, CausalCCC was developed to reconstruct causal gene-gene interaction pathways by integrating upstream and downstream signals [26]. Similarly, SigXTalk utilizes hypergraph learning to quantify signal fidelity and specificity, explicitly modeling the crosstalk between pathways that was previously overlooked [27]. To decode the functionally influential CCC, NicheNet [28] utilized downstream signaling changes to assess the activity of the upstream receptors. To depict observed heteromeric molecular complexes, CellChat [21] arranged data source of interactions involved in ligands, receptors and corresponding cofactors.

Furthermore, CCC inference based on LRIs can remold cellular structure of tissue by integrating spatial transcriptomics data. CSOmap [29] and SpaOTsc [30] recapitulated cellular spatial information through LRIs. Advanced methods like SpaCcLink now account for downstream intracellular signals to decode spatial communication patterns [31], while STCase employs interpretable graph neural networks to uncover niche-specific CCC events that vary across microenvironments [32]. Addressing patient heterogeneity, scACCorDiON applies optimal transport to analyze sample-specific communication events in large disease cohorts [33]. SpaTalk [34] designed a non-negative linear model and scored the L-R-target signaling network involving in spatially proximal cells by deconvoluting cell-type composition. Additionally, Giladi et al. [35] presented a sequencing-based model PIC-seq to decode cellular dialogues by sequencing physically communicating cells. PIC-seq incorporated cell ranking of physically communicating cells and scRNA-seq and systematically depicted L-R crosstalk between cells. It provides a direct tool to dissect intercellular communication specific pathways. Consequently, a comprehensive knowledge of LRIs can efficiently facilitate the deciphering of cellular crosstalk

However, despite the advancements of these sophisticated tools in modeling spatial contexts, downstream pathways, and diverse signaling modalities, most existing LRI prediction methods still suffer from limitations including database dependence, limited generalizability, and lack of sequence-level biochemical modeling. Moreover, standard machine learning models often ignore the sequential dependency of amino acids within proteins. Although ligand-receptor interactions are not temporal events, proteins are composed of amino acids arranged in a specific linear order, which determines their physicochemical properties and binding affinities. To address this, we hypothesize that capturing the long-range dependencies within amino acid sequences is crucial for accurate LRI prediction.

Thus, in this manuscript, we propose a computational model called CellAL for predicting potential interacting L-R pairs and further decoding cellular communication. CellAL integrates sequence-derived descriptors, XGBoost feature prioritization, and feature-wise attention for heterogeneous ligand–receptor features to address the aforementioned limitations. After testing the performance of CellAL, we employ it to deduce cellular crosstalk within melanoma. This work has three contributions:A deep learning-based model CellAL is devised to predict potential interacting L-R pairs by integrating L-R feature extraction through BioTriangle, L-R feature selection with XGBoost, and L-R classification using Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network with the attention mechanism.Expression thresholding, expression product, and specific expression of L-R pairs are combined to evaluate CCC.Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) is inferred to communicate with melanoma cells.

Step 2: L-R feature selection

Feature selection is a powerful data preprocessing technique in machine learning. It aims to design an algorithm to select the more informative features and improve the model performance [41]. The obtained L-R features using BioTriangle are high-dimensional and contains much robust information, which could cause the curse of dimension, overfitting, increased computational complexity, and reduced interpretability of the model. XGBoost [42] is a wide-used end-to-end tree growth model. It exhibits good ability when dealing with sparse data. To reduce the L-R feature dimension, we adopt XGBoost for scoring L-R feature importance. By ranking the L-R feature importance and selecting the most informative features, we can reduce the dimension of L-R features, improve the performance, avoid the overfitting, and enhance the interpretability of the model.

Although LSTM demonstrates good accuracy in traditional classification tasks, it has limitations. First, regardless of the length of the input sequence, LSTM encodes the sequence as a fixed-length hidden state vector through a loop connection and the gating mechanism. As a result, sequence information may be lost or the raw data may not be adequately expressed, especially for long or complex input sequences. It makes the model difficult to capture subtle changes and important features in the input data, resulting in poor performance and generalization ability of the model. Moreover, the output in LSTM’s intermediate state is underutilized. The LSTM performance can be improved by increasing the maximum pooling layer, but it may result in a large amount of information loss [44, 45].

The attention mechanism is an important technique in the field of deep learning, which is inspired by the human visual system [46]. This mechanism sets different weights for different components of the input data, enabling the model to prioritize information that is more important to the task. Based on the attention mechanism, the model can better focus attention on information that are most critical to the task and ignore other less important information when processing data [47]. Adding the attention mechanism to LSTM facilitates maintaining information as possible as and makes LSTM more efficient when processing sequence data. Thus, we present LSTM with the attention mechanism to classify L-R pairs.

Cellular communication inference

Step 1: L-R filtering

CCC is commonly mediated by interacting L-R pairs. To test CCC intensity within a tissue, first, we filtrate L-R pairs from known and predicted pairs via a threshold: an L-R pair is viewed as interacting if its linkage probability is larger than the threshold, otherwise, it is no-interacting. Subsequently, the filtrated pairs are further filtered based on scRNA-seq data from GEO [48]: when their ligand and/or receptor has no expression within a tissue, they will be viewed as no-mediating signaling. As a result, we can better focus on biologically relevant L-R pairs and dissect cellular crosstalk accurately.

Results

Experimental setup

Area Under Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve (AUC) and Area Under Precision-Recall (PR) curve (AUPR) are two commonly-used indicators to evaluate the performance of machine learning models [50, 51]. AUC is in the range of [0–1], and the larger the value is, the better the model performance is. AUC with 0.5 denotes that the classification accuracy has no difference with the random guess, and AUC with less than 0.5 denotes the poorer classification accuracy. AUPR is in the range of [0–1], with a value closer to 1 denoting better classification accuracy and 0 denoting the poorer classification accuracy. While AUC is a widely used standard metric for classification performance, it can sometimes be over-optimistic in highly imbalanced datasets. Therefore, we also employed the Area Under the Precision-Recall curve (AUPR), which is recognized as a more informative metric for imbalanced data, to comprehensively evaluate the model performance alongside AUC. So the combination of AUC and AUPR can fully evaluate the model performance in different situations. Using AUC and AUPR as main indictors, we assessed the performance of CellAL and other L-R classification models in addition to precision, recall, accuracy, and F1-score.

The CellAL model was compared with four state-of-the-art protein-protein interaction identification models, i.e., ACT-SVM [52] (SVM for simplicity), Decision Tree [53] (DT for simplicity), PPISP-XGBoost [54] (XGBoost for simplicity), and siamese Residual recurrent convolutional neural network (RCNN) [55] (PIPR for simplicity). Table 3 shows their parameters. To ensure the robustness of our model and avoid overfitting, we strictly implemented a rigorous 5-fold cross-validation strategy, repeated 20 times to minimize random bias. Performance comparison between CellAL with other methods

To estimate the performance of CellAL, we compared it with four classical PPI prediction models, i.e., SVM [52], DT [56], XGBoost [54], and PIPR [55], as well as two state-of-the-art methods, CellEnBoost [23] and CellComNet [57]. SVM [52] mapped a protein sequence to digital features and combined these features as input to SVM for predicting PPIs. DT classified protein-protein pairs by recursively constructing the structure of a decision tree. XGBoost [54] extracted characteristics of proteins through feature extraction methods, preprocessed features to obtain more optimized representations, and used synthetic minority oversampling and nuclear principal component analysis to alleviate class imbalances and remove redundant features, finally feeding these features into XGBoost to uncover PPI sites. PIPR [55] combined the Siamese architecture of deep RCNN to incorporate local features and context information for PPI prediction. CellEnBoost [23] utilizes an ensemble approach—comprising LightGBM, AdaBoost, and CNNs—to predict LRIs, supported by three scoring methods for CCC inference. Similarly, CellComNet [57] leverages deep neural networks and heterogeneous Newton boosting for LRI identification, using a joint scoring scheme to assess CCC intensity.

Table 4 and Fig 2 present a comprehensive performance comparison of CellAL against four classical methods (SVM, DT, XGBoost, and PIPR) across four datasets. These illustrations evaluate key metrics including precision, recall, accuracy, F1-score, AUC, and AUPR, along with their average performance across the datasets. As visualized in Fig. 2, CellAL consistently achieved the best performance across these indicators compared to the classical models. Furthermore, Fig 3 characterizes the ROC and PR curves, extending the comparison to include advanced ensemble and deep learning-based methods (CellEnBoost and CellComNet). The results demonstrated that CellAL significantly outperformed the four classical methods across all datasets. When compared with the advanced methods (CellEnBoost and CellComNet), CellAL exhibited highly competitive performance. Notably, CellAL achieved the highest AUPR values on Datasets 1, 2, and 3 (0.8579, 0.8244, and 0.8187, respectively) and the highest AUC on Dataset 4 (0.8366). Since AUPR is a critical metric for evaluating performance on imbalanced biological datasets, these results highlight the robustness of CellAL in identifying true interacting pairs with high precision. In summary, CellAL proved to have strong LRI prediction ability, comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art methods in key performance indicators. CCC inference within melanoma

Recently, the incidence of melanoma has continuously risen in human with white skin in developed countries. Melanoma accounts for 1.7% of cancer diagnoses worldwide. In the United States, it ranks the fifth among the most common cancers [61, 62]. Since 1975, its incidence in the United States has increased by over 320% [63]. scRNA-seq has achieved unprecedented cellular resolution at the whole genome scale and provides abundant gene expression information, enabling us to explore cellular heterogeneity of tumors [64]. In this section, we used CellAL to systematically analyze cellular crosstalk within human melanoma.

To estimate cellular crosstalk intensity within melanoma, we conducted a systematic analysis for LRI-based CCC in the melanoma tissue. First, its scRNA-seq information were achieved from the GEO database (entry code: GSE72056) [65]. We utilized the cell type annotations provided by the original study. To address dropouts, gene expression was averaged per cell type. A ligand-receptor pair was considered potentially interacting only if the cluster-specific expression of both proteins exceeded their respective global mean expression plus one standard deviation (dynamic thresholding), ensuring robustness against low-expression noise. The data include seven cell types: melanoma malignant cells, T cells, B cells, macrophages (Macro), endothelial cells (Endo), CAFs, and natural killer (NK) cells. Next, the high-confident L-R pairs predicted by CellAL and known LRIs on Dataset 1 were filtered in combination with its scRNA-seq data. For an L-R pair, when its ligand and receptor are expressed at the same time in the tissue, the pair was retained, otherwise it was removed. Third, CellAL constructed the cellular crosstalk network involving the seven cell types by combining a comprehensive scoring strategy based on expression threshold, expression product, and expression specificity. The L-R pairs mediating cellular crosstalk were ranked and the top three L-R pairs were obtained.

Conclusion

In this manuscript, we introduced CellAL, a deep learning framework integrating LSTM networks with an attention mechanism to predict LRIs from sequence-derived biochemical features. By effectively capturing long-range dependencies, CellAL transcends the reliance on static databases inherent in traditional methods, enabling the discovery of novel interactions. Our extensive evaluations confirm that CellAL not only significantly outperforms classical machine learning models but also achieves superior AUPR scores compared to state-of-the-art methods like CellEnBoost and CellComNet, demonstrating exceptional robustness in handling class-imbalanced biological data.

The framework’s practical utility was further established by deciphering complex crosstalk between malignant cells and stromal components (CAFs and macrophages) in human melanoma tissue. In this context, while our aggregated scoring strategy does not quantify statistical probability relative to a random null model as permutation-based methods do, it explicitly prioritizes biological relevance by balancing signal magnitude with specificity. This approach enables the robust ranking of potential interactions, particularly for the novel pairs identified by CellAL.

However, the current model’s exclusive reliance on sequence features constitutes a limitation, as it may overlook spatial constraints and conformational dynamics determined by 3D protein structures. Consequently, future work will focus on integrating structural information and multi-omics data to further enhance prediction accuracy and provide deeper biological interpretability for exploring tumor microenvironments

Equivalence of germs (of mappings and sets) over 𝕜 \Bbbk vs that over 𝕂 \mathbbm{K} Thanks: I was supported by the Israel Science Foundation, grants No. 1910/18 and 1405/22
Dmitry Kerner

Abstract
. Consider ℝ \mathbb{R} -analytic mapping-germs, ( ℝ n , o ) → f , f ~ ( ℝ m , o ) . (\mathbb{R}^{n},o)\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f,{\tilde{f}}}}$}\ \!(\mathbb{R}^{m},o). They can be equivalent (by coordinate changes) ℂ \mathbb{C} -analytically, but not ℝ \mathbb{R} -analytically. However, if the transformation of ℂ \mathbb{C} -equivalence is I ​ d Id modulo higher order terms, then it implies the ℝ \mathbb{R} -equivalence. On the other hand, starting from ℂ \mathbb{C} -analytic map-germs ( ℂ n , o ) → f , f ~ ( ℂ m , o ) , (\mathbb{C}^{n},o)\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f,{\tilde{f}}}}$}\ \!(\mathbb{C}^{m},o), and taking any field extension ℂ ↪ 𝕂 , \mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{K}, one has: if f ∼ 𝕂 f ~ f\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathbbm{K}}}$}\ \!{\tilde{f}} then f ∼ ℂ f ~ . f\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathbb{C}}}$}\ \!{\tilde{f}}. These (quite useful) properties seem to be not well known. We prove slightly stronger properties in a more general form: • for Maps ⁡ ( X , Y ) , {\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right), where X , Y X,Y are (formal/analytic/ 𝕜 \Bbbk -Nash) germs of spaces, with arbitrary singularities, over a base ring 𝕜 ; \Bbbk; • for the classical groups of (right/left-right/contact) equivalence of Singularity Theory; • for faithfully-flat extensions of rings 𝕜 ↪ 𝕂 . \Bbbk\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{K}. In particular, for arbitrary extensions of fields. The case “ 𝕜 \Bbbk is a ring” is important for the study of deformations/unfoldings. E.g. it implies the statement for field extensions: if a family of 𝕜 \Bbbk -maps { f t } \{f_{t}\} is 𝕂 \mathbbm{K} -trivial then it is also 𝕜 \Bbbk -trivial. Similar statements for germs of spaces (“isomorphism over 𝕂 \mathbbm{K} vs isomorphism over 𝕜 \Bbbk ”) follow by the standard reduction “Two maps are contact equivalent if and only if their zero sets are ambient isomorphic”. This study involves the contact equivalence of maps with singular targets, which seems to be not well-established. We write down the relevant part of this theory.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Consider mapping-germs
{\rm Maps}\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right).
Here
\Bbbk
is an arbitrary field,
(\Bbbk^{n},o)
is the (algebraic, resp. analytic, formal) germ of the affine space.
See §
2
for notations and conventions.

These mapping-germs are traditionally studied up to the right (
\mathscr{R}
), left-right (
{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}
or
\mathscr{A}
), and contact (
{\mathscr{K}}\!
) equivalences. The case of analytic maps,

{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{R}^{n},o),\!(\mathbb{R}^{m},o)\right)\!
and
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{C}^{n},o),\!(\mathbb{C}^{m},o)\right)\!,
was intensively studied through 20’th century, see
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
]
.

Many results of
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!
-equivalences were extended (in numerous works) from
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
to arbitrary fields,
see Chapter 3 of the second edition of
[
Gr.Lo.Sh
]
. The extension of
{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}
-equivalence to arbitrary fields was done in
[
Kerner.24
]
.
Below
\mathscr{G}
is one of the groups
{\mathscr{L}},\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},

{\mathscr{C}},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
see §
3.1
.

The classical approach is: first to study the complex-analytic situation, then to deduce results in the real-analytic case.
Hence the old question is to relate the real and complex equivalence of real map-germs. Suppose
f,{\tilde{f}}\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{R}^{n},o),\!(\mathbb{R}^{m},o)\right)\!
are
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
-equivalent, i.e. their complexifications are equivalent as elements of
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{C}^{n},o),\!(\mathbb{C}^{m},o)\right)\!.
This does not imply their
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivalence. The simplest example is the Morse critical point,
\sum(\pm)x^{2}_{i}
. Indeed, the real right equivalence,
\mathscr{R}_{\mathbb{R}},
preserves the signature of this quadratic form,
(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{-}).
(Thus e.g.
\sum x^{2}_{i}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\not\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{R}_{\mathbb{R}}}}$}\ \!-\sum x^{2}_{i}.
) The real contact equivalence,
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{\mathbb{R}},
can swap the signs of all the eigenvalues,

(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{-})\leftrightarrow(\lambda_{-},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{+}).
But the difference
|\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}|
is still preserved.
However one might guess:

“The real and complex equivalence of real mapping-germs can differ only at low orders.”

Namely, if
{\tilde{f}}=g_{\mathbb{C}}f,
where the element
g_{\mathbb{C}}\!\in\!\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
is identity modulo higher order terms, then
f\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{R}}}}$}\ \!{\tilde{f}}.

Even if one studies
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{C}^{n},o),\!(\mathbb{C}^{m},o)\right)\!,
occasionally one has to extend the base-field. For example, some proofs go via deformation arguments, i.e. additional parameters are introduced,
\mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}(t)=:\mathbbm{K}.
Then, having established the
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
-equivalence, one would like to deduce the
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
-equivalence. The natural guess is: “This should work, because
\mathbb{C}
is algebraically closed”.

1.2.
The results

​We convert these guesses into stronger statements in more general situation:

•

For
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
whose source
X\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{n},o)
and target
Y\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
are (formal or analytic or algebraic) germs over a ring
\Bbbk,
and with arbitrary singularities.

•

For a large class of ring extensions
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}.
(In particular for arbitrary field extensions.)

The precise statement is in Theorem
5.1
, for faithfully flat ring extensions. Now we give just the conclusion for (arbitrary) field extensions
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}.

1.

(For
char(\Bbbk)=0
)  If
{\tilde{f}}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}}}$}\ \!f
by an element whose linear part is unipotent, then

{\tilde{f}}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}}}$}\ \!f.

2.

Suppose the base-field is algebraically closed, of any characteristic.

If
{\tilde{f}}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}}}$}\ \!f
then

{\tilde{f}}\!\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}}}$}\ \!\!f.

Part 1 is sharp, as we show by explicit examples in §
5.1.1
. While part 2 is “naturally expected”, part 1 seems “a bit unexpected”. (At least for some people in Singularities.)

Below we list some corollaries of Theorem
5.1
and other results.

•

Working over base rings (not just base fields) is important for deformations and unfoldings. E.g. it implies,
Corollary
5.6
, for
char(\Bbbk)=0
:

Let
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
be any extension of fields. If a family
f_{t}
is
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
-trivial, then it is also
\mathscr{G}
-trivial.

(Roughly: the
\Bbbk
-part of the
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
-constant stratum locally coincides with the
\mathscr{G}_{\Bbbk}
-constant stratum.)
This seems to be new even in the classical case of
\mathbb{R}\!\subset\!\mathbb{C}.

Example
5.7
.

f\in{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
is stable as a
\Bbbk
-map if and only if it is stable as a
\mathbbm{K}
-map.

•

The “
\Bbbk
vs
\mathbbm{K}
question” is finitely determined. Namely, if two maps
f,{\tilde{f}}\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
are
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
-equivalent, then their
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence can be verified at a finite jet. (Here
f,{\tilde{f}}
are not assumed finitely determined.)
More precisely, Corollary
5.9
reads in
char=0
:

1.

Every map
f\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
satisfies:
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}f\cap(\{f\}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{N}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X})\!\subset\!\mathscr{G}f
for some
N\!\in\!\mathbb{N}.

2.

Moreover, this number
N
can be chosen uniformly for the whole
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
-orbit of
f
.

The smallest such

is of course crucial for various applications, but the proof is based on Artin’s
\beta
-function, for which not many bounds are known.

•

Though the
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
-equivalence does not imply the
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence, we have at least the finiteness of
\mathscr{G}
-orbits.

Corollary
5.10
.

The real part of the complex orbit,
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{C}}f\cap{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right),
splits into a
finite
number of real orbits,
\amalg\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\alpha}.

In fact Corollary
5.10
is for finite extensions of “fields of type
F
”, e.g.
\mathbb{R}
or
p
-adic fields.

•

The classical group-actions on spaces of map-germs,
\mathscr{G}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\left(X,\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right),
for
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},\mathscr{R},

{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{C}},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
are well-studied.
Maps with singular targets,
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right),
were studied only in rather particular cases, e.g.
[

]
,
[
Nuñ.-Ball. Tomazella
]
.
We could not find the general references for the actions

.

Giving the full exposition of this topic “
\mathscr{G}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)

We define the tangent spaces to the groups at unit element,

In full analogy with the classical “Lie-algebra action on manifolds” one gets the action

For each map we get the

Specifying a filtration on

•

Two auxiliary tools are obtained: the

The germs of spaces

The source and the target of our maps,

•

X\!=\!\rm{Spec}(R_{X})

R_{X}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

is one of the rings
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(J_{X}+J_{Y})$}\right.},

R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},
the base ring
\Bbbk
is either a field, or
\Bbbk={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk_{o}[\![t]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.},

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},
either
\Bbbk

\Bbbk={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk_{o}\{t\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.},

\Bbbk_{o}
is a normed field.

1
1

1

that preserve the ideal

Namely, the following sequence is exact:

(2.1)

0\to J_{X}\cdot Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}\to Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}Log(J_{X})\to Der_{X}\to 0,

where
Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}Log(J_{X}):=\{\xi\!\in\!Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}|\ \xi(J_{X})\!\subseteq\!J_{X}\}.

The
R_{X}
-module
Der_{X}
is finitely-generated. Indeed, it is the quotient of the module
Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}Log(J_{X}),
the latter being finitely generated by
Remark
2.1
.

Applying the module of derivations to an element
f\in R_{X}
we get the ideal
Der_{X}(f)\!\subseteq\!R_{X}.
For an element
f\in R^{\oplus m}_{X}
we get the submodule

Der_{X}(f)\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

2.3.
Maps of scheme-germs

A (formal/analytic/Nash) map
f\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
is defined by the corresponding homomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras,
f^{\sharp}\!\in\!Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X}).
All our homomorphisms are local.

•

In the case
J_{Y}=0
one has maps to a
\Bbbk
-smooth target,
{\rm Maps}\left(X,\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right).
We write this explicitly.
With the chosen generators
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\!\subset\!R_{Y}
each map
X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ \!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
is presented by
a tuple of (formal/analytic/algebraic) power series,
f:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\!\in\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Vice versa, every element of
{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}
defines a map
X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ \!Y.
This identifies the space of maps,

{\rm Maps}\left(X,\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right)={\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

•

For an arbitrary target
Y\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o),
with
R_{Y}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![y]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle y\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
a map
X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ \!Y
is still represented by
an element
f\!\in\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.
But now the condition
f^{\sharp}\!\in\!Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X})
means:
f^{\sharp}(J_{Y})\!\subseteq\!J_{X}\!\subset\!R_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}.

This embeds the space of maps as a subset:

(2.2)

{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)=Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X})=\{f\!\in\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}\ |\quad f^{\sharp}(J_{Y})\!\subseteq\!J_{X}\}.

For not
\Bbbk
-smooth targets the subset
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)\!\subset\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\!\cdot\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}
is not closed under addition.

2.4.
Filtrations on
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)

Take a filtration by
\Bbbk
-submodules
R^{\oplus m}_{X}\!=\!\tilde{M}^{0}\!\supset\!\tilde{M}^{1}\!\supset\!\cdots.

Suppose this filtration is equivalent to the filtration
I^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X},
for some ideal
I\!\subset\!R_{X}.

Namely:

(2.3)

\tilde{M}^{j}\!\supset\!I^{d_{j}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}\quad\text{ and }\quad I^{j}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}\!\supset\!\tilde{M}^{d_{j}},\quad\text{ for each }j\text{ and a corresponding }d_{j}\gg j.

Take the corresponding filtration on the space of maps,
M^{\bullet}:={\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)\cap\tilde{M}^{\bullet}.

Explicitly:
M^{j}=\{f\!\in\!\tilde{M}^{j}|\ f^{\sharp}(J_{Y})\!\subseteq\!J_{X}\}.

Example 2.3
.

​​
i.

For
\Bbbk
-smooth targets,
Y\!=\!(\Bbbk^{m},o),
we get just the submodules
M^{j}\!=\!\tilde{M}^{j}\!\subset\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

​​
ii.

For any
Y\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
and
\tilde{M}^{\bullet}={\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}\!\cdot\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}
one has:

M^{j}={\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}\!\cdot\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}=\{f\in Hom(R_{Y},R_{X})\ |\quad f({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y})\!\subseteq\!{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}_{X}\}.

The
M^{\bullet}
-order of maps is defined in the standard way,
ord(f):=sup\{d|\ f\!\in\!M^{d}\}.

In many applications one needs just the filtration
I^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.
However we work with the slightly more general version,
\tilde{M}^{\bullet},
as it is used in the proof of Corollary
5.5
.
In particular, we do not assume
\tilde{M}^{1}\!\subseteq\!{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

2.5.
The tangent space to
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)

In §
3.4
we work with tangent spaces of various groups of equivalence. Hence we need the tangent space to
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
at an element
f,
denote it
T_{(Maps,f)}.
It is obtained in the standard way,
[
Hartshorne
, pg.80]
.
Fix a set of generators
(q_{1},\dots,q_{r})
of
J_{Y}\!\subset\!R_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)},
these are power series. Take the matrix of their derivatives
[q^{\prime}_{\bullet}]\!\in\!Mat_{r\times m}(R_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)}).
Evaluate its entries at
f,
we get the matrix
[q^{\prime}_{\bullet}|_{y=f}]\!\in\!Mat_{r\times m}(R_{X}).

Then

(2.4)

T_{(Maps,f)}:=\{v|\quad[q^{\prime}_{\bullet}|_{f}]\cdot v=0\in R^{\oplus r}_{X}\}=ker\big[R^{\oplus m}_{X}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{[q^{\prime}_{\bullet}|_{f}]}}$}\ \!R^{\oplus r}_{X}\big]\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

By the direct check,
T_{(Maps,f)}\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}
is an
R_{X}
-submodule, and it does not depend on the choice of generators
q_{\bullet}.

By Remark
2.1
this submodule is finitely generated.

Example 2.4
.

i.

For
J_{Y}=0,
i.e.
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
we get:
T_{(Maps,f)}=R^{\oplus m}_{X},
a free module of rank
m.
In particular, this tangent space is independent of
f.

ii.

For any ideal
0\neq J_{Y}\!\subseteq\!(y)^{2},
and the constant map,
f=0,
we get
T_{(Maps,0)}=R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

For non-constant maps we get in general
T_{(Maps,f)}\!\subsetneq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

2.6.
Faithfully flat extensions, see e.g.
[
Gr.Lo.Sh
, Appendix B]

An extension of rings,
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K},
is called faithfully-flat (f.f.) if the following conditions hold for any (not necessarily finitely-generated)
\Bbbk
-modules:

•

(faithfulness) If
V\neq 0
then
\mathbbm{K}\otimes V\neq 0.

•

(flatness) If the sequence
0\to V\to U\to W\to 0
is exact, then the sequence
0\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes V\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes U\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes W\to 0
is exact.

Example 2.5
.

i.

For
\Bbbk
a field, any ring extension
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
is f.f.

ii.

For any ring
\Bbbk
the extension
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\Bbbk[\![x]\!]
is f.f.
And similarly for
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\Bbbk\{x\}
and
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\Bbbk\langle x\rangle.

iii.

For any field extension
\Bbbk_{o}\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}_{o}
the extension of rings
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk_{o}[\![t]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.}\!\subset\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\mathbbm{K}_{o}[\![t]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.}
is f.f.

Take a f.f. extension of rings
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}.
Then the natural map
V\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes V,
by
v\to 1\otimes v,
is an embedding.
Below we identify
V
with its image
1\otimes V\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}\otimes V.
A
\Bbbk
-submodule
V\!\subset\!W
leads to a
\mathbbm{K}
-submodule
\mathbbm{K}\otimes V\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}\otimes W.
We get the obvious inclusion
(\mathbbm{K}\otimes V)\cap W\supseteq V.

Lemma 2.6
.

If the extension
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
is faithfully-flat, then
(\mathbbm{K}\!\otimes\!V)\!\cap\!W\!=\!V
.

This is used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Proof.
Denote
W^{\prime}:=(\mathbbm{K}\otimes V)\cap W,
and consider the exact sequence

0\to V\to W^{\prime}\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$W^{\prime}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$V$}\right.}\to 0.
Applying
\mathbbm{K}\otimes
we get the exact sequence

0\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes V\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes W^{\prime}\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$W^{\prime}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$V$}\right.}\to 0,

by flatness.
Here the map
\mathbbm{K}\otimes V\to\mathbbm{K}\otimes W^{\prime}
is an isomorphism. Therefore

\mathbbm{K}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$W^{\prime}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$V$}\right.}=0.
The faithfulness of
\mathbbm{K}\otimes
gives then:
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$W^{\prime}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$V$}\right.}=0.

\blacksquare

3.1.
The groups
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{C}},{\mathscr{K}}\!

The (formal/
\Bbbk
-analytic/
\Bbbk
-Nash) automorphisms of
X
over the base
\rm{Spec}(\Bbbk)
are defined via their algebraic counterparts,
\Bbbk
-linear automorphisms of the structure ring,
Aut_{X}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X}).

Similarly we denote
Aut_{Y}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y}).

Example 3.1
.

i.

In the
\Bbbk
-smooth case,
{\rm Maps}\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right),
these automorphisms are the coordinate changes in the source and the target.

ii.

In the general case any automorphism
\Phi_{X}\in Aut_{X}
is presentable as a coordinate change of the ambient germ
(\Bbbk^{n},o)
that preserves the source
X\!\subset\!(\Bbbk^{n},o).
And similarly for
\Phi_{Y}\in Aut_{Y}.

These automorphisms act on maps, defining the standard
group actions of Singularity Theory:

•

(the right group)
\mathscr{R}\!:=Aut_{X}\!:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X}).
The action
\mathscr{R}\!\circlearrowright\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
goes by
(\Phi_{X},f)\!\to\!f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}.

•

(the left group)
{\mathscr{L}}\!:=Aut_{Y}\!:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y}).
The action
{\mathscr{L}}\!\circlearrowright\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
goes by
(\Phi_{Y},f)\!\to\!\Phi_{Y}\circ f
.

•

(the left-right or
\mathscr{A}
-group) The action
{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}\!:={\mathscr{L}}\times\mathscr{R}\!\circlearrowright\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
goes by
(\Phi_{Y},\Phi_{X},f)\to\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}.

•

(the
y
-part of the contact group) The subgroup
{\mathscr{C}}<Aut_{X\times Y}
consists of automorphisms of
X\times Y
that preserve the fibres of the projection
X\times Y\to X

and preserve “the zero-level slice”
X\times\{o\}\!\subset\!X\times Y.
Therefore
{\mathscr{C}}
acts as
Id_{X}
on
X\times\{o\},

and restricts to automorphisms of the central fibre
\{o\}\times Y.

Explicitly, fix some generators
J_{Y}\!=\!\langle q_{\bullet}(y)\rangle\!\subset\!R_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)},
then the action
{\mathscr{C}}\!\circlearrowright\!X\!\times\!Y
is presentable by
(x,y)\!\to\!(x,C(x,y)),

where
q_{\bullet}(C(x,y))\!\in\!R_{X\times Y}\cdot J_{Y},
and
C(x,y)\!=\!\Phi_{Y}(y)+(\dots),
with
\Phi_{Y}\!\in\!Aut_{Y}
and
(\dots)\!\in\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}\!\cdot\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}.

The action of this group on maps,
{\mathscr{C}}\!\circlearrowright\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right),
is defined via the graph, by
f(x)\!\rightsquigarrow\!C(x,f(x)).

•

The contact group is the semi-direct product
{\mathscr{K}}\!:={\mathscr{C}}\rtimes Aut_{X}.
Note that
{\mathscr{C}}\lhd{\mathscr{K}}\!,
a normal subgroup.
The action
{\mathscr{K}}\!\circlearrowright X\times Y
goes by
(x,y)\to(\Phi_{X}(x),C(x,y)).

The action
{\mathscr{K}}\!\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
is defined via the graph of
f,
i.e.
f\rightsquigarrow C(x,f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}).

•

When the target is
\Bbbk
-smooth,
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
the contact group-action is “reduced” to its linearized version,
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin}=GL_{m}(R_{X})\rtimes Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X}).
Namely, the orbits of the two groups coincide:
{\mathscr{K}}\!f={\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin}f.

See
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
, Proposition 4.2]
for
f\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right)
with
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},
and
[
Kerner.24
, §3.1]
for
f\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\left(X,\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right).

When the target is singular,
Y\!\subsetneq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o),
the reduction of
{\mathscr{K}}\!
to
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin}
is more subtle, see
[
Kerner.prep
, §2]
.

Below
\mathscr{G}
is one of these groups,
{\mathscr{L}},\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{C}},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
and we get the traditional notions of (left, right, left-right, contact) equivalence of maps,
f\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}}}$}\ \!{\tilde{f}}.

3.4.

\boldsymbol{T_{\mathscr{G}}\times{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)\to T(\rm{Maps})}

3.6.
The exponential and logarithmic maps
T_{\mathscr{G}}\rightleftarrows\mathscr{G}

Let
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.}
and
R_{Y}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![y]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
for
\Bbbk\!\supseteq\!\mathbb{Q}.
(Thus
char(\Bbbk)=0.
)
The relation between the groups and their tangent spaces is given by the standard exponential/logarithmic maps. Namely:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{C}},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
then
exp,ln:T_{\mathscr{G}^{(j)}}\rightleftarrows\mathscr{G}^{(j)}
for
j\geq 1.

The exponential/logarithmic maps of filtration-unipotent operators are well known, e.g.
§III.5.4 of
[
Bourbaki.Lie
]
or pg.27 of
[
Serre.Lie
]
.
The lemma is well-known for
{\rm Maps}\left(X,\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right),
with the filtration
I^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X},
and the groups
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
see e.g.
[
Bel.-Ker.
, §3.5]
and
[
B.G.K.22
, Theorem 3.6]
. We need this result for singular targets,
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right).
Therefore we just add the needed ingredients in our more general situation.

Proof.
It is enough to verify the statement for the groups
{\mathscr{L}}^{(j)},

\mathscr{R}^{(j)},

{\mathscr{C}}^{(j)}.
Then the case of composite groups,
({\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R})^{(j)},

{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(j)}
follows.

\boldsymbol{exp}\!:T_{\mathscr{G}^{(j)}}\to\mathscr{G}^{(j)}.
For every
\xi\in T_{\mathscr{G}^{(j)}}
the action of the operator
exp(\xi)\circlearrowright R^{\oplus m}_{X}
is well defined. Thus it is enough to verify:
exp(\xi)
acts on the subset
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
and is unipotent for the filtration
\tilde{M}^{\bullet}\!\subset\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

The part
exp(\xi)\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
is verified case-wise. Let
f\in{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right),
then:

•

\mathscr{R}\!:

exp(\xi_{X})\in Aut_{X},
therefore
exp(\xi_{X})(f(x))=f(exp(\xi_{X})(x))\in{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right).

•

{\mathscr{L}}\!:

exp(\xi_{Y})\in Aut_{Y},
therefore
exp(\xi_{Y})(f)\in{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right).

•

{\mathscr{C}}\!:
here
\xi(J_{Y})\!\subseteq\!R_{X\times Y}\cdot J_{Y}=0\!\subset\!R_{X\times Y}.
Denoting

{\tilde{f}}\!:=exp(\xi)f
we get:
{\tilde{f}}^{\sharp}(J_{Y})\!\subseteq\!R_{X\times Y}\cdot J_{Y},
i.e.
{\tilde{f}}^{\sharp}\!\in\!Hom(R_{Y},R_{X}),
i.e.
{\tilde{f}}\in{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right).

Finally, the
\tilde{M}^{\bullet}
-unipotence of
exp(\xi)
is immediate,
(exp(\xi)-Id)\tilde{M}^{d}=(\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{l}}{l!})\tilde{M}^{d}\!\subseteq\!\tilde{M}^{d+j}.

\boldsymbol{ln}\!:\mathscr{G}^{(j)}\to T_{\mathscr{G}^{(j)}}.
For every
g\in\mathscr{G}^{(j)}
the action of the operator
ln(g)\circlearrowright R^{\oplus m}_{X}
is well defined. Thus it is enough to verify:
ln(g)\in T_{\mathscr{G}}
and is nilpotent for the filtration
\tilde{M}^{\bullet}\!\subset\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

•

The facts
ln(g)\in Der_{X}
for
g\in Aut_{X}^{(1)},
and
ln(g)\in Der_{Y}
for
g\in Aut_{Y}^{(1)}
are well known, see e.g.

[
Bel.-Ker.
, §3.5]
and
[
B.G.K.22
, Theorem 3.6]
.

•

For the group
{\mathscr{C}}<Aut_{X\times Y}
we denote
\xi:=ln(g).
Thus
\xi\in Der_{X\times Y},
as before. Moreover,
we have
\xi(R_{X\times Y})\!\subseteq\!R_{X\times Y}\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y},
as
g\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}\!\subset\!R_{X\times Y}\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}.
And
\xi
is
x
-linear, as
g
preserves the fibres of the projection
X\times Y\to Y.
Therefore
\xi\in R_{X\times Y}\otimes_{R_{Y}}Der_{Y}Log({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}).

Finally, the
\tilde{M}^{\bullet}
-nilpotence of

is immediate,
ln(g)\tilde{M}^{d}=-(\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{(1-g)^{l}}{l})\tilde{M}^{d}\!\subseteq\!\tilde{M}^{d+j}.

\blacksquare

4.1.
Maps of finite singularity type

This notion is used below only to quote the Artin approximation for the particular case of non-finitely determined maps, over the analytic rings
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
for the groups
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}.

A map
X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ \!Y
is called
of finite singularity type
if its restriction to the critical locus,

X\supset Crit\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{|}}}$}\ \!Y,
is a finite morphism.
In the classical case of
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{C}^{n},o),\!(\mathbb{C}^{m},o)\right)\!
this notion is classical,
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
, pg.224]
.
For the basic properties in the case
{\rm Maps}\left(X,\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right)
see

[
Kerner.24
, §3.6.2]
, for the case
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)
see
[
Kerner.25
, §4.4]
.

When

and [

or

] we assume in addition the following “lifting properties”:

•

the approximate integrability of derivations,
[
Kerner.25
, Definition 4.1]

•

and the approximate lifting of automorphisms

[

.

We do not restate them in detail, as they are not used below.

4.2.
Artin approximation for

Let

Fix two map-germs

4.2.1.
Ordinary
\mathscr{G}
-Artin approximation (“from formal equivalence to Nash/analytic equivalence”)

The verification of the
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence often goes at the level of formal power series. Namely, we work with the completions of the rings at their maximal ideals:
{\widehat{R}}_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{${\hat{\Bbbk}}[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

{\widehat{R}}_{Y}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{${\hat{\Bbbk}}[\![y]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.}.

(If
\Bbbk
is a field then
{\hat{\Bbbk}}=\Bbbk.

Then
\hat{\mathscr{G}}
is one of the corresponding groups:

(4.1)

Aut_{\hat{\Bbbk}}({\widehat{R}}_{X}),\hskip 34.14322ptAut_{\hat{\Bbbk}}({\widehat{R}}_{Y}),\hskip 34.14322ptAut_{\hat{\Bbbk}}({\widehat{R}}_{Y})\times Aut_{\hat{\Bbbk}}({\widehat{R}}_{X}),\hskip 34.14322pt\hat{\mathscr{K}}\!:=\hat{\mathscr{C}}\rtimes Aut_{\hat{\Bbbk}}({\widehat{R}}_{X}).

Having established the formal equivalence, “

In the case [
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}

(4.2)

satisfying:

\Bbbk

[

•

For the group

and for

4.2.2.

\mathscr{G}
-Artin approximation (“from order-by-order equivalence to ordinary equivalence”)

In some cases we cannot even reach the conclusion on formal power series “

We only get the equality modulo higher order terms:

(4.3)

``{\tilde{f}}\equiv g_{d}\cdot f\ mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\quad\text{ for each $d>1$ and a corresponding }g_{d}\!\in\!\mathscr{G}."

Here the sequence of group-elements

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}

{\tilde{f}}=(\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}g_{d})f

\mathscr{G}f

{\mathfrak{m}}

\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}\!\cdot\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

{\tilde{f}}\!\in\!\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\!\cdot\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}
for each

Then
{\tilde{f}}\!\in\!\cap_{d}(\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\!\cdot\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}),
the latter being the filtration closure
of the orbit
\mathscr{G}f\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Let

\mathscr{G}^{(\bullet)}<\mathscr{G}.

In the case [
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}
and the map is formal or analytic, and not finitely
\mathscr{G}
-determined] we assume:

(4.4)

the base field is
\aleph_{0}
-complete, and, in the analytic case,
f
is of finite singularity type.

Examples of
\aleph_{0}
-complete fields are: finite fields and uncountable fields that are algebraically closed. The fields

are not

\aleph_{0}

[

Theorem 4.2
.

[

The orbits
\mathscr{G}f

Namely:
\cap_{d}(\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X})=\mathscr{G}f

,
\{C^{(y)}_{m,l}\}

\Bbbk_{o}

\Phi_{Y}\circ f={\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi_{X},

\Bbbk_{o}

\mathfrak{P}\!\subset\!\Bbbk_{o}[C^{(x)}_{**},C^{(y)}_{**},z_{x},z_{y}].

\mathbbm{K}
.

\Bbbk_{o}
.

\Bbbk_{o}.

\Bbbk_{o}
.
By Hilbert Nullstellensatz we get: the defining ideal of this subscheme is the whole polynomial ring of this affine space.
But then the ideal
\mathfrak{P}
contains

, hence the whole system cannot have

\mathbbm{K}
.

•

(The case of the whole group

\Phi_{Y,N}(y)
be power series corresponding to this solution. (We assign arbitrary values to the constants
\{C^{(x)}_{m,l}\}_{|m|+|l|>N},\{C^{(y)}_{m,l}\}_{|m|+|l|>N},
e.g. zeros.)

\Phi_{Y,N}\circlearrowright(\Bbbk^{m},o).

Altogether, we get an element

Therefore

where

Moreover, for any

Now invoke the strong-

-Artin approximation, Theorem

, to get

•

The case of the groups

for

is done by a slight modification of the argument. We are looking for an element

).

as in (

) are
converted into equations in

These equations are polynomial. As before, we get an approximate solution

i.e.

(5.11)

\Phi_{Y}{\tilde{f}}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\equiv}\limits^{mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}}$}\ \!\Phi_{X}f,\quad\Phi_{Y}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\in}\limits^{mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}}$}\ \!{\mathscr{L}}^{(j)},\quad\Phi_{X}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\in}\limits^{mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}}$}\ \!\mathscr{R}^{(j)}.

By approximating

Finally invoke the Strong Artin approximation again, to get

\blacksquare

5.2.
The criterion for equivalence with unipotent linear part

5.2.1.

Recall that a linear operator on a vector space,
\Phi\circlearrowright V,
is called unipotent if the operator
\Phi-Id\circlearrowright V
is nilpotent.
The
unipotent filtration of
\Phi
is defined as

(5.12)

V_{0}:=V\supset V_{1}:=Im(\Phi-Id)\supset V_{2}:=Im(\Phi-Id)^{2}\supset\cdots

Thus
\Phi
preserves all the subspaces
V_{\bullet},
and acts as
Id
on all the quotients
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$V_{\bullet}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$V_{\bullet+1}$}\right.}.

5.2.2.

Let
\Bbbk
be an arbitrary field, and take the germs of spaces,
X=Spec(R_{X}),

Y=Spec(R_{Y}).
(We assume
J_{X}\!\subseteq\!(x)^{2}
and
J_{Y}\!\subseteq\!(y)^{2}
.)
Take their “first jets”,

(5.13)

jet_{1}(X):=Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(x)^{2}$}\right.})\cong\Bbbk^{n},\hskip 28.45274ptjet_{1}(Y):=Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{Y}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(y)^{2}$}\right.})\cong\Bbbk^{m}.

Accordingly we have the projection
{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)\to{\rm Maps}(jet_{1}(X),jet_{1}(Y))\cong Hom_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk^{n},\Bbbk^{m}).

For each transformation
g\in\mathscr{G}
we take its linear part,
jet_{1}(g)\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}(jet_{1}(X),jet_{1}(Y)).

This gives the homomorphism of groups:

(5.14)

\mathscr{R}\to jet_{1}(\mathscr{R})\leq GL_{\Bbbk}(n),\quad{\mathscr{L}}\to jet_{1}({\mathscr{L}})\leq GL_{\Bbbk}(m),\quad{\mathscr{C}}\to jet_{1}({\mathscr{C}})\leq GL_{\Bbbk}(m).

Similarly:
{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}\to jet_{1}({\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R})\leq GL_{\Bbbk}(n)\times GL_{\Bbbk}(m)
and

{\mathscr{K}}\!\to jet_{1}({\mathscr{K}}\!)\leq GL_{\Bbbk}(n)\times GL_{\Bbbk}(m).

Definition 5.4
.

1.

An element
g\in{\mathscr{C}}
is called unipotent if the linear operator
jet_{1}(g)\circlearrowright Hom_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk^{n},\Bbbk^{m})
is unipotent.
Take the subset of all the unipotent elements
\mathscr{G}^{unip}\!\subset\!\mathscr{G}
.

2.

For any field extension,
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K},
denote by
(\mathscr{G}^{unip})_{\mathbbm{K}}\!\subset\!\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
the subset of all the unipotent elements,
whose unipotent filtrations (on
Hom_{\mathbbm{K}}(\mathbbm{K}^{n},\mathbbm{K}^{m})
) are induced from
\Bbbk,
i.e. are of the form
\mathbbm{K}\otimes_{\Bbbk}V_{\bullet}.

Observe that subsets
\mathscr{G}^{unip}\!\subset\!\mathscr{G}
and
(\mathscr{G}^{unip})_{\mathbbm{K}}\!\subset\!\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
are not necessarily subgroups.
In addition
(\mathscr{G}^{unip})_{\mathbbm{K}}\!\subseteq\!(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}})^{unip},
and the inclusion is often proper.

5.2.3.

Let
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
be an arbitrary extension of fields of char=0, e.g.
\mathbb{Q}\!\subset\!\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}\!\subset\!\mathbb{C}.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be one of the groups
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{C}},{\mathscr{K}}\!.

In the case [
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R},
and
f
is analytic, and not finitely-
\mathscr{G}
-determined] we assume:
f
is of finite singularity type.

Corollary 5.5
.

If
{\tilde{f}}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{(\mathscr{G}^{unip})_{\mathbbm{K}}}}$}\ \!f
then
{\tilde{f}}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}^{unip}}}$}\ \!f.

Proof.
Suppose
{\tilde{f}}=g_{\mathbbm{K}}\cdot f,
for a unipotent element
g_{\mathbbm{K}}\in(\mathscr{G}^{unip})_{\mathbbm{K}}.

Thus the operator
jet_{1}(g_{\mathbbm{K}})\circlearrowright Hom_{\mathbbm{K}}(\mathbbm{K}^{n},\mathbbm{K}^{m})=:\!V
is unipotent with respect to the filtration
\mathbbm{K}\otimes V_{\bullet},
for some filtration

({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(x)$}\right.})^{\oplus m}=:\!V_{0}\supset V_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq V_{m\cdot n}=0,
by
\Bbbk
-vector spaces.
Using the projection
R_{X}\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\pi}}$}\ \!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(x)$}\right.}
we lift this filtration to
R^{\oplus m}_{X}:

(5.15)

\tilde{M}^{0}:=R^{\oplus m}_{X}\supseteq\tilde{M}^{1}:=\pi^{-1}V_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\tilde{M}^{mn-1}:=\pi^{-1}V_{mn-1}\supseteq

\supset\tilde{M}^{mn}:=(x)\cdot\tilde{M}^{0}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\tilde{M}^{2mn-1}:=(x)\cdot\tilde{M}^{mn-1}\supset\tilde{M}^{2mn}:=(x)^{2}\cdot\tilde{M}^{0}\supseteq\cdots

Thus
(x)\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}\!\subseteq\!\tilde{M}^{j}\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}
for
0\leq j\leq mn.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
the quotient
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\tilde{M}^{j}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\tilde{M}^{j+1}$}\right.}
is a vector space over
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(x)$}\right.},
of
dim=1.

And this filtration is equivalent to
(x)^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Finally we claim:
g_{\mathbbm{K}}\in\mathscr{G}^{(1)}_{\mathbbm{K}}
for the filtration
\mathbbm{K}\otimes\tilde{M}^{\bullet}.
Indeed, present
g_{\mathbbm{K}}
by a power series, as in (
5.9
). The linear part
jet_{1}(g_{\mathbbm{K}})
is
V_{\bullet}
-unipotent by the assumption. And the terms of order
\geq 2
are unipotent as well.

Finally we invoke part 1 of Theorem
5.1
, and the statement follows.

\blacksquare

Step 2.

X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ \!Y.
For [
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}
and
f
-analytic] we assume
f
is of finite singularity type.
Complexifying all the objects we get the complex orbit,
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{C}}f\!\subset\!{\rm Maps}(X_{\mathbb{C}},Y_{\mathbb{C}}).
Take its real part,

\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{C}}f\cap{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right).
It splits into a finite disjont union of the real orbits.

Remark 5.12
.

One would like to have the finite splitting
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}f\cap{\rm Maps}\left(X,Y\right)=\amalg_{finite}\mathscr{G}f_{\alpha}
for more general field extensions, removing the assumptions on
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
in Corollary
5.10
.

i.

For non-finite field extensions this finiteness fails trivially. Let
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}\!\subseteq\!{\bar{\Bbbk}},
where the extension
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
is not finite.
(E.g.
\mathbb{Q}\!\subset\!\mathbb{R}
.) Fix a sequence of elements
c_{\bullet}\!\in\!\mathbbm{K}
satisfying:
\frac{c^{2}_{i}}{c^{2}_{j}}\neq\Bbbk
for all
i\neq j.

Take
f(x)=x^{2}.
All the maps
c^{2}_{j}\cdot x^{2}
are
\mathscr{R}_{\mathbbm{K}}
equivalent to
x^{2},
but pairwise not
\mathscr{R}_{\Bbbk}
-equivalent.

ii.

When the extension
\Bbbk\!\subset\!\mathbbm{K}
is finite, but
\Bbbk
is not “of type
F
”, the statement still fails, i.e.
the
\Bbbk
-part of a
G_{\mathbbm{K}}
-orbit can split into infinite number of
G_{\Bbbk}
-orbits.

The following example was given by Mikhail Borovoi.
Let
\Bbbk
be a field of
char(\Bbbk)=0,
and assume
\sqrt{-1}\not\in\Bbbk.
Define the action
SL(2,\Bbbk)\circlearrowright Mat^{sym}_{2\times 2}(\Bbbk)\cap SL(2,\Bbbk)

by
(u,s)\to usu^{T}.
Take the field extension
\mathbbm{K}=\Bbbk(\sqrt{-1}).

•

We claim:
SL(2,\mathbbm{K})
has only one orbit in
Mat^{sym}_{2\times 2}(\mathbbm{K})\cap SL(2,\mathbbm{K}).

Indeed, the stabilizer of
{1\hskip-2.84544pt\rm I}
is the subgroup
SO(2,\Bbbk)<SL(2,\Bbbk).

This is a 1-dimensional
\Bbbk
-torus splitting over
\mathbbm{K}.

In other words
SO(2,\Bbbk)\simeq\mathbbm{K}^{*},
the multiplicative group of
\mathbbm{K}.

For any field extension
\Bbbk\!\subset\!L

the set of orbits of
SL(2,L)
in
Mat^{sym}_{2\times 2}(L)\cap SL(2,L)
is in a canonical bijection with the kernel

ker[H^{1}(L,SO(2))\to H^{1}(L,SL(2))]
.
This fundamental fact is
[
Serre.Galois
]
, Section I.5.4, page 50, Corollary 1 of Proposition 36.

For
L=\mathbbm{K}
, we have
H^{1}(\mathbbm{K},SO(2))=H^{1}(\mathbbm{K},\mathbbm{K}^{*})={1}
.
This is Hilbert’s Theorem 90, e.g.
[
Serre.Fields
]

Section X.1, page 150, Proposition 2.
Thus over
\mathbbm{K}
we have only one orbit, i.e.
SL(2,\mathbbm{K})
acts on
Mat^{sym}_{2\times 2}(\mathbbm{K})\cap SL(2,\mathbbm{K})
transitively.

•

The set of orbits of
SL(2,\Bbbk)\circlearrowright Mat^{sym}_{2\times 2}(\Bbbk)\cap SL(2,\Bbbk)
is in a canonical bijection with
H^{1}(\mathbbm{K}/\Bbbk,SO(2)):=H^{1}(Gal(\mathbbm{K}/\Bbbk),SO(2,\mathbbm{K})),
see
[
Serre.Galois
]
, Section I.5.4, page 50, Corollary 1 of Proposition 36.
Here
Gal(\mathbbm{K}/\Bbbk)
is the group of order 2, whose non-trivial element
\tau
acts on
SO(2,\mathbbm{K})\simeq\mathbbm{K}^{\times}
by sending
x\in\mathbbm{K}^{\times}
to
{}^{\tau}\!x^{-1}
, where
x\mapsto{}^{\tau}\!x
is the standard action of
\tau
on
\mathbbm{K}
.

As
SO(2)
is an abelian group, we can use the theory of abelian Galois cohomology.
By definition,
H^{1}(Gal(\mathbbm{K}/\Bbbk),SO(2,\Bbbk))=\Bbbk^{\times}/Nm(\mathbbm{K}^{\times}),
where the norm map acts by

Nm(a+b\sqrt{-1})=a^{2}+b^{2}\!\in\!\Bbbk.

See
[
Serre.Galois
]
, Section I.2.2, page 10.

Finally (for
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R}
) the group
\mathbb{R}^{\times}/Nm(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is of order 2.
But (for
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Q}
) the group
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/Nm(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})^{\times})
is infinite. E.g. let
p
be a prime of the form
4n+3.

Then
p
is not a sum of two squares in
\mathbb{Z}
, because
3\ mod\ 4
is not a sum of two squares in
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore
p
is not a sum of two squares in
\mathbb{Q}.

This gives infinitely many non-equivalent symmetric matrices in
Mat^{sym}_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{Q}).

5.3.4.
A global implication

Below we work with graded rings,
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[x]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

R_{Y}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[y]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
where
J_{X},J_{Y}
are generated by homogeneous polynomials.
Take a non-zero graded map
X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ \!Y.
Projectivizing all the objects we get the (rational) map of projective schemes
\mathbb{P}X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\mathbb{P}f}}$}\ \!\mathbb{P}Y,
see e.g. pg.160-169 of
[
Hartshorne
]
.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be one of the groups
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}\mathscr{R}.
Recall the standard fact: if two graded maps
X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f,{\tilde{f}}}}$}\ \!Y
are formally-
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent, then they are graded-
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent. Therefore the maps
\mathbb{P}f,\mathbb{P}{\tilde{f}}
are projectively equivalent.

Part 1 of Theorem
5.1
is trivial in this case. Indeed, a graded automorphism that is unipotent (for the graded filtration) is necessarily identity. Part 2 is well-known.

But Corollary
5.6
gives the (well-known?) statement:

Corollary 5.13
.

(char=0) Take a family of projective morphisms
\mathbb{P}X\ \!\lower 1.29167pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\mathbb{P}f_{t}}}$}\ \!\mathbb{P}Y.
If this family is globally-
\mathbbm{K}
-trivial, then it is globally-
\Bbbk
-trivial.

Pathwise Patching: A Geometric Construction of Quasi-Sure Solutions to G-SDEs and the Fundamental Role of the Path Space
Guangqian Zhao

Abstract
This paper develops a rigorous geometric framework for constructing quasi-sure solutions to stochastic differential equations under model uncertainty. Departing from classical measure-specific approaches, we introduce a pathwise patching methodology that explicitly builds universal solutions directly on the path space. Our construction leverages the fundamental property of pathwise uniqueness in stochastic differential equations. We prove that when an SDE admits a unique strong solution under each probability measure in a family 𝒫 \mathscr{P} , and under appropriate average measure conditions on the family of measures, our method yields a universally measurable process that serves as the unique quasi-sure solution to the corresponding G-SDE. The key insight of our approach is that pathwise uniqueness ensures the compatibility of solutions across different probability measures, allowing them to be seamlessly patched together into a single canonical object. This work establishes that probability measures serve as filters that select plausible paths from an underlying path space reality, rather than defining the solution itself. The resulting framework provides enhanced conceptual clarity and mathematical rigor, offering a unified perspective on G-expectation theory and paving the way for robust computational methods in quantitative finance and stochastic control under model uncertainty.

1
Introduction

The theory of
G
-expectation, pioneered by Peng [1], provides a powerful framework for modeling Knightian uncertainty in stochastic systems. At its core, this theory deals with the
G
-stochastic differential equation (GSDE), which generalizes classical SDEs to settings where the probability model itself is ambiguous. While traditional existence proofs are rigorous, they often focus on arguments specific to a chosen probability measure. Our work aims to complement these approaches by enhancing the inherent geometric intuition of the quasi-sure construction.

This paper explores the perspective that the path space itself can be regarded as the fundamental domain upon which stochastic objects may be defined, while probability measures may serve as lenses focusing on different aspects of this reality. This path-centric viewpoint forms the core methodological principle of our approach.

Consider the path space
\Omega=C([0,T],R)
. A path
\omega\in\Omega
possesses intrinsic properties, such as its quadratic variation
\langle B\rangle_{t}(\omega)
, which is determined in a pathwise manner. However, when we endow this space with different probability measures, we effectively choose different sets of paths to focus on. For instance:
- Under measure
P
(the law of a standard Brownian motion), we focus on paths where
\langle B\rangle_{t}(\omega)=t

P
-a.s.
- Under a different measure
Q
(e.g., the law of
2W_{t}
), we focus on paths where
\langle B\rangle_{t}(\omega)=4t

Q
-a.s.

The apparent discrepancy in the observed quadratic variation is not a contradiction; it simply reflects that each measure highlights a different subset of paths. The path itself remains unchanged—only our probabilistic lens changes. This insight motivates our pathwise patching methodology: we seek to construct a universal solution directly on the path space by synthesizing the perspectives offered by each measure in a family
\mathscr{P}
.

Our approach proceeds in three steps:

1.

For each measure
P\in\mathscr{P}
, we solve the GSDE under
P
, obtaining a solution
X^{P}
defined on a set
\Omega_{P}
with
P(\Omega_{P})=1
.

2.

We observe that the solutions
X^{P}
and
X^{Q}
agree on the intersection
\Omega_{P}\cap\Omega_{Q}
due to pathwise uniqueness—a deterministic property of the paths themselves.

3.

We patch these solutions together to form a unified process
X
defined on the union
\bigcup_{P\in\mathscr{P}}\Omega_{P}
, which is quasi-surely defined.

The resulting process
X
constitutes a quasi-sure solution to the GSDE. It satisfies the equation for
P
-almost every path for every
P\in\mathscr{P}
, and it is universally measurable with respect to
\mathscr{P}
. This construction aims to make the quasi-sure property more geometrically transparent: we explicitly define the solution on the “good sets” of each measure and identify the exceptional set as the complement of these sets.

This work attempts to offer insights in the following aspects:

•

Geometric Intuition:
The quasi-sure property can be visualized through the union of good sets and the polar exceptional set.

•

Pathwise Perspective:
The solution emerges as an intrinsic object on the path space, independent of any specific probabilistic lens.

•

Computational Implications:
This perspective may suggest numerical schemes that approximate the quasi-sure solution by sampling under different measures.

The proof of our main result rests on two pillars:

1.

Pathwise Consistency:
The solution
X_{t}(\omega)
is determined solely by the path
\omega
and the equation’s coefficients, ensuring consistency across measures.

2.

Universal Measurability:
The process
X
is measurable with respect to the universally completed
\sigma
-algebra, making it a well-defined stochastic process under every
P\in\mathscr{P}
.

This measure-independent nature provides the underlying consistency for our construction and accounts for the efficacy of the pathwise patching method. We hope this work provides a complementary foundation for interpreting advanced theories like
G
-expectation, highlighting their pathwise properties.

2
Literature Review and Theoretical Context

The pathwise patching methodology developed in this paper emerges from a rich intellectual tradition spanning multiple domains of stochastic analysis. This review situates our work within this broader context, tracing the evolution of key ideas from foundational concepts to contemporary developments.

2.1
Foundations of G-Expectation and Stochastic Analysis Under Uncertainty

The mathematical framework for handling model uncertainty was fundamentally advanced by Peng’s pioneering work on nonlinear expectation theory [1, 3, 4]. His development of the G-expectation framework provided a rigorous mathematical foundation for stochastic analysis under volatility uncertainty, replacing traditional linear expectations with sublinear expectations that can effectively capture Knightian uncertainty. The introduction of G-Brownian motion [3, 4] as the fundamental stochastic process in this framework enabled the development of stochastic integration and differential equations under model ambiguity.

The functional analytic underpinnings of this theory were further developed by Denis, Hu, and Peng [2], who established the function spaces and capacity theory related to sublinear expectation, providing crucial tools for analyzing G-Brownian motion path spaces. This work introduced concepts of quasi-sure analysis that would prove essential for handling polar sets in path spaces under multiple probability measures.

Parallel developments in stochastic analysis under model uncertainty emerged through the work of Soner, Touzi, and Zhang [8, 9], who established the framework of quasi-sure stochastic analysis through aggregation. Their approach provided a powerful methodology for unifying stochastic objects across different probability measures, demonstrating that properties holding P-almost surely for every probability measure P in a family could be robustly analyzed through aggregation techniques. The martingale representation theorem under G-expectation [8] established important connections between these different approaches to model uncertainty.

2.2
Pathspace Methods and Rough Path Theory

A complementary strand of research developed around pathwise approaches to stochastic analysis, focusing on the intrinsic properties of paths rather than their probabilistic measures. Lyons’ groundbreaking work on rough path theory [27, 41] revolutionized the study of differential equations driven by low-regularity signals, introducing concepts like path signatures and rough integrals that enabled the analysis of highly irregular paths. This theory was further developed by Friz and Victoir [43],Gubinelli[60] and Hairer’s theory of regularity structures [28, 44] provides a powerful mathematical framework for analyzing singular stochastic partial differential equations, and the work of Gubinelli, Imkeller, and Perkowski [61] employs tools from harmonic analysis to study a related class of equations using paracontrolled distributions.

In financial mathematics, Dupire’s functional Itô calculus [55] and its subsequent development by Cont and Fournié [56, 57] provided a pathwise framework for analyzing path-dependent financial derivatives. These approaches emphasized the deterministic structure underlying stochastic processes and aligned with the philosophical perspective that paths themselves represent fundamental objects of study.

The connection between these pathspace methods and quasi-sure analysis becomes particularly significant when considering that different probability measures may focus on different aspects of the same underlying pathspace reality. This synthesis forms the conceptual foundation for our pathwise patching approach, which leverages the deterministic structure of paths while acknowledging the multiplicity of probabilistic perspectives.

2.3
Connections with Classical Stochastic Analysis and Applications

The relationship between G-expectation theory and classical stochastic analysis has been extensively explored through various connecting principles. Fundamental works in classical stochastic analysis [25, 34, 35, 36] provided essential references for understanding the extensions and generalizations required in the G-framework. The development of Malliavin calculus [46, 47, 48] offered powerful tools for studying regularity properties of stochastic variables.

In financial applications, the G-expectation framework has proven particularly valuable for derivative pricing and risk management under volatility uncertainty [22, 26, 33]. The ability to capture model ambiguity makes G-expectation especially suitable for robust pricing and hedging in incomplete markets. Similarly, in stochastic control, the dynamic programming principles developed under G-expectation [19, 24] have enabled more robust approaches to optimal control under model uncertainty.

The synthesis of these diverse approaches—G-expectation theory, quasi-sure analysis, pathspace methods, and classical stochastic analysis—creates the theoretical foundation upon which our pathwise patching methodology is built. Our approach leverages the philosophical insight from pathspace methods that paths are fundamental objects, while utilizing the rigorous probabilistic framework of quasi-sure analysis to handle model uncertainty. The geometric construction we develop provides a transparent bridge between these perspectives, demonstrating how pathwise uniqueness enables the seamless integration of solutions across different probabilistic lenses.

2.4
Future Research Directions

The integration of these theoretical frameworks suggests several promising research directions. The extension of our pathwise patching methodology to path-dependent systems represents a natural next step, potentially leveraging functional Itô calculus [55, 56] to handle more complex dependency structures. Developing a robust Malliavin calculus that transcends individual probability measures remains a significant challenge, though our framework may provide new insights into this problem.

From a computational perspective, the pathwise nature of our construction suggests new numerical methods that could leverage pathwise simulation under multiple models while maintaining consistency across different probabilistic perspectives. Applications in mathematical finance, particularly for robust pricing and risk management under model uncertainty, represent immediate practical directions for implementing these theoretical advances.

The theoretical synthesis achieved through our pathwise patching approach thus not only provides a rigorous foundation for constructing quasi-sure solutions but also opens multiple avenues for future research that bridges previously distinct domains of stochastic analysis.

Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
be a measurable space, and let
\mathscr{P}
be a family of probability measures on this space. We recall the following fundamental concepts:

Definition 3.1

(Quasi-sure property)
.

A property
\pi
holds
quasi-surely
(q.s.) if there exists a set
A\in\mathscr{F}
such that
\pi
holds on
A
, and
P(A^{c})=0
for all
P\in\mathscr{P}
.

Definition 3.2

(Universally Measurable Sigma-Algebra)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
be a measurable space, and let
\mathscr{P}
be a family of probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
. For each
P\in\mathscr{P}
, let
\overline{\mathscr{F}}^{P}
denote the completion of
\mathscr{F}
with respect to
P
, defined as:

\overline{\mathscr{F}}^{P}=\{A\cup N:A\in\mathscr{F},N\subseteq B\text{ for some }B\in\mathscr{F}\text{ with }P(B)=0\}.

The
universally measurable sigma-algebra

\mathscr{F}^{U}
with respect to
\mathscr{P}
is defined as the intersection:

\mathscr{F}^{U}=\bigcap_{P\in\mathscr{P}}\overline{\mathscr{F}}^{P}.

Definition 3.3

(Universally Measurable Function)
.

A function
X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
universally measurable
with respect to
\mathscr{P}
if it is measurable with respect to
\mathscr{F}^{U}
, i.e., for every Borel set
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
X^{-1}(B)\in\mathscr{F}^{U}
.

We begin by formally defining the G-stochastic differential equation (G-SDE) in the quasi-sure framework, which is fundamental to our analysis.

Remark 3.1

(The Role of Universal Measurability)
.

In the context of a single probability measure
P
, it is standard practice to work with the
P
-completion of the sigma-algebra. This allows one to modify a process on
P
-null sets arbitrarily while preserving its measurability .

However, in our framework with a family
\mathscr{P}
of probability measures, a more stringent measurability condition is required. A set that is null under one measure
P\in\mathscr{P}
may be non-null under another
Q\in\mathscr{P}
. If a process is defined arbitrarily on a
P
-null set, it risks failing to be measurable with respect to the sigma-algebra completed by
Q
.

Universal measurability with respect to
\mathscr{P}
is precisely the condition that ensures a function remains measurable under the completion of every measure
P\in\mathscr{P}
. This guarantees that the stochastic process is well-defined and amenable to probabilistic analysis from the perspective of each and every measure in the family, which is the foundational prerequisite for any quasi-sure analysis.

Definition 3.4

(G-Stochastic Differential Equation)
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a family of probability measures on the path space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
. A
G-stochastic differential equation
is defined as an equation of the form:

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+h(t,X_{t})d\langle B\rangle_{t}+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0},

which is required to hold
\mathscr{P}
-quasi-surely (q.s.). That is, for every
P\in\mathscr{P}
, the equation holds
P
-almost surely.

Definition 3.5

(Sublinear Expectation and G-Expectation)
.

The
sublinear expectation
(or
G-expectation
) associated with the family
\mathscr{P}
is defined for any measurable functional
F
on the path space as:

\mathbb{E}^{G}[F]:=\sup_{P\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{P}[F],

where
\mathbb{E}^{P}
denotes the classical expectation under measure
P
. This sublinear expectation captures the worst-case scenario over the family of probability measures
\mathscr{P}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The G-SDE framework provides a robust approach to stochastic analysis under model uncertainty. The driving process
B_{t}
is typically assumed to be a continuous martingale under each
P\in\mathscr{P}
with absolutely continuous quadratic variation
d\langle B\rangle_{t}=\mu_{t}dt
, where
0<\underline{\sigma}\leq\mu_{t}\leq\overline{\sigma}<\infty

\mathscr{P}
-q.s. This boundedness condition ensures the well-posedness of the equations across the entire family of measures.

Remark 3.3
.

The quasi-sure formulation of G-SDEs naturally connects with the theory of sublinear expectations. Solutions to G-SDEs are processes that satisfy the equation simultaneously under all probability measures in
\mathscr{P}
, modulo polar sets. This formulation provides a mathematical foundation for analyzing stochastic systems under Knightian uncertainty, where the underlying probabilistic model is not uniquely specified.

4
The Two-Measure Case

Before proceeding with the construction, we explicitly state the fundamental assumption that underpin our framework. The condition will be assumed to hold throughout the remainder of this paper.

Assumption 1

(Well-Posedness Under Individual Measures and Their Averages)
.

For every probability measure
P
in the family
\mathscr{P}
, and for every pair
P,Q\in\mathscr{P}
, we assume that the stochastic differential equation admits a unique strong solution under:

1.

each individual measure
P\in\mathscr{P}
, and

2.

the average measure
\frac{P+Q}{2}
for every pair
P,Q\in\mathscr{P}
.

This two-level well-posedness ensures that the equation has unique strong solutions not only under each individual perspective but also under their average combinations, providing the necessary consistency for our pathwise patching construction.

Lemma 4.1

(
Strong Uniqueness Implies Pathwise Uniqueness
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P)
be a probability space and consider the stochastic differential equation:

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+h(t,X_{t})d\langle B\rangle_{t}+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t},\quad X_{0}=\xi

where
B_{t}
is a continuous martingale under
P
with quadratic variation process
\langle B\rangle_{t}
satisfying
d\langle B\rangle_{t}=\mu_{t}dt
where
0<c\leq\mu_{t}\leq C<\infty
. If this SDE admits a unique strong solution under
P
, then it also satisfies pathwise uniqueness.

Specifically, pathwise uniqueness means that there exists a set
\Omega_{P}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{P})=1
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega_{P}
, all solutions coincide. That is, the solution process is uniquely determined by the initial condition and the realization of the driving martingale path on this ”good set”
\Omega_{P}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The canonical process
B_{t}
is assumed to be a martingale under each probability measure
P\in\mathscr{P}
. This martingale property is preserved under convex combinations: for any
P,Q\in\mathscr{P}
, the process
B_{t}
remains a martingale under the average measure
\frac{P+Q}{2}
.

To see this, let
\mathbb{E}^{P}
and
\mathbb{E}^{Q}
denote expectations under
P
and
Q
respectively. For any bounded
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable random variable
Z
and
s\leq t
, we have:

\mathbb{E}^{\frac{P+Q}{2}}[B_{t}Z]=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}^{P}[B_{t}Z]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}^{Q}[B_{t}Z]=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}^{P}[B_{s}Z]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}^{Q}[B_{s}Z]=\mathbb{E}^{\frac{P+Q}{2}}[B_{s}Z],

where the second equality follows from the martingale property under
P
and
Q
. This establishes the martingale property under the average measure.

Consequently, the stochastic integral is driven by a continuous martingale whose quadratic variation is absolutely continuous and bounded between positive constants under each probability measure and under the average measure. Under such conditions, standard SDE theory (e.g., coefficients satisfying Lipschitz and linear growth conditions) guarantees the existence of a unique strong solution for each individual measure and their averages, fulfilling the requirements of Assumption
1
.

In practice, while we only require the existence of solutions under average measures for our construction, verifying the conditions typically involves treating all equations in the family as being driven by continuous martingales with absolutely continuous quadratic variation bounded between positive constants. This unified perspective does not substantially increase the complexity of verification, as the standard existence and uniqueness conditions apply uniformly across the entire family of measures.

Let
P
and
Q
be two probability measures . Consider the GSDE:

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+h(t,X_{t})d\langle B\rangle_{t}+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0}.

(4.1)

Under measure
P
, the quadratic variation
\langle B\rangle_{t}
satisfies
d\langle B\rangle_{t}=\sigma_{P}^{2}dt

P
-a.s. Similarly, under
Q
, we have
d\langle B\rangle_{t}=\sigma_{Q}^{2}dt

Q
-a.s.

For the measure

4.1
) becomes the classical SDE:

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+h(t,X_{t})\sigma_{P}^{2}dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t}.

(4.2)

By standard SDE theory , under appropriate conditions on the coefficients, there exists a unique solution
X^{P}_{t}
defined
P
-almost surely. That is, there exists a null set
N_{P}\subset\Omega
with
P(N_{P})=0
such that for all

Similarly, for measure

Example 4.2

(Classical Cases Satisfying the Assumption)
.

Our framework applies to a wide range of classical stochastic differential equations where the driving noise has absolutely continuous quadratic variation bounded between positive constants. Below are several important examples that satisfy Assumption
1
:

1.

Lipschitz coefficients with bounded volatility:
Consider coefficients
b(t,x)
,
h(t,x)
,
\sigma(t,x)
that are globally Lipschitz continuous in
x
and satisfy linear growth conditions. When the quadratic variation satisfies
0<c\leq\frac{d\langle B\rangle_{t}}{dt}\leq C<\infty
for all measures in
\mathscr{P}
, standard SDE theory guarantees the existence of a unique strong solution under each individual measure and their averages.

2.

One-dimensional SDEs with Yamada-Watanabe conditions:
For one-dimensional equations, the Yamada-Watanabe theorem provides existence and uniqueness under weaker conditions than Lipschitz continuity. If the coefficients satisfy:

•

|b(t,x)-b(t,y)|\leq K|x-y|

•

|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)|\leq\rho(|x-y|)
where
\rho
is increasing with
\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\rho^{-2}(u)du=\infty
for some
\epsilon>0

and the quadratic variation remains bounded away from 0 and infinity, then unique strong solutions exist.

3.

SDEs with monotone coefficients:
Consider coefficients satisfying the monotonicity condition:

2\langle x-y,b(t,x)-b(t,y)\rangle+\|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)\|^{2}\leq K|x-y|^{2}

and the coercivity condition:

2\langle x,b(t,x)\rangle+\|\sigma(t,x)\|^{2}\leq K(1+|x|^{2})

Under bounded quadratic variation, these conditions ensure existence and uniqueness of strong solutions.

4.

Stochastic volatility models:
In financial applications, consider models where:

dX_{t}=\mu X_{t}dt+\nu_{t}X_{t}dB_{t}

with
\nu_{t}
being an adapted process satisfying
0<c\leq\nu_{t}\leq C
. Under each measure where the quadratic variation is determined by
\nu_{t}
, strong solutions exist uniquely when
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy appropriate regularity conditions.

In all these cases, the key observation is that the boundedness and absolute continuity of the quadratic variation allow us to treat the equations as being driven by martingales with uniformly bounded characteristics. This uniform boundedness ensures that the standard existence and uniqueness theorems apply simultaneously across all measures in
\mathscr{P}
and their average combinations.

Remark 4.2

(Functional Itô Formula and Pathwise Patching)
.

The functional Itô formula provides a pathwise decomposition of stochastic processes, expressing the evolution of functionals in terms of horizontal and vertical derivatives. This pathwise characterization aligns perfectly with our geometric construction of quasi-sure solutions.

In our framework, if we consider a path-dependent SDE of the form:

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{\cdot})dt+\sigma(t,X_{\cdot})dB_{t}

where the coefficients depend on the entire path
X_{\cdot}
, the functional Itô formula establishes the pathwise dynamics of any sufficiently regular functional
F(t,X_{\cdot})
.

Crucially, when such path-dependent SDEs satisfy our fundamental assumption (existence and uniqueness of strong solutions under each individual measure and their average combinations), the pathwise nature of the functional Itô formula ensures that:

•

The dynamics are determined path-by-path, independent of the specific probability measure

•

The solutions under different measures agree on the intersection of their typical sets

•

Our pathwise patching methodology can be directly applied to construct a universal solution

This observation significantly extends the applicability of our framework to path-dependent equations, which are ubiquitous in mathematical finance and stochastic control. The combination of functional Itô calculus with our pathwise patching approach provides a rigorous foundation for handling model uncertainty in path-dependent settings, without requiring additional modifications to our construction.

5.1

We now present the core construction of this paper: a universal process defined intrinsically on the path space that serves as the quasi-sure solution to the GSDE. The following definition is the mathematical formulation of the principle that the path is fundamental.

Definition 5.1

2.

For any path

from

\mathscr{P}
-polar (

This definition directly captures the geometric intuition of patching: the universal process

6.3
Computational Implications

The pathwise patching perspective offers a transformative way of thinking about computation under uncertainty. It shifts the goal from seeking a single ”best” answer under one model, to acknowledging and embracing the multiplicity of plausible scenarios.

Our framework suggests a more robust approach:

1.

Use all your lenses:
Look at the world through every lens (measure
P

2.

Focus on the common reality:
Instead of getting stuck arguing which lens is ”correct,” you realize they are all observing the
same underlying reality
—the actual path
\omega

actually is
on that path, regardless of which lens you used to discover it.

3.

Synthesize a robust view:
By combining these observations, you can form a single, unified picture of the possible outcomes that is not overly reliant on any one perspective. This final picture is far more resilient because it has already taken into account all the different ways the world could behave.

In practical terms, this means that to compute a robust price or risk measure, one would:

•

Simulate many future scenarios (paths) under a

of different model assumptions (different
P

•

For each simulated path, calculate the outcome based on the
path’s own behavior
, not based on the model that generated it.

•

Aggregate these results to see the full range of possible outcomes, especially focusing on the most adverse ones.

This approach provides a computational blueprint . It is the numerical counterpart to the theoretical quasi-sure concept: just as quasi-sure analysis ensures a property holds under all models, this computational method ensures our financial calculations are capitalised against plausible futures.

Conclusion

This paper has established a geometric framework for constructing quasi-sure solutions to stochastic differential equations under model uncertainty through systematic pathwise patching. Our main contributions include:

•

Developing a pathwise patching methodology that explicitly builds universal solutions directly on the path space

•

Demonstrating that pathwise uniqueness ensures compatibility across different probability measures

•

Proving that the constructed solution is universally measurable and serves as the unique quasi-sure solution

Beyond these contributions, our framework naturally extends to path-dependent equations under model uncertainty. The pathwise nature of our construction makes it particularly suitable for analyzing stochastic systems where the dynamics depend on the entire history of the process, with applications in mathematical finance, stochastic control, and related fields.

Furthermore, once the unique quasi-sure solution is obtained through our patching methodology, a natural question arises regarding its regularity properties. Specifically, we can investigate the Malliavin differentiability of the solution across the family of probability measures. This line of inquiry leads to the fundamental question of whether there exists a
robust Malliavin derivative
—a notion of derivative that remains consistent and well-defined across all measures in our family.

The development of such a cross-measure Malliavin calculus would provide powerful analytical tools for sensitivity analysis, density estimation, and other applications that remain valid under model uncertainty. However, constructing a robust Malliavin derivative that transcends individual probability measures presents significant theoretical challenges and represents a promising direction for future research.

Our work thus not only provides a solid foundation for studying stochastic equations under model uncertainty but also opens several exciting research avenues, including the extension to path-dependent systems and the development of a unified Malliavin calculus that remains consistent across different probabilistic perspectives.

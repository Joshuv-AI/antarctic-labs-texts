Single-session multi-segment EUS-guided biliary drainage in surgically altered anatomy: Three-route drainage for high-grade hilar obstruction (with video)
Authors: Masaki Miyazawa, Shotaro Kawase, Hajime Takatori
Published: 2026

A 49-year-old man who had undergone pancreaticoduodenectomy for pancreatic head cancer developed obstructive jaundice (total bilirubin 28 mg/dL) while receiving a KRAS G12C inhibitor. Computed tomography revealed recurrent tumor infiltration at the choledochojejunostomy site with hilar biliary obstruction (Bismuth type IV) [Figure 1]. Balloon enteroscopy–assisted endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography was impossible because the anastomosis was completely invaded by tumor, and percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage was high risk owing to ascites. Therefore, we performed single-session multi-segment EUS-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD) [Video 1]. An oblique-viewing EUS scope was advanced to the tumor-obstructed portion of the afferent limb [Figure 2A], which allowed visualization of the dilated right posterior ducts [Figure 2B]. The biliary branches B7 and B6 were each punctured using a 19-G needle (EZ Shot 3; Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), and a 7-Fr plastic stent (7-Fr 10 cm Type IT plastic stent; Gadelius Medical Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) was deployed into each branch, achieving 2 independent EUS-guided hepaticojejunostomy (HJS) routes [Figure 3]. The left hepatic duct (B2) was then visualized from the remnant stomach and the same 7-Fr plastic stent was deployed by EUS-guided hepaticogastrostomy (HGS) [Figure 4], completing bilateral functional drainage across 3 isolated ducts within a single session. Jaundice improved after EUS-BD, enabling systemic therapy at day 7, with no recurrence of jaundice during the subsequent 2 months until death from disease progression.

EUS-BD techniques such as EUS-HGS and EUS-HJS have expanded rescue options for complex biliary obstruction.[1–4] To our knowledge, this represents the first report of 3 EUS-BD plastic stents (EUS-HJS ×2 and EUS-HGS) placed in a single session for high-grade hilar biliary obstruction after pancreaticoduodenectomy. This case demonstrates that advancing an EUS scope into the afferent limb to assess the feasibility of EUS-HJS is worthwhile, as it may eliminate the need for percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage, reduce procedural burden, and help maintain patient quality of life.

Supplementary Videos

Video 1: Single-session multi-segment EUS-guided biliary drainage for high-grade hilar biliary obstruction after pancreaticoduodenectomy. The video demonstrates advancement of the EUS scope into the tumor-obstructed afferent limb, puncture of the right posterior ducts (B7 and B6) with placement of 2 transjejunal plastic stents (EUS-HJS × 2), followed by transgastric puncture of the left hepatic duct (B2) and stent placement (EUS-HGS), achieving bilateral drainage within a single session.

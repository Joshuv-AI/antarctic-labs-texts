Families of curves in Vinberg representations
Jef Laga, Beth Romano

Abstract
Inspired by orbit parametrizations in arithmetic statistics, we explain how to construct families of curves associated to certain nilpotent elements in ℤ / m ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} -graded Lie algebras, generalizing work of Thorne to the m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 case and the non-simply laced case. We classify such families arising from subregular nilpotents in stable gradings and interpret almost all orbit parametrizations associated with algebraic curves appearing in the literature in this framework. As an extended example, we give a “Lie-theoretic” proof of the integral orbit parametrization of 5 5 -Selmer elements of elliptic curves over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , using a ℤ / 5 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} -grading on a Lie algebra of type E 8 E_{8} .

.

Although we focus on subregular-adapted

If

Two
\mu_{m}
-actions on
H
, seen as homomorphisms
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\colon\mu_{m}(\bar{k})\rightarrow\Aut(H_{\bar{k}})
, are said to be
H
-conjugate if
\theta^{\prime}=\Ad(h)\circ\theta\circ\Ad(h)^{-1}
for some
h\in H(k)
.
Two such homomorphisms are said to be
H^{\mathrm{ad}}
-conjugate if we only require
h
to define a
k
-rational element of
H^{\mathrm{ad}}(k)
, in other words if there exists an element
\varphi\in H^{\mathrm{ad}}(k)
such that
\theta^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\theta\circ\varphi^{-1}
.
Note that if
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
are
H
-conjugate, then they are also
H^{\mathrm{ad}}
-conjugate.
The converse holds if
k
is algebraically closed.

Let
\theta\colon\mu_{m}(\bar{k})\rightarrow\Aut(H_{\bar{k}})
be a
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
.
Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the Lie algebra of
H
.
Taking derivatives, we obtain a
\mu_{m}
-action on
\mathfrak{h}
, still denoted by
\theta\colon\mu_{m}(\bar{k})\rightarrow\Aut(\mathfrak{h}_{\bar{k}})
.
For
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, let

\mathfrak{h}_{i}=\{x\in\mathfrak{h}\colon\theta(\zeta)(v)=\zeta^{i}v\text{ for all }\zeta\in\mu_{m}(\bar{k})\}.

These subspaces define a
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathfrak{h}
, in the sense that we have a direct-sum decomposition

\displaystyle\lieh=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\lieh_{i}

into linear subspaces satisfying
[\lieh_{i},\lieh_{j}]\subset\lieh_{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
G=(H^{\theta})^{\circ}
be the identity component of the fixed-point subgroup under the action.
Then
G
has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{h}_{0}
.
The adjoint representation of
H
on
\mathfrak{h}
restricts to a representation of
G
on
V:=\mathfrak{h}_{1}
.

We have just associated, to any
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
, a representation
V
of an algebraic group
G
.
We refer to the pair
(G,V)
as a
Vinberg representation
, and the study of the invariant theory of this pair is known as
Vinberg theory

[
61
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Suppose that
H
is semisimple.
If
H
is also adjoint (meaning it has trivial center) or simply connected, then the natural map
\Aut(H)\rightarrow\Aut(\mathfrak{h})
is an isomorphism (cf.
[
13
, Section 1.5]
,
[
42
, Section 3.1]
).
So giving a
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
is equivalent to giving one on
\mathfrak{h}
, which is equivalent to giving a
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathfrak{h}
.
In general, we use the setting of
\mu_{m}
-actions on
H
rather than
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-gradings on
\mathfrak{h}
, since the definition of
G
involves
H
, not just
\lieh
, and sometimes there are multiple natural choices for
H
.

We now summarize some of the highlights of Vinberg theory, referring to
[
61
]
(or the more modern
[
39
,
35
]
) for proofs.
We assume for these results and the remainder of the paper that
H
is reductive.
Note that
H
and
\theta
can be defined over a subfield of
k
that can be embedded in
\mathbb{C}
.
So for any property of
(H,\theta)
whose validity is invariant under base extension, we may assume
k=\mathbb{C}
and freely cite references that only treat this case.

We say an element
x\in V
is semisimple, nilpotent or regular if it is so when considered as an element of
\lieh
.
A subspace
\mathfrak{c}\subset V
is called a
Cartan subspace
if it consists of semisimple elements, satisfies
[\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}]=0
, and is maximal with these properties (among subspaces of
V
).

Proposition 2.2
.

1.

G=(H^{\theta})^{\circ}
is reductive.

2.

If
x\in V
has Jordan decomposition
x=x_{s}+x_{n}
where
x_{s},x_{n}
are commuting elements that are semisimple and nilpotent respectively, then
x_{s},x_{n}\in V
.

3.

Every semisimple
x\in V
is contained in a Cartan subspace of
V
.
Every two Cartan subspaces are
G(\bar{k})
-conjugate.

Proof.

The first two parts are justified in
[
61
, Section 1]
.
The last part is
[
61
, Section 3, Theorem 1]
.
∎

We call a triple
(e,h,f)
an
\liesl_{2}
-triple
of
\lieh
if
e,h,f
are nonzero elements of
\lieh
satisfying the following relations:

[h,e]=2e,\quad[h,f]=-2f,\quad[e,f]=h.

The classical Jacobson–Morozov Theorem states that every nonzero nilpotent element in
\lieh
can be completed to an
\liesl_{2}
-triple.
If
\lieh
is
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-graded, we say an
\liesl_{2}
-triple is
normal
if
e\in\lieh_{1}
,
h\in\lieh_{0}
and
f\in\lieh_{-1}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Graded Jacobson–Morozov)
.

Every nonzero nilpotent
e\in\lieh_{1}
is contained in a normal
\liesl_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
.
Any two normal
\liesl_{2}
-triples
(e,h,f)
and
(e,h^{\prime},f^{\prime})
are
Z_{G}(e)(k)
-conjugate.

Proof.

In the case when
k
is algebraically closed, this is
[
62
, Theorem 1]
. The case when

follows from the same reasoning as the proof of
[
56
, Lemma 2.17]
.
∎

The next proposition describes the basic geometric invariant theory of the representation
(G,V)
.
Let

\displaystyle\pi\colon V\rightarrow V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G=\Spec k[V]^{G}

(2.1)

be the GIT quotient map.

Proposition 2.4
.

1.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\subset V
be a Cartan subspace and
W(\mathfrak{c})\coloneqq N_{G}(\mathfrak{c})/Z_{G}(\mathfrak{c})
. Then the inclusion
\mathfrak{c}\subset V
induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}W(\mathfrak{c})\simeq V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G.

The group
W(\mathfrak{c})
is a finite pseudo-reflection group, so the quotient is isomorphic to affine space.

2.

If
k
is algebraically closed and
b\in(V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G)(k)
, then the fiber
\pi^{-1}(b)
consists of finitely many
G(k)
-orbits.
It contains a unique closed
G(k)
-orbit, the set of semisimple elements with invariants

.

3.

If
k
is algebraically closed and
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(k)
, then
\pi(v_{1})=\pi(v_{2})
if and only if the semisimple parts of
v_{1}
and

in their Jordan decompositions are

-conjugate.

4.

The quotient map
\pi\colon V\rightarrow V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
is flat, and every fiber has dimension
\dim V-\dim\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

We may assume
k=\mathbb{C}
.
These claims then follow from
[
61
, Sections 2,4]
(see also
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
), except the flatness of

.
This flatness follows from the fact that the source and domain of

are smooth, its fibers are equidimensionsal, and “Miracle Flatnes”
[
36
, Theorem 23.1]
.
∎

2.2
Stable and principal gradings

Maintain notation as in the previous section, so
H
is a reductive group with
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta
, and
(G=(H^{\theta})^{\circ},V=\mathfrak{h}_{1})
is the corresponding Vinberg representation. Of particular interest to us are stable gradings.

Definition 2.5
.

A vector
v\in V
is

(in the sense of geometric invariant theory) if the
G
-orbit of

is closed and its stabilizer
Z_{G}(v)
is finite (as an algebraic group).
We say the Vinberg representation
(G,V)
is
stable
if

contains stable vectors for the action of

.

We say a
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta
on
H
is
stable
if the associated Vinberg representation
(G,V)=((H^{\theta})^{\circ},\mathfrak{h}_{1})
is stable.

Remark 2.6
.

All the known examples of Vinberg representations that are employed in arithmetic statistics (for example those appearing in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
2
]
) are stable.
If
(G,V)
is stable, then the stable vectors form a Zariski open subset

with the following two desirable properties.
Firstly, the geometric orbits (i.e. those over an algebraically closed field) in

are separated by their invariants, by Proposition
2.4
.
Secondly, if
v\in V^{s}(k)
with
\pi(v)=b
, and
V_{b}:=\pi^{-1}(b)

is the subscheme of elements with invariants

, then
G(k)\backslash V_{b}(k)
is in natural bijection with the pointed kernel of the maps on Galois cohomology
\ker(\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,Z_{G}(v))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,G))
, see
[
3
, Section 2, Proposition 1]
.
Since
\Stab_{G}(v)
is a finite group scheme, it can often be related to a torsion subgroup

of an abelian variety

, so the set of orbits
G(k)\backslash V_{b}(k)
is a natural target for the descent map
A(k)/nA(k)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,A[n])
and may contain Selmer groups when

is a global field; this is the case for all the references cited above.

Example 2.7
.

If
H
is a torus, then the stable
\mu_{m}
-actions on

are in bijection with the elliptic order-
m
automorphisms of
H
. In this case, if
\theta
is stable, then

is finite,

is trivial, and every element of

is stable.

Stable
\mu_{m}
-actions on simple algebraic groups

over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero have been classified
[

, §7.1, §7.2]
in terms of

-regular elliptic conjugacy classes of (twisted) Weyl groups.
We recall the classification in the next subsection and some properties of stable gradings. We assume some basic knowledge about semisimple Lie algebras and split reductive groups, which can be found, for example, in
[
27
]
,
[
55
]
,
[
13
]
. For relevant background about finite-order automorphisms of Lie algebras, see
[

]
.

Let
H
be a reductive group over a field
k
of characteristic zero.
Assume that there exists a pinning
(T,B,\{E_{i}\})
of
H

[
13
, Definition 1.5.4]
.
This is the data of a maximal torus
T\subset H
(which determines a set of roots
\Phi\subset X^{*}(T_{\bar{k}})=\Hom(T_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{G}_{m,\bar{k}})
), a Borel subgroup
B\subset H
containing
T
(which determines a set of simple roots
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}\}\subset\Phi
) and a generator
E_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha_{i}}
for each root space
\alpha_{i}
.
We require
T,B
and the set
\{E_{i}\}
to be defined over
k
, but we don’t require the individual elements
E_{i}
to be defined over
k
.
Such a pinning exists if and only if
H
is quasi-split (for example, when
k=\bar{k}
), and in that case every two pinnings are conjugate by a unique element of
H^{\mathrm{ad}}(k)
.
An automorphism of
H
is said to be
pinned
if it preserves
T
,
B
and the set
\{E_{i}\}
.

Recall that
H^{\mathrm{ad}}
is the quotient of
H
by its center
Z_{H}
and that we have an exact sequence

1\rightarrow H^{\mathrm{ad}}\xrightarrow{\Ad}\Aut_{H}\rightarrow\Out_{H}\rightarrow 1.

Let
\Theta_{H}\subset\Aut_{H}
denote the subgroup (scheme) of pinned automorphisms of
H
.
Then
\Theta_{H}
defines a splitting of the above sequence, giving rise to the identification
\Aut(H)=H^{\mathrm{ad}}\rtimes\Theta_{H}
.
If
H
is split and semisimple, then
\Theta_{H}
is constant and can be identified with a subgroup of the automorphism group
\Aut(D)
of the Dynkin diagram
D
of
\mathfrak{h}
.
If
H
is additionally adjoint (in other words,
H=H^{\mathrm{ad}}
) or simply connected, then
\Theta_{H}
equals
\Aut(D)
.
See
[
13
, Section 1.5]
for these claims.

Given a
\mu_{m}
-action

\theta\colon\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Aut_{H}
,
we can postcompose
\theta
with
\Aut_{H}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
to obtain a homomorphism
\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
.
There exists a unique divisor
e
of
m
such that the latter homomorphism factors as
\mu_{m}\rightarrow\mu_{e}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
, where
\mu_{m}\rightarrow\mu_{e}
is raising to the
m/e
-th power, and
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\hookrightarrow\Out_{H}
is injective.
We call
\tau
the
outer type
of
\theta
.
If
\theta
is
H_{\bar{k}}
-conjugate to
\theta^{\prime}
, they have the same outer type.

To recall the classification of stable gradings, we first define principal gradings.
Write
\check{\rho}
for the sum of the fundamental co-weights corresponding to our choice of simple roots; it is the unique element of
X_{*}(T^{\mathrm{ad}})=\Hom(\mathbb{G}_{m},T^{\mathrm{ad}})
satisfying
\langle\alpha_{i},\check{\rho}\rangle=1
for all
i
.
Let
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\hookrightarrow\Out_{H}
be an injection of group schemes.
If a primitive
e
th root of unity
\zeta_{e}\in k
exists and is fixed, giving
\tau
is the same as giving an element
\vartheta\in\Out_{H}(k)
of order
e
.
Let
m\geq 1
be an integer divisible by
e
.
Let
\theta\colon\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Aut_{H}
be defined by

\displaystyle\theta(\zeta)=\Ad(\check{\rho}(\zeta))\circ\tau(\zeta^{m/e})\text{ for }\zeta\in\mu_{m}.

(2.2)

Since
\check{\rho}
and
\vartheta
commute, this defines a
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
.

Definition 2.8
.

We say a
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta:\mu_{m}\to\Aut_{H}
is

(respectively

) if it is
H({\bar{k}})
-conjugate (respectively
H^{\mathrm{ad}}(k)
-conjugate) to one of the form (
2.2
) for some outer type
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\hookrightarrow\Out_{H}
and integer
e
dividing
m
.

Example 2.9
.

If
\tau
is trivial (in other words,
\theta
is an inner automorphism), then (
2.2
) simply becomes
\Ad(\check{\rho}(\zeta))
and we can describe the associated grading in the following way: given a root
\alpha\in\Phi
, let
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
be its decomposition as a sum of simple roots. The root height of
\alpha
is then
0pt(\alpha):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}
. The grading of
\mathfrak{h}
corresponding to
\theta
is then given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0pt(\alpha)\equiv 0\\
\mod m\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0pt(\alpha)\equiv j\\
\mod m\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha}\quad(\text{if }j\neq 0).

(Here
\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha}
denotes the root space corresponding to
\alpha\in\Phi
.) If
\tau\neq 1
, a similar but more complicated explicit description can be given.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
H
is quasi-split.
A
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta
on
H
is split principal if and only if there exists a regular nilpotent
E\in V(k)
.

Proof.

Fix a pinning
(T,B,\{E_{i}\})
of
H
.
If
\theta
is split principal, then after
H^{\mathrm{ad}}(k)
-conjugation we may assume it equals the
\mu_{m}
-action (
2.2
) associated to this pinning.
Then
E=\sum E_{i}
is a regular nilpotent in
V(k)
, see
[
31
, Section 5, Corollary 5.3]
.
Conversely, suppose
V(k)
contains a regular nilpotent
E
.
Complete
E
to a normal
\liesl_{2}
-triple
(E,X,F)
using Lemma
2.3
.
Let
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\hookrightarrow\Out_{H}
be the outer type of
\theta
, and let
\theta_{0}
be the split principal
\mu_{m}
-action associated to
\tau
and the pinning
(T,P,\{E_{i}\})
, inducing a grading
\mathfrak{h}=\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{i}^{\prime}
.
Let
E_{0}=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{\ell}
.
Then
E_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{\prime}(k)
is regular nilpotent; complete it to a normal
\liesl_{2}
-triple
(E_{0},X_{0},F_{0})
with respect to
\theta_{0}
.

Since all regular nilpotent elements are conjugate and the stabilizer of

is trivial
[

, there exists a unique element

conjugating
(E,X,F)
to
(E_{0},X_{0},F_{0})
, which by uniquess must necessarily be defined over
k
.
So after conjugating
\theta
by
\varphi
we may assume
(E,X,F)=(E_{0},X_{0},F_{0})
.
If
\zeta\in\bar{k}
is a root of unity of order
m
, then
Then
\theta_{0}(\zeta)^{-1}\circ\theta(\zeta)
is an inner automorphism of
H
that preserves

and

.
This automorphism must be trivial by the first sentence of the paragraph, hence

∎

(Type

2.3
Classification of stable gradings

Keep the notation of the previous subsection.
Let

Since an element

preserves
T
, it induces an element of
\Aut(\Lambda,\Phi)
, still denoted by
\vartheta
.
Hence we may consider the coset
W\vartheta
in
\Aut((\Lambda,\Phi))
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Suppose that
k
is algebraically closed, that
H
is simple and let
\zeta\in k
be a primitive
m
th root of unity.
Let
\theta\colon\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Aut_{H}
be a
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
, with associated Vinberg representation
(G,V)
.
Let
\vartheta
be the image of
\theta(\zeta)
in
\Out_{H}(k)\simeq\Theta_{H}(k)
, in other words the unique element of
\Theta_{H}
such that
\theta(\zeta)\in H^{\mathrm{ad}}\vartheta
.
Then
(G,V)
is stable if and only if
\theta
is principal of order

and there exists an elliptic
\mathbb{Z}
-regular element of
W\vartheta\subset\Aut(\Lambda,\Phi)
of order
m
.

Proof.

This is
[
41
, Corollary 14]
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Suppose that
k
is algebraically closed,
H
is simple,
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\hookrightarrow\Out_{H}
an injective homomorphism, and
m
an integer divisible by
e
.
Then there exists at most one
H
-conjugacy class of stable
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
of outer type
\tau
, since such a
\mu_{m}
-action must be principal by Theorem
2.13
.
It is therefore justified to speak of “the” stable grading on
H
of order
m
and outer type
\tau
, provided it exists.

Since elliptic
\mathbb{Z}
-regular elements of automorphism groups of irreducible root systems can be classified, stable
\mu_{m}
-actions on simple adjoint groups can be classified and the result is given in
[
41
, Section 8]
.
We highlight a few examples here.

Example 2.15
.

If
m=2
, then for any root system
\Phi
,
-1
is the unique
\mathbb{Z}
-regular elliptic automorphism of
\Phi
of order
m
.
So there is a unique conjugacy class of stable
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-gradings on every simple Lie algebra
\lieh
. The principal 2-gradings of
\SO_{2n+1}
and
\Sp_{2n}
described in Examples
2.11
and
2.11
are the stable 2-gradings for these groups, since
\Out_{H}
is trivial in these cases.

Example 2.16
.

Assume
H
is simple. The Weyl group
W
has a conjugacy class of
Coxeter elements
, which are elliptic and
\mathbb{Z}
-regular. The order of a Coxeter element is the Coxeter number
h
, which is given by
\max\{0pt(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}+1
(here
0pt(\alpha)
is defined as in Example
2.9
, and this maximum does not depend on choice of simple roots).
The inner stable
\mu_{h}
-action on
H
is called the
Coxeter grading
. For this grading we have

G=T
and

V=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha_{i}},

where
\alpha_{0}\in\Phi
is the unique root of lowest height.
If
\{v_{0},\dots,v_{\ell}\}
is a basis of root vectors of
V
, then a vector
v=\sum c_{i}v_{i}
is stable if and only if
\prod c_{i}\neq 0
.
Note that if
\mathfrak{h}
has an inner stable
m
-grading, then
m
is the order of a regular element in
W
, so
m
divides at least one of the invariant degrees of
\mathfrak{h}
by
[
51
, Theorem 4.2(i)-(ii)]
, hence
m\leq h
.
So
h
is maximal in this sense.

The tables of
[
41
]
only describe stable gradings under the assumption that
H
is simple, and one might wonder whether other interesting examples of stable gradings arise from replacing a simple group with a semisimple group. The answer, essentially, is no. To see this, assume
H
is semisimple and
k
is algebraically closed. For simplicity, assume
H
is simply connected, so
H=H^{1}\times\dots\times H^{n}
is a product of simple groups. (It is enough to consider this case since every stable grading of a semisimple group lifts to a stable grading of its simply connected cover.) The automorphism
\theta
induces a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
via
\theta(H^{i})=H^{\sigma(i)}
for all
i
.

As a special case, assume
\sigma
is the
n
-cycle
(1\penalty\ 2\dots n)
.
In this case all of the simple factors
H^{i}
are isomorphic,
n|m
, and
\theta^{n}
preserves
H^{i}
for each
i
. We see that
\theta^{n}:\mu_{m/n}\to\Aut(H^{1})
is an
m/n
-grading on
H^{1}
. It’s not hard to see that projection
H\to H^{1}
gives an isomorphism
H^{\theta}\to H^{\theta^{n}}
, and projection
\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{1}
induces an isomorphism from the
\zeta_{m}
-eigenspace for
\theta
to the
\zeta_{m}^{n}
-eigenspace for
\theta^{n}
. Thus
\theta
is a stable
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
if and only if
\theta^{n}
is a stable
\mu_{m/n}
-action on
H^{1}
.

Generalizing from this case, for each orbit
\mathcal{O}
for the action of
\langle\sigma\rangle
on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, we see that
\theta
restricts to a
\mu_{m}
-action on
H^{\mathcal{O}}:=\prod_{i\in\mathcal{O}}H^{i}
. The
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
is stable if and only if the restricted
\mu_{m}
-action on
H^{\mathcal{O}}
is stable for each orbit
\mathcal{O}
. By the previous case, this
\mu_{m}
-action is stable if and only if the action of
\theta^{\lvert\mathcal{O}\rvert}
is stable on some (equivalently any)
H^{j}
for
j\in\mathcal{O}
.
Thus every Vinberg representation
(G,V)
arising from a simply connected, semisimple group
H
is of the form
G\simeq G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{d}
,
V\simeq V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{d}
, where each
(G_{i},V_{i})
is a Vinberg representation arising from a simple group
H_{i}
, and
(G,V)
is stable if and only if each
(G_{i},V_{i})
is stable.

Similarly, one can use an understanding of stable gradings for semisimple groups, along with Example
2.7
, to describe stable gradings for reductive groups. Indeed, if
H
is reductive, then

\theta:\mu_{m}\to\Aut_{H}
induces a
\mu_{m}
-action on both the derived subgroup
H^{\mathrm{der}}
and the center
Z_{H}
.
It’s not hard to see that the grading given by
\theta
is stable if and only if
(Z_{H})^{\theta}
is finite and the corresponding grading of
H^{\mathrm{der}}
is stable. Using this idea, one can show that the assumption that
H
is simple is unnecessary in Theorem
2.13
, but we choose not to give the details here, since it would be too much of a digression.

For later use, we record two properties of stable
\mu_{m}
-actions.

Lemma 2.17
.

Suppose that
\theta
is stable
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
and that
x\in\lieh_{1}
is semisimple.
Let
L=Z_{H}(x)
.
Then
L
is a (connected) reductive group, and
\theta
restricts to a stable
\mu_{m}
-action on
L
.

Proof.

We may assume
k
is algebraically closed.
The connectedness of
L
is
[
54
, Corollary 3.11]
.
Choose a Cartan subspace
\mathfrak{c}\subset\lieh_{1}
containing
x
.
Since every stable element is semisimple (
[
41
, Lemma 13]
), hence contained in a Cartan subspace, and every two Cartan subspaces are conjugate,
\mathfrak{c}
contains stable vectors for the
G
-action.
But such vectors lie in
\Lie(L)_{1}
and are also stable for the
(L^{\theta})^{0}
-action, proving the lemma.
∎

The
discriminant polynomial

\Delta_{0}\in k[\lieh]^{H}
is the image of
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}\alpha\in k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
under the Chevalley restriction isomorphism
k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}\xrightarrow{\sim}k[\lieh]^{H}
; it is independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{t}
.
For
x\in\lieh
we have
\Delta_{0}(x)\neq 0
if and only if
x
is regular semisimple.
Let
\Delta\in k[V]^{G}
be the restriction of
\Delta_{0}
to
V
.

Proposition 2.18
.

Suppose that
\theta
is a stable
\mu_{m}
-action on
H
and that
(G,V)
is the associated Vinberg representation.
Let
x\in V
.
Then
x
is stable if and only if
x
is regular semisimple if and only if
\Delta(x)\neq 0
.
Moreover, if
x
is stable, then for every primitive
m
th root of unity
\zeta
, the induced automorphism
\theta(\zeta)
on the maximal torus
Z_{H}(x)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-regular and elliptic.

Proof.

If
x
is stable, then
x
is regular semisimple by
[
41
, Lemma 13]
.
Conversely, assume
x
is regular semisimple.
By the previous lemma, the action of
\theta
on the maximal torus
S=Z_{H}(x)\subset H
is stable.
Since the conjugation action of
S
on itself is trivial, this is equivalent to the statement that
(S^{\theta})^{\circ}
is finite.
This implies that
S^{\theta}
and hence
Z_{G}(x)\subset Z_{H}(x)^{\theta}=S^{\theta}
is finite. Since
x
is semisimple, the orbit
G\cdot x
is closed by a result Vinberg
[
61
, Prop. 3]
.
Combining the last two sentences shows that
x
is indeed stable for the
G
-action.
This argument also justifies the final sentence of the proposition.
∎

2.4
Stable gradings over general fields

Let
H/k
be a reductive group.
We make some remarks about when principal gradings on
H_{\bar{k}}
descend to gradings on
H
; this section will not be used later in the paper.
Recall that given a
\mu_{m}
-grading
\theta\colon\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Aut_{H}
, there is an associated outer type
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\hookrightarrow\Out_{H}
.

Lemma 2.19
.

Suppose
H
is a quasi-split reductive group over
k
.
Let
\theta_{0}
be a principal
\mu_{m,\bar{k}}
-action on
H_{\bar{k}}
with outer type
\tau_{0}\colon\mu_{e,\bar{k}}\rightarrow\Out_{H_{\bar{k}}}
.
Then there exists a
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta
on
H
such that
\theta_{\bar{k}}
is
H_{\bar{k}}
-conjugate to
\theta_{0}
if and only if
\tau_{0}
is
\Gamma_{k}
-equivariant, i.e. descends to a (necessarily unique) homomorphism
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
.

Proof.

If such a
\theta
exists, then its outer type
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
must have the property that
\tau_{\bar{k}}=\tau_{0}
, since the outer types of two
H_{\bar{k}}
-conjugate
\mu_{m}
-actions must agree.
Conversely, if
\tau_{0}
descends to a homomorphism
\tau\colon\mu_{e}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
, we may take
\theta
to be the split principal
\mu_{m}
-action associated to
\tau
and a pinning of
H
.
∎

Example 2.20
.

In the notation of the lemma, suppose that
\tau_{0}
is trivial, i.e.,
e=1
.
Then every principal
\mu_{m}
-action on
H_{\bar{k}}
-can be defined over
k
.
Similarly, if
e=2
, then every finite group scheme of order
e
is isomorphic to
\mu_{2}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, so
\tau_{0}
is automatically Galois equivariant.

Suppose that
H
is quasi-split and
H_{\bar{k}}
is adjoint and simple.
By the classification of Dynkin diagrams and their automorphism groups, we can describe
\Out_{H}
very explicitly.
If
H
is not of type
D_{4}
, then
\Out_{H}
is trivial or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mu_{2}
, and by Example
2.20
every principal
\mu_{m}
-action on
H_{\bar{k}}
(in particular, every stable
\mu_{m}
-action) can be defined over
k
by taking split principal gradings.

It remains to consider
H
of type
D_{4}
.
Then
\Out_{H}
is a finite étale group scheme with
\Out_{H,\bar{k}}\simeq S_{3}
.
Since
H
is quasi-split, the isomorphism class of
H
determines a homomorphism
c\colon\Gamma_{k}\rightarrow S_{3}
, in other words, an étale
k
-algebra
K
of degree
3
.
The group scheme
\Out_{H}
is a twist of the group
S_{3}
, with cocycle given by the composition
\ad\circ c\colon\Gamma_{k}\rightarrow\Aut(S_{3})
, where
\ad\colon S_{3}\rightarrow\Aut(S_{3})
denotes conjugation; see
[
21
, Section 2 and Section 5.2]
for these claims.
From this description, it is easy to work out explicitly which injective homomorphisms
\mu_{3,\bar{k}}\rightarrow\Out_{H_{\bar{k}}}
can be defined over
k
.
By postcomposing
c
with the sign homomorphism
\mathrm{sgn}\colon S_{3}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
, we get a homomorphism
\Gamma_{k}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
, in other words a quadratic etale
k
-algebra
K_{0}
.
Let
C\leq\Out_{H}
be the unique subgroup scheme of order
3
.
This is a twist of
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
with Galois action cut out by the quadratic character
\mathrm{sgn}\circ c
.
It follows that
C\simeq\mu_{3}
if and only if
\mathrm{sgn}\circ c
corresponds to the Galois action on
\mu_{3}
, if and only if
K_{0}\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k
as
k
-algebras, where
\omega
is a primitive third root of unity.
So there exists an injective homomorphism
\tau\colon\mu_{3}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
if and only if
K_{0}\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k
.

Now suppose
k=\mathbb{Q}
and let
H
be the quasi-split adjoint group of type
D_{4}
corresponding to the etale cubic algebra
K=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\times\mathbb{Q}
.
Then
K_{0}=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
so by the previous paragraph there exists a (unique up to inversion) injective homomorphism
\tau\colon\mu_{3}\rightarrow\Out_{H}
.
Let
(T,B,\{E_{i}\})
be a pinning of
H
and let
\theta
be the split principal
\mu_{3}
-action associated to this pinning and
\tau
.
Let
(G,V)
be the associated Vinberg representation.
Then
G\simeq\PGL_{3}
and
V=\Sym^{3}(3)
is the space of ternary cubic forms, with action given by
([g]\cdot F)(X,Y,Z)=F((X,Y,Z)\cdot g)/\det(g)
where
[g]\in\PGL_{3}
and
F\in\mathbb{Q}[X,Y,Z]_{\deg=3}
.
This is the representation used in the study of
3
-descent on elliptic curves
[
9
]
.
We have just exhibited it as a Vinberg representation arising from a quasi-split group of type
D_{4}
.
By Lemma
2.19
, this
\mu_{3}
-action cannot be defined on the split form of type
D_{4}
, so the added generality of considering quasi-split forms is necessary to incorporate this natural example.

2.5
Centralizers

Let
H
be a reductive group over
k
with
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta\colon\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Aut_{H}
, giving rise to a
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mathfrak{h}=\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{i}
on the Lie algebra of
H
.
If
x\in\mathfrak{h}
, write
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{i}}(x)=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\cap\mathfrak{h}_{i}
for the intersection of the centralizer of
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
with
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
.
For later purposes, we record some lemmas concerning the dimensions of these centralizers.

Lemma 2.21
.

If
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{-1}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

\dim\mathfrak{h}_{n}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(x)=\dim\mathfrak{h}_{1-n}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1-n}}(x).

If
x
is additionally semisimple, then
\dim\mathfrak{h}_{n}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(x)
is independent of
n
.

Proof.

The second statement is in
[
39
, Proposition 2.1]
. The first statement follows from modifying the proof of that proposition; we provide the details.
Since there is a
\theta
-stable direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{h}=[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]\oplus\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
with
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]
semisimple, and since the lemma is obvious when
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian, we may assume
\mathfrak{h}
is semisimple.
Let
\kappa
be the Killing form on
\mathfrak{h}
.
If
x\in\mathfrak{h}
let
\kappa_{x}
be the bilinear form defined by
\kappa_{x}(y,z)=\kappa(x,[yz])=\kappa([x,y],z)
.
Since
\kappa
is nondegenerate, the radical of
\kappa_{x}
equals the centralizer
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)
.
Moreover,
\kappa(\mathfrak{h}_{i},\mathfrak{h}_{j})=0
unless
i+j=0
, so
\kappa_{x}(\mathfrak{h}_{i},\mathfrak{h}_{j})=0
unless
i+j=1
.
Therefore
\kappa_{x}
induces a perfect pairing between
\mathfrak{h}_{n}/\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(x)
and
\mathfrak{h}_{1-n}/\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1-n}}(x)
for each
n
, so
\dim\mathfrak{h}_{n}/\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(x)=\dim\mathfrak{h}_{1-n}/\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1-n}}(x)
.
∎

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
e\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}
be a nilpotent element, and let
(e,h,f)
be a normal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple containing
e
. Then

\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(e)=\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(f)

Proof.

The triple
(e,h,f)
determines a homomorphism
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
of Lie algebras.
The restriction of the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{h}
on itself to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
defines an action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
Using the representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, we see that

\mathfrak{h}=[\mathfrak{h},e]\oplus\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f).

Since the summands on the right hand side are
\theta
-stable, we can intersect both sides of this equality with
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and get

\mathfrak{h}_{1}=[\mathfrak{h}_{0},e]\oplus\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(f).

Taking dimensions, we have

\dim\mathfrak{h}_{1}=\dim[\mathfrak{h}_{0},e]+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(f)=\dim\mathfrak{h}_{0}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(e)+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(f).

The result then follows directly from Lemma
2.21
.
∎

Lemma 2.23
.

Assume that
\theta
is stable and let
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{-1}
.
Then

\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(x)-\dim(V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G)=\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(x).

(2.3)

In particular,

\dim V=\dim\mathfrak{h}_{0}+\dim(V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G).

(2.4)

Proof.

We first prove (
2.4
).
Let
v\in V
be a stable element.
Then
v
is regular semisimple and
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(v)=\{0\}
, by Proposition
2.18
.
Moreover
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(v)
is a Cartan subspace of
V=\mathfrak{h}_{1}
.
By Proposition
2.4
,
\dim(V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G)=\dim\mathfrak{c}
.
By Lemma
2.21
,
\dim\mathfrak{h}_{1}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(v)=\dim\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(v)
.
Combining the last three sentences shows (
2.4
).
Lemmas
2.21
and (
2.4
) then shows (
2.3
).
∎

2.6
The nilpotent cone

Of particular interest to us are the nilpotent elements of a graded Lie algebra, since they will be used to construct transverse slices and families of curves in §
3
.
We prove some basic facts about these nilpotent elements here.
Let
H
be a reductive group over
k
with
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta
.
Recall the GIT quotient
\pi\colon V\rightarrow V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
from (
2.1
).
Let
\mathcal{N}
be the (scheme-theoretic) fiber of
\pi
above
0
.
For every field extension
K/k
,
\mathcal{N}(K)
equals the subset of nilpotent elements of
V(K)
; this follows from Proposition
2.4
.
We are especially interested in the
G(k)
-action on
\mathcal{N}(k)
.
If
k
is algebraically closed, then there are finitely many orbits for this action.
These nilpotent orbits have been extensively studied, especialy when
m=1
; see
[
12
]
for the
m=1
case and
[
62
,
60
,
15
]
for the general case.

If
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\subset\mathcal{N}
are (geometric)
G
-orbits, then they are locally closed subvarieties of
\mathcal{N}
; we write
\mathcal{O}_{1}\preceq\mathcal{O}_{2}
if
\mathcal{O}_{1}
is contained in the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
.
This defines a partial order on the finite set of
G
-orbits on
\mathcal{N}
.
Note that
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{1})<\dim(\mathcal{O}_{2})
if
\mathcal{O}_{1}\preceq\mathcal{O}_{2}
and
\mathcal{O}_{1}\neq\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

We assume for the remainder of this section that
\theta
is stable.

Lemma 2.24
.

\dim\mathcal{N}=\dim G
.

Proof.

Since
\pi\colon V\rightarrow V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
is flat,
\dim\mathcal{N}=\dim V-\dim(V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G)
.
By Lemma
2.23
,
\dim(V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G)=\dim V-\dim\lieh_{0}
.
So
\dim\mathcal{N}=\dim\lieh_{0}=\dim G
.
∎

Lemma 2.25
.

If
e\in\mathcal{N}
, the
G
-orbit of
e
(a locally closed subset of
\mathcal{N}
) has codimension
\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\lieh_{0}}(e)
.

Proof.

This follows from the equalities
\dim G\cdot e=\dim G-\dim Z_{G}(e)=\dim\mathcal{N}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\lieh_{0}}(e)
.
(Recall from §
1.4
that
Z_{G}(e)
has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{z}_{\lieh_{0}}(e)
.)
∎

Recall that an element
x\in\mathfrak{h}
is called regular if
\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)
equals the reductive rank of
H
.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
e\in V
be regular nilpotent.

1.

If
H
is adjoint, then
Z_{H^{\theta}}(e)=\{1\}
and
Z_{G}(e)=\{1\}
.

2.

If
H
is adjoint and
e^{\prime}\in V
is another regular nilpotent, there exists a unique
h\in H^{\theta}(k)
such that
h\cdot e=e^{\prime}
.

3.

The closure of the
G
-orbit of
e
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

1.

The closed subgroup
Z_{H^{\theta}}(e)
of
H^{\theta}\subset H
has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}(e)
.
Since
\theta
is stable, Lemma
2.23
and
[
39

2.

3
Constructing transverse slices

Following Slodowy and Thorne, we construct for each nilpotent
e\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}
a subvariety

such that the morphism

is a transverse slice to the
G
-action on

.
If the
G
-orbit of

has codimension one in

,

is a family of curves.
We make some observations about the families of curves arising in this way, before classifying such families arising from subregular nilpotent elements in Section
4
.
As before, let
k
be a field of characteristic zero.

3.1
Transverse slices

Let
L
be an algebraic group over
k
acting on a variety
Y/k
.
Let
y\in Y(k)
.
Following Slodowy
[
50
, §5.1]
, we say a locally closed subvariety
S\subset Y
is a
transverse slice
to the
L
-action on
Y
at

if

•

y\in S(k)
,

•

the action map
L\times S\rightarrow Y
is smooth,

and the dimension of
S
is minimal among locally closed subvarieties satisfying these two properties.

If
Y
is smooth, then such a transverse slice always exists, and its dimension equals the codimension of the
L
-orbit of

; see
[
50
, Lemma 1 and Remark 2]
.
Moreover the tangent spaces satisfy
T_{y}(L\cdot y)\oplus T_{y}S=T_{y}Y
.

3.2
Diagonalizable actions on affine spaces

Let
T
be a closed subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
.
Let
X^{*}(T_{\bar{k}})=X^{*}(T)
be its character group.
Let
V
be a
k
-vector space equipped with a linear
T
-action, i.e., a homomorphism of algebraic groups
\lambda\colon T\rightarrow\GL(V)
.
If
w\in X^{*}(T)
define the weight space
V_{w}=\{v\in V\colon\lambda(t)(v)=w(t)v\text{ for all }t\in T(\bar{k})\}
.
We will often make use of the following standard lemma
[
11
, Chapter 3, Section 8.4]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

V
is a direct sum of weight spaces: there exists a basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
V
and elements
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\in X^{*}(T)
such that
\lambda(t)(v_{i})=w_{i}(t)v_{i}
for all

.

The elements

are called the

of the
T
-action.
For example, if

, then
X^{*}(T)=\mathbb{Z}
hence the weights are simply integers.
If
T=\mu_{m}
, then the weights are elements of
X^{*}(T)=\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

If
V,W
are two vector spaces with linear
T
-action, we say an algebraic morphism
f\colon V\rightarrow W
is
quasi-homogeneous of weight

(d_{1},\dots,d_{m};w_{1},\dots;w_{n})
if
f
is
T
-equivariant,
V
has weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
, and
W
has weights
d_{1},\dots,d_{m}
. (Here we count weights with multiplicity.)
The next lemma is proved in
[
50
, Section 8.1, Lemma 3]
and will be useful in Section
3.4
.

Lemma 3.2
.

In the above notation, suppose
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Suppose we are given a morphism
f\colon V\rightarrow W
that is quasi-homogeneous of weight
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n};d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
, each
d_{i}
is positive, and the central fiber
f^{-1}(0)
is zero dimensional.
Then
f
is an isomorphism.

3.3
Graded Slodowy slices

Let
H/k
be a reductive group with
\mu_{m}
-action
\theta\colon\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Aut_{H}

(G,V)

Let

be a nonzero nilpotent element.
We explain how to construct an explicit transverse slice to the

-action at

By the graded Jacobson–Morozov Theorem (Lemma
2.3

and

(e,h,f)
is an
\liesl_{2}
-triple.
The centralizer

\theta

\lieh

Define the affine linear subspaces

\displaystyle S_{e}

\displaystyle:=(e+\mathfrak{z}_{\lieh}(f))\subset\lieh,

(3.1)

\displaystyle X_{e}

\displaystyle:=(e+\mathfrak{z}_{\lieh}(f))\cap V=e+\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(f)\subset V.

(3.2)

Write
\varphi\colon X_{e}\rightarrow B
for the restriction of
\pi\colon V\rightarrow V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G=B
to

.

Remark 3.3
.

The construction of

and

depends on a choice of

. Since any two normal
\liesl_{2}

Slodowy has shown that

at

.
The next theorem generalizes Slodowy’s result to graded Lie algebras.
It has been proved by Thorne in the

Theorem 3.4
.

In the above notation,
X_{e}
is a transverse slice to the
G
-action on
V
at
e
.
More precisely,

1.

The multiplication map
G\times X_{e}\rightarrow V
is smooth;

2.

\dim X_{e}+\dim(G\cdot e)=\dim V
; and

3.

The map
\varphi\colon X_{e}\rightarrow B

The proof of Theorem

follows from adapting arguments of Thorne
[

, §3]
in a straightforward manner, which are itself based on arguments of Slodowy
[
50
, §7.4]
.
We start by defining important
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mu_{m}
-actions on
S_{e}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions on

that will be useful in the proof and in Section

.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{h}
spanned by
\{e,h,f\}
.
The adjoint action of
\mathfrak{h}
on itself restricts to an action of
\mathfrak{m}\simeq\liesl_{2}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
Let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow H
be the unique homomorphism whose derivative at

equals

.
Then
\Ad(\lambda)\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\GL(\mathfrak{h})
defines a linear
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathfrak{h}
.
On the other hand,
\theta
is a
\mu_{m}
-action on
\mathfrak{h}
that commutes with
\Ad(\lambda)
since

.
These combine to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mu_{m}
-action
\Ad(\lambda)\times\theta
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
This action preserves
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f)
, so by Lemma
3.1
, there exists a basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f)
and elements

of

such that

has weight

.
Write

, where

and

.
If
x\in S_{e}
then

for some

, and we have

\displaystyle\Ad(\lambda(t))(x)

\displaystyle=t^{2}e+\sum c_{i}t^{n_{i}}v_{i},

\displaystyle\theta(\zeta)(x)

\displaystyle=\zeta e+\sum c_{i}\zeta^{k_{i}}v_{i}.

These expressions tell us how to modify

so that

becomes a fixed point for the new action.
Define a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action
\rho
on
\mathfrak{h}
by the formula
\rho(t)(v)=t^{2}\Ad(\lambda(t^{-1}))(v)
.
Define a
\mu_{m}
-action
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{h}
by the formula
\sigma(\zeta)(v)=\zeta\theta(\zeta^{-1})(v)
.
These combine to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mu_{m}
-action
\rho\times\sigma
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
This action preserves
S_{e}
and

.
Viewing

as an affine space with origin

,
\rho\times\sigma
is a linear
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mu_{m}
-action and its weights are
(2-n_{i},1-k_{i})
for

.
The next lemma shows that

is the unique fixed point for this action:

Lemma 3.5
.

The weights of the
\rho
-action on
S_{e}
are
\geq 2
.

Proof.

In the above notation, this is equivalent to showing that
n_{i}\leq 0
for all
i
.
By the representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, the subspace of
\mathfrak{h}
generated by
\{\ad(e)^{k}(v_{i})\colon k\geq 0\}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{m}
.
If it has dimension
m_{i}
, then
n_{i}=1-m_{i}
.
Since
m_{i}\geq 1
,
n_{i}\leq 0
.
∎

The scalar
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathfrak{h}
induces a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathfrak{h}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}H
, which we simply denote by

.
The
\mu_{m}
-action on

induces one on
\mathfrak{h}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}H

. (This

-action only depends on the outer type of

Define a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mu_{m}

Then the GIT quotient morphism

on both sides.

We may now restrict all these considerations to

and

.
By definition, the fixed-point locus of

under

, so

restricts to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on

.
Proposition

can be reinterpreted as saying that the inclusion

induces a closed immersion

whose image equals the

-fixed point locus of

-action on

.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

1.

The differential of the action map
\mu\colon G\times X_{e}\rightarrow V
at
(1,e)
equals, after identifying

with

with

.
Since both summands are

\displaystyle V=[\lieg,e]\oplus(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f)\cap V),

Therefore

[

.
Moreover,

is smooth at every point of

.
Define a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on

via

and on

via

Then

is equivariant with respect to these actions.
The open subset of points in

-equivariant.
By Lemma

2.

We have

3.

We first prove that

Since both

-stable subset of

Since the

-action on

is surjective.

∎

An important and well understood special case of the above construction is when

Proposition 3.6

Assume that

Proof.

This is

.
∎

In other words, the transverse slice

constructed from a regular

Such a section is called a Kostant section (or sometimes a Kostant–Weierstrass slice).
If

3.4
Families of curves

Assume
\theta
is stable and
e\in V
is a nilpotent element, giving rise to a transverse slice
\varphi\colon X_{e}\rightarrow B
associated to some choice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
containing
e
.
We will be interested in the situation where
X_{e}\rightarrow B
is a family of curves, i.e. the fibers have dimension
1
.
This is equivalent, by Lemma
2.25
, to the condition
\dim Z_{G}(e)=\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)=1
.
So assume the latter condition for the remainder of this section.
We make some general observations about such families of curves.
We already know by Theorem
3.4
that
\varphi\colon X_{e}\rightarrow B
is affine, surjective, with one-dimensional fibers, and that the action map
G\times X_{e}\rightarrow V
is smooth.
Write
C=\varphi^{-1}(0)
for the central fiber, a singular affine curve.

The next lemma determines which nilpotent orbits appear in
C
: it is exactly those which lie above

in the closure partial ordering.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a nilpotent orbit in
V
. Then
\mathcal{O}\cap C\neq\varnothing
if and only if the closure
\bar{\mathcal{O}}
of
\mathcal{O}
contains
e
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}\cap C\neq\varnothing
.
Then
\bar{\mathcal{O}}\cap C
is a closed nonempty
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-stable subset of
C
, so must contain
e
, by the same logic as the proof of Lemma
3.12
.
So
e\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}
.
Conversely, suppose that
e\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}
.
Then every
G
-stable open subset of
V
that contains
e
also contains
\mathcal{O}
.
Since the morphism
m\colon G\times X_{e}\rightarrow V

Lemma 3.8

Definition 3.9

3.5
Coxeter gradings

As a first example, we now describe nilpotent orbits and graded Slodowy slices for Coxeter gradings of simple Lie algebras, as described in Example
2.16
. Let
H
be simple and split over
k
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}
be simple roots for
\mathfrak{h}
, and let
\alpha_{0}
be the lowest root. Then
\{e_{\alpha_{i}}\mid i\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
, and
G=T
is a torus. The ring of invariants for the action of
G
on
V
is generated by the polynomial

\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}e_{\alpha_{i}}\mapsto\prod_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}^{c_{i}},

where the exponents
c_{i}
are defined by
-\alpha_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
c_{0}=1
.

A vector
v=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}e_{\alpha_{i}}
is nilpotent if and only if
a_{i}=0
for some
i
.
If
v
is nilpotent, then the dimension of its centralizer
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(v)
is
\#\{i\mid a_{i}=0\}-1
.
Thus a nilpotent vector
v
has trivial centralizer in
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if exactly one
a_{i}=0
. If
k
is algebraically closed, this tells us that there are exactly
\ell+1
nilpotent
G
-orbits of maximal dimension and
\frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell+1)
nilpotent orbits with centralizer in
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension 1.

Let
E=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}b_{i}e_{\alpha_{i}}
be a nilpotent with trivial centralizer in
\mathfrak{g}
and
b_{j}=0
. We have
X_{E}=E+\spn\{e_{\alpha_{j}}\}
, so the nilpotent
E
is reduced in the sense of Definition
3.9
if and only if
c_{j}=1
, which occurs if and only if
E
is regular. (Here we’re using the fact that if
c_{j}=1
, then
\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}\}\setminus\{\alpha_{j}\}
forms a set of simple roots, which one can see using the affine Dynkin diagram for
H
cf.
[
29
, Section 1.8]
).
A nilpotent
e=\sum a_{i}e_{\alpha_{i}}
is in the closure of
G\cdot E
if and only if
a_{j}=0
, and
e
is good as defined above if and only if
a_{i}=0
implies
c_{i}=1
.

Fix
j,k\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
with
j\neq k
, and let
e=\sum_{i\neq j,k}e_{\alpha_{i}}
.
We can complete
e
to a normal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple with
f=\sum_{i\neq j,k}a_{i}e_{-\alpha_{i}}
and all
a_{i}\neq 0
. Then
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}(f)=\spn\{e_{\alpha_{j}},e_{\alpha_{k}}\}
. If we consider the restriction of the map
V\to V\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
to
X_{e}
, the fiber over
c
is given explicitly by
\{e+be_{\alpha_{j}}+de_{\alpha_{k}}\mid b^{c_{j}}d^{c_{k}}=c\}
, i.e. it is a curve of the form
x^{c_{j}}y^{c_{k}}=c
.

Remark 3.15
.

We may also consider
twisted Coxeter gradings
, which generalize the Coxeter gradings considered here. Given a pinned automorphsim
\vartheta
, one may define the twisted Coxeter number
h_{\vartheta}
(cf.
[
42
, Section 5]
), and then the twisted Coxeter gradings are principal
h_{\vartheta}
-gradings of the form
\check{\rho}\vartheta
. A grading of this form is rank one, the subgroup
G
is a subtorus of
T
, and the ring of invariants is described explicitly in
[
58
, Section 4.2]
.
Both nilpotent orbits and corresponding graded Slodowy slices can be described in a completely analagous way to the (non-twisted) Coxeter grading.

4
Subregular curves

Let

-action on

In this section we completely describe the construction of Section

Specifically, Theorem

follows from Theorems

Our approach builds upon and resembles Slodowy’s method to explicitly determine the family of subregular surfaces

4.1
Subregular-adapted gradings

Recall that a nilpotent element

If

is simple and

-orbit of subregular elements.

Definition 4.1
.

A nonzero nilpotent element

is called
\theta
-subregular

is subregular and

\mu_{m}
-action

if there exists a

-subregular nilpotent element in

(We add the condition that

is nonzero to exclude the degenerate case that

In general, not every subregular element

satisfies

satisfying

is subregular (see the appendix for examples of this phenomena in the stable
8
-grading of

We single out the
\theta
-subregular nilpotents as an interesting class of nilpotents whose transverse slices

have good properties.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose

is
\theta
-subregular and that

is a nilpotent element with the property that

Then

is regular.
In particular,

is good (in the sense of Section

Proof.

The hypotheses imply that
e\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muH\cdot E\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subset\mathfrak{h}
.
Since
e
is subregular, this implies

is regular or subregular.
For the sake of contradiction, assume we are in the latter case and write
\mathcal{O}=H\cdot E=H\cdot e
for the subregular orbit in
\mathfrak{h}
.
A result of Richardson
[
44
, Lemma 3.1]
(see also
[
61
, Section 2.3]
) shows that there is a decomposition
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{O}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathcal{O}_{r}
into connected components such that each
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is a
G
-orbit.
By assumption, we may order these orbits so that
e\in\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
E\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
.
Since
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is closed in

,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cap\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
.
By assumption,

lies in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cap\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
.
So

This is a contradiction, so

is indeed regular.
Since every regular nilpotent is reduced,

is good.
∎

Now we consider the GIT quotient map

associated to a
\theta
-subregular nilpotent element

If

The following is the analogue of
[

If

Proof.

Recall from §

Denoting by

Lemma 4.5

So

By Proposition
4.3

Since

-action on

-action on

Z_{G}(\kappa_{\eta})_{\bar{k}}\simeq\Hom(E_{\eta}[5]_{\bar{k}},\mu_{5,\bar{k}})
.
The existence of the Weil pairing on
E_{\eta}[5]
shows that there is an isomorphism
\Hom(E_{\eta}[5]_{\bar{k}},\mu_{5,\bar{k}})\simeq E_{\eta}[5](\bar{k})
.
We conclude that
Z_{G}(\kappa_{\eta})_{\bar{k}}\simeq E_{\eta}[5](\bar{k})
.
∎

5.5
The orbit parametrization

Let
k/\mathbb{Q}
be a field and
b\in B^{s}(k)
.
Then every element in
V_{b}(\bar{k})
is stable and
G(\bar{k})
-conjugate to
\kappa_{b}
by Proposition
2.4
(2).
However,
V_{b}(k)
might break up into multiple
G(k)
-orbits.
If
v\in V_{b}(k)
, then the transporter scheme
T(v,\kappa_{b})=\{g\in G(\bar{k})\colon g\cdot v=\kappa_{b}\}
is a torsor under the stabilizer
Z_{G}(\kappa_{b})
.
The following lemma is a special case of
[
3
, Proposition 1]
:

Lemma 5.10
.

The map
v\mapsto T(v,\kappa_{b})
induces a bijection

\displaystyle G(k)\backslash V_{b}(k)\xleftrightarrow{1:1}\ker(\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,Z_{G}(\kappa_{b}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,G)).

(Here
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,-)
denotes Galois cohomology.)

We call an isomorphism of group schemes
Z_{G}(\kappa|_{B^{s}})\simeq E^{s}[5]
over
B^{s}
a “universal isomorphism.”
Such an isomorphism exists by Theorem
5.9
.
It induces an isomorphism
Z_{G}(\kappa_{b})\simeq E_{b}[5]
of Galois modules and hence, by combining it with the previous lemma, a map
V_{b}(k)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,E_{b}[5])
.
The affine curve
C_{b}\subset E_{b}
is defined as a closed subscheme of
V_{b}
, so we have a tautological inclusion
\iota\colon C_{b}(k)\rightarrow V_{b}(k)
.
Composing these two maps defines a map
C_{b}(k)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,E_{b}[5])
.

On the other hand, since
E_{b}
is an elliptic curve, there is a
5
-descent map
E_{b}(k)\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,E_{b}[5])
, associating to every
P\in E_{b}(k)
to isomorphism class of the
E_{b}[5]
-torsor
\{Q\in E_{b}(\bar{k})\colon 5Q=P\}
.

Theorem 5.11
.

There exists a universal isomorphism such that for every field
k/\mathbb{Q}
and every
b\in B^{s}(k)
, the following diagram commutes and is functorial in
k
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{b}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V_{b}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E_{b}(k)/5E_{b}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,E_{b}[5])}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

Proof.

The proof is identical to
[
56
, Theorem 4.15]
and is omitted.
∎

We henceforth fix a universal isomorphism satisfying the conclusion of Theorem
5.11
.
We immediately obtain the following orbit parametrization:

Corollary 5.12
.

For every field
k/\mathbb{Q}
and
b\in B^{s}(k)
, there is a unique injection

\displaystyle\eta_{b}\colon E_{b}(k)/5E_{b}(k)\hookrightarrow G(k)\backslash V_{b}(k),

(5.3)

functorial in
k
, with the following properties:

1.

\eta_{b}
maps
0\in E_{b}(k)/5E_{b}(k)
to the orbit
G(k)\cdot\kappa_{b}
of the Kostant section.

2.

If
P\in C_{b}(k)
, then
\eta_{b}(P\mod 5)
lies in the orbit of
P
, seen as an element of
C_{b}(k)\subset V_{b}(k)
.

Proof.

Since the two properties guarantee uniqueness, it suffices to prove the existence of
\eta_{b}
.
Consider the composition

f\colon E_{b}(k)/5E_{b}(k)\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,E_{b}[5])\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,Z_{G}(\kappa_{b}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,G).

We claim that
f(P)
is trivial for every
P\in E_{b}(k)/5E_{b}(k)
.
If
P
is itself trivial, this is clear, since
\delta(P)
is trivial.
If
P\neq 0
, then
P
lies in the image of the map
C_{b}(k)\rightarrow E_{b}(k)
, and so the triviality follows from Theorem
5.11
and Lemma
5.10
.

It follows that for every
P\in E_{b}(k)/5E_{b}(k)
, there exists a unique element
\eta_{b}(P)
of
G(k)\setminus V_{b}(k)
that corresponds to
f(P)
under Lemma
5.10
.
This map satisfies the conclusions of the corollary by construction.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

For every
b\in B^{s}(\mathbb{Q})
, the injection (
5.3
) extends to an injection
\Sel_{5}E_{b}\rightarrow G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash V_{b}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Corollary
5.12
, we need to show that the composite map
\Sel_{5}E_{b}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E_{b}[5])\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},Z_{G}(\kappa_{b}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},G)
is trivial.
Let
c\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},G)
be an element in the image of this map.
By definition of the
5
-Selmer group and the previous corollary, we know that the restriction
c_{v}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},G)
is trivial, for every place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Since
G
is split semisimple by Lemma
5.1
, a Hasse principle for
\mathrm{H}^{1}(-,G)
implies that
c=1
, see
[
34
, Proposition 6.8]
.
∎

5.6
Integral representatives

We now show that when
k=\mathbb{Q}
, the image of the map (
5.3
) has integral representatives away from small primes, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We first need to fix integral structures on the objects involved.
Using Lemma
5.1
, fix isomorphisms
G\simeq(\SL_{5}\times\SL_{5})/\mu
and
V\simeq(5)\boxtimes\wedge^{2}(5)
.
Since
(\SL_{5}\times\SL_{5})/\mu
and
(5)\boxtimes\wedge^{2}(5)
are defined over
\mathbb{Z}
, we may view
G
as a group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
and
V
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module so that these isomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{Z}
.
We redefine
B=\Spec(\mathbb{Z}[c_{20},c_{30}])
.
We redefine
C=\Spec(\mathbb{Z}[x,y,c_{20}])
and
\psi\colon C\rightarrow B,(x,y,c_{20})\mapsto(c_{20},y^{2}-x^{3}-c_{20}x)
.
We let
E
be the closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}B
defined by the same Weierstrass equation (
5.2
).

Proposition 5.14
.

There exists an integer
N\geq 1
with the following property:
for every prime
p
and
b=(c_{20},c_{30})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
with
\Delta(b)\neq 0
, every element in the image of the map
\eta_{b}\colon E_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\backslash V_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

has a representative in
\frac{1}{N}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\iota\colon C_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow V_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an algebraic map of affine spaces over
\mathbb{Q}
.
By spreading out (in other words, examining denominators), there exists an integer
N_{1}\geq 1
such that
N_{1}\cdot\iota(x)\in V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for all
p
and elements
(x,y,c_{20})\in C(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
that satisfy
5^{2}x,5^{3}y,c_{20}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Similarly, there exists an integer
N_{2}\geq 1
such that
N_{2}\cdot\kappa_{b}\in V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for all
p
and
b\in B(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
We claim that
N=N_{1}N_{2}
satisfies the properties of the proposition.

Indeed, fix a prime
p
and a class
\alpha\in E_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/5E_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. It follows from formal group considerations that:

•

If
p\neq 5
, then either
\alpha=0
or
\alpha
is represented by a point
(x,y)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

•

If
p=5
, then either
\alpha=0
or
\alpha
is represented by a point
(x,y)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with
(5^{2}x,5^{3}y)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

If
\alpha=0
, then
\kappa_{b}
is a representative of
\eta_{b}(\alpha)
that lies in
\frac{1}{N_{2}}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset\frac{1}{N}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
If
\alpha\neq 0
, it is represented by a point
P=(x,y)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with
(5^{2}x,5^{3}y)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
Then
\iota(P)
is a representative of
\eta_{b}(P)
(by Corollary
5.12
) which lies in
\frac{1}{N_{1}}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset\frac{1}{N}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
∎

Remark 5.15
.

We do not obtain exact integral representatives but only up to a uniformly bounded integer
N
; this is usually enough for the purposes of determining the average size of
\Sel_{5}E_{b}
.
Also note that it would be possible to analyze the squarefree discriminant case and obtain
[
7
, Proposition 11]
using Lie theory (in a manner entirely analogous to
[
34
, Section 7.4]
), but we have chosen to not pursue this.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer satisfying the conclusion of Proposition
5.14
.
Let
v\in V_{b}(\mathbb{Q})
be a representative for an orbit in the image of
\eta_{b}
.
Then there is a finite set of primes
S
such that
v\in V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for all
p\not\in S
.
By Proposition
5.14
, for each
p\in S
there is an element
g_{p}\in G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that
g_{p}\cdot v\in\frac{1}{N}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Lemma
5.1
and the choice of integral structure on
G
shows that
G
is a split semisimple group over
\mathbb{Z}
, in other words a Chevalley group.
Such a group has class number
1
.
Therefore there exists an element
g\in G(\mathbb{Q})
such that
gg_{p}^{-1}\in G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for all
p\in S
and
g\in G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for all
p\not\in S
.
By construction,
g\cdot v\in\frac{1}{N}V(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for all primes
p
, so
g\cdot v\in\frac{1}{N}V(\mathbb{Z})
, as desired.
∎

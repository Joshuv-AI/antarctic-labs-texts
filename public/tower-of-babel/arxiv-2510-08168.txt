Asymptotic Behaviour of the weak inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow
Chaoqun Gao, Yong Wei, Rong Zhou

Abstract
. We first establish a local gradient estimate for anisotropic p p -harmonic functions. A key feature of our estimate is that the constant remains bounded as p → 1 p\to 1 ; consequently, in the limit p → 1 p\to 1 , this estimate yields the local gradient estimate for weak solutions of the inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow (IAMCF). As an application, we show that the weak IAMCF is asymptotic to the expanding Wulff shape solution at the infinity, thereby extending the result of Huisken and Ilmanen in [ 7 ] to the anisotropic case.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}~(n\geq 2)
be an open bounded subset with smooth boundary. The weak inverse mean curvature flow starting from
\Omega
is a locally Lipschitz function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the degenerate elliptic equation

\mathrm{div}\left(\dfrac{Du}{|Du|}\right)=|Du|\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(1.1)

in certain variational sense, with
\{u\leq 0\}=\Omega
. The existence of the weak solution was proved by Huisken and Ilmanen
[
7
]
using the elliptic regularization method. An alternative approach was discovered by Moser
[
11
]
, and the idea is that the weak solution
u
of IMCF can also be interpreted as the limit when
p\to 1^{+}
of
u_{p}=(1-p)\log v_{p}
, where
v_{p}
is the
p
-capacity potential of
\Omega
and so
u_{p}
satisfies

\begin{cases}\mathrm{div}(|Du_{p}|^{p-2}Du_{p})=|Du_{p}|^{p}&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\
u_{p}=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\\
u_{p}\to\infty&\text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

(1.2)

This idea was extended subsequently to IMCF in general Riemannian manifolds
[
9
,
10
]
.

The asymptotical behavior of the weak solution
u
was studied in
[
7
, Lemma 7.1]
using a weak blow down argument. This was extended by Benatti, Fogagnolo, and Mazzieri
[
1
]
to weak solution of IMCF in asymptotically conical manifolds. The result in the Euclidean space states as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
7
, Lemma 7.1]
)
.

The weak inverse mean curvature flow starting from an open bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary satisfies

u(x)=(n-1)\log|x|-\log\frac{|\partial\Omega^{*}|}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}+o(1)\quad\text{as}\quad|x|\to\infty,

(1.3)

where
\Omega^{*}
denotes the strictly outward minimizing hull of
\Omega
. Moreover, the expanding sphere solution is the only solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
with compact level sets.

The proofs in
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
both rely on the gradient estimate
|Du|\leq C/d(x,0)
(a consequence of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
) and then proceed to estimate the eccentricity of the level sets of
u
for large
t
.

In this paper, we extend the result in Theorem
1.1
to the inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow (IAMCF).

Recall that a function
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is called a
Minkowski norm
, if it defines a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition:
D^{2}(\frac{1}{2}F^{2})
is positive definite on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded domain with smooth boundary. The weak solution of IAMCF starting from
\Omega
with respect to the anisotropic function
F
is a locally Lipschitz function
u
satisfying the following degenerate elliptic equation:

\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}(DF(Du))=F(Du)&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\\
u\to\infty&\text{as}\ |x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

(1.4)

in certain variational sense. By using Moser’s approximating scheme, Della Pietra, Gavitone and Xia
[
6
]
proved the existence of the weak solution of (
1.4
). This was further extended by Cabezas-Rivas, Moll and Solera
[
2
]
to IAMCF for general anisotropic function
F
which includes the crystalline case. The idea is to consider the following approximating equation involving the anisotropic
p
-Laplacian

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p,F}u_{p}=F^{p}(Du_{p})&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\\
u_{p}=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\\
u_{p}\to\infty&\text{as}\ |x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

(1.5)

where

\Delta_{p,F}u:=\operatorname{div}\left(F^{p-1}(Du)DF(Du)\right).

By deriving the global gradient estimate of
u_{p}
, the weak solution of IAMCF is obtained as the limit of
u_{p}
as
p\to 1^{+}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
,
6
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded domain with smooth boundary. Then there exists a unique weak solution
u
of IAMCF (
1.4
). If there exist two constants
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
such that
\mathcal{W}_{r_{1}}\subset\Omega\subset\mathcal{W}_{r_{2}}
, then

u_{r_{2}}\leq u\leq u_{r_{1}},

(1.6)

where

u_{r_{i}}=(n-1)\log\left(F^{\circ}\left(\frac{\cdot}{r_{i}}\right)\right),\ i=1,2.

Here
F^{\circ}
denotes the dual norm of
F
and
\mathcal{W}_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~F^{\circ}(x)<r\}
denotes the Wulff shape of radius
r
.

1.1.
Local gradient estimate

As the first result of our paper, we derive a local gradient estimate for the anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions. Let

\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:F^{\circ}(x-x_{0})<R\}

denote the Wulff shape centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
R
(see §
2.1
for details). Assume that
v
is a positive anisotropic
p
-harmonic function in the Wulff shape
\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0})
, i.e.
\Delta_{p,F}v=0
in
\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0})
. It’s direct to see that
u=(1-p)\log v
satisfies

\Delta_{p,F}u=F^{p}(Du),\quad\text{in}~\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0}).

(1.7)

We prove the following local gradient estimate for
u
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
. Assume that
v
is a positive anisotropic
p
-harmonic function on the Wulff shape
\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0})
. Then
u=(1-p)\log v
satisfies

\max\limits_{\mathcal{W}_{R/2}(x_{0})}F(Du)\leq\dfrac{C(n,F)}{R},

(1.8)

where
C=C(n,F)
is uniform for
1<p\leq 2
.

Our proof of (
1.8
) uses the technique of Kotschwar and Ni
[
9
]
for the local gradient estimate for positive
p
-harmonic functions on general Riemannian manifolds with sectional curvature bounded from below, which is inspired by the gradient estimate of Cheng-Yau
[
3
]
for positive harmonic functions. Let
f=\frac{1}{2}F^{2}(Du)
. We first derive a Bochner type formula for
\mathscr{L}f
, where
\mathscr{L}
is the linearized operator of the nonlinear equation (
1.7
). To estimate the local upper bound of
f
, we multiply
f
by a suitable cut-off function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0}))
, derive a differential inequality satisfied by
Q=f\eta
at its interior maximum point and then apply the maximum principle. The computation is much involved, and a difficulty arises in handling the term containing the third derivative of the Minkowski norm squared
G(\xi):=\frac{1}{2}F^{2}(\xi)
, which vanishes in the isotropic case. To overcome this, we employ a key property: there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(F)>0
depending only on
F
such that for all vectors
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following inequality holds:

FD^{3}G|_{\xi}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\leq C_{1}\left(D^{2}G|_{\xi}(\alpha,\alpha)\cdot D^{2}G|_{\xi}(\beta,\beta)\cdot D^{2}G|_{\xi}(\gamma,\gamma)\right)^{1/2}.

This inequality is valid because both sides are homogeneous of degree zero and
D^{2}G
is positive definite. Using this estimate, we are able to control the term involving the third derivative of
G
. We note that a similar property, under certain smallness constraints on
C_{1}>0
, has also been used in the interior gradient estimate for anisotropic mean curvature flow in
[
5
]
.

A remarkable feature of Kotschwar-Ni’s gradient estimate
[
9
]
for positive
p
-harmonic functions is that the constant in their estimate does not blow up when
p\to 1
, which effectively leads to the gradient estimate for the weak solution of IMCF. Recently, Mari, Rigoli, and Setti
[
10
]
extended the gradient estimate of Kotschwar and Ni
[
9
]
to the case where
\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(n-1)h(r)
, with
h(r)
being a radial function satisfying certain conditions. Our gradient estimate (
1.8
) for positive anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions is also uniform for
p
, and thus we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded domain with smooth boundary. Assume that the origin lies in the interior of
\Omega
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,F,\Omega)>0
such that the weak solution
u
of the IAMCF (
1.4
) starting from
\Omega
satisfies

F(Du)(x)\leq\dfrac{C}{F^{\circ}(x)}

(1.9)

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

We remark that, in addition to the maximum principle method used by Cheng-Yau
[
3
]
and Kotschwar-Ni
[
9
]
, the Moser iteration technique is also a powerful tool in deriving gradient estimates. Wang and Zhang
[
12
]
employed the Moser iteration to derive a local gradient estimate for positive
p
-harmonic functions on manifolds
M
satisfying
\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(n-1)\kappa^{2}
; however, the constant in their estimate becomes unbounded as
p\to 1
. This was further extended by Xia
[
16
]
to local gradient estimates for Finsler
p
-harmonic functions on Finsler manifolds with weighted Ricci curvature bounded below, where the constant again blows up as
p\to 1^{+}
. As a special case of Xia’s result, the following local gradient estimate holds for anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\max\limits_{\mathcal{W}_{R/2}(x_{0})}F(Du)\leq\dfrac{C(n,p,F)}{R},

(1.10)

However, compared with our estimate in (
1.8
), the constant
C(n,p,F)
in (
1.10
) becomes unbounded as
p\to 1
. For this reason, in Theorem
1.3
we only focus on the range
1<p\leq 2
, with the main purpose being the application to the weak IAMCF as
p\to 1
.

1.2.
Asymptotical behaviour of weak IAMCF

Utilizing the gradient estimate (
1.9
) and adapting the methods of
[
7
, Lemma 7.1]
and
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
, we prove the following anisotropic counterpart of Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
u
be the weak solution of IAMCF (
1.4
) starting from an open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Then

u(x)=(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)+\log\left(\dfrac{|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}}{|\partial\Omega^{*}|_{F}}\right)+o(1)\quad\text{as}\ F^{\circ}(x)\to\infty,

(1.11)

where
\Omega^{*}
is the strictly outward
F
-minimizing hull of
\Omega
. Moreover, the expanding Wulff shape is the only solution to (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
with compact level sets.

Remark 1.6
.

The asymptotical behavior of the solution
u_{p}
to the equation (
1.5
) was studied by Xia-Yin
[
13
]
. However, since
u_{p}
converges to the weak IAMCF
u
only locally uniformly as
p\to 1^{+}
, we cannot directly deduce the asymptotical behavior (
1.11
) of
u
by simply letting
p\to 1^{+}
in the asymptotical behavior of
u_{p}
, due to the inherent non-commutativity of limits. Despite this, it is shown a posteriori that the two are indeed consistent.

For weak IMCF (
1.1
), Huisken-Ilmanen
[
8
]
proved that the level set
\partial\{u<t\}
becomes smooth after a sufficiently large time. This combined with Theorem
1.1
implies the asymptotical behavior of higher order derivatives of
u
. It would be natural to ask the following question.

Question 1.7
.

Whether the weak IAMCF (
1.4
) becomes smooth after a sufficiently large time?

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some preliminaries for anisotropic geometry and weak inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow. In Section
3
, we prove the local gradient estimate for positive anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions, as well as for weak solutions of IAMCF. In Section
4
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
, the asymptotic behaviour of the weak IAMCF.

2.
Preliminaries

This section provides the necessary preliminaries for anisotropic geometry and weak solution of the inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow.

2.1.
Minkowski norm and Wulff shape

A function
F\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\right)
is called a
Minkowski norm
if

(1)

F
is a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
F
is convex, even,
1
-homogeneous with
F(\xi)\geq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
F(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=0
;

(2)

F
satisfies a uniformly elliptic condition:
D^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}F^{2}\right)
is positive definite in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.

The dual norm of
F
is defined as

F^{\circ}(x):=\sup\limits_{\xi\neq 0}\dfrac{\langle x,\xi\rangle}{F(\xi)},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.1)

which is also a Minkowski norm. This definition implies the anisotropic Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq F^{\circ}(x)F(\xi),\quad\forall\ x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.2)

with equality holding if and only if
\xi=F(\xi)DF^{\circ}(x)
and
x=F^{\circ}(x)DF(\xi)
. Here
D
denotes the gradient operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following properties relate
F
and its dual
F^{\circ}
(see e.g.
[
14
,
4
]
):

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle DF^{\circ}(x),x\rangle=F^{\circ}(x),

\displaystyle\ \ \langle DF(\xi),\xi\rangle=F(\xi).

(2.3)

\displaystyle F\left(DF^{\circ}(x)\right)=1,

\displaystyle\ \ F^{\circ}\left(DF(\xi)\right)=1,

(2.4)

\displaystyle F^{\circ}(x)DF\left(DF^{\circ}(x)\right)=x,

\displaystyle\ \ F(\xi)DF^{\circ}\left(DF(\xi)\right)=\xi.

(2.5)

Given a Minkowski norm
F
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the associated Wulff shape is defined as

\mathcal{W}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:F^{\circ}(x)<1\right\}.

For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{r}(x_{0})=r\mathcal{W}+x_{0}
the scaled and translated Wulff shape, which satisfies

\mathcal{W}_{r}(x_{0})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:F^{\circ}(x-x_{0})<r\right\}.

When
F
is the Euclidean norm,
\mathcal{W}_{r}(x_{0})
coincides with the Euclidean ball
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x_{0})
of radius
r
centered at
x_{0}
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. The anisotropic perimeter
|\partial\Omega|_{F}
of
\Omega
is defined as

|\partial\Omega|_{F}=\int_{\partial\Omega}F(\nu)d\mu,

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\Omega
.

2.2.
Weak inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow

Given an open bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth mean-convex boundary
\partial\Omega
, the classical inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow (IAMCF) starting from
\partial\Omega
describes a family of smooth closed hypersurfaces
X:M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
evolving according to

\partial_{t}X(x,t)=\dfrac{1}{H_{F}}\nu_{F}(x,t),

(2.6)

where
H_{F}
and
\nu_{F}=DF(\nu)
denote the anisotropic mean curvature and the anisotropic unit normal vector of the evolving hypersurface
M_{t}=X(M,t)
, respectively. Suppose that the evolving hypersurface
M_{t}=\partial E_{t}
are given by level sets of a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
E_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u(x)<t\}
. If
u
is smooth and
Du\neq 0
, then (
2.6
) is equivalent to the degenerate elliptic equation

\text{div}(DF(Du))=F(Du)

(2.7)

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and
\Omega=\{u<0\}
.

The weak solution of (
2.7
) was defined in
[
6
]
via the minimizing principle as in Huisken-Ilmanen
[
7
]
for the isotropic case.
Given an open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary, a locally Lipschitz function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a weak IAMCF starting from
\Omega
, if for every locally Lipschitz function
\varphi
with
\{u\neq\varphi\}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
containing
\{u\neq\varphi\}
we have

J_{F,u}^{K}(u)\leq J_{F,u}^{K}(\varphi)

and
\Omega=\{u<0\}
, where the functional is defined as

J_{F,u}^{K}(\varphi)=\int_{K}\left[F(D\varphi)+\varphi F(Du)\right]\mathrm{d}x.

We say that
u
is a proper solution if in addition
\lim\limits_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=+\infty
.

The weak solution of (
2.7
) can also be defined by set functional.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
E
minimizes
J_{F,u}
in a set
U
(minimizes on the outside, minimizes on the inside, resp.) if

J_{F,u}^{K}(E)\leq J_{F,u}^{K}(G)

for any
G
such that
E\triangle G\subset\subset U
(
G\supset E
,
G\subset E
resp.) and any compact set
K
containing
E\triangle G
. Here
E\triangle G=(E\backslash G)\cup(G\backslash E)
, and

J_{F,u}^{K}(G)=\int_{\partial^{*}G\cap K}F(\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{G\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x

for a set
G
of locally finite perimeter, and
\partial^{*}G
denotes the reduced boundary of
G
.

Then a locally Lipschitz function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a weak solution of IAMCF if for each
t>0
,
E_{t}=\{u<t\}
minimizes
J_{F,u}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Therefore, we also say that
E_{t}
is a weak solution of IAMCF with initial data
E_{0}=\Omega
.

We have the following comparison principle:

Lemma 2.3

(see
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
)
.

Let
\{E_{t}\}_{t>0}
and
\{F_{t}\}_{t>0}
be two weak solutions of IAMCF with initial data
E_{0}
,
F_{0}
respectively, and
E_{0}\subset F_{0}
, then
E_{t}\subset F_{t}
as long as
E_{t}
is precompact.

In particular, for a given
E_{0}
there exists at most one solution
\{E_{t}\}_{t>0}
of (
2.7
) such that
E_{t}
is precompact.

We state the following two properties of weak IAMCF, whose proofs closely follow those of Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 3.3 in
[
7
]
, respectively. The minor modification involves replacing the Euclidean norm of
Du
with
F(Du)
.

Lemma 2.4

(Compactness)
.

Let
u_{i}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.4
) in open sets
U_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

u_{i}\to u,\ \ U_{i}\to U

locally uniformly, and for each
K\subset\subset U
,

\sup\limits_{K}F(Du_{i})\leq C(K)

for large
i
. Then
u
is a solution of (
1.4
) in
U
.

Lemma 2.5

(Smooth flow)
.

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{c\leq t<d}
be a smooth family of hypersurfaces with positive anisotropic mean curvature that solves (
2.6
) classically. Let
u=t
on
M_{t}
,
u<c
in the region bounded by
M_{c}
, and set
E_{t}=\{u<t\}
. Then for
c\leq t<d
,
E_{t}
minimizes
J_{F,u}
in
E_{d}\backslash\overline{E_{c}}
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. In other words, smooth flows of IAMCF satisfy the weak formulation in the domain they foliate.

Proof.

For any
t\in(c,d)
, we need to show that: for any set
G
with finite perimeter satisfying
E_{t}\triangle G\subset\subset E_{d}\backslash\overline{E_{c}}
and any compact set
K
containing
E_{t}\triangle G
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap K}F(\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{E_{t}\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}G\cap K}F(\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{G\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x.

(2.8)

Consider the vector field
\nu_{F,u}=DF(Du)
. For
A=E_{t}\backslash G
or
G\backslash E_{t}
, the equation (
2.7
) implies

\int_{A}\mathrm{div}(\nu_{F,u})\mathrm{d}x-\int_{A}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x=0

(2.9)

Using the divergence theorem, property (
2.3
), and equation (
2.9
), we compute

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap K}F(\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{E_{t}\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap K}\nu_{\partial^{*}E_{t}}\cdot\nu_{F,u}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{E_{t}\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap K}\mathrm{div}(\nu_{F,u})\mathrm{d}x-\int_{E_{t}\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\cap K}\mathrm{div}(\nu_{F,u})\mathrm{d}x-\int_{G\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}G\cap K}\nu_{\partial^{*}G}\cdot\nu_{F,u}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{G\cap K}F(Du)\mathrm{d}x.

(2.10)

Applying anisotropic Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) yields

\nu_{\partial^{*}G}\cdot\nu_{F,u}\leq F(\nu_{\partial^{*}G})F^{\circ}(\nu_{F,u})=F(\nu_{\partial^{*}G}).

Substituting this inequality into the last line of (
2.2
) establishes (
2.8
).
∎

3.
Local gradient estimate of anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions

In this section, we prove the local gradient estimates for anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions as well as for the weak solution of IAMCF.

Let
u
solve the equation (
1.7
) in the Wulff shape
\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0})
. By setting
G(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}F^{2}(\xi)
, we rewrite (
1.7
) as

\mathrm{div}\left(G^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(Du)G_{\xi}(Du)\right)=2G^{\frac{p}{2}}(Du).

(3.1)

Let

A|_{\xi}=D^{2}G(\xi)+\dfrac{p-2}{2}\dfrac{DG(\xi)\otimes DG(\xi)}{G(\xi)},

which is a uniformly positive definite matrix for
p>1
. Consider the linear elliptic operator:

\mathscr{L}(\varphi)=\mathrm{div}\left(G^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(Du)A|_{Du}(D\varphi)\right)-pG^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(Du)\langle G_{\xi}(Du),D\varphi\rangle.

(3.2)

In the following, we use the convention that

F^{\circ}
, we also have

F(Du_{s}(x))=sF(Du(sx))\leq s\cdot\frac{C(n,F,\Omega)}{F^{\circ}(sx)}=\frac{C(n,F,\Omega)}{F^{\circ}(x)}

(4.2)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{s}
.

Therefore, the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem implies precompactness of
(u_{s})_{s\geq 1}
with respect to local uniform convergence on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Furthermore, the compactness theorem (see Lemma
2.4
) ensures that any limit point
v(x)=\lim\limits_{s_{k}\to\infty}u_{s_{k}}(x)
solves the weak IAMCF (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and satisfies the same estimates

\displaystyle(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)-(n-1)\log r_{2}\leq v(x)\leq(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)-(n-1)\log r_{1},

(4.3)

and

F(Dv)(x)\leq\dfrac{C(n,F,\Omega)}{F^{\circ}(x)}

(4.4)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.

Step 2.
We prove that every limit
v
of
(u_{s})_{s\geq 1}
has the form

v(x)=(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)+\gamma

(4.5)

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Define the ratio function
\theta_{v}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\theta_{v}(t)=\dfrac{R_{v}(t)}{r_{v}(t)}

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{v}(t)\leq F^{\circ}(x)\leq R_{v}(t)\}
is the smallest anisotropic annulus containing
\{v=t\}
. Note that the level set
\{v=t\}
is compact. This follows from the
C^{0}
estimate (
4.3
), which implies that
\{v=t\}
is contained in a bounded annular region
\left\{r_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{n-1}}\leq F^{\circ}(x)\leq r_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{n-1}}\right\}
away from the origin. Consequently,
\theta_{v}(t)
is well-defined and finite for all
t
. Clearly,
\theta_{v}(t)\geq 1
and
\theta_{v}(t)=1
if and only if
\{v=t\}
is the boundary of a Wulff shape. We next show that
\theta_{v}(t)\equiv 1
.

Firstly, we prove that
t\mapsto\theta_{v}(t)
is non-increasing. Indeed, for any
\tilde{t}>t
and any
x\in\{v=\tilde{t}\}
, the comparison principle (Lemma
2.3
) implies

r_{v}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\tilde{t}-t}{n-1}}\leq F^{\circ}(x)\leq R_{v}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\tilde{t}-t}{n-1}}.

Taking suprema and infima yields

R_{v}(\tilde{t})=\sup\limits_{x\in\{v=\tilde{t}\}}F^{\circ}(x)\leq R_{v}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\tilde{t}-t}{n-1}},

and

r_{v}(\tilde{t})=\inf\limits_{x\in\{v=\tilde{t}\}}F^{\circ}(x)\geq r_{v}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\tilde{t}-t}{n-1}}.

Hence,

\theta_{v}(\tilde{t})=\dfrac{R_{v}(\tilde{t})}{r_{v}(\tilde{t})}\leq\dfrac{R_{v}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\tilde{t}-t}{n-1}}}{r_{v}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\tilde{t}-t}{n-1}}}=\dfrac{R_{v}(t)}{r_{v}(t)}=\theta_{v}(t)

for any
\tilde{t}>t
.

Secondly, we show that there exists a function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\theta_{h}(t)
is constant and is equal to
\theta_{h}(1)=\sup_{t}\theta_{v}(t)\in[1,+\infty)
. In fact, for any
0<s\leq 1
, consider the rescaled functions

v_{s}(x)=v(sx)-(n-1)\log s,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

Then
v_{s}
satisfies the same estimates as in (
4.3
) and (
4.4
). By Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, there exists a function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(v_{s})_{s\leq 1}
locally uniformly converges to
h
as
s\to 0^{+}
.
Since

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in\{v_{s}=t\}}F^{\circ}(x)=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{y\in\{v=(n-1)\log s+t\}}F^{\circ}\left(\frac{y}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{y\in\{v=(n-1)\log s+t\}}\frac{1}{s}F^{\circ}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{s}R_{v}((n-1)\log s+t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{s}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{n-1}}R_{v}((n-1)\log s)

and

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{x\in\{v_{s}=t\}}F^{\circ}(x)=

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{y\in\{v=(n-1)\log s+t\}}F^{\circ}\left(\frac{y}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{s}r_{v}((n-1)\log s+t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{s}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{n-1}}r_{v}((n-1)\log s),

we have

\theta_{v_{s}}(t)=\theta_{v}((n-1)\log s).

Therefore,

\theta_{h}(t)=\lim\limits_{s\to 0^{+}}\theta_{v_{s}}(t)=\lim\limits_{s\to 0^{+}}\theta_{v}((n-1)\log s)=\sup\limits_{-\infty<s<+\infty}\theta_{v}(s),

which is finite.

Lastly, we prove that

\theta_{h}(1)=1.

Suppose by contradiction that
\theta_{h}(1)>1
, then the level set
\{h=0\}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{h}(0)\leq F^{\circ}(x)\leq\theta_{h}(1)r_{h}(0)\}
, and it touches both the Wulff shapes
\{F^{\circ}(x)=r_{h}(0)\}
and
\{F^{\circ}(x)=\theta_{h}(1)r_{h}(0)\}
but differs from both boundary Wulff shapes. We perturb
\{F^{\circ}(x)\leq r_{h}(0)\}
outward and
\{F^{\circ}(x)\leq\theta_{h}(1)r_{h}(0)\}
inward along their anisotropic normals of the boundary to obtain two domains
U^{-}
and
U^{+}
respectively satisfying:

•

\{F^{\circ}(x)\leq r_{h}(0)\}\subset U^{-}\subset\{h\leq 0\}
and
\{h\leq 0\}\subset U^{+}\subset\{F^{\circ}(x)\leq\theta_{h}(1)r_{h}(0)\}
;

•

U^{-}
and
U^{+}
are star-shaped with smooth strictly
F
-mean convex boundaries (i.e., the anisotropic mean curvature of the boundary is positive).

Starting from
\partial U^{-}
and
\partial U^{+}
, the smooth solutions of inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow exist for all time (see
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
) and coincide with the corresponding weak solutions (see Lemma
2.5
). Denote by
(U_{t}^{-})_{t\geq 0}
and
(U_{t}^{+})_{t\geq 0}
the two weak (and smooth) IAMCF starting at
U^{-}
and
U^{+}
, respectively. By the strong maximum principle for smooth flows, we have

F^{\circ}(x)>r_{h}(0)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{n-1}}\quad\mathrm{for}\ x\in\partial U_{t}^{-}

and

F^{\circ}(x)<\theta_{h}(1)r_{h}(0)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{n-1}}\ \quad\mathrm{for}\ x\in\partial U_{t}^{+}.

The weak comparison principle (see Lemma
2.3
) also yields the containment

U_{t}^{-}\subset\{h\leq t\}\subset U_{t}^{+}.

Then it follows that
\theta_{h}(t)<\theta_{h}(1)
, contradicting the constancy of
\theta_{h}
.

In conclusion
\theta_{h}(t)\equiv 1
. Since
1\leq\theta_{v}(t)\leq\theta_{h}(t)=1
, we conclude that
\theta_{v}(t)\equiv 1
and thus
v
has the form (
4.5
).

Step 3.
We prove that the constant
\gamma
in (
4.5
) is given by

\gamma=\log\left(\dfrac{|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}}{|\partial\Omega^{*}|_{F}}\right).

(4.6)

For the weak solution
u
of IAMCF starting from
\Omega
, we consider the auxiliary function

w=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{u-\gamma}{n-1}}.

Let
v
be a limit of the rescaled solution
(u_{s})_{s\geq 1}
in (
4.1
). There exists a subsequence
s_{k}\to+\infty
such that

u_{s_{k}}\to v=(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)+\gamma

locally uniformly. Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
k_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\{F^{\circ}(x)\leq\dfrac{s_{k}}{1+\varepsilon}\right\}\subset\{w\leq s_{k}\}\subset\left\{F^{\circ}(x)\leq\dfrac{s_{k}}{1-\varepsilon}\right\},\ \ \forall\ k\geq k_{\varepsilon}.

(4.7)

Since the Wulff shape and
\text{int}\{u\leq t\}
are both strictly outward
F
-minimizing, the inclusion relationship of sets (
4.7
) implies the following inequality for the anisotropic perimeters

\left|\left\{F^{\circ}(x)=\dfrac{s_{k}}{1+\varepsilon}\right\}\right|_{F}\leq|\partial\{w\leq s_{k}\}|_{F}\leq\left|\left\{F^{\circ}(x)=\dfrac{s_{k}}{1-\varepsilon}\right\}\right|_{F}.

By the exponential growth of the anisotropic perimeter under weak IAMCF (
[
6
, Proposition 3.4]
), we have

\displaystyle|\partial\{w\leq s_{k}\}|_{F}=

\displaystyle|\partial\{u\leq(n-1)\log s_{k}+\gamma\}|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\gamma}s_{k}^{n-1}|\partial\Omega^{*}|_{F},

where
\Omega^{*}
is the strictly outward
F
-minimizing hull of
\Omega
. We also have
|\partial\mathcal{W}_{r}|_{F}=r^{n-1}|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}
for the anisotropic perimeter of the Wulff shape. Therefore,

\left(\frac{s_{k}}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{n-1}|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\gamma}s_{k}^{n-1}|\partial\Omega^{*}|_{F}\leq\left(\frac{s_{k}}{1-\varepsilon}\right)^{n-1}|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}.

Dividing both sides by
s_{k}^{n-1}|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}>0
yields

(1+\varepsilon)^{1-n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\gamma}\frac{|\partial\Omega^{*}|_{F}}{|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{1-n}.

The result (
4.6
) follows by taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Finally, for
x
with
F^{\circ}(x)\to\infty
,

\displaystyle u(x)-(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)=

\displaystyle u\left(F^{\circ}(x)\cdot\frac{x}{F^{\circ}(x)}\right)-(n-1)\log F^{\circ}(x)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle(n-1)\log F^{\circ}\left(\frac{x}{F^{\circ}(x)}\right)+\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(\dfrac{|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}}{|\partial\Omega^{*}|_{F}}\right).

This gives the asymptotical behavior (
1.11
) of
u
in Theorem
1.5
. The proof in Step 2 implies that the expanding Wulff shape is the only solution to (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
with compact level sets.

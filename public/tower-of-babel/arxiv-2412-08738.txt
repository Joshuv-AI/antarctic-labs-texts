An arithmetic intersection for squares of elliptic curves with complex multiplication
Elisa Lorenzo García, Christophe Ritzenthaler, Fernando Rodríguez Villegas

Abstract
. Let C C be a genus 2 2 curve with Jacobian isomorphic to the square of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by a maximal order in an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant − d < 0 -d<0 . We show that if the stable model of C C has bad reduction over a prime p p then p ≤ d / 4 p\leq d/4 . We give an algorithm to compute the set of such p p using the so-called refined Humbert invariant introduced by Kani. Using results from Kudla-Rapoport and the formula of Gross-Keating, we compute for each of these primes p p its exponent in the discriminant of the stable model of C C . We conclude with some explicit computations for d < 100 d<100 and compare our results with an unpublished formula by the third author.

How does our work compare to
[

]
? It seems to us that they consider a slightly different arithmetic intersection problem. Instead of looking at the cycle of

for a given

and

(for instance) with the set of points in the moduli space with a given

with respect to

is therefore a sub-cycle of the one they consider. Looking at formula (3.6) of

with the last entry equal to 1 give an intersection point. However, this first impression is not correct as for instance for the second polarization in the case

In our case as well, the set of matrices

Quadratic forms on symmetric endomorphisms

All the material in this section is classical or covered by Kani in his various articles, in particular in

2.1.
Refined Humbert invariant

Let
(A,\lambda)
be a principally polarised abelian variety of dimension
g
over a perfect field
k
. The
Rosati involution
(with respect to
\lambda
) is defined on
\End(A)
by the involution
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{{\dagger}}=\lambda^{-1}\hat{\alpha}\lambda
. We will denote
\Ends(A,\lambda)=\{\alpha\in\End(A),\,\alpha=\alpha^{{\dagger}}\}
.
To any
\alpha\in\End(A)
, one can associate a degree
2g
polynomial
P_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
defined for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by
P_{\alpha}(n)=\deg(\alpha-n)
. By
[
CS86
, Prop.12.9, p.125]
, it is also the characteristic polynomial of
\alpha
acting on
T_{\ell}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
for all
\ell
different from the characteristic of
k
and if
k\subset\mathbb{C}
, it is also the characteristic polynomial of the rational representation of
\alpha
. The
trace
of
\alpha
on this space does not depend on
\ell
, belongs to
\mathbb{Z}
and will be denoted
\Tr(\alpha)
.

By
[
CS86
, Th.17.3, p.138]
, the bilinear form
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\Tr(\alpha\beta^{{\dagger}})
on
\End(A)
is positive definite and restrict to a positive definite quadratic form
\Ends(A,\lambda)\to\mathbb{Z}
by
\alpha\mapsto\Tr(\alpha^{2})
. Similarly,
Kani introduced another quadratic form on
\Ends(A,\lambda)
:

Definition 2.1
.

One calls

\displaystyle q_{(A,\lambda)}:

\displaystyle\Ends(A,\lambda)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{4}(2g\Tr(\alpha^{2})-\Tr(\alpha)^{2})

the
refined Humbert invariant
.

Remark 2.2
.

The fact that
q_{(A,\lambda)}
has values in
\mathbb{Z}
boils down to the fact that
\Tr(\alpha)
is even for symmetric endomorphisms (see Remark
2.7
).

Note that for any integer
m
, one has
q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha+m)=q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha)
so that this form is well defined on the quotient

L^{Kani}:=\Ends(A,\lambda)/\mathbb{Z}\cdot 1_{\End(A)}.

Moreover letting
\alpha^{\prime}=2g\alpha-\Tr(\alpha)\cdot 1_{\End(A)}
, we see that
4g^{2}\cdot q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha)=q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha^{\prime})=g/2\cdot\Tr({\alpha^{\prime}}^{2})\geq 0
, so the form
q_{(A,\lambda)}
is a positive definite form on
L^{Kani}
.

2.2.
Gross lattice

Instead of working on this quotient lattice, one can introduce another lattice

L:=\tau(\Ends(A,\lambda))\;\textrm{where }\tau(\alpha)=g\alpha-\Tr(\alpha)/2,

which we call the
Gross lattice
by analogy with the genus 1 case
[
Gro87
]
. Clearly
\Tr(\tau(\alpha))=0
so
L\subset L_{0}:=\{\alpha\in\Ends(A,\lambda),\,\Tr(\alpha)=0\}
, the latter being called in the literature the space of
special endomorphisms
. The kernel of
\tau
is
\mathbb{Z}\cdot 1_{\End(A)}
so for
\beta\in L
, the image in
L^{Kani}
of
\alpha=\tau^{-1}(\beta)
is well defined and realises an isomorphism between
L
and
L^{Kani}
. We therefore have the following relation
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ends(A,\lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{Kani}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces iso}

Moreover

q_{(A,\lambda)}(\beta)=\frac{g}{2}\Tr(\beta^{2})=g^{2}q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha).

Hence, the lattice
(L,\frac{1}{g^{2}}\cdot q_{(A,\lambda)})
and
(L^{Kani},q_{(A,\lambda)})
are isometric and in particular their Gram matrices are equivalent.

2.3.
The case
g=2

From now on we restrict to
g=2
so that
q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha)=\Tr(\alpha^{2})-\frac{1}{4}\Tr(\alpha)^{2}
. We let
q:=q_{(A,\lambda)}
when there is no ambiguity and let

b_{q}:(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\left(q(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-q(\alpha_{1})-q(\alpha_{2})\right)

be the bilinear form associated to
q
.
We want to prove several integrality and congruence results on
q
and
b_{q}
. In order to do this, we will use Kani’s expression of
q
in terms of intersection of divisors.

One embeds the Néron-Severi group
\NS(A)
in
\Ends(A,\lambda)
by sending a divisor
D
to
\alpha={\lambda}^{-1}\phi_{D}
, where
\phi_{D}(x)=t_{x}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{D}^{-1}
. Over
\bar{k}
, the map is surjective and in particular the identity of
\Ends(A,\lambda)
corresponds to an effective divisor
\theta
such that
\phi_{\theta}=\lambda
. If
\alpha\in\Ends(A)
corresponds to
D
, then the degree of
\alpha
is equal to the degree of
\phi_{D}
since
{\lambda}
is an isomorphism. By the Geometric Riemann-Roch theorem, one has
\deg\alpha=(\frac{1}{2!}(D.D))^{2}
. In particular
(\theta.\theta)=2
.
One rewrites
q
in terms of intersection of divisors on
A
as in
[
Kan16
]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

For
D\in\NS(A)
, let
\alpha=\lambda^{-1}\phi_{D}\in\Ends(A,\lambda)
. Then

(2.1)

q(\alpha)=(D.\theta)^{2}-2(D.D).

The expression (
2.1
) is what Kani actually calls the
refined Humbert invariant
.
This equality follows from the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(A,\lambda)
be a principally polarised abelian variety over
k
of dimension
g
and
\alpha\in\Ends(A,\lambda)
. Let
\ell

. There exists a basis of

such that the matrix of
\alpha
acting on
T_{\ell}(A)
in this base can be written

(2.2)

M_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\id_{2}&M_{12}&\ldots&M_{1g}\\
\ad(M_{12})&\ddots&\cdots&M_{2g}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\ad(M_{1g})&\cdots&&\alpha_{g}\id_{2}\end{pmatrix}

where
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
M_{ij}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
for
i<j
and
\ad()
is the adjucate operator.

Proof.

Let
e_{\ell}^{\theta}:T_{\ell}(A)\times T_{\ell}(\hat{A})\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
be the Weil pairing relative to the polarization
\lambda
associated to
\theta
, i.e.
e_{\ell}^{\theta}(x,y)=e_{\ell}(x,\lambda(y))
.
Fix a basis
a_{1},\ldots,a_{2g}
of

T_{\ell}(A)
such that
e_{\ell}^{\theta}
is given by the diagonal block matrix
J,\cdots,J
where

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Write the matrix of
\alpha
acting on
T_{\ell}(A)
with
2\times 2
-blocks as

M_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&M_{12}&\ldots&M_{1g}\\
M_{21}&\ddots&\cdots&M_{2g}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
M_{g1}&\cdots&\cdots&M_{gg}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\alpha
is a symmetric endomorphism for the polarization
\lambda
, the following holds:

e_{\ell}^{\theta}(a_{i},\alpha(a_{j}))=e_{\ell}^{\theta}(\alpha^{\dagger}(a_{i}),a_{j})=e_{\ell}^{\theta}(\alpha(a_{i}),a_{j})=-e_{\ell}^{\theta}(a_{j},\alpha(a_{i})).

This equality
implies
JM_{ij}=-M_{ji}J
. Since they are

-matrices, this implies
M_{ji}=\ad(M_{ij})
, which, in addition, for
i=j
, implies that

are multiples of the identity.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

2.4.
Decomposable abelian surfaces

Kani gives a generalization in all characteristics of the characterization of decomposable abelian surfaces initially proved in terms of Humbert invariant over
\mathbb{C}
for abelian surfaces with real multiplication.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
Kan16
, Prop.6]
)
.

Let
(A,\lambda)
be a principally polarised abelian surface over a field
k
. Then
(A,\lambda)
is decomposable over
k
if and only if there exists
\alpha\in L^{Kani}=\Ends(A,\lambda)/1_{\End(A)}
(resp.
\beta\in L=\tau(\Ends(A,\lambda))
) such that
q(\alpha)=1
(resp.
q/4(\beta)=1
).

Kani’s proof relies on several results on the divisors of abelian surfaces. We would like to give here a different proof based on direct computations with endomorphisms. An element
\beta=\tau(\alpha)\in L
(and more generally in
L_{0}
) has a representation on
T_{\ell}(A)
of the form

\begin{pmatrix}b&M\\
M^{\ad}&-b\end{pmatrix}.

In particular
\beta^{2}=(b^{2}+\det(M))\cdot 1_{\End(A)}
. Now,
q(\beta)=4(b^{2}+\det(M))=4q(\alpha)
, so we see that
\beta^{2}=q(\alpha)\cdot 1_{\End(A)}
. Hence, we can again reformulate the statement by asking alternatively for the existence of
\beta\in L
such that
\beta^{2}=1_{\End(A)}
. If
(A,\lambda)
is decomposable, it is isomorphic to
E_{1}\times E_{2}
with the product polarization
1\times 1
and

\beta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}=\tau\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right)\in L\subset\Ends(E_{1}\times E_{2},1\times 1)

satisfies the statement. Conversely, assume that we can find such a
\beta=\tau(\alpha)\in L
. Since
q(\alpha)=1=\Tr(\alpha^{2})-(\Tr(\alpha)/2)^{2}
, we see that
\Tr(\alpha)/2
must be odd since
\Tr(\alpha^{2})
is even. So
\beta-1=2\alpha-(\Tr(\alpha)/2-1)\cdot 1_{\End(A)}
is divisible by
2
. This proves that
e_{1}=(\beta+1_{\End(A)})/2
and
e_{2}=(1_{\End(A)}-\beta)/2
are two endomorphisms of
A
such that
e_{1}e_{2}=0
. Hence
E_{1}=\im(e_{1})\subset\ker(e_{2})
is a proper subvariety of
A
, hence an elliptic curve and similarly for
E_{2}=\im(e_{2})
. Moreover the morphism
x\mapsto(e_{1}(x),e_{2}(x))
is an isomorphism from
A
to
E_{1}\times E_{2}\subset A
with inverse
e_{1}+e_{2}
. It remains to show that the polarization
\lambda
is decomposable:
computing the Weil pairing for
(x_{1},0)\in E_{1}\times E_{2}
and
(0,x_{2})\in E_{1}\times E_{2}
, we see that
e^{\lambda}((x_{1},0),(0,x_{2}))=e^{\lambda}(e_{1}(x_{1},0),e_{2}(0,x_{2}))=e^{\lambda}((x_{1},0),e_{1}e_{2}(0,x_{2}))=1
since
e_{1}e_{2}=0
.

Remark 2.11
.

What about
g>2
? If
(A,\lambda)\simeq(A_{1}\times A_{2},\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2})
where
(A_{i},\lambda_{i})
are principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension

, then the symmetric endomorphism

satisfies
q_{(A,\lambda)}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{4}(2g\Tr(\alpha^{2})-\Tr(\alpha)^{2})=g_{1}g_{2}
. This is therefore a necessary condition.

Conversely, a symmetric endomorphism
\alpha
with
q(\alpha)=g_{1}g_{2}
defines an endomorphism
\beta=g\alpha-\Tr(\alpha)/2\cdot 1_{\End(A)}
with

\Tr(\beta)=0,\;\Tr(\beta^{2})=2gg_{1}g_{2}\;\textrm{and }q(\beta)=g^{2}g_{1}g_{2}.

The endomorphism
e_{1}=\frac{\beta+g_{1}\cdot 1_{\End(A)}}{g}
is well-defined because
(\Tr(\alpha)/2)^{2}\equiv{g_{1}}^{2}\mod{g}
and we can take

instead of
\alpha
if needed. We also define
e_{2}=1_{\End(A)}-e_{1}
. It remains to check whether
e_{1}e_{2}=0
: this happens if and only if
\beta^{2}+(g_{1}-g_{2})\beta-g_{1}g_{2}=0
.
This is always the case for
g=2
and
g_{1}=1
. For
g=3
and
g_{1}=1
for instance, Lemma
2.4
only implies that we have
\beta^{3}-3\beta-\Tr(\beta^{3})/6=0
. So, the condition
\beta^{2}-\beta-2=0
has to be checked. If
\Ends(A,\lambda)
can be explicitly computed (as we will see in Section
3
this is the case for

and more generally for
E^{g}
), this computation is doable since there will be only a finite number of
\beta\in L
with
(q/g^{2})(\beta)=g_{1}g_{2}
since

is a positive definite form on the lattice

. In addition, since

is self-adjoint, it diagonalises and it is equivalent to check whether

For

3.6.
An algorithm to test the decomposability at a prime
\mathfrak{p}

Having all these explicit computations at hand, Algorithm
1
determines if a given
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of decomposable reduction by looking for an element of norm 1 in the lattice
\bar{L}
with positive definite Gram matrix of
q/4
. If this element exists, it is unique up to a sign.

Proposition 3.10
.

If there is an element
\ell_{0}\in\bar{L}
with
(q/4)(\ell_{0})=1
then
\ell_{0}
is unique up to a sign.

Proof.

As we have seen in the proof of Theorem
2.10
, an endomorphism
\ell_{0}\in\bar{L}
with
q/4(\ell_{0})=1
leads to symmetric idempotents
e_{1}=(\ell_{0}+1_{\End(\bar{A}})/2
and
e_{2}=1_{\End(\bar{A})}-e_{1}
and to an isomorphism of principally polarised abelian surfaces
(E^{2},\lambda)\rightarrow(\im(e_{1})\times\im(e_{2}),\lambda_{0})
where
\lambda_{0}
is the product polarization
1\times 1
. Reciprocally, given such an isomorphism
\phi:\,(E^{2},\lambda)\rightarrow(E_{1}\times E_{2},\lambda_{0})
, let us define
e_{1}=\phi^{-1}\circ\pi\circ\phi
where
\pi\in\End(E_{1}\times E_{2})
is defined by
(x,y)\to(x,0)
and
e_{2}=1-e_{1}
. We see that
e_{1}^{2}=e_{1}
and
e_{1}^{{\dagger}}=\lambda^{-1}\circ\hat{e}_{1}\circ\lambda=e_{1}
since
\lambda=\hat{\phi}\lambda_{0}\phi
and
\lambda_{0}^{-1}\pi\lambda_{0}=\pi
. Let us define
\ell_{0}=\tau(e_{1})\in\bar{L}
. Using Remark
2.8
, since
e_{1}
is symmetric and
e_{1}^{2}-e_{1}=0
we see that
\Tr(e_{1}^{2})=\Tr(e_{1})=2
and therefore
q/4(\ell_{0})=1
.
In
[
GHR19
, Prop. 3.3]
, it is proved that the isomorphism between
(E^{2},\lambda)
and

is unique up to interchanging

and

and exchanging

Lemma 4.4

Let

{{}^{t}m}&1\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})

We have

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{pmatrix}\textrm{adj}(S)&0\\

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}\det(S)\textrm{Id}_{n-1}&\textrm{adj}(S)m\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}\det(S)&0&0&*\\

5.
Primes of potentially decomposable reduction

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a maximal order in an imaginary quadratic field
K
and
P\in M_{2}(\mathcal{O})
be a hermitian matrix of determinant
1
which induces an indecomposable polarization
\lambda=\lambda_{0}P
on the square of any elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}
. Let
A_{2}
be the moduli space of principally polarised abelian surfaces.

Instead of looking at a particular elliptic curve and prime
\mathfrak{p}
over
p
, it is more natural to look at all Galois conjugates at the same time, i.e. all elliptic curves with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}
or all primes
\mathfrak{p}
over
p
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a prime
p
is a prime of
potential decomposable reduction
(PDR) if there exists a prime
\mathfrak{p}
over
p
in the Hilbert class field
H
of
K
and an elliptic curve
E/H
with CM by
\mathcal{O}
such that
(E^{2},\lambda)
has geometrically decomposable reduction at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Remark 5.2
.

We need to add “geometrically” to include the possible cases
p=d/4
which appears, for instance, when
d=8
. Indeed, only for such cases, the reduction of
(E^{2},\lambda)
is not decomposable over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
but possibly over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Since the elliptic curves
E
with CM by
\mathcal{O}
form a Galois orbit under
\Gal(H/K)
, it is redundant to go trough
\mathfrak{p}
and
E
simultaneously. In this section, we therefore fix a prime
\mathfrak{p}
over each
p
. All the results will be independent of the choice of these
\mathfrak{p}
.

Since
P\in M_{2}(K)
, the action of
\Gal(H/K)
does not affect the polarization
\lambda_{0}P
and we can consider the
K
-cycle given by the Galois orbit of
(E^{2},\lambda_{0}P)
. Actually, as it was shown in the proof of
[
GHR19
, Prop.4.1]
,
(E^{2},\lambda_{0}\bar{P})
is isomorphic to
(E^{2},\lambda_{0}P)
, so by taking the orbit under
\Gal(H/\mathbb{Q})
, we get a
\mathbb{Q}
-cycle where each
(E^{2},\lambda_{0}P)
is counted twice. We can therefore consider the
\mathbb{Q}
-cycle

\textrm{CM}(\mathcal{O},P):=\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)}((E^{\sigma})^{2},\lambda_{0}P)\subset A_{2}.

Notice that this cycle is still not necessarily reduced: an extreme case is the one studied in
[
GHR19
]
where all
((E^{\sigma})^{2},\lambda_{0}P)
are isomorphic, hence the support of the cycle is a single point in the moduli space.

5.1.
Arithmetic intersection

The formulation above brings us closer to an arithmetic intersection problem. Let
\mathcal{A}_{2}
be the moduli stack of principally polarised abelian schemes of relative dimension
2
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}
be the locus in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
of decomposable principally polarised abelian schemes.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
p
be a prime and
\mathfrak{p}|p
a prime ideal in
H
. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-cycle of
\mathcal{A}_{2}

\mathcal{CM}(\mathcal{O},P,p):=\bigsqcup(\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{2},\lambda_{0}P)

where, for
\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)
,
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
is the smooth model of
E^{\sigma}
over the integer ring
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
H_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Since our polarization
\lambda_{0}P
is indecomposble, this intersection is proper and we can define an arithmetic intersection multiplicity.

Proposition 5.4
.

A prime
p
is a prime of PDR if and only if the arithmetic intersection multiplicity
e_{\mathcal{O},P,p}:=(\mathcal{G}_{1}.\mathcal{CM}(\mathcal{O},P,p))\neq 0
.

since
\mathcal{P}
is principal.

6.
Factorization of the discriminant of genus
2
curves

In
[
Igu79
, Thm. 1]
, Igusa computes generators for the ring of Siegel modular forms for
\Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
with integral Fourier coefficients. More precisely, let

\mathcal{A}_{2}
be the moduli stack of principally polarised abelian schemes of relative dimension
2
. Let
\pi:\mathcal{V}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2}
be the universal abelian scheme and
\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{V}_{2}/\mathcal{A}_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2}
the rank
g
bundle, usually called
Hodge bundle
, induced by the relative regular differential forms of degree one on
\mathcal{V}_{2}
over
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. The relative canonical bundle over
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is the line bundle

\bm{\omega}=\bigwedge^{2}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{V}_{2}/\mathcal{A}_{2}}.

Igusa computes 15 generators for

S_{2}=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma(\mathcal{A}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{Z},\bm{\omega}^{\otimes h}),

the space of (geometric) Siegel modular forms over
\mathbb{Z}
.
The generators are expressed as polynomials in the algebraic theta constants defined by Mumford
[
Mum91
, Appendix I]
. As explained in
[
IKL+19
, Sec.3]
, by pulling back these sections to the space of Teichmüller forms for the moduli stack of curves
\mathcal{M}_{2}
, one can give expressions of the modular forms in terms of Igusa invariants
\underline{J}=(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{8},J_{10})
. After the correct choice of basis of differentials, we can make this map an equality, as Igusa does in his formula
[
Igu67
, p. 848]
and as we will do as well to keep the notation simple. We start with four modular forms in
S_{2}

\underline{\varphi}=(\varphi_{4},\varphi_{6},\varphi_{10},\varphi_{12})
:
\varphi_{m}
for
m=4,6
are the Einsenstein modular forms
\psi_{m}
in
[
Igu62
, p.189]
;
\varphi_{10}=-2^{2}\chi_{10}
and
\varphi_{12}=2^{2}\cdot 3\chi_{12}
where
\chi_{m}
are the normalised cusp forms in p. 195 in
loc. cit.
. They all are polynomials in the theta constants with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
(some theta constants can be divided by
1/2
and still have integral Fourier coefficients, see
[
Ich96
, p.415]
). Instead of the locus of Jacobians, one can also choose to restrict to the products of elliptic curves, as described in
[
Igu79
, p. 168]
.
All this allows Igusa to give a
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
-scheme isomorphisms between the coarse moduli space of curves
M_{2}
and an open sub-scheme of an affine scheme in
A_{2}

[
Igu60
, Thm. 2]
, and similarly for products of elliptic curves in
[
Igu79
, Lemma 15]
. We unify these two loci in this section. For simplicity in the presentation, we first present the case away from characteristic
2
or
3
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}][\underline{\varphi}]

with graduation given by the index of the variables, and let
X=\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}][\underline{\varphi}]
.
There is an isomorphism between the coarse moduli space of principally polarised abelian surfaces
A_{2}
and the open set
D(\varphi_{10})\cup D(\varphi_{12})
of
X
.

Proof.

The moduli space
A_{2}
decomposes into the union of the locus of Jacobian of genus 2 curves and the products of elliptic curves with the product polarization, which corresponds to the locus
\mathcal{G}_{1}
introduced previously. Recall that
\varphi_{10}=-2^{2}\cdot\chi_{10}=-\frac{1}{2^{12}}\prod_{\varepsilon\;\textrm{even}}\theta[\varepsilon]^{2}
where
\theta[\varepsilon]
are the theta constants. This is a primitive Siegel modular form over
\mathbb{Z}
and its zero locus inside
A_{2}
is
2\cdot\mathcal{G}_{1}
.

Hence, if
\varphi_{10}\neq 0
, we work over the locus of Jacobians and following
[
Igu67
, p. 848]
, we have the relations:

\displaystyle\varphi_{4}=J_{2}^{2}-2^{3}\cdot 3\cdot J_{4},\,\varphi_{6}=J_{2}^{3}-2^{2}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot J_{2}J_{4}+2^{3}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot J_{6},\,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{10}=J_{10}\text{ and }\varphi_{12}=J_{2}J_{10}.

They realise an isomorphism between the principal open
D(\varphi_{10})
of
X
and the coarse moduli space of smooth genus 2 curves
{M}_{2}
.

If
\varphi_{10}=0
, we consider the geometric point
E\times E^{\prime}
where
E
(resp.
E^{\prime}
) has invariants
(c_{4}:c_{6}:\Delta)
(resp.
(c^{\prime}_{4}:c^{\prime}_{6}:\Delta^{\prime})
) as in
[
Sil92
, p. 42]
. The product
E\times E^{\prime}
is uniquely determined by
(c_{4}c^{\prime}_{4}:c_{6}c^{\prime}_{6}:\Delta\Delta^{\prime})
since
j\cdot j^{\prime}=\frac{(c_{4}c^{\prime}_{4})^{3}}{\Delta\Delta^{\prime}}
and
j+j^{\prime}=1728+\frac{(c_{4}c^{\prime}_{4})^{3}-(c_{6}c^{\prime}_{6})^{2}}{1728\Delta\Delta^{\prime}}
.
The 10 even theta constants at
E\times E^{\prime}
are products of the theta constants for each elliptic curve, see for instance
[
RF74
, Chap.1, Thm. 11]
. Hence, an easy computation using the expressions of the Siegel modular forms in terms of theta constants in
[
Igu67
, p. 848]
gives

(6.2)

\varphi_{4}=c_{4}c^{\prime}_{4},\,\varphi_{6}=c_{6}c^{\prime}_{6},\,\varphi_{10}=0\text{ and }\varphi_{12}=2^{2}\cdot 3\cdot\Delta\Delta^{\prime}\neq 0.

These formula realise an explicit isomorphism between
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and the locus of polarised product of elliptic curves. The last 2 sets of equations allow to extend the isomorphism on the full
A_{2}
.
∎

For the case of characteristics
2
and
3
, we also need to include the following Siegel modular forms with, despite a first impression, have integral Fourier coefficients:

\displaystyle\varphi_{24}

\displaystyle=2^{-3}\cdot 3^{-1}\cdot(\varphi_{12}^{2}-\varphi_{10}^{2}\varphi_{4}),

\displaystyle\varphi_{36}

\displaystyle=2^{-3}\cdot 3^{-3}\cdot(\varphi_{6}\varphi_{10}^{3}-\varphi_{12}^{3}+2^{2}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot\varphi_{12}\varphi_{24}),

\displaystyle\varphi_{48}

\displaystyle=2^{-2}\cdot(\varphi_{12}\varphi_{36}-\varphi_{24}^{2}).

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}[\underline{\varphi}^{\prime}]=\mathbb{Z}[\varphi_{4},\varphi_{6},\varphi_{10},\varphi_{12},\varphi_{24},\varphi_{36},\varphi_{48}]/(R_{24},R_{36},R_{48})
with graduation given by the index of the variables and the relations being given by:

\displaystyle R_{24}=2^{3}\cdot 3\cdot\varphi_{24}-\varphi_{12}^{2}+\varphi_{10}^{2}\varphi_{4},

(6.3)

\displaystyle R_{36}=2^{3}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot\varphi_{36}-\varphi_{6}\varphi_{10}^{3}+\varphi_{12}^{3}-2^{2}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot\varphi_{12}\varphi_{24},\,R_{48}=2^{2}\cdot\varphi_{48}-\varphi_{12}\varphi_{36}-\varphi_{24}^{2}.

Set
X^{\prime}=\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb{Z}[\underline{\varphi^{\prime}}]/(R_{24},R_{36},R_{48})

There is an isomorphism between the coarse moduli space of principally polarised abelian surfaces
A_{2}
and the open set
D(\varphi_{10})\cup D(\varphi_{12})\cup D(\varphi_{24})\cup D(\varphi_{36})\cup D(\varphi_{48})
, of
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as the one of Proposition
6.1
. We only need the new identities:

•

For Jacobians of genus 2 curves

\displaystyle\varphi_{4}=J_{2}^{2}-2^{3}\cdot 3\cdot J_{4},\,\varphi_{6}=J_{2}^{3}-2^{2}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot J_{2}J_{4}+2^{3}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot J_{6},\,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{10}=J_{10},\,\varphi_{12}=J_{2}J_{10},\,\varphi_{24}=J_{4}J_{10}^{2},\,\varphi_{36}=J_{6}J_{10}^{3},\,\varphi_{48}=J_{8}J_{10}^{4}.

•

For products of elliptic curves

\displaystyle\varphi_{4}=c_{4}c^{\prime}_{4},\,\varphi_{6}=c_{6}c^{\prime}_{6},\,\varphi_{10}=0,\,\varphi_{12}=2^{2}\cdot 3\cdot\Delta\Delta^{\prime},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{24}=2\cdot 3\cdot\Delta^{2}{\Delta^{\prime}}^{2},\,\varphi_{36}=2^{2}\cdot\Delta^{3}{\Delta^{\prime}}^{3},\,\varphi_{48}=3\Delta^{4}{\Delta^{\prime}}^{4}.

∎

Remark 6.3
.

In
[
Igu79
, Thm. 1]
, Igusa needs more Siegel modular forms to generate the ring
S_{2}
than we use in Proposition
6.2
. He does indeed describe the Satake compactification of
A_{2}
,
[
Igu62
, Sec. 9]
, when
we describe here a strict sublocus since we do not consider points for which
\phi_{10}=\phi_{12}=0
.

The previous isomorphisms allow us to established a simple relation between the normalised valuations of the Siegel modular forms and of the invariants over a discrete valuation ring
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
with valuation
v
. We recall that we defined
the normalised valuation of
J_{10}
at
\mathfrak{p}
by

v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{10})-10\cdot\min\left(\frac{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{n})}{n},\,n\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}\right).

Similarly, for the set
\underline{\varphi^{\prime}}
(or
\underline{\varphi}
), we can define

v_{\underline{\varphi^{\prime}}}(\varphi_{10})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varphi_{10})-10\cdot\min\left(\frac{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varphi_{n})}{n},\,n\in\{4,6,10,12,24,36,48\}\right).

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a discrete valuation ring with valuation
v
. Let
x\in A_{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a point corresponding to the Jacobian of a stable model of a genus
2
curve defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
with decomposable reduction. Then,

v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))=6v_{\underline{\varphi^{\prime}}}(\varphi_{10}(x)).

Proof.

We do the proof when the residue field is of characteristic different from 2 or 3, the proof is simply more cumbersome for the latter cases as one needs to consider the extra Siegel modular forms in Proposition
6.2
. By (
6.2
), since the special fiber is the product of two elliptic curves, the geometric situation is described by

v(\varphi_{4}(x))\geq 4v,\,v(\varphi_{6}(x))\geq 6v,\,v(\varphi_{10}(x))=10v+w,\,v(\varphi_{12}(x))\geq 12v,

with at least one of the inequalities being an equality. So, we get
v_{\underline{\varphi}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{10}(x))=w
.
Reversing the equalities (
6.1
) we obtain:

\displaystyle J_{2}=\varphi_{12}/\varphi_{10},\,J_{4}=2^{-3}\cdot 3^{-1}(\varphi_{12}^{2}/\varphi_{10}^{2}-\varphi_{4}),

(6.6)

\displaystyle J_{6}=2^{-3}\cdot 3^{-3}(\varphi_{6}+2^{-1}\cdot 3^{2}\varphi_{4}\varphi_{12}/\varphi_{10}-2^{-1}\varphi_{12}^{3}/\varphi_{10}^{3}),\,J_{10}=\varphi_{10}.

Hence,

v(J_{2}(C))\geq 2v-w,\,v(J_{4}(C))\geq 4v-2w,\,v(J_{6}(C))\geq 6v-3w\text{ and }v(J_{10}(C))=10v+w,

with at least one of the inequalities being an equality.
So, we conclude that

v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))=10v+w-5(2v-w)=6w

and the result follows.

∎

It remains to connect these normalised valuations with the local intersection multiplicity in Section
5.3
. For sake of simplicity, we explain here again the case of characteristic of the residue field different from 2 and 3. Proposition
6.1
shows that locally around
x\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
, we can take
\varphi_{12}=1
and the points we are interested in have local ring

\mathcal{O}_{\varphi_{10}\cap\mathcal{CM}(\mathcal{O},P,p),x}=\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varphi_{4},\varphi_{6},\varphi_{10}]/(\varphi_{4}-x_{1},\varphi_{6}-x_{2},\varphi_{10}-x_{3},\varphi_{10})=\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{v(x_{3})}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Now
v(x_{3})
is the normalised valuation
v_{\underline{\varphi}}(\varphi_{10}(x))
and since
2\cdot\mathcal{G}_{1}=(\varphi_{10})
, we see that
v_{\underline{\varphi}}(\varphi_{10}(x))
is twice the length of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}\cap\mathcal{CM}(\mathcal{O},P,p),x}
. Hence

(6.7)

v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))=6\cdot v_{\underline{\varphi}}(\phi_{10}(x))=12\cdot\textrm{length}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}\cap\mathcal{CM}(\mathcal{O},P,p),x})

We therefore conclude with our final result.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a maximal order in a quadratic imaginary field
K
of discriminant
-d<0
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}
defined over the Hilbert class field
H
of
K
. Let
(E^{2},\lambda_{0}P)
be a principally polarised abelian surface corresponding to the Jacobian of a curve
C
over
H
. Let
p
be a prime of potential decomposable reduction. If
p
is inert in
\mathcal{O}
or
p=d/4
(resp.
p
is ramified and different from
d/4
), then the exponent at
p
,
\sum_{\mathfrak{p}|p}v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))
, of the absolute normalised discriminant is
12\cdot e_{\mathcal{O},P,p}
(resp.
6\cdot e_{\mathcal{O},P,p}
).

Proof.

Using (
6.7
), we would simply get a factor 12 if running on the Galois conjugates of
C
. But in this section, we decided to fix the curve
C
and to run through the
\mathfrak{p}|p
instead. We therefore need Lemma
3.1
to see the correspondence between the
\mathfrak{p}|p
ad the Galois conjugates. This translates into:

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)}v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C^{\sigma})),\text{ if }p\text{ is inert in }\mathcal{O},\\
\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)}v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C^{\sigma}))/2,\text{ if }p\text{ is ramified and }p\neq d/4,\\
\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)}v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C^{\sigma})),\text{ if }p=d/4.\end{cases}

∎

Example 6.6
.

Let us conclude with our running example for
d=163
. In Example
5.7
, we got
e_{\mathcal{O},P,17}=1
. Since
17
is inert, we shall get
12\cdot 1
for the exponent of the absolute normalised discriminant at 17. Now
D=J_{10}(C)
and since
\mathfrak{p}=(17)
is inert in
H=K
, we see that
v(J_{2})=v(J_{4})=v(J_{6})=v(J_{8})=0
so that
v(D)=v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))=12
and the results are coherent.

For the interesting case of
p=2
, we get

(v(J_{2}(C)),v(J_{4}(C)),v(J_{6}(C)),v(J_{8}(C)),v(J_{10}(C)))=(6,9,14,16,32)\\
=(2+2\cdot 2,1+2\cdot 4,2+2\cdot 6,0+2\cdot 8,12+2\cdot 10)

and therefore
v_{\underline{J}}(J_{10}(C))=12
without introducing the correction factor
2^{-12}
in Equation (
1.2
).

Experimental results, observations and conjectures

In this section, we first run experiments for
\mathcal{O}
of class number
1
(Table

)
and then for

(Tables

7.1.
Explicit construction.

Giving a maximal order
\mathcal{O}=Z[\omega]
of an imaginary quadratic field

of discriminant
-d

\mathcal{O}

(i)

following
[

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

for each prime

[

7.3.
Observations

Formula (
7.1
) works amazingly well when
d
is a prime. When
d
is an odd composite number, it fails detecting primes that divide
d
(cases denoted
*
in the table) which may lead to some side effects that we observe on other primes (cases denoted
**
in the table).

We also observe in our experiments that the primes output by the refined sieve with the congruences relations (
4.3
) and which are inert or ramified in
\mathcal{O}
seems to actually be the set of primes of PDR directly, with the possible exception of having an extra prime dividing
d
. In other words, we conjecture that if a matrix

(7.2)

T=\begin{pmatrix}\Gram(L)&\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\\
z\end{array}\\
\begin{array}[]{ccc}x&y&z\end{array}&1\end{pmatrix}>0

with
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
satisfying the congruences (
4.3
) has a determinant divisible by a prime
p\nmid d
then
p
is a prime of PDR. For
p|d
, it is less clear as
we found exceptions for
d<200
. For certain polarizations the pairs

(d,p)=\{(40,5),(91,7),(104,13),(120,5),(136,17),(168,7),(184,7),(187,11),(195,13)\}

do appear for matrices
T
but
p
is not a PDR for the respective polarization.
If the conjecture is true, it means that we can detect a prime of PDR simply in terms of these conditions without doing computations with the embeddings of
\mathcal{O}
in the quaternion orders. However, as observed in Remark
4.9
, some matrices
T
do not come from true solutions in
\Ends(\bar{E}^{2},\bar{\lambda})
. A possible refinement would be to keep only the matrices
T
for which the Gross-Keating invariants do exist: this would exclude the second matrix of the Remark
4.9
since its Gross-Keating invariants are
(0,0,2)
and this would give a fractional value for
e_{\ell_{0}}
. However, this condition does not reduce the list of exceptions above.

CP Violation in Charmed Meson Decays into Final States with η ′ \eta^{\prime}
Carolina Bolognani, Ulrich Nierste, Stefan Schacht, K. Keri Vos

Abstract
We derive Standard Model predictions for the CP asymmetries of singly-Cabibbo suppressed D → P ​ η ′ D\rightarrow P\eta^{\prime} decays, where P = K , π , η P=K,\pi,\eta . Our predictions are based on the approximate SU(3) F symmetry of QCD and include first-order symmetry-breaking effects in a systematic way. The underlying symmetry leads to correlations between different decay modes. To this end we predict the correlations between a CP dir ​ ( D + → π + ​ η ′ ) a_{\rm CP}^{\rm dir}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}) and a CP dir ​ ( D s + → K + ​ η ′ ) a_{\rm CP}^{\rm dir}(D_{s}^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime}) as well as a CP dir ​ ( D 0 → π 0 ​ η ′ ) a_{\rm CP}^{\rm dir}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime}) and a CP dir ​ ( D 0 → η ​ η ′ ) a_{\rm CP}^{\rm dir}(D^{0}\to\eta\eta^{\prime}) . Our results can be used to probe the Standard Model with future measurements by LHCb, Belle II and BESIII. At the same time, such future measurements permit the extraction of the key theory parameter related to the ratio of color-suppressed to color-favoured contributions to the decay amplitudes. Future data on both branching ratios and CP asymmetries will automatically improve our predictions and thereby also their sensitivity to physics beyond the Standard Model.

Notation

We use the notation introduced in Ref.
24
and recall here only some key elements.
The underlying quark-level transitions of singly Cabibbo-suppressed (SCS) charm decays are
c\to d\overline{d}u
and
c\to s\overline{s}u
.
We write the amplitude of SCS decays as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{SCS}}(d)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{sd}\mathcal{A}_{sd}(d)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)\,,

(1)

with

\displaystyle\lambda_{sd}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{d}}{2}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{d}}{2}\,,

(3)

and the combinations of elements of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix

\displaystyle\lambda_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv V_{cq}^{*}V_{uq}\,.

(4)

Consequently, we write the CKM factors that appear in
c\to s\overline{s}u
and
c\to d\overline{d}u
transitions as

\displaystyle\lambda_{s}=\lambda_{sd}-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2},

(5)

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}=-\lambda_{sd}-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2},

(6)

respectively. The Cabibbo suppression of
\lambda_{b}
versus
\lambda_{sd}
makes the
\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)
contribution to the branching ratios negligible,
i.e.
, we have to a very good approximation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(d)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}(d)\left|\lambda_{sd}\mathcal{A}_{sd}(d)\right|^{2}\,,

(7)

where the phase space function is given as
63
;
62

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(d)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{D}}{16\pi m_{D}^{3}}\sqrt{\left(m_{D}^{2}-(m_{P_{1}}-m_{P_{2}})^{2}\right)\left(m_{D}^{2}-(m_{P_{1}}+m_{P_{2}})^{2}\right)}

(8)

Eq. (
1
) makes clear that in SCS charm decays, CP violation emerges from the interference of CKM-leading with the CKM-subleading amplitudes.
The direct CP asymmetries are defined as

a_{CP}^{\textrm{dir}}(d)=\frac{\left|\mathcal{A}^{\text{SCS}}(d)\right|^{2}-\left|\mathcal{\overline{A}}^{\text{SCS}}(\overline{d})\right|^{2}}{\left|\mathcal{A}^{\text{SCS}}(d)\right|^{2}+\left|\mathcal{\overline{A}}^{\text{SCS}}(\overline{d})\right|^{2}},

(9)

and, neglecting subleading powers of
\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{sd}\sim 10^{-3}
, given as
42
;
68
;
65

a_{CP}^{\textrm{dir}}(d)=\mathrm{Im}\frac{\lambda_{b}}{\lambda_{sd}}\mathrm{Im}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)}{\mathcal{A}_{sd}(d)}

(10)

which depends on the relative strong phase between the CKM-leading and -subleading amplitude.
For the
\left|\eta_{8}\right>
and
\left|\eta_{0}\right>
states we use the definitions

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{8}\right>

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\left|u\overline{u}\right>+\left|d\overline{d}\right>-2\left|s\overline{s}\right>}{\sqrt{6}}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{0}\right>

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\left|u\overline{u}\right>+\left|d\overline{d}\right>+\left|s\overline{s}\right>}{\sqrt{3}}\,,

(12)

respectively. Furthermore, we write the hadronic matrix elements as

\displaystyle\left<P\eta\right|H\left|D\right>

\displaystyle=\,\cos\theta\left<P\eta_{8}\right|H\left|D\right>-\sin\theta\left<P\eta_{0}\right|H\left|D\right>

(13)

\displaystyle\left<P\eta^{\prime}\right|H\left|D\right>

\displaystyle=\,\sin\theta\left<P\eta_{8}\right|H\left|D\right>^{\prime}+\cos\theta\left<P\eta_{0}\right|H\left|D\right>^{\prime}\,,

(14)

with a mixing angle that respects the SU(3)
F
power counting

\theta=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon\sim 30\%
is the parameter of SU(3)
F
breaking. The mixing angle
\theta
will subsequently always appear together with
SU(3)
F
-breaking matrix elements into which it can be absorbed.
As laid out in detail in Ref.
24
, the large mass splitting
m_{\eta}\neq m_{\eta^{\prime}}
leads to the inequalities

\displaystyle\left<P\eta_{8}\right|H\left|D\right>^{\prime}\neq\left<P\eta_{8}\right|H\left|D\right>\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\left<P\eta_{0}\right|H\left|D\right>^{\prime}\neq\left<P\eta_{0}\right|H\left|D\right>\,.

(16)

The difference between these matrix elements is an
\mathcal{O}(1)
effect that goes beyond the SU(3)
F
power counting.
This leads to a decoupling of the
D\rightarrow P\eta^{\prime}
system from
D\rightarrow P\eta
and
D\rightarrow PP
,
as long as no further assumptions on the dependences of the matrix elements on
m_{\eta^{(\prime)}}
are adopted.

3
Topological Decomposition of CKM-subleading Amplitudes

3.1
Decomposition

The topological decomposition of the CKM-leading amplitudes
\mathcal{A}_{sd}(d)
is given in Ref.
24
, parametrized by topological tree (T), colour-suppressed (C), annihilation (A), and exchange (E) topologies. Here, we derive the corresponding CKM-subleading amplitudes
\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)
, which, through interference with the leading amplitudes lead to CP violation.
We show our result for the complete decomposition of
\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)
in terms of topological amplitudes
\mathcal{T}_{i}
and the
SU(3)_{F}
-breaking topologies
\mathcal{T}_{i}^{(1)}
in
Tables
3
,
4
and
5
in Appendix
A
. We note that the subleading amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{b}
consists of a combination of topologies already present in
\mathcal{A}_{sd}(d)
and defined in Ref.
24
and additional topologies that only contribute to
\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)
that we define in Fig.
4
. We label the topologies depending on whether the
\eta_{0}
singlet is formed by the outgoing quark or antiquark by
18
and
81
, respectively. In addition, the
11
and
88
subscript indicate the contribution from the suppressed singlet state of the
\eta
and the suppressed octet state of the
\eta^{\prime}
, respectively. Notably, additional topologies that appear are penguin (P) and penguin annihilation (PA) diagrams, for which we use the notation
61

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\equiv P_{d}+P_{s}-2P_{b}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle PA

\displaystyle\equiv PA_{d}+PA_{s}-2PA_{b}\,,

(18)

where the respective subscript indicates the flavour of the quark running in the loop. Analogous definitions apply to
P_{88}^{(1)}
,
P_{11}^{(1)}
and

. Here,

are the hairpin penguin diagrams,

Like penguin diagrams, such diagrams also contribute to the branching ratios, but only in the combination

\displaystyle P_{H,\mathrm{break}}\equiv P_{H,s}-P_{H,d}\,,

(19)

where the index denotes the quark that runs in the loop.
The broken hairpin penguin diagrams contributing to the CKM-leading amplitudes

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{sd}(D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\supset\frac{3}{\sqrt{6}}P_{H,\mathrm{break}}^{(1)}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{sd}(D^{0}\rightarrow\eta\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\supset\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}P_{H,\mathrm{break}}^{(1)}\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{sd}(D^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\supset\frac{3}{\sqrt{3}}P_{H,\mathrm{break}}^{(1)}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{sd}(D_{s}^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\eta)

\displaystyle\supset\frac{3}{\sqrt{3}}P_{H,\mathrm{break}}^{(1)}\,,

(23)

can be absorbed into
P^{(1)}_{18,\mathrm{break}}
and
P^{(1)}_{81,\mathrm{break}}

24

\mathcal{A}_{b}(d)
and
\mathcal{A}_{sd}(d)

3.2
CP asymmetry sum rules

From the redefined topological decomposition given in Table
1
as well as the corresponding table for the CKM-leading amplitudes in Ref.
24
, we can also read off the
SU(3)_{F}
limit amplitudes

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{SCS}}_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{sd}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{C}_{81}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\overline{C}_{18}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{C}_{81}\right)\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{SCS}}_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}(D^{0}\to\eta\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{sd}\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{C}_{81}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{2}}\overline{C}_{18}-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}\hat{C}_{81}\right)\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{SCS}}_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{sd}\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{T}_{18}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\overline{C}_{18}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{T}_{18}\right)\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{SCS}}_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}(D_{s}^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{sd}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{T}_{18}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\overline{C}_{18}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{T}_{18}\right)\,.

(36)

Previously, when considering only the leading amplitude in Ref.
24
, we noticed a decoupling of the neutral and charged modes as can be seen above. We note that in the
SU(3)_{F}
limit, the subleading terms depend on a new
SU(3)_{F}
limit parameter
\overline{C}_{18}
that connects the charged and neutral modes. We discuss this parameter in more detail below.

From the amplitudes above, the
SU(3)_{F}
-limit sum rules for the direct CP asymmetries follow

\displaystyle\left.a_{CP}^{\text{dir}}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime})\right|_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}

\displaystyle=-3\,\left.a_{CP}^{\text{dir}}(D^{0}\to\eta\eta^{\prime})\right|_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\left.a_{CP}^{\text{dir}}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})\right|_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}

\displaystyle=-\,\left.a_{CP}^{\text{dir}}(D_{s}^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime})\right|_{\text{SU(3)${}_{F}$-lim.}}\,.

(38)

We can obtain a modified version of the sum rules, accounting for part of SU(3)
F
-breaking effects in analogy to the sum rule given in Ref.

\displaystyle\frac{a_{CP}^{\rm dir}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime})}{a_{CP}^{\rm dir}(D^{0}\to\eta\eta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-3\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{P}(D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime})}{\mathcal{P}(D^{0}\rightarrow\eta\eta^{\prime})}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to\eta\eta^{\prime})}{\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\eta^{\prime})}}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{CP}^{\rm dir}(D^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})}{a_{CP}^{\rm dir}(D_{s}^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{P}(D^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})}{\mathcal{P}(D_{s}^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\eta^{\prime})}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{B}(D_{s}^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime})}{\mathcal{B}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})}}\,,

(40)

where we choose to also divide out the phase space factors, see Eq. (
8
).
The form of Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
) is motivated through the dependence of
a_{CP}^{\mathrm{dir}}(d)
on the CKM-leading amplitude in Eq. (
10
). Note that the exact form of the SU(3)
F
-limit sum rules is somewhat ambiguous and depends on how exactly the SU(3)
F
limit is defined.

4
Predictions for CP Asymmetries

4.1
Numerical Inputs and Setup of the Analysis

The general setup of our frequentist statistical analysis
is described in detail in the context of the branching ratio fit performed in Ref.
24
.
In particular, we use the same branching ratio inputs as in Ref.
63
.
Note that for consistency with our previous work we therefore do not include the recent additional measurement of
\mathcal{B}(D^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime})
from
12
, which is fully consistent with the world average.
In addition to the eight branching ratio data measurements, measurements of direct CP asymmetries are available for the two charged decay modes.
The HFLAV
16
averages are

\displaystyle\left.a^{\text{dir}}_{CP}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})\right|_{\text{exp}}

\displaystyle=(0.4\pm 0.2)\%\,,\quad\text{\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{HeavyFlavorAveragingGroupHFLAV:2024ctg, CLEO:2009fiz, Belle:2011tmj, LHCb:2022pxf}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}

(41)

\displaystyle\left.a^{\text{dir}}_{CP}(D_{s}^{+}\to K^{+}\eta^{\prime})\right|_{\text{exp}}

\displaystyle=(6.0\pm 18.9)\%\,.\quad\text{\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{HeavyFlavorAveragingGroupHFLAV:2024ctg, CLEO:2009fiz}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}

(42)

The measurement in Eq. (
42
) has no constraining power due to
its large uncertainty. Its very large central value would artificially influence the global minimum of our fit while only
marginally constraining the involved parameters. Therefore, we do not include the measurement Eq. (
42
) in our fit. Note that we focus here on direct CP violation, while in the experiment, time-integrated or time-dependent measurements are performed.
In case of the neutral
D^{0}
decays, such effects have to be subtracted from the experimental results
41
;
55
;
64
.

As in the branching ratio analysis of Ref.
24
, we impose constraints on the size of
SU(3)_{F}
-breaking in the subleading amplitudes of all SCS decays

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{A}_{b,\cancel{\mathrm{{SU(3)}}}}(D\to P\eta^{\prime})}{\mathcal{A}_{b,\mathrm{{SU(3)-lim}}}(D\to P\eta^{\prime})}\right|\leq\varepsilon\,,

(43)

where
\varepsilon
is the amount of
SU(3)_{F}
-breaking allowed.
Note that consequently, we impose this constraint separately on both the CKM-leading and CKM-subleading part of the amplitudes.

The
\chi^{2}
function is determined as

\chi^{2}=(\vec{y}_{\text{data}}-\vec{y}_{\text{theo}})^{T}{\text{Cov}^{-1}}(\vec{y}_{\text{data}}-\vec{y}_{\text{theo}})\,,

(44)

where
\vec{y}_{\text{data}}
refers to the experimental input,
Cov
is the covariance matrix for the experimental results and
\vec{y}_{\text{theo}}
is the theoretical parametrisation of the respective observables based on Table
1
. We minimise this function using the Sequential Least SQuares Programming (SLSQP) algorithm implemented in SciPy
57
;
71
. For all results, minimisations are performed 100 times from random starting points for all parameters.

The
\Delta\mathcal{A}_{b}
parameters of Table
1
are normalised to
|\hat{T}_{18}|
and all phases are defined relative to the tree amplitude.
The fit contains
17+8=25
parameters
for
8+1=9
observables,
with a total of
8+4=12

SU(3)_{F}
-breaking constraints on the amplitudes.

A
\chi^{2}
scan of the allowed
SU(3)_{F}
breaking
\varepsilon
reproduces the pattern of Figure 2 of Ref.
24
. In the limit of
SU(3)_{F}
symmetry for both
\mathcal{A}_{sd}
and
\Delta\mathcal{A}_{b}
,
i.e.

\varepsilon=0
, the scan yields a
5.6\sigma
discrepancy with respect to a perfect description of the data,
\chi^{2}=0
, which is reached for
\varepsilon=50\%
, consistent with the branching ratio only fit and confirming the exclusion of the
SU(3)_{F}
limit. At
30\%

SU(3)_{F}
-breaking, the same
\chi^{2}=6.01
of the branching ratio fit is obtained, which
indicates that the additional CP violating parameters absorb the effect of the single experimental result.
In the following, we fix

\displaystyle\varepsilon=30\%\,.

(45)

in all fits and calculate the
\Delta\chi^{2}
with respect to the corresponding global minimum.

We stress that the physical constraints on SU(3)
F
-breaking that we impose on the fit always apply to parts of amplitudes, not to single parameters.
In that way, our fit results remain independent of the chosen reparametrisation of parameters.

For technical reasons, we include very wide bounds on the SU(3)
F
-breaking parameters
\mathcal{T}^{(1)}_{i}
. These bounds have no physical meaning and are just a technical choice when performing the fit.
We checked that our results are not touching this boundary, and are therefore independent of this choice.

\Delta\chi^{2}

The SU(3)
F
limit parameter
\overline{C}_{18}
, which is a combination of color-suppressed tree and penguin diagrams, plays an important role for the prediction of the considered CP asymmetries, as it is the only SU(3)
F
limit parameter involved which is not constrained by branching ratio measurements.
In Fig.
1
we show its
\Delta\chi^{2}
profile. Starting from
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|\sim 2
, we observe a plateau with
\Delta\chi^{2}=0
,
i.e.
, a perfect description of the data.
This is a reflection of the lack of data on CP asymmetries to constrain the fit: For a significantly large enough
\overline{C}_{18}
, its relative phase to the CKM-leading amplitude can always be tuned such that the single measured CP asymmetry is perfectly described.
At smaller magnitudes, however, the
\Delta\chi^{2}
increases, with
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|=1
yielding
\Delta\chi^{2}=0.95
.
The expectation from a
1/N_{c}
power counting
27
;
73
would be
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|=1/3
, which gives
\Delta\chi^{2}=2.45
, corresponding to a tension with the data at
1.6\sigma
.

In order to make predictions for the CP asymmetries it is necessary to impose a constraint on
\overline{C}_{18}
. Once more data on CP asymmetries is available, it will be possible to determine

\left|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}\right|
directly from the data. Until then, for the remainder of our work, we impose a conservative constraint of

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\overline{C}_{18}}{\hat{T}_{18}}\right|\leq 3\,.

In Fig.

Our analysis indicates how far the CP asymmetries can deviate from these sum rules once SU(3)
F
-breaking contributions are taken into account. If future measurements deviate from these correlations, or find individual CP asymmetries beyond our predictions, this could indicate that
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|
is larger than our conservative bound in Eq. (

Hints for a corresponding related enhancement have been seen in the
D\rightarrow\pi\pi

e.g.
the fit results for the ratio of penguin over tree diagrams with central values of
\sim 5

1
. We stress that in our case, due to the reparametrization Eq. (
29

D\to P\eta^{\prime}
, we would obtain non-vanishing CP asymmetries even in the limit where all penguin diagrams are zero.

New measurements of the CP asymmetries are therefore of key importance. Such future measurements could then be used to extract the parameter
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|
directly from data.
In order to facilitate this extraction we provide in Fig.
3
the allowed ranges for the CP asymmetries as a function of
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|
. Once a CP asymmetry is measured, the corresponding interval of
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|
can be read off from the corresponding plot.
Several CP asymmetry measurements would then allow to check for consistency between the different modes and the validity of our
SU(3)_{F}
-based analysis.

In particular, Figure
3(a)
shows that the current data for
a_{CP}^{dir}(D^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})
prefers

\displaystyle|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|\gtrsim 1\,.

(47)

With future more precise data on
a_{CP}^{dir}(D^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime})

1/N_{c}

|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|\sim 0.3
.

5
Conclusions

The direct CP asymmetries of SCS
D\rightarrow P\eta^{\prime}
decays are basically uncharted territory and offer a big opportunity to further explore CP violation in the charm sector. We provide SM predictions based on the approximate SU(3)
F
symmetry of QCD including first order breaking effects that allow the interpretation of future data. In our global analysis we allow for
30\%
linear
SU(3)_{F}
-breaking effects.

The major results of this paper are the correlations between the four CP asymmetries obtained from our global theory fit to the available
D\rightarrow P\eta^{\prime}
data in Fig.
2
. These plots show how far the CP asymmetries can deviate from their SU(3)
F
limit sum rules, and serve as a future test of the consistency of the data.

Compared to the CKM-leading amplitude relevant for branching ratio studies, the CKM-subleading part, crucial for CP violation, is described by one additional
SU(3)_{F}
limit parameter
\overline{C}_{18}
which also contains the penguin topologies.
The current measurement of the
D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}
CP asymmetry places a weak constraint on the ratio
|\overline{C}_{18}/\hat{T}_{18}|\gtrsim 1
, which probes the CKM-subleading over CKM-leading amplitudes. Further constraining this ratio from experimental data is key to understanding CP violation in charm, not only in
D\to P\eta^{\prime}
but also beyond, and to probe expectations based on the
1/N_{c}
power counting. Our predictions displayed in Fig.
3
can be used to extract this ratio directly from data, allowing for the early identification of possible deviations from the Standard Model.

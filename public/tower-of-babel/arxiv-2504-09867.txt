Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated with Laguerre expansions and higher order Riesz transforms
The Anh Bui, Xuan Thinh Duong

Abstract
. Let ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} be the Laguerre differential operator which is the self-adjoint extension of the differential operator L ν := ∑ i = 1 n [ − ∂ 2 ∂ x i 2 + x i 2 + 1 x i 2 ​ ( ν i 2 − 1 4 ) ] L_{\nu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\left(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\right] initially defined on C c ∞ ​ ( ℝ + n ) C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}) as its natural domain, where ν ∈ [ − 1 / 2 , ∞ ) n \nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . In this paper, we first develop the theory of Hardy spaces H ℒ ν p H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}} associated with ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} for the full range p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] p\in(0,1] . 1.
Introduction

For each
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, we consider the Laguerre differential operator

\displaystyle L_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\Big(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\Big],\quad\quad x\in(0,\infty)^{n}.

The
j
-th partial derivative associated with
L_{\nu}
is given by

\delta_{\nu_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then the adjoint of
\delta_{\nu_{j}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is

\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

It is straightforward that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{*}\delta_{\nu_{i}}=L_{\nu}{-2}(|\nu|+n).

Let
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
, and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
be multi-indices. The Laguerre function
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is defined as

\varphi_{k}^{\nu}(x)=\varphi^{\nu_{1}}_{k_{1}}(x_{1})\ldots\varphi^{\nu_{n}}_{k_{n}}(x_{n}),\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where
\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}
are the one-dimensional Laguerre functions

\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}(x_{i})=\Big(\frac{2\Gamma(k_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(k_{i}+\nu_{i}+1)}\Big)^{1/2}L_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(x_{i}^{2})x_{i}^{\nu_{i}+1/2}e^{-x_{i}^{2}/2},\quad x_{i}>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

Note that for
\nu_{i}>-1
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}
denotes the Laguerre polynomial of degree
k_{i}
and order
\nu_{i}
outlined in
[
20
, p.76]
.

Then it is well-known that the system
\{\varphi_{k}^{\nu}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
. Moreover, each
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
is an eigenfunction of
L_{\nu}
corresponding to the eigenvalue of
4|k|+2|\nu|+2n
, i.e.,

(1)

L_{\nu}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\varphi_{k}^{\nu},

where
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\ldots+\nu_{n}
and
|k|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}
. The operator
L_{\nu}
is positive and symmetric in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
.

If
\nu=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\ldots,-\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
L_{\nu}
becomes the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Note that the Riesz transforms associated to the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
were investigated in
[
29
,
30
]
.

The operator

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}

defined on the domain
\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)|\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\}
is a self-adjoint extension of
L_{\nu}
(the inclusion
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\subset\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
may be easily verified), has the discrete spectrum
\{4\ell+2|\nu|+2n:\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and admits the spectral decomposition

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(4\ell+2|\nu|+2n)P_{\nu,\ell}f,

where the spectral projections are

P_{\nu,\ell}f=\sum_{|k|=\ell}\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}.

Moreover,

(2)

\delta_{\nu_{j}}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}}\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}},\ \ \delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}+1}\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}},

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here and later on we use the convention that
\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}}=0
if
k_{j}-1<0
and
\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}}=0
if
\nu_{j}-1<0
. See for example
[
25
]
.

The first goal of the paper is to develop a new theory on Hardy spaces and BMO-type spaces associated with the Laguerre operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. The study of function spaces associated with operators is an active area of research in harmonic analysis, attracting significant interest. See, for example,
[
10
,
16
,
31
,
7
,
11
,
18
,
28
,
5
,
32
]
and references therein. A key problem in this direction is to characterize Hardy spaces via new atomic decompositions as in
[
11
,
32
,
5
]
, rather than using abstract atom definitions based on the underlying operator as in
[
10
,
16
,
31
]
.

Let us provide an overview of this research direction. Let
L=-\Delta+V
be a Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
, where
V
is a nonnegative potential satisfying a suitable reverse Hölder inequality. Define

(3)

\rho(x)=\sup\Big\{r>0:\frac{1}{r^{n-2}}\int_{B}V(x)dx\leq 1\Big\}.

Let
p\in(0,1]
. A function
a
is called a
(p,\rho)
-atom associated to the ball
B(x_{0},r)
if

(i)

{\rm supp}\,a\subset B(x_{0},r)
;

(ii)

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|B(x_{0},r)|^{-1/p}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n(1/p-1)
if
r<\rho(x_{0})
.

Then we define the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the completion of all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\inf\Big\{\Big(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}:f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$},\text{where $a_{j}$ are $(p,\rho)$ atoms}\Big\}.

We also have the Hardy
H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined as the completion of all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-tL}f|\|_{p}.

Under the reverse Hölder condition

(4)

\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)^{n/2}dx\Big)^{2/n}\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)dx,

for all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it was shown in
[
11
]
that
H^{1}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H^{1}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincide. In particular, for polynomial potentials
V
, these spaces coincide for all
p\in(0,1]
(see
[
12
]
). Later, Yang and Zhou
[
32
]
extended these results to Hardy spaces on metric space
(X,d)
equipped with a doubling measure in the sense of Coifmann and Weiss
[
9
]
by introducing a critical function
\rho
satisfying

(5)

\rho(y)\lesssim\rho(x)\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{k_{0}}{k_{0}+1}}

for some
k_{0}>0
. They developed atomic Hardy spaces
H^{1}_{\rho}(X)
and provided maximal function characterizations, leading to applications in settings such as Lie groups, doubling manifolds, and Heisenberg groups.

In the Laguerre setting, similar results were shown in
[
13
]
for
n=1
,
\nu>-1/2
, and
p=1
, later extended to
n\geq 1
,
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
, and
p\in(\frac{n}{n+1},1]
in
[
3
]
. This paper advances the theory further by covering the full range
p\in(0,1]
(Theorems
4.4
and
4.7
). In addition, we also investigate the BMO function type spaces, which will be proved as
the dual spaces of the Hardy spaces. The extension presents challenges since:

•

The potential
V
in
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
is neither a polynomial nor satisfies (
4
).

•

The critical function (
6
) does not satisfy (
5
) as in
[
32
]
.

•

The heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
lacks certain conditions, including the Hölder continuity (see
[
5
]
).

•

Extending results from
p\in(\frac{n}{n+1},1]
to
p\in(0,1]
requires new techniques (see Section 3). Note that, until now, such results for the full range
p\in(0,1]
were only known in the setting of the Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and on the Heisenberg group with a
nonnegative polynomial
potential; see
[
12
,
4
]
. In our setting, the potential is not a polynomial, and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
can be viewed as a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which introduces additional difficulties.

The second goal of this paper is to investigate the boundedness of the higher-order Riesz transform

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{n},

where
\delta_{\nu}^{k}=\delta_{\nu_{n}}^{k_{n}}\ldots\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{k_{1}}
. The study of Riesz transforms in the context of orthogonal expansions was initiated by Muckenhoupt and Stein
[
23
]
, and since then, significant contributions have been made, particularly concerning Riesz transforms associated with Laguerre and Hermite operators. See, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
29
,
30
]
and the references therein.

In the one-dimensional case (
n=1
), higher-order Riesz transforms were studied in
[
1
]
for
\nu>-1
. However, the techniques in
[
1
]
do not extend naturally to higher dimensions. In the case
n\geq 2
, only the first-order Riesz transforms

\delta_{\nu_{j}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2},\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

(not the higher-order transforms) have been analyzed. Specifically, it was shown in
[
25
]
that these transforms are Calderón-Zygmund operators provided that
\nu_{j}\in\{-1/2\}\cup(1/2,\infty)
, and this result was later extended to the full range
\nu_{j}\in[-1/2,\infty)
in
[
3
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, the boundedness of higher-order Riesz transforms in dimensions
n\geq 2
remains an open problem due to several challenges. First, while the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the first-order Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu_{j}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
follows directly from spectral theory, proving the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the higher-order Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
for
|k|\geq 2
is significantly more involved. Second, obtaining kernel estimates for these operators requires precise control over the higher-order derivatives of the heat kernel associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, which have been absent in the literature.

Motivated by these gaps, we aim to establish that the higher-order Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
are Calderón-Zygmund operators for
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
(see Theorem
5.1
). This range is sharp, as in general, if
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, the Riesz transforms may fail to be Calderón-Zygmund operators, even in the one-dimensional case (see
[
1
]
). Furthermore, we show that these operators are bounded on our new Hardy space and the corresponding BMO-type space, completing the characterization of their boundedness (see Theorem
5.2
). Our results not only extend those in
[
25
,
3
,
2
]
but also provide a comprehensive description of the boundedness properties of higher-order
Riesz transforms associated to the Lageurre operators.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some properties of the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
, revisit the theory of the Hardy space associated with operators, including its maximal characterization and the theory of tent spaces. Section 3 is dedicated to proving kernel estimates related to the heat semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
. In particular, we prove estimates related to the higher-order derivatives of the heat kernel. Section 4 is devoted to the theory of Hardy spaces and BMO type spaces associated to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. Finally, Section 5 provides the
proofs of the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
( Theorem
5.2
).

and

to denote positive constants that are independent of the main parameters involved but whose values may differ from line to line. We will write

and

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will establish some preliminary results regarding the critical functions and the theory of Hardy spaces associated to an abstract operator.

2.1.
Critical functions

For
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
, define

(6)

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},1,x_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\Big\},

where
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}:=\{j:\nu_{j}>-1/2\}
.

It is easy to see that

(i)

If
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\emptyset
, then we have

\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},1\Big\}\sim\frac{1}{1+|x|},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\neq\emptyset
, then

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},x_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\Big\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

(iii)

If
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\Big\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

In this case, the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
is independent of
\nu
. More precisely, if
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
, then

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\rho_{\nu^{\prime}}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\Big\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, define

\rho_{\nu_{j}}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\times\min\{1,x_{j}^{-1}\},\ \ \ &\nu_{j}=-1/2,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\min\{x_{j},x_{j}^{-1}\},\ \ \ &\nu_{j}>-1/2.\end{cases}

Then we have

(7)

\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim\min\Big\{\rho_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1}),\ldots,\rho_{\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\Big\}.

We recall a couple of important properties regarding the critical function in
[
3
, Lemma 2.1 & Corollary 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then for every
y\in 4B(x,\rho_{\nu}(x))
,

\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq\rho_{\nu}(y)\leq 2\rho_{\nu}(x).

Corollary 2.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. There exist a family of balls
\{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
and a family of functions
\{\psi_{\xi}:\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
;

(ii)

\{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})/5):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
is pairwise disjoint;

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))}\lesssim 1
;

(iv)

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{\xi}\subset B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))
and
0\leq\psi_{\xi}\leq 1
for each
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
;

(v)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\psi_{\xi}=1
.

As the following estimate is standard, we omit the proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a>0
. Then there exist
C,b>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{at}\Big)\frac{1}{s^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{as}\Big)dz\leq C\frac{1}{(t+s)^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{b(t+s)}\Big)

for all

and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

2.2.
Hardy spaces associated to operators

On our setting
(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},|\cdot|,dx)
, we consider an operator
L
satisfying the following two conditions:

(A1)

L
is a nonnegative, possibly unbounded, self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
;

(A2)

L
generates a semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
whose kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
admits a Gaussian upper bound. That is, there exist two positive finite constants
C
and
c
so that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle|p_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

We now recall the definition of the Hardy spaces associated to
L
in
[
16
,
18
]
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
. Then the Hardy space
H_{S_{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is
defined as the completion of

\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):S_{L}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\}

under the norm
\|f\|_{H_{S_{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|S_{L}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
, where the square function
S_{L}
is defined as

S_{L}f(x)=\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<t}|t^{2}Le^{-t^{2}L}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Definition 2.4

(Atoms associated to
L
)
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
. A function
a
supported in a ball
B
is called a
(p,M)_{L}
-atom if there exists a
function
b
in the domain of
L^{M}
such that

(i)

a=L^{M}b
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}L^{k}b\subset B,\ k=0,1,\dots,M
;

(iii)

\|L^{k}b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq r_{B}^{2(M-k)}|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}},\ k=0,1,\dots,M
.

Then the atomic Hardy spaces associated to the operator
L
are defined as follows:

Definition 2.5

(Atomic Hardy spaces associated to
L
)
.

Given
p\in(0,1]
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j}
is an atomic
(p,M)_{L}
-representation if

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
, each
a_{j}
is a
(p,M)_{L}
-atom,
and the sum converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. The space
H^{p}_{L,at,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is then defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):f\ \text{has an atomic
$(p,M)_{L}$-representation}\right\},

with the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L,at,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\inf\left\{\left(\sum|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}:f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{is an atomic $(p,M)_{L}$-representation}\right\}.

The maximal Hardy spaces associated to
L
are defined as follows.

Definition 2.6

(Maximal Hardy spaces associated to
L
)
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, we define the maximal function by

\mathcal{M}_{L}(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-tL}f(x)|.

Given
p\in(0,1]
, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\mathcal{M}_{L}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right\},

with the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|\mathcal{M}_{L}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

The following theorem is taken from
[
28
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
, and
M>\frac{n}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)
. Then the Hardy spaces
H_{S_{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,
H^{p}_{L,at,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
coincide with equivalent norms.

2.3.
Tent spaces

The tent spaces, which were first introduced in
[
8
]
, become an effective tool in the study of function spaces in harmonic analysis including Hardy spaces. In this section we will recall the definitions of the tent spaces and their fundamental properties in
[
8
]
. We begin with some notations in
[
8
]
.

•

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we denote

\Gamma(x):=\big\{(t,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\big(0,\infty\big):|x-y|\leq t\big\}.

•

For any closed subset
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, define

R(F)=\cup_{x\in F}\Gamma(x).

•

If
O
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, then the tent over
\widehat{O}
is defined as

\widehat{O}=\big(R(O^{c})\big)^{c}.

For a measurable function
F
defined in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}
, we define

\mathcal{A}(F)(x)=\Big(\int_{\Gamma(x)}|F(y,t)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\mathcal{C}(F)(x)=\underset{x\in B}{\sup}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\widehat{B}}|F(y,t)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

\mathcal{C}_{p}(F)(x)=\underset{x\in B}{\sup}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\widehat{B}}|F(y,t)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \ \ \text{ for $0<p\leq 1$}.

The following definition is taken from
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.8

(The tent spaces)
.

The tent spaces are defined as follows.

•

For
0<p<\infty
, we define
T_{2}^{p}:=\{F:\ \mathcal{A}(F)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
with the norm
\|F\|_{T^{p}_{2}}=\|\mathcal{A}(F)\|_{p}
which is a Banach space for
1\leq p<\infty
.

•

For
p=\infty
, we define
T_{2}^{\infty}=\big\{F:\ \mathcal{C}(F)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big\}
with the norm
\|F\|_{T_{2}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathcal{C}(F)\|_{\infty}
which is a Banach space.

•

For
0<p\leq 1
, we define
T_{2}^{p,\infty}=\big\{F:\|\mathcal{C}_{p}(F)\|_{p}<\infty\big\}
with the norm
\|F\|_{T_{2}^{p,\infty}}=\|\mathcal{C}_{p}F\|_{\infty}
. Obviously,
T^{1,\infty}_{2}=T_{2}^{\infty}
.

We also recall duality results of the tent spaces.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
8
]
)
.

(i)

The following inequality holds, whenever
f\in T_{2}^{1}
and
g\in T_{2}^{\infty}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}|f(x,t)g(x,t)|\frac{dxdt}{t}\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\mathcal{A}(f)(x)\mathcal{C}(g)(x)dx.

(ii)

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
f\in T^{p}_{2}
and
g\in T_{2}^{p^{\prime}}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}|f(y,t)g(y,t)|\frac{dydt}{t}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\mathcal{A}(f)(x)\mathcal{A}(g)(x)dx.

(iii)

If
0<p\leq 1
, then the dual space of
T_{2}^{p}
is
T_{2}^{p,\infty}
. More precisely, the pairing

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}f(x,t)g(x,t)\frac{dxdt}{t}

realizes
T_{2}^{p,\infty}
as the dual of

.

3.
Some kernel estimates

This section is devoted to establishing some kernel estimates related to the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. These estimates play an essential role in proving our main results. We begin by providing an explicit formula for the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, similarly to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}
the self-adjoint extension of the differential operator

L_{\nu_{j}}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+x_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{j}^{2}}(\nu_{j}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})

on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as the natural domain. It is easy to see that

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}.

Let
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
and let
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(8)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j}).

For
\nu_{j}\geq-1/2
,
j=1,\ldots,n,
, the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
is given by

(9)

p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
and
I_{\alpha}
is the usual Bessel funtions of an imaginary argument defined by

I_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\alpha+2k}}{k!\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)},\ \ \ \ \alpha>-1.

See for example
[
11
,
25
]
.

Note that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, we can rewrite the kernel
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
as follows

(10)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{1-r^{1/2}}{1+r^{1/2}}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(-\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

The following properties of the Bessel function
I_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>-1
are well-known and are taken from
[
20
]
:

(11)

I_{\alpha}(z)\sim z^{\alpha},\ \ \ 0<z\leq 1,

(12)

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}+S_{\alpha}(z),

where

(13)

|S_{\alpha}(z)|\leq Ce^{z}z^{-3/2},\ \ z\geq 1,

(14)

\frac{d}{dz}(z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha}(z))=z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha+1}(z)

and

(15)

0<I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z),\ \ \ z>0.

Let
\nu>-1
, we have

I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)=[I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)]-[I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)].

Applying (
15
),

I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z).

Hence,

|I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)|\leq\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z)+|I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)|.

On the other hand, since
\alpha+1>-1/2
, we have

0<I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)<2(\alpha+2)\frac{I_{\alpha+2}(z)}{z}<2(\alpha+2)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ z>0.

See for example
[
24
]
.

Consequently,

(16)

|I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)|<(4\alpha+6)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ \alpha>-1,z>0.

From (
15
), (
16
) and (
9
), for
\alpha>-1
we immediately obtain the following, for
t>0
and
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,

(17)

p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\alpha+2}(x,y)=2(\alpha+1)\frac{1-r}{2r^{1/2}xy}p_{t}^{\alpha+1}(x,y)

and

(18)

|p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\alpha+1}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1-r}{2r^{1/2}xy}p_{t}^{\alpha+1}(x,y),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i,j,h\geq 0\atop i+j+h\leq 2m+k}\frac{x^{i}}{x^{j}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big)^{-(i+j)}\frac{e^{-t/4}}{t^{(h+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i,j,h\geq 0\atop i+j+h\leq 2m+k}\frac{1}{t^{(i+j)/2}}\frac{e^{-t/4}}{t^{(h+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(k+2m+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}.

This completes our proof.
∎

4.
Hardy spaces associated to the Laguerre operator and its duality

In this section, we develop the theory of Hardy spaces adapted to the Laguerre operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. We begin by introducing the Hardy space via an atomic decomposition involving a new local atom related to the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
. Next, we characterize the Hardy space using the maximal function associated with the heat semigroup of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. As a counterpart to the Hardy space, we also study a new BMO type space, which we establish as the dual of the Hardy space. The novelty of our theory lies in its extension to the full range
p\in(0,1]
.

4.1.
Hardy spaces associated to the Laguerre operator

We first introduce a new atomic Hardy space related to the critical function.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
,
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be the function as in (
6
). A function
a
is called a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-atom associated to the ball
B(x_{0},r)
if

(i)

{\rm supp}\,a\subset B(x_{0},r)
;

(ii)

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|B(x_{0},r)|^{-1/p}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n(1/p-1)
if
r<\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
.

If
\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})=1
, the
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-atom coincides with the local atoms defined in
[
15
]
. Therefore, we can view a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom as a local atom associated to the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
.
Let
p\in(0,1]
. We say that
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
is an atomic
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-representation if

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
, each
a_{j}
is a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-atom,
and the sum converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. The space
H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is then defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):f\ \text{has an atomic
$(p,\rho_{\nu})$-representation}\right\}

under the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\inf\Big\{\Big(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}:f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{is an atomic $(p,\rho_{\nu})$-representation}\Big\}.

Remark 4.2
.

For a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom
a
associated with the ball
B(x_{0},r)
, if
r>\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
, by the argument used in the proof of Theorem 3.2 in
[
32
]
, it can be verified that we can decompose
a=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\sim 1
and
a_{j}
is a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom associated with the ball
B(x_{j},r_{j})
with
r_{j}=\rho(x_{j})
for each
j
. For this reason, in Definition
4.1
, we might assume that
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
for any
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom associated to
B(x_{0},r)
.

We next introduce the Hardy space associated with the Laguerre operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
we define

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

The maximal Hardy spaces associated to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
are defined as follows.

Definition 4.3
.

Given
p\in(0,1]
, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is defined as the completion of

\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\}

under the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
, where
\nu_{\min}
is defined by (
39
). For
p\in(\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}},1]
, we have

H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

with equivalent norms.

If all
\nu_{j}
are large, then we have the larger range of
p
, i.e.,
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}
is close to 0. Note that the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
defined in (
6
) does not satisfy the property (
5
) as in
[
32
, Definition 2.2]
. In addition, the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
does not the Hölder continuity condition, which played an essential roles in
[
32
,
5
]
. Consequently, the approaches outlined in
[
32
,
5
]
(including
[
7
]
) cannot be directly applied in our specific context. This necessitates the development of new ideas and a novel approach.

It is worth noting that the equivalence between the atomic Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
was established in
[
11
]
for the specific case of
n=1,p=1
, and
\nu>-1/2
. However, the approach employed in
[
11
]
is not applicable to higher-dimensional cases.

We now give the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
:

We first prove that
H^{p}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Let
a
be a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-atom associated with a ball
B:=B(x_{0},r)
. By Remark
4.2
, we might assume that
r\leq\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
. We need to verify that

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{p}\lesssim 1.

To do this, we write

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}(4B)}+\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}((4B)^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{1}+E_{2}.

By Hölder’s inequality and the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}(4B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|4B|^{1/p-1/2}\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{2}(4B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|4B|^{1/p-1/2}\|a\|_{L^{2}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1.

It remains to take care of the second term
E_{2}
. We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
r=\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
.
By Proposition
3.5
, for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy,

where
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
for
y\in B
. This, together with the fact that
|x-y|\sim|x-x_{0}|
for
x\in(4B)^{c}
and
y\in B
, further imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}((4B)^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B|^{1-1/p}\Big[\int_{(4B)^{c}}\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{p\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{np}}dx\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Case 2:
r<\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
.
Using the cancellation property
\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
|\alpha|\leq\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor=:N_{p}
and the Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu}(x,x_{0})]a(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}\Big[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N_{p}}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,x_{0})}{\alpha!}(y-x_{0})^{\alpha}\Big]a(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}\sum_{|\alpha|=N_{p}+1}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,x_{0}+\theta(y-x_{0}))}{\alpha!}(y-x_{0})^{\alpha}a(y)dy\Big|

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
.

Since
y\in B
we have
\rho_{\nu}(x_{0}+\theta(y-x_{0}))\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
and
|x-[x_{0}+\theta(y-x_{0})]|\sim|x-x_{0}|
for all
x\in(4B)^{c}
,
y\in B
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, by Proposition
3.10
we obtain, for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{|y-y_{0}|}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{|y-y_{0}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{r}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle=F_{1}+F_{2}.

For the term
F_{1}
, it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p},

where in the last inequality we used the fact
r\leq|x-x_{0}|
for
x\in(4B)^{c}
.

For
F_{2}
, since
r<\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}\Big)^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{{}^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Taking this and the estimate of
F_{1}
into account, we obtain

\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{{}^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Therefore,

|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{{}^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}|B|^{1-1/p},

which implies that

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\|_{p}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

This completes the proof of the first step.

It remains to show that
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.
To do this, let
f\in H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. By Theorem
2.7
we can write
f
as a linear decomposition of
(p,N)_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
atoms with
N>n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
. Assume that
a
is a
(p,N)_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
atom associated with a ball
B
. If
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
, then
a
is also a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
a\in H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
in the case
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
. To do this, we recall that
a
satisfies the following properties

(i)

a=\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{N}b
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}b\subset B,\ k=0,1,\dots,M
;

(iii)

\|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq r_{B}^{2(N-k)}|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}},\ k=0,1,\dots,N
.

We first claim that for any multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|<N
, we have

(42)

\Big|\int(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}a(x)\,dx\Big|\leq|B|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{B}}\Big)^{N-|\alpha|}.

Indeed, from (i) we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}a(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{B}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{N}b(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{B}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}b(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}||b(x)|\,dx.

For
x\in B
, using Lemma
2.1
it can be verified that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{|\alpha|}r_{B}^{|\alpha|-j}\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-j}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-|\alpha|}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\int(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}a(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-|\alpha|}}\|b\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B|^{1-1/p}\frac{r_{B}^{2N}}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-|\alpha|}}=|B|^{1-1/p}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{B}}\Big)^{2N-|\alpha|}.

This confirms (
42
).

We now prove that
a\in H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as long as
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
. Recall that
S_{0}(B)=B,S_{j}(B)=2^{j}B\setminus 2^{j-1}B,j\geq 1
. Set
\omega=\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{j}
be the span of the polynomials
\big\{(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\big\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
on
S_{j}(B)
corresponding to the inner product space given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}:=\fint_{S_{j}(B)}f(x)g(x)\,dx.

Let
\{u_{j,\alpha}\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{V}_{j}
obtained via the Gram–Schmidt process applied to
\big\{(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\big\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}

which, through homogeneity and uniqueness of the process, gives

(43)

\displaystyle u_{j,\alpha}(x)=\sum_{|\beta|\leq\omega}\lambda_{\alpha,\beta}^{j}(x-x_{B})^{\beta},

where for each
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq\omega
we have

(44)

\displaystyle|u_{j,\alpha}(x)|\leq C\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\lambda_{\alpha,\beta}^{j}|\lesssim(2^{j}r_{B})^{-|\beta|}.

Let
\{v_{j,\alpha}\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
be the dual basis of
\big\{(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\big\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
in
\mathcal{V}_{j}
;
that is, it is the unique collection of polynomials such that

(45)

\displaystyle\langle v_{j,\alpha},(\cdot-x_{B})^{\beta}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta},\qquad|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq\omega.

Then we have

(46)

\displaystyle\|v_{j,\alpha}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(2^{j}r_{B})^{-|\alpha|},\qquad\forall\;|\alpha|\leq\omega.

Now let
P:={\rm proj}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}(a)
be the orthogonal projection of
a
onto
\mathcal{V}_{0}
. Then we have

(47)

\displaystyle P=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,u_{0,\alpha}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}u_{0,\alpha}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}v_{0,\alpha}.

Let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{j_{0}}r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})>2^{j_{0}-1}r_{B}
. Then we write

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}v_{0,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\Big[\frac{v_{j,\alpha}}{|S_{j}(B)|}-\frac{v_{j+1,\alpha}}{|S_{j+1}(B)|}\Big]+\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\frac{v_{j_{0}-1,\alpha}}{|S_{j_{0}-1}(B)|}.

Hence, we can decompose

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=(a-P)+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\Big[\frac{v_{j,\alpha}}{|S_{j}(B)|}-\frac{v_{j+1,\alpha}}{|S_{j+1}(B)|}\Big]+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\frac{v_{j_{0}-1,\alpha}}{|S_{j_{0}-1}(B)|}

\displaystyle=a_{1}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}a_{2,j,\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}a_{3,\alpha}.

Let us now outline the important properties of the functions in the above decomposition.
For
a_{1}
we observe that for all
|\alpha|\leq\omega
,

(48)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}a_{1}\subset B,

\displaystyle\int a_{1}(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx=0,

\displaystyle\|a_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|B|^{-1/p}.

Note that the property

\int a_{1}(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\alpha|\leq\omega$}

implies that

\int a_{1}(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\alpha|\leq\omega$}.

Hence, in this case
a_{1}
is a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom.

Next, for
a_{2,j,\alpha}
, it is obvious that
\operatorname{supp}a_{2,j,\alpha}\subset 2^{j+1}B
. In addition, from (
45
), we have

\int a_{2,j,\alpha}(x)(x-x_{B})^{\beta}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\beta|\leq\omega$},

which implies

\int a_{2,j,\alpha}(x)x^{\beta}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\beta|\leq\omega$}.

We now estimate the size of
a_{2,j,\alpha}
. Using (
42
), we have

\displaystyle\|a_{2,j,\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{j}r_{B})^{-|\alpha|}|2^{j}B|^{-1}\Big|\int a(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j}B|^{-1-|\alpha|/n}|B|^{1-1/p}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{2N-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(2N+n-n/p)}|2^{j}B|^{-1/p}.

This means that
a_{2,j,\alpha}
is a multiple of a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom, which further implies that

\Big\|\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}a_{2,j,\alpha}\Big\|_{H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\lesssim 1,

as long as
N>n(1/p-1)
.

Next, for
a_{3,\alpha}
we first have
\operatorname{supp}a_{3,\alpha}\subset 2^{j_{0}}B
. Moreover, using (
42
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle\|a_{2,j,\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{j_{0}}r_{B})^{-|\alpha|}|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1}\Big|\int a(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1-|\alpha|/n}|B|^{1-1/p}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B}))}\Big)^{2N-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j_{0}(2N+n-n/p)}|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1/p},

as long as
N>n(1/p-1)
.

It follows that
a_{3,\alpha}
is a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom and
\|a_{3,\alpha}\|_{H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim 1
.

This completes our proof.
∎

.

4.2.
Campanato spaces associated to the Laguerre operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}

For the duality of the Hardy space, we introduce the Campanato space associated to the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{M}

denote the set of all polynomials of degree at most
M
.
For any
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
P_{B}^{M}g
the
minimizing polynomial

of
g
on the ball
B
with homogeneous degree
\leq M
, which means that
P_{B}^{M}g
is the unique polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}

such that,

(49)

\displaystyle\int_{B}[g(x)-P(x)]x^{\alpha}dx=0\quad\mbox{for every }|\alpha|\leq M.

It is known that if
g
is locally integrable, then
P^{M}_{B}g
uniquely exists (see
[
17
]
). Recall that for any polynomial
P
of degree at most
M
, any ball
B
and any multi-index
\alpha
, we have

(50)

\sup_{x\in B}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}P(x)|\lesssim_{\alpha,M}\sup_{x\in B}|P(x)|\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \sup_{x\in B}|P(x)|\lesssim_{M}\fint_{B}|P(x)|dx.

which, together with Lemma 4.1 in
[
21
]
, implies that

(51)

\sup_{x\in B}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}P_{B}^{M}g(x)|\lesssim\frac{r_{B}^{-|\alpha|}}{|B|}\int_{B}|g(x)|dx.

We define the local Campanato spaces associated to critical functions as follows.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. Let
\rho_{\nu}
be the critical function as in (
6
).
Let
s\geq 0
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The local Campanato space
BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
associated to
\rho_{\nu}
is defined to
be the space of all locally
L^{q}
functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
such that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}&:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)-P^{M}_{B}f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]\\
&\quad\quad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]<\infty,\end{split}

with the usual modification when
q=\infty
. Here,
x_{B}
and
r_{B}
denote the center and radius of the ball
B
, respectively.

Note that for each ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
if
r_{B}\leq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
, then
B
is identical to its extension
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|y-x_{B}|<r_{B}\}
. Therefore, in the above definition the polynomial
P_{B}^{M}f
is well-defined.

In Proposition
4.10
below, we will show that the function spaces in the above definition are independent of
the choices of
1\leq r\leq\infty,M\geq\lfloor s\rfloor
for
s>0
and with
1\leq r<\infty,M\geq 0
for
s=0
, where
\lfloor s\rfloor
denotes the greatest integer
less than or equal to
s
. Hence, we define the space
BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as any space
BMO^{s,r,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

with
1\leq r\leq\infty,M\geq\lfloor s\rfloor
for
s>0
and with
1\leq r<\infty,M\geq 0
for
s=0
.

We have the following result regarding the duality of the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
, where
\nu_{\min}
is given in (
39
). Let
\rho_{\nu}
be defined
as in (
6
). Then for
p\in(\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}},1]
, we have

\displaystyle\big(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\big)^{\ast}=BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),\quad\mbox{where }s:=n(1/p-1).

An equivalent norm
of the Campanato space is as follows (see Theorem 1 in
[
17
]
):
For any locally
L^{q}
function
u
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{B:{\rm balls}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|u(x)-P_{B}^{M}u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right].

We first recall the following result concerning independence of
q
for the spaces
BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
See Theorem 3 in
[
17
]
.

Proposition 4.8
.

(a)

If
s>0
,
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
and
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M_{1},M_{2}\geq\lfloor s\rfloor
, then
BMO^{s,q,M_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=BMO^{s,r,M_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with equivalent norms;

(b)

If
1\leq q,r<\infty
and
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{0,q,M_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{C}^{0,r,M_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In what follows, for a ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
let
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be the extension of
B
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{B}|<r_{B}\}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. Let
\rho
be the critical function as in (
6
).
Let
s\geq 0
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then

\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\sim\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right],

where
\tilde{f}
is the zero extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward that

\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right].

It remains to show that

\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Recall that

\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sim\sup_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\ {\rm balls\,in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|\tilde{f}(x)-P^{M}_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\tilde{f}(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

Note that for any ball
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
P^{M}_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\tilde{f}=0
if
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\emptyset
.
This, together with (
51
), implies that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|\tilde{f}(x)-P^{M}_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\tilde{f}(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|\tilde{f}(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset\\
x_{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]+

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset\\
x_{B}\notin\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset\\
x_{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset\\
x_{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

For all balls
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset
and
x_{B}\notin\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, take a
x_{0}\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset
.
Then we have
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\subset B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x_{0},2r_{B})
. Consequently, for such a ball
B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x_{0},2r_{B})\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x_{0},2r_{B})\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x_{0},2r_{B})\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

which implies that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\neq\emptyset\\
x_{B}\notin\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\right]\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\rho_{\nu}
be the function in (
6
). Then we have:

(i)

If
s>0
,
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
and
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M_{1},M_{2}\geq\lfloor s\rfloor
, then
BMO^{s,q,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=BMO^{s,r,M_{2}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
;

(ii)

If
1\leq q,r<\infty
and
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{0,q,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=\mathcal{C}^{0,r,M_{2}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Proof.

We only give the proof of part (i) since the proof of (ii) is similar.

To prove part (i) it suffices to prove that

BMO^{s,q,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=BMO^{s,1,M}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
. By Lemma
4.9
and Hölder’s inequality,
BMO^{s,q,M_{2}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\subset BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Hence it remains to prove that
BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\subset BMO^{s,q,M_{2}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

To see the latter, let
f\in BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Denote by
\tilde{f}
the zero extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that, by part (i) of
Proposition
4.8
, Lemma
4.9
, (
51
) and the fact
|B|\sim|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|
for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{BMO^{s,q,M_{2}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\sim\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm balls\,in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{q}dx\right]^{1/q}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,1,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm balls\ in\ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\tilde{f}(x)-P_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}^{M_{2}}\tilde{f}(x)|^{q}dx\right]^{1/q}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm balls\ in\ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|P_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}^{M_{2}}\tilde{f}(x)|^{q}dx\right]^{1/q}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,1,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,q,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm balls\ in\ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left[\sup_{x\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|P_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}^{M_{2}}\tilde{f}(x)|\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm balls\,in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|^{s/n}}\left[\frac{1}{|B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|\tilde{f}(x)|dx\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\sim 2\|\tilde{f}\|_{BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm balls\,in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{B}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|dx\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This implies that
BMO^{s,1,M_{1}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\subset BMO^{s,q,M_{2}}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and completes the proof.
∎

Due to Proposition

In order to prove Theorem

Since

[

As a consequence of

Lemma 4.11
.

Let

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\,K_{(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})}\subset\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:|x-y|\leq t\},

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
s\geq 0
,

\Phi
be defined as in Lemma

k>\frac{s}{2}

(55)

\sup_{B:\,{\rm balls}}\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\leq C\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Proof.

The lemma was proved in
[
3
]
for the case
s\in(0,1)

by
\displaystyle f_{B}:=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f

Let
B

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

Case 1:

4.11

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(f\chi_{4B})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}.

Note that by the spectral theory, the Littlewood-Paley square function

g\mapsto\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})g|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

is bounded on

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(f\chi_{3B})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

Case 2:
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})/4
. In this case, we write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}

\displaystyle\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(f-P^{M}_{4B}f)(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})P^{M}_{4B}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}.

Similarly to Case 1, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(f-P^{M}_{4B}f)(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})\big[(f-P^{M}_{4B}f)\chi_{4B}\big](x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\|(f-P^{M}_{4B}f)\chi_{4B}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

Hence it remains to show that

(56)

\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})P^{M}_{4B}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

By Lemma
4.11
, we have, for
x\in B
and
t<r_{B}
,

(57)

\displaystyle|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})P^{M}_{4B}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{2B}t^{2k}\varphi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}_{\nu})(x,y)\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}(P^{M}_{4B}f)(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2k}\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}(P^{M}_{4B}f)(z)|.

Setting
B_{\rho}=B(x_{B},\rho_{\nu}(x_{B}))
, then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}(P^{M}_{4B}f)(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}[P_{4B}^{M}f(z)-P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)]|+r_{B}^{-2k}\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)|

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

For the term
E_{2}
, using the expression

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}=\Biggl\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\right]\Biggr\}^{k},

the fact
\rho_{\nu}(x)\lesssim\min\{1/|x|,x_{i}:i\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\}
, (
50
) and Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)|\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})^{-2k}\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}}|P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)|.

This, together with (
51
), Lemma
4.9
and Lemma
4.10
, further implies that

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})^{-2k}\fint_{B_{\rho}}|P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)|d(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})^{-2k}\fint_{B_{\rho}}|f(z)-P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)|dz+\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})^{-2k}\fint_{B_{\rho}}|f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})^{-2k}|B_{\rho}|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO_{\rho}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{B}^{s-2k}\|f\|_{BMO_{\rho}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

To estimate
E_{1}
, let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum_{j=2}^{k_{0}-1}\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}[P_{2^{j}B}^{M}f(z)-P_{2^{j+1}B}^{M}f(z)]|+\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}[P_{2^{k_{0}}B}^{M}f(z)-P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)]|\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum_{j=2}^{k_{0}-1}\sup_{z\in 2^{j}B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}[P_{2^{j}B}^{M}f(z)-P_{2^{j+1}B}^{M}f(z)]|+\sup_{z\in 2^{k_{0}}B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}[P_{2^{k_{0}}B}^{M}f(z)-P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)]|\right].

Using the expression

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}=\Biggl\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\right]\Biggr\}^{k},

the fact that
\rho_{\nu}(x)\lesssim\min\{1/|x|,x_{i}:i\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\}
, (

2.1
and the fact that
r_{B}\leq\rho(x_{B})/4

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=2}^{k_{0}-1}(2^{j}r_{B})^{-2k}\fint_{2^{j}B}|P_{2^{j}B}^{M}f(z)-P_{2^{j+1}B}^{M}f(z)|dz+\rho(x_{B})^{-2k}\fint_{2^{k_{0}}B}|P_{2^{k_{0}}B}^{M}f(z)-P_{B_{\rho}}^{M}f(z)|dz

\displaystyle=:E_{11}+E_{12}.

For

\displaystyle E_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=2}^{k_{0}-1}(2^{j}r_{B})^{-2k}\Big[\fint_{2^{j}B}|f(z)-P_{2^{j}B}^{M}f(z)|dz+\fint_{2^{j}B}|f(z)-P_{2^{j+1}B}^{M}f(z)|dz\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=2}^{k_{0}-1}(2^{j}r_{B})^{-2k}|2^{j}B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO_{\rho_{\nu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{B}^{s-2k}\|f\|_{BMO_{\rho_{\nu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

as long as

.

Similarly,

E_{12}\lesssim r_{B}^{s-2k}\|f\|_{BMO_{\rho_{\nu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Collecting the estimates of

,
E_{11}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in 4B}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{k}P^{M}_{4B}f(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{B}^{s-2k}\|f\|_{BMO_{\rho_{\nu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This, along with (

(58)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\varphi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{BMO_{\rho_{\nu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\frac{r_{B}^{2s-4k}}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}t^{4k}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{BMO_{\rho_{\nu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

as along as

.

This completes our proof.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
,
\rho_{\nu}
be as in (

\Phi
be as in Lemma

Then for every

and every
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-atom
a
,

(60)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx=C(k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t},

Proof.

[
3
, Lemma 4.11]
proved the result for
e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
instead of
\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})
. While the proof in this setting follows the spirit of
[
3
, Lemma 4.11]
, some major modifications are required. For the readers’ convenience, we provide the details here.

Set

\displaystyle F(x,t):=(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})f(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad G(x,t):=t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x).

It follows from Lemma
4.12
, Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
4.4
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{p}(F)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(G)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|S_{L}a\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C.

Hence by Lemma
2.9
(iii) the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}F(x,t)G(x,y)\frac{dxdt}{t}

is well-defined.

On the other hand, for each
t>0
,

(61)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}F(x,t)G(x,y)\frac{dxdt}{t}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}f(x){(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}{(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dt}{t}dx.\end{split}

We will show that

(62)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon,N>0\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\frac{dt}{t}\right|\leq c_{a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.

To do this, we first prove that

(63)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|\leq c_{a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.

In what follows we denote by
K_{K(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})}(x,y)
the kernel of the functional calculus
F(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})
for a bounded function
F:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
. By Proposition
3.12
, (
53
) and (
54
), we have

(64)

\displaystyle\left|K_{(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}p_{t^{2}}^{\nu}(x,y)K_{(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k+1}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})}(y,z)dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(y,t)}\frac{C_{k}}{t^{2n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{k}}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}.

Suppose
a
is a
(p,\rho)
-atom associated to the ball
B:=B(x_{B},r_{B})
with
r_{B}\leq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
. If
|x|\leq 2(1+|x_{B}|)
, then

(65)

\begin{split}\left|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|&\leq C_{k}\|a\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)dy\\
&\leq c_{k}\|a\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq c_{k,a}(1+|x_{B}|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}\\
&\leq c_{k,a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.\end{split}

If
|x|\geq 2(1+|x_{B}|)\geq 2r_{B}
, then
|x-z|\sim|x|
for
z\in B
. This, together with (
64
) and the fact
\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq\frac{1}{|x|}
, yields that for
|x|\geq 2(1+|x_{B}|)\geq 2r_{B}
,

(66)

\displaystyle\left|(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\int_{B}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{k}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{t|x|}\right)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c_{k,a}|x|^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}\sim C_{k,a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.

Combining (
65
) and (
66
) yields (
63
).

We now turn to prove (
62
). To do this, we define, for
\lambda\in[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\eta(\lambda)=\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}t^{2k}\Phi(t)t^{2}e^{-t^{2}}\frac{dt}{t}.

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, one can check that
\eta
is smooth on
[0,\infty)
and decays rapidly at infinity.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}}\eta^{(2\ell+1)}(\lambda)=0\quad\text{for all $\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$}.

Hence
\eta
can be extended to an even Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}
. We claim that the kernel of
\eta(t\sqrt{L})
satisfies

(67)

\displaystyle\left|K_{\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{t^{Q}}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}

for arbitrary
N
.
Indeed, let
m>n/2+\gamma_{\nu}
be sufficiently large (depending on
N
), using Proposition
3.5
and the formula

\displaystyle(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{-m}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(m)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ut^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}e^{-u}u^{m-1}du,

one can show that

\displaystyle\left|K_{(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{-m}}(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{t^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}

for arbitrary
N>0
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left|K_{\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left|K_{(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{-m}}(x,z)\left|K_{(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{m}\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(z,y)\right|dz\right|dz

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\frac{1}{t^{Q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left|K_{(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{m}\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(x,z)\right|\left(1+\frac{|x-z|}{t}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left|K_{(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{m}\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(z,y)\right|\left(1+\frac{|y-z|}{t}\right)^{N}dz.

A standard argument as in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 3.4]
shows that there exists a sufficiently large integer
m
(depending on
N
) such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left|K_{(I+t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{m}\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(z,y)\right|\left(1+\frac{|y-z|}{t}\right)^{N}dz\leq C.

Hence

\displaystyle\left|K_{\eta(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}

which gives (
67
).

With the estimate (
67
) on hand, we can proceed as in the proof of (
63
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|\eta(t\sqrt{L})a(x)|\leq C_{a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon,N>0\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon,N>0\end{subarray}}\left|\eta(\varepsilon\sqrt{L})a(x)-\eta(N\sqrt{L})a(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left|\eta(\varepsilon\sqrt{L})a(x)\right|+\sup_{N>0}\left|\eta(N\sqrt{L})a(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.

This verifies (
62
).

This, together with Lemma
4.13
, implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|f(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}{(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dt}{t}\Big|dx<\infty.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\frac{dt}{t}=C(k)a\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

Therefore, (
60

).

This completes our proof.

which implies that

(70)

\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B_{\xi})}\lesssim|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},\end{split}

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Combining (
69
) and (
70
) yields (
68
).

Assume that
\ell\in(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}
.
For each index
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
we define

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}f:=\ell(\psi_{\xi}f),\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

By (
68
),

\displaystyle|\ell_{\xi}(f)|\leq C\|\psi_{\xi}f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Hence there exists
g_{\xi}\in L^{2}(B_{\xi})
such that

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}(f)=\int_{B_{\xi}}f(x)g_{\xi}(x)dx,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

We define
g=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}1_{B_{\xi}}g_{\xi}
. Then, if
f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
a_{i}
is a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
-atom,
i=1,\cdots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\ell(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\ell(a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\ell_{\xi}(a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{B_{\xi}}a_{i}(x)g_{\xi}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}}f(x)g(x)dx.

Suppose that
B=B(x_{B},r_{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
with
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
, and
0\not\equiv f\in L_{0}^{2}(B)
, that is,

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
\operatorname{supp}f\subset B
and
\displaystyle\int_{B}f(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq M
. Arguing similarly to the proof of (
68
),

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

Hence

\displaystyle|\ell(f)|=\left|\int_{B}fg\right|\leq\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

From this we conclude that
g\in(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}
and

\|g\|_{(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

Using the fact that (see Folland and Stein
[
14
, p. 145]
)

\|g\|_{(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}}=\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}}\|g-P\|_{L^{2}(B)},

we obtain

(71)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:ball\\
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}|B|^{1/2-1/p}\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}}\|g-P\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}.

Moreover, if
B
is a ball with
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
f\not\equiv 0

and
\operatorname{supp}f\subset B
, then similarly to (
68
),

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim|B|^{1/2-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\ell(f)|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}fg\Big|\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence

(72)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm ball}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}|B|^{1/2-1/p}\|g\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}.

From (
71
) and (
72
) it follows that
g\in BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}},\quad\mbox{where }s=n(1/p-1).

The proof of Theorem
4.7
is thus complete.
∎

5.
Boundedness of Riesz transforms on Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}

In this section, we will study the boundedness of the higher-order Riesz transforms. We first show in Theorem
5.1
below that the higher-order Riesz transforms are Calderón-Zygmund operators. Then, in Theorem
5.2
we will show that the higher-order Riesz transforms are bounded on our new Hardy spaces and new BMO type spaces defined in Section 4.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
,
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
and a multi-index
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Denote by
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)
the associated kernel of
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. Then the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. That is,
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
nd its kernel satisfies the following estimates:

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\ \ \ x\neq y

and

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle+|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y,x)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y^{\prime},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{x-y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

We would like to emphasize that Theorem
5.1
is new, even when
n=1
. In fact, in the case of
n=1
,
the boundedness of the Riesz transform was explored in
[
25
,
1
,
2
]
. In
[
25
]
, the Riesz operator was
decomposed into local and global components. The local part emerged as a local Calderón-Zygmund operator, while the global part was
effectively dealt with by using weighted Hardy’s inequalities. In
[
1
,
2
]
, the investigation leaned on the connections
between the Riesz transform associated with the Laguerre operator and that associated with the Hermite operator on
\mathbb{R}
. It’s important to note that the methods employed in
[
25
,
1
,
2
]
do not imply the boundedness of the Riesz transform on weighted
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Returning to the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}\mathcal{L}^{-1/2}\nu
, we successfully establish its boundedness on the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and the Campanato spaces
BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, thus providing a comprehensive account of the boundedness properties of the Riesz transform.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
,
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Then for
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
and
s=n(1/p-1)
, we have

(i)

the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
;

(ii)

the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

In the specific case where
n=1,p=1
, and
\nu>-1/2
, the boundedness of the Riesz transform on
BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
s=0
was established in
[
6
]
. The approach employed in this proof leveraged the structure of the half-line
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and exploited the connection between the Riesz transform and the Hermite operator. In addition, item (i) in Theorem
5.2
is new, even in the case of
n=1
.

We first prove that
\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
be a multi-index. Then the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Proof.

We will prove the proposition by induction.

\bullet
For
|k|=1
, the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/1}
is quite straightforward. Indeed, for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, by (
2
) we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu_{j}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(4|\alpha|+2|\nu|+2n)^{-1/2}\langle f,\varphi_{\alpha}^{\nu}\rangle\delta_{\nu_{j}}varphi_{\alpha}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{-2\sqrt{\alpha_{j}}}{\sqrt{4|\alpha|+2|\nu|+2n}}\langle f,\varphi_{\alpha}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{\alpha-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}},

which implies that

\|\delta_{\nu_{j}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}|\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle|^{2}=\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

\bullet
Assume that the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/1}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
|k|\leq\ell
for some
\ell\geq 1
. We need to show that
\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/1}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
|k|=\ell+1
. Fix a multi-index
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
with
|k|=\ell+1
. If all indices
k_{j}
is less than or equal to
1
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, the
L^{2}
-boundedness follows by using the similar argument to that of the case
|k|=1
. Hence, without loss of generality, we might assume that
k_{1}\geq 2
. Then we consider two cases.

Case 1:
\nu_{1}>-1/2
. From (
2
), it can be verified that

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}=\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-\ell/2}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}.

Since
\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, it suffices to prove
\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-\ell/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. To do this, we write

\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}^{-\ell/2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}^{\ell/2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-\ell/2}.

By the spectral theory,
\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{\ell/2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-\ell/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. We need only to show that
\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Notice that by (
21
)

\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}=\Big(\delta_{{\nu_{1}}+1}+\frac{1}{x_{1}}\Big)^{k_{1}-1}=\delta_{{\nu_{1}}+1}^{k_{1}-1}+\frac{k_{1}-1}{x_{1}}\delta^{k_{1}-2}_{{\nu_{1}}+1},

which implies

\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}=\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}+\frac{k_{1}-1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}.

The first operator
\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
by the inductive hypothesis. Hence, it suffices to prove that the operator

f\mapsto\frac{k_{1}-1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}f

is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

By (
8
), Proposition
3.9
and the fact
|k-2e_{1}|=\ell-1
, we have, for a fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\ell/2}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}p_{t}^{\nu+e_{1}}(x,y)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{(n-1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big)^{-(\nu_{1}+3/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\min\{\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big)^{-\epsilon},\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big)^{-(\nu_{1}+3/2)}\},

which, together with the fact
\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})\lesssim x_{1}
, implies

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{k_{1}-1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-\ell/2}f(y)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|\leq\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})|x-y|^{n-1}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big)^{-\epsilon}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle+\int_{|x-y|>\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})|x-y|^{n-1}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big)^{-(\nu_{1}+3/2)}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|\leq\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\Big)^{1-\epsilon}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x)

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|>\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1})}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{1}+1/2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

It follows that the operator
\frac{k_{1}-1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, which completes the proof in the case
\nu_{1}>-1/2
.

Case 2:
\nu_{1}=-1/2

We first have

\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{2}=(-\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{*}+2x)\delta_{\nu_{1}}=(-\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{1}}+1)+2x_{1}\delta_{\nu_{1}},

which implies

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}=-\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}+\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}+\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\big[x_{1}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}\big].

For the first operator we have

\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell-1)/2}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{(\ell-1)/2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}.

The operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell-1)/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
due to
|k-2e_{1}|=\ell-1<\ell
and the inductive hypothesis, meanwhile the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{(\ell-1)/2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}=\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
by using the similar argument to that of the case
|k|=1
above and (
1
). Hence,
\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

For the second operator, we have

\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}=\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell-1)/2}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1}.

The operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell-1)/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
due to
|k-2e_{1}|=\ell-1<\ell
and the inductive hypothesis; and the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
due to the spectral theory and the spectral gap of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. Consequently,
\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

For the third operator, we have

\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\big[x_{1}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}\big]=x_{1}\delta^{k-e_{1}}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}+(k-2)\delta^{k-2e_{1}}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}.

The operator
x_{1}\delta^{k-e_{1}}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}
by using the argument similarly to the case
\nu\neq-1/2
; meanwhile the operator
\delta^{k-2e_{1}}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}
is exactly the second operator above and is also bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Therefore, the operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\big[x_{1}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(\ell+1)/2}\big]
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

This completes our proof.
∎

The following results establish the kernel bounds for the Riesz transforms.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
be a multi-index. Denote by
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. Then we have, for
x\neq y
and
j=1,\ldots,n
,

(73)

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)},

(74)

|\partial_{y_{j}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}

and

(75)

|\partial_{x_{j}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Similarly, by using Proposition
3.13
we obtain the second and the third estimates.

This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
be a multi-index. Denote by
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. Then we have, for
t>0
and
x\neq y
and
j=1,\ldots,n
,

(76)

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}

and

(77)

|\partial_{x_{j}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{k}_{\nu}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{|k|/2}\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t+u}^{\nu}(x,y)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{|k|/2}}{(t+u)^{|k|/2}}\frac{1}{(t+u)^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{c(t+u)}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u^{|k|/2}}{t^{|k|/2}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{cu}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Similarly, by using Proposition
3.13
we obtain the second estimates.

This completes our proof.
∎

We are ready to give the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
:

From (
73
), we have

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\ \ \ x\neq y.

We now prove the Hölder’s continuity of the Riesz kernel. If
|y-y^{\prime}|\geq\max\{\rho_{\nu}(y),\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})\}
, then from (
73
) we have

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|+|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big[\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}+\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}

If
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\max\{\rho_{\nu}(y),\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})\}
, then by Lemma
2.1
we have
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})
; moreover, for every
z\in B(y,2|y-y^{\prime}|)
,
\rho_{\nu}(z)\sim\rho_{\nu}(y)
. This, together with the mean value theorem and (
74
), gives

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|y-y^{\prime}|\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big]\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big]\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

Since
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(y)
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

where and
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
.

For the same reason, since
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{x-y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

Similarly, by using (
75
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y,x)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y^{\prime},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

This completes our proof.
∎

We now give the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
:

Fix
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
M>n(1/p-1)
.

(i) By Proposition
4.5
and Theorem
4.4
,
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+\vec{M})_{\nu}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, where
\vec{M}=(M,\ldots,M)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}
is defined as in (
41
). Consequently, it suffices to prove that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 1

for all
(p,M)_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
atoms
a
.

Let
a
be a
(p,M)_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
atom associated to a ball
B
. We have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}

\displaystyle+\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}.

Using the
L^{2}
-boundedness of both
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+k+\vec{M}}}f|
and the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
and the Hölder inequality, by the standard argument, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

For the second term, using
a=\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{M}b
,

e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a=e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{M-|k|/2}b.

We have

\displaystyle e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{M-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{|k|/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{M}e^{-u\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,y)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{t}\ldots\frac{du}{u}+c\int_{t}^{\infty}\ldots\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, by Proposition
3.5
, Proposition
3.11
and Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{|k|/2}|[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{M}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}](z,x)||p_{u}^{\nu}(z,y)|dz\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u^{|k|/2}}{t^{M+|k|/2}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t^{M}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{|k|/2}|p_{u}^{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}(x,z)||[\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{M}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}](z,y)|dz\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u^{M}}\frac{1}{u^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{cu}\Big)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{|x-y|^{2}}\ldots+\int_{|x-y|^{2}}^{\infty}\ldots

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+(k+2\vec{M})_{\nu}}}\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\Big[\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}|b(y)|dy\Big]^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\Big[\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|b(y)|dy\Big]^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\frac{\|b\|_{1}^{p}}{|x-x_{B}|^{(n+2M)p}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\frac{r_{B}^{2Mp}|B|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{B}|^{(n+2M)p}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

as long as
M>n(1/p-1)
.

(ii) By the duality in Theorem
4.7
, it suffices to prove that the conjugate
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}

is bounded on the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. By Theorem
4.4
, we need only to prove that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 1

for all
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atoms
a
.

Suppose that
a
is a
(p,\rho_{\nu})
atom associated to a ball
B
. Then we write

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}+\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}.

Since
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|
and
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, by the Hölder’s inequality and the standard argument, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

For the second term, we consider two cases.

Case 1:
r_{B}=\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
.
By Proposition
5.5
(a), for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}|\delta^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(y,x)||a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r^{\gamma_{\nu}}}{|x-x_{B}|^{n+\gamma_{\nu}}}|B|^{1-1/p},

where we used the fact
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
for
y\in B
(due to Lemma
2.1
) in the second inequality.

It follows that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Case 2:
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
.

Using the cancellation property
\int a(x)dx=0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}[e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}(x,y)-e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}(x,x_{B})]a(y)dy\Big|.

By mean value theorem, Proposition
5.5
and the fact that
\rho_{\nu}(y_{j})\gtrsim\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
and
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
for all
y\in B
, we have, for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\Big(\frac{|y-x_{B}|}{|x-y|}+\frac{|y-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}+\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B}\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle=F_{1}+F_{2}.

For the term
F_{1}
, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|}|B|^{1-1/p},

where in the last inequality we used

\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\leq\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}},

since both
\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}
and
\gamma_{\nu}
are less than or equal to
1
.

For
F_{2}
, since
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
and
\gamma_{\nu}\leq 1
, we have
\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\leq\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Taking this and the estimate of
F_{1}
into account, we obtain

\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}

for all
x\in(4B)^{c}
.
Consequently,

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

∎

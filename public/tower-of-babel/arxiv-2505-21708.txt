Second order spin- 𝟏 / 𝟐 1/2 bosons
A. de la C. Rangel-Pantoja, I. Díaz-Saldaña, Carlos A. Vaquera-Araujo

Abstract
The canonical quantization of a field theory for spin- 1 / 2 1/2 massive bosons that satisfy the Klein-Gordon equation is presented. The spin-statistics theorem is evaded by the redefinition of the dual field, rendering the theory pseudo-Hermitian. The Hamiltonian is bounded from below with real eigenvalues, and the theory is consistent with microcausality and invariant under parity, charge conjugation and time reversal.



In this letter, we introduce a Second Order Pseudo-Hermitian theory (SOPHY) for massive bosons
\psi
transforming under the
(\tfrac{1}{2},0)\oplus(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
representation of the restricted Lorentz group (RLG) that obey the Klein-Gordon equation.

The evasion of the spin-statistics connection in a pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theory was first studied in
[
1
]
, where a second order formalism describing the dynamics of
N
-component complex symplectic fermions transforming as scalars in the
(0,0)
representation of the RLG was discussed. Recently, there has been a renewed interest in pseudo-Hermitian theories with flipped statistics. In particular, in
[
2
]
the quantization of spin-
1/2
bosons satisfying the Dirac equation was studied, and in
[
3
]
a bosonic theory for Elko spinors with Wigner degeneracy was analyzed. In this work, we study the canonical quantization of the simplest Lorentz-invariant SOPHY for spin-
1/2
bosons, with the opposite statistics of the theory presented in
[
4
]
.

A pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theory is described by a Lagrangian that satisfies

\mathcal{L}^{\#}\equiv\eta^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\eta=\mathcal{L}.

(1)

for some operator
\eta
.
This departure of Hermiticity was studied in the context of quantum mechanics in
[
5
,
6
]
generalizing the results of
PT
-symmetric systems introduced in
[
7
]
. Recently, there has been progress in the development of consistent pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theories
[
8
]
, in particular in pseudo-Hermitian quantum mechanics, there are two important results: 1) the energy spectrum of the theory is real and 2) the time evolution is unitary upon the definition of a suitable internal product of states based on the
\eta
operator.

The Lagrangian for the
\psi
field is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}=\partial^{\mu}\widehat{\psi}\partial_{\mu}\psi-m^{2}\widehat{\psi}\psi,\end{split}

(2)

where
\widehat{\psi}
is the modified dual field that renders the theory pseudo-Hermitian.
As pointed out in
[
4
]
, in contrast to Dirac theory, our second-order formalism describes a field with eight degrees of freedom and mass dimension one. The most general solution to the Klein-Gordon equation
for our spin-
1/2
fields can be written in terms of two independent Dirac spinors
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
of the form
[
9
]

\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}(\psi_{1}+\gamma^{5}\psi_{2}),

(3)

the mode expansions of
\psi
and
\widehat{\psi}
are given by

\displaystyle\psi(x)=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\sqrt{m\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s}\bigg\{\left(u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{1s}_{\mathbf{p}}+\gamma^{5}u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)e^{-ip\cdot x}+\left(v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{1s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}+\gamma^{5}v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)e^{ip\cdot x}\bigg\},

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}(x)=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\sqrt{m\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s}\bigg\{\left(\bar{u}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{1s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}+\bar{u}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}\gamma^{5}a^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)e^{ip\cdot x}-\left(\bar{v}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{1s}_{\mathbf{p}}+\bar{v}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}\gamma^{5}b^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigg\},

(4)

with
\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=+\sqrt{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}+m^{2}}
,
p^{\mu}=(\omega_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})
, and

u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\phi^{s}\\
\sqrt{p\cdot\overline{\sigma}}\phi^{s}\end{pmatrix},\qquad v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\chi^{s}\\
-\sqrt{p\cdot\overline{\sigma}}\chi^{s}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

are the positive and negative energy solutions of the Dirac free equation, where
\sigma^{\mu}=(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})
,
\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}=(1,-\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. Here, the reference spinors are explicitly given by

\phi^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\phi^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad\chi^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad\chi^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

(6)

satisfying
\chi^{s}=-i\sigma^{2}\phi^{s*}
. The spinors
u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}
,
v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}
are normalized as

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bar{u}^{r}_{\mathbf{p}}u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=2m\delta^{rs},&&\bar{v}^{r}_{\mathbf{p}}v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=-2m\delta^{rs},\\
\end{array}

(7)

and are orthogonal to each other
\bar{u}^{r}_{\mathbf{p}}v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=\bar{v}^{r}_{\mathbf{p}}u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=0
.
Their completeness relations are

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sum_{s}u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}\bar{u}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=\not{p}+m,&&\sum_{s}v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}\bar{v}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}=\not{p}-m.\\
\end{array}

(8)

The dual field in terms of
\eta
, is given by

\widehat{\psi}=\eta^{-1}\bar{\psi}\eta,

(9)

where
\bar{\psi}
is the Dirac adjoint
\bar{\psi}=\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}
and the explicit solution for
\eta
is

\eta=\exp\left[i\pi\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{s}\left(a^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}+b^{1s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{1s}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)\right].

(10)

This operator satisfies
\eta=\eta^{-1}=\eta^{\dagger}
, which means that it is simultaneously Hermitian and unitary, and therefore, we have
\eta^{2}=1
.
It can be straightforwardly verified that this operator
\eta^{-1}(\widehat{\psi}\psi)^{\dagger}\eta=\widehat{\psi}\psi
,
fulfills the pseudo-Hermiticity of Eq. (
2
), in the light of Eq. (
1
).

The equal-time canonical quantization prescription for boson fields is given by

\begin{split}\left[\psi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x},t),\pi_{\psi}{}_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)\right]&=\left[\widehat{\psi}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x},t),\pi_{\widehat{\psi}}{}_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)\right]\\
&=i\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}).\end{split}

(11)

with
\alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,4
, and canonical momenta for the field and the dual defined by

\pi_{\psi}=\partial_{t}\widehat{\psi}=\dot{\widehat{\psi}},\qquad\qquad\pi_{\widehat{\psi}}=\partial_{t}\psi=\dot{\psi},

(12)

respectively.
The imposition of Eq.(
11
) yields the following commutation relations for the momentum-space field operators:

\begin{split}\left[a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}},a^{kr\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]&=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{jk}\delta^{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}),\\
\left[b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}},b^{kr\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]&=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{jk}\delta^{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}),\end{split}

(13)

with all other commutators vanishing. Notice that the role of the operator
\eta
is to change the sign of
[a^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}},a^{2r\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]
and
[b^{1s}_{\mathbf{p}},b^{1r\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]
with respect to that which would be obtained using the Dirac adjoint instead.
In addition, the theory features the correct microcausality structure,
i.e.
, the commutator of the field with itself and the commutator of the dual with itself vanish

\commutator{\psi_\alpha(x)}{\psi_\beta(y)}=\commutator{\widehat{\psi}_\alpha(x)}{ \widehat{\psi}_\beta(y)}=0,

(14)

while the commutator of the field and its dual takes the expected form for a boson field

\begin{split}&\left[\psi_{\alpha}(x),\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)\right]=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\Delta(x-y)\\
&\quad=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}2{\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}}\bigg\{e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}-e^{ip\cdot(x-y)}\bigg\},\end{split}

(15)

where
\Delta(x-y)
is the Schwinger’s Green function.

The Hamiltonian and the momentum operators for our field have the form

\begin{split}H&=:\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\bigg(\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\dot{\psi}+\nabla\widehat{\psi}\cdot\nabla\psi+m^{2}\widehat{\psi}\psi\bigg):,\\
\mathbf{P}&=:-\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\bigg(\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\nabla\psi+\nabla\widehat{\psi}\dot{\psi}\bigg):,\end{split}

(16)

where
:\ :
stands for normal ordering. Explicit calculation leads to

\begin{split}P^{\mu}&=:\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}p^{\mu}\sum_{j,s}\left(a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}+b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right):\\
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}p^{\mu}\sum_{j,s}\left(a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}+b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}\right),\end{split}

(17)

with
P^{\mu}=(H,\mathbf{P})
. Thus, the energy is bounded from below, making the canonical quantization consistent. Notice that the free Hamiltonian is actually Hermitian when written in terms of the momentum-space operators.
Moreover, as can be seen explicitly in Eq. (
2
), the theory possesses a symmetry under global
U(1)
transformations
\psi\to e^{i\theta}\psi,\ \widehat{\psi}\to e^{-i\theta}\widehat{\psi}
, with
\theta
being a constant real parameter. The corresponding conserved charge is

\begin{split}Q&=:i\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\bigg(\widehat{\psi}\dot{\psi}-\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\psi\bigg):\\
&=:\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j,s}\left(a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}-b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right):\\
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j,s}\left(a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}-b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}\right).\end{split}

(18)

There is actually a larger global symmetry in the free theory; due to the commutation relation between the field and its dual
\commutator{\psi_\alpha(x)}{ \widehat{\psi}_\beta(x)}=0
, obtained from Eq. (
15
) when
x=y
, the Lagrangian in Eq. (
2
) can be written as

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\Psi^{T}\Xi\,\partial_{\mu}\Psi-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\Psi^{T}\Xi\,\Psi,

(19)

where we have defined the column matrix

\Psi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\psi}^{T}(x)\\
\psi(x)\end{pmatrix},

(20)

and
\Xi
is a
8\times 8
matrix, written in
4\times 4
blocks as

\Xi=\begin{pmatrix}0_{4\times 4}&1_{4\times 4}\\
1_{4\times 4}&0_{4\times 4}\end{pmatrix}=\sigma^{1}\otimes 1_{4\times 4}.

(21)

Thus, Eq. (
19
) is symmetric under the global transformations
\Psi\to\Psi^{\prime}=M\Psi
with
M^{T}\Xi M=\Xi
. This group of transformations is isomorphic to
O(8,\mathbb{C})
. Writing
M=e^{\alpha X}
, with
\alpha
as a complex parameter, the generators
X
satisfy
X^{T}\Xi+\Xi X=0
. A suitable basis for the 28 independent generators is

1_{2\times 2}\otimes A_{1},\qquad\sigma^{1}\otimes A_{2},\qquad\sigma^{2}\otimes A_{3},\qquad\sigma^{3}\otimes S,

(22)

where
A_{i},S
,
i,=1,2,3
are arbitrary antisymmetric, and symmetric
4\times 4
matrices, respectively.
The discrete transformations of the field and its dual under parity (
P
), charge conjugation (
C
) and time reversal (
T
) follow from the transformation properties of their momentum-space operators

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\mathrm{P}^{-1}=-i(-1)^{j-1}a^{js\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},\quad\mathrm{P}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\mathrm{P}^{-1}=-i(-1)^{j-1}b^{js\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\mathrm{C}^{-1}=-b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}},\quad\quad\quad\mathrm{C}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\mathrm{C}^{-1}=a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\mathrm{T}^{-1}=2sa^{j(-s)\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},\quad\quad\quad\mathrm{T}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\mathrm{T}^{-1}=2sb^{j(-s)\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},

and are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\psi(x)\mathrm{P}^{-1}=i\gamma^{0}\psi(\mathcal{P}x),\quad\mathrm{P}\widehat{\psi}(x)\mathrm{P}^{-1}=-i\widehat{\psi}(\mathcal{P}x)\gamma^{0},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}\psi(x)\mathrm{C}^{-1}=\mathcal{C}\widehat{\psi}^{\,T},\qquad\mathrm{C}\widehat{\psi}\mathrm{C}^{-1}=-\psi^{T}\mathcal{C},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}\psi(x)\mathrm{T}^{-1}=\mathcal{C}\gamma^{5}\psi(\mathcal{T}x),\qquad\mathrm{T}\widehat{\psi}\mathrm{T}^{-1}=-\widehat{\psi}(\mathcal{T}x)\gamma^{5}\mathcal{C},

with
\mathcal{P}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
,
\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
, and
\mathcal{C}=-i\gamma^{2}\gamma^{0}
in the chiral representation.
The corresponding pseudo-Hermitian bilinears transform under
P
as

\begin{split}\mathrm{P}\widehat{\psi}\psi\mathrm{P}^{-1}&=\widehat{\psi}\psi,\\
\mathrm{P}\widehat{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi\mathrm{P}^{-1}&=-\widehat{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi,\\
\mathrm{P}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\mathrm{P}^{-1}&=\mathcal{P}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\nu}\psi,\\
\mathrm{P}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi\mathrm{P}^{-1}&=-\mathcal{P}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{5}\psi,\\
\mathrm{P}\widehat{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi\mathrm{P}^{-1}&=\mathcal{P}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}\mathcal{P}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\widehat{\psi}\sigma^{\lambda\rho}\psi,\end{split}

where
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=i[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]/2
. Similarly, the transformations of the bilinears under
C
and
T
are

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}\widehat{\psi}\psi\mathrm{C}^{-1}&=\widehat{\psi}\psi,\\
\mathrm{C}\widehat{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi\mathrm{C}^{-1}&=\widehat{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi,\\
\mathrm{C}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\mathrm{C}^{-1}&=-\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi,\\
\mathrm{C}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi\mathrm{C}^{-1}&=+\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi,\\
\mathrm{C}\widehat{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi\mathrm{C}^{-1}&=-\widehat{\psi}\sigma^{\lambda\rho}\psi,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\mathrm{T}\widehat{\psi}\psi\mathrm{T}^{-1}&=\widehat{\psi}\psi,\\
\mathrm{T}\widehat{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi\mathrm{T}^{-1}&=-\widehat{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi,\\
\mathrm{T}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\mathrm{T}^{-1}&=-\mathcal{T}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\nu}\psi,\\
\mathrm{T}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi\mathrm{T}^{-1}&=-\mathcal{T}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\widehat{\psi}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{5}\psi,\\
\mathrm{T}\widehat{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi\mathrm{T}^{-1}&=-\mathcal{T}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}\mathcal{T}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\widehat{\psi}\sigma^{\lambda\rho}\psi,\end{split}

and therefore, the free theory is invariant under each discrete symmetry simultaneously, and also under
CPT
.

We conclude our analysis of the free theory with the Feynman propagator, that can be straightforwardly written as

\begin{split}&[S_{F}(x-y)]_{\alpha\beta}=\bra{0}T[\psi_{\alpha}(x)\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)]\ket{0}\\
&=\bra{0}[\theta(x^{0}-y^{0})\psi_{\alpha}(x)\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)+\theta(y^{0}-x^{0})\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)\psi_{\alpha}(x)]\ket{0}\\
&=\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[\frac{i\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon}\right]e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)},\end{split}

(25)

as expected for a field that satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation.

Regarding interactions, since our fields have mass dimension one, it is possible to introduce renormalizable quartic self-interactions analogous to those studied for fermion fields in
[
10
,
11
]
in the context of the naive Hermitian second-order theory, where it was shown that this class of theories has a rich set of renormalization group equations. Besides, this field can be identified as a WIMP dark matter candidate that interacts with the standard model fields through a Higgs portal, in an analogous way to the spin-
1/2
fermion model introduced in
[
12
]
.

Summarizing, in this letter, we have presented the canonical quantization of a massive boson field transforming under the
(\tfrac{1}{2},0)\oplus(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
representation of the RLG, and obeying the Klein-Gordon equation. The quantization of the free theory is consistent, and the spin-statistics connection is evaded through the redefinition of the field dual. The resulting formalism is surprisingly simple, as it is directly built with two independent Dirac fields, and it belongs to a class of second order pseudo-Hermitian theories, SOPHIES for short, that include those studied in
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
, where Hermiticity is replaced by the less stringent relation displayed in Eq.(
1
).

A third-order conservation law for the Kirchhoff-Pokhozhaev equation
Chiara Boiti, Renato Manfrin

Abstract
. We prove that the special Kirchhoff equation studied by Pokhozhaev in [ 7 , 8 ] admits a third-order conservation law. We further show that if the energy of the solution is sufficiently small, then the L 2 L^{2} -norms of the derivatives up to third order of the solution remain uniformly bounded with respect to time.

1.
Introduction and results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}
be a bounded, smooth domain or
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\hskip 0.5pt
. Given a continuous function

m:[0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
, let us consider the quasi-linear Kirchhoff wave equation

(1.1)

u_{tt}-m\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)\Delta u=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times[0,T),

for some
T>0
, with the boundary condition

(1.2)

u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times[0,T),

if
\hskip 0.5pt\Omega\hskip 0.5pt
is a bounded, smooth domain. As it is known, equation (
1.1
) is of variational type and each solution has a conserved energy. Namely, if
\hskip 1.0ptu\hskip 1.0pt
is a sufficiently regular solution of
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
), then the first-order functional

(1.3)

I_{1}:=\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{2}dx+M\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right),

where
M(h)=\int_{0}^{h}m(s)ds\hskip 0.5pt
, remains constant in
\hskip 0.5pt[0,T)\hskip 0.5pt
. For the Kirchhoff equation, in the general form (
1.1
), this is
essentially the only known conservation law.

In 1974 Pokhozhaev
presented in
[
7
]
a second-order conservation low for the special
Kirhhoff equation

(1.4)

u_{tt}-\frac{\Delta u}{\big(a\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+b\big)^{2}}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times[0,T),

with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
not both zero. More precisely:

Let
\hskip 0.5ptu\hskip 0.5pt
be a sufficiently regular function in
\hskip 0.5pt\Omega\times[0,T)\hskip 0.5pt
such that

(1.5)

a\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+b\neq 0\quad\text{for}\quad t\in[0,T);

if
\hskip 0.5ptu\hskip 0.5pt
is a solution of (
1.4
), (
1.2
) then the second-order functional

(1.6)

I_{2}\,:=\,(a\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b)\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\|\Delta u\|^{2}}{a\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b}-{a}\left(\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\!\nabla u_{t}\hskip 1.0ptdx\right)^{2},

where
\,\|\cdot\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|\cdot|^{2}dx
,  remains constant in
[0,T)
.

Still in
[
7
]
, Pokhozhaev proved the remarkable fact that (
1.4
) is the unique case in which the Kirchhoff
equation (
1.1
) admits
a second order conservation law. That is, (
1.1
)
has a second-order conservation law if and only if

(1.7)

m(s)=\frac{1}{(as+b)^{2}}\hskip 0.5pt,

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
not both zero. See also
[
2
]
for a simple derivation of the expression (
1.6
).

The existence of a conservation law for a second-order functional such as (
1.6
) is important because it implies, under suitable assumptions, a-priori estimates for
\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|^{2}
.
In turn, these a-priori estimates lead, as shown in
[
1
]
,
to the proof of the global solvability of the initial boundary value problems and Cauchy problems for (
1.4
). For example, a-priori estimates for
\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|^{2}
can be easily obtained from (
1.6
) in the following cases:

(i)
If
a,b>0
, (
1.6
) implies

(1.8)

\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}\leq I_{2}b^{-1}

since
\hskip 1.0pta\|\nabla u\|^{2}\cdot\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}-a(\hskip 0.5pt\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\!\nabla u_{t}\hskip 1.0ptdx)^{2}\geq 0\hskip 1.0pt
. Then, for
t\geq 0
,

\|\nabla u(t)\|\leq\|\nabla u(0)\|+\sqrt{{I_{2}}b^{-1}}\hskip 1.0ptt.

Therefore

\,\left|\frac{d}{dt}\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{I_{2}b^{-1}}\left(\|\nabla u(0)\|+\sqrt{{I_{2}}b^{-1}}\hskip 1.0ptt\right).

This inequality was used in
[
8
]
and then in
[
6
]
to prove global solvability.

(ii)
If
a<0
,
b>0
and
\,a\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b>0\hskip 1.0pt
(i.e.
\|\nabla u\|^{2}<b|a|^{-1}
), then by (
1.6
)

\displaystyle I_{2}\geq|a|\left(\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{t}\right)^{2}=|a|\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\right)^{2}.

Therefore, we have

(1.9)

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{{I_{2}}{|a|^{-1}}}.

This case was considered in
[
5
]
.

In both cases
(i)
and
(ii)
, the boundedness of
\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|^{2}
, together with the conservation law for
\hskip 0.5ptI_{1}\hskip 0.5pt
, allows the extension of the local solution beyond any fixed
T>0
, leading to a global solution. Moreover, in case
(ii)
, the global
existence of solutions was established under weak regularity assumptions on the initial data,
lower than those required by the functional
I_{2}
(see
[
5
]
).

In this paper, we show that (
1.4
) has also a third-order conservation law (see
Theorem
1.1
) and then we prove, under the assumption of small initial data, the boundeness of the
L^{2}
- norms of the derivatives of the solutions up to third order (see Theorem
1.5
).

More precisely, choosing
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
for simplicity, setting

(1.10)

s=s(t):=\|\nabla u\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}dx,

we prove the following third-order conservation law for the Kirchhoff-Pokhozhaev equation

(1.11)

\displaystyle{u_{tt}-\frac{\Delta u}{\big(a\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+b\big)^{2}}=0}\quad\;\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
be a function defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T)
, for some
T>0
, and suppose

(1.12)

u\in C^{k}([0,T);H^{3-k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),\quad\text{for}\quad k=0,\hskip 1.0pt1

and

(1.13)

q=q(t)=a\hskip 0.5pt\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b\neq 0\quad\text{in}\quad[0,T).

If
\hskip 0.5ptu
is a solution of (
1.11
), then the third-order functional

(1.14)

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle:=\hskip 1.0ptq\hskip 1.0pt{\|\Delta u_{t}\|^{2}}+\frac{\|\nabla(\Delta u)\|^{2}}{q}-{q^{\prime}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u\hskip 0.5pt\Delta u_{t}\hskip 1.0ptdx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\hskip 1.0pt{q^{\prime}}^{2}\hskip 1.0pt\left({q\hskip 1.0pt\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}}+\frac{\|\Delta u\|^{2}}{q}\right)-\frac{a}{16}\left(\frac{a^{2}\hskip 0.5pt{s^{\prime}}^{4}}{4}+{q^{2}\thinspace{s^{\prime\prime}}^{2}}\right)

remains constant in
[0,T)
.

Remark 1.2
.

In (
1.14
)
s^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}
are the derivatives of
\hskip 1.0pts(t)
defined in (
1.10
). Under the assumptions
of Theorem
1.1
, we easily have

(1.15)

\displaystyle s^{\prime}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u\cdot\!\nabla u_{t}\hskip 0.5pt\hskip 0.5ptdx\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad s^{\prime\prime}=2\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{2}{q^{2}}\|\Delta u\|^{2},

where the expression of
\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime\prime}
is obtained taking into account that
\hskip 1.0ptu
is a solution of (
1.11
).

Note that, while the first-order functional
I_{1}
is always positive definite (i.e.
>0
if

\hskip 0.5ptu\hskip 0.5pt
is not constant),

I_{2}
and
I_{3}
are positive definite
only under some additional assumptions, such as
(i)
or
(ii)
above. With the following two lemmas, we prove that the functionals
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
are positive definite when
\hskip 0.5pta\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
,
\hskip 0.5ptb>0\hskip 0.5pt

and
I_{1}
is sufficiently small.

More precisely, let us consider
\hskip 0.5ptI_{1}\hskip 0.5pt
in the case of the
Kirchhoff-Pokhozhaev equation (
1.11
) with
\hskip 0.5pta\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
and
\hskip 0.5ptb>0\hskip 0.5pt
.
That is, according to (
1.3
), the functional

(1.16)

I_{1}\,:=\,\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}\frac{ds}{(a\hskip 0.5pts+b)^{2}}\,=\,\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{b}\hskip 1.0pt\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}{a\hskip 1.0pt\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b}\hskip 1.0pt,

where, obviously, we must assume

(1.17)

\|\nabla u\|^{2}<\frac{b}{|a|}\quad\text{if}\quad a<0\hskip 0.5pt.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
\hskip 0.5pta\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
,
\hskip 0.5ptb>0\hskip 0.5pt
. Then, we have that

(1.18)

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{6|a\hskip 0.5ptb|}\;\quad\Rightarrow\;\quad\frac{b}{2}\leq q\leq\frac{3}{2}\hskip 1.0ptb\quad\text{and}\quad|a|\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2},

for all
\hskip 0.5ptt\in[0,T)
, where

(1.19)

\lambda\,:=\,q\hskip 1.0pt\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}{q}\hskip 1.0pt.

Furthermore, when
\hskip 0.5pt\lambda\hskip 0.5pt
is small and
\hskip 0.5ptq>0\hskip 0.5pt
, we also have:

Lemma 1.4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
hold, and also assume that
q>0
in
[0,T)
.

If
\hskip 0.5ptu
is a solution of (
1.11
), then
the functionals
\hskip 0.5ptI_{2},I_{3}\hskip 0.5pt
satisfy the inequalities:

(1.20)

\frac{2}{3}\,I_{2}\,\leq\,q\hskip 1.0pt{\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}}+\frac{\|\Delta u\|^{2}}{q}\,\leq\,2\,I_{2},\quad\text{if}\quad|a|\lambda\leq 2\hskip 0.5pt;

(1.21)

\frac{2}{3}\,I_{3}\,\leq\,q\hskip 1.0pt{\|\Delta u_{t}\|^{2}}+\frac{\|\nabla(\Delta u)\|^{2}}{q}\,\leq\,2\,I_{3},\quad\text{if}\quad|a|\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}\hskip 1.0pt.

The previous two lemmas now lead to the following result:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\hskip 0.5pta\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
,
\hskip 0.5ptb>0\hskip 0.5pt
.
Let
u
be a solution of (
1.11
) that satisfies (
1.12
) and such that

(1.22)

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{6|a\hskip 0.5ptb|}\hskip 1.0pt.

Then
\hskip 1.0pt\frac{b}{2}\leq q\leq\frac{3}{2}\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip 1.0pt
and the estimates (
1.20
), (
1.21
) are verified
for all
\hskip 1.0ptt\in[0,T)
.

In particular, this means that the
L^{2}
-norms

\hskip 1.0pt\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{k}u\|\hskip 1.0pt
, for
\hskip 1.0pt1\leq|\alpha|+k\leq 3\hskip 1.0pt
,
are uniformly bounded in
[0,T)
, independently of
\hskip 1.0ptT>0
.

Remarks 1.6
.

In the last statement of Theorem
1.5
, concerning the boundedness of the
L^{2}
-norms of the derivatives of the solution, we can simply assume
\hskip 0.5ptab\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
. In fact, the equation (
1.11
) does not change if we replace
\hskip 0.5pta\hskip 0.5pt
with
\hskip 0.5pt-a\hskip 0.5pt
and
\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip 0.5pt
with
\hskip 0.5pt-b\hskip 0.5pt
, respectively. We have used the condition
\hskip 0.5pta\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
,
\hskip 0.5ptb>0\hskip 0.5pt
only for simplicity in the statements and proofs of Lemma
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
.

Furthermore, in Theorem
1.5
, we have not considered the cases
\hskip 0.5pta=0\hskip 0.5pt
,
\hskip 0.5ptb\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
and
\hskip 0.5pta\neq 0\hskip 0.5pt
,
\hskip 0.5ptb=0\hskip 0.5pt
.
The first is trivial, since (
1.11
) becomes a linear wave equation.
The second is, in a certain sense, a degenerate case because when

equation (

) does not have the solution
\hskip 0.5ptu\equiv 0\hskip 0.5pt
.

Note that equation (
1.11
) keep the regularity of the data also on the solution, but Theorem

gives extra information about boundedness of the derivatives

\hskip 0.5ptI_{1}\hskip 0.5pt
and
\hskip 0.5ptI_{2}\hskip 0.5pt
.

Finally, we note that all the previuos results remain valid if
\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}

is a bounded smooth domain, replacing the Fourier transform with the
Fourier series expansion in eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator (as in
[
8
]
) and adopting the appropriate

The paper is organized as follows:
we apply the partial Fourier transform with respect to the

).
In Section

As special cases, we immediately get, in Section

.
Finally, in Sections

and
1.4

(and hence Theorem
1.5
).

2.
A quadratic form for a Liouville type equation

Let us consider the Liouville-type (cf.
[
4
, §6.2]
) second-order ordinary differential equation

(2.1)

w_{tt}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}\hskip 0.5pt|\xi|^{2}\hskip 0.5ptw=0\quad\text{for}\quad t\in[0,T),

depending on the parameter
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\hskip 0.5pt
, with

q=q(t)\in C^{4}([0,T)),\quad q(t)\neq 0\quad\text{in}\quad[0,T).

Following
[
3
, § 2]
, given a complex-valued solution

w=w(\xi,t)

of (
2.1
), we consider the
quadratic form
\hskip 1.0pt{\pazocal E}={\pazocal E}(\xi,t)
defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\pazocal E}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=0}^{1}\alpha_{i}|\xi|^{4-2i}\left(|w_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{1}\beta_{i}|\xi|^{4-2i}\Re(\overline{w}\hskip 0.5ptw_{t})+\gamma_{0}|\xi|^{2}|w_{t}|^{2},

where
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}(t),\beta_{i}=\beta_{i}(t)
and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(t)

are suitable
C^{1}
real functions in
[0,T)
to be determined later in order that

(2.3)

\frac{d}{dt}{\pazocal E}(\xi,t)=\beta_{1}^{\prime}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\overline{w}w_{t}).

To this aim we first compute

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\alpha_{0}|\xi|^{4}\left(|w_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}\right)+\beta_{0}|\xi|^{4}\Re(\overline{w}\hskip 0.5ptw_{t})+\gamma_{0}|\xi|^{2}|w_{t}|^{2}\right]

taking into account that
\hskip 1.0ptw\hskip 1.0pt
solves (
2.1
) and hence

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|w_{t}|^{2}=w_{tt}\bar{w}_{t}+w_{t}\bar{w}_{tt}=-\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}w\bar{w}_{t}-\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}w_{t}\bar{w}=-\frac{2}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\bar{w}w_{t}),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Re(\bar{w}w_{t})=\Re(\bar{w}_{t}w_{t}+\bar{w}w_{tt})=|w_{t}|^{2}-\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\bar{w}w)=|w_{t}|^{2}-\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}.

Substituting (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and
\frac{d}{dt}|w|^{2}=2\Re(\bar{w}w_{t})

into (
2.4
), we get:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left[\alpha_{0}|\xi|^{4}\left(|w_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}\right)+\beta_{0}|\xi|^{4}\Re(\overline{w}\hskip 0.5ptw_{t})+\gamma_{0}|\xi|^{2}|w_{t}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{q^{2}}\right]|\xi|^{6}|w|^{2}+\left[\alpha_{0}^{\prime}+\beta_{0}\right]|\xi|^{4}|w_{t}|^{2}+\left[\beta_{0}^{\prime}-2\frac{\gamma_{0}}{q^{2}}\right]|\xi|^{4}\Re(\overline{w}\hskip 0.5ptw_{t})

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{\prime}|\xi|^{2}|w_{t}|^{2}.

Similarly,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left[\alpha_{1}|\xi|^{2}\left(|w_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}\right)+\beta_{1}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\overline{w}\hskip 0.5ptw_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{q^{2}}\right]|\xi|^{4}|w|^{2}+\left[\alpha_{1}^{\prime}+\beta_{1}\right]|\xi|^{2}|w_{t}|^{2}+\beta_{1}^{\prime}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\overline{w}\hskip 0.5ptw_{t}).

Let us now find

\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}
satisfying

(2.9)

{\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{q^{2}}=0}\\
\displaystyle{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}+\beta_{0}=0\phantom{\int^{8}_{8}}}\\
\displaystyle{\beta_{0}^{\prime}-2\frac{\gamma_{0}}{q^{2}}=0,}\end{cases}}

and then
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
such that

(2.10)

{\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{q^{2}}=0}\\
\displaystyle{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}+\beta_{1}=-\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\hskip 0.5pt,}\end{cases}}

so that the desired equality (
2.3
) is satisfied.

From the first two equations of (
2.9
) we find

(2.11)

\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{q^{2}}=\frac{2}{q}\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{q}\right)^{\prime}=0.

Hence, we have

(2.12)

\alpha_{0}=C_{0}\hskip 0.5ptq,\quad\beta_{0}=-C_{0}\hskip 0.5ptq^{\prime},\quad\gamma_{0}=-\frac{C_{0}}{2}\hskip 0.5ptq^{2}\hskip 0.5ptq^{\prime\prime},

with
C_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
arbitrary. Next, from system (
2.10
), we find

(2.13)

\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{q^{2}}=\frac{2}{q}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{\prime}}{q^{2}}=0,

and hence

(2.14)

\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q}\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{2}\thinspace\frac{\gamma_{0}^{\prime}}{q}=\frac{C_{0}}{4}(2q^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}+qq^{\prime\prime\prime})

because of (
2.12
). Therefore

(2.15)

\alpha_{1}=\frac{C_{0}}{4}\hskip 0.5pt\hskip 0.5ptq\left(\frac{{q^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}+qq^{\prime\prime}\right)+C_{1}\hskip 0.5ptq,

with
C_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
arbitrary. Finally, from the second equation of (
2.10
), we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{0}^{\prime}-\alpha_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{0}}{2}(q^{2}q^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{C_{0}}{4}\left[q\left(\frac{{q^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}+qq^{\prime\prime}\right)\right]^{\prime}-C_{1}q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{0}}{4}\left(\frac{{q^{\prime}}^{3}}{2}-qq^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}-q^{2}q^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)-C_{1}\hskip 0.5ptq^{\prime}.

We have thus found
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
such that (
2.3
)
is satisfied.

3.
The quadratic form in the case of equation (

)

2

1.11

\hskip 0.5ptu\hskip 0.5pt
satisfies all the assumptions of Theorem

.

\hskip 0.5ptu\hskip 0.5pt

From (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\overline{w}w_{t})d\xi=\frac{1}{2}\thinspace s^{\prime}\hskip 0.5pt,

gives

(3.7)

\frac{d}{dt}E=\beta_{1}^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\overline{w}w_{t})d\xi=\frac{1}{2}\thinspace\beta_{1}^{\prime}\thinspace s^{\prime}\hskip 0.5pt.

Now, using the explicit definition of
\beta_{1}=\beta_{1}(t)
given in (
2.16
), it is easy to show that
the quantity
\beta_{1}^{\prime}\thinspace s^{\prime}
is the derivative of a polynomial in
\hskip 0.5pts,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\hskip 0.5pt
and
\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime\prime\prime}\hskip 0.5pt
. We have indeed:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{\prime}\thinspace s^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(\beta_{1}\thinspace s^{\prime}\thinspace\big)^{\prime}-\beta_{1}\thinspace s^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(\beta_{1}\thinspace s^{\prime}\thinspace\big)^{\prime}+\frac{C_{0}}{4}\left(\frac{{q^{\prime}}^{3}}{2}-qq^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}-q^{2}q^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)s^{\prime\prime}+C_{1}\hskip 0.5ptq^{\prime}\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(\beta_{1}\thinspace s^{\prime}\thinspace\big)^{\prime}+\frac{C_{0}}{4}\left(\frac{a^{3}\,{s^{\prime}}^{3}}{2}-a^{2}(as+b)s^{\prime}s^{\prime\prime}-a(as+b)^{2}s^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime\prime}+C_{1}\hskip 0.5pta\hskip 0.5pt\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime}\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(\beta_{1}\thinspace s^{\prime}\thinspace\big)^{\prime}+\frac{C_{0}\hskip 0.5pta}{8}\left(\frac{a^{2}\hskip 0.5pt{s^{\prime}}^{4}}{4}-{(as+b)^{2}\thinspace{s^{\prime\prime}}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{C_{1}\hskip 0.5pta}{2}\thinspace\big({s^{\prime}}^{2}\big)^{\prime}.

Therefore, taking into account (
3.7
), we have proved that the quantity

(3.9)

Q\hskip 1.0pt:=\hskip 1.0ptE-\frac{\beta_{1}\thinspace s^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{C_{0}\hskip 0.5pta}{16}\left(\frac{a^{2}\hskip 0.5pt{s^{\prime}}^{4}}{4}-{(as+b)^{2}\thinspace{s^{\prime\prime}}^{2}}\right)-\frac{C_{1}\hskip 0.5pta}{4}\thinspace{s^{\prime}}^{2}

remains constant in
[0,T)
.

Finally, let us compute
\hskip 0.5ptQ\hskip 0.5pt
explicitly, taking into account (
3.6
) and the definitions
of the coefficients
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}
and
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
given in (
2.12
), (
2.15
) and (
2.16
). We have:

(3.10)

\displaystyle Q\,={}

\displaystyle C_{0}\left(q\hskip 1.0pt{\||\xi|^{2}w_{t}\|^{2}}+\frac{\||\xi|^{3}w\|^{2}}{q}\right)-C_{0}\hskip 1.0ptq^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{4}\Re(\overline{w}w_{t})d\xi-\frac{C_{0}}{2}q^{2}q^{\prime\prime}{\||\xi|w_{t}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{C_{0}}{4}q\hskip 0.5pt\left(\frac{{q^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}+qq^{\prime\prime}\right)+C_{1}q\right]\left({\||\xi|w_{t}\|^{2}}+\frac{\||\xi|^{2}w\|^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{0}\hskip 0.5pta}{16}\left(\frac{a^{2}\hskip 0.5pt{s^{\prime}}^{4}}{4}-{q^{2}\thinspace{s^{\prime\prime}}^{2}}\right)-\frac{C_{1}\hskip 0.5pta}{4}\thinspace{s^{\prime}}^{2},

where
\,C_{0},C_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\,
are arbitrary constants.

1.1

\hskip 1.0ptq^{\prime\prime}=a\hskip 0.5pts^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.0pt

(4.4)

\displaystyle Q=

5.
Proof of Lemma
1.3

We note that, having
a\neq 0
and
b>0
,
the functional
I_{1}

is (in any case) well defined for
\hskip 1.0pt\|\nabla u\|^{2}<\frac{b}{|a|}\hskip 1.0pt
and we can rewrite the expression (
1.16
) as

(5.1)

I_{1}=\|u_{t}\|^{2}+{\frac{1}{b}}\hskip 1.0pt\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}{q},

where
\hskip 0.5ptq=a\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b>0\hskip 0.5pt
.
Now, it is easy to see that

(5.2)

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3|a|b}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\frac{b}{2|a|}\hskip 0.5pt.

In fact, if
\hskip 1.0ptI_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3|a|b}\,
, then (
5.1
) gives

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\frac{q}{3|a|}=\frac{a}{3|a|}\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\frac{b}{3|a|}

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\hskip 0.5pt\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{a}{3|a|}\right)\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\frac{b}{3|a|}.

Therefore
\hskip 0.5pt\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\frac{b}{2|a|}\hskip 0.5pt
and hence we immediately get

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\,\leq\,q=a\|\nabla u\|^{2}+b\,\leq\,\frac{3}{2}\hskip 1.0ptb\hskip 0.5pt.

That is, the first statement in (
1.18
) is satisfied.

Finally, let us prove the second statement of (
1.18
). Taking into account the definition (
1.19
)
of
\hskip 0.5pt\lambda\hskip 0.5pt
, we note that

(5.4)

|a|\lambda\leq|a|qI_{1}+|a|bI_{1}=|a|(q+b)I_{1}\hskip 0.5pt.

Therefore, if
\hskip 1.0ptI_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3|a|b}\,
, from (
5.3
) and
(
5.4
) we obtain that

(5.5)

|a|\lambda\leq 3|a|b\hskip 1.0ptI_{1}\hskip 0.5pt.

Hence, we find that

(5.6)

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{6|a|b}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;|a|\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.5pt.

1.4

(6.1)

\displaystyle|s^{\prime}|

Therefore, we deduce that

(6.2)

|a|\,\frac{{s^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(q\hskip 1.0pt\||\xi|w_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\||\xi|^{2}w\|^{2}}{q}\right),

.

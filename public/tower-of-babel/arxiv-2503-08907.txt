From Models To Experiments: Shallow Recurrent Decoder Networks on the DYNASTY Experimental Facility
Carolina Introini a , Stefano Riva a , J. Nathan Kutz b , Antonio Cammi c,a,∗

Abstract
The Shallow Recurrent Decoder networks are a novel paradigm recently introduced for state estimation, combining sparse observations with high-dimensional model data. This architecture features important advantages compared to standard data-driven methods including: the ability to use only three sensors (even randomly selected) for reconstructing the entire dynamics of a physical system; the ability to train on compressed data spanned by a reduced basis; the ability to measure a single field variable (easy to measure) and reconstruct coupled spatio-temporal fields that are not observable and minimal hyper-parameter tuning. This approach has been verified on different test cases within different fields including nuclear reactors, even though an application to a real experimental facility, adopting the employment of in-situ observed quantities, is missing. This work aims to fill this gap by applying the Shallow Recurrent Decoder architecture to the DYNASTY facility, built at Politecnico di Milano, which studies the natural circulation established by internally heated fluids for Generation IV applications, especially in the case of Circulating Fuel reactors. The RELAP5 code is used to generate the high-fidelity data, and temperature measurements extracted by the facility are used as input for the state estimation. The results of this work will provide a validation of the Shallow Recurrent Decoder architecture to engineering systems, showing the capabilities of this approach to provide and accurate state estimation.



I
Introduction

Fast, accurate and reliable
digital twin
of nuclear and nuclear-related engineering plants
Grieves_DT
are of growing interest in the nuclear community
mohanty_development_2021
as an innovative way of monitoring and controlling a nuclear power plant. One of the key capabilities of models comprising a digital twin should be to infer the high-dimensional state of the system from sparse sensor measurements
argaud_sensor_2018
. Furthermore, such models have to be accurate and quick to solve so that the digital twin can be used in online monitoring, control or sensitivity analysis. Thus, an important property desired in digital twins is the ability to handle data coming from the physical system and combine their information with the background knowledge of mathematical models to, on one hand, account for un-modelled physics in the latter, and on the other hand, account for unseen phenomena in the former
haik_real-time_2023
;
riva2024multiphysics
.

The development of digital twins is essentially related to the possibility of generating accurate surrogate models of the systems. They are mainly based on Reduced Order Modelling (ROM)
lassila_model_2014
;
gong_efficient_2022
or Machine Learning (ML) architectures
brunton_data-driven_2022
;
cheng_efficient_2024
. The former includes methods which aim at generating surrogate models on a low-dimensional latent space spanned by a set of interpretable basis functions, with which to retrieve a reduced representation of the variables of interest
rozza_model_2020
. ML has emerged as a different paradigm to better deal with non-linear dynamics and large amounts of data
brunton_data-driven_2022
; however, ML methods typically lack interpretability and are affected by poor extrapolation capabilities. In this work, a combination of these approaches, the SHallow REcurrent Decoder (SHRED) network
williams2022data
;
kutz_shallow_2024
is investigated. This architecture has been proven to be an accurate proxy to surrogate models of high-fidelity data, at a comparatively negligible computational cost. The SHRED architecture is an example of a data-driven methodology that builds a surrogate model through a training and learning process; mathematically, it is a generalization of the separation of variables method for solving Partial Differential Equations (PDEs)
shredrom
.

SHRED is a deep learning model that exploits the separation of time/parameters and space to build a reduced-order, non-linear model. The first separation is performed through the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)
lassila_model_2014
;
rozza_model_2020
(a linear algebra technique onto which most ROM methods are built) that can generate a suitable coordinate interpretable system
brunton_data-driven_2022
in the form of a set of basis functions. Integrating the SVD into SHRED has been proven to be highly useful, enabling a compressive training approach and heavily reducing the computational costs of the learning process
kutz_shallow_2024
. This technique has already been used to reconstruct high-dimensional data in the field of plasma dynamics
kutz_shallow_2024
, innovative reactor concepts
riva2024robuststateestimationpartial
and parametric fluid dynamics datasets
shredrom
, showing an outstanding capability in generating accurate, reliable and efficient surrogate models.

. The sensors time series are used to construct a latent temporal sequence model which is mapped to the compressive representations of all spatio-temporal field variables. The compressive representations can then be mapped to the original state space by the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).

However, it is yet to be studied how SHRED performs in an experimental facility in combining both model and real data directly collected on the system. Compared to synthetic data, experimental measures are not perfect, not to mention the existing discrepancies between data and model
maday_parameterized-background_2014
. To fulfil this next step in the analysis of the SHRED architecture, the DYNASTY experimental facility (DYnamics of NAtural circulation for molten SalT internallY heated), built at Politecnico di Milano
benzoni2023preliminary
, is used as a test case. This system has been built to study the natural circulation dynamics for internally heated fluids, a topic of primary concern for the development of circulating fuel reactors such as the Molten Salt Fast Reactor
GenIV-RoadMap
. In particular, the RELAP5 (R5) code
fletcher1992relap5
is used to generate the high-dimensional data. The R5 model will be used to provide the mathematical background, and the SHRED architecture will be verified and validated on the experimental data, which will represent the input for the architecture: the focus will be on reconstruction for unknown parameters and forecasting in time outside the training database.

II
Shallow Recurrent Decoder Network

The SHallow REcurrent Decoder is a novel neural network architecture
williams2022data
;
kutz_shallow_2024
designed to map measurement trajectories
\mathbf{y}
to a latent space
\mathbf{v}
, effectively encoding the dynamics of a high-dimensional system. SHRED combines three key components: a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network
hochreiter1997long
to learn the temporal dynamics, a Shallow Decoder Network (SDN)
erichson2020shallow
for mapping measurements back to the latent space, and the compression capabilities of the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)
brunton_data-driven_2022
. The architecture is illustrated in Figure
1
: the LSTM leverages Takens embedding theory to capture temporal patterns, while the SDN reconstructs latent representations that are subsequently decompressed using the SVD.

SHRED offers several advantages over traditional data-driven reduced-order modelling approaches. These include: (1) requiring as few as three sensors, even randomly placed; (2) enabling training on compressed data, which drastically reduces computational costs and allows for laptop-level training in minutes
kutz_shallow_2024
; (3) effectively handling of multi-physics (coupled) datasets even when only measurements from a single variable are available
riva2024robuststateestimationpartial
; (4) minimal hyperparameter tuning, as the same architecture can be applied across a wide range of problems
williams2022data
;
kutz_shallow_2024
;
riva2024robuststateestimationpartial
;
shredrom
.

Figure 2:
DYNASTY natural circulation loop
cammi2019dynamics
: on the left, a picture of the real facility is provided, whereas on the right the scheme of the system is reported with the main components of the facility.

The SHRED architecture has been implemented in Python utilizing the PyTorch package, starting from the original work by
williams2022data
. The code used in this paper is openly available at
https://github.com/ERMETE-Lab/NuSHRED
. The architecture, shown in Figure
1
, is composed as follows: both the LSTM and SDN networks consist of two hidden layers, the first having 64 neurons each, the second having 350 and 400 neurons in the two layers. Even though SHRED was initially developed for a single parameter configuration
williams2022data
;
riva2024robuststateestimationpartial
;
kutz_shallow_2024
, the same architecture extends pretty easily to parametric datasets with minimal modifications
shredrom
. These adjustments mainly involve compressing the entire parametric dataset using the SVD, as SHRED naturally supports the inclusion of multiple trajectories corresponding to varying parameters. In fact, SHRED can map the signals of the parametric measures to the correspondent latent representation. Furthermore, the parameters
\boldsymbol{\mu}
can be incorporated either as an input (if known) or as an output (if estimation is required).

For parametric datasets, compression via SVD is a crucial step. Given a snapshot matrix
\mathbb{X}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}\times N_{t}}
for a specific parameter
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{h}
represents the spatial degrees of freedom and
N_{t}
the number of time steps, the SVD computes a basis
\mathbb{U}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}\times r}
of rank
r
. This basis allows the generation of a latent representation
\mathbb{V}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}}=\left(\mathbb{U}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}}\right)^{T}\mathbb{X}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times N_{t}}
, which embeds the temporal dynamics required for SHRED training. When working with parametric datasets, a common basis capturing the dominant spatial physics is essential. If enough RAM is available, the snapshots for all parameters can be stacked as
\mathbb{X}=\left[\mathbb{X}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}}|\mathbb{X}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}2}|\dots|\mathbb{X}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}{N_{p}}}\right]
to directly perform the SVD onto the full database. Otherwise, hierarchical SVD must be performed. However, the model used in this work is a 1D model; thus, the dimension of the spatial mesh is limited: the stacking procedure and the SVD onto the whole dataset can then be performed, obtaining a general SVD basis for all the parametric instances.

In the end, as already said, SHRED model can be trained in minutes even on a personal laptop
kutz_shallow_2024
. Due to this, it becomes cheap to train different SHRED models for different sensor configurations, i.e., for other random sensor locations, to obtain more than one estimation of the state such that a measure of the uncertainty associated with the prediction can be provided. This way of proceeding can be useful to generate state estimations which are robust against noisy measurements collected on the physical system itself.

III
DYNASTY Experimental Facility

The experimental facility DYNASTY (DYnamics of NAtural circulation for molten SalT internallY heated) has been built at the Energy Labs of Politecnico di Milano to study the dynamics of natural circulation in the presence of an internal distributed heat source
benzoni2023preliminary
. The loop has been entirely constructed using AISI316 stainless steel to withstand the expected high operative temperatures of different working fluids, with pipes 38 mm in diameter and 2 mm in thickness. DYNASTY is composed of four legs arranged in a rectangular shape, with three of them featuring external heating strips (GV1, GV2, GO1-2) to simulate the internal heat source (a reasonable approximation as the diameter/height ratio of the facility is large), as shown in Figure
2
. The heat sink is represented by the upper finned tube, optionally cooled from below through an air fan. Various heating configurations can be experimentally set up, enabling either simultaneous activation of all strips or individual operation of one leg at a time, and performing fully passive cooling or active cooling by activating the fan. In the present work, a RELAP5/MOD3.3
fletcher1992relap5
DYNASTY model is utilized to simulate the experiment in the so-called VHHC-GV1 configuration (vertically heated-horizontally cooled, with the left vertical leg as heat source), with fully passive cooling. This is just one of the possible experimental setups, for the interested reader refer to
cammi2019dynamics
;
benzoni2023preliminary
.

DYNASTY is equipped with four fluid J-type thermocouples, located at the corners of the facility and labelled from TC1 to TC4: they have an absolute uncertainty of ±0.5°C on the measured value, with additional noise added by the connected electronic system. Additionally, a sensor for measuring the mass flow rate is installed in the centre of the bottom horizontal leg GO1, with an estimated uncertainty of ±0.25 g/s. Measures are recorded through a Data Acquisition and Control System which saves the recorded data (eventually removing spikes) every second and then stores them into MATLAB matrices.

III.1
The RELAP5 model of DYNASTY

The RELAP5 (R5) code has been developed at the Idaho National Engineering Laboratory for the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission
fletcher1992relap5
. R5 is a versatile code designed for modelling thermal-hydraulic transients in nuclear and non-nuclear systems involving steam, water, non-condensable gases, and solutes. Based on a one-dimensional, non-homogeneous, non-equilibrium transient model with a partially implicit numerical scheme, it balances accuracy in capturing first-order effects with simplicity for cost-effective, sensitivity, and parametric analyses. It supports simulations of thermal hydraulics, neutronics, and control systems using generic component models and features robust input checking for error detection. The two-fluid non-equilibrium model in R5 includes phasic continuity, momentum, and energy equations for each phase. These equations are structured around differential stream tubes, using time and one spatial dimension as independent variables, and are expressed in terms of time- and volume-averaged dependent variables. The solved equations, involving the conservation laws for mass, momentum and energy are provided in
mangal2012capability
.

The R5 nodalization of DYNASTY is depicted in Figure
3
. The four DYNASTY legs are modelled as PIPE components, with the heater and cooler arranged to reproduce the VHHC configuration. Except for the cooler, where forced convection occurs due to an airflow generated by the fan, all remaining pipes exchange heat with the ambient air via free convection: for this, the Churchill-Chu correlation was chosen
bergman2011fundamentals
. All the control volumes composing the loop are 100 mm long, for a total of 120 nodes (30 nodes per length).

The natural circulation is established from the left leg (PIPE-201), where, through an R5 heat structure, the fluid is heated, imposing the power level as in the related experiment
benzoni2023preliminary
. At the same time, the fan is turned on. The power level is enough to create the required buoyancy force that allows the fluid to win the gravitational and friction pressure drops. Regarding the latter, the distributed pressure drops were taken into account, considering a reasonable pipe roughness of 50
\mu
m. Instead, the concentrated pressure drop coefficients of the elbows and the T-junction in the top right corner were calculated from
idelchik1987handbook
, while the pressure drop coefficient of the Coriolis flow meter (Hendress+Hauser
®
Promass F80 DN25) was derived from its technical data sheet.

The numerical simulation reproduces the startup of natural circulation observed in the selected experiment, occurring when the fan and power are both activated. The RELAP5 model of DYNASTY, used to generate data for the SHRED algorithm, is already validated against experimental data from various transients (the relative paper is currently under review). For brevity, this work focuses exclusively on the VHHC-GV1 case; however, the proposed method is fully general and independent of the specific system configuration.

R5 nodalization of the DYNASTY experimental facility. The red zone corresponds to the heated section for the VHHC-GV1 case, whereas the blue zone corresponds to the finned cooler.

IV
Numerical Results

This work aims to investigate the application of SHRED to a real experimental facility, DYNASTY, serving both as verification and validation of the reconstruction capabilities of this novel ML architecture. In particular, both the Full Order Model (FOM) and experimental data collected on the physical facility are used as input measurements. The fluid is considered incompressible inside the loop and hence measuring the mass flow rate in a single point allows for a global estimation as well; for this reason, only the TCs will be used. Different cases and sensor positions will be analysed:

1.

the SHRED surrogate model is first verified using synthetic sensors randomly positioned within the domain to assess its accuracy compared to the background model in case of a parametric dataset (Section
IV.1
);

2.

then, the sensor positions will be constrained to the experimental configuration: the SHRED is still trained using the background knowledge of the model (using parametric snapshots), and the data collected on the facility will be used to reconstruct the state in the whole domain: therefore, the ability to locally correcting the model information with the data will be investigated (Section
IV.2
);

3.

lastly, the forecast capabilities of SHRED for time instants beyond the training dataset are investigated, considering a single transient simulated for a long interval of time. The model knowledge is used for training up to a certain time, and then real data are used to reconstruct the whole state for times outside the training range (Section
IV.3
).

For all cases, compressive training of SHRED is adopted: instead of mapping the measures to spatial points, the data are reduced with the SVD, adopting rank
r=10
, based on the decay of the singular values of the snapshot matrix
NUTHOS24_pDMD
(not reported for the sake of brevity). The training process of the SHRED models has been performed on a workstation with an Intel Core i7-9800X CPU with clock speed 3.80 GHz. The adopted version of SHRED using compressive training allows for a strong reduction of computational costs, keeping only the dominant features, and minimal hyper-parameter tuning on the architecture
kutz_shallow_2024
;
riva2024robuststateestimationpartial
. As the RELAP5 model adopts incompressible conditions for the fluid, and experimental data are available for the fluid temperature and the mass flow, the only relevant field that can be used as input data for the SHRED architecture is the fluid temperature.

IV.1
Verification of SHRED for parametric scenarios

The snapshots are generated from the R5 code, considering, for this case, the parameter pair
\boldsymbol{\mu}=[P,h]
given
P
(W) the power provided to each control volume of the R5 discretisation and
h\;\left(\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^{2}\text{ K}}\right)
the heat transfer coefficient at the cooler of the experimental facility; in addition, time
t
(s) can be considered as a pseudo-parameter for the evolution of the system in the time domain, and thus in the ROM framework it is commonly added to the parametric vector for generalization. The parametric (including time) domain
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is then defined as the Cartesian product of three intervals pertaining to each parameter

\begin{split}&t\in[0,2000]\,s\\
&P\in[25.5,45.5]\,W\\
&h\in[55.0,75.0]\,\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^{2}\text{ K}}\end{split}

(1)

For the SHRED training, the trajectories are not split along time, whereas the other parameters are divided into train (75%), test (12.5%) and validation (12.5%) sets using the random split of
sklearn
, as displayed in Figure
4
and according to standard practices in Machine Learning: therefore, the focus of this work is not on forecast but rather on parameter extrapolation and interpolation: given trajectories for certain parameters, the aim is to retrieve a general SHRED model that can interpolate the missing trajectories pertaining to parameter pairs not belonging to the training set. Among the different parametric cases, experimental data are available for the case with
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{exp}}=\left[35.5\,\text{W},\;65.0\,\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^{2}\text{ K}}\right]
for the GV1 experiment (for more details on the experimental data, see
BENZONI2023104486
).

For this work, only synthetic data are used for the training phase, that is, data coming from the R5 model: the experimental data are used for the a-posteriori testing of the SHRED model, checking if it is as accurate as the R5 model compared to the local measurements; therefore, the parameter pair corresponding to the experimental configuration is excluded from the training set. Sensor positions are then randomly selected in the whole domain: to evaluate the performance of SHRED against random noise and increase its robustness, 10 different random configurations are considered as in
riva2024robuststateestimationpartial
.

The training of each configuration takes approximately 10 minutes, with an average relative error (in
l_{2}
Euclidean norm) on the mean prediction of the SVD coefficients for the experimental configuration equal to 5.33%; for the decoding back to the full-order space, the error is
6\cdot 10^{-3}
%, meaning that the reconstruction made by SHRED is the same as the FOM itself.

Figure

shows a comparison between the SHRED reconstruction and the FOM on the whole domain at the final time of the transient, alongside the temperature evolution in the locations of the physical experimental sensors. The main takeaway is that SHRED is as accurate as the FOM, both spatially and temporally. Both the spatial distribution and the temporal evolution of the temperature field predicted by SHRED are in good agreement with the model. The aforementioned figure also shows the time evolution of the experimental measures: these show some discrepancies with the R5 model, which therefore cannot be considered as the ’best possible model’ despite providing a good description of the behaviour of transients in the DYNASTY facility (the code validation will be matter of future publication). Therefore, the next step will be the discussion of the model correction capabilities of a SHRED model trained with R5 and tested with experimental data as an additional input.

IV.2
Validation of SHRED for parametric scenarios

Figure 6:
Validation of SHRED using real data for parametric scenarios at
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{exp}}=\left[35.5\,\text{W},\;65.0\,\frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^{2}\text{ K}}\right]

This second case includes the experimental data as an additional input during the online phase, serving as a validation of the SHRED model. The snapshots are the same as Section
IV.1
, and the same random split of Figure
4
is used. The available experimental sensors are four thermocouples, thus, a single configuration is considered: the temperature from R5 from 3 TCs positions are used to train the neural network; during the test phase, experimental data from three out of four thermocouples are used as additional input of SHRED, with the unselected one used as a validation point for the estimation. In the following, TC1-TC3-TC4 will be used for training and TC2 for validation; comparable results have been obtained for the other cases. The training procedure is similar to the previous section, obtaining similar errors both at the reduced and high-dimensional levels.

The experimental data are then used in the online phase to validate the SHRED model. It is worth stressing that also in this case, the parameter pair corresponding to the experimental configuration is not included in the training, therefore it is an ’unknown’ configuration for SHRED. Figure
6
presents the contour plots of the FOM and the SHRED in the DYNASTY loop at the last time step of the FOM and the dynamics of the measures of both models and real data: the SHRED follows the evolution of the input experimental measures (TC1, TC3 and TC4) and it can be observed that the SHRED model locally corrects the background knowledge, preferring the information of the real data compared to the model; for the unselected thermocouple (TC2) the correction capabilities are less evident, even though the ramp between 10 and 15 minutes is better predicted by the SHRED compared to the FOM itself; on the other hand, the SHRED underestimates the temperature in TC2 for the last minutes, underlying that there is a local correction but for a global improvement of the estimation more sensors might be necessary.

This investigation shows that the SHRED architecture is a mature data-driven technique which can be adopted with real data: even if the background knowledge of the FOM is not perfect but still sufficiently close to reality, SHRED is able to locally correct the model and to produce accurate estimations (within the experimental uncertainty) locally better than the starting FOM, as similarly observed in
taddei_model_2016
. Extensions of SHRED to handle imperfect modelling even outside the locations where sensors are available are currently under study.

On the other hand, some discrepancy can be observed when real data are used: the local prediction of the input thermocouples shows again how SHRED can locally correct (where measures are collected) and can predict future states outside the training range; conversely, the results for the validation point of TC2 show an underestimation of temperature. This result shows that SHRED cannot entirely account for unmodelled physics

V
Conclusions

The Shallow Recurrent Decoder network is a novel architecture able to map the trajectories of 3 measurements to the full state of the system, representing an innovative way of generating reliable reduced order models for monitoring engineering systems. This work focuses on the application of SHRED to a real experimental facility, investigating and assessing its performance not only when synthetic data are used but also when real data collected on the system are used as additional input, thus assessing if SHRED can extrapolate/correct the background information provided by the model. DYNASTY, a natural circulation loop at Politecnico di Milano, born to study the dynamics of the Molten Salt Fast Reactor, has been selected as the case study, given the presence of experimental data and a model with quite fast computational times. This work then has a two-fold objective, verifying and validating the capabilities of SHRED both for parametric datasets and for temporal forecasts. The RELAP5 code is used to model the thermal hydraulics of the system considering different experimental setups, varying according to the power provided to the system or the fan speed at the cooler.

As a main result, SHRED can correctly reconstruct the state of the system even for parameter pairs not included in the training set, compared to the starting FOM. However, the hypothesis of ’perfect model’ is not always valid, as models are inherently affected by uncertainty either related to the knowledge of parameters (such as thermophysical properties) or simplifying assumptions; indeed, the RELAP5 code shows some discrepancies compared to experimental data. Therefore, the possibility of correcting the SHRED prediction obtained from the use of synthetic model data, by adding the sensor measurements as additional inputs during the online phase, has been assessed as well. The architecture has been analysed in 2 different cases, for parametric and forecast scenarios. The former shows that SHRED can accurately follow and update the background knowledge of the model with experimental measures, especially near the locations of the physical sensors. On the other hand, the latter shows good accuracy overall, even though some discrepancies are found far away from the sensors. In the end, SHRED can be considered a mature data-driven architecture for state estimation in engineering systems, due to its simple configuration, its extremely cheap training times, its capability of integrating experimental data, and the ability to produce reliable and efficient surrogate models, useful for control and monitoring.

On The Triviality Of m m -Modified Conformal Vector Fields


Abstract
We prove that a compact Riemannian manifold M M does not admit any non-trivial m m -modified homothetic vector fields. In the corresponding case of an m m -modified conformal vector field V V , we establish an inequality that implies the triviality of V V . Further, we demonstrate that an affine Killing m m -modified conformal vector field on a non-compact Riemannian manifold M M must be trivial. Finally, we show that an m m -modified gradient conformal vector field is trivial under the assumptions of polynomial volume growth and convergence to zero at infinity.

Introduction

In recent years, there has been an epistemic curiosity among researchers to study Ricci solitons, which correspond to self-similar solutions of the Ricci flow equation. A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be a Ricci soliton if it satisfies the equation

\frac{1}{2}\Lie_{V}g+Ric=\lambda g,

(1.1)

where
\Lie
denotes the Lie-derivative operator,
V
is a smooth vector field on
M
,
g
is the Riemannian metric, Ric is the Ricci tensor of
g
and
\lambda
a real constant. If
V
is the gradient of a smooth function
f
, i.e.,
V=\nabla f
, then
(M,g,f,\lambda)
is called a gradient Ricci soliton. As a generalization of Einstein metrics, gradient Ricci solitons, gradient Ricci almost solitons, Catino
[
5
]
defined a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold as an
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
satisfying the equation

Ric+Hessf-\mu df\otimes df=\lambda g,

(1.2)

where it was shown that a complete
(M,g)
satisfying (
1.2
) with harmonic Weyl tensor and zero radial Weyl curvature is locally a warped product with
(n-1)
-dimensional Einstein fiber. In particular, if

\mu=\frac{1}{m}

for a real
m
such that
0<m\leq\infty
, then (
1.2
) becomes

Ric+Hessf-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df=\lambda g,

(1.3)

where the left-hand side is called the
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor. For
\lambda
constant, (
1.3
) defines an
m
-quasi Einstein manifold (see Case, Shu and Wei
[
4
]
, He, Petersen and Wylie
[
6
]
). As a generalization of (
1.3
), Limoncu
[
7
]
and Barros and Ribeiro, Jr.
[
3
]
considered the following equation

\frac{1}{2}\Lie_{V}g+Ric-\frac{1}{m}v\otimes v=\lambda g,

(1.4)

where
v
is the
1
-form metrically associated to the potential vector field
V
and
\lambda
is a constant. We note that as
m\to\infty
, (
1.4
) reduces to the classical Ricci soliton equation defined by (
1.1
). It has been shown by Poddar et al.
[
8
]
that an
m
-quasi-Einstein manifold (
\lambda
constant in (
1.3
)) with constant scalar curvature admitting a non-parallel closed conformal vector field is Einstein. Thus, considering the metric
g
in (
1.4
) to be both Einstein and quasi-Einstein, Zhang and Chen
[
11
]
studied the following equation

\frac{1}{2}\Lie_{V}g-\frac{1}{m}v\otimes v=\sigma g,

(1.5)

where
\sigma
is a real constant. We will call the vector field
V
satisfying equation (
1.5
) with
0<m<\infty
, as an
m
-modified homothetic vector field. If the constant
\sigma
is considered as a function on
M
, then the vector field
V
is known as an
m
-modified conformal vector field, and trivial when
V=0
. Evidently, as
m\to\infty
, (
1.5
) reduces to a conformal vector field

\Lie_{V}g=2\sigma g.

(1.6)

If
\sigma
is constant in (
1.6
), then
V
is said to be a homothetic vector field and Killing if
\sigma=0
. If
V=\nabla f
in (
1.5
), for some smooth function
f
on
M
, then it is said to be an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field and equation (
1.5
) then assumes the form

Hessf-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df=\sigma g,

(1.7)

where
Hess
denotes the Hessian of
f
with respect to
g
. For more details on an
m
-modified conformal vector field, we refer the reader to the work of Zhang and Chen
[
11
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the geometry of an
m
-modified conformal vector field
V
on a compact and non-compact smooth Riemannian manifold
M
, respectively. In the latter case, we study
V
when it is affine Killing, followed by
V
when it is gradient and
M
is complete.

2
m
-Modified Conformal Vector Fields On Compact Riemannian Manifolds

2.1
Triviality Results

Zhang and Chen
[
11
]
showed that a connected, compact Riemannian manifold of dimension at least
2
admits no
m
-modified conformal vector field of constant length. It is also well-known that a homothetic vector field on a compact Riemannian manifold is Killing (see Yano
[
10
]
). Motivated by this result, we prove that a compact Riemannian manifold admits no non-trivial
m
-modified homothetic vector fields.

Theorem 2.1

Any
m
-modified (
m
finite) homothetic vector field on a compact Riemannian manifold is trivial.

Proof.
Tracing equation (
1.5
) gives

divV=\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}+n\sigma.

(2.1)

Equation (
1.5
) can also be exhibited as

g(\nabla_{X}V,Y)+g(\nabla_{Y}V,X)=\frac{2}{m}g(V,X)g(V,Y)+2\sigma g(X,Y).

(2.2)

The exterior derivative
dv
of the
1
-form
v
is given by

g(\nabla_{X}V,Y)-g(\nabla_{Y}V,X)=2(dv)(X,Y).

(2.3)

As
dv
is skew-symmetric, we define a tensor field of type
(1,1)
given by

dv(X,Y)=g(FX,Y).

(2.4)

Evidently,
F
is skew self-adjoint, i.e.,
g(FX,Y)=-g(X,FY)
. Thus, (
2.4
) assumes the form

g(\nabla_{X}V,Y)-g(\nabla_{Y}V,X)=2g(FX,Y).

(2.5)

Adding equations (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) side by side, and factoring out
Y
gives

\nabla_{X}V=\frac{1}{m}g(V,X)V+\sigma X+FX.

(2.6)

Now, we compute the covariant derivative of the squared
g
-norm of
V
using (
2.6
) as follows

\nabla_{X}|V|^{2}=2g(\nabla_{X}V,V)=\frac{2}{m}g(V,X)|V|^{2}+2\sigma g(X,V)+2g(FX,V).

(2.7)

Factoring out
X
from (
2.7
) gives

\nabla|V|^{2}=\frac{2}{m}|V|^{2}V+2\sigma V-2FV.

Taking the divergence on both sides of the preceding equation entails

\Delta|V|^{2}=\frac{2}{m}div(|V|^{2}V)+2\sigma divV-2divFV.

i.e.,

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|V|^{2}=\frac{1}{m}(V|V|^{2}+|V|^{2}divV)+\sigma divV-divFV.

Substituting
X
with
V
in (
2.7
), using it in the above equation, and further simplification leads us to

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|V|^{2}=\bigg(\frac{2}{m}|V|^{2}+divV\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}+\sigma\bigg)-2divFV.

(2.8)

Eliminating
|V|^{2}
between (
2.1
) and (
2.8
) shows that

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|V|^{2}=3(divV)^{2}+(3-5n)\sigma divV+2n(n-1)\sigma^{2}-2divFV.

(2.9)

Integrating equation (
2.9
) over compact
M
and using divergence theorem provides,
divV=0
and
\sigma=0
. The use of the foregoing equations in (
2.1
) at once gives
V=0
, i.e.,
V
is trivial. This completes the proof.

In order to set the context for our next result, we recall the following result
[
9
]
of Yano “If a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
admits a conformal vector field
V
satisfying
Ric(V,V)\leq 0
, then
V
is parallel, and if the Ricci curvature is negative definite, then the conformal vector field
V
is zero”. Motivated by this and Theorem 2.1, we examine the case when
V
is an
m
-modified conformal vector field, and establish the following triviality result.

Theorem 2.2

Any
m
-modified (
m
finite) conformal vector field
V
on a compact Riemannian manifold satisfying the inequality

Ric(V,V)\leq\frac{2(1-n)}{nm^{2}}(max|V|)^{4}

is zero, and hence trivial.

Proof.
Let us recall the following integral formula (formula 1.11, Yano
[
9
]
) for any smooth vector field
V
on a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
:

\int_{M}[Ric(V,V)+\frac{1}{2}|\Lie_{V}g|^{2}-|\nabla V|^{2}-(divV)^{2}]dv_{g}=0,

(2.10)

where
dv_{g}
is the volume element of
(M,g)
. Tracing the equation

\Lie_{V}g=2\sigma g+\frac{2}{m}v\otimes v,

(2.11)

of an
m
-modified conformal vector field
V
, we get

divV=\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}+n\sigma.

(2.12)

Next, using (
2.11
) we compute

|\Lie_{V}g|^{2}=4n\sigma^{2}+\frac{4}{m^{2}}|V|^{4}+\frac{8\sigma}{m}|V|^{2}.

(2.13)

Squaring (
2.12
) provides

\frac{2}{n}(divV)^{2}=2n\sigma^{2}+\frac{2}{nm^{2}}|V|^{4}+\frac{4\sigma}{m}|V|^{2}.

The use of this equation in (
2.13
) yields

\frac{1}{2}|\Lie_{V}g|^{2}=\frac{2}{n}(divV)^{2}+\frac{2}{m^{2}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{n}\bigg)|V|^{4}.

Substituting this value of
\frac{1}{2}|\Lie_{V}g|^{2}
in (
2.10
), we get

\int_{M}[Ric(V,V)+\frac{2}{m^{2}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{n}\bigg)|V|^{4}]dv_{g}=\int_{M}[|\nabla V|^{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{2}{n}\bigg)(divV)^{2}]dv_{g}.

This implies that

\int_{M}Ric(V,V)dv_{g}\geq\int_{M}\frac{2}{m^{2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}-1\bigg)|V|^{4}dv_{g}.

(2.14)

Noting that
|V|^{4}\leq(max|V|)^{4}
, the above inequality implies that

\int_{M}Ric(V,V)dv_{g}\geq\int_{M}\frac{2}{m^{2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}-1\bigg)(max|V|)^{4}dv_{g}.

At this point, using our hypothesis, we conclude that
max|V|=0
and so
V=0
, completing the proof.

3
m
-Modified Conformal Vector fields On Non-compact Riemannian Manifolds

3.1
Triviality Results

3.1.1
Triviality Of An Affine Killing
m
-modified Conformal Vector Field

We know that a conformal vector field
V
which is also affine Killing (i.e., satisfies
\Lie_{V}\nabla=0
, and preserves the geodesics along with their affine parameters), is homothetic. This can be easily verified by the integrability condition (Yano
[
10
]
):

(\Lie_{V}\nabla)(X,Y)=(X\sigma)Y+(Y\sigma)X-g(X,Y)\nabla\sigma,

(3.1)

for a conformal vector field
V
:
\Lie_{V}g=2\sigma g
. Using
\Lie_{V}\nabla=0
in (
3.1
) and contracting with respect to
X
and
Y
shows that
\sigma
is constant, i.e.,
V
is homothetic. This suggests the following question: “What can be said about an
m
-modified (
m
finite) conformal vector field
V
which is also affine Killing?”. It turns out that this condition places a severe restriction on
V
so much so that
V
becomes zero, i.e., trivial. More precisely, we prove

Theorem 3.1

An
m
-modified (
m
finite) conformal vector field which is also affine Killing, is trivial.

Proof.
First, tracing the
m
-modified conformal vector field equation

\Lie_{V}g=2\sigma g+\frac{2}{m}v\otimes v,

(3.2)

we get

divV=n\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}.

(3.3)

Next, we recall the following commutation formula (Yano
[
10
]
):

\displaystyle(\Lie_{V}\nabla_{X}g-\nabla_{X}\Lie_{V}g-\nabla_{[V,X]}g)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g((\Lie_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)-g((\Lie_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),Y)

\forall\>V,X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Noting that
\nabla g=0
, and
\Lie_{V}\nabla=0
by hypothesis, we see that the commutation formula implies that
\nabla_{X}\Lie_{V}g=0
, i.e.,
\Lie_{V}g
is parallel. Using (
3.2
) in it, we get

(X\sigma)g(Y,Z)+\frac{1}{m}[(\nabla_{X}v)(Y)v(Z)+v(Y)(\nabla_{X}v)Z]=0.

(3.4)

Contracting it with respect to
Y
and
Z
gives

nX\sigma+\frac{1}{m}\nabla_{X}|V|^{2}=0

and hence

n\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}=c\>(a\>constant)

(3.5)

Contracting (
3.4
) at
X
and
Z
gives

Y\sigma+\frac{1}{m}[(\nabla_{V}v)Y+(divV)v(Y)]=0.

(3.6)

Equations (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) imply that
divV=c
. Using this in (
3.6
) gives

\nabla\sigma+\frac{1}{m}[\nabla_{V}V+cV]=0.

(3.7)

Now equation (
3.2
) operated on
X
,
V
(
X
arbitrary) provides

\frac{1}{2}\nabla|V|^{2}+\nabla_{V}V=2\bigg(\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}\bigg)V.

Eliminating
\nabla_{V}V
between this equation and (
3.7
), we obtain

m(n+2)\nabla\sigma=2[2(n-1)\sigma-3c]V.

Its inner product with
V
gives

m(n+2)V\sigma=2[2(n-1)\sigma-3c]|V|^{2}.

(3.8)

Now, from equation (
3.2
) we get

2g(\nabla_{V}V)=2\sigma|V|^{2}+\frac{2}{m}|V|^{4}

i.e.,

V(|V|^{2})=2|V|^{2}\bigg(\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}\bigg)

Using (
3.5
) in the above, we find that

nV\sigma=2(n\sigma-c)[(1-n)\sigma+c]

Eliminating
V\sigma
between this and (
3.8
) we obtain

(n\sigma-c)\bigg[\frac{n+2}{n}((1-n)\sigma+c)-((1-n)\sigma+3c)\bigg]=0.

So, either
\sigma=\frac{c}{n}
, or
-\frac{c}{n-2}
. That is
\sigma
is constant, and hence from (
3.5
),
|V|
is constant. Turning our attention back to equation (
3.4
), we see that it reduces to

(\nabla_{X}v)(Y)V+v(Y)\nabla_{X}V=0.

Its inner product with
V
yields
(\nabla_{X}v)Y=0
or
V=0
. In the first case,
\nabla_{X}V=0
which implies
\Lie_{V}g=0
and hence
divV=0
and therefore
c=0
. This, combined with (
3.5
) shows

n\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}=0.

(3.9)

Also, equation (
3.2
) gives

\sigma|V|^{2}+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{4}=0

i.e.,

|V|^{2}\bigg[\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}\bigg]=0.

As
V
is parallel,
|V|
is constant. Hence, from the above equation we conclude that, either
V=0
, or
\sigma+\frac{1}{m}|V|^{2}=0
. The last case, in conjunction with (
3.9
) shows that
\sigma=0
and
V=0
. Thus,
V
is trivial, completing the proof.

3.1.2
A Structure Equation For An
m
-Modified Gradient Conformal Vector Field

For
0<m<\infty
, consider
u=e^{-\frac{f}{m}}
. Then we have,

\nabla u=-\frac{1}{m}e^{-\frac{f}{m}}\nabla f,

i.e.,

\frac{m}{u}Hessu=\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df-Hessf.

Therefore the fundamental equation of an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field, given by (
1.7
), can be written as

Hessu=\lambda g

(3.10)

where
\lambda=-\frac{1}{m}e^{-\frac{f}{m}}\sigma
is a smooth function on
M
. Thus, in the gradient case, when
m
is finite, we will use equation (
3.10
) to study (
1.7
).

Remark.
A compact Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature admitting a non-Killing gradient conformal vector field is isometric to a sphere (see Yano
[
10
]
). In view of equation (
3.10
), this result of Yano is also true for any compact Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature admitting an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field with
\sigma\neq 0
.

We now derive a structure equation for an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field with associated function
u=e^{-\frac{f}{m}}
on a Riemannian manifold
M
.

Lemma 3.1

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold admitting an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field. Then

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=|Hessu|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

(3.11)

where
u=e^{-\frac{f}{m}}
.

Proof.
Let us express equation (
3.10
) as

\nabla_{X}\nabla u=\lambda X,

for an arbitrary smooth vector field
X
.
Using the above equation and the formula
R(Y,X)Z=\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{[Y,X]}Z
, we compute
R(Y,X)\nabla u
, to get

R(Y,X)\nabla u=(Y\lambda)X-(X\lambda)Y.

(3.12)

Contracting (
3.12
) with respect to
Y
gives

Ric(X,\nabla u)=(1-n)g(\nabla\lambda,X).

Substituting
\nabla u
for
X
, in the above equation gives

Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)=(1-n)g(\nabla\lambda,\nabla u).

(3.13)

Now, we take the
g
-trace of (
3.10
) to get
\Delta u=n\lambda
, and use it in the well-known Bochner formula:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=|Hessu|^{2}+Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+g(\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u)

to get,

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=|Hessu|^{2}+Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+ng(\nabla\lambda,\nabla u)

(3.14)

Comparing equations (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=|Hessu|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u).

This completes the proof.

3.1.3
Triviality Of
m
-Modified Gradient Conformal Vector Field Via Polynomial Volume Growth

Given a polynomial function
\sigma:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, we say that
M
has polynomial volume growth like
\sigma(r)
if there exists
p\in M
such that

Vol(B(p,r))=\mathcal{O}(\sigma(r)),

as
r\to\infty
, where
Vol
denotes the volume related to the metric
g
and
B(p,r)
is the geodesic ball centered at
p
with radius
r
. Our next result pertains to the triviality of an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field whenever
M
has polynomial volume growth under certain Ricci curvature condition. More precisely, we establish the following

Theorem 3.2

Let
(M,g)
be a connected, oriented, complete, non-compact Riemannian manifold admitting an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field and whose Ricci curvature satisfies
Ric\leq-\alpha g
, for some positive constant
\alpha
. If
M
has polynomial volume growth and
|\nabla u|
,
|\nabla\nabla u|\in L^{\infty}(M)
, where
u=e^{-\frac{f}{m}}
, then
f
is constant (trivial).

Proof.
We prove it by contradiction. Suppose
f
is non-constant on
M
. Thus,
u
is non-constant on
M
. Let us consider the function
w=|\nabla u|^{2}
and a smooth vector field
X=\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}
. In view of (
3.11
) and our hypothesis, we have

divX=\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=2\bigg\{|Hessu|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\bigg\}\geq\frac{2\alpha}{n-1}w.

Also,

g(\nabla w,X)=|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}\geq 0.

Now, from Kato’s inequality, we get

|X|=2|\nabla u||\nabla|\nabla u||\leq 2|\nabla u||\nabla\nabla u|<\infty,

since
|\nabla u|
,
|\nabla\nabla u|\in L^{\infty}(M)
. As
M
has polynomial volume growth, we invoke the following result of Alías et al.
[
2
]
, “Let
M
be a connected, oriented, complete, non-compact Riemannian manifold and
X
be a bounded smooth vector field on
M
. Let
w
be a non-negative smooth function on
M
such that
g(\nabla w,X)\geq 0
and
divX\geq\alpha^{\prime}w
on
M
, for some positive constant
\alpha^{\prime}
. If
M
has polynomial volume growth, then
w
vanishes identically on
M
”, to conclude that
|\nabla u|^{2}
vanishes identically on
M
, thereby arriving at a contradiction. Therefore,
u
is constant and consequently
f
is constant. This completes the proof.

3.1.4
Triviality Of
m
-Modified Gradient Conformal Vector Field Via Convergence to Zero At Infinity

A continuous function
u\in C^{0}(M)
is said to converge to zero at infinity if it satisfies the condition

\lim_{d(x,x_{0})\to\infty}u(x)=0,

where
d(.,x_{0}):M\to[0,\infty)
denotes the Riemannian distance of a complete, non-compact Riemannian manifold
M
measured from a fixed point
x_{0}\in M
. In the following theorem, we prove the triviality of an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field by way of convergence to zero at infinity of the function
|\nabla u|
, for
u=e^{-\frac{f}{m}}
, under certain Ricci curvature condition.

Theorem 3.3

Let
(M,g)
be a complete, non-compact, Riemannian manifold admitting an
m
-modified gradient conformal vector field and
Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\leq 0
and
|\nabla u|
converges to zero at infinity, where
u=e^{-\frac{f}{m}}
. Then,
f
is constant (trivial).

Proof.
We prove this by contradiction. Suppose
f
is not a constant on
M
. Then,
u
is a non-constant function on
M
. Consider the function
w=|\nabla u|^{2}
, which is non-negative, non-identically vanishing, and converges to zero at infinity. Now, consider the smooth vector field
X=\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}
on
M
. Thus, we have

g(\nabla w,X)=|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}\geq 0.

From equation (
3.11
) and our hypothesis that
Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\leq 0
, we observe that the right-hand side of (
3.11
) is non-negative.
Thus,

divX=\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=2\bigg\{|Hessu|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\bigg\}\geq 0.

Hence, invoking the result of Alías et al.
[
1
]
, “Let
(M,g)
be a complete, non-compact Riemannian manifold and
X
be an arbitrary smooth vector field on
M
. Assume that there exists a non-negative, non-identically vanishing function
w\in C^{\infty}(M)
which converges to zero at infinity and
g(\nabla w,X)\geq 0
. If
divX\geq 0
on
M
, then
g(\nabla w,X)\equiv 0
on
M
”, we get,
w=|\nabla u|^{2}
is a constant. Since it converges to zero at infinity,
w\equiv 0
on
M
, and hence
f
is constant on
M
, a contradiction to our initial assumption regarding
f
. Therefore,
f
is constant. This completes the proof.

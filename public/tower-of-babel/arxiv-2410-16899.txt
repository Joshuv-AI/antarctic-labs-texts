On the image of higher signature maps
Samuel Lerbet

Abstract
Given a smooth variety X X over the field ℝ \mathbb{R} of real numbers and a line bundle ℒ \mathcal{L} on X X with associated topological line bundle L = ℒ ⁡ ( ℝ ) L=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}) , we study the quadratic real cycle class map γ ~ ℝ c : CH ~ c ​ ( X , ℒ ) → H c ​ ( X ⁡ ( ℝ ) , ℤ ⁡ ( L ) ) \widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}:\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L)) from the c c -th Chow-Witt group of X X to the c c -th cohomology group of its real locus X ⁡ ( ℝ ) X(\mathbb{R}) with coefficients in the local system ℤ ⁡ ( L ) \mathbb{Z}(L) associated with L L . We focus on the cases c ∈ { 0 , d − 2 , d − 1 , d } c\in\{0,d-2,d-1,d\} where d d is the dimension of X X and we formulate a precise conjecture on the image of γ ~ ℝ \widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}} in terms of the exponents of its cokernel that is corroborated by the results obtained in those codimensions.

1.  Introduction

The complex cycle class map.

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety (by convention, an algebraic variety over a field
k
is a separated
k
-scheme of finite type). The
complex cycle class map
in codimension
c
is a group homomorphism

\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})

from the Chow group of codimension
c
cycles on
X
to the integral singular cohomology of
X(\mathbb{C})
in degree
2c
. The Hodge conjecture predicts the image of
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X)
when
X
is projective. The map
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X)
is defined as follows. Let
i_{Y}:Y\hookrightarrow X
be a subvariety of
X
of codimension
c
. Choose a resolution of singularities
p:\widetilde{Y}\rightarrow Y
of
Y
. The map
i_{Y}\circ p(\mathbb{C}):\widetilde{Y}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow X(\mathbb{C})
induced on complex loci is a proper map of complex analytic manifolds which are naturally oriented as smooth manifolds. Therefore it induces a pushforward homomorphism
i_{Y}\circ p(\mathbb{C})_{*}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(\widetilde{Y}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*+2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
in integral singular cohomology and we set
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X)[Y]=i_{Y}\circ p(\mathbb{C})_{*}(1)
where
1\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\widetilde{Y}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}
. If
Y
is smooth, then this is exactly the class of the submanifold
Y(\mathbb{C})
of
X(\mathbb{C})
in the cohomology of
X(\mathbb{C})
hence the name of the map
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Assume that
X
is projective. Then the elements of the image of
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X)
are not arbitrary. Indeed, complex analysis on the compact analytic manifold
X(\mathbb{C})
may be used to identify a subgroup
\Hdg^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
of
\mathrm{H}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
of
(c,c)
-Hodge classes
through which
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X)
factors. Then the variety
X
is said to satisfy the integral Hodge conjecture in codimension
c
if
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\rightarrow\Hdg^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
is surjective. It is possible for this property to fail to be satisfied: the first examples date back to Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH62
]
who constructed classes that are torsion, and are thus automatically
(c,c)
-Hodge classes, but that do not lie in the image of the complex cycle class map. Nevertheless, in view of the considerable impact of the Hodge conjecture on the development of complex algebraic geometry over the last eighty years, the
rational
Hodge conjecture being still open, it seems natural to define and study counterparts of this conjecture in other areas of algebraic geometry.

The Borel–Haefliger cycle class map.

In this paper, we are interested in
real
algebraic geometry. In this context, it is well-known that one cannot directly copy the construction described previously to define a cycle class map with values in the
integral
cohomology of the real locus
X(\mathbb{R})
of a smooth variety
X
over
\mathbb{R}
. This is because the smooth manifold
X(\mathbb{R})
does not necessarily admit a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued orientation; nor do the real loci of resolutions of singularities of the subvarieties of
X
, and resolutions of singularities need not be compatible with orientations if they exist. Thus we do not have a proper pushforward map in
integral
cohomology of the real loci in this setting. However, since every smooth manifold has exactly one
\mathbb{Z}/2
-valued orientation, one can define as above a
mod
2
cycle class map called the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map

\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)

that was first constructed in
[
BH61
]
. The image of
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X)
, denoted by
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
, has been abundantly studied, for it is closely related to classical problems in real algebraic geometry. As an example of such a problem, we can cite the approximation of submanifolds of
X(\mathbb{R})
by real loci of subvarieties of
X
. Nevertheless, the information carried by mod
2
cohomology is too coarse for one to be satisfied by the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}
as an analogue of the complex cycle class map
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}
. Any remedy to this observation should however be compatible with
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}
in a suitable sense in view of its significance. To the author’s knowledge, there are essentially two analogues of the complex cycle class map in real algebraic geometry, relying on entirely different ideas.

The equivariant cycle class map
\gamma_{G}
.

This map was first constructed by Krasnov in
[
Kra92
]
; this construction is described in detail in
[
BW20
]
. The idea here is to replace
X(\mathbb{R})
with
X(\mathbb{C})
together with the antiholomorphic involution
\sigma:X(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow X(\mathbb{C})
induced by complex conjugation, which recovers
X(\mathbb{R})
as the set of fixed points of
\sigma
. One views
\sigma
as an action of the Galois group
G
of the extension
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}
; techniques from equivariant homotopy theory then become available. More precisely, there is an equivariant cycle class map

\gamma_{G}^{c}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{G}}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))

for every smooth projective real algebraic variety
X
, where
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{G}}^{*}
denotes Borel equivariant cohomology and
\mathbb{Z}(c)
is the
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}(c)=\sqrt{-1}^{c}\mathbb{Z}
with the action of
G
induced by complex conjugation. This map refines the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X)
in the sense that there exists a homomorphism
\pi:\mathrm{H}_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
fitting in a commutative triangle of the form
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\gamma_{G}^{c}(X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

(this follows from the case
i=0
of
[
BW20
, Theorem 1.18]
). As in the complex case, the elements of
\operatorname{Im}\gamma_{G}^{c}(X)
are not arbitrary: they satisfy Hodge-theoretic and topological constraints that together determine a subgroup
\Hdg_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))_{0}
of
\mathrm{H}_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))
. Benoist and Wittenberg then say that the real integral Hodge conjecture for codimension
c
cycles on
X
holds if the homomorphism
\gamma_{G}^{c}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\rightarrow\Hdg_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))_{0}
is surjective
[
BW20
, Definition 2.2]
.

As in the complex case, not every smooth projective real algebraic variety satisfies the real integral Hodge conjecture of
[
BW20
]
. However, it is very useful as a framework to study problems in real algebraic geometry as it is intimately related to the size of the group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
. Benoist and Wittenberg provide the first known examples of smooth projective varieties
X
over
\mathbb{R}
such that the restriction of the homomorphism
\pi:\mathrm{H}_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
to the subgroup
\Hdg_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))_{0}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{G}^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(c))
is surjective, yet the inclusion
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)\varsubsetneq\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
of the subgroup of algebraic mod
2
classes is strict. For such a variety
X
, the existence of non-algebraic classes in
\mathrm{H}^{*}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is not explained by Hodge theory or by topology: it must come from the failure of
X
to satisfy the real integral Hodge conjecture.

In this approach, the remedy to the insufficiency of
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X)
is to upgrade the
target
to a more refined invariant of
X
, namely the equivariant cohomology of
(X(\mathbb{C}),\sigma)
: the
source
remains the Chow groups of
X
. Motivic homotopy theory provides us with a set of algebraic tools that lead to a different proposition, where the Chow groups are lifted to Chow
–Witt
groups: this is the main object of investigation of this paper.

The quadratic real cycle class map.

Given a smooth variety
X
over
\mathbb{R}
, this is a homomorphism

\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}(X):\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{*}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})

of graded rings: details can be found in
[
Hor+21
]
but we recall the construction below. Here the source
\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{*}(X)
is the
Chow–Witt ring
of
X

[
Fas07
]
, which refines the Chow ring of
X
in the sense that there is a graded ring homomorphism
r:\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{*}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
. The quadratic real cycle class map
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X)
lifts the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X)
in the sense that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{c}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{c}(X)}

induced by the comparison map
r
and reduction mod
2
of the coefficients is commutative.

The most naive conjecture one can make about the quadratic real cycle class map
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X)
is then to predict that this map is surjective. This is too optimistic: there are already obstructions in the case where
c=0
and
\dim(X)=1
, namely the case of connected components of curves. One of the goals of this paper is to propose a refined statement that is more likely to be true. We eventually arrive at the following conjecture:

Conjecture
.

For every smooth
\mathbb{R}
-variety
X
of dimension
d
and every
0\leqslant c\leqslant d
, the image of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X)
contains
2^{d-c}\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, the number
d-c
in this exponent cannot be improved in general.

We refer to Conjecture
6.5
below for a more precise formulation.

Contents.

The organisation of this paper is as follows. The first section is preliminary: we explain the objects of interest of the article and some of their basic properties, included a twisted version

\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L}):\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))

of the constructions previously discussed; here the twist is a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
. In the second section, we study the map
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{d}(X,\mathcal{L})
where
d
is the dimension of
X
. The main observation here is that the analysis of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{d}(X,\mathcal{L})
can be reduced for a number of questions to that of the mod
2
cycle class maps studied in
[
CS96
]
thanks to Lemma
3.2
below. In the third section, we draw some immediate corollaries concerning the map
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
and we show conditional results on the map
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{d-2}(X,\mathcal{L})
. The fourth section similarly contains results on the case of connected components, namely the map
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
. In the final section, we first observe that studying the image of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
is only relevant integrally: we prove a quantitative version of a result of Jacobson that implies that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{R}}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
is surjective for every
c
and every
(X,\mathcal{L})
after inverting
2
(see in particular the more precise Corollary
6.3
). We then come to the aforementioned refined conjecture, taking twists into account, and we observe that the results of the previous section allow us to conclude that it holds true for curves and for surfaces, and in a number of significant cases for threefolds. Hornbostel independently studied similar questions to those investigated in this article in
[
Hor24
]
, especially quadratic real cycle class maps for surfaces, and there is some overlap with the present results (and proofs) which we signal below.

2.  Preliminaries

2.1
General considerations

Given a set
S
, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}[S]
the free abelian group generated by
S
; it is naturally a ring if
S
is a group. We do not make any distinction between abelian groups
A
with additive action of the group
G
(namely, action by group automorphisms) and
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-modules
A
. For example, if
G
acts on a set
S
on the right, then
\mathbb{Z}[S]
has a natural structure of right
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-module inherited from this action. This extends to sheaves in an obvious way.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a site and let
x
be an object of
\mathsf{C}
. We say that
x
is
of cohomological dimension
\leqslant d
if
\mathrm{H}^{i}(x,\mathbf{A})=0
for every
i>d
and every abelian sheaf
\mathbf{A}
on
\mathsf{C}
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
X
be a scheme. Then
X
is
of Zariski cohomological dimension
\leqslant d
if
\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathscr{F})=0
for every
i>d
and every sheaf
\mathscr{F}
of abelian groups on the topological space
X
. If the underlying topological space of
X
is Noetherian, then by
[
Gro57
, Théorème 3.6.5]
, the scheme
X
is of Zariski cohomological dimension
\leqslant\dim(X)
where
\dim(X)
is the dimension of
X
as a scheme.

Let
(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
be a locally ringed space. We use the terminologies of line bundles on
X
and locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules of rank
1
interchangeably. These objects form a groupoid
\underline{\Pic}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
(we underline
\Pic
to distinguish this category and its set of isomorphism classes of objects which, under the operation induced by the tensor product of modules, is the Picard group of
X
), denoted by
\underline{\Pic}(X)
when no confusion on
\mathscr{O}_{X}
can arise. We denote the residue field of
X
at a point
x
by
\kappa(x)
and the maximal ideal of the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
by
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
. Suppose now that
X
is a scheme. The set of points
x
of
X
of codimension
p
(that is, such that the irreducible closed subset
\overline{\{x\}}
of
X
is of codimension
p
) is denoted by
X^{(p)}
. Given a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on the scheme
X
and a point
x\in X
, we set
\mathcal{L}(x)=\mathcal{L}\otimes\kappa(x)
. For every
x\in X
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is finitely generated, we denote by
\Lambda_{x}
the determinant of
(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}=\Hom_{\kappa(x)}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2},\kappa(x))
; thus
\Lambda_{x}
is a
\kappa(x)
-vector space of dimension
1
. If
X
is regular at
x
, then
\Lambda_{x}=\Lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}
where
p
is the codimension of
x
.

Throughout we fix a field
k
that is perfect, infinite of characteristic not
2
. Eventually we will take
k
to be an extension of the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers so the reader may safely assume that
k
is of characteristic
0
in this preliminary section.

Twist by a line bundle.

Let
(\mathsf{C},\mathscr{O})
be an essentially small ringed site, that is, an essentially small site
\mathsf{C}
together with a sheaf
\mathscr{O}
of rings. The reader may wish to think of
\mathsf{C}
as a full subcategory of the category of
X
-schemes for some scheme
X
, endowed with a subcanonical topology, and of
\mathscr{O}
as the sheaf
\Hom_{X}(\text{--},\mathbb{A}_{X}^{1})
, or of
\mathsf{C}
as the site of open subsets of a ringed space
(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
and of
\mathscr{O}
as the sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{X}
. An
\mathscr{O}
-module
\mathcal{L}
is then locally free of rank
1
if every
c\in\mathsf{C}
has a covering sieve
R
such that for every
c^{\prime}\rightarrow c
in
R
, there exists an isomorphism
\mathcal{L}_{|c^{\prime}}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{|c^{\prime}}
of modules on
\mathsf{C}/c^{\prime}
with the induced ringed site structure.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a locally free
\mathscr{O}
-module of rank
1
. A section
s
of
\mathcal{L}
over
c\in\mathsf{C}
is
invertible
if the morphism
\mathscr{O}_{|c}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{|c}
of modules over
\mathsf{C}/c
carrying
a\in\mathscr{O}(c^{\prime})
to
as_{|c^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism. We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
the subpresheaf of invertible sections. Since being invertible is a local property for a given section of
\mathcal{L}
, the presheaf
\mathcal{L}^{0}
is a sheaf. The sheaf
\mathscr{O}^{\times}:u\mapsto\mathscr{O}(u)^{\times}
of units of
\mathscr{O}
acts on
\mathcal{L}^{0}
by left multiplication and
\mathcal{L}^{0}
is an
\mathscr{O}^{\times}
-torsor for this action, that is, the
\mathscr{O}^{\times}
-sheaf
\mathcal{L}^{0}
is locally isomorphic to
\mathscr{O}^{\times}
acting on itself by left multiplication. Now let
\mathscr{A}
be a right
\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}^{\times}]
-module. We define
\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{L})
as the abelian sheaf on
\mathsf{C}
associated with the presheaf
c\mapsto\mathscr{A}(c)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}^{\times}(c)]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{L}^{0}(c)]
: we call
\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{L})
the twist of
\mathscr{A}
by
\mathcal{L}
. For fixed
\mathcal{L}
, the construction
\mathscr{A}\mapsto\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{L})
is an exact functor from the category of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}^{\times}]
-modules to the category of abelian sheaves on
\mathsf{C}
. This is because exactness can be checked locally for sheaves and any local isomorphism
\mathcal{L}_{|c}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{|c}
induces an isomorphism
\mathscr{A}_{|c}\simeq\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{L})_{|c}
for every right
\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}^{\times}]
-module
\mathscr{A}
. Moreover, since the construction
\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{L})=\mathscr{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}^{\times}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{L}^{0}]
is given by a tensor product, the functor
\mathscr{A}\mapsto\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{L})
commutes with colimits.

Example 2.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space. Denote by
\mathscr{C}_{X}
the sheaf on
X
assigning to each open subset
U
of
X
the ring of continuous maps from
U
to
\mathbb{R}
. The pair
(X,\mathscr{C}_{X})
is then a locally ringed space. Let
\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\times}
denote the sheaf of units of
\mathscr{C}_{X}
. Given a section
f
of
\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\times}
, we denote by
\sign(f)
the locally constant function to
\{\pm 1\}
carrying
x
to
+1
if
f(x)>0
and to
-1
if
f(x)<0
: this is the
sign
of
f
. The assignment
f\mapsto\sign(f)
determines a morphism of sheaves of groups from
\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\times}
to the sheaf of locally constant functions on
X
to
\{\pm 1\}
, which we still denote by
\{\pm 1\}
. This last sheaf acts additively on every abelian sheaf on
X
by multiplication so every abelian sheaf on
X
can be regarded as a
\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\times}]
-module. Therefore for every
L\in\underline{\Pic}(X,\mathscr{C}_{X})
and every abelian sheaf
\mathscr{A}
on
X
, the twist
\mathscr{A}(L)
of
\mathscr{A}
by
L
is defined. For example, if
2\mathscr{A}(U)=0
for every open subset
U
of
X
, then the action of
\{\pm 1\}
on
\mathscr{A}
is trivial, hence so is the action of
\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\times}
and the sheaf
\mathscr{A}(L)
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathscr{A}
. We will be particularly interested in the case where
\mathscr{A}
is the constant sheaf
\mathbb{Z}/2^{N}\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
N
. For every abelian group
A
inducing a constant sheaf on
X
, which we still denote by
A
, the sheaf
A(L)
is locally constant with stalk
A
as the twist
\mathscr{A}(L)
is isomorphic to
\mathscr{A}
when
L
is trivial.

The exponents of an abelian group.

We recall the definition of this notion:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and let
e\geqslant 0
be an integer. We write
eA
for the image of the endomorphism
x\mapsto ex
of
A
. We say that
A

has exponent
e
if
eA=0
. More generally, a presheaf
\mathbf{F}
of abelian groups on a category
\mathsf{C}
has exponent
e
if
\mathbf{F}(x)
has exponent
e
for every object
x
of
\mathsf{C}
.

Exponents will often be interpreted as bounding below the size of a subgroup in the following way.

Example 2.5
.

If
A^{\prime}
is a subgroup of an abelian group
A
, then
A/A^{\prime}
has exponent
e
if, and only if, we have an inclusion
eA\subseteq A^{\prime}
of subgroups of
A
.

Remark 2.6
.

Although local epimorphisms are not sectionwise surjective, if
\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
is a local epimorphism of abelian presheaves on a site
\mathsf{C}
where
\mathbf{A}
has exponent
e
and
\mathbf{B}
is a sheaf (for example, if
\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
is an epimorphism of sheaves or a sheafification morphism), then
\mathbf{B}
also has exponent
e
. This is because for any
\sigma\in\mathbf{B}(c)
where
c\in\mathsf{C}
, the equality
e\sigma=0

locally
holds as
\sigma
locally comes from a section of
\mathbf{A}
: since
\mathbf{B}
is a sheaf by assumption, this guarantees that
e\sigma=0
in
\mathbf{B}(c)
. For example, for every abelian sheaf
\mathbf{A}
on
\mathsf{C}
, the sheaf
\mathbf{A}/e
has exponent
e
since there is a local epimorphism from the presheaf
c\mapsto\mathbf{A}(c)/e
to
\mathbf{A}/e
. We also note that if
\mathbf{A}
is an abelian sheaf on

Generalities.

Let

Definition 2.14
.

A

-graded strictly

.

Given a homotopy module

.

The category of homotopy modules is abelian and its embedding in the category of graded abelian sheaves is exact.

the

-stable

Rost–Schmid complexes.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be a strictly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaf and let
U\in\mathrm{Sm}_{k}^{\prime}
. Then the
Rost–Schmid complex

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(U;\mathbf{M})
is a complex of abelian groups constructed in
[
Mor12
, Chapter 5]
. Its components are of the form

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(U;\mathbf{M})^{p}=\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(p)}}\mathrm{M}_{-p}(\kappa(x),\Lambda_{x})

where we recall that
\Lambda_{x}
is the determinant of
(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}
, and its differentials are defined using residue homomorphisms and transfers. This construction is contravariant in étale morphisms. In particular, for every essentially smooth
k
-scheme
X
, this yields presheaves
U\mapsto\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(U;\mathbf{M})^{p}
on
\mathrm{Nis}(X)
and on
\mathrm{Zar}(X)
that are in fact sheaves for the Nisnevich and Zariski topology respectively. Localisation at the codimension
0
points yields a map
\mathbf{M}(U)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{M}(\kappa(x))
for every
U\in\mathrm{Nis}(X)
(the
\kappa(x)
-vector space
\Lambda_{x}
is
canonically
isomorphic to
\kappa(x)
if
x
has codimension
0
). The resulting complex
0\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{|Nis(X)}\rightarrow\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(\text{--};\mathbf{M})^{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(\text{--};\mathbf{M})^{d}\rightarrow 0
is then a resolution of
\mathbf{M}_{|\mathrm{Nis}(X)}
, and restricts to a resolution of
\mathbf{M}_{|\mathrm{Zar}(X)}
, by flasque sheaves, see
[
Mor12
, Theorem 5.41, Lemma 5.42]
. Consequently, for every
i
, the Nisnevich and Zariski cohomology groups of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbf{M}
in degree
i
are both given by the cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(X;\mathbf{M}))
of the complex
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(X;\mathbf{M})
(
[
Mor12
, Corollary 5.43]
). We denote these coinciding Nisnevich and Zariski cohomology groups by
\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{M})
.

Now assume that
\mathbf{M}\cong\mathbf{N}_{-1}
is the contraction of a strictly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaf
\mathbf{N}
. Then as noted in Example
2.17
, the sheaf
\mathbf{M}
has a natural
\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}_{k}^{\times}]
-module structure: consequently, every component and differential of the Rost–Schmid complex of
\mathbf{M}
on an essentially smooth
k
-scheme
X
, including the cohomological degree
0
, can be twisted by any line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
, see
[
Mor12
, Remark 5.13, 5.14]
. This yields a complex
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(Y,f^{*}\mathcal{L};\mathbf{M})
for every object
f:Y\rightarrow X
of
\mathrm{Nis}(X)
, usually more simply denoted by
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(Y,\mathcal{L};\mathbf{M})
, and thus a complex
0\rightarrow\mathbf{M}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(\text{--},\mathcal{L};\mathbf{M})^{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(\text{--},\mathcal{L};\mathbf{M})^{d}\rightarrow 0
of sheaves on
\mathrm{Nis}(X)
and on
\mathrm{Zar}(X)
. As in the untwisted case, this complex is in fact a flasque resolution of the restriction of
\mathbf{M}(\mathcal{L})
to
\mathrm{Nis}(X)
(resp. to
\mathrm{Zar}(X)
). Consequently, as before, the Nisnevich and Zariski cohomology groups of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbf{M}(\mathcal{L})
coincide and are denoted by
\mathrm{H}^{*}(X,\mathbf{M}(\mathcal{L}))
, and they can be computed using the Rost–Schmid complex: the group
\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{M}(\mathcal{L}))
coincides with
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,\mathcal{L};\mathbf{M}))
for every
i
. This twisted case follows from Morel’s results.

The Rost–Schmid complex allows us to deduce many properties of strictly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaves from corresponding properties of their values on

. Here is a very easy incarnation of this principle.

Example 2.18
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be an abelian sheaf on
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
. If
\mathrm{M}(F)=0
for every finitely generated field extension

, then

since

embeds into

for every

(see also

, a morphism

of strictly

of rank

of sheaves on

Definition 2.21
.

The

of an essentially smooth

is

Chow–Witt groups refine Chow groups in the following sense. The Rost–Schmid complex
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{M}})
exhibits
\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{M}})
as the cokernel

\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(c-1)}}\kappa(x)^{\times}\xrightarrow{\partial_{\mathrm{RS}}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(c)}}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{M}})\rightarrow 0

since for every field
F
, we have isomorphisms
\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)\cong F^{\times}
and
\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)\cong\mathbb{Z}
and the group
\mathrm{K}_{-1}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)
vanishes. It turns out that the relevant differential
\partial_{\mathrm{RS}}
of the Rost–Schmid complex is the divisor map of
[

, Section 1.3]
hence
\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{M}})=\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)
. Thus there is a comparison map

induced by the morphism
\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{MW}}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{M}}
of sheaves. Tensoring the above exact sequence with
\mathbb{Z}/2
also yields an exact sequence

\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(c-1)}}\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{M}}(\kappa(x))/2\xrightarrow{\partial_{\mathrm{RS}}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(c)}}\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{M}}(\kappa(x))/2\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)/2\rightarrow 0

since
(\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}/2)_{-m}\cong\mathbf{K}_{n-m}^{\mathrm{M}}/2
by Proposition

, so that
\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)/2=\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{K}_{c}^{\mathrm{M}}/2)
. Moreover, the Nisnevich cohomological dimension of

is bounded above by its Krull dimension as noted in Remark

and thus

vanishes if

, like the usual Chow groups.

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
m\geqslant n
be integers and let
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
. Then the group
\mathrm{H}^{m+1}(X,2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})
vanishes.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.16
, the isomorphism
(\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})_{-p}\cong\mathbf{K}_{n-p}^{\mathrm{M}}
of sheaves induces an isomorphism
(2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})_{-p}\cong 2\mathbf{K}_{n-p}^{\mathrm{M}}
for every
p\geqslant 0
. Thus
(2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})_{-(m+1)}\cong 2\mathbf{K}_{n-m-1}^{\mathrm{M}}
: since Milnor
\mathrm{K}
-theory vanishes in negative degrees and as
n-m-1<0
, it follows that
(2\mathrm{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})_{-(m+1)}(F)\cong 2\mathrm{K}_{n-m-1}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)=0
for every finitely generated extension
F
of
k
. Now the Rost–Schmid complex of
2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}
exhibits
\mathrm{H}^{m+1}(X,2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})
as a subquotient of
\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(m+1)}}(2\mathrm{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})_{-(m+1)}(\kappa(x))
which vanishes.
A fortiori
, the group
\mathrm{H}^{m+1}(X,2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})
vanishes as required.
∎

The following lemma will allow us to substitute the cohomology of
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
for the Chow–Witt groups in many questions of images for quadratic real cycle class maps.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
, let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
and let
m\geqslant n
be integers. Then the map
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
of sheaves of Diagram
(
2.2
)
induces an epimorphism
\mathrm{H}^{m}(X,\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{m}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))
of abelian groups.

This lemma applies in particular to the map
\mathrm{H}^{n}(X,\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}(\mathcal{L}))=\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{n}(X,\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{n}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))
.

Proof.

By the affirmation of the Milnor conjecture on quadratic forms in
[
OVV07
]
, the morphism
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{n}
of sheaves given on symbols by
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\mapsto\llangle a_{1}\rrangle\otimes\cdots\otimes\llangle a_{n}\rrangle
factors into an isomorphism
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}/2\cong\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{n}
so that we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow 2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{n}\rightarrow 0

of abelian sheaves on
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
. By the fibre product square (
2.2
) of sheaves on
X
(and commutation of pullbacks with kernels which are particular cases of limits), we get an exact sequence

0\rightarrow 2\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow 0

of sheaves on
X
. It induces an exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{m}(X,\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{m}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{m+1}(X,{2\mathbf{K}}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}})

of abelian groups. The last group in this exact sequence vanishes by Lemma
2.22
hence the homomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{m}(X,\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{m}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))
is surjective as required.
∎

2.4
Comparing sheafification and unramified sheaves

In this subsection, we compare sheafification of global constructions and unramified sheaves in two contexts: mod
2
étale cohomology and Witt groups. The results of this subsection (which are well-known to experts) guarantee that we can use constructions and results that rely on either seamlessly.

Unramified étale cohomology.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic different from
2
. For every
n\geqslant 0
, we denote by
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
the
n
-th étale cohomology group of
F
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/2
. The graded abelian group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{*}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
underlies a graded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-algebra whose multiplication is the cup-product
\cup
. Given
a\in F^{\times}
, we denote by
(a)\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{1}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
the associated symbol in Galois cohomology. The assignment
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\mapsto(a_{1})\cup\cdots\cup(a_{n})
extends to a well-defined homomorphism
\mathrm{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)/2\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
of groups and these assemble into a morphism
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)/2\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{*}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
of graded rings. By the affirmation of the Milnor conjecture in
[
Voe03
]
, the morphism
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)/2\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{*}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is an isomorphism of graded rings. Moreover, as was already used in the proof of Lemma
2.23
, the morphism
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)/2\rightarrow\overline{\mathrm{I}}^{*}(F)
given on symbols by
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\mapsto\llangle a_{1}\rrangle\otimes\cdots\otimes\llangle a_{n}\rrangle
is an isomorphism of graded rings by
[
OVV07
]
. In particular, we have an isomorphism
\overline{\mathrm{I}}^{*}(F)\cong\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{*}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
modulo which for every unit
a
in
F^{\times}
, the element
\llangle a\rrangle=\langle 1,-a\rangle
of
\overline{\mathrm{I}}^{1}(F)
corresponds to
(a)\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{1}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

The groups
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(\text{--},\mathbb{Z}/2)
underly an unramified
\mathsf{F}_{k}
-datum in the sense of
[
Mor12
, Definition 2.9]
and thus induce a sheaf
\mathbf{H}^{n}
on
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
. These sheaves arrange into a homotopy module
\mathbf{H}^{*}
by the results of
[
Mor12
, Section 2.3]
. By
e.g.

[
EKM08
, Propositions 101.8, 101.9]
, the isomorphisms
\overline{\mathrm{I}}^{*}(F)\cong\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{M}}(F)/2\cong\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{*}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)
provided by the affirmation of the Milnor conjecture are compatible with residue homomorphisms: consequently, they determine isomorphisms
\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{*}\cong\mathbf{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{M}}/2\cong\mathbf{H}^{*}
of homotopy modules. On the other hand, given a smooth
k
-variety
X
, one can consider the sheaf
\mathscr{H}^{n}
on
X
associated with the presheaf
U\mapsto\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(U,\mathbb{Z}/2)
. For every open subscheme
U
of
X
, there is a localisation morphism
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(U,\mathbb{Z}/2)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)
that factors through the subgroup
\mathbf{H}^{n}(U)
. This construction determines a morphism
\mathscr{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{n}
of sheaves on
X
.

Theorem 2.24
.

Let
X
be a smooth
k
-variety. Then the map
\mathscr{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{n}
of sheaves on
X
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
Y
be an essentially smooth
k
-scheme. In
[
BO74
]
, the authors construct a cohomological coniveau spectral sequence with
\mathrm{E}_{1}
-page of the form
\mathrm{E}_{1}^{p,q}(Y)=\bigoplus_{x\in Y^{(p)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{q-p}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)
, converging to
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{p+q}(Y,\mathbb{Z}/2)
. By their main result
[
BO74
, (4.2) Theorem]
in the local case, extended to semi-local schemes in
[
CHK97
, Proposition 2.1.2]
, the line
q=n
of the
\mathrm{E}_{1}
-page is an acyclic complex if
Y
is the semi-localisation of a smooth
k
-variety at a finite set. Therefore the sheaves
U\mapsto\mathrm{E}_{1}^{p,n}(U)
on
X
, together with the differentials
d_{1}^{p,n}
of the
\mathrm{E}_{1}
-page, induce a resolution of
\mathscr{H}^{n}
by acyclic sheaves. This computes the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page as
\mathrm{E}_{2}^{p,q}(X)=\mathrm{H}^{p}(X,\mathscr{H}^{q})
and, taking

, shows that the sequence of abelian groups

0\rightarrow\mathscr{H}^{n}(U)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{0,n}}\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(1)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n-1}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)

is exact for every open subset
U
of
X
. By
[
CO89
, Proposition 1.3]
, the differential
d_{1}^{0,n}
is given by the usual residue homomorphism
\partial
in Galois cohomology defined in
e.g.

[
EKM08
, Subsection 99.5]
. But by the very definition of the homotopy module
\mathbf{H}^{*}
, the group
\mathbf{H}^{n}(U)
consists of the unramified classes in
\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)
, namely it is the kernel of the morphism
\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)\xrightarrow{\partial}\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(1)}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{n-1}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2)
. Hence the map
\mathscr{H}^{n}(U)\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{n}(U)
is an isomorphism, as required.
∎

Thus we do not have to distinguish between
\mathscr{H}^{n}
and
\mathbf{H}^{n}
on smooth
k
-varieties. In the sequel, we will use the notation
\mathscr{H}^{n}
(as in
[
CS96
]
).

Symmetric bilinear forms on schemes.

Let
X
be a scheme and let
\mathcal{L}\in\underline{\Pic}(X)
. These data determine an exact category with duality structure on the category of locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules of finite rank in the sense of
[
Bal05
]
, with duality
\mathscr{E}^{\vee}=\underline{\Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(\mathscr{E},\mathcal{L})
(internal
\Hom
in the category of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules) and usual biduality. Its Witt group in the sense of Balmer, denoted by
\mathrm{W}(X,\mathcal{L})
, controls non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms with values in
\mathcal{L}
(hereafter
\mathcal{L}
-forms) modulo metabolic forms. The tensor product of forms determines an operation
\mathrm{W}(X,\mathcal{L})\times\mathrm{W}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{W}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
that turns
\mathrm{W}(X)=\mathrm{W}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
into a ring and
\mathrm{W}(X,\mathcal{L})
into a
\mathrm{W}(X)
-module. Inside the ring
\mathrm{W}(X)
lies the ideal
\mathrm{I}(X)
of even rank forms. We denote by
\mathrm{I}^{n}(X)
the
n
-th power of this ideal and extend this notation to
n\leqslant 0
by setting
\mathrm{I}^{n}(X)=\mathrm{W}(X)
in this case. Taking
X=\Spec F
where
F
is a field recovers the basic set-up of the theory of symmetric bilinear forms over fields.

Let
U
be an open subset of
X
. Every invertible section
a\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)^{\times}
determines a rank
1
form
\langle a\rangle:x\otimes y\mapsto axy
on
\mathscr{O}_{U}
, and multiplication by these forms determines an action of
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)^{\times}
on
\mathrm{W}(U)
on the right by group automorphisms that is functorial in
U
. In particular, this action preserves the rank so it induces an action on
\mathrm{I}^{n}(U)
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This construction induces a
\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times}]
-module structure on the sheaf
\mathscr{I}^{n}
on
X
associated with the presheaf
U\mapsto\mathrm{I}^{n}(U)
. We then denote by
\mathscr{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
the twist of
\mathscr{I}^{n}
by
\mathcal{L}
.

The above constructions are functorial with respect to arbitrary morphisms of schemes. In particular, for every open subscheme
U
of
X
, there is a localisation map
\mathrm{I}^{n}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)^{\times}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{L}^{0}(U)]\rightarrow\prod_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{I}^{n}(\kappa(x),\mathcal{L}(x))
. In fact, assuming that
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{k}^{\prime}
, this map factors through the subgroup
\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(U)
of
\bigoplus_{x\in U^{(0)}}\mathrm{I}^{n}(\kappa(x),\mathcal{L}(x))
, where we write
\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
for
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
to avoid the cumbersome notation
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})(U)
. The resulting homomorphism
\mathrm{I}^{n}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)^{\times}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{L}^{0}(U)]\rightarrow\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(U)
is functorial with respect to open immersions, yielding a morphism
\mathscr{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
of sheaves on
X
by sheafification.

Theorem 2.25
.

Let
X
be an essentially smooth
k
-scheme and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. Then the morphism
\mathscr{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
of sheaves on
X
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to check that this morphism is an isomorphism on stalks. By definition of
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
, it then suffices to show that if
A
is an essentially smooth local
k
-algebra with fraction field
K
and
L
is a line bundle on
\Spec A
, then denoting by
\partial
the twisted second residue homomorphism for Witt groups, the sequence

of abelian groups is exact. This follows, by induction on
n
, from two ingredients (see also
[
Gil07
, Corollary 7.8]
for an analogous argument in the context of coherent Witt groups). The first is purity for the Witt group
[
OP99
]
, namely exactness of the above sequence for Witt groups, thus for
n\leqslant 0
. The second is the affirmation of the universal exactness of the Gersten complex for Milnor
\mathrm{K}
-theory in
[
Ker09
]
which implies the exactness of the above complex for
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{M}}/2
and thus for
\overline{\mathrm{I}}^{*}
thanks to the affirmation of the Milnor conjecture.
∎

Thus as in the previous subsection, no distinction between
\mathscr{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
is required when dealing with sheaves on
X
. In the sequel, we will use the notation
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
or
\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
.

The quadratic real cycle class map

Jacobson’s signature map.

We recall Jacobson’s signature morphism of sheaves from
[

]
. Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety over
\mathbb{R}
; denote by
\iota:X(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion of the locus
X(\mathbb{R})
of real points of

into

. We endow

with the Euclidean topology for which

underlies a smooth manifold, and with the sheaf
\mathscr{C}_{X(\mathbb{R})}
of continuous functions with values in
\mathbb{R}
. Since polynomials are continuous for the Euclidean topology, the map

is continuous and in fact underlies a morphism

of locally ringed spaces, where the morphism
\iota^{\sharp}:\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\iota_{*}\mathscr{C}_{X(\mathbb{R})}
of sheaves restricts the sections of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
to the real locus. If

lies in

, and is locally constant as noted in
[

The quadratic real cycle class map for
\mathbf{I}

Theorem 2.31

Let

Proposition 2.32
.

Let

be a finitely generated extension of transcendence degree
d
. Then multiplication by
\llangle-1\rrangle
induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{I}^{n}(F)\rightarrow\mathrm{I}^{n+1}(F)
for every

and an epimorphism
\mathrm{I}^{d}(F)\rightarrow\mathrm{I}^{d+1}(F)
.

Proof.

If
-1
is a square in
F
, then
F
can be viewed as a finitely generated extension of
\mathbb{C}
of transcendence degree
d
. By
[
EKM08
, Theorem 97.7]
, the field
F
is then a
C_{d}
-field in the sense of
[
EKM08
, Section 97]
: this means that every homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
in
m>n^{d}
variables vanishes at a point in
F^{m}
distinct from
(0,\ldots,0)
. In particular, every quadratic form in
>2^{d}
variables with coefficients in
F
is isotropic, namely has a nontrivial zero. This applies to the
(d+1)
-fold Pfister forms
\llangle a_{1}\rrangle\otimes\cdots\otimes\llangle a_{d+1}\rrangle
where each
a_{i}
is a unit in
F
, which have dimension
2^{d+1}>2^{d}
. Now by
[
EKM08
, Corollary 9.11]
, isotropic Pfister forms are hyperbolic, namely have zero Witt class in
\mathrm{W}(F)
. By
[
EKM08
, Corollary 4.9]
, the subgroup
\mathrm{I}^{d+1}(F)
of
\mathrm{W}(F)
is generated by
(d+1)
-fold Pfister forms: we conclude that the group
\mathrm{I}^{d+1}(F)
vanishes. Therefore the map
\otimes\llangle-1\rrangle:\mathrm{I}^{d}(F)\rightarrow\mathrm{I}^{d+1}(F)=0
is surjective. Since
\mathrm{I}^{n}(F)\subseteq\mathrm{I}^{d+1}(F)
for every

, the group
\mathrm{I}^{n}(F)
also vanishes for every such
n
, hence multiplication by
\llangle-1\rrangle
is an isomorphism from

to

.

Now assume that
-1
is not a square in
F
and consider the field

. Then the extension

is finitely generated of transcendence degree

so again by

, Theorem 97.7]
, the field

is a

-field. Consequently, by the argument of the previous paragraph, the group

vanishes and thus so does its subgroup

. We then deduce from

that for every

\mathrm{I}^{n+1}(F)
is torsion free, so that the map

is injective. Since

in the Witt ring, we conclude that

is surjective and that

, as required.
∎

,

and

respectively. In particular, the component sheaves of these homotopy modules are

\rho(L):\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2^{d+1-n}(L))
. In particular, since
0\in\operatorname{Im}\gamma_{n/d+1}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
, the inverse image
2^{d+1-n}\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
of
\{0\}
is contained in
\operatorname{Im}\gamma_{n}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
. The following corollary is then immediate:

Corollary 4.3
.

For every
0\leqslant n\leqslant d
and every
c\geqslant 0
, the group
\Coker\gamma_{n}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
.

4.2
Applications to classes of curves and surfaces

Let again
X
be a connected smooth
\mathbb{R}
-variety of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
, and set
L=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R})
. In this subsection, we apply the results of the previous subsection to obtain exponents for the cokernel of the map

in the cases

(unconditionally on

) and

(in a number of significant cases).

Proposition 4.4
.

The image of the morphism
\gamma^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
is the inverse image of the subgroup
\operatorname{Im}\overline{\gamma}^{d-1}(X)
of
\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
under the reduction mod
2
homomorphism
\rho(L):\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
. In particular, the group
\Coker\gamma^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2
.

Remark 4.5
.

Recall that for every
n\geqslant 0
, modulo the natural isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{n}(X,\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{n})\cong\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)/2
, the morphism
\overline{\gamma}^{n}(X)
coincides with the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{n}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)/2\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{n}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
. Therefore in Proposition
4.4
, the group
\operatorname{Im}\overline{\gamma}^{d-1}(X)
is precisely the subgroup
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
of algebraic classes mentioned in the introduction. Hence Proposition
4.4
tells us that
\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
is precisely the subgroup of
\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
whose elements are those classes whose reduction mod
2
is algebraic in the usual sense. This was also observed in
[
Hor24
, Corollary 5.3]
which also contains the final statement of Proposition
4.4
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.4
is obvious if
X(\mathbb{R})
is empty (both groups are zero) so we may assume that
X(\mathbb{R})\neq\emptyset
. We check that the hypotheses of Lemma
4.2
apply with
(m,n,c)=(d,d-1,d-1)
, namely for the inclusion
\mathbf{I}^{d}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{d-1}(\mathcal{L})
in degree

. It suffices to verify that the map
\gamma_{d}^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L}):\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
is surjective, and that the map
\gamma^{d}(X,\mathcal{L}):\mathrm{H}^{d}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
is injective. The injectivity statement is part of Theorem
3.5
since
X(\mathbb{R})
is non-empty by assumption. Similarly, the exact sequence (
3.2
) of sheaves determines an exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d}(X,\overline{\mathbf{K}})

whose first map is identified by the isomorphism
\gamma_{d+1}^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L}):\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L}))\cong\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
with the map
\gamma_{d}^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
. The group
\mathrm{H}^{d}(X,\overline{\mathbf{K}})
vanishes by Proposition
3.3
again because

is not empty. Consequently, the map
\gamma_{d}^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
is surjective as required. Finally, since
0
lies in the group
\operatorname{Im}\overline{\gamma}^{d-1}(X)
, the subgroup
\rho(L)^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\overline{\gamma}^{d-1}(X))=\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
of

contains

. This implies the final statement of Proposition

and completes the proof.
∎

The following proposition, which provides a description of the cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d}(\mathcal{L}))
in a number of meaningful cases, will also allow us to analyse the cycle class map

Assume that

The proof of the above proposition (replacing

Corollary 4.8
.

Assume that the group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{2d-1}(X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}/2)

Proof.

Note that if

is empty, the conclusions of Corollary

) of sheaves. Consequently, the cokernel of the morphism

with

∎

5.  The quadratic real cycle class map for connected components

Let again
X
be a smooth real algebraic variety of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{L}\in\underline{\Pic}(X)
; we set
L=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R})
. Using the method of the previous section, we can also analyse the case of connected components, namely the map

\gamma^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}):\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathbf{W}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L)).

Denote by
\mathcal{C}
the set of connected components of
X(\mathbb{R})
and by
\mathcal{C}(L)
the subset of
\mathcal{C}
whose elements are those connected components
V
of
X(\mathbb{R})
such that
L_{|V}
is trivial. Note that
\mathcal{C}(L)=\mathcal{C}
if, and only if, the locally free sheaf
L
is globally free. Moreover, the group
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
is the free abelian group on the set
\mathcal{C}
, and the abelian group
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
is freely generated by
\mathcal{C}(L)
. This is a consequence of the following computation of sections of locally constant sheaves on
X(\mathbb{R})
(see in particular
[
Hor+21
, Proposition 2.58]
): given
V\in\mathcal{C}
, we have

\mathrm{H}^{0}(V,\mathbb{Z}(L))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if}\;L_{|V}\;\text{is\;trivial}\\
0&\text{else}.\end{array}\right.

We then have the following theorem of Krasnov and van Hamel (independently).

Theorem 5.1

(Krasnov, van Hamel)
.

The map
\overline{\gamma}_{d}^{0}(X):\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{d})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is surjective.

In fact, modulo the isomorphism
\mathscr{H}^{d}\cong\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{d}
provided by the affirmation of the Milnor conjecture, the morphism
\overline{\gamma}_{d}^{0}(X)
was constructed by Colliot-Thélène and Parimala in
[
CP90
, Section 2.1]
; denote it by
h_{d}^{0}
. By definition of
\mathscr{H}^{d}
, there is a sheafification map
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathscr{H}^{d})
and the morphism
\beta:\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
of
[
Ham00
, Theorem 2.8]
factors as
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathscr{H}^{d})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{d}^{0}}

(see in particular
[
Sch94
, (7.19.1)]
for a comparison of van Hamel’s map and the signature map
h_{d}^{0}
of Colliot-Thélène and Parimala). Then van Hamel observes in
[
Ham00
, Theorem 2.8]
that the stronger claim that the map
\beta:\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is surjective follows from
[
Kra95
, Corollary 3.2]
, whose proof works for arbitrary

smooth and connected although it is only stated for

projective in Krasnov’s work. Another argument is given in
[
BW20
, Remark 1.6 (ii)]
, that reads as follows. Every connected component
V
of
X(\mathbb{R})
has an equivariant fundamental class

in

which can be regarded as an element

of
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)
using Cox’s isomorphism
\mathrm{H}_{G}^{*}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}/2)\simeq\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)

. The image of

under

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)=\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{Z}/2\cdot[W]

. In particular, the image of
\beta
, which is a subgroup of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

Corollary 5.2
.

The map
\gamma_{d}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}):\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))

using Proposition

. Suppose until the end of the present section

Lemma 5.5
.

The group
\Gamma(L)
is the inverse image under the reduction map
\rho(L)
of the subgroup

of

Assume that
L
is trivial. Since
G
is generated by
\overline{\varepsilon}
and
\rho(\varepsilon)=\overline{\varepsilon}
, it follows that
\rho^{-1}(G)=\Ker\rho+\mathbb{Z}\cdot\varepsilon
, where
\Ker\rho=2\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
. This completes the proof in this case.

Now assume that

is non-trivial (observe that this assumption implies that

is non-empty). Let

be a connected component of

such that

is non-trivial. Projection onto the summand corresponding to

, whose generator we denote by

, induces a commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}\cdot[V]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/2\cdot[V]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{V}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\pi}_{V}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rho_{|\mathbb{Z}\cdot[V]}}}

as required.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

We have an equality
\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})=\Gamma(L)
of subgroups of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
with

\gamma^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
is the group
\rho(L)^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\overline{\gamma}^{0}(X))

, and using its notations, it now suffices to prove that

. Under the isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\overline{\mathbf{W}})\cong\mathrm{CH}^{0}(X)/2
, the map
\overline{\gamma}^{0}(X)
is the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map
\gamma_{\mathrm{BH}}^{0}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{0}(X)/2\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)

in
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
which is precisely
\overline{\varepsilon}
.
∎

6.  A refined conjecture on the image of the quadratic real cycle class map

As indicated in the introduction, it is not reasonable to expect the map
\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
to be surjective in general as the following basic obstruction indicates.

Example 6.1
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth variety over
\mathbb{R}
of dimension
\leqslant 3
. By
[
BW02
, Corollary 7.2]
, the map
\mathrm{W}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathbf{W})
induced by sheafification is then an isomorphism. It follows that
\gamma^{0}(X)
and the global signature map
\sign:\mathrm{W}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
have the same image. We view the elements of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
as families of integers indexed by the connected components of
X(\mathbb{R})
. If
b\in\mathrm{W}(X)
, then since the rank and signature of a symmetric bilinear form over
X
are equal mod
2
, the integers appearing in the tuple
\sign(b)
are either all even or all odd. This condition defines a subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
which is strict if
X(\mathbb{R})
has at least two connected components. In fact,
[
Kne76
, Theorem (10.4) iv)]
states that the image of the signature map is precisely
\Gamma
if the dimension
d
of
X
is
1
. This is precisely Proposition
5.6
with trivial twisting line bundle in view of the isomorphism
\mathrm{W}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathbf{W})
; since
[
BW02
, Corollary 7.2]
also yields an isomorphism
\mathrm{W}(X,\mathcal{L})\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathbf{W}(\mathcal{L}))
, we may regard Proposition
5.6
as a generalisation of Knebusch’s computation to arbitrary twisting line bundle.

Let
X
be a smooth
\mathbb{R}
-variety of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
; set
L=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R})
. Analytic techniques are not available on the smooth manifold
X(\mathbb{R})
. As a result, there is no natural candidate subgroup of
\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
through which
\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
could factor; in this regard, we note that the inverse image of
\operatorname{Im}\gamma_{c/d+1}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
under the reduction homomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2^{d+1-c}(L))
that we mentioned following Lemma
4.2
is just as mysterious as
\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
in general. To estimate the defect of the quadratic real cycle class map to be surjective, we will instead study the exponents of
\Coker\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
or, equivalently, the integers
e
such that
\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
contains
e\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R})\mathbb{Z}(L))
—the morphism
\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
is closer to being surjective the smaller
e
is. Jacobson’s theorem
2.30
provides the following result.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(n,c)
be a pair of integers such that
n\leqslant d
. Then
\Ker\gamma_{n}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2^{2(d+1-n)}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L})
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L})
, resp.
\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})
) denote the kernel (resp. cokernel, resp. image) of the multiplication by
\llangle-1\rrangle^{\otimes(d+1-n)}
map from
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
to
\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})
. Recall that
\llangle-1\rrangle=2
in the Witt ring hence this map is also multiplication by
2^{d+1-n}
(more precisely, it is the factored map of the multiplication map
\cdot 2^{d+1-n}:\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
by the subsheaf
\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
).

We claim that the sheaves
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L})
have exponent
2^{d+1-n}
. Indeed, for the kernel
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L})
, this is true because the morphism
\otimes\llangle-1\rrangle^{\otimes(d+1-n)}
is multiplication by
2^{d+1-n}
up to composition with a monomorphism. On the other hand, since
\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})
is a subsheaf of
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
, there is an inclusion
2^{d+1-n}\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq 2^{d+1-n}\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})=\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})
of subsheaves of
\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})
. It follows that
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L})=\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})/\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})
is a quotient of
\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})/2^{d+1-n}\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})
, which has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
, so the sheaf
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L})
also has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
. We conclude that the cohomology groups of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L})
have exponent
2^{d+1-n}
.

Up to the isomorphism
\gamma_{d+1}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
, the morphism
\gamma_{n}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
coincides with the map
f:\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L}))
induced in cohomology by the homomorphism
\otimes\llangle-1\rrangle^{\otimes(d+1-n)}
of sheaves, hence it suffices to prove that
\Ker f
has exponent
2^{2(d+1-n)}
. The morphism
f
factors through cohomology with coefficients in the image
\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})
as

\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))\xrightarrow{u}\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L}))\xrightarrow{u^{\prime}}\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L}))

where
u
and
u^{\prime}
are induced by the morphisms
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})
of sheaves respectively, hence
\Ker f
is an extension of
\Ker u
by
\Ker u^{\prime}
. It now suffices to show that
\Ker u
and
\Ker u^{\prime}
have exponent
2^{d+1-n}
. The exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow 0

of sheaves on
X
induces an exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))\xrightarrow{u}\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L}))

of abelian groups. Thus
\Ker u
is a quotient of
\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{L}))
which has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
as already observed so that
\Ker u
has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
. Similarly, the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow 0

induces an exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{c-1}(X,\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{L}))\xrightarrow{u^{\prime}}\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{d+1}(\mathcal{L}))

of abelian groups, where
\mathrm{H}^{c-1}(X,\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{L}))
has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
as previously observed. We deduce as before that
\Ker u^{\prime}
has exponent
2^{d+1-n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

This proposition, together with Corollary
4.3
, are quantitative versions of the following result of Jacobson.

Corollary 6.3

(
[
Jac17
, Corollary 8.9 (2)]
)
.

For every pair
(n,c)
of integers, the map

\gamma_{n}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]:\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L}))\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}[1/2](L))

obtained by inverting
2
is an isomorphism.

Remark 6.4
.

This stands in sharp contrast with the status of the rational Hodge conjecture whose validity is unknown: we are really interested in integral statements.

The exponents obtained in Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
6.2
are not optimal. For instance, if
n=c=0
and
d=1
, it follows from Knebusch’s work
[
Kne76
]
cited in Example
6.1
that
\Ker\gamma^{0}(X)
and
\Coker\gamma^{0}(X)
have exponent
2
, whereas Proposition
6.2
gives the exponent
2^{2(1+1-0)}=16
for the kernel and Corollary
4.3
gives the exponent
2^{1+1-0}=4
for the cokernel.

We contend that the exponent of Corollary
4.3
for the cokernel can be improved, but only slightly. To state our conjecture precisely, consider the following assertions, parametrised by triples
(X,\mathcal{L},c)
where
X
is a smooth
\mathbb{R}
-variety of dimension
d
, the letter
\mathcal{L}
denotes a line bundle on
X
inducing a locally free module
L=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R})
on
X(\mathbb{R})
and
c
is an integer such that
0\leqslant c\leqslant d
.

P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
:
The cokernel of the map
\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L}):\mathrm{H}^{c}(X,\mathbf{I}^{c}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
has exponent
2^{d-c}
.

Note that by Lemma
2.29
, Assertion
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
is equivalent to

\widetilde{P}(X,\mathcal{L},c)
:
The cokernel of the map
\widetilde{\gamma}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L}):\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))
has exponent
2^{d-c}
.

For brevity, we only consider the property
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
. We can now state the refined conjecture mentioned in the introduction:

Conjecture 6.5
.

For every triple
(X,\mathcal{L},c)
as above, Assertion
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
holds. Moreover, the exponent
2^{d-c}
predicted by Assertion
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
is the best possible in the following sense: for every pair
(c,d)
of non-negative integers such that
c<d
, there exists a smooth variety
X
over
\mathbb{R}
of dimension
d
and a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
such that
\Coker\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
does not have exponent
2^{d-c-1}
.

We observe that a number of positive results towards this conjecture have been collected in the previous sections:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth
\mathbb{R}
-variety of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. Then Assertions
P(X,\mathcal{L},i)
holds for
i\in\{d,d-1,0\}
. Moreover
P(X,\mathcal{L},d-2)
also holds if the étale cohomology group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{2d-1}(X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}/2)
vanishes.

Proof.

In codimension
d
, we have to prove that
\Coker\gamma^{d}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2^{d-d}=1
, namely that this cokernel vanishes, in other words that
\gamma^{d}(X,\mathcal{L})
is surjective: this is Theorem
3.5
. In codimension
d-1
, we have to show that
\Coker\gamma^{d-1}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2^{d-(d-1)}=2
: this is the final statement of Proposition
4.4
. In codimension
d-2
, we have to prove that
\Coker\gamma^{d-2}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2^{d-(d-2)}=4
if
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{2d-1}(X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}/2)
vanishes: this is Corollary
4.8
. In codimension
0
, it remains to establish that
\Coker\gamma^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
has exponent
2^{d}
: this is Proposition
5.3
.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

We refer the reader to
[
CS96
, Examples 4.3.1]
for examples of satisfaction of the étale cohomological vanishing condition of Theorem
6.6
. We mention here that it is satisfied for affine varieties, for cohomological dimension reasons, and for proper varieties
X
such that
X(\mathbb{C})
is simply connected since, by Poincaré duality, the group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{2d-1}(X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}/2)
is then isomorphic to
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{1}(X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}/2)
which vanishes under the simple connectedness assumption.

We now enumerate cases covered by Theorem
6.6
and comment on the optimality of exponents.

Example 6.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth real curve and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. Then by Theorem
6.6
, Assertion
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
is satisfied for all
c
since the set of possible codimensions is
\{1,0\}=\{d,0\}
. Moreover, the exponents predicted by Conjecture
6.5
are optimal. Indeed, we only have to check that there exists a smooth real curve
X
such that the map
\gamma^{0}(X):\mathbf{W}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
is not surjective. As explained in Example
6.1
, this map can be identified with the global signature morphism
\sign:\mathrm{W}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
whose image is strict if
X(\mathbb{R})
is not connected, for example if
X=\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus 0
.

Example 6.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth real surface and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. Then again, by Theorem
6.6
, Assertion
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
is satisfied for all
c
since the set of possible codimensions is
\{2,1,0\}=\{d,d-1,0\}
. Note that the exponent
2^{2}=4
is optimal for surfaces in codimension
0
: there exist smooth surfaces
X
such that
\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{0}(X)
does not contain
2\mathrm{H}^{0}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
. For example, according to
[
Mon00
, Proposition 5.1]
, this is the case if
X
is projective, geometrically connected and rational (that is, the complex variety
X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C}
is birational to
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
), and
X(\mathbb{R})
has at least two connected components.

Example 6.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth real
3
-fold and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. Then Assertion
P(X,\mathcal{L},c)
is satisfied for every
c\in\{d=3,d-1=2,d=0\}
, and in codimension
c=1
if
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}^{5}(X\times_{\mathbb{R}}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}/2)
vanishes, for example if
X
is affine or if
X
is proper and
X(\mathbb{C})
is simply connected.

Example 6.11
.

Let
X=\mathbb{A}^{d}\setminus 0
with
d\geqslant 2
. Then since
X
is an open subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{d}
, the restriction map
\mathrm{CH}^{d-1}(\mathbb{A}^{d})\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{d-1}(X)
is surjective and thus
\mathrm{CH}^{d-1}(X)
vanishes since
\mathrm{CH}^{d-1}(\mathbb{A}^{d})=0
by
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance of Chow groups (recall that
d-1\geqslant 1
). It follows that the homomorphism
\overline{\gamma}^{d-1}(X):\mathrm{CH}^{d-1}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is the zero map, thus by Proposition
4.4
, the image of
\gamma^{d-1}(X)
is given by

\rho^{-1}(\{0\})=\Ker\rho=2\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})

where
\rho:\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is the reduction mod
2
homomorphism. But
X(\mathbb{R})
is homotopic to the
(d-1)
-sphere
\mathrm{S}^{d-1}
hence
\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
and thus
2\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})\neq\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
. It follows that
\Coker\gamma^{d-1}(X)=\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})/2\mathrm{H}^{d-1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
is non-zero, that is, it does not have exponent
1
. Thus
2=2^{d-(d-1)}
is the best possible exponent in codimension
d-1
. Combined with Example
6.9
, we then see that the exponents predicted by Conjecture
6.5
are optimal for surfaces.

Remark 6.12
.

Let
X
be a smooth
\mathbb{R}
-variety of dimension
d
, let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
and let
c
be an integer such that
0\leqslant c\leqslant d
; we set
L=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R})
. Although analysis does not appear to provide a clear candidate subgroup containing
\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
, one could instead take inspiration from the topological conditions used in
[
BW20
]
based on Steenrod squares. Indeed, note that the twisted Steenrod squares
\Sq_{L}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*+1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}(L))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*+1}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
provide the differentials of the first page of the Bockstein spectral sequence associated with the filtration

\cdots\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}(L)\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}(L)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}(L)\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}(L)

of
\mathbb{Z}(L)
. On the other hand, one can consider the Pardon spectral sequence studied in
[
Par
]
, which is the spectral sequence associated with the filtered sheaf

\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n+1}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{I}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathbf{W}(\mathcal{L}).

We note that Jacobson’s signature homomorphisms induce a morphism
\mathbf{W}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\iota_{*}\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{L})
of filtered sheaves (where
\iota:X(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion as usual) and thus a morphism of spectral sequences from Pardon’s construction to the Bockstein spectral sequence. Moreover, the classes in the cohomology of
\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathcal{L})
coming from higher terms of the filtration can be identified by vanishing conditions for differentials in higher pages, and the image of these classes under the quadratic real cycle class map must then be killed by the corresponding differentials. One can therefore envision to propose a candidate subgroup for the image of
\gamma^{c}(X,\mathcal{L})
in terms of these vanishing conditions, which could incidentally shed light on Conjecture
6.5
. We hope to pursue this approach in future work.
